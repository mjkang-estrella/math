## M1 dilation products

B_n(S)=chi(y(S)*y([n]S)) has a concrete O(log q) pointwise evaluator and a
constant-point-update consecutive evaluator. For p not dividing2n its odd divisor
support is E[2n] minus E[2], size4n²−4. A compact circuit can indeed have large
geometric conductor. L0's abstract mod-square divisor lemma is valid under odd
#E(Fp), but only bounds degree by ACTUAL odd support. Its blanket claim that
every EDS/net term or arbitrary product of lines has bounded support is false.
Character equality after removing a square also excludes its zeros and poles.

A normalization-independent identity is
`y(S)*y([n]S)=psi_2(S)*psi_(2n)(S)/(4*psi_n(S)^4)` where defined.
Do not adopt the raw omega_n formula without conventions. Nor adopt its uniform
minimal-endomorphism estimate: small CM discriminants are possible. Moreover
`B_(n^-1)(k)=B_n(n^-1*k)` implies identical Fourier-magnitude multisets; a large
integer n need not evade a small-degree representative.

Root adds a simple gate: B_n is even under S->−S, since each y-character gains
chi(−1) and their product gains1. With zero at identity/undefined points and odd q,
its full canonical parity correlation is exactly0 by pairing k with q−k.
Thus the original parity reduction cannot apply to this TWO-factor M1. This
does not exclude nonzero Fourier coefficients, a different target predicate,
or the proposer's three-factor variant. It further separates the actual bridge
obligation from an unproved 'large degree means large bias' suggestion.

No explicit n rule, useful heavy frequency or paid discovery method is supplied.
Typical-amplitude claims are heuristics, not rejection proofs. Searching millions
of spectra would not falsify all unspecified structured rules or establish scaling.

## M2 structured interval products

For F_s(S)=product_(j=1..s)(x(S)−x(jH)), [Bernstein–De Feo–Leroux–Smith,
Theorem4.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.10118) supports square-root-size structured
root-product evaluation, with index-system setup, polynomial arithmetic and storage
charged. Reviewer supplies explicit blocks: J=1..b, centers b+1+(2b+1)t, and K
the omitted centers/remainder, all O(sqrt(s)) size. This supports the x-window;
the proposed y-window extension still lacks its exact formula/exceptions.

The increment's endpoint divisor parity is correct. The ratio is square-equivalent
to a constant times the stated line times(x−x(H)), away from exceptional points.
But for2s<q the character has zeros on ±1..±s and conventionally at0. A multiplicative
update cannot leave a zero mask and recover a nonzero value; boundary restarts cost
another square-root evaluation. The exceptional line/vertical points also count.

The reviewer derives, for p≈q and1<=L<=s with s+L<q/2,
`|A(L)| << ((L+1)*sqrt(p)+s+L)/q` after restoring masked evaluations.
Moving-average/Cauchy gives
`|Bhat(a)|² << 1/M + M*sqrt(p)/q + (s+M)/q`.
Taking M≈q^(1/4) gives envelope `q^(-1/4)+s/q`; the O(q^(-1/8)) amplitude
bound follows only in the stated s<=q^(3/4) range. It is not a unit-constant
finite threshold and gives no evidence any coefficient approaches its upper bound.

## Recovery bridge and scope

Given a KNOWN nonzero frequency a of magnitude>=epsilon, independent uniform r
gives the exact demodulated mean
`E[B(Q+rG)*exp(−2pi*i*a*r/q)]=Bhat(a)*exp(2pi*i*a*k/q)`.
Bounded-sample concentration permits epsilon^(-2) samples for constant phase
precision, with base-phase calibration, doubled-target reconstruction and logarithmic
confidence overhead. Heavy-frequency discovery S(epsilon), point construction,
all setup and verification remain paid. This is a conditional bridge, not an
epsilon^(-2) complete ECDLP algorithm or universal information-theoretic optimum.

For M1, independent predictor costs are polylogarithmic. For M2 they multiply
the square-root evaluator cost: roughly sqrt(s)*epsilon^(-2), plus discovery.
The claimed additive cost needs a correlated-block variance proof, not merely
cheap consecutive updates. The raw expectation retained a random starting phase
without the necessary demodulation; spectral flatness and leakage remain unproved.

The explicit square-root product circuit can show why THAT G1 implementation
loses, not close all compact circuits. The accepted earlier reduction has total
epsilon^(-6) cost; its optional epsilon^(-4) query reuse was never a full-work
exponent result. Do not conflate them in M2's comparison. No pilot until an actual
magnitude rule and its discovery cost survive review; no generic Fourier machinery.
