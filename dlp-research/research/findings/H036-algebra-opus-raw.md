## Distinct mechanisms examined and why each fails

| # | Mechanism (public-input bridge) | Why it fails | Prior coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Move the problem into a bigger curve's Jacobian.** Glue E to an auxiliary curve to get a genus-g curve C over F_p whose Jacobian contains E, then run index calculus there. | Index calculus in genus g costs about p^(2−2/g): p^1 at g=2 and p^(4/3) at g=3, both worse than p^(1/2). The large-genus subexponential regime costs roughly exp(c·√(g·log p·log(g·log p))) with g ≳ log p, still far above p^(1/2). The GHS-style gains need an extension-field subfield, which a prime field lacks. | Kani was dismissed in the records only in the isogeny-secret sense. This exponent calculation for the transfer itself is new. |
| 2 | **Lift to an elliptic surface over F_p(t)** (function-field analogue of xedni) with explicit sections and a computable lattice. | Specialising at t₀ sends the section lattice Z^r onto Z/q. The relations that would reveal k live in a kernel of index q, and finding them requires the unknown logarithms of the specialised sections. This is the existing lift-zero barrier moved to F_p(t). | Lift-zero barrier, H003 and H026 lift scopes |
| 3 | **Combine relations across an ℓ-isogeny class** (Vélu maps, union of factor bases). | Rational isogenies are bijections on the prime-order groups, so they only relabel points. Pulled-back relations keep the same Semaev degree, so the solving bottleneck is unchanged. | Petit–Kosters–Messeng maps; volcano dismissal in H024/H027 raw reports |
| 4 | **Infinite-order endomorphisms** used for orbit-style speedups. | Frobenius acts as 1 on E(F_p), so any endomorphism acts there as a scalar already known mod q. The only orbit speedups need finite-order cheap maps, which is the automorphism baseline. | H030 automorphism reconciliation |
| 5 | **Pohlig–Hellman through ℓ-torsion or extension fields.** | G has prime order q, so its projection onto any ℓ-part is zero. Pairings need an embedding degree, which is infeasible here. | MOV and pairing dismissals |
| 6 | **Representation technique** (the BCJ/Howgrave-Graham–Joux approach from subset sum). | It needs a homomorphic filter onto a smaller quotient group. A prime-order group has none, and coordinate hashes are not homomorphic, so it reduces to plain collision search. | H007 additive screens |
| 7 | **Use the unordered pair {x(P+Q), x(P−Q)} as the collision key** (computed inversion-free from x_P, x_Q). | A match needs two independent coincidences, about 1/q² per pair. That is strictly worse than H034's separate keys. | New, but dominated by H034 |
| 8 | **Factor base generated by the walk itself, with 3-term x-relations.** | This is H034's paired sums or H017's SP3 under another name. | H034, H017 |

Each row's argument works as its own falsifier, and none needs a numerical test. Rows 1 and 2 are the only ones not already settled in the records. Their exponent and index arguments are paper-level, so a quick independent review would be worthwhile before you file them as rejected.
