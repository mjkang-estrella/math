## Shapes examined

**Side point from one addition.** Galbraith–Wang–Zhang (ePrint 2015/605, the paragraph after the \(X{+}Y\) / \(X{-}Y\) observation) already record that computing \(X{-}Y\) with \(X{+}Y\) does not fit a one-direction rho walk. The incremental cost of that second point sits in \([1/3,2/5]\) of a \(5M{+}S\) step. The image semiconjugates the walk only when it is the predecessor, which has coefficient \(0\) on \(k\). A further image priced at half a step or more falls under the H018 ratio \((1+\delta c)/\sqrt{1+\delta}>1\).

**Cheap \(\pm G\) runs.** The H023 translation at \(5M\) with no squaring, and the H024 slope step at \(4M{+}S\), are real savings on a single translation. On the reflection quotient every memoryless \(\pm G\) rule is a path of 2-cycles, so those steps do not mix. Across two length-\(L\) strides the useful relations are the \(O(L)\) alignments \(T_0=\pm T_0'+rG\) with \(|r|\le 2sL\) (H025), so the work scales as \(\sqrt{qL}\). Used as a scanner against a random stored set, the same translation is baby-step giant-step with giant-step cost \(5M\), above the x-only giant at about \(2.5M{+}S\). A later clock or unmarked portal that breaks those 2-cycles is the parked H027 / H030 / H031 family.

**Doubling and halving branches.** On a prime-order group, multiplication by \(2\) is bijective on the negation quotient, so the visited set does not shrink. The x-only doubling core is \(2M{+}2S{+}I\), about \(5M{+}2S\) once inverses are batched, above \(5M{+}S\). A fixed list of doublings contributes \(O(L)\) cross-seed ratios, not \(L^2\) independent hits (H024). Prime-field halving still extracts a square root. The halving iteration in Zhang–Wang (ePrint 2011/461) and the matching remark in Galbraith–Gaudry (ePrint 2015/1022) are binary-field algorithms.

**Isogeny steps and summation relations.** An \(\mathbb{F}_p\)-isogeny sends the rational subgroup to another curve of order \(q\), still without rational 2-torsion, so the walk on the image is the same full-range problem. A degree-\(q\) map is not a per-step operator. Prime-field Semaev index calculus, including the single-relation variant of Amadori–Pintore–Sala (ePrint 2017/609), is reported not to beat rho; Yokoyama–Yasuda–Takahashi–Kogure (J. Math. Cryptol. 14, 2020) bound the naive summation version the same way. Treating a Gröbner or resultant call as free would be an unpaid oracle.

**Quotients, self-reductions, and storage tuning.** A second involution composes with negation to a translation, so the joint quotient is not a second \(\sqrt{2}\). A rational function induces a positive-density identification of rational points only for \(n=\pm 1\). Emitting \(Q_i=u_iQ+v_iG\) covers only the preimage union of the searched sets (H033). Distinguished-point rate, doubling escapes from fruitless cycles, and shorter endpoint keys are part of the Bernstein–Lange–Schwabe / Wang–Zhang negation ledger. The one-field slope key saves a stored field element after a \(2M\) conversion and leaves the walk cost unchanged.

## What would still be a card

A later card needs one named public map on the negation quotient, an affine label update with a nonzero coefficient of \(k\) on the hits that are counted, step cost strictly below one batched \(5M{+}S\) addition on those hits, and a success law in which the expected number of useful hits grows like \((\text{paid samples})^2/q\). Bounded \(\pm G\) strides, one extra point from the same addition, a phase clock, and an unnamed decomposition oracle do not meet that description. No such map is in hand, so inventing a falsifier for an unnamed operator would not be a candidate.
