## Corrected construction

Let E/Fp have a rational cyclic subgroup generated by G of odd prime order q,
p>3, and Q=kG. For primitive r-th root zeta in Fq define mu=zeta^-1 and
G_L=(G,[mu]G), V=(Q,[mu]Q). Setup includes the two scalar multiplications.
The nonzero eigenline is L*={(P,[mu]P):P in <G>,P!=O}.

For r=4, sigma4(P1,P2)=(-P2,P1) acts as [zeta] on L. The raw report's
mu=-zeta^-1 instead yields eigenvalue -zeta. For r=6 and
zeta²-zeta+1=0, sigma6(P1,P2)=(P1-P2,P1) acts as [zeta].

With iota²=-1 in Fp or Fp², phi4=y1+iota*y2 satisfies
phi4(sigma4(P))=iota*phi4(P). If p=3 mod4, iota is outside Fp, so on
L* both ordinates are finite and nonzero and phi4 cannot vanish. If iota is
in Fp this argument does not apply: nonvanishing needs separate evidence.

For primitive sixth root w in Fp or Fp², define

    phi6(P1,P2)=y(P1)+w^-1*y(P1-P2)-w^-2*y(P2).

Then phi6(sigma6(P))=w*phi6(P). Substitution uses w³=-1;
w=-1 would satisfy that equation but would NOT supply full sextic information.
On L*, the three ordinate arguments are nonidentity since mu!=0,1.

Nonvanishing is essential. On a rational j=0 curve whose order-three map
(x,y)->(beta*x,y) acts on the subgroup by lambda, choose zeta=-lambda.
Then y(zeta*P)=y(zeta^-1*P)=-y(P), and P-[mu]P=[zeta]P. Consequently

    phi6(P,[mu]P)=y(P)*(1-w^-1+w^-2)=0.

Thus the named sextic observable can vanish identically on the eigenline.
This is an algebraic obstruction for that observable under these hypotheses,
not a rejection of every equivariant function or every E² construction.

## Exact conditional transfer identity

Let S be a complete transversal of Fq*/<zeta>, r=ord(zeta)=ord(w), and
phi(sigma(P))=w*phi(P). Assume phi finite and nonzero at every s*G_L,
s in S. Equivariance extends that property to L*. For k!=0 write uniquely

    k*s=zeta^e(s)*pi(s),  pi a permutation of S.

Multiplying scalar equalities, then function equalities, gives

    zeta^(sum e(s))=k^((q-1)/r),
    product_S phi(s*V) / product_S phi(s*G_L)=w^(sum e(s)).

This identifies roots through the abstract correspondence zeta->w; it does
not assume an embedding Fq into Fp. Handle k=0 through Q=O separately.
A nonzero reference product certifies its factors, but evaluating that product
and constructing/enumerating S are paid work. No fast evaluator, sharing
algorithm or competitive joint scalar recovery follows from this identity.

## Restricted finite-order dimension screen

Suppose A in GL_g(Z) has FINITE integer-matrix order N and q does not divide N.
If reduction modulo q has an eigenvalue of exact order r, its characteristic
polynomial contains Phi_r and hence phi(r)<=g. Reason: over Q the characteristic
polynomial factors into cyclotomic polynomials with indices dividing N; their
reductions have roots of the corresponding exact orders because q is tame.
An infinite-order integer matrix with a large-order eigenvalue modulo q is
outside this statement. The matrix's order and an eigenline's order must not
be silently equated.

Moreover

    r/phi(r)^2 = product_(ell^a || r) ell^(2-a)/(ell-1)^2 <= 2.

Only ell=2,a=1 contributes a factor above one. Therefore r<=2g².
Under the ADDITIONAL stipulated price of g ordinary additions per step and
an ideal random r-class collision model, the relative arithmetic proxy to
ideal negation rho is g*sqrt(2/r)>=1. This is a conditional comparison,
not a lower bound on fused coordinate arithmetic or on useful stopping time.

Literal coordinatewise addition on E² pays two point additions. Literal
order-six key construction may add a third for P1-P2. Order-four key selection
still pays comparisons, signs and any normalization. These schedules do not
prove every representation must pay two or three additions. Labels, useful
collision probability, cycle handling, DP tails, starts, replay, memory and
final verification all remain outside the ideal group-addition ratio.
For a class match R=[zeta^e]R', labels require b-zeta^e*b'!=0 for ordinary
division recovery of k; all coefficient work is over Fq and final kG=Q is paid.
