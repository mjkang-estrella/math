## Shifted involution: literal cost, not a canonicalization lower bound

iota(P)=-P-G has(q+1)/2 classes and is conjugate to ordinary negation by
translation through G/2. Labels change(a,b)->(-a-1,-b). It gives no extra
fold. Explicitly materializing iota(P) before a table jump pays two regular
additions with dependent batches. Under an ASSUMED matched useful-hit law,
this literal schedule loses the single-addition arithmetic comparison.

Quotient size does not establish a rho stopping constant, and no fruitless-label
probability follows without a label model. Comparing two coordinates cheaply
does not imply their equality; equality also occurs at the nonzero fixed point
-G/2. Thus both raw cost-impossibility implications fail. Jump-table setup
O(r) additions requires an actual incremental schedule, not arbitrary labels.
H019's s=1 arithmetic ratio7sqrt(2)/(9sqrt(pi))≈.62 is scoped high-memory
comparison, not a measured runtime or all-budget result.

## Exact one-field endpoint identifier

For iota=-P-G, put u=x(P)-g,v=x(P+G)-g, G=(g,yG). The invariant slope
lambda through G satisfies

    lambda=(3g²+A-u*v)/(2yG),
    u+v=lambda²-3g,
    u*v=3g²+A-2yG*lambda.

Proof: substitute the line y=yG+lambda*(x-g) into the cubic and divide by
the known factor x-g. In shifted coordinate t=x-g, the residual quadratic
is t²+(3g-lambda²)t+(3g²+A-2yG*lambda). Its roots are exactly u,v,
including a repeated/tangent root. Therefore lambda determines the entire
unordered admissible pair. The vertical O/-G class uses an infinity sentinel.

For H023's reflection G-P, use the chord through -G instead:

    lambda=(u*v-(3g²+A))/(2yG),
    u=x(P)-g, v=x(P-G)-g.

The same residual-root argument applies with the sign of yG reversed; the
vertical O/G class is its sentinel. A prepared inverse of2yG and one product
u*v make the finite endpoint cost2M plus additions. Active states still use
two fields. A one-field slope endpoint with full equality is exact, not a
fingerprint; whole-pair hashing can separately select distinguished status.
All setup, endpoint construction, labels/replay and actual storage are paid.

This refines H023's record options: two-field endpoint storage is not mandatory.
It supplies no cheaper arbitrary table jump, additional quotient advantage or
mixing/stopping law. No complete solver saving follows from the smaller key.

## Cubic square-class predicate is constant

For a nonsingular short-Weierstrass curve with p>3 and odd FULL rational
group order, the cubic f has no rational root (no nonzero rational2-torsion),
so it is irreducible. Let L=F_(p³), theta a root, z=x-theta for an affine
rational point(x,y). Then

    Norm(z)=f(x)=y²!=0,
    z^((p³-1)/2)=Norm(z)^((p-1)/2)=1.

Thus every such z is a square. That particular square-class filter accepts
every affine rational point. No descent theorem needs to be assumed.
A chosen square root/coefficient can still vary. A root chosen from x alone
cannot distinguish P from -P; halving must handle orientation. Unique rational
halving is not a lower bound on the cost of all halving algorithms. General
finite-field square-root extraction is not uniformly a single exponentiation.
Retain only the constant predicate screen, not an exclusion of all root signals.

## Global integer defect: exact amplitude bound, no universal filter rejection

For any finite group and integer-valued f, assume

    |f(X+Y)-f(X)-f(Y)| <= D  for EVERY X,Y.

Average over Y. Translation permutes the summands, so the average defect is
-f(X); hence |f(X)|<=D and image size<=2*floor(D)+1. No normalization is
needed. Therefore D=o(q^(1/3)) cannot coexist with Omega(q^(1/3)) different
integer key values. This is the precise incompatible pair of hypotheses.

It does not prove that every defect window retains every candidate. Example:
f(k)=k in[0,q-1] on Z/q has defect<=q. For inputs both q-1 the predicted
window intersects that image in just q-2,q-1. This counters the raw inference;
it does not supply a computable ECDLP key. Structured restricted pairs might
also have a useful index, which needs its own cost analysis.

One violating pair refutes a specified global bound; a sampled median cannot
verify it. Homomorphisms to additive Fp are forced trivial only if q!=p;
homomorphisms from an odd-order group to a finite2-group are trivial. Recovery
coefficient probabilities require a distribution; one target-shifted list can
instead give coefficient1 deterministically. No universal bounded-defect merge
impossibility, new numerical test or main-goal improvement is established.
