## Hybrid walk and fixed baby table: conditional formula

Assume the IDEAL survival law S(t)=exp(-(t²+2*b*t)/q), with b=beta*sqrt(q)
distinct signed babies. This is an assumption about reporting, useful collisions
and the walk; no actual negation/DP process is proved to obey it here. Define

    g(beta)=integral_0^infinity exp(-u²-2*beta*u) du
           =exp(beta²)*(sqrt(pi)/2)*erfc(beta),
    g'(beta)=2*beta*g(beta)-1.

With baby cost c_b, walk cost c_w and EXTRA per-walk-point lookup cost P,
the corresponding leading field/model invoice is

    W_hybrid/sqrt(q)=c_b*beta+(c_w+P)*g(beta), beta>0.

The no-table baseline is c_w*g(0), with NO unnecessary lookup charge. Thus a
positive constant lookup price creates a right-limit gap P*g(0), and its
small-beta gain over that baseline is

    -P*g(0)+(c_w+P-c_b)*beta+O(beta²).

The report's (2.5M-P)*beta gain expression is wrong. Its negative right
derivative c_b-c_w-P does not imply that arbitrarily small tables help against
the no-lookup baseline. The correct conditional break-even requirement is

    P < [c_w*(g(0)-g(beta))-c_b*beta]/g(beta).

A separate matched-memory BSGS comparison and ALL other overhead still apply.
Sol's final review preserves a CONDITIONAL region: for c_b<c_w, small fixed
beta>0 and sufficiently small positive P, this invoice can beat no-table rho.
Against the report's static signed-BSGS invoice c_b*(beta+1/(4*beta)), also
require (c_w+P)*g(beta)<c_b/(4*beta). Both conditions can hold in that ideal
model. This does not establish the best matched-memory comparator or actual
complete-work superiority. For beta(q)->0, the allowable lookup price is
P_rho(beta)=(c_w-c_b)*beta/g(0)+O(beta²); a fixed positive price eventually
defeats this particular operator. With P=0 its relative saving still tends to
zero. Withdraw the raw report's blanket rejection rather than hiding this
conditional opening. No realistic memory or hardware price is assumed.
One H035 hash formula costing M+A is not a lower bound on every permissible
table lookup. Likewise the report's beta<=2^(-80) assertion has no memory budget;
for a128-bit subgroup order it would mean less than one stored record. Reject
that numerical resource claim and all purported calibrated practical conclusions.

For table growth b(t)=gamma*t, the raw exp(-(1+gamma)*t²/q) law corresponds
only to ONE-WAY detection: new walk points query existing babies, while new
babies merely insert. At P=0, work scales as
(c_w+c_b*gamma)/sqrt(1+gamma). For c_w=5M+S,c_b=2.5M+S its derivative at0 is
S/2>=0 and the derivative stays nonnegative for gamma>=0. That restricted
ideal-model minimum is correct. If new babies also query ALL prior walk points,
the cross term doubles and the denominator becomes sqrt(1+2*gamma); retaining
and searching those walk points is a different paid-memory/detection contract.
DP archives or a hard baby cap require still other laws. No universal hybrid
closure follows. Native Sol independently checked these derivative corrections.

## DP-filtered table: exact coverage, not an exact waiting law

Native Astra accepted this narrow statement. Condition on a target-independent
table S of M>=1 known scalar values and fixed target-independent offsets a_j.
A signed hit of Q-a_j*G implies k in (a_j+S) union(a_j-S). For uniform k,

    Pr(T>t) >= max(1-2*M*t/q,0),
    E[T] >= q/(4*M)-O(1).

This counts distinct covered scalar values; overlap only weakens coverage.
Capped searches inherit the truncated survival sum, not free fallback work.
Direct infinity tests add their explicitly counted candidates if zero is absent
from S. Literal translated-query hits have target coefficient1 and are useful;
more general walk labels can produce null relations.

Independent geometric trials of success probability2M/q would instead have
mean q/(2M). The raw report conflates the two models. A filtered long progression
is not automatically equivalent to a contiguous shorter progression, and the
coverage lower bound does not show identical giant work.

For a sign-invariant distinguished predicate, a true signed hit on a stored
distinguished point also satisfies the predicate. Skipping other lookups can
therefore save table traffic without losing THESE direct matches. Whether that
pays for constructing N rather than M babies requires a complete matched
predicate/filter/memory ledger. Extra baby arithmetic alone does not settle it.
Unstored babies, however, do not automatically contribute detectable trails:
that needs an actual common transition, reporting and recovery construction.

## Generic encodings do not make adaptive restart useless

Conditional on a complete formal-query/equality transcript, deferred random
encoding strings need not distinguish compatible scalar values; a uniform prior
may remain uniform over that compatible set. Equality and noncollision outcomes
still constrain k and may affect later query choices. The next success chance
depends on the chosen formal coefficients and surviving constraints, not elapsed
time or physically retained records alone.

The raw inference to deterministic-cutoff optimality is invalid. Astra's
elementary runtime counterexample is an independent-start process with equal
probability of an observable null self-loop (recognized after one step) or
success after L>1 steps. The mode is independent of the secret. Immediate abort
of observed loops costs L+1 in expectation. A common deterministic cutoff below
L never succeeds; one at least L costs at least2L in expectation. This models
informative prognosis without a secret predictor. It is not a claimed ECDLP
construction or improvement for any particular walk.

Keeping information weakly dominates discarding it only if retention is free
and can be ignored. With memory limits, lookup, replay or construction costs,
that premise fails. A DP endpoint archive is not proved maximal retained
coverage. Its benefit depends on transitions, coalescence, reporting delays
and record policies. No universal restart/search-policy closure is accepted.
