## Semidirect scope gate

Primary: Arif and Imran, [arXiv2508.05048v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05048v1),
2025-08-07. Root read Problem1, Definition1, §3.2/Theorem6 and the concluding
identity-case discussion. The advertised constant-size EC orbit uses a
nonidentity geometric automorphism. Theorem6 explicitly states this restriction;
its claim is not a general ordinary-ECDLP solver. Native Sol confirms this scope.

Root derivation: for alpha=id, the sum of t iterates of P is[t]P, with period q
when P has prime order q. With alpha=-id it alternates O,P and reveals only
t parity. In general, a nontrivial finite-order geometric alpha has vanishing
trace endomorphism, so the corresponding orbit is short. A general target
Q=[k]P is not thereby converted into a short-orbit instance recovering k.
For the rational-point problem alpha must preserve E(Fp). Nonzero geometric
endomorphisms are isogenies with nonzero compositions, justifying cancellation
in (alpha-1)(1+alpha+...+alpha^(n-1))=0. With p>3 the automorphism order is at
most6. Enumeration uses constantly many group operations, not constant bit
cost; it recovers the exponent only modulo the actual short period.

The absent operation is an efficient full-input transfer preserving the unknown
scalar, with its advice, cost and injectivity obligations accounted for.

Furthermore, the abstract cyclic group generated by P has q-1 automorphisms
[m], m in Fq*, whereas the geometric curve automorphisms preserving O are a
different class. The small geometric bound must not be applied to every abstract
group automorphism. The identity observation does not refute the restricted
Theorem6. No generic hardness theorem is asserted by this screen.

## Published algorithm correctness/cost gate

Primary: Gupta et al., [official Jnanabha2025 paper, pp23–28](https://docs.vijnanaparishadofindia.org/jnanabha/jnanabha_volume_55SI_v1_2025/55_1_p3-23-28.pdf).

BSGS for random-point logarithms. Section5 lists its square-root term but then
attributes the total to later polynomial/linear-algebra work. The supplied
example begins with P=(3,7) on y²=x³−23x+47 modulo97.
Native Astra independently confirmed the primary text and found no stated
assumption repairing these objections.

Root substitution: 7²=49 modulo97, while3³−23*3+47=5 modulo97. Thus the
stated P is not on the stated curve. No scalar-multiplication example based
on that tuple is valid as printed. This is arithmetic, not a synthetic trial.

Root cost objection: a later inexpensive phase does not erase a paid BSGS
subproblem. Without a bounded relation-generation and recovery contract, the
printed total cannot establish sub-square-root complete work. An O(sqrt(q))
upper-bound component alone is not a universal lower-bound proof; the issue
is the unsupported exclusion of its actual work and the missing executable
reduction. No attempt is made to repair the paper into a new attack. The independent
review also identifies missing definitions connecting integer primes to EC
factor-base points, relations, linear equations and arbitrary-target recovery.
For the literal full-range BSGS operation, its square-root-size table/work must
be charged; no applicable single-target amortization contract is supplied.
