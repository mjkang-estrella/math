## Multi-translate cancellation: a candidate-set lemma

Work in an odd prime-order subgroup generated by G. Suppose the geometric
odd-valuation supports of the supplied rational functions are exactly the
distinct labelled points A_i in this subgroup. Their actual construction and
evaluation are part of the input cost. Fix a_0 in a nonempty A_0 and nonzero
t_i modulo q. If

    D_0 symmetric_difference (D_1-[t_1 k]G) symmetric_difference ... = empty,

then some translated support must contain [a_0]G. Consequently

    k belongs to C = union_(i>=1) {(a-a_0)/t_i : a in A_i},
    |C| <= S = sum_(i>=1) |A_i|.

This is a necessary candidate superset, not the exact cancellation set K.
Checking all remaining support parities can recover K at paid cost. Merely
knowing a secret is in K still requires locating and verifying it. The lemma
does not cover unlabelled support or empty D_0 without further arguments.

The raw rational-function construction needs correction. A prescribed reduced
odd divisor must have even degree; for sum D of its points one needs a divisor
H with D+2H principal. Group-sum zero by itself does not supply the claimed
odd support or a cheap evaluation circuit. In the full odd-order rational
group, rational half-classes exist, but constructing representatives remains
paid. Products of x-x([a]G) have supports at BOTH [a]G and -[a]G, with parity
cancellations for repeated abscissae, not arbitrary one-sided index sets.

Empty odd support makes the product a constant times a rational square when
the FULL rational group has odd order: its half-divisor class is rational
two-torsion and hence trivial. Proper subgroups do not imply that ambient
condition. Values at zeros/poles still need explicit conventions. For a
nonempty tame Kummer ramification divisor of degree r, an appropriate
character-sum argument gives an r sqrt(p) scale bound on the unnormalized
sum; subgroup restrictions require the unramified-character averaging and
nontriviality hypotheses. Removed even-order zeros/poles add exceptions and
cannot be ignored merely because they are absent from odd support. Root read
[Kohel–Shparlinski, sections2–3](https://www.i2m.univ-amu.fr/perso/david.kohel/pub/character.pdf):
its stated section3 theorem concerns additive characters, so a Kummer
application requires this separate character/conductor check.

Finite random samples do NOT decide cancellation exactly. A usable gap,
exception policy, confidence over all decisions and false-positive recovery
are needed. Partial cancellation can change the available bound; an upper
bound is not a proof that every residual signal is undetectable.

The proposed q/sqrt(S) cost is not a general lower bound. It assumes a Boolean
cover-search schedule with q/S-scale coverage and sqrt(S) actual cost per
test. A soft-O evaluation upper bound cannot establish the latter lower bound;
adaptive/composed tests and shared preprocessing need their own accounting.
Moreover O(S) candidate checking does not dominate a putative O(sqrt(S)) test.
The submitted construction supplies neither the required exact test nor a
competitive complete decoder. Retain the candidate-set lemma, withdraw the
universal cost rejection, and do not admit an experiment on this report.

## Exact elliptic model for the aligned trace operator

This repairs a concrete construction gap in the raw report. Let p>3 and
E: y^2=f(x)=x^3+a_E*x+b_E be nonsingular. Let A be a nonzero odd-order rational
point, set a=x(A), v=y(A), b=x(2A), and T=-2A. Then v!=0 and f(b)!=0.
For U=P-A, the proposed product satisfies the rational-function identity

    h(P)=(x(P)-a)(x(P+T)-a)
        =4 v^2 (b-x(U))/(x(U)-a)^2.

Root obtained the identity from divisors and the leading term at O. Astra
independently expanded the addition formulas: with c=3a^2+a_E and
d=-a^3+a_E*a+2b_E, the numerators c*x+d +/- 2v*y multiply to
4v^2(b-x)(x-a)^2. Thus the identity also holds after rational cancellation
when b=a, including order3; the original pole locations must still be handled.

On the double cover w^2=h, set z=w*(x(U)-a)/(2v). It gives

    C_A: y^2=f(b-z^2).

The sextic is squarefree: f has distinct roots and f(b)!=0. Hence its smooth
completion has genus2. The involution (z,y)->(-z,y) has quotient E. The other
involution (z,y)->(-z,-y) gives the explicit genus1 quartic

    E'_b: V^2=u f(b-u),    u=z^2, V=z*y.

Its four roots are distinct and (u,V)=(0,0) is rational, so its smooth
completion is an elliptic curve. A standard elliptic point-count backend can
be applied after a paid model conversion; no runtime or hardware conversion
is asserted here. Computing A=-[2^-1]T is paid scalar arithmetic, not free.

An elementary exact count avoids relying on an unspecified Prym model.
With chi(0)=0 and a'_p=p+1-#E'_b(F_p), define

    S_b = sum_(U affine in E(F_p)) chi(b-x(U)).

There are 1+chi(f(x)) affine points at each x, and sum_x chi(b-x)=0. Therefore

    S_b = sum_u chi(u f(b-u)) = -a'_p-chi(-1).

The final equality uses the quartic's leading coefficient -1: its smooth
completion has 1+chi(-1) rational points at infinity. The original h-sum,
omitting its poles U=+/-A and assigning h=0 at U=O, is exactly

    -a'_p-chi(-1)-2chi(b-a).

This is a FULL rational-group sum, not automatically a proper-subgroup sum.
Astra independently verified the model, exceptional terms and order3 case.
Thus the public exact-trace operator is constructively specified at fixed
degree; it does not need an inverse-square sampling estimator.

It still lacks a scalar decoder. Range size alone establishes neither
injectivity nor independence of two or three affine queries. Enumerating
curves of a given trace and solving a rational j-invariant equation, even if
efficient, returns point coordinates rather than the unknown scalar. That
last recovery step needs a new algorithm or paid labelled preprocessing.
Class numbers, special fibers, rational models/twists and enumeration costs
also require actual analysis. The raw universal square-root decoder lower
bound and the alleged SEA cost of 2^20 group operations are unsupported and
withdrawn. No timing measurement was supplied. Replacing a point feature by
an exact trace can be useful only with a justified extraction advantage.

## Primary-source and predictor qualifications

Root recovered [Hastad–Naslund's full paper, section4](https://johanhastad.se/hnrsaacm.pdf),
beyond the proposer's abstract-only read. Theorem4.2 has a bit-position
restriction; Definition4.3/Theorem4.4 treat biased bits using conditional
prediction advantage, not merely accuracy above one half. With the specified
non-negligible advantage, the reduction outputs a polynomial-size candidate
list containing the secret with probability at least1/2, or factors the
modulus. At prime modulus the factor alternative is unavailable; verify list
members against Q. This is an existing conditional reduction, not a predictor
construction. No quantitative extraction polynomial was established here.

For any trained predictor, costs include training, point/feature generation,
validation, inference, the reduction and final verification. Frozen independent
holdout data supports a finite distribution-specific estimate with stated
confidence; disjoint scalars alone do not prove generalization, forbid embedded
advice, or establish scaling. Accuracy must be measured against the appropriate
biased-bit baseline. Affine-orbit queries for a fixed nonzero secret must meet
the reduction's distribution/hypotheses. The raw proposed finite training run
was not admitted or executed, and its event counts are estimates, not evidence.

Root also read [KKK's ANTS paper, sections1–2](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~sgal018/ANTS/papers/Kaluderovic-Kleinjung-Kostic.pdf).
It reports square-root time and fourth-root query scale for the linear
Legendre PRF; limited-query costs are separate from total preprocessing.
The paper explicitly assumes short-sequence uniqueness for its chosen length.
This is a comparator for its stated oracle, not an ECDLP decoder, a current
exhaustive literature result, or a lower bound on other public signals.

The raw table's additional exclusions are not accepted. Ordinary curves do
not universally have only negation as usable symmetry. Degree gcd(e,p-1)
counts the kernel of a power map on F_p^*, not its additive-character degree:
over F_5, x->x^3 has gcd(3,4)=1 but is not an affine function. A homomorphic
isogeny does not by itself make every feature correlation degree4. Prime-to-p
torsion lifts/formal logarithms and Frobenius observations do not rule out
every other coordinate or endomorphism operator. For a B-element scalar set,
|+/-A+/-A|<=2B^2+1 unconditionally (the extra1 permits zero); after zero is
dispatched, there are at most2B^2 nonzero outputs. Near-attainment, unlike this
counting upper bound, needs additive-geometry hypotheses. Actual evaluator
cost is separate. No broad negative
theorem follows from these incomplete candidate sketches.
