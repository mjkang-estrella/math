## Known-point columns really change the cancellation argument

For the prior polynomial-common-composite setup let common pole degree satisfy
0<D<q, with labelled pole sum a_h G+b_h Q, Q=[d]G. Set

    lambda=(a_h+b_h*d)/D  in F_q.

A complete fiber row of degree d_r satisfies sum_P m_rP*log_G(P)=d_r*lambda.
Remove a set of points K with KNOWN NUMERICAL logarithms. A linear combination
c of rows cancelling the other columns has residual known multiplicities w_K
and t=sum_r c_r*d_r=sum_K w_K. It follows that

    t*lambda=sum_K w_K*log_G(K).

This can determine d when t*b_h!=0. The point O contributes degree1 and scalar0.
Thus the previous zero-Q-coefficient argument applies only when the full degree
functional cancels. It cannot reject all methods using known-point anchors.
Useful dependencies still need an executable paid generator; this identity
supplies no cheap way to find one.

## Exact degree-two subcase

Let k=F_p, p>3, and #E(k)=q an odd prime. Take distinct rational centers T1,T2,
fi(P)=x(P-Ti), and rational outer maps Hi. Seek nonconstant h=Hi(fi). The map fi
is a separable degree-two quotient by Ri(P)=2Ti-P, so k(fi)=k(E)^<Ri>.
A common h is invariant under both reflections, hence their composition,
translation by delta=2(T1-T2). This nonzero point has order q. Translation acts
freely on all geometric points and preserves pole multiplicities of h.
Consequently each pole orbit contributes a multiple of q to its degree. Since
h is nonconstant, its pole degree is positive. Composition with fi also gives
deg h=2deg Hi. Therefore 2q divides deg h, even for inseparable outer maps.

More decisively, delta generates E(k), so this translation is transitive on
ALL rational inputs. Every common h is constant on E(k) as a P1-valued map,
including poles. Raw affine 0/0 representations must be interpreted as rational
maps, not as extra distinguishable outputs. Any selector using only h(P)'s
value accepts all rational points or none for fixed public parameters. If the
construction itself depends on Q, its constant may also depend on Q; we do not
infer that every such public value contains no scalar information. This
conclusion does not dismiss additional operations or different fiber partitions.

## Compact common composites exist: degree is not evaluation cost

Let pi be p-power Frobenius and alpha=pi-[1]. Its differential is -1, so alpha
is a separable nonzero isogeny. Its geometric kernel is exactly E(k), of size q;
thus deg alpha=q. This standard separable-degree/kernel fact is supported by
[Sutherland, Theorem5.8](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2023/LectureNotes5.pdf),
which root opened and checked. The following application is our derivation.

Set h0=x composed with alpha. It is separable of degree2q and has pole divisor
2*sum_(P in E(k))(P). It is invariant under rational translations and negation,
hence under all rational reflections. Because alpha(T)=O for rational T,

    h0(P)=h0(P-T)=H(x(P-T))

for the SAME H in k(t), deg H=q. In particular h0(P)=infinity for all rational P.
The example is compactly evaluable via Frobenius and the group law despite its
large degree. Its existence forbids using degree2q alone as a work lower bound.

There is an exact classification in this subcase. The group generated by R1,R2
consists of all q rational translations and their negations and has order2q.
Finite-group fixed-field theory and deg h0=2q give

    k(f1) intersection k(f2) = k(h0).

Every nonconstant common composite is K(h0), K in k(t), with degree
2q*deg K; K may be inseparable. Its rational-input value is K(infinity).
These arguments also hold if q=p; they do not assert hardness for anomalous
curves or replace their applicable stronger baseline.

## Candidate operations and decision gate

Single-center complete fibers are reflection pairs {P,2T-P}, including their
ramified multiplicities. Distinct fibers have disjoint support. With unknown
point columns cancelled, a useful combination must retain entirely known
fibers (or a previously discovered useful labelled equality). The supplied
operation is therefore a paid query for two known-log points summing to 2T;
no new inversion primitive is obtained merely by naming the fiber.

Distinct centers allow richer reflection graphs and compact common composites,
but the common-value selector above is vacuous. A proposed improvement must
provide an operation extracting useful known-anchor dependencies from those
partitions, charge point generation, equality lookup, preprocessing, failed
paths and extraction, and explain why its complete work beats the baseline.
A broad lower bound for coordinate-conditioned graph traversal is NOT proved.
The proposer is separately screening the literal predeclared affine-word
operation; its final scoped cost report is pending at this checkpoint.

General higher-degree functions, nonreflection quotients, rational centers not
in the full prime-order setting, and other divisor arrangements remain outside
this screen. Known-point cancellation loophole remains real in that larger
space. No expensive function-field engine or numerical pilot is justified by
this operation alone.
