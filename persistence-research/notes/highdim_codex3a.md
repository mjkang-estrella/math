## Method and validation requirements

All certificates retain the existing nd format: prime kills and exact-size leaves. Unordered domains cover every distinct ordering at three or more far digits; smaller slices use all distinct ordered coefficient permutations. No certificate is accepted until checkhd.py and verification/v14_checknd_hd.py both pass when invoked directly as python3 -I -B, with no builtin monkeypatching. The -B option prevents bytecode writes outside this work area. Finite lengths below slice floors rely on the existing E/E2 enumeration through 600 digits.

## Search implementation and early findings

**COMPUTED.** `continue.py` uses copies of the incoming generator, driver, and
number-theory helpers. Search-only acceleration caches subgroup tests and
expansions under permutations of equal coefficients. It restores every labelled
child explicitly; the certificate is still an ordinary nd tree. Stabilizers of
equal coefficients also reduce duplicate evaluations within a parent. A 24-case
comparison of complete explicit lift survivor sets against the original generator
passed (`depth_diagnosis.log`). No checker builtins are changed. Cyclic garbage
collection is disabled only in one-configuration search workers; each worker exits
before taking another configuration. Checker processes use their original logic.

The first target-32 `10|9,9,9,9` complete tree has 751196 nodes and used
160606234 search lifts; it took 72.158 seconds with the standard early-7 schedule
and search symmetry caching. This is a candidate closure until both direct
checker results are present. The other target-32 configuration survives an
increased modulus cap of 10^24, with a unbounded and w=0. The original depth
residue has signatures a>=21 and w=0. Refinements by 2,5,7,11,13 each leave one
survivor; refinement by 3 leaves 27. See `depth_diagnosis.json` for the precise
residue, primes, and counts. Adding factor 11 to the breadth schedule did not
close it. These are limitations of the attempted covers, not existence results.

**COMPUTED.** An exhaustive prime sieve through 5000000 searched decimal orders
<=3000 supported on 2,3,5,7,11,13, retaining nontrivial subgroup index and excluding
already supplied primes. It found 21 new supply entries (`supply_scan.json`).
Examples: p=12289, ord(10)=384, index=6; p=10753, ord(10)=512, index=2;
p=38237, ord(10)=121, index=11. These are added as search hints only; any prime
actually used is recomputed independently by both certificate checkers. Most
require additional powers/factors beyond the baseline schedule.

All attempted schedules, budgets, outcomes, and per-configuration checkpoints are
in `history.jsonl`. Interrupted exploratory campaigns retain completed records;
unfinished residue trees are discarded. Final summaries merge checkpoints rather
than treating any interrupted attempt as a completed proof.
