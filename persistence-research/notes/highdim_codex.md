## Equation and domain

**PROVED.** If a decimal integer of length L has low-position excess
S = sum_{p<3}(d_p-1)10^p and remaining non-1 digits d_i at distinct positions
q_i >= 3, then

    X = 9n = 10^L + sum_i c_i 10^{q_i} + K,
    c_i = 9(d_i-1), K = 9S-1.

For the targets here, 5 does not divide the digit product, so a 7-smooth n
has no factor 5. On a slice with K > 0, t0 = v2(K), and all free coordinates
including L at least qmin > t0, v2(X) = t0. The equation to certify is
X = 2^{t0} 3^a 7^w. The exponent a is the exponent in X, hence includes the
factor 9; allowing smaller a in the sieve only enlarges the search domain.

**PROVED.** `run_target.slices` covers every placement: recursively fix a free
digit in any position between its current floor and v2(K), or raise its floor
above v2(K). Fixing adds c_i 10^{q_i} to K. Repeat with the new valuation.
The output certificate domains may be larger than the actual pieces: floors
are relaxed and fixed-position collisions may be admitted. This is safe for
exclusion, but any recorded solutions must be checked against the actual digit
configuration. It is a cover, not a claimed disjoint partition. If L is below
a final certificate floor, it belongs to a finite region that must be checked
separately. Existing E/E2 lattice enumerations cover L <= 600.

## Two-phase method and certificate soundness

**PROVED.** A node specifies one residue vector r modulo a common modulus M.
Let j = v3(M). Since 10^{3^j} = 1 modulo 3^{j+2}, the residue of X modulo
3^{j+2} is constant on the node. Its valuation either determines a exactly,
or implies a >= j+2. When 6 divides M, writing i = v7(M), the period
6*7^i modulo 7^{i+1} similarly determines w exactly or gives w >= i+1.
Before that alignment w >= 0 is the safe range.

**PROVED.** For an auxiliary prime p > 7 with ord_p(10) dividing M, compute
T = X/2^{t0} modulo p and divide by the lower-bound factors 3^{a0}7^{w0}.
If T = 0 there is no solution. Otherwise the normalized residue must be in
the subgroup generated by the unbounded bases: {1}, <3>, <7>, or <3,7>.
In the cyclic group F_p^*, their orders are respectively 1, ord_p(3),
ord_p(7), and lcm(ord_p(3),ord_p(7)). Failure of the corresponding power
test is a valid `prime` kill. A checker recomputes primality and orders;
the prime-supply cache is a search aid, not a trusted premise.

**COMPUTED method.** Phase A refines breadth first by a schedule of small
factors, for example (2,3,7,3,2,2,2,3,5). Its product is
2^4*3^3*5*7. These moduli align useful coset tests before excessive
3-adic refinement. In particular, when both a and w are unbounded, removing
their lower powers does not change membership in <3,7>; the useful extra
information comes from aligned decimal periods. Phase B deepens individual
survivors, usually by 3 when only a is unbounded, or 7 when only w is.
This schedule is a heuristic for finding finite covers, not a termination
theorem. **CONJECTURAL:** larger budgets or better schedules may close more
of the currently budget-limited configurations.

**PROVED.** Each internal node partitions its full residue class into all
lifts modulo M*f. Every lift must be an explicit child or fail a listed
prime test. The root is the entire space modulo 1. A finite tree therefore
excludes all integer points except its exact-size (`sz`) leaves. At an
`sz` leaf both a and w are exact, so X has one fixed integer value; the
checker enumerates every domain point attaining it and compares the exact
solution list. `sz` is an exact resolution, not an additional exclusion
rule. All exclusion kills in these certificates are plain `prime` kills;
there are no pin or pin2 kills. Node/time exhaustion establishes nothing
about existence and is reported as BUDGET.

## Every ordering is covered

**PROVED.** In `unordered` mode each c_i remains attached to its labelled
coordinate q_i, while q_i are pairwise distinct and qmin <= q_i < L.
There is no inequality comparing different q_i. Thus the domain is the
union of every positional ordering of the far digits. Different equal-digit
labellings may duplicate a placement, which does not lose coverage.
Prime kills act on full residue classes without an ordering assumption, so
they remain valid on this union. Exact-size leaves must enumerate the same
unordered domain; both checkers implement that explicitly.

**PROVED.** For slices with <= 2 far digits the existing driver instead uses
ordered certificates (q1 < q2 < ... < L) for every DISTINCT permutation of
the coefficient multiset. Their union covers every placement. One ordered
certificate with unequal coefficients never covers the reversed ordering.
This is the defect found and repaired in REPORT_E and REPORT_E2, and is why
configuration counts alone are not a coverage proof.
