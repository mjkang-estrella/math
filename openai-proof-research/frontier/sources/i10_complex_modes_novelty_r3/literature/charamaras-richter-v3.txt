    The starting point of our discussion is an old conjecture of Chowla concerning the Liouville
function λ(n) := (−1)Ω(n) . It asserts that asymptotically there is no correlation among the
sign patterns of λ(n) along consecutive integers.

Conjecture 1.1 (Chowla [Cho87]; see also [Ram18]). For any k ∈ N,

                         En∈[N ] λ(n)λ(n + 1) · · · λ(n + k − 1) = oN →∞ (1).                          (1.1)

    If the Cesàro average in (1.1) is replaced by a logarithmic average, then the case k = 2 of
(1.1) was proved in [Tao16].

Theorem 1.2 (cf. [Tao16, Theorem 2]). We have
                                     log
                                  En∈[N ] λ(n)λ(n + 1) = oN →∞ (1).
    In [HR21], Helfgott and Radziwill established double-logarithmic savings in the above,
while the best known result to date is due to Pilatte [Pil23] who proved Theorem 1.2 with
logarithmic savings.
    The method used in [Tao16] was later extended in [TT18] to establish the logarithmi-
cally averaged Chowla’s conjecture for any odd k. Albeit significant recent progress com-
bining ideas from number theory, higher order Fourier analysis, and ergodic theory (cf.
[Sar09, EAKPLdlR17, Fra17, Tao17, FH18, FKPL18, GLdlR21, KMT23, MRT+ 23, MSTT23,
KLRT24, MRS+ 26]), Chowla’s conjecture, and the more general Elliott’s conjecture1 (cf. [Ell92,
MRT15]), remain open for other k, even in the case of logarithmic averages.
    A different relation for the sequence Ω(n), introduced in [Ric21], draws inspiration from
an ergodic-theoretic perspective.

Theorem 1.3 (cf. [Ric21]). For any bounded arithmetic function a : N → C, we have

                         En∈[N ] a(Ω(n)) = En∈[N ] a(Ω(n) + 1) + oN →∞ (1).                            (1.2)

   We remark that if a(n) = (−1)n then (1.2) reduces to the k = 1 case of Chowla’s
conjecture, which is a well-known equivalent form of the Prime Number Theorem. For
generalizations of (1.2) and its connections to ergodic theory see [BR22, Loy23, Cha25].

                               1.2. Proof strategy for Theorem A
In broad strokes, we follow the approach introduced by Tao in [Tao16] to prove Theorem 1.2,
which builds on results of Matomäki-Radziwill-Tao in [MRT15]. The biggest difference in our
approach is that the results in [MRT15] cannot be used directly, as they apply exclusively to
strongly aperiodic 3 multiplicative functions, and the functions we are dealing with here are
not even multiplicative.
    The starting point in the proof of Theorem A is reducing our problem to the case of
multiplicative functions. After replacing the Cesàro averages in the left hand side of (1.3)
with logarithmic ones, then using Fourier analysis, the Erdős-Kac theorem and a theorem of
Helfgott and Radziwill in [HR21], we can reduce bounding
