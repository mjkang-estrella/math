1.1. Computational model. We use the arithmetic model of [21], in which additions, subtractions,
multiplications, divisions, and exact comparisons of real numbers have unit cost. An algorithm is
strongly polynomial if its operation count is polynomial in the number of input entries, independently
of their magnitudes and encoding lengths, and if, on rational inputs, all numbers computed have bit
lengths polynomial in the total input length. We apply this definition with ε fixed.
   The bit length of a rational number r, denoted as ℓ(r), is the sum of the binary lengths of
its numerator and denominator in lowest terms. Elementary functions are evaluated by rational
approximation, with the cost included in the operation count.

1.2. Related work. Gamarnik and Katz [13] obtained a (1 + ε)n approximation for 0–1 matrices in
polynomial time under the assumption that the support graph has bounded maximum degree and
vertex expansion bounded away from zero. For arbitrary 0–1 matrices, their algorithm achieves the
same approximation factor in time exp(O(ε−1/2 n2/3 log3 n)). Their algorithm uses the approximation
scheme of Bayati, Gamarnik, Katz, Nair, and Tetali [8] for the matching partition function M λ|M | ,
                                                                                                P
where the sum is over all matchings. For fixed activity λ and bounded maximum degree, these
authors gave a deterministic fully polynomial-time approximation scheme based on correlation decay.
Gamarnik and Katz use vertex expansion to bound the contribution of nonperfect matchings when
λ is sufficiently large.
   For matrices satisfying a ≤ Aij ≤ 1 for all i, j, where a > 0 is fixed, matrix scaling gives
a polynomial-factor approximation in polynomial time [21, 7]. Barvinok obtained more accurate
approximations in quasipolynomial time, first for real or complex matrices whose entries are sufficiently
close to 1 [5], and subsequently for all real matrices satisfying a ≤ Aij ≤ 1 [6]. In the latter setting,
his algorithm approximates log per A to additive error ζ ∈ (0, 1) in time nOa (log n+log(1/ζ)) . Recently,
Yi [30] gave an FPTAS for matrices with entries in {0} ∪ [a, 1], where a > 0 is fixed, provided every
row and column has at least (1/2 + γ)n nonzero entries for a fixed γ > 0.
   Barvinok’s algorithms use his interpolation method, which approximates the logarithm of a
polynomial from its firstP few    coefficients, using bounds on the locations of its zeros. For the
polynomial ZG (z) =         z |M | , where the sum is over all matchings of G, the Heilmann–Lieb
                          M
theorem [17] places all zeros on the negative real axis. Barvinok [4] used this to approximate the
total number of matchings from the first few coefficients of ZG . Patel and Regts [25] showed how to
compute the required coefficients efficiently on bounded-degree graphs, obtaining polynomial-time
approximation algorithms from suitable bounds on the zeros. We refer to [3] for a general account of
this method.
   The Bethe permanent is computable by convex optimization [28] and is a lower bound for the
permanent [15]. The authors [12] proved that its approximation factor improves from 2n/2 to 22n/g
if the support graph has girth at least g, for even g ≥ 4. This comparison is sharp and holds for
arbitrary nonnegative edge weights.

Independent work. After finishing this paper and right before submitting it, we learned of
independent work of Kudria, Luo, and Majid [20] which provides an exp(o(n))-factor approximation
of the permanent in deterministic polynomial time. We believe that by replacing the exact Taylor
polynomial in Section 3 by a suitable approximation, our algorithm can also be modified to achieve
this guarantee.

have even length. But u and v lie in different parts of the bipartite graph, so the path must have
odd length, which is a contradiction. Hence v does not lie on this path. Swap the two colors along
this path. After the swap, the blue matching avoids both u and v, while the red edges still form a
matching of G.

only if a swap occurred. In this case, the component containing u is the same alternating path, and
swapping its colors again recovers the original pair. If there is no red edge at u, the map was the
identity. The map preserves the multiset of edges, and hence preserves the product weight wR wB .
