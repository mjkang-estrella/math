L8:     ON THE REGULARITY OF SOLUTIONS OF THE
L9:     INHOMOGENEOUS INFINITY LAPLACE EQUATION
L10:     ERIK LINDGREN
L11:     Abstract. We study the inhomogeneous infinity Laplace equation and prove
L12:     that for bounded and continuous inhomogeneities, any blow-up is linear but
L13:     not necessarily unique. If in addition, the inhomogeneity is assumed to be
L14:     C 1 , then we prove that any solution is differentiable, i.e., that any blow-up is
L15:     unique.
L16:     1. Introduction
L17:     1.1. The problem. We study the inhomogeneous infinity Laplace equation
L18:     ∆∞ u = f in B1 ,
L19:     (1.1)
L20:     u = g on ∂B1 ,
L21:     for f ∈ L∞ (B1 ) ∩ C(B1 ) and g ∈ C(∂B1 ). Here
L22:     ∆∞ u = uxi uxj uxi xj ,
L23:     as it was introduced by Aronsson in [Aro67]. This equation and its homogeneous
L24:     sibling arise in many different contexts. The operator ∆∞ has a natural connection
L25:     to the problem of finding optimal Lipschitz extensions. One way to see this is to
L26:     regard the equation as a limit of the p-Laplace equation, as p → ∞. There is also a
L27:     “tug-of-war” game approach to the infinity Laplace equation mentioned in among
L28:     others [PSSW09]. The operator is also suggested to be used in image processing,
L29:     see [CMS98].
L30:     The infinity Laplace operator has recently attracted much attention. In [ACJ04],
L31:     various properties are discussed. In particular, it is settled that solutions to the
L32:     homogeneous infinity Laplace equation enjoys the so-called comparison with cones
L33:     property. It also turns out that this is equivalent to being a solution of the infinity
L34:     Laplace equation, see [CEG01]. Until quite recently, the best regularity known
L35:     for infinity harmonic functions was that they can be approximated arbitrarily well
L36:     by linear functions at small scales, see for instance [CE00]. This is not in general
L37:     enough to be differentiable. But then in [Sav05] and [ES08], the C 1 and C 1,α
L38:     regularity of solutions were proved in dimension two. Later, in [ES11], it was
L39:     proved in higher dimensions that the solution is differentiable, but without any
L40:     sort of continuity estimate. For the inhomogeneous equation, some uniqueness and
L41:     stability results are obtained in [LW08]. The main contribution of this paper is
L42:     to provide results similar to those in [CE00] and [ES11] for the inhomogeneous
L43:     equation.
