                                                   ON THE WAIST AND WIDTH INEQUALITY IN COMPLETE
                                                     3-MANIFOLDS WITH POSITIVE SCALAR CURVATURE




arXiv:2308.04044v1 [math.DG] 8 Aug 2023
                                                                    YEVGENY LIOKUMOVICH AND ZHICHAO WANG


                                                 Abstract. We show that a complete non-compact 3-manifold with scalar curvature bounded
                                                 below by a positive constant admits a singular foliation by surfaces of controlled area and
                                                 diameter.




                                                                                   1. Introduction
                                             In [7, §3.10, Width/Waist Conjecture] Gromov conjectured that a complete manifold with
                                          scalar curvature R ≥ 6 admits a singular foliation by surfaces of area and diameter bounded by
                                          a universal constant. This conjecture was proved for compact 3-manifolds in [10]. In this paper
                                          the result is generalized to complete non-compact 3-manifolds.
                                          Theorem 1.1. Let M be a complete non-compact 3-manifold with scalar curvature R ≥ 6.
                                          Then there exists a proper Morse function f : M → R, such that for every t ∈ R each connected
                                          component Γ of f −1 (t) satisfies
                                                                                    diamM (Γ) < 18π;
                                                                                     Area(Γ) ≤ 33π.
                                            For compact manifolds it was proved in [10] that one can construct a foliation by surfaces of
                                          controlled area and diameter and genus at most 2 (the maximal Heegaard genus of a compact
                                          3-manifold with positive scalar curvature). We conjecture that it should also be possible to
                                          prove a similar genus bound in Theorem 1.1.
                                             Recall that the Urysohn 1-width of a Riemannian three-manifold M is the infimum of real
                                          numbers d ≥ 0 with the property that there exist a Morse function f : M → R such that
                                          for all t ∈ R, each connected component of f −1 (t) has diameter bounded by d from above.
                                          For three-manifolds with R ≥ 6 and zero first Betti number, Gromov-Lawson [4] proved an
                                          upper bound for Urysohn 1-width by considering level sets of the distance function to a fixed
                                          point; in [7, §3.10, Property A], Gromov proved the Urysohn 1-width upper bound without
                                          the topological conditions; in [2] Chodosh and Li proved the Urysohn 1-width upper bound for
                                          3-dimensional mu-bubbles in 4-manifolds with positive scalar curvature. For closed manifolds,
                                          the work of Maximo and the first author [10] implies a new proof of the Urysohn 1-width bound.
                                          Inspired by their work, the second author with Zhu proved the Urysohn 1-width upper bound
                                          for mean convex domains (possibly non-compact) with non-negative Ricci curvature in [15].
                                          Urysohn width bounds were used in [3] to obtain topological classification results for four- and
                                          five-manifolds with positive scalar curvature. As a direct corollary, Theorem 1.1 gives a new
                                          proof of the Urysohn 1-width upper bound of three-manifolds with R ≥ 6.

Idea of the proof. Our proof relies on methods from [10], construction of µ-bubbles and ideas
of Gromov and Song on construction of minimal hypersurfaces in non-compact manifolds ([5],
[14], see also [1]).
   We first cut the complete three-manifold (M, g) along minimal surfaces with index less than
or equal to 1. As a result, if these surfaces are countable (e.g. the metric g is bumpy), then such
a cutting process decomposes M into countably many regions. Then by adapting an argument
of Gromov [5] and Song [14], the interior of these regions does not contain any closed minimal
surfaces. These regions are said to be geometrically prime, a generalization of a similar notion
in the compact case [10].
   The key step is to decompose the geometrically prime regions into countably many small
pieces with diameter and boundary area upper bounds; see Definition 3.1 for the concept of
admissible regions.
   For prime regions with an unstable boundary component, we will use µ-bubble techniques and
constrained minimization to construct finitely many surfaces with controlled size to decompose
it as a union of an admissible region and finitely many regions with exactly one mean convex
boundary component; see Lemma 3.3. By repeating this argument, we obtain the desired
decomposition for geometrically prime regions with unstable boundary components.
   For those geometrically prime regions whose boundaries are stable minimal surfaces (or the
boundary is empty) we use arguments in [5, 14] to obtain a two-sided surface with controlled
size and non-vanishing mean curvature. Then for the mean convex region, the argument in
the last paragraph gives the desired decomposition. For the mean concave region, we cut off
an admissible region so that its complement consists of finitely many connected regions with
the property that their boundary contains exactly one non-minimal component (the others are
stable minimal surfaces). Moreover, each non-minimal boundary component has controlled size
and non-vanishing mean curvature. Observe that we have dealt with these cases. Thus, by
repeating the process, we obtain the desired decomposition.
   It remains to construct a Morse foliation for each admissible regions. To do this, we regard
the region as a subdomain in geometrically prime regions and then consider mean curvature
flow starting on the mean convex surfaces. Note that the diameter upper bound may not be
preserved in the flow. To obtain the diameter bound we intersect level sets of the flow with
admissible regions and make necessary surgeries so that they have regularity of level sets of a
Morse function, similarly to how it was done in [10] and [1]. Combining all of the foliations
together finishes the proof.

Outline. This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present some known results. We
first introduce the bumpy metric theorem in Section 2.1; the area and diameter bounds for
minimal surfaces with index less than or equal to one and µ-bubbles are provided in Section 2.2.
Finally, in Section 2.3, we introduce the concept of geometrically prime regions and a dichotomy
proposition by adapting arguments from [5, 14]. Section 3 is devoted to three decomposition
lemmas. They are used to decompose geometrically prime regions into compact domains of
controlled diameter and boundary area. In Section 4, we construct foliation in each compact
region and prove our main theorem.

2.1. Bumpy metrics. Let M be a complete (possibly non-compact) Riemannian manifold. We
say that metric g on M is bumpy if there are no closed minimal submanifolds with non-zero
Jacobi fields. By [20, Theorem 2.1] (see also [17, Theorem 2.1]) for any metric g on M there
exists a sequence gi of smooth bumpy metrics on M with |g − gi |C 3 → 0. Hence, without any
loss of generality we can assume that our metric is bumpy. We will also use the bumpy metric
theorem with partially fixed metrics.
Theorem 2.1 ([20, Theorem 3.1]). Let (N, γ0 ) be a complete Riemannian manifold. Let U ⊂ N
be an open set. Consider the class Γ of smooth metrics γ ′ on N such that γ ′ and γ0 agree on
N \ U . Then a generic metric γ ∈ Γ has the following property: if Σ is a closed, minimal
immersed submanifold of N and if each connected component of Σ intersects U , then Σ has no
nontrivial Jacobi fields.

2.3. Geometrically prime regions. We will use the concept of geometrically prime regions,
which was defined in [10, Definition 2.5]. Note that the region here may be non-compact.
Definition 2.6. A region N is geometrically prime if
   (1) N \ ∂N does not contain any closed embedded minimal surfaces;
   (2) each connected component of ∂N is a compact minimal surface with index less than or
       equal to 1.
Remark 2.7. Let N be a geometrically prime region with R ≥ 6. We conclude that each
embedded closed two-sided surface Σ ⊂ N \ ∂N separates N . Suppose not, then there exists
an area minimizer Σ in the homology class. Then Σ is compact by Lemma 2.3. This gives a
contradiction.
