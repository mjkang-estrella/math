                                                                BOUNDS

                                                  Abstract. We investigate the geometric implications of spectral curvature
                                                  bounds, extending classical rigidity results in scalar curvature geometry to the




arXiv:2604.04052v1 [math.DG] 5 Apr 2026
                                                  spectral setting. By systematically employing the warped µ-bubble method,
                                                  we show classification theorems for stable weighted minimal hypersurfaces in
                                                  3-manifolds with nonnegative spectral scalar curvature, and we establish band
                                                  width estimates for both spectral Ricci and spectral scalar curvatures. Further-
                                                  more, we prove some splitting theorems under spectral curvature conditions,
                                                  including a spectral version of the Geroch conjecture for manifolds with arbi-
                                                  trary ends and a result related to the Milnor conjecture.




                                                                              1. Introduction
                                             A celebrated result in scalar curvature geometry is the resolution of the Geroch
                                          conjecture due to Schoen-Yau [SY79a]. The conjecture states that an n-dimensional
                                          torus does not admit a metric of non-negative scalar curvature. Schoen-Yau used
                                          a minimal hypersurface approach and it has become a major tool, and there are
                                          other approaches using spinors [GL83] and the technique of harmonic functions of
                                          Stern [Ste22].
                                             A weaker version of curvature, called the spectral curvature, which is defined as
                                          the first eigenvalue of an elliptic operator involving the Laplace-Beltrami operator
                                          and the curvature, recently has found its place in several important problems. For
                                          example, the spectral curvature is useful in the stable Bernstein conjecture [CL23],
                                          [CLMS24], [Maz24] and the aspherical conjecture [CL24]. The earliest occurrence
                                          of such a notion seems to be in [SY83].
                                             We will use the following definition of the spectral curvatures using a positive
                                          function.

   We now state our first result regarding the weighted minimal hypersurface, which
is a classification result of stable weighted surfaces in a 3-manifold of non-negative
spectral curvature. The result is an analog of [FCS80, Theorem 3], [CG00] which
classifies stable minimal surfaces in a 3-manifold of non-negative scalar curvature.
Theorem 1.4. Let 0 ⩽ γ < 4, (M 3 , g) be a 3-dimensional complete manifold with
spectral nonnegative scalar curvature and Σ be a stable, complete, oriented weighted
minimal surface with weight uγ in (M, g). Then there are two possibilities:
(a) Σ is compact, then Σ is a sphere or a torus, in the case of a torus, Σ is flat, if
    further Σ is weighted area-minimizing, then (M, g) locally splits;
(b) Σ is non-compact and 0 ⩽ γ < 3, then Σ is conformally equivalent to the
    complex plane C or a cylinder A; Σ is flat if it is conformally equivalent to a
    cylinder.
Remark 1.5. The case with Σ being non-compact is more subtle than [FCS80,
Theorem 3] in Theorem 1.4 and subsequent Theorem 1.6. It is due to a question
posed in [FCS80, Remark 1] regarding the inverse spectral properties of the elliptic
operator −∆Σ + aK where a ∈ R and K is the Gaussian curvature of the surface Σ.
Here, we have made use of [BC14]. Because of this, we need the condition 0 ⩽ γ < 3;

and an extra assumption on the volume growth of Σ is required when 3 ⩽ γ < 4,
the case which we do not discuss in Theorem 1.4.
   The strategy of proving the compact case of Theorem 1.4 is as follows: we use the
stability and the spectral curvature condition (with the Gauss-Bonnet theorem) to
show that Σ is infinitesimally rigid (cf. [FCS80]); second, construct a foliation {Σt }
with Σ be a leaf using Theorem 2.4; third, determine the sign of the weighted mean
curvature of each leaf using the curvature condition again; fourth, show that every
nearby Σt is also minimizing using the first variation (cf. [BBN10]). We can show a
global splitting if we assume an additional topological condition, see Theorem 1.23
(cf. [CG00]).
   This strategy for proving the rigidity will be used in Subsection 1.2 and the band
width estimates in Subsection 1.3 with suitable adaptations. To avoid repetition,
we unify and streamline these proofs by introducing the variables Z and W in
Subsection 2.3. The key differences left are estimating the mean curvature of a leaf
of the foliation and calculating the rigid metrics.

1.2. Constant weighted mean curvature. The concept of a (Riemannian) band
is introduced by Gromov in his studies of metric inequalities [Gro18]. It is a Rie-
mannian manifold with at least two boundaries. We can group the boundaries into
two groups ∂− M and ∂+ M allowing the band width to be defined as the distance
from ∂− M to ∂+ M .
    We fix the notation of band, let Σ be an oriented hypersurface homologous to
∂− M , we choose the direction of the unit normal of Σ such that it points outward
from the region bounded by Σ and ∂− M and we denote it by νΣ . In particular, we
choose the direction of the unit normal ν− of ∂− M pointing to the inside of M and
the unit normal ν+ of ∂+ M outside of M .
    By using hypersurfaces of constant weighted mean curvature, we can show some
rigidity theorems for n-dimensional torical bands which are simply T n−1 × [−1, 1]
with a smooth metric g. Here, T n denotes the n-dimensional torus.

    Now let’s turn to the Ricci curvature. The Bonnet-Myers theorem is a funda-
mental result regarding the control of diameter by a Ricci curvature bound. More
specifically, it states that if a complete manifold with Ricg ⩾ n − 1, then its diame-
ter must be less than π. This can be interpreted as a band type estimate: any band
with Ricg ⩾ n − 1 must have its width less than π. Indeed, for a closed manifold, we
can remove a pair of points which realizes the diameter, and the manifold becomes
a band in some sense. This interpretation was achieved by [HKKZ25] via spacetime
harmonic function techniques.
    Now, we apply the same interpretation for the Bonnet-Myers theorem of spectral
Ricci curvature which was shown by Antonelli-Xu [AX24]. Their theorem states that
if a complete manifold has positive spectral Ricci curvature with 0 ⩽ γ < 4/(n − 1),
then the diameter of the manifold is bounded. This result has been further extended
to the spectral Bakry-Emery curvatures [CH26, Yeu25, Wu26]. With the alternative
interpretation, we can show the band with estimates with different curvature bounds
and a rigidity statement.

   The article is organized as follows:
   In Section 2, we introduce basics of warped µ-bubbles including the first, second
variation formulas.
   In Section 3, we make use of the weighted minimal hypersurfaces, in particular,
we prove Theorems 1.4 and 1.6.
   In Section 4, we develop the rigidity results Theorems 1.8, 1.11 and 1.9 by using
hypersurfaces of constant weighted mean curvature.
   In Section 5, by selecting suitable µ we prove several band width estimates which
include a band width interpretation of the Bonnet-Myers theorem (Theorem 1.14).
