## 3. Three precise screens

**F136: sharp length-two separation.** For every smooth projective complex n-fold and ample L, ask whether K_X+(n+2)L separates every length-two subscheme. This is the sharp very-ampleness question, stronger than global generation and different from generating ordinary jets at one very general point. The source's reduced S descends a value in L_x; a double point needs the quotient by m_x² in a chosen direction, and two points need independently assignable values. Merely repeating the one-point nonvanishing proof does not produce the required evaluation rank. The entire isolation/lifting ending was checked, including the rational profile and fixed-part corrections, before making this scope statement. No impossibility theorem is asserted. This screen is deferred, not nominated; the earlier minimizer machinery was not extended to simultaneous fat-centre constraints.

**C136: retain a supplied common complement while choosing one section through finitely many base points.** Here is a paid exact restricted theorem. Work over C. Let f:X→Z be a Fano type contraction of normal quasi-projective integral varieties, dim X=d, and let Z be affine of positive dimension. Suppose an effective rational Λ is given on all X with (X,Λ) klt and N(K_X+Λ) Cartier and linearly trivial, for a fixed positive integer N. For every finite nonempty set F of closed points in Z there are one nonzero regular g on Z vanishing at F and one rational t=t(d,N)>0 such that

\[
 \Theta=\Lambda+t f^*\operatorname{div}(g)\ge\Lambda,
 \quad (X,\Theta)\text{ is }1/(2N)\text{-lc},
 \quad M(K_X+\Theta)\sim0
\]

with integral Cartier M=lcm(N,denominator(t)). The same t and M work for every size and placement of F. This is not a theorem about preserving arbitrary independently supplied local complement germs.

Proof: integrality of N(K+Λ) on a resolution implies every positive log discrepancy lies in (1/N)Z, so (X,Λ) is 1/N-lc. Apply the Cartier operation at each point with this gap. Its valuative implication gives lct(m_z O_X)≥τ(d,1/N). For J=∩_(z∈F)m_z, localization near a fibre equals that single point ideal and away from those fibres J is the unit ideal. Thus the same threshold holds for JO_X. Since Z is affine, choose finitely many global generators of J and principalize their pullbacks together with the pair. A general linear combination is nonzero, has fixed part equal to the resolved ideal and a smooth residual divisor transverse to all resolution strata. Choose once a rational 0<t<min(τ/2,1/4). The pair with coefficient 2t is lc by that general-member argument; its average with (X,Λ) is 1/(2N)-lc. Finally div(g) is principal, so M(K+Θ) is Cartier and linearly trivial for the displayed M. This checks every discrepancy and the whole X, not just each original point separately. No infinite generic intersection is used, even if the underlying field is countable; C is retained here to match the stated screen.

**W136: selected weighted maximality question.** Fix smooth integral projective complex X of dimension n≥3 and ample L, V=L^n. Ask for δ=δ(X,L)>0 such that, for every finite positive weight vector w=(w_i) with max w_i<δ and Σw_i^n≤V, the class

 D(w)=\pi^*L-\sum_iw_iE_i                                      \tag{W}
\]

is nef at very general distinct tuples. There is no threshold uniform over dimension/volume/families, no prescribed special points, and no relative symplectic assertion. The positive-characteristic analogue uses the geometric generic tuple; it does not demand a tuple over the original countable field. The boundary Σw_i^n=V cannot be ample. The ordinary weighted Seshadri constant for fixed positive q is sup{t:D(tq) nef}, with curve denominator Σq_i mult_(p_i)C, and upper bound (V/Σq_i^n)^(1/n). The two routes below do not prove W. They give actual sufficient regions and a common explicit obstruction to those regions. W is a research question retained here, not a claim of worldwide openness or priority.

## 4. Route one — mixed Hermite jets through the complete graded construction

Fix D≥1 with DL very ample and put

\[
 \ell=\min(1/D,D^{n-1}V),\qquad a_0=\ell/100.
\]

**Paid mixed-jet assertion.** If w_i>0, M=max w_i<a₀, and

\[
                        M^{n-1}\sum_iw_i<V,                \tag{J1}
\]

then, for all sufficiently large integers k, H⁰(X,kL) separates the simultaneous ordinary jets of orders m_i=⌊kw_i⌋ at a nonempty Zariski-open set of distinct tuples. A very general complex tuple satisfies all these eventual conditions. At the geometric generic tuple the same assertion holds in positive characteristic, including n=2. The degree threshold depends on (X,L), the weights, r and the strict slack; it is not made effective or uniform in this proof.

Choose M<a<a₀ with a^(n−1)S<V, S=Σw_i, and set b=V/a^(n−1). The complete source flag construction has graded supports of cardinality h⁰(kL) in Δ(1/D,…,1/D,D^(n−1)V). Successive two-intercept reshaping gives supports F_k of the same cardinality in

\[
 P_a=\Delta(a,\ldots,a,b),\qquad \operatorname{vol}P_a=V/n!.
\]

Every step keeps gradedness and backwards finite jet transfer. Indeed the nodal two-branch valuation preserves products and row labels; the shears are unimodular; compression bounds row lengths without assuming every intermediate exponent occurs; the primitive-direction choice tunes a **real** intercept. Because a<ell/100, the source requirement a max(3/A,2/B)<1/16 holds at every reshaping step. These are existing lower operations, not new lemmas claimed by I136.

The new explicit finite interpolation bookkeeping is as follows. Choose distinct nonzero c_i and torus points q_i=(1,…,1,c_i). For t≥0 put

\[
 H_k(t)=\sum_i(m_i+1-t)_+,
 \qquad E_{\mathbf m}=\{(\beta,j):|\beta|\le m_{\max},\ 0\le j<H_k(|\beta|)\}.
\]

Expand each prescribed jet as Σ_α(x′−1)^α h_(i,α)(Y−c_i), where |α|≤m_i and deg h_(i,α)≤m_i−|α|. For each α, the Chinese remainder theorem provides f_α(Y) of degree less than H_k(|α|), satisfying exactly these congruences modulo (Y−c_i)^(m_i+1−|α|) whenever the exponent is positive. Then Σ_α(x′−1)^α f_α(Y) realizes every jet. Its expanded monomials lie in E_m because β≤α coordinatewise and H_k is decreasing. This uses coefficient jets and coprime powers, not factorial denominators, so it works in all characteristics.

The convex function t/a+H_k(t)/b on [0,m_max] has its maximum at an endpoint. At zero it is ≤(kS+r)/b; at m_max it is ≤kM/a+r/b. Thus

\[
 \frac{|\beta|}{ka}+\frac{j}{kb}
 \le\max(M/a,S/b)+r/(kb).                                 \tag{J2}
\]

Choose max(M/a,S/b)<λ<1. For all sufficiently large k, E_m⊂kλP_a. Translate by a lattice vector kv_k with v_k→v in the positive orthant, choosing v+λP_a compactly inside P_a. Source interior filling applies: full density plus gradedness forces every lattice point of that fixed interior compact set into F_k for all sufficiently large k. Multiplication by the translation monomial is a unit on all torus jet algebras, so interpolation survives.

**Mixed transfer is paid, not inferred from an equal-jet title.** In the analytic source use the finite matrix consisting, at point i, of coefficient derivatives of orders ≤m_i. Choose one exposing weight for all basis columns in this fixed degree, normalize those columns, and take their limits. The nonzero maximal minor remains nonzero for a sufficiently small parameter. Different point blocks merely have different numbers of rows. In the formal positive-characteristic proof replace its common truncated ring by

\[
 B_i=k[[s]][\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_n]/(\delta)^{m_i+1}.
\]

The substitution z_a=s^(λ_a)(Q_(i,a)+δ_a) and the same column normalization give a matrix into ⊕B_i whose maximal minor is a unit modulo s. After inverting s each block is the corresponding smooth jet algebra; repeated centres are excluded because two repeated-point quotients cannot independently attain arbitrary constant values. The open rank locus has a k((s))-point, hence is nonempty, and has k-points over an algebraically closed k. Torus coordinate changes and row compressions are the same finite-matrix operations. Thus all composed backwards transfers apply to unequal m_i. The parameters may depend on k and the vector; a single degeneration in all degrees is not claimed. Gradedness of the support families is fixed before those finite transfer choices.

On U_r, ⊕_i pr_i*P^(m_i)(kL) is locally free, and evaluation is a morphism from the fixed space H⁰(X,kL); its maximal minors give an open surjectivity locus. In C a countable intersection over the required integer degrees has nonempty very-general complement. In characteristic p every such nonempty open contains the generic point of integral U_r, and field extension preserves surjectivity. This proves the assertion without selecting a rational tuple over a countable field.

For an integral curve C meeting marked points, choose at one such point a nonzero degree-m_i class in the associated graded of its one-dimensional local domain and zero jets at the others. The section does not contain C and vanishes to order at least m_i at every marked point. The local estimate length(R/(f))≥m_i e(R), proved by f^q and the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial, gives kL·C≥Σm_i mult_iC. Divide by k and let k→∞. Exceptional curves have nonnegative intersection with D(w), so D(w) is nef. A countable dense set of rational vectors in (J1), followed by closure of the nef cone, gives the common-locus real-weight version, including limits with M<a₀ and M^(n−1)S≤V. For a finite subscheme supported at p_i the conclusion only passes automatically if its ideal contains m_(p_i)^(m_i+1); arbitrary length is not interchangeable with ordinary order.

**Exact limitation of this route.** Its interpolation diagram contains the long-axis segment of asymptotic length kS and the transverse segment of length kM. Fitting this diagram in the one elongated simplex necessarily requires a≥M and b≥S. Since a^(n−1)b=V, this forces M^(n−1)S≤V. The limitation is therefore inherent to this particular container argument, not merely a named missing lemma. It does not say that the full linear system fails to interpolate outside that region.

For n=3, take N weights 2b_N and N weights b_N, where

\[
 b_N=\frac{99}{100}\left(\frac{V}{9N}\right)^{1/3}.
                                                               \tag{J3}
\]

Then Σw_i³=(99/100)³V<V and max w_i→0, whereas M²S=(4/3)(99/100)³V>V. Thus arbitrarily diffuse strictly volume-admissible weights evade (J1). Closing W requires a mixed support/interpolation or degeneration construction using the true sum Σw_i^n, rather than this envelope loss. It must retain the graded/density and backwards-rank interfaces; no such construction is asserted here.

## 5. Route two — convex aggregation of equal-subset nef classes

Let q₀=r₀(X,L) be a source equal-weight threshold. For each fixed r≥q₀ choose a tuple simultaneously good for every subset S of size q≥q₀. Each coordinate projection U_r→U_q is dominant; pulling back the countably many proper closed exclusions keeps them proper, and there are finitely many subsets. The same statement at geometric generic tuples follows from dominance and base change. This pays the common-tuple quantifier.

Put c_q=(V/q)^(1/n). On the blowup of all r points, the class

\[
 D_S=\pi^*L-c_{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}E_i
\]

is the pullback of the nef class on the subset blowup and is nef. Therefore any nonnegative weights λ_S with Σλ_S≤1 give a nef class with exceptional vector z=Σλ_S c_(|S|)1_S; use the remaining coefficient on π*L. If 0≤w_i≤z_i, curve tests show D(w) remains nef: intersections increase on strict transforms and stay nonnegative on exceptional curves. This establishes a concrete convex feasibility criterion, not a claim that all volume-admissible vectors lie in that region.

A readily usable sufficient scalar test results by sorting w₁≥⋯≥w_r>0 and putting w_(r+1)=0. Raise the first q₀ entries to w₁, leaving the rest unchanged. Decompose that dominating vector into initial-segment indicators. The coefficient on q₀ entries is w₁−w_(q₀+1); that on q>q₀ entries is w_q−w_(q+1). Consequently D(w) is nef if

\[
 \mathcal C(w)=V^{-1/n}\left[
 q_0^{1/n}(w_1-w_{q_0+1})+
 \sum_{q=q_0+1}^{r}q^{1/n}(w_q-w_{q+1})\right]\le1.       \tag{C1}
\]

All constants are explicit once the qualitative source threshold is supplied. This is a finite construction and works on one common tuple. It needs no stronger claim about all subspaces, Hilbert-scheme boundedness or uniform families.

**A separating inequality for the entire convex method.** Even grant all equal-subset vectors for 1≤q≤r, stronger than the actual source. In dimension three with r=2N, put y=(4,…,4,1,…,1), each repeated N times. The largest dot product with a size-q generator is

\[
 V^{1/3}\begin{cases}4q^{2/3},&q\le N,\\
 (q+3N)q^{-1/3},&N\le q\le2N.
 \end{cases}
\]

The first branch is increasing. The second decreases until 3N/2 and then increases, so its maximum is at an endpoint. Its value at 2N is smaller than at N because 5/2^(1/3)<4, equivalently 125<128. Thus every vector in the downward convex hull satisfies

\[
 y\cdot w\le4V^{1/3}N^{2/3}.                              \tag{C2}
\]

For the actual strictly volume-admissible vector (J3), y·w=9Nb_N=(99/100)9^(2/3)V^(1/3)N^(2/3), which is larger: cubing gives (99/100)³·81>64. Hence even the enlarged convex method fails on this family, although its maximum weight tends to zero. This is a constraint-respecting obstruction to the method, **not a nonnefness example**. The weights might well satisfy W by another construction.

The route pays a common-locus, all-subsets convex positivity theorem and an exact dual obstruction. To prove W one would need genuinely new unequal-weight nef classes or a nonlinear geometric construction beyond aggregation of equal ones. Renaming the weighted jet-transfer lemma would not overcome (C2).
