Abstract
We establish three generalizations of the Küronya-Lozovanu jet separation criterion via Newton-Okounkov bodies: if an inverted standard simplex of size n+k+εn+k+\varepsilon is contained in all infinitesimal Newton-Okounkov bodies at xx, then KX+DK_{X}+D separates kk-jets at xx. We prove (1) a canonical-free version with a computable multiple m⁡(D)m(D); (2) a multipoint extension for simultaneous jet separation; and (3) a combination of both. Proofs use Trusiani’s framework and Nadel vanishing. We conclude with explicit computations for a double cover of a product of elliptic curves.
1 Introduction
1.1 Background and Motivation
Throughout this paper, we work over ℂ\mathbb{C}, and all varieties XX are assumed to be irreducible, smooth, projective algebraic varieties of dimension nn.
The theory of Newton-Okounkov bodies, introduced by [10, 11], [1], and [4], provides a powerful tool for studying divisors on projective varieties through convex geometry. This construction associates to a divisor a convex body in Euclidean space that encodes asymptotic information about the corresponding linear series. A refinement of this theory is the infinitesimal Newton-Okounkov body, also developed by [1], which offers certain advantages such as independence from auxiliary flag choices in favorable situations. The extension from single-point to multipoint configurations has been pursued by [12] and [13], providing finer geometric invariants.
On the algebraic side, the notion of kk-jet ampleness, systematically studied by [3], generalizes classical positivity conditions: 00-jet ampleness corresponds to global generation, while 11-jet ampleness corresponds to very ampleness. This concept is also referred to as generation of jets or high-order embeddings in the literature. A local variant is jet separation (see [16, Definition 5.1.15]), which requires that global sections separate jets at a given point up to a specified order, and can be viewed as a pointwise version of jet ampleness.
The connection between Newton-Okounkov bodies and positivity notions was established by [6, 15]. In particular, [15] provides a criterion for jet separation at a single point using infinitesimal Newton-Okounkov bodies: the inclusion of an appropriately sized inverted standard simplex guarantees the desired jet separation property.
Theorem 1.1 ([15], Proposition 4.9).
If there exist a positive real number ε\varepsilon and a natural number kk such that
Δn+k+ε−1⊆ΔY∙′​(π∗​(D))\Delta^{-1}_{n+k+\varepsilon}\subseteq\Delta_{Y^{\prime}_{\bullet}}(\pi^{*}(D))
for every infinitesimal flag Y∙′Y^{\prime}_{\bullet} over xx, where Δ−1\Delta^{-1} is inverted standard simplex of side length n+k+ϵn+k+\epsilon. Then KX+DK_{X}+D separates kk-jets.
A natural question is whether this criterion extends to the multipoint setting, where one requires simultaneous jet separation at a prescribed finite set of points. This is the main focus of the present paper. We note that [12] also addresses multipoint jet separation criteria, but based on a different construction of multipoint Newton-Okounkov bodies. Our approach instead builds on Trusiani’s framework [13], which is particularly well-suited for infinitesimal constructions.
The motivation for studying multipoint jet separation criteria via Okounkov bodies is closely related to classical questions about effective positivity of line bundles, for example Matsusaka’s Big Theorem.
Theorem 1.2 (Matsusaka’s Big Theorem [7]).
Let LL be an ample divisor and BB a nef divisor on a projective variety XX of dimension nn. Set ρL=(Ln)\rho_{L}=(L^{n}), ρK=(KX⋅Ln−1)\rho_{K}=(K_{X}\cdot L^{n-1}), and ρB=(B⋅Ln−1)\rho_{B}=(B\cdot L^{n-1}). Then there exists an integer M1=M1​(ρL,ρK,ρB)M_{1}=M_{1}(\rho_{L},\rho_{K},\rho_{B}), depending only on these intersection numbers and the dimension nn, such that m​L−BmL-B is very ample for all m≥M1m\geq M_{1}.
In the toric setting, such positivity questions admit purely combinatorial answers in terms of polytope data: [8] proved Fujita’s very ampleness conjecture for toric varieties using combinatorial properties of associated polytopes, and [9] characterized kk-jet ampleness via intersection numbers with invariant curves and lattice lengths of polytope edges. Our work contributes to this philosophy by providing combinatorial criteria for jet separation—a local positivity notion—using the convex geometry of multipoint Newton-Okounkov bodies that determine for which values of kk a given line bundle LL generates kk-jets at all prescribed points.
A key distinction of our work is that we provide criteria for kk-jet ampleness directly in terms of the line bundle LL itself, rather than relying on the adjoint bundle KX+LK_{X}+L. Since global generation and very ampleness correspond to 00- and 11-jet ampleness respectively, our multipoint criterion provides a computational framework for testing such positivity phenomena via convex geometry.

2.1 Newton-Okounkov Bodies
Definition 2.1 (Newton-Okounkov Body).
Let XX be an irreducible projective variety of dimension nn, and fix an admissible flag
Y∙:X=Y0⊇Y1⊇Y2⊇…⊇Yn−1⊇Yn={p​t},Y_{\bullet}:X=Y_{0}\supseteq Y_{1}\supseteq Y_{2}\supseteq\ldots\supseteq Y_{n-1}\supseteq Y_{n}=\{pt\},
where codimX⁡(Yi)=i\operatorname{codim}_{X}(Y_{i})=i and each YiY_{i} is non-singular at the point YnY_{n}.
Given a big divisor DD on XX, we define a valuation-like function
νY∙:H0​(X,𝒪X​(D))−{0}→ℤn\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}:H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))-\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by setting ν1​(s)=ordY1⁡(s)\nu_{1}(s)=\operatorname{ord}_{Y_{1}}(s), and inductively defining νi​(s)\nu_{i}(s) as the order of vanishing along YiY_{i} of the restriction of ss to Yi−1Y_{i-1} after dividing by the appropriate power of a local equation of Yi−1Y_{i-1}.
The graded semigroup of DD is defined as:
Γ(D)={(νY∙(s),m)∣0≠s∈H0(X,𝒪X(mD)),m≥0}⊂ℕn×ℕ\Gamma(D)=\{(\nu_{Y_{\bullet}}(s),m)\mid 0\neq s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)),m\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}

Δ⁡(D)=ΔY∙​(D)=Σ⁡(Γ)∩(ℝn×{1})\Delta(D)=\Delta_{Y_{\bullet}}(D)=\Sigma(\Gamma)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{1\})
where Σ⁡(Γ)\Sigma(\Gamma) is the closed convex cone spanned by Γ⁡(D)\Gamma(D).
Remark 2.2.
The construction of the NO-body does not require the choice of a flag. A flag is merely one means to obtain a valuation ν:K​(X)∗→ℤn\nu:K(X)^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n} of rank nn, from which we define the NO-body Δ⁡(D,ν)\Delta(D,\nu) via the associated value semigroup. Such valuations can be constructed in many ways. This valuation-theoretic perspective, developed systematically in [4] and further extended in [5], provides a more intrinsic framework that encompasses the flag construction as a special case.
A notable example is the jet-valuation νj​e​t\nu_{jet}, which arises from applying an invertible linear transformation to the valuation vector associated with an infinitesimal flag. This transformation, known as jet separation, establishes a natural correspondence between NO-bodies with respect to νj​e​t\nu_{jet} and those with respect to the infinitesimal flag. More precisely, if the jet valuation is given by
νj​e​t=(ν1,ν2,…,νk),\nu_{jet}=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{k}),
then the infinitesimal flag valuation is defined by the transformation
νi​n​f=(ν1+ν2+…+νk,ν2,ν3,…,νk).\nu_{inf}=(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+...+\nu_{k},\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\ldots,\nu_{k}).
This linear change of coordinates induces a corresponding transformation between the associated NO-bodies, as illustrated in Figure 1.

2.2 Jet Separation and Jet Ampleness
Definition 2.11 (Separation of jets).
Let LL be a line bundle on a projective variety XX, and fix a smooth point x∈Xx\in X with maximal ideal 𝔪x⊆𝒪X\mathfrak{m}_{x}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}. One says that |L||L| separates ss-jets at xx if the natural map
H0​(X,L)⟶H0​(X,L⊗𝒪X,xi/𝔪xs+1)H^{0}(X,L)\longrightarrow H^{0}\bigl(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X,x_{i}}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{s+1}\bigr)
taking sections of LL to their ss-jets is surjective.
For a divisor DD on XX, we say that DD separates ss-jets at xx if the line bundle 𝒪X​(D)\mathcal{O}_{X}(D) separates ss-jets at xx.
Thus LL (or DD) separates 00-jets if and only if LL (or 𝒪X​(D)\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)) is free at xx, and in this case LL (or DD) separates 11-jets if and only if the derivative dx​ϕ|L|d_{x}\phi_{|L|} (or dx​ϕ|D|d_{x}\phi_{|D|}) at xx of the regular map is injective.
Definition 2.12 (k-Jet Ampleness).
Let XX be an nn-dimensional projective manifold and LL a line bundle on XX. For a nonnegative integer kk, we say that LL is kk-jet ample if, given any NN integers k1,…,kNk_{1},\ldots,k_{N} such that k+1=∑i=1Nkik+1=\sum_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}, and any NN distinct points {x1,…,xN}⊂X\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\subset X, the evaluation map
H0(X,L)→H0(X,L⊗𝒪X/(⊗i=1N𝔪xiki))→0H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}\bigl(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}/(\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{x_{i}}^{k_{i}})\bigr)\to 0
is surjective, where 𝔪xi\mathfrak{m}_{x_{i}} denotes the maximal ideal at xix_{i}.
For a divisor DD on XX, we say that DD is kk-jet ample if the line bundle 𝒪X​(D)\mathcal{O}_{X}(D) is kk-jet ample.
Note that:
•
LL (or DD) is spanned (i.e., globally generated) if and only if it is 00-jet ample
•
LL (or DD) is very ample if and only if it is 11-jet ample
Remark 2.13.
Jet ampleness can be viewed as a generalization of jet separation to multiple points. It requires that global sections can simultaneously realize any prescribed jet data at any finite collection of points, with the total order of jets constrained by the ampleness parameter kk.
Definition 2.14 (k-Jet Ampleness Supported on a 0-cycle).
Let XX be an nn-dimensional projective manifold, LL a line bundle on XX, and let Z={x1,…,xN}⊂XZ=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\}\subset X be a 0-cycle of distinct points. For a nonnegative integer kk, we say that LL is kk-jet ample supported on ZZ if, for any integers k1,…,kNk_{1},\ldots,k_{N} satisfying
k+1=∑j=1Nkj,k+1=\sum_{j=1}^{N}k_{j},
the evaluation map
H0(X,L)→H0(X,L⊗𝒪X/(⊗i=1N𝔪xiki))→0H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}\bigl(X,L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}/(\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathfrak{m}_{x_{i}}^{k_{i}})\bigr)\to 0
is surjective, where mxjm_{x_{j}} denotes the maximal ideal at xjx_{j}.
For a divisor DD on XX, DD is said to be kk-jet ample supported on ZZ if 𝒪X​(D)\mathcal{O}_{X}(D) is kk-jet ample supported on ZZ.
Theorem 2.15 ([14] Theorem 2.1).
Let XX be a smooth projective variety and B⊂XB\subset X a smooth divisor. Let MM be a line bundle on XX such that 𝒪X​(d​M)≅𝒪X​(B)\mathcal{O}_{X}(dM)\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(B) and let π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X be the cyclic covering of degree dd defined by MM. Let LL be a line bundle on XX and kk a non-negative integer. If L−q​ML-qM is (k−q)(k-q)-jet ample for q=0,…,min⁡(k,d−1)q=0,\ldots,\min(k,d-1), then π∗​L\pi^{*}L is kk-jet ample.

Theorem 3.1 (Canonical-Free Jet Separation).

Δn+k+ε−1⊆ΔY~∙​(m​π∗​(D))\Delta^{-1}_{n+k+\varepsilon}\subseteq\Delta_{\tilde{Y}_{\bullet}}(m\pi^{*}(D))
for some ε>0\varepsilon>0 and natural number kk, the divisor (m+m⁡(D))⋅D(m+m(D))\cdot D separates kk-jets at xx.
Proof.
This is a special case of Theorem 3.4 with N=1N=1. The complete proof will be given.

Theorem 3.4 (Canonical-Free Multipoint Jet Separation).
Let XX be an nn-dimensional smooth projective variety over ℂ\mathbb{C}, let DD be an ample divisor on XX, and let Z={p1,…,pN}Z=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}\} be a 0-cycle of distinct smooth points on XX. Consider the blow-up π:X¯=BlZ​(X)→X\pi:\overline{X}=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)\to X along ZZ.
There exists m⁡(D)m(D) (depending only on DD) such that m⁡(D)⋅D−KXm(D)\cdot D-K_{X} is ample. For any positive integer mm with the property that
Δn+k+ε−1⊆ΔY~∙,j​(m​π∗​(D))\Delta_{n+k+\varepsilon}^{-1}\subseteq\Delta_{\tilde{Y}_{\bullet},j}(m\pi^{*}(D))
for some ε>0\varepsilon>0, natural number kk, and for all j=1,…,Nj=1,\ldots,N and every tuple of infinitesimal flags Y~∙=(Y~∙(1),…,Y~∙(N))\tilde{Y}_{\bullet}=(\tilde{Y}^{(1)}_{\bullet},\ldots,\tilde{Y}^{(N)}_{\bullet}) with Y~∙(i)\tilde{Y}^{(i)}_{\bullet} centered at pip_{i},
the divisor (m+m⁡(D))⋅D(m+m(D))\cdot D separates kk-jets at the points p1,…,pNp_{1},\ldots,p_{N} simultaneously.

Strategy. Combining the techniques from the previous two theorems, we first construct an auxiliary ample divisor m⁡(D)⋅D−KXm(D)\cdot D-K_{X}, then use the subadditivity of Newton-Okounkov bodies and the property that ample divisor bodies contain the origin to translate the assumption on single-point bodies to inclusions for multipoint bodies, and finally apply Nadel vanishing to obtain jet separation.

Since XX is a projective variety, DD is an ample Cartier divisor, and KXK_{X} is a Cartier divisor, by [16, Example 1.2.10], there exists m⁡(D)m(D) such that m⁡(D)⋅D−KXm(D)\cdot D-K_{X} is ample. We decompose
(m+m⁡(D))⋅D=KX+(m⁡(D)⋅D−KX)+m​D.(m+m(D))\cdot D=K_{X}+\big(m(D)\cdot D-K_{X}\big)+mD.
Step 2: Subadditivity of Newton-Okounkov bodies.
For each j=1,…,Nj=1,\ldots,N and any infinitesimal flag Y~∙(j)\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet} at pjp_{j}, by [1, Corollary 4.12], we have
ΔY~∙(j)​((m⁡(D)⋅π∗​(D)−π∗​(KX))+m⋅π∗​(D))⊇ΔY~∙(j)​(m⁡(D)⋅π∗​(D)−π∗​(KX))+ΔY~∙(j)​(m⋅π∗​(D)).\Delta_{\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet}}((m(D)\cdot\pi^{*}(D)-\pi^{*}(K_{X}))+m\cdot\pi^{*}(D))\supseteq\Delta_{\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet}}(m(D)\cdot\pi^{*}(D)-\pi^{*}(K_{X}))+\Delta_{\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet}}(m\cdot\pi^{*}(D)).
Step 3: Ample divisor body contains the origin.
Since m⁡(D)⋅D−KXm(D)\cdot D-K_{X} is ample, by [15, Corollary 4.2] and [15, Definition 2.5], for any infinitesimal flag Y~∙(j)\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet} at pjp_{j}, we have
𝟎∈ΔY~∙(j)​(m⁡(D)⋅π∗​(D)−π∗​(KX)).\mathbf{0}\in\Delta_{\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet}}(m(D)\cdot\pi^{*}(D)-\pi^{*}(K_{X})).
Step 4: From single-point bodies to multi-point bodies.
The assumption gives Δn+k+ε−1⊆ΔY~∙,j​(m​π∗​(D))\Delta_{n+k+\varepsilon}^{-1}\subseteq\Delta_{\tilde{Y}_{\bullet},j}(m\pi^{*}(D)) for all j=1,…,Nj=1,\ldots,N.
By Lemma 2.9, for any infinitesimal flag Y~∙(j)\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet} at pjp_{j}:
ΔY~∙,j​(m​π∗​(D))⊆ΔY~∙(j)​(m​π∗​(D)).\Delta_{\tilde{Y}_{\bullet},j}(m\pi^{*}(D))\subseteq\Delta_{\tilde{Y}^{(j)}_{\bullet}}(m\pi^{*}(D)).
