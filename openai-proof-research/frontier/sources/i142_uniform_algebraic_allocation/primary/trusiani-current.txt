Multipoint Okounkov bodies
Antonio Trusiani
Note: antonio.trusiani91@gmail.com.
Abstract
Starting from the data of a big line bundle LL on a projective manifold XX with a choice of N≥1N\geq 1 different points on XX we provide a new construction of NN Okounkov bodies which encodes important geometric features of (L→X;p1,…,pN)(L\to X;p_{1},\dots,p_{N}) such as the volume of LL, the (moving) multipoint Seshadri constant of LL at p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N}, and the possibility to construct Kähler packings centered at p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N}. Toric manifolds and surfaces are examined in detail.
Keywords: Okounkov body, Seshadri constant, packings problem, projective manifold, ample line bundle.
2010 Mathematics subject classification: 14C20 (primary); 32Q15, 57R17 (secondary).
1 Introduction
Okounkov in [Oko96] and [Oko03] found a way to associate a convex body Δ⁡(L)⊂ℝn\Delta(L)\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n} to a polarized manifold (X,L)(X,L) where n=dimℂXn=\dim_{\mathbbm{C}}X. Namely,
Δ(L):=⋃k≥1{νp​(s)k:s∈H0​(X,k​L)∖{0}}¯\Delta(L):=\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\Big\{\frac{\nu^{p}(s)}{k}\,:\,s\in H^{0}(X,kL)\setminus\{0\}\Big\}}
where νp​(s)\nu^{p}(s) is the leading term exponent at pp with respect to a total additive order on ℤn\mathbbm{Z}^{n} and holomorphic coordinates centered at p∈Xp\in X (see subsection 2.4). This convex body is now called Okounkov body.
Okounkov’s construction was inspired by toric geometry, indeed in the toric case, if LPL_{P} is a torus-invariant ample line bundle, Δ⁡(LP)\Delta(L_{P}) is essentially equal to the polytope PP.
The same construction works even if LL is a big line bundle, i.e. a line bundle such that VolX​(L):=lim supk→∞n!kn​dimℂH0​(X,k​L)>0\mathrm{Vol}_{X}(L):=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}\dim_{\mathbbm{C}}H^{0}(X,kL)>0, as proved in [LM09], [KKh12] (see also [Bou14]) and the Okounkov body captures the volume of LL since
VolX​(L)=n!​Volℝn​(Δ⁡(L)).\mathrm{Vol}_{X}(L)=n!\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbbm{R}^{n}}\big(\Delta(L)\big).
Moreover if >> is the lexicographical order then the (n−1)−(n-1)-volume of any not trivial slice of the Okounkov body given by Δ(L)∩{x1=t}\Delta(L)\cap\{x_{1}=t\} is related to the restricted volume of L−t​YL-tY along YY where YY is a smooth irreducible divisor such that Y|Up={z1=0}Y_{|U_{p}}=\{z_{1}=0\}.
Another invariant which can be encoded by the Okounkov body is the (moving) Seshadri constant ϵS​(‖L‖,p)\epsilon_{S}(||L||;p) (see [Dem90] in the ample case, or [Nak03] for the extension to the big case). In fact, as Küronya-Lozovanu showed in [KL15a], [KL17], if the Okounkov body is defined using the deglex order11

As observed by Witt Nyström in [WN15], one can restrict ourselves to considering the essential Okounkov body Δ​(L)ess\Delta(L)^{\mathrm{ess}} to get the same characterization of the moving Seshadri constant. This last object is defined as Δ​(L)ess:=⋃k≥1Δk​(L)ess\Delta(L)^{\mathrm{ess}}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\Delta^{k}(L)^{\mathrm{ess}}, where Δk​(L)=Conv⁡({ν⁡(s)k:s∈H0​(X,k​L)∖{0}})\Delta^{k}(L)=\mathrm{Conv}(\{\frac{\nu(s)}{k}\,:\,s\in H^{0}(X,kL)\setminus\{0\}\}) and the essential part of Δk​(L)\Delta^{k}(L) consists of its interior as subset of ℝ≥0n\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}^{n} with its natural induced topology.
Seshadri constants are also defined for a collection of different points. For a nef line bundle LL, the multipoint Seshadri constant of LL at p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N} is given as
ϵS​(L,p1,…,pN):=infCL⋅C∑j=1Nmultpj​C.\epsilon_{S}(L;p_{1},\dots,p_{N}):=\inf_{C}\frac{L\cdot C}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{mult}_{p_{j}}C}.
In this paper we introduce a multipoint version of the Okounkov body. More precisely, for a fixed big line bundle LL on a projective manifold XX of dimension nn and p1,…,pN∈Xp_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in X different points, we construct NN Okounkov bodies Δj​(L)⊂ℝn\Delta_{j}(L)\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n} for j=1,…,Nj=1,\dots,N.
Definition 1.1.
Let LL be a big line bundle and let >> be a fixed total additive order on ℤn\mathbbm{Z}^{n}.
Δj(L):=⋃k≥1{νpj​(s)k:s∈Vk,j}¯⊂ℝn\Delta_{j}(L):=\overline{\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\Big\{\frac{\nu^{p_{j}}(s)}{k}\,:\,s\in V_{k,j}\Big\}}\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n}

We observe that the multipoint Okounkov body of LL at pjp_{j} is obtained by considering all sections whose leading term in pjp_{j} is strictly smaller than those at the other points.
They are convex compact sets in ℝn\mathbbm{R}^{n} but, unlike the one-point case, for N≥2N\geq 2 it can happen that some Δj​(L)\Delta_{j}(L) are empty (Remark 3.8). The definition does not depend on the order of the points.

Next we recall another interpretation of the one point Seshadri constant: ϵS​(L,p)\epsilon_{S}(L;p) is equal to the supremum of rr such that there exists an holomorphic embedding f:(Br​(0),ωs​t)→(X,L)f:(B_{r}(0),\omega_{st})\to(X,L) with the property that f∗​ωs​tf_{*}\omega_{st} extends to a Kähler form ω\omega with cohomology class c1​(L)c_{1}(L) (see Theorem 5.1.22 and Proposition 5.3.17. in [Laz04]). This result is a consequence of a deep analysis in symplectic geometry by McDuff-Polterovich ([MP94]), where they dealt with the symplectic packings problem (in the same spirit, Biran in [Bir97] proved the symplectic analog of the Nagata’s conjecture).
Subsequently Kaveh in [Kav16] showed how the one-point Okounkov body can be used to construct a sympletic packing. Along the same lines Witt Nyström in [WN15] introduced the torus-invariant domain Ω⁡(L):=μ−1​(Δ​(L)ess)\Omega(L):=\mu^{-1}\big(\Delta(L)^{\mathrm{ess}}\big) (called Okounkov domain) for μ:ℂn→ℝn\mu:\mathbbm{C}^{n}\to\mathbbm{R}^{n}, μ⁡(z1,…,zn):=(|z1|2,…,|zn|2)\mu(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=(\lvert z_{1}\rvert^{2},\dots,\lvert z_{n}\rvert^{2}), and showed how it approximates the polarized manifold.

1.1 Organization
Section 2 contains some preliminary facts on singular metrics, base loci of divisors and Okounkov bodies.
In section 3 we develop the theory of multipoint Okounkov bodies: the goal is to generalize some results in [LM09] for N≥1N\geq 1. We prove here Theorem A.
Section 4 is dedicated to show Theorem 3.
In section 5 we introduce the notion of moving multipoint Seshadri constants. Moreover we prove Theorem B, connecting the moving multipoint Seshadri constant in a more analytical language in the spirit of [Dem90], and deduce the connection between the moving multipoint Seshadri constant and Kähler packings.
The last section 6 deals with the two aforementioned particular cases: toric manifolds and surfaces.

2 Preliminaries
2.1 Singular metrics and (currents of) curvature
Let LL be a holomorphic line bundle over a projective manifold XX. A smooth (hermitian) metric φ\varphi is the collection of an open cover {Uj}j∈J\{U_{j}\}_{j\in J} of XX and of smooth functions φj∈𝒞∞​(Uj)\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U_{j}) such that on each not-empty intersection Ui∩UjU_{i}\cap U_{j} one has φi=φj+ln⁡|gi,j|2\varphi_{i}=\varphi_{j}+\ln|g_{i,j}|^{2} where gi,jg_{i,j} are the transition function defining the line bundle LL. Note that if sjs_{j} are nowhere zero local sections with respect to which the transition function are calculated then |sj|=e−φj|s_{j}|=e^{-\varphi_{j}}. The curvature of a smooth metric φ\varphi is given on each open UjU_{j} by d​dc​φjdd^{c}\varphi_{j} where dc=i4​π(∂−∂¯)d^{c}=\frac{i}{4\pi}(\partial-\bar{\partial}) so that d​dc=i2​π​∂∂¯dd^{c}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}. It is a global (1,1)−(1,1)-form on XX, so for convenience we use the notation d​dc​φdd^{c}\varphi. The metric is called positive if the (1,1)−(1,1)-form d​dc​φdd^{c}\varphi is a Kähler form, i.e. if the functions φj\varphi_{j} are strictly plurisubharmonic. By the well-known Kodaira Embedding Theorem, a line bundle admits a positive metric iff it is ample.
Demailly in [Dem90] introduced a weaker notion of metric: a (hermitian) singular metric φ\varphi is given by a collection of data as before but with the weaker condition that φj∈Ll​o​c1​(Uj)\varphi_{j}\in L^{1}_{loc}(U_{j}). If the functions φj\varphi_{j} are also plurisubharmonic, then we say that φ\varphi is a singular positive metric. Note that d​dc​φdd^{c}\varphi exists in the weak sense, indeed it is a closed positive (1,1)−(1,1)-current (we will call it the current of curvature of the metric φ\varphi). We say that d​dc​φdd^{c}\varphi is a Kähler current if it dominates some Kähler form ω\omega. By Proposition 4.2.4.2. in [Dem90] a line bundle is big iff it admits a singular positive metric whose current of curvature is a Kähler current.
In this paper we will often work with ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-line bundles, i.e. formal linear combinations of line bundles. Moreover since we will only consider projective manifolds, we will often identify an ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-line bundle as a class of ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-divisors modulo linear equivalence and its first Chern class as a class of ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-divisors modulo numerical equivalence.
2.2 Base loci
We recall here the construction of the base loci (see [ELMNP06]).
Given a ℚ−\mathbbm{Q}-divisor DD, let 𝔹⁡(D):=⋂k≥1Bs⁡(k​D)\mathbbm{B}(D):=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\mathrm{Bs}(kD) be the stable base locus of DD where Bs⁡(k​D)\mathrm{Bs}(kD) is the base locus of the linear system |k​D||kD|. The base loci 𝔹+​(D):=⋂A𝔹⁡(D−A)\mathbbm{B}_{+}(D):=\bigcap_{A}\mathbbm{B}(D-A) and 𝔹−​(D):=⋃A𝔹⁡(D+A)\mathbbm{B}_{-}(D):=\bigcup_{A}\mathbbm{B}(D+A), where AA varies among all ample ℚ−\mathbbm{Q}-divisors, are called respectively augmented and restricted base locus of DD. They are invariant under rescaling and 𝔹−​(D)⊂𝔹⁡(D)⊂𝔹+​(D)\mathbbm{B}_{-}(D)\subset\mathbbm{B}(D)\subset\mathbbm{B}_{+}(D). Moreover as described in a work of Nakamaye, [Nak03], the restricted and the augmented base loci are numerical invariants and can be extended to the Neron-Severi space (for a real class it is enough to consider only ample ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-divisors AA such that D±AD\pm A is a ℚ−\mathbbm{Q}-divisor). The stable base loci do not, see Example 1.1.1.1. in [ELMNP06], although by Proposition 1.2.6.1.2.6. in [ELMNP06] the subset where the augmented and restricted base loci are equal is open and dense in the Neron-Severi space N1​(X)ℝ\mathrm{N}^{1}(X)_{\mathbbm{R}}.
Thanks to the numerical invariance of the restricted and augmented base loci, we will often talk of restricted and/or augmented base loci of a ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-line bundle LL. Moreover the restricted base locus can be thought as a measure of the nefness since DD is nef iff 𝔹−​(D)=∅\mathbbm{B}_{-}(D)=\emptyset, while the augmented base locus can be thought as a measure of the ampleness since DD is ample iff 𝔹+​(D)=∅\mathbbm{B}_{+}(D)=\emptyset. Moreover 𝔹−​(D)=X\mathbbm{B}_{-}(D)=X iff DD is not pseudoeffective while 𝔹+​(D)=X\mathbbm{B}_{+}(D)=X iff DD is not big.
2.3 Additive Semigroups and their Okounkov bodies
We briefly recall some notions about the theory of the Okounkov bodies constructed from additive semigroups (the main references are [KKh12] and [Bou14], see also [Kho93]).
Let S⊂ℤn+1S\subset\mathbbm{Z}^{n+1} be an additive subsemigroup not necessarily finitely generated. We denote by C⁡(S)C(S) the closed cone in ℝn+1\mathbbm{R}^{n+1} generated by SS, i.e. the closure of the set of all linear combinations ∑iλi​si\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}s_{i} with λi∈ℝ≥0\lambda_{i}\in\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0} and si∈Ss_{i}\in S. In this paper we will exclusively work with semigroups SS such that the pair (S,ℝn×ℝ≥0)(S,\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}) is admissible, i.e. S⊂ℝn×ℝ≥0S\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}, or strongly admissible, i.e. it is admissible and C⁡(S)C(S) intersects the hyperplane ℝn×{0}\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\{0\} only in the origin (see section §​1.2\lx@sectionsign 1.2 in [KKh12]). We recall that a closed convex cone CC with apex the origin is called strictly convex iff the biggest linear subspace contained in CC is the origin, so if (S,ℝn×ℝ≥0)(S,\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}) is strongly admissible then C⁡(S)C(S) is strictly convex.
Definition 2.1.
Let (S,ℝn×ℝ≥0)(S,\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}) be an admissible pair. Then
Δ⁡(S):=π⁡(C⁡(S)∩{ℝn×{1}})\Delta(S):=\pi\big(C(S)\cap\{\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\{1\}\}\big)
is called Okounkov convex set of (𝐒,ℝ𝐧×ℝ≥𝟎)(\mathbf{S},\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbf{n}}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\mathbf{\geq 0}}), where π:ℝn+1→ℝn\pi:\mathbbm{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbbm{R}^{n} is the projection to the first nn coordinates. If (S,ℝn×ℝ≥0)(S,\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}) is strongly admissible, Δ⁡(S)\Delta(S) is also called Okounkov body of (𝐒,ℝ𝐧×ℝ≥𝟎)(\mathbf{S},\mathbbm{R}^{\mathbf{n}}\times\mathbbm{R}_{\mathbf{\geq 0}}).
Remark 2.2.
The convexity of Δ⁡(S)\Delta(S) is immediate, while it is not hard to check that it is compact iff the pair is strongly admissible. Moreover SS generates a subgroup of ℤn+1\mathbbm{Z}^{n+1} of maximal rank iff Δ⁡(S)\Delta(S) has interior not-empty.
The following result is well-known and it has many interesting consequences.

5 Local Positivity
5.1 Moving Multipoint Seshadri Constant
Definition 5.1.
Let LL be a nef line bundle on XX. The quantity
ϵS​(L,p1,…,pN):=infL⋅C∑i=1Nmultpi​C\epsilon_{S}(L;p_{1},\dots,p_{N}):=\inf\frac{L\cdot C}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{mult}_{p_{i}}C}
where the infimum is over all irreducible curves C⊂XC\subset X passing through at least one of the points p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N} is called the multipoint Seshadri constant at p1,…,pN\mathbf{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}} of 𝐋\mathbf{L}.
This constant has played an important role in the last three decades and it is the natural extension of the Seshadri constant introduced by Demailly in [Dem90].
The following Lemma is well-known and its proof can be found for instance in [Laz04], [BDRH+09]:
Lemma 5.2.
Let LL be a nef line bundle on XX. Then

where μ:X~→X\mu:\tilde{X}\to X is the blow-up at Z={p1,…,pN}Z=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\}, EiE_{i} is the exceptional divisor above pip_{i} and where the infimum on the right side is over all positive dimensional irreducible subvarieties VV containing at least one point among p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N}.
The characterization of Lemma 5.2 allows to extend the definition to nef ℚ−\mathbbm{Q}-line bundles by homogeneity and to nef ℝ−\mathbbm{R}-line bundles by continuity.

About the Nagata’s Conjecture:
One modern version of the Nagata’s conjecture says that for a choice of very general points p1,…,pN∈ℙ2{p_{1},\dots,p_{N}\in\mathbbm{P}^{2}} and N≥9N\geq 9, the ample line bundle 𝒪ℙ2​(1)\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1) has maximal multipoint Seshadri constant at p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N}, i.e. ϵS​(𝒪ℙ2​(1),N)=1/N\epsilon_{S}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1);N)=1/\sqrt{N} where to simplify the notation we did not report the points since they are very general. Thanks to Theorems A, B, we can then read the Nagata’s conjecture in the following way.
Conjecture 6.12 ([Nag58], Nagata’s Conjecture).
For N≥9N\geq 9 very general points in ℙ2\mathbbm{P}^{2}, let {Δj​(𝒪ℙ2​(1))}j=1N\{\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1))\}_{j=1}^{N} be the multipoint Okounkov bodies calculated from a family of valuations νpj\nu^{p_{j}} associated to a family of infinitesimal flags centered respectively at p1,…,pNp_{1},\dots,p_{N}. Then the following equivalent statements hold:
i)
ϵS​(𝒪ℙ2​(1),N)=1/N\epsilon_{S}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1);N)=1/\sqrt{N};
ii)
Δj​(𝒪ℙ2​(1))=1N​Σ2\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\Sigma_{2}, where Σ2\Sigma_{2} is the standard 2−2-symplex;
iii)
Ωj​(𝒪ℙ2​(1))=B1N​(0)\Omega_{j}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1))=B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}}(0);
Remark 6.13.
It is well know that the conjecture holds if N≥9N\geq 9 is a perfect square. And a similar conjecture (called Biran-Nagata-Szemberg’s conjecture) claims that for any ample line bundle LL on a projective manifold of dimension nn there exist N0=N0​(X,L)N_{0}=N_{0}(X,L) big enough such that ϵS​(L,N)=LnNn\epsilon_{S}(L;N)=\sqrt[n]{\frac{L^{n}}{N}} for any N≥N0N\geq N_{0} very general points, i.e. it is maximal. This conjecture can be similarly read through the multipoint Okounkov bodies as Δj​(L)=LnNn​Σn\Delta_{j}(L)=\sqrt[n]{\frac{L^{n}}{N}}\Sigma_{n} for any N≥N0N\geq N_{0} very general points at XX.

Remark 6.16.
We recall that Biran in [Bir97] gave an homological criterion to check if a 4−4-dimensional symplectic manifold admits a full symplectic packings by NN equal balls for large NN, showing that (ℙ2,ωF​S)(\mathbbm{P}^{2},\omega_{FS}) admits a full symplectic packings for N≥9N\geq 9. Moreover it is well-known that for any N≤9N\leq 9 the supremum over all rr such that {(Br​(0),ωs​t)}j=1N\{(B_{r}(0),\omega_{st})\}_{j=1}^{N} packs into (ℙ2,𝒪ℙ2​(1))(\mathbbm{P}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1)) coincides with the supremum over all rr such that (ℙ2,ωF​S)(\mathbbm{P}^{2},\omega_{FS}) admits a symplectic packings of NN balls of radii rr (called Gromov width). Therefore by Theorem 3 and Corollary 5.15 the Nagata’s conjecture is true iff the Gromov width of NN balls on (ℙ2,ωF​S)(\mathbbm{P}^{2},\omega_{FS}) coincides with the multipoint Seshadri constant of 𝒪ℙ2​(1)\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{P}^{2}}(1) at NN very general points.
