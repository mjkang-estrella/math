                                                 Abstract. We prove a new signed elementary symmetric function expansion of the chro-
                                                 matic quasisymmetric function of any natural unit interval graph. We then use a sign-
                                                 reversing involution to prove a new combinatorial formula for K-chains, which are graphs
                                                 formed by joining cliques at single vertices. This formula immediately implies e-positivity
                                                 and e-unimodality for K-chains. We also prove a version of our signed e-expansion for
                                                 arbitrary graphs.




                                                                                   1. Introduction
                                             In 1995, Stanley [49] introduced the chromatic symmetric function XG (x), a generaliza-
                                          tion of the chromatic polynomial of a graph G, and famously conjectured with Stembridge
                                          [49, 53] that XG (x) is e-positive whenever G is the incomparability graph of a (3 + 1)-free
                                          partially ordered set. Guay-Paquet [32] showed that it suffices to prove e-positivity for the
                                          smaller class of natural unit interval graphs. Shareshian and Wachs [45] defined a further
                                          generalization called the chromatic quasisymmetric function XG (x; q), showed that if G is
                                          a natural unit interval graph, then XG (x; q) is in fact symmetric; and conjectured that it is
                                          e-positive and e-unimodal for such graphs. This notorious problem has received considerable
                                          attention. Many authors proved e-positivity for certain families of graphs [7, 13, 15, 17, 22,
                                          27, 28, 29, 36, 39, 40, 54, 55, 56], studied related positivity problems [9, 10, 20, 21, 23, 24,
                                          25, 26, 30, 32, 42, 47], and explored alternative generalizations of the chromatic symmetric
                                          function [1, 6, 8, 18, 19, 31, 51]. The Stanley–Stembridge conjecture is also closely related to
                                          positivity of immanants of Jacobi–Trudi matrices [53], the cohomology of regular semisimple
                                          Hessenberg varieties [2, 11, 14, 33, 35, 46], and characters of Hecke algebras evaluated at
                                          Kazhdan–Lusztig basis elements [16, 48].

   Stanley was interested in characterizing e-positivity of XG (x) in order to generalize positiv-
ity results of immanants of Jacobi–Trudi matrices [53]. The celebrated Stanley–Stembridge
conjecture [49, Conjecture 5.1], [53, Conjecture 5.5] asserts that we have e-positivity for
incomparability graphs of (3 + 1)-free partially ordered sets and Guay-Paquet [32, Theorem
5.1] showed that it suffices to prove this for the smaller class of natural unit interval graphs.
The following quasisymmetric refinement by Shareshian and Wachs has seen connections to
Hessenberg varieties and Hecke algebras.
Conjecture 2.3. [45, Conjecture 1.3] If G is a natural unit interval graph, then XG (x; q) is
e-positive and e-unimodal.
Remark 2.4. An abstract graph is called a unit interval graph if its vertices can be labelled 1
through n so that (2.2) holds. The name comes from an equivalent characterization as a graph
whose vertices are unit intervals of the real line and whose edges join intersecting intervals.
It is convenient to consider natural unit interval graphs, where the adjective “natural” means
that such a labelling is given. The chromatic quasisymmetric function does not depend on
the choice of labelling, as long as (2.2) is satisfied [45, Proposition 4.2].
Remark 2.5. Let G = ([n], E) be a natural unit interval graph and consider the graph
G′ = ([n], {{n + 1 − j, n + 1 − i} : {i, j} ∈ E}) given by reversing the vertex labels. If we
restrict to n variables and associate a proper colouring κ : [n] → [n] of G to κ′ : [n] → [n] by
setting κ′ (i) = n+1−κ(n+1−i), it follows that XG (x1 , . . . , xn ; q) = XG′ (xn , . . . , x1 ; q). Then
we have XG (x; q) = XG′ (x; q) by symmetry in the xi variables. This proves palindromicity
in q and shows that we could have equivalently defined XG (x; q) using descents instead of
ascents. Similarly, we can switch the roles of i and (n + 1 − i) in everything that follows, for
example, we can use increasing trees instead of decreasing trees.

                                      5. Further directions
  We conclude by proposing some further avenues of study for the Stanley–Stembridge
conjecture. First, we can continue to seek sign-reversing involutions on forest triples to
prove e-positivity and e-unimodality for other graphs. The author checked by computer
that every natural unit interval graph with at most 9 vertices has a nice involution.
Problem 5.1. Let G be a natural unit interval graph. Find a nice involution for G to prove
that XG (x; q) is e-positive. Furthermore, by enumerating the fixed points, find an explicit
e-expansion to prove that XG (x; q) is e-unimodal.
     For example, Dahlberg [22, Theorem 4.5] proved that for the class of triangular ladders
(5.1)                     Pn,2 = ([n], {{i, j} : 1 ≤ j − 2 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ n}),
the chromatic symmetric function XPn,2 (x) is e-positive. We could try to strengthen this
result by finding a nice involution to prove a quasisymmetric version. More specifically,
because triangular ladders are built by successively joining a new vertex to two vertices, we
could take an inductive approach.
Problem 5.2. Let G = ([n], E) be a natural unit interval graph with {1, 2} ∈ E and let ϕ
be a nice involution for G. Find a nice involution for the natural unit interval graph
(5.2)               ([n + 1], {{1, 2}, {1, 3}} ∪ {{i + 1, j + 1} : {i, j} ∈ E}).
Furthermore, by enumerating the fixed points, prove that XPn,2 (x; q) is e-unimodal.
   Much of the progress toward the Stanley–Stembridge conjecture considers a particular
natural unitP   interval graph G and shows that every coefficient cµ (q) of the e-expansion
XG (x; q) = µ cµ (q)eµ is a positive polynomial. We could also consider a dual approach
where we fix a particular partition µ and show positivity for every natural unit interval graph
G. Upcoming work of Sagan and the author [43] proves that cµ (q) is positive whenever
µ1 ≤ 3. Hwang [37, Theorem 5.13] proved that cµ (q) is positive whenever µ is a hook,
