                  4. BPS cohomology and geometric representation theory
   Since the right-hand side of the isomorphism Φ in Theorem 2.2 is the underlying space of an algebra,
it is natural to wonder if HA carries an interesting algebra structure. Indeed it does, and it is often
considerably more interesting than a free supercommutative algebra; Φ is more akin to a Poincaré–
Birkhoff–Witt (PBW) type isomorphism13 than an actual isomorphism of algebras. To define the BPS
Lie algebra that plays a role similar to that of the Lie algebra g in the PBW theorem, we delve into
the theory of cohomological Hall algebras (CoHAs).

                     6. BPS cohomology beyond moduli stacks of objects
   Many stacks of interest are not moduli stacks of objects in a linear category. For instance, let M
be a real oriented closed three-manifold without boundary, and consider the stack MG (M ) of G-local
systems for G a connected reductive group not equal to GLn . By [56], this stack has a (-1)-shifted
symplectic structure, and by [53] it carries orientation data, so that we can define the DT sheaf φDT as in
§2.6. It is not immediately clear how to define the BPS sheaf or integrality for MG (M ). For example if
G is semisimple, the decomposition of p∗ φDT into cohomologically shifted mixed Hodge modules starts
in degree zero, not one, where p : MG (M ) → MG (M ) is the good moduli space morphism.
