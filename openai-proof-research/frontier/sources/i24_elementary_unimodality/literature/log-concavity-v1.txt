                                                             A 13-VERTEX COUNTEREXAMPLE TO e-LOG-CONCAVITY
                                                                FOR CHROMATIC QUASISYMMETRIC FUNCTIONS

                                                                                                 BORIS KAFIDOV


                                                   A BSTRACT. We exhibit a connected natural unit interval graph on 13 vertices whose chromatic
                                                   quasisymmetric function has an elementary-basis coefficient that is positive, palindromic, and
                                                   unimodal, but not log-concave. For the Hessenberg function
                                                                                   h = (2, 4, 4, 6, 7, 10, 10, 10, 10, 12, 12, 13, 13)
                                                   and λ = (6, 5, 1, 1), the coefficients of q5 , q6 , q7 in [eλ ] XG(h) (x; q) are 1, 6, 38; hence 62 < 1 · 38. This
                                                   disproves Conjecture 5.3 of Abreu–Nigro and its later formulations by Tom and by Sagan–Tom.
                                                   The calculation is certified by a standalone deterministic verifier using exact integer and rational
                                                   arithmetic.

                                                                                              1. I NTRODUCTION
                                             For a natural unit interval graph G, the chromatic quasisymmetric function XG (x; q) is
                                           symmetric in x and admits an expansion
                                                                                        XG (x; q) =          ∑         c λ ( q ) eλ .
                                                                                                         λ⊢|V ( G )|

                                           Abreu and Nigro conjectured that every polynomial cλ (q) is log-concave [1, Conjecture 5.3].
                                           The same assertion was later formulated by Tom [4, Conjecture 5.6] and by Sagan and Tom [2,
                                           Conjecture 4.2]. Abreu and Nigro report exhaustive verification for indifference graphs on at
                                           most 12 vertices [1, p. 19].
                                             The purpose of this note is to give an exact counterexample on 13 vertices. The failure is small:
                                           the relevant inequality misses by 2. Moreover, the coefficient polynomial remains positive,
                                           palindromic, and unimodal. Thus the example does not challenge the Shareshian–Wachs e-
                                           positivity or e-unimodality conjectures; it only refutes the stronger log-concavity assertion. To
                                           the best of the author’s knowledge, this is the first reported counterexample.
                                             The proof is computer-assisted but fully finite. We derive the two recurrences implemented
                                           by the accompanying verifier, record its complete input and output certificate, and use only
                                           exact arithmetic. No model-generated claim is treated as mathematical evidence; the role of
                                           generative-AI tools is disclosed at the end of the paper.

Proof. In the product eλ = ∏i eλi , row i records the distinct variables chosen from the factor eλi .
Its row sum is λi , while column j records the exponent of x j and therefore has sum µ j . This
correspondence is bijective. Comparing monomial coefficients gives the displayed system. □
  The matrix B is a change-of-basis matrix and therefore invertible. Its entries can themselves
be obtained recursively: choose the columns occupied by the first row, reduce their remaining
capacities by one, and continue. Thus both sides of the system in theorem 3.2 are computable
using only finite subset enumeration and integer addition.

                                          4. C ONSEQUENCE AND SCOPE
  Theorem 2.1 disproves the universal e-log-concavity assertion of Abreu–Nigro, Tom, and
Sagan–Tom. It does not disprove e-positivity or e-unimodality: the offending coefficient itself
has both properties. The example therefore separates log-concavity from the weaker shape
properties that motivated the conjecture.
  The published order-12 computation of Abreu and Nigro places this witness at the first order
beyond their reported verification. We make no independent minimal-order claim here, and
the priority statement in the introduction is necessarily limited to the author’s knowledge of
the public record as of 22 July 2026.
