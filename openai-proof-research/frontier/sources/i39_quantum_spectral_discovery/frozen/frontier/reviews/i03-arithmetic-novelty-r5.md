## 1. What has improved, and what has not been claimed

AP states that for **each fixed** modulus M and reduced class b, the decreasing normalized logarithms of the prime factors of p−1, counted with multiplicity, converge in every finite joint distribution to PD(1), with p sampled among primes in that class. It includes b−1 nonunit modulo M. Its one-factor consequence is, for fixed 0 < θ < 1/2,

\[
 \frac{\#\{x<p\le2x:p\equiv b\pmod M,\ P^+(p-1)>x^{1-\theta}\}}
 {\pi(2x)-\pi(x)}\longrightarrow
 \frac{-\log(1-\theta)}{\varphi(M)}.
\]

A+ states that for every integer a with |a| ≥ 2 which is not a positive square, some c_a > 0 bounds the proportion of primes with ord_p(a) = p−1 below in every sufficiently large dyadic interval. Thus the cumulative relative **lower** density is positive. Negative squares and odd perfect powers are included. Constants and thresholds may depend on a; M is fixed before every limit. There is no assertion of a natural density, Artin's density formula, an effective threshold, growing-modulus uniformity, or primitive-root positivity in every reduced class.

The closest accepted final Artin theorem is P, `sections/01-introduction.tex`, `thm:primitive-root`/`thm:main`: c_a x/(log x)^2 in every sufficiently large dyad. Its next paragraph explicitly contrasts that with the predicted x/log x scale. A+ reaches that scale with a lower bound and index **one**, beyond the bounded-index R004. This is a substantive strengthening even though the precise density remains unresolved. The comparison comes from the [pinned primary manuscript P](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/Primitive-roots-for-every-admissible-integer-base-October-4-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex), not an inference from MathDB metadata.

The closest accepted PD theorem is D, `sections/01-introduction.tex`, `thm:main`: the same full joint law over all primes, without a finite progression label. That conclusion alone does not determine its joint distribution with the label. AP adds this missing joint information. It is not obtained by applying conditional probability to D's limiting marginal. [Pinned D introduction](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/The-Poisson-Dirichlet-Law-for-Prime-Predecessors-September-24-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex).

## 3. Stronger accepted inputs and possible older shortcuts

The search screened the complete manuscript catalogue and path tree, then read the relevant actual statements. It was not a line-by-line reading of all 719 manuscripts. Besides families 011 and 029 and their companions, I checked the following plausible sources of a stronger automatic implication.

P's `thm:hecke-zero-free` applies to all finite-order Hecke characters over every cyclotomic field containing μ_12, with common width 10^−6. P's `spl:field-bounds` and `spl:smooth-estimate` already give the all-q splitting control used in A+; the manuscript's later restricted-q proposition must not obscure that stronger input. The deterministic polynomial factorization companion, `sections/10-analytic.tex`, `analytic:hecke` and `analytic:explicit`, imports exactly that width and states a uniform smoothed prime-ideal asymptotic. It does not upgrade the width to GRH or assert primitive-root density. [Pinned factorization analytic section](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/Deterministic-Polynomial-Factorization-over-Prime-Fields-October-4-2026/build/sections/10-analytic.tex).

The stronger numerical 7/8 half-plane in the quasi-Riemann manuscript, `build/paper.tex`, `thm:main`, covers Dirichlet L-functions and finite-order Hecke L-functions over Q(√−3). It does not assert the same width for arbitrary cyclotomic Kummer fields. The 11/12 companion is weaker, not a source of GRH. Neither furnishes level-one Elliott–Halberstam distribution. [Pinned QRH source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/build/paper.tex).

A particularly relevant older primary theorem is **Amora Nongkynrih, “A conditional proof of Artin's conjecture for primitive roots,” C. R. Math. Rep. Acad. Sci. Canada 23 (2001), 46–52, Theorem 1 on p. 47**. It proves N_2(x) ≫ x/log x if every squarefree-k field Q(ζ_k,2^(1/k)) has a zero-free half-plane of width greater than

\[
 \tfrac12\exp(-12A/5)\simeq0.2038,
 \qquad A=\prod_q(1-1/(q(q-1))).
\]

The **factor 1/2 is present in the actual paper**; an indexed survey excerpt omits it. I checked the [journal scan](https://mathreports.ca/download/3615/) and preserved its [theorem-page image](../sources/i03_arithmetic_novelty_r5/nongkynrih-theorem-p47.png). Even extending P's standard abelian factorization/descent to squarefree k supplies only width 10^−6. That is far too narrow. This established conditional theorem therefore does not collapse A+ into a routine application of the accepted Hecke statement. The historical Hooley weaker-than-GRH observation discussed on p. 46 also requires a much wider strip.

The accepted joint Dickman theorem for consecutive **integers**, `thm:main`, has ordinary integer sampling; it does not give joint prime-conditioned factor statistics. The accepted binary corrected Elliott theorem, `thm:elliott` and `cor:affine`, concerns fixed bounded multiplicative functions, with a uniformly nonpretentious factor. Prime indicators and the moving large-prime-factor statistics needed here are not an instance of that statement. Its fixed-progression formulation is not a theorem about PD for prime predecessors. Their pinned introductions and hashes are saved. These checks exclude specific tempting substitutions; they are not a claim that every possible consequence of the corpus has been classified.

## 4. Primary literature for the final statements

**Shifted-prime PD.** Ford–Konyagin–Luca, *Prime chains and Pratt trees*, §6, Conjecture 5, formulates marginal PD for p−1 and explains that Elliott–Halberstam would imply it. It does not derive AP by conditioning a marginal law. The probabilistic ranking/stick-breaking passage is inherited mathematics, not novelty here. [Author paper](https://www.ford126.web.illinois.edu/wwwpapers/chains.pdf).

The strongest directly relevant general theorem located is **Bharadwaj–Rodgers, *Large prime factors of well-distributed sequences*, Theorem 7**. I read v4 and checked the publisher's 2026 text: Theorem 7 assumes 1-well-distribution; Lemma 8 supplies factorial correlations only on sum t_i < σ from σ-well-distribution; Proposition 2 supplies level 1/2 for shifted primes. Theorem 11 is an **upper** bound for the frequency of a prime factor above u^(1−ε). It cannot supply the needed lower reservoir. Remark 9 proposes a weaker divisor-support hypothesis but does not prove that hypothesis for the progression-restricted prime sequence. A level-one result for that sequence would indeed make AP largely standard; no such input was identified in the accepted corpus. [Primary v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11884v4), [published paper](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008439526101982).

**Primitive roots in progressions.** Moree, *On primes in arithmetic progression having a prescribed primitive root. II*, Theorem 1, gives the GRH-conditional asymptotic π_g(x;f,a)=δ(a,f,g)x/log x+O_{f,g}(x log log x/log²x). Theorems 2–4 evaluate and constrain that density. This is stronger information under a stronger unavailable hypothesis, not an established duplicate under P's strip. It also cautions against claiming primitive-root positivity in every class. [Primary v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.3062v1).

**Recent fixed-base and index results.** Goldmakher–Martin–Péringuey, *Refinements of Artin's primitive root conjecture*, §8, Theorems 8.1 and 8.8, gives unconditional upper bounds on small index-factor events. Its lower bound for an expectation measures index deficiency, not primitive-root abundance. Its exact densities use GRH. [Primary v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19601v1). Paul Pollack's *Counting primes with a given primitive root, uniformly*, Theorem 1.1, also explicitly assumes GRH; its uniformity in the base does not remove that premise. [Primary v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05601v1).

Murty–Naik, *A non-abelian large sieve and Artin's primitive root conjecture*, Theorem 1 and Remark 1.1, assumes a particular nonabelian-large-sieve inequality and yields ≫ x/log²x, first for 2 and then other admissible bases. The paper's partial unconditional estimates are not that full hypothesis. Even the theorem's conclusion is below A+'s scale. [Primary v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21573v1).

**An overlapping public claim really exists.** Carella, *Simple Proof of the Primitive Root Conjecture*, arXiv:1707.06517v14, Theorem 1.1, claims an unconditional positive-density asymptotic for every admissible fixed integer. Thus “no prior paper claims this conclusion” would be false. The [actual manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.06517v14) and [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06517v14) were retrieved; the arXiv version date is 14 February 2026, notwithstanding MathDB's “undated” wording and the manuscript's separate August date.

This is not accepted-corpus input or an established theorem for this review. There is also a concrete reason not to promote it on the strength of its theorem heading: for a nonprimitive u, the inner nonzero additive-character sum in its Lemma 6.1 is exactly −1 for each primitive exponent, so the displayed p-contribution is −φ(p−1)/p. The stated general lemma includes u=1, contradicting its claimed power-saving absolute bound. Equation (7.3) additionally inserts δ(u) into the trivial-character contribution without an identity justifying it. This short check is not a full referee report; it suffices to show that this public claim is not a verified duplicate of A+. Its existence remains relevant to priority wording.

## 5. Why the new bridge clears the nonroutine gate reasonably

The novelty-bearing unit is **RC with its compatible extraction and A+ application**, not the finite congruence identity alone. The work selects both physical residues before transference; after exact stripping and reassembly, one full small shared prime interval mixes the common unit dilation. For a unit d modulo M,

\[
 \frac1{\varphi(M)}\sum_{t\in(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)^\times}
  1_{tw=r}\,1_{t(w+d)=r+1}
 =\frac1{\varphi(M)}1_{w=rd}.
\]

Subtracting the congruences fixes t=d^−1, so this includes nonunit w and r. It recovers the desired raw class and specifies, rather than suppresses, the changed comparison class. Once the two free products are split into residue classes, finite Fourier shifts place the restricted endpoint energy within the source's larger fixed logarithmic denominator range. The progression restriction then remains outside the invariant core throughout the allocation step.

The ingredients of this paragraph are individually familiar: character orthogonality, an orbit calculation, Siegel–Walfisz, Cauchy, Fourier translation and a CRT sieve are not discoveries. Much of AP's extraction and all the probability identification are adaptations of D. The arithmetic reservoir-to-A+ argument was already supplied by the round-2 reviewer and is part of this project's existing work, not a second new theorem to count separately.

Nevertheless the exact combination supplies a joint cancellation assertion missing from the final accepted correlation contract. The superficially simpler one-endpoint averaging erases the class; insertion into the invariant core breaks a hypothesis; mixing before dyadic reassembly leaves a shared-label-dependent cutoff. The corrected construction resolves all three, and the comparison-energy argument is required beyond bare permission to insert physical multipliers. A theorem producing a small *residual* pairing is not automatically a theorem producing the required raw restricted correlation. These are concrete mathematical reasons to regard the bridge as a meaningful new argument within the corpus framework.

Its consequence matters quantitatively. The union of small-prime-avoiding classes has mass w(Y) ≍_a 1/log Y. AP supplies the **common explicit** factor −log(1−θ), while the global Kummer error is O_a(1/Y) plus o(1). Fixing sufficiently large Y closes that comparison. An unspecified positive proportion in each class, the old thin reservoir, or a single class of mass 1/φ(M_Y) would not close this budget. This upgrades a thin primitive-root set to a positive proportion for every admissible base, rather than merely producing another formulation of R004.

My judgment is therefore stronger than “correct new-to-project corollary,” but narrower than “a new general analytic method” or “verified first proof worldwide.” The result is a substantial plausible new deduction, supported by a specific nonautomatic bridge. The bounded search cannot measure how readily another expert could discover the same lift, nor rule out unpublished or unindexed work. Those uncertainties do not justify automatically treating every argument built from accepted lemmas as routine.
