## 1. Reflection and finite multicyclicity: PASS

The residue reflection gives `T_m(−1−x)=2^m−1−T_m(x)`. With the prescribed two central weights this implies `β_j(−1−x)=β_(−j−1)(x)` for every integer j. Haar measure is reflection invariant. Consequently the stated C is an antiunitary involution, and direct evaluation gives

`(CSC f)(x,j)=β_j(x) f(x,j+1)=(S* f)(x,j)`.

Antiunitarity preserves closed spans, so the equivalence of r-multicyclicity is valid for every positive integer r. It neither constructs a cyclic vector nor proves infinite multiplicity.

For finite-rank F, adjoining `ran F` to cyclic generators for A+F gives a closed A-invariant span L that is also A+F-invariant. This proves the m+r bound; replacing F by −F proves the converse. The equality of cyclic spans generated by `ran F` also follows by invariance under both operators. More explicitly, for *any* factorization F=UW, `N_A(W)=N_(A+F)(W)`: either kernel intersection is invariant under both operators because W vanishes on it. No hidden injectivity or surjectivity assumption on the factorization is necessary for this last identity.

## 2. Scalar comparison and supercyclicity: PASS

The uniform source bounds give `γ_j≤β_j` for j≥0 and `γ_j≥β_j` for j<0. Hence solving the proposed recurrence gives measurable entries `0<d_j≤1` and `d_(j+1)β_j=γ_j d_j`. Thus J is bounded and injective and satisfies `JS=B_∞J`. Its range is dense, for example because J is self-adjoint and has trivial kernel.

For finite-coordinate f and g, the logarithm of the largest forward product on the support of f equals

`−5n²−300n^(4/3)+O_f(n)`,

whereas the largest inverse product on the support of g has logarithm `5n²+O_g(n)`. Fixed endpoint changes in the fractional-power sum are `O(n^(1/3))` and therefore covered by O(n). Since only finitely many coordinate coefficients occur, taking L² norms adds harmless constants. This verifies the displayed product estimate with its coefficient 300.

For nonzero f,g set `|λ_n|=sqrt(||B_∞^(−n)g||/||B_∞^n f||)`. Each of the two errors in the transitivity construction is the square root of the norm product and tends to zero. Finite-coordinate vectors with arbitrary L² base coefficients are dense, and H is separable, so the countable-base Baire argument applies. The supercyclicity claim is sound even with infinite coordinate multiplicity. No converse transfer along J is justified or used.

## 3. Conditional rank-two similarity family: PASS

For N=uφ with φ(u)=0, direct multiplication gives `N²=0` and

`(I−N)S(I+N)−S=(Su)φ−u(φS+φ(Su)φ)`.

The absence of eigenvalues for S and S* follows from quasinilpotence and their injectivity. Therefore u,Su and φ,φS are independent, U has rank two, W is onto, and UW has rank exactly two. Similarity gives quasinilpotence. Together with the established logarithmic-square determinant-growth argument, this gives D≡1.

The stated controllability and observability simplifications are exact. If S is cyclic, reflection gives a cyclic functional φ₀. Since S has dense range, a cyclic u also satisfies `closure span{S^n u:n≥1}=H`. Unless φ₀(u)=0 already, some positive n has φ₀(S^n u)≠0. The selected nonzero λ makes `φ=φ₀(S^n−λI)` annihilate u. The invertible commuting operator `S^n−λI` preserves cyclicity of the representing functional. This closes the orthogonality step without any genericity assumption.

The transported coordinate tails are indeed proper closed ordinary invariant subspaces. Thus the conditional minimal realization would not prove an invariant-subspace counterexample.

## 4. Gaussian stability under determinant one: PASS

For c>0 and `||Q^n||≤A exp(−cn²)`, completion of the square bounds the power series by

`||A_z||≤C exp((log R)²/(4c))`, `|z|≤R`, `R≥1`.

The residual Gaussian sum is uniformly bounded over its real center; no extra power of log R is needed. Determinant one makes the finite matrix inverse its adjugate and makes the Woodbury expression an entire resolvent. For positive integer r, the adjugate degree is at most r−1. The resulting bound is precisely

`C' R^r exp((r+1)(log R)²/(4c))`.

Cauchy's estimate optimized at `log R=2c(n−r)/(r+1)` proves the proposed exponent for every n≥r, including R=1 when n=r. The rank-zero case should simply be interpreted as F=0 and the original estimate; the adjugate degree argument is only needed for r≥1. This is a harmless endpoint convention, not a gap. The rank-two substitution c=5/2 is correct.

## 5. Even-base estimates and the global V bound: PASS

An even x never has residue `T_m(x)=2^m−1`, so all noncentral positive increments are exactly 100 and all noncentral negative increments are exactly −100. Substitution into the actual forward and backward formulas gives the two displayed logarithmic bounds with the indices as written. In particular the backward boundary term uses `a_q^−`, not `a_(q−1)^−`.

The real maximum of `400t^(1/3)−45t` is `(800/3)sqrt(80/27)<460`. Thus the positive-index bound is at most `505−45j` and the negative-index bound at most `560−45|j|`. Normalization divides by L≥1. Together with b≤1 this proves the report's weaker uniform bound on every even base, with off-conull bases covered by b=0.

The exact n-step coefficient is `product_(r=0)^(n−1)b_(j−r)(x+r)`. Its even-base visits have coordinate spacing two. For m selected successive visits,

`min_j sum_(r=0)^(m−1)|j−2r|=floor(m²/2)`.

This proves (V) uniformly in x and j. To verify the final constant analytically, add n², use n≤2m+1, and obtain the upper bound `−18.5m²+564m+46`. Its maximum on the real line is `46+564²/74<5000`. The resulting `||V^n||≤exp(5000−n²)` holds for every integer n≥0, not only eventually.

## 6. T_a and its adjoint: PASS

With the specified measurable a in [1,2], `B_a=M_aV` is bounded, positive, injective, and infinite rank. Scalar multipliers commute with S, so B_a commutes with S. The orbit bound `||B_a^n||≤2^n||V^n||`, binomial theorem, and `k²+(n−k)²≥n²/2` give exactly the report's two Gaussian bounds. Hence T_a and T_a* have spectrum {0}.

The coordinate formulas, with the multiplier at its output base, are

`(T_a f)(x,l)=β_(l−1)(x)f(x,l−1)+a(x)b_(l+1)(x−1)f(x−1,l+1)`,

`(T_a* f)(x,l)=β_l(x)f(x,l+1)+a(x+1)b_l(x)f(x+1,l−1)`.

They yield exactly both recurrences in §7.3. In particular the adjoint recurrence has numerator `a(x)b_(j+1)(x−1)` and denominator `β_(j+1)(x−1)`; there is no misplaced base translation.

For completeness, explicit uniform versions of the asymptotic pair estimates are, for j≥0,

`log|r_(j+2)(x−1)r_j(x)| ≥ 25j−510−2log2−400[j^(1/3)+(j+2)^(1/3)]`,

and, for j≤−4,

`log|r*_(j+2)(x−1)r*_j(x)| ≤ −25|j|+695+2log2+800|j|^(1/3)`.

Only one even-base b estimate per pair is used; the other b≤1 has the correct favorable direction in each inequality. These establish (E1) and (E2).

For each parity, `z_k(y)=f(y−k,2k+p)` is a unitary disintegration because Haar measure is translation invariant. Countably many coordinate equations may be enforced on one conull set. The two-step multiplier in (E1) is eventually uniformly greater than two, forcing nonzero forward subsequences to grow. The two-step multiplier in (E2) is eventually uniformly less than one half, forcing growth toward negative infinity after reversing the recurrence. Since all coefficients are nonzero on the invariant conull set, any nonzero sequence has such nonzero subsequences. Fubini therefore proves both kernels are zero. The exclusion of nonzero finite-dimensional and proper finite-codimensional closed invariant subspaces follows exactly as stated over the complex field.

## 7. Invariant ideals and limits: PASS

For a T_a-invariant closed lattice ideal, positivity and solidity imply S- and B_a-invariance on positive elements. For arbitrary complex f, `|Sf|≤S|f|` and `|B_af|≤B_a|f|` extend this to all elements. Multiplication by 1/a then gives V-invariance, and ideals are invariant under all bounded scalar multipliers. Accepted transitivity forces the ideal to be zero or H.

The argument is valid for lattice ideals and does not apply to arbitrary closed linear invariant subspaces. An invertible lattice isomorphism would transport S's proper invariant ideals, so that narrower similarity class is excluded; arbitrary bounded similarity is not excluded. Empty point spectra likewise leave all possible invariant half-spaces untouched.
