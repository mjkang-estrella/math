This supplies the missing simultaneous separation, including mixed orders and all large degrees. ### 3.3 New examples and an actual limitation

For n=3, N weights 2b and N weights b with b=(V/(9N))^(1/3) have A1=8V/9 and A2=V/9. Use t=8/9 and any fixed eta<1/9. For sufficiently large N the entrance holds, so the **volume-boundary** class is nef. Its .99 multiple is precisely the I136 diffuse example. It lies outside both I136 sufficient methods, so this is an actual enlargement of their paid region, not a renamed convex combination. No divisibility of N is needed. More generally, two constant blocks with volume fractions in a fixed compact subinterval attain their combined volume bound for all sufficiently small individual weights, with one entrance for all lengths.

A tempting generalization assigns one slender fan cell to each weight. This has a real obstruction. A unimodular axis cell Delta(a,...,a,c) allocated exact capacity w^n has c=w^n/a^(n-1). Along the one-parameter subtorus in its last coordinate, every polynomial in its degree-k support restricts to a Laurent polynomial with exponent span at most kc+O(1). Its jet image on that subtorus has dimension at most kc+O(1)+1. Full ordinary floor(kw)-jets at one point would surject onto the corresponding one-dimensional jets, requiring c>=w. If 0<w<a, exact volume allocation gives c<w and this is impossible. Integral translations do not change exponent span, and unimodular changes merely change the tested subtorus. Thus fine exact-budget singleton fan cells cannot implement the target by this interpolation argument, however small w is. This does not obstruct adaptive cells of other shapes, grouped allocations, or W136.

The exact missing operation here is a simultaneous full-rank allocation with total capacity arbitrarily close to sum w_i^n whose local entrances remain bounded below by one delta(X,L), independent of the number and mass of cells. The two-cell construction pays determinant gluing and smoothing of finite evaluation matrices; it does not pay that uniform shape/packing operation.

## 4. Route B: algebraic normal-cone refinement on the same X

The relevant prior operation is explicit, not hidden in an equal-weight title. Roé–Ross, [0804.1662v1](https://arxiv.org/html/0804.1662v1), Theorem 1.6, allows unequal weighted blocks in projective-space bubbles. Its normal-cone construction, weighted proof and semicontinuity ending were read in full. For d=1 the paper credits Biran's earlier ample-divisor construction. We give the nef-divisor form needed here, including the actual base change and passage to very general distinct points; the following is not nominated as a new gluing theorem.

**Refinement operation.** Suppose the blowup of X at distinct coarse points has the nef real class pi*L-sum a_i E_i. For each i suppose the blowup of P^n at a finite distinct configuration has nef class H-sum_j b_ij F_ij, with b_ij>0. Then the refined class with coefficients a_i b_ij is nef at a very general tuple of the resulting length on X. Coarse points not refined can be kept as one-point blocks with b=1. The root and bubble classes may be at their nef boundaries.

### 4.1 Construction, smoothing and curve control

First take rational coefficients. Blow up X times a smooth pointed curve at the points (p_i,0). Its central fibre is the union of Bl_{p_i} X and bubbles P(T_{p_i}X plus C), each isomorphic to P^n, meeting the main component along its exceptional P^(n-1). Move the chosen bubble configurations by projective automorphisms so that all lie outside the attaching hyperplane. In local étale coordinates, the arcs `x=t v_ij` have exactly those points in the affine chart of the bubble. After a common étale base change at zero they give algebraic sections. Shrink the base so these sections are pairwise disjoint; sections in different bubbles are already distinct centrally. Blow up their union. This is a projective flat family; near these centres the family is smooth, and the central fibre has main component Bl_{p_i} X and bubble components Bl_{q_ij} P^n. Away from zero its fibres are blowups of the **original X**, with its unchanged complex structure, at distinct points. Nothing identifies an infinitely near central tuple with an ordinary general tuple without this family.

Let B_i denote the original bubble divisor on the total normal-cone space and G_ij the exceptional divisors of the section blowups. The real/rational divisor on the latter total space is

\[
 \mathcal D=\operatorname{pr}_X^*L-\sum_i a_i B_i-\sum_{ij}a_i b_{ij}G_{ij},
\]

where B_i means its total pullback under the section blowup. Its restriction to the main component is pi*L-sum a_i E_i. On bubble i it is a_i(H-sum b_ij F_ij), because -B_i restricts to H there. The section exceptional divisors are disjoint from the attaching hyperplanes; restrictions agree on every gluing locus. Therefore every curve of the central fibre has nonnegative intersection: every irreducible curve is contained in a component. This is actual intersection control, not volume counting.

To pass to nearby fibres, choose a relatively ample divisor A on this projective family. For every rational epsilon>0, D+epsilon A is ample on each central component and hence on the whole central fibre. After clearing denominators, openness of ampleness gives a neighborhood where it is fibrewise ample. At the geometric generic fibre all these conditions hold, so D is nef by closedness of the nef cone. Over C one can also choose a single nonzero parameter outside the countably many exceptional closed sets, obtaining an ordinary complex tuple with the desired nef class.

For a fixed rational vector, the universal blowup over U_r has countably many relative Hilbert schemes of curves. A family of curves with negative intersection has proper closed image in U_r, since a nef tuple just constructed exists; intersection degree is constant on each connected Hilbert component. Thus nefness holds at a very general tuple. This uses the curve criterion and properness; it does not assert that the nef locus is one Zariski open. For real inputs, approximate every coefficient a_i and b_ij from below by positive rational numbers. Coordinatewise decrease preserves the starting and bubble nef classes. Apply the rational construction on one common countable locus and take limits. This retains the real products and boundary coefficients. The argument also justifies finite repeated refinement without a hidden infinite smoothing assertion.

### 4.2 Exact finite-tree boundary classes

Let R>=r0(X,L). The stipulated source supplies the equal root coefficient a=(V/R)^(1/n). A bubble may split into q equal children with coefficient factor q^(-1/n) whenever the equal-weight maximality statement on P^n is available. In particular all q>=r0(P^n,O(1)) are allowed.

There is also an elementary exact supply q=m^n for every integer m>=1. The finite morphism `[x_0:...:x_n] -> [x_0^m:...:x_n^m]` has m^n distinct unramified points over a general torus point. Pulling back the globally generated ideal-twisted linear system of hyperplanes through that point gives the globally generated divisor mH-sum F on the blowup of its fibre. Indeed the pulled-back ideal is the reduced fibre ideal and becomes invertible on that blowup. Thus H-(1/m)sum F is nef. A projective automorphism puts this finite configuration away from the attaching hyperplane. This supplies exact small branching without invoking eventual thresholds on P^n.

Take any finite forest with R roots and allow such equal branching at any node; nodes may remain leaves. A leaf reached through branch numbers q1,...,qh has weight

\[
 w_{\rm leaf}=(V/R)^{1/n}(q_1\cdots q_h)^{-1/n}.       \tag{B139}
\]

Induction using the refinement operation proves nefness on the same fixed X at a very general distinct tuple. The nth-power sum is exactly V, since each split preserves its parent's nth-power sum. Arbitrarily deep **finite** trees are allowed, and coordinatewise smaller vectors are also nef. For each final length there are countably many such combinatorial patterns, so one common very-general locus can support all of them. No infinite collision or compactness of arbitrary degenerations is claimed.

For n=3 and N divisible by eight, take R=9N/8 roots with coefficient 2(V/(9N))^(1/3). Keep N roots unchanged and split each of the remaining N/8 roots into eight. The leaves are N weights 2b and N weights b with b=(V/(9N))^(1/3). For sufficiently large N the root threshold holds. Thus this second, geometric construction also crosses the I136 obstruction and attains its boundary, independently of the support partition.

### 4.3 Arithmetic obstruction to equal-branch refinement alone

Even grant **every** positive integer branch number and every equal root count. Every leaf's normalized volume w_i^n/V is the reciprocal of an integer R times a product of branch numbers. Consider odd N and N weights each of the two sizes

\[
 (2V/(3N))^{1/n},\qquad (V/(3N))^{1/n}.
\]

Their nth-power sum is V, and the maximum tends to zero. The first normalized volume 2/(3N) is not a reciprocal integer when N is odd. Any coordinatewise dominating full-volume tree vector would have to agree coordinatewise, since its total volume is also V; unused positive leaves only worsen that inequality. Thus these vectors are not produced by the downward closure of equal-root/equal-branch trees.

Taking a limit of those tree vectors at a **fixed positive target vector of fixed length** does not remove this obstruction: reciprocal integers in a neighborhood of a fixed positive number form a finite set. The same argument applies if finitely many unselected leaves are discarded; retained total volume tending to one forces equality in the limit. This is an obstruction to the stated tree method, not to general algebraic degenerations or W136. Route A actually handles this two-level profile when N is sufficiently large. The methods are complementary; neither individual obstruction is claimed against all combinations of all algebraic operations.

The remaining geometric operation would have to supply non-equal bubble classes with enough adjustable volume fractions, or a different central configuration whose smoothing admits arbitrary diffuse leaf capacities with no fixed loss. Roé–Ross's weighted theorem accepts such bubble classes as inputs; it does not produce them from their volume inequality. Repeated equal splitting and rational approximation alone cannot provide them at the required boundary.
