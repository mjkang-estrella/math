## 3. Construction I: work directly in the conditioned ensemble

Write `q_n=μ_n(A_n)` and `D_n=log(1/q_n)`. All auxiliary dimensions below are fixed unless explicitly stated otherwise.

### 3.1 Exact marked reversibility and its small rejection cost

**Lemma 1.** Let μ be uniform on the simple labelled d-regular graphs of girth greater than g, with nonempty support. Given G, independently sample k uniform oriented edges. Fix a transposition of two list positions. Perform its ordinary endpoint switch when the whole marked list is an induced matching and the new graph still has girth greater than g; otherwise leave the entire marked state unchanged. This map is an involution preserving the joint uniform graph/list law. The probability that the intended transposition cannot be performed is at most

`C_(d,k) (d−1)^g/n`.

**Proof.** Every graph/list atom has weight `1/(|supp μ|(nd)^k)`. On accepted states the endpoint transposition is its own inverse, the updated marked endpoints remain an induced matching, and both graphs lie in the conditioning set. On rejected states it is the identity. Therefore the map is an exact weight-preserving involution. This argument needs no irreducibility or mixing theorem.

For the estimate, the induced-matching failure is `O_(d,k)(1/n)` for every d-regular graph, by uniform endpoint marginals and the degree bound. For the two relevant edges ab and cd, additionally require that every endpoint of one edge have graph distance greater than g from every endpoint of the other. A radius-g ball has at most

`V_d(g)=1+d((d−1)^g−1)/(d−2)`

vertices. Conditional on the first edge, the second edge's endpoints have uniform marginals. The probability of violating separation is at most `4V_d(g)/n`.

Remove ab and cd. A new cycle using one new edge would give an old cross-path of length at most g−1, contrary to separation. A new cycle using both new edges decomposes into two old paths. Either one is between the two separated endpoint sets, or the paths respectively connect a to b and c to d without their removed edges. In the latter case each old path has length at least g, since G had no cycle of length at most g. Neither case permits a new cycle of length at most g. This proves the bound. ∎

For `g=g_n`, this is `O_(d,k)(n^(α−1))→0`, with no division by q_n. The sufficient separation condition need not itself be invariant under reversal; the **actual acceptance rule**, not that sufficient event, defines the involution. Products of a fixed number of transpositions have the same vanishing bound, since every accepted step preserves the joint law.

There is no claim that this constrained switch chain is irreducible. In fact the finite diagnostic finds all ten triangle-free cubic graphs on six labels to be absorbing for nontrivial girth-preserving two-edge switches. Stationarity and the asymptotic switch identity are the properties used here.

### 3.2 Entropy counting can pay the rarity directly

**Lemma 2 (conditional type count).** Let `N_(T,Q)(G)` be the number of colorings with source symmetrized star/edge histograms (T,Q), alphabet size M, and put `F(T,Q)=nH(T)−(nd/2)H(Q)`. Suppose the number of possible histograms is at most J and

`E_(μ_n) N_(T,Q)≤exp(F(T,Q)+a_n)`

for every histogram. For any event A of positive probability q and t>0, with conditional probability at least `1−e^(−t)`, simultaneously for all histograms,

`N_(T,Q)≤exp(F(T,Q)+a_n+log J+log(1/q)+t)`.

**Proof.** The conditional expectation is at most the unconditional expectation divided by q. Markov's inequality bounds each failure by `e^(−t)/J`; sum over at most J histograms. This is a proof for arbitrary A, not an assumption that conditioning preserves typical colorings. ∎

The accepted source type-count proof supplies `a_n+log J=O_d(M^(d+1)log n)` after the positive simplicity factor. Therefore if `M^(d+1)log n≤n^(1−κ)` for fixed κ>0, choose `t=n^(1−κ/2)` (decrease κ to at most one if needed). Under A_n the additional normalized entropy cost is

`D_n/n=Θ_d(n^(α−1)/log n)`.

It is polynomially small and can be absorbed by decreasing the source entropy exponent. The failure probability is `exp(−n^(1−κ/2))` under the conditioned law. Fixed-dimensional Gaussian smoothing and its quantization retain this budget **provided** the source spectral regularity/delocalization inputs hold there. This pays the counting cost; it does not prove those spectral inputs.

**(CS)** `there exist c_0,c_1>0, B<∞ and deterministic L_n=n^o(1) such that μ_n^g(Spec_n^c)→0`.

All constants may depend on fixed d,E,α, but not on n. An exact sufficient unconditional inequality is

`μ_n(A_n ∩ Spec_n^c)=o(exp(−Λ_(g_n)))`.  (CS')

Nor does A_n itself imply Spec_n. To make the geometry objection concrete, choose d-regular graphs H_m on m vertices with girth greater than `ceil(2α log_(d−1)m)`; MWW ensures existence for all sufficiently large admissible m. ## 4. Construction II: remove the short cycles and transport the spectral law

This construction starts with G∼μ_n, rather than trying to analyze μ_n^g directly. MWW's expectation formula (2.2) gives

`E Σ_(r≤g_n) r C_r(G)=O_d((d−1)^g_n)=O_d(n^α)`.

Its §3 removes a selected r-cycle by an r-switch using r remote edges; (3.2) and (3.3) count forward and backward choices, subject to explicit separation and cycle-overlap conditions. This suggests a sequence with at most `n^(α+o(1))` changed edges on a high-probability good-cycle event. That suggestion alone is not an algorithm with the right final law. Two independent bills remain.

### 4.1 Counting the levels is not transporting their uniform measures

For adjacent finite cycle-count levels X,Y, let `a(x,y)` count allowed marked removals, `f(x)=Σ_y a(x,y)` and `b(y)=Σ_x a(x,y)`. Starting uniformly on X and choosing uniformly among the removals available at the current x yields

`ν(y)=|X|^(−1) Σ_x a(x,y)/f(x)`.

Double-counting only gives `Σ_x f(x)=Σ_y b(y)`. Even precise average estimates on the two sides do not make ν uniform. The two-source/ two-target incidence array

`a=[[1,0],[1,1]]`

has f=(1,2), b=(2,1), but ν=(3/4,1/4). No unknown spectral behavior is needed for this obstruction.

MWW's average forward/backward counts prove a ratio of cardinalities; their conclusion is a distributional counting theorem, not a spectral transport kernel. A correct construction would need exact rejection weights, near-uniform pointwise degree control or another law correction, with cumulative error tending to zero over the whole descent. The needed statement is, for a specified removal kernel K_n,

**(TL)** `||μ_n K_n − μ_n^g||_TV→0`.

TL is not proved here. Merely adding inverse-fiber weights is not a solution, as I13's explicit variance example warns. The source uniform switch mixing bound does not become a conditioned mixing bound upon rejecting short cycles; the six-vertex diagnostic already separates reversibility from irreducibility. That finite obstruction does not disprove an asymptotic sampling theorem.

### 4.2 The microscopic spectral comparison is a separate issue

A single two-edge switch has rank at most two; a sequence of s switches has adjacency difference of rank at most 2s. The empirical distribution functions therefore differ by at most `2s/n`. This is enough for a macroscopic law when s=o(n). In microscopic coordinates `x=n(λ−E)`, the **unnormalized** counting functions can differ by 2s, and integration by parts gives for y>0

`|Tr(n(A−E)−z)^−1 − Tr(n(A'−E)−z)^−1| ≤ (2πs)/y`, `Im z=y`.

The factor follows from integrating `|(t−z)^−2|`, whose integral is π/y. Thus s=n^(α+o(1)) is not small at fixed microscopic height. This is a valid upper bound, not evidence that the actual difference must be large.

The loss occurs already for one genuine regular-graph switch. K3,3 has characteristic polynomial `t^6−9t^4`; switching edges 03 and 14 to 01 and 34 gives the triangular prism, with characteristic polynomial `t^6−9t^4−4t^3+12t²`. The zero multiplicity changes from four to two. The independent integer-matrix calculation reproduces both polynomials. This is a rank-only obstruction, not a counterexample to G, not a family of typical conditioned inputs, and not a claim that delocalization plus all source estimates are insufficient.

Even a delocalized first-order estimate is too large without cancellation: under source normalization the derivative of an eigenvalue for a unit-size edge perturbation is of order `n^(−1+o(1))`. Summing `n^α` adaptive removals permits a microscopic shift `n^(α+o(1))`. The source moments concern **uniform sampled edges**. Edges selected because they lie on the cycles being removed are biased by a rare graph event. Conditional moment errors cannot be divided by the tiny fraction of such edges without a new estimate, and endpoint distributions on those cycles are not supplied by the source's ordinary channel law.

A sufficient distributional comparison for a specified K_n, separate from TL, is

**(SC)** `E_(G∼μ_n,H∼K_n(G,·)) [L_h(H)−L_h(G)]→0` for every `h≥0` in `C_c(R)`,

where `L_h(G)=exp[−Σ_i h(nρ_d(E)(λ_i(G)−E))]`.

One could instead prove a stronger vanishing expectation of the absolute difference, but it is not necessary and is not assumed. To derive SC by telescoping needs cumulative drift and tail bounds for the actual cycle-biased operations, not a deterministic rank estimate. Neither TL nor SC is proved. A counterexample to deterministic surgery stability does not decide SC or G.

## 6. Complete technical percolation deduction P

Here is the promised proof without assuming that the perturbed graph has an invariant automorphism action.

### 6.1 Connection-operator bound

Let T=T_p. For each connection in the union choose a finite simple open path and split it at its j added edges. The j+1 base-percolation legs have disjoint witnesses. The added edges are distinct and independent of the base field. For prescribed ordered intermediate vertices, BK and independence bound the probability by the product of the corresponding T entries and b entries. Sum first over the intermediate vertices and then over j. Overcounting paths only increases this nonnegative sum. Thus, entrywise,

`T_union ≤ Σ_(j≥0) T(BT)^j`.

This uses finite-path events, so finite domain/edge exhaustions and monotone convergence justify it on the countable graph. Since `||BT||≤||B||M<1`, the series converges in operator norm and has norm at most `M/(1−M||B||)`. Nonnegative entrywise domination implies the same ℓ² bound for T_union by applying the dominating operator to |f|, first on finite supports. All norms use counting measure. This proves the quantitative assertion.

### 6.2 Infinite clusters without symmetry of the perturbation

In the base p-percolation, `θ_*:=inf_x P_p(|C_x|=∞)>0`. Indeed p>p_c gives a positive value somewhere; a fixed finite path and positive association give positivity at every vertex, and finitely many vertex orbits give a positive minimum. Adding edges preserves this lower bound.

Let N be the number of infinite clusters in the union. For a finite vertex set F, let S_F be the number of its vertices in infinite clusters, and `Q_F=Σ_(x,y∈F) 1_{x↔y}`. On `{N≤K}`, clustering the counted vertices and Cauchy–Schwarz give `S_F²≤K Q_F`. Hence, writing `M'=M/(1−M||B||)`,

`θ_*|F| ≤ E S_F ≤ √(K E Q_F)+|F|P(N>K) ≤ √(KM'|F|)+|F|P(N>K)`.

Let |F| tend to infinity. For every K, `P(N>K)≥θ_*`; then `P(N=∞)≥θ_*>0`. The event `{N=∞}` is invariant under changing finitely many of the independent base/added edge coordinates: each edge insertion or deletion changes the infinite-cluster count by at most a finite amount. It is consequently a tail event, and the product zero-one law gives probability one. The summable row hypothesis ensures local finiteness almost surely by the first Borel–Cantelli lemma, at every vertex by countability; there is no hidden quasi-transitivity assertion about the union. ∎

This extension is useful but **technical only**. The entire operator estimate is the inspected classical/source blue-edge expansion with an inhomogeneous kernel. The loss of invariance is resolved by a short count/Cauchy–Schwarz argument and the elementary tail law. No new critical exponent, quantitative threshold gap in graph parameters, or robustness to correlated additions follows. At p=p_c, an ℓ² bound alone does not imply critical nonpercolation; the positive uniform survival lower bound used above is absent. The proof should not be recycled for that claim.

## 7. Weighted sampling: exactly what remains beyond comparison

Let `m=(Σ_i d_i)/2` be the number of selected edges, constant on Ω_d, and suppose `|log w_e|≤β`. Write `W(G)=∏_(e∈G)w_e`, and P_0 for the source's uniform lazy switch chain. Then

`e^(−βm)≤W(G)≤e^(βm)`.

For an allowed off-diagonal move, detailed balance gives the Metropolis conductance

`π_w(G)P_w(G,H)=min(W(G),W(H))P_0(G,H)/Z`.

Let W_min and W_max be the extreme state weights and N=|Ω_d|. Comparing Dirichlet forms yields `D_w≥(N W_min/Z)D_0`; the variational expression `Var(f)=min_c E(f−c)²` yields `Var_w≤(N W_max/Z)Var_0`. Therefore

`gap(P_w)≥(W_min/W_max)gap(P_0)≥e^(−2βm)/[24n² binom(n,4)]`.

For one-state spaces mixing is immediate. Otherwise `log(1/π_min)≤log N+2βm≤binom(n,2)log 2+2βm`. These estimates give polynomial mixing when βm is at most a fixed multiple of log n. They give no polynomial bound for fixed β>0 and general m. This is a concrete comparison reduction and **technical only**, not a proof of W.

A source-based route to W would require, for weighted pair conditional expectations `E_a^w` in L²(π_w), a uniform nonzero-spectrum bound such as

## 3. A finite rejection-absorption lemma

Let `(X,S)` have a marked probability law `π(dx)P_x(ds)`. Let `T` be a measure-preserving involution, writing `x'` for the graph coordinate of `T(x,s)`. Let `A` be an event depending on `x`. Define on the conditioned marked space

\[
T_A(x,s)=\begin{cases}T(x,s)&x'\in A,\\(x,s)&x'\notin A.\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]

The restriction is an exact involution preserving `π(·|A)P_x`. Indeed its accepted subset consists of pairs with both graph coordinates in `A`; `T` interchanges them and preserves their original weights. Its complement consists of fixed points. This uses no resampling until success and no division by an acceptance rate.

Suppose `U⊂V` are graph events, `W` is another graph event, and

\[
\sup_{x\in A\cap U}P_x(x'\notin V)\le\varepsilon_1,\qquad
\sup_{x\in A\cap V}P_x(x'\notin W)\le\varepsilon_2,\qquad
\sup_{x\in A}P_x(x'\notin A)\le r<1.
\tag{4}
\]

Then

\[
\pi(U\setminus W\mid A)\le\frac{\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2}{1-r}.
\tag{5}
\]

**Proof.** Set `B=A∩(U\W)`. Split marked states over `B` into rejected states, accepted states whose images are outside `V`, and accepted states whose images are in `V`. The first mass is at most `rπ(B|A)`, pointwise in the graph. The second is at most `ε_1`. Map the third by `T_A`: its image starts in `A∩V` and its original `T` image lies in `B⊂W^c`, so its mass is at most `ε_2`. Measure preservation gives `(1−r)π(B|A)≤ε_1+ε_2`. ∎

In particular, neither `A` nor the rejection event is assumed independent of spectral data. The probabilities in (4) concern the original independent-mark experiment on each fixed input, including marks subsequently rejected by (3).

**Why normalizing admissible marks is different.** If graph `x` has acceptance probability `a(x)`, choosing marks from `P_x(·|accepted)` introduces `1/a(x)`. Reversal changes this to `1/a(x')`; equality of original marked weights does not cancel it. The accepted-move chain has stationary graph law proportional to `π(x)a(x)`, when the original proposal is reversible. An exact three-state/two-mark check below gives output `(3/4,1/4)` from a uniform two-state input after retry-until-success, with stationary law `(2/3,1/3)`. Thus that suggested repair is not used. Formula (5) retains the original fibers and their independence before rejection.
