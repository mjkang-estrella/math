                                           Removing Boundary Smoothness from the Hot-Spots Conclusion
                                                         in the Simple-Eigenvalue Case
                                                                                       Krzysztof Burdzy

                                                                                       October 7, 2026

                                                                                           Abstract
                                                    We give a reduction from a smooth-domain theorem excluding interior critical points of
                                                first positive Neumann eigenfunctions to a non-strict hot-spots conclusion on arbitrary bounded
                                                simply connected planar domains with compact Neumann resolvent and simple first positive
                                                eigenvalue. The main analytic step is spectral convergence under exhaustion by conformal
                                                images of concentric disks. A reflection extension, conformal invariance of Dirichlet energy, and
                                                compactness of the Sobolev embedding give a direct proof. A truncation argument justifies
                                                square integrability of the transferred extensions without any boundary regularity. The smooth-
                                                domain theorem is an external input; the approximation argument is proved here in full. The
                                                paper was generated by ChatGPT with prompts from the listed author.

6    Passage of the hot-spots conclusion to the limit
Proof of Theorem 2.1. Normalize u in L2 (Ω). By Proposition 5.1 and simplicity, there are rn ↑ 1
and first eigenfunctions un on Ωrn such that

                                  un −→ u locally uniformly in Ω.

    First consider a possible unbounded supremum. For every δ > 0, the set

                                    Kδ = {x ∈ Ω : dist(x, ∂Ω) ≥ δ}

is a compact subset of Ω, so u is bounded on Kδ . If supΩ u = +∞, its supremum in every boundary
layer must therefore also be +∞, and (3) follows immediately.
    We may consequently assume M = supΩ u < ∞. Suppose (3) fails. The boundary-layer suprema
have a limit by monotonicity. There are then δ > 0 and η > 0 such that

                       u(x) ≤ M − 3η      whenever x ∈ Ω and dist(x, ∂Ω) < δ.                       (15)

It follows that M is attained on Kδ , say at x0 .
    Set K = Kδ/2 . Then K ⋐ Ω, x0 ∈ int K, and every x ∈ ∂K has dist(x, ∂Ω) = δ/2. Thus (15)
implies
                                 u(x0 ) = M,      max u ≤ M − 3η.
                                                    ∂K

For sufficiently large n, K ⊂ Ωrn and uniform convergence on K gives

                               un (x0 ) > M − η,       max un < M − 2η.
                                                       ∂K

The continuous function un attains its maximum over K at a point yn ∈ int K. This is an interior
local maximum in Ωrn , so ∇un (yn ) = 0. Since Ωrn is smooth and simply connected, this contradicts
the smooth-domain input. Hence (3) holds. Applying the same argument to −u proves (4).

Proof of Corollary 2.2. Continuity on the compact set Ω implies uniform continuity. Consequently
the limiting supremum in shrinking interior boundary layers equals max∂Ω u: one inequality follows
by taking subsequential limits of points in the layers, and the other by approaching a maximizing
boundary point from within Ω. The analogous statement holds for the minimum. Apply Theo-
rem 2.1.


7    Scope of the argument
Remark 7.1 (Where simplicity is used). Neither Lemma 4.1 nor Proposition 5.1 requires simplicity.
Simplicity is used only to ensure that a prescribed first eigenfunction on Ω is, up to sign, the limit of
first eigenfunctions on the approximating domains. Without simplicity, the same argument proves
(3)–(4) for at least one nonzero element of the first eigenspace. It does not establish them for every
element of that eigenspace.
Remark 7.2 (Which smooth-domain assertion is needed). The maximum of un over K in the proof
need not be its global maximum over Ωrn . Thus the proof uses the absence of interior local extrema
supplied by the stronger smooth-domain input. The mere assertion that global extremal values
occur on the boundary of each smooth approximating domain would not, by itself, justify this step
under only local uniform convergence.


                                                   6
Remark 7.3 (Relation to reflected Brownian motion). Burdzy and Chen [1] establish weak con-
vergence of reflecting Brownian motions under increasing-domain approximation, subject to the
hypotheses and interpretations specified there. That result motivates the exhaustion approach.
The present proof instead works directly with Dirichlet energy and compactness and establishes the
needed spectral convergence without invoking a stochastic convergence theorem or inferring spectral
convergence from strong resolvent convergence alone.
