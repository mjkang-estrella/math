On the largest prime factors of p-1 and p+1
Genheng Zhao
Address: Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences,
55 Zhongguancun East Road, Haidian District, Beijing 100190, China
Email address: zhaogenheng@amss.ac.cn
Abstract.
We prove that each of the inequalities
P^{+}(p+1)>P^{+}(p-1) and P^{+}(p-1)>P^{+}(p+1) holds for a
positive proportion of primes. The argument combines a logarithmic
balance identity with a uniform Selberg sieve over a thin cofactor strip.
The same transfer mechanism applies to either shift and converts a
one-sided large-prime-factor reservoir into an unconditional ordering
result in both directions.
Key words and phrases:
largest prime factor, shifted primes, Selberg sieve, Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem
2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11N13, 11N35
1. Introduction
For n>1, let P^{+}(n) denote its largest prime factor, and set
P^{+}(1)=1. All logarithms are natural. We write
\displaystyle G(x)
\displaystyle=\#\{p\leq x:P^{+}(p+1)>P^{+}(p-1)\},
\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(x)
\displaystyle=\#\{p\leq x:P^{+}(p-1)>P^{+}(p+1)\}.
where p always denotes a prime.
The comparison of largest prime factors at neighbouring arguments has
a long history. The Erdős–Turán conjecture predicts that
P^{+}(n)<P^{+}(n+1) holds for a set of integers of asymptotic density
1/2. Erdős and Pomerance [ 3 ] established a positive
lower density in 1978. Subsequent quantitative refinements include
the published bound 0.2017 of Lü and Wang [ 6 ] .
In a different direction, Teräväinen
[ 9 , Theorem 1.16] proved that the logarithmic
density is 1/2, and Tao–Teräväinen
[ 8 , Corollary 1.16] obtained the ordinary density 1/2
at all scales outside an exceptional set of logarithmic density zero.
Neither statement asserts the ordinary density limit at every scale.
These results concern an unrestricted integer variable; restricting
the middle argument to primes introduces an additional distribution
problem and does not follow by restriction of an integer-density
estimate. For the prime-variable ordering considered here,
Wang proved a positive-proportion result under the
Elliott–Halberstam conjecture [ 11 ] ; his survey
[ 10 ] discusses the prime-variable problem.
Our purpose is to obtain unconditional positive-proportion conclusions in
both directions.
Theorem 1.1 .
Each ordering occurs for a positive proportion of primes. More precisely,
G(x)\gg\frac{x}{\log x},\qquad\widetilde{G}(x)\gg\frac{x}{\log x},
and hence
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{G(x)}{\pi(x)}>0,\qquad\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{G}(x)}{\pi(x)}>0.
Remark 1.2 .
The proof permits a numerical positive lower coefficient, but we do not
calculate it here since it would be very tiny. For instance, the elementary reservoir estimate in
Proposition 2.3 permits
\theta=11/20 and \delta=1/4; the remaining sieve and Euler-product
constants may be bounded coarsely. Our emphasis is the mechanism rather
than a small numerical value. No effective starting point is asserted,
since we use the classical Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem.
The key feature is a transfer from one-sided large prime factors to a
comparison of two adjacent shifts. The one-sided problem has its own
classical development: Goldfeld [ 4 ] obtained a
positive-proportion result for P^{+}(p+a)>x^{\theta}, for fixed nonzero
a, beyond \theta=1/2, with exponent 0.583\ldots;
Baker and Harman [ 2 ] later reached 0.677.
The formulation recorded by
Akbary–Hambrook [ 1 , Theorem 1.1] supplies, for any fixed
1/2<\theta\leq 0.677, a \delta>0 such that
\#\{p\leq x:P^{+}(p+1)>x^{\theta}\}\geq\delta\frac{x}{\log x}
for sufficiently large x. For the proof below, it is enough to obtain
the weaker choice \theta=11/20, \delta=1/4 directly, as we do in
Proposition 2.3 .
Such a reservoir alone does not decide
which of P^{+}(p-1) and P^{+}(p+1) is larger. We bridge that gap
by dividing the reverse orderings according to their distance from
the diagonal.
Write Q=P^{+}(p+1) and R=P^{+}(p-1). Within the reservoir
Q>x^{\theta}, a balance identity for the logarithms above
\sqrt{x+1} shows that a small number of desired
orderings forces a small number of reverse orderings with
R>x^{\eta}Q, for any fixed \eta>0.
This is an averaged constraint, not a pointwise relation between
Q and R. For the remaining reverse orderings, write
p+1=2aQ and p-1=2bR. The ratio restriction confines the
cofactors to the thin multiplicative strip
b<a\leq 2x^{\eta}b. A three-linear-form upper-bound sieve, followed
by harmonic summation over this strip, gives
\#\{p\leq x:Q(p)>x^{\theta},\ Q(p)<R(p)\leq x^{\eta}Q(p)\}\ll_{\theta}\eta\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}.
The small factor \eta is the decisive gain: summation over the
unrestricted cofactor box would lose it. The same argument works with
the roles of p+1 and p-1 reversed. In that case the reservoir is
R>x^{\theta}, the undesired near-diagonal ordering is
R<Q\leq x^{\eta}R, and the cofactor relation aQ-bR=1 forces
a<b; the identical uniform sieve then applies on the reflected thin
strip.
A second feature is that the sieve estimate is uniform in cofactors
which grow with x. We apply a classical Selberg upper-bound theorem
whose constants depend only on fixed local sieve conditions, and
verify those conditions and the remainder uniformly. Thus no
prime-tuple asymptotic or unverified dependence on growing coefficients
is needed. Combining the two controls and choosing a small fixed
\eta completes the transfer.
For comparison, Yang’s recent preprint [ 12 , Theorem 1.6]
treats P_{y}^{+}(n), the largest prime factor of n not exceeding y.
It gives a positive-proportion ordering result for shifted primes when
y=x^{\alpha} and 0<\alpha<17/32, which does not directly imply
the untruncated ordering considered here. Cofactor sieves also occur
in that work; the transfer above relies on their combination with
logarithmic balance and a thin strip.
Section 2 establishes the logarithmic estimates and
the far-from-diagonal bounds in both directions. Section 3
treats the two reflected near-diagonal sets by the same uniform sieve
and thin-strip summation. Section 4 closes both arguments. All external analytic
inputs are classical published results.
2. Logarithmic estimates and far reverse orderings
2.1. The balance identity
For primes p\leq x, write
Q(p)=P^{+}(p+1),\quad R(p)=P^{+}(p-1),\quad Z=\sqrt{x+1},
and define
L_{-}(p)=\mathbf{1}_{R(p)>Z}\log R(p),\qquad L_{+}(p)=\mathbf{1}_{Q(p)>Z}\log Q(p).
Lemma 2.1 .
As x\to\infty,
(1)
\sum_{p\leq x}\bigl(L_{-}(p)-L_{+}(p)\bigr)=o(x).
In fact the proof gives O(x\log\log x/\log x).
Proof.
Let \Lambda be the von Mangoldt function and let \pi(x;d,c) count
primes at most x congruent to c modulo d.
Since \log n=\sum_{d\mid n}\Lambda(d),
(2)
\sum_{3\leq p\leq x}\log\frac{p-1}{p+1}=\sum_{d\leq x+1}\Lambda(d)\bigl(\pi(x;d,1)-\pi(x;d,-1)\bigr)+\log 3.
This is exact for x\geq 3: the only correction is removal of p=2,
whose contribution is -\log 3. There is no discrepancy at d=x+1.
The left side is O(\log\log x) because
|\log((p-1)/(p+1))|\ll 1/p.
Apply the classical Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem in its maximal
\psi-form, as recorded in [ 1 , Introduction] , with saving
exponent A=3. Choose its associated fixed B, enlarge it to
B\geq 6, and put D=x^{1/2}/(\log x)^{B}.
Here \psi(t;d,c)=\sum_{n\leq t,\ n\equiv c\pmod{d}}\Lambda(n).
Thus
\sum_{d\leq D}\max_{(c,d)=1}\max_{2\leq t\leq x}\left|\psi(t;d,c)-\frac{t}{\varphi(d)}\right|\ll\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}.
Removing prime powers costs at most O(\sqrt{x}\log^{2}x) per modulus,
uniformly for t\leq x. Partial summation against 1/\log t
then gives the \pi-form below: its kernel has bounded total
variation on [2,x], and the summed prime-power and lower-endpoint
errors are O(D\sqrt{x}\log^{2}x+D)\ll x/\log^{4}x.
Consequently
\sum_{d\leq D}\max_{(c,d)=1}\left|\pi(x;d,c)-\frac{\operatorname{Li}(x)}{\varphi(d)}\right|\ll\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}.
The main terms in the two reduced classes 1,-1 cancel, giving
(3)
\left|\sum_{d\leq D}\Lambda(d)\bigl(\pi(x;d,1)-\pi(x;d,-1)\bigr)\right|\ll\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}.
For D<d\leq Z, Brun–Titchmarsh
[ 1 , Lemma 3.1(f), equation (3.11)] gives
\pi(x;d,\pm 1)\ll\frac{x}{\varphi(d)\log(x/d)}\ll\frac{x}{\varphi(d)\log x}.
The constants are uniform since d\leq\sqrt{x+1}<x and
\log(x/d)\geq(1/2+o(1))\log x.
We shall use
(4)
\sum_{n\leq y}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\varphi(n)}=\log y+O(1).
Indeed, the classical estimate
\sum_{n\leq y}\Lambda(n)/n=\log y+O(1) implies this because
\sum_{q}\sum_{k\geq 1}\log q\left(\frac{1}{\varphi(q^{k})}-\frac{1}{q^{k}}\right)=\sum_{q}\frac{\log q}{(q-1)^{2}}<\infty.
Subtracting ( 4 ) at Z and D therefore yields
(5)
\sum_{D<d\leq Z}\Lambda(d)\bigl(\pi(x;d,1)+\pi(x;d,-1)\bigr)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}\left(1+\log\frac{Z}{D}\right)\ll\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}.
An integer at most x+1 has at most one prime divisor greater than Z,
and that divisor cannot occur to the second power. Thus the terms d=q>Z
in ( 2 ) give exactly L_{-}(p)-L_{+}(p).
The remaining terms d=q^{k}>Z, k\geq 2, are bounded in absolute value
by a prime-power error E_{\rm pp}. Each class modulo q^{k} contains
at most x/q^{k}+1 positive integers at most x, so
E_{\rm pp}\ll\sum_{2\leq k\leq K}\ \sum_{Z<q^{k}\leq x+1}\log q\left(\frac{x}{q^{k}}+1\right),\qquad K=\left\lfloor\frac{\log(x+1)}{\log 2}\right\rfloor.
For k=2, Chebyshev’s bound \sum_{q\leq t}\log q\ll t and partial
summation give
x\sum_{q>(x+1)^{1/4}}\frac{\log q}{q^{2}}+\sum_{q\leq(x+1)^{1/2}}\log q\ll x^{3/4}+x^{1/2}.
For k\geq 3, put y_{k}=(x+1)^{1/(2k)}. Uniformly for real y\geq 1
and integers k\geq 2,
(6)
\sum_{n>y}\frac{\log n}{n^{k}}\ll y^{1-k}\log(2y).
For completeness, split the integers into 2^{j}y<n\leq 2^{j+1}y.
The contribution of the jth block is at most an absolute constant times
y^{1-k}2^{-j(k-1)}(\log(2y)+(j+1)\log 2); summing the geometric
series proves ( 6 ) with a constant independent of k.
It follows that the kth contribution is
\ll x^{1/2+1/(2k)}\log x+(x+1)^{1/k}\ll x^{2/3}\log x+x^{1/3}.
There are O(\log x) such k, whence
(7)
E_{\rm pp}\ll x^{3/4}+x^{2/3}\log^{2}x=o(x).
Combining ( 2 ), ( 3 ), ( 5 )
and ( 7 ) proves the lemma. Restoring p=2 costs only O(1).
∎
2.2. One-sided reservoirs for both shifts
Lemma 2.2 .
For either sign s\in\{-1,1\}, as x\to\infty,
(8)
\sum_{p\leq x}\mathbf{1}_{P^{+}(p+s)>Z}\log P^{+}(p+s)=\frac{x}{2}+O\!\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right).
For s=-1, the term p=2 is understood as zero.
Proof.
The proof of Lemma 2.1 also estimates each small-divisor
sum separately. For D=x^{1/2}/\log^{B}x, its small-modulus part is
\displaystyle\sum_{d\leq D}\Lambda(d)\pi(x;d,-s)
\displaystyle=\operatorname{Li}(x)\sum_{d\leq D}\frac{\Lambda(d)}{\varphi(d)}+O(x/\log^{2}x)
\displaystyle=\operatorname{Li}(x)(\log D+O(1))+O(x/\log^{2}x)
\displaystyle=\frac{x}{2}+O\!\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right).
The part D<d\leq Z is bounded by ( 5 ).
The quantitative prime number theorem in the form
\vartheta(t)=\sum_{p\leq t}\log p=t+O(t/\log t)
[ 1 , Lemma 3.1(d)] gives
\sum_{3\leq p\leq x}\log(p+s)=x+O(x/\log x): replacing
\log(p+s) by \log p costs O(\log\log x).
The prime-power discrepancy above Z is bounded by ( 7 ).
Subtracting the small-divisor sum from the total logarithmic mass
therefore gives ( 8 ). The p=2 endpoint costs O(1).
∎
Proposition 2.3 .
For 1/2<\beta<1, put
\Delta(\beta)=\frac{1}{2}+2\log(2-2\beta).
Then, for either sign s\in\{-1,1\},
(9)
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log x}{x}\#\{p\leq x:P^{+}(p+s)>x^{\beta}\}\geq\Delta(\beta).
In particular this coefficient is positive for
\beta<1-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-1/4}.
Proof.
Fix s\in\{-1,1\} and \beta>1/2. Eventually x^{\beta}>Z.
At most one prime factor of p+s exceeds Z, so, apart from the
harmless endpoint p=2 when s=-1, the logarithmic mass removed
between Z and x^{\beta} is exactly
B_{s}(x;\beta)=\sum_{Z<q\leq x^{\beta}}(\log q)\pi(x;q,-s).
Brun–Titchmarsh [ 1 , Lemma 3.1(f), equation (3.11)]
bounds this uniformly in the sign by
2x\sum_{Z<q\leq x^{\beta}}\frac{\log q}{(q-1)\log(x/q)}.
Replacing q-1 by q changes this bound by o_{\beta}(x),
since \log(x/q)\geq(1-\beta)\log x and
\sum_{q>Z}\log q/q^{2}\ll 1/Z.
Use the Mertens estimate A(t)=\sum_{q\leq t}\log q/q=\log t+O(1).
Partial summation against 1/\log(x/t) gives
\displaystyle\sum_{Z<q\leq x^{\beta}}\frac{\log q}{q\log(x/q)}
\displaystyle=\int_{Z}^{x^{\beta}}\frac{dt}{t\log(x/t)}+O_{\beta}(1/\log x)
\displaystyle=\log\frac{\log(x/Z)}{(1-\beta)\log x}+O_{\beta}(1/\log x)
\displaystyle=-\log(2-2\beta)+o_{\beta}(1).
The partial summation error follows because the endpoint values and
the total variation of 1/\log(x/t) on this interval are
O_{\beta}(1/\log x).
Lemma 2.2 now implies, uniformly for the fixed sign s,
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
P^{+}(p+s)>x^{\beta}\end{subarray}}\log P^{+}(p+s)\geq\Delta(\beta)x+o_{\beta}(x).
Each logarithm is at most \log(x+1), proving
( 9 ). If \Delta(\beta)\leq 0, the resulting
lower bound is simply vacuous.
∎
Remark 2.4 .
See [ 1 , Theorem 2.5] for the same expression \Delta in the
quantitative minus-shift setting. The argument above treats both signs
directly using standard published inputs: Bombieri–Vinogradov,
Brun–Titchmarsh, Mertens’ theorem, and the prime number theorem.
An explicit density coefficient does not give an effective starting
point, since the BV estimate used in Lemma 2.2 is ineffective.
2.3. Orderings far from the diagonal
Fix 1/2<\theta<1 and define
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)=\{p\leq x:Q(p)>x^{\theta}\}.
For fixed \eta>0, put
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}(x)=\{p\in\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x):R(p)>x^{\eta}Q(p)\}.
Lemma 2.5 .
For every fixed \eta>0,
(10)
\#\mathcal{F}_{\eta}(x)\leq\frac{G(x)}{\eta}+o_{\eta}\!\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).
Proof.
For sufficiently large x, x^{\theta}>Z. Every prime in
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}(x) contributes to the balance sum an amount
\log\frac{R(p)}{Q(p)}>\eta\log x.
The truncated logarithm is nondecreasing for t\geq 1.
Hence primes with Q(p)\leq R(p) contribute nonnegatively, and only
primes counted by G(x) can contribute negatively, by at most
\log(x+1) each. Therefore
\eta\log x\,\#\mathcal{F}_{\eta}(x)\leq G(x)\log(x+1)+o(x).
After division, replacing \log(x+1)/\log x by 1 costs
O(1/\log x), even using the trivial G(x)\leq x.
This proves ( 10 ); the error is for fixed \eta, as required.
∎
For the opposite ordering define
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\theta}(x)=\{p\leq x:R(p)>x^{\theta}\},\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\eta}(x)=\{p\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\theta}(x):Q(p)>x^{\eta}R(p)\}.
Lemma 2.6 .
For every fixed \eta>0,
(11)
\#\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\eta}(x)\leq\frac{\widetilde{G}(x)}{\eta}+o_{\eta}\!\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).
Proof.
Apply the balance identity with the sign reversed. Every prime in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\eta}(x) contributes to
L_{+}(p)-L_{-}(p) an amount
\log\frac{Q(p)}{R(p)}>\eta\log x.
Primes with R(p)\leq Q(p) contribute nonnegatively to this reversed
balance sum, while only primes counted by \widetilde{G}(x) can
contribute negatively, and each such contribution has absolute value
at most \log(x+1). Hence
\eta\log x\,\#\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\eta}(x)\leq\widetilde{G}(x)\log(x+1)+o(x),
which gives ( 11 ) exactly as in
Lemma 2.5 .
∎
3. A uniform sieve over a thin cofactor strip
3.1. Cofactors and local densities
Put
\mathcal{N}_{\eta}(x)=\{p\in\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x):Q(p)<R(p)\leq x^{\eta}Q(p)\}.
For later use in the opposite direction also put
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\eta}(x)=\{p\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\theta}(x):R(p)<Q(p)\leq x^{\eta}R(p)\}.
For p\in\mathcal{N}_{\eta}(x), write q=Q(p), r=R(p). Since
q,r>x^{\theta}>3, these factors are odd and we can write uniquely
(12)
p+1=2aq,\qquad p-1=2br,\qquad aq-br=1.
Consequently (a,b)=1, and for x\geq 1 the explicit bounds are
(13)
1\leq a,b\leq x^{1-\theta}.
Also
\frac{a}{b}=\frac{r}{q}\frac{p+1}{p-1},\qquad p+1\geq q>x^{\theta}.
Thus, for sufficiently large x depending only on \theta,
(14)
b<a\leq 2x^{\eta}b.
One of p-1,p+1 is divisible by 4 and one by 3. Since q,r>3,
the remaining factors 2,3 occur in a or b, so 6\mid ab.
Fix a coprime pair satisfying 6\mid ab and ( 13 ).
The congruences
n\equiv-1\pmod{2a},\qquad n\equiv 1\pmod{2b}
are compatible modulo their common factor 2, and have a unique solution
modulo 2ab. Choose 1\leq n_{0}<2ab, and set
(15)
\displaystyle L_{1}(m)
\displaystyle=bm+B_{1},
\displaystyle B_{1}
\displaystyle=\frac{n_{0}+1}{2a},
\displaystyle L_{2}(m)
\displaystyle=2abm+B_{2},
\displaystyle B_{2}
\displaystyle=n_{0},
\displaystyle L_{3}(m)
\displaystyle=am+B_{3},
\displaystyle B_{3}
\displaystyle=\frac{n_{0}-1}{2b}.
For triples from ( 12 ) these are q,p,r respectively.
All intercepts are nonnegative integers, and leading coefficients are
positive. With A_{1}=b,A_{2}=2ab,A_{3}=a, the determinants are
(16)
A_{1}B_{2}-A_{2}B_{1}=-b,\quad A_{1}B_{3}-A_{3}B_{1}=-1,\quad A_{2}B_{3}-A_{3}B_{2}=-a.
Define
(17)
H(n)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell>3\\
\ell\mid n\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-3}.
Lemma 3.1 (Primitive forms and local densities) .
The three forms are primitive and pairwise nonproportional.
For the number \rho(\ell) of roots of L_{1}L_{2}L_{3} modulo \ell,
(18)
\rho(2)=\rho(3)=1,\qquad\rho(\ell)=\begin{cases}3,&\ell>3,\ \ell\nmid ab,\\
1,&\ell>3,\ \ell\mid ab.\end{cases}
In particular 0<\rho(\ell)<\ell at every prime, and
(19)
\mathfrak{S}(a,b)=\prod_{\ell}(1-\rho(\ell)/\ell)(1-1/\ell)^{-3}=C_{3}H(ab),
where C_{3}=9\prod_{\ell>3}(1-3/\ell)(1-1/\ell)^{-3} is an
absolutely convergent positive constant.
Proof.
The defining congruences give (2ab,n_{0})=1. If an odd prime divided
both b and B_{1}, then n_{0}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell} and
n_{0}+1\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}, an impossibility. If 2\mid b,
a is odd and n_{0}\equiv 1\pmod{4}, so (n_{0}+1)/(2a) is odd.
Thus (b,B_{1})=1. Similarly (a,B_{3})=1: when 2\mid a,
b is odd and n_{0}\equiv-1\pmod{4}, so B_{3} is odd.
The nonzero determinants establish nonproportionality.
If \ell>3 and \ell\nmid ab, all three coefficients are invertible
and ( 16 ) gives three distinct roots. If an odd \ell\mid a,
then \ell\nmid b, L_{2}\equiv-1 and
L_{3}\equiv-b^{-1}\pmod{\ell}, while L_{1} has exactly one root.
If an odd \ell\mid b, the symmetric calculation gives L_{2}\equiv 1
and L_{1}\equiv a^{-1}\pmod{\ell}, with only L_{3} having a root.
Since exactly one of a,b is divisible by 3, this also proves
\rho(3)=1.
If 2\mid a, L_{2} and L_{3} are always odd and L_{1} has one root
modulo 2. If 2\mid b, L_{2} and L_{1} are always odd and L_{3}
has one root. This proves ( 18 ).
The factors at 2,3 in ( 19 ) have product 9;
at \ell>3 dividing ab the ratio to the generic factor is
(\ell-1)/(\ell-3). At the remaining primes the generic factor is
1+O(\ell^{-2}), proving convergence.
∎
3.2. Uniform Selberg sieve
We use Richert’s classical upper-bound sieve in the form of
[ 7 , Theorem 11.3, p. 135] ; see also
[ 5 , Chapters 4–5] . The point of using this formulation is that
its implied constant depends only on the fixed sieve parameters occurring
in (\Omega_{1}) and (\Omega_{2}(\kappa)) (and on the fixed exponent in the
condition z\leq X^{A}), not on the coefficients of the linear forms.
We record the verification in our family because a and b grow with x.
For real z>2 put P(z)=\prod_{\ell<z}\ell and
F(m)=L_{1}(m)L_{2}(m)L_{3}(m). Extend \rho multiplicatively to
squarefree integers, with \rho(1)=1. For every squarefree d, the
Chinese remainder theorem gives
(20)
A_{d}:=\#\{1\leq m\leq M:d\mid F(m)\}=M\frac{\rho(d)}{d}+r_{d},\qquad|r_{d}|\leq\rho(d).
Indeed, there are exactly \rho(d) relevant residue classes modulo d,
and each contains M/d+O(1) integers in [1,M]. Thus Richert’s
remainder condition (R), namely |r_{d}|\leq\omega(d) for squarefree d,
holds with his local-density function \omega(d)=\rho(d).
Lemma 3.2 (Uniform sifted-set bound) .
For every fixed A>0, uniformly for M\geq 2 and
3<z\leq M^{A},
(21)
\#\{1\leq m\leq M:(F(m),P(z))=1\}\ll_{A}H(ab)\frac{M}{\log^{3}z},
where the implied constant is independent of a,b,M,z.
Proof.
We apply Richert’s Theorem 11.3 with X=M and
\omega(\ell)=\rho(\ell). Let us check the hypotheses with constants
independent of a and b.
Richert’s condition (\Omega_{1}) is
0\leq\frac{\omega(\ell)}{\ell}\leq 1-\frac{1}{A_{1}}.
By ( 18 ),
0\leq\frac{\rho(\ell)}{\ell}\leq\frac{3}{5}=1-\frac{1}{5/2},
so (\Omega_{1}) holds with the absolute choice A_{1}=5/2.
His one-sided dimension condition (\Omega_{2}(3)) asks for an absolute
A_{2} such that, for 2\leq u\leq v,
\sum_{u\leq\ell<v}\frac{\rho(\ell)\log\ell}{\ell}\leq 3\log\frac{v}{u}+A_{2}.
Since \rho(\ell)\leq 3, this follows immediately from Mertens’ estimate
\sum_{u\leq\ell<v}\frac{\log\ell}{\ell}=\log\frac{v}{u}+O(1),
with an absolute A_{2}. Finally, ( 20 ) is precisely condition
(R).
Richert’s theorem therefore gives, for z\leq M^{A},
\#\{1\leq m\leq M:(F(m),P(z))=1\}\ll_{A}MV(z),
where
V(z)=\prod_{\ell<z}\left(1-\frac{\rho(\ell)}{\ell}\right).
and the implied constant depends only on A,A_{1},A_{2} and the fixed
sieve dimension 3. In particular, it is uniform in the growing
coefficients a,b.
By ( 18 ), for z>3,
V(z)=\frac{1}{3}\prod_{3<\ell<z}(1-3/\ell)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}3<\ell<z\\
\ell\mid ab\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-3}\ll\frac{H(ab)}{\log^{3}z}.
Here Mertens’ product formula, together with the absolutely convergent
generic Euler product in ( 19 ), gives the first factor
\ll(\log z)^{-3}; extending the second product to all prime divisors
of ab only increases it. This proves ( 21 ).
∎
Lemma 3.3 .
Fix 1/2<\theta<1. Uniformly for coprime a,b satisfying
6\mid ab and ( 13 ), let N_{\theta}(a,b;x) count integers
0\leq m\leq M, where
(22)
M=\left\lfloor\frac{x-n_{0}}{2ab}\right\rfloor,
for which all three values in ( 15 ) are prime and
L_{1}(m),L_{3}(m)>x^{\theta}. Then
(23)
N_{\theta}(a,b;x)\ll_{\theta}H(ab)\frac{x}{ab\log^{3}x}.
Proof.
Since n_{0}<2ab and ab\leq x^{2-2\theta},
(24)
M=\frac{x}{2ab}+O(1)\asymp\frac{x}{ab},\qquad M\gg x^{2\theta-1},\qquad\log M\asymp_{\theta}\log x.
All constants here are uniform in a,b once x is sufficiently large
in terms of the fixed \theta. In particular M\to\infty uniformly.
The single endpoint m=0 costs at most 1.
Put
\kappa=\frac{2\theta-1}{8},\qquad z=x^{\kappa}.
Then 0<\kappa<\theta. Moreover ( 24 ) gives, uniformly
in a,b,
z^{4}=x^{(2\theta-1)/2}\leq M
for all sufficiently large x depending only on \theta; hence
z\leq M^{1/4}. Thus Lemma 3.2 applies with the fixed
choice A=1/4.
For a triple counted by N_{\theta}(a,b;x) with m\geq 1,
L_{1},L_{3}>x^{\theta}, while from ( 15 )
L_{2}=2aL_{1}-1>x^{\theta}-1>z
for sufficiently large x. Since all three values are prime and exceed
z, their product is coprime to P(z). Lemma 3.2 therefore
gives
N_{\theta}(a,b;x)\ll_{\theta}1+H(ab)\frac{M}{\log^{3}z}\ll_{\theta}1+H(ab)\frac{x}{ab\log^{3}x}.
Finally, H(ab)\geq 1 and ( 24 ) imply
x/(ab\log^{3}x)\to\infty uniformly, so the endpoint term is absorbed.
This proves ( 23 ).
∎
3.3. Harmonic summation over the strip
Lemma 3.4 (Mean and interval harmonic bounds) .
There is an absolute constant C_{H} such that, for T\geq 1,
(25)
A_{H}(T):=\sum_{n\leq T}H(n)\leq C_{H}T,\qquad\sum_{n\leq T}\frac{H(n)}{n}\leq C_{H}(1+\log T).
For all real 1\leq U<V,
(26)
\sum_{U<n\leq V}\frac{H(n)}{n}\leq C_{H}\left(1+\log\frac{V}{U}\right).
Proof.
Let c be multiplicative, with c(1)=1, c(2)=c(3)=0,
c(\ell)=2/(\ell-3) for primes \ell>3, and
c(\ell^{k})=0 for every prime \ell and k\geq 2.
Expanding the local factors gives H(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}c(d).
The coefficients are nonnegative, so
A_{H}(T)=\sum_{d\leq T}c(d)\left\lfloor\frac{T}{d}\right\rfloor\leq T\sum_{d\geq 1}\frac{c(d)}{d}=C_{H}T,
where
C_{H}=\prod_{\ell>3}\left(1+\frac{2}{\ell(\ell-3)}\right)<\infty.
For real endpoints partial summation is exactly
\sum_{U<n\leq V}\frac{H(n)}{n}=\frac{A_{H}(V)}{V}-\frac{A_{H}(U)}{U}+\int_{U}^{V}\frac{A_{H}(t)}{t^{2}}\,dt.
Discard the nonpositive term and use A_{H}(t)\leq C_{H}t to obtain
( 26 ). For the full harmonic sum, add H(1)=1=A_{H}(1)
to the exact identity with U=1. The negative boundary term cancels,
leaving A_{H}(T)/T+\int_{1}^{T}A_{H}(t)t^{-2}\,dt\leq C_{H}(1+\log T).
This includes T=1 and does not enlarge C_{H}.
∎
Proposition 3.5 .
For fixed 1/2<\theta<1 and every \eta>0,
(27)
\#\mathcal{N}_{\eta}(x)\ll_{\theta}\eta\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}.
The implied constant and a sufficiently large threshold for x are
independent of \eta.
Proof.
Each p\in\mathcal{N}_{\eta}(x) determines a unique (a,b) satisfying
( 13 ), ( 14 ), (a,b)=1, 6\mid ab.
Lemma 3.3 , applied before discarding the restrictions, gives
\#\mathcal{N}_{\eta}(x)\ll_{\theta}\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<a\leq 2x^{\eta}b\\
a,b\leq x^{1-\theta}\\
(a,b)=1,\ 6\mid ab\end{subarray}}\frac{H(ab)}{ab}.
For every positive integer pair, H(ab)\leq H(a)H(b); shared prime
factors can only increase the right side. With T=x^{1-\theta},
we can now discard the arithmetic restrictions and the upper bound on
the inner variable a. Lemma 3.4 gives
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b<a\leq 2x^{\eta}b\\
a,b\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{H(ab)}{ab}
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{b\leq T}\frac{H(b)}{b}\sum_{b<a\leq 2x^{\eta}b}\frac{H(a)}{a}
\displaystyle\leq C_{H}(1+\log 2+\eta\log x)\sum_{b\leq T}\frac{H(b)}{b}
\displaystyle\ll\log x(1+\eta\log x).
All constants here are absolute (apart from the fixed \theta in
the sieve). Multiplication by x/\log^{3}x proves ( 27 ).
∎
Proposition 3.6 (Reflected thin-strip bound) .
For fixed 1/2<\theta<1 and every \eta>0,
(28)
\#\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\eta}(x)\ll_{\theta}\eta\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x}{\log^{2}x}.
The implied constant and a sufficiently large threshold for x are
independent of \eta.
Proof.
Let p\in\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\eta}(x) and write
q=Q(p), r=R(p) and
p+1=2aq,\qquad p-1=2br,\qquad aq-br=1.
Here q>r>x^{\theta}, so the box bounds ( 13 ), coprimality
(a,b)=1, and 6\mid ab are unchanged. Moreover a<b.
Indeed, if a\geq b, then, since q and r are distinct odd primes,
1=aq-br=a(q-r)+(a-b)r\geq q-r\geq 2,
a contradiction. Also
\frac{b}{a}=\frac{q}{r}\frac{p-1}{p+1}<\frac{q}{r}\leq x^{\eta},
so the cofactors lie in the reflected strip
(29)
a<b\leq x^{\eta}a.
For each such pair the same linear forms ( 15 ) have prime
values q,p,r, and Lemma 3.3 applies without any change.
Consequently, after discarding (a,b)=1 and 6\mid ab and using
H(ab)\leq H(a)H(b), with T=x^{1-\theta} we have
\#\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\eta}(x)\ll_{\theta}\frac{x}{\log^{3}x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<b\leq x^{\eta}a\\
a,b\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{H(a)H(b)}{ab}.
Using Lemma 3.4 first in the b-variable gives
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a<b\leq x^{\eta}a\\
a,b\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{H(a)H(b)}{ab}
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a\leq T}\frac{H(a)}{a}\sum_{a<b\leq x^{\eta}a}\frac{H(b)}{b}
\displaystyle\leq C_{H}(1+\eta\log x)\sum_{a\leq T}\frac{H(a)}{a}
\displaystyle\ll\log x(1+\eta\log x).
Multiplication by x/\log^{3}x proves ( 28 ).
∎
4. Completion of the argument
Proposition 4.1 (Square-root-breaking criterion) .
Fix 1/2<\theta<1. Suppose there is \delta>0 such that,
for all sufficiently large x,
(30)
\#\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)\geq\delta\frac{x}{\log x}.
Then G(x)\gg_{\theta,\delta}x/\log x.
Proof.
For p>3, equality Q(p)=R(p) is impossible. A common largest
prime factor would divide 2, making both p-1,p+1 powers of 2.
If p-1=2^{u} and p+1=2^{v}, then
2^{u}(2^{v-u}-1)=2, forcing u=1,v=2,p=3.
Thus, up to a bounded endpoint exception,
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)\subseteq\{p\leq x:Q(p)>R(p)\}\cup\mathcal{F}_{\eta}(x)\cup\mathcal{N}_{\eta}(x).
By ( 30 ), Lemma 2.5 and
Proposition 3.5 , there is C_{\theta}>0, independent of
\eta, such that for every fixed \eta>0,
(31)
\delta\frac{x}{\log x}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)G(x)+C_{\theta}\eta\frac{x}{\log x}+o_{\theta,\eta}\!\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).
Fix \eta>0 with C_{\theta}\eta<\delta/2.
Then take x sufficiently large that the last error has absolute value
at most \delta x/(4\log x). It follows explicitly that
G(x)\geq\frac{\delta\eta}{4(1+\eta)}\frac{x}{\log x}.
The order of choices is \theta,\delta, then C_{\theta}, then a fixed
\eta, and finally x\to\infty; no error estimate uniform as
\eta\to 0 is used.
∎
Proposition 4.2 (Reverse square-root-breaking criterion) .
Fix 1/2<\theta<1. Suppose there is \delta>0 such that,
for all sufficiently large x,
(32)
\#\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\theta}(x)\geq\delta\frac{x}{\log x}.
Then
\widetilde{G}(x)\gg_{\theta,\delta}\frac{x}{\log x}.
Proof.
For p>3 equality Q(p)=R(p) is impossible, as observed in the
proof of Proposition 4.1 . Hence, up to a bounded
endpoint exception,
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\theta}(x)\subseteq\{p\leq x:R(p)>Q(p)\}\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\eta}(x)\cup\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\eta}(x).
Using Lemma 2.6 and
Proposition 3.6 , we obtain, for every fixed
\eta>0,
\delta\frac{x}{\log x}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\widetilde{G}(x)+C^{\prime}_{\theta}\eta\frac{x}{\log x}+o_{\theta,\eta}\!\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right),
where C^{\prime}_{\theta} is independent of \eta. Choosing \eta and then
x in the same order as in Proposition 4.1 proves the
claim.
∎
Proof of Theorem 1.1 .
Use Proposition 2.3 with
\theta=11/20. The bound -\log(9/10)\leq 1/9 gives
\Delta(11/20)>1/4. For s=1 the strict liminf margin yields
( 30 ) with \delta=1/4 for all sufficiently large
x, so Proposition 4.1 gives
G(x)\gg x/\log x.
For s=-1 the same proposition gives
( 32 ), again with \delta=1/4 for all
sufficiently large x. Proposition 4.2
therefore gives
\widetilde{G}(x)\gg x/\log x.
The prime number theorem converts both estimates into the asserted
positive lower proportions.
∎
