                                                                                 Abstract
                                                Let a and f be coprime positive integers. Let g be an integer. Under
                                                the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) it follows by a result of H.W.
                                                Lenstra that the set of primes p such that p ≡ a(mod f ) and g is a primitive
                                                root modulo p has a natural density. In this note this density is explicitly
                                                evaluated with an Euler product as result.


                                          1     Introduction
                                          Let g ∈ Z\{0}. Let Pg denote the set of primes p such that g is a primitve
                                          root mod p. It was conjectured by Emil Artin in 1927 that Pg is infinite in case
                                          g is in G, the set of integers not equal to −1 or a square. Clearly, if g is not
                                          in G then Pg is finite. There is no integer g for which Artin’s primitive root
                                          conjecture (as it is usually called) has been proved. However, Heath-Brown [5] in
                                          a classical paper, basing himself on a breakthrough paper of Gupta and Murty
                                          [4], established a result which implies, for example, that there are at most two
                                          primes q for which Pq is finite. In 1967 Hooley [6] established Artin’s conjecture
                                          under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH). Moreover,
                                          he showed that under that assumption the set Pg has a natural density, which
                                          he evaluated (his result is Theorem 2 below with a = 1 and f = 1). It turns out
                                          that this density is a rational number (depending on g) times the Artin constant

5      Applications
1). Hooley’s theorem. Setting a = 1 and f = 1 in Theorem 3 we obtain Hooley’s
theorem [6].
2). a = 1. Setting a = 1 in Theorem 2 we obtain Theorem 4 of [12]. Notice
that of all the progressions mod f , the progression 1(mod f ) is the easiest to deal
with, since trivially c1 (n) = 1 for every n.
3). Rodier’s conjecture. Rodier [17], in connection with a coding theoretical
problem, conjectured that the density of the primes in P2 such that p ≡ −1, 3 or
19(mod 28) is A/4. From Theorem 2 it follows however that, under GRH, this
density is 21A/82 (cf. [9]): for each progression ai (mod 28) under consideration
we find that δ(ai , 28, 2) = A(ai , 28, 1)/ϕ(28) = 21A/246..
4). Zero density. Lenstra [7, Theorem 8.3] gave a sketch of a proof of the following
result.

that if δ(a, f, g) = 0, then actually Pa,f,g is finite. In each of these cases, there
are obstructions not going beyond quadratic reciprocity. So, assuming GRH,
loosely speaking δ(a, f, g) = 0 if and only if there is an elementary obstruction,
or obstruction coming from quadratic reciprocity.
5). Equidistribution. If S is any set of integers, denote by S(x) the number of
integers in S not exceeding x. For given integers a and f, denote by S(x; f, a) the
number of integers in S not exceeding x that are congruent to a modulo f. We
say that S is weakly uniformly distributed mod f (or WUD mod f for short) if
S(x) → ∞ (x → ∞) and for every a coprime to f,
                                               S(x)
                                S(x; f, a) ∼         .
                                               ϕ(f )
The progressions a(mod f ) such that the latter asymptotic equivalence holds and
S(x) → ∞ (x → ∞) are said to get their fair share of primes from S. Thus S is
weakly uniformly distributed mod f if and only if all the progressions mod f get
their fair share of primes from S. Narkiewicz [15] has written a nice survey on the
state of knowledge regarding the (weak) uniform distribution of many important
arithmetical sequences. Let Dg denote the set of natural numbers f such that Pg
is weakly uniformly distributed modulo f .

does not occur, for if Pg is WUD mod f , then Pg must also be WUD mod δ for
every divisor δ of f . It remains to show that when g is exceptional we have that
Pg is WUD mod 2m 3n , with m ≥ 0 and n ≥ 1 arbitrary. This follows easily from
Theorem 2 and the calculation done in the case f = 3.                                ✷
6). Optimal normal basis. Let q be an odd given prime power and m a natural
number. One can wonder whether there exists an extension Fqn of Fqm such that
Fqn has an optimal normal basis over Fq . A number theoretic question that arises
in this context, see [1], is whether there is a prime p such that p ≡ 1(mod m)
and q is a primitive root modulo p and if yes to provide a small upper bound for
the smallest such prime. The latter part of the question seems very difficult, but
the results obtained in this paper allow one to shed some light on the first part
of the question [3].
