Both errors vanish. Spins in Λ_(2L)\Λ_L remain available to the circuit and participate in the physical heat bath; they were not removed, independently resampled, or replaced by an infinite-volume environment. This proves F150.

## 5. Route A — a paid moving-parameter arm/pivotal criterion

Here is a genuinely finite-volume bound that records the missing quantities instead of hiding them in a fixed-β o(1). Let λ_(β,L) be the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of −L for the full finite μ_(2L,β) heat bath. It is positive. For 0<v≤τ_β≤R, spectral decomposition makes q convex and decreasing and gives

`q_(β,L)(v)−1/4 ≤ ∫_v^τ H_(β,L)(z) dz + (R−τ)H_(β,L)(τ) + (1/4)e^(−λ_(β,L)R)`.       (A150)

Indeed integrate −q′ from v to τ using (H), from τ to R using the nonincreasing function −q′, and use the finite-chain spectral gap after R. There is no assumed uniform mixing rate in this argument.

A version removes every unknown spatial-comparison constant. Put a_*=(1+e^(12βc))⁻¹, c_*=a_*^14/16, τ_*=c_*²/(10⁶·2^16), K_*=50/c_*. In the **same** Λ_(2L) stationary chain, define

`B_(β,L)(z)=Σ_(x∈Λ_L)P[x is pivotal for Cross_L in both σ_0 and σ_z]`,
`J_(β,L)(z)=K_* B_(β,L)(z)`.

Pivotal is a property of the configuration with x omitted; all exterior spins are retained. The upper proof of Proposition 4.8 with the uniform finite-energy constants gives −q′≤J for 0≤z≤τ_* uniformly for β≤βc. Its explicit 1/2 times the sum in (9) yields K_*. Therefore (A150) also holds with τ_*,J in place of τ_β,H, for every such β. This is a pair-pivotal/mixing criterion measured in the required finite model, not a renamed assertion of target decorrelation.

For a temperature set W_L⊂[β_*,βc), choose r>0, ρ_L↓0 and R_(β,L)=τ_*+λ_(β,L)⁻¹log(1/ρ_L). If, for v_L=2L^(−r),

`sup_(β∈W_L) { ∫_(v_L)^τ_* J_(β,L)(z) dz + [log(1/ρ_L)/λ_(β,L)] J_(β,L)(τ_*) } →0`,        (MA)

then (A150) has final error at most ρ_L/4. Formula (F), with δ=min(r/4,1/2), proves success 1/2+o(1) **uniformly over β∈W_L and circuits**. The sufficient exponent r is independent of d,c; the localization exponents s,t adapt to d,c. One may instead use the sharper H and its stated β-dependent τ when those quantities are controlled. This is the strongest moving-parameter dynamical conclusion paid here: an explicit conditional theorem in finite-volume arm/pivotal and mixing quantities. It is **not** verified for W_L=[β_*,βc−L^(−κ)] for any κ>0.

The full proof suggests a possible restart above a temperature-dependent spatial scale: control α at the restart scale, obtain uniform ratio exponents above it, keep all logarithmic separation buffers inside L, and control the relaxation remainder. What is missing is precisely those uniform spin-arm and relaxation estimates with matching finite free boundary. A polynomial bound on a conventional correlation length alone pays none of the four obligations automatically. Fixed compact β intervals and a diagonal sequence chosen after convergence would add only technical uniformity and would not answer NC150.

## 6. Route B — conditional product spectrum, with its variance cost

Fix one color class I in Λ_(2L). Condition on η=σ_(I^c), including all outside-of-crossing spins. Write g_η and f_η in the biased product Fourier basis of μ(·|η), and define the finite-model quantity

`T_(β,L,I)(k)=E_η Σ_(S⊂I, |S|<k) |ĝ_η(S)|²`.

Uniform (P) on the restricted circuit, retaining total N, and Cauchy–Schwarz on the low/high orthogonal pieces give

`|E_μ f_C g_L|≤√T_(β,L,I)(k)+2e^(−N^s),   k≥N^t`.                       (B150)

For the low part use E||f_low||²≤1; for the high part use E||f_high||²≤4e^(−2N^s) and E||g_high||²≤1. Hence for any δ>0 and W_L, the concrete condition `sup_(β∈W_L)T_(β,L,I)(ceil(m_L^δ))→0` is another sufficient condition for the uniform circuit conclusion, with t=δ/(2c), s=δ/(4c). It involves no time or spectral gap and so is a different mechanism from Route A.

The zero conditional Fourier coefficient cannot be dropped. Exactly,

`T_(β,L,I)(k)≥E[E(g_L|η)²]=1−E Var(g_L|η)≥1−D_(μ,I)(g_L)`                 (V)

for k≥1, where the last inequality is the conditional product Poincaré identity Var(g_η)≤Σ_i∈I E Var_i(g_η). In particular unconditional balancedness does not imply conditional balancedness. Any revealment proof for T must bound this conditional-mean term as well as the other low levels. No such bound is established uniformly in the requested polynomial window. Independent coarse blocks have not been assumed. Replacing raw spins by arbitrary block crossings would change the circuit input through unallowed classical preprocessing; a coupling alone does not supply QAC0 gates. A full-density comparison would also reintroduce I147's exponential loss. Equation (B150) is an actual estimate in the requested finite law, with the distributional conditioning and variance term paid, rather than an analogy from a rare-event example.

For comparison, an exact correlated spectral alternative is available: if W_(β,L)(A)=||1_[0,A](−L)g_L||², then orthogonal spectral splitting gives `|<f,g>|≤√W_(β,L)(A)+√(D(f)/A)`. Constants include the mean eigenspace. Without an actual low-spectral-weight estimate for this crossing, this supplies no additional temperature theorem and is not nominated.

### A concrete obstruction for both proposed routes

`K_(β,L)(g)=inf_h {||g−h||²_(μ_β)+D_β(h)}=<g,A_β(I+A_β)⁻¹g>_(μ_β)`.

The infimum runs over all real functions on the **same finite** spin cube; completion of the square gives minimizer h=(I+A_β)⁻¹g. By the Laplace representation of the resolvent,

`K_(βc,L)(g_L)=(1/2)∫₀^∞ e^(−t) NS_(βc,t)(g_L) dt→0`.                         (K)

Dominated convergence is justified by NS≤4. No claim D_βc(g_L)→0 is needed.

There is an exact finite-volume transfer with a **bounded** density cost on its stated scale. Let Δ=βc−β≥0 and e_L=48L²+8L. The edge sum lies in [−e_L,e_L], so μ_β/μ_βc≤e^(2e_LΔ). Also |∂_β log c_x|≤12. The edge representation D_β(F)=(1/2)Σ_(σ,x)μ_β(σ)c_(β,x)(σ)[F(σ^x)−F(σ)]² implies

`D_β(F)≤e^((2e_L+12)Δ)D_βc(F),    K_(β,L)(g)≤e^((2e_L+12)Δ)K_(βc,L)(g)`.      (K2)

For the second inequality use the critical minimizer as a trial function, bounding its squared-error and energy terms separately. For every fixed A<∞ the multiplier is at most e^(124A), uniformly for L≥1 and 0≤Δ≤A L⁻². Thus K_(β,L)(g_L)→0 uniformly on this window. For 0<t≤T<∞, the elementary spectral inequality 1−e^(−tx)≤2max(1,T)x/(1+x) gives NS_(β,t)(g_L)≤4max(1,T)K_(β,L)(g_L)→0. At bounded, and in particular polynomially vanishing, physical times, `<g_L,P_tg_L>→1`. Condition (MA) cannot hold there.

The conditional-product obstruction follows too. Let A_(β,I)=Σ_(i∈I)(I−E_i). The E_i commute on each conditional product fiber. Its spectrum there is {0,1,…,|I|}, and its zero projection is E[·|η]. Hence `E Var(g_L|η)≤2<g_L,A_(β,I)(I+A_(β,I))⁻¹g_L>`. The variational formula and D_(β,I)≤D_β bound the right side by 2K_(β,L)(g_L). Therefore T_(β,L,I)(k)≥1−2K_(β,L)(g_L)→1 for every color I and k≥1, uniformly on the same window. This pays the conditional-revelation cost in the actual model without inferring a small raw Dirichlet form from fixed-time stability.

In particular these two criteria cannot prove NC150 with κ≥2, since its upper endpoint lies in this window. **This does not refute NC150 for κ≥2 or for any κ:** stability of a target is not a circuit for that target. It leaves every smaller positive κ unassessed. Unlike an exponential full-density shortcut at fixed temperature, (K2) states and pays its bounded-change regime exactly.
