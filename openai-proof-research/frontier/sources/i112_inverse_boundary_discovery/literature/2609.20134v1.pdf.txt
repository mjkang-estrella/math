in [5, 6, 11]. With partial boundary data, both parameters can be recovered when µ is

approach combines boundary determination with real analyticity. Under suitable smoothness
hypotheses, boundary measurements determine the Taylor coefficients of the Lamé parameters

continuation [13]. These results motivate the search for uniqueness beyond nearly constant
shear moduli and real-analytic Lamé parameters.
   In this paper, we show how the structure and regularity of the shear modulus lead to unique
identifiability in isotropic elasticity. Symmetry and separation of variables yield uniqueness
for smooth coefficients, while quasianalyticity provides a complementary route by propagating
boundary information into the interior. Our main results are as follows.
    (i) Axisymmetric shear moduli. We establish unique identifiability of axisymmetric
        shear moduli on domains of revolution from full torsional boundary data in the bounded
        measurable setting, and from partial torsional data in the smooth setting.
(ii) Separation of variables. We prove that partial boundary data uniquely determine a
     smooth shear modulus admitting either a multiplicative or an additive separation on a
     product domain, even when both factors or both summands are unknown.
(iii) Directional quasianalyticity. We establish unique identifiability of smooth shear
      moduli that are quasianalytic in one fixed direction from full boundary data, without
      requiring the domain to be convex.
In each of the smooth settings above, the full elastic Dirichlet-to-Neumann map uniquely
determines both Lamé parameters, with the structural or quasianalytic condition imposed
only on the shear modulus.

Remark 1.1. Under either data equality in Theorem 1.2, the factors in case (i) are determined
up to (α, β) 7→ (cα, c−1 β), where c > 0. In case (ii), the summands are determined up to
(a, b) 7→ (a + c, b − c), where c ∈ R. The normalizations β(0) = 1 and b(0) = 0 give individual

  Theorem 1.3 uses quasianalyticity along lines parallel to the fixed direction e. On each
connected component of a line section, boundary determination provides all derivatives of µ
at an endpoint, and quasianalyticity determines µ throughout that component. Since this
argument applies to each component separately, the domain need not be convex.
Remark 1.3. Theorem 1.3 extends to shear moduli that are quasianalytic along a prescribed
family of smooth curves. When these curves start on the observed boundary and cover Ω,
localized boundary data determine µ throughout Ω, while full boundary data determine both
Lamé parameters; see Proposition 5.1.
Remark 1.4. Theorem 1.3 also holds with C {M } (J) replaced by any prescribed linear subspace
Q(J) ⊂ C ∞ (J) satisfying (1.6) for every q ∈ Q(J) and s∗ ∈ J.
Remark 1.5. Theorem 1.3 extends uniqueness for real-analytic Lamé parameters to a strictly
larger class of smooth coefficients. The shear modulus need only be quasianalytic in one fixed
direction, allowing classes strictly larger than the real-analytic class [14]. No analyticity is
required in the transverse variables or for λ.

                             5. Directional quasianalyticity
  In this section, we prove Theorem 1.3 by combining boundary determination with quasi-

component gives uniqueness of µ throughout the domain without requiring convexity. The
recovery of λ then follows from uniqueness for a known shear modulus. Section 5.1 proves
Theorem 1.3, and Section 5.2 extends the argument to prescribed smooth curves and localized
data.
