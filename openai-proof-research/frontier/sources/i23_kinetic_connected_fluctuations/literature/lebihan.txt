                                                  Abstract. We provide a rigorous justification of the linearized Boltzmann and Landau equations for
                                                  interacting particle systems with long-range interaction. The result shows that for a system of N Hamil-
                                                  tonian particles governed by truncated power law potentials of the form Uεeff (r) ∼ |r/εeff |−s near r ≈ 0
                                                  (with εeff the effective radius of the particles ), the covariance of the equilibrium fluctuations converges
                                                  to solutions of kinetic equations in appropriate scaling limits εeff → 0 and N → ∞, corresponding to a
                                                  low density regime . We prove that in dimension 3 , for s ∈ (1, ∞), the limiting system approaches the




arXiv:2408.03597v2 [math.AP] 8 Jan 2026
                                                  uncutoff linearized Boltzmann equation for the scaling N εeff 2 = 1 . The Coulomb singularity s = 1
                                                  appears as a threshold value. Kinetic scaling limits with s ∈ (0, 1] universally converge to the linearized
                                                  Landau equation, and we prove the onset of the Coulomb logarithm for s = 1.

                                                     1. Introduction
   In kinetic theory, a gas of particles can be modeled by a large system of N classical particles interacting
via a potential U (·/εeff ), with εeff the effective radius of a particle . The dynamics is Hamiltonian and
obeys the Newton laws with assigned potential U .            In dimension 3, the power laws Us (r) := r−s ,
s ≥ 1 play a fundamental role, in particular the Coulomb case s = 1. One of the goals of kinetic theory
is the description of such a gas in the limit N → ∞, εeff → 0. Of course, the asymptotic behavior
depends on the relation between εeff and N . Collisional kinetic theory concerns low density scalings,
where the occupied volume N εeff 3 goes to 0. The good scaling limit looks to be the Boltzmann–Grad
limit N εeff 2 = 1, at least for s > 1 (see [Gra49]). It corresponds to a regime such that on a time interval
of length 1, any particle passes nearby another particle (at distance less than εeff , see Remark 1.1 for a
comment on this scaling).
   In the case s > 1, if at time 0 the particles are "sufficiently independent", the distribution of a typical
particle f (t, x, v) is a solution of the Boltzmann equation (introduced by Maxwell in 1867 [Max03] and
Boltzmann in 1871 [Bol96])

1.2. State of the art. Now we recall some results about the derivation of the Boltzmann and Landau
equation from a particle system .
   In the nonlinear setting, the only results hold for potentials U (·) supported in a ball {x ∈ R3 , |x| ≤ R}.
In the Boltzmann-Grad scaling N εeff 2 = 1, the distribution of a typical particle follows the Boltzmann
equation up to a time O(1/R2 ). The first derivation was performed by Lanford [Lan75] for hard spheres
(i.e. exp(−Uhs (r)) = 1r>1 ) and King [Kin75] for more general compactly supported potentials (see also
[GSRT13, PSS14, Den18, BGSRS18]). The previous results have two defects. They are valid only up to
a small time (for the atmosphere at temperature 20◦C and pressure 105 Pa, the validity time is 10−9 s),
and the results apply only to a compactly supported interaction potential. A first long-time result out of
equilibrium is [IP89], in a setting where the dispersive effects are dominant. More recently, Deng, Hani
and Ma provide a long time result out of equilibrium [DHM24] for more general initial datum f0 . Their
result holds up to the minimum between the existing time of f (t), the solution of the Boltzmann equation
with initial data f0 , and a time O(log | log N |α ) for some α ∈ (0, 1) given by a sampling time strategy.
   For the Landau equation, the unique results hold only at time 0 (see [BPS13, Win21, Due21]): the
authors obtain the equality
                                    (∂t f )|t=0 = −v · ∇x f0 + QL (f0 , f0 ).
Note that in [BPS13, Win21], the authors do not look at the real particle system but at a simplified
version (they truncate the BBGKY hierarchy). In [Due21], Duerinckx proves the consistency of the
Lenard–Balescue equation, which can be understood as a modification of the Landau equation. However,
the scaling is far from the collisional scaling that we are treating here.
   This is not the first attempt to derive a linear version of the Boltzmann or Landau equations.
   A linear equation can be obtained from the study of the Lorentz gas: one fixes a background of
obstacles distributed with respect to the Poisson measure of parameter N . One follows a unique particle
that interacts pairwise with the obstacles via the potential U (·/εeff ) with N ε2 = 1. In the limit N → ∞,
the density of the tagged particle f (t, x, v) follows the linear Boltzmann equation

remains controllable. We observe that there are two reasons for the set of parameters to explode: the
pathology (recollisions or multiple meetings) and an uncontrolled number of particles n.
    We now introduce two samplings, one to control regular collisions and one to control recollisions and
multiple encounters .
    The first sampling has a relatively large step θ := β log 1| log ε| (for some constant β large enough). We
stop the pseudotrajectory development at time t − kθ if there are more than 2k particles involved in the
pseudotrajectory. Hence, the number of particles at time 0 remains controlled. This sampling follows
[BGSR16, BGSR17]8.
    The second sampling has a shorter step, δ := ε1/12 . We stop the expansion at time ts := t − kδ if
the pseudotrajectory has at least one recollision on [ts , t] (but no recollision on [ts + δ, t]). Imposing
recollisions creates an additional geometric condition, and thus, an extra-smallness gain.
    Unfortunately, we still have too many possible histories. In order to reduce their number, we follow
the idea of [LB25] and separate the pseudotrajectories into two categories. In non-pathological pseudo-
trajectories, there is no recollision nor multiple encounters on the time interval [ts , ts + δ]. We are in
a setting close to the case without recollision, and we only need C n parameters (C a fixed constant) to
describe the histories.
    We explain now how to treat the pathological recollisions part. The initial data ZN (0) is conditioned
such that on each interval [kδ, (k + 1)δ] a particle can encounter 9 with only a finite number of particles
γ (we will take γ := 12d). Hence, for a pseudotrajectory z1 (t, Zn , history), the history has to describe
first a partition of [n] into small clusters of particles that interact together on [0, δ] and how they really
interact. As the size of each cluster is uniformly bounded, the number of histories is at most of order C n
for some C > 1.

Remark 3.8 (Comparison with the strategy of [LB25, LB23]). As explained in Remark 3.5, we used in

      • The tree pseudotrajectories have already been used in the description of gas interacting via com-
         pactly supported potential [Gra49, Kin75, GSRT13, PSS14, Ayi17, Cat18]. One of the encountered
         difficulties is the treatment of multiple interactions, which have to be treated separately from binary
         interactions even before imposing some conditioning. Our presentation avoids this difficulty.
      • In [LB25], we needed two conditionings (see Section 2.3 of [LB25]). The first one is symmetric,
         and looks like Υε (we denote it Υ̃ε ) : we impose the same condition on all the particles. We
         also needed a second conditioning, which is asymmetric: we construct an indicator function Xin :

         of in . Then we made the decomposition
