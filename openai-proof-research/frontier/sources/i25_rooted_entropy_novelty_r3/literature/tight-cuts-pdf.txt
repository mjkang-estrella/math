                                                                                              Abstract
                                                         An edge cut C of a graph G is tight if |C ∩ M | = 1 for every perfect matching
                                                     M of G. Barrier-cuts and 2-separation cuts, also referred to as ELP-cuts, are two
                                                     important types of tight cuts in matching covered graphs. Edmonds, Lovász and
                                                     Pulleyblank [Brick decompositions and the matching rank of graphs, Combinatorica
                                                     2(3) (1982) 247-274] proved that if a matching covered graph has a non-trivial tight
                                                     cut, then it also has a non-trivial ELP-cut. In conﬁrmation of a conjecture proposed
                                                     by Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty, Chen et. al. [Laminar tight cuts in matching
                                                     covered graphs, J. Comb. Theory, Ser. B, 150 (2021) 177-194] showed that if C
                                                     is a non-trivial tight cut of a matching covered graph G, then G has at least one
                                                     C-sheltered non-trivial barrier or a 2-separation cut that is laminar with C.
                                                         In this paper, we present a complete characterization of non-trivial tight cuts
                                                     in matching covered graphs, from which the result of Chen et. al. can be derived
                                                     directly. Moreover, we show that the lower bound of the number of C-sheltered
                                                     non-trivial barrier or a 2-separation cut that is laminar with C in the result of Chen
                                                     et. al. is sharp.

                                                   Keywords: matching covered graphs; tight cuts; ELP-cuts.

   Let ∂(X) be an edge cut of G. Denoted by G/(X → x) the graph obtained from
G by contracting X to a singleton x (and removing any resulting loops). The graphs
G/(X → x) and G/(X → x) are ∂(X)-contractions of G.
    A connected non-trivial graph G is matching covered if each edge in G lies in a perfect
matching. An edge cut ∂(X) is tight of G if |∂(X) ∩ M| = 1 for every perfect matching
M of G. A trivial edge cut is obviously a tight cut. A matching covered graph without
non-trivial tight cuts is called a brace if it is bipartite, and is called a brick if it is non-
bipartite. There is a procedure called a tight cut decomposition, due to Lovász [8], which
can be applied to a matching covered graph G to produce a list of unique braces and
bricks. In particular, any two applications of the tight cut decomposition procedure yield
the same number of bricks [8], which is called the brick number of G. Using the tool of
tight cut decomposition, many problems about matching covered graphs can be reduced
to braces and bricks.
    Let G be a matching covered graph. We denote by o(H) the number of odd components
of a graph H. A nonempty vertex set B of G is a barrier if o(G − B) = |B|. An edge
cut C of G is a barrier-cut if there exists a barrier B of G and an odd component Q of
G − B such that C = ∂(V (Q)). A vertex set S of G is a 2-separation if |S| = 2, G − S is
disconnected and each of the components of G − S is even. Let {u, v} be a 2-separation
of G, and let us divide the components of G − {u, v} into two nonempty subgraphs G1
and G2 . The cuts ∂(V (G1 ) + u) and ∂(V (G1 ) + v) are both 2-separation cuts associated
with {u, v} of G. Barrier-cuts and 2-separation cuts, called ELP-cuts, play an important
role in tight cut decomposition (it can be checked that ELP-cuts are tight cuts). The
following theorem, called ELP-Theorem, due to Edmonds, Lovász, and Pulleyblank [5],
is a fundamental result of matching theory.
Theorem 1.1 ([5]). Every matching covered graph that has a non-trivial tight cut has a
non-trivial barrier or a 2-separation.
    The proof of Theorem 1.1 given by Edmonds, Lovász, and Pulleyblank is based on
linear programming techniques [5]. Szigeti [9] provided a purely graph-theoretical proof of
this theorem. Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty [2] gave an alternative proof of ELP-Theorem
by using properties of barriers in graphs that has a perfect matching.

    A barrier B is C-sheltered if B is a subset of a shore of C. Note that for a tight cut C, a
C-sheltered barrier-cut is laminar with C. Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty [2] conjectured
that given any non-trivial tight cut C in a matching covered graph that is not an ELP-cut,
there exists a non-trivial ELP-cut D that is laminar with C, and proved this conjecture
for bicritical graphs and also for matching covered graphs with brick number at most
two. For a matching covered graph G and a non-trivial tight cut C, let ELP G (C) be the
collection of C-sheltered non-trivial barrier-cuts and 2-separation cuts that are laminar
with C. Chen et. al. proved the conjecture of Carvalho, Lucchesi and Murty [2], as stated
below.
Theorem 1.2 ([4]). Let C be a non-trivial tight cut of a matching covered graph G. Then
|ELP G (C)| ≥ 1.

  1) there exists a set of 2-separations {F1 , F2 , . . . , Fs } ⊂ F such that F = F1 , F ′ = Fs
     and |Fi ∩ Fi+1 | = 1 for i = 1, 2, . . . , s − 1; and

  2) if |F ∩ F ′ | = 1 and Y (resp. Y ′ ) is the vertex set of a component of G − F (resp.
     G−F ′ ) such that Y ⊂ Y ′ , then all vertices in each odd component of G[Y ′ \(Y ∪F )]
     lie in the shore of ∂(X) that is diﬀerent from the one containing the only vertex in
     F ∩ F ′ , and all vertices in each even component of G[Y ′ \ (Y ∪ F )] lie in the same
      shore of ∂(X); if Y ∪F contains no 2-separations in F \{F }, then Y ⊂ X or Y ⊂ X.

Specially, a 2-separation cut is a GS-cut. We say F is the set of 2-separations of G
associated with ∂(X). It should be noted that if ∂(X) is a GS-cut of G and e ∈ ∂(X),
then at least one end point of e lies in a 2-separation F of G satisfying |F ∩ X| = 1.
For any F ∈ F , if there exists a vertex w ∈ V (G) such that F ∩ F ′ ⊆ {w} for each

separation of F , then there exists a component of G − F , say G1 , such that V (G1 ) ∪ F
contains no 2-separations in F \ {F }.

2     Preliminaries
    In this section, we will present several useful results. The following theorem was
established by Tutte.

Theorem 2.1 ([10]). A graph G has a perfect matching if and only if o(G − S) ≤ |S| for
every S ⊆ V (G).

    The following proposition follows Theorem 2.1 directly.

Proposition 2.2. Let G be a matching covered graph and let B be a non-trivial barrier
of G. Then E(G[B]) = ∅ and G − B contains no even components.

   The following are some properties of tight cuts in matching covered graphs that will
be used later.
Proposition 2.3 ([5]). Let G be a matching covered graph and let ∂(X) and ∂(Y ) be two
tight cuts such that |X ∩ Y | is odd. Then ∂(X ∩ Y ) and ∂(X ∪ Y ) are also tight cuts of
G. Furthermore, no edge connects X ∩ Y to X ∩ Y .

Proposition 2.4 ([8]). Let G be a matching covered graph, and let C be a tight cut of
G. Then, both C-contractions are matching covered. Moreover, if G′ is a C-contraction
of G, then a tight cut of G′ is also a tight cut of G. Conversely, if a tight cut of G is an
edge cut of G′ , then it is also tight in G′ .

    It is known that every matching covered graph with four or more vertices is 2-connected

in [4] for example).

Corollary 2.5. Let G be a matching covered graph, and let C = ∂(X) be a tight cut of
G. Then, both G[X] and G[X] are connected.

3     Essential GS-cuts
Proposition 3.1. Let {x, y} be a 2-separation of a matching covered graph G with xy ∈
E(G). Assume that G1 and G2 are connected induced subgraphs of G such that V (G) =
V (G1 ) ∪ V (G2 ) and V (G1 ) ∩ V (G2 ) = {x, y}. For i = 1, 2, let Xi be a subset of V (Gi )
such that |Xi | is odd, x ∈ Xi and xy ∈ ∂(Xi ). Then, for X = X1 ∪ X2 , ∂(X) is tight in
G if and only if ∂(Xi ) is tight in Gi for both i = 1 and 2.

Proof. As {x, y} is a 2-separation of G, both |V (G1 )| and |V (G2 )| are even. Thus, it can be
veriﬁed that for any perfect matching M of G, M ∩ ∂(V (G1 ) \ {y}) contains either exactly
an edge xu for some u ∈ V (G2 ) \ {x}, or exactly an edge yv for some v ∈ V (G1 ) \ {y}.
It follows that ∂(V (G1 ) \ {y}) is a tight cut of G. So G1 and G2 are matching covered
graphs by Proposition 2.4. Note that ∂(X1 ) ⊂ ∂(X). Thus, if ∂(X) is tight in G, then
∂(X1 ) is tight in G1 , and so is ∂(X2 ) in G2 .
    Now we show the suﬃciency. As |Xi | is odd for i = 1, 2, and X1 ∩X2 = {x}, |X| is odd.
Suppose to the contrary that ∂(X) is not tight, that is |M ∩ ∂(X)| 6= 1 for some perfect

Proposition 3.2. Every essential GS-cut of a matching covered graph is tight.
Proof. Let G be a matching covered graph. We ﬁrst prove the following claim on GS-cuts.
Claim 1: Every GS-cut of G is tight in G.
    Let ∂(X) be a GS-cut of a G and F be the set of 2-separations of G associated with
∂(X). For any 2-separation F = {x, y} ∈ F , we may assume that xy ∈ E(G). It is
because if ∂(X) is tight in G with xy ∈ E(G), then ∂(X) is also tight in G − xy by the
deﬁnition of a tight cut (it can be checked that G is matching covered if and only if G−xy
is matching covered).
    We denote the number of 2-separation cuts crossing ∂(X) by λ. We will show the
claim by induction on λ. As a 2-separation results in at least two 2-separation cuts, we
have λ ≥ 2. If λ = 2, assume that D ∈ F . Then ∂(X) is a 2-separation cut associated
with D. So ∂(X) is tight. We assume that Claim 1 holds whenever λ < k, where k ≥ 3.
Now we consider the case λ = k.
    Let D1 be an end-2-separation in F such that one component of G − D1 , say H1 ,
satisﬁes the condition that V (H1 ) ∩ F = ∅ for each F ∈ F .
    Then by the deﬁnition of a GS-cut, all the vertices of V (H1 ) lie in one shore of ∂(X).
Therefore, V (H1 ) ∪ {x1 } is a 2-separation cut of G, where x1 ∈ D1 , and x1 and all
the vertices of V (H1 ) lie in the same shore of ∂(X). Let G1 = G/(V (H1 ) ∪ {x1 }) and
G2 = G/(V (H1 ) ∪ {x1 }). By the deﬁnition of a GS-cut, ∂(X ∩ V (G1 )) is also a GS-
cut of G1 . By inductive hypothesis, the edge cut ∂(X ∩ V (G1 )) is tight in G1 . Note
that ∂(X ∩ V (G2 )) is a trivial edge cut of G2 , which is tight. So Claim 1 follows from
Proposition 3.1.
   Now we are going to complete the proof. Let ∂(Y ) be an essential GS-cut. Then
we may contract some ∂(Y )-sheltered non-trivial barriers to get a GS-cut ∂(Y ′ ) of the
resulting graph, say G′ . It is known that barrier-cuts are tight. By Claim 1, ∂(Y ′ ) is tight
in G′ . So ∂(Y ) is a tight cut of G by applying Proposition 2.4 repeatedly.

Theorem 3.3. Let C be a non-trivial GS-cut of a matching covered graph G. Then,
|ELP G (C)| ≥ 2.
Proof. Let C = ∂(X) be a non-trivial GS-cut of G, where X ⊂ V (G). Assume that the
brick number of G is at most one. Then C is a barrier-cut by Proposition 4.18 in [6].
This is a contradiction to the assumption that C is a GS-cut.
    Now we assume that the brick number of G is at least two, and F is the set of
2-separations of G associated with C. By the deﬁnition of a GS-cut, there exists an
end-2-separation F1 of F . Then there is a component G1 of G − F1 such that V (G1 ) ∪ F1
contains no 2-separation in F \ {F1 }. Then V (G1 ), together with a vertex in F1 , forms
one shore of a 2-separation cut which is associated with F1 and laminar with C, as all the
vertices in G1 lie in the same shore of C.
    If V (G1 ) contains no 2-separations in F \{F1 }, then V (G1 ) ∪ F1 , together with a vertex
in F1 , forms one shore of a 2-separation cut which is associated with F1 and laminar with
C, as either V (G1 ) ∪ F1 ⊂ X or V (G1 ) ∪ F1 ⊂ X. So we assume that there exists a
component G2 of G − F1 such that G1 6= G2 , and V (G2 ) ∪ F1 contains some 2-separation
in F \ {F1 }. Let F2 be an end-2-separation of F with F2 ⊂ (V (G2 ) ∪ F1 ). Then, there is
a component G3 of G − F2 such that V (G3 ) ∪ F2 contains no 2-separation in F \ {F2 }.
   Observe that all the vertices of G3 lie in the same shore of C by the deﬁnition of a
GS-cut. Therefore, V (G3 ), together with a vertex in F2 , forms one shore of a 2-separation
cut which is associated with F2 and laminar with C. So the result follows.

5     Remarks
    The proof of Theorem 1.3 does not rely on Theorem 1.2. By the structure of an
essential GS-cut, Theorem 1.2 can be inferred by Theorem 1.3 (note that if C is a barrier-
cut, then C is an ELP-cut laminar with itself). Recall that if C is a non-ELP tight cut
of a matching covered graph G, then, by Theorem 1.2, |ELP G (C)| ≥ 1. And the lower
bound is sharp by the remarks of Theorem 3.3. If C is a barrier-cut of G, |ELP G (C)|
may also be 1. For example, in Hn′ , C ′ = ∂({v1 , v2 , . . . , v2n+1 }) is the only ELP-cut. So
|ELP Hn′ (C ′ )| = 1.
