   The Hessian of the volume polynomial connects the spectral problem (1.3) to earlier work
on polyhedral geometry. Izmestiev [Izm10] studied the support-parameter space of polytopes
with prescribed facet normals and constructed a Colin de Verdière matrix from the negative
volume Hessian. Fillastre and Izmestiev [FI17] described irredundancy domains and the
decomposition of their interiors into type cones. Together, the two works provide a blueprint
for extending smooth centro-affine geometry to the polytopal setting.
   The degree-one Hodge–Riemann relation is precisely the second Minkowski inequality. In
a simple type chamber, it is equivalent to concavity of h 7→ Vol(P (h))1/n under linear support
interpolation. The proof of Theorem 1.1 uses the degree-two Hodge–Riemann relation. Van
Handel [vH23a] formulated the higher-degree Hodge–Riemann relations as mixed-volume
inequalities and used the degree-two relation to disprove Fedotov’s conjectured extension
of Shephard’s inequalities. We also mention that Shenfeld and van Handel [SvH19] proved
the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality by the Bochner method, including a finite-dimensional
spectral argument for polytopes. See also their later work, where they treat the equality cases
of Minkowski’s quadratic inequality and the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality [SvH22, SvH23].
