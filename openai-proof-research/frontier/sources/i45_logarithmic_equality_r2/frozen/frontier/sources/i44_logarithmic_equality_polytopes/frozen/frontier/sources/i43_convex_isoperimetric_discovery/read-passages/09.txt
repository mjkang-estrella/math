1: \section{Classical inputs and the two-volume reduction}
2: \label{pre:section}
3: 
4: We work on
5: \[
6:  T=\mathbb R^3/(2\mathbb Z^3),
7:  \qquad V(E)=\frac{|E|}{8},
8:  \qquad A(E)=\frac{|\partial E|}{8}.
9: \]
10: Here and below perimeter is understood in the finite-perimeter sense; a
11: minimizer is represented by its regular region. The map from the unit torus
12: to \(T\) doubles lengths. Consequently \(V\) is the original volume fraction,
13: whereas \(A\) is one half of the original perimeter. The outward unit normal
14: is \(N\), and \(h=k_1+k_2\) is the sum of the principal curvatures in the
15: metric of \(T\), positive on a ball. It is not divided by eight.
16: 
17: Standard compactness and regularity for isoperimetric regions imply existence
18: at every \(0<V<1\), with smooth embedded boundary of constant mean curvature.
19: In a flat metric this boundary is real analytic: its local CMC graph
20: equation is analytic elliptic, so
21: \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Blatt} applies. The second variation is
22: nonnegative on all volume-preserving variations. We use the following
23: classical structural theorem in precisely its unrestricted, identity-group
24: case.
25: 
26: \begin{theorem}[Classical flat-manifold structure]\label{pre:structure}
27: Let \(E\) be an isoperimetric region in a complete orientable flat
28: three-manifold, with closed boundary \(\Sigma\).
29: Either \(\Sigma\) is a union of parallel planar surfaces, or \(\Sigma\)
30: is connected. In the connected case:
31: \begin{enumerate}
32: \renewcommand{\labelenumi}{\textup{(\roman{enumi})}}
33: \item genus zero gives a round sphere;
34: \item genus one gives a flat quotient of a plane or a circular cylinder;
35: \item the genus is at most four;
36: \item in genus two or three, \(H^2|\Sigma|\le 2\pi\), where \(H=h/2\);
37: in genus four, \(H=0\).
38: \end{enumerate}
39: \end{theorem}
40: 
41: These statements are Theorems~5 and~7 of Hauswirth--P\'erez--Romon--Ros
42: \cite{HPRR}, applied with the identity symmetry group. In particular the
43: orientation-preserving hypothesis of their Theorem~7 is satisfied. Their
44: genus-one assertion builds on the stability results of
45: Ritor\'e--Ros \cite{RitoreRos92,RitoreRos96}. Existence and regularity in the
46: compact torus setting are recorded in \cite[Theorem~3]{HPRR}.
47: We call a minimizing boundary \emph{standard} if it is planar, spherical,
48: or a circular-cylinder quotient. Every nonstandard minimizing boundary
49: is therefore connected of genus \(g\ge2\) and satisfies
50: \begin{equation}
51:  h^2 A\le\pi.
52:  \label{pre:curvature}
53: \end{equation}
54: Indeed \(h^2A=H^2|\Sigma|/2\), and the genus-four case has \(h=0\).
55: 
56: \subsection{The standard competitors}
57: 
58: For \(0\le V\le1\), set \(v=\min(V,1-V)\) and
59: \begin{equation}
60:  F(V)=\min\left\{
61:        (9\pi/2)^{1/3}v^{2/3},\ \sqrt{\pi v},\ 1
62:        \right\},
63:  \qquad V_1=\frac{4\pi}{81},\quad V_2=\frac1\pi.
64:  \label{pre:envelope}
65: \end{equation}
66: The first, second, and third expressions are the respective minima on
67: \(0\le v\le V_1\), \(V_1\le v\le V_2\), and \(V_2\le v\le1/2\).
68: 
69: \begin{lemma}\label{pre:standard}
70: The function \(F\) is attained by standard competitors. Every standard
71: minimizing boundary has \(A\ge F(V)\); equality gives exactly the balls,
72: shortest-geodesic tubes, and coordinate slabs in
73: Theorem~\ref{intro:main}, including the indicated ties and complements.
74: \end{lemma}
75: 
76: \begin{proof}
77: A sphere in \(T\) lifts to an embedded sphere in \(\mathbb R^3\).
78: Its distinct lattice translates have disjoint interiors: equal-radius
79: spheres with intersecting interiors would have intersecting boundaries.
80: Thus it bounds an embedded ball on one side. For its radius \(R\),
81: \[
82:  W=\frac{\pi R^3}{6},\qquad A=\frac{\pi R^2}{2}
83:        =(9\pi/2)^{1/3}W^{2/3},
84: \]
85: where \(W\) denotes the ball-side volume. Since \(W\ge v\), this area
86: is at least the first expression in \eqref{pre:envelope}.
87: 
88: For a compact circular-cylinder quotient, the stabilizer of its lifted
89: axis contains a nonzero axial lattice translation. Its primitive core
90: therefore has length \(2k\), with \(k=|m|\ge1\) for a primitive nonzero
91: \(m\in\mathbb Z^3\). Embeddedness gives a solid tube on one side, with
92: \[
93:  W=\frac{k\pi R^2}{4},\qquad A=\frac{k\pi R}{2}
94:        =\sqrt{\pi kW}\ge\sqrt{\pi v}.
95: \]
96: This comparison concerns the area of the given tube and does not require
97: a shortest tube of the possibly larger volume \(W\) to embed.
98: If such a boundary minimizes, \(A=I(V)\le F(V)\), where \(I\) is the
99: isoperimetric profile. Equality throughout forces \(k=1\), \(W=v\),
100: and \(v\) in the closed cylindrical branch. The corresponding sphere
101: comparison similarly forces \(W=v\) and the spherical branch.
102: 
103: A connected closed planar surface has a primitive integral normal
104: \(m\) and physical area \(4|m|\). Disjoint planar surfaces must be
105: parallel, and a single such torus does not separate \(T\). At least
106: two are required to bound a region. Their total normalized area is
107: therefore at least one, with equality only for two tori with
108: \(|m|=1\). Their intervening regions are exactly coordinate slabs.
109: 
110: Finally the branches in \eqref{pre:envelope} are realized by an embedded
111: ball, a shortest tube, and a coordinate slab, respectively. At the last
112: volume of its branch the ball radius is \(2/3\); the largest tube radius
113: in its branch is \(2/\pi\). Both are less than the transverse injectivity
114: radius one. The branch intersections are exactly \(V_1,V_2\).
115: Complements give the remaining volumes. This proves both attainment
116: and the asserted equality classification.
117: \end{proof}
118: 
119: \subsection{Upper supports for the profile}
120: 
121: \begin{lemma}\label{pre:supports}
122: The profile \(I:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)\) is continuous, concave, and satisfies
123: \(I(V)=I(1-V)\). If a minimizer at \(V\in(0,1)\) has sum curvature \(h\),
124: there is a smooth local upper support \(C\) for \(I\) at \(V\) such that
125: \(C'(V)=h\) and \(C''(V)\le0\). If its boundary is connected of genus \(g\),
126: then at contact
127: \begin{equation}
128:  C^2C''=-h^2C+\pi(1-g).
129:  \label{pre:parallel}
130: \end{equation}
131: Every such contact curvature is nonnegative for \(V<1/2\) and
132: nonpositive for \(V>1/2\).
133: \end{lemma}
134: 
135: \begin{proof}
136: Compactness of finite-perimeter sets gives lower semicontinuity of \(I\).
137: A local volume-changing smooth variation of a minimizer gives upper
138: semicontinuity at every interior volume. Since \(0\le I\le F\), continuity
139: also holds at the endpoints. Complementation gives the symmetry.
140: 
141: Let \(K=k_1k_2\) be the intrinsic Gauss curvature of \(\Sigma\).
142: Let \(a(\tau)\) and \(w(\tau)\) be normalized area and volume under
143: outward normal parallel displacement by \(\tau\). At \(\tau=0\),
144: \[
145:  w'=A,\qquad a'=hA,\qquad w''=hA,\qquad
146:  a''=\frac18\int_\Sigma 2K\,dS.
147: \]
148: Small parallels are embedded and \(w'>0\); expressing their area as a
149: function \(C(w)\) provides the upper support. The chain rule gives
150: \[
151:  C'=h,\qquad
152:  C^2C''=\frac18\int_\Sigma 2K\,dS-h^2A.
153: \]
154: For connected \(\Sigma\), Gauss--Bonnet gives \eqref{pre:parallel}.
155: For an arbitrary boundary,
156: \[
157:  \frac18\int_\Sigma(2K-h^2)\,dS
158:        =-\frac18\int_\Sigma(k_1^2+k_2^2)\,dS\le0,
159: \]
160: so \(C''\le0\). These are the parallel-variation formulas of
161: \cite[Section~3]{HPRR}, with the stated normalization.
162: 
163: For completeness, this contact property implies concavity without
164: differentiability of \(I\). Suppose \(I(c)<L(c)\) for a chord \(L\) on
165: \([a,b]\) and \(a<c<b\). Choose \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small
166: that \(I(c)<L(c)+\varepsilon(c-a)(c-b)\).
167: The minimum of
168: \[
169:  I(x)-L(x)-\varepsilon(x-a)(x-b)
170: \]
171: is negative and occurs at an interior point \(x_0\).
172: Adding this minimum to the strictly convex quadratic produces a function
173: \(q\le I\) with equality at \(x_0\).
174: Any local upper support there satisfies \(C''(x_0)\ge q''=2\varepsilon\),
175: a contradiction.
176: 
177: For a concave function, the slope of a smooth upper support lies between
178: its one-sided derivatives:
179: \(I'_+(V)\le h\le I'_-(V)\).
180: The secant joining the equal values at \(V\) and \(1-V\) has slope zero.
181: Concavity therefore gives the asserted signs on either side of \(1/2\).
182: No curvature conclusion at exactly \(1/2\) is needed at this stage.
183: \end{proof}
184: 
185: \begin{proposition}[Two-volume reduction]\label{pre:reduction}
186: If every minimizer at \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) is standard, then
187: \(I=F\) on \([0,1]\), and every minimizer is one of the standard regions
188: of Lemma~\ref{pre:standard}.
189: \end{proposition}
190: 
191: \begin{proof}
192: The assumption, existence, and Lemma~\ref{pre:standard} give \(I=F\) at
193: \(V_1,V_2\), and hence also at \(1-V_2,1-V_1\).
194: On each open interval between consecutive points in
195: \[
196:  0,\ V_1,\ V_2,\ 1-V_2,\ 1-V_1,\ 1,
197: \]
198: the function \(F\) is smooth and
199: \begin{equation}
200:  F''+\frac{(F')^2}{F}\ge0.
201:  \label{pre:branch-ode}
202: \end{equation}
203: This follows directly for its power-law and constant branches.
204: 
205: If \(I-F\) had a negative minimum on one of the closed intervals,
206: it would occur in its interior. A minimizer there must be nonstandard
207: by Lemma~\ref{pre:standard}. For its upper support,
208: \(C=I<F\), \(C'=F'\), and \(C''\ge F''\) at contact. Hence
209: \[
210:  C''+\frac{(C')^2}{C}
211:        \ge F''+\frac{(F')^2}{F}\ge0,
212: \]
213: whereas \eqref{pre:parallel} makes the left-hand side
214: \(\pi(1-g)/C^2<0\). Thus \(I=F\) throughout.
215: 
216: Now consider any nonstandard minimizer at an interior point of one of
217: the smooth intervals. Its support touches \(F=I\) from above, so
218: \(C'=F'\) and \(C''\ge F''\), giving the same contradiction with \(C=F\).
219: The partition points are covered by the hypothesis and complementation.
220: In particular \(1/2\) belongs to the smooth constant interval, so it is
221: included. Lemma~\ref{pre:standard} completes the equality classification.
222: \end{proof}
223: 
224: The profile comparison and the classification inside each smooth branch
225: are Milman's Theorem~1.3 and Propositions~4.4--4.6 \cite{Milman}, based
226: on the differential method of Hauswirth--P\'erez--Romon--Ros.
227: The endpoint hypothesis here additionally excludes nonstandard
228: co-minimizers at the transitions. Establishing that hypothesis is the
229: task of the remaining geometric argument.
