212:       We now briefly describe the strategy of the proof of Theorem 1.1. The first step is to show
213: that strict stability implies local minimality with respect to W 2,p -perturbations, see Theorem 3.9.
214: This is accomplished by constructing suitable volume-preserving flows connecting the critical set E
215: to a given close competitor F and by carefully analyzing the continuity properties of the quadratic
216: form ∂ 2 J along the flow (see Theorem 3.7). A technical difficulty in this analysis comes from the
217: translation invariance, since we have to avoid the degenerate directions at all times. This issue is
218: dealt with in Lemma 3.8, where it is shown that given any set F sufficiently W 2,p -close to E, one
219: can always find a translation of F such that the function describing the boundary of the new set
220: has small component in T ⊥ (∂E).
221:       The second step is to show that any W 2,p -local minimizer is in fact an L1 -local minimizer.
222: This is done by a contradiction argument: we assume that there exists a sequence Eh of sets such
223: that |Eh | = |E|, and Eh → E in L1 , but inequality (1.4) fails along the sequence. Then, following
224: an idea used in [16] for a two dimensional problem related to epitaxial growth, we replace the
225: sequence Eh with a new sequence Fh of minimizers of suitable penalized problems, tailored in
226: such a way that (1.4) still fails. Using regularity techniques we then show that in fact the sets Fh
227: have uniformly bounded curvatures and converge to E strongly in W 2,p , thus contradicting the
228: W 2,p -local minimality of E. A penalization approach via regularity has been recently used also in
