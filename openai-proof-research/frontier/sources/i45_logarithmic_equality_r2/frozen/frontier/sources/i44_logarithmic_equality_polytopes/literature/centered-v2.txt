                                                      The logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski and logarithmic Minkowski inequalities are proved for
                                                  convex bodies whose centroids are at the origin, with equality precisely for independent positive
                                                  dilations of common direct summands. This characterizes when centered convex bodies have
                                                  the same cone-volume measure. The Lp Brunn–Minkowski and Minkowski inequalities are also
                                                  obtained for every p > 0, with equality only for positive dilates, and the corresponding uniqueness
                                                  of centered Lp surface area measures is established.

    The volume and mixed-volume inequalities for p ≥ 1 belong to the classical Brunn–Minkowski–
Firey theory [14, 22, 28]; at p = 1, centering removes the translation freedom in the classical
equality case. The conclusions for 0 < p < 1 follow here for arbitrary centered pairs, without a
symmetry assumption. The optimized inequalities are formulated on all bodies containing the origin
and provide the common proof. The exception at p = n in (1.11) is forced by the homogeneity
Sp (cK, ·) = cn−p Sp (K, ·), not by a loss of regularity.
    The logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture was introduced by Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and
Zhang [6]. They proved the origin-symmetric planar case, including its equality cases, and related
the volume inequality to the logarithmic Minkowski inequality. The centered formulations, with the
common direct-sum equality structure, are recorded in [3, Conjectures 3.2 and 3.4]. The distinction
between centering and symmetry is essential: a logarithmic Wulff combination of centered bodies
need not be centered, and the odd translation modes cannot simply be discarded.
    Several earlier results impose geometric symmetries or special structure. Saroglou proved
the logarithmic inequality for unconditional pairs [27]. Böröczky and Kalantzopoulos treated
pairs invariant under common systems of linear reflections whose fixed hyperplanes intersect
only at the origin, and analyzed equality [5]. Rotem obtained the complex-body case through
complex interpolation [26]. Van Handel established the local logarithmic inequality for origin-
symmetric zonoids [31], while Tao, Xiong and Xiong studied the logarithmic Minkowski inequality
and uniqueness for cylinders [30]. These hypotheses are distinct from the unrestricted centroid
condition used here.
    A complementary line of work studies the second variation of volume. Colesanti, Livshyts
and Marsiglietti established a local logarithmic result near the Euclidean ball [9]. Kolesnikov
and Milman developed the spectral formulation of local Lp inequalities [20]; Chen, Huang, Li
and Liu obtained the global symmetric inequality for p sufficiently close to one, and proved the
equivalence of the local and global symmetric formulations [8]; Putterman gave another proof of the
equivalence [25]. Milman’s centro-affine approach gives further curvature-dependent and isomorphic
logarithmic Minkowski inequalities [23]. More recently, Iffland treated bodies of revolution, allowing
nonsymmetric comparison bodies under a weighted centering condition [17]; this condition is not
the ordinary centroid condition on the comparison body. Lu proved the global symmetric inequality

under n − 2 common orthogonal reflection symmetries [21]. Xi gave a new planar argument using
dilation position [32].
    The connection with cone-volume measures also has an existence side. Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang
and Zhang characterized the nonzero finite even measures realized by origin-symmetric convex
bodies through the subspace concentration condition [7]. Henk and Linke established the subspace
concentration inequality for centered polytopes [16], and Böröczky and Henk extended it to general
centered convex bodies [4]. These necessary concentration inequalities do not constitute an existence
theorem for arbitrary noneven data. Theorem 1.3 instead classifies the ambiguity between existing
centered realizations; the direct-sum exceptions are consistent with the cylinder phenomena in [30].
    The analytic starting point is the moment-coordinate argument of the September 23, 2026 preprint
[24], which states the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality for arbitrary origin-symmetric convex
bodies. Its compact adjoint identities and tensor calculation are reproduced below without symmetry
assumptions. The retained-remainder approach to equality comes from the predecessor manuscript
[29]. The present proof replaces the even test space by the orthogonal complement of all affine
functions and allows the limiting target to carry a nonconstant exponential density. Neither the
symmetric volume theorem nor an equality classification is used as an unproved implication in the
argument below.
    The moment-measure results of Berman–Berndtsson and Cordero-Erausquin–Klartag supply the
underlying coordinates [1, 10]. Klartag’s estimate bounds their Hessian from above [18], and Fathi
identifies the target-coordinate Hessian as a Stein kernel [12]. Related links between homogeneous
variance inequalities and local Lp inequalities were developed by Kolesnikov, Livshyts and Rotem
[19]. The equality limit uses quantitative stability of moment measures [2], local convergence of
log-concave densities [11], and interior Monge–Ampère estimates [13]. The first variation of the
optimized functional is obtained from the weighted Aleksandrov formula in [15, Lemma 2.4].
    The centered class need not be preserved by Wulff interpolation. To avoid applying an inequality
outside its hypotheses, we work with

3     Proof of Theorem 1.1
    We first record the analytic facts that are used for both functional concavity and its equality
limit. The remaining argument is the proof of Theorem 1.1; its equality part establishes a stronger
implication for affine segments of H, needed in Section 4.

3.1    Moment coordinates and analytic preliminaries

potential with gradient image equal to the interior of the body, unique up to source translation.

Authors’ note. Since 2018, the first two authors—then fourth-year undergraduate students—have
been studying the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality and the non-symmetric logarithmic
Minkowski problem under the supervision of the third author. Over the subsequent eight years, we
continued to think about these problems and obtained some results concerning the existence and
uniqueness of solutions to the non-symmetric logarithmic Minkowski problem.
    After OpenAI released a preprint giving a proof of the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality
for origin-symmetric convex bodies, the second author, with the assistance of GPT, obtained a
complete characterization of the equality cases together with a proof. We then provided GPT with
both the proof of the origin-symmetric logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality and the argument
for its equality cases, and asked it to investigate the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture for
general convex bodies with centroid at the origin. Approximately half an hour later, GPT proposed
the core idea underlying the proof developed in the present paper. Through many subsequent rounds

                                                         31
of interaction, the arguments leading to the main theorems of the current manuscript gradually
took shape.
    We used several separate GPT sessions to conduct adversarial checks of the key calculations,
limiting arguments, equality cases, and possible logical gaps. These checks do not substitute for
independent human verification. The authors have not yet completed an independent verification of
the manuscript, and we therefore present this work as a proposed proof requiring further careful
scrutiny. We will continue to examine the manuscript line by line and continue to refine both the
mathematical arguments and the exposition.
    We do not intend to submit this manuscript to a traditional academic journal. We hope to
make this work openly and fully available to the mathematical community, both in the hope that it
may provide useful material for future work in the area and to commemorate the problems that
accompanied us throughout much of our doctoral research.
    Finally, we are deeply grateful to Károly J. Böröczky, Erwin Lutwak, Deane Yang, and Gaoyong
Zhang for formulating the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture for origin-symmetric convex
bodies, and to Martin Henk for proposing its extension to convex bodies with centroid at the origin.
These beautiful and challenging problems have inspired our research for many years.
