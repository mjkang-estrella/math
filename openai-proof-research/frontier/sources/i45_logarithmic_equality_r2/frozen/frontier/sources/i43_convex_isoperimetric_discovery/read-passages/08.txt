36: \begin{abstract}
37: We prove the isoperimetric conjecture for the cubic flat three-torus
38: and classify all minimizers, including the equality cases at the
39: transition volumes $4\pi/81$ and $1/\pi$. The minimizing regions are balls,
40: circular tubes about shortest closed geodesics, coordinate slabs, and
41: their complements.
42: \end{abstract}
43: 
44: \section{Introduction}
45: \label{intro:section}
46: 
47: The isoperimetric conjecture for the cubic flat three-torus predicts a
48: particularly simple sequence of minimizing regions: balls, circular tubes
49: about shortest closed geodesics, and slabs between parallel coordinate tori,
50: followed by their complements.  It is a concrete instance of the periodic
51: isoperimetric problem, in which the local Euclidean geometry coexists with
52: global topological constraints.  We resolve this conjecture and classify
53: every minimizing region, including all equality cases at the two transition
54: volumes.
55: 
56: \begin{theorem}\label{intro:main}
57: For \(V\in(0,1)\), put \(v=\min(V,1-V)\).
58: Among finite-perimeter subsets of \(\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3\)
59: of volume \(V\), the minimum perimeter is
60: \begin{equation}
61:  I_{\rm unit}(V)=
62:  \min\left\{(36\pi)^{1/3}v^{2/3},\ 2\sqrt{\pi v},\ 2\right\}.
63:  \label{intro:profile}
64: \end{equation}
65: For \(0<V\le1/2\), every minimizing region is, up to an ambient
66: isometry and a null set, one of the following:
67: \[
68: \begin{array}{c|c}
69: \text{volume range}&\text{region}\\ \hline
70: 0<V\le 4\pi/81 & \text{a ball of radius }(3V/(4\pi))^{1/3}\\[2pt]
71: 4\pi/81\le V\le1/\pi
72:  &\text{a solid circular tube of radius }\sqrt{V/\pi}\\
73:  &\text{about a shortest closed geodesic}\\[2pt]
74: 1/\pi\le V\le1/2
75:  &\text{a slab of width }V\text{ between coordinate tori}.
76: \end{array}
77: \]
78: Both listed types occur at each common endpoint of adjacent ranges,
79: and there are no further equality cases.
80: For \(V>1/2\), the minimizing regions are the complements of those
81: at volume \(1-V\). At zero or full volume the conclusion is trivial.
82: \end{theorem}
83: 
84: The ball--cylinder--slab prediction appears explicitly in the periodic
85: isoperimetric problem of Hauswirth, P\'erez, Romon, and Ros
86: \cite[Introduction]{HPRR} and in Ros's account of the cubic torus and cube
87: \cite[Section~1.6]{Ros}. Reflection relates the periodic problem to
88: relative perimeter in a cube: the canonical regions become an eighth
89: ball about a vertex, a quarter cylinder about an edge, and a region
90: cut off by a coordinate plane. The remaining geometric difficulty is to exclude
91: connected boundaries of higher genus.
92: 
93: Several parts of the predicted profile were known. Hadwiger proved
94: minimality of a coordinate cut at half volume for the cube
95: \cite{Hadwiger}; Barthe and Maurey recovered this result by Gaussian comparison
96: \cite[Theorem~7 and the following discussion]{BartheMaurey}.
97: Morgan and Johnson's sharp small-volume comparison implies that balls
98: minimize on a compact flat manifold for sufficiently small volumes
99: \cite[Theorem~4.4]{MorganJohnson}.
100: Acerbi, Fusco, and Morini proved that coordinate slabs are the only
101: minimizers near half volume in the cubic three-torus, up to translations
102: and coordinate permutations \cite[Theorem~5.3]{AcerbiFuscoMorini}.
103: These small-volume and near-half-volume neighborhoods were obtained
104: without effective bounds reaching the proposed transitions.
105: 
106: The geometric tools also have a substantial history. Coordinate
107: reflection symmetry for orthogonal translation lattices is attributed
108: to Hsiang in Ritor\'e's thesis \cite[Theorem~1.19]{RitoreThesis}.
109: Ritor\'e and Ros developed the low-genus stability and structure
110: results for flat three-manifolds \cite{RitoreRos92,RitoreRos96}.
111: Hauswirth, P\'erez, Romon, and Ros established the connectedness,
112: genus and curvature--area estimates used here and the differential
113: comparison method for the isoperimetric profile \cite{HPRR}.
114: The coordinate graph property after reflection is Ritor\'e's
115: observation, recorded with its stability proof by
116: Ros \cite[Proposition~2.3]{Ros}.
117: 
118: Milman obtained effective ranges for the canonical phases, including
119: the cubic spherical range up to volume fraction $0.120582\ldots$
120: \cite[Theorem~1.2]{Milman}. He also made explicit the reduction of the
121: profile conjecture to $4\pi/81$ and $1/\pi$, using the method of
122: Hauswirth, P\'erez, Romon, and Ros
123: \cite[Theorem~1.3 and Section~4.3]{Milman}. This reduction classifies
124: all minimizers inside the smooth branches once the endpoint profile
125: values are known; additional co-minimizers at the transitions still
126: have to be excluded. We establish those endpoint exclusions directly.
127: 
128: The main new ingredient is a quantitative use of nested planar
129: sections at the cylinder--slab transition. Milman's base-profile
130: comparison identifies the lack of nesting among planar minimizers as
131: an obstruction to sharpness \cite[Corollary~2.8 and the surrounding
132: discussion]{Milman}. Here the planar isoperimetric inequality alone
133: leaves a deficit in the central curvature range. A calibration of the
134: complementary corner in the bottom section controls both edge extents;
135: nesting then forces a definite length excess in intermediate strip
136: sections. A Jensen estimate and the section flux identity turn that
137: excess into a strict bound for the total area. The other configurations
138: are excluded by scalar inequalities, and explicit outward arithmetic
139: bounds complete the remaining one-variable estimate.
140: 
141: Section~\ref{pre:section} reduces the classification to
142: \(V_1=4\pi/81\) and \(V_2=1/\pi\).
143: Reflection and volume-constrained stability place any nonstandard
144: minimizer in a coordinate-monotone position in a unit cube, with one of
145: the two vertex patterns of Section~\ref{geo:section}.
146: Section~\ref{slice:section} derives the flux, projection, and coarea
147: identities for planar sections. Section~\ref{slice:early-exclusions} uses them to
148: exclude the four-vertex pattern at both volumes and the three-vertex
149: pattern at \(V_1\).
150: Section~\ref{nest:section} improves the planar lower bound for the
151: remaining three-vertex pattern at \(V_2\).
152: Section~\ref{completion:section} assembles the endpoint exclusions with
153: the two-volume reduction and restores the unit-torus normalization.
154: Appendix~\ref{num:elementary-estimates} proves the scalar inequality
155: used to exclude the four-vertex pattern, and Appendix~\ref{num:section}
156: certifies the remaining one-variable bounds.
157: The reproducible exact calculations are included with the source.

165: \section{Completion of the proof}
166: \label{completion:section}
167: 
168: \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{intro:main}]
169: By Proposition~\ref{pre:reduction}, it suffices to exclude nonstandard
170: minimizers at \(V_1,V_2\) in the normalized period-two torus.
171: Proposition~\ref{geo:patterns} leaves the three-vertex and four-vertex
172: patterns. Proposition~\ref{slice:four-vertex} excludes the four-vertex
173: pattern at both volumes, and Proposition~\ref{slice:three-first}
174: excludes the three-vertex pattern at \(V_1\).
175: 
176: Suppose the latter pattern occurs at \(V_2\).
177: Its occupied-side choice and
178: Proposition~\ref{slice:three-second-parameters} give
179: \(h\in[.64,2.25]\) with \(A\le1\).
180: Proposition~\ref{nest:area-bound} gives
181: \[
182:  A-1\ge
183:  \begin{cases}
184:  B(h)+S(h),&1.02\le h\le1.62,\\
185:  B(h),&\text{otherwise}.
186:  \end{cases}
187: \]
188: Proposition~\ref{num:scalar-positive} makes the right-hand side strictly
189: positive throughout the required interval, contradicting \(A\le1\).
190: 
191: The two-volume reduction and Lemma~\ref{pre:standard} now give all
192: volumes and all equality cases. Returning to the unit torus multiplies
193: normalized area by two and divides lengths by two. This yields
194: \eqref{intro:profile} and the radii and widths stated in the theorem.
195: \end{proof}
