1: \section{Introduction}
2: \label{sec:introduction}
3: 
4: For a convex body $K\subset\R^n$, its projection body $\Pi K$ is the
5: origin-symmetric convex body whose support function is
6: \[
7:  h_{\Pi K}(u)=\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}
8:        \bigl(\operatorname{proj}_{u^\perp}K\bigr),
9:        \qquad u\in S^{n-1}.
10: \]
11: Here a convex body is compact, convex, and has nonempty interior, and
12: $h_L(x)=\max_{y\in L}\ip{x}{y}$ denotes its support function.
13: Thus $\Pi K$ records the volumes of the orthogonal shadows of $K$ in
14: all directions. Write $|L|=\operatorname{vol}_n(L)$ and let $B_2^m$
15: be the Euclidean unit ball in $\R^m$, with volume $\kappa_m$.
16: The normalized projection volume is
17: \[
18:  R_n(K)=\frac{|\Pi K|}{|K|^{n-1}}.
19: \]
20: It is invariant under translations and invertible linear maps: the
21: classical contravariance identity is
22: $\Pi(TK)=|\det T|T^{-t}\Pi K$; see
23: \cite[Equation~(1)]{Ludwig2002}. We verify this identity directly in
24: Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
25: 
26: Petty's projection-volume conjecture asserts, for $n\ge3$, that
27: $R_n$ is minimized precisely by ellipsoids \cite{Petty1971}.
28: The ball satisfies $\Pi B_2^n=\kappa_{n-1}B_2^n$, so the proposed
29: minimum is fixed by the Euclidean case. This is an affine isoperimetric
30: problem in which directional projection data are assembled into a
31: second body before its volume is measured. The classical Petty
32: inequality for the \emph{polar} of the projection body concerns a
33: different functional; see \cite[Section~18.6]{Gardner2002}.
34: We prove the unpolarized conjecture in every dimension at least four.
35: 
36: \begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
37: For every integer $n\ge4$ and every convex body $K\subset\R^n$,
38: \begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
39:  \frac{|\Pi K|}{|K|^{n-1}}
40:  \ge \kappa_{n-1}^{\,n}\kappa_n^{\,2-n}.
41: \end{equation}
42: Equality holds if and only if $K=a+TB_2^n$ for some $a\in\R^n$ and some
43: invertible linear map $T$.
44: \end{theorem}
45: 
46: The theorem includes bodies without symmetry or boundary regularity.
47: Its equality assertion is global and imposes no proximity to an
48: ellipsoid.
49: 
50: \subsection*{Previous results and methods}
51: 
52: One approach studies a body together with its second projection body.
53: The class-reduction inequality $R_n(\Pi K)\le R_n(K)$, together with
54: its equality condition, reduces the study of minimizers to bodies
55: homothetic to $\Pi^2K$; see \cite[Sections~1--2]{Saroglou2015}.
56: Saroglou and Zvavitch proved a local minimum theorem under the
57: hypothesis that the density of the surface-area measure, after an
58: invertible linear change of variables, is sufficiently close to that
59: of the ball in $L^\infty$ \cite[Theorem~1.3]{SaroglouZvavitch2017}.
60: Ivaki established local rigidity for $C^2$ solutions of
61: $\Pi^2K=cK$ near the ball after an invertible linear change of
62: variables \cite{Ivaki2018}. These results use the harmonic structure
63: of the projection operator and the special role of the directions
64: arising from linear changes of coordinates.
65: 
66: Chen, Feng, Li, Xi, and Xu proved the unrestricted three-dimensional
67: inequality, including ellipsoid equality
68: \cite[Theorem~1.1]{ChenEtAl2026}. Mielke-Sulz proved the conjecture
69: for bodies of revolution in every dimension $n\ge3$
70: \cite{MielkeSulz2026}. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} treats arbitrary convex
71: bodies in the remaining dimensions. Combining it with the separately
72: proved three-dimensional theorem of Chen et al. establishes the
73: conjecture for every $n\ge3$; that external theorem is not used in our
74: proof for $n\ge4$.
75: 
76: Lutwak reformulated the conjecture as a lower bound for a two-body
77: integral with kernel $|u\cdot v|$ against surface-area measures
78: \cite{LutwakPetty1990}; see the explicit formulation in
79: \cite[Introduction, item~(i)]{Saroglou2015}.
80: Our reduction uses the same pairing for a broader class of positive
81: measures satisfying a lower bound on integrals of support functions.
82: 
83: Our proof uses the classical first-variation and spherical-transform
84: frameworks, but requires estimates and an affine normalization that
85: apply to arbitrary bodies. The Wulff variations are closely related
86: to the Aleksandrov variations for dual curvature measures developed
87: by Huang, Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
88: \cite{HuangLutwakYangZhang2016}. The variational representation of a
89: support functional belongs to the $L_p$ dual Minkowski framework;
90: compare Huang and Zhao \cite{HuangZhao2018}. We prove the needed
91: representation directly, including its logarithmic endpoint.
92: The cosine and Funk spectra are classical
93: \cite{OlafssonPasqualeRubin2013}; we derive their multipliers in our
94: normalization. The further tasks are a strict estimate for all norms
95: and a continuous choice of variational minimizers through changes of
96: matrix rank. The latter permits a global choice of affine coordinates,
97: where proximity to the ball is unavailable.
98: 
99: \subsection*{The argument}
100: 
101: The proof passes from a body to a positive measure on vectors.
102: After translating and normalizing $|K|=\kappa_n$, the first variation
103: of volume gives a bounded, spanning probability measure $\eta_K$ on
104: $\R^n$ such that
105: \[
106:  \int h_M\,d\eta_K\ge (|M|/\kappa_n)^{1/n}
107: \]
108: for every origin-symmetric convex body $M$.
109: The same measure represents $h_{\Pi K}(y)$ as a fixed multiple of
110: $\int|x\cdot y|\,d\eta_K(x)$.
111: Theorem~\ref{thm:bilinear} bounds the pairing
112: \[
113:  \iint|x\cdot y|\,d\eta(x)d\zeta(y)
114: \]
115: for any two bounded, spanning positive measures satisfying this
116: support-functional inequality. Applying the result to the measures
117: of $K$ and its volume-normalized projection body yields the desired
118: lower bound. Sharpness in the pairing returns equality to the
119: homothety case of the first variation of volume.
120: 
121: Two ingredients have statements independent of projection bodies.
122: Theorem~\ref{thm:norm-estimate} bounds the higher spherical harmonics
123: of a power of an arbitrary norm by a difference of its spherical
124: means. The estimate is strict except for the Euclidean norm, and no
125: smoothness is needed. Proposition~\ref{prop:representation} represents
126: a positive support functional using the gradient of a variationally
127: selected norm. A spherical sign test bounds the pairing below by a
128: bilinear form involving the cosine and Funk transforms. The strict norm estimate
129: controls the contribution of harmonics of degree at least four.
130: 
131: The degree-two component must be removed by choosing affine
132: coordinates; it is not a zero eigenspace of the sign-test operator.
133: Proposition~\ref{prop:position} makes this choice by extending the
134: family of variational minimizers continuously to singular matrices.
135: At that boundary the gauges become seminorms. An explicit calculation
136: in their missing directions gives an inward-pointing matrix field,
137: and a fixed-point argument yields an invertible matrix at which the
138: degree-two component vanishes. This position is needed for only one
139: of the two measures. The other is transformed contragrediently, which
140: preserves their pairing. These constructions and the strict norm
141: estimate also supply the equality case.
142: 
143: Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with the separate three-dimensional
144: result of Chen et al. gives several sharp inequalities in dimensions
145: $n\ge3$. Lutwak's implication yields Euclidean-ball lower bounds for
146: lower-degree projection-body ratios, in which intrinsic volumes replace
147: the volumes of shadows. Haddad's $p=1$ implication bounds the integral of
148: the absolute determinant of gradients below by the product of critical
149: Lebesgue norms. Its
150: diagonal case strengthens
151: the Sobolev--Zhang affine $L^1$ inequality. The theorem also gives the
152: sharp Holmes--Thompson isoperimetric inequality in normed spaces, where
153: equality requires an ellipsoidal norm ball and a body homothetic to it.
154: The full statements below distinguish the hypotheses and equality
155: conclusions of these consequences.
156: 
157: Section~\ref{sec:gauges} establishes the volume and gauge facts used in
158: the reduction. Section~\ref{sec:spherical} derives the sign test and
159: its harmonic multipliers. Section~\ref{sec:norm} proves the strict norm
160: estimate, including the scalar inequalities in every dimension.
161: Section~\ref{sec:position} constructs the variational representation
162: and affine position. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} proves the bilinear
163: inequality and deduces Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
164: Section~\ref{sec:consequences} develops its consequences. We use the classical
165: Brunn--Minkowski inequality with its homothety equality case and
166: Brouwer's fixed-point theorem in their stated forms; the first-variation
167: reduction, harmonic estimates, variational construction, and equality
168: argument are proved below.

170: \subsection*{Notation}
171: 
172: Throughout Sections~\ref{sec:gauges}--\ref{sec:conclusion}, $n\ge4$,
173: $p=n-1$, and $\sigma$ is uniform probability on
174: $S^{n-1}$. The symbol $\E$ denotes integration against $\sigma$, and
175: \[
176:  b_n=\E|u_1|.
177: \]
178: Repeated spherical integrals use independent directions.
179: A symmetric body always means an origin-symmetric body.
180: When a function on the sphere is used as a degree-one homogeneous test
181: on vectors, it is extended by positive homogeneity and given value zero
182: at the origin. Euclidean gradients are denoted by $\nabla$; the
183: tangential gradient and Laplacian are $\gradS$ and $\lapS$.
