1: \section{From a variance bound to volume}
2: \label{sec:reduction}
3: 
4: We first deduce Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from the variance bound below.
5: For a probability measure $\mu$, write
6: $\E_\mu f=\int f\,d\mu$ and
7: $\Var_\mu(f)=\E_\mu(f-\E_\mu f)^2$.
8: 
9: \begin{proposition}[Variance bound]\label{prop:variance}
10: Let $n\ge2$, $N\ge1$, $q\ge2$ be an even integer, and $\eps>0$.
11: Let $p_1,\ldots,p_N\in\R^n\setminus\{0\}$ and $h_1,\ldots,h_N>0$.
12: Set
13: \begin{equation}\label{eq:potential}
14:  \psi_i(x)=\left(\frac{\langle p_i,x\rangle}{h_i}\right)^q,
15:  \qquad V(x)=\frac{\eps}{2}|x|^2+\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i(x),
16:  \qquad d\mu=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\,dx,
17: \end{equation}
18: where $Z=\int_{\R^n}e^{-V(x)}\,dx$. For all real $b_1,\ldots,b_N$,
19: \begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
20:  \Var_\mu\left(\sum_{i=1}^N b_i\psi_i\right)
21:  \le\sum_{i=1}^N b_i^2\E_\mu\psi_i.
22: \end{equation}
23: \end{proposition}
24: 
25: Here the Gaussian term makes $Z$ finite, even if the vectors $p_i$
26: do not span $\R^n$. All polynomial moments are finite. The proof of
27: Proposition~\ref{prop:variance} occupies Sections~\ref{sec:moment}--\ref{sec:variance}.
28: 
29: 
30: The proof will establish the dual estimate
31: \[
32:  \sum_{i=1}^N\frac{(\E_\mu u\psi_i)^2}{\E_\mu\psi_i}
33:  \le \E_\mu u^2
34:  \qquad\text{for every centered even }u\in L^2(\mu).
35: \]
36: Here centered means $\E_\mu u=0$, and the denominators are positive
37: because each $p_i\ne0$ and $\mu$ has a positive density.
38: Applied to $u=\sum_i b_i\psi_i-\E_\mu\sum_i b_i\psi_i$, this gives
39: \eqref{eq:variance} by Cauchy--Schwarz.  We first prove the dual estimate
40: on a class of smooth compactly supported tests, then use density to
41: reach every such $u$.  This explains the roles of the density and
42: tensor lemmas in the analytic proof.
43: 
44: \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}]
45: Suppose first that $n\ge2$. Fix nonzero spanning vectors
46: $p_1,\ldots,p_N$ and positive endpoint widths $h_i^0,h_i^1$. For
47: $t\in[0,1]$, define
48: \[
49:  h_i(t)=(h_i^0)^{1-t}(h_i^1)^t,\qquad
50:  P(t)=\{x:|\langle p_i,x\rangle|\le h_i(t)\text{ for all }i\}.
51: \]
52: The spanning condition makes each $P(t)$ bounded; positivity of the
53: widths gives nonempty interior. Use these widths in \eqref{eq:potential}
54: and denote the resulting integral by $Z_{q,\eps}(t)$.
55: For fixed $q,\eps$, its first two derivatives may be taken under the
56: integral: on a compact interval of $t$, the differentiated integrands
57: are bounded by a polynomial times $e^{-\eps|x|^2/2}$.
58: With $b_i=-q\log(h_i^1/h_i^0)$ one has
59: \[
60:  \dot V=\sum_i b_i\psi_i,\qquad
61:  \ddot V=\sum_i b_i^2\psi_i,
62: \]
63: and hence
64: \[
65:  \frac{d^2}{dt^2}\log Z_{q,\eps}(t)
66:  =\Var_{\mu_t}(\dot V)-\E_{\mu_t}\ddot V\le0
67: \]
68: by Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}. Therefore
69: \begin{equation}\label{eq:partition-concavity}
70:  Z_{q,\eps}(\lambda)\ge
71:  Z_{q,\eps}(0)^{1-\lambda}Z_{q,\eps}(1)^\lambda.
72: \end{equation}
73: 
74: Keep $\eps>0$ fixed and let $q$ tend to infinity through even integers.
75: For each fixed $t$, the integrands converge almost everywhere to
76: $e^{-\eps|x|^2/2}\mathbf1_{P(t)}$. The exceptional set lies in the
77: finitely many hyperplanes $\langle p_i,x\rangle=\pm h_i(t)$ and
78: has measure zero. The common integrable bound
79: $e^{-\eps|x|^2/2}$ gives
80: \[
81:  Z_{q,\eps}(t)\longrightarrow
82:  \int_{P(t)}e^{-\eps|x|^2/2}\,dx.
83: \]
84: Apply this at $t=0,\lambda,1$ in \eqref{eq:partition-concavity}, then
85: let $\eps\downarrow0$. Monotone convergence gives
86: \begin{equation}\label{eq:slabs}
87:  |P(\lambda)|\ge |P(0)|^{1-\lambda}|P(1)|^\lambda.
88: \end{equation}

91: 
92: Figure~\ref{fig:slab-exhaustion} illustrates how additional slab
93: directions refine an outer approximation.  To pass to all directions,
94: let $K,L$ be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Choose nested finite
95: sets $D_m\subset S^{n-1}$ that contain the coordinate vectors and
96: whose union is dense in the sphere. Define
97: \[
98:  P_m(t)=\bigcap_{p\in D_m}
99:  \{x:|\langle p,x\rangle|\le h_K(p)^{1-t}h_L(p)^t\}.
100: \]
101: The bodies $P_m(t)$ decrease with $m$ and are all contained in the bounded
102: body $P_1(t)$. By continuity of the support functions and their
103: evenness,
104: \[
105:  \bigcap_m P_m(t)=\mathcal W[h_K^{1-t}h_L^t].
106: \]
107: Indeed any point satisfying the dense set of inequalities satisfies
108: all of them by continuity in the direction. Conversely, the Wulff
109: inequalities imply the absolute-value constraints by evenness.
110: At $t=0,1$ these intersections are $K,L$, respectively, by the
111: supporting-half-space characterization of a convex body.
112: Continuity of measure from above, applied at $0,\lambda,1$ in
113: \eqref{eq:slabs}, proves \eqref{eq:main}.
114: 
115: Finally, in dimension one write $K=[-a,a]$ and $L=[-b,b]$ with
116: $a,b>0$. Their Wulff interpolation is the interval with radius
117: $a^{1-\lambda}b^\lambda$, whose length is
118: $(2a)^{1-\lambda}(2b)^\lambda$. Thus \eqref{eq:main} is equality.
119: The endpoints $\lambda=0,1$ are equality in every dimension.
120: \end{proof}
