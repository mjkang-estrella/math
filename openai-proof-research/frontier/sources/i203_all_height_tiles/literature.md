## External primary papers read in place

**Mihail N. Kolountzakis, Lattice Tilings by Cubes: Whole, Notched and Extended**, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 5 (1998), R14. [Publisher PDF](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/viewFile/v5i1r14/pdf/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.37236/1352). Read the full 11-page retained publisher PDF. Theorem 2 is the lattice Fourier criterion. Sections 2.1–2.3, especially (13)–(20), give notched/extended-square identities. Their positive-product branch already supplies the square-covering operation used here. I203 does not claim a new notched-square theorem. Its new-to-project conclusion is the explicit optimal shape/lattice selection for an entire elliptic path, not the single-matrix square-covering identity.

**Sigrid Grepstad and Mihail N. Kolountzakis, Bounded common fundamental domains for two lattices**, [arXiv:2507.00604v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00604v1), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2025.110776). Read the introduction, the exact Theorems 1–2, notation and references from I201's retained copy (pp. 1–3, part of 4 and 12–13). Theorem 1 gives a bounded finite-polytopal common fundamental domain for any two equal-volume lattices. This is an endpoint theorem; it neither asserts coverage by a selected domain at intermediate heights nor fixes the all-height optimum. Its discussion also distinguishes multiple-lattice questions from Steinhaus. The entire proof was not reread in this round.

**Sigrid Grepstad, Mihail N. Kolountzakis and Emmanuil Spyridakis, Bounded lattice tiles that pack with another lattice**, [arXiv:2508.07972v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07972v1). Read the introduction through Theorem 1.2 and its containing-tile interpretation, from I201's retained copy (pp. 1–4). A tile of the denser lattice that packs the sparser one can be extended to a bounded tile of the sparser lattice. Thus two unequal-volume lattices do admit a common cover at the larger covolume. This does not solve three or continuum constraints. I203 uses no claim to have reread the later proof.

## New retained full-read primary papers

**Mihail N. Kolountzakis and Effie Papageorgiou, Functions tiling with several lattices**, [arXiv:2106.11701v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11701v1), later Journal of Fourier Analysis and Applications 28, 68 (2022). Full 16-page v1 text read, retained as `kolountzakis-papageorgiou-2021v1.pdf`. Theorem 2.3 gives examples requiring support diameter linear in the number of lattices. Section 2 distinguishes convolution functions, indicator tiles and support size; Section 3 distinguishes bounded set-theoretic domains from measurable ones; Section 4 develops finite-group models. These distinctions motivated the quantitative mesh/diameter check and the fractional obstruction route. The I203 set-cover construction does not follow by rounding their soft tiles. Their introduction and §2.1 discuss the Steinhaus Fourier-zero obstruction; I203 has zeros forced only at its endpoints, not the full constant-covolume rotation family.

**Maria Loukaki and Emmanouil Spyridakis, Three subgroup common transversal in abelian groups**, [arXiv:2606.14319v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14319v1). Full 28-page v1 text read, retained as `loukaki-spyridakis-2026v1.pdf`. Theorem A classifies common transversals of three equal-index subgroups of a finite abelian group by a Sylow-2 obstruction. Theorem B transfers this to three equal-volume full-rank lattices with discrete sum. Section 8 identifies the elementary parity example already present in Kolountzakis 1997. These hypotheses do not fit three distinct heights of the elliptic path, which have unequal covolumes unless the heights are opposites. The paper supplies no obstruction to the constructed varying-covolume covers. Reading was for comparison, not a source audit or a second research family.

## Search scope and novelty boundary

Primary searches were run early for multiple-lattice common domains, measurable Steinhaus sets and simultaneous covers. The 2026 three-subgroup paper was identified during that check. After the explicit matrices were found, searches were repeated for simultaneous planar lattice covers, deformed square covers, rotation/contraction, trace-zero matrices, and elliptic parallelogram covers. The Kolountzakis author publication list was consulted for source identification. Most other hits concerned elliptic curves, ordinary sphere covering, finite lattice-point counts, or the union of rotated disk arrangements; these were excluded. Search pages and metadata were not retained or byte-authenticated.

No worldwide priority assertion rests on those searches. The pointwise square lemma has explicit prior overlap. The nomination is the all-k optimal construction for the precise unrestricted-shape/unrestricted-lattice problem, verified by the identity in the analytic record.
