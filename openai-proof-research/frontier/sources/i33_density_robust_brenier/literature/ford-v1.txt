                                                 QUANTITATIVE STABILITY, COERCIVITY AND UNIQUENESS OF
                                                               OPTIMAL TRANSPORT PLANS


                                                                                           WILLIAM FORD1


                                                   Abstract. For probability measures on Rd supported in fixed compact sets, we prove that
                                                   quadratic optimal transport plans are quantitatively stable in Wasserstein distance under per-
                                                   turbation of both marginal measures, assuming that one of the initial measures satisfies an
                                                   upper Ahlfors regularity condition with exponent strictly greater than d − 1. Under the same
                                                   assumption, we also prove novel quantitative stability results for optimal transport maps.




arXiv:2609.35574v1 [math.OC] 28 Sep 2026
                                                      Furthermore, we prove a coercivity theorem which states that any probability measure on the
                                                   product space must be quantitatively close to the set of optimal plans, if it has similar marginals
                                                   and a similar transport cost to the optimal value.
                                                      Finally, we prove a quantitative uniqueness theorem which acts as a quantitative counterpart
                                                   to Brenier’s theorem. The Wasserstein diameter of the set of optimal plans is controlled by the
                                                   distance of one marginal measure to a regular measure for which uniqueness holds. In this way,
                                                   “almost uniqueness” of optimal plans is quantified by the source measure being “almost regular”.
                                                      Examples are provided which prove that the exponents of all estimates are sharp.
                                                   Keywords: optimal transport, quantitative stability, uniqueness, coercivity, Brenier theorem

1.1. Contributions. For a subset E of Euclidean space, denote by P(E) the set of probability
measures on E. Throughout this paper we assume d ≥ 2. For marginals ρ and µ, we denote by
Γ(ρ, µ) the set of all solutions to problem (1.1), and γρ→µ will denote any optimiser to (1.1).
   The four main results of this paper are stated below: stability of optimal plans under perturba-
tion of both marginals and optimal maps with fixed source, coercivity of the objective with respect
to transport plans on R2d with arbitrary marginals, and quantitative uniqueness of solutions near
a regular source measure. All the theorems are proven in the text for Wq distances between plans
and Lq between maps, for any q ≥ 1. In the introduction we present only the case q = 2 for
readability. The principal assumption in all theorems is that one of the measures satisfies the
following regularity condition.

1.2. Existing results. Simple examples in [19] involving discrete measures show that without
any regularity assumptions, problem (1.1) is not quantitatively stable. OT stability literature can
be divided into two categories of assumptions: local stability in the neighbourhood of a transport
problem with a regular map/potential, and local stability in the neighbourhood of a transport
problem with at least one regular marginal measure. All stability results in this paper are of the
second type.
   For the first type of result, in [23] it is proved that with fixed probability density ρ and some
target µ, if Tρ→µ is Lipschitz, then OT maps are locally quantitatively stable in L2 (ρ) with respect
to W1 perturbations of the target µ. This is generalised to bi-marginal plan stability under the
same assumption in [29]. Assuming the transport from ρ0 to µ0 has a class C 1,1 Brenier potential
(see (1.7) below for the definition), Li and Nochetto establish the control
                                                                                         12
          ∀ρ1 ∈ P(X )∀µ1 ∈ P(Y), W2 (γρ0 →µ0 , γρ1 →µ1 ) ≲ W2 (ρ0 , ρ1 ) + W2 (µ0 , µ1 ) ,

for a constant depending on X , Y, and the Lipschitz constant of ∇ϕρ0 →µ0 . We also mention [21]
which adapts this assumption to a larger class of transport costs, as well as [31] which derives
statistical results under related regularity assumptions. Unfortunately, in applications, such reg-
ularity assumptions can be difficult to verify and need not hold. OT maps are often not even
continuous, let alone Lipschitz, even between regular densities.

   Coercivity of problem (1.1) has also received some study. In [29], Li and Nochetto prove that
any transport problem satisfying the same C 1,1 regularity assumption on a Brenier potential is
coercive in a similar sense to our Theorem 4.2 presented above. In [33], Mérigot proves two
coercivity results for plans and maps with respect to both dual and primal suboptimality with
fixed marginals. These form the basis for our Theorem 4.5 and Theorem 4.4. We also mention
[10] which proves coercivity of the distance of a plan to the subgradient of the Brenier potential
with respect to the primal suboptimality gap, as well as [12, 19] which prove a strong convexity
inequality for the dual problem which acts as a dual coercivity result for Brenier potentials.
   The current literature on uniqueness of the primal problem is, to the best of our knowledge,
only qualitative. The foundational results of Brenier and McCann [8, 32] state that if ρ vanishes
on all sets of Hausdorff dimension d − 1, then solutions to (1.1) are unique for any µ, and the
optimal plan is induced by a transport map. Our assumption A(M, θ) can be in some sense seen
as a quantitative strengthening of this Brenier-McCann hypothesis. The sharp hypotheses for
qualitative uniqueness have since been identified as slightly weaker. To guarantee that the optimal
transport is unique and map induced for any µ, it is necessary and sufficient that ρ vanishes on
any hypersurface which can be locally written as the graph of a difference of convex functions;
see [24, 34]. For the dual OT problem, quantitative uniqueness has been recently investigated
by the author, in [20]. Here almost connectedness of one marginal measure’s support implies a
quantitative bound on the diameter of the set of optimal dual potentials.

4.4. Stability of optimal maps. The following theorem provides a stability bound for optimal
transport maps with respect to perturbation of both marginals, again extending a result [33,
Theorem 3.3] to general exponents q and θ, as well as improving the numerical constant.

for optimal transport maps. In general, as soon as both marginals are perturbed, a Brenier map
Tρ1 →µ1 need not exist, since ∂ϕρ1 →µ1 may be multivalued on a set of positive ρ1 measure. Here

of any convex function is well defined ρ0 almost everywhere.

choice for what values it should take outside the support of ρ1 , and so the gradients of two different
