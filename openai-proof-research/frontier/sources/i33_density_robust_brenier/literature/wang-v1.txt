                                                                                    Abstract
                                                      We prove quantitative stability estimates for quadratic optimal transport
                                                  maps by regularizing a Brenier potential and estimating the error in the du-
                                                  ality gap. The source has an Lp density, p > 1, relative to a probability
                                                  measure with a BV density. When one target lies in a fixed ball, we obtain
                                                  the W2 Hölder exponent (p−1)/(4p−2). The constant depends on the dimen-
                                                  sion through three explicit source quantities: the variance, the total variation
                                                  of the reference density and the Lp norm of the weight. The proof uses a
                                                  second-order bound on the averaged regularized Fenchel gap. By restricting
                                                  the slopes of the potential, we also treat targets with a bounded moment of
                                                  any order greater than two. In both estimates, the condition is imposed on
                                                  one target, while the other can be any probability measure with finite second
                                                  moment. For Gaussian and isotropic log-concave sources, the constants grow
                                                  polynomially with the dimension. For Lp changes of Gaussian measure, the
                                                  factor in the bounded-target estimate is d(p−1)/(4p−2) . For each p, a fixed
                                                  source in two dimensions proves optimality of both stability exponents in this
                                                  source class. A Gaussian construction provides polynomial lower bounds on
                                                  the dimension dependence.


                                          1 Introduction and main results
                                          Let ρ ∈ P2 (Rd ) be absolutely continuous. For µ ∈ P2 (Rd ), denote by Tµ = ∇ϕµ
                                          the Brenier map from ρ to µ, for the quadratic cost. Quantitative stability asks
                                          how ∥Tµ − Tν ∥L2 (ρ) depends on a Wasserstein distance between µ and ν. We allow
                                          discrete targets and impose a support or higher-moment bound on one target. All
                                          norms are Euclidean, and Wr denotes the usual r-Wasserstein distance.
                                              Stability under regularity assumptions goes back to an argument of Ambrosio,
                                          reported by Gigli [11, Proposition 3.3 and Corollary 3.4]. If the optimal map is
                                          Lipschitz, the conjugate potential is strongly convex, and the duality gap controls
                                          the squared distance to the optimal graph. Li and Nochetto [14, Proposition 3.1]
                                          formulate this coercivity for transport plans and use it to prove stability under per-
                                          turbations of both marginals. Their Theorem 3.5 treats arbitrary P2 perturbations
                                          when the reference optimal map is Lipschitz.
                                             Key words and phrases. Optimal transport, quantitative stability, regularization, convex
                                          duality, bounded variation.

     For compact targets, Berman [3] proved stability estimates with dimension-
dependent exponents under upper and lower bounds on a source density over a con-
vex body. Mérigot, Delalande and Chazal [16, Theorem 3.1] proved the dimension-
independent exponent 2/15 for uniform sources on convex bodies. Delalande and
                                               1/6
Mérigot [7, Theorem 4.2] improved this to W1 for source densities bounded above
and away from zero on a convex body. They also treated unbounded targets un-
der moment assumptions [7, Corollary 4.4]. Letrouit and Mérigot [13] used gluing
arguments to treat more general sources, including log-concave densities with full
                                                  1/4
support in Rd . Mérigot [15] proved the sharp W1 estimate for bounded, compactly
supported source densities, using quantitative regularity estimates for convex func-
tions due to Carlier, Delalande and Mérigot [5]. Ford [10] extended this approach
to upper Ahlfors regular sources and to stability of plans under changes in both
marginals.
                                                                     1/3
     The OpenAI preprint [18, Theorem 1.1] proves the sharp W2 estimate for
uniform sources on compact convex bodies in dimensions d ⩾ 2, with both targets
in a fixed compact set.
     In this paper, we consider sources given by Lp changes of BV reference densities,
with possibly unbounded support. We prove optimal Hölder exponents for W2
stability under a support or higher-moment bound on one target, with explicit
constants. The examples proving optimality use a fixed source with unbounded,
disconnected support for each p.
     We regularize the Brenier potential to obtain a Lipschitz gradient and apply
the coercivity argument above. It then remains to estimate the regularization error.
We use the Moreau–Yosida envelope [17, 1], whose conjugacy identity ϕ∗t = ϕ∗ +
t|·|2/2 makes the first-order changes of the two potentials cancel after integration
against the optimal plan. A BV integration-by-parts estimate bounds the error by
O(t2 ). Interpolation extends this estimate to Lp changes of density. Quantitative
Fenchel–Young inequalities involving proximal maps have also been used in optimal
transport by Carlier [4].
     The constants depend on the dimension through the source parameter Sp in
(2). For log-concave sources, this dependence is polynomial. For an Lp change of
Gaussian measure with a fixed bound on the weight, the factor in the bounded-
target estimate is dαp .
     In both estimates, the second target ranges over all of P2 (Rd ). For bounded,
compactly supported source densities, a one-sided support condition follows from
Mérigot [15, Theorem 3.4] and the coupling argument in Lemma 8; see Remark 9.
Our moment estimate assumes a bound of any order q > 2 on one target. The
moment estimates of Delalande–Mérigot [7, Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.4] assume
higher-moment bounds on both targets.

AI use The use of potential regularization to prove stability arose in a dialogue

stability estimate for compactly supported targets using heat regularization. The
authors then asked it to determine whether this exponent was optimal, extend the
