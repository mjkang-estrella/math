## 5. Route I — expose a minimal kernel support before testing fresh rows

This route proves an exponential separation for exact small supports. It deliberately does not turn an approximate sparse-vector net into an exact-support statement or conversely.

**Lemma S3 (three nonzero coefficients).** Put r=max(p,q), s=min(p,q), and

`a_p=max{3r^2s, r^3+2rs^2}`.

For independent p-signs X_1,X_2,X_3, every three nonzero real coefficients a,b,c and every real t satisfy

`P(a X_1+b X_2+c X_3=t)<=a_p<Q`.

Proof. The level plane contains no cube edge because no coefficient is zero. Distinct vertices in it therefore have Hamming distance at least two. An antipodal pair cannot share this plane with a third vertex: after orienting one vertex to (1,1,1), their plane has level zero; a third vertex would force the sum on the coordinates on which it differs to vanish, and the complementary sum too, forcing a singleton coefficient to vanish. If there are three vertices, their pairwise distances are two. After a cube symmetry they are three vertices in one parity class. Their equations force equal absolute coefficients, and the fourth vertex of that parity class is not in the plane. Hence there are at most three vertices. A three-vertex set of pairwise distance two has total probability one of

`3r^2s, 3rs^2, r^3+2rs^2, s^3+2r^2s`.

The first two are at most 3r^2s; the last is at most r^3+2rs^2 because their difference is `(r-s)(r^2-rs+s^2)>=0`. For a two-vertex set at distance two or three, its weight is at most `max(r^3+rs^2,r^3+s^3,2r^2s)`, again at most a_p; one vertex is also covered. Finally `3r^2s<=4/9<1/2<=Q`, and

`Q-(r^3+2rs^2)=s(r^2-rs+s^2)>0`.

This proves the strict gap for every fixed p. Conditioning on any other independent signs proves the same bound for a linear form with at least three nonzero coefficients. ∎

A **circuit support** is an inclusion-minimal nonempty support of a vector in ker A_n. For a specified support S of size k, circuit minimality implies that the n-by-k matrix A_[n],S has rank exactly k-1: two independent null vectors would have a nonzero linear combination with a canceled coordinate and smaller support. Select k-1 independent rows I; union over at most `binom(n,k-1)` choices. Expose all entries in A_I,S, respecting the symmetry by exposing each upper-triangular variable only once. If this exposed matrix has a one-dimensional nullspace with every coordinate nonzero, choose a canonical normal v (for example the first coordinate normalized to one); otherwise this choice of I cannot witness the circuit.

For j outside S union I the entries A_j,S are mutually independent p-signs. They are also independent of the exposed variables: an equality of the unordered index pairs would force j into S union I. Different such rows use disjoint upper-triangular variables. Conditional on the exposure, S3 gives probability at most a_p for each equation A_j,S v=0. The remaining rows and internal symmetric constraints can be dropped for an upper bound. Thus for `3<=k<=n/2`,

`P(some circuit of size k)<=binom(n,k)binom(n,k-1) a_p^(n-2k+1)`.       (S1)

All uncountably many candidate coefficient vectors have been replaced by the normal of finitely many exposed row sets. No net, independence between full symmetric rows, or independent lower triangle is assumed.

Let h(x)=-x log x-(1-x)log(1-x). Choose a fixed `0<delta_p<1/4` so small that

`2h(delta_p)-2delta_p log a_p <= (1/2)log(Q/a_p)`.

Such a choice exists because the left side tends to zero. Put `kappa_p=(1/2)log(Q/a_p)>0`. The usual binomial entropy bound, for k<=delta_p n, gives from S1

`P(some circuit of size 3,...,floor(delta_p n)) <= n Q^n exp(-kappa_p n)` . (S2)

The factor a_p in S1 only improves this bound. This is an explicit rate after fixing the displayed delta; constants depend on p and are not endpoint-uniform. If the range is empty the probability is zero.

**Exact elementary events.** For one pair i<j,

`P(row_i=row_j)=c3 Q^(n-2)`,

`P(row_i=-row_j)=pq(2pq)^(n-2)` .                                  (S3)

Outside the pair the first event requires equality of two independent signs per coordinate. Its 2-by-2 corner requires `A_ii=A_ij=A_jj`, giving c3. For the second, the corner requires `A_ii=A_jj=-A_ij`, giving `p^2q+pq^2=pq`, and every external coordinate gives 2pq. This pays the often-missed diagonal/shared-entry constraint.

Let `lambda_p=max(c3,Q^2)<Q`. Two different signed pair events on the same pair are disjoint. For two pairs sharing one vertex, each outside coordinate must satisfy a prescribed signed relation between three signs, whose probability is at most c3; for two disjoint pairs the four independent entries give at most Q^2. Ignoring the remaining corner constraints, each intersection is at most `lambda_p^(n-4)` for n>=4. There are fewer than 2n^4 unordered pairs of signed events. Bonferroni therefore proves the finite bound

`0<=L_n-P(E_n)<=2n^4 lambda_p^(n-4)` .                              (S4)

In particular `P(E_n)=L_n(1+O_p(n^2 (lambda_p/Q)^n))`, and the probability of two distinct pair relations, conditional on E_n, has the same exponential upper bound. This is a bound on intersections, not an independence or Poisson-law assertion.

Let S_n(delta) be the event that ker A_n has a nonzero vector supported on at most floor(delta n) coordinates. A support of size one is impossible because all entries are nonzero. A two-coordinate relation forces its two coefficients to have equal absolute value, hence gives exactly E_n. Every vector has a minimal circuit inside its support. Combining S2–S4, for all sufficiently large n,

`P(S_n(delta_p))=L_n(1+O_p(n^2 e^(-c_p n)))`,

`P(E_n^c | S_n(delta_p))=O_p(e^(-kappa_p n)/n)` .                   (S5)

Here c_p can be any smaller positive constant than both kappa_p and log(Q/lambda_p), with a changed finite multiplicative constant. This is a conditional structural classification under an **actual sparse-kernel event**, not under all singularity. It is a useful quantitative partial, proved without using the source's singularity upper bound.

**Why this route does not close M-full.** Increasing k to a macroscopic fraction consumes the exponent in the two support/row-selection binomial costs. For k near n there may be no fresh rows at all. The source's tail-compressible estimate also allows a fixed exponent slack at the leading rate Q, and its approximate vectors cannot be replaced by the exact circuits in S2. Neither bound eliminates singular matrices whose every circuit is larger than delta_p n. The full target needs a new bound on that remaining sector of size `o(n^2 Q^n)`, not just a further assertion of Var or rank concentration.

## 6. Route II — compute the planted conditional quotient and pay its changed law

This route starts on a rare event but derives its exact conditional distribution instead of assuming the unconditioned theorem is uniform in a planted row or diagonal.

Condition on `E^+_12={row_1=row_2}`. Delete the second row and column to obtain B of order n-1, and let R be the n-by-(n-1) matrix that duplicates its first coordinate. Then exactly

`A=R B R^T`, `rank A=rank B`, `corank A=1+corank B`.                (Q1)

The upper-triangular entries of B are independent **under this conditioning**, because the equality event separates into constraints on disjoint original sets of variables. Their laws are

`P(B_11=+1 | E^+_12)=p^3/c3`,

`P(B_1j=+1 | E^+_12)=p^2/Q  (j>=2)`,

`P(B_ij=+1 | E^+_12)=p  (2<=i<=j)` .                             (Q2)

Thus for p=1/2, B is exactly the smaller ordinary symmetric fair-sign matrix. If `s_m(p)=P(A_m is singular)`, the exact identity is

`P(corank A_n>=2 | E^+_12)=s_(n-1)(1/2)`  when p=1/2.             (Q3)

For general fixed p the planted law is **not** an ordinary p-law. Relative to that law its density is bounded, pointwise, by

`C_p (r/Q)^(n-2)`,  `C_p=r^2/c3`,  `r=max(p,q)`.                  (Q4)

Indeed a cross-entry density ratio is p/Q on +1 and q/Q on -1; the corner ratio is p^2/c3 or q^2/c3. Every other coordinate has ratio one. Hence

`P(corank A_n>=2 | E^+_12) <= C_p (r/Q)^(n-2) s_(n-1)(p)` .       (Q5)

Invoking only the stipulated rate `s_m(p)=exp(m log Q+o_p(m))` gives

`P(corank A_n>=2 | E^+_12) <= exp(n log r+o_p(n))`.                (Q6)

This decays exponentially because p is fixed in (0,1). For the fair case Q3 gives the sharper exact source expression. No arbitrary diagonal-change theorem, rank-one-addition theorem, or uniform bound for varying row biases has been imported; Q4 explicitly pays the only changed coordinates.

When p=1/2, a fixed diagonal sign congruence converts E^-_12 to E^+_12 and preserves the original fair law, so Q3 also holds for the opposite-pair event. When p!=1/2 such a congruence does not preserve the p-law. We do not use it: the total probability of opposite-pair events divided by the equal-pair sum is exactly

`[pq/c3] [(2pq)/Q]^(n-2)`,                                      (Q7)

which is exponentially small. Sum Q5 over equal pairs, use S4 for the denominator, and use Q7 (or Q3 at fairness) for opposite pairs. It follows that for some `d_p>0` and all sufficiently large n,

`P(corank A_n!=1 | E_n) <= C'_p exp(-d_p n)` .                    (Q8)

For example at bias one may take any d_p smaller than `min{-log r, log(Q/(2pq))}` after reducing it to absorb the source o(n); at fairness take any d_p<log 2. The threshold in n is not made effective by the source's asymptotic rate.

Two distinct signed pair relations are linearly independent (opposite signs on the same pair are impossible). Thus corank one on E_n implies a unique such pair. Combining Q8 with S5 proves the paid conditional theorem:

**M132-sparse.** For every fixed p in (0,1), there are delta_p,c_p,C_p>0 and N_p such that for every n>=N_p, conditional on S_n(delta_p), with probability at least `1-C_p exp(-c_p n)` the matrix has corank one and a unique pair of equal/opposite rows. Its sparse-event probability has the asymptotic in S5. At p!=1/2 the pair is equal, with an exponentially small conditional exception; at p=1/2 the two signs are symmetric. Constants and N_p depend on p. No conclusion about conditioning on all singularity is included.

**A larger exact planted construction.** In the fair model, fix any partition of [n] into m nonempty classes and any prescribed coordinate signs d_i. Condition on the relations `row_i=d_i d_j row_j` within each class. Apply the deterministic congruence D=diag(d_i). The resulting matrix is constant on every block of the partition. The constraints involve disjoint sets of independent upper-triangular entries; each block's common sign is independently fair, even for a diagonal block. Therefore the quotient B is exactly an m-by-m ordinary symmetric fair-sign matrix, and `A=D R B R^T D` for the class-incidence matrix R. Conditional on the specified relations,

`corank A=n-m+corank B` .                                        (Q9)

In particular, whenever m->infinity, the prescribed relation space is the entire kernel except with probability `2^(-m+o(m))`, uniformly over the fixed partition shapes and the prescribed signs. Uniformity follows from exact equality of the quotient laws, not from a uniform-source perturbation theorem. For fixed m the error is s_m and need not vanish. In the biased unsigned case a block containing w independent original entries has conditional + probability `p^w/(p^w+q^w)`; weights are `w=|C_a||C_b|` off diagonal and `w=|C_a|(|C_a|+1)/2` on diagonal. This quotient generally has many different biases and is not covered by the fixed-p source rate. We claim no extension of Q9's decay to arbitrary biased partitions.

**Countertests and exact remaining operation.** A full conditional rank theorem cannot follow just from Q1: outside E_n there is no duplicate to collapse. Moreover the unrestricted assertion that a planted equality always causes exactly one lost rank is false at finite n—B can itself be singular with the strictly positive probability in Q3. Our exponential error retains that possibility. An arbitrary diagonal modification or p_n approaching an endpoint may destroy the decay in Q4–Q6. These restrictions are necessary to the proof, not technical words hiding a stronger claim.

Q1–Q9 are useful normalized rare-event constructions, but their gain over the complete source is limited: exact conditioning, a quotient map, and an explicitly bounded density convert the source's already-paid smaller-dimensional exponential rate. Route I uses a classical circuit exposure/small-ball union bound. Neither mechanism supplies a new dense-kernel estimate. **The contribution is technical, not a ninth substantial finding.**
