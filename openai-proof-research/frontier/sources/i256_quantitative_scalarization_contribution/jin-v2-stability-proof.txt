L784: 
L785: (Compactness of closed convex sets). Let $\left({K}_{n}\right)$ be a sequence of closed convex subsets of $\mathbb{C}$ containing 0. After passage to a subsequence, there is a closed convex set ${K}_{\infty }$ containing 0 such that
L786: $$dist(\phantom{\rule{0.166667em}{0ex}}·\phantom{\rule{0.166667em}{0ex}},{K}_{n})⟶dist(\phantom{\rule{0.166667em}{0ex}}·\phantom{\rule{0.166667em}{0ex}},{K}_{\infty })\phantom{\rule{1.em}{0ex}}\mathrm{locally}\phantom{\rule{4.pt}{0ex}}\mathrm{uniformly}\phantom{\rule{4.pt}{0ex}}\mathrm{on}\phantom{\rule{4.pt}{0ex}}\mathbb{C}.$$
L787: 
L788: (37)
L789: Every compact subset of $int{K}_{\infty }$ is then eventually contained in $int{K}_{n}$. If ${K}_{\infty }$ is bounded, the sets ${K}_{n}$ are eventually uniformly bounded and ${d}_{H}({K}_{n},{K}_{\infty })\to 0$.
L790: 
L791: Proof.
L792: Set ${\ell }_{n}\left(z\right)=dist(z,{K}_{n})$. These functions are convex and 1-Lipschitz, and satisfy $0\le {\ell }_{n}\left(z\right)\le \left|z\right|$. Arzelà–Ascoli and a diagonal argument give a locally uniform limit ℓ. Put ${K}_{\infty }=\{z:\ell \left(z\right)=0\}$; this is a closed convex set containing 0.
L793: For fixed z, choose a nearest point ${w}_{n}\in {K}_{n}$. Then $|{w}_{n}|\le |z|+{\ell }_{n}\left(z\right)\le 2\left|z\right|$, so a subsequence converges to a point w. Local uniform convergence gives $\ell \left(w\right)=0$, and $|z-w|=\ell \left(z\right)$. Thus $dist(z,{K}_{\infty })\le \ell \left(z\right)$. The reverse inequality follows from the 1-Lipschitz property of ℓ and its vanishing on ${K}_{\infty }$. This proves (cite57†37 ).
L794: Suppose a compact set $L\subset int{K}_{\infty }$ is not eventually contained in $int{K}_{n}$. Passing to a subsequence, choose ${z}_{n}\in L\setminus int{K}_{n}$. Separation gives unit complex numbers ${\nu }_{n}$ such that
L795: 
L796: $$Re\left(\overline{{\nu }_{n}}(w-{z}_{n})\right)\le 0\phantom{\rule{2.em}{0ex}}(w\in {K}_{n}).$$
L797: Pass to subsequences with ${z}_{n}\to z\in L$ and ${\nu }_{n}\to \nu $, where $\left|\nu \right|=1$. Every $w\in {K}_{\infty }$ is the limit of points ${w}_{n}\in {K}_{n}$, by (cite57†37 ). Therefore
L798: 
L799: $$Re\left(\overline{\nu }(w-z)\right)\le 0\phantom{\rule{2.em}{0ex}}(w\in {K}_{\infty }),$$
L800: 
L801: contradicting $z\in int{K}_{\infty }$.
L802: Finally, suppose ${K}_{\infty }$ is bounded, and choose $R>{sup}_{z\in {K}_{\infty }}\left|z\right|$. If infinitely many ${K}_{n}$ contained points of modulus greater than R, their convexity and inclusion of 0 would give points ${q}_{n}\in {K}_{n}$ with $|{q}_{n}|=R$. A convergent subsequence would have a limit $q\in {K}_{\infty }$ with $\left|q\right|=R$, contrary to the choice of R. Hence ${K}_{n}\subset R\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ eventually.
L803: Uniform convergence of the distance functions on this fixed compact set now gives ${d}_{H}({K}_{n},{K}_{\infty })\to 0$. □
