Abstract
The packing stability in symplectic geometry was first noticed by Biran [Bir97]: the symplectic obstructions to embed several balls into a manifold disappear when their size is small enough. This phenomenon is known to hold for all closed manifolds with rational symplectic class (see [Bir99] for the 44-dimensional case, and [BH11, BH13] for higher dimensions), as well as for all ellipsoids [BH13].
In this note, we show that packing stability holds for all closed, and several open, symplectic 44-manifolds.
1 Introduction
In [Bir97, Bir99], Biran discovered the packing stability phenomenon: in some symplectic 44-manifolds, the symplectic obstructions to pack NN identical balls disappear when NN becomes large enough. He later generalized this result to every closed symplectic 44-manifold with rational symplectic class ([ω]∈H2​(M,ℚ)[\omega]\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})).
In this paper we generalize these results in several directions, by applying the singular inflation technique developed in [Ops13b, MO13]. We generalize Biran’s packing stability to all symplectic 44-manifolds, and also to a class of open symplectic 44-manifolds including ellipsoids and domains we call pseudo-balls.
For these manifolds, we establish not just packing stability but strong packing stability, which we define now. The definition is in the spirit of Biran’s first results on the subject [Bir97]. Throughout B⁡(λ)B(\lambda) will denote an open ball of capacity λ\lambda (see Definition 1.3) and we will write U​↪𝜔​VU\overset{\omega}{\hookrightarrow}V for a symplectic embedding.
Definition 1.1.
A symplectic manifold XX has property 𝒫⁡(λ)\mathcal{P}(\lambda) if for all collection of real numbers (λi)(\lambda_{i}) with λi<λ​∀i\lambda_{i}<\lambda\;\forall i, ⊔B⁡(λi)​↪𝜔​X\sqcup B(\lambda_{i})\overset{\omega}{\hookrightarrow}X if and only if the volume obstruction 12​∑λi2≤Vol⁡(X)\frac{1}{2}\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}\leq\mathrm{Vol}(X) is satisfied. We say that XX has the strong packing stability if it satisfies 𝒫⁡(λ)\mathcal{P}(\lambda) for some λ\lambda. We then define
Λ⁡(X):=inf{λ|X​ verifies ​𝒫​(λ)}.\Lambda(X):=\inf\{\lambda\;|\;X\text{ verifies }\mathcal{P}(\lambda)\}.
If there exists a μ\mu such that for all λ<μ\lambda<\mu we have ⊔B⁡(λ)​↪𝜔​X\sqcup B(\lambda)\overset{\omega}{\hookrightarrow}X if and only if the volume obstruction is satisfied, then we say that XX has packing stability. (In other words, we only consider embeddings by equally sized balls.)
Remark 1.2.
Since the volume inequality used is not strict, this definition even of packing stability is stronger than those used previously (for example in [BH11]) which ask for embeddings only if the union of balls has strictly smaller volume. Of course, to obtain embeddings in the case of equal volume, it is necessary for us to work with open balls. Such embeddings were called very full fillings in [LMS12], section 77.
Our main result is the following.
Theorem 1.
All closed 44-dimensional symplectic manifolds have strong packing stability.
To prove this theorem we will first need to establish strong packing stability for some open symplectic manifolds, and these results are interesting in themselves. To define these domains we work in ℂ2\mathbb{C}^{2} with its standard symplectic form.
Definition 1.3.
Let U⊂ℂ2U\subset\mathbb{C}^{2} be the interior of a domain of the form {(z,w)∈ℂ2|(π​|z|2,π​|w|2)∈P}\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\;|\;(\pi|z|^{2},\pi|w|^{2})\in P\} where PP is a subset of the first quadrant in ℝ2\mathbb{R}^{2}.
If PP is the convex hull P=Conv​⟨(0,0),(a,0),(0,b)⟩P=\text{Conv}\,\langle(0,0),(a,0),(0,b)\rangle then we say UU is a symplectic ellipsoid E⁡(a,b)E(a,b). A ball of capacity aa is an ellipsoid B⁡(a)=E⁡(a,a)B(a)=E(a,a), and we write τ​E​(a,b)\tau E(a,b) for E⁡(τ​a,τ​b)E(\tau a,\tau b).
If P=Conv​⟨(0,0),(0,a),(b,0),(α,β)⟩P=\text{Conv}\,\langle(0,0),(0,a),(b,0),(\alpha,\beta)\rangle for some a>αa>\alpha, b>βb>\beta and a,b<α+βa,b<\alpha+\beta then we say that UU is a pseudo-ball T⁡(a,b,α,β)T(a,b,\alpha,\beta).
Now we can state the following.
Theorem 2.
The 44-dimensional ellipsoids have the strong packing stability. Moreover, the function Λ⁡(E⁡(1,a))\Lambda(E(1,a)) is locally bounded on ℝ∗\mathbb{R}^{*}.
Note that since any rational symplectic manifold has full packing by an ellipsoid [Ops07], this theorem clearly implies Biran’s original result.
Corollary 1.4.
All closed symplectic 44-manifolds with rational symplectic class have the packing stability.
Strong packing stability for ellipsoids does not immediately imply packing stability for all closed manifolds, however, because the following remains a conjecture.
Conjecture 1.
Every (closed) 44-dimensional symplectic manifold is fully packed by one ellipsoid.
Nevertheless we will show that any symplectic 44 manifold can be decomposed (up to a subset of volume 00) into finitely many open symplectic manifolds, each of which has a Hamiltonian toric free action with convex associated polytope, namely ellipsoids and pseudo-balls. Theorem 1 will then follow from Theorem 2 and the following strong packing stability for pseudo-balls.
Theorem 3.
The pseudo-balls have the strong packing stability. Moreover, the function Λ⁡(T⁡(a,b,α,β))\Lambda(T(a,b,\alpha,\beta)) is locally bounded on {α<a<α+β,β<b<α+β}⊂ℝ4\{\alpha<a<\alpha+\beta,\beta<b<\alpha+\beta\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}.
Related results.
In [BH11, BH13], the first two authors proved that rational symplectic manifolds in all dimensions have packing stability. It remains an open question whether higher dimensional rational manifolds have strong packing stability. The proof argued as in Corollary 1.4 by first showing that all ellipsoids have packing stability and then remarking that any rational symplectic manifold admits a volume filling embedding from an ellipsoid. The ellipsoid packing stability ultimately relied on an embedding result for 44-dimensional ellipsoids.
Theorem.
[Buse-Hind] There exists a continuous function f:[1,∞)→[1,∞)f:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty) such that if b>f⁡(a)b>f(a) then there exists a symplectic embedding λ​E​(1,b)→E⁡(1,a)\lambda E(1,b)\to E(1,a) if any only if the volume condition is satisfied.
This immediately implies ellipsoid packing stability in four dimensions since an ellipsoid E⁡(1,k)E(1,k) can be fully filled by kk balls. The proof of the above theorem in [BH13] relied on Embedded Contact Homology, although the theorem in fact also follows from the strong packing stability we establish here. Indeed, work of McDuff says that an ellipsoid embedding in dimension 44 exists whenever there exists an embedding of a union of balls of appropriate sizes. It would be interesting to compare the corresponding bounds on the function ff.
Recent work of Latschev, McDuff and Schlenk in [LMS12] implies packing stability of 44-dimensional tori with linear symplectic forms (most of which are irrational). Indeed, if TT is a linear 44-dimensional torus not a product of 22-dimensional tori of equal area then we have an embedding ⊔B⁡(λi)​↪𝜔​X\sqcup B(\lambda_{i})\overset{\omega}{\hookrightarrow}X whenever the volume obstruction 12​∑λi2<Vol⁡(X)\frac{1}{2}\sum\lambda_{i}^{2}<\mathrm{Vol}(X) is satisfied. For the product T⁡(μ,μ)T(\mu,\mu) of two tori of area μ\mu this remains true given the additional condition that λi<μ\lambda_{i}<\mu for all ii. However whether or not there exist very full fillings of tori under just these hypotheses remains an open question.

3 Packing stability of ellipsoids
3.1 Background results on ellipsoid embeddings
We recall two facts on embedding of ellipsoids. The first one, stated in [McD09], relates ellipsoid embeddings to ball packings.
Theorem 4 (McDuff, [McD09]).
For each a∈ℚ+a\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}, there is an in integer p⁡(a)p(a) and weights 𝐰⁡(a):=(w1​(a),…,wp⁡(a)​(a)){\bf w}(a):=(w_{1}(a),\dots,w_{p(a)}(a)), such that for all rational symplectic 44-manifolds (X,ω)(X,\omega), τ​E​(1,a)​↪𝜔​X\tau E(1,a)\overset{\omega}{\hookrightarrow}X if and only if ⊔τ​B​(wi)​↪𝜔​X\sqcup\tau B(w_{i})\overset{\omega}{\hookrightarrow}X.
The second one follows readily from the proof of the previous theorem, in particular the construction of the wi​(a)w_{i}(a).
Lemma 3.1.
Fix a∈ℝa\in\mathbb{R}. There exists ε0\varepsilon_{0} (that depends on aa), such that for all ε<ε0\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0} and a′a^{\prime} with |a′−a|<ε|a^{\prime}-a|<\varepsilon, p′​(a)>p​(a)p^{\prime}(a)>p(a) and 𝐰⁡(a′){\bf w}(a^{\prime}) verifies the following:
|wi​(a′)−wi​(a)|<ε∀i≤p,|wi​(a′)|<ε∀i>p.\begin{array}[]{ll}|w_{i}(a^{\prime})-w_{i}(a)|<\varepsilon&\forall i\leq p,\\

We will need to apply not only Theorem 4, but also the blowing up procedure in a neighborhood of an ellipsoid which it relies on. Therefore we briefly recall this procedure now, following [McD09], section 33.
First of all, the number of steps in a continued fraction expansion gives us a function p:ℚ+→ℕp:\mathbb{Q}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}. Then McDuff shows that corresponding to each rational ellipsoid E=E⁡(a,b)E=E(a,b) and pp-tuple δ∈ℝp\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{p} of small (admissible) numbers, where p=p⁡(b/a)p=p(b/a), there is a sequence of symplectic spheres S1,…,SpS_{1},\dots,S_{p} which are JJ-holomorphic for a tame almost-complex structure on a suitable pp-fold blow-up of ℂ2\mathbb{C}^{2}. The complement of ∪Sj\cup S_{j} in a small neighborhood VV of ∪Sj\cup S_{j} is symplectomorphic to a neighborhood of the boundary in a carefully chosen but arbitrarily small domain containing E⊂ℂ2E\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}. The sequence of curves SjS_{j} is constructed recursively starting with a neighborhood U0⊂ℂ2U_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2} of EE which is defined in terms of the choice of δ\delta. The precise algorithm for the blowing-up can be found in [McD09], here we just outline briefly how the singular set arises.
Step 11. At the first stage we blow-up a ball of size k1=a​w1​(b/a)+δ1=a+δ1k_{1}=aw_{1}(b/a)+\delta_{1}=a+\delta_{1} inside U0U_{0}. Step 22. Now we blow-up a ball of size k2=a​w2​(b/a)+δ2k_{2}=aw_{2}(b/a)+\delta_{2} inside U1U_{1}. This ball will intersect E1E_{1} and {z1=0}\{z_{1}=0\} along complex lines while avoiding C1C_{1}. Thus the blow-up U2U_{2} will contain an exceptional divisor E2E_{2}, the proper transform of E1E_{1}, the proper transform C2C_{2} of {z1=0}\{z_{1}=0\} and still C1C_{1}.
Step ii. Again we blow-up a ball of a certain size ki=a​wi​(b/a)k_{i}=aw_{i}(b/a) to produce a blow-up UiU_{i} of Ui−1U_{i-1} with an exceptional divisor EiE_{i}. The construction is performed so that the volume of UpU_{p} is of order δ\delta and all points originally in the ellipsoid EE eventually lie in one of the balls to be blown-up. The curve SjS_{j} is the proper transform of EjE_{j}. Thus its self-intersection is −1-1 minus the number of subsequent balls which intersect the transform of EjE_{j}. The construction is such that Sp=EpS_{p}=E_{p} is the only curve with intersection −1-1 and the SjS_{j} for j<pj<p all have area of order δ\delta.

When we reverse the construction we may assume that any given holomorphic curve which intersects this SjS_{j} in a single point transversally is blown-down to a curve intersecting our neighborhood of EE in the corresponding axis.
3.2 Ellipsoid embeddings in good positions relative to a given curve
In this section we give the proof of Lemma 1.6, and follow that notation. First note that since we start with embeddings of closed ellipsoids, and since the area inequalities for components of CC are strict, the embeddings extend to disjoint rational ellipsoids which still satisfy the inequalities. Hence we may assume that all of our ellipsoids are rational. Moreover when choosing parameters δ\delta as in section 3.1 we will assume that the embeddings of disjoint closed ellipsoids extend to embeddings of disjoint neighborhoods of size δ\delta.
As in section 2 we normalize so that lines in ℙ2\mathbb{P}^{2} have area 11.

5 Packing stability of irrational closed symplectic 44-manifolds
The object of this section is the proof of Theorem 1. We begin with the general decomposition of symplectic 44-manifolds, then derive packing stability as a consequence of this and our results for open manifolds.
5.1 Decomposition of symplectic 44-manifolds with irrational symplectic class
We have already referred to the fact that a rational symplectic manifold is always fully packed by a single ellipsoid. In particular, strong packing stability for ellipsoids implies strong packing stability for all rational symplectic manifolds. Since Conjecture 1 remains open packing stability for ellipsoids does not necessarily imply the same for irrational manifolds, but we now recall some results obtained in [Ops13b] which allow us to split a symplectic manifold with irrational symplectic class into finitely many standard pieces, namely ellipsoids and pseudo-balls. We claim that MM satisfies the packing stability with constant Λ′=min⁡(Λ,2​δ)\Lambda^{\prime}=\min(\Lambda,\sqrt{2\delta}). (Note that a ball of capacity 2​δ\sqrt{2\delta} has volume δ\delta.) Indeed, suppose we have a sequence of balls, each of capacity less that Λ′\Lambda^{\prime}. If the total volume of the balls is less than 11 we may include some additional ones. Then we can partition the balls into mm subsets such that the sum of the volumes of the balls in the iith block lies within δ\delta of the volume of the iith piece of our decomposition of MM.
Now we claim that we can perturb the discs Di,Di,jD_{i},D_{i,j} to discs Di′,Di,j′D_{i}^{\prime},D_{i,j}^{\prime} which verify the four conditions listed above but are such that the volume of the associated piece of our decomposition is equal to the total volume of the corresponding subset of balls. We do this in order. First we perturb 𝒜1\mathcal{A}_{1} by less than ε1\varepsilon_{1} to get an 𝒜1′\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1} such that E⁡(𝒜1′,α1)E(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{1},\alpha_{1}) has the volume of the first subset of balls.
