Abstract
Let B2​n​(R)B^{2n}(R) denote the closed 2​n2n-dimensional symplectic ball of area RR,
and let Σg​(L)\Sigma_{g}(L) be a closed symplectic surface of genus gg and area LL.
We prove that there is a symplectic embedding ⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)×Σg​(L)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(R))×Σg​(L)\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\times\Sigma_{g}(L)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(R))\times\Sigma_{g}(L) if and only if there exists a symplectic embedding ⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(R))\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(R)).
This lies in contrast with the standard higher dimensional ball packing problem ⨆i=1kB2​n​(Ri)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B2​n​(R))\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{2n}(R_{i})\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{2n}(R)) for n>2n>2, which we conjecture (based on index behavior for pseudoholomorphic curves) is controlled entirely by Gromov’s two ball theorem and volume considerations.
We also deduce analogous results for stabilized embeddings of concave toric domains into convex domains, and we establish a stabilized version of Gromov’s two ball theorem which holds in any dimension.
Our main tools are: (i) the symplectic blowup construction along symplectic submanifolds, (ii) an h-principle for symplectic surfaces in high dimensional symplectic manifolds, and (iii) a stabilization result for pseudoholomorphic holomorphic curves of genus zero.

1 Introduction
1.1 Background and main results
The symplectic ball packing problem, which asks when a disjoint union of 2​n2n-dimensional symplectic balls of prescribed sizes can be symplectically embedded into a fixed 2​n2n-dimensional symplectic ball, is similar in spirit but quite distinct in substance from its more famous Riemannian cousin, namely the sphere packing problem (see e.g. [CS13]).
Let B2​n(R):={π∑i=1n|zi|2≤R}⊆ℂnB^{2n}(R):=\left\{\pi\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}\leq R\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n} denote the closed 2​n2n-dimensional ball of area RR (i.e. radius OPENR/π)\sqrt{R/\pi}), and let int⁡(B2​n​(R)){\operatorname{int}}(B^{2n}(R)) denote its interior.
We endow these with the restriction of the standard symplectic two-form ωstd=∑i=1nd​xi∧d​yi\omega_{\operatorname{std}}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i}.
Classically, note that any symplectic embedding
⨆i=1kB2​n​(Ri)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B2​n​(R))\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{2n}(R_{i})\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{2n}(R))
must satisfy
∑i=1kRin≤Rn\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}R_{i}^{n}\leq R^{n},
since symplectic embeddings preserve the standard volume form 1n!​ωstd∧n\tfrac{1}{n!}\omega_{\operatorname{std}}^{\wedge n}.
The first nontrivial result on symplectic ball packings appeared in Gromov’s foundational 1985 paper on pseudoholomorphic curves.
THEOREM 1.1 (Gromov’s two ball theorem [Gro85]).
Given n∈ℤ≥1n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1} and R1,R2,R∈ℝ>0R_{1},R_{2},R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}, any symplectic embedding B2​n​(R1)​⨆B2​n​(R2)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B2​n​(R))B^{2n}(R_{1})\bigsqcup B^{2n}(R_{2})\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{2n}(R)) must satisfy R1+R2<RR_{1}+R_{2}<R.
Subsequently, in dimension four many more refined symplectic packing obstructions for multiple balls were found.

Symplectic embedding problems are well-studied in dimension four, partly due to the existence of powerful gauge theoretic invariants like Seiberg–Witten theory, and partly thanks to useful tools for pseudoholomorphic curves in low dimensions such as positivity of intersections and automatic transversality.
For comparison, in higher dimensions we have the following conjecture concerning symplectic ball packings.
CONJECTURE A.
For n≥3n\geq 3 and R1,…,Rk,R∈ℝ>0R_{1},\dots,R_{k},R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0} for some k∈ℤ≥1k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}, there exists a symplectic embedding

if and only if the following hold:
•
(volume obstruction) R1n+⋯+Rkn<RnR_{1}^{n}+\cdots+R_{k}^{n}<R^{n}
•
(two ball obstruction) Ri+Rj<RR_{i}+R_{j}<R for all 1≤i<j≤k1\leq i<j\leq k.
Indeed, for essentially formal reasons (based on a Fredholm index computation in §7) there cannot be any stronger obstructions coming from pseudoholomorphic curves, at least under the standard paradigm for obstructing symplectic embeddings via closed or punctured curves.
In the spirit of Y. Eliashberg’s “holomorphic curves or nothing” philosophy, which posits that all nontrivial obstructions in symplectic geometry come from some kind of pseudoholomorphic invariants or classical invariants, we then expect higher dimensional ball packings to exhibit complete flexibility beyond the volume and two ball obstructions (see §7 for more on what is currently known).
Nevertheless, we show that a certain stabilization procedure exhibits considerable rigidity for symplectic packing problems in higher dimensions. Fixing g∈ℤ≥1g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1} and L∈ℝ>0L\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}, let Σg​(L)\Sigma_{g}(L) denote a closed symplectic surface of genus gg and area LL.
Given a symplectic ball B2​n​(R)B^{2n}(R), we equip B2​n​(R)×Σg​(L)B^{2n}(R)\times\Sigma_{g}(L) with the product symplectic form.
Our main result is:
THEOREM B.
There exists a symplectic embedding ⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)×Σg​(L)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(R))×Σg​(L)\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\times\Sigma_{g}(L)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(R))\times\Sigma_{g}(L) if and only if there exists a symplectic embedding ⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(R))\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(R)).

Other stabilized embedding problems
Since ball packings are known to control various symplectic embeddings problems, we can use Theorem B to deduce analogous embedding results for stabilizations of other domains.
McDuff [McD09] showed that each symplectic embedding problem between four-dimensional rational ellipsoids is equivalent to some symplectic ball packing problem, where the sizes of the balls are read off from the area factors of the ellipsoids.
Cristofaro-Gardiner subsequently generalized this to a large class of four-dimensional toric domains as follows:
THEOREM 1.3 ([CG19, Thm. 2.1]).
Given a four-dimensional rational concave toric domain XΩ14X_{\Omega_{1}}^{4} and a four-dimensional rational convex toric domain XΩ24X_{\Omega_{2}}^{4}, there exists a symplectic embedding XΩ1​↪𝑠​int⁡(XΩ2)X_{\Omega_{1}}\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(X_{\Omega_{2}}) if and only if there exists a ball packing
(⨆i=1kB4​(ai))⊔(⨆i=1ℓB4​(bi))​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(b)),\displaystyle\left(\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(a_{i})\right)\sqcup\left(\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}B^{4}(b_{i})\right)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(b)),
(2)
where (a1,…,ak)(a_{1},\dots,a_{k}) is the weight sequence of Ω1\Omega_{1} and (b,b1,…,bℓ)(b;b_{1},\dots,b_{\ell}) is the negative weight sequence of Ω2\Omega_{2}.
We briefly recall the definitions of (rational) concave and convex toric domains and their weight sequences in §6.
The basic idea is that a four-dimensional concave toric domain canonically decomposes into a collection of symplectic balls, and similarly for the complement of a four-dimensional convex toric domain inside of a larger circumscribed ball.
By adapting this picture to the stabilizations XΩi×Σg​(L)X_{\Omega_{i}}\times\Sigma_{g}(L) and applying Theorem B, we get the following stabilized analogue of Theorem 1.3.

COROLLARY F.
For each x∈ℝ≥1x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}, there exists a symplectic embedding
E⁡(1,x)×Σg​(L)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(μ))×Σg​(L)E(1,x)\times\Sigma_{g}(L)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(\mu))\times\Sigma_{g}(L)

Note that there has been considerable recent interest in pushing four dimensional symplectic embedding results into higher dimensions by a different type of stabilization, namely taking products with ℝ2\mathbb{R}^{2}, or more generally ℝ2​N\mathbb{R}^{2N}.
By analogy with the definition of fB4​(x)f_{B^{4}}(x), for N∈ℤ≥1N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1} put
fB4×ℝ2​N​(x):=inf{μ|E⁡(1,x)×ℝ2​N​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(μ))×ℝ2​N}.f_{B^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(x):=\inf\{\mu\;|\;E(1,x)\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(\mu))\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\}.
It is known [HK14, CGH18, CGHM18, McD18, Sie22] that, for any N∈ℤ≥1N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}, fB4×ℝ2​N​(x)f_{B^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(x) coincides with fB4​(x)f_{B^{4}}(x) for all 1≤x≤τ4:=7+3​521\leq x\leq\tau^{4}:=\tfrac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}, while fB4×ℝ2​N​(x)=3​xx+1f_{B^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(x)=\tfrac{3x}{x+1} for infinitely many values of xx in (τ4,∞)(\tau^{4},\infty).
It is a subtle problem to determine which four-dimensional obstructions persist after stabilizing with ℝ2​N\mathbb{R}^{2N} and which do not. For instance, for xx sufficiently large we have fB4​(x)=xf_{B^{4}}(x)=\sqrt{x}, i.e. volume is the only obstruction, while conjecturally (and definitely for certain values) we have fB4×ℝ2​N​(x)=3​xx+1f_{B^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(x)=\tfrac{3x}{x+1}, coming from Hind’s folding embedding [Hin15].
By contrast, Corollary F states that all four-dimensional obstructions persist after stabilizing with Σg​(L)\Sigma_{g}(L).
Remark 1.
As another illustration of the difference between stabilizing with Σg​(L)\Sigma_{g}(L) versus ℝ2​N\mathbb{R}^{2N}, let us note that in the latter case the analogue of Theorem B is entirely flexible.
Namely, for any k∈ℤ≥1k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1} and R1,…,Rk,R∈ℝ>0R_{1},\dots,R_{k},R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0} with R1,…,Rk≤RR_{1},\dots,R_{k}\leq R there exists a symplectic embedding
⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)×ℝ2​N​↪𝑠​B4​(R)×ℝ2​N.\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}B^{4}(R)\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}.
This is a consequence of the fact that we can symplectically embed a disjoint union of finitely many copies of ℝ2​N\mathbb{R}^{2N} into a single copy of ℝ2​N\mathbb{R}^{2N}.
Note that the condition R1,…,Rk≤RR_{1},\dots,R_{k}\leq R is necessary by Gromov’s nonsqueezing theorem.
Remark 2.
In the embedding problems discussed above one could take the source to be either a compact domain or its interior, and similarly for the target, with the distinction between these choices being mostly academic (up to swapping inequalities with strict inequalities) since we are interested in optimal embeddings.
For a more refined discussion see also [McD11, Rmk. 1.3] or [CG19, Cor. 1.6].
1.2 Open problems
We now mention a few open problems which seem to go beyond the techniques of this paper.
More general stabilizations by symplectic surfaces
The stabilized packing problem addressed by Theorem B has the following natural extension.
For k∈ℤ≥1k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}, let Σg1​(L1),…,Σgk​(Lk)\Sigma_{g_{1}}(L_{1}),\dots,\Sigma_{g_{k}}(L_{k}) denote closed symplectic surfaces with genera g1,…,gk∈ℤ≥0g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} and areas L1,…,Lk∈ℝ>0L_{1},\dots,L_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0} respectively.

Note that by homology class considerations we must have Li=κi​LL_{i}=\kappa_{i}L for some κi∈ℤ≥1\kappa_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}, for each i=1,…,ki=1,\dots,k.

consider the embedding problem

Stabilizing by higher dimensional manifolds
In Theorem B, the fact that Σg​(L)\Sigma_{g}(L) is two-dimensional allows us to appeal to an h principle for symplectic surfaces in symplectic manifolds of dimension at least six (see 4.3).
As we explain in §4, a similar h principle holds for higher dimensional symplectic submanifolds of codimension at least four, but it is no longer guaranteed that the relevant formal obstructions vanish.
Nevertheless, we can ask:
QUESTION 1.6.
Let NN be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension 2​n≥42n\geq 4. Is it still true that ⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)×N​↪𝑠​B4​(R)×N\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\times N\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}B^{4}(R)\times N if and only if ⨆i=1kB4​(Ri)​↪𝑠​B4​(R)\bigsqcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}B^{4}(R_{i})\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}B^{4}(R)?
1.3 Sketch of proof of main theorem
We end this introduction by giving a brief outline of the proof of Theorem B, while also outlining the main components of the paper.
The “if” direction is immediate, since a four-dimensional embedding can be stabilized to a six-dimensional one. The proof of the “only if” direction is based on the symplectic ball packing obstructions coming from exceptional curves in blowups of ℂ​ℙ2\mathbb{CP}^{2}, following [MP94].
It relies on the following three observations.
•
First we note that the existence of pairwise disjoint embeddings ιi:B4​(Ri)×Σg​(L)​↪𝑠​int⁡(B4​(R))×Σg​(R)\iota_{i}:B^{4}(R_{i})\times\Sigma_{g}(L)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{int}}(B^{4}(R))\times\Sigma_{g}(R) for i=1,…,ki=1,\dots,k implies the existence of a symplectic blowup along the collection of symplectic surfaces ι1​({0}×Σg​(L)),…,ιk​({0}×Σg​(L))\iota_{1}(\{0\}\times\Sigma_{g}(L)),\dots,\iota_{k}(\{0\}\times\Sigma_{g}(L)) in ℂ​ℙ2​(R)×Σg​(L)\mathbb{CP}^{2}(R)\times\Sigma_{g}(L) with respective sizes R1,…,RkR_{1},\dots,R_{k} (here ℂ​ℙ2​(R)\mathbb{CP}^{2}(R) is equipped with the Fubini–Study form normalized so that a line has area RR).
We denote this blowup by

2 Blowing up along symplectic submanifolds
In this section we discuss the symplectic blowup construction along a symplectic submanifold, highlighting the main invariance properties we will need for the proof of Theorem B.
While the symplectic blowup at a point is well-studied (see e.g. [MP94] or [MS17, §7.1]), the analogue for symplectic submanifolds is somewhat less explored. Here we roughly follow the outline given in [MS17, §7.1].
After reviewing the blowup construction along a symplectic ball embedding B2​n​(R)​↪𝑠​M2​nB^{2n}(R)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}M^{2n}, we extend this fiberwise to construct the symplectic blowup along an embedding B2​k​(R)×Σ2​n−2​k​↪𝑠​M2​nB^{2k}(R)\times\Sigma^{2n-2k}\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}M^{2n}.
The properties we will need of this construction are summarized in Proposition 2.2,
whose proof is provided in §2.3.
2.1 The symplectic blowup at a point
In order to set up notation let us first briefly review the symplectic blowup at a point pp in a symplectic manifold M2​nM^{2n}. Recall that this depends on a size parameter R∈ℝ>0R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0} and a symplectic embedding ι:B2​n​(R)​↪𝑠​M\iota:B^{2n}(R)\overset{s}{\hookrightarrow}M.

Remark 4.
In Section 7.1 of [MS12a] it is explained how to take the symplectic blow up of a symplectic submanifold with nontrivial symplectic normal bundle (See also [McD84, GS89]). The conclusion of Proposition 2.2 is still true for these more general blowups, however the proof of (B) becomes more difficult to write down because one needs to keep track of all the choices made during the blowup construction. Since in this paper we only need the simplest case as described in Definition 2.1, we content ourselves to studying blowups in this simpler setting.

Remark 5.
We expect Theorem 3.1 to admit an analogue in the setting of punctured pseudoholomorphic curves, with MM a completed symplectic cobordism and NN a closed symplectic manifold. After stabilization the punctured holomorphic curves will have punctures asymptotic to Morse-Bott families of Reeb orbits. In this setting one can use Wendl’s automatic transversality for punctured curves [Wen10] (take all punctures to be “free” along Morse-Bott families of Reeb orbits) in lieu of the regularity criterion from [MS12a].
Remark 6.
We observe that the genus zero assumption is essential in the above results. We expect higher genus curves will disappear after a generic perturbation because their indices are negative. To be precise, after stabilizing by a symplectic manifold of dimension 2​n2n, the Fredholm index of a curve changes by n⁡(2−2​g)n(2-2g).
The g=1g=1 curve scenario is very curious; in the case of closed curves, suppose we start with a rigid JJ-holomorphic curve in MM. After we stabilize with NN we do get an dimN\dim N dimensional family of non-transverse JJ-holomorphic curves, all of which are index zero. After we perturb we would expect a discrete family will survive the perturbation and become transverse curves. The number of such curves we expect to be counted by an obstruction bundle. We give a lower dimensional manifestation of this phenomenon below.
Example 3.3.
Let M=(S1×S1,ω)M=(S^{1}\times S^{1},\omega) denote the two dimensional torus, with the standard almost complex structure. Then there exists exactly one genus one JJ-holomorphic curve u:T2→Mu:T^{2}\rightarrow M, and this curve is transverse (assuming we can vary the domain complex structure). Now stabilize with a symplectic surface of genus gg, which we write as (Σg,ω)(\Sigma_{g},\omega).
Now consider S1×S1×ΣgS^{1}\times S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g}. With the split almost complex structure we see a 2-dimensional family of non-transverse index zero holomorphic tori parametrized by g∈Σgg\in\Sigma_{g}. We now think the ambient space S1×S1×ΣgS^{1}\times S^{1}\times\Sigma_{g} as S1S^{1} times the mapping torus of the identity map on Σg\Sigma_{g}. Now perturb the mapping torus of the identity map to the mapping torus of a small non-degenerate Hamiltonian HH (with appropriate choice of almost complex structure). The curves in the homology class [T2]×[pt][T^{2}]\times[{\operatorname{pt}}] that survive are precisely those corresponding the degree 1 periodic orbits of XHX_{H}; we have nonzero such counts; and this count is given by Taubes’ Gromov invariant, as explained in section 2.6 of [Hut14].
