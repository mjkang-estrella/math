Abstract.
Let MM be a closed manifold. We introduce a family of Legendrian isotopy invariants for Legendrians in J1​MJ^{1}M, which we collectively call Legendrian higher torsion. Given a choice of a class ℱ\mathcal{F} of fibre bundles over MM, equipped with suitable unitary local systems, the Legendrian higher torsion of a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M is the subset of H∗​(M,𝐑)H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) consisting of higher Reidemeister torsion cohomology classes of fibre bundles WW over MM in the class ℱ\mathcal{F} such that Λ\Lambda admits a generating function on a stabilization of WW. For the class of tube bundles in the sense of Waldhausen we call the invariant tube torsion. We show that the tube torsion of a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is well-defined when the stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O is trivial (for example when LL is a nearby Lagrangian homotopy sphere) and it consists of a union of cosets of a normalized version of the Pontryagin character. We also identify a distinguished coset, invariant under Hamiltonian isotopy of LL, which we call nearby Lagrangian torsion. We do not know whether nearby Lagrangians must have trivial tube torsion, as would follow from the nearby Lagrangian conjecture. However, we show that there exist Legendrians Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M with nontrivial tube torsion whose projection Λ→M\Lambda\to M is homotopic to a diffeomorphism.

1. Introduction
1.1. Main results
Fix a closed, connected, smooth manifold MM. We denote by J1​MJ^{1}M its 1-jet space, where we recall J1​M=T∗​M×𝐑J^{1}M=T^{*}M\times\mathbf{R} with coordinates (q,p,z)∈J1​M(q,p,z)\in J^{1}M corresponding to q∈Mq\in M, p∈Tq∗​Mp\in T^{*}_{q}M and z∈𝐑z\in\mathbf{R}, with the contact form d​z−p​d​qdz-pdq endowing J1​MJ^{1}M with its standard contact structure. In this article we introduce a family of new K-theoretic Legendrian isotopy invariants for Legendrian submanifolds Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M, which we call Legendrian higher torsion. These invariants are defined in terms of generating functions for Λ\Lambda and have several incarnations depending on the class of fiber bundles one chooses to consider as the possible domains of generating functions for Λ\Lambda.
In general, one has notion of a torsion pair, which consists of a pair (W,ρ)(W,\rho) where F→W→MF\to W\to M is a fiber bundle and ρ:π1​W→U⁡(1)\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1) is a rank 1 unitary local system such that H∗​(F,𝐂ρ)H_{*}(F;\mathbf{C}^{\rho}) is a unipotent π1​M\pi_{1}M-module. If FF is closed no more data is needed, but if FF has boundary or is non-compact one should also fix the behavior near the boundary or at infinity for the class of functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} we allow ourselves to consider as possible generating functions. For a torsion pair (W,ρ)(W,\rho) the theory of higher torsion characteristic classes due to the second author and J. Klein produces cohomology classes τk​(W,ρ)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) which are invariants of (W,ρ)(W,\rho) up to fibered diffeomorphism of fiber bundles (compatible with the fixed behavior at infinity) and up to the notion of stable equivalence which replaces WW with W×𝐑4W\times\mathbf{R}^{4} and f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} with fs​(w,x1,x2,y1,y2)=f⁡(w)+x12+x22−y12−y22f^{\text{s}}(w,x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})=f(w)+x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2} [Igu02, Igu05, IK93a, IK93b, K89].
When H∗​(F,𝐂ρ)=0H_{*}(F,\mathbf{C}^{\rho})=0, for k=0k=0 the class τ0​(W,ρ)∈H0​(M,𝐑)≃𝐑\tau_{0}(W,\rho)\in H^{0}(M;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R} recovers the usual Reidemeister torsion of the fiber FF with respect to ρ|π1​F\rho|_{\pi_{1}F} and for k>0k>0 the class τk​(W,ρ)\tau_{k}(W,\rho) may be thought of as a parametrized generalization of this Reidemeister torsion.
Equipped with the theory of higher torsion classes of fiber bundles, we define the kk-th Legendrian higher torsion of a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M in a fixed collection ℱ\mathcal{F} of torsion pairs to be the subset τk​(Λ,ℱ)⊂H2​k​(W,ρ)\tau_{k}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{2k}(W,\rho) consisting of all degree 2​k2k higher torsion characteristic classes τk​(W,ρ)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) of torsion pairs (W,ρ)(W,\rho) in the class ℱ\mathcal{F} such that Λ\Lambda admits a generating function on a stabilization of the fiber bundle W→MW\to M. We denote τ⁡(Λ,ℱ)=∐kτk​(Λ,ℱ)⊂H∗​(M,𝐑)\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})=\coprod_{k}\tau_{k}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) the total Legendrian higher torsion of Λ\Lambda in the fixed collection ℱ\mathcal{F}.
The starting point of the theory of Legendrian higher torsion is the following consequence of the homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopies (up to stabilization).
Theorem 1.1.
The subset τ⁡(Λ,ℱ)⊂H∗​(M,𝐑)\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) is a Legendrian isotopy invariant of Λ\Lambda.
For example, in the article [AI] the first and second authors implicitly gave a concrete application of these ideas in the special case in which ℱ\mathcal{F} consists of oriented circle bundles S1→W→ΣS^{1}\to W\to\Sigma over a fixed orientable surface Σ\Sigma, equipped with rank 1 unitary local systems which send the oriented fiber to a fixed nn-root of unity ≠1\neq 1 for ±n\pm n the Euler number of WW. In that specific case it is computed in [AI] that for Legendrians in a certain class (called mesh Legendrians), all of which in particular admit generating functions on circle bundles, the degree 2 Legendrian higher torsion τ1​(Λ,ℱ)\tau_{1}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F}) of a mesh Legendrian Λ⊂J1​Σ\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma in H2​(Σ,𝐑)≃𝐑H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R} records the Euler number (up to sign) of the circle bundle that was used to generate it. It follows from this computation that each mesh Legendrian admits a generating function on a circle bundle of Euler number ±n\pm n for a unique positive integer nn, even after stabilization of the circle bundle. In particular it follows that this number is an invariant of the mesh Legendrian.
In fact, in [AI] a variant of Legendrian higher torsion called Legendrian Turaev torsion was defined for a general class of Legendrians called Euler Legendrians. For mesh Legendrians the Legendrian Turaev torsion allowed one to also recover the sign of this Euler number. The Legendrian Turaev torsion was then used in [AI] to distinguish pairs of Legendrians which were formally Legendrian isotopic but not Legendrian isotopic, and in fact such that the isomorphism class of the Legendrian contact homology dga and microlocal sheaf category was unable to distinguish them (at least with traditional coefficients).
Although different flavors of the invariant may be useful for different purposes, our main focus in the present article is a specific version of Legendrian higher torsion which we call tube torsion. This version is particularly relevant to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, the study of which is the primary motivation for this work. Tube torsion is Legendrian higher torsion for the class ℱ\mathcal{F} of tube bundles in the sense of Waldahusen [Wal82]. Tube bundles have gained direct relevance to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture after the existence theorem for (twisted) generating functions on tube bundles for nearby Lagrangians proved by Abouzaid, Courte, Guillermou and Kragh in [ACGK]. When working with tube bundles we will omit the class ℱ\mathcal{F} from the notation, and as will be explained, the data of the unitary local system may also be disregarded. Although the precise definition of the invariant will be given in Definition 3.25 once the notions of tube bundles and generating functions of tube type are properly formulated, we give for now the following provisional definition.
Definition 1.2.
The tube torsion of a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M is the collection τ⁡(Λ)⊂H∗​(M,𝐑)\tau(\Lambda)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) of higher torsion classes τk​(W)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) of tube bundles W→MW\to M on which Λ\Lambda admits a generating function.
Remark 1.3.
Since the class of tubes is stable, i.e. closed under stabilization, we do not have to add up to stabilization in the above definition.
The main properties of tube torsion are summarized in the following result. We will denote by p:K​O0​(M)→H∗​(M,𝐑)p:KO^{0}(M)\to H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) a suitably normalized version of the Pontryagin character adapted to the definition of higher torsion, see Appendix B. Let ζ⁡(s)=∑n=1∞n−s\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s} be the standard zeta function. For E→ME\to M a real vector bundle we denote p⁡(E)=∑kpk​(E)p(E)=\sum_{k}p_{k}(E) with pk​(E)∈H4​k​(M,𝐑)p_{k}(E)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}) given by
pk​(E)=12​(−1)k​ζ​(2​k+1)​c​h2​k​(E⊗𝐂).p_{k}(E)=\frac{1}{2}(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k+1)ch_{2k}(E\otimes\mathbf{C}).
Theorem 1.4.
The tube torsion of a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M satisfies the following:
(1)
τ⁡(Λ)\tau(\Lambda) is invariant under Legendrian isotopy of Λ\Lambda.
(2)
τ⁡(Λ)\tau(\Lambda) is a union of cosets of pp, so we may view τ⁡(Λ)\tau(\Lambda) as a subset of coker​(p)\text{coker}(p).
(3)
for the zero section M⊂J1​MM\subset J^{1}M we have τ​(Λ)=im​(p)\tau(\Lambda)=\text{im}(p), the trivial coset.
Remark 1.5.
For WW a tube bundle the lack of a unitary local system means we are drawing invariants from K⁡(𝐑)K(\mathbf{R}) rather than K⁡(𝐂)K(\mathbf{C}) and this forces the odd classes τ2​k+1​(W)\tau_{2k+1}(W) to be zero. So the only possible nontrivial classes are the even classes τ2​k​(W)∈H4​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}) and hence the tube torsion of a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M is a disjoint union τ⁡(Λ)=∐kτ2​k​(Λ)\tau(\Lambda)=\coprod_{k}\tau_{2k}(\Lambda) with τ2​k​(Λ)⊂H4​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{2k}(\Lambda)\subset H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}).
Note that by definition, we have τ⁡(Λ)=∅\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing if Λ\Lambda does not admit a generating function on any tube bundle. For example, since the fiberwise Morse theory of a tube bundle in the sense considered in the present article has Euler characteristic ±1\pm 1 we will certainly have τ⁡(Λ)=∅\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing if the projection Λ→M\Lambda\to M does not have degree ±1\pm 1. When τ⁡(Λ)=∅\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing we say that the tube torsion is not defined.
So for which Legendrians is tube torsion defined? A particularly elusive class of Legendrians is given by the Legendrian lifts of nearby Lagrangians. We recall that for LL and MM closed, connected, smooth nn-dimensional manifolds, a nearby Lagrangian is an exact Lagrangian embedding L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M. A nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M admits a generating function on some tube bundle if and only if the stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O is nullhomotopic [ACGK]. Therefore the tube torsion of the Legendrian lift Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M of any nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M with stably trivial Gauss map is defined, and we denote it τ⁡(L)⊂H∗​(M,𝐑)\tau(L)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}).
Furthermore, in [ACGK] a procedure for producing a generating function on a tube bundle for a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is given once the input of a nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O is fixed, using the doubling trick which Guillermou introduced in [Gui12]. We do not know whether any generating function on a tube bundle for a nearby Lagrangian is given by this procedure. We say that a generating function for LL which does arise by this procedure is natural and denote by ν⁡(L)⊂τ⁡(Λ)\nu(L)\subset\tau(\Lambda) the subset consisting of those higher torsion classes in τ⁡(Λ)\tau(\Lambda) of tube bundles W→MW\to M where the generating function for LL on WW is natural. We call ν⁡(L)\nu(L) the nearby Lagrangian torsion of LL. It satisfies the following properties:
Theorem 1.6.
Let L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M be a nearby Lagrangian with trivial stable Gauss map. The nearby Lagrangian torsion of LL satisfies the following:
(1)
ν⁡(L)\nu(L) is invariant under Hamiltonian isotopies of LL.
(2)
ν⁡(L)\nu(L) is a single coset of pp, so we get a well-defined element ν​(L)∈coker​(p)\nu(L)\in\text{coker}(p).
(3)
for the zero section we have ν​(M)=τ​(M)=im​(p)\nu(M)=\tau(M)=\text{im}(p).
Remark 1.7.
Although the tube torsion τ⁡(Λ)\tau(\Lambda) of the Legendrian lift Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M of a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is invariant under Legendrian isotopies of Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M, the nearby Lagrangian torsion ν⁡(L)\nu(L) is not obviously invariant under Legendrian isotopies of Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M. Concretely, if L1,L2⊂T∗​ML_{1},L_{2}\subset T^{*}M are two nearby Lagrangians with Legendrian isotopic Legendrian lifts, then we do not know whether it must be the case that ν⁡(L1)=ν⁡(L2)\nu(L_{1})=\nu(L_{2}), even though Theorem 1.4 guarantees that τ⁡(L1)=τ⁡(L2)\tau(L_{1})=\tau(L_{2}).
Example 1.8.
In [ACGK] it was also shown that the stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O of a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is always trivial on homotopy groups. Since LL and MM are homotopy equivalent, this implies that L→U/OL\to U/O is always nullhomotopic when MM has the homotopy type of a sphere. So for nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres, generating functions on tube bundles always exist and therefore the tube torsion and nearby Lagrangian torsion of a nearby Lagrangian homotopy sphere are always defined. Both invariants are automatically trivial for degree reasons unless the dimension of MM is of the form n=4​kn=4k. For concreteness, say M=S4​kM=S^{4k} is the standard sphere (though the discussion is similar if MM is any exotic sphere of dimension divisible by 4). The only possible nontrivial higher torsion class of a tube bundle W→S4​kW\to S^{4k} is τ2​k​(W)∈H4​k​(S4​k,𝐑)≃𝐑\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(S^{4k};\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}. Next, we introduce some terminology to distinguish from the case in which nearby Lagrangian torsion isn’t defined.
Definition 1.10.
We say that a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M has trivial tube torsion when τ​(Λ)=im​(p)\tau(\Lambda)=\text{im}(p). We say that a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M has trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion when ν​(L)=im​(p)\nu(L)=\text{im}(p).
Remark 1.11.
If a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M has trivial tube torsion, then it automatically has trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion.
We may formulate the resulting obstruction to Legendrian and Hamiltonian isotopy to the zero section as follows:
Corollary 1.12.
If a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M is Legendrian isotopic to the zero section, then it has trivial tube torsion. In particular, if a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section, then it has trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion.
The famous nearby Lagrangian conjecture states that any nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section. The following question is therefore natural.
Question 1.13.
Must a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M have trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion?
An affirmative answer to this question would provide further evidence towards the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, whereas a negative answer would disprove it. Note that according to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture the Legendrian lift Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M of a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M would in particular be Legendrian isotopic to MM, though even this weaker form of the conjecture is not known. It seems conceivable that there could exist a nearby Lagrangian with trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion but nontrivial tube torsion, which would also disprove the nearby Lagrangian conjecture.
Remark 1.14.
The invariant ν⁡(L)\nu(L) is an invariant of a single nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M. It is a parametrized torsion invariant of a family of acyclic chain complexes associated to LL. The space of parameters is MM, and for a single parameter m∈Mm\in M the relevant chain complex is (at least conjecturally) C​F∗​(Tm​M,L)CF^{*}(T_{m}M,L), see Section 4.5. However, we take the generating function viewpoint in the present article and use the fiberwise Morse complex C∗​(fm)C_{*}(f_{m}) of a natural generating function ff for LL instead. The work by Courte and Porcelli [CP25] gives a vanishing result for the parametrized higher torsion of a family of nearby Lagrangians Lz⊂T∗​ML_{z}\subset T^{*}M,z∈Zz\in Z, where for a single parameter the relevant chain complex is C​F∗​(M,Lz)CF^{*}(M,L_{z}) for M⊂T∗​MM\subset T^{*}M the zero section, though [CP25] also takes the viewpoint of generating functions. The work [CP25] is closer to a parametric generalization of the result [AK] of vanishing of Whitehead torsion for a single nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M, which concerns C​F∗​(M,L)CF^{*}(M,L). Indeed, the invariants τ⁡(L)\tau(L) and ν⁡(L)\nu(L) have no non-parametric analogue; the fiber of a tube bundle is simply connected so its Whitehead group is trivial. It’s possible the two viewpoints may be reconciled by some assembly type map.
In the broader class of Legendrians which are not necessarily lifts of nearby Lagrangians, we show that the theory of tube torsion is nontrivial in that Legendrians with nontrivial tube torsion abound. In fact, we establish the existence of Legendrians which have nontrivial tube torsion and which are diffeomorphic to the base, so that the conjectural triviality of tube torsion for nearby Lagrangians would in any case not follow from known constraints on the topology of nearby Lagrangians.
To formulate the precise statement, we will use the viewpoint of Waldhausen [Wal82] where a fibration sequence ℋ∞→𝒯∞→B​G\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG is constructed. Here ℋ∞\mathcal{H}_{\infty} is the space of stable h-cobordisms of a point (which classifies bundles of h-cobordisms of a disk up to stable equivalence), 𝒯∞\mathcal{T}_{\infty} is the stable space of tubes (which classifies tube bundles up to stable equivalence), and B​GBG is the classifying space for stable spherical fibrations, so G=limnGnG=\lim_{n}G_{n} for GnG_{n} the space of self homotopy-equivalences of SnS^{n}. In particular, this means given a bundle H→V→MH\to V\to M of h-cobordisms of a disk, one may attach VV to a trivial tube bundle M×TM\times T, where TT is the standard tube, to obtain a tube bundle T→W→MT\to W\to M. The tube bundle WW has the same higher torsion as VV.
Theorem 1.15.
Let H→V→MH\to V\to M be a bundle of hh-cobordisms of a disk and let T→W→MT\to W\to M the resulting tube bundle. There exists a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M which admits a generating function on WW and whose projection to the base Λ→M\Lambda\to M is homotopic to a diffeomorphism. In particular the higher torsion classes τk​(V)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(V)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) of VV are contained in the tube torsion τ⁡(Λ)⊂H∗​(M,𝐑)\tau(\Lambda)\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) of Λ\Lambda.
The higher torsion of tube bundles arising from h-cobordism bundles as in Theorem 1.15 is in general not contained in the image of the (normalized) Pontryagin character, and hence this result shows that Legendrians with nontrivial tube torsion exist in abundance. Unfortunately, we do not know how to visualize a single explicit example of a Legendrian with nontrivial tube torsion. The simplest possible example would live in J1​S4J^{1}S^{4}.
Problem 1.16.
Describe explicitly, or draw the front of a Legendrian in J1​S4J^{1}S^{4} with nontrivial tube torsion.
Although we do not know whether nearby Lagrangians must always have trivial tube torsion, or even whether they must always have trivial nearby Lagrangian torsion, in the upcoming work [AIS] we will prove the triviality of nearby Lagrangian torsion under the additional assumption that the front admits a ruling satisfying certain technical conditions, which we call a restricted ruling.
Finally we note that if a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M has a stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O which is not nullhomotopic, then it cannot admit any generating function, so that tube torsion is not defined. However, such an LL would already be a counterexample to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, since a Hamiltonian isotopy between LL and the zero section would in particular yield a nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map.
1.2. Structure of the article
In Section 2 we define a general class of invariants which we collectively call Legendrian higher torsion. In Section 3 we specialize to a specific version tailored to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, which we call tube torsion, and prove the existence of Legendrians which are diffeomorphic to the base and which have nontrivial tube torsion. In Section 4 we identify a distinguished coset of the tube torsion of a nearby Lagrangian, which we call nearby Lagrangian torsion, and prove that it is a Hamiltonian isotopy invariant. In Appendix A we give some background on functions with moderate singularities and in Appendix B we give some background on the definition of higher torsion invariants of fiber bundles.

2. Legendrian higher torsion
In this section we present a general construction of Legendrian invariants using higher torsion classes, which are certain characteristic classes of fiber bundles of smooth manifolds arising from algebraic K-theory.
2.1. Fiber bundles and fibrations at infinity
Let MM be a closed, connected smooth manifold. We consider fiber bundles of smooth manifolds F→W→MF\to W\to M and functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} on the total space, which we will often think of as families of functions fm:Fm→𝐑f_{m}:F_{m}\to\mathbf{R} on the fiber Fm≃FF_{m}\simeq F, parametrized by m∈Mm\in M. In the simplest case FF is a closed manifold. When FF is not a closed manifold we will have to add conditions to the possible functions g:F→𝐑g:F\to\mathbf{R} we consider. We use the terminology of [EG98] in the following definition.
Definition 2.1.
A function f:F→𝐑f:F\to\mathbf{R} is a fibration at infinity if there exists a finite segment [−a,a]⊂𝐑[-a,a]\subset\mathbf{R} and a compact subset K⊂f−1​[−a,a]⊂FK\subset f^{-1}[-a,a]\subset F such that the restriction of ff to the following three subsets fibers them over their respective images:
(1)
f−1(−∞,a]→(−∞,a]f^{-1}(-\infty,a]\to(-\infty,a]
(2)
f−1[a,∞)→[a,∞)f^{-1}[a,\infty)\to[a,\infty)
(3)
(f−1​[−a,a])∖K→[−a,a](f^{-1}[-a,a])\setminus K\to[-a,a]
As noted in [EG98, Section 0.2.1], a function f:F→𝐑f:F\to\mathbf{R} is a fibration at infinity if and only if FF admits a complete Riemannian metric with respect to which ‖d​f‖≥ε>0\|df\|\geq\varepsilon>0 outside of a compact subset.

The following are fibrations at infinity.

A non-degenerate quadratic form q:𝐑N→𝐑q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}.
(2)
A function f:𝐑N→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} of class C1C^{1} which is 2-homogeneous, i.e. f⁡(λ​v)=λ2​f​(v)f(\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}f(v), outside of a compact subset K⊂𝐑NK\subset\mathbf{R}^{N} and such that the function g:SN−1→𝐑g:S^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R} given by g⁡(v)=limλ→+∞f⁡(λ​v)g(v)=\lim_{\lambda\to+\infty}f(\lambda v) is a smooth function which has 00 as a regular value.
(3)
A function f:𝐑N→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} which is a bounded C1C^{1}-distance from a function as in (2).
(4)
The direct sum of two fibrations at infinity on the product domain.
For a first contact with higher Reidemeister torsion it will be sufficient to consider, for FF a closed manifold, the class of fiber bundles W→MW\to M over MM whose fiber is diffeomorphic to FF, together with their stabilizations. By the stabilization of a fiber bundle we will mean the fiberwise product with a trivial 𝐑4​N\mathbf{R}^{4N} bundle, which yields a new fiber bundle
F×𝐑4​N→W×𝐑4​N→M.F\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to M.
On this bundle (with non-compact fibers) we distinguish the following class of functions.
Definition 2.3.
Given MM and FF closed, smooth manifolds and F→W→MF\to W\to M a fiber bundle, we denote by 𝒮⁡(W)\mathcal{S}(W) the class of all of functions f:W×𝐑4​N→𝐑f:W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R} which are fibrations at infinity of the form
f⁡(w,x,y)=g⁡(w)+ε⁡(w,x,y)+‖x‖2−‖y‖2,(w,x,y)∈W×𝐑2​N×𝐑2​Nf(w,x,y)=g(w)+\varepsilon(w,x,y)+\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2},\qquad(w,x,y)\in W\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}
where g:W→𝐑g:W\to\mathbf{R} is a smooth function and ε\varepsilon is a smooth function with compact support. Functions f:W×𝐑4​N→𝐑f:W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R} in the class 𝒮⁡(W)\mathcal{S}(W) will be said to be admissible.
Remark 2.4.
Note that if f:W×𝐑4​N→𝐑f:W\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R} is admissible then in particular with respect to any Riemannian metric on WW it remains a bounded C1C^{1}-distance from the standard fiberwise quadratic form:
q⁡(w,x,y)=‖x‖2−‖y‖2,(w,x,y)∈W×𝐑2​N×𝐑2​N.q(w,x,y)=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2},\qquad(w,x,y)\in W\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}.

If instead of starting with a fiber bundle of closed manifolds F→W→MF\to W\to M we started with a fiber bundle whose fiber FF has boundary (or is non-compact), then some fixed asymptotic behavior near the boundary (or at infinity) for the functions g:W→𝐑g:W\to\mathbf{R} as in the above definition should be specified.

2.2. The Legendrian invariant
Let us first consider the case where the fiber FF is closed.
Definition 2.7.
A torsion pair (W,ρ)(W,\rho) consists of a fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M and a unitary local system ρ:π1​W→U⁡(1)\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1) such that H∗​(F,𝐂ρ)H_{*}(F;\mathbf{C}^{\rho}) is a unipotent π1​M\pi_{1}M-module.
Here H∗​(F,𝐂ρ)H_{*}(F;\mathbf{C}^{\rho}) denotes the homology of the fiber with the twisted complex coefficients given by ρ|π1​F\rho|_{\pi_{1}F} and by unipotent we mean that the π1​M\pi_{1}M-module H∗​(F,𝐂ρ)H_{*}(F;\mathbf{C}^{\rho}) admits a filtration by π1​M\pi_{1}M submodules so that the action of π1​M\pi_{1}M on the successive subquotients is trivial (a basepoint b∈Mb\in M should be chosen but this choice is immaterial).
Warning 2.8.
We should really write (π,ρ)(\pi,\rho) for π:W→M\pi:W\to M the fibration but we will abuse notation and write (W,ρ)(W,\rho) instead of (π,ρ)(\pi,\rho) throughout.
Given a torsion pair (W,ρ)(W,\rho), one has its higher Reidemeister torsion, which consists of cohomology classes τk​(W,ρ)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}), whose construction is reviewed in Appendix B.
By a Legendrian submanifold we will mean throughout a Legendrian embedding Λ↪J1​M\Lambda\hookrightarrow J^{1}M of a closed smooth manifold Λ\Lambda, i.e. compact and without boundary. By an immersed Legendrian we will mean a Legendrian immersion Λ→J1​M\Lambda\to J^{1}M of a closed smooth manifold Λ\Lambda, though we will abuse notation and denote both embedded and immersed Legendrians by Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M. To make the connection between functions on fiber bundles over MM and Legendrians in J1​MJ^{1}M we need an additional condition on the functions we consider. The condition is generic, and is the standard definition of a generating function for a Legendrian in a 1-jet space. Recall that the vertical differential of a function f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} on the total space of a fiber bundle π:W→M\pi:W\to M is the restriction dv​fd_{v}f of the usual differential d​f:T​W→𝐑df:TW\to\mathbf{R} to the vertical distribution ker⁡(d​π)⊂T​W\ker(d\pi)\subset TW.
Definition 2.9.
A function f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} is said to be a generating function if the vertical differential dv​fd_{v}f is transverse to zero.
If ff is a generating function, then ff determines a Legendrian immersion from the fiberwise singular locus Σf={dvf=0}⊂W\Sigma_{f}=\{d_{v}f=0\}\subset W into J1​MJ^{1}M. Indeed, the condition that dv​f⋔0d_{v}f\pitchfork 0 means that the graph Γ⁡(d​f)⊂T∗​W\Gamma(df)\subset T^{*}W intersects transversely the coisotropic sumanifold X⊂T∗​WX\subset T^{*}W consisting of covectors which are zero on the fibers Tm​FT_{m}F, m∈Mm\in M. The symplectic reduction of the coisotropic submanifold XX is naturally identified with T∗​MT^{*}M and the image of the submanifold X∩Γ⁡(d​f)=Γ⁡(d​f)|ΣfX\cap\Gamma(df)=\Gamma(df)|_{\Sigma_{f}} under this reduction is an immersed exact Lagrangian submanifold of T∗​MT^{*}M whose Legendrian lift (via the values of ff) is an immersed Legendrian submanifold Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M.
When moreover Λ\Lambda is an embedded Legendrian submanifold of J1​MJ^{1}M (which is the case generically), one says that the Legendrian submanifold Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M is generated by ff.
Now fix a collection ℱ\mathcal{F} of torsion pairs (W,ρ)(W,\rho). Examples of reasonable collections to consider include but are not limited to:
(1)
The singleton consisting of a fixed WW and a fixed ρ\rho.
(2)
The collection of all (W,ρ)(W,\rho) for a fixed WW but all possible ρ\rho for that WW.
(3)
The collection of all possible torsion pairs (W,ρ)(W,\rho) with fiber a fixed closed manifold FF.
Definition 2.10.
Let Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M be a Legendrian submanifold. The Legendrian higher torsion of Λ\Lambda in the class ℱ\mathcal{F} is the subset τ⁡(Λ,ℱ)⊂H∗​(M,𝐑)\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})\subset H^{*}(M;\mathbf{R}) consisting of all higher Reidemeister torsion classes τk​(W,ρ)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) of torsion pairs (W,ρ)(W,\rho) in ℱ\mathcal{F} such that Λ\Lambda admits a generating function f∈𝒮⁡(W)f\in\mathcal{S}(W).
Theorem 2.11.
If Λ0,Λ1⊂J1​M\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}\subset J^{1}M are Legendrian isotopic, then τ⁡(Λ0,ℱ)=τ⁡(Λ1,ℱ)\tau(\Lambda_{0},\mathcal{F})=\tau(\Lambda_{1},\mathcal{F}).
Proof.
As follows from the homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopy up to stabilization, if Λ0\Lambda_{0} admits a generating function in the class 𝒮⁡(W)\mathcal{S}(W), then so does Λ1\Lambda_{1}. The desired result then follows immediately. The homotopy lifting property for generating functions under Legendrian isotopies has a long history [Sik86, C96], but the formulation which is needed at this level of generality may be found in [EG98].
∎
2.3. Suitable classes of fiber bundles
Of course, given a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M, for most choices of ℱ\mathcal{F} we will have τ⁡(Λ)=∅\tau(\Lambda)=\varnothing, since many Legendrians don’t admit any generating function and even those which do will only admit generating functions on certain fiber bundles. More generally, if one wishes to use generating function theory to study a particular class of Legendrians, then one should make a judicious choice for the class ℱ\mathcal{F} of fiber bundles and notion of admissible generating function under consideration.
As a first example, if Λ\Lambda is disjoint from a single fiber Tx​M×𝐑⊂J1​MT_{x}M\times\mathbf{R}\subset J^{1}M then it won’t admit a generating function on any fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M with fiber FF a closed manifold, even after stabilization. In particular, if one wishes to study compact Legendrian submanifolds of J1​𝐑n=𝐑2​n+1J^{1}\mathbf{R}^{n}=\mathbf{R}^{2n+1} one should consider a theory of generating functions with zero fiberwise Morse homology. In the n=1n=1 case one could consider as in [JT] generating functions f:𝐑×𝐑k+1→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{k+1}\to\mathbf{R} on the trivial 𝐑k+1\mathbf{R}^{k+1}-bundle over 𝐑\mathbf{R} which are linear-quadratic at infinity, i.e. such that for (w,v,z)∈𝐑×𝐑k×𝐑(w,v,z)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\times\mathbf{R} there holds outside of a compact subset f⁡(w,v,z)=Q⁡(v)+L⁡(z)f(w,v,z)=Q(v)+L(z) for QQ a nondegenerate quadratic form and LL a nondegenerate linear form.
If instead one is interested in studying Legendrian braids in J1​S1J^{1}S^{1}, then one could consider as in [T01] generating functions f:S1×𝐑k→𝐑f:S^{1}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}\to\mathbf{R} on the trivial 𝐑k\mathbf{R}^{k}-bundle over S1S^{1} which are quadratic at infinity, i.e. such that for (w,v)∈S1×𝐑k(w,v)\in S^{1}\times\mathbf{R}^{k} there holds outside of a compact subset f⁡(w,v)=Q⁡(v)f(w,v)=Q(v) for QQ a nondegenerate quadratic form.
Other classes of fiber bundles may be useful for other purposes. We explain the simplest example of a nontrivial application of the theory of Legendrian higher torsion known to us, in which the class of fiber bundles under consideration consists of circle bundles over orientable surfaces. In [AI] the first two authors investigated a class of Legendrian submanifolds which admit generating functions on such circle bundles. Given an oriented circle bundle over an oriented surface S1→W→ΣS^{1}\to W\to\Sigma, with Euler number n∈𝐙n\in\mathbf{Z}, and given a rank 1 unitary local system ρ:π1​W→U⁡(1)\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1) such that the oriented fiber S1S^{1} gets sent to u−1u^{-1} for u≠1u\neq 1 an nn-th root of unity, the higher Reidemeister torsion τ1​(W,ρ)∈H2​(Σ,𝐑)≃𝐑\tau_{1}(W,\rho)\in H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R} is a constant multiple of the Euler number of the circle bundle up to sign, as was computed by the second author and J. Klein in [IK93a, IK93b]. Concretely, we have
τ1​(W,ρ)=n​Im​(ℒ2​(u))\tau_{1}(W,\rho)=n\,\,\text{Im}\left(\mathcal{L}_{2}(u)\right)
where ℒ2\mathcal{L}_{2} is the dilogarithm
ℒ2​(z)=∑k=1∞zkk2.\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{2}}.
That the higher Reidemeister torsion up to sign survives as a stable invariant of the circle bundle and is equal to the Euler class is a nontrivial fact. This fact is a special case of a deep result of Bökstedt [B82], and may also be deduced from the theory of higher Reidemeister torsion, see [Igu02], concretely Chapter 7 (complexified Bökstedt theorem). In [IK93a, IK93b] a more direct calculation for this special case is given.
The class of Legendrians Λ⊂J1​Σ\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma which were studied in [AI] are called mesh Legendrians. Each mesh Legendrian Λ\Lambda is determined by combintarial data and admits an explicit generating function on a circle bundle of Euler number ±eΛ\pm e_{\Lambda} for a non-negative integer eΛ∈𝐙≥0e_{\Lambda}\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0} which is defined in terms of the combinatorial data. In [AI] a calculation was carried out to determine the possible 2-parametric fiberwise Morse complex bifurcations that a generating function for the mesh Legendrian Λ\Lambda could support, on any such circle bundle over Σ\Sigma.
In particular it was shown that for any n∈𝐙≥0n\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0} a mesh Legendrian Λ\Lambda only admits a generating function on the circle bundle S1→W→ΣS^{1}\to W\to\Sigma of Euler number ±n\pm n if n=eΛn=e_{\Lambda}, even after stabilization of the circle bundle. Hence for the singleton class ℱ={(W,ρ)}\mathcal{F}=\{(W,\rho)\} consisting of a circle bundle S1→W→ΣS^{1}\to W\to\Sigma of Euler number ±n\pm n and ρ:π1​W→U⁡(1)\rho:\pi_{1}W\to U(1) a rank 1 unitary local system which sends the oriented fiber S1S^{1} to u−1u^{-1} for u≠1u\neq 1 an nn-th root of unity, the Legendrian higher torsion of a mesh Legendrian Λ⊂J1​Σ\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma in the class ℱ\mathcal{F} consists of the singleton
τ1​(Λ,ℱ)={eΛ​Im​(ℒ2​(u))}⊂H2​(Σ,𝐑)≃𝐑\tau_{1}(\Lambda,\mathcal{F})=\{e_{\Lambda}\text{Im}(\mathcal{L}_{2}(u))\}\subset H^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbf{R})\simeq\mathbf{R}
if n=eΛn=e_{\Lambda}, and is empty otherwise. This proves:
Theorem 2.12 (implicit in [AI]).
The Legendrian higher torsion of a mesh Legendrian Λ⊂J1​Σ\Lambda\subset J^{1}\Sigma recovers (and uniquely determines) the Euler number (up to sign) of the unique circle bundle that may be used to generate it, even after stabilization of the tube bundle and/or Legendrian isotopy of Λ\Lambda.
In fact in this specific situation the invariant can be refined to a Turaev type torsion which is also sensitive to the sign of the Euler number, and in this refined version the invariant was defined and used in [AI] to distinguish Legendrian isotopy classes of mesh Legendrians in J1​ΣJ^{1}\Sigma in the same formal Legendrian class. The computations of [AI] can be thought of as a proof of concept that higher torsion invariants may sometimes be calculated for explicit Legendrians.
Our final example of a class ℱ\mathcal{F} of fiber bundles which one may want to consider for the definition of a Legendrian higher torsion invariant, and the most important class for the purposes of the present article, is the class of tube bundles. Roughly speaking, tube bundles are the most general class of fiber bundles on which one might hope to construct generating functions for nearby Lagrangians. Hence tube bundles provide the natural class of fiber bundles in which to define a Legendrian higher torsion invariant that may be relevant in the context of the nearby Lagrangian conjecture. We discuss tube bundles and the associated Legendrian higher torsion invariant, which we call tube torsion, in Section 3 below.
2.4. Stable Gauss map
Given a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M, for the Legendrian higher torsion to be defined, i.e. for τ⁡(Λ,ℱ)\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F}) to be nonempty, in addition to the necessity of making a suitable choice for the class ℱ\mathcal{F} of fiber bundles (and unitary local systems) under consideration, it is also necessary that the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O is trivial, i.e. nullhomotopic Indeed, the triviality of the stable Gauss map is a necessary condition for the existence of any generating function, even in a very weak sense. Let us briefly recall the definition of the stable Gauss map.
Let πLag:J1​M=T∗​M×𝐑→T∗​M\pi_{\text{Lag}}:J^{1}M=T^{*}M\times\mathbf{R}\to T^{*}M denote the Lagrangian projection and consider the symplectic vector bundle E=πLag∗​T​(T∗​M)|ΛE=\pi_{\text{Lag}}^{*}T(T^{*}M)|_{\Lambda} over Λ\Lambda. Note that EE is equipped with two polarizations, i.e. Lagrangian sub-bundles, namely G=T​ΛG=T\Lambda (since πLag|Λ:Λ→T∗​M\pi_{\text{Lag}}|_{\Lambda}:\Lambda\to T^{*}M is a Lagrangian immersion) and V=πLag∗​ν|ΛV=\pi_{\text{Lag}}^{*}\nu|_{\Lambda}, where ν\nu is the Lagrangian distribution on T∗​MT^{*}M tangent to the fibers of the cotangent bundle projection T∗​M→MT^{*}M\to M. The stable Gauss map measures the stable difference between these two polarizations.
Concretely, pick a Lagrangian sub-bundle HH of EE which is transverse to VV, so that we have a isomorphism of symplectic vector bundles E≃H⊕H∗E\simeq H\oplus H^{*} under which VV gets identified with H∗H^{*}. There exists some real vector bundle H′H^{\prime} over Λ\Lambda such that H⊕H′H\oplus H^{\prime} is a trivial real vector bundle. Then H′⊕(H′)∗⊕E≃(H⊕H′)⊕(H⊕H′)∗H^{\prime}\oplus(H^{\prime})^{*}\oplus E\simeq(H\oplus H^{\prime})\oplus(H\oplus H^{\prime})^{*} is isomorphic to a trivial symplectic vector bundle Λ×𝐂m\Lambda\times\mathbf{C}^{m}. The Lagrangian sub-bundle H′⊕G⊂(H′⊕(H′)∗)⊕EH^{\prime}\oplus G\subset(H^{\prime}\oplus(H^{\prime})^{*})\oplus E may thus be viewed as a Lagrangian sub-bundle of the trivial symplectic vector bundle Λ×𝐂m\Lambda\times\mathbf{C}^{m}, and hence gives a map Λ→Um/Om\Lambda\to U_{m}/O_{m}. After letting m→∞m\to\infty we get the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O, which is easily verified to be independent of choices up to homotopy.
We say that the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O of a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M is trivial when it is nullhomotopic. That the triviality of the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O is a necessary condition for LL to admit any kind of generating function was shown by Giroux in [G90]. In particular, no matter what the class of fiber bundles ℱ\mathcal{F} under consideration is, for Legendrian higher torsion to be defined, i.e. for τ⁡(Λ,ℱ)\tau(\Lambda,\mathcal{F}) to be nonempty, we must have that the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O is trivial.
We will say that the front of Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M has moderate singularities if the front projection
Λ↪J1​M→J0​M=M×𝐑\Lambda\hookrightarrow J^{1}M\to J^{0}M=M\times\mathbf{R}
only has cusp singularities. This property is closely related to the triviality of the stable Gauss map. Indeed, for Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M a Legendrian with moderate singularities, consider the co-oriented hypersurface (S,ν)(S,\nu) where S⊂ΛS\subset\Lambda is the locus of points whose front have a cusp and ν\nu is the Maslov co-orientation of SS in Λ\Lambda, see [A]. For simplicity let us assume that Λ\Lambda is closed, connected and orientable. The co-oriented cycle (S,ν)(S,\nu) determines a class in Hn−1​(Λ,𝐙)H_{n-1}(\Lambda;\mathbf{Z}), where n=dimΛn=\dim\Lambda, which is Poincaré dual to the Maslov class μ1∈H1​(Λ,𝐙)\mu_{1}\in H^{1}(\Lambda,\mathbf{Z}). We have μ1=0\mu_{1}=0 if and only if there exists a bounding chain for (S,ν)(S,\nu), i.e. a collection of subdomains Ω1,…,Ωk⊂Λ\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{k}\subset\Lambda with disjoint boundaries such that ∐j∂Ωj=S\coprod_{j}\partial\Omega_{j}=S and for each component of SS realized as (part of) the boundary of Ωj\Omega_{j} the Maslov-coorientation ν\nu on this component agrees with the outwards pointing coorientation determined by Ωj\Omega_{j}. We then have:
Proposition 2.13.
Let Λ\Lambda be a closed, connected, smooth manifold and Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M a Legendrian embedding. If the front of Λ\Lambda has moderate singularities and if its Maslov class μ1∈H1​(Λ,𝐙)\mu_{1}\in H^{1}(\Lambda,\mathbf{Z}) is trivial, then the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O is trivial. In fact, any bounding chain for the pair (S,ν)(S,\nu) determines a homotopy class of nullhomotopy of the Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O.
Proof.
When Λ\Lambda has the homotopy type of a sphere of dimension >1>1 we have μ1=0\mu_{1}=0 for trivial reasons and the argument is given in [AD22]. The general case may be proved by a similar line of reasoning, see [B27] for details. ∎
Remark 2.14.
To be more precise, the statement of Proposition 2.13 should be that any bounding chain determines a homotopy of Lagrangian distributions between the stabilizations of GG and VV in the stabilization of the symplectic vector bundle EE, with notation as in the definition of the stable Gauss map above. So although the stable Gauss map Λ→U/O\Lambda\to U/O is only well-defined up to homotopy, we may still speak of a homotopy class of nullhomotopy of the stable Gauss map by defining this to be a homotopy of Lagrangian distributions between the stabilizations of GG and VV.
Note that if L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is a nearby Lagrangian, then the front of the Legendrian lift of LL has moderate singularities if and only if the tangencies of LL with respect to the foliation of T∗​MT^{*}M by the cotangent leaves Tm∗​MT^{*}_{m}M, m∈Mm\in M, has quadratic tangencies. When this is the case we will say that L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M has moderate tangencies. From Proposition 2.13 and the fact that nearby Lagrangians always trivial Maslov class [A12] it follows that any nearby Lagrangian with moderate tangencies has stably trivial Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O, and moreover there is a distinguished choice of homotopy class of nullhomotopy given any bounding chain for the cusp locus.
3. Legendrian tube torsion
In this section we focus on a specific version of Legendrian higher torsion which we call Legendrian tube torsion.
3.1. Generating functions for nearby Lagrangians
We recall that a nearby Lagrangian is an exact Lagrangian submanifold L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M, where LL and MM smooth manifolds which are closed, connected and of the same dimension. The nearby Lagrangian conjecture states that any nearby Lagrangian is isotopic through nearby Lagrangians to the zero section.
For a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M to admit a generating function on a fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M, it is necessary that the fiberwise Morse homology of the generating function have Euler characteristic ±1\pm 1. Indeed, the projection L→ML\to M is known to be a homotopy equivalence [FSS, Na09, A12, Kra13, Gui12], and in particular has degree ±1\pm 1.
The most basic example to keep in mind is the trivial bundle M×𝐑4​NM\times\mathbf{R}^{4N} with a generating function f:M×𝐑4​N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{4N}\to\mathbf{R} which is equal to the standard fiberwise quadratic form ‖x‖2−‖y‖2\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2} outside of a compact subset, (x,y)∈𝐑2​N×𝐑2​N(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{2N}\times\mathbf{R}^{2N}. We will call such generating functions standard quadratic at infinity. Note that the zero-section can be generated by such a function (namely the constant family of standard quadratic forms F⁡(m,x,y)=‖x‖2−‖y‖2F(m,x,y)=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}). Therefore, if the nearby Lagrangian conjecture is true, then by the homotopy lifting property for generating functions up to stabilization it would follow that any nearby Lagrangian admits a generating function standard quadratic at infinity.
While existence of such generating functions for nearby Lagrangians is not known, there has been recent progress on existence of generating functions for nearby Lagrangians on the more general class of so-called tube bundles. The class of tube bundles is a class of fiber bundles which was introduced by Waldhausen in his manifold approach to the algebraic K-theory of spaces [Wal82]. The precise definition of a tube bundle that we will work with in the present article will be given in Section 3.2 below, but let us note that in particular the fiberwise Morse homology of our notion of generating function of tube type has rank 1 concentrated in a single degree, just like a nondegenerate quadratic form. The foundational result of the article [ACGK] by Abouzaid, Courte, Guillermou and Kragh is the following.
Theorem 3.1 ([ACGK]).
A nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M with trivial stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O admits a generating function of tube type.
So the condition that L→U/OL\to U/O is trivial, which as explained in Section 2.4 is necessary for the existence of any kind of generating function, is in fact both necessary and sufficient for a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M to admit a generating function of tube type. It is not known whether every nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M must have stably trivial Gauss map. However, it was shown in [ACGK] that if L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M is a nearby Lagrangian, then L→U/OL\to U/O is trivial on homotopy groups. In particular, if MM is a homotopy sphere, then any nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M has stably trivial Gauss map and hence there holds:
Theorem 3.2 ([ACGK]).
If MM is a homotopy sphere, then any nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M admits a generating function of tube type.
Finally, the discussion at the end of Section 3 in [ACGK] (see the proof of Theorem 3.26 and preceding lemmas) makes it apparent that tube bundles are in some sense the most general class of fiber bundles on which one may expect a nearby Lagrangian to admit a generating function. The class of tube bundles therefore seems a reasonable class of fiber bundles to study in the context of the nearby Lagrangian conjecture, particularly in the case of nearby Lagrangian homotopy spheres.
3.2. Functions of tube type
Rather than the model for tube bundles and functions of tube type used in [ACGK] we will use a convenient model for the space of tubes which was developed in work of the first author joint with Abouzaid, Courte and Kragh in [AACK]. That Theorem 3.1 also holds for this model was proved in [AACK]. First we will need to introduce a few preliminary notions.
Definition 3.3.
A C1C^{1}-function g:𝐑N→𝐑g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a function of quadratic tube type if it satisfies the following:
(1)
gg is homogeneous of degree 2, i.e. g⁡(λ​v)=λ2​g​(v)g(\lambda v)=\lambda^{2}g(v).
(2)
gg has no critical point other than the origin.
(3)
gg is homotopic through functions satisfying (1) and (2) to a non-degenerate quadratic form.
Note that in general the homogenization of a smooth function h:SN−1→𝐑h:S^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R}, i.e. g⁡(v)=‖v‖2​h​(v/‖v‖)g(v)=\|v\|^{2}h(v/\|v\|) for v≠0v\neq 0 and g⁡(0)=0g(0)=0, will only be of class C1C^{1}.
Definition 3.4.
A smooth function f:𝐑N→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a function of tube type if there exists a function of quadratic tube type g:𝐑N→𝐑g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} such that ‖∇f−∇g‖≤C\|\nabla f-\nabla g\|\leq C for some constant C>0C>0.
Remark 3.5.
Note that if gg exists, then it is uniquely determined by ff, indeed g⁡(v)=limλ→∞1λ2​f​(λ​v)g(v)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}f(\lambda v).
Let 𝒯k,Nq\mathcal{T}_{k,N}^{q} denote the space of functions of quadratic tube type on 𝐑N\mathbf{R}^{N} such that the non-degenerate quadratic form in condition (3) has index kk, and let 𝒯k,N\mathcal{T}_{k,N} be the space of functions of tube type on 𝐑N\mathbf{R}^{N} such that the associated function of quadratic tube type is in 𝒯k,Nq\mathcal{T}_{k,N}^{q}. We also denote:
𝒯Nq=∐k=0N𝒯k,Nq,𝒯q=∐N=0∞𝒯Nq,𝒯N=∐k=0N𝒯k,N,𝒯=∐N=0∞𝒯N.\mathcal{T}^{q}_{N}=\coprod_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{T}^{q}_{k,N},\quad\mathcal{T}^{q}=\coprod_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}^{q}_{N},\quad\mathcal{T}_{N}=\coprod_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{T}_{k,N},\quad\mathcal{T}=\coprod_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{T}_{N}.
Note that if fi:𝐑Ni→𝐑f_{i}:\mathbf{R}^{N_{i}}\to\mathbf{R} is a function of tube type for i=1,2i=1,2, then f1⊕f2:𝐑N1×𝐑N2→𝐑f_{1}\oplus f_{2}:\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R} is also of tube type, so we have an operation 𝒯k1,N1×𝒯k2,N2↦𝒯k1+k2,N1+N2\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},N_{1}}\times\mathcal{T}_{k_{2},N_{2}}\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{k_{1}+k_{2},N_{1}+N_{2}} which endows 𝒯\mathcal{T} with the structure of a topological monoid. We also have the topological submonoid of functions of quadratic tube type 𝒯q⊂𝒯\mathcal{T}^{q}\subset\mathcal{T}. Clearly the inclusion 𝒯q⊂𝒯\mathcal{T}^{q}\subset\mathcal{T} is an equivalence as the linear interpolation (1−t)​f+t​g(1-t)f+tg of a function of tube type ff with its associated function of quadratic tube type gg gives a deformation retraction of 𝒯\mathcal{T} onto 𝒯q\mathcal{T}^{q}.
We also denote 𝒬k,N\mathcal{Q}_{k,N} for the space of non-degenerate quadratic forms q:𝐑N→𝐑q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} of index kk and
𝒬N=∐k=0N𝒬k,N,𝒬=∐N=0∞𝒬N.\mathcal{Q}_{N}=\coprod_{k=0}^{N}\mathcal{Q}_{k,N},\qquad\mathcal{Q}=\coprod_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{Q}_{N}.
Note that 𝒬k,N⊂𝒯k,Nq\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\subset\mathcal{T}_{k,N}^{q}, and that 𝒬\mathcal{Q} is a submonoid of 𝒯q\mathcal{T}^{q} and hence also of 𝒯\mathcal{T}.
Definition 3.6.
Let MM be a closed, connected, smooth manifold.
(1)
A fibered function of tube type is a smooth function f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} such that for each m∈Mm\in M the restriction fm:𝐑N→𝐑f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is of tube type, i.e. such that fm∈𝒯Nf_{m}\in\mathcal{T}_{N} for each m∈Mm\in M.
(2)
A fibered function of quadratic tube type is a C1C^{1} function f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} such that for each m∈Mm\in M the restriction fm:𝐑N→𝐑f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is quadratic of tube type, i.e. such that fm∈𝒯Nqf_{m}\in\mathcal{T}_{N}^{q} for each m∈Mm\in M.
(3)
A fibered function of rigid tube type is a smooth function f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} such that for each m∈Mm\in M the restriction fm:𝐑N→𝐑f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a non-degenerate quadratic form, i.e. if fm∈𝒬Nf_{m}\in\mathcal{Q}_{N} for each m∈Mm\in M.
Remark 3.7.
Since MM is connected, the index kk of any such fibered function of tube type is well-defined.
To a fibered function of tube type f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} we may associate the fibered function of quadratic tube type g:M×𝐑N→𝐑g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} such that for each m∈Mm\in M the restriction gm:𝐑N→𝐑g_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is the function of quadratic tube type associated to the restriction fm:𝐑N→𝐑f_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}.
Fibered functions of tube type over MM form a monoid. Fibered functions of quadratic tube type over MM form submonoid of the monoid of fibered functions of tube type over MM, and fibered quadratic functions of rigid tube type form yet a smaller submonoid.
We will reserve the following terminology for the monoid operation on fibered functions of tube type when one of the two functions is rigid.
Definition 3.8.
Let f:M×𝐑N1→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\to\mathbf{R} be a fibered function of tube type and let q:M×𝐑N2→𝐑q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R} be a fibered function of rigid tube type. We call f⊕q:M×𝐑N1×𝐑N2→𝐑f\oplus q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbf{R}^{N_{2}}\to\mathbf{R} the twisted stabilization of ff by qq.
By a stabilization without the adjective twisted we will mean a stabilization with respect to a constant fibered function of rigid tube type q:M×𝐑N→𝐑q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}, i.e. q⁡(x,v)=Q⁡(v)q(x,v)=Q(v) for some fixed non-degenerate quadratic form Q:𝐑N→𝐑Q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}. In fact, typically we will stabilize by the standard form on 𝐑4\mathbf{R}^{4} given by q⁡(x,y)=‖x‖2−‖y‖2q(x,y)=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2} for (x,y)∈𝐑2×𝐑2(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{R}^{2}, and will refer to such a stabilization as a standard stabilization.
Definition 3.9.
Let q:M×𝐑N→𝐑q:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} be a fibered function of rigid tube type, so that q​(m,x)=Qm​(x)q(m,x)=Q_{m}(x) for Qm:𝐑N→𝐑Q_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} a family of non-degenerate quadratic forms. Let E→ME\to M be the real vector bundle whose fiber EmE_{m} over m∈Mm\in M is the negative eigenspace of the matrix (∂2Qm/∂xi​∂xj)i,j(\partial^{2}Q_{m}/\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j})_{i,j}. We call E→ME\to M the stable bundle of QQ.
Remark 3.10.
Alternatively, a fibered function of ridid tube type is classified by a map M→𝒬M\to\mathcal{Q}, the notion of stable bundle for a non-degenerate quadratic form determines a map 𝒬k,N→B​O​(k,N)\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\to BO(k,N), and hence the stable bundle of a fibered function of ridid tube type is classified by the composition M→𝒬→𝐙×B​OM\to\mathcal{Q}\to\mathbf{Z}\times BO. Note that the map 𝒬k,N→B​O​(k,N)\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\to BO(k,N) is a homotopy equivalence, with homotopy inverse the map B​O​(k,N)→𝒬k,NBO(k,N)\to\mathcal{Q}_{k,N} which sends a vector subspace E⊂𝐑NE\subset\mathbf{R}^{N} of dimension kk to the quadratic form Q⁡(x)=‖xE⟂‖2−‖xE‖2Q(x)=\|x_{E^{\perp}}\|^{2}-\|x_{E}\|^{2}, where x=xE+xE⟂x=x_{E}+x_{E^{\perp}} is the decomposition of x∈𝐑Nx\in\mathbf{R}^{N} into its EE and E⟂E^{\perp} components.
3.3. Torsion of fibered functions of tube type
Note that the second condition in Definition 3.3 is equivalent to asking that gg is transverse to zero outside of the origin, so for a function of quadratic tube type g:𝐑N→𝐑g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} the set T(g)={g≤0}∩SN−1T(g)=\{g\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1} is a codimension-zero submanifold of SN−1S^{N-1} (a priori only C1C^{1}, but C1C^{1} submanifolds have unique smoothings up to contractible choice of isotopy). By the third condition of Definition 3.3 this submanifold is isotopic in SN−1S^{N-1} to T(q)={q≤0}∩SN−1T(q)=\{q\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1} for qq a non-degenerate quadratic form. If E−E^{-} is the negative eigenspace of qq, then this domain is a tubular neighborhood of the equator E−∩SN−1E^{-}\cap S^{N-1} and in particular is diffeomorphic to Sk−1×DN−kS^{k-1}\times D^{N-k} for kk the index of qq and Dm={∥x∥≤1}⊂𝐑mD^{m}=\{\|x\|\leq 1\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{m} the unit disk.
Definition 3.11.
A rigid tube is the codimension zero submanifold T⁡(q)⊂SN−1T(q)\subset S^{N-1} for qq a non-degenerate quadratic form. The index of a rigid tube is the index of qq.
Definition 3.12.
A tube is a codimension zero submanifold T⊂SN−1T\subset S^{N-1} which is isotopic in SN−1S^{N-1} to a rigid tube. The index of a tube is the index of any rigid tube it is isotopic to.

Definition 3.13.
A tube bundle is a fiber bundle W→MW\to M over a smooth manifold MM such that
(1)
WW is a codimension zero submanifold of M×SN−1M\times S^{N-1}
(2)
the map W→MW\to M is the restriction to WW of the projection M×SN−1→MM\times S^{N-1}\to M.
(3)
for each x∈Mx\in M the fiber over xx is a tube Tx⊂SN−1T_{x}\subset S^{N-1}.
If for each x∈Mx\in M the fiber of a tube bundle W→MW\to M is a rigid tube Tx=T⁡(qx)⊂SN−1T_{x}=T(q_{x})\subset S^{N-1}, where qx:𝐑N→𝐑q_{x}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a family of nondegenerate quadratic forms, then we call WW a rigid tube bundle. If g:M×𝐑N→𝐑g:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a fibered function of quadratic tube type, then we will denote by T⁡(g)→MT(g)\to M the associated tube bundle T(g)={g≤0}∩SN−1T(g)=\{g\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}. If f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a fibered function of tube type, we will denote T⁡(f)=T⁡(g)T(f)=T(g) the tube bundle associated to the underlying fibered function of quadratic tube type gg.
Warning 3.14.
If f:𝐑N→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} is a function of tube type, then T⁡(f)T(f) is not {f≤0}∩SN−1\{f\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}. It is {g≤0}∩SN−1\{g\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1} for g⁡(x)=‖x‖2​limλ→∞f⁡(λ​x)g(x)=\|x\|^{2}\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda x). The same comment applies (fiberwise) for fibered functions of tube type f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} and their associated tube bundle T⁡(f)→MT(f)\to M.
Definition 3.15.
We will say that a tube bundle W→MW\to M of index kk is orientable if the action of π1​M\pi_{1}M on the homology of the fiber Hk−1​(T,𝐙)≃𝐙H_{k-1}(T;\mathbf{Z})\simeq\mathbf{Z} is trivial.

So τ2​k+1​(W)=0\tau_{2k+1}(W)=0 in H4​k+2​(M,𝐑)H^{4k+2}(M;\mathbf{R}) and we are left with the classes τ2​k​(W)∈H4​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{2k}(W)\in H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}).

3.6. The space of tubes
We briefly compare the model for the space of tubes considered in this article with that of Waldhausen [Wal82] in order to translate some relevant results from the literature. Recall that 𝒯N,k\mathcal{T}_{N,k} is the space of all functions f:𝐑N→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} of tube type, see Definition 3.4. The space 𝒯N,k\mathcal{T}_{N,k} deformation retracts onto the subspace 𝒯N,kq\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q} of functions g:𝐑N→𝐑g:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} of quadratic tube type, see Definition 3.3.
Any tube T⊂SN−1T\subset S^{N-1} has two non-negative integers associated to it, namely the ambient dimension NN, so that T⊂SN−1T\subset S^{N-1} is a codimension zero submanifold, and its index kk, which is the integer k≥0k\geq 0 such that T⊂SN−1T\subset S^{N-1} is isotopic to T⁡(q)⊂SN−1T(q)\subset S^{N-1} for q:𝐑N→𝐑q:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} a non-degenerate quadratic form of index kk. The standard tube of type (N,k)(N,k) is TN,k=T(qk,N)={qk,N≤0}∩SN−1T_{N,k}=T(q_{k,N})=\{q_{k,N}\leq 0\}\cap S^{N-1} for qk,N:𝐑N→𝐑q_{k,N}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} the standard index kk quadratic form ‖x‖2−‖y‖2\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}, (x,y)∈𝐑N−k×𝐑k(x,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{N-k}\times\mathbf{R}^{k}. By definition a tube of type (N,k)(N,k) is a codimension zero submanifold T⊂SN−1T\subset S^{N-1} which is isotopic to the standard tube of type (N,k)(N,k). One may consider the space 𝒯~N,kq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} of all tubes of type (N,k)(N,k), for example using a simplicial space model, or otherwise.
Any reasonable model for 𝒯~N,kq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} would come equipped with a map 𝒯N,kq→𝒯~N,kq\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} given by g↦T⁡(g)g\mapsto T(g) whose fiber may be identified with the space of all functions h:SN−1→𝐑h:S^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R} such that T={h≤0}T=\{h\leq 0\}, with ∂T={h=0}\partial T=\{h=0\} a regular level set of hh. For fixed TT, the space of such hh is convex, hence contractible. Such a function hh determines a unique function of quadratic tube type g⁡(v)=‖v‖2​h​(v/‖v‖)g(v)=\|v\|^{2}h(v/\|v\|). For any reasonable model the map 𝒯N,kq→𝒯~N,kq\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} is a Serre fibration and hence one has a weak equivalence 𝒯N,kq≃𝒯~N,kq\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q}\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} and hence also a weak equivalence 𝒯N,k≃𝒯~N,kq\mathcal{T}_{N,k}\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k}.
Waldhausen considered in [Wal82] the following similar though slightly different notion of tubes in terms of partitions. For EE a kk-dimensional linear subspace of 𝐑N−2\mathbf{R}^{N-2}, consider the codimension zero submanifold with boundary TE⊂𝐑N−1T_{E}\subset\mathbf{R}^{N-1} obtained by attaching to the half-space {xN−1≤0}\{x_{N-1}\leq 0\} inside 𝐑N−1\mathbf{R}^{N-1} a standard (N−1)(N-1)-dimensional index kk handle along the unit sphere of E⊂{xN−1=0}=𝐑N−2E\subset\{x_{N-1}=0\}=\mathbf{R}^{N-2}. More concretely, TET_{E} is the union in 𝐑N−1\mathbf{R}^{N-1} of the half-space {xN−1≤0}\{x_{N-1}\leq 0\} and an ε\varepsilon-neighborhood of the kk-dimensional unit sphere of the (k+1)(k+1)-dimensional subspace of 𝐑N−1\mathbf{R}^{N-1} spanned by E⊕0⊂𝐑N−1E\oplus 0\subset\mathbf{R}^{N-1} and ∂/∂xN−1\partial/\partial x_{N-1}, suitably smoothed so that TET_{E} is a smooth codimension zero submanifold with boundary. Waldhausen called such a codimension zero submanifold TE⊂𝐑N−1T_{E}\subset\mathbf{R}^{N-1} a rigid tube. Since the thickening and smoothing are homotopically canonical, for fixed kk and NN the space of rigid tubes TET_{E} in 𝐑N−1\mathbf{R}^{N-1} as above has the homotopy type of B​O​(k,N−1)BO(k,N-1), the Grassmannian of real kk-dimensional linear subspaces of 𝐑N−1\mathbf{R}^{N-1}.
Waldhausen defined a tube to be any codimension zero submanifold T⊂𝐑N−1T\subset\mathbf{R}^{N-1} which is the image of a rigid tube under a smooth isotopy of 𝐑N−1\mathbf{R}^{N-1}, fixed outside of a compact set. We will denote the space of tubes in the sense of Waldhausen by 𝒯~N,kp\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p}, where pp stands for partition. We may also form a suitable space of functions of tube type f:𝐑N−1→𝐑f:\mathbf{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbf{R} in the sense of Waldhausen, (so that in particular {f≤0}⊂𝐑N−1\{f\leq 0\}\subset\mathbf{R}^{N-1} is a tube in the sense of Waldhausen) and denote it by 𝒯N,kp\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p}. Any reasonable model would again come equipped with an equivalence 𝒯N,kp→𝒯~N,kp\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p} given by f↦{f≤0}f\mapsto\{f\leq 0\}. See [ACGK] for a precise definition of a space of functions of tube type very close to the space 𝒯N,kp\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p} described above.
The difference between the model 𝒯~N,kq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} for the space of tubes considered in this article and the Waldhausen model 𝒯~N,kp\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{N,k}^{p} in terms of partitions, at the (N,k)(N,k) level, amounts to requiring the tubes in 𝒯~N,kq\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{q}_{N,k} to be standard in a fixed (N−1)(N-1)-dimensional closed ball B⊂SN−1B\subset S^{N-1}, say B={xN≥0}∩SN−1B=\{x_{N}\geq 0\}\cap S^{N-1}. More concretely, with respect to the fixed diffeomorphism φ:DN−1→B\varphi:D^{N-1}\to B given by

The monoid 𝒯q=∐N,k𝒯N,kq\mathcal{T}^{q}=\coprod_{N,k}\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{q} consisting of functions of quadratic tube type which we consider in the present article was introduced in [AACK], and the function space version of 𝒯∞\mathcal{T}_{\infty} is realized by implementing stabilization of 𝒯q\mathcal{T}^{q} as direct sum with suitable standard non-degenerate quadratic forms. We also have a stable space 𝒬∞\mathcal{Q}_{\infty} given by stabilizing N,k→∞N,k\to\infty in 𝒬N,k\mathcal{Q}_{N,k} in the same way, and the inclusion 𝒬k,N→𝒯k,N\mathcal{Q}_{k,N}\to\mathcal{T}_{k,N} stabilizes to a map 𝒬∞→𝒯∞\mathcal{Q}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}.

3.7. Legendrians with nontrivial tube torsion
We are now ready to establish for general MM the existence of Legendrians Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M such that the projection Λ→M\Lambda\to M is homotopic to a diffeomorphism and which have nontrivial tube torsion. In particular this shows that the vanishing of the tube torsion of nearby Lagrangians L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M predicted by the nearby Lagrangian conjecture cannot rely solely on the fact that the projection L→ML\to M is a homotopy equivalence (or even from a conjectural expectation that the projection L→ML\to M is homotopic to a diffeomorphism, which is not known).
Recall that given a bundle of h-cobordisms of a disk V→MV\to M, the stable equivalence class of the bundle is classified by the homotopy class of a map M→ℋ∞M\to\mathcal{H}_{\infty}, see Section 3.6. By the fibration sequence ℋ∞→𝒯∞→B​G\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}\to BG of Waldhausen [Wal82] there is an associated tube bundle W→MW\to M which geometrically is obtained by attaching VV onto a trivial bundle of tubes M×T→MM\times T\to M.
We recall that Theorem 1.15 states that given a bundle of h-cobordisms of a disk V→MV\to M, there exists a Legendrian Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M whose projection Λ→M\Lambda\to M is homotopic to a diffeomorphism and which admits a generating function on the tube bundle W→MW\to M obtained from VV via the map ℋ∞→𝒯∞\mathcal{H}_{\infty}\to\mathcal{T}_{\infty}.

4. Nearby Lagrangian torsion
4.1. Tube torsion of nearby Lagrangians
Recall from Section 2.4 that for a nearby Lagrangian L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M to have any chance of admitting any kind of generating function, the stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O of LL must be trivial, i.e. nullhomotopic. As was also discussed in Section 3.1, this condition is also sufficient to ensure existence of generating functions of tube type:
Theorem 4.1 ([ACGK]).
Let L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M be a nearby Lagrangian with trivial stable Gauss map L→U/OL\to U/O. Then LL admits a generating function of tube type f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}.
To be precise, in [ACGK] this result was proved for a slightly different notion of function of tube type, the space of which is close to the space we denoted by 𝒯N,kp\mathcal{T}_{N,k}^{p} in Section 3.6. Given a Legendrian submanifold Λ⊂J1​M\Lambda\subset J^{1}M, a local generating function for Λ\Lambda consists of a generating function f:U→𝐑f:U\to\mathbf{R} where U⊂M×𝐑NU\subset M\times\mathbf{R}^{N} is some open subset. Let L⊂T∗​ML\subset T^{*}M be a nearby Lagrangian with moderate tangencies and f:M×𝐑N→𝐑f:M\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} a generating function of tube type for LL. Must it be the case that τ2​k​(f)∈im​(pk)\tau_{2k}(f)\in\text{im}(p_{k})?

In the future work [AIS] we show that the answer is affirmative if we assume that the front of LL carries a restricted ruling, which is a higher dimensional version of the familiar notion of a ruling of a 1-dimensional Legendrian front. It would be interesting to know if there is a Floer theoretic proof of the triviality of nearby Lagrangian torsion, as predicted by the nearby Lagrangian conjecture. Indeed, one might expect the Floer theoretic setup to be more sensitive to the property of being an exact Lagrangian submanifold than the Morse theoretic approach via generating functions.
In the Floer set-up, the embeddedness implies there are no pseudo-holomorphic disks with a single boundary puncture that arise in as limiting-bubbles in Gromov compactness. Algebraically, this enables one to avoid relying on so-called augmentations from Legendrian/symplectic field theory.
In contrast, it is far from clear how the lack of Reeb chords is related to the paramerized Morse theory of a generating function, which after all is a fiberwise phenomenon.
Indeed, the Morse-theoretic theory of higher torsion via generating functions makes sense for Legendrians in J1​MJ^{1}M, whether or not they have Reeb chords.

A.2. The stable bundle
Let F→W→MF\to W\to M be a fiber bundle and let f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} be a function with moderate singularities. The fiberwise singular set Σf⊂W\Sigma_{f}\subset W is a smooth submanifold of WW of the same dimension as MM. It has a stratification Σf=⋃kΣf,k\Sigma_{f}=\bigcup_{k}\Sigma_{f,k} where Σf,k\Sigma_{f,k} is the closure of the set of fiberwise Morse critical points of index kk. Let Cf,k⊂ΛfC_{f,k}\subset\Lambda_{f} consist of the set of fiberwise cubic (birth/death) critical points of ff of index kk and k+1k+1. So the boundary of Λf,k\Lambda_{f,k} consists of the disjoint union of Cf,k−1C_{f,k-1} and Cf,kC_{f,k}.
On the interior of Σf,k\Sigma_{f,k} we have a rank kk real vector bundle γf,k→Σf,k\gamma_{f,k}\to\Sigma_{f,k} consisting of the negative eigenspace of the fiberwise Hessian along the fiberwise critical locus. Each γf,k\gamma_{f,k} extends to the boundary Cf,k​∐Cf,k−1C_{f,k}\coprod C_{f,k-1} of Σf,k\Sigma_{f,k} as sub-bundles of the fiberwise tangent bundle of WW, but along Cf,kC_{f,k} the dimensions of γf,k\gamma_{f,k} and γf,k+1\gamma_{f,k+1} jump by one. More precisely, we have the relation γf,k⊕νk=γf,k+1\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\nu_{k}=\gamma_{f,k+1} where νk\nu_{k} is the kernel of the Hessian, a rank 1 bundle along Cf,kC_{f,k}. So we may form a vector bundle over Σf=⋃kΣf,k\Sigma_{f}=\bigcup_{k}\Sigma_{f,k} by using the canonical co-orientation to glue the vector bundles γf,k⊕εN−k\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k} on Σf,k\Sigma_{f,k}, where ε\varepsilon denotes the trivial rank 1 bundle, to a rank NN vector bundle on Σf\Sigma_{f}. γf,k⊕εN−k≃γf,k⊕νk⊕εN−k−1≃γf,k+1⊕εN−k−1.\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k}\simeq\gamma_{f,k}\oplus\nu_{k}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k-1}\simeq\gamma_{f,k+1}\oplus\varepsilon^{N-k-1}.

A.3. Framed functions
For F→W→MF\to W\to M a fiber bundle, a framed function f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} is roughly speaking a function with moderate singularities whose stable bundle γf\gamma_{f} is trivial and moreover equipped with a trivialization. More precisely, one requires the existence of isomorphisms
φk:γf,k→≃εk\varphi_{k}:\gamma_{f,k}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\varepsilon^{k}
which are compatible with the above gluing maps. We will refer to the following result as the framed function theorem.
Theorem A.1.
For F→W→MF\to W\to M a fiber bundle, given a closed subset A⊂WA\subset W and a framed function g:O​p​(A)→𝐑g:Op(A)\to\mathbf{R} there exists a framed function f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} which agrees with gg on O​p​(A)Op(A).
In particular, for any fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M framed functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} exist, and if f0f_{0} and f1f_{1} are two such then there exists a framed function F:W×[0,1]→𝐑F:W\times[0,1]\to\mathbf{R} on the product fiber bundle F×[0,1]→W×[0,1]→M×[0,1]F\times[0,1]\to W\times[0,1]\to M\times[0,1] such that F|W×0=f0F|_{W\times 0}=f_{0} and FW×1=f1F_{W\times 1}=f_{1}.
Theorem A.1 in particular implies, and is essentially equivalent to, the fact that a suitable model for the space of framed functions on the fiber h:F→𝐑h:F\to\mathbf{R} is contractible, even in a strong relative form. The high connectivity of the space of framed functions was first established by work of the second author [Igu87]. The strongest form is given by the Extension Theorem 1.1.1 of [EM12], slightly reformulated as Theorem A.1 above. The fact that framed functions may be chosen and are independent of choices is central to the definition of higher torsion invariants.
We note the inevitability of relying on some result in the theory of simplification of singularities to even establish the existence of functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} with moderate singularities. This existence is not a priori guaranteed because the condition of having only moderate singularities, though C3C^{3}-stable, is far from C∞C^{\infty}-generic in general. Indeed, C∞C^{\infty}-generic functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} will have terrible and completely unclassifiable fiberwise singularities.

Appendix B Higher torsion of fiber bundles
B.1. Higher torsion classes
Higher torsion invariants are characteristic classes of smooth fiber bundles F→W→MF\to W\to M of smooth manifolds, defined under suitable conditions. The theory has analytic treatments [BL95, BG01], homotopy-theoretic treatments [DWW] and axiomatic treatments [Igu08], in addition to the Morse-theoretic perspective we adopt in the present article, following the book [Igu02].
From the Morse-theoretic viewpoint, a fiber bundle may be studied by considering smooth functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} and analyzing the parametrized Morse theory of the restrictions
fm:=f|Fm:Fm→𝐑f_{m}:=f|_{F_{m}}:F_{m}\to\mathbf{R}
of ff to the fiber, parametrized by m∈Mm\in M. When f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} has moderate singularities in the sense of Appendix A, after fixing a generic fiberwise metric one may construct the family of Morse chain complexes C∗​(fm:𝐙⁡[π1​W])C^{*}(f_{m}:\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W]) where we may take the coefficients in 𝐙⁡[π1​W]\mathbf{Z}[\pi_{1}W]. In this appendix we briefly recall how this data may be used to yield the higher torsion classes.

See [Ra]. Since |Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)||\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| is rationally equivalent to Ω​K​(𝐂)\Omega K(\mathbf{C}), we are able to obtain universal higher torsion invariants
τk∈H2​k​(|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|,𝐑).\tau_{k}\in H^{2k}(|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|;\mathbf{R}).
These universal classes will be used to define higher torsion. The explicit cohomology class used in [Igu02] comes from the Dupont formulation of the Kamber-Tondeur form [D76].
B.3. Definition of higher torsion
Consider again a fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M, where we now assume that π1​M\pi_{1}M acts unipotently on the rational fiber homology H∗​(Fb,𝐐)H_{*}(F_{b};\mathbf{Q}). We would like to define cohomology classes in H2​k​(M,𝐑)H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) by pulling back the class τk\tau_{k} via the map
ξ:M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|\xi:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
given by a choice of fiberwise (generalized) Morse function f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} and choice of metric.
As explained above, τk\tau_{k} is the restriction to |Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)||\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| of the universal higher torsion class τk∈H2​k​(Wh∙h​(𝐂,U⁡(1)),𝐑)\tau_{k}\in H^{2k}(\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},U(1));\mathbf{R}) given in [Igu05, subsection 2.7] and ξ\xi is the “expansion functor”, see [Igu05, Def 2.18].
At this point questions of existence and uniqueness of such ff arise. Although it is a (nontrivial) fact that fiberwise generalized Morse functions exist on any fiber bundle, it is not true that any two such functions will be joined by a family of such functions. Indeed, the map ξ:M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|\xi:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| obtained by the above construction may not be unique up to homotopy.
There is a canonical way to select the correct map M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| by means of a framed function. For any fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M, and any two framed functions f0,f1:W→𝐑f_{0},f_{1}:W\to\mathbf{R}, by Theorem A.1 there exists a framed function on the product bundle W×[0,1]→M×[0,1]W\times[0,1]\to M\times[0,1] which restricts to f0f_{0} and f1f_{1} over M×{0}M\times\{0\} and M×{1}M\times\{1\} respectively. This gives a map
M×[0,1]→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|M\times[0,1]\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|
which may be viewed as a homotopy between the maps M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| given by f0f_{0} and f1f_{1}. Hence by choosing a framed function on WW we get a well-defined and distinguished homotopy class of map
ξ0:M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|.\xi_{0}:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|.
Definition B.1.
Assume that π1​M\pi_{1}M acts unipotently on H∗​(Fb,𝐐)H_{*}(F_{b};\mathbf{Q}). The higher torsion classes τk​(W)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) are defined as the pullback of the universal classes τk∈H2​k​(|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|,𝐑)\tau_{k}\in H^{2k}(|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)|;\mathbf{R}) under the homotopically unique map ξ0:M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|\xi_{0}:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| determined by a framed function on WW.
Example B.2.
The degree zero class τ0​(W,ρ)∈H0​(M,𝐑)\tau_{0}(W,\rho)\in H^{0}(M;\mathbf{R}) is just the usual Franz-Reidemeister torsion of the fiber twisted by the local system ρ|π1​F\rho|_{\pi_{1}F}, maybe after taking absolute value and natural logarithm depending on conventions [Fra35, Rei35].
B.4. Higher Reidemeister torsion with twisted coefficients
We can generalize the above discussion to fiber bundles F→W→MF\to W\to M equipped with a rank 1 unitary local system ρ:π1​M→U⁡(1)\rho:\pi_{1}M\to U(1) with finite image GG such that the action of π1​M\pi_{1}M on the twisted fiber homology H∗​(Fm,𝐂ρ)H_{*}(F_{m};\mathbf{C}^{\rho}) is unipotent. The framed function theorem still gives us a well-defined map (up to homotopy)
ξρ:M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,G)|\xi_{\rho}:M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|
and we get classes τk​(W,ρ)∈H2​k​(M,𝐑)\tau_{k}(W,\rho)\in H^{2k}(M;\mathbf{R}) by pulling back the universal classes. In fact, if real coefficients are used instead of complex coefficients, then the odd classes automatically vanish, i.e. the pullback of τ2​k+1\tau_{2k+1} to |Wh∙h​(𝐑,G)||\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{R},G)| is zero, G={±1}G=\{\pm 1\}. So to get nontrivial odd torsion classes τ2​k+1​(W,ρ)∈H4​k+2​(M,𝐑)\tau_{2k+1}(W,\rho)\in H^{4k+2}(M;\mathbf{R}) one needs to use a nontrivial unitary local system.
The use of unitary local systems can be convenient depending on the application, for example in [AI] the higher Reidemeister torsion of circle bundles over the sphere is shown to compute a stable version of the Euler number. However, for the specific version of higher torsion we will focus on in the present article there is no local system. Indeed we will use 𝐙\mathbf{Z} coefficients, and so we will only get the even classes τ2​k\tau_{2k} in H4​k​(M,𝐑)H^{4k}(M;\mathbf{R}).
B.5. The framing principle
A useful result for computations of higher torsion is the framing principle, which gives the precise correction term needed to compute the higher Reidemeister torsion for a function f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} with moderate singularities, even if the stable bundle EE is not trivial (and hence ff is not frameable). The formula [Igu02, Theorem 4.11] is the following.
Theorem B.3.
If f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} is a function with moderate singularities, then
(1)
τ⁡(W,ρ)=∑jτj​(f,ρ)+12​∑k(−1)k​ζ​(2​k+1)​π∗Σ​f​(c​h2​k​(γf⊗𝐂))\tau(W,\rho)=\sum_{j}\tau_{j}(f,\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}(-1)^{k}\zeta(2k+1)\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}\left(ch_{2k}(\gamma_{f}\otimes\mathbf{C})\right)
where the terms are defined below.
•
τj​(f,ρ)∈H2​j​(M,𝐑)\tau_{j}(f,\rho)\in H^{2j}(M;\mathbf{R}) is the pull-back of the universal class τj\tau_{j} under M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,G)|M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|, the mapping given by the family of Morse chain complexes associated to the family of functions FmF_{m}, ,m∈M,m\in M. We are assuming that ρ\rho has a finite image G⊂U⁡(1)G\subset U(1).
•
Σf\Sigma_{f} is the fiberwise singular set of ff. It has the same dimension as the base MM and is a union Σf=⋃Σf,k\Sigma_{f}=\bigcup\Sigma_{f,k} as discussed in Appendix A.
•
γf\gamma_{f} is the stable bundle of ff as also discussed in Appendix A.
•
The map π∗Σ​f:H4​k​(Σ)→H4​k​(M)\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}:H^{4k}(\Sigma)\to H^{4k}(M) is, at the cochain level, the alternating sum of push-down operators:
π∗Σ​f=∑i(−1)i​π∗:C4​k​(Σf,i)→C4​k​(M).\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\pi_{\ast}:C^{4k}(\Sigma_{f,i})\to C^{4k}(M).
Explicitly, given a 4​k4k cocycle φ\varphi on Σf\Sigma_{f} we take the restriction φi\varphi_{i} of φ\varphi on each Σf,i\Sigma_{f,i}. This is well-defined away from the boundary of Σf,i\Sigma_{f,i}. The image of this in C4​k​(M)C^{4k}(M) is the 4​k4k-cocycle which takes a small 4​k4k simplex σ\sigma in MM, takes all sections σ~i\tilde{\sigma}_{i} of σ\sigma to Σf,i\Sigma_{f,i} and evaluates
π∗Σ​f​(φ)​(σ)=∑(−1)i​φi​(σ~i).\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}(\varphi)(\sigma)=\sum(-1)^{i}\varphi_{i}(\tilde{\sigma}_{i}).
The undefined terms cancel since, when σ~i\tilde{\sigma}_{i} reaches the boundary of Σf,i\Sigma_{f,i}, it is at a cusp which is the same location as a section σ~i+1\tilde{\sigma}_{i+1} (or σ~i−1\tilde{\sigma}_{i-1}). The alternating signs makes these undefined terms equal with opposite sign. So, π∗Σ​f\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f} is well-defined. See [Igu02, Proposition 4.8] for more details of this argument. It is shown there that π∗Σ​f\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f} is a related to the Becker-Gottlieb transfer trMW:H∗​(W)→H∗​(M)\text{tr}_{M}^{W}:H^{\ast}(W)\to H^{\ast}(M) by trMW=π∗Σ​f∘i∗\text{tr}_{M}^{W}=\pi_{\ast}^{\Sigma f}\circ i^{\ast} where i∗:H∗​(W)→H∗​(Σf)i^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(W)\to H^{\ast}(\Sigma_{f}) is the restriction map induced by the inclusion i:Σf→Wi:\Sigma_{f}\to W.
Note that the correction term in (1) is nonzero only in degrees 4​k4k. In degrees 2​j=4​k+22j=4k+2, when jj is odd, there is no correction term (i.e. the correction term is zero.)
Also note that the term ∑jτj​(f)\sum_{j}\tau_{j}(f) changes by a sign if the indices of the fiberwise Morse chain complexes are all shifted by one in either direction. More precisely, the index shift operator on Wh∙h​(𝐂,G)\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G) induces map |Wh∙h​(𝐂,G)|→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,G)||\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)|\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},G)| which sends τj\tau_{j} to −τj-\tau_{j} [Igu05, Theorem 3.10]. However, in this paper we are always suspending an even number of times. So there is no change of sign.
To summarize:
We can use the family of Morse chain complexes C⁡(fm)C(f_{m}), m∈Mm\in M of a function with moderate singularities f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} to compute the higher torsion of WW over MM if we add correction terms in degree 4​k4k given by a suitable multiple of the push-down to H∗​(M)H^{\ast}(M) of the Pontryagin character of the stable bundle γf\gamma_{f} over Σf\Sigma_{f}.
B.6. Fibers with boundary
Higher torsion invariants may also be defined for fiber bundles F→W→MF\to W\to M where the fiber FF is a compact manifold with boundary. In the absolute case we just use functions f:(W,∂W)→([0,1],1)f:(W,\partial W)\to([0,1],1) with moderate singularities such that for each fiber FF we have that ∂F\partial F is a regular level set. More generally, it suffices to impose the condition that d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 for outwards pointing tangent vectors vv along ∂F\partial F. However it is also convenient to consider relative versions in which the fiberwise homology of the function computes a relative homology group. As this relative case is directly relevant to the present article, we discuss some examples below.
For example, the construction may be applied in the case in which the fiber FF is a cobordism on a manifold XX, so that ∂F=∂0F​∐∂1F\partial F=\partial_{0}F\coprod\partial_{1}F and we have an identification ∂0F≃X\partial_{0}F\simeq X. More precisely, this means that ∂W=∂0W​∐∂1W\partial W=\partial_{0}W\coprod\partial_{1}W and we have a trivialization of the fiber bundle ∂0W→M\partial_{0}W\to M, i.e. a fibered diffeomorphism ∂0W≃M×X\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X. In this case higher torsion is defined using functions (W,∂0W,∂1W)→([0,1],0,1)(W,\partial_{0}W,\partial_{1}W)\to([0,1],0,1) with moderate singularities and yields an invariant of such fiber bundles WW equipped with a trivialization ∂0W≃M×X\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X, or more generally such that d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 for outwards pointing tangent vectors vv along ∂1F\partial_{1}F and d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 for inwards pointing tangent vectors vv along ∂0F\partial_{0}F.
The fiber FF could be also be a compact manifold with corners, thought of as a cobordism on a compact manifold with boundary (or even corners) XX. In this case the boundary ∂F\partial F is divided into a horizontal boundary ∂hF\partial_{h}F and a vertical boundary ∂vF\partial_{v}F, with ∂vF≃∂0F​∐∂1F\partial_{v}F\simeq\partial_{0}F\coprod\partial_{1}F and an identification ∂0F≃X\partial_{0}F\simeq X as before. The boundary ∂W\partial W of such a fiber bundle F→W→MF\to W\to M is similarly decomposed, and such fiber bundles should be equipped with a fibered diffeomorphism ∂0W≃M×X\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X. The torsion is again defined using functions (W,∂0W,∂1W)→([0,1],0,1)(W,\partial_{0}W,\partial_{1}W)\to([0,1],0,1) with moderate singularities, or more generally such that d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 for outwards pointing tangent vectors vv along ∂1F\partial_{1}F and d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 for inwards pointing tangent vectors vv along ∂0F\partial_{0}F.
Finally, yet another variant is the case in which FF is a compact manifold with boundary and ∂0F⊂∂F\partial_{0}F\subset\partial F is a codimension zero submanifold, identified with some fixed compact manifold with boundary XX. Such bundles F→W→MF\to W\to M come equipped with a trivialization of the sub-bundle ∂0W⊂∂W\partial_{0}W\subset\partial W, i.e. a fibered diffeomorphism ∂0W≃M×X\partial_{0}W\simeq M\times X. The torsion is computed using functions with moderate singularities f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} which have ∂0W\partial_{0}W as a regular level set of f|∂Wf|_{\partial W}, such that inwards pointing vectors vv along the interior of ∂0F\partial_{0}F satisfy d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 and such that outwards pointing vectors vv along ∂F∖∂0F\partial F\setminus\partial_{0}F satisfy d​f​(v)>0df(v)>0 (so the fiberwise Morse homology computes H∗​(F,∂0F)H_{*}(F;\partial_{0}F) as before).
In any of these cases, we speak of the torsion of the bundle pair (W,∂0W)(W,\partial_{0}W) and there is a simple formula relating the higher torsion of WW, of ∂0W\partial_{0}W and of the pair (W,∂0W)(W,\partial_{0}W). Indeed, from Corollary 3.18 on page 39 in [Igu05] we have the following result:
Theorem B.4 ([Igu05]).
For a bundle pair (W,∂0W)(W,\partial_{0}W) we have
τk​(W)=τk​(W,∂0W)+τk​(∂0W)\tau_{k}(W)=\tau_{k}(W,\partial_{0}W)+\tau_{k}(\partial_{0}W)
B.7. Noncompact fibers
The same construction works if the fiber FF is noncompact and the bundle W→MW\to M is equipped with a reference fibration at infinity f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R}. In this case τk​(W,ρ)\tau_{k}(W,\rho) depends on the choice of fibration at infinity, though this may be omitted from the notation if there is no danger of confusion. For example, one could define and compute higher torsion using functions with moderate singularities which are homotopic through fibrations at infinity to our reference fibration at infinity. The fiberwise Morse homology is computing H∗​(F,∂0F)H_{*}(F,\partial_{0}F) where ∂0F\partial_{0}F is a sublevel set f|F<−cf|_{F}<-c for c>0c>0 very large, so this version should also be thought of as a relative form of higher torsion.
In certain applications the bundle WW is assumed to be trivialized outside of a compact set and it makes sense to demand that the admissible functions f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} agree with some fixed fibration at infinity outside of this compact set. With such a restriction imposed, the class τk​(W,ρ)\tau_{k}(W,\rho) does not depend on the choice of admissible function since deformations of admissible functions have compact support.
A very precise formulation of the restriction to compact sets is given in [Igu02, Lemma 3.20] where singularities of a family of functions are confined to a family of “convex sets” which are basically cobordisms embedded in the fiber. The family of convex sets gives a family of chain complexes which embeds in the chain complex of the entire fiber in the case when the fiber is compact. In general, we can define the family of total chain complexes to be that given by any family of convex sets which contains all the critical points of the function. By [Igu02, Lemma 3.20], these all give the same chain complex. So, the family of chain complexes and thus the higher torsion is well-defined. The fiber also has a well-defined Euler characteristic χ⁡(F)\chi(F) given by this finite chain complex. In the case of functions of tube type we have χ⁡(F)=±1\chi(F)=\pm 1.
In any event, higher Reidemeister torsion is a stable invariant, which means that if WW is replaced by the fiberwise stabilization W×𝐑4W\times\mathbf{R}^{4} and the function with mild singularities f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} is replaced with f⁡(w)+‖x‖2−‖y‖2f(w)+||x||^{2}-||y||^{2} for (w,x,y)∈W×𝐑2×𝐑2(w,x,y)\in W\times\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{R}^{2}, then the homotopy class of the map M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| and sign of the fiber Euler characteristic χ⁡(F)\chi(F) will remain unchanged.
Furthermore, by the framing principle, if f:W→𝐑f:W\to\mathbf{R} is replaced by f⊕q:W×𝐑N→𝐑f\oplus q:W\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}, where f⊕q⁡(w,v)=f⁡(w)+qπ⁡(w)​(v)f\oplus q(w,v)=f(w)+q_{\pi(w)}(v) for qm:𝐑N→𝐑q_{m}:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R} a family of non-degenerate quadratic forms on 𝐑N\mathbf{R}^{N} of even index parametrized by m∈Mm\in M, then the homotopy class of the map M→|Wh∙h​(𝐂,1)|M\to|\text{Wh}_{\bullet}^{h}(\mathbf{C},1)| changes but in a controlled way: the pullback of the universal class τk\tau_{k} changes precisely by adding χ⁡(F)\chi(F) times a (certain multiple) of the Pontraygin character of the stable bundle of the family qmq_{m}, m∈Mm\in M, i.e. the stable vector bundle formed by the negative eigenspaces of qmq_{m}. The χ⁡(F)\chi(F) factor comes from the fact that the composition of p∗:H∗​(M)→H∗​(W)p^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(M)\to H^{\ast}(W) with the Becker-Gottlieb transfer t​rMW:H∗​(W)→H∗​(M)tr_{M}^{W}:H^{\ast}(W)\to H^{\ast}(M) is multiplication by χ⁡(F)\chi(F).
