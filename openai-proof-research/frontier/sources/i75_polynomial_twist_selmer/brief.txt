## 2. Three distinct precise targets, and early rejections

### A. Thin polynomial twists with local prescription — pursued, open

For every fixed elliptic curve `E/Q` of conductor `N`, fix a positive integer `M` divisible by `8N`. Put

\[
 P(n)=M^2n^2+1,\quad \mathcal S(X)=\{1\le n\le X:P(n)\text{ squarefree}\},
 \quad b_E=(1-w(E))/2\in\{0,1\}.
\]

**Target C73-M** is

\[
 \sum_{n\in\mathcal S(X)}[a(E^{(P(n))})-b_E]=o_{E,M}(X). \tag{M73}
\]

All constants and limits are for fixed `E,M`; no uniformity in curve height is claimed even conjecturally in (M73). The weaker **C73-D** is

\[
 \#\{n\in\mathcal S(X):a(E^{(P(n))})>b_E\}=o_{E,M}(X). \tag{D73}
\]

These are proposed targets, not theorems or claims of established worldwide novelty. Their positive squarefree parameters have density zero in the source twist variable. They are not another fixed congruence condition on all squarefree integers. Sections 3–6 give two constructions and the exact missing inputs.

For comparison, the following **fixed simultaneous law is technical**. Fix finitely many `E_i/Q`, and `Q=8∏N_i`. Sample positive squarefree `d≤Y` with `d≡1 mod 8` and `(d,Q)=1`. Let `U={r mod Q:(r,Q)=1,r≡1 mod 8}`. The limiting joint distribution of `(a(E_i^d))` is the distribution of

\[
 \left(\frac{1-w(E_i)\chi_r(-N_i)}2\right)_i
 \quad\text{for }r\text{ uniform in }U. \tag{J73}
\]

Here `χ_r(-N_i)` means the fundamental-discriminant symbol determined by a positive `d≡r mod Q`. Proof: the union of the finitely many exceptional sets in G is `o(Y)`; the sampling set has positive density; outside the union, functional-equation parity identifies the rank. Squarefree integers are equidistributed in these finitely many reduced classes, by the squarefree sieve. Thus (J73) follows by a finite union and a finite residue count. It does **not** assert independent fair coins: `E_1=E_2` already gives identical ranks. Even distinct indices can have dependent root characters. S's auxiliary-field lemma already uses this type of simultaneous selection. This is a concrete reduction, not a novelty verdict based on a missing printed formula.

### B. A relative Selmer converse over an imaginary quadratic field — open

The precise screen was: for every imaginary quadratic `K`, every **non-CM** elliptic curve `E/K`, every prime `p`, and `r∈{0,1}`, does

\[
 \operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf Z_p}\mathrm{Sel}_{p^\infty}(E/K)=r
 \implies \operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E/K,s)=\operatorname{rank}E(K)=r,

\]

The Selmer group uses all local Kummer conditions, including primes above `p`; ordinary/good reduction is **not** silently assumed. IQ supplies the analytic `L`-function and the exact shift, but does not supply the needed converse class or height reciprocity over `K`. The `Q` case, including full BSD and agreement of **all** prime-power coranks once Sha is finite, is already B plus the Kummer sequence. ### C. A family-uniform elliptic-square exceptional-height bound — open

Fix a number field `K` and a projective embedding of a compactification of `A₂`. With its fixed absolute logarithmic projective/Chow heights, the screen asks: for every integer `D≥1`, do there exist `C_{K,D}>0` and finite exponents `α_D,β_D` such that every geometrically irreducible closed Hodge-generic `C⊂A₂` defined over `K` with degree at most `D`, and every `s∈C(Qbar)` with `A_s` geometrically isogenous to `E_s²`, `E_s` non-CM, satisfy

\[
 h(s)\le C_{K,D}(1+h_{\rm Chow}(\bar C))^{\alpha_D}
                         (1+[K(s):K])^{\beta_D}? \tag{H73}
\]

Changing the embedding or height convention changes the fixed constants. Only the non-CM square locus is requested, not CM points or the quaternionic branch. A bound with a computable constant would be stronger still; none is claimed here.

Z2's fixed-curve selected-lift bound and its fixed-degree descent supply a bound for each fixed `C`, but its choice of center, covers, local analytic data, and restoration of exceptional points are not bounded in terms of these family invariants. Taking a maximum over finitely many bounded-height `K`-curves would give an unspecified function at each cutoff, not the displayed polynomial or an effective constant. Nor does qualitative finiteness make the exceptional locus constructible in a parameter space. The exact unpaid step is control of those constants and omitted-point heights by the right side of (H73). There is no counterexample to (H73) here.

The weaker finite-rank translated statement is already Z's complete `X×{a}` construction, with its stronger no-proper-translate hypothesis. The complex-field qualitative extension is the Barroero–Dill reduction described above. Both were abandoned as already-paid operations. Neither is a family-uniform height theorem, and the Hodge/geometry-partner route is not invoked.

## 3. Normalizations and proved preparation for the thin target

For `n∈S(X)`, `d=P(n)>0` is odd, squarefree, `d≡1 mod 8N`, and coprime to `2N`. It is its own positive fundamental discriminant. G's coprime-twist formula gives `w(E^d)=w(E)`: every local factor of `χ_d(N)` is one, and `χ_d(-1)=1`. Thus `a(E^d)≡b_E mod 2` and `a(E^d)≥b_E`. The deficit in (M73) is nonnegative and, when nonzero, at least two. We are not asserting half of each rank in this sign-fixed family.

PF applies to the fixed separable quadratic `P`, since its value at zero is one. For an odd prime `p∤M`, let `z_p=1+(-1/p)`, either zero or two. Its simple roots lift uniquely modulo `p²`. If `p|M` there are no roots, and there are no roots at 2 since `M` is even. Consequently

\[
 |\mathcal S(X)|=c_M X+o_M(X),\qquad
 c_M=\prod_{p\nmid 2M}(1-z_p/p^2)>0. \tag{SF}
\]

Positivity follows also directly from convergence of `∑2/p²` and the absence of a zero factor. No coefficient-uniform error was used.

**Generic rank-zero check.** The isotrivial elliptic curve `E^(M²t²+1)/Q(t)` has Mordell–Weil rank zero; this is an elementary geometric precaution, not a statistical result. The double cover `v²=M²t²+1` is rational, with

\[
 t=(u-u^{-1})/(2M),\qquad v=(u+u^{-1})/2.
\]

Every `Q(u)`-point of the constant elliptic curve extends to a morphism `P¹→E` by properness. A nonconstant such morphism is impossible by Riemann–Hurwitz in characteristic zero. Hence `E(Q(u))=E(Q)`. The twisting involution is `u↦-1/u`; an anti-invariant constant point satisfies `P=-P`, and is 2-torsion. This proves the rank assertion, even after extending constants to `Qbar`. It does not bound specialization ranks. The surface is isotrivial; calling it non-isotrivial would be wrong. CM and non-CM constant curves both satisfy this check.

**Why the full twist theorem cannot simply be pulled back.** The map `n↦P(n)` is injective on positive integers and maps `[1,X]` into `[1,M²X²+1]`. G's exceptional bound `o(Y)` becomes only `o(X²)`, not `o(X)`. M's large-rank count becomes `O(X²/k²)`. A density-zero subset of twist parameters can contain every polynomial value. For an exact logical countermodel, add two to a minimal-parity rank sequence only at values `P(n)`; the changed set has size `O(√Y)`. Its global rank-zero/rank-one densities and mean are unchanged asymptotically, and its sufficiently high rank tails vanish identically, while **every** selected polynomial value has rank excess two. This is not an elliptic-curve counterexample and does not refute (D73) or (M73). It refutes the proposed inference from these marginal contracts alone.

## 4. Construction I: polynomial-character model and analytic mollification

This construction retains the source analytic mechanism but changes the sampling measure honestly. It does not assume that the source comparison theorem survives a change of index.

### 4.1 Exact local law and finite-prime conditioning

For an odd prime `p∤M`, let `Z_p=(P(U)/p)` with `U` uniform modulo `p`. Then

\[
 \Pr(Z_p=0)=z_p/p,\quad
 \Pr(Z_p=1)=\frac{p-z_p-1}{2p},\quad
 \Pr(Z_p=-1)=\frac{p-z_p+1}{2p},\quad \mathbb E Z_p=-1/p. \tag{L1}
\]

Proof: `# {(u,v):v²=M²u²+1}=p-1`, since `(v-Mu)(v+Mu)=1` is bijective with a nonzero field element. The same count is `p+∑_u(P(u)/p)`, proving the sum `-1`. The zero count is `z_p`; solve for the other two counts. If `p|M`, `Z_p=1` deterministically. CRT makes the variables at **distinct fixed primes** independent in a complete residue average. These are elementary exact operations; no independence of elliptic ranks follows.

If we impose `p²∤P(U)` and sample modulo `p²`, there are `p²-z_p` allowed residues, and the **conditional** law is

\[
 \Pr(Z_p=0\mid p^2\nmid P)=\frac{z_p(p-1)}{p^2-z_p},\quad
 \Pr(Z_p=\pm1\mid p^2\nmid P)
 =\frac{p(p-z_p\mp1)}{2(p^2-z_p)}. \tag{L2}
\]

Indeed nonzero classes modulo `p` have `p` lifts and all survive; each zero class has `p-1` surviving lifts. Thus the first moment is `-p/(p²-z_p)`, and the second is `p(p-z_p)/(p²-z_p)`. At `p|M` the law remains deterministic. At 2 it is also deterministic in our family.

For any **fixed** finite collection of primes, (L2) with independence is the actual limiting law conditional on `n∈S(X)`. To prove this without a growing-modulus assumption, let `W_z(n)` exclude squares only at primes at most `z`, with `z` beyond the specified primes. It is periodic, and its mean is the finite Euler product `c_z`. Since `W_z≥1_S`, (SF) gives

\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}X^{-1}\sum_{n\le X}(W_z(n)-1_S(n))=c_z-c_M\to0.
\]

A bounded fixed local test changes its mean by at most this quantity. CRT evaluates its `W_z` mean; first send `X→∞`, then `z→∞`. Divide by `c_M>0`. This is a classical periodic approximation, not a new bridge. In particular the source's symmetric integer law is wrong here already at `p=3`: the mean is `-1/3`, not zero.

### 4.2 A proved restricted Gram comparison

Fix `M`. For arbitrary complex coefficients supported on odd `m≤L`, define

\[
 A_n=\sum_{m\le L\atop m\text{ odd}}a_m(P(n)/m),\qquad
 g_M(q)=\prod_{p^e\parallel q}g_{M,p}(e),
\]

where the symbol is Jacobi, `g_{M,p}(e)=1` if `p|M`, otherwise it is `-1/p` for odd `e` and `1-z_p/p` for positive even `e`; `g_M(1)=1`. Then for every positive integer `X`,

\[
 \left|X^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^X|A_n|^2-
       \sum_{m,l}a_m\overline{a_l}\,g_M(ml)\right|
 \le \frac2X\left(\sum_{m\le L}m|a_m|\right)^2. \tag{G73}
\]

Proof: for odd `q`, the function `(P(n)/q)` has period `rad(q)` and is bounded by one in modulus. Its mean over one period is `g_M(q)` by (L1) and CRT. Split `[1,X]` into full periods plus one incomplete period; the discrepancy from the full mean is at most `2 rad(q)/X≤2q/X`. Expand `|A_n|²` exactly, apply this to `q=ml`, and take absolute values. This includes square indices, zero character values, the constant coefficient, and complex coefficients; no assertion of independent prime **factorizations** occurs.

For coefficients `|a_m|≤τ(m)/√m`, this proves an error `O(L³(1+log L)²/X)`: `∑_{m≤L}τ(m)≤L(1+log L)` by counting divisors, and multiply by `√L`. Hence it gives a vanishing error for `L≤X^(1/3-ε)` for fixed `ε>0`. This elementary range is not claimed optimal. With a fixed squarefree presieve of period `B=∏_{p≤z}p²`, the same proof costs at most `2B/X (∑m|a_m|)²`, with the **unnormalized** CRT mean `c_z` times the corresponding partly conditioned product model. Conditioning would additionally divide by `c_z`. This explicitly pays the presieve period.

### 4.3 Why the source-length argument is still missing

On a dyadic parameter block `X<n≤2X`, admissible twist size is comparable to `Y=M²X²`, and the square root of its conductor is comparable to `Y`, with fixed-`E,M` constants. Source M uses main polynomial length up to `Y^(1-Δ)`, and `Δ≤1/2`. Expressed in `X`, this is `L≈X^(2(1-Δ))`, at least `X`, while (G73) only pays `L<X^(1/3)`. The displayed error would scale as `X^(5-6Δ)` up to logs and fixed constants, still at least `X²` when `Δ≤1/2`. The comparison estimate cannot be inserted into the source proof. This refutes **that substitution**, not the target.

A concrete missing replacement can be phrased at the Gram level. Fix a smooth nonnegative `Ψ` supported in `(1,2)`, put `Y=M²X²`, and let `c_q(x)` be the coefficients obtained from any proposed main-polynomial/mollifier construction after the smooth substitution `d/Y=x²+Y^(-1)`. Their length, multiplier coefficient sums and scaled derivatives must meet the budgets specified in M above. For those specific coefficients, define the exact discrepancy

\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal E_X={}&\frac1X\sum_n\Psi(n/X)\mu^2(P(n))
       \left|\sum_{q\text{ odd}}c_q(n/X)(P(n)/q)\right|^2\\
 &-c_M\int\Psi(x)\sum_{q,r\text{ odd}}
       c_q(x)\overline{c_r(x)}g_M^{\rm sf}(qr)\,dx.
\end{split} \tag{PC73}
\]

Here `g_M^sf` uses the moments in (L2); powers of two have their fixed value one. A constant or subtracted main term is included in `c_1`. The desired source-strength input is `|E_X|≤C exp(-cΔ log Y)`, uniformly for the source range of `Δ`, derivative orders and piece indices, with constants depending only on fixed `E,M,Ψ` and the allowed fixed seminorm bounds. No such estimate is established here. This is a required estimate for the particular constructed coefficients, not a claim that every arbitrary coefficient family obeys it. On the compact support away from zero, the displayed change of smooth variable has bounded derivatives of every fixed order; it does not pay the arithmetic discrepancy.

Even (PC73) alone would **not** close the target. One must redo the model Euler-product positivity/mollification with its biased local law, pay the removal of large square divisors in the weighted sums, and obtain the terminal-series estimate and a large-rank count at the required range. The elementary unweighted error `c_z-c_M` cannot be multiplied by a growing `|A_n|²` bound for free. More decisively, M's tail estimate did not prove minimal-rank density even for ordinary twists; it invoked G for that final step. A polynomial-family low-rank input is still needed unless a different analytic nonvanishing argument proves it. No optimization of `3/5`, no terminal-tail summation, and no change in the symmetric model is presented as a solution.

## 5. Construction II: higher Selmer levels and the analytic interface

This construction replaces analytic nonvanishing by arithmetic low corank. For `d=P(n)`, write

\[
 s(n)=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf Z_2}\mathrm{Sel}_{2^\infty}(E^d/\mathbf Q).
\]

Parity from G gives `s(n)≡b_E mod 2`; in particular `s(n)≥b_E`. At a good index `s(n)=b_E`, G and B imply the exact analytic rank, Mordell–Weil rank, whole-Sha finiteness and normalized BSD formula. This costs no new prime-ordinary or rational-torsion assumption. The problem is producing enough good indices, not deriving another pointwise converse.

Following the actual finite-level convention in Smith's Notation 1.8, let `r_k(n)` be the maximum `r` for which `(Z/2^k)^r` embeds in the full `2^∞`-Selmer group. It decreases to `s(n)`. Abstractly a cofinitely generated group with divisible part `(Q₂/Z₂)^s` and finite part `⊕Z/2^{e_i}` has

\[
 r_k=s+\#\{i:e_i\ge k\}. \tag{T73}
\]

This describes the invariant, not an assertion that a finite 2-descent computes it without accounting for the Kummer/torsion maps. Full `Sel₂` dimension alone is not full corank. Finite symplectically paired summands can survive arbitrarily many prescribed levels while corank is zero. The diagnostic uses only this abstract algebra, not conjectural Sha samples.

A sufficient, precise **missing** polynomial Selmer estimate is

\[
 \lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac1X
 \#\{n\in\mathcal S(X):r_k(n)>b_E\}=0. \tag{SC73}
\]

For each `k`, `{s>b_E}⊂{r_k>b_E}`; hence (SC73) implies `#{s>b_E}=o(X)`, and the pointwise converse proves (D73). This is a one-direction implication. Pointwise convergence `r_k(n)↓s(n)` does not justify interchanging the two limits. (SC73) is not claimed necessary for (D73).

Why not use the polynomial-twist moment theorem? Koymans–Smith, Theorem 1.4 and Example 7.9, genuinely applies with `F=Q`, `A=E` and `P=M²u²+1`. By evenness of `P` its signed integer-box average is, up to its vanishing single `u=0` contribution, the positive-input average. Thus for every fixed `κ>0` it bounds

\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac1X\sum_{1\le n\le X}e^{\kappa\operatorname{rank}E^{P(n)}(\mathbf Q)}<\infty. \tag{KS73}
\]

Restricting a nonnegative sum to `S(X)` and using (SF) also gives a bound in its normalized probability measure, with a `1/c_M` cost. This is a direct application of an actual primary theorem, not an I73 contribution. The finite isogeny minimization in Example 7.9 controls a rank **upper bound**. It does not show that the normalized 2-Selmer size is minimal almost surely, nor determine higher-level survival of the finite part. Its polynomial sieve explicitly leaves the largest prime entries uncontrolled. Those entries cannot be filled in by Smith's all-integer distribution. Our local law (L2), or small-prime residue equidistribution, does not determine those global Selmer constraints.

For (M73), even (SC73) leaves analytic rank on the exceptional indices unpaid. The exact decomposition is

\[
 \sum_{n\in S(X)}(a(E^{P(n)})-b_E)
 =\sum_{n\in S(X):s(n)>b_E}(a(E^{P(n)})-b_E). \tag{W73}
\]

M's Jensen bound applies to these admissible twists and gives `a(E^P(n))≪_{E,M}log(3X)`. Consequently a **sufficient stronger** version of (SC73) is

\[
 \#\{n\in S(X):s(n)>b_E\}=o_{E,M}(X/\log X). \tag{SC73+}
\]

Alternatively combine (D73) with the **missing analytic uniform-integrability condition**

\[
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac1X
 \sum_{n\in S(X):a(E^{P(n)})>R}a(E^{P(n)})=0. \tag{AT73}
\]

Proof of this alternative is ordinary truncation: the part `b_E<a≤R` costs at most `R·o(X)` for fixed `R`; the remaining part is (AT73); take `X` first, then `R`. The algebraic bound (KS73) controls **algebraic** rank on a sparse exceptional set by Cauchy–Schwarz. It cannot be substituted for (AT73), since analytic and algebraic rank equality there is exactly unproved. Likewise a bound on `rank E(Q)` alone does not imply low Selmer corank because of the divisible Sha contribution.

If Construction I could supply `#{n∈S(X):a(E^P(n))>k}≪X/k²` for `k≤(log X)^(3/5)`, Jensen and M's same layer-cake operation would yield a tail mass `≪X/R+X(log X)^(-1/5)`, proving (AT73). This explicitly separates the two missing tasks: polynomial low-corank/minimal-rank density and polynomial analytic tail control. It does not label either conditional reduction a solved theorem.

## 6. Contribution assessment and stopping point

The thin target is not known here to be false. Its generic function-field rank is zero, its local prescription is compatible, and its squarefree denominator has a paid positive density. These checks remove elementary obstructions; they do not establish statistical minimality. A fresh source theorem controlling the polynomial higher-Selmer distribution, or an analytic polynomial-family nonvanishing/weighted-tail mechanism, would be needed. The current constructions stop at (SC73)/(SC73+) and (PC73) together with the remaining analytic model/tail costs. No exact printed match was used as positive novelty evidence.

Every completed deduction has a concrete technical reduction:

* fixed simultaneous twist law: G's exceptional-set union, root formula, fixed squarefree residue counting;
* density-one full BSD: G's low-corank density plus B's exact pointwise formula;
* fixed finite-rank translates: Z's existing product-and-finite-preimage construction;
* qualitative complex abelian ZP: Z plus the checked Barroero–Dill trace reduction;
* (SF), (L1), (L2), (G73): PF/fixed periodic approximation, the conic residue count, CRT and a periodic remainder;
* generic rank zero: the rational double cover and absence of a nonconstant morphism `P¹→E`;
* (KS73) and the conditional tail/transfer implications: the inspected polynomial rank-moment theorem and elementary truncation.

The negative tests refute particular shortcuts only: the symmetric integer character model on a quadratic progression, marginal-to-thin pullback, and identification of finite-level Selmer size with corank. They are not counterexamples to the analytic target, (S73), or (H73). A toy finite-set family is not offered as a geometric counterexample to uniform ZP. No MathDB score or priority claim is made.
