                                                 A BSTRACT. We continue the investigation of the distribution of ℓ∞ -Selmer groups in
                                                 degree ℓ twist families of Galois modules over number fields begun in [Smi1]. Building
                                                 off the work on higher Selmer groups in that part, we find conditions under which we
                                                 can compute the distribution of the ℓ∞ -Selmer groups for a given degree ℓ twist family.
                                                 Along the way, we show that the average rank in the quadratic twist family of any given
                                                 abelian variety over a number field is bounded.

   The notion of a favored twist generalizes substantially, and the natural extension of
the divide-and-conquer approach seen above allows us to prove Theorem 1.1.
   Once we restrict to favored twists, the distribution of fixed point Selmer ranks is still
affected by the structure of the Galois module N. Our main result, Theorem 2.14, puts a
number of technical conditions on N and its submodules. A module that does not satisfy
these conditions will have a distinct distribution of ω-Selmer groups in its favored twist
family compared to any module that satisfies these conditions. We hope that future work
will give us more information about the wide variety of distributions that can appear if
these technical conditions do not hold.

1.1. Our method. We introduced the          Q notion of a grid in [Smi1]. To briefly recap, a
grid is defined from a product space s∈S Xs together with some auxiliary data, where
the Xs are finite sets of primes of Q obeying some mild conditions. From the auxiliary
data, we may associate any point (ps )s∈S in this product space with a twist χ. As we
proved in [Smi1, Proposition 4.8], the group Selω N χ can be calculated from the classes
[ps ] for all s ∈ S, the spins [ps , ps ] for all s ∈ S, and the symbols [ps , pt ] for all distinct
s, t ∈ S. We reprove this result in an explicit form in Section 5.2.
   Using this, we may express a given moment of the size of Selω N χ for χ varying
in a grid of twists as a sum of terms defined from classes, spins, and symbols. When
a given term in this sum involves a symbol [ps , pt ], we can often prove that the term
has negligble contribution to the overall sum either by using the Chebotarev density
theorem or by using the bilinear equidistribution result for symbols proved as [Smi1,
Theorem 5.2]. Once we remove the terms proved to be negligible using this method, the
remaining terms can be controlled using linear algebra.
   Our need to invoke the Chebotarev density theorem and the bilinear equidistribution
result pressures us to consider grids where the collections of primes Xs are large relative
to their height. But our need to carve natural families of twists into such grids pressures
us in the opposite direction. Fortunately, there is a middle ground that works. We give
one possible specification for such grids in Section 8.

1.2. An overview of this paper. Section 2 gives the necessary notation to state Theo-
rem 2.14, the main result for the distribution of ℓ∞ -Selmer groups in twist families of
this two-part paper. This result is proved by combining the results on ω-Selmer groups
of this paper with the results for higher Selmer groups in [Smi1].
   Theorem 2.14 is largely interesting for its applications to class groups and Selmer
groups of abelian varieties. We go through some of these applications in Section 3. This
is where the theorems mentioned in the introduction to [Smi1] are all proved.
   Section 4 explores why the average ω-Selmer rank in families of unfavored twists
tends to infinity. This involves certain terms known as Tamagawa ratios, which also
appear in the definition of cofavored submodules. The moments of sizes of Selmer
groups in grids can be expressed as sums over the cofavored submodules of the powers
of N, so we need to have a classification of these submodules. Using the technical
conditions appearing in Theorem 2.14, we are able to find such a classification.

   Sections 5, 6, and 7 all concern the moments of the sizes of the ω-Selmer group in
grids of twists. Section 5 gives the initial reduction of such a moment to a sum of
terms involving spins and symbols. In Section 6, the terms that cannot be proved to be
negligible using analytic number theory are classified and roughly estimated. Section 7
then gives a more precise estimate for these moments under the conditions of Theorem
2.14.
   The highly analytic Section 8 carves natural families of twists into grids where the
estimates of the previous three sections apply. In Section 9, we use this work to give
rough upper bounds for the moments of sizes of ω-Selmer groups in natural twist fami-
lies. These estimates are refined in Section 10, where the work in [Smi1] is incorporated
to prove the main result of this project, Theorem 2.14.

3.2. Quadratic twists of abelian varieties. Generalizing from elliptic curves, we may

symmetric line bundle L on A defined over F , but might not arise from a line bundle
defined over F . However, the obstruction to the existence of this bundle is given by an
element cλ of the 2-Selmer group of A [26, 25], and this Selmer element is universal
across the quadratic twist family of A [17]. The technical conditions force us to assume

quadratic refinement q : A[2] → ±1 of the Weil pairing on A[2] induced by λ [20,
p. 314f.]. Taking K = F (A[2]), we must have that Gal(K/F ) is a subgroup of the
orthogonal group O(q) of automorphisms of A[2] preserving this quadratic form.
