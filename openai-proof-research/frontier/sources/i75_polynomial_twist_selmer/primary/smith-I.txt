                                                 A BSTRACT. In this paper and its sequel, we develop a technique for finding the distri-
                                                 bution of ℓ∞ -Selmer groups in degree ℓ twist families of Galois modules over number
                                                 fields. Given an elliptic curve E over a number field satisfying certain technical condi-
                                                 tions, this technique can be used to show that 100% of the quadratic twists of E have
                                                 rank at most 1. Given a prime ℓ and a number field F not containing µ2ℓ , this method
                                                 also shows that the ℓ∞ -class groups in the family of degree ℓ cyclic extensions of F
                                                 have a distribution consistent with the Cohen–Lenstra–Gerth heuristics.
                                                     For this work, we develop the theory of the fixed point Selmer group, which serves
                                                 as the base layer of the ℓ∞ -Selmer group. This first paper gives a technique for finding
                                                 the distribution of ℓ∞ -Selmer groups in certain families of twists where the fixed point
                                                 Selmer group is stable. In the sequel paper, we will give a technique for controlling
                                                 fixed point Selmer groups.

1.3. An overview of the method.
1.3.1. 2-Selmer groups in quadratic twist families. Given an abelian variety A over a
number field F , and given d in F × , we find that the 2-torsion subgroups of A and Ad
are isomorphic over F . More generally, given a module N acted on continuously by the
absolute Galois group GF of F , and given a continuous homomorphism χ : GF → ±1,
we can define the quadratic twist N χ of N, and we find that there is an equivariant
isomorphism

of the 2-torsion submodules of these Galois modules.
   The isomorphism (1.1) may be exploited to study the distribution of 2-Selmer groups
in quadratic twist families of a given Galois module. This was first done by Heath-
Brown, who found the distribution of 2-Selmer ranks in the quadratic twist family of
the congruent number curve A : y 2 = x3 − x over Q [15]. This method was adapted
to the study of 4-class ranks of families of quadratic fields by Fouvry and Klüners [8].
Heath–Brown’s result was later generalized to quadratic twist families of elliptic curves
obeying Assumption 1.1 (3) by Kane [18].
   Other previous results on the distribution of 2-Selmer groups apply to less natural
families of twists. This includes the work of Gerth [11], Swinnerton–Dyer [35], and
Klagsbrun, Mazur, and Rubin [19]. The families appearing in these three papers are

1.3.3. The three parts of forcing equidistribution. This observation about 2k -Selmer
groups in grids still leaves us far away from proving an equidistribution result like The-
orem 1.5. Without further work, if the 2k -Selmer ranks of the Ad have a lopsided dis-
tribution over Y , the same can be true for the whole grid. To prove an equidistribution
result, we need to arrange it so that, however the 2k -Selmer groups behave over Y , the
result will still be an equidistributed set of 2k -Selmer groups. The process of forcing
equidistribution breaks into an algebraic part, an analytic part, and a combinatorial part.
   The algebraic part is given as Theorem 8.12. In this result, we show that the behavior
of higher Selmer groups in a grid partially depends on a symbol [pa , pb ] defined for
certain primes pa , pb of Q. The symbol encodes information about how pa behaves at pb
relative to a fixed number field; we will define this object formally in Section 3.
   The analytic part is the bilinear equidistribution result for symbols appearing in Sec-
tion 5. This is a result we end up using three times: in addition to its application towards
forcing equidistribution of higher Selmer ranks, it also appears in the regridding of Sec-
tion 6 and in our calculations for the distribution of fixed point Selmer ranks in [Smi2].
However, it is only the application towards forcing equidistribution that requires the full
strength of this result, in the form of Corollary 8.18.
   Combining these two ideas gives us substantial control on how the Selmer groups
over X behave relative to the Selmer groups over Y . We need to engineer this control
so that, no matter how the Selmer groups over Y behave, the result is an approximately
equidistributed collection of Selmer groups over the whole grid X. This is the role that
the combinatorial result Proposition 7.9 fills.

1.4. The organization of this paper. After going through some necessary notation in
Section 2, we introduce the notions of classes, spins, and symbols of primes in Section
3. These objects suffice to calculate the fixed point Selmer group of a twist coming from
a grid, as we will show with Propositions 4.6 and 4.8 after defining twistable modules,
Selmer groups, and grids. Given a twist χ in the grid, we call the collection of twists in
the grid whose classes, spins, and symbols all agree with those of χ the grid class of χ.
   We will control higher Selmer ranks by controlling certain pairings derived from the
Cassels–Tate pairing on a specified grid class. We develop this theory in Section 4.1
and give a heuristic for how these pairings are distributed in Section 4.2. This section
ends with Theorem 4.18, the main result of this paper, which gives a sufficient set of
conditions on the grid class for our heuristic to hold.
   In Section 5, we prove an equidistribution result for symbols [p, q] for pairs of primes
(p, q) taken from a large product X1 × X2 .
   As mentioned above, our algebraic methods for manipulating higher Selmer groups
requires a grid of twists with isomorphic fixed point Selmer groups. When proving
Theorem 4.18, we run into the problem that the grid class of a twist is usually not a
grid. Our solution to this is to carve the given grid class into smaller grids, a process
we complete in Section 6. This section also reduces the scope of Theorem 4.18 to the
behavior of a single matrix coefficient of the Cassels–Tate pairing.
   In the self-contained Section 7, we introduce the algebra and combinatorics needed
to mainpulate Selmer groups in grids of twists. We then apply this theory in Section 8
to finish the proof of Theorem 4.18.

4.3. Grid classes that are ready for higher work. Theorem 4.18 gives the distribution
of higher Selmer groups in specialized collections of twists, but we may apply it to prove
