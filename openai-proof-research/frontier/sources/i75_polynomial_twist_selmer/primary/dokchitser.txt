                                                 Abstract. We investigate how various invariants of elliptic curves,
                                                 such as the discriminant, Kodaira type, Tamagawa number and real
                                                 and complex periods, change under an isogeny of prime degree p. For
                                                 elliptic curves over l-adic fields, the classification is almost complete (the
                                                 exception is wild potentially supersingular reduction when l = p), and
                                                 is summarised in a table.

                                                                            1. Introduction
                                             We address the question how various invariants of elliptic curves, such as
                                          the discriminant, Kodaira type, Tamagawa number and real and complex
                                          periods, change under an isogeny. As every isogeny factors as a composition
                                          of endomorphisms and isogenies of prime degree, throughout the paper we
                                          just consider a fixed isogeny
                                                                               φ : E −→ E ′
                                          of prime degree p. The first result is a slight extension of a theorem of
                                          Coates ([3], appendix), relating the discriminants ∆E and ∆E ′ :
                                          Theorem 1.1. Let K be a field of characteristic 0 and φ : E → E ′ a
                                          p-isogeny of elliptic curves over K. If p > 3, then ∆pE /∆E ′ is a 12th power
                                          in K. For p = 2, 3 this is a 3rd, respectively 4th power.

                  2. ∆(E ′ ) = ∆(E)p up to 12th powers

curves E and E ′ . Here we work over an arbitrary field of characteristic 0,
so these are discriminants of some (not necessarily minimal) Weierstrass
models. They depend on the choice of models, and are well-defined up to
12th powers.

                 3. Discriminants and Kodaira types I
   Throughout §3-§6 we follow the notation of §1.1. In particular, K is a
finite extension of Ql , and φ : E/K → E ′ /K is a p-isogeny.
Theorem 3.1. Suppose E/K has additive potentially good reduction. Then
E has tame reduction (equivalently, has conductor exponent 2) if and only if
      • l ⩾ 5, or
      • l = 3 and E has Kodaira type III, III∗ , I∗0 , or
      • l = 2 and E has Kodaira type IV, IV∗ .
In this case E ′ is tame as well, and
                 0 < δ, δ′ < 12     and      δ′ ≡ p δ   mod 12.

Proof. For the first statement, see [23] IV.9, Table 4.1 for l ⩾ 5, [15] Thm. 1
for l = 3, and [14] Prop 8.20 for l = 2. From [23] IV.9, Table 4.1 it also
follows that 0 < δ, δ′ < 12 in all these cases. The last congruence follows
from Theorems 2.1–2.3.                                                       
Theorem 3.2. Suppose E/K has additive potentially good reduction and is
not a quadratic twist of a curve with good reduction. Then
     • If l = 2, 3 or l ≡ −1 mod 12 then E is potentially supersingular.
     • If l ≡ 1 mod 12, then E is potentially ordinary.
     • If l ≡ 5 mod 12, then E is potentially ordinary if and only if its
       Kodaira type is III or III∗ .
     • If l ≡ 7 mod 12, then E is potentially ordinary if and only if its
       Kodaira type is II, II∗ , IV or IV∗ .
Proof. Let K nr be the maximal unramified extension of K, and F/K nr the
(finite) extension cut out by the Galois action on any ℓ-adic Tate module
of E for ℓ 6= l. By the criterion of Néron-Ogg-Shafarevich, F is the unique
minimal Galois extension of K nr where E has good reduction.
   The Galois group Gal(F/K nr ) has order at least 3, since E is not a qua-
dratic twist of a curve with good reduction (cf. Lemma A.3). As explained
in [21] proof of Thm. 2, it acts faithfully on the reduced curve Ẽ defined
over the residue field of F as a group of automorphisms. This forces j(Ẽ) to
be either 0 or 1728, see e.g. [22] Thm. III.10.1. If l = 2 or 3, then 1728 = 0
is a supersingular j-invariant, as asserted.
   Now suppose l > 3. By [23] IV.9, Table 4.1, either a) E has reduction
type II, II∗ , IV or IV∗ and j(E) reduces to 0, or b) E has reduction type
III, III∗ and j(E) reduces to 1728. The j-invariant 0 is ordinary if and only
if l ≡ 1 mod 3, and 1728 is ordinary if and only if l ≡ 1 mod 4; see [22]
Ex. V.4.4, V.4.5.                                                           
Corollary 3.3. Suppose l = p and E has additive tame potentially good
reduction. If the reduction is potentially ordinary, then δ = δ′ and E, E ′
have the same Kodaira type. If the reduction is potentially supersingular,
then δ = 12 − δ′ and E, E ′ have opposite Kodaira types (II ↔ II∗ , III ↔ III∗ ,
IV ↔ IV∗ , I∗0 ↔ I∗0 ).
Proof. By Theorem 3.1, we have δ, δ′ < 12. Also, if δ 6= δ′ , then δ 6≡ pδ
mod 12, equivalently 12 ∤ δ(p−1). Exchanging E, E ′ if necessary, the possi-
bilities with δ 6= δ′ are (cf. [23] IV.9, Table 4.1)
       • δ = 2, δ′ = 10, p ≡ 2 mod 3,
       • δ = 4, δ′ = 8, p ≡ 2 mod 3,
       • δ = 3, δ′ = 9, p ≡ 3 mod 4.
By Theorem 3.2, these are precisely the cases of potentially supersingular
reduction unless E is quadratic twist of a curve with good reduction. In
the latter case, l cannot be 2 (as E has tame reduction), so E and E ′ have
Kodaira type I∗0 and δ = δ′ = 6.                                          
