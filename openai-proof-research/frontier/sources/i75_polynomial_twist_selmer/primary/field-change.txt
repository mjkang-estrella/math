1.2.2. Decomposing class groups. In [4], Cohen and Lenstra gave a conjectural heuris-
tic describing how the class group Cl K varies as K moves through the quadratic ex-
tensions of Q. These heuristics omitted the 2-primary part of the class group, and Gerth
[10] later supplemented that work with a prediction for the distribution of the groups
2(Cl K)[2∞ ]. For imaginary quadratic fields, Gerth’s prediction is now known to be
correct [27]. By considering 2(Cl K) rather than Cl K, Gerth avoided the subgroup
(Cl K)[2], which can be directly computed using genus theory and is typically large.
   A related question concerns the behavior of Selmer groups of elliptic curves in qua-
dratic twist families, where one fixes an elliptic curve E/Q and studies the behavior of
the Selmer groups of the quadratic twists E χ /Q as χ varies over quadratic characters.
See e.g. [12, 13, 27].
   These are analogous problems, but they belong to different genres of arithmetic sta-
tistical questions. Both kinds of problem start with a fixed GQ -module M. In the first,
one asks how SelK M varies as K moves through a family of number fields. In the
   FIELD CHANGE FOR THE CASSELS–TATE PAIRING AND APPLICATIONS TO CLASS GROUPS             5

second, one asks how SelQ M χ varies as χ moves through a family of twists. In the
particular examples considered above, M is taken either to be Q/Z with the unramified
local conditions, or the divisible Tate module of E with the standard local conditions.
   We can often translate between these two kinds of problems. Suppose K/F is a qua-
dratic extension of number fields, and take χ to be its corresponding quadratic character.
If M is a GF -module for which multiplication by 2 is invertible, the module IndK/F M
splits as a direct sum
                                 IndK/F M ∼ = M ⊕ M χ.
For class groups, for example, we have a decomposition
         (Cl ∗ K)[ℓ∞ ] ∼
                       = SelF IndK/F Qℓ /Zℓ ∼    = (Cl ∗ F )[ℓ∞ ] ⊕ SelF (Qℓ /Zℓ )χ
                                              

for every odd prime ℓ, where the local conditions are set as in Section 7.
   If multiplication by 2 on M is not invertible, the relationship between SelK M and
SelF M χ can be more complicated. In this case, the Cassels–Tate pairing proves to be
a convenient tool for moving from Selmer groups over extensions to Selmer groups of
twists. As an example, we will prove the following as the l = 2 case of Proposition 7.4.
Proposition 1.5. Take K/F to be a quadratic extension of number fields with associated
quadratic character χ, and take S to be the set of places of F not dividing 2 where K/F
is ramified. Suppose there is no element of F × \ (F × )2 which lies in (Fv× )2 at every
place v in S, and which has even valuation at all remaining finite primes.
   Then we have an exact sequence
         0 → (Cl ∗ K)Gal(K/F ) [2∞ ] −−→ (Cl ∗ K)[2∞ ] −−→ Sel(Q2 /Z2 )χ → 0
of finite Gal(K/F )-modules.
   The first term (Cl ∗ K)Gal(K/F ) [2∞ ] is essentially determined from genus theory, mak-
ing it an analogue of the subgroup (Cl K)[2] in Gerth’s conjecture. Meanwhile, the final
term Sel(Q2 /Z2 )χ , which agrees with 2(Cl K)[2∞ ] in the situation considered by Gerth,
is more in line with what the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics can handle.
   The idea of studying the ℓ∞ part of the class group of K by studying the module
IndK/F Qℓ /Zℓ and its irreducible components is not new; see e.g [23, 1.6.4], [6], or [28].
   The novelty of our work lies in the ability to handle “bad” choices of ℓ.

2.1. Conventions. We will keep the same notation as in [19, Section 2]. In an effort to
be self-contained, we recall some of our conventions now.
   Throughout, F will denote a global field with fixed separable closure F s /F and abso-
lute Galois group GF = Gal(F s /F ). For each place v of F , Fv denotes the completion
of F at v, Fvs denotes a fixed separable closure of Fv , and Gv denotes the Galois group
of Fvs /Fv . We write Iv for the inertia subgroup of Gv . For each place v we fix an
embedding F s ֒→ Fvs , via which we view Gv as a closed subgroup of GF .
   Given a topological group G, a discrete G-module M, and i ≥ 0, H i (G, M) will
denote the continuous group cohomology of G acting on M. We often work with these
groups using the language of inhomogeneous i-cochains [21, Section 2], the group of
which we denote C i (G, M). We denote by Z i (G, M) the subgroup of C i (G, M) con-
sisting of i-cocycles. Given φ in C i (GF , M) or H i (GF , M), we write φv as for the
restriction of φ to Gv .
   Finally, given a GF -module M, we write M ∨ for the dual GF -module Hom(M, (F s )× ),
and denote by ∪M the cup product constructed from the standard evaluation pairing
M × M ∨ → (F s )× (cf. [19, Notation 2.2]).
