## The half-spaces and proof

Define

    M₊={f∈H : h_(−j)=C^j h_j for every j≥1},
    M₋={f∈H : h₀=0 and h_(−j)=−C^j h_j for every j≥1}.

**Proposition.** Both are closed T_a-invariant subspaces of infinite dimension and infinite codimension. Their intersection is zero, and their sum is dense and not closed.

For closure, each coordinate defect f↦w_(−j)⁻¹f_(−j)∓C^j w_j⁻¹f_j is bounded on H, so its kernel is closed. Intersect these kernels, and for M₋ also the central-coordinate kernel. M₊ contains the full central E coordinate. M₋ contains a copy of E supported at coordinates ±1 with normalized values (g,−Cg). Both are infinite-dimensional. The j=1 defect map for M₊ surjects onto E; the central-coordinate map for M₋ surjects onto E. They vanish on the respective spaces, proving infinite codimension.

For invariance put k_j=w_j⁻¹(T_a f)_j. For j≥2, (1) gives k_(−j)=±C^j k_j. In the plus case the boundary is

    k_(−1)=C²h₂+Ch₀=Ck₁.

In the minus case k₀=h_(−1)+Ch₁=0 and k_(−1)=−C²h₂=−Ck₁. Since T_a is bounded, its output is in H; these coordinate identities prove invariance in the original Hilbert norm.

Injectivity of C gives M₊∩M₋={0}. Given any finite pair of normalized coordinates (u,v) at (j,−j), use density of ran C^j to approximate v by C^j r. The plus pair generated by (u+r)/2 and minus pair generated by (u−r)/2 sum to (u,C^j r). Fixed-coordinate multiplication by w_(−j) preserves convergence. Finite-coordinate vectors are dense in H, proving density of M₊+M₋.

To show the sum is proper, one needs the actual unbounded normalizer, not a numerical truncation. On the positive-measure cylinder F_m={x:x mod 2^m=2^m−1}, all dyadic residue blocks up to m wrap under x↦x+1. With n=8^m, the source's exact `eq:b-positive` implies

    log b̃_n(x) ≥100(2^m−1)|I_m|
               =700(2^m−1)8^(m−1).

The boundary term and omitted smaller blocks are nonnegative. Intersecting F_m with the source conull set preserves positive measure. Thus ess sup L=∞, and the multiplier coefficient of C has essential infimum zero. The injective operator C is not onto: otherwise the bounded inverse theorem would force its multiplication coefficient to be bounded away from zero.

Since w_(−1)=e^10 and w₁=e^(−10), the actual coordinate-one graph operator is K₁=e^20 C, also not onto. The negative coordinate of any vector in M₊+M₋ lies in ran K₁. Choose v outside this range and place it alone at coordinate −1. The resulting vector is not in the sum. Hence the sum is proper and dense, so is not closed.

## Scope and why this closes the candidate

M₊ is an invariant vector sublattice but not an ideal. Moreover S commutes with T_a and sends a nonzero central vector outside M₊, so M₊ is not hyperinvariant. The earlier absence of invariant ideals therefore never excluded these ordinary invariant subspaces. Quasinilpotence and empty point spectra also do not exclude them.

The construction is the elementary reflection symmetry of h_(j−1)+Ch_(j+1), with fixed-coordinate bounds making its graph closed. It extends to bounded commuting forward/backward operator-weighted shifts whose forward blocks are individually boundedly invertible. Supplementary conclusions require their stated extra hypotheses: injectivity for zero intersection, dense range for dense sum, and positivity for the lattice claims. No global similarity or bounded reflection involution is available here.

The audited report identifies M₊ exactly as the closed span of all central-coordinate orbits. It does not classify all invariant subspaces or resolve the ordinary ISP. No substantial-new-finding count is increased.
