Exotic Dehn twists and homotopy coherent group actions

Sungkyung Kang1 · JungHwan Park2 · Masaki Taniguchi3

Received: 26 September 2024 / Accepted: 29 September 2025 / Published online: 10 October 2025
© The Author(s) 2025


Abstract
We consider the question of extending a smooth homotopy coherent finite cyclic
group action on the boundary of a smooth 4-manifold to its interior. As a result,
we prove that Dehn twists along any Seifert homology sphere, except the 3-sphere,
on their simply connected positive-definite fillings are infinite order exotic.


1 Introduction

Dehn twists are fundamental in the study of mapping class groups of surfaces. In
this article, we study their generalization in dimension 4. Let Y be a closed oriented
3-manifold with a nontrivial element ϕ ∈ π1 (Diff+ (Y )), based at the identity. Then ϕ
induces a self-diffeomorphism of Y × [0, 1],
                                                            (︁        )︁
                 Φ : Y × [0, 1] → Y × [0, 1];      (s, t) ↦→ ϕt (s), t .

Given a smooth embedding of Y × [0, 1] into a compact smooth 4-manifold X, the
self-diffeomorphism Φ can be extended to a self-diffeomorphism of X. The resulting
diffeomorphism, tX : X → X, is called a 4-dimensional Dehn twist on X along Y .
Moreover, if Y is the boundary of X, then we have a Dehn twist supported in a collar
neighborhood of the boundary, and we call such a diffeomorphism a 4-dimensional
boundary Dehn twist. While Dehn twists can be considered in any dimension, we will
henceforth omit the dimension, as this article focuses solely on 4-dimensional Dehn
twists.

    The first nontrivial Dehn twist can be obtained from the work of Baraglia and
Konno [10]. Their results imply that the boundary Dehn twist on K3 ∖ B̊ 4 is not
isotopic to the identity rel. boundary (see [26, Sect. 2] for more detailed explanations).
Furthermore, Kronheimer and Mrowka [26, Theorem 1.1] proved that the Dehn twist
on K3#K3 along the separating S 3 is not isotopic to the identity. Lin [29] extended
this result and proved that it is not smoothly isotopic to the identity even after a
stabilization with S 2 ×S 2 . These results are obtained by using a one-parameter family
of Bauer-Furuta invariants and also the Pin(2)-equivariant version of it.
    While the above results are restricted to the case when Y = S 3 , the general case
when Y ̸= S 3 has garnered significant attention recently (see, e.g., [33, Sect. 1.2]
and [24]). In [24], the authors study Dehn twists and boundary Dehn twists along
Brieskorn spheres using 2-parameter families of Seiberg-Witten theory. Here, the
Dehn twist along a Brieskorn sphere Y is induced by the S 1 -action, which represents
a nontrivial element in π1 (Diff+ (Y )) (see [33, Proposition 8.8]). They prove that for
each nonnegative integer n, the boundary Dehn twists on any smooth, compact, sim-
ply connected, positive-definite 4-manifold bounded by Σ(2, 3, 6n + 7) are not iso-
topic to the identity rel. boundary [24, Theorem 1.1]. In particular, since Σ(2, 3, 13)
and Σ(2, 3, 25) bound smooth contractible 4-manifolds [1, 16], the boundary Dehn
twists on these contractible smooth 4-manifolds are also not isotopic to the identity
rel. boundary [24, Theorem 1.3] (they also obtain results related to Dehn twists after
a stabilization of S 2 × S 2 [24, Theorem 1.7 and Theorem 1.8] and Dehn twists on
closed 4-manifolds [24, Theorem 1.10]).
    Here, we recall an important property of Dehn twists along S 3 , which provides
the main motivation for this article. Since π1 (Diff+ (S 3 )) ∼= π1 (SO(4)) ∼= ℤ2 , we see
that each Dehn twist along S 3 has at most order two in the mapping class group. In
contrast, for general 3-manifolds, such as Seifert fibered spaces, we may expect them
to induce infinite order Dehn twists. In this article, we demonstrate that such infinite
order Dehn twists exist and can be found in any positive-definite filling of Brieskorn
homology spheres:

Theorem 1.1 Let Y ̸= S 3 be a Brieskorn homology sphere, and let X be a smooth,
compact, positive-definite 4-manifold filling of Y with b1 (X) = 0. Then, the boundary
Dehn twist along Y has infinite order in π0 (Diff+ (X, ∂X)).

   We note that such phenomena cannot be expected for arbitrary smooth fillings
of Brieskorn spheres. Indeed, for each Brieskorn sphere, the boundary Dehn twist
on its canonical negative-definite resolution is smoothly isotopic to the identity rel.
boundary [31, 32].
   Beyond Σ(2, 3, 13) and Σ(2, 3, 25), there are many other Brieskorn spheres that
bound smooth positive-definite 4-manifolds. For instance, Σ(p, ps ± 1, ps ± 2) when
p is odd, and Σ(p, ps − 1, ps + 1) when p is even and s is odd, bound contractible
4-manifolds (see, e.g., [1, 13, 16] for more examples). Moreover, there are Brieskorn
spheres that bound smooth rational homology balls even when they do not bound
any smooth contractible 4-manifold, with Σ(2, 3, 7) being the most well-known such
example (see, e.g., [2, 17, 39] for more examples). In a similar vein, for each positive
integer n, Σ(2, 3, 6n + 1) also bounds a smooth positive-definite 4-manifold, as it
can be realized as a +1-Dehn surgery on twist knots.

   The topological mapping class group of every compact, simply connected, topo-
logical 4-manifold has been computed by Orson and Powell [33]. In the special case
where the compact, simply connected, topological 4-manifold is bounded by a ra-
tional homology S 3 , they show that the topological isotopy type of an orientation-
preserving self-homeomorphism fixing the boundary is determined by its induced
action on the intersection form (see [33, Theorem C] for the precise statements).
Combined with our main theorem we have the following corollary. Here, given a
smooth 4-manifolds X, we say a diffeomorphism f ∈ Diff+ (X, ∂X) is exotic if f is
isotopic to the identity rel. boundary through elements of Homeo+ (X, ∂X) but not
through elements of Diff+ (X, ∂X).

Corollary 1.2 Let Y ̸= S 3 be a Brieskorn homology sphere, and let X be a smooth,
compact, positive-definite 4-manifold filling of Y with π1 (X) = 0. Then, the boundary
Dehn twist along Y is exotic and has infinite order in π0 (Diff+ (X, ∂X)).          □

   The study of exotic diffeomorphisms was initiated by Ruberman in [35, 36], where
he demonstrated the first examples. Recently, this topic has been studied more ex-
tensively, leading to many interesting related results [9, 20, 22, 24, 26, 27, 29]. It
turns out that among exotic diffeomorphisms, those induced by Dehn twists have the
special property of producing loops of homeomorphisms that cannot be extended to
loops of diffeomorphisms. To be more precise, given a closed oriented 3-manifold Y
bounding a smooth 4-manifold X, there is the following fiber sequence:

                          Diff+ (X, ∂X) ↪→ Diff+ (X) → Diff+ (Y ),

as well as an analogous fiber sequence for homeomorphism groups (see [33, Ap-
pendix A] and [24, Sect. 6]). If a boundary Dehn twist on X along Y , induced by
a nontrivial element ϕ ∈ π1 (Diff+ (Y )), is exotic, then ϕ lifts to π1 (Homeo+ (X))
but not to π1 (Diff+ (X)) (see [24, Sect. 6] for details). In particular, this implies
that if there exists an exotic boundary Dehn twist on X along Y , then the inclusion
map Diff+ (X) ↪→ Homeo+ (X) induces a non-surjective map on their fundamental
groups. In particular, the results of [10, 24, 26, 29] imply that the corresponding maps
for K3 ∖ B̊ 4 and (K3#S 2 × S 2 ) ∖ B̊ 4 are non-surjective, and the same holds for any
smooth contractible 4-manifolds bounded by Σ(2, 3, 13) and Σ(2, 3, 25). For closed
4-manifolds, the only known such example is provided by [11, Theorem 1.3], which
proved that the corresponding map is non-surjective for K3.
   In this setting, Corollary 1.2 implies that for smooth, compact, simply connected,
positive-definite 4-manifolds bounded by any Brieskorn homology sphere, the map
induced by the inclusion is not only non-surjective but also has infinitely many ele-
ments that are not contained in its image:

Corollary 1.3 Let Y ̸= S 3 be a Brieskorn homology sphere, and let X be a smooth,
compact, simply connected, positive-definite filling of Y . If i∗ is the map induced
by the inclusion Diff+ (X) ↪→ Homeo+ (X) on their fundamental groups, then the
Coker i∗ contains a subgroup isomorphic to ℤ.                                     □

   Before we lay out the whole strategy of our proof, we briefly highlight the essence
of our result. Specifically, our main theorem is proved by showing that for each
positive-definite filling with vanishing b1 of a Brieskorn homology sphere, there is
a large enough prime p such that there is no homotopy coherent ℤp -action on the
smooth positive-definite filling that extends the ℤp -action on the boundary, where the
boundary action is induced by the S 1 -action (see Sects. 2 and 3 for a more detailed
explanation of the relationship between homotopy coherent actions and Dehn twists).
In particular, it implies that for each positive-definite filling with vanishing b1 , there
is no ℤp -action that extends the ℤp -action on the boundary, as long as p is large
enough. We note that this phenomenon, and indeed more general results regarding
the extendability of ℤp -actions, was observed earlier. Anvari and Hambleton [3, 4]
proved that the ℤp -action induced by the S 1 -action on Σ(a1 , a2 , a3 ) do not extend to
any smooth contractible filling (see also [28]). Baraglia and Hekmati [7, 8] extended
this result to smooth positive-definite fillings with vanishing b1 of Σ(a1 , a2 , . . . , ar )
under mild assumptions, implying that for a fixed Brieskorn homology sphere, the
nonextension result holds for all but finitely many primes (see [7, Corollary 1.7 and
Corollary 1.14] for precise statements). In fact, the work of Baraglia and Hekmati is
crucially used in our proof, as we will see below.

2 Homotopy coherent ﬁnite group actions

We begin by recalling the Borel construction for ℤp -actions on a manifold. Let X
be a smooth manifold, possibly with boundary, equipped with a smooth ℤp -action,
where p is an odd prime. We can then define a smooth X-bundle (i.e. a X-bundle
with structure group Diff+ (X)) as follows:

                                 X → (X × Eℤp )/ℤp → Bℤp .

While this is probably the simplest definition of Borel construction, we will provide
a way to construct it by gluing trivial X-bundles over simplices, with a view towards
applying it for Dehn twists.

3 Dehn twists and group actions

Let Y be a closed oriented 3-manifold with an S 1 -action that induces a nontrivial
element ϕ ∈ π1 (Diff+ (Y )), based at the identity. Specifically, the smooth S 1 -action
on Y is given by e2πit · s := ϕt (s), where e2πit ∈ S 1 and s ∈ Y . Let Φ be the induced
diffeomorphism of Y × [0, 1], defined by
                                                              (︁       )︁
                Φ : Y × [0, 1] → Y × [0, 1];        (s, t) ↦→ ϕt (s), t .

Thus, if Y bounds a smooth, compact 4-manifold X, then there is a boundary Dehn
twist on X along Y . Note that they are only well-defined up to isotopy rel. boundary.
In other words, we treat a boundary Dehn twist as an isotopy class of diffeomor-
phisms rel. boundary rather than a specific concrete diffeomorphism.
