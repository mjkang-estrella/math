\[
 N_l\le \frac{A_l}{h_l^n-u_l^n}+1,
 \qquad A_l\le(1+\epsilon)^{n-1}\sum_{i\text{ in bin }l}w_i^n.
\]

Assign one standard unimodular simplex of capacity h_l^n to each group. A cube of side h_l has n! such simplices. Rounding the number of groups up to full cubes costs less than n!h_l^n in addition to A_l/(1−K^(−n)). Thus the total requested **cube capacity** is bounded by

\[
 FQ+n!\sum_{l\ge0}(2Ku_l)^n=FQ+R_n.        \tag{1}
\]

This is where the infinite possible list of weight levels is paid: its geometric series is finite, independent of the number of occupied bins. It is not discarded as an unspecified small tail. Every point belongs to an actual finite group.

There is enough geometrically located cube supply as follows. Take all H-grid cubes whose closures lie in P's interior. For 4Hc_P<1 their union contains

\[
 2H\mathbf1+(1-4Hc_P)P.
\]

Indeed a point of this inner simplex has every coordinate ≥2H and weighted coordinate sum ≤1−2Hc_P; the closure of a containing H-grid cube has positive coordinates and weighted sum ≤1−Hc_P. Consequently the available capacity is at least

\[
 V(1-4Hc_P)^n\ge V(1-4nHc_P)\ge V(1-8nKc_PM). \tag{2}
\]

Pack requested dyadic cubes in nonincreasing side length into these finitely many H-cubes. A free cube can be subdivided into 2^n children. Before a request of side h, every remaining free cube has side at least h and volume an integer multiple of h^n. If the total capacity fits, a free cube exists and can be subdivided to supply it. This proves termination of the finite allocation algorithm, including widely differing scales. A scalar volume comparison by itself would not prove this packing; the common dyadic hierarchy does.

To make every local envelope strictly smaller than its cell capacity, use group cutoff (1−zeta)h_l^n for one sufficiently small zeta>0. In (1), replace 1−K^(−n) by 1−zeta−K^(−n). The strict inequality (U) persists for small zeta. A single item fits because K≥400. This pays the local strictness rather than claiming boundary jet separation. The cells can now be contracted slightly and translated into their relative interiors, retaining strict envelope inequalities and M_group<h_l/100 after contraction.

### 3.2 Local interpolation and the global maximal minor

Triangulate each cube by the n! simplices with vertices

\[
 0,\ h e_{\pi(1)},\ h(e_{\pi(1)}+e_{\pi(2)}),\ldots,h\mathbf1
\]

and translate to its location. The cumulative-column matrix is integral unimodular. Each simplex is therefore a torus-coordinate image of Δ(h,…,h), whose local entrance and mixed envelope have just been paid. Apply the complete source graded reshape, mixed CRT and backward finite transfer on each contracted axis simplex. These are genuine local interpolation inputs, with fixed graded families. Rounded translations and unimodular coordinate changes put their selected columns inside fixed compact subsets of their assigned simplex interiors for all large k.

The interpolation is simultaneous, not merely separately successful. Within one cube, the convex piecewise affine function

\[
 \phi(y)=\sum_{i<j}|y_i-y_j|
\]

has the permutation-order simplices as its linearity chambers. On their strict interiors the affine pieces have unique maxima. The A139 normalization gives entries z^(phi(u)−ell_j(u)), so the full selected matrix tends to the direct sum of the n! local minors. Empty simplices need no rows or columns. This gives an actual nonzero minor for the cube's whole group of points.

Then glue sibling dyadic cubes by successive coordinate hyperplanes. Glue top-grid cubes similarly, cutting the finite grid into slabs and then individual cubes. At each node use the already obtained child **simultaneous** minors and the same strict hyperplane determinant limit. Choose its parameter after the child parameters. There are only finitely many nodes for the given vector and degree; induction terminates at the proved local inputs. A hanging-node adaptive triangulation is not assumed globally coherent. Hierarchical gluing of finite minors is enough, and avoids that extra assumption.

All selected exponents lie in one fixed compact subset of the original P's interior. Original graded saturation includes them for every sufficiently large integer k. The original flag transfer then gives a full mixed evaluation minor in H^0(X,kL). Coincidence would obstruct constant interpolation, so the centres are distinct. Degreewise rank openness and a countable intersection over k provide one very-general tuple for this vector. No gradedness of the final assembled degreewise support union is asserted or needed.

For a common real-vector locus, at fixed r use countably many rational epsilon,K and positive rational vectors satisfying (U). Strictness allows any real vector satisfying (U) to be approximated from below by such vectors with nearby rational parameters. Their nef classes converge on one countable common locus. Thus every such real class is nef. The all-large-degree rank claim for a fixed real vector follows directly from its finite allocation above; it is not inferred from closedness of rank at boundary.

### 3.3 What this proves and what it does not

For every fixed sigma in (0,1), choose epsilon small and K≥400 large so that F(1−sigma)<1. Next choose delta_sigma(X,L)>0 such that the rounding charge R_n and boundary charge 8nKc_PM V fit the remaining fixed gap whenever M<delta_sigma. Then

\[
 \max w_i<\delta_\sigma,\qquad \sum_i w_i^n\le(1-\sigma)V
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D(w)\text{ nef}.       \tag{3}
\]

This covers **arbitrary ratios, all lengths and arbitrarily many levels**, with a single threshold for that fixed slack. It is more than a bounded-level or bounded-ratio result. As a useful asymptotic estimate, for sufficiently small M take K=M^(−1/(n+1)) and epsilon=M^(n/(n+1)). Then F−1 and the boundary charge divided by V are O_{X,L,n}(M^(n/(n+1))), while R_n is O_n(M^(n(n−1)/(n+1))). Thus a volume deficit C_{X,L,n}M^(n/(n+1)) suffices for large enough fixed C. Equivalently, arbitrary volume-feasible profiles acquire nefness after a multiplicative shrinkage 1−O_{X,L,n}(M^(n/(n+1))). Constants are not claimed optimal.

At Q=V, (U) cannot hold: F>1, R_n>0 and the available-volume bound is below V. Sending epsilon→0 and K→infinity changes the entrance/boundary charges; at a fixed positive vector M does not tend to zero. Therefore (3), or the asymptotic error estimate, does **not** prove the exact boundary with one delta. This is a limitation of this quantitative construction, not a proof that every adaptive allocation loses volume.

## 4. Route V142: continuous unequal bubble supply and the combined closure

### 4.1 A usable continuous boundary construction

Apply A139 to (P^n,O(1)) with D=1, V=1 and eta=1/4. Choose an integer q so large that

\[
 (3/(4q))^{1/n}<1/1600.
\]

For **every real** t in [1/4,3/4], the bubble class with q entries (t/q)^(1/n) and q entries ((1−t)/q)^(1/n) is nef at its exact volume boundary. This is an actual local nef input established by A139, not W136 on projective space.

On the fixed X choose R≥r0(X,L) equal roots a=(V/R)^(1/n). Independently choose t_s in [1/4,3/4] at each root s. B139 then supplies the boundary vector

\[
 \underbrace{a(t_s/q)^{1/n},\ldots,a(t_s/q)^{1/n}}_{q},\quad
 \underbrace{a((1-t_s)/q)^{1/n},\ldots,a((1-t_s)/q)^{1/n}}_{q}
 \quad(s=1,\ldots,R).                          \tag{4}
\]

It has 2qR entries and total volume V. It can have 2R distinct levels and irrational normalized volume fractions. Finite repetition at selected leaves gives further such classes. The algebraic families, gluing restrictions, smoothing and fixed-X transfer are exactly the audited arbitrary-input operation; no infinite tower is used. Countably many rational parameter approximations from below and finite patterns yield one common locus at each final length before real limits. Irrational t need not define a rational line bundle on the total space.

This **does improve the combined region**: equal trees have only reciprocal normalized volumes, whereas generic choices in (4) do not. At full volume the two-block envelope primitive alone has at most two distinct positive levels, whereas (4) generically has many. Thus the separate reciprocal and singleton-cell obstructions are not barriers to this combination. The continuous parameters, however, are attached to groups of q equal children, not independently to arbitrary specified leaves.

### 4.2 A new obstruction to a precisely defined combined supply

Define G to be the supply generated by the paid equal classes and power-morphism equal splits, I136 one-envelope classes, and A139 two-envelope classes, using finite B139 refinements, permutation, deletion, coordinatewise decrease, finite convex combination and fixed-length limits. Inputs at an internal bubble are on (P^n,O(1)); root inputs are on the fixed (X,L). To strengthen this supply we may grant all equal root/branch counts, including an identity singleton, and their downward closures. The actual A/J entrance restrictions are retained. This definition does **not** include arbitrary unspecified nef bubble classes merely because B139 could accept them. Nor does it assert that all further consequences of the complete upstream source lie in G.

**V142 obstruction.** For r≥2 a positive, full-volume vector with pairwise distinct coordinates does not belong to this generated closure G_r.

Normalize root volume to one, so coordinates are v_i=w_i/V^(1/n). All seed vectors have sum v_i^n≤1. Equality in an envelope inequality forces equality in

\[
 \sum_{i\in I}v_i^n\le (\max_{i\in I}v_i)^{n-1}\sum_{i\in I}v_i.
\]

Every positive coordinate in that block must then equal its maximum. Therefore a full-volume J seed is equal, and a full-volume A seed has at most two constant blocks. Its entrance forces more than two positive entries: the normalized maximum of a J seed is at most 1/100 and that of an A seed at most 1/400 (at most 1/800 on the projective-space bubbles). The least intercept is no bigger than capacity^(1/n), giving these bounds from the respective entrances; on (P^n,O(1)), min(1,eta)=eta<1/2. The root bound 1/400 suffices too. Every nonidentity equal seed has at least two children of equal capacity. Hence every nontrivial boundary seed has a repeated coordinate. More usefully, every edge of every nonidentity primitive has nth-power factor at most 1/2. Unary equal contractions can be suppressed, retaining their capacity loss.

A finite full-volume tree with r≥2 leaves has a deepest nontrivial node. Its children are terminal; two have equal weights. Thus the leaf vector has a repeated coordinate. This alone would not cover limits of trees with unbounded depth, losses or discarded leaves. Those cases are paid next.

Fix a positive target v of length r with sum v_i^n=1 and let mu=min_i v_i>0. Suppose retained, possibly decreased leaf vectors converge to v. Their retained total volumes tend to one, so **all** discarded volume, downward loss and local seed deficits together tend to zero. This follows from the telescoping identity for a finite tree: root capacity equals final total leaf capacity plus the sum, over nodes, of parent capacity times its relative seed deficit. All terms are nonnegative.

For sufficiently late approximants, every retained terminal weight is at least mu/2; every ancestor on such a path therefore has capacity at least (mu/2)^n. Each nonidentity step reduces capacity by at least a factor two. After suppressing unary contractions the depth on a retained path is at most ceil(log_2((2/mu)^n)). The union of the r retained paths has boundedly many nodes; there are finitely many pruned combinatorial shapes. Pass to a subsequence with one such shape and convergent edge weights. At each relevant node, its seed deficit and the total omitted-child capacity tend to zero, since its parent capacity is bounded below. Every retained child has positive limiting capacity.

For equal primitives the positive limiting children remain equal. For envelope primitives, the envelope defect tends to zero, so their positive limiting coordinates are constant within at most two blocks. The same uniform small-coordinate entrance remains true in the limit; an envelope node cannot collapse to a full-volume unary or binary node. No omitted positive capacity remains. The resulting **finite pruned limit tree** is full-volume and has a deepest nontrivial node with repeated terminal weights. These are coordinates of the fixed target v, contradicting their pairwise distinctness. Unretained branches may be arbitrarily deep; no bound on their depth was needed. This is a compactness proof for the retained paths, not an assertion that an arbitrary infinite normal-cone tower smooths.

Convex combinations do not evade the conclusion. The positive l_n unit ball is strictly convex for n>1. At v its supporting functional is y_i=v_i^(n−1), with dual norm one. Outside any neighborhood of v in this compact unit ball, y·x≤1−c for some c>0. If finite convex combinations converge to v, their y-averages tend to one; there must therefore be underlying generated finite-tree vectors converging to v. This has just been excluded. Internal finite convex combinations can be pushed to the leaves: refinement multiplies coefficients, so expansion is a finite convex combination of the refined choices; missing convex mass is the zero vector. Downward operations and deletion commute with this domination argument. Fixed-length intermediate limits can likewise be approximated before each finite continuous operation. Hence the obstruction covers the stated generated closure, including all these operations in combination.

For a concrete diffuse sequence take

\[
 p_i=\frac{2i}{r(r+1)},\qquad w_i=(Vp_i)^{1/n},\quad i=1,\ldots,r.
\]

The positive normalized volumes are distinct, sum to one and have maximum 2/(r+1)→0. Each fixed-r vector is excluded from G_r by the argument above. These are **not** non-nef examples; no obstructing algebraic curve is exhibited. New bubble inputs, different central components, or other adaptive interpolation operations can escape G. In particular the strict-slack construction of §3 is not declared to lie in this primitive grammar.

### 4.3 Why continuous fractions have not finished W136

Formula (4) removes reciprocal arithmetic and permits independently varying fractions at many nodes, but it retains terminal repetitions. The closure obstruction shows that merely iterating the currently paid envelope seeds, even with convexification, real parameters and vanishing discarded volume, cannot supply every fixed positive boundary profile. A successful refinement route must terminate in additional **proved** unequal bubble inputs (or use a different central degeneration) allowing independently prescribed terminal capacities with no final loss. Postulating W136 for P^n would assume precisely an unpaid local input. Finite truncation with residual volume is only a slack result, and no limit of thresholds tending to zero is substituted for the fixed-vector requirement.

## 5. Decision and exact remaining statement

The strongest new paid result is (U)/(3): fixed-slack arbitrary-profile nefness on the same X, uniform in length, weight ratios and number of levels, with explicit allocation losses and a genuine global mixed maximal minor. The continuous boundary classes (4) demonstrate a real improvement from combining A139 and B139. V142 gives a new fixed-positive-profile obstruction to the **defined combined generated supply**, stronger than the separate old reciprocal and slender-cell limitations.

These are technical deductions from the complete stipulated operations, classical determinant cutting and weighted algebraic refinement. No absent-title argument establishes substantiality. They neither prove W136 nor disprove it. The exact missing mechanism is a no-final-loss arbitrary-profile allocation with one local maximum-weight entrance independent of slack, or corresponding proved unequal algebraic bubble/central inputs with fixed-X transfer and the same uniform entrance. Boundary limits must retain that same entrance. W136 remains parked; no nomination is forced.
