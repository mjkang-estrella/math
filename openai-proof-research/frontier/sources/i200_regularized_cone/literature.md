## External primary comparisons

1. **Tim Netzer and Raman Sanyal, Smooth Hyperbolicity Cones are Spectrahedral Shadows**, [arXiv:1208.0441v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0441v1). All eight PDF pages were read through text extraction; the full PDF is retained here as [netzer-sanyal-1208.0441v1.pdf](netzer-sanyal-1208.0441v1.pdf), SHA-256 `4ee0218bcd4ce05f6ccc00983ccaf3392bfe64994c2046ca4bb41b509406c162`. Theorem 1.1 assumes smoothness of the defining polynomial at every nonzero boundary point. Theorem 3.1 and its proof cover a factorwise condition using local curvature and a finite cover. Page 7 describes approximation by smooth liftable cones. The I200 family retains corners, so neither theorem is applied as a lift of K_delta. The retrieved PDF bears an internal November 27, 2024 date despite its v1 identifier; the URL and exact byte hash, which match the I198 retrieval, are recorded without silently relabeling its version.

2. **Claus Scheiderer, Smooth hyperbolicity cones are second-order cone representable**, [arXiv:2509.17121v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17121v1). Read the introduction, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, definitions in section 2 through 2.6, and selected tensor/curvature passages 3.11–3.13 and 4.1–4.4. The full paper was not read or retained. Theorem 1.1 permits local Nash defining functions and gives blocks of size at most two; Theorem 1.2 concerns compact convex sets with Nash-smooth boundary and strictly positive curvature. The I200 corner with distinct supporting hyperplanes fails even the Nash hypothesis. The tensor-evaluation criterion is recognized as a potentially different route, but is not invoked to prove any new statement here.

3. **Ronan Quarez, Piecewise Certificates of Positivity for matrix polynomials**, [arXiv:1001.1277v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.1277v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.1277v1). Read the abstract/introduction and the complete section-4 proof of Theorem 4.1 with Lemma 4.2, plus Theorem 4.3 and definition 3.3. These give piecewise positivity certificates for positive-definite homogeneous matrix polynomials/biforms. They do not assert affine semidefinite representability of the matrix-fractional cone built from the polynomial. In I200 the missing implication is precisely how to turn certificates whose scalar coefficients depend on y into an exact affine lift with singular X allowed. No full PDF copy is retained and no full-paper read is claimed.

4. **D. V. Matyukhin, On positive-definite biquadratic forms that cannot be represented as the sum of squares of bilinear forms**, [primary bibliographic page](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=vmumm&option_lang=eng&paperid=3694&wshow=paper), Vestnik Moskov. Univ. Ser. 1 Mat. Mekh. (1995), no. 2, 29–33. Only metadata/title were inspected. Its full Russian proof was not obtained and supplies no proof dependency. The title already warns against identifying strict positivity with bilinear SOS. The fixed-degree closedness and threshold assertions in I200 are proved directly, rather than attributed to this uninspected proof.

## Early check, final check and novelty limits

Searches were made early for positive-definite quadratic matrix maps, smooth-cone lifts and positive-Gram representability, and repeated after the final statements for positive quadratic regularization of hyperbolicity cones and strict-positive matrix shadows. Results about the 2026 source family on secondary catalogue/expository sites were excluded as proof and novelty evidence. No absence of a search hit establishes novelty.

The decisive contribution comparison is constructive: the large-delta theorem uses an ordinary coefficient Gram matrix, a rank-one bound, and the source's existing generalized Schur-complement operation. The denominator-cleared moment patch and its positive curvature calculation explain a precise failed route but do not yield a new nonliftability theorem. Neither is nominated as a substantial finding. The small-delta status remains unresolved within this record, rather than being asserted to be a literature-wide open problem.
