The stipulated sample cap is exp(CB); bit work is polynomial in n,L and that cap on every trace stream. Fixed p gives quasipolynomial complexity; q<=n^(-kappa) gives polynomial complexity for fixed kappa>0. The decoder uses positive Boolean moment matrices, exact rank-statistic identities, a quantitative wrong-prefix separation, dyadic tolerances and capped rational feasibility iterations. The latest-anchor proof uses contiguous masks, common-channel powers, compact Fourier support and exact cancellation of earlier anchors; it is a sample theorem, not a replacement efficient decoder. The fixed-q lower operation constructs actual hard word pairs, using alternating blocks, signed defects, conditional rank kernels and matched global offsets. It gives superpolynomially small one-trace TV separation, not just an abstract testing example.

Family 139's entire proof was read: jets, tensor divergence, centering, numerical circuits, query compiler, final conditional descent, and query lower bound. The model is C2, supplied minimizer 0, V(0)=0 and I<=Hessian V<=2I, exact real value/gradient queries, fixed TV 1/10, and a worst-run C_epsilon d^epsilon query cap. Computation between queries is unrestricted. The noisy-mean compiler, exact physical-query shifts, finite routine height, fixed-seed Lipschitz bounds, reserved Gaussian noise and localization/coupling are already lower operations. No finite-precision or bit algorithm follows from this model; exact real answers can carry unbounded information.

Three precise screened targets are:

* **W152:** arbitrary binary-capacity tables with rational product activities w(X)=product theta_ij^(X_ij), a TV-tau sampler with every-run polynomial bit cost, and a normalizer in a compact exponent/mantissa or additive-log representation. Dimensions vary and zeros remain allowed. Plain interval conditioning is exhausted and is replaced by this genuinely weighted target.
* **H152:** known rational position-dependent retentions p_i in a fixed interval [p_-,p_+] subset (0,1), an efficient worst-case decoder using exp(polylog n) traces and polynomial work in that sample count and all channel encodings. This is distinct from estimating one scalar, letter-dependent thinning, iid trace-law contamination, and independent output bit flips.
* **R152:** in the family-139 model, tolerate uniformly bounded adversarial value/gradient errors of a small dimension-independent size, while retaining the worst-run subpolynomial query bound and TV 1/10. Replies may depend on past queries/replies but cannot inspect future private random draws. No consistency with an approximate potential, unbiasedness, or continuity of the errors is assumed.

None is asserted to be absent from all literature. No family needed replacement as a whole; the exhausted subdirections were removed before development. I77's weighted graph-switch mechanism and the I29/I61–I71 entropy-rounding branch remain parked.

### 2.2 Weighted tables: encoding and the precise obstruction to this reduction

A real obstruction already occurs for 2 by 2 tables with both margins (N,N). Write X_t=((t,N-t),(N-t,t)), 0<=t<=N, and give the two diagonal cells activity 2 and the other cells activity 1. Then

    w(X_t)=4^t,    Z=(4^(N+1)-1)/3.

For binary N, an ordinary numerator/denominator encoding of any positive rational within relative error 1/4 of Z needs Omega(N) numerator bits. Thus a standard polynomial-bit FPRAS output for this *unnormalized* weighted Z is impossible in that encoding, irrespective of mixing. This does not obstruct sampling, or a compact scientific-notation/logarithmic answer. Expanding each capacity into N unary positions likewise fails the requested binary model.

The complete operative weighted-table reduction in Chen–Liu, arXiv:2610.06724v1, Appendix C.9, explicitly uses unary capacities and polynomial total capacity, log-concave entry-weight tables, and polynomial logarithmic coefficient range. Its factorial-polarization proof expands coordinate i into c_i labels and invokes its subset-convolution theorem in that expanded dimension. Appendix A.5 separately pays rational bit arithmetic. Those hypotheses cover many weighted instances but do not remove the binary expansion in W152. This is an applicability comparison, not a claim that their general theorem is false or insufficient in its own model.

The stipulated cell-bound construction avoids that expansion by scaled large-cell bins. In its weight evaluator the softened penalty has base-two logarithmic Lipschitz constant <=1/8 on the rescaled box, so adjacent bins have comparable weights, bounded population correction, and conductance >=1/(10^4 d^2 B_bin). A product activity contributes a linear log weight with variation proportional to capacity times log(theta) on those same bins. In the displayed 2 by 2 example this variation is Omega(N/B_bin). Therefore polynomially many equal bins do not preserve the source's bounded correction/adjacent-ratio interface. Choosing the source's penalty/annealing length to dominate the entire activity range also costs Omega(N). Log-concavity of the added linear tilt alone does not pay either cost.

The unpaid task is an adaptive weighted large-cell evaluator with polynomial binary encoding, polynomial mixing or another sampling mechanism, and controlled normalizer ratios *without* a bound polynomial in numerical total capacity or total log-weight range. This is more specific than “weighted signatures”; diagonal reweighting of a finite signature matrix is not the missing algorithm. The 2 by 2 geometric distribution itself is easy to sample, so this is a genuine obstruction to the examined encoding/bin reduction, not a complexity lower bound for W152. **PARK W152.**

### 2.3 Trace reconstruction: an efficient technical noise extension and an actual contamination barrier

Let D and zeta be the degree cap and dyadic tolerance from the efficient decoder, chosen with 4 zeta below its wrong-prefix gap. Let N_D be its number of rank-set statistics; D=O(Y^2), where Y=1+log(n)/(1+log(1/max(q,1/n)))<=2X. A statistic T_I is the product of retained bits at ranks in I, set to zero when the trace is too short, with |I|<=D.

Suppose, after deletion, each existing bit at output rank j in trace t is independently flipped with a *known* rational probability e_tj<1/2, independently of the deletion process, with 1-2e_tj>=a>0. Rates can vary by rank and trace; their encodings are part of the input. Replace that bit Y_tj by

    U_tj=(Y_tj-e_tj)/(1-2e_tj),
    S_tI=product_(j in I) U_tj, or zero if the trace is too short.

Conditional independence gives E[S_tI | unflipped trace]=T_I exactly. Also |S_tI|<=A:=a^(-D). Independent, possibly nonidentically distributed traces therefore obey the ordinary bounded-variable concentration estimate. Taking

    M >= 2^18 A^2 zeta^(-2) log(64(N_D+1))

gives simultaneous empirical error <=zeta/16 with probability at least 31/32. If an adversary then replaces at most alpha M whole observed records, with alpha<=zeta/(64A), the extra error is <=2A alpha<=zeta/32. Clamp/decode malformed records as a fixed bounded record so the range bound holds. Dyadic rounding of each statistic to error <=zeta/64 leaves the decoder's zeta/8 empirical entrance intact. The wrong-prefix infeasibility and correct-prefix positive-semidefinite slack are therefore unchanged.

Products and sums of at most D rational factors have polynomial encoding length in D and the channel bit lengths. The existing capped rational feasibility algorithm thus retains every-run polynomial bit work in n, all channel encodings, and M. For fixed p,a, M remains quasipolynomial; in the small-deletion regime q<=n^(-kappa), D is bounded, so a>=n^(-b) still gives polynomial sample and bit bounds for fixed kappa,b. This does not protect masks against insertions, erasures of unknown positions, or adversarial flips inside each trace at unrestricted rates. It is a direct bounded-statistic debiasing deduction after the full efficient decoder, hence **technical**, not a substantial finding. It does not rely on sample identifiability alone, and its error accounting is not M times a per-trace TV bound.

There is a stronger negative conclusion for iid Huber contamination. For two actual deletion-trace laws P,Q at TV distance v, their contamination neighborhoods intersect whenever

    v <= alpha/(1-alpha).

Indeed the pointwise maximum of their densities has mass 1+v. Take R=(1-alpha)max(P,Q) plus any nonnegative residual measure of mass 1-(1-alpha)(1+v). Then R=(1-alpha)P+alpha H_P=(1-alpha)Q+alpha H_Q for probability laws H_P,H_Q. Every number of iid observations from R has identical law in the two experiments. Uniform success greater than 1/2 is impossible. The stipulated fixed-deletion hard word pairs have v smaller than every inverse power of n, so any fixed inverse-polynomial contamination level defeats worst-case exact reconstruction for those pairs, even with unlimited samples and computation. Applying a common bit-flip channel contracts TV and preserves this barrier. This statement is about iid mixture contamination; it does not silently assert the same exact boundary for a deterministic replacement budget.

For H152 itself, the exact first-moment generating function is

    sum_i x_i p_i product_(j<i)(1-p_j+p_j z).

The common powers used by the stipulated rank/mask proofs are gone. Hartung–Holden–Peres 1708.02216v1, read through its full proof ending, proves exp(O(n^(1/3))) sample bounds under its weak-monotonicity or two-periodicity hypotheses. Its letter-dependent branch estimates per-letter rates and thins them. The position-dependent proof uses the displayed product, an arc estimate and pairwise string tests; it does not supply the uniform quasipolynomial efficient decoder sought here. Constants retain those channel hypotheses. In particular, thinning an output bit by its unknown original position is not an available operation.

The exact missing decoder entrance is a lower bound, uniform over the proposed heterogeneous channels and wrong-prefix positive moment matrices, of the form

    inf_(wrong-prefix feasible moment m) max_(|I|<=D)
        |E_m T_I - E_x T_I| >= exp(-polylog n),
    D=polylog n,

together with polynomial encoding of the corresponding coefficient map and the positive correct-prefix slack. Coefficient computability alone does not establish the separation. General TV coupling only gives the baseline sample-count-times-channel-error loss and is not nominated. **PARK H152.**

### 2.4 Strongest route I: quantitative stability of the compiled query algorithm

The following paid partial of R152 uses the *actual* compiler, not continuity of an arbitrary oracle transcript. For every fixed epsilon,gamma>0 there are constants c,C>0 such that errors

    |Vhat(x)-V(x)| <= c d^(-gamma),
    ||ghat(x)-gradient V(x)||_2 <= c d^(-gamma)

permit TV <=1/10 with at most C d^epsilon queries on every run in the original real-query model. The error bound is Euclidean, not coordinatewise. The supplied true minimizer and C2 Hessian assumptions are retained. This assertion does not give a finite-bit algorithm, arbitrary TV accuracy, or dimension-independent error tolerance.

Here is the quantitative mechanism. Write delta=d^(-e), lambda<=delta, and use B_d for a constant times a fixed power of log(d), allowed to increase a fixed number of times. Fix the source compiler parameters K0,t,w first, with its bounded routine height H and bounded expression depth D0. Its center and final coefficient sums satisfy

    B_d lambda A <= B_d delta^(1/3),
    A_f <= C for mean routines M,   A_f <= Cr for sampling routines S.

The Gaussian seed transformations and all scalar coefficients are oracle-independent. Compare an exact run and an erroneous run using the *same fixed seed list*. If the primitive gradient error is at most u uniformly, terminal routines differ by at most u for M and ru for S before the reserved Gaussian is added. No statistical assumptions on that error are used.

For a nonterminal routine, induct on routine height. Hold anchors fixed and let E_i bound the discrepancy of the supplied center at expression node i. The true child routine has fixed-seed center Lipschitz bound B_d lambda; compare the erroneous child to the true child at the erroneous center, then compare the two true children. Thus

    E_i <= Cr Delta_anchor + A B_d u
                + A B_d lambda max_(earlier nodes) E_j.

For sufficiently large d the last coefficient is <1/2. Bounded expression depth gives E_i<=B_d(r Delta_anchor+A u). At the final expression the discrepancy is bounded by

    A_f B_d u + A_f B_d lambda max_i E_i,

with the direct anchor term also included where present. For S its Gaussian anchor is unchanged; for M its anchor is supplied by a child S and has error <=B_d r u. The exact M's anchor Lipschitz bound B_d lambda r makes this extra cost <=B_d lambda r^2 u. Consequently every reachable routine obeys

    |Mhat-M| <= B_d u,
    |Shat_pre-S_pre| <= B_d r u.                         (R1)

This induction tolerates different errors at repeated queries and adaptive errors. It never applies the Gaussian absorption identity to the erroneous oracle and never differentiates it. The constants remain polylogarithmic because routine height and expression depth are fixed; they are not iterated once per individual query.

Draw each routine's unused reserve *after* all its oracle calls. Conditional on the completed calls and earlier randomness, the reserve is fresh and independent. Gaussian translation smoothing therefore converts (R1) to

    W2(Shat_pre,S_pre) <= B_d r u,
    TV(Shat,S) <= B_d r u / eta,                        (R2)

where eta/2 is its reserved standard deviation. This remains valid conditional on any past adversary state. Anticipating access to the future private reserve is not in the model.

For the outer proximal sampler, h=delta/2 and g_y(z)=sqrt(h) gradient V(y+sqrt(h)z), so u=sqrt(h) times the original gradient-error bound e_g. At top scale r=eta=1, rescaling to original coordinates gives per-step W2 error <=B_d h e_g. The ideal smoothed proximal transition contracts W2 by 1/(1+h). Including the source intrinsic error and its second-moment estimate yields the scalar recurrence

    a_(j+1) <= (1-c0 h) a_j
                 + B_d sqrt(h) delta^K0 sqrt(d)
                 + B_d h e_g.                         (R3)

The small intrinsic coefficient multiplying a_j is absorbed as in the source. After O(h^(-1)log d) steps, the added adversarial term is <=B_d e_g. The source's final Gaussian regularization costs h^(-1/2), leaving added TV <=B_d e_g/sqrt(h).

For a stateful adversary, the hybrid comparison retains its state explicitly. At a fixed incoming state, disintegrate the actual joint law of (observation, next adversary state) into its observation marginal and the conditional next-state kernel. Replace only the observation marginal by the ideal one and keep that conditional kernel (extended arbitrarily on a null set). The joint TV cost equals the marginal TV cost. Under a prefix of these replacements, each observation has its ideal law conditional on the whole past, while the auxiliary adversary state can have any law. Thus the ideal-history moment estimates remain valid and later computation is the same kernel on the enlarged state. The incoming noised variable can be replaced in the same way, retaining its actual conditional adversary-state law. This pays the stateful hybrid step without claiming closeness of full oracle transcripts.

The final conditional descent also remains controlled. At level j its radius is r_j=2^(-j/2), and (R2) with eta=1 costs B_d r_j sqrt(h)e_g. A history-by-history kernel replacement, uniform in the adversary state, sums these costs to B_d sqrt(h)e_g, since sum r_j is finite. It does not condition a supposedly independent exact trajectory on future success. At the terminal Gaussian approximation the contraction iteration z -> -r_J g_y(x_J+r_J z) adds at most r_J sqrt(h)e_g/(1-delta r_J^2) to its center error; Gaussian translation pays this directly. The source's ideal-history intrinsic errors and final Gaussian base error are unchanged. Thus overall

    TV(output,pi_V) <= o(1) + B_d e_g/sqrt(h).           (R4)

Choose 0<e<min(epsilon/2,gamma), then K0>2+1/e and sufficiently small t as in the source. Its query bound B_d delta^(-(1+t)) is O(d^epsilon), and d^(-gamma)/sqrt(h) tends to zero even after B_d. All loops and query locations remain defined for every bounded reply sequence. This pays the inverse-power-error partial.

For the finitely many smaller dimensions, retain the source's capped Gaussian rejection fallback. The approximate value is used with downward slack: acceptance exp(-Vhat(x)+|x|^2/2-e_v), which lies between e^(-2e_v) and one times the exact acceptance, since V>=|x|^2/2. For its finite query cap, coupling the accept/reject uniforms bounds added failure by that cap times 2e_v. Taking c sufficiently small for these finitely many dimensions completes the uniform statement. This uses approximate values only in the fallback; no polynomial bit cost is inferred for its real arithmetic or Gaussian draws.

Correctness and contribution differ here. The new bookkeeping gives an explicit inverse-power tolerance and a pathwise proof for inconsistent errors, but the decisive fixed-seed stability, small coefficient product, finite-depth compiler and reserved-noise mechanism are supplied lower operations. This is classified **technical**, not a substantial nomination.

### 2.5 Strongest route II: consistent potentials, and where that mechanism stops

For comparison, if the replies are exact derivatives of a single C2 potential W with the same Hessian bounds and supplied minimizer, and ||gradient(W-V)||<=eta uniformly, interpolation V_s=(1-s)V+sW gives

    d/ds pi_(V_s)(f) = -Cov_(pi_(V_s))(f,W-V).

The Poincare constant is at most one for every s. Hence Var(W-V)<=eta^2, and the L1 norm of the density derivative is at most eta. Integration gives TV(pi_W,pi_V)<=eta/2. Truncation justifies the calculation for the Lipschitz, at-most-linear difference W-V under these Gaussian-tailed laws. Uniform value error e_v separately gives the elementary density-ratio bound e^(2e_v)-1. Running the source sampler for W and adding this perturbation bound is only standard source stitching.

A permitted inconsistent oracle need not fit this route. In d>=2 let V(x)=3|x|^2/4 and let J rotate the first two coordinates by ninety degrees, annihilating the others. Return the exact value V(x) and gradient

    gradient V(x) + eta Jx/sqrt(1+|x|^2).

The Euclidean error is at most eta, vanishes at the supplied minimizer, and has nonzero curl at zero (antisymmetric derivative eta J). It is not the gradient of any C2 approximate potential. This is an actual oracle on an allowed target, not an abstract countermodel. It obstructs the consistent-potential proof; it does not prove impossibility of robust sampling. Likewise, the centering and adjoint identities for an exact score cannot be reapplied to this perturbed field without an error estimate.

The remaining quantitative problem for the dimension-independent R152 target is to replace the R4 cost B_d e_g/sqrt(h) by a bound <=C e_g (or another dimension-uniform quantity small at a fixed e_g), through a direct stability/regularization or invariant-law argument valid for arbitrary bounded, potentially nonconservative replies. Selecting h=d^(-e) and making e smaller does not remove its growth for fixed error. Repetition of a TV-1/10 sampler is not an accuracy amplifier. A compactness-only modulus of transcript continuity would pay neither this inequality nor the query cap.

The current primary comparison supports these distinctions. Chen–Chewi–Daskalakis–Rakhlin, PMLR 336 (2026), assumes iid unbiased oracle samples and controls their tails. Its FORS proof uses exact conditional centering, clips path-integral estimators, and compares the resulting density; its proximal proof sums conditional TV errors. Those proofs and their relevant technical lemmas were read through their endings. Their guarantee is not an arbitrary bounded adversarial-error theorem. Chen–Chewi–Lu–Zhang, Smoothed Picard HMC, **2609.06906v2** (September 29), has exact gradient/proximal interfaces and expected query bounds. Its fixed-seed Lipschitz and centered Gaussian fluctuation estimates explain why smooth numerical stability is already part of the primary landscape. Only the recorded operative statements and selected stability proofs are claimed read for that paper; its entire high-accuracy companion proof is not claimed read or used. These comparisons do not establish worldwide novelty of R1–R4, and R152 is not nominated. **PARK R152.**
