Abstract.
Okounkov bodies,
which are closed convex sets defined for big line bundles,
have rich information on the line bundles.
On the other hand,
Seshadri constants are invariants which measure the positivity
of line bundles.
In this paper,
we prove that Okounkov bodies give lower bounds of Seshadri constants.
Key words and phrases: Okounkov body, Seshadri constant, graded linear series
2010 Mathematics Subject Classification14C20;14M25
1. Introduction
In this paper,
we investigate a relation between
Okounkov bodies and Seshadri constants.
Okounkov bodies were introduced by Lazarsfeld and Mustaţă [LM]
and independently by Kaveh and Khovanskii [KK],
based on the work of Okounkov [Ok1, Ok2].
First, recall the definition of Okounkov bodies.
Let XX be a variety of dimension nn,
z=(z1,…,zn)z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}) a local coordinate system at a smooth point p∈Xp\in X,
and >> a monomial order on ℕn\mathbb{N}^{n}.
Then we obtain a valuation
ν=νz,>:𝒪X,p∖{0}→ℕn\nu=\nu_{z,>}:\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{n}
as follows;
for f∈𝒪X,p∖{0}f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\setminus\{0\},
we expand it as a formal power series
f=∑u∈ℕncu​zuf=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{u}z^{u}
and set
ν⁡(f):=min⁡{u|cu≠0},\nu(f):=\min\{\,u\,|\,c_{u}\not=0\},
where the minimum is taken with respect to the monomial order >>.
Let LL be a big line bundle on a projective variety XX.
Then we can define the Okounkov body Δ⁡(L)\Delta(L) of LL as follows.
Fix an isomorphism Lp≅𝒪X,pL_{p}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X,p}.
Then this isomorphism naturally induces Lpk≅𝒪X,pL^{k}_{p}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X,p} for any k≥0k\geq 0,
and we have the map

We write ν⁡(H0​(X,k​L))⊂ℕn\nu(H^{0}(X,kL))\subset\mathbb{N}^{n} for the image of H0​(X,k​L)∖{0}H^{0}(X,kL)\setminus\{0\} by this map.
Note that ν​(H0​(X,k​L))\nu(H^{0}(X,kL)) does not depend on the choice of the isomorphism Lp≅𝒪X,pL_{p}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
because ν\nu maps any unit element
in 𝒪X,p\mathcal{O}_{X,p} to 0∈ℕn0\in\mathbb{N}^{n}.
Then
ΓL=ΓL,z,>:=⋃k∈ℕ{k}×ν⁡(H0​(X,k​L))⊂ℕ×ℕn\Gamma_{L}=\Gamma_{L,z,>}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{k\}\times\nu(H^{0}(X,kL))\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}
is a semigroup by construction.
We define the Okounkov body of LL with respect to zz and >> by
Δ⁡(L)=Δz,>​(L):\displaystyle\Delta(L)=\Delta_{z,>}(L):
=closed convex hull​(⋃k≥11k​ν​(H0​(X,k​L)))\displaystyle=\text{closed convex hull}\,\Big(\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\nu(H^{0}(X,kL))\Big)
=Σ⁡(ΓL)∩({1}×ℝn),\displaystyle=\Sigma(\Gamma_{L})\cap(\{1\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),
where Σ⁡(ΓL)\Sigma(\Gamma_{L}) is the closed convex cone spanned by ΓL\Gamma_{L}.
More generally,
we can define the Okounkov body for a graded linear series.
That is,
for a graded linear series W∙W_{\bullet} associated to a line bundle LL on a (not necessarily projective) variety XX,
we set
ΓW∙=ΓW∙,z,>:=⋃k∈ℕ{k}×ν⁡(Wk)⊂ℕ×ℕn.\Gamma_{W_{\bullet}}=\Gamma_{W_{\bullet},z,>}:=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{k\}\times\nu(W_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}.
We define the Okounkov body of W∙W_{\bullet} with respect to zz and >>
by Δ⁡(W∙)=Δz,>​(W∙):=Σ⁡(ΓW∙,z,>)∩({1}×ℝn)\Delta(W_{\bullet})=\Delta_{z,>}(W_{\bullet}):=\Sigma(\Gamma_{W_{\bullet},z,>})\cap(\{1\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Remark 1.1.
In [LM],
they use “admissible flags” instead of local coordinate systems.
Essentially, there is no difference if >> is the lexicographic order.
Local coordinate systems are used in [BC] for instance.
On the other hand,
Demailly [De] defined an interesting invariant, Seshadri constant,
which measures the local positivity of an ample line bundle at a point.
Seshadri constants relate to
jet separation of adjoint bundles,
Ross-Thomas’ slope stabilities of polarized varieties [RT],
Gromov width (an invariant in symplectic geometry) [MP], and so on.
Nakamaye [Na] defined Seshadri constants at very general points
for big line bundles.
For a detailed treatment of Seshadri constants,
we refer the reader to [La, Chapter 5] or [B+].
We extend the notion of Seshadri constants slightly.
Usually,
Seshadri constants are defined in a numerical way.
However,
we adopt an equivalent definition in terms of jet separation
since we treat graded linear series in this paper.
Let W∙W_{\bullet} be a “birational” graded linear series associated to a line bundle LL on a variety XX
(see Definition 3.2 for the definition of a birational graded linear series).
Using jet separation (cf. [De, Theorem 6.4],
[ELMNP, Proposition 6.6]),
we define the Seshadri constant ε⁡(W∙,1)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};1) of W∙W_{\bullet} at a very general point by
ε⁡(W∙,1):=supk>0s⁡(Wk,1)k∈ℝ+∪{+∞},\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};1):=\sup_{k>0}\frac{s(W_{k};1)}{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\},
where s⁡(Wk,1)s(W_{k};1) is the supremum of integers s≥−1s\geq-1
such that the natural map
Wk↪H0​(X,Lk)→Lk⊗𝒪X/𝔪ps+1W_{k}\hookrightarrow H^{0}(X,L^{k})\rightarrow L^{k}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathfrak{m}_{p}^{s+1} is surjective
for a very general point p∈Xp\in X.
When LL is a big line bundle on a projective variety XX and W∙={H0​(X,k​L)}kW_{\bullet}=\{H^{0}(X,kL)\}_{k},
it is easy to show W∙W_{\bullet} is birational.
Hence we can consider the Seshadri constant of W∙W_{\bullet} at a very general point,
which we denote by ε⁡(L,1)\varepsilon(L;1) or ε⁡(X,L,1)\varepsilon(X,L;1).
To relate Seshadri constants with Okounkov bodies,
we introduce an invariant for a convex set.
For an integral polytope Δ⊂ℝn\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} of dimension nn,
we can consider an invariant ε⁡(Δ,1):=ε⁡(XΔ,LΔ,1)\varepsilon(\Delta;1):=\varepsilon(X_{\Delta},L_{\Delta};1),
where (XΔ,LΔ)(X_{\Delta},L_{\Delta}) is the polarized toric variety corresponding to Δ\Delta.
To generalize this invariant to an nn-dimensional convex set Δ\Delta,
we introduce a “monomial” birational graded linear series WΔ,∙W_{\Delta,\bullet} associated to 𝒪(ℂ×)n\mathcal{O}_{(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}} on (ℂ×)n(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
(see Definition 4.1).
Thus we can define ε⁡(Δ,1):=ε⁡((ℂ×)n,WΔ,∙,1)\varepsilon(\Delta;1):=\varepsilon((\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n},W_{\Delta,\bullet};1).
In this paper, we show the following theorem,
which states that Okounkov bodies give lower bounds on Seshadri constants.
Theorem 1.2 (Special case of Theorem 5.8).
Let XX be a projective variety of dimension nn,
and fix a local coordinate system z=(z1,…,zn)z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}) on XX at a smooth point
and a monomial order >> on ℕn\mathbb{N}^{n}.
For a big line bundle LL on XX,
it holds that
ε⁡(L,1)≥ε⁡(Δz,>​(L),1)\varepsilon(L;1)\geq\varepsilon(\Delta_{z,>}(L);1).
We explain the idea of the proof of Theorem 1.2
when LL is ample.
The strategy is to compare two graded linear series
{H0​(X,k​L)}k\{H^{0}(X,kL)\}_{k} and W​(ΓL)∙:={⨁u∈ν⁡(H0​(X,k​L))ℂ​xu}kW(\Gamma_{L})_{\bullet}:=\{\bigoplus_{u\in\nu(H^{0}(X,kL))}\mathbb{C}x^{u}\}_{k}.
Note that ⨁u∈ν⁡(H0​(X,k​L))ℂ​xu⊂ℂ⁡[ℕn]\bigoplus_{u\in\nu(H^{0}(X,kL))}\mathbb{C}x^{u}\subset\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}^{n}] is nothing but
the degree kk-th part of the graded ring ℂ⁡[ΓL]\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{L}].
If ΓL\Gamma_{L} is finitely generated,
Anderson [An] showed that ⨁kH0​(X,k​L)\bigoplus_{k}H^{0}(X,kL) degenerates to ℂ⁡[ΓL]\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{L}].
In other words,
(X,L)(X,L) degenerates to the (not necessarily normal) toric variety (Proj⁡ℂ⁡[ΓL],𝒪⁡(1))(\Proj\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{L}],\mathcal{O}(1)).
Since Seshadri constants have a lower semicontinuity,
we have ε⁡(X,L,1)≥ε⁡(Proj⁡ℂ⁡[ΓL],𝒪⁡(1),1)\varepsilon(X,L;1)\geq\varepsilon(\Proj\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{L}],\mathcal{O}(1);1).
It is easy to check that ε⁡(Proj⁡ℂ⁡[ΓL],𝒪⁡(1),1)=ε⁡(XΔ⁡(L),LΔ⁡(L),1)=ε⁡(Δ⁡(L),1)\varepsilon(\Proj\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{L}],\mathcal{O}(1);1)=\varepsilon(X_{\Delta(L)},L_{\Delta(L)};1)=\varepsilon(\Delta(L);1),
and Theorem 1.2 follows in this case.
Unfortunately,
ΓL\Gamma_{L} is not finitely generated in general.
Hence we cannot use the degeneration of the section ring ⨁kH0​(X,k​L)\bigoplus_{k}H^{0}(X,kL).
Instead, we degenerate linear series as in [CM],
that is,
we degenerate H0​(X,k​L)H^{0}(X,kL) to W​(ΓL)k=⨁u∈ν⁡(H0​(X,k​L))ℂ​xuW(\Gamma_{L})_{k}=\bigoplus_{u\in\nu(H^{0}(X,kL))}\mathbb{C}x^{u} for each kk separately.
Then we can show s⁡(H0​(X,k​L),1)≥s⁡(W​(ΓL)k,1)s(H^{0}(X,kL);1)\geq s(W(\Gamma_{L})_{k};1)
and compare the Seshadri constants of LL and W​(ΓL)∙W(\Gamma_{L})_{\bullet}.
Thus even if we show Theorem 1.2 only for an ample line bundle LL,
we have to treat the graded linear series W​(ΓL)∙W(\Gamma_{L})_{\bullet}.
This is the reason why we extend the notion of Seshadri constants to graded linear series.
The definition of Seshadri constants can be easily generalized to the multi-point case
(cf. [La],[B+]).
That is,
we can define the Seshadri constants ε⁡(W∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}) and ε⁡(Δ,𝐦)\varepsilon(\Delta;\mathbf{m})
for a weight 𝐦=(m1,…,mr)∈ℝ+r\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}.
Theorem 1.2 is generalized
to the multi-point case and birational graded linear series as follows.
Theorem 1.3 (=Theorem 5.8).
Let W∙W_{\bullet} be a birational graded linear series associated to a line bundle LL
on an nn-dimensional variety XX.
Fix a local coordinate system z=(z1,…,zn)z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}) on XX at a smooth point
and a monomial order >> on ℕn\mathbb{N}^{n}.
Then ε⁡(W∙,𝐦)≥ε⁡(Δz,>​(W∙),𝐦)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})\geq\varepsilon(\Delta_{z,>}(W_{\bullet});\mathbf{m})
holds for any r∈ℤ+r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+} and 𝐦∈ℝ+r\mathbf{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}.
This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some notations and conventions.
In Section 3, we define Seshadri constants of graded linear series and investigate basic properties.
In Section 4, we study Seshadri constants of monomial graded linear series.
In Section 5, we give the proof of Theorem 1.3.
Throughout this paper,
we consider varieties or schemes over the complex number field ℂ\mathbb{C}.

2. Notations and conventions
We denote by ℕ,ℤ,ℚ,ℝ\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R}, and ℂ\mathbb{C} the set of all
natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, real numbers, and complex numbers respectively.
In this paper, ℕ\mathbb{N} contains 00.
Set ℤ+=ℕ∖0{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\mathbb{N}\setminus 0, ℝ+={x∈ℝ|x>0}{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,x>0\}.
For a real number t∈ℝt\in\mathbb{R},
the round up of tt is denoted by ⌈t⌉∈ℤ\lceil t\rceil\in\mathbb{Z}.
For a subset S⊂ℝnS\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}, we denote by Σ⁡(S)\Sigma(S) the closed convex cone spanned by SS.
For t∈ℝt\in\mathbb{R},
we set t​S={t​u|u∈S}tS=\{\,tu\,|\,u\in S\}.
For another subset S′⊂ℝnS^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
S+S′={u+u′|u∈S,u′∈S′}S+S^{\prime}=\{u+u^{\prime}\,|\,u\in S,u^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}\} is the Minkowski sum of SS and S′S^{\prime}.
For simplicity of notation,
we denote S+(−S′)S+(-S^{\prime}) by S−S′S-S^{\prime}.
For u′∈ℝnu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
S+u′={u+u′|u∈S}S+u^{\prime}=\{u+u^{\prime}\,|\,u\in S\} is
the parallel translation of SS by u′u^{\prime}.
For a convex set Δ⊂ℝn\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},
the dimension of Δ\Delta is the dimension of the affine space spanned by Δ\Delta.
We denote by Δ∘\Delta^{\circ} the interior of Δ\Delta.
A subset P⊂ℝnP\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} is called a polytope if it is the convex hull of a finite set in ℝn\mathbb{R}^{n}.
A polytope PP is integral
if all its vertices are contained in ℤn\mathbb{Z}^{n}.
For a variety XX, we say a property holds at a general point of XX
if it holds for all points in the complement of a proper algebraic subset.
A property holds at a very general point of XX
if it holds for all points in the complement of the union of countably many proper subvarieties.
Throughout this paper, a divisor means a Cartier divisor.
Thus we use the words “divisor”, “line bundle”, and “invertible sheaf ” interchangeably.
For divisors DD and D′D^{\prime},
the inequality D≥D′D\geq D^{\prime} means D−D′D-D^{\prime} is effective.
3. Seshadri constants of graded linear series
In this section,
we define Seshadri constants of graded linear series.
Definition 3.1.
Let LL be a line bundle on a (not necessarily projective) variety XX,
and WW a subspace of H0​(X,L)H^{0}(X,L).
For r∈ℤ+r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+} and 𝐦=(m1,…,mr)∈ℤr\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r},
we say that WW separates 𝐦\mathbf{m}-jets at smooth rr points p1,…,prp_{1},\ldots,p_{r} in XX
if the natural map
W→L/(L⊗⨂i=1r𝔪pimi+1)=⨁i=1rL/𝔪pimi+1​L\ W\rightarrow L\big/\Big(L\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{m}_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}+1}\Big)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}L/\mathfrak{m}_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}+1}L
is surjective,
where we regard 𝔪pimi+1=𝒪X\mathfrak{m}_{p_{i}}^{m_{i}+1}=\mathcal{O}_{X} for mi≤−1m_{i}\leq-1.
We say WW generically separates 𝐦\mathbf{m}-jets
if WW separates 𝐦\mathbf{m}-jets at general rr points in XX.
By definition,
any WW generically separates 𝐦\mathbf{m}-jets
if mi≤−1m_{i}\leq-1 for any ii.
For W⊂H0​(X,L)W\subset H^{0}(X,L) and 𝐦=(m1,…,mr)∈ℝ+r\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r},
we define s⁡(W,𝐦)∈ℝ∪{+∞}s(W;\mathbf{m})\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\} to be
s⁡(W,𝐦)=sup{t∈ℝ|W​generically separates ⌈t​𝐦⌉-jets},s(W;\mathbf{m})=\sup\{\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,W\ \text{generically separates $\lceil t\mathbf{m}\rceil$-jets}\},
where ⌈t​𝐦⌉=(⌈t​m1⌉,…,⌈t​mr⌉)\lceil t\mathbf{m}\rceil=(\lceil tm_{1}\rceil,\ldots,\lceil tm_{r}\rceil).
When dimW<+∞\dim W<+\infty, we have
s⁡(W,𝐦)=max⁡{t∈ℝ|W​generically separates ⌈t​𝐦⌉-jets}∈ℝ.s(W;\mathbf{m})=\max\{\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,W\ \text{generically separates $\lceil t\mathbf{m}\rceil$-jets}\}\in\mathbb{R}.
When W=H0​(X,L)W=H^{0}(X,L),
we denote s⁡(W,𝐦)s(W,\mathbf{m}) by s⁡(L,𝐦)s(L;\mathbf{m}).
Suppose XX is a variety of dimension nn
and LL is a line bundle on XX.
Let W∙={Wk}k∈ℕW_{\bullet}=\{W_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}} be a graded linear series associated to LL,
i.e., WkW_{k} is a subspace of H0​(X,k​L)H^{0}(X,kL) for any k≥0k\geq 0 with W0=ℂW_{0}=\mathbb{C},
such that
⨁k≥0Wk\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}W_{k} is a graded subalgebra of the section ring ⨁k≥0H0​(X,k​L)\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(X,kL).
When all WkW_{k} are finite dimensional,
W∙W_{\bullet} is called of finite dimensional type.
Definition 3.2.
A graded linear series W∙W_{\bullet} on a variety XX is birational if
the function field K⁡(X)K(X) of XX is generated by {f/g∈K(X)|f,g∈Wk,g≠0}\{\,f/g\in K(X)\,|\,f,g\in W_{k},g\not=0\} over ℂ\mathbb{C}
for any k≫0k\gg 0.
When W∙W_{\bullet} is of finite dimensional type,
this is clearly equivalent to the condition that
the rational map defined by |Wk||W_{k}| is birational onto its image for any k≫0k\gg 0,
which is Condition (B) in [LM, Definition 2.5].
Now we define Seshadri constants of graded linear series.
Definition 3.3.
Let W∙W_{\bullet} be a birational graded linear series associated to a line bundle LL on a variety XX.
For 𝐦=(m1,…,mr)∈ℝ+r\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r},
we define the Seshadri constant of W∙W_{\bullet} at very general points with weight 𝐦\mathbf{m} to be
ε⁡(X,W∙,𝐦)=ε⁡(W∙,𝐦):=supk>0s⁡(Wk,𝐦)k∈ℝ+∪{+∞}.\varepsilon(X,W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}):=\sup_{k>0}\dfrac{s(W_{k};\mathbf{m})}{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}.
Note that s⁡(Wk,𝐦)>0s(W_{k};\mathbf{m})>0 holds for k≫0k\gg 0 by the birationality of W∙W_{\bullet}.
When Wk=H0​(X,k​L)W_{k}=H^{0}(X,kL) for any kk,
we denote it by ε⁡(X,L,𝐦)\varepsilon(X,L;\mathbf{m}) or ε⁡(L,𝐦)\varepsilon(L;\mathbf{m}).
Remark 3.4.
The definition of Seshadri constants by jet separation is due to Theorem 6.4 in [De].
See also [La] or [ELMNP].
We treat the definition by blowing ups in Lemmas 3.9 and 3.10.
Remark 3.5.
By definition,
s⁡(W,𝐦)≤s⁡(W′,𝐦)s(W;\mathbf{m})\leq s(W^{\prime};\mathbf{m}) holds for
subspaces W⊂W′W\subset W^{\prime} in H0​(X,L)H^{0}(X,L).
Thus ε(W∙;𝐦)≤ε(W∙′;𝐦)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})\leq\varepsilon(W_{\bullet}^{{}^{\prime}};\mathbf{m}) holds for W∙,W∙′W_{\bullet},W_{\bullet}^{{}^{\prime}} associated to LL
if Wk⊂Wk′W_{k}\subset W_{k}^{{}^{\prime}} for any kk
(we write W∙⊂W∙′W_{\bullet}\subset W_{\bullet}^{{}^{\prime}} for such W∙,W∙′W_{\bullet},W_{\bullet}^{{}^{\prime}}).
For an injection L↪L′L\hookrightarrow L^{\prime} between line bundles on XX
and a subspace W⊂H0​(X,L)W\subset H^{0}(X,L),
s⁡(W,𝐦)s(W;\mathbf{m}) does not change if we regard WW as a subspace of H0​(X,L′)H^{0}(X,L^{\prime})
because we consider jet separation at general points.
Hence ε⁡(W∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}) does not change for W∙W_{\bullet} associated to LL
if we consider that W∙W_{\bullet} is associated to L′L^{\prime}.
Let π:X′→X\pi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X be a birational morphism
and W∙W_{\bullet} a graded linear series associated to LL on XX.
Then we can consider that W∙W_{\bullet} is a graded linear series on X′X^{\prime} associated to π∗​L\pi^{*}L
by the natural inclusion H0​(X,k​L)⊂H0​(X′,k​π∗​L)H^{0}(X,kL)\subset H^{0}(X^{\prime},k\pi^{*}L).
By a similar reason to above,
we have ε⁡(X,W∙,𝐦)=ε⁡(X′,W∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(X,W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\varepsilon(X^{\prime},W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}).
By the following lemma,
we may assume that W∙W_{\bullet} is of finite dimensional type in many cases
when we prove properties of ε⁡(X,W∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(X,W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}).
Lemma 3.6.
Let W∙W_{\bullet} be a birational graded linear series.
Suppose that W1,∙⊂W2,∙⊂⋯⊂Wl,∙⊂⋯⊂W∙W_{1,\bullet}\subset W_{2,\bullet}\subset\cdots\subset W_{l,\bullet}\subset\cdots\subset W_{\bullet}
is an increasing sequence of birational graded linear series in W∙W_{\bullet}
such that Wk=⋃l=1∞Wl,kW_{k}=\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}W_{l,k} for each kk.
Then it holds that ε⁡(W∙,𝐦)=suplε⁡(Wl,∙,𝐦)=limlε⁡(Wl,∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\sup_{l}\varepsilon(W_{l,\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\lim_{l}\varepsilon(W_{l,\bullet};\mathbf{m}).
Proof.

we have

5.2. Proof of Theorem 1.3
Since Seshadri constants are lower semicontinuous (cf. [La, Example 5.1.11]),
degenerations are useful to bound Seshadri constants from below.
For example,
Biran [Bi] degenerates varieties to reducible schemes,
and gives lower bounds of (multi-point) Seshadri constants on ℙ2\mathbb{P}^{2}.
In [It],
the author uses toric degenerations to obtain lower bounds of Seshadri constants on some non-toric varieties.
We also use degenerations to prove Theorem 1.3.
Although we would like to degenerate ⨁kWk\bigoplus_{k}W_{k} to ℂ⁡[ΓW∙]=⨁kVν⁡(Wk)\mathbb{C}[\Gamma_{W_{\bullet}}]=\bigoplus_{k}V_{\nu(W_{k})},
the semigroup ΓW∙\Gamma_{W_{\bullet}} is not finitely generated in general,
even if ⨁kWk\bigoplus_{k}W_{k} is finitely generated.
Instead,
we degenerate WkW_{k} to Vν⁡(Wk)V_{\nu(W_{k})} for each kk separately.
Then we can use the lower semicontinuity of jet separation, as in [CM].
This is the reason why we define Seshadri constants in terms of jet separation.
First,
we show that the Seshadri constants of W​(Γ)∙W(\Gamma)_{\bullet} and WΔ⁡(Γ),∙W_{\Delta(\Gamma),\bullet} coincide
for a birational semigroup Γ\Gamma.
Lemma 5.6.
For a birational semigroup Γ⊂ℕ×ℕn\Gamma\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{n} and 𝐦∈ℝ+r\mathbf{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r},
we have ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γ),𝐦)=ε⁡(W​(Γ)∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma);\mathbf{m})=\varepsilon(W(\Gamma)_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}).
Proof.
By definition,
it holds that
ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γ),𝐦)=lims⁡(Vk​Δ​(Γ),𝐦)k,ε⁡(W​(Γ)∙,𝐦)=lims⁡(VΓk,𝐦)k.\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma);\mathbf{m})=\lim\dfrac{s(V_{k\Delta(\Gamma)};\mathbf{m})}{k},\quad\varepsilon(W(\Gamma)_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\lim\dfrac{s(V_{\Gamma_{k}};\mathbf{m})}{k}.
Since Γk\Gamma_{k} is contained in k​Δ​(Γ)k\Delta(\Gamma) for any kk,
ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γ),𝐦)≥ε⁡(W​(Γ)∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma);\mathbf{m})\geq\varepsilon(W(\Gamma)_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}) is clear.
Hence it is enough to show the opposite inequality.
When Γ\Gamma is finitely generated,
there exists u~=(l,u)∈Γ⊂ℕ×ℕn\tilde{u}=(l,u)\in\Gamma\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{n} such that
(Σ⁡(Γ)+u~)∩(ℕ×ℕn)⊂Γ(\Sigma(\Gamma)+\tilde{u})\cap(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{n})\subset\Gamma
by [Kh, §3, Proposition 3] (see also [LM, Subsection 2.1]).
This induces (k​Δ​(Γ)∩ℕn)+u⊂Γk+l(k\Delta(\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{N}^{n})+u\subset\Gamma_{k+l},
from which we have s⁡(Vk​Δ​(Γ),𝐦)=s⁡(V(k​Δ​(Γ)∩ℕn)+u,𝐦)≤s⁡(VΓk+l,𝐦)s(V_{k\Delta(\Gamma)};\mathbf{m})=s(V_{(k\Delta(\Gamma)\cap\mathbb{N}^{n})+u};\mathbf{m})\leq s(V_{\Gamma_{k+l}};\mathbf{m}).
Thus we obtain
ε⁡(W​(Γ)∙,𝐦)=limks⁡(VΓk,𝐦)k\displaystyle\varepsilon(W(\Gamma)_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\lim_{k}\dfrac{s(V_{\Gamma_{k}};\mathbf{m})}{k}
=limks⁡(VΓk+l,𝐦)k\displaystyle=\lim_{k}\dfrac{s(V_{\Gamma_{k+l}};\mathbf{m})}{k}
≥limks⁡(Vk​Δ​(Γ),𝐦)k=ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γ),𝐦).\displaystyle\geq\lim_{k}\dfrac{s(V_{k\Delta(\Gamma)};\mathbf{m})}{k}=\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma);\mathbf{m}).
In the general case,
we take an increasing sequence Γ1⊂Γ2⊂⋯⊂Γ\Gamma^{1}\subset\Gamma^{2}\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma
such that each Γi\Gamma^{i} is a finitely generated birational subsemigroup of Γ\Gamma
and ⋃i≥1Γi=Γ\bigcup_{i\geq 1}\Gamma^{i}=\Gamma.
Then it is easy to show
⋃iΔ​(Γi)∘=Δ​(Γ)∘\bigcup_{i}\Delta(\Gamma^{i})^{\circ}=\Delta(\Gamma)^{\circ}.
By Lemmas 4.5 and 4.7, we have
ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γ),𝐦)=ε⁡(Δ​(Γ)∘,𝐦)=limε⁡(Δ​(Γi)∘,𝐦)=limε⁡(Δ⁡(Γi),𝐦).\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma);\mathbf{m})=\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma)^{\circ};\mathbf{m})=\lim\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma^{i})^{\circ};\mathbf{m})=\lim\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma^{i});\mathbf{m}).
Since each Γi\Gamma^{i} is finitely generated,
we can apply the first part of the proof of this lemma.
Hence we have
ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γi),𝐦)=ε⁡(W​(Γi)∙,𝐦)≤ε⁡(W​(Γ)∙,𝐦).\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma^{i});\mathbf{m})=\varepsilon(W(\Gamma^{i})_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})\leq\varepsilon(W(\Gamma)_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}).
Thus we obtain ε⁡(Δ⁡(Γ),𝐦)=limε⁡(Δ⁡(Γi),𝐦)≤ε⁡(W​(Γ)∙,𝐦)\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma);\mathbf{m})=\lim\varepsilon(\Delta(\Gamma^{i});\mathbf{m})\leq\varepsilon(W(\Gamma)_{\bullet};\mathbf{m}).
∎
Broadly speaking, the geometrical meaning of Lemma 5.6
is that Seshadri constants of ample line bundles (on non-normal toric varieties) at very general points do not change by normalizations.
In fact,
when Γ\Gamma is finitely generated,
(Proj⨁kWΔ⁡(Γ),k,𝒪(1))=(Projℂ[Σ(Γ)∩(ℕ×ℕn)],𝒪(1))(\Proj\bigoplus_{k}W_{\Delta(\Gamma),k},\mathcal{O}(1))=(\Proj\mathbb{C}[\Sigma(\Gamma)\cap(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}^{n})],\mathcal{O}(1))
is nothing but the normalization of the toric variety (Proj⨁kW(Γ)k,𝒪(1))=(Projℂ[Γ],𝒪(1))(\Proj\bigoplus_{k}W(\Gamma)_{k},\mathcal{O}(1))=(\Proj\mathbb{C}[\Gamma],\mathcal{O}(1)).
Now we show the key proposition by using degeneration of global sections as [CM].
Roughly speaking,
this proposition states that W⊂H0​(X,L)W\subset H^{0}(X,L) generically separates no less jets than Vν⁡(W)V_{\nu(W)}.

Now we can show the main theorem easily.
Theorem 5.8 (=Theorem 1.3).
Let W∙W_{\bullet} be a birational graded linear series associated to a line bundle LL
on an nn-dimensional variety XX.
Fix a local coordinate system z=(z1,…,zn)z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}) on XX at a smooth point
and a monomial order >> on ℕn\mathbb{N}^{n}.
Then ε⁡(W∙,𝐦)≥ε⁡(Δz,>​(W∙),𝐦)\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})\geq\varepsilon(\Delta_{z,>}(W_{\bullet});\mathbf{m}) holds for any r∈ℤ+r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+} and 𝐦∈ℝ+r\mathbf{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}.

Then we have Γk=ν⁡(Wk)⊂ℕn\Gamma_{k}=\nu(W_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}^{n} and Δ⁡(Γ)=Δz,>​(W∙)\Delta(\Gamma)=\Delta_{z,>}(W_{\bullet}) by definition.

it holds that
ε⁡(W∙,𝐦)=lims⁡(Wk,𝐦)k≥lims⁡(Vν⁡(Wk),𝐦)k=lims⁡(VΓk,𝐦)k.\varepsilon(W_{\bullet};\mathbf{m})=\lim\dfrac{s(W_{k};\mathbf{m})}{k}\geq\lim\dfrac{s(V_{\nu(W_{k})};\mathbf{m})}{k}=\lim\dfrac{s(V_{\Gamma_{k}};\mathbf{m})}{k}.

we have

Remark 5.9.
The inequality in Theorem 5.8 is not equality in general.
Let LL be an ample line bundle
on a projective variety XX.
Let zz be a local coordinate system at pp
and EE the prime divisor on XX defined by the first coordinate z1z_{1} of zz around pp.
If >> is the lexicographic order,
Δz,>​(L)\Delta_{z,>}(L) is contained in [0,a]×ℝdimX−1[0,a]\times\mathbb{R}^{\dim X-1},
where a=sup{t>0|L−t​E​ is effective}a=\sup\{\,t>0\,|\,L-tE\text{ is effective}\} (cf. [LM]).
Hence ε⁡(Δz,>​(L),1)≤a\varepsilon(\Delta_{z,>}(L);1)\leq a holds (cf. [It, Theorem 3.6]).
If we choose zz so that E∈|m​L|E\in|mL| for m≫0m\gg 0,
we have ε⁡(Δz,>​(L),1)≤a=1/m<ε⁡(L,1)\varepsilon(\Delta_{z,>}(L);1)\leq a=1/m<\varepsilon(L;1).
