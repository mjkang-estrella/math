Abstract.
Let (X,B)(X,B) be an ϵ\epsilon-lc pair of dimension dd with a closed point x∈Xx\in X. Birkar and Shokurov conjectured that there is an effective Cartier divisor HH passing through xx such that (X,B+t​H)(X,B+tH) is lc near xx, where tt is a positive real number depending only on d,ϵd,\epsilon. We prove that this conjecture is equivalent to Shokurov’s conjecture on boundedness of klt complements on Fano fibrations and we confirm it in dimension 2. As a corollary, we prove the boundedness of klt complements on Fano fibrations over surfaces.
1. Introduction
We work over the field of complex numbers ℂ\mathbb{C}. All varieties are assumed to be quasi-projective.
The theory of complements was introduced by Shokurov when he investigated log flips on threefolds [Sh92], which
turns out to be a very powerful tool in birational geometry. It has played an important role in several breakthroughs in birational geometry, for example, the proof of the Borisov-Alexeev-Borisov conjecture (the boundedness of Fano varieties) [Bi19, Bi21] and the openness of K-semistability in families of log Fano pairs [Xu20, BLX22]. See [Sh00, Sh04, Liu18, HLS19, Sh20, Bi22, HL22, HLL22, CHL23] for more applications.
Shokurov [Sh00, Sh04] proposed conjectures on the boundedness of lc (resp. klt) complements, i.e. the existence of lc (resp. klt) nn-complements for some bounded natural number nn. The boundedness of lc complements was proved by Birkar [Bi19] for pairs on Fano type fibrations with boundary coefficients in a hyperstandard set, which was later generalized to more general boundary coefficients [HLS19, Sh20]. On the other hand, the conjecture on the boundedness of klt complements is still wide open except some partial results (see Remark 1.2 below).
We now state precisely Shokurov’s conjecture on the boundedness of klt complements for the case when boundary coefficients belong to a finite set of rational numbers.
Conjecture 1.1 (Shokurov).
Let dd be a natural number, ϵ\epsilon be a positive real number and ℜ⊂[0,1]\mathfrak{R}\subset[0,1] be a finite set of rational numbers. Then there is a natural number nn depending only on d,ϵ,ℜd,\epsilon,\mathfrak{R} satisfying the following. Assume (X,B)(X,B) is a pair, X→ZX\to Z is a contraction and z∈Zz\in Z is a closed point such that
•
(X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc of dimension dd,
•
the coefficients of BB are in ℜ\mathfrak{R},
•
XX is of Fano type over ZZ, and
•
−(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) is nef over ZZ.
Then KX+BK_{X}+B has a monotonic klt nn-complement over zz. That is, there is a ℚ\mathbb{Q}-divisor Λ\Lambda on XX such that over some neighbourhood of zz we have
•
Λ≥B\Lambda\geq B,
•
(X,Λ)(X,\Lambda) is klt, and
•
n⁡(KX+Λ)∼0/Zn(K_{X}+\Lambda)\sim 0/Z.
Remark 1.2
(1) It is expected that KX+BK_{X}+B has not just a klt nn-complement but also an ϵ\epsilon-lc nn-complement, even for more general boundary coefficients. See [Sh20, Conjecture 5] and [CH21, Conjecture 1.1] for stronger versions of Conjecture 1.1.
(2) The condition in Conjecture 1.1 that
•
(X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc and −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) is nef over ZZ,
can be replaced by another equivalent condition that
•
KX+BK_{X}+B has an ϵ\epsilon-lc ℝ\mathbb{R}-complement over ZZ, that is, there is an ϵ\epsilon-lc pair (X,B+)(X,B^{+}) such that B+≥BB^{+}\geq B and KX+B+∼ℝ0/ZK_{X}+B^{+}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0/Z.
Indeed, if the former holds, as XX is of Fano type over ZZ, −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) is semiample over ZZ and hence the latter follows; if the latter holds, we can run an MMP on −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) over ZZ to make −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) nef over ZZ and in this process the ϵ\epsilon-lc property of (X,B)(X,B) will be preserved because of the existence of ϵ\epsilon-lc ℝ\mathbb{R}-complement.
(3) When dimZ=0\dim Z=0, Conjecture 1.1 is equivalent to the Borisov-Alexeev-Borisov conjecture [Bi19, Bi21].
(4) When dimZ=1\dim Z=1, Conjecture 1.1 was confirmed by Birkar [Bi23, Corollary 1.4].
(5) In the toric case, Conjecture 1.1 was proved by Ambro [Am22, Theorem 1.2].
(6) When dimX=2\dim X=2, an affirmative answer of Conjecture 1.1 was given in [Bi04, Theorem 1.6] and in [CH21, Theorem 1.1].
(7) When X=ZX=Z, dimX=3\dim X=3, ϵ≥1\epsilon\geq 1 and XX is terminal, Conjecture 1.1 was proved in [HLL22, Theorem 1.4].
(8) When X=ZX=Z and (X∋z,B)(X\ni z,B) has a δ\delta-plt blow-up (Definition 2.26) for some fixed δ>0\delta>0, Conjecture 1.1 follows from [HLS19, Theorem 1.6]. In particular, Conjecture 1.1 holds when X=ZX=Z and (X∋z,B)(X\ni z,B) is an exceptional singularity (see Definition 2.28).
(9) When (X,Γ)→Z(X,\Gamma)\to Z is a (d,r,ϵ)(d,r,\epsilon)-Fano type fibration (see [Bi22, Definition 1.1]) and B≤ΓB\leq\Gamma with −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) being big over ZZ, Conjecture 1.1 was proved in [Bi22, Theorem 1.7]. Roughly speaking, Conjecture 1.1 holds when ZZ belongs to a bounded family and the “degree” of KX+BK_{X}+B is in some sense bounded.
(10) When (X,B)→Z(X,B)\to Z is a semi-stable morphism ([FM18, 2.7]) and m​KZmK_{Z} is Cartier for some fixed mm, Conjecture 1.1 was proved in [FM18, Theorem 1.4].
To attack the above conjecture, Birkar and Shokurov proposed the following two conjectures. Birkar pointed out to me that these two conjectures are equivalent and can imply Conjecture 1.1.
Conjecture 1.3 (Birkar-Shokurov).
Let dd be a natural number and ϵ\epsilon be a positive real number. Then there is a positive real number tt depending only on d,ϵd,\epsilon satisfying the following. Assume (X,B)(X,B) is a pair, f:X→Zf:X\to Z is a contraction and z∈Zz\in Z is a closed point such that
•
(X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc of dimension dd and dimZ>0\dim Z>0,
•
XX is of Fano type over ZZ, and
•
−(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) is nef over ZZ.
Then there exists an effective Cartier divisor HH on some neighbourhood UU of z∈Zz\in Z such that z∈Supp⁡Hz\in\Supp H and (X,B+t​f∗​H)(X,B+tf^{*}H) is lc over UU.
Conjecture 1.4 (Birkar-Shokurov).
Let ss be a natural number and ϵ\epsilon be a positive real number. Then there is a positive real number tt depending only on s,ϵs,\epsilon satisfying the following. Assume (Z,Δ)(Z,\Delta) is an ϵ\epsilon-lc pair of dimension ss with a closed point z∈Zz\in Z. Then there exists an effective Cartier divisor HH on some neighbourhood of z∈Zz\in Z such that z∈Supp⁡Hz\in\Supp H and (Z,Δ+t​H)(Z,\Delta+tH) is lc near zz.
Remark 1.5
(1) Conjecture 1.4 is a special case of Conjecture 1.3 when X=ZX=Z.
(2) As in Remark 1.2(2), in Conjecture 1.3 the condition that (X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc and −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) is nef over ZZ can be replaced by another equivalent condition that KX+BK_{X}+B has an ϵ\epsilon-lc ℝ\mathbb{R}-complement over ZZ.
(3) At a meeting of the COW seminar at City, University of London on 7th February 2018, Birkar raised Conjecture 1.3 in the special case when Z=𝔸nZ=\mathbb{A}^{n}, X→ZX\to Z is a weighted blow up at the origin and B=0B=0 (see [SS21, Conjecture 1.2]). It was proved by G. Sankaran and F. Santos in [SS21, Theorem 1.3] that Conjecture 1.3 holds in this case.
(4) Ambro had independently considered a version of these conjectures in private work. He [Am22, Theorem 3.12] proved Conjecture 1.3 in the toric setting with an additional condition that the coefficients of BB belong to a fixed DCC set.
(5) When dimZ=1\dim Z=1, Conjecture 1.3 was proved by Birkar in [Bi23, Theorem 1.1]. See [Bi16, Theorem 1.4] for another proof of Conjecture 1.3 in the special case when dimZ=1\dim Z=1, KX+B∼ℝ0/ZK_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0/Z and (F,Supp⁡B|F)(F,\Supp B|_{F}) is log bounded where FF is a general fiber of ff.
Relations among the above conjectures. Our first main result is on the relations among Conjecture 1.1, 1.3 and 1.4.
Theorem 1.6.
Let ℓ\ell be a natural number. The following are equivalent:
(1)
Conjecture 1.1 holds when dimZ≤ℓ\dim Z\leq\ell;
(2)
Conjecture 1.1 holds when X→ZX\to Z is birational, B=0B=0 and dimZ≤ℓ\dim Z\leq\ell.
(3)
Conjecture 1.3 holds when dimZ≤ℓ\dim Z\leq\ell.
(4)
Conjecture 1.3 holds when X→ZX\to Z is birational, B=0B=0 and dimZ≤ℓ\dim Z\leq\ell.
(5)
Conjecture 1.4 holds when dimZ≤ℓ\dim Z\leq\ell.
It is worth to mention that the proof of the above theorem relies on [Bi23, Theorem 1.1], which relates the singularities on the base space of a Fano type fibration with the singularities on the total space.
The surface case. We confirm Conjecture 1.4 in dimension 2. As a corollary, we prove the boundedness of klt complements on Fano fibrations over surfaces.
Theorem 1.7.
Let (Z,Δ)(Z,\Delta) be a pair of dimension 2 with a closed point z∈Zz\in Z such that mld⁡(Z∋z,Δ)≥ϵ\mld(Z\ni z,\Delta)\geq\epsilon with 0<ϵ≤10<\epsilon\leq 1. Then there exists an effective Cartier divisor HH on some neighbourhood of z∈Zz\in Z such that z∈Supp⁡Hz\in\Supp H and (Z,Δ+t​H)(Z,\Delta+tH) is lc near zz, where t=ϵ2/24t=\epsilon^{2}/24.
Here we denote by mld⁡(Z∋z,Δ)\mld(Z\ni z,\Delta) the infimum of the log discrepancy of EE with respect to (Z,Δ)(Z,\Delta) where EE runs over all prime divisors over ZZ with centerZ⁡E=z\cent_{Z}E=z. Note that the condition mld⁡(Z∋z,Δ)≥ϵ\mld(Z\ni z,\Delta)\geq\epsilon is weaker than that (Z,Δ)(Z,\Delta) is ϵ\epsilon-lc near zz.
Corollary 1.8.
Conjecture 1.1, 1.3 and 1.4 hold when dimZ≤2\dim Z\leq 2.
Remark 1.9
The following example indicates that the order O⁡(ϵ2)O(\epsilon^{2}) in Theorem 1.7 is optimal.
Example 1.10
Let Z=𝔸2Z=\mathbb{A}^{2} with coordinates x,yx,y and let z∈Zz\in Z be the origin. Let
Δ=2​m−1m2⋅(xm+ym+1=0)\Delta=\frac{2m-1}{m^{2}}\cdot(x^{m}+y^{m+1}=0)
where m∈ℕm\in\mathbb{N}.
By [Ch22, Lemma A.1] we have
mld⁡(Z∋z,Δ)=inf{p1+p2−min⁡{λ​m​p1,λ⁡(m+1)​p2}∣(p1,p2)∈ℕ2}\mld(Z\ni z,\Delta)=\inf\{p_{1}+p_{2}-\min\{\lambda mp_{1},\lambda(m+1)p_{2}\}\mid(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\}
where λ=2​m−1m2\lambda=\frac{2m-1}{m^{2}}. It is not hard to check that the above expression is equal to 1m\frac{1}{m}. So we have
mld⁡(Z∋z,Δ)=1m.\mld(Z\ni z,\Delta)=\frac{1}{m}.
Let EE be the exceptional divisor obtained by the weighted blow up at zz with wt​(x,y)=(m+1,m)\textbf{wt}(x,y)=(m+1,m). Then
a⁡(E,Z,Δ)=1ma(E,Z,\Delta)=\frac{1}{m}. Moreover, the vanishing order of the pullback of xx (resp. yy) along EE is m+1m+1 (resp. mm). So for any curve HH on ZZ passing through zz, (Z,Δ+t​H)(Z,\Delta+tH) is not lc near zz when t>1m2t>\frac{1}{m^{2}}.
The toric case. As mentioned in Remark 1.5, in the toric case Conjecture 1.3 was proved by Ambro [Am22, Theorem 3.12] with an additional condition that the coefficients of BB belong to a fixed DCC set. In the following theorem we remove this additional condition.
Theorem 1.11.
Conjecture 1.3 holds when f:X→Zf:X\to Z is a toric contraction between toric varieties, (X,B)(X,B) is a toric pair and z∈Zz\in Z is a torus invariant closed point.
Corollary 1.12.
Conjecture 1.4 holds when (Z,Δ)(Z,\Delta) is a toric pair and z∈Zz\in Z is a torus invariant closed point.
Remark 1.13
(1) See Theorem 8.3 for a stronger version of Theorem 1.11, which asserts that Conjecture 1.3 holds in the toric case, even when we replace the condition that (X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc by a weaker condition that mld(X/Z∋z,B)≥ϵ\mld(X/Z\ni z,B)\geq\epsilon, that is, a⁡(E,X,B)≥ϵa(E,X,B)\geq\epsilon for any prime divisor EE over XX with f⁡(centerX⁡E)=zf(\cent_{X}E)=z.
(2) After this paper was completed, Ambro informed me that he
had proved Theorem 1.11 with an effective estimate for tt (under the weaker condition that mld(X/Z∋z,B)≥ϵ\mld(X/Z\ni z,B)\geq\epsilon).
The case for exceptional singularities.
We confirm Conjecture 1.4 for singularities admitting δ\delta-plt blow-ups (Definition 2.26) for some fixed δ>0\delta>0. As a corollary, we prove the boundedness of klt complements over an exceptional singularity (Definition 2.28) Z∋zZ\ni z of dimension 3.
Theorem 1.14.
Fix a positive real number δ\delta. Conjecture 1.4 holds when (Z∋z,Δ)(Z\ni z,\Delta) has a δ\delta-plt blow-up.
Theorem 1.15.
Fix a DCC set 𝔇⊂[0,1]\mathfrak{D}\subset[0,1]. Conjecture 1.4 holds when there is a boundary Ω\Omega on ZZ such that
•
Ω≤Δ\Omega\leq\Delta,
•
(Z∋z,Ω)(Z\ni z,\Omega) is an exceptional singularity, and
•
the coefficients of Ω\Omega are in 𝔇\mathfrak{D}.
Corollary 1.16.
Conjecture 1.3 and 1.4 hold when Z∋zZ\ni z is an exceptional singularity.
Corollary 1.17.
Conjecture 1.1 holds when Z∋zZ\ni z is an exceptional singularity and dimZ=3\dim Z=3.

2. Preliminaries
We will freely use the standard notations and definitions in [KM98, BCHM]. A contraction f:X→Zf:X\rightarrow Z is a projective morphism of normal varieties with f∗​𝒪X=𝒪Zf_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}.
2.1. Divisors and ideals
Let XX be a normal variety, DD be an ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor on XX and 𝔞\mathfrak{a} be a non-zero ideal sheaf on XX. For a prime divisor TT on XX, we denote by μT​D\mu_{T}D the coefficient of TT in DD and denote by μT​𝔞\mu_{T}\mathfrak{a} the smallest vanishing order of ss along TT, where ss runs over all sections of 𝔞\mathfrak{a} at the generic point of TT. For a prime divisor TT over XX, i.e. a prime divisor on some birational model π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X, by μT​𝔞\mu_{T}\mathfrak{a} we mean μT​π∗​𝔞\mu_{T}\pi^{*}\mathfrak{a} and by μT​D\mu_{T}D we mean μT​π∗​D\mu_{T}\pi^{*}D in the case when DD is an ℝ\mathbb{R}-Cartier ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor on XX.
2.2. Pairs and singularities
Definition 2.1
A sub-pair (X,B)(X,B) consists of a normal variety XX and an ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor BB on XX such that KX+BK_{X}+B is ℝ\mathbb{R}-Cartier. A sub-pair (X,B)(X,B) is called a pair if BB is effective. If (X,B)(X,B) is a pair and the coefficients of BB are at most 11, we call BB a boundary on XX.
Definition 2.2
Let (X,B)(X,B) be a sub-pair and EE be a prime divisor on some birational model π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X. Denote by centerX⁡E\cent_{X}E the center of EE on XX, i.e. the image of EE on XX under the morphism π\pi.
We may write KY+BY=π∗​(KX+B)K_{Y}+B_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B) for some uniquely determined BYB_{Y}. Then the log discrepancy a⁡(E,X,B)a(E,X,B) of EE with respect to (X,B)(X,B) is defined as 1−μE​BY1-\mu_{E}B_{Y}.
Definition 2.3
Let ϵ\epsilon be a non-negative real number.
A sub-pair (X,B)(X,B) is said to be ϵ\epsilon-lc (resp. ϵ\epsilon-klt, lc, klt) if a⁡(E,X,B)≥ϵa(E,X,B)\geq\epsilon (resp. >ϵ>\epsilon, ≥0\geq 0, >0>0) for any prime divisor EE over XX. In the case when B=0B=0, we also say XX is ϵ\epsilon-lc (resp. ϵ\epsilon-klt, lc, klt). A pair (X,B)(X,B) is said to be plt (reps. ϵ\epsilon-plt) if a⁡(E,X,B)>0a(E,X,B)>0 (resp. >ϵ>\epsilon) for any exceptional prime divisor EE over XX.
Definition 2.4
A germ (X∋x,B)(X\ni x,B) consists of a pair (X,B)(X,B) and a closed point x∈Xx\in X. We say (X∋x,B)(X\ni x,B) is an ϵ\epsilon-lc (resp. ϵ\epsilon-klt, lc, klt) germ if (X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc (resp. ϵ\epsilon-klt, lc, klt) near xx. When B=0B=0, we use X∋xX\ni x instead of (X∋x,0)(X\ni x,0).
Definition 2.5
Let (X,B)(X,B) be a sub-pair, f:X→Zf:X\to Z be a projective morphism and z∈Zz\in Z be a (not necessarily closed) point. We say (X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc (or ϵ\epsilon-klt, lc, klt) over zz if (X,B)(X,B) is so over some neighbourhood of zz. We call EE a prime divisor over X/Z∋zX/Z\ni z if EE is a prime divisor over XX with f⁡(centerX⁡E)=z¯f(\cent_{X}E)=\overline{z} and we denote
mld(X/Z∋z,B):=inf{a(E,X,B)∣\displaystyle\mld(X/Z\ni z,B):=\inf\{a(E,X,B)\mid~
E is a prime divisor over X/Z∋z}.\displaystyle\text{$E$ is a prime divisor over $X/Z\ni z$}\}.
In the case when Z=XZ=X, z=xz=x and ff is the identity morphism, we call EE a prime divisor over X∋xX\ni x if EE is a prime divisor over XX with centerX⁡E=x¯\cent_{X}E=\overline{x} and we use mld⁡(X∋x,B)\mld(X\ni x,B) instead of mld(X/Z∋z,B)\mld(X/Z\ni z,B).
We remark that mld(X/Z∋z,B)≥0\mld(X/Z\ni z,B)\geq 0 if and only if (X,B)(X,B) is lc over zz (see [HJL22, Lemma 2.8]). However, mld(X/Z∋z,B)≥ϵ\mld(X/Z\ni z,B)\geq\epsilon is weaker than (X,B)(X,B) is ϵ\epsilon-lc over zz.
2.3. b-divisors
Let XX be a normal variety. A b-divisor D over XX is a collection of ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisors DY\textbf{D}_{Y} on YY, where YY runs over all birational models over XX, such that σ∗​DY1=DY2\sigma_{*}\textbf{D}_{Y_{1}}=\textbf{D}_{Y_{2}} for any birational morphism σ:Y1→Y2/X\sigma:Y_{1}\rightarrow Y_{2}/X.
Let 𝐃{\bf{D}} be a b-divisor over XX and Y0Y_{0} be a birational model over XX. We say 𝐃{\bf{D}} descends on Y0Y_{0} if DY0\textbf{D}_{Y_{0}} is an ℝ\mathbb{R}-Cartier ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor and DY=σ∗​DY0\textbf{D}_{Y}=\sigma^{*}\textbf{D}_{Y_{0}} for any birational morphism σ:Y→Y0/X\sigma:Y\rightarrow Y_{0}/X.
Let X→UX\rightarrow U be a projective morphism. We say that a b-divisor D over XX is b-nef/U/U if 𝐃{\bf{D}} descends on some birational model Y0Y_{0} over XX and DY0\textbf{D}_{Y_{0}} is nef/U/U.
We denote by 0 the b-divisor 𝐃{\bf{D}} such that DY=0\textbf{D}_{Y}=0 for every birational model YY over XX.
2.4. Generalized pairs
Definition 2.6
A generalized sub-pair (g-sub-pair for short) (X,B+𝐌)/U(X,B+{\bf{M}})/U consists of a normal variety XX associated with a projective morphism X→UX\rightarrow U, an ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor BB on XX, and a b-nef/U/U b-divisor 𝐌{\bf{M}} over XX.
A g-sub-pair (X,B+𝐌)/U(X,B+{\bf{M}})/U is called a generalized pair (g-pair for short) if BB is effective.
We may drop UU when we emphasize the structures of (X,B+𝐌)(X,B+{\bf{M}}) that are independent of the choice of UU, for example, the singularities of (X,B+𝐌)(X,B+{\bf{M}}).
Definition 2.7
Let (X,B+𝐌)(X,B+{\bf{M}}) be a g-sub-pair and EE be a prime divisor on some birational model π:Y→X\pi:Y\to X. We can write
KY+BY+𝐌Y=π∗​(KX+B+𝐌X)K_{Y}+B_{Y}+{\bf{M}}_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+B+{\bf{M}}_{X})
for some uniquely determined BYB_{Y}. Then the log discrepancy of EE with respect to (X,B+𝐌)(X,B+{\bf{M}}) is defined as 1−μE​BY1-\mu_{E}B_{Y} and denoted by a⁡(E,X,B+𝐌)a(E,X,B+{\bf{M}}).
Definition 2.8
Let ϵ\epsilon be a non-negative real number.
A g-sub-pair (X,B+𝐌)(X,B+{\bf{M}}) is said to be ϵ\epsilon-lc (resp. ϵ\epsilon-klt, lc, klt) if a⁡(E,X,B+𝐌)≥ϵa(E,X,B+{\bf{M}})\geq\epsilon (resp. >ϵ>\epsilon, ≥0\geq 0, >0>0) for any prime divisor EE over XX.
In the case that 𝐌=0{\bf{M}}=\textbf{0}, all definitions of g-pairs coincide with those of usual pairs.
2.5. Canonical bundle formula
Let f:X→Zf:X\rightarrow Z be a contraction between normal varieties with dimZ>0\dim Z>0 and let Z→UZ\to U be a projective morphism. Let (X,B)(X,B) be a pair such that KX+B∼ℝ0/ZK_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0/Z and (X,B)(X,B) is lc over the generic point of ZZ. Then KX+B∼ℝf∗LK_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}f^{*}L for some ℝ\mathbb{R}-Cartier ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor LL on ZZ.
For any prime divisor DD on ZZ, denote by tDt_{D} the largest number such that (X,B+tD​f∗​D)(X,B+t_{D}f^{*}D) is lc over the generic point of DD. This makes sense even if DD is not ℚ\mathbb{Q}-Cartier because we only need the pullback of DD over the generic point of DD where ZZ is smooth. We set BZ=∑D(1−tD)​DB_{Z}=\sum_{D}(1-t_{D})D where DD runs over all prime divisors on ZZ
and set MZ=L−KZ−BZM_{Z}=L-K_{Z}-B_{Z}. The former is called the discriminant divisor and the latter is called the moduli divisor. By construction, we have the so called canonical bundle formula
KX+B∼ℝf∗(KZ+BZ+MZ).K_{X}+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}f^{*}(K_{Z}+B_{Z}+M_{Z}).
Let σ:Z′→Z\sigma:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z be a birational morphism from a normal variety Z′Z^{\prime} and let X′X^{\prime} be a resolution of the main component of X×ZZ′X\times_{Z}Z^{\prime} with induced morphisms τ:X′→X\tau:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X and f′:X′→Z′f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}. Similarly we can define the discriminant divisor BZ′B_{Z^{\prime}} and the moduli divisor MZ′M_{Z^{\prime}} for the contraction (X′,B′)→Z′(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\rightarrow Z^{\prime}. One can check that σ∗​BZ′=BZ\sigma_{*}B_{Z^{\prime}}=B_{Z} and

Hence there exist b-divisors 𝐁,𝐌{\bf{B}},{\bf{M}} over ZZ such that 𝐁Z′=BZ′{\bf{B}}_{Z^{\prime}}=B_{Z^{\prime}} and 𝐌Z′=MZ′{\bf{M}}_{Z^{\prime}}=M_{Z^{\prime}} for any birational model Z′Z^{\prime} over ZZ, which are called the discriminant b-divisor and the moduli b-divisor respectively.
It was shown by Kawamata [Ka97, Ka98] and Ambro [Am04, Am05] that 𝐌{\bf{M}} is a b-nef/U/U b-divisor over ZZ (see [Hu21, Section 3] for ℝ\mathbb{R}-coefficients). Lemma 2.9.
Keep the notation and assumptions as above. If (Z,BZ+𝐌)(Z,B_{Z}+{\bf{M}}) is an lc g-pair, then (X,B)(X,B) is lc.
Proof.
Suppose that (X,B)(X,B) is not lc. Since (X,B)(X,B) is lc over the generic point of ZZ by assumption, there is a prime divisor SS over XX such that a⁡(S,X,B)<0a(S,X,B)<0 and SS is vertical over ZZ. 2.6. Fano type fibrations
Let X→ZX\to Z be a contraction between normal varieties. We say XX is of Fano type over ZZ if one of the following three equivalent conditions holds ([PS09, Lemma-Definition 2.6]):
•
there is a klt pair (X,C)(X,C) such that −(KX+C)-(K_{X}+C) is ample over ZZ,
•
there is a klt pair (X,C)(X,C) such that −(KX+C)-(K_{X}+C) is big and nef over ZZ, and
•
−KX-K_{X} is big over ZZ and there is a klt pair (X,C)(X,C) such that KX+C∼ℝ0/ZK_{X}+C\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0/Z.
Assume XX is of Fano type over ZZ and DD is an ℝ\mathbb{R}-Cartier ℝ\mathbb{R}-divisor on XX. By the base point free theorem, if DD is nef over ZZ, then DD is semi-ample over ZZ. In particular, if D≡0/ZD\equiv 0/Z, then D∼ℝ0/ZD\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0/Z. By [BCHM], one can run an MMP on DD over ZZ and the MMP ends with a good minimal model or a Mori fiber space over ZZ.

(3) Let (X,B)(X,B) be an lc pair such that −(KX+B)-(K_{X}+B) is nef over ZZ. 2.10. Toric varieties
A toric variety is a normal variety XX of dimension dd that contains a torus TXT_{X} (that is, isomorphic to (ℂ∗)d(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{d}) as a dense open subset, together with an action TX×X→XT_{X}\times X\to X of TXT_{X} on XX that extends the natural action of TXT_{X} on itself.
A toric morphism is a morphism f:X→Yf:X\to Y between toric varieties such that ff maps the torus TX⊂XT_{X}\subset X into TY⊂YT_{Y}\subset Y and f|TXf|_{T_{X}} is a group homomorphism.
A toric contraction is a contraction f:X→Yf:X\to Y so that ff is a toric morphism. We remark that if f:X→Yf:X\to Y is a toric contraction, then XX is of Fano type over YY.
A toric pair is a pair (X,B)(X,B) so that XX is a toric variety and BB is invariant under the torus action of TXT_{X}.
We refer to [Fu93] or [CLS11] for the general theory of toric varieties.

The goal of this section is to prove Conjecture 1.4 in dimension 2. We begin with some properties of klt germs.
