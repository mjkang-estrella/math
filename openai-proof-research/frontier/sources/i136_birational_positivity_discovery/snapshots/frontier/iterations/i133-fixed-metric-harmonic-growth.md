For \(r\ge2\), \(f_\infty=ar+b_\infty\), \(b_\infty=(1-a)\int_1^2s\beta(s)ds>0\). This limit is smooth, complete, Euclidean on \(B_1\), Ricci nonnegative (in fact sectional nonnegative), and AVR \(a^2\), directly from concavity, \(a\le f'\le1\), and the volume formula.

### 4.2 Explicit uniform AVR tail

For \(r\ge2\), the compact slope drop is completed, so \(f_n'(r)\le a+\delta_n\). Thus

\[
ar\le f_n(r)\le(a+\delta_n)r+2,\qquad f_n(r)\le r.
\]

For \(R\ge2\), \(V_n(R)=3R^{-3}\int_0^Rf_n^2dr\), and direct integration, with \(a+\delta_n\le1\), yields

\[
0\le V_n(R)-a^2\le2\delta_n+\delta_n^2+\frac6R+\frac{12}{R^2}+\frac8{R^3}. \tag{II.4}
\]

The last term bounds the inner \(r\le2\) contribution. For \(n\ge N\), replace the first two terms by \(2^{1-N}+2^{-2N}\). Each of the finitely many \(n<N\) has \(V_n(R)\to a^2\). ### 4.3 Radial comparison forces growth constants to diverge

Suppose \(g=dr^2+f^2g_{S^2}\) for \(r<T\), \(f=r\) on \(B_1\), \(0<f\le r\), \(0<f'\le1\). The regular radial harmonic solution \(R_\ell=r^\ell\) on the core satisfies

\[
(f^2R_\ell')'=\ell(\ell+1)R_\ell.
\]

For \(\ell\ge1\), positivity and increasingness continue, since \(f^2R_\ell'\) increases while \(R_\ell>0\); no first zero or first loss of positive derivative is possible. With \(\mathcal L_\ell y=(f^2y')'-\ell(\ell+1)y\), calculation gives

\[
\mathcal L_\ell(r^\ell)=\ell r^{\ell-2}
[(\ell-1)f^2+2rff'-(\ell+1)r^2]\le0.
\]

The Wronskian \(W=f^2(R_\ell'r^\ell-R_\ell\ell r^{\ell-1})\) starts at zero and satisfies \(W'=-R_\ell\mathcal L_\ell(r^\ell)\ge0\). Hence

\[
R_\ell(r)\ge r^\ell\qquad(1\le r<T). \tag{II.5}
\]

For any \(d>(k+1)^2\) dimensional \(W\subset\mathcal H_k(g_n)\), restriction to the common core is injective by unique continuation. Its Taylor projection through degree \(k\) has a nonzero kernel. Separation on the whole round warped region \(r<T_n\) expands that vector as \(\sum_{\ell\ge k+1}R_\ell(r)Y_\ell\). \[
C_{\rm basis}\ge
\sqrt{\frac{2k+5}{4\pi d}}\frac{T_n}{2^{k+1}}. \tag{II.6}

Use \(r=T_n/2\), strictly within the round region. Fix \(k=k_j\), \(d=d_j=\lceil c(k_j+1)^2\rceil\), while \(n\ge j\to\infty\). Distance equals \(r=|x|\), so the bound concerns both coordinate and geometric growth. Neither changing basis nor selecting a different \(d_j\)-dimensional subspace evades it.

This proves that a common core and uniform volume tail, even with every other finite-stage geometric constraint, do not control normalized fixed-degree growth constants. It is stronger than dilation escape, obtained from complete stipulated operations, not a repaired original claim.

### 4.4 Direct exact count on the limit

For \(r\ge2\), put \(\rho=r+b_\infty/a\). The separated equation has powers \(\rho^{\alpha_\ell}\), \(\rho^{-1-\alpha_\ell}\), where

\[
\alpha_\ell=(\sqrt{1+4\ell(\ell+1)/a^2}-1)/2.
\]

For \(\ell\ge1\), the growing coefficient of the core-regular solution is positive: a negative coefficient eventually makes the solution negative; a zero coefficient makes it decrease, contradicting the positive increasing solution. The \(\ell=0\) regular solution is constant. Angular orthogonality and the pointwise bound force all coefficients with \(\alpha_\ell>k\) to vanish. Conversely every mode with \(\alpha_\ell\le k\) is smooth entire and has that growth. Thus

\[
h_k(g_\infty)=(L(k)+1)^2,\qquad
L(k)=\max\{\ell\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}:\ell(\ell+1)\le a^2k(k+1)\}. \tag{II.7}

Equivalently \(L(k)=\lfloor(\sqrt{1+4a^2k(k+1)}-1)/2\rfloor\); the inequality definition specifies exact thresholds. For integer \(k\ge1\), \(a<1\) implies \(L(k)\le k-1\). The limit loses all claimed violations. This is a direct ODE proof, independent of external comparison theorems.
