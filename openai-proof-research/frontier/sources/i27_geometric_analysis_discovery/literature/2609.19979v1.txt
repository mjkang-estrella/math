                                                                                            Abstract
                                                     We give an alternative proof of a theorem of Brena, De Lellis and Franceschini (arXiv:2503.00649):
                                                 the support of a stationary integral d dimensional varifold in an open subset of Rn is (H d , d) rec-
                                                 tifiable of class (k, α) for every positive integer k and every 0 < α < 1, and in particular of
                                                 class C ∞ . Our proof is independent of theirs and proceeds by different means. It compares
                                                 the varifold, at every scale and at H d almost every point, with the graph of a solution of the
                                                 Euler–Lagrange system of a smoothed area integrand, and thereby derives an excess-decay
                                                 estimate that is faster than any power of the scale; the conclusion then follows from Santilli’s
                                                 characterisation of higher order rectifiability together with Whitney’s extension theorem.

1     Introduction
Let V be a d dimensional integral varifold in an open subset U of Rn that is stationary, i.e.,
δV = 0. By Allard’s theorem (see [All72]) the support of the weight measure ∥V ∥ carries
a d rectifiable structure. That this structure is as smooth as one may presently hope in the absence of
a regularity theory for spt ∥V ∥ itself, was established by Brena, De Lellis and Franceschini [BDLF25].
The purpose of the present paper is to give an alternative proof of their theorem, along a route
that is independent of theirs; we claim no other novelty, and we compare the two arguments in
detail below.

1.1 Theorem (see 10.5 and 10.8). Let U ⊆ Rn be open and let V ∈ IVd (U ) satisfy δV = 0. Then
spt ∥V ∥ is (H d , d) rectifiable of class (k, α) for every k ∈ P and 0 < α < 1; in particular it is
(H d , d) rectifiable of class C ∞ .

    Here rectifiability of class (k, α) (respectively C ∞ ) is understood in the measure-theoretic sense:
the carrier of ∥V ∥ is covered by a countable union of d dimensional submanifolds of class C k,α
(respectively C ∞ ). Equivalently, by the characterisation of Anzellotti–Serapioni and Santilli ([AS94]
and [San19, 1.2, 5.6]), at H d almost every point spt ∥V ∥ admits an approximate Taylor expansion
of order (k, α) (respectively of every finite order). No regularity of spt ∥V ∥ as a set is asserted or
used; the singular examples of stationary varifolds are not excluded, and whether the complement
of Allard’s regular set is H d null remains, after more than fifty years, open.
    The result is the natural endpoint of a sequence of successive improvements. Combining Allard’s
interior regularity with Almgren’s multiple valued functions, as observed by Brakke [Bra78] (the
attribution is made explicitly in [BDLF25, §1]), one obtains rectifiability of class (1, α) for every
α < 1; Menne [Men13] established rectifiability of class C 2 , which is optimal in the wider class
of varifolds of locally bounded first variation. The endpoint was reached by Brena, De Lellis and
Franceschini [BDLF25], whose priority we acknowledge. The present work, begun years before and
carried out in ignorance of theirs, arrives at the same conclusion by different means.

Method of proof
The proof rests on a single excess-decay estimate, iterated at H d almost every point. Fix a point
at which spt ∥V ∥ possesses an approximate tangent plane T . At scale s we measure the deviation
of V from a smooth comparison surface Ms by a height excess ζ(s), the scale-invariant quadratic
mean of dist(·, Ms ) against ∥V ∥ over a cylinder of radius s about the point. The comparison
surface Ms = graph vs is the graph over T of a C ∞ function vs solving the Euler–Lagrange system
LG (vs ) = 0 of a fixed smoothed area integrand G, fitted to V at the scale s. The main estimate 9.5
is a geometric decay

in which the factor 0 < κ < 1 may be prescribed arbitrarily small at the cost of enlarging
certain structural constants. Iteration (see 10.2) converts this into ζ(s) ≤ κ−1 (s/r)α ζ(r) with rate

α = 20  log2 (1/κ); hence, into decay faster than any prescribed power of the scale.

    The passage from decay to smoothness is then measure-theoretic. Telescoping the comparison
jets across dyadic scales and applying Chebyshev’s inequality (10.4) shows that decay of order
α ≥ k + 2 forces spt ∥V ∥ to be approximately differentiable of order (k, α′ ) at H d almost every
point; Santilli’s characterisation of higher order rectifiability [San19, 5.6] then yields rectifiability
of class (k, α). As k is arbitrary, one obtains rectifiability of every finite order (10.5). Finally, the
approximate Taylor expansions of all orders are available on one common set of full measure, so
Whitney’s C ∞ extension theorem upgrades the conclusion to C ∞ (10.7, 10.6 and 10.8), by an
argument we owe to Menne.
    The decay estimate occupies the bulk of the paper. Its proof rests on five ingredients: lower
bounds for the reach of a graph in terms of the second derivatives of the graph function (§3),
which is what makes the nearest point projection onto the comparison surface available on the
region where the estimates are run; a reverse-Caccioppoli, or tilt-excess, inequality relative to a
C 2 comparison manifold — “height controls the tilt” — worked out in §4 and §5; an QQ valued
Lipschitz approximation of V over T , with estimates for the bad set on which V is not well
approximated by a graph; interior Schauder estimates of every order for the system LG and
its linearisation, developed here in a self-contained way in the spirit of [Fed69, 5.2.15]; and the
construction of the comparison functions vs with the prescribed behaviour.

Comparison with the work of Brena, De Lellis and Franceschini
Our strategy and that of [BDLF25] agree in their broad architecture and diverge in most of the
technical execution. Since the two works were carried out independently, we describe the parallel
in some detail.
    The common ground is the following. Both proofs replace the linear building block of Allard’s
ε-regularity — the best approximating plane in the classical Allard–De Giorgi decay lemma —
by a best approximating smooth solution of a minimal-surface-type system, and both keep the
ratio of consecutive scales fixed while driving the decay factor (κ here, γ in [BDLF25]) to be
arbitrarily small; this is precisely what produces decay faster than any power, rather than at a
single Hölder rate. The idea of enlarging the class of comparison objects is not new: Savin, in
[Sav18], approximates by harmonic quadratic polynomials in place of linear maps and so passes
from C 1,α to C 2,α estimates for his notion of viscosity solution of the minimal surface system; both
works considered here push the device to C ∞ . Both derive the requisite energy estimate from a
Caccioppoli-type tilt-excess inequality for V regarded as a generalised graph over the comparison
surface, both control the higher multiplicity Q > 1 and the possible “holes” by an Almgren-style
QQ valued Lipschitz approximation together with a density-deficit term — our bad-set estimates,
their parameter η, which is vacuous when Q = 1 — and both conclude by a Whitney-type patching
that exploits the mutual closeness, in every C k norm, of the comparison surfaces attached to
nearby scales.
    The differences are substantial. First, the comparison surface and the parametrisation of V .
In [BDLF25] the reference objects are genuine classical minimal surfaces M , and V is written as a
QQ valued graph over the normal bundle of the curved surface M ; the decay lemma updates M to
a new minimal surface at the finer scale, and the analysis is intrinsic to the moving curved surface,
governed by the distance function d2M and its elliptic inequality ∆L 21 d2M + δ 2 d2M ≥ 14 |L − Tp(z) M |2 .
In the present paper the comparison surfaces Ms = graph vs are, throughout, graphs over the
fixed approximate tangent plane T ; there is neither a normal-bundle parametrisation nor a moving
frame, and the curvature of the comparison surface enters solely through vs and its derivatives.
    Second, the comparison equation. The reference [BDLF25] retains the exact minimal-surface
system, regularising only the average in order to update the surface, at the cost that the surface
obtained in the limit is smooth but not shown to be minimal (their Theorem 1.3). We instead
compare against solutions of LG (vs ) = 0 for a fixed smoothed area integrand G — an elliptic
regularisation performed at the level of the integrand rather than of the surface. The integrand G
is uniformly elliptic and smooth, which is exactly what our higher order estimates require and
what removes the degeneracy of the area integrand at large slopes.
    Third, the elliptic estimates. In [BDLF25] the regularity of the comparison surfaces is imported

from the classical theory of the minimal-surface system; here it is proved from scratch. In §6
the operators LF , TA and the negative norm ∥ · ∥−1,1 are developed following [Men13], and 6.24
establishes interior Schauder estimates of arbitrary order by a self-contained higher order tower
modelled on [Fed69, 5.2.15]. The tower proper — 6.20, 6.22 and 6.23 — appeals neither to [GT01]

is forced by our need to control the constants at all orders simultaneously.
    Fourth, the route from decay to smoothness. The reference [BDLF25] passes directly to C ∞ :
their quantitative closeness of two comparison minimal surfaces feeds a Whitney construction
that produces the C ∞ graphs at once. Our argument is instead factored through the theory of
approximate differentiability: the decay first yields, via 10.4, approximate differentiability of a
definite finite order (k, α), which by Santilli’s theorem [San19] is equivalent to rectifiability of
class (k, α); the C ∞ statement is obtained as a separate step from the finite-order statements on
a common set of full measure. This modularity isolates the geometric measure theoretic content
(Santilli’s characterisation) from the analytic content (the decay), and yields, as a by-product, the
sharp finite-order statement of 10.5. At the level of prerequisites, finally, both papers build on
Almgren’s multiple valued functions, but they stand differently to Menne’s [Men13] C 2 rectifiability:
[BDLF25] quotes it as the previous state of the art, and as one of the treatments that adopt
Almgren’s original approximation scheme, but does not otherwise rest on it, whereas here it is
a working tool — the operators LF , TA and the norm ∥·∥−1,1 of §6 follow [Men13, 3.1]. The analytic
core of [BDLF25] rests on tilt-excess and Lipschitz-approximation apparatus of De Lellis–Spadaro
type, whereas ours continues the line of [Men10, Men13] and of Federer’s treatment of elliptic
systems.

10.5 Theorem (rectifiability of every finite order). Let U ⊆ Rn be open and V ∈ IVd (U ) satisfy
δV = 0. Then spt ∥V ∥ is (H d , d) rectifiable of class (k, α) for every k ∈ P and 0 < α < 1; in
particular ∥V ∥ is carried by a countable union of d dimensional submanifolds of Rn of class C k
for every k ∈ P.
Proof. We may assume d ≥ 2: if d = 0 then spt ∥V ∥ is countable, and if d = 1 then, by the structure
theorem for stationary one-dimensional integral varifolds [AA76], H 1 almost all of spt ∥V ∥ consists
of relatively open subarcs of straight lines; in either case spt ∥V ∥ is (H d , d) rectifiable of class C ∞ ;
hence, of every class (k, α).
    Now suppose d ≥ 2, fix k and α, and let A be as in 10.4. Then A is H d measurable
with H d (A ∩ K) < ∞ for every compact K ⊆ U (as ∥V ∥ is a Radon measure and ∥V ∥ =
QH d A locally with Q ≥ 1), and by 10.4 A is approximately differentiable of order (k, α)
with dim ap Tan(A, a) = d at H d almost every a ∈ A. Exhausting U by compact sets and
applying [San19, 5.6] (equivalently [San19, 1.2]) on each, we conclude that A is (H d , d) rectifiable
of class (k, α). Since Θ∗d (∥V ∥, z) ≥ 1 for z ∈ spt ∥V ∥ by [All72, 3.5(1)(a)(c)], the monotonicity
formula [All72, 5.1(2)] and the upper semicontinuity of the density [All72, 8.6], the comparison
theorem [Fed69, 2.10.19(1)] gives H d (spt ∥V ∥ ∼ A) ≤ ∥V ∥(U ∼ A) = 0; hence, spt ∥V ∥ too is
(H d , d) rectifiable of class (k, α). Since ∥V ∥ = QH d A and ∥V ∥(U ∼ A) = 0, the varifold is
carried by A; whence, the final assertion.

differentiability of every order holds on a common set of full measure.

Tool and computational resource disclosure
As said in the introduction, most of the manuscript was written by hand by the author. To finish
the work the tools Claude AI and Claude Code have been employed mostly using the Opus 5
model. In particular, the LLM has been used to fill many gaps, adjust the constants, check
consistency, verify some conjectures, draft the introduction, and proof-read the paper. Claude
Code has generated a lot of LATEX code basing on the initial draft, the literature enumerated in the
bibliography, and author’s guidance. Especially, the second half of section 6, starting from 6.14,
was written entirely by Claude Code (supported by Federer’s book) – this is rather standard PDE
theory but the author could not find the precise estimates in the form needed for this proof in the
literature. Also section 10 is a production by Claude Code. However, a private communication
of Menne was the basis for generating section 10. Section 3 was initially written entirely by Menne
and then adjusted by the author via the AI tool. All other parts where written originally by the
author and then transformed using Claude.
    The author takes full responsibility for the final outcome of these actions presented here but
he must also admit that he has not thoroughly checked all the content generated by the AI.
Nonetheless, convinced that postponing it would not improve the quality, he decided to publish
the paper.
