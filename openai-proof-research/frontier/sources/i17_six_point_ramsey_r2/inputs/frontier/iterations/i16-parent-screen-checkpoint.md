## Potential six-point circle boundary

The pinned *A classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations*, `thm:classification`, characterizes a finite affinely spanning configuration with coordinate field F by a matrix P over F tensor_Q F whose tensor evaluations at every point vanish and whose multiplied spatial block is the identity. The distinction between the tensor equations and their multiplied scalar equations is essential.

The same source's `cons:quadrilateral` proves that every set of at most five distinct circle points is Ramsey. It proves a nine-point algebraically independent negative example and cites Pálvölgyi's earlier seven-point negative example. Source `cons:twelve` gives a separate explicit derivation obstruction. These results do not alone settle every six-point circle configuration.

Primary lead, not yet fully inspected: Dömötör Pálvölgyi, *A cyclic non-Ramsey heptagon*, arXiv:2609.23327, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23327. The search-result abstract establishes only that the paper claims a seven-point circle counterexample. No conclusion about its six-point scope or novelty follows until the complete current paper is read. Search absence is not novelty evidence.

An exploratory algebraic formulation uses the rational circle parameter t and the projectively equivalent row q(t)=(1,t,t^2). The tensor evaluations become bivariate polynomials of bidegree at most (2,2) at paired parameters (T_i,U_i). The ordinary circle equation corresponds, up to the fixed coordinate change and scale, to the bilinear matrix J with J_02=J_20=1/2 and J_11=-1; q(t)^T J q(u)=(t-u)^2/2.

A proposed next calculation is to determine whether that double-diagonal polynomial has an exact tensor-kernel lift for six points. Generic matrix rank over the full fraction field does not settle this: denominators that vanish under multiplication cannot be inverted when preserving the prescribed multiplied block. Formal jets at U=T are only necessary compatibility tests unless an exact lift theorem is proved. Likewise specializing transcendental T_i to rational numerical values is not a homomorphism of the entire coordinate-field tensor ring; a successful finite experiment would not establish Ramsey status for generic transcendental points.
