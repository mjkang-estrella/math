    The next step is the main tool in this approach and consists in a gradient bound for solutions to (3.4).
The idea was introduced by P.-L. Lions in [26, Lessons 27/11 and 4/12 2009], following the lines of the
classical Bernstein method. It was recently refined in [28] to handle the general case with x-dependent
nonlinearities. In the aforementioned results, Ω was assumed to be the flat torus. We extend here this kind
of estimates for the case of bounded domains with Neumann conditions.
