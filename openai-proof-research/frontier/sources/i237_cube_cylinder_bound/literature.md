# I237 literature: cube cylinder lower bound

2026-10-12. The local copies below were actually inspected at the scopes stated. Sources are evidence for their own statements; unsuccessful searches are not evidence of worldwide novelty. No assertion that the list exhausts the literature is made. The proof and contribution decisions are separate: the proposed 2sqrt(3) bound is correct, but does not merit a tenth substantial finding on the evidence here.

## Direct primary comparison

**Bezdek–Litvak, Covering convex bodies by cylinders and lattice points by flats (2009), arXiv v1.** [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.4842v1); [local PDF](bezdek-litvak-2009v1.pdf), [extraction](bezdek-litvak-2009v1.txt). Read introduction, definitions, Theorem 2.1, Section 3 including Theorem 3.1, proof and both remarks (pp. 1–8); the lattice sections are not used. The theorem permits measurable bases and gives the directionwise sum at least 1/d, and at least 1 for ellipsoids. The ball proof on pp. 6–7 explicitly uses p(x)=(1-|x|^2)^(-1/2) inside the ball, zero outside, with affine-line integral pi. Thus I237's density principle, not just its general philosophy, is old. The unit ball in C yields total base area at least pi. The source does not state the cube truncation/cap calculation or a cube lower bound of 4 in this read scope.

**Bezdek–Litvak, Packing convex bodies by cylinders (2016), arXiv v2 of 21 November 2015.** [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05115v2); [local PDF](bezdek-litvak-packing-2016v2.pdf), [extraction](bezdek-litvak-packing-2016v2.txt). Read pp. 1–6 through Theorems 3.1, 4.2, 4.4 and 4.6, and Section 6 (pp. 9–13); Section 5's packing formula/proof was also inspected. Theorem 3.1 supplies multiplicity r but keeps 1/d for general line-axis covers. Theorems 4.2/4.4/4.6 and 5.1 bound packing costs, not arbitrary cover costs. Section 6 uses hyperplane sections and planar plank packing. Reversing its inequalities would be invalid. The same old singular-ball density occurs again. No stronger cube cover theorem is stated in the inspected results.

**Akopyan–Karasev–Petrov, Bang's problem and symplectic invariants (2019), arXiv v3, 29 December 2019.** [Primary v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.0871v3); [local PDF](akopyan-karasev-petrov-latest.pdf), [extraction](akopyan-karasev-petrov-latest.txt). The filename records the unversioned retrieval endpoint; the downloaded title page identifies v3, and its hash fixes the bytes. Read introductory discussion, Section 4 including Definition 4.1, Conjecture 4.2 and Lemmas 4.3–4.4, and all of Section 8 (pp. 16–18). Section 8.1 explicitly credits measure duality/linear programming, then treats weighted covers of Euclidean balls. Theorem 8.2 and Corollary 8.3 concern ball covers with axes of dimension at least two, with the one-dimensional-axis ball theorem credited to Bezdek–Litvak. They do not replace the target ball with a cube. Conjecture 4.2 concerns convex symplectic cylinders in even-dimensional space and is a conjecture, not a cube theorem for arbitrary measurable bases. This later edition closes the version gap in I212's earlier v1-only screen for the present task.

**Ball, The plank problem for symmetric bodies (1991), arXiv math/9201218v1.** [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9201218v1); [local PDF](ball-1991v1.pdf), [extraction](ball-1991v1.txt). Read pp. 1–3: definition of a plank, Theorems 1–2, and the cube/symmetric-body hyperplane-avoidance corollary. A plank is defined by a single real functional. This proves the relative-width bound for normed balls, including the cube, but not a sum of planar base areas for line-axis cylinders. Its explicit cube passage is about avoiding hyperplanes with a homothetic subcube. The rest of the proof was not needed or audited. Neither this statement nor the complex-ball result discussed in AKP licenses a lower bound of 4 here.

**Falconer, Function space topologies defined by sectional integrals and applications to an extremal problem (1980).** [Primary publisher](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100056553); [local PDF](falconer-1980.pdf), [extraction](falconer-1980.txt). Read pp. 81–82, Section 3 (pp. 85–87), pp. 89–94 including Theorems 4.3–4.4, 5.1–5.3, Corollary 5.4 and Theorem 6.1, and the bibliography. Its initial definition makes the sections hyperplanes H(t,theta). In R^3 these are planes. The positive-density result M=2Ar in dimensions two/three therefore cannot be substituted for the line-integral capacity here. The optimization viewpoint and averaging over simplex symmetries are already present. Some old formula glyphs extract poorly; the dimension/type distinction is explicit in prose and definitions, and no delicate OCR formula is used to prove the cube bound. The Croft references are a plane measure problem and mimeographed problem notes, not a read cube theorem.

## Source-chain checks and bounded survey checks

**Bezdek, Tarski's plank problem revisited (2009), v1.** [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.4637v1); [local PDF](bezdek-plank-2009v1.pdf), [extraction](bezdek-plank-2009v1.txt). Read Section 3, pp. 4–6: Problem 3.1, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3, Problem 3.4. It attributes the half-area question and tetrahedral equality example to Bang, and reports the same general and ellipsoid bounds. Its discussion explicitly separates the plank specialization. It supplies no cube constant of 4.

**Bezdek–Khan, The geometry of homothetic covering and illumination (2016), v2.** [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06040v2); [local PDF](bezdek-khan-2016v2.pdf), [extraction](bezdek-khan-2016v2.txt). Read Section 4.3, pp. 15–16: Definition 3, Conjectures 4.11/4.13/4.15, Theorems 4.12/4.14. This separates the symmetric-body plank equality from the general line-axis cylinder question and ellipsoid results. The half-area directionwise statement is explicitly a conjecture there. Other cube mentions located by source-local search concern homothetic covering, illumination or covering index, not the present area-cost infimum. Those other sections were not comprehensively reviewed.

**Verreault, Plank theorems and their applications: a survey, arXiv v2 (2025 revision; publication 2026).** [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.05540v2); [local HTML](verreault-2025v2.html), [text extraction](verreault-2025v2.txt). Read Question 4.14 and adjacent text, Theorem 4.9 (complex-ball cylinders), plus source-local cube/cylinder occurrences and associated short passages. The question reports the universal one-third result. Its pre-release open-status language is superseded here by the stipulated pinned tetrahedral counterexample. The displayed cube occurrences concern hyperplane vertex covers and hyperplane avoidance, not an arbitrary-cylinder area lower bound. This bounded survey check is not a comprehensive post-2025 priority review.

**Döring–de Jonge–Yang, Coverage of the unit cube by dynamic Boolean models (2025), v1.** [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22286v1); [local PDF](doering-de-jonge-yang-2025v1.pdf), [extraction](doering-de-jonge-yang-2025v1.txt). Read abstract, introduction and Section 1.1 through Theorem 1.1 (pp. 1–4, opening of p. 5). This plausible cube/cylinder search hit studies a Poisson number of random trajectories and the asymptotic coverage radius as the intensity grows. A high-probability radius asymptotic is not a universal finite-cover lower bound on summed arbitrary base areas. No use of its proofs or later theorems is made.

**Bang (1951), original paper.** A direct primary AMS PDF retrieval returned HTTP 403 Forbidden. No copy was retained and no reading is claimed. Historical attribution here relies on the read Bezdek/Bezdek–Litvak statements; this is an explicit access limitation.

## Comparison conclusion

The exact old operation is the singular-ball certificate, while the new local calculation is cube truncation at R=2/sqrt(3). The directly read statements provide no matching constant or stronger arbitrary-axis cube theorem, but this does not establish their absence elsewhere. The cube result is not literally the accepted tetrahedral polynomial specialization: it has a different body and different density. Nevertheless, unlike R015's sharp weighted chord envelope, it adds only disjoint-cap integration and scalar calculus to the old method. The genuinely source-dependent ruled-family obstruction is immediate once finite realization is stipulated. Retain both as technical observations; do not count a tenth substantial result.
