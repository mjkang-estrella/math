                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                      In this paper we continue to rigorously establish the predictions in ground breaking work in statistical
                                                  physics by Decelle, Krzakala, Moore, Zdeborová (2011) regarding the block model, in particular in the
                                                  case of q = 3 and q = 4 communities.
                                                      We prove that for q = 3 and q = 4 there is no computational-statistical gap if the average degree is
                                                  above some constant by showing it is information theoretically impossible to detect below the Kesten-
                                                  Stigum bound.
                                                      The proof is based on showing that for the broadcast process on Galton-Watson trees, reconstruction
                                                  is impossible for q = 3 and q = 4 if the average degree is sufficiently large. This improves on the
                                                  result of Sly (2009), who proved similar results for regular trees for q = 3. Our analysis of the critical
                                                  case q = 4 provides a detailed picture showing that the tightness of the Kesten-Stigum bound in the
                                                  antiferromagnetic case depends on the average degree of the tree.
                                                      Our results prove conjectures of Decelle, Krzakala, Moore, Zdeborová (2011), Moore (2017), Abbe
                                                  and Sandon (2018) and Ricci-Tersenghi, Semerjian, and Zdeborová (2019). Our proofs are based on a
                                                  new general coupling of the tree and graph processes and on a refined analysis of the broadcast process
                                                  on the tree.


                                           1      Introduction
                                           In this paper we study the block model and establish a number of exact phase transitions that were conjec-
                                           tured in the case of more than two communities.
                                               The block model is a random graph model generalizing the famous Erdos-Renyi random graph [ER+ 60]
                                           and is a special case of inhomegnuous random graphs, see e.g. [BJR07]. It has been studied extensively in
                                           statistics as a model of communities [HLL83], see e.g. [SN97, BC09, RCY11] and in computer science as a
                                           model to study the average case behavior of clustering algorithms, see e.g. [DF89, JS98, CK01, McS01, CO10].
                                           The papers above mostly concentrate on cases where the average degree is at least of order log n, where n
                                           is the number of nodes in the graph. We focus on the sparse regime where the average degree is constant.
                                           The model is defined as follows:

     The threshold dλ2 = 1 is called the Kesten-Stigum (KS) bound. It is a natural threshold for the
trade off between noise and duplication. It was first discovered in the context of the broadcast process
on trees in work of Kesten and Stigum [KS66], and has later played important role in understanding the
broadcast process [BRZ95, Iof96b, Iof96a, EKYPS00, MP03, JM04] as we discuss below, in Phylogenetic
reconstruction [Mos04, DMR11, MRS11, RS17] and the block model which we discuss next. We note that,
in the case of q = 2, it is known to be tight for reconstruction in all of these models [BRZ95, Iof96b, Iof96a,
EKYPS00, Mos04, Mas14, MNS18, BLM15]
     For the block model, it was also conjectured in [DKMZ11] that the optimal algorithm for detection is
Belief-Propagation. While we are still unable to analyze Belief-Propagation for detecting block models, it
is possible to analyze related algorithms based on non-backtracking or self-avoiding walks (as was proposed
by [KMM+ 13]). Thus in a series of works [Mas14, MNS18, BLM15, AS15, AS18] part I. of Conjecture 1.2
was established. In particular, it was established in [AS18] that whenever dλ2 > 1, there exists a polynomial
time algorithm to detect communities.
     Parts II and III predict the regime where one can expect a computational-statistical gap, meaning a gap
between what is achievable information theoretically and what is achievable with known computationally
efficient algorithms. Some special cases of part III of the conjecture are given rigorous support in [BMNN16,
AS18]. In particular [BMNN16] find regimes where detection is information-theoretically possible below the
Kesten-Stigum threshold when λ < 0 and q ≥ 5 and when λ > 0 and q ≥ 11. The paper [AS18] find such
regimes for small d when q = 4, λ < 0.
     After the work [DKMZ11], a number of refined conjectures were made in [Moo17, AS18, RTSZ19] regard-
ing the regimes where computational-statistical gap exists for q = 3, 4. In particular [RTSZ19] conjectures
that:
Conjecture 1.3. For the block model:

   I. q = 3. When q = 3 it is information theoretically impossible to detect communities whenever dλ2 < 1.
  II. q = 4, λ > 0. When q = 4, λ > 0, it is information theoretically impossible to detect communities
      whenever dλ2 < 1.
 III. q = 4, λ < 0. When q = 4, λ < 0, there is a critical degree d∗ such that if d > d∗ then it is impossible
      to detect when dλ2 < 1 but if d < d∗ it is possible to detect by a (not necessarily efficient) algorithm in
      a regime where dλ2 < 1.

     It was shown in [AS18] that whenever dλ2 > 1, there exists a polynomial time algorithm to detect
communities, thus Theorem 1.5 and the result of [AS18] altogether imply that there exists no statistical-

     The seminal work [COKPZ18] used the so-called “quiet planting” method to show that in the anti-
ferromagnetic regime λ < 0, i.e. a < b, there exists the so-called condensation threshold dcond (q, λ) from
physics [MM09], such that detection is impossible if d < dcond (q, λ) while there exists an (not necessarily
efficient) algorithm that detects the communities if d > dcond (q, λ). The work [COKPZ18] deals with a variety
of models beyond the block model, and verifies much of the conjecture from physics [KMRT+ 07]. However,

stochastic optimization, matches the Kesten-Stigum bound or not.

recent work [DM22] conjectured that even when q = 2, the formula for the asymptotic mutual information in
the case λ > 0 has a completely different form than in the case λ < 0. Our results show that the information
theoretic threshold equals the Kesten-Stigum bound regardless of λ > 0 or λ < 0 when q ∈ {3, 4} and d large
enough.

    The broadcast process on the trees was first studied in the context of multi-type branching processes,
where Kesten and Stigum [KS66] identified the bound dλ2 = 1 as a critical threshold for the behavior of the
fluctuations of this model. Some related models were studied in trying to understand spin-glasses [CCST86,
CCST90, CCC+ 90]. The interest in this model grew significantly when it was established that for q = 2
the Kesten-Stigum (KS) bound is tight for reconstruction. This was established first for regular trees
[BRZ95, Iof96b] and then on general trees [EKYPS00, Iof96a] where the degree is replaced by the branching
number [Lyo89].
    The fact that the KS bound is not tight for reconstruction on trees (i.e. reconstruction is solvable for
a broadcast model at some λ and d-ary tree satisfying dλ2 < 1 and q ≥ 3 states) was first established
in [Mos01] with much tighter results obtained by [Sly09, Sly11] as we discuss below. Interestingly, the KS
bound is tight for all q for count-reconstruction [MP03] and for robust reconstruction [JM04]. There are also
interesting conjectures relating the computational complexity of estimating the root from the leaves to the
KS bound [MMS20, KM22]. To read more about the broadcast model see [Mos04, Mos22].

   Open Problems. We note that while this paper provides some sharp results showing that there is no
computational-statistical gap for block models when q = 3 and in some cases where q = 4, some interesting
challenges remain, including:
    • Can one prove Theorem 1.5 for q = 3 and small d? Can this be done for q = 4 and λ ≥ 0?
    • Can we improve on [BMNN16, AS18] and chart more accurately the regimes below the KS bound,
      where it is information theoretically possible to detect?
    • The tightness of KS bound for reconstruction on general trees was established by [EKYPS00] for q = 2
      case, where the degree is replaced by the branching number [Lyo89]. It is thus natural to try to extend
      Theorem 1.12 from Galton-Watson trees to general trees.
    • It was conjectured in [RTSZ19] that in the setting of Theorem 1.13 for q = 4 and the antiferromagnetic
      case, when d > d∗ the KS bound is sharp. It is natural to try and prove this. By the coupling argument
      in our paper this would imply that detection is impossible for the block model for the same parameters.


2     Proof overview
In this section, we introduce the main ideas to prove our main results. We first explain how Theorem 1.12
implies Theorem 1.5 by coupling the block model to multiple independent broadcast models. Then, we
introduce the high level ideas to establish Theorem 1.12 and Theorem 1.13.



                                                         6
2.1    Coupling SBM to multiple broadcast processes
We prove Theorem 1.5 by establishing that nonreconstruction on the tree implies nonreconstruction on the
corresponding stochastic block model.
Theorem 2.1. If there is nonreconstruction on the Poisson(d) Galton-Watson tree for the broadcast process
with second eigenvalue λ then detection is information theoretically impossible on the stochastic block model
G(n, q, na , nb ) for (a, b) corresponding to (d, λ) by equation (1).
    Results of [MNS15] proved the analogue of Theorem 2.1 in the case of the symmetric 2-state SBM
by establishing an approximate Markov random field property. Namely, given the observed graph, the
conditional distribution of a neighborhood of u, conditional on all the spins outside the neighborhood of u,
is approximately the same if we only condition on the spins on the boundary of the neighborhood of u. This
is more subtle than it looks at first, as to establish it, one has to account for the information contained in
the absence of edges from the neighborhood to the remainder of the graph. In Section 6 we present a new
approach to prove Theorem 2.1 for the general stochastic block model G(n, q, na , nb ).
    The connection between these two notions of nonreconstruction follows from the fact that the local weak
limit of the SBM and its spins is the Poisson(d) Galton-Watson tree with the symmetric channel broadcast
model. This means that we can couple the local neighborhood of a vertex in the SBM with Galton-Watson
tree including the spins. In fact we show that we can simultaneously couple the neighborhoods of ⌊n1/5 ⌋
randomly chosen vertices ui at a time.
    We show that asymptotically the Maximum a Posteriori (MAP) estimator cannot correctly assign vertices
with rate greater than q1 asymptotically. We do so by partitioning the ⌊n1/5 ⌋ local neighborhoods according
to their tree topology and the spin configuration on their boundary configuration. By the symmetry of the
model, the spin configurations within the neighborhoods are exchangeable within each partition class. By
our coupling and the fact that we are in the nonreconstruction regime on the tree, in most partition classes
the root has each given color in about q1 fraction of ui . It follows that even with the additional information of
the boundary configurations, the MAP estimator correctly assigns only 1q + o(1) spins asymptotically. Since
this holds for a randomly chosen set, we also cannot do better than a 1q + o(1) fraction on the whole graph
which implies (2). We believe that this new approach applies to a wide range of settings.
Proof of Theorem 1.5. The result follows from Theorem 2.1 because if (a − b)2 ≤ q(a + (q − 1)b) then
dλ2 ≤ 1 and so for large enough d and q ∈ {3, 4} by Theorem 1.12 we have nonreconstruction on the
Poisson(d) Galton-Watson tree.

Lemma 3.1. For arbitrary tree T , consider a broadcast model in Definition 1.8. If there is nonreconstruction
at λ, then for any c ∈ [0, 1], there is also nonreconstruction at cλ.
Proof. In the broadcast model the transition matrix from parent to child is given by Kλ = λIq + (1 − λ)Jq ,
where Iq is the q × q identity matrix and Jq is the rank one transition matrix where all entries are 1/q.
Therefore we have:
                                              Kcλ = Kc Kλ ,
and note that since c > 0, the markov chain with matrix Kc , copies with probability c and randomizes
uniformly, otherwise. This implies that if σ i (n) is a sample of the broadcast process with λ and η i (n) is the
process with cλ, then η i can be obtain from σ i by the following random process:
 (1) Perform edge percolation on T with probability c.
 (2) For vertices v at level n that are at the same component of the root, let η (i) (v) = σ (i) (v).
 (3) For vertices in other components, run the broadcast process on each component with a uniformly
     random chosen root and parameter λ.
Note that the process to generate η from σ does not depend on the root and therefore

                                     dT V (η i (n), η j (n)) ≤ dT V (σ i (n), σ j (n)),

which implies the statement of the lemma.
