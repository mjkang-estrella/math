### 5.2 A genuine univalent subclass, and its exact tail coefficients

Suppose additionally that f is meromorphic in a neighborhood of the closed disk, holomorphic and univalent in D, and normalized as above. Its derivative has no zeros in D. Any boundary zero of f' is simple: if it had order m>=2 at zeta, the radial size would be `O((1-r)^m)`, contradicting the distortion lower bound `|f'(r zeta)|>=(1-r)/(1+r)^3`. There are finitely many such zeros. Poles of f give zeros of h, not poles. Thus the only poles of h near the closed disk are simple boundary poles zeta_j, with residues

`c_j=1/f''(zeta_j)`.

Subtracting their principal parts gives a g analytic on a neighborhood of the closed disk and therefore bounded there. The criterion applies with `M=sum_j|c_j|` and its actual bound B. Neither parameter has yet been bounded uniformly over this class.

There are exact one-map tail asymptotics:

`lambda² area_D{|h_f|>lambda} -> (pi/2) sum_j |c_j|²`,

`lambda^4 area_W{|phi'|>lambda} -> (pi/4) sum_j |c_j|²`.

For completeness, take disjoint fixed neighborhoods of the finitely many poles. Outside them h is bounded. In one neighborhood write `h=c/(z-zeta)+b(z)` with `|b|<=A`. For lambda>A, the high-level set is sandwiched between its intersections with disks of radii `|c|/(lambda+A)` and `|c|/(lambda-A)`. The area of a small disk centered on the unit circle intersected with D is `(pi/2)rho²+O(rho³)`. This proves the first limit. For the inverse-map limit use

`area_W{|phi'|>lambda}=integral_{|h|>lambda}|h|^-2 dA`.

Uniformly near the pole, `|h|^-2=|z-zeta|²/|c|²[1+O(|z-zeta|)]`. Integrating over the same sandwich gives `(pi/4)|c|² lambda^-4+o(lambda^-4)`. Summing the finitely many disjoint contributions proves both limits. If there are no boundary critical points, h is bounded and both limits are zero. This proof is local and quantitative at each fixed map; uniform asymptotics over a family would require common separation and remainder bounds.

This class includes the Koebe map `f(z)=z/(1-z)²`. At zeta=−1 its reciprocal derivative is

`h(z)=(1-z)^3/(1+z)=-z²+4z-7+8/(1+z)`.

Here `c=8`, so the two tail coefficients are exactly `32pi` and `16pi`. In a sufficiently small interior half-neighborhood of −1, the weighted square integral has the same integrability as

`integral_0^a d rho/[rho (log(1/rho))^gamma]`.
