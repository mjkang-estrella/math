# I221 literature and reading limits

Status: early contribution screen, 2026-10-12. The proposed Coulomb/Riesz laws remain hypotheses. This bibliography records what was actually read; an excerpt is not a claim of full-paper coverage.

## Critical comparison

**Carioni, Del Grande, Iglesias, Schönberger, From plans to maps: Nonlocal regularization of optimal transport, arXiv:2609.05062v1, 4 September 2026.** [Versioned primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05062v1).

Read the existing 859-line excerpt completely, in chunks 1–290, 291–599, 600–859. It contains PDF pages 1–9 and 20–24, not the intervening pages. Most relevant: Examples 2.8 and 4.6, Proposition 4.1, Corollary 4.3, Proposition 4.4, Remark 4.5. The version and abstract were independently checked on arXiv; its HTML introductory and regularization definitions were also read.

The close model is a uniform vertical source segment and two parallel target segments with squared Euclidean cost, exponent q>1 and fractional order alpha with alpha q<1. Example 4.6 supplies exact-image alternating maps with cost excess of order N^-2 and regularization of order N^(alpha q), plus matching penalized bounds of order epsilon^(2/(2+alpha q)). The lower proof is branchwise monotone rearrangement and an interpolation inequality for the primitive of a phase indicator. The comparison is strong: the proposed fractional exponent and balance already occur. The paper does not thereby state the full-dimensional calibrated Coulomb theorem. Its general framework does not automatically cover singular Coulomb costs, varying smooth densities that vanish at boundaries, or a pair constrained by a three-cycle.

No proof of Theorem 2.9, Sections 3 or 5–6, or bibliography beyond what appears in the retained excerpt was read. The referenced interpolation papers have not been exhaustively traced. The p=1 claim requires separate justification.

## Requested gradient-penalization comparison

**De Pascale, Louet, Santambrogio, The Monge problem with vanishing gradient penalization: vortices and asymptotic profile, arXiv:1407.7022v3.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.7022v3).

Read the existing 142-line PDF-pages-1–3 excerpt in place, and new PDF-page-3–4 and page-5 extracts completely. Thus introductory Theorems 1.1/1.2 and Proposition 1.1, assumptions, interpretation and the exact leading excess were read; no full proof of the recovery construction or the later asymptotic estimates was read. Some corresponding HTML passages were inspected.

Theorem 1.1 is the exact-image density comparison; Theorem 1.2 supplies the logarithmic penalized asymptotic discussed in the review. Its next-order Gamma-limit is not fully identified. The positivity and domain assumptions must be retained.

## Cyclic Coulomb relaxation

**Colombo and Di Marino, Equality between Monge and Kantorovich multimarginal problems with Coulomb cost, author version dated 11 April 2013; journal publication 2015.** [Author PDF](https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/2127/MultiColDim.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10231-013-0376-0).

Read the existing 210-line excerpt completely: PDF pages 1–4 and the start of the next page. Theorems 1.1 and 1.4, Remark 1.3, Theorem 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and the statement/proof beginning of Lemma 2.3. No claim to have read the full proof of the multimarginal cyclic construction.

Exact measure-preserving N-cycles approaching the Kantorovich value are already known. Their existence is not a new contribution here. The missing feature for this task is a rate measured by the actual maps' BV/fractional complexity, with a matching all-competitor lower estimate.

## Mixing, interface and microstructure

**Cinti and Otto, Interpolation inequalities in pattern formation, arXiv:1509.01189v1; JFA 271 (2016).** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01189v1), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2016.05.007).

New PDF-pages-1–4 extract completely read: abstract, introduction, Propositions 1.1–1.3, definitions and scaling discussion. The paper discusses the established competition of interface and negative-norm/transport energy in coarsening and branching. Proposition 1.1 couples BV and H^-1; Proposition 1.3 couples BV and W2. Neither is a literal citation for the proposed weighted submeasure/branch lemma without reduction. No proof or later propositions read. This is prior art for the method, not a prior proof of the Coulomb conclusion.

**Seis, Maximal mixing by incompressible fluid flows, arXiv:1309.0556v1.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0556v1).

New extracts covering PDF pages 1–8 read completely. In particular, equation (3), Lemmas 1–2 and the proof of Lemma 1 on page 8. Lemma 2 bounds the inverse BV complexity by a transport-based mixing length for periodic mean-zero binary phases. The proof of Lemma 2, which begins on the following page, was not read. The time-dependent exponential mixing-rate result should not be confused with a static Coulomb cost bound. Only the static interpolation precedent is used here.

**Sagiv and Steinerberger, Transport and Interface: an Uncertainty Principle for the Wasserstein distance, arXiv:1905.07450.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07450).

Abstract/search-discovery only. Records the transport-versus-interface theme; not used as a substitute for a theorem with different hypotheses. No stored copy, full proof or version-specific quantitative reliance.

## Coulomb plan smoothing and duality

**Bindini and De Pascale, Optimal transport with Coulomb cost and the semiclassical limit of density functional theory, JEP 4 (2017), 909–934.** [Primary PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jep.59.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5802/jep.59).

Read a new PDF-page-10 extract (printed page 917) completely, including Proposition 3.7 and its setup. Web PDF passages on printed pages 911–916 defined the density class and stated Theorems 1.1/1.2 and 3.3/3.4; selected passages on printed pages 924–925 exposed Proposition 3.16. The latter were selective reads, not full-page or full-proof coverage.

Proposition 3.7 keeps exact marginals, approximates a plan separated from the collision diagonal by absolutely continuous plans, controls square-root H^1 energy by a constant times epsilon^-2 plus fixed density energy, and preserves the Coulomb cost in the limit. The gradient is a plan-density/wavefunction gradient, not a map derivative. This does not settle the requested deterministic BV law.

**De Pascale, Optimal Transport with Coulomb cost. Approximation and duality, arXiv:1503.07063v2.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07063v2).

New PDF-pages-1–3 extract completely read, as well as the arXiv abstract. Introductory duality formula and the purpose of discrete approximation/bounded dual maximizers checked. The later theorem proofs were not read. No map-regularity exponent is attributed to this paper.

## Nearby multimarginal non-Monge context

**Friesecke, Mass splitting in the time-discrete generalized Euler equations and non-Monge solutions in multi-marginal optimal transport, arXiv:2601.02616v1.** [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02616v1).

New PDF-pages-1–5 and 9–10 extracts completely read. Pages 9–10 distinguish a non-Monge optimizer from nonexistence of any Monge optimizer and discuss Coulomb, repulsive harmonic, and spline costs. The paper concerns generalized Euler mass splitting; the inspected passages do not supply the proposed cost-complexity theorem. Its January 2026 account of the open Coulomb question is historical context, superseded for this task by the accepted pinned result. No full mathematical proof of its new examples was read.

The pinned manuscript cites Gerolin–Kausamo–Rajala for the repulsive harmonic shared-component mechanism; its actual citation and the source's description were read, as was Friesecke's discussion. GKR's original full theorem/proof was not retrieved, so this screen does not pronounce on every quantitative consequence of that paper. Similarly, the original Rosenblatt paper, Louet thesis, later citations of the September 2026 paper, and all literature in other languages were not exhaustively checked. These are limits on worldwide novelty, not reasons to treat standard building blocks as new.

## Search and source limitations

Discovery included the exact DPLS arXiv identifier; transport/interface and Wasserstein/BV/fractional interpolation terms; Colombo–Di Marino cyclic equivalence; Coulomb map approximation and regularity; and mass-splitting regularization scaling. Queries with broad terms returned substantial irrelevant material. Search misses have no evidentiary role in the verdict. Only the named primary works and the pinned source support substantive comparisons; secondary aggregator snippets were discovery aids.

An attempted guessed identifier 1806.00190 was unrelated (Markov decision processes), and was not used. The arXiv HTML of 2609.05062 did not expose the full later Example 4.6 in its retrieved view; the actual saved PDF excerpt supplies that comparison. No claim of a complete citation-chain or worldwide novelty search is made.

Existing I217 source copies were reused. New copies below are text extracts of specifically named PDF pages, not whole PDFs. All newly retained text was fully read. Original PDF bytes were streamed through pdftotext and were not retained; their whole-file hashes are therefore not claimed. Some PDFs show an internal compilation date different from the arXiv version date; versioned retrieval URLs, not that internal date, identify the artifact.
