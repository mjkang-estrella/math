                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                      The classical capacity of a quantum channel generally requires regularization because input
                                                  codewords may be entangled across channel uses. We remove this regularization for generalized
                                                  amplitude-damping channels (GADCs) and determine their exact strong-converse exponent throughout
                                                  the full parameter range. The exponent is a single-letter transform of the sandwiched Rényi Holevo
                                                  information and is attained by product input codewords with collective decoding. The key ingredient is
                                                  a decomposition of the joint output into positive operators whose triangular factors have identical scalar
                                                  Gram matrices after norm compression. A sharp triangular Schatten-norm compression inequality
                                                  then establishes maximum output norm multiplicativity for diagonally sandwiched GADCs tensored
                                                  with arbitrary completely positive maps. This yields additivity of the sandwiched Rényi Holevo
                                                  information at every order above one. Consequently, the classical capacity equals the known one-copy
                                                  Holevo information and admits a scalar optimization. Thus, entangled input codewords improve
                                                  neither the capacity nor the optimal exponential decay of decoding success above capacity.

                                           1     Introduction
                                           The classical capacity of a quantum channel is the highest rate at which classical information can be
                                           transmitted with vanishing error. The Holevo–Schumacher–Westmoreland theorem expresses this capacity
                                           as [Hol73, Hol98, SW97]
                                                                                                      1

where χ denotes the Holevo information. The optimization allows input states entangled across arbitrarily
many channel uses, and this regularization is necessary in general [Has09]. A central question is therefore
whether a channel’s one-copy Holevo information already determines its classical capacity. Even for qubit
channels, removing the regularization requires control of outputs produced by arbitrary entangled inputs.
    The generalized amplitude-damping channel (GADC) models a two-level system exchanging energy
with a thermal environment [KSW20]. It describes spin relaxation in quantum devices, including
superconducting circuits [CB08], and has applications to optical losses with low-temperature background
noise [ZLW+ 17]. We write Aγ,N for the channel with damping probability γ and equilibrium occupation
N ; its action is given in (8). The zero-temperature case N = 0 is the amplitude-damping channel.
    The one-copy Holevo information of the GADC is known through a scalar optimization [HF07,
KSW20]. Earlier work developed computable bounds on the regularized capacity [WXD18, KSW20,
FF21, FST22]. Recently, Tang, Zhu, Bai, and Wang [TZBW26] determined the classical capacity of
amplitude damping through a triangular block-matrix entropy inequality. Their result establishes Holevo
additivity with arbitrary other channels for qubit channels admitting a pure output. A GADC with
0 < γ, N < 1 has no pure output, so this criterion does not cover the finite-temperature case.
    In this work, we resolve the regularization problem for the full GADC family and determine its exact
strong-converse exponent. For a channel N , let Ps⋆ (n, r; N ) denote the optimal average decoding success
probability over n uses with at least 2nr messages. We allow arbitrary entangled input codewords and
collective decoding, but no entanglement assistance. The strong-converse exponent is

Yang [WWY14], which relates the sandwiched Rényi information radius to maximum output norms
of sandwiched maps. Our main technical result establishes the required multiplicativity for GADCs
sandwiched by arbitrary nonnegative diagonal operators (Theorem 4). Phase covariance makes these
diagonal operators sufficient for the reference-state optimization. The construction decomposes the joint
output into a convex combination of two positive operators with triangular factors. Replacing each block
of these factors by its Schatten norm produces scalar matrices with identical Gram matrices. A sharp

fractional trace powers. It establishes the previously missing range 2 < p < 4, complementing the known
range p ⩾ 4 [Kin03, Aud08].

3    Additivity of Holevo Rényi information
We prove the additivity statement in Theorem 1 through three steps. First, we establish a Schatten-norm
compression inequality for triangular block matrices. We then apply it to prove multiplicativity of the
maximum output norm for diagonally sandwiched GADCs. Finally, phase covariance and the norm
representation (21) convert this multiplicativity into additivity of Holevo Rényi information, following the
framework of [WWY14].

3.3   Additivity of Holevo Rényi information
We now apply Theorem 4 to the information-radius representation. Phase covariance reduces the one-copy
