   A natural question to ask regarding the Penrose inequality is whether almost
equality would imply that the manifold is close to the Schwarzschild manifold in
some topology. This is called the stability problem for the Penrose inequality.
Recently, there has been growing interest in studying similar stability problems.
Notably, the author and Antoine Song proved a stability result for the positive
mass theorem (see [DS25] and the references therein).

manifold is stable for the Penrose inequality in the pointed measured Gromov-
Hausdorff topology, modulo negligible domains (cf. Definition 2.3). More precisely,
we have the following:

   We give a remark on the boundary area perturbations in Theorem 1.1. Notice
that in Theorem 1.2, we can ensure that Σbi , the major part of the boundary of
Ei , lies in a small neighborhood of the original boundary ∂Mi , but in Theorem
1.1, this is not necessarily true in general. In [LS12, Example 5.2], Lee-Sormani
constructed manifolds with almost equality in the Penrose inequality that are close
to Schwarzschild spaces with a cylinder of arbitrary length appended to the bound-
ary. See also [MS15]. In other words, we need to cut out the long necks in these
examples such that the remaining part has almost the same boundary area and is
stable for the Penrose inequality. Such phenomena are consistent with our stability
results.
   It would also be an interesting question to prove similar results for other topolo-
gies. See, for example, [LS12, LS14, LNN23, KKL21, Don24], etc., and the refer-
ences therein for related results.

address this, we note that a favorable property of the pointed measured Gromov-
Hausdorff convergence modulo negligible domains is that f also converges to f∞ in
the W 1,2 -sense (cf. Lemma 6.1). Therefore, the elliptic equations satisfied by f are
preserved in this convergence, and f∞ also satisfies an elliptic equation on R3 . The
fact that the area of the boundary ∂E converges to 0 has been essential here. The
elliptic equation satisfied by f∞ , together with the control on the gradient, would
imply the rigidity of f∞ (cf. Section 6).

4], we can apply these properties of the functions f and metrics h to prove the main
theorems.
