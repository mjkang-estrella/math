   Our stability results can be taken as generalizations of the main theorem in
[DS23]. In particular, the case when A0 = 0 in Theorem 1.1 was proved in [DS23].
Our proof to the main theorems will be built on the tools developed in [DS23] as
well as some new techniques.
   We give a remark about the topology used in these main theorems. It would be
an interesting question to prove similar results for other topology. See for example
[LS14, LNN20, KKL21, Don22] etc. and the references therein for related results.
A key step in our proof is that we can develop W 1,2 -convergence along the pointed
measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence modulo negligible spikes (c.f. Section 6).
This type of analysis has been studied extensively in cases where the Ricci curvature
has a uniform lower bound, see among others [CC96, Gig15] etc. So our analysis
can be viewed as a generalization from Ricci curvature to scalar curvature, which
may have its own interests.

neighborhood of the original boundary ∂M . In [MS15], for some non-standard

convergence, and f∞ also satisfies an elliptic equation on R3 . The fact that the
area of the boundary ∂E converges to 0 has been used essentially here. The elliptic
equation satisfied by f∞ together with some growth conditions would imply the
rigidity of f∞ (c.f. Section 6).
   Finally, using metric geometry tools and similar arguments in [DS23, Section 4],
we can utilize these properties of f and h to prove the main theorems.

recall some definitions for the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology.
   Assume (X, dX , x), (Y, dY , y) are two pointed metric spaces. The pointed Gromov-

f : (X, dX , x) → (Y, dY , y) is called an ε-pointed Gromov-Hausdorff approximation
(or ε-pGH approximation) if it satisfies the following conditions:
     (1) f (x) = y;
     (2) B(y, 1ε ) ⊂ Bε (f (B(x, 1ε )));
     (3) |dX (x1 , x2 ) − dY (f (x1 ), f (x2 ))| < ε for all x1 , x2 ∈ B(x, 1ε ).
The pGH-distance is defined by
dpGH ((X, dX , x),(Y, dY , y)) :=
                   inf{ε > 0 : ∃ ε-pGH approximation f : (X, dX , x) → (Y, dY , y)}.

metric space (X, d, p) in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff topology, if the following
holds
