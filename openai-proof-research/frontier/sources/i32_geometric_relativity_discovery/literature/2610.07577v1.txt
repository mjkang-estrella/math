   Our focus in this article is to provide a matched conditioning law under simultaneous density and
moment constraints and its power-logarithm case, including rough and singlar targets. It is designed
to answer the question: if an approximate feasible plan is obtained whose transport cost is known to
be close to the ground true within a given tolerance and an approximate optimal map is constructed,
then what we can say about how close the constructed map is to the true optimal map? The answer
to this question will have its own numerical benefits, but it will also allow us to treat the quantitative
stability of optimal transport maps without touching the potentials. As implied by the recent work
[22], we believe going from potential to map could cause loss of information if the goal is stability of
Brenier map itself.

   We ask how accurately a small objective gap determines the optimal map, uniformly over a specified
class of marginals. This is a quantitative stability question in practice: does a nearly optimal feasible
plan approximate the optimal map, and at what rate?
   There are two aspects of this question. The first is conditioning which views the question as the
conversion of objective gap into squared map error, i.e. a bound of the form
                                     Dν (σ) ⩽ t =⇒ Eν (σ) ⩽ ω(t),
where ω is a conditioning modulus. Its size measures how much map error a given objective tolerance
permits under required assumptions. It provides a suﬀicient upper error modulus. For a fixed class C
of probability marginals, define
                         ΩC (t) = {Eν (σ) : (ρ, ν) ∈ C, σ ∈ Π(ρ, ν), Dν (σ) ⩽ t}.                    (1.5)
This is the smallest class-uniform conditioning modulus. An upper bound ΩC (t) ⩽ Cφ(t) answers how
small a cost tolerance is suﬀicient for desired solution accuracy. A matching lower bound ΩC (t) ⩾ cφ(t)
answers whether a uniformly better conversion can follow from the same requirement. Together they
describe the intrinsic worst-case information provided by the cost gap. This is a class-uniform stability,
ΩC (t) → 0 as t → 0. By (1.4), it also justifies the barycentric reconstruction.
                            SHARP CONDITIONING OF OPTIMAL TRANSPORT                                     3


   The other aspect is data perturbation: how does the optimal map change when a marginal changes?
The two aspects are closedly related but not the same. Uniform stability implies indivisual stability,
but the converse does not hold. Failure of uniform stability does not rule out stability for a fixed pair
of marginals.
