## Primary external papers inspected

**Comets–Zeitouni, Ann. Probab. 32 (2004), 880–914**, [author-hosted published PDF](https://cims.nyu.edu/~zeitouni/pdf/AOP226.pdf), DOI `10.1214/aop/1079021467`. Read the introduction/model/assumptions through the start of Section 2, the retained A5/moment definitions, Theorem 3.4 statement, and concluding remarks. Equation (2.1) gives the environment-independent forcing device. The introduction already explains that forcing restores regeneration in finite-dependent environments, leaving duration tails to control. Theorem 3.4 assumes the integrability condition; A3/A4 are quantitative drift conditions. This is prior art for regeneration, not a proof from qualitative one-direction escape alone. No claim that its full renewal or renormalization proofs were read.

**Guerra Aguilar, arXiv:1711.01258v3 (2018)**, [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01258), later Ann. Probab. (2019), DOI `10.1214/18-AOP1330`. Read introduction, Definitions 1.1–1.2, Theorem 1.3, and Remark 4.6 plus the next section's setup. The theorem assumes (T), strong mixing (SM) or (SMG), and a relation between mixing and ellipticity. Remark 4.6 explains that finite dependence permits exact regeneration and removes that extra relation. It does not remove (T). The historical named issue addressed is the mixing-environment analogue of Sznitman's transient conditions, discussed by Comets–Zeitouni Section 6(3); Guerra already makes progress on that issue, so simply invoking (T) would be prior-art overlap.

**Bramson–Zeitouni–Zerner (2006)**, [author primary manuscript](https://cims.nyu.edu/~zeitouni/pdf/trees38.pdf), [arXiv:math/0501533](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0501533). Read the introduction through the organization/construction sketch, and the open-problem statements. Theorem 3 is a stationary uniformly elliptic polynomially mixing example with positive probability of positive speed in each of two opposite directions. Its construction uses directed forests and growing insulation. The inspected theorem is neither finite-dependent nor a zero-speed trap. Its failure of the zero-one conclusion, together with the stipulated finite-range theorem, excludes it from the target class. The construction and mixing proofs were not read, so no independent claim about the precise dependence range of particular construction variables is made.

**Guo, Ann. IHP 50 (2014), 375–402**, [published primary PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPB_2014__50_2_375_0.pdf), DOI `10.1214/12-AIHP534`. Read introduction, Definition 1, condition (*), Theorem 2, and scope remarks (through original extracted line 158). The theorem is a conditional LLN and allows v_+=v_-=0. It therefore cannot establish the desired strict positivity even if its inclusion statement for finite-dependent environments is used. No implication from unconditional finite dependence to a conditional Gibbs density comparison is imported into I232's proofs.
