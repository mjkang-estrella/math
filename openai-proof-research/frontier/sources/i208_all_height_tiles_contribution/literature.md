## New full-read primary sources

**Heidi Burgiel and Vignon Oussa, Pairs of Full-Rank Lattices With Parallelepiped-Shaped Common Fundamental Domains**, [arXiv:1507.04671v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.04671v1), retained as `burgiel-oussa-2015v1.pdf`, 13 pages. The served PDF has a July 2015 arXiv identifier/header and an internal October 23, 2018 date; this discrepancy is recorded rather than silently resolved. Full text read in overlapping page chunks.

Proposition 3 gives a difference-body criterion for common parallelepipeds; Proposition 4 constructs them for triangular unipotent matrices; Proposition 5 adds families; Proposition 6 gives planar nonexistence examples. The fixed pair of lattices and equal covolumes are material hypotheses. This is stronger prior-art context than merely general measurable common domains, but it does not state I203's continuum covering theorem. It establishes that independent domain/lattice bases and shear-based common parallelograms are existing techniques.

**Fei Xue, On the lattice point covering problem in dimension 2**, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 26(2) (2019), P2.9, [publisher PDF](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v26i2p9/pdf/), retained as `xue-2019.pdf`, 11 pages. Full text read as pp. 1–5 and 6–11.

Theorem 6 relates meeting the integer lattice in every translated/rotated position to covering radii. Theorem 9 supplies a sufficient condition via Steiner symmetrization and inscribed shear parallelograms; Theorem 4 determines thresholds for specified regular polygons. This addresses a fixed integer lattice and uncoupled rotations, not the I203 optimization. The original Niven–Zuckerman (1967), its correction, Fáry (1950), and Henk–Tsintsifas (2007) sources were identified via references/search, not read. Xue's restatements of those results are not claimed as independently read primary originals.

**Darrin Speegle, From Fractional to Set Tilings for Pairs of Lattices**, [arXiv:2606.15297v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15297v1), retained as `speegle-2026v1.pdf`, 13 pages. Full text read as pp. 1–5, 6–10 and 11–13. Theorem 2.2 is an infinite integer-margin matrix rounding theorem. Theorem 3.1 extracts a possibly nonmeasurable set from the pointwise support of a function satisfying two exact lattice tiling identities with integer multiplicities. The final example obstructs the analogous extraction for three lattices. These exact-identity, two-lattice and measurability limitations preclude applying it to I203's continuum covering inequalities. No publication-status inference beyond the retrieved version is needed.

## Existing primary sources, read in place

**Mihail N. Kolountzakis, Lattice Tilings by Cubes: Whole, Notched and Extended** (1998), [publisher PDF](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/viewFile/v5i1r14/pdf/), `../i201_elliptic_covering/kolountzakis-1998-publisher.pdf`. Full 11-page extracted text read, with pp. 8–11 separately reread. Theorem 2 gives the lattice Fourier criterion; Theorem 7 and equations (13)–(20) supply the notched/signed-rectangle structure. For the positive-product parameters the square indicator minus a nonnegative rectangle indicator has constant lattice sum one. Thus the square covers, including arbitrary individual shear size. Theorem 8 treats the opposite-sign extended-cube branch. The independent coordinate matching in I203 is not stated there, but its entire pointwise covering mechanism is already available. Closed-set coverage follows by local finiteness and closure; that upgrade is paid source material.

**Sigrid Grepstad and Mihail N. Kolountzakis, Bounded common fundamental domains for two lattices**, [arXiv:2507.00604v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00604v1), `../i201_elliptic_covering/grepstad-kolountzakis-2025v1.pdf`. Read pp. 1–4 only: abstract, introduction, Theorems 1–2, the Gabor application and notation. Theorem 1 provides a bounded finite-polytopal domain for any two equal-volume lattices. No full-proof reading is claimed. No interpolation conclusion is present in the stated result.

**Sigrid Grepstad, Mihail N. Kolountzakis and Emmanuil Spyridakis, Bounded lattice tiles that pack with another lattice**, [arXiv:2508.07972v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07972v1), `../i201_elliptic_covering/grepstad-kolountzakis-spyridakis-2025v1.pdf`. Read pp. 1–4 only, through Theorem 1.2 and its containing-domain interpretation. A bounded tile for the denser lattice packs with the sparser lattice and can be included in a bounded domain of the latter. This gives a two-height cover at maximum covolume, but no single construction simultaneously handles all heights. The paper explicitly warns against obtaining such conclusions by arbitrary dilation of a chosen domain.

**Mihail N. Kolountzakis and Effie Papageorgiou, Functions tiling with several lattices**, [arXiv:2106.11701v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11701v1), `../i203_all_height_tiles/kolountzakis-papageorgiou-2021v1.pdf`. Read pp. 1–5: introduction, convolution construction, Theorems 2.1–2.3 and the start of the latter's proof. Not full-read in this review. This suffices to check the distinction between exact soft tiling identities and indicator covering, and the absence of a diameter-uniform continuum construction from the statements used. I do not rely on the unexamined later sections.

**Maria Loukaki and Emmanouil Spyridakis, Three subgroup common transversal in abelian groups**, [arXiv:2606.14319v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14319v1), `../i203_all_height_tiles/loukaki-spyridakis-2026v1.pdf`. Read pp. 1–4 only, including exact Theorems A–B, the lattice reduction and the start of §2. Not the full proof. Theorem B requires three equal-volume lattices with discrete sum. Three distinct heights of the elliptic path cannot all be maximum-volume heights: only the two endpoints have that volume. Its hypotheses do not yield a contrary obstruction or a solution here.

## Search scope, reformulations and limits

Searches were run on 2026-10-12 for simultaneous lattice covering, common parallelograms/fundamental domains, good pairs, continuous deformation/interpolation of covering lattices, affine paths, and rotating/contracting square or parallelogram covers. The useful expansion beyond I203's search set was the common-parallelepiped literature and lattice-point-covering literature. A recent fractional-to-set tiling paper was also found and read. Hits on ordinary simultaneous packing/covering constants, elliptic curves, combinatorial lattice paths and periodic frameworks do not have the needed family-of-lattices quantifier and were not used as evidence. Search snippets are leads, not proof sources, and are not byte-authenticated.

The exact reformulation used for comparison is elementary: with A=J and t=tan(theta), I+tJ=sec(theta)R_theta. Hence the covering condition is equivalent to

    cos(theta) R_(-theta) F + Gamma = R²,
    |theta|<=arctan(k).

It involves both rotation and a specified contraction; it is not the fixed-size all-rotation covering property or exact Steinhaus tiling. Alternatively, in the chosen F-coordinates the path is the line segment between an upper and a lower shear basis. Its intermediate determinant decreases below the endpoint determinant, so its interior constraints are coverings, not common fundamental-domain constraints. The report explains the exact substitution into the old square lemma.

No exhaustive search of all languages, historical geometry-of-numbers books, unpublished work or specialist knowledge is claimed. The 1967 original/correction, original Stein/Schmerl articles, Han–Wang, and Pfander–Rashkov–Wang were not fully read; their results are not silently treated as full-read inputs. A 2026 Spyridakis common-domain preprint was identified in search but not read or relied upon, since the actual 2025 common-domain theorem already suffices for the limited comparison made. Thus “no exact duplicate found” is not a novelty certificate.
