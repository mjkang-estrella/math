                                                   Abstract. We exhibit smooth polarized surfaces (Y, H) over C carrying no nonzero arith-
                                                   metically Cohen–Macaulay bundle of any rank. Equivalently, their section rings are three-
                                                   dimensional normal N-graded C-domains, with an isolated singularity at the vertex, admit-
                                                   ting no nonzero finitely generated graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay module. To the author’s
                                                   knowledge no such ring was previously known. Their existence contrasts with the theorem
                                                   of Hartshorne, Hochster and Peskine–Szpiro that a three-dimensional N-graded domain over a
                                                   perfect field of characteristic p > 0 always has one. As a consequence, in characteristic zero
                                                   Hochster’s small Cohen–Macaulay conjecture admits no graded refinement.
                                                      The nonexistence criterion is numerical: for |H| base-point-free with finite morphism, H 2 <
                                                   KY2 − 8χ(OY ) = τ (Y ), the signature of Y over C, leaves no nonzero E with H 1 (Y, E(tH)) = 0
                                                   for all t. For Ulrich bundles, being semistable, this is Bogomolov’s inequality; what is new
                                                   is that no stability hypothesis and no condition on the Hilbert polynomial are needed, the
                                                   Harder–Narasimhan filtration of E being compared instead with the Horrocks splitting of its
                                                   direct image on P2 . Hirzebruch’s Hesse surfaces with Hn = 4An − En , n ≥ 3, qualify. In
                                                   dimension m the obstruction reads (m + 1)H m ≥ (KX   2
                                                                                                          − 2c2 (X)) · H m−2 , the inequality Lopez
                                                   proved for Ulrich bundles; products and complete intersections inside them give examples in
                                                   every dimension ≥ 3, and the threefold examples show that the characteristic-p hypothesis in
                                                   the criterion of Shimomoto–Tavanfar is essential.




                                                                                         Introduction
                                              Hochster’s small Cohen–Macaulay conjecture asks whether every complete local ring admits
                                           a nonzero finitely generated module whose depth equals the dimension of the ring, a maximal
                                           Cohen–Macaulay (MCM) module [20, 21]. It holds in dimension ≤ 2 and is open in dimension
                                           ≥ 3, in characteristic zero even for localizations of affine domains at maximal ideals [36]. In
                                           dimension three and characteristic p > 0 it is known for complete weakly F -split rings [33], for
                                           complete local domains with algebraically closed residue field admitting a nonzero F -invariant
                                           integrable derivation [34, Thm. 3.4], for pseudo-graded rings [34], and, in graded form, for
                                           graded rings: a three-dimensional N-graded domain finitely generated over a perfect field of
                                           characteristic p has a graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay module. This theorem was obtained
                                           independently by Hartshorne, Hochster and Peskine–Szpiro, as recorded in [21, Thm. 1 and
                                           Cor. 2]; see [20, 32] and the account in [36].
                                              The purpose of this note is to show that the graded statement has no analogue in characteristic
                                           zero: there are three-dimensional normal N-graded C-domains, with an isolated singularity
                                           at the vertex, which admit no nonzero finitely generated graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay

one maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules are excluded but modules of higher rank exist; here

   The examples are section rings R(Y, H) = t≥0 H 0 (Y, OY (tH)) of polarized smooth surfaces,
and the mechanism is geometric. When |H| is base-point-free with finite associated morphism,
graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over R(Y, H) correspond exactly to the vector bun-
dles E on Y with H 1 (Y, E(tH)) = 0 for all t ∈ Z, the arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay (aCM)
bundles with respect to H (Lemma 3.2 below; for H very ample this is classical, cf. [15, 16];
the correspondence is characteristic-free; the new input for the nonexistence conclusion is Theo-
rem 1.1). For ample Q-divisors in characteristic p, Shimomoto–Tavanfar give a different existence
criterion: the existence of a graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay module is characterized by the
existence of a coherent sheaf with maximal Cohen–Macaulay stalks [36, Cor. 4.5(i)]. This is a
local criterion, not the aCM-bundle correspondence used here, and it is satisfied by OX on every
smooth X; Corollary 5.4 below shows that its nontrivial implication fails in characteristic zero.
That corollary is stated for smooth threefolds, rather than for the surfaces of Corollary 1.4, only
because [36, Cor. 4.5] is stated for dim X ≥ 3. The main result of this note, Theorem 1.1, assumes
no stability property whatever: for H ample and base-point-free, if H 2 < KY2 − 8χ(OY ), then
(Y, H) carries no aCM bundle at all, of any rank. For a semistable bundle the same conclusion
is a direct consequence of Bogomolov’s inequality, and is the argument by which [1] excludes Ul-
rich bundles, these being automatically semistable; what Theorem 1.1 adds is the unstable case
(Remark 2.8). Its proof pushes E forward to P2 by a finite projection, where Horrocks’ theorem
splits it, and compares the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of E with the splitting type through
a majorization inequality; Bogomolov’s inequality enters only through the Harder–Narasimhan
factors, and is the single ingredient of the proof that fails in characteristic p. In the language
of Boij–Söderberg theory, to which the Ulrich question of [10] was connected later, the cone of
cohomology tables of vector bundles on such a pair contains no nonzero table with vanishing
h1 -row (Remark 6.2).
   Over C the number KY2 − 8χ(OY ) is the signature τ (Y ) of the intersection form on H 2 (Y, Z),
so the obstruction reads: if (Y, H) carries an aCM bundle, then the degree of (Y, H) is at least
the signature of Y . By the classification of surfaces, τ (Y ) > 0 only for Y = P2 and for surfaces
of general type; since an ample divisor on P2 has H 2 ≥ 1 = τ (P2 ), the obstruction can only
occur on surfaces of general type of positive signature, and it is strongest, for a given χ(OY ),
on ball quotients (Remark 1.6). The Hesse surfaces Yn of Hirzebruch [19, 2], the (Z/n)11 -covers
of the plane blown up at the nine flexes of the Hesse pencil, branched along the twelve lines of
the Hesse arrangement and the nine exceptional curves, carry the divisor Hn = 4An − En of [1],
with Hn2 = 7n9 , while τ (Yn ) = (3n2 − 11)n9 . The base-point-freeness of Hn and the finiteness
of its morphism, which is all that Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 require, are proved in Section 4 from
the structure of the cover, so that the statements of Corollary 1.4 about the section rings are
independent of [1] (Remark 1.5); the very ampleness of Hn proved in [1] only identifies the
standard graded ring A(Yn , Hn ) of Theorem 1.3 with the homogeneous coordinate ring of Yn
itself. In scope the two papers differ: [1] constructs the pairs (Yn , Hn ) and excludes Ulrich
bundles on them and on further families of covers, whereas Theorem 1.1 applies to aCM bundles
of arbitrary rank, hence to all graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over the section rings,

   Throughout, S denotes either the section ring R or, when H is very ample, the homogeneous
coordinate ring A, and m = S+ . A graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay (MCM) module over S is
a nonzero finitely generated Z-graded S-module M with depthm M = 3, the depth being taken
at the irrelevant ideal: depthm M ≥ j means Hmi (M ) = 0 for i < j. Since the modules Hmi (M )
are m-torsion, they are unchanged by localization at m, so depthm M is also the depth of Mm
over the local ring Sm .
Theorem 1.3. If H 2 < KY2 −8χ(OY ), then R(Y, H) admits no graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay
module. If moreover H is very ample, the same holds for A(Y, H). Under the same hypothesis
neither ring is Cohen–Macaulay: depthm R = 2, while depthA+ A = 2 if A = R (i.e. if Y ⊂ Ph0 −1
is projectively normal) and depthA+ A = 1 otherwise. In both cases the punctured spectrum is
smooth, so the vertex is an isolated, non-Cohen–Macaulay singularity.

Corollary 1.4. Let Yn (n ≥ 3) be the Hesse surface of exponent n of Section 4: the smooth

is drawn here about ungraded maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over the localization of these

Remark 1.5 (provenance). Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 are proved in Sections 2 and 3 for arbitrary
(Y, H) as above. The surfaces Yn are Hirzebruch’s [19]; the divisor Hn was introduced in [1]. The
statements of Corollary 1.4 concerning R(Yn , Hn ) use nothing from [1]: the base-point-freeness
of Hn and the finiteness of φHn are proved in Proposition 4.1 from the structure of the cover,
and the invariants Hn2 , KY2n and χ(OYn ) are derived in Section 4 for every n (for n = 3 all
invariants are recomputed). The very ampleness of Hn proved in [1, Thm. 1.2] enters only the
statement about A(Yn , Hn ), which it defines, and is quoted there as a hypothesis.

                                           6. Remarks
Remark 6.1 (Ulrich bundles and Ulrich modules). Ulrich bundles on (Y, H) are the bundles
E with H i (Y, E(−jH)) = 0 for all i and j = 1, 2; equivalently, by the Hilbert-polynomial
characterization [10, Prop. 2.1 and Cor. 2.2], the aCM bundles with χ(E(tH)) = 12 rk(E)H 2 (t +
1)(t + 2); for H very ample they correspond to the graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules
over A(Y, H) whose minimal graded free resolution over the ambient polynomial ring P =
Symk H 0 (Y, OY (H)) is linear and begins in degree zero. The linearity in this characterization
refers to the P -free resolution; the A(Y, H)-free resolution need not be linear: when A = A(Y, H)
is a hypersurface ring P/(f ) with deg f = q ≥ 2, for instance, the minimal A-free resolution
of an Ulrich module is the infinite resolution, 2-periodic up to a grading shift by q, associated
with a matrix factorization φψ = ψφ = f · id in which φ is linear and ψ has degree q − 1,
so that its maps alternate between degrees 1 and q − 1 (1 and 2 for a cubic surface, 1 and 1
for a quadric). In the present setting that equivalence is immediate from Lemma 2.2: writing

   The existence of an Ulrich sheaf with full support on every projective variety was asked in
[10]; see [4] for an introduction. On a surface an Ulrich bundle is semistable [8, Thm. 2.9], so
that Bogomolov’s inequality forces H 2 ≥ KY2 − 8χ(OY ) ([3] for Picard rank one, [1] in general);
the non-existence of Ulrich bundles on the Hesse pairs is [1, Thm. 1.2], proved by Bogomolov’s
inequality for the (semistable) Ulrich bundle itself, and Lopez [27] obtains varieties of every
dimension without Ulrich bundles from the numerical obstruction that Section 5 extends to
aCM bundles. Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 5.1 remove the semistability, at the cost of the
majorization argument, and exclude all finitely generated graded MCM modules. An Ulrich
bundle is aCM with a prescribed Hilbert polynomial and is automatically semistable; the aCM
condition prescribes neither the Hilbert polynomial nor a stability property.
   Accordingly, whereas the existence of Ulrich bundles is a delicate question, aCM bundles are
abundant wherever they have been studied — on cubic surfaces they have been studied in every
rank and classified in rank two [7, 14], and on every arithmetically Cohen–Macaulay embedded
variety OX is one, its coordinate ring being a graded maximal Cohen–Macaulay module over
