## 5. Route A — explicit corridor identity minors in the actual map

Retain H's exact parameters. Let s be the largest integer ≤√n of n's parity, `k=(n−s)/2`, `m=(n+s)/2`, `v=kn²`, `u=mn²`, and

`a=ceil(v/(√n−1)), α=a/(a+v), ρ=k/m, b=ceil(u α^ρ/(1−α^ρ)), D=h(v,a)h(u,b), h(r,t)=binom(r+t−1,t)`.

Use its schedule `VU^(ℓ_1)…VU^(ℓ_k)` with each ℓ_i∈{1,2}. Thus the first layer is V, last is U, and there are no adjacent V layers. `F_f=f(∂_V,U)` goes from bidegree (a,b) to (a−k,b+m).

**A106 (author proof; technical explicit all-field minor).** For every corridor width `1≤t≤n`, set

`v_c=t+(k−1)t²`, `u_c=t+(m−1)t²`.

Provided `a≥k,b≥0` and the complementary variable sets are nonempty (as they are for H's sufficiently large n), the matrix of `F_IMM` over the integers contains an identity submatrix of order

`J_t = t^(2k−1) h(v−v_c,a−k) h(u−u_c,b)`.                         (A)

In particular its rank over **every** field is at least J_t. The same minor works for the contraction map in §6.

**Proof.** Label corridor vertices 1,…,t. Active V edges are the t first-layer edges with left endpoint 1 and the t² edges in each other V layer with both endpoints in the corridor. Active U edges are all t² corridor edges except that the last U layer has just t edges ending at 1. All remaining ambient variables, including other first/last-layer entries, are ballast variables.

Choose independently one active edge in each V layer. There are `t·(t²)^(k−1)=t^(2k−1)` such V monomials. They can all be completed to paths: a one-U gap has its endpoints already determined, and a two-U gap may use internal vertex 1. Fix that completion for each V monomial. Conversely its U monomial uniquely determines every V edge: each V layer's endpoints are read from its neighboring U edges, with the first endpoint fixed at 1. Distinct V monomials therefore receive distinct U completions.

For each chosen V monomial q and each pair of ballast monomials r,z of degrees a−k,b, take column `qr ⊗ z` and row `r ⊗ u(q)z`. Consider any possible entry between such selected columns and rows. Every selected output has active V degree zero, while a selected input has active V degree k. Any contributing path must therefore differentiate all k active input variables. No ballast V variable is differentiated, so the V ballast monomials must match. Similarly selected inputs have active U degree zero and selected outputs active U degree m; all m multiplied U variables must be active, and the U ballast must match. The selected output completion then determines the path's V monomial uniquely. Hence there are no off-diagonal entries. On the diagonal all differentiated active exponents are 1 and the path coefficient is 1; the entry is exactly 1. This proves (A) over Z, without reduction of an unspecified minor. ∎

**Quantitative consequence.** Taking `t=floor(n^(1/4))` gives

`rank_F F_IMM ≥ D exp(−C_A n)`                                  (A1)

for an absolute C_A, all sufficiently large n and every field F.

To check the budget directly, `t≥n^(1/4)/2`, `v_c/v,u_c/u=O(n^(−3/2))`, and `a,b=Θ(n^(5/2))`. The exact binomial ratios give

`h(v,a−k)/h(v,a) ≥ ((a−k+1)/(a+v))^k ≥ α^k exp(−2k²/a)`

and, for `c/r≤1/2`,

`h(r−c,A)/h(r,A) = ∏_(j=0)^(A−1)(1−c/(r+j)) ≥ exp(−2Ac/r)`.

Consequently

`J_t/D ≥ t^(2k−1) α^k exp(−2k²/a−2a v_c/v−2b u_c/u)`.

Use `α≥n^(−1/2)` and the lower bound on t. The power prefactor is at least `2^(−2k+1)n^(−1/4)`, and the remaining exponent is −O(n). This proves (A1) with absolute constants. Unrestricted circuit coefficients never enter this rank statement.

**Why this paid minor is insufficient, even after tuning t.** The certificate size itself remains exponentially small in n. For t>0 put `x=t²/√n`. Reverse the previous binomial estimates using `1−y≤exp(−y)`:

`J_t/D ≤ t^(2k−1) (a/(a+v−1))^k · exp(−(a−k)v_c/(v+a−k)−b u_c/(u+b))`.

Uniformly for `1≤t≤n`, `v_c≥(k−1)t²`, `u_c≥(m−1)t²`. H's parameter estimates imply, for all sufficiently large n, that the exponent penalty is at least `.8 n x`; the prefactor logarithm is at most `k log x−log t+o(1)`. Here `k/n∈[.49,.5]`. For λ in that interval, the maximum of `λ log x−.8x` over x>0 is `λ(log(λ/.8)−1)<−.7`. Thus `J_t/D≤exp(−.7n+o(1))` uniformly. This is an obstruction to **this corridor certificate**, not an upper bound on the full IMM rank.

The source circuit bound is only `2S²D exp(C√n)n^(−√n/100)`. Comparing it with (A1) gives no asymptotic gate lower bound: the −O(n) loss overwhelms √n log n. Simply choosing a wider corridor, adding the sizes of overlapping minors, or invoking rank subadditivity in the wrong direction does not fix this.

## 6. Route B — contraction modules, a full circuit comparison, and rank torsion

A naive Hasse substitution fails multiplicativity: on one variable, `D^[1]D^[1]=2D^[2]`, whereas the substitution of x² as `D^[2]` omits that coefficient; in characteristic two the discrepancy is immediate. Instead use a different module, and pay its entire circuit comparison.

For each degree A let `Γ_V(A)` have basis `e_ν` indexed by weak compositions ν of A into v parts. Define commuting contraction operators

`c_i e_ν = e_(ν−e_i)` if ν_i>0, and zero otherwise.

They are the duals of multiplication on ordinary symmetric powers; this definition requires no factorial inverses. Keep ordinary multiplication on U. For every polynomial Q define `T^c_Q=Q(c_V,U)` on the full graded module, and set

`F^c_g=(g_[k,m])(c_V,U): Γ_V(a)⊗K[U]_b → Γ_V(a−k)⊗K[U]_(b+m)`,
`R_c(g)=rank F^c_g`.

**B106 (author proof; technical complete upper comparison).** Over every field, with arbitrary circuit constants and the exact shared-gate convention,

`R_c(g) ≤ 2S²D exp(C√n)n^(−√n/100)`                            (B)

for every circuit in the target model computing homogeneous g of degree n, with an absolute C and threshold.

**Proof.** Contractions on distinct variables commute, as do their products with U multiplication. The assignment from the polynomial ring to endomorphisms is therefore an algebra homomorphism, including repeated powers and all characteristic-p identities. The degree-A module has exactly h(v,A) dimensions. Rank is subadditive under addition and unchanged by nonzero scalar multiplication.

For homogeneous factors Q_j of degrees e_j with Σe_j=n, resolve each into bidegrees `(i_j,e_j−i_j)` and consider assignments with Σi_j=k. Set `δ_j=i_j−λe_j`, `λ=k/n`. Apply factors with δ_j>0 first. The resulting contribution factors through `Γ_V(a−I)⊗K[U]_(b+J)`, with the same `I=Σ_(δ>0)i_j≤k` and `J=Σ_(δ>0)(e_j−i_j)≤m` as H. Its dimension ratio is bounded by `2α^(I−ρJ)=2∏q^|δ_j|`, with `q=α^((1+ρ)/2)`. Therefore

`R_c(∏Q_j) ≤ 2D ∏ H_(e_j)`, `H_e=Σ_(i=0)^e q^|i−λe|`.

This proves the entire algebraic interface needed for H's product estimate without Leibniz, differentiation identities, or characteristic restrictions. Its real integer-degree estimates then apply unchanged: all-small factor degrees give `2D exp(C√n)n^(−√n/20)`; if one factor has degree at least `n/(4s)`, expand exactly that occurrence into at most S products of linear forms. Their degree-one contractions give the same `H_1` factors; other repeated occurrences remain unexpanded. The estimate is `2SD exp(C√n)n^(−3√n/160)`. At most S distinct predecessors of the output sum give (B) after weakening the exponent to 1/100. Constants, zero gates, degree-zero factors and repeated product wires are handled exactly as in H's source proof. This completes both cases and the shared-circuit count. ∎

Frobenius is respected, not ignored: `(c_x+c_y)^p=c_x^p+c_y^p`, and `c_x^p` need not be zero. In contrast ordinary ∂_x^p is zero on the ordinary polynomial module in characteristic p. The module change removes that particular nilpotence without claiming all rank obstructions disappear.

**B1 (rational comparison and specialization).** Over Q, the degreewise map `e_ν↦V^ν/ν!` conjugates contractions to ordinary derivatives, so `rank_Q F^c_IMM=rank_Q F_IMM`. Thus H supplies `r_n:=rank_Q F^c_IMM≥D exp(−C_H√n)`. For a field of characteristic p>a the same factorial conjugation is valid between the two maps **over that field**. It does not equate either rank with its rational rank.

In every characteristic, ordinary rank is at most contraction rank. To prove this, deform the lowering coefficient at positive occupation j from j to j+z, using an indeterminate z. These lowering maps commute. At z=0 they give ordinary derivatives. Over K(z), rescaling e_ν by the inverse product of `(j+z)` for `1≤j≤ν_i` conjugates them to coefficient-one contractions. Rank cannot increase under z=0 specialization. The U operators are unchanged. This pays a useful dominance statement but still supplies no finite-field lower rank near r_n.

**B2 (explicit torsion obstruction).** Let `f_r=Σ_(i=1)^r v_i u_i=IMM_(r,2)` on its active endpoint variables. On the multidegree sector in which every pair `(v_i,u_i)` has combined exponent 1, take V degree p and U degree p−1, with `r=2p−1`. The contraction matrix has columns indexed by p-subsets S, rows by (p−1)-subsets T, and entry `1_(T⊂S)`. Every row has exactly p ones, so the all-ones column vector is in its kernel modulo p.

This square matrix is nonsingular over Q. Here is a proof rather than a numerical assertion. On the real Boolean subset spaces let D delete one element and U add one, with orthonormal subset bases, so U=D*. On level j, direct counting gives `DU−UD=(r−2j)I`. If Dx=0 at level j=p with r=2p−1, then

`||Ux||²−||Dx||²=(r−2p)||x||²=−||x||²`,

which forces x=0. The two middle levels have equal dimension, proving nonsingularity. This real proof establishes the rational rank of this **explicit** integer block, while the mod-p kernel proves its actual rank drop.

For p=2 it is the matrix with zero diagonal and ones off diagonal, determinant 2. For p=3 it is a 10×10 block in a degree-two IMM polynomial, already showing why p greater than the degree of the polynomial does not ensure rational rank persists. No claim is made that such a small block controls the full asymptotic IMM rank.

There is also a literal determinant-2 submatrix in H's balanced map for all sufficiently large n. Freeze all path edges except the first VU pair, let that pair traverse three possible intermediate vertices and return to vertex 1, and choose the three columns corresponding to two distinct active V variables and the complementary active U variable. Select the three output rows with one active V variable and the two complementary U variables. Give every other V path edge occupation 1, and add the required a−k−1 and b−1 degrees to inactive endpoint variables. A selected row–column shift determines its entire path; paths using other edges cannot contribute. The resulting three-by-three submatrix is exactly the same determinant-2 matrix, for ordinary and contraction operators. Thus the actual map is **not totally unimodular**. This refutes a universal-unimodularity route, not the existence of some very large unit minor.

Edge rescaling cannot repair a bad fixed minor. Multiplying variable x_e by a nonzero parameter z_e scales a path term by its edge product. In any determinant term the total signed shift equals the sum of its fixed row exponents minus fixed column exponents. Hence every permutation term has the same z-monomial factor (with the V sign reversed), so a minor is multiplied by a monomial, not turned into a polynomial with new independent coefficients. Equivalently this is diagonal row/column conjugation. Divisibility of its integer coefficient by p persists. This rules out a simple random edge-weight cure.

## 7. Quantitative large-prime partial, with all costs explicit

This is retained as a technical consequence, not a substantial nomination and not the all-field target.

**C106.** Let `L_n=n^(n−1)` and let r_n be as in B1. For every prime p and every field F of characteristic p,

`rank_F F^c_IMM ≥ max(0, r_n(1−log L_n/(2 log p)))`.            (C)

Consequently there are absolute c=1/400 and n_0 such that the requested gate lower bound holds in the restricted regime

`n≥n_0,  char F=p ≥ n^(n−1)`.

**Proof.** In ordinary monomial/contraction bases the matrix A of `F^c_IMM` has entries 0 or 1. A row–column shift determines every edge of a path, so at most one path contributes to any entry. Each column has at most L_n nonzero entries, the total number of paths. Take any nonzero r_n-minor B over the integers. Its column Euclidean norms are at most √L_n, so Hadamard gives

`0<|det B|≤L_n^(r_n/2)`.

If `s=rank_(F_p) A`, then `rank_(F_p) B≤s`. Gaussian elimination over the local ring `Z_(p)`, pivoting on units, or equivalently Smith reduction over Z, shows `p^(r_n−s)` divides det B when s<r_n: after all unit pivots the remaining square block has all entries divisible by p. Thus

`(r_n−s) log p ≤ (r_n/2) log L_n`,

which proves (C). Extending from F_p to any F preserves matrix rank. The case s≥r_n and the vacuous negative right side cause no issue.

When p≥L_n, the factor is at least 1/2. B1 and B give

`(D/2) exp(−C_H√n) ≤ 2S²D exp(C√n)n^(−√n/100)`.

Hence `S≥(1/2) exp(−(C_H+C)√n/2)n^(√n/200)`, which is at least `n^(√n/400)` after one absolute increase of n_0. All constants are field-independent in this stated regime. ∎

More generally any positive factor in (C) can be inserted into this comparison with its square root; there is no unrecorded prime dependence. For a fixed positive-characteristic field, `p≥n^(n−1)` eventually fails. Therefore C106 cannot establish an asymptotic statement for even one fixed prime, much less all primes with one threshold. It is stronger quantitative bookkeeping than merely saying “finitely many bad primes for each n,” but uses only H's rational rank, the paid contraction upper comparison and elementary determinant divisibility. No nomination follows.

## 8. Exact remaining bridge and failed shortcuts

The deepest paid combination is (B) plus the explicit all-field minor (A), and the large-prime estimate (C). The exact missing sufficient inequality is

`rank_(F_p) F^c_IMM ≥ D exp(−C_0√n)`                           (U)

for one absolute C_0, every prime p and all sufficiently large n, with H's exact balanced schedule and degrees. The analogous estimate for the ordinary source map would also suffice. Neither is proved here. A somewhat weaker lower estimate with logarithmic loss strictly smaller than `(√n log n)/100` would also suffice to give some absolute c>0; the corridor loss O(n) misses even that weaker threshold.

A new pivot mechanism must select roughly `D exp(−O(√n))` independent rows and columns, perhaps with prime-dependent choices, rather than relying on all minors being units. An alternative measure must establish its own all-field lower bound while preserving (or replacing quantitatively) the fully paid circuit estimate. The contraction route already pays the latter, but rational positivity and coefficient-one entries do not pay the former. The Boolean incidence blocks show exactly where cancellation can survive the module change.

Forbes's logical transfer cannot reverse the rank inequality: vanishing integer minors stay zero modulo p, whereas nonzero integer minors can die. Trace positivity, complex square-root factorial bases, and an unspecified integer minor are not finite-characteristic proofs. Small-characteristic Frobenius and repeated derivatives have been accounted for explicitly above, and arbitrary coefficients cause no extra hypothesis in the upper estimate. No counterexample to the target circuit lower bound itself has been found.
