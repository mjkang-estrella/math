We say that convex sets A, B satisfy the p-Brunn–Minkowski (or simply p-BM inequality) if
                                             p           p             p
(4)                    |λ · A +p (1 − λ) · B| n ≥ λ|A| n + (1 − λ)|B| n .
It was shown by Firey [29] that (4) holds for all convex bodies containing the origin and p ≥ 1.
As in the classical case corresponding to p = 1, (4) implies the uniqueness of a solution to
a variant of the Minkowski problem, the so-called p-Minkowski problem (see explanations
in [6], Remark 9.47).
Unlike p > 1, the case p < 1 contains many open questions. First, it is well-known that (4)
fails even in the class of convex sets. A natural assumption for (4) is that A, B are symmetric
convex sets. For the non-symmetric case, the p-Brunn-Minkowski inequality with 0 < p < 1
is, in general, not true, because it is known that the corresponding p-Minkowski problem
can have many solutions (see Chen–Li–Zhu [15]). For p < 0 the inequality (4) fails even for
symmetric sets (see [6], Subsection 9.4, Chen–Li-Zhu [15], Li–Liu–Lu [47]).
It was conjectured in the seminal paper [8] of Böröczky–Lutwak–Zhang–Yang that (4) holds
for all symmetric convex sets and p ∈ [0, 1). Note that the validity of (4) for some p = p0
implies (4) for all p > p0 ; thus, the strongest version of the conjectured inequality (4) is
the 0-BM inequality, which is understood in the limiting sense and is called the logarithmic
Brunn–Minkowski (log Brunn–Minkowski) inequality.
In this paper, we work only with a local version of (4). In the case p = 0, this local version
was derived by Colesanti, the second-named author, and Marsiglietti [18] (Theorem 6.5), and
for other p, it was derived by Emanuel Milman and the first-named author in [44]. It was
shown later in (Chen–Huang–Li–Liu [14], Putterman [54]) that the local version is equivalent
to the global one.

D. Hilbert in his proof of the classical Brunn–Minkowski inequality, but this fact seems to
be not well-known, and the local Brunn–Minkowski inequality was rediscovered by Colesanti
in [17]. The general local p-BM inequality established in [44] has the form

Let us make a short overview of the results about p-Brunn–Minkowski inequality. The log-
Brunn–Minkowski conjecture (p = 0) for symmetric sets was advertised and verified for the
plane in Böröczky–Lutwak–Yang–Zhang [8] (their result was slightly generalized in Ma [48],
and an alternative proof of the planar case was given in Putterman [54]). The necessary
and sufficient conditions for the existence of a solution to the log-BM problem were also
outlined in [8]. The third-named author has proved the log-BM conjecture for complex
convex bodies [55]. Saroglou [56], [57] extended the consideration of the problem to all
log-concave measures and studied relations to the celebrated B-conjecture from the 90s,
popularized by Latala [45]. In particular, he showed that an affirmative solution to the log-
Brunn–Minkowski conjecture implies an affirmative solution to the B-conjecture. In addition,
an affirmative solution to the strong B-conjecture for the uniform measure on the cube (in
any dimension) implies an affirmative solution to the log-Brunn–Minkowski problem. In
fact, before it was conjectured, the log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for unconditional convex
bodies had already been verified by Cordero-Erausquin, Fradelizi, Maurey [20], and was
later re-proved by Saroglou. Colesanti–Livshyts–Marsiglietti [18] derived the local form of
the log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture from the global form, and verified that it is correct for
balls, while Kolesnikov–Milman [43] proved the local p-Brunn-Minkowksi to be true for all
symmetric convex sets with p ∈ (p(n), 1], where p(n) < 1 and limn→∞ p(n) = 1. They also
proved it for some special types of sets, including lnp -balls within some range of p depending on
