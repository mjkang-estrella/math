Abstract.
We consider a nonlinear Schrödinger equation with double power nonlinearity
i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{d}}u+\epsilon|u|^{p-1}u=0,\ \ \epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\},\ \ 1<p<\frac{4}{d}
in \mathbb{R}^{d} ( d=1,2,3 ). Classical variational arguments ensure that H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) data with \|u_{0}\|_{2}<\|Q\|_{2} lead to global in time solutions, where Q is the ground state of the mass critical problem ( \epsilon=0 ). We are interested by the threshold dynamic \|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2} and in particular by the existence of finite time blow up minimal solutions. For \epsilon=0 , such an object exists thanks to the explicit conformal symmetry, and is in fact unique from the seminal work [22]. For \epsilon=-1 , simple variational arguments ensure that minimal mass data lead to global in time solutions. We investigate in this paper the case \epsilon=1 , exhibiting a new class of minimal blow up solutions with blow up rates deeply affected by the double power nonlinearity. The analysis adapts the recent approach [31] for
the construction of minimal blow up elements.
Key words and phrases: blow-up, nonlinear Schrödinger equation, double power nonlinearity, minimal mass, critical exponent

1. Introduction
We consider the following double power nonlinear Schrödinger equation in \mathbb{R}^{d}
{\rm(NLS)}\ \ \left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{d}}u+\epsilon|u|^{p-1}u=0,\\
u_{|t=0}=u_{0},\end{array}\right.\ \ 1<p<1+\frac{4}{d},\ \ \epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}.
(1)
This model corresponds to a subcritical perturbation of the classical mass critical problem \epsilon=0 which rules out the scaling symmetry of the problem. It is well-known (see e.g [6] and the references therein) that for any u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) , there exists a unique maximal solution u\in\mathcal{C}((-T_{\star},T^{\star}),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})) \cap \mathcal{C}^{1}((-T_{\star},T^{\star}),H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})) of (1). Moreover, the mass (i.e. L^{2} norm) and energy E of the solution are conserved by the flow where:
E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2+\frac{4}{d}}\lVert u\rVert_{2+\frac{4}{d}}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}-\epsilon\frac{1}{p+1}\lVert u\rVert_{p+1}^{p+1}.
Moreover, there holds the blow up criterion:
T^{\star}<+\infty\ \ \mbox{implies}\ \ \lim_{t\uparrow T^{\star}}\lVert\nabla u(t)\rVert_{2}=+\infty.
(2)
In this paper, we are interested in the derivation of a sharp global existence criterion for (1) in connection with the existence of minimal mass blow up solutions of (1).
1.1. The mass critical problem
Let us briefly recall the structure of the mass critical problem \epsilon=0 . In this case, the scaling symmetry
u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
acts on the set of solutions and leaves the mass invariant
\|u_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}=\|u(\lambda^{2}t,\cdot)\|_{2}.
From variational argument [32], the unique ([3, 14]) up to symmetry ground state solution to
-\Delta Q+Q-|Q|^{\frac{4}{d}}Q=0,\ \ Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \ Q>0,\ \ \mbox{$Q$ radial}
attains the best constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
\|u\|^{2+\frac{4}{d}}_{{2+\frac{4}{d}}}\leq C\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{4}{d}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2},
so that
\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \ E_{\rm crit}=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2+\frac{4}{d}}\lVert u\rVert_{2+\frac{4}{d}}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\left[1-\left(\frac{\|u\|_{2}}{\|Q\|_{2}}\right)^{\frac{4}{d}}\right].
(3)
Together with the conservation of mass and energy and the blow up criterion (2), this implies the global existence of all solutions with data \|u_{0}\|_{2}<\|Q\|_{2} . In fact, there holds scattering, see [10] and references therein.
At the threshold \|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2} , the pseudo-conformal symmetry
\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{d}{2}}}u\left(\frac{1}{t},\frac{x}{t}\right)e^{i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}
(4)
applied to the solitary wave solution u(t,x)=Q(x)e^{it} yields the existence of the following explicit minimal blow up solution
S(t,x)=\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{d}{2}}}Q\left(\frac{x}{|t|}\right)e^{-i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|t|}}e^{\frac{i}{|t|}},\quad\|S(t)\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2},\quad\|\nabla S(t)\|_{2}\mathop{\sim}_{t\sim 0^{-}}\frac{1}{|t|}.
(5)
From [22], minimal blow up elements are classified in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) in the following sense
\|u(t)\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ T^{*}<+\infty\ \ \mbox{imply}\ \ u\equiv S
up to the symmetries of the flow. Note that the minimal blow up dynamic (5) can be extended to the super critical mass case \|u_{0}\|_{2}>\|Q\|_{2} (see [5]) and that it corresponds to an unstable threshold dynamics between global in time scattering solutions and finite time blow up solutions in the stable blow up regime
\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}\mathop{\sim}_{t\sim T^{*}}\sqrt{\frac{\log|\log|T^{*}-t||}{T^{*}-t}}.
(6)
We refer to [26] and references therein for an overview of the existing literature for the L^{2} critical blow up problem.
1.2. The case \epsilon=-1
Let us now consider the case of a defocusing perturbation. First, there are no solitary waves with subcritical mass \|u_{0}\|_{2}<\|Q\|_{2} from a standard Pohozaev integration by parts argument. At the threshold, we claim:
Lemma 1 (Global existence at threshold for \epsilon=-1 ).
Let \epsilon=-1 .
Let u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) with \|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2} , then the solution of (1) is global and bounded in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) .
The proof follows from standard concentration compactness argument, see Appendix A. The global existence criterion of Lemma 1 is sharp in the sense that for all \alpha^{*}>0 , we can build an H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) finite time blow up solution to (7) with \|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2}+\alpha^{*} and blow up speed given by the log-log law (6). This is a consequence of the strong structural stability of the log log regime and the proof would follow the lines of [28, 29, 30].
1.3. The case \epsilon=1
We now turn to the case \epsilon=1 for the rest of the paper, i.e. we consider the model
i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{d}}u+|u|^{p-1}u=0\quad\hbox{where}\quad 1<p<1+\frac{4}{d}.
(7)
First, from mass and energy conservation, using (3) and (72), H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) solutions with \|u_{0}\|_{2}<\|Q\|_{2} are global and bounded in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . However, large time scattering is not true in general, even for small L^{2} solutions, since there exist arbitrarily small solitary waves.
Lemma 2 (Small solitary waves).
For all M\in(0,\|Q\|_{2}) , there exists \omega(M)>0 and a Schwartz radially symmetric solution of
\Delta Q_{M}-\omega(M)Q_{M}+Q_{M}^{1+\frac{4}{d}}+Q_{M}^{p}=0,\ \ \|Q_{M}\|_{2}=M.
The proof follows from classical variational methods, see Appendix B.
The main result of this paper is the existence of a minimal mass blow up solution for (7), in contrast with the defocusing case \epsilon=-1 .
Theorem 1 (Existence of a minimal blow up element).
Let d=1,2,3 and 1<p<1+\frac{4}{d} . Then for all energy level E_{0}\in\mathbb{R} , there exist t_{0}<0 and a radially symmetric Cauchy data u(t_{0})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) with
\|u(t_{0})\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2},\ \ E(u(t_{0}))=E_{0},
such that the corresponding solution u(t) of (7) blows up at time T^{*}=0 with speed:
\|\nabla u(t)\|_{2}=\frac{C(p)+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)}{|t|^{\sigma}}
(8)
for some universal constants
\quad\sigma=\frac{4}{4+d(p-1)}\in\left(\tfrac{1}{2},1\right),\ \ C(p)>0.
Comments on the result.
1. On the existence of minimal elements.
Since the pioneering work [22], it has long been believed that the existence of a minimal blow up bubble was related to the exceptional pseudo conformal symmetry (4), or at least to the existence of a sufficiently sharp approximation of it, see [2, 16]. However, a new methodology to construct minimal mass elements for a inhomogeneous (NLS) problem, non perturbative of critical (NLS), was developed in [31], and later successfully applied to problems without any sort of pseudo conformal symmetry, [4, 12, 19]. More generally, the heart of the matter is to be able to compute
the trajectory of the solution on the soliton manifold, see [13, 18] for related problems for two solitary waves motion. The present paper adapts this approach which relies on the direct computation of the blow up speed and the control of non dispersive bubbles as in [15].
Observe that the blow up speed (8) is quite surprising since it approaches the self simiar blow up speed {|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}} as p\to\left(1+\frac{4}{d}\right)^{-} .
2. Uniqueness. A delicate question investigated in [4, 19, 31] is the uniqueness of the minimal blow up element. Such a uniqueness statement should involve Galilean drifts since the Galilean symmetry applied to (7) is an L^{2} isometry and automatically induces minimal elements with non trivial momentum. Uniqueness issues lie within the general question of classifying the compact elements of the flow in the Kenig-Merle road map [11]. A more limited question is to determine the global behavior of the minimal element for negative time, which is poorly understood in general. Here, at least in the case E_{0}\geq 0 , one can see from Virial type estimates that the solution is global in negative time.
3. Detailed structure of the singular bubble. The analysis provides the following detailed structure of the blow up bubble
u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}(t)}Q\left(\frac{x}{\lambda(t)}\right)e^{-i\sigma\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}e^{i\gamma(t)}+v\left(t,{x}\right)
(9)
where Q is the mass critical ground state, and
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\lVert v(t)\rVert_{2}=0,\quad\lambda(t)\sim C_{p}{|t|^{\sigma}}\quad\hbox{as $t\to 0^{-}$},
for some constant C_{p}>0 .
Note also that the dimension restriction d\in\{1,2,3\} is for the sake of simplicity but not essential.
The construction of the minimal blow up element for (1) can be viewed as part of a larger program of understanding what kind of blow up speeds are possible for (NLS) type models. Let us repeat that log-log type solutions with super critical mass can be constructed for (1), but then the question becomes: do these examples illustrate all possible blow up types, at least near the ground state profile? The recent series of works [19, 20, 21] for the mass critical gKdV equation indicate that this is a delicate problem, and that the role played by the topology used to measure the perturbation is essential. More generally, symmetry breaking perturbations are very common in nonlinear analysis, and while they are expected to be lower order for generic stable blow up dynamics, our analysis shows that they can dramatically influence the structure of unstable threshold dynamics such as in our case minimal blow up bubbles.

1.4. Notation
Let us collect the main notation used throughout the paper. For the sake of simplicity, we work in the radial setting only. The L^{2} scalar product and L^{q} norm ( q\geq 1 ) are denoted by
\left(u,v\right)_{2}=\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(x)\bar{v}(x)dx\right),\quad\|u\|_{q}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.
We fix the notation:
f(z)=|z|^{\frac{4}{d}}z;\quad g(z)=|z|^{p-1}z;\quad F(z)=\frac{1}{\frac{4}{d}+2}|z|^{\frac{4}{d}+2};\quad G(z)=\frac{1}{p+1}|z|^{p+1}.
Identifying \mathbb{C} with \mathbb{R}^{2} , we denote the differential of these functions by df , dg , dF and dG . Let \Lambda be the generator of L^{2} -scaling i.e.
\Lambda=\frac{d}{2}+y\cdot\nabla.
The linearized operator close to Q comes as a matrix
L_{+}:=-\Delta+1-\left(1+\frac{4}{d}\right)Q^{\frac{4}{d}},\qquad L_{-}:=-\Delta+1-Q^{\frac{4}{d}}.
and the generalized kernel of
\begin{pmatrix}0&L_{-}\\
-L_{+}&0\end{pmatrix}
is non-degenerate and spanned by
the symmetries of the problem (see [14, 33] for the original results and [8] for a short proof). It is completely described in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) by the relations (we define \rho as the unique radial solution to L_{+}\rho=|y|^{2}Q )
L_{-}Q=0,\quad L_{+}\Lambda Q=-2Q,\quad L_{-}|y|^{2}Q=-4\Lambda Q,\quad L_{+}\rho=|y|^{2}Q.
(10)
Denote by \mathcal{Y} the set of radially symmetric functions f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) such that
\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\quad\exists C_{\alpha},\ \kappa_{\alpha}>0,\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}(1+|x|)^{\kappa_{\alpha}}Q(x).
It follows from the kernel properties of L_{+} and L_{-} , and from well-known properties of the Helmholtz kernel (see [1] for the properties of Helmholtz kernel (i.e. Bessel and Hankel functions) and [9, Appendix A] or proof of Lemma 3.2 in [27] for related arguments) that
\displaystyle\forall g\in\mathcal{Y},\ \exists f_{+}\in\mathcal{Y},\ L_{+}f_{+}=g,
(11)
\displaystyle\forall g\in\mathcal{Y},\ \left(g,Q\right)_{2}=0,\ \exists f_{-}\in\mathcal{Y},\ L_{-}f_{-}=g.
(12)
It is also well known (see e.g. [23, 24, 31, 34]) that L_{+} and L_{-} verify the following coercivity property: there exists \mu>0 such that for all \varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}+i\varepsilon_{2}\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) ,
\left\langle L_{+}\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1}\right\rangle+\left\langle L_{-}\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{2}\right\rangle\geq\mu\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\left(\varepsilon_{1},Q\right)_{2}^{2}+\left(\varepsilon_{1},|y|^{2}Q\right)_{2}^{2}+\left(\varepsilon_{2},\rho\right)_{2}^{2}\right).
(13)
Throughout the paper, C denotes various positive constants whose exact values may vary from line to line but are of no importance in the analysis. When an inequality is true up to such a constant, we also use the notation \lesssim , \gtrsim or \approx .
2. Construction of the blow-up profile
In this section, we define the blow-up profile which is relevant to construct the minimal mass solution – see Proposition 3 below.
2.1. Blow up profile
Let us start with some heuristic arguments justifying the construction.
As usual in blow up contexts, we look for a solution of the following form, with rescaled variables (s,y) :
u(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}(s)}w(s,y)e^{i\gamma(s)-i\frac{b(s)|y|^{2}}{4}},\qquad\frac{ds}{dt}=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}},\qquad y=\frac{x}{\lambda(s)},
where the function w , and the time dependent parameters \lambda>0 , b and \gamma are to be determined
satisfying the following equation
iw_{s}+\Delta w-w+f(w)+\lambda^{\alpha}g(w)\\
-i\left(b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\right)\Lambda w+(1-\gamma_{s})w+(b_{s}+b^{2})\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}w-b\left(b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\right)\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}w=0,
(14)
where
\alpha=2-\frac{d(p-1)}{2}\in(0,2).
Since we look for blow up solutions, the parameter \lambda(s) should converge to zero as s\to\infty .
Therefore,
w(s,y)=Q(y),\qquad b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}=b_{s}+b^{2}=1-\gamma_{s}=0
(15)
is a solution of (14) at the first order, i.e. when neglecting \lambda^{\alpha}|w|^{p-1}w .
However, the first order error term \lambda^{\alpha}Q^{p} cannot be neglected in the minimal mass blow up analysis (while it could be neglected easily in the log-log regime where \lambda\sim e^{-e^{\frac{c}{b}}} ). Therefore, starting from Q , we need to look for a refined blow up ansatz.
Actually, to close the analysis for any \alpha\in(0,2) , we need to remove error terms at any order of \lambda^{\alpha} and b in the equation of w . It is important to note that in the process of constructing the approximate solution, we cannot exactly solve (14) since we need to introduce new terms in the equation (due to degrees of freedom necessary to construct the ansatz) that will modify the modulation equations in (15). These terms (gathered in the time dependent function \theta(s) below) are responsible for the specific blow up law obtained in Theorem 1.
Fix K\in\mathbb{N} , K\gg 1 ( K>20/\alpha is sufficient in the proof of Theorem 1), and
\Sigma_{K}=\{(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\ |\ j+k\leq K\}.
Proposition 3.
Let \lambda(s)>0 and b(s)\in\mathbb{R} be \mathcal{C}^{1} functions of s such that \lambda(s)+|b(s)|\ll 1 .
(i) Existence of a blow up profile.
For any (j,k)\in\Sigma_{K} , there exist real-valued functions P_{j,k}^{+}\in\mathcal{Y} , P_{j,k}^{-}\in\mathcal{Y} and \beta_{j,k}\in\mathbb{R} such that
P(s,y)=\tilde{P}_{K}(y;b(s),\lambda(s)) , where \tilde{P}_{K} is defined by
\tilde{P}_{K}(y;b,\lambda):=Q(y)+\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}}b^{2j}\lambda^{(k+1)\alpha}P_{j,k}^{+}(y)+i\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}}b^{2j+1}\lambda^{(k+1)\alpha}P_{j,k}^{-}(y)
(16)
satisfies
i\partial_{s}P+\Delta P-P+f(P)+\lambda^{\alpha}g(P)+\theta\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}P=\Psi_{K}
where \theta(s)=\tilde{\theta}(b(s),\lambda(s)) ,
\tilde{\theta}(b,\lambda)=\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}}b^{2j}\lambda^{(k+1)\alpha}\beta_{j,k}
and
\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(e^{\frac{|y|}{2}}\left(|\Psi_{K}(y)|+|\nabla\Psi_{K}(y)|\right)\right)\lesssim\lambda^{\alpha}\left(\Big|b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Big|+\left|b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta\right|\right)+(|b|^{2}+\lambda^{\alpha})^{K+2}.
(17)
(ii) Rescaled blow up profile. Let
P_{b}(s,y):=P(s,y)e^{-i\frac{b(s)|y|^{2}}{4}}.
(18)
Then
i\partial_{s}P_{b}+\Delta P_{b}-P_{b}+f(P_{b})+\lambda^{\alpha}g(P_{b})-i\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda P_{b}\\
=-i\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b\right)\Lambda P_{b}+(b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta)\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}P_{b}+\Psi_{K}e^{-i\frac{b|y|^{2}}{4}}.
(19)
(iii) Mass and energy properties of the blow up profile.
Let
P_{b,\lambda,\gamma}(s,y)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}}P_{b}\left(s,\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)e^{i\gamma}.
Then,
\left|\frac{d}{ds}\int|P_{b,\lambda,\gamma}|^{2}\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\alpha}\left(\Big|b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Big|+\left|b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta\right|\right)+(|b|^{2}+\lambda^{\alpha})^{K+2},
(20)
\left|\frac{d}{ds}{E(P_{b,\lambda,\gamma})}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\left(\Big|b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Big|+\left|b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta\right|+(|b|^{2}+\lambda^{\alpha})^{K+2}\right).
(21)
Moreover, for any (j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}, there exist \eta_{j,k}\in\mathbb{R} such that
\left|E(P_{b,\lambda,\gamma})-\frac{\int|y|^{2}Q^{2}}{8}\mathcal{E}(b,\lambda)\right|\lesssim\frac{(b^{2}+\lambda^{\alpha})^{K+2}}{\lambda^{2}},
(22)
where
\mathcal{E}(b,\lambda)=\frac{b^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-2}+\lambda^{\alpha-2}\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K},j+k\geq 1}b^{2j}\lambda^{k\alpha}\eta_{j,k}.
(23)
See a similar construction of a blow up profile at any order of b in [27].
One sees in (19) the impact of the subcritical nonlinearity g(u) on the blow up law
b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta=0 , which differs from the unperturbed equation b_{s}+b^{2}=0 , and leads to leading order to \lambda^{\alpha}\approx b^{2} , see (32).

2.2. Approximate blow up law
For simplicity of notation, we set
\beta=\beta_{0,0}=\frac{2d(p-1)}{p+1}\frac{\lVert Q\rVert_{p+1}^{p+1}}{\lVert yQ\rVert_{2}^{2}}.
First, we find a relevant solution to the following approximate system
b_{s}+b^{2}-\beta\lambda^{\alpha}=0,\quad b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}=0.
(31)
Indeed, for |b|+\lambda\ll 1 , \beta\lambda^{\alpha} is the main term in \theta , and the only term in \theta that will modify at the main order the blow up rate.
Lemma 4.
Let
\lambda_{\rm app}(s)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}s^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}},\quad b_{\rm app}(s)={\frac{2}{\alpha s}}.
(32)
Then (\lambda_{\rm app}(s),b_{\rm app}(s)) solves (31) for s>0 .
Proof.
We compute:
\left(\frac{b^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\right)_{s}=2\frac{b}{\lambda}\frac{b_{s}+b^{2}}{\lambda}=-2\beta\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\lambda^{\alpha-2},
and so
\frac{b^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-2}=c_{0}.
(33)
Taking the constant c_{0}=0 , and using b=-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}>0 , we find
\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}}.
Therefore,
\lambda(s)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}}s^{-\frac{2}{\alpha}},\quad b(s)=-\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}(s)={\frac{2}{\alpha}}\frac{1}{s}
is solution of (31).
∎
Remark 1.
We now express this solution in the time variable t_{\rm app} related to \lambda_{\rm app} . Let
{dt_{\rm app}}={\lambda_{\rm app}^{2}}ds=\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}}\right)^{-\frac{4}{\alpha}}s^{-\frac{4}{\alpha}}ds.
Therefore (with the convention that t_{\rm app}\to 0^{-} as s\to+\infty )
t_{\rm app}=-C_{s}s^{-\frac{4-\alpha}{\alpha}}\quad\hbox{where}\quad C_{s}=\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\beta}{2-\alpha}}\right)^{-\frac{4}{\alpha}}.
(34)
As a consequence, we obtain for t_{\rm app}<0 ,

(35)
b_{\rm app}(t_{\rm app})=C_{b}|t_{\rm app}|^{\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}},\quad\hbox{where}\quad C_{b}=\frac{2}{\alpha}C_{s}^{-\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}}.
(36)
Now, we choose suitable initial conditions b_{1} and \lambda_{1} for b(s) and \lambda(s) at some large time s_{1} , first to adjust the value of the energy of P_{b,\lambda,\gamma} (up to the small error term in (22)) and second to be able to close the perturbed dynamical system of (\lambda,b) at the end of the proof (see proof of Lemma 16 below).

Note that the function \mathcal{F} is related to the resolution of the system (33)
for c_{0}=C_{0} , see proof of Lemma 16.

Let s_{1}\gg 1 . There exist b_{1} and \lambda_{1} such that
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\lambda_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\lambda_{\rm app}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s_{1})}-1\right|+\left|\frac{b_{1}}{b_{\rm app}(s_{1})}-1\right|\lesssim s_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+s_{1}^{2-\frac{4}{\alpha}},

Let \lambda_{1} and b_{1} be given by Lemma 5 for this value of s_{1} . Let u(t) be the solution of (7) for t\leq t_{1} , with data
u(t_{1},x)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d}{2}}}P_{b_{1}}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda_{1}}\right).
(44)
As long as the solution u(t) satisfies (41), we consider its decomposition (\lambda,b,\gamma,\varepsilon) from Lemma 6 and we define the rescaled time s by
s=s_{1}-\int_{t}^{t_{1}}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(\tau)}d\tau.
(45)
The heart of the proof of Theorem 1 is the following result, giving uniform backwards estimates on the decomposition of u(s) on [s_{0},s_{1}] for some s_{0} independent of s_{1} .
Proposition 7 (Uniform estimates in rescaled time).
There exists s_{0}>0 independent of s_{1} such that
the solution u of (7) defined by (44)
exists and satisfies (41) on [s_{0},s_{1}] . Moreover, its decomposition
u(s,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}(s)}\left(P_{b}+\varepsilon\right)\left(s,y\right)e^{i\gamma(s)},\qquad y=\frac{x}{\lambda(s)},
satisfies the following uniform estimates on [s_{0},s_{1}] ,
\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{H^{1}}\lesssim s^{-(K+1)},\quad\left|\frac{\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s)}{\lambda_{\rm app}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s)}-1\right|+\left|\frac{b(s)}{b_{\rm app}(s)}-1\right|\lesssim s^{-\frac{1}{2}}+s^{2-\frac{4}{\alpha}}.
(46)
In addition,
|E(P_{b,\lambda,\gamma}(s))-E_{0}|\leq O(s^{-6}).
Let us insist again that the key point in Proposition 7 is that s_{0} and the constants in the estimates are independent of s_{1}\to+\infty .
3.2. Proof of Theorem 1 assuming Proposition 7
First, we convert the estimates of Proposition 7 in the original time variable t . We claim:
Lemma 8 (Estimates in the t variable).
There exists t_{0}<0 such that under the assumptions of Proposition 7, for all t\in[t_{0},t_{1}] ,
\displaystyle b(t)=C_{b}|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}}(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)),\ \ \lambda(t)=C_{\lambda}|t|^{\frac{2}{4-\alpha}}(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1))
(47)
\displaystyle\lVert\varepsilon(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}\lesssim|t|^{\frac{(K+1)\alpha}{4-\alpha}}
(48)
\displaystyle|E(P_{b,\lambda,\gamma}(t))-E_{0}|=o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)
(49)
Proof of Lemma 8.
Using (46), (45), for all large s<s_{1} ,
t_{1}-t(s)=\int_{s}^{s_{1}}\lambda^{2}(\sigma)d\sigma=\int_{s}^{s_{1}}\lambda^{2}_{\rm app}(\sigma)\left[1+O(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}})+O(\sigma^{2-\frac{4}{\alpha}})\right]d\sigma.
Recall that t_{\rm app} given by (34) corresponds to the normalization
t_{\rm app}(s)=-\int_{s}^{+\infty}\lambda^{2}_{\rm app}(\sigma),\quad t_{\rm app}(s_{1})=t_{1},
from which we obtain
t(s)=t_{\rm app}(s)(1+o(1))=-C_{s}s^{-\frac{4-\alpha}{\alpha}}\left[1+o(1)\right].
The estimates of Lemma 8 now follow directly follow from (32) and Proposition 7 (see the definition of C_{\lambda} and C_{b} in (35) and (36)).

Now, we finish the proof of Theorem 1 assuming Proposition 7.
Proof of Theorem 1.
Let (t_{n})\subset(t_{0},0) be an increasing sequence such that \lim_{n\to\infty}t_{n}=0 . For each n , let u_{n} be the solution of (7) on [t_{0},t_{n}] with final data at t_{n}
u_{n}(t_{n},x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}(t_{n})}P_{b(t_{n})}\left(\frac{x}{\lambda(t_{n})}\right),
(50)
where \lambda(t_{n})=\lambda_{1} and b(t_{n})=b_{1} are given by Lemma 5
for s_{1}=|C_{s}^{-1}t_{n}|^{-\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}} ,
so that u_{n}(t) satisfies the conclusions of Proposition 7 and of Lemma 8 on the interval [t_{0},t_{n}] .
The minimal mass blow up solution for (7) is now obtained as the limit of a subsequence of (u_{n}) . In a first step, we prove that a subsequence of (u_{n}(t_{0})) converges to a suitable initial data. Indeed, from Lemma 8, we infer that (u_{n}(t_{0})) is bounded in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . Hence there exists a subsequence of (u_{n}(t_{0})) (still denoted by (u_{n}(t_{0})) and
u_{\infty}(t_{0})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) such that
u_{n}(t_{0})\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}(t_{0})\quad\text{weakly in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ as }n\to+\infty.
Now, we obtain strong convergence in H^{s} (for some 0<s<1 ) by direct arguments. Let \chi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1] be a smooth cut-off function such that \chi\equiv 0 on [0,1] and \chi\equiv 1 on [2,+\infty) .
For R>0 , define \chi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1] by \chi_{R}(x)=\chi(|x|/R) .
Take any \delta>0 . By the expression of u_{n}(t_{n}) in (50), we can choose R large enough (independent of n ) so that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}(t_{n})|^{2}\chi_{R}dx\leq\delta.
(51)
It follows from elementary computations that
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}|^{2}\chi_{R}dx=2\,\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\chi_{R}\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,\bar{u}_{n}dx.
Hence from the geometrical decomposition
u_{n}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}^{\frac{d}{2}}(t)}\left(P_{b_{n}(t)}+\varepsilon_{n})(t,y)\right)e^{i\gamma_{n}(t)},\qquad y=\frac{x}{\lambda_{n}(t)},
and the smallness (47)-(48) of \varepsilon_{n} and \lambda_{n} we infer
\left|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}(t)|^{2}\chi_{R}dx\right|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda_{n}(t)R}\left(e^{-\frac{R}{2\lambda_{n}(t)}}+\lVert\varepsilon_{n}(t)\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right)\leq\frac{C}{R}|t|^{\left(-\frac{2}{\alpha}+K+1\right)\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}}.
Integrating between t_{0} and t_{n} , we obtain
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}(t_{0})|^{2}\chi_{R}dx\leq\frac{C}{R}|t_{0}|^{\left(-\frac{2}{\alpha}+K+1\right)\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}+1}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}(t_{n})|^{2}\chi_{R}dx.
Combined with (51), for a possibly larger R , this implies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{n}(t_{0})|^{2}\chi_{R}dx\leq 2\delta.
We conclude from the local compactness of Sobolev embeddings that for 0\leq s<1 :
u_{n}(t_{0})\to u_{\infty}(t_{0})\quad\text{strongly in }H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \text{ as }n\to+\infty.
Let u_{\infty}(t) be the solution of (7) with u_{\infty}(t_{0}) as initial data at t=t_{0} . From [6, 7] there exists 0<s_{0}<1 such that the Cauchy problem for (7) is locally well-posed in H^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . This implies that u_{\infty} exists on [t_{0},0) and for any t\in[t_{0},0) ,
u_{n}(t)\to u_{\infty}(t)\quad\text{strongly in }H^{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\text{ weakly in }H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\text{ as }n\to+\infty.
Moreover,
since \lim_{n\to\infty}\int u_{n}^{2}(t_{n})=\int Q^{2} , we have \int u_{\infty}^{2}=\int Q^{2} . By weak convergence in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) and the estimates from Lemma 8 applied to u_{n} , u_{\infty}(t) satisfies (41), and denoting (\varepsilon_{\infty},\lambda_{\infty},b_{\infty},\gamma_{\infty}) its decomposition, we have by standard arguments (see e.g. [24]), for any t\in[t_{0},0) ,
\lambda_{n}(t)\to\lambda_{\infty}(t),\quad b_{n}(t)\to b_{\infty}(t),\quad\gamma_{n}(t)\to\gamma_{\infty}(t),\quad\varepsilon_{n}(t)\rightharpoonup\varepsilon_{\infty}(t)\quad\hbox{$H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$ weak, as $n\to\infty$.}
The uniform estimates on u_{n} from Lemma 8 give, on [t_{0},0) ,
b_{\infty}(t)=C_{b}|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}}\left(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)\right),\quad\lambda_{\infty}(t)=C_{\lambda}|t|^{\frac{2}{4-\alpha}}\left(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)\right),\ \ \|\varepsilon_{\infty}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim|t|^{\frac{(K+1)\alpha}{4-\alpha}},
(52)
\frac{b_{\infty}(t)}{\lambda_{\infty}^{2}(t)}=\frac{C_{b}}{C_{\lambda}^{2}}|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{4-\alpha}-\frac{4}{4-\alpha}}\left(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)\right)=\frac{2}{4-\alpha}\frac{1}{|t|}\left(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)\right)=\frac{\sigma}{|t|}\left(1+o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)\right),
(53)
which justifies the form (9) and the blow up rate (8).
Finally, we prove that E(u_{\infty})=E_{0} .
Let t_{0}<t<0 . We have by (49) and (22),
\mathcal{E}(b_{n}(t),\lambda_{n}(t))-\frac{8E_{0}}{\int|y|^{2}Q^{2}}=o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)
where the o_{t\uparrow 0}(1) is independent of n ,
and thus
\mathcal{E}(b_{\infty}(t),\lambda_{\infty}(t))-\frac{8E_{0}}{\int|y|^{2}Q^{2}}=o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)
Using (22), we deduce
E(P_{b_{\infty},\lambda_{\infty},\gamma_{\infty}}(t))-E_{0}=o_{t\uparrow 0}(1)
and thus, by (52),
E(u_{\infty}(t))-E_{0}=o_{t\uparrow 0}(1).
Thus, by conservation of energy and passing to the limit t\uparrow 0 , we obtain E(u_{\infty}(t))=E_{0} .

3.3. Bootstrap estimates
The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Proposition 7.
We use a bootstrap argument involving the following estimates:
\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{H^{1}}<s^{-K},\quad\left|\frac{\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s)}{\lambda_{\rm app}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s)}-1\right|+\left|\frac{b(s)}{b_{\rm app}(s)}-1\right|<s^{-\delta(\alpha)}
(54)
for some small enough universal constant \delta(\alpha)>0 .
The following value is suitable in this paper
\delta(\alpha)=\min\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{2}{\alpha}-1\right)>0.
(55)
For s_{0}>0 to be chosen large enough (independently of s_{1} ), we
define
s_{*}=\inf\{\tau\in[s_{0},s_{1}];\eqref{eq:smallness-4-s}\text{ holds on }[\tau,s_{1}]\}.
(56)
Observe from (38) that
\left|\frac{\lambda_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\lambda_{\rm app}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s_{1})}-1\right|+\left|\frac{b_{1}}{b_{\rm app}(s_{1})}-1\right|\lesssim s_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}+s_{1}^{2-\frac{4}{\alpha}}\ll s_{1}^{-\delta(\alpha)},
for s_{1} large, and hence by the definition (44) of u(s_{1}) , s_{*} is well-defined and s_{*}<s_{1} .
In §5, §6 and §7, we prove that (46) holds on [s_{*},s_{1}] .
By a standard continuity argument, provided that s_{0} is large enough, we obtain s_{*}=s_{0} which implies Proposition 7. The main lines of the proof are as follows: first, we derive modulation equations from the construction of P_{b} ,
second we control the remaining error using a mixed Energy/Morawetz functional first derived in [31].
4. Modulation equations
In this section, we work with the solution u(t) of Proposition 7 on the time interval [s_{*},s_{1}] (see (54)-(56)). We justify that the dynamical system satisfied by the modulation parameters \lambda,b is at the main order given by (31).
Define
\Mod(s)=\begin{pmatrix}b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\\
b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta\\
1-\gamma_{s}\end{pmatrix}.
Lemma 9 (Modulation equations and additional orthogonality).
For all s\in[s_{*},s_{1}] ,
|\Mod(s)|\lesssim\frac{1}{s^{K+2}},
(57)
|\left(\varepsilon(s),Q\right)_{2}|\lesssim\frac{1}{s^{K+1}}.
(58)
Proof of Lemma 9.
The proofs of the two estimates are combined.
Since \varepsilon(s_{1})\equiv 0 , we may define
s_{**}=\inf\{s\in[s_{*},s_{1}];\ |\left(\varepsilon(\tau),P_{b}\right)_{2}|<\tau^{-(K+2)}\text{ holds on }[s,s_{1}]\}.
We work on the interval [s_{**},s_{1}] .
Since P_{b} verifies equation (19), we obtain the following equation for \varepsilon :
i\varepsilon_{s}+\Delta\varepsilon-\varepsilon+ib\Lambda\varepsilon+(f(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-f(P_{b}))+\lambda^{\alpha}(g(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-g(P_{b}))\\
-i\left(b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\right)\Lambda(P_{b}+\varepsilon)+(1-\gamma_{s})(P_{b}+\varepsilon)+(b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta)\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}P_{b}\\
=-\Psi e^{-i\frac{b|y|^{2}}{4}}.
(59)
where \Psi:=\Psi_{K} . Recall that equation (59) combined with the orthogonality conditions chosen on \varepsilon – see (43) – contains the equations of the modulation parameters. Technically, one differentiates in time the orthogonality conditions for \varepsilon , then uses the equation (59) on \varepsilon and the estimate (17) on the error term \Psi .
Here, as in [31], the orthogonality conditions are chosen to obtain quadratic control in \varepsilon . Since it is a standard argument (see e.g. [25, 28, 31]), we only sketch relevant computations.
Consider for example the orthogonality condition \left(\varepsilon,i\Lambda P_{b}\right)_{2}=0 . Differentiating in s , we obtain
\left\langle\varepsilon_{s},i\Lambda P_{b}\right\rangle+\left\langle\varepsilon,i\partial_{s}(\Lambda P_{b})\right\rangle=0.
Since
\frac{d}{ds}(\Lambda P_{b})=\left((\Lambda P)_{s}-i\frac{b_{s}}{4}|y|^{2}\Lambda P\right)e^{-i\frac{b}{4}|y|^{2}},
and
(\Lambda P)_{s}=\lambda^{\alpha}\Bigg(\alpha\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\bigg(Z+\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}}kb^{2j}\lambda^{k\alpha-1}(P_{j,k}^{+}+bP_{j,k}^{-})\bigg)\\
+b_{s}\bigg(\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}}2jb^{2j-1}\lambda^{k\alpha}P_{j,k}^{+}+\sum_{(j,k)\in\Sigma_{K}}(2j+1)b^{2j}\lambda^{k\alpha}P_{j,k}^{-}\bigg)\Bigg),
proceeding as in the proof of Proposition 3, and using the properties of the functions P_{j,k}^{\pm} , we note that
\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{\frac{y}{2}}\left|\frac{d}{ds}(\Lambda P_{b})(y)\right|\right)\lesssim|\Mod(s)|+b^{2}(s)+\lambda^{\alpha}(s).
Thus, by (54),
|\left(\varepsilon,i\partial_{s}(\Lambda P_{b})\right)_{2}|\lesssim\|\varepsilon(s)\|_{2}\left(|\Mod(s)|+b^{2}(s)+\lambda^{\alpha}(s)\right)\\
\lesssim s^{-2}|\Mod(s)|+s^{-(K+2)}.
Next, we write \left\langle\varepsilon_{s},i\Lambda P_{b}\right\rangle=-\left\langle i\varepsilon_{s},\Lambda P_{b}\right\rangle and we use the equation of \varepsilon .
We start by the contribution of the first line of (59).
Remark that by (54),
\displaystyle f(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-f(P_{b})
\displaystyle=e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\left(f\left(P+e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right)-f(P)\right)=e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}df(P)\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right)+O(|\varepsilon|^{2})
\displaystyle=e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}df(P)\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right)+O(s^{-2}|\varepsilon|),
\lambda^{\alpha}\left(g(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-g(P_{b})\right)=O(\lambda^{\alpha}|\varepsilon|)=O(s^{-2}|\varepsilon|),
and
\Delta\varepsilon+ib\Lambda\varepsilon=e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\Delta\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right)+b^{2}\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}\varepsilon,\qquad\Lambda P_{b}=e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\left(\Lambda P-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}P\right).
Therefore, using (54) and P=Q+O_{H^{1}}(s^{-2}) (see the definition of P in (16)), we have
\left\langle-\Delta\varepsilon+\varepsilon-ib\Lambda\varepsilon-(f(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-f(P_{b}))+\lambda^{\alpha}\left(g(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-g(P_{b})\right),\Lambda P_{b}\right\rangle\\
=\left\langle-\Delta\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right)+e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon-pQ^{p-1}\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right),\Lambda Q-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}Q\right\rangle+O(s^{-2}\|\varepsilon\|_{2})\\
=\left\langle L_{+}\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right),\Lambda Q\right\rangle-\frac{b}{2}\left\langle L_{-}\left(e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon\right),i|y|^{2}Q\right\rangle+O(s^{-2}\|\varepsilon\|_{2})\\
=\left\langle e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon,L_{+}(\Lambda Q)\right\rangle-\frac{b}{2}\left\langle e^{ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\varepsilon,iL_{-}(|y|^{2}Q)\right\rangle+O(s^{-2}\|\varepsilon\|_{2})\\
=-2\left(\varepsilon,e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}Q\right)_{2}+2b\left(\varepsilon,ie^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\Lambda Q\right)_{2}+O(s^{-2}\|\varepsilon\|_{2})\\
=-2\left(\varepsilon,P_{b}\right)_{2}+2b\left(\varepsilon,i\Lambda P_{b}\right)_{2}+O(s^{-2}\|\varepsilon\|_{2})=O(s^{-(K+2)}).
Note that we have used algebraic relations from (10), then (54), \left(\varepsilon,i\Lambda P_{b}\right)_{2}=0 and the definition of s_{**} .
The part corresponding to the second line of (59) gives
\left(-i\left(b+\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\right)\Lambda(P_{b}+\varepsilon)+(1-\gamma_{s})(P_{b}+\varepsilon)+(b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta)\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}P_{b},\Lambda P_{b}\right)_{2}\\
=-(b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta)\lVert yP_{b}\rVert_{2}^{2}+O(|\Mod(s)|\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})\\
=-(b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta)(\lVert yQ\rVert_{2}^{2}+O\left(s^{-2})\right)+O(s^{-2}|\Mod(s)|).
Finally, from the estimate (17) on \Psi , we have
\left|\left(\Psi,\Lambda P-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}P\right)_{2}\right|\lesssim s^{-2}|\Mod(s)|+s^{-2(K+2)}.
Combining the previous estimates, we find
|b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta|\lesssim s^{-2}|\Mod(s)|+s^{-(K+2)}.
Using the other orthogonality conditions in (43) in a similar way, together with (10), we find
|\Mod(s)|\lesssim s^{-2}|\Mod(s)|+s^{-(K+2)}.
We deduce that for all s\in[s_{**},s_{1}] ,
|\Mod(s)|\lesssim s^{-(K+2)}.
(60)
By conservation of the L^{2} norm and (44), we have
\lVert u(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert u(s_{1})\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert P_{b}(s_{1})\rVert_{2}^{2}.
Thus, by (42),
\left(\varepsilon(s),P_{b}\right)_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert u(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}-\lVert P_{b}(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}-\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert P_{b}(s_{1})\rVert_{2}^{2}-\lVert P_{b}(s)\rVert_{2}^{2}\right).
Moreover, by (20), (54) and (60),
\frac{d}{ds}\int|P_{b}|^{2}\lesssim s^{-(K+4)}.
Integrating and combining the previous estimates with (54), we obtain, for all s\in[s_{**},s_{1}] ,
|\left(\varepsilon(s),P_{b}\right)_{2}|\lesssim s^{-(K+3)}.
(61)
Therefore, s_{**}=s_{*} and the estimates (60) and (61) are proved on
[s_{*},s_{1}] . Since |P_{b}-Q|\lesssim Q^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{-1} , we obtain (58).

5. The mixed energy Morawetz monotonicity formula
In this section, following [31], we introduce a mixed Energy/Morawetz functional to control the remaining part of the solution in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . First, define the energy of \varepsilon
H(s,\varepsilon):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla\varepsilon\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(F(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-F(P_{b})-dF(P_{b})\varepsilon)dy\\
-\lambda^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(G(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-G(P_{b})-dG(P_{b})\varepsilon)dy.
Note that as in [31], the time derivative of the linearized energy H for \varepsilon cannot be controlled alone, and one has to add a virial type functional such as
\frac{b}{2}\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\left(\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}\right)\nabla\varepsilon\bar{\varepsilon}dy.
In practice, due to the lack of control on \lVert y\varepsilon\rVert_{2} , we replace \frac{1}{2}|y|^{2} by a function whose gradient is bounded, which we introduce now.
Let \phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R} be a smooth even and convex function, nondecreasing on \mathbb{R}^{+} ,
such that
\phi(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}r^{2}&\text{ for }&r<1,\\
&3r+e^{-r}&\text{ for }&r>2,\end{aligned}\right.
and set \phi(x)=\phi(|x|) .
Let A\gg 1 to be fixed. Define \phi_{A} by \phi_{A}(y)=A^{2}\phi\left(\frac{y}{A}\right) and
J(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\phi_{A}\cdot\nabla\varepsilon\bar{\varepsilon}dy.
Finally, set
S(s,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{4}(s)}(H(s,\varepsilon)+b(s)J(\varepsilon(s))).
The relevance of the functional S lies on the following two properties.
Proposition 10 (Coercivity of S ).
For any s\in[s_{*},s_{1}] ,
S(s,\varepsilon(s))\gtrsim\frac{1}{\lambda^{4}(s)}\left(\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+O(s^{-2(K+1)}\right).
Proposition 11.
For any s\in[s_{*},s_{1}] ,
\frac{d}{ds}\left[S(s,\varepsilon(s))\right]\gtrsim\frac{b}{\lambda^{4}(s)}\left(\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+O(s^{-2(K+1)}\right).
The rest of this section is organized as follows. We first prove Proposition 10 in §5.1.
In §5.2 we compute the time derivative of H and in §5.3, the time derivative of J .
We finish the proof of Proposition 11 in §5.4.
5.1. Coercivity of S
We prove Proposition 10. We first claim a coercivity property for H , consequence of the properties of L_{+} and L_{-} (see (13)) and of the orthogonality conditions of \varepsilon (see (43)).
Lemma 12 (Coercivity of H ).
For all s\in[s_{*},s_{1}] ,
H(s,\varepsilon)\gtrsim\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+O(s^{-2(K+1)}).
Proof.
From the orthogonality conditions (43), (58), and estimates (54), the following holds:
\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon,|y|^{2}Q\right)_{2}
\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon,|y|^{2}P_{b}\right)_{2}+O(|b|\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})+O(\lambda^{\alpha}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})=O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}),
\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon,i\rho\right)_{2}
\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon,i\rho_{b}\right)_{2}+O(|b|\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})=O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}),
\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon,Q\right)_{2}
\displaystyle=O(s^{-(K+1)}).
From (54), we have
\lambda^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(G(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-G(P_{b})-dG(P_{b})\varepsilon)dx=O(s^{-2}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}).
Next, (denoting \varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}+i\varepsilon_{2} ),
\left|F(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-F(P_{b})-dF(P_{b})\varepsilon-\left(1+\frac{4}{d}\right)Q^{\frac{4}{d}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{\frac{4}{d}}\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right|\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}|y|}|\varepsilon|^{3}+|\varepsilon|^{2+\frac{4}{d}}+|\varepsilon|^{2}(|b|+\lambda^{\alpha}).
Thus, from (54),
\left|\int F(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-F(P_{b})-dF(P_{b})\varepsilon-\left(1+\frac{4}{d}\right)Q^{\frac{4}{d}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{\frac{4}{d}}\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right|\lesssim O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}),
and
\left|H(s,\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle L_{+}\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle L_{-}\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{2}\right\rangle\right|\lesssim O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}).
Combining these estimates with the coercivity properties of L_{+} , L_{-} (see (13)),
we obtain the result.
∎
Since
|bJ(\varepsilon)|\leq|b|\lVert\nabla\phi_{A}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\lesssim O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2})
(from (54)), Lemma 12 implies
Proposition 10.

Lemma 13.

5.2. Time variation of the energy of \varepsilon

Now, we compute \left\langle D_{\varepsilon}H(s,\varepsilon),\varepsilon_{s}\right\rangle .
Note that (59) rewrites

(62)
where

Recall from §3.3 that our objective is to prove s_{*}=s_{0} by improving estimates (54) into (46). Therefore, it is sufficient to prove the following lemma which closes the bounds (54) provided \delta(\alpha)>0 has been chosen small enough (e.g. as in (55)).
Lemma 16 (Refined estimates).
For all s\in[s_{*},s_{1}] ,
\displaystyle\lVert\varepsilon(s)\rVert_{H^{1}}\lesssim s^{-(K+1)},
(65)
\displaystyle\left|\frac{\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s)}{\lambda_{\rm app}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(s)}-1\right|+\left|\frac{b(s)}{b_{\rm app}(s)}-1\right|\lesssim s^{-\frac{1}{2}}+s^{2-\frac{4}{\alpha}}.

Proof.
First, we prove (65).
From Proposition 10, and the expression of S , there exists a universal constant \kappa>1 such that for any s\in[s_{*},s_{1}] ,

which contradicts the a priori bound from the energy conservation law and (3):
E_{0}=E(u)\geq E_{\rm crit}(u)+\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u|^{p+1}\geq\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u|^{p+1}.
Appendix B Proof of Lemma 2
For the sake of simplicity, we give the proof only for d\geq 2 . The case d=1 would require an additionnal (standard) concentration compactness argument (see [32]). For M<\|Q\|_{2} , set
A_{M}=\{u\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ \|u\|_{2}=M\}
and consider the minimization problem
I_{M}=\inf_{u\in A_{M}}E(u).
First, we claim
-\infty<I_{M}<0.
(71)
Indeed, from (3) and
\int|u|^{p+1}\leq C_{\rm GN}(p)\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{2}^{\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{p+1-\frac{d(p-1)}{2}},
(72)
with 1<p<1+\frac{4}{d} , we note that
I_{M}>-\infty and that any minimizing sequence is bounded in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) .
Let u\in A_{M} and v_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}u(\lambda x), then v_{\lambda}\in A_{M} and
E(v_{\lambda})=\lambda^{2}\left[E_{\rm crit}(u)-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2-\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}}\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u|^{p+1}\right].
In particular, for 0<\lambda\ll 1 and u\not\equiv 0 , E(v_{\lambda})<0 and (71) follows.
Second, let u_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(\lambda x) , so that
E(u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{\frac{4}{p-1}+2-d}\left[\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u|^{p+1}\right]-\frac{\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}(2+\frac{4}{d})-d}}{2+\frac{4}{d}}\int|u|^{2+\frac{4}{d}}.
We observe that
\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}E(u_{\lambda})_{|\lambda=1}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{p-1}+2-d\right)\left[\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\int|u|^{p+1}\right]-\frac{\frac{2}{p-1}(2+\frac{4}{d})-d}{2+\frac{4}{d}}\int|u|^{2+\frac{4}{d}}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\left(\frac{4}{p-1}+2-d\right)E(u)-\frac{\frac{4}{d}}{2+\frac{4}{d}}\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{d}{2}\right)\int|u|^{2+\frac{4}{d}}.
Together with \|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}-\frac{d}{2}}\|u\|_{2} , which implies
\frac{d}{d\lambda}{\|u_{\lambda}\|_{2}}_{|\lambda=1}>0 , this proves that
I(M)\text{ is decreasing in }M.
(73)
To finish, let (u_{n}) be a minimizing sequence. Up to a subsequence and from the standard radial compactness of Sobolev embeddings (see [3])
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \ u_{n}\to u\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{q},\ \ 2<q\leq 2+\frac{4}{d}.
Hence
E(u)\leq I_{M}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|u\|_{2}\leq M.
From (73) and the definition of I_{M} , we deduce \|u\|_{2}=M and E(u)=I_{M} . From a standard Lagrange multiplier argument, u satisfies
\Delta u+|u|^{1+\frac{4}{d}}u+|u|^{p-1}u=\omega u
for a constant \omega\in\mathbb{R} . The sign \omega>0 now follows from a standard Pohozaev type argument.
