Abstract.
We establish the existence of local stable manifolds for semiflows generated
by nonlinear perturbations of nonautonomous ordinary linear
differential equations in Banach spaces, assuming the existence of a general
type of nonuniform dichotomy for the evolution operator that contains the
nonuniform exponential and polynomial dichotomies as a very particular case.
The family of dichotomies considered allow situations for which the
classical Lyapunov exponents are zero. Additionally, we give new examples of
application of our stable manifold theorem and study the behavior of the
dynamics under perturbations.
Key words and phrases: Invariant manifolds, nonautonomous differential equations,
nonuniform generalized dichotomies
2000 Mathematics Subject Classification37D10, 34D09, 37D25
1. Introduction
The concept of nonuniform hyperbolicity was introduced by
Pesin [10, 11, 12] and
generalizes the classical concept of (uniform) hyperbolicity by allowing the
rates of expansion and contraction to vary from point to point. For
nonuniformly hyperbolic trajectories, Pesin [10] was able
to obtain a stable manifold theorem in the finite dimensional setting. Then, in
[14] Ruelle gave a proof of this theorem based on the
study of perturbations of products of matrices occurring in Oseledets’
multiplicative ergodic theorem [8]. Another proof, based
on the classical work of Hadamard, was obtained by Pugh and Shub
in [13] and uses graph transform techniques. In the
infinite dimensional setting, Ruelle [15] proved, following
his approach in [14], a stable manifold theorem in Hilbert
spaces under some compactness assumptions. For transformations in Banach spaces
and under some compactness and invertibility assumptions, Mañé established
the existence of stable manifolds in [7] and
in [16] Thieullen weakened Mañé’s hypothesis.
In the context of nonautonomous differential equations, stable manifold
theorems were also obtained, assuming that the evolution operator have bounds
that are nonuniform, more precisely assuming that the evolution operator admits
a nonuniform exponential dichotomy, a notion introduced by Barreira and Valls
in [1] and inspired both in the classical notion of
exponential dichotomy introduced by Perron in [9] and in the
notion of nonuniformly hyperbolic trajectory introduced by Pesin
in [10, 11, 12]. For more
details we refer the reader to the book [2].
Recently it has been addressed the problem of obtaining stable manifolds for
perturbations of linear ordinary differential equations assuming the existence
of nonuniform dichotomies that are not exponential. Namely,
in [5] were obtained local and global stable manifolds
for polynomial dichotomies and in [4] it was proved the
existence of global stable manifolds for a generalized type of dichotomy that
includes both the polynomial and exponential cases. Also in
[3] Barreira and Valls obtained
local stable manifolds for perturbations of linear equations assuming a
dichotomy that follows growth rates of the form \e^{\rho(t)} where
\rho:\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}\to\mathds{R}^{+}_{0} is an increasing differentiable function satisfying
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{\log t}{\rho(t)}=0.
(1)
This definition, in spite of being very general, does not include some of the
growth rates considered in this paper, namely, due to (1), this
definition do not include for example the polynomial case studied in
[5] and the growth rates of
Examples 3.4
and 3.5. In the discrete time setting, the
existence of global and local stable manifolds for perturbations of some
nonuniform polynomial dichotomies was discussed
in [6].
The main objective of this paper is to obtain local stable manifolds for
perturbations of nonautonomous linear ordinary differential equations, assuming
that the evolution operator associated with the linear equation admits a
dichotomy with growth rates given by increasing functions that go to infinity
(and therefore more general than the mentioned above). In fact we do not need
to assume condition (1) and we allow growth rates given by
non-differentiable functions as well as different growth rates in the uniform
and in the nonuniform parts of the dichotomy. We also would like to emphasize
that the dichotomies considered here include as a particular case the ones
considered in [1] and [5] and
the theorems proved there, respectively, for nonuniform exponential dichotomies
and for nonuniform polynomial dichotomies, are particular cases of the result
presented in this paper. We also give new examples of growth rates to which our
local stable manifold theorem can be applied. We emphasize that the Lyapunov
exponent considered in [2], for Hilbert spaces, is
zero or infinity for most of the dichotomies considered in this paper.
The content of the paper is as follows: in Section 2 we
establish the setting, we define the dichotomies and we state the main theorem;
in Section 3 we give examples of nonuniform
(\mu,\nu) -dichotomies for each differentiable growth rates in our family of
growth rates and examples of growth rates that verify the conditions of the
main theorem;
in Section 4 we prove the main theorem; finally, in
Section 5, we study how the manifolds obtained vary
with the perturbations considered.
2. Main result
Let X be a Banach space and denote by B(X) the space of bounded linear
operators acting on X . Given a continuous function A\colon\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}\to B(X) , we consider the initial value problem
v^{\prime}=A(t)v,\ v(s)=v_{s}
(2)
with s\geq 0 and v_{s}\in X . We assume that each solution
of (2) is global and denote the evolution operator associated
with (2) by T(t,s) , i.e., v(t)=T(t,s)v_{s} for t\geq 0 .
We say that an increasing function \mu\colon\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}\to[1,+\infty[ is a
growth rate if \mu(0)=1 and
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mu(t)=+\infty.
Let \mu and \nu be growth rates. We say that equation (2)
admits a nonuniform (\mu,\nu) -dichotomy in \mathds{R}^{+}_{0} if, for each t\geq 0 , there are projections P(t) such that
P(t)T(t,s)=T(t,s)P(s),\ t,s\geq 0
and constants D\geq 1 , a<0\leq b and \varepsilon\geq 0 such that, for every t\geq s\geq 0 ,
\displaystyle\|T(t,s)P(s)\|\leq D\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon},
(3)
\displaystyle\|T(t,s)^{-1}Q(t)\|\leq D\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-b}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon},
(4)
where Q(t)=\Id-P(t) is the complementary projection. When \varepsilon=0 we say
that we have a uniform (\mu,\nu) -dichotomy or simply a
(\mu,\nu) -dichotomy.
For each t\geq 0 , we define the linear subspaces
E(t)=P(t)X\quad\text{ and }\quad F(t)=Q(t)X.
Without loss of generality, we always identify the spaces E(t)\times F(t)
and E(t)\oplus F(t) as the same space and we equip these spaces with the
norm given by
\|(x,y)\|=\|x\|+\|y\|
for each (x,y)\in E(t)\times F(t) . The unique solution of (2)
can be written in the form
v(t)=\left(U(t,s)\xi,V(t,s)\eta\right),\ t\geq s
where v_{s}=\left(\xi,\eta\right)\in E(s)\times F(s) and
U(t,s):=P(t)T(t,s)P(s)\quad\text{ and }\quad V(t,s):=Q(t)T(t,s)Q(s).
In this paper we are going to address the problem of obtaining stable manifolds
for the nonlinear problem
v^{\prime}=A(t)v+f(t,v),\ v(s)=v_{s}
(5)
when equation (2) admits a nonuniform (\mu,\nu) -dichotomy and
there are c>0 and q>1 such that the perturbations f\colon\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}\times X\to X verify, for u,v\in X and t\in\mathds{R}_{0}^{+} , the following conditions
\displaystyle f(t,0)=0,
(6)
\displaystyle\|f(t,u)-f(t,v)\|\leq c\|u-v\|(\|u\|+\|v\|)^{q}.
(7)
Note that, making v=0 in (7), we have
\|f(t,u)\|\leq c\|u\|^{q+1}.
(8)
for every u\in X .
Writing the unique solution of (5) in the form
(x(t,s,v_{s}),y(t,s,v_{s}))\in E(t)\times F(t),
problem (5) is equivalent to the following problem
\displaystyle x(t)=U(t,s)\xi+\int_{s}^{t}U(t,r)f(r,x(r),y(r))\,dr,
(9)
\displaystyle y(t)=V(t,s)\eta+\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)f(r,x(r),y(r))\,dr,
(10)
where v_{s}=(\xi,\eta)\in E(s)\times F(s) .
For each (s,v_{s}) we consider the semiflow
\Psi_{\tau}(s,v_{s})=\left(s+\tau,x(s+\tau,s,v_{s}),y(s+\tau,s,v_{s})\right),\tau\geq 0.
(11)
Given \delta>0 and a decreasing function \beta\colon\mathds{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathds{R}_{0}^{+}
we use the following notation
G_{\delta,\beta}=\bigcup\limits_{s\in\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}}\left\{s\right\}\times B_{s,\delta,\beta},
where B_{s,\delta,\beta} is the open ball of E(s) centered at 0 and with
radius \delta\beta(s) .
Let \mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} be the space of functions
\phi\colon G_{\delta,\beta}\to X
such that, for every (s,\xi),(s,\bar{\xi})\in G_{\delta,\beta} the following
conditions hold
\displaystyle\phi(s,\xi)\in F(s),
(12)
\displaystyle\phi(s,0)=0,
(13)
\displaystyle\|\phi(s,\xi)-\phi(s,\bar{\xi})\|\leq\|\xi-\bar{\xi}\|.
(14)
By (13), putting \bar{\xi}=0 in (14), we
immediately conclude that
\|\phi(s,\xi)\|\leq\|\xi\|
(15)
for every (s,\xi)\in G_{\delta,\beta} . We equip the space
\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} with the metric defined by
\|\phi-\psi\|^{\prime}=\sup\left\{\dfrac{\|\phi(s,\xi)-\psi(s,\xi)\|}{\|\xi\|}:(s,\xi)\in G_{\delta,\beta},\ \xi\neq 0\right\}
(16)
for every \phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} . By (15) it
follows that, for every (s,\xi)\in G_{\delta,\beta} , \|\phi(s,\xi)\|\leq\delta\beta(s)\leq\delta\beta(0) and this implies that \mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta}
is a complete metric space with the metric given by (16).
We also have to consider the space \mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} of continuous
functions \phi:G\to X , with
G=\bigcup_{s\in\mathds{R}_{0}^{+}}\,\{s\}\times E(s),
such that \phi(s,\xi)\in F(s) for every (s,\xi)\in G , the restriction
\phi|_{G_{\delta,\beta}}\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} and
\phi(s,\xi)=\phi\left(s,\dfrac{\delta\beta(s)\xi}{\|\xi\|}\right)\text{ whenever $\xi\not\in B_{s,\delta,\beta}$.}
Furthermore, since for every \phi\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} , we have a unique
Lipschitz extension of \phi to \bigcup_{t\geq 0}\left\{t\right\}\times\overline{B_{t,\delta,\beta}} , where \overline{B_{t,\delta,\beta}} is the
closure of the ball B_{t,\delta,\beta} , there is a one-to-one correspondence
between \mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} and \mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} . This one-to-one
correspondence allow us to defined a metric in \mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} using the
metric in \mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} . Namely, this metric can be defined by
\|\phi-\psi\|^{\prime}=\|(\phi-\psi)|_{G_{\delta,\beta}}\|^{\prime},
(17)
for every \phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} and where the right hand side
is given by (16). With this metric \mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} is
a complete metric space. Moreover, given \phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} ,
it follows that
\displaystyle\|\phi(s,\xi)-\phi(s,\bar{\xi})\|\leq 2\|\xi-\bar{\xi}\|
(18)
\displaystyle\|\phi(s,\xi)-\psi(s,\xi)\|\leq\|\phi-\psi\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|
(19)
for every (s,\xi),(s,\bar{\xi})\in G .
For every \phi\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} we define the graph
\mathcal{V}_{\phi,\delta,\beta}=\left\{(s,\xi,\phi(s,\xi)):(s,\xi)\in G_{\delta,\beta}\right\}.
(20)
We now formulate our stable manifold theorem.
Theorem 2.1.
Given a Banach space X , let f:\mathds{R}^{+}_{0}\times X\to X be a function
satisfying (6) and (7) for some c>0
and q>1 . Suppose that equation (2) admits a nonuniform
(\mu,\nu) -dichotomy in \mathds{R}^{+}_{0} for some growth rates \mu and \nu , D\geq 1 , a<0\leq b and \varepsilon\geq 0 . Assume that
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mu(t)^{a-b}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon}=0
(21)
and
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mu(r)^{aq}\nu(r)^{\varepsilon}\,dr\text{ is convergent}.
(22)
Define the functions \beta,\tilde{\beta}\colon\mathds{R}_{0}^{+}\to\mathds{R}_{0}^{+} by
\beta(t)=\dfrac{\mu(t)^{a}}{\nu(t)^{\varepsilon(1+1/q)}\left(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}\mu(r)^{aq}\nu(r)^{\varepsilon}\,dr\right)^{1/q}}.
(23)
and \tilde{\beta}(t)=\beta(t)\nu(t)^{-\varepsilon} and suppose that
\beta(t)\text{ and }\mu(t)^{a}\beta(t)^{-1}\text{ are decreasing.}
(24)
Then, for every C>D , choosing \delta>0 sufficiently small,
there is a unique \phi\in\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\beta} such that
\Psi_{\tau}\left(\mathcal{V}_{\phi,\frac{\delta}{C},\tilde{\beta}}\right)\subset\mathcal{V}_{\phi,\delta,\beta}
(25)
for every \tau\geq 0 , where \Psi_{\tau} is given by (11) and
\mathcal{V}_{\phi,\frac{\delta}{C},\tilde{\beta}} and \mathcal{V}_{\phi,\delta,\beta}
are given by (20). Furthermore, given s\geq 0 , we
have
\|\Psi_{t-s}(p_{s,\xi})-\Psi_{t-s}(p_{s,\bar{\xi}})\|\leq 2C\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon}\|\xi-\bar{\xi}\|\\
for every t\geq s and \xi,\bar{\xi}\in B_{s,\frac{\delta}{C},\tilde{\beta}} , where
p_{s,\xi}=(s,\xi,\phi(s,\xi)) .
3. Examples
We start with an example of a nonautonomous linear equation that admits a
nonuniform (\mu,\nu) -dichotomy with arbitrary differentiable growth rates
\mu and \nu .
Example 3.1.
Let \varepsilon>0 and a<0\leq b . Put \omega=\varepsilon/2 and let \mu,\nu
be arbitrary differentiable growth rates. The differential
equation in \mathds{R}^{2} given by
\begin{cases}u^{\prime}=\left(\dfrac{a\mu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}+\dfrac{\omega\nu^{\prime}(t)}{\nu(t)}(\cos t-1)-\omega\log\nu(t)\sin t\right)u\\[8.53581pt]
v^{\prime}=\left(\dfrac{b\mu(t)}{\mu^{\prime}(t)}-\dfrac{\omega\nu^{\prime}(t)}{\mu(t)}(\cos t-1)+\omega\log\mu(t)\sin t\right)v\end{cases}
(26)
has the following evolution operator
T(t,s)(u,v)=(U(t,s)u,V(t,s)v),
where
\displaystyle U(t,s)=\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}e^{\omega\log\nu(t)(\cos t-1)-\omega\log\nu(s)(\cos s-1)},
\displaystyle V(t,s)=\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{b}e^{-\omega\log\nu(t)(\cos t-1)+\omega\log\nu(s)(\cos s-1)}.
Using the projections P(t)\colon\mathds{R}^{2}\to\mathds{R}^{2} defined by P(t)(u,v)=(u,0) we have
\displaystyle\|T(t,s)P(s)\|=\left|U(t,s)\right|\leq\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon}
\displaystyle\|T(t,s)^{-1}Q(t)\|=\left|V(t,s)^{-1}\right|\leq\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-b}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon}
and thus (26) admits a (\mu,\nu) -dichotomy. Moreover,
if t=2k\pi and s=(2k-1)\pi , k\in\mathds{N} , then
U(t,s)=\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon}
and this ensures us that the nonuniform part can not be removed.
Now we will give examples of application of Theorem 2.1.
Example 3.2.
If \mu(t)=\nu(t)=\e^{t} , we get the local stable manifold theorem
obtained by Barreira and Valls in [1]. In fact,
in this case condition (21) becomes a+\varepsilon<b and
condition (22) becomes aq+\varepsilon<0 . The function \beta
is given by
\beta(t)=\left|a\textperiodcentered q+\varepsilon\right|^{1/q}\e^{-\varepsilon(1+2/q)t}
which is a decreasing function and
\mu(t)\beta(t)^{-1}=\left|a\textperiodcentered q+\varepsilon\right|^{1/q}\e^{(a+\varepsilon(1+2/q))t}
is decreasing if a+\varepsilon(1+2/q)<0 .
Example 3.3.
Making \mu(t)=\nu(t)=1+t , we get the local stable manifold theorem
obtained by the present authors in [5]. In this case,
condition (21) becomes a+\varepsilon<b and
condition (22) becomes aq+\varepsilon+1<0 . Moreover,
\beta(t)=\left|a\textperiodcentered q+\varepsilon+1\right|^{1/q}(1+t)^{-(\varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q)t}
is a decreasing function and
\mu(t)\beta(t)^{-1}=\left|a\textperiodcentered q+\varepsilon+1\right|^{1/q}(1+t)^{(a+\varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q)t}
is a decreasing function if a+\varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q<0 .
In the next examples, we consider new growth rates for which
Theorem 2.1 holds. Recall that Example 3.1 allows us to
construct an example of a differential equation whose evolution operator has a
dichotomy with the growth rates given.
Example 3.4.
Consider
\mu(t)=(1+t)(1+\log(1+t))^{\lambda}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \nu(t)=1+\log(1+t)
with \lambda>0 . Then
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mu(t)^{a-b}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon}=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\,(1+t)^{a-b}(1+\log(1+t))^{(a-b)\lambda+\varepsilon}=0
and
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mu(t)^{aq}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon}\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{aq}(1+\log(1+t))^{aq\lambda+\varepsilon}\,dt
is convergent if aq<-1 or aq=-1\ \wedge\ \varepsilon-\lambda<-1 . When
aq=-1 and \varepsilon-\lambda<-1 we have that
\beta(t)=(\lambda-\varepsilon-1)^{1/q}(t+1)^{-1/q}(1+\log(1+t))^{-\varepsilon(1+2/q)-1/q}
is a decreasing function and
\mu(t)^{a}\beta(t)^{-1}=(\lambda-\varepsilon-1)^{-1/q}(1+\log(1+t))^{\varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q}
is decreasing if \varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q<0 . Hence, taking into
account that \varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q<0 implies that \varepsilon-\lambda<-1 ,
for these growth rates we have a local stable manifold theorem if aq=-1
and \varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q<0 .
Example 3.5.
Let
\mu(t)=(1+t)(1+\log(1+t))(1+\log(1+(\log(1+t)))^{\lambda}
and
\nu(t)=1+\log(1+(\log(1+t))
with \lambda>0 . Then
\begin{split}&\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mu(t)^{a-b}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon}\\
&=\lim_{t\to+\infty}\,(1+t)^{a-b}(1+\log(1+t))^{a-b}(1+\log(1+\log(1+t)))^{(a-b)\lambda+\varepsilon}\\
&=0\end{split}
and
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mu(t)^{aq}\nu(t)^{\varepsilon}\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{aq}(1+\log(1+t))^{aq}(1+\log(1+\log(1+t)))^{aq\lambda+\varepsilon}\,dt
is convergent if aq<-1 or aq=-1\ \wedge\ \varepsilon-\lambda<-1 . When
aq=-1 and \varepsilon-\lambda<-1 we have that
\beta(t)=(\lambda-\varepsilon-1)^{1/q}(t+1)^{-1/q}(1+\log(1+t))^{-1/q}(1+\log(1+\log(1+t)))^{-\varepsilon(1+2/q)-1/q}
is a decreasing function and
\mu(t)^{a}\beta(t)^{-1}=(\lambda-\varepsilon-1)^{-1/q}(1+\log(1+\log(1+t)))^{\varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q}
is decreasing if \varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q<0 . Hence, if aq=-1 and
\varepsilon(1+2/q)+1/q-\lambda/q<0 we have again a local stable manifold
theorem.
4. Proof of Theorem 2.1
From (9) and (10) we conclude that, to
prove (25), we must have
\displaystyle x(t,\xi)=U(t,s)\xi+\int_{s}^{t}U(t,r)f(r,x(r,\xi),\phi(r,x(r,\xi)))\,dr,
(27)
\displaystyle\phi(t,x(t,\xi))=V(t,s)\phi(s,\xi)+\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)f(r,x(r,\xi),\phi(r,x(r,\xi)))\,dr
(28)
for every s\geq 0 , t\geq s and \xi\in B_{s,\frac{\delta}{C},\tilde{\beta}} . We are going to prove
that (27) and (28) hold using the Banach fixed
point theorem.
Thus, let \mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} be the space of functions
x:[s,+\infty[\times B_{s,\delta,\beta}\to X
such that, for every t\geq s and \xi,\bar{\xi}\in B_{s,\delta,\beta} , we
have
\displaystyle x(t,\xi)\in E(t),
(29)
\displaystyle x(s,\xi)=\xi,\ x(t,0)=0,
(30)
\displaystyle\|x(t,\xi)-x(t,\bar{\xi})\|\leq C\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon}\|\xi-\bar{\xi}\|.
(31)
Making \bar{\xi}=0 in (31) we obtain the following estimate
\|x(t,\xi)\|\leq C\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon}\|\xi\|\leq C\delta\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon}\beta(s).
(32)
We equip \mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} with the metric defined by
\|x-y\|^{\prime}=\sup\left\{\dfrac{\|x(t,\xi)-y(t,\xi)\|}{\|\xi\|}\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{-a}\nu(s)^{-\varepsilon}\colon t\geq s,\ \xi\in B_{s,\delta,\beta}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}
(33)
for every x,y\in\mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} . With this metric
\mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} is a complete metric space.
Given x\in\mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} and \phi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} we use
the following notation
\phi_{x}(r,\xi)=\phi(r,x(r,\xi))\text{ and }f_{x,\phi}(r,\xi)=f(t,x(r,\xi),\phi_{x}(r,\xi)).
Lemma 4.1.
For every \phi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} , choosing \delta>0
sufficiently small, there is one and only one x=x_{\phi}\in\mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} such that
x(t,\xi)=U(t,s)\xi+\int_{s}^{t}U(t,r)f_{x,\phi}(r,\xi)\,dr
(34)
for every t\geq s and \xi\in B_{s,\delta,\beta} . Moreover, choosing
\delta>0 sufficiently small, we have
\|x_{\phi}(t,\xi)-x_{\psi}(t,\xi)\|\leq C\left(\dfrac{\mu(t)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{a}\|\xi\|\cdot\|\phi-\psi\|^{\prime}
(35)
for every \phi,\psi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}_{\delta,\beta} , every t\geq s and every
\xi\in B_{s,\delta,\beta} .
Proof.
In \mathcal{B}_{s,\delta,\beta} we define an operator J=J_{\phi} by
(Jx)(t,\xi)=U(t,s)\xi+\int_{s}^{t}U(t,r)f_{x,\phi}(r,\xi)\,dr.
Obviously, (Jx)(s,\xi)=\xi for every \xi\in B_{s,\delta,\beta} and
from (30), (13) and (6)
it follows that (Jx)(t,0)=0 for every t\geq s . Moreover, Jx
satisfies (29) for every t\geq s and every \xi\in B_{s,\delta,\beta} .
By (7) and (18) it follows for every r\geq s and every \xi\in B_{s,\delta,\beta} that
\displaystyle\|f_{x,\phi}(r,\xi)-f_{x,\phi}(r,\bar{\xi})\|
\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|x(r,\xi)-x(r,\bar{\xi})\|+\|\phi_{x}(r,\xi)-\phi_{x}(r,\bar{\xi})\|\right)\times
\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}\times(\|x(r,\xi)\|+\|\phi_{x}(r,\xi)\|+\|x(r,\bar{\xi})\|+\|\phi_{x}(r,\bar{\xi})\|)^{q}
\displaystyle\leq 3^{q+1}c\,\|x(r,\xi)-x(r,\bar{\xi})\|\left(\|x(r,\xi)\|+\|x(r,\bar{\xi})\|\right)^{q}
and by (31) and (32) we get the following
estimate
\|f_{x,\phi}(r,\xi)-f_{x,\phi}(r,\bar{\xi})\|\leq 2^{q}3^{q+1}c\,C^{q+1}\delta^{q}\left(\dfrac{\mu(r)}{\mu(s)}\right)^{\!aq+a}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon(q+1)}\beta(s)^{q}\|\xi-\bar{\xi}\|.
(36)
From (3), the last estimate and because by (23) we
have
\mu(s)^{-aq}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon(q+1)}\beta(s)^{q}\displaystyle\int_{s}^{+\infty}\mu(r)^{aq}\nu(r)^{\varepsilon}\,dr=1,
(37)
we obtain the following estimate
\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|U(t,r)\|\cdot\|f_{x,\phi}(r,\xi)-f_{x,\phi}(r,\bar{\xi})\|\,dr

&\quad+2^{q}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|\mu(s)^{-aq}\nu(s)^{\varepsilon q}\beta(s)^{q}\int_{s}^{t}\mu(r)^{aq}\nu(r)^{\varepsilon}\,dr\\
&\leq 3^{q+1}C^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|f-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|+2^{q}3^{q+1}cC^{q}D\delta^{q}\|x_{\phi}-x_{\bar{\phi}}\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|\\
&\quad+2^{q}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|.\end{split}
and using (37) this implies that
\begin{split}&\|x_{\phi}-x_{\bar{\phi}}\|^{\prime}\\
&\leq 3^{q+1}C^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|f-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}+2^{q}3^{q+1}cC^{q}D\delta^{q}\|x_{\phi}-x_{\bar{\phi}}\|^{\prime}+2^{q}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}.\end{split}
Thus, for \delta>0 such that 2^{q}3^{q+1}cC^{q}D\delta^{q}<1/2 we have
\|x_{\phi}-x_{\bar{\phi}}\|^{\prime}\leq 2\cdot 3^{q+1}C^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|f-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}+2^{q+1}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}.
It follows from the last estimate, (46) and
2^{q}3^{q+1}cC^{q}D\delta^{q}<1/2 that
\|\phi(s,\xi)-\bar{\phi}(s,\xi)\|^{\prime}\leq 2\cdot 3^{q+1}C^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|f-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|+2^{q+1}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}\|\xi\|
for every (s,\xi)\in G_{\delta,\beta} .
Hence we get
\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}\leq 2\cdot 3^{q+1}C^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|f-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}+2^{q+1}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}
and if we choose \delta>0 sufficiently small such that 2^{q+1}3^{q}cC^{q+1}D\delta^{q}<1/2 we obtain
\|\phi-\bar{\phi}\|^{\prime}\leq 4\cdot 3^{q+1}C^{q+1}D\delta^{q}\|f-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}
and this proves the theorem.
