1 Introduction
The classical capacity of a quantum channel is the highest rate at
which classical information can be transmitted with vanishing error.
The Holevo–Schumacher–Westmoreland theorem expresses this capacity
as [Hol73, Hol98, SW97]
\displaystyle C({\cal N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\chi({\cal N}^{\otimes n}),
(1)
where \chi denotes the Holevo information.
The optimization allows input states entangled across arbitrarily
many channel uses, and this regularization is necessary in
general [Has09].
A central question is therefore whether a channel’s one-copy
Holevo information already determines its classical capacity.
Even for qubit channels, removing the regularization requires
control of outputs produced by arbitrary entangled inputs.
The generalized amplitude-damping channel (GADC) models a two-level
system exchanging energy with a thermal
environment [KSW20].
It describes spin relaxation in quantum devices, including
superconducting circuits [CB08], and has
applications to optical losses with low-temperature background
noise [ZLW+17].
We write {\cal A}_{\gamma,N} for the channel with damping probability
\gamma and equilibrium occupation N; its action is given
in (8).
The zero-temperature case N=0 is the amplitude-damping channel.
The one-copy Holevo information of the GADC is known through
a scalar optimization [HF07, KSW20].
Earlier work developed computable bounds on the regularized
capacity [WXD18, KSW20, FF21, FST22].
Recently, Tang, Zhu, Bai, and Wang [TZBW26]
determined the classical capacity of amplitude damping through
a triangular block-matrix entropy inequality.
Their result establishes Holevo additivity with arbitrary other
channels for qubit channels admitting a pure output.
A GADC with 0<\gamma,N<1 has no pure output,
so this criterion does not cover the finite-temperature case.
In this work, we resolve the regularization problem for the
full GADC family and determine its exact strong-converse exponent.
For a channel \mathcal{N}, let
P_{\mathrm{s}}^{\star}(n,r;\mathcal{N}) denote the optimal average
decoding success probability over n uses with at least 2^{nr} messages.
We allow arbitrary entangled input codewords and collective
decoding, but no entanglement assistance.
The strong-converse exponent is
\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sc}}(r,\mathcal{N})
\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log P_{\mathrm{s}}^{\star}(n,r;\mathcal{N}).
(2)
It describes the optimal exponential decay of decoding success
at rates above the classical capacity.
Cheng and Tomamichel [CT26, Theorem 5]
proved that the limit exists for every r\geqslant 0 and
\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sc}}(r,\mathcal{N})
\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\left[r-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})\right].
(3)
Thus, determining the exponent reduces to removing the
regularization of the sandwiched Rényi Holevo information
\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}, defined in Section 2.
In contrast to the entropy argument of [TZBW26],
this approach requires control at every order \alpha>1,
not only in the limit \alpha\to 1^{+}.
Theorem 1 (Strong converse exponent).
For every \gamma,N\in[0,1], \alpha>1, and positive integer n,
\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}({\cal A}_{\gamma,N}^{\otimes n})
\displaystyle=n\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}({\cal A}_{\gamma,N}).
(4)
For every r\geqslant 0, the limit in (2)
exists and satisfies
\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{sc}}(r,{\cal A}_{\gamma,N})
\displaystyle=\sup_{\alpha>1}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}[r-\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})].
(5)
Taking \alpha\to 1^{+} in (4) gives
the following capacity formula.
Corollary 2 (Classical capacity).
For every \gamma,N\in[0,1],
\displaystyle C({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})
\displaystyle=\chi({\cal A}_{\gamma,N}).
(6)
The main task is to establish (4). We follow the norm-based approach of Wilde, Winter, and
Yang [WWY14], which relates the sandwiched
Rényi information radius to maximum output norms of sandwiched maps.
Our main technical result establishes the required multiplicativity
for GADCs sandwiched by arbitrary nonnegative diagonal operators
(Theorem 4).
Phase covariance makes these diagonal operators sufficient for
the reference-state optimization.
The construction decomposes the joint output into a convex combination
of two positive operators with triangular factors.
Replacing each block of these factors by its Schatten norm
produces scalar matrices with identical Gram matrices.
A sharp triangular norm-compression inequality bounds each term
by the same one-copy output norm.
This matching property makes the argument applicable even when
the channel has no pure output.
The norm-compression proof extends the log-determinant method
of [TZBW26] from entropy to fractional trace powers.
It establishes the previously missing range 2<p<4,
complementing the known range
p\geqslant 4 [Kin03, Aud08].
