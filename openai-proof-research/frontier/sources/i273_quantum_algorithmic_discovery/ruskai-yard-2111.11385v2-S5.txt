5 Second derivative increases
5.1 Key inequality of Gour-Friedland
To simplify the notation we write \Delta_{B} for \Delta_{X_{B}} etc.
and use {\cal I} to denote the identity operator acting on d\times d matrices. The key result needed to treat the second term in (17) is
\displaystyle 2\phi(\pm\Delta_{B}\otimes\Delta_{C})\leq\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})\otimes{\cal I}_{C}+{\cal I}_{B}\otimes\phi(\Delta_{C}^{2})
(30)
This follows immediately from (24) and the spectral theorem applied to
to \Delta_{B} and \Delta_{C}
This inequality is a substitute for additivity. It implies subadditivity of Q[X,Y]
which leads to superadditivity of D_{2}[X,Y]. In particular, (30)
allows us to easily to treat the basic case
of product perturbations. It also plays an important role in reducing the general
case to that of products because the presence of either {\cal I}_{B} or {\cal I}_{C}
allows us to eliminate many cross terms.
Before beginning the proof, we make the following useful observation.
Remark 6
Let {\cal A} and {\cal B} be vector spaces of matrices and D\in{\cal A}\otimes{\cal B}.
Then one can always write D=\sum_{k}\omega_{k}A_{k}\otimes B_{k} with
\omega_{k}\in{\bf C},\,A_{k}\in{\cal A},\,B_{k}\in{\cal B} and \hbox{\rm Tr}\,A_{j}^{\dagger}A_{k}=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,B_{j}^{\dagger}B_{k}=0.
This is essentially the singular value decomposition of the operator that maps
A\in{\cal A} to \hbox{\rm Tr}_{\cal A}\,A^{\dagger}D\in{\cal B}, which is sometimes referred to as the
Schmidt decomposition.
5.2 Product perturbations in X_{B}^{\perp}\otimes X_{C}^{\perp}
We assume that S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}_{B}} and S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}_{C}} have local minima
at X_{B} and X_{C} respectively.
We first consider the case in which Y_{BC}=Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C}\in{\cal K}^{o}_{B}\otimes{\cal K}^{o}_{C} is a simple tensor product
with \hbox{\rm Tr}\,X_{B}Y_{B}^{\dagger}=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,X_{C}Y_{C}^{\dagger}=0.
If we write Y_{BC}=W_{BC}+iZ_{BC} with W_{BC}=W_{BC}^{\dagger} and Z_{BC}=Z_{BC}^{\dagger}, neither W_{BC} nor Z_{BC}
will be a simple tensor product in general. From the analysis in Section 3.3 we could separately consider
perturbations of the form W_{BC}=W_{B}\otimes W_{C} and Z_{BC}=iZ_{B}\otimes Z_{C}, but this does not simplify the argument.
We find that (30) implies
\displaystyle 2\,Q[X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C}]
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle 2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W_{BC}\phi(\Delta_{B}\otimes\Delta_{C})W_{BC}+2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z_{BC}\phi(-\Delta_{B}\otimes\Delta_{C})]Z_{BC}
(31)
\displaystyle\leq
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W_{BC}\,\big[\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})\otimes{\cal I}_{C}\big](W_{BC})+\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W_{BC}\,\big[{\cal I}_{B}\otimes\phi(\Delta_{C}^{2})\big](W_{BC})
\displaystyle+~\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z_{BC}\big[\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})\otimes{\cal I}_{C}\big](Z_{BC})+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z_{BC}\,\big[{\cal I}_{B}\otimes\phi(\Delta_{C}^{2})\big](Z_{BC})
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C})\big[\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})\otimes{\cal I}_{C}\big](Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C})^{\dagger}
\displaystyle~+~\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C})\,\big[{\cal I}_{B}\otimes\phi(\Delta_{C}^{2})\big](Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C})^{\dagger}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{B}\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})(Y_{B}^{\dagger})+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{C}\,\phi(\Delta_{C}^{2})(Y_{C}^{\dagger})
(32)
where we used (27) to obtain (31).
Only the final step above
requires Y_{BC} to be tensor product.
It then follows from (32) and (28) that
\displaystyle D_{2}[X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{B}\otimes Y_{C}]
\displaystyle\geq
\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\Big(D_{2}[X_{B},Y_{B}]+D_{2}[X_{B},iY_{B}]+D_{2}[X_{C},Y_{C}]+D_{2}[X_{C},iY_{C}]\Big)
(33)
\displaystyle\geq
\displaystyle 0
with strict inequality when both minima are non-degenerate
5.3 General perturbations
First, observe that for X_{B}\otimes X_{C}\in{\cal K}_{B}^{o}\otimes{\cal K}_{C}^{o}
\displaystyle(X_{B}\otimes X_{C})^{\perp}={\rm span}\{(X_{B}\otimes X_{C}^{\perp})\cup(X_{B}^{\perp}\otimes X_{C})\cup(X_{B}^{\perp}\otimes X_{C}^{\perp})\}
(34)
where it is understood that X_{B}^{\perp} and X_{C}^{\perp} are subspaces of {\cal K}_{B} and {\cal K}_{C} respectively.
Remark 6 implies that any matrix in (X_{B}^{\perp}\otimes X_{C}^{\perp})^{o} can be written in the form
\displaystyle\sum_{j}\xi_{j}Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j}\hbox{~~ with ~~}\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{B}^{j}(Y_{B}^{k})^{\dagger}=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{C}^{j}(Y_{C}^{k})^{\dagger}=\delta_{jk}\,.
(35)
Therefore, the most general perturbation we need to consider can be written in the form
\displaystyle Y_{BC}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle u_{1}X_{B}\otimes Y_{C}^{0}+u_{2}Y_{B}^{0}\otimes X_{C}+\sum_{j>0}\xi_{j}Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j}
(36)
where Y_{B}^{0}\in(X_{B}^{\perp})^{o} and Y_{C}^{0}\in(X_{C}^{\perp})^{o}.
Note, however, that \hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{B}^{0}(Y_{B}^{j})^{\dagger} and \hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{B}^{0}(Y_{B}^{j})^{\dagger}
are not zero in general.
The normalization \hbox{\rm Tr}_{BC}Y_{BC}^{\dagger}Y_{BC}=1
implies |u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2}+\sum_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{2}=1.
After applying (30), every cross term with a coefficient of u_{j}\overline{u}_{k} has either \hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}\,X_{B}(Y_{B}^{0})^{\dagger}=0
or \hbox{\rm Tr}_{C}\,X_{C}(Y_{C}^{0})^{\dagger}=0. Similarly, every cross-term with a coefficient of \xi_{j}\overline{\xi}_{k} is zero when j\neq k
by (35).
However, since \hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{C}^{0}(Y_{C}^{j})^{\dagger}\neq 0 in general, the cross terms with coefficient u_{1}\overline{\xi}_{j}
require more care. In this case, we can conclude that, e.g.,
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}\,X_{B}\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})(Y_{B}^{j})^{\dagger})=\hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}\,\phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})(X_{B})Y^{\dagger}=\phi(1)\hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}\,X_{B}Y_{B}^{\dagger}=0
(37)
where we used he fact that \Delta_{B} is self-adjoint with respect to the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, and
\Delta_{X}(X)=L_{X}R_{X}^{-1}(X)=X so that \phi(\Delta_{B}^{2})(X_{B})=\phi(1)X_{B}.
Thus, we can conclude that
\displaystyle Q(X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{BC})=|u_{1}|^{2}Q(X_{B}\otimes X_{C},X_{B}\otimes Y_{C}^{0})
(38)
\displaystyle~+|u_{2}|^{2}Q(X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{B}^{0}\otimes X_{C})+\sum_{j>1}|\xi_{j}|^{2}Q(X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j})\,.
The term -2\hbox{\rm Tr}\,YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2} in (25) also has non-trivial cross terms
with coefficient u_{k}\overline{\xi}_{j}, e.g.
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}_{BC}(X_{B}\otimes Y_{C}^{0})(Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j})^{\dagger}\log(X_{B}^{2}\otimes X_{C}^{2})=\alpha_{j}\big(\hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}X_{B}(Y_{B}^{j})^{\dagger}\log X_{B}^{2}\big)
where \alpha_{j}\equiv\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y_{C}^{0}(Y_{C}^{j})^{\dagger}. Although these terms are not zero,
they appear in pairs as
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}\,\big(\omega X_{B}(Y_{B}^{j})^{\dagger}+\overline{\omega}Y_{B}X_{B}\big)\log X_{B}^{2}=D_{1}[X_{B}\,,\overline{\omega}Y_{B}]
(39)
where \omega=u_{1}\overline{\xi}_{j}\alpha.
Because (39) is of the form (10) and by assumption S(X_{B}^{2}) is a critical point,
(39) is zero.
Thus we can replace Y_{BC}Y_{BC}^{\dagger} in \hbox{\rm Tr}\,YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2} by
\displaystyle|u_{1}|^{2}(X_{B}\otimes Y_{C}^{0})(X_{B}\otimes Y_{C}^{0})^{\dagger}+|u_{2}|^{2}(Y_{B}^{0}\otimes X_{C})(Y_{B}^{0}\otimes X_{C})^{\dagger}+\sum_{j>0}|\xi_{j}|^{2}(Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j})(Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j})^{\dagger}\Big]
(40)
Finally, note that the first term in (25) is S(X^{2})=\big[|u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2}+\sum_{j>0}|\xi_{j}|^{2}\big)S(X^{2})\big].
Thus we conclude that for any normalized perturbation of the form (36)
\displaystyle D_{2}[X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{BC}]
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle|u_{1}|^{2}D_{2}[X_{B},Y_{B}^{0}]+|u_{2}|^{2}D_{2}[X_{C},Y_{C}^{0}]+\sum_{j>0}|\xi_{j}|^{2}D_{2}[X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{B}^{j}\otimes Y_{C}^{j}]
(41)
\displaystyle\geq
\displaystyle 0
where we used (33) and strict inequality holds when both minima are non-degenerate.
This completes the proof of Theorem 2 when X is square and nonsingular.
We emphasize again that we do not compare
D_{2}[X_{B}\otimes X_{C},Y_{BC}] to D_{2}[X_{B},Y_{B}]+D_{2}[X_{C},Y_{C}]. Instead
we use the fact that the second derivative is positive for arbitrary perturbations in {\cal K}_{B}
and {\cal K}_{C} so that we can bound the second derivative by a sum of positive terms.
