3.2 Multiplicativity of maximum output norm
The compression inequality now yields multiplicativity of the maximum
output norm for the GADC sandwiched by any nonnegative diagonal operator.
Theorem 4 (Sandwiched norm multiplicativity).
For every \gamma,N\in[0,1], every nonnegative diagonal H,
every finite-dimensional completely positive map \Phi,
and every 1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty,
\displaystyle\nu_{p}(({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}\otimes\Phi)
\displaystyle=\nu_{p}(({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H})\nu_{p}(\Phi).
(30)
The input and output dimensions of \Phi are arbitrary,
and neither map is required to preserve trace.
The proof decomposes the joint output into a convex combination
of two positive operators with triangular factors.
The decomposition parameter is chosen from the block norms so that
both factors have the same scalar Gram matrix after compression.
Theorem 3 and the triangle inequality then
reduce the joint output norm to a one-copy output norm
times a scalar controlled by \nu_{p}(\Phi).
Proof.
First assume 1\leqslant p<\infty and 0<\gamma,N<1.
Convexity of the Schatten norm reduces the optimization to
normalized pure inputs
|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle\otimes|u\rangle+|1\rangle\otimes|v\rangle,
where \langle u|u\rangle+\langle v|v\rangle=1.
Since \Phi is completely positive, the intermediate output is
positive semidefinite and admits the factorization
\displaystyle R
\displaystyle:=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Phi)(|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|)=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi(|u\rangle\!\langle u|)&\Phi(|u\rangle\!\langle v|)\\
\Phi(|v\rangle\!\langle u|)&\Phi(|v\rangle\!\langle v|)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}X\\
Y\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}X^{\dagger}&Y^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}.
(31)
Set m=\|X\|_{2p}^{2}+\|Y\|_{2p}^{2}.
If m=0, the output vanishes and the desired bound is immediate.
Otherwise, set q=\|Y\|_{2p}^{2}/m and first assume
that both X and Y are nonzero, so 0<q<1.
Step 1: Decompose the output into triangular factors.
Write t=t_{\gamma,N}(q) and D=D_{\gamma,N}(q).
The interior assumptions ensure 0<t<1 and D>0.
Set \lambda_{0}=\gamma(1-N)qt/D and
\lambda_{1}=\gamma N(1-q)(1-t)/D.
The output admits the decomposition
\displaystyle({\cal A}_{\gamma,N}\otimes\operatorname{id})(R)
\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{1}\lambda_{j}M_{j}M_{j}^{\dagger},
(32)
where
\displaystyle M_{0}
\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\dfrac{(1-\gamma)q}{t}}\,X&\sqrt{\dfrac{D}{qt}}\,Y\\[5.69046pt]
\sqrt{\dfrac{t}{q}}\,Y&0\end{pmatrix},
\displaystyle M_{1}
\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\dfrac{1-t}{1-q}}\,X&0\\[5.69046pt]
\sqrt{\dfrac{(1-\gamma)(1-q)}{1-t}}\,Y&\sqrt{\dfrac{D}{(1-q)(1-t)}}\,X\end{pmatrix}.
(33)
Direct block multiplication verifies (32).
The identity D=\gamma(1-N)qt+\gamma N(1-q)(1-t)
shows that the positive weights sum to one.
The factor M_{1} is triangular, and M_{0} becomes triangular
after exchanging its block rows.
Write H=h_{0}|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+h_{1}|1\rangle\!\langle 1| with h_{0},h_{1}\geqslant 0.
For j=0,1, let B_{j} be the scalar matrix obtained by replacing
each block of (H\otimes I)M_{j} by its Schatten 2p-norm.
The choice of q ensures that both scalar Gram matrices coincide:
\displaystyle B_{j}B_{j}^{\dagger}
\displaystyle=m({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}(|\psi_{q}^{+}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{q}^{+}|),\qquad j=0,1.
(34)
Indeed, substitute \|X\|_{2p}=\sqrt{m(1-q)} and
\|Y\|_{2p}=\sqrt{mq}, and use
D=t(1-t)-(1-\gamma)q(1-q).
The result is precisely the matrix in (11),
multiplied by m and sandwiched by H.
Step 2: Norm compression via Theorem 3.
We obtain the norm comparison
\displaystyle\|(({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}\otimes\operatorname{id})(R)\|_{p}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(a)}}{=}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{1}\lambda_{j}(H\otimes I)M_{j}M_{j}^{\dagger}(H\otimes I)\right\|_{p}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(b)}}{\leqslant}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\lambda_{j}\|(H\otimes I)M_{j}\|_{2p}^{2}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(c)}}{\leqslant}\sum_{j=0}^{1}\lambda_{j}\|B_{j}\|_{2p}^{2}
\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{1}\lambda_{j}\|B_{j}B_{j}^{\dagger}\|_{p}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(d)}}{=}m\|({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}(|\psi_{q}^{+}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{q}^{+}|)\|_{p}.
(35)
Step (a) substitutes the output decomposition (32).
Step (b) uses the triangle inequality and
\|AA^{\dagger}\|_{p}=\|A\|_{2p}^{2}.
Step (c) is Theorem 3 at order 2p\geqslant 2.
For M_{0}, apply the same block-row permutation to
(H\otimes I)M_{0} and B_{0}; Schatten norms are unchanged.
The nonnegative diagonal operator H preserves the zero blocks.
The next equality again uses the relation between the two Schatten norms.
Step (d) uses the common Gram matrix in (34)
and the normalization \lambda_{0}+\lambda_{1}=1.
It remains to bound the scalar factor m and the one-copy output norm:
\displaystyle\|(({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}\otimes\Phi)(|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|)\|_{p}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(e)}}{\leqslant}m\|({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}(|\psi_{q}^{+}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{q}^{+}|)\|_{p}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(f)}}{=}\bigl[\|\Phi(|u\rangle\!\langle u|)\|_{p}+\|\Phi(|v\rangle\!\langle v|)\|_{p}\bigr]\cdot\|({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}(|\psi_{q}^{+}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{q}^{+}|)\|_{p}
\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{(g)}}{\leqslant}(\langle u|u\rangle+\langle v|v\rangle)\nu_{p}(\Phi)\nu_{p}(({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H})
\displaystyle=\nu_{p}(\Phi)\nu_{p}(({\cal A}_{\gamma,N})_{H}).
(36)
Step (e) is (35), using the definition of R.
Step (f) uses XX^{\dagger}=\Phi(|u\rangle\!\langle u|),
YY^{\dagger}=\Phi(|v\rangle\!\langle v|), and
\|X\|_{2p}^{2}=\|XX^{\dagger}\|_{p}.
Step (g) follows from homogeneity and the definition of the maximum output
norm, since |\psi_{q}^{+}\rangle is normalized.
The final equality uses the normalization of the input state.
If q=0 or q=1, exactly one of X,Y is nonzero.
Then R is a computational-basis projection tensored with
a positive matrix, and the comparison between the first and last
expressions in (35) holds with equality.
For fixed p<\infty, continuity in \gamma,N extends the bound
to the boundary of [0,1]^{2}, without evaluating singular factorization formulas.
Zero diagonal entries of H are already permitted.
Finally, taking p\to\infty gives the operator-norm endpoint.
Maximizing over inputs proves the upper bound.
Product inputs give the reverse inequality because Schatten
norms multiply on tensor products, completing the proof.
∎
For integer p, sandwiched norm multiplicativity also follows from
the entrywise-positive map theorem of King, Nathanson, and
Ruskai [KNR05, Theorem 3].
The GADC sandwiched by any nonnegative diagonal operator
satisfies that theorem’s matrix-unit positivity condition.
Theorem 4 extends this conclusion
to every real order p\geqslant 1.
In particular, the range 1<p<2 is essential for taking
the order-one limit that determines the capacity.
