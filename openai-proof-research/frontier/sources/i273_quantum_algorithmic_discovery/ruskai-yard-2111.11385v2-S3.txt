3 Derivates of entropy expressions
3.1 Derivatives of the entropy
Let \rho(t) be a one parameter family of density matrices
which is twice differentiable and has full rank in a neighborhood of t=0.
The derivatives of S[\rho(t)]=-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho(t)\log\rho(t) can be easily obtained from
those for \log\rho(t). For completeness,
these are derived using an integral representation for \log\rho in Appendix B.
It is well known [7] and shown in Appendix B. that
\displaystyle\frac{d~}{dt}\log\rho(t)\Big|_{t=t_{1}}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho(t_{1})+uI}\rho^{\prime}(t_{1})\frac{1}{\rho(t_{1})+uI}du
(5)
Next, observe that the derivative of \log\rho(t) is always orthogonal to \rho(t), i.e.,
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho(t)+uI}\rho^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{\rho(t)+uI}du
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{\rho(t)+uI}\rho(t)\frac{1}{\rho(t)+uI}du\qquad
(6)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\prime}(t)\rho(t)\rho(t)^{-1}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\prime}(t)=0
where we used the cyclicity of the trace.
It follows immediately from (6) that
\displaystyle\frac{d~}{dt}S[\rho(t)]=-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\prime}(t)\log\rho(t)-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho(t)\frac{d~}{dt}\log\rho(t)=-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\prime}(t)\log\rho(t)
(7)
\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}~}{dt^{2}}S[\rho(t)]
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)\log\rho(t)-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\rho^{\prime}(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho(t)+uI}\rho^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{\rho(t)+uI}\,du
(8)
3.2 Derivatives in matrix form
We now consider X,Y\in{\cal K}_{B} a subspace of M_{d_{B}} as described in Section 2.2
with X>0 positive definite, \hbox{\rm Tr}\,X^{2}=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,YY^{\dagger}=1. We also assume that \hbox{\rm Tr}\,XY^{\dagger}=0 and
define X(t)\equiv\sqrt{1-t^{2}}X+tY, so that \hbox{\rm Tr}\,X(t)X(t)^{\dagger}=1. Since X^{2} is non-singular
we can apply (7) and (8) to
\displaystyle\rho(t)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,X+tY)(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,X+tY)^{\dagger}
(9)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle(1-t^{2})X^{2}+t\sqrt{1-t^{2}}(YX+XY^{\dagger})+t^{2}YY^{\dagger}\qquad
to obtain
\displaystyle D_{1}[X,Y]\equiv\frac{d~}{dt}S[\rho(t)]\bigg|_{t=0}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(YX+XY^{\dagger})\log X^{2}=-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\Gamma_{1}\log X^{2}
(10)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(Y+Y^{\dagger})\,X\log X^{2}
\displaystyle D_{2}[X,Y]\equiv\frac{d^{2}~}{dt^{2}}S[\rho(t)]\bigg|_{t=0}=-2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\Gamma_{2}\log X^{2}-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\Gamma_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{X^{2}+uI}\Gamma_{1}\frac{1}{X^{2}+uI}\,du
(11)
where \Gamma_{1}=YX+XY^{\dagger}=\rho^{\prime}(0) and
\Gamma_{2}=YY^{\dagger}-X^{2}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\prime\prime}(0).
It is convenient to write
\displaystyle D_{2}[X,Y]=R[X,Y]-Q[X,Y]
(12)
where
\displaystyle R[X,Y]
\displaystyle\equiv
\displaystyle-2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2})-2\,S(X^{2})
(13)
\displaystyle Q[X,Y]
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(YX+XY^{\dagger})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{X^{2}+uI}(YX+XY^{\dagger})\frac{1}{X^{2}+uI}\,du~.
(14)
Observe that -2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2}) is the only positive term in the second derivative.
Therefore, if S(X^{2}) is local minimum, this term must dominate.
3.3 Modular operator expression for second derivative
We can rewrite (14) in an elegant and useful way using the modular operator
formalism.
Let L_{A}(W)=AW and R_{B}(W)=WB denote the operations of left and right multiplication
on M_{d}. Since L_{A}R_{B}(W)=AWB=R_{B}L_{A}(W), the operators
L_{A},R_{B} commute even when AB\neq BA.
When A,B are Hermitian, positive semi-definite, or positive definite,
then L_{A} and R_{B} are correspondingly Hermitian, positive semi-definite, and positive definite with respect to the
Hilbert Schmidt inner product. Moreover f(L_{A})=L_{f(A)} etc.
Now write Y=W+iZ with11
1
If we had not assumed that X>0, we could obtain an equivalent result by writing
X=\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}\,V and letting VY^{\dagger}=W-iZ with
W={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(VY^{\dagger}+YV^{\dagger}) and Z=i{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(VY^{\dagger}-YV^{\dagger}).
W={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(Y^{\dagger}+Y)=W^{\dagger} and Z=i{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(Y^{\dagger}-Y)=Z^{\dagger}.
Then
\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle(W+iZ)X+X\,(W-iZ)
(15)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle(L_{X}+R_{X})W)-i(L_{X}-R_{X})(Z)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle M_{+}(W)-iM_{-}(Z)\qquad\hbox{with}\qquad M_{\pm}=L_{X}\pm R_{X}\,.
It follows from (10) that D_{1}[X,Y]=2D_{1}[X,W]=-2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,WX\log X^{2}.
To treat D_{2}[X,Y] we will use the following well-known formula [7] [5, Appendix A]
which follows from (B.5) in Appendix B and holds whenever \rho>0 is positive definite.
\displaystyle\frac{\log L_{\rho}-\log R_{\rho}}{L_{\rho}-R_{\rho}}(\Gamma)=\frac{\log\Delta_{\rho}}{L_{\rho}-R_{\rho}}(\Gamma)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\rho+tI}\,\Gamma\,\frac{1}{\rho+tI}\,dt
(16)
where \Delta_{\rho}=L_{\rho}R_{\rho}^{-1} is the modular operator.
Then (14) becomes
\displaystyle Q[X,Y]=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\Gamma_{1}\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{L_{X^{2}}-R_{X^{2}}}(\Gamma_{1})=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\Gamma_{1}\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}\,.\Gamma_{1}
(17)
Then the fact that M_{\pm} and \Delta_{X^{2}} all commute implies that (17) can be written as
\displaystyle Q[X,Y]
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,\big[M_{+}(W)-iM_{-}(Z)\big]\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{L_{X^{2}}-R_{X^{2}}}\big[M_{+}(W)-iM_{-}(Z)\big]
(18)
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,M_{+}(W)\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}M_{+}(W)-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,M_{-}Z\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}M_{-}(Z)
\displaystyle\qquad-i\hbox{\rm Tr}\,M_{+}(W)\frac{\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}M_{-}(Z)-i\hbox{\rm Tr}\,M_{-}(Z)\frac{\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}M_{+}(W)\,.
Since the cyclicity of the trace implies \hbox{\rm Tr}\,M_{\pm}(A)B=\pm\hbox{\rm Tr}\,AM_{\pm}(B) this becomes
\displaystyle Q[X,Y]
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,M_{+}\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}M_{+}(W)+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,M_{-}\frac{\log\Delta_{X^{2}}}{M_{+}M_{-}}M_{-}(Z)
(19)
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,\frac{M_{+}}{M_{-}}\log\Delta_{X^{2}}W+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,\frac{M_{-}}{M_{+}}\log\Delta_{X^{2}}Z
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,\frac{\Delta_{X}+I}{\Delta_{X}-I}\log\Delta_{X^{2}}\,W+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,\frac{\Delta_{X}-I}{\Delta_{X}+I}\log\Delta_{X^{2}}\,Z
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,\phi(\Delta_{X})(W)+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,\phi(-\Delta_{X})(Z)
(20)
where we used
\displaystyle\frac{M_{\pm}}{M_{\mp}}=\frac{L_{X}\pm R_{X}}{L_{X}\mp R_{X}}=\frac{\Delta_{X}\pm 1}{\Delta_{X}\mp 1}
(21)
and define
\displaystyle\phi(a)\equiv\frac{a+1}{a-1}\log a^{2}~.
(22)
The properties of \phi, which plays a key role, are summarized in Appendix A,
where we also include two proofs of the following inequality of Gour and Friedland [3, Lemma 6].
\displaystyle 2\phi(bc)\leq\phi(b)+\phi(-b)+\phi(c)+\phi(-c).
(23)
Since \phi(a)+\phi(-a)=\phi(a^{2}), this can be rewritten as
\displaystyle 2\phi(bc)\leq\phi(b^{2})+\phi(c^{2}).
(24)
We can summarize the results of this section in the following
Theorem 5
When X>0 is positive definite,
\displaystyle D_{2}[X,Y]
\displaystyle\equiv
\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}~}{dt^{2}}S\Big((\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,X+tY)(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,X+tY)^{\dagger}\Big)\bigg|_{t=0}
(25)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-2\,S(X^{2})-2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2}-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,\phi(\Delta_{X})(W)-\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,\phi(-\Delta_{X})(Z)
with W,Z as defined above (15) and \phi(a) in (22).
Observe that \phi(\pm\Delta_{X}) is self-adjoint with respect to the Hilbert Schmidt inner product so that when Y=W+iZ,
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,\phi(\pm\Delta_{X})(W)+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,\phi(\pm\Delta_{X})(Z)
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,(W+iZ)\phi(\pm\Delta_{X})(W-iZ)
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y\phi(\pm\Delta_{X})(Y^{\dagger}).
(26)
which implies
\displaystyle\hbox{\rm Tr}\,W\,\phi(\Delta_{X}^{2})(W)+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Z\,\phi(\Delta_{X}^{2})(Z)
\displaystyle=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y\phi(\Delta_{X}^{2})(Y^{\dagger})\,.
(27)
Observe that we can also write
\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}D_{2}[X,Y]+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}D_{2}[X,iY]
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-2\,S(X^{2})-2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2}+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y[\phi(\Delta_{X})+\phi(-\Delta_{X})](Y^{\dagger})
(28)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-2\,S(X^{2})-2\,\hbox{\rm Tr}\,YY^{\dagger}\log X^{2}+\hbox{\rm Tr}\,Y\phi(\Delta_{X}^{2})Y^{\dagger}
