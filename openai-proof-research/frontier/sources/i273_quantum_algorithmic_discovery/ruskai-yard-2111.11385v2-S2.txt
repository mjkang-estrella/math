2 Reformulations
2.1 Matrix reformulation
Recall that a quantum channel \Phi:M_{d_{A}}\mapsto M_{d_{B}} is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPT) map
which can be represented using an auxiliary space {\bf C}_{d_{E}} of dim d_{E}\leq d_{A}d_{B} via the Stinespring
representation in the form
\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)=\hbox{\rm Tr}_{E}\,K\rho K^{\dagger},
(2)
where K:{\bf C}_{d_{A}}\mapsto{\bf C}_{d_{B}}\otimes{\bf C}_{d_{E}} satisfies K^{\dagger}K=I_{A}.
Since the minimal output entropy is the optimization of a concave function over a convex set, it suffices to consider pure
inputs |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|. Then K|\psi\rangle\in{\bf C}_{d_{B}}\otimes{\bf C}_{d_{E}}.
If we interpret u,v as column vectors then \Omega|u\otimes v\rangle=uv^{T} extended by linearity takes vectors in
{\bf C}_{d_{B}}\otimes{\bf C}_{d_{E}} to d_{B}\times d_{E} matrices.
If |\psi\rangle\in{\bf C}_{d_{A}} and X=\Omega\big(K|\psi\rangle\big) then X:{\bf C}_{d_{E}}\mapsto{\bf C}_{d_{B}}
so that X is a d_{B}\times d_{E} matrix and
\displaystyle XX^{\dagger}=\Phi(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)=\hbox{\rm Tr}_{E}\,K|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|K^{\dagger}
(3)
The entropy S\big(XX^{\dagger}\big)
is the entanglement entropy of the bipartite state W|\psi\rangle. Thus the problem of finding
the minimal output entropy of a quantum channel is equivalent to finding
the minimal entanglement entropy of
a bipartite state |\psi_{BE}\rangle in the subspace KK^{\dagger}({\bf C}_{d_{B}}\otimes{\bf C}_{d_{E}}).
Moreover, if {\cal K}\equiv\Omega\big[KK^{\dagger}({\bf C}_{d_{B}}\otimes{\bf C}_{d_{E}})\big]
is the corresponding subspace of d_{B}\times d_{E} matrices this is
equivalent to finding \inf_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}}S(XX^{\dagger}) where {\cal K}^{o}=\{X\in{\cal K}:\hbox{\rm Tr}\,XX^{\dagger}=1\} is
the unit sphere in {\cal K}. Ke have thus proved the following
Theorem 1
Let \Phi:M_{d_{A}}\mapsto M_{d_{B}} be a quantum channel and {\cal K} the subspace of d_{B}\times d_{E} matrices
defined via the Stinespring representation as above. Then the entropy S[\Phi(\rho)] has a local minimum at
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi| if and only if S(XX^{\dagger}) with X\in{\cal K}^{o} has a local minimum at X=\Omega K|\psi\rangle.
In this framework X^{T}\overline{X}=\Phi^{C}(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|) is the output of the complementary channel.
Since XX^{\dagger} and X^{\dagger}X have the same non-zero eigenvalues, S(XX^{\dagger})=S(X^{\dagger}X)=S(X^{T}\overline{X}).
Note that X^{\dagger}X=\hbox{\rm Tr}_{B}\overline{K}|\overline{\psi}\rangle\langle\overline{\psi}|K^{T} is an output of the complementary channel associated with \overline{K}.
It follows from Theorem 1 that the local additivity of the minimal output entropy of a pair of
quantum channels is equivalent to the following.
Theorem 2
If S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}_{B}} and S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}_{C}} have local minima
at X_{B} and X_{C} respectively, then
S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in({\cal K}_{B}\otimes{\cal K}_{C})^{o}} has a local minimum at X_{B}\otimes X_{C}.
This result is proved under the assumption
that X_{B} and X_{C} are square positive definite matrices in Sections 3 to 5.
In the next subsection we carefully describe the framework needed to consider the general case.
In Section 6 we complete the proof by showing that the general case can be reduced to that of a
square non-singular matrix.
2.2 Reduction to square non-singular and positive definite forms
Whether or not S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}} has a local minimum at X_{c} is determined
by directional derivatives associated with perturbations of the form
X(t)\equiv\sqrt{1-t^{2}}X_{c}+tY with X_{c}\in{\cal K}^{o},Y\in{\cal K}^{o}.
Then S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}} has a local minimum at X_{c}
if and only if g(t)=S[X(t)X(t)^{\dagger}]
has a local minimum at t=0 for all Y\in{\cal K}^{o}.
For X_{c}\in{\cal K}^{o}, let P_{B} be the projection onto (\ker X_{c}X_{c}^{\dagger})^{\perp} and
P_{E} the projection onto (\ker X_{c}^{\dagger}X_{c})^{\perp} so that X_{c}=P_{B}X_{c}P_{E}.
The following theorem, which is proved in Section 6, implies
that to determine whether or not S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}} has a
local minimum at X_{c} it suffices to consider directional derivatives X(t)\equiv\sqrt{1-t^{2}}X_{c}+tY
with Y\in(P_{B}{\cal K}P_{E})^{o}
Theorem 3
Let X_{c}=P_{B}X_{c}P_{E} as above. Then S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}}
has a local minimum at X_{c} if and only if S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in(P_{B}{\cal K}P_{E})^{o}} has a local minimum
at X_{c}.
Since \hbox{\rm Tr}\,P_{B}=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,P_{E}\equiv d, this essentially reduces the problem to
d\times d matrices on the restriction to P_{B}{\cal K}P_{E} with X having full
rank d. Using a variant of the polar decomposition, we can write
X=\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}\,V with V\in M_{d} unitary.
Since the entropy is invariant under unitary transformations, we can replace
\displaystyle\sqrt{1-t^{2}}X+tY=(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}+tYV^{\dagger})V
(4)
by (\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}+tYV^{\dagger}). Thus, we can assume that X=\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}} is positive
definite by changing the perturbation from Y to YV^{\dagger}.
In terms of our original formulation, we can identify V with a partial isometry of rank d with
VV^{\dagger}=P_{B} and V^{\dagger}V=P_{E}. Then Y\in P_{B}{\cal K}P_{E}=P_{B}{\cal K}V^{\dagger}V
if and only if YV^{\dagger}\in P_{B}{\cal K}V^{\dagger}. Note that elements of P_{B}{\cal K}V^{\dagger}
map {\bf C}_{d_{B}}\mapsto{\bf C}_{d_{B}} and {\cal K}_{B}\equiv P_{B}{\cal K}V^{\dagger} can be
identified with a subspace of M_{d_{B}}.
Thus, we can replace X\in{\cal K} by the positive definite matrix
X=\sqrt{XX^{\dagger}}\in{\cal K}_{B} by considering perturbations Y\in{\cal K}_{B}=P_{B}{\cal K}V^{\dagger}.
Remark 4
Thus
S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}} has a local minimum at X_{c}=\sqrt{X_{c}X_{c}^{\dagger}}V\in{\cal K}^{o} if and only if
a) S(XX^{\dagger})\big|_{X\in{\cal K}^{o}_{B}} has a local minimum at at |X_{c}^{\dagger}|\equiv\sqrt{X_{c}X_{c}^{\dagger}}\in{\cal K}^{o}_{B} or, equivalently,
b) g(t)=S[X(t)X(t)] with X(t)=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\sqrt{X_{c}X_{c}^{{\dagger}}}+tY has a local minimum at
t=0 for all Y\in{\cal K}^{o}_{B}.
Thus, we can (and do) henceforth assume that X is positive definite and
consider X(t)=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}X+tY with X,Y in the unit sphere {\cal K}^{o} of some fixed subspace of M_{d}.
For each fixed Y, g(0) corresponds to a local minimum of S(XX^{\dagger}) on {\rm span}\{X,Y\}.
When finding the derivatives of S[X(t)X(t)^{\dagger}] we assume XY^{\dagger}=0. There is no loss of
generality in this restriction because any Y_{1}\in{\cal K}^{o} can be written as aX+bY_{2} with Y_{2}\in X^{\perp}.
so that Y_{1}\in{\rm span}\{X,Y_{2}\}, where the span is taken over {\bf C}. When we use
X(t)=\sqrt{1-t^{2}}X+tY_{2} with XY^{\dagger}=0 we adopt the convention that t is real
and “for all Y\in X^{\perp}” includes the entire ray bY with b\in{\bf C} and |b|=1.
If we write Y=W+iZ with W=W^{\dagger} and Z=Z^{\dagger}, then our assumption that X>0 implies that if
\hbox{\rm Tr}\,XY^{\dagger}=0 then \hbox{\rm Tr}\,XW=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,XZ=\hbox{\rm Tr}\,XY=0. It will follow from (10) in the next section that
the condition \tfrac{d~}{dt}S[X(t)X(t)^{\dagger}]=0, which determines the critical points, requires only perturbations
W=W^{\dagger} in X^{\perp}. Whether these critical points correspond to local maxima, local minima, or saddle points,
requires only perturbations W and iZ with W=W^{\dagger}\in X^{\perp}. and Z=Z^{\dagger}\in X^{\perp}.
This is easily seen from (19) in the proof of Theorem 5.
