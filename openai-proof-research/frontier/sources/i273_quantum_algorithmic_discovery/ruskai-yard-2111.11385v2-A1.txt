Appendix A Proof of key inequaity
We present two proofs of the key inequality (24), i.e., 2\phi(ab)\leq\phi(a^{2})+\phi(b^{2})
with \phi(a)=\frac{a+1}{a-1}\log a^{2} as in (22),. Before doing so, we make some observations useful in both arguments.
a) a>0 implies \phi(-a)\leq\phi(a) so that it suffices to prove (24) for a,b>0.
b) It is straightforward to verify that \phi(x) is continuous on (0,\infty) with a minimum at x=1 satisfying \phi(1)=4.
c) \phi(a)=\phi(a^{-1}) and equality holds in (24), when a=b..
Elementary proof: We now assume a,b>0 and observe that (24) is equivalent to
\displaystyle\frac{ab+1}{ab-1}\big[\log a+\log b\big]~
\displaystyle b\leq~\frac{a^{2}+1}{a^{2}-1}\log a+\frac{b^{2}+1}{b^{2}-1}\log b
which is equivalent to
\displaystyle\frac{b-a}{ab-1}\big[\zeta(a)-\zeta(b)\big]~
\displaystyle\geq~0\qquad\hbox{with}\qquad\zeta(x)=\frac{x\log x}{x^{2}-1}
(A.1)
Now observe that \zeta(x)=\zeta(x^{-1}) and
\zeta^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{x^{2}-1}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\phi(x^{2})\Big). Thus, when x>1, \zeta^{\prime}(x)<0 and
\zeta us strictly decreasing for x>1. We can assume b>a so that it suffices to consider three cases:
\bullet~ When 1<a<b, this implies \zeta(b)<\zeta(a) so that (A.1) holds.
\bullet~ When a<1<b and ab>1, then 1<a^{-1}<b and \zeta(b)<\zeta(a^{-1})=\zeta(a) so that (A.1) holds.
\bullet~ When a<1<b and ab<1, then 1<b<a^{-1} so that \zeta(b)>\zeta(a^{-1})=\zeta(a) which
again implies (A.1).
Combining this with the continuity of \phi(a) at a=1 proves (24).   QED
