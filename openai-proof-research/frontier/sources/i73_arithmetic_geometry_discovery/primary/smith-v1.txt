                                                  A BSTRACT. Given an elliptic curve E/Q, we show that 50% of the quadratic twists of
                                                  E have 2∞ -Selmer corank 0 and 50% have 2∞ -Selmer corank 1. As one consequence,
                                                  we prove that the Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture implies Goldfeld’s conjec-
                                                  ture. Previously, this result was known by work of the author for elliptic curves over Q
                                                  satisfying certain technical conditions.
                                                     As part of this work, we determine the distribution of 2-Selmer ranks in the quadratic
                                                  twist family of E. In the cases where this distribution was not already known, it is
                                                  distinct from the model for distributions of 2-Selmer groups constructed by Poonen and
                                                  Rains.

1.1. Balanced isogenies and the distribution of 2-Selmer groups. For most elliptic
curves over Q, Theorem 1.1 was established by [16, Theorem 1.2]. For that theorem,
we needed to assume that E did not have a balanced isogeny, a term we now define:
Definition 1.6. A degree 2 Q-isogeny ϕ : E → E0 of elliptic curves will be called a
balanced isogeny if it satisfies
                                  Q(E[2]) = Q(E0 [2]).
Here, Q(E[2]) denotes the minimal extension of Q over which every point in E[2] is
rational.
   With this defined, it will be convenient to split the elliptic curves E/Q into five cases
as follows:
        Case I: Either E(Q)[2] = 0, or E(Q)[2] ∼       = (Z/2Z)2 and E has no balanced
        isogeny.
        Case II: E(Q)[2] ∼  = Z/2Z and, taking ϕ : E → E0 to be the unique degree 2
        Q-isogeny, ϕ is not balanced and E0 (Q)[2] is not (Z/2Z)2 .
        Case III: E(Q)[2] ∼   = Z/2Z and, taking ϕ : E → E0 to be the unique degree 2
        Q-isogeny, E0 (Q)[2] ∼  = (Z/2Z)2 .
        Case IV: There is a unique balanced isogeny ϕ : E → E0 defined on E.
       Case V: There are two distinct balanced isogenies defined on E.
 If E is in Case I or Case II, Theorem 1.1 follows for E from [16, Theorem 1.2]. The
main goal of this paper is to establish this theorem for Cases IV and V.
Theorem 1.7. Suppose E has a balanced isogeny. Then Theorem 1.1 holds for E.
   This result implies that Theorem 1.1 holds for curves in Case III, the one remaining
case. After all, if E is in Case III, there is a Q isogeny ϕ : E → E0 to a curve E0 in
either Case I, IV, or V. Since we have
(1.3)                              r2∞ (E d ) = r2∞ (E0d )
for all nonzero integers d, Theorem 1.1 will follow for E since it holds for E0 by either
Theorem 1.7 or [16, Theorem 1.2].

1.2. Isogeny tricks. In [16], we gave a method for finding the distribution of 2k -Selmer
ranks in certain quadratic twist families of Galois modules where the 2-Selmer structure
was fixed. This method made a number of assumptions on the underlying 2-Selmer
structure, and the resulting theorem was all-or-nothing; the paper made no partial claims
in the case where the assumptions were not satisfied.

some of the technical conditions are not satisfied. Our result is Theorem 2.4; with an
eye to future applications, this result is stated in greater generality than is needed for the
rest of the paper.

1.3. Outline of the paper. In Section 2, we consider the machinery for controlling
the distribution of higher Selmer groups given in [16] and modify it so it gives partial
answers for a wider range of questions.
   In Section 3, we assemble the necessary background on curves in Cases IV and V to
streamline the process of determining the distribution of 2-Selmer ranks in their qua-
dratic twist families. With this done, we apply the methods of [17] to determine refined
moments of the 2-Selmer groups in these families in Section 4, and then reconstruct the
distribution of ranks from these moments in Section 5. Finally, in Section 6, we prove
Theorem 1.17 by applying the work of Section 2.

    2. G ENERAL RESULTS ON THE DISTRIBUTION OF HIGHER S ELMER RANKS
   The work on the distribution of higher Selmer groups in [16] is stated at a couple
of different levels of generality. First, there is [16, Theorem 4.18], which gives the
distribution of higher Selmer groups in certain subsets of grids of twists subject to a
number of technical conditions. This is proved as a consequence of [16, Theorem 6.2],
which is an equidistribution statement for the Cassels–Tate pairing over certain finer
subsets of the grid of twists, and which is proved subject to the same technical conditions
as the former theorem.

conditions weakened. The original proof of this result is long and difficult. Fortunately,
there are relatively few places where the technical conditions we are modifying are used
in the original proof. So, to avoid redundancy, we shall prove our generalized form of
