Abstract
We prove packing stability for any closed symplectic manifold with rational cohomology class. This will rely on a general symplectic embedding result for ellipsoids which assumes only that there is no volume obstruction and that the domain is sufficiently thin relative to the target. We also obtain easily computable bounds for the Embedded Contact Homology capacities which are sufficient to imply the existence of some volume preserving embeddings in dimension 44.
1 Introduction
We will study symplectic embeddings, that is, embeddings f:(N,σ)→(M,ω)f:(N,\sigma)\to(M,\omega) between symplectic manifolds such that f∗​ω=σf^{*}\omega=\sigma. As our domains will be ellipsoids or unions of balls in Euclidean space it is sometimes convenient to introduce the weaker notion (N,σ)↪(M,ω)(N,\sigma)\hookrightarrow(M,\omega) to mean that λ⁡(N,σ):=(N,λ​σ)→(M,ω)\lambda(N,\sigma):=(N,\lambda\sigma)\to(M,\omega) for all 0<λ<10<\lambda<1. We note that when MM is a ball and NN is an ellipsoid or a disjoint union of balls in dimension 44, then (N,σ)↪(M,ω)(N,\sigma)\hookrightarrow(M,\omega) implies there exists a symplectic embedding (N̊,σ)→(M,ω)(\mathring{N},\sigma)\to(M,\omega) from the interior N̊\mathring{N} of NN, see [12], [13], Corollary 1.61.6. However no such results are known in higher dimension. In any case, we say that (N,σ)(N,\sigma) fully fills (M,ω)(M,\omega) if vol⁡(N,σ)=vol⁡(M,ω)\mathrm{vol}(N,\sigma)=\mathrm{vol}(M,\omega) and (N,σ)↪(M,ω)(N,\sigma)\hookrightarrow(M,\omega).
Our main theorem is an embedding result for ellipsoids. To state this, we denote by E⁡(a1,…,an)⊂ℝ2​nE(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n} the ellipsoid
E(a1,…,an)={∑i=1nπ⁡(xi2+yi2)ai≤1}.E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\pi(x_{i}^{2}+y_{i}^{2})}{a_{i}}\leq 1\right\}.
Ellipsoids inherit a symplectic structure from the standard form ω0=∑i=1nd​xi∧d​yi\omega_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}\wedge dy_{i} on ℝ2​n{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}. In our notation, the ball of capacity cc is written B2​n​(c)=E⁡(c,…,c)B^{2n}(c)=E(c,\dots,c) and λ​E​(a1,…,an)=E⁡(λ​a1,…,λ​an)\lambda E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=E(\lambda a_{1},\dots,\lambda a_{n}). Any given ellipsoid is symplectomorphic to the same ellipsoid with its factors permuted. Unless otherwise stated, when describing an ellipsoid we will list the factors in increasing order.
Theorem 1.1.
There exists a constant S⁡(b1,…,bn)S(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}) such that if ana1>S\frac{a_{n}}{a_{1}}>S and a1​…​an≤b1​…​bna_{1}\dots a_{n}\leq b_{1}\dots b_{n} there exists an embedding
E⁡(a1,…,an)↪E⁡(b1,…,bn).E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\hookrightarrow E(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}).
This will be established using a technique from Buse-Hind [4] which generates higher dimensional embeddings from lower dimensional ones, together with some new ellipsoid embeddings in dimension 44.
Combining with work of Biran [2] and Opshtein [17], we also have a full filling result valid for general rational symplectic manifolds. Recall that the manifold (M,ω)(M,\omega) is rational if [ω]∈H2​(M,ℚ)⊂H2​(M,ℝ)[\omega]\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})\subset H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}}).
Theorem 1.2.
If (M2​n,ω)(M^{2n},\omega) (or (M2​n,λ​ω)(M^{2n},\lambda\omega) for any λ>0\lambda>0) is rational, then there exist a constant S⁡(M)>0S(M)>0 such that for all ellipsoids E⁡(a1,…,an)E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}) with ana1>S⁡(M)\frac{a_{n}}{a_{1}}>S(M) we have a volume filling embedding E⁡(a1,…,an)↪(M,ω).E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\hookrightarrow(M,\omega).
In the paper [9], section 33, Cieliebak, Hofer, Schlenk and Latschev define generalized symplectic capacities defined in terms of a fixed symplectic manifold (M,ω)(M,\omega). If MM has dimension 2​n2n then we get a capacity on the space of 2​n2n dimensional ellipsoids defined by

Our result can be interpreted as saying that c(M,ω)​E​(a1,⋯,an)c^{(M,\omega)}E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}) coincides with the normalized volume capacity
v(M,ω)​E​(a1,⋯,an):=vol⁡(E⁡(a1⋅…⋅an))vol⁡(M)nv^{(M,\omega)}E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}):=\sqrt[n]{\frac{{\rm vol}(E(a_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{n}))}{{\rm vol}(M)}}
whenever MM is rational and ana1>S⁡(M)\frac{a_{n}}{a_{1}}>S(M), that is, whenever the ellipsoid is sufficiently thin.
The existence of an embedding between 44-dimensional ellipsoids is determined by the Embedded Contact Homology (ECH) capacities of M. Hutchings, see [10]. Namely, there is a sequence of numbers 𝒩​(a,b)​(k)\mathcal{N}(a,b)(k), n≥0n\geq 0, associated to a pair (a,b)(a,b) and according to D. McDuff, [14], we have E⁡(a1,a2)↪E⁡(b1,b2)E(a_{1},a_{2})\hookrightarrow E(b_{1},b_{2}) if and only if 𝒩⁡(a1,a2)​(k)≤𝒩⁡(b1,b2)​(k)\mathcal{N}(a_{1},a_{2})(k)\leq\mathcal{N}(b_{1},b_{2})(k) for all k≥0k\geq 0. We will give quadratic bounds on a related piecewise linear function, see Proposition 2.5. One consequence is the following.
Theorem 1.3.

2.1 Description of ECH and some new estimates
The key ingredients for our study are results of Hutchings [10] and McDuff [14] on Embedded Contact Homology which together give necessary and sufficient conditions for a four dimensional ellipsoid embedding.
A four dimensional Liouville domain is a compact exact symplectic manifold (X,ω)(X,\omega) such that there exists a contact form on ∂X\partial X which is a primitive of ω|∂X\omega|_{\partial X}. Hutchings associates to such (X,ω)(X,\omega) an increasing sequence of real numbers ck​(X,ω)c_{k}(X,\omega) for k≥0k\geq 0 called the ECH capacities. The term capacity is justified by the following.
Theorem 2.1.
(Hutchings [10])

4 Full filling by an ellipsoid
In this section we present Opshtein’s result, which is a refinement of Biran’s polarization theorem, that any rational symplectic manifold can be fully filled by an ellipsoid, that is, we prove the following.
Theorem 4.1 ([16] [17]).
Let (M,ω)(M,\omega) be rational. Then there exist b1,…,bnb_{1},\dots,b_{n} such that there exists a full filling E⁡(b1,…,bn)↪(M,ω)E(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\hookrightarrow(M,\omega).
Given Theorem 4.1 we can prove Theorems 1.4 and 1.6. Theorem 1.2 is also a direct application of Theorems 4.1 and 1.1.
Proof of Theorem 1.4. Given (M,ω)(M,\omega) rational, we consider the fully filling ellipsoid E⁡(b1,…,bn)E(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}) given by Theorem 4.1. By Theorem 1.1 there exists a constant S⁡(b1,…,bn)S(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}) such that there exists a full filling γ​E​(1,…,1,k)↪E⁡(b1,…,bn)\gamma E(1,\dots,1,k)\hookrightarrow E(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}) for any k≥Sk\geq S. But from [4], Lemma 4.14.1 there also exists a full filling
⊔kB(1)↪E(1×(n−1),k)\sqcup_{k}B(1)\hookrightarrow E\big(1^{\times(n-1)},k\big)
for any k∈ℕk\in{\mathbb{N}}. Putting the two maps together, we obtain a full filling of E⁡(b1,…,bn)E(b_{1},\dots,b_{n}), and hence (M,ω)(M,\omega), by kk balls of capacity γ\gamma. This means that (M,ω)(M,\omega) has packing stability as required. ∎

Remark 5.2.
Still following our methods here as well as those from [4], the estimates for the upper bound on the stability numbers presented here may be improved if we can improve, by a much more complicated number theoretical investigation of the ECH vectors, the four dimensional embedding result from Theorem 1.3. However, in the case when the target manifold is ℂ​P3{\mathbb{C}}P^{3} the inequality Ns​t​a​b≤21N_{stab}\leq 21 cannot be improved in this way as it relies at the last step on a sharp estimate from McDuff- Schlenk giving the precise range for volume filling ellipsoids in the unit ball. It remains a very interesting question to find methods to obstruct symplectic volume fillings of ellipsoids or disjoint balls into the unit six dimensional ball. As will be shown in [5], it appears that obstructions arising from symplectic field theory that have been succesful in [11] when targets were cylinders fail here.
