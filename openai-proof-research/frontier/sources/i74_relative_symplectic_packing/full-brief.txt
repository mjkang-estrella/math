## 3. Exact strongest target and conventions

Let `B(c)={z∈C^n:π|z|²≤c}`, with standard form `ω₀=Σdx_j∧dy_j`. Every embedding below is smooth and symplectic on an open neighborhood of the indicated closed balls. Distinct closed images are disjoint.

**RP.** For every integer `n≥3`, `R>0`, integers `s,k≥1`, positive capacities `a_1,…,a_s,b_1,…,b_k`, and **every** prescribed embedding

`e: ⨆_(j=1)^s B(a_j) → Int B(R)`,

suppose, for the combined list `c=(a,b)`,

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{s}a_j^n+\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_i^n<R^n,
 \qquad c_u+c_v<R\quad(u\ne v).
 \tag{RP-adm}
\]

Then there should exist embeddings of the `B(b_i)` into

\[
 U_e=\operatorname{Int}B(R)\setminus\bigcup_j e_j(B(a_j))
 \tag{1}
\]

with disjoint compact images. The maps e are preserved exactly, not just their capacities or homology classes. The ambient symplectic form stays ω₀. There is no bound on Hamiltonian energy, no countable-family assertion, and no limiting boundary equality.

Necessity is immediate from P's necessary direction applied to the final finite union. Let `E(c;R)` denote the space of such labeled packings. RP asks that the forgetful map `E(a,b;R)→E(a;R)` be onto when (RP-adm) holds. P proves that the domain is nonempty. Nonemptiness is not surjectivity. Connectedness of `E(a;R)` by suitable smooth isotopies would suffice, but is a **stronger sufficient route**, not an equivalent reformulation of RP and not an established input here.

Conformal normalization is safe: conjugating by `z↦z/√R` scales capacities to c/R, with pullback factors canceling between source and target. It does not create a Hamiltonian energy bound on the original fixed form.

## 4. Construction I: transport a source packing, and where it stops

### 4.1 Complete isotopy-extension partial

**Proposition I.** Suppose a packing E of the combined list exists and its a-prefix `e_0` is connected to the prescribed `e_1` by a smooth family `e_t`, `0≤t≤1`, of disjoint symplectic embeddings defined on fixed slightly enlarged balls. Then the b-balls of E can be transported to an extension of `e_1`. The transport is Hamiltonian with support in a compact subset of `Int B(R)`.

**Proof.** On each enlarged domain set `v_t(e_t(z))=∂_t e_t(z)`. Differentiation of `e_t*ω₀=ω₀` gives

\[
 d\alpha_t=0,\qquad \alpha_t=e_t^*(\iota_{v_t}\omega_0).
\]

Each domain is a ball, so a smooth primitive is given in source coordinates by

\[
 F_t(z)=\int_0^1\alpha_t(uz)[z]\,du,
 \qquad dF_t=\alpha_t.
 \tag{2}
\]

Use the Hamiltonian convention `ι_Xω₀=−dH`. Define `H_t(e_t(z))=−F_t(z)` on the moving images. The domains have full dimension, so this equality gives `X_(H_t)=v_t` there. Shrink the fixed enlargements once. Compactness in t supplies positive distance to the target boundary and between their closed images. Smooth time-dependent cutoffs, equal to one near the requested balls and supported in the moving enlarged images, extend H by zero to the target. One can choose them by pulling fixed domain cutoffs through `e_t`; their supports remain disjoint and compact. The time-dependent field is smooth, uniformly compactly supported, and has a flow `χ_t` for all `t∈[0,1]`. Uniqueness gives `χ_t e_0=e_t` on every prescribed closed ball. Apply χ₁ to the other compact images of E. A diffeomorphism preserves disjointness and symplecticity, and every map retains a neighborhood of its compact source. ∎

Formula (2) pays the flux issue; exactness is on the actual ball domains, not assumed for the entire complement. No pseudoholomorphic transversality theorem is used. The proof gives finite energy for the chosen smooth compact path, but **no uniform Hofer bound** in capacities or slack.

P supplies E. Proposition I supplies the entire extension over any such path starting at its prefix, and a pathwise parametric lift. What remains is a path to an arbitrary e with **unchanged source capacities and a common compact interior trace**. This is a geometric construction problem, not a consequence of P's numerical criterion.

### 4.2 A concrete failure of the tempting Alexander path

Even for a globally symplectic embedding of a ball, the rescaled derivative path can leave the target. In one conjugate pair put

\[
 f(q,p)=(q,p+4q-4q^3),
 \tag{3}
\]

and let f act identically on the remaining pairs. It is symplectic, since it is a momentum shear by a function of q. On the Euclidean closed unit ball, `|f(z)|≤1+max_|q|≤1 |4q(1−q²)|=1+8/(3√3)<3`. Thus it is a valid embedding `B(π)→Int B(9π)`, with a neighborhood by strictness.

The symplectic Alexander formula `f_t(z)=t^(−1)f(tz)`, extended at t=0 by Df(0), instead gives `p↦p+4q−4t²q³`. At `q=1,p=0,t=1/2`, its norm squared is `1+3²=10>9`. The path escapes the prescribed target. This is an exact counterexample to that **method**, not to RP. Translating the center does not repair this example, since f(0)=0. Shrinking the source avoids the escape but changes the problem; restoring its radius is exactly the missing inflation step in the four-dimensional comparison.

### 4.3 Complete centered-large-ball partial and Moser boundary

**Proposition S.** RP holds for `s=1`, `a_1≥R/2`, and the standard centered inclusion. It also holds when the prescribed ball image is the image of that centered ball under a compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of the target. Its prescribed parameterization remains untouched: only the new balls are transported.

**Proof.** Normalize R=1. Choose rational `a>a_1` and `r_i>b_i` with `a>1/2`, `r_i<1−a`, and `Σr_i^n<1−a^n`; finite strictness allows this. P's proof of `app:large` for n≥4, or `six:large` for n=3, embeds the other balls compactly in the open shell `a<π|z|²<1`. The prescribed central ball remains untouched. The shell transfer keeps the original standard form and the exact class used in the source. Compose with the stated Hamiltonian diffeomorphism for its image version. ∎

This is explicitly **technical and already present as a lower source operation**. It is not a new relative theorem for arbitrary embeddings.

A separate shortcut would blow up arbitrary e, retain the same formal cohomology class, and interpolate to P's Kähler model. The missing assertion is that the forms lie in a common appropriate symplectic path, with boundary/divisor data controlled. Cohomology alone does not make linear interpolation symplectic. Locally, in dimension six,

\[
 \omega_0=dx_1\wedge dy_1+dx_2\wedge dy_2+dx_3\wedge dy_3,
 \quad
 \omega_1=-dx_1\wedge dy_1-dx_2\wedge dy_2+dx_3\wedge dy_3
 \tag{4}
\]

are exact, nondegenerate and have the same symplectic orientation. Their midpoint has rank two. This does not disprove existence of a different path; it refutes the inference from equal class to a valid linear Moser path. P pays this by the same-complex-structure Kähler cone, which is not supplied for an arbitrary symplectic blowup. Preservation of exceptional divisors as sets also does not, by itself, restore the originally prescribed ball maps.

## 5. Construction II: direct relative redistribution in the complement

This approach does not move e. It tries to place P's handled-surface model in `U_e`, carry out the comparison with support avoiding the protected region, and return by a relative exact deformation. The two actual operations below expose why a global mean alone is insufficient, while also identifying what can be retained.

### 5.1 Necessary orbit-average inequalities and an explicit finite solution

Consider first a compact Hamiltonian fiber V and a proposed comparison with a **uniform** gap d>0:

\[
 P(qu)+K(u)-K(qu)\ge d.
 \tag{5}
\]

For any q-invariant probability measure μ, integration forces `∫P dμ≥d`. In particular, if q fixes a protected point u, then `P(u)≥d`; if q preserves a positive-volume set E, then `mean_E P≥d`. These are deductions from (5), not imported Floer constraints.

The absolute source hypotheses do not imply the fixed-region condition. On `Δ=[0,1]` let `P(p)=p−1/4`. It is affine, has mean 1/4, and is strictly positive on the outer region `p≥1/2`. These satisfy the shape/mean interface of `cmp:comparison`. But any q required to fix the disk `p≤1/8` violates (5) there, independently of K. Thus adding “q is the identity near an arbitrary interior set” to the source comparison is false. This only obstructs that relative comparison, **not RP**, because RP does not require that this particular P/model encode its prescribed balls.

For a fixed permutation q of a finite set, the exact analogue is completely solvable. On each orbit O let `a_O=|O|^(−1)Σ_(u∈O)P(u)`. Choose K at one point arbitrarily and then define

`K(qu)=K(u)+P(qu)−a_O`.

The sum around O is zero, so this is consistent, and the left side of (5) equals `a_O` everywhere on O. Hence the optimal uniform gap over all K is exactly `min_O a_O`. This proves both the necessary and sufficient finite criterion. It is not a construction of a Hamiltonian q with favorable orbits.

There is also a genuine continuous sufficient identity, with no compactness inference hidden in it. If a smooth q and smooth P satisfy

\[
 \inf_{u\in V}\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N P(q^j u)\ge d>0
 \tag{6}
\]

for one finite N, set

\[
 K(u)=-\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(N-j)P(q^j u).
 \tag{7}
\]

A direct telescoping calculation makes (5)'s left side exactly the average in (6). Thus (6) gives an actual smooth transfer function. For merely continuous P, K is continuous and can be smoothed on a neighborhood with less than d/4 uniform error, retaining a positive gap. **But K is generally not toric**, q has not been constructed, and neither orbit averages nor smoothing impose the source's face commutation. This identity is a technical partial, not RP renamed as an assumption: it supplies a transfer function once a distinctly dynamical finite-orbit estimate is available, and identifies the extra interfaces that remain.

### 5.2 Why a small uniform error does not pay the new symplectic interface

For general horizontal coefficients on a two-dimensional base, write

\[
 \Omega=\omega_V+d(A\,ds+B\,dt),\qquad
 h=\partial_sB-\partial_tA,
 \quad
 \{A,B\}_0=\sum_j(A_{x_j}B_{y_j}-A_{y_j}B_{x_j}).
\]

Expansion of the top exterior power gives the exact identity

\[
 \frac{\Omega^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}
   =(h-\{A,B\}_0)\frac{\omega_V^m}{m!}\wedge ds\wedge dt.
 \tag{8}
\]

One derivation separates the pure vertical/base term from pairs of mixed terms. For each conjugate pair the latter contributes `−(A_xB_y−A_yB_x)`, and all terms with three base covectors vanish. The sign can be checked already at m=1 with `A=x,B=y`: the coefficient is h−1. Thus the orientation-compatible nondegeneracy condition is `h−{A,B}_0>0`, not just h>0.

For ε>0 and an integer k use, in a coordinate patch,

`A=ε sin(kx)`,  `B=s+ε sin(ky)`.

Here h=1, but the bracket at x=y=0 is `ε²k²`. With ε=1/L and k=L², both added coefficients tend uniformly to zero while the bracket is L². Interpolating their amplitude by θ crosses degeneracy at `θ=1/L`. Cutoffs identically one near the chosen point retain the computation. This refutes a C⁰-error-only justification for arbitrary horizontal perturbations.

**Comparison with the strongest source operation is essential.** P already handles its nontoric suspended region as the pullback of `ω_V+d(Gβ)+dγ`, where there is only one base covector β; two mixed terms then wedge to zero. Its remaining packing layers and outside corrections are toric, so the other dangerous brackets vanish. Equation (8) is not a criticism of that proof. It identifies the extra cost of a proposed obstacle-dependent extension that changes both horizontal coefficients or destroys those toric outside corrections. Keeping the original one-covector architecture could avoid this particular cost, but no construction here does that while respecting arbitrary e.

The next analytic inequality for that attempted extension is explicit:

\[
 \inf_{\lambda,s,t,u}\bigl[
 \partial_sB_\lambda-\partial_tA_\lambda
 -\{A_\lambda,B_\lambda\}_0\bigr]>0,
 \quad \text{with the variation primitive zero near the protected image.}
 \tag{9}
\]

It must hold through the entire compact deformation, together with outer-face moment commutation. At the comparison stage, one also needs (6) for a permitted q, which is impossible in the fixed-region example above. A valid adapted model would first have to place its protected set where all necessary invariant averages are positive. Neither an adapted model of enough volume nor the relative q is obtained. Equations (6) and (9) are concrete unpaid interfaces, **not asserted sufficient premises for RP without the geometry**, and no universal positive-mean relative comparison is claimed.

### 5.3 Complete fallback: small unequal balls with fixed volume slack

The following theorem constructs embeddings directly in the actual complement, without an isotopy of e, a blowup identification, or a global model.

**Proposition D.** Let `n≥3` and let U be any bounded open subset of standard `R^(2n)` with positive volume. Set `V=n! vol(U)`. For every `δ∈(0,V)` there is `ρ=ρ(U,δ)>0` such that, for every finite list of positive capacities `b_i≤ρ` with

\[
 \sum_i b_i^n\le V-\delta,
 \tag{10}
\]

the closed balls `B(b_i)` have a disjoint neighborhood symplectic embedding into U. In particular this holds for every complement (1), with `V=R^n−Σa_j^n`, preserving e exactly.

**Proof, including the covering and finite assignment.** First choose finitely many pairwise disjoint closed Euclidean balls `D_j⊂U`, of capacities c_j, such that

\[
 C:=\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_j^n>V-\delta/2.
 \tag{11}
\]

Here is an elementary construction, avoiding an unjustified full-volume finite cover. For a bounded open residual set O, dyadic cubes with closure inside O have interiors covering O up to a null set after taking the maximal such cubes at sufficiently small allowed scales. They are countable and interior-disjoint; choose finitely many totaling more than half the volume of O. In each selected cube place the centered closed Euclidean ball of radius one quarter the side length. The balls are strictly separated from every face, lie in O, and cover a fixed positive fraction `α_(2n)/2` of O, where `α_d=vol(B_eucl^d(1))/4^d`. Remove these finitely many **closed** balls. The residual is again open. After finitely many rounds its volume is less than `δ/(2n!)`, since it decreases at least geometrically. All balls over all rounds are pairwise disjoint compact subsets of U; their finite collection has the required separation and yields (11). Cube and ball boundaries have zero volume. No infinite collection or limiting embedding is used.

Choose

\[
 0<\rho\le\min\left\{\frac14\min_jc_j,
                         \left(\frac{\delta}{4J}\right)^{1/n}\right\}.
 \tag{12}
\]

Let `v_i=b_i^n≤h:=ρ^n` and bin budgets `C_j=c_j^n`. Assign the v_i in any fixed order by next fit: put the next item into the current bin only if the resulting load is **strictly** below C_j; otherwise leave that bin permanently and try the next. If an item remains after the last bin is rejected, each discarded bin has unused budget at most h. Therefore the total already assigned volume is at least `C−Jh`, and hence the total S of all items is at least that amount. But

\[
 C-Jh>V-\delta/2-\delta/4>V-\delta\ge S,
 \tag{13}
\]

a contradiction. Thus all items are assigned, every bin load is strictly below C_j, and any two capacities in bin j have sum at most `2ρ≤c_j/2<c_j`. P applies in each D_j. Compose with its standard Euclidean translation, then combine the finite packings. Strict source/target containment and finitely many compact images allow neighborhoods to be shrunk until all are disjoint and contained in U. Every symplectic form is the original ω₀, so there is no cohomological or flux change.

For `U=U_e`, each smooth embedded closed ball has volume `a_j^n/n!` and boundary of zero ambient volume. Their disjointness gives the displayed residual V. All new compact images lie in U, hence have positive separation from the prescribed images. The old maps are never altered. ∎

This is a quantified, actual partial: the number and individual small capacities are unrestricted after ρ is chosen. The threshold depends on the **actual complement geometry and δ**, not only n, capacities, or the total volume. It is **not strong packing stability** with one threshold valid as δ↓0, and it gives no uniformity under arbitrary smooth deformations of e. Its construction has no tail to pass to a limit. Although it uses P in this presentation, fragmentation and asymptotic filling are classical mechanisms; I make no originality claim for D and classify it technical.

Fixed-bin fragmentation cannot remove its loss merely by invoking total volume. Two bins of volume budget 1 cannot hold three indivisible items of size 3/5, despite total 9/5<2. This obstructs only assignment to those bins. For n≥3, two capacity-1 containers and the three separate requested capacities `(3/5)^(1/n)` each satisfy P's absolute inequalities in a target of capacity 21/10; the example is not a symplectic nonembedding theorem. More refined or jointly adapted containers might work, but D supplies no such mechanism for arbitrary large b_i.

## 6. Audit of the remaining scope

RP is not proved, and no counterexample to RP was found. The constructions establish:

1. **I:** a complete Hamiltonian extension over any supplied smooth compact isotopy of prefixes on fixed collars; a true counterexample to the naive fixed-target Alexander formula.
2. **S:** the relative centered-large-ball source operation and its Hamiltonian images, with the six-dimensional shell interface retained.
3. **D:** extension of arbitrary prescribed finite packings by sufficiently small unequal balls for every fixed positive residual-volume slack.
4. The exact comparison orbit conditions, finite-orbit solution, finite Birkhoff transfer function, horizontal-curvature identity, and narrowly scoped countermodels.

These are technical partials. None is a completed substantial candidate. The direct route needs an adapted complement model plus a relative q satisfying a uniform finite-orbit estimate, and either P's one-covector/toric compatibility or the paid bound (9). The transport route needs a fixed-capacity path, with a constant-class positive deformation if using blowups. The volume/pairwise inequalities do not by themselves furnish either construction. Removing δ from D is a different stronger uniformity question and is not inferred by a diagonal limit.

All closed-source/open-target, compact support, source neighborhoods, finite disjointness, and normalization conditions are explicit above. No action filtration or Floer transversality result is assumed for RP, I, S or D. O∞ is parked precisely because persistence of periods and nondegeneracy is unpaid; L∞ is parked precisely because invariant identification and fixed-base infinite realization are unpaid. No stabilization, cover, surgery, or arbitrary product is used to preserve those missing properties.
