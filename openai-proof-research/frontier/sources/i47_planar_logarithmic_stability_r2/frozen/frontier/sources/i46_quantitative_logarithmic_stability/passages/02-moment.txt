1: \section{Moment coordinates on the whole space}\label{sec:moment}
2: 
3: The variance argument uses a change of variables whose Jacobian is a
4: positive Hessian.  The following lemma supplies this change of variables
5: and the uniform upper bound needed for the later cutoff arguments.
6: 
7: \begin{lemma}[Moment coordinates]\label{lem:moment}
8: Let $n\ge2$, $\eps>0$, and let $V\in C^\infty(\R^n)$ be even with
9: $D^2V\ge\eps I$.  Set
10: \[
11:  Z=\int_{\R^n}e^{-V(x)}\,dx,
12:  \qquad d\mu(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\,dx.
13: \]
14: There is an even $\varphi\in C^\infty(\R^n)$ such that
15: $d\nu(z)=e^{-\varphi(z)}\,dz$ is a probability measure,
16: $\nabla\varphi$ is a diffeomorphism of $\R^n$ onto $\R^n$, and
17: \begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
18:  (\nabla\varphi)_\#\nu=\mu,
19:  \qquad 0<D^2\varphi\le\frac4\eps I,
20:  \qquad
21:  \log\det D^2\varphi
22:    =V(\nabla\varphi)+\log Z-\varphi.
23: \end{equation}
24: Moreover, $\varphi(z)\ge c|z|-b$ for some $c>0$ and $b<\infty$.
25: The lower Hessian bound in \eqref{eq:moment} means positive definiteness
26: at every point; it need not be uniform on $\R^n$.
27: \end{lemma}
28: 
29: \begin{proof}
30: Evenness and uniform convexity give
31: $V(x)\ge V(0)+\eps|x|^2/2$, so $0<Z<\infty$.
32: For $R\ge2$, write $B_R=\{x:|x|<R\}$ and set
33: \[
34:  Z_R=\int_{B_R}e^{-V(x)}\,dx,
35:  \qquad d\mu_R=Z_R^{-1}\mathbf1_{B_R}e^{-V(x)}\,dx.
36: \]
37: We first use two compact-support results.  The moment theorem of
38: Berman--Berndtsson \cite[Theorem~1.1]{BermanBerndtsson2013}, applied to
39: the body $\overline B_R$ and the positive smooth density
40: $e^{-V}/Z_R$, gives a smooth convex potential $\varphi_R$, unique up to
41: translation, for which $\nabla\varphi_R:\R^n\to B_R$ is a
42: diffeomorphism and
43: \begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-truncated}
44:  \det D^2\varphi_R
45:    =Z_R\exp\{V(\nabla\varphi_R)-\varphi_R\}.
46: \end{equation}
47: The centering hypothesis holds by evenness.  Integrating the equation
48: through this diffeomorphism shows that
49: $d\nu_R=e^{-\varphi_R}\,dz$ is a probability and
50: $(\nabla\varphi_R)_\#\nu_R=\mu_R$.
51: Klartag's estimate \cite[Proposition~3.1 and Remark~3.5]{KlartagArxiv2013}
52: gives
53: \[
54:  0<D^2\varphi_R\le CI,\qquad C=4/\eps.
55: \]
56: Indeed, the ball has smooth positively curved boundary, and
57: $\rho_R=V+\log Z_R$ is convex and smooth, with itself and all its
58: derivatives bounded on $B_R$, and $D^2\rho_R\ge\eps I$.
59: The constant $C$ is independent of $R$.
60: 
61: Translate the unique preimage of $0$ to $0$.  It is the unique minimum
62: of $\varphi_R$.  Reflection gives a second potential for the same
63: target with its minimum at $0$; uniqueness up to translation therefore
64: makes $\varphi_R$ even.  For each fixed $R$, take the preimages of
65: the slopes $\pm(R/2)e_i$.  Their supporting planes give
66: \[
67:  \varphi_R(z)\ge\frac{R}{2\sqrt n}|z|-b_R
68: \]
69: with a finite, possibly $R$-dependent constant $b_R$.
70: Thus $\nu_R$ has an exponential tail.  In particular, since
71: $|\nabla\varphi_R|\le R$, integration of
72: $\operatorname{div}(z e^{-\varphi_R})$ with expanding compact cutoffs
73: is justified and yields
74: \begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-source-identity}
75:  \E_{\nu_R}\,z\cdot\nabla\varphi_R(z)=n.
76: \end{equation}
77: 
78: We now obtain bounds independent of $R$.  Put
79: $m_R=\varphi_R(0)$ and $g_R=\varphi_R-m_R\ge0$.
80: The Hessian bound implies
81: $g_R(y)\le C|y|^2/2$ and $|\nabla\varphi_R(y)|\le C|y|$.
82: For arbitrary $y,z$, set $x=\nabla\varphi_R(z)$.  Supporting planes
83: at $z$ and $-z$ give the single pointwise inequality
84: \[
85:  g_R(y)\ge g_R(z)+|x\cdot y|-x\cdot z.
86: \]
87: Integrate with respect to $\nu_R$, discard the nonnegative
88: $\E_{\nu_R}g_R$, and use \eqref{eq:moment-source-identity}:
89: \[
90:  g_R(y)\ge\int|x\cdot y|\,d\mu_R(x)-n.
91: \]
92: On $B_1$ the density of $\mu_R$ is at least
93: $d_0=Z^{-1}\exp(-\max_{\overline B_1}V)>0$.
94: Rotational symmetry of the ball therefore bounds the integral below
95: by $c|y|$, where $c=d_0\int_{B_1}|x_1|\,dx>0$.  Hence
96: \begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-coercivity}
97:  c|y|-n\le g_R(y)\le\frac C2|y|^2,
98:  \qquad
99:  \left(\frac{2\pi}{C}\right)^{n/2}
100:  \le e^{m_R}=\int e^{-g_R(y)}\,dy
101:  \le e^n\int e^{-c|y|}\,dy.
102: \end{equation}
103: This bounds $m_R$ on both sides and gives one integrable exponential
104: majorant for all the densities $e^{-\varphi_R}$.
105: 
106: It remains to obtain a smooth limit and to verify that its gradient
107: reaches every point.  On each fixed ball, the preceding estimates
108: bound $\varphi_R$ and $\nabla\varphi_R$ uniformly.  Since
109: $Z_2\le Z_R\le Z$, equation \eqref{eq:moment-truncated} bounds
110: $\det D^2\varphi_R$ below by a positive constant there.
111: Together with $D^2\varphi_R\le CI$, this gives a uniform local bound
112: $\delta I\le D^2\varphi_R\le CI$: if the determinant is at least
113: $d>0$, every eigenvalue is at least $d/C^{n-1}$.
114: The right-hand sides
115: \[
116:  f_R=V(\nabla\varphi_R)+\log Z_R-\varphi_R
117: \]
118: of the logarithmic equations are uniformly Lipschitz on that ball,
119: because
120: $\nabla f_R=D^2\varphi_R\nabla V(\nabla\varphi_R)
121: -\nabla\varphi_R$ is uniformly bounded.
122: 
123: To apply an interior uniformly elliptic estimate, choose a smooth
124: concave scalar function $s$ equal to $\log$ on a slightly larger
125: interval than $[\delta,C]$, with $s'$ bounded above and bounded below
126: by positive constants on $\R$.  Such an extension is obtained by
127: smoothly continuing the decreasing derivative $1/t$ to positive
128: constant tails.  For symmetric matrices $Q$, the spectral function
129: $\mathcal F(Q)=\tr s(Q)$ is smooth, concave and uniformly elliptic:
130: its derivative has eigenvalues $s'(\lambda_i(Q))$, and its second
131: variation has coefficients $s''(\lambda_i)$ and the nonpositive
132: divided differences of $s'$.  It agrees with $\log\det$ on the
133: Hessians under consideration.  The nonhomogeneous Evans--Krylov
134: estimate \cite[Corollary~2.3]{Wang2012} applied to
135: $\mathcal F(D^2\varphi_R)=f_R$ gives uniform interior
136: $C^{2,\alpha}$ bounds for some $\alpha>0$.
137: 
138: Each $\varphi_R$ is already smooth.  Its derivative
139: $w_{R,k}=\partial_k\varphi_R$ satisfies
140: \[
141:  (D^2\varphi_R)^{-1}:D^2w_{R,k}
142:    =\nabla V(\nabla\varphi_R)\cdot\nabla w_{R,k}-w_{R,k},
143: \]
144: where $Q:N=\tr(Q^TN)$.  The principal coefficients and the
145: right-hand side are uniformly $C^\alpha$ on smaller balls.
146: Interior Schauder estimates
147: \cite[Theorem~2.20 and Corollary~2.21]{FernandezRealRosOton2023}
148: give uniform $C^{3,\alpha}$ bounds for $\varphi_R$, and iteration
149: gives bounds for every derivative on compact sets.
150: A diagonal subsequence, still indexed by $R$, converges locally smoothly to an even
151: $\varphi$ with positive definite Hessian, the same upper cap, and
152: the logarithmic equation in \eqref{eq:moment}.
153: The bound $\varphi_R(z)\ge c|z|-b$, with $b$ independent of $R$,
154: also passes to $\varphi$.
155: 
156: The common exponential majorant from \eqref{eq:moment-coercivity}
157: proves $\int e^{-\varphi}=1$ by dominated convergence.  For each
158: bounded continuous $h$, it also gives
159: \[
160:  \int h(\nabla\varphi)e^{-\varphi}
161:  =\lim_{R\to\infty}\int h(\nabla\varphi_R)e^{-\varphi_R}
162:  =\lim_{R\to\infty}\int h\,d\mu_R
163:  =\int h\,d\mu.
164: \]
165: Thus $(\nabla\varphi)_\#\nu=\mu$.
166: Positive definite Hessian makes the gradient locally invertible and
167: injective, since for $z\ne w$,
168: \[
169:  (\nabla\varphi(z)-\nabla\varphi(w))\cdot(z-w)
170:  =\int_0^1 D^2\varphi(w+t(z-w))[z-w,z-w]\,dt>0.
171: \]
172: Its image is dense: an open ball missed by the image would have zero
173: pushforward mass, contrary to the everywhere positive density of
174: $\mu$.  Fix $x_0\in\R^n$.  Density permits finitely many image
175: points $y_j=\nabla\varphi(z_j)$ whose convex hull contains a ball
176: $B_r(x_0)$ with $r>0$; one may take small perturbations of the
177: points $x_0\pm e_i$.  Their supporting planes imply
178: \[
179:  \varphi(z)-x_0\cdot z
180:  \ge\max_j\{(y_j-x_0)\cdot z+\varphi(z_j)-y_j\cdot z_j\}
181:  \ge r|z|-b_{x_0}.
182: \]
183: This function attains a minimum, at which
184: $\nabla\varphi=x_0$.  The gradient is therefore onto, and its local
185: smooth inverses combine to a global smooth inverse.
186: \end{proof}
187: 
188: We will write $x=\nabla\varphi(z)$ and
189: $\tau=D_z^2\varphi$, evaluating $\tau$ at the corresponding point
190: when using $x$ coordinates.  Thus
191: $\tau(x)=D_z^2\varphi((\nabla\varphi)^{-1}(x))$ is smooth, even,
192: positive definite and bounded above by $(4/\eps)I$.
193: The gradient is odd, as is its inverse.  Finally, a compactly
194: supported smooth function of $x$ is compactly supported after
195: composition with $\nabla\varphi$: its support lies in the image of
196: a compact set under the continuous inverse map.  Consequently the
197: compact integrations by parts below are valid in either coordinate
198: system.
