1: \section{A tensor estimate for the divergence tests}\label{sec:tensor}
2: 
3: The gradient and Hessian pairings in \eqref{eq:test-pairing} will be
4: estimated using two quadratic energies, one for $W$ and one for $B$.
5: The following lemma bounds their sum by $\E u^2$. Its proof uses weighted
6: integration by parts and the moment equation; homogeneity enters only
7: when the lemma is applied to the summands $\psi_i$ in
8: Section~\ref{sec:variance}.
9: 
10: \begin{lemma}[Moment-coordinate tensor estimate]\label{lem:tensor}
11: Let $d\mu=Z^{-1}e^{-V}dx$ and $d\nu=e^{-\varphi}dz$ be smooth
12: probability measures on $\R^n$.  Suppose $D_z^2\varphi$ is positive
13: definite and $x=\nabla\varphi(z)$ is a smooth diffeomorphism pushing
14: $\nu$ to $\mu$.
15: For a real $f\in C_c^\infty(\R_x^n)$, put
16: \[
17:  \tau=D_z^2\varphi,\quad M=\tau^{-1},\quad H=D_x^2V,
18:  \quad B=\tau(D_x^2f)\tau,
19:  \quad W=\delta_\mu B,
20:  \quad u=\delta_\mu W.
21: \]
22: View source fields in target coordinates by the inverse moment map.
23: Then
24: \begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-estimate}
25:  \E\tr\bigl((D_xW)^2\bigr)
26:  \ge \E\tr(MBHB),
27: \end{equation}
28: and
29: \begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-energy}
30:  \E u^2\ge\E\tr(MBHB)+\E W^THW.
31: \end{equation}
32: Here $(D_xW)_{ij}=\partial_{x_j}W_i$, and the square in
33: \eqref{eq:tensor-estimate} is a matrix square.
34: \end{lemma}
35: 
36: \begin{proof}
37: All test fields used in the integrations by parts below have compact support.
38: Weighted Bochner calculations underlie many variance estimates; see
39: Bakry--\'Emery \cite[Proposition~3]{BakryEmery1985} and the
40: transport-Hessian formulas in \cite[Section~4.3]{KLR2026}.
41: Here the required identity is for a vector field, so we derive it
42: explicitly and retain the matrix trace $\tr((D_xW)^2)$.
43: First, for any such vector field $W$ and $u=\delta_\mu W$,
44: \[
45:  \partial_{x_i}u
46:  =H_{ij}W_j+(V_{x_j}-\partial_{x_j})\partial_{x_i}W_j.
47: \]
48: Pairing with $W_i$ and using adjunction gives the weighted identity
49: \begin{equation}\label{eq:bochner}
50:  \E u^2
51:  =\E W_i\partial_{x_i}u
52:  =\E W^THW
53:    +\E(\partial_{x_j}W_i)(\partial_{x_i}W_j)
54:  =\E W^THW+\E\tr\bigl((D_xW)^2\bigr).
55: \end{equation}
56: Thus it remains to prove \eqref{eq:tensor-estimate}.
57: Its proof compares two expansions, integrates their derivative terms
58: by parts, and writes the resulting difference as a sum of squares.
59: 
60: Recall $h=D_x^2f$ and $P=\tau h$ from \eqref{eq:test-fields}, and let
61: $(C_i)_{kl}=C_{ikl}$, where $C=D_z^3\varphi$.
62: The moment equation \eqref{eq:moment} reads
63: \[
64:  V(x(z))=\varphi(z)+\log\det\tau(z)-\log Z.
65: \]
66: Its first derivative gives
67: \[
68:  \tau_{ik}V_{x_k}=x_i+c_i,
69:  \qquad c_i=\tr(MC_i).
70: \]
71: Since $\partial_jM=-MC_jM$, differentiating once more gives
72: \[
73:  (\tau H\tau)_{ij}
74:  =\tau_{ij}+M_{kl}\varphi_{klij}
75:    -\tr(MC_iMC_j)-C_{ijk}V_{x_k}.
76: \]
77: To combine the fourth-derivative term and the term involving
78: $\nabla_xV$ into a weighted divergence, note that
79: \[
80:  \partial_dM_{dk}
81:  =-M_{da}C_{abd}M_{bk}=-c_bM_{bk},
82:  \qquad
83:  \partial_dM_{dk}-x_dM_{dk}=-V_{x_k}.
84: \]
85: Full symmetry of $D_z^4\varphi$ therefore yields
86: \begin{equation}\label{eq:twice-moment}
87:  (\tau H\tau-\tau)_{ij}
88:  =(\partial_d-x_d)(M_{dk}C_{ijk})
89:    -\tr(MC_iMC_j).
90: \end{equation}
91: 
92: For the other expansion, the commutator
93: $[\partial_l,\partial_k^*]=\tau_{kl}$ gives
94: \[
95:  D_zW=B+R,\qquad R_{al}=\partial_k^*\partial_lP_{ak}.
96: \]
97: Both $D_zW$ and $B$ are symmetric, so $R$ is symmetric.
98: Since $D_xW=(B+R)M$ and $MBM=h$, expansion followed by one
99: integration by parts gives
100: \begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-expansion}
101:  \E\tr\bigl((D_xW)^2\bigr)
102:  =\E\tr(MBMB)
103:   +2\E(\partial_kh_{al})(\partial_lP_{ak})
104:   +\E\tr(MRMR).
105: \end{equation}
106: The last term is nonnegative:
107: $\tr(MRMR)=\|M^{1/2}RM^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$.
108: On the right side of \eqref{eq:tensor-estimate}, the coefficient of
109: $\tau H\tau$ in the trace contraction is
110: \[
111:  Q=MBMBM=h\tau h.
112: \]
113: Substituting \eqref{eq:twice-moment} and integrating its divergence
114: term by parts gives
115: \begin{equation}\label{eq:H-expansion}
116:  \E\tr(MBHB)
117:  =\E\tr(MBMB)
118:    -\E(\partial_dQ_{ij})M_{dk}C_{ijk}
119:    -\E Q_{ij}\tr(MC_iMC_j).
120: \end{equation}
121: The common first term cancels.  We now evaluate the three remaining
122: contracted terms in coordinates where $\tau=I$ at the point under
123: consideration.
124: 
125: This normalization uses a constant dual change of coordinates.
126: For an invertible constant matrix $T$, set
127: \[
128:  z=Tz',\qquad x'=T^Tx,\qquad
129:  f'(x')=f(T^{-T}x').
130: \]
131: The corresponding normalized potentials and partition function are
132: \[
133:  \varphi'(z')=\varphi(Tz')-\log|\det T|,
134:  \qquad V'(x')=V(T^{-T}x'),
135:  \qquad Z'=|\det T|Z.
136: \]
137: They preserve both probability measures by change of variables,
138: $x'=\nabla_{z'}\varphi'$, and the moment equation.  The chain rule gives
139: \[
140:  \begin{array}{lll}
141:  \tau'=T^T\tau T,& B'=T^TBT,& R'=T^TRT,\\
142:  M'=T^{-1}MT^{-T},& h'=T^{-1}hT^{-T},& H'=T^{-1}HT^{-T},\\
143:  Q'=T^{-1}QT^{-T},& P'=T^TPT^{-T},& W'=T^TW.
144:  \end{array}
145: \]
146: Each $z$-derivative index, including all three indices of $C$,
147: transforms covariantly with $T$; the same law holds for $\partial_k^*$.
148: Also $D_{x'}W'=T^T(D_xW)T^{-T}$.
149: It follows that every contracted scalar in
150: \eqref{eq:trace-expansion} and \eqref{eq:H-expansion} is invariant.
151: For example, in
152: $(\partial_kh_{al})(\partial_lP_{ak})$ each index pairs one
153: covariant and one contravariant factor.
154: At a fixed point we may therefore take $T=\tau^{-1/2}$, using
155: the value of $\tau$ at that point.  All derivatives are computed
156: under this constant transformation.  No moving frame is
157: differentiated; the integrations by parts have already been completed.
158: 
159: In these normalized coordinates, put
160: \[
161:  S_{ijk}=f_{x_ix_jx_k},\qquad J_{ijk}=h_{ia}C_{ajk}.
162: \]
163: The tensor $S$ is fully symmetric, and $J$ is symmetric in its last
164: two indices.  Write $J^{(12)}_{ijk}=J_{jik}$, and use the Euclidean
165: tensor norm and inner product in this frame.
166: The three contractions are
167: \begin{align}
168:  2(\partial_kh_{al})(\partial_lP_{ak})
169:    &=2\|S\|^2+2\langle S,J\rangle,\label{eq:normalized-cross}\\
170:  (\partial_dQ_{ij})C_{ijd}
171:    &=2\langle S,J\rangle+\langle J,J^{(12)}\rangle,
172:        \label{eq:normalized-derivative}\\
173:  Q_{ij}\tr(C_iC_j)&=\|J\|^2.\label{eq:normalized-cubic}
174: \end{align}
175: For \eqref{eq:normalized-cross}, use
176: $\partial_kh_{al}=S_{alk}$ and
177: $\partial_lP_{ak}=C_{ail}h_{ik}+S_{akl}$.
178: For \eqref{eq:normalized-derivative}, differentiating the two factors
179: $h$ in $Q=h\tau h$ gives the two copies of $\langle S,J\rangle$.
180: Differentiating its middle factor gives
181: \[
182:  \sum_{iabjd}h_{ia}C_{abd}h_{bj}C_{ijd}
183:  =\sum_d\tr(hC_dhC_d)
184:  =\langle J,J^{(12)}\rangle.
185: \]
186: Finally, \eqref{eq:normalized-cubic} follows by expanding
187: $Q=h^2$ and the trace, using the full symmetry of $C$.
188: 
189: Thus, apart from the nonnegative $R$ term, the integrand of
190: \eqref{eq:trace-expansion} minus \eqref{eq:H-expansion} is
191: \[
192:  2\|S\|^2+4\langle S,J\rangle
193:    +\|J\|^2+\langle J,J^{(12)}\rangle.
194: \]
195: Full symmetrization of $J$ is
196: \[
197:  (\operatorname{Sym}J)_{ijk}
198:  =\frac{J_{ijk}+J_{jik}+J_{kij}}3.
199: \]
200: Its norm satisfies
201: \[
202:  \|\operatorname{Sym}J\|^2
203:  =\frac{\|J\|^2+2\langle J,J^{(12)}\rangle}{3},
204:  \qquad
205:  \langle S,\operatorname{Sym}J\rangle=\langle S,J\rangle.
206: \]
207: Consequently the preceding integrand equals
208: \begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor-squares}
209:  2\|S+\operatorname{Sym}J\|^2
210:  +\frac16\|J-J^{(12)}\|^2\ge0.
211: \end{equation}
212: This pointwise calculation proves \eqref{eq:tensor-estimate};
213: combining it with \eqref{eq:bochner} proves
214: \eqref{eq:tensor-energy}.
215: \end{proof}
216: 
217: For the soft slab potentials, $H$ is the sum of a positive quadratic
218: part and the positive semidefinite Hessians of the homogeneous
219: summands.  Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor} will control each summand through
220: the two terms on the right side of \eqref{eq:tensor-energy}.
