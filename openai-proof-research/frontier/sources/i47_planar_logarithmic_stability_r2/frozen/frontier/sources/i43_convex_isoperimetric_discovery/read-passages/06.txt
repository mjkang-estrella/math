1: \section{The variance inequality}
2: \label{sec:variance}
3: 
4: We now apply the moment-coordinate estimates to the potential
5: \eqref{eq:potential}. In this section all expectations are with
6: respect to its probability $\mu$.
7: 
8: \begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:variance}]
9: The potential is smooth and even, and $D^2V\ge\eps I$.
10: Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} therefore supplies the moment coordinates,
11: with $0<\tau\le (4/\eps)I$. Put
12: \[
13:  H_i=D_x^2\psi_i,\qquad a_i=\E\psi_i.
14: \]
15: Each $H_i$ is positive semidefinite, and $a_i>0$ since $p_i\ne0$
16: and $\mu$ has positive density everywhere. Homogeneity gives
17: \begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneity}
18:  x\cdot\nabla\psi_i=q\psi_i,
19:  \qquad H_ix=(q-1)\nabla\psi_i.
20: \end{equation}
21: The adjunction identity of Section~\ref{sec:identities} also gives
22: \begin{equation}\label{eq:mi}
23:  m_i:=\E\tr(\tau H_i)=\E x\cdot\nabla\psi_i=q a_i.
24: \end{equation}
25: To justify it for these noncompact functions, apply
26: $\E x_j k=\E\tau_{jk}\partial_{x_k}k$ to
27: $k=\eta_R\partial_{x_j}\psi_i$ and sum in $j$, where $\eta_R$
28: is a smooth cutoff equal to one on $B_R$ and zero outside $B_{2R}$.
29: The extra term is bounded in absolute value by
30: $C R^{-1}\E[|\nabla\psi_i|\mathbf1_{\{|x|\ge R\}}]$, which tends
31: to zero. The other terms converge because $\tau$ is bounded and
32: all polynomial moments are finite.
33: 
34: Take an even $f\in C_c^\infty(\R^n)$ and use
35: \[
36:  B=\tau D_x^2f\,\tau,\qquad W=\delta_\mu B,
37:  \qquad u=\delta_\mu W=A(A-1)f,
38:  \qquad M=\tau^{-1}.
39: \]
40: Applying \eqref{eq:test-pairing} to $F=\psi_i$ gives, for each $i$,
41: \begin{equation}\label{eq:di}
42:  d_i:=\E u\psi_i=\E W\cdot\nabla\psi_i=\E\tr(BH_i).
43: \end{equation}
44: Two Cauchy--Schwarz estimates will give complementary bounds for
45: these same numbers $d_i$.
46: 
47: First apply the Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, including
48: integration, to the matrix fields
49: $H_i^{1/2}\tau^{1/2}$ and $H_i^{1/2}B\tau^{-1/2}$. Their pairing
50: is $\tr(H_iB)$, so
51: \begin{equation}\label{eq:first-cs}
52:  d_i^2\le m_i\,\E\tr(MBH_iB).
53: \end{equation}
54: This does not require $B$ to be positive semidefinite.
55: Second, \eqref{eq:homogeneity}--\eqref{eq:di} give
56: \[
57:  \E x^TH_i x=(q-1)m_i,
58:  \qquad \E x^TH_iW=(q-1)d_i.
59: \]
60: Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive semidefinite form
61: $(X,Y)\mapsto\E X^TH_iY$ therefore yields
62: \begin{equation}\label{eq:second-cs}
63:  \E W^TH_iW\ge(q-1)\frac{d_i^2}{m_i}.
64: \end{equation}
65: 
66: Since $H=D^2V=\eps I+\sum_iH_i$, Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor} and
67: the weighted Bochner identity \eqref{eq:bochner} imply
68: \begin{align}
69:  \E u^2
70:  &\ge\E\tr(MBHB)+\E W^THW\notag\\
71:  &\ge\sum_i\left(\E\tr(MBH_iB)+\E W^TH_iW\right)
72:  \ge\sum_i\frac{q d_i^2}{m_i}
73:  =\sum_i\frac{d_i^2}{a_i}.
74:  \label{eq:dual-bound}
75: \end{align}
76: The discarded terms are nonnegative, since $M$ is positive definite
77: and $B$ is symmetric. This is the required estimate on the dense
78: class of double divergences.
79: 
80: By Lemma~\ref{lem:density}, such $u$ are dense in the centered even
81: subspace of $L^2(\mu)$. Each $\psi_i\in L^2(\mu)$, so all pairings
82: $u\mapsto\E u\psi_i$ are continuous. Thus \eqref{eq:dual-bound}
83: holds for every centered even $u\in L^2(\mu)$.
84: For $F=\sum_i b_i\psi_i$, choose $u=F-\E F$. Then
85: \[
86:  \|u\|_2^2=\sum_i b_i d_i
87:  \le\left(\sum_i b_i^2a_i\right)^{1/2}
88:       \left(\sum_i d_i^2/a_i\right)^{1/2}
89:  \le\left(\sum_i b_i^2a_i\right)^{1/2}\|u\|_2.
90: \]
91: If $\|u\|_2=0$, the conclusion is immediate; otherwise division and
92: squaring prove \eqref{eq:variance}. This completes the analytic
93: assertion and, by Section~\ref{sec:reduction}, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
94: \end{proof}
