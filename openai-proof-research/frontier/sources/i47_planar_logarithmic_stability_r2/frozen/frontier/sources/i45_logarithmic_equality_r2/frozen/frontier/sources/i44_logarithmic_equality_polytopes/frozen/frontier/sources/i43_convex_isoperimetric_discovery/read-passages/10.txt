4: We consider the three-vertex pattern at volume $V=1/\pi$, under the
5: contradictory assumption $A\leq1$.  The slicing direction is the missing
6: axis, so that the top slices are empty.  Proposition
7: \ref{slice:three-second-parameters} gives
8: $0.64\leq h\leq2.25$ and $s\geq t=t(h)$, where
9: \eqref{slice:t-definition} defines $t$ by
10: $h^2t^2=\pi(1-t)$.  In particular, $h>0$ and $0<t<1$.
11: We use the flux identity
12: \[
13:  \int_{\partial E_z}\alpha\,d\ell=hv(z),\qquad p(0)=hs,
14: \]
15: from \eqref{slice:three-flux}, and the coarea identities
16: \eqref{slice:coarea}.  All perimeters in this Section are relative to the
17: unit square.  All terminating decimals denote exact rational numbers.
18: 
19: The argument first gives a lower bound depending only on $h$ and $s$.
20: It then improves that bound on an interval of slice areas by retaining
21: the fact that every slice is contained in the bottom slice.  This is
22: especially useful when a slice changes from a strip to the complement
23: of a corner region.

25: \subsection{An integrated flux inequality}
26: 
27: Fix $\lambda=0.98$. Subtracting $\lambda$ times the flux from the
28: surface-area coarea density gives $1/\alpha-\lambda\alpha$, whereas the
29: decrease of slice area has density $\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}/\alpha$.
30: At a constant value $0<\alpha<1$, their ratio is $g(\alpha)$, where
31: \[
32:  g(X)=\frac{1-\lambda X^2}{\sqrt{1-X^2}},\qquad 0\leq X<1.
33: \]
34: The flux determines only the arclength mean $hv/p$ of $\alpha$,
35: not its pointwise values. The next lemma uses convexity to obtain
36: a whole-slice comparison from this mean. To make that comparison
37: compatible with perimeter lower bounds, we use a non-increasing
38: minorant of $g$: replacing $p$ by a smaller quantity increases
39: the argument $hv/p$ and does not increase the resulting lower bound.
40: 
41: Set
42: \begin{equation}\label{nest:weight}
43:  \begin{gathered}
44:  D=\frac{\sqrt{48}}7,\qquad \ell=0.28,\\
45:  G(X)=g(X_0),\qquad X_0=\min\{X,D\},\quad X\geq0.
46:  \end{gathered}
47: \end{equation}
48: Direct differentiation gives
49: \begin{equation}\label{nest:weight-derivative}
50:  -\frac{d}{dr}g(\sqrt r)
51:    =\frac{0.48-0.49r}{(1-r)^{3/2}},\qquad 0\leq r<1.
52: \end{equation}
53: Thus $g$ decreases up to $D$, increases thereafter, and
54: $g(D)=7/25=\ell$.  Consequently $G$ is non-increasing, takes values
55: in $[\ell,1]$, and satisfies $G(X)\leq g(X)$ for $0\leq X<1$.
56: Its definition for $X\geq1$ also covers perimeter lower bounds
57: for which the substituted argument exceeds one.

59: \begin{lemma}[Integrated flux]\label{nest:integrated-flux}
60: At every nonempty regular slice, with area $v$ and perimeter $p$,
61: \begin{equation}\label{nest:jensen}
62:  \int_{\partial E_z}\frac{d\ell}{\alpha}-\lambda hv
63:  \geq
64:  G(hv/p)\int_{\partial E_z}
65:        \frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}}{\alpha}\,d\ell.
66: \end{equation}
67: If a nonnegative measurable function $\gamma$ satisfies
68: $G(hv(z)/p(z))\geq\gamma(v(z))$ at the nonempty regular levels, then
69: \begin{equation}\label{nest:area-coordinate}
70:  A\geq\frac{\lambda h}{\pi}+\int_0^s\gamma(v)\,dv.
71: \end{equation}
72: \end{lemma}
73: 
74: \begin{proof}
75: For fixed $J\in[0,1]$, let
76: \[
77:  f_J(X)=\frac1X-\lambda X-J\frac{\sqrt{1-X^2}}X,
78:  \qquad 0<X\leq1.
79: \]
80: On $(0,1)$,
81: \[
82:  X^3f_J''(X)=2-J\frac{2-3X^2}{(1-X^2)^{3/2}}\geq0.
83: \]
84: Indeed, the function $(2-3r)/(1-r)^{3/2}$ decreases from $2$ to $0$
85: on $[0,2/3]$, its derivative being
86: $-3r/[2(1-r)^{5/2}]$; it is nonpositive on $[2/3,1)$.
87: Convexity extends to the continuous endpoint value at $X=1$.
88: 
89: Use normalized arclength on the slice curve as a probability measure.
90: The flux identity says that the mean of $\alpha$ is
91: $m=hv/p\in(0,1]$.  Take $J=G(m)$.  For $m<1$,
92: \[
93:  f_J(m)=\frac{\sqrt{1-m^2}}m\bigl(g(m)-G(m)\bigr)\geq0;
94: \]
95: for $m=1$, $f_J(1)=1-\lambda>0$.  Jensen's inequality now yields
96: $\int f_J(\alpha)\,d\ell\geq0$, which is
97: \eqref{nest:jensen}.
98: 
99: Down-set position gives $N_z\geq0$, hence
100: $\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}=N_z$.  The coarea identities therefore turn
101: \eqref{nest:jensen} into
102: \[
103:  A-\lambda hV
104:  \geq\int_0^1G(hv(z)/p(z))\,(-v'(z))\,dz
105:  \geq\int_0^1\gamma(v(z))\,(-v'(z))\,dz.
106: \]
107: The integrands are set to zero at empty or exceptional levels.
108: Lemma~\ref{slice:regularity}, with $r=0$, identifies the last integral
109: with $\int_0^s\gamma(v)\,dv$. Since $V=1/\pi$, this proves
110: \eqref{nest:area-coordinate}.
111: \end{proof}

113: \begin{lemma}[Planar patterns and the baseline bound]
114: \label{nest:planar-patterns}
115: Every nonempty regular slice satisfies
116: \begin{equation}\label{nest:planar-bound}
117:  p\geq p_*(v):=
118:  \min\{\sqrt{\pi v},\ 1,\ \sqrt{\pi(1-v)}\}.
119: \end{equation}
120: Put $a_0=1/\pi$, $b_0=1-1/\pi$, and define
121: \begin{equation}\label{nest:baseline}
122:  B(h)=\frac{\lambda h}{\pi}
123:        +\int_0^{t(h)}G\!\left(\frac{hv}{p_*(v)}\right)\,dv-1.
124: \end{equation}
125: At $v=0$ the argument in the integrand is understood by its limit.
126: Then
127: \begin{equation}\label{nest:baseline-slack}
128:  A-1\geq B(h)+\ell(s-t).
129: \end{equation}
130: \end{lemma}
131: 
132: \begin{proof}
133: The far vertex $(1,1)$ is absent even from the bottom slice and hence
134: from every slice.  A nonempty regular slice has, by monotonicity,
135: one, two adjacent, or three occupied square vertices.  In the first
136: case the slice is confined to the occupied corner.  Reflection across
137: its two adjacent sides gives a bounded planar set of area $4v$ and
138: perimeter $4p$; the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality gives
139: $p^2\geq\pi v$.  In the three-vertex case the same argument applies
140: to the opposite-corner complement and gives $p^2\geq\pi(1-v)$.
141: 
142: In the two-vertex case, after interchanging the square coordinates if
143: necessary, the occupied vertices form the bottom edge.  This entire
144: edge is occupied and the opposite edge is vacant.  Each vertical
145: segment crosses the separating curve exactly once: monotonicity
178: \begin{lemma}[Bottom calibration and nesting]
179: \label{nest:bottom-calibration}
180: Each $L\in\{L_x,L_y\}$ satisfies
181: \begin{equation}\label{nest:extent-bound}
182:  hs\geq\frac{1-s}{L}+\frac{\pi L}{4}.
183: \end{equation}
184: Consequently, a strip slice, written as a decreasing subgraph across
185: either square coordinate, has smaller endpoint at most
186: \begin{equation}\label{nest:endpoint-bound}
187:  \begin{split}
188:  e&=1-\frac2\pi
189:        \left(hs-\sqrt{h^2s^2-\pi(1-s)}\right)\\
190:   &\leq1-\frac{2ht}{\pi}+QY,
191:  \qquad
192:  Y=\sqrt{s-t},\qquad
193:  Q=\frac2\pi\sqrt{\pi+2h^2t}.
194:  \end{split}
195: \end{equation}
196: \end{lemma}
197: 
198: \begin{proof}
199: Use coordinates $(\xi,\eta)$ measured from the complement's corner,
200: so that its monotone separating curve runs from $(0,L_y)$ to
201: $(L_x,0)$ with $d\xi\geq0$, $d\eta\leq0$.
202: For $L=L_y$, the vector
203: \[
204:  \left(\frac\eta L,-\sqrt{1-\frac{\eta^2}{L^2}}\right)
205: \]
206: has unit length.  Pairing it with the tangent differential and
207: integrating gives
208: \[
209:  \begin{split}
210:  hs&\geq
211:  \frac1L\int\eta\,d\xi
212:    -\int\sqrt{1-\eta^2/L^2}\,d\eta\\
213:  &=\frac{1-s}{L}+\int_0^L\sqrt{1-\eta^2/L^2}\,d\eta
214:  =\frac{1-s}{L}+\frac{\pi L}4.
215:  \end{split}
216: \]
217: The first integral is the complement area.  This calculation uses
218: only monotonicity of the curve, and so requires no convexity.
219: Exchanging $\xi$ and $\eta$ proves the same estimate for $L_x$.
220: Both extents are positive, because the complement has positive area.
221: 
222: Solving the quadratic inequality \eqref{nest:extent-bound} gives,
223: for each extent,
224: \[
225:  L\geq\frac2\pi
226:     \left(hs-\sqrt{h^2s^2-\pi(1-s)}\right).
227: \]
228: The discriminant is nonnegative by $hs\geq\sqrt{\pi(1-s)}$.
229: A strip $y=f(x)$ is contained in the bottom slice, so its smaller
230: endpoint satisfies $f(1)\leq1-L_y\leq e$.  For a strip $x=f(y)$
231: the same argument uses $L_x$.  Thus one common bound controls both
232: orientations without imposing a symmetry on the two extents.
233: 
234: Finally, write $s=t+Y^2$ and use $h^2t^2=\pi(1-t)$ to obtain
235: \[
236:  h^2s^2-\pi(1-s)
237:    =(\pi+2h^2t)Y^2+h^2Y^4.
238: \]
239: Its square root is at most
240: $\sqrt{\pi+2h^2t}\,Y+hY^2$.  Substituting this upper bound in
241: $e$ cancels the term $hY^2$ coming from $hs$, and proves
242: \eqref{nest:endpoint-bound}.
243: \end{proof}

260: \begin{lemma}[Placement and the three slice types]
261: \label{nest:shape-gain}
262: On $1.02\leq h\leq1.62$ one has
263: \begin{equation}\label{nest:interval-placement}
264:  u-d_m\geq\frac{1033}{2700}>0.38>a_0,
265:  \qquad u\leq b_0,\qquad u<t\leq s,\qquad hu\leq0.96.
266: \end{equation}
267: Define
268: \begin{equation}\label{nest:shape-function}
269:  H(q)=\min\left\{q^2,\frac{\pi(1-q)}{c d_m}\right\},
270:  \qquad 0\leq q\leq1.
271: \end{equation}
272: If $\delta>0$, put $q=(v-u+\delta)/\delta$ for
273: $u-\delta\leq v\leq u$. Every regular slice in this area interval satisfies
274: \begin{equation}\label{nest:improved-perimeter}
275:  p^2\geq1+c\delta^2H(q).
276: \end{equation}
277: \end{lemma}
278: 
279: \begin{proof}
280: The definition of $u$ gives $u\geq0.96/1.62=16/27$,
281: $u\leq b_0$, and $hu\leq0.96$.  Hence the first assertion follows
282: from $16/27-0.21=1033/2700$, using $\pi>3$.
283: Moreover,
284: \[
285:  h^2u^2\leq0.96^2<1\leq\pi(1-u).
286: \]
287: The function $x\mapsto h^2x^2+\pi x-\pi$ is strictly increasing
288: for $x\geq0$ and vanishes at $t$, so $u<t$.
289: This proves \eqref{nest:interval-placement}; in particular
290: $[u-\delta,u]\subset[a_0,b_0]\cap[0,s]$, where $p_*=1$.
291: For the remaining assertion assume $\delta>0$. The definition of
292: $\delta$ and \eqref{nest:endpoint-bound} then give
293: \begin{equation}\label{nest:strip-endpoint}
294:  e\leq u-P+QY\leq u-\delta.
295: \end{equation}
296: 
297: We verify \eqref{nest:improved-perimeter} separately for all three
298: slice types of Lemma~\ref{nest:planar-patterns}.
299: 
300: \emph{Occupied corner.}
301: Here $p^2\geq\pi v$.  Since $v>0.38$ and $\pi>3$,
302: \[
303:  p^2-1>0.14>c d_m^2=0.11907\geq c\delta^2H(q).
304: \]
305: In the last step we used $H(q)\leq q^2\leq1$.
306: 
307: \emph{Opposite-corner complement.}
308: Here $p^2\geq\pi(1-v)$, and $v=u-\delta+\delta q$ gives
309: \[
310:  \begin{split}
311:  p^2-1&\geq\pi(b_0-v)
312:  \geq\pi\delta(1-q)\\
313:  &\geq c\delta^2\frac{\pi(1-q)}{c d_m}
314:  \geq c\delta^2H(q).
315:  \end{split}
316: \]
317: This includes the endpoint $q=1$, $u=b_0$, where $H(1)=0$.
318: 
319: \emph{Strip.}
320: Write the strip as $y=f(x)$, interchanging the two square coordinates
321: if required.  Its smooth decreasing graph has $f(1)\leq e$ by
322: Lemma~\ref{nest:bottom-calibration}.  Integration by parts and
323: \eqref{nest:strip-endpoint} give
324: \[
325:  \int_0^1x|df|=\int_0^1f(x)\,dx-f(1)
326:     =v-f(1)\geq v-u+\delta=:j=\delta q.
327: \]
328: Set $k=2.5j\leq2.5d_m=0.525$.  Pair the graph tangent
329: $(dx,|df|)$ with the unit vector
330: $(\sqrt{1-k^2x^2},kx)$ to obtain
331: \begin{equation}\label{nest:strip-calibration}
332:  p\geq kj+\int_0^1\sqrt{1-k^2x^2}\,dx.
333: \end{equation}
334: For $0\leq w\leq0.525^2$,
335: \[
336:  \sqrt{1-w}\geq1-\frac w2-\frac{w^2}5.
337: \]
338: Indeed the right side is positive and its square minus $1-w$ is
339: $w^2(-3/20+w/5+w^2/25)\leq0$; the expression in parentheses is
340: increasing and is negative at $w=0.525^2$.
341: It follows from \eqref{nest:strip-calibration} that
342: \[
343:  \begin{split}
344:  p&\geq1+\left(\frac{35}{24}-\frac{25j^2}{16}\right)j^2\\
345:   &\geq1+\frac{26677}{19200}j^2
346:    \geq1+1.35j^2.
347:  \end{split}
348: \]
349: Squaring gives $p^2\geq1+2.7j^2\geq1+c\delta^2H(q)$.
350: This establishes the same estimate for every slice type, even if
351: the type or strip orientation changes within the area interval.
352: Exceptional levels are not needed in this argument and have zero
353: measure after the substitution in Lemma~\ref{nest:integrated-flux}.
354: \end{proof}

356: \subsection{From perimeter improvement to an area gain}
357: 
358: The perimeter improvement has size proportional to $\delta^2$ on
359: an interval of slice areas of width $\delta$. We will show that it
360: adds an explicit positive multiple of $\delta^3$ to the total area
361: bound. The derivative estimate below converts the improvement of $p$
362: into an improvement of $G(hv/p)$; the moments of $H$ then average
363: that improvement over the interval. Together with the already obtained
364: term $\ell(s-t)$, this will leave a one-variable minimization over
365: the unknown bottom area $s$.

504: \begin{proposition}[The nesting area bound]\label{nest:area-bound}
505: For the three-vertex configuration at $V=1/\pi$ with $A\leq1$,
506: \[
507:  A-1\geq B(h)\qquad(0.64\leq h\leq2.25).
508: \]
509: On $1.02\leq h\leq1.62$ define
510: \begin{equation}\label{nest:gain-definition}
511:  S(h)=\inf_{y\geq0}
512:  \left\{\ell y^2+
513:    C_0(h)\min\{d_m,(P-Qy)_+\}^{\,3}\right\}.
514: \end{equation}
515: Then
516: \begin{equation}\label{nest:area-gain}
517:  A-1\geq B(h)+S(h).
518: \end{equation}
519: \end{proposition}
520: 
521: \begin{proof}
522: The first assertion follows from \eqref{nest:baseline-slack}.
523: For the second, first suppose $\delta>0$ and restrict to the area
524: interval $[u-\delta,u]$ of Lemma~\ref{nest:shape-gain}.  Put
525: $K=c\delta^2H(q)$.  Since $p\geq\sqrt{1+K}$ and $G$ is
526: non-increasing,
527: \[
528:  G(hv/p)\geq G\!\left(\frac{hv}{\sqrt{1+K}}\right).
529: \]
530: On this interval the baseline uses $p_*=1$, and
531: $hv\leq hu\leq0.96<D$.
532: Thus the whole squared-argument interval
533: $[(hv)^2/(1+K),(hv)^2]$ lies in the differentiable branch of $G$.
534: By \eqref{nest:weight-derivative} and
535: Lemma~\ref{nest:derivative-bound},
536: \begin{equation}\label{nest:pointwise-gain}
537:  \begin{split}
538:  G\!\left(\frac{hv}{\sqrt{1+K}}\right)-G(hv)
539:  &\geq \int_{(hv)^2/(1+K)}^{(hv)^2}0.91\sqrt r\,dr\\
540:  &\geq\frac{0.91(hv)^3K}{(1+K)^{3/2}}
541:  \geq\frac{0.91(hv)^3c\delta^2H(q)}{p_2^{3/2}}.
542:  \end{split}
543: \end{equation}
544: The second inequality bounds $\sqrt r$ below by its value at the
545: left endpoint, and the last uses $1+K\leq p_2$.
546: 
547: Changing variables $v=u-\delta+\delta q$ supplies a further factor
548: $\delta$.  Jensen's inequality for the convex function $v^3$ on
549: $v\geq0$, with probability density $H/M$, gives
550: \[
551:  \begin{split}
552:  \int_0^1 (u-\delta+\delta q)^3H(q)\,dq
553:  &\geq M\bigl(u-(1-q_m)\delta\bigr)^3\\
554:  &\geq M\bigl(u-(1-q_m)d_m\bigr)^3.
555:  \end{split}
556: \]
557: All bases are positive by \eqref{nest:interval-placement}.
558: Integrating \eqref{nest:pointwise-gain} and applying
559: Lemma~\ref{nest:coefficient-bound} therefore gives an additional
560: area contribution at least $C_0\delta^3$ beyond the baseline.
561: If $\delta=0$, that conclusion holds trivially with zero gain;
562: no variable $q$ is then introduced.
563: 
564: Together with the baseline contribution from $[t,s]$, this proves
565: \[
566:  A-1\geq B(h)+\ell(s-t)+
567:  C_0\min\{d_m,(P-Q\sqrt{s-t})_+\}^{\,3}.
568: \]
569: Taking the infimum over all $y\geq0$ weakens this inequality and
570: yields \eqref{nest:area-gain}.
571: \end{proof}
