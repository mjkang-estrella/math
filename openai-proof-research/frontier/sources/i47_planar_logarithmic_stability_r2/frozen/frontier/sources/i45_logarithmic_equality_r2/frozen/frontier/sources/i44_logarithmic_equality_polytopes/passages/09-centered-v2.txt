141:     The volume and mixed-volume inequalities for p ≥ 1 belong to the classical Brunn–Minkowski–
142: Firey theory [14, 22, 28]; at p = 1, centering removes the translation freedom in the classical
143: equality case. The conclusions for 0 < p < 1 follow here for arbitrary centered pairs, without a
144: symmetry assumption. The optimized inequalities are formulated on all bodies containing the origin
145: and provide the common proof. The exception at p = n in (1.11) is forced by the homogeneity
146: Sp (cK, ·) = cn−p Sp (K, ·), not by a loss of regularity.
147:     The logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture was introduced by Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and
148: Zhang [6]. They proved the origin-symmetric planar case, including its equality cases, and related
149: the volume inequality to the logarithmic Minkowski inequality. The centered formulations, with the
150: common direct-sum equality structure, are recorded in [3, Conjectures 3.2 and 3.4]. The distinction
151: between centering and symmetry is essential: a logarithmic Wulff combination of centered bodies
152: need not be centered, and the odd translation modes cannot simply be discarded.
153:     Several earlier results impose geometric symmetries or special structure. Saroglou proved
154: the logarithmic inequality for unconditional pairs [27]. Böröczky and Kalantzopoulos treated
155: pairs invariant under common systems of linear reflections whose fixed hyperplanes intersect
156: only at the origin, and analyzed equality [5]. Rotem obtained the complex-body case through
157: complex interpolation [26]. Van Handel established the local logarithmic inequality for origin-
158: symmetric zonoids [31], while Tao, Xiong and Xiong studied the logarithmic Minkowski inequality
159: and uniqueness for cylinders [30]. These hypotheses are distinct from the unrestricted centroid
160: condition used here.
161:     A complementary line of work studies the second variation of volume. Colesanti, Livshyts
162: and Marsiglietti established a local logarithmic result near the Euclidean ball [9]. Kolesnikov
163: and Milman developed the spectral formulation of local Lp inequalities [20]; Chen, Huang, Li
164: and Liu obtained the global symmetric inequality for p sufficiently close to one, and proved the
165: equivalence of the local and global symmetric formulations [8]; Putterman gave another proof of the
166: equivalence [25]. Milman’s centro-affine approach gives further curvature-dependent and isomorphic
167: logarithmic Minkowski inequalities [23]. More recently, Iffland treated bodies of revolution, allowing
168: nonsymmetric comparison bodies under a weighted centering condition [17]; this condition is not
169: the ordinary centroid condition on the comparison body. Lu proved the global symmetric inequality
170:

174: under n − 2 common orthogonal reflection symmetries [21]. Xi gave a new planar argument using
175: dilation position [32].
176:     The connection with cone-volume measures also has an existence side. Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang
177: and Zhang characterized the nonzero finite even measures realized by origin-symmetric convex
178: bodies through the subspace concentration condition [7]. Henk and Linke established the subspace
179: concentration inequality for centered polytopes [16], and Böröczky and Henk extended it to general
180: centered convex bodies [4]. These necessary concentration inequalities do not constitute an existence
181: theorem for arbitrary noneven data. Theorem 1.3 instead classifies the ambiguity between existing
182: centered realizations; the direct-sum exceptions are consistent with the cylinder phenomena in [30].
183:     The analytic starting point is the moment-coordinate argument of the September 23, 2026 preprint
184: [24], which states the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality for arbitrary origin-symmetric convex
185: bodies. Its compact adjoint identities and tensor calculation are reproduced below without symmetry
186: assumptions. The retained-remainder approach to equality comes from the predecessor manuscript
187: [29]. The present proof replaces the even test space by the orthogonal complement of all affine
188: functions and allows the limiting target to carry a nonconstant exponential density. Neither the
189: symmetric volume theorem nor an equality classification is used as an unproved implication in the
190: argument below.
191:     The moment-measure results of Berman–Berndtsson and Cordero-Erausquin–Klartag supply the
192: underlying coordinates [1, 10]. Klartag’s estimate bounds their Hessian from above [18], and Fathi
193: identifies the target-coordinate Hessian as a Stein kernel [12]. Related links between homogeneous
194: variance inequalities and local Lp inequalities were developed by Kolesnikov, Livshyts and Rotem
195: [19]. The equality limit uses quantitative stability of moment measures [2], local convergence of
196: log-concave densities [11], and interior Monge–Ampère estimates [13]. The first variation of the
197: optimized functional is obtained from the weighted Aleksandrov formula in [15, Lemma 2.4].

1844: for origin-symmetric convex bodies, the second author, with the assistance of GPT, obtained a
1845: complete characterization of the equality cases together with a proof. We then provided GPT with
1846: both the proof of the origin-symmetric logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality and the argument
1847: for its equality cases, and asked it to investigate the logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture for
1848: general convex bodies with centroid at the origin. Approximately half an hour later, GPT proposed
1849: the core idea underlying the proof developed in the present paper. Through many subsequent rounds
