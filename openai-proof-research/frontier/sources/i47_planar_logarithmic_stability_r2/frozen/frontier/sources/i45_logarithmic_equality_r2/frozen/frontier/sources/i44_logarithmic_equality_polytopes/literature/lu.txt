  Let us briefly recall the known results and approaches to log-Brunn-Minkowski in-
equality. The planar case was proved in [4].
Theorem 1.1 (Böröczky-Lutwak-Yang-Zhang). If K and L are origin-symmetric convex
bodies in the plane, then for all λ ∈ [0, 1],
(1.5)                  V2 ((1 − λ) · K +0 λ · L) ≥ V2 (K)1−λ V2 (L)λ .
When λ ∈ (0, 1), equality holds if and only if K and L are dilates or K and L are
parallelograms with parallel sides.
   For unconditional bodies, the logarithmic inequality follows from the coordinatewise
geometric-mean inequality of Bollobás and Leader [2] and Cordero-Erausquin, Fradelizi,
and Maurey [7]; the equality analysis of Saroglou [24] was completed in [3]. Böröczky
and Kalantzopoulos [3] proved it for bodies invariant under n linear reflections whose
fixed hyperplanes intersect only at the origin. Rotem [23] established the complex case.
Yang and Zhang [30] considered convex bodies in the relative Bonnesen class in R3 .
Tao, Xiong, and Xiong [26] focused on origin-symmetric cylinder cases in R3 . Xi and
Leng [29] obtained a nonsymmetric planar extension for bodies put in dilation position
after suitable translations.
   Kolesnikov and Milman [15] developed a new method to derive local Brunn-Minkowski
inequality by studying its infinitesimal form. They obtained local Lp -Brunn–Minkowski
inequalities for C+2 origin-symmetric convex bodies when p ∈ [1 − cn−3/2 , 1). Chen,
Huang, Li, and Liu [6] and Putterman [22] gave a direct equivalence between the local
                   LOGARITHMIC BRUNN–MINKOWSKI UNDER REFLECTIONS                                                     3

and global formulations based on PDE methods and strongly isomorphic polytopes, re-
spectively. Van Handel [28] proved the local logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality
for zonoids. Iffland [14] proved the local logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski inequality for
bodies of revolution by operator theory. More recently, Milman [19] interpreted the con-
jecture spectrally in centro-aﬀine differential geometry and obtained global results under
curvature pinching assumptions. Recently, Hu and Ivaki [13] developed a polyhedral ver-
sion of Milman’s centro-aﬀine spectral framework for full-dimensional origin-symmetric
polytopes and optimized spectral estimate.
   We consider a different symmetry class: n − 2 orthogonal hyperplane reflections,
together with origin symmetry. The remaining two-dimensional fibers retain origin-
symmetry, so that Theorem 1.1 can be combined with the Prékopa–Leindler inequality.

references for the theory of convex bodies are provided by the books of Gardner [10],
Gruber [11], Leichtweiss [16], Schneider [25], and Thompson [27].

convex subset with nonempty interior. In this paper, unless otherwise stated, all convex
bodies are assumed to contain the origin in their interior. We write M |E to denote the
orthogonal projection of a compact convex set M onto a linear subspace E in Rn .
   The support function hK : Rn → R of a compact convex body K in Rn is defined, for
x ∈ Rn , by
