1: \section{Volume and measure consequences}\label{sec:consequences}
2: 
3: With Theorem~\ref{thm:main} proved, we derive the additive volume
4: inequality stated in the introduction and then apply Saroglou's
5: transfer theorem to even log-concave measures.
6: 
7: \subsection{The symmetric \texorpdfstring{$L_p$}{Lp} volume inequality}
8: \label{subsec:lp-proof}
9: 
10: \begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:lp}]
11: At $\lambda=0,1$, the assertion follows from
12: $\mathcal W[h_K]=K$ and $\mathcal W[h_L]=L$. Suppose $0<\lambda<1$.
13: Set
14: \[
15:  a=|K|^{1/n}>0,\qquad b=|L|^{1/n}>0,\qquad
16:  K_0=a^{-1}K,\qquad L_0=b^{-1}L,
17: \]
18: so that $|K_0|=|L_0|=1$. Define
19: \[
20:  c=\bigl((1-\lambda)a^p+\lambda b^p\bigr)^{1/p}>0,
21:  \qquad \theta=\frac{\lambda b^p}{c^p}\in(0,1).
22: \]
23: Then $1-\theta=(1-\lambda)a^p/c^p$. For every $u\in S^{n-1}$,
24: the support identities $h_K=a h_{K_0}$ and $h_L=b h_{L_0}$, followed
25: by the weighted arithmetic--geometric mean inequality, give
26: \begin{align*}
27:  \bigl((1-\lambda)h_K(u)^p+\lambda h_L(u)^p\bigr)^{1/p}
28:  &=c\bigl((1-\theta)h_{K_0}(u)^p+
29:               \theta h_{L_0}(u)^p\bigr)^{1/p}\\
30:  &\ge c h_{K_0}(u)^{1-\theta}h_{L_0}(u)^\theta.
31: \end{align*}
32: All support values here are positive. The Wulff definition is monotone
33: in its support bound and satisfies $\mathcal W[cf]=c\mathcal W[f]$ for
34: $c>0$. Hence
35: \[
36:  c\mathcal W[h_{K_0}^{1-\theta}h_{L_0}^\theta]
37:  \subset
38:  \mathcal W\!\left[
39:   \bigl((1-\lambda)h_K^p+\lambda h_L^p\bigr)^{1/p}
40:  \right].
41: \]
42: By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the unscaled body on the left has volume at
43: least $1$, because $K_0,L_0$ are origin-symmetric and have unit volume.
44: The body on the right therefore has volume at least $c^n$. Raising this
45: bound to the positive power $p/n$ gives
46: \eqref{eq:lp}, since $c^p=(1-\lambda)|K|^{p/n}+\lambda|L|^{p/n}$.
47: \end{proof}
48: 
49: \subsection{Even log-concave measures and the \texorpdfstring{$(B)$}{(B)}-conjecture}
50: \label{sec:measure}
51: 
52: We now pass from volume to integration against even log-concave measures.
53: A nonnegative Radon measure $\mu$ on $\R^n$ is \emph{log-concave} if
54: \[
55:  \mu((1-\theta)A+\theta B)
56:  \ge \mu(A)^{1-\theta}\mu(B)^\theta
57:  \qquad(0<\theta<1)
58: \]
59: for all compact sets $A,B\subset\R^n$; it is \emph{even} if
60: $\mu(A)=\mu(-A)$ for every Borel set $A$. Radon measures are finite on
61: compact sets, but their total mass need not be finite. We use the analogous
62: multiplicative definition of log-concavity for nonnegative functions, so
63: the identically zero function is included. Endpoint products in an
64: interpolation inequality are understood as the corresponding endpoint
65: values.
66: 
67: Saroglou's transfer theorem states that the logarithmic Brunn--Minkowski
68: inequality for Lebesgue measure in a dimension $d$ implies the same
69: inequality in dimension $d$ for every even log-concave density
70: \cite[Theorem~3.1]{Saroglou2016}. The density may vanish and need not be
71: normalized. This theorem is stated for densities with respect to
72: $d$-dimensional Lebesgue measure. The reduction below makes explicit how
73: it also gives the measure statement when the measure has lower-dimensional
74: support.
75: 
76: \begin{corollary}[Measure inequality and scalar $(B)$-conjecture]
77: \label{cor:measure}
78: Let $n\ge1$, let $\mu$ be an even log-concave Radon measure on $\R^n$, and
79: let $K,L\subset\R^n$ be origin-symmetric convex bodies. Then, for every
80: $0\le\lambda\le1$,
81: \begin{equation}\label{eq:measure-logbm}
82:  \mu\bigl(\mathcal W[h_K^{1-\lambda}h_L^\lambda]\bigr)
83:  \ge \mu(K)^{1-\lambda}\mu(L)^\lambda.
84: \end{equation}
85: Consequently, for every origin-symmetric convex body $K\subset\R^n$, the
86: function
87: \begin{equation}\label{eq:b-function}
88:  \R\ni t\longmapsto \mu(e^tK)
89: \end{equation}
90: is log-concave.
91: \end{corollary}
92: 
93: \begin{proof}
94: Fix $0<\lambda<1$ and write
95: $D=\mathcal W[h_K^{1-\lambda}h_L^\lambda]$.
96: The assertion is immediate when $\mu$ is the zero measure. Otherwise,
97: choose a closed ball $C$ centered at the origin large enough that
98: $K\cup L\cup D\subset C$ and $0<\mu(C)<\infty$. This is possible because
99: the three bodies are compact and $\mu$ is a nonzero Radon measure. The
100: probability measure
101: \[
102:  \nu(A)=\frac{\mu(A\cap C)}{\mu(C)}
103: \]
104: is even and log-concave: for compact $A,B$, convexity of $C$ gives
105: \[
106:  (1-\theta)(A\cap C)+\theta(B\cap C)
107:  \subset ((1-\theta)A+\theta B)\cap C,
108: \]
109: and the defining inequality for $\mu$ applies. Since $K,L,D\subset C$,
110: the common factor $\mu(C)$ cancels from \eqref{eq:measure-logbm}. It
111: therefore suffices to prove that inequality for $\nu$.
112: 
113: Let $E$ be the affine hull of $\operatorname{supp}\nu$. Evenness gives
114: $E=-E$, so $E$ is a linear subspace. If $\dim E=0$, then
115: $\nu=\delta_0$; all three bodies contain $0$, and equality holds.
116: Suppose $d=\dim E\ge1$ and identify $E$ isometrically with $\R^d$.
117: By inner regularity, the compact-set definition
118: of log-concavity for $\nu$ is equivalent to the Borel-set formulation
119: using inner measure. Borell's characterization
120: \cite[Theorem~1.1, with $s=0$]{Borell1974}, applied to $\nu$ on $E$,
121: represents it as an affine image of a log-concave density in a dimension
122: $k\le d$. Since its support spans $E$, the affine map has rank $d$.
123: Thus $k=d$, the map is invertible, and $\nu$ has a log-concave density
124: $f$ with respect to Lebesgue measure on $E$. Evenness of $\nu$ gives
125: $f(x)=f(-x)$ almost everywhere; replacing $f(x)$ by
126: $\sqrt{f(x)f(-x)}$ gives an even log-concave representative of the same
127: density.
128: 
129: Set $K_E=K\cap E$ and $L_E=L\cap E$. These are origin-symmetric convex
130: bodies in $E$, since $K$ and $L$ contain neighborhoods of $0$. Define
131: their relative support functions for every $v\in E$ by
132: \[
133:  h^E_{K_E}(v)=\max_{y\in K_E}\langle y,v\rangle,
134:  \qquad
135:  h^E_{L_E}(v)=\max_{y\in L_E}\langle y,v\rangle.
136: \]
137: The relative Wulff body in $E$ is
138: \[
139:  D_E=\bigcap_{v\in E}
140:  \left\{x\in E:\langle x,v\rangle
141:  \le h^E_{K_E}(v)^{1-\lambda}h^E_{L_E}(v)^\lambda\right\}.
142: \]
143: Using all $v\in E$ rather than only unit vectors gives the same body by
144: homogeneity. Let $P_E$ be the orthogonal projection onto $E$. For
145: $x\in D_E$ and $u\in S^{n-1}$,
146: \begin{align*}
147:  \langle x,u\rangle
148:  &=\langle x,P_Eu\rangle\\
149:  &\le h^E_{K_E}(P_Eu)^{1-\lambda}
150:        h^E_{L_E}(P_Eu)^\lambda\\
151:  &\le h_K(u)^{1-\lambda}h_L(u)^\lambda.
152: \end{align*}
153: Indeed, $h^E_{K_E}(P_Eu)=\max_{y\in K\cap E}\langle y,u\rangle\le h_K(u)$,
154: and likewise for $L$; if $P_Eu=0$, the middle product is $0$.
155: Thus
156: \begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-wulff-inclusion}
157:  D_E\subset D\cap E.
158: \end{equation}
159: Theorem~\ref{thm:main} in dimension $d$, followed by Saroglou's transfer
160: theorem \cite[Theorem~3.1]{Saroglou2016} in that same dimension, gives
161: \[
162:  \nu(D_E)\ge \nu(K_E)^{1-\lambda}\nu(L_E)^\lambda.
163: \]
164: Since $\nu$ is supported on $E$, inclusion
165: \eqref{eq:relative-wulff-inclusion} yields
166: \[
167:  \nu(D)=\nu(D\cap E)\ge\nu(D_E)
168:  \ge\nu(K)^{1-\lambda}\nu(L)^\lambda,
169: \]
170: as required. At $\lambda=0,1$, the Wulff bodies are $K,L$, respectively,
171: and the endpoint convention gives equality.
172: 
173: Finally, for $s,t\in\R$, the support identity $h_{e^sK}=e^s h_K$ gives
174: \[
175:  \mathcal W\!\left[h_{e^sK}^{1-\lambda}h_{e^tK}^\lambda\right]
176:  =\mathcal W\!\left[e^{(1-\lambda)s+\lambda t}h_K\right]
177:  =e^{(1-\lambda)s+\lambda t}K.
178: \]
179: Applying \eqref{eq:measure-logbm} to $e^sK$ and $e^tK$ proves
180: \[
181:  \mu\bigl(e^{(1-\lambda)s+\lambda t}K\bigr)
182:  \ge \mu(e^sK)^{1-\lambda}\mu(e^tK)^\lambda.
183: \]
184: This is exactly the log-concavity in \eqref{eq:b-function}, and is the
185: scalar-dilation argument of Saroglou \cite[Corollary~3.2]{Saroglou2016}.
186: \end{proof}
