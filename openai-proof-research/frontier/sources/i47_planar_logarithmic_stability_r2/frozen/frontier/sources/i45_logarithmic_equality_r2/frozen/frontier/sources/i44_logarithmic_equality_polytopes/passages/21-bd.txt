115:     Figalli, Maggi, Pratelli [37] proved a factor of the form γ ∗ (n) = cn−14
116: for some absolute constant c > 0, which was improved to cn−7 by Segal [87],
117: and subsequently to cn−5.5 by Kolesnikov, Milman [66], Theorem 12.12.
118: The current best known bound for γ ∗ (n) is cn−5 (log n)−1 , which follows by
119: combining the general estimate of Kolesnikov-Milman [66], Theorem 12.2,
120: with the logarithmic bound of Klartag [63] on the Cheeger constant of a
121: convex body in isotropic position improving on Yuansi Chen’s work [24] on
122: the Kannan-Lovász-Simonovits conjecture. Harutyunyan [51] conjectured
123: that γ ∗ (n) = cn−2 is the optimal order of the constant, and showed that it
124: can’t be of smaller order. Actually, Segal [87] observed that Dar’s conjecture
125: in [29] would imply that we may choose γ ∗ (n) = cn−2 for some absolute
126: constant c > 0.
127:     We note that recently, various breakthrough stability results about ge-
128: ometric functional inequalities have been obtained. Fusco, Maggi, Pratelli
129: [43] proved an optimal stability version of the isoperimetric inequality (whose
130: result was extended to the Brunn-Minkowski inequality by Figalli, Maggi,
131: Pratelli [36, 37], see also Eldan, Klartag [33]). Stonger versions of the Borell-
132: Brascamp-Lieb inequality are provided by Ghilli, Salani [46] and Rossi,
133: Salani [83], and of the Sobolev inequality by Figalli, Zhang [40] (extend-
134: ing Bianchi, Egnell [9] and Figalli, Neumayer [38]), Nguyen [78] and Wang
135: [95], and of some related inequalities by Caglar, Werner [21]. Related in-
136: equalities are verified by Colesanti [26], Colesanti, Livshyts, Marsiglietti [27],
137: P. Nayar, T. Tkocz [76, 77], Xi, Leng [90].

166:     The cone volume measure or L0 -surface area measure VK on S n−1 ,
167: whose study was initiated independently by Firey [42] and Gromov and
168: Milman [49], has become an indispensable tool in the last decades (see say
169: Barthe, Guédon, Mendelson, Naor [8], Naor [75], Paouris, Werner [79]).
170: If a convex body K contains the origin, then its cone volume measure is
171: dVK = n1 hK dSK where hK is the support function of K and the total
172: measure is the volume of K.

224:      We say that A ∈ GL(n) is a linear reflection associated to a linear (n−1)-
225: space H ⊂ Rn if A fixes the points of H and det A = −1. In this case, there
226: exists u ∈ Rn \H such that Au = −u where the invariant subspace Ru
227: is uniquely determined (see Davis [30], Humphreys [57], Vinberg [94]). It
228: follows that a linear reflection A is a classical ”orthogonal” reflection if and
229: only if A ∈ O(n). We say that the linear reflections A1 , . . . , An associated to
230: linear (n−1)-spaces H1 , . . . , Hn ⊂ Rn are independent if H1 ∩. . .∩Hn = {o}.
231: In this case, the n linear (n − 1)-spaces H1 , . . . , Hn ⊂ Rn are also called
232: independent. We also observe that if a compact convex set K ⊂ Rn is
233: invariant under n independent linear reflections, then K is centered.
