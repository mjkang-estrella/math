                                                                                       Abstract
                                                       We prove the log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture for convex bodies with symme-
                                                   tries to n independent hyperplanes, and discuss the equality case and the unique-
                                                   ness of the solution of the related case of the logarithmic Minkowski problem. We
                                                   also clarify a small gap in the known argument classifying the equality case of the
                                                   log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture for unconditional convex bodies.

                                          1       Introduction

                                          The classical Brunn-Minkowski inequality and the Minkowski problem form the core of
                                          various areas in fully nonlinear partial differential equations, probability, additive combi-
                                          natorics and convex geometry (see Schneider [68], Trudinger, X.-J. Wang [72] and Tao,
                                          Vu [71]). For recent related work in the theory of valuations, algorithmic theory and
                                          the Gaussian setting, see Jochemko, Sanyal [44], Kane [45], Gardner, Gronchi [34] and
                                          Gardner, Zvavitch [35]. Extending it, Lutwak [53] initiated the rapidly developing new
                                          Lp -Brunn-Minkowski theory.
                                              The classical Minkowski’s existence theorem due to Minkowski and Aleksandrov de-
                                          scribes the so called surface area measure SK on S n−1 (the case p = 1) of a convex body
                                          K in Rn where the regularity of the solution is well investigated by Nirenberg [59], Cheng
                                          and Yau [19], Pogorelov [61] and Caffarelli [15]. After the first major results about the
                                          Lp -Brunn-Minkowski inequality and Lp -Minkowski problem for a range of p by Firey
                                          [31], Lutwak [53], Chou, Wang [20] and Hug, Lutwak, Yang, Zhang [39], the recent pa-
                                          pers Kolesnikov, Milman [48], Milman [55], Bianchi, Böröczky, Colesanti, Yang [5], Chen,
                                          Huang, Li [16], Ivaki [42] present new developments.

                                          last decades (see Barthe, Guédon, Mendelson, Naor [4], Naor [57], Paouris, Werner [60],
                                          Böröczky, Henk [11]). If a convex body K contains the origin, then its cone volume mea-
                                          sure is dVK = n1 hK dSK where hK is the support function of K and VK (S n−1 ) = V (K)

finite non-trivial Borel even measure µ on S n−1 is a cone volume measure if and only if

    However, the characterization of the cone volume measure of non-origin symmetric
convex bodies is wide open. While Chen, Li, Zhu [17] have recently verified that the
subspace concentration condition is sufficient to ensure that a measure on S n−1 is a cone
volume measure, not even a meaningful conjecture is known about the right necessary
conditions. All what is known concerning characterization is that Böröczky, Hegedűs [10]
characterized the restriction of a cone volume measure to an antipodal pair of points.
    Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang, Zhang [12] proposed the logarithmic Brunn-Minkowski con-
jecture Conjecture 1 in the even case. Before stating it, we recall the Brunn-Minkowski
inequality, which says that for every pair of convex bodies K and L in Rn , and for every
λ ∈ (0, 1), one has
                                         1                  1          1
                     V ((1 − λ)K + λL) n ≥ (1 − λ)V (K) n + λV (L) n ,                  (3)

with equality if and if K and L are homothetic (i.e. L = γK + x for γ > 0, x ∈ Rn ); or,
equivalently
                          V ((1 − λ)K + λL) ≥ V (K)1−λ V (L)λ                        (4)
with equality if and only if K and L are translates. See Gardner [33] or Schneider [68] for
more details. Analytically, the Brunn-Minkowski inequality has numerous realizations as
a Poincare-type inequality (see, e.g. Colesanti [21], Colesanti, Hug, Saorin-Gomez [23],
Colesanti, Livshyts, Marsiglietti [22], Kolesnikov, Milman [47]). This approach also led to
strong stability versions of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality by Figalli, Maggi, Pratelli [29]

proof of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality from around 1900 (see Bonnesen, Fenchel [7]),
which argument was further developed by Alexandrov in the 1930’s, see Kolesnikov,
Milman [47] for a modern presentation of their ideas.

equality may provide a really bad estimate, which may be insufficient for certain applica-
tions. Below we describe a few conjectures robustly strengthening the Brunn-Minkowski
inequality.

    Let us summarize what is known about the Log-Brunn-Minkowski Conjecture 1 and
Log-Minkowski Conjecture 2. Concerning planar bodies, Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang, Zhang
[12] verified both conjectures in origin symmetric case, and Xi, Leng [75] proved Conjec-
ture 1 in full generality. Turning to higher dimensions, besides the cases of unconditional
convex bodies (see below) and complex bodies by Rotem [65], these conjectures are proved
when K is close to be an ellipsoid in the sense of Hausdorff metric by a combination of the
local estimates by Kolesnikov, Milman [48] and the use of the continuity method in PDE
by Chen, Huang, Li [16]. Another even more recent proof of this result based on Alexan-
drov’s approach of considering the Hilbert-Brunn-Minkowski operator for polytopes and
[48] is due to Putterman [64]. In addition, an isomorphic version of the Logarithmic
Minkowski Problem is verified in Milman [55].
    We note that the conjectured uniqueness of the solution of the Logarithmic, or L0 -
Minkowski problem (1) for even positive C ∞ function f has a special role within the
Lp -Minkowski Problems as if p < 0, then it is known that the even solution may not be
unique (see Jian, Lu, Wang [43], Li, Liu, Lu [51], Milman [56]).

Actually, the paper [66] contains a small gap concerning the equality case, and we clarify
the argument in the Appendix Section 7.

2   Some properties of convex compact sets and the L0 -sum

In this section, we collect some known facts about convex bodies. For some of these
statements we have not found a reference, and in these cases, we provide the short
arguments.
    For notions in convexity, see Schneider [68]. In the Euclidean n-space Rn , we denote
the standard inner product by h·, ·i, the Euclidean norm by | · |, the volume (Lebesgue
measure) by V (·), and the k-dimensional Hausdorff measure by Hk . We denote the
Euclidean unit ball centered at the origin and sphere in Rn by B2n and S n−1 = ∂B2n ,
respectively. For a convex subset K of Rn , we write ∂K and relintK to denote the relative
boundary and the relative interior with respect to the affine hull of K, respectively, and
intK the interior of K with respect to Rn . A convex body is a compact convex subset
with non-empty interior. We write M|E to denote the orthogonal projection of a compact
convex set M into a linear subspace E in Rn .
    The support function hK : Rn → R of a compact convex set K in Rn is defined, for
x ∈ Rn , by

                               hK (x) = max{hx, yi : y ∈ K}.

Note that support functions are positively homogeneous of degree one and subadditive.
A vector u ∈ Rn \{o} is an exterior normal vector at a boundary point x ∈ ∂K if

                                       hx, ui = hK (u),

and it is called a unit exterior normal if u ∈ S n−1 . A point x ∈ ∂K is called smooth
boundary point if there exists a unique exterior unit normal. We denote by ∂ ′ K the set
of all smooth boundary points. It is well known that the set of all non-smooth boundary
points of a convex body has Hn−1 -measure equal to 0. The spherical image map

                                      νK : ∂ ′ K → S n−1

irreducible subspaces E1 , . . . , Ek , k ≥ 1, satisfying that

   • Rn = ⊕ki=1 Ei ;

   • Ei and Ej are orthogonal for i 6= j;

   • G = G1 × . . . × Gk where Gi ⊂ O(Ei ) acts irreducibly on Ei .

This decomposition corresponds to the irreducible representations coming from the action
of the closure of G in O(n), see Humphreys [40] for representations of compact groups.
    Typical example for a finite group G ⊂ O(n) generated by reflections through n
independent hyperplanes and acting irreducibly on Rn is the symmetry group of a regular
polytope P in Rn whose centroid is the origin (see McCammond [54] or Humphreys [41]).
For example, if P is a regular simplex, then the n independent hyperplanes might be the
perpendicular bisectors of the n edges meeting at a fixed vertex of P .

of a finite Coxeter group, and dicusses the fundamental properties. These Weyl chambers
partition Rn into simplicial cones (see Lemma 3 (ii) and (iii)).

5   From log-B.M. for Lebesgue measure to log-B.M. with e−φ(x) dx where φ(x)

A function f : Ω → [0, ∞) on a convex subset Ω ⊂ Rn is called log-concave if f ((1 −
s)x + sy) ≥ f (x)1−s f (y)s for any s ∈ [0, 1] and x, y ∈ Ω; namely, if f = e−φ(x) for a
convex function φ : Rn → R ∪ ∞. Analogously, a measure ν on Rn is called log-concave if
dν(x) = f (x) dx for a log-concave function f on Rn (which property is equivalent saying
that ν((1 − λ)A + λ B) ≥ ν(A)1−λ ν(B)λ for any A, B ⊂ Rn compact according to Borell
[9]).

measures, the logarithmic-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for the Lebesgue measure implies
the logarithmic-Brunn-Minkowski inequality for any log-concave measure. In other words,
according to Theorem 3.1 in [67], if (8) holds for any o-symmetric convex bodies K, L in
Rn , then for any even convex ϕ : Rn → (−∞, ∞] function, we have
