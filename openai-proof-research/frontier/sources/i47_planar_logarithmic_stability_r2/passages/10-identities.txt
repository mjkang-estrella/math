1: \section{Adjoints in moment coordinates}\label{sec:identities}
2: 
3: We retain the probability measures
4: \[
5:  d\mu(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\,dx,
6:  \qquad d\nu(z)=e^{-\varphi(z)}\,dz,
7:  \qquad x=\nabla\varphi(z),
8: \]
9: and the smooth diffeomorphism constructed above.  Put
10: \[
11:  \tau=D_z^2\varphi,\qquad M=\tau^{-1}.
12: \]
13: In target coordinates, these symbols denote the same matrix fields
14: evaluated at $z=(\nabla\varphi)^{-1}(x)$.
15: Expectation under either measure, with functions identified by this map,
16: is denoted by $\E$.
17: Throughout the coordinate calculations, repeated indices are summed
18: from $1$ to $n$, and $Q:L=\tr(Q^TL)$ denotes matrix contraction.
19: 
20: We first construct the compact test functions used in the variance
21: argument and establish an identity that will also identify the
22: obstruction to their density.  Write
23: \[
24:  \partial_k=\partial_{z_k},\qquad
25:  \partial_k^*=x_k-\partial_k.
26: \]
27: The latter is the adjoint of $\partial_k$ in $L^2(\nu)$ because
28: $\partial_k\varphi=x_k$.  In target coordinates, write $\delta_\mu$
29: for negative weighted divergence:
30: \[
31:  \delta_\mu W
32:  =\sum_j\bigl(V_{x_j}W_j-\partial_{x_j}W_j\bigr).
33: \]
34: On a matrix, $\delta_\mu$ acts on each row, producing a vector.
35: For a smooth row field $P_{ak}$ and a compact test $g$, the chain rule
36: and adjunction give
37: \[
38:  \E g\,\partial_k^*P_{ak}
39:  =\E(\partial_k g)P_{ak}
40:  =\E g_{x_i}\tau_{ik}P_{ak}.
41: \]
42: Consequently
43: \begin{equation}\label{eq:row-adjoint}
44:  \partial_k^*P_{ak}=(\delta_\mu(P\tau))_a.
45: \end{equation}
46: Taking $P$ to be the identity matrix also gives $\delta_\mu\tau=x$.
47: This is Fathi's moment-map construction of a Stein kernel
48: \cite[Theorem~2.3 of the cited arXiv version]{Fathi2019}: the matrix
49: field $\tau$ expresses integration by parts against $x$ through
50: $\E x_j g=\E\tau_{jk}g_{x_k}$.
51: 
52: Thus the differential operator
53: \begin{equation}\label{eq:operator-A}
54:  Af=\delta_\mu(\tau\nabla_x f)
55:    =\partial_k^*f_{x_k}
56:    =x\cdot\nabla_x f-\tau:D_x^2f
57: \end{equation}
58: is formally symmetric, with
59: \begin{equation}\label{eq:A-form}
60:  \E gAf=\E\nabla_xg\cdot\tau\nabla_xf
61: \end{equation}
62: for compact smooth tests.  These formulas involve no operator-domain
63: assertion. This is the negative of the moment-Hessian diffusion
64: generator studied by Klartag \cite[Section~6]{KlartagArxiv2013};
65: for our sign convention, $Ax_j=x_j$.
66: 
67: For $f\in C_c^\infty(\R_x^n)$, define
68: \begin{equation}\label{eq:test-fields}
69:  h=D_x^2f,\qquad P=\tau h,\qquad B=\tau h\tau,
70:  \qquad W_a=\partial_k^*P_{ak},\qquad u=\delta_\mu W.
71: \end{equation}
72: All these fields have compact support in both coordinates: the inverse
73: image of a compact target set is its compact image under the continuous
74: inverse diffeomorphism.  By \eqref{eq:row-adjoint},
75: $W=\delta_\mu B$.  For every smooth function $F$ in target coordinates,
76: two adjunctions give
77: \begin{equation}\label{eq:test-pairing}
78:  \E uF=\E W\cdot\nabla_xF=\E B:D_x^2F.
79: \end{equation}
80: The fields are compactly supported, so $F$ need not be.
81: Thus the same pairing can be estimated through either the first or the
82: second derivatives of $F$.
83: To identify the range of these tests, we also use the gradient identity
84: \begin{equation}\label{eq:W-gradient}
85:  W=\nabla_z\bigl[((A-1)f)(x(z))\bigr]
86:    =\tau\nabla_x(A-1)f,
87:  \qquad u=A(A-1)f.
88: \end{equation}
89: To check it directly, put
90: $C_{ijk}=\partial_{z_i}\partial_{z_j}\partial_{z_k}\varphi$.
91: This tensor is fully symmetric, and expansion gives
92: \[
93:  W_a=x_k\tau_{ai}f_{x_ix_k}
94:        -C_{aik}f_{x_ix_k}
95:        -\tau_{ai}\tau_{jk}f_{x_ix_kx_j}.
96: \]
97: Differentiating $(A-1)f$ in $z_a$ gives exactly these terms: the
98: first-gradient terms from $x\cdot\nabla_xf$ and $-f$ cancel.
99: The formula for $u$ follows from \eqref{eq:operator-A}.
100: In particular, $D_zW$ is symmetric.
101: 
102: The following identity is the moment-coordinate specialization of
103: the transport-Hessian Bochner formula of Kolesnikov, Livshyts and
104: Rotem \cite[Proposition~4.8]{KLR2026}. We give its direct derivation
105: in the present notation. Two compact integrations by parts give
106: \begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian}
107:  \begin{aligned}
108:  \E(Af)(A-1)f
109:  &=\E\nabla_x f\cdot\tau\nabla_x(A-1)f
110:    =\E\nabla_x f\cdot W\\
111:  &=\E h:B
112:    =\E\bigl\|\tau^{1/2}D_x^2f\,\tau^{1/2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
113:  \end{aligned}
114: \end{equation}
115: Here $\|Q\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\tr(Q^TQ)$.
116: The differential expression $A$ satisfies $A1=0$ and $Ax_j=x_j$,
117: so $A(A-1)$ annihilates every affine function.
118: Section~\ref{sec:density} uses \eqref{eq:hessian} to prove that these
119: are the only $L^2$ obstructions to density. Centering removes constants,
120: and evenness removes linear functions.
