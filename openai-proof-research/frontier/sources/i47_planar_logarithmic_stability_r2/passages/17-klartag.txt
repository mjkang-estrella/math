8: Abstract We discuss a certain Riemannian metric, related to the toric Kähler-
9: Einstein equation, that is associated in a linearly-invariant manner with a given log-
10: concave measure in Rn . We use this metric in order to bound the second derivatives
11: of the solution to the toric Kähler-Einstein equation, and in order to obtain spectral-
12: gap estimates similar to those of Payne and Weinberger.

16: 1 Introduction
17: 
18: In this paper we explore a certain geometric structure related to the moment measure
19: of a convex function. This geometric structure is well-known in the community of
20: complex geometers, see, e.g., Donaldson [13] for a discussion from the perspective
21: of Kähler geometry.
22:    Our motivation stems from the Kannan-Lovasź-Simonovits conjecture [17, Sec-
23: tion 5], which is concerned with the isoperimetric problem for high-dimensional
24: convex bodies. Essentially, our idea is to replace the standard Euclidean metric by
25: a special Riemannian metric on the given convex body K. This Riemannian metric
26: has many favorable properties, such as a Poincaré inequality with constant one, a
27: positive Ricci tensor, the linear functions are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian, etc.
28: Perhaps this alternative geometry does not deviate too much from the standard Eu-
29: clidean geometry on K, and it is conceivable that the study of this Riemannian
30: metric will turn out to be relevant to the Kannan-Lovasź-Simonovits conjecture.

56:      An absolutely-continuous probability measure on Rn is called log-concave if
57:  it is supported on an open, convex set K ⊂ Rn , and its density takes the form
58:  exp(−ρ) where the function ρ : K → R is convex. An important example of a log-
59:  concave measure is the uniform probability measure on a convex body in Rn . Here
60:  we assume that µ is log-concave and furthermore, we require that the following
61:  conditions are met:
62: (2) The convex set K ⊂ Rn is bounded, the function ρ is C ∞ -smooth, and ρ and its
63:     derivatives of all orders are bounded in K.
64:  Under these regularity assumptions, we can assert that
65: (3) The convex function ψ is finite and C ∞ -smooth in the entire Rn .
66:  The validity of (3) under the assumption (2) was proven by Wang and Zhu [29] and
67:  by Donaldson [13] via the continuity method. Berman and Berndtsson [5] explained
68:  how to deduce (3) from (2) by using Caffarelli’s regularity theory [9]. In fact, the
69:  argument in [5] requires only the boundness of ρ, and not of its derivatives, see also
70:  the Appendix in Alesker, Dar and Milman [2]. Since the function ψ is smooth, it
71:  follows from (1) that the transport equation
