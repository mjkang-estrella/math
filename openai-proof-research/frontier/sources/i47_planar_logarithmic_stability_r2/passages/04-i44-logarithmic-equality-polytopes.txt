492: ## 6. Constructive pivot: a cubic obstruction to quantitative equality
493: 
494: A genuine remaining target is a uniform power estimate on a ball-bounded class, allowing arbitrarily many facets and arbitrary bodies. Fix n≥2, 0<r<R and 0<τ<1/2. Let 𝓔 be the set of all origin-symmetric full-dimensional equality pairs described by E, and define
495: 
496: \[
497:  d_{\mathcal E}(K,L)=\inf_{(K',L')\in\mathcal E}
498:        \max\{d_H(K,K'),d_H(L,L')\}.
499: \]
500: 
501: One can ask for α<∞ and c>0 depending only on n,r,R,τ such that Δ_λ(K,L)≥c d_𝓔(K,L)^α whenever rB⊂K,L⊂RB and λ∈[τ,1−τ]. No such finite exponent is proved here. Compactness plus the equality classification gives only a qualitative modulus: otherwise a convergent sequence of pairs with deficit→0 and distance bounded below would limit to an equality pair. Continuity follows from uniform positive supports and Wulff inclusions; distance to any set is 1-Lipschitz. This is a standard technical consequence, not a finding.

503: **Proposition Q (quadratic rigidity fails already in dimension two).** There are symmetric six-facet pairs K_t,L_t, contained between fixed balls, for which
504: 
505: \[
506:  d_{\mathcal E}(K_t,L_t)\asymp t,\qquad
507:  \Delta_{1/2}(K_t,L_t)=\tfrac12t^3+O(t^4),\qquad t\downarrow0.       \tag{6.1}
508: \]
509: 
510: Thus any estimate of the indicated form for a class containing these pairs must have α≥3. This is a quantitative obstruction, not an E counterexample and not a substantial finding under the brief.
511: 
512: **Proof.** Define P_δ(s) by |x|≤e^s, |y|≤e^{−s}, |x+y|≤2−δ. Set δ=t, K_t=P_t(−t), L_t=P_t(t). For all sufficiently small t>0 these are genuine hexagons, tending to the square. All three paired facets remain true along −t≤s≤t, so in dimension two the normal fan is constant. On each cone between adjacent normals a support is the same positive linear combination of its two facet widths. Hölder proves that the finite-facet midpoint body satisfies every full interpolated support constraint. The reverse inclusion uses the facet directions. Hence M_{1/2}=P_t(0). Put d=t+2cosh t−2. The endpoint areas are 4−d², and the midpoint area is 4−t². Therefore
513: 
514: \[
515:  \Delta_{1/2}=\log\frac{4-t^2}{4-(t+2\cosh t-2)^2}
516:              =\tfrac12t^3+O(t^4).
517: \]
518: 
519: The endpoints lie O(t) from the common square, itself an equality pair, giving the upper distance bound. In the plane every pair in 𝓔 is either homothetic or a pair of parallelograms. For a homothetic pair, the determinant of the two width vectors (h(e₁),h(e₂)) is zero. Our endpoint determinant is e^{−2t}−e^{2t}=−4t+O(t³). On bounded width vectors this determinant is Lipschitz. A pair within Hausdorff η of K_t,L_t must therefore have η≥c₁t if homothetic; far-away comparison pairs already satisfy this bound.

Consequently the three vectors v=(1,1/2), w=(1/2,1), v+w=(3/2,3/2) lie in one vertex normal cone of every such parallelogram Q, and
