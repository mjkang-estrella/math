Abstract
We show that for log-concave real random variables with fixed variance the Shannon
differential entropy is minimized for an exponential random variable, answering a 2010
question of Bobkov and Madiman [1]. This gives a sharp reversal of the celebrated
entropy maximization theorem due to Boltzmann, in the log-concave case. We apply
this result to derive upper bounds on capacities of additive noise channels with log-
concave noise. We also improve constants in the reverse entropy power inequalities
for log-concave random variables.

Keywords entropy · variance · log-concave random variables

Mathematics Subject Classification 94A17 · 60E15

5 Concluding Remark

Let us mention that the n-dimensional version of this problem, lower bounds on the
entropy among all log-concave random vectors with a fixed covariance structure has a
well known connection to Bourgain’s slicing problem, a longstanding open question in
convex geometry, which was resolved2 by Klartag and Lehec [18], using new bounds,
due to Guan [19], on the average trace of an isotropic random vector’s covariance
matrix as it undergoes “Stochastic Localization”, see [20]. See Bizeul [21] for another
proof and [22–24] for background. For a log-concave random variable X in Rn , its

see [17], known to imply the slicing conjecture.

The resolution of Bourgain’s slicing problem gives upper bound on L X independent
of dimension. In this formulation, Theorem 1.1 says that when n = 1, L X ≤ 1e with
equality when X is an exponential distribution. This gives an affirmative resolution,
in dimension n = 1 case, of the so-called “strong slicing conjecture”, see Fradelizi
and Sola [26], that the isotropic constant of a log-concave random variable in Rn
attains its maximum at the product of exponential distributions. Building on the work
of Klartag [27], [26] shows that the strong slicing conjecture implies the functional
version of the Mahler conjecture. Here, the Mahler conjecture is the longstanding
conjecture in convex geometry that the volume product of a convex body and its polar
is minimized for the simplex, see [28] for further background. Unfortunately, the tools
developed here are not tractable outside of dimension one and do not seem useful
toward bounding L X in general.
