1: \section{Density of the even tests}\label{sec:density}
2: 
3: The Hessian identity identifies affine functions as the possible
4: obstructions to density.  We now show that these are the only
5: obstructions, using cutoffs in the $x$ coordinates.
6: 
7: \begin{lemma}[Density of even tests]\label{lem:density}
8: Let $d\mu=\rho(x)\,dx$ be a probability measure on $\R^n$ with a smooth,
9: strictly positive, even density.  Let $\tau$ be a smooth even symmetric
10: matrix field with $0<\tau(x)\le\Lambda I$ for some finite $\Lambda$, and set
11: \[
12:  Af=x\cdot\nabla f-\tau:D^2f.
13: \]
14: Suppose that $A$ has the integration formula
15: \[
16:  \E[hAf]=\E[\nabla h\cdot\tau\nabla f]
17:  \qquad(f,h\in C_c^\infty(\R^n))
18: \]
19: and satisfies the compact Hessian identity~\eqref{eq:hessian}.
20: Then
21: \[
22:  \overline{\{A(A-1)f:f\in C_c^\infty(\R^n),\ f\text{ even}\}}^{\,L^2(\mu)}
23:  =\{g\in L^2(\mu):g\text{ even},\ \E g=0\}.
24: \]
25: \end{lemma}
26: 
27: \begin{proof}
28: Write $\Gamma(f,h)=\nabla f\cdot\tau\nabla h$ and
29: $\Gamma(f)=\Gamma(f,f)$.  The integration formula also holds when one
30: factor is smooth and the other has compact support, by inserting a
31: cutoff around that support.  In particular $\E Ak=0$ for compactly
32: supported smooth $k$.  Since $A$ preserves parity, the displayed range
33: consists of centered even functions.
34: 
35: Let $g$ be a centered even $L^2(\mu)$ function orthogonal to this range.
36: It also annihilates $A(A-1)f$ for odd $f$, because that image is odd.
37: Formal symmetry therefore gives
38: \[
39:  \mathcal P g=0\quad\text{in distributions},\qquad \mathcal P=A(A-1).
40: \]
41: Indeed, if $\mathcal P^t$ denotes the transpose with respect to
42: Lebesgue measure and $d\mu=\rho\,dx$, weighted formal symmetry gives
43: $\mathcal P^t(\rho g)=\rho\mathcal P g$ in distributions.
44: Orthogonality makes the left side zero, and smooth positivity of
45: $\rho$ gives the displayed equation.  The principal symbol of $\mathcal P$ is
46: $(\xi\cdot\tau(x)\xi)^2$, which is positive for every $\xi\ne0$.
47: Interior elliptic regularity consequently gives $g\in C^\infty$
48: \cite[Theorem~14.2]{Dyatlov2026}.  Only positivity on compact sets is
49: needed here.
50: 
51: \smallskip
52: \noindent\emph{Splitting the equation in $L^2$.}
53: Choose $0\le\eta_r\le1$ smooth, equal to one on $B_r$, zero outside
54: $B_{2r}$, with $|\nabla\eta_r|\le C/r$ and $|D^2\eta_r|\le C/r^2$,
55: where $r\ge1$.  The upper bound on $\tau$ gives constants $B,K$,
56: independent of $r$, such that
57: \begin{equation}\label{eq:density-cutoff}
58:  \Gamma(\eta_r)^{1/2}\le B/r,
59:  \qquad |A\eta_r|\le K.
60: \end{equation}
61: For the second estimate, use $|x|\le2r$ on the support of the cutoff
62: derivatives.  Those derivatives, including $A\eta_r$, vanish outside
63: $B_{2r}\setminus B_r$.
64: 
65: Put $v=Ag$, initially just a smooth function, and write
66: \[
67:  G=\|g\|_2,\qquad
68:  X_r=\|\eta_r^2v\|_2,\qquad
69:  Y_r^2=\E[\eta_r^2\Gamma(g)].
70: \]
71: Here $G$ is finite by hypothesis, while $X_r,Y_r$ are finite by compact
72: support.
73: Testing $\mathcal P g=0$ against $\eta_r^4g$ gives
74: $\E[v(A-1)(\eta_r^4g)]=0$.
75: The product rule $A(ab)=aAb+bAa-2\Gamma(a,b)$ yields the exact expansion
76: \[
77:  \begin{split}
78:  (A-1)(\eta_r^4g)
79:  ={}&\eta_r^4v
80:  +g\bigl(4\eta_r^3A\eta_r-12\eta_r^2\Gamma(\eta_r)-\eta_r^4\bigr)\\
81:  &-8\eta_r^3\Gamma(\eta_r,g).
82:  \end{split}
83: \]
84: Thus Cauchy--Schwarz and~\eqref{eq:density-cutoff} imply
85: \begin{equation}\label{eq:density-two-estimates}
86:  X_r\le aG+\frac{8B}{r}Y_r,
87:  \qquad
88:  Y_r^2\le GX_r+\frac{2B}{r}GY_r,
89:  \qquad a=1+4K+12B^2.
90: \end{equation}
91: The first inequality is immediate if $X_r=0$; otherwise divide the
92: resulting estimate for $X_r^2$ by $X_r$.  For the second, integrate by
93: parts in $\E[\eta_r^2gAg]$:
94: \[
95:  Y_r^2=\E[\eta_r^2gv]-2\E[\eta_rg\Gamma(\eta_r,g)].
96: \]
97: Combining~\eqref{eq:density-two-estimates} gives
98: $Y_r^2\le aG^2+10BGY_r$, so both $Y_r$ and $X_r$ are bounded uniformly
99: in $r$.  Exhaustion by balls proves $v=Ag\in L^2(\mu)$.  We may now set
100: \[
101:  w=g-v\in L^2(\mu),\qquad Aw=0,\qquad Av=v.
102: \]
103: 
104: \smallskip
105: \noindent\emph{The harmonic part is constant.}
106: Testing $Aw=0$ with $\eta_r^2w$ and using
107: \eqref{eq:density-cutoff} gives
108: \[
109:  \bigl(\E[\eta_r^2\Gamma(w)]\bigr)^{1/2}
110:  \le\frac{2B}{r}\|w\|_2.
111: \]
112: On any fixed ball, let $r\to\infty$.  Then $\Gamma(w)=0$ there;
113: strict positivity of $\tau$ shows that $w$ is constant.
114: 
115: \smallskip
116: \noindent\emph{The eigenvalue-one part is linear.}
117: The analogous test of $Av=v$ gives, with
118: $Z_r^2=\E[\eta_r^2\Gamma(v)]$,
119: \[
120:  Z_r^2\le\|v\|_2^2+\frac{2B}{r}\|v\|_2Z_r.
121: \]
122: Hence $\E\Gamma(v)<\infty$.  For $f_r=\eta_rv\in C_c^\infty$,
123: the product rule now proves directly that
124: \begin{equation}\label{eq:density-approximation}
125:  f_r\longrightarrow v,\qquad Af_r\longrightarrow v
126:  \quad\text{in }L^2(\mu).
127: \end{equation}
128: Indeed,
129: \[
130:  Af_r=\eta_rv+vA\eta_r-2\Gamma(\eta_r,v).
131: \]
132: The middle term has norm at most
133: $K\|v\mathbf{1}_{B_{2r}\setminus B_r}\|_2\to0$.  For the last term, use
134: \[
135:  \|\Gamma(\eta_r,v)\|_2
136:  \le\frac{B}{r}(\E\Gamma(v))^{1/2}\longrightarrow0.
137: \]
138: Apply~\eqref{eq:hessian} to these compactly supported functions:
139: \[
140:  \E\bigl[\|\tau^{1/2}D^2f_r\,\tau^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\bigr]
141:  =\|Af_r\|_2^2-\E[f_rAf_r]\longrightarrow0.
142: \]
143: On each fixed ball, $f_r=v$ for all sufficiently large $r$.  Positivity
144: of the density and of $\tau$ therefore forces $D^2v=0$ on that ball.
145: Thus $v$ is affine, and $Av=v$ removes its constant term.
146: 
147: We have proved that every $L^2$ distributional solution of $\mathcal P g=0$ is
148: affine.  Our $g=w+v$ is even, so it is constant, and its mean is zero.
149: Hence $g=0$.  Taking the orthogonal complement in the centered even
150: subspace proves the lemma.
151: \end{proof}
