### S119: combining candidate membership and sampling before a K bound

Continue to use P's valid dominated hybrid, with test rate `s≤c_-=t/[K(1-t)+t]`, `0<t≤1/4`, positive integer κ≥4 and η=2^-12. Define the ω-weighted safe statistic `σ_i^ω=Σ_(e safely sampled in i)ω_e`. Weights ω are fixed conditional on W,H,Dmask. Apply the source per-label squared-exit argument and fair parity; the weighted baseline loss is at most `|Dmask∩U_i|/κ` because ω≤1 and Y is independent. Thus

`E[σ_i^ω|W,H]≥(η/4)Σ_(e∈Y_i)ω_e d_e - n_i/κ`,

where `d_e=P(Dmask_e=1|W,H_e=0)=r_e/(4B_e)`.

Pointwise, weighted safe labels spanned by the rank witnesses have total weight at most their **unweighted** count, bounded by κz_i; the others lie in the residual. In the high-rank branch `σ_i^ω≤n_i<κz_i`, and on the unlisted branch both vanish. W119 therefore pays the complete focal transfer:

`κ E[z_i|W,H]≥(η/4)Σ_(e∈Y_i)ω_e d_e - [(1000 logκ+2)/κ] n_i`. (SR)

The extra 1 from W119 was bounded by n_i/κ. No weighted density optimizer or new independence claim is hidden in (SR).

There is also an exact one-loss cancellation for the **positive term**, before excluding small groups. For fixed W and any fixed nonnegative coefficients `v_e` supported on `I*(W)`, sum over H and Dmask:

`E[Σ_(e∈I*(W)\H) v_e ω_e 1_(Dmask_e=1)|W] = Σ_(e∈I*(W)) v_e a r_e/(8 B_e Z_e)`. (SC)

Indeed `P(H=0,Dmask=1|W)=r/(8Z)`, while ω=a/B. This combines the two events instead of separately using their lower bounds.

For `r∈[1/K,K]`,

`BZ/r=a²/(2r)+[a(1-a/2)+a(1-a)/2]+(1-a)(1-a/2)r≤K`.

The three nonnegative coefficients sum to 1 (substitute r=1), and each of `1/r,1,r` is at most K. Hence the right side of (SC) is at least `(a/(8K))Σv_e`. Taking v_e equal to rounded weights gives at least `a OPT_M(u)/(8K)`, and the η/4 safe coefficient gives `ηa OPT_M(u)/(32K)` before baseline, residual and small-group deductions. This is genuinely a combined single K loss for the leading safe mass. It does not require evaluating r. Both directions of domination are used to bound r and 1/r.

The source operations needed for (SR)/(SC) have all been checked: predictable reverse queries retain the heterogeneous guard rates; weighted summation is taken only after each valid per-label inequality; the same T can be reused across endpoint analyses by linearity; the focal residual family is fixed before focal masks and includes all listing outcomes. These deductions leave the pathwise algorithm unchanged.

### Exact remaining costs: no constant tuning claim

The all-order statistic still satisfies, for each true group,

`E λ_i≥s E(z_i-c_i)`,

`c_i≤|Cguard∩U_i|+Σ_(l>i)(|Cguard∩U_l|+|Dmask∩U_l|/κ)`.

These are P's complete deterministic telescope and focal-test thinning, not new assumptions. Combining them with (SR), geometric level weights b_i, and the all-order bound yields the explicit lower bound

`E min_π u(A_main) ≥ (s/(2κ)) { (η/4) EΣ_(i large) b_i Σ_(e∈Y_i)ω_e d_e - [(1000 logκ+2)/κ] EΣ_(i large)b_i n_i - κ EΣ_(i large)b_i c_i }`. (BUD)

Here “large” means n_i≥κ; unlisted statistics are zero as in the source. (SC) controls the positive term only when all optimum labels are included. Small-group loss and the maximum branch still need their original separate accounting. The negative cardinality and path costs are not ω-weighted merely because the residual is. Finally `s≤c_-` is still roughly t/K, and rank conversion still divides by κ. Thus a one-loss leading safe count in (SC) is **not** a one-loss payoff theorem.

To make the remaining hybrid price explicit, try s=t. Fix W,H,Dmask and one ordinary reverse transition, freeze its input state, and then condition on its complete transcript τ. Let J(τ) be its departing coordinates. The fresh ordinary guard vector has rates c_j. The hybrid replaces rates on J by t and keeps survivor rates c_j. Exit from the focal flat is a decreasing Boolean event E. Put `δ_j=(t-c_j)_+` on J and zero elsewhere, and `q_j(z)=c_j+zδ_j` for `0≤z≤1`. Increasing only the positive differences dominates the actual hybrid, so its exit probability h_τ satisfies

`h_τ≥p_τ-Δ_τ`,

`Δ_τ=∫_0^1 Σ_(j∈J) δ_j P_(q(z),-j)(j is pivotal for E) dz`. (PIV)

Here p_τ denotes the pre-step ordinary exit probability evaluated at the frozen input; it is constant as that complete transcript varies over one pre-step cylinder. To prove (PIV), the probability of a finite Boolean event under product rates is a multiaffine polynomial. Differentiating in one rate gives minus the pivotal probability for a decreasing event. Integrate the polynomial derivative along q(z); decreasing any other rates from c to the actual smaller t can only increase exit probability. No independence of the ordinary and hybrid outputs is asserted.

Multiplying by the actual ordinary-exit indicator and taking expectations gives a lower bound on joint exits of `E[1_exit p_τ]-E[1_exit Δ_τ]`. The first term is the squared hazard mass from P; the second is an explicit **pivotal overload**. With the usual birth/parity and sampled-optimum weights, define b(e) to be e’s rounded weight (not its birth endpoint). The aggregate extra loss is

`Ξ=Σ_e Σ_b E[ b(e) ω_e 1_(e∈Dmask∩Y) 1_(ordinary exit of e at b) Δ_(e,b,τ) ]`, (XI)

with only valid endpoints included and parity charged in the same way as the positive term. One may omit parity indicators for a conservative larger Ξ. For coordinates with H=Dmask=0, `c_j=tr_j/(1-t+tr_j)`; for the others c_j=t. Both-sided domination guarantees c_j>0 and bounds its odds but does not by itself bound the sum of pivotals of an adaptively chosen transition. The exact unpaid safety estimate is that Ξ, together with the baseline/small-group deductions, consume at most a fixed fraction of the `ηa/(32K) EOPT_M(u)` positive mass **while** the residual and path terms in (BUD) and the factor 1/κ are paid without another K. A coordinatewise union bound `Δ≤Σδ_j` has no rank or welfare budget and cannot supply this estimate.

This is a calculated residual obstruction, not a proposed lemma counted as progress. (W119), (SR), (SC), (BUD) and (PIV) are paid identities/inequalities. The required aggregate bound for (XI) and rank-to-payoff costs is unproved. Returning to c_± and tuning κ,B,t,s would return to A116c's uniform-rate loss; I do not do that.

### Adaptive exchange check

The ABHM stored-basis refresh rule is a second way to try avoiding blanket thinning. Its deterministic invariant `A⊆B(W)` remains valid for arbitrary values: never displace an accepted basis member, and store each accepted label's actual value. It uses O(n²) queries. Its exact distributional invariant, however, relies on more than feasibility.

Condition on a coordinate's distinct unordered endpoints low,high. Put `θ=P(sample is high|endpoints)=r(high)/(r(low)+r(high))`. Two-sided domination allows `θ/(1-θ)∈[K^-2,K²]`. Suppose the exchange is `B_1=B_0-f+e` with f already processed and, for the purpose of testing the strongest natural invariant, the conditional acceptance probability of f in state 0 is β. A feasible update cannot flip state 0 when f is accepted. Let it flip state 0 when f is rejected with probability A, and state 1 with probability B. Preserving the stored-bit marginal θ requires

`(1-θ)(1-β)A=θB`.

If the new processed basis member e is to have the same conditional acceptance probability β in state 1, its accepted mass must be θβ, so

`B=β`, `A=θβ/[(1-θ)(1-β)]`. (EX)

The extreme θ is attained by the normalized two-point laws with D(high)=1/(K+1), Q(high)=K/(K+1), and complementary low-atom masses; no inadmissible conditional rate is invented. For θ=β=1/2, this is exactly the source's A=1,B=1/2. For an unknown biased θ a constant β forces A to depend on θ; requiring A≤1 for all θ up to `K²/(1+K²)` gives `β≤1/(1+K²)`. This obstructs that fixed-thinning, marginal-preserving invariant, **not all adaptive exchange algorithms**. Weighted or nonstationary invariants could differ. Moreover, the full-data/full-seed adversary may select the prefix itself using unexposed orientations and coins, so even a repaired fixed-prefix invariant would still need a simultaneous-order guarantee. Neither (EX) nor conditional orientation odds is substituted for such a guarantee.

**Route II disposition.** The weighted residual and safe-mass cancellation improve the understanding of where losses arise, but do not implement unknown likelihood weights or close (BUD)/(XI). The route is parked at these exact quantities, rather than repeatedly tuning constants or asserting a balanced training law.
