## 3. Common scalar input: an already-paid short-distance gap

Let `b=sqrt(3)/2`, `a=b^(-1/2)`, and let A be the triangular lattice generated by `a(1,0)` and `a(1/2,sqrt(3)/2)`. It has covolume one. Fix alpha>0 and put `G(x)=exp(-pi alpha |x|^2)`. There is a real radial Schwartz function f with `q=G-f>=0`, `fhat>=0`, and

`E_A:=sum_(v in A, v!=0) G(v)=fhat(0)-f(0)`.

Moreover there is an explicit `c_alpha>0` such that

`q(x)>=c_alpha (1-b|x|^2)^2`, for `|x|<=a`.                         (G134)

This is extracted from A's **lower** operation, not inferred from mere optimality. In A's variables `s=b|x|^2`, `h=17/50`, `z=[pi(beta/b-h)]^(-1)`, and normalization `D_beta=Q1 z exp(1/z)`, the left half-cell at the first positive node 1 has both deleted-jet margins strictly greater than `.032`. Since the corresponding value and derivative jets match, both `G_beta-f_beta` and `fhat_beta` are bounded below on `0<=s<=1` by

`.03 exp(-pi h) (1-s)^2/D_beta`.

Here beta>=1, `Q1>.157`, and z>0. For alpha>=1 use beta=alpha. For alpha<1 use U's exact duality `G_alpha-f_alpha=beta fhat_beta`, beta=1/alpha, so multiply that constant by beta. The conservative rational `.03` is below the source margin; it is a deduction for a fresh theorem, not a repair of an original audit. Constants may depend on alpha; no useful uniformity at alpha=0 or infinity is claimed. We do not rerun or truth-audit the stipulated atomic certificate.

## 4. Route one: periodic excess to original local cells

**P134.** Let `T=R^2/Lambda` have area N, an integer, and shortest nonzero period at least `8a`. Let P be any N distinct sites. Define its periodic ordered energy, retaining nonzero images of a site itself, by

`E_T(P)=N^(-1) sum_(i,j,lambda; (i,j,lambda)!=(i,i,0)) G(p_i-p_j+lambda)`.

Put `Delta=E_T(P)-E_A`. Let H_a be the centered regular hexagon of inradius a/2, and let V_i be the Euclidean lift of the periodic Voronoi cell centered at p_i. A site is epsilon-bad if V_i is not a six-sided polygon, or

`inf_(R in SO(2)) d_H(V_i-p_i,R H_a)>epsilon a`.

For `0<epsilon<=1`, there is a finite `C_alpha`, independent of N, Lambda and P, such that

`#bad/N <= min(1, C_alpha epsilon^(-2) Delta^(1/3))`.             (P134)

The claim is local: different good cells may use different rotations. Compatible tori `Lambda=mA`, m>=8, are included. No displacement labelling or separation hypothesis on P is imposed.

**Exact residual and deletion.** Poisson summation gives, with `S(w)=sum_i exp(2pi i w.p_i)`,

`Delta=N^(-1)sum_off q(p_i-p_j+lambda)
       +N^(-2)sum_(w in Lambda*, w!=0) fhat(w)|S(w)|^2`.        (4.1)

All sums converge absolutely; the zero Fourier mode uses area=N. Thus Delta>=0. For `0<h<=1/2`, put `r=a sqrt(1-h)`. Delete every site incident to a pair at torus distance <r, and let K be the deletion count. The number of deleted vertices is at most the number of directed short-pair incidences. G134 and (4.1) give

`k:=K/N <= Delta/(c_alpha h^2)`.                               (4.2)

If every site is deleted, bounds below are read with their trivial cap one. Otherwise complete the survivors to a maximal r-separated set on T. Such a finite extension exists: disjoint radius-r/2 disks give an absolute finite cardinality bound, so successive insertions terminate. It has M sites, `M=N-K+J`, where J is the number added, and covering radius at most r. This is an existence construction, not an efficient implementation claim.

**The packing-to-cell lemma, with its losses.** Normalize a saturated Voronoi cell by r. It contains B_(1/2) and lies in B_1. Its neighbors lie within distance 2r. Disjoint radius-r/2 disks around those neighbors and its center fit in B_(5r/2), so each cell has at most 24 sides. The systole hypothesis makes all these comparisons Euclidean. Every cell has at least three sides. Euler's formula on the torus, including nongeneric vertices, gives `sum m_i<=6M`.

For real x>2 define `F(x)=x tan(pi/x)/4`, `F6=F(6)=sqrt(3)/2=1/a^2`, and `ell=F'(6)<0`. Moving each supporting line inward to distance 1/2 can only decrease area. If some sides disappear, the resulting polygon has fewer sides, which only strengthens the bound. For a tangential m-gon, its consecutive outward-normal half-gaps t_j sum to pi and its area is `(1/4)sum tan(t_j)`. Jensen therefore gives normalized area at least F(m). Also

`F''(x)=(pi/x)^2 sec^2(pi/x)tan(pi/x)/(2x)>0`.

Consequently, for each actual cell area A_i,

`d_i=A_i/r^2-F6-ell(m_i-6)>=0`,

`sum d_i <= B:=N/r^2-M/a^2
             <= (N/a^2)[h/(1-h)+k]`.                         (4.3)

The first inequality uses `ell<0` and `sum(m_i-6)<=0`. It also gives `M<=N/(1-h)`, hence

`J/N<=k+h/(1-h)`.                                            (4.4)

Set `nu=min_(3<=m<=24,m!=6)[F(m)-F6-ell(m-6)]>0`. At most B/nu cells are nonhexagonal.

For completeness, a uniform scalar-to-Hausdorff estimate for the hexagons is proved here, so no unconstructed compactness modulus is hidden. Let P0 be a hexagon containing B_(1/2), contained in B_1, with area `F6+gamma`. Let Q be obtained by moving its six supports to distance 1/2. If Q has at most five sides, then `gamma>=F(5)-F6`. Otherwise write its half-gaps t_j and `t0=pi/6`. Define

`c_t=min(tan(t0/2), [tan(t0/2)-tan(t0)-sec^2(t0)(t0/2-t0)]/t0^2)>0`.

For all `0<=t<pi/2`,

`tan t-tan t0-sec^2(t0)(t-t0)>=c_t(t-t0)^2`.                   (4.5)

On `[t0/2,pi/2)` this follows by integrating `tan''>=2tan(t0/2)` along the interval to t0; below t0/2 the convex tangent gap increases as t decreases and `(t-t0)^2<=t0^2`. Hence `sum(t_j-t0)^2<=4gamma/c_t`. Take

`gamma0=min((F(5)-F6)/2, c_t pi^2/2304, 1/4)`.

For gamma<=gamma0 every normal gap differs from pi/3 by at most pi/12. Align one normal with the regular hexagon. Cumulative angle errors and the vertex formula `(1/2)sec(t_j)` at the normal bisector give

`inf_R d_H(Q,R H_1)<=32 sqrt(gamma/c_t)`,

where H_1 has inradius 1/2. For example the cumulative normal error is at most `24sqrt(gamma/c_t)`, the bisector error at most `26sqrt(gamma/c_t)`, and the radius is at most `1/sqrt(2)` with derivative at most `1/sqrt(2)` on this range; these bounds imply the displayed constant.

Also `d_H(P0,Q)<=sqrt(2gamma)`. Indeed choose a supporting direction realizing the support-function difference d, a point x of P0 at height H, and the two points `±n_perp/2` in Q. The triangle they span has a cap above Q's support line of area `d^2/(2H)>=d^2/2`, since H<=1. This cap is in P0 and outside Q. For gamma>=gamma0 the crude distance two suffices. Thus the valid universal constant

`Cgeo=max(32/sqrt(c_t)+sqrt(2), 2/sqrt(gamma0))`

gives `inf_R d_H(P0,R H_1)<=Cgeo sqrt(gamma)` for every such hexagon. Among the saturated cells, the fraction failing the epsilon-r version is therefore at most

`(B/N)[nu^(-1)+Cgeo^2 epsilon^(-2)]`.                         (4.6)

**Return to the original sites and cells.** A removed or added point has at most 25 survivor centers within 2r, by the same disjoint-disk area bound. Mark those centers and the K deleted sites. An unmarked survivor's saturated cell has all its active neighboring sites among the original sites, and no removed point within 2r can cut it. Since the cell lies in B_r about its center, removed points farther away cannot cut it either. Its original and saturated cells therefore coincide. At most

`K+25(K+J) <= N[51k+25h/(1-h)]`                              (4.7)

original sites are lost in this conversion. This step is essential; stability of the modified packing alone would not prove P134 for the original cells.

Equations (4.2)–(4.7) give, for epsilon-r comparison,

`#bad/N <= C(1+epsilon^(-2))[h+Delta/(c_alpha h^2)]`.

Moreover `d_H(H_r,H_a)=(a-r)/sqrt(3)<=a h/sqrt(3)`. Choose h proportional to `(Delta/c_alpha)^(1/3)` and use epsilon/2 in (4.6) when h is small enough. If h exceeds epsilon/2 or 1/2, the claimed right side is at least one after increasing C_alpha, so the trivial cap applies. When Delta=0, let h decrease to zero at each fixed epsilon; the finite bad count must be zero. This proves P134. For a concrete common constant in P134 and B134, one may take

`C0=101+3a^(-2)[nu^(-1)+4Cgeo^2]`,

`C_alpha=2(C0+1)max(1,c_alpha^(-1/3))`.

Indeed for `h=(Delta/c_alpha)^(1/3)<=min(1/2,epsilon/2)`, the displayed charges are at most `C0 epsilon^(-2)(h+k)` and k<=h; outside that range this constant makes the bound trivial. No optimal exponent or coherent orientation theorem is claimed.

## 5. Route two: finite windows with an honest density and boundary budget

The periodic Fourier sum cannot be applied to an arbitrary clipped configuration without changing its energy. This route instead completes a positive-definite square against a larger uniform background; no periodization of the energy is used.

Let `Q_R=[-R,R]^2`, R>=10a, and let P consist of N>0 distinct points of Q_R, with **no** separation or occupancy bound. Choose any padding w>0 and write

`e_R=N^(-1)sum_(i!=j)G(p_i-p_j)`, `A_f=fhat(0)`,

`D=Q_(R+w)`, `V=4(R+w)^2`, `d=N/V`, `P_D=8(R+w)`,

`T_f(w)=integral_(|z|>=w)|f(z)| dz`, `M_f=integral |z||f(z)| dz`.

All these integrals are finite because f is Schwartz. Define the **observable budget**

`Gamma=e_R-E_A+A_f(1-d)+2d T_f(w)+d(P_D/V)M_f`.                (W134)

Then Gamma>=0 and

`N^(-1)sum_(i!=j)q(p_i-p_j)<=Gamma`.                          (5.1)

To prove this, let mu be counting measure on P and nu Lebesgue measure on D. With `Q_f(mu,nu)=integral f(x-y)dmu(x)dnu(y)`, Fourier positivity gives `Q_f(mu-dnu,mu-dnu)>=0`. Each point is distance at least w from D's complement, so `Q_f(mu,nu)>=N(A_f-T_f(w))`. The overlap formula and the elementary translation bound `|D|-|D intersect(D+z)|<=P_D |z|` give `Q_f(nu,nu)<=V A_f+P_D M_f`. Therefore

`Q_f(mu,mu)/N >= d A_f-2d T_f(w)-d(P_D/V)M_f`.

Subtract the diagonal f(0), add the off-diagonal q sum, and use `E_A=A_f-f(0)`. This proves (5.1). It remains valid for arbitrarily clustered input because the uniform tail bound is paid once per point, not once per pair. It does not identify centered disk density with local density.

Here is a finite-window cell consequence with every additional charge visible. Define

`D_R=|4(R+4a)^2/N-1|`,

`beta_R=#(P intersect (Q_R \ Q_(R-4a)))/N`.

For finite P use its full-plane Voronoi cells (boundary cells may be unbounded); if P is the clipping of a locally finite infinite configuration, use that configuration's full cells. With the same epsilon-bad convention as P134,

`#bad/N <= min(1, beta_R+C_alpha epsilon^(-2)[D_R+Gamma^(1/3)])`.
                                                                    (B134)

**Proof.** Delete endpoints of short pairs as before, with `k<=Gamma/(c_alpha h^2)`. Embed the survivors in the square torus of side `2R+8a`. Opposite copies of Q_R are separated by 8a, so this creates no new pair shorter than r. Saturate geometrically on that torus. Its area is `V_t=4(R+4a)^2`, and the same polygon proof now gives

`B/N<=a^(-2)[(t+h)/(1-h)+k]`,

`J/N<=(t+h)/(1-h)+k`, where `t=V_t/N-1`.

These upper bounds are nonnegative by their derivation; replacing t by D_R only enlarges them. The packing-to-cell lemma and the 25-neighbor modification charge are unchanged. Additionally discard the beta_R original centers within 4a of the window boundary. At any remaining unmarked center the saturated cell lies in B_r, all its determining original sites occur in the same Q_R, and exterior sites are too far to cut it. Thus it equals the original full-plane cell, including when there are arbitrary sites outside Q_R. This gives a bound `beta_R+C epsilon^(-2)[D_R+h+Gamma/(c_alpha h^2)]` before the same optimization and trivial-large-budget split used in P134. This proves B134.

B134 is a usable estimate for arbitrary finite windows, not a claim that its budget is always small. If an infinite configuration additionally has **centered-square** density one, its D_R and beta_R tend to zero. Taking w=sqrt(R) makes the two f-tail/perimeter charges tend to zero. If its square-window Gaussian energy approaches E_A only along a subsequence, B134 gives vanishing bad-cell proportion only along that subsequence. A mere centered-disk density hypothesis and liminf energy from U is not renamed this square-window statement. No rate, uniform-in-center local density, stationary Palm statement, or interchange of infinite-volume and expectation limits is inferred. Establishing a comparable conclusion under a different density/energy convention requires paying that passage.

The two routes share the elementary polygon lemma but pay different analytic interfaces. P134 uses an exact periodic Fourier residual with no boundary term. W134/B134 use a finite signed-measure square, measured density error, and explicit boundary mass before making a purely geometric auxiliary torus. Neither discards the original configuration's bad sites without charging them.

## 6. Constraint-respecting obstructions to stronger global rigidity

**Slow periodic waves.** On `T_m=R^2/(mA)`, m>=8, use exactly m² sites

`p_(j,k)=j e1+k e2+eta sin(2pi j/m)e`, `0<=j,k<m`,

where e is any fixed unit vector and `0<eta<a/8`. The density is exactly one and separation is at least `a-2eta`. Write u for the periodic displacement. For each nonzero lattice vector v, summing `u(x+v)-u(x)` over the period gives zero. Consequently the entire first Taylor term of the energy cancels **exactly**. The Gaussian Hessian satisfies

`||D^2G(z)|| <= (2pi alpha+4pi^2 alpha^2 |z|^2)exp(-pi alpha |z|^2)`.

If v=j e1+k e2, the dual-basis coordinate obeys `|j|<=2|v|/(sqrt(3)a)`. Hence

`|u(x+v)-u(x)|<=4pi eta |v|/(sqrt(3)a m)`.

Also this increment is at most 2eta, so along the Taylor segment `|z|>=3|v|/4`, `|z|<=5|v|/4`. Absolute summability then proves

`0<=Delta_m<=C_alpha eta^2/m^2`,                              (6.1)

where one valid constant is

`(8pi^2/(3a^2)) sum_(v in A\{0}) |v|^2
 [2pi alpha+(25/4)pi^2 alpha^2 |v|^2]exp(-(9/16)pi alpha |v|^2)`.

The lower bound follows from the nonnegative residual (4.1), not a numerical observation. The variance is exactly

`inf_t m^(-2)sum_(j,k)|u(j,k)-t|^2=eta^2/2`.                  (6.2)

Thus there is no period-independent bound, or vanishing modulus in the excess, for this **labelled displacement distance to one translation of A**. This does not assert a lower bound after arbitrary relabelling and rotation. Relative local strains are O(eta/m), consistent with P134 and with the primary first-shell/spectral mechanisms. A lower bound controlling those strains is not a bound controlling absolute displacement.

**Two infinite grains.** Fix `w>a`, a rotation R_theta not preserving the triangular lattice's six nearest-neighbor directions, and any translation t. Define

`C=(A intersect {x1<=-w}) union ((R_theta A+t) intersect {x1>=w})`.

This is simple and uniformly separated, with an empty strip of fixed width. Counting fundamental cells cut by a disk or a straight boundary gives `#(C intersect B_R)=pi R^2+O(R)`, so centered-disk density is exactly one in the limit. Its Gaussian ordered energy satisfies

`sum_(x!=y in C intersect B_R)G(x-y)=E_A #(C intersect B_R)+O_alpha,w(R+1)`.
                                                                    (6.3)

Here is the boundary estimate underlying (6.3). Uniform separation bounds the number of sites in any unit square by an absolute constant. The Gaussian interaction tail of a site at distance s from a boundary is bounded by `C_alpha exp(-c_alpha s^2)` after summing unit annuli. For either lattice, the number of sites in a unit-width band at distance j from the clipping circle or grain interface is `O(R+j+1)`. Multiplying by that tail and summing j gives O(R+1) for omitted interactions with the site's complete same-orientation lattice. Cross-grain interactions have the same bound: for each site their sum decays in its distance to the interface, and the band count is again O(R+j+1). The fixed empty strip changes counts by O(R). These estimates prove the stated ordered-pair normalization and the limit without exchanging a conditional infinite sum.

Consequently C has **exact limiting** Gaussian energy E_A and is not one lattice: it has a bi-infinite empty strip wider than a lattice cell and two macroscopically present different nearest-neighbor direction sets. The interface is a zero-density defect, while each bulk grain is locally crystalline. Even deleting a single lattice point preserves the limiting density and Gaussian energy, which already defeats literal defect exclusion. No claim like (6.3) is made for unscreened Coulomb/Riesz energies: their background, cutoff, and boundary costs require a separate analysis. Neither construction contradicts any stipulated minimum theorem.
