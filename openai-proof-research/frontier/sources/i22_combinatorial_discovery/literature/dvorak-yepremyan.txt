                                           1       Introduction

                                           Hadwiger’s conjecture famously states that every Kk -minor-free graph is (k − 1)-colorable. This is
                                           known to be true when k ≤ 6, as shown by Robertson, Seymour and Thomas [14]. The validity of
                                           the conjecture for general k is a widely open and extremely challenging problem. Let f (k) denote
                                           the maximum chromatic number of Kk -minor-free graphs, so Hadwidger’s conjecture is the claim
                                           that f (k) = k − 1. For more than 30 years, the best partial result towards the conjecture was that
                                           f (k) = O(k log1/2 k), which follows from the tight bounds on the average degree of Kk -minor-free
                                           graphs [11, 16]. This barrier was broken in 2019 by Norin, Postle and Song [12] who improved
                                           the bound to O(k log1/4+o(1) k). This started a series of improvements culminating in the current
                                           best bound of O(k log log k) by Delcourt and Postle [8]. A key element of these improvements is
                                           an understanding of the chromatic number of small Kk -minor-free graphs. Indeed, Delcourt and
                                           Postle [8] shows that f (k) = O(f ′ (k)), where f ′ (k) denotes the maximum chromatic number of
                                           Kk -minor-free graphs with O(k log4 k) vertices.

                                           The analogous problem has been also studied in the list coloring setting. A list assignment for a
                                           graph G is a function L that to each vertex v assigns a list L(v) of available colors, and an L-coloring
                                           of G is a proper coloring φ such that φ(v) ∈ L(v) for every v ∈ V (G). The minimum integer s such
                                           that G can be L-colored for every assigment L of lists of size at least s is called the list chromatic

maximum of the list chromatic numbers of Kk -minor-free graphs. Hadwiger’s conjecture is known
to be false in the list coloring setting; Steiner [15] proved that fl (k) ≥ (2 − o(1))k. On the other
hand, Linear Hadwiger’s Conjecture for list coloring proposes that fl (k) = O(k). The best partial
result is by Postle [13], who proved that fl (k) = O(k(log log k)6 ). Again, an important starting
point for the argument is a bound on the list chromatic number of small graphs.
Dvořák and Postle [9] introduced correspondence coloring (also known as DP-coloring), a further
generalization of list coloring by refining the constraints on coloring adjacent vertices; this notion
makes it possible to use certain inductive arguments not available in the list coloring setting. The
correspondence assignment for a graph G is a pair (L, M ), where L is a list assignment and M =
{Me : e ∈ E(G)} assigns a (not necessarily perfect) matching Me between {u}×L(u) and {v}×L(v)
to every edge e = uv ∈ E(G). We say that (L, M ) is an ℓ-correspondence assignment if L assigns
at least ℓ colors to each vertex. An (L, M )-coloring of G is any assignment φ of colors to vertices
of G such that φ(v) ∈ L(v) for every v ∈ V (G) and for every e = uv ∈ E(G), the vertices (u, φ(u))
and (v, φ(v)) are non-adjacent in Me . The correspondence chromatic number of G is the smallest
integer ℓ such that G has an (L, M )-coloring for every ℓ-correspondence assignment (L, M ).
The correspondence chromatic number has attracted a lot of attention. Among its interesting
properties, let us mention the following results by Bernshteyn [3, 4]: Every graph of average degree
d has correspondence chromatic number at least Ω(d/ log d), while every triangle-free graph of
maximum degree ∆ has correspondence chromatic number at most O(∆/ log ∆). Consequently,
every triangle-free d-regular graph has correspondence chromatic number Θ(d/ log d).
Let fc (k) denote the maximum correspondence chromatic number of Kk -minor-free graphs. Clearly
fc (k) ≥ fl (k), and thus Hadwiger’s conjecture does not hold in the correspondence chromatic num-
                                                                                         √
ber setting. However, unlike the list coloring case, the best known bound fc (k) = O(k log k) still
matches the maximum average degree. One of the reasons why the arguments from the list coloring
setting do not apply is that we do not know how to bound the correspondence chromatic number
of small graphs. Indeed, as Norin (personal communication) pointed out, nontrivial bounds are
not known even for dense random graphs; this is very relevant for the considerations of Hadwiger’s
conjecture, since the tight lower bounds on the average degree of Kk -minor-free graphs come exactly
from such random graphs.
As the main result of this note, we give an upper bound on the correspondence chromatic number of
Erdős-Rényi random graphs with fixed density of edges; the bound implies that Linear Hadwiger’s
conjecture for correspondence coloring holds asymptotially almost surely.

Theorem 1.1. For every fixed positive p ≤ 1, Erdős-Rényi random graph G(n, 1 − p) a.a.s. has
                                             √
correspondence chromatic number at most O(n/ log n).

The Hadwiger number h(G) of a graph G is the maximum k such that Kk is a minor of G. For
                                            √
fixed 0 < p < 1, we have h(G(n, p)) = Θ(n/ log n)) a.a.s., see [5]. Hence, Theorem 1.1 supports
Linear Hadwiger’s conjecture for correspondence coloring.

Corollary 1.2. For every fixed 0 ≤ p ≤ 1, Erdős-Rényi random graph G(n, p) a.a.s. has corre-

The best lower bound for Theorem 1.1 that we are aware of comes from the well-known fact that
for positive p ≤ 1, the random graph G(n, 1 − p) a.a.s. has independence number Ω(log n), and thus
χ(G(n, 1 − p)) = Ω(n/ log n). Let us remark that the list chromatic number of G(n, 1 − p) is known
to be O(n/ log n) a.a.s., see e.g. [1].
We have already mentioned the general lower bound of Bernshteyn [3], stating that every graph of av-
erage degree d has correspondence chromatic number at least Ω(d/ log d); this also gives Ω(n/ log n)
as a lower bound for the correspondence chromatic number of G(n, 1 − p) for any positive p < 1.
The lower bound of Bernshteyn comes from considering a random ℓ-correspondence assignment, an
assignment (L, M ) such that L(v) = {1, . . . , ℓ} for each vertex v and Me is chosen independently
uniformly at random among the perfect matchings between {u} × {1, . . . , ℓ} and {v} × {1, . . . , ℓ}
for each edge e = uv. It is natural to ask whether a better analysis would not give an improved
lower bound in the case the graph we color is also random. However, as our second result, we show
that this cannot be the case, even when we consider the coloring of complete graphs.

Theorem 1.3. There exists a function ℓ(n) = Θ(n/ log n) such that if (L, M ) is a random ℓ(n)-
correspondence assignment for the complete graph Kn , then Kn is a.a.s. (L, M )-colorable.

As both extreme cases of the correspondence assignment (matching the list coloring vs. completely
random) for G(n, 1 − p) only requre O(n/ log n) colors, we suspect that the upper bound given in
Theorem 1.1 can be improved.

Conjecture 1.4. For every fixed positive p ≤ 1, Erdős-Rényi random graph G(n, 1 − p) a.a.s. has
correspondence chromatic number Θ(n/ log n).

It is our hope that this conjecture will actually turn out to be false, as this would give another inter-
esting example of a difference of behavior between the list and correspondence chromatic numbers.
