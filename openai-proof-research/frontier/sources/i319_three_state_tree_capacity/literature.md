## Exact capacity and linear sufficiency precedents

**Evans, Kenyon, Peres, Schulman, _Broadcasting on trees and the Ising model_**, Ann. Appl. Probab. 10 (2000), 410–433. [Author PDF](https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~schulman/Papers/evansKPS00Ising.pdf); [alternate author copy](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~will/papers/noisytrees.pdf). Existing local rendered primary pages 3–4 were read because their text extraction was garbled. Theorem 1.2 assigns binary broadcast edge resistance `(1-theta^2) theta^(-2|e|)` and obtains a lower reconstruction bound through a linear estimator whose variance is electrical flow energy. This establishes prior art for the weighting and sufficiency mechanism. It is a binary result; it does not prove three-state nonlinear necessity. Fresh access to the first PDF timed out, so no new body was retained.

**Pemantle and Peres, _The critical Ising model on trees, concave recursions and nonlinear capacity_**, Ann. Probab. 38 (2010), 184–206. [Primary arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503137). Read the existing PDF-derived text, Sections 2–3 and relevant complete arguments in Sections 4–5. Section 2 defines worst-ray $L^p$ capacity, with exponent (p-1), and identifies $p=2$ with conductance. Theorem 2.1 treats free-boundary Ising with squared channel resistances; Theorem 2.2 treats plus-boundary Ising with $L^3$ capacity and unsquared resistances. Their stated interaction hypotheses bound channels away from the endpoints; Section 4 explains subdivision and the near-perfect obstruction. The free-boundary proof in Section 5 uses a scalar likelihood ratio, its odd symmetry, and a conditional expectation estimate in Lemmas 5.1–5.3. That scalar mechanism is not a three-state proof. Critical polynomial corrections to exponential growth can change capacity while preserving the branching-number boundary, so a branching number alone does not decide the present target at equality.

The new analysis supplies its own weighted flow/innovation proof for three states, including all positive variable/perfect edges. It claims no novelty for this spectral extension or the electrical formalism.

## Arbitrary-tree Potts results do not automatically give the target

**Gu and Polyanskiy, _Non-linear Log-Sobolev inequalities for the Potts semigroup and applications to reconstruction problems_**, Comm. Math. Phys. 404 (2023), 769–831. [Primary author paper](https://people.lids.mit.edu/yp/homepage/data/nlsi_potts.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05444). Existing primary text was read at Theorems 3, 5, 6 and the complete Section 4 general-tree argument. They obtain sharp one-channel nonlinear KL contraction and an arbitrary-tree nonreconstruction test using `br(T) eta_KL(PC_lambda,uniform)<1`. Their proof combines entropy subadditivity with channel contraction and extracts a bounded flow. It provides relevant general-tree Potts prior art; its theorem is not the requested exact weighted quadratic-capacity comparison.

The mismatch is substantive even for a fixed positive parameter: KL contraction on all posterior laws need not equal $\lambda^2$ when there are three states. For instance, for the near-uniform single-channel orbit with amplitude $t>0$, its KL information is $I(t)=t^2-t^3/3+O(t^4)$, from the exact expansion of the three likelihood coordinates. Therefore
\[
 I(\lambda t)/I(t)=\lambda^2[1+(1-\lambda)t/3+O(t^2)]>\lambda^2
\]
for sufficiently small $t>0$ and fixed $0<\lambda<1$. Thus a generic KL contraction bound cannot simply be assigned the squared-channel weight. This observation is an elementary expansion, not a new theorem or a refutation of a more specialized potential.

**Formentin and Külske, _A symmetric entropy bound on the non-reconstruction regime of Markov chains on Galton–Watson trees_**, Electron. Commun. Probab. 14 (2009), 587–596. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.2962). Read the theorem, Proposition 3.3/equation (15), and its proof using expected boundary relative entropies. Expected symmetrized entropy adds across independent branches, including distinct subtree experiments, while its one-channel contraction yields a sufficient nonreconstruction bound. The three-state corpus credits this exact recursion and adds a centered-log correction; neither attribution makes that correction uniformly subadditive for mixed positive subtree laws. Ordinary uniqueness and extremality/reconstruction must also be distinguished.

**Gu and Polyanskiy, _Uniqueness of BP fixed point for the Potts model and applications to community detection_**, COLT 2023. [Primary PMLR page and PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/gu23a.html), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14688). Read Lemmas 19–20, the discussion of general chi-squared non-subadditivity, and complete Appendix D.1. Lemma 20 gives almost-subadditivity for a weak symmetric experiment with a multiplicative **positive** error of order `epsilon^(1/5)`. It does not give a negative quadratic saving. Our exact diluted-exclusion examples illustrate why small average information by itself cannot supply that stronger assertion. General chi-squared non-subadditivity is known; the examples here are not advertised as a new literature discovery.

## Robust reconstruction and homogeneous results

**Janson and Mossel, _Robust reconstruction on trees is determined by the second eigenvalue_**, Ann. Probab. 32 (2004), 2630–2649. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0406447), [author PDF](https://www2.math.uu.se/~svantejs/papers/sj151.pdf). Read definitions, Theorems 1.2 and 3.3, Proposition 3.4, and the bounded-degree general-tree proof using antichains. These distinguish strong-noise/erasure robustness from ordinary boundary reconstruction. The principal inequalities are strict spectral/branching-number inequalities. Their local almost-linear control is insufficient to infer ordinary nonreconstruction or an exact capacity criterion at the critical boundary.

**Sly, _Reconstruction of symmetric Potts models_**, primary manuscript [arXiv:0811.1208](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.1208). Inspected the actual regular-tree model and threshold statements: the three-state sharp threshold for sufficiently large degree is homogeneous prior art. It is not an arbitrary-tree weighted capacity theorem.

**Mossel, Sly, Sohn, _Exact phase transitions for stochastic block models and reconstruction on trees_**, [primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03362). Inspected Assumptions 1.10–1.11, Theorem 1.12, and their explicit general-tree question. The theorem gives large-mean Galton–Watson sharpness for three/four states under offspring hypotheses; it does not pay a mixed-message arbitrary-tree criterion. The pinned family-229 source strengthens the homogeneous thresholds and is accepted as stipulated. Repeating those conclusions or the three-community SBM theorem is outside this round's contribution.

## Contribution decision

No target theorem is completed. The strongest unconditional arbitrary-tree conclusion is positive capacity sufficiency with its dimensionless lower bound; its mechanism is standard. The exact mixed identity and the small-support saving are technical partials. The actionable obstacle is proving a positive-channel/product-preserved three-state constraint that bounds the remainder in $U$, or replacing quadratic information by a potential with paid channel loss and converse comparison. No worldwide priority, publication readiness, or new accepted finding is asserted.
