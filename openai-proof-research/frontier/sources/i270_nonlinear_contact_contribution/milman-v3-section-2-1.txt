2.1 Base-Induced Isoperimetric Profile




Given a Borel subset E of a slab (M^{n}_{T},g), we denote for all t\in[0,T] by E_{t} the (Borel) vertical section




E_{t}:=\{y\in M_{0}^{n-1}\;;\;(t,y)\in E\}.



and for all y\in M_{0}^{n-1} by E^{y} the (Borel) horizontal section:




E^{y}:=\{t\in[0,T]\;;\;(t,y)\in E\}.



We assume that E is of locally finite perimeter in (M^{n}_{T},g), and set:




V_{E}(t):=V_{\mu_{0}}(E_{t})~,~A_{E}(t):=A_{\mu_{0}}(E_{t}).



In that case, it is known that V_{E}(t) is a function of bounded variation on [0,T] (see [4, Section 3]). We denote by DV_{E} its distributional derivative, and decompose it into its absolutely continuous and singular parts as follows:




DV_{E}=V^{\prime}_{E}(t)dt+D_{S}V_{E}.



It is also known that E_{t} is of locally finite perimeter in (M_{0}^{n-1},g_{0}) for almost all t\in[0,T], and that A_{E}(t) is a Borel function [4, Theorem 2.4]. The following is a known computation (see e.g. [18, Lemma 2.4], [4, Proposition 3.4], [16, Section 4]); for completeness, we sketch a proof omitting the technical details:




Proposition 2.5.



Let (M_{T},g,\mu_{T}) denote a weighted Riemannian slab, and let E be a set of locally finite perimeter in (M_{T},g). Then:




A_{\mu_{T}}(E)\geq\frac{1}{T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{V^{\prime}_{E}(t)^{2}+A_{E}(t)^{2}}dt+\left\|D_{S}V_{E}\right\|\right).

(2.2)


Equality holds if for every t,s\in[0,T], either E_{t}\subset E_{s} or E_{s}\subset E_{t}, and for almost every t\in[0,T], \left\langle\mathfrak{n}_{E}(t,y),e_{1}\right\rangle is constant for \mathcal{H}^{n-2}-almost all y\in\partial^{*}E_{t}.





Here \left\|D_{S}V_{E}\right\| denotes the total-variation norm of the singular measure D_{S}V_{E}.




Sketch of Proof.



We assume for simplicity that E has smooth boundary. The perimeter of the vertical part of the boundary (where \left\langle\mathfrak{n}_{E}(x),e_{1}\right\rangle=1) is lower-bounded by \left\|D_{S}V_{E}\right\| (times the horizontal density \frac{1}{T}) since \mu_{0}(E_{t+}\Delta E_{t-})\geq\left|\mu_{0}(E_{t+})-\mu_{0}(E_{t-})\right|, and equality holds if E_{t+}\subset E_{t-} or E_{t-}\subset E_{t+}. Once the vertical part is taken care of, we may assume V_{E} is absolutely continuous and calculate by the co-area formula:




A_{\mu_{T}}(E)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(\partial^{*}E)_{t}}\frac{\Psi_{\mu_{0}}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(y)}{\left|\sin\alpha(t,y)\right|}dt,



where \alpha(t,y) is the angle between the normal \mathfrak{n}_{E}(t,y)
and the horizontal direction e_{1}, so that \cos\alpha(t,y)=\left\langle\mathfrak{n}_{E}(t,y),e_{1}\right\rangle.
On the other hand, the rate of vertical change in E_{t} per horizontal movement is given by \cot\alpha(t,y),
and so




V^{\prime}_{E}(t)=\int_{(\partial^{*}E)_{t}}\cot(\alpha(t,y))\Psi_{\mu_{0}}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(y).



Since 1/|\sin\alpha|=\Phi(\cot\alpha) for \Phi(z)=\sqrt{1+z^{2}} which is convex, we may apply Jensen’s inequality to obtain:




\displaystyle A_{\mu_{T}}(E)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{(\partial^{*}E)_{t}}\Phi(\cot\alpha(t,y))\Psi_{\mu_{0}}(y)d\mathcal{H}^{n-2}(y)




\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}A_{E}(t)\Phi(V^{\prime}_{E}(t)/A_{E}(t))dt




\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{V^{\prime}_{E}(t)^{2}+A_{E}(t)^{2}}dt,



with equality if for almost every t\in[0,T], \alpha(t,y) is constant for \mathcal{H}^{n-2}-almost every y\in(\partial^{*}E)_{t} (which for almost all t coincides \mathcal{H}^{n-2}-almost-everywhere with \partial^{*}E_{t} by [4, Theorem 2.4]).
∎





Since A_{E}(t)\geq\mathcal{I}_{0}(V_{E}(t)) and \frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}V_{E}(t)dt=V(E), we are naturally led to the following:




Definition 2.6 (Base-Induced Isoperimetric Profile).



The base-induced isoperimetric profile \mathcal{I}^{b}_{T}=\mathcal{I}^{b}_{T}(\mathcal{I}_{0}) is defined as:




\mathcal{I}^{b}_{T}(\bar{v}):=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{T}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\sqrt{v^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{0}(v(t))^{2}}dt+\left\|D_{S}v\right\|\right)\;;\;\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}v(t)dt=\bar{v}\right\},

(2.3)


where the infimum is over all functions v:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,1] of bounded variation.





In fact, whenever \mathcal{I}_{0} is continuous, a standard compactness argument for BV functions ensures that the above infimum is always attained.
Note that by testing constant and heavyside functions, we trivially have \mathcal{I}^{b}_{T}\leq\min(\mathcal{I}_{0},\frac{1}{T}).
Moreover, an immediate consequence of Proposition 2.5 is:




Corollary 2.7.



For every weighted Riemannian slab, \mathcal{I}_{T}\geq\mathcal{I}^{b}_{T}.





Unfortunately, in general, the converse inequality need not hold, due to the fact that isoperimetric minimizers on the base which realize \mathcal{I}_{0}(\bar{v}) may not be nested as a function of \bar{v}, creating some extra vertical perimeter, or more generally, not satisfy equality in (2.2). However, we can trivially state:




Corollary 2.8 (Nested and Aligned Base Minimizers).



Assume that there exists a nested family of isoperimetric minimizers for the base (M_{0},g_{0},\mu_{0}), namely a mapping [0,1]\ni\bar{v}\mapsto E_{0}(\bar{v}) of Borel subsets of (M_{0},g_{0}) so that A_{\mu_{0}}(E_{0}(\bar{v}))=\mathcal{I}_{0}(\bar{v}) and




\bar{v}_{1}\leq\bar{v}_{2}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;E_{0}(\bar{v}_{1})\subset E_{0}(\bar{v}_{2}).



Consider the set E^{1} in the slab (M_{1},g,\mu_{1}) of width 1 whose sections are given by E^{1}_{\bar{v}}:=E_{0}(\bar{v}), and assume that E^{1} is a Borel set of locally finite perimeter and that for all \bar{v}\in(0,1), \left\langle\mathfrak{n}_{E^{1}}(\bar{v},y),e_{1}\right\rangle is constant for almost all y\in\partial^{*}E^{1}_{\bar{v}}. 
Then \mathcal{I}_{T}=\mathcal{I}^{b}_{T} for any slab with base (M_{0},g_{0},\mu_{0}) (and width T>0).





Note that the above assumption is satisfied for a Gaussian base (\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\left|\cdot\right|^{2},\gamma^{n-1}), but not when the base is a 2-dimensional Torus (or square), since the minimizers cannot be nested in the latter case.




Proof of Corollary 2.8.



Given a function v:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,1] of bounded variation, consider the Borel set E\subset(M_{T},g) with sections E_{t}=E_{0}(v(t)). By the definition of \mathcal{I}^{b}_{T}, it is enough to show that equality holds in (2.2), and so it is enough to show that the sufficient condition for equality given by Proposition 2.5 is satisfied. Indeed, the nestedness assumption guarantees the first part of the condition, and the second part (which need only hold outside the null-set of t’s where v is non-differentiable) holds since to first order around a given slice E_{t}, E coincides with E^{1} around the slice E^{1}_{v(t)} (after stretching E^{1} appropriately to match the value of v^{\prime}(t)).
∎





Remark 2.9.



The reader will note the similarity with the celebrated reformulation by Bobkov [11] of the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality (say on \mathbb{R}) in the following functional form:




\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{v^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}(v(t))^{2}}d\gamma^{1}(t)\geq\mathcal{I}_{\gamma}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)d\gamma^{1}(t)\right),

(2.4)


valid for all (say) locally Lipschitz functions v:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1].
