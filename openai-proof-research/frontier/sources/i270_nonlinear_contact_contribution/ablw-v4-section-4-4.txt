4.4 Second order limit




To obtain the Second Gamma Limit, Theorem 4.5, and the more detailed description of limiting configurations, we must analyze the remainder term in (4.9). As the analysis in the case of strict triangle inequalities (1.5) (leading to minimizers with double bubbles) or in case \beta_{0}=0 (yielding only single bubbles) is essentially identical to the unweighted case of our previous paper [1], we will concentrate on the case \beta_{1}=0, in which core shells are favored.




We subtract the first \Gamma-limit from E_{\eta}, which (from the decomposition (4.8), (4.9)) we expect to have scale |\log\eta|^{-1}.
For v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}, let




\displaystyle F_{\eta}(v_{\eta}):=|\log\eta|\left[E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})-\overline{e_{0}}(M)\right].

(4.19)


For the second \Gamma-limit we consider v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta} for which F_{\eta}(v_{\eta}) is bounded. For these v_{\eta}, Proposition 4.3 applies, and we may thus assume that (along subsequences) the detailed concentration structure described by (4.13), (4.14), (4.15) is observed. Given Lemma 2.6, each limiting cluster A^{k}=(A^{k}_{1},A^{k}_{2}) is either a core shell \mathcal{C}^{m^{k}_{1}}_{m^{k}_{2}} (if m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}>0,) or a single bubble (in case one of m_{i}^{k}=0.) We recall that minimization of \mathcal{G}_{\sigma} does not determine the centers of core shells; this will be addressed below.




Next we define the second order limit function, starting with its domain, which is suggested from the first order limit Theorem 4.2 and the structure described in Proposition 4.3.
For K\in\mathbb{N}, m_{1}^{k}\geq 0, m_{2}^{k}\geq 0 and m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k}>0, the sequence K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}) is defined by




\displaystyle(K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}))^{k}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}),&1\leq k\leq K,\\
&(0,0),&K+1\leq k<\infty.\end{aligned}\right.



Let {\cal{M}}_{M} be the set of optimal sequences made of all clusters for the problem (4.7):




\displaystyle{\cal{M}}_{M}:=\Bigg\{K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}):K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})\text{ minimizes \eqref{mine0} for }M_{i},i=1,2,



\displaystyle\text{ and }\ \overline{e_{0}}(m^{k})=e_{0}(m^{k}),\ m^{k}=(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})\Bigg\}.



Let Y_{M} denote the space of all measures v_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}m^{k}\delta_{x^{k}} with \{x^{1},\dots,x^{K}\} distinct points in {\mathbb{T}^{2}} and K\otimes m^{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{M}. That is, v_{0}\in Y_{M} represents the limit (in the sense of distributions) of a sequence of energy minimizers v_{\eta} of mass M, which obey the conclusions of Proposition 4.3. We recall that the finiteness of these components is a consequence of Corollary 3.2.




We now describe the terms appearing in the second \Gamma-limit, which arise from passage to the limit in \Phi_{\eta}, defined in (4.9). First, the “self-interaction” terms making up \Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}). For m=(m_{1},m_{2}), m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0 and not both zero, define




f_{0}(m)=\inf\biggl\{\sum_{i,j=1,2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy+m_{i}\,m_{j}\,R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)\right]\ :\ \\
\ \ A=(A_{1},A_{2})\ \text{minimizes $\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}$ with $|A_{\ell}|=m_{\ell}$, $\ell=1,2$}\biggr\}

(4.20)


The minimization here is only pertinent in case m_{1},m_{2}>0, that is, where A=\mathcal{C}^{m_{1}}_{m_{2}} is a core shell; for single bubbles the minimizers of \mathcal{G}_{\sigma} with one of m_{i}=0 are unique up to rigid motion, and the set above is a singleton. It is in this term that energy minimization of the nonlocal energy resolves the degeneracy in the local isoperimetric problem, in favor of centered core shells:




Proposition 4.4.



Let \beta_{1}=0, and m=(m_{1},m_{2}) with m_{1},m_{2}>0.


(a) 



If \Gamma_{11}>\Gamma_{12}, then the minimum in f_{0}(m) is attained by a concentric core shell A=\mathcal{C}^{m_{1}}_{m_{2}}.


(b) 



If \Gamma_{11}<\Gamma_{12}, then the minimum in f_{0}(m) is attained by a core shell A=\mathcal{C}^{m_{1}}_{m_{2}} whose inner boundary circle is tangent to the outside circle.







Proof.



Given m_{1},m_{2}>0, from Lemma 2.6 we know that all minimizers of \mathcal{G}_{\sigma} with m_{1},m_{2}>0 are core shells, that is they are composed of two (round) balls B_{1}=B_{r_{1}}(0), B_{2}=B_{r_{2}}(p), with r_{1}>r_{2}, and the outer domain A_{1}=B_{1}\setminus B_{2}, with inner disk A_{2}=B_{2}. The distance between p (the center of B_{2}) and 0 must be small enough that A_{2}\subset A_{1}. We must choose p to minimize the quantity




\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}\left[I_{ij}+m_{i}m_{j}R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)\right],\quad\text{where}\quad I_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy.



Notice that I_{22} is independent of the location of p, as are of course the terms m_{i}m_{j}R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0).




For any x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{2}, by the Mean Value Theorem for harmonic functions,




\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dy=|B_{2}|\log{1\over|x-p|}.



Thus,




\displaystyle I_{12}
\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{2}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx=\frac{m_{2}}{2\pi}\left[\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx-\int_{B_{2}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx\right]




\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx-I_{22}.






We claim that I_{12}=I_{12}(p) is maximized when p=0 and minimized when the circles are tangent. By symmetry we may assume p=(t,0), with t\geq 0 lying in an interval t\in[0,\alpha] for which A_{2}\subset A_{1}. Differentiating,




{d\over dt}I_{12}(t,0)={1\over 2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}{x_{1}-t\over|x-(t,0)|^{2}}dx.

(4.21)


Let \omega_{t}=\{x\in B_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}}\ |\ x_{1}\geq t\}, and \tilde{\omega}_{t} its reflection in the line \{x_{1}=t\}. As the integrand in (4.21) is odd around the line \{x_{1}=t\}, and
B_{1}\setminus(\omega_{t}\cup\tilde{\omega}_{t})\subset\{x_{1}<t\}, we have \forall t\in(0,\alpha],




{d\over dt}I_{12}(t,0)={1\over 2\pi}\int_{B_{1}\setminus(\omega_{t}\cup\tilde{\omega}_{t})}{x_{1}-t\over|x-(t,0)|^{2}}dx<0.



Thus, I_{12}(t,0) is even and strictly monotone decreasing in t\in[0,\alpha], so it attains its maximum value at t=0 and minimum at the extreme value t=\alpha, where the circles are tangent. This concludes the proof of the claim.




To complete the argument, we note that,




\displaystyle I_{11}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy-I_{12}




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-I_{12}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy-\int_{B_{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy\right]




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-2I_{12}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy-I_{22}.



As I_{22} and \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy are independent of the location of p, we have




\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}I_{ij}
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{11}}{2}(-2I_{12}(p))+\Gamma_{12}I_{12}(p)+\text{ terms independent of }p

(4.22)



\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-(\Gamma_{11}-\Gamma_{12})I_{12}(p)+\text{ terms independent of }p.



The stated conclusions then follow, depending on the sign of the coefficient (\Gamma_{11}-\Gamma_{12}).
∎





We note that in the case \Gamma_{11}<\Gamma_{12} it may be difficult to observe tangential core shells, since that choice of strong repulsion between the two phases may result in core shells being split into single bubbles.




With this satisfactory resolution of the question of the specific geometry of optimal core shells, we may now define the second \Gamma-limit functional, by including the formal limits of the two remainder terms from (4.9).
For v_{0}\in Y_{M}, we define




F_{0}(v_{0})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}f_{0}(m^{k})+\sum_{k,\ell=1\atop k\neq\ell}^{K}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}m_{i}^{k}m_{j}^{\ell}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(x_{i}^{k}-x_{j}^{\ell}),

(4.23)


and F_{0}(v_{0})=+\infty otherwise. The first term in (4.23) is thus already completely determined by the first \Gamma-limit and the fine structure of core shells as determined by Proposition 4.4, and only the spatial distribution of droplets is at play in F_{0}.




Theorem 4.5 (Second \Gamma-limit).



We have




\displaystyle F_{\eta}\overset{\Gamma}{\longrightarrow}F_{0}\ \text{ as }\ \eta\rightarrow 0.



That is,


(a) 



Let v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta} be a sequence with \sup_{\eta>0}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})<\infty. Then there exists a subsequence \eta\to 0 and v_{0}\in Y_{M} such that
v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0} (in the weak topology of the space of measures), and




\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim\inf}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\geq F_{0}(v_{0}).




(b) 



Let v_{0}\in Y_{M} with F_{0}(v_{0})<\infty. Then there exists a sequence v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0} weakly as measures, such that




\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim\sup}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq F_{0}(v_{0}).









The tools required for the proof of the second limit are essentially included in our paper [1], in the unweighted case. We provide a slightly more detailed sketch here for the case \beta_{1}=0, as the paper [1] concentrated on the first \Gamma-limit and the structure of minimizers and did not give details for the second \Gamma-limit.




Sketch of the proof of Theorem 4.5.



We begin with compactness and the lower limit. Take a sequence \eta_{n}\to 0 and assume v_{\eta_{n}}\in X_{\eta} is a sequence with bounded F_{\eta_{n}}(v_{\eta_{n}}). (Recall F_{\eta} is defined in (4.19).) For convenience, we abuse notation and denote this sequence (and all forthcoming subsequences) simply as v_{\eta}, and \eta\to 0 along this sequence (and eventually, certain subsequences.) By the boundedness of F_{\eta}(v_{\eta}) we immediately conclude that




\lim_{\eta\to 0}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})=\overline{e_{0}}(M),



and hence Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.3 (Lemma 3.5 in [1]) apply to the sequence v_{\eta}. As a consequence, v_{\eta}=\eta^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}} splits into at most countably many indecomposable clusters, \Omega_{\eta}=\bigcup_{k}\Omega_{\eta}^{k}, whose energy E_{\eta}(v_{\eta}) breaks down via (4.8). Moreover, by Corollary 3.2 in the core shell case \beta_{1}=0 minimizers of \overline{e_{0}}(M) can only have a finite number K\in{\mathbb{N}} of nontrivial components. Hence,




\displaystyle E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})-\overline{e_{0}}(M)
\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A^{k}_{\eta})-\overline{e_{0}}(M)+|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}(A_{\eta}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}k}},\xi^{k}_{\eta})




\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}e_{0}(|A^{k}_{\eta}|)-\overline{e_{0}}(M)+|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta})




\displaystyle\geq|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta}),



since \overline{e_{0}}(M)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}e_{0}(m^{k}) whenever \sum_{k=1}^{K}m^{k}=M. That is, \Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta})\leq F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq C are uniformly bounded as \eta\to 0.




Next, we pass to the limit in \Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}) in (4.9). By (4.16), for each k, the clusters |A^{k}_{\eta}\triangle A^{k}|\to 0. In addition, by Lemma 4.1 (c), the diameters diam\,{A^{k}_{\eta}}\leq C are uniformly bounded in k,\eta. As \log{1\over|x-y|} is locally integrable, we may then pass to the limit using dominated convergence,




\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})=\sum_{i,j=1,2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy+m_{i}\,m_{j}\,R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)\right]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}f_{0}(m^{k}).

(4.24)





Since \Phi^{1} converges, we conclude that the second interaction term \Phi^{2}_{\eta}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},\{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}) is also bounded above. To pass to the limit in this term, we first note that if for some pair k\neq\ell, d(\xi^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{\ell}_{\eta})\geq\delta_{k,\ell}>0 for all \eta>0, then by a similar argument as above we may pass to the limit in this k,\ell term and obtain the corresponding term in F_{0} (see (4.23).) We claim that this must be the case for all pairs k\neq\ell. Indeed, we assume for some k\neq\ell (and a subsequence \eta\to 0 that r_{\eta}^{k,\ell}:=d(\xi^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{\ell}_{\eta})\to 0, then recall that by Proposition 4.3 (iii) we must nevertheless have
for i,j=1,2,




\displaystyle\int_{A_{i,\eta}^{k}}\int_{A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\xi_{\eta}^{k}+\eta\tilde{x}-\xi_{\eta}^{\ell}-\eta\tilde{y})\,d\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{y}
\displaystyle=\int_{A_{i,\eta}^{k}}\int_{A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}}\left[{1\over 2\pi}\log{1\over|\xi^{k}_{\eta}-\xi^{\ell}_{\eta}|}+R_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\xi_{\eta}^{k}+\eta\tilde{x}-\xi_{\eta}^{\ell}-\eta\tilde{y})\right]d\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{y}




\displaystyle=|A_{i,\eta}^{k}||A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}|\left({1\over 2\pi}|\log r^{k,\ell}_{\eta}|+R_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(0)+o(1)\right).



Since |A^{k}|>0 for k=1,\dots,K, there exists i,j\in\{1,2\} for which |A^{k}_{i}||A^{\ell}_{j}|>0, and that term will be unbounded above. This contradicts the upper bound on \Phi^{2}_{\eta}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},\{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}), and hence we conclude that the points \{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k=1,\dots,K} must remain distinct as \eta\to 0. Passing to a further subsequence if necessary, we may assume \xi^{k}_{\eta}\to x^{k}\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}}, with distinct x^{k}\neq x^{\ell}, k\neq\ell, and




\liminf_{\eta\to 0}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\geq\liminf_{\eta\to 0}\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta})\geq F_{0}(v_{0}),



where v_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}m^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in Y_{M}.
Note that (repeating an argument from above) by (4.16) and the uniform boundedness of the diameters of A^{k}_{\eta}, we may also conclude that v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0} in the weak topology on the space of measures on {\mathbb{T}^{2}}.
This concludes the proof of the compactness and lower limit of the second \Gamma-limit.




The construction of a recovery sequence for part 2 is straightforward: given points x^{k} and masses m^{k}=(m^{k}_{1},m^{k}_{2}), it suffices to assemble a superposition of \eta-rescaled minimizers of e_{0}(m^{k}) at each x^{k}. When the minimizing cluster A^{k} is a core shell, the central disk is positioned
centered when \Gamma_{12} is small according to Proposition 4.4.∎
