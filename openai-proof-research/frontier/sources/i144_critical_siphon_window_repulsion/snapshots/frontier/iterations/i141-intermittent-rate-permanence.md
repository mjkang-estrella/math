## 4. Route I: nonlinear chronological propagation under a siphon condition

A nonempty species set Z is a siphon if every reaction with a product in Z has a reactant in Z. Assume additionally:

**(SC)** For every siphon Z there is a nonzero w_Z≥0 with support contained in Z and w_Z·Delta_e=0 for every reaction e.

It is enough to check minimal siphons. On the fixed positive class let m_Z=w_Z·c>0. There are finitely many Z; choose

`epsilon=min(1, min_Z m_Z/(2||w_Z||_1)) >0`.

If there are no siphons, use epsilon=1. At any starting state x(s)∈P, put J_0={i:x_i(s)≥epsilon}. Its complement cannot contain a siphon: otherwise the conserved m_Z would be strictly less than epsilon||w_Z||_1≤m_Z/2. Starting from J_0, repeatedly add every product species of a reaction all of whose reactants already belong to J_r. If a nonempty complement remains when this process stops, that complement is a siphon, a contradiction. Therefore the closure contains all species after at most n rounds. This is species-support logic with all reactants required; it does not replace the complex graph by a species Markov chain.

Define the positive decreasing thresholds

`delta_0=epsilon, gamma=alpha exp(−C(d+1)T),`

`delta_(r+1)=min(exp(−CT)delta_r, gamma delta_r^d), r=0,...,n−1`.  (F3)

All delta_r≤1. If every species of J_r has concentration at least delta_r at time s+rT, an old species retains exp(−CT)delta_r at the next endpoint. A newly added species i has y_ei=0 and y'_ei≥1 for an edge with all source species in J_r. Integer stoichiometry gives (Delta_ei)_+≥1 and x^y_e≥delta_r^|y_e|≥delta_r^d. Formula F2 then gives gamma delta_r^d. Induction proves x_i(s+nT)≥delta_n for all i. Padding completed rounds simply uses the old-species bound.

Apply this at s=t−nT for every t≥nT. It proves

`x(t) in {x in closure(P): x_i≥delta_n for all i}, for every t≥nT`.  (F4)

The displayed set is compact and lies in P. Constants are uniform over all admissible schedules and, in this restricted case, over all positive initial points of the fixed class. This stronger entrance statement is compatible with I138's obstruction because that autocatalytic network has an unprotected siphon. The proof does not assert that the displayed floor set is invariant under every fresh schedule.

A genuine nonlinear, rank-two example is **A+B reversible with C, C reversible with D**. Its positive classes satisfy A+C+D=u>0 and B+C+D=v>0 and have compact closure. The minimal siphons are {A,C,D} and {B,C,D}, protected by those two conservation laws. ## 5. Route II: a window return argument for arbitrary rank-one classes

Here assume **dim S=1**, with any number of species, arbitrary nonnegative integer complexes and any number of linkage classes. Apart from self-loops, the graph is weakly reversible. Compact closure makes P an open bounded segment. Parametrize it as

`x(z)=a+vz, 0<z<L`,

where a and a+vL are the two boundary endpoints. Choose the orientation toward the second endpoint. Write Delta_e=c_e v. At the lower endpoint Z={i:a_i=0} is nonempty, v_i>0 for i∈Z, and sigma=sum_(i∈Z)v_i>0. For a complex y define p_y=sum_(i∈Z)y_i. Then

`p_(y')−p_y = c_e sigma`.  (R1)

Discard self-loops. In a nontrivial linkage class, the minimum p is achieved at a complex with a path to a larger p. The first edge on such a path already leaves the minimum: two distinct complexes in that linkage class differ by a nonzero multiple of v, so R1 prohibits a zero p difference. Let p be the minimum of p_y over all nontrivial linkage classes. Select an increasing edge e_* from a p-minimizer. Every decreasing edge has source order at least p+1, because its target has smaller **integer** order at least p. This remains true across linkage classes; isolated complexes do not enter p.

Fix 0<rho_0≤min(1,L/3) so all coordinates outside Z stay bounded below by positive numbers on [0,rho_0]. Factor

`x(z)^y=z^(p_y) g_y(z)`,

where g_y is continuous and strictly positive on that closed interval. Its factors are v_i^y_i for i∈Z and (a_i+v_i z)^y_i for i outside Z. Set g_y^−=min g_y>0, g_y^+=max g_y, and

`a_*=c_(e_*) g_(y_e*)^− >0,`

`D=max(1, K sum_(c_e<0) |c_e| g_(y_e)^+ rho_0^(p_y−p−1))`.

The actual scalar equation, with no averaging and no ratio linearization, obeys for 0<z≤rho_0

`z' ≥ a_* k_(e_*)(t) z^p −D z^(p+1) ≥ −D z`.  (R2)

Define

`c_*=(a_* alpha/2) exp(−pDT)`,

`rho=min(rho_0, a_* alpha/[2DT 2^(p+1) exp((2p+1)DT)])`.  (R3)

These numbers depend only on graph,class,K,T,alpha and the fixed parametrization. The p=0 case is included.

**Window increment.** Suppose z([s,s+T])⊂(0,rho]. If z(s+T)>2z(s), the increment exceeds z(s). Otherwise the last inequality in R2 gives, for every u in the window,

`exp(−DT) z(s) ≤ z(u) ≤ exp(DT) z(s+T) ≤2 exp(DT)z(s)`.

The upper estimate here is conditional on this particular nondoubling return. It is obtained backwards from the endpoint; no general two-sided relative bound for a producing species is assumed. Integrating the first inequality in R2, using PE on the one selected edge and z(s)≤rho, yields

`z(s+T)−z(s)`
`≥ a_* alpha exp(−pDT) z(s)^p − DT 2^(p+1)exp((p+1)DT) z(s)^(p+1)`
`≥ c_* z(s)^p`.

In both cases,

`z(s+T)−z(s) ≥ min(z(s), c_* z(s)^p)>0`.  (R4)

This is a proved return-map inequality on every window that stays in the band. It neither integrates an instantaneous minimum nor freezes an active affine label.

**Entry and protection after exit.** If 0<z(0)<rho and the trajectory has not yet reached rho, all successive full windows stay in the band and their endpoints increase by at least

`eta(z(0))=min(z(0),c_* z(0)^p)>0`.

Thus it reaches rho by time

`T[1+ceil(rho/eta(z(0)))]`.  (R5)

This bound is intentionally loose and handles exact endpoint equalities. Once a trajectory has reached or started above rho, every later open excursion below rho starts at rho. Such an excursion has length <T: a whole T-window beginning at rho and staying at most rho would contradict R4. Within the excursion, R2 gives z≥rho exp(−DT). Therefore after the first exit,

`z(t)≥delta_left:=rho exp(−DT)` for all subsequent t.  (R6)

For a compact initial Q, let z_Q=min_(x(z)∈Q)z>0 and replace z(0) in R5 by min(z_Q,rho); eta is nondecreasing for every integer p≥0. Initial points already above rho need no transient. Repeat the argument with w=L−z at the other endpoint, with its own p,a_*,D,rho,c_* and delta_right. Both bands were chosen of width ≤L/3. At the maximum of the two compact-initial entry bounds, and at every later time,

`delta_left ≤ z(t) ≤ L−delta_right`.  (R7)

This compact segment lies in P and is uniform over all PE schedules. Its eventual-membership property does not say every point in it is safe under every fresh schedule. Measurable solutions and global finite-time positivity were already paid in Section 2.5. Rank zero is the singleton case.
