                                                   Abstract. We prove that there is a unique p0 ∈ [0, 1), which can be characterized by the eigen-
                                                   value of Hilbert operator related to a convex body, that the even Lp Minkowski problem has a
                                                   unique solution for p ≥ p0 , and the uniqueness fails for infinitely many convex bodies if p < p0 .
                                                   The previous results by many experts in the field assert that the uniqueness holds for p > p0 .

short we can say that the uniqueness is closed if it is open in p. Our result is a consequence of the

   The equivalence between the even Lp Brunn-Minkowski conjectures and the even Lp Minkowski
conjecture are well-known, see [7] and in particular [39, Theorem 1.1, Theorem 2.1] for the most
recent update. In particular Putterman [41, Proposition 1.5] proved that a local version of Lp -Brunn-
Minkowski inequality implies the global version of the inequality, verifying a conjecture in [29]. Her
results in particular imply that the Lp -Brunn-Minkowski inequalities hold at p = p0 . Our result
above (Theorem 4) provides one missing piece in the picture of the equivalence of these conjectures.
The other new ingredient of our result is to provide the strict eigenvalue estimate (12). We actually
prove the following,
                    4
Theorem 5. For K ∈ K+,e , then λ1,e (−LK ) cannot have a local minimum.
   As this manuscript was being completed, we note that S. Wang also obtained similar results
in[50], where he proved that if the local log Brunn-Minkowski inequality holds, then the equality
occurs only for homothetic convex bodies. It’s equivalent to that λ1,e (−LK ) > 0. However, our
approach here is very different from [50]. Kolesnikov and Milamn [29, Proposition 5.14] conjectured
that inf λ1,e is taken by the cube, which is known to be n + 1 [29, Theorem 10.2] (note that we
use a different normalization and dimension convention). Our result shows that if the conjecture
                                                                       4
inf λ1,e = n + 1 is true, then indeed λ1,e (−LK ) > n + 1 for any K ∈ K+,e . For p < 0, it is known
that both (4) and (5) fail in general and there are many previous known non-uniqueness results
[47, 2, 18, 28, 26, 30, 31, 29] etc. The Lp Brunn-Minkowski theory and Lp Minkowski problem
have attract much attention over last several decades after the seminal work of Lutwak [35], see
                                                    4
for instance [4, 5, 8, 14, 18, 25, 27, 36, 46, 49, 54, 55] etc. The existence and uniqueness of the Lp
Minkowski problem are well understood when p ≥ 1. When p < 0, the uniqueness fails even restricted
to smooth origin-symmetric convex bodies [28]. When 0 ≤ p < 1, the uniqueness of solutions to
the Lp -Minkowski problem turns out to be really challenging and it remains a very important open
problem in the field. We refer in particular to the recent survey paper by K. Böröczky [6] and [29, 39]
for an overview and more references of the problem and related topics in the subject.
   The work of Kolesnikov and Milman [29] indicates the significance of the study of the first even
eigenvalue λ1,e (−LK ) for the uniqueness conjecture of the even Logarithm Minkowski problem. Built
on the progress in [29, 16, 41], our results above give a characterization of the uniqueness to the
even Lp Minkowski conjecture. In [38], E. Milman provides an elegant proof that the maximum of
λ1,e (−LK ) equals 2(n + 1), and the maximum is achieved precisely by all the ellipsoids. The study
of infimum turns out to be much more delicate. For nonsmooth convex body K, −LK does not have
an apparent meaning as a regular differential operator. There are several notions of λ1,e introduced
for nonsmooth case [29, 41, 38], see in particular [38, Section 1.4] for a detailed discussion. It is
proved [29] that λ1,e (−LK ) is invariant under the action of a general linear transformation of Rn+1 ,

                             λ1,e (−LT (K) ) = λ1,e (−LK ), T ∈ GL(n + 1).
