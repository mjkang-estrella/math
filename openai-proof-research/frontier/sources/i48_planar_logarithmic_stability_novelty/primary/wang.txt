                                                        MONOTONICITY OF THE DEFICIT IN THE LOCAL
                                                           LOG-BRUNN-MINKOWSKI INEQUALITY

                                                                                          SHOUDA WANG


                                                 Abstract. We establish a monotonicity property of the deficit associated with the local
                                                 log–Brunn–Minkowski inequality (LLBM) under addition of line segments. As a corollary, if
                                                 the LLBM holds for a convex body K, then it also holds for K + Z for any zonoid Z, which in




arXiv:2510.19061v2 [math.MG] 4 Nov 2025
                                                 particular yields a new proof of the inequality for zonoids. Moreover, assuming the LLBM is
                                                 valid, we prove that equality in the LLBM for smooth convex bodies with C 2 support functions
                                                 occurs only for homothetic bodies.




                                                                                        1. Introduction

                                            The classical Brunn–Minkowski inequality is a fundamental tool in convex geometry asserting
                                          the log-concavity of the volume functional: for convex bodies K, L ⊂ Rn and t ∈ [0, 1],

                                                                     Vol tK + (1 − t)L ≥ Vol(K)t Vol(L)1−t .
                                                                                       

                                          It is an important object of study in various areas of geometry, analysis, and combinatorics, see,
                                          for example, [5], [4], and [13]. As a generalization of the classical Brunn-Minkowski inequality,
                                          Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang [2] conjectured the log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality: for
                                          origin-symmetric convex bodies K, L,

                                                                          Vol K t L1−t ≥ Vol(K)t Vol(L)1−t ,

                                          where K t L1−t is defined later in Section 2. The log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality also has many
                                          interesting connections to other problems. Indeed, it is related to the uniqueness of Minkowski’s
                                          problem for cone volume measures [2], to a spectral gap estimate for the Hilbert operator asociated
                                          to symmetric convex bodies [7], to an inequality for the Gaussian density [10], just to name a few.
                                          We refer to the survey [1, Sec.3 & 4] for more detailed discussions.

The equivalence of these three inequalities is much harder to establish than the classical setting
due to the non-linearity of the map t 7→ K 1−t Lt . Nevertheless, the equivalence of the log-Brunn-
Minkowski and the log-Minkowski inequality was shown in [2] and the equivalence of the local
log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality to the the first two inequalities was shown by combining [7] with
[8]. Therefore, the study of the log-Brunn-Minkowski (log-BM) conjecture is reduced to the study
of the local log-Brunn-Minkowski (LLBM) conjecture, which is the focus of the current note.

Prior work. The dimension 2 case of the log-BM conjecture was settled in the original paper
[2], while the conjecture in complete generality stays wide open in higher dimensions.
   The log-BM conjecture has also been verified when both K and L satisfy certain symmetry
assumptions. Saroglou [10] proved log-BM when K and L are both unconditional. Böröczky
and Kalantzopoulos [3] proved log-BM for convex bodies invariant under reflections through n
independent hyperplanes. Rotem [9] verified the log-BM for complex convex bodies.
   Note that these results put the same restriction on K and L, which is natural for log-BM since
log-BM is symmetric about K and L up to replacing t by 1 − t. However, for the log-Minkowski
and LLBM inequality, K and L play a very different role. In particular, Kolesnikov and Milman
[7, Cor.5.4] pointed out that the LLBM for a fixed K and all L is equivalent to a lower bound on
the spectral gap of the Hilbert operator associated to K. Therefore it is natural to think of L
merely as a “test body” and K as the “base body”. And then it is natural to ask for a class of
base bodies K such that log-Minkowski/LLBM holds for all test bodies L.
   In [14], this spectral interpretation was used to prove LLBM for zonoids, from which it follows
that the log-Minkowski inequality also holds for zonoids K and all L. The equality cases of
                                                                                                 3


LLBM for zonoids were also characterized in [14]. Xi [15] provided another proof of the log-
Minkowski inequality for zonoids by proving a reverse log-Minkowski inequality and showing a
reverse-to-forward principle. This approach also allowed him to characterize the equality cases of
the log-Minkowski inequality for zonoids.


Main ideas of this paper. The starting point of this note is to provide a different proof of
LLBM for zonoids. Our approach differs from the two previous results in that we work directly
with the deficit of LLBM and prove that the deficit is monotone with respect to adding an interval
to the base body K under a certain normalization condition on L.

                                        2. Preliminaries

2.1. A convex body in Rn is a convex compact subset of Rn . The support function of a convex
body K is defined by hK (u) := maxx∈K ⟨x, u⟩. The Minkowski sum of K with L is defined
as K + L := {a + b : a ∈ K, b ∈ L}. The Minkowski difference of K and L is defined as
K ÷ L := {x ∈ Rn : x + L ⊆ K}. The Minkowski sum can be expressed via support functions as

                    K + L = {x ∈ Rn : ∀u ∈ S n−1 , ⟨x, u⟩ ⩽ hK (u) + hL (u)}.

This motivates the definition of K t L1−t . Indeed, as above we can write the weighted arithmetic
mean tK + (1 − t)L as

            tK + (1 − t)L = {x ∈ Rn : ∀u ∈ S n−1 , ⟨x, u⟩ ⩽ thK (u) + (1 − t)hL (u)}.

Hence similarly, for K and L containing the origin in their interior (i.e. hK (u), hL (u) > 0 for all
u ̸= 0), it is natural to define the weighted geometric mean of K and L as

                   K t L1−t := {x ∈ Rn : ∀u ∈ S n−1 , ⟨x, u⟩ ⩽ hK (u)t hL (u)1−t }.

Denote by Kns the set of all origin symmetric convex bodies with nonempty interior. We will only
consider the log-BM, log-Minkowski, LLBM for convex bodies in the class Kns .

          4. Equality cases for smooth bodies with C 2 support functions

  In this section, we will assume the LLBM inequality is true and study the equality cases of
LLBM for smooth bodies with C 2 support functions. It turns out that the monotonicity principle
proved in Theorem 1.2 can be adapted to prove that only trivial equality cases appear in this
case.

4.1. Recall that a convex body is called smooth if all of its boundary points have a unique unit
normal vector. For two convex bodies K, L, denote the Minkowski subtraction by K ÷ L := {x ∈

the case we need. The proof of Fact 4.2 follows principally the same lines as Schneider’s proof.
