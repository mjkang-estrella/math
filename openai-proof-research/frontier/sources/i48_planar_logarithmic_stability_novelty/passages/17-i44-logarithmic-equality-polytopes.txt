49: 
50: Put F(f)=log|W[e^f]| on even continuous functions. If A=W[e^f] and B=W[e^g], the logarithmic Wulff combination of A and B lies in W[e^{(1−t)f+tg}]. The accepted inequality gives concavity of F on this whole vector space.
51: 
52: Here is the potentially vulnerable contact argument explicitly. At a regular boundary point x of A, compactness of the sphere ensures an active constraint: otherwise e^f(u)−u·x has a positive minimum, making x interior. The normal of an active constraint must be the unique normal N_A(x). Thus e^f(N_A(x))=h_A(N_A(x)). Regular boundary points have full boundary area, so contact holds S_A-almost everywhere. This does not assert contact for all directions.
53: 
54: For k(t,u)=e^{f(u)+tg(u)}, the difference quotient converges uniformly to e^f g. Putterman's Lemma 2.1 applies to this positive bound, including inactive directions, and gives
55: 
56: \[
57:  DF(f)g=|A|^{-1}\int e^f g\,dS_A
58:        =n\int g\,d\nu_A,\qquad \nu_A=h_A dS_A/(n|A|).
59: \]
60: 
61: This is a continuous linear functional in g. A concave function's directional derivative is a global upper support: restrict to a line and use its decreasing chord slopes. If F is affine on [f,g], the upper support at f also contacts g. At a point where the two-sided directional derivatives exist, any upper supporting functional equals the derivative, by testing both signs of every direction. Thus all derivatives on the equality segment agree. Testing all even continuous functions distinguishes these even measures. Conversely, equal derivatives at the endpoints squeeze F(g)−F(f) between equal numbers and force affinity. An interior chord equality forces affinity on the entire segment by elementary concavity. Contact plus continuity upgrades almost-everywhere contact to contact on the support of the common measure.
