297: ### 4.4 Linear algebra and global reconstruction
298: 
299: Choose τ₀=D²φ(0). From Aτ₀=τ₀Aᵀ, the matrix τ₀⁻¹/²Aτ₀¹/² is real symmetric. A is therefore diagonalizable over R, even when zero eigenvalues or repeated eigenvalues occur. Use one constant dual transformation to make A symmetric. One can multiply τ₀⁻¹/² by (detτ₀)¹/(2n), so the transformation is volume preserving; this avoids changing the normalized volume during the argument.
300: 
301: Now A commutes with D²φ everywhere. Relative to its distinct eigenspaces Eᵢ all mixed Hessian blocks vanish. Because the source domain is **all Rⁿ**, integrating along coordinate segments gives φ(z)=c+Σφᵢ(zᵢ), with linear terms included in φᵢ. Its gradient image is the product of the individual gradient images. Taking closures gives C=ΣCᵢ, each a bounded full-dimensional relative body; projections of the symmetric C make the factors symmetric and centered at zero. The normal support of a product is contained in the union of the factor subspaces, since the boundary pieces with at least two boundary factors have zero (n−1)-area.
302: 
303: For clarity, reconstruction from the surface-area support S does not require all normals to be regular. Let P=∩_{u∈S}{x:x·u≤h_K(u)}. S positively spans Rⁿ, so P is bounded, and K⊂P. On S their supports agree. Thus V₁(K,P)=|K|. Ordinary Minkowski's first inequality yields |P|≤|K|, and inclusion implies P=K. The same applies to L and C. This classical inequality needs no equality characterization here.
304: 
305: The common normal support now separates the constraints into the eigenspaces, giving K=ΣKᵢ, L=ΣLᵢ. For a normal u∈Eᵢ, Ax·u=λᵢh_C(u) on its supporting boundary. The flux relation gives f(u)=−λᵢ for surface-area-almost every such direction. Continuity, and the fact that distinct eigenspace spheres are disjoint compact sets, extends it to the entire common support in Eᵢ. Reconstruction within the factor gives Lᵢ=e^{−λᵢ}Kᵢ. Since tr A=Σλᵢdim Eᵢ=0, the normalized volume ratio is one, as required.

307: If A=0, there is one eigenspace and f=0 on the common support, so K=L after normalization. A scalar A also vanishes because tr A=0. Repeated eigenvalues merely merge factors; uniqueness of the finest decomposition is not claimed. Undoing the constant linear map permits nonorthogonal complementary primal blocks, and undoing endpoint normalization restores arbitrary positive dilation factors.

312: ## 5. C: facet-centroid invariant and ridge compatibility
313: 
314: Index one unit normal uᵢ per antipodal true facet pair; hᵢ(t)=aᵢe^{bᵢt}, |P(t)|=Ce^{st}, and wᵢ=2hᵢAᵢ/|P| are fixed and positive by the inherited reduction. This exact positive-mass statement concerns a finite fixed equality path only.
315: 
316: Perturb uᵢ to uᵢ+ηr and its opposite to its negative, holding every width fixed. The source finite-slab inequality allows nonunit spanning vectors. Boundedness persists for small η. Away from the facet boundary the normal velocity is −r·x. Integrating the swept layer gives the volume derivative −2∫_{Fᵢ}r·x. To justify this at nonsimple vertices, remove a δ-neighborhood of the finite facet skeleton. Its contribution divided by |η| is O(the removed facet area)+o(1); overlaps near transverse fixed hyperplanes have order O(η²) after localization. Let η→0 and then δ→0. Facet boundaries have zero facet area. This proves the two-sided first variation without genericity.

322:                        +t\frac{c_i(1)}{h_i(1)}.

327: For every independent n-tuple of signed normals a candidate vertex is linear in its exponential supports. Each feasibility event is an exponential polynomial with at most n+1 distinct rates. A nonzero such function has at most n real zeros: divide by its least exponential, differentiate, and use Rolle inductively. Identically zero events are retained as persistent incidences. Thus a finite partition into open strata exists, including paths entirely on nonsimple walls.
328: 
329: On a stratum, pulling triangulations express Jᵢ=∫_{Fᵢ}x dH^{n−1} as a homogeneous polynomial of degree n in the support vector. Unit normals fix the area-to-oriented-determinant conversion; signs are fixed on each nondegenerate simplex. Hence cᵢ/hᵢ=2Jᵢ/(wᵢCe^{st}) is a finite real exponential sum on any nonempty stratum. An affine exponential sum is constant: its second derivative and exponential independence eliminate every nonzero rate. Since the vector function was globally affine, its slope is zero on the whole interval. This proves C, including endpoints and persistent walls.

331: For the additional ridge balance, write all signed facets separately. In the hyperplane of facet k the ridge inequality has bound (h_ℓ−h_k cosθ)/sinθ. Its velocity is the stated d′_{kℓ}. The derivative of the centroid consists of the normal translation b_kh_ku_k plus the sum of boundary velocities times (ridge centroid−facet centroid), weighted by ridge area/A_k. Subtract the same calculation for uniform dilation at rate b_k, and use c′_k=b_kc_k. Multiplying by h_kA_k/|P| gives exactly I44 (3.7).
332: 
333: For n≥3, a ridge that disappears while all true facets survive collapses in its (n−2)-dimensional affine intersection, so its measure and first moment vanish. Surviving ridge measures and first moments converge by the fixed-plane section description. For n=2 the zero-dimensional ridge measure is one and cannot be argued to vanish by shrinking length. Instead a polygon with a fixed irredundant cyclic list of true normals has a fixed adjacency list; each vertex belongs to exactly two edges, and an adjacency change would require loss of a true edge. No such disappearance occurs on this equality path. Thus the original continuity claim is valid also in dimension two, with this distinct reason. Zero-weight nonexistent ridges should be interpreted through their moment integrals rather than assigned centroids.

Each r-kernel is r¹/ᑫe^{−r} for the ψᵢψⱼ moment, giving Γ(1)²=1 in the limit.

If the intersection is feasible only in smaller dimension, its (n−2)-area is zero and contributes zero. In n=2 the affine fiber is a point; a feasible intersection of two distinct true facets is a vertex with exactly two incident facets, so the same statement, using H⁰, is valid.

Thus (4.1), including nonsimple cases, follows.
