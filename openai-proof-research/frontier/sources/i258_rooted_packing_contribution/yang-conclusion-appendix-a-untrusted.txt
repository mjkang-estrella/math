7     Concluding remarks
Theorem 3.3 makes the Hall-function class F (P), rather than individual neighborhoods, the
central complexity parameter. Two-sided biased brackets and small local subpattern families
give the applications above. Extending the method requires controlling this complexity
relative to the least candidate count; the obstruction in Section 6.2 rules out relying on
spreadness alone with fixed roots.
    A separate graph-theoretic difficulty remains: stronger bracketing would not remove the
use of Proposition 2.1 in its stated degeneracy range, or the low-degeneracy base assumption
in Theorem 5.1. Progress toward (1.2) must address both the controlled-maximum-degree
component and rooted matching.
    Finally, the distinction between a target graph and a general increasing graph property
is essential. For H ∈ I, the graph reduction gives pk (I) ≤ rk (H), but a bound on rk (H) in
terms of q(H) cannot simply be replaced by one in terms of q(I): monotonicity gives only

                                           q(I) ≤ q(H).

As observed in [1, Section 8], even a positive answer to the graph-decomposition conjecture
would therefore not automatically settle the full discrete convexity problem. The results
here concern fixed ordinary-threshold decompositions and the corresponding consequences
for single-target containment properties.

accumulated errors are at most Qη and 2Qη, respectively, while the pointwise inequalities
persist. Finally, all DNFs with at most M terms can be represented by M slots, each
containing one of the Q patterns or a vacant slot. This gives at most (Q + 1)M functions. A
vacant slot is different from the empty-set term.

Remark A.3 (Why two-sided biased brackets are retained). Lovett, Wu, and Zhang [7] prove
sharper DNF approximation bounds under the uniform measure. For DNFs, their subfor-
mula construction gives a pointwise lower approximation. Our use of stochastic domination
requires an increasing upper bracket as well, and the error must be controlled under µρ , with
ρ possibly tending to zero. We therefore use the explicit two-sided biased construction above,
rather than substitute a uniform-measure approximation bound without those properties.
