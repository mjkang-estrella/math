   Gromov [7] and Sturm [9] proved independently that every CAT(0) space satisfies
the ⊠-inequalities. The name “⊠-inequalities” is based on a notation used by Gromov
[7]. Sturm [9] called these inequalities the weighted quadruple inequalities. The present
author [10] proved the following theorem.
Theorem 1.3 ([10]). If a metric space X satisfies the ⊠-inequalities, then X satisfies
all CAT(0) quadratic metric inequalities on 5 points.
  It follows from Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.3 that a metric space X with |X| ≤ 5
admits an isometric embedding into a CAT(0) space if and only if X satisfies the ⊠-
inequalities. On the other hand, Nina Lebedeva constructed a 6-point metric space that
satisfies the ⊠-inequalities but does not admit an isometric embedding into any CAT(0)
space (see the arXiv version of [1, §7.2] or Theorem 3.1 in this paper). Therefore, the
validity of the ⊠-inequalities is not a sufficient condition for even a 6-point metric space
to admit an isometric embedding into a CAT(0) space.

to be the first explicit example of a CAT(0) quadratic metric inequality that is not a

of the form (1.2) as well (see [12, Corollary 1.5]).

   In this section, we recall the 6-point metric space constructed by Nina Lebedeva,

   For any two points a and b in a Euclidean space, we denote by [a, b] the line segment

literature. The argument is slightly indirect because we rely on previously published
results. For this purpose, we begin by recalling the definition and some properties of
the Cycl4 (0) condition introduced by Gromov [7], which is closely related to the (2 + 2)-
comparison. A metric space X is said to satisfy the Cycl4 (0) condition if and only if for
any points x, y, z, w ∈ X, there exist points x̃, ỹ, z̃, w̃ in the Euclidean plane R2 with

condition are distinct because if they are not distinct, {x, y, z, w} consists of at most
three points in a metric space and clearly can embed isometrically into the Euclidean
plane. Gromov [7] established the following fact.

if X satisfies the Cycl4 (0) condition.
