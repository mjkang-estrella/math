                                                                                             Abstract
                                                In this article we relate word and subgroup growth to certain functions that arise in the quantifi-
                                                cation of residual finiteness. One consequence of this endeavor is a pair of results that equate the
                                                nilpotency of a finitely generated group with the asymptotic behavior of these functions. The sec-
                                                ond half of this article investigates the asymptotic behavior of two of these functions. Our main
                                                result in this arena resolves a question of Bogopolski from the Kourovka notebook concerning
                                                lower bounds of one of these functions for nonabelian free groups.

                                          1 Introduction
                                          The goals of the present article are to examine the interplay between word and subgroup growth, and
                                          to quantify residual finiteness, a topic motivated and described by the first author in [1]. These two
                                          goals have an intimate relationship that will be illustrated throughout this article.
                                          Our focus begins with the interplay between word and subgroup growth. Recall that for a fixed finite
                                          generating set X of Γ with associated word metric ||·||X , word growth investigates the asymptotic
                                          behavior of the function
                                                                           wΓ,X (n) = |{γ ∈ Γ : ||γ ||X ≤ n}| ,
                                          while subgroup growth investigates the asymptotic behavior of the function

                                                                               sΓ (n) = |{∆ ⊳ Γ : [Γ : ∆] ≤ n}| .

                                          To study the interaction between word and subgroup growth we propose the first of a pair of ques-
                                          tions:
                                          Question 1. What is the smallest integer FΓ,X (n) such that for every word γ in Γ of word length at
                                          most n, there exists a finite index normal subgroup of index at most FΓ,X (n) that fails to contain γ ?

Our first result, which relies on Inequality (1), is the following.
Theorem 1.1. If Γ is a finitely generated linear group, then the following are equivalent:
  (a) FΓ,X (n) ≤ (log(n))r for some r.
  (b) Γ is virtually nilpotent.
For finitely generated linear groups that is not virtually nilpotent, Theorem 1.1 implies FΓ,X (n) 
(log(n))r for any r > 0. For this class of groups, we can improve this lower bound. Precisely, we
have the following result—see Section 2 for the definition of .
Theorem 1.2. Let Γ be a group that contains a nonabelian free group of rank m. Then

The motivation for the proof of Theorem 1.2 comes from the study of FZ,X (n), where the Prime
Number Theorem and least common multiples provide lower and upper bounds for FZ,X (n). In
Section 4, we extend this approach by generalizing least common multiples to finitely generated
groups (a similar approach was also taken in the article of Hadad [7]). Indeed with this analogy,
Theorem 1.2 and the upper bound of n3 established in [1], [11] can be viewed as a weak Prime
Number Theorem for free groups since the Prime Number Theorem yields FZ (n) ≃ log(n). Re-
cently, Kassabov–Matucci [8] improved the lower bound of n1/3 to n2/3 . A reasonable guess is that
FFm ,X (n) ≃ n, though presently neither the upper or lower bound is known. We refer the reader to
[8] for additional questions and conjectures.

The asymptotic growth of FΓ,X (n), GΓ,X (n), and related functions arise in quantifying residual finite-
ness, a topic introduced in [1] (see also the recent articles of the authors [2], Hadad [7], Kassabov–
Mattucci [8], and Rivin [11]). Quantifying residual finiteness amounts to the study of so-called
divisibility functions. Given a finitely generated, residually finite group Γ, we define the divisibility
function DΓ : Γ• −→ N by
                                    DΓ (γ ) = min {[Γ : ∆] : γ ∈
                                                               / ∆} .
The associated normal divisibility function for normal, finite index subgroups is defined in an iden-
tical way and will be denoted by D⊳  Γ . It is a simple matter to see that FΓ,X (n) is the maximum value
of D⊳Γ  over all non-trivial elements in BΓ,X (n). We will denote the associated maximum of DΓ over
this set by max DΓ (n).
The rest of the introduction is devoted to a question of Oleg Bogopolski, which concerns max DΓ,X (n).
It was established in [1] that log(n)  max DΓ,X (n) for any finitely generated group with an element
of infinite order (this was also shown by [11]). For a nonabelian free group Fm of rank m, Bogopolski
asked whether max DFm ,X (n) ≃ log(n) (see Problem 15.35 in the Kourovka notebook [10]). Our next
result answers Bogopolski’s question in the negative—we again refer the reader to Section 2 for the
definition of .
Theorem 1.4. If m > 1, then max DFm ,X (n)  log(n).
We prove Theorem 1.4 in Section 5 using results from Section 4. The first part of the proof of
Theorem 1.4 utilizes the material established for the derivation of Theorem 1.2. The second part of
the proof of Theorem 1.4 is topological in nature, and involves a careful study of finite covers of the
figure eight. It is also worth noting that our proof only barely exceeds the proposed upper bound
of log(n). In particular, at present we cannot rule out the upper bound (log(n))2 . In addition, to
our knowledge the current best upper bound is n/2 + 2, a result established recently by Buskin [3].
In comparison to our other results, Theorem 1.4 is the most difficult to prove and is also the most
surprising. Consequently, the reader should view Theorem 1.4 as our main result.

1. Function comparison and basic facts. For a pair of functions f1 , f2 : N → N, by f1  f2 , we
mean that there exists a constant C such that f1 (n) ≤ C f2 (Cn) for all n. In the event that f1  f2 and
f2  f1 , we will write f1 ≃ f2 .
This notion of comparison is well suited to the functions studied in this paper. We summarize some
of the basic results from [1] for completeness.
Lemma 2.1. Let Γ be a finitely generated group.
  (a) If X,Y are finite generating sets for Γ then FΓ,X ≃ FΓ,Y .
  (b) If ∆ is a finitely generated subgroup of Γ and X,Y are finite generating sets for Γ, ∆ respec-
      tively, then F∆,Y  FΓ,X .

  (c) If ∆ is a finite index subgroup of Γ with X,Y as in (b), then FΓ,X  (F∆,Y )[Γ:∆] .
We also have a version of Lemma 2.1 for residual girth functions.
Lemma 2.2. Let Γ be a finitely generated group.
  (a) If X,Y are finite generating sets for Γ, then GΓ,X ≃ GΓ,Y .
  (b) If ∆ is a finitely generated subgroup of Γ and X,Y are finite generating sets for Γ, ∆ respec-
      tively, then G∆,Y  GΓ,X .

  (c) If ∆ is a finite index subgroup of Γ with X,Y as in (b), then GΓ,X  (G∆,Y )[Γ:∆] .
As the proof of Lemma 2.2 is straightforward, we have opted to omit it for sake of brevity. As a
consequence of Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2, we occasionally suppress the dependence of the generating set
in our notation.
