Related work. This work advances the study of the complexity of log-concave sampling; an
introduction to the subject and an overview of existing approaches can be found in [Che26].
    Our use of Gaussian smoothing is reminiscent of score-based diffusion models [SDWMG15,
SE19, HJA20, SSDK+ 21] and stochastic localization [Eld13], which also rely on score functions for
Gaussian convolutions of a target distribution. Here, we do not learn score functions from data: we
use queries to the unsmoothed score to construct nearly unbiased estimators of the smoothed score,
and control their bias and fluctuations in the sampling analysis.
    Hamiltonian Monte Carlo was introduced as hybrid Monte Carlo in lattice field theory [DKPR87];
see [Nea11] for its development in statistics. Early analyses quantified its favorable dimension
dependence through asymptotic scaling results for product targets [BPR+ 13]. Subsequent works
established non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for ideal HMC [LSV18, BREZ20, MS21, CV22].
More recent work has focused on accelerated convergence of randomized HMC and related piecewise
deterministic processes, including convergence in relative entropy [LW22, MSWW26, MW26].
    A substantial literature studies numerical discretization, both for unadjusted HMC [MV18, MS19,
BRE23, BRM25, GLBMM25, BRMW26, ZAC26] and for Metropolized HMC [CDWY20, LST20,
CGJ26, ZAC26]. Representative results achieve d1/4 dimension dependence under quantitative
third derivative bounds, such as structured or Frobenius-Lipschitz Hessian assumptions [MV18,
CGJ26, ZAC26]. Higher-order integrators and polynomial collocation yield still better dimension
dependence under stronger smoothness and structural assumptions [MS17, LSV18]. Our departure
is to improve the dimension dependence without imposing higher-order smoothness on the original
potential: Gaussian smoothing provides the regularity needed for accurate integration, while we
control the error from estimating the smoothed score using queries to the unsmoothed score.
    The recursive warm start framework builds heavily on the proximal sampler [LST21, CCSW22].
The main analytic mechanism for warm start generation is regularization along the heat flow, leading
to reverse transport inequalities. Such inequalities are dual formulations of dimension-free Harnack
inequalities, originating in Wang’s work [Wan97, Wan04]; see also [BGL15, AC25] for the transport
formulation. Related regularization arguments have been used to generate algorithmic warm starts


                                                          4
with underdamped Langevin [AC24] and HMC [ZAC26], and to establish KL divergence guarantees
via the shifted composition framework [AC26, ACZ26]. Coupling-based regularization has also
yielded total variation bounds [BRE23] and divergence guarantees for unadjusted HMC [BRMW26].
These works analyze regularization for particular sampling dynamics. Our framework instead applies
heat flow regularization to the output of any smoothed sampler, yielding a general route to KL,
mixed TV and Rényi, or genuine Rényi warm starts.

3.1   The smoothing trick
For a smoothing variance η > 0, write

                            πη := π ∗ N(0, ηI) ,            Πη := πη ⊗ N(0, I) .

The key algorithmic idea of this paper is to pick a smoothing parameter η > 0 and to sample
from the smoothed distribution πη instead of the original target π. If Law(Y ) ≈ πη , then we can
recover a sample from π via the RGO: for X ∼ Rη,Y , we have Law(X) ≈ π.
    The smoothed potential Vη , defined by e−Vη := e−V ∗ N(0, ηI), is given by

higher-order derivative bounds, allowing us to apply higher-order integrators. The catch is that (3.1)
is given as a conditional expectation, and is therefore not explicitly computable. Write gη := ∇Vη .

h−1/2 due to cancellations. In our analysis, the “deterministic” component consists of two terms:
