## 3. Route one: relative orbit averaging and a uniform cusp estimate

### 3.1 What orbit averaging actually pays

Write mu for the complete packet, m for Haar probability on X_n, and A for the positive diagonal group. Every selected hull-class subpacket is A-invariant and nu≤k mu. Choose a regular a∈A. For f∈C_c(X_n), set g=f−m(f) and g_T=T^(-1) sum_{j=0}^{T−1}g(·a^j). Invariance, Jensen and domination give the concrete estimate

\[
 |\nu(f)-m(f)|^2\le k\,\mu(|g_T|^2)
 \le k\left(m(|g_T|^2)+|\Delta_\mu(T,f)|\right),\qquad
 \Delta_\mu(T,f)=\mu(|g_T|^2)-m(|g_T|^2).                 \tag{A}
\]

For fixed T the squared test is a compactly supported continuous function plus a constant; complete-packet convergence therefore makes Delta tend to zero. Haar ergodicity of a gives m(|g_T|^2)→0. Here that ergodicity can be checked by the usual unitary contraction argument: an a-invariant vector is invariant under each root unipotent subgroup contracted by either a^j or a^(−j); these generate SL_n(R), whose invariant functions on the quotient are constant. The mean ergodic theorem then applies. Taking the packet limit **before** T→infinity proves uniform equidistribution for every fixed bound k≤k_0, even for arbitrary A-invariant subcollections of mass ≥1/k_0. Domination and the full-packet cusp bound give no escape at this scope.

The exact correlation identity is

\[
 m(|g_T|^2)=T^{-2}\sum_{|j|<T}(T-|j|)\langle g,g\circ a^j\rangle.
\]

Summable correlations would bound this by C_f/T, but the source gives no discriminant-uniform error for the moving test |g_T|^2. Even if one separately supplied a typical error d^(−beta) times a Sobolev norm growing like exp(cT), balancing at T proportional to log d would only make (A) useful for k=o(log d), not k=d^delta. This conditional calculation diagnoses the cost; it does not assume the missing effective bound.

The averaging mechanism is already present in [Aka–Einsiedler, arXiv:1403.2016v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.2016), whose complete proof was read through its ending. Their quadratic closed-geodesic result combines effective Duke with mixing and pays the reciprocal-density/log-discriminant loss. Their polynomially sparse arbitrary subcollections can fail to equidistribute; that quadratic construction is **not** a counterexample to C145's prime-degree algebraic cosets. Neither the bounded-index deduction nor an unspecified compactness modulus is nominated as new substantial work.

### 3.2 A paid local-type-uniform moment bound

This part uses more than weak convergence. Retain the source normalization

    D=|Disc(K)|, q=[RM:M], Q=q sqrt(D), kappa=Res_{s=1} zeta_K(s),
    S={rational primes dividing q},
    Z_S=product_{ell in S} product_{p|ell}(1−ell^(−f_p))^(−1).

The order discriminant d satisfies d≤D q^(2n)≤Q^(2n), so d→infinity implies Q→infinity. These inequalities do not identify d with D. The integer q and multiplier order are invariant under the prescribed local type. Local unit containment probabilities P(a) satisfy the **already supplied exact identity**

\[
 \sum_{\mathbf v}\frac{P(\mathbf v)}{d(\mathbf v)}=Z_S/q,             \tag{L}
\]

where d(v) is the norm of the S-supported part of an integral ideal. It allows dependence between local components; independent-coordinate probabilities are not assumed. The full packet, with all labels/signs and the actual volume weights, satisfies

\[
 \mu(E_b)=\frac1{\sqrt D\,\kappa}
       \sum_{\mathfrak a\ne0}P(\mathfrak a)\mathcal V_b(N\mathfrak a/Q),\qquad
 E_b(\Lambda)=\sum_{0\ne x\in\Lambda}b(x).                         \tag{U0}
\]

This holds for nonnegative Borel b by Tonelli, including the singular positive test below. The strongest analytic operation, with A_0=eta=1, gives for Q sufficiently large only in terms of n

\[
 \sum_{N\mathfrak a\le x}P(\mathfrak a)\le C_n\kappa x/q,
 \qquad Q^{3/4}\le x\le Q.                                      \tag{L1}
\]

The source already proves the residue bound kappa^(−1)≪_n log(3D), including a bounded-D treatment, and all local-type uniformity in (L1). No new subconvexity or local shell estimate is claimed here.

Fix 0<p<n and alpha=p/n. For every x≥1, a useful complementary **small-norm** estimate is

\[
 \sum_{N\mathfrak a\le x}P(\mathfrak a)(N\mathfrak a)^{-\alpha}
 \le C_{n,p}\frac{Z_S}{q}\,x^{1-\alpha}(1+\log x)^{n-1}.           \tag{L2}
\]

To prove it, split each ideal into its S-supported part of norm d and its coprime part of norm b. The coefficient counting the latter is at most d_n(b), even at ramified primes. Elementary summation/partial summation gives

    sum_{b≤z} d_n(b)b^(−alpha) ≤ C_{n,p} z^(1−alpha)(1+log z)^(n−1).

One can obtain this directly by summing the last factor in b=b_1...b_n and the reciprocals of the first n−1 factors. Multiplying by P(v)d^(−alpha) leaves P(v)/d; (L) now gives (L2). In particular the crucial q^(−1) survives. Dropping local weights before this step would lose precisely the uniformity in local type.

Let lambda_infinity be the shortest nonzero sup-norm in the unimodular lattice and H=max(1,lambda_infinity^(−1)). For R_0>1 use

    b(x)=||x||_infinity^(−p) 1_{0<||x||_infinity<1/R_0}.

For a fixed absolute coordinate product z, the log-Haar measure of the cube ||x||_infinity<t, summed over signs, is

\[
 B_z(t)=\frac{2^n}{(n-1)!}\bigl(\log(t^n/z)_+\bigr)^{n-1}.
\]

Consequently, integrating t^(−p) against dB_z(t), substituting t=z^(1/n)e^u, and extending the upper endpoint to infinity gives

\[
 \mathcal V_b(z)\le \frac{2^n n^{n-1}}{p^{n-1}}z^{-p/n}
                    1_{z<R_0^{-n}}.                             \tag{F}
\]

The integral used is the convergent gamma integral of e^(−pu)u^(n−2). This also explains why a product-fiber estimate is needed instead of bounding the singular observable by a fixed bounded test. On each lattice, H^p 1_{H>R_0}≤E_b because a shortest vector contributes that amount.

Split the ideal sum (U0) at U=Q^(3/4). Estimate the lower range with (L2). Its contribution is bounded by

\[
 C_{n,p}\kappa^{-1}Z_S(1+\log Q)^{n-1}
       Q^{-(1-p/n)/4}.
\]

For U<N a≤Q/R_0^n, dyadic summation or partial summation using (L1) bounds the weighted fractional sum by C_{n,p}(kappa/q)(Q/R_0^n)^(1−p/n). The prefactor Q^(p/n)/(sqrt(D) kappa) then gives C_{n,p} R_0^(p−n). If the upper endpoint is below U this range is empty; there is no hidden R_0-dependent threshold. We have proved, uniformly over K and arbitrary local types, for every R_0>1 and sufficiently large Q,

\[
 \boxed{\mu(H^p;H>R_0)\le C_{n,p}
 \left[R_0^{p-n}+B_p(Q)\right],\quad
 B_p(Q)=\kappa^{-1}Z_S(1+\log Q)^{n-1}Q^{-(1-p/n)/4}.}             \tag{UI145}
\]

Here Z_S≪_{n,epsilon}q^epsilon: every sufficiently large prime factor is at most ell^epsilon, and finitely many small primes contribute a fixed constant. Since q≤Q and kappa^(−1)≪log Q, choosing epsilon=(1−p/n)/16 and absorbing logarithms shows

    B_p(Q) ≤ C_{n,p} Q^(−(1−p/n)/8)

for sufficiently large Q. Constants and thresholds need not be explicit algorithms. Bounded finitely many fields cause no exception when q grows.

Thus full packets have uniformly integrable H^p for **every p<n**, uniformly over local types. Complete-packet convergence extends to each continuous f with |f|≤C H^p: use a slightly larger exponent p'<n, compact truncation by H, (UI145), and then let the cutoff grow. Haar integrability/tail control follows from bounded continuous truncations and the same estimate. This conclusion is a technical consequence of the supplied unfolding and local estimates plus the displayed fractional summation; it is not an effective equidistribution rate. It is not a statement about higher-rank cusp heights, arbitrary products of Siegel transforms, or all unbounded observables.

For a selected coset, domination gives the actual relative estimate

\[
 \nu_{c,H}(H^p;H>R_0)\le C_{n,p}k\,[R_0^{p-n}+B_p(Q)].             \tag{RUI}
\]

When k grows, choose R_0=k^(2/(n−p)); the right side is O(1/k+kB_p(Q)). If k≤d^delta and delta<(1−p/n)/(16n), then kB_p(Q)→0, using d≤Q^(2n). This is control beyond a **growing cutoff**, not uniform integrability at a fixed cutoff for all those subpackets. Weak convergence of fixed tests does not pay the growing cutoff test or the missing term Delta in (A). This is the exact point where this route stops short of C145.

## 4. Route two: class-character unfolding and almost-all cosets

### 4.1 A concrete arithmetic identity

Let h=|B| and let a_I(f) be the probability average over all local labels and signs above a maximal hull class I, including its real torus integration. Then mu(f)=h^(−1)sum_I a_I(f). For a unitary class character chi define

\[
 F_\chi(f)=h^{-1}\sum_{I\in B}\chi(I)a_I(f).
\]

With H-perp the k characters trivial on H, character orthogonality gives the **exact** identity

\[
 \nu_{c,H}(f)-\mu(f)
       =\sum_{\substack{\chi\in H^\perp\\\chi\ne1}}
                 \overline{\chi(c)}F_\chi(f),                    \tag{C1}
\]

and its exact mean-square version

\[
 \frac1k\sum_{c\in B/H}|\nu_{c,H}(f)-\mu(f)|^2
       =\sum_{\substack{\chi\in H^\perp\\\chi\ne1}}|F_\chi(f)|^2.\tag{C2}
\]

For a nonnegative Siegel test E_b of finite packet integral, the source's complete unfolding, now retaining the class character, gives

\[
 F_\chi(E_b)=\frac1{\sqrt D\,\kappa}
       \sum_{\mathfrak a\ne0}P(\mathfrak a)
       \chi([\mathfrak a])^{-1}\mathcal V_b(N\mathfrak a/Q).       \tag{C3}
\]

The inverse is essential: a=(alpha)I^(−1) has class I^(−1). Absolute convergence follows from the positive untwisted integral; nonnegative extended integrals can first be truncated. All local probabilities, signs, hull normalization and volume weights are unchanged. Formula (C3) is a concrete construction for the selected local-type model, not a conjectured general Waldspurger formula.

Taking absolute values before summing removes all the class-character cancellation and returns (U0)/(L1)/(L2). The exact local normalization pays the q-dependence but does not cancel chi. The principal zeta residue does not become a residue for a nontrivial class character. In particular, function-field Ramanujan in family 014 is unrelated to these number-field class-character periods and is not inserted as a premise. Moreover, one-vector Siegel transforms do not on their own determine all compactly supported tests on X_n. Even a cancellation estimate for (C3) alone would need an additional argument before proving C145.

### 4.2 What can be proved for arbitrary indices

The route has an unconditional positive partial at **every** index, without a rate. For centered f∈C_c(X_n) and its averages g_T from §3, invariance of every hull-class measure and Jensen yield

\[
 \frac1h\sum_I|a_I(f)-m(f)|^2\le\mu(|g_T|^2),
 \qquad
 \frac1k\sum_{c\in B/H}|\nu_{c,H}(f)-m(f)|^2\le\mu(|g_T|^2).      \tag{C4}
\]

The second inequality holds for **every** subgroup H, since a coset average is a further average of the a_I. Fix T, take d→infinity using full-packet convergence, then take T→infinity using Haar ergodicity. This proves

    sup_{H≤B} (1/[B:H]) sum_c |nu_{c,H}(f)−m(f)|^2 → 0.

For each fixed test and tolerance the proportion of bad cosets tends to zero, uniformly in the subgroup, even if its index is maximal. This is neither convergence of every coset nor a quantitative estimate for its exceptional set. Compactly supported cutoff tests similarly show no escape for most cosets in this sense. Bounded index then upgrades the average to the maximum, recovering §3.1.

For a fixed continuous f with |f|≤C H^p, p<n, (UI145) and truncation strengthen the corresponding **mean absolute** statement:

\[
 \sup_{H\le B}\frac1{[B:H]}\sum_c
       |\nu_{c,H}(f)-m(f)|\longrightarrow0.                       \tag{C5}
\]

Indeed the mean of the coset tail integrals equals the full-packet tail integral exactly, so no k is lost when averaging tails. First use (C4) on a fixed compact truncation and then the full-packet moment tail. An L^2 version for general such f is not asserted unless enough higher moments are available. This is a paid relative statement for unbounded tests, not an inference from weak convergence alone.

These mean estimates and (C3) are technical extensions of ordinary ergodic averaging/unfolding, not substantial nominations. To reach the **worst** coset in C145, (C1)–(C2) would, for example, be sufficient with

\[
 \sum_{\substack{\chi\in H^\perp\\\chi\ne1}}|F_\chi(f)|^2
          =o(1/k)                                               \tag{Gap}
\]

uniformly for k≤d^delta, for a measure-determining class of tests together with tightness. Current arguments give o(1) without the factor 1/k. Cauchy–Schwarz/orthogonality alone reintroduces sqrt(k) in the maximum and cannot pay (Gap). Alternatively one could pay the l^1 sum in (C1) directly. No such cancellation estimate is obtained here.

A finite character calculation illustrates precisely the insufficiency of the mean information: on B=(Z/2Z)^m, give one class value 1 and every other class value 0. The variance of singleton cosets tends to zero as |B|→infinity while the worst deviation tends to one. The diagnostics verify the Fourier/Parseval identities and this loss. This is a **finite harmonic-analysis interface example**, not a model of actual torus packets, a Galois counterexample or an obstruction proving C145 false. The concrete paid progress consists of (UI145), (RUI), (C3)–(C5); no mathematical counterexample is claimed.
