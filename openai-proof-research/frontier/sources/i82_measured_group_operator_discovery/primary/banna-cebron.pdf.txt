 Operator-Valued Matrices with Free or Exchangeable
                      Entries
                            MARWA BANNA AND GUILLAUME CÉBRON

        Abstract. We study matrices whose entries are free or exchangeable noncommutative
        elements in some tracial W ∗ -probability space. More precisely, we consider operator-valued
        Wigner and Wishart matrices and give for the associated Cauchy transforms explicit rates of
        convergence to operator-valued semicircular elements over some subalgebra. Applications to
        block random matrices with a Wigner or circulant structure and in independent or correlated
        blocks are also given. Our approach relies on a noncommutative extension of the Lindeberg
        method and Gaussian interpolation techniques.




                                          1. Introduction
   One of the first and fundamental results in random matrix theory is that the empirical
spectral distribution of a Wigner matrix whose entries are i.i.d. random variables converges
in distribution to the semicircular law. In the noncommutative setting, Voiculescu introduced
semicircular systems in his fundamental paper [37] and showed that N × N matrices in free
circular/semicircular operators form a semicircular system for any N ≥ 1. The distributions
of matrices with free R-diagonal elements and creation/annihilation operators as entries
were then studied in [22] and [31] respectively. However, for identically distributed free
entries, one need to take the limit as N → ∞ to give a nice explicit description of the
matrix distribution [30] and obtain the free analogue of Wigner’s theorem. Matrices with
noncommutative entries were also studied in [35, 5, 9, 18]. In this paper, we prove that
the distribution of Wigner/Wishart matrices in free entries with possibly different variances
is close to that of an operator-valued semicircular element over some subalgebra. More
precisely, we provide explicit quantitative estimates for the associated Cauchy transforms
which can be passed in some cases to the Kolmogorov distance.
   Another interesting topic in random matrix theory is the asymptotic spectral behavior
of random block matrices [11, 29, 25] . Such matrices are special cases of operator-valued
matrices for they can be seen as matrices with entries in some noncommutative algebra. In
particular, i.i.d. random matrices are exchangeable in the noncommutative space of random
matrices and fit nicely in the framework of matrices with exchangeable entries in some
noncommutative probability space.

   The main contribution of this paper is an extension of Voiculescu’s result to operator-
valued matrices with exchangeable entries. Indeed, we relax the freeness hypothesis and
consider the case where the matrix entries consist of a finite family of exchangeable ele-
ments in some finite-dimensional W ∗ -probability space (M, τ ); i.e. their distribution with
respect to τ is invariant under permutations. Again, we prove that the distribution of
Wigner/Wishart matrices is close to that of an operator-valued semicircular element over
some subalgebra and give precise quantitative estimates for the associated Cauchy trans-
forms.
   As applications, we consider random matrices with independent blocks, as in [11], and
random matrices in which the blocks are themselves correlated but have independent i.i.d.
entries, as in [29, 25]. In each case, we give quantitative estimates on the associated Cauchy
transforms which might lead to convergence in Kolmogorov distance.
   A main ingredient in our proof concerns an independence structure hidden behind ex-
changeability. In fact, we prove in Section 3.2 that sums of exchangeable operators are
close in distribution to the expectation of sums of independent averaged Gaussian operators.
The distribution of matrices in random operators, is then shown to be close to that of an
operator-valued semicircular element. The appearance of independent Gaussian operators
might be surprising or seem unnatural but it could be explained by the fact that invariance
under permutations is a commutative concept.
   Our approximation technique relies on the Lindeberg method which we extend to Cauchy
transforms in the noncommutative setting. This method is also known in probability theory
by the replacement trick and has been first introduced by Lindeberg [17] to give an alternative
elegant and illustrative proof of the CLT of sums of independent random variables. It has
later become widely applied in various problems and specially for establishing explicit rates of
convergence. Contributions and refinements were later carried on this method to extend it to
more general functions than sums and also for dependent variables. In the context of random
matrices, it was first extended by Chatterjee [6] to study matrices with exchangeable entries,
by Tao and Vu [33, 34] to study the universality of local laws, by Banna, Merlevède and
Peligrad [2, 3, 1] to study matrices with correlated entries, . . . etc. In the noncommutative
setting, this method was employed to polynomials in [12] to generalize Voiculescu’s free CLT
and in [8] to prove an invariance principle for multilinear homogeneous sums in free elements.
In both cases, it was used in the free case and to give explicit estimates on moments. In this
paper, we extend this method to Cauchy transforms and refine it so that we still get good
quantitative estimates also in the exchangeable case.
   The paper is organized as follows: we recall in Section 2 some basic definitions and tools
from free probability theory. In Section 3, the Lindeberg method is extended to the noncom-
mutative setting and then applied to general sums in free or exchangeable elements. Being of
independent interest, the proof of the main step of the exchangeable case is given separately
in Section 7. These results are then applied in Section 4 to study the distribution of operator-
valued Wigner matrices with free or exchangeable coefficients which is in turn extended in
Section 5 to cover operator-valued Wishart matrices. Finally applications to block random
matrices with a Wigner or circulant structure and in independent or correlated blocks are
given in Section 6.

  In this section, we fix the notation and review some concepts and tools from the scalar-
and operator-valued free probability theory.
Noncommutative probability spaces. We refer to a pair (M, τ ), consisting of a von
Neumann algebra M and a faithful normal tracial state τ : M → C, as a tracial W ∗ -
probability space. The operator norm is denoted by k · kL∞ (M,τ ) , or by k · k∞ when the
context is clear. The Lp -norms are defined, for all p ≥ 1 and all a ∈ M, by
                                               kakLp (M,τ ) = [τ |a|p ]1/p ,
and can be denoted by k · kLp when the context is sufficiently clear.
  If (M, τ ) and (N , ϕ) are two tracial W ∗ -probability spaces, the tracial W ∗ -probability
space (M ⊗ N , τ ⊗ ϕ) is the tensor product M ⊗ N of von Neumann algebra endowed
with the unique faithful normal tracial state τ ⊗ ϕ such that, for all a ∈ M and b ∈ N ,
τ ⊗ ϕ(a ⊗ b) = τ (a)ϕ(b). For all a ∈ M and b ∈ N and p ∈ [1, ∞] we have
                             ka ⊗ bkLp (M⊗N ,τ ⊗ϕ) = kakLp (M,τ ) · kbkLp (N ,ϕ) .
Finally, for all a ∈ M and b ∈ N , we let a ⊗∗ b := a ⊗ b + a∗ ⊗ b∗ .
Exchangeability. Elements x1 , . . . , xn ∈ M are said to be exchangeable with respect to τ if,
for any polynomial P in 2n noncommutative variables, and any permutation σ : {1, . . . , n} →
{1, . . . , n}, we have
             τ [P (x1 , . . . , xn , x∗1 , . . . , x∗n )] = τ [P (xσ(1) , . . . , xσ(n) , x∗σ(1) , . . . , x∗σ(n) )].
An infinite sequence (xn )n≥1 in M is called exchangeable with respect to τ if, for any n ≥ 1,
x1 , . . . , xn ∈ M are exchangeable. The tail subalgebra Mtail is defined as ∩n≥1 Bn , where Bn
is the von Neumann subalgebra generated by (xk )k≥n .

4.1. Free entries. In this section, we go back to Voiculescu’s result on obtaining semi-
circular distributions from matrices with free circular/semicircular entries. More precisely,
Voiculescu proved in [37] that, for any N ≥ 1, an N × N Wigner matrix with free circular
off-diagonal entries and free semicircular diagonal entries, is itself semicircular. This result
was later generalized in [22, Corollary 3.3] to matrices with free self-adjoint elements on the
diagonal and free R-diagonal elements (introduced in [23]) otherwise. The authors give, for
any N ≥ 1, the distribution of the matrix in terms of its R-transform under some summation
conditions on the free cumulants of the entries of each row. In general, one need to take the
limit as N → ∞ to give an explicit description of the limiting distribution of the matrix. For
the case of free identically distributed entries, a semicircular element appears in the limit
[30, 18].
   In this section, we show that a Wigner matrix with free entries, that can possess different
variances, is close in distribution to a matrix in free circular/semicircular elements with
the same variance structure. The latter is an operator-valued semicircular element over the
subalgebra of scalar diagonal matrices. Moreover, we give explicit quantitative estimates for
the associated Cauchy transforms on the upper complex half-plane.
