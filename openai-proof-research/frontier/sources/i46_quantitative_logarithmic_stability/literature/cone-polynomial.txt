   This discrete problem can be extended to the continuous setting, i.e., to
the space of all convex bodies and the corresponding general logarithmic
Minkowski problem is a cornerstone of modern convex geometry. The asso-
ciated partial differential equation for the logarithmic Minkowski problem is
the following Monge-Ampère type equation on the unit sphere: For a given
function f : Sn−1 → (0, ∞), solve for the support function h : Sn−1 → (0, ∞)
of a convex body,
                              h det(hij + hδij ) = f,
where hij is the covariant derivative of h with respect to an orthonormal
frame on Sn−1 and δij is the Kronecker delta. The Monge-Ampère equation
has a close relation to the optimal transport with quadratic cost [13].
   The cone-volumes are instrumental for computing Wachspress coordinates
[23], which define the adjoint of a polytope and thereby its canonical form.
The canonical form was introduced in the context of positive geometries and
scattering amplitudes in quantum field theory [2]. The adjoint of a polytope
appears in many different mathematical contexts (cf. [24]) and is relevant
for convex optimization [31].
   The cone-volume measure has found numerous important applications
in convex geometry and analysis. In particular, its properties have been
used to establish reverse affine isoperimetric inequalities [19, 37]. Since the
work of Gromov and Milman [17], it has become a central tool, with further
applications to functional inequalities, asymptotic geometric analysis, and
probability theory [4, 29, 30]. For its history, relevance and impact we refer

              3. Semialgebraic Sets and Cone-Volumes
  First we recall that a semialgebraic set in Rm is the finite union of sets of
the form
                {x ∈ Rm : fi (x) ≥ 0, i ∈ I, gj (x) > 0, j ∈ J}

semialgebraic set, see [6, Theorem 2.2.1.].

are parallel (cf. [34]). In our setting this implies that if for b, b ∈ Rm
