13: ### 1.1 Family 091: unrestricted logarithmic equality — selected
14: 
15: For each fixed integer n≥2, arbitrary full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex bodies K,L in R^n, and 0<λ<1, write
16: 
17: \[
18:  M_t=\mathcal W[h_K^{1-t}h_L^t],\qquad
19:  \Delta_\lambda(K,L)=\log|M_\lambda|-(1-\lambda)\log|K|-\lambda\log|L|.
20: \]
21: 
22: **Target E:** Δλ=0 if and only if there are nonzero complementary linear subspaces F₁,…,Fₘ, origin-symmetric relative convex bodies Kⱼ⊂Fⱼ, and cⱼ>0 such that
23: 
24: \[
25:  \mathbb R^n=F_1\oplus\cdots\oplus F_m,\quad
26:  K=K_1+\cdots+K_m,\quad L=c_1K_1+\cdots+c_mK_m.       \tag{E}
27: \]
28: 
29: Subspaces need not be orthogonal. Simultaneous GL(n) transformations and independent positive scalar dilations of K,L preserve the question; translations are not allowed. At λ=0,1 every pair has equality, so those endpoints are excluded. In dimension one all bodies are intervals and E is immediate. No lower-dimensional or singular-measure equality claim is folded into E.

A proof without reflection, smoothness, fixed normal fan, or nondegeneracy assumptions would materially complete the accepted inequality. In particular it would give uniqueness for normalized cone-volume data when one body is strictly convex, up to its unavoidable overall dilation. No MathDB score is assigned.

82: ## 2. A complete technical reduction: equality freezes normalized cone-volume data
83: 
84: Let C_even(S^{n−1}) be the real even continuous functions, and define
85: 
86: \[
87:  \mathcal F(f)=\log|\mathcal W[e^f]|,\qquad
88:  \nu_K=\frac{h_K\,dS_K}{n|K|}.
89: \]
90: 
91: Every Wulff body here has nonempty interior because e^f has a positive minimum. ν_K is a probability measure. The following statements require no smoothness or combinatorial assumptions.
92: 
93: **Proposition 2.1 (technical).** Under L, F is concave on the entire vector space C_even. It has directional derivative
94: 
95: \[
96:  D\mathcal F(f)[g]=n\int g\,d\nu_{\mathcal W[e^f]}.                 \tag{2.1}
97: \]
98: 
99: For arbitrary symmetric K,L the following are equivalent:
100: 
101: 1. Δλ(K,L)=0 at one λ∈(0,1);
102: 2. t↦log|M_t| is affine on [0,1];
103: 3. ν_K=ν_L.
104: 
105: Moreover, under these conditions ν_{M_t}=ν_K for every t∈[0,1], and
106: 
107: \[
108:  h_{M_t}(u)=h_K(u)^{1-t}h_L(u)^t\quad (u\in\operatorname{supp}\nu_K).       \tag{2.2}
109: \]
110: 
111: **Proof.** If A=W[e^f], B=W[e^g], then h_A≤e^f and h_B≤e^g. Thus their logarithmic combination is contained in W[e^{(1−t)f+tg}], and L gives concavity. Alexandrov's first-variation formula for positive continuous support bounds gives
112: 
113: \[
114:  D\mathcal F(f)[g]=|A|^{-1}\int e^f g\,dS_A.
115: \]
116: 
117: The contact relation e^f=h_A holds S_A-almost everywhere; hence (2.1). This is the classical Wulff variation used in BLYZ and Putterman's Lemma 2.1, not a new regularity theorem. Both-sided directional derivatives suffice below; no Banach-space inverse theorem is assumed.
118: 
119: A concave function equal to its endpoint chord at an interior point equals that chord throughout the segment. For any concave differentiable F, a tangent linear functional is a global upper supporting functional. If F is affine on [f,g], its upper support at f also contacts g. A supporting functional at a point of directional differentiability is unique (test ±εv). Therefore D F(f)=D F(g), and likewise every intermediate point has that same derivative. Equation (2.1), tested on all continuous even functions, proves equality of the even measures ν.
120: 
121: Conversely, if ν_K=ν_L, the upper-support inequalities at f=log h_K and g=log h_L give
122: 
123: \[
124:  D F(g)[g-f]\le F(g)-F(f)\le D F(f)[g-f].
125: \]
126: 
127: Both endpoints agree. The chord is therefore a common tangent and F is affine along it. Finally (2.2) follows from the contact relation and continuity: a continuous nonnegative difference which vanishes almost everywhere for a measure vanishes on its support. ∎
128: 
129: This proposition is an application of concavity, first variation and supporting hyperplanes. It is **not** nominated as a substantial theorem. Lu's Section 5 already uses the corresponding first-variation/affine-segment operation in its symmetry class; van Handel's Section 5 explains the cone-volume uniqueness issue.
130: 
131: A useful exact consequence is that, if an equality pair consists of polytopes, the **actual facet normals** at the endpoints and at all intermediate M_t are the same finite set. Indeed support of ν is exactly the signed facet-normal set, and a polytope is the intersection of its facet half-spaces. In one representative of each antipodal pair u_i, write h_i(t)=a_i e^{b_i t}. Then all facets stay positive and
132: 
133: \[
134:  |M_t|=Ce^{st},\quad
135:  \frac{2h_i(t)A_i(t)}{|M_t|}=w_i>0,\quad
136:  \sum_iw_i=n,\quad s=\sum_iw_i b_i.                         \tag{2.3}
137: \]
138: 
139: Here u_i is unit and A_i is the area of one facet in that pair. Formula (2.3) freezes the cone-volume weights, not the entire normal fan: ridges and vertices can still change. There is no facet-count-uniform lower bound on w_i for near equality.
140: 
141: E is now precisely the classification of these constant-normalized-cone-volume equality families. Equation (2.3) is a restriction, not a proof that the normals split into complementary blocks.

143: ## 3. The easy direction of E and an exact degeneration
144: 
145: For a direct sum as in E, use a simultaneous linear map to make the subspaces orthogonal. In dual block directions the constraints force M_t⊂Σc_j^t K_j. For an arbitrary u with block components u_j, Hölder gives
146: 
147: \[
148:  \sum_j c_j^t h_{K_j}(u_j)
149:  \le (\sum_jh_{K_j}(u_j))^{1-t}
150:       (\sum_jc_jh_{K_j}(u_j))^t.
151: \]
152: 
153: Thus the reverse inclusion holds too. The determinant of the block dilation is Πc_j^{t dim F_j}; consequently Δλ=0. Linear equivariance returns the nonorthogonal case. This proves sufficiency, including repeated c_j and m=1. It does not prove necessity.
154: 
155: Even on **indecomposable**, uniformly bounded bodies there is no fixed positive local gap modulo overall dilation. In R² let
156: 
157: \[
158:  P_\delta(t)=\{|x|\le e^t,\ |y|\le e^{-t},\ |x+y|\le2-\delta\},\quad 0<\delta<1.
159: \]
160: 
161: For |t| sufficiently small all six facets are present and
162: 
163: \[
164:  |P_\delta(t)|=4-(2\cosh t-2+\delta)^2,\qquad
165:  -\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\log|P_\delta(t)|\bigg|_{0}
166:  =\frac{4\delta}{4-\delta^2}\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{3.1}
167: \]
168: 
169: The bodies at t=0 contain a fixed Euclidean ball and lie in a fixed ball. A full-dimensional planar direct sum is a parallelogram; these are genuine hexagons, hence indecomposable. The slope vector (1,−1,0) stays nonzero modulo constants. Its squared norm for the paired normalized cone-volume weights is 2/(2+δ), so the curvature-to-norm ratio is exactly 2δ/(2−δ), which tends to zero. This is not just loss of coercivity in unused facets. It rules out a uniform spectral surplus on this class. It does not refute E or a stability estimate measuring distance to the full decomposable equality set. The example is consistent with the known planar theorem and is retained as a diagnostic, not a new named-problem solution.

171: ## 4. Constructive approach A: extract geometry from the soft-slab remainder
172: 
173: Fix a finite spanning direction list p_i, positive widths a_i, and slopes b_i, with h_i(t)=a_i e^{tb_i}. For even q and ε>0 define
174: 
175: \[
176:  \psi_i=(\langle p_i,x\rangle/h_i(t))^q,\quad
177:  Z_{q,\epsilon}(t)=\int\exp(-\epsilon|x|^2/2-\sum_i\psi_i)\,dx.
178: \]
179: 
180: The **exact** logarithmic curvature is
181: 
182: \[
183:  D_{q,\epsilon}(t):=-\partial_t^2\log Z_{q,\epsilon}(t)
184:  =q^2\left[\sum_i b_i^2\mathbb E\psi_i-
185:                  \operatorname{Var}(\sum_i b_i\psi_i)\right]\ge0.       \tag{4.1}
186: \]
187: 
188: The factor q² cannot be dropped. The source uses slopes −qb_i in `prop:variance`. Its tensor computation retains the nonnegative R-square and, in its constant normalized frame,
189: 
190: \[
191:  2\|S+\operatorname{Sym}J\|^2+\tfrac16\|J-J^{(12)}\|^2,
192: \]
193: 
194: as well as the ε-energy and the two Cauchy–Schwarz remainders for each summand. Density then passes the **inequality** to the centered score; it gives no q,ε-uniform inverse for A(A−1).
195: 
196: There is a precise Hilbert-space way to retain what is paid. Let a_i=Eψ_i for this paragraph, c_i=b_i√a_i, σ²=Var(Σb_iψ_i), and, if σ>0, u=(Σb_iψ_i−EΣb_iψ_i)/σ. Let (Tu)_i=E(uψ_i)/√a_i. The source's dual inequality is ||T||≤1, and
197: 
198: \[
199:  \sum_i b_i^2a_i-\sigma^2
200:  =\|c-\sigma Tu\|^2+\sigma^2(1-\|Tu\|^2).                  \tag{4.2}
201: \]
202: 
203: The σ=0 case is handled directly, with no division. For a sequence of the source's double-divergence tests converging to u in L², the scalar dual defects converge; each retained nonnegative energy is bounded by those defects. This is a useful constructive start toward rigidity, but it does not produce a limiting displacement field, Hessian, or decomposition. In particular the bounds on τ and its inverse used in the proof deteriorate in the hard-boundary limit.
204: 
205: For λ∈(0,1), put Gλ(t)=(1−λ)t for t≤λ and λ(1−t) for t≥λ. Twice integrating (4.1) gives exactly
206: 
207: \[
208:  \log Z(\lambda)-(1-\lambda)\log Z(0)-\lambda\log Z(1)
209:  =\int_0^1G_\lambda(t)D_{q,\epsilon}(t)\,dt.                \tag{4.3}
210: \]
211: 
212: Use the source's limit order: q→∞ at fixed ε, then ε↓0, and, for arbitrary bodies, then increase the finite direction exhaustion. This yields Δλ. Thus equality gives a vanishing **integrated** defect in that iterated limit. It does not justify setting the finite-q tensor squares to zero or exchanging these limits with a spectral inverse.

214: ### 4.1 The q=2 model gives an actual splitting mechanism
215: 
216: With ε=0 and q=2, spanning ensures integrability and
217: 
218: \[
219:  Z=\pi^{n/2}\det Q(t)^{-1/2},\quad
220:  Q(t)=\sum_i a_i^{-2}e^{-2b_it}p_ip_i^T.
221: \]
222: 
223: Cauchy–Binet expands det Q as a positive sum over n-element bases B of the vector configuration, with coefficients det(p_B)²Π_{i∈B}a_i⁻² and exponents −2Σ_{i∈B}b_i t. Therefore
224: 
225: \[
226:  -\partial_t^2\log Z=2\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_t}
227:            (\sum_{i\in B}b_i).                              \tag{4.4}
228: \]
229: 
230: All basis probabilities π_t are positive. Equality holds exactly when all basis sums are equal. Basis exchange implies b_i=b_j whenever i,j belong to the same matroid component: for a circuit containing them, extend the circuit with j removed to a basis, then exchange i for j. Conversely, a basis takes exactly the rank of each component, so slopes constant on each component give constant sums. Components span complementary subspaces. This proves finite Gaussian splitting, not E. It uses a classical determinant/variance operation.

232: ### 4.2 Why this mechanism fails to pass directly to q=∞
233: 
234: Take genuine endpoint boxes K=[−1,1]² and L=[−2,2]×[−1/2,1/2]. Include directions e₁,e₂,e₁+e₂ with their **actual endpoint support values**:
235: 
236: \[
237:  (h_1,h_2,h_3)(t)=(2^t,2^{-t},2(5/4)^t).
238: \]
239: 
240: For 0<t<1, Hölder is strict, so h₁+h₂<h₃. The diagonal slab is redundant, and the hard intersection is the box of area four. The three direction vectors form one matroid component, while their slopes (log2,−log2,log(5/4)) are not constant. Consequently the q=2 curvature is strictly positive but the limiting hard-volume curve is affine. This is a genuine equality pair, not a counterexample to E: its two coordinate factors are precisely the predicted decomposition.
241: 
242: The loss is quantitatively explicit. On any compact interior t-interval let ρ(t)=(h₁+h₂)/h₃<1. Let Z₀ be the product soft integral omitting the diagonal slab, and Z₁ include it, both with ε=0. With α=h₁/(h₁+h₂), convexity of |·|^q gives
243: 
244: \[
245:  |(x+y)/h_3|^q\le\rho^q\big[\alpha|x/h_1|^q+(1-\alpha)|y/h_2|^q\big].
246: \]
247: 
248: Under the product density, each power has expectation 1/q (one integration by parts, or a Gamma(1/q) variable). Jensen for exp(−z) yields
249: 
250: \[
251:  0\le\log(Z_0/Z_1)\le\rho(t)^q/q.                       \tag{4.5}
252: \]
253: 
254: At the endpoints ρ=1 the same bound is 1/q. This shows exactly how a nonconstant slope in a connected finite direction system becomes invisible. The integrals at ε=0 are justified separately by spanning/coercivity, or by ε↓0 at each fixed q; they are used as a diagnostic, not as an unauthorized interchange in the source proof.
255: 
256: Proposition 2.1 offers a repair for **exact polytopal equality**: discard nonfacet normals and use the common true-facet set. It does not give an active-mass lower bound for arbitrary approximations or near optimizers. The missing analytic construction is a coercive boundary trace of the retained squares on those true facets, strong enough to identify a single linear dilation on each complementary normal component. No such boundary trace estimate has been established here.

258: ## 5. Constructive approach B: volume polynomials and resonance
259: 
260: This approach avoids moment coordinates. Let P(h)={x: |⟨u_i,x⟩|≤h_i, 1≤i≤N}, with one unit normal per antipodal facet pair. In an open simple type chamber all vertices are linear functions of h; triangulating the fixed combinatorial boundary from the origin gives a homogeneous degree-n polynomial
261: 
262: \[
263:  |P(h)|=Q(h)=\sum_{|\alpha|=n}c_\alpha h^\alpha.             \tag{5.1}
264: \]
265: 
266: The coefficients need not be positive. This construction is part of the established Putterman/support-polynomial framework, also developed in [Hu–Ivaki v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21670), Sections 3–4. Their main theorem only bounds a **locally minimizing** even eigenvalue by n−1+1/(n−1); it neither reaches n in n≥3 nor classifies the kernel at n. It cannot be invoked as the missing rigidity lemma.
267: 
268: **Proposition 5.1 (technical restricted partial).** Suppose h_i(t)=a_i e^{b_it}, a_i>0, lies in one open simple type chamber on a nonempty interval J, and every listed pair is a facet pair. Suppose log|P(h(t))| is affine on J. If α·b are pairwise distinct for the nonzero monomials in (5.1), then N=n and P(h(t)) is a parallelotope. In particular the conclusion holds if the b_i are rationally independent.
269: 
270: **Proof.** Substitution gives a finite sum Σc_αa^αe^{(α·b)t}=Ce^{st}. Distinct real exponentials are linearly independent on any open interval (differentiate through order equal to the number of exponents minus one and use the Vandermonde determinant). Thus exactly one monomial of Q is nonzero. All its exponents are nonnegative integers and sum to n. Moving a paired support outward has derivative ∂Q/∂h_i=2A_i>0 throughout this chamber. Hence that monomial has α_i>0 for every i, so N≤n. Bounded full dimensionality requires the N normals to span R^n, so N≥n. Therefore N=n, all α_i=1, and the invertible normal-coordinate map sends P to a box. ∎
