30:                                            where hK and hL are the support functions of K and L, respectively. The log-Brunn-Minkowski
31:                                            conjecture (see Boroczky, Lutwak, Yang, Zhang [1]) states that
32:                                            (2)                                 |λK +0 (1 − λ)L| ≥ |K|λ |L|1−λ
33:                                            for every pair of symmetric convex sets K and L. Important applications and motivations for this
34:                                            conjecture can be found in [2], [3]. In particular, it is equivalent to the famous B-conjecture (see
35:                                            [7]). Note that the straightforward inclusion
36:                                                                              λK +0 (1 − λ)L ⊂ λK + (1 − λ)L
37:                                            tells us that (2) is stronger than the classical Brunn-Minkowski inequality (1).
38:                                               It is not difficult to see that the condition of symmetry in (2) is necessary. Böröczky, Lutwak,
39:                                            Yang and Zhang [1] showed that this conjecture holds for n = 2. Saroglou [19] proved that the
40:                                            conjecture is true when the sets K and L are unconditional (i.e. they are symmetric with respect
41:                                            to every coordinate hyperplane). Rotem [18] showed that log-Brunn-Minkowski conjecture holds
42:                                            for complex convex bodies.
43:                                               Recently, local versions of the log-Brunn-Minkowski inequality have been considered. In the
44:                                            paper [6] the authors prove the following fact. Let R > 0 and let φ ∈ C 2 (Sn−1 ); then there
45:                                            exists a > 0 such that if the support functions of K and L are Reǫ1 φ , Reǫ2 φ , respectively, with
46:                                            0 ≤ ǫ1 , ǫ2 < a, then (2) holds. In this note we improve the previous result; in the next statement,
47:                                            which is our main result, B2n denotes the unit ball in Rn .
