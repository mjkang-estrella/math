590:    The uniqueness of cone-volume measure is related to Firey’s worn stone problem. In determining
591: the ultimate shape of a worn stone, Firey [11] showed that if the cone-volume measure of a smooth
592: origin-symmetric convex body in Rn is a constant multiple of the Lebesgue measure (on S n−1 ),
593: then the convex body must be a ball. This established uniqueness for the worn stone problem for
594: the symmetric case. In R3 , Andrews [2] established the uniqueness of solutions to the worn stone
595: problem by showing that a smooth (not necessarily symmetric) convex body in R3 must be a ball
596: if its cone volume measure is a constant multiple of Lebesgue measure on S 2 .
