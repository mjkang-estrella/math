A well-known conjecture of Artin states that if a a is an integer not equal to 0 , ± 1 0,\pm 1 or a perfect square, then there exist infinitely many primes p p such that a a is a primitive root ( mod ​ p ) (\text{mod }p) .
In this article, we study a generalization of the classical (abelian) large sieve inequality in non-abelian settings. Assuming the non-abelian large sieve inequality, we provide a proof of Artin’s primitive root conjecture.
Further, using duality techniques, we derive unconditional results towards the conjectured
non-abelian large sieve inequality. Key words and phrases: Artin’s primitive root conjecture, Non-abelian large sieve inequality, Duality principle 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification 11A07, 11N05, 11N13, 11N36, 11N56, 11R32, 11R45, 11T23 1. Introduction and statements of results Gauss in his Disquisitiones Arithmeticae observed that for any prime p p not equal to 2 2 and 5 5 , the period of the decimal expansion of 1 p \frac{1}{p} is equal to the
smallest positive integer k k such that 10 k ≡ 1 ​ ( mod ​ p ) . 10^{k}~\equiv~1~(\text{mod }p). This is called the order of 10 ​ ( mod ​ p ) 10~(\text{mod }p) . Thus, by Lagrange’s theorem,
the period is maximal if and only if 𝔽 p ∗ = ⟨ 10 ⟩ \mathbb{F}_{p}^{{}^{*}}=\langle 10\rangle , in which case, 10 10 is called a primitive root ( mod ​ p ) (\text{mod }p) .
Gauss raised the question of how often 10 10 is a primitive root ( mod ​ p ) (\text{mod }p) but made no specific conjecture. The nineteenth-century mathematicians knew that if p p is prime of the form p = 4 ​ q + 1 p=4q+1 with q q prime,
then 2 2 is a primitive root ( mod ​ p ) (\text{mod }p) . It was not until 1927 1927 that
a precise conjecture was formulated. In a private conversation
with H. Hasse in September 1927 1927 , E. Artin [ 1 ] formulated a precise conjecture
which states that if a a is an integer not equal to 0 , ± 1 0,\pm 1 or a perfect square,
then there exist infinitely many primes p p such that a a is a primitive root ( mod ​ p ) (\text{mod }p) .
Further, there exists a constant A ⁡ ( a ) A(a) depending on a a such that N a ​ ( x ) = # ⁡ { p ≤ x : a ​ is a primitive root ​ ( mod ​ p ) } ∼ A ⁡ ( a ) ​ x log ⁡ x , as ​ x → ∞ . N_{a}(x)~=~\#\{p\leq x~:~a\text{ is a primitive root }(\text{mod }p)\}~\sim~A(a)\frac{x}{\log x},\phantom{m}\text{as }x\to\infty. This conjecture is still open, though significant progress has been made in this direction.
In 1967, assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), C. Hooley [ 9 ] proved both Artin’s primitive root conjecture and an asymptotic formula for N a ​ ( x ) N_{a}(x) and gave an explicit formula for A ⁡ ( a ) A(a) .
In 1984, R. Gupta and the first author [ 5 ] proved unconditionally that
there exists a set of 13 13 numbers such that Artin’s primitive root conjecture
is true for at least one of these 13 13 numbers.
The size of this set was reduced to 7 7 by R. Gupta, the first author and
V. Kumar Murty [ 6 ] . In 1986, D. R. Heath-Brown [ 8 ] refined further the size of this set to 3 3 (also see [ 15 ] ).
At the moment, this seems to be the best result using unconditional methods. The first author in his Ph.D. thesis [ 14 ] introduced a non-abelian large sieve inequality and initiated
a new approach towards resolving the Artin’s primitive root conjecture.
