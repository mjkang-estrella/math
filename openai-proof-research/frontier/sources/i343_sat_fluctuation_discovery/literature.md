## Current primary comparators

**David B. Wilson (2002), “On the critical exponents of random k-SAT.”** [Author preprint, arXiv:math/0005136v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0005136); [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.10050). Read Corollary 4 and its proof, together with the bystander comparison around Theorem 1/Lemma 3. This applies to fixed-clause sampling both with and without replacement and separates fixed survival levels by Ω(√n). It does not by itself identify a limiting distribution. This report does not substitute a pointwise density estimate for its stated level-separation result.

**Gaia Carenini (2026), “A polynomial scaling window for random k-SAT and a proof of the satisfiability conjecture.”** [ECCC TR26-229](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/229/); [primary PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/229/download/). The report was made public October 5, 2026. Read pp. 1–11, including Theorems 1.1/1.3, replacement/occupation arguments, the variance proof, and Section 5. Its fixed-level window is O(n^{1/2+1/k}), and its variance estimate is O(n^{1+2/k}), centered at the finite-size expectation. Its terminal-lifetime and frozen-coordinate discussions explain limitations of arbitrary-residual-set comparisons. These statements do not give a hitting-time CLT or fluctuation-scale exponential tail. The stipulated linear-variance family is sharper on variance.

**Arnab Chatterjee, Amin Coja-Oghlan, Noela Müller, Connor Riddlesden, Maurice Rolvien, Pavel Zakharov, and Haodong Zhu (2024), “The Number of Random 2-SAT Solutions Is Asymptotically Log-Normal.”** [APPROX/RANDOM primary proceedings PDF](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol317-approx-random2024/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2024.39/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2024.39.pdf); [full-version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03302). Read the model/main-result scope and Section 2.8, especially Proposition 13. Its statistic is a logarithmic solution count in the satisfiable 2-SAT regime. The normal approximation verifies small jumps and concentration of squared martingale increments using additional correlated-instance/spatial-mixing analysis. Neither that statistic nor those estimates transfer to a first-unsatisfiable k ≥ 3 process.

**Stéphane Boucheron, Olivier Bousquet, Gábor Lugosi, Pascal Massart (2005), “Moment inequalities for functions of independent random variables.”** [Primary author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0503651); [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1214/009117904000000856). Read definitions of V⁺/V⁻ and Theorems 1–2, pp. 3–5, plus the martingale comparison. High centered moments require corresponding norms of conditional resampling costs; a bound only on their expectation is not the required input. Theorem 2 supplies a possible all-order transfer if such cost norms are proved. Our finite-range transfer is also proved independently by Taylor induction.

**Martingale CLT criterion.** Brown's original [1971 article, DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177693494](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177693494) was identified, but the publisher view exposed only an iframe. The actual triangular-array criterion was read in [Péter Major's authored proof notes](https://www.renyi.hu/~major/probability/martingale.pdf), pp. 2–4, conditions (1.2)–(1.3), with the beginning of the proof also inspected. It requires predictable conditional variance convergence and conditional Lindeberg. We independently prove Lindeberg for k ≥ 4 and leave conditional variance convergence as GAP-G. The conditional CLT implication uses this standard theorem; it is not a new theorem supplied by a literature search.
