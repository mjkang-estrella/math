5. Rectangular completely bounded extremals




Let \mathfrak{A} be a finite-dimensional operator algebra with unity and
let





\theta:\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C})



be a homomorphism preserving the identity. For
F\in M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}), the amplification
\theta^{(s,r)}(F) acts from \mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d} to
\mathbb{C}^{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}.




For x\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}, let E_{x} be the smallest subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{r} such that x\in E_{x}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}. Its dimension is the Schmidt
rank of x. The compression below is the rectangular form of the
finite-level argument associated with Smith’s lemma [34].




Lemma 5.1.



One has




(5.1)

\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}=\max_{1\leq r,s\leq d}\left\lVert\theta^{(s,r)}\right\rVert.



At every fixed rectangular level, the norm is attained on the closed
unit ball of M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}).





Proof.



Let F\in M_{n}(\mathfrak{A}) with \left\lVert F\right\rVert\leq 1, and choose unit
vectors x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d} such that





\left\lvert\left\langle\theta^{(n)}(F)x,y\right\rangle\right\rvert=\left\lVert\theta^{(n)}(F)\right\rVert.



Let E_{x},E_{y}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} be the coefficient subspaces of x and y,
and put r=\dim E_{x}, s=\dim E_{y}. Then r,s\leq d. The compression





P_{E_{y}}F|_{E_{x}}:E_{x}\longrightarrow E_{y}



does not increase the matrix norm and preserves the displayed matrix
coefficient. This proves that the left side of
(5.1) is bounded by the right side. The
reverse inequality follows by adjoining zero rows and columns.




The algebra M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}) is finite-dimensional. Its closed
unit ball is compact, and the map
F\mapsto\left\lVert\theta^{(s,r)}(F)\right\rVert is continuous. Hence the norm is
attained.
∎





The next lemma is the norm-attaining finite-dimensional case of the
variational lemma of Hartz and McCarthy
[21, Lemma 4.1]. We include the short proof in the
form needed below. We use the standard automorphism of the operator
ball [23, Theorem 8.19].




Lemma 5.2.



Let \mathcal{A} be an operator algebra with unity, and let
\theta:\mathcal{A}\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C}) be a homomorphism preserving the identity. Suppose that
F\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}) satisfies \left\lVert F\right\rVert\leq 1 and





C=\left\lVert\theta^{(n)}\right\rVert>1,\qquad R=\theta^{(n)}(F),\qquad\left\lVert Rx\right\rVert=C



for some unit vector x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}. Then




(5.2)

\left\langle Rx,(D\otimes I)x\right\rangle=0\qquad(D\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})).







Proof.



Represent \mathcal{A} completely isometrically as a subalgebra of \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K}) containing I_{\mathcal{K}} and identify D\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C}) with
D\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}. For \left\lVert E\right\rVert<1, the map





m_{E}(X)=(I-EE^{*})^{-1/2}(X+E)(I+E^{*}X)^{-1}(I-E^{*}E)^{1/2}



maps the closed operator ball into itself. Let
\widetilde{D}=D\otimes I_{d}. For small t\in\mathbb{C}, the Neumann series
shows that m_{tD}(F)\in M_{n}(\mathcal{A}). Since \theta(1)=I and \theta is multiplicative,





m_{t\widetilde{D}}(R)=\theta^{(n)}(m_{tD}(F)).



Consequently, \left\lVert m_{t\widetilde{D}}(R)\right\rVert\leq C.




Since \left\lVert R\right\rVert\leq C and \left\lVert Rx\right\rVert=C, one has
R^{*}Rx=C^{2}x. Expansion at t=0 gives





m_{t\widetilde{D}}(R)x=Rx+t\widetilde{D}x-\overline{t}\,R\widetilde{D}^{*}Rx+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}).



Therefore





\left\lVert m_{t\widetilde{D}}(R)x\right\rVert^{2}=C^{2}+2(1-C^{2})\operatorname{Re}\left(\overline{t}\,\left\langle Rx,\widetilde{D}x\right\rangle\right)+O(\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{2}).



The left side is at most C^{2}. Varying the argument of t and then
letting t\to 0 proves (5.2).
∎





Theorem 5.3.



Suppose that \mathfrak{A} is finite-dimensional and
\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}>1. There are positive integers r,s and
an element F\in M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}) such that




(5.3)

r+s\leq d,\qquad\left\lVert F\right\rVert\leq 1,\qquad\left\lVert\theta^{(s,r)}(F)\right\rVert=\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}.



If r+s is minimal, norming input and output vectors have Schmidt
ranks r and s, and their Schmidt-vector families in \mathbb{C}^{d} are
mutually orthogonal.





Proof.



Put C=\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}>1. By
Lemma 5.1, choose a pair (r,s) with r+s
minimal among the pairs at which C is attained, and choose an
extremal F\in M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}). There are unit vectors





x\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d},\qquad y\in\mathbb{C}^{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}



such that




(5.4)

\theta^{(s,r)}(F)x=Cy,\qquad\theta^{(s,r)}(F)^{*}y=Cx.



Minimality implies that the Schmidt ranks of x and y are r and
s. Otherwise, the compression in Lemma 5.1
would give an extremal with a smaller sum.




On \mathbb{C}^{s}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{r}, define





\widehat{F}=\begin{pmatrix}0&F\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widehat{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widehat{y}=\begin{pmatrix}y\\
0\end{pmatrix}.



Then \left\lVert\widehat{F}\right\rVert\leq 1 and
\theta^{(r+s)}(\widehat{F})\widehat{x}=C\widehat{y}. Hence
\left\lVert\theta^{(r+s)}\right\rVert=C. Apply
Lemma 5.2 to \widehat{F} and \widehat{x}. Using
(5.4), we obtain





\left\langle\widehat{y},(D\otimes I)\widehat{x}\right\rangle=0\qquad(D\in M_{r+s}(\mathbb{C})).



Taking





D=\begin{pmatrix}0&D_{0}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad D_{0}\in M_{s,r}(\mathbb{C}),



gives




(5.5)

\left\langle y,(D_{0}\otimes I)x\right\rangle=0\qquad(D_{0}\in M_{s,r}(\mathbb{C})).






Take Schmidt decompositions





x=\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e_{j}\otimes\xi_{j},\qquad y=\sum_{i=1}^{s}b_{i}f_{i}\otimes\eta_{i},



where a_{j},b_{i}>0, and where (\xi_{j}) and (\eta_{i}) are orthonormal
families in \mathbb{C}^{d}. Taking D_{0} to be a matrix unit in
(5.5) gives





\left\langle\eta_{i},\xi_{j}\right\rangle=0\qquad(1\leq i\leq s,\ 1\leq j\leq r).



Thus the two Schmidt-vector families are mutually orthogonal, and
r+s\leq d.
∎





The first variation also gives simplicity of the largest singular
value at a minimal rectangular extremal in the following cases.




Proposition 5.4.



Let F\in M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}) satisfy
(5.3), with r+s minimal, and put
C=\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}>1.
If r+s=d or d\leq 3, then the largest singular value of
R=\theta^{(s,r)}(F) is simple.





Proof.



Let \mathcal{K}=\ker(R^{*}R-C^{2}I). Every nonzero vector in
\mathcal{K} norms R after normalization. Applying
Lemma 5.2 to the square matrix obtained by placing F
in an upper-right corner, as in the preceding proof, gives





\left\langle Rx,(D\otimes I)x\right\rangle=0\qquad(x\in\mathcal{K},\ D\in M_{s,r}(\mathbb{C})).



For each fixed D, polarization on the complex vector space
\mathcal{K} therefore gives





\left\langle Ru,(D\otimes I)v\right\rangle=0\qquad(u,v\in\mathcal{K}).



Let E,G\subset\mathbb{C}^{d} be the smallest subspaces for which





\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes E,\qquad R\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{C}^{s}\otimes G.



Taking D to be a matrix unit shows that E\perp G.
Minimality of r+s implies that every nonzero x\in\mathcal{K}
and Rx have Schmidt ranks r and s, respectively. Hence





\dim E\geq r,\qquad\dim G\geq s.






Suppose first that r+s=d. Orthogonality forces
\dim E=r and \dim G=s. After choosing a basis of E, identify
\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes E with M_{r}(\mathbb{C}). Under this identification,
\mathcal{K} is a complex linear subspace whose nonzero elements
are invertible. If it contained linearly independent matrices
X,Y, then \det(X+tY) would be a polynomial of degree r
with nonzero leading coefficient \det Y. A complex root would
give a nonzero singular element X+tY\in\mathcal{K}, a
contradiction. Thus \dim\mathcal{K}=1.




If d\leq 3 and r+s<d, then r=s=1 and d=3.
In this case E=\mathcal{K} and G=R\mathcal{K} under the natural
identifications. Since R is injective on \mathcal{K},
orthogonality gives
2\dim\mathcal{K}=\dim E+\dim G\leq 3.
Again \dim\mathcal{K}=1.
∎





The corresponding scalar functional-calculus statement is the exact
similarity amplification theorem of Aouichaoui and O’Loughlin
[1, Theorem 3.2].




Theorem 5.5.



Let \mathfrak{A} be a finite-dimensional operator algebra with unity and
let \theta:\mathfrak{A}\to M_{d}(\mathbb{C}) be a homomorphism preserving the identity with





C=\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}>1.



For every \gamma\geq 1, there is a positive invertible matrix
S_{\gamma}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}) such that




(5.6)

\cond(S_{\gamma})=\gamma,\qquad\left\lVert\operatorname{Ad}_{S_{\gamma}^{-1}}\circ\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}=\gamma C,



where
\operatorname{Ad}_{S^{-1}}(X)=S^{-1}XS.





Proof.



Choose a minimal rectangular extremal as in
Theorem 5.3. Thus there are
F\in M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}) and unit vectors
x\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}, y\in\mathbb{C}^{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d} such that





\left\lVert F\right\rVert\leq 1,\qquad\theta^{(s,r)}(F)x=Cy.



Let E and G be the subspaces of \mathbb{C}^{d} spanned by the Schmidt
vectors of x and y, respectively. Theorem 5.3
gives E\perp G. Define





S_{\gamma}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,P_{E}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}P_{G}+P_{(E\oplus G)^{\perp}}.



Then S_{\gamma} is positive and
\cond(S_{\gamma})=\gamma. Moreover,





(I_{r}\otimes S_{\gamma})x=\sqrt{\gamma}\,x,\qquad(I_{s}\otimes S_{\gamma}^{-1})y=\sqrt{\gamma}\,y.



Therefore






\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{Ad}_{S_{\gamma}^{-1}}\circ\theta\right)^{(s,r)}(F)x





\displaystyle=(I_{s}\otimes S_{\gamma}^{-1})\theta^{(s,r)}(F)(I_{r}\otimes S_{\gamma})x=\gamma Cy.



This proves the lower bound in
(5.6). The reverse bound follows from





\left\lVert\operatorname{Ad}_{S_{\gamma}^{-1}}\circ\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}\leq\left\lVert S_{\gamma}^{-1}\right\rVert\left\lVert S_{\gamma}\right\rVert\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}=\gamma C.



∎
