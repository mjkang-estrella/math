2. The numerical-range functional calculus




For K\subset\mathbb{C} compact and convex, give M_{s,r}(\mathcal{A}(K)) the
matrix norm





\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{K}=\max_{z\in K}\left\lVert F(z)\right\rVert.



These matrix norms make \mathcal{A}(K) an operator algebra with unity. Let
A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}) and put K=W(A). The functional calculus is the homomorphism preserving the identity





\rho_{A}:\mathcal{A}(K)\longrightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad\rho_{A}(f)=f(A).



Let





\mathcal{I}_{A}=\ker\rho_{A},\qquad\mathfrak{A}_{A}=\mathcal{A}(K)/\mathcal{I}_{A}.



We equip \mathfrak{A}_{A} with the quotient matrix norms. Thus, for
F\in M_{s,r}(\mathcal{A}(K)),




(2.1)

\left\lVert F+M_{s,r}(\mathcal{I}_{A})\right\rVert=\inf\{\left\lVert F+G\right\rVert_{K}:G\in M_{s,r}(\mathcal{I}_{A})\}.



The quotient is an operator algebra with unity. We use the standard
completely isometric representation of an abstract operator algebra
when a concrete representation is needed [31, Chapter 16].




The homomorphism \rho_{A} induces an injective homomorphism preserving the identity





\theta_{A}:\mathfrak{A}_{A}\longrightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C}),\qquad\theta_{A}(f+\mathcal{I}_{A})=f(A).






Proposition 2.1.



Let A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}). Then




(2.2)

\theta_{A}(\mathfrak{A}_{A})=\alg(A),\qquad\dim\mathfrak{A}_{A}=\deg m_{A}\leq d,



where m_{A} is the minimal polynomial of A. Moreover,




(2.3)

\left\lVert\theta_{A}\right\rVert=\psi(A),\qquad\left\lVert\theta_{A}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{cb}}=\psi_{\mathrm{cb}}(A).



The same equality holds at every fixed rectangular level.





Proof.



The image of the polynomial calculus is \alg(A). Since this algebra
is finite-dimensional, it is closed. Polynomial approximation on K
therefore gives
\rho_{A}(\mathcal{A}(K))=\alg(A). The first isomorphism theorem gives the
first equality in (2.2). A monogenic matrix algebra
has basis I,A,\ldots,A^{m-1}, where m=\deg m_{A}. This proves the
dimension formula.




Fix r,s and let c_{s,r}(A) be the norm of the rectangular
functional calculus on M_{s,r}(\mathcal{A}(K)). For
X=F+M_{s,r}(\mathcal{I}_{A}), the definition of the quotient norm gives





\left\lVert\theta_{A}^{(s,r)}(X)\right\rVert=\left\lVert F(A)\right\rVert\leq c_{s,r}(A)\left\lVert F+G\right\rVert_{K}\qquad(G\in M_{s,r}(\mathcal{I}_{A})).



Taking the infimum over G shows
\left\lVert\theta_{A}^{(s,r)}\right\rVert\leq c_{s,r}(A). Conversely,





\left\lVert F+M_{s,r}(\mathcal{I}_{A})\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{K},



so
c_{s,r}(A)\leq\left\lVert\theta_{A}^{(s,r)}\right\rVert. Thus, equality holds at each
rectangular level. Taking r=s=1 and then the supremum over all
levels proves (2.3). It is immaterial whether the
suprema are taken over functions in \mathcal{A}(K) or over polynomials,
because entrywise polynomial approximation is uniform at every fixed
matrix level.
∎





We shall repeatedly use approximate lifts from the quotient.




Lemma 2.2.



Let X\in M_{s,r}(\mathfrak{A}_{A}) and suppose that \left\lVert X\right\rVert\leq 1.
For every \varepsilon>0, there is
F\in M_{s,r}(\mathcal{A}(W(A))) such that




(2.4)

F+M_{s,r}(\mathcal{I}_{A})=X,\qquad\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{W(A)}\leq 1+\varepsilon.







Proof.



This is the definition of the quotient matrix norm in
(2.1).
∎





The scalar and complete ratios are unchanged by taking adjoints.




Lemma 2.3.



For A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}),





\psi(A^{*})=\psi(A),\qquad\psi_{\mathrm{cb}}(A^{*})=\psi_{\mathrm{cb}}(A).







Proof.



One has W(A^{*})=\overline{W(A)}. For a scalar function f, set
f^{\#}(z)=\overline{f(\overline{z})}. For a matrix-valued function
F=(f_{ij}), set





F^{\#}(z)=\bigl(\overline{f_{ji}(\overline{z})}\bigr).



Then F^{\#}(A^{*})=F(A)^{*} and
\max_{W(A^{*})}\left\lVert F^{\#}\right\rVert=\max_{W(A)}\left\lVert F\right\rVert. The assertions follow
in both directions.
∎
