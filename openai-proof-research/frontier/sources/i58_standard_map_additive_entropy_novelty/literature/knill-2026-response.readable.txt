Positive entropy for Standard Map by Open AI
By
oliverknill
October 7, 2026
October 8, 2026
The September 8 event in mathematics was the solution to a millenium problem. Even more brutal (for me at least) is the October 6 announcement of the solution of hundreds of open mathematics problems. It is a slaughter house! One of them is the solution of a problem I had been working on for more than 10 years: the standard map T(x,y) = (2x-y+c sin(x),x) has positive entropy for large enough c. There are 722 manuscripts there. An other problem is a solution of the Poritski conjecture: integrable billiards are ellipses. The two problems also appear on my favorite list of open problems in Hamiltonian dynamics. There are also some results about polygonal billiards. One consolidation for mathematicians is that the solutions I have looked at until now are not what one would call “elegant”. I’m frankly not that interested even in what has been written by AI. Here is a link to my javascript animation of the standard map. It contains at the bottom some remark on one approach I had tried very early as a graduate student (embed it into an entire function in higher dimensional complex space and use plurisubharmonicity). I made once in 1999 a website, after spending 2 years almost entirely on this problem. At a time, when I believed to have a solution.
An other development is a new dump repository for AI slop math papers. It is called hexagon math. This is the current garbage bin for mathematical results obtained “for free” or as one of the foundars of the project wrote: results that “bubble up”. Maybe it is a good idea to take pressure from the ArXiv, which has been flooded by AI generated papers. It is not easy to keep up with all this. For me, the fall of two of my favorite problems (the Standard map problem and Birkhoff Billiard problem on which I both worked for years) is kind of sad. The good news is that every open problem solved produces 10 more open problems. For the standard map, the solution is (from what I saw until now) just plain ugly. Also for the Poritski conjecture, there should be a better way to see this. In the first case, we still do not know what happens for smaller c. The standard conjecture is that entropy bounded below by log(c/2). For the Poritski problem, one can ask similar questions in any dimension. Assume a smooth convex billiard in dimension d has the property that it is integrable in the sense that caustics foliate a neighborhood of the boundary, then it should be special, maybe even an ellipsoid. I’m actually less confident in higher dimensions and it could well be that some higher dimensional nice symmetric spaces also produce integrable billiards. I myself worked on caustics in this paper including also some three dimensional billiards.
