1    Introduction
The ∞-Laplace equation, denoted as

                     ∆∞ v := D 2 vDv · Dv = 0 in a domain U ⊂ Rn with n ≥ 2,

is a nonlinear, highly degenerate second-order elliptic equation, particularly, one that is not in diver-
gence form. In this context, we will be working with its viscosity solutions as defined by Crandall-
Ishii-Lions in their work [19]. These solutions are commonly referred to as ∞-harmonic functions in
the domain U , and you can find the specific definitions in Section 2 of this text.
   The concept of ∞-harmonic functions originated with Aronsson in the 1960s [1, 2, 3], as he
was investigating the Euler-Lagrange equation for the absolute minimization of the L∞ -functional
                                                      1,∞
defined as F (v, U ) = supU |Dv|2 . A function v ∈ W loc  (U ) is considered as an absolute minimizer if
the following condition holds:

        F (v, V ) ≤ F (w, V ) whenever V ⋐ U and w ∈ C 0 (V ) ∩ W 1,∞ (V ) with w|∂V = v|∂V .

The existence of absolute minimizers is discussed in Aronsson’s work [5]. Jensen’s research [35]
provided a crucial link between ∞-harmonic functions and absolute minimizers. He also established
their uniqueness. For alternative approaches to prove uniqueness, you can refer to the works of
Barles-Busca [10], Crandall et al [18], Peres et al [52] and Armstrong-Smart [7].
   The ∞-Laplace equation is not only a crucial topic in mathematics but also finds applications
in a wide range of fields, including shape metamorphism, surface reconstruction, image processing,
computer vision, tug-of-war games, Lipschitz learning, and more. For specific references, you can
consult works such as [17, 20, 21, 23, 25, 49, 52].
   In shape metamorphism and surface reconstruction, the task of finding suitable reconstruction
functions is of utmost importance and has diverse applications across scientific disciplines. According
F. Peng, Y. Zhang and Y. Zhou                                                                          3


to the “equal importance criteria” introduced by Cong-Parvin [22, 23], these reconstruction functions
are solutions to the Dirichlet problem:

                           ∆∞ u = 0 in Ω; u = 0 on ∂Ω0 and u = 1 on ∂Ω1 .                           (1.1)

Here, Ω = Ω0 \ Ω1 , with Ω0 being a bounded domain in Rn , and Ω1 being a subdomain with its
closure Ω1 residing within Ω0 . In some cases, Ω1 may reduce to a compact connected subset of
Ω0 without an interior. Such a set Ω is commonly referred to as a ring domain. Additionally, in
the context of the “interpolation algorithm via propagation” devised by Casas-Torres [25] for image
processing, interpolation functions are also required to solve the Dirichlet problem (1.1). For more
precise details, please refer to Section 1.3 of the relevant literature.
   Jensen’s work [35] provides a significant insight into problem (1.1). According to Jensen, this
problem has a unique viscosity solution, commonly referred to as the ∞-harmonic potential in Ω.
Throughout this paper, we will denote this unique solution as u. Notably, u serves as an ∞-harmonic
function within the domain Ω and adheres to the Dirichlet boundary values u = 0 on ∂Ω0 and u = 1
on ∂Ω1 . Since the Dirichlet boundary values are Lipschitz continuous, it follows that u is in C 0,1 (Ω).
   In the context of meeting the regularity requirements for reconstruction functions in shape meta-
morphism and surface reconstruction, as well as the regularity needed for interpolation functions
in the interpolation algorithm via propagation, several questions arise regarding the regularity of
∞-harmonic potentials. These questions, detailed in Section 1.3 for further motivation, can be sum-
marized as follows:

  Question 1.1. Can the ∞-harmonic potential u in Ω be shown to have C 1 regularity, meaning
    that u ∈ C 1 (Ω)?

  Question 1.2. Does the length of the (partial) derivative of u, denoted as |Du|, exhibit some
    Sobolev regularity? Specifically, does |Du| have zero partial derivative along the direction of
    Du within Ω, as suggested by the equation?

  Question 1.3. Is it valid to assert that u is twice differentiable almost everywhere? Furthermore,
    what regularity can be attributed to the distributional second-order derivatives of u?

    These questions delve into the regularity properties of ∞-harmonic potentials and their derivatives,
which are crucial in various applications. Further insights into their motivations can be found in
Section 1.3.
    The study of the regularity of ∞-harmonic potentials, especially considering Questions 1.1-1.3,
has inherent significance. It not only addresses important issues but also provides insights into the
potential C 1 and higher-order regularity of ∞-harmonic functions, which is a central topic in this
field. In particular, one of the long-standing conjectures is as follows:

      Conjecture: ∞-harmonic functions are continuously differentiable, i.e., they are C 1 .        (1.2)

Notably, thanks to the ∞-harmonic function x4/3 − y 4/3 discovered by Aronsson [5], it is natural to
inquire whether ∞-harmonic functions exhibit C 1,1/3 regularity and Sobolev W 2,γ regularity with
γ < 3/2. Several significant contributions in the literature have addressed these questions, including
works by Crandall-Evans [15], Savin [55], Evans-Savin [29], Evans-Smart [30, 31], Koch wtih two of
the authors [38], and Dong and the authors [26], among others.

   A function v ∈ W 1,p (U ) is termed p-harmonic if it serves as a weak solution to the p-Laplace

In the quest for achieving C 1 -regularity or higher-order regularity for ∞-harmonic functions (and, in
particular, addressing Conjecture (1.2)), a natural idea is to establish C 1 -regularity or higher-order
regularity for p-harmonic functions uniformly across all large values of p. This would effectively
approximate ∞-harmonic functions with p-harmonic functions. However, this endeavor presents
significant challenges. While p-harmonic functions have been shown to possess C 1,α regularity and
some higher-order regularity, these results are heavily dependent on the specific value of p and are
not uniform for all large values of p. (References: [32, 34, 46, 47, 58], among others).
   Moreover, in two-dimensional space (n = 2), although Savin [55] has demonstrated that ∞-
harmonic functions are C 1 , the problem of approximating ∞-harmonic functions v in C 1 by p-
harmonic functions vp with the same Dirichlet boundary value, or equivalently, showing that vp has
C 1 -regularity uniformly across all large values of p, remains a challenging open question. The most
recent developments, as presented in [43, 26], aided by [38], have shown that for any α > 0, |Dvp |α
converges to |Dv|α in L2loc , and det D[|Dvp |α Dvp ] weakly converges to det D[|Dv|α Dv] in the sense
of measures. These findings represent significant progress toward understanding the regularity of
∞-harmonic functions and their approximation by p-harmonic functions.
   To initiate our exploration, we seek to gain insights into the C 1 and higher-order approximation
questions for a specific class of ∞-harmonic functions, such as the ∞-harmonic potential u within Ω.
For this purpose, let us consider 2 < p < ∞ and introduce up as a p-harmonic potential within Ω.
up is defined as follows:
   up is a unique p-harmonic function in Ω with Dirichlet boundary values up = i on ∂Ωi . In other
words, it is the unique weak solution to the Dirichlet problem:

                           ∆p u = 0 in Ω; up = 1 on ∂Ω1 and up = 0 on ∂Ω0 .                           (1.3)

Based on the findings of [11], we know that up converges to u in C 0,γ (Ω) with γ ∈ (0, 1).
   In the context of the previous discussions and the motivation provided, we can now address the
following inquiries related to the approximation by p-harmonic functions.

  Question 1.1A. Is it accurate to assert that up converges to u in C 1 (Ω)? Equivalently, does up
    belong to C 1 (Ω) uniformly for all values of p?

  Question 1.2A. Does |Dup | exhibit Sobolev regularity that is uniform across different values of
    p? Alternatively, does |Dup | converge to |Du| within a certain Sobolev space?

  Question 1.3A. Can we establish that up converges to u in specific function spaces with second-
    order regularity? Moreover, what kind of regularity does the second-order derivative D 2 up
    possess, and is this regularity uniform across a range of values for p > 2?

These questions aim to explore the regularity properties of p-harmonic functions as they approximate
∞-harmonic functions, providing insights into the convergence and regularity behavior in the context
of different values of p.
   In the two-dimensional space, i.e. n = 2, some progress has been made toward addressing Ques-
tions 1.2A and 1.3A as in [43, 26]. Furthermore, in the specific case where both Ω0 and Ω1 are
convex, Lindgren-Lindqvist [42, 43] have made significant strides. They have demonstrated that
|Dup | converges to |Du| locally uniformly. However, the question of whether Dup converges to Du
locally and uniformly remains an open question, referred to as Question 1A. These developments
represent substantial advancements in our understanding of the regularity and convergence behavior
of p-harmonic functions with respect to ∞-harmonic functions in two dimensions.

1.1   Main results: regularity and approximation in convex rings
The primary objective of this paper is to offer partial answers to Questions 1.1-1.3 by addressing, in
part, Questions 1.1A-1.3A within the context of arbitrary convex ring domains in dimensions n ≥ 2.
   We say Ω is a convex ring in Rn with n ≥ 2 if Ω = Ω0 \ Ω1 is a ring domain, and both of Ω0 and
Ω1 are convex. Here Ω1 is a compact subset of Ω0 . Denote by u the ∞-harmonic potential in Ω, and
for p ∈ (2, ∞), by up the p-harmonic potential in Ω. We extend u and up continuously to whole Ω0
by setting u = up = 1 in Ω1 .
   Our initial result is a comprehensive answer to Question 1.1 and Question 1A.

Theorem 1.1. We have u ∈ C 1 (Ω) and |Du| 6= 0 in Ω. Moreover up → u in C 1 (Ω) as p → ∞.
   Consequently, for each t ∈ (0, 1) the level set {x ∈ Ω|u(x) = t} is a C 1 -regular (n − 1)-dimensional
surface.

   In dimension n = 2, u ∈ C 1 (Ω) can be attributed to the remarkable result on the C 1 regularity
of ∞-harmonic functions established by Savin [55]. Notably, Theorem 1.1 provides a significant out-
come, demonstrating that Dup → Du uniformly in each compact subset of Ω. This result effectively
resolves the previously mentioned open question raised by Lindgren-Lindqvist [42, 43]. It’s worth
noting that one should not expect the uniform convergence of Dup → Du accross entire domain Ω,
as witted by the example in [43]: In Ω = B(0, 1) \ {0} ⊂ R2 , the ∞-harmonic potential u(x) = 1 − |x|
                                               p−2
and the p-harmonic potential up (x) = 1 − |x| p−1 with p > 2. Obviously, the convergence Dup → Du
is not uniform in Ω.
