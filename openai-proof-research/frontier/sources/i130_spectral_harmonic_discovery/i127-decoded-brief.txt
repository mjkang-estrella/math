All four statements avoid an endpoint assertion. Positive measure is not interior. L2 regularity is not smoothness. Constants from localization, Frostman normalization, separation, radial Lp and fixed derivative budgets are retained. Separate existential good pins do not imply simultaneous persistence; that would require a common relation or a quantitative union bound.

## 4. Route one: retain the pin in the shell reconstruction

Choose two separated positive-mass compact pieces of E carrying probabilities mu and nu with Frostman exponent `1<s<dim_H E`. This is possible by restricting a non-atomic Frostman measure to separated support neighborhoods. Finite s-energy, when needed, is obtained by first choosing a strictly larger Frostman exponent. All normalization constants are fixed before shell number N grows. In F's planar setup the pair filter can remain constant. Write `D_x(y)=|x-y|` and use its positive smooth weight `W(x,y)=D_x(y)^(-1/2)` on the separated compact product.

The useful exact bridge is the following **fibrewise monotone shell lemma**. Let (X,nu) be a probability space with standard Borel structure. Let `alpha_N,x` be jointly measurable positive finite kernels on a fixed compact interval, decreasing in N for nu-almost every x, with integrable initial mass. Let `tau_N,x` be finite complex kernels. Fix smooth low-frequency multipliers Q_N with scale `2^N`, uniformly bounded L1 convolution norms, and `P_N=Q_N-Q_(N-1)` supported in shells `A_N={2^(N-1)<=|xi|<=2^(N+1)}` (harmless fixed changes of shell widths are allowed). Suppose

\[
 \sum_N\int_X\|\alpha_{N,x}-\tau_{N,x}\|_{TV}\,d\nu(x)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sum_N\left(\int_X\int_{A_N}|\widehat\tau_{N,x}(\xi)|^2\,d\xi\,d\nu(x)\right)^{1/2}<\infty. \tag{R1}
\]

Then `nu(dx) alpha_infinity,x(dt)` is absolutely continuous with respect to `nu(dx)dt`, and `alpha_infinity,x` is absolutely continuous for nu-almost every x. If its total mass is positive and its fibres are supported on `Delta_x E`, a positive-nu-measure set of pins has positive-Lebesgue-measure distances. The constants depend only on the chosen cutoffs and the displayed finite norms. No decay rate of `alpha_N-alpha_infinity`, no smooth density and no global L2 density are asserted.

**Proof.** All convolutions below act only in t. Plancherel and the second sum give convergence of `G=sum P_N tau_N` in `L²(nu(dx)dt)`. The first sum gives convergence of `H=sum P_N(alpha_N-tau_N)` in L1. Set `zeta_n=alpha_n-alpha_(n+1)>=0`; its integrated masses telescope and are summable. Thus

\[
 Z_M=\sum_{n\ge K}(Q_{\min(n,M)}-Q_{K-1})\zeta_n
 \longrightarrow Z\quad\hbox{in }L^1(\nu\,dt). \tag{R2}
\]

Indeed every summand has L1 norm at most a fixed constant times the mass of zeta_n, and the summands with `n<=M` have already stabilized. Finite telescoping gives

\[
 Q_M\alpha_\infty=Q_{K-1}\alpha_\infty+
 \sum_{N=K}^M P_N\tau_N+
 \sum_{N=K}^M P_N(\alpha_N-\tau_N)-Z_M. \tag{R3}
\]

The first term is L1, G is L2 and hence locally L1 on the product with any bounded t interval, and H,Z are L1. Test against a bounded measurable function of x and a smooth compactly supported function of t; approximate-identity convergence identifies the limit with `nu(dx)alpha_infinity,x(dt)`. A countable determining family in t and disintegration gives the fibre assertion. Its nonnegative density is globally integrable because the measure is finite. A nonzero positive absolutely continuous finite measure cannot be supported on a null set. This proves the lemma. It is F's entire monotone-shell argument in mixed spaces, including the non-rate telescoping term; it is a **technical extension**, not a substantial result.

**What source restoration does pay.** Define the positive fibre kernels by pushing forward `W 1_(Gamma_N)(x,y) mu(dy)`, and the approximants by also inserting F's positive mask `Theta_N(x,y)`. The source proves the integral of `W 1_(Gamma_N)(1-Theta_N)` summable. Tonelli therefore pays exactly the first sum in R1, not merely cancellation in its unpinned average. The retained product mass is positive. Smooth weights bounded above and below do not affect positivity of the distance sets.

**What source oscillation does not pay.** Put `f_N(x,xi)=hat tau_N,x(xi)` and normalize nu to mass one. The exact identity is

\[
 \int_X|f_N(x,\xi)|^2d\nu(x)=
 \left|\int_X f_N(x,\xi)d\nu(x)\right|^2+
 \frac12\iint|f_N(x,\xi)-f_N(z,\xi)|^2d\nu(x)d\nu(z). \tag{R4}
\]

The first term is the unpinned shell quantity paid by F. The second is a pin-variance term. A sufficient missing estimate is square-root summability over N of its integral on A_N. Re-expanding F's graph proof does not set this term to zero: that proof integrates `(x,x',y,y')` independently. Replacing `x'` by x places the pin measure on a diagonal and destroys the asserted independent-endpoint graph input. A delta mass at x is not an s-Frostman pin probability with uniform constants.

The uniform bounded-spatial-multiplier clause also fails to do this job by itself. For a pin partition into cells Q, random signs control a sum of squares of the **integrals** `int_Q f_N dnu`; the desired diagonal norm is the limit of `sum_Q nu(Q)^(-1)|int_Q f_N dnu|²`. The inverse masses are additional losses. Normalizing a restriction changes its Frostman constant by `nu(Q)^(-1)`; omitting that factor silently creates the unavailable uniformity.

**Counterexample to the abstract unpinning implication.** Let nu have a nonzero smooth compactly supported probability density on `(1,2)`, and set `alpha_N,x=tau_N,x=delta_x`, for every N. Monotonicity and restoration error zero hold. The unpinned average is smooth and has arbitrarily decaying Fourier shells. Every fibre is an atom, however, and its shell energy equals the shell's length, comparable to `2^N`. This is an actual family of positive kernels, not an inference from a diagram. It is not asserted to arise from the Euclidean distances of one set E. It disproves only the abstract passage from unpinned shell decay to pinned positivity.

**Route-one outcome.** R1–R3 prove the full reconstruction bridge. R4 isolates the unpaid diagonal-pin oscillation. Source restoration and regularization alone do not pay it, and no new diagonal graph estimate has been established.

## 5. Route two: keep the local pin-energy cost in tube removal

This route follows the actual point where Liu's regular-pin proof uses its hypothesis, rather than assuming that an arbitrary Frostman measure has a regular positive-mass part. In its Proposition 3.3, small-mass pin cubes are removed using a covering bound coming from packing regularity. Conditional tube bounds in the remaining cubes depend on their normalized mass. Lemma 4.4 then inflates compatible masks through prescribed intermediate scales, retaining the curvature constraint on successive radii. The final proof closes a positive power after the regularity loss is made small. I read that chain through its ending. The following calculation retains the conditional-mass cost before it is discarded.

### 5.1 A complete mass-sensitive incidence bound

Let mu,nu be compactly supported planar probabilities with separation in `[a,b]`, `a>0`. Fix a partition of the pin region into half-open squares Q of side `0<r<=1`, with `q_Q=nu(Q)>0`, and set `nu_Q=nu|Q/q_Q`. Assume `I_1(nu)<infinity`. For `0<theta<1`, take in each Q a finite catalogue of tubes T of width comparable to `theta r` and length comparable to r, in theta-separated directions, with a bounded number of overlaps at each point in each direction. Enlarged oriented tubes `tilde T` of width `C theta` and bounded length cover the corresponding rays through the separated source region. Geometry constants, including a,b and catalogue overlap, are fixed.

Let G be a pair relation such that for every T the source projection of `G intersect (T times tilde T)` has mu-mass at most `A theta`. A tube is **heavy** if `nu_Q(T)>B theta`, with `A,B>=1`. Then the mass of admitted pairs lying in a heavy tube and its extension is at most

\[
 C A B^{-1/2}\,\mathcal E_\nu(r),\qquad
 \mathcal E_\nu(r):=\sum_Qq_Q\,[rI_1(\nu_Q)]^{1/2}
 =\sum_Q[rI_1(\nu|Q)]^{1/2}. \tag{R5}
\]

**Proof.** In one direction the sum of `nu_Q(T)` is bounded by overlap, so at most `C/(B theta)` tubes are heavy. There are `C/theta` directions. For two distinct points y,z in Q, the number of catalogue tubes containing both is at most

\[
 C\min\{\theta^{-1},r/|y-z|\}\le Cr/|y-z|.
\]

The angular width restriction gives this count; bounded transverse overlap supplies its constant. Integrating gives `sum_T nu_Q(T)² <= Cr I_1(nu_Q)`. The diagonal has zero product mass since I1 is finite. A union bound, the assumed source projection bound and Cauchy–Schwarz now give, in Q,

\[
 q_Q A\theta\sum_{T\,heavy}\nu_Q(T)
 \le Cq_Q A\theta(B\theta^2)^{-1/2}[rI_1(\nu_Q)]^{1/2}.
\]

Sum over Q to prove R5. No lower mass law or dimension-regular restriction has been inserted.

The source projection hypothesis has an explicit radial way to be paid at one scale. Suppose radial pushforwards of mu at nu-almost every pin have densities f_y on the circle, with
`int ||f_y||_p^p dnu(y)<=L`, some `p>1`. Remove all pairs `(y,x)` lying in `T times tilde T` for catalogue tubes whose **full** extended source mass exceeds `A theta`. For a fixed y, such extensions lie in angular caps of size `C theta`. Their union is contained in the level set where the circular maximal function of f_y exceeds `cA`. Holder and its Lp bound give deleted source mass at most `C A^(1-p)||f_y||_p^p`. Integration costs `CL A^(1-p)`. Every remaining T has either no admitted pair or full source mass at most `A theta`, exactly the projection hypothesis above. Thus the complete one-scale bound is

\[
 (\mu\times\nu)(\hbox{discarded or admitted-heavy pairs})
 \le CL A^{1-p}+CA B^{-1/2}\mathcal E_\nu(r). \tag{R6}
\]

Using the **full** extended tube mass matters: controlling the source set separately at each pin would not automatically bound its projection over all pins in T. The argument here does not make that quantifier error. Several nested scales require the union of their removal relations and the common smooth masks used by the actual oscillatory proof; R6 alone is not that multiscale theorem.

If nu is t-Frostman with `1<t<=2` and constant C_nu, the layer-cake formula, with the integral cut off at the diameter of Q, yields

\[
 r I_1(\nu_Q)\le C_t C_\nu r^t/q_Q,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_\nu(r)\le C_t C_\nu^{1/2}r^{t/2}\sum_Q\sqrt{q_Q}. \tag{R7}
\]

On cells satisfying the actual lower bound `q_Q>=c r^t`, their contribution is bounded by `C(t,C_nu,c) sum_Q q_Q`. An ordinary ball lower bound is not silently substituted for a lower bound on every occupied half-open dyadic cube. The latter can fail at cell boundaries. Liu's low-mass deletion is one way to pay the needed restriction. At t=1 the energy integral has a logarithmic divergence; R7 is asserted only for t>1.

R5–R7 are complete technical consequences of tube-pair counting and radial maximal bounds. The cost `mathcal E_nu(r)` is the precise term this attempt exposes; giving it a name does not pay it.

### 5.2 An actual Frostman measure with unbounded local energy cost

For each `1<s<2` construct a probability nu in the unit square as follows. At a **free** binary digit level use all four children with equal weights. At a **frozen** level use only the lower-left child. If f(n) is the number of free levels among the first n, put `m(n)=2f(n)`. Choose successive free-block endpoints b_j so large compared with the preceding block endpoint that `m(b_j)/b_j ->2`. Freeze next until

\[
 a_j=\lfloor m(b_j)/s\rfloor,
 \qquad L_j=a_j-b_j,
\]

then start the next free block. Choose the initial free block large enough and all b_j increasing. At every depth, `m(n)>=sn-O(1)`; the ratio tends to s at the frozen endpoints and to 2 at the free endpoints. Infinitely many free levels make the measure nonatomic. Every occupied depth-n square has mass `2^-m(n)`.

**Frostman and dimension proof.** A ball at scale `2^-n` meets only boundedly many depth-n squares, so `nu(B(z,r))<=C_s r^s`. Covers at a_j have `2^m(a_j)` squares of diameter `sqrt(2)2^-a_j`, giving `dim_H supp nu<=s`; the Frostman bound gives equality. Thus this is a genuine s-Frostman compact example, with `I_1(nu)<infinity`.

Every measurable positive-nu-mass set A has upper box dimension two: at a free endpoint its covering count is at least a fixed constant times `nu(A)2^m(b_j)`, and the exponent tends to two. It also has packing dimension two. Indeed in any countable bounded cover of A some covering member has positive outer nu-mass, hence upper box dimension two by the same count. The countable-cover characterization of packing dimension then gives two. As `dim_H A<=s<2`, **no positive-nu-mass restriction has equal Hausdorff and packing dimensions**. This does not forbid E from containing a nu-null subset of another kind; it specifically refutes the positive-mass regular-restriction shortcut.

At radius `r_j=2^-b_j`, all mass of each occupied Q lies in its one descendant of side `r_j 2^-L_j`. Consequently

\[
 I_1(\nu_Q)\ge (\sqrt2 r_j 2^{-L_j})^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mathcal E_\nu(r_j)\ge2^{-1/4}2^{L_j/2}
 =r_j^{-(1/s-1/2)+o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{R8}
\]

This survives **any fixed positive-mass restriction** of nu, renormalized to probability: within each of its nonempty Q the support has the same small diameter, and the new cell masses still sum to one. These assertions use actual measures and support geometry, not just a formal profile. One may put a smooth source probability in a separated disk, making the source radial Lp bounds harmless while preserving this pin-energy obstruction.

Thus a uniform bound for `mathcal E_nu(r)` on arbitrary s-Frostman pins is false, including after fixed positive-mass restriction. This counterexample is not a counterexample to A or D: R5 is a sufficient upper estimate that can overcount. At the concentration scales it is conceivable that an adaptive incidence argument, extra oscillation, or a new profile payment could do better. None has been proved here.

### 5.3 The remaining multiscale obligation

For `A=R^epsilon` and `B=R^delta`, R6 costs
`CL R^(-epsilon(p-1))+C R^(epsilon-delta/2) mathcal E_nu(r)`.
The gain is available when the actual local-energy cost fits that budget. R8 prevents imposing a uniform constant on all intermediate pin scales. Simply skipping a long frozen interval changes the ball-inflation ratio and its curvature/mask requirements; it is not a free deletion of a line from the proof. F's regularization does retain the full mass profile, but its two-depth payment is attached to independently integrated endpoint energies. It has not been shown to pay the diagonal-pin energy spikes in the multiscale Mizohata–Takeuchi estimate.

A sufficient next operation would be a common-mask, pin-diagonal shell estimate satisfying R1 for arbitrary separated s-Frostman source/pin measures, or a multiscale tube-removal and inflation estimate that explicitly pays costs such as R8 and still leaves a summable shell gain. These are sufficient routes, not conditions asserted necessary for every proof of A. No compactness argument here produces a quantitative power. Neither the source's `delta^-epsilon` maximal estimate nor finite exact incidences supplies this missing operation automatically.

## 6. Counterchecks, significance and decision

The selected theorem is existential. An **all-pins** substitute is false even for compact sets of dimension greater than one: take a zero-length Cantor set `C subset [1,2]` of dimension a in `(0,1)`, and
`E={0} union {r omega:r in C, omega in S¹}`. Polar coordinates on bounded separated charts give dimension `1+a`, but `Delta_0 E=C union {0}` has zero length. The isolated pin is in E; no assertion about a positive-mass set of bad pins follows. D's proposed exceptional bound is not refuted by this one bad pin.

No scale-free numerical lower bound for the length is claimed: dilation changes length, and even normalized problems retain Frostman, separation and retained-mass constants. Exact finite distinct distances do not control approximate annuli. Positive measure does not force an interval; a generic L2 function is not smooth. Three separately chosen good vertices do not supply one noncollapsed triangle with a positive joint configuration measure.

A is a real named pinned Falconer question, and the read Liu v3 statement identifies a concrete boundary of the available positive-measure result. F's unpinned shell proof and Liu's regular-pin proof contain substantial prior operations; the bridges here were deliberately compared against them through their endings. R1–R3 are a mixed-space reproduction of an existing reconstruction. R5–R7 retain a cost in an existing tube-pair argument. R8 is a classical variable-branching construction used to falsify that cost's alleged uniformity. None independently clears the project's substantiality gate. The fresh work does isolate an analytic obstruction distinct from P112 and I37, but **A, B, C and D are not proved by this report; A is not refuted**. No finding is nominated.
