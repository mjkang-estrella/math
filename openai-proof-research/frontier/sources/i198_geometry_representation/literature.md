## External primary comparisons

Exact retrieved PDF byte hashes and page scopes are in the authentication record. None of these selectively read PDFs is retained here as a full-read copy. Historical open-problem language is interpreted at the date of each paper, not as an objection to the stipulated 2026 corpus.

1. **Tim Netzer and Raman Sanyal, Smooth Hyperbolicity Cones are Spectrahedral Shadows.** [arXiv:1208.0441v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.0441v1), PDF pages 1–3, with page 2 separately reread. Theorem 1.1 assumes every nonzero boundary point is smooth for the defining polynomial. Page 2 explicitly discusses approximation of arbitrary hyperbolicity cones by spectrahedral shadows via strictly hyperbolic perturbation. Used to reject unrestricted smoothing-preserves-nonliftability, not to resolve the particular Q+δ||y||²I family. The requested v2 URLs returned 404; no nonexistent version is cited as read.

2. **Claus Scheiderer, Smooth hyperbolicity cones are second-order cone representable.** [arXiv:2509.17121v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17121v1), PDF pages 1–2; HTML introduction and definitions additionally inspected. Theorem 1.1 gives a lift with blocks of size at most two under Nash-smooth boundary hypotheses. Theorem 1.2 concerns compact convex semialgebraic sets with Nash-smooth, strictly positively curved boundary. This is a smooth-class positive result, not a quantitative size bound. The source bibliography mentions v2; the precise external version read here is v1.

3. **Claus Scheiderer, Spectrahedral shadows.** [arXiv:1612.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07048), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1118981). Abstract/bibliographic page and the search-returned §5.4 passage only; no full proof read. This supports historical context for local nonliftability methods. The I198 proof dependencies for the moment obstruction are the accepted corpus lemmas, not an uninspected external proof.

4. **Hamza Fawzi, João Gouveia, Pablo Parrilo, Richard Robinson and Rekha Thomas, Positive semidefinite rank.** [arXiv:1407.4095v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.4095v1), PDF pages 8–9. Theorem 2.12 gives lower semicontinuity for fixed-size finite nonnegative matrices; Lemma 2.13 provides bounded factors after scaling. Read as an early check that compactness of psd factorizations is established methodology. No claim that this finite-matrix result alone supplies a rate for cone approximation, or a full Hausdorff-continuity theorem for arbitrary shadows, is made.

5. **Károly Bezdek and Alexander Litvak, Covering convex bodies by cylinders and lattice points by flats.** [arXiv:0903.4842v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.4842v1), PDF pages 3–6. Theorem 3.1 states the dimension-dependent directionwise lower bound and the ellipsoid improvement; the affine invariance is described just before it. The Fubini/slicing framework is standard context for the rotational counterexample. The general-case proof continues beyond the selected pages and was not read through its ending. A mistaken retrieval of 0903.4846v1 was a different Bezdek paper, identified and unused.

6. **Christian Ikenmeyer, On McKay's propagation theorem for the Foulkes conjecture.** [arXiv:1509.04957v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.04957v1), PDF pages 1–7. Theorem 1.3 is canonical-map propagation; §5, Claim 5.1 reduces transfer injectivity to GL₂ weight spaces. The explicit determinant-times-monomial weight vectors are methodological prior art for I198's whole-power cancellation. Pages after the start of the appendix were not inspected. Neither this propagation theorem nor its proof is claimed as new; the precise I198 threshold is not attributed to it.

7. **Man-Wai Cheung, Christian Ikenmeyer and Sevak Mkrtchyan, Symmetrizing Tableaux and the 5th case of the Foulkes Conjecture.** [arXiv:1509.03944v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03944v1), PDF pages 1–4. Theorem 6 distinguishes injectivity at (5,6) from canonical kernels at (5,5) and (6,6). This prevents using equality of diagonal representations as canonical invertibility. Computations and their implementation were not rerun. The v2 URL returned 404.

8. **Claudiu Raicu, Steven Sam and Jerzy Weyman, On some modules supported in the Chow variety.** [arXiv:2108.10910v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10910v1), PDF pages 3–6. Theorem 2.3 identifies the normalization; Proposition 2.7 establishes finite generation; Remark 2.9 and the subsequent exact sequence describe the characteristic-zero finite-length quotient and the canonical Foulkes–Howe map. These passages were read through their displayed proofs. The paper's later regularity arguments were not read. This comparison supports the distinct conductor route, but supplies no new bound for its last step.

9. **Michel Brion, Sur certains modules gradués associés aux produits symétriques.** [SMF primary publication page](https://smf.emath.fr/node/43668), 1997, Séminaires et Congrès 2, 157–183. Metadata inspected; the site offers download after email submission, which was not performed. The full theorem/proof was not obtained. The statement that its effective bound depends on dimension is used only as the accepted corpus's historical comparison, not claimed as independently verified here. This limitation alone would prevent an unqualified priority claim for F210; source-operation overlap already suffices for its technical-only verdict.

## Recheck and conclusion

Searches for Foulkes canonical stabilization, the proposed square threshold, quadratic bounds, smooth-cone lifts, and cylinder-covering bounds were made before and after settling the statements. No absence of a search result is treated as evidence of worldwide novelty. Likewise the 095 form perturbation keeps exactly the strict sign tested in its accepted moment proof. The output therefore retains complete technical proofs but makes no substantial nomination.
