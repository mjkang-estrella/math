         A Smooth Nonquadratic Entire Affine Maximal Graph
                        in Dimension Ten
                                                           OpenAI

                                                  October 5, 2026


                                                          Abstract
         We construct a smooth nonquadratic entire graph in dimension ten that solves the classical
     affine maximal equation and has positive-definite Hessian everywhere. This gives a smooth
     counterexample to the entire-graph affine Bernstein assertion in dimension ten. Hessian
     positivity is pointwise; no global uniform lower bound or completeness of the Berwald–Blaschke
     metric is asserted.

    The classical problem has several formulations involving different notions of completeness.
Chern formulated the two-dimensional entire-graph problem [2]. Calabi proved rigidity in dimension
two assuming both Euclidean completeness and completeness of the affine metric [1]. Trudinger and
Wang later proved that affine completeness implies Euclidean completeness for locally uniformly
convex hypersurfaces of dimension at least two [6]. For the entire-graph problem, Trudinger and
Wang proved that smooth locally uniformly convex entire affine maximal graphs over R2 are
elliptic paraboloids [5]. In the same paper, they exhibited the singular dimension-ten example

Hessian everywhere. Theorem 1.1 supplies a smooth example satisfying precisely these stronger
pointwise requirements. The global section-based uniform convexity hypothesis in [5, Corollary 4.3]
is stronger than the Hessian positivity assumed here.
    Related equations with w = (det D2 u)−θ admit nonquadratic Euclidean-complete examples
for other ranges of θ [3, 4]. The latter range extends to θ = n/(n + 1), but does not include the
classical value θ = 11/12 when n = 10.

by a smooth strictly convex radial function with F (0) = 1. Section 2 gives a dimension-independent
reduction of this ansatz to two second-order equations for positive functions F and P . Section 3
constructs their radial solution smoothly through the origin. The main obstacle is then global
existence: a priori the auxiliary function P could vanish at a finite radius. Section 4 rules this out
using a bounded invariant region for logarithmic derivatives, and completes the proof. The decisive
change of derivative variables makes each component of the resulting vector field nondecreasing in
the other two variables on a suitable box. A single stationary corner then controls all of its upper
faces.
