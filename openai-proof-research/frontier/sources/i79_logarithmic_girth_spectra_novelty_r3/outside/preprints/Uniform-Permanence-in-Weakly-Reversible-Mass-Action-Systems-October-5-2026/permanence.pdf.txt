                                                    Abstract
          We prove the permanence conjecture for finite weakly reversible mass-action systems with
      fixed positive reaction rates. Every positive stoichiometric compatibility class admits one
      compact convex forward-invariant set that every positive trajectory in that class enters in
      finite time, even when the class is unbounded. The set depends only on the network, the
      rates, and the class; the entry time may depend on the initial state. Thus, after its entry time,
      every concentration satisfies the same positive lower and finite upper bounds throughout that
      class.


1     Introduction
Weak reversibility requires every reaction in a chemical reaction network to have a directed
return path. It is natural to ask whether this graph condition alone prevents concentrations from
approaching zero or infinity. Permanence asks for more: after a transient, all trajectories allowed
by the same conservation laws must satisfy the same positive lower and finite upper bounds. We
prove this conclusion for arbitrary finite weakly reversible networks with fixed positive reaction
rates.

1.1   The problem and main result
Let d ≥ 1. A finite reaction network consists of a set of complexes C ⊂ Zd≥0 and a set of directed
reactions R ⊂ C × C. We write y → y ′ for (y, y ′ ) ∈ R. The network is weakly reversible if for
every y → y ′ ∈ R there is a directed path from y ′ to y. A linkage class is a connected component
of the underlying undirected graph. Assign a positive constant ky→y′ to each reaction. The
mass-action equation is

    A global positive trajectory is persistent if lim inf t→∞ xi (t) > 0 for every i. These lower
bounds may depend on the trajectory. We call the system permanent on P if every solution
starting in P is global and positive, and there is one compact set K ⊂ P that eventually contains
each such solution. The entry time may depend on the initial point, but K may not. This is the
classwise uniformity in the permanence question [2, Definition 4.1].

Theorem 1.1. Let C ⊂ Zd≥0 be finite, let R ⊂ C × C be weakly reversible, and fix positive rate
constants in (1.1). For every positive stoichiometric class P there is a nonempty compact convex
set KP ⊂ P with the following properties. Every initial point x0 ∈ P gives a unique global positive
solution, and there is a finite T (x0 ) ≥ 0 such that

                                   x(t) ∈ KP         (t ≥ T (x0 )).

Moreover, KP is forward invariant. In particular, there is εP ∈ (0, 1), depending only on the
network, the fixed rates, and P , such that every such solution satisfies

                          εP ≤ xi (t) ≤ ε−1
                                         P      (1 ≤ i ≤ d, t ≥ T (x0 )).                     (1.2)

    Thus the fixed-positive-rate, weakly reversible formulation of the permanence conjecture
holds, including on unbounded classes. The uniformity in (1.2) is within each fixed class and
rate choice; the transient time is allowed to depend on x0 .

1.2   Historical context and the contribution
The structural theory of mass-action systems begins with the work of Horn and Jackson and
of Feinberg [8, 5]. A system is complex-balanced if it has a positive state at which the total
incoming and outgoing reaction fluxes agree at each complex. For a complex-balanced system,
the classical theory gives a unique positive equilibrium in each positive stoichiometric class and
an entropy-type Lyapunov function. Complex balance imposes conditions on the rates as well
as the graph; weak reversibility by itself does not impose these balance identities. Feinberg’s
discussion of weakly reversible systems [6, Remark 6.1.E] raised the question of excluding limiting
states with a zero concentration. Persistence excludes approach to the boundary even along
subsequences, and permanence adds upper bounds and uniformity over initial points.
    Important partial results developed both the dynamical picture and the geometric methods.
Anderson proved that every positive trajectory of a complex-balanced system with one linkage
class converges to its class’s positive equilibrium [1, Corollary 4.11]. Craciun, Nazarov, and
Pantea proved permanence for two-species weakly reversible systems, as part of their more
general theorem for endotactic networks [4, Theorem 6.4]. Their endotactic condition expresses
an inward-pointing property of the reactions seen in every direction. Pantea proved persistence
of bounded weakly reversible trajectories when dim S = 2, with no restriction to two species [10,
Theorem 5.1]. Gopalkrishnan, Miller, and Shiu proved permanence under the geometric condition
of strong endotacticity, which includes every weakly reversible network with one linkage class
[7, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]. Boros and Hofbauer later gave another proof of the single-linkage
permanence theorem, producing a compact forward-invariant absorbing set [2, Theorem 4.2].
These permanence results also permit rates varying in time within fixed positive bounds.
    Craciun’s toric-differential-inclusion approach constructs invariant regions by comparing
monomials in logarithmic coordinates. The revised manuscript of September 23, 2026 proves
persistence for bounded positive trajectories of weakly reversible systems, including rates bounded

above and away from zero; its complex-balanced application establishes global convergence [3,
Section 8, final remark]. The general weakly reversible persistence statement there assumes
boundedness of the trajectory. Our theorem treats fixed rates and supplies one eventual bound
for every trajectory in a class.
    The immediate input to the present argument is the affine approximation lemma and trapping
construction of [9]. That paper proves that every prescribed positive initial point belongs
to a compact convex forward-invariant set contained in the positive orthant. Such sets give
boundedness and persistence, but may depend on the initial point. We reproduce the affine
lemma and its proof in Section 2, and adapt the trapping construction in Section 3. The
additional arguments establish strict increase away from a trapping set and connect trapping
sets at different scales. They turn bounds for individual trajectories into permanence on a fixed
class. Monomial comparisons and invariant regions have the earlier antecedents just described;
the affine approximation lemma is credited to [9, Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 2.2].

1.3    Proof overview
Fix one representative c ∈ P . For 0 < h < 1, set Bh = [h, h−1 ]d . Every x ∈ Bh has a unique
representation x = (hp1 , . . . , hpd ) with p ∈ [−1, 1]d . The affine lemma constructs a finite concave

whose slopes rλ ∈ [−1, 1]d are independent of h. A label λ is active at x when it attains
this minimum. For any prescribed tolerance E : [−1, 1]d → (0, ∞), the construction ensures
∥rλ − p∥∞ < E(rλ ) for every active label, uniformly for small h. We choose the tolerance so
that every nonzero comparison rλ · v, where v is a coordinate vector or a difference of distinct
complexes, has the same sign as p · v. To preserve these signs, our tolerance shrinks near a
hyperplane r · v = 0; on the hyperplane, the zero comparison imposes no restriction. This is why
the affine lemma allows a discontinuous tolerance. When rλ · v ̸= 0, finiteness of the slope family
also gives a uniform positive lower bound for |p · v|.
    At sufficiently small scales, the labels active at c have zero slope, so Fh (c) is the global
maximum of Fh . Its plateau

                                  Kh := {x ∈ Rd : Fh (x) = Fh (c)}

is compact and convex, contains c, and satisfies Kh ⊂ int B√h (Proposition 3.1). The restriction
to P now supplies the decisive strictness condition. A member of the affine family whose slope
lies in S ⊥ is constant on c + S, so its value on P is at least Fh (c). Such a label cannot be active
at a point of P \ Kh .
     For any active label λ, order the complexes by the levels rλ · y, and separate consecutive
levels by cuts. If a downward reaction crosses a cut, its directed return path contains an upward
crossing. Monomials at lower source levels dominate those at higher levels, so one upward flux
exceeds the entire downward flux for sufficiently small h. The net upward flux across each cut is
therefore nonnegative, and is positive whenever a reaction crosses it. Summing these fluxes times
the gaps between levels gives rλ · f (x) ≥ 0, with strict inequality when rλ ∈   / S ⊥ . Compactness
at each fixed scale makes the strict inequality uniform over all active labels to which it applies
(Proposition 3.2). Thus Fh is nondecreasing along solution segments in Bh and increases at a
fixed positive rate on Bh ∩ (P \ Kh ). A trajectory in P that stays in Bh for all later times must
reach Kh in finite time and remain there.

Corollary 2.2 (Uniform activity bound). Let m ≥ 1, a < b, and E : [a, b]m → (0, ∞) be as in
