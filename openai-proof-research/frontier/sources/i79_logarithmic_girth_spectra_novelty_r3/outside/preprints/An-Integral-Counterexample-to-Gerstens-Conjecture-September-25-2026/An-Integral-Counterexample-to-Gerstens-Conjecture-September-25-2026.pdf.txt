                                               Abstract
         We construct a two-dimensional ramified regular local ring A of mixed characteristic (0, 5)
     for which K5 (A) → K5 (Frac A) has a nonzero kernel. This disproves Gersten’s conjecture in
     its unrestricted integral form.


1    Introduction
For a Noetherian regular local domain A with fraction field F , Gersten’s conjecture asserts
exactness of the canonical augmented residue complex
                                                        M
                     0 −→ Kn (A) −→ Kn (F ) −→                  Kn−1 (κ(p)) −→ · · · .                 (1.1)
                                                       ht p=1

Here n ≥ 0, the groups are integral Quillen K-groups, and the residues come from localization
and the codimension filtration. The first assertion is generic injectivity: a class that vanishes
after passage to the fraction field must already vanish on A. We construct a counterexample to
this assertion.

Theorem 1.1. Let V be the ring of integers in the unramified extension of Q5 of degree eight,
and put
                           A = V [x, y]/(5 + x4 + y 4 ) (5,x,y) .
                                                       

Then A is a Noetherian regular local domain of dimension two and
                                                                   
                                  ker K5 (A) −→ K5 (Frac A) ̸= 0.

Consequently the unrestricted integral Gersten conjecture has a negative answer.

    The maximal ideal of A is (x, y), and 5 ∈ (x, y)4 . Its special fiber has dimension one and
embedding dimension two at the closed point. Thus the regular total ring is ramified and is not
smooth over V . This distinction is central both to the example and to its relation with positive
results on Gersten’s conjecture.




                                                   1
History and related results. Gersten’s questions about transfer and local higher K-theory
[6], together with his work on exact sequences [7], place the conjecture in the study of localization
and residue maps. Quillen formulated it for arbitrary regular local rings and proved it for
semilocalizations of finite-type algebras over a field at regular primes [18, §7, Conjecture 5.10
and Theorem 5.11]. Panin established the full equicharacteristic case [15, Theorem A].
     In mixed characteristic, Gillet and Levine’s relative theorem reduces the smooth local case
to the associated discrete valuation ring case [8]. Geisser and Levine prove finite-coefficient
Gersten exactness for semilocal rings essentially smooth over a discrete valuation ring, for every
finite coefficient order, including powers of the residue characteristic [5, Theorem 8.2]; their
text credits B. Kahn for pointing out this consequence. Skalit proved finite-coefficient Gersten
exactness for arbitrary unramified regular local rings, again for every finite coefficient order [20,
Corollary 5.7]. In mixed characteristic (0, p), unramified means p ∈ / m2 ; our ring is excluded since
5 ∈ m4 . Version 1 of Druzhinin’s preprint states a result for essentially smooth local Henselian
schemes over a discrete valuation ring [1, Theorem 4.5]. The smoothness hypotheses exclude the
structure map V → A of Theorem 1.1.
     Feld’s finite-coefficient example is a close methodological predecessor [2, Theorem 1.1 and
Corollary 5.1]. It gives a degree-two augmentation kernel modulo three at a ramified regular
local ring whose integral K2 map is injective. Its supported pushforward, character averaging,
cyclotomic scalar extension, conjugate splitting and divisor-class detection also organize our
construction. In that example, an integral lift of the coefficient kernel has nonzero but three-
divisible generic image, as follows from integral injectivity and the coefficient exact sequence.
An integral generic kernel therefore requires an additional argument. Here a vanishing result
for integral K4 permits a cyclotomic coefficient class to lift to integral K5 . Two Bott factors
contribute the square of the cyclotomic character, whose signs are realized by an elliptic quotient
of the Fermat quartic.
     Our companion paper, An integral degree-three Gersten counterexample [14, Theorem 1.1],
constructs an integral K3 kernel at a different ramified ring: the local ring at the special point
[0 : 0 : 1] of the cyclotomic quintic model (X − Z)5 − XY 4 + Y 5 + ζZ 5 = 0 over the integers
of Q5 (ζ), where ζ is a primitive fifth root of unity. Its proof uses a Steinberg relation and a
norm valuation test on coherent K-theory [14, Lemma 4.2, Proposition 3.1, and §5]. Here the
global arithmetic lift, smooth Picard specialization criterion and quartic construction are proved
directly. Neither proof uses results of the other paper, and neither determines a smallest degree
of failure.
     Our proof detects the reduction modulo five of an integral class already killed over the fraction
field. It therefore also gives generic noninjectivity with coefficients Z/5 for the same ring. This
detection does not determine the order of the integral class or whether its rationalization is
nonzero. The conclusion concerns the unrestricted integral conjecture and does not settle its
rational-coefficient variant.

2    Coefficients and Bott multiplication
Our first goal is to control multiplication by a chosen coefficient class, including its interaction
with localization. These operations have two uses. In the arithmetic construction they carry
surjectivity of a valuation map to the degree needed to remove the integral lifting obstruction.
In the geometric argument they translate a relation among supported coefficient classes into
ordinary tame symbols.

A     A claimed vanishing theorem for principal divisors
Mochizuki’s Theorem 5 in version 8 of [13] asserts that, for a commutative Noetherian local
ring B and a nonzerodivisor g, the inclusion from finitely generated projective B/gB-modules to
finitely generated B-modules of projective dimension at most one induces zero on K-theory. This
statement reaches the exact support in the present paper: Lemma 6.1 gives B = A, g = Y0 x−X0 y,
and A/gA = O, with g a regular parameter. Composing the claimed zero map with the inclusion
into coherent A-modules would annihilate i∗ ce.
     We give a counterexample to the functor assertion in [13, Lemma 13(III)], which is used
in that proof. The counterexample already has both B and B/gB regular. It concerns the
supplied proof of the vanishing assertion; a failure of this construction alone does not disprove
the assertion itself. The proof of Theorem 1.1 above is independent of it.
