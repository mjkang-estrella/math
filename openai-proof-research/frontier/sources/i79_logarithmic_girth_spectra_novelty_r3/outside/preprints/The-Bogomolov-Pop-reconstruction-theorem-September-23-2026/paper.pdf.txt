                                                Abstract
        We prove the Bogomolov–Pop reconstruction conjecture for function fields of transcendence
     degree at least two over arbitrary algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from ℓ.
     The pro-ℓ abelian-by-central datum determines the perfect closure and its constant field, with
     precisely the Frobenius and ℓ-adic unit ambiguities.

1    The reconstruction problem
Birational anabelian geometry asks how much of a field can be read from its Galois groups. Over
algebraically closed constants, the arithmetic Galois action disappears. Bogomolov’s program
proposes that a particularly small quotient still remembers every function field of dimension at least


                                                     1
two: the quotient in which commutators are central. We prove the Isom form of this assertion,
including surfaces and the prime 2.

1.2    Previous work and the proof
Bogomolov introduced the abelian-by-central approach to birational reconstruction [Bog91]. The
local approach detects valuations through commuting elements and their associated multiplicative
characters [BT02, Top17]. Bogomolov and Tschinkel proved the surface case over algebraic closures
of finite fields [BT08], followed by higher-dimensional reconstruction [BT11]; Pop established a
corresponding birational reconstruction theorem [Pop12a]. The precise Isom formulation, including
its scalar and Frobenius ambiguities, is recorded in [Top16, Introduction, Conjecture 1].

                                                     2
    Pop’s global reconstruction theorem recovers a function field from its total decomposition graph
together with suitable rational quotients [Pop12b, Pop12a]. His later reconstruction theorem with
divisorial inertia applies in relative transcendence degree greater than two [Pop18, Theorem 1.1]; its
additional inertia datum must itself be recovered to solve the conjecture from the group alone. The
local theory of commuting pairs supplies quasi-divisorial valuations, which can be nontrivial on the
constants [Pop18, Top19]. Pop’s minimized decomposition theory also recognizes certain residual
curves over algebraic closures of finite fields, with Topaz’s appendix treating the required residue
characteristic ℓ case [Pop15].
    Related reconstruction results use additional structure. Silberstein studies the full absolute Galois
group under a prime-field-closure hypothesis [Sil13]. Topaz’s integral cohomological reconstruction
uses a specified arithmetic Galois action in transcendence degree at least three [Top19, Theorems A
and B]; his Torelli-type theorem uses mixed Hodge structures and cup products over constants
embedded in C [Top25]. These results concern different input data. Here only the original pro-
ℓ abelian-by-central datum is supplied, and neither the constant field nor geometric inertia is
distinguished in advance.

described above.

3     Recovering the local data
The commutator detects a relation between multiplicative characters. That relation recovers quasi-
prime divisors, including those that value constants nontrivially. At this stage their residue fields
may have characteristic ℓ. We therefore work with character groups throughout and use a Galois
interpretation only at the top field. For the commuting-pair approach to valuation detection and its
development via rigid elements, see [Bog91, BT02, Top17]. We use the character-space formulation
below to include the required residue fields.

3.2   Character groups of valuations
We regard equivalent valuations as the same valuation. Write v ≤ w if v is a coarsening of w. For a
valuation v of F , let Ov , mv , F v and vF denote its valuation ring, maximal ideal, residue field and
value group. Set

                  Uv = Ov× ,       Uv1 = 1 + mv ,       Iv = Uv⊥ ⊂ Dv = (Uv1 )⊥ ⊂ WF ,

and write Iv = Iv ⊗ Qℓ and Dv = Dv ⊗ Qℓ . These are the minimized inertia and decomposition
groups. When both F and F v have characteristic different from ℓ, they agree with the usual abelian
inertia and decomposition groups. Their definitions above impose no characteristic restriction.
    A subset of GF is valuative if it is contained in Iv for some valuation v. We use the following
local character theorem [Top19, Fact 2.1, Lemma 2.5 and Corollary 2.7]:

   1. Every valuative subset Σ has a unique coarsest valuation vΣ with Σ ⊂ IvΣ . It coarsens every
      valuation with this property.

   2. In an alternating subspace S, the valuative elements form a valuative subspace Σ of codimension
      at most one, and S ⊂ DvΣ .

3.3    An intrinsic description of quasi-prime divisors
The next argument follows the character-space proof of [Top19, Fact 3.2 and Theorem 3.3]. We
include it because we need it also in residue characteristic ℓ, whereas that section of the cited paper
is stated in a cohomological setting with characteristic different from ℓ.

Lemma 3.3. For F/κ as above, in any characteristic, s is the maximum dimension of an alternating
subspace of GF . If S has dimension s, its valuative subspace is Iu for its associated valuation u,
and u attains equality in (4).

Proof. For every valuation u, characters kill the divisible constant group, so

                                       dimQℓ Iu ≤ rrank(uF/uκ).

Let S be alternating and let Σ be its valuative subspace, with associated valuation u. If S = Σ, the
asserted dimension bound follows from (4). Otherwise Σ has codimension one in S. A character in
S \ Σ ⊂ Du has nonzero residual character. Thus F u ̸= κu, and trdeg(F u/κu) ≥ 1. Consequently

                                    dim S ≤ rrank(uF/uκ) + 1 ≤ s.

This also bounds arbitrary alternating subspaces, by applying the argument to their finite-dimensional
subspaces.
   There is a full discrete flag at a smooth closed point on a model of F/κ. Its inertia space has
dimension s and is alternating by the valuation inequality for x and 1 − x. Thus the maximum
equals s.
   If dim S = s and S = Σ, all inequalities

                                    s ≤ dim Iu ≤ rrank(uF/uκ) ≤ s

are equalities. If S ̸= Σ, all inequalities

                    s − 1 ≤ dim Iu ≤ rrank(uF/uκ) ≤ s − trdeg(F u/κu) ≤ s − 1

are equalities. In both cases Σ = Iu and u has no transcendence defect.

Lemma 6.4. Let M/P be a regular field extension, let q be prime to the characteristic, and suppose
µq ⊂ P . If g ∈ M × becomes a q-th power in M P , then its class in M × /M ×q comes from a unique
class in P × /P ×q .

Proof. A q-th root r of g is separable over M , so it already belongs to M P sep : the remaining
constant extension is purely inseparable. Choose a finite Galois extension P ′ /P with r ∈ M P ′ .

this group. By Hilbert’s Theorem 90, there is b ∈ P ′× with these same ratios. Then r/b ∈ M and
bq ∈ P , giving g = bq (r/b)q . Finally, if an element of P has a q-th root in M , that root is algebraic
over P and therefore belongs to P . This proves uniqueness.

7     From the recovered quotients to the field
We have recovered the geometric data needed for the last step. We now specify that data and Pop’s
reconstruction theorems, so that their application uses exactly the original group input.
    For a function field F/κ, a divisorial flag is a sequence of valuations obtained by taking a prime
divisor trivial on κ, then a prime divisor on its residue field, and continuing in this way. The empty
flag is included. The total decomposition graph records, at each such flag, the abelian pro-ℓ group of
the residue field, and at each next divisorial valuation its inertia and decomposition subgroups and
the quotient map to the next residue group. Thus the graph retains the group maps as well as the
flags. At a one-variable field over algebraically closed constants, its terminal data are precisely the
point inertia lines: the residual groups at closed points vanish, so point decomposition equals point
inertia.
    We use the following two results of Pop.

Proposition 7.1 (Pop’s reconstruction theorems). Let F/κ and F ′ /κ′ be function fields of tran-
scendence degree greater than one over algebraically closed fields of characteristic different from
ℓ.

    1. From ΠcF → ΠaF and the union of its true divisorial inertia groups, one recovers the total
       decomposition graph, functorially under class-two isomorphisms whose abelianizations preserve
       this union.

    2. Suppose an isomorphism between the total decomposition graphs of F and F ′ is compatible
       with all their geometric rational quotient diagrams. Then its map on the top abelian groups is
       induced, up to one unit in Zℓ , by an isomorphism of perfect closures in the opposite direction
       carrying the constant fields onto each other. That field isomorphism is unique modulo Frobenius
       twists in positive characteristic, and unique in characteristic zero.

In (2), a geometric rational quotient is restriction to κ(u) ⊂ F with F/κ(u) regular, together with the
point inertia and decomposition data on its target. Compatibility means that restriction commutes
with the graph isomorphisms and the induced isomorphisms of these targets.

Proof of Theorem 1.1. A field isomorphism α : K i → Li induces a contravariant isomorphism of
absolute Galois groups, well defined up to an inner automorphism. Purely inseparable extension does
not change these groups. Abelianization removes the inner ambiguity, and the induced class-two
map preserves the commutator. Thus it gives a member of Isomc (ΠaL , ΠaK ). In positive characteristic,
absolute Frobenius on algebraic closures commutes with field automorphisms; composition with
any integer Frobenius power therefore gives the same allowed orbit. This proves well-definedness of
ΦK,L .
    Conversely, let φ : ΠaL → ΠaK be bracket-compatible. Lemma 2.3 lifts it to a class-two iso-
morphism. After choosing Tate identifications, let Θ be its dual (1). Proposition 3.5 recovers the
common relative transcendence degree and the quasi-divisorial pairs. The finite residual tests and
the uniform support bound prove the completed curve-subfield correspondence in Theorem 6.1. The
recognition statements in Section 6 then show that φ preserves true divisorial inertia and every
relatively algebraically closed rational quotient, including its point lines.
    Proposition 6.7 gives the actual commuting quotient diagrams in (14). Their vertical maps are
surjective, and their target isomorphisms φE match all point inertia lines. For rational targets these
are the entire point decomposition data. Thus the same fixed φ is compatible with every geometric
rational quotient; no independent rescaling of the targets is needed.
    Part (1) of Proposition 7.1 gives the total decomposition-graph isomorphism. The quotient
squares also commute on these graphs. Indeed, restriction along an actual regular inclusion
E = k(t) ⊂ K is a geometric rational quotient morphism [Pop12a, Section 2, Embeddings and
Restrictions]. At a flag vertex its map is obtained by restricting to the decomposition subgroup
and passing to the inertia quotient [Pop12b, Definition/Remark 24 and Definition 25]. If the flag
restricts trivially to E, its inertia is killed, and the top square descends to the residual square.
Otherwise its nonzero inertia image lies in a unique point line of E, where decomposition equals
inertia and the residue group is trivial. The same description applies to P ⊂ L. Local images can
have finite index: saturation identifies the point line, but the actual subgroup images and maps are
retained. Applying these restriction and quotient operations successively along each flag therefore
proves compatibility for the same fixed φ and φE .
    Part (2), applied to the recovered rational quotients, gives an isomorphism α : K i → Li with
α(k) = l and [φ] = [α∗ ]. Choices of Tate identifications are absorbed by the one allowed global unit.
This proves surjectivity. It also proves that different characteristics cannot give a bracket-compatible
isomorphism.

identifies them modulo Frobenius in positive characteristic, and identifies them outright in charac-

the definition of IsomiF (K, L). This proves injectivity and completes the theorem.
