                  Bloch’s conjecture for surfaces with pg = 0

                                               Abstract
        We prove Bloch’s conjecture for smooth connected projective complex surfaces with pg = 0:
     the Albanese homomorphism on integral degree-zero zero-cycles is an isomorphism.


1    Introduction
For a smooth connected projective complex surface S, write

                             pg (S) = h0 (S, ωS ),       q(S) = h1 (S, OS ).

The Chow group CH0 (S) is the group of integral zero-cycles modulo rational equivalence, where the
relations are generated by divisors of rational functions on curves in S. The degree of a complex
point is one, and CH0 (S)0 denotes the kernel of the degree map. The Albanese homomorphism
albS : CH0 (S)0 → Alb(S)(C) is surjective. Bloch’s conjecture predicts that it is an isomorphism
when pg (S) = 0. We prove the conjecture. The main theorem treats the case q = 0, when
the Albanese variety is a point and the prediction is that any two complex points are rationally
equivalent.
Theorem 1.1. Let S be a smooth connected projective surface over C with pg (S) = q(S) = 0. Then

                                         deg : CH0 (S) −→ Z

is an isomorphism, where CH0 (S) has integral coefficients.
  Theorem 1.1 imposes no minimality, fundamental-group, or surface-construction hypothesis.
Combining it with the classical non-general-type case gives the full statement.
Corollary 1.2 (Bloch’s conjecture for surfaces with pg = 0). Let S be a smooth connected projective
surface over C with pg (S) = 0. Then the Albanese homomorphism

                                   albS : CH0 (S)0 −→ Alb(S)(C)

is an isomorphism of abelian groups, where CH0 (S) has integral coefficients.
   For a surface of general type, pg = 0 forces q = 0, so Theorem 1.1 applies. The other cases are
the theorem of Bloch–Kas–Lieberman [BKL76]. We give the precise reduction in Section 8.1.
   These zero-cycle statements also have consequences for rational Chow motives. For pg = q = 0,
every rational Chow class on a power of S or a Hilbert scheme of points is determined by its
cohomology class. We state the precise consequences and their motivic antecedents after the proof
overview.

                                                     1
History and antecedents
Mumford showed that a nonzero holomorphic two-form prevents the zero-cycles on a surface from
admitting a finite-dimensional parametrization modulo rational equivalence [Mum69]. Bloch’s
conjecture predicts the converse at the level of the Albanese kernel: if pg = 0, degree and the
Albanese image should account for all rational-equivalence classes of zero-cycles [Blo10]. Bloch, Kas,
and Lieberman proved this integral assertion for every surface of Kodaira dimension less than two
[BKL76, pp. 135–136]. Thus the remaining general-type problem concerns pg = q = 0, where the
Albanese variety carries no information and the degree map is predicted to determine a zero-cycle
completely.
    Several successful approaches use additional geometry of the surface. The group-action method
of Inose–Mizukami [IM79] and its extension by Barlow [Bar85] compare zero-cycles with their
images on suitable quotient surfaces. Bauer’s treatment of Inoue surfaces with K 2 = 7 gives a later
application, expressed through relations among the quotient correspondences [Bau14, Theorem 0.2
and Section 1]. Pedrini–Weibel recast involution arguments using the transcendental motive: for
a bidouble cover, vanishing of the transcendental motives of the three intermediate quotients
forces its own vanishing [PW16, Theorem 2.5]. Their applications include numerical Godeaux and
classical Campedelli examples, Keum–Naie, Burniat, and Inoue surfaces, giving examples for each
1 ≤ K 2 ≤ 8.
    A different route uses families of surfaces and the actions of correspondences on zero-cycles. For
a family of regular surfaces whose fibered self-product has a rationally connected smooth projective
compactification, Voisin shows that a relative correspondence acting trivially on holomorphic two-
forms acts nilpotently on degree-zero zero-cycles. She applies this theorem to Catanese surfaces
[Voi14, Theorems 0.3 and 0.6]. Passing to suitable quotients then gives Bloch’s conjecture for Barlow
surfaces. This extends the earlier result for Barlow surfaces carrying extra symmetries to the general
members of that family. The present argument requires no prescribed family, covering construction,
or automorphism of the surface.
    Recent preprints propose proofs with overlapping scope. Guletskiı̆’s Theorem A assumes
algebraicity of total cohomology and claims vanishing of the Albanese kernel over algebraically
closed fields of characteristic zero [Gul25, Theorem A and Section 2.1]. Over C, this cohomological
assumption is equivalent to pg = q = 0, by Hodge theory and the Lefschetz (1, 1) theorem, so it
covers the full scope of Theorem 1.1. His proposed argument studies a hypersurface section through
the diagonal in the square of the surface, together with a nilpotence argument for correspondences.
Banerjee proposes a separate proof for minimal general-type surfaces with pg = q = 0 and K 2 = 9,
using pluricanonical curves and their Jacobians [Ban26, Theorems 1.1 and 2.1]. We cite these as
proposed proofs; the argument developed here obtains its Chow relation from virtual localization on
a Quot scheme.
    The use of the diagonal to control zero-cycles has its antecedents in Bloch–Srinivas [BS83].
Surface Quot obstruction theories and localization relations were developed by Marian–Oprea–
Pandharipande [MOP17], with subsequent virtual intersection theory in [OP21] and its K-theoretic
counterpart in [AJLOP21]. Ciocan-Fontanine–Kapranov constructed derived Quot schemes [CFK01].
We use the derived moduli and determinant constructions of Toën–Vaquié and Schürg–Toën–Vezzosi
[TV07, STV15] to impose the determinant constraint. The Chow calculation adapts the nested-
Hilbert-scheme recursion of Ellingsrud–Göttsche–Lehn [EGL01] by leaving two surface factors
unintegrated throughout. This is the step that produces a correspondence identity rather than only
a numerical intersection formula.

The mechanism of the proof
The rational cohomology of a surface with pg = q = 0 is generated by algebraic classes, but
cohomological identities alone do not settle rational equivalence. After the classical and birational
reductions, we work on a minimal surface of general type or a point blowup X of that surface. The
objective is a relation
                                0 = c[ΔX ] + Γ in CH2 (X × X)Q ,                                 (1.1)
where c ̸= 0 and Γ is a sum of external products of cycles. The diagonal acts as the identity on
zero-cycles, whereas every external product of total codimension two kills degree-zero zero-cycles.
Thus the relation gives CH0 (X)0 ⊗ Q = 0. Constructing this Chow relation and detecting its
coefficient are the two main stages.
    For line bundles L1 , L2 , D, consider rank-zero quotients of L1 ⊕ L2 whose determinant has Picard
class D. Opposite torus weights on the summands give a localization formula. We construct its
obstruction theory and the compatible fixed theory using a derived determinant fiber normalized at
a point of X (Sections 3 and 4). A fixed quotient splits into a quotient of each line bundle, each
described by an effective divisor and a zero-dimensional subscheme. The divisor line bundles T, H
satisfy T + H = D; on an irregular surface this determinant condition also relates their Picard
parameters.
    Two second-Chern-character insertions of the universal quotient leave a class on X × X. We
choose the virtual dimension so that equivariant degree forces its codimension-two part to vanish. To
calculate it in Chow, we retain both output copies of X throughout the Hilbert-scheme recursion of
Ellingsrud–Göttsche–Lehn [EGL01]. The recursion replaces the point incidence classes by diagonals
identifying surface coordinates. Terms joining both outputs contribute a multiple of [ΔX ]; terms
with separated outputs contribute external products. For each fixed fiber expression, this procedure
chooses its diagonal coefficient from the intersection numbers of its line bundles and the Chern
numbers of the surface. The total c is the weighted sum over the fixed contributions that occur
(Section 5). The rule does not require a diagonal decomposition on an individual surface to be
unique.
    For a minimal general-type surface with pg = q = 0 and canonical class K, one has b2 = 10 − K 2 .
When K 2 ≤ 8, its lattice has a direction orthogonal to the canonical class. A lattice construction
and at most three point blowups provide a class α with α2 < 0 and Quot data with the following
property. For each contributing fixed pair with αH = 0, pairing with α ⊠ α kills its external terms.
Every remaining contributing pair has zero point lengths, so its chosen diagonal coefficient is zero,
while its pairing is (αH)2 . Consequently the full relation gives

Motivic consequences
The full zero-cycle statement also has consequences for rational Chow motives. Write h(Y )Q for the
rational Chow motive of a smooth projective complex variety Y , and t2 (S) for the transcendental
degree-two summand defined by Kahn–Murre–Pedrini. Let L denote the effective Lefschetz motive,
whose Betti realization is Q(−1) in degree two. A rational Chow motive is pure Tate if it is a finite
direct sum of powers of L.

Corollary 1.3 (Motivic consequences). Let S be a smooth connected projective surface over C with
pg (S) = 0. Then h(S)Q is finite-dimensional in the sense of Kimura and t2 (S) = 0.
    If, in addition, q(S) = 0, then

                                     h(S)Q ≃ 1 ⊕ L⊕b2 (S) ⊕ L2 .

In this case, for every m, n ≥ 0, the rational Chow motives of the power S m and the Hilbert scheme
S [n] of length-n zero-dimensional subschemes are pure Tate, with S 0 = S [0] = Spec C. For Y = S m
or Y = S [n] , the rational cycle-class maps satisfy
                                ∼
               cliY : CHi (Y )Q −−→ H 2i (Y, Q),   H 2i+1 (Y, Q) = 0   for every i ≥ 0.
    These are consequences of established motivic criteria. In the framework of Kimura’s finite-
dimensional motives [Kim05], Guletskiı̆–Pedrini proved the equivalence with Bloch’s conjecture for
surfaces with pg = 0 and recorded the resulting Tate decomposition when q = 0 [GuP03, Theorem 27
and Remark 28]. The surface decomposition of Kahn–Murre–Pedrini identifies vanishing of the
Albanese kernel with t2 (S) = 0 [KMP07]; the Hilbert-scheme assertion then uses the motive formula
of de Cataldo–Migliorini [dCM02]. In particular, every rational Chow class on the stated powers and
Hilbert schemes is determined by its cohomology class. This conclusion uses the Tate decomposition,
not finite-dimensionality alone. Section 8.2 gives the proof.
    Section 2 first passes to minimal surfaces of general type. Section 3 constructs the determinant-
fixed theory and its fixed part; Section 4 computes the resulting virtual classes and the divisor
cosection. Section 5 proves the marked Chow relation and its action on zero-cycles. Sections 6 and 7
prove the two nonvanishing statements. Section 8.1 gives the integral conclusion, and Section 8.2
derives the motivic consequences.

Conventions
All schemes and cohomology groups are over C. The definitions of CH0 (S) and albS above,
Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2 and its proof, Lemma 2.1, and Section 8.1 use integral Chow groups.
Elsewhere Chow groups have rational coefficients; in particular, all Chow motives in Section 8.2
have rational coefficients. We use cohomological indexing for complexes. Line bundles are written
additively; in intersections the same symbol denotes their first Chern class. For a perfect complex,
its Euler class with a nonzero torus weight is interpreted in localized equivariant Chow theory.
Statements about virtual classes always use the specified perfect obstruction theories. A comparison
of their classes in K-theory is used to compute Chern classes only after the relevant obstruction
bundle or compatible map of theories has been identified.

  (i) X is a smooth connected projective complex surface with pg = q = 0;

3.1   Derived arrows and classical families
Ciocan-Fontanine and Kapranov constructed a derived Quot scheme whose truncation is the classical
Quot scheme and whose tangent cohomology is given by Ext groups from the kernel to the quotient
[CFK01, Theorem 4.3.2]. Here we use a perfect-arrow moduli description to retain the maps needed
for the based determinant fiber.
    We use the derived moduli stack of perfect complexes and its morphism stack as constructed
in [TV07, Theorem 3.6, Definition 3.18, and Corollaries 3.17, 3.19, and 3.29]. For smooth proper
X these stacks are locally geometric and locally of finite presentation. The morphism stack with
target fixed to V also has a direct description: over a perfect source F its fiber is the derived linear
mapping stack associated to RHom(F, V ). This complex is perfect, and its formation commutes
with derived base change, because X is smooth and proper.

then gives the required open neighborhood in Y . Applying this to the source and cone proves the
asserted openness.
