                 Cyclic length and chromatic fixed-point loss

                                               Abstract
          For a finite p-group G and a subgroup H, we prove that the optimal chromatic fixed-point
      loss equals the shortest length of a subnormal chain from H to G with cyclic quotients. The
      equality holds at every prime and every nonnegative height, resolving positively the equality
      proposed by Kuhn and Lloyd.


1    Introduction
The chromatic Smith problem asks how much information about fixed points at one subgroup is
forced by chromatic acyclicity at another. Fix a prime p, and let K(i) denote Morava K-theory
at p, with K(0) = HQ. For a finite p-group G, a subgroup H ≤ G, and an integer n ≥ 0, define
rn (G, H) to be the least nonnegative integer r such that

                          K(n + r)∗ (ΦH X) = 0      =⇒     K(n)∗ (ΦG X) = 0                           (1.1)

for every finite p-local genuine G-spectrum X. Here ΦH X denotes the underlying spectrum of the
geometric fixed points, and finite spectra include compact retracts.
    Let ℓ(G, H) be the least length of a subnormal chain

                                    H = G0 ⊴ G1 ⊴ · · · ⊴ Gs = G

whose successive quotients are cyclic. We set ℓ(G, G) = 0. Such chains exist in finite p-groups.
The cyclic chromatic Smith theorem, applied along a chain, gives rn (G, H) ≤ ℓ(G, H). Our result
is the converse.

Theorem 1.1. For every prime p, finite p-group G, subgroup H ≤ G, and integer n ≥ 0,

                                         rn (G, H) = ℓ(G, H).

    Thus the optimal loss is independent of the height. For every integer 0 ≤ r < ℓ(G, H), there
is a finite genuine G-spectrum whose geometric H-fixed points are K(n + r)-acyclic and whose
geometric G-fixed points have nonzero K(n)-homology. The theorem includes rational fixed-point
detection at n = 0.
    The embedding of H matters. For example, in G = Cp2 × Cp , take H1 = pCp2 × 0 and
H2 = 0 × Cp . Both subgroups are isomorphic to Cp , but G/H1 ∼        = Cp × Cp and G/H2 ∼     = Cp 2 .
Chains above Hi correspond to chains in these quotients. The first quotient requires two cyclic
steps, while the second requires one. Thus rn (G, H1 ) = 2 and rn (G, H2 ) = 1 at every target height.
A cyclic quotient of order p2 still counts only once.

                                                   1
History and significance
The classical starting point is Smith theory: for a finite complex with an action of a finite p-
group, mod-p acyclicity passes to fixed points. Floyd strengthened this to an inequality between
total mod-p Betti numbers [6, Theorem 4.4]; the finite p-group statement follows by iteration
from prime-order actions. Chromatic Smith theory asks which implications survive when mod-p
homology is replaced by individual Morava theories.
     Building on the nonequivariant thick subcategory theorem of Hopkins and Smith [12], Balmer
and Sanders identified all primes of the category of finite genuine equivariant spectra. They reduced
its topology to subgroup–height inclusions and computed the case Cp , crediting Strickland’s earlier
work [2, Introduction, Theorems 4.9 and 4.14, and Section 7]. Barthel, Hausmann, Naumann,
Nikolaus, Noel, and Stapleton determined the finite abelian p-group case: the loss for H ≤ G is the
minimum number of generators of G/H [3, Theorem 2.3]. This replaced the original order-based
prediction by a rank bound; already Cp2 has loss one rather than two. The sharp elementary abelian
examples use the partition-complex work of Arone, Dwyer, Lesh, and Mahowald [3, Theorems 2.2
and 4.1]. Balderrama and Kuhn later gave an elementary proof of the abelian chromatic Smith
theorem, including target height zero [1, Theorem 1.1 and Section 3.2].
     For nonabelian groups, a general lower bound is the rank of the largest elementary abelian
quotient of G in which H maps to the identity. This can be smaller than the cyclic-chain
upper bound. Kuhn and Lloyd proved that chromatic acyclicity implications are equivalent to
corresponding inequalities between Morava homology dimensions, allowing representation-theoretic
constructions to give stronger lower bounds [15, Theorem 2.2]. They obtained equality with ℓ
for every subgroup pair inside a central extension C2 → G → E with E elementary abelian [15,
Theorem 2.13]. In particular, a noncentral subgroup of order two in D8 has loss two, although
the elementary-quotient lower bound is one. Their discussion of the general problem expresses
“cautious hope” that rn (G, H) = r+ (G, H) = ℓ(G, H), and separately asks whether the loss is
independent of height [15, Section 2.2]. Theorem 1.1 establishes the proposed equality, and hence
height independence, for all finite p-groups.
     The remaining obstacle is sharpness for an arbitrary embedded subgroup. The Hopkins–Kuhn–
Ravenel characters are indexed by maps from Znp , whose images are abelian [10, Theorem C].
Thus they cannot directly supply characters with full nonabelian image. General character rings
with other source groups already appear in Kuhn’s framework [13, Section 2]. Here we select a
profinite source from the finite groups detected by telescopic cochains, derive constraints on all its
open subgroups by relative integration, and use those constraints to prove the required fixed-point
vanishing estimates.
     Combined with the conjugacy and p-subnormal reductions of Balmer–Sanders, the theorem
determines the remaining finite-height prime inclusions for arbitrary finite groups [2, Proposi-
tion 6.11]; Corollary 7.5 gives the precise test. Their description of the infinite-height inclusions and
of Thomason subsets then determines the thick tensor ideals [2, Corollaries 8.19, 10.5, and 10.6].
The sharpness witnesses in this paper are obtained by separation of primes.

To prove it at positive l, we rationalize morphisms in the tensor category of dualizable T (l)-local

   The key additional information comes from relative integration over finite groupoids. Strick-
land’s Frobenius formulation of HKR character theory already expresses such integration through
automorphism-group weights [16, Section 10 and Theorem 10.2]. We derive the weights for the
source Γ just constructed. Applying them to groupoids of labeled finite sets, coinduced from an
open subgroup, gives congruences

                                                        |X V | − |X| ∈ ph+1 Z(p)

for every open subgroup U ≤ Γ, every finite continuous U -set X, and every h ≥ 0. The same
construction gives |Homcts (Γ, F )|/|F | ∈ Z(p) for every finite group F . Together with the finite-
section bound, these arithmetic conditions force a closed subnormal series with Zp -factors and
length at most l. The purely profinite argument uses shrinking semidirect products and an explicit
p-adic limit of subgroup counts; it may be useful independently of the chromatic application.
    Next, ring nilpotence and the generalized characters of Hopkins, Kuhn, and Ravenel [12, 10]
upgrade (1.2) to
                         T (k) ∧ UP (LT (l) S) ̸= 0 =⇒ k + ℓ(P, 1) ≤ l.                        (1.3)
Chromatic ambidexterity and its torsion-nilpotence consequence [5] allow this step to use arbitrary
nonzero commutative local algebras. A product with increasingly large cyclic p-groups separates
the height k from the cyclic length.
    Finally, finite chromatic fracture converts (1.3) into vanishing estimates for the right adjoints to
subgroup geometric fixed points. The support argument adapts the strategy of Balmer–Sanders [2,
proof of Theorem 9.1, (B)⇒(A)]. At a least height where the proposed bound fails, the estimates
isolate the offending subgroup component from the other components. In the finite-height tensor-
triangular spectrum, this forces the support of a compact object to split into two nonempty
disjoint closed parts, although that support is the closure of a point and hence irreducible. The
contradiction supplies the lower bound for every embedded subgroup.
    Published inputs are recalled in Section 2; the character construction, congruences, profinite
theorem, higher-height estimate, and support argument occupy Sections 3 to 7, respectively.


2    Chromatic and equivariant preliminaries
We work p-locally. The category SpG is the stable category of genuine G-spectra on a complete
universe; its compact objects are retracts of p-local finite genuine G-CW spectra. All limits, tensor
products, and fixed-point functors are derived. When a localized category is used, its tensor
product is the corresponding localized smash product.

2.2    Genuine fixed points and cofree spectra
Inflation of an ordinary spectrum D will be denoted by inf G D. It satisfies

                                    ΦH (inf G D) ≃ D            (H ≤ G).

Geometric fixed points are exact and symmetric monoidal, preserve finite objects, and jointly

2.3    Ambidexterity and Burnside marks
The relevant ambidexterity belongs to the development of chromatic Tate vanishing and higher
semiadditivity. Kuhn proved telescopic finite-group Tate vanishing [14, Theorem 1.5]; Hopkins
and Lurie established higher ambidexterity in the Morava-local category [11, Theorem 0.0.2]. The
telescopic form below is due to Carmeli, Schlank, and Yanovski. None of these uses identifies
telescopic localization with Morava localization on arbitrary spectra.
    We use the published ambidexterity theorem for SpT (l) , SpK(l) , and K(l)-local El -modules
[5, Theorem 5.3.1 and Corollary 5.3.2]. In these categories every finite groupoid is ambidextrous.
Integration along maps of finite groupoids satisfies composition, homotopy base change, and the
projection formula; for finite coverings it is the ordinary sum transfer [5, Section 3.1]. Cochains on
a finite groupoid with values in the unit are dualizable [5, Proposition 3.3.7]. Tensoring preserves
finite-groupoid limits, since it preserves their ambidextrous colimits [5, Corollary 3.2.4]. Thus the
canonical exterior product of unit-cochains is an algebra equivalence
                                        1BY ⊗ 1BZ ≃ 1B(Y ×Z)
for finite groups Y, Z, and similarly for finite groupoids.
    For a finite group F , its rational Burnside ring is the product of copies of Q indexed by
conjugacy classes of subgroups. The I-mark of a finite F -set X is |X I |. We denote by eFI the
idempotent with mark one at the conjugacy class of I and zero at all others. A finite F -set X
acts on cochains by multiplication by the transfer of 1 along X // F → BF ; for an orbit, this is
restriction followed by transfer. This agrees with the genuine Burnside action. The full mark eFF
will be used to isolate full geometric fixed points.

3     Rational characters from finite groups
Fix a prime p and a positive height l, and put 1 = LT (l) S. Starting from a finite group P with
(UP (1))Q ̸= 0, we will construct a pro-p group Γ ↠ P whose finite sections require at most l
generators. The same construction will describe rational cochain summands by evaluation at
continuous homomorphisms from Γ to finite groups. These evaluation coordinates will allow
integration to impose the arithmetic conditions used in Section 5. The construction uses finite-
groupoid ambidexterity and elementary abelian sharpness, without requiring Γ to be abelian.

Lemma 5.2. Let E be a pro-p group.

7.4   The general finite-group consequence
For a finite group J, let Op (J) denote its smallest normal subgroup of p-power index. We continue
to use tested-height indices and work in the p-local genuine category.

In particular, the numerical test is applied to a qualifying conjugate in the finite p-group H/N .
