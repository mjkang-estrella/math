     Positive scalar curvature and uniform codimension-two width
                                                   OpenAI

                                            September 23, 2026


                                                   Abstract
         We prove the quantitative continuous form of Gromov’s scalar-curvature conjecture in every
      dimension n ≥ 4. Every complete connected smooth boundaryless n-manifold with scalar
      curvature at least one admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most
      n−2 whose entire fibers have diameter bounded only in terms of n. We also obtain the continuous
      macroscopic-dimension conclusion for universal covers of closed manifolds with positive scalar
      curvature in every dimension n ≥ 2.

1    Introduction
A lower bound for scalar curvature is a local condition. Gromov’s macroscopic dimension conjecture
predicts a global consequence: at a controlled scale, two dimensions can be collapsed. We prove the
quantitative form of this assertion for arbitrary complete manifolds in dimensions at least four.
    Throughout, scalar curvature means the ordinary pointwise Riemannian invariant. Fixed-scale
macroscopic scalar curvature uses ball-volume comparison at a prescribed positive radius [24,
Definitions 1.2–1.3].
    For a metric space X and an integer d ≥ 0, define its Urysohn d-width by

                                UWd (X) = inf sup diamX f −1 (y),
                                             f   y∈K

where the infimum runs over continuous maps f : X → K to simplicial complexes of dimension at
most d. The complexes have their usual polyhedral topology. The diameter is that of the entire
fiber, including all its components; empty fibers may be omitted.
Theorem 1.1. For every integer n ≥ 4, there is a finite constant Cn such that every connected
complete smooth Riemannian n-manifold (M, g), without boundary and with Scalg ≥ 1, admits a
continuous map f : M → K to a simplicial complex of dimension at most n − 2 satisfying

                                 diamg f −1 (y) ≤ Cn    (y ∈ K).

In particular, UWn−2 (M, g) ≤ Cn .
    The constant is independent of M , its topology, and its geometry. The theorem applies to
compact and noncompact manifolds, with no orientability, spin, covering, or bounded geometry
assumption. If Scalg ≥ σ 2 > 0, applying the theorem to σ 2 g gives UWn−2 (M, g) ≤ Cn /σ.
    For a closed connected Riemannian manifold, let FillRad(M, g) be Gromov’s filling radius [17],
defined by the Kuratowski embedding and the fundamental class, with coefficients in Z if M is
orientable and in Z2 otherwise [32, Definition 3.1]. The next corollary gives the closed-manifold
case in dimensions at least four of Gromov’s filling-radius Conjecture A− [20, §3].

Corollary 1.4 (Aspherical obstruction). No closed smooth aspherical manifold of dimension n ≥ 4

Proof. It suffices to consider a connected manifold; passing to its orientation double cover if necessary
preserves the hypotheses. Suppose such a metric exists. Its scalar curvature has a positive minimum,

scalar-curvature lower bound. The deck group acts isometrically and cocompactly. The scaled form
of Theorem 1.1 gives a continuous map from X to an (n − 2)-complex with uniformly bounded

of OpenAI [34, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2].

1.1   History and significance
Gromov introduced macroscopic dimension in his study of positive scalar curvature [18, §2]. His
quantitative conjecture already asks whether a complete manifold with a positive scalar-curvature
lower bound can be mapped continuously to a polyhedron of codimension two with all point inverses
bounded at the curvature scale [18, §2 12 ]; see also [22, p. 3180, Comments after Conjecture 3.2]. The
diameter condition concerns each entire fiber, including all its components. In the universal cover
of a closed manifold the corresponding macroscopic-dimension assertion asks only for some finite
bound. The continuous invariant and the version requiring a Lipschitz map to a uniformly metrized
complex are distinct [13, Definitions 1.1–1.2].
    The pointwise curvature hypothesis cannot be replaced by its fixed-scale macroscopic counterpart.
Kumar and Sen construct closed manifolds in every dimension at least four with a prescribed positive
fixed-scale macroscopic scalar-curvature lower bound and arbitrarily large Urysohn codimension-two
width [24, Theorem A].
    The low-dimensional cases have a substantial earlier history. Gromov and Lawson obtained
geometric width bounds for closed three-manifolds [19, Corollary 10.11], and Bolotov proved
that the universal cover of a closed three-manifold cannot have macroscopic dimension two [8].
For closed three-manifolds with positive scalar curvature, Liokumovich and Maximo constructed
Morse functions with curvature-scale bounds for the areas and ambient diameters of connected
level components [25, Theorem 1.1]. Liokumovich and Wang constructed proper Morse functions
on complete noncompact three-manifolds with uniformly positive scalar curvature, with uniform
diameter bounds for connected level components [26, Theorem 1.1]. We use that result and an
explicit passage to the Reeb graph for the three-dimensional closed-cover consequence; the surface
case follows from the Bonnet–Myers argument.
    The aspherical obstruction in dimensions four and five was established by Chodosh and Li [9,
Theorem 2], with an independent proof in dimension five by Gromov [23, §7, Main Corollary].
    For the macroscopic-dimension conjecture, Bolotov proved the closed spin case under the Strong
Novikov Conjecture and a cohomological-dimension condition [5]. Bolotov and Dranishnikov then


                                                   4
obtained the codimension-two conclusion for closed spin manifolds under the Strong Novikov
Conjecture and injectivity of the connective-to-periodic real K-homology map [6, Theorem 4.3].
For closed orientable totally non-spin manifolds of dimension at least five, they also showed that a
macroscopic dimension drop by one implies a drop by two [7, Theorem 3.4]. Daher and Dranishnikov
established the analogous implication for closed orientable totally non-spin four-manifolds with
residually finite fundamental group [10, Theorem 3.3]. These topological advances concern universal
covers and finite bounds; the present quantitative statement is formulated for arbitrary complete
manifolds with a bound depending only on dimension after scalar normalization.
    The geometric method uses the minimal-hypersurface approach of Schoen and Yau, in which
stability and the Gauss equation pass scalar-curvature information to hypersurfaces [35, Section 1],
and the torical symmetrization mechanism described by Gromov [21, Section 2]. Gromov’s µ-bubble
method uses prescribed mean curvature to localize the hypersurfaces entering scalar-curvature
descent [18, §5 56 ]. The weighted variational formulation and its relation to warped-circle geometry
and stability are developed by Chodosh and Li [9, Section 3 and Remark 11].
    Removing singular regions while retaining scalar-curvature information is also central to
Lohkamp’s splitting theory [27, §1.2 and Theorem 3.15]. In the construction below, profile updates
exclude singular points from the regions where the next scalar bound is required; the wall and both
retained sides are then regularized together.
    Recent results also address quantitative width and singular descent. Antonelli proves width
bounds under a positive scalar-curvature lower bound, an almost-nonnegative Ricci lower bound,
and uniform noncollapse, with constants depending on the volume lower bound [3, Theorem 1.4]. Bi
and Zhu develop singular scalar-curvature descent for enlargeability obstructions and cubical-width
inequalities [4, Theorem B and Section 4]. The present construction retains both bulk sides and the
wall through countably many cuts, with compatible product ends.

1.2   The proof and its main constructions
Fix an original length D, depending only on n, and a separated net (xi ) in (M, g). The inner balls
of radius D cover M , and the closed outer balls of radius 2D are locally finite. Each center has its
own label. We construct a continuous collapse map q : M → M with dg (q(x), x) < 1, together with
compatible walls separating the inverse images of each inner ball and the exterior of its outer ball.
The map q records original positions while cylinders are inserted along the cuts. The final walls
have no common intersection of n − 1 distinct labels. Continuous sign coordinates ti : M → [0, 1]
are chosen to equal 1 on the q-preimage of the inner ball and 0 on the q-preimage of the exterior of
its outer ball. Their transition regions lie in wall neighborhoods chosen simultaneously to preserve
the intersection bound. They give the desired map: at most n − 2 coordinates are fractional at a
point, and some coordinate is one. Equality of all coordinates confines both collapse images to the
same original outer ball, giving a bound for the entire fiber.
    Three points are essential to make this construction uniform and to allow arbitrary complete
metrics.
    First, the separations must become as wide as prescribed without spending curvature in proportion
to the number of labels. For a metric h and a smooth function t, put

                                  D(h, t) = Scalh +2∆h t − |dt|2h .                             (1.1)

Fix a background metric h0 throughout a round. The local stretching estimate changes h mono-
tonically and increases t, while paying for its loss with a decrease in trh h0 . The pressure law in
Theorem 2.1 provides a quadratic budget in t. This makes the initial scale depend only on n, even
when there is no bound on the overlap multiplicity of the original balls. Trace losses telescope over

                                                 5
a countable locally finite round. Each label stops after its own finite number of rounds, so the entire
construction stabilizes on every compact set (Theorem 4.3). Diagonal circle warps convert the drift
scalar bound to an ordinary scalar-curvature inequality on every required finite product.
    Second, a cut produces both extended bulk sides and a wall of one lower base dimension. We
keep all these pieces, called packets, with compatible product collars. Each label has two open forced
cores, one of each sign, on which that sign must be retained. A label is decided at a point when
the point lies in one of its cores. On an m-dimensional packet, positive scalar curvature is required
only where at least m − 1 remaining labels are outside their forced cores. Minimizing walls may be
singular in high dimensions. Near the dangerous singular region, additional pressure profiles make
the remaining labels decided. Hardy estimates, compactness across the bulk–cylinder seam, and a
maximum estimate for concentrating potentials pay for this shielding. A guarded regularization
and positive supersolutions then restore smooth scalar-curvature control. The full finite-diagram
arguments are proved in Appendices A to E. They include the stability and variation computations
used here; classical elliptic and geometric-measure-theory results are cited with their hypotheses at
their points of use.
    Third, a countable list needs more than finiteness on compact subsets. After a first radial
cut, only finitely many labels and warp factors are nonconstant on any whole packet and on each
finite incident diagram needed by a step. All other labels are already forced; their potentials form
a summable list of spatial constants. Approximations reproduce those constants exactly. This
invariant permits the finite packet arguments to be iterated with successively smaller positive
budgets, without a common positive floor for all labels. An intersection of n − 1 distinct walls
would produce a complete one-dimensional packet. The positive scalar-curvature invariant on its
finite warped product yields a Riccati inequality that cannot hold on the complete arclength line
(Theorem 6.5), giving the required wall-intersection bound.
    Finally, the analytic cylinders are used only through finite truncations in the topological
reconstruction. Successive collar compressions are supported over the corresponding original outer
balls. On one fixed copy of M , the collapse maps and the old wall statuses are therefore eventually
unchanged near every point. This local stabilization, together with a locally finite thickening of the
walls, gives continuity in the ordinary polyhedral topology. The resulting constant is
                                            Cn = 4D + 2,
where the original scale D is chosen in terms of n before any metric-dependent analytic estimates.
The construction does not aim to optimize this constant.
    The proof follows two separate inputs. Sections 2–4 construct the geometric stretching and its
scalar reserve. Section 5 states the finite-label cutting procedure, whose complete proof occupies
Appendices A–E. Section 6 proves its countable-label adaptation, and Section 7 constructs the
width map. Section 8 verifies the uniform simplex metric and proves the closed-cover consequence
in dimensions two and three.
Convention 1.5. The Laplacian is ∆ = div grad, and round spheres have positive scalar curvature.
For a hypersurface with unit normal ν, its second fundamental form is B(v, w) = ⟨∇v ν, w⟩, and its
mean curvature is tr B. Integration and divergence use Riemannian densities, so no orientation is
needed. Distances in the final fiber bound are always measured in the original metric g.

individually labelled normal coordinates. After slightly raising the entry thresholds, each closed tail

D     Global shielding and extension to cofaces
The local annular cost does not add once for every singular piece: a concentration argument bounds
a new shrinking cost by the maximum of the old and local form coefficients. We prove that assertion
first, then transport each chosen addition to cofaces, and finally show that only finitely many
