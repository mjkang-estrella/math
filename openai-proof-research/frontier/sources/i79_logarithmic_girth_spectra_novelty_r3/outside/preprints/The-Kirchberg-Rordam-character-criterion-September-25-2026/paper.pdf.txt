                                               Abstract
        We prove the Kirchberg–Rørdam character criterion: for every free ultrafilter on N, a
     nonzero unital separable complex C ∗ -algebra absorbs the Jiang–Su algebra if and only if its
     norm central-sequence algebra has no characters. We also prove that the infinite minimal tensor
     power of every nonzero unital separable complex C ∗ -algebra without characters is Z-stable
     and contains a unital copy of Z.


1    Introduction
For a nonzero unital C ∗ -algebra A and a free ultrafilter ω on N, write

                  Aω = ℓ∞ (N, A) {(an ) : lim ‖an ‖ = 0},         Fω (A) = Aω ∩ A′ ,
                                   /
                                            n→ω

where A is identified with its diagonal image in Aω . A character is a nonzero ∗-homomorphism to
C; on a unital algebra it is automatically unital. We write Z for the Jiang–Su algebra introduced
by Jiang and Su [JS]. An algebra is Z-stable if it is isomorphic to its minimal tensor product with
Z.
   Tensorial absorption can be recognized through central copies of the absorbed algebra. The
theory of strongly self-absorbing algebras developed by Toms and Winter makes this principle
precise [TW]. In the case of Z, Kirchberg and Rørdam proved that a unital separable algebra is
Z-stable exactly when its norm central-sequence algebra contains, with the same unit, a separable
subhomogeneous algebra without characters [KR, Theorem 2.5]. Here subhomogeneity means a
common finite bound on the dimensions of irreducible representations. The absence of characters
on that subalgebra plainly forces the same absence on the whole central-sequence algebra. The
question is whether this elementary obstruction is already sufficient.
   Kirchberg and Rørdam asked whether the absence of characters on Fω (A) characterizes Jiang–
Su absorption among unital separable C ∗ -algebras [KR, Question 3.1]. Their work relates this
question to tensor powers of algebras without characters [KR, Question 3.7 and Proposition 3.9].
The character question also appears as Problem LXXX in the recent collection of Schafhauser,
Tikuisis, and White [STW, Section 22]. We prove the proposed characterization.
Theorem 1.1. Let A be a nonzero unital separable complex C ∗ -algebra, and let ω be any free
ultrafilter on N. Then

                        Fω (A) has no characters       ⇐⇒      A∼
                                                                = A ⊗min Z.

   The hypothesis uses the norm central-sequence algebra. The theorem applies to all such A,
with no nuclearity, simplicity, trace, dimension, or comparison assumption. Its main ingredient is
the following finite construction, in which separability is unnecessary.

1.1     Earlier results and the remaining construction
Dadarlat and Toms connected Jiang–Su absorption with infinite tensor powers [DT]. Their
Theorem 1.1 shows that the infinite minimal tensor power of a unital separable algebra is Z-stable
whenever it contains a same-unit subhomogeneous characterless subalgebra. The construction
in their Theorem 6.2 gives, for each fixed prime dimension-drop algebra, a unital map into
any separable recursive subhomogeneous algebra of finite topological dimension whose minimum
matrix size is sufficiently large relative to that dimension. Our finite theorem begins only with
absence of characters on D and uses maximal tensor products, so it must construct the required
subhomogeneous image without such a structural hypothesis.
    A positive element is full if the closed two-sided ideal it generates is the whole ambient algebra.
Several regularity criteria explain why finding the subhomogeneous image is useful. Robert and
Tikuisis showed that two full orthogonal positive elements in the central-sequence algebra suffice
for absorption when the original separable algebra has finite nuclear dimension [RT, Theorem 1.2].
More recently, Perera, Thiel, and Vilalta characterized Z-stability of separable algebras by purity
of the central-sequence algebra [PTV, Theorem 3.6]. Purity imposes comparison and divisibility
conditions on its Cuntz semigroup. The present argument assumes neither finite nuclear dimension
nor these comparison and divisibility properties.
    The companion [OAI, Theorem 1.4] proves that a finite maximal tensor power of every nonzero
unital characterless algebra contains two full orthogonal positive elements, without separability
or nuclearity. Its Gaussian and pure-state-excision proof is different from the representation-
label argument developed below. We also use the companion’s commuting-copy lemma [OAI,
Lemma 9.3], with the primary reindexing provenance in [KR, Proposition 2.2 and Corollary 2.3].
    The bridge from full orthogonal elements to a homomorphism from the matrix cone C0 ((0, 1]) ⊗
M2 that sends t |→ te11 to a full element already appears in the proof of [KR, Proposition 6.1].

We give a complete construction using representation-stable tensor labels. A matrix-cone map
can equivalently be viewed as a completely positive contractive order-zero map on a matrix
algebra, meaning one that preserves orthogonality of positive elements, as in Winter and Zacharias
[WZ, Corollary 4.1]; below we construct the cone homomorphism directly. Fullness of its first
matrix corner is not by itself the dimension-drop criterion. The additional task is to produce two
exactly equivalent orthogonal positive contractions, of the form v ∗ v and vv ∗ for a single element
v, and compare their unit defect with a positive cutoff of one of them. This is the input to the
stable-rank-free implication of Rørdam and Winter [RW, Proposition 5.1, (ii)⇒(iv)], whose needed
form is proved explicitly in Section 3.

1.2     Proof overview
We give the finite construction in two stages. First, a finite-dimensional space of selfadjoints
detects noncommutativity in every representation of D. An incidence argument supplies tensor
labels that remain linearly independent after arbitrary rank-at-least-two maps on the individual
legs. These labels yield two selfadjoints that do not commute in any irreducible representation of
a suitable tensor power. A double commutator then has full positive and negative parts, and a
second use of the labels assembles finitely many bridges between them into one full square-zero
element.
     Second, the square-zero element gives a matrix-cone homomorphism with a full first corner. A
continuous simplex of embeddings of M2 organizes this cone across a finite tensor power. Two
orthogonal equivalent positive elements leave a defect concentrated near the origin of a cube.
Disjoint regions near its coordinate axes support elements that absorb this defect after a further
finite amplification. The resulting comparison is precisely the one required by the Rørdam–Winter
dimension-drop criterion. This comparison is the step that turns full orthogonal elements into a
unital I(2, 3) map.
     Section 2 records the comparison conventions and structural criteria, with the dimension-drop
conversion proved in Section 3. Section 4 constructs this cone homomorphism, and Section 5
proves the finite comparison. Section 6 deduces both theorems and Corollary 1.3. All tensor
exponents and comparison constants are allowed to depend on the algebra D.


2     Preliminaries
2.1     Tensor products and positive elements
All algebras are complex. All tensor products in the construction are maximal, and we abbreviate
D⊗max n to D⊗n . We specify minimal tensor products explicitly. Algebraic tensor products
are denoted by ⊙ over C and by ⊗R over R. Representations called irreducible are nonzero
and nondegenerate, hence unital when the represented algebra is unital. An element is full if
its generated closed two-sided ideal is the whole algebra. Equivalently, it is nonzero in every
irreducible representation: a proper ideal has a nonzero irreducible representation of its quotient.

direct sum. We use only the following elementary rules:

2.2    The dimension-drop criterion
We say that positive elements a1 , a2 ∈ B are equivalent if a1 = v ∗ v and a2 = vv ∗ for one v ∈ B.
This is the exact relation required in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.1 (Rørdam–Winter). Let B be a unital C ∗ -algebra. Suppose that a1 , a2 ∈ B are
orthogonal positive contractions with a1 = v ∗ v, a2 = vv ∗ for some v ∈ B, and that, for some ε > 0,

                                       1B − a1 − a2 ≾ (a1 − ε)+ .

Then there is a unital ∗-homomorphism I(2, 3) → B.

    This is [RW, Proposition 5.1, (ii)⇒(iv), with n = 2]. Section 3 supplies a complete proof using
their method, with the support projections, scalar endpoint fiber, and weighted flip identity made
explicit. The stable-rank-one hypothesis appearing elsewhere in that proposition is needed only for
(i)⇒(ii). It is not a hypothesis of the implication stated here. Orthogonality ensures a1 + a2 ≤ 1B ,
so the defect in the display is positive.

2.3    Central-sequence inputs
We use the following results of Kirchberg and Rørdam for the fixed free ultrafilter ω.

Proposition 2.2 (Kirchberg–Rørdam). Let A be a nonzero unital separable C ∗ -algebra.

   1. If D ⊂ Fω (A) is separable and contains the ambient unit, then for every m ≥ 1 there is a
      unital ∗-homomorphism D⊗max m → Fω (A).

   2. A ∼
        = A ⊗min Z if and only if Fω (A) contains, with the ambient unit, a separable subhomoge-
      neous C ∗ -algebra without characters.

   The first assertion is the unital specialization of the commuting-copies lemma in [OAI,
Lemma 9.3], whose primary source is [KR, Proposition 2.2 and Corollary 2.3]. For unital A,
the annihilator ideal used in that companion is zero: an element annihilating every element of A
annihilates its unit. Thus its F (A) is precisely the norm central-sequence algebra used here. This
particular lemma has no nuclear-dimension assumption. We include the short specialization to
make its quantifiers explicit.

4     A matrix-cone homomorphism with a full corner
We first construct a full square-zero element in a finite tensor power of a unital C ∗ -algebra without
characters. Its polar decomposition will then give the matrix-cone homomorphism needed in the
next section. The absence of characters is detected by finitely many commutators. The main
issue is to combine those finitely many tests into one element that survives every irreducible
representation of a maximal tensor product, including representations that are not spatial tensor
products.

Lemma 4.2. Let π : B1 ⊗max · · · ⊗max Bq → B(H) be an irreducible representation, where the

partition into nonempty blocks.

Proof. Every represented block contains π(1) = 1H . The represented blocks commute, and this
commutation persists under weak-operator closure. A central element of the closure of one block
commutes both with that block and with every complementary block. It therefore belongs to the
commutant of the full irreducible image, which consists of the scalars. This proves factorhood for
each block.

    These lemmas concern algebraic tensor independence. They require neither faithfulness of the
restrictions to the tensor legs nor a spatial decomposition of the represented factors.
