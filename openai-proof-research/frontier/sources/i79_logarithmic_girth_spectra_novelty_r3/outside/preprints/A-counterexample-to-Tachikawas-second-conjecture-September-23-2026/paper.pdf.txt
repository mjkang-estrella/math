           A counterexample to Tachikawa’s second conjecture
                                              OpenAI

                                       September 23, 2026


                                              Abstract
        We disprove Tachikawa’s second conjecture by constructing a finite-dimensional symmetric
     algebra over k = F2 (q, H1 , H2 ) with a finite-dimensional nonprojective module whose self-
     extension groups vanish in every positive degree. The associated endomorphism algebra also
     gives counterexamples to the classical, generalized, and strong Nakayama conjectures, the
     Auslander–Gorenstein conjecture, and the Wakamatsu tilting conjecture. These conclusions
     persist after every extension of k.


1    Introduction
Let A be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field k. A finite-dimensional A-module M is self-
orthogonal if ExtiA (M, M ) = 0 for every integer i > 0. Tachikawa’s second conjecture asserts
that every finite-dimensional self-orthogonal module over a finite-dimensional self-injective algebra
is projective. This question belongs to the homological conjectures surrounding quasi-Frobenius
algebras and dominant dimension; see Tachikawa’s 1973 monograph [26, pp. VI–VII] and the account
of the conjecture in [6, p. 7288]. The Auslander–Reiten conjecture, originating in [1], asks for
finite-dimensional algebras whether the condition ExtiA (M, M ⊕ A) = 0 for every i > 0 forces
M to be projective. For a self-injective algebra, the groups ExtiA (M, A) vanish automatically, so
Tachikawa’s second conjecture is its self-injective case.
    Write D = Homk (−, k). An algebra A is symmetric if A ≃ DA as A-bimodules; in particular, a
symmetric algebra is self-injective. We prove the following result in this class.
Theorem 1.1. Let
                                         k = F2 (q, H1 , H2 ),
where q, H1 , H2 are algebraically independent. There exist a finite-dimensional associative unital
symmetric k-algebra A and a finite-dimensional unital left A-module M such that M is nonprojective
and
                            ExtiA (M, M ) = 0    for every integer i > 0.
In particular, Tachikawa’s second conjecture is false.
   Since A is self-injective, the same module satisfies ExtiA (M, M ⊕ A) = 0 for every i > 0. Thus
Theorem 1.1 also gives a symmetric counterexample to the Auslander–Reiten conjecture.

Related results. Schulz’s earlier example of a nonprojective module without positive self-
extensions uses an algebra over a skew field, outside the finite-dimensional-over-a-field setting
considered here; see [23] and the distinction recalled in [10, Introduction]. His quantum-exterior
example has nonzero first self-extension but no higher self-extensions [24, Example 7]; the parameter-
shifting syzygies in that example are an antecedent for our starting algebra. For symmetric Artin

algebras with radical cube zero and nonzero radical square that admit such modules. The classifi-
cation singles out quantum exterior and double Nakayama algebras with parameters that are not
roots of unity [13, Theorem 1.1].
    Chen, Fang and Xi characterize the symmetric case through stratifying ideals and give conditional
mirror-reflective constructions from gendo-symmetric counterexamples to the Nakayama conjecture
[8, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]. Chen and Xi relate self-orthogonal Nakayama-stable generators over self-
injective algebras to additional categorical and Gorenstein–Morita conditions [6, Theorem 1.2 and
Corollary 1.4]. Their further criterion assumes virtual Gorensteinness of the relevant endomorphism
algebra [7, Theorem 1.3]. Zhang and Zhou prove the self-injective case for split algebras with radical
cube zero [28, Corollary 4.2] and subsequently treat Igusa–Todorov algebras [29, Theorem 3.2]. Xia
proves the Auslander–Reiten conjecture for quantum complete intersections [27, Corollary 4.3]. In a
different direction, Böhmler and Marczinzik construct a nonprojective module over a commutative
local self-injective algebra whose first two self-extension groups vanish, while the third does not [3,
Theorem 3.1 and the end of Section 3]. Theorem 1.1 establishes vanishing in every positive degree.

sion of an Artin algebra implies the Auslander–Reiten conjecture for that algebra [12, Theorem 3.5].
This reduction has a self-injective target, not a symmetric target in general. Our construction
obtains a symmetric algebra by establishing the additional complete-resolution vanishing needed
for transfer to the ordinary trivial extension. Enomoto and Marczinzik also prove an equivalence
among the universal versions of the Auslander–Reiten, generalized Nakayama, Nakayama, and both
Tachikawa conjectures over a fixed commutative Artinian coefficient ring [12, Theorem 1.1].

Homological consequences. The pair (A, M ) gives counterexamples to several conjectures
formulated through injective resolutions and tilting modules. Corollary 1.2 realizes five such failures
using one endomorphism algebra and two associated modules. We state the relevant conjectures
in their finite-dimensional left-module forms before giving the corollary. For a finite-dimensional
algebra Γ, write
                                   0 −→ Γ Γ −→ I 0 −→ I 1 −→ · · ·
for its minimal injective resolution. Its dominant dimension is the supremum of the integers d ≥ 0
for which I j is projective whenever 0 ≤ j < d, and is infinite when every I j is projective. The
classical Nakayama conjecture asserts that infinite dominant dimension forces self-injectivity. The
generalized Nakayama conjecture asserts that every indecomposable injective occurs as a summand
of some I j ; equivalently, for every simple module S, some ExtiΓ (S, Γ) with i ≥ 0 is nonzero. The
strong Nakayama conjecture asserts that a finite module V with ExtiΓ (V, Γ) = 0 for all i ≥ 0 is zero.

Corollary 1.2 (Homological conjectures). Let A, M be as in Theorem 1.1. For every field extension

                                      ΓK = EndAK (AK ⊕ MK )op .

Then ΓK is a finite-dimensional K-algebra with the following properties, in the category of finite left
ΓK -modules.
   1. Every term of the minimal injective resolution of ΓK is projective, but idΓK ΓK = ∞.

   2. There is a nonzero simple module S such that ExtiΓK (S, ΓK ) = 0 for every i ≥ 0.

   3. There is a projective-injective Wakamatsu tilting module W which is not tilting.
Consequently the classical, generalized, and strong Nakayama conjectures, the Auslander–Gorenstein

An exact complex P of projectives is totally acyclic if HomG (P, G) is exact. If c0 P = M , we call
P a complete resolution of M . The comparison below also applies when G is not self-injective.

developed by Buchweitz [5]. We give the comparison and truncation arguments in our conventions.

self-orthogonal module MK . Choose a nonprojective indecomposable summand N in one block B
of AK . The block B is indecomposable and symmetric, and ExtiB (N, N ) = 0 for every i > 0, since
these groups are direct summands of ExtiAK (MK , MK ).
    Chen, Fang and Xi [8, proof of Theorem 5.13] construct from such a pair, over any field, reduced
mirror-reflective algebras Tn that are indecomposable and symmetric. Their Corollary 5.12(1), with
Definition 4.7, gives an idempotent stratification of length at least n. Theorem 1.2(2) supplies the
stated successive nontrivial standard recollements of unbounded derived categories. Taking n = L
proves the claim.
