                                                Abstract
          We prove that strict comparison in the extended-functional sense implies Jiang–Su absorption
      for separable simple nuclear non-elementary C ∗ -algebras. This resolves the corresponding
      implication of the Toms–Winter regularity problem, including nonunital algebras and allowing
      unbounded traces. More generally, every separable nuclear C ∗ -algebra whose Cuntz semigroup
      is almost unperforated and fully almost divisible absorbs the Jiang–Su algebra.


1     Introduction
Comparison asks whether numerical size determines order among positive operators. Divisibility
asks whether those operators can be split into pieces of nearly equal size. For nuclear C ∗ -algebras,
these questions are tied to tensorial absorption of the Jiang–Su algebra Z:

                                             A∼
                                              = A ⊗min Z.
Jiang and Su constructed Z as a simple infinite-dimensional unital algebra with the Elliott invariant
of the complex numbers [19]. Its absorption is a central regularity property in the classification
program. The purpose of this paper is to pass from comparison and divisibility in the Cuntz
semigroup to this tensorial regularity, including algebras without a unit and algebras with ideals.

1.1   Comparison and the simple theorem
All algebras in this paper are complex. Let K be the compact operators on a separable infinite-
dimensional Hilbert space. For a, b ∈ (A ⊗ K)+ , write a ≾ b if vj∗ bvj → a in norm for some sequence
(vj ) in A ⊗ K. Mutual comparison defines the Cuntz classes [a]. Their ordered semigroup Cu(A)
has addition given by orthogonal sum and suprema of increasing sequences [12]. A functional on
Cu(A) is a map to [0, ∞] preserving zero, addition, order, and increasing suprema. Write F(Cu(A))
for the cone of all these functionals.
     We use the following precise extended-functional convention for strict comparison: for every
x, y ∈ Cu(A),                                                )
                       φ(x) ≤ φ(y) for every φ ∈ F(Cu(A)),

                       φ(x) < φ(y) whenever 0 < φ(y) < ∞
The second line concerns the same cone of functionals. Arithmetic at zero and infinity is extended

    Rørdam proved that Z-absorption forces almost unperforation of the finite-matrix Cuntz
semigroup and, in the simple exact unital setting, the corresponding trace comparison theorem [26,
Theorem 4.5 and Corollary 4.6]. The reverse implication is the strict-comparison-to-Z-stability part
of the Toms–Winter regularity problem; see [30, Conjecture 5 and Problem XVIII]. Theorem 1.1
gives a positive resolution of this implication with the extended-functional convention above. Its
nonunital formulation includes stably projectionless algebras and allows unbounded traces.
    The comparison argument has a stronger intermediate scope. Theorem 5.1 proves, for every
simple exact non-elementary A satisfying Equation (1.1), that each x ∈ Cu(A) and integer N ≥ 1
admit a single class u with N u ≤ x ≤ (N + 1)u. Neither separability nor nuclearity enters that
theorem. Nuclearity is used in constructing central maps from this order-theoretic information.

1.2   The nonsimple absorption mechanism
The first order property we require is almost unperforation:

                      (l + 1)x ≤ ly   =⇒     x≤y        (x, y ∈ Cu(A), l ≥ 1).                   (1.2)

The second is full almost divisibility: for every x ∈ Cu(A) and integer N ≥ 1, there is u ∈ Cu(A)
such that
                                         N u ≤ x ≤ (N + 1)u.                                 (1.3)
Here l is also an integer. We call the conjunction of these two properties (C). The same class u
must satisfy both inequalities in Equation (1.3).
   The comparison convention in Equation (1.1) is equivalent to almost unperforation of Cu(A) [15,
Proposition 6.2]. Lemma 2.2 explains this equivalence, including the zero and infinite values. Thus
the extended-functional formulation agrees with the usual Cuntz-semigroup comparison condition.
   To state the central conclusion, fix a free ultrafilter ω on N. The norm ultrapower and its central
annihilator quotient are

             Aω = ℓ∞ (N, A)/{(an ) : lim ∥an ∥ = 0},      Fω (A) = (Aω ∩ A′ )/ Ann(A),
                                      n→ω

where A is the constant copy and Ann(A) = {z ∈ Aω : zA = Az = 0}. For separable A, a sequential
approximate unit represents the unit of Fω (A). The zero algebra absorbs Z immediately; the
central-map statement below is formulated for nonzero algebras.

Theorem 1.2 (Absorption from condition (C)). Let A be a nonzero separable nuclear C ∗ -algebra
satisfying (C). For every fixed free ultrafilter ω on N, there is a unital ∗-homomorphism Z → Fω (A).
Consequently A = ∼ A ⊗min Z.

Corollary 1.3 (The canonical Cuntz-semigroup map). Let A be a separable nuclear C ∗ -algebra,
and let ι : A → A ⊗min Z be ι(a) = a ⊗ 1Z . If Cu(ι) : Cu(A) → Cu(A ⊗min Z) is an isomorphism,
then A ∼= A ⊗min Z.

    The isomorphism in this hypothesis is the map induced by ι. Corollary 1.3 resolves positively
implication (iii)⇒(ii) in the nonsimple Toms–Winter problem [30, Section 22, Problem LXXV]. It
applies to every separable nuclear A with the indicated canonical map; an additional restriction on
its elementary ideal subquotients is unnecessary.
    Condition (C) depends only on the ordered-semigroup structure. Consequently, the same
absorption theorem also removes the requirement that the Cuntz-semigroup isomorphism be the
canonical one.

stable finiteness. Separability and nuclearity remain hypotheses.

1.3    History and the main obstacles
The comparison of positive elements and their dimension functions goes back to Cuntz and Blackadar–
Handelman [13, 5]. The completed Cuntz semigroup of Coward–Elliott–Ivanescu [12] makes in-
creasing suprema part of the invariant. Elliott–Robert–Santiago’s correspondence [15] identifies its
functionals with lower semicontinuous extended quasitraces. For exact algebras, Haagerup’s additiv-
ity theorem [17], in the extended form of Blanchard–Kirchberg recalled in [15, Remark 4.3], makes
these quasitraces traces. The associated rank is dτ (a) = limn τ (a1/n ): the functional corresponding
to τ sends [a] to dτ (a).
    The reverse comparison implication has a substantial history of partial results. Matui and
Sato proved it for unital separable simple nuclear infinite-dimensional algebras with finitely many
extremal traces, using excision in small central sequences and property (SI) [22, Theorem 1.1]. Sato,
Kirchberg–Rørdam, and Toms–White–Winter extended the tracial-boundary approach to nonempty
compact extreme boundaries of finite covering dimension [29, Corollary 1.2], [21, Corollary 7.9], [32,
Corollary 4.7]. Castillejos, Evington, Tikuisis, and White isolated the remaining uniform central
divisibility in trace: for simple separable unital nuclear algebras with nonempty tracial state space,
strict comparison together with uniform property Γ is equivalent to Z-stability [8, Theorem 5.6].
Uniform property Γ requires approximately central partitions that split the trace values of finitely
many elements evenly, uniformly over all traces. The present argument constructs central maps
from condition (C) without imposing a tracial-boundary or uniform-Γ hypothesis.
    The Toms–Winter problem also links these properties to finite nuclear dimension, a finite-colour
form of completely positive approximation. Winter’s absorption theorem and the converse of Castille-
jos, Evington, Tikuisis, White, and Winter establish the equivalence of finite nuclear dimension
and Z-stability in the simple separable unital nuclear non-elementary setting [34, Corollary 6.3] [9,
Theorem A]. The comparison implication is the remaining direction addressed here.
    The distinction between comparison and divisibility is already visible in the range of possible
rank functions of positive elements. Thiel proved that strict comparison yields almost divisibility
and the canonical Cuntz-semigroup isomorphism for separable simple unital non-elementary algebras
of stable rank one [31, Corollary 8.12]. Theorem 5.1 uses exactness without a stable-rank-one
hypothesis. The fractional spectral discretization in Dadarlat–Toms [14, Theorem 5.1 and its proof]
follows earlier rank-realization ideas of Brown–Perera–Toms [7, Section 5]. Our first construction
moves trace values into a hereditary subalgebra of small rank before taking integer spectral cuts.
The resulting error is at most one support rank, regardless of the norm of the transferred element
or the number of spectral levels. Proposition 5.4 gives the unital uniform-trace version of this
elementary construction.
    For nonunital algebras, there may be no normalized trace space on which all errors can be
bounded. The substitute is one fixed nonzero spectral cut of the element being divided. Simplicity

                                                   3
makes its rank positive and finite for every functional that is finite and positive somewhere, even
when the original element has infinite rank. We measure all rounding errors relative to this same
cut and make them summable. Zero functionals and functionals infinite on every nonzero class are
handled separately. This keeps the full extended trace cone available without requiring a common
normalization; the precise three-way distinction is proved in Lemma 3.2.
     Comparison and divisibility are also studied together through the notion of pureness. For the
completed semigroup, the usual almost divisibility condition asks for N u ≤ x and x′ ≤ (N + 1)u
whenever x′ is compactly contained in x; together with almost unperforation this defines pureness
[2]. Full almost divisibility as used here implies that condition. For separable simple unital
non-elementary algebras of locally finite nuclear dimension, Winter proved that pureness implies
Jiang–Su absorption [34, Corollary 6.2]. Under the same local nuclear-dimension hypothesis, he
also obtained an abstract criterion using W (A), the Cuntz semigroup of positive elements in finite
matrices over A [34, Corollary 6.4]. Corollary 1.4 instead uses the completed invariant Cu(A) and
has no local nuclear-dimension assumption. The tensor-product approach to regularity of abstract
Cuntz semigroups was developed by Antoine, Perera, and Thiel [2]; more recent work of Antoine,
Perera, Thiel, and Vilalta characterizes pureness by controlled comparison and functional divisibility
[3, Theorem C]. Regularity of Cu(A) and regularity of the central sequence algebra are distinct
inputs. In particular, Perera, Thiel, and Vilalta characterize Jiang–Su absorption by pureness of
the central sequence algebra for every separable A [24, Theorem 3.6]. Theorem 1.2 constructs the
required central structure from the stated order properties of Cu(A) itself.
     For the central construction, a matrix cone means a completely positive contractive order-zero
map from Mk : it preserves orthogonality of positive elements. Its weight is the image of 1Mk .
Norm-central cones take values in Aω ∩ A′ , with coordinate maps whose centrality and order-zero
errors tend to zero along ω.
     Robert and Tikuisis developed a nonsimple route from regularity to central structure using
finite nuclear dimension [25, Theorem 1.1]. For separable algebras of finite nuclear dimension, they
characterized absorption by the existence of two full orthogonal elements in the central sequence
algebra [25, Theorem 1.2]. Our hypothesis is instead the order condition (C), with no bound on
nuclear dimension. The nonsimple problem has a different obstruction. A positive element can be
large in every representation of interest without a single uniform integer controlling all the associated
ideal comparisons. At the same time, traces may be finite only after hereditary localization. The
proof treats these two forms of largeness separately. Pure-state compression and support shrinking
construct norm-visible central matrix cones. Convex order-zero nuclear approximations, originating
in the work of Hirshberg, Kirchberg, and White [18] and refined by Brown, Carrión, and White [6],
supply cones large for bounded traces. We localize the latter estimates and then remove a relative
trace ideal to obtain norm centrality.
     The matrix-cone calculus uses Winter and Zacharias’s structure theorem for order-zero maps
[35]. The final packing step is an instance of Gabe’s approximate domination theorem [16]; its
weighted-inclusion form is proved here. Choi–Effros lifting and Arveson extension allow us to retain
completely positive maps during diagonalization [10, 4]. The absorption step uses the structural
properties of Z established by Jiang–Su, Toms–Winter, and Rørdam–Winter [19, 33, 27]. Nawata’s
nonunital central dimension-drop criterion [23, Proposition 5.1] is implemented here by an explicit
lifting and intertwining argument.

    Section 2 introduces the common Cuntz-semigroup and trace conventions, together with the

two sides of the divisibility sandwich, completing the simple route to condition (C).
    Section 6 begins the common absorption argument. It derives condition (C) from the canonical
Cuntz-semigroup map and develops its consequences for rank comparison and divisible matrix

constructions. Sections 7 and 8 construct the norm-visible cones. Two-support shrinking preserves
the representations detecting a prescribed positive cut while making cross terms small. Pure-

visibility supplies finite coordinate ideal multiplicities. Sections 9 and 10 construct the tracial cones.
Two support compressions keep their errors independent of the number of nuclear approximation
summands; hereditary localization and a relative trace ideal turn tracial centrality into norm
centrality.

fixed ideal multiplicities. Spectral margins first produce exact coordinate Cuntz inequalities and
then bound the comparison witnesses independently of matrix size. This permits a slowly growing
supply of orthogonal equivalent central slots. Section 12 packs finite simultaneous compressions into
those slots; approximate-unit tests make the packed element bounded and central. Section 13 turns
the resulting relations into dimension-drop maps, constructs a single unital central copy of Z, and
performs the nonunital intertwining. Section 14 assembles the four stated results.

2.2   Extended traces and rank
We allow all lower semicontinuous extended traces τ : A+ → [0, ∞], without dense finiteness. Thus
τ is additive and positively homogeneous on positives, is lower semicontinuous in norm, vanishes at
zero, and satisfies τ (x∗ x) = τ (xx∗ ). For b ≥ 0, write

                                          dτ (b) = lim τ (b1/j ),
                                                   j→∞

first rescaling to a contraction when necessary. The rank is unchanged by positive scalar rescaling.
Traces are extended to matrices and to stabilization using the unnormalized matrix trace: on a
positive infinite matrix, the extended trace is the sum of the traces of its diagonal entries. Additivity
and lower semicontinuity justify these sums, and traciality follows by summing the squared moduli
of the matrix entries. For a positive contraction b and s > 0,

                              τ (b) ≤ dτ (b),     dτ ((b − s)+ ) ≤ s−1 τ (b).                      (2.3)

trace we use its normal extension to the bidual, so dτ (b) = τ (pb ).

2.3   The comparison convention
The extended-functional convention in Equation (1.1) is equivalent to almost unperforation. The

13     Dimension-drop relations and Jiang–Su absorption
Proposition 12.4 supplies a matrix cone and an element filling its defect. We first turn their
relations into unital maps from individual prime dimension-drop algebras. These maps need not
be compatible: nuclear approximation and bounded completely positive lifts will instead produce
a single unital map Z → Fω (A). Finally, half-flip unitaries and an approximate intertwining give
A∼ = A ⊗ Z.

                                                              55
     This route from central dimension-drop maps to absorption is established. For separable, possibly
nonunital algebras, Nawata [23, Proposition 5.1] states the criterion using the sequence quotient. It
fits the strongly self-absorbing framework of Toms–Winter [33]; a formulation for arbitrary strongly
self-absorbing algebras is given in [24, Theorem 2.2]. We give the lifting and intertwining steps for
the fixed ultrafilter quotient used here.

13.3    From prime blocks to a central copy of Z

13.4    Connected half-flip unitaries and their lifts
Lemma 13.5. The unitary groups of Z and Z ⊗ Z are path connected. If q : D → D/J is a unital
quotient map, every unitary in the identity component of U(D/J) lifts to a unitary in D.

Proof. We first check connectedness for a prime block I(r, s), with gcd(r, s) = 1. A unitary is a
continuous path in U (rs) whose endpoints belong to

                               H0 = U (r) ⊗ 1s ,        H1 = 1r ⊗ U (s).

Both endpoint groups are connected. Attach a path in H0 from 1 to the initial value and a path in
H1 from the final value to 1. Attaching these paths gradually, while reparametrizing the original
path, is a homotopy through paths satisfying the same endpoint conditions. The result is a based
loop in U (rs). Changing the attached H0 path by an endpoint loop changes its determinant winding
by an arbitrary multiple of s; changing the H1 path changes it by an arbitrary multiple of r. Bezout’s
identity therefore permits choices for which the total winding is zero. The determinant identifies
π1 (U (rs)) with Z, so this loop contracts with its basepoint fixed. That contraction retains endpoint
value 1, and hence remains in the prime block. Thus every unitary of I(r, s) is connected to 1.
    For a unitary in the injective unital inductive limit Z, choose a sufficiently close element of a
stage. Its two unitary defects are small, so its polar part is a unitary in the same stage and is still
close to the original unitary. Two unitaries of distance less than 1 are joined by the logarithm path
for their quotient. Connectedness of the stage unitary group proves connectedness of U(Z). Strong
self-absorption gives an isomorphism Z ⊗ Z ∼   = Z, proving the same assertion for the tensor square.
    Finally, a unitary v connected to 1 in D/J is a finite product of exponentials of selfadjoints.
To see this, subdivide a continuous unitary path finely enough that consecutive values differ by
less than 1, and take a selfadjoint logarithm of each consecutive quotient. Lift these finitely many
selfadjoints through q and multiply their exponentials in D. The resulting unitary lifts v.

Lemma 13.6. Let A be a separable C ∗ -algebra, and suppose there is a unital homomorphism

                                 ∥W ∗ (a ⊗ z)W − (acz ) ⊗ 1∥ ≤ δ∥a∥.                            (13.12)

The preceding construction has used separability, nuclearity, and condition (C), rather than the
canonical-map hypothesis. We now apply it to the comparison and Cuntz-semigroup criteria in the

Proof of Theorem 1.2. Fix the free ultrafilter in the theorem. Proposition 13.4, through the sup-

Proof of Corollary 1.4. The forward implication follows from functoriality of Cu under ∗-
