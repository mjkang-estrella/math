                                                  Abstract
         We give a negative answer to Kirchberg’s norm-ultrapower embedding problem. We construct
      an explicit separable unital full group C ∗ -algebra that admits no unital embedding into B ω , for
      any nonzero unital nuclear C ∗ -algebra B and any free ultrafilter ω on N. In particular, it does
      not embed unitally into O2ω .

1    The embedding problem
Throughout, C ∗ -algebras are complex, and an embedding is an injective ∗-homomorphism. We state
unit preservation explicitly. For a nonzero unital C ∗ -algebra B and a free ultrafilter ω on N, its
norm ultrapower is

                      B ω = ℓ∞ (N, B)/Iω (B),         Iω (B) = {(bn ) : lim ∥bn ∥ = 0}.
                                                                         ω

Here ℓ∞ (N, B) is the algebra of bounded sequences, and the quotient uses the operator norm. The
Cuntz algebra O2 is the universal unital C ∗ -algebra generated by two isometries s1 , s2 satisfying
s1 s∗1 + s2 s∗2 = 1; it was introduced by Cuntz [2].



                                                      1
    The embedding theorem of Kirchberg–Phillips [8] states that every separable unital exact C ∗ -
algebra embeds unitally into O2 . Here exactness means that minimal tensoring preserves short
exact sequences. Passing from O2 to its norm ultrapower asks how far this universality extends
beyond exact algebras. The techniques behind the embedding theorem led Kirchberg to consider
this possibility, as recounted in [6, Section 1].
    Kirchberg’s embedding problem asks whether every separable unital C ∗ -algebra admits a unital
embedding into O2ω ; see Goldbring–Sinclair [6, Section 3.1]. We give an explicit counterexample.
The unital formulation is equivalent to the formulation without unit preservation [6, Section 3.1].
This question concerns norm ultrapowers of a nuclear C ∗ -algebra. It is distinct from the tensor-norm
formulation of Kirchberg’s conjecture and from Connes’ embedding problem, as emphasized in [6,
Section 1].

Theorem 1.1. Let

                       G = Z[1/2]3 ⋊ SL3 (Z) × Z ,        (M, k) · v = 2k M v,
                                                      


and let A = C ∗ (G) be its full group C ∗ -algebra. Then A is separable and unital. For every nonzero
unital nuclear C ∗ -algebra B and every free ultrafilter ω on N, there is no unital embedding

                                             A −→ B ω .

In particular, A does not embed unitally into O2ω .

    The group is countable and independent of both B and ω; no separability assumption on B
is needed. The proof treats an arbitrary fixed nuclear B throughout. Specializing to the nuclear
algebra O2 gives the negative answer to the embedding problem; for its nuclearity, see [11, Section 4,
pp. 80–82]. Section 6 gives the consequences for existential closure, permanence of O2ω -embeddability
under crossed products, and good nuclear witnesses, with the relevant notions defined there.

1.1   Previous work and the new obstruction
Recent approaches study computability and tensor norms. Fox–Goldbring–Hart [5] derive
computability-theoretic consequences of a positive answer, using the computable presentation of O2 .
Goldbring–Sinclair give a conditional route to a negative answer through a weakly explicit minimal
tensor norm: an effective choice of universal formulas approximating the minimal tensor-norm
predicates to a fixed accuracy. They leave this property of O2 open [7, Definition 4.16, Theorem 4.18
and Question 4.20]. Our obstruction instead comes from the representation theory of an explicit
discrete group.
    The group configuration already appears in the nonfiniteness arguments of Sauers [13, Section 2.4]
and Eckhardt [4, Theorem 3.1 and Example 3.2]. They show that the full algebra of the ascending
HNN extension G is nonfinite: it contains a proper isometry v, so v ∗ v = 1 but vv ∗ ̸= 1. Its
property (T) base group Γ = Z3 ⋊ SL3 (Z) and its finite-index scalar self-embeddings are recorded
in de Cornulier [3, Proposition 1.1 and its proof]. Sauers and Eckhardt also construct non-MF
groups: groups admitting no injective homomorphism into the unitary group of any norm matrix
        Q         L
corona j Mdj / j Mdj , with positive integer dimensions dj . Here the product consists of bounded
sequences and the direct sum is the ideal of sequences tending to zero in norm [4, Theorem 4.3][13,
Sections 3–5]. Eckhardt’s example is an associated wreath product. The target class here is different:
nonfiniteness of C ∗ (G) alone cannot exclude a unital embedding into O2ω , which itself contains
proper isometries.

    The additional ingredient is a finiteness statement for approximate finite-dimensional representa-
tion types inside a nuclear algebra (Lemma 4.3). Its tensor-product argument follows Wassermann’s
method [16], as explained by Manuilov–Thomsen [10, Section 2.1]. The statement applies to an
arbitrary tuple of unitaries, even when the tuple satisfies no group relations. This flexibility is
essential because coordinate lifts of an ultrapower representation are only approximately mul-
tiplicative. A dimension-independent estimate allows the representation type to vary with the
coordinate. Finite-index induction then relates members of the resulting finite sets by composition
with the self-embedding. These two passages turn the group configuration into the norm-ultrapower
obstruction.

1.2    Proof outline
The subgroup Γ = Z3 ⋊ SL3 (Z) of G has property (T). Conjugation by the generator t of the
last Z-factor sends Γ onto the index-eight subgroup θ(Γ), where θ(v, M ) = (2v, M ). There is a
finite-dimensional irreducible representation π∗ of Γ such that π∗ ◦ θj is inequivalent to π∗ for every
j ≥ 1. Section 2 verifies these facts.
    Fix a finite generating set S of Γ for which property (T) gives a spectral gap. Suppose a unital
embedding A → B ω exists, and choose unitary lifts xn (g) ∈ B of its group unitaries. For each n,
consider the nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations π for which the tuple
(xn (s) ⊗ π̄(s))s∈S has spectral minimum below a fixed small threshold for the sum of squared
displacements from 1. Here π̄ is the conjugate representation. Sections 3 and 4 specify the threshold
and show that the resulting set Dn of representation classes is finite.
    Write π ≺ ρ when π occurs as a unitary subrepresentation of ρ, and call π ′ a predecessor of π if
π ≺ π ′ ◦ θ. Faithfulness of the embedding places [π∗ ] in Dn on a set belonging to ω. In Section 5, the
spectral gap for every sequence of finite-dimensional representations supplies, on one set belonging
to ω, the simultaneous implication

                        [π] ∈ Dn    =⇒     some [π ′ ] ∈ Dn satisfies π ≺ π ′ ◦ θ.

Starting at π∗ and repeatedly choosing a predecessor must give a repetition in the finite set Dn .
Irreducibility then makes π∗ periodic under composition with θ, a contradiction.

4    Finiteness of approximate representation matches
We prove that, for a fixed unitary tuple u in a nuclear algebra, only finitely many finite-dimensional
irreducible representation classes satisfy d(u, π) < a. The tuple need not satisfy any group relations.
This distinction will allow us to apply the result to coordinate lifts from a norm ultrapower.
    We retain the notation and constants of Section 3. For C ∗ -algebras B and C, the minimal
tensor product B ⊗min C is realized by faithful representations on separate Hilbert spaces; the
maximal tensor product B ⊗max C is characterized by pairs of commuting representations on one
Hilbert space. We use the tensor-norm characterization of nuclearity developed by Takesaki and
Lance [14, 9]: B is nuclear if these two tensor norms agree for every C. We first obtain a lower
bound when the second tensor factor has no finite-dimensional subrepresentations. The passage
from a vector to its reduced density operator removes the arbitrary unitaries in the first factor.

5     From an ultrapower embedding to a finite contradiction
To prove Theorem 1.1, fix a nonzero unital nuclear C ∗ -algebra B and a free ultrafilter ω on N. A
set of indices is called ω-large if it belongs to ω. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that there is a
unital injective ∗-homomorphism
                                            Φ : C ∗ (G) −→ B ω .
We will associate a finite set of irreducible representations of Γ to each coordinate of Φ. Conjugation
by t will force these sets to contain predecessors under restriction by θ. The resulting finite chains
contradict the choice of π∗ .

5.4    Finite chains force periodicity
The analytic argument is complete. We finish with the elementary consequence of finiteness that
makes the predecessor property incompatible with π∗ . Consider a chain of finite-dimensional
irreducible unitary representations τ0 = π, τ1 , . . . of Γ whose classes lie in a finite set D and satisfy
τi ≺ τi+1 ◦ θ. Finiteness gives indices l ≥ 0 and p ≥ 1 with τl ≃ τl+p . Figure 1 separates the initial
segment of this chain from the repeated segment. The key point is that periodicity at the repeated
class forces every pullback of that representation to remain irreducible, so that periodicity reaches
the starting class.

Lemma 5.4. Let θ : Γ → Γ be a homomorphism, and let D be a finite set of equivalence classes of
nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations of Γ. Suppose that every [π] ∈ D has

translations Z3 , whereas π∗ ◦ θp acts trivially on them: θp multiplies these translations by 2p , and

G and the algebra C ∗ (G) are independent of these choices. Thus the same algebra has no unital
embedding into B ω for any such B and any free ω. Taking B = O2 proves the stated negative
answer to Kirchberg’s embedding problem.


6    Consequences for existential closure and nuclear approximation
We spell out the model-theoretic consequences of the negative answer to Kirchberg’s embedding
problem. In the unital C ∗ -algebra language, quantifier-free formulas are continuous combinations
of norms of ∗-polynomials; variables range over prescribed norm balls. A unital algebra E is
existentially closed if, for every unital inclusion E ⊆ F , every finite parameter tuple a from E, and
every quantifier-free formula φ(a, y) in a finite tuple y,
                                        inf φ(a, y) = inf φ(a, y),
                                        y∈E              y∈F

where y ranges over the corresponding products of prescribed norm balls. Thus an extension cannot
improve the infimum of a finite quantifier-free test over E [6, Section 2.1].
    A parameter-free finite condition consists of finitely many strict inequalities φj (x) < rj with
quantifier-free φj . It is satisfiable when some tuple in a unital C ∗ -algebra satisfies them. The last
assertion below makes the failure of good nuclear witnesses explicit as a positive lower bound on
the approximation error of every finite-dimensional completely positive contractive factorization of
every realizing tuple.
    The KEP equivalences of Goldbring–Sinclair [6, Proposition 3.2 and Theorems 3.3 and 3.7] give
these direct consequences of Theorem 1.1.
