                                                        Abstract
          We prove that every bounded Hochschild cocycle of degree at least two on a complex von
      Neumann algebra, with values in the algebra itself, has a bounded primitive. Together with the
      established degree-one inner-derivation theorem, this resolves the Kadison–Ringrose cohomology
      conjecture positively.


1    Introduction
The continuous Hochschild cohomology of an operator algebra measures the obstruction to solving its
bounded cocycle equations. The theory also connects these equations with stability of multiplication
under small perturbations [SS95, Chapter 7]. For a von Neumann algebra acting on itself by
multiplication, the degree-one equation is the derivation equation, and its vanishing is the inner-
derivation theorem of Kadison and Sakai [Kad66, Sak66]. The higher-degree theory was developed
by Johnson, Kadison, and Ringrose [KR71a, KR71b, JKR72]. Throughout, boundedness means
ordinary operator-norm boundedness of a multilinear map. No complete-boundedness condition is
imposed.
    For a complex von Neumann algebra M , let Cbk (M, M ) be the Banach space of bounded complex
k-linear maps M k → M , with norm ∥f ∥ = sup∥a1 ∥,...,∥ak ∥≤1 ∥f (a1 , . . . , ak )∥, and put Cb0 (M, M ) = M .

   The Kadison–Ringrose cohomology conjecture asks whether Hbk (M, M ) = 0 for every complex
von Neumann algebra M and every positive integer k. Sinclair and Smith trace this self-coefficient
formulation to 1967 [SS98b, p. 3376]. Our main result settles the higher degrees.
Theorem 1.1. Let M be a complex von Neumann algebra and let k ≥ 2. Every f ∈ Cbk (M, M )
satisfying df = 0 has the form f = dg for some g ∈ Cbk−1 (M, M ). Consequently Hbk (M, M ) = 0.
   Together with the inner-derivation theorem, Theorem 1.1 resolves the Kadison–Ringrose coho-
mology conjecture positively.

1.1   Prior work
Kadison and Ringrose proved vanishing for type I and hyperfinite von Neumann algebras [KR71a,
Theorem 4.4] [KR71b, Theorem 3.1]. Their extended-cobounding theorem also gives a primitive for
an M -valued cocycle on a general concrete von Neumann algebra M ⊂ B(H) when the primitive
may take values in B(H) [KR71b, Theorem 2.4]; keeping the values in the original algebra is the
essential self-coefficient problem here. Johnson, Kadison, and Ringrose then identified continuous
cohomology with separately normal cohomology [JKR72, Theorem 5.6]. The completely bounded
methods of Christensen, Effros, and Sinclair [CES87] established vanishing whenever the type
II1 central summand absorbs the hyperfinite II1 factor, including all algebras without a type II1
summand; see the precise restatement in [CPSS03, p. 636, equation (1.1)].
    The remaining finite-algebra problem has several substantial positive cases. Sinclair and Smith
proved vanishing for algebras with separable predual and a Cartan subalgebra [SS98a]; Cameron
removed the separability hypothesis for type II1 factors with a Cartan maximal abelian subalge-
bra [Cam09, Theorem 3.2]. Christensen, Pop, Sinclair, and Smith proved vanishing for all type II1
factors with property Γ, including nonseparable ones [CPSS03, Theorem 7.2]. Qian and Shen
extended this direction to type II1 algebras with separable predual and their generalized prop-
erty Γ [QS15, Theorem 6.4]. In degree two, Pop and Smith proved vanishing for the tensor product
of any two type II1 von Neumann algebras, with no separability assumption [PS10, Theorem 4.5].
Theorem 1.1 removes the structural restrictions on the finite algebra in every degree k ≥ 2.
    The Cartan argument of Sinclair and Smith also provides a useful methodological comparison.
They show that complete boundedness in the last cocycle variable suffices. They use a projection
of Christensen and Sinclair [CS95] from completely bounded linear maps onto right module maps,
in the form recalled in [SS98b, Theorem 3.4], to obtain a primitive [SS98b, Section 4]. Thus the
passage from averaging one variable to a module identity already has a central role in the earlier
theory.

1.2   The new ingredients
We obtain the needed module identity by averaging over a single unitary random walk. Averaging
one cocycle input first gives only a harmonic map; the main new input is a Liouville theorem that
makes such a map commute with right multiplication. Suppose that M is a type II1 algebra with
separable predual and faithful normal tracial state τ , and write ∥x∥2 = τ (x∗ x)1/2 . Let U(M ) denote
its unitary group. We construct a symmetric, countably supported probability measure μ on U(M ),
with a 2-dense set of atoms, such that

No positivity or complete-boundedness assumption is imposed. The measure is fixed before the
harmonic map is chosen.
    For a separately normal cocycle, Akemann’s continuity theorem [Ake67, Corollary III.10] supplies
the stated continuity in the last input. A uniform continuity modulus survives the averaging limit,
even though that limit need not remain normal, and also passes to the tracial ultrapowers used in
the Liouville proof.
    The proof of (1.2) combines probabilistic and algebraic arguments. The finite free configurations
come from Popa’s construction of free-independent sequences [Pop95], in the formulation of his later
ultraproduct independence theorem [Pop14]. These give finite unitary configurations with prescribed
small word traces. A hierarchy of increasingly rare, increasingly large sets of such unitaries gives a

single random walk whose long independent products approach free Haar joint moments along a
subsequence. Only moment convergence is required.
    Second, we use the classical harmonic-martingale construction [Fur63, p. 415] to obtain an
L2 (M, τ )-valued martingale. A nonconstant limit would produce free Haar unitaries g1 , g2 , . . . in
a tracial ultrapower and distinct a, b ∈ M . The coordinatewise lift of T to that ultrapower sends
each nonempty signed word w with pairwise distinct unsigned indices to aw or bw, according to
the sign of its first letter; here a, b are identified with their constant images. We prove that these
identities are impossible for a bounded map with the stated continuity. The argument averages free
generators, discards repeated surviving indices using Haagerup’s free-group length estimate [Haa79],
and passes to a unitary polar part. Positive and negative powers of that unitary would then carry
different left multipliers. Uniformly bounded sine sums and a spectral partition force the multipliers
to agree. This rigidity statement, proved in Theorem 4.1, is independent of the cocycle problem.
    Finally, in this tracial type II1 setting, for a separately normal cocycle f we take a pointwise
ultraweak cluster point of the Cesàro averages in its last input. The Liouville theorem identifies
the resulting fixed map as a right module map in that input. Averaging the original cocycle
equation then gives a primitive g with ∥g∥ ≤ ∥f ∥. Together with compactness, this uniform estimate
removes the separability assumption. We then partition the type II1 summand into central pieces
admitting faithful normal tracial states. Lemma 5.4 corrects mixed inputs from distinct pieces, and
uniform bounds permit their assembly. The classical vanishing theorem handles the complementary
summand.

1.3     Conventions and classical reductions
All von Neumann algebras and maps are complex, and subalgebras are unital unless otherwise
stated. A finite algebra is called type II1 here when it has no nonzero type I central summand; its
center need not be trivial. A map f : M k → M is separately normal if it is ultraweakly continuous
in each input with the others fixed. For a finite algebra with faithful normal tracial state, the
2-norm on bounded sets determines the strong-star topology. The inherited traces on subalgebras
and tracial ultrapowers will usually also be denoted by τ .
    We use the following established cohomology results.
Proposition 1.2 (Classical reductions). Let M be a von Neumann algebra and k ≥ 2.
    (i) If f ∈ Cbk (M, M ) is a cocycle, there are a separately normal bounded cocycle f0 and a bounded
        (k − 1)-cochain b such that f = f0 + db.
 (ii) If M has no type II1 central summand, then Hbk (M, M ) = 0.
    Part (i) is the Johnson–Kadison–Ringrose normal-reduction theorem [JKR72, Lemma 5.4 and
Theorem 5.6]; its formulation for self-coefficients is recalled in [CPSS03, Section 2]. Part (ii) combines
the classical type I and properly infinite vanishing results; see [KR71a, Theorem 4.4], [CES87], and
[CPSS03, p. 636, equation (1.1)]. Both statements concern ordinary continuous cohomology and
the actual image of d. These are the only cohomological inputs in the proof.
    Section 2 constructs the walk. Section 3 proves its Liouville property using Theorem 4.1, proved
in Section 4. Section 5 constructs bounded primitives and completes the reductions to arbitrary M .

further conditions whose failure probabilities tend to zero to be imposed simultaneously at any fixed
stage of a later selection argument.


3     A Liouville theorem for the walk
We show that a bounded harmonic map for the walk of Theorem 2.1, subject to continuity on
bounded sets in the tracial 2-norm, is left multiplication by its value at 1. The probabilistic part of
the proof produces free Haar unitaries such that, on words with pairwise distinct unsigned indices, a
single map acts by one of two left multipliers according to the sign of the first letter. Theorem 4.1,
proved in Section 4, forces those multipliers to coincide for bounded linear maps with the same
continuity in any finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state.

5     Bounded primitives for Hochschild cocycles
We first apply Theorem 3.3 to separately normal cocycles on a type II1 algebra with separable
predual and a faithful normal tracial state. The resulting uniform bound for a primitive will allow
us first to remove separability and then to combine central summands. Throughout this section,
normality of a linear map means ultraweak continuity, and separate normality has the corresponding
meaning for multilinear maps.

5.3    Removing the separability assumption
Uniform primitive bounds and pointwise ultraweak compactness were used to remove separability
in [CPSS03, Section 7]. As in that argument, conditional expectations allow the approximating
subalgebras to be chosen independently for each finite set of inputs.
Proposition 5.3. Let M be any type II1 von Neumann algebra admitting a faithful normal tracial
state. Every separately normal cocycle f ∈ Cbk (M, M ), k ≥ 2, has a bounded primitive g with
∥g∥ ≤ ∥f ∥.

unital von Neumann subalgebra NE containing E, the finite set f (E k ), and unital matrix systems of
every size m ≥ 2. Such systems exist in a type II1 algebra: the unit can be divided into m equivalent
orthogonal projections [Bla06, Examples III.2.5.4(iii)], and partial isometries between them supply
the matrix units. Adjoining one system for each m adds only countably many generators.

separable: ∗-polynomials with coefficients in Q + iQ in a countable set of generators are dense in the
generated C ∗ -algebra in norm, and bounded strong approximation makes that algebra 2-norm dense
in NE . The faithful tracial representation thus gives NE separable predual. Moreover NE has no
type I central part. A nonzero homogeneous type In central summand would inherit a unital copy
of Mm (C) for every m, whereas its n-dimensional fibers cannot admit a unital copy when m > n.
Every finite type I algebra decomposes into its homogeneous type In summands. Since NE is finite,
it is therefore of type II1 .

Hbk (M, M ) = 0 for every complex von Neumann algebra M and every k ≥ 2, with the actual image
of d in the definition of cohomology and with no complete-boundedness hypothesis.
