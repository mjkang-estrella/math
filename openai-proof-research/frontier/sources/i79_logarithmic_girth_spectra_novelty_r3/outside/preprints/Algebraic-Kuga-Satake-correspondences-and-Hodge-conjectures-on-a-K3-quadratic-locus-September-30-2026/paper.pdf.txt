       Abstract. We prove the rational Hodge and generalized Hodge conjectures in every cohomological
       degree of every self-power of a projective complex K3 surface whose transcendental quadratic space,
       with its cup-product form, admits a rational isometric embedding in U⊕2           4
                                                                                Q ⊥ ⟨−1⟩ . This includes
       every ample P -polarized K3 surface, including all Picard jumps, for P = U⊕D8 (−1)⊕D4 (−1). On
       this locus we construct algebraic correspondences inducing every prescribed standard even-Clifford

       exact standard even-Clifford Kuga–Satake tensor implies both conjectures for every self-power.

1.3. Context and proof strategy. Geometric constructions for special K3 families include
Paranjape’s curve-product and Prym construction for six-line double planes [29, Sections 2–4],
and Bolognesi–Laterveer’s treatment of order-three non-symplectic automorphisms whose fixed
locus contains a curve [5, Theorem 4.4 and Section 6]. Floccari and Fu explicitly distinguish an
algebraic embedding from the prescribed Kuga–Satake map [18, Conjectures 2.4 and 2.6]; they
prove algebraicity of the latter for the K3 surfaces associated with projective singular OG6-varieties
[18, Corollary 4.7].
   For the lattice P used here, Poon computes the Clifford algebra and constructs a modular map
to quaternionic abelian eightfolds [31, Theorem 3.1.4 and Section 4]. Van Geemen and Verra study
related quaternionic Prym eightfolds and algebraic quaternionic classes [39, Sections 3–4]. These
modular and quaternionic-class constructions do not by themselves give the prescribed K3-to-
abelian correspondence. The Clifford and half-spin descriptions are classical [37, Proposition 6.3
and Example 6.6]; our recovery argument keeps the full even module and both tensor orientations.
   For powers of a K3 surface, Zarhin’s description of its Hodge group reduces tensor Hodge classes
to classical invariant theory [43]. When the endomorphism field of the transcendental Hodge
structure is CM, ordinary HC is already known for every self-power [41, Corollary 2.3]. The
totally real case also requires relative determinant tensors; Varesco identifies these generators
and explains why classes from the square alone need not suffice [41, Theorem 2.16]. For specified
four-dimensional families with generic Picard number 16, he also derives ordinary HC for all powers
from algebraicity of the Kuga–Satake correspondences [41, Theorem 0.2].

   The prescribed tensor and all periods. Section 7 upgrades a curve injection at a full special-
orthogonal period to every rational Hodge map into the full even-Clifford tensor square. Among
the nontrivial constituents, weight-one effectivity leaves the two half-spin representations; a
multiplicity-space calculation and rational Hodge-Hom descent recover the prescribed tensor. The

9.3. Hilbert schemes and moduli spaces.
Corollary 9.5. Let S be a smooth projective complex K3 surface admitting a rational isometric
embedding (TS , q) ,→ VP . For every n, r ≥ 1, rational HC and rational GHC hold in every degree
of (S [n] )r , where S [n] is the Hilbert scheme of length-n subschemes of S.
   More generally, let α ∈ Br(S) and let M be either a smooth projective moduli space of Gieseker-
stable α-twisted sheaves on S, or a smooth projective moduli space of σ-stable objects in Db (S, α)
for a generic Bridgeland stability condition σ. Then rational HC and rational GHC hold in every
degree of M r for every r ≥ 1. If M is hyperkähler, the same conclusions hold for every smooth
projective hyperkähler variety birational to M and all its self-powers.
Proof. The assertion for zero-dimensional M is immediate. Write h(−) for the Chow motive with
rational coefficients. The decomposition of de Cataldo–Migliorini [10, Theorem 6.2.1] expresses
h(S [n] ) as a sum of Tate twists of summands of motives of powers of S; the summands corresponding
to symmetric products are cut out by rational averaging projectors. For either stated moduli
space, Bülles [8, Theorem 0.1] likewise makes h(M ) a direct summand of a finite sum of Tate
twists of motives h(S k ). Tensoring these split correspondences gives analogous splittings for every
self-power, again through Tate twists of powers of S. Thus these varieties are motivated by S in
the sense of Arapura. Theorem 1.2 supplies HC and GHC for every power of S, so Arapura’s
transfer lemma [2, Lemma 4.2], which retains geometric coniveau with its Tate shifts, gives both
conjectures for every power of each indicated variety. Finally, Rieß’s specialization correspondence
for birational smooth projective hyperkähler varieties [32, Theorem 3.2 and Section 3.3] gives
an isomorphism of Chow motives, as formulated in [8, Proposition 1.4]. This proves the last
assertion.                                                                                         □
