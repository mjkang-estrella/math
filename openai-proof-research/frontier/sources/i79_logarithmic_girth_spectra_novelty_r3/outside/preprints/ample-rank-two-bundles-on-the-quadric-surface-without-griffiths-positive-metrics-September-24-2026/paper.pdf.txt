                                               Abstract
        We give counterexamples to Griffiths’ conjecture in rank two on the quadric surface P1 × P1 .
     We construct an explicit bundle G whose coordinatewise power pullbacks, tensored with O(1, 1),
     are ample for every positive power, but admit no smooth strictly Griffiths-positive Hermitian
     metric for all sufficiently large powers.

1    Introduction
A holomorphic vector bundle E on a projective variety is ample when the tautological quotient line
bundle OP(E) (1) is ample; here P(E) parametrizes one-dimensional quotients [12, Proposition 3.2].
A smooth Hermitian metric is strictly Griffiths-positive if its Chern curvature is positive in every
pair of nonzero tangent and fiber directions. Such a metric implies ampleness. Griffiths’ conjecture
asks for the converse on smooth complex projective varieties [11, Problem (0.9)].

rank two on the quadric surface.

Historical context and related results
Hartshorne developed the algebraic theory of ample vector bundles through symmetric powers,
global generation, and the tautological line bundle [12]. Griffiths’ original question used the term
“cohomologically positive”; his definition of “ample” in that article included a first-jet generation
condition [11, Definitions (0.2)–(0.3), Problem (0.9)]. The conjecture above is the modern formulation
of that question. Its significance is the proposed passage from algebraic positivity to a Hermitian
curvature condition on the bundle itself, which can then enter differential-geometric vanishing
arguments.
    For line bundles the algebraic and curvature notions agree [26, Proposition 1.15]. On complex
curves, Hartshorne characterized ampleness by positivity of the degrees of all nonzero quotient
bundles [13, Theorem 2.4]. Umemura proved the metric equivalence first for elliptic curves and
then for compact Riemann surfaces [25, 26]; see in particular [26, Theorem 2.6] and the later work
of Campana–Flenner [3]. These results make a surface the first possible base dimension for the
phenomenon in Theorem 1.1.
    Several positive results recover strong curvature after modifying the bundle. Nakano positivity
is a stronger curvature condition than Griffiths positivity. Berndtsson proved Nakano positivity of
E ⊗ det E for ample E through direct-image curvature [1, Theorem 1.3]. Liu–Sun–Yang established
Nakano and dual-Nakano positivity for determinant-twisted symmetric powers and for sufficiently
large symmetric powers of ample bundles [16, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and Corollary 1.3]. The determinant
twists and symmetric powers are part of these conclusions; they do not supply a Griffiths-positive
metric on E itself.
    Analytic approaches to that remaining question include Demailly’s Hermitian–Yang–Mills system
and subsequent curvature functionals [5, 6]. Pingali identified a limitation of the initial system
and solved a related system for direct sums of ample line bundles on Riemann surfaces [21, 22].
Murakami subsequently obtained an analytic proof of the full curve case through the Demailly
system [19, Theorem 1.2]. These developments concern smooth Hermitian metrics and clarify the
analytic difficulty of recovering them from ampleness.
    The requirement that the metric be Hermitian is essential to the problem. Kobayashi charac-
terized ampleness of E by negatively curved strongly pseudoconvex complex Finsler structures on
E ∗ [14, Theorems 4.1 and 5.1]. His term “convex” means positivity of the complex fiber Hessian
of the squared metric [14, Section 2]. Their squared norms are homogeneous of degree two but
need not be Hermitian quadratics in the fiber variable. More recently, Lempert showed that an
equivariant fiberwise rule applied to a prescribed positive tautological metric can fail to produce
even a semipositive Hermitian metric [15, Theorem 2.1]. His examples are direct sums of a positive
line bundle and also carry positive Hermitian metrics. That result identifies an obstruction to the

    In a preprint posted on September 22, 2026, Du–Xie state the existence of a rank-two ample
bundle on the abelian surface C × C, where C is the elliptic curve y 2 = x3 − x, without a
smooth Griffiths-semipositive metric [7, Theorem 1.1]. Their accompanying preprint gives a first-jet
characterization of Griffiths positivity in terms of an adjoint-Levi obstruction [8, Theorem 1.1]. The
construction here is on the rational quadric and supplies a specified power-map family, an all-m
ampleness certificate, and the general limiting statement of Proposition 4.1. Its metric conclusion is
the absence of smooth strictly Griffiths-positive metrics for all sufficiently large m.
    A subsequent preprint of Liu–Wan, posted on September 29, 2026, states the existence of
ample rank-two bundles on every Hirzebruch surface, including P1 × P1 , that are slope-stable with
respect to suitable ample polarizations and admit no smooth strongly pseudoconvex Finsler metric
of semipositive Kobayashi curvature [17, Theorem 1.1]. That curvature condition is imposed on
the original bundle; Kobayashi’s ampleness characterization above uses negative curvature on its
dual. The result concerns a different construction and a stronger metric exclusion than the strict
Hermitian conclusion proved here.

Incompatible invariances. Restricting q to either line subbundle makes its squared seminorm

even when the seminorm has zeros. Invariance under AB forces this measure to be supported on its
two fixed points. Both A and B preserve this nonempty support. Since a permutation of at most
two points whose order divides three is trivial, each would fix every support point, giving a common
fixed point. Section 5 proves this contradiction, and Section 6 assembles the theorem.

5.3   The incompatible invariances
We now use the special dynamics of the two matrices. Concentration of invariant measures on
proper projective subsets is a classical mechanism; see [10, Lemma 2 and the proof of Lemma 3].
The following elementary argument gives precisely the rank-two statement needed here.

Lemma 5.3. The projective transformations induced by the matrices A and B in (2.1) preserve no
nonzero finite positive Borel measure on P1 .

Proof. Suppose that µ is such a measure. It is invariant under T = AB. The two eigenvalues of AB
have distinct moduli, so after a projective change of coordinate T acts as z 7→ λz with R := |λ| > 1.
The Borel sets
                           Cn = {z ∈ C∗ : Rn ≤ |z| < Rn+1 },       n ∈ Z,
are pairwise disjoint, cover C∗ , and satisfy T (Cn ) = Cn+1 . Each has the same measure. Finiteness
and positivity imply that this common measure is zero, and countable additivity gives µ(C∗ ) = 0.
The half-open annuli assign every boundary circle to exactly one set, so this argument applies
equally to atomic and diffuse measures and to nonreal λ.
    Thus the nonempty support of µ consists of one or two of the fixed points of T . Invariance under
A and B preserves this support as a set: the inverse image of an open neighborhood of the image
of a support point has positive measure. Both projective transformations have order three. Their
induced permutations on a set of at most two points are therefore trivial. Each point of the support
would be fixed by both A and B, and hence would be a common eigenline of the two matrices.
Lemma 2.1 excludes such a line.

    The preceding results rule out exactly the object supplied by Proposition 4.1.

Proposition 5.4. For the bundle G of Proposition 2.3, every finite nonnegative plurisubharmonic
function on the total space of F = G∨ whose restriction to each fiber is a Hermitian quadratic form
is identically zero.

Proof. If such a function were nonzero, Lemma 5.1 would give a nonzero squared seminorm on
OP1 (−b) invariant under the lifts of A and B. Lemma 5.2 would then produce a finite positive
measure on P1 of mass 2πb > 0, invariant under both projective transformations. This contradicts
Lemma 5.3.


6     Proof of the main theorem
Proof of Theorem 1.1. Take the algebraic rank-two bundle G of Proposition 2.3. By Proposition 3.1,
every Em = fm   ∗ G ⊗ P is ample in the quotient convention.

    If the set of positive integers m for which Em has a smooth strictly Griffiths-positive metric were
infinite, it would contain a sequence tending to infinity. Proposition 4.1 would then give a finite,
nonnegative, nonzero psh function on the total space of G∨ that is a Hermitian positive semidefinite
quadratic form on every fiber. Proposition 5.4 excludes precisely such a function. Consequently
that set of integers is finite. Choose m0 larger than all its elements, taking m0 = 1 if the set is
empty. Every m ≥ m0 gives the required counterexample.
    The limit in the proof is permitted to lose rank, but not to vanish identically or to lose its
quadratic structure at any base point. These are exactly the properties needed to transfer the graph
relations to a nonzero invariant measure. The argument requires no uniform estimates for the input
metrics or their curvature, and it does not determine an effective value of m0 .
