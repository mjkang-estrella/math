                                                Abstract
        We prove that every inclusion N ⊂ M of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals
     and a faithful normal conditional expectation contains an expected amenable subalgebra P ⊂ N
     such that P ′ ∩ c(M ) = N ′ ∩ c(M ), where c(M ) denotes the continuous core of M . This resolves
     the relative bicentralizer conjecture in this setting.


1    Introduction
An inclusion N ⊂ M is expected if it admits a faithful normal conditional expectation EN : M → N .
Such an expectation makes the inclusion compatible with modular theory: a faithful normal state
on N extends through EN to a state on M whose modular action preserves N . For a faithful
normal state ψ on M , the continuous core is c(M ) = M ⋊σψ R, with its canonical copy of M .
    We ask whether an amenable expected subalgebra of N can impose exactly the same commuta-
tion constraints as N on the whole core. Equality of commutants inside M alone does not answer
this question: the core also contains the unitaries implementing the modular action. Our main
theorem retains these additional constraints.

Theorem 1.1. Let N ⊂ M be a unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras with separable preduals,
and suppose that there is a faithful normal conditional expectation EN : M → N . There exist a
unital amenable von Neumann subalgebra P ⊂ N and a faithful normal conditional expectation
F : N → P such that
                                     P ′ ∩ c(M ) = N ′ ∩ c(M ).
Here amenable means injective.

   The canonical core isomorphism associated with a change of faithful normal state fixes M , so
the stated relative-commutant equality is independent of the state used to define the core.

History and the relative conjecture. Popa’s solution of Kadison’s problem for irreducible
expected inclusions with separable preduals and type II bottom factor provides a model for finding
amenable subalgebras that retain prescribed commutation properties [15]. For type III factors,
modular asymptotic centralization supplies a related approach. The bicentralizer of a faithful
normal state consists of those elements that commute asymptotically with every bounded sequence
asymptotically centralizing the state in predual norm. Connes introduced the problem of its
triviality in the classification of injective factors [3, 4]. Haagerup solved the amenable type III1
case, completing the uniqueness theorem for the injective type III1 factor [5]. For type III1 factors


                                                    1
with separable predual, he characterized triviality by norm-density of faithful normal states whose
centralizers have trivial relative commutant [5, Theorem 3.1]. His proof uses Popa’s construction
to find a maximal abelian subalgebra of such a centralizer that remains maximal abelian in the
ambient factor; the expectations onto the centralizer and onto its abelian subalgebra compose [5,
proof of Theorem 3.1].
    Masuda introduced a relative bicentralizer in the finite-index setting [13, Definition 4.1]. Ando,
Haagerup, Houdayer, and Marrakchi constructed the canonical relative bicentralizer flow for
expected inclusions with type III1 bottom factor [2, Theorem A]. This extends the absolute flow
discovered by Haagerup and independently by Marrakchi, as described in that paper. The structural
results of Houdayer and Isono [6, Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.5] and Marrakchi’s absolute
flow-ergodicity theorem [9, Theorem D] underlie the absolute reduction used here. Marrakchi
formulated the relative bicentralizer conjecture and proved that, for separable preduals, it is
equivalent to the conclusion of Theorem 1.1 [10, Conjecture C and Theorem D]. Our theorem
therefore resolves that conjecture positively, including inclusions with arbitrary centers and types.
    Marrakchi’s recent Theorem B [11] gives an expected amenable subalgebra P ⊂ N preserving
N ′ ∩ M for general expected inclusions with separable preduals. Theorem 1.1 preserves the
commutant in the continuous core. Applying the earlier result with upper algebra c(M ) would
require an expectation from c(M ) onto N , which the original inclusion does not supply. The
binormal-state and zero-shift completely positive map of [11, Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2(1)] are essential
inputs to the proof below.

The absolute problem and the recent announcement. Houdayer and Marrakchi have
announced that every type III1 factor admitting a faithful normal state has trivial bicentralizer [7,
Theorem G]; their theorem statement has no separability restriction. Section 3 gives an alternative
proof of its separable-predual instance as part of our relative argument. The multiplication maps
and spectral resonance of [7, Section 8] are closely related to this absolute comparison. Their paper
also develops relative bicentralizer theory for tracial group actions; the general expected-inclusion
core-commutant conclusion of Theorem 1.1 is obtained here through the relative tensor and core
argument.
    The companion [14, Theorem 4.1 and Section 5.1] develops a complementary absolute argument
through bounded recovery: for a state with scalar centralizer, a fixed multiplication defect whose
modularly averaged squared norm has positive limsup along unit vectors with spectral supports
shrinking to one point can be detected by uniformly bounded algebra elements. The binormal-state
identity used here also admits the iteration that yields the relative estimate uniformly in tensor
length and flow shifts.

    For an irreducible expected inclusion of factors, Kadison’s problem asks for a MASA in N

Proof structure. The analytic work concerns the case where N is a type III1 factor. Choose
a faithful normal state φ on N and extend it by EN . The relative bicentralizer B ⊂ M consists
of elements that commute asymptotically with every bounded sequence in N asymptotically
centralizing φ. It carries a state-preserving flow γ. Section 2 recalls these objects and the binormal-
state identity used below. Existing bicentralizer criteria reduce our task to showing that the
extension of γ to B ⋊σφ R has fixed algebra N ′ ∩ c(M ).
    The connection with multiplication is the central point. Set H = L2 (N, φ), K = L2 (B, φ), and
Ω = φ1/2 . Consider maps from H ⊗ K to L2 (M, φ), initially given for x ∈ N and a ∈ B by
                           R(xΩ ⊗ aΩ) = xaΩ,          L(xΩ ⊗ aΩ) = axΩ.
We prove that these maps are isometries and that
                                        R = L exp(iX ⊗ Y ),
where X generates the modular action on H and Y generates the bicentralizer flow on K. After a
change of coordinates in the core representation, N acts on H alone, whereas an element fixed by
the bicentralizer flow acts independently of the H coordinate. They therefore commute. This is
the required fixed-point criterion.
    The absolute theorem supplies the isometry assertion. If that theorem failed, the standard
reduction would give a nontrivial factor equal to its own bicentralizer and with scalar modular
fixed algebra. Its two multiplication maps become isometries after averaging the modular action.
A binormal eigenstate identity gives their spectral comparison; reflection then forces a spectral
lattice condition incompatible with modular ergodicity. Section 3 carries out this argument, and
Section 4 applies the absolute theorem to obtain the unaveraged relative isometries.
    To prove the relative comparison, Section 5 packages words of arbitrary tensor length into
states on one fixed operator algebra. The binormal-state identity yields an estimate uniform in
tensor length, all intervening flow parameters, and the location of the total modular energy. The
packaging maps are completely positive but may be nonnormal; a spectral cutoff argument justifies
the functional calculus they require. Section 6 removes the energy restriction by repeatedly testing
a hypothetical discrepancy. Averaging the comparison contraction L∗ R with its proposed unitary
value loses norm wherever they differ. Random shifts capture such a difference with probabilities
whose sum diverges, while the same probabilities bound the propagated error. The resulting
expected norm loss exceeds the available norm, so the comparison is exact.
    Finally, Section 7 proves the core fixed-point identity and derives the weak Dixmier property:
every weak* closed convex hull of an N -unitary orbit in c(M ) meets N ′ ∩ c(M ). Marrakchi’s
characterization then supplies the amenable subalgebra. Existing results handle the other factor
types; a central decomposition and averaging over the center give the general inclusion.
