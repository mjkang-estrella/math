                                                 Abstract
         We prove that every type II1 factor with separable predual is generated by one operator,
      equivalently by two self-adjoint operators. Combined with the established direct-integral
      reduction to this case, this gives an affirmative solution of the generator problem for von
      Neumann algebras with separable predual.


1    Introduction
For a subset S of a von Neumann algebra M, write W ∗ (S) for the smallest unital von Neumann
subalgebra containing S. Thus adjoints are included and generation is taken in the weak operator
topology. The generator problem asks whether every von Neumann algebra with separable
predual has the form W ∗ ( x ) for one bounded operator x. Equivalently, it asks for two self-adjoint
generators: their sum a + ib recovers a and b as its real and imaginary parts.
   We prove this assertion for type II1 factors by first establishing a relative generation theorem.
For a finite factor with normalized trace τ, put ∥ x ∥2 = τ ( x ∗ x )1/2 . An inclusion P ⊂ M is irreducible
when P′ ∩ M = C1. Write U ( M ) for the unitary group of M.

Theorem 1.1. Let P ⊂ M be an irreducible inclusion of type II1 factors, with M of separable predual. The
set
                                 {u ∈ U ( M) : W ∗ ( P, u) = M}
is a dense Gδ subset of the unitary group U ( M ) for the trace 2-norm topology.

Theorem 1.2. Every type II1 factor M with separable predual is generated by two self-adjoint elements.
Equivalently, there is an x ∈ M such that M = W ∗ ( x ).

   Throughout the paper, separable for a finite von Neumann algebra means that its predual is
separable. This ensures separability of L2 ( M, τ ); it does not require operator-norm separability of
M.

Earlier work. The type I, hyperfinite, and properly infinite cases of the generator problem were
established by Pearcy, Suzuki and Saitô, and Wogen, respectively [11, 20, 23]; see also the historical
discussion in [19, Section 3]. Willig’s direct-integral theorem reduces the general question to
type II1 factors [22, Corollary 2]. Consequently, Theorem 1.2 also gives single generation of
every von Neumann algebra with separable predual. This last consequence uses the established
direct-integral reduction, whereas our proof below concerns finite factors.


                                                     1
    Much of the progress in the finite case has used additional structure. Behncke established
single generation for tensor products of two II1 factors [1]. Popa treated factors with Cartan
subalgebras [13]; Ge and Popa treated factors with property Γ and recovered the tensor-product
case [6]; and Ge and Shen obtained arithmetic group examples with a small-support refinement [7].
Shen’s generator invariant unified these and other positive classes [18]. Dykema, Sinclair, Smith,
and White established its exact amplification formula and sharpened its connection with the
number of self-adjoint generators [4].
    Sherman showed, in particular, that finite generation of every countably generated II1 factor
would suffice for a positive answer [19, Theorem 3.8(3)]. More recently, Gao, Kunnawalkam
Elayavalli, Patchell, and Tan proved single generation when a countable unitary generating
family lies in one sequential-commutation orbit in an ultrapower: successive members can be
joined there by finite chains with Haar intermediate unitaries and commuting adjacent terms [5,
Theorem A]. Popa’s work on tight decompositions studies stable single generation, which requires
every amplification of a factor to be singly generated [17].
    Requiring generators to vary continuously with a family of factors leads to a different problem.
Hayes constructed a W ∗ -bundle—a continuous family of tracial von Neumann algebras—over a
compact metrizable base, with separable-predual II1 fibers, for which no finite family of continu-
ous sections generates every fiber [9, Theorem 1.1].
    Relative generation by one unitary is already available for finite-index inclusions, by the
downward basic construction used in [4, Lemma 6.2]: the associated Jones projection e generates
the extension over the subfactor, and 1 − 2e is unitary. Theorem 1.1 applies to every irreducible
inclusion and also establishes genericity of the relative generators.
    The universal conclusion of Theorem 1.2 also makes the generator invariants G and Gsa vanish
for every separable-predual II1 factor. It implies that Voiculescu’s microstates free entropy dimen-
sions δ and δ0 can change under replacement of a finite self-adjoint von Neumann generating
tuple. Section 6 gives these deductions and their precise scope.

The main estimate and the proof. Our argument combines Popa’s free-independence construc-
tion in tracial ultraproducts [14, 15] with a perturbation whose effect accumulates along a long
word. Given a unitary u and a target unitary z, we coordinate a perturbation of u across its
repeated occurrences in a word with     √ coefficients from P. With a small parameter α fixed, the
perturbation has 2-norm of order α/ ℓ, where ℓ is the number of occurrences. The resulting word
has correlation with a test vector ξ equal to (α/2)τ (ξ ∗ z) up to an error of order α2 ∥ξ ∥2 + 1/ℓ.
    The essential estimate is uniform in ℓ. An operator-norm bound on each perturbation sep-
arately would grow with the length of the product. We instead dominate all coefficients of
the nonlinear terms by a free-group convolution operator with nonnegative coefficients. The
length-one case of Haagerup’s inequality [8, Lemma 1.4] bounds its norm independently of ℓ. This
controls the full exponential remainder, including cancellations between words. Proposition 3.2
states the precise bounds. The witness word need only retain a fixed positive correlation as ℓ → ∞.
The free variables and perturbation are first constructed in a tracial ultrapower. For each fixed
word length, choosing one coordinate of their representing sequences gives the required unitaries
in M and P, with an arbitrarily small additional error.
    For a fixed vector ξ ∈ L2 ( M, τ ), the norm of its projection onto L2 (W ∗ ( P, u), τ ) is a bounded
lower semicontinuous function of u. Baire’s theorem supplies common continuity points for a
countable dense family of vectors, and contractivity extends continuity to every vector. If W ∗ ( P, u)
were proper at such a point, a nonzero bounded vector orthogonal to it would have projection
norm zero. The perturbation estimate gives nearby unitaries for which that projection norm has a

fixed positive lower bound, a contradiction.
    Finally, Popa’s irreducible hyperfinite embedding theorem [12, 16] lets us choose P generated
by two self-adjoint elements. Relative generation then gives four self-adjoint generators for every
separable-predual II1 factor. Applying this uniform bound to a half-trace corner, and using an
explicit two-by-two matrix encoding, gives Theorem 1.2. This encoding belongs to the established
matrix-generation techniques of Douglas–Pearcy and Wogen [3, 23]; we use the von Neumann
algebra form of the matrix encoding in [4, Proposition 1.1].
    Section 2 states the exact independence input and constructs the free Haar unitaries required
in the ultrapower. Section 3 proves the uniform perturbation estimate. Section 4 carries out
the continuity argument and identifies the full relative-generator locus as Gδ . Section 5 gives
the corner and matrix construction proving single generation. Section 6 treats the general von
Neumann algebra, generator-invariant, and free-entropy consequences.

6     Consequences for generation and free entropy
The universal statement of Theorem 1.2 has consequences beyond the relative generation theorem.
We first apply classical reductions, then compare two generating tuples of a free group factor.

6.1   General von Neumann algebras and generator invariants
Willig’s direct-integral reduction [22, Corollary 2], together with the established type I and
properly infinite cases, reduces the generator problem for von Neumann algebras with separable
predual to the II1 factor case. Theorem 1.2 therefore gives single generation for every von
Neumann algebra with separable predual.
    Let G and Gsa be the generator invariants defined in [4, Definition 2.7]. They are nonnegative
infima over finite generating sets and finite projection partitions; Gsa restricts the generating sets
to self-adjoint elements. For every complex II1 factor M with separable predual and normalized
trace, we have
                                       G ( M ) = Gsa ( M ) = 0.
Indeed, by [4, Corollary 5.1], a positive G-value for one such factor would force a nonsingly
generated factor in the same class, contrary to the universal assertion of Theorem 1.2. The cited
generation convention need not include the unit. To pass to that convention, replace a generator x
by y = x + λ1 with λ > ∥ x ∥. The invertible y has support projection 1 in its possibly nonunital
von Neumann closure; that closure then contains x and equals M. Nonnegativity gives G ( M ) = 0,
and [4, Theorem 5.5] gives Gsa ( M ) = 2G ( M ) = 0. These values are the defining infima; no
generating set or projection partition attaining zero is asserted.

6.2   Dependence of free entropy dimensions on generators
For a bounded finite self-adjoint tuple Z in a tracial von Neumann algebra, let δ( Z ) and δ0 ( Z )
denote Voiculescu’s microstates free entropy dimension and its modified version, respectively [21,
Section 2.4]. The W ∗ -invariance question asks whether either number is the same for any two
finite self-adjoint tuples generating the same tracial von Neumann algebra; see [21, Section 2.6].




                                                 15
Corollary 6.1. For every integer n ≥ 3, the factor L(Fn ) with its canonical trace has two finite self-
adjoint generating tuples on which both δ and δ0 take different values. In particular, neither dimension is
determined by the generated tracial von Neumann algebra.

Proof. For a bounded self-adjoint d-tuple Z, one has δ( Z ) ≤ d and δ0 ( Z ) ≤ d. Both dimensions
equal n for a freely independent variance-one semicircular n-tuple; see [21, Section 2.4(a), (c), and
(d)] for δ and [2, Section 5] for δ0 . The standard semicircular n-tuple generates L(Fn ) as a von
Neumann algebra. A self-adjoint pair supplied by Theorem 1.2 generates the same factor and has
each dimension at most 2 < n. These two tuples give the assertion.

    A separate isomorphism argument in An isomorphism of the free group factors [10, Corollary 7.1]
gives a stronger fixed-factor conclusion: each of δ, δ0 and the nonmicrostates dimensions δ∗ , δ⋆
attains every integer value n ≥ 2 on finite self-adjoint W ∗ -generating tuples of L(F2 ). That
conclusion uses the trace-preserving isomorphisms between free group factors established in the
cited paper. Corollary 6.1 uses only the single-generation theorem proved here and the standard
dimension bounds above.
