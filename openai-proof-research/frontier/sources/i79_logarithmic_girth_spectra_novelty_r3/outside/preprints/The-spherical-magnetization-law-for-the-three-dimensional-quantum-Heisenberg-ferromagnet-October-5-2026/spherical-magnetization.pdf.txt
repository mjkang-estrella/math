                                              Abstract
         We prove the spherical magnetization law for the three-dimensional nearest-neighbor
     isotropic quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet at every fixed positive quantum spin and every
     sufficiently low fixed positive temperature. As the even periodic cubes grow, the symmetric
     zero-field magnetization converges in moments to a uniform direction with a deterministic
     positive magnitude. This magnitude equals the right derivative at zero field of the infinite-
     volume pressure. The law is expressed through self-adjoint linear combinations of the spin
     components and requires no joint measurement of noncommuting observables.

1    Introduction
An isotropic ferromagnet has no preferred direction in zero field. In a finite periodic volume, its
Gibbs state is therefore rotation invariant even at temperatures where magnetized equilibrium
states exist. The natural description of the ordered phase is a magnetization of fixed length and
uniformly distributed direction. Rotation invariance alone does not establish this description:
it allows a mixture of different lengths. The question addressed here is whether the symmetric
finite-volume state selects a single length, and whether that length is the magnetization selected
by an infinitesimal field.

1.2    Context and antecedents
The dimensional distinction in this problem is fundamental. Mermin and Wagner [12] excluded
spontaneous ferro- or antiferromagnetic order at positive temperature for finite-range isotropic
Heisenberg models in dimensions one and two. In higher dimensions, infrared bounds proved
continuous-symmetry breaking for classical models [7] and ordering for quantum antiferromag-
nets [6]. These quantum antiferromagnetic results do not supply the isotropic ferromagnetic
ordering input used here. Another line of work establishes the low-temperature free-energy asymp-
totic predicted by spin-wave theory [5]. That asymptotic concerns a different limit from the field
derivative or the zero-field magnetization distribution at a fixed positive temperature.

                                                                   2
    The spherical distribution itself has clear antecedents. Ueltschi’s description of three-
dimensional loop models [23, Section 8.1] derives the transform sinh(hm)/(hm) for the spin- 12
ferromagnet conditional on a conjectured Poisson–Dirichlet law for macroscopic loops. Björnberg,
Fröhlich, and Ueltschi [2, Theorem 2.1] prove the spherical transform for the complete-graph
quantum Heisenberg model at arbitrary fixed spin. Our setting has nearest-neighbor interactions
on the three-dimensional lattice. We prove its magnetization law without assuming a distribution
for macroscopic loop lengths.
    The existence of spontaneous magnetization and the identification of its zero-field distribution
are distinct questions. The companion manuscript [15] constructs magnetized equilibrium states
for the isotropic nearest-neighbor quantum Heisenberg ferromagnet in dimensions at least three,
for every fixed spin at sufficiently low positive temperatures. The companion [14] establishes the
low-temperature asymptotic of the pressure-defined magnetization for nonnegative symmetric
finite-range interactions whose support generates Z3 . We use finite exchange and geometric
estimates from these works. The present argument adds spatial control on periodic tori and a
pressure argument excluding translation-ergodic equilibrium states with different magnetization
magnitudes.
    The exchange representation builds on permutation and random-walk representations of
quantum spins [17, 4], Tóth’s random-stirring representation [21], and its higher-spin formulation
through symmetric slots [13]. The finite pin estimates used below draw on the stability and
negative dependence theory of Borcea, Brändén, and Liggett [3]. Our zero-free matrix products use
the contraction principle associated with Asano’s and Ruelle’s developments of Lee–Yang theory
[1, 18, 19]; the contraction needed here is proved explicitly. Mean entropy, equilibrium states, and
pressure tangents are treated within the variational framework of Lanford and Robinson [10, 11].
We recall the required facts and prove the additional replica and harmonic-selection statements.

second fact identifies the local magnetization length to which the first fact applies.

affine function xa − a2 to a limiting pressure tangent. Large positive x selects the branch xm − m2 ;
analytic continuation of that branch cannot dominate a competing branch of smaller length at
every positive x. The spatial estimate, the replica argument, and the passage from random zero-free
functions to harmonic pressure tangents are the main additional constructions of this paper.

1.4   Conventions
We suppress S in expectations and usually suppress β, L when no confusion is possible. Write
X, Y, Z for the site spin components 1, 2, 3, in the standard representation where X, Z are real
and Y is imaginary. Set H0 = −βHS,L . Constants c, C may depend on the fixed spin and on
fixed geometric choices; Cβ may also depend on the fixed inverse temperature. We first choose a
sufficiently large finite β, and only then choose lower thresholds for L and spatial scales. All torus
volume limits are through even L.
    The finite exchange inputs are developed in Section 2, and the deterministic lattice estimates
imported from the companions are stated with their hypotheses where they are used. Positivity of
m follows directly from our torus sparse-pin estimate in Corollary 3.6; no sharp low-temperature
asymptotic is needed. We increase β0 (S) as needed below.

3     Sparse pins on the torus
We now show that a sparse collection of prescribed slot points is unlikely to lie on cycles avoiding
the field pins. This will supply the geometric control of available slots used in Section 4. The
induction follows the pinned-boundary argument of [14, Section 4], itself developed from [15,
Sections 5 and 7]. On the torus there is no pinned boundary: a small uniform field supplies the
killing, and cutting off flows to infinity leaves an error that this killing must absorb.
