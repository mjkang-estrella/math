                                                 Abstract
        We prove sharp contraction of the information carried by a binary channel under independent
     symmetric noise on a uniform discrete cube. At fixed initial information, a noisy coordinate retains
     the most information. The Boolean specialization resolves the Courtade–Kumar conjecture
     and gives an output-entropy refinement. We also establish a stronger mean-dependent entropy-
     production bound. The proof combines an explicit three-point optimizer for the local joining
     problem, two entropy capacities, and a common-output thinning inequality, followed by dimension
     induction and integration along the noise semigroup.


1    The contraction theorem and its mechanism
A one-bit summary of independent fair bits can be chosen before those bits pass through independent
binary symmetric channels. Which summary retains the most information about the noisy obser-
vation? Kumar and Courtade formulated the conjecture that a single input coordinate is optimal
at every noise level and in every dimension [13, 7]. We prove a sharp comparison for arbitrary
randomized binary summaries, together with a mean-dependent bound on their instantaneous
information loss.

1.1   Prior work and related results
Ordentlich, Shayevitz, and Weinstein [17] obtained asymptotically sharp high-noise bounds for
balanced Boolean functions. Samorodnitsky [19] proved the conjecture for all Boolean functions in
a dimension-independent high-noise range. Yu’s work on Φ-stability and local optimality [21, 22]
enlarged the proved range for balanced functions; the latter gives 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 0.914 by a computer-
assisted argument. Javanmard and Woodruff [10] extended Courtade and Kumar’s coordinatewise
sum bound [7, Theorem 1] from balanced functions to arbitrary output bias and refined high-noise
entropy estimates.
    Several nearby formulations clarify the role of the full noisy vector. Pichler, Piantanida, and
Matz [18] proved coordinate optimality when the observation is also reduced to one Boolean output.
At fixed output mean, Li and Médard [15] identified lexicographic optimizers at sufficiently low
noise and related high-noise optimizers to first-level Fourier weight, in dimension-dependent ranges.
Barnes and Özgür [1] proved the equivalence of the balanced conjecture and a symmetrized Li–
Médard moment inequality. Kindler, O’Donnell, and Witmer [11] proved fixed-mean Gaussian and
spherical analogues. These fixed-mean questions are distinct from attainability of the refined upper
curve in Corollary 1.2.
    Chen, Gohari, and Nair [6] developed the differential approach that we use: prove a local
entropy-production inequality, lift it by induction on the cube dimension, and integrate along the
noise semigroup. Their approach builds on the auxiliary receiver method of Gohari and Nair [8].
The August 2026 revision of Chen–Gohari–Nair disproves one proposed auxiliary inequality [6,
Appendix B]; the local comparisons below are proved directly. Their symmetric optimizer is a
predecessor of our arbitrary-mean joining problem, as explained in Section 3.
    Recent preprints announce proofs of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture by several routes. Chen,
Gohari, Javanmard, Lin, Mirrokni, Nair, and Woodruff [5] use a computer-assisted Bellman inequal-
ity; Ky and Tran [14] develop entropy-production and spectral estimates with computer-assisted
verification; Mahdavifar and Beirami [16] use entropy flow, Fourier analysis, and bounds for pivotal
sets; and Kramer and Saglam [12] prove a finite-noise bound that retains the Boolean output bias.

3    A joining cost with one entropy budget
The energy decomposition (1.5) leaves the cost of joining the two faces of a coordinate split. We
minimize this cost at fixed child means and average conditional entropy. Allowing arbitrary coupled
pair laws is a valid relaxation because only a lower bound on the actual joining cost is needed; no
independence of the faces is assumed. The symmetric fixed-entropy problem of Chen–Gohari–Nair
[6, Theorem 4 and Section IV-C] has an optimizer supported on a reflected interior pair and the
two equal-output corners. Chen et al. [5, Section 3.1, Theorem 3.10] characterize related three-point
optimizers when both means and both entropy moments are prescribed, under explicit contact and
mass conditions. Here we prescribe the two means but constrain only the average entropy. Pricing
this common entropy budget gives the optimizer throughout the domain, including the transition
from two corners to one.

7     From the linear reserve to the sharp rate
The local reserve inequality does not by itself give the sharp rate F (I) at every state. We now join
their maximum
                                   Bbm (I) = max{Bm (I), F (I)}.
