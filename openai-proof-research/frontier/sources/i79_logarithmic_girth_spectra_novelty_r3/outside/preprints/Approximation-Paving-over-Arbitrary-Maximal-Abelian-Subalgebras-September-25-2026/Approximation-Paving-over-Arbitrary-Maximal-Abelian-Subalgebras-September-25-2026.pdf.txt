Approximation Paving over Arbitrary Maximal Abelian Subalgebras
                                                 OpenAI

                                          September 25, 2026


                                                Abstract
         We prove the approximation-paving conjecture of Popa and Vaes for every maximal abelian
     subalgebra of a complex von Neumann algebra. For each 0 < ε < 1, every self-adjoint operator
     is a strong limit of self-adjoint operators of norm at most three times its norm, each admitting a
     norm paving with error at most ε times the approximant’s own norm. The number of projections
     is at most Cε−6 for a universal constant C. No separability or conditional-expectation hypothesis
     is required.


1    Introduction
Let A be a maximal abelian unital ∗-subalgebra, or MASA, of a complex von Neumann algebra M .
For a finite partition P = (pi )ri=1 of the unit by projections in A, write

Norm paving asks whether CP (z) can be made close in operator norm to an element of A. Ap-
proximation paving allows a bounded perturbation of z within any prescribed strong-operator
neighborhood before choosing the partition.
    Kadison and Singer posed the pure-state extension problem for the atomic diagonal MASA of B(ℓ2 )
[KS59]. Anderson formulated its diagonal paving counterpart [Anderson79], and Marcus, Spielman
and Srivastava resolved it by mixed characteristic polynomials [MSS15]. For general MASAs,
ordinary norm paving of the original operator is too restrictive: in the separable-predual setting, it
characterizes type I algebras with a normal expectation onto the MASA [PV15, Theorem 3.3]. Popa
and Vaes introduced strong-operator and approximation versions of paving and conjectured that
every MASA has both properties [PV15, Definitions 2.2–2.3 and Conjecture 2.8(1)]. We resolve the
approximation-paving assertion of that conjecture positively.
    Popa proved norm paving in ultraproducts of singular finite MASAs, and hence strong-operator
paving in the original finite inclusions [Popa14]. Popa and Vaes established strong-operator and
approximation paving for type I algebras with separable predual and for Cartan inclusions in
amenable algebras or arising from free ergodic profinite actions [PV15, Sections 3–4]. For singular
finite MASAs they obtained quadratic paving bounds [PV15, Theorem 5.1], later sharpened in
[PV16]. These restricted cases do not provide the expectation-free approximation theorem below.

The measured argument. The first part of the proof develops a measurable version of one-sided
matrix paving for nonsingular equivalence relations. The finite paving-polynomial framework of
[RL20, RS21] supplies the starting point. Its stable expected determinants yield local spectral
probabilities and spectral-shift functions. A buffered pinning lemma selects a color while increasing
the diagonal penalty by a fixed universal factor and controlling the increase of positive-root mass.
The initial root estimate from [RS21, Lemma 24 and proof of Theorem 25] is derived here from
[MSS15, Theorems 4.1 and 5.1], using positive-defect completion and deletion of added Gram
columns.
    Nonsingularity is handled by passing to the Maharam extension, where mass transport is
invariant, and weighting the matrix by the height variable. Uniform comparisons between slabs allow
the pinning decisions to descend to a single coloring of the base. Sparse random updates then control

                                                   2
the accumulated cost. Only local independence is needed; auxiliary graph colorings do not increase
the number of colors in the final paving. The slab comparison and buffered-pinning estimates isolate
the two uniformity issues in this passage from finite matrices to measurable operators.

The operator-algebra argument. For a MASA in the centralizer of a faithful normal state, we
first pave the algebra generated by its normalizers, using the Feldman–Moore representation [FM77]
and the measured theorem. The complementary part has diffuse left–right kernels. We adapt the
free-product matrix dilation of [PV15, proof of Theorem 5.1], using random diagonal phases to
obtain asymptotic freeness with respect to the induced state. The proof controls the moments and
their concentration without assuming traciality, using analytic right multiplication for the modular
group.
     Finally, a separable reduction preserves the partition bounds. The corner carrying centralizing
normal positive functionals has a normal expectation; on the complementary corner, almost disjoint
conjugate states produce positive contractions with small diagonal compressions. A three-corner
construction preserves the original off-diagonal entries until the final partition is chosen. This is the
step that removes the normal-expectation hypothesis.
     All new estimates needed for these passages are proved below. We use established structure
theorems for conditional expectations, standard form, type I algebras and free products, with their
hypotheses specified at the points of application.
     Section 2 fixes the state-topology tools; Sections 3–5 prove the measured paving theorem,
Section 6 handles the state-preserving case, and Section 7 removes the remaining hypotheses.

3     Finite mixed polynomials and buffered pinning
We develop a rule for fixing one vertex’s color while controlling the total positive-root mass of an
expected characteristic polynomial. The rule will be applied repeatedly in the measured paving
argument; its constants must therefore be independent of the matrix size and of previous color
choices. We first identify the rooted mass and the change caused by one pin, then prove the
inequality that relates them.

6.1   The normalizer algebra
Let N be the von Neumann algebra generated by the groupoid normalizers of A in M . These are
the partial isometries v with v ∗ v, vv ∗ ∈ A such that conjugation by v carries Av ∗ v onto Avv ∗ . The
algebra N contains A. Since the modular group of φ fixes A pointwise, it carries every groupoid
normalizer to another with the same supports and the same induced corner isomorphism. Hence N is
globally modular invariant. Takesaki’s theorem [Takesaki72] gives a normal φ-preserving conditional
expectation EN : M → N ; uniqueness of the state-preserving expectation onto A gives

                                            EA EN = EA .                                           (6.2)

   The inclusion A ⊂ N is Cartan. For completeness, groupoid regularity here implies regularity by
unitary normalizers. Represent A on a standard probability space. A normal isomorphism between

two corners of A is represented by a nonsingular measurable partial bijection. On its fixed-point

                                       v + v ∗ + 1 − v ∗ v − vv ∗

expectation, as required for the Cartan theorem.

measure given by φ|A , and identifies N with the algebra of a countable nonsingular equivalence
relation, allowing a scalar unitary cocycle. We use the representation on square-integrable columns,
with the right base point integrated against µ and counting measure in its orbit. An algebra
element acts by left matrix multiplication on the orbit column. In the twisted case, changing
the base point changes the matrix by diagonal unitary conjugacy, by the scalar cocycle identity.

normal conditional expectation, bimodularity gives F (CQ (z)) = F (z). The bounded compression

span, including products, is strongly dense. A finite such expression has off-diagonal support in a

degree. Operator-norm bounds in the column representation give the same matrix bounds for almost
every orbit. All models and cocycle identities below may be restricted to a common conull invariant
set for the countably many operators involved.

This operator is self-adjoint. The difference y − x is block diagonal: its f -block has norm at most 2,
its g-block has norm at most 1, and its h-block has norm at most 2. Hence
                                         ∥y − x∥ ≤ 2,             ∥y∥ ≤ 3.                             (7.10)
The g-block of the difference annihilates F ′ . Choosing the other two approximation errors sufficiently
small therefore ensures
                               ∥(y − x)ξ∥ < min{δ, 1/4}         (ξ ∈ F ′ ).                       (7.11)
In particular ∥y∥ > 1/2, by applying this inequality to ξ0 .
    Use the projections of Pf , the projections of Ph , and the single projection g as distinct members
of one A-partition P, padding with zeros in absent corners. It has the size in Equation (7.8) and
kills every off-diagonal block between f, g, h. With d = df on f and zero on g + h, we obtain
                                  d = d∗ ∈ A,            ∥CP (y) − d∥ ≤ γ.
Since compression is norm-contractive, ∥d∥ ≤ ∥y∥ + γ. Clip d by functional calculus to the interval
[− ∥y∥ , ∥y∥], obtaining a ∈ A with ∥a∥ ≤ ∥y∥ and ∥a − d∥ ≤ γ. It follows that
                                  ∥CP (y) − a∥ ≤ 2γ = ε/2 ≤ ε ∥y∥ .
Equations (7.10) and (7.11) give the remaining requirements. The partition size depends only on ε,
since the bound in Theorem 6.1 is uniform over the state-preserving inclusions used here. Finally,
scaling y and a by the original ∥x∥, and first dividing the requested strong tolerance by that norm,
proves the theorem with the universal constant κ = 3.

7.4   Consequences for related paving formulations
The main theorem concerns norm paving of bounded approximants. The two introductory conse-
quences use the normal-expectation hypothesis to pass between that formulation and strong-operator
paving of the original operator. The quadratic consequence additionally uses the separate companion
theorem.

Proof of Corollary 1.2. By Theorem 1.1, every such x is (ε/12, R(ε/12); 3)-approximately pavable
in the terminology of [PV15, Definition 2.3]. Apply [PV15, Proposition 2.4(1)] with ε′ = ε/6. Its
error is 2 · 3 · (ε/6) = ε, so Rs (ε) = R(ε/12) suffices.

Proof of Corollary 1.3. By [OpenAI26, Theorem 1.1], every self-adjoint x ∈ M is (ε/2, rε/2 ) so-
pavable, where rε/2 ≤ Cε−2 for a universal C. Since ε/2 < ε, [PV15, Proposition 2.4(2)] makes
x (ε, rε/2 ; 3)-approximately pavable. Its proof supplies self-adjoint approximants and diagonal
witnesses, with paving error relative to the approximant’s norm. The strong-limit net in [PV15,
Definition 2.3] supplies the finite-vector conclusion with the fixed count N (ε) = rε/2 .
