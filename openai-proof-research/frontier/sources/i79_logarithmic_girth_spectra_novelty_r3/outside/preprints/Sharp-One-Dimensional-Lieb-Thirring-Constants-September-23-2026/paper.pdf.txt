                                               Abstract
          We resolve affirmatively the remaining cases of the scalar one-dimensional Lieb–Thirring
      conjecture: for every 12 < γ < 32 , the optimal constant is the one-bound-state constant. The
      estimate holds for every nonnegative potential in Lγ+1/2 (R), with all negative eigenvalues
      included.

1.1   History and relation to prior work
Lieb and Thirring introduced these spectral inequalities in their work on the kinetic energy of
fermions and the stability of matter [15, 16]. At exponent one, optimizing over the potential
makes the spectral inequality equivalent to a lower bound on the kinetic energy of an orthonormal
family in terms of its density. On the line, for a finite orthonormal family  (u ) ⊂ H 1 (R), this

arbitrarily many bound states can improve on its constant in the lower exponent range. We verify
the one-state equality potential and its normalization directly.
    The scalar upper endpoint predates the general Lieb–Thirring inequalities. Inverse-scattering
trace identities for the Korteweg–de Vries equation give the sharp value L3/2,1 = 3/16 [22, 8]; Lieb
and Thirring explicitly credit Gardner, Greene, Kruskal and Miura in [16, Section 4(B)]. Aizenman
and Lieb’s monotonicity argument extends the semiclassical equality to all larger exponents [1]. At
the other endpoint, Weidl first proved finiteness of L1/2,1 [21], and Hundertmark, Lieb and Thomas
obtained its sharp value 1/2 [11, Theorem 1]. Laptev and Weidl established the operator-valued
upper-endpoint estimate and used dimension lifting to obtain the sharp semiclassical constants
for every dimension and γ ≥ 3/2 [13, Theorems 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1].
    Quantitative estimates and orthonormal-function methods narrowed the remaining gap.
Hundertmark, Laptev and Weidl proved Lγ,1 ≤ 2Lcl        γ,1 for 1/2 ≤ γ < 3/2 [10, Theorem 4.1]. At
exponent one, Dolbeault, Laptev and Loss extended the method of Eden         √ and Foias to matrix-

were 1.456 by Frank, Hundertmark, Jex and Nam [7, Theorem 1] and the reported 1.44655 of Corso
and Ried [3, Corollary 1.7]. These bounds preceded the sharp exponent-one theorem described
below. In the full one-dimensional strict interval, Levitt’s numerical investigation observed
convergence to the one-state profile or splitting into increasingly separated copies [14, Section 4.1].
Such computations support the conjectured value but do not bound the contribution of every
orthonormal family. That uniform bound is the obstacle addressed by our action inequality.

4/(3 3π). Their proof extends Benguria and Loss’s scalar cumulative-mass argument [2, Section 2]
to orthonormal families through a matrix correction      √ to the kinetic energy. Their operator-
valued extension also gives the semiclassical ratio 2/ 3 in every dimension at exponent one [19,
Theorem 2 and Section 2]. Our proof retains the individual eigenvalue scales in a finite-interval
action inequality. Its main analytic step is a rank-one comparison for an energy-dependent
regularized matrix field. A continuation argument chooses a lower triangular coefficient matrix
and a path joining two prescribed matrix endpoints. Together these yield the action inequality
for every 1/2 < γ < 3/2. Theorem 1.1 therefore resolves the remaining open-interval cases of
the one-dimensional Lieb–Thirring conjecture positively; the case γ = 1 is already covered by
Read–Schulz.
