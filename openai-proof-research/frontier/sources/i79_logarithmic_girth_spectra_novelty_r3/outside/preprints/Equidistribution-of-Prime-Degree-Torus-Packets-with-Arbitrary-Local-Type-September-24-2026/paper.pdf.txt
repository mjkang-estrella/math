                                                 Abstract
          We prove the packet form of the higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem for totally
      real fields of any fixed prime degree at least five, allowing arbitrary local homothety types of
      full lattices. As the multiplier-order discriminant tends to infinity, the volume-weighted packet
      measures converge to Haar probability measure, with no escape of mass.


1    Introduction
Compact diagonal orbits in the space of unimodular lattices encode ideal classes and units of
totally real number fields. The higher-dimensional Duke equidistribution problem asks whether
arithmetically complete families of these orbits become uniformly distributed as their discriminants
grow. The grouping matters: one local homothety type specifies a complete packet, whereas a
multiplier order alone can combine several packets. We prove the prime-degree packet statement for
every such local type.

The convergence is weak convergence of probability measures. In particular, it holds against every

    Theorem 1.1 permits nonmaximal orders of unbounded index and arbitrary local module types.
The field discriminants may remain bounded or grow independently of those indices. The statement
is separate for each local homothety type; it makes no assertion about an individual orbit, a
convergence rate, or composite degrees.

The maximal-order specialization
Take M = OK . Every lattice in its local type is a fractional OK -ideal, and its homothety class is an
ordinary ideal class. Thus the packet is obtained by embedding one representative of each ideal
class, normalizing its covolume to one, and taking the distinct sign translates of its positive diagonal
orbit. For every sequence of totally real fields of degree n with | Disc(K)| → ∞, the volume-weighted
measures of these packets converge to mn by Theorem 1.1. This construction also explains why an
individual ideal class is not the object being averaged.
    For general M , the same maximal-order ideal classes remain the global parameters, but each
carries a finite set of local module choices. Proposition A.1 in Appendix A identifies this construction
with the pushforward of adelic torus Haar probability, independently of the rational basis and finite-
lattice presentation. The theorem therefore includes all these presentations of the maximal-order
specialization, as well as arbitrary noninvertible modules over nonmaximal orders.

Context and prior work
Linnik’s ergodic approach connected arithmetic distribution problems with dynamics [8]. Duke proved
equidistribution of the length-weighted closed geodesics associated with fundamental real-quadratic
discriminants on the modular surface [1, Theorem 1]. In higher rank, Einsiedler, Lindenstrauss,
Michel, and Venkatesh developed the relationship between arithmetic separation and entropy for
periodic torus orbits [3]. Their cubic equidistribution theorem combines subconvexity, local analysis,
and measure classification [4, Theorem 1.4]. Their packet construction already distinguishes local
homothety from the coarser condition of having the same multiplier order [4, Section 5.5].
    The higher-prime-degree extension described in [4, Section 1.6.3] is conditional on subconvexity.
Khayutin subsequently obtained entropy bounds using arithmetic separation and Galois invariants,
under maximal-order and two-transitivity hypotheses [6, Theorem 1.1]. Lemke Oliver, Thorner,
and Zaman proved equidistribution for sequences of orders in totally real fields of each fixed prime
degree at least five, outside a small exceptional family of fields [7, Theorem 2.3]. Their result allows
nonmaximal orders; the exceptional family has at most On,ε (X ε ) fields of discriminant at most X.
Theorem 1.1 treats every field and every full-lattice local type in the stated prime degrees. It gives
an unconditional affirmative answer to this packet form of the higher-dimensional Duke problem,
including sequences in a fixed field with increasing order index.

                                                   2
The arithmetic ingredient
The proof follows the counting-to-rigidity strategy of Einsiedler–Lindenstrauss–Michel–Venkatesh
[4, Section 2.7.1]: packet averages of lattice-vector counts control the cusp and small ordinary balls,
and these bounds feed into positive-entropy rigidity. Their cubic argument obtains asymptotics for
these averages using global and local subconvexity [4, Sections 2.7.2–2.7.3]. For the same rigidity
step, we obtain the needed upper bounds by retaining the Dedekind-zeta residue in the unfolded
ideal count. Stark’s exceptional-zero descent [14], together with Shiu’s theorem in Pollack’s uniform
formulation [13, 11], supplies the required bounds without a subconvexity hypothesis. Odd degree
excludes the quadratic subfields that occur in Stark’s exceptional-zero alternative.
    For an arbitrary prescribed local type, we keep maximal-order ideal counting but insert local
containment probabilities: at each prime, the weight records the probability that a vector belongs
to a randomly chosen unit translate of the prescribed module. The essential estimate is a small
positive moment of the local norm, uniform over full local modules whose maximal-order span is the
full ring. Its proof uses a unit in each component projection, not independence of the components
or invertibility of the module. The unit-mass argument in [4, Lemma 9.7] is an antecedent; here we
prove the exact shell normalization and the positive moment for arbitrary local modules with this
full-span condition.
    The exact unfolding belongs to the classical Hecke method, presented in the packet setting in [4,
Section 10]. The dynamical conclusion uses the two-sided tube criterion of [3, Corollary 3.3] and the
prime-degree measure classification of Einsiedler, Katok, and Lindenstrauss [2, Corollary 1.4]. We
give the residue estimate, local-module bounds, and the passage to entropy in almost every ergodic
component below.
