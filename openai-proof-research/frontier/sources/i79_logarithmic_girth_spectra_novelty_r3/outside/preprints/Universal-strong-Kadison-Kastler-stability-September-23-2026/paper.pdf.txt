                                                 Abstract
          We prove that sufficiently close unital von Neumann algebras are conjugate by a unitary
      arbitrarily close to the identity. The tolerance depends only on the prescribed distance of that
      unitary from the identity, uniformly over all algebras, representations, and Hilbert spaces. This
      resolves the strong Kadison–Kastler conjecture.

1    Introduction
For a von Neumann algebra M ⊆ B(H), write M1 for its closed operator-norm unit ball. The
Kadison–Kastler distance between two such algebras is
                                    (                                        )
                   d(M, N ) = max       sup inf ∥x − y∥,      sup inf ∥x − y∥ .                (1.1)
                                      x∈M1 y∈N1              y∈N1 x∈M1

Kadison and Kastler introduced this metric in 1972 [28]. Their perturbation problem asks whether
closeness of the unit balls forces spatial isomorphism, implemented by a unitary close to the
identity. We prove the uniform form of this assertion.

Theorem 1.1 (Strong Kadison–Kastler stability). For every ε > 0 there is δ = δ(ε) > 0 such that
the following holds. If H is any complex Hilbert space and M, N ⊆ B(H) are unital von Neumann
algebras with identity IH and d(M, N ) < δ, there is a unitary u ∈ B(H) satisfying

                                  uM u∗ = N,          ∥u − IH ∥ < ε.

   The tolerance is independent of the algebras, their type, their representations, and the
cardinalities of their preduals and Hilbert spaces. Thus Theorem 1.1 resolves the strong Kadison–
Kastler conjecture positively. Its hypothesis concerns the ordinary distance (1.1).

1.1   Historical context
Early positive results treated type I algebras: Phillips proved spatial isomorphism, and Christensen
obtained a conjugating unitary close to the identity [10, 34]. The work of Christensen, Johnson, and
Raeburn–Taylor established the amenable case [11, 26, 37]. Christensen subsequently developed
the one-sided theory of near inclusions: an amenable von Neumann algebra sufficiently nearly
contained in another can be moved into it by a small unitary [13]. His earlier work also treated
close von Neumann subalgebras of a common finite von Neumann algebra [12]. The common finite
ambient algebra is an essential hypothesis of that result; merely assuming that both represented
algebras are finite gives a different problem. In a different common-ambient setting, Ino proved
near-identity unitary conjugacy for sufficiently close subalgebras of a countably decomposable
ambient algebra when both admit conditional expectations of finite probabilistic index from that
algebra [25, Theorem 3.2].
    The corresponding development for C ∗ -algebras clarifies the role of the conclusion. Christensen,
Sinclair, Smith, White, and Winter proved that sufficiently close C ∗ -algebras acting on a separable
Hilbert space are spatially isomorphic if one of them is norm separable and nuclear [17, 18].
Johnson’s examples exclude a uniform bound on the displacement of an implementing unitary that
tends to zero with the algebra distance; they do not exclude spatial isomorphism [27].
    For nonamenable von Neumann algebras, Cameron, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and
Wiggins constructed strongly stable factors of the form (L∞ (X) ⋊ SLn (Z)) ⊗ R, where n ≥ 3,
the action is free, ergodic, and probability preserving on a standard nonatomic probability space,
and R is the hyperfinite type II1 factor [6, 8]. Their proof combines amenable near-inclusion
methods, a common standard representation, and bounded cohomology. Their later work transfers
property Γ and Cartan structure between sufficiently close type II1 factors with separable predual,
and also studies stability of solidity, strong solidity, and tensor-product structure [9].
    The passage between representations is tied to Kadison’s similarity problem: whether a
bounded homomorphism from a C ∗ -algebra into B(H) is conjugate by an invertible operator to a
∗-homomorphism. Christensen’s work on property Γ factors provides a foundational special case
[14, 15]. Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, and White established perturbation results for the similarity
property and operator-algebraic invariants [16]; Cameron and collaborators then proved that the
similarity property is equivalent to continuity of commutants, with the required uniformity over
representations [7]. These results explain why ordinary norm closeness, control at all matrix levels,
and implementation on the original Hilbert space must be kept distinct throughout a proof of
strong stability. The companion manuscript [33] also proves a uniform commutator estimate,
using invariant tensors and a free-corner Hankel test. The amplification argument here instead
uses free-word randomization and is proved independently. Neither commutator estimate alone
constructs a nearby multiplicative correspondence between two algebras; Appendix A compares
their scope and this remaining perturbation step.
    The unit-ball metric supplies nearby elements but does not specify a linear or multiplicative
correspondence. We must construct such a correspondence and control its implementation on the
given Hilbert space, with estimates independent of the algebras and representations.

1.2   Proof overview
We first work on separable Hilbert spaces. For finite factors, two arguments meet in Section 5.
The first supplies matrix norm control: for a separably acting type II1 factor M ⊆ B(H) and
h = h∗ , Theorem 3.1 bounds all matrix amplifications of x 7→ [h, x] by an absolute multiple of
its ordinary operator norm. It combines Haagerup’s cyclic similarity estimate [21] and Popa’s


                                                  3
independence theorem for tracial ultraproducts [36] with a free-word randomization argument.
The constant is independent of matrix size and representation.
   A separate argument constructs an isomorphism close in ordinary norm to the inclusion.
Using the common-position construction of [8, Lemma 4.10], we move the factors to a tracial
representation in which one vector represents both traces, and the left and right actions of a
common hyperfinite subfactor R agree. The inclusion is irreducible, meaning R′ ∩ M = CI.
   For a countable strongly dense subgroup G of the unitary group of M , choose nearby unitaries
vg ∈ N . Their multiplication errors are uniformly small. Section 4 constructs a probability
space D with an amenable G-action preserving null sets. An adaptation of the averaging and
polar-correction method for approximate representations [5, 30] corrects the nearby unitaries to
measurable fields Wg : D → U(N ) satisfying

                                     Wg (x)Wh (g −1 x) = Wgh (x).

Here G need not be amenable: the correction produces a cocycle over its amenable action,
not a representation on the original space. On D2 , a tracial averaging argument and double
ergodicity force a suitable equivariant vector field to be constant. We give the unitary-coefficient
double-ergodicity argument in full, using the bilateral random-walk viewpoint of Kaimanovich;
Björklund gives the corresponding unitary formulation and another proof [3, 29]. The resulting
linear bijection preserves adjoints and squares and is close to the inclusion; proximity excludes
reversal of multiplication. Transporting this isomorphism back and applying the commutator
estimate gives a small unitary on the original Hilbert space.
    For infinite factors, a common copy of B(ℓ2 ) identifies the ordinary gap with the supremum
of all matrix gaps. Tensoring with one fixed hyperfinite factor gives type III1 factors with an
irreducible hyperfinite finite subfactor admitting a conditional expectation. This preparation uses
Marrakchi’s absorption criterion for a trivial bicentralizer and Haagerup’s construction of the
expected hyperfinite subfactor [22, 32]. Houdayer and Marrakchi’s September 2026 preprint proves
triviality of the bicentralizer for every type III1 factor with a faithful normal state [24, Theorem G];
the preparation here uses only the earlier absorption criterion.
    Small adjustments align the subfactor’s left and right actions and provide a common cyclic
separating vector Ω whose two vector states centralize R. For either algebra, the closure of
xΩ 7→ x∗ Ω is its Tomita operator. Theorem 6.5 shows that small matrix gaps force its two
graphs to be close when the output is multiplied by any c > 0, uniformly in c. This uniformity
permits comparison of the modular actions. A uniform infinitesimal estimate for type III factors
controls norm-continuous paths of unitaries whose conjugations nearly preserve the algebra. Using
Arveson’s nest distance formula [2], analytic smoothing brings the modular comparison into this
setting. Limits of conjugation maps yield, at each time, a unitary whose conjugation preserves
one algebra and which is close to the other algebra’s modular unitary. Averaging corrects their
multiplication law. Section 7 thereby obtains a single small change of position making the first
algebra invariant under that modular group.
    We form crossed products using this common action. Their unit balls are close at every
matrix size, and continuous decomposition identifies one crossed product as a type II∞ factor
[41, 42]. After aligning matrix units, its finite corner and the corresponding nearby corner are both
finite factors. The finite-factor theorem conjugates these corners and hence the crossed products.
Averaging gives a unital completely positive (UCP) map between the base algebras, meaning that
it preserves the identity and positivity at every matrix size. A state slice gives a second UCP
map between the original factors. The criterion in Theorem 2.4, derived from Ricard–Roydor’s


                                                   4
multiplication perturbation theorem [38, Proposition 3.2], turns both maps into small spatial
isomorphisms. It does not require the averaging maps to be normal.
    Finally, aligning centers and selecting measurable unitaries assembles the factor results on
separable spaces. For an arbitrary Hilbert space, finite sets of operators and vectors lie in suitable
separable restrictions. Extend their small conjugating unitaries by the identity. A subnet of the
conjugation maps converges pointwise in the weak operator topology to a UCP map. The finite
tests put its range in the required algebra, and the same criterion finishes the proof. All tolerances
depend only on the requested final unitary displacement.

2     Perturbation tools and a completely positive criterion
Several steps of the proof produce an averaged map instead of a homomorphism. The main result
of this section, Theorem 2.4, turns such a map into a small unitary conjugacy. All constants in
this section are independent of the algebras and Hilbert spaces.

     Inner products are linear in their first variable. A normal map between von Neumann algebras
is an ultraweakly continuous map. A map is unital completely positive, abbreviated UCP, if it
preserves the identity and each of its matrix amplifications is positive. Such maps are completely
contractive.
     We repeatedly use the following elementary compactness facts. A bounded operator ball is
compact in the weak operator topology (WOT). A net of UCP maps with fixed domain and
codomain B(H) has a subnet converging pointwise in WOT: use the product of the operator balls
indexed by domain elements. The limit is UCP, since linearity, the identity, and every finite matrix
positivity inequality pass to the limit. If the maps are within a in cb norm of a fixed map, so is
their limit; apply WOT closedness of norm balls at each fixed matrix size. If their values lie in
a fixed von Neumann algebra, the limit has the same property. Normality need not pass to this
limit and will not be required.
     For clarity, O(t) denotes a bound Ct for an absolute constant C, valid below an absolute
positive threshold for t. Constants may change between displays. A function tending to zero will
always be chosen uniformly over the explicitly stated class of algebras.

2.2    The amenable embedding theorem
The following form of Christensen’s theorem supplies the small changes of position used in the
proof [13].

Theorem 2.1 (Amenable near inclusions). Let R, Q ⊆ B(H) be von Neumann algebras with
identity I, with R amenable. If R ⊂γ Q and γ < 1/100, there is a unitary v ∈ (R ∪ Q)′′ such that

                                   vRv ∗ ⊆ Q,        ∥v − I∥ ≤ 150γ.

If also Q ⊂γ R and γ < 1/101, the same conclusion holds with vRv ∗ = Q.

    This is [8, Theorem 3.3(i)], with its strict near-inclusion convention. The location of v in
(R ∪ Q)′′ is useful: any algebra commuting with both R and Q also commutes with the change of
position. We apply the theorem to abelian algebras, hyperfinite finite factors, and type I algebras,
all of which are amenable.

2.3    From commutators to intertwiners
For h ∈ B(H) write adh (x) = [h, x] = hx − xh.

                                        dist(h, A′ ) ≤ ∥adh |A ∥cb .                                 (2.2)

                                    φ(h) = ⟨[I ⊗ h, p]ξ, η⟩.

2.5   Tensor products and averaging
Lemma 2.5. Let A, B ⊆ B(H) be unital von Neumann algebras with dcb (A, B) ≤ γ. If K is
separable, then
                          dcb (A ⊗ B(K), B ⊗ B(K)) ≤ γ.

finite-dimensional unital algebras, then

                                     dcb (A ⊗ D, B ⊗ D) ≤ γ.

Proof. Compress a contraction of Mn (A ⊗ B(K)) by increasing finite-rank projections on K. In a
corner of rank r, choose an opposite contraction in Mnr (B) within γ plus arbitrarily small slack,
and extend it by zero on the complement. The compressions of the original contraction converge
strongly to it. A bounded WOT cluster of the opposite contractions is in Mn (B ⊗ B(K))1 and is
within γ, by WOT closedness of the norm bound, choosing the approximation slack to tend to
zero along the compressions. Reverse the argument and take the supremum over n.
    Now start with a contraction in Mn (A ⊗ D). The first assertion supplies a nearby contraction
in Mn (B ⊗ B(K)). Average it by conjugation over the unitary group of each finite-dimensional

stage of D′ . The input commutes with these groups, so the bound is preserved. A WOT cluster
of the averages commutes with D′ and still lies in Mn (B ⊗ B(K)). The von Neumann tensor

Mn (B ⊗ D). Reverse and amplify as before.

Lemma 2.6 (Invariant operator means). Let G be a discrete amenable group and m a left invariant
mean on ℓ∞ (G): thus m is a positive unital linear functional with m(f (h−1 ·)) = m(f ) for every
h ∈ G. For a bounded family Tg ∈ B(K), define

                                     ⟨(mg Tg )ξ, η⟩ = mg ⟨Tg ξ, η⟩.

This averaging map from bounded operator families to B(K) is UCP, commutes with multiplication
on either side by a fixed operator, and preserves weak-operator closed linear subspaces. If ∥Tg −T ∥ ≤

9     Centers and arbitrary Hilbert spaces
We now pass from Theorem 8.4 to arbitrary von Neumann algebras. There are two steps. On

implementers. On an arbitrary Hilbert space, separable restrictions instead supply a completely
positive map into the neighboring algebra. The UCP criterion then gives a unitary on the original
Hilbert space. The uniformity of Theorem 8.4 is essential in both steps.

A     Uniformity and commutator estimates
The proof distinguishes ordinary norm control, control at every matrix size, and implementation on
the original Hilbert space. We compare the uniformity required for these steps with the commutator
estimate in the similarity companion, and then record how the small-unitary conclusion controls
both matrix gaps and commutants.

A.1    Uniformity over factors and representations
Fix an abstract type II1 factor M with separable predual. A stability statement uniform over its
representations would assign, for each ε > 0, a number δM (ε) > 0 that works for every faithful
normal unital representation ρ : M → B(H) and every unital von Neumann algebra N ⊆ B(H): if
d(ρ(M ), N ) < δM (ε), then a unitary within ε of the identity conjugates ρ(M ) onto N . Even with
this representation uniformity, the tolerance may depend on M .
    The quantifier order for universal stability is stronger: the tolerance must be chosen before the
factor as well as before its representation. Positivity of each δM (ε) does not imply
                                            inf δM (ε) > 0.
                                            M

Consequently, a representation-transfer argument with absolute constants preserves an already
universal tolerance but does not by itself remove factor dependence from its input.
    In Theorem 5.1, uniformity is obtained before the concluding reductions. The common-position
theorem has absolute constants, the boundary argument in Theorem 5.9 gives an isomorphism with
an absolute ordinary-norm bound, and Theorem 3.1 supplies an absolute amplification constant.
Their combination gives the single modulus ηfin for all separably acting finite factors. Sections 8
and 9 propagate this uniform control through finite corners, measurable assembly, and separable
restrictions.

A.2    Two commutator arguments
The amplification theorem needed here concerns selfadjoint implementers of commutators on
separably acting type II1 factors. The similarity companion proves a broader estimate: for
every unital von Neumann algebra P ⊆ B(K), every Y ∈ B(K), every integer n ≥ 1, and every
X ∈ Mn (P ),
                                ∥[Y (n) , X]∥ ≤ C∥adY |P ∥∥X∥,
where Y (n) is the diagonal amplification on K n and C is absolute, with no separability assumption
[33, Theorem 1.2].
    Both arguments use cyclic representation estimates and tracial free products to obtain bounds
independent of matrix size. In the present paper, the column estimate of Theorem 3.2 controls
randomized matrix coefficients. The free-word estimates bound their commutators, while the
averaged products converge weakly to a positive scalar multiple of the identity in (3.30). Together
these facts yield Theorem 3.1.
    The companion’s central intermediate result is a corner estimate. It works in a tracial free
product L = D ∗ A0 , where D is a hyperfinite II1 factor generated by increasing full matrix algebras
and A0 is a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state. Equip L with the
free-product trace. Writing Lx for left multiplication by x on L2 (L), suppose that G ∈ B(L2 (L))
commutes with LD and that, for some e ≥ 0,

                                 ∥[G, Lx ]∥ ≤ e∥x∥         (x ∈ pLp),

where p ∈ D is a projection of trace 1/m for an integer m ≥ 2. For a trace-zero selfadjoint unitary
s ∈ A0 , the companion bounds the compression of [G, Ls ] to L2 (A0 ) by C0 e, with C0 independent
of m [33, Theorem 3.1]. Its proof analyzes invariant tensors of reduced words, approximates
operators by right multipliers on small right supports, and obtains the trace-independent bound
from a Hankel compression [33, Sections 3–5]. Using Popa’s independence theorem, the companion
transfers this estimate to finite factors; passage through a countable amplification treats their
arbitrary separable normal representations [33, Section 6 and Proposition 7.1].
    The finite stability proof here uses Theorem 3.1 directly. For the near-identity isomorphism
θ : M → N constructed there, the switch operator on H ⊕ H has commutator with x 7→ x ⊕ θ(x)
whose off-diagonal entries are x − θ(x) and its negative. Applying the amplification theorem to
this paired representation converts the ordinary bound on θ − id into a cb bound. Theorem 2.3
then supplies the unitary on the original H. This identifies the precise role of commutator control
in the stability argument.

A.3    Matrix gaps and commutants at the conclusion
Corollary A.1. For every ε > 0 there is δ > 0, independent of the algebras, their representations,
and their Hilbert spaces, such that unital von Neumann algebras M, N ⊆ B(H) with the same
identity and d(M, N ) < δ satisfy

                                dcb (M, N ) < ε,        d(M ′ , N ′ ) < ε.

Proof. Choose δ from Theorem 1.1 with unitary displacement ε/2. The resulting unitary satisfies
uM u∗ = N and ∥u − I∥ < ε/2. Conjugation by its diagonal amplification identifies the unit balls
of Mn (M ) and Mn (N ) and moves each contraction by at most 2∥u − I∥, independently of n. The
same estimate applies to the commutants because uM ′ u∗ = N ′ .


                                                   66
    Since d(M, N ) ≤ dcb (M, N ), this corollary shows that the ordinary and completely bounded
distances induce the same uniform structure on the unital von Neumann algebras in each B(H),
with tolerances uniform over H. It also gives uniform continuity of the commutant operation for
the ordinary distance. These controls follow at the conclusion from one small implementing
unitary; the separate estimates in the proof are what make construction of that unitary possible.
