                                                  Abstract
         We prove that the full static displacement-to-traction map uniquely determines both real
      smooth Lamé moduli on every bounded connected smooth domain in R3 , provided µ > 0 and
      3λ + 2µ > 0 on the closure. This resolves the smooth three-dimensional isotropic elastic Calderón
      uniqueness problem under these positivity assumptions.

    Thus Theorem 1.1 gives a positive resolution of the smooth three-dimensional isotropic elastic
Calderón uniqueness problem under (1.1). It uses the full zero-frequency boundary operator in
fixed Euclidean coordinates. The coefficients need not be analytic or close to constants, and their
agreement near the boundary is not an additional hypothesis.

History and significance. The inverse problem asks whether static boundary measurements
determine both spatially varying elastic moduli. The scalar conductivity problem posed by Calderón
[1] and the complex geometric optics uniqueness theorem of Sylvester and Uhlmann [17] are
important antecedents, but elasticity couples two moduli through vector displacements. Nakamura
and Uhlmann recovered the elastic boundary Taylor series [14]. Their original global claim [13]
was corrected in [15]. The erratum identifies two defects: the prescribed initial-value problem for a
planar matrix transport need not be solvable, and substitution of the corrected complex geometric
optics solutions yields a pseudodifferential equation where a partial differential equation had been
asserted. Its corrected theorem proves uniqueness when both ∥∇µj ∥C m are sufficiently small.
    Eskin and Ralston developed higher-order matrix complex geometric optics expansions using
invertible planar transport frames, together with restricted elasticity uniqueness results [6, 7].
A direct inspection of the differential map from their auxiliary vector–scalar fields to physical
displacement [7, Section 3] shows that it is noninjective. The reduction alone therefore does not
transfer equality of the physical boundary maps to equality of unrestricted auxiliary Cauchy data.
The proof here transfers exact physical solutions while retaining their actual divergence.
    In two dimensions, Imanuvilov and Yamamoto proved global uniqueness for smooth coefficients
satisfying µ > 0 and λ + µ > 0, without a smallness assumption [11]. In three dimensions, Lin and
Nakamura gave local boundary reconstruction at finite regularity [12]; Tan and Liu obtained boundary
jets on smooth Riemannian manifolds and global recovery under their analytic hypotheses [18]. A
recent preprint of Chen, Jiang, Liu, and Tao treats structured shear moduli under axisymmetry,
separation, or directional quasianalyticity in the specified geometries [3]. Theorem 1.1 treats the
full smooth class under (1.1).

Conventions. All differential identities are complexified by linearity. Dot products of complex
vectors, including the null condition and orthogonality, use the complex bilinear extension of the
Euclidean product. Sobolev norms and Hilbert-space adjoints use the usual Hermitian structure.
Equality of the real boundary maps extends complex linearly to the complex boundary data used in
the proof.

Proof strategy. The proof converts equality of the boundary maps into an exact exterior
comparison of physical solutions. Boundary determination first gives matching boundary jets, from
which we construct common smooth extensions of the coefficients outside Ω. Given a solution in
the first medium, equality of displacement and traction then lets us glue a solution in the second

    Fix a nonzero complex null vector θ, so θ · θ = 0, and write Dθ = θ · ∇. The real and imaginary
parts of θ span a plane on which Dθ is a nonzero multiple of a Cauchy–Riemann operator; the
remaining real coordinate is transverse to these planes. For solutions with exponential phase eτ θ·x ,
the leading displacement–divergence pair (a, b) has a ∈ Hθ = {a ∈ C3 : θ · a = 0} and b ∈ C. The

normalization, and satisfies a compatibility identity for the defect div u − d. Its supported Carleman
estimate, together with a compatible high-order transport recursion and a correction using the
physical elasticity operator, realizes a full local frame in Eθ by exact displacements. The construction
controls both the leading pair and its weighted first derivatives. Sections 3 and 4 prove these facts.

2    Boundary reduction and physical transfer
Throughout the proof, the two coefficient pairs satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1 and have
equal displacement-to-traction maps.

Lemma 2.1 (Common exterior extension). The two pairs admit real smooth extensions to R3 that
satisfy (1.1) everywhere, coincide on R3 \ Ω, and equal a common constant admissible pair outside a
ball.

Proof. By Theorem 1.2 of Tan and Liu [18], equality of the elastic Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps
determines all boundary partial derivatives of both Lamé moduli. Our inequalities imply λ +
µ > µ/3 > 0, so their positivity condition holds. Their definition of the boundary operator is
λ(div u)n + µ(∇u + (∇u)T )n, so its Euclidean specialization agrees with (1.3). Consequently the
two pairs have identical jets at every point of ∂Ω. Lin and Nakamura also give local boundary
reconstruction, with curved boundaries discussed in Section 4 of [12].
    Smoothly extend the first pair to a neighborhood of Ω. By compactness and strict positivity,
shrink the neighborhood so that (1.1) still holds there. Choose a smooth cutoff equal to one on a
smaller neighborhood of Ω and supported in the first neighborhood. Interpolate with any constant
pair satisfying (1.1). The admissible set in the (λ, µ) plane is convex, so this produces a smooth
admissible global extension of the first pair, constant outside a compact set.
    For the second pair use its prescribed values in Ω and the first extension on the complement.
Equality of all jets makes this piecewise definition smooth across each boundary component.
Positivity holds on both sides and on the boundary. This proves the assertion, including when the
complement of Ω has bounded components.

    Fix these extensions and retain the notation λj , µj and Lj = Lλj ,µj for them. Choose r > 0
so that Ω ⊂ Br and the extensions are constant outside Br . Here BR is the open ball of radius R
centered at the origin.

6     Recovery of the coefficients
The holomorphic comparison from Proposition 5.3 is constrained by the geometry of its polarization
bundle. We first show that this comparison is the identity, and then recover the coefficients from
the transport matrices.

6.1   The bundle on the null conic
Let H → C be the holomorphic bundle with fiber Hθ , and let N ⊂ H be the null line subbundle
with fiber Cθ. Thus E = H ⊕ O, where O denotes the trivial holomorphic line bundle on C. On
P1 (C), let O(−1) denote the tautological line bundle and O(−2) its tensor square.
