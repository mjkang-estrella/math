      Relative independence of the separable quotient problem

                                               Abstract
        The separable quotient problem asks whether every infinite-dimensional Banach space has a
    separable infinite-dimensional quotient. We prove that this statement is independent of ZFC,
    relative to the consistency of ZFC with a measurable cardinal. This holds over both the real and
    complex fields. For spaces of norm density ℵ1 , we obtain independence relative to the consistency
    of ZFC alone.

1      Introduction
Every infinite-dimensional Banach space contains a separable infinite-dimensional closed subspace:
take the closed span of a countable linearly independent set. The corresponding assertion for
quotients is the separable quotient problem, traditionally attributed to Banach and Mazur. For a
scalar field F ∈ {R, C}, let SQF denote the statement

       Every infinite-dimensional Banach space over F admits a bounded linear surjection onto
       a separable infinite-dimensional Banach space.

Equivalently, each such space X has a closed subspace M for which X/M is separable and infinite
dimensional. The equivalence follows by applying the open mapping theorem to the induced bijection
from a space modulo the kernel of a surjection.
    We work with the axiom of Choice throughout. Write c = 2ℵ0 for the cardinality of the continuum.
For an uncountable cardinal κ, a probability measure on P(κ) is κ-additive if it is additive on
pairwise disjoint families of cardinality strictly less than κ; the sum over such a family means the
supremum of its finite subsums. We say that κ is real-valued measurable if it carries such a measure
vanishing on singletons. In particular, the hypothesis below requires a measure on every subset of c,
with this stronger additivity property.
    The two implications below are proved in ZFC. Their hypotheses are then realized in models at
the indicated consistency strengths; this gives the relative-independence conclusion.

Theorem 1.1. For each F ∈ {R, C}, ZFC proves the following implications.

    (i) If c is real-valued measurable, then SQF holds.

 (ii) If the continuum hypothesis holds, then SQF fails.

Consequently, if ZFC with a measurable cardinal is consistent, then SQF is independent of ZFC.
Consistency of its failure requires only consistency of ZFC.

   The CH counterexamples already occur at norm density ℵ1 , the smallest possible nonseparable
density (Corollary 9.5). At this density, an earlier positive theorem yields a sharper consistency
conclusion. Let SQℵF1 denote the assertion that every Banach space over F of norm density exactly
ℵ1 has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient.

Corollary 1.2. If ZFC is consistent, then SQℵF1 is independent of ZFC for each F ∈ {R, C}.

    The positive side of this corollary uses the density criterion of Saxon and Sánchez Ruiz described
below, together with Martin’s axiom. The negative side uses the counterexample constructed here.
The measurable cardinal hypothesis in Theorem 1.1 concerns the unrestricted quotient assertion.

History and methods. The history and many positive cases of the problem are surveyed in [4, 8].
Brech notes that the problem is not stated explicitly in Banach’s 1932 book and identifies Rosenthal’s
1969 paper as an early written formulation [4, p. 74]. Johnson and Rosenthal developed the weak-star
basic-sequence method and proved that every separable infinite-dimensional Banach space has an
infinite-dimensional quotient with a Schauder basis [15]. Argyros, Dodos and Kanellopoulos proved
the positive answer for every dual Banach space [2, Theorem 15]. Their Proposition 16, based on
work of Hagler and Johnson [13], also shows that an unconditional basic sequence in X ∗ gives a
separable infinite-dimensional quotient of X with an unconditional basis. This implication is central
to our positive argument. Morris and Yost give a common sufficient condition covering both dual

                                                   2
spaces and spaces that are the closed linear span of a weakly compact subset [17]. More recently,
Patri states a c0 -quotient criterion for L∞ spaces admitting an infinite-dimensional Schur quotient,
in which weakly convergent sequences converge in norm. He applies the criterion to a class of
nonseparable Bourgain–Pisier spaces [19].
    Earlier set-theoretic positive results depend on the density of the space. Write b for the least
cardinality of a family in NN unbounded under eventual domination, where f ≤∗ g means f (n) ≤ g(n)
for all sufficiently large n. Saxon and Sánchez Ruiz proved that every infinite-dimensional Banach
space of density less than b has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient [21]; see also [4, Theorem 2.1].
In particular, the positive assertion at density ℵ1 already follows under b > ℵ1 . For large densities,
Dodos, Lopez-Abad and Todorčević proved, relative to infinitely many strongly compact cardinals,
consistency with GCH of a positive answer for every Banach space of density at least ℵω [6].
The positive implication here applies to every infinite-dimensional Banach space. The negative
construction uses conditional norming operations in the tradition of Gowers–Maurey [12], branch
functionals as in James-tree constructions [14, 1], and coding by order positions in coherent finite
ordinal closures [3, Section 2.4]. We give the particular construction and all its estimates in full.
    We write BZ and SZ for the closed unit ball and unit sphere of a Banach space Z, and [zi : i ∈ I]
for a norm-closed linear span. All separability and density assertions about subsets of dual spaces
refer to the norm topology unless another topology is specified.

The common criterion. For a closed subspace F ⊂ X ∗ , define the evaluation map
                                RF : X −→ F ∗ ,         (RF x)(f ) = f (x).
A separable infinite-dimensional quotient exists precisely when some infinite-dimensional closed F
has norm-separable evaluation image. In the necessary direction F may itself be chosen separable.
Section 2 proves this criterion, including the surjectivity step. The two constructions in the paper
use this same criterion in opposite ways.
    For the positive implication, start with a putative counterexample X and a separable infinite-
dimensional subspace E ⊂ X ∗ . The measure first forces RE (BX ) to have density c: a smaller
dense set would allow simultaneous summable evaluations on a suitable basic sequence, and hence
a separable quotient. From the large evaluation image we construct a seminormalized family in
X ∗ which vanishes almost everywhere at each point of X. Odell–Rosenthal supplies sequential
bidual approximations. Coding these approximations lets us use ordinary Borel Fubini to select an
unconditional basic sequence from that family. This contradicts the Hagler–Johnson consequence
above. The Borel coding is what permits the integration argument; no interchange theorem for
arbitrary power-set measures is needed.
    For the negative implication, we build a dual space E = X0∗ with coordinate vectors indexed by
ω1 . We construct paths from successive finitely supported functionals with bounded partial sums;
each path therefore defines a functional on E. Each piece carries an integer weight. The coding
makes distinct paths share only finitely many weights, yielding uniform estimates for combinations
of their tails. A block argument then gives, on every infinite-dimensional subspace of E, uncountably
many norm-separated restrictions of path functionals. CH lets us assign all these paths to coordinates
of E. A small perturbation of the quotient map E ∗ → E ∗ /X0 produces a new predual X whose
evaluations approximate all path cosets. Its evaluation image on every separable infinite-dimensional
subspace of X ∗ ≃ E is nonseparable, excluding all separable infinite-dimensional quotients. The
perturbation lemma in Section 9 isolates this change of predual from the combinatorial construction.

Organization. Section 2 develops the evaluation criterion and a selection lemma for strictly
singular operators. Section 3 proves the positive implication over R, and Section 4 transfers it

Proposition 2.2 (Evaluation criterion). For an infinite-dimensional real or complex Banach space
X, the following are equivalent:

   1. X admits a bounded linear surjection onto a separable infinite-dimensional Banach space.

   2. There is a closed infinite-dimensional subspace F ⊂ X ∗ for which RF (X) is norm separable.

3     A measure on the continuum gives separable quotients
We prove the positive implication over the real field. The argument has two steps. First we show
that failure of a separable infinite-dimensional quotient forces an evaluation image of maximal
density, and use that image to construct a dual family whose evaluation at each fixed vector is
almost surely zero. We then code the family by sequences in a separable space and select an infinite
sequence satisfying all finite suppression estimates.

3.1   The measure hypothesis
Throughout this section let κ = c = 2ℵ0 , regarded as its initial ordinal. Assume that µ is a probability
measure on P(κ), vanishes on singletons, and is κ-additive in the following convention: for every
disjoint family (Aξ )ξ<λ with λ < κ,
                                            (︂ ⋃︂    )︂       ∑︂
                                        µ           Aξ =            µ(Aξ ),
                                             ξ<λ              ξ<λ

where a sum of nonnegative terms is the supremum of its finite subsums. In particular, µ is countably
additive, and a union of fewer than κ null sets is null: disjointify such a family before applying the
displayed identity. No additivity assertion for families of size κ is intended.

Lemma 3.1. The cardinal κ is regular. The measure µ is atomless, and for each positive integer N
the set κ has a partition into N sets of measure 1/N .

Proof. Every set of cardinality less than κ is null. If cf(κ) < κ, a cofinal family of fewer than κ
initial segments would therefore express κ as a null set.
    Fix an injection b : κ → {0, 1}N . If A were a positive atom, then for each successive bit one
of the two cells would have full measure in the preceding cell of A. This gives decreasing subsets
An ⊂ A of measure µ(A) on which the first n bits are fixed. Their intersection has at most one point,
whereas countable additivity gives it measure µ(A) > 0. This contradiction proves atomlessness.
    We include the elementary divisibility argument. Every positive set has a positive subset of
measure at most any given ε > 0: repeatedly split a positive set into two positive sets and retain
the smaller part. A maximal disjoint family of such small positive subsets of a measurable set A is
countable, since the measure is finite, and covers A modulo a null set. For 0 ≤ t ≤ µ(A), successive
unions of these pieces either attain measure t, converge upwards to t, or first cross t. In the last
case the union before that crossing has measure in (t − ε, t). Thus one can approximate t from
below with error at most ε. Apply this construction repeatedly in the remainder of A, with errors
tending to zero, and take the union. It has measure exactly t. Removing sets of measure 1/N one
at a time gives the required partition.

3.4    The positive conclusion
Theorem 3.5. Suppose that c carries a c-additive probability measure on its full power set that
vanishes on singletons, with additivity understood for disjoint families of cardinality strictly less than
c. Then every infinite-dimensional real Banach space has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient.

Proof. Suppose that X is a counterexample. A consequence of the Hagler–Johnson Theorem, in
the precise form of [2, Proposition 16], says that an unconditional basic sequence in X ∗ gives a
separable infinite-dimensional quotient of X. Thus X ∗ contains no such sequence. Choose an
infinite-dimensional separable closed subspace E ⊂ X ∗ . It contains no isomorphic copy of ℓ1 , whose
usual basis would give an unconditional basic sequence in X ∗ .
    Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 give a seminormalized family fα = hα RE with the pointwise null-support
property. Lemma 3.4 then produces an unconditional basic sequence in X ∗ , the desired contradiction.


   The real positive implication is now complete. The next section transfers it to complex Banach
spaces; Section 10 supplies a model of its measure hypothesis from a measurable cardinal.

satisfies the rules of Section 5. We first show that different paths have almost disjoint weight sets.
We then use the Type II operation to obtain convergence on all paths simultaneously, and show
that this already controls every functional on a block subspace.

The next step makes the path coding usable inside arbitrary subspaces. We must be able to prescribe
the next weight of a path and still find a bounded vector on which its next piece has a substantial
value. All constants in this section are uniform in the family and in the prescribed weight.

on its color with successive ordinal supports. In the mixed family, its supports are successive in

of its members; successive blocks use successive finite sets of indices. We use the parameters

10     Set-theoretic models and consistency bounds
The analytic implications concern Banach spaces inside a fixed model of set theory. We now verify
that their hypotheses give the two required relative-consistency statements. Choice is retained
throughout.
    Real-valued measurability and its strict additivity convention are as defined in the introduction;
this agrees with [5, Definition 1.1]. Atomlessness is a separate property and is not part of that
definition.
    We use the following preservation theorem of Solovay [22, Theorem 7], in the formulation
recorded in [5, Fact 1.27].

Theorem 10.1 (Solovay). If κ is real-valued measurable, then forcing with a measure algebra
preserves its real-valued measurability. In particular, in the extension there is a κ-additive probability
measure on the full power set of κ in that extension, vanishing on singletons.

    Here a measure algebra is the Boolean algebra of measurable sets modulo null sets of a probability
space, with zero omitted when it is used as a forcing notion. The special case of Theorem 10.1 used
below also has a direct proof in [10, Section 2, Lemmas 2.1–2.4]. That construction measures every
subset of κ in the extension, is countably additive, and makes unions of fewer than κ null sets null.
These properties give the stated additivity: in a disjoint family of size less than κ, only countably
many members have positive measure, and the union of the remaining members is null.

Proposition 10.2. If ZFC with a measurable cardinal is consistent, then ZFC with a real-valued
measurable continuum is consistent.

Proof. Work in a ground model of ZFC with a measurable cardinal κ. A κ-complete nonprincipal
ultrafilter on κ gives a {0, 1}-valued probability measure witnessing its real-valued measurability.

    We first record the cardinal arithmetic needed to control the continuum. The cardinal κ is
regular: sets of size less than κ are null, so a cofinal union of fewer than κ such sets cannot cover κ.

each ξ < λ, the ultrafilter chooses one of the two sets on which the statement ξ ∈ e(α) is constant.
Their intersection belongs to the ultrafilter by κ-completeness, although injectivity makes that
intersection have at most one element. This is impossible. Every countable sequence of ordinals
below κ is therefore bounded, and

10.1    Independence at density ℵ1
For the restricted assertion, the positive consistency bound can be lowered using the earlier small-
density theorem of Saxon and Sánchez Ruiz [21]: every infinite-dimensional real Banach space of
norm density less than b has a separable infinite-dimensional quotient; see [4, Theorem 2.1] for its
statement and a proof sketch. We explain the set-theoretic hypothesis that makes this theorem
apply at density ℵ1 .
     The axiom MAℵ1 states that, in any nonempty partial order with the countable chain condition,
every collection of at most ℵ1 dense sets is met by a filter. Full Martin’s axiom, denoted by MA,
requires this conclusion for every collection of fewer than c dense sets. The axiom MAℵ1 implies
b > ℵ1 . To see this, fix a family A ⊂ NN of cardinality at most ℵ1 . A condition is a pair (s, A),
where s is a finite sequence of natural numbers and A is a finite subset of A. A stronger condition
(t, B) extends s, contains A in B, and satisfies t(n) ≥ f (n) for every f ∈ A and every new coordinate
n of t. Conditions with a fixed stem s have common extensions for every finite subcollection: retain
s and take the union of their finite sets. There are only countably many stems, so the partial order
is σ-centered and in particular has the countable chain condition.
     For each f ∈ A, the conditions with f ∈ A form a dense set. For each n, the conditions whose
stem has length at least n also form a dense set, since finitely many functions can be dominated
when a stem is extended. A filter meeting all these sets has a union of stems g ∈ NN . Once a
condition has included f in its finite set, every common strengthening with a sufficiently long stem
dominates f on the new coordinates. Directedness of the filter therefore gives f ≤∗ g. Thus A is
bounded, proving b > ℵ1 .

Proof of Corollary 1.2. Starting with a ground model of ZFC and GCH, the forcing construction in
[16, Chapter VIII, Theorem 6.3] gives a model of Martin’s axiom with c = ℵ2 . Gödel’s constructible-
universe theorem and the relative-consistency interpretation of forcing therefore give

                             Con(ZFC) =⇒ Con(ZFC + MA + c = ℵ2 ).

This model satisfies MAℵ1 , hence b > ℵ1 by the preceding argument. The small-density theorem
gives a separable infinite-dimensional quotient for every real Banach space of norm density ℵ1 . A
complex Banach space has the same norm density as its underlying real space, so Proposition 4.1
gives the corresponding complex conclusion.
    On the other side, consistency of ZFC gives a CH model, and Corollary 9.5 supplies real and
complex counterexamples of norm density ℵ1 there. Consequently, for each field F ∈ {R, C},
