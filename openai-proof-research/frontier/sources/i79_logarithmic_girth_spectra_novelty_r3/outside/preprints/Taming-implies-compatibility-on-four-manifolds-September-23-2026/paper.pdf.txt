            Taming implies compatibility on four-manifolds
                                                  OpenAI

                                                  Abstract
         We prove that every smooth almost complex structure on a closed four-manifold which is
      tamed by a symplectic form is compatible with a symplectic form. This gives a positive solution
      to Donaldson’s tamed-to-compatible conjecture.


1    Introduction
Let X be a closed smooth four-manifold and let J be a smooth almost complex structure on X. A
smooth real two-form ω is symplectic if it is nondegenerate and closed, meaning dω = 0. A real
two-form ω tames J if ω (v, Jv) > 0 for every nonzero tangent vector v. It is compatible with J if, in
addition, ω ( Ju, Jv) = ω (u, v). A taming form is automatically nondegenerate; thus a closed taming
form is symplectic.
    Donaldson asked whether the existence of a taming symplectic form on a closed four-manifold
implies the existence of a compatible one [2, Section 5.2, Question 2]. This existence question is
distinct from his a priori estimate conjecture for a prescribed-volume equation. The elementary
projection ω 7→ 12 (ω + ω ( J ·, J ·)) shows the obstacle: it preserves positivity on complex lines but
generally loses closedness. Our main result answers the existence question affirmatively.
Theorem 1.1. Suppose that a smooth almost complex structure J on a closed connected smooth real four-
manifold X is tamed by a symplectic form. Then there is a smooth symplectic form compatible with J.
    The almost complex structure in Theorem 1.1 is the given one. The conclusion imposes no
condition on the cohomology class of the compatible form. In particular, it does not assert that an
arbitrary taming class contains a compatible representative.
    Combining Theorem 1.1 with Li and Zhang’s cone comparison gives the following cohomologi-
cal refinement.
Corollary 1.2 (Taming and compatible cones). Let X, J satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1, and give X
the orientation induced by J. Let K tJ , K cJ ⊂ H 2 ( X; R) be the real de Rham classes represented by J-taming
and J-compatible symplectic forms, respectively. Let H J− be the subspace represented by smooth closed real
two-forms α satisfying α( Ju, Jv) = −α(u, v). Then

                                              K tJ = K cJ + H J− ,

where the right side is a Minkowski sum. Write b2+ ( X ) for the positive index of the intersection form. If
b2+ ( X ) = 1, then K tJ = K cJ . In that case, every J-taming symplectic form ω has a smooth J-compatible
symplectic representative η with [η ] = [ω ] in H 2 ( X; R).
Proof. Theorem 1.1 makes K cJ nonempty. Li and Zhang’s four-dimensional cone comparison [12,
Corollary 1.1] therefore gives the cone identity and, when b2+ = 1, equality of the two cones. This
equality gives the claimed representative.

The argument and its main estimate
Fix an auxiliary J-Hermitian metric. If no compatible form exists, separation produces a nonzero
positive current P that annihilates closed invariant forms. An elliptic correction gives T = P + Q,
which annihilates every closed two-form, with Q initially an anti-invariant distribution. Represent
P as a positive weighted average of unit complex-line bivectors, and let µ be the resulting measure
on X, its trace measure. Corrected radial test functions give µ( Br ( x )) ≤ Cr2 . Regularization by a
finite power of the Hodge resolvent then proves Q ∈ L2 and an angular estimate: over pairs of
complex lines in this representation whose base points are less than r apart, the integral of their
squared difference, after parallel transport, is O(r4 ).
    The first regularized pairing only bounds ∥ Q∥2 , because the negative error in this pairing is
only uniformly bounded. To remove that error, we distinguish the points where P has positive
