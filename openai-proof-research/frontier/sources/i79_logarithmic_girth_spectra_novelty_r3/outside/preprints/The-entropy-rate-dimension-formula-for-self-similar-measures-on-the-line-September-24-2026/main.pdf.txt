                                               Abstract
        We prove that the Hausdorff dimension of every finite real self-similar measure equals the
     minimum of one and its random-walk entropy rate divided by its Lyapunov exponent. Exact
     overlaps are allowed, and the contraction ratios may be unequal and negative. This resolves the
     entropy-rate dimension conjecture.

1.1   Background and proof strategy
The dimension of a self-similar set is classical when its pieces are sufficiently separated. Moran’s
construction [13] and Hutchinson’s iterated-function-system framework [11] give the similarity-
dimension formula under the open set condition. Hutchinson also established the invariant-measure
construction used above [11, Theorem 4.4(1)]. Removing separation leads to the exact-overlap
problem: can dimension fall below its natural upper bound when distinct words never define the same
map? The set version is associated with Simon [18]; see also the formulation and historical discussion
in [22, Section 1]. The entropy-rate formulation addresses the additional loss of information when
exact overlaps do occur.
    Bernoulli convolutions provide an early and influential model for this question. These are the
laws of j≥0 εj λj , where 0 < λ < 1 and the εj are independent equiprobable signs. Erdős proved
         ∑︁

singularity for reciprocal Pisot parameters in (1/2, 1) [5]. A Pisot number is a real algebraic integer
greater than one whose other conjugates have modulus less than one. Solomyak proved absolute
continuity with an L2 density for almost every parameter in (1/2, 1) [19]; Peres–Solomyak later
gave a simpler proof [14]. Garsia introduced the discrete entropy rate in this setting and related it
to singularity [9, Theorem 1.2]. These results illustrate the sensitivity to overlaps and arithmetic;
absolute continuity and Hausdorff dimension are distinct questions.
    Hochman showed that dimH µ < min{1, H(p)/χ} forces superexponential concentration of
cylinder maps [10, Theorem 1.1], using inverse theorems for entropy growth under convolution. For
unequal ratios his entropy formulation already keeps track of the full affine map, including its slope
[10, Theorem 1.4]. The entropy-rate formula under weak exponential separation follows from his
work; the version allowing exact collisions is proved in Bárány–Verma [3, Theorem 3.5]. For Bernoulli
convolutions, Breuillard–Varjú developed approximation by algebraic parameters with controlled
entropy [4]. Building on these and earlier entropy methods, Varjú proved full dimension for every
transcendental parameter in (1/2, 1) [21, Theorem 3]. Rapaport proved the no-exact-overlap formula
for algebraic contraction ratios and arbitrary real translations, allowing signed unequal ratios [15,
Theorem 2].
    Rapaport–Varjú extended entropy and approximation methods to homogeneous three-map
systems [16]. For equal weights, their results include an exceptional parameter set of Hausdorff
dimension zero and the no-overlap formula when λ > 2−2/3 [16, Corollary 1.4 and Theorem 1.8].
Rapaport–Varjú proved the entropy-rate formula, including exact overlaps, for rational translations
and a positive common ratio [16, Theorem A.1]. Feng–Feng proved the no-exact-overlap formula for
algebraic translations and a signed common ratio [6, Theorem 1.2]. The distinction in Theorem 1.1
is that the entropy rate of the full affine-map walk is sharp even when exact collisions occur, with
no arithmetic or separation assumption.
    Absence of exact overlaps does not itself supply a quantitative separation estimate. Baker
[1, Theorem 1.3] and, independently, Bárány–Käenmäki [2, Theorem 2.1] constructed systems
without exact overlaps whose distinct cylinders approach one another at arbitrarily prescribed
superexponential rates. Thus the entropy carried by extremely close maps must be handled without
bounding the smallest positive separation.
    Our proof combines the entropy retained by this map walk with a finite-law estimate that
detects information across arbitrarily fine scales. It uses translation averaging, developed by
Wang [23, Section 4.1], and the entropy calculus and fair-pair arguments of Varjú [20, Section 2.2,
Propositions 20–21, and Lemma 22]. Uniform nonsaturation has an antecedent for Bernoulli
convolutions in Breuillard–Varjú [4, Lemma 13]. We prove the required entropy estimates here; the
only external dimension theorem used in the proof is exact dimensionality.
    The argument has three steps. First, for a finite real law ν, compare its exact entropy with the


                                                  3
entropy of its grid cell at a fixed scale. The difference is information hidden inside those cells. A
bound for the sum of several independent copies forces this information to be witnessed by fair
(equal-weight) two-point laws at smaller scales. The estimate is independent of the smallest distance
between support points.
     Second, group the coding symbols into blocks and condition on their symbol counts. This is the
block-type disintegration of Galicer–Saglietti–Shmerkin–Yavicoli [8, Section 6.4 and Lemma 6.6], also
developed by Saglietti–Shmerkin–Solomyak [17, Lemma 6.2]; Käenmäki–Orponen [12, Section 2.2]
explicitly allow repeated indexed maps in this construction. Here the counts fix each block’s signed
contraction. For a sequence of such blocks, its translation then identifies its complete composed map.
Conditioning costs only the entropy of the counts, so the map-entropy rate gives a lower bound for
the remaining translation entropy even when addresses collide. The same fixed contractions give a
small sumset bound. A hypothetical dimension deficit therefore yields a linear amount of fair-pair
mass across a finite band of very fine scales.
     Third, each pair produces a positive entropy gain against the unobserved tail. The bands
may end at arbitrarily large depths. We choose successive block lengths only after the preceding
endpoints are known. At each observation scale their retained blocks are disjoint, so their gains
telescope to a bounded total. Averaging over observation scales gives a fixed positive contribution
from every band, a contradiction as the number of bands grows.
     Section 2 proves the entropy calculus, nonsaturation, and two-point gain. Section 3 proves the
finite-law estimate. Section 4 applies it to conditional map laws, and Section 5 arranges the disjoint
gains and completes the proof. Section 6 derives the no-overlap measure and attractor formulas,
followed by the homogeneous and three-map specializations. No quantitative bound on the fine-scale
endpoints is needed.

1.2   The entropy-rate upper bound
We prove the upper bound before turning to the contradiction argument. Fix a positive integer
n. Use the finite set of distinct maps in the support of Gn as a new alphabet, giving each map
g its exact probability qg = P(Gn = g) > 0. Grouping independent coding symbols into n-blocks
shows that this new system has the same measure µ. Its one-symbol entropy is H(Gn ) and its mean
contraction depth is nχ, since coincident maps have the same absolute slope.
    For 0 < ε < nχ, the strong law shows that almost every address in this block system eventually
has, at every length j, a prefix of probability at least 2−j(H(Gn )+ε) and absolute contraction at most
2−j(nχ−ε) . There are at most 2j(H(Gn )+ε) such prefixes. For each fixed eventual starting length
J, let EJ be the closed set of addresses satisfying both conditions for every j ≥ J. Its compact
coding image is covered by intervals of diameter at most C2−j(nχ−ε) , where C bounds the attractor
diameter. The total ath power of these diameters tends to zero when a > (H(Gn ) + ε)/(nχ − ε).
The countable union over eventual starting lengths is a full-measure Borel set with this dimension
bound. The case of zero attractor diameter is immediate. Letting ε ↓ 0, then taking the infimum
over n, gives
                                           d ≤ min{1, h/χ}.                                        (1.3)
In particular, the conclusion of Theorem 1.1 already holds when h = 0, including a single-map
system. To prove equality in all other cases, it suffices to rule out

                                          d < 1,       dχ < h.                                    (1.4)

These inequalities are the contradiction hypothesis in Sections 4 and 5.

Lemma 2.4 (Two-point gain). Let X be bounded and J an independent fair bit. For 0 < s < v < t,

                           Gs,t (X + vJ) − Gs,t (X) ≥ ∆v (X) − s/v − v/t.                       (2.13)

                              Da := Ha (Y ) − Ha (X) = I(J; Za | Ua ).

   To compare s and v, use independent shifts Us , Uv and the same sample Y . The information
chain rule gives
                   I(J; Zv | Us , Uv ) ≤ I(J; Zs | Us , Uv ) + H(Zv | Zs , Us , Uv ).
The first two terms are Dv and Ds because the unused shift is independent. For each fixed fine grid
and each of its cells of positive probability, the conditional sample law is independent of Uv . That
cell, of length s < v, contains a boundary of the v-grid on a proportion s/v of its shifts. Otherwise
the coarse label is fixed; when a boundary occurs there are at most two coarse labels. Averaging
therefore gives H(Zv | Zs , Us , Uv ) ≤ s/v, whence Ds ≥ Dv − s/v.

                              Dt ≤ I(J; Zt , X | Ut ) = I(J; Zt | X, Ut ).

proof allows atoms, since boundary coincidences occur on null sets of shifts. Finally, any fair pair is
a translate of {0, v} after ordering its two points; translation invariance proves the last assertion,
including for reflected pairs.

6    Overlap conventions and dimension of the attractor
We first reconcile the two usual word-length conventions for an exact overlap. The following
observation also appears in [10, footnote 3]. The nonzero contraction hypothesis is useful here as
well as in the proof of the measure theorem.

Lemma 6.1. For a finite family of maps x ↦→ ri x + ti with 0 < |ri | < 1, an equality of maps for
two distinct nonempty words of arbitrary lengths implies such an equality for two distinct words of
the same length.

Proof. Suppose φu = φv and u ̸= v. If u were a proper prefix of v, writing v = uw and cancelling
the invertible map φu would give φw = id. This is impossible because a nonempty word has absolute
contraction less than one. The same applies with the words interchanged. Now φuv = φu ◦ φv =
φv ◦ φu = φvu . The words uv and vu have the same length and are distinct: if they were equal,
their first min{|u|, |v|} symbols would show that either u = v or the shorter word is a proper prefix
of the longer.

Corollary 6.2 (No exact overlaps). Let Φ be a finite nonempty indexed family of real similarities
with 0 < |ri | < 1 and no exact overlaps. For every strictly positive probability vector p,

                                 dimH µΦ,p = min{1, H(p)/χ(Φ, p)}.

Proof. At every length n, distinct words give distinct complete maps. Hence Gn is an injective
function of its symbol word, so H(Gn ) = nH(p) and hRW = H(p). Apply Theorem 1.1.
