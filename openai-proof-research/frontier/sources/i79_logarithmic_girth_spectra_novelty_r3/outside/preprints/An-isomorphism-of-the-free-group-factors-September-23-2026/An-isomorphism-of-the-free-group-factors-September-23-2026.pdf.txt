                     An isomorphism of the free group factors

                                                 Abstract
         We solve the free group factor isomorphism problem affirmatively by proving that L(F2 ) and
      L(F3 ) are isomorphic as tracial von Neumann algebras. The classical free group factor alternative
      then implies that all interpolated free group factors, including L(F∞ ), are isomorphic and have
      fundamental group R>0 .


1    Introduction
For a discrete group G, its group von Neumann algebra is
                   L(G) = {λ(g) : g ∈ G}′′ ,       (λ(g)ξ)(h) = ξ(g −1 h),     ξ ∈ ℓ2 (G),
with canonical trace τ (x) = ⟨xδe , δe ⟩. Write Fn for the free group on n generators and F∞ for the
free group on countably infinitely many generators. The free group factors arose in Murray and von
Neumann’s foundational work [9]. Their nonhyperfiniteness distinguished them from the hyperfinite
factor, but did not distinguish their ranks; see the historical account in [10, p. 518]. The question
whether L(Fm ) and L(Fn ) are isomorphic for distinct m, n ≥ 2 became a central problem in the
classification of finite factors. Dykema’s introduction [3, p. 123] records the early history and credits
Kadison with raising the rank-isomorphism question.
    Free probability supplied a way to study these factors through their generating distributions.
Voiculescu developed the reduced-free-product framework [14] and its semicircular and circular
models [15]. His compression results showed that the fundamental group of L(F∞ ) contains every
positive rational, and Rădulescu extended this to every positive real number [10]. Dykema and
Rădulescu independently constructed the interpolated factors L(Fr ) for real r > 1 and established
their amplification calculus [3, 11]. These results relate changes of rank to normalized corners. They
also give a dichotomy: the interpolated free group factors are either all isomorphic or pairwise
nonisomorphic. The version including the infinite parameter is due to Rădulescu [11, Corollary 4.7];
see also the precise restatement in [5, Theorem 1.1].
    The amplification calculus thus links rank to corner size, while the dichotomy reduces the
classification to two alternatives. To choose the isomorphic alternative, it suffices to construct
an isomorphism between two distinct finite ranks. Our constructive result is the following rank
comparison.
Theorem 1.1 (Isomorphism of consecutive ranks). For every integer n ≥ 3 there is a unital
trace-preserving normal ∗-isomorphism
                                            L(Fn ) ∼
                                                   = L(Fn+1 ).
    The construction gives a freely generating Haar n-tuple inside L(Fn+1 ). Applying it at ranks
three and four, and then using the established amplification formula, gives the headline conclusion.

    This gives an affirmative solution to the free group factor isomorphism problem. Together with
the classical dichotomy, this proves that all interpolated free group factors, including the infinite-rank
factor, are isomorphic. Their fundamental groups are therefore R>0 . We state and prove these
consequences in Corollary 6.1; the constructive rank comparison itself does not use interpolation.
    Free entropy offered another approach to distinguishing ranks. A free semicircular n-tuple
generates L(Fn ) [15, Remarks 1.10–1.11] and has free entropy dimension δ = n [17, Section 2.4(c),(d)].
Voiculescu asked whether δ of a finite self-adjoint tuple depends only on the tracial von Neumann
algebra it generates [17, Section 2.6]. Such invariance would distinguish the finite ranks. Our
isomorphisms give the opposite conclusion: Corollary 7.1 gives a negative answer and proves the
same failure for δ0 , δ ∗ , and δ ⋆ . Each of the four dimensions attains every integer value n ≥ 2 on finite
self-adjoint generating tuples of L(F2 ). The proof transports semicircular tuples for the microstates
dimensions δ, δ0 and group-algebra tuples for the nonmicrostates dimensions δ ∗ , δ ⋆ ; the two tuple
families need not coincide.

2    Tracial preliminaries and a free-sum estimate
Throughout, (M, τ ) is a von Neumann algebra with faithful normal tracial state. We write

                             ∥x∥2 = τ (x∗ x)1/2 ,     ∥x∥ = operator norm of x.

The Hilbert space completion of M in ∥·∥2 is L2 (M, τ ), with inner product ⟨x, y⟩ = τ (y ∗ x), linear in
the first variable. Left multiplication gives its faithful normal representation of M . A ∗-isomorphism
is a bijective complex-linear map preserving multiplication and adjoints. A linear map between
von Neumann algebras is normal if it is ultraweakly continuous. We use standard finite von
Neumann algebra facts, including bounded Borel functional calculus, Kaplansky density, and the
trace-preserving conditional expectation onto a von Neumann subalgebra. The expectation extends
to the orthogonal projection of L2 (M, τ ) onto the subalgebra’s L2 space. Section 6 recalls the
amplification convention separately.
    A family (Bi )i∈I of unital subalgebras is free if, for every integer r ≥ 1 and every choice of
elements satisfying the conditions below,

             τ (b1 · · · br ) = 0   whenever   bj ∈ Bij ,   τ (bj ) = 0,   ij ̸= ij+1 (1 ≤ j < r).

Freeness of unital ∗-algebras passes to their generated von Neumann algebras. Indeed, bounded
strong approximation gives bounded L2 approximation in a finite tracial algebra; telescoping a
product shows continuity of its trace under such approximations. Centering the approximants proves
the assertion. Freeness also persists when disjoint collections of the free components are grouped:
their algebraic spans consist of scalars and reduced products of centered elements, and the same
definition applies to the resulting reduced products.

Lemma 2.2 (Identification by a generating Haar tuple). For a freely generating Haar d-tuple
U in M , the assignment λ(xj ) 7→ Uj extends to a unital normal trace-preserving ∗-isomorphism

Proof. The vectors Ug , indexed by g ∈ Fd , are orthonormal because τ (Uh∗ Ug ) = τ (Uh−1 g ) = 1g=h .

    Consequently the map V : ℓ2 (Fd ) → L2 (M, τ ) with V δg = Ug is unitary. It intertwines λ(xj )
with left multiplication by Uj . Conjugation by V identifies their double commutants. The double
commutant on the right is the faithful normal left representation of W ∗ (U ) = M , so this spatial
identification gives the claimed isomorphism. It preserves the trace because V δe = 1.

    In particular, two freely generating Haar tuples of the same size in M are related by a normal
trace-preserving automorphism. The component algebras of a Haar tuple are free: expanding
centered Laurent polynomials reduces the assertion to the nonidentity group-word trace, and
bounded approximation gives freeness of the von Neumann closures.
    For completeness, L(Fd ) is a II1 factor for d ≥ 2. Its canonical trace is τ (x) = ⟨xδe , δe ⟩, which
is faithful and normal. Every nonidentity reduced word has infinitely many distinct conjugates:
choose a letter a whose powers cause no cancellation at either end of ak ga−k . At most two of the 2d
letters are excluded, and the reduced lengths then tend to infinity. Fourier coefficients of a central
element are constant on conjugacy classes and form an ℓ2 vector, so every coefficient off the identity
vanishes. Faithfulness of the tracial representation implies that the center consists of scalars. The
infinitely many orthonormal group unitaries show that the factor is infinite-dimensional.

7     Free entropy dimensions and von Neumann generators
For a bounded self-adjoint tuple Z in a tracial von Neumann algebra, let δ(Z) and δ0 (Z) denote
Voiculescu’s microstates free entropy dimensions. The second is the modified version using entropy in
the presence of the perturbing semicircular family. Write δ ∗ (Z) and δ ⋆ (Z) for the nonmicrostates di-
mensions defined through nonmicrostates free entropy χ∗ and free Fisher information Φ∗ , respectively.
We use the conventions in [17, Section 2.4] and [2, Section 4.1.1].
    By their definitions, all four quantities depend only on the joint tracial ∗-distribution of the tuple.
This is weaker than invariance under a change of von Neumann generators: two tuples can generate
the same von Neumann algebra without having the same joint distribution. The isomorphisms
proved above show that the four dimensions do change under such a replacement.
Corollary 7.1 (Dependence on von Neumann generators). Let M = L(F2 ) with its canonical trace.
For every integer n ≥ 2 there are a self-adjoint n-tuple X (n) and a self-adjoint 2n-tuple Y (n) such
that
                                   W ∗ (X (n) ) = W ∗ (Y (n) ) = M
and
                        δ(X (n) ) = δ0 (X (n) ) = n,            δ ∗ (Y (n) ) = δ ⋆ (Y (n) ) = n.
Consequently, none of δ, δ0 , δ ∗ , δ ⋆ is invariant under changing a finite self-adjoint W ∗ -generating
tuple of a tracial von Neumann algebra. Each takes unbounded values on such generating tuples of
the single factor L(F2 ).
