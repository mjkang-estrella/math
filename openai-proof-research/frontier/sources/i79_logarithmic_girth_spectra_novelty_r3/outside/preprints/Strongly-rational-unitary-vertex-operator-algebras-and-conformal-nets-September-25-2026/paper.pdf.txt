                                                 Abstract
          Every simple unitary strongly rational vertex operator algebra generates a completely
      rational conformal net. All its simple grading-restricted modules are unitarizable, its canonical
      fusion forms are positive, and the Carpi–Weiner–Xu functor gives a braided unitary tensor
      equivalence from its grading-restricted finite-length module category onto the finite-index
      sectors of the net. Gui’s extension theorems then identify normalized irreducible finite-index
      local extensions of these nets with simple CFT-type conformal extensions of the vertex operator
      algebras.

1     Introduction
Vertex operator algebras and conformal nets are two mathematical formulations of chiral conformal
field theory. A vertex operator algebra records field modes and their algebraic identities. A
conformal net assigns von Neumann algebras of bounded observables to intervals of the circle.
Their expected correspondence has both an analytic and a representation-theoretic part. Formal
commutation of fields must lead to commuting local algebras, and algebraic modules must
correspond to Hilbert-space sectors with the same fusion and braiding.
    We establish this correspondence for every simple unitary strongly rational complex vertex
operator algebra. The functor in the result is the Carpi–Weiner–Xu construction: it completes a
unitary module and integrates its actual closed smeared fields. The tensor product on the net side
is Connes fusion, with the Doplicher–Haag–Roberts braiding.

    The equivalence also compares extensions of the two theories. A conformal VOA extension
enlarges V while preserving its vacuum and conformal vector; a local net extension enlarges
the interval algebras compatibly with their conformal symmetries. Gui’s comparison theorems
identify their finite-index counterparts through the corresponding commutative algebra objects.

1.2   The correspondence problem and earlier work
Carpi, Kawahigashi, Longo and Weiner made strong locality the analytic condition for constructing
a conformal net from a simple unitary VOA [5, Theorems 6.8 and 8.1]. Their examples include
unitary affine and Virasoro VOAs and the moonshine VOA, and their construction is compatible
with unitary subalgebras. They conjectured strong locality for every simple unitary VOA [5,
Conjecture 8.18]. Theorem 1.1 establishes this assertion under strong rationality and then compares
the full representation theories in that setting. The locality issue is substantial: commuting
unbounded fields on a common domain need not generate commuting von Neumann algebras.
     The two representation theories already possess rich tensor structures. Huang’s results give
rigidity and modularity for strongly rational VOAs [22, 23], while Kawahigashi–Longo–Müger
establish modularity for completely rational conformal nets [25]. Identifying these structures
requires more than constructing the vacuum net. The Carpi–Weiner–Xu functor integrates
unitary modules through their closed fields and is fully faithful on strongly integrable modules
[18, Theorem 4.3], [14, Theorem 4.24]. Gui’s tensor comparison then identifies VOA fusion and
braiding with Connes fusion and DHR braiding under charged-field hypotheses [17, Corollary 3.23].
     Complete unitarity, full braided equivalence and complete rationality are already known for
affine VOAs at positive integral levels, even positive-definite lattice VOAs, parafermion VOAs at
positive integral levels, and type ADE discrete-series W -algebras. Gui’s results also cover finite
tensor products of these classes and regular cosets of unitary inclusions between such tensor products
[17, Theorem I]. His extension results propagate the comparison through further extensions and
coset constructions under their stated hypotheses [14, Theorem 6.18 and Example 6.19]. Here the
analytic and positivity hypotheses for the tensor embedding are established from strong rationality
and unitarity, and a separate reconstruction argument proves that every finite-index sector is in
its image.
     In the converse direction, Henriques and Tener construct a unitary VOA from a conformal net
with finite-dimensional vacuum energy spaces, recover a VOA from its associated net under their
AQFT-locality condition, and reconstruct modules from net representations [20, Theorems A–C
and 8.9]. Their AQFT-locality condition does not include polynomial energy bounds. Their
annulus representation theory and bounded vacuum operations for multidisc embeddings provide
the geometric tools used here [21, 19]. Those constructions start from an existing net. In this paper
they enter after the independent proof of strong locality, first for creation fields and ultimately for
the reconstruction of arbitrary irreducible sectors.
     Two earlier analytic methods are especially important to the proof. Carpi, Tanimoto and
Weiner showed that optimal energy order for Hermitian primary generators implies strong locality
[7, Theorem 3.7], with the correction [6]. Carpi and Tomassini obtained a C2 exponential
bound and used a contracting estimate to improve exponential growth to polynomial growth [8,
Proposition 3.17 and Theorem 4.5]. Here a finite-C2 filtration supplies the first contraction for
a generating family. A sharper normal-product count and a second contraction then yield the
optimal order required for locality.

form framework express candidate VOA fusion inner products through ordinary vertex operators
[18, 30]; Gui further developed their canonical normalization and geometric positivity [16]. Tener’s
positivity theorem for regular VOAs assumes a unitary structure on the algebraic fusion product
and a corresponding net representation of the source module [30, Theorem 4.4]. Our argument
constructs positivity on the full algebraic fusion object before presuming that it is unitarizable.
To reach the remaining simple modules, we use Carnahan–Miyamoto’s fixed-point regularity and
the Barron–Dong–Mason permutation construction [3, 2]. The required permutation product rests
on the extension tensor structures of Creutzig–Kanade–McRae and McRae [9, 28] and the one-slot
intertwiner correspondence of Dong–Li–Xu–Yu [11, Theorems 5.5 and 5.9].

1.3   Proof strategy
The central ordering issue is that a positive form on V does not initially give positive forms on
its modules or fusion products. We first work with modules already carrying such forms. An
intertwining operator is viewed as a charged field, with a charge module, a source module and a
target module. Its creation case has the vacuum V as source. This supplies a starting point for
the following sequence.
1. From C2 -cofiniteness to a net. In Section 3, a finite generating family controls all normal
   products, and positivity of certain zero modes gives an exponential starting bound. Two
   contraction inequalities first give polynomial growth and then, for Hermitian quasi-primary
   fields of weight d ≥ 2, the energy order d − 1 needed for locality. A sharper normal-product
   count and a Möbius estimate supply the second contraction.
2. Integrating modules with given positive forms. In Section 4, a fixed doubled annulus bounds local
   insertions by the norm of the inserted state. A Möbius factorization makes a round-annulus
   estimate uniform as the insertion approaches the circle. These estimates give polynomial
   bounds and bounded local regularizations for creation fields. Section 5 uses their total ranges
   to construct normal local-algebra actions on every already unitary module, with equality of the
   actual closed smeared fields.
3. Positive fusion for unitary inputs. Creation operators give positive Gram matrices in the
   vacuum local algebra. The normal representations just constructed transfer those matrices to
   any unitary source module. In Section 6, rigidity supplies a nondegenerate invariant form on the
   algebraic fusion product, and positivity of polynomial moments separates its conformal-energy
   spaces. This proves positivity of the canonical fusion form. The resulting charged-field bounds
   then apply to every intertwining operator between unitary modules.
4. Unitarizing all simple modules. Fix a simple module P , with graded contragredient P ∨ , set
   D = V ⊗ V , and let U be its flip-fixed subalgebra. The transposition-twisted D-module T
   obtained from the vacuum V is unitary over U . In Section 7, the permutation product detects
   every simple constituent of R = P ⊗ P ∨ , restricted to U , inside the positive fusion product
   T ⊠U T ∨ . Thus R has a positive U -invariant form. The action of the anti-invariant part of D
   produces a second such form; their sum is invariant under all of D. Restriction to a copy of P
   gives the required positive V -invariant form.
5. From a tensor embedding to all sectors. Section 8 now verifies Gui’s hypotheses for the CWX
   braided unitary tensor embedding, so every module gives a finite-index sector. Section 9 starts

                                                 5
    with an arbitrary irreducible locally normal net representation, without an index or finite-energy-
    multiplicity assumption. Its rotation spectrum is discrete. Henriques–Tener reconstruction,
    including [20, Remark 9.8], and algebraic regularity produce an ordinary simple module inside
    it; a reducing-projection argument identifies the whole sector with that module. The finite
    list of sectors, the split property and the Longo–Xu dichotomy give complete rationality.
    Decomposition into irreducibles then proves the equivalence for all additive finite-index objects.

   Optimal energy order is needed for ordinary fields to generate a local net. For charged fields,
polynomial bounds combined with bounded annulus regularization suffice.


2     Algebraic and analytic conventions
Throughout, V has the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1. We collect the algebraic inputs that do not
require positivity of all modules, and fix the operator conventions used in the estimates.

2.1    Modules, duality and fusion
By the regularity theorem of Abe–Buhl–Dong [1, Theorem 4.5], every weak V -module is an
algebraic direct sum of ordinary simple modules. There are finitely many simple isomorphism
classes. Their lowest conformal weights are rational [10, Theorem 11.3]. These weights are not
assumed nonnegative before a positive invariant form is known. In the grading-restricted category,
each simple occurs with finite multiplicity: infinitely many copies would give an infinite-dimensional
space at its lowest weight. Thus the categories used here have finite length.
    The Huang–Lepowsky tensor theory supplies the fusion product M ⊠ N and its universal
intertwining operator. For a target K, postcomposition identifies

sums over intermediate conformal-energy projections [22, Theorems 3.5 and 3.7, Remark 3.8].

hypotheses [23, Theorems 1.1, 3.8 and 4.6]. In particular the graded contragredient M ∨ is a
categorical dual. No positive form on M is needed for these algebraic assertions.
    We use ordinary, nonlogarithmic intertwining operators; the modules under consideration are
semisimple with diagonalizable L0 . Their skew-symmetry and contragredient transformations are

    All Hermitian inner products in the paper are linear in their first variable. A unitary module is
a finite orthogonal sum of unitary simples: the grade-wise orthogonal complement of a submodule
is invariant by the mode adjoint identities. A homomorphism between finite-length unitary
modules extends to a bounded operator on their completions, and its adjoint is again a module
homomorphism. To see the boundedness, decompose into simple types. On an irreducible type
invariant positive forms differ by a positive scalar, by Schur’s lemma; the remaining maps act on
finite-dimensional multiplicity spaces.
    The spectrum of L0 on a unitary module is nonnegative. At a lowest vector of weight h, the
relation [L1 , L−1 ] = 2L0 gives ∥L−1 x∥2 = 2h∥x∥2 . Every weight in that simple module is h + n for
an integer n ≥ 0; finite orthogonal sums give the general case. Constants in our estimates may
depend on the fixed module; uniformity over all modules is unnecessary.

    Polynomial mode bounds imply that smooth smearings and their formal adjoints map smooth
vectors continuously to smooth vectors. Indeed Fourier coefficients of a smooth test decay faster
than every power, and commuting (1 + L0 )k across a mode costs only a polynomial in its energy
shift. Finite-energy truncations converge in all these seminorms. Consequently the closures of a
polynomially bounded smear from finite-energy vectors and from smooth vectors agree. We will
repeatedly use this explicit core.
    For a charged field, smearing is performed on an interval with a chosen angular lift; its physical
normalization is z d Y(a, z). Fractional powers use the branch specified by that lift. Changing the
normalization by a smooth nowhere-zero phase merely changes the test function. At a reflected
coordinate the conjugate branch is always used.
    Positive-energy unitary Virasoro representations integrate to the centrally extended covering
group of circle diffeomorphisms and satisfy the usual linear energy bounds [5, Proposition 3.2,

Remark 3.3 and Theorem 3.4]; see also [31, Theorem 5.2.1 and Proposition 5.3.1]. The resulting
Möbius and diffeomorphism operators preserve the smooth space. We use their fixed conformal
normalization; in particular the Virasoro relation [L1 , L−1 ] = 2L0 fixes the additive constant in
L0 . Annulus actions and their Sobolev properties will be introduced only after the local net has
been constructed.
    For a closed densely defined operator T , its graph is a closed subspace of the direct sum of
its source and target Hilbert spaces. The entries of the orthogonal graph projection are bounded
operators. Graph projections allow us to state precisely that a normal representation carries one
closed field to another, or that a bounded projection reduces a closed field. Agreement on a dense
set alone will not be used as a substitute for agreement of closed graphs.

2.4    The scalar case
If V = CΩ, the conformal vector is zero, every grading-restricted module is finite dimensional in
degree zero, and all fields are constant scalar fields. The net on the one-dimensional vacuum space
is the scalar net. It is irreducible, split and strongly additive, and its two-interval index is one. Its
additive dualizable representation category is the category of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.
Creation, fusion and CWX integration are the usual tensor product over C, with the flip braiding.
Thus Theorem 1.1 holds in this case.
    We assume V ̸= CΩ henceforth. Its central charge is positive: ∥ν∥2 = c/2, so c = 0 would
force ν = 0, and then CFT type would force V = V0 = CΩ. The nontriviality assumption is used
in the flip-orbifold argument and in the usual conformal-net sector theory.

3      Energy bounds and strong locality
Our first analytic task is to construct the vacuum net. We do this without assuming that any
module has already been integrated to a net representation. The argument has two stages. A
finite set of quasi-primary generators first gives polynomial energy bounds of unspecified order. A
second estimate improves the order to the conformal weight minus one, which is the order needed
for the strong-locality criterion.

Theorem 3.1. Let V be a simple unitary strongly rational vertex operator algebra, and let W be
a grading-restricted unitary V -module. Every vertex operator on W satisfies polynomial energy
bounds. More precisely, if a ∈ Vd is quasi-primary, d ≥ 2, and (−1)d Θa = a, then there are
constants Ca > 0 and pa ∈ Z≥0 such that

                  ∥a[m]ξ∥ ≤ Ca (1 + |m|)pa ∥(1 + L0 )d−1 ξ∥,                m ∈ Z,       ξ ∈ W.        (3.1)

Weight-one fields and the Virasoro field satisfy energy bounds of at most linear order. The constants
may depend on V , W , and a.

    The scalar VOA satisfies the theorem directly. We assume for the proof that V ̸= CΩ, and fix
W . Since W is a finite orthogonal sum of ordinary unitary modules, it suffices to establish all
estimates on those summands and take the maximum of the finitely many constants. Write W (h)
for the energy-h subspace, with W (h) = 0 when h is absent. These spaces are finite dimensional
and mutually orthogonal. Unitarity of the positive-energy Virasoro representation gives h ≥ 0.
We use the spectral projections
