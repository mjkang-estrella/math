                                               Abstract
         We construct a finite three-electron Coulomb molecule whose spin-summed ground-state
     density cannot be reproduced by any ground-state ensemble of noninteracting electrons in a
     single real, spin-independent local potential in L3/2 (R3 ) + L∞ (R3 ). The molecule has two
     equal positive integer nuclear charges, specified by an exact finite nonnumerical formula.


1    Introduction
The density-functional variational viewpoint originates in the work of Hohenberg and Kohn [9].
Uniqueness of a density–potential mapping and existence of a representing potential are separate
questions; see the review [15] for their precise distinctions. Kohn–Sham density functional
theory seeks to reproduce the density of an interacting electronic ground state by noninteracting
electrons in a common local potential [11]. The constrained-search formulations of Levy and Lieb
distinguish the existence of states with a prescribed density from the existence of a potential
for which those states minimize the energy [13, 14]. Allowing ensembles enlarges the latter
class: several degenerate noninteracting ground states may be mixed to obtain a density that
is unavailable from one determinant. Ullrich and Kohn make this distinction explicit in their
treatment of singlet ground-state ensembles [20]. Thus a failure of a particular orbital ansatz
does not settle the ensemble representability question.

have a noninteracting ground-state ensemble representation in V. No restriction is placed on
the degeneracy of a putative representing ground space or on the complex spinful states in

spin-dependent potentials, or nonlocal one-particle operators.

exchange–correlation functional. More precisely, Corollary 8.1 excludes a potential in V rep-

domain.

1.1   Relation to earlier work
Explicit prescribed densities can already fail noninteracting v-representability in V. Wagner and
collaborators discuss a positive two-electron density with finite kinetic energy whose interior
cusp forces its inverse potential outside this class [21, Section IV.G, Eq. (36)]. Their example is
specified directly, without a construction as a finite Coulomb ground-state density.
    Trushin, Erhard, and Görling report numerical indications of failures of ground-state nonin-
teracting representability from atomic density inversions [19]. Open-shell inversion and symmetry
restrictions are further investigated in [4]. The result here is an analytic exclusion for every
ground-state ensemble of a specified finite Coulomb system, in the potential class V.
    Existence results for other settings must be distinguished from this question. The theorem
of Chayes, Chayes, and Ruskai concerns quantum lattice systems with a fixed bounded kinetic-
plus-interaction operator [2]. Corso proves a rigorous Kohn–Sham formulation for spinless
fermions on a bounded one-dimensional interval with generalized Neumann boundary conditions,
allowing distributional potentials [3]. On a one-dimensional torus, Sutter and collaborators
prove ensemble representability of strictly positive H 1 densities of prescribed particle number
for spinful electrons, again allowing distributional potentials [18]. These results do not supply a
representability theorem for three-dimensional Coulomb densities in V.
    The asymptotic mechanism behind our construction was investigated by Gori-Giorgi, Gál,
and Baerends [8]. When the leading removal amplitude has a nodal plane, coupling to other
ionic channels can give the density the same exponential decay rate on and off that plane, with
an additional polynomial suppression on the plane. Their analysis identifies the role of the
angular part of the Laplacian and the resulting difficulties for a regular Kohn–Sham potential.
Gori-Giorgi and Baerends subsequently studied the effective equation for the square root of
the density and exact harmonically confined examples [7]. That effective equation is distinct
from the common local potential determined below by two orbitals and an ensemble kinetic
certificate. Here we prove the necessary estimates for a finite Coulomb system, and supply two
further ingredients: a certificate valid against all ground-state ensembles and an obstruction to
the entire sum space V.
