                                                Abstract
           For separable nuclear C ∗ -algebras with no nonzero elementary ideal subquotients, finite
       nuclear dimension is equivalent to Jiang–Su stability. More generally, dimnuc (A0 ⊗ Z) ≤ 1
       for every separable nuclear A0 . This proves Robert and Tikuisis’s Conjecture (C1) and
       the nuclear-dimension equivalence in the nonsimple Toms–Winter regularity question.


1     Introduction
Nuclear dimension and Jiang–Su absorption describe two forms of regularity in a nuclear
C ∗ -algebra. Nuclear dimension counts the order-zero pieces needed in finite-dimensional
completely positive approximations; an order-zero map preserves orthogonality of positive
elements. Jiang–Su stability means an isomorphism A ∼   = A ⊗ Z, where Z is the algebra intro-
duced by Jiang and Su [JS]. The first condition gives a bound on approximation complexity,
while the second expresses invariance under tensoring with a fixed infinite-dimensional algebra.
Their relationship connects noncommutative dimension with the structure and classification
of nuclear C ∗ -algebras.
     The obstruction to passing from finite dimension to absorption is already visible in a
matrix algebra: it has nuclear dimension zero but does not absorb Z. The relevant exclusion
must apply throughout the ideal structure. A C ∗ -algebra A is nowhere scattered if no quotient
I/J, for closed two-sided ideals J ⊆ I ⊆ A, is a nonzero elementary algebra K(H). This is
equivalent to the absence of finite-dimensional irreducible representations in every hereditary
subalgebra [TV, Theorem 3.1].

Theorem 1.1 (Nonsimple nuclear regularity). Let A be a separable nuclear complex C ∗ -
algebra with no nonzero elementary ideal subquotients. The following conditions are equivalent:
    1. dimnuc (A) < ∞;
    2. A ∼
         = A ⊗ Z;
    3. dimnuc (A) ≤ 1.

   The implication from finite nuclear dimension to absorption proves Conjecture (C1) of
Robert and Tikuisis [RT]. Together the two implications establish the nuclear-dimension
equivalence in the nonsimple Toms–Winter regularity question of Schafhauser–Tikuisis–White
[STW, Section 22, Problem LXXV]. Here and throughout, unadorned tensor products are
spatial. The maximal tensor products used in the independent splitting theorem below carry
an explicit subscript.
   The companion [OAI-Cu, Corollaries 1.3 and 1.4] adds Cuntz-semigroup criteria to the
main equivalence.

    The bound is optimal uniformly over this class. Nuclear dimension zero for a separable

dimensional with no projections other than 0 and 1. Thus dimnuc (Z) = 1.
    The absorption implication uses a separate theorem. A positive element is full if it
generates the whole ambient algebra as a closed two-sided ideal, and a character is a nonzero
∗-homomorphism to C.

Theorem 1.4 (Full signs in a finite maximal tensor power). For every nonzero unital complex
C ∗ -algebra D with no characters, there is a finite integer n ≥ 1 such that D⊗max n contains
two full orthogonal positive elements.

     No separability or nuclearity is assumed in this theorem. Its finite conclusion answers
affirmatively Kirchberg and Rørdam’s Question 6.2 [KR], which asks for this splitting property
in the infinite maximal tensor power. The passage to the infinite power is by its canonical
unital inclusion, which preserves fullness and orthogonality.
     The companion [OAI, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2] obtains another finite endpoint under the
same hypotheses on D: for some m ≥ 1, there is a unital ∗-homomorphism from the prime
dimension-drop algebra I(2, 3) to D⊗max m . For every free ultrafilter ω on N, it also proves
that a nonzero unital separable complex C ∗ -algebra A is Z-stable exactly when its norm
central-sequence algebra Aω ∩ A′ has no characters. The proof below uses only the full-sign
endpoint of Theorem 1.4.

1.1   Background and the two obstacles
Winter and Zacharias introduced nuclear dimension as a noncommutative covering dimension
and established its basic permanence properties [WZ2]. Their order-zero structure theorem
[WZ1] makes a colour into a homomorphism multiplied by a commuting positive weight.
Toms and Winter’s theory of strongly self-absorbing algebras [TW] explains why absorption
can supply commuting tensor factors without changing the isomorphism class. In the
simple setting, Matui–Sato proved that a unital separable simple nuclear quasidiagonal
algebra with a unique trace has decomposition rank at most one after tensoring with the
universal UHF algebra [MS, Theorem 4.2]. Decomposition rank requires the same order-
zero decomposition as nuclear dimension, together with contractivity of the entire return
map; its bounds therefore also bound nuclear dimension. Sato–White–Winter established
the bound three for separable unital simple nuclear Z-stable algebras with a unique trace


                                              2
[SWW, Theorem B]. The two-coloured classification work of Bosa–Brown–Sato–Tikuisis–
White–Winter obtained nuclear dimension one for non-AF separable unital simple nuclear
Z-stable algebras whose trace simplex is nonempty and Bauer [BBSTWW, Theorem F].
Castillejos–Evington–Tikuisis–White–Winter removed the trace-boundary restriction in the
unital case [CETWW, Theorem B], and Castillejos–Evington treated the nonunital case [CE,
Theorem A].
    Nonsimple antecedents reduce dimension in specific structural classes. Tikuisis–Winter
proved decomposition rank at most two after Z-stabilization for algebras locally approximable
by hereditary subalgebras of C(X, K), with X compact Hausdorff [TiW, Theorem 4.1]. Elliott–
Niu–Santiago–Tikuisis extended the bound two to locally subhomogeneous Z-stable algebras
[ENST, Theorem A]. In the purely infinite direction, Szabó proved dimnuc (A ⊗ O∞ ) ≤ 3
for every separable nuclear A [Sza, Theorem 2.3]. Bosa–Gabe–Sims–White then established
nuclear dimension at most one for separable nuclear O∞ -stable algebras [BGSW, Theorem A].
Their homomorphism theorem gives the same bound for a nuclear O∞ -stable homomorphism
from a separable exact domain to an arbitrary target [BGSW, Theorem C].
    The nonsimple dimension problem has an additional difficulty: a trace may be bounded
on one ideal and infinite outside it, and stably finite and purely infinite ideal quotients may
coexist. The appropriate trace cone therefore consists of all lower semicontinuous extended
traces, as developed by Elliott–Robert–Santiago [ERS]. Our estimates are relative to the
support rank of a spectral cut generating the ideal being tested. This rank measures the
trace of the support, rather than the trace value of the cut itself. When it is finite, a relative
estimate controls the error; rank zero forces the error rank to vanish. When it is infinite, a
separate ideal-containment argument forces the comparison target to have infinite rank. The
construction begins with the convex order-zero approximations of Hirshberg–Kirchberg–White
[HKW], in the refinement of Brown–Carrión–White with asymptotically order-zero incoming
maps [BCW]. It localizes them inside hereditary corners, and rounds their convex weights to
obtain one matrix-valued order-zero return map.
    The comparison stage uses two distinct forms of regularity. At each coordinate, exactness
and Z-stability turn a uniform extended-rank gap into Cuntz comparison, using Rørdam’s
almost-unperforation theorem [Rør] and Elliott–Robert–Santiago’s comparison criterion [ERS,
Proposition 6.2]. In the supported relative commutant of the ultrapower, we instead use
abstract dimension functions. Gabe’s order-zero formulation [Gabe] of the Kirchberg–Rørdam
cone criterion [KR05] supplies finite compression sums from pointwise ideal containment.
Packing these sums yields comparisons inside that commutant. Fullness, divisibility in a full
corner, and almost unperforation verify a further hereditary-subalgebra condition. The unitary-
equivalence criterion of Bosa–Brown–Sato–Tikuisis–White–Winter [BBSTWW], which adapts
Robert–Santiago’s positive-element classification method [RS], turns the comparisons into
two weighted conjugacies.
    For the reverse implication, Winter proved that a separable simple unital nonelementary
algebra of finite nuclear dimension absorbs Z [Win, Corollary 6.3]. Tikuisis extended this to
separable algebras of finite nuclear dimension with finitely many ideals and no elementary
quotient of an ideal [Tik, Corollary 8.7]. Robert–Tikuisis obtained further nonsimple cases
under restrictions on the primitive ideal space and subquotients [RT, Corollaries 7.11 and 7.16],
and reduced absorption for separable algebras of finite nuclear dimension to the existence
of two full orthogonal positive elements in the central sequence algebra [RT, Theorem 1.2].
Winter–Zacharias’s finite covering-number bound [WZ2, Proposition 4.3] rules out characters
in that algebra under the nowhere-scattered hypothesis. The remaining step is precisely a
full splitting construction. Kirchberg and Rørdam isolated the character and tensor-splitting
questions in [KR]. Our finite tensor construction supplies the needed splitting by combining
a uniform Gaussian sign estimate with pure-state excision. The latter is the method of


                                                3
Akemann–Anderson–Pedersen [AAP]; we include its short proof using Kadison transitivity
[Kad].
    Recent Cuntz-semigroup approaches provide a complementary perspective. Antoine–
Perera–Thiel–Vilalta relate finite nuclear dimension and the Global Glimm Property to
comparison and divisibility [APTV, Theorem 6.5]; Perera–Thiel–Vilalta characterize absorp-
tion through the corresponding regularity of central sequence algebras [PTV, Theorem 3.6].
Our reverse implication instead uses the finite covering bound and the full-sign theorem to
meet the Robert–Tikuisis criterion directly.

1.2   Proof overview
The two implications are independent until the final assembly. Figure 1 records their interfaces.
    For the dimension bound, we first work with a unital separable nuclear A, without
absorption. In Section 3, quasicentral ideal cuts give finitely many families of hereditary
pieces. The pieces are orthogonal within each fixed threshold family, and averaging the family
compressions retains the prescribed tests. We fix the pieces and their comparison bounds
by finite sums of ideal generators before choosing inner approximation errors. This ordering
turns norm errors into bounds relative to the support ranks of those generators, uniformly
over all traces for which the ranks are finite.
    Section 4 places the matrix model in A ⊗ Z1 ⊗ Z2 ⊗ Z3 . The first Z factor carries the
model. In the norm ultrapower, a positive contraction q identifies a part on which the model
agrees with the original inclusion. The second factor supplies arbitrarily many orthogonal
copies of a norm-one positive contraction. Relative trace estimates compare the error part
with this common part, and a spare spectral cut bounds the comparison witnesses before
they pass to the ultrapower.
    Section 5 constructs a supporting map and uses approximate domination to pack the
error into the common part. This controls dimension functions in a supported relative
commutant without assuming that commutant exact. A full corner, its divisibility, and almost
unperforation give the hereditary-subalgebra condition needed for unitary equivalence. The
constant third factor supplies a full-spectrum positive contraction k. Functional-calculus
comparison gives unitary conjugacies for the two weights k and 1 − k. Their sum gives exactly
two colours for a corner inclusion. In Section 6, stabilization and hereditary permanence
preserve those two colours, and unitization handles the nonunital algebra. This proves
Theorem 1.3.
    For absorption, Section 7 constructs a real tensor that takes both signs on every product
of sufficiently separated pairs of unit vectors. A Hamming packing provides exponentially
many weakly correlated Gaussian evaluations. Their failure probability decays fast enough
to control a net of all admissible configurations. In Section 8, a finite commutator witness
converts the absence of characters into uniform separation of pure-state expectation vectors.
Excision transfers the tensor evaluations to every irreducible representation of the maximal
tensor product. The positive and negative parts of the resulting selfadjoint element are
therefore both full.
    Finally, Section 9 uses the norm central sequence algebra F (A), with its annihilator
quotient in the nonunital case. Finite covering excludes characters; a separable characterless
subalgebra containing 1F (A) has a finite tensor splitting by Theorem 1.4. Commuting
reindexed copies give a unital homomorphism from that tensor power into F (A). Fullness
survives this map, so Robert–Tikuisis’s criterion gives absorption and completes Theorem 1.1.

4     The common part and localized trace comparison
The matrix model will now be placed in a Jiang–Su tensor factor by an order-zero map. Its
expectation is still asymptotically the identity. This produces a common part of the model
and the original inclusion. The remaining part is small in a sense that can be compared
uniformly over all lower semicontinuous extended traces, including traces that are infinite
away from an ideal.
