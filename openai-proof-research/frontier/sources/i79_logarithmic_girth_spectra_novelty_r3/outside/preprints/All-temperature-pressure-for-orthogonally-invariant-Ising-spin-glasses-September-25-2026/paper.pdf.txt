All-temperature pressure for orthogonally invariant Ising spin glasses
                                                     OpenAI

                                          September 25, 2026


                                                    Abstract
         We determine the limiting pressure of an Ising spin glass with Haar orthogonal eigenvectors,
      a compact limiting spectral law, and no asymptotic outliers, at every fixed temperature. The
      pressure converges in expectation and almost surely to a variational formula. We also give
      the limiting pressure with deterministic external fields whose empirical laws converge in first-
      moment transport distance, and derive a formula for the zero-field ground-state energy by taking
      temperature to zero.


1     Introduction
The eigenvalues of an interaction matrix describe its quadratic form on Euclidean space, but an
Ising spin system samples that form only on the vertices of a cube. Randomizing the eigenvectors
by Haar orthogonal conjugation leads to the problem of determining the thermodynamic pressure
from the limiting spectral distribution. We solve this problem for every fixed temperature when the
limiting spectral law is compactly supported and there are no asymptotic outliers. The formula
retains the hierarchy of replica overlaps familiar from the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model, while
the spectral law enters through its edge-extended R-transform. The rigorous pressure formulas in
[5, 12, 11] impose high-temperature conditions.

1.3   Context
For the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model [27], Parisi introduced a hierarchical order parameter [25].
Guerra established the associated variational upper bound for the pressure [15], and Talagrand
proved the matching formula [28]. The random-overlap-structure variational principle of Aizenman,
Sims, and Starr provides a complementary cavity framework [1].
    Replica analyses of the random orthogonal model and more general orthogonally invariant Ising
interactions developed spectral representations and replica-symmetric and one-step replica-symmetry-
breaking free-energy predictions [19, 9]. Bereyhi, Müller, and Schulz-Baldes developed formal
finite-step replica-symmetry-breaking formulas for rotationally invariant quadratic optimization
[4, Equations (114)–(115) and Appendix D]. Bhattacharya and Sen proved the zero-field pressure
formula in a high-temperature regime [5, Theorem 1.2]. Fan and Wu established a replica-symmetric
formula with external fields at sufficiently high temperature [12, Theorem 1.3], and Fan, Misiakiewicz,
Wang, and Wen obtained an explicit high-temperature criterion for a constant external field through
dynamical mean-field analysis [11, Corollary 2.8]. The rank-one spherical-integral theorem of
Guionnet and Maïda explains the spectral-edge extension in Equation (1.4) [16, Theorem 1.6 in the
cited arXiv version]. It determines an annealed spherical integral, rather than the quenched Ising
pressure. Theorem 1.1 treats every fixed temperature under Equation (1.2).

2    Overlap arrays and cascade calculus
The rotation and cavity arguments use limits of several overlap arrays at once. This section shows
that the Ghirlanda–Guerra identities reduce these limits to ordered scalar quantiles, and gives the
Gaussian integration rules for their finite-step approximations. We include proofs of the structural
facts needed below; they are instances of the ultrametricity and synchronization theory developed
in [23, 24]. The cascade construction and its marking calculus are closely related to [6].

possible sums. Applying these functions to the quantile of Q12 supplies the simultaneous quantiles
and all the stated conclusions.

2.2    Finite cascades and hierarchical marks
We use Ruelle’s Poisson cascade construction [26]. The conditional marking argument follows the
Poisson-rescaling calculus of Bolthausen and Sznitman [6, Appendix, Proposition A.2]; the proof
below states the fractional-moment conditions needed for the possibly unbounded marks used here.
     Proposition 2.3 identifies the order of the overlaps. We now construct the finite-step arrays
and describe how their marks change under a Gibbs weight. This calculation will apply both to
Gaussian linear fields and to the quadratic Gaussian factors in the cavity argument.
     Fix the interval weights w0 , . . . , wk−1 > 0 and endpoints 0 = ζ0 < ζ1 < · · · < ζk = 1. At every

parameters.

6     The cavity lower bound
The cavity comparison belongs to the variational approach of Aizenman, Sims, and Starr [1]. Here

8.3   A product of constrained blocks
For the lower bound, fix m as above and choose an integer n such that nγj , nγj (1 + mj )/2, and nρa

fresh within-group frames, perturbation comparison, and quadratic Gaussian transformation are

    First, each position within a regular block is exchangeable with the same position in every other

regular position is at most (N + n)/K. For fixed n this is bounded uniformly in N , and the finite
number of cavity coordinates consequently has the projection tightness required for the restricted
Gibbs tests. Second, averaging a cavity spin product over its n positions, against a bounded test of
the total overlap, equals the analogous average over all regular sites. The difference from the full
total overlap is bounded by twice the fraction of remainder sites. These observations give exactly
the tightness and overlap-test identities used in Lemma 6.2 and Proposition 6.6.
