         Exact asymptotic moduli in a Daugavet subspace of L1
                                                      OpenAI

                                                      Abstract
        For an infinite-dimensional real subspace of L1 whose unit ball is totally bounded in measure
     and whose norm has the Daugavet property, we compute the averaged midpoint and one-sided
     asymptotic moduli at every unit center. They are max{t/2, t − 1} and max{0, t − 2}, respectively.
     The same weak-neighborhood geometry excludes every equivalent asymptotically uniformly
     convex norm. A quantitative realization of the Kadets–Werner construction supplies a space
     with both hypotheses.


1    Introduction
How much can two-sided midpoint growth differ from one-sided asymptotic convexity? We answer
this quantitatively for certain real subspaces of L1 . The norm is fixed throughout the computation:
it is the inherited L1 norm. The obstruction to AUC renorming will require a separate argument
that applies to every equivalent norm.

unit sphere, and cof(X) for its closed linear subspaces of finite codimension. Given a norm N , a
vector x with N (x) = 1, and t > 0, set

The averaged midpoint modulus δbN (t) and the one-sided modulus δ N (t) are their respective infima
over unit centers. Thus a subspace of finite codimension is chosen for each center before the
perturbing direction is chosen. The norm N is asymptotically uniformly convex (AUC) if δ N (t) > 0
for every t > 0.
    For each fixed y, both the average and the maximum of N (x + ry) and N (x − ry) are even convex
functions of r. Thus unit directions give the same inner infimum in HN and in its maximum-based
counterpart. Let mN be the modulus obtained by replacing the average in HN by the maximum
and then taking the infimum over unit centers. It satisfies 12 mN (t) ≤ δbN (t) ≤ mN (t). Indeed, the
upper bound is immediate; for the lower bound, intersect a candidate subspace with the kernel of
a norm-one functional norming x. Both perturbed norms are then at least one, and the average
of their excesses is at least half their maximum excess. Taking the infima and suprema gives the
comparison. Positivity of the averaged modulus is therefore the usual asymptotic midpoint uniform
convexity (AMUC).

    Dilworth, Kutzarova, Randrianarivony, Revalski, and Zhivkov introduced AMUC and asked
whether AMUC and AUC are equivalent up to renorming [5, Definition 2.2 and the question in
Section 5]. Baudier answered this question negatively by proving AMUC for subspaces of L1
whose unit balls are relatively compact in measure and applying this to the Daugavet example
of Kadets and Werner [2, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1]. We calculate both moduli exactly under
these two geometric hypotheses. Baudier’s proof uses the L1 splitting identity of Brézis and Lieb [4,
Theorem 1] along almost-everywhere convergent subsequences. Our lower estimate instead uses a
finite net of pointwise truncations and the sign functionals attached to that net.
    Recall that a Banach space has the Daugavet property if ∥I + T ∥ = 1 + ∥T ∥ for every rank-one
bounded operator T on the space. For functions on a probability space, convergence in measure is
induced by the metric
                              d(f, g) = inf{a > 0 : P(|f − g| > a) < a}.                         (1)
Total boundedness for this metric means that every positive radius admits a finite covering by balls
of that radius. The following theorem combines this compactness assumption with the Daugavet
property.
Theorem 1.1. Let E be an infinite-dimensional closed real subspace of L1 (Ω, P), where P is a
probability measure. Suppose its closed unit ball is totally bounded for convergence in measure and
its inherited norm has the Daugavet property. Then every x ∈ SE and t > 0 satisfy

                    HE (x, t) = max{t/2, t − 1},           DE (x, t) = max{0, t − 2}.

Consequently these are also the formulas for δbE (t) and δ E (t). The space E admits no equivalent
AUC norm.
   In particular, the averaged modulus has linear growth near the origin, whereas the one-sided
modulus remains zero up to t = 2. Baudier also gives a universal linear lower bound for the maximum-
based modulus of the Kadets–Werner space [2, final paragraph]. Our refinement determines the
exact averaged and one-sided curves at every unit center under the two geometric hypotheses.

    The two assumptions enter different parts of the proof. In Section 2, pointwise clipping at height
|x|/t turns the measure-precompact ball into a norm-compact set. Finitely many sign functionals
then force at least half the L1 mass of every unit direction in their common kernel to remain outside
the clip, up to an arbitrarily small error. This gives the lower averaged bound. Section 3 develops
the other input: every weak neighborhood of a unit vector contains points almost at distance two
from it. This is the needed case of Shvydkoy’s weak-open characterization of the Daugavet property
[8, Lemma 3], obtained from the slice characterization of Kadets, Shvidkoy, Sirotkin, and Werner [7,
Lemma 2.2]. In Section 4, correcting a finite-dimensional quotient coordinate turns those almost
diametral differences into directions in any prescribed subspace of finite codimension, giving both
upper curves. Section 5 uses the same weak-neighborhood property at a near-maximizer of an
arbitrary equivalent norm to rule out AUC renorming. Section 6 then constructs an increasing
sequence of finite-dimensional spaces whose closed union has both required properties. At each step,
multiplying selected old unit vectors by independent positive variables creates new vectors almost at
distance two from every old unit vector, while averages of the new vectors recover the selected ones.
A uniform estimate places the entire enlarged unit ball close in measure to the old ball. Summable
errors preserve total boundedness through the limit, and the averaging property gives the Daugavet
slice condition.

6     Constructing the measure-precompact Daugavet space
The preceding results apply to a space with two rather different properties: its unit ball is small for
convergence in measure, while its slices contain vectors almost at distance two from any prescribed
unit vector. We now construct such a space. The construction is due to Kadets and Werner [6,
Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 2.5], who refine the method of Bourgain and Rosenthal [3]. We give all the
estimates needed for the finite-dimensional enlargement and its iteration. The explicit multipliers
below and the constants 10, 54, and 108 belong to the quantitative realization given here; the
enlargement and iteration scheme is the credited construction.
    The idea is to multiply an old unit vector by a positive random variable which has mean one
but is small with high probability. Each product has small L1 overlap with every old unit vector, so
the norm of their sum is almost two. Averaging sufficiently many products recovers the old vector
in norm. The main additional task is to control every vector in the new unit ball, which permits
the iteration to preserve precompactness in measure.

                              d(f, g) = inf{a > 0 : P(|f − g| > a) < a}.                           (4)
