           Bounded recovery for modular spectral averages

                                              Abstract
          We prove a bounded spectral recovery theorem for a von Neumann algebra with
      a faithful normal state whose centralizer consists only of scalars. A positive averaged
      squared norm for an arbitrary bounded Hilbert-space operator on shrinking modular
      spectral bands can be recovered on uniformly bounded algebra elements with shrinking
      spectral support. We apply this theorem to spectral intertwining rigidity and obtain an
      alternative proof of bicentralizer triviality for type III1 factors with separable predual.


1    Introduction
Spectral localization in the standard Hilbert space of a von Neumann algebra does not by
itself produce uniformly bounded elements of the algebra. This distinction matters when an
argument starts with spectral vectors but its asymptotic identities apply only to bounded
operator sequences. Our main analytical result, Theorem 4.1, provides a bounded recovery
principle for this situation.
    Let M be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state ϕ. Write Mϕ for the
fixed-point algebra of its modular automorphism group σ ϕ . We call ϕ ergodic when Mϕ = C1.
On the standard Hilbert space H = L2 (M ), put ξ = ϕ1/2 , D = log ∆ϕ , and Ut = eitD . For
an arbitrary fixed T ∈ B(H), consider unit vectors supported in spectral intervals of D
shrinking to a real number s. Theorem 4.1 shows that a positive limiting averaged value
of ∥T Ut h∥2 on these vectors can be recovered as a positive value of ∥T vξ∥2 on a sequence
of uniformly bounded v ∈ M . The recovered vectors remain in intervals shrinking to s,
with widths at most four times the original widths. The averaging uses a fixed translation-
invariant mean obtained from symmetric interval averages. The exact quantifiers and mean
are given in Theorems 3.4 and 4.1.
    The only structural assumption in this recovery theorem is Mϕ = C1; neither a bicen-
tralizer condition nor a second action is required. This assumption supplies a broad class of
applications: Marrakchi and Vaes proved that ergodic faithful normal states form a dense Gδ
in the normal state space of every type III1 factor with separable predual [14, Theorem A].

The bicentralizer BC(M, ϕ) consists of those a ∈ M for which [a, wn ] → 0 strongly whenever

asserts that this algebra is scalar for every type III1 factor with separable predual. Connes
developed the problem in his classification program for injective factors [3, 4]. Haagerup
proved the amenable case, completing the uniqueness theorem for the injective type III1
factor [5].

    Houdayer and Isono proved that a nonscalar bicentralizer of a separable type III1 fac-
tor is itself a type III1 factor and equals its own bicentralizer for the restricted state [8,
Proposition 3.4 and Theorem 3.5]. Ando, Haagerup, Houdayer, and Marrakchi established
the canonical relative bicentralizer flow [1, Theorem A], following the flow construction of
Haagerup and its independent discovery by Marrakchi. Marrakchi proved that every non-
identity time of the absolute flow is ergodic, without a fullness assumption [11, Theorem D].
His subsequent work develops the binormal-state and relative-core machinery [12, 13].

The absolute application and the recent announcement. Houdayer and Marrakchi
have announced triviality of the bicentralizer for every type III1 factor admitting a faithful
normal state, with no separability condition in the theorem statement [9, Theorem G]. The
application proved here is an alternative proof of the separable-predual instance of that
conclusion.
Theorem 1.1. Let M be a type III1 factor with separable predual and let ϕ be any faithful
normal state on M . Then
                                  BC(M, ϕ) = C1.
    By [8, Corollary 3.6], Theorem 1.1 also implies that every type III1 factor with separable
predual admits a maximal abelian subalgebra with a faithful normal conditional expectation.
This is an existence statement for the factor; the expectation need not preserve the scalar-
centralizer state used in bounded recovery.
    The independent analytical contribution is the bounded recovery theorem, which applies
to arbitrary T without assuming a bicentralizer intertwining law. Houdayer and Marrakchi
obtain the same symmetric spectral relation used below through multiplication isometries
and a singular KMS state on the continuous core [9, Section 8.4, Propositions 8.8–8.10].
Here modular averaging and bounded recovery lead to that relation through two interval
estimates.

The rigidity statement. A continuous action on M means a u-continuous action on its
automorphism group. Bounded recovery gives the following spectral proof of rigidity.
Theorem 1.2 (Spectral intertwining rigidity). Let M be a von Neumann algebra with a
faithful normal state ϕ such that Mϕ = C1. Let b : R ↷ M be a continuous ϕ-preserving
action. Suppose that, for every s ∈ R, every uniformly bounded sequence (wn ) in M , and
every a ∈ M ,
             ∥ϕwn − es wn ϕ∥M∗ −→ 0       =⇒       wn a − bs (a)wn −→ 0   strongly.        (1.1)
Then bs = idM for every s ∈ R.

    The conclusion also follows from the announced absolute theorem. Indeed, at s = 0,
hypothesis (1.1) implies BC(M, ϕ) = M . If M ̸= C1, its scalar centralizer makes it a type
III1 factor by [14, Lemma 2.1]; the announced theorem would then force M = C1. Our
proof instead derives Theorem 1.2 directly from bounded recovery and uses it to prove
Theorem 1.1. For that application to a self-bicentralizing factor, the modular centralizer is
scalar, the bicentralizer flow satisfies (1.1), and that flow is ergodic. A trivial ergodic action
has scalar underlying algebra.

Recovery and the spectral argument. The recovery proof combines modular averaging
with the fourth-moment and polar-cutoff devices of Haagerup and Musat [6, Equation (2.10),
Lemma 2.6, and Remark 2.8]. The strategy of recovering bounded witnesses by randomiza-
tion, fourth-moment control, and truncation appears in the tracial spectral-gap argument
of Ioana and Vaes [10, pp. 127–130]. Related fourth-moment control underlies Marrakchi’s
binormal-state implementation [12, Section 2, Claim 2.2]. Here modular orbit averages
supply the moment bounds, and a final Fourier filter restores the shrinking spectral bands.
The averaging time is chosen separately for each initial bounded representative of a spectral
vector; these representatives need not be uniformly bounded. Norm convergence in the
predual controls a varying second-moment operator, and independent circle phases give a
uniform fourth-moment bound. One fixed polar truncation threshold then suffices. Fourier
localization restores the spectral support without increasing operator norms by a factor
depending on the width of the spectral interval. The Fourier operations belong to the
standard spectral-subspace framework for automorphism groups [2]; the bounds needed here
are proved explicitly.
    For the spectral application, implement the state-preserving action by Vs (aξ) = bs (a)ξ
and write Vs = eisQ . These unitaries commute with Ut , so (D, Q) has a joint spectral
support S ⊂ R2 . The recovered bounded sequences let (1.1) force averaged intertwining
defects to vanish on bands shrinking to a fixed modular energy. A spectral partition then
gives an interval estimate without requiring a rate uniform in that energy. Applying this
estimate twice, with the operators interchanged and the time reversed, gives

This symmetric relation is the final rigidity mechanism. It forces the joint support to be
countable or to lie on a coordinate axis. Scalarity of the modular centralizer excludes
nonzero modular eigenvalues, by the bounded-eigenoperator extraction and KMS argument
of [14, Lemma 2.1], leaving only the axis on which the flow generator vanishes.

2    Bicentralizers and the reduction
We recall the definition to fix both the topology and the quantifiers. For a faithful normal
state ϕ, put ∥x∥ϕ = ϕ(x∗ x)1/2 . The bicentralizer consists of those a ∈ M for which, for
every ε > 0, there is δ > 0 such that

                  u ∈ U (M ),   ∥uϕ − ϕu∥M∗ < δ       =⇒      ∥u∗ au − a∥ϕ < ε.

This is Haagerup’s unitary formulation of the usual bicentralizer [5, proof of Lemma 1.2].
Equivalently, its elements asymptotically commute strongly with every uniformly bounded

sequence that asymptotically centralizes ϕ in predual norm. We use established structural
results in this equivalent form.

Proposition 2.1 (Established bicentralizer results). Let M be a type III1 factor with
separable predual and ϕ a faithful normal state.

 (i) If B = BC(M, ϕ) is nonscalar, then B is a type III1 subfactor with a normal ϕ-preserving
     conditional expectation, and
                                       BC(B, ϕ|B ) = B.

(ii) There is a continuous ϕ-preserving action β ϕ : R∗+ ↷ BC(M, ϕ) characterized by

              ∥wn ϕ − λϕwn ∥M∗ −→ 0           =⇒       wn a − βλϕ (a)wn −→ 0   ∗ -strongly

     for every λ > 0, every uniformly bounded sequence (wn ) in M , and every a ∈ BC(M, ϕ).

(iii) The fixed-point algebra of β ϕ is C1.

Sources and hypotheses. The restricted-state identity in (i) is [8, Proposition 3.4]; the type
dichotomy and expectation are [8, Theorem 3.5]. The bicentralizer is globally invariant
under the modular group by [8, Proposition 3.3], so its canonical expectation preserves ϕ.
These results impose no amenability or fullness hypothesis.
    For (ii), apply [1, Theorem A(ii)] to the identity inclusion M ⊂ M . Continuity is
part of the flow construction. The expectation-preservation identity in [1, Theorem A(iv)]
specializes to preservation of ϕ, because the relative commutant is C1.
    Finally, [11, Theorem D] states, for any σ-finite type III1 factor, that every βλϕ with
λ ̸= 1 is ergodic. This is stronger than (iii). Fullness is not assumed in this Theorem.

Proof of Theorem 1.1, assuming Theorem 1.2. Suppose that BC(M, ϕ) is nonscalar. Re-
place (M, ϕ) by (B, ϕ|B ) using Theorem 2.1(i). The restricted state is faithful and normal.
The predual is still separable: if E : M → B is the normal expectation, B∗ ∋ ρ 7→ ρ◦E ∈ M∗
is an isometric embedding. We may therefore assume

                                BC(M, ϕ) = M,              M ̸= C1.

    Every unitary u ∈ Mϕ centralizes ϕ exactly. The unitary definition of the bicentralizer
gives u∗ au = a for every a ∈ M . Since unitaries span Mϕ , and M is a factor, it follows that
Mϕ = C1.
    Set bs = βeϕ−s . The premise of (1.1) is equivalent to

                                  ∥wn ϕ − e−s ϕwn ∥M∗ −→ 0.

Theorem 2.1(ii) therefore gives exactly (1.1), and also continuity and state preservation. By
Theorem 1.2, b is trivial. By Theorem 2.1(iii), it is ergodic. Hence M = C1, contradicting
the reduction. The contradiction applies to every original pair (M, ϕ).

imply a predual norm estimate, and modular averaging must control the moments of an
operator even when its norm is large. We establish these facts here. The two norm limits in
Theorem 3.3 will control different terms in the fourth-moment expansion in Section 4.

Proof of Theorem 1.2. By Theorem 5.3, the symmetric bilinear form

                                   b((r, p), (q, ℓ)) = rℓ + qp

                                          αβt2 ∈ πZ.
