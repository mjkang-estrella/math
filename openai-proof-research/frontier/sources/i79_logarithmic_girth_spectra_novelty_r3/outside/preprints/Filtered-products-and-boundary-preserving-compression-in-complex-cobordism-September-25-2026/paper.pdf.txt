                                              Abstract
          We prove that, in sufficiently large smash powers, an M U -null restriction to a
      finite pointed subcomplex becomes stably null on the entire union of products with a
      fixed positive proportion of restricted factors. This coherent vanishing theorem yields
      boundary-preserving compression of cube-valued maps on every compact metrizable
      input space. When the torus slot bundle embeds continuously into a trivial bundle of
      rank less than twice the source rank, the compression places a positive proportion of
      slots on their boundaries in arbitrarily large powers and fixes every original boundary
      slot exactly. An example at equality shows that the strict rank inequality cannot be
      removed.


1     Introduction
We study when a map into a product of cubes can be moved onto many coordinate boundaries
while retaining every boundary value already present. The input spaces may have arbitrarily
large, or infinite, covering dimension. The obstruction we use is a relative point class in
complex cobordism, tested against a family of vectors supported in only a few cube factors.
Our main results convert the vanishing of this class into compression on arbitrarily large
Cartesian powers.
    The proof has two coherence requirements. A cobordism-null map on one subcomplex
must give a single stable nullhomotopy on a union of products, including all their intersections.
Subsequently, stable avoidance data on small coordinate boxes must agree on adjacent
simplices. We prove both compatibility statements explicitly. A padding construction then
removes the dimension restriction needed to turn stable avoidance into an actual map.

1.1   Coherent products
Write S for the sphere spectrum and M U for the complex cobordism spectrum. A based
map is stably null if its suspension spectrum is nullhomotopic. The unit S → M U sends
a stable cohomotopy class to its complex-cobordism class. Our first result concerns the
difference between vanishing on a subcomplex and vanishing on an entire family of products.
Theorem 1.1 (Filtered products). Let G ⊂ E be a finite pointed CW pair, let d ≥ 0 be an
integer, and let v : E → S d be a based map. Suppose that the composite
                                               Σ∞ v
                            Σ∞ G −→ Σ∞ E −−−→ Σd S −→ Σd M U
is nullhomotopic. For ℓ ≥ 1, let Uℓ ⊂ E ∧ℓ be the union of the subcomplexes in which at least
⌈ℓ/2⌉ factors are required to belong to G. Then, for every sufficiently large ℓ, the restriction
                                       v ∧ℓ |Uℓ : Uℓ −→ S dℓ
is stably nullhomotopic.

     The conclusion concerns the union itself. It therefore supplies a nullhomotopy that can
be pulled back along any map into that union. The fraction 1/2 may be replaced by any
fixed positive fraction; see Remark 2.6. The proof also gives a more general compatible
lift for an arbitrary commutative ring spectrum, before invoking a vanishing theorem for
M U . Its target is the total fiber of a cube of unit maps. To produce one null cone over the
punctured cube, we use simplex parameters that collapse the entire constrained union at
once; the number of restricted factors determines how many simplex degrees collapse.

1.3    Background and the proof mechanism
The nilpotence theorem of Devinatz–Hopkins–Smith [4] provides the stable-homotopy
background for detecting smash-nilpotence by complex cobordism. Here we need the
quantitative connective form developed by Mathew [10, Section 3.4] and recorded for BP in
Burklund’s appendix [3, Appendix B]. Its cutoff is sublinear in the degree and uniform over
all connective spectra at a fixed prime. This uniform statement, together with explicit upper
cell bounds, converts a linearly growing Adams filtration into vanishing. The relation between
unit-fiber powers, Adams towers and the cobar construction is standard [11, Section 2.1,
Propositions 2.11 and 2.14]. The compatible null cone on the whole constrained product
union is constructed in Section 2.
     The geometric step belongs to the collapse-and-duality framework of Thom and Atiyah [15,
1]. We prove the precise local duality needed for finite unions of coordinate planes, including
its variance under changes of boxes and arrangements. A single relative nullhomotopy
then gives a compatible stable lift over all simplices. Related stable obstruction criteria
for sections in a connectivity range appear in [8, Theorems 4.2–4.3] and [7, Corollary 3.5].
Here relative support and nested boxes keep the lift compatible and control its displacement.
Freudenthal stabilization, in the form recalled in [5, Corollary 4.24], turns it into a small
actual displacement when the parameter dimension is in the indicated range. The singular
arrangement and diagram compatibility assertions used here are proved in Section 3.
     The bundle criterion is related to the complementary-bundle Chern obstructions used
by Hirshberg–Phillips in their lower bounds for the radius of comparison; see the proof of
Theorem 3.3 in their author preprint [6]. We work with a supported point class in M U .
The characteristic-class framework is classical; see Quillen [13] and the precise orientation
and Thom formulas in [9, Lectures 5–6]. If r = pn, the complementary bundle has rank less
than r, which forces the product of the r square-zero Chern classes to vanish. A framed real

                                                  3
Thom class and relative excision then give vanishing of the supported point class. These
steps retain the whole sparse parameter locus.
    The resulting theorem can be used in cover constructions. Fix a homeomorphism from
the cube to a simplex carrying boundary to boundary. A boundary output then has at
least one zero simplex coordinate. Preserving an original boundary vector retains the
entire corresponding simplex point, and hence every coordinate that was already zero;
this is the agreement used in the companion’s reconstruction lemma [12, Lemma 4.3].
The companion [12, Theorem 1.1] uses Theorem 1.3, together with dynamical comparison
and cover reconstruction, to prove rc(C(X) ⋊h Z) = 12 mdim(X, h) for every minimal
homeomorphism h of an infinite compact metrizable space X, with both sides allowed to be
infinite. Here rc denotes radius of comparison and mdim denotes mean dimension.
    Here is the sequence of constructions in the proof.

    (i) A point test for f : P → C(t, n) collapses the difference f (x) − z near the origin, with
        z in a prescribed parameter arrangement. Product point tests are smash products.
        Theorem 1.1 therefore promotes M U -vanishing of a sparse test to stable vanishing for
        a large batch of copies.

    (ii) Compatible local duality converts stable test vanishing into a map avoiding the sparse
         arrangement. An elementary radial normalization creates boundary slots and restores
         all original boundary vectors exactly.

 (iii) At each new batch, we keep only the old output as a parameter. Its dimension is
       bounded by its ambient cube. A fixed number of initial unchanged batches then keeps
       every step within the stable avoidance range. The resulting boundary fraction is at
       least a/8 for arbitrarily large powers.

 (iv) The torus-bundle embedding forces the M U point test to vanish. For an arbitrary
      compact metrizable Y , finitely many auxiliary coordinates give a polyhedral model,
      while including every coordinate of f preserves f exactly. Compression on that model
      pulls back with the same boundary agreement.

   Section 2 proves the filtered-product theorem. Section 3 develops the point tests, local
duality and stable avoidance. Section 4 proves the general M U -null boundary-compression
theorem by padding. Section 5 establishes the bundle criterion and the exact compact-to-
polyhedral reduction, completing Theorem 1.3.

1.4     Conventions
Finite polyhedra and their pairs may be subdivided as needed. A quotient K/L means
K+ /L+ , where + adjoins a disjoint basepoint; in particular, K/∅ = K+ . Suspension spectra,
smash products, cofibers and limits of spectra are derived. The notation F (K, E) denotes
the function spectrum. We retain suspension shifts whenever connectivity or cell dimensions
are used. A spectrum is b-connective when its homotopy groups in degrees below b vanish.

2.2   The quantitative nilpotence input
The preceding proposition gives Adams filtration at least the number of restricted factors.

is sublinear in the degree and uniform over connective spectra at a fixed prime. We state
it as a null action on a truncated sphere, the form needed below for finite sources which
may have negative-degree cells. We write τ<k X for the Postnikov truncation of X retaining
homotopy groups in degrees below k.

5.2   Compact parameter spaces and exact factorization
Lemma 5.3 (Finite parameter reduction). Fix positive integers p, n and a real number a. Let
Y be compact metrizable, let f : Y → C(2p + 1, n) be continuous, and put Z = Zan (2p + 1, n).
Suppose the slot bundle on T (f, Z) embeds into a trivial complex bundle of nonnegative
integer rank K. There are a finite polyhedron P , a continuous map j : Y → P , and a PL
map fP : P → C(2p + 1, n) such that

                                            f = fP ◦ j,

and the slot bundle on T (fP , Z) also embeds into a trivial bundle of rank K.
Proof. If Y is empty, take P empty and the unique maps. Otherwise put M = (2p + 1)n
and r = pn. Represent the embedding on the compact test locus by a K-by-2r matrix A,
by first projecting onto the fixed source bundle. Extend its entries to Y × Z by the Tietze
theorem [14, Theorem 1.10.5]. Choose δ > 0 such that on the central test locus

                          ∥A(y, z)v∥ ≥ 4δ∥v∥           for v ∈ (Hn )f (y)−z .             (5.5)

Compactness of its unit sphere bundle supplies this number. If the locus is empty the
condition is vacuous and any δ > 0 may be used.
   The real algebra on Y × Z generated by continuous real functions on Y and the Euclidean
coordinates of z contains the constants and separates points. By the real Stone–Weierstrass

                                                  23
theorem [14, Theorem 1.10.18], the real and imaginary entries of A can be uniformly
approximated by elements of this algebra. Only finitely many functions on Y occur in these
approximants. Rescale them into [−1, 1] and denote them by φ1 , . . . , φq . Include every
coordinate of f explicitly, by setting

                     j0 (y) = (f (y), φ1 (y), . . . , φq (y)) ∈ W = [−1, 1]M +q .

The approximating matrix has the form B(j0 (y), z), where B is a matrix of polynomials on
W × Z. Choose the approximation so that its operator-norm error is less than δ everywhere
on Y × Z. By Equation (5.5),

                    ∥B(j0 (y), z)v∥ ≥ 3δ∥v∥      on the original slot bundle.             (5.6)

    Let C = j0 (Y ) and let f0 : W → [−1, 1]M be projection onto the first M coordinates.
The bundle over a point (u, z) with f0 (u) − z ∈ Ln depends only on f0 (u) − z. Consequently
Equation (5.6) gives the same bound at every such point of C × Z, regardless of the choice
of a preimage of u in Y .
    For completeness, consider the following compact bad set:

                  B = {(u, z, v) : u ∈ W, z ∈ Z, ∥v∥ = 1, f0 (u) − z ∈ Ln ,
                                    Π(f0 (u) − z)v = v, ∥B(u, z)v∥ ≤ 2δ}.

Here Π is the continuous orthogonal projection onto Hn . The projection of B onto W is
compact and disjoint from C, by Equation (5.6). Its complement is therefore a relative open
neighborhood of C in W . Subdivide W into sufficiently small closed cubes and take the
union P of all cubes meeting C. Finiteness and the positive distance from C to the bad
projection ensure that P is contained in this neighborhood. If the bad projection is empty,
any such subdivision suffices. This construction is relative to W ; it does not extend the first
M coordinates beyond the unit cube.
   Regard j0 as a map j : Y → P and put fP = f0 |P . The map fP is coordinate-linear and
hence PL. On T (fP , Z), the restriction of B(u, z) to the slot bundle is bounded below by 2δ
on unit vectors, and thus is the required continuous bundle embedding. Finally fP ◦ j = f
by the choice of the first M coordinates.

5.3   Completion of the compact-input theorem
The factorization just proved retains the cube map exactly, while moving the auxiliary
embedding data to a finite polyhedron. We can therefore apply boundary compression there
and pull it back without losing its pointwise preservation clause.

Proof of Theorem 1.3. Apply Lemma 5.3 to obtain j : Y → P and fP with the same bundle
embedding and f = fP ◦ j. By Lemma 5.2 the M U point test of fP vanishes. Theorem 4.2
gives the stated compression maps on P u for arbitrarily large u. Composing with j ×u gives
the maps on Y u . The factorization of f is exact, so the boundary agreement also pulls back
exactly.

Remark 5.4 (The strict rank threshold). The bound K < 2pn in Theorem 1.3 cannot in
general be replaced by K ≤ 2pn. Take n = 1, let Y = C(2p + 1, 1), and let f be the identity.
For every 0 < a < 1/16 the sparse set is Za (2p + 1, 1) = {0}, so the torus locus is L and the
canonical inclusion H ⊂ L × C2p supplies an embedding at the endpoint K = 2p.
   Suppose that, for some u ≥ 1, a continuous map G : B → B, where B = C(2p + 1, u),
has at least one boundary slot everywhere and fixes every original boundary slot. Its image


                                                 24
lies in the total boundary ∂B. For x ∈ ∂B, at least one boundary slot of x remains fixed
throughout the straight-line homotopy (1 − s)x + sG(x), 0 ≤ s ≤ 1. This homotopy therefore
stays in ∂B, showing that G|∂B has degree one. But G : B → ∂B extends this restriction
over the cube, forcing its degree to be zero. This contradiction rules out such a map for
every u. In particular, the positive bound (a/8)u in Theorem 1.3, which requires at least
one boundary slot, is impossible at this endpoint.
