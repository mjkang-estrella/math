An explicit counterexample to the Auslander–Reiten conjecture
                                               OpenAI

                                               Abstract
          We disprove the Auslander–Reiten conjecture for Artin algebras. We construct a
      finite-dimensional algebra Λ over k = F2 (q, H1 , H2 ) and a finite-dimensional nonprojective
      left module Z such that ExtiΛ (Z, Z) = ExtiΛ (Z, Λ) = 0 for every i > 0. The module
      is Gorenstein-projective, so the same example also disproves the Gorenstein-projective
      conjecture. Both counterexamples persist after every extension of k.

1     Introduction
The Auslander–Reiten conjecture asks whether a finitely generated module M over an Artin
algebra R must be projective when

                          ExtiR (M, M ⊕ R) = 0       for every i > 0.                     (1.1)

Here an Artin algebra is finite over a commutative Artinian central base ring. In particular,
every finite-dimensional algebra over a field is an Artin algebra. The conjecture originates in
the work of Maurice Auslander and Idun Reiten on the generalized Nakayama conjecture [2];
its module and generator formulations are recalled in [12, Introduction].
    We give a counterexample. A module is called Gorenstein-projective if it is a cokernel
in a doubly infinite exact complex of finite projectives that remains exact after applying
HomR (−, R). The Gorenstein-projective conjecture asks whether a finite Gorenstein-projective
module M with ExtiR (M, M ) = 0 for every i > 0 must be projective [16, p. 2]. The ambient
algebra is not assumed to be Gorenstein. Total acyclicity gives ExtiR (M, R) = 0 for i > 0, so
this conjecture is precisely the restriction of the Auslander–Reiten conjecture to Gorenstein-
projective modules.

Theorem 1.1. Let k = F2 (q, H1 , H2 ), where q, H1 , H2 are algebraically independent. There
exist a finite-dimensional k-algebra Λ and a finite-dimensional nonprojective left Λ-module Z
such that
                   ExtiΛ (Z, Z) = 0 = ExtiΛ (Z, Λ)    for every integer i ≥ 1.
The module Z is Gorenstein-projective. The semisimple quotient is Λ/ rad Λ ≃ k 8 , so the
algebra is split with eight isomorphism classes of simple modules; also rad(Λ)4 ̸= 0. These
conclusions persist after every field extension of k.

    Thus both conjectures fail for Artin algebras, including finite-dimensional algebras over
an algebraically closed field of characteristic two: one can extend scalars to an algebraic
closure of k. The parameters play essential roles in the proof. The powers of q give an infinite
resolution with uniform kernel formulas, and H1 /H2 has infinite multiplicative order. The
algebraic closure in this assertion contains these transcendental elements.
    The final algebra is triangular and noncommutative. The companion article [17, The-
orem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2] constructs a symmetric counterexample over the same field
by a different bimodule construction and a transfer requiring control of negative complete
self-extensions, and develops the homological consequences of its associated endomorphism
algebra. The contribution here is the explicit eight-simple Gorenstein-projective example and
its persistence under every field extension. Each paper contains its own complete proof. The
commutative restriction is not resolved by either construction; see [5, Appendix A] for its
relationship with the various Auslander and Tachikawa conditions. The generator formulation
also fails: Z ⊕ Λ is a nonprojective generator with no positive self-extensions. Indeed Ext is
additive in both arguments, and all positive Ext groups with first argument Λ vanish.

1.1   Earlier constructions and restricted results
The positive cases developed along several lines. For the commutative Noetherian formulation,
Auslander, Ding and Solberg treated complete local complete intersections by lifting across


                                               2
regular sequences [1, Proposition 1.9]. Huneke and Leuschke proved the conjecture for excellent
commutative Cohen–Macaulay normal domains containing the rational numbers, among
other classes [12, Theorem 0.1]. Christensen and Holm established a general cohomological
criterion: every left-noetherian ring satisfying Auslander’s condition satisfies the conjecture
[5, Theorem A]. That condition requires, for each fixed finitely generated first module, a
common bound after which Ext vanishes for every finitely generated second module whose
Ext groups vanish eventually.
    Algebras with radical cube zero form another important positive class. Huneke, Şega
and Vraciu treated commutative Artinian local rings [13, Theorem 4.1(1)]. For split finite-
dimensional local algebras with radical cube zero, Ringel and Zhang proved the stronger
assertion that every nonprojective module with no positive Ext to the algebra has a nonzero
first self-extension [18, Theorem 1.5]. Zhang and Zhou subsequently proved the conjecture
for split finite-dimensional algebras with radical cube zero [23, Theorem 1.1]. Their result
for Igusa–Todorov Artin algebras includes all Artin algebras with radical cube zero [24,
Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 4.4]. The algebra constructed here has nonzero fourth radical
power, as proved in Proposition 9.1.
    Schulz’s construction of a nonprojective module without self-extensions [19] is an important
antecedent, but its particular skew-field example lies outside the Artin-algebra setting above;
see [7, Introduction]. A separate distinction is between all-degree and eventual vanishing:
both summands in (1.1) must vanish in every positive degree.
    Schulz also exhibited two-dimensional modules over quantum exterior algebras whose
syzygies move through a parameter family and whose self-extensions vanish eventually, while
the first self-extension remains nonzero [20, Example 7]. The corresponding quantum exterior
algebra occurs as a corner of our starting algebra. We use the same parameter-shifting
mechanism for a resolution over a larger algebra, then remove every positive self-extension by
two independent twists. For indecomposable weakly symmetric algebras over an algebraically
closed field with J 3 = 0 ̸= J 2 , Erdmann characterizes those admitting nonprojective modules
with eventually vanishing self-extensions [9]; eventual vanishing alone does not give (1.1). Xia
proves the Auslander–Reiten conjecture for quantum complete intersections [22], including
the quantum exterior algebras used in Schulz’s parameter-shifting examples.
    Vanishing in a finite range also differs from the hypothesis of (1.1). Böhmler and Marcz-
inzik [3, Theorem 3.1 and the end of Section 3] construct a characteristic-two commutative
self-injective example with vanishing first and second self-extensions, and nonzero third
self-extension.
    Tachikawa’s second conjecture is the self-injective case of (1.1). Enomoto proves that this
conjecture for the two-fold trivial extension of an Artin algebra implies the Auslander–Reiten
conjecture for that algebra [8, Theorem 3.1]. His reduction produces a self-injective algebra.
The symmetric construction in our companion and the structural assertions of Theorem 1.1
are separate contributions.

2     Stable maps and complete resolutions
We first fix the language needed to pass from extension classes to an ordinary finite module.
All algebras and modules in the paper are finite-dimensional over the indicated field. Modules
are left modules unless a side is specified. Write DV = Homk (V, k) and Re = R ⊗k Rop ; an
R-bimodule is a left Re -module. All tensor products without a subscript are over k.
    Complexes have homological differentials di : Pi → Pi−1 and shifts P [a]i = Pi−a . Thus
a degree-a cohomology class is represented by a chain map P → Q[a]. Throughout the
construction

where the three parameters are algebraically independent. Characteristic two removes all
differential, shift, and interchange signs.
     For any finite algebra R, the space HomR (M, N ) is the quotient of HomR (M, N ) by maps
factoring through finite projective modules. An algebra R is symmetric if it has a linear
form t : R → k such that t(ab) = t(ba) and (a, b) 7→ t(ab) is nondegenerate. The resulting
bimodule isomorphism R ≃ DR shows that finite projective R-modules are injective. Every
finite module embeds in a finite projective: dualize a finite projective surjection onto its dual.
     For symmetric R, set M [−1] = ΩM , the kernel of a projective surjection onto M , and
set M [1] to be the cokernel of an embedding of M into a projective. These are well defined
on stable maps and inverse to each other there. Lifting through projective surjections
and extending into injectives give the maps on shifts; different choices differ by projective
factorizations. A short exact sequence induces a stable triangle and long exact sequences of
stable Hom groups in all integer degrees. One concrete cone of u : M → N is the cokernel of
(u, i) : M → N ⊕ I, where i : M ,→ I is a projective embedding. Its sequence is

    A complete resolution of M over a symmetric algebra is a doubly infinite exact complex
P of finite projectives with M = coker(d1 : P1 → P0 ). Splicing a projective resolution and an
injective coresolution constructs one. A complex of finite projectives over an arbitrary finite
algebra is totally acyclic if both it and HomR (P, R) are exact. Because every term is finitely
generated, exactness into R also gives exactness into every projective target, by direct sums
and summands. A module is Gorenstein-projective if it is such a cokernel in a totally acyclic
complex. Complete resolutions over symmetric algebras are totally acyclic. We will prove the
precise comparison with full Hom complexes in the next section; this also applies over the
final algebra, which need not be self-injective.
    For an A-bimodule F , the notation F M means F ⊗A M . If F is projective as a left and
as a right A-module, this is an exact functor preserving finite projectives, hence induces
a functor on stable modules compatible with shifts. We always mean these two separate
projectivity conditions when describing a bimodule as projective on both sides. They are
weaker than projectivity over Ae .


3     From two stable actions to a finite module
The last step of the construction uses a finite bimodule F and a stable map v : X → F ⊗A X.
Two actions on maps out of X will determine all positive self-extensions of a module over a
triangular algebra. We prove this conversion independently of the particular bimodule. The
module itself is a finite quotient; complete resolutions are used to compute its extensions.
