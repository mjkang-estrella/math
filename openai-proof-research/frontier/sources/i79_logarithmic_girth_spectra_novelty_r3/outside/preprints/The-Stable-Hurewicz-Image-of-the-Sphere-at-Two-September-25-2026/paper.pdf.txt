                                                 Abstract
         We prove Curtis’s conjecture: in every positive degree, the mod-two stable Hurewicz image
     of the sphere is spanned by the images of η, ν, σ and the Kervaire-invariant-one classes that exist.
     Consequently, Eccles’s conjecture holds for every sphere S n with n > 0.

1    Introduction
For n ≥ 0, let QS n = colimr Ωr S n+r , and let Q0 S 0 be its basepoint component. For d > 0, the
identification πd (Q0 S 0 ) = πdS gives the mod-2 Hurewicz homomorphism

                                       hd : πdS −→ Hd (Q0 S 0 ; F2 ).

It sends a representative S d → Q0 S 0 to the image of the mod-2 fundamental class. Curtis’s
conjecture describes the positive-degree image of these maps [10]. The target has an explicit
homology algebra, while the stable homotopy groups in the source contain many families whose
images are difficult to determine. The image problem asks which of the readily described homology
classes are represented by maps from spheres.
    Curtis proposed the Hopf–Kervaire description in his study of the Dyer–Lashof and lambda
algebras [10]. May’s subsequent account records a gap found by Wellington in the proposed proof
[25, p. 484, footnote 3]. We prove the following form of the conjecture.



                                                      1
                                 L
Theorem 1.1. The image of d>0 hd is spanned over F2 by the Hurewicz images of the Hopf-
invariant-one classes η, ν, σ, in degrees 1, 3, 7, and the Kervaire-invariant-one classes θj , in degrees
2j+1 − 2, j ≥ 1, whenever they exist. In particular, Curtis’s conjecture is true in all positive degrees.

   Here Kervaire invariant one means that the corresponding framed manifold, under the Pontryagin–
Thom identification, has Arf invariant one. The notation θj is used only when such a class exists.
The theorem does not posit existence in any additional degree.
    Eccles’s conjecture concerns the analogous realization problem for QX. In the formulation
recorded in [33, pp. 14–15], for a path-connected CW complex X of finite type, a positive-dimensional
sphere map to QX with nonzero mod-2 Hurewicz image has stable adjoint detected either by
homology or by a primary cohomology operation in its mapping cone. Theorem 1.1 gives this
conclusion when X is a positive-dimensional sphere.
   For n > 0, write en ∈ Hn (QS n ; F2 ) for the bottom spherical class, and write σ∗n for n-fold
homology suspension from Q0 S 0 .

spherical class in H∗ (QS n ; F2 ) is either en , or a nonzero class of the form

                             σ∗n hd (α),     (α, d) ∈ {(η, 1), (ν, 3), (σ, 7)}.

stably spherical class and Hopf-invariant-one classes.

Proof. For a spherical representative f : S n+d → QS n with d > 0, adjunction gives f0 : S d → Q0 S 0 ,

and Kervaire images. The latter are squares killed by the first homology suspension, leaving the
stated Hopf possibilities; this is the Curtis-to-Eccles sphere implication recorded in [33, Chapter 5,
pp. 81–82]. In degree n, πn (QS n ) = π0S = Z gives the bottom image represented by the unit
S n → QS n ; connectivity excludes lower degrees.

   Adams’s Hopf invariant one theorem identifies the Hopf cases [3]. Browder’s work relates the
framed Kervaire invariant to the classes h2j in the mod-2 Adams spectral sequence [5]. Hill–Hopkins–

their preprint, Lin, Wang, and Xu prove existence in that dimension [23, Theorems 1.1 and 1.4].
This endpoint result is separate from the upper bound used in Corollary 1.3.

   On the homology side, the free infinite-loop-space calculation describes a large algebra generated
by Dyer–Lashof operations [9]. A spherical class must be primitive and annihilated by positive
Steenrod operations, but these necessary conditions alone do not establish its realization. The
theorem concerns the image of actual stable homotopy classes.
    Eccles and Zare give restrictions on spherical squares and their roots, including the exclusion
of higher iterated squares [12, Theorem 3.10 and Lemma 3.12]. Section 4 proves the exclusion of
fourth powers by spectrum pairings and integral characteristic classes; the conclusion itself is already
present in that prior work. The Dickson-algebra approach to spherical classes also appears in Hung
and Peterson’s study of the Lannes–Zarati homomorphism and products in the unstable Adams
E2 -term [17]. Hunter’s cohomological construction of the Curtis–Wellington spectral sequence
exhibits Dickson-algebra quotients in a width filtration [18, arXiv version 2, Theorem 1.2].
    The argument here combines classical detection by mapping-cone operations with an obstruction
for the specific Steenrod modules dual to primitives. The cone of an actual sphere map supplies
a nonzero extension. A detecting functional inherited from the preceding suspension level then
restricts that extension through the algebraic obstruction proved in Section 5.
     The free-algebra model constructed below is compatible with Kuhn’s structured splitting
and completion maps [20]; we prove the required finite weight projections and their suspension
compatibility directly. For the final passage from leading weight to Adams filtration, we use Kuhn’s
lifting theorem [21] and the derived ideal-power constructions of [22].

Outline of the proof. The proof is organized around the last nonzero homology suspension
of a spherical class. For a representative f0 : S d → Q0 S 0 , let fj : S d+j → QS j be its successive
adjoints and put zj = h(fj ). Suspension gives zj+1 = σ∗ zj , and the homology degree bounds imply
that zj = 0 for j > d. Thus a nonzero initial image has a last nonzero term ze . We show that
this terminal class is a square ze = w2 whose primitive, Steenrod-annihilated root still has nonzero
suspension. At a positive terminal level, the preceding spherical suspension forces the root to have
odd degree. At level zero, we instead exclude fourth powers using spectrum pairings and integral
characteristic classes (Section 4). The mapping cone of the next adjoint then converts the square
into a nonzero Steenrod-module extension (Section 3).
    At positive level n, weight counts the inputs of an extended power. The primitives of weight 2k ,
k ≥ 1, are dual to Mk (n) = Σn dn0 Dk , where Dk is a binary Dickson algebra and d0 is its generator of
degree 2k − 1; the bottom summand is M0 (n) = Σn F2 . Section 2 constructs these modules, identifies
the dual of homology suspension with inclusion of the next d0 -ideal, and supplies compatible finite
weight projections at level zero. Section 5 shows that the cone extension on a nonbottom weight,
when its detecting functional lifts from the preceding suspension level, is possible only for n = k = 1
and in a power-of-two degree. Cartier halving and an injection on even-degree Tor cycles retain the
exponent-floor information needed for this restriction.
   Section 6 assembles the argument. At a positive terminal level the obstruction leaves only the
bottom weight, giving stable Hopf detection. At terminal level zero it forces the root to have leading
weight two, so the original spherical class has leading weight four. Kuhn’s theorem bounds its
Adams filtration by two, and Browder’s identification gives Kervaire detection. Additivity of the
detecting invariants then gives the asserted span.

2    Weights, primitives, and homology suspension

completion requires a filtration in place of the positive-level splitting.

2.4    The weight filtration at level zero
At positive levels the structured splitting gives a direct sum of weights. At level zero we instead

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Every positive-degree stable class with nonzero Hurewicz image has a last
nonzero suspension. Propositions 6.2 and 6.3 show that it has the stated nonzero Hopf or Kervaire
invariant. These two degree cases are disjoint.

any Kervaire-invariant-one representative θj ; its existence in this case is already supplied by the
class under discussion. Each detecting invariant is additive with values in F2 . For the stable Hopf
invariant, attach two top cells to the same bottom sphere by representatives α and β. If u is the
bottom class and aα , aβ are the relative top classes, naturality on the two one-cell cofibers gives
Sqd+1 u = H(α)aα + H(β)aβ . Pinching the attaching sphere induces a map from the cofiber of
α + β to this two-attachment cofiber; it pulls both relative classes to the single top class. Naturality
therefore gives H(α + β) = H(α) + H(β). For the Kervaire invariant, disjoint union of framed
manifolds takes their quadratic forms to orthogonal sums and adds their Arf invariants. Subtracting
the chosen representative therefore gives an invariant-zero class. If this difference had nonzero
Hurewicz image, the proved necessity would force its invariant to be one, a contradiction. Thus its
Hurewicz image is zero.
    Every nonzero image is consequently the image of the chosen Hopf or Kervaire representative
in that degree. These representative images themselves belong to the image, so they span it. No
existence assertion for an additional θj is used or obtained from this last step. This proves Curtis’s
conjecture in all positive degrees.
