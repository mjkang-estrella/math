                                                Abstract
          We give a negative answer to the hyperinvariant-subspace problem by constructing, on every
      infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space, a nonzero bounded norm-quasinilpotent
      operator with no nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace. Its commutant is a proper
      strongly closed unital transitive complex operator algebra.

1    Introduction
Let H be a complex Hilbert space and let B(H) denote its bounded linear operators. The commutant
of S ∈ B(H) is
                                 {S}′ = {A ∈ B(H) : AS = SA}.

                                                    1
A closed subspace is hyperinvariant for S if every operator in {S}′ leaves it invariant. An operator
algebra is transitive if its only closed common invariant subspaces are {0} and H. Thus S has no
nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace precisely when its commutant is transitive.
    The hyperinvariant-subspace problem asks whether every bounded nonscalar operator on an
infinite-dimensional separable complex Hilbert space has a nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant
subspace. The construction below gives a negative answer. Its purpose is to exhibit the mechanism
explicitly: the ordinary invariant subspaces of each weighted-shift fiber are coordinate tails, and a
second, commuting operator moves those tails backward. Invariance under the commutant therefore
imposes a strict descent that cannot persist on a probability space.

norm-quasinilpotent operator with no nonzero proper closed hyperinvariant subspace. Its commutant
is a proper strongly closed unital transitive complex operator algebra.
   Here the strong operator topology is pointwise norm convergence on H. Consequently the
commutant in the corollary also gives a negative answer to the question whether every unital
transitive operator algebra is strongly dense in B(H).

1.2   Context and relation to the companion
Douglas and Pearcy studied the connection between hyperinvariant subspaces and transitive alge-
bras [4]. A major positive result is Arveson’s density theorem: a transitive algebra on a separable
Hilbert space which contains a maximal abelian self-adjoint subalgebra of B(H) is strongly dense in
B(H) [1, Theorem 3.3]. The scalar multiplier algebra used here has infinite multiplicity. Section 6
gives an explicit operator commuting with every such multiplier that is not itself a scalar multiplier,
so the maximality hypothesis is absent.
    Domar’s work on invariant subspaces of weighted translation spaces [3] and Grabiner’s work
on unicellular shifts [6] provide the classical lattice ideas behind the fibers. Section 2 proves
the precise quadratic weighted-translation criterion we need. Its convex-envelope and support-
separation argument follows Domar’s method. The proof includes both normalized convolution-tail
estimates; no general criterion for arbitrary weights is assumed. Our additional task is to link
the fibers by a bounded commuting operator that moves their invariant tails backward. Opposite
logarithmic changes at the two ends of the bilateral sequence make this possible, and one measurable
normalization controls the operator over the whole base.
    Atzmon and Sodin constructed completely indecomposable bilateral weighted shifts with spectrum
the unit circle [2]; their 1999 paper left open whether those shifts have proper hyperinvariant
subspaces. Here the extra commutant action is supplied by transport between different fibers.
For a study of operators with shift-type invariant subspaces, see Kérchy [7]. The invariant- and
hyperinvariant-subspace problems must remain distinct: the Kérchy–Pearcy preprint claiming an
equivalence was withdrawn after a counterexample to its Theorem 3 [8]. The operator constructed
here preserves every fixed tail L2 (X; K≥h ); its hyperinvariant conclusion is proved directly.
    The companion paper [9] constructs, for every prescribed irrational rotation, a continuous
circle weight whose operator in the rotation factor has no nontrivial invariant projection in that
factor. It derives the existence conclusion of Corollary 1.2 by the usual projection argument; see
also Dykema [5, Proposition 2.1]. The present construction has a different purpose: it exhibits
the ordinary invariant tails, the bounded operator that moves them backward, and the explicit
estimate (1). The two proofs use different constructions. Our only proof imports from the companion
are the two elementary facts stated below.

1.3   Proof overview and elementary inputs
The proof first makes the possible fiber subspaces completely explicit. Section 2 classifies invariant
subspaces of a weighted translation whose logarithmic weights have opposite quadratic growth at
the two ends. Section 3 constructs weights over Z2 satisfying that criterion in every fiber. Their
decay also gives the uniform power estimate, which proves norm-quasinilpotence of the operator on
H0 rather than merely of its individual fibers.
    To couple the fibers, we prescribe a map from ej at x to a positive multiple of ej−1 at θx.
Commutation with the forward shifts determines a recurrence for those multiples. Section 4 solves
the recurrence in both directions. The logarithmic changes of the weights under θ have opposite
signs at the two ends; the coefficients therefore decay in both directions. Dividing all coefficients in
each fiber by their common supremum yields the contraction V without changing commutation.
    Finally, a subspace invariant under every scalar multiplier has an orthogonal projection repre-
sented by a measurable projection field. Invariance under S makes each fiber a tail K≥s(x) . Section 5
uses the dense images under Vx to obtain

                              s(θx) ≤ s(x) − 1         whenever s(x) ∈ Z.

Probability preservation excludes finite labels, and ergodicity excludes a nonconstant mixture of full
and zero fibers. Section 6 then proves the corollary and records the structure of the example.
   We use the following two elementary inputs, with the stated scope. Their complete proofs are in
the companion [9].
    1. Fields from scalar multipliers [9, Lemma 3.1]. For a probability space (Y, ν), K = ℓ2 (Z), and
       A ∈ B(L2 (Y ; K)) commuting with every scalar multiplier, there is an essentially bounded field
       Ay ∈ B(K), unique almost everywhere, such that

                                    (Af )(y) = Ay f (y),               ∥Ay ∥ ≤ ∥A∥         a.e.

       For every v ∈ K, the map y |→ Ay v is measurable. Products and adjoints act fiberwise; an
       orthogonal projection has orthogonal projection fibers outside one null set.

    2. The commutant algebra [9, Lemma 1.2]. For any bounded nonscalar operator on a complex
       Hilbert space, its commutant is a proper strongly closed unital complex algebra. If its only
       closed common invariant subspaces are zero and the whole space, that algebra is transitive.
The first input requires no base transformation, covariance, trace or factor assumption. We apply it
only after proving reduction by all scalar multipliers. The second requires neither separability nor
quasinilpotence; the constructed operator will satisfy its nonscalarity hypothesis. The field input is
used in Section 5, and the commutant input in Section 6.

6     The commutant and structural consequences
Proof of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2. Proposition 3.2 proves the fiber classification and norm
statement in Theorem 1.1, and Proposition 3.3 gives the uniform power bound and nonzeroness.
Proposition 4.3 constructs the commuting contraction with its dense tail images. Section 5 proves
transitivity of the algebra generated by S, V , and the scalar multipliers. These establish all assertions
of the theorem.
    That transitive algebra is contained in {S}′ , so any closed {S}′ -invariant subspace is zero or
all of H0 . The nonzero norm-quasinilpotent operator S is nonscalar. The commutant lemma [9,
Lemma 1.2], with this S on the complex Hilbert space H0 , now implies that {S}′ is a proper strongly
closed unital complex algebra. Together with the lattice conclusion, it is transitive.
    Finally, the finite residue classes form a countable generating algebra on X, so L2 (X) and H0
are separable. The space H0 is infinite-dimensional. Every infinite-dimensional separable complex
Hilbert space is unitarily isomorphic to H0 ; conjugation by such a unitary transfers S, its norm
estimates, its commutant, and the invariant-subspace conclusions. This proves the corollary.

6.1   Ordinary invariant subspaces and compactness
For every h ∈ Z, the subspace L2 (X; K≥h ) is closed, nonzero, proper, and S-invariant. It is not
invariant under V : the image of the constant field eh has its nonzero coordinate at h − 1 almost
everywhere. Thus the construction separates ordinary invariance from hyperinvariance in an explicit
way.
    Each fiber Sx is compact. Indeed a± k ≥ c(k + 1) implies βj (x) → 0 as |j| → ∞, and cutting off
all but finitely many shift weights approximates Sx in operator norm. The operator S on H0 is not
compact. There is an infinite orthonormal sequence (φn ) in L2 (X), since the Nm disjoint residue
classes give Nm orthogonal indicators for every m. The orthonormal vectors φn ⊗ e0 have images

                                       S(φn ⊗ e0 ) = e−c φn ⊗ e1 .

These images are orthogonal with a common positive norm and have no norm-convergent subsequence.

6.2   The multiplier algebra has infinite multiplicity
Let Q0 be the projection onto L2 (X) ⊗ Ce0 . This gives a concrete witness to the properness of the
commutant: on 1 ⊗ e0 ,

                         Q0 S(1 ⊗ e0 ) = 0,      SQ0 (1 ⊗ e0 ) = e−c (1 ⊗ e1 ),

so Q0 ∈/ {S}′ .
    The same projection commutes with every scalar multiplier, but is not itself a scalar multiplier.
If Q0 = Mg , its action on 1 ⊗ e0 would give g = 1 almost everywhere, whereas its action on 1 ⊗ e1 is
zero. The abelian self-adjoint algebra {Mg : g ∈ L∞ (X)} is therefore not maximal abelian in B(H0 ).
Arveson’s maximal-abelian inclusion theorem [1, Theorem 3.3] does not apply to this subalgebra of
the transitive commutant.

6.3   Atomic finite families and a diffuse joint algebra
We finish with a spectral property of the constructed weights. This subsection is independent of the
transitivity proof.
    Let |S| = (S ∗ S)1/2 . Identify H0 with L2 (X × Z, µ × #), where # is counting measure, and let
U ej = ej+1 on every fiber. Then

                             S = U |S|,       (|S|f )(x, j) = βj (x)f (x, j).

All weights are positive, so |S| is injective with dense range and U is the unitary polar part of S.
Define
                  An = U −n |S|U n (n ∈ Z);         (An f )(x, j) = βj+n (x)f (x, j).
For a family of operators, W ∗ denotes the smallest strongly closed unital self-adjoint algebra
containing that family. A nonzero projection in an abelian von Neumann algebra is minimal if it
contains no smaller nonzero projection in that algebra. Such an algebra is atomic when its identity
is the strong sum of its minimal projections, and diffuse when it has no minimal projection. We will
show that W ∗ (An : n ∈ F ) is atomic for every finite F ⊂ Z, whereas W ∗ (An : n ∈ Z) is diffuse.
    For any finite F ⊂ Z and fixed j, the tuple (βj+n (x))n∈F depends on finitely many residues
Tm (x). Because the residues are nested, it depends on one residue TM (x) and takes finitely many
values. Allowing the countably many values of j gives a countable range for the joint tuple on
X × Z. Its nonempty level sets have positive measure: on every fixed coordinate slice they are finite
unions of residue classes. These level sets give the minimal projections of the generated abelian
algebra, and their sum is the identity. Thus W ∗ (An : n ∈ F ) is atomic. In particular, |S| has a
pure-point spectral resolution.
