                                                   Abstract
         We prove the equivariant cohomological Hikita conjecture for arbitrary finite quivers, including
      loops and multiple arrows. For any dimension and framing vectors, any commuting flavor torus,
      and a stability character whose semistable locus is stable and has free gauge action, the equivariant
      cohomology of the Nakajima variety is canonically isomorphic to the coordinate ring of the
      scheme-theoretic fixed locus of the stability cocharacter on the flavor-deformed Coulomb branch.
      This is an isomorphism of graded algebras over the common coefficient ring, retaining nilpotents
      and including the empty case.


1     Introduction
Hikita’s conjecture relates two different kinds of algebra: the cohomology of a symplectic resolution
and the functions on a fixed subscheme of a corresponding Coulomb branch. The scheme structure
is essential. Even when the underlying fixed-point set is small, its nilpotent functions can encode
positive-degree cohomology. For quiver gauge theories, the tautological classes on the Higgs side
and the equivariant parameters on the Coulomb side give a common coefficient ring. The question
is whether the relations among those coefficients agree.
    We prove this agreement for arbitrary finite quivers, under the precise regularity hypothesis
stated below. The proof keeps the coefficient maps and the full fixed schemes throughout. Its two
main comparisons have different purposes: a calculation for the full topological torus identifies the
cohomology algebra, while a separate argument identifies that torus-fixed scheme with the fixed
scheme of the stability cocharacter.

1.2   History and the comparison problem
A classical model is the description of type-A Springer-fiber cohomology through scheme-theoretic
intersections of nilpotent orbit closures with a Cartan subalgebra, developed by de Concini–Procesi
and Tanisaki [11, 40]. In the setting of symplectic duality [3], Hikita formulated a fixed-scheme
realization of cohomology and established it for the Hilbert scheme of points in the plane, with
related type-A Spaltenstein and smooth hypertoric examples [18]. These examples explain why the
infinitesimal structure of the fixed locus, rather than its set of points, is central to the question.
    Nakajima proposed a mathematical Coulomb-branch construction for three-dimensional gauge
theories [36]; Braverman–Finkelberg–Nakajima constructed its commutative convolution algebra
[4, 5], extending the affine-Grassmannian convolution framework of Bezrukavnikov–Finkelberg–
Mirković [1]. For quiver gauge theories the corresponding Higgs quotient is a Nakajima variety

                                                     2
[35]. The coefficient-compatible formulation has important predecessors. Kamnitzer–Tingley–
Webster–Weekes–Yacobi record Nakajima’s equivariant conjecture in the affine-Grassmannian
setting and strengthen its specialized form by requiring compatibility with tautological classes [26,
Conjectures 8.9–8.10]. Hilburn–Kamnitzer–Weekes formulate the equivariant quantized refinement
over the common coefficient algebra [19, Section 5.7, Conjecture 5.26]. Krylov–Shlykov state the
general-quiver comparison for the full topological-torus fixed scheme as equality of coefficient-map
kernels [29, Section 8.1, Conjecture 8.1]. We first establish that equality and then compare the
full-torus fixed scheme with the stability-cocharacter fixed scheme. Thus compatibility with the
coefficient maps is part of the conjectural formulation itself.
    Krylov–Shlykov prove the Gieseker, or framed Jordan-quiver, case [29]. Dumanski–Krylov
give the formulation used here and compare cohomological, equivariant, and K-theoretic versions
[13]. Their finite-type conclusion includes types A, D, E6 ; in types E7 , E8 it imposes the framing
restrictions in [13, Corollary 2.17]. Their Remark 2.18 explains why the earlier unrestricted ADE
assertion must be qualified. Recent developments also include Setiabrata’s Hikita surjectivity result
for the base-affine-cotangent example [39], and the refined quantum Hikita theorem of Dinkins–
Karpov–Krylov for ADE quivers with minuscule-supported framing and for the Jordan quiver [12,
Theorem B]. Theorem 1.1 treats the cohomological algebra for all the finite quiver data above.
It does not assert the quantized statement in [13, Equation (2.2)], nor extend the theorem to
arbitrary reductive gauge groups; outside this setting, proposed Hikita-type identifications can fail
[21, Example 6.7].
    Several established tools enter the argument in distinct ways. BFN abelianization turns minuscule
convolution classes into explicit Laurent expressions, and Weekes’s generation theorem makes these
classes sufficient for arbitrary quivers [4, 5, 42]. McGerty–Nevins’s ordinary and equivariant Kirwan
surjectivity results supply the tautological presentation on the Higgs side [32, Theorem 1.2 and
Corollary 1.5]. Hausel’s arithmetic Fourier-transform formula computes the Betti numbers that
eventually determine the size of the coefficient quotient [16]. Character-linearized GIT and the
topological Kempf–Ness correspondence connect regular stability to the flat hyperkähler quotient
construction [27, 22, 20, 35]. Our use of these inputs is explained at their precise interfaces below.
    The finite-dimensional critical-model viewpoint is related to Nakajima’s original construction and
to Botta–Tamagni’s critical lattice and quasimap models [36, 2]. Here the specific stable-pair test
proves an inclusion of coefficient ideals; it does not require their entire Coulomb-action construction.
Deformation arguments also appear in Krylov–Shlykov’s Gieseker proof [29, Remark 1.15]. Our loop-
removal step uses subgroup localization [6], with the prequotient perspective of [13, Proposition 2.3],
and an explicit descent for homogeneous ideals. These connections distinguish the established
methods from the particular comparison arguments carried out here.
    The difficulty is not just to match dimensions. One must identify the relations under the specified
maps, with their grading and nilpotents, for arbitrary flavors and for every regular stability. Neither
a calculation on closed fixed points nor an isomorphism after inverting generic parameters establishes
that assertion. A further distinction is that a regular character can satisfy ν · b = 0 for an integer
vector 0 < b ≤ v. Therefore the full topological-torus calculation does not by itself identify the
cocharacter-fixed quotient.

Lemma 2.1. For the purposes of Theorem 1.1, one may assume that the flavor action assigns a
character to each arrow summand and acts diagonally on each framing multiplicity space. Adding or

Proof. Decompose N into G-isotypic summands. A commuting torus acts on each multiplicity

so this coordinate gives a common vector-space factor in every finite truncation. The normalized
equivariant Thom isomorphism removes this factor, compatibly with convolution, both gradings,
and the coefficient map. This proves the claim on both sides.

Lemma 2.4 (Local constancy of regular stability). Let θ be a rational character parameter for
which every semistable point of µ−1 (0) is stable and has trivial stabilizer. There is a neighborhood U
of θ in X ∗ (G) ⊗Z R such that the stable and semistable loci are unchanged for every rational θ′ ∈ U .
One may choose such a θ′ outside all proper hyperplanes θ′ · b = 0 with 0 < b ≤ v, and then clear
its denominators by a positive integer. Empty loci are included.

Proof. We identify a positive multiple of a rational character with the same stability condition. Fix
a maximal torus of G and the finite set of its weights on N ⊕ N ∗ . For a point x and g ∈ G, let
C(gx) be the cone generated by the weights occurring in gx. The affine Hilbert–Mumford criterion
says that x is semistable precisely when the character belongs to every such cone, and is stable
with finite stabilizer precisely when it belongs to all their full interiors in the real weight space;
see [27] and [22, Propositions 2.5 and 2.7]. Although g varies, only finitely many cones occur. A
sufficiently small neighborhood of θ remains inside each cone whose interior contains θ, and outside
each cone that does not contain θ. A θ-stable point therefore remains stable, and a θ-unstable point
remains unstable. Regularity accounts for all points of the closed subvariety µ−1 (0). This proves
the assertion about the entire loci, including when they are empty. The geometric quotient and its
principal gauge bundle are unchanged, as is freeness. Choose a rational point in this neighborhood
avoiding the finitely many proper hyperplanes specified in the statement, and then rescale by a
positive integer. If G is trivial, its character space is a point and the list of bounded nonzero
dimension vectors is empty, so the assertion is immediate.

    The Kirwan map R → HF∗ (X) is surjective. For nonempty X, Lemma 2.4 permits a rational
perturbation θ of ν that preserves the entire stable locus D and avoids θ · b = 0 for 0 < b ≤ v. Its
extension (θ, −θ · v) is nondegenerate for the framed dimension vector (v, 1): a proper subvector has
final coordinate zero, or its nonzero complement does. Clearing denominators preserves stability.
The quotient, its principal gauge bundle, and its tautological bundles are unchanged, so ordinary
Kirwan surjectivity applies with the same coefficient map [32, Definition 3.1 and Theorem 1.2]; see

also [13, Section 2.1]. The torus-equivariant conclusion is [32, Corollary 1.5]; we recall its argument

even. For empty X the assertion means the unique surjection to the zero ring.

Coulomb algebra as a relation between such sheaves.

8    The cocharacter fixed scheme
We now compare the fixed scheme for the chosen cocharacter with the fixed scheme for the full
topological torus. This comparison uses only minuscule generation, a root-support consequence of
the loop-nilpotent Hall algebra, and the regularity of the stability. In particular, it does not use
Theorem 7.4 or assume coefficient surjectivity onto the cocharacter fixed quotient.
     If ν · b ̸= 0 for every 0 < b ≤ v, Proposition 2.2 immediately gives the comparison: every
nontrivial positive or negative minuscule generator has nonzero ν-weight. The argument below also
covers regular characters that lie on some of these dimension hyperplanes.
     For example, take two disconnected vertices, no arrows, and v = w = (1, 1), with ν = (1, −1).
Each Higgs factor is a point with free stable gauge action, so this character is regular, although
ν · (1, 1) = 0. The joint positive monopole is a product of monopoles of weights 1 and −1, and hence
vanishes in the cocharacter fixed quotient: weight zero alone does not imply survival.
     To treat these characters, we adjoin a rank-one framing vertex. If a nontrivial minuscule generator
of the original Coulomb algebra survived in its cocharacter fixed quotient, the corresponding class and
its complementary class would both survive in the enlarged cocharacter fixed quotient. Polynomial
Hall-algebra generation would then decompose both nonzero complementary dimension vectors
into indecomposable dimension vectors of weight zero for the extended character. These two
decompositions supply at least two blocks of a semistable direct sum; after removing the one
ineffective diagonal scalar, its stabilizer remains positive-dimensional, contradicting regularity.

show that the nonzero dimension-vector support of these generators is ∆Γ [23, Proposition 3.14
and §3.10]. Here the Kac identity retains the full cohomological grading: the coefficient of td/2
is (−1)d dim H d . A zero Kac polynomial therefore forces every graded piece of the corresponding
BPS space to vanish. Proposition 3.14 of that source transfers this vanishing to the loop-nilpotent
BPS space. The concrete twist in Equation (8.1) preserves these homogeneous word spans. Here
polynomial form refers to equivariant parameters over Q, not integral cohomology.
    Apply the generation statement to eb,g or fb,g , and retain dimension-vector degree b. All
nonconstant Hall degrees are nonnegative, so the result is a finite S-linear combination of products
of BPS descendants with degrees in ∆Γ summing to b. Applying Lemma 8.1 gives the corresponding
finite identity in CS . Its coefficients lie in S; no parameter from S is inverted in this identity. Every
factor has degree at most b ≤ d; no assertion about vanishing of entire Hall components outside
this bound is needed.
    Specialization. Now extend scalars to C and apply the specialization homomorphism of Lemma 8.1.
Each finite identity becomes an identity in AΓ,d,F . The signs in the images of the distinguished
elements are nonzero constants and can be absorbed in its coefficients. This operation is performed
on the abstract polynomial-form algebra and its map, not on the ambient polynomial representation
of the shuffle algebra. An element whose ambient representative is divisible by ℏ need not belong to
ℏ times the polynomial shuffle half, and is therefore not discarded before applying the map.
    General dressings. It remains to allow an arbitrary stabilizer-invariant dressing p. At each
vertex, the polynomial algebra symmetric separately in the selected and complementary variables
is generated by the polynomials symmetric in all variables together with those symmetric in the
selected variables. Indeed, each complementary power sum is the total power sum minus the selected
power sum. Thus p is a finite sum of coefficient polynomials times selected-variable dressings g. The
already proved identities for g give Equation (8.3).
