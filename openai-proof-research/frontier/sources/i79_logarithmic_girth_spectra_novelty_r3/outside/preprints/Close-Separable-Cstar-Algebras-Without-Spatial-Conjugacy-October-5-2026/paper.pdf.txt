                              Close Separable C ∗-Algebras
                              Without Spatial Conjugacy

                                                Abstract
         We disprove the separable C ∗ -algebraic spatial form of the Kadison–Kastler conjecture. For
      every ε > 0, we construct unital, norm-separable C ∗ -algebras on a common separable complex
      Hilbert space, with the same identity and Kadison–Kastler distance less than ε, that are not
      conjugate by any unitary. The two algebras have the same von Neumann closure.


1    Introduction
For C ∗ -algebras A, B ⊆ B(H), write A1 and B1 for their closed unit balls. Their Kadison–Kastler
distance is                           (                                     )
                      dKK (A, B) = max      sup inf ∥a − b∥ , sup inf ∥b − a∥ .                         (1)
                                           a∈A1 b∈B1            b∈B1 a∈A1

The spatial form of the perturbation problem asks whether sufficiently close algebras must be related
by uAu∗ = B for a unitary u ∈ B(H). Kadison and Kastler initiated this problem in [6]. In its
separable C ∗ -algebraic form, one asks for a constant δ > 0 valid for all norm-separable C ∗ -algebras
on separable Hilbert spaces; no bound on ∥u − I∥ is required. The following theorem resolves
negatively this separable C ∗ -algebraic spatial form of the Kadison–Kastler conjecture, even for
unital algebras with a common identity.

Theorem 1.1. For every ε > 0 there are a separable complex Hilbert space H and unital, norm-
separable C ∗ -algebras A, B ⊆ B(H) with common identity IH such that

                                     dKK (A, B) < ε,       A′′ = B ′′ ,

but no unitary u ∈ B(H) satisfies uAu∗ = B.

    The scope of this statement is important. Johnson constructed arbitrarily close separable
nuclear algebras for which implementing unitaries cannot be chosen close to the identity [5];
those examples are nevertheless spatially conjugate. Choi and Christensen constructed arbitrarily
close nonisomorphic algebras [2], but norm nonseparability is essential to those examples. These
distinctions, and the resulting separable spatial formulation, are discussed in [3]. In the positive
direction, Christensen, Sinclair, Smith, White, and Winter proved spatial conjugacy for sufficiently
close separable nuclear C ∗ -algebras on a separable Hilbert space [3, Theorem 4]; see [4, Theorem 5.4]
for the full proof. Theorem 1.1 imposes no nuclearity assumption. It makes no assertion of abstract
nonisomorphism and does not give a counterexample for von Neumann algebras.

This equality is invariant under normalizers of D and passes to simultaneous finite-family norm

In fact, P0 and Pt themselves are conjugate by a unitary close to the identity. The obstruction
concerns their positions inside D, and the sequence construction makes it an obstruction to every
spatial conjugacy of the resulting algebras.
    For the close inclusions we use a real-parameter deformation of the regular representation of the
free group on countably many generators. This representation-theoretic ingredient belongs to the
Pytlik–Szwarc family [7]; its uniform-topology behavior at the regular representation is discussed
by Burger, Ozawa, and Thom [1, Theorem 4.6]. We give a direct tree construction and prove
the uniform estimate needed here. The additional mechanism is the combination of the relative
tensor-norm test with the convergent-sequence reduction. The reduction is stated separately, so it
can be used for other pairs of inclusions.
    Section 2 proves the reduction. Section 3 constructs the representations, and Section 4 establishes
the tensor-norm obstruction. Section 5 assembles the counterexamples. All Hilbert spaces are
complex, and all operator tensor products below act on the indicated Hilbert-space tensor products.
No abstract choice of C ∗ -tensor norm is needed. Unital subalgebras are understood to contain the
identity of their ambient operator algebra.


2    A convergent-sequence obstruction
We explain how the limit condition records an inclusion P ⊆ D. The resulting algebras preserve
the distance between limit algebras, but their spatial conjugacies are constrained by the coordinate
algebra D.

Proposition 2.1. Let L be a nonzero separable Hilbert space, let D ⊆ B(L) be a unital norm-
separable C ∗ -algebra with Z(D) = CIL , and let P, Q ⊆ D be unital C ∗ -subalgebras containing IL .

Conversely, a central element (xn ) commutes with all finite-support D-valued sequences, so each xn

the en . The same holds for ED (Q).

Corollary 2.4. Under the hypotheses of Proposition 2.1, suppose that P has the spatial tensor-norm
identity relative to D, whereas Q does not. Then ED (P ) and ED (Q) are not spatially conjugate. If
W ∈ B(L) is unitary and W P W ∗ = Q, then nevertheless

                                        dKK (ED (P ), ED (Q)) ≤ 2 ∥W − IL ∥ .

Proof. A spatial conjugacy would give the unitaries in Proposition 2.1(ii), contradicting Lemma 2.3.

stated tensor-norm obstruction, no unitary normalizing D can conjugate P onto Q.

5    The counterexamples
Proof of Theorem 1.1. Given ε > 0, choose 0 < t < 1 so small that 2c(t) < ε. Use the algebras
P0 , Pt ⊆ D ⊆ B(L) from Section 4, and put
                                M
                          H=          L,       A = ED (P0 ),     B = ED (Pt ).
                                n≥1

The space H is separable. Lemma 4.2 supplies the scalar center of D, so Proposition 2.1 shows that
A, B are unital norm-separable C ∗ -algebras with common identity and that A′′ = B ′′ . The same
lemma supplies a unitary W with W P0 W ∗ = Pt and ∥W − IL ∥ ≤ c(t). By Corollary 2.4,

                                           dKK (A, B) ≤ 2c(t) < ε.

Finally, Lemmas 4.3 and 4.5 show that the relative tensor-norm identity holds for P0 but not for Pt .
Corollary 2.4 therefore excludes every unitary conjugating A onto B.

   The common ideal c0 (N, D) makes the von Neumann closures agree, while the norm-limit
condition retains the distinction between the two inclusions.
