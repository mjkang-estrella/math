     The rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties

                                                  Abstract
          We prove the rational Hodge conjecture for complex abelian varieties with complex multipli-
      cation: every rational Hodge class on such a variety is a rational linear combination of algebraic
      cycle classes. As consequences, we obtain the generalized Hodge conjecture for CM abelian
      varieties, the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties over finite fields, and the Hodge standard
      conjecture for abelian varieties in arbitrary characteristic.


1    Introduction
For a smooth projective complex variety X, the rational Hodge conjecture asks whether every
rational cohomology class of type ( p, p) is a rational linear combination of codimension-p algebraic
cycle classes. We prove this assertion for abelian varieties with complex multiplication, in every
dimension and codimension.
    A complex abelian variety A is of CM type, or simply CM, if End( A) ⊗Z Q contains a commu-
tative semisimple Q-algebra of dimension 2 dim A. We include the point by convention. Finite
products and powers of CM abelian varieties are again CM.

Theorem 1.1. Let A be a complex CM abelian variety. For every integer p ≥ 0, the Betti cycle-class map

                               clB : CH p ( A)Q −→ H 2p ( A, Q) ∩ H p,p ( A)

is surjective, where CH p ( A)Q = CH p ( A) ⊗Z Q and the intersection is taken in H 2p ( A, C). In particular,
the rational Hodge conjecture holds in every codimension on every finite product of complex CM abelian
varieties and on every power of such a product.

    The theorem also has consequences beyond the algebraicity of individual CM Hodge classes.
The generalized Hodge conjecture predicts algebraic supports of the codimension allowed by the
Hodge types of a cohomology substructure. We obtain it for CM abelian varieties by applying
the theorem to correspondences from auxiliary CM varieties, following Hazama and Abdulali
[16, 1]. Two theorems of Milne then give the Tate conjecture for all abelian varieties over finite
fields, which asks for algebraic representatives of Galois-invariant ℓ-adic classes, and the Hodge
standard conjecture for abelian varieties in arbitrary characteristic, which asserts positivity of the
intersection form on primitive algebraic classes [25, 26]. Section 8 states these conclusions with
their coefficient and support conventions and explains the separate implications.

1.1    Hodge classes on CM abelian varieties
The codimension-one case of the Hodge conjecture is the Lefschetz (1, 1) theorem. Hodge for-
mulated the broader problem by asking for an extension of this algebraicity criterion to higher-
dimensional cycles [18, p. 184]. Cup products of divisor classes therefore give algebraic Hodge
classes in higher degree. For an abelian variety, H ∗ ( A, Q) = Λ∗ H 1 ( A, Q), but this description of
its cohomology does not make every Hodge class a linear combination of divisor products. The
problem is to construct cycles for the remaining classes.
     Weil classes provide an important instance of this difficulty. Suppose an imaginary quadratic
field K acts on an abelian variety of dimension 2n, with its two embeddings occurring with equal
multiplicity in H 1,0 . Then Λ2nK H ( A, Q) supplies a two-dimensional rational space of classes of
                                    1

type (n, n). These determinant classes and their generalization to CM fields are discussed in [10,
Proposition 4.4]. Their Hodge type follows from the multiplicity condition; algebraicity requires a
geometric construction. Weil classes need not lie in the algebra generated by divisor classes; this
occurs already on abelian fourfolds [30, Theorem 0.1]. Recent work of Markman proves algebraicity
of the Weil classes on polarized abelian sixfolds of Weil type for an imaginary quadratic field,
with polarization discriminant −1, and deduces the Hodge conjecture for all abelian fourfolds [21,
Theorem 1.5.1 and Corollary 1.6.1]. The sixfold theorem assumes an imaginary quadratic action,
without requiring the full CM condition of Theorem 1.1.
     Deligne proved that every Hodge class on an abelian variety is absolute Hodge: after transport to
a conjugate variety through de Rham cohomology, it remains a Hodge class [10, Main Theorem 2.11].
This establishes an arithmetic compatibility shared by algebraic cycle classes, but does not by itself
produce algebraic cycles. CM varieties play a central role in that argument. Their Hodge groups
are tori, and their degree-one cohomology splits, after extending coefficients, into lines indexed by
field embeddings. An embedding-line tensor belongs to the scalar extension of the rational Hodge
classes exactly when every scalar conjugate has equally many holomorphic and antiholomorphic
factors. This is Pohlmann’s balance criterion [33]; see [28, §1.2(c)] and [13, Theorem 3.1].
     The CM arguments of Deligne and André reduce these Hodge classes to Weil-type classes on
auxiliary CM abelian varieties [10, 2]. More precisely, the formulation in [28, Theorem 1], attributed
there to André, expresses a CM Hodge class as a sum of pullbacks of split Weil classes. Here split
refers to the Hermitian polarization form having a totally isotropic subspace of half its dimension.
This reduction identifies classes whose algebraicity would suffice; it does not supply their algebraic
representatives in all dimensions.
     There is also a reduction to relations involving only four labels. Hazama’s arrangement method
spans its rational balance kernel by antipodal pairs and four-label square relations [16, Theo-
rem 7.13]; his Corollary 7.14.1 gives the corresponding codimension-two algebraicity reduction.
Gao and Ullmo give equivalent quadratic indicator relations and use them, after adjoining auxiliary
CM factors, to generate Hodge-induced period relations [13, Theorems 7.1–7.2]. Their conclusion
concerns relations among periods. Our task is to construct algebraic representatives for the cor-
responding four-factor tensors and to compose those representatives as correspondences. This
distinction matters: the reduction uses auxiliary factors and polarization contractions, rather than
asserting that low-codimension classes generate the Hodge ring of a fixed power.

1.3    Theta forms and their CM sources
Section 4 constructs the period on a compact arithmetic quotient of the complex two-ball. Its unitary
group has signature (2, 1) at one distinguished embedding and is definite at all the other real places.
After the elementary normalization in Section 2, all four lines U (vi )1 are holomorphic at that
embedding. To each type the construction assigns a family of holomorphic theta one-forms. Their
values span a cotangent fiber, so two such families supply a wedge that is a nonzero holomorphic
two-form.
    To compare the two sides of the four-factor relation, we realize each pair of types by an
orthogonal decomposition of the same Hermitian plane. The balance relation says that the two
decompositions have the same local sign multiplicities. Weak approximation produces a rational
second decomposition whose compact-place data are as close as desired to those of the first. At the
distinguished embedding, the total period data depend only on the plane and remain unchanged.
    A rank-two theta tensor packages the wedges into one total two-form. We keep its finite theta
input and the compact quotient fixed while varying the compact-place data. The total form varies
continuously, so its pairing with the initial nonzero form stays close to the nonzero self-pairing. At
the two rational endpoints it is a finite sum of wedges of the required one-forms. Factoring these
endpoints and passing to a common finite cover therefore gives a nonzero mixed period with two
holomorphic and two antiholomorphic factors.
    The construction uses Weil’s rational theta covariance and the unitary splittings of the Weil
representation [37, 19, 5]. Kudla and Millson developed differential-form theta kernels in this

unitary setting [20, Introduction and Theorem 3]. The projection turning the rank-two tensor into
wedges has a close predecessor in the pure-cocycle product calculation of Bergeron, Millson, and
Mœglin [3, Lemmas 5.7 and 5.11]. We derive the required tensor identity in coordinates and use
the fixed-input variation to obtain the mixed period.
    These are initially complex one-form classes. The surface criterion also requires that they come
from specified rational CM Hodge structures. Sections 5 and 6 establish this arithmetic input.
First we realize the ball quotients in a projective moduli space and construct algebraic Hecke
endomorphisms of the product of its components’ Albanese varieties. Counting ordinary isogeny
neighbors gives a polynomial relation between these operators and Frobenius. We then compute
the Hecke parameters of the theta forms. The Frobenius relation and the degree-one Weil weight
force slope zero or one on the relevant Hecke summands, with the choice determined by the
prescribed CM signs.
    The Hecke projectors preserve the Hodge filtration, so weak admissibility turns these extreme
slopes into pure Hodge types. Applying the calculation to every scalar conjugate identifies the
complete CM type of each summand. Scalar descent then expresses every relevant theta class
as a complex linear combination of images of the selected CM line under rational Hodge maps.
Section 7 puts the finite families on one projective surface, expands the nonzero period, and applies
the surface criterion. This completes the four-factor construction and hence the proof of the main
theorem.

2     CM tensors and four-factor relations
This section reduces Theorem 1.1 to the algebraicity of a specified family of tensors in the fourth
cohomology of products of four CM abelian varieties. The reduction changes the CM types of
individual factors while preserving their total Hodge type; its intermediate factors may therefore
be absent from the original variety. We first record how to work with individual embedding lines
and recover rationality after extending coefficients. We then construct the auxiliary CM varieties
and assemble arbitrary balanced tensors from four-factor relations by algebraic correspondences.

2.2      Rank-one CM structures
For a polarizable rational Hodge structure H of weight one, let J ( H ) be the smallest rational
algebraic subgroup of GL( H ) containing the Hodge circle. The circle acts by z on H 1,0 and by z̄ on
H 0,1 , for |z| = 1. It preserves an alternating polarization. Its connectedness shows that it lies in the
rational identity component of J ( H ); minimality therefore makes J ( H ) connected.

Lemma 2.3. For a complex abelian variety A, the following conditions are equivalent:
     (i) J ( H 1 ( A, Q)) is a torus.
    (ii) H 1 ( A, Q) is a direct sum of rational Hodge structures, each of rank one over a CM field acting by
         Hodge endomorphisms.
    (iii) End( A) ⊗Z Q contains a commutative semisimple algebra of rational dimension 2 dim A.

Proof. The zero-dimensional case is immediate. Put H = H 1 ( A, Q), and first assume that J ( H )
is a torus. Its associative envelope E0 ⊂ EndQ ( H ) is a commutative semisimple algebra: after
splitting the torus, its elements act diagonally by scalars on the weight spaces, so the envelope is a
subalgebra of a product of fields and is reduced. This description descends to Q.
    Let ∗ be the polarization adjoint. It preserves E0 , since it sends every symplectic torus element
to its inverse. Every element of E0 commutes with the complex structure I supplied by the Hodge
circle. Consequently ∗ is also the adjoint for the positive definite real form obtained by inserting I
in the polarization. In particular,

                                        Tr HR ( a∗ a) > 0   (0 ̸= a ∈ E0 ⊗Q R).

The involution cannot exchange two primitive factors of the real finite étale algebra E0 ⊗ R: for the
idempotent e of one factor, this would give e∗ e = 0. On a complex factor it cannot be the identity,
since for its idempotent e one would have (ie)∗ (ie) = −e, of negative trace. Thus it acts as complex
conjugation on each complex factor. There are no real factors. On the nonzero module of such a
factor the circle would act by real scalars, and a continuous homomorphism from the circle to R×
is trivial; this contradicts the weight-one characters.
     It follows that each rational field factor K of E0 is CM. Indeed, it has only complex archimedean
factors, and its involution satisfies τ ( a∗ ) = τ ( a) at every embedding. Its fixed field is therefore
totally real and has index two. On the corresponding K-module the circle acts by K ⊗ R-scalars.
Splitting that module into K-lines thus gives rational Hodge substructures. This proves (ii), without
discarding multiplicities.
     The separate scalar actions on the field lines in (ii) form a commutative semisimple Hodge-
endomorphism algebra of dimension dimQ H. Lemma 2.2 realizes it in End( A) ⊗ Q, proving (iii).
Conversely, write an algebra as in (iii) as ∏ j K j . Its faithful action on H has a positive rank m j over
each K j , and

6     CM sources of the theta one-forms
We now prove Theorem 5.1, which identifies the rational CM sources of the theta classes. Section 5
constructed an algebraic Hecke action and a cubic relation with Frobenius. We calculate the active

to isolate one Frobenius slope on every full character space. The Hecke projectors preserve the
Hodge filtration, so these slopes determine the Hodge types of all scalar conjugates. Scalar descent
will then give the required rational Hodge maps.

8     Generalized Hodge and arithmetic consequences
Theorem 1.1 applies to products of CM abelian varieties and therefore algebraizes rational Hodge
correspondences between them. We use this to obtain the algebraic supports predicted by the
generalized Hodge conjecture, following Abdulali’s effective-realization argument [1, Lemma 1
and Theorems 3–4]. Hazama had earlier reduced this implication to codimension-two Hodge
classes on a special family of CM abelian varieties [16, Theorem 8.3]. We then apply Milne’s
Hodge-to-Tate and polarization theorems to obtain the Tate and Hodge standard conjectures for
abelian varieties. A final application uses CM transport to algebraize specialized Hodge classes on
arbitrary good-reduction abelian varieties.

8.1    The generalized Hodge conjecture
A pure rational Hodge structure is effective if all its nonzero Hodge components have nonnegative
indices. Recall that V (r ) p,q = V p+r,q+r , so the twist V (r ) has weight wt(V ) − 2r. The Hodge circle
of a pure Hodge structure V acts on V p,q by z p−q , for |z| = 1. Let J (V ) be the smallest algebraic
subgroup of GL(V ) over Q whose real points contain that circle. A polarizable V is of CM type
if J (V ) is a torus, with the trivial group allowed. Every Hodge substructure of the cohomology
of a CM abelian variety is of CM type: its Hodge-circle group is an image of the torus acting on
degree-one cohomology.

Remark 8.5 (Algebraicity of specialized Hodge classes). Theorem 1.1 also combines with the CM
transport of [32, Corollary 3.4] to give a specialization statement for abelian varieties that need
not be CM. Fix a prime p and a p-adic place of Q. If A/Q has good reduction A0 /F p at that

place, every rational Hodge class on A specializes to the class of one rational algebraic cycle on A0 ,
simultaneously in all prime-to-p realizations and in crystalline cohomology after extension to the

of a specialized CM Hodge class by a rational homomorphism of special fibers. Theorem 1.1
algebraizes the CM class; specialization and Chow pullback after clearing the homomorphism’s
