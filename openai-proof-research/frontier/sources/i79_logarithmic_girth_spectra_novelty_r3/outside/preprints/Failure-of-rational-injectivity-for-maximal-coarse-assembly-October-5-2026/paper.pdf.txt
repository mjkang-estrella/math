                                            Abstract
         We construct a uniformly discrete bounded-geometry space whose maximal coarse
      assembly map has an infinite-order element in its kernel. The space is a coarse disjoint
      union of finite connected graphs of uniformly bounded degree, so maximal coarse
      assembly need not be rationally injective even for such graph unions.


1    Introduction
Coarse assembly relates the topology visible at bounded scales in a metric space to the
indices of operators controlled by its large-scale geometry. Its rational injectivity asks
whether a non-torsion topological class can have zero coarse index. We prove that this can
occur even when the analytic target is the maximal Roe algebra and the space is a coarse
disjoint union of finite graphs of bounded degree.

1.1   Context and prior work
Roe developed coarse cohomology and coarse index theory as tools for large-scale geometry
and index-theoretic questions on noncompact manifolds [15]. Higson and Roe formulated
and studied the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture in this framework [10]. The connection
with Novikov-type questions is that injectivity retains the topological information carried
by higher indices. The present theorem concerns the non-equivariant coarse assembly map
displayed above.
     Several fundamental positive results relate assembly to geometric control. Yu proved
coarse assembly isomorphism for bounded-geometry spaces of finite asymptotic dimension [20,
Theorem 7.1] and established the coarse Baum–Connes conjecture for bounded-geometry
spaces admitting a uniform embedding into Hilbert space [21]. Skandalis, Tu, and Yu
developed the coarse-groupoid approach and its relation to these embedding methods [17].
These results explain the importance of geometric hypotheses when seeking injectivity or
isomorphism theorems.
     Earlier failures of injectivity also show why bounded geometry and the domain of
assembly matter. Yu constructed a coarse-assembly kernel class for a space without bounded
geometry [20, Section 8, Proposition 8.1]. Dranishnikov, Ferry, and Weinberger constructed
integral index-kernel examples with locally finite K-homology as domain; their kernel classes
disappear under coarsening [6, Section 6, Remark 6.2]. Here the class belongs to the actual
Rips colimit for a bounded-geometry graph union and remains nonzero after rationalization.
     Finite quotients and expanding graphs also exposed limitations of general assembly
assertions. Higson, Lafforgue, and Skandalis constructed counterexamples in group and
groupoid settings and proved reduced coarse nonsurjectivity for certain unions of congruence
Cayley graphs [9, Section 6]. Nonsurjectivity is a different obstruction from the kernel in
Theorem 1.1. Their separate Hausdorff-groupoid kernel construction combines an odd Bott
class with a Kazhdan projection: a nonzero full-assembly image becomes zero after reduction
[9, Section 5]. This is a methodological predecessor, but its behavior also makes it essential
to distinguish the two analytic targets.
     The maximal Roe algebra and its assembly map were developed in work of Gong,
Wang, and Yu [7] and Oyono-Oyono and Yu [14]; the latter analyzed maximal assembly for
specific families of expanders arising from finite group quotients. Willett and Yu further
studied higher indices for expanding and large-girth graph families [19]. Špakula and
Willett proved that the maximal-to-reduced comparison induces a K-theory isomorphism
for spaces admitting a coarse embedding into Hilbert space [18]. Chen, Wang, and Yu
proved the maximal coarse Baum–Connes conjecture under the more general hypothesis of
a fibred coarse embedding into Hilbert space [4]. More recent injectivity results concern
finite-group extensions whose kernel and quotient families admit such fibred embeddings [5];
maximal isomorphism results also hold for coarse fibrations with uniformly property-A fibers,
a fibred-coarsely-embeddable base, and uniform coarse equivalences between fibers and
preimages of fixed-radius balls [8]. Finite fibers alone do not supply these uniform hypotheses.
Theorem 1.1 shows that rational injectivity cannot hold for all bounded-geometry graph
unions without further assumptions.

1.2   The construction and the proof
The base graphs are three-dimensional discrete tori Bj whose side lengths tend to infinity.
Over each Bj we place a finite quotient of SL3 (Z), obtaining a graph Yj with Cayley edges
inside the fibers. Transport around the first two base circles is given by commuting elements
of that quotient. These elements cease to commute at a higher congruence level. The graph
Xj joins two copies of Bj through Yj : its middle has a long product collar, and toward
either end the fiber quotients are reduced one level at a time until they become trivial. This
keeps all vertex degrees bounded. Section 2 gives the construction.

and all edge lengths are at least one: a ball of radius R contains no more vertices than a

4     Maximal Roe algebras and the coarse index
The infinite-order class is now constructed. It remains to show that its two end contributions
have the same maximal index. The subsequent averaging and sliding arguments will use
operator formulas on the standard modules. We therefore establish the norm estimates
that allow those formulas to be used in the maximal completion, and then verify the
required naturality of the index. These are standard features of maximal Roe theory; see
[14, Section 2] and [16, Section 2]. We give the details needed here because the distinction
between operator norm and maximal norm is essential to the proof.
