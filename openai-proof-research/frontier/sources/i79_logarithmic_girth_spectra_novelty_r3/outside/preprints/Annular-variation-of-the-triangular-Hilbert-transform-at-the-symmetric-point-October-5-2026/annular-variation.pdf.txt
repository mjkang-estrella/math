           Annular variation of the triangular Hilbert transform
                          at the symmetric point

                                              Abstract
        We prove the annular r-variation estimate for the triangular Hilbert transform from complex
     L3 × L3 to L3/2 for every r > 2. The partitions may depend on the output point and range
     over all positive scales. The estimate yields the two-endpoint maximal bound and joint almost-
     everywhere and norm principal values, and resolves the symmetric scalar triangular Hilbert
     transform problem.

1.1   History and related work
The flat operator occurs in Demeter and Thiele’s study of bilinear averages for two commuting
transformations [3, Section 6]. The scalar form (1.3) is a basic entangled singular integral: each input
sees one pair of the three variables, and the pairs form a cycle. This dependence differs from the
bipartite configurations in many multilinear estimates. Kovač, Thiele and Zorin-Kranich established
estimates for a Walsh model with two general inputs and a third input of specified structure [15,
Theorem 1.7]. Their paper also records the cyclic trace formulation and the equivalence of the flat
and simplex forms.
    Quantitative cancellation over a finite range of scales was developed in several stages. Tao proved
sublogarithmic bounds for one-dimensional multilinear Hilbert transforms [21], and Zorin-Kranich
obtained sublogarithmic cancellation for simplex Hilbert transforms [22]. Durcik, Kovač, Škreb and
Thiele proved square-function and norm-variation estimates that yield square-root cancellation for
the triangular form over finitely many smooth scales [7, Corollary 5]. Durcik, Kovač and Thiele
obtained power-type cancellation
                               √︁ for hard truncations [6, Theorem 1]. Permuting and interpolating
their (4, 4, 2) bounds gives a log(R/ε) bound at the symmetric tuple. These estimates retain a
dependence on the scale range. Moreover, norm-variation estimates for a fixed list of scales do not
place the supremum over output-dependent partitions inside the output norm as in (1.2).
    Other results gain cancellation by averaging a direction parameter [8, 19] or by introducing
curvature [1, 11]. Those mechanisms are distinct from the flat problem considered here. The broader
role of variation in strengthening maximal estimates and controlling convergence is illustrated by
Lépingle’s martingale inequality [17], the variational inequalities of Jones, Seeger and Wright [12],
and the variation-norm Carleson theorem [20]. These results provide context rather than an input
to our proof.
    The energy method here is related to telescoping arguments for entangled forms: Kovač’s twisted
paraproduct and Bellman-function framework [14, 13], and Durcik’s continuous Gaussian telescoping
[4, 5]. The obstruction presented by a triangular cycle is discussed in [13, Section 6.2]. We work
with the finite matrix energy tr(P 3/2 ) for P ≥ 0, establish a mixed trace inequality, and compare its
heat derivatives at neighboring vertices. All matrix and heat estimates required below are proved in
this paper.

    For smooth annuli, we write the kernel as a scale integral of convolutions of three one-variable
Gaussian derivative windows. Each convolution is an integral over window centers whose sum is
zero; with the centers fixed, the three window factors depend separately on a, b, c. On a finite grid
the resulting sums are cyclic matrix traces: the sampled pairwise inputs supply three matrices, and
the window factors supply diagonal insertions. We localize the matrices with positive Gaussian
weights. At each vertex we place the two incident weighted matrices side by side to form a row
matrix R. The energy tr((R∗ R)3/2 ) has the same degree-three scaling as the trilinear form. A
mixed trace inequality bounds the localized trace by quadratic costs from the Hessians of these
energies. With the matrices fixed, heat identities control the accumulated costs by initial energies
after integration over centers. Those integrated energies are bounded by sums of cubed L3 norms.
    There are two different costs in passing from one annulus to many. For smooth annuli, entries of
the matrix formed from the dual input switch on and off at their chosen endpoints. We estimate
                                                                       √
the energy jumps entrywise, then rescale the dual input to obtain a n bound. For hard annuli,
estimating each endpoint error separately would give a linear loss. We instead group endpoints in
dyadic radius intervals. Disjointness gives uniform size and derivative bounds for each group even
when it has many jumps.
    The main additional analytic tool is the frequency-block estimate of Proposition 6.1. It handles
modulated Gaussian windows with coefficients depending jointly on scale, frequency, and output
position. For uniformly bounded coefficients, a pointwise square-integral bound over scales and
frequencies suffices, uniformly in the frequency size. To obtain that uniformity, we narrow one
Gaussian variance and average its center shifts. This expresses each oscillatory insertion as an
average of derivatives of narrower Gaussians. Auxiliary heat flows retaining only one input edge
control the extra derivative terms. A two-dimensional Gaussian estimate then bounds the energy
changes at the ends of the scale intervals without a loss from the narrow variance. This estimate
and the rough-kernel family bound in Proposition 7.2 are formulated separately from the annular
application.
