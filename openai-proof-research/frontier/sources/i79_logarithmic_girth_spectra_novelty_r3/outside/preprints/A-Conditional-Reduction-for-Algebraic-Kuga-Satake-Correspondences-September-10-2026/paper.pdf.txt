                                                 Abstract
          For a polarized K3 surface whose primitive cohomology has full orthogonal Hodge group,
      one algebraic correspondence inducing a nonzero map from that cohomology to the second
      cohomology of an abelian variety suffices to recover the prescribed full Kuga–Satake corre-
      spondence. We give an equivalent condition using holomorphic one-forms on a generically
      finite surface cover. If this input holds very generally in a polarized component, the prescribed
      correspondence is algebraic throughout that component, for every choice of standard data on
      the transcendental part. The existence of the initial correspondence remains a hypothesis.

1     Introduction and statements
The Kuga–Satake construction associates an abelian variety to a polarized Hodge structure of K3
type. Its original purpose was to place the second cohomology of a K3 surface inside a tensor
construction on the first cohomology of an abelian variety [12]. The resulting embedding is a
Hodge morphism. Its realization by an algebraic correspondence is a further assertion, usually
called the algebraicity of the Kuga–Satake correspondence; see [10, Chapter 4] and [6]. This
assertion is a particular instance of the rational Hodge conjecture.
    We prove two conditional reductions for this algebraicity problem. At a point where the
primitive cohomology has full orthogonal Hodge group, an algebraic correspondence inducing any
nonzero map from primitive cohomology to the second cohomology of an abelian variety suffices
to recover the specified map into the full even-Clifford target. Very-general existence of such a
map then implies algebraicity of the specified correspondence at every point of the same polarized
component. These reductions do not establish the existence of the initial map and therefore do
not resolve the unrestricted Kuga–Satake conjecture.
    The proof uses three devices which may be useful in related algebraicity questions. Independent
operations on the two weight-one factors recover every multiplicity, and rational descent handles
nonsplit Clifford algebras. Proper Hilbert parameter spaces specialize the entire prescribed flat
class, including its rational coefficients, over an analytic period chart. Finally, signed square roots
compare quadratic polarizations through operations on the abelian factors. This last step does not
require algebraicity of a Hodge endomorphism of the K3 surface.

1.1    Conventions and the exact target
All varieties in the statements are over C, and cohomology is rational Betti cohomology unless a
different field is displayed. We write CHr (−)Q for codimension-r cycles modulo rational equivalence
with rational coefficients. A subscript records dimension, so CHa denotes cycles of dimension a.
We write NS(S) for the Néron–Severi group, Hg for the Hodge group, HomHS for rational Hodge
maps, and F • for the Hodge filtration. The Tate twist (1) shifts Hodge bidegrees by (−1, −1).

Theorem 1.2 (Specialization in a polarized component). Fix a component of the moduli space
of primitively amply polarized K3 surfaces of degree 2d. Assume that condition (i) or condition

     Here it suffices that, about each desired point, the input holds outside a countable union of
proper closed analytic subsets of a full polarized period chart. In particular an input outside
countably many proper algebraic subsets of the moduli component suffices. The witness cycles,
abelian varieties, or surface covers need not vary in a family.

Corollary 1.3. If the hypothesis of Theorem 1.2 holds in the required polarized components in
every degree, then (1.2) holds for every projective K3 surface.

Proof. Every projective K3 surface has an ample integral class. Divide it to primitivity in H 2 (S, Z);
the resulting integral (1, 1) class is the first Chern class of a line bundle, a positive power of which
is ample. Hence it is itself ample, and its square is a positive even integer. Apply Theorem 1.2 in
its component.

    An input in one polarized component yields the conclusion in that component. Constructing
a very-general correspondence or surface cover with the stated property remains the external
existence problem. The hypotheses concern actual algebraic cycles; absolute Hodge or motivated
classes do not supply this input.

4     Recovering the full Clifford correspondence at a general point
The argument in this section uses the equality

                   Hg(V ) = SO(V, q),        V = h⊥ ⊂ H 2 (S, Q),        dim V = 21.                 (4.1)

There is no genericity assumption on the abelian variety that supplies the initial algebraic
correspondence. The principal point is to recover every multiplicity in the full Clifford construction
from one nonzero map.

4.1    The generic Hodge group
Proposition 4.1. In any sufficiently small, full-dimensional marked polarized period chart,
Equation (4.1) holds outside a countable union of proper closed analytic subsets. At every point
satisfying Equation (4.1), the Hodge structure V is irreducible and T (S) = V .

Proof. Polarized local Torelli identifies such a chart with an open subset of the domain of oriented
positive two-planes in the fixed real quadratic space VR ; see [10, Chapter 6]. The infinitesimal
generators of the Hodge circles over any such open subset span so(VR , q). Indeed, under the natural
identification so(VR , q) ≃ Λ2 VR , each generator is a nonzero multiple of the bivector of its positive
plane. A linear functional vanishing on all these bivectors is an alternating bilinear form vanishing
on an open set of ordered pairs of vectors. It therefore vanishes identically.
    The Hodge group is a connected rational algebraic subgroup of SO(V, q). Connectedness
follows because its rational identity component contains the connected Hodge circle and hence,
by minimality, is the whole group. If this subgroup is proper, its Lie algebra is a nonzero proper
rational subspace L ⊂ so(V, q). Set r = dim L, and choose a rational generator τL of Λr L.
Conjugation by the circle preserves L and acts trivially on its real determinant line: a continuous
character from a compact connected circle to R× is trivial. Thus τL , viewed in Λr End(V ), has
Hodge type (0, 0).
    There are only countably many possible rational subspaces L. For each one, the condition that
τL have type (0, 0) is closed analytic in the period chart. In fact the tensor has weight zero and
is real, so this condition is equivalent to τL ∈ F 0 , a holomorphic condition. The locus is proper.
Otherwise LR would be preserved by every circle in the chart, hence by the adjoint actions of their
infinitesimal generators. The spanning assertion above would make LC an ideal of the simple Lie
algebra so21 (C), a contradiction.
    Finally, the standard representation of SO(V, q) is absolutely irreducible and has no nonzero
invariants. Consequently V has no proper nonzero rational Hodge substructure and no nonzero
rational vector of type (1, 1). Since the primitive part of NS(S)Q consists of such vectors,
NS(S)Q = Qh and T (S) = V .

Assume Equation (4.1). Let B be a complex abelian variety, put U = H 1 (B, Q), and write

                               W + = C + (V, q),         W − = C − (V, q)

for the full Clifford parity spaces with their weight-one Hodge structures. The Hodge circle on
these spaces acts by left multiplication by its lift to Spin(V, q). Let H be the joint Hodge group of
V, U, W + , W − , and let h be its rational Lie algebra.

Lemma 4.2. There is a decomposition into commuting rational ideals

                                    h = g ⊕ k,          g ≃ so(V, q),

where k is the kernel of the action on V . The ideal k acts trivially on W + and W − , and g acts on
them by the usual left spin action.

Proof. The group H is connected by the same circle argument as in Proposition 4.1. It is reductive.
To see this directly, each of the four pure summands is semisimple as a rational H-module: an
invariant rational subspace is a Hodge substructure, and a polarization provides a rational Hodge
complement. This is the usual semisimplicity argument for polarizable Hodge structures; compare
[2, Proposition 3.6]. Their direct sum is a faithful semisimple module. The unipotent radical has
nonzero invariants in each irreducible constituent; normality makes those invariants H-stable, so it
acts trivially on every constituent. Faithfulness forces the radical to be trivial.
    Projection to V maps H onto Hg(V ) = SO(V, q). Indeed, its closed rational image contains the
projected circle and hence contains Hg(V ). Conversely, the preimage of Hg(V ) contains the joint
circle, so minimality places H inside that preimage. The reductive rational Lie algebra h is the
direct sum of its center and its rational simple ideals. Exactly one simple ideal maps nontrivially
to the absolutely simple quotient so(V, q): its image is the whole quotient, and the commuting
images of all other ideals must vanish. The nonzero map from that simple ideal is an isomorphism.
This gives the asserted rational decomposition.
    On V ⊕ W + ⊕ W − , the image of H is contained in the rational algebraic image of Spin(V, q)

Remark 6.3. The proof never uses an algebraic correspondence representing P , any pi , or any Rs
on the K3 surface. They enter only the identities that produce weight-one intertwiners and the
finite rational descent. In particular P need not be scalar. The argument applies with additional
real multiplication or complex multiplication endomorphisms, and requires no description of the
special Hodge group. It applies also in ranks 2, 3, and 4. In rank 2, the full even-Clifford space is
two-dimensional and Spin2 → SO2 , over an algebraic closure, is the squaring map on Gm with
kernel µ2 . The faithful left Clifford action and the connected-lift argument remain valid. Higher
small ranks introduce no exception. We make no assertion that all Hodge embeddings for a smaller
special Hodge group are generated by these operations.

7    Completion of the reductions
Proof of Theorem 1.1. Proposition 4.1 gives T = V . Proposition 2.4 proves the equivalence
of conditions (i) and (ii). From condition (i), Theorem 4.5 realizes the full primitive Clifford
multiplication. Proposition 6.2 and Lemma 3.2 then give condition (iii) with the stipulated data.
Conversely, condition (iii) gives condition (i) by taking B = AS × AS and α = ΓS .

Proof of Theorem 1.2. By Proposition 2.4, either assumed input gives the nonzero correspondence
very generally. Corollary 5.5, followed by the inverse right multiplier in Lemma 3.2, algebraically
realizes mq on V at the desired point. Proposition 6.1 gives mq|T on T . Proposition 6.2 and
Lemma 3.2 then give the prescribed quadratic form, parity identification, duality, and isogenous
realization, hence (1.2). The operations on the abelian factors preserve the degree shift, so the
resulting cycle has codimension two.

possible transcendental ranks. They require the prescribed multiplication tensor at those points;
they do not assert generation of every Hodge embedding for a smaller special Hodge group. The
mathematical content of the reduction is conditional: producing a very-general nonzero algebraic
