                      Integrability of split tangent bundles
                        on rationally connected manifolds

                                              Abstract
        We prove that both summands of every specified holomorphic splitting of the tangent
     bundle of a smooth rationally connected projective complex manifold into two positive-rank
     subbundles are integrable. This proves Höring’s conjecture on rationally connected projective
     manifolds. Höring’s product theorem then gives a product decomposition compatible with
     the specified splitting.


1    Introduction
A product of complex manifolds has a tangent bundle split into the pullbacks of the tangent
bundles of its factors. A converse must keep track of more than the abstract bundles. For a
specified decomposition
                                       TX = E1 ⊕ E2 ,
the embeddings of E1 and E2 in TX are part of the data, and a compatible product must identify
these subbundles with its factor directions. A necessary local condition is integrability: the
holomorphic local sections of each Ei must be closed under the Lie bracket.
    We prove that rational connectedness forces this local condition. Here and below, “two
general points” means all pairs in some nonempty Zariski open subset of X × X. A smooth
connected projective complex manifold is rationally connected if two general points lie on the
image of a morphism P1 → X.

Theorem 1.1 (Automatic integrability). Let X be a smooth connected projective complex
manifold of dimension at least two. Suppose that two general points of X lie on the image of a
morphism P1 → X. For every specified holomorphic decomposition

                                           TX = E1 ⊕ E2

into subbundles of positive rank, both E1 and E2 are integrable.

   The theorem has no further rank or positivity hypothesis. It proves the smooth rationally
connected assertion formulated in [8, Conjecture 1.5]. Höring’s earlier product theorem then
answers the global question for the same specified splitting.

Proof. Höring’s [6, Theorem 1.4] applies to a smooth connected rationally connected projective
complex manifold with a specified splitting of its tangent bundle and at least one integrable
summand. By Theorem 1.1, these hypotheses hold. His theorem gives an isomorphism with a
product that identifies the specified summands with the factor tangent bundles. Each factor
is smooth. It is projective because its realization as a slice of the product is a closed complex
submanifold of X, and a projective embedding of X restricts to an embedding of that slice.

Context and scope
Beauville studied how a tangent-bundle splitting on a compact Kähler manifold can lead to
a product of its universal cover [3]. An abstract product need not make a given summand
integrable: Höring’s [6, Example 2.14] gives a nonintegrable summand on a product of an abelian
surface with P1 . Rational connectedness rules out that example and supplies the abundance of
rational curves used in our proof.
    Several earlier results establish compatible products under positivity or rank assumptions.
Campana and Peternell treated Fano manifolds with a two-summand splitting when one summand
has rank one or two, and hence every two-summand splitting on a Fano manifold of dimension at
most five [4, Theorems 3.5–3.6 and Corollary 3.7]. Höring’s [6, Theorem 1.4] showed that, for a
rationally connected projective manifold, integrability of one summand already gives a compatible
product. He then isolated the remaining question by conjecturing that at least one summand is
integrable [7, Conjecture 1.2]. His later low-rank result applies to a uniruled projective manifold
whose tangent bundle splits into summands all of rank at most two, and proves that at least one
of them is integrable [7, Lemma 4.21].
    The recent Fano-type theorem [8, Theorem 1.1] establishes integrability with algebraic leaves
for split tangent sheaves on normal Q-factorial projective klt varieties of Fano type. In the
smooth Fano case it yields an actual compatible product [8, Corollary 1.3]. The present theorem
removes this positivity condition for smooth rationally connected manifolds. Smoothness matters:
[8, Example 4.3] gives a singular rationally connected example with a nonintegrable summand.
Section 5 applies the smooth theorem to general fibers of the rationally connected quotient,
supplying the integrability premise in Höring’s structure theorems for uniruled compact Kähler
manifolds.

Proof strategy
The construction that drives the proof does not require rational connectedness. Given any
holomorphic map g : P1 → X into a smooth projective manifold with a split tangent bundle, we
construct
                           F : P1 × P1 −→ X,     F (s, s) = g(s),
with the first factor direction tangent to E1 and the second tangent to E2 . We call F a tangent
box. The name records these tangencies; the map may have critical points and need not give
local coordinates.
    Write Pi : TX → Ei ⊂ TX for the holomorphic projectors defined by the splitting. The diagonal
first carries a formal solution. In coordinates u = s, v = t − s, the equation ∂v Fb = P2 (Fb )∂u Fb

recursively separates the derivative of g into its two summands. The positive normal bundle of the
diagonal then bounds the number of global formal sections of a twisted line bundle. Comparing
that bound with pairs of polynomial sections forces the formal projective coordinate ratios to be
rational. A local cancellation makes these ratios holomorphic near a diagonal patch. Finally,
on a resolution of the rational map, the two tangency identities force every exceptional curve
to be contracted. The resulting map is holomorphic on the entire product. The section-count
argument is given directly for holomorphic formal series; the algebraic formal-function literature
provides broader context [5, 1].
    The splitting also provides the projected-bracket partial connection ∇iξ e = Pi [ξ, e] on Ei in
directions of the complementary summand. On a tangent box it gives transport along the fibers
of pri , which vary the complementary factor, and hence
                                         F ∗ Ei ≃ pr∗i (g ∗ Ei ),
normalized to be the identity on the diagonal. Thus, on a factor curve, the corresponding
summand retains the bundle g ∗ Ei , while the complementary summand is trivial.
    Rational connectedness enters only in the last step. A two-point family of rational curves
supplies sections of g ∗ Ei that vanish at one marked point and span the summand at another.
Applying the bracket tensor 2 Ei → Ej to two such sections on a factor curve gives a section of
                               V

a trivial bundle on P1 . It is constant and vanishes at the first mark, so it vanishes at the second.
The spanning property then kills the bracket tensor on an open set of X, and hence everywhere.
The use of rational curves to annihilate this tensor has a direct antecedent in [6, Section 2.B and
Lemma 3.18]; the tangent box supplies the trivial complementary target in arbitrary rank.

2.3   Complementary tangency removes indeterminacy
Rational reconstruction has produced a map only away from its base points. We next use the
specified splitting to show that no base point can remain: each exceptional curve would have to
map tangentially to both complementary summands, forcing its image to be a point.

                            dR(T (1) ) ⊂ R∗ E1 ,        dR(T (2) ) ⊂ R∗ E2 .

a holomorphic two-form on that curve and therefore vanishes. Horizontal sections satisfy an
everywhere nonsingular holomorphic linear ordinary differential equation. Local existence and
uniqueness give continuation along every path, with reverse transport as its inverse. Flatness
and simple connectedness of P1 make this transport path-independent. Starting at the fiber’s
diagonal point therefore identifies its bundle with the corresponding fiber of g ∗ Ei .
    These identifications depend holomorphically on both variables. Fix a base point and a path
in its fiber from the diagonal to a desired endpoint. Cover the path by a finite chain of coordinate
disks and bundle frames, and shrink the base neighborhood so that the same chain applies to
nearby fibers. In each disk the coefficient matrix of the horizontal equation is holomorphic in
the base and fiber coordinates. Picard iteration converges normally on smaller disks by the
exponential-series estimate, so its solution matrix is holomorphic in the base and the start and
end coordinates. Composing these local transports and frame changes gives a holomorphic
identification near the chosen point of S. Path independence makes the local identifications
agree globally. Transport at the starting diagonal point is the identity, giving Θi and its stated
normalization.

5      Consequences for the rationally connected quotient
The main theorem also applies to the general fiber of the rationally connected quotient of a
uniruled compact Kähler manifold. That fiber is a smooth projective manifold even when the
ambient manifold is not projective. Its tangent splitting is therefore integrable, which supplies
the premise in Höring’s structure theorems [7].

Corollary 5.1. Let X be a connected uniruled compact Kähler manifold with a holomorphic
splitting TX = V1 ⊕ V2 . Let Z be a general fiber of its rationally connected quotient and set
Wi = TZ ∩ Vi |Z . For each i = 1, 2, there is an almost-holomorphic fibration ϕi : X 99K Yi whose
general fiber Fi is rationally connected and, for compatible choices Fi ⊂ Z, satisfies

                                       TFi = Wi |Fi ⊂ Vi |Fi .

If Wi = 0, one may take ϕi = idX , with point fibers.
    If rank V1 = 2, exactly one of the following holds:

    (i) W1 = V1 |Z , and X admits an analytic fiber bundle X → Y with rationally connected general
        fiber and TX/Y = V1 .

 (ii) W1 is a line bundle, and there is an equidimensional holomorphic map X → Y whose
      general fiber F is a rational curve with TF ⊂ V1 |F .

(iii) W1 = 0, equivalently TZ ⊂ V2 |Z .

Proof. Use the published version of [7]; its definition of an almost-holomorphic fibration includes
the identity case. Choose Z over a regular value in the fibration locus of the quotient supplied by
[7, Definition 2.9 and Theorem 2.10]. Then Z is an actual smooth connected compact submanifold
of X. It inherits a Kähler form and is rationally connected. Campana’s theorem, recalled in the
remark after [7, Definition 2.8], makes Z projective.
    By [7, Proposition 3.16], the ambient complementary projections preserve TZ . Their restric-
tions are complementary holomorphic idempotents. An idempotent has locally constant rank, so
on connected Z their images are the holomorphic subbundles Wi , and

                                           TZ = W1 ⊕ W2 .

If both ranks are positive, then dim Z ≥ 2, and Theorem 1.1 makes both Wi integrable. Otherwise
they are 0 and TZ , which are integrable as well.
    This is the general-fiber integrability hypothesis of [7, Theorem 1.3], which gives the fibrations
ϕi and the stated tangent identities. Its proof includes the identity when Wi = 0. If rank V1 = 2,
[7, Theorem 1.4] gives the three conclusions according as rank W1 is 2, 1, or 0. These ranks are
mutually exclusive and exhaustive, proving the stated trichotomy.


A     Elementary elimination of surface base points
Elimination of indeterminacy by point blowups is a classical part of the birational theory of
smooth surfaces; see [2]. The next lemma supplies exactly the resolution statement used in
Lemma 2.5. We include the argument to make explicit the finite termination of the point-blowup
process, using the colength of the local base ideal as the decreasing positive integer.

Lemma A.1. A rational map R : P1 × P1 99K PN is holomorphic outside a finite set B. A finite
sequence of point blowups over B gives a smooth projective surface µ : S ′ → P1 × P1 on which
R ◦ µ extends holomorphically. The fibers of µ are connected; its exceptional components are
smooth curves.

Proof. Clear denominators to represent R by a tuple of bihomogeneous polynomials of the same
bidegree, and remove all common factors. The factors can be taken bihomogeneous: the highest
and lowest degrees in each variable group are additive in products.
    In any product affine chart, the dehomogenized tuple h0 (x, y), . . . , hN (x, y) has no common
nonconstant factor. Otherwise bihomogenizing such a factor would give a common factor of the
original tuple. In C(x)[y], the tuple therefore has gcd one. A univariate Bézout identity, with
denominators cleared, puts a nonzero polynomial in x in the ideal (h0 , . . . , hN ). Interchanging the
variables gives a nonzero polynomial in y. The common zero set in the affine chart is finite. At
each common zero, the ideal in centered formal coordinates contains powers of both coordinates
and consequently has finite colength. There are four product affine charts, so the global base set
B is finite.
    If B = ∅, the map is already holomorphic, and S ′ = S, µ = idS give the conclusion. Assume
from now on that B ̸= ∅.
    It remains to resolve such a base point. Let

B     Two-point evaluation from rational connectedness
We prove Lemma 4.1 using only polynomial presentations of maps from P1 , analytic subsets, and
the holomorphic constant-rank theorem. This isolates the precise two-point variation property
used in the proof. For broader background on rationally connected varieties, see [10, 9].
    We first record an elementary covering observation. If A is a proper closed analytic subset
of a connected second-countable complex manifold M , then A is covered by countably many
complex submanifolds of dimension smaller than dim M . The covering submanifolds need not
be contained in A. To see this, choose a countable cover of A by sufficiently small connected
coordinate neighborhoods on which A is defined by holomorphic functions. In each neighborhood
choose a non-identically-zero defining function f . Such a function exists because a proper analytic
subset has empty interior. At a zero of f , let r ≥ 1 be its finite order. Some derivative of order
r − 1 vanishes there and has nonzero differential. Thus that point belongs to the regular zero
locus of one of the partial derivatives of f . There are countably many such derivatives and charts.
Their regular zero loci, with connected components separated, give the asserted covering.

Proof of Lemma 4.1. Embed X in PN . Every holomorphic map C = P1 → PN is represented by
a tuple of homogeneous polynomials of a degree d ≥ 0, with no common zero. Indeed its pullback
of OPN (1) is OC (d), and its homogeneous coordinate sections supply such a tuple. For fixed d,
these tuples form an open subset Md of a finite-dimensional vector space. Openness follows from
compactness of C. There is a universal holomorphic evaluation map C × Md → PN .

                                                  14
   We cover the tuples whose maps land in X by countably many manifold families all of
whose maps land in X. Start with the connected components of Md . For a current connected
submanifold M , either every tuple maps C into X, in which case accept this family, or there are
τ ∈ M and c ∈ C with G(c, τ ) ∈/ X. In the latter case

                                       {η ∈ M : G(c, η) ∈ X}

is a proper closed analytic subset of M , and contains every desired tuple in M . Use the covering
observation to replace it by countably many lower-dimensional submanifolds, and repeat the
same test on each. All desired tuples survive. Extra points introduced by the covering cause
no problem: each new manifold is tested anew. Dimension strictly decreases whenever a family
is not accepted. In dimension zero the test accepts precisely the desired points. Finite depth
and countable branching give countably many accepted families covering all the desired tuples.
On an accepted family the universal evaluation factors holomorphically through the embedded
submanifold X.
    Perform this construction for every d. The resulting countably many two-point evaluation
maps to X × X have images containing general pairs: a rational curve through two distinct points
can be reparametrized to take those values at the fixed distinct points a, b. By the convention in
the introduction, these images contain a nonempty Zariski open subset of X × X. It is also open
in the usual topology and has positive measure in smooth local coordinates.
    Suppose, for a contradiction, that every one of these evaluation maps has complex rank
everywhere less than 2 dim X. The image of each is then measure zero. Here is a direct verification
of that fact for a holomorphic map ϕ : M → X × X with the stated rank bound. We may take
M connected. On the open set of maximum rank, the constant-rank theorem places the image
locally in a submanifold of smaller dimension than X × X. Countably many local charts give
a measure-zero image. If the maximum rank is zero, this already treats all of M . Otherwise
its complement is a proper closed analytic subset. Cover that subset by countably many lower-
dimensional submanifolds and apply induction on dim M . The strict rank bound persists on
every such restriction, including the extra points of a covering submanifold. The zero-dimensional
case is immediate. This proves the asserted measure-zero property.
    A countable union of measure-zero images cannot contain that open subset. Some family
therefore has a point where two-point evaluation is submersive. Restrict its parameter space to
the nonempty open set where the differential is surjective; call the resulting manifold T .
    Finally, a parameter tangent vector differentiates the holomorphic family to a global section
of g ∗ TX . Surjectivity of the two-point evaluation differential provides, for every w ∈ Tg(b) X, such
a section with values 0, w at a, b. Projecting with Pi gives the last assertion of Lemma 4.1.
