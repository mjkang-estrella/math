                                                  Abstract
         We prove the strong isotropic constant conjecture: in each dimension, simplices are the
      unique maximizers of the isotropic constant among convex bodies. We also prove the sharp
      entropy bound h(f ) ≥ m + 12 log det Cov(f ) for every log-concave probability density on Rm ,
      with equality precisely for invertible affine images of products of one-sided exponential laws.

uniqueness of the maximizing body [6, 28]. In higher dimensions, geometric variation arguments
have imposed restrictions on possible maximizers. Rademacher proved that a simplicial polytope
maximizing the isotropic constant among all convex bodies must be a simplex [27]. More recent
restrictions on local maximizers in terms of decomposability appear in [16]. We prove the sharp
inequality for arbitrary convex bodies and classify all equality cases.

Equality holds if and only if f is, almost everywhere, the density of M (E1 , . . . , Em )T + b, where
M is an invertible real m × m matrix, b ∈ Rm , and the Ei are independent random variables with
density e−u 1{u≥0} .

    Sharpness follows from a product of m independent exponential variables of mean one: its
entropy is m and its covariance is the identity. Both sides of (1.4) increase by log | det A| under
an invertible affine change x 7→ Ax + b. Thus the theorem asserts that a log-concave density with
identity covariance has entropy at least m.
    Bobkov and Madiman developed the relation between entropy bounds for log-concave measures
and the slicing problem [1]. Fradelizi and Marín Sola formulated the sharp entropy statement
(1.4) and proved its equivalence, across dimensions, with the simplex bound [13, Conjecture 3(iii)
and Proposition 5.2]. In dimension one, Melbourne, Nayar, and Roberto proved the sharp bound,
attained by a one-sided exponential distribution [23, Theorem 1.1]. Theorem 1.2 establishes the
inequality and identifies all equality cases in every dimension. The cone reduction used here follows
the preceding geometric and entropic work; we include it both to derive the dimensional constant
and to transfer the equality classification to convex bodies.

1.1   Ideas of the proof
We first assume that f = e−V is smooth and that the Hessian of V is bounded above and below
by positive multiples of the identity. Let ∇ϕ transport standard Gaussian measure to f , and put
J = D2 ϕ. Brenier’s theorem and Caffarelli’s regularity and contraction theory supply this positive
definite matrix field and its uniform bounds [3, 5, 9, 22]. The scalar model is the transport from a
Gaussian variable to a mean-one exponential variable,

                                    t(a) = − log Φ(−a),           q(a) = t′ (a),

where Φ is the standard Gaussian distribution function. Since q is an increasing bijection onto
(0, ∞), spectral calculus defines the symmetric matrix field A = q −1 (J).
    Two features of this parametrization drive the proof. First, an affine change of the target can
be chosen so that EA = 0, where expectation is Gaussian. This nonlinear normalization is proved
by a homotopy and a compactness argument; ordinary covariance normalization does not give the
required identity. Second, differentiating the transport equation and using convexity of V gives
an energy estimate for A. The estimate retains a nonnegative term involving pairs of distinct
eigenvalues of A. Discarding that term would lose the control needed for matrix functions whose
derivatives do not commute.

5    Approximation of log-concave densities
