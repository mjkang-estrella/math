                                              Abstract
       We prove the finite case of the Benson–Etingof–Ostrik conjecture: every finite symmetric
    tensor category over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic p > 0 admits a k-linear
    exact faithful strong symmetric monoidal functor to a finite higher Verlinde category Verpn (k).
    The result includes characteristic two, and the level n may depend on the category.

1    Introduction
Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0. A finite symmetric tensor category
over k is an essentially small k-linear abelian rigid symmetric monoidal category with biexact tensor
product, finite-dimensional morphism spaces, finite-length objects, finitely many isomorphism
classes of simple objects, enough projectives, and End(1) = k. A fiber functor between such
categories is a k-linear exact faithful strong symmetric monoidal functor. The higher Verlinde
categories of Benson–Etingof–Ostrik form a tower

                                Verp (k) ⊂ Verp2 (k) ⊂ Verp3 (k) ⊂ · · ·

of finite symmetric tensor categories [2, 7]. The first level is the semisimplification of Repk (Z/p):
one quotients by morphisms f : X → Y satisfying tr(gf ) = 0 for all g : Y → X. Its simple objects
are represented by Jordan blocks of lengths 1, . . . , p − 1 [18, Section 2.3]. Thus Ver2 is the category
of vector spaces and Ver3 is the category of supervector spaces with its sign symmetry. The higher
levels need not be semisimple. They are finite abelian envelopes of quotients of the category of
tilting SL2 -modules (modules with both Weyl and dual Weyl filtrations), where the tensor ideal
generated by the Steinberg module of highest weight pn − 1 is killed at level n [2, Sections 3.1
and 4.1, Theorem 4.2]. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1. For every prime p, every algebraically closed field k of characteristic p, and every
finite symmetric tensor category C over k, there are an integer n ≥ 1 and a fiber functor

                                           C −→ Verpn (k).

    This proves the finite case of the Benson–Etingof–Ostrik conjecture [2, Conjecture 1.4], including
characteristic two. The full conjecture concerns symmetric tensor categories of moderate growth:
for each object, the lengths of its tensor powers grow at most exponentially. Its target is the union
of the higher Verlinde tower. Here both the projective generator and the finite set of simple classes
are essential. The theorem places the image in a single finite level, whose height may depend on C;
it makes no assertion about arbitrary nonfinite categories of moderate growth.
    A finite symmetric tensor category is incompressible if every k-linear exact symmetric tensor
functor out of it is fully faithful. Theorem 1.1 and the higher Verlinde subcategory classification
of Benson–Etingof–Ostrik [2] classify these finite categories, giving the finite case of Coulembier–
Etingof–Ostrik’s Conjecture B [9]. In characteristic two, their subterminality theorem further
gives uniqueness up to tensor isomorphism of the functor into the tower union. Their generalized
Tannaka theorem also realizes every finite category as representations of an affine group scheme
internal to a chosen finite level, with the canonical fundamental-group compatibility. Section 8
gives the precise statements and deductions.

Background and contribution
In characteristic zero, Deligne’s theorem makes supervector spaces a common target for fiber
functors from symmetric tensor categories of moderate growth [12]. In positive characteristic, Ostrik
proved that symmetric fusion categories admit fiber functors to Verp [19, Theorem 1.5]. Etingof–
Ostrik constructed the ordinary Frobenius functor for nonsemisimple categories; Coulembier
independently constructed its underlying additive functor as the external Frobenius twist [6,
Section 4.3]. Etingof–Ostrik established its symmetric monoidal structure and characterized the
finite categories on which it is exact by the existence of a fiber functor to Verp [18, Proposition 5.1

                                                   2
and Theorem 8.1]. Coulembier–Etingof–Ostrik extended that characterization to categories of
moderate growth [8].
    The higher levels are needed because the ordinary Frobenius functor need not be exact. The
characteristic-two examples of Benson–Etingof [1] led to the all-prime tower of Benson–Etingof–
Ostrik [2], constructed independently through abelian envelopes by Coulembier [7]. Coulembier–
Flake developed higher Frobenius functors from elementary abelian group representations [11].
Their construction becomes symmetric monoidal once a specific restricted-tilting conjecture is
established. Besides the rank-one case, the conjecture was already known for p = r = 2 [11,
Remark 5.4.4(1) and Section 6]. Their subsequent work proved that assertion for substantial
classes of modules, including modules of Loewy length at most two and Carlson modules, and
for the subcategory they generate under tensor products, duals, syzygy shifts, finite sums, and
summands [10, Theorem 2]. For rank two they also established the assertion for every cyclic
summand of the relevant Steinberg tensor product [10, Theorem 3].
    Our independent representation-theoretic contribution is the full restricted-tilting conjecture,
proved in Theorem 3.1. For r ≥ 1, embed Er = (Z/p)r in the upper unipotent subgroup of SL2 (k),
and write R for restriction along this embedding. Let Str−1 be the simple SL2 -module of highest
weight pr−1 − 1. For every finite-dimensional Er -module D, we show that

                                       R(Str−1 ) ⊗ D ∼
                                                     = R(T )

for a tilting SL2 -module T . This is [11, Conjecture 5.4.2(1)], also formulated as [10, Conjecture 1].
The proof lifts D to the additive group, extends the associated equivariant vector bundle from
the punctured plane across the origin, and uses a formal comparison of fibers after tensoring by
the Steinberg module. The bounded-weight tilting input is the established [2, Lemma 3.3], in the
extension-closed form also used in [10, proof of Lemma 3.1.6]. The geometric comparison is the
additional step that treats arbitrary D.

Proof strategy
The restricted-tilting theorem supplies the hypothesis for the Coulembier–Flake construction.
For each embedding of Er as above, Section 4 therefore produces an additive strong symmetric
monoidal functor

Here ⊠ denotes the Deligne tensor product of finite abelian categories. The functor is initially
                         r
semilinear: Φ(af ) = ap Φ(f ) for a ∈ k and a morphism f . Its exactness depends on the embedding.
    To select an exact functor, choose a projective generator P of C. The regular permutation
                                                r
action of Er on pr letters makes Hom(P, P ⊗p ) an Er -module. Section 5 proves that this module
is nonprojective for some r, using projectivity detection and the symmetrizer criterion of [8,
Lemma 4.10]. At the least such r, rank support selects an embedding for which Φ is exact
(Theorem 6.5 in Section 6).
    It remains to remove the C factor. Section 7 compares the Frobenius–Perron dimensions on
that side of Φ: if S is simple and A ⊠ B is a simple constituent of Φ(S), then the dimension of A
is at most that of S. Equality forces S into the Frobenius-exact subcategory of C and makes A the
only possible first component. In the finite graph with arrows S → A, every cycle therefore lies in
that subcategory and cannot be left. After finitely many iterations, all first components lie there,
so the image lies in its Serre closure on the first side. This adapts the finite-simple descent of [18,
Section 10] to the exact higher functor. The Serre-closure results of that paper provide a fiber

2     Finite tensor categories and products
Throughout, k is algebraically closed of characteristic p > 0. Unless explicitly stated otherwise,
group representations and rational algebraic-group modules are finite dimensional over k. A tensor
functor is strong monoidal; when called symmetric it also preserves the given symmetries. We first
relate the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1 to the usual finite-tensor-category conventions.

2.1   The finite-category conventions
Lemma 2.1. Under the conventions of Theorem 1.1, the unit of C is simple and C has a projective
generator P . The functor ω = HomC (P, −) is exact and faithful, and C is equivalent as a k-linear
abelian category to the category of finite-dimensional modules over a finite-dimensional k-algebra.

Proof. We recall the unit argument of Deligne–Milne [13, Proposition 1.17]. Let i : U ,→ 1 and
V = coker i. The epimorphism V ⊗ U ← U induced by 1 ↠ V has zero composite with the
monomorphism V ⊗ i : V ⊗ U → V . Thus V ⊗ U = 0. Put W = ker(i∗ : 1 → U ∗ ). For a subobject
j : T ,→ X, adjunction and exactness give

                     T ⊗U =0        ⇐⇒     (i∗ ⊗ idX )j : T −→ U ∗ ⊗ X is zero.

Consequently W ⊗ X is the largest subobject of X annihilated by tensoring with U . Taking
X = 1, V gives W ⊗ U = 0 and W ⊗ V ≃ V . Tensoring 0 → U → 1 → V → 0 with W now shows
that the natural map W → V is an isomorphism. Hence 1 = U ⊕ W . Since End(1) = k has no
nontrivial idempotents, 1 is simple.
    Choose representatives S1 , . . . , St of the simple objects and projective objects Qi with epimor-
                            L
phisms Qi ↠ Si . Set P = i Qi . Induction on length, using projectivity to lift an epimorphism
onto a simple quotient, shows that every object is a quotient of a finite direct sum of copies of P .
Thus P is a projective generator. Evaluation by P is exact; it is faithful because a nonzero image
of a morphism receives a nonzero map from P , which lifts across the epimorphism onto that image.
Writing A = End(P )op , finite presentations by copies of P identify C with A-modules of finite
dimension. Indeed, evaluation is fully faithful on such presentations, and every finite A-module
has a presentation by finite free A-modules. The algebra A is finite-dimensional by the Hom-space
hypothesis.

    We fix such a P . In particular there is an epimorphism P ↠ 1: the unit is one of the Si . The
finite-algebra description also supplies projective covers of all simple objects whenever they are
needed below.

2.2   Deligne products
For finite k-linear abelian categories A and B, their Deligne product A ⊠ B represents k-bilinear
functors that are right exact in each variable. If A ≃ A-mod and B ≃ B-mod, then A ⊠ B ≃
(A ⊗k B)-mod. Since k is algebraically closed, its simple objects are the exterior products of simple
objects of the two categories. Deligne products of finite tensor categories carry the componentwise
tensor structure.

4     From restricted tiltings to a higher Frobenius functor
Fix r ≥ 1, put q = pr , and choose an injective homomorphism E = (Z/p)r ,→ U (k). Write R for
restriction from SL2 , and put W = Verpr (k). Theorem 3.1 supplies the representation-theoretic
input for the higher Frobenius construction of Coulembier and Flake. This section records that
construction, explains how to evaluate it at projectives, and gives the exactness criterion we will
use.
    We will repeatedly use the following elementary fact. In a rigid abelian tensor category with
simple unit, tensoring by a nonzero object Y is exact and faithful. Indeed, the coevaluation
1 → Y ⊗ Y ∗ is a nonzero monomorphism. Tensoring it by X shows that X ⊗ Y = 0 forces X = 0.
Exactness then shows that tensoring by Y detects nonzero morphisms through their images. An
exact faithful functor reflects exactness as well.

4.1   The functor on group representations
Let T be the category of tilting SL2 -modules, and let Ir be its tensor ideal generated by Str . The
canonical functor
                                        Σr : T /Ir ,−→ W
is fully faithful [2, Theorem 4.2(i)]. For every s ≥ 1, T (a) vanishes modulo Is exactly when
a ≥ ps − 1 [2, Section 3.1]. Stable restriction factors through a faithful functor T /Ir → Stab(E)
[11, Theorem 5.3.5]. Here Stab(E) denotes the quotient of Repk (E) by morphisms factoring
through projective modules.
    The assertion that R(Str−1 ) ⊗ D is a restricted tilting module for every D ∈ Repk (E) is

8     Classification, uniqueness, and reconstruction
We combine Theorem 1.1 with the established structure of the higher Verlinde categories and
generalized Tannaka duality. The resulting statements concern finite symmetric tensor categories;
the uniqueness assertions apply in characteristic two.

8.1    Finite incompressible symmetric tensor categories
For a finite symmetric tensor category C over k, incompressibility means that every k-linear exact
strong symmetric monoidal functor from C to a symmetric tensor category over k is fully faithful.
This is the finite-source form of incompressibility in [9, Section 3.2]. The symmetry condition is
part of the definition here.
    Write Veck for finite-dimensional vector spaces and, when p > 2, sVeck for finite-dimensional
supervector spaces with the sign symmetry. For pm > 2, Ver+   pm (k) denotes the neutral component
of the Z/2-grading constructed from the even highest-weight tilting modules in [2, Section 4.1].
The low levels satisfy

                   Ver2 (k) ≃ Veck ,     Ver3 (k) ≃ sVeck ,     Ver+
                                                                   3 (k) ≃ Veck ;

see [2, Theorem 4.5(iii), Corollary 4.11, and Example 4.52]. In particular, the plus notation is
used only when pm > 2.
Corollary 8.1 (Finite incompressible classification). Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic
p > 0. A finite symmetric tensor category over k is incompressible if and only if it is equivalent as
a k-linear symmetric tensor category to a category in the corresponding row:

                      p = 2 Ver2m (k) (m ≥ 1), Ver+ 2m (k) (m ≥ 2),
                                           +
                      p = 3 Ver3m (k), Ver3m (k) (m ≥ 1),
                      p ≥ 5 Veck , sVeck , Verpm (k), Ver+
                                                         pm (k) (m ≥ 1).

Proof. Let C be incompressible and choose a fiber functor F : C → Verpn (k) by Theorem 1.1. Let

                       FPdim(C) ≤ FPdim(I),            FPdim(C) ≥ FPdim(I).

For sVeck in odd characteristic, a symmetric tensor functor sends the odd invertible generator
to an odd invertible object. It cannot be isomorphic to the unit, since their self-symmetries are
respectively −1 and 1. The functor is therefore fully faithful on the two simple objects, and hence
on all their finite direct sums.

    Every finite tensor category has moderate growth: multiplication by the class of a fixed object
on the finite-rank Grothendieck group is a finite nonnegative matrix, which bounds the lengths of
its tensor powers exponentially. Thus the corollary is the finite case of [9, Conjecture B].

the fixed-stage assertion as well.

8.3   Generalized Tannaka reconstruction
Let V be a finite symmetric tensor category over k. An affine group scheme internal to V is
specified by a commutative Hopf algebra O(G) in Ind(V). The fundamental group

                                        π(V) := Aut⊗ (IdV )

acts canonically on every object of Ind(V). Following [9, Sections 4.1.1–4.1.4], a V-group is an
internal affine group scheme G with a homomorphism ϕ : π(V) → G such that the coaction
on O(G) induced by conjugation through ϕ equals its canonical π(V)-coaction. The category
Rep(G, ϕ) consists of G-representations in V whose restriction along ϕ is the canonical π(V)-action.
Write ω : Rep(G, ϕ) → V for the forgetful functor.

Corollary 8.3 (Reconstruction over a finite higher Verlinde level). Let k be algebraically closed
of characteristic p > 0, and let C be a finite symmetric tensor category over k. Choose n ≥ 1 and
a fiber functor F : C → V = Verpn (k) as in Theorem 1.1. There is a V-group (G, ϕ) and a k-linear
symmetric tensor equivalence
                                                ∼
                                         E:C−   → Rep(G, ϕ)
with a monoidal natural isomorphism ω ◦ E ∼
                                          = F . One may take

                            G = Aut⊗ (F ),       ϕ : π(V) −→ Aut⊗ (F )

to be the canonical homomorphism induced by F .

Proof. The finite-category conventions, including the simple unit supplied by Lemma 2.1, make
C and V pretannakian categories in the terminology of [9]. The chosen F is a tensor functor in
that paper’s sense. Its Theorem 4.2.1 assigns to F the V-group displayed above, and the inverse
assignment is the forgetful functor from Rep(G, ϕ). The resulting equivalence over V is precisely
the one stated here.

    The classification and existence assertions here remain confined to finite categories. Uniqueness
is asserted in characteristic two, both in the tower union and, whenever a fiber functor exists, at
each fixed finite stage. The existence theorem supplies some finite height depending on C; it does
not supply existence at every prescribed height. In the reconstruction, G is asserted to be affine;
no finiteness or finite-type property of this group scheme is used or claimed.
