  Asymptotically minimal maxima of real Littlewood polynomials

                                                Abstract
         We prove that the minimum possible maximum modulus       √ on the unit circle of a polynomial
     with N consecutive real coefficients in {−1, 1} is (1 + o(1)) N , as N tends to infinity through
     all integers. This disproves the real-sign analogue of Erdős’s fixed relative-gap conjecture. As a
     consequence, the largest binary merit factor at length N tends to infinity through all integer
     lengths, disproving Turyn’s conjecture.

1.1   Context and comparison
The Shapiro–Rudin construction, obtained √ earlier by Shapiro and independently by Rudin, gives
real-sign√polynomials with maximum O( N ); at dyadic lengths its paired identity gives √ an upper
bound 2N [22, Theorem I and its proof]. Balister proved the all-length bound 6N − 2 − 1
for its initial segments [2, √
                             Theorem 1]. A different problem asks for upper and lower bounds by
fixed positive multiples of N throughout the circle. Such two-sided flat Littlewood polynomials
were constructed by Balister, Bollobás, Morris, Sahasrabudhe and Tiba, whose proof combines
the Shapiro–Rudin construction with discrepancy theory [3]. These results control the scale of the
maximum, but do not make the upper constant tend to one.
    The coefficient class matters. Complex coefficients of modulus one allow ultraflat constructions.
Littlewood used quadratic phases near this Parseval scale, Kahane obtained ultraflatness, and
Bombieri and Bourgain gave quantitative constructions [18, 17, 5]. The smoothed quadratic phases
and Poisson summation used in [5, Sections 2 and 7] are analytic predecessors of the sampling
argument below. Their bounded-to-unimodular correction also develops a method of Körner [5,
Section 8]. The present proof must instead preserve real coefficients and round them to signs.

binary merit factors into a uniquely ergodic binary Morse shift with simple spectrum whose zero-

proof]. Section 8 gives the precise spectral statement and its deduction from Theorem 1.1.

A     Comparison with contrary flatness claims
Corollary 7.1 conflicts with several stated nonflatness results. We compare the precise versions listed
in the bibliography and examine the proposed obstructions. This discussion is independent of the
construction proving Theorem 1.1.
    An earlier preprint of el Abdalaoui asserted nonexistence of square-L2 -flat real Littlewood
sequences [8, Theorem 3.3]. Its definition fixes both endpoint signs to +1 [8,√Equation (2.1)].
Changing at most two endpoint signs of our length-N polynomials costs at most 4/ N in normalized
uniform norm. Their normalized maxima still tend to one, so the proof of Corollary 7.1 gives
square-L2 flatness with this convention as well.
