             The joint Dickman law for consecutive integers

                                                OpenAI

                                                Abstract
         Let P+ (n) denote the largest prime factor of n.     We prove that log P+ (n)/ log n and
      log P+ (n + 1)/ log n are asymptotically independent in ordinary natural density, with Dickman
      marginals. This resolves the Erdős–Pomerance joint Dickman conjecture positively and implies
      that the ordering P+ (n) < P+ (n + 1) has natural density 1/2.

1.1   Previous work
Erdős and Pomerance [7] formulated the joint independence problem and proved that each ordering
has positive lower natural density. Their explicit lower bound was 0.0099. They also proved that for
every ε > 0, some δ > 0 makes the number of integers n < X satisfying X −δ < P+ (n)/P+ (n + 1) <
X δ less than εX for all sufficiently large X [7, Theorem 1]. The lower-density bound was increased
to 0.05544 by de la Bretèche, Pomerance and Tenenbaum [5, Section 3], and to 0.05866 by an
observation of Fouvry recorded in the same section. Wang [27, 28] obtained 0.1063 and 0.1356, and
Lü and Wang [15] obtained 0.2017. Yang’s recent preprint [30, Theorem 1.4] gives 0.280 for both
orderings. These are bounds on lower natural densities; they do not assert that a natural density
exists.

                                                     2
    For the joint law itself, Teräväinen [26, Theorem 1.14] proved the predicted product in logarith-
mic density. That is, for fixed 0 < a, b < 1, the indicator in Theorem 1.1, averaged with weight 1/n
and normalization 1/ log X, tends to ρ(1/a)ρ(1/b). His Theorem 1.16 gives logarithmic density
1/2 for the ordering, while Theorem 1.19 proves positive lower natural density for every nonde-
generate rectangle of normalized largest-prime-factor values inside (0, 1)2 . Tao and Teräväinen [24,
Remark 3.3, equation (50)] then obtained the joint product law for ordinary averages outside an
exceptional set of scales of logarithmic density zero; their Corollary 1.16 gives the corresponding or-
dering result. Wang [29] proved the ordinary joint law under the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture for
friable integers. Jiang, Lü and Wang [13] established averaged-over-shift forms of the conjectures, a
different conclusion from a result at the fixed shift one.
    The more recent work of Tao and Teräväinen [25, Theorem 1.8] gives a quantitative joint law
outside an exceptional set of scales, with uniformity in the smoothness parameters and a power
saving in log X. Theorem 1.1 concerns fixed parameters and has no exceptional scales; it does
not assert their quantitative error term or their uniformity for growing parameters. Relative to
the results just described, the distinction is therefore the unconditional ordinary limit at every
sufficiently large scale, not a new marginal distribution or a logarithmically averaged independence
statement.

7   Smoothing channels on logarithmic and residue space
The site model admits a useful smoothing operation: choose a fair subset of the primes at one site
and record the logarithm and a residue of its product. We prove that this operation suppresses
residue dependence in operator norm and that its logarithmic outputs are uniformly approximable
on a fixed coarse partition. Operator norm, rather than convergence for each fixed test, is needed
because the single-site tests used later may depend on the position and on an externally conditioned
sample.
    All limits in this section are as B → ∞. The regularity parameters fixed in Section 3 remain fixed,

estimates (3.3)–(3.4) and the independent site law. An occurrence of x inside a channel denotes a
logarithmic coordinate, not the original counting scale.

Combining the Fourier inversion with Lemma 11.1 gives, for every pair of rational c, d ∈ (0, 1),

                      1
                lim       #{1 ≤ n < x : P+ (n) ≤ x c , P+ (n + 1) ≤ x d } = ρ(1/c)ρ(1/d).        (11.6)
               x →∞ x
               x ∈N

We first extend this fixed-scale law to arbitrary real exponents and real counting endpoints. Fix
c, d ∈ (0, 1) and rational exponents c− < c < c+ and d− < d < d+ in (0, 1). Given real X, put
x = ⌊ X ⌋ + 1. Then x/X → 1, the integer ranges n ≤ X and n < x agree, and for all sufficiently
large X,
                               x c− ≤ X c ≤ x c+ ,   x d− ≤ X d ≤ x d+ .
Thus the normalized count over 1 ≤ n ≤ X with thresholds X c , X d lies between x/X times the two
normalized counts in (11.6) at the lower and upper rational exponents. Taking lower and upper
limits through real X, then letting the rational exponents approach c, d, proves

                                1
                           lim    #{2 ≤ n ≤ X : P + ( n ) ≤ X c , P + ( n + 1) ≤ X d }
                           X →∞ X                                                                (11.7)
                               = D (c) D (d) = ρ(1/c)ρ(1/d)           (0 < c, d < 1).

The omission of n = 1 changes at most one summand. In (11.7), the exponents are fixed and the
limit is through all real X.
   This also gives the fixed-scale upper-tail independence law discussed by Erdős and Pomer-
ance [7, p. 311]:

                                1
                           lim    #{2 ≤ n ≤ X : P + ( n ) > X c , P + ( n + 1) > X d }
                           X →∞ X                                                                (11.8)
                               = (1 − D (c))(1 − D (d))       (0 ≤ c, d ≤ 1).

For interior exponents this follows by inclusion–exclusion from (11.7) and the two marginal laws.
The marginal for n + 1 is the one in Lemma 11.1, since shifting its counting range changes only
O(1) terms. Replacing either strict comparison in (11.8) by a weak one has the same limit. Indeed,
for fixed c > 0, equality P+ (m) = X c is impossible unless X c is a prime p; in that case every such m
is divisible by p, giving only O( X 1−c + 1) = o ( X ) possibilities for m ≤ X + 1. At exponent zero,
the upper-tail condition holds for every m ≥ 2, and equality can occur only at m = 1. At exponent
one, an upper-tail condition on m ≤ X + 1 has only O(1) possible integers. These observations
give the endpoint cases of (11.8) as well. Its right-hand side is continuous on [0, 1]2 by (11.5).

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Fix a, b ∈ (0, 1) and choose fixed exponents c− < a < c+ and d− < b < d+ in
(0, 1). For each fixed ε ∈ (0, 1), all sufficiently large real X satisfy, uniformly for εX ≤ n ≤ X,

                                  X c− ≤ n a ≤ X c+ ,        X d− ≤ n b ≤ X d+ .

On this interval, the fixed-scale event at the lower exponents is contained in the moving-threshold
event of Theorem 1.1, which is contained in the fixed-scale event at the upper exponents. The
discarded initial interval costs at most ε + O(1/X ) in normalized counting. The limit through all real
X in (11.7) therefore bounds the lower and upper limits of the desired count by the corresponding
products at the lower and upper exponents, with that error. Let ε ↓ 0, then let the exponents
approach a, b. Continuity of D gives the claimed product through all real endpoints, with ordinary,
unweighted counting.
