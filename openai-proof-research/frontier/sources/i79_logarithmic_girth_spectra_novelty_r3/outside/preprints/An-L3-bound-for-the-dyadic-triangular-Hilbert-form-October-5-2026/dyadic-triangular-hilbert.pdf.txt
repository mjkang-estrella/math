            An L3 bound for the dyadic triangular Hilbert form
                                                           OpenAI

                                                           Abstract
          We prove a uniform L3 × L3 × L3 estimate for the dyadic triangular Hilbert form with
      unrestricted real inputs. The sum of the absolute local contributions over any finite set of scales
      is bounded by forty times the product of the input norms. In particular, the bound allows
      coefficients of modulus at most one to vary independently among admissible interval triples.

1.1   Context and relation to earlier work
The continuous triangular Hilbert form has the same cyclic arrangement of inputs as (1.1), integrated
over R3 against the principal-value kernel 1/(x0 + x1 + x2 ). Its symmetric L3 estimate is formulated
by Thiele in [3, Section 9, Problem 13]. That contribution also displays the precise dyadic model
(1.2), with one coefficient for each scale, and asks for the analogous bound. Theorem 1.1 answers
this dyadic question affirmatively and gives the stronger sum of absolute local contributions. The
continuous form and its dyadic model are distinct operators; the theorem here is a statement about
the latter.
    Kovač, Thiele and Zorin-Kranich introduced a dyadic triangular model with coefficients allowed
to vary among interval triples [6, Equations (1.5)–(1.6)]. Their Theorem 1.7 gives uniform estimates
when one input has either a specified dependence on a Walsh linear combination of its coordinates or
a specified fiberwise character structure. Those cases already encompass dyadic versions of Carleson’s
operator and uniform bounds for the bilinear Hilbert transform, as explained in their Appendix A.
Our estimate allows all three inputs to vary freely. The cyclic operator-trace representation used
below also appears in their Equation (1.5).
    Another line of work measures cancellation through the growth of truncated norms. Tao
obtained sublogarithmic growth for one-dimensional multilinear Hilbert transforms [9, Theorem 1.2];
Zorin-Kranich proved the corresponding sublinear growth in the number of smooth scales for
simplex Hilbert transforms [10, Theorem 1.3]. Durcik, Kovač and Thiele then obtained power-type
cancellation in both the continuous and dyadic settings [2, Theorems 1 and 4]. For the triangular
dyadic form, their bound over m consecutive scales has a factor m1/2 at exponents (4, 4, 2); cyclic
permutation and multilinear interpolation give the same growth at (3, 3, 3). Theorem 1.1 removes
this scale dependence at the symmetric tuple.
    The strategy of controlling an entangled form by the change of a nonnegative energy has
precedents in Kovač’s Bellman-function framework [4, Section 2] and his telescoping identity for
the twisted paraproduct [5, Lemma 2]. These provide a methodological precedent, rather than a
bound for the present triangular form. In particular, Kovač explicitly identifies the obstruction to
applying that framework to non-bipartite graphs, including the triangle, in [4, Section 6.2]. Here
the energy is a trace of a matrix power, and the averaging is over the four children selected by the
XOR constraint.
    The matrix energy and the dimension-independent trace inequality are taken from the companion
manuscript [7, Sections 2–3, Lemmas 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1]; we reproduce their real finite-dimensional
proofs. Their underlying matrix calculus is classical: the square-root derivative is a special case
of the divided-difference formula in [1, Section 1, Equation (1)], and the order comparison uses
monotonicity of the positive square root [8, Theorem, p. 309].
