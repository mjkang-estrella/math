                         The sharp factor-of-IID threshold
                      for the free Ising model on regular trees
                                                  OpenAI

                                           September 26, 2026


                                                  Abstract
          For every integer d ≥ 3 and inverse temperature β ≥ 0, the free zero-field ferromagnetic
      Ising measure on the infinite d-regular tree is a factor of IID exactly when tanh β ≤ (d − 1)−1/2 .
      We prove the positive implication, including equality, resolving the conjecture of Nam, Sly and
      Zhang; the strict converse is known. The spin factor can be chosen to commute with every tree
      automorphism on every label input.


1    Introduction
Can the free Ising state on a regular tree be generated from independent vertex labels without
choosing a root? The broadcast description of the state begins at a vertex and propagates a random
spin along the edges. Its law does not depend on that vertex. A factor construction must also
remove the distinguished vertex from the rule that generates the spins.
    Let Td = (V, E) be the infinite d-regular tree, d ≥ 3, and put b = d − 1 and θ = tanh β for β ≥ 0.
The free zero-field Ising law µd,β is obtained by choosing a fair spin at one vertex and, independently
along edges directed away from it, letting a child agree with its parent with probability (1 + θ)/2.
Its marginal on any finite connected subtree D is

This expression also verifies that the law is invariant under all tree automorphisms and independent
of the chosen broadcast root.
    We call a spin law a factor of IID if it is the law of Φ(U ) for independent uniform labels (Uv )v∈V
and a measurable map Φ : [0, 1]V → {−1, 1}V satisfying

          Φ(gU ) = gΦ(U ) almost surely for each fixed g ∈ Aut(Td ),               (gx)v = xg−1 v .         (1.1)

The exceptional null set may depend on g. For spin laws this convention is equivalent to requiring a
Borel factor equivariant on every input: Lemma 8.5 constructs such a version. The input contains
no distinguished root or end. Measurability does not require a spin to be determined by finitely
many labels; no finitary coding or coding-radius estimate is asserted.
Theorem 1.1. For every integer d ≥ 3 and every β ≥ 0, the free zero-field Ising law µd,β is a factor
of IID in the sense of (1.1) if and only if

                                           tanh β ≤ (d − 1)−1/2 .

In this range the factor can be chosen to commute with every automorphism on every label input.

in Lyons [12, Section 3 and Corollary 3.2]. We use the quantitative correlation bound of Backhausz,

    For the free state on Td , reconstruction from distant levels is possible exactly when bθ2 > 1;
equivalently, the free Gibbs state is extremal exactly when bθ2 ≤ 1. For the regular-tree result,
including equality, see the Bethe-lattice work of Bleher, Ruiz, and Zagrebnov [3] and Ioffe [9].
Ioffe [10] and Evans, Kenyon, Peres, and Schulman [6] developed branching-number criteria on
general trees away from equality. Pemantle and Peres [17, Theorem 2.1] later gave an electrical-
capacity criterion for free-state extremality for couplings bounded above and away from zero, which
also covers critical cases.
    Reconstruction asks whether distant spins retain information about a fixed spin. The factor
question asks for a rule that generates the entire law from independent labels. Nonreconstruction
supplies no such rule, and a weak limit of factor laws need not itself be a factor [12, Corollary 3.3].
Thus equality requires a construction that retains measurable dependence on its independent input.
    In the uniqueness range θ ≤ b−1 , including equality, a finite-cluster construction gives the factor;

the same driving Brownian motions. This is the compatibility principle for conditional copies [11,

stochastic integrals directly.

integrable in time.
