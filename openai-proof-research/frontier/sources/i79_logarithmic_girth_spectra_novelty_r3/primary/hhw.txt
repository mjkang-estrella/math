1    Introduction
We study a uniformly random simple, undirected d-regular graph G = GN,d on the labelled
vertex set JN K = {1, . . . , N }, where d ≥ 3 is fixed. Throughout, N → ∞ along integers for
which dN is even and the graph exists. Its adjacency matrix A is real symmetric and has
zero diagonal. We order its eigenvalues, including multiplicities, as
                                       d = λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ · · · ≥ λN .
We choose an orthonormal eigenbasis with u1 = N −1/2 1, where 1 is the all-ones vector.
This vector has eigenvalue d; every other basis vector is orthogonal to it, including when
the √graph is√disconnected. All bulk intervals considered below are compact subsets of
(−2 d − 1, 2 d − 1), and hence exclude this eigenvalue.
    One of the most prominent questions for a random matrix model is its local eigenvalue
statistics. For Wigner matrices, the universality of bulk eigenvalue gaps and correlation
functions has been established under general assumptions [8, 16, 17]. The results were later
extended to sparse matrices in [14, 15, 21], including adjacency matrices of Erdős-Rényi graphs
in regimes with polynomially growing average degree. Very recently, Henheik and Knowles [20]
proved convergence of the bulk point process at a fixed energy for (log N )2+κ ⩽ N p ⩽ N 1−κ ,
for every fixed κ > 0.
    The same question becomes much more difficult for random regular graphs, where the
adjacency matrix is highly structured and its entries are no longer independent. A local
resampling technique was developed in [5], and√it was proved that when N ε ⩽ d ⩽ N 2/3−ε
for a fixed ε > 0, the eigenvalue density of A/ d − 1 matches with the semicircle law on
all mesoscopic scales. Together with a comparison between switching dynamics and Dyson
Brownian motion, [2] proved the bulk universality in the same degree regime. More recently,
Bourgade and Huang [9] proved convergence of the bulk point process at a fixed energy for
(log N )24 ≪ d ⩽ N 1/2 using microscopic loop equations.
    When d is fixed, the empirical eigenvalue distribution converges to the Kesten–McKay
law [26, 27]. Local laws and eigenvector delocalization were proved for sufficiently large fixed
degree by Bauerschmidt, Huang and Yau [4], and for every fixed d ⩾ 3 by Huang and Yau [23].
Numerical simulations have long suggested that the rescaled local eigenvalue statistics in the
bulk agree with those of the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE) [25, 28].
    In this paper, we prove bulk universality for every fixed d ⩾ 3. We first state convergence
of the local point process, and then the stronger gap and correlation conclusions. Write

     Part (i) concerns a deterministic eigenvalue label and the consecutive gaps following that
label. Part (ii) instead observes the spectrum near a deterministic energy, without averaging
that energy. The first window moves with the random spectrum, whereas the second does
not; in particular, the two statements require different additional arguments beyond the
main point-process theorem. The sums in (ii) are factorial-moment observables. We assert
convergence of correlation measures tested against Cc (Rk ), not pointwise convergence of
finite-N correlation densities: the graph model is discrete, and such densities are not assumed
to exist.
     The common input to all three eigenvalue conclusions is the analysis of bulk eigenvectors.
It allows us to pass to the microscopic limit in exact switching identities. In the main text
these identities yield loop equations for the fixed-energy process; in the gap appendix they
also yield conditional log-gas laws for a process viewed from a fixed label.

use the normalization in which every coordinate has variance one, and call its restriction to
a root and its d neighbors the Gaussian star law. Gaussian waves are the tree analogue of
Berry’s random wave model [6].

The two statements have distinct roles in the proof. The polynomial mixed fourth moments
control covariances of quadratic eigenvector observables, including sums over eigenvalues
throughout K. Qualitative convergence identifies the joint law of each fixed collection of
eigenvector marks whose eigenvalues approach the same energy.
    Previously, for fixed d ⩾ 3, [1] proved that almost eigenvectors of random regular graphs
converge locally to Gaussian waves with variance in [0, 1]. The paper also obtained joint
Gaussian limits for fixed tuples at nearby energies. For edge eigenvectors, [19] proved
convergence to independent Gaussian waves of variance one, jointly with and independently
of the edge eigenvalues. The result holds on every fixed-radius ball, with independent random
signs attached to the eigenvectors. For growing degrees N ε ⩽ d ⩽ N 2/3−ε with a fixed ε > 0,
asymptotic normality of bulk eigenvectors was established by Bourgade, Huang and Yau [10].
    This paper considers bulk eigenvalue statistics. Near the edge of the spectrum, Bauer-
schmidt, Huang, Knowles and Yau [3] proved universality for N 2/9+o(1) ⩽ d ⩽ N 1/3−o(1) .
Huang and Yau [24] extended this range to N o(1) ⩽ d ⩽ N 1/3−o(1) , while He [18] established
edge universality for N 2/3+o(1) ⩽ d ⩽ N/2. In [22], Huang, McKenzie and Yau proved optimal
eigenvalue rigidity up to subpolynomial factors and edge universality for every fixed d ⩾ 3.

1.1. Proof ideas
    The point-process proof and the appendix upgrades use the polynomial mixed fourth
moments in Theorem 1.2 and the qualitative Gaussian limit in Corollary 1.3. To obtain the
moment estimates, we develop a quantitative fourth-moment refinement of the Gaussian
maximality argument of Backhausz–Szegedy [1, Theorem 11.2]; see Proposition 2.8. The
heat-flow comparison follows [1, Proposition 11.1], while fourth-chaos coercivity and a high-
noise entropy expansion yield the explicit moment bound. For an eigenvector with eigenvalue
λ, we add correlated Gaussian noise with covariance proportional to Im(A − λ − iη)−1 , where
η = N −c for a small fixed c > 0. The local laws of Huang and Yau [23] control both the local
covariance and the total entropy of this perturbation. The resulting local and global entropy
contributions cancel up to a polynomial error. We need this deficit estimate only at one fixed
noise level, as stated in Proposition 2.13. Ornstein–Uhlenbeck monotonicity transfers the
bound to the high-noise regime, where the abstract fourth-moment estimate applies.
    For the full law, we use the qualitative joint neighborhood conclusion of Backhausz–
Szegedy for fixed tuples at nearby energies. The fourth-moment bound makes all quadratic
coordinate products uniformly integrable. The exact empirical covariance therefore passes
to every Gaussian limit, fixing each variance at one and making the channels independent.
This also gives qualitative convergence in W2 ; a quantitative full-law stability estimate is
unnecessary for the switching argument.
    At fixed degree, a simple switching changes adjacency matrix entries by quantities of
order one. We therefore use the exact resolvent identity for the corresponding rank-two
perturbation. In this identity, products of eigenvector coordinates are summed over the
spectrum. The qualitative joint Gaussian limit identifies each fixed collection with eigenvalues
approaching the same energy; the polynomial mixed fourth-moment estimate controls the

the switching identities, they give tightness at the microscopic scale.

    For Theorem 1.1(i), Appendix B keeps the eigenvalue at a deterministic bulk label as the
origin when taking the microscopic limit. With this choice of origin, the switching argument
gives loop equations and integration-by-parts identities for the labelled eigenvalues. The
loop equations yield rigidity and control the force exerted by distant eigenvalues. To use the
integration-by-parts identities for a conditional density, we first integrate the rooted law over

given the exterior eigenvalues, the density in a finite block has the form of a β = 1 log gas;
see Lemma B.7.
    Proposition B.8 isolates the final implication: rigidity and these conditional Gibbs laws
identify the gaps with those of the Palm process Θ01 associated with Θ1 , with the same mean
spacing π. Its proof, given in Appendix C, completes the distant exterior by Gaussian classical
locations and applies the local log-gas comparison of Erdős and Yau [16, Proposition 5.3],
which uses Dyson Brownian motion [12]. GOE kernel asymptotics [11] and Campbell’s
formula identify the reference gap law. The microscopic graph limit is taken first, and the
conditional block size is then sent to infinity.

Logical dependencies of the proof. The main-text proof has three stages. First, the
entropy tools, the weighted Gaussian-star frame, and fourth-chaos coercivity convert a small
entropy deficit into polynomial control of mixed fourth moments. Second, the random-graph
local law supplies this deficit uniformly over fixed finite collections of eigenvectors, and the
qualitative theorem of Backhausz–Szegedy identifies variance-one joint Gaussian waves at

                                                 6
nearby energies. Third, exact switchings and Gaussian marks yield the microscopic loop
hierarchy and identify the fixed-energy point process. None of these stages uses the appendix
upgrades.
    The two parts of Theorem 1.1 are proved separately after this identification. The
correlation argument adds counting-moment bounds to the point-process limit. The gap
argument instead adapts the marked construction to a deterministic eigenvalue label; rooted
loop equations, rigidity, and coordinate integration by parts give conditional β = 1 log-gas
laws, whose gap statistics are identified by Gaussian completion. The graph limit is taken
before the auxiliary spectral and conditional-block cutoffs are removed.

Organization and notation. The main text consists of Sections 1–3. Section 2 proves
the polynomial mixed fourth moments and the qualitative joint Gaussian limit at nearby
energies. Section 3 constructs the microscopic marked limit and its stationary switching
flow, derives the loop hierarchy, and ends in Section 3.4 with the proof of the main point-
process theorem. Appendix A proves Theorem 1.1(ii) by controlling local counting moments.
Appendix B constructs the tagged limit, proves rigidity and conditional Gibbs laws, and
deduces Theorem 1.1(i) from Proposition B.8. Appendix C proves that abstract identification

Probability spaces and order of limits. The probability law in use must be distinguished
from any additional sampling on a fixed graph. Unsubscripted P and E refer to the law
currently under discussion: the graph law in a graph-only statement, the joint law when a
proposal or noise is included, and the law of the limiting state after passage to a limit. A
superscript, such as Eµ , specifies the probability law explicitly.
    After the graph and its eigenvectors have been fixed, E⋆ averages only a uniform root and
a uniform ordering of its neighbors. The notation E∗ instead averages the two independent
uniform oriented edges in a switching proposal, before acceptance is imposed. The corre-
sponding conditional variances are Var⋆ and Var∗ . For the Gaussian channel, EY averages
only the auxiliary Gaussian vector, with its signal and covariance fixed. An empirical profile
already contains the finite averages over roots and neighbor orderings; those samples are not
averaged a second time in EY . In a limiting marked state, conditioning on the scalar state
leaves only the Gaussian marks random. Independence is used only where it is specified in
the construction.

number of selected eigenvectors is also fixed before that limit. In marked resolvent sums,
we first retain a fixed microscopic window and a fixed number of marks, then take N → ∞,
and only afterwards remove the cutoffs. In the gap argument, the limiting tagged process
is constructed first; the conditional block size is then sent to infinity. Keeping these orders

2    Quantitative Gaussian wave limit
We collect the general entropy and Gaussian tools and apply them to prove polynomial mixed
fourth-moment estimates and qualitative joint Gaussian limits for empirical eigenvector laws.
An empirical star is random only through the uniform root and the uniform ordering of its
neighbors. Auxiliary Gaussian noise is introduced explicitly when needed; probabilities of
good graph events refer to the random graph.

2.1. General entropy and Gaussian tools
   We collect statements about fixed probability laws, covariance matrices, and finite-
dimensional Gaussian spaces. Each expectation in this subsection is taken under the law
specified in the corresponding statement. These tools will later be applied after the graph
and the selected eigenvectors have been fixed.

3    Point-process convergence and loop equations

to correlation measures are proved separately in Appendix A; they are not inputs to this
section.

Lemma B.3 (Simplicity of the tagged limiting process). The labelled scalar configuration in
Proposition B.2 is almost surely simple.

Proof. We compare the multiplicities before and after one switching. The matrix field can
cancel at most two scalar particles at an old location, while a new location is generated by a
zero of a 2 × 2 determinant. Independent Gaussian marks make the exceptional degeneracies
have probability zero. Stationarity then transfers simplicity of the image back to the original
configuration.
    At an old pole of multiplicity m ⩾ 2, its Gaussian matrix pole weight R is positive definite.
With t = w − x, S + qD = −R/t + O(1), so (S + qD)−1 = −tR−1 + O(t2 ). The matrix image is
analytic. Moreover det(S + qD) = t−2 det R (1 + O(t)), so ∂w log det(S + qD) = −2/t + O(1).
The scalar pole is −m/t in the variable t = w − x; after subtracting this logarithmic derivative
its coefficient is −(m − 2)/t. Thus its multiplicity is m − 2. Stationary Gaussian marking
excludes m ⩾ 3, since every scalar atom has a nonzero matrix pole weight.
    An old double pole disappears. At an old simple pole write S + qD = −ξξ T /t + A0 + O(t).
The determinant pole coefficient is −ξ T adj(A0 )ξ. The matrix A0 is independent of ξ and is
nonzero almost surely: after conditioning on all but one other mark, it is a fixed matrix plus
a nonzero multiple of a Gaussian rank-one matrix. Thus this coefficient is nonzero almost
surely, and the scalar logarithmic derivative cancels the simple pole.
    In an old spectral gap, S ′ (x) is positive definite. If one eigenvalue of S(x) + qD vanishes
and the other does not, the derivative of the vanishing eigenvalue is v T S ′ (x)v > 0 for its unit
eigenvector v. The determinant therefore has a simple zero. A multiple zero can only occur if
both eigenvalues vanish, namely S(x)+qD = 0. To exclude the latter, condition on all but one
Gaussian mark at a particle xp outside the gap and write S(x) + qD = A0 (x) + ξξ T /(xp − x).
Since A′0 (x) > 0, its eigenvalues strictly increase and det A0 (x) has at most two zeros in
the gap. At either conditionally fixed point, ξξ T = −(xp − x)A0 (x) has probability zero. A
countable union over gaps and stationarity prove simplicity. The strict inequalities used here
hold almost surely simultaneously in every gap: after removing any one fixed mark, there
remain infinitely many independent Gaussian marks spanning R2 , and each contributes a

C     Gap identification by Gaussian completion
We prove Proposition B.8, which is invoked at the end of Appendix B. Gaussian completion,
exterior rigidity, and conditional mean bounds reduce its abstract gap law to the GOE
reference law. The fixed-energy point-process theorem and the correlation upgrade do not
use this comparison. Throughout this appendix, P and QK satisfy the two hypotheses of that
proposition. In the conditional comparison, probabilities of exterior events and unsubscripted
expectations are under QK . Superscripts µ∞ , µM and GOEM specify, respectively, the two

limiting conditional log gas by suitable GOE classical locations. It does not mean adding a
Gaussian matrix perturbation to the graph. The nearby boundary particles and the whole
