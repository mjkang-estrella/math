Blow-up for energy supercritical defocusing
nonlinear Schrödinger equations in dimensions three, four and five
Kevin Buck, Alvaro Carballeira, Javier Gómez-Serrano, Jia Shi
Abstract.
We prove finite time blow-up from smooth, radial, compactly supported initial data for the energy supercritical defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-|u|^{p-1}u=0 in \mathbb{R}^{d} , for (d,p)\in\{(3,27),(4,9),(5,7)\} . The solutions are asymptotically self-similar, their L^{\infty} norm grows at the scaling rate (T-t)^{-1(p-1)} , and their critical Sobolev norm \dot{H}^{s_{c}} diverges. This closes the conjecture of Bourgain [6] on global well-posedness and scattering for the defocusing energy supercritical equation in dimensions three and four. The proof relies on a shooting argument and is computer-assisted.

1. Introduction
We consider the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(1)
i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-|u|^{p-1}u=0,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.
The scaling symmetry u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x) leaves the homogeneous Sobolev norm \dot{H}^{s_{c}} invariant, where
s_{c}=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2}{p-1}.
The energy supercritical regime corresponds to s_{c}>1 , or equivalently p>1+\frac{4}{d-2} for d\geq 3 . In this range, the scaling critical regularity lies above the energy regularity, and conservation of mass and energy does not provide an a priori bound on the critical Sobolev norm. Throughout the paper, we restrict to (d,p)\in\{(3,27),(4,9),(5,7)\} , all in the energy supercritical regime. Our main result constructs finite-time, asymptotically self-similar blow-up solutions from smooth, radial, compactly supported initial data.
Theorem 1.1.
For every (d,p)\in\{(3,27),(4,9),(5,7)\} , there are radial initial data
u_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}) whose corresponding solution
of (1) blows up at some finite time T>0 . The solution belongs to
C([0,T);H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})) for every integer m\geq 0 .
Moreover, there are \Omega\in\mathbb{R} ,
\mu>0 , and a smooth radial self-similar profile Q , such that, with
\tau=\log\frac{T}{T-t} ,
\left\|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{i\Omega\tau}u\left(t,\sqrt{T-t}\,\cdot\right)-Q\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=O((T-t)^{\mu})\quad\text{as }t\uparrow T.
Consequently,
(2)
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}+O((T-t)^{\mu})}{(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\quad\text{as }t\uparrow T,
and
\lim_{t\uparrow T}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}=\infty.
Remark 1.2.
Merle, Raphaël, Rodnianski and Szeftel [52] constructed smooth, well localized radial solutions blowing up in finite time for (d,p) equal to (5,9) , (6,5) , (8,3) or (9,3) . Theorem 1.1 extends the existence of finite time blow-up to dimensions three and four, which were left open in their work, and to the nonlinearity p=7 in dimension five. The blow-up dynamics in dimension five are also different. Their construction is governed at leading order by a self-similar compressible Euler flow, with quantum pressure treated as a perturbation, and yields a blow-up rate which breaks the NLS scaling. In contrast, our solutions are asymptotically self-similar at the NLS scaling and obey the rate (2).
Remark 1.3.
The choice of the three pairs in Theorem 1.1 is related to the computer-assisted profile construction. As explained in the proof of Lemma 3.10, the profile ODE is numerically easier to solve for large p . Thus, we first locate a profile in that regime and then use a continuation method in (d,p) to the desired pair. We selected (3,27) , (4,9) , and (5,7) because this continuation and the subsequent estimates close with enough margin for these cases. Our nonrigorous shooting experiments find profiles for many other energy-supercritical parameters, although the rigorous construction in this paper is restricted to the stated cases.
1.1. Historical background
The flow (1) conserves the mass and energy functionals
(3)
M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{2}\,dx,\qquad E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u|^{p+1}\,dx,
and it is invariant under the scaling u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x) , which preserves the homogeneous norm \dot{H}^{s_{c}} . The Cauchy problem is locally well-posed in H^{s} for s\geq s_{c} by Strichartz estimates [33, 17, 16], and the conservation laws yield global well-posedness in the mass subcritical and energy subcritical ranges. At the critical levels, the conserved quantities control precisely the scaling invariant norm, and the defocusing problem is by now completely understood: global well-posedness and scattering hold in the energy-critical case [5, 35, 23, 59, 73] and in the mass-critical case [66, 41, 27, 28, 29]. In the energy supercritical regime s_{c}>1 considered here, no conservation law controls the critical norm, and the available results are conditional. Using the concentration compactness and rigidity method of Kenig–Merle [37, 38, 39, 40], Killip–Visan [42] and Li–Li [45] proved that a solution which remains bounded in \dot{H}^{s_{c}} is global and scatters; see [50] for the most recent extension of the admissible exponents in high dimensions and [11] for blow-up criteria below the scaling regularity. In particular, the maximal solution issued from smooth data either is global or its \dot{H}^{s_{c}} norm blows up in finite time. An analogous conditional scattering result for the defocusing \dot{H}^{1/2} -critical equation in dimensions d\geq 4 was proved by Murphy [55].
This collection of facts led to the belief that the a priori bound on the critical norm should always hold. Bourgain [6] explicitly conjectured that the defocusing energy supercritical Schrödinger and wave equations are globally well-posed and scatter; see also the discussions in [40, 42]. Numerical simulations of Colliander–Simpson–Sulem [24] supported this belief. Global regularity was proved for logarithmically supercritical wave equations [63, 25] and for a supercritical wave equation in two dimensions [61], and large global solutions were constructed for supercritical wave equations in [43] and for the energy supercritical NLS in [4]. Indications in the opposite direction were the finite time blow-up constructed by Tao [64, 65] for defocusing wave and Schrödinger systems sharing the same scaling and conservation laws as (1), and the norm inflation results at low supercritical regularity of [22, 69].
The conjecture was disproved by Merle–Raphaël–Rodnianski–Szeftel [52], who constructed C^{\infty} well localized radial initial data leading to finite time blow-up of (1) for (d,p) equal to (5,9) , (6,5) , (8,3) or (9,3) . Their mechanism is hydrodynamical. Through the Madelung transform u=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\psi} [51], equation (1) becomes the compressible Euler system with adiabatic exponent \gamma=\frac{p+1}{2} , forced by a quantum pressure term proportional to \nabla\big(\Delta\sqrt{\rho}/\sqrt{\rho}\big) . In the blow-up regime of [52], the quantum pressure is a perturbation. The leading order dynamics are those of compressible Euler, and the blow-up profiles are the smooth radial imploding self-similar profiles constructed in the companion paper [53]; see also [54] for the corresponding singularity formation for the compressible Euler and Navier–Stokes equations. The Eulerian regime requires the self-similar blow-up speed r to exceed 2 , which is only possible when d\geq 5 , and the existence of blow-up solutions in dimensions d=3,4 was left open [52, Section 1.4]. The solutions obtained in this way are of type II, meaning that they blow up at a rate different from the self-similar one and break scaling. Building on their non-radial implosion construction for compressible fluids [14], Cao-Labora–Gómez-Serrano–Shi–Staffilani [12] extended this construction to non-radial data for (d,p)=(8,3) , with smooth periodic initial data on \mathbb{T}^{8} and with smooth initial data on \mathbb{R}^{8} which do not vanish at infinity. The hydrodynamical formulation of (1) with the quantum pressure retained has also been studied on its own in the theory of quantum fluids; see Antonelli–Marcati [1, 2] and Carles–Danchin–Saut [15] for finite energy weak solutions and for the precise relation with (1).
Exact self-similar solutions u(t,x)=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{-i\Omega\tau}Q(x/\sqrt{T-t}) are the basic blow-up objects of the focusing mass supercritical NLS. Their existence was observed numerically; see [10, 58] and the book [62], and rigorous constructions are recent: Bahri–Martel–Raphaël [3] in the slightly supercritical regime, Dahne–Figueras [26] far from the critical exponent, including the three dimensional cubic case, Donninger–Schörkhuber [31] and Donninger–Lichtnecker [30] for the cubic and nearly cubic three dimensional equations, and Cao-Labora–Gómez-Serrano–Shi–Staffilani [13] for the hydrodynamic formulation. Their finite codimensional stability was established by Li [46]; see also [47, 48]. In the defocusing case, on the contrary, admissible self-similar solutions were expected not to exist [52, Section 1.3]. Self-similar solutions with finite energy are indeed excluded by energy conservation and scaling, see (7) below. Theorem 1.1 shows that, once the finite energy requirement on the profile is dropped, smooth radial self-similar profiles of the defocusing equation exist for (d,p)\in\{(3,27),(4,9),(5,7)\} , and that they govern the blow-up of smooth, compactly supported initial data at the self-similar rate (2). In the profile equation (6) the Laplacian and the nonlinearity enter at the same order, so the quantum pressure is not a perturbation and no compressible Euler regime is involved. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first construction of finite time blow-up for the defocusing NLS in dimensions three and four.
One of the fundamental pillars of the proof relies on a computer-assisted argument. The main paradigm is to replace floating-point numbers on a computer with rigorous bounds. These bounds are then propagated through every operation performed by the computer, taking into account any error made throughout the process. We refer the reader to the books [70, 56] and to the survey [34] for a general treatment of computer-assisted proofs in PDE. In our case, the computer certifies that the solution regular at the origin and the solution with a pure slow tail at infinity can be matched at an intermediate radius, following the shooting and matching strategy of Dahne–Figueras [26]. Closest to our setting, van den Berg–Groothedde–Williams [71] proved the existence of a localized radial solution of a singular Ginzburg–Landau ordinary differential equation. They rewrite the equation as an integral equation by means of a Green function, and prove with computer assistance that the resulting map is a contraction around a numerical approximation. Computer-assisted constructions of self-similar blow-up profiles have appeared in [9, 8] for compressible Euler, in [20, 18, 19] for the Hou–Luo model, the Boussinesq and the incompressible Euler equations, and in [26, 31] for the focusing NLS. Our implementation uses the Arb library [36] and the code is available at [7].
1.2. Strategy of the proof
We explain the main steps in the proof of Theorem 1.1. The construction consists of finding a smooth self-similar profile with prescribed behavior at infinity, and then producing solutions with localized initial data which converge to this profile in rescaled variables. The first step is computer-assisted and the second follows from spectral estimates and a finite-codimensional nonlinear stability argument.
We seek a radial profile Q and a frequency \Omega\in\mathbb{R} such that
u(t,x)=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{-i\Omega\tau}Q(y),\qquad\tau=\log\frac{T}{T-t},\qquad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}},
solves (1). This gives the profile equation (6), in which dispersion and the nonlinearity both enter at leading order. After fixing the constant phase, regularity at the origin leaves two real shooting parameters, Q(0)=A>0 and \Omega , with Q^{\prime}(0)=0 . At infinity, the linearized equation has two distinct asymptotic modes,
Q_{\mathrm{slow}}(r)\sim C_{s}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}+2i\Omega},\qquad Q_{\mathrm{fast}}(r)\sim C_{f}e^{-ir^{2}/4}r^{-d+\frac{2}{p-1}-2i\Omega}.
Since the second mode has a factor with a quadratic phase, each derivative introduces a growing factor. A nonzero fast mode has derivatives outside of L^{2} at every integer order j>s_{c} , whereas the slow mode has derivatives in L^{2} at those orders. We therefore impose C_{f}=0 and C_{s}\neq 0 , as required by the stability argument.
To construct such a profile, we match a solution regular at the origin to a solution with a pure slow tail at infinity, following the shooting approach of [26]. Near the origin, a finite Taylor expansion and a contraction argument give an enclosure of the exact solution. At infinity, we construct a finite asymptotic expansion and control its remainder again using a contraction argument. To do this we obtain a right inverse built from the two linear modes at infinity. This gives an exact solution at infinity with the prescribed slow coefficient and no fast component, together with an expansion to every algebraic order. At an intermediate radius, we match these two solutions. These are three real conditions in the three parameters (A,\Omega,K) , where K=|C_{s}|^{p-1} . A zero of the matching map joins the solutions up to a constant phase and yields the global profile of Theorem 3.1.
The need for computer assistance arises in connecting the two endpoint constructions. For the fixed pairs (d,p) in Theorem 1.1, our argument has no small parameter which controls the intermediate nonlinear regime. The local expansions alone therefore do not establish that the matching conditions can be satisfied. We overcome this difficulty by deriving explicit bounds for the endpoint remainders, the propagation of errors on finite intervals, and the parameter derivatives of the shooting problem. Interval arithmetic then verifies a finite collection of strict inequalities near an approximate matching zero. Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem subsequently gives an exact zero. All computational assertions are collected in Lemma 3.10 and the code can be found in the supplementary material and at [7].
The resulting profile has infinite energy because of its slow tail, so the exact self-similar solution does not yet give Theorem 1.1. To localize it, we use a finite-codimensional stability argument and write the rescaled solution as V=Q+v . We work in the radial space
\mathcal{X}_{s}=\dot{H}^{s}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})\cap\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}),\qquad s_{c}<s<d/2,\qquad k_{\star}\in\mathbb{N}\text{ sufficiently large}.
This space contains Q , embeds into L^{\infty} , and is a Banach algebra. Moreover, cutting off the slow tail produces an arbitrarily small perturbation in \mathcal{X}_{s} . Our spectral analysis follows the strategy of obtaining dissipativity modulo a compact perturbation; see [52, 54, 9, 21]. To apply complex spectral theory, we use the matrix formulation and the associated \mathcal{J} -invariance argument as in [60, 46]. The decay of the profile and its derivatives gives quadratic spatial decay for the potential coefficients of the linearized operator. An energy estimate, together with compact-perturbation theory, shows that the essential growth bound of the linearized semigroup is negative. After removing the finitely many neutral and unstable generalized eigenspaces, the linearized semigroup decays exponentially on the remaining stable subspace; see Proposition 4.7.
The nonlinear remainder is at least quadratic in v in the \mathcal{X}_{s} norm. We establish nonlinear stability using the standard Lyapunov–Perron construction; see [67, Section 9.2]. A contraction argument selects the neutral and unstable components of the initial perturbation as functions of its stable component, so that v decays exponentially in self-similar time. This defines a finite-codimensional manifold of initial data for V .
To construct localized data on this manifold, we use a finite-dimensional localization argument related to the constructions in [49, 46]. We first replace Q by \chi_{R}Q , where \chi_{R} is a smooth radial cutoff at a large radius R . We then add a small correction g_{R} from a fixed finite-dimensional space of smooth compactly supported radial functions. A further contraction chooses g_{R} so that V(0)=\chi_{R}Q+g_{R} belongs to this manifold. This proves Proposition 4.8 and gives smooth compactly supported initial data for the original equation.

2. Self-similar profile equation
We seek exact self-similar solutions of (1) in the complex variable. Denote the nonlinearity by
N(q)=|q|^{p-1}q=q^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\overline{q}^{\,\frac{p-1}{2}}.
For T>0 , use the similarity coordinates
(4)
\tau=\log\frac{T}{T-t},\qquad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}}.
Thus d\tau/dt=(T-t)^{-1} and t\uparrow T corresponds to \tau\to\infty . For \Omega\in\mathbb{R} , set
(5)
u(t,x)=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{-i\Omega\tau}Q(y),\qquad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}}.
Since \partial_{t}\tau=(T-t)^{-1} , \partial_{t}y=y/[2(T-t)] , substitution into (1) gives the profile equation
(6)
\Delta_{y}Q-|Q|^{p-1}Q+\Omega Q+i\left(\frac{1}{p-1}Q+\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla_{y}Q\right)=0.
If a nonzero bounded smooth profile Q exists globally, (5) gives an exact solution with
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}.
However, these solutions may not have finite energy (3). For the exact ansatz (5), scaling gives
(7)
E(u(t))=(T-t)^{s_{c}-1}E(Q).
If E(Q)<\infty and the associated solution lies in a class where energy is conserved, then s_{c}>1 forces E(Q)=0 and thus Q=0 . Thus the nontrivial profiles must be localized to prove Theorem 1.1, as in [52].
We now restrict to radial self-similar profiles and study their asymptotic behavior at the origin and at infinity. For a radial profile, write Q(y)=Q(r) with r=|y| . Equation (6) becomes
(8)
Q^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}Q^{\prime}-|Q|^{p-1}Q+\Omega Q+i\left(\frac{1}{p-1}Q+\frac{r}{2}Q^{\prime}\right)=0,\qquad r>0.
After fixing the constant phase, we prescribe
(9)
Q(0)=A>0,\qquad Q^{\prime}(0)=0.
Smooth radial profiles extend evenly across r=0 . Write Q(r)=A+Br^{2}+O(r^{4}) , so that
Q^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{d-1}{r}Q^{\prime}(r)=2dB+O(r^{2}),\qquad\frac{r}{2}Q^{\prime}(r)=O(r^{2}).
The coefficients in (8) therefore give 2dB-A^{p}+\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)A=0 , and hence
Q(r)=A+\frac{A}{2d}\left(A^{p-1}-\Omega-\frac{i}{p-1}\right)r^{2}+O(r^{4}).
At infinity, the two leading behaviors are determined by the linear equation
q^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)q^{\prime}+\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q=0.
We first study this asymptotic regime formally. Exact linear solutions with these asymptotics are constructed in Subsection 2.1. For an algebraic ansatz q(r)=r^{\alpha} , substitution gives
q^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)q^{\prime}+\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q=r^{\alpha}\left[\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}+\frac{i\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+d-2)}{r^{2}}\right].
Thus the leading coefficient vanishes precisely when \alpha=-\frac{2}{p-1}+2i\Omega . This balance is consistent with the nonlinear equation. Thus q(r)=C_{s}r^{-2/(p-1)+2i\Omega} , and the nonlinearity becomes
|q|^{p-1}q=|C_{s}|^{p-1}r^{-2}q.
Both the radial Laplacian and the nonlinearity are therefore of order r^{-2} relative to q .
The second behavior includes a quadratic phase oscillatory factor. Substituting q(r)=e^{-ir^{2}/4}r^{\beta} , for which
\frac{q^{\prime}}{q}=-\frac{ir}{2}+\frac{\beta}{r},\qquad\frac{q^{\prime\prime}}{q}=-\frac{r^{2}}{4}-i\left(\beta+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{\beta(\beta-1)}{r^{2}},
gives
q^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)q^{\prime}+\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q=q\left[\Omega+i\left(\frac{1}{p-1}-\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)+\frac{\beta(\beta+d-2)}{r^{2}}\right].
The terms of order r^{2}q cancel, and the remaining leading coefficient vanishes when \beta=-d+\frac{2}{p-1}-2i\Omega . For this ansatz, |q|^{p-1}=r^{(-d+2/(p-1))(p-1)} , so the nonlinearity is again lower order. The formal balances yield the following tail modes, which we refer to as slow and fast modes according to their leading terms,
\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{slow}}(r)
\displaystyle\sim C_{s}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}+2i\Omega},
\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{fast}}(r)
\displaystyle\sim C_{f}e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}r^{-d+\frac{2}{p-1}-2i\Omega}.
The fast oscillation limits derivative integrability. For a nonzero fast mode and an integer j\geq 0 , formally differentiating its asymptotic expansion gives
\frac{d^{j}}{dr^{j}}Q_{\mathrm{fast}}(r)=C_{f}\left(-\frac{i}{2}\right)^{j}e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}r^{-d+\frac{2}{p-1}-2i\Omega+j}\left(1+O(r^{-2})\right).
Their squared integrability at infinity is equivalent to convergence of
\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2(-d+\frac{2}{p-1}+j)}r^{d-1}\,dr=\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{2j-d-1+\frac{4}{p-1}}\,dr.
This integral converges exactly when j<d/2-2/(p-1)=s_{c} . Thus a nonzero fast mode has derivatives not in L^{2} at every integer order j>s_{c} . Derivatives of the slow mode instead decay like r^{-2/(p-1)-j} and are square-integrable exactly when j>s_{c} . Hence the slow mode cannot cancel the failure of integrability of the fast mode. However, the nonzero slow tail is not in L^{2} , nor are its first derivatives in L^{2} . Our approach is to remove the fast mode and then localize the slow mode to obtain initial data in every H^{m} .
This section focuses on the construction of a profile satisfying
C_{f}=0,\qquad C_{s}\neq 0,
while the localization of the profile will be studied in Section 4.
For A>0 and \Omega\in\mathbb{R} , let Q_{A,\Omega} denote the unique maximal regular solution of (8)–(9). Note that equation (8) is singular at the origin. The following argument shows that this equation defines a unique smooth radial solution starting at the origin, and that this solution cannot vanish while it exists. This will be used later for the construction matching an interior solution to a slow tail at infinity.
Lemma 2.1.
For each A>0 and \Omega\in\mathbb{R} , the profile problem has a
unique maximal smooth radial solution Q_{A,\Omega} on [0,R_{*}) .
It never vanishes, and if R_{*}<\infty , then
\sup_{r<R_{*}}|Q_{A,\Omega}(r)|=\infty .
The solution depends continuously on (A,\Omega) in C^{1} on
compact intervals of existence.
Proof.
Multiplication of (8) by an integrating factor r^{d-1}e^{ir^{2}/4} gives
\bigl(r^{d-1}e^{\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}Q^{\prime}(r)\bigr)^{\prime}=r^{d-1}e^{\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}\left(N(Q(r))-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)Q(r)\right).
For a regular solution, the expression inside the derivative tends to zero at the origin. Integration therefore yields
(10)
Q^{\prime}(r)=r^{1-d}e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}\int_{0}^{r}\sigma^{d-1}e^{\frac{i\sigma^{2}}{4}}\left(N(Q(\sigma))-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)Q(\sigma)\right)\,d\sigma.
We construct a solution by integrating this identity once more and a fixed point argument. For R>0 , consider the closed ball \mathcal{B}_{R}=\{q\in C([0,R];\mathbb{C}):\|q-A\|_{\infty}\leq 1\} and the map
(\mathcal{T}q)(r)=A+\int_{0}^{r}t^{1-d}e^{-\frac{it^{2}}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d-1}e^{\frac{i\sigma^{2}}{4}}\left(N(q(\sigma))-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q(\sigma)\right)\,d\sigma\,dt.
The integrand in the outer integral extends continuously to zero. Since N is polynomial in the real and imaginary parts, there are constants M,L>0 such that, whenever |z-A|,|w-A|\leq 1 ,
\left|N(z)-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)z\right|\leq M,\qquad\left|N(z)-N(w)-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)(z-w)\right|\leq L|z-w|.
For q\in\mathcal{B}_{R} and 0\leq r\leq R , the exponential factors have modulus one, so
\displaystyle|(\mathcal{T}q)(r)-A|
\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{r}t^{1-d}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d-1}\left|N(q(\sigma))-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q(\sigma)\right|\,d\sigma\,dt
\displaystyle\leq M\int_{0}^{r}t^{1-d}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d-1}\,d\sigma\right)dt=\frac{M}{d}\int_{0}^{r}t\,dt=\frac{Mr^{2}}{2d}.
Similarly, for q,\widetilde{q}\in\mathcal{B}_{R} ,
\displaystyle|(\mathcal{T}q)(r)-(\mathcal{T}\widetilde{q})(r)|
\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{r}t^{1-d}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d-1}\bigl|N(q(\sigma))-N(\widetilde{q}(\sigma))-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)(q(\sigma)-\widetilde{q}(\sigma))\bigr|\,d\sigma\,dt
\displaystyle\leq L\int_{0}^{r}t^{1-d}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d-1}|q(\sigma)-\widetilde{q}(\sigma)|\,d\sigma\,dt=\frac{Lr^{2}}{2d}\|q-\widetilde{q}\|_{\infty}.
Taking the supremum over r\in[0,R] gives
\|\mathcal{T}q-A\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{MR^{2}}{2d},\qquad\|\mathcal{T}q-\mathcal{T}\widetilde{q}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{LR^{2}}{2d}\|q-\widetilde{q}\|_{\infty}.
Choose R so that MR^{2}/(2d)\leq 1 and LR^{2}/(2d)<1 . The contraction mapping theorem gives a unique fixed point. It satisfies (10), with |Q^{\prime}(r)|\leq Mr/d , and hence is C^{1} up to zero with Q(0)=A and Q^{\prime}(0)=0 . Differentiating the integral formula for r>0 shows that it solves the profile equation there. Every regular solution with these initial data lies in \mathcal{B}_{R} on a sufficiently short interval and satisfies the same fixed-point equation. This proves local uniqueness.
To verify smoothness at the origin, substitute \sigma=r\theta in (10):
Q^{\prime}(r)=r\int_{0}^{1}\theta^{d-1}e^{\frac{ir^{2}(\theta^{2}-1)}{4}}\left(N(Q(r\theta))-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)Q(r\theta)\right)\,d\theta.
The even extension Q(-r)=Q(r) is C^{1} , since Q^{\prime}(0)=0 , and satisfies this identity for negative r as well. If this extension is C^{j} , differentiation under the integral on the fixed interval [0,1] shows that the right-hand side is C^{j} . Thus Q is C^{j+1} , and induction proves smoothness and evenness. A smooth even function of r defines a smooth radial function of x .
Away from zero the equation is a smooth first-order system for (Q,Q^{\prime}) , so local uniqueness gives a unique maximal continuation on [0,R_{*}) . If R_{*}<\infty and Q is bounded there, then N(Q)-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)Q is bounded and (10) bounds Q^{\prime} on [0,R_{*}) . The differential equation then bounds Q^{\prime\prime} on [R_{*}/2,R_{*}) . Consequently Q and Q^{\prime} have finite limits at R_{*} , and local existence for the first-order system extends the solution past R_{*} . This contradiction proves the continuation criterion. The contraction construction also gives continuous dependence on the parameters.
Finally, set \rho=|Q|^{2} and J=2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{Q}Q^{\prime}) . Taking twice the imaginary part of (8) multiplied by \overline{Q} gives, for r>0 ,
J^{\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}J+\frac{2}{p-1}\rho+\frac{r}{2}\rho^{\prime}=0.
Here the nonlinear term and \Omega|Q|^{2} are real, and \rho^{\prime}=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline{Q}Q^{\prime}) . It follows that
\frac{d}{dr}\left[r^{d-1}\left(J+\frac{r}{2}\rho\right)\right]=s_{c}r^{d-1}\rho.
Since Q is bounded and Q^{\prime}(r)=O(r) at zero, the expression in brackets tends to zero there. Integration yields
(11)
r^{d-1}\left(2\operatorname{Im}(\overline{Q}Q^{\prime})+\frac{r}{2}|Q|^{2}\right)=s_{c}\int_{0}^{r}\sigma^{d-1}|Q(\sigma)|^{2}\,d\sigma.
Because s_{c}>0 and Q(0)=A>0 , continuity makes the right-hand side strictly positive for every r\in(0,R_{*}) . If Q(r)=0 , the left-hand side would vanish. Hence Q has no zeros on [0,R_{*}) .
∎
2.1. Linear exterior modes
The linear part of the radial profile equation (8) is
(12)
L_{\Omega}q=q^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)q^{\prime}+\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q,\qquad r>0.
Set
(13)
\mu=\frac{1}{p-1}-i\Omega,\quad\kappa=\frac{d}{2}-\mu,
so that \Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}=i\mu , \operatorname{Re}\mu=\frac{1}{p-1}>0 , and \operatorname{Re}\kappa=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}>0 . The following lemma constructs a fundamental system of solutions of the linear equation L_{\Omega}q=0 on (0,\infty) , with the previously stated asymptotics at infinity.
Lemma 2.2.
The equation L_{\Omega}q=0 on (0,\infty) has solutions given by
(14)
\displaystyle S_{\Omega}(r)
\displaystyle=\frac{4^{-\mu}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}t}{4}}t^{\mu-1}(1+it)^{\frac{d}{2}-1-\mu}\,dt,
\displaystyle F_{\Omega}(r)
\displaystyle=\frac{4^{-\kappa}e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}}{\Gamma(\kappa)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}t}{4}}t^{\kappa-1}(1-it)^{\frac{d}{2}-1-\kappa}\,dt.
Both integrals converge absolutely for every r>0 .
Proof.
The change of variables x=r^{2}/4 , with q(r)=g_{\mu}(x) , transforms L_{\Omega}q=0 into
xg_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}+(d/2+ix)g_{\mu}^{\prime}+i\mu g_{\mu}=0.
To exploit the affine dependence of the coefficients on x , seek a Laplace integral representation
g_{\mu}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}h_{\mu}(t)\,dt.
Substituting this ansatz and integrating the terms containing x by parts formally gives
xg_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}+(d/2+ix)g_{\mu}^{\prime}+i\mu g_{\mu}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xt}\bigl(t(t-i)h_{\mu}^{\prime}+\bigl((2-d/2)t+i(\mu-1)\bigr)h_{\mu}\bigr)\,dt,
provided the boundary term [e^{-xt}t(t-i)h_{\mu}(t)]_{0}^{\infty} vanishes. Thus it suffices to solve the first-order equation
t(t-i)h_{\mu}^{\prime}+\bigl((2-d/2)t+i(\mu-1)\bigr)h_{\mu}=0,\quad\text{equivalently}\quad\frac{h_{\mu}^{\prime}}{h_{\mu}}=\frac{\mu-1}{t}+\frac{i(d/2-1-\mu)}{1+it}.
Integration yields, up to a multiplicative constant,
h_{\mu}(t)=t^{\mu-1}(1+it)^{\frac{d}{2}-1-\mu}.
This function is O(t^{1/(p-1)-1}) at zero and O(t^{d/2-2}) at infinity. Consequently its Laplace integral converges absolutely for x>0 , can be differentiated under the integral sign to every order, and has the required vanishing boundary term. The preceding calculation is therefore justified. Multiplication by 4^{-\mu}/\Gamma(\mu) gives the stated formula for S_{\Omega} .
To obtain the second solution, extract the quadratic oscillatory factor by seeking q(r)=e^{-ix}g_{\mu}(x) , again with x=r^{2}/4 . Direct substitution gives
L_{\Omega}q=e^{-ix}\bigl(xg_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}+(d/2-ix)g_{\mu}^{\prime}-i\kappa g_{\mu}\bigr),
since \mu+\kappa=d/2 . The equation in parentheses is the preceding transformed equation with i replaced by -i and \mu by \kappa . The same Laplace integral construction therefore gives the function t^{\kappa-1}(1-it)^{d/2-1-\kappa} . It is integrable at zero because \operatorname{Re}\kappa>0 and has polynomial growth at infinity, so the same convergence and integration-by-parts arguments apply. Multiplication by 4^{-\kappa}/\Gamma(\kappa) and restoration of the factor e^{-ix} yield F_{\Omega} . These representations are consistent with the standard confluent hypergeometric integral formulas; see [57, Sections 13.2 and 13.4].
Changing variables t\mapsto 4t/r^{2} gives
\displaystyle S_{\Omega}(r)
\displaystyle=r^{-2\mu}\left(1+\frac{4i\mu(d/2-1-\mu)}{r^{2}}+O_{\Omega}(r^{-4})\right),
\displaystyle F_{\Omega}(r)
\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}r^{-2\kappa}\left(1+O_{\Omega}(r^{-2})\right).
The same rescaling applied to the differentiated integrals justifies differentiation of these asymptotic expansions. Abel’s identity and the leading asymptotics then give
(15)
W_{\Omega}=S_{\Omega}F_{\Omega}^{\prime}-S_{\Omega}^{\prime}F_{\Omega}=-\frac{i}{2}r^{1-d}e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}.
Thus W_{\Omega}(r)\neq 0 for every r>0 , proving that S_{\Omega} and F_{\Omega} form a fundamental system.
∎
For a nonlinear profile with a nonzero slow tail, C_{s} denotes the leading slow coefficient. We define the fast coefficient C_{f} by requiring that there exist constants c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C} with
(16)
Q(r)=C_{s}r^{\beta}\bigl(1+c_{1}r^{-2}+c_{2}r^{-4}\bigr)+C_{f}F_{\Omega}(r)+o(r^{-d+\frac{2}{p-1}}),\qquad\beta=-\frac{2}{p-1}+2i\Omega.
Note that here F_{\Omega} carries the factor e^{-ir^{2}/4} . Comparing the distinct decay rates in (16) determines C_{s} and C_{f} uniquely. For our parameter pairs, 4/(p-1)<d<6+4/(p-1) , so the fast mode decays faster than the leading slow mode, while r^{\operatorname{Re}\beta-6}=o(r^{-d+2/(p-1)}) .
The exterior construction below proves this expansion with C_{f}=0 . Considering subsequent nonlinear slow terms is necessary in this definition since, in the case of (d,p)=(5,7) , the first term of F_{\Omega} is of order O(r^{-14/3}) , while the first two nonlinear slow terms are of orders O(r^{\beta-2})=O(r^{-7/3}) and O(r^{\beta-4})=O(r^{-13/3}) , respectively.
3. Construction of the self-similar profile
To construct a profile with C_{f}=0 and C_{s}\neq 0 , we first construct the slow exterior solution and obtain rigorous bounds on its value and derivative at a finite radius. We then match it to the regular-origin solution by Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem. All statements established by computation are collected in Lemma 3.10. The code is available in the supplementary material and at [7]. The estimates that justify the computation are proved below.
The computer-assisted shooting and matching strategy used in this subsection is inspired by the work in [26]. As in their construction, we build solutions satisfying the prescribed conditions at the origin and at infinity and certify that they match at an intermediate radius. Related computer-assisted constructions have appeared in many works, for example, [72, 44].
Use the parameters
\vartheta=(A,\Omega,K),\qquad A>0,\quad K>0,
where A is the value of the profile at the origin, \Omega is the self-similar frequency, and K=|C_{s}|^{p-1} relates to the leading coefficient of the slow tail at infinity. We introduce the variable K because it naturally enters the nonlinear expansion at infinity. Let \vartheta_{0}=(A_{0},\Omega_{0},K_{0}) be the parameters supplied by the computer-assisted code. These parameters were found using a shooting and continuation method to discover approximate solutions of the profile equation. They can be found in the accompanying code. Their approximate values are
\begin{array}[]{c|rrr}(d,p)&A_{0}&\Omega_{0}&K_{0}\\
\hline\cr(3,27)&0.0501485544258024&-1.3966245573341954&70.8930132091120\\
(4,9)&0.0014126481622802&-2.9442997039706108&230.5787044287510\\
(5,7)&0.0409508188010580&-1.7226834479870780&120.4293553317432\end{array}
Define the parameter cube
(17)
D=\{\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\rho\},\qquad\rho=\begin{cases}2^{-240},&(d,p)=(3,27),\\
2^{-320},&(d,p)=(4,9),\\
2^{-220},&(d,p)=(5,7).\end{cases}
This small parameter cube is needed in the computer-assisted proof to control the propagation of the error bound. Our shooting method is extremely sensitive to the parameters, and the smallness of \rho , together with the use of multiple-precision arithmetic, is necessary to obtain a rigorous enclosure of the solution. The next theorem shows that the parameters (A_{0},\Omega_{0},K_{0}) can be perturbed inside the parameter cube D to yield a global regular profile with a slow tail at infinity.
Theorem 3.1.
There is a \vartheta_{*}=(A_{*},\Omega_{*},K_{*})\in D such that
the regular profile Q_{A_{*},\Omega_{*}} is global and has
C_{f}=0,\qquad|C_{s}|=K_{*}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}>0.
In particular, the profile is smooth, regular at the origin, and nonvanishing at every finite radius. At infinity it has a slow expansion to every algebraic order, as specified in Corollary 3.5, up to a constant unit phase.
To construct this profile, we first build a finite slow-tail expansion and bound its residual. Next, using the two linear modes at infinity, we construct a right inverse whose corrections have zero limiting modal coefficients, and use it to obtain an exact exterior solution with C_{s}=K^{1/(p-1)} and C_{f}=0 . The exterior construction is then shown to have a full slow expansion and to depend continuously on the parameters.
We then construct a finite Taylor approximation at the origin and use a contraction argument for the integral equation to enclose the exact regular solution starting from the origin and its initial data. We then define the exact and finite three-component matching maps; a zero of the exact map joins the inner and outer solutions up to a constant unit phase. Finally, flow estimates propagate the finite inner and outer enclosures, the computer-assisted verification checks the resulting boundary, propagation, nonvanishing, and matching bounds, and the Brouwer fixed point theorem yields the required matching zero.
3.1. Exterior solution at infinity
We now start by constructing a finite approximation to the slow exterior solution for the whole nonlinear equation. The leading slow asymptotic mode of the linear exterior equation L_{\Omega}q=0 is K^{1/(p-1)}r^{\beta} , where \beta=-2/(p-1)+2i\Omega . We correct this mode by a finite expansion in inverse even powers of r , chosen so that the first J orders of the nonlinear profile equation cancel. For J\geq 1 , define
(18)
P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)=K^{\frac{1}{p-1}}r^{\beta}e^{\Phi_{J}(r^{-2})},\quad\Phi_{J}(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{J}\ell_{n}t^{n}.
To derive a recursive relation for the coefficients, write t=r^{-2} and temporarily regard \Phi(t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\ell_{n}t^{n} as a formal power series. The logarithmic derivative of the corresponding formal ansatz with respect to \log r is
\beta-2t\Phi^{\prime}(t)=:F(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}t^{n},
and hence
f_{0}=\beta,\qquad f_{n}=-2n\ell_{n},\qquad n\geq 1.
We now define the two remaining series that enter after substitution:
(19)
\displaystyle e^{(p-1)\operatorname{Re}\Phi(t)}
\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}E_{n}t^{n},
(20)
\displaystyle F(t)^{2}+(d-2)F(t)-2tF^{\prime}(t)
\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}H_{n}t^{n}.
Differentiating (19) and comparing coefficients gives
(21)
E_{0}=1,\qquad E_{n}=\frac{p-1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\operatorname{Re}\ell_{j}\,E_{n-j},\qquad n\geq 1.
Expanding (20) gives directly
H_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}f_{j}f_{n-j}+(d-2-2n)f_{n},\qquad n\geq 0,
Finally, after division by the corresponding formal ansatz, equation (8) formally reads
t\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}(H_{n}-KE_{n})t^{n}-i\Phi^{\prime}(t)\right)=0.
Thus the coefficient of t^{n+1} vanishes precisely when
(22)
\ell_{n+1}=\frac{i}{n+1}(KE_{n}-H_{n}),\qquad n\geq 0.
In particular, the case n=0 yields \ell_{1}=i\{K-\beta(\beta+d-2)\} . Thereafter, the coefficients are determined successively: once \ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n} are known, so are E_{n} and H_{n} , and the last formula determines \ell_{n+1} . The approximation (18) retains the first J coefficients of this formal expansion. The next part of the construction concerns the analysis of the residual of this finite approximation.
We next estimate the residual of P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} on [r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty) . In the definitions above, we now take \Phi=\Phi_{J} and hence regard F and H as polynomials. We set the rescaled variables
u=(r_{\mathrm{out}}/r)^{2},\qquad L_{n}=\ell_{n}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2n}\quad(1\leq n\leq J),
and introduce the rescaled polynomials
\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u):=\Phi_{J}(r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2}u)=\sum_{n=1}^{J}L_{n}u^{n},\qquad G(u):=H(r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2}u)=\sum_{n=0}^{2J}G_{n}u^{n}.
Thus the new variable satisfies 0\leq u\leq 1 and \Phi_{J}(r^{-2})=\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u) . For \nu\in\mathbb{R} , let X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} be the space of continuous complex functions on [r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty) with weighted norm
\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}:=\sup_{r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}}}r^{\nu}|f(r)|.
The preceding coefficient recursions yield the following estimates. We need to have explicit constants in the bounds to use them in the computer-assisted proof.
Lemma 3.2.
Recall that N(q)=|q|^{p-1}q . For any \chi>1 , define
(23)
\mathcal{B}=\sum_{n=J}^{2J}|G_{n}|+K\chi^{-J}\exp\left((p-1)\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}|\chi^{n}\right),\qquad M=K^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}|\right),
Then the residual of P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} is
(24)
L_{\Omega}P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})=P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)\left\{\frac{u}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}}\left(G(u)-Ke^{(p-1)\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)}\right)-iu\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\prime}(u)\right\}.
Moreover, for 0\leq u\leq 1 , we have the estimates
(25)
\displaystyle\left|\frac{u}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}}\left(G(u)-Ke^{(p-1)\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)}\right)-iu\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\prime}(u)\right|
\displaystyle\leq r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2}\mathcal{B}\,u^{J+1},
\displaystyle\|L_{\Omega}P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}
\displaystyle\leq Mr_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J}\mathcal{B},\qquad\nu=\frac{2}{p-1}+2J+2.
Proof.
Direct substitution of P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} into the profile equation, using the definitions of F and G , gives (24). The coefficient recursions from above make every coefficient through degree J of the expression in braces vanish.
Write
e^{(p-1)\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}e_{m}u^{m},\qquad\exp\left((p-1)\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}|u^{n}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}u^{m}.
The Cauchy product formula gives |e_{m}|\leq b_{m} for every m\geq 0 , and b_{m}\geq 0 . After factoring out u/r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2} , the term involving \Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\prime} has degree at most J-1 . Hence the cancellations through degree J in the expression in braces are equivalent to cancellation of the coefficients of degrees 0,\ldots,J-1 in the bracket, and thus
G(u)-Ke^{(p-1)\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)}-ir_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\prime}(u)=\sum_{m=J}^{2J}G_{m}u^{m}-K\sum_{m=J}^{\infty}e_{m}u^{m}.
For 0\leq u\leq 1 and \chi>1 , it follows that
\displaystyle\left|G(u)-Ke^{(p-1)\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)}-ir_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\prime}(u)\right|
\displaystyle\leq u^{J}\left(\sum_{m=J}^{2J}|G_{m}|+K\sum_{m=J}^{\infty}|e_{m}|\right)
\displaystyle\leq u^{J}\left(\sum_{m=J}^{2J}|G_{m}|+K\chi^{-J}\sum_{m=J}^{\infty}b_{m}\chi^{m}\right)
\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}u^{J},
because \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}\chi^{m}=\exp((p-1)\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}|\chi^{n}) . Multiplication by u/r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2} proves the first bound in (25).
Finally, for 0\leq u\leq 1 , |\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}| . Hence |P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)|\leq Mr^{-2/(p-1)} for r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}} . Combining this amplitude bound with u=(r_{\mathrm{out}}/r)^{2} gives the second estimate in (25).
∎
To correct P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} to an exact exterior solution, write Q^{\mathrm{out}}=P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+h . Then
L_{\Omega}h=N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+h)-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})-\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{out}},
where the exterior residual is \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{out}}(r):=(L_{\Omega}P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})(r)-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)) . Here L_{\Omega} is the linear exterior operator from (12). We therefore need an inverse for L_{\Omega} whose output has zero limiting slow and fast modal coefficients, so that the prescribed values of C_{s} and C_{f} are unchanged.
Recall the weighted space X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} defined above. For f\in X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} , use the variation of parameters formula to define
(\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f)(r):={}S_{\Omega}(r)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{F_{\Omega}(\sigma)f(\sigma)}{W_{\Omega}(\sigma)}\,d\sigma\\
-F_{\Omega}(r)\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{S_{\Omega}(\sigma)f(\sigma)}{W_{\Omega}(\sigma)}\,d\sigma.
The following lemma shows that this gives a right inverse to L_{\Omega} on the weighted space X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} and provides explicit bounds for the inverse. These bounds must be compatible with the computer-assisted verification, so the constants are tracked explicitly.
Lemma 3.3.
Suppose r_{\mathrm{out}}\geq 32 ,
\nu>d-\frac{2}{p-1} , and |\Omega|\leq\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}} , where
\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}:=\begin{cases}7/5,&(d,p)=(3,27),\\
59/20,&(d,p)=(4,9),\\
7/4,&(d,p)=(5,7).\end{cases}
Then, for every f\in X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} , the function \mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f belongs to C^{2}([r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty)) and satisfies the equality L_{\Omega}\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f=f . Moreover, there is an explicit constant \mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} , independent of \Omega and f , such that
(26)
\|\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}},\qquad\|(\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f)^{\prime}\|_{\nu-1,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\leq\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}.
Proof.
Recall \mu and \kappa defined by (13). We first normalize S_{\Omega} and F_{\Omega} using these parameters and derive estimates on the rescaled functions. Let
\widehat{S}_{\Omega}(r):=r^{2\mu}S_{\Omega}(r),\qquad\widehat{F}_{\Omega}(r):=e^{\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}r^{2\kappa}F_{\Omega}(r).
Rescaling (14), we obtain \widehat{S}_{\Omega}=H_{\mu}^{+} and \widehat{F}_{\Omega}=H_{\kappa}^{-} , where
H_{z}^{\pm}(r):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}\left(1\pm\frac{4it}{r^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1-z}\,dt.
We estimate both integrals at once. Write z=x+i\omega , with x>0 and |\omega|\leq\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}} , and set
\alpha:=\frac{d}{2}-1-z,\qquad c(x):=\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}+\max\left\{0,\frac{d}{2}-2-x\right\}.
Since |\arg(1\pm iv)|\leq v and \log|1\pm iv|\leq v for v\geq 0 , the principal powers satisfy
|(1\pm iv)^{\alpha-1}|=\exp\left[\left(\frac{d}{2}-2-x\right)\log|1\pm iv|+\omega\arg(1\pm iv)\right]\leq e^{c(x)v}.
Integrating the derivative in v therefore gives
(27)
|(1\pm iv)^{\alpha}-1|\leq|\alpha|\int_{0}^{v}e^{c(x)w}\,dw\leq|\alpha|ve^{c(x)v}.
For the stated parameter pairs, c(x)\leq 59/20+1/2<32^{2}/4 . Thus \delta_{x}:=1-4c(x)/32^{2}>0 . Subtracting the Gamma integral \Gamma(z)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}\,dt=1 , we infer, for r\geq 32 ,
\displaystyle|H_{z}^{\pm}(r)-1|
\displaystyle\leq\frac{4|\alpha|}{r^{2}|\Gamma(z)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(1-4c(x)/r^{2})t}t^{x}\,dt
\displaystyle=\frac{4|\alpha|\Gamma(x+1)}{r^{2}|\Gamma(z)|}\left(1-\frac{4c(x)}{r^{2}}\right)^{-x-1}\leq\frac{4|\alpha|\Gamma(x+1)}{r^{2}|\Gamma(z)|}\delta_{x}^{-x-1}.
To estimate the derivative, we compute
r\frac{d}{dr}\left(1\pm\frac{4it}{r^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}=\mp\frac{8i\alpha t}{r^{2}}\left(1\pm\frac{4it}{r^{2}}\right)^{\alpha-1}.
The derivative integrand is bounded by a constant times t^{x}e^{-\delta_{x}t} , uniformly for r\geq 32 ; the original integrand is bounded by a constant times e^{-t}t^{x-1}+t^{x}e^{-\delta_{x}t} , by (27). These integrable bounds justify differentiation under the integral and yield
|r(H_{z}^{\pm})^{\prime}(r)|\leq\frac{8|\alpha|}{r^{2}|\Gamma(z)|}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x}e^{-\delta_{x}t}\,dt\\
=\frac{8|\alpha|\Gamma(x+1)}{r^{2}|\Gamma(z)|}\delta_{x}^{-x-1}.
It remains to bound the Gamma quotient uniformly in \omega . Euler’s product formula gives
\frac{\Gamma(x)}{|\Gamma(x+i\omega)|}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}}{(x+n)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\leq R(x),
where
R(x):=\prod_{n=0}^{15}\left(1+\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}}{(x+n)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left[\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}}{2}\left((x+16)^{-2}+(x+16)^{-1}\right)\right].
Indeed, \log(1+t)\leq t bounds the logarithm of the product tail, and the integral comparison gives
\sum_{n=16}^{\infty}(x+n)^{-2}\leq(x+16)^{-2}+\int_{16}^{\infty}(x+t)^{-2}\,dt=(x+16)^{-2}+(x+16)^{-1}.
Define the explicit constants
\displaystyle D(x)
\displaystyle:=4x\sqrt{\left(\frac{d}{2}-1-x\right)^{2}+\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}}\,R(x)\delta_{x}^{-x-1},
\displaystyle D_{S}
\displaystyle:=D\left(\frac{1}{p-1}\right),\qquad D_{F}:=D\left(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}\right).
Using \Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x) and applying the preceding bounds with z=\mu,\kappa , we obtain
(28)
\displaystyle|\widehat{S}_{\Omega}(r)-1|
\displaystyle\leq D_{S}r^{-2},
\displaystyle|r\widehat{S}_{\Omega}^{\prime}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq 2D_{S}r^{-2},
\displaystyle|\widehat{F}_{\Omega}(r)-1|
\displaystyle\leq D_{F}r^{-2},
\displaystyle|r\widehat{F}_{\Omega}^{\prime}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq 2D_{F}r^{-2}.
Returning to the original modes, we compute
S_{\Omega}^{\prime}(r)=r^{-2\mu-1}\left(-2\mu\widehat{S}_{\Omega}+r\widehat{S}_{\Omega}^{\prime}\right),\qquad F_{\Omega}^{\prime}(r)=e^{-\frac{ir^{2}}{4}}r^{-2\kappa}\left[\widehat{F}_{\Omega}^{\prime}-\left(\frac{ir}{2}+\frac{2\kappa}{r}\right)\widehat{F}_{\Omega}\right].
Hence (28), together with |\mu|\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}} and |\kappa|\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}\right)^{2}+\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}} , yields
(29)
\displaystyle r^{\frac{2}{p-1}}|S_{\Omega}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}},
\displaystyle r^{\frac{2}{p-1}+1}|S_{\Omega}^{\prime}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}},
\displaystyle r^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}}|F_{\Omega}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}},
\displaystyle r^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}-1}|F_{\Omega}^{\prime}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}},
for r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}} , where we choose
\displaystyle A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}:=\max\Biggl\{
\displaystyle 1+\frac{D_{S}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}},\quad 1+\frac{D_{F}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}},
\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2}}+\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{D_{S}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{2D_{S}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}},
\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{\left(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}\right)^{2}+\Omega_{\mathrm{bd}}^{2}}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{D_{F}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{2D_{F}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{4}}\Biggr\}.
Here we used r^{-j}\leq r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-j} for j=2,4 to make the bounds uniform on the exterior interval.
Now we will show that the inverse is well-defined and use the bounds on the unscaled modes to derive bounds on this inverse. Let f\in X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} and write
I_{F}(r):=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{F_{\Omega}(\sigma)f(\sigma)}{W_{\Omega}(\sigma)}\,d\sigma,\qquad I_{S}(r):=\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{S_{\Omega}(\sigma)f(\sigma)}{W_{\Omega}(\sigma)}\,d\sigma.
For each of the three parameter pairs, d-\frac{2}{p-1}>\frac{2}{p-1} , so the assumption on \nu implies both \nu>d-\frac{2}{p-1} and \nu>\frac{2}{p-1} . Using |W_{\Omega}(r)|=r^{1-d}/2 from (15) and (29), we obtain the absolutely convergent tail estimates
(30)
\displaystyle|I_{F}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq 2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\int_{r}^{\infty}\sigma^{\frac{2}{p-1}-\nu-1}\,d\sigma=\frac{2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}}{\nu-\frac{2}{p-1}}\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}r^{\frac{2}{p-1}-\nu},
\displaystyle|I_{S}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq 2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\int_{r}^{\infty}\sigma^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}-\nu-1}\,d\sigma=\frac{2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}}{\nu+\frac{2}{p-1}-d}\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}r^{d-\frac{2}{p-1}-\nu}.
Thus u:=\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f=S_{\Omega}I_{F}-F_{\Omega}I_{S} is well-defined. Since f is continuous, variation of parameters gives u\in C^{2}([r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty)) and L_{\Omega}u=f . In particular, the endpoint terms cancel in the first derivative:
u^{\prime}=S_{\Omega}^{\prime}I_{F}-F_{\Omega}^{\prime}I_{S}.
Combining (29) and (30) with these formulas yields
\displaystyle r^{\nu}|u(r)|
\displaystyle\leq 2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\nu-\frac{2}{p-1}}+\frac{1}{\nu+\frac{2}{p-1}-d}\right)\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}},
\displaystyle r^{\nu-1}|u^{\prime}(r)|
\displaystyle\leq 2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{2}\left(\frac{r^{-2}}{\nu-\frac{2}{p-1}}+\frac{1}{\nu+\frac{2}{p-1}-d}\right)\|f\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}.
Since r\geq 32 , taking suprema proves (26) with
\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}:=2A_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\nu-\frac{2}{p-1}}+\frac{1}{\nu+\frac{2}{p-1}-d}\right).
All constants depend only on d , p , \Omega_{\mathrm{bd}} , r_{\mathrm{out}} , and \nu , and are therefore independent of \Omega and f .
∎
We now use this right inverse to correct the finite approximation in the weighted exterior space X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}} . More precisely, we seek an additive correction h such that Q^{\mathrm{out}}=P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+h solves the full nonlinear profile equation. We solve for this correction using a fixed point argument. Its weighted decay will preserve C_{s}=K^{1/(p-1)} and C_{f}=0 .
Proposition 3.4.
Suppose r_{\mathrm{out}}\geq 32 , K>0 , J\geq 2 , and \Omega satisfies the
frequency bound in Lemma 3.3. Set
\nu=2/(p-1)+2J+2>d-2/(p-1) .
If there is a \delta_{\mathrm{ext}}>0 such that
(31)
\displaystyle p\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2}(M+\delta_{\mathrm{ext}})^{p-1}<1,
\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}M\mathcal{B}}{r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2}}\leq\left[1-p\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2}(M+\delta_{\mathrm{ext}})^{p-1}\right]\delta_{\mathrm{ext}},
\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}<\frac{K^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{M},
then there is a smooth nonvanishing exterior solution Q^{\mathrm{out}} with C_{s}=K^{1/(p-1)} and C_{f}=0 . For r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}} it satisfies
(32)
|Q^{\mathrm{out}}-P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J+2}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-2J-2},\qquad|(Q^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime}-(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})^{\prime}|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J+2}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-2J-1}.
Proof.
We apply the inverse from Lemma 3.3 to the correction equation. The three conditions in (31) ensure, respectively, contraction, invariance of the correction ball, and nonvanishing of the resulting profile.
Since \operatorname{Re}\beta=-2/(p-1) ,
|P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)|=K^{\frac{1}{p-1}}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}e^{\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)}.
For 0\leq u\leq 1 ,
|\operatorname{Re}\Phi_{r_{\mathrm{out}}}(u)|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}|u^{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{J}|\operatorname{Re}L_{n}|.
Therefore
|P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)|\geq\frac{K^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}{M}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}.
This lower bound will prevent the correction from producing a zero.
Consider the closed ball \{h\in X_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}:\|h\|_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J+2}\} . For h in this ball and r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}} ,
|h(r)|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J+2}r^{-\nu}=\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J+2}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-2J-2}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}.
Consequently, |P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+h|\leq(M+\delta_{\mathrm{ext}})r^{-2/(p-1)} . We consider the map
(33)
h\longmapsto\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}\{N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+h)-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})-\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{out}}\}.
Since \|DN(q)\|\leq p|q|^{p-1} , the first condition in (31) makes (33) a contraction. At zero, the inverse estimate and (25) give the norm bound \mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}Mr_{\mathrm{out}}^{2J}\mathcal{B} . Thus the second condition in (31) makes this ball invariant. Banach’s fixed-point theorem gives h , and the derivative estimate in Lemma 3.3 yields (32).
The inverse identity gives the profile equation. Since
|h(r)|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}<K^{\frac{2}{p-1}}M^{-1}r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}\leq|P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}(r)|,
the profile is nonvanishing. Smoothness follows from the equation. Also, r^{2\mu}Q^{\mathrm{out}}\to K^{1/(p-1)} by the enclosure. Moreover, for J\geq 2 the approximation P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} agrees with |C_{s}|r^{\beta}(1+c_{1}r^{-2}+c_{2}r^{-4}) , the slow part in (16), up to O(r^{-2/(p-1)-6}) , where c_{1}=\ell_{1} and c_{2}=\ell_{2}+\ell_{1}^{2}/2 . Both this difference and h are o(r^{-d+2/(p-1)}) . The definition of the nonlinear fast coefficient therefore gives C_{f}=0 .
∎
In this proposition the parameter \delta_{\mathrm{ext}} measures the size of the correction. In the following argument, we will use two different choices of this parameter. The first one will be used to show uniqueness of the exterior solution, and the second choice will be used inside the computer-assisted argument.
The exterior construction above is initially carried out at one finite order. To use the resulting profile in the later global estimates, we need a single exterior solution with an expansion to every algebraic order, rather than a different solution at each truncation order.
Corollary 3.5.
The exterior solution constructed in
Proposition 3.4 satisfies,
for every integer J\geq 1 ,
(34)
\displaystyle Q^{\mathrm{out}}
\displaystyle=P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+O(r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-2J-2}),
\displaystyle(Q^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime}
\displaystyle=(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})^{\prime}+O(r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-2J-3}).
In particular, its algebraic asymptotic expansion contains only the slow mode.
Proof.
Fix an order J\geq 2 . With \chi=2 , the residual constant in (23) remains bounded as r_{\mathrm{out}}\to\infty , while M,K^{2/(p-1)}/M\to K^{1/(p-1)} . Set \delta_{\mathrm{ext}}=2\mathfrak{C}_{\nu,r_{\mathrm{out}}}M\mathcal{B}/r_{\mathrm{out}}^{2} . For sufficiently large r_{\mathrm{out}} , both \delta_{\mathrm{ext}} and the contraction constant tend to zero. Hence the three conditions in (31) hold. Thus, for each fixed J , Proposition 3.4 applies and gives an exact exterior solution
Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}=P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}+O(r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-2J-2}),\qquad r\geq R_{J}.
We now show that these constructions, that depend on J , define one solution. Let Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J_{1})} and Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J_{2})} be two of them, and work on the common region r\geq R_{*}:=\max\{R_{J_{1}},R_{J_{2}}\} . Since J_{1},J_{2}\geq 2 , their difference w satisfies
L_{\Omega}w=N(Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J_{1})})-N(Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J_{2})})=:f,\qquad w=O(r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-6}),\qquad f=O(r^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-8}).
Since both terms in w-\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f decay faster than the slow and fast modes, its coefficients in the fundamental system \{S_{\Omega},F_{\Omega}\} vanish. Then this homogeneous solution is zero, and hence w=\mathcal{L}_{\Omega,\infty}^{-1}f . If |Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J_{1})}|,|Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J_{2})}|\leq M_{*}r^{-2/(p-1)} , then
|f|\leq pM_{*}^{p-1}r^{-2}|w|.
Lemma 3.3 consequently gives
\|w\|_{2/(p-1)+6,r_{\mathrm{out}}}\leq p\mathfrak{C}_{2/(p-1)+6,r_{\mathrm{out}}}M_{*}^{p-1}r_{\mathrm{out}}^{-2}\|w\|_{2/(p-1)+6,r_{\mathrm{out}}}.
For sufficiently large R_{*} the coefficient is less than one, so w=0 on the common exterior region. ODE uniqueness then extends this equality throughout the common interval of regularity. We denote the resulting common solution simply by Q^{\mathrm{out}} . The solution certified at the computational r_{\mathrm{out}} satisfies the same tail bounds by (32), so this uniqueness argument identifies it with Q^{\mathrm{out}} , first on a tail and then on [r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty) by ODE uniqueness.
The estimate for Q_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} now applies to this common Q^{\mathrm{out}} , proving the first line of (34). Applying the derivative estimate in (32) at order J+1 , and using (P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J+1)})^{\prime}-(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})^{\prime}=O(r^{-2/(p-1)-2J-3}) , proves the second line. The case J=1 follows by truncating the expansion at order two, for both the value and the derivative.
∎
The matching argument that will be used varies (\Omega,K) over the parameter cube D . It therefore requires \bigl(Q^{\mathrm{out}}(r_{\mathrm{out}}),(Q^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime}(r_{\mathrm{out}})\bigr) to depend continuously on (\Omega,K) . The three inequalities in the following corollary are the same as those in (31), except that they are now required to hold uniformly.
Corollary 3.6.
Suppose there is a single \delta_{\mathrm{ext}}>0
such that all three conditions (31)
hold with this same \delta_{\mathrm{ext}} for every (\Omega,K) satisfying
|\Omega-\Omega_{0}|\leq\rho and |K-K_{0}|\leq\rho ,
with \rho as in (17).
Then the map
(\Omega,K)\longmapsto\bigl(Q^{\mathrm{out}}(r_{\mathrm{out}}),(Q^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime}(r_{\mathrm{out}})\bigr),
is continuous.
Proof.
Let (\Omega_{n},K_{n})\to(\Omega,K) in the parameter rectangle above, and denote the corresponding exterior solutions by Q_{n}^{\mathrm{out}} . The coefficients of P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} depend continuously on (\Omega,K) . Thus (32) bounds Q_{n}^{\mathrm{out}} and (Q_{n}^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime} uniformly on every compact interval of [r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty) , and the profile equation also bounds (Q_{n}^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime\prime} . By Arzelà–Ascoli and a diagonal extraction, every subsequence has a further subsequence converging in C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty)) .
Any such limit solves the profile equation at (\Omega,K) and inherits the enclosure (32), by passing to the limit at each r\geq r_{\mathrm{out}} . Its difference from Q^{\mathrm{out}} at (\Omega,K) is therefore O(r^{-2/(p-1)-2J-2}) , hence O(r^{-2/(p-1)-6}) because J\geq 2 . The uniqueness argument in Corollary 3.5 identifies the two solutions on a sufficiently distant tail, and ODE uniqueness extends their equality to [r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty) .
Consequently the entire sequence converges to Q^{\mathrm{out}} in C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([r_{\mathrm{out}},\infty)) . Evaluation at r_{\mathrm{out}} proves the claimed continuity.
∎
3.2. Interior solution at the origin
We next construct and enclose the profile starting from its data at the origin. First, an even Taylor polynomial is chosen so that the low orders of the profile equation vanish, leaving an explicitly computable finite residual. We then use the integral formula with zero initial data to correct this polynomial to the exact solution Q^{\mathrm{in}} . The resulting value and derivative bounds at r=r_{\mathrm{in}} provide the initial data needed to propagate the inner solution to the matching radius.
Fix r_{\mathrm{in}}>0 and an integer I\geq 1 . Define the inner Taylor polynomial, truncated at order I , by
P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{I}c_{n}r^{2n},\qquad c_{0}=A,
where the coefficients are determined by the recurrence
(35)
\displaystyle 2(n+1)(2n+d)c_{n+1}
\displaystyle=[z^{n}]\bigl(C_{n}(z)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\overline{C}_{n}(z)^{\,\frac{p-1}{2}}\bigr)-\left(\Omega+i\left(\frac{1}{p-1}+n\right)\right)c_{n},
\displaystyle C_{n}(z)
\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}z^{j}.
The recurrence is used for 0\leq n<I . Here [z^{n}] denotes coefficient extraction, and the bar conjugates coefficients. The residual \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{in}}=L_{\Omega}P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)}-N(P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)}) is a polynomial in r^{2} . Its coefficients below degree I vanish identically. We denote its coefficients by d_{n} , so that
(36)
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{in}}(r)=\sum_{n=I}^{pI}d_{n}r^{2n}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{in}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,r_{\mathrm{in}})}\leq H_{0}:=\sum_{n=I}^{pI}|d_{n}|r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2n}.
A direct contraction converts this finite residual bound into a bound for the actual origin solution. Explicit constants are needed in our computer-assisted argument. In the following lemma we set L_{0}=\left|\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right|+p(1/2)^{p-1} , which will be used as a Lipschitz bound for the full right-hand side in a neighborhood of the origin. We will close a fixed point argument in the ball of radius
\delta_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{2r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}H_{0}}{2d-r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}L_{0}}.
Lemma 3.7.
Suppose that |A|\leq 1/2 and set r_{\mathrm{in}} small enough so that
(37)
\frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}L_{0}}{2d}<1,\qquad\sup_{[0,r_{\mathrm{in}}]}|P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)}|+\delta_{\mathrm{in}}<\frac{1}{2},
then there is an exact solution Q^{\mathrm{in}} of (8) with Q^{\mathrm{in}}(0)=A , (Q^{\mathrm{in}})^{\prime}(0)=0 , and
(38)
|Q^{\mathrm{in}}(r_{\mathrm{in}})-P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)}(r_{\mathrm{in}})|\leq\delta_{\mathrm{in}},\qquad|(Q^{\mathrm{in}})^{\prime}(r_{\mathrm{in}})-(P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)})^{\prime}(r_{\mathrm{in}})|\leq\frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}}{d}(H_{0}+L_{0}\delta_{\mathrm{in}}).
Proof.
The inverse with zero data h(0)=h^{\prime}(0)=0 of the ODE h^{\prime\prime}+((d-1)/r+ir/2)h^{\prime}=f is
(T_{0}f)(r)=\int_{0}^{r}t^{1-d}e^{-\frac{it^{2}}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{d-1}e^{\frac{i\sigma^{2}}{4}}f(\sigma)\,d\sigma\,dt.
It satisfies
|(T_{0}f)^{\prime}(r_{\mathrm{in}})|\leq\frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}}{d}\|f\|_{\infty}\qquad\text{ so }\qquad\|T_{0}f\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}{2d}\|f\|_{\infty}.
The definition of \delta_{\mathrm{in}} and the first inequality in (37) give
\frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}H_{0}}{2d}+\frac{r_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}L_{0}}{2d}\delta_{\mathrm{in}}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{in}}.
Hence the map
h\longmapsto T_{0}\{N(P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)}+h)-N(P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)})-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)h-\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{in}}\}
contracts on \|h\|_{\infty}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{in}} . Its fixed point gives the regular-origin solution from (10), by uniqueness, and the bounds for T_{0} give (38). The same uniqueness identifies the constructed function with the smooth profile.
∎
3.3. Matching the inner and outer solutions
This subsection sets up the computer-assisted matching argument by comparing the exact inner and outer solutions selected at the origin and at infinity. We define the corresponding matching maps and state the criterion that will transfer rigorous finite computations to the exact problem.
For the computer-assisted argument, we set the parameters r_{\mathrm{in}},r_{\mathrm{m}},r_{\mathrm{out}} , which represent an inner, matching and outer radius respectively. We use I,J for truncation orders of Taylor polynomials and exterior expansions respectively. These parameters are fixed in the code. Recall the parameter cube is defined by (17).
For each \vartheta=(A,\Omega,K) , we compare two solutions on each shooting interval. The solutions Q^{\mathrm{in}} and Q^{\mathrm{out}} are selected by the desired exact boundary conditions Q^{\mathrm{in}}(0)=A , (Q^{\mathrm{in}})^{\prime}(0)=0 , while Q^{\mathrm{out}} has C_{f}=0 and C_{s}=K^{1/(p-1)} . Meanwhile \widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{in}} and \widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{out}} instead start from computable data. Specifically,
(\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{in}},(\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{in}})^{\prime})(r_{\mathrm{in}})=(P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)},(P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)})^{\prime})(r_{\mathrm{in}}),\qquad(\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{out}},(\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{out}})^{\prime})(r_{\mathrm{out}})=(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)},(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})^{\prime})(r_{\mathrm{out}}).
All four functions solve the same profile ODE. The computable solutions \widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{in}} and \widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{out}} are the ones that we consider in the computer-assisted argument while the true solutions Q^{\mathrm{in}} and Q^{\mathrm{out}} are not explicit, as they exist only by the fixed point arguments above. We bound their difference using estimates from the contraction arguments.
For \sigma\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\} , define the exact and finite-data profile state vectors
\mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma}(r):=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{\vartheta}^{\sigma}(r)\\
(Q_{\vartheta}^{\sigma})^{\prime}(r)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma}(r):=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma}(r)\\
(\widetilde{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma})^{\prime}(r)\end{pmatrix}.
Where these solutions exist and are nonzero, set
\mathcal{H}\begin{pmatrix}q\\
v\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}\log|q|\\
r_{\mathrm{m}}\operatorname{Re}(v/q)\\
r_{\mathrm{m}}\operatorname{Im}(v/q)\end{pmatrix},
and define for \vartheta=(A,\Omega,K) the matching maps
(39)
\begin{aligned} \mathcal{M}(\vartheta)&:=\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{in}})-\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{out}}),\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\vartheta)&:=\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{in}})-\mathcal{H}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}^{\mathrm{out}}),\end{aligned}\qquad\text{at }r=r_{\mathrm{m}}.
These maps measure the mismatch between the inner and outer solutions at the matching radius r_{\mathrm{m}} . Logarithmic coordinates are chosen so that the difference is a ratio as opposed to an absolute difference. The factor r_{\mathrm{m}} makes r_{\mathrm{m}}v/q the derivative of \log q with respect to \log r , whose real and imaginary parts are on a dimensionless scale. A zero of the exact map \mathcal{M} is precisely the condition needed to join the two solutions, while the finite map \widetilde{\mathcal{M}} is the computable object whose value and derivative are used in the computer-assisted existence argument.
The map \mathcal{M} is continuous by Lemma 2.1 and Corollary 3.6, together with existence and nonvanishing on both shooting intervals from Lemma 3.10. The initial data P_{\mathrm{in}}^{(I)} , P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} depend real analytically on the parameters. By C^{1} dependence of the ODE flow on initial data and parameters, and smoothness of \mathcal{H} for q\neq 0 , the map \widetilde{\mathcal{M}} is C^{1} on its domain of regularity. Its derivative is computed using
(40)
D\mathcal{H}\begin{pmatrix}q\\
v\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Re}(u/q)\\
r_{\mathrm{m}}\operatorname{Re}(w/q-vu/q^{2})\\
r_{\mathrm{m}}\operatorname{Im}(w/q-vu/q^{2})\end{pmatrix}.
If \mathcal{M}(\vartheta)=0 , a constant unit phase makes the inner and outer Cauchy data agree at r_{\mathrm{m}} . Then ODE uniqueness joins them into the desired global profile without changing C_{f}=0 or |C_{s}|=K^{1/(p-1)} . The next proposition establishes a criterion for the existence of such a zero.
Proposition 3.8.
Suppose \mathcal{M} and \widetilde{\mathcal{M}} are defined on D ,
with the regularity stated above and radius \rho as in
(17). Let B be an invertible real
3\times 3 matrix. If
\displaystyle\|B\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\vartheta_{0})\|_{\infty}
\displaystyle\leq Y,
\displaystyle\sup_{D}\|B(\mathcal{M}-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})\|_{\infty}
\displaystyle\leq E,
\displaystyle\sup_{D}\|I-BD\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\|_{\infty}
\displaystyle\leq Z,\qquad Y+E+Z\rho<\rho,
then \mathcal{M} has a zero in D . The matrix norm is the operator norm induced by the vector infinity norm.
Proof.
The continuous map T(\vartheta)=\vartheta-B\mathcal{M}(\vartheta) satisfies
\displaystyle\|T(\vartheta)-\vartheta_{0}\|_{\infty}
\displaystyle\leq\|B\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\vartheta_{0})\|_{\infty}+Z\|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|B(\mathcal{M}-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})(\vartheta)\|_{\infty}
\displaystyle\leq Y+Z\rho+E<\rho.
The middle term follows from convexity of D and
\displaystyle\bigl\|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}-B\bigl(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\vartheta)-\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\vartheta_{0})\bigr)\bigr\|_{\infty}=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\bigl[I-BD\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\vartheta_{0}+t(\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}))\bigr](\vartheta-\vartheta_{0})\,dt\right\|_{\infty}
\displaystyle\leq Z\|\vartheta-\vartheta_{0}\|_{\infty}.
Thus T maps the compact convex cube D into itself. Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem gives a fixed point, and invertibility of B makes it a zero of \mathcal{M} .
∎
3.4. Computer-assisted proof of the matching conditions
We check the inner shooting from r_{\mathrm{in}} to r_{\mathrm{m}} and the outer shooting from r_{\mathrm{out}} back to r_{\mathrm{m}} by the same stepwise estimates. On each mesh step, we have four polynomials that approximate the solution and its three derivatives with respect to A,\Omega,K .
Because \widetilde{\mathcal{M}} is defined by the finite shooting solutions at r_{\mathrm{m}} , its value and derivative are bounded by propagating the Cauchy data through the profile equation. The profile ODE can be written as a first-order system for (q,v)=(Q,Q^{\prime}) using the vector field
(41)
f_{\Omega}\left(r,\begin{pmatrix}q\\
v\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
N(q)-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)q-\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)v\end{pmatrix}.
We write Df_{\Omega} for the derivative with respect to (q,v) . On either mesh, we suppress the label \sigma\in\left\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\right\} on \mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma}=(Q_{\vartheta}^{\sigma},(Q_{\vartheta}^{\sigma})^{\prime}) and \widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma}=(\widetilde{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma},(\widetilde{Q}_{\vartheta}^{\sigma})^{\prime}) . The exact and finite-data solutions satisfy
(\mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta})^{\prime}=f_{\Omega}(r,\mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta}),\qquad(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})^{\prime}=f_{\Omega}(r,\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}).
To bound D\widetilde{\mathcal{M}} , differentiate \widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta} with respect to its three real parameters. This gives the three variational equations
\displaystyle(\partial_{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})^{\prime}
\displaystyle=Df_{\Omega}(r,\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})\partial_{A}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta},
\displaystyle(\partial_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})^{\prime}
\displaystyle=Df_{\Omega}(r,\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})\partial_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})_{1}\end{pmatrix},
\displaystyle(\partial_{K}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})^{\prime}
\displaystyle=Df_{\Omega}(r,\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})\partial_{K}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}.
Finally, the propagation estimates require bounds on the Jacobian and its variation. We use the Euclidean norms on \mathbb{C} and \mathbb{C}^{2} , viewed as real vector spaces, and their induced operator norms for derivatives. Direct differentiation of q^{(p+1)/2}\overline{q}^{\,(p-1)/2} gives
\begin{gathered}DN(q)u=\frac{p+1}{2}|q|^{p-1}u+\frac{p-1}{2}q^{2}|q|^{p-3}\overline{u},\\
\|DN(q)\|\leq p|q|^{p-1},\qquad\|D^{2}N(q)\|\leq p(p-1)|q|^{p-2}.\end{gathered}
We now explain how to propagate solutions and bound the error along a mesh step. Consider a mesh step r(u)=r_{*}+hu , 0\leq u\leq 1 , where h\neq 0 may have either sign, and set r_{\min}=\min\{r_{*},r_{*}+h\}>0 . Let P(u) approximate \widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}}(r(u)) , and write p_{q} for its first component. For each parameter label \xi\in\{A,\Omega,K\} , let P_{\xi}(u) approximate \left.\partial_{\xi}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}(r(u))\right|_{\vartheta=\vartheta_{0}} . Primes on these polynomials denote differentiation with respect to u . Their integrated residuals satisfy
(42)
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\bigl\|P^{\prime}-hf_{\Omega_{0}}(r(u),P)\bigr\|\,du
\displaystyle\leq\delta_{0},

\displaystyle(r_{Q})_{+}

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{bd}})_{+}

Lemma 3.9.
Assume the residual bounds (42),
the starting bounds
(43)–
(44),
and the enclosure bounds (45) defining
M_{\mathrm{step}} and V_{\xi} .
If
(48)
(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly}})_{+}+(r_{Q})_{+}+(\delta_{\mathrm{bd}})_{+}<\varepsilon,
then the following bounds hold for every u\in[0,1]
\begin{gathered}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}}-P\|\leq(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly}})_{+},\qquad\sup_{\vartheta\in D}\|\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}}\|\leq(r_{Q})_{+},\qquad\sup_{\vartheta\in D}\|\mathbf{Q}_{\vartheta}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}\|\leq(\delta_{\mathrm{bd}})_{+},\\

Proof.
We first bound the growth of perturbations to the profile flow. For every (\eta,\zeta)\in\mathbb{C}^{2} , direct differentiation of (41) gives
Df_{\Omega}(r,(q,v))(\eta,\zeta)=\left(\zeta,DN(q)\eta-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)\eta-\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)\zeta\right).
If |q|\leq M_{\mathrm{step}} , then \|DN(q)\|\leq pM_{\mathrm{step}}^{p-1} . Moreover, the term -ir\zeta/2 has zero real inner product with \zeta . The radial damping decreases the norm when h>0 , whereas for h<0 its contribution is at most (d-1)/r_{\min} . Consequently,
(49)
\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left\langle(\eta,\zeta),\frac{h}{|h|}Df_{\Omega}(r,(q,v))(\eta,\zeta)\right\rangle
\displaystyle\leq\left(1+|\Omega|+\frac{1}{p-1}+pM_{\mathrm{step}}^{p-1}\right)|\eta||\zeta|
\displaystyle+\frac{d-1}{r_{\min}}\mathbf{1}_{\{h<0\}}|\zeta|^{2}
\displaystyle\leq L\bigl(|\eta|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}\bigr).
We give the details for the polynomial error. Set
\mathcal{E}(u)=\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}}(r(u))-P(u),\qquad\mathcal{R}(u)=P^{\prime}(u)-hf_{\Omega_{0}}(r(u),P(u)).
The fundamental theorem of calculus applied to the vector field yields
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(u)=h\int_{0}^{1}Df_{\Omega_{0}}\bigl(r(u),P(u)+\tau\mathcal{E}(u)\bigr)\mathcal{E}(u)\,d\tau-\mathcal{R}(u).
Thus (49) implies, wherever \mathcal{E}(u)\neq 0 ,
\frac{d}{du}\|\mathcal{E}(u)\|=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{E}(u),\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(u)\rangle}{\|\mathcal{E}(u)\|}\leq|h|L\|\mathcal{E}(u)\|+\|\mathcal{R}(u)\|.
Multiplying by e^{-|h|Lu} and using (42), for 0\leq u\leq 1 we obtain
\displaystyle\|\mathcal{E}(u)\|
\displaystyle\leq e^{|h|Lu}\|\mathcal{E}(0)\|+\int_{0}^{u}e^{|h|L(u-t)}\|\mathcal{R}(t)\|\,dt
\displaystyle\leq e^{|h|Lu}\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly}}+\int_{0}^{u}\|\mathcal{R}(t)\|\,dt\right)\leq g\bigl(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly}}+\delta_{0}\bigr).
This proves the first line of (46). For \widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}-\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}} , the additional source \bigl(0,-(\Omega-\Omega_{0})(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}})_{1}\bigr) has norm at most \rho M_{\mathrm{step}} , giving the second line. The exact and finite solutions at fixed parameter solve the same ODE, giving the third line.
Subtracting the center variational equation from its polynomial approximation gives an additional source bounded by p(p-1)M_{\mathrm{step}}^{p-2}V_{\xi}(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly}})_{+} , together with (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly}})_{+} for the frequency derivative, proving the bound for (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly},\xi})_{+} . For the variation across the parameter cube,
\|Df_{\Omega}(r,\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta})-Df_{\Omega_{0}}(r,\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta_{0}})\|\leq p(p-1)M_{\mathrm{step}}^{p-2}(r_{Q})_{+}+\rho.
Also \|\left.\partial_{\xi}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{\vartheta}\right|_{\vartheta=\vartheta_{0}}\|\leq V_{\xi}+(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{poly},\xi})_{+} throughout the step. The difference of the frequency sources is bounded by (r_{Q})_{+} when \xi=\Omega and vanishes when \xi=A,K . Subtracting the variational equations and applying Gronwall, including the additional source bounded by |h|(r_{Q})_{+} when \xi=\Omega , proves the last estimate.
These estimates are initially valid up to a possible first exit from the enclosure. Their strict sum bound excludes such an exit. The vector field is smooth for r>0 , and bounded solutions continue, proving existence through the step. Finally, (47) and the enclosure bound give a positive lower bound for the modulus.

Suppose a finite endpoint satisfies |q|\geq q_{\min} and |v|\leq v_{\max} , while the exact endpoint is within Euclidean distance \delta_{\mathrm{bd}}<q_{\min} . For the inner endpoints, the amplitude and slope bounds are
\begin{aligned} \left|\log|Q^{\mathrm{in}}|-\log|\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{in}}|\right|&\leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{bd}}}{q_{\min}-\delta_{\mathrm{bd}}},\\

The first step is to generate approximate profiles. A broad exploration in the parameters (d,p,A,\Omega,K) was carried out to look for solutions of the ODE (8). 4. Self-similar blow-up
Fix the pure slow profile of Theorem 3.1, and write
A=A_{*},\qquad\Omega=\Omega_{*},\qquad Q=Q_{A,\Omega}.
All functions and spaces in this section are radial, and constants may depend on the fixed profile. The construction below uses a finite-codimension strategy as in Li [46], together with the stable/unstable semigroup framework of [8]. The evolution is studied around the stationary profile, and localization is implemented by a direct finite-dimensional correction to obtain a finite-energy solution.
In the coordinates of (4), write
u(t,x)=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{-i\Omega\tau}V(\tau,y),\qquad\tau=\log\frac{T}{T-t},\qquad y=\frac{x}{\sqrt{T-t}}.
The rescaled equation is
(51)
\partial_{\tau}V=\mathcal{A}_{0}V-iN(V),\qquad\mathcal{A}_{0}=i\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla_{y}-\frac{1}{p-1}+i\Omega.
The profile satisfies \mathcal{A}_{0}Q=iN(Q) . Hence V=Q+v obeys
(52)
\partial_{\tau}v=\mathcal{A}_{Q}v+\mathcal{F}(v),
Set
(53)
V_{\mathrm{diag}}=\frac{p+1}{2}|Q|^{p-1},\qquad V_{\mathrm{off}}=\frac{p-1}{2}|Q|^{p-3}Q^{2}.
Then the linearized operator \mathcal{A}_{Q} and the nonlinear remainder \mathcal{F} are
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{Q}h
\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{0}h-iV_{\mathrm{diag}}h-iV_{\mathrm{off}}\overline{h},
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v)
\displaystyle=-i\bigl[N(Q+v)-N(Q)-V_{\mathrm{diag}}v-V_{\mathrm{off}}\overline{v}\bigr].
We first translate the asymptotics and nonvanishing of Q into global pointwise bounds for the profile and its derivatives. These bounds place Q in the perturbation spaces used below and ensure that cutting off its tail produces a small perturbation. They also show that the coefficients of the linearized operator decay quadratically, with an additional power of decay for each derivative, as needed for the subsequent spectral estimates.
Lemma 4.1.
There are constants c_{*}>0 and C_{Q,j} such that
(54)
|Q(y)|\geq c_{*}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{2}{p-1}},\qquad|\partial^{\nu}Q(y)|\leq C_{Q,j}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{2}{p-1}-j},\qquad|\nu|=j\geq 0.
In particular,
(55)
|\partial^{\nu}V_{\mathrm{diag}}(y)|+|\partial^{\nu}V_{\mathrm{off}}(y)|\leq C_{Q,j}\langle y\rangle^{-2-j}.
Proof.
Nonvanishing and the nonzero leading slow coefficient give the lower bound. Write r=|y| and take the approximation P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} from Corollary 3.5, with the phase of Q , and put w_{J}=Q-P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)} . Its equation is
\displaystyle w_{J}^{\prime\prime}={}
\displaystyle-\left(\frac{d-1}{r}+\frac{ir}{2}\right)w_{J}^{\prime}-\left(\Omega+\frac{i}{p-1}\right)w_{J}
\displaystyle+N(Q)-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)})-r_{J},
\displaystyle r_{J}={}
\displaystyle L_{\Omega}P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}-N(P_{\mathrm{out}}^{(J)}).
The explicit residual satisfies r_{J}^{(k)}=O(r^{-2/(p-1)-2J-2-k}) . Starting with the bounds for w_{J},w_{J}^{\prime} in that corollary, differentiation of this equation gives w_{J}^{(j)}=O(r^{-2/(p-1)-2J-4+j}) for each fixed j\geq 1 , provided J\geq j-2 . Increasing J proves the radial derivative bounds; smoothness at zero and the formulas for Cartesian derivatives give (54). The coefficient bounds follow by the product rule.

4.1. Functional setting
Recall that s_{c}=d/2-2/(p-1) . For s_{c}<s<d/2 , let
\mathcal{X}_{s}=\dot{H}^{s}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})\cap\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}),\qquad\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}^{2}=\||D|^{s}h\|_{2}^{2}+\||D|^{k_{\star}}h\|_{2}^{2},
where k_{\star}\in\mathbb{N} satisfies
k_{\star}\geq 3,\qquad k_{\star}>\frac{d}{2}+2\|V_{\mathrm{off}}\|_{\infty}.
This ensures (s_{c}-k_{\star})/2+\|V_{\mathrm{off}}\|_{\infty}\leq(s_{c}-s)/2 .
We use its underlying real Hilbert space for the scalar perturbation equation, since \mathcal{A}_{Q} is real-linear. The \dot{H}^{s} representative is fixed by the embedding into L^{2d/(d-2s)} . Equivalently, it is the completion of radial C_{c}^{\infty} in the displayed norm. We record the details of the interpolation estimate that will be used below. For h\in C_{c}^{\infty} and R>0 , Fourier inversion and Cauchy–Schwarz give
(56)
\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\widehat{h}\|_{1}
\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq R}|\widehat{h}(\xi)|\,d\xi+\int_{|\xi|>R}|\widehat{h}(\xi)|\,d\xi
\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{|\xi|\leq R}|\xi|^{-2s}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\left(\int_{|\xi|>R}|\xi|^{-2k_{\star}}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}}
\displaystyle\lesssim_{s}R^{\frac{d}{2}-s}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+R^{\frac{d}{2}-k_{\star}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}}.
Choosing R=(\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}}/\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{s}})^{1/(k_{\star}-s)} balances the two terms and yields
(57)
\|h\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{s}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\frac{k_{\star}-\frac{d}{2}}{k_{\star}-s}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}-s}{k_{\star}-s}}\lesssim_{s}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
Here we used the inequality A^{\theta}B^{1-\theta}\leq\theta A+(1-\theta)B .
Lemma 4.2.
For every s_{c}<s<d/2 , the profile Q belongs to \mathcal{X}_{s} .
Proof.
Fix a smooth radial cutoff \chi , equal to one on |y|\leq 1 and zero on |y|\geq 2 , and set \chi_{R}(y)=\chi(y/R) and \eta(y)=\chi(y)-\chi(2y) . For every R\geq 1 ,
Q=\chi_{R}Q+\sum_{j\geq 1}Q_{j,R},\qquad Q_{j,R}(y)=\eta(y/(2^{j}R))Q(y).
For L=2^{j}R , set g_{j,R}(Y)=L^{2/(p-1)}\eta(Y)Q(LY) . These functions are supported in 1/2\leq|Y|\leq 2 , and (54) gives \|g_{j,R}\|_{H^{k_{\star}}}\leq C_{Q} , uniformly in j,R . Scaling in dimension d yields, for k=s,k_{\star} ,
\|Q_{j,R}\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}=L^{s_{c}-k}\|g_{j,R}\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\leq C_{Q}(2^{j}R)^{s_{c}-k}.
Since k>s_{c} , the series converges in \mathcal{X}_{s} and locally to (1-\chi_{R})Q , with
(58)
\|(1-\chi_{R})Q\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\leq\sum_{j\geq 1}\|Q_{j,R}\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\lesssim_{Q,s}R^{s_{c}-k},\qquad k=s,k_{\star}.
As \chi_{R}Q\in C_{c}^{\infty} , this also proves Q\in\mathcal{X}_{s} .
∎
Estimate (56) gives \|\widehat{h}\|_{1}\lesssim_{s}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}} . Hence, for k=s,k_{\star} , the inequality |\xi|^{k}\lesssim_{k}|\eta|^{k}+|\xi-\eta|^{k} and Young’s convolution inequality give
\|fg\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\lesssim_{k}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\|\widehat{g}\|_{1}+\|\widehat{f}\|_{1}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}.
Thus \mathcal{X}_{s} is an algebra:
(59)
\|fg\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq C_{s}\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
Since p is odd, \mathcal{F}(v) is a polynomial in Q,\overline{Q},v,\overline{v} whose terms have degree at least two in v,\overline{v} . Lemma 4.2 and the algebra estimate therefore give
(60)
\|\mathcal{F}(v)-\mathcal{F}(w)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq C_{Q,s}\bigl(\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}+\|w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}+\|v\|^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}+\|w\|^{p}_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\bigr)\|v-w\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
Lemma 4.3.
\mathcal{A}_{Q} generates a strongly continuous group S_{Q}(\tau) . Moreover, for any v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{s} , there exists T>0 such that a unique mild solution in C([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{s}) solves (52) with initial data v_{0} .
Proof.
The unperturbed operator \mathcal{A}_{0} generates the group S_{0}(\tau)=e^{\tau\mathcal{A}_{0}} , given by
(61)
(S_{0}(\tau)h)(y)=e^{(-\frac{1}{p-1}+i\Omega)\tau}\bigl(e^{i(1-e^{-\tau})\Delta}h\bigr)(e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}y),\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R}.
For k=s,k_{\star} , unitarity of the Schrödinger group on \dot{H}^{k} and \|h(\lambda\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}=\lambda^{k-d/2}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k}} give
\|S_{0}(\tau)h\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}=e^{\frac{(s_{c}-k)\tau}{2}}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k}},
since s_{c}=d/2-2/(p-1) . Thus we have for \tau\geq 0 ,
\|S_{0}(\tau)h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq e^{\frac{(s_{c}-s)\tau}{2}}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
For radial C_{c}^{\infty} data, continuity at \tau=0 follows from dominated convergence in Fourier space and continuity of dilations. Density and the preceding locally uniform operator bound extend this to every h\in\mathcal{X}_{s} , proving strong continuity.
Moreover, V_{\mathrm{diag}} and V_{\mathrm{off}} belong to \mathcal{X}_{s} by Lemma 4.2 and the algebra estimate (59). Thus \mathcal{A}_{Q}-\mathcal{A}_{0} is a bounded real-linear operator on \mathcal{X}_{s} . The bounded perturbation theorem yields a strongly continuous group S_{Q}(\tau)=e^{\tau\mathcal{A}_{Q}} , with D(\mathcal{A}_{Q})=D(\mathcal{A}_{0}) . Now we show the local well-posedness of (52) in \mathcal{X}_{s} . Using the Duhamel formula, we consider
(62)
v(\tau)=S_{Q}(\tau)v_{0}+\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{Q}(\tau-\sigma)\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma.
Estimate (60) makes \mathcal{F} locally Lipschitz near zero. The local bound on S_{Q} thus makes the equation (62) a contraction on a suitable ball in C([0,T];\mathcal{X}_{s}) for T>0 small, proving local existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence for (52).
∎
For the cutoff \chi_{R} in the proof of Lemma 4.2, the estimate (58) shows that localization is small in \mathcal{X}_{s} . The profile bounds and annular scaling give the estimate for the commutator \|[\mathcal{A}_{0},\chi_{R}]Q\|_{\dot{H}^{k}}\lesssim_{Q,s}R^{s_{c}-k}\to 0 for k=s,k_{\star} . Differentiating (61) on Schwartz data gives C^{\infty}_{c,\mathrm{rad}}\subset D(\mathcal{A}_{0}) . Also, the algebra estimate and (58) imply \chi_{R}N(Q)\to N(Q) in \mathcal{X}_{s} . Since \chi_{R}Q\in D(\mathcal{A}_{0}) and
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\chi_{R}Q)=i\chi_{R}N(Q)+[\mathcal{A}_{0},\chi_{R}]Q\longrightarrow iN(Q),
closedness of \mathcal{A}_{0} yields Q\in D(\mathcal{A}_{0}) and \mathcal{A}_{0}Q=iN(Q) . Thus the profile Q is a mild solution of
(63)
Q=S_{0}(\tau)Q-i\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{0}(\tau-\sigma)N(Q)\,d\sigma.
Consequently, subtracting Q identifies a solution of the full nonlinear equation with the \mathcal{X}_{s} perturbation.
4.2. Spectral theory for the linearized operator
Our spectral analysis follows the strategy of obtaining dissipativity
modulo a compact perturbation; see
[52, 54, 9].
We represent the lower-order energy remainder by a compact operator
using the Riesz representation theorem, in a manner analogous to
the construction in [21].
We use the matrix formulation and the associated
\mathcal{J} -invariance argument; see
[60, 46].Fix s\in(s_{c},d/2) and set \gamma_{s}=(s_{c}-s)/2<0 .
The spectral analysis takes place
on the complex Hilbert space
\mathbf{X}_{s}=\mathcal{X}_{s}\times\mathcal{X}_{s},\qquad\|(f,g)\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}^{2}+\|g\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}^{2}\bigr).
Scalar perturbations are identified with the real-linear subspace
(64)
\iota h=\begin{pmatrix}h\\
\overline{h}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{X}_{s,+}=\iota\mathcal{X}_{s}=\{\mathbf{h}\in\mathbf{X}_{s}:\mathcal{J}\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{h}\},\qquad\mathcal{J}\begin{pmatrix}f\\
g\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{g}\\
\overline{f}\end{pmatrix}.
Thus \iota is a real-linear isometry and \mathcal{J} is an antilinear isometric involution.
Let Ch=\overline{h} and \overline{\mathcal{A}_{0}}=C\mathcal{A}_{0}C=-i\Delta-\tfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla_{y}-\frac{1}{p-1}-i\Omega , with domain CD(\mathcal{A}_{0}) . The matrix linearized operator is
\mathbf{A}_{Q}=\mathbf{A}_{0}+\mathbf{B},\qquad\mathbf{A}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{0}&0\\
0&\overline{\mathcal{A}_{0}}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbf{B}=\begin{pmatrix}-iV_{\mathrm{diag}}&-iV_{\mathrm{off}}\\
i\overline{V_{\mathrm{off}}}&iV_{\mathrm{diag}}\end{pmatrix},
where the coefficients V_{\mathrm{diag}} , V_{\mathrm{off}} are defined in (53). Now we apply the bounded perturbation theorem as in the argument of Lemma 4.3 to the diagonal group
\mathbf{S}_{0}(\tau)=\begin{pmatrix}S_{0}(\tau)&0\\
0&CS_{0}(\tau)C\end{pmatrix}.
Thus \mathbf{A}_{Q} , with domain D(\mathcal{A}_{0})\times CD(\mathcal{A}_{0}) , generates a strongly continuous group \mathbf{S}_{Q}(\tau) . Direct computation gives
\mathcal{J}\mathbf{A}_{Q}=\mathbf{A}_{Q}\mathcal{J},\qquad\mathbf{A}_{Q}\iota=\iota\mathcal{A}_{Q},\qquad\mathbf{S}_{Q}(\tau)\iota=\iota S_{Q}(\tau).
The first identity holds on D(\mathbf{A}_{Q}) , which \mathcal{J} preserves, and the second on D(\mathcal{A}_{Q}) ; the group identity follows by uniqueness. In particular, the matrix evolution preserves \mathbf{X}_{s,+} and agrees there with the scalar linearized evolution.
Lemma 4.4.
There is a bounded compact operator \mathcal{R}
on \mathbf{X}_{s} such that
(65)
\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathbf{A}_{Q}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}\leq\gamma_{s}\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}^{2}+\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{R}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}},\qquad\mathbf{h}\in D(\mathbf{A}_{Q}).
The operator \mathbf{A}_{Q}-\mathcal{R} , with domain D(\mathbf{A}_{Q}) , generates a strongly continuous semigroup \mathbf{U} , and
(66)
\|\mathbf{U}(\tau)\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}\to\mathbf{X}_{s}}\leq e^{\gamma_{s}\tau},\qquad\tau\geq 0.
Proof.
We first establish the compactness of the lower-order error terms \mathbf{B}\mathbf{h} in the energy estimate. Let \psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) be radial, equal to one on the unit ball, and supported in the ball of radius two. Let h=h_{\mathrm{lo}}+h_{\mathrm{hi}} , where h_{\mathrm{lo}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\psi(|\xi|)\mathcal{F}h is the low frequency part of h . Since s<d/2 , we have
\|\partial^{\beta}h_{\mathrm{lo}}\|_{\infty}\lesssim_{\beta}\left(\int_{|\xi|\leq 2}|\xi|^{2|\beta|-2s}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
For the high-frequency part, we have \|h_{\mathrm{hi}}\|_{H^{k_{\star}}}\lesssim\|h\|_{\dot{H}^{k_{\star}}} . These estimates give the localization bound below. Let \chi_{L}(y)=\chi(y/L) , where \chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2}) is radial and equals one on B_{1} . For each fixed L , localization maps \mathcal{X}_{s} boundedly into H^{k_{\star}} . Hence, choosing \max\{s,d/2\}<r<k_{\star} , Rellich compactness shows that h\mapsto\chi_{L}h is compact into H^{r} . Since multiplication by any of the factors u\in\{V_{\mathrm{diag}},V_{\mathrm{off}},\overline{V_{\mathrm{off}}}\} is bounded on H^{r} and (\chi_{L}u)h=u(\chi_{L}h) , the corresponding localized multiplication operators are compact from \mathcal{X}_{s} into \dot{H}^{s} .
For the tails, (55) and the same dyadic scaling argument as in Lemma 4.2 give, for u\in\{V_{\mathrm{diag}},V_{\mathrm{off}},\overline{V_{\mathrm{off}}}\} ,
\|(1-\chi_{L})u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\lesssim L^{\frac{d}{2}-2-s}\lesssim L^{s_{c}-s}.
Together with the algebra estimate (59), this gives
\|(1-\chi_{L})V_{\mathrm{diag}}h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}+\|(1-\chi_{L})V_{\mathrm{off}}h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\lesssim L^{s_{c}-s}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
Since s>s_{c} , the right-hand coefficient tends to zero. Therefore
\mathbf{h}\longmapsto|D|^{s}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h}\quad\text{is compact from }\mathbf{X}_{s}\text{ into }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2}).
Now let
\mathcal{C}\mathbf{h}=[\nabla^{k_{\star}},\mathbf{B}]\mathbf{h}.
Each component is a finite sum of terms (\partial^{\alpha}u)\partial^{\beta}h , with u\in\{V_{\mathrm{diag}},V_{\mathrm{off}},\overline{V_{\mathrm{off}}}\} where |\alpha|\geq 1 and |\alpha|+|\beta|=k_{\star} . The localized term \chi_{L}(\partial^{\alpha}u)\partial^{\beta}h is compact into L^{2} by local H^{k_{\star}} control, Rellich compactness, and |\beta|\leq k_{\star}-1 . For its tail, (54) gives:
\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{L})(\partial^{\alpha}u)\partial^{\beta}h_{\mathrm{lo}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(1-\chi_{L})(\partial^{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{\beta}h_{\mathrm{lo}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
\displaystyle\lesssim L^{d/2-2-|\alpha|}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}},
\displaystyle\|(1-\chi_{L})(\partial^{\alpha}u)\partial^{\beta}h_{\mathrm{hi}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(1-\chi_{L})(\partial^{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial^{\beta}h_{\mathrm{hi}}\|_{L^{2}}
\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-2-|\alpha|}\|h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
Both exponents are negative because d\leq 5 and |\alpha|\geq 1 . Thus \mathcal{C} is compact from \mathbf{X}_{s} into L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{2}) as well. It follows that
\mathcal{K}\mathbf{h}=(|D|^{s}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h},\mathcal{C}\mathbf{h}):\mathbf{X}_{s}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is compact, where \mathcal{Y} is the Hilbert direct sum of these two L^{2} spaces, with \|(f,g)\|_{\mathcal{Y}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}) .
Next we work on the energy estimate. The calculations are first made on radial Schwartz pairs. Differentiating the free-group norm identity following (61) gives
\operatorname{Re}\langle|D|^{r}\mathbf{A}_{0}\mathbf{h},|D|^{r}\mathbf{h}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{s_{c}-r}{2}\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{r}}^{2},\qquad r=s,k_{\star}.
At the top order,
\nabla^{k_{\star}}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{h})=\mathbf{B}\nabla^{k_{\star}}\mathbf{h}+\mathcal{C}\mathbf{h}.
Because V_{\mathrm{diag}} is real, the diagonal terms of \mathbf{B} make zero real contribution. For any pair of tensors \mathbf{w} ,
\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\|V_{\mathrm{off}}\|_{\infty}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.
Our choice of k_{\star} implies
\frac{s_{c}-k_{\star}}{2}+\|V_{\mathrm{off}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{s_{c}-s}{2}=\gamma_{s}.
Define the bounded isometry
\mathcal{E}\mathbf{h}=(|D|^{s}\mathbf{h},\nabla^{k_{\star}}\mathbf{h}):\mathbf{X}_{s}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y},\qquad\|\mathcal{E}\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathcal{Y}}=\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}.
Combining the two derivative levels gives
(67)
\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathbf{A}_{Q}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}\leq\gamma_{s}\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}^{2}+\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{K}\mathbf{h},\mathcal{E}\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}.
The free energy identity holds for every \mathbf{h}\in D(\mathbf{A}_{0}) . The estimate for the bounded perturbation \mathbf{B} extends from radial Schwartz pairs to \mathbf{X}_{s} by density. Since D(\mathbf{A}_{Q})=D(\mathbf{A}_{0}) , their combination proves (67) on D(\mathbf{A}_{Q}) .
Use inner products linear in the first argument. The sesquilinear form
\mathfrak{k}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g})=\langle\mathcal{K}\mathbf{h},\mathcal{E}\mathbf{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}
is bounded, since |\mathfrak{k}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g})|\leq\|\mathcal{K}\|\,\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}} . By Riesz representation, there is a unique bounded complex-linear operator \mathcal{R}:\mathbf{X}_{s}\to\mathbf{X}_{s} satisfying
\langle\mathcal{R}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}=\mathfrak{k}(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{g}),\qquad\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{E}^{*}\mathcal{K}.
The operator \mathcal{R} is compact because \mathcal{K} is compact and \mathcal{E}^{*} is bounded. In particular,
\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{R}\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}=\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal{K}\mathbf{h},\mathcal{E}\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}.
Thus the preceding energy estimate proves (65) directly.
Since \mathbf{A}_{Q} already generates \mathbf{S}_{Q} , bounded perturbation by -\mathcal{R} gives the auxiliary group \mathbf{U} , with generator \mathbf{A}_{Q}-\mathcal{R} and domain D(\mathbf{A}_{Q}) . This yields
\operatorname{Re}\langle(\mathbf{A}_{Q}-\mathcal{R})\mathbf{h},\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}\leq\gamma_{s}\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}^{2},\qquad\mathbf{h}\in D(\mathbf{A}_{Q}).
Then
\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|\mathbf{U}(\tau)\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}^{2}=\operatorname{Re}\langle(\mathbf{A}_{Q}-\mathcal{R})\mathbf{U}(\tau)\mathbf{h},\mathbf{U}(\tau)\mathbf{h}\rangle_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}\leq\gamma_{s}\|\mathbf{U}(\tau)\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbf{X}_{s}}^{2}.
Gronwall’s inequality and density prove (66).
∎
We recall the following theorem.
Theorem 4.5 ([32, Corollary IV.2.11]).
Suppose that A:D(A)\subset X\to X generates a strongly continuous
semigroup T=(T(t))_{t\geq 0} on a complex Banach space X .
Denote its growth bound and essential growth bound by
\omega_{0}(T) and \omega_{\mathrm{ess}}(T) :
\displaystyle\omega_{0}(T)
\displaystyle=\inf\{w\in\mathbb{R}:\exists M_{w}<\infty,\ \|T(\tau)\|\leq M_{w}e^{w\tau}\text{ for all }\tau\geq 0\},
\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{ess}}(T)
\displaystyle=\inf\{w\in\mathbb{R}:\exists M_{w}<\infty,\ \|T(\tau)\|_{\mathrm{ess}}\leq M_{w}e^{w\tau}\text{ for all }\tau\geq 0\},
where \|L\|_{\mathrm{ess}}=\inf\{\|L-K\|:K\text{ compact}\} and \sigma(A) denotes the full spectrum of A . Then
\omega_{0}(T)=\max\left\{\omega_{\mathrm{ess}}(T),\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}\operatorname{Re}\lambda\right\}.
For each real number w>\omega_{\mathrm{ess}}(T) , the set
\sigma(A)\cap\bigl\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq w\bigr\}
is finite, and its associated spectral projection has finite rank. In particular, its elements are isolated eigenvalues of A with finite algebraic multiplicity.
By [32, Proposition IV.2.12], compact perturbations preserve the essential growth bound, so
(68)
\omega_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathbf{S}_{Q})=\omega_{\mathrm{ess}}(\mathbf{U})\leq\omega_{0}(\mathbf{U})\leq\gamma_{s}<0.
Then in our case Corollary 4.5 gives the following proposition.
Proposition 4.6.
For every \delta>0 ,
\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{Q})\cap\{\lambda:\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq\gamma_{s}+\delta\}
is a finite set of isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity. In particular, the sum of the generalized eigenspaces with \operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0 is finite-dimensional.
We now introduce spectral projections to separate the stable part of the linearized evolution from the finite-dimensional neutral and unstable parts. We denote the stable and unstable projections by P_{\mathrm{st}} and P_{\mathrm{un}} , respectively.
Proposition 4.7.
There are bounded real-linear projections P_{\mathrm{un}} and
P_{\mathrm{st}}=I-P_{\mathrm{un}} , commuting with \mathcal{A}_{Q} and S_{Q} , such
that \operatorname{Ran}P_{\mathrm{un}} is finite-dimensional. For some
\omega>0 and C<\infty ,
(69)
\|S_{Q}(\tau)P_{\mathrm{st}}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}\to\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq Ce^{-\omega\tau},\qquad\tau\geq 0.
For every \eta>0 , we also have
\|S_{Q}(-\tau)P_{\mathrm{un}}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}\to\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq C_{\eta}e^{\eta\tau},\qquad\tau\geq 0.
Proof.
By Proposition 4.6, there exists \gamma_{s}<-\omega<0 such that the set
\Lambda_{\mathrm{un}}=\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{Q})\cap\{\lambda:\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq-\omega\}=\sigma(\mathbf{A}_{Q})\cap\{\lambda:\operatorname{Re}\lambda\geq 0\}
is finite and consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity. Let \mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}} be the spectral projection associated with \Lambda_{\mathrm{un}} , and set \mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{st}}=I-\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}} . The standard spectral argument (e.g., [8, Lemma 7.11]) gives the closed invariant decomposition
\mathbf{X}_{s}=\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}}\oplus\operatorname{Ran}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{st}}.
From Corollary 4.5 and (68), we have for \tau\geq 0 ,
\|\mathbf{S}_{Q}(\tau)\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{st}}\|\leq Ce^{-\omega\tau}.
From the definition of \Lambda_{\mathrm{un}} , all eigenvalues have nonnegative real part. Jordan normal form therefore gives, for all \eta>0 and \tau\geq 0 ,
\|\mathbf{S}_{Q}(-\tau)\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}}\|\leq C_{\eta}e^{\eta\tau}.
Moreover, since \mathcal{J} from (64) commutes with \mathbf{A}_{Q} , we have
\mathcal{J}(z\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{Q})^{-1}\mathcal{J}=(\bar{z}\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{A}_{Q})^{-1}.
Let \mathbf{P}_{\lambda} be the Riesz projection for \lambda . Then
\mathcal{J}\mathbf{P}_{\lambda}\mathcal{J}=\mathbf{P}_{\bar{\lambda}}.
Since \Lambda_{\mathrm{un}} is invariant under conjugation, we have \mathcal{J}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{J}=\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}} . Thus \mathcal{J} commutes with the projection \mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}} . Hence \mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{un}} preserves \mathbf{X}_{s,+} and induces bounded real-linear projections
P_{\alpha}=\iota^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{\alpha}\iota,\qquad\alpha\in\{\mathrm{un},\mathrm{st}\},
with the required commutation properties.

We record the two spectral directions induced by the symmetries of (1). Phase invariance and time translation give, respectively,
\mathcal{A}_{Q}(iQ)=0,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{Q}\left(-\frac{1}{p-1}Q+i\Omega Q-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla Q\right)=-\frac{1}{p-1}Q+i\Omega Q-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla Q.
Indeed, the first identity follows by differentiating the constant phase, and the second one follows by differentiating the blow-up time. They can also be checked directly from the profile equation. The symbol bounds of Lemma 4.1 show that both eigenfunctions belong to D(\mathcal{A}_{Q}) . Hence both directions lie in \operatorname{Ran}P_{\mathrm{un}} , and thus this space has dimension at least two. The construction below selects all neutral and unstable components at once, and therefore requires no separate modulation of the blow-up time or of the constant phase. We do not determine whether the two symmetry modes exhaust this space.
4.3. Stability and localization
The following proposition gives a finite-codimensional stability result for the linearized evolution. This result allows us to construct global solutions to the nonlinear equation.
Proposition 4.8.
For the fixed s and \omega above, and every 0<\mu<\omega ,
there is \epsilon_{0}>0 such that, for every
\xi\in P_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal{X}_{s} with
\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}<\epsilon_{0} , the perturbation equation
(52) admits a global solution v with
v(0)=\xi-\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{Q}(-\sigma)P_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma
and
(70)
\sup_{\tau\geq 0}e^{\mu\tau}\|v(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\lesssim\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
Moreover, for every sufficiently large R , such data can be chosen with Q+v(0)=\chi_{R}Q+g_{R} , where g_{R} lies in a fixed finite-dimensional subspace of radial C_{c}^{\infty} and tends to zero in every H^{m} as R\to\infty .
Proof.
To establish the decay estimate
(70), we follow the classical
Lyapunov–Perron construction; see, for example,
[67, Section 9.2]. We then obtain smooth compactly
supported initial data by a finite-dimensional localization
argument related to the constructions in
[49, 46].
We prescribe the stable component \xi and choose the unstable component to cancel the backward evolution of the nonlinear term.
For \xi\in P_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal{X}_{s} , solve the equation
(71)
\displaystyle v(\tau)={}
\displaystyle S_{Q}(\tau)\xi+\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{Q}(\tau-\sigma)P_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma-\int_{\tau}^{\infty}S_{Q}(\tau-\sigma)P_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma.
Choose 0<\eta<2\mu in Proposition 4.7. We use the norm \|v\|_{\mu}:=\sup_{\tau\geq 0}e^{\mu\tau}\|v(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}} . For \|v\|_{\mu}\leq 1 , (60) with w=0 gives, since \mathcal{F}(0)=0 ,
\|\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq C_{Q,s}\|v(\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}^{2}\leq C_{Q,s}e^{-2\mu\sigma}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}.
Here we used \|v(\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\leq e^{-\mu\sigma}\|v\|_{\mu}\leq 1 . Hence, for every \tau\geq 0 , we have estimates
\displaystyle e^{\mu\tau}\left\|\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{Q}(\tau-\sigma)P_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma\right\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
\displaystyle\leq CC_{Q,s}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{\mu\tau}e^{-\omega(\tau-\sigma)}e^{-2\mu\sigma}\,d\sigma
\displaystyle\leq\frac{CC_{Q,s}}{\omega-\mu}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2},
\displaystyle e^{\mu\tau}\left\|\int_{\tau}^{\infty}S_{Q}(\tau-\sigma)P_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma\right\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}
\displaystyle\leq C_{\eta}C_{Q,s}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\mu\tau}e^{\eta(\sigma-\tau)}e^{-2\mu\sigma}\,d\sigma
\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\eta}C_{Q,s}}{2\mu-\eta}\|v\|_{\mu}^{2}.
For \|v\|_{\mu},\|w\|_{\mu}\leq 1 , the same bounds hold with \mathcal{F}(v)-\mathcal{F}(w) in the integrands and \|v\|_{\mu}^{2} replaced by (\|v\|_{\mu}+\|w\|_{\mu})\|v-w\|_{\mu} . Together with (69) and (60), this makes the right-hand side of the equation (71) a contraction on a ball of radius C\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}} in
\left\{v\in C([0,\infty);\mathcal{X}_{s}):\sup_{\tau\geq 0}e^{\mu\tau}\|v(\tau)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}<\infty\right\}.
The contraction mapping theorem therefore gives a fixed point v on [0,\infty) . Subtracting S_{Q}(\tau) times (71) at \tau=0 from the same equation at time \tau yields
v(\tau)=S_{Q}(\tau)v(0)+\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{Q}(\tau-\sigma)\mathcal{F}(v(\sigma))\,d\sigma.
This shows the existence of a global solution to (52), with the decay estimate (70).
Now we work on the localization. For each \xi\in B_{\epsilon_{0}}(0)\cap P_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal{X}_{s} , let v_{\xi} denote the unique fixed point constructed above for that stable datum. Define the map \Phi:B_{\epsilon_{0}}(0)\cap P_{\mathrm{st}}\mathcal{X}_{s}\to P_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{X}_{s} by
\Phi(\xi):=P_{\mathrm{un}}v_{\xi}(0)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{Q}(-\sigma)P_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{F}(v_{\xi}(\sigma))\,d\sigma.
The integral converges in \mathcal{X}_{s} by the preceding estimates, and v_{\xi}(0)=\xi+\Phi(\xi) . In particular, \Phi(0)=0 since v_{0}=0 . The same estimates for differences give
(72)
\|\Phi(\xi)-\Phi(\eta)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\lesssim\bigl(\|\xi\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}+\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\bigr)\|\xi-\eta\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}.
To localize, choose a finite-dimensional real space F\subset C^{\infty}_{c,\mathrm{rad}} for which P_{\mathrm{un}}|_{F}:F\to P_{\mathrm{un}}\mathcal{X}_{s} is an isomorphism. Density of C^{\infty}_{c,\mathrm{rad}} in \mathcal{X}_{s} ensures such a choice. By (58), (\chi_{R}-1)Q\to 0 in \mathcal{X}_{s} . Now we show that the equation
(73)
g_{R}=(P_{\mathrm{un}}|_{F})^{-1}\left[\Phi\bigl(P_{\mathrm{st}}((\chi_{R}-1)Q+g_{R})\bigr)-P_{\mathrm{un}}((\chi_{R}-1)Q)\right]
is a contraction on a ball in F of radius C\|(\chi_{R}-1)Q\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}} when R is sufficiently large. Denote the right-hand side of (73) by T_{R}(g) . By (72), \Phi(0)=0 , and boundedness of the projections and (P_{\mathrm{un}}|_{F})^{-1} , for g,h in this ball we have
\begin{aligned} \|T_{R}(g)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}&\leq K\|(\chi_{R}-1)Q\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}+K(1+C)^{2}\|(\chi_{R}-1)Q\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}^{2},\\
\|T_{R}(g)-T_{R}(h)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}&\leq K(1+C)\|(\chi_{R}-1)Q\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\|g-h\|_{\mathcal{X}_{s}}\end{aligned},
with K independent of C and R . Fixing C>2K and then taking R large makes T_{R} preserve this ball and have Lipschitz constant less than one; its arguments also lie in the domain of \Phi . Its solution satisfies P_{\mathrm{un}}((\chi_{R}-1)Q+g_{R})=\Phi(P_{\mathrm{st}}((\chi_{R}-1)Q+g_{R})) , so v(0)=(\chi_{R}-1)Q+g_{R} lies on the constructed graph and Q+v(0)=\chi_{R}Q+g_{R}\in C_{c}^{\infty} . Since F is fixed and finite-dimensional, g_{R}\to 0 in every H^{m} .

Proof of Theorem 1.1.
Fix 0<\mu<\omega and choose localized data from Proposition 4.8. By (57) and (70), V=Q+v is uniformly bounded and
\|V(\tau)-Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{-\mu\tau}.
Its initial datum is smooth and compactly supported. Rewriting the Duhamel formula for v relative to S_{0} and using (63) shows that V solves (51). Higher regularity persists, and the tame product estimate yields, for every integer m\geq 3 for which V(0)\in H^{m} ,
(74)
\|V(\tau)\|_{H^{m}}\leq\|V(0)\|_{H^{m}}\exp\left(\left(\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\tau+C_{m}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|V(\sigma)\|_{\infty}^{p-1}\,d\sigma\right).
Consequently, a bounded L^{\infty} norm prevents finite-time breakdown and propagates all initial Sobolev regularity. Uniqueness and (74) therefore give V\in C([0,\infty);H^{m}) for every integer m\geq 0 . For any T>0 , undoing (4) gives
u(t,x)=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{-i\Omega\tau}V(\tau,y).
Therefore,
\left\|(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}e^{i\Omega\tau}u\left(t,\sqrt{T-t}\,\cdot\right)-Q\right\|_{\infty}=O((T-t)^{\mu}),
and hence
\|u(t)\|_{\infty}=(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\|Q\|_{\infty}+O((T-t)^{\mu})\right).
The resulting solution lies in C([0,T);H^{m}) for every m , with smooth compactly supported initial data. Since Q(0)=A>0 , we have \|Q\|_{\infty}>0 , and T is its maximal time of existence. Finally, (54) gives |Q(r)|\gtrsim r^{-2/(p-1)} for r\geq 1 . Therefore,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|Q(x)|^{\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}\,dx\gtrsim\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{d-1}r^{-d}\,dr=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r}=\infty,
so Q\notin L^{d(p-1)/2} . As V(\tau)\to Q pointwise, Fatou’s lemma gives \|V(\tau)\|_{L^{d(p-1)/2}}\to\infty . Sobolev embedding and scaling invariance yield
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}}\gtrsim\|u(t)\|_{L^{\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}}=\|V(\tau)\|_{L^{\frac{d(p-1)}{2}}}\longrightarrow\infty,
which completes the proof.
∎
