1.2. Contribution and proof strategy. Our proof adapts the qualitative-to-quantitative compact-
ness method of Sobral and Teixeira [21], developed for compact families of uniformly elliptic
equations preserved by their natural rescalings. For the p-Laplace equation, affine subtraction
generates a family of shifted equations, and the argument must account for limits in which the
shifts become unbounded; see [5] for the shifted framework. Uniform local Hölder estimates,
independent of the shift, provide compactness for normalized solutions.
   After passing to a subsequence, either the shifts converge, or their lengths tend to infinity. In
the first case, the limit is p-harmonic after adding an affine function. In the second case, the
directions of the shifts converge to a unit vector e, and the limit solves a linear uniformly elliptic
equation with the constant-coefficients matrix I + ( p − 2)e ⊗ e. The first limit has the assumed
qualitative regularity, while the second is smooth. Thus, every limiting function h satisfies
                              ∥h − h(0) − Dh(0) · x ∥ L∞ ( Br ) ≤ Lh r1+γ
for sufficiently small r, where Lh may depend on h.
   Fix 0 < β < γ. The factor r γ− β allows us to absorb Lh by choosing a sufficiently small radius
for each limiting function. The resulting strict affine approximation persists under small uniform
perturbations. These neighborhoods cover the compact family of limits, so a finite subcovering
provides finitely many admissible radii and affine slopes, independent of the solution and the
shift.
   At each step of the iteration, we choose one of these radii and subtract the corresponding affine
approximation. The positive minimum of the selected radii allows estimates at the resulting
scales to control all intermediate radii. The affine slopes converge, giving a uniform first-order
expansion with remainder of order 1 + β. Applying the construction at every interior point and
comparing the resulting expansions yields the uniform gradient estimate.
