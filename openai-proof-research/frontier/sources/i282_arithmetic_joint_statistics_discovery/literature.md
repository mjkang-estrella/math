## Strongest collision and what it removes

Gorodetsky's [versioned Corollary 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14190v1#S2) applies to arbitrary probability distributions on integers. Its use with a nonnegative macroscopic tuple weight is already within its advertised scope once the actual smooth-divisor remainder is proved. The present partial's extra remainder proof is a standard count swap below the BV level; [Bharadwaj–Rodgers, Lemma 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11884v4) gives the matching restricted large-factor mechanism. These together are the strongest methodological prior-art collision. I282 P is therefore technical, even though its combined marked formulation is useful.

Ford's [Theorem 1, v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03803v4) already gives the entire small-factor-vector marginal. Calling its Gaussian, Poisson, truncated-factor or slowly growing process consequences a discovery would not pay the new bridge. His theorem concerns one shifted prime; an arbitrary large-factor bias changes the divisibility remainders. The precise difference is (B) in the analytic record.

The pinned J factorizes whole fixed bin-count vectors, while completed I07 §9 already passes to full joint PD at `(n,n+1)`. This is the strongest **project duplication**. No point-process independence was inferred solely from the largest-factor catalogue theorem.

## Other close primary statements

[Tao–Teräväinen, 2512.01739v2, Theorem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01739v2) already permits moving smoothness parameters with an absolute logarithmic saving on its good endpoint set. Combining that rate with J's all-scale qualitative limit does not remove the exceptional set while preserving the rate or a relative rare-event conclusion. Their work blocks a novelty claim based merely on writing a growing parameter in the smoothness law.

[Gorodetsky–Grimmelt, 2307.13585v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13585v2), introduction, Theorem 1.1 and Propositions 1.2–1.3, treats the `2^ω(n−p)`-weighted fixed-target prime-complement distribution. The actual divisibility correction is displayed in Proposition 1.3; it is a different distribution from an ordinary random prime predecessor. The published title is *On an Erdős–Kac-type conjecture of Elliott*, DOI [10.1093/qmath/haae026](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haae026). The versioned preprint has a different title. A Gaussian marginal is not substituted for (M).

[Arratia, 1305.0941v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.0941v1), abstract, §1.1 and located Theorem 5 statement, develops small-factor independent models and PD couplings for uniformly sampled **ordinary integers**. This is a primary background lead, not a theorem used to prove I282; its detailed coupling theorems were not audited here. It prevents claiming that the general idea of connecting small factors and PD is new.
