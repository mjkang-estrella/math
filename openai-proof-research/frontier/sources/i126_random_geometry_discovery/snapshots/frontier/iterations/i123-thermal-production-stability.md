`epsilon_B=||(id tensor Phi_B)omega - omega_C tensor Phi_B(omega_E)||_1`
`<=2 C_(y,L) sqrt(2 ell_B)+8sqrt(F/(L+1))`.  (B2)

This follows from the same proved operations, not an external quantitative recovery theorem. The exact C marginal energy is `t e+(1-t)nr<=F`; replacing it by F is a uniform upper bound.

### 4.2 All growing cutoffs and the tradeoff

The same Weyl test now loses

`A_y=(2y+3/2)/[4(1-t)]+R(1-2t)^2/[4t(1-t)]`.

The second term comes from the original thermal port and is unchanged. For any valid upper bound `0<epsilon_B<=e^-4`, the dyadic estimate and both W120 cutoffs give, without optimizing away a constant,

`d <= sqrt(binomial(K+n,n)) { Q^(2n)(B0 Q²+2)² epsilon_B exp(2A_y Q²)/n! + J/Q² }^(1/2)`
`     +2(sqrt E+sqrt(nr))/sqrt(K+1)`,  (B3)

where `d=||rho-tau_r||_1`, `J=8(E+nr)+8n`, `B0=2E+n+R`, K>=0, Q>0, and the separate filter cutoff L>=0 appears in B2. Thus the filter cutoff, frequency cutoff and number cutoff are all visible. If the upper bound in B2 exceeds e^-4, this formula supplies no small-deficit conclusion via W120; one may only use the cap two.

Taking y comparable to L makes B1 polynomial in L, but increases A_y linearly in L. This cannot be ignored. More generally, the coefficient of `ell_B^(1/4)` in the characteristic-function bound contains the exponential

`exp{ A_y Q² + (L/2)log(1+1/(2y)) }`.

For y>=1/2, `log(1+1/(2y))>=1/(2y+1)>=1/(4y)`. Therefore its exponent is at least

`y Q²/[2(1-t)] + L/(8y) >= Q sqrt L/[2 sqrt(1-t)]`.  (B4)

For 0<y<1/2 it is at least `(L/2)log2`. The remaining prefactor in B1 is positive and does not cancel this loss. To make the explicit error terms in B2 and B3 polynomially small in a desired trace tolerance, L and K must grow polynomially, and `sqrt(rank P_K)/Q` must also be small; Q must grow. B4 prevents this particular composition of bounds from acquiring a polynomial signal budget by optimizing y. In addition the filter tail is amplified when inserted into B3. This is a lower bound on a cost in a derived upper bound, **not** a lower bound on the actual deficit or a universal impossibility theorem for all bounded tests.

### 4.3 The additional remainders do not yet cancel that cost

Let `r_B=(r+B_aux)/2`, `V_B=E/(2n)+B_aux/2`, and `x_B=g^-1(S(Phi_Brho)/n)`. Original EPnI actually gives `r_B<=x_B<=V_B` on this entropy slice. Exactly as for M3,

`P_B>=nt(1-t)(x_B-r_B)²/[2V_B(V_B+1)]`.

Thus, writing `z_B=S(Phi_Brho)-ng(r_B)>=0`,

`z_B<=n h(r_B) sqrt[2V_B(V_B+1)P_B/(nt(1-t))]`.  (B5)

This uses the real plus remainder, rather than discarding it. But the exact relative-entropy identity is

`D(Phi_Brho||tau_rB)=h(r_B)(e-nr)/2-z_B`.

It only yields the unconditional smoothed trace estimate `||Phi_Brho-tau_rB||_1<=sqrt[h(r_B)(E-nr)]`. That bound tends to zero as B_aux tends to infinity for **every** admissible input. Hence its improvement with auxiliary temperature is not evidence that the original input is close to tau_r. The positive conditional remainder C_B cannot be replaced by a scalar entropy-photon-number inequality with arbitrary quantum memory. The available linear conditional inequality supplies its sign and its inclusion in delta, but no bound that absorbs B4.

The attempted combination therefore stops at a precise obligation: an inequality converting `P_B+C_B+ell_B` to bounded input-test errors with polynomial cutoff constants, or an equivalent estimate `d<=C K^a delta^beta+C'K^-1/2` with fixed finite a and positive beta and all additional tails absorbed. B1–B5 do not give it. No constraint-respecting all-power-defeating sequence was constructed. The original displaced-tail family remains exactly the failed candidate audited in §2.4; changing the auxiliary bath is not a new physical counterexample.
