                                                      A BSTRACT. We prove a dimension-free comparison between the expectation threshold q(F ) and the fractional
                                                      expectation threshold qf (F ) for any nontrivial increasing family F on a finite ground set. Specifically, we show
                                                      that there is a universal constant K > 0 such that

                                                      discrete Convexity Conjecture, we obtain a dimension-independent bound toward the conjecture.

We will in fact prove the following equivalent statement (up to changing the universal constant K):
(3)         for every p ∈ (0, 1), if F is weakly p-small, then it is p/(K · max{1, log log(1/p)})-small.

Proof of the equivalence. Write q, qf for q(F), qf (F). That (3) implies Theorem 1.2 follows immediately from
q ≤ qf and the fact that f (t) = max{1, log log(1/t)} is non-increasing.
   To see the converse: fix the universal constant K ≥ 1 in Theorem 1.2, and suppose that F is weakly
p-small. Since Conjecture 1.1 holds when q is bounded away from zero (after adjusting the constant L), we
may assume that q is sufficiently small, say, q ≤ min{1/100, 1/K 3 }. Thus Theorem 1.2 gives that p ≤ qf ≤
Kq log log(1/q), and hence

   To place Theorem 1.2 in context, we first review some related background. Conjecture 1.1 has motivated
a number of partial results. In particular, the conjectured constant-factor comparison has been established
under various additional assumptions on the family F or the function λ [3, 8, 5, 4, 13, 7, 6].
   In the unrestricted setting, the best previously known general bound is obtained by combining the results
of Pham [13, Theorem 1.2] and Fischer and Person [5, Theorem 10]:
(4)                                         qf (F) ≤ Cq(F ) · max{1, log log |V |}1.

   We remark that Theorem 1.2 strengthens (4), up to a change in the universal constant. To see this, note
that (4) is equivalent to
(5)                for every p ∈ (0, 1), weakly p-small implies p/(C · max{1, log log |V |})-small.
If p ≤ 1/(2|V |), taking G to be the family of all singleton subsets of V shows that every nontrivial increasing
family is p-small. Therefore, even the conclusion of (2) automatically holds in this case. On the other hand,
if p > 1/(2|V |), then
                                 max{1, log log(1/p)} = O (max{1, log log |V |}) .
Thus, (3) implies (5).

                                                      3. D ISCUSSION

    We now discuss the connection with the discrete version of Talagrand’s Convexity Conjecture. For p ∈
[0, 1], let µp denote the distribution of Vp on 2V ; that is, for every A ⊆ V , µp ({A}) = p|A| (1 − p)|V \A| , and
µp (A) = P(Vp ∈ A) = A∈A µp ({A}) for every A ⊆ 2V .
                        P

    For a family A ⊆ 2V and a positive integer k, set
                         A(k) := {S ⊆ V : S ⊆ A1 ∪ · · · ∪ Ak for some A1 , . . . , Ak ∈ A},
and A(k) := 2V \ A(k) . Thus A(k) is the increasing family of sets not contained in any union of k members
of A.

Conjecture 3.1 (Talagrand [14, 15]). There exist a positive integer k and a universal constant L ≥ 1 such that, for
every p ∈ (0, 1), finite set V , and family A ⊆ 2V , if µp (A) ≥ 1 − 1/(2k) then A(k) is (p/L)-small.

   Hua, Song, and Tudose [9] resolved the original Gaussian version of Talagrand’s Convexity Conjecture.
Using an argument of Talagrand, they deduced the following partial result bound toward Conjecture 3.1;
see [9, Corollary 1.4 and Appendix A].

Theorem 3.2. There exist ϵ > 0 and L ≥ 1 so that for any finite set V , p ∈ (0, 1), and any family A ⊆ 2V , if
µp (A) ≥ 1 − ϵ then A(3) is pL -small.

   For comparison, a result of Pham and the author [12, Theorem 1.1] implies that, for any A ⊆ 2V with
µp (A) ≥ 1/2, the family A(1) is (p/(C log ℓ(A)))-small for a universal constant C, where ℓ(A) is the max-
imum of 2 and the size of a largest minimal element of A(1) . Since A(3) ⊆ A(1) , this also gives the same
      A DIMENSION-FREE COMPARISON BETWEEN EXPECTATION THRESHOLDS AND FRACTIONAL EXPECTATION THRESHOLDS                              5


smallness bound for A(3) . The two bounds from [9] and [12] are not uniformly comparable; we refer the
readers to the discussion following [9, Corollary 1.4] for further discussion.

Remark 3.3. A key requirement of Conjecture 3.1 is that the smallness parameter depend only on the den-
sity parameter p, independently of the size of the ground set. The importance of dimension independence
was emphasized by Talagrand in [15, Section 1].

   Recently, Li [10, Corollary 3.1] proved a fractional version of Conjecture 3.1. His result is formulated in
terms of spread probability measures. By the duality relation between spread measures and being weakly-
small [15, Proposition 6.7], it yields the following statement in our terminology:

Theorem 3.4. For every finite set V , p ∈ (0, 1), and family A ⊆ 2V satisfying µp (A) > 1/2, the family A(2) is
weakly (p/2)-small.

   Combining Theorem 1.2 with Theorem 3.4, we obtain the following dimension-free bound.

Corollary 3.5. There is a universal L > 0 such that, for every finite set V , p ∈ (0, 1), and A ⊆ 2V satisfying
µp (A) > 1/2, the family A(2) is p/(L max{1, log log(1/p)})-small.3
