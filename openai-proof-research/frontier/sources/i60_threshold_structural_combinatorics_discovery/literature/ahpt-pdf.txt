                                              A REFORMULATION OF THE DISCRETE CONVEXITY CONJECTURE VIA k-THRESHOLDS


                                                                  RUBEN ASCOLI, XIAOYU HE, JINYOUNG PARK, AND MICHEL TALAGRAND


                                                   A BSTRACT. We introduce the notion of “k-thresholds” and show that Talagrand’s discrete convexity conjecture
                                                   is equivalent to the assertion that, for some universal integer k ≥ 2, the k-threshold of every increasing family
                                                   is at most a universal constant times its expectation threshold. We prove a reduction theorem that bounds the k-
                                                   threshold of any increasing graph property in terms of ordinary thresholds of graphs in suitable decompositions
                                                   of its members. As a consequence, we determine, up to a constant factor, the k-threshold of every fixed graph
                                                   in terms of a natural k-density parameter. We also prove that k = 2 suffices for several classical spanning graph
                                                   containment properties. More generally, we establish the conjectured comparison between k-thresholds and ex-
                                                   pectation thresholds for broad classes of graph containment properties whose target graphs have low degeneracy.

                                                                                               1. I NTRODUCTION

                                             The convexity conjecture of the fourth author asks, roughly, whether one can “create” convexity in a
                                           constant number of steps regardless of the dimension of the ambient space. We refer the reader to [17]
                                           for background on the conjecture, and to [9] for a recent proof by Hua, Song, and Tudose. In his original
                                           paper [17], the fourth author also introduced a discrete version of the conjecture, which he later revisited
                                           in slightly different forms in [19, 20]. This discrete conjecture, stated as Conjecture 1.2 below, remains open
                                           and is the main focus of the present paper.
                                             We approach the discrete convexity conjecture by introducing the new notion of k-thresholds, which
                                           allows us to reformulate the conjecture in the language of threshold phenomena. We then develop this
                                           viewpoint for graph properties by proving a reduction theorem that bounds k-thresholds in terms of ordi-
                                           nary thresholds. Using this reduction, we verify the reformulated conjecture for several broad classes of
                                           graph containment properties.

Conjecture 1.2 (Talagrand [17]). There exist a positive integer k and a universal constant L ≥ 1 such that, for

      Observe that, under the assumptions of Conjecture 1.2, the family D(k) already has small µp -measure
since µp (D(k) ) ≤ 1 − µp (D) ≤ 1/(2k), and the content of the conjecture is the stronger conclusion that D(k)
is (p/L)-small. In other words, apart from a family whose smallness is certified by a cover, every subset of

      We introduce the notion of k-thresholds and use it to give an equivalent formulation of Conjecture 1.2.

Definition 1.3 (k-threshold). For an increasing family I ⊆ 2X and a positive integer k, the k-threshold of I,
denoted by pk (I), is the infimum of the set of p ∈ [0, 1] such that

(2)                 for any decreasing D ⊆ 2X with µp (D) ≥ 1 − 1/(2k), we have D(k) ∩ I ̸= ∅.

Note that (2) holds for every p > pk (I). Indeed, as p increases, µp (D) decreases for every decreasing family
D, so the collection of families satisfying µp (D) ≥ 1 − 1/(2k) can only shrink. Furthermore, observe that for
any increasing family I, pk (I) is non-increasing in k since D(k) ⊆ D(k+1) for any decreasing family D and
positive integer k.
      Recall that the threshold2 for I, pc (I), is the unique p such that µp (I) = 1/2. (Existence and uniqueness
of pc follow from the fact that, for every nontrivial increasing family I, µp (I) is continuous and strictly
increasing in p.) When k = 1 the condition (2) fails exactly when µp (I c ) ≥ 1/2, so p1 (I) = pc (I), and
k-thresholds are a generalization of the usual notion of thresholds. Following [11], define the expectation
threshold of a property I ⊆ 2X by

Theorem 1.8. Let k be a positive integer and I be an increasing graph property. Then, for any H ∈ I,

                                                            pk (I) ≤ rk (H).

Consequently, pk (I) ≤ minH∈I rk (H).

1.2. Applications to graph containment properties. We apply Theorem 1.8 to obtain bounds for several
classes of graph containment properties. Throughout this subsection, n tends to infinity, and the target
graph H may depend on n unless stated otherwise.

Fixed graphs. We begin with the case in which H is a fixed graph, meaning that H does not depend on n.
In this setting, for every fixed positive integer k, we determine the correct order of magnitude of pk (H) by
proving matching upper and lower bounds in terms of a natural k-density parameter dk (H).
    Define the maximum density of H by

                                 d(H) = max{eJ /vJ : J is a subgraph of H with vJ ≥ 1}.

Beyond fixed graphs. We next establish Conjecture 1.4 for several graph containment properties whose target
graphs H grow with n.
  We first prove Conjecture 1.4 with k = 2 when the target graph belongs to one of several classical span-
ning graph classes. Recall that an F -factor on [n] is a collection of n/vF copies of F whose vertex sets
partition [n], where we always assume vF |n. For example, if F is a single edge, then an F -factor is a perfect
matching.

Theorem 1.11. Let H be a spanning tree whose maximum degree is bounded by a fixed constant, the m-th power
of a Hamiltonian cycle for some fixed m ≥ 1, or an F -factor for some fixed graph F with at least one edge. Then
p2 (H) = O(q(H)), where the implicit constant may depend on the maximum degree bound, m, or F , as appropriate,
but not on n.

  Allowing larger values of k, we next establish Conjecture 1.4 for a broader class of graph containment
properties. Recall that the degeneracy of a graph H, denoted by degen(H), is the smallest integer d such that
every nonempty subgraph of H has minimum degree at most d. Equivalently, degen(H) is the smallest d
for which the vertices of H admit an ordering such that every vertex has at most d neighbors preceding it.

Theorem 1.12. For every positive integer D, there exist a positive integer k = k(D) and a constant L = L(D) such
that every graph H with degen(H) ≤ D satisfies pk (H) ≤ Lq(H).

  We further extend this result to graphs with degeneracy up to order log n/ log log n, at the cost of impos-
ing a maximum-degree condition.

Theorem 1.13. For every C, ϵ > 0, there exist a positive integer k = k(C, ϵ) and a constant L = L(C, ϵ) such that
every graph H with degen(H) ≤ C logloglogn n and maximum degree at most n1−ϵ satisfies pk (H) ≤ Lq(H).

3.4. Low degeneracy and the Nash-Williams theorem. In the proof of Theorem 1.12, we use the Nash-
Williams theorem to decompose low-degeneracy graphs into forests. Recall that the arboricity of a graph is
the minimum number of forests into which its edges can be partitioned.

Theorem 3.6 (Nash-Williams [13]). Let H = (V, E) be a graph and k ≥ 1 be an integer. The edge set E can be
partitioned into at most k forests if and only if for every non-empty subset of vertices U ⊆ V , the induced subgraph
H[U ] has at most k(|U | − 1) edges.

     The following immediate consequence relates arboricity to degeneracy.

Corollary 3.7. Let H be a graph with the degeneracy at most D. Then the arboricity of H is at most D.

                                      8. D ISCUSSION AND OPEN PROBLEMS

     The k-threshold reformulation established in Proposition 1.5 suggests a new route toward Conjecture 1.2.
For graph containment properties, Theorem 1.8 makes this route concrete by reducing the problem of
bounding k-thresholds to finding decompositions whose pieces have small ordinary thresholds. This moti-
vates the following decomposition conjecture.

Conjecture 8.1. There exist an integer k ≥ 2 and a constant L ≥ 1 such that, for every n and every graph H on at
most n vertices, H admits a k-decomposition (H1 , . . . , Hk ) satisfying pc (Hi ) ≤ Lq(H) for every i ∈ [k].

     By Theorem 1.8, Conjecture 8.1 would imply that Conjecture 1.4 holds for every graph containment
property. Our results establish this conclusion for several broad classes of target graphs, but the present
methods do not appear to extend to arbitrary graphs.
     One limitation arises from the logarithmic loss in the general bound pc (J) = O(pE (J) log3 n). In the proof
of Theorem 1.13, we decompose H into graphs of lower degeneracy and show that their expectation thresh-
olds are sufficiently smaller than that of H to absorb this loss. Once degen(H) = ω(log n/ log log n), the
argument no longer provides a sufficient gap to absorb the factor log3 n. Extending our approach beyond
this range may therefore require sharper upper bounds on ordinary thresholds or a different decomposition
argument.
     The maximum-degree assumption in Theorem 1.13 enters for a different reason: it is used in Lemma 7.1
to bound the number of automorphisms of subgraphs of H. It would be interesting to determine whether
this assumption can be removed.
     One may isolate the difficulty created by high-degree vertices as follows. Given a graph H of degeneracy
O(log n/ log log n), partition its vertices according to whether their degrees are at least n1−ϵ . Let Hh and Hl
be the subgraphs induced by the high and low degree vertices, respectively, and let H ′ consist of the edges
between Hh and Hl . The methods developed here can treat Hh and Hl , leaving the bipartite graph H ′ as
the main remaining obstacle.
     A separate question is whether more than two pieces are ever necessary for graph containment proper-
ties. Our results show that k = 2 suffices for several classical spanning target graphs, but we do not know
whether this holds for every graph.

Question 8.2. Does there exist a sequence of graphs H = Hn such that p2 (H) ≫ q(H)?

     We note that even a proof of Conjecture 8.1 would not immediately settle Conjecture 1.4 for arbitrary
increasing graph properties. Indeed, if I is an increasing graph property and G ∈ I, then Theorem 1.8
gives pk (I) ≤ rk (G). The conjectured decomposition bound would control rk (G) in terms of q(G) = q(IG ),
whereas the desired conclusion involves q(I). Since IG ⊆ I, one only has q(I) ≤ q(G), which goes in the
opposite direction of the comparison needed for our purposes. Extending the present approach to general
increasing graph properties therefore appears to require additional ideas.
     Finally, we remark that the proof of Theorem 1.8 extends with minor changes to r-uniform hypergraph
properties for every fixed r ≥ 2. It is natural to ask whether the resulting reduction yields any nontrivial
bounds on k-thresholds for hypergraph containment properties.
