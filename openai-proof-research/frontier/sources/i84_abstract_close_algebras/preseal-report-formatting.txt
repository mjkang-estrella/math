## 2. Independent ORIGINAL audit of I82 §9

**Proposition S (original statement).** If (D\ne0) is a separable unital C*-algebra with (Z(D)=\mathbb C1), and (P,Q\subset D) are unital subalgebras, then

\[
 E_D(P)\cong E_D(Q)
\]

if and only if there is one unital *-isomorphism (\theta:P\to Q) and one sequence (\alpha_n\in\operatorname{Aut}(D)) such that

\[
 \|\alpha_n(p)-\theta(p)\|\longrightarrow0\quad\text{for every }p\in P. \tag{S}
\]

Here *-isomorphism means complex-linear C*-algebra isomorphism. An onto *-isomorphism between these unital algebras automatically preserves the identity. No uniform convergence on (P_1) is asserted. Separability is harmless but not needed for this equivalence.

**Forward direction, with permutation indexing.** Each coordinate projection (e_n) belongs to the center. If (x=(x_n)) is central, commutation with all finitely supported (D)-sequences gives (x_n\in Z(D)). Thus (x_n=a_n1); norm convergence is precisely scalar convergence. Conversely every convergent scalar sequence is central, since its scalar limit belongs to (P). Therefore the center is (c), and its minimal nonzero projections are exactly (e_n). There is no additional central atom at infinity: a projection in (c) has eventually constant values zero or one, and every nonzero such projection contains a coordinate atom.

An isomorphism (\Phi) permutes these atoms: (\Phi(e_n)=e_{\sigma(n)}) for a bijection (\sigma:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N). Their closed generated ideal is precisely (c_0(\mathbb N,D)), which is therefore characteristic between the two sequence algebras. The limit map has this kernel and range (P) or (Q); it induces one isomorphism (\theta:P\to Q). Identifying both coordinate corners with (D) defines automorphisms (\beta_n:D\to D). Multiplying by (e_{\sigma(n)}) shows, for every sequence (d),

\[
 (\Phi(d))_{\sigma(n)}=\beta_n(d_n).
\]

Apply this to the constant sequence (p). Its image converges to (\theta(p)). Every bijection of (\mathbb N) sends tails eventually outside each prescribed finite set: the preimage of a finite set is finite. Hence (\beta_n(p)\to\theta(p)). Take (\alpha_n=\beta_n). This is a single sequence for all (p), not separate subsequences selected for different elements.

**Reverse direction and inverse.** Given (S), define

\[
 \Psi((d_n))=(\alpha_n(d_n)).
\]

If (d_n\to p), then

\[
 \|\alpha_n(d_n)-\theta(p)\|
 \le\|d_n-p\|+\|\alpha_n(p)-\theta(p)\|\to0.
\]

Thus (\Psi) is a well-defined isometric *-homomorphism into (E_D(Q)). If (b_n\to q\in Q), put (p=\theta^{-1}(q)). Its coordinatewise inverse satisfies

\[
 \|\alpha_n^{-1}(b_n)-p\|
 =\|b_n-\alpha_n(p)\|
 \le\|b_n-q\|+\|q-\alpha_n(p)\|\to0.
\]

It lies in (E_D(P)), so (\Psi) is onto. A coordinate permutation could be added in either direction and would not affect convergence. This proves the exact original proposition.

## 3. The actual ambient algebra and characteristic ideal

For (g\ne e), the source formula for (T_g=\pi_t(g)\lambda(g)^*) says that (T_g\delta_y=\delta_y) outside the finite geodesic ([e,g]), and that this path span is invariant. On that span,

\[
 T_g\delta_{h_k}=r\xi_{h_k}-t\delta_{h_{k+1}} (k<|g|),\qquad
 T_g\delta_g=\xi_g,\qquad r=\sqrt{1-t^2}.
\]

Consequently (T_g-1) has rank at most (|g|+1), and

\[
 \pi_t(g)-\lambda(g)=(T_g-1)\lambda(g)\in\mathcal K. \tag{3.1}
\]

This is a pointwise finite-rank assertion for every (g). It is not a uniform bound on rank, or a bounded global change of basis (\delta_x\mapsto\xi_x); the source explicitly warns that the latter map need not be bounded.

It follows that, for every (0<t<1),

\[
 B:=C^*(A,\mathcal K)=A+\mathcal K,\qquad
 D_t=D=A\otimes_{\min}B,\qquad J:=A\otimes_{\min}\mathcal K\lhd D. \tag{3.2}
\]

Here both tensor products are concretely the closures of elementary products in the original faithful spatial representations on (K\otimes K); this is the defining realization of the minimal tensor product. The sum (A+\mathcal K) is closed because it is the inverse image of the closed C*-image of (A) under the Calkin quotient. Products of the displayed generators prove both inclusions in (3.2). Multiplying elementary tensors proves the ideal assertion. **No tensor-exactness theorem or formula for (D/J) is used.**

We give elementary proofs of the needed properties of (A), instead of silently assuming Powers simplicity and uniqueness of trace. The functional

\[
 \tau(a)=\langle a\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle
\]

is a tracial state, by calculation on group polynomials and norm density. It is faithful: (\tau(a^*a)=0) implies (a\delta_e=0); commutation with all right translations gives (a\delta_x=0) for every (x), whence (a=0).

**Fresh-generator averaging.** Let (x=\sum_{h\ne e}c_h\lambda(h)) have finite support. Choose distinct free generators (u_1,\ldots,u_N) absent from that support and let (R_i) project onto words beginning with (u_i). These projections are mutually orthogonal. For (y_i=\lambda(u_i)x\lambda(u_i)^*), reduced-word cancellation gives

\[
 (1-R_i)y_i(1-R_i)=0.
\]

Indeed, if a word does not begin with (u_i), every (u_i h u_i^{-1}) sends it to a word beginning with (u_i), since (h\ne e) uses none of that fresh letter. Write (y_i=R_i y_i+(1-R_i)y_iR_i). Orthogonality of the output spaces in the first sum, and Cauchy-Schwarz on the mutually orthogonal input spaces in the second, give

\[
 \Big\|N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N y_i\Big\|\le 2\|x\|/\sqrt N. \tag{3.3}
\]

Every tracial state has the same value on all conjugates of (x). Thus (3.3) forces that value to be zero. Density proves that (\tau) is the unique tracial state of (A). For (0\ne a\ge0), (\tau(a)>0); approximate (a-\tau(a)1) by a trace-zero group polynomial and use (3.3). Finite averages of conjugates of (a) become arbitrarily close to (\tau(a)1). Any closed nonzero ideal containing (a) therefore contains an invertible element, and equals (A). Every nonzero C*-ideal contains such a positive (a). Hence (A) is simple. This is the classical Powers averaging mechanism specialized to infinitely many fresh generators, not a new simplicity theorem.

**Simplicity of (J).** If (I\lhd J) is nonzero, a nonzero matrix coefficient of an element of (I), compressed and moved by (1\otimes E_{ij}), gives a nonzero element of (I\cap(A\otimes E_{11})). This intersection is an ideal of that corner, which is (A). Simplicity gives (1\otimes E_{11}\in I), and multiplication by matrix units gives all of (A\odot\mathcal K). Thus (I=J). Nonzero coefficients exist because finite-rank compressions converge in norm on (J).

**Essentiality in the actual (D).** Let (e_n=1\otimes\sum_{j=1}^n E_{jj}\in J), using a fixed enumeration of (G). These converge strongly to the identity on (L). If (dJ=0), then (de_n=0) for all (n), so (d=0). If a nonzero ideal (I\lhd D) had (I\cap J=0), then (IJ=0), contradicting this annihilator statement. Thus every nonzero ideal of (D) intersects (J), and simplicity of (J) implies it contains (J). Therefore **(J) is the unique minimal nonzero ideal of (D), and is characteristic under every automorphism of (D)**.

This proof does not identify (J) with the compact operators on (L). Those are different objects: its first tensor factor is the infinite-dimensional reduced group algebra.

## 4. Trace uniqueness with the exact domain paid

Define the canonical trace on (J_+) by

\[
 T(x)=\sum_{j=1}^\infty\tau(x_{jj})\in[0,\infty]. \tag{4.1}
\]

Finite matrix corners show that this is additive, homogeneous, faithful, and lower semicontinuous. The trace identity follows, initially for (x\in J), by expanding

\[
 T(x^*x)=\sum_{i,j}\tau(x_{ij}^*x_{ij})
        =\sum_{i,j}\tau(x_{ij}x_{ij}^*)=T(xx^*),
\]

with monotone nonnegative sums. Finite matrix corners are norm dense and have finite trace on positive elements. More precisely, (x^{1/2}e_nx^{1/2}\le x), these elements converge in norm to (x\in J_+), and their traces equal (T(e_nxe_n)\le n\|x\|). Thus (T) is densely finite and semifinite, with (T(1\otimes E_{11})=1).

**Trace-ray lemma.** Every nonzero lower-semicontinuous trace (S:J_+\to[0,\infty]) whose finite positive domain is norm dense equals (cT), for (0<c<\infty).

To prove finiteness on a projection (p\in J), choose (y\in J_+) with (S(y)<\infty) and (\|y-p\|<1/2). Then (pyp\ge p/2), while

\[
 S(pyp)=S(y^{1/2}py^{1/2})\le S(y).
\]

So (S(p)<\infty). Its restriction to (p_1Jp_1=A\otimes E_{11}) is a bounded tracial functional. Uniqueness of (\tau) gives (c\tau), where (c=S(p_1)). Equivalence of the matrix projections and traciality give the same normalization on all corners; bounded traces vanish on off-diagonal entries. Hence (S=cT) on every (e_nJe_n=M_n(A)). For arbitrary (x\in J_+),

\[
 S(e_nxe_n)=S(x^{1/2}e_nx^{1/2})\le S(x),\qquad e_nxe_n\to x\text{ in norm}.
\]

Lower semicontinuity supplies the other inequality in the limit, so (S(x)=\lim_n S(e_nxe_n)=cT(x)). If (c=0), this says (S=0); nonzeroness excludes it. Finiteness of (c) was proved, not assumed. This proves the lemma.

For (\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(D)), characteristicity makes (\beta=\alpha|_J\in\operatorname{Aut}(J)). Its pullback (T\circ\beta) is again nonzero, lower semicontinuous and densely finite. Consequently

\[
 T(\beta(x))=c_\alpha T(x)\quad(x\in J_+),\qquad
 0<c_\alpha<\infty,\qquad c_{\alpha^{-1}}=c_\alpha^{-1}. \tag{4.2}
\]

No assertion that (c_\alpha=1) is needed. Unbounded traces with nondense finite domain, including zero/infinity ideal weights, are not covered by the trace-ray lemma. This distinction also prevents misuse of the much larger trace cone in the Razak–Jacelon companion.

## 5. Route I: explicit trace representation and normal extension

Let (\mathfrak n_T=\{x\in J:T(x^*x)<\infty\}), and let (\mathcal H_T=L^2(J,T)) be its completion. Left multiplication by (d\in D) defines (\Pi(d)[x]=[dx]), since (dx\in J) and

\[
 T(x^*d^*dx)\le\|d\|^2T(x^*x).
\]

This representation is faithful: if (d) annihilates all square-integrable vectors, faithfulness of (T) shows (de_n=0), so essentiality gives (d=0).

There is an explicit unitary identification

\[
 \mathcal H_T\cong L^2(A,\tau)\otimes K\otimes\overline K
                 \cong K\otimes K\otimes\overline K=L\otimes\overline K,
 \qquad [a\otimes E_{ij}]\mapsto a\delta_e\otimes\delta_i\otimes\overline{\delta_j}. \tag{5.1}
\]

Inner products agree by (4.1). Finite matrices over group polynomials are dense in this Hilbert norm: truncate square-summable matrix coefficients, then approximate their finitely many (L^2(A,\tau)) entries by group polynomials. Conversely all the elementary basis vectors in (5.1) occur. On these vectors, left multiplication by (a\otimes b\in A\odot B) is (a\otimes b\otimes1). Bounded extension therefore gives

\[
 \Pi(d)=d\otimes1_{\overline K}\quad(d\in D). \tag{5.2}
\]

This is the indispensable identification of the **chosen** representation class. Unique trace alone would not justify it.

For any (\alpha\), define on the entire square-integrable domain

\[
 U_\alpha[x]=c_\alpha^{-1/2}[\beta(x)]. \tag{5.3}
\]

Equation (4.2) says this is isometric. The inverse uses (\beta^{-1}) and the reciprocal scaling, so it is onto and extends to a unitary on the same fixed (\mathcal H_T). For (d\in D,x\in\mathfrak n_T),

\[
 U_\alpha\Pi(d)[x]=c_\alpha^{-1/2}[\alpha(d)\beta(x)]
                  =\Pi(\alpha(d))U_\alpha[x].
\]

Therefore

\[
 U_\alpha(d\otimes1)U_\alpha^*=\alpha(d)\otimes1\qquad(d\in D). \tag{5.4}
\]

This proves simultaneous spatial implementation after one fixed countable amplification, with no smallness condition on (\alpha), (U_\alpha), or (c_\alpha).

**Both directions of the normal-class assertion.** In the original representation,

\[
 J''=D''=M:=\lambda(G)''\,\overline\otimes\,B(K),
\]

since (J) strongly generates the displayed algebra, and (D\subset M). Thus (\Pi(D)''=M\otimes1). Conjugation by (U_\alpha) is a normal onto automorphism of (M\otimes1). The amplification map (x\mapsto x\otimes1) is a faithful normal *-isomorphism onto this image, with normal inverse given there by any unit-vector slice on the third factor. Transporting conjugation gives a normal automorphism (\widetilde\alpha:M\to M) extending (\alpha). Apply the same construction to (\alpha^{-1}); the extensions are inverses because they agree with the identity on the ultraweakly dense (D). This also proves quasi-equivalence of the original representation and its twist by (\alpha), and not merely a one-way normal homomorphism. Normality of the inverse and faithfulness are explicit.

We do **not** deduce that (\widetilde\alpha) is implemented on the original (L). Representation multiplicity and trace scaling can obstruct that inference in general. Formula (5.4) is all that is required. A representation with an additional summand annihilating (J) would not satisfy (5.1). For example, the defining representation of (\mathcal K+\mathbb C1) augmented by its scalar quotient has a different von Neumann closure from its trace-ideal representation. This illustrates why the actual column/trace identification must be proved; it is not a counterexample to A82 or to a statement about its automorphisms.

## 6. Multiplicity: the tensor test survives amplification in both directions

Here is a general elementary amplification lemma, separate from the ideal argument. For unital (P\subset D\subset B(H)) and any nonzero Hilbert space (F), let (P^F=P\otimes1\subset D^F=D\otimes1\subset B(H\otimes F)). The property

\[
 \Big\|\sum_i p_i z_i\Big\|\le\Big\|\sum_i p_i\otimes z_i\Big\|_{\min}
 \quad(p_i\in P,z_i\in D') \tag{6.1}
\]

holds if and only if it holds for (P^F\subset D^F). The same equivalence holds with equality everywhere in place of (6.1).

**Proof.** If (6.1) holds, the product *-homomorphism (P\odot D'\to B(H)) extends to a contractive *-homomorphism on (P\otimes_{\min}D'), hence is completely contractive. If it is isometric, it is completely isometric. The amplified commutant is (D'\overline\otimes B(F)); this follows by taking matrix coefficients on (F). For finite families (Z_i) in this commutant, compress on (F) to any finite-dimensional projection (q). The coefficients (qZ_iq) belong to (M_r(D')), and the compression of the product sum is the matrix amplification of the original product map. Its norm is bounded by (or equals, in the isometric case)

\[
 \Big\|\sum_i p_i\otimes qZ_iq\Big\|.
\]

These are exactly the matching compressions of the spatial tensor sum. Direct the finite-dimensional projections to (1_F). For every bounded operator (X), these two-sided compressions converge strongly to (X), and their norm supremum is (\|X\|) (test finite-support approximations to unit vectors). Taking suprema proves the amplified inequality or equality. This treats the full von Neumann tensor commutant, not just its algebraic elementary tensors. Conversely, take (Z_i=z_i\otimes1_F). Both norms are precisely the original norms, because tensoring with a nonzero identity preserves norm. ∎

Apply the lemma with (F=\overline K). The source's good inclusion therefore has the full tensor identity in (\mathcal H_T). The bad source witness (p_i=Q_t(s_i),z_i=\rho(s_i)\otimes1_K) remains a bad witness after amplification:

\[
 \Big\|\sum_i\Pi(p_i)(z_i\otimes1)\Big\|\ge mt
 >2\sqrt m\ge
 \Big\|\sum_i\Pi(p_i)\otimes(z_i\otimes1)\Big\|. \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus amplification has not erased the distinction. As a check specialized to these actual algebras, the source unitary (V(v\otimes\delta_x)=\lambda(x)^*v\otimes\delta_x), amplified by (1_{\overline K}), commutes with the entire amplified commutant. It sends (\Pi(P_0)) to the copy of (A) acting only on the middle (K) factor. The commutant acts only on the first and third factors, so its product norm is exactly the spatial tensor norm. This independently verifies the good half of the amplification argument on the actual object.

Now suppose (E_D(P_0)\cong E_D(P_t)). Proposition S supplies (\theta\) and (\alpha_n). Formula (5.4) supplies spatial normalizers (U_{\alpha_n}) of (\Pi(D)), all on the same (\mathcal H_T), with

\[
 U_{\alpha_n}\Pi(p)U_{\alpha_n}^*\to\Pi(\theta(p))\quad(p\in P_0)
\]

in norm. The accepted source simultaneous-normalizer lemma, applied to this representation, would transfer the good tensor identity to (\Pi(P_t)), contradicting (6.2). **This proves A82 by Route I.** No construction of a unitary between original sequence representations is assumed.

## 7. Route II: an intrinsic trace-ideal inclusion invariant

The same trace structure permits an intrinsic formulation. Let (D) be any unital C*-algebra with a characteristic essential ideal (J) having a unique ray of nonzero densely finite lower-semicontinuous traces, and assume a faithful such trace exists. Let (\Pi_T) be left multiplication on (L^2(J,T)). Define (\mathsf C_D(P)) for unital (P\subset D) by the requirement

\[
 \Big\|\sum_i\Pi_T(p_i)Z_i\Big\|
 \le\Big\|\sum_i\Pi_T(p_i)\otimes Z_i\Big\|_{\min}
 \quad\text{for all finite families }p_i\in P,\ Z_i\in\Pi_T(D)'. \tag{7.1}
\]

This says precisely that the product representation is continuous for the minimal tensor norm. It does not assert relative weak injectivity, WEP, or a min=max identity for every tensor partner.

The ideal is specified intrinsically, and choosing another normalization of its trace gives a unitarily equivalent left representation: ([x]_T\mapsto c^{-1/2}[x]_{cT}). Thus (7.1) depends only on the abstract inclusion (P\subset D), provided the stated ideal and trace-ray hypotheses hold. There is no additional choice of the original Hilbert-space representation in its definition.

For every automorphism of (D), the construction (5.3), which works under precisely these hypotheses, conjugates (\Pi_T(D)) to itself and implements that automorphism. Conjugating the product by (U_\alpha) and the spatial tensor sum by (U_\alpha\otimes U_\alpha) proves

\[
 \mathsf C_D(P)\iff\mathsf C_D(\alpha(P)). \tag{7.2}
\]

**Exact simultaneous-limit closure.** Suppose (S) holds and (\mathsf C_D(P)) holds. For prescribed finite (q_1,\ldots,q_k\in Q), use the fixed elements (p_i=\theta^{-1}(q_i)), and put (q_{i,n}=\alpha_n(p_i)). By (7.2) the inequality holds for every list (q_{i,n}). For any fixed (Z_i\in\Pi_T(D)'), the product error and the spatial tensor error are both at most

\[
 \sum_i\|q_{i,n}-q_i\|\,\|Z_i\|\longrightarrow0. \tag{7.3}
\]

Passing to the limit proves (\mathsf C_D(Q)). Surjectivity of (\theta) ensures every finite family in (Q) is tested. Conversely (S) also gives (\alpha_n^{-1}(q)\to\theta^{-1}(q)) for each fixed (q\in Q), by the inverse estimate in §2, so the property transfers in both directions. This is closedness for precisely the required pointwise simultaneous limit, not a claim about unrelated weak limits or arbitrary Kadison–Kastler limits.

For the actual (D), §§3–5 verify all intrinsic hypotheses, §6 proves (\mathsf C_D(P_0)), and (6.2) refutes (\mathsf C_D(P_t)). Proposition S and (7.2)–(7.3) again exclude an abstract isomorphism of the sequence algebras. **Route II is complete on the actual objects.** It shares the trace-ideal lemma with Route I and is an intrinsic packaging of that mechanism, not a second independent finding.

## 8. Exact conclusion, scope, and rejected stronger bridges

**Theorem A84.** For the precise source deformation and algebras in §1, for every (t\in(0,1)),

\[
 E_{D_t}(P_0)\not\cong E_{D_t}(P_t)
\]

as abstract complex C*-algebras. Consequently the source's actual sequence examples are arbitrarily close, unital, norm-separable, abstractly nonisomorphic C*-algebras on a common separable Hilbert space, with the same identity and the same von Neumann closure. Their two-sided distance is at most (2c(t)\to0). This final closeness assertion uses the already-paid source bound, not an upgrade of a one-sided near inclusion.

Every ingredient needed for the abstract conclusion is proved above or is a precisely specified accepted source operation. There is no remaining A82 GAP in this author proof. Independent review remains necessary, especially of trace-domain finiteness, (5.1), and passage from finite commutant matrices in §6.

The following tempting shortcuts are explicitly not the proof:

- Scalar center controls sequence coordinates; it does not on its own make an automorphism spatial or normal. The characteristic ideal, trace ray, and exact representation identification supply that step.
- The automorphism preserves a trace **up to scale**. We neither force scale one nor identify the original representation's multiplicity with standard form. The normalization in (5.3) and the extra \(\overline K\) factor pay those issues.
- A finite-rank difference of each group operator is proved from the actual tree formulas. It is not an assumption that a globally bounded deformation conjugator exists. We do not assume the quotient tensor sequence is exact.
- A tensor test in an arbitrary representation is not an abstract invariant. Here its canonical choice is furnished by the intrinsic ideal and trace; both the full amplified commutant and the reverse amplification direction have been checked.
- Relative weak injectivity uses different tensor quantifiers. Replacing (7.1) with that notion without a theorem would leave a gap. The direct continuity invariant avoids that substitution.
- Similarity of a bounded homomorphism to some *-homomorphism does not identify it with a specified representation. No such use is made of the pinned similarity theorem.
- The theorem separates (P_0)'s sequence algebra from each positive-parameter sequence algebra. It does not assert pairwise nonisomorphism among all the (t>0) sequence algebras, exclude arbitrary embeddings, or classify their ideal lattices or K-theory.
