## 1. The binary derivative contraction fails for Potts

Use centered log coordinates h for a strictly positive posterior p=softmax(h). For a q-state symmetric channel with parameter 0<λ<1, its edge update is

\[
 \Phi(h)=\operatorname{center}\big(\log(c+\lambda p_i)\big)_{i=1}^q,
 \qquad c=(1-\lambda)/q.
\]

The discarded additive constant has no effect on the posterior. Suppose p₁=p₂=a and let v=(1,−1,0,…,0), a centered tangent vector. Then p·v=0, so the softmax derivative sends v to av. The log derivative and centering consequently give the exact eigenvector identity

\[
 D\Phi(h)v=\frac{\lambda a}{(1-\lambda)/q+\lambda a}\,v.
\]

This eigenvalue exceeds λ precisely when a>1/q. Such positive posterior vectors exist for every q>2, whereas equality of the two probabilities in the binary case forces a=1/2.

For λ=1/2, the explicit examples are:

| q | p | Derivative eigenvalue | 4 times its square |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | (2/5,2/5,1/5) | 6/11 | 144/121>1 |
| 4 | (3/8,3/8,1/8,1/8) | 3/5 | 36/25>1 |

Thus at b=d−1=4, where bλ²=1, a uniform bound by λ on each centered-log edge derivative is false. The examples have strictly positive coordinates and the inequality persists in a neighborhood; this is not a singular-boundary artifact.

This refutes one proposed proof step only. It neither disproves Potts factor-of-IID at that parameter nor rules out an averaged, posterior-weighted covariance bound. A change of metric or an averaged estimate would have to be supplied and checked through the innovation coupling and critical comparison; citing the binary response estimate cannot supply it.

## 2. Three independent Ising factors give only the elementary range

A second attempt uses three independent free binary broadcasts S¹,S²,S³ of common edge parameter θ and outputs

\[
 Y_v=(S_v^1S_v^3,S_v^2S_v^3)\in\{-1,1\}^2.
\]

On each edge let η₁,η₂,η₃ be their independent multiplicative noises, each of mean θ. The output noise is (η₁η₃,η₂η₃). Its three nonconstant character means are all θ². Fourier inversion on the four-element group gives probability (1+3θ²)/4 for the identity noise and (1−θ²)/4 for each other noise. Edge noises are independent and the root is uniform. Therefore the output is exactly the four-state symmetric broadcast with parameter λ=θ².

If the three binary processes are generated by independent applications of the accepted factor map, their products are also a factor. But the binary hypothesis only allows θ≤1/√b, so this construction reaches λ≤1/b. It does not reach the desired range 1/b<λ≤1/√b. The obtained range already has the ordinary finite-cluster divide-and-color construction. This algebraic transfer is not a substantial new finding.

## 3. Precise remaining work

The candidate needs a finite-state replacement for the binary posterior-response and conditional-copy comparison estimates. A useful theorem must apply to the *Gaussian observation laws* actually used by the factor construction, with integrable control as observation time tends to zero, and prove that innovations determine the observations/spins. Boundary nonreconstruction alone is insufficient. Nor may one assume the four-state preserved posterior class from the capacity proof is closed under every Gaussian experiment: its cited closure covers channels P_t and conditionally independent products, not arbitrary observation degradation.

At present no such replacement or alternative construction is supplied. Keep this route as an explicitly unresolved option. A future task should address this matrix response problem, not repeat the scalar argument or count the elementary quotient as a new result.
