## Closest quantitative literature actually read

**Fusco–Maggi–Pratelli (2008):** [publisher PDF](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v168-n3-p06.pdf), *The sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality*, Theorem 1.1; pp. 941–944 (PDF pages 1–4) read. Its relative perimeter deficit is Q286's Euclidean deficit. Its asymmetry divides by (r^n), so equals (\omega_n\alpha_{\rm geo}) in Euclidean space. It proves the sharp square-root estimate for arbitrary finite-volume Borel sets, with dimensional constant. No connectedness or smoothness restriction is needed. This is the nearest exact collision for a Euclidean specialization.

**Figalli–Maggi–Pratelli (2010):** [author PDF](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~afigalli/papers-pdf/A-mass-transportation-approach-to-quantitative-isoperimetric-inequalities.pdf), *A mass transportation approach to quantitative isoperimetric inequalities*, Theorem 1.1, (1.8), (1.20)–(1.28), Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 3.5; relevant introduction and selection/endpoint passages read. The ambient space and anisotropy are Euclidean, with a fixed bounded convex Wulff body. Theorem 1.1 has a dimension-only asymmetry constant. The proof supplies a derivative estimate for a transport map and removes an exceptional subset to obtain a controlled trace estimate. The radial endpoint resembles I286's R286. Neither the ambient map estimate nor the variable-Jacobian transfer needed here is provided by that theorem.

**Hoisington (2021 preprint):** [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06860), *Hypersurfaces, Geodesics and Isoperimetric Inequalities in Cartan–Hadamard Manifolds*. Introduction statements, Theorem 1.4, Theorem 1.6, Proposition 4.2, and the passages immediately around Proposition 4.2 were read. Theorem 1.4 refines Yau's comparison under a strictly negative sectional-curvature upper bound by a chord integral and has hyperbolic-ball equality. Proposition 4.2 has a nonpositive-curvature chord bound and Euclidean-ball equality. These are actual quantitative geometric refinements, but they do not state squared geodesic-ball volume asymmetry controlled by Q286's relative Euclidean perimeter deficit.

**Bögelein–Duzaar–Scheven (2015), access qualification:** [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00526-015-0928-9), *A sharp quantitative isoperimetric inequality in hyperbolic n-space*. The full publisher PDF request redirected to a subscription article page; no full paper was read. An accessible [author talk](https://wmii.uwm.edu.pl/dirac/f/Verena_Boegelein.pdf), dated 2019-10-08, was read, especially its last two hyperbolic frames and preceding deficit/normal-oscillation definitions. It displays (D(E)\ge c\beta(E)^2), with (c=c(n,R_o)), in hyperbolic space. The final display omits the parameter range and does not supply a self-contained hyperbolic definition of every symbol. This is enough to flag the space-form collision, not to import a precise journal theorem or claim a universal constant across all Cartan–Hadamard metrics.
