## Main tools and predecessors

* [Horodecki, Horodecki and Horodecki, *Separability of mixed states: necessary and sufficient conditions*](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9605038): Theorems 2–3 and proof, displayed lines 109–126; also witness/map discussion 51–112. Theorem 3 is the external `2×2`/`2×3` PPT separability dependency. Its positive-map classification is established literature, not audited afresh.
* [Huber, Lami, Lancien and Müller-Hermes, *High-Dimensional Entanglement in States with Positive Partial Transposition*, displayed v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.04975): §V.1 selected theorem/proof passages 478–515, and §V.2 through the complete Theorem V.3 proof, 525–559. It already proves the nonmaximal-Schmidt-number property of PT-invariant states. S329 independently reconstructs its qutrit specialization; that ingredient is not novel.
* [Christandl, Müller-Hermes and Wolf, *When Do Composed Maps Become Entanglement Breaking?*](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.01266): §III.1, 266–280; local-product reformulation, 492–504; Appendix A, 678–737. Its qutrit endomap corollaries and Schmidt-number machinery predate this task. The manuscript's Section IV.B is displayed as IV.2 in the HTML view. Restricting representing maps to qutrit **endomaps** would miss the source's arbitrary rectangular definition; Q329 explicitly treats that freedom.
* [Vértesi and Brunner, *Disproving the Peres conjecture: Bell nonlocality from bound entanglement*, 1405.4502v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.4502v2): Section II and Discussion, 53–108. This is the analytic two-qutrit PPT Bell predecessor, independently checked here and **excluded from `C`** by Q329. Its zero entanglement-distillation conclusion does not establish zero unrestricted key. Q329 does not decide its key rate.
* [Yu and Oh, *A family of nonlocal bound entangled states*](https://arxiv.org/html/1509.08991): definitions/main theorem/Bell construction, 38–106, and discussion 157–159; displayed v1. It gives bipartite PPT Bell families in every `d≥3`, with a Hardy-derived inequality and explicit measurements. It does not provide the needed two-PPT-map representation in the read passages. The `d≥4` cases remain a factorization lead, not an I329 certificate.
* [Augusiak and Horodecki, *Bound entanglement maximally violating Bell inequalities: quantum entanglement is not equivalent to quantum security*](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0405187): the parent's authenticated reading describes a four-party Smolin result. I relied on the parent's scope comparison, not a new full reading. Four-party absence of common key is not the present bipartite target.

## Later primary comparisons

* [Farkas et al., *Bell nonlocality is not sufficient for the security of standard device-independent quantum key distribution protocols*, displayed 2103.02639v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02639): methods/model 29–70, Werner argument 71–108, Discussion 123–126. The result concerns fresh-copy measurements, publicly announced settings and classical postprocessing. The discussion explicitly distinguishes this from general key uselessness. It cannot replace the source-model zero-`K_D` theorem.
* [Tendick, Kampermann and Bruß, *Activation of nonlocality in bound entanglement*, displayed 1904.07899v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.07899): setup 29–60 and result 96–110. The state/filter/Bell setup is three-party and fully biseparable across the bipartite cuts. Grouping laboratories does not turn this into a nonlocal bipartite cut. No source-model zero-key certificate was imported.
* [Roch i Carceller and Tavakoli, *Bound entangled states are useful in prepare-and-measure scenarios*, 2410.15388v2, 13 February 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15388): Introduction and Scenario, 34–63, plus subsequent source-reference leads. Its communication goes from Alice and Bob to Charlie, who measures the messages. The reported correlation inequality is not an ordinary bipartite Bell inequality. Its different operational setting is why it is not substituted for the target.
* [Hiesmayr, Popp and Sutter, *Bipartite Bound Entanglement*, displayed 2406.13491v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13491): §§5.3.6 and 5.6, selected passages 910–933 and 1015–1053. Used as a **secondary discovery guide**, not as a technical proof premise. It distinguishes positive key in PPT states from entanglement nondistillability and points to later Bell constructions. Relevant primary tools above were checked directly.
