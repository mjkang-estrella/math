## What is being judged

For each fixed r>=1 and every prime q and d>=r, an XOR oracle hides H<=F_q^d of dimension d-r by equality of labels exactly on its cosets. The target returns the unique RREF basis of H with certainty, using uniform polynomial worst-case gates, workspace, classical generation and charged queries in d+log q+m. Polynomial constants and degree may depend on r. Its non-oracle gates are NOT, CNOT, Toffoli, H, S, inverses and singly controlled versions. r is known. No quotient coordinates are input.

The original-basis removal matters. Merely observing that a quotient has order q^r would leave its coordinates unknown. The following explicit operation-level reduction includes that removal and its success law. This is a reduction to R021's credited construction and counting interface, rather than an assertion that R021's modular DLog theorem, treated as an opaque callable, accepts arbitrary oracle labels.

## Full model-preserving reduction

1. For a candidate coordinate subset I of size r, let V be its coordinate columns. For each w=e_j, prepare r+1 dyadic exponent registers and compute f(Vx+yw) with ordinary reversible field arithmetic and a charged XOR query. Clear arithmetic scratch before applying r+1 copies of the same flagged polygonal transform used by R021. All register sizes are O_r(log q), apart from the d-coordinate oracle input and m-bit label. No oracle evaluation on an unknown quotient coordinate is requested.

2. On a genuine quotient basis, write w=Va mod H for analysis. The selected-bin label is (s,s dot a). Its normalized character overlap factors into r+1 of R021's scalar sums S. Nonzero arguments use R021's already paid |S|>Q/3 estimate; zero arguments use S(0,0)=Q. Thus p_s>=9^(-(r+1))q^(-r). r independent trials labeled by B in GL_r give p_B>=9^(-r(r+1))q^(-r^2). Decode a=B^(-1)ell and verify f(w-Va)=f(0). The characters and all q^r quotient labels occur only in the proof, never in the circuit.

3. Given the verified numerical a, label equality imposes r linear congruences x_i-x_i'+a_i(y-y')=qz_i. Use R021's quarter-floor cells and its same Stirling/binomial weight lift. The raw count has 5r+4 integer coordinates and degree 2(r+1); at most 7r+6 coordinates suffice after the lift. This is the same fixed-dimensional counting operation with a different fixed dimension, not a counting problem with dimension depending on d or log q. The common y couples the equations, but does not create a bilinear unknown, a new class of weight or an input-dependent counting dimension. Probabilities have denominator dividing Q^(4(r+1)), and r-fold products remain polynomial-bit dyadics.

4. Apply R021's ordinary/guess two-mode completion separately to each B, with the stated tau and precision. This gives exactly |GL_r(F_q)|tau total mass, bounded below by a positive constant depending only on r. Its multiplicity is the closed product for GL_r; no q-sized list is generated. Fixed repetition and the already accepted unique-output quarter-success compiler make the core certain on its promise. Every off-promise branch remains a bounded unitary computation with guarded arithmetic; it has no asserted success guarantee.

5. Guess r increasing coordinate indices using r ceil(log2 d) fair bits. For every e_j run the core, then check every relation. Valid I always succeeds. If I is invalid, some e_j is outside the span of its quotient images, so no candidate coefficients can make all relation checks pass. On success the relation vectors span H and ordinary elimination gives its unique RREF basis. Thus the raw outer success law is exactly p(H)=N_H/2^(r ceil(log2 d)), not an unspecified probability of discovering a basis.

6. Given a verified RREF basis, compute an annihilator matrix A and count its invertible r-column submatrices by at most d^r rank tests. This computes N_H without additional knowledge of H before success. At least one such subset exists; p(H)>=2^(-r ceil(log2 d)). The accepted witness-computable compiler therefore applies with a polynomial repetition bound, polynomial-bit exact p(H), a unique padded RREF encoding and a charged row-membership verifier. For q=2, replace the core by at most 2^r coefficient tests; the same outer construction applies. All controlled queries can be implemented with two XOR calls and a controlled copy. Inverses, verification and reflections charge their queries too.

Detailed formulas, bounds and the negative-basis argument are independently derived in [analysis.md](../experiments/i338_bounded_corank_hsp_contribution/analysis.md). ## Contribution assessment

R021's substantial operation was the conjunction of a uniformly useful selected event and efficient exact evaluation of its witness-dependent squared norm. Both remain the same operation here. Tensoring a fixed number of the scalar estimates, adding a fixed number of linear congruences, using the existing fixed-degree weight lift and changing the completion label from j to B are technical deductions. They require checking, but do not establish a second substantial contribution by themselves.

The unknown coordinate-basis issue is a real obligation absent from the scalar theorem statement. Here it is discharged by finite coordinate generation, verification and a polynomial-size witness-dependent count. The count enumerates coordinate subsets, not field elements, and fixed r is precisely what keeps it polynomial. It satisfies the accepted compiler's existing restrictive hypotheses directly. No new exact subgroup-state conversion or general exact Fourier sampler is constructed. This resolves the full outer problem technically; it is not an argument based only on the easier supplied-basis core.

The inspected literature does not justify a DUPLICATE verdict in this precise gate model. Their larger group range and query bounds do not implement those operations in the prescribed finite basis. The published 2026 abstract explicitly credits the known-order condition; its full proof was not available in this review. See [literature.md](../sources/i338_bounded_corank_hsp_contribution/literature.md) for precise coverage. No priority conclusion is inferred from missing keyword hits.
