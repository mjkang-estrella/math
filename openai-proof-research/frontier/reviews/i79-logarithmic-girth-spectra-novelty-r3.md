## 3. The scale is real, but rarity alone is not significance

Write `b=d−1`, `α=.01`, `g_n=max(3,floor(α log_b n))`, and `q_n=P(girth>g_n)` for the uniform simple labelled fixed-`d` model. MWW's Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 are the relative zero-cycle result, not its weaker distributional statement with an additive error. The hypotheses hold because `b^(2g_n−1)=O(n^.02)=o(n)`. For sufficiently large n,

`q_n = exp(−Σ_{r=3}^{g_n} b^r/(2r) + o(1))`,

`−log q_n = Θ_d(n^.01/log n)` and `log(1/q_n)/n = Θ_d(n^−.99/log n)`.

The floor changes bounded factors, not these orders. The max with 3 affects only an initial segment. The relative formula also ensures eventual nonempty support through the admissible sizes. MWW treats uniform labelled graphs, as required. Its forward/backward cycle-switch counts are counting estimates; they do not construct an exactly uniform spectral transport kernel. [MWW primary PDF](https://users.monash.edu/~nwormald/papers/girth.pdf).

Consequently an arbitrary fixed polynomial exceptional-probability estimate cannot simply be divided by `q_n`. Nor may one choose a polynomial exponent depending on n in a theorem whose constants and thresholds depend on a fixed exponent. In contrast, the entropy **per vertex** surcharge vanishes with a polynomial margin. The two facts coexist: the event is too rare for naive local-law transfer, yet cheap for an exponential type count. I76's target is substantially rarer than fixed-girth conditioning, but moving to a rarer regime does not by itself prove that the new deduction requires a substantial new idea.

Normalizations were checked. I76 uses ordinary adjacency and the Kesten–McKay density `ρ_d`, with graph coordinates `nρ_d(E)(λ−E)` and GOE coordinates `(n/π)μ`. HY uses `A/√b`, requiring both parameter and resolvent rescaling. B uses the deterministic **expected root spectral density** of the iid-potential tree operator, not a random environment-specific intensity. Fixed positive w precedes n; none of these comparisons justifies w tending to zero jointly with n.

## 4. A: concrete reduction and why the classification is TECHNICAL

The closest stipulated theorem is clean `thm:main`, with the same degree, energy, GOE normalization and Laplace-functional conclusion, but without girth conditioning. Its `prop:spectral-input` is an actual prerequisite, and `03-entropy.tex` uses it for smoothing covariance, delocalization and a Gaussian determinant. Its later fixed-channel switching/insertion operations become ensemble-independent only **after** those prerequisites, type counts and marked symmetries are established.

Here is the reduction supplied by the original I76, evaluated conditionally on its correctness. This identifies both the real work and what was already available.

1. **Retain HY's entire marked experiment.** HY §§3–4 already uses independent far-edge marks on a fixed input, a preserved exposed-ball fiber, and a marked involution. I76 adds rejection of outputs outside the girth event. Rejection is the identity on the entire marked state. It does not replace the proposal by independent draws from an acceptance-conditioned fiber. This restriction of an involution is elementary, but applying it requires verifying the actual marks, fiber weights and reverse indicators, not merely saying “switching is reversible.” Those objects are already constructed in HY Propositions 3.3–3.5.
2. **Keep the rejection correlated with the same defect.** HY's proof of (4.27), using (4.28)–(4.30), already compares the same bad graph event through the two pointwise improvements. I76 splits its mass `p` into accepted and rejected marks. Uniform pointwise rejection at most `r` gives `p ≤ ε₁+ε₂+rp`, hence `p ≤ (ε₁+ε₂)/(1−r)`. This is the general elementary status of the “rejection-absorption lemma”: a reversible-flow decomposition and solving a linear inequality, not a new concentration theorem. HY had already supplied the two fixed-input improvement estimates and the relevant defect organization.
3. **Pay the real geometry bill.** The proposal has polylogarithmically many far edges. Endpoint marginals in the punctured graph are bounded by `2/n` eventually. Requiring far pairs to be separated from the boundary and each other by more than g gives `r_n ≤ C μ(|T|+μ)b^g/n = n^−.99 polylog(n)`. The old-path cycle argument must handle every selected subset of the simultaneous boundary switches, including shared boundary vertices. This is a genuine application obligation. It is nevertheless a bounded-degree ball count and a multi-edge version of the girth-preserving transposition argument already available in I72 and the two corpus companions above. No new resolvent cancellation is derived here.
4. **Reuse continuation, then weaken the interface.** If the original pointwise estimates and parameters are valid, the existing vertex/grid continuation sums `O(n^3)` defects after dividing each by `1−r_n`. It does not sum `n^3 r_n` or multiply a denominator at every continuation step. Full all-index rigidity is not part of this submission. Its replacement is a scalar log determinant: anchor at a large imaginary height using matching short closed-walk moments, then integrate the imaginary trace resolvent down to `η=n^−a`. The weak local-law height powers are integrable, and the tree regularization error is `O(η)`. This is the usual derivative identity for logarithmic potentials plus an analytic power-series anchor. It introduces no new rigidity estimate.
5. **Reuse the downstream source operations at their original limits.** The type count adds `log(1/q_n)`, then the source's deterministic Gaussian entropy/moment machinery runs on the proposed weaker spectral event. Fixed-channel rejected switches cost `o(1)` before n tends to infinity. Signed tails, limiting marked symmetry, increasing-channel Gaussian insertion, density adjustment and GOE compact-count upgrades remain the stipulated source operations. The long audit of their interfaces is necessary for validity; it is not an independently new universality mechanism.

The application burden in steps 1, 3 and 4 is not dismissed because step 2 is short. A lemma can have an elementary proof and a substantial application. Here, however, the inputs already organize the hard local-law improvement pointwise on the original fibers, and already compare precisely the defect that can absorb rejection. Once those original hypotheses are met, the new quantitative costs are a ball-size bound, the factor `(1−r_n)^−1`, an entropy surcharge and a resolvent integral. This is a concrete lower-proof reduction, stronger than saying that a title was not found.

Thus A genuinely enlarges the ensemble conclusion and cannot be obtained by naively conditioning the final source theorem. ## 6. General ensembles and current public primary literature

Local tree geometry and invariance under relabelling alone cannot imply GOE. Randomly relabel the disjoint union of m identical high-girth d-regular graphs on m vertices, with n=m². Taking the component girth above `2α log_b m` gives the required girth scale. The law is permutation-invariant and locally tree-like; every eigenvalue multiplicity is divisible by m. If compact microscopic counts were tight, these multiples force their positive-count probabilities to vanish; if not tight, they cannot converge to GOE either. This is a counterexample to the proposed **general implication**, not to I76's uniform conditional ensemble. MWW ensures the component graphs exist eventually. Relabelling changes no eigenvalue.

The entropy framework imposes more than local weak convergence. Quantitative count events, preserved variance and appropriate covariances are actual hypotheses. Qualitative Gaussian-wave identification also does not identify eigenvalue locations or provide all the microscopic tail/symmetry inputs. I found no valid shortcut from “sublinear entropy loss” alone to A.

They are not deterministic assertions on all geometrically tree-like graphs and do not state I76's conditioned theorem. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00963).
* **He–Huang–Wang, arXiv:2610.08127v1**, posted 6 October 2026. This current public preprint proves fixed-energy bulk universality for uniform simple fixed-degree graphs, including d=3.
