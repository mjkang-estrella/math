 COMPLETE CROUZEIX EQUALITY WITHOUT A SCALAR COEFFICIENT
                      COMPRESSION

                                              MYEONGJIN KANG


       Abstract. We give an explicit irreducible matrix N of order six with unit-disk numerical range
       and a 2 × 2 matrix polynomial P satisfying max|z|≤1 ∥P (z)∥ = 1 and ∥P [N ]∥ = 2. For every pair of
                                                                                               √
       constant complex unit coeﬃcient vectors u, v, we nevertheless have ∥(v ∗ P u)(N )∥ ≤ 849/16 < 2.
       The matrix is twice a member of the classical Li–Spitkovsky–Shukla family. The simple top singular
       pair of P [N ] gives a ﬁnite certiﬁcate of equality and of the obstruction to scalar compression. All
       assertions follow from explicit matrix calculations.

Crouzeix’s scalar conjecture and its complete√ formulation appear in [2, 3]. Crouzeix and Palencia
[4] prove a general bound with constant 1 + 2. Proofs of the scalar conjecture are given in the
preprints [5, 6]. The preprints [10, 11] address the complete inequality with constant two. The
explicit theorem below uses none of these general bounds. The rectangular compression theory of
[1, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.3] already permits higher-rank Schmidt families in abstract norming
vectors. We give a concrete polynomial instance with a uniform obstruction to rank-one coeﬃcient
compression.
   One may ask whether constant unit vectors u, v ∈ Cm always give a scalar polynomial v ∗ P u with
the same attained norm. Assuming the complete Crouzeix bound and a Faber coeﬃcient estimate
from [10], the author proved this conclusion for n ≤ 4 in [7]. The example below shows that the
conclusion fails in general.
   The base matrix is N = 2A, where A is a Li–Spitkovsky–Shukla matrix√[9], in the form reproduced
                                                                                                √
in Lawrence [8, Section 4]. Its parameters are x = 3/5, y = 4/5, c = 14/8, and ξ = η = 1/ 2.
They satisfy x2 + y 2 = ξ 2 + η 2 = 1 and 0 < c < 1/2. We make no claim of a new disk matrix
or a new Hermitian pencil with repeated support eigenvalues. Our construction pairs this matrix
with a polynomial whose norming vectors have Schmidt rank two. An explicit gap below the top
singular value then excludes all constant scalar coeﬃcient compressions. The proof is independent
of a general complete Crouzeix bound and of scalar equality classiﬁcations.

Remark 4.1. Scalar polynomial equality classiﬁcations, such as [1, Theorem 12.2], address scalar
polynomials normalized by their own supremum norms. The estimate here bounds the operator
norm of every compression of one ﬁxed, normalized matrix polynomial. No passage from nonat-
tainment by an individual polynomial to a strict supremum over all scalar polynomials is used.
