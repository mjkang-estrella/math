\begin{abstract}
We prove a path-space upgrade of the finite-dimensional hard-sphere
fluctuation theorem on an entire supplied regular Boltzmann interval.
The setting is the deterministic, nonequilibrium, grand-canonical gas in
three-dimensional whole space, with exact microscopic centering and no time
smoothing. Relative to the pinned finite-history interfaces, one collective
time translation of a connected history gives a shrinking-window factor at
its sharp factorial-cumulant scale. This also localizes redundant contacts,
whose times are determined rather than independent coordinates. A
prefix-measurable global chronology indicator is retained through the
translation and the flux-tree estimate. Initial-link cylinders preserve the
connected activity scale, and a fixed Gaussian reserve keeps summability
independent of the later moment order. A decorated final-slab output kernel
controls the number of jumping particles. Third moments at scale
$\mu^{-3/2}$, dyadic chaining, and a uniform exact-centering comparison yield
$J_1$ convergence and C-tightness for every finite vector of compact smooth
tests, with the Gaussian covariance of the credited finite-dimensional theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The gas, the target, and the result}\label{sec:statement}
We work on $\R_x^3\times\R_v^3$, with $z=(x,v)$. All surface integrals on
$S^2$ use ordinary, unnormalized area measure. For $\beta>0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bolnorm}
 \norm g_{\Bol,\beta}
 =\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z^3}
     \sup_{\substack{|x-a|\le1\\v\in\R^3}}
           e^{\beta|v|^2}|g(x,v)|.
\end{equation}
For relative velocity $w=v-v_*$ and $\omega\in S^2$ use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision}
 B(w,\omega)=[w\cdot\omega]_+,\qquad
 v'=v-(w\cdot\omega)\omega,\qquad
 v_*'=v_*+(w\cdot\omega)\omega.
\end{equation}
The Boltzmann collision operator is
\[
 Q(f,f)(x,v)=\int_{\R^3\times S^2}
       B(v-v_*,\omega)(f'f_*'-ff_*)\dd v_*\dd\omega,
\]
with all four factors evaluated at the same position $x$.

\begin{assumption}[The kinetic hypothesis]\label{ass:kinetic}
The function $f_0\ge0$ is a smooth probability density and, for some
$\beta>0$,
\[
 \norm{f_0}_{\Bol,2\beta}+\norm{\nabla_xf_0}_{\Bol,2\beta}<\infty.
\]
There is a nonnegative classical solution of
$(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=Q(f,f)$, with initial datum $f_0$, on a
prescribed finite interval $[0,T]$, $T>0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular}
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\sup_{x,v}e^{2\beta|v|^2}f_t(x,v)<\infty.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
This is exactly Assumption \texttt{ass:kinetic} of the pinned source
\cite{H}. No smallness or global existence assumption is added.

For sphere diameter $\eps>0$ set $\mu=\eps^{-2}$ and
\[
 \mathcal D_N^\eps=\{Z_N:|x_i-x_j|>\eps\text{ for }i\ne j\}.
\]
The initial law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \Prob_\eps(N,dZ_N)=\mathcal Z_\eps^{-1}\frac{\mu^N}{N!}
       \ind_{\mathcal D_N^\eps}(Z_N)\prod_{i=1}^Nf_0(z_i)\dd Z_N,
       \qquad N=0,1,2,\ldots.
\end{equation}
The empty configuration is included. From the sampled initial configuration,
the particles move
by deterministic free flight and elastic hard-sphere collisions. We use the
almost-everywhere flow used in \cite{H}, and adopt right-continuous velocities
(post-collision values at collision times) as our convention. For each fixed
particle number $N$, the initial law has a Lebesgue density on
$\mathcal D_N^\eps$. At any prescribed time the contact configurations lie
on a finite union of codimension-one surfaces $|x_i-x_j|=\eps$, of
Lebesgue measure zero. The measure-preserving hard-sphere flow therefore
makes their preimages null, outside its already null exceptional set.
Conditioning on $N$ and summing over its countably many values (with no
contact for $N=0,1$) proves that a collision at a fixed time has probability
zero. Thus this convention leaves all fixed-time inputs from H unchanged.
There is no random collision rule or physical resampling.

For real $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\R^6)$ define the unsmoothed fluctuation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field}
 X_t^\eps(\phi)=\mu^{-1/2}
    \left(\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(z_i(t))
                 -\E\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(z_i(t))\right).
\end{equation}
The centering is the exact microscopic expectation at each time. It is not
replaced by $\int f_t\phi$.

Write
\[
 \Delta\phi=\phi(x,v')+\phi(x,v_*')-\phi(x,v)-\phi(x,v_*).
\]
The Gaussian target has weak drift and noise form
\begin{align}
 A_t\phi&=v\cdot\nabla_x\phi+
    \int B(v-v_*,\omega)f_t(x,v_*)\Delta\phi\dd v_*\dd\omega,
       \label{eq:drift}\\
 C_t(\phi,\psi)&=\frac12\int B(v-v_*,\omega)f_t(x,v)f_t(x,v_*)
       \Delta\phi\Delta\psi\dd x\dd v\dd v_*\dd\omega.
       \label{eq:noise}
\end{align}
Let $U(r,t)$ be the backward propagator of $A_t$ constructed in \cite[Section
2, \texttt{lem:target-propagator}]{H}. The target is the centered jointly
Gaussian field with covariance
\begin{align}
 K_{\phi,\psi}(t,s)
 ={}&\int f_0(z)U(0,t)\phi(z)U(0,s)\psi(z)\dd z\notag\\
 &+\int_0^{t\wedge s}C_r\bigl(U(r,t)\phi,U(r,s)\psi\bigr)\dd r.
       \label{eq:covariance}
\end{align}
Its initial covariance is $\int f_0\phi\psi$, with no fixed-number
subtraction. Equivalently it solves
$d\zeta_t(\phi)=\zeta_t(A_t\phi)dt+dW_t(\phi)$ with the independent initial
field and Gaussian noise prescribed by \eqref{eq:noise}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:kinetic}, relative to the source interfaces stated
in Section~\ref{sec:inputs}, for every finite list of real
$\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_d\in C_c^\infty(\R^6)$,
\[
 (X^\eps_\cdot(\phi_1),\ldots,X^\eps_\cdot(\phi_d))
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 (\zeta_\cdot(\phi_1),\ldots,\zeta_\cdot(\phi_d))
\]
in $D([0,T],\R^d)$ with the Skorokhod $J_1$ topology. The sequence is
C-tight, and the target is the continuous version of the Gaussian field
with covariance \eqref{eq:covariance}.
\end{theorem}
The interval is the whole supplied $[0,T]$. The theorem concerns the actual
gas \eqref{eq:law}, not an independently restarted gas or an integrated
observable. We make no weak-convergence assertion for the uniform-topology
Borel space on all of $D$.

Bodineau--Gallagher--Saint-Raymond--Simonella proved short-time
nonequilibrium process convergence for a periodic gas \cite{BGSSshort},
and long-time equilibrium process convergence by an invariant-measure
argument \cite{BGSSlong}. Deng--Hani--Ma supply the regular-lifespan
framework \cite{DHM}; their Section 1.4.3 identifies fluctuations as a
further direction. The pinned source \cite{H} gives the finite-dimensional
law on the regular lifespan and explicitly leaves path-space tightness
outside its conclusion. The new operation here is the collective
shrinking-window estimate in Section~\ref{sec:window}. The moment,
continuity, chaining, and centering arguments are credited separately.
These comparisons do not assert worldwide priority.

\subsection{One auxiliary dynamics and factorial normalization}
Fix the dimensional collision-excess cap $\Gamma$ first and then a large
fixed number $L$ of slabs, with $\tau=T/L$. H uses the size cutoffs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoffs}
 A_L=|\log\eps|,\qquad \Lambda_i=A_i^{30},\qquad
 A_{i-1}=\Lambda_i^{30},
\end{equation}
with harmless integer rounding. Within each slab an incoming collision is
accepted if adjoining it to the current collision graph leaves its component
with at most $\Lambda_i$ labels and excess at most $\Gamma$; otherwise both
particles continue straight. At an interface only this collision prehistory
is reset. Phase points and records are pasted without a jump. This is H's
single auxiliary pasted dynamics; the target physical flow is not reset.
It preserves $N$ and has finitely many accepted collisions. We put hats on
its fields and center them by their own exact expectations.

If $F_{h,t}^\eps$ is its ordered factorial measure normalized by $\mu^h$,
then, for a nonnegative measurable test $H_0$,
\[
 F_{h,t}^\eps(H_0)=\mu^{-h}\E
       \sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_h\text{ distinct}}H_0(z_{i_1}(t),\ldots,z_{i_h}(t)).
\]
The same definition allows specified records on the designated labels.
Normalized factorial cumulants satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
 F_{h,t}^\eps=\sum_{\pi\in\calP([h])}
                   \bigotimes_{V\in\pi}c_{|V|,t}^\eps.
\end{equation}
H02, Proposition \texttt{prop:initial}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N}
 F_{h,0}^\eps\le f_0^{\otimes h},\qquad
 \E(N)_h\le\mu^h,\qquad
 \sup_{\eps\le\eps_0}\E(N/\mu)^p<\infty
       \quad\text{for every fixed integer }p.
\end{equation}
The factorial-mass bound persists under the number-preserving dynamics.

A scalar record is initially zero and is unchanged by collisions. It has
updates $y\mapsto y+\phi_j(z)$ at prescribed pulse times. Its sum records the corresponding
multitime empirical sum. H07, \texttt{eq:limit-factorial-ordinary}, gives
for $S=\sum_i y_i(T)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary}
 \kappa_j(S)=\sum_{\pi\in\calP([j])}\mu^{|\pi|}
     \int c_{|\pi|,T}^\eps(d\xi)
                    \prod_{V\in\pi}y_V^{|V|}.
\end{equation}
The blocks $V$ are associated with distinct particle slots in a fixed order.
This is a finite formal identity through order $j$: expand
$\prod_i[1+(e^{u y_i}-1)]$ and take its logarithm. Each factor receives a
nonempty set of differentiations, giving the displayed partitions. No
uniform radius of exponential integrability is needed.

\subsection{The exact one-slab root-output kernel}\label{subsec:kernels}
Here we record the finite identity used later for a nonadditive flag. For a
finite particle set $V_0$ let $\Psi_C$ be the autonomous capped history of
$C\subset V_0$, and let $\kappa_C$ indicate that its accepted collision graph
is connected. Singletons have $\kappa_C=1$. For disjoint autonomous clusters
let $a(C,D)$ indicate the existence of an incoming cross-contact that would
be accepted with their prefix prehistories, and set $\chi(C,D)=1-a(C,D)$.
H04, Lemma \texttt{lem:autonomous-partition}, states, pointwise on its common
full-measure set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:autonomous}
 H_0(\Psi_{V_0}|_S)=\sum_{\mathcal C\in\calP(V_0)}
    \prod_{C\in\mathcal C}\kappa_C
    \prod_{\{C,D\}\subset\mathcal C}\chi(C,D)
                      H_0(\Psi_{\mathcal C}|_S)
\end{equation}
for every bounded measurable test of the root histories. The selected
juxtaposition is the actual capped history; no independence of input is
assumed.

For a cluster block $V_c$ write
$u(V_c)=\sum_{G\text{ connected on }V_c}\prod_{e\in G}(-a_e)$ and
$u(\{C\})=1$. The rooted forest exploration stops when a rooted cluster
component reaches $M_i=\lfloor\Lambda_i/2\rfloor$. Its retained root-output
kernel, for roots $S=[s]$ and reached labels $V_0=[s+j]$, is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal K_{S,V_0}(Z,dZ'_S)
 =\sum_{\mathcal C\in\calP(V_0)}\prod_{C\in\mathcal C}\kappa_C
 \sum_{\substack{\mathcal B\in\calP(\mathcal C)\\
       (\cup_{C\in V_c}C)\cap S\ne\varnothing,\ |V_c|<M_i}}
 \prod_{V_c\in\mathcal B}u(V_c)
                   \delta_{\Psi_{\mathcal C}(Z)|_S}(dZ'_S).
       \label{eq:kernel}
\end{align}
The inner restrictions hold for every block. The corresponding operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 \mathcal L_{i,s}F=\sum_j\frac{\mu^j}{j!}
                        \int F_{s+j}(dZ)\mathcal K_{S,V_0}(Z,\cdot).
\end{equation}
The sum is finite under the caps. H04's one-slab identity is
$F'_{s}=\mathcal L_{i,s}F+R_s$, with
$|R_s(H_0)|\le(\mathcal E_{i,s}F)(|H_0|)$, where $\mathcal E$ is a positive
stopped-forest kernel with the same output map. Each forest exploration
branch has multiplicity at most one. Spectator clusters retain mutual
compatibility and cancel by \eqref{eq:autonomous} before integration. The
fresh-label identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh}
 \frac1{(n-s)!}\binom{n-s}{j}=\frac1{j!(n-s-j)!}.
\end{equation}
It yields exactly the coefficient in \eqref{eq:operator}. This kernel
identity, including the output before pushforward, is an imported interface.

\subsection{Resummed diagrams and errors}\label{subsec:expansion}
H04 defines ordinary independent one-root inputs $g_i$ by the entire retained
one-root map on independent inputs, and proves their Gaussian Bol envelopes
and convergence to $f_{i\tau}$, including partial slabs. Required record
lines continue through all interfaces to zero. Other one-root blocks
attached through only one line can be resummed into the ordinary $g_i$.
This is an exact finite regrouping, not a factorization of the true interface
law. Forgetting a record commutes with the finite hierarchy and the
regrouping.

The retained hierarchy factors over its global components. Finite inversion
of \eqref{eq:partition} therefore keeps exactly the globally connected
diagrams; initial cumulant blocks count as connections, artificial dummy
links do not. H04, Theorem \texttt{thm:marked-expansion}, supplies for fixed
root order $h$ and a fixed record list bounded in number by $r_{\rm rec}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an unweighted total-variation error at most $C_h e^{-c\Lambda_L}$
 between the actual cumulant and the retained connected sum;
\item for every assigned power $q>0$, an auxiliary $K$ after that order and
 list such that the retained complexity tail $\rho>K$ has absolute variation
 at most $\eps^q$;
\item the original fresh-label coefficients, with denominator
 $\prod_i j_i!$, and at bounded complexity
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:factorials}
 n_i-j_i=O_{K,h,r_{\rm rec},L}(1),\qquad
 \prod_i\frac1{j_i!}\le
     \frac{(1+\sum_i n_i)^{C_{K,h,r_{\rm rec},L}}}{\prod_i n_i!}.
 \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
All constants in an exponential base per ordinary atom are independent of
$K$ and of the subsequently fixed moment order. Distinguished choices cost
fixed polynomials; in the coarse regime they cost cutoff powers. These
claims follow from the actual finite kernels and positive stopped bounds,
not from a postulated uniform limit theorem.

The coarse tail has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarse}
 C_{h,r_{\rm rec}}\eps^{-C_*h-C_{r_{\rm rec}}+c_*K/2},
\end{equation}
where $r_{\rm rec}$ is the fixed record bound, corresponding to H04's
parameter $m$. H03's allowed boundary modifications
include a top-root deficit $\eps^{-C_*h}$ and at most
$\eps^{-q_0/81}$ for $q_0$ specified lines requiring dummy initial
continuations. The auxiliary dummy straight roots have unit-mass Gaussian
Bol inputs; their activities and root normalizations cancel. They are used
only for coarse domination and removed before the bounded-complexity
physical-link analysis. Their number is fixed by the root/record list,
not by the number of collisions. All stopped bounds are uniform on a
partial final slab and assert no Gaussian weight for a general TV error.

\subsection{Physical charts and sharp summation}\label{subsec:charts}
A C-atom scatters a pair elastically; an O-atom records an incoming encounter
with straight continuation. On replacing bounded-order initial cumulants
by H06's forest domination, each initial edge has weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:links}
 \ind_{|x_p(0)-x_q(0)|\le R_{K,D}\eps}+\eps^D.
\end{equation}
There are at most $K$ such edges. They join lines born at zero.
First retain only their physical indicator terms. For a graph with $m$
distinct birth lines, $c$ global components, $p$ physical initial edges,
$a_i$ dynamic mergers and $n_i$ lines in slab $i$, H06 gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forestcounts}
 a:=\sum_i a_i=m-c-p,\qquad
 \sum_i(n_i-a_i)=O_K(1),\qquad 0\le a_i\le n_i.
\end{equation}
The number of redundant contacts is $O_K(1)$. A continued physical line
is one vertex and has no new birth variable at an interface.

H06, Lemma \texttt{lem:sharp-merger-coordinates}, is a change of variables
on each prescribed physical incoming straight-segment branch. Its coordinates
are independent birth velocities, one common translation per global
component, the relative affine origins of physical initial forest edges,
and $(t_g,\omega_g)$ at every dynamic merger. Its merger Jacobian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
 \eps^2[(v-v_*)\cdot\omega_g]_+dt_g\dd\omega_g.
\end{equation}
A redundant contact has no free parameter: for a relative straight flight
$r(t)=d+tu$ on the common segment interval $I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:redundant}
 \int_I\delta(|r(t)|-\eps)[-u\cdot r(t)/\eps]_+dt
   =\ind_{\{\text{an entering intersection occurs in }I\}}.
\end{equation}
There is at most one entering root on the whole relative flight. Merger
coordinates have multiplicity one on the specified physical branch. Their
activity is $\mu^{m-h}$; the initial forest solve has determinant one.

The source fresh-label and energy-bin summation gives, at fixed complexity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-summability}
 C_K(1+n)^{\gamma_K}(C\tau\sqrt L)^n\mu^{1-h},\qquad n=\sum_i n_i,
\end{equation}
for connected absolute diagram measures. The original physical link volume
$O(\eps^3)$ supplies an extra $\eps$ per initial link. Erasing a tail edge
in \eqref{eq:links} increases the component count by at most one; erasing
at most $K$ costs at most $\eps^{-2K}$. Thus $D>2K+q_{\rm tail}$ makes all terms with
an initial tail $O(\eps^{q_{\rm tail}}\mu^{1-h})$, where $q_{\rm tail}>0$
is the assigned tail accuracy, distinct from $M_i$ and $M_\phi$. We will reproduce the summation steps
needed after the new enlargement, rather than assume
\eqref{eq:source-summability} continues to hold there.

H05, Proposition \texttt{prop:cutoff}, gives, for every sufficiently large

This is a simultaneous path comparison, not a moment estimate on the

\section{A fixed Gaussian reserve and one choice of slabs}\label{sec:budget}
The order of choices is essential. Let $\beta_*=\beta/4$, before any moment
order or complexity threshold. H03's exponents satisfy
$\beta_i\ge9\beta/20>\beta_*$. For each fixed candidate $L$ and
$\eps\le\eps_0(L)$, H04's one-root estimate gives
\[
 \norm{g_i-f_{i\tau}}_{\Bol,\beta_i}\le\eps^{\theta_i}\le1.
\]
H03, Lemma \texttt{lem:kinetic-envelopes}, bounds
$\sup_{t\le T}\norm{f_t}_{\Bol,\beta}$ in terms of the kinetic data.
Consequently there is a constant $B_0$ depending only on those data, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-envelope}
 \sup_i\norm{g_i}_{\Bol,\beta_*}\le B_0
       \quad (\eps\le\eps_0(L)).
\end{equation}
The smallness threshold may depend on $L$; the envelope constant does not.
This prevents a circular choice of $L$ from an $L$-dependent atom base.

For birth velocities $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ put $E_0=\sum_p|v_p|^2$. Integrating
one spatial translation by a spatial $L^1$ norm and the remaining inputs
by their spatial suprema gives $C^m e^{-\beta_*E_0}$.
Reserve a fraction now, independently of later $K$: any fixed-degree
polynomial satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polyabsorb}
 (1+E_0)^{p/2}\le C_{p,\beta_*}e^{\beta_*E_0/4}.
\end{equation}
The remaining $e^{-3\beta_*E_0/4}$ supplies both a velocity-volume integral
and a dyadic suppression. For $E_0\in[P/2,P]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reserve}
 e^{-3\beta_*E_0/4}
       \le e^{-\beta_*E_0/4}e^{-\beta_*P/4}.
\end{equation}
The lowest bin $E_0<1$ is treated separately.

All per-birth, normal, energy-bin, and ordinary-atom constants determined by
this reserve are fixed independently of $K$ and the order. Choose $L$ once
large enough for the source coarse/cutoff thresholds at these exponents
and for $CT/\sqrt L<1$. Only after this choice specify an observation list,
a moment order, an accuracy power, and $K,D$. H permits every sufficiently
large fixed $L$ and the entire supplied $T$ is retained. No later increase
of $K$ or of a fixed moment order changes the auxiliary dynamics.

\section{The collective contact-window estimate}\label{sec:window}
For a retained connected diagram and a designated line $\ell$, let
$N_J(\ell)$ be the number of its C-atoms incident to $\ell$ with time in a
bounded interval $J$. We use absolute birth inputs and retain the prescribed
history domains. Bounded record multipliers can be replaced by their
suprema.

\begin{lemma}[Contact windows]\label{lem:window}
For fixed root order $h$, fixed complexity bound $K$, fixed initial sharpness
$D$, and a fixed bounded record list, the physical-link connected diagrams
$\mathfrak D_K$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 \sum_{\mathsf D\in\mathfrak D_K}\int N_J(\ell)\dd|\mathsf D|
        \le C\mu^{1-h}|J|.
\end{equation}
The constant is uniform in sufficiently small $\eps$, in $J$, and in all
record or pulse positions. The counted C-atom may be a dynamic merger or a
redundant contact.
\end{lemma}
We prove the lemma in several steps. No quantitative disappearance of cycles
is used.

\subsection{Take the physical chart before enlarging it}\label{subsec:physical}
Fix a graph $G$, its C/O choices, slab assignments, prescribed global event
order, physical initial forest, and merger/redundant classification. Choose
one marked C-slot $a_*$ incident to $\ell$. The global order is the ordinary
chronological refinement in the source enumeration; it adds no independent
permutation factor. In the connected case the physical coordinates are
\[
 \xi=(v,X,y,(t_g,\omega_g)_g),\qquad
 d\xi=dv\dd X\dd y\dd t\dd\omega,
\]
with $a=m-1-p$ dynamic mergers. Let $j_i$ be the fresh labels in slab $i$.
To display all discrete weights, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-weight}
 \mathfrak a_G=\mathfrak b_G\prod_i\frac1{j_i!},
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak b_G$ contains the fixed graph's forest-domination and
other discrete coefficients. They will be summed with H's factorial count;
none is hidden in a constant that discards the denominator. The physical
change of variables gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W0}
 \int\ind_{t_{a_*}\in J}\dd|\mathsf D_G|
 \le\mathfrak a_G\mu^{m-h}\eps^{2(m-1-p)}
      \int F_G(\xi)\ind_{t_{a_*}(\xi)\in J}\dd\xi.
\end{equation}
Here $F_G$ contains the birth factors, physical-link balls, merger fluxes,
and all physical segment, lifetime, acceptance, slab, and order indicators.
Equation \eqref{eq:jacobian} is used on this physical chart only. What follows
is a positive majorant after that change, not a new global Jacobian claim.

Let $A_p$ be the affine origin at zero of line $p$'s pre-first-event segment,
whose independent velocity is $v_p$. This need not be the eventual
piecewise path's value at zero. For initial forest edges orient $p_l$ to
$q_l$ and set $y_l=A_{q_l}-A_{p_l}$. Their endpoints are born at zero,
so these are the actual initial position differences on the physical chart.
The forest determines each initial group up to a common translation. A
positive-interface birth has its own independent input; an interface
continuation does not. At the end choose $X=A_{\rm rep}$ for a fixed
representative.

\subsection{A prefix-measurable chronological extension}\label{subsec:prefix}
Start all $m$ lines on their pre-first affine segments on the whole real
axis. For each group keep its old segments, current incoming affine segments,
and one free common translation. Process the prescribed global slots:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At a merger between distinct groups, with prescribed $t_g,\omega_g$,
 solve the source contact equation
 $X_1-X_2=-d(t_g)-\eps\omega_g$. Translate every segment already constructed
 in the moved group by the same vector. All earlier internal contacts,
 initial $y_l$, times, and velocities are preserved. Keep one common
 translation for the joined group. Use the current incoming segments
 extrapolated to $t_g$ and require positive incoming flux.
\item At a redundant slot use the two current segments. Take their unique
 transversal entering root at or after the later preceding incident event
 time; a missing preceding time means $-\infty$. Abort if no such root
 exists. This root and its normal are determined, with no extra variable.
\item In both cases require the new time to be strictly later than the
 preceding global slot; the first lower bound is $-\infty$. Abort otherwise.
 On success perform its elastic C-update or straight O-update and continue.
\end{enumerate}
Write $R_G^{\rm ext}$ for the incoming/existence tests and $Q_G$ for the
strict global order tests. Their product is the product of prefix success
indicators, extended by zero at the first failure. No future trajectory is
needed on a failed branch. The forest solve and entering quadratic roots
are measurable prefix operations. After fixing the final common $X$, the
successful construction is unique and is a chronological all-time path.

There is one important distinction between physical null sets and extended
rejection. If a redundant O-update is followed by another redundant slot
on that same unchanged relative flight, the entering-root rule can return
exactly the same time. This is a structural tie on the extended chart, not
a null-set assertion. Strict $Q_G$ rejects it with weight zero. Such a
branch cannot describe two physical incoming contacts on a fixed segment
pair. The tie and its rejection are shift invariant. Other physical
grazing and simultaneous-contact exclusions retain the source's
almost-everywhere convention.

All $m$ lines in this extension are present at every real time. Elastic
C-updates conserve pair energy and O-updates change none. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-energy}
 \sum_{p=1}^m|v_p^{\rm current}|^2=E_0
\end{equation}
on every successful prefix. In particular any slab's subset has energy at
most $E_0$, even when an extended event falls outside a physical lifetime.

The input factor remains
$|g_{(p)}(x_p^\xi(b_p),v_p)|$ at its original fixed birth time $b_p$.
On a nonphysical branch its independent second argument $v_p$ may differ
from the instantaneous velocity at $b_p$. This is allowed for a positive
extension, not asserted to be a gas law. It agrees on the physical domain.
Every spatial argument remains $X+\delta_p$, and its Gaussian argument is the
independent pre-first velocity.

Define, on successful branches and by zero after failure,
\begin{align}
 F_G^{\rm ext,Q}(\xi)
 ={}&\prod_p|g_{(p)}(x_p^\xi(b_p),v_p)|
     \prod_l\ind_{|y_l|\le R_{K,D}\eps}\notag\\
 &\cdot\prod_g[(\Delta v_g)\cdot\omega_g]_+
      \ind_{t\in\mathcal D}R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G,
       \label{eq:extension}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal D$ prescribes the merger slabs and relative merger order.
Records have already been bounded; they are not reinterpreted on this
extension.

\begin{lemma}[Physical domination]\label{lem:domination}
On the physical chart $F_G\le F_G^{\rm ext,Q}$ and the marked time agrees
with its extended time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the slots. A physical merger solves the same relative-translation
equation. A physical redundant contact lies on the current common segment
interval, which is contained in the extension's forward ray. The straight
relative flight has at most one entering root, so the physical root is the
computed root. Every physical global order test succeeds. Thus the paths and
velocities coincide throughout the physical lifetimes and $Q_G=1$ there.
Every omitted physical factor is between zero and one.
\end{proof}

For comparison, the local entering test alone is insufficient. With
unit diameter and incoming collinear data there are merger slots at $1/5$
and $2/5$, then a determined redundant time $7/15$, then a merger prescribed
at $9/20$. All local fluxes can be positive, but $7/15>9/20$.
The construction above rejects the final slot. The accompanying exact
counterchart documents this failure of the chronology-free extension; it is
not a counterexample to the physical gas.

\subsection{The collective shear}\label{subsec:shear}
For $\theta\in\R$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shear}
 \Phi_\theta(v,X,y,t,\omega)=
 \bigl(v,X-\theta v_{\rm rep},
       (y_l-\theta(v_{q_l}-v_{p_l}))_l,t+\theta\mathbf1,\omega\bigr).
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Covariance and measure]\label{lem:shear}
The shear preserves success or failure and its prefix, $R_G^{\rm ext}$,
$Q_G$, and all fluxes. On successful histories,
\[
 x_p^{\Phi_\theta\xi}(s)=x_p^\xi(s-\theta)
       \quad\text{for all }p,s.
\]
Every computed event time increases by $\theta$. The map preserves
$dv\dd X\dd y\dd t\dd\omega$ and has inverse $\Phi_{-\theta}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Time translation changes each pre-first affine origin by
$A_p\mapsto A_p-\theta v_p$, giving precisely the $X,y$ shear. At a merger,
translate all old segments of the moved group. Its contact equation at
$t_g+\theta$ is the old equation at $t_g$, and the incoming sign is the
same. At a redundant slot the relative flight and its preceding-time lower
bound translate together, so its entering root and normal have the claimed
covariance. The global order comparison uses two times both increased by
$\theta$. This proves covariance and matching aborts by prefix induction;
structural ties also remain ties. Future group translations preserve the
old internal conditions. Uniqueness of the final forest solve fixes $X$.
The affine-origin argument does not use the generally false equality
$x_p(-\theta)=A_p-\theta v_p$ for a full path that has already scattered.
For fixed $v$ the map translates $(X,y,t)$. On the full parameter space it
has block triangular derivative with identity diagonal, and leaves the
sphere variables fixed. The inverse proves measure preservation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Average first, retaining chronology}\label{subsec:average}
Put $\Theta=\tau$. For the extended marked integral, change variables and
average to obtain
\begin{align}
 I_G&:=\int F_G^{\rm ext,Q}(\xi)
                     \ind_{t_{a_*}^{\rm ext}(\xi)\in J}\dd\xi\notag\\
 &=\frac1\Theta\int_0^\Theta\!\int
     F_G^{\rm ext,Q}(\Phi_\theta\eta)
           \ind_{t_{a_*}^{\rm ext}(\eta)+\theta\in J}\dd\eta\dd\theta.
       \label{eq:average}
\end{align}
All integrands are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies. First integrate $X$.
Its coefficient in every birth position is the identity, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatial}
 \int\prod_p|g_{(p)}(X+\delta_p,v_p)|\dd X
 \le\norm{g_{(p_0)}(\cdot,v_{p_0})}_1
         \prod_{p\ne p_0}\norm{g_{(p)}(\cdot,v_p)}_\infty
 =:\mathfrak B(v)\le C^m e^{-\beta_*E_0}.
\end{equation}
The bound is independent of the displacements, including their dependence
on the shift and on fixed birth times.

For $u_l=v_{q_l}-v_{p_l}$, a single simultaneous shift yields the containing
cylinder
\begin{align}
 \ind_{|y_l-\theta u_l|\le R_{K,D}\eps}
 &\le\ind_{y_l\in\Cyl_l},\qquad
 \Cyl_l=[0,\Theta]u_l+B(0,R_{K,D}\eps),\notag\\
 |\Cyl_l|&\le\pi R_{K,D}^2\eps^2\Theta|u_l|
                    +\frac{4\pi}3R_{K,D}^3\eps^3.
       \label{eq:cylinder}
\end{align}
Replacing simultaneous membership by the product of these containing sets
is a positive upper bound; no separate shift is introduced per initial link.
The time-window integral is at most
\[
 \int_0^\Theta\ind_{t+\theta\mathbf1\in\mathcal D}
          \ind_{t_{a_*}^{\rm ext}+\theta\in J}\dd\theta
 \le |J|\ind_{t\in\mathcal D_\Theta},\qquad
 \mathcal D_\Theta=\mathcal D-[0,\Theta]\mathbf1.
\]
The fluxes and both prefix indicators are shift invariant. Thus
\begin{align}
 I_G\le\frac{|J|}{\Theta}\int dv\,\mathfrak B(v)
  \int dy\prod_l\ind_{y_l\in\Cyl_l}
  \int_{\mathcal D_\Theta}dt\int d\omega\,
          R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G\prod_g[(\Delta v_g)\cdot\omega_g]_+.
       \label{eq:W1}
\end{align}
Chronology has not been dropped in this formula.

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous simplex enlargement]\label{lem:simplex}
If $S_a=\{s<t_1<\cdots<t_a<s+\tau\}$ and $a\ge1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex}
 |S_a-[0,\Theta]\mathbf1|
   =\frac{\tau^a}{a!}+\Theta\frac{\tau^{a-1}}{(a-1)!}
   =\frac{\tau^a}{a!}\left(1+\frac{a\Theta}{\tau}\right).
\end{equation}
The empty simplex has zero-dimensional volume one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the ordered gaps $t_k-t_1$. The first-time length is $\tau-(t_a-t_1)$
in $S_a$ and increases by $\Theta$ in the enlargement. Integrating over
the gap simplex gives the original volume and $\Theta$ times its
$(a-1)$-dimensional gap volume, proving the formula. For $a=1$ there are
no gap coordinates and the length is $\tau+\Theta$.
\end{proof}
Since $\mathcal D$ is a product of slab merger simplices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:time-volume}
 |\mathcal D_\Theta|\le
 \prod_i\frac{\tau^{a_i}}{a_i!}(1+a_i)
 \le(1+n)^L\prod_i\frac{\tau^{a_i}}{a_i!},\qquad \Theta=\tau.
\end{equation}
This also covers no-merger graphs. A partial final slab can be enlarged
to its full assigned slab before the estimate.

\subsection{Prefix flux sum, factorials, and normalization}\label{subsec:flux}
Keep $(v,y,t,\omega)$ fixed in \eqref{eq:W1} and sum over the discrete tree
of successive merger-pair choices. At a node the preceding choices and all
intervening redundant slots have already been processed. Its incoming
velocities are common to every possible next pair; no future pair determines
a past reflection. Prefix failure has weight zero. On the energy bin
$E_0\le P$, \eqref{eq:all-energy} gives for the slab-$i$ lines
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux}
 \sum_{p<q}[(v_p^{\rm cur}-v_q^{\rm cur})\cdot\omega]_+
 \le\sum_{p<q}|v_p^{\rm cur}-v_q^{\rm cur}|
 \le(n_i-1)\sum_p|v_p^{\rm cur}|
 \le n_i^{3/2}\sqrt P.
\end{equation}
A child failing an existence or strict-order test contributes zero.
Induction bounds the flux products, with $R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G$ retained,
by $\prod_i(n_i^{3/2}\sqrt P)^{a_i}$. Normal integration costs only a
fixed constant per merger. Only after this prefix bound is it legitimate
to discard $R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G\le1$ for the positive time-volume bound
\eqref{eq:time-volume}. No nonchronological velocity history is used in
the induction.

The coefficients \eqref{eq:discrete-weight} were fixed by the finite forest
exploration before the physical change of variables. Neither the shear nor
positive enlargement introduces fresh labels, another cluster partition,
or a new order permutation. H06's fresh-label count bounds all coefficients
apart from ordinary merger-pair choices by
\[
 C_K(1+n)^{\gamma_K}C^a\prod_i\frac1{n_i!}.
\]
The $O_K(1)$ redundant labels, initial-link vertices, and crossing roots
cost fixed polynomials. Marking an incident C-slot costs at most
$n+O_K(1)$ additional choices.

Integrating the initial-link cylinders contributes
\[
 C_K\eps^{2p}(1+E_0)^{p/2},
\]
because \eqref{eq:cylinder} includes both its $\eps^2\tau|u_l|$ and
$\eps^3$ terms, $p\le K$, and $|u_l|\le2\sqrt{E_0}$. In the connected case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scale}
 \mu^{m-h}\eps^{2(m-1-p)}\eps^{2p}=\mu^{1-h}.
\end{equation}
The original extra $\eps$ per physical initial link is lost, but the
connected root normalization is preserved. This also covers a connected
graph with no dynamic merger, where $p=m-1$.

\subsection{The enlarged summation closes}\label{subsec:summation}
Apply the fixed reserve \eqref{eq:polyabsorb}--\eqref{eq:reserve}.
The velocity integral of $e^{-\beta_*E_0/4}$ is $C^m$ and the energy sum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-sum}
 \sum_{P=1,2,4,\ldots}e^{-\beta_*P/4}P^{a/2}
        \le C^{a+1}(1+a)^{a/2}.
\end{equation}
This follows by comparison with a gamma integral and Stirling's inequality;
the lowest bin uses $P^{a/2}=1$. Its base depends on $\beta_*$, not on
$K$, $p$, or the moment order. The numerical base need not equal the old
unmodified one; its independence of these later parameters is what matters.

Up to fixed polynomials, the remaining size expression is H's
\begin{equation}\label{eq:before-stirling}
 C^{n+a}\tau^a(1+a)^{a/2}
                  \prod_i\frac{n_i^{3a_i/2}}{a_i!n_i!}.
\end{equation}
Put $\nu_i=n_i-a_i\ge0$; \eqref{eq:forestcounts} gives
$\sum_i\nu_i=O_K(1)$. Factorials imply
\[
 \frac{n_i^{3a_i/2}}{a_i!n_i!}
    \le e^{2n_i}(1+n_i)^{\nu_i}n_i^{-n_i/2}.
\]
Indeed replace $1/a_i!$ by $(1+n_i)^{\nu_i}/n_i!$ and use
$n_i!\ge(n_i/e)^{n_i}$, dropping further nonpositive powers.
Zero counts contribute one. Also
\[
 \tau^a\le\tau^n\max(1,\tau^{-1})^{O_K(1)},\qquad
 (1+a)^{a/2}\le C_K(1+n)^{O_K(1)}n^{n/2},
\]
and convexity of $u\log u$ gives $\prod_i n_i^{n_i}\ge(n/L)^n$.
Combining \eqref{eq:W0}--\eqref{eq:before-stirling} proves the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W2}
 \frac{|J|}{\tau}\,C_K(1+n)^{\gamma_K+L+1}
           (C\tau\sqrt L)^n\mu^{1-h}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $L$, the number of size vectors with total $n$ is polynomial in
$n$. Section~\ref{sec:budget} chose $C\tau\sqrt L=CT/\sqrt L<1$
independently of that polynomial degree. Summing \eqref{eq:W2} proves
Lemma~\ref{lem:window}. The marked contact never acquired an independent
time coordinate; its unit-speed motion came from one collective shear.

\section{Moving pulses and increment cumulants}\label{sec:increments}
Fix real $\phi\in C_c^\infty$ and write
$M_\phi=\norm\phi_\infty$ and
$D_\phi=\norm{v\cdot\nabla_x\phi}_\infty$. These are finite. For
$0\le s<t\le T$, put $\delta=t-s$ and
\[
 S_{s,t}=\sum_i[\phi(\hat z_i(t))-\phi(\hat z_i(s))].
\]
This is H's scalar record with pulses $-\phi$ at $s$, $+\phi$ at $t$,
always ending at the same full time $T$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:increments}
For every fixed integer $j\ge1$ and every $q>0$, uniformly in $s,t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulant-bound}
 |\kappa_j(S_{s,t})|\le C_j\mu\delta+C_{j,q}\eps^q.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every assigned $q>0$,
\begin{align}
 \E|\widehat X_t(\phi)-\widehat X_s(\phi)|^4
     &\le C(\delta^2+\delta/\mu)+C_q\eps^q,\label{eq:fourth}\\
 \E|S_{s,t}|&\le C\mu\delta+C_q\eps^q.\label{eq:absolute-increment}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On each actual retained history a designated line with no displayed C-atom
in $(s,t]$ flies freely there. Refining every autonomous C-cluster by its
complete ordered accepted history, as in H04, makes this statement literal.
O-atoms change no velocity. Its scalar mark therefore satisfies the positive
majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mark-bound}
 |y_\ell|\le D_\phi\delta+2M_\phi N_{(s,t]}(\ell).
\end{equation}
Only powers of the single final scalar record are used to test the actual
cumulant. The two endpoint states in this inequality are a pointwise
history argument, not an illicit two-record test of an endpoint measure.

Apply \eqref{eq:ordinary}. For $h=|\pi|\le j$, use the retained connected
expansion, its high-complexity tail, and the initial-link expansion
\eqref{eq:links}. On the physical-link part, all other record factors are
bounded by fixed powers of $2M_\phi$; one factor is bounded by
\eqref{eq:mark-bound}. The unmarked absolute mass is
$C\mu^{1-h}$ by the source summation, and the window term is
$C\mu^{1-h}\delta$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:window}. The factor $\mu^h$ in
\eqref{eq:ordinary} gives $C\mu\delta$.

We justify a single accuracy choice uniform in both pulses. Fix $j$ and the
desired power $q$. Keep exactly these two pulses and at most $j$ designated
labels to the fixed endpoint $T$. Their update maps have
$|y|\le2M_\phi$ uniformly in $s,t$. Compatibility and acceptance do not
use the mark. In absolute estimates, remove pulse-to-segment selection
restrictions; the possible placements cost only a fixed polynomial in size,
or a cutoff power in the coarse regime. The stopped kernels and crude TV
operator depend only on the test supremum. Thus the positive kernel proofs
in Section~\ref{subsec:expansion} provide one majorant for all
$(s,t)\in[0,T]^2$, not just constants for each fixed observation list.

Choose a coarse accuracy $A>q+2j+2$, then one $K$ making
\eqref{eq:coarse} at most $\eps^A$ for all $h\le j$. Choose a tail target
$q_{\rm tail}>q+2j+2$ and then $D>2K+q_{\rm tail}$. Multiplication by $\mu^h$ leaves
$O(\eps^{A-2j})$ for the coarse part and
$O(\eps^{q_{\rm tail}-2})$ for an initial tail
$O(\eps^{q_{\rm tail}}\mu^{1-h})$. The unweighted variation
$e^{-c\Lambda_L}$ beats every fixed power. These estimates use only bounded
powers of $y$, or $|y|$, with no Gaussian weight for a generic microscopic
remainder. Higher assigned powers first absorb every finite cross product
below. None of these choices changes $L$ or the dynamics.
This proves \eqref{eq:cumulant-bound}.

The centered fourth-moment identity gives
\[
 \E|\widehat X_t-\widehat X_s|^4
     =\mu^{-2}\bigl(\kappa_4(S_{s,t})+3\kappa_2(S_{s,t})^2\bigr).
\]
Insert the preceding estimates and relabel the arbitrary error power,
using $\delta\le T$, to get \eqref{eq:fourth}. For
\eqref{eq:absolute-increment}, positivity of the actual one-particle
measure $c_1=F_1$ gives
$\E|S_{s,t}|\le\E\sum_i|y_i|=\mu c_1(|y|)$.
Its same connected decomposition and \eqref{eq:mark-bound} prove the bound.
If $\phi=0$ all statements are immediate.
\end{proof}
The remainder is an arbitrary assigned power, not a qualitative cycle
remainder. This distinction is needed when passing to microscopic scales.

\section{A final-slab history flag}\label{sec:flags}
We now derive the additional finite-kernel operation needed for a microscopic
modulus. Let $(i-1)\tau\le a<b\le i\tau$ and stop the hierarchy at $b$.
For a designated particle $p$ put
\[
 r_p=\ind_{\{\text{its velocity jumps in }(a,b]\}}.
\]
This is a bounded measurable function of its final-slab history; it is not
asserted to be recoverable from its endpoint.

\begin{lemma}[Decorated output]\label{lem:decorated}
On the output space $(z_p(b),r_p)$, the factorial hierarchy of the same
pasted dynamics has the retained connected-diagram expansion and the
unweighted TV, coarse, and initial-tail decomposition of
Section~\ref{subsec:expansion}, uniformly in $a,b$ and the partial endpoint.
For each fixed $h$ and arbitrary $q>0$ its actual decorated cumulant satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-cumulant}
 \left|\mu^h c_{h,b}^{\eps,\mathrm{dec}}(r_1\cdots r_h)\right|
       \le C_h\mu(b-a)+C_{h,q}\eps^q.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:kernel} replace the final delta, for each fixed autonomous
partition, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decorated-delta}
 \delta_{((z_p^{\Psi_{\mathcal C}}(b),
                   r_p(\Psi_{\mathcal C}|_{(a,b]})))_{p\in S}}.
\end{equation}
Use the identical replacement in the positive stopped kernel. The caps give
finite accepted histories, so the flag is measurable on the source's common
full-measure history space. Apply \eqref{eq:autonomous} to bounded measurable
tests of this new output. Its finite Janossy/forest proof still cancels
spectators pointwise before integration; \eqref{eq:fresh} still leaves
$\mu^j/j!$. Thus the one-slab identity holds as an identity of measures
on the decorated space, not only after endpoint projection.

Earlier slabs carry no jump flag. Compose their ordinary unmarked kernels
with this final decorated kernel. In the final slab keep all designated
histories explicit. A final independent singleton may be denoted
$g_{i,b}^{\mathrm{dec}}$; it is the decorated one-root output on independent
ordinary lower inputs. It is not the ordinary unmarked $g_i$ followed by an
attempt to recover its flag. Lower one-root factors can retain their
canonical ordinary resummations.

For coarse bookkeeping, the finite one-root definitions can be opened along
the at most $h$ designated physical lines down to zero, with a constant
record if needed. This is the source's record-preserving finite regrouping
and projection operation. It duplicates neither an input nor an interface
birth. Any needed bottom continuation can be supplied with at most $h$
source dummy straight-root links at fixed cost $\eps^{-h/81}$, together
with the allowed top-root deficit $\eps^{-C_*h}$. Their fixed powers and
complexity are absorbed by the subsequent $K$; they are removed before the
low-complexity physical-link estimate. The flag remains a final-output
function. No record or line is introduced per collision.

Decorated outputs factor over disjoint global components because each root
flag depends only on its own component history. Expand lower independent
sums finitely for this algebra. Assigning fresh labels to components yields
the same multinomial identity as in H04, Lemma
\texttt{lem:diagram-labels}. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decorated-partition}
 \widehat F_{b,S}^{\mathrm{dec}}
       =\sum_{\pi\in\calP(S)}\bigotimes_{V\in\pi}D_{b,V}^{\mathrm{dec}}.
\end{equation}
Finite inversion keeps exactly the connected decorated diagrams. An
independent decorated singleton contributes only to order one; it cannot
create a connection at order two or higher. Its order-one history integrals
are final-slab diagrams with Gaussian Bol-bounded ordinary births, to which
the same window estimate applies.

The variation argument must take place before the new pushforward.
At the level of each positive history kernel, \eqref{eq:decorated-delta}
preserves its mass. We do not lift a TV estimate of an unmarked endpoint
measure to its histories. Rather, for any bounded decorated test $H_0$,
\[
 |R_s^{\mathrm{dec}}(H_0)|
       \le\norm{H_0}_\infty(\mathcal E_{i,s}F_{i-1})(1).
\]
In the recursion
$\Delta_i^{\mathrm{dec}}=R_i^{\mathrm{dec}}+
\mathcal L_i^{\mathrm{dec}}\Delta_{i-1}$, the lower error is the ordinary
unmarked one. The decorated kernel's crude TV operator bound is unchanged:
its positive partition/forest majorant sees only the supremum of $H_0$.
The source stopped-variation induction and its partial-final-slab bounds
therefore apply. The fixed bookkeeping losses above have the allowed form
already absorbed in that induction.

The actual normalized decorated factorial mass is
$\mu^{-|S|}\E(N)_{|S|}\le1$, and restricting by flags only lowers it.
The retained decorated measure has variation at most
$1+e^{-c\Lambda_i}$ at fixed orders. In the difference of the actual and
retained finite partition polynomials every term has a variation-error
factor; the other factors have these bounded masses. Thus the cumulant
variation error is still $C_he^{-c\Lambda_i}$.

For the high-complexity tail, the positive coarse integrand depends on
$a,b$ only through a multiplier at most one and a shorter final time domain.
There are only $L$ final slab indices. The same fixed root losses and
\eqref{eq:coarse} give one $K$ for every $a,b$ at a fixed assigned accuracy.
Choose the initial-tail sharpness afterwards, as in
Section~\ref{sec:increments}. All of these are unweighted TV statements.

Finally refine each autonomous C-cluster by its complete ordered accepted
C history, as in H04's one-root bridge and stopped-variation proof. The
root velocity changes only at displayed C-atoms, so on each physical retained
history
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-count}
 r_p\le N_{(a,b]}(p).
\end{equation}
Bound all other flags by one and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:window}, after removing
artificial coarse-estimate links. The normalization $\mu^h$ gives
$C\mu(b-a)$. Choose the unweighted error powers before that multiplication
to obtain the actual remainder $C_{h,q}\eps^q$ in
\eqref{eq:flag-cumulant}.
\end{proof}
Restricting the decorated output to $r=1$ commutes with tensor products in
\eqref{eq:decorated-partition}. It is thus the ordinary factorial hierarchy
of the point process retaining just the jumping particles. The lemma is a
separate consequence of the finite history kernel, not an application of
the additive-pulse theorem to a nonadditive endpoint mark.

\section{A microscopic modulus}\label{sec:micro}
For a real path $Y$ write
$w(Y,u)=\sup_{|t-s|\le u}|Y_t-Y_s|$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \alpha=\mu^{-3/2}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:micro}
For every real compact smooth $\phi$,
$w(\widehat X^\eps(\phi),2\alpha)\to0$ in probability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $I_k=(k\alpha,(k+3)\alpha]\cap(0,T]$, for
$0\le k\le\lceil T/\alpha\rceil$. Any pair $s<t$ with $t-s\le2\alpha$
lies in the closure of $I_k$ for $k=\lfloor s/\alpha\rfloor$.
For small $\eps$, $3\alpha<\tau$; split $I_k$ at its possible one interface
into at most two pieces $I_k^\iota$ inside individual slabs. Define
\[
 \Count(I)=\#\{p:\hat v_p\text{ jumps in }I\}=\sum_p r_p.
\]
A particle with no jump in $(k\alpha,t]$ flies freely; its test change
between $s,t$ is at most $3\alpha D_\phi$. Otherwise use $2M_\phi$.
Pathwise,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:micro-path}
 \sup_{s,t\in\overline{I_k}}\left|\sum_p
          [\phi(\hat z_p(t))-\phi(\hat z_p(s))]\right|
   \le3\alpha D_\phi N+2M_\phi\sum_\iota\Count(I_k^\iota).
\end{equation}

For root cardinality $h=1,2,3$, the factorial cumulants of this restricted point process are
those in Lemma~\ref{lem:decorated}. For $|I|\le3\alpha$ they obey the
\emph{same actual error convention}
\[
 |\mu^h c_{h}^{\eps,\mathrm{dec}}(r^{\otimes h})|
       \le C_h\mu\alpha+C_{h,q}\eps^q.
\]
Here $h$ denotes block cardinality, $d$ remains the test-vector dimension,
and $b$ the slab endpoint; no $\mu^h$ is inserted again
in this already-normalized remainder. The exact factorial moment identity
and the diagonal identity are
\begin{align}
 \E(\Count(I))_j
  &=\sum_{\pi\in\calP([j])}\prod_{V\in\pi}
             \mu^{|V|}c_{|V|}^{\eps,\mathrm{dec}}(r^{\otimes|V|}),
                      \quad j=1,2,3,\label{eq:count-partition}\\
 \Count(I)^3&=(\Count(I))_3+3(\Count(I))_2+\Count(I).
       \label{eq:count-diagonals}
\end{align}
Since $x=\mu\alpha=\mu^{-1/2}\le1$ and $\eps^q\le1$, every finite product
is bounded by $C_j(x+\eps^q)$, increasing the assigned source accuracy first
if necessary. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-third}
 \E\Count(I)^3\le C(\mu\alpha+\eps^q).
\end{equation}
All falling-factorial diagonal terms have been kept.

Markov's inequality and a union over $O(T/\alpha)$ pieces, with threshold
$\eta\sqrt\mu$, give
\begin{align}
 \Prob\!\left(\max_{k,\iota}\Count(I_k^\iota)>\eta\sqrt\mu\right)
 &\le\frac{C(T/\alpha)(\mu\alpha+\eps^q)}{\eta^3\mu^{3/2}}
 \le C\eta^{-3}(\mu^{-1/2}+\eps^q)\notag\\
 &\le C\eta^{-3}(\mu^{-1/2}+\mu^3\eps^q)\longrightarrow0,
               \qquad q>6.\label{eq:micro-union}
\end{align}
The last, weaker form uses a common error choice convenient for chaining;
the first form aligns directly with the actual error in
\eqref{eq:flag-cumulant}. Since $\mu^3\eps^q=\eps^{q-6}$, $q>6$ suffices.
A second-moment union based on its leading term would give only
$(T/\alpha)(\mu\alpha)/(\eta^2\mu)=T/\eta^2$, which does not vanish.

The free-flight term in \eqref{eq:micro-path}, after division by
$\sqrt\mu$, is
$3D_\phi\mu^{-1}(N/\mu)\to0$ in probability by \eqref{eq:N}.
For exact centering, \eqref{eq:absolute-increment} gives uniformly for
$|t-s|\le2\alpha$
\[
 \mu^{-1/2}|\E S_{s,t}|\le C\mu^{1/2}\alpha+C_q\eps^q
                   =C\mu^{-1}+o(1).
\]
All intervals are half-open $(a,b]$. The phase paths are right-continuous;
a jump at the left endpoint is already reflected in its endpoint state,
and a right-endpoint jump belongs to the count. Pasting creates no interface
jump. Thus these conventions cover grid endpoints and interface splits.
The three bounds prove the proposition.
\end{proof}

\section{Chaining, continuity, and exact-centering transfer}\label{sec:finish}
\begin{lemma}[Chaining to the microscopic scale]\label{lem:chaining}
Let $Y^\eps$ be real cadlag processes on $[0,T]$, with $Y_0^\eps$ tight,
and suppose
\[
 \E|Y_t^\eps-Y_s^\eps|^4
  \le C(|t-s|^2+|t-s|/\mu)+R_\eps,
 \qquad R_\eps\alpha^{-2}\to0.
\]
If $w(Y^\eps,2\alpha)\to0$ in probability for \eqref{eq:alpha}, then
$Y^\eps$ is C-tight in the $J_1$ Skorokhod space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the chaining exponent $\vartheta=1/8$, distinct from the shift
parameter $\theta$. Let $t_{k,j}=jT2^{-k}$ and let $k_\eps$ be the largest
$k$ with $T2^{-k}\ge\alpha$. The finest spacing lies in $[\alpha,2\alpha)$.
For $Z_k=\max_j|Y_{t_{k,j+1}}-Y_{t_{k,j}}|$ the union bound gives
\[
 \Prob(Z_k>2^{-k\vartheta})\le
    2^k\{C(T^22^{-2k}+T2^{-k}/\mu)+R_\eps\}2^{4k\vartheta}.
\]
Summing from $k_0$ to $k_\eps$ gives three terms bounded respectively by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic}
 C\sum_{k\ge k_0}2^{-k/2},\qquad
 C\mu^{-1}(T/\alpha)^{1/2}=C\mu^{-1/4},\qquad
 CR_\eps(T/\alpha)^{3/2}\le CR_\eps\alpha^{-2}.
\end{equation}
On the complementary event, successive dyadic approximations give for
nearby finest-grid points a bound
$Z_{k_0}+2\sum_{k>k_0}Z_k\le C_\vartheta2^{-k_0\vartheta}$.
For arbitrary $s,t$ with $|t-s|\le T2^{-k_0-1}$ pass to adjacent
finest-grid points; each endpoint error is bounded by $w(Y,2\alpha)$.
For sufficiently fine terminal grids the possible fixed-level edge
adjustments are absorbed in $C_\vartheta$. Hence
\[
 w(Y,T2^{-k_0-1})\le
       C_\vartheta2^{-k_0\vartheta}+2w(Y,2\alpha)
\]
outside the exceptional probabilities in \eqref{eq:dyadic}. First let
$\eps\to0$ and then $k_0\to\infty$. Thus, for each $\eta>0$,
\[
 \lim_{u\downarrow0}\limsup_{\eps\to0}
       \Prob\bigl(w(Y^\eps,u)>\eta\bigr)=0.
\]
To verify also the supremum-tightness hypothesis of
\cite[Proposition VI.3.26]{JS}, partition $[0,t]$ into intervals of length
at most a fixed $u>0$. Deterministically,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}|Y_t^\eps|
 \le |Y_0^\eps|+\lceil T/u\rceil\,w(Y^\eps,u).
\]
Given a probability tolerance, the double limit first supplies a fixed
$u$ for which $w(Y^\eps,u)\le1$ outside a set of that tolerance in the
$\limsup_{\eps\to0}$. Tight initial values then supply a finite bound for
$|Y_0^\eps|$ at the same tolerance. With this $u$ fixed, the displayed
inequality proves asymptotic tightness of the suprema. Together with the
continuous-modulus limit, this gives C-tightness by the cited criterion. This is standard tightness machinery;
small individual jumps alone would not prove it.
\end{proof}

For clarity we reproduce the Gaussian continuity and uniform-centering
deductions already established in antecedent notes to this work. They are
not new claims here; the accompanying provenance identifies the archived
notes and their precise sections.
H02 constructs the target on
\[
 E_a^0=\{h\in C(\R^6):e^{-a|v|^2}h\in C_0(\R^6)\},
 \qquad \norm h_a=\sup e^{-a|v|^2}|h|,
\]
with bounded maps to $L^2(\Omega)$ for $a<\beta$, uniformly in time.
For compact smooth $\phi$, $r\mapsto A_r\phi$ is continuous and bounded
in $E_a^0$ for $a>0$. The source weak identity and field/source bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-gaussian}
 \zeta_t(\phi)=\zeta_0(\phi)+\int_0^t\zeta_r(A_r\phi)\dd r+W_t(\phi),
 \qquad
 \E\int_0^T|\zeta_r(A_r\phi)|^2\dd r<\infty.
\end{equation}
The integrand is strongly measurable in $L^2$ by the source propagator
construction. Its indefinite integral has continuous paths by
Cauchy--Schwarz. The Gaussian noise has
$\E|W_t(\phi)-W_s(\phi)|^4\le C|t-s|^2$, so Kolmogorov yields a continuous
modification. Equation \eqref{eq:weak-gaussian} gives the continuous version
of each $\zeta(\phi)$, with all joint covariances unchanged. A finite
intersection of probability-one events handles a finite list.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For each coordinate, \eqref{eq:fourth} has
$R_\eps=C_q\eps^q$ with arbitrary $q>6$, so
$R_\eps\alpha^{-2}=C_q\eps^{q-6}\to0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:micro} and Lemma~\ref{lem:chaining} give C-tightness
of the pasted coordinate. Its initial value has the same initial ensemble
as the true flow and is tight by H's initial finite-dimensional theorem.
For a fixed vector, its continuous modulus is bounded by the sum of the
coordinate moduli, and its initial vector is tight. It is therefore C-tight.

Let $S_t=\sum_i\phi(z_i(t))$ and
$\widehat S_t=\sum_i\phi(\hat z_i(t))$. On the coupling
\eqref{eq:coupling}, simultaneously in all times,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}|S_t-\widehat S_t|
       \le2M_\phi N\ind_{E_\eps}.
\]
H\"older's inequality with the fixed $22$nd moment of $N/\mu$ gives
\begin{align}
 \sup_{t\le T}\frac{|\E S_t-\E\widehat S_t|}{\sqrt\mu}
 &\le C\eps^{-1}\Prob(E_\eps)^{21/22}\notag\\
 &\le C\eps^{-1+(21/20)(21/22)}
       =C\eps^{1/440}\longrightarrow0.
       \label{eq:transfer}
\end{align}
The coupling exponent is $\eta_0=1/20$; it is distinct from the arbitrary
probability threshold $\eta$ in \eqref{eq:micro-union}. Off $E_\eps$ the
uncentered paths coincide at every time and $\Prob(E_\eps)\to0$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sup-transfer}
 \norm{X^\eps(\phi)-\widehat X^\eps(\phi)}_\infty\to0
                 \quad\text{in probability}.
\end{equation}
This works for a fixed finite vector. The identity time change bounds its
$J_1$ distance by the supremum distance. The modulus inequality transfers
C-tightness. No fourth-moment bound for the true flow restricted to the
exceptional event is asserted or required.

For each fixed $\eps$, particle number is finite almost surely and
$\E N<\infty$. Bounded-test sums are cadlag; their expectations are cadlag
by dominated convergence using $M_\phi N$. The centered vector is therefore
a random element of the usual $J_1$ space, whose Borel sigma-field is
generated by coordinate evaluations. Every subsequential limit of the
C-tight family is supported on continuous paths. Evaluation at any time
is continuous there, so H's joint finite-dimensional convergence identifies
its finite-dimensional laws with those of \eqref{eq:covariance}.
Finite-dimensional laws determine the law on $C([0,T],\R^d)$, giving a
unique subsequential law and convergence of the whole sequence.

On a Skorokhod representation of a $J_1$-convergent subsequence with
continuous limit, paths converge uniformly almost surely. This is a
consequence on that representation, not weak convergence in the
nonseparable uniform-topology Borel space on all cadlag paths.
\end{proof}

\begin{abstract}
We prove a path-space upgrade of the finite-dimensional hard-sphere
fluctuation theorem on an entire supplied regular Boltzmann interval.
The setting is the deterministic, nonequilibrium, grand-canonical gas in
three-dimensional whole space, with exact microscopic centering and no time
smoothing. Relative to the pinned finite-history interfaces, one collective
time translation of a connected history gives a shrinking-window factor at
its sharp factorial-cumulant scale. This also localizes redundant contacts,
whose times are determined rather than independent coordinates. A
prefix-measurable global chronology indicator is retained through the
translation and the flux-tree estimate. Initial-link cylinders preserve the
connected activity scale, and a fixed Gaussian reserve keeps summability
independent of the later moment order. A decorated final-slab output kernel
controls the number of jumping particles. Third moments at scale
$\mu^{-3/2}$, dyadic chaining, and a uniform exact-centering comparison yield
$J_1$ convergence and C-tightness for every finite vector of compact smooth
tests, with the Gaussian covariance of the credited finite-dimensional theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The gas, the target, and the result}\label{sec:statement}
We work on $\R_x^3\times\R_v^3$, with $z=(x,v)$. All surface integrals on
$S^2$ use ordinary, unnormalized area measure. For $\beta>0$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bolnorm}
 \norm g_{\Bol,\beta}
 =\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z^3}
     \sup_{\substack{|x-a|\le1\\v\in\R^3}}
           e^{\beta|v|^2}|g(x,v)|.
\end{equation}
For relative velocity $w=v-v_*$ and $\omega\in S^2$ use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision}
 B(w,\omega)=[w\cdot\omega]_+,\qquad
 v'=v-(w\cdot\omega)\omega,\qquad
 v_*'=v_*+(w\cdot\omega)\omega.
\end{equation}
The Boltzmann collision operator is
\[
 Q(f,f)(x,v)=\int_{\R^3\times S^2}
       B(v-v_*,\omega)(f'f_*'-ff_*)\dd v_*\dd\omega,
\]
with all four factors evaluated at the same position $x$.

\begin{assumption}[The kinetic hypothesis]\label{ass:kinetic}
The function $f_0\ge0$ is a smooth probability density and, for some
$\beta>0$,
\[
 \norm{f_0}_{\Bol,2\beta}+\norm{\nabla_xf_0}_{\Bol,2\beta}<\infty.
\]
There is a nonnegative classical solution of
$(\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla_x)f=Q(f,f)$, with initial datum $f_0$, on a
prescribed finite interval $[0,T]$, $T>0$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular}
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}\sup_{x,v}e^{2\beta|v|^2}f_t(x,v)<\infty.
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
This is exactly Assumption \texttt{ass:kinetic} of the pinned source
\cite{H}. No smallness or global existence assumption is added.

For sphere diameter $\eps>0$ set $\mu=\eps^{-2}$ and
\[
 \mathcal D_N^\eps=\{Z_N:|x_i-x_j|>\eps\text{ for }i\ne j\}.
\]
The initial law is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
 \Prob_\eps(N,dZ_N)=\mathcal Z_\eps^{-1}\frac{\mu^N}{N!}
       \ind_{\mathcal D_N^\eps}(Z_N)\prod_{i=1}^Nf_0(z_i)\dd Z_N,
       \qquad N=0,1,2,\ldots.
\end{equation}
The empty configuration is included. From the sampled initial configuration,
the particles move
by deterministic free flight and elastic hard-sphere collisions. We use the
almost-everywhere flow and right-continuous velocity convention of \cite{H}.
There is no random collision rule or physical resampling.

For real $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\R^6)$ define the unsmoothed fluctuation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field}
 X_t^\eps(\phi)=\mu^{-1/2}
    \left(\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(z_i(t))
                 -\E\sum_{i=1}^N\phi(z_i(t))\right).
\end{equation}
The centering is the exact microscopic expectation at each time. It is not
replaced by $\int f_t\phi$.

Write
\[
 \Delta\phi=\phi(x,v')+\phi(x,v_*')-\phi(x,v)-\phi(x,v_*).
\]
The Gaussian target has weak drift and noise form
\begin{align}
 A_t\phi&=v\cdot\nabla_x\phi+
    \int B(v-v_*,\omega)f_t(x,v_*)\Delta\phi\dd v_*\dd\omega,
       \label{eq:drift}\\
 C_t(\phi,\psi)&=\frac12\int B(v-v_*,\omega)f_t(x,v)f_t(x,v_*)
       \Delta\phi\Delta\psi\dd x\dd v\dd v_*\dd\omega.
       \label{eq:noise}
\end{align}
Let $U(r,t)$ be the backward propagator of $A_t$ constructed in \cite[Section
2, \texttt{lem:target-propagator}]{H}. The target is the centered jointly
Gaussian field with covariance
\begin{align}
 K_{\phi,\psi}(t,s)
 ={}&\int f_0(z)U(0,t)\phi(z)U(0,s)\psi(z)\dd z\notag\\
 &+\int_0^{t\wedge s}C_r\bigl(U(r,t)\phi,U(r,s)\psi\bigr)\dd r.
       \label{eq:covariance}
\end{align}
Its initial covariance is $\int f_0\phi\psi$, with no fixed-number
subtraction. Equivalently it solves
$d\zeta_t(\phi)=\zeta_t(A_t\phi)dt+dW_t(\phi)$ with the independent initial
field and Gaussian noise prescribed by \eqref{eq:noise}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:kinetic}, relative to the source interfaces stated
in Section~\ref{sec:inputs}, for every finite list of real
$\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_d\in C_c^\infty(\R^6)$,
\[
 (X^\eps_\cdot(\phi_1),\ldots,X^\eps_\cdot(\phi_d))
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 (\zeta_\cdot(\phi_1),\ldots,\zeta_\cdot(\phi_d))
\]
in $D([0,T],\R^d)$ with the Skorokhod $J_1$ topology. The sequence is
C-tight, and the target is the continuous version of the Gaussian field
with covariance \eqref{eq:covariance}.
\end{theorem}
The interval is the whole supplied $[0,T]$. The theorem concerns the actual
gas \eqref{eq:law}, not an independently restarted gas or an integrated
observable. We make no weak-convergence assertion for the uniform-topology
Borel space on all of $D$.

Bodineau--Gallagher--Saint-Raymond--Simonella proved short-time
nonequilibrium process convergence for a periodic gas \cite{BGSSshort},
and long-time equilibrium process convergence by an invariant-measure
argument \cite{BGSSlong}. Deng--Hani--Ma supply the regular-lifespan
framework \cite{DHM}; their Section 1.4.3 identifies fluctuations as a
further direction. The pinned source \cite{H} gives the finite-dimensional
law on the regular lifespan and explicitly leaves path-space tightness
outside its conclusion. The new operation here is the collective
shrinking-window estimate in Section~\ref{sec:window}. The moment,
continuity, chaining, and centering arguments are credited separately.
These comparisons do not assert worldwide priority.

\subsection{One auxiliary dynamics and factorial normalization}

with harmless integer rounding. Within each slab an incoming collision is

single auxiliary pasted dynamics; the target physical flow is not reset.
It preserves $N$ and has finitely many accepted collisions. We put hats on
its fields and center them by their own exact expectations.

If $F_{h,t}^\eps$ is its ordered factorial measure normalized by $\mu^h$,
then, for a nonnegative measurable test $H_0$,
\[
 F_{h,t}^\eps(H_0)=\mu^{-h}\E
       \sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_h\text{ distinct}}H_0(z_{i_1}(t),\ldots,z_{i_h}(t)).
\]
The same definition allows specified records on the designated labels.
Normalized factorial cumulants satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
 F_{h,t}^\eps=\sum_{\pi\in\calP([h])}
                   \bigotimes_{V\in\pi}c_{|V|,t}^\eps.
\end{equation}
H02, Proposition \texttt{prop:initial}, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:N}
 F_{h,0}^\eps\le f_0^{\otimes h},\qquad
 \E(N)_h\le\mu^h,\qquad
 \sup_{\eps\le\eps_0}\E(N/\mu)^p<\infty
       \quad\text{for every fixed integer }p.
\end{equation}
The factorial-mass bound persists under the number-preserving dynamics.

A scalar record is initially zero and is unchanged by collisions. At pulses it has updates
$y\mapsto y+\phi_j(z)$ at fixed pulses. Its sum records the corresponding
multitime empirical sum. H07, \texttt{eq:limit-factorial-ordinary}, gives
for $S=\sum_i y_i(T)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinary}
 \kappa_j(S)=\sum_{\pi\in\calP([j])}\mu^{|\pi|}
     \int c_{|\pi|,T}^\eps(d\xi)
                    \prod_{V\in\pi}y_V^{|V|}.
\end{equation}
The blocks $V$ are associated with distinct particle slots in a fixed order.
This is a finite formal identity through order $j$: expand
$\prod_i[1+(e^{u y_i}-1)]$ and take its logarithm. Each factor receives a
nonempty set of differentiations, giving the displayed partitions. No
uniform radius of exponential integrability is needed.

\subsection{The exact one-slab root-output kernel}\label{subsec:kernels}
Here we record the finite identity used later for a nonadditive flag. For a
finite particle set $V_0$ let $\Psi_C$ be the autonomous capped history of
$C\subset V_0$, and let $\kappa_C$ indicate that its accepted collision graph
is connected. Singletons have $\kappa_C=1$. For disjoint autonomous clusters
let $a(C,D)$ indicate the existence of an incoming cross-contact that would
be accepted with their prefix prehistories, and set $\chi(C,D)=1-a(C,D)$.
H04, Lemma \texttt{lem:autonomous-partition}, states, pointwise on its common
full-measure set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:autonomous}
 H_0(\Psi_{V_0}|_S)=\sum_{\mathcal C\in\calP(V_0)}
    \prod_{C\in\mathcal C}\kappa_C
    \prod_{\{C,D\}\subset\mathcal C}\chi(C,D)
                      H_0(\Psi_{\mathcal C}|_S)
\end{equation}
for every bounded measurable test of the root histories. The selected
juxtaposition is the actual capped history; no independence of input is
assumed.

For a cluster block $V_c$ write
$u(V_c)=\sum_{G\text{ connected on }V_c}\prod_{e\in G}(-a_e)$ and
$u(\{C\})=1$. The rooted forest exploration stops when a rooted cluster
component reaches $M_i=\lfloor\Lambda_i/2\rfloor$. Its retained root-output
kernel, for roots $S=[s]$ and reached labels $V_0=[s+j]$, is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal K_{S,V_0}(Z,dZ'_S)
 =\sum_{\mathcal C\in\calP(V_0)}\prod_{C\in\mathcal C}\kappa_C
 \sum_{\substack{\mathcal B\in\calP(\mathcal C)\\
       (\cup_{C\in V_c}C)\cap S\ne\varnothing,\ |V_c|<M_i}}
 \prod_{V_c\in\mathcal B}u(V_c)
                   \delta_{\Psi_{\mathcal C}(Z)|_S}(dZ'_S).
       \label{eq:kernel}
\end{align}
The inner restrictions hold for every block. The corresponding operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
 \mathcal L_{i,s}F=\sum_j\frac{\mu^j}{j!}
                        \int F_{s+j}(dZ)\mathcal K_{S,V_0}(Z,\cdot).
\end{equation}
The sum is finite under the caps. H04's one-slab identity is
$F'_{s}=\mathcal L_{i,s}F+R_s$, with
$|R_s(H_0)|\le(\mathcal E_{i,s}F)(|H_0|)$, where $\mathcal E$ is a positive
stopped-forest kernel with the same output map. Each forest exploration
branch has multiplicity at most one. Spectator clusters retain mutual
compatibility and cancel by \eqref{eq:autonomous} before integration. The
fresh-label identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fresh}
 \frac1{(n-s)!}\binom{n-s}{j}=\frac1{j!(n-s-j)!}.
\end{equation}
It yields exactly the coefficient in \eqref{eq:operator}. This kernel
identity, including the output before pushforward, is an imported interface.

\subsection{Resummed diagrams and errors}\label{subsec:expansion}
H04 defines ordinary independent one-root inputs $g_i$ by the entire retained
one-root map on independent inputs, and proves their Gaussian Bol envelopes
and convergence to $f_{i\tau}$, including partial slabs. Required record
lines continue through all interfaces to zero. Other one-root blocks
attached through only one line can be resummed into the ordinary $g_i$.
This is an exact finite regrouping, not a factorization of the true interface
law. Forgetting a record commutes with the finite hierarchy and the
regrouping.

The retained hierarchy factors over its global components. Finite inversion
of \eqref{eq:partition} therefore keeps exactly the globally connected
diagrams; initial cumulant blocks count as connections, artificial dummy
links do not. H04, Theorem \texttt{thm:marked-expansion}, supplies for fixed
root order $h$ and fixed record list:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an unweighted total-variation error at most $C_h e^{-c\Lambda_L}$
 between the actual cumulant and the retained connected sum;
\item for every assigned power $q>0$, an auxiliary $K$ after that order and
 list such that the retained complexity tail $\rho>K$ has absolute variation
 at most $\eps^q$;
\item the original fresh-label coefficients, with denominator
 $\prod_i j_i!$, and at bounded complexity
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:factorials}
 n_i-j_i=O_{K,h,L}(1),\qquad
 \prod_i\frac1{j_i!}\le
     \frac{(1+\sum_i n_i)^{C_{K,h,L}}}{\prod_i n_i!}.
 \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
All constants in an exponential base per ordinary atom are independent of
$K$ and of the subsequently fixed moment order. Distinguished choices cost
fixed polynomials; in the coarse regime they cost cutoff powers. These
claims follow from the actual finite kernels and positive stopped bounds,
not from a postulated uniform limit theorem.

The coarse tail has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarse}
 C_{h,r}\eps^{-C_*h-C_r+c_*K/2},
\end{equation}
where $r$ is a fixed bound on records. H03's allowed boundary modifications
include a top-root deficit $\eps^{-C_*h}$ and at most
$\eps^{-q_0/81}$ for $q_0$ specified lines requiring dummy initial
continuations. The auxiliary dummy straight roots have unit-mass Gaussian
Bol inputs; their activities and root normalizations cancel. They are used
only for coarse domination and removed before the bounded-complexity
physical-link analysis. Their number is fixed by the root/record list,
not by the number of collisions. All stopped bounds are uniform on a
partial final slab and assert no Gaussian weight for a general TV error.

\subsection{Physical charts and sharp summation}\label{subsec:charts}
A C-atom scatters a pair elastically; an O-atom records an incoming encounter
with straight continuation. On replacing bounded-order initial cumulants
by H06's forest domination, each initial edge has weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:links}
 \ind_{|x_p(0)-x_q(0)|\le R_{K,D}\eps}+\eps^D.
\end{equation}
There are at most $K$ such edges. They join lines born at zero.
First retain only their physical indicator terms. For a graph with $m$
distinct birth lines, $c$ global components, $p$ physical initial edges,
$a_i$ dynamic mergers and $n_i$ lines in slab $i$, H06 gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forestcounts}
 a:=\sum_i a_i=m-c-p,\qquad
 \sum_i(n_i-a_i)=O_K(1),\qquad 0\le a_i\le n_i.
\end{equation}
The number of redundant contacts is $O_K(1)$. A continued physical line
is one vertex and has no new birth variable at an interface.

H06, Lemma \texttt{lem:sharp-merger-coordinates}, is a change of variables
on each prescribed physical incoming straight-segment branch. Its coordinates
are independent birth velocities, one common translation per global
component, the relative affine origins of physical initial forest edges,
and $(t_g,\omega_g)$ at every dynamic merger. Its merger Jacobian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
 \eps^2[(v-v_*)\cdot\omega_g]_+dt_g\dd\omega_g.
\end{equation}
A redundant contact has no free parameter: for a relative straight flight
$r(t)=d+tu$ on the common segment interval $I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:redundant}
 \int_I\delta(|r(t)|-\eps)[-u\cdot r(t)/\eps]_+dt
   =\ind_{\{\text{an entering intersection occurs in }I\}}.
\end{equation}
There is at most one entering root on the whole relative flight. Merger
coordinates have multiplicity one on the specified physical branch. Their
activity is $\mu^{m-h}$; the initial forest solve has determinant one.

The source fresh-label and energy-bin summation gives, at fixed complexity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-summability}
 C_K(1+n)^{d_K}(C\tau\sqrt L)^n\mu^{1-h},\qquad n=\sum_i n_i,
\end{equation}
for connected absolute diagram measures. The original physical link volume
$O(\eps^3)$ supplies an extra $\eps$ per initial link. Erasing a tail edge
in \eqref{eq:links} increases the component count by at most one; erasing
at most $K$ costs at most $\eps^{-2K}$. Thus $D>2K+M$ makes all terms with
an initial tail $O(\eps^M\mu^{1-h})$. We will reproduce the summation steps
needed after the new enlargement, rather than assume
\eqref{eq:source-summability} continues to hold there.

\subsection{The Gaussian field, finite-dimensional limit, and coupling}
H02 gives the target covariance \eqref{eq:covariance}, its weak identity on
tests with small Gaussian growth, and the bounded field maps described in
Section~\ref{sec:finish}. H01, Theorem \texttt{thm:main}, gives joint
finite-dimensional convergence for every fixed finite time/test list.
H05, Proposition \texttt{prop:cutoff}, gives, for every sufficiently large
fixed $L$, a coupling with the same initial configuration such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupling}
 \Prob(E_\eps)\le C\eps^{1+\eta_0},\qquad \eta_0=1/20,
\end{equation}
where $E_\eps$ is any true/pasted discrepancy at any time in $[0,T]$.
This is a simultaneous path comparison, not a moment estimate on the
exceptional event. These interfaces, \eqref{eq:N}, and the history package
above are the imported results used in the proof.

\section{A fixed Gaussian reserve and one choice of slabs}\label{sec:budget}
The order of choices is essential. Let $\beta_*=\beta/4$, before any moment
order or complexity threshold. H03's exponents satisfy
$\beta_i\ge9\beta/20>\beta_*$. For each fixed candidate $L$ and
$\eps\le\eps_0(L)$, H04's one-root estimate gives
\[
 \norm{g_i-f_{i\tau}}_{\Bol,\beta_i}\le\eps^{\theta_i}\le1.
\]
H03, Lemma \texttt{lem:kinetic-envelopes}, bounds
$\sup_{t\le T}\norm{f_t}_{\Bol,\beta}$ in terms of the kinetic data.
Consequently there is a constant $B_0$ depending only on those data, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-envelope}
 \sup_i\norm{g_i}_{\Bol,\beta_*}\le B_0
       \quad (\eps\le\eps_0(L)).
\end{equation}
The smallness threshold may depend on $L$; the envelope constant does not.
This prevents a circular choice of $L$ from an $L$-dependent atom base.

For birth velocities $v_1,\ldots,v_m$ put $E_0=\sum_p|v_p|^2$. Integrating
one spatial translation by a spatial $L^1$ norm and the remaining inputs
by their spatial suprema gives $C^m e^{-\beta_*E_0}$.
Reserve a fraction now, independently of later $K$: any fixed-degree
polynomial satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polyabsorb}
 (1+E_0)^{p/2}\le C_{p,\beta_*}e^{\beta_*E_0/4}.
\end{equation}
The remaining $e^{-3\beta_*E_0/4}$ supplies both a velocity-volume integral
and a dyadic suppression. For $E_0\in[P/2,P]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reserve}
 e^{-3\beta_*E_0/4}
       \le e^{-\beta_*E_0/4}e^{-\beta_*P/4}.
\end{equation}
The lowest bin $E_0<1$ is treated separately.

All per-birth, normal, energy-bin, and ordinary-atom constants determined by
this reserve are fixed independently of $K$ and the order. Choose $L$ once
large enough for the source coarse/cutoff thresholds at these exponents
and for $CT/\sqrt L<1$. Only after this choice specify an observation list,
a moment order, an accuracy power, and $K,D$. H permits every sufficiently
large fixed $L$ and the entire supplied $T$ is retained. No later increase
of $K$ or of a fixed moment order changes the auxiliary dynamics.

\section{The collective contact-window estimate}\label{sec:window}
For a retained connected diagram and a designated line $\ell$, let
$N_J(\ell)$ be the number of its C-atoms incident to $\ell$ with time in a
bounded interval $J$. We use absolute birth inputs and retain the prescribed
history domains. Bounded record multipliers can be replaced by their
suprema.

\begin{lemma}[Contact windows]\label{lem:window}
For fixed root order $h$, fixed complexity bound $K$, fixed initial sharpness
$D$, and a fixed bounded record list, the physical-link connected diagrams
$\mathfrak D_K$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 \sum_{\mathsf D\in\mathfrak D_K}\int N_J(\ell)\dd|\mathsf D|
        \le C\mu^{1-h}|J|.
\end{equation}
The constant is uniform in sufficiently small $\eps$, in $J$, and in all
record or pulse positions. The counted C-atom may be a dynamic merger or a
redundant contact.
\end{lemma}
We prove the lemma in several steps. No quantitative disappearance of cycles
is used.

\subsection{Take the physical chart before enlarging it}\label{subsec:physical}
Fix a graph $G$, its C/O choices, slab assignments, prescribed global event
order, physical initial forest, and merger/redundant classification. Choose
one marked C-slot $a_*$ incident to $\ell$. The global order is the ordinary
chronological refinement in the source enumeration; it adds no independent
permutation factor. In the connected case the physical coordinates are
\[
 \xi=(v,X,y,(t_g,\omega_g)_g),\qquad
 d\xi=dv\dd X\dd y\dd t\dd\omega,
\]
with $a=m-1-p$ dynamic mergers. Let $j_i$ be the fresh labels in slab $i$.
To display all discrete weights, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-weight}
 \mathfrak a_G=\mathfrak b_G\prod_i\frac1{j_i!},
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak b_G$ contains the fixed graph's forest-domination and
other discrete coefficients. They will be summed with H's factorial count;
none is hidden in a constant that discards the denominator. The physical
change of variables gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W0}
 \int\ind_{t_{a_*}\in J}\dd|\mathsf D_G|
 \le\mathfrak a_G\mu^{m-h}\eps^{2(m-1-p)}
      \int F_G(\xi)\ind_{t_{a_*}(\xi)\in J}\dd\xi.
\end{equation}
Here $F_G$ contains the birth factors, physical-link balls, merger fluxes,
and all physical segment, lifetime, acceptance, slab, and order indicators.
Equation \eqref{eq:jacobian} is used on this physical chart only. What follows
is a positive majorant after that change, not a new global Jacobian claim.

Let $A_p$ be the affine origin at zero of line $p$'s pre-first-event segment,
whose independent velocity is $v_p$. This need not be the eventual
piecewise path's value at zero. For initial forest edges orient $p_l$ to
$q_l$ and set $y_l=A_{q_l}-A_{p_l}$. Their endpoints are born at zero,
so these are the actual initial position differences on the physical chart.
The forest determines each initial group up to a common translation. A
positive-interface birth has its own independent input; an interface
continuation does not. At the end choose $X=A_{\rm rep}$ for a fixed
representative.

\subsection{A prefix-measurable chronological extension}\label{subsec:prefix}
Start all $m$ lines on their pre-first affine segments on the whole real
axis. For each group keep its old segments, current incoming affine segments,
and one free common translation. Process the prescribed global slots:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At a merger between distinct groups, with prescribed $t_g,\omega_g$,
 solve the source contact equation
 $X_1-X_2=-d(t_g)-\eps\omega_g$. Translate every segment already constructed
 in the moved group by the same vector. All earlier internal contacts,
 initial $y_l$, times, and velocities are preserved. Keep one common
 translation for the joined group. Use the current incoming segments
 extrapolated to $t_g$ and require positive incoming flux.
\item At a redundant slot use the two current segments. Take their unique
 transversal entering root at or after the later preceding incident event
 time; a missing preceding time means $-\infty$. Abort if no such root
 exists. This root and its normal are determined, with no extra variable.
\item In both cases require the new time to be strictly later than the
 preceding global slot; the first lower bound is $-\infty$. Abort otherwise.
 On success perform its elastic C-update or straight O-update and continue.
\end{enumerate}
Write $R_G^{\rm ext}$ for the incoming/existence tests and $Q_G$ for the
strict global order tests. Their product is the product of prefix success
indicators, extended by zero at the first failure. No future trajectory is
needed on a failed branch. The forest solve and entering quadratic roots
are measurable prefix operations. After fixing the final common $X$, the
successful construction is unique and is a chronological all-time path.

There is one important distinction between physical null sets and extended
rejection. If a redundant O-update is followed by another redundant slot
on that same unchanged relative flight, the entering-root rule can return
exactly the same time. This is a structural tie on the extended chart, not
a null-set assertion. Strict $Q_G$ rejects it with weight zero. Such a
branch cannot describe two physical incoming contacts on a fixed segment
pair. The tie and its rejection are shift invariant. Other physical
grazing and simultaneous-contact exclusions retain the source's
almost-everywhere convention.

All $m$ lines in this extension are present at every real time. Elastic
C-updates conserve pair energy and O-updates change none. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-energy}
 \sum_{p=1}^m|v_p^{\rm current}|^2=E_0
\end{equation}
on every successful prefix. In particular any slab's subset has energy at
most $E_0$, even when an extended event falls outside a physical lifetime.

The input factor remains
$|g_{(p)}(x_p^\xi(b_p),v_p)|$ at its original fixed birth time $b_p$.
On a nonphysical branch its independent second argument $v_p$ may differ
from the instantaneous velocity at $b_p$. This is allowed for a positive
extension, not asserted to be a gas law. It agrees on the physical domain.
Every spatial argument remains $X+d_p$, and its Gaussian argument is the
independent pre-first velocity.

Define, on successful branches and by zero after failure,
\begin{align}
 F_G^{\rm ext,Q}(\xi)
 ={}&\prod_p|g_{(p)}(x_p^\xi(b_p),v_p)|
     \prod_l\ind_{|y_l|\le R_{K,D}\eps}\notag\\
 &\cdot\prod_g[(\Delta v_g)\cdot\omega_g]_+
      \ind_{t\in\mathcal D}R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G,
       \label{eq:extension}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal D$ prescribes the merger slabs and relative merger order.
Records have already been bounded; they are not reinterpreted on this
extension.

\begin{lemma}[Physical domination]\label{lem:domination}
On the physical chart $F_G\le F_G^{\rm ext,Q}$ and the marked time agrees
with its extended time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the slots. A physical merger solves the same relative-translation
equation. A physical redundant contact lies on the current common segment
interval, which is contained in the extension's forward ray. The straight
relative flight has at most one entering root, so the physical root is the
computed root. Every physical global order test succeeds. Thus the paths and
velocities coincide throughout the physical lifetimes and $Q_G=1$ there.
Every omitted physical factor is between zero and one.
\end{proof}

For comparison, the local entering test alone is insufficient. With
unit diameter and incoming collinear data there are merger slots at $1/5$
and $2/5$, then a determined redundant time $7/15$, then a merger prescribed
at $9/20$. All local fluxes can be positive, but $7/15>9/20$.
The construction above rejects the final slot. The accompanying exact
counterchart documents this failure of the chronology-free extension; it is
not a counterexample to the physical gas.

\subsection{The collective shear}\label{subsec:shear}
For $\theta\in\R$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shear}
 \Phi_\theta(v,X,y,t,\omega)=
 \bigl(v,X-\theta v_{\rm rep},
       (y_l-\theta(v_{q_l}-v_{p_l}))_l,t+\theta\mathbf1,\omega\bigr).
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Covariance and measure]\label{lem:shear}
The shear preserves success or failure and its prefix, $R_G^{\rm ext}$,
$Q_G$, and all fluxes. On successful histories,
\[
 x_p^{\Phi_\theta\xi}(s)=x_p^\xi(s-\theta)
       \quad\text{for all }p,s.
\]
Every computed event time increases by $\theta$. The map preserves
$dv\dd X\dd y\dd t\dd\omega$ and has inverse $\Phi_{-\theta}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Time translation changes each pre-first affine origin by
$A_p\mapsto A_p-\theta v_p$, giving precisely the $X,y$ shear. At a merger,
translate all old segments of the moved group. Its contact equation at
$t_g+\theta$ is the old equation at $t_g$, and the incoming sign is the
same. At a redundant slot the relative flight and its preceding-time lower
bound translate together, so its entering root and normal have the claimed
covariance. The global order comparison uses two times both increased by
$\theta$. This proves covariance and matching aborts by prefix induction;
structural ties also remain ties. Future group translations preserve the
old internal conditions. Uniqueness of the final forest solve fixes $X$.
The affine-origin argument does not use the generally false equality
$x_p(-\theta)=A_p-\theta v_p$ for a full path that has already scattered.
For fixed $v$ the map translates $(X,y,t)$. On the full parameter space it
has block triangular derivative with identity diagonal, and leaves the
sphere variables fixed. The inverse proves measure preservation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Average first, retaining chronology}\label{subsec:average}
Put $\Theta=\tau$. For the extended marked integral, change variables and
average to obtain
\begin{align}
 I_G&:=\int F_G^{\rm ext,Q}(\xi)
                     \ind_{t_{a_*}^{\rm ext}(\xi)\in J}\dd\xi\notag\\
 &=\frac1\Theta\int_0^\Theta\!\int
     F_G^{\rm ext,Q}(\Phi_\theta\eta)
           \ind_{t_{a_*}^{\rm ext}(\eta)+\theta\in J}\dd\eta\dd\theta.
       \label{eq:average}
\end{align}
All integrands are nonnegative, so Tonelli applies. First integrate $X$.
Its coefficient in every birth position is the identity, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatial}
 \int\prod_p|g_{(p)}(X+d_p,v_p)|\dd X
 \le\norm{g_{(p_0)}(\cdot,v_{p_0})}_1
         \prod_{p\ne p_0}\norm{g_{(p)}(\cdot,v_p)}_\infty
 =:\mathfrak B(v)\le C^m e^{-\beta_*E_0}.
\end{equation}
The bound is independent of the displacements, including their dependence
on the shift and on fixed birth times.

For $u_l=v_{q_l}-v_{p_l}$, a single simultaneous shift yields the containing
cylinder
\begin{align}
 \ind_{|y_l-\theta u_l|\le R_{K,D}\eps}
 &\le\ind_{y_l\in\Cyl_l},\qquad
 \Cyl_l=[0,\Theta]u_l+B(0,R_{K,D}\eps),\notag\\
 |\Cyl_l|&\le\pi R_{K,D}^2\eps^2\Theta|u_l|
                    +\frac{4\pi}3R_{K,D}^3\eps^3.
       \label{eq:cylinder}
\end{align}
Replacing simultaneous membership by the product of these containing sets
is a positive upper bound; no separate shift is introduced per initial link.
The time-window integral is at most
\[
 \int_0^\Theta\ind_{t+\theta\mathbf1\in\mathcal D}
          \ind_{t_{a_*}^{\rm ext}+\theta\in J}\dd\theta
 \le |J|\ind_{t\in\mathcal D_\Theta},\qquad
 \mathcal D_\Theta=\mathcal D-[0,\Theta]\mathbf1.
\]
The fluxes and both prefix indicators are shift invariant. Thus
\begin{align}
 I_G\le\frac{|J|}{\Theta}\int dv\,\mathfrak B(v)
  \int dy\prod_l\ind_{y_l\in\Cyl_l}
  \int_{\mathcal D_\Theta}dt\int d\omega\,
          R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G\prod_g[(\Delta v_g)\cdot\omega_g]_+.
       \label{eq:W1}
\end{align}
Chronology has not been dropped in this formula.

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous simplex enlargement]\label{lem:simplex}
If $S_a=\{s<t_1<\cdots<t_a<s+\tau\}$ and $a\ge1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplex}
 |S_a-[0,\Theta]\mathbf1|
   =\frac{\tau^a}{a!}+\Theta\frac{\tau^{a-1}}{(a-1)!}
   =\frac{\tau^a}{a!}\left(1+\frac{a\Theta}{\tau}\right).
\end{equation}
The empty simplex has zero-dimensional volume one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the ordered gaps $t_k-t_1$. The first-time length is $\tau-(t_a-t_1)$
in $S_a$ and increases by $\Theta$ in the enlargement. Integrating over
the gap simplex gives the original volume and $\Theta$ times its
$(a-1)$-dimensional gap volume, proving the formula. For $a=1$ there are
no gap coordinates and the length is $\tau+\Theta$.
\end{proof}
Since $\mathcal D$ is a product of slab merger simplices,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:time-volume}
 |\mathcal D_\Theta|\le
 \prod_i\frac{\tau^{a_i}}{a_i!}(1+a_i)
 \le(1+n)^L\prod_i\frac{\tau^{a_i}}{a_i!},\qquad \Theta=\tau.
\end{equation}
This also covers no-merger graphs. A partial final slab can be enlarged
to its full assigned slab before the estimate.

\subsection{Prefix flux sum, factorials, and normalization}\label{subsec:flux}
Keep $(v,y,t,\omega)$ fixed in \eqref{eq:W1} and sum over the discrete tree
of successive merger-pair choices. At a node the preceding choices and all
intervening redundant slots have already been processed. Its incoming
velocities are common to every possible next pair; no future pair determines
a past reflection. Prefix failure has weight zero. On the energy bin
$E_0\le P$, \eqref{eq:all-energy} gives for the slab-$i$ lines
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flux}
 \sum_{p<q}[(v_p^{\rm cur}-v_q^{\rm cur})\cdot\omega]_+
 \le\sum_{p<q}|v_p^{\rm cur}-v_q^{\rm cur}|
 \le(n_i-1)\sum_p|v_p^{\rm cur}|
 \le n_i^{3/2}\sqrt P.
\end{equation}
A child failing an existence or strict-order test contributes zero.
Induction bounds the flux products, with $R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G$ retained,
by $\prod_i(n_i^{3/2}\sqrt P)^{a_i}$. Normal integration costs only a
fixed constant per merger. Only after this prefix bound is it legitimate
to discard $R_G^{\rm ext}Q_G\le1$ for the positive time-volume bound
\eqref{eq:time-volume}. No nonchronological velocity history is used in

The coefficients \eqref{eq:discrete-weight} were fixed by the finite forest
exploration before the physical change of variables. Neither the shear nor

 C_K(1+n)^{d_K}C^a\prod_i\frac1{n_i!}.
\]
The $O_K(1)$ redundant labels, initial-link vertices, and crossing roots

Integrating the initial-link cylinders contributes

 C_K\eps^{2p}(1+E_0)^{p/2},
\]
because \eqref{eq:cylinder} includes both its $\eps^2\tau|u_l|$ and

 \mu^{m-h}\eps^{2(m-1-p)}\eps^{2p}=\mu^{1-h}.
\end{equation}
The original extra $\eps$ per physical initial link is lost, but the

\subsection{The enlarged summation closes}\label{subsec:summation}
Apply the fixed reserve \eqref{eq:polyabsorb}--\eqref{eq:reserve}.
The velocity integral of $e^{-\beta_*E_0/4}$ is $C^m$ and the energy sum is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy-sum}
 \sum_{P=1,2,4,\ldots}e^{-\beta_*P/4}P^{a/2}
        \le C^{a+1}(1+a)^{a/2}.
\end{equation}
This follows by comparison with a gamma integral and Stirling's inequality;
the lowest bin uses $P^{a/2}=1$. Its base depends on $\beta_*$, not on
$K$, $p$, or the moment order. The numerical base need not equal the old
unmodified one; its independence of these later parameters is what matters.

Up to fixed polynomials, the remaining size expression is H's
\begin{equation}\label{eq:before-stirling}
 C^{n+a}\tau^a(1+a)^{a/2}
                  \prod_i\frac{n_i^{3a_i/2}}{a_i!n_i!}.
\end{equation}
Put $d_i=n_i-a_i\ge0$; \eqref{eq:forestcounts} gives
$\sum_i d_i=O_K(1)$. Factorials imply
\[
 \frac{n_i^{3a_i/2}}{a_i!n_i!}
    \le e^{2n_i}(1+n_i)^{d_i}n_i^{-n_i/2}.
\]
Indeed replace $1/a_i!$ by $(1+n_i)^{d_i}/n_i!$ and use
$n_i!\ge(n_i/e)^{n_i}$, dropping further nonpositive powers.
Zero counts contribute one. Also
\[
 \tau^a\le\tau^n\max(1,\tau^{-1})^{O_K(1)},\qquad
 (1+a)^{a/2}\le C_K(1+n)^{O_K(1)}n^{n/2},
\]
and convexity of $u\log u$ gives $\prod_i n_i^{n_i}\ge(n/L)^n$.
Combining \eqref{eq:W0}--\eqref{eq:before-stirling} proves the bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W2}
 \frac{|J|}{\tau}\,C_K(1+n)^{d_K+L+1}
           (C\tau\sqrt L)^n\mu^{1-h}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $L$, the number of size vectors with total $n$ is polynomial in
$n$. Section~\ref{sec:budget} chose $C\tau\sqrt L=CT/\sqrt L<1$
independently of that polynomial degree. Summing \eqref{eq:W2} proves
Lemma~\ref{lem:window}. The marked contact never acquired an independent
time coordinate; its unit-speed motion came from one collective shear.

\section{Moving pulses and increment cumulants}\label{sec:increments}
Fix real $\phi\in C_c^\infty$ and write
$M_\phi=\norm\phi_\infty$ and
$D_\phi=\norm{v\cdot\nabla_x\phi}_\infty$. These are finite. For
$0\le s<t\le T$, put $\delta=t-s$ and
\[
 S_{s,t}=\sum_i[\phi(\hat z_i(t))-\phi(\hat z_i(s))].
\]
This is H's scalar record with pulses $-\phi$ at $s$, $+\phi$ at $t$,
always ending at the same full time $T$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:increments}
For every fixed integer $j\ge1$ and every $q>0$, uniformly in $s,t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cumulant-bound}
 |\kappa_j(S_{s,t})|\le C_j\mu\delta+C_{j,q}\eps^q.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every assigned $q>0$,
\begin{align}
 \E|\widehat X_t(\phi)-\widehat X_s(\phi)|^4
     &\le C(\delta^2+\delta/\mu)+C_q\eps^q,\label{eq:fourth}\\
 \E|S_{s,t}|&\le C\mu\delta+C_q\eps^q.\label{eq:absolute-increment}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On each actual retained history a designated line with no displayed C-atom
in $(s,t]$ flies freely there. Refining every autonomous C-cluster by its
complete ordered accepted history, as in H04, makes this statement literal.
O-atoms change no velocity. Its scalar mark therefore satisfies the positive
majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mark-bound}
 |y_\ell|\le D_\phi\delta+2M_\phi N_{(s,t]}(\ell).
\end{equation}
Only powers of the single final scalar record are used to test the actual
cumulant. The two endpoint states in this inequality are a pointwise
history argument, not an illicit two-record test of an endpoint measure.

Apply \eqref{eq:ordinary}. For $h=|\pi|\le j$, use the retained connected
expansion, its high-complexity tail, and the initial-link expansion
\eqref{eq:links}. On the physical-link part, all other record factors are
bounded by fixed powers of $2M_\phi$; one factor is bounded by
\eqref{eq:mark-bound}. The unmarked absolute mass is
$C\mu^{1-h}$ by the source summation, and the window term is
$C\mu^{1-h}\delta$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:window}. The factor $\mu^h$ in
\eqref{eq:ordinary} gives $C\mu\delta$.

We justify a single accuracy choice uniform in both pulses. Fix $j$ and the
desired power $q$. Keep exactly these two pulses and at most $j$ designated
labels to the fixed endpoint $T$. Their update maps have
$|y|\le2M_\phi$ uniformly in $s,t$. Compatibility and acceptance do not
use the mark. In absolute estimates, remove pulse-to-segment selection
restrictions; the possible placements cost only a fixed polynomial in size,
or a cutoff power in the coarse regime. The stopped kernels and crude TV
operator depend only on the test supremum. Thus the positive kernel proofs
in Section~\ref{subsec:expansion} provide one majorant for all
$(s,t)\in[0,T]^2$, not just constants for each fixed observation list.

Choose a coarse accuracy $A>q+2j+2$, then one $K$ making
\eqref{eq:coarse} at most $\eps^A$ for all $h\le j$. Choose a tail target
$M>q+2j+2$ and then $D>2K+M$. Multiplication by $\mu^h$ leaves
$O(\eps^{A-2j})$ for the coarse part and
$O(\eps^{M-2})$ for an initial tail
$O(\eps^M\mu^{1-h})$. The unweighted variation
$e^{-c\Lambda_L}$ beats every fixed power. These estimates use only bounded
powers of $y$, or $|y|$, with no Gaussian weight for a generic microscopic
remainder. Higher assigned powers first absorb every finite cross product
below. None of these choices changes $L$ or the dynamics.
This proves \eqref{eq:cumulant-bound}.

The centered fourth-moment identity gives
\[
 \E|\widehat X_t-\widehat X_s|^4
     =\mu^{-2}\bigl(\kappa_4(S_{s,t})+3\kappa_2(S_{s,t})^2\bigr).
\]
Insert the preceding estimates and relabel the arbitrary error power,
using $\delta\le T$, to get \eqref{eq:fourth}. For
\eqref{eq:absolute-increment}, positivity of the actual one-particle
measure $c_1=F_1$ gives
$\E|S_{s,t}|\le\E\sum_i|y_i|=\mu c_1(|y|)$.
Its same connected decomposition and \eqref{eq:mark-bound} prove the bound.
If $\phi=0$ all statements are immediate.
\end{proof}
The remainder is an arbitrary assigned power, not a qualitative cycle
remainder. This distinction is needed when passing to microscopic scales.

\section{A final-slab history flag}\label{sec:flags}
We now derive the additional finite-kernel operation needed for a microscopic
modulus. Let $(i-1)\tau\le a<b\le i\tau$ and stop the hierarchy at $b$.
For a designated particle $p$ put
\[
 r_p=\ind_{\{\text{its velocity jumps in }(a,b]\}}.
\]
This is a bounded measurable function of its final-slab history; it is not
asserted to be recoverable from its endpoint.

\begin{lemma}[Decorated output]\label{lem:decorated}
On the output space $(z_p(b),r_p)$, the factorial hierarchy of the same
pasted dynamics has the retained connected-diagram expansion and the
unweighted TV, coarse, and initial-tail decomposition of
Section~\ref{subsec:expansion}, uniformly in $a,b$ and the partial endpoint.
For each fixed $h$ and arbitrary $q>0$ its actual decorated cumulant satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-cumulant}
 \left|\mu^h c_{h,b}^{\eps,\mathrm{dec}}(r_1\cdots r_h)\right|
       \le C_h\mu(b-a)+C_{h,q}\eps^q.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:kernel} replace the final delta, for each fixed autonomous
partition, by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decorated-delta}
 \delta_{((z_p^{\Psi_{\mathcal C}}(b),
                   r_p(\Psi_{\mathcal C}|_{(a,b]})))_{p\in S}}.
\end{equation}
Use the identical replacement in the positive stopped kernel. The caps give
finite accepted histories, so the flag is measurable on the source's common
full-measure history space. Apply \eqref{eq:autonomous} to bounded measurable
tests of this new output. Its finite Janossy/forest proof still cancels
spectators pointwise before integration; \eqref{eq:fresh} still leaves
$\mu^j/j!$. Thus the one-slab identity holds as an identity of measures
on the decorated space, not only after endpoint projection.

Earlier slabs carry no jump flag. Compose their ordinary unmarked kernels
with this final decorated kernel. In the final slab keep all designated
histories explicit. A final independent singleton may be denoted
$g_{i,b}^{\mathrm{dec}}$; it is the decorated one-root output on independent
ordinary lower inputs. It is not the ordinary unmarked $g_i$ followed by an
attempt to recover its flag. Lower one-root factors can retain their
canonical ordinary resummations.

For coarse bookkeeping, the finite one-root definitions can be opened along
the at most $h$ designated physical lines down to zero, with a constant
record if needed. This is the source's record-preserving finite regrouping
and projection operation. It duplicates neither an input nor an interface
birth. Any needed bottom continuation can be supplied with at most $h$
source dummy straight-root links at fixed cost $\eps^{-h/81}$, together
with the allowed top-root deficit $\eps^{-C_*h}$. Their fixed powers and
complexity are absorbed by the subsequent $K$; they are removed before the
low-complexity physical-link estimate. The flag remains a final-output
function. No record or line is introduced per collision.

Decorated outputs factor over disjoint global components because each root
flag depends only on its own component history. Expand lower independent
sums finitely for this algebra. Assigning fresh labels to components yields
the same multinomial identity as in H04, Lemma
\texttt{lem:diagram-labels}. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decorated-partition}
 \widehat F_{b,S}^{\mathrm{dec}}
       =\sum_{\pi\in\calP(S)}\bigotimes_{V\in\pi}D_{b,V}^{\mathrm{dec}}.
\end{equation}
Finite inversion keeps exactly the connected decorated diagrams. An
independent decorated singleton contributes only to order one; it cannot
create a connection at order two or higher. Its order-one history integrals
are final-slab diagrams with Gaussian Bol-bounded ordinary births, to which
the same window estimate applies.

The variation argument must take place before the new pushforward.
At the level of each positive history kernel, \eqref{eq:decorated-delta}
preserves its mass. We do not lift a TV estimate of an unmarked endpoint
measure to its histories. Rather, for any bounded decorated test $H_0$,
\[
 |R_s^{\mathrm{dec}}(H_0)|
       \le\norm{H_0}_\infty(\mathcal E_{i,s}F_{i-1})(1).
\]
In the recursion
$\Delta_i^{\mathrm{dec}}=R_i^{\mathrm{dec}}+
\mathcal L_i^{\mathrm{dec}}\Delta_{i-1}$, the lower error is the ordinary
unmarked one. The decorated kernel's crude TV operator bound is unchanged:
its positive partition/forest majorant sees only the supremum of $H_0$.
The source stopped-variation induction and its partial-final-slab bounds
therefore apply. The fixed bookkeeping losses above have the allowed form
already absorbed in that induction.

The actual normalized decorated factorial mass is
$\mu^{-|S|}\E(N)_{|S|}\le1$, and restricting by flags only lowers it.
The retained decorated measure has variation at most
$1+e^{-c\Lambda_i}$ at fixed orders. In the difference of the actual and
retained finite partition polynomials every term has a variation-error
factor; the other factors have these bounded masses. Thus the cumulant
variation error is still $C_he^{-c\Lambda_i}$.

For the high-complexity tail, the positive coarse integrand depends on
$a,b$ only through a multiplier at most one and a shorter final time domain.
There are only $L$ final slab indices. The same fixed root losses and
\eqref{eq:coarse} give one $K$ for every $a,b$ at a fixed assigned accuracy.
Choose the initial-tail sharpness afterwards, as in
Section~\ref{sec:increments}. All of these are unweighted TV statements.

Finally refine each autonomous C-cluster by its complete ordered accepted
C history, as in H04's one-root bridge and stopped-variation proof. The
root velocity changes only at displayed C-atoms, so on each physical retained
history
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flag-count}
 r_p\le N_{(a,b]}(p).
\end{equation}
Bound all other flags by one and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:window}, after removing
artificial coarse-estimate links. The normalization $\mu^h$ gives
$C\mu(b-a)$. Choose the unweighted error powers before that multiplication
to obtain the actual remainder $C_{h,q}\eps^q$ in
\eqref{eq:flag-cumulant}.
\end{proof}
Restricting the decorated output to $r=1$ commutes with tensor products in
\eqref{eq:decorated-partition}. It is thus the ordinary factorial hierarchy
of the point process retaining just the jumping particles. The lemma is a
separate consequence of the finite history kernel, not an application of
the additive-pulse theorem to a nonadditive endpoint mark.

\section{A microscopic modulus}\label{sec:micro}
For a real path $Y$ write
$w(Y,u)=\sup_{|t-s|\le u}|Y_t-Y_s|$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \alpha=\mu^{-3/2}.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:micro}
For every real compact smooth $\phi$,
$w(\widehat X^\eps(\phi),2\alpha)\to0$ in probability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $I_k=(k\alpha,(k+3)\alpha]\cap(0,T]$, for
$0\le k\le\lceil T/\alpha\rceil$. Any pair $s<t$ with $t-s\le2\alpha$
lies in the closure of $I_k$ for $k=\lfloor s/\alpha\rfloor$.
For small $\eps$, $3\alpha<\tau$; split $I_k$ at its possible one interface
into at most two pieces $I_k^\iota$ inside individual slabs. Define
\[
 \Count(I)=\#\{p:\hat v_p\text{ jumps in }I\}=\sum_p r_p.
\]
A particle with no jump in $(k\alpha,t]$ flies freely; its test change
between $s,t$ is at most $3\alpha D_\phi$. Otherwise use $2M_\phi$.
Pathwise,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:micro-path}
 \sup_{s,t\in\overline{I_k}}\left|\sum_p
          [\phi(\hat z_p(t))-\phi(\hat z_p(s))]\right|
   \le3\alpha D_\phi N+2M_\phi\sum_\iota\Count(I_k^\iota).
\end{equation}

For $d\le3$, the factorial cumulants of this restricted point process are
those in Lemma~\ref{lem:decorated}. For $|I|\le3\alpha$ they obey the
\emph{same actual error convention}
\[
 |\mu^d c_{d}^{\eps,\mathrm{dec}}(r^{\otimes d})|
       \le C_d\mu\alpha+C_{d,q}\eps^q.
\]
The block size $d$ is not the endpoint $b$; no $\mu^d$ is inserted again
in this already-normalized remainder. The exact factorial moment identity
and the diagonal identity are
\begin{align}
 \E(\Count(I))_p
  &=\sum_{\pi\in\calP([p])}\prod_{V\in\pi}
             \mu^{|V|}c_{|V|}^{\eps,\mathrm{dec}}(r^{\otimes|V|}),
                      \quad p=1,2,3,\label{eq:count-partition}\\
 \Count(I)^3&=(\Count(I))_3+3(\Count(I))_2+\Count(I).
       \label{eq:count-diagonals}
\end{align}
Since $x=\mu\alpha=\mu^{-1/2}\le1$ and $\eps^q\le1$, every finite product
is bounded by $C_p(x+\eps^q)$, increasing the assigned source accuracy first
if necessary. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-third}
 \E\Count(I)^3\le C(\mu\alpha+\eps^q).
\end{equation}
All falling-factorial diagonal terms have been kept.

Markov's inequality and a union over $O(T/\alpha)$ pieces, with threshold
$\eta\sqrt\mu$, give
\begin{align}
 \Prob\!\left(\max_{k,\iota}\Count(I_k^\iota)>\eta\sqrt\mu\right)
 &\le\frac{C(T/\alpha)(\mu\alpha+\eps^q)}{\eta^3\mu^{3/2}}
 \le C\eta^{-3}(\mu^{-1/2}+\eps^q)\notag\\
 &\le C\eta^{-3}(\mu^{-1/2}+\mu^3\eps^q)\longrightarrow0,
               \qquad q>6.\label{eq:micro-union}
\end{align}
The last, weaker form uses a common error choice convenient for chaining;
the first form aligns directly with the actual error in
\eqref{eq:flag-cumulant}. Since $\mu^3\eps^q=\eps^{q-6}$, $q>6$ suffices.
A second-moment union based on its leading term would give only
$(T/\alpha)(\mu\alpha)/(\eta^2\mu)=T/\eta^2$, which does not vanish.

The free-flight term in \eqref{eq:micro-path}, after division by
$\sqrt\mu$, is
$3D_\phi\mu^{-1}(N/\mu)\to0$ in probability by \eqref{eq:N}.
For exact centering, \eqref{eq:absolute-increment} gives uniformly for
$|t-s|\le2\alpha$
\[
 \mu^{-1/2}|\E S_{s,t}|\le C\mu^{1/2}\alpha+C_q\eps^q
                   =C\mu^{-1}+o(1).
\]
All intervals are half-open $(a,b]$. The phase paths are right-continuous;
a jump at the left endpoint is already reflected in its endpoint state,
and a right-endpoint jump belongs to the count. Pasting creates no interface
jump. Thus these conventions cover grid endpoints and interface splits.
The three bounds prove the proposition.
\end{proof}

\section{Chaining, continuity, and exact-centering transfer}\label{sec:finish}
\begin{lemma}[Chaining to the microscopic scale]\label{lem:chaining}
Let $Y^\eps$ be real cadlag processes on $[0,T]$, with $Y_0^\eps$ tight,
and suppose
\[
 \E|Y_t^\eps-Y_s^\eps|^4
  \le C(|t-s|^2+|t-s|/\mu)+R_\eps,
 \qquad R_\eps\alpha^{-2}\to0.
\]
If $w(Y^\eps,2\alpha)\to0$ in probability for \eqref{eq:alpha}, then
$Y^\eps$ is C-tight in the $J_1$ Skorokhod space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the chaining exponent $\vartheta=1/8$, distinct from the shift
parameter $\theta$. Let $t_{k,j}=jT2^{-k}$ and let $k_\eps$ be the largest
$k$ with $T2^{-k}\ge\alpha$. The finest spacing lies in $[\alpha,2\alpha)$.
For $Z_k=\max_j|Y_{t_{k,j+1}}-Y_{t_{k,j}}|$ the union bound gives
\[
 \Prob(Z_k>2^{-k\vartheta})\le
    2^k\{C(T^22^{-2k}+T2^{-k}/\mu)+R_\eps\}2^{4k\vartheta}.
\]
Summing from $k_0$ to $k_\eps$ gives three terms bounded respectively by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dyadic}
 C\sum_{k\ge k_0}2^{-k/2},\qquad
 C\mu^{-1}(T/\alpha)^{1/2}=C\mu^{-1/4},\qquad
 CR_\eps(T/\alpha)^{3/2}\le CR_\eps\alpha^{-2}.
\end{equation}
On the complementary event, successive dyadic approximations give for
nearby finest-grid points a bound
$Z_{k_0}+2\sum_{k>k_0}Z_k\le C_\vartheta2^{-k_0\vartheta}$.
For arbitrary $s,t$ with $|t-s|\le T2^{-k_0-1}$ pass to adjacent
finest-grid points; each endpoint error is bounded by $w(Y,2\alpha)$.
For sufficiently fine terminal grids the possible fixed-level edge
adjustments are absorbed in $C_\vartheta$. Hence
\[
 w(Y,T2^{-k_0-1})\le
       C_\vartheta2^{-k_0\vartheta}+2w(Y,2\alpha)
\]
outside the exceptional probabilities in \eqref{eq:dyadic}. First let
$\eps\to0$ and then $k_0\to\infty$. Together with tight initial values,
this is the continuous-modulus criterion for C-tightness
\cite[Proposition VI.3.26]{JS}. This is standard tightness machinery;
small individual jumps alone would not prove it.
\end{proof}

For clarity we include the credited Gaussian continuity and uniform
centering deductions, previously established in the project record I340.
H02 constructs the target on
\[
 E_a^0=\{h\in C(\R^6):e^{-a|v|^2}h\in C_0(\R^6)\},
 \qquad \norm h_a=\sup e^{-a|v|^2}|h|,
\]
with bounded maps to $L^2(\Omega)$ for $a<\beta$, uniformly in time.
For compact smooth $\phi$, $r\mapsto A_r\phi$ is continuous and bounded
in $E_a^0$ for $a>0$. The source weak identity and field/source bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-gaussian}
 \zeta_t(\phi)=\zeta_0(\phi)+\int_0^t\zeta_r(A_r\phi)\dd r+W_t(\phi),
 \qquad
 \E\int_0^T|\zeta_r(A_r\phi)|^2\dd r<\infty.
\end{equation}
The integrand is strongly measurable in $L^2$ by the source propagator
construction. Its indefinite integral has continuous paths by
Cauchy--Schwarz. The Gaussian noise has
$\E|W_t(\phi)-W_s(\phi)|^4\le C|t-s|^2$, so Kolmogorov yields a continuous
modification. Equation \eqref{eq:weak-gaussian} gives the continuous version
of each $\zeta(\phi)$, with all joint covariances unchanged. A finite
intersection of probability-one events handles a finite list.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For each coordinate, \eqref{eq:fourth} has
$R_\eps=C_q\eps^q$ with arbitrary $q>6$, so
$R_\eps\alpha^{-2}=C_q\eps^{q-6}\to0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:micro} and Lemma~\ref{lem:chaining} give C-tightness
of the pasted coordinate. Its initial value has the same initial ensemble
as the true flow and is tight by H's initial finite-dimensional theorem.
For a fixed vector, its continuous modulus is bounded by the sum of the
coordinate moduli, and its initial vector is tight. It is therefore C-tight.

Let $S_t=\sum_i\phi(z_i(t))$ and
$\widehat S_t=\sum_i\phi(\hat z_i(t))$. On the coupling
\eqref{eq:coupling}, simultaneously in all times,
\[
 \sup_{t\le T}|S_t-\widehat S_t|
       \le2M_\phi N\ind_{E_\eps}.
\]
H\"older's inequality with the fixed $22$nd moment of $N/\mu$ gives
\begin{align}
 \sup_{t\le T}\frac{|\E S_t-\E\widehat S_t|}{\sqrt\mu}
 &\le C\eps^{-1}\Prob(E_\eps)^{21/22}\notag\\
 &\le C\eps^{-1+(21/20)(21/22)}
       =C\eps^{1/440}\longrightarrow0.
       \label{eq:transfer}
\end{align}
The coupling exponent is $\eta_0=1/20$; it is distinct from the arbitrary
probability threshold $\eta$ in \eqref{eq:micro-union}. Off $E_\eps$ the
uncentered paths coincide at every time and $\Prob(E_\eps)\to0$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sup-transfer}
 \norm{X^\eps(\phi)-\widehat X^\eps(\phi)}_\infty\to0
                 \quad\text{in probability}.
\end{equation}
This works for a fixed finite vector. The identity time change bounds its
$J_1$ distance by the supremum distance. The modulus inequality transfers
C-tightness. No fourth-moment bound for the true flow restricted to the
exceptional event is asserted or required.

For each fixed $\eps$, particle number is finite almost surely and
$\E N<\infty$. Bounded-test sums are cadlag; their expectations are cadlag
by dominated convergence using $M_\phi N$. The centered vector is therefore
a random element of the usual $J_1$ space, whose Borel sigma-field is
generated by coordinate evaluations. Every subsequential limit of the
C-tight family is supported on continuous paths. Evaluation at any time
is continuous there, so H's joint finite-dimensional convergence identifies
its finite-dimensional laws with those of \eqref{eq:covariance}.
Finite-dimensional laws determine the law on $C([0,T],\R^d)$, giving a
unique subsequential law and convergence of the whole sequence.

On a Skorokhod representation of a $J_1$-convergent subsequence with
continuous limit, paths converge uniformly almost surely. This is a
consequence on that representation, not weak convergence in the
nonseparable uniform-topology Borel space on all cadlag paths.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope, provenance, and supporting verification}\label{sec:scope}
The conclusion retains actual unsmoothed trajectories, exact microscopic
centering, the whole-space ensemble including $N=0$, and the entire
supplied regular interval. It does not assert a distribution-valued path
limit beyond every fixed finite test vector, regularity of a Boltzmann
solution beyond the assumed interval, or a quantitative process-convergence
rate. The auxiliary cutoff coupling is not independent resampling at slab
interfaces.
