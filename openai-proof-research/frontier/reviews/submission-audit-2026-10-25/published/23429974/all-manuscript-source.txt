\begin{abstract}
Relative to the pinned complete Crouzeix inequality and its exterior Faber
operations, and to Jin's geometric circle criterion, we prove that actual
factor-two equality for one finite matrix-valued polynomial forces the
numerical range to be a nondegenerate disk in every finite dimension.
We retain the Jensen deficit in finite diagonal dual tests. Strong exterior
boundary convergence and a triple-variance Fatou argument then make the
diagonal compressed resolvents constant, without a rank-one assumption.
An ordered constant test and equality in Parseval produce a fixed
matrix polynomial channel that is unitary on the boundary. Its rational
inverse is holomorphic in the exterior and vanishes at infinity. The
determinant therefore realizes the boundary as a full polynomial level
set. The numerical-range Hermitian tangent pencil supplies the additional
hypothesis of the credited circle criterion. The argument allows coefficient
rank greater than one and assumes no initial boundary smoothness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, dependencies, and conventions}\label{sec:statement}
For $A\in M_n(\C)$ let
\[
 W(A)=\{v^*Av:v\in\C^n,\ \norm v=1\}.
\]
This is a compact convex set containing $\spec A$. All operator norms
are Euclidean. For $F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^D z^k C_k$, $C_k\in M_m(\C)$,
we use the base-first evaluation
\[
 F[A]=\sum_{k=0}^D A^k\otimes C_k.
\]
The Hilbert--Schmidt norm is unnormalized:
$\norm C_{\HS}^2=\tr(C^*C)$. A bar denotes entrywise conjugation,
$\mathsf T$ denotes ordinary transpose, and $*$ denotes adjoint.
Circle integrals use $d\tau(e^{it})=dt/(2\pi)$; $L^2$ norms of matrix
fields use the Hilbert--Schmidt norm.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For all finite integers $n,m\geq1$, every $A\in M_n(\C)$ and every
finite $M_m(\C)$-valued polynomial $F$, put
\[
 M_0=\max_{z\in W(A)}\norm{F(z)}.
\]
If $M_0>0$ and $\norm{F[A]}=2M_0$, then $W(A)$ is a closed disk of
positive radius.
\end{theorem}

There is no dimension or degree restriction, initial irreducibility
hypothesis, boundary regularity hypothesis, or simple support eigenvalue
hypothesis. The hypothesis concerns one attained polynomial witness;
it does not concern a possibly nonattained supremum over witnesses.
The proof also gives the following structure.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:channel}
Normalize $M_0=1$. Let $B$ be any unitarily irreducible reducing block
of $A$ with $\norm{F[B]}=2$, and let $K=W(B)$. There are fixed
subspaces $E,G\subset\C^m$ of the same positive dimension $r$ and
isometries $U:\C^r\to E$, $V:\C^r\to G$ such that
\[
 J(z)=V^*F(z)U
\]
is a square polynomial, $F(z)E\subset G$ for every $z$, and $J$ is
unitary on $\partial K$. Its inverse $Q=J^{-1}$ is rational, holomorphic
on $\C\setminus K$, continuous up to $\partial K$, and $Q(\infty)=0$.
The nonconstant polynomial $f=\det J$ satisfies the full identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-statement}
 \{|f|<1\}=\Int K,\qquad \{|f|=1\}=\partial K.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

We assume the complete inequality and analytic exterior Faber operations
of OpenAI \cite{Direct} at the immutable revision
\begin{center}\texttt{fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb}.\end{center}
These are stipulated inputs. We state the exact operations needed in
Section~\ref{sec:framework}, rather than auditing that upstream theorem.
The structural companion \cite{Structural} fixes the distinction between
the original matrix's mass-two positive resolvent and the normalized
positive representation of a similar matrix. We use the original field.
The only external specialized geometric results used are Jin v2
Lemma~8 (a curved exposed arc) and Proposition~6 (the circle criterion)
\cite{Jin}; their hypotheses are stated at their applications. Jin's
scalar equality theorem is not applied to the matrix witness.
Classical conformal mapping, Herglotz representation, Jordan boundary
extension, Hardy uniqueness, and scalar maximum principles are used in
their usual one-variable forms. The outer-limit argument is included.

The additional mechanism is the retained finite-test Jensen deficit,
its triple-variance limit, and the fixed polynomial channel and rational
inverse extracted from the constant diagonal fields. These steps permit
$r>1$. They make no assertion that the specified $F$ has a constant
scalar coefficient channel attaining two.

\section{Active block and the analytic exterior framework}\label{sec:framework}
Normalize $F$ by $M_0$. A finite reducing decomposition of $A$ gives
unitarily irreducible blocks; evaluation decomposes as their direct sum,
up to the fixed tensor permutation. Select a block $B\in M_d(\C)$ with
$\norm{F[B]}=2$. The credited complete bound and $W(B)\subset W(A)$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockmax}
 2\leq2\max_{W(B)}\norm F\leq2.
\end{equation}
Thus its maximum is one. A scalar block cannot attain two. If $K=W(B)$
had empty interior, translation and rotation would make all quadratic
forms real; polarization would make the transformed $B$ Hermitian,
contradicting irreducibility for $d\geq2$.

If $Bv=\zeta v$ at a boundary point with $\norm v=1$, choose a supporting
unit normal $\nu$. The positive Hermitian slack
$\Rea(\bar\nu\zeta)I-\Rea(\bar\nu B)$ has zero quadratic form at $v$
and annihilates it. Combining this with $Bv=\zeta v$ gives
$B^*v=\bar\zeta v$. The line through $v$ reduces $B$, a contradiction.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-spectrum}
 \spec B\subset\Int K.
\end{equation}
Fix unit top singular vectors $x,y$ with $F[B]x=2y$, $F[B]^*y=2x$,
and write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vec}
 x=\sum_{i=1}^m Xe_i\otimes e_i,\quad
 y=\sum_{i=1}^m Ye_i\otimes e_i,\quad
 \norm X_{\HS}=\norm Y_{\HS}=1.
\end{equation}
These $d$-by-$m$ matrices are fixed throughout the approximation.

For clarity we record the exact credited analytic-domain operations
from \cite[Theorem \texttt{lem:faber}, Proposition
\texttt{prop:coefficients}, Theorems \texttt{lem:resolvent} and
\texttt{thm:domain}]{Direct}. On a regular real-analytic convex outer
domain $\Omega\supset K$, its normalized exterior map has a conformal
collar and expansion
\[
 h(\lambda)=c\lambda+c_0+O(\lambda^{-1}),\quad c>0,\qquad q=\lambda h'.
\]
The Faber polynomials $b_k$ are specified by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:faber}
 \frac{q(\lambda)}{h(\lambda)-z}=\sum_{k\geq0}b_k(z)\lambda^{-k},
 \quad b_0=1,\quad \deg b_k=k.
\end{equation}
Their boundary decomposition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-faber}
 b_k(h(\mu))=\mu^k+\sum_{\ell\geq1}s_{k\ell}\mu^{-\ell}\quad(k\geq1).
\end{equation}
The mixed series is absolutely uniformly convergent on $\T^2$. The
continuous kernel, off its removable diagonal,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
 \kappa(\lambda,\mu)=2\Rea\frac{q(\lambda)}{h(\lambda)-h(\mu)}
\end{equation}
is nonnegative and has both angular marginals one. Its integral operator
\[
 (\mathcal Tu)(\mu)=\int_\T\kappa(\lambda,\mu)u(\lambda)d\tau(\lambda)
\]
is an $L^2$ contraction for Hilbert-valued fields. For a finite
polynomial $G=\sum_{k=0}^N b_k C_k$, put
$u(\lambda)=\sum_{k=1}^N C_k\lambda^k$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomposition}
 G\circ h=C_0+u+\mathcal Tu,
\end{equation}
with disjoint constant, positive, and negative Fourier supports. In
particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-bound}
 \sum_{k=0}^N\norm{C_k}_{\HS}^2\leq\norm{G\circ h}_2^2
 \leq\norm{C_0}_{\HS}^2+2\sum_{k=1}^N\norm{C_k}_{\HS}^2.
\end{equation}

The original resolvent field is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent}
 R=q(hI-B)^{-1}=\sum_{k\geq0}b_k(B)\lambda^{-k},\qquad H=R+R^*\succeq0.
\end{equation}
At fixed $\Omega$ this series is absolutely uniformly convergent and
has mean $I$. Positivity also follows directly by congruence:
\[
 (hI-B)^*H(hI-B)=\bar q(hI-B)+q(hI-B)^*\succeq0.
\]
Set $P=X^*RX$, $Z=Y^*RY$, $M=Y^*RX$, $L=X^*RY$, and use subscript
$k$ for the coefficient of $\lambda^{-k}$. Put $a^2=\sum_{k\geq0}
\norm{M_k}_{\HS}^2$. For any finite polynomial $G=\sum b_k C_k$,
the base-first tensor convention gives the bilinear pairing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairing}
 y^*G[B]x=\sum_{k,i,\ell}(M_k)_{i\ell}(C_k)_{i\ell}.
\end{equation}
For a normalized polynomial $\widetilde F$ with singular value $\gamma$
and the same pair $x,y$, the two multiplication orders give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordered}
 y^*(\widetilde F G)[B]x=\gamma x^*G[B]x,\qquad
 y^*(G\widetilde F)[B]x=\gamma y^*G[B]y.
\end{equation}
Cauchy--Schwarz in \eqref{eq:pairing}, the lower bound
\eqref{eq:coefficient-bound}, and $\norm{\widetilde F}\leq1$ on the
boundary imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordered-bound}
 \gamma|x^*G[B]x|\leq a\norm{G\circ h}_2,\qquad
 \gamma|y^*G[B]y|\leq a\norm{G\circ h}_2.
\end{equation}
The products are kept in their displayed orders. These formulas and
analytic convex outer approximation are the credited Faber input;
all equality and limiting deductions below are written out.

\section{Dimension-independent exterior convergence}\label{sec:outer}
Translate an interior point of $K$ to zero. By
\cite[Lemma \texttt{lem:approx}]{Direct}, take regular analytic convex
outer domains converging to $K$ in Hausdorff distance. For fixed
$0<\rho<R_0$ arrange
\[
 \rho\overline\D\subset K\subset\Omega_j\subset R_0\D.
\]
Write their maps as $h_j=c_j\lambda+c_{0j}+O(\lambda^{-1})$, $c_j>0$,
and put $q_j=\lambda h_j'$. We include the convergence details since
strong convergence, rather than weak convergence, will be used in
quadratic expressions.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:outer}
After passing to a subsequence, $h_j|_\T\to h|_\T$ uniformly and
$q_j\to q$ strongly in $L^2(\T)$, with $|q_j|,|q|\leq4R_0$ and
$q\ne0$ almost everywhere. The map $h$ is the normalized conformal
bijection from the exterior disk onto $\C\setminus K$ and extends to
a homeomorphism $\T\to\partial K$. Its boundary derivative is $q/\lambda$,
and $q$ is an outward normal almost everywhere. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-convergence}
 R_j=q_j(h_jI-B)^{-1}\longrightarrow R=q(hI-B)^{-1}
\end{equation}
strongly in $L^2$, with a common $L^\infty$ bound, and
$H_j=R_j+R_j^*\to H=R+R^*\succeq0$ in $L^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Schwarz's lemma applied to $\rho/h_j(1/w)$ in the unit disk
gives $c_j\geq\rho$. Applied to $1/\Phi_j(R_0/w)$, where
$\Phi_j=h_j^{-1}$, it gives $c_j\leq R_0$. The mean of $h_j|_\T$ gives
$|c_{0j}|\leq R_0$. On $\T$, the tangent is $i\lambda h_j'$ and its
argument derivative is $\Rea(1+\lambda h_j''/h_j')\geq0$ by convexity.
The harmonic minimum principle and Herglotz representation in the
reciprocal disk yield probability measures $\mu_j$ on $\T$ with
\begin{align}
 1+\lambda h_j''/h_j'&=\int_\T\frac{\lambda+\xi}{\lambda-\xi}\,d\mu_j(\xi),\notag\\
 \ell_j(\lambda):=\log(h_j'/c_j)
 &=2\int_\T\log(1-\xi/\lambda)\,d\mu_j(\xi).\label{eq:herglotz}
\end{align}
The logarithms are the branches zero at infinity. The sign follows
from $\lambda(d/d\lambda)\log(1-\xi/\lambda)=\xi/(\lambda-\xi)$.
Their real parts are at most $\log4$, so $|h_j'|\leq4R_0$.

Pass to $c_j\to c\geq\rho$ and weak convergence $\mu_j\to\mu$.
The coefficient of $\lambda^{-k}$ in $\ell_j$ is
$-2\int\xi^k d\mu_j/k$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logtails}
 \sum_{k>N}|\widehat\ell_j(-k)|^2\leq4\sum_{k>N}k^{-2}
\end{equation}
uniformly. Coefficient convergence and this tail bound imply strong
$L^2$ convergence $\ell_j\to\ell$. The limit is finite almost everywhere
and $\Rea\ell\leq\log4$. For $\Rea s,\Rea t\leq\log4$,
\[
 |e^s-e^t|=\left|(s-t)\int_0^1e^{t+\theta(s-t)}d\theta\right|
 \leq4|s-t|.
\]
Thus $h_j'=c_je^{\ell_j}\to ce^\ell$ strongly in $L^2$ and the latter
is nonzero almost everywhere. This proves the derivative and $q_j$
convergence with their upper bounds; no lower bound for $|h_j'|$ is used.

The derivative bound makes $h_j|_\T$ equi-Lipschitz, and their values
are bounded by $R_0$. Subsequence compactness gives a uniform boundary
limit consistent with a locally uniform exterior holomorphic limit.
Indeed $|h_j-c_j\lambda|\leq2R_0$ on and outside $\T$ by the maximum
principle. The leading coefficient $c\geq\rho$ prevents a constant
limit, so the limit is univalent. Every point of $K$ is omitted by
$h$ by Hurwitz. For completeness, the inverses $\Phi_j$ on compact
subsets of $\C\setminus K$ satisfy
\[
 |\Phi_j(w)|\leq(|w|+2R_0)/\rho.
\]
A diagonal normal-family limit $\Phi$ has $|\Phi|\geq1$ and is nonconstant:
$|\Phi(w)|\geq(|w|-2R_0)/R_0$ at large $w$. The minimum-modulus
principle gives $|\Phi|>1$. Passing in the compositions proves
$h\circ\Phi=\mathrm{id}$ and $\Phi\circ h=\mathrm{id}$. This identifies
the exterior domain. Uniform boundary convergence and Hausdorff
convergence show $h(\T)=\partial K$. Since $\partial K$ is a Jordan
curve, the Jordan boundary theorem makes this extension a homeomorphism.
In particular boundary injectivity is not inferred from uniform
convergence alone.

Local holomorphic convergence identifies the exterior derivative with
$h'$, and Hardy coefficient uniqueness identifies its boundary trace
with $ce^\ell$. Equivalently one may integrate on exterior circles
and take their $L^2$ boundary limits. Passing the inequalities
$\Rea[\bar q_j(\lambda)(h_j(\lambda)-w)]\geq0$ on an almost-everywhere
convergent subsequence, first for a countable dense subset of $K$ and
then for all $w\in K$, makes $q$ an outward normal almost everywhere.

Finally \eqref{eq:interior-spectrum} gives a compact neighborhood of
$\partial K$ on which the actual fixed resolvent $(zI-B)^{-1}$ is
bounded. Uniform convergence of $h_j$ gives uniform convergence of
these resolvents; the derivative convergence proves
\eqref{eq:R-convergence} and the common boundedness. Positivity of
$H_j$ passes on an almost-everywhere subsequence. This uses no
resolvent estimate in terms of spectral distance alone.
\end{proof}

\section{Equality residuals and constant diagonal fields}\label{sec:residual}
Use subscripts $j$ on the fields $P_j,Z_j,M_j,L_j$ defined in
Section~\ref{sec:framework}. Put $S=X^*X$, $T=Y^*Y$,
$t_j=\max_{\overline\Omega_j}\norm F\to1$, and $\gamma_j=2/t_j\to2$.
Apply the source tests to $F/t_j$ with the fixed pair \eqref{eq:vec}.
All compressed fields converge strongly in $L^2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:outer};
therefore all their Fourier arrays converge strongly in $\ell^2$.
Set
\[
 a_j^2=\norm{M_j}_2^2\to a^2,\quad
 D_{Xj}=\norm{P_j+P_j^*}_2^2,\quad D_{Yj}=\norm{Z_j+Z_j^*}_2^2.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:residual}
There are nonnegative residuals
\begin{align}
 E_{Xj}&=4a_j^2/\gamma_j^2-D_{Xj},&
 E_{Yj}&=4a_j^2/\gamma_j^2-D_{Yj},\notag\\
 E_{Bj}&=D_{Xj}+D_{Yj}-2\norm{M_j+L_j^*}_2^2,&
 Z^{\rm res}_j&=3\norm{Y^*X}_{\HS}^2+
 \sum_{k\geq1}\norm{X^*b_{kj}(B)Y}_{\HS}^2,\label{eq:residuals}
\end{align}
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualsum}
 E_{Xj}+E_{Yj}+E_{Bj}+2Z^{\rm res}_j
 =2(4/\gamma_j^2-1)a_j^2\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Consequently $Y^*X=0$, $L=0$, $a>0$, and, almost everywhere,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isotropic}
 \Delta J_0\Delta=0,\quad
 \Delta=\begin{pmatrix}P+P^*&M^*\\M&Z+Z^*\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
 \quad J_0=\diag(I_m,-I_m).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The weighted finite dual tests in \eqref{eq:ordered-bound} and the
upper bound \eqref{eq:coefficient-bound} give
\[
 \norm S_{\HS}^2+\tfrac12\sum_{k\geq1}\norm{X^*b_{kj}(B)X}_{\HS}^2
 \leq a_j^2/\gamma_j^2,
\]
and the same assertion for $Y,T$. Parseval gives $D_{Xj}$ as four
times the displayed left side, so $E_{Xj},E_{Yj}\geq0$.
The positive compression
$\Delta_j=[X\ Y]^*H_j[X\ Y]$ has lower off-diagonal block
$M_j+L_j^*$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace-slack}
 0\leq\tr(\Delta_jJ_0\Delta_jJ_0)
 =\norm{\Delta_j^{1/2}J_0\Delta_j^{1/2}}_{\HS}^2
\end{equation}
proves $E_{Bj}\geq0$ after integration. The constant coefficient of
$M_j+L_j^*$ is $2Y^*X$. Its other coefficients are $M_{kj}$ and
$L_{kj}^*$ at opposite frequencies. Hence
\[
 \norm{M_j+L_j^*}_2^2=a_j^2+Z^{\rm res}_j.
\]
Adding gives \eqref{eq:residualsum}. The right side tends to zero
because $a_j$ is bounded. Every residual therefore tends to zero,
including $Y^*X=0$ and $\norm{L_j}_2\to0$. Strong convergence gives
$L=0$. Also $a_j^2/\gamma_j^2\geq\norm S_{\HS}^2>0$, so $a>0$.

For the quadratic passage, expand
\[
 \Delta_jJ_0\Delta_jJ_0-\Delta J_0\Delta J_0
 =(\Delta_j-\Delta)J_0\Delta_jJ_0+
 \Delta J_0(\Delta_j-\Delta)J_0.
\]
Strong $L^2$ convergence and trace Cauchy--Schwarz give convergence
in $L^1$. The limiting nonnegative trace has integral zero by
$E_{Bj}\to0$. Thus $\Delta^{1/2}J_0\Delta^{1/2}=0$ almost everywhere,
and multiplying by $\Delta^{1/2}$ on both sides gives
\eqref{eq:isotropic}. No convergence of matrix square roots is needed.
\end{proof}

We now retain the deficit that the non-strict coefficient bound discards.
Write $A_{kj}=X^*b_{kj}(B)X$ and let $\mathcal T_j$ be the kernel
operator \eqref{eq:kernel} for $h_j$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite diagonal test with its deficit]\label{lem:finite}
For each fixed $j$ and finite $N$, set
\begin{align*}
 C_0&=\overline S,& C_k&=\tfrac12\overline{A_{kj}}\ (1\leq k\leq N),
 &G_{jN}&=\sum_{k=0}^N b_{kj}C_k,\\
 u_{jN}(\lambda)&=\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^N\overline{A_{kj}}\lambda^k,
 &d_{jN}&=\norm S_{\HS}^2+\tfrac12\sum_{k=1}^N\norm{A_{kj}}_{\HS}^2,
 &\delta_{jN}&=\norm{u_{jN}}_2^2-\norm{\mathcal T_ju_{jN}}_2^2.
\end{align*}
Then $\delta_{jN}\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-deficit}
 \norm{G_{jN}\circ h_j}_2^2=d_{jN}-\delta_{jN},\qquad
 \gamma_j^2d_{jN}^2\leq a_j^2(d_{jN}-\delta_{jN}).
\end{equation}
At fixed $j$, let $N\to\infty$. The limits $u_j,d_j,\delta_j$ exist
in the corresponding $L^2$ or scalar senses, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficit-zero}
 0\leq\delta_j\leq d_j-\gamma_j^2d_j^2/a_j^2\longrightarrow0,
 \quad d_j=D_{Xj}/4\to a^2/4,\quad
 u_j=\tfrac12\overline{P_j-S}\to u=\tfrac12\overline{P-S}\text{ in }L^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The three disjoint Fourier pieces \eqref{eq:decomposition} have squared
norm $\norm S_{\HS}^2+\norm{u_{jN}}_2^2+
\norm{\mathcal T_ju_{jN}}_2^2=d_{jN}-\delta_{jN}$.
The paired diagonal value $x^*G_{jN}[B]x$ is exactly $d_{jN}$ by
the bilinear pairing, and \eqref{eq:ordered-bound} proves the second
inequality. Contractivity gives nonnegativity of the deficit.
At fixed $j$, square summability of $A_{kj}$ gives $u_{jN}\to u_j$;
contractivity gives convergence of its image and of the deficits.
Taking $N\to\infty$ yields the displayed bound. Lemma~\ref{lem:residual}
gives $D_{Xj}\to a^2$ and $\gamma_j\to2$, so its right side tends
to zero. Strong convergence of $P_j$ gives the last assertion.
Only finite polynomials have been inserted in the ordered tests.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hilbert-valued triple variance]\label{lem:variance}
Let $\kappa_j\geq0$ have both angular marginals one. For a field $u_j$
with values in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and its integral
operator $\mathcal T_j$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triple}
 2\bigl(\norm{u_j}_2^2-\norm{\mathcal T_ju_j}_2^2\bigr)
 =\iiint_{\T^3}\kappa_j(\lambda,\mu)\kappa_j(\nu,\mu)
 \norm{u_j(\lambda)-u_j(\nu)}^2\,d\tau(\lambda)d\tau(\nu)d\tau(\mu).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the squared difference. For fixed $\mu$, the two squared-norm
terms each integrate to $\int\kappa_j(\lambda,\mu)\norm{u_j(\lambda)}^2
 d\tau(\lambda)$ because the $\nu$ marginal is one. The cross term
is $-2\norm{\mathcal T_ju_j(\mu)}^2$. Integrating in $\mu$ and using
the other marginal gives \eqref{eq:triple}. Truncation or Tonelli
justifies the expansion for $L^2$ fields. The factor two is retained.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Constant diagonal fields at arbitrary rank]\label{prop:constant}
The limiting fields satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant}
 X^*R(\lambda)X=S,\qquad Y^*R(\lambda)Y=T
 \quad\text{for almost every }\lambda\in\T.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Select a common subsequence with $q_j\to q$ and $u_j\to u$ almost
everywhere. Uniform convergence of $h_j$ and injectivity of
$h|_\T$ imply, away from null diagonals and derivative exceptional sets,
\[
 \kappa_j(\lambda,\mu)\longrightarrow
 \kappa(\lambda,\mu)=2\Rea\frac{q(\lambda)}{h(\lambda)-h(\mu)}\geq0.
\]
Apply Fatou to the nonnegative triple integrand in \eqref{eq:triple}.
By \eqref{eq:deficit-zero},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fatou}
 \kappa(\lambda,\mu)\kappa(\nu,\mu)
 \norm{u(\lambda)-u(\nu)}_{\HS}^2=0
 \quad\text{for almost every triple}.
\end{equation}
No marginal identity for the limit kernel is asserted. No uniform
strict-contraction constant or operator-norm kernel convergence is used.

Jin \cite[Lemma 8]{Jin}, applied using \eqref{eq:interior-spectrum},
supplies a nonempty open regular analytic boundary arc of positive
curvature with singleton exposed faces. Shrink it so that each point
lies on no nontrivial boundary segment. Its preimage under the boundary
homeomorphism is a nonempty open circle interval $I$, with positive
Haar measure. For $\mu\in I$ and almost every $\lambda\ne\mu$, the
nonzero outward normal $q(\lambda)$ has strictly positive supporting
gap against $h(\mu)$. A zero gap would place the two points on the
same support line, making their segment a boundary segment containing
$h(\mu)$. Therefore $\kappa(\lambda,\mu)>0$ for these pairs. Fubini
permits a choice of $\mu\in I$ for which \eqref{eq:fatou} holds for
almost every $(\lambda,\nu)$. It follows that $u$ is constant almost
everywhere. Its mean is zero, so $u=0$ and $P=S$. Repeating
Lemmas~\ref{lem:finite}--\ref{lem:variance} and this argument with
the second ordered test, $Y$, and $Z_j$ gives $Z=T$.
\end{proof}

\section{Rational cross field and the fixed coefficient channel}\label{sec:channel}
Define rational functions in the physical variable by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational}
 C(z)=Y^*(zI-B)^{-1}X,\qquad
 r_0(z)=\tr[X^*(zI-B)^{-1}X].
\end{equation}
Since $\tr S=1$, \eqref{eq:constant} gives $q\,r_0\circ h=1$ almost
everywhere. The compressed exterior resolvent is bounded and holomorphic,
including at infinity. Hardy uniqueness in the reciprocal disk extends
its constant boundary identity throughout the exterior. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-rational}
 q(\lambda)=1/r_0(h(\lambda)),\qquad
 M(\lambda)=\mathcal M(h(\lambda)),\quad \mathcal M(z)=C(z)/r_0(z).
\end{equation}

We check nonvanishing of the denominator in both the open exterior and
on the boundary. For $z\notin K$, strict separation supplies a unit
$\nu$ such that $D=\bar\nu(zI-B)$ has positive definite real part.
Then
\[
 \Rea(D^{-1})=D^{-*}(\Rea D)D^{-1}\succ0,\qquad
 D^{-1}=\nu(zI-B)^{-1}.
\]
As $XX^*$ is nonzero and positive,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r-nonzero}
 \Rea[\nu r_0(z)]=\tr(XX^*\Rea D^{-1})>0.
\end{equation}
Thus there is no exterior zero. By spectral separation $r_0$ is
continuous near $\partial K$. The boundary identity and $|q|\leq4R_0$
give $|r_0\circ h|\geq(4R_0)^{-1}$ almost everywhere on $\T$. This
continuous function satisfies the same inequality everywhere: failure
at one point would give failure on an open circle interval, of positive
Haar measure. The boundary homeomorphism transfers the bound to all
of $\partial K$. This uses full angular support, without assuming an
unproved equivalence with arclength measure.

It follows that $\mathcal M$ is rational, holomorphic outside $K$ and
in a neighborhood of its boundary, and continuous up to the boundary.
Since $Y^*X=0$, $C(z)=O(z^{-2})$ and
$r_0(z)=z^{-1}+O(z^{-2})$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-infty}
 \mathcal M(z)=O(z^{-1})\quad(z\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Now \eqref{eq:isotropic} and \eqref{eq:constant} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
 \Delta=\begin{pmatrix}2S&M^*\\M&2T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M^*M=4S^2,\quad MM^*=4T^2,\quad MS=TM.
\end{equation}
In particular $S,T$ have the same positive eigenvalues and equal
positive rank $r$; $\ker M=\ker S$ and $\ran M=\ran T$ almost everywhere.

\begin{lemma}[The ordered constant test]\label{lem:constant-test}
On the boundary, almost everywhere,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordered-constant}
 F(h)\overline S=\tfrac12\overline M.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $s=\norm S_{\HS}>0$. Constancy of $P$ gives
$D_{Xj}\to4s^2$, so the vanished residual yields $a=2s$.
Use the constant polynomial $G=\overline S$ in the first identity
\eqref{eq:ordered}. The Faber coefficients of $(F/t_j)\overline S$
are finite, of degree at most $D=\deg F$. They converge to the
nonnegative Fourier coefficients $U_k$ of $F(h)\overline S$:
this follows from uniform boundary convergence, since the nonnegative
part of each Faber polynomial is exactly its leading circle power.
Thus $U_k=0$ for $k>D$. Passing the finite pairing \eqref{eq:pairing}
gives the positive real identity
\[
 2s^2=\sum_{k\geq0,i,\ell}(M_k)_{i\ell}(U_k)_{i\ell}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz, Parseval, and $\norm{F(h)}\leq1$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-chain}
 2s^2\leq a\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq0}\norm{U_k}_{\HS}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}
 \leq a\norm{F(h)\overline S}_2\leq as=2s^2.
\end{equation}
Every step is equality. Equality in the bilinear coefficient
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, with the positive real phase, gives
$U_k=\overline{M_k}/2$ for every $k$, including $M_k=0$ above degree
$D$. Equality in the Parseval step removes the entire negative Fourier
part of $F(h)\overline S$. Finally $M_0=Y^*X=0$. Combining the full
Fourier expansions proves \eqref{eq:ordered-constant}. The order
$F\overline S$, entrywise conjugation, and factor $1/2$ follow from
the stated pairing; none can be replaced by an adjoint or a reversed
product.
\end{proof}

Take $E=\ran\overline S$, $G=\ran\overline T$ and fixed isometries
$U:\C^r\to E$, $V:\C^r\to G$. Equations
\eqref{eq:gram}--\eqref{eq:ordered-constant} give
\[
 (F(h)\overline S)^*(F(h)\overline S)
 =\tfrac14M^{\mathsf T}\overline M=\overline S^{\,2}.
\]
Thus $F(h)$ is isometric on $E$ and maps it into $G$ almost everywhere.
The polynomial $(I-VV^*)F(z)U$ vanishes at infinitely many distinct
boundary points, hence vanishes identically. Therefore
$J=V^*FU$ is a square polynomial, unitary almost everywhere on the
boundary, and everywhere there by continuity and full angular support.

Set $S_E=U^*\overline S U\succ0$. The restriction calculation is
\begin{align}
 \overline S U&=US_E,\notag\\
 J(h)S_E&=V^*F(h)\overline S U
          =\tfrac12V^*\overline M U,\label{eq:restriction}\\
 S_EJ(h)^*&=\tfrac12U^*M^{\mathsf T}V.\notag
\end{align}
Consequently the rational matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 Q(z)=\tfrac12S_E^{-1}U^*\mathcal M(z)^{\mathsf T}V
\end{equation}
is holomorphic outside $K$, continuous at its boundary, and
$Q(\infty)=0$. The transpose in \eqref{eq:Q} is ordinary transpose,
not adjoint. The inverse Gram factor multiplies on the left.
On the boundary $Q=J^*$, so $JQ=I$. Uniqueness of rational identities
on infinitely many boundary points gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 J(z)Q(z)=I
\end{equation}
identically as a rational identity. In particular $Q=J^{-1}$ throughout
the exterior. This proves the fixed-channel and inverse assertions
of Theorem~\ref{thm:channel}.

\section{Full determinant level, geometry, and restoration}\label{sec:geometry}
For unit vectors $u,v$, apply the exterior scalar maximum principle to
$u^*Qv$. It is bounded by one on the boundary and zero at infinity.
Thus $\norm Q\leq1$ outside $K$. If equality held at an exterior
point, choose norming $u,v$ there; the scalar maximum principle would
make $u^*Qv$ constant of modulus one, contradicting its value at infinity.
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-inverse}
 \norm{Q(z)}<1\quad(z\notin K).
\end{equation}
Let $f=\det J$. This is a nonconstant polynomial: from
\eqref{eq:inverse}, $\det J\det Q=1$, whereas $Q(\infty)=0$ implies
$\det Q\to0$. In the interior, the matrix maximum principle gives
$\norm J\leq1$, and hence $|f|\leq1$; nonconstancy and the scalar
maximum principle make this strict. On the boundary $|f|=1$.
Outside, \eqref{eq:strict-inverse} means every singular value of $J$
is greater than one, so $|f|>1$. We have proved the full identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-level}
 \{z\in\C:|f(z)|<1\}=\Int K,\qquad
 \{z\in\C:|f(z)|=1\}=\partial K.
\end{equation}
This proves the remaining assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:channel}.
It explicitly excludes any additional level components in the exterior.

We verify regularity before applying the geometric input. If
$f'(z_0)=0$ at a boundary point, write
$f(z)-f(z_0)=(z-z_0)^k g(z)$ with $k\geq2$, $g(z_0)\ne0$.
Choose nonzero $\alpha$ so
$\Rea(\overline{f(z_0)}\alpha^kg(z_0))<0$.
For all sufficiently small $t>0$, the $k$ points
$z_0+t\alpha e^{2\pi i\ell/k}$, $0\leq\ell<k$, have $|f|<1$.
By \eqref{eq:full-level} they lie in $\Int K$. Their average is $z_0$,
contradicting convexity of the interior. Hence $f'\ne0$ on all of
$\partial K$, which is smooth real analytic by the implicit function
theorem. If it contained a nontrivial segment, the real polynomial
$|f(z_0+t\xi)|^2-1$ would vanish on an interval and hence on the entire
real line. This contradicts compactness of the full level
\eqref{eq:full-level}. Thus $K$ is strictly convex.

Here is the exact credited circle criterion
\cite[Proposition 6]{Jin}: if a compact strictly convex body has boundary
\[
 \partial K=\{z:V_0(z)\ne0,\ |U_0(z)/V_0(z)|=1\},
\]
for coprime polynomials with $\deg U_0>\deg V_0$, with
$(U_0/V_0)'\ne0$ on that boundary, and if the tangents to an open
boundary arc lie in a Hermitian determinant curve
$\det(sI+uH_0+vL_0)=0$ in line coordinates $s+uX+vY=0$, then the
boundary is a circle. Its projective-dual contact argument, including
Jin's Proposition~5, is credited geometry, not an additional theorem
claimed here.

Apply it with $U_0=f$, $V_0=1$. All its level, degree, regularity,
and strict-convexity hypotheses have just been checked. For the remaining
hypothesis take
\[
 H_0=\Rea B,\qquad L_0=\Ima B.
\]
Every tangent to the smooth convex boundary is a support line. Orient
$s+uX+vY=0$ so $sI+uH_0+vL_0\succeq0$. A unit vector attaining a
point on that line has zero quadratic form for this pencil and lies
in its kernel. Consequently every such tangent satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
 \det(sI+u\Rea B+v\Ima B)=0.
\end{equation}
This pays the Hermitian determinant hypothesis. A convex polynomial
lemniscate alone would not pay it. The credited criterion now gives
$K=a_0+\rho_0\overline\D$ with $\rho_0>0$.

It remains to recover all reducing summands, not just the active block.
The polynomial $g(z)=f(a_0+\rho_0z)$ has modulus one on $\T$. Its
Laurent identity
\[
 g(z)\overline{g(1/\bar z)}=1\quad(z\ne0)
\]
follows from equality on the circle. It forbids any nonzero root.
The fundamental theorem of algebra therefore gives
$g(z)=\eta z^k$, $|\eta|=1$, $k\geq1$. For every $z\in W(A)$,
\[
 \norm{J(z)}\leq\norm{F(z)}\leq1,\qquad |f(z)|\leq1.
\]
The monomial identity forces $W(A)\subset a_0+\rho_0\overline\D$;
$W(B)\subset W(A)$ gives the reverse inclusion. Restoring the initial
normalization proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Scope, antecedents, and verification}\label{sec:scope}
The result concerns all finite matrix and coefficient dimensions, but
only actual polynomial equality with positive maximum. It gives no
uniform strict gap on nondisks, stability estimate, rational or arbitrary
holomorphic witness extension, nonattained-supremum conclusion, or
classification of all equality witnesses.

Jin's scalar equality theorem is all-dimensional, while the complete
constant comparison of \AA hag--Czy\.z--Virtanen
\cite[Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 12.4]{ACV} gives complete disk rigidity
through base dimension three. Their scalar polynomial classification
in Theorem 12.2 is also credited prior work. These statements do not
provide the arbitrary-rank channel extracted here.

Earlier project result R016 proves specified-witness scalarization and
disk rigidity through base dimension four. The campaign's dimension-five
extension is a credited technical antecedent. Their dimension-independent
outer convergence and residual mechanism are reproduced in
Sections~\ref{sec:outer}--\ref{sec:residual}; their later rank-one
argument is not used. Earlier result R019 gives an attained rank-two
matrix polynomial witness on a six-dimensional disk matrix for which
no constant scalar coefficient compression attains two. It remains
compatible with the present theorem. These antecedents are not new
results of this paper. Their exact project records and hashes are
listed in the accompanying provenance and source map.

The contribution asserted for review is the deficit-to-constant-field
bridge and its fixed matrix-channel determinant continuation, relative
to the credited inputs. This is not a worldwide priority assertion.
Independent proof and contribution reviews of the frozen discovery
argument have respectively returned PASS and SUBSTANTIAL, with bounded
