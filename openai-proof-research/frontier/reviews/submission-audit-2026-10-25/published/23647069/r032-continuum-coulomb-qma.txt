              Quantum veriﬁcation of full-continuum
                    Coulomb energy with unit nuclei
                                            Myeongjin Kang

                                            October 17, 2026


                                                 Abstract
         We prove QMA membership for the electronic Coulomb spectral-inﬁmum problem with an
     explicitly listed set of distinct rational unit-charge nuclei, a unary number of spin-1/2 electrons,
     and inverse-input-length threshold separation. The minimization is over the full antisymmetric
     continuum space. No binding, attained ground state, nuclear separation, physical diameter,
     supplied orbital basis, or state-preparation promise is imposed. An IMS decomposition over
     all inner particle numbers, followed by exterior fermionic packets, gives one ﬁnite box even
     for unbound inﬁma. Hardy estimates control a Coulomb cap, and bounded-potential spectral
     compression gives a physical momentum cutoﬀ. Exact shifted sampling on the retained band
     supplies a uniform potential-form estimate without a conﬁguration-space volume factor. Explicit
     symmetry and cutoﬀ projections then give soundness for arbitrary quantum witnesses. Together
     with the stipulated unit-charge continuum hardness theorem of OpenAI, this proves QMA-
     completeness for the same promise problem.

    The source theorem is used for hardness only and is taken as a premise at repository revision
fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb. Membership is proved below. Neither statement
assumes neutrality, strict binding, an eigenvector at the inﬁmum, an excitation gap, a selected spin
sector, a magnetic ﬁeld, conﬁnement, or an orbital basis. Nuclear positions may be exponentially
large or exponentially close, subject only to their binary descriptions and distinctness. A witness
need not be eﬃciently preparable. The result does not include exponentially large binary nuclear
charges; the companion hardness theorem [2] has that diﬀerent input class.

Closest upper bounds. O’Gorman, Irani, Whitﬁeld and Feﬀerman prove QMA-completeness
with a supplied ﬁnite orbital basis [3]. Their published Appendix D, Corollary 1, adds positive
unit nuclei while retaining that basis; Section V distinguishes the complete-space question. Matrix-
element estimates inside that basis do not give the input-uniform continuum comparison needed
here. Zheng, Leng, Liu and Wu prove StoqMA membership for smooth few-body potentials with
polynomially bounded C 1 norm on Dirichlet boxes whose side lengths are polynomially bounded
above and below [4, Theorem 3.3]. Their proof uses ﬁnite diﬀerences and a unary coordinate
embedding. This pays the bounded smooth-box problem. Direct rescaling of an exponentially
long box changes the derivative and kinetic scales, and their scalar minimum does not impose the
fermionic sector.
    Real-space simulation provides further antecedents. Kivlichan et al. control Fourier truncation
and sampled norms, with general estimates containing a supremum-to-volume factor and a stronger
derivative premise for their polynomial-spacing corollary [5, Theorem 4, Corollary 5, Lemmas 9–15].
Childs et al. use Fourier-tail estimates with a high-derivative parameter; their Coulomb discussion
uses a periodic box and a softened interaction [6, Sections 2.2, 2.5 and Appendix B]. Schuch and
Verstraete’s Hubbard membership and designed-ﬁeld realization are earlier complexity antecedents
[7]. We do not substitute any of these dynamic or supplied-basis interfaces for a uniform spectral-
inﬁmum certiﬁcate.

The veriﬁcation interface. Hardy inequalities, IMS localization, spectral compression, Fourier
orthogonality, quantum Fourier transforms, and ﬁnite-group projections are standard tools. Sector-
valued localization for correlated states is classical: Lewin’s Proposition 3.1 and Example 3.3 ex-
plicitly construct localized Fock states by partial traces and retain every particle-number sector [8].
The ionization companion [9] credits that interface; its strict-binding and radius results are not used
here. Our contribution is the complete uniform conversion from the singular, unconﬁned fermionic
input to a ﬁnite-register veriﬁer, with both energy inequalities and all-witness soundness. The
comparison is bounded by these inspected primary interfaces, not a worldwide-priority assertion.

6    A uniform validity instrument
Each of the D spatial registers has log2 q qubits, and there are N spin qubits. The following ideal
projections have uniform polynomial-size approximations in a ﬁxed universal gate set.
    First apply the inverse Fourier transform, interpret each index as a signed residue in [−q/2, q/2),
and compare its absolute value with K. Reject on failure; uncompute the comparison and return to
position representation. Next measure every reﬂection involution j 7→ −j (mod q) by a controlled-
reﬂection test, accepting only its −1 eigenspace. The cutoﬀ and these odd projectors commute.
Their full product commutes with particle permutations because each particle has the same three
restrictions. Individual odd projectors need not commute with those permutations.
    To impose fermionic symmetry, use digits di ∈ {1, . . . , i} for i = 1, . . . , N . A permutation is
uniquely speciﬁed by inserting label i at position di in the previously ordered list of 1, . . . , i − 1.
Uniform independent digits therefore give a uniform permutation. Starting from the ordered register
blocks, the corresponding permutation can be implemented using at most N (N − 1)/2 controlled
adjacent swaps: at stage i, move block i left to slot di . A padded sequence tests, for each possible
swap position, whether it must be performed. Each test is a comparison on O(log N ) bits. The
           P
parity is i (i − di ) (mod 2), computable by reversible arithmetic. If one chooses the inverse
convention for the resulting permutation action, inversion preserves both the uniform distribution
and the sign. Each swapped block contains its three spatial registers and its spin qubit.

permutation and sign phase, then uncompute auxiliary classical data and reverse the digit prepa-
ration. Accept only if the digit ancilla returns to its initial zero state. The accepted operator

7     Arithmetic, energy testing and ampliﬁcation
The grid coordinates have polynomial binary length although their values and the grid size may be
exponential. Folding and translating require rational arithmetic of polynomial bit cost. For each
of the O(N M + N 2 ) kernels, compare the exact squared distance with ε2 . Below that threshold,
including exact coincidence, return ε−1 . Otherwise compute the inverse square root to the needed
absolute accuracy. One may bisect its value in [0, ε−1 ], testing a candidate using rational squared
comparisons. The number and bit cost of bisection steps are polynomial. Summing at suﬃciently
small per-term error evaluates the potential uniformly over all grid points.
    Kinetic values are also computable in polynomial time: indices have polynomial bit length, π 2 is
computable to the needed precision, and admitted physical frequencies are bounded by W . Oﬀ the
accepted band one may clamp the kinetic value to [0, Tmax ]; this keeps approximate instruments
bounded and does not change their action on A. There is no need to enumerate grid points or
evaluate an uncapped collision.

is impossible. Otherwise reject if a < −C or b ≤ −C, when a YES instance is impossible. Outside

    Repeat on an odd number r of registers, taking r to be the least odd integer at least 10γ −2 ,
and accept a majority. Tensor copies of a good witness give completeness at least 2/3 by the
Bernoulli tail bound. For soundness, let Bacc be the one-register acceptance POVM eﬀect. On a

largest eigenvalue by 1/3. This applies to entangled witnesses across the registers; no independence
promise is imposed on Merlin. The witness size and uniform circuit construction remain polynomial
in L. This proves Theorem 1, and [1, Theorem 1] gives Corollary 2.


8    Scope, provenance and formal support
The cap bound treats arbitrarily close nuclei. The binary grid handles exponentially distant ones.
The all-k localization and exterior wedge include ions, escaping electrons and unattained inﬁma.
Every spin sector is retained. The uniform form estimate and the actual projections prevent invalid
high-frequency, aliased or wrong-statistics witnesses from evading soundness. These are analytic
and circuit arguments, not numerical tests. The polynomial total charge is essential to the estimates
and follows from the explicit unit-nucleus list; no binary-large-charge extension or eﬃcient witness
preparation follows.
   The hardness premise is the exact theorem at the pinned revision stated in Section 1; it is
not re-proved here. The original scope and the earlier feasibility sketch were recorded separately
before completion of this proof. Independent internal proof and contribution reviews accepted
the unchanged scope; these are not external peer review or a worldwide-priority certiﬁcate. The
supporting record gives source hashes, actual primary reading limits, reproducible build instructions
and the exact correspondence with the reviewed proof.

Scoped formal support. The companion Lean development proves normalized ﬁnite-character
Parseval identities for an injective family of additive characters, preservation under speciﬁed unit-
modulus coeﬃcient phases, and the translation identity on a ﬁnite abelian group. It specializes to
characters of Z/qZ and proves injectivity of the centered residue modes under 2K + 1 ≤ q. Sepa-
rately, for a supplied complex representation of a ﬁnite group, it proves the formula, idempotence,
character-eigenvector range and commutation properties of the character-weighted average, includ-
ing the permutation-sign specialization. Scalar lemmas prove the total error bound from the four
assumed intermediate energy inequalities, the fair-test identity, quantitative YES/NO margins, and
soundness after a subnormalized weight and an assumed acceptance approximation error.
    These are supporting ﬁnite-algebraic and scalar statements. They do not formalize continuous
torus integration or the production of phases by real translations, the product-grid and spin re-
alization, the concrete particle representation, or Hilbert-space self-adjointness and orthogonality
of the average. Hardy/IMS localization, the exterior wedge, the Coulomb cap, operator-domain
and Ritz estimates, the Lipschitz quadrature argument, quantum instruments and their polynomial
circuit approximations, and entangled-witness ampliﬁcation remain in the human proof above. In
particular the assumed scalar energy and acceptance bounds are not Lean proofs of their analytic
or circuit hypotheses. Exact declarations, compiler/dependency versions, source hashes, controls
and coverage limits are recorded in the accompanying support ﬁles; no complete formalization of
Theorem 1 is claimed.
