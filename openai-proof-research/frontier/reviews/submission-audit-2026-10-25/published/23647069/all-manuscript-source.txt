\paragraph{Scoped formal support.}
The companion Lean development proves normalized finite-character Parseval
identities for an injective family of additive characters, preservation under
specified unit-modulus coefficient phases, and the translation identity on a
finite abelian group. It specializes to characters of $\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$
and proves injectivity of the centered residue modes under
$2K+1\leq q$. Separately, for a supplied complex representation of a finite
group, it proves the formula, idempotence, character-eigenvector range and
commutation properties of the character-weighted average, including the
permutation-sign specialization. Scalar lemmas prove the total error bound
from the four assumed intermediate energy inequalities, the fair-test
identity, quantitative YES/NO margins, and soundness after a subnormalized
weight and an assumed acceptance approximation error.

These are supporting finite-algebraic and scalar statements. They do not
formalize continuous torus integration or the production of phases by real
translations, the product-grid and spin realization, the concrete particle
representation, or Hilbert-space self-adjointness and orthogonality of the
average. Hardy/IMS localization, the exterior wedge, the Coulomb cap,
operator-domain and Ritz estimates, the Lipschitz quadrature argument,
quantum instruments and their polynomial circuit approximations, and
entangled-witness amplification remain in the human proof above. In
particular the assumed scalar energy and acceptance bounds are not Lean
proofs of their analytic or circuit hypotheses. Exact declarations,
compiler/dependency versions, source hashes, controls and coverage limits
are recorded in the accompanying support files; no complete formalization
of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is claimed.

\begin{abstract}
We prove QMA membership for the electronic Coulomb spectral-infimum problem
with an explicitly listed set of distinct rational unit-charge nuclei, a unary
number of spin-$1/2$ electrons, and inverse-input-length threshold separation.
The minimization is over the full antisymmetric continuum space. No binding,
attained ground state, nuclear separation, physical diameter, supplied orbital
basis, or state-preparation promise is imposed. An IMS decomposition over all
inner particle numbers, followed by exterior fermionic packets, gives one
finite box even for unbound infima. Hardy estimates control a Coulomb cap, and
bounded-potential spectral compression gives a physical momentum cutoff.
Exact shifted sampling on the retained band supplies a uniform potential-form
estimate without a configuration-space volume factor. Explicit symmetry and
cutoff projections then give soundness for arbitrary quantum witnesses.
Together with the stipulated unit-charge continuum hardness theorem of OpenAI,
this proves QMA-completeness for the same promise problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and antecedents}
\label{sec:statement}
An input lists $M\geq1$ pairwise distinct points
$R_1,\ldots,R_M\in\Q^3$, an integer $N\geq1$ in unary, and rationals $a<b$.
Every coordinate and threshold is encoded as a signed binary numerator and
positive binary denominator in lowest terms, with the fixed standard
self-delimiting encoding of \cite{unit}. Let $L$ be the total binary and unary
input length, and require $b-a\geq L^{-1}$. Every nucleus has charge one.
In atomic units the electronic Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H=-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N\Delta_{x_i}
 -\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{\alpha=1}^M\frac1{|x_i-R_\alpha|}
 +\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac1{|x_i-x_j|}.
\end{equation}
Its Hilbert space and form domain are
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal H_N&=\bigwedge^N\bigl(L^2(\R^3)\otimes\C^2\bigr),\\
 \mathcal Q_N&=H^1(\R^{3N};(\C^2)^{\otimes N})\cap\mathcal H_N.
\end{align*}
Antisymmetry exchanges spatial and spin coordinates together. We use the
self-adjoint operator of the closed, lower-bounded Coulomb form and set
$E=\inf\spec H$. Nuclear repulsion is omitted. The YES promise is $E\leq a$;
the NO promise is $E\geq b$. No requirement is imposed outside the promise.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The promise problem above belongs to QMA. The verification circuits and
witnesses have size polynomial in $L$, uniformly over all allowed inputs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:complete}
Assuming the unit-charge continuum hardness theorem \cite[Theorem 1]{unit},
the same promise problem is QMA-complete under deterministic classical
polynomial-time many-one reductions.
\end{corollary}

The source theorem is used for hardness only and is taken as a premise at
repository revision \texttt{fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb}.
Membership is proved below. Neither statement assumes neutrality, strict
binding, an eigenvector at the infimum, an excitation gap, a selected spin
sector, a magnetic field, confinement, or an orbital basis. Nuclear positions
may be exponentially large or exponentially close, subject only to their
binary descriptions and distinctness. A witness need not be efficiently
preparable. The result does not include exponentially large binary nuclear
charges; the companion hardness theorem \cite{binary} has that different
input class.

\paragraph{Closest upper bounds.}
O'Gorman, Irani, Whitfield and Fefferman prove QMA-completeness with a supplied
finite orbital basis \cite{ogorman}. Their published Appendix D, Corollary 1,
adds positive unit nuclei while retaining that basis; Section V distinguishes
the complete-space question. Matrix-element estimates inside that basis do
not give the input-uniform continuum comparison needed here.
Zheng, Leng, Liu and Wu prove StoqMA membership for smooth few-body potentials
with polynomially bounded $C^1$ norm on Dirichlet boxes whose side lengths are
polynomially bounded above and below \cite[Theorem 3.3]{zheng}. Their proof
uses finite differences and a unary coordinate embedding. This pays the
bounded smooth-box problem. Direct rescaling of an exponentially long box
changes the derivative and kinetic scales, and their scalar minimum does not
impose the fermionic sector.

Real-space simulation provides further antecedents. Kivlichan et al.\ control
Fourier truncation and sampled norms, with general estimates containing a
supremum-to-volume factor and a stronger derivative premise for their
polynomial-spacing corollary \cite[Theorem 4, Corollary 5, Lemmas 9--15]{kiv}.
Childs et al.\ use Fourier-tail estimates with a high-derivative parameter;
their Coulomb discussion uses a periodic box and a softened interaction
\cite[Sections 2.2, 2.5 and Appendix B]{childs}. Schuch and Verstraete's
Hubbard membership and designed-field realization are earlier complexity
antecedents \cite{sv}. We do not substitute any of these dynamic or
supplied-basis interfaces for a uniform spectral-infimum certificate.

\paragraph{The verification interface.}
Hardy inequalities, IMS localization, spectral compression, Fourier
orthogonality, quantum Fourier transforms, and finite-group projections are
standard tools. Sector-valued localization for correlated states is classical:
Lewin's Proposition 3.1 and Example 3.3 explicitly construct localized Fock
states by partial traces and retain every particle-number sector
\cite{lewin}. The ionization companion \cite{ionization} credits that
interface; its strict-binding and radius results are not used here. Our
contribution is the complete uniform conversion from the singular, unconfined
fermionic input to a finite-register verifier, with both energy inequalities
and all-witness soundness. The comparison is bounded by these inspected
primary interfaces, not a worldwide-priority assertion.

\section{Uniform form estimates and the energy range}
\label{sec:forms}
Throughout, norms and integrals sum over spin indices. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base}
 T[\psi]=\frac12\sum_i\norm{\nabla_i\psi}_2^2,
 \qquad C=4NM^2,\qquad g=b-a,\qquad
 \eta=\min\{1,g/100\},\qquad D=3N.
\end{equation}
We have $M,N\leq L$ and $\eta^{-1}\leq100L$.

Three-dimensional Hardy, with the other coordinates fixed, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hardy}
 \int\frac{|\psi|^2}{|x_i-y|^2}\leq4\norm{\nabla_i\psi}_2^2.
\end{equation}
The center $y$ may be a nucleus or another particle coordinate. Integrating
all remaining variables preserves the constant. For normalized $\psi$,
Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequality imply
\begin{align}
 \sum_{i,\alpha}\int\frac{|\psi|^2}{|x_i-R_\alpha|}
 &\leq2M\sum_i\norm{\nabla_i\psi}_2
 \leq2\sqrt2 M\sqrt N\sqrt{T[\psi]}
 \leq\tfrac12T[\psi]+C.\label{eq:attraction}
\end{align}
Repulsion is nonnegative, so the Coulomb form $q$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive}
 q[\psi]\geq\tfrac12T[\psi]-C\norm\psi_2^2.
\end{equation}
The version of Young's inequality with an arbitrary positive kinetic
coefficient proves infinitesimal form bounds for all the finitely many
Coulomb terms. It justifies the closed-form convention in
Section~\ref{sec:statement}. The same estimates hold on Dirichlet open
subsets by zero extension.

In particular $E\geq-C$. To see $E\leq0$, choose $N$ normalized smooth
packets of width $R$ with disjoint supports separated by at least $R^2$.
Their normalized wedge, with any chosen spin labels, has kinetic energy
$O(N/R^2)$ and repulsion $O(N^2/R^2)$. Attraction is nonpositive. Letting
$R\to\infty$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:range}
 -C\leq E\leq0.
\end{equation}
No minimizing vector is required.

For $\varepsilon>0$ set
$v_\varepsilon(r)=\min\{r^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1}\}$, including
$v_\varepsilon(0)=\varepsilon^{-1}$, and cap every attractive and repulsive
kernel in $q$ to obtain $q_\varepsilon$. Since
$0\leq r^{-1}-v_\varepsilon(r)\leq\varepsilon/r^2$, Hardy gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capform}
 |q_\varepsilon[\psi]-q[\psi]|
 \leq8\varepsilon(M+N)T[\psi].
\end{equation}
For a pair $i<j$, use the $i$ coordinate in \eqref{eq:hardy}; each coordinate
is counted at most $N-1$ times. The cap reduces attraction and keeps
repulsion nonnegative, so \eqref{eq:coercive} also holds for $q_\varepsilon$.
No separation parameter enters either estimate.

\section{One finite box, including escaping electrons}
\label{sec:box}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boxchoices}
 A_0=1+\max_\alpha\norm{R_\alpha}_\infty,\quad R_0=2A_0,
 \quad s=\left\lceil\frac{100(NM+N^2+N+1)}\eta\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
All nuclei lie in the ball of radius $R_0$. Choose a Lipschitz function
$\theta(r)$ equal to $0$ for $r\leq R_0+s$ and to $\pi/2$ for
$r\geq R_0+2s$, with $|\theta'|\leq2/s$. Let
$\chi_{\rm in}(x)=\cos\theta(|x|)$ and
$\chi_{\rm out}(x)=\sin\theta(|x|)$. Their squares sum to one and their
squared gradients sum to at most $4/s^2$.

For $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ put
\[
 \psi_S=\prod_{i\in S}\chi_{\rm in}(x_i)
          \prod_{i\notin S}\chi_{\rm out}(x_i)\psi.
\]
The product partition has $\sum_S\norm{\psi_S}_2^2=\norm\psi_2^2$.
The IMS identity, with kinetic coefficient $1/2$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IMS}
 q[\psi]\geq\sum_S q[\psi_S]-\frac{2N}{s^2}\norm\psi_2^2.
\end{equation}
Lipschitz cutoffs suffice for this form identity; they can also be smoothly
approximated while retaining the bounds. This use of all sectors is the
classical localization mechanism discussed in \cite{lewin}.

Let $E_k^{\rm ball}$ be the $k$-electron Coulomb Dirichlet infimum, with the
same nuclei, in $B(0,R_0+2s)$, and set $E_0^{\rm ball}=0$. For a fixed $S$,
fixing the outside positions and spins leaves, for almost every slice, an
$H^1_0$ spinor antisymmetric among the $|S|$ inside particles. The common
inside cutoff preserves this antisymmetry and vanishes at the ball boundary.
After normalizing a nonzero slice, its internal form is at least
$E_{|S|}^{\rm ball}$; multiplying by its squared norm and integrating gives
the same bound for the original unnormalized slice. Full antisymmetry across
an inside and an outside label is not needed in this step.

Every outside electron is at distance at least $s$ from every nucleus.
Discarding its nonnegative kinetic energy and all repulsions involving it
therefore gives
\[
 q[\psi_S]\geq\left(E_{|S|}^{\rm ball}-NM/s\right)\norm{\psi_S}_2^2.
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:IMS} and taking arbitrary near-infima proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allk}
 \min_{0\leq k\leq N}E_k^{\rm ball}
 \leq E+\frac{NM}s+\frac{2N}{s^2}.
\end{equation}
In particular the argument retains the vacuum sector and unbound states.

Conversely fix $k$ and a normalized near-minimizer for $E_k^{\rm ball}$.
Add $N-k$ packets supported in cubes of side $s$, centered at
\[
 c_j=(R_0+2s+(4j+2)s,0,0),\qquad 1\leq j\leq N-k.
\]
Each packet is the product of the three normalized first Dirichlet sine
modes, extended by zero, with spin up. Its kinetic energy is
$3\pi^2/(2s^2)<15/s^2$. All new supports are separated from the inner ball
and from one another by at least $s$.

Wedge these packets with the arbitrary correlated inner $k$-particle state.
This is an isometry because their one-particle support spaces are
orthogonal. More explicitly, expansion of the antisymmetrization gives
disjoint assignments of coordinate labels to support regions; their
normalization factors cancel. The same expansion preserves all internal
energy expectations. Every new repulsive pair costs at most $1/s$, and
there are at most $N^2/2$ of them. Added attraction is nonpositive.

Let $B$ be the least power of two no smaller than
$\lceil R_0+(4N+6)s\rceil$, and set $Q=(-B,B)^3$. Every support just used
lies inside $Q$. If $E_Q$ is the $N$-particle Dirichlet infimum in $Q$,
zero extension and the preceding construction give
\begin{align}
 E\leq E_Q
 &\leq\min_k E_k^{\rm ball}+\frac{15N}{s^2}+\frac{N^2}{2s}
 \leq E+\eta.\label{eq:boxerror}
\end{align}
Indeed $NM/s+N^2/(2s)+17N/s^2<\eta$ by \eqref{eq:boxchoices}.
No algorithm must identify the minimizing $k$: it proves existence of a
witness with exactly $N$ particles. Both $k=0$ and $k=N$ are legal.
Although $B$ can be exponential, $\log B=O(L+\log s+\log N)$.

\section{Capping and physical momentum compression}
\label{sec:compression}
On $Q$ choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
 \varepsilon=\frac{\eta}{128(M+N)(C+2)},\qquad
 \alpha=8\varepsilon(M+N)=\frac{\eta}{16(C+2)}.
\end{equation}
Write $E_\varepsilon$ for the capped antisymmetric Dirichlet infimum.
An uncapped near-minimizer has
$T\leq2(C+\eta+o(1))$ by \eqref{eq:coercive} and \eqref{eq:boxerror}.
Its capped energy thus proves
$-C\leq E_\varepsilon\leq E_Q+\eta/8\leq2$.
Conversely a capped near-minimizer has $T\leq2(C+2+o(1))$.
Using \eqref{eq:capform} in the reverse direction and taking limits yields,
with slack,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capenergy}
 |E_\varepsilon-E_Q|\leq\eta/4.
\end{equation}
This explicitly pays for capping repulsion as well as attraction.

The capped multiplication potential $V_\varepsilon$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VGJ}
 \norm{V_\varepsilon}_\infty\leq B_V:=\frac{N(M+N)}\varepsilon,
 \qquad \operatorname{Lip}(V_\varepsilon)\leq
 G:=\frac{2N(M+N)}{\varepsilon^2},\qquad J=C+2+B_V.
\end{equation}
A nuclear term has Lipschitz constant at most $\varepsilon^{-2}$ and a pair
term at most $\sqrt2\varepsilon^{-2}$ in all $D$ coordinates; summing proves
the bound. It is independent of nuclear clustering.

The Dirichlet kinetic operator $T_Q$ has compact resolvent. A bounded
self-adjoint potential preserves its operator domain and compact resolvent.
Particle permutations commute with both terms, so this remains true on
the antisymmetric subspace. There is a normalized ground vector $\psi$ for
the capped operator, in $\Dom(T_Q)$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tnorm}
 \norm{T_Q\psi}_2\leq |E_\varepsilon|+B_V\leq J.
\end{equation}
No analytic regularity of an uncapped Coulomb eigenfunction is assumed.

Set $\ell=2B$, translate the cube to $(0,\ell)^3$, and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
 P=\lceil64J/\eta\rceil,\qquad K=\ell P.
\end{equation}
Let $F$ keep the product sine modes with $1\leq n_j\leq K$ in every scalar
coordinate, with all spin indices retained. The same cutoff in every
particle coordinate commutes with permutations. For $p=F\psi$ and
$r=(1-F)\psi$, an omitted mode has kinetic eigenvalue at least $P^2/2$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:Tnorm} therefore implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
 \norm r_2^2\leq\frac{4J^2}{P^4},\qquad
 \ip r{T_Qr}\leq\frac{2J^2}{P^2}.
\end{equation}
Since $(H_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)\psi=0$,
\begin{align*}
 \ip p{(H_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)p}
 &=\ip r{(H_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)r}
 \leq\frac{2J^2}{P^2}+\frac{4J^3}{P^4}.
\end{align*}
Here $J\geq1$, $P^2\geq8J$, $\norm p_2^2\geq3/4$, and
$4J^2/P^2\leq\eta/8$. Normalizing $p$ and using the variational lower
bound for every retained vector gives the compressed minimum $E_F$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ritz}
 E_\varepsilon\leq E_F\leq E_\varepsilon+\eta/8.
\end{equation}
The absolute scalar physical frequencies are below $W=4P$, since $\pi<4$.
Thus the retained kinetic norm is at most $T_{\max}=8DP^2$.
All quantities in \eqref{eq:VGJ} and $P,W,T_{\max}$ are polynomial-valued
in $L$. The integer $K$ need not be, but its binary length is polynomial.

\section{Sampling with a uniform form bound}
\label{sec:sampling}
Oddly extend each sine coordinate from $(0,\ell)$ to the circle of length
$2\ell$. Multiplication by $2^{-D/2}$ makes the resulting extension an
$L^2$ isometry. On the torus, represent each coordinate by
$t\in[-\ell,\ell]$, fold it to $|t|\in[0,\ell]$, translate by $-B$, and
evaluate the original capped potential. Call this even extension
$V^{\rm ext}$. The fold is $1$-Lipschitz for circle distance, including its
joins; hence
$\norm{V^{\rm ext}}_\infty\leq B_V$ and
$\operatorname{Lip}(V^{\rm ext})\leq G$.
Kinetic and potential forms agree under the normalized odd extension.
All extensions commute with particle permutations.

Choose $q$ to be the least power of two satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q>4K,\qquad h:=2\ell/q\leq
 \frac{\eta}{16D(G+2B_VW+1)}.
\end{equation}
This choice is computable by rational comparisons. It has polynomial
binary length. We use the grid $x_j=2\ell j/q$, interpreted modulo $2\ell$,
for $j\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^D$.

For explicit normalization, the orthonormal torus modes, indexed by $n\in\mathbb Z^D$, are
\[
 e_n(t)=(2\ell)^{-D/2}\exp(\pi i n\cdot t/\ell).
\]
A bandlimited spinor is $f=\sum_{n\in[-K,K]^D}c_ne_n$, with vector-valued
spin coefficients. Define the sampling vector by
\[
 (S_uf)_j=h^{D/2}f(x_j+u).
\]
Every frequency difference lies in $[-2K,2K]^D$, and $2K<q$.
The identity $\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}e^{2\pi i mj/q}=0$ for nonzero
$|m|<q$ therefore proves, for every shift $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isometry}
 \norm{S_uf}_2^2=\norm f_2^2=\sum_n\norm{c_n}_{\C^{2^N}}^2.
\end{equation}
The phase of a shift does not change this calculation. It also proves
inner-product preservation, including for translated differences. There is
no dimension-dependent approximation factor.

For normalized bandlimited $f$, integration of its directional derivative
along a translation gives
\[
 \norm{f(\cdot+u)-f}_2\leq |u|\norm{\nabla f}_2
 \leq W\sqrt D\,|u|.
\]
For $u\in[0,h)^D$, \eqref{eq:isometry} gives
$\norm{S_uf-S_0f}_2\leq WDh$.
Set
\[
 A_u(f)=h^D\sum_j V^{\rm ext}(x_j+u)|f(x_j+u)|^2,
\]
with spin summation understood. Both sample vectors have norm one.
The elementary inequality
$\sum_j\bigl||v_j|^2-|w_j|^2\bigr|\leq2\norm{v-w}_2$
for unit vectors, and the Lipschitz bound for the potential, imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 |A_u(f)-A_0(f)|\leq G\sqrt D\,h+2B_VWDh.
\end{equation}
Averaging $A_u$ over one grid cell reproduces the exact torus integral,
by partition into translated cells. From \eqref{eq:q},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadrature}
 \left|A_0(f)-\ip f{V^{\rm ext}f}\right|\leq\eta/16
\end{equation}
for every normalized bandlimited state. By homogeneity and the Hermitian
spectral characterization this is an operator-norm bound on the compressed
potential; it holds for mixed states as well. It has no wavefunction
supremum norm, high derivative, or factor involving $\ell^D$.

The $q$-point discrete Fourier transform identifies $S_0$ with position
and momentum registers. It preserves the kinetic values
$\frac12(\pi/\ell)^2\sum_j n_j^2$.
Let $\mathcal A$ be the joint subspace of signed frequencies $|n_j|\leq K$,
odd parity in each scalar coordinate, and full particle antisymmetry.
Reflection of a position register is $j\mapsto-j\pmod q$.
Oddness removes zero modes; the cutoff excludes the Nyquist frequency.
Thus $\mathcal A$ is exactly the sampled Dirichlet antisymmetric sine
space, with no additional boundary modes.

Let $E_{\rm disc}$ be the minimum on $\mathcal A$ of the compressed sum of
that kinetic diagonal and the sampled potential diagonal. Equations
\eqref{eq:boxerror}, \eqref{eq:capenergy}, \eqref{eq:Ritz}, and
\eqref{eq:quadrature} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total}
 |E_{\rm disc}-E|\leq
 \eta+\eta/4+\eta/8+\eta/16<2\eta.
\end{equation}
The lower inequality applies to every valid witness. The potential need
not preserve the cutoff: only its compressed quadratic form is measured.

\section{A uniform validity instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}
Each of the $D$ spatial registers has $\log_2q$ qubits, and there are $N$
spin qubits. The following ideal projections have uniform polynomial-size
approximations in a fixed universal gate set.

First apply the inverse Fourier transform, interpret each index as a signed
residue in $[-q/2,q/2)$, and compare its absolute value with $K$. Reject
on failure; uncompute the comparison and return to position representation.
Next measure every reflection involution $j\mapsto-j\pmod q$ by a
controlled-reflection test, accepting only its $-1$ eigenspace. The cutoff
and these odd projectors commute. Their full product commutes with particle
permutations because each particle has the same three restrictions.
Individual odd projectors need not commute with those permutations.

To impose fermionic symmetry, use digits $d_i\in\{1,\ldots,i\}$ for
$i=1,\ldots,N$. A permutation is uniquely specified by inserting label $i$
at position $d_i$ in the previously ordered list of $1,\ldots,i-1$.
Uniform independent digits therefore give a uniform permutation.
Starting from the ordered register blocks, the corresponding permutation
can be implemented using at most $N(N-1)/2$ controlled adjacent swaps:
at stage $i$, move block $i$ left to slot $d_i$. A padded sequence tests,
for each possible swap position, whether it must be performed. Each test
is a comparison on $O(\log N)$ bits. The parity is
$\sum_i(i-d_i)\pmod2$, computable by reversible arithmetic. If one chooses
the inverse convention for the resulting permutation action, inversion
preserves both the uniform distribution and the sign. Each swapped block
contains its three spatial registers and its spin qubit.

Prepare uniform digit states, compute the swap controls and sign, perform
the controlled block permutation and sign phase, then uncompute auxiliary
classical data and reverse the digit preparation. Accept only if the digit
ancilla returns to its initial zero state. The accepted operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign}
 \mathsf A=\frac1{N!}\sum_{\pi\in S_N}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)U_\pi.
\end{equation}
Inversion makes it self-adjoint; multiplying the group sums proves
$\mathsf A^2=\mathsf A$. Its range is exactly
$U_\pi\psi=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\psi$ for every $\pi$.
For $N=1$ it is the identity. No $N!$-term enumeration is performed.

Since \eqref{eq:sign} commutes with the full previous projection, the
accepted Kraus operator is precisely $\Pi_{\mathcal A}$. Any input density
operator $\rho$ therefore leaves the subnormalized state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subnorm}
 \Pi_{\mathcal A}\rho\Pi_{\mathcal A}
\end{equation}
on success; failure is rejection. This is an actual instrument, not a
postselection promise about the witness.

For clarity on uniform gate complexity, a uniform interval of size $i$
can be prepared through a depth-$O(\log i)$ binary decision tree. Given a
prefix, reversible arithmetic counts the allowed integers in its two
children and computes the required square-root rotation angle. Counts are
computed from the prefix rather than by enumerating all nodes. Computing
an angle to $t$ bits and implementing rotations controlled on those bits
costs polynomial time in $\log i$ and $t$; rounding errors can be made
inverse polynomial. Reversing the circuit gives its inverse.
Approximate Fourier transforms, comparisons, controlled permutations and
fixed-gate synthesis also have polynomial cost at inverse-polynomial
operator accuracy. A telescoping bound over the polynomial number of
stages controls the acceptance error uniformly over all witnesses,
\emph{before} conditioning. Small success probabilities cannot amplify
that error.

\section{Arithmetic, energy testing and amplification}
\label{sec:verification}
The grid coordinates have polynomial binary length although their values
and the grid size may be exponential. Folding and translating require
rational arithmetic of polynomial bit cost. For each of the
$O(NM+N^2)$ kernels, compare the exact squared distance with
$\varepsilon^2$. Below that threshold, including exact coincidence, return
$\varepsilon^{-1}$. Otherwise compute the inverse square root to the
needed absolute accuracy. One may bisect its value in
$[0,\varepsilon^{-1}]$, testing a candidate using rational squared
comparisons. The number and bit cost of bisection steps are polynomial.
Summing at sufficiently small per-term error evaluates the potential
uniformly over all grid points.

Kinetic values are also computable in polynomial time: indices have
polynomial bit length, $\pi^2$ is computable to the needed precision, and
admitted physical frequencies are bounded by $W$. Off the accepted band
one may clamp the kinetic value to $[0,T_{\max}]$; this keeps approximate
instruments bounded and does not change their action on $\mathcal A$.
There is no need to enumerate grid points or evaluate an uncapped collision.

We first remove extreme thresholds using \eqref{eq:range}. Accept if
$b>0$ or $a\geq0$, when a NO instance is impossible. Otherwise reject if
$a<-C$ or $b\leq-C$, when a YES instance is impossible. Outside the promise
any outcome is permitted. In the remaining case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thresholdwindow}
 -C\leq a<b\leq0.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization}
 t=(a+b)/2,\qquad Q_*=T_{\max}+B_V+C+1,
 \qquad \gamma=g/(10Q_*).
\end{equation}
After the validity instrument, choose fairly between two diagonal tests.
For a Fourier kinetic outcome $x$, accept with probability
$1/2-(x-t)/(2Q_*)$. For a position potential outcome $y$, accept with
probability $1/2-y/(2Q_*)$. Both probabilities belong to $[0,1]$.
On a normalized valid state $\rho$, their average is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:accept}
 \frac12-\frac{\tr(\rho H_{\rm disc})-t}{4Q_*}.
\end{equation}
The computed probabilities can be implemented by a random-bit register
and comparator, or by a synthesized rotation. All arithmetic, gate and
measurement-probability errors can together be made at most $\gamma/10$
in acceptance, uniformly over inputs. This has polynomial cost: after
\eqref{eq:thresholdwindow}, $g\leq C$, all normalization constants are
polynomial-valued, and $g\geq L^{-1}$. Thus $\gamma^{-1}$ is polynomial.

For a YES input, \eqref{eq:total} supplies a valid witness of energy at most
$a+2\eta$. The ideal validity test accepts it surely, and its ideal
acceptance is at least
\[
 \frac12+\frac{g/2-2\eta}{4Q_*}\geq\frac12+\gamma,
\]
since $\eta\leq g/100$ gives a margin at least $0.12g/Q_*$.
For a NO input every normalized valid state has energy at least
$b-2\eta$, so its ideal acceptance is at most $1/2-\gamma$.
For an arbitrary witness, \eqref{eq:subnorm} multiplies such a nonnegative
acceptance probability by its surviving weight, at most one. Hence the
same soundness bound holds. Circuit approximations leave margins
$1/2+0.9\gamma$ and $1/2-0.9\gamma$.

Repeat on an odd number $r$ of registers, taking $r$ to be the least odd
integer at least $10\gamma^{-2}$, and accept a majority. Tensor copies of
a good witness give completeness at least $2/3$ by the Bernoulli tail
bound. For soundness, let $B_{\rm acc}$ be the one-register acceptance
POVM effect. On a NO instance,
$\norm{B_{\rm acc}}\leq1/2-0.9\gamma$.
The majority effect is a sum of tensor products of $B_{\rm acc}$ and
$I-B_{\rm acc}$. Their product eigenbasis reduces each eigenvalue to a
Bernoulli majority probability with each success parameter at most that
bound. Monotonicity and the same tail estimate bound the largest eigenvalue
by $1/3$. This applies to entangled witnesses across the registers; no
independence promise is imposed on Merlin. The witness size and uniform
circuit construction remain polynomial in $L$. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and \cite[Theorem 1]{unit} gives
Corollary~\ref{cor:complete}.

\section{Scope, provenance and formal support}
\label{sec:scope}
The cap bound treats arbitrarily close nuclei. The binary grid handles
exponentially distant ones. The all-$k$ localization and exterior wedge
include ions, escaping electrons and unattained infima. Every spin sector
is retained. The uniform form estimate and the actual projections prevent
invalid high-frequency, aliased or wrong-statistics witnesses from evading
soundness. These are analytic and circuit arguments, not numerical tests.
The polynomial total charge is essential to the estimates and follows
from the explicit unit-nucleus list; no binary-large-charge extension or
efficient witness preparation follows.

The hardness premise is the exact theorem at the pinned revision stated
in Section~\ref{sec:statement}; it is not re-proved here. The original
scope and the earlier feasibility sketch were recorded separately before
completion of this proof. Independent internal proof and contribution
reviews accepted the unchanged scope; these are not external peer review

\begin{abstract}
We prove QMA membership for the electronic Coulomb spectral-infimum problem
with an explicitly listed set of distinct rational unit-charge nuclei, a unary
number of spin-$1/2$ electrons, and inverse-input-length threshold separation.
The minimization is over the full antisymmetric continuum space. No binding,
attained ground state, nuclear separation, physical diameter, supplied orbital
basis, or state-preparation promise is imposed. An IMS decomposition over all
inner particle numbers, followed by exterior fermionic packets, gives one
finite box even for unbound infima. Hardy estimates control a Coulomb cap, and
bounded-potential spectral compression gives a physical momentum cutoff.
Exact shifted sampling on the retained band supplies a uniform potential-form
estimate without a configuration-space volume factor. Explicit symmetry and
cutoff projections then give soundness for arbitrary quantum witnesses.
Together with the stipulated unit-charge continuum hardness theorem of OpenAI,
this proves QMA-completeness for the same promise problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and antecedents}
\label{sec:statement}
An input lists $M\geq1$ pairwise distinct points
$R_1,\ldots,R_M\in\Q^3$, an integer $N\geq1$ in unary, and rationals $a<b$.
Every coordinate and threshold is encoded as a signed binary numerator and
positive binary denominator in lowest terms, with the fixed standard
self-delimiting encoding of \cite{unit}. Let $L$ be the total binary and unary
input length, and require $b-a\geq L^{-1}$. Every nucleus has charge one.
In atomic units the electronic Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H=-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N\Delta_{x_i}
 -\sum_{i=1}^N\sum_{\alpha=1}^M\frac1{|x_i-R_\alpha|}
 +\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac1{|x_i-x_j|}.
\end{equation}
Its Hilbert space and form domain are
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal H_N&=\bigwedge^N\bigl(L^2(\R^3)\otimes\C^2\bigr),\\
 \mathcal Q_N&=H^1(\R^{3N};(\C^2)^{\otimes N})\cap\mathcal H_N.
\end{align*}
Antisymmetry exchanges spatial and spin coordinates together. We use the
self-adjoint operator of the closed, lower-bounded Coulomb form and set
$E=\inf\spec H$. Nuclear repulsion is omitted. The YES promise is $E\leq a$;
the NO promise is $E\geq b$. No requirement is imposed outside the promise.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The promise problem above belongs to QMA. The verification circuits and
witnesses have size polynomial in $L$, uniformly over all allowed inputs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:complete}
Assuming the unit-charge continuum hardness theorem \cite[Theorem 1]{unit},
the same promise problem is QMA-complete under deterministic classical
polynomial-time many-one reductions.
\end{corollary}

The source theorem is used for hardness only and is taken as a premise at
repository revision \texttt{fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb}.
Membership is proved below. Neither statement assumes neutrality, strict
binding, an eigenvector at the infimum, an excitation gap, a selected spin
sector, a magnetic field, confinement, or an orbital basis. Nuclear positions
may be exponentially large or exponentially close, subject only to their
binary descriptions and distinctness. A witness need not be efficiently
preparable. The result does not include exponentially large binary nuclear
charges; the companion hardness theorem \cite{binary} has that different
input class.

\paragraph{Closest upper bounds.}
O'Gorman, Irani, Whitfield and Fefferman prove QMA-completeness with a supplied
finite orbital basis \cite{ogorman}. Their published Appendix D, Corollary 1,
adds positive unit nuclei while retaining that basis; Section V distinguishes
the complete-space question. Matrix-element estimates inside that basis do
not give the input-uniform continuum comparison needed here.
Zheng, Leng, Liu and Wu prove StoqMA membership for smooth few-body potentials
with polynomially bounded $C^1$ norm on Dirichlet boxes whose side lengths are
polynomially bounded above and below \cite[Theorem 3.3]{zheng}. Their proof
uses finite differences and a unary coordinate embedding. This pays the
bounded smooth-box problem. Direct rescaling of an exponentially long box
changes the derivative and kinetic scales, and their scalar minimum does not
impose the fermionic sector.

Real-space simulation provides further antecedents. Kivlichan et al.\ control
Fourier truncation and sampled norms, with general estimates containing a
supremum-to-volume factor and a stronger derivative premise for their
polynomial-spacing corollary \cite[Theorem 4, Corollary 5, Lemmas 9--15]{kiv}.
Childs et al.\ use Fourier-tail estimates with a high-derivative parameter;
their Coulomb discussion uses a periodic box and a softened interaction
\cite[Sections 2.2, 2.5 and Appendix B]{childs}. Schuch and Verstraete's
Hubbard membership and designed-field realization are earlier complexity
antecedents \cite{sv}. We do not substitute any of these dynamic or
supplied-basis interfaces for a uniform spectral-infimum certificate.

\paragraph{The verification interface.}
Hardy inequalities, IMS localization, spectral compression, Fourier
orthogonality, quantum Fourier transforms, and finite-group projections are
standard tools. Sector-valued localization for correlated states is classical:
Lewin's Proposition 3.1 and Example 3.3 explicitly construct localized Fock
states by partial traces and retain every particle-number sector
\cite{lewin}. The ionization companion \cite{ionization} credits that
interface; its strict-binding and radius results are not used here. Our
contribution is the complete uniform conversion from the singular, unconfined
fermionic input to a finite-register verifier, with both energy inequalities
and all-witness soundness. The comparison is bounded by these inspected
primary interfaces, not a worldwide-priority assertion.

\section{Uniform form estimates and the energy range}
\label{sec:forms}
Throughout, norms and integrals sum over spin indices. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base}
 T[\psi]=\frac12\sum_i\norm{\nabla_i\psi}_2^2,
 \qquad C=4NM^2,\qquad g=b-a,\qquad
 \eta=\min\{1,g/100\},\qquad D=3N.
\end{equation}
We have $M,N\leq L$ and $\eta^{-1}\leq100L$.

Three-dimensional Hardy, with the other coordinates fixed, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hardy}
 \int\frac{|\psi|^2}{|x_i-y|^2}\leq4\norm{\nabla_i\psi}_2^2.
\end{equation}
The center $y$ may be a nucleus or another particle coordinate. Integrating
all remaining variables preserves the constant. For normalized $\psi$,
Cauchy--Schwarz and Young's inequality imply
\begin{align}
 \sum_{i,\alpha}\int\frac{|\psi|^2}{|x_i-R_\alpha|}
 &\leq2M\sum_i\norm{\nabla_i\psi}_2
 \leq2\sqrt2 M\sqrt N\sqrt{T[\psi]}
 \leq\tfrac12T[\psi]+C.\label{eq:attraction}
\end{align}
Repulsion is nonnegative, so the Coulomb form $q$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive}
 q[\psi]\geq\tfrac12T[\psi]-C\norm\psi_2^2.
\end{equation}
The version of Young's inequality with an arbitrary positive kinetic
coefficient proves infinitesimal form bounds for all the finitely many
Coulomb terms. It justifies the closed-form convention in
Section~\ref{sec:statement}. The same estimates hold on Dirichlet open
subsets by zero extension.

In particular $E\geq-C$. To see $E\leq0$, choose $N$ normalized smooth
packets of width $R$ with disjoint supports separated by at least $R^2$.
Their normalized wedge, with any chosen spin labels, has kinetic energy
$O(N/R^2)$ and repulsion $O(N^2/R^2)$. Attraction is nonpositive. Letting
$R\to\infty$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:range}
 -C\leq E\leq0.
\end{equation}
No minimizing vector is required.

For $\varepsilon>0$ set
$v_\varepsilon(r)=\min\{r^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1}\}$, including
$v_\varepsilon(0)=\varepsilon^{-1}$, and cap every attractive and repulsive
kernel in $q$ to obtain $q_\varepsilon$. Since
$0\leq r^{-1}-v_\varepsilon(r)\leq\varepsilon/r^2$, Hardy gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capform}
 |q_\varepsilon[\psi]-q[\psi]|
 \leq8\varepsilon(M+N)T[\psi].
\end{equation}
For a pair $i<j$, use the $i$ coordinate in \eqref{eq:hardy}; each coordinate
is counted at most $N-1$ times. The cap reduces attraction and keeps
repulsion nonnegative, so \eqref{eq:coercive} also holds for $q_\varepsilon$.
No separation parameter enters either estimate.

\section{One finite box, including escaping electrons}
\label{sec:box}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boxchoices}
 A_0=1+\max_\alpha\norm{R_\alpha}_\infty,\quad R_0=2A_0,
 \quad s=\left\lceil\frac{100(NM+N^2+N+1)}\eta\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
All nuclei lie in the ball of radius $R_0$. Choose a Lipschitz function
$\theta(r)$ equal to $0$ for $r\leq R_0+s$ and to $\pi/2$ for
$r\geq R_0+2s$, with $|\theta'|\leq2/s$. Let
$\chi_{\rm in}(x)=\cos\theta(|x|)$ and
$\chi_{\rm out}(x)=\sin\theta(|x|)$. Their squares sum to one and their
squared gradients sum to at most $4/s^2$.

For $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ put
\[
 \psi_S=\prod_{i\in S}\chi_{\rm in}(x_i)
          \prod_{i\notin S}\chi_{\rm out}(x_i)\psi.
\]
The product partition has $\sum_S\norm{\psi_S}_2^2=\norm\psi_2^2$.
The IMS identity, with kinetic coefficient $1/2$, yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IMS}
 q[\psi]\geq\sum_S q[\psi_S]-\frac{2N}{s^2}\norm\psi_2^2.
\end{equation}
Lipschitz cutoffs suffice for this form identity; they can also be smoothly
approximated while retaining the bounds. This use of all sectors is the
classical localization mechanism discussed in \cite{lewin}.

Let $E_k^{\rm ball}$ be the $k$-electron Coulomb Dirichlet infimum, with the
same nuclei, in $B(0,R_0+2s)$, and set $E_0^{\rm ball}=0$. For a fixed $S$,
fixing the outside positions and spins leaves, for almost every slice, an
$H^1_0$ spinor antisymmetric among the $|S|$ inside particles. The common
inside cutoff preserves this antisymmetry and vanishes at the ball boundary.
After normalizing a nonzero slice, its internal form is at least
$E_{|S|}^{\rm ball}$; multiplying by its squared norm and integrating gives
the same bound for the original unnormalized slice. Full antisymmetry across
an inside and an outside label is not needed in this step.

Every outside electron is at distance at least $s$ from every nucleus.
Discarding its nonnegative kinetic energy and all repulsions involving it
therefore gives
\[
 q[\psi_S]\geq\left(E_{|S|}^{\rm ball}-NM/s\right)\norm{\psi_S}_2^2.
\]
Combining this with \eqref{eq:IMS} and taking arbitrary near-infima proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allk}
 \min_{0\leq k\leq N}E_k^{\rm ball}
 \leq E+\frac{NM}s+\frac{2N}{s^2}.
\end{equation}
In particular the argument retains the vacuum sector and unbound states.

Conversely fix $k$ and a normalized near-minimizer for $E_k^{\rm ball}$.
Add $N-k$ packets supported in cubes of side $s$, centered at
\[
 c_j=(R_0+2s+(4j+2)s,0,0),\qquad 1\leq j\leq N-k.
\]
Each packet is the product of the three normalized first Dirichlet sine
modes, extended by zero, with spin up. Its kinetic energy is
$3\pi^2/(2s^2)<15/s^2$. All new supports are separated from the inner ball
and from one another by at least $s$.

Wedge these packets with the arbitrary correlated inner $k$-particle state.
This is an isometry because their one-particle support spaces are
orthogonal. More explicitly, expansion of the antisymmetrization gives
disjoint assignments of coordinate labels to support regions; their
normalization factors cancel. The same expansion preserves all internal
energy expectations. Every new repulsive pair costs at most $1/s$, and
there are at most $N^2/2$ of them. Added attraction is nonpositive.

Let $B$ be the least power of two no smaller than
$\lceil R_0+(4N+6)s\rceil$, and set $Q=(-B,B)^3$. Every support just used
lies inside $Q$. If $E_Q$ is the $N$-particle Dirichlet infimum in $Q$,
zero extension and the preceding construction give
\begin{align}
 E\leq E_Q
 &\leq\min_k E_k^{\rm ball}+\frac{15N}{s^2}+\frac{N^2}{2s}
 \leq E+\eta.\label{eq:boxerror}
\end{align}
Indeed $NM/s+N^2/(2s)+17N/s^2<\eta$ by \eqref{eq:boxchoices}.
No algorithm must identify the minimizing $k$: it proves existence of a
witness with exactly $N$ particles. Both $k=0$ and $k=N$ are legal.
Although $B$ can be exponential, $\log B=O(L+\log s+\log N)$.

\section{Capping and physical momentum compression}
\label{sec:compression}
On $Q$ choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
 \varepsilon=\frac{\eta}{128(M+N)(C+2)},\qquad
 \alpha=8\varepsilon(M+N)=\frac{\eta}{16(C+2)}.
\end{equation}
Write $E_\varepsilon$ for the capped antisymmetric Dirichlet infimum.
An uncapped near-minimizer has
$T\leq2(C+\eta+o(1))$ by \eqref{eq:coercive} and \eqref{eq:boxerror}.
Its capped energy thus proves
$-C\leq E_\varepsilon\leq E_Q+\eta/8\leq2$.
Conversely a capped near-minimizer has $T\leq2(C+2+o(1))$.
Using \eqref{eq:capform} in the reverse direction and taking limits yields,
with slack,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:capenergy}
 |E_\varepsilon-E_Q|\leq\eta/4.
\end{equation}
This explicitly pays for capping repulsion as well as attraction.

The capped multiplication potential $V_\varepsilon$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:VGJ}
 \norm{V_\varepsilon}_\infty\leq B_V:=\frac{N(M+N)}\varepsilon,
 \qquad \operatorname{Lip}(V_\varepsilon)\leq
 G:=\frac{2N(M+N)}{\varepsilon^2},\qquad J=C+2+B_V.
\end{equation}
A nuclear term has Lipschitz constant at most $\varepsilon^{-2}$ and a pair
term at most $\sqrt2\varepsilon^{-2}$ in all $D$ coordinates; summing proves
the bound. It is independent of nuclear clustering.

The Dirichlet kinetic operator $T_Q$ has compact resolvent. A bounded
self-adjoint potential preserves its operator domain and compact resolvent.
Particle permutations commute with both terms, so this remains true on
the antisymmetric subspace. There is a normalized ground vector $\psi$ for
the capped operator, in $\Dom(T_Q)$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tnorm}
 \norm{T_Q\psi}_2\leq |E_\varepsilon|+B_V\leq J.
\end{equation}
No analytic regularity of an uncapped Coulomb eigenfunction is assumed.

Set $\ell=2B$, translate the cube to $(0,\ell)^3$, and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
 P=\lceil64J/\eta\rceil,\qquad K=\ell P.
\end{equation}
Let $F$ keep the product sine modes with $1\leq n_j\leq K$ in every scalar
coordinate, with all spin indices retained. The same cutoff in every
particle coordinate commutes with permutations. For $p=F\psi$ and
$r=(1-F)\psi$, an omitted mode has kinetic eigenvalue at least $P^2/2$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:Tnorm} therefore implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tails}
 \norm r_2^2\leq\frac{4J^2}{P^4},\qquad
 \ip r{T_Qr}\leq\frac{2J^2}{P^2}.
\end{equation}
Since $(H_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)\psi=0$,
\begin{align*}
 \ip p{(H_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)p}
 &=\ip r{(H_\varepsilon-E_\varepsilon)r}
 \leq\frac{2J^2}{P^2}+\frac{4J^3}{P^4}.
\end{align*}
Here $J\geq1$, $P^2\geq8J$, $\norm p_2^2\geq3/4$, and
$4J^2/P^2\leq\eta/8$. Normalizing $p$ and using the variational lower
bound for every retained vector gives the compressed minimum $E_F$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ritz}
 E_\varepsilon\leq E_F\leq E_\varepsilon+\eta/8.
\end{equation}
The absolute scalar physical frequencies are below $W=4P$, since $\pi<4$.
Thus the retained kinetic norm is at most $T_{\max}=8DP^2$.
All quantities in \eqref{eq:VGJ} and $P,W,T_{\max}$ are polynomial-valued
in $L$. The integer $K$ need not be, but its binary length is polynomial.

\section{Sampling with a uniform form bound}
\label{sec:sampling}
Oddly extend each sine coordinate from $(0,\ell)$ to the circle of length
$2\ell$. Multiplication by $2^{-D/2}$ makes the resulting extension an
$L^2$ isometry. On the torus, represent each coordinate by
$t\in[-\ell,\ell]$, fold it to $|t|\in[0,\ell]$, translate by $-B$, and
evaluate the original capped potential. Call this even extension
$V^{\rm ext}$. The fold is $1$-Lipschitz for circle distance, including its
joins; hence
$\norm{V^{\rm ext}}_\infty\leq B_V$ and
$\operatorname{Lip}(V^{\rm ext})\leq G$.
Kinetic and potential forms agree under the normalized odd extension.
All extensions commute with particle permutations.

Choose $q$ to be the least power of two satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
 q>4K,\qquad h:=2\ell/q\leq
 \frac{\eta}{16D(G+2B_VW+1)}.
\end{equation}
This choice is computable by rational comparisons. It has polynomial
binary length. We use the grid $x_j=2\ell j/q$, interpreted modulo $2\ell$,
for $j\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^D$.

For explicit normalization, the orthonormal torus modes, indexed by $n\in\mathbb Z^D$, are
\[
 e_n(t)=(2\ell)^{-D/2}\exp(\pi i n\cdot t/\ell).
\]
A bandlimited spinor is $f=\sum_{n\in[-K,K]^D}c_ne_n$, with vector-valued
spin coefficients. Define the sampling vector by
\[
 (S_uf)_j=h^{D/2}f(x_j+u).
\]
Every frequency difference lies in $[-2K,2K]^D$, and $2K<q$.
The identity $\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}e^{2\pi i mj/q}=0$ for nonzero
$|m|<q$ therefore proves, for every shift $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isometry}
 \norm{S_uf}_2^2=\norm f_2^2=\sum_n\norm{c_n}_{\C^{2^N}}^2.
\end{equation}
The phase of a shift does not change this calculation. It also proves
inner-product preservation, including for translated differences. There is
no dimension-dependent approximation factor.

For normalized bandlimited $f$, integration of its directional derivative
along a translation gives
\[
 \norm{f(\cdot+u)-f}_2\leq |u|\norm{\nabla f}_2
 \leq W\sqrt D\,|u|.
\]
For $u\in[0,h)^D$, \eqref{eq:isometry} gives
$\norm{S_uf-S_0f}_2\leq WDh$.
Set
\[
 A_u(f)=h^D\sum_j V^{\rm ext}(x_j+u)|f(x_j+u)|^2,
\]
with spin summation understood. Both sample vectors have norm one.
The elementary inequality
$\sum_j\bigl||v_j|^2-|w_j|^2\bigr|\leq2\norm{v-w}_2$
for unit vectors, and the Lipschitz bound for the potential, imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 |A_u(f)-A_0(f)|\leq G\sqrt D\,h+2B_VWDh.
\end{equation}
Averaging $A_u$ over one grid cell reproduces the exact torus integral,
by partition into translated cells. From \eqref{eq:q},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadrature}
 \left|A_0(f)-\ip f{V^{\rm ext}f}\right|\leq\eta/16
\end{equation}
for every normalized bandlimited state. By homogeneity and the Hermitian
spectral characterization this is an operator-norm bound on the compressed
potential; it holds for mixed states as well. It has no wavefunction
supremum norm, high derivative, or factor involving $\ell^D$.

The $q$-point discrete Fourier transform identifies $S_0$ with position
and momentum registers. It preserves the kinetic values
$\frac12(\pi/\ell)^2\sum_j n_j^2$.
Let $\mathcal A$ be the joint subspace of signed frequencies $|n_j|\leq K$,
odd parity in each scalar coordinate, and full particle antisymmetry.
Reflection of a position register is $j\mapsto-j\pmod q$.
Oddness removes zero modes; the cutoff excludes the Nyquist frequency.
Thus $\mathcal A$ is exactly the sampled Dirichlet antisymmetric sine
space, with no additional boundary modes.

Let $E_{\rm disc}$ be the minimum on $\mathcal A$ of the compressed sum of
that kinetic diagonal and the sampled potential diagonal. Equations
\eqref{eq:boxerror}, \eqref{eq:capenergy}, \eqref{eq:Ritz}, and
\eqref{eq:quadrature} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total}
 |E_{\rm disc}-E|\leq
 \eta+\eta/4+\eta/8+\eta/16<2\eta.
\end{equation}
The lower inequality applies to every valid witness. The potential need
not preserve the cutoff: only its compressed quadratic form is measured.

\section{A uniform validity instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}
Each of the $D$ spatial registers has $\log_2q$ qubits, and there are $N$
spin qubits. The following ideal projections have uniform polynomial-size
approximations in a fixed universal gate set.

First apply the inverse Fourier transform, interpret each index as a signed
residue in $[-q/2,q/2)$, and compare its absolute value with $K$. Reject
on failure; uncompute the comparison and return to position representation.
Next measure every reflection involution $j\mapsto-j\pmod q$ by a
controlled-reflection test, accepting only its $-1$ eigenspace. The cutoff
and these odd projectors commute. Their full product commutes with particle
permutations because each particle has the same three restrictions.
Individual odd projectors need not commute with those permutations.

To impose fermionic symmetry, use digits $d_i\in\{1,\ldots,i\}$ for
$i=1,\ldots,N$. A permutation is uniquely specified by inserting label $i$
at position $d_i$ in the previously ordered list of $1,\ldots,i-1$.
Uniform independent digits therefore give a uniform permutation.
Starting from the ordered register blocks, the corresponding permutation
can be implemented using at most $N(N-1)/2$ controlled adjacent swaps:
at stage $i$, move block $i$ left to slot $d_i$. A padded sequence tests,
for each possible swap position, whether it must be performed. Each test
is a comparison on $O(\log N)$ bits. The parity is
$\sum_i(i-d_i)\pmod2$, computable by reversible arithmetic. If one chooses
the inverse convention for the resulting permutation action, inversion
preserves both the uniform distribution and the sign. Each swapped block
contains its three spatial registers and its spin qubit.

Prepare uniform digit states, compute the swap controls and sign, perform
the controlled block permutation and sign phase, then uncompute auxiliary
classical data and reverse the digit preparation. Accept only if the digit
ancilla returns to its initial zero state. The accepted operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign}
 \mathsf A=\frac1{N!}\sum_{\pi\in S_N}\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)U_\pi.
\end{equation}
Inversion makes it self-adjoint; multiplying the group sums proves
$\mathsf A^2=\mathsf A$. Its range is exactly
$U_\pi\psi=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\psi$ for every $\pi$.
For $N=1$ it is the identity. No $N!$-term enumeration is performed.

Since \eqref{eq:sign} commutes with the full previous projection, the
accepted Kraus operator is precisely $\Pi_{\mathcal A}$. Any input density
operator $\rho$ therefore leaves the subnormalized state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subnorm}
 \Pi_{\mathcal A}\rho\Pi_{\mathcal A}
\end{equation}
on success; failure is rejection. This is an actual instrument, not a
postselection promise about the witness.

For clarity on uniform gate complexity, a uniform interval of size $i$
can be prepared through a depth-$O(\log i)$ binary decision tree. Given a
prefix, reversible arithmetic counts the allowed integers in its two
children and computes the required square-root rotation angle. Counts are
computed from the prefix rather than by enumerating all nodes. Computing
an angle to $t$ bits and implementing rotations controlled on those bits
costs polynomial time in $\log i$ and $t$; rounding errors can be made
inverse polynomial. Reversing the circuit gives its inverse.
Approximate Fourier transforms, comparisons, controlled permutations and
fixed-gate synthesis also have polynomial cost at inverse-polynomial
operator accuracy. A telescoping bound over the polynomial number of
stages controls the acceptance error uniformly over all witnesses,
\emph{before} conditioning. Small success probabilities cannot amplify
that error.

\section{Arithmetic, energy testing and amplification}
\label{sec:verification}
The grid coordinates have polynomial binary length although their values
and the grid size may be exponential. Folding and translating require
rational arithmetic of polynomial bit cost. For each of the
$O(NM+N^2)$ kernels, compare the exact squared distance with
$\varepsilon^2$. Below that threshold, including exact coincidence, return
$\varepsilon^{-1}$. Otherwise compute the inverse square root to the
needed absolute accuracy. One may bisect its value in
$[0,\varepsilon^{-1}]$, testing a candidate using rational squared
comparisons. The number and bit cost of bisection steps are polynomial.
Summing at sufficiently small per-term error evaluates the potential
uniformly over all grid points.

Kinetic values are also computable in polynomial time: indices have
polynomial bit length, $\pi^2$ is computable to the needed precision, and
admitted physical frequencies are bounded by $W$. Off the accepted band
one may clamp the kinetic value to $[0,T_{\max}]$; this keeps approximate
instruments bounded and does not change their action on $\mathcal A$.
There is no need to enumerate grid points or evaluate an uncapped collision.

We first remove extreme thresholds using \eqref{eq:range}. Accept if
$b>0$ or $a\geq0$, when a NO instance is impossible. Otherwise reject if
$a<-C$ or $b\leq-C$, when a YES instance is impossible. Outside the promise
any outcome is permitted. In the remaining case
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thresholdwindow}
 -C\leq a<b\leq0.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization}
 t=(a+b)/2,\qquad Q_*=T_{\max}+B_V+C+1,
 \qquad \gamma=g/(10Q_*).
\end{equation}
After the validity instrument, choose fairly between two diagonal tests.
For a Fourier kinetic outcome $x$, accept with probability
$1/2-(x-t)/(2Q_*)$. For a position potential outcome $y$, accept with
probability $1/2-y/(2Q_*)$. Both probabilities belong to $[0,1]$.
On a normalized valid state $\rho$, their average is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:accept}
 \frac12-\frac{\tr(\rho H_{\rm disc})-t}{4Q_*}.
\end{equation}
The computed probabilities can be implemented by a random-bit register
and comparator, or by a synthesized rotation. All arithmetic, gate and
measurement-probability errors can together be made at most $\gamma/10$
in acceptance, uniformly over inputs. This has polynomial cost: after
\eqref{eq:thresholdwindow}, $g\leq C$, all normalization constants are
polynomial-valued, and $g\geq L^{-1}$. Thus $\gamma^{-1}$ is polynomial.

For a YES input, \eqref{eq:total} supplies a valid witness of energy at most
$a+2\eta$. The ideal validity test accepts it surely, and its ideal
acceptance is at least
\[
 \frac12+\frac{g/2-2\eta}{4Q_*}\geq\frac12+\gamma,
\]
since $\eta\leq g/100$ gives a margin at least $0.12g/Q_*$.
For a NO input every normalized valid state has energy at least
$b-2\eta$, so its ideal acceptance is at most $1/2-\gamma$.
For an arbitrary witness, \eqref{eq:subnorm} multiplies such a nonnegative
acceptance probability by its surviving weight, at most one. Hence the
same soundness bound holds. Circuit approximations leave margins
$1/2+0.9\gamma$ and $1/2-0.9\gamma$.

Repeat on an odd number $r$ of registers, taking $r$ to be the least odd
integer at least $10\gamma^{-2}$, and accept a majority. Tensor copies of
a good witness give completeness at least $2/3$ by the Bernoulli tail
bound. For soundness, let $B_{\rm acc}$ be the one-register acceptance
POVM effect. On a NO instance,
$\norm{B_{\rm acc}}\leq1/2-0.9\gamma$.
The majority effect is a sum of tensor products of $B_{\rm acc}$ and
$I-B_{\rm acc}$. Their product eigenbasis reduces each eigenvalue to a
Bernoulli majority probability with each success parameter at most that
bound. Monotonicity and the same tail estimate bound the largest eigenvalue
by $1/3$. This applies to entangled witnesses across the registers; no
independence promise is imposed on Merlin. The witness size and uniform
circuit construction remain polynomial in $L$. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, and \cite[Theorem 1]{unit} gives
Corollary~\ref{cor:complete}.

\section{Scope, provenance and formal support}
\label{sec:scope}
The cap bound treats arbitrarily close nuclei. The binary grid handles
exponentially distant ones. The all-$k$ localization and exterior wedge
include ions, escaping electrons and unattained infima. Every spin sector
is retained. The uniform form estimate and the actual projections prevent
invalid high-frequency, aliased or wrong-statistics witnesses from evading
soundness. These are analytic and circuit arguments, not numerical tests.
The polynomial total charge is essential to the estimates and follows
from the explicit unit-nucleus list; no binary-large-charge extension or
efficient witness preparation follows.

The hardness premise is the exact theorem at the pinned revision stated
in Section~\ref{sec:statement}; it is not re-proved here. The original
scope and the earlier feasibility sketch were recorded separately before
completion of this proof. Independent internal proof and contribution
reviews accepted the unchanged scope; these are not external peer review
or a worldwide-priority certificate. The supporting record gives source
hashes, actual primary reading limits, reproducible build instructions and
the exact correspondence with the reviewed proof.
