   WEIGHTED CAPACITY FOR FOUR-STATE RECONSTRUCTION WITH
                 VARIABLE EDGE CHANNELS

                                              MYEONGJIN KANG


       Abstract. Assuming a speciﬁed moment-saving inequality for a preserved class of four-state poste-
       rior laws, we characterize reconstruction on deterministic locally ﬁnite trees with variable symmetric
       ferromagnetic edge channels by positivity of a weighted nonlinear capacity. The channel parameters
       may approach either zero or one, and perfectly transmitting edges are allowed. We give quantitative
       comparisons between capacity and limiting posterior information with constants independent of the
       tree, its degrees, and the edge parameters. The proof uses local posterior estimates valid without
       channel smoothing and a ﬂow estimate controlled by branch splitting. The capacity is unchanged
       by replacing a chain by its composite channel; on a ray, it gives the exact criterion that the product
       of the channel parameters is positive.




                                              1. Introduction
   Reconstruction on a tree asks whether spins observed far from the root retain information about
its spin. For irregular trees, nonlinear capacities can distinguish models with the same exponential
growth rate. Pemantle and Peres [4] developed capacity criteria and concave recursions for critical
Ising models on trees. The dependence of reconstruction on the number of states is studied, in
particular, by Sly [5] and Mossel, Sly, and Sohn [1].
   For the symmetric four-state model, the ﬁxed-channel capacity theorem of [2] uses a moment-
saving inequality for a preserved class of posterior laws from [3]. We take this inequality and
its closure properties as an explicit assumption. Starting from that input, we prove a capacity
criterion for variable edge channels on every locally ﬁnite tree. The main issue is uniformity as
channel parameters approach one: estimates obtained by bounding posterior denominators away
from zero no longer suﬃce. A bound using the posterior numerator instead removes this restriction.
A second estimate uses ﬂow splitting to control the accumulated information loss, including along
long chains with nearly perfect transmission.

5.2. Zero channels.
Corollary 5.1. Assume Assumption 2.1, and allow 0 ≤ λe ≤ 1. Let T+ be the connected component
of the root after deleting all zero-channel edges. Deﬁne capacity using (1.3)–(1.4) on T+ . Then
Theorem 1.1, including its quantitative bounds, remains valid with C(T+ ) in place of C(T ).
Proof. Realize each channel by copying the parent with probability λe and otherwise drawing a
fresh uniform spin, using independent edge randomness. Across a zero edge the child is a fresh
uniform spin independent of its parent. Group removed vertices by the ﬁrst zero edge on their
path from the root. Each resulting subtree depends only on its fresh initial spin and subsequent
independent randomness. Jointly, all their observations are independent of both the root and all
observations in T+ . Deleting them leaves the root posterior at every depth unchanged. Apply the
theorem to the positive-channel tree T+ . If it is ﬁnite, distant levels are empty and conservation
forces every ﬂow to vanish.                                                                       □
5.3. The role of local ﬁniteness. Local ﬁniteness cannot be omitted without changing the state-
ment. Give the root, for each k ≥ 1, a disjoint child chain of length k, and take every channel to
be perfect. There is no inﬁnite ray. Conservation forces the ﬂow on each ﬁnite chain to vanish,
working backward from its leaf. Even with countably additive conservation at the root, every ﬂow
has mass zero. Yet each level n contains a spin equal to the root, from any chain of length at least
n. Thus mn = 3 and an = 3/4 for every n, although the capacity is zero.
