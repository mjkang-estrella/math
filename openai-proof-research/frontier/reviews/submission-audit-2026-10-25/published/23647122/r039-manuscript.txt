       Removing the moment assumption from a stipulated
                   planar no-bigeodesics theorem
                                           Myeongjin Kang

                                           October 18, 2026


                                                Abstract
         Taking the pinned OpenAI planar no-bigeodesics theorem and its speciﬁed proof inputs as
     established, we give a deduction for every nonatomic law on ﬁnite nonnegative edge weights,
     without a moment or direction condition. A dependent circuit cap preserves exactly the set of
     geometric bigeodesics and supplies an integrable metric. The capped law does not have upward
     ﬁnite energy. We replace the required ﬁnite-energy operations by a dominated coupling of lifted
     limiting graphs, a transfer of labels and selected rays under underlying-coordinate modiﬁcations,
     and a changed-vertex barrier for independent half-plane splices. A cubic wall completes the
     coalescence surgery. The ﬁnal budget and perturbation argument is the stipulated source’s
     argument with status-preserving probability kernels. The additional class is precisely the laws
     with inﬁnite second moment of the minimum of four independent weights. Classical contour
     surgery, shape theory, and the source’s ﬁnal counting argument are credited separately from
     these replacement steps.

Reproducibility. The mathematical deduction is conditional on the stipulated source identiﬁed in
Section 1. The full theorem is not formally veriﬁed. The accompanying README.md and build.py
give a fresh-directory PDF build; Section 8 describes the exact limited Lean support. Original
prose is licensed CC BY 4.0; original supporting code is MIT licensed. No institutional aﬃliation
is asserted.

1    Scope, stipulated input, and attribution
Let E be the unoriented nearest-neighbor edges of Z2 . For positive weights w, write Tw (x, y) for
the inﬁmum of the costs of ﬁnite walks from x to y, counting every traversal. A ray is an inﬁnite
simple path whose ﬁnite segments minimize cost; a bigeodesic is such a path indexed by Z. All
weights in this paper are ﬁnite. A nonatomic nonnegative law assigns probability one to strictly
positive weights simultaneously on the countable edge set.
Theorem 1.1. Let G be any nonatomic Borel probability measure on [0, ∞), and let (te )e∈E be
independent with common law G. Under the stipulated source convention below, almost surely there
is no bigeodesic for Tt . The assertion holds simultaneously over all bi-inﬁnite simple paths, without
a prescribed direction or any moment assumption.

Proposition 2.2 (Exact preservation). On one event, the sets of geometric t- and s-bigeodesics
are exactly equal. Every edge of each such path is unchanged. Every s-ray from a ﬁxed root x is
uncapped outside the ﬁnite set Ex , simultaneously over all rays from that root.
Proof. Call e changed if se < te , so se = Ke . A bigeodesic in either metric using e must enter and
leave Ce . Its segment between contacts bracketing e costs at least Ke , whereas a shorter boundary
arc costs at most M Le /2. Thus neither kind of bigeodesic uses a changed edge.
    For the nontrivial implication, let γ be a t-bigeodesic and let π be a ﬁnite s-walk between
a, b ∈ γ. Eliminate a changed occurrence e of π. If both endpoints lie outside or on Ce , replace
the bracketed crossing by a boundary arc. If just a lies inside, replace the preﬁx through e by a
segment of γ from a to a boundary contact and then a boundary arc to a later contact of π; reverse
the construction if just b is inside. The part of γ between its outermost contacts with Ce costs at
most M Le /2, by t-minimality, so the inserted portion costs at most M Le . If both endpoints are
inside, replace all of π by γ[a, b], contained in that same outermost-contact interval and costing at
most M Le /2.
    Every removed portion contains an edge of s-cost Ke ≥ 2M Le , and every inserted edge lies on
the unchanged circuit or the unchanged γ. The number of changed occurrences strictly decreases,
with no recursive creation of changed edges. After ﬁnitely many steps the walk is entirely unchanged
and costs no more than the original s-walk. Its t-cost equals its s-cost, so t-minimality of γ proves s-
minimality. Conversely an unchanged s-geodesic is t-minimal because s ≤ t. The proof is pathwise
and takes no union over an uncountable path space.

Remark 4.2. The same proof applies to a kernel changing a ﬁxed deterministic ﬁnite set of original
coordinates whose output marginal has a bounded density relative to the underlying product law,
on an event where that kernel increases the originals. The joint underlying law is ﬁxed across the
approximants, the density bound gives the same domination, and strict uncapping plus the two tight
localization events give the identical stability conclusion. If its upward event is only measurable,
include that event’s indicator in the joint lift. Its discrete convergence makes the event persist
in the approximants, where monotonicity holds; the entire underlying pair-and-indicator marginal
is ﬁxed at every approximation and hence in the limit. In particular, same-status resampling
has output density one, while each ﬁnite tilt has a bounded product density. This extends the
explicit reference and label couplings below to those kernels; it does not assert cap conditional
ﬁnite energy or stability under arbitrary uncontrolled modiﬁcations. The later edges and regions
are deterministic once their site and radius are ﬁxed; there are only countably many such choices.
A random support is not being covered by an unstated uniform kernel bound.

polygon and exits at a boundary edge or vertex, where rounding gives a boundary target vertex.

for the precise DH approximants.

A bigeodesic in the individual region at k has every edge of height ≤ R inside Ak (R) by its cone
width; higher edges are never raised. Passing to the two-site union therefore leaves all of its edges
unchanged, while competitors only increase. The same holds for the selected path at the second
site. A near raised edge of the second path lies outside the ﬁrst region by the ﬁxed spacing, so in
the ﬁrst ﬁeld it still has its base cap cost and in the union its cost is strictly larger. The common
Busemann pair for equal labels and the source’s vanishing-excess detour through this strict edge
rule out repeated marked labels. This is exactly the geometric use of the near raise; the large radius
pays only the toll.
    S Section 8’s base-comparator ﬁlter loses at most p/8, leaving qm ≥ p/8. For each ﬁxed m,
the comparison separation S(m) is ﬁnite. Reﬁne Πj and discard marks with a same-bin neighbor
within S(m). Distinct labels and ﬁnitely many neighbors make this ﬁlter disappear pointwise as
j → ∞. Apply (10) successively in each bin; Busemann increments telescope and their linear parts
leave the sum WI of bin widths. The bound is qm,j ≤ ℓWI /m. Bounded convergence therefore
yields
                              p/8 ≤ qm ≤ ℓWI /m       for every integer m.
The constants p, ℓ, WI were ﬁxed before m, a contradiction for large m. Sites, ﬁnite regions, unions,
partition endpoints and chosen radii form countable collections, so all inherited full events can
be imposed simultaneously. Random-label equalities were already pathwise. There are no cap
bigeodesics. Proposition 2.2 proves Theorem 1.1 for the remaining laws, and S covers (1).

8.1   Limited deterministic formal support
The accompanying frozen Lean support has 14 support declarations and 17 control declarations. Its
principal all-walk statement concerns a graph with a nonnegative changed weight function, a ﬁnite
set of changed edges, a ﬁnite absolute-change budget, calibrated old ray segments, radial additivity

late segments minimize over all ﬁnite walks. The passage from edge-simple competitors to walks
uses loop erasure and nonnegativity. This supports the deterministic argument of Lemma 5.1.

assumptions supplying calibration, coercivity or ﬁnite inﬂuence. They do not formalize the circuit

The ﬁnite controls illustrate hypotheses and counterexamples; their number is not a count of
independently proved probabilistic lemmas.
