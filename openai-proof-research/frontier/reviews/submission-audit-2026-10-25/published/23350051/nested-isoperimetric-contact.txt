  SHARP BALL–TUBE AND BALL–SLAB NESTING COSTS IN THE CUBIC
                       THREE-TORUS

                                              MYEONGJIN KANG


       Abstract. Assume the isoperimetric proﬁle and equality classiﬁcation for the unit cubic three-
       torus stated below. We determine the leading perimeter cost of nesting a ﬁxed-volume ball inside a
       slightly thinner optimal tube or slab, allowing both interfaces to deform. For ball–tube contact the
       cost is K(R)ε2 , with an explicit positive coeﬃcient. For ball–slab contact it is 2πε2 /| log ε|. Both
       limits hold for the minimum over all nested ﬁnite-perimeter pairs, without symmetry or regularity
       assumptions on competitors. A common two-volume correction and a perimeter lattice comparison
       give scalar quasiminimality of exact minimizers. Improved convergence then reduces the lower
       bounds to a ﬁxed-circle trace problem and a shrinking-annulus capacity estimate.

   The independent scalar minimizers touch along a circle in the tube problem and at two points
in the slab problem. A point on a two-dimensional interface has zero H 1 capacity, which accounts
for the diﬀerent scales. Logarithmic response in droplet contact is classical, both for rigid plates
[2, 3] and deformable droplet and bubble contacts [4, 5]. Alama et al. [6, Theorem 4.5] obtain a
logarithmic second-order selection term for optimal shells in a nonlocal problem. Here we determine
the sharp nonlinear minimum over all ﬁnite-perimeter nested pairs, with both interfaces free. The
quadratic forms, Green functions, and planar capacity calculation used below are classical tools.

                             2. A common regularity reduction
  Let BR be a radius-R ball, CR the radius-R tube about the z-axis, and SR = {−R < z < R}
the coordinate slab. In either geometry there is a patch of ∂BR strictly inside the outer set and a
patch of the outer boundary disjoint from the ball. These free patches permit simultaneous volume
correction before any regularity of the approximating pair has been established.
Lemma 2.1. Let (E0 , F0 ) be either (BR , CR ) or (BR , SR ) in the ranges above. For nested pairs
(A, B) suﬃciently close to (E0 , F0 ) in L1 × L1 , with P (A) + P (B) ≤ M , two suﬃciently small
volume errors of total magnitude q can be corrected by one smooth ambient diﬀeomorphism. The
corrected pair remains nested and its summed perimeter is at most P (A) + P (B) + CM q.
Proof. Choose smooth vector ﬁelds X1 , X2 supported in the two free patches, with positive outward
ﬂux through the respective boundary. Their ﬂuxes through the other limiting set vanish. For the
composition Φs of their ﬂows, the two-volume map is

At (E0 , F0 ) and s = 0 its derivative is diagonal with positive entries. The map has uniform C 2
bounds in s for all measurable pairs. Its derivatives vary continuously in L1 × L1 because the

Lemma 2.2. Suppose exact minimizing pairs (Ej , Fj ) for either problem converge in L1 × L1 to
its limiting pair, with uniformly bounded perimeters, and put M = supj (P (Ej ) + P (Fj )) < ∞. For
all suﬃciently large j, both components are scalar ω-minimizers with one constant ω independent

Proof. The perimeter lattice inequality states
                             P (A ∩ B) + P (A ∪ B) ≤ P (A) + P (B).
It follows by strict BV approximation and the gradient identity for minimum and maximum of two
smooth functions. Given a competitor H for Ej , use the nested pair (H ∩ Fj , H ∪ Fj ). Pointwise
checking the three states of Ej ⊂ Fj gives
                          |(H ∩ Fj ) △ Ej | + |(H ∪ Fj ) △ Fj | = |H △ Ej |.

If P (H) ≥ P (Ej ) the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise the new summed perimeter is uniformly
bounded. When ρ < ρ0 for a ﬁxed suﬃciently small ρ0 , its L1 change and its two volume errors are
small enough for Lemma 2.1. Correct the volumes and use paired minimality to obtain
                             P (Ej ) + P (Fj ) ≤ P (H) + P (Fj ) + C|H △ Ej |.
The small-ball volume bound proves the assertion for ρ < ρ0 . For larger balls choose also ω ≥ M/ρ30
and use the global perimeter bound. For an outer competitor replace the pair by (Ej ∩ H, Ej ∪ H);
the same identity and cancellation apply.                                                        □
   We use the improved-convergence theorem stated in Acerbi–Fusco–Morini [1, Deﬁnition 2.5 and
Theorem 4.2]. A sequence of scalar ω-minimizers on the ﬂat torus, with bounded perimeter and L1
limit a C 2 set, eventually has a global normal-graph boundary over that limit; the graph heights
tend to zero in C 1,α for every α < 1/2. AFM state this classical regularity result without proof
and refer to earlier regularity theory. Lemma 2.2 veriﬁes its hypotheses separately for the two
components. Contact between the limiting interfaces causes no extra hypothesis in this scalar
application. The theorem also applies to the disconnected slab boundary.
   We use the open representatives bounded by these graphs, with their occupied side ﬁxed by L1
convergence. Inclusion almost everywhere then implies Ej ⊂ Fj pointwise and the same contain-
ment for the inner boundary. This regularity statement concerns exact minimizers only. Every
competitor has energy at least the exact minimum, which will extend the lower bounds to the
original class.
