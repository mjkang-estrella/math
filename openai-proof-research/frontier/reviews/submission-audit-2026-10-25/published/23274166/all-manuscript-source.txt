\begin{abstract}
Assume the sharp finite Gaussian propeller inequality and its finite
equality classification. We prove that the deficit in the squared
first-moment functional controls the square of the Gaussian measure
distance to a propeller, with one positive constant independent of
the ambient dimension and the number of cells. Cells may have
arbitrarily small measure, and no masses are prescribed. The proof
passes to countable Gaussian score arrays, excludes infinite-support
equality cases, and bounds the gain from all additional scores by the
three-halves power of their total squared norm and the squared common
mean of the leading triple. A quadratic estimate for that triple and
a Gaussian margin bound then give the partition estimate. The
square-root dependence of distance on deficit is sharp already for
three planar sectors. The universal constant is positive but implicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Let $\gamma_d$ denote standard Gaussian measure on $\R^d$. For a
measurable partition $\mathcal A=(A_1,\ldots,A_k)$, modulo null sets,
put
\[
 z_i=\int_{A_i}x\,d\gamma_d(x),\qquad
 F(\mathcal A)=\sum_i\|z_i\|^2,\qquad B=\frac3{2\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
For $d\ge2$ and $k\ge3$, let $\cP_{d,k}$ be the partitions formed
by three sectors of angle $2\pi/3$ in a two-dimensional linear
subspace, extended along its orthogonal complement, with $k-3$
empty cells. All orthogonal transformations and label permutations
are allowed. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance}
 d_P(\mathcal A)=\inf_{\mathcal P\in\cP_{d,k}}
           \frac12\sum_{i=1}^k\gamma_d(A_i\mathbin\triangle P_i),
 \qquad \Delta(\mathcal A)=B^2-F(\mathcal A).
\end{equation}
For a fixed alignment, the sum in \eqref{eq:distance} is the
probability of a label disagreement.

Our external input is stated explicitly.
\begin{assumption}[Finite propeller inequality and rigidity]\label{ass:finite}
For all finite positive integers $d,k$ and all measurable Gaussian
partitions, $F(\mathcal A)\le B^2$. If equality holds, then
$d\ge2$, $k\ge3$ and $\mathcal A\in\cP_{d,k}$ modulo null sets.
\end{assumption}

The inequality is Theorem 1.1 of the pinned preprint \cite{OpenAI}.
Its scalar and determinant elimination, read at the equality endpoint,
together with the three-dimensional uniqueness statement of Heilman,
Jagannath and Naor \cite[author version, Remark 1.4]{HJN}, supplies
the rigidity input. Appendix~\ref{app:input} records this dependence
and the endpoint conditions. We retain Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}
in the statement rather than presenting the sharp all-dimensional
inequality as a result proved here.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}, there is a numerical constant
$c_P>0$ such that, for every finite $d\ge2$, $k\ge3$ and every
measurable partition of $(\R^d,\gamma_d)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
                         \Delta(\mathcal A)\ge c_P d_P(\mathcal A)^2.
\end{equation}
The exponent $1/2$ in the corresponding bound on $d_P$ cannot be
increased, even for $d=2$ and $k=3$.
\end{theorem}

There are no prescribed masses, lower bounds on nonzero masses or
regularity assumptions on the cells. The origin is fixed; translations
are not among the symmetries. In particular, after relabelling, the
total mass outside three cells is at most $c_P^{-1/2}\sqrt\Delta$,
and each of the three masses differs from $1/3$ by at most this amount.
The infimum over propellers is attained: the choices of labels are
finite, orthonormal two-frames form a compact set, and sector
indicators are continuous in Gaussian measure under changes of frame.

Khot and Naor \cite{KN} introduced the first-moment problem in kernel
clustering and developed its Gaussian-score duality. Heilman,
Jagannath and Naor \cite{HJN} proved the sharp inequality in dimension
three. A direct quantitative predecessor is Heilman
\cite[Lemma 2.8]{Heilman}. For three cells, his estimate gives
$d_P\le18\Delta^{1/4}$ after translating his squared $L^2$ indicator
distance into disagreement probability. The present assertion allows
an arbitrary finite number of cells, with a constant independent of
both that number and the dimension, and gives the sharp exponent.

This uniformity requires control of cells whose masses vanish.
For example, let $t_1,t_2,t_3$ be a centered equilateral triple with
$\sum_i\|t_i\|^2=1$, and append $N$ mutually orthogonal scores
$\sqrt{E/N}\,e_j$ perpendicular to its plane. Multiply the triple
by $\sqrt{1-E}$. Every one of the $N+3$ scores wins on a set of
positive measure, whereas its expected maximum is at least
$B\sqrt{1-E}$. The resulting score partition has deficit at most
$B^2E$, by dual pairing. Thus near-optimality cannot give a uniform
bound on the number of nonempty cells.

We prove uniform entry into a neighborhood of the equilateral triple
by compactifying sorted score Gram matrices. An infinite-support
equality case is excluded by a two-competitor Gaussian integral.
Within that neighborhood, the combined gain from the additional
scores is cubic in their size. This gives a quadratic score estimate,
which transfers to \eqref{eq:main} through a squared-distance pairing.

\section{Countable Gaussian scores}
Let $\mathcal H$ be a separable real Hilbert space with an isonormal
Gaussian process $X(h)$, so that $\E X(h)X(h')=\langle h,h'\rangle$.
For $v=(v_i)_{i\ge1}$ with $\sum_i\|v_i\|^2<\infty$, define
\[
 M(v)=\E\sup(0,X(v_1),X(v_2),\ldots).
\]
Finite lists are padded with zero vectors. There is no assertion of
an identity-covariance Gaussian random vector taking values in an
infinite-dimensional Hilbert space.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:countable}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:finite},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:countable}
                      M(v)\le B\Bigl(\sum_i\|v_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For any countable measurable partition of the Gaussian probability
space, its first-chaos moments $z_i\in\mathcal H$ satisfy
$\sum_i\|z_i\|^2\le B^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a finite list in a finite-dimensional span, partition the space
according to the largest score, including zero. Pairing the scores
with the cell moments and applying Cauchy--Schwarz proves
\eqref{eq:countable}. For a countable list,
$\E\sum_i X(v_i)^2=\sum_i\|v_i\|^2<\infty$.
Thus the scores lie in $\ell^2$ almost surely. Positive maxima of
finite heads increase to the supremum, and monotone convergence gives
the same bound.

For an event $A$, Riesz representation defines $z(A)$ by
$\langle z(A),h\rangle=\E[\mathbf1_A X(h)]$.
The map from the indicator to its moment is an $L^2$ contraction.
For a countable partition and any finitely supported vector array
$(h_i)$, its assigned score, with zero on untested labels, is at most
the positive maximum. Hence
$\sum_i\langle h_i,z_i\rangle\le B(\sum_i\|h_i\|^2)^{1/2}$.
Duality on finite arrays proves the claimed squared-norm bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:equalitymoments}
Suppose $\sum_i\|v_i\|^2=1$ and $M(v)=B$. Its nonzero scores define
an almost surely unique winning partition with moments $z_i=Bv_i$.
Moreover, $\sum_i z_i=0$ strongly and
$\langle z_i,z_j\rangle\le0$ for $i\ne j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The score sequence tends to zero almost surely, so a positive
supremum is attained. Choose the least maximizing index. Let $A_0$
be the event that all scores are nonpositive, and assign vector zero
to this label. The pairing is integrable by the $L^2(\ell^2)$ bound.
With $z_i=z(A_i)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:countable} gives
\[
 B=\sum_{i\ge0}\langle v_i,z_i\rangle
       \le\Bigl(\sum_{i\ge0}\|z_i\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le B.
\]
Equality gives $z_i=Bv_i$, including $z_0=0$. If $A_0$ had positive
probability, any fixed nonzero score would be strictly negative
almost everywhere on it, contrary to its zero first moment there.
Duplicate nonzero scores would leave a later winning cell empty,
again contradicting $z_i=Bv_i$. Distinct scores tie only on null
Gaussian hyperplanes, and there are countably many such pairs.

The indicators of increasing finite unions of winning cells converge
to one in $L^2$. Contraction of the moment map therefore proves
strong convergence of $\sum_i z_i$ to zero. Merging two cells raises
the squared moment sum by $2\langle z_i,z_j\rangle$. The bound in
Lemma~\ref{lem:countable} forces this inner product to be nonpositive.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:equality}
Every norm-one countable list attaining $M(v)=B$ consists of a
centered equilateral triple of vector norms $1/\sqrt3$ and zero
vectors, up to isometry and relabelling.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We first exclude infinite support. Choose three fixed nonzero moments
$z_1,z_2,z_3$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:equalitymoments}. Put
$r_j=\|z_j\|/B$ and $c=\min_{j\le3}r_j^2>0$.
If there are infinitely many nonzero moments, choose other indices $i$
with $t=\|z_i\|/B\to0$. For such an index set
\[
 e_i=z_i/\|z_i\|,\quad T=X(e_i),\quad
 a_j=-\frac{\langle z_i,z_j\rangle}{\|z_i\|^2},\quad
 w_j=z_j/B+a_jt e_i,\quad Y_j=X(w_j)\quad(j\ne i).
\]
The inner-product signs and strong sum give $a_j\ge0$ and
$\sum_{j\ne i}a_j=1$. The residual variables $Y_j$ are independent
of $T$, with covariance entries
\[
 H_{jk}=\langle z_j,z_k\rangle/B^2-a_ja_kt^2\le0\quad(j\ne k),
 \qquad h_j=H_{jj}=r_j^2-a_j^2t^2.
\]
On the winning cell $A_i$, we have $T\ge0$ and
$Y_j\le(1+a_j)tT$. Its moments satisfy
$\E\mathbf1_{A_i}T=Bt$ and $\E\mathbf1_{A_i}Y_j=0$.

For distinct competitors $j,k$, put
$D_{jk}=\sqrt{h_jh_k-H_{jk}^2}$. If it is positive, the variables
$U=Y_j/((1+a_j)t)$ and $V=Y_k/((1+a_k)t)$ have joint density at most
$t^2(1+a_j)(1+a_k)/(2\pi D_{jk})$. They are independent of $T$.
Taking a positive part and enlarging the integration region gives
\[
 Bt=\E\mathbf1_{A_i}(T+U+V)
       \le\E\bigl[\mathbf1_{\{T\ge0,\ U\le T,\ V\le T\}}
                                     (T+U+V)_+\bigr].
\]
For $T=s\ge0$, the integral of $(s+u+v)_+$ on $u,v\le s$ is
$(3s)^3/6$. Since $\E T_+^3=2/\sqrt{2\pi}$, substitution yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
                         D_{jk}\le\frac3\pi t(1+a_j)(1+a_k).
\end{equation}
If $D_{jk}=0$, this inequality is automatic. This is the
two-competitor integral in \cite[Section 4]{OpenAI}; the preceding
argument verifies the conditions needed for countable lists.

For small $t$, $h_j\ge c-t^2>0$ for $j=1,2,3$. Their correlation
matrix is positive semidefinite with nonpositive off-diagonal entries
$\rho_{jk}$. Testing it on $(1,1,1)$ gives
$\sum_{j<k}|\rho_{jk}|\le3/2$, and hence
$\sum_{j<k}\rho_{jk}^2\le3/2$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinitecontradiction}
 \frac32(c-t^2)^2\le\sum_{j<k\le3}D_{jk}^2
             \le10(3/\pi)^2t^2.
\end{equation}
For the upper bound, $a_1+a_2+a_3\le1$ implies
$\sum_{j<k}(1+a_j)^2(1+a_k)^2<10$. One direct verification is
to increase the sum to one and put
$q=a_1a_2+a_1a_3+a_2a_3$, $r=a_1a_2a_3$. The expression becomes
$9+2q+q^2-8r\le9+2/3+1/9=88/9<10$.
Letting $t\to0$ in \eqref{eq:infinitecontradiction} is impossible.

Thus the list has finite support. Its winning cells depend only on
the finite-dimensional span of those vectors, and their moments
attain $B^2$. Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} makes this partition a
propeller. The identities $z_i=Bv_i$ then give precisely the stated
equilateral triple and zero vectors.
\end{proof}

\section{A uniform neighborhood of the equality arrays}
For a finite norm-one list, sort its vectors by decreasing norm and
pad with zeros. Write
\[
 e(v)=B-M(v),\quad \mu=\tfrac13(v_1+v_2+v_3),\quad u_i=v_i-\mu\ (i\le3),
\]
\[
 \tau^2=\sum_{i>3}\|v_i\|^2,\qquad
 E=\tau^2+3\|\mu\|^2,\qquad S^2=\sum_{i\le3}\|u_i\|^2=1-E.
\]
Let $\alpha\ge\beta\ge0$ be the two singular values of the matrix
with columns $u_1,u_2,u_3$, counting zeros when its rank is smaller
than two. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 E_0=(B/1600)^2,\qquad
 \eta=\inf\{e(v):E\ge E_0\text{ or }\beta\le1/(2\sqrt2)\}.
\end{equation}
The infimum ranges over all finite dimensions and all finite norm-one
lists. Ties in the ordering can be resolved arbitrarily.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:compactness}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:finite}, $\eta>0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider a sequence of sorted norm-one lists with $e(v^{(n)})\to0$.
Their Gram matrices have diagonal entries at most $1/i$. A diagonal
subsequence converges entrywise to a positive semidefinite matrix
$Q$ with $\tr Q\le1$. Realize $Q$ as the Gram matrix of a countable
list in a separable Hilbert space.

Expected maxima converge along this subsequence. To check the tails,
for any $a>0$ the Gaussian tail bound and integration give
\begin{align}\label{eq:tail}
 \E\sup_{i>N}X(v_i^{(n)})_+
 &\le a+\sum_{i>N}\frac{e^{-ia^2/2}}{ia}\notag\\
 &\le a+\frac{e^{-(N+1)a^2/2}}
                   {(N+1)a(1-e^{-a^2/2})}.
\end{align}
The right side tends to zero with
$a=\sqrt{4\log(N+1)/(N+1)}$, uniformly in $n$ and in the
correlations. The limit list satisfies the same bound.
For each finite head, couple the Gaussian vectors by the continuous
positive square roots of their covariance matrices. This proves
$L^1$ convergence of the positive maxima, including singular limits.
Together with \eqref{eq:tail}, it proves convergence of $M$.

The limit therefore has $M=B$. Lemma~\ref{lem:countable} forces
$\tr Q=1$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:equality} identifies it as the
Gram matrix of an equilateral triple and zeros. The decreasing order
places that triple first. For the finite lists, $E$ has the head
formula $1-\sum_{i\le3}\|u_i\|^2$, so it is continuous in those
Gram entries, as are the singular values. Thus $E\to0$ and
$\beta\to1/\sqrt2$. Such a sequence cannot remain in the closed
set defining \eqref{eq:eta}. This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

The lemma gives a single gap over all dimensions and list lengths.
It gives no power law by itself. The rate comes from the local
estimate in the next section.

\section{Local stability with extra scores}
Let $\cT_{d,k}$ be the norm-one arrays in $(\R^d)^k$ consisting
of a centered equilateral triple and zeros, with arbitrary labels.
We prove a uniform squared Euclidean distance estimate for the scores.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:activation}
If $E<E_0$ and $\beta>1/(2\sqrt2)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:activation}
 M(v)\le M_3(u)+400E^{3/2},\qquad
 M_3(u)=\E\max_{i\le3}X(u_i).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The centered triple spans a plane $L$. A reference equilateral triple
of total squared norm one has singular values $1/\sqrt2$ and
inradius $1/(2\sqrt3)$. Applying the linear map carrying it to
$(u_i)$ gives, for $R=\|g_L\|$,
\[
                    \max_{i\le3}X(u_i)\ge hR,\qquad h=1/(4\sqrt3).
\]
The planar projection of $v_j-\mu$ for $j>3$ has norm at most
$\tau+\|\mu\|\le2\sqrt E\le h/2$. This also bounds the projection
of the appended zero competitor minus $\mu$. Write
\[
 Z=\Bigl(\sum_{j>3}X(v_j^\perp)^2\Bigr)^{1/2}+|X(\mu^\perp)|.
\]
It is independent of the planar radius $R$. Comparing every extra
score, including zero, with the leading three gives pointwise
\[
 0\le\max(0,X(v_1),\ldots)-\max_{i\le3}X(v_i)
                                  \le(Z-hR/2)_+.
\]
For any centered Gaussian vector with covariance trace $q^2$,
$\E\|G\|^4\le3q^4$, hence $\E\|G\|^3\le3^{3/4}q^3$.
Using $(a+b)^3\le4(a^3+b^3)$ and
$\E|X(\mu^\perp)|^3\le2\sqrt{2/\pi}\|\mu\|^3$ yields
\[
                            \E Z^3\le12E^{3/2}.
\]
This depends only on the covariance trace, not its rank.
For $z\ge0$, the density $r e^{-r^2/2}$ of $R$ gives
\[
 \E_R(z-hR/2)_+\le\int_0^{2z/h}(z-hr/2)r\,dr=32z^3.
\]
The expected common score $X(\mu)$ is zero. Integration proves
$M(v)-M_3(u)\le384E^{3/2}\le400E^{3/2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:triangle}
In the same region,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 e(v)\ge\frac B4 E+\frac3{64\sqrt{2\pi}}(\alpha-\beta)^2.
\end{equation}
There is $t\in\cT_{d,k}$ supported on the first three labels with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar}
 \|v-t\|^2=2-\sqrt2(\alpha+\beta)
                         \le2E+2(\alpha-\beta)^2.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3$ be the side lengths of the centered
triangle. Integrating its support function over directions gives its
perimeter, so polar Gaussian integration gives
\[
              M_3(u)=\frac{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3}{2\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
The support-function identity follows by integrating each supporting
vertex on its normal-angle interval; the endpoint terms sum to the
edge lengths. The radial factor is $\E R/(2\pi)=1/(2\sqrt{2\pi})$.
We have $\sum_i\ell_i^2=3S^2$ and $\ell_i\le\sqrt2 S$.
Writing the three squared lengths as
$S^2+(\alpha^2-\beta^2)\cos(2\theta_i-\phi)$, with edge angles
separated by $\pi/3$ modulo sign, gives
\[
 \sum_{i<j}(\ell_i^2-\ell_j^2)^2
                              =\frac92(\alpha^2-\beta^2)^2.
\]
Consequently,
\begin{align*}
 3S-\sum_i\ell_i
 &=\frac{\sum_{i<j}(\ell_i-\ell_j)^2}{3S+\sum_i\ell_i}
   \ge\frac{3(\alpha^2-\beta^2)^2}{32S^3},\\
 BS-M_3(u)&\ge\frac3{64\sqrt{2\pi}}(\alpha-\beta)^2.
\end{align*}
For the last line use $(\alpha+\beta)^2\ge S^2$ and $S\le1$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:activation}, $1-\sqrt{1-E}\ge E/2$, and
$400\sqrt E\le B/4$ now prove \eqref{eq:local}.

Polar decomposition of the centered three-column matrix aligns it
with an equilateral triple whose two singular values are $1/\sqrt2$.
The common mean is orthogonal, in array space, to this centered
triple. Thus the optimal aligned inner product is
$(\alpha+\beta)/\sqrt2$, giving the equality in \eqref{eq:polar}.
Put $q=(\alpha-\beta)^2/S^2\in[0,1]$ and note that
$\sqrt2(\alpha+\beta)=2S\sqrt{1-q/2}$. The inequalities
$1-S\le E$, $1-\sqrt{1-a}\le a$, and $S\ge1/2$ imply
\[
 2-\sqrt2(\alpha+\beta)\le2E+(\alpha-\beta)^2/S
                            \le2E+2(\alpha-\beta)^2.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:score}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cv}
 c_0=\frac3{128\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad c_V=\min\{c_0,\eta/4\}>0.
\end{equation}
For every finite norm-one array with $d\ge2$ and $k\ge3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
                         B-M(v)\ge c_V\dist(v,\cT_{d,k})^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the local region this follows from
\eqref{eq:local}--\eqref{eq:polar}. Outside it,
Lemma~\ref{lem:compactness} gives $e(v)\ge\eta$, whereas the
squared distance between two unit arrays is at most four.
Sorting merely permutes labels and does not change the distance.
\end{proof}

\section{From scores to partitions}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Suppose first that $F=F(\mathcal A)>0$, and put $v_i=z_i/\sqrt F$.
Assigning each point its actual label shows $M(v)\ge\sqrt F$.
Choose a closest $t\in\cT_{d,k}$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:score};
the minimum exists by compactness. Its winning cells define a
propeller $\mathcal P$ with the corresponding labels. The mean
loss from assigning the label of $\mathcal A$ to these scores is
\begin{align}\label{eq:calibration}
 J&=\E\bigl[\max_i X(t_i)-X(t_{\mathcal A(g)})\bigr]
       =B-\sum_i\langle t_i,z_i\rangle\notag\\
  &=B-\sqrt F+\frac{\sqrt F}{2}\|v-t\|^2\notag\\
  &\le\left(1+\frac B{2c_V}\right)(B-\sqrt F)
   \le\left(B^{-1}+(2c_V)^{-1}\right)\Delta.
\end{align}
The equality uses that both arrays have norm one.

Let $G(g)$ be the difference between the winning score of $t$ and
the highest score with a different label. The three active pair
differences are standard normal. For $0<s\le1$, their three
absolute slabs have total probability at most $3\sqrt{2/\pi}s$.
All inactive scores are the same zero score. An inactive score is
within $s$ of the winner only if
$R/(2\sqrt3)\le s$, where $R$ is the planar Gaussian radius.
That event has probability at most $6s^2$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
                       \Prob(G\le s)\le3\sqrt{2/\pi}s+6s^2\le9s.
\end{equation}
For $k=3$ the zero-score term is unnecessary, but harmless.
If $\epsilon$ is the probability of a label disagreement, then
$J\ge s(\epsilon-9s)$. Take $s=\epsilon/18$ to obtain
$J\ge\epsilon^2/36\ge d_P(\mathcal A)^2/36$.
Together with \eqref{eq:calibration}, this proves \eqref{eq:main} with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp}
              c_P=\left[36\left(B^{-1}+(2c_V)^{-1}\right)\right]^{-1}.
\end{equation}
If $F=0$, then $\Delta=B^2$ and $d_P\le1$, and the same constant
works because $c_P\le B/36<B^2$.

For sharpness, in the plane take consecutive sectors of openings
$2\pi/3+t$, $2\pi/3-t$ and $2\pi/3$, for small $t>0$.
A sector of angle $a$ has first-moment norm $\sin(a/2)/\sqrt{2\pi}$.
It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp}
                 \Delta=\frac{1-\cos t}{4\pi},\qquad d_P=\frac{t}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
For the distance, the largest cell has excess mass $t/(2\pi)$ over
$1/3$, a lower bound for disagreement under every alignment. Keeping
two original boundaries fixed attains that lower bound. Thus
$\Delta/d_P^2\to\pi/2$, excluding any uniform estimate
$d_P\le C\Delta^a$ with $a>1/2$.
\end{proof}

The constant \eqref{eq:cp} is specified through the positive infimum
\eqref{eq:eta}; no decimal value or optimal constant is asserted.
All list lengths in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are finite. Countable
arrays enter only to prove the uniform finite-list estimate.

\appendix
\section{The finite equality input}\label{app:input}
We explain how the finite rigidity clause in
Assumption~\ref{ass:finite} relates to the cited sources. This keeps
the quantitative proof separate from the underlying sharp theorem.
The revision of \cite{OpenAI} is fixed in the bibliography.
Its main theorem gives the finite bound $B^2$, and its
\texttt{ext:duality} gives the equivalent finite Gaussian-score
bound. The equality case is obtained from its proof as follows.

For a finite equality partition, dual pairing shows that its cells
are winning cells for their moment scores. A cell with zero moment
cannot have positive measure: on such a winning cell every nonzero
score is nonpositive, whereas the moment of that cell is zero.
Merging any pair of cells shows that the off-diagonal moment products
are nonpositive. Merge zero-product pairs until every product is
strictly negative. The Gram matrix then satisfies
\[
 a^TQa=\sum_{i<j}(-\langle z_i,z_j\rangle)(a_i-a_j)^2,
\]
so its rank is $m-1$, where $m$ is the remaining active count.

For $m\ge5$, the scalar estimates in \cite{OpenAI},
Lemma 3.1, and the two residual inequalities in its Corollary 4.2
remain valid at objective value $C=B^2$. In their notation,
\[
 r_i=\|z_i\|/B,\quad P_i=\gamma(A_i),\quad
 r_1\ge\cdots\ge r_m=t>0,\quad L=r_1^2+r_2^2,\quad c=r_3^2,
\]
they assert
\begin{gather*}
 r_i\le2/3,\qquad P_i\ge A r_i^2,\qquad
 P_i\ge0.415r_i+0.15r_i^2,\\
 A=0.929\ (m=5),\qquad A=0.884\ (m\ge6),\\
 (c-t^2)(L-c-2t^2)\le8(3/\pi)^2t^2,\qquad
 \tfrac32(c-t^2)^2\le10(3/\pi)^2t^2.
\end{gather*}
Here all terminating decimals are exact rational numbers. The
halfspace estimate $r_i<2/3$ under strict excess becomes $r_i\le2/3$;
the scalar intervals already include this endpoint. The one-score
deletion and translation inequalities use optimality and the
full-rank score structure. Replacing $C>B^2$ by $C=B^2$ preserves
their non-strict lower bounds. The pairwise density integral likewise
preserves its non-strict conclusion, as in \eqref{eq:pair} above.

The elimination in \cite[Section 5 and Appendix A]{OpenAI} uses
only the displayed inequalities and the normalizations
$\sum_i r_i^2=\sum_iP_i=1$. It obtains $t<0.12$ and $c>2.6t$
with strict rational margins. These contradict
\[
 10(3/\pi)^2t^2\ge1.5(c-t^2)^2
       >1.5(2.6-0.12)^2t^2,
\]
since $1.5(2.6-0.12)^2-10(3/\pi)^2>0.1066$.
Thus its exclusion still applies at equality. We use those scalar
estimates and their finite certificates as part of the pinned source
input, not as numerical experiments of this paper.

For $m\le4$, the score partition factors through a space of dimension
at most three. The uniqueness statement in the Princeton author
version of \cite[Remark 1.4]{HJN} makes it a three-cell propeller.
This remark is version-specific and is absent from arXiv v2.
No earlier zero-product merge was possible: the last such merge
would start with a four-active-cell equality partition, which also
factors through dimension at most three and contradicts that
uniqueness statement. This gives finite rigidity before merging.
The computer-assisted three-dimensional theorem is an external
input; its computation is not rerun here.
