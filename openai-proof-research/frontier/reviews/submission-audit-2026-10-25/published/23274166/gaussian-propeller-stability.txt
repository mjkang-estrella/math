       DIMENSION- AND LABEL-UNIFORM STABILITY OF GAUSSIAN
                          PROPELLERS

                                               MYEONGJIN KANG


       Abstract. Assume the sharp ﬁnite Gaussian propeller inequality and its ﬁnite equality classiﬁca-
       tion. We prove that the deﬁcit in the squared ﬁrst-moment functional controls the square of the
       Gaussian measure distance to a propeller, with one positive constant independent of the ambient
       dimension and the number of cells. Cells may have arbitrarily small measure, and no masses are
       prescribed. The proof passes to countable Gaussian score arrays, excludes inﬁnite-support equal-
       ity cases, and bounds the gain from all additional scores by the three-halves power of their total
       squared norm and the squared common mean of the leading triple. A quadratic estimate for that
       triple and a Gaussian margin bound then give the partition estimate. The square-root dependence
       of distance on deﬁcit is sharp already for three planar sectors. The universal constant is positive
       but implicit.

                                Appendix A. The finite equality input
   We explain how the ﬁnite rigidity clause in Assumption 1.1 relates to the cited sources. This
keeps the quantitative proof separate from the underlying sharp theorem. The revision of [4] is
ﬁxed in the bibliography. Its main theorem gives the ﬁnite bound B 2 , and its ext:duality gives
the equivalent ﬁnite Gaussian-score bound. The equality case is obtained from its proof as follows.
   For a ﬁnite equality partition, dual pairing shows that its cells are winning cells for their moment
scores. A cell with zero moment cannot have positive measure: on such a winning cell every nonzero
score is nonpositive, whereas the moment of that cell is zero. Merging any pair of cells shows that
the oﬀ-diagonal moment products are nonpositive. Merge zero-product pairs until every product is
strictly negative. The Gram matrix then satisﬁes
