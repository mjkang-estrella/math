\title{The sharp minimum multiplicity\linebreak in a staircase tensor square}
\author{Myeongjin Kang}\date{October 18, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Let $\rho_m=(m,m-1,\ldots,1)$ and $N_m=m(m+1)/2$.
Assuming the pinned cyclic-polytabloid source theorems specified here,
we prove that every irreducible complex representation of $S_{N_m}$,
other than the trivial and sign representations, occurs in
$S^{\rho_m}\otimes S^{\rho_m}$ with multiplicity at least $m-1$,
for every $m\ge2$. The bound is sharp at $(N_m-1,1)$.
The proof quantitatively evaluates an inherited recursion for a specified
cyclic submodule. An asymmetric invariant has four exceptional shapes.
Two nonexceptional predecessors handle diagrams with large first-four-row
capacity; explicit rigged configurations give a uniform Kostka bound in
the complementary range. Exact finite calculations supply the starts through
order eight. The uniform estimate is presented as new to this project;
new-to-literature priority is not claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, notation, and dependencies}\label{sec:intro}
All representations in this article are finite-dimensional complex
representations. Tensor products of representations of one symmetric group
carry its diagonal action. For partitions $\alpha,\beta,\lambda\vdash n$, write
\[
 g(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)
 =\dim\Hom_{S_n}(S^\lambda,S^\alpha\otimes S^\beta).
\]
Partitions are padded by zero parts when necessary; $\lambda^{\trp}$ denotes
conjugation and $\len(\lambda)$ denotes the number of positive parts.
Put $\rho_m=(m,m-1,\ldots,1)$ and $N_m=m(m+1)/2$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every integer $m\ge2$ and every $\lambda\vdash N_m$ with
$\lambda\notin\{(N_m),(1^{N_m})\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 g(\rho_m,\rho_m,\lambda)\ge m-1.
\end{equation}
Moreover $g(\rho_m,\rho_m,(N_m-1,1))=m-1$.
\end{theorem}

The source theorems of OpenAI \cite{cyclic,universal} are used at the fixed
revision
\begin{center}\small\path{fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb}.\end{center}
Their truth is an explicit premise of this work. We do not reprove or audit
them. Section~\ref{sec:source} states exactly which module-level conclusions
are used; ordinary Saxl positivity by itself would not suffice.
The other non-elementary combinatorial input is the standard positive
rigged-configuration formula for Kostka numbers, in the convention established
in Section~\ref{sec:rc} from Kirillov--Schilling--Shimozono \cite{KSS}.
The finitely many starts are exact computations described in
Section~\ref{sec:finite}. Every remaining all-order estimate is proved below.

The band multiplicity recursion, including the rule of taking the maximum
of separate quotient bounds, was developed in I19, Section~4 \cite{I19}.
The dominance-module and Kostka refinement was developed in I21,
Section~2.1 \cite{I21}, using the source's explicit maps. Neither is a new
construction here. The residual result is the uniform evaluation of this
recursion: a fixed exceptional set, a predecessor estimate, and a quantitative
low-capacity Kostka bound. The high-capacity predecessor argument was supplied
by parent author assistance and is incorporated in full in
Section~\ref{sec:high}; that assistance is part of the author development,
not an independent review.

Our claim concerns the ordinary tensor square. It does not give the
multiplicity separately in its symmetric and exterior squares, in the
cyclic submodule, over the integers, or in positive characteristic.
In particular it does not resolve the earlier signed-square target of
I19 and I21. The familiar corner formula supplies sharpness, not a new
representation-theoretic input.

\section{The cyclic source and the inherited recursion}\label{sec:source}
Let
\[
 B_m=\{(i,j)\in\Z_{\ge1}^2:i+j\le m+1\},\qquad G_m=S_{B_m}.
\]
For an ordered block of $r$ positions, alternate the tensor
$e_1\otimes\cdots\otimes e_r$ without normalization. Taking the tensor
product of these alternating tensors over rows of $B_m$ gives $v_R$;
taking them over columns gives $v_C$. Rows are ordered by increasing
column index and columns by increasing row index. With $E=\C^m$,
these are tensors in $E^{\otimes B_m}$. The pinned source identifies
both orbit spans with $S^{\rho_m}$ and defines
\[
 W_m=\C[G_m](v_R\otimes v_C)\subseteq S^{\rho_m}\otimes S^{\rho_m}.
\]
This fixes the smaller cyclic modules used throughout; they are never
replaced by the full smaller tensor squares.

\begin{proposition}[Source contracts]\label{prop:contracts}
The following hold for these specified modules.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Every $S^\lambda$, $\lambda\vdash N_m$, occurs in $W_m$.
\item For $s\in\{1,2\}$ and $h=m-s\ge1$, there is a surjection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 W_m\twoheadrightarrow
 \Ind_{S_{N_h}\times S_{N_m-N_h}}^{S_{N_m}}
       (W_h\boxtimes U_{m,s}).
\end{equation}
Here $U_{m,s}$ is the cyclic module of the specified alternating row and
column tensors on $B_m\setminus B_h$.
\item $U_{m,1}\cong S^{(m)}$. For $m\ge3$, $U_{m,2}$ contains at least
one copy of every $S^\eta$ with $\eta\vdash2m-1$ and $\len(\eta)\le4$.
\item If $K_{\alpha,\mu}$ is the Kostka number, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kbound}
 [W_m:S^\lambda]\ge K_{\lambda^{\trp},\rho_m}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Input references and multiplicity justification]
Part (i) is \cite[\path{dominance.tex}, \path{thm:cyclic-saxl}]{cyclic}.
Part (ii) is its \path{band-quotient.tex},
\path{prop:band-quotient}. That proposition gives the entire induced
module, not an unspecified submodule: disjoint high-letter coordinate
sectors realize the disjoint-coset model of induction. Part (iii) is
\path{path:one-band} and \path{prop:two-band} in
\path{path-band.tex}. Only this support inclusion is needed. The
stronger identification of the whole width-two band using the
multiplicity-free two-row square is inherited context \cite{I19,I21,BB},
not an additional premise of our lower bound.

For (iv), recall the actual source dominance maps. If $H_R$ is the row
subgroup, its signed average gives $v_R\otimes u\in W_m$, where
$u=\sum_{h\in H_R}h v_C\ne0$ is $H_R$-fixed. The alternating line in
$S^{\rho_m}|_{H_R}$ is one-dimensional. Reassociating a sign twist into
the second factor therefore sends this tensor to a nonzero multiple of
$z=v_R\otimes v_R$. Thus an isomorphic image of $W_m\otimes\sgn$
contains $Z_m=\C[G_m]z$.
The source's pair-letter/content projection sends $z$ to
\[
 c_m t_m,\qquad c_m=(-1)^{\sum_{r=1}^m\binom r2}\prod_{r=1}^m r!\ne0,
\]
where the orbit of $t_m$ is the word basis of $M^{\rho_m}$.
It follows that $Z_m$ surjects onto $M^{\rho_m}$. Semisimplicity and
Young's rule give (iv), with multiplicities. These maps appear in \cite{cyclic} under the labels
\begin{flushleft}\small
\path{dom:signed-average}, \path{dom:repeated-row-cyclic},\\
\path{dom:noncancellation}, \path{dom:permutation-quotient}.
\end{flushleft}
I21 proves additionally $Z_m\cong M^{\rho_m}$; we only need the surjection.
\end{proof}

Write $c_{\nu,\eta}^{\lambda}$ for a Littlewood--Richardson coefficient.
Following the multiplicity framework and max rule of
I19, Section~4 \cite{I19}, with the I21 Kostka refinement \cite{I21},
define $R_1((1))=1$ and, for $m\ge2$, define $R_m(\lambda)$ as the maximum of
\begin{align}
 &1,\qquad K_{\lambda^{\trp},\rho_m},\label{eq:Rdef}\\
 &\sum_{\nu\vdash N_{m-1}}R_{m-1}(\nu)c_{\nu,(m)}^{\lambda},\notag\\
 &\sum_{\substack{\nu\vdash N_{m-2},\ \eta\vdash2m-1\\\len(\eta)\le4}}
       R_{m-2}(\nu)c_{\nu,\eta}^{\lambda}
       \quad\text{if }m\ge3.\notag
\end{align}
Induction and Proposition~\ref{prop:contracts} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rvalid}
 R_m(\lambda)\le[W_m:S^\lambda],\qquad
 g(\rho_m,\rho_m,\lambda)\ge
       \max\{R_m(\lambda),R_m(\lambda^{\trp})\}.
\end{equation}
Contributions within one quotient are added. Separate quotient bounds
are maximized; their images have not been proved independent. The second
inequality uses self-conjugacy of the staircase and sign invariance of the
\emph{ambient square}. No sign invariance of $W_m$ is assumed.

For $h\ge5$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exceptions}
 \mathcal E_h=\{(N_h),(1^{N_h}),H_h,J_h\},\qquad
 H_h=(h,1^{N_h-h}),\quad J_h=(h,2,1^{N_h-h-2}).
\end{equation}
We prove the asymmetric invariant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariant}
 R_h(\nu)\ge h-1\quad(h\ge5,\ \nu\vdash N_h,\ \nu\notin\mathcal E_h).
\end{equation}
The exact bases at $h=5,6$ and finite steps at $m=7,8$ appear in
Section~\ref{sec:finite}. Sections~\ref{sec:wide}--\ref{sec:assembly}
prove the step for $m\ge9$, using only \eqref{eq:invariant} at $m-1,m-2$.

\section{Wide diagrams}\label{sec:wide}
Suppose $m\ge9$, $\lambda\vdash N_m$, $\lambda_1>m$, and $\lambda$ is
not a row. If $\lambda$ is not a rectangle, its horizontal $m$-strip
predecessors are counted by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hstripcount}
 [x^m]\prod_i(1+x+\cdots+x^{\lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1}}).
\end{equation}
Indeed a horizontal strip is precisely the interlacing
$\lambda_i\ge\nu_i\ge\lambda_{i+1}$. There are at least two positive
gaps, and $0<m<\lambda_1$. A product of at least two nonconstant initial
geometric sums has every strictly interior coefficient at least two.
For two factors this follows directly by counting bounded pairs with
fixed sum; multiplying by another factor preserves the assertion by
convolution. Thus there are at least two predecessors.

Put $h=m-1$. At least one predecessor is outside $\mathcal E_h$.
If $(N_h)$ is a predecessor, $\lambda$ has at most two rows and none of
the other exceptions fits. If $(1^{N_h})$ is a predecessor, $\lambda$
is a hook; its two predecessors have consecutive first-row lengths,
which cannot both lie among the exceptional hook widths $1,h$.
The remaining possibility that all predecessors are exceptional would
require both $H_h$ and $J_h$. Their interlacing implies
$\lambda_2\le h$ and $\lambda_3\le1$. But then
\[
 \nu'=(h+1,1^{N_h-h-1})
\]
also interlaces $\lambda$: its first row lies between $\lambda_1$ and
$\lambda_2$, its second between $\lambda_2$ and $\lambda_3$, and its
remaining parts agree with $J_h$. It is not exceptional.
One good predecessor contributes $m-2$, and another contributes at
least one. The one-band term proves $R_m(\lambda)\ge m-1$.

If $\lambda=(a^b)$ is a rectangle, then $a\ge m+1$ and $b\ge2$.
Choose
\begin{align*}
 \eta_1&=(m+1,m-2),&\nu_1&=(a^{b-2},a-m+2,a-m-1),\\
 \eta_2&=(m,m-1),&\nu_2&=(a^{b-2},a-m+1,a-m).
\end{align*}
Zero parts are omitted. The rectangle-complement LR identity gives
$c_{\nu_i,\eta_i}^{\lambda}=1$. Both predecessors are outside
$\mathcal E_{m-2}$. For $b\ge3$ their width is $a>m-2$ and they are
not rows. For $b=2$ they have at most two rows, and $\nu_2$ has two
positive parts. The only potential one-row case for $\nu_1$ would have
$a=m+1$ and $N_m=2(m+1)$, forcing $m=4$. Thus the two-band term is at
least $2(m-3)\ge m-1$. This finishes the wide case.

\section{Two predecessors at high capacity}\label{sec:high}
The next lemma incorporates the parent author-side construction
\cite{parenthigh}, with its full proof. Let
$C(\lambda)=\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3+\lambda_4$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:high}
Let $m\ge7$, $h=m-2$, $n=N_h$, and $\lambda\vdash N_m$ satisfy
$\lambda_1\le m$ and $C(\lambda)\ge2m+1$. There are two distinct
$\nu\vdash n$, both outside $\mathcal E_h$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourstrip}
 \lambda_i\ge\nu_i\ge\lambda_{i+4}\quad\text{for every }i.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $\mu=(\lambda_5,\lambda_6,\ldots)$ and consider the skew diagram
$S=\lambda/\mu$ with coordinate order:
$(i,j)\le(i',j')$ if $i\le i'$ and $j\le j'$. Partitions
$\mu\subseteq\nu\subseteq\lambda$ correspond exactly to order ideals
of $S$. An interval between comparable cells lies inside the skew
diagram, so this is also the order generated by row and column adjacencies.
The desired rank is
\[
 k=n-|\mu|=C-(2m-1),\qquad k\ge2,\quad C-k=2m-1\ge13.
\]
Write $H_j=\lambda^{\trp}_j$ and $L_j=\max(1,H_j-3)$. Column $j$
of $S$ consists of rows $L_j,\ldots,H_j$, so has at most four cells;
both endpoint sequences are nonincreasing. Put $w=\lambda_1$.

We first prove that every rank $r$ with $2\le r\le C-2$ has at least
two ideals. Suppose $A$ were the unique rank-$r$ ideal, and write
$B=S\setminus A$. Every $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ must satisfy $a\le b$.
The alternative $b<a$ contradicts downward closure. If they were
incomparable, adjoining the relation $b<a$ would still give a partial
order. A linear extension of that order has a first-$r$ ideal of the
original order different from $A$, a contradiction.

Let $c$ be the greatest column meeting $A$ and $d$ the least column
meeting $B$. Cross-comparability forces $c\le d$. If $c<d$, column one
lies wholly in $A$ and column $w$ wholly in $B$, whence $H_1\le L_w$.
Since $H_1\ge H_w\ge L_w$, this forces $H_1=H_w=1$ and
$C=w\le m$, impossible. The same argument applies if $1<c=d<w$.
If $c=d=1$, all of $A$ is a prefix of column one and column $w$ lies
in $B$, so $L_1+r-1\le L_w\le L_1$, giving $r\le1$.
If $c=d=w$, all of $B$ is a suffix of column $w$ and column one lies
in $A$, so $H_w-|B|+1\ge H_1\ge H_w$, giving $|B|\le1$.
Finally $w=1$ would give $C\le4$. Every case is impossible.

If no exceptional shape is an interval predecessor, this rank count
at $k$ finishes the proof. Suppose an exception fits. The row $(n)$
cannot fit, since $n>m\ge\lambda_1$. If the column fits, then
$\lambda_5\le1$; if $H_h$ fits, $\lambda_6\le1$; if $J_h$ fits,
$\lambda_7\le1$. Thus every column after the first has height at
most six. Also
\[
 \len(\lambda)\ge n-h=(m-2)(m-3)/2\ge10.
\]
The four cells of $S$ in column one consequently lie in rows at least
seven. They are incomparable with all other cells of $S$, which lie
in rows at most six and columns at least two. Therefore $S$ is the
disjoint union of a four-cell chain and a poset $S'$ of size $C-4$.

A finite poset has an ideal of every size from zero to its cardinality,
by taking prefixes of a linear extension. Since $k\le C-4$, for each
$a=0,\ldots,\min(4,k)$ combine a length-$a$ prefix of the separated
chain with a rank-$(k-a)$ ideal of $S'$. This gives at least
$\min(4,k)+1$ predecessors. At most two exceptions fit simultaneously:
their lengths are $n,n-h+1,n-h$, whereas every predecessor has length
in the interval $[H_1-4,H_1]$, of width four, and $h\ge5$.
For $k\ge3$, at least four predecessors therefore leave two usable ones.

It remains that $k=2$, or $C=2m+1$. The construction gives three
predecessors, whose lengths differ by at most two. The column cannot
coexist with either $H_h$ or $J_h$, because the corresponding length
differences are $h-1\ge4$ and $h\ge5$. If both $H_h,J_h$ fit, then
$\lambda_6\le1$ and $\lambda_5\ge1$. The number of cells of $\mu$
outside its first column is $\lambda_5-1$. Since $J_h$ has $h$ cells
outside its first column and $|J_h/\mu|=2$, we get
$\lambda_5\ge h-1=m-3$. This contradicts
\[
 C\ge4\lambda_5\ge4(m-3)>2m+1\qquad(m\ge7).
\]
So at most one exception fits, leaving two usable predecessors.
\end{proof}

Condition~\eqref{eq:fourstrip} says that every column of $\lambda/\nu$
has at most four cells. Filling each column, from top to bottom, by
$1,2,3,4$ as needed gives a semistandard skew tableau. In row $i$,
column $j$, the entry is $i-\nu^{\trp}_j$, which weakly increases
with $j$. Hence $s_{\lambda/\nu}(1,1,1,1)>0$. By the LR expansion,
some $\eta$ with $\len(\eta)\le4$ has $c_{\nu,\eta}^{\lambda}>0$.
The two predecessors of Lemma~\ref{lem:high} contribute separately to
the same two-band term in \eqref{eq:Rdef}. Under the inductive hypothesis
it is at least $2(m-3)\ge m-1$.

\section{A uniform low-capacity Kostka bound}\label{sec:low}
\subsection{The configuration convention}\label{sec:rc}
For a partition $\beta$, put
\[
 Q_j(\beta)=\sum_i\min(j,\beta_i),\qquad
 b_j(\beta)=\#\{i:\beta_i=j\}.
\]
For partitions $\alpha,\mu$ of the same integer, a configuration is a
sequence of partitions $\nu^{(1)},\nu^{(2)},\ldots$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RCsize}
 |\nu^{(k)}|=\sum_{i>k}\alpha_i,\qquad \nu^{(0)}=\mu,
\end{equation}
and nonnegative vacancies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vacancies}
 P_j^{(k)}=Q_j(\nu^{(k-1)})-2Q_j(\nu^{(k)})+Q_j(\nu^{(k+1)})\ge0
 \quad(k,j\ge1).
\end{equation}
There is \emph{no upper cutoff on string lengths $j$ at $\mu_1$}.
The size constraints alone make the relevant choices finite.
The positive configuration formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fermionic}
 K_{\alpha,\mu}=
 \sum_{\nu\text{ admissible}}\prod_{k,j\ge1}
 \binom{P_j^{(k)}+b_j(\nu^{(k)})}{b_j(\nu^{(k)})}.
\end{equation}

For precision about conventions, \eqref{eq:fermionic} follows from
\cite[Section~2.2, (2.4)--(2.6), Theorem~2.10]{KSS} at $q=1$.
In their notation take target shape $\alpha^{\trp}$ and rectangles
$R_a=(1^{\mu_a})$, each a \emph{column}. Their size constraints become
\eqref{eq:RCsize}; their $\xi^{(1)}(R)$ is $\mu$ and all other
$\xi^{(k)}(R)$ are empty, giving \eqref{eq:vacancies}.
The multiplicity on their left is the coefficient of $s_{\alpha^{\trp}}$
in $\prod_a e_{\mu_a}$. Applying the standard involution
$\omega(s_\alpha)=s_{\alpha^{\trp}}$, $\omega(h_r)=e_r$, and Young's
rule identifies it with $K_{\alpha,\mu}$.
KSS's partitions and vacancy indices range over all positive lengths.
This explicitly supplies the unbounded convention needed below, independently
of the matrix-width wording in \cite[Section~1]{Kirillov}.
Kirillov's maximal configuration and one-cell perturbation
\cite[Corollary~1.3 and proof of Theorem~2.1]{Kirillov} are the inherited
operations we now quantify.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:low}
Let $d\ge8$, $N=N_d$, and $L\vdash N$ satisfy
$L_1\le d-1$ and $L_1+L_2+L_3+L_4\le2d$.
Then $K_{L^{\trp},\rho_d}\ge d$, except possibly at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowexceptions}
 (1^N),\quad (2,1^{N-2}),\quad
 (d-1,1^{N-d+1}),\quad(d-1,d-1,1^{N-2d+2}).
\end{equation}
On the three noncolumn exceptions a single configuration has weight $d-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $w=L_1$, $t=\#\{i:L_i\ge2\}$, $s=\#\{i:L_i=1\}$,
$\alpha=L^{\trp}$, and
\[
 D_j=\sum_{i\le j}((\rho_d)_i-L_i).
\]
For $4\le j\le d-1$, monotonicity of prefix averages and the capacity bound give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dmiddle}
 D_j\ge\frac{j(d-j+1)}2\ge d-1.
\end{equation}
For $j\ge d$, $D_j$ is the area below row $j$ of $L$, hence is
nonnegative. If $w\le d-2$, then $D_1\ge2,D_2\ge3,D_3\ge3$.
If $w=d-1$, then $D_1=1$, $D_2=d-L_2\ge1$ and $D_3\ge d-2$:
there are at least four rows and $L_1+L_2+L_3\le2d-1$.
In particular every $D_j$ is nonnegative.

The maximal configuration is
$\nu^{(k)}=(\alpha_{k+1},\alpha_{k+2},\ldots)$.
Its first vacancy is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxvac}
 P_j^{(1)}=D_j+\min(j,t+s)-\min(j,t).
\end{equation}
It equals $D_j$ for $j\le t$ (and also for all $j$ if $s=0$).
For $k\ge2$ all vacancies are nonnegative, and the vacancy is zero
at every occupied length of $\nu^{(k)}$. Its weight is consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maxweight}
 M=\prod_{j=1}^{t}
    \binom{D_j+L_j-L_{j+1}}{L_j-L_{j+1}}.
\end{equation}
If $s=0$, the actual first-partition multiplicity at $j=t$ is
$L_t-1$, rather than $L_t$; however $D_t=0$, so both displayed and
actual factors are one. Formula~\eqref{eq:maxweight} remains valid.
For $w=1$ we have the column exception; henceforth $w\ge2$.

The conjugate of $\nu^{(1)}$ is $q_j=\max(L_j-1,0)$.
For $1\le u<v\le t$, replace it by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:move}
 q'_j=q_j+\mathbf1_{j=u}-\mathbf1_{j=v}.
\end{equation}
If $q'$ is a partition and $D_j\ge2$ on $u\le j<v$, this is an
admissible distinct configuration. Indeed $Q_j(\nu^{(1)})$ increases
by one exactly on $[u,v)$, so the first vacancies decrease by two
and the second increase by one there. All other vacancies stay unchanged.
That interval lies in $[1,t)$, where \eqref{eq:maxvac} equals $D_j$.
Let $W$ denote the weight of the modified configuration.

When $u=1,v=t,t\ge3$, $q'$ is always a partition. The first-partition
multiplicities at lengths $1$ and $t-1$ are both at least one, at
distinct positions. Their factors are at least $d-w-1$ and
$D_{t-1}-1$, respectively. For $5\le t\le d$, \eqref{eq:Dmiddle} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wmiddle}
 W\ge(d-w-1)(d-2).
\end{equation}
For $t\ge d+1$, put $v_0=L_t\ge2$. Then $D_{t-1}=v_0+s$.
At every occupied second-partition length $j<t$, the old second vacancy
was zero and becomes one. These factors multiply to at least
\begin{align*}
 &\prod_{j<t}\bigl(1+\max(L_j-2,0)-\max(L_{j+1}-2,0)\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{25mm}\ge1+\sum_{j<t}
      \bigl(\max(L_j-2,0)-\max(L_{j+1}-2,0)\bigr)=w-v_0+1.
\end{align*}
Thus, whenever this move is admissible,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wlong}
 W\ge(d-w-1)(v_0+s-1)(w-v_0+1)\ge(d-w-1)(w-1).
\end{equation}
The last inequality uses
$(v_0-1)(w-v_0+1)\ge w-1$ for $2\le v_0\le w$.

\paragraph{Widths $3\le w\le d-2$.}
If $t=1$, \eqref{eq:maxweight} is $\binom{d-1}{w-1}\ge d$.
Suppose $2\le t\le4$. There are unit rows because
$N_d>4(d-2)$ for $d\ge8$.
For $t=2$, put $v=L_2\ge2$. The $j=2$ factor is
$\binom{2d-2-w}{v-1}\ge d$, since its upper argument is at least $d$
and $1\le v-1\le d-3$.
For $t=3$ with $L_3\ge3$, we have $D_3\ge d-2$ and final drop
$L_3-1\ge2$, giving a factor at least $\binom d2\ge d$.
If $L_3=2$, then $D_3\ge3d-5-2(d-2)=d-1$, again sufficient.
For $t=4$, $D_4\ge2d-6\ge d$, and the final nonzero drop suffices.

Now let $t\ge5$. All $D_j\ge2$ for $1\le j<t$, by the first three
bounds, \eqref{eq:Dmiddle}, and the remaining row of length at least two
when $j\ge d$. The move $u=1,v=t$ is admissible.
If $3\le w\le d-3$, \eqref{eq:Wmiddle} gives
$W\ge2(d-2)\ge d$ when $t\le d$. Otherwise \eqref{eq:Wlong} gives
$W\ge2(d-4)\ge d$, the minimum of $(d-w-1)(w-1)$ on this width range.
If $w=d-2$, the capacity condition prevents the first four rows from
all having length $w$. There is a positive drop at some $j\le3$, with
$D_j\ge2$, and hence $M\ge3$. Equations~\eqref{eq:Wmiddle} and
\eqref{eq:Wlong} give respectively $W\ge d-2$ and $W\ge d-3$.
The two distinct configurations add in \eqref{eq:fermionic}, so $M+W\ge d$.

\paragraph{Width two.}
Write $L=(2^t,1^s)$. The case $t=1$ is a listed exception.
For $2\le t\le d-1$,
\[
 D_t=t(d-2)-t(t-1)/2\ge d-1
\]
by concavity and its endpoint values. For $t=d$,
$D_t=s=d(d-3)/2\ge d-1$. The final nonzero drop in
\eqref{eq:maxweight} therefore gives at least $d$.
For $t>d$ and $s\ge d-1$, the same factor is at least $s+1\ge d$.

It remains that $t>d$ and $0\le s\le d-2$. From $2t+s=N_d$ and
$d\ge8$ follows $t-2\ge d$. Use instead
\[
 \nu^{(1)}=(t-2,2),\qquad \nu^{(k)}=\varnothing\quad(k\ge2).
\]
The size is correct. The first vacancies at $j=1,2$ are $d-4$ and
$2d-9$. On $2\le j\le t-2$ they equal the concave sequence
$Q_j(\rho_d)-2j-4$, whose other endpoint is $s$; for $j\ge t-2$
they equal $s$. They are all nonnegative. Second vacancies are
$Q_j(\nu^{(1)})\ge0$. The distinct occupied lengths $2,t-2$ give weight
\[
 (2d-8)(s+1)\ge d.
\]
This configuration illustrates why no cutoff at string length $d$ may
be imposed in \eqref{eq:fermionic}.

\paragraph{Width $d-1$.}
For $t=1$ we have the listed hook. If $t=2$, put $v=L_2$. Then
\[
 M=(d-v)\binom{d-1}{v-1}.
\]
It is at least $2(d-1)\ge d$ for $2\le v\le d-2$; $v=d-1$ is
the listed double-row exception.
Suppose $t\ge3$, put $v=L_2$ and $a=d-v$. As there are at least four
rows, the capacity bound gives $L_3+L_4\le a+1$, whence $L_3\le a$.
Also $v\le d-2$, so $a\ge2$.
If $a<d/2$, set $b=v-L_3\ge d-2a\ge1$. The first two maximal
factors give
\[
 M\ge a\binom{a+b}{b}\ge a(a+b)\ge a(d-a)\ge d.
\]
If $a\ge d/2$, use \eqref{eq:move} with $u=2,v=t$.
It is a partition since the new second entry is $L_2\le d-2=q_1$.
The vacancy conditions follow from $D_2=a\ge2$, $D_3\ge d-2$,
\eqref{eq:Dmiddle}, and a remaining row of length at least two.
At length one the first multiplicity decreases by one while its vacancy
stays one, giving the factor $a-1$. At length two the new first
multiplicity is at least one and the vacancy is $a-2$, giving a factor
at least $a-1$. If $t=3$, that multiplicity increases by two, and the
same estimate holds. Hence $W\ge(a-1)^2\ge d$ for integral
$a\ge d/2$, $d\ge8$.
Finally, direct substitution into \eqref{eq:maxweight} gives weight one
on the column and $d-1$ on each other shape in \eqref{eq:lowexceptions}.
All cases are covered.
\end{proof}

\section{Closing the uniform induction}\label{sec:assembly}
Let $m\ge9$ and assume \eqref{eq:invariant} at $m-1,m-2$.
The wide case was proved in Section~\ref{sec:wide}. For
$\lambda_1\le m$ and $C(\lambda)\ge2m+1$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:high} and its LR consequence prove the desired step.
It remains that $\lambda_1\le m$ and $C(\lambda)\le2m$.

If $\lambda_1\le m-1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:low} gives
$K_{\lambda^{\trp},\rho_m}\ge m-1$ except at the column, already in
$\mathcal E_m$. If $\lambda_1=m$, the first column of any semistandard
tableau of shape $\lambda^{\trp}$ and content $\rho_m$ is forced to
be $1,\ldots,m$. Deleting it subtracts one from every content
multiplicity and removes the last letter. Conversely any remaining
tableau may have this column prepended: an entry in row $i$ is at
least $i$, so row weak increase is preserved. Thus, for
$L=(\lambda_2,\lambda_3,\ldots)$ and $h=m-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deletecolumn}
 K_{\lambda^{\trp},\rho_m}=K_{L^{\trp},\rho_h}.
\end{equation}
Here $|L|=N_h$ and $L_1+L_2+L_3\le h+1$.
If $L_1=h+1$, then $L_2=L_3=0$, contradicting $|L|=N_h>h+1$.
If $L_1=h$, then $L_2+L_3\le1$; this permits at most one further
positive part, again contradicting the area. Therefore $L_1\le h-1$.
Also $L_4\le(h+1)/3$, so the top-four sum is at most
$4(h+1)/3\le2h$. Lemma~\ref{lem:low} applies at $h\ge8$.

Its column and width-two hook correspond exactly to $H_m,J_m$.
The double-$(h-1)$ exception cannot occur because its first three parts
sum to $2h-1>h+1$. The remaining width-$(h-1)$ hook gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical}
 \lambda=(m,m-2,1^{N_m-2m+2}).
\end{equation}
This family is handled inside one two-band quotient, as follows.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:critical}
For $m\ge5$, set $h=m-2$, $n=N_h$, and
$\nu=(2,1^{n-2})$. For $\lambda$ in \eqref{eq:critical},
\[
 \sum_{\substack{\eta\vdash2m-1\\\len(\eta)\le4}}
 c_{\nu,\eta}^{\lambda}\ge2.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\lambda=(m,m-2,1^{n+1})$, the skew shape $\lambda/\nu$
is the disjoint union of a bottom column of four cells and
$D=(m,m-2)/(2,1)$. Jacobi--Trudi and Pieri give
\begin{align*}
 s_D&=h_{m-2}h_{m-3}-h_mh_{m-5}\\
    &=s_{(m-1,m-4)}+s_{(m-2,m-3)}.
\end{align*}
Multiplying by $e_4$ gives, by adding a vertical four-strip, the two
distinct constituents $(m,m-3,1,1)$ and $(m-1,m-2,1,1)$,
each with coefficient at least one.
\end{proof}

For $m\ge7$, $\nu$ is outside $\mathcal E_{m-2}$. In the uniform
induction its contribution is at least $2(m-3)\ge m-1$. This completes
the step at every $m\ge9$.

\section{Exact finite starts and reproducibility}\label{sec:finite}
This section states the finite part of the proof explicitly. It is a
finite exhaustive calculation, not evidence substituted for a universal
argument. The supplied standard-library Python code uses integers and
\path{Fraction} throughout; it requires no downloaded data, private
source program, network access, floating point, or modular arithmetic.

\subsection{Algorithms and their mathematical contracts}
Partitions of $n$ are enumerated by selecting their first part $j$ and
recursively enumerating partitions of $n-j$ with largest part at most $j$.
Horizontal-strip predecessors are enumerated by
$\lambda_i\ge\nu_i\ge\lambda_{i+1}$ with prescribed area.
Deleting the cells occupied by the largest entry of a semistandard tableau
therefore gives the exact Kostka recurrence
\[
 K_{\lambda,(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r)}
 =\sum_{\lambda/\nu\text{ horizontal }\mu_r\text{-strip}}
      K_{\nu,(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{r-1})}.
\]
The four-strip predecessor enumeration imposes weak decrease, total area,
and $\lambda_i\ge\nu_i\ge\lambda_{i+4}$ directly.

Characters are computed by Murnaghan--Nakayama. For a partition with
$l$ parts, form the beta set $\{\lambda_i+l-i:1\le i\le l\}$.
Replacing a bead $x$ by an unoccupied nonnegative $x-k$ enumerates a
removable $k$-border strip, with sign $(-1)^b$ where $b$ is the number
of crossed beads. Recursion gives $\chi^\lambda(\mu)$.
For cycle type $\mu=(1^{a_1}2^{a_2}\cdots)$ put
$z_\mu=\prod_j j^{a_j}a_j!$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteg}
 g(\rho,\rho,\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}
       \frac{\chi^\rho(\mu)^2\chi^\lambda(\mu)}{z_\mu}.
\end{equation}
For class functions $a,b$ on symmetric groups of two prescribed degrees,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteind}
 \chi_{\Ind(a\boxtimes b)}(\mu)
 =\sum_{u\sqcup v=\mu}\frac{z_\mu}{z_u z_v}\chi_a(u)\chi_b(v).
\end{equation}
The sum distributes the multiset of cycles between the two degrees.
Character inner products then compute the exact induced multiplicities
in \eqref{eq:Rdef}. At every induction the verifier checks integrality,
nonnegativity, and the induced dimension. Small-square calculations also
check staircase orthogonality, the hook-length dimension, the total square
dimension, and both linear coefficients.

\subsection{Finite conclusions}
Formula~\eqref{eq:finiteg} gives the minima $1,2,3$ at $m=2,3,4$.
Starting from $R_1$, the verifier computes the four terms of
\eqref{eq:Rdef} exactly through $m=6$. It proves \eqref{eq:invariant}
at $h=5,6$ for all $172$ and $788$ nonexceptional labels.

At $m=7,8$, each nonexceptional label is checked in the following order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the one-band sum, giving weight $m-2$ to each nonexceptional
predecessor and weight one to each exception;
\item the exact Kostka number;
\item the four-strip predecessor sum, giving weight $m-3$ to each
nonexceptional predecessor and weight one to each exception;
\item the explicit critical-family identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:critical}.
\end{enumerate}
The third test is a valid lower bound on one two-band quotient: each
predecessor has at least one four-row LR constituent, as proved after
Lemma~\ref{lem:high}. The fourth test invokes a proved symbolic identity;
it is not inferred from the existence of a shape in a list.
The exhaustive counts are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrrrr}\toprule
$m$&$N_m$&all labels&one-band&Kostka&four-strip&critical\\\midrule
7&28&3718&2349&1120&244&1\\
8&36&17977&12161&4948&863&1\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The four excluded labels are exactly $\mathcal E_m$ in each row.
Every retained label has certified lower bound at least $m-1$.
This establishes the two finite induction steps without ever substituting
ambient-square multiplicities for the smaller cyclic multiplicities.

\section{Conclusion and attribution}\label{sec:end}
The finite starts and Sections~\ref{sec:wide}--\ref{sec:assembly} prove
\eqref{eq:invariant} for every $h\ge5$ by strong induction.
For $m\ge5$, a label other than row and column is either outside
$\mathcal E_m$ or is $H_m$ or $J_m$. In the latter cases its transpose
has first row $N_m-m+1$ or $N_m-m$, respectively, greater than $m$,
and is not a row. It is therefore outside $\mathcal E_m$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:Rvalid} proves \eqref{eq:main}. The cases $m=2,3,4$
are supplied by Section~\ref{sec:finite}.

For sharpness, $S^{(N-1,1)}$ is the nontrivial summand of the natural
permutation module. Branching and Frobenius reciprocity give
\[
 g(\rho_m,\rho_m,(N_m-1,1))
 =\dim\End_{S_{N_m-1}}(\Res S^{\rho_m})-1=m-1,
\]
because the staircase has $m$ distinct removable corners and branching
is multiplicity-free. This finishes Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\paragraph{Inherited operations and the residual estimate.}
The cyclic support, specified full induced quotients, and pair-letter
projection are source results \cite{cyclic}. I19's multiplicity recursion
and max rule \cite{I19}, I21's exact dominance module \cite{I21}, and
Kirillov's maximal configuration and one-cell perturbation \cite{Kirillov}
are explicitly inherited. The companion \cite{universal} already uses
capacity and balance to prove support. The residual here is the uniform
four-exception estimate for the cyclic recursion, including the predecessor
count and the low-capacity quantitative bound. The high-capacity lemma is
credited parent author assistance \cite{parenthigh}; the remaining
configuration analysis and full induction originated in the author packet
\cite{authorpacket}. Neither parent assistance nor finite replay is an
independent review.

\paragraph{Literature comparison and claim boundary.}
Vallejo's fixed-tail formulas and asymptotics \cite[Section~8]{Vallejo}
allow a fixed partition tail while the staircase order grows; they do not
supply the all-label quantifier used here. Bessenrodt--Bowman--Sutton
\cite[Section~5]{BBS} give lower bounds for specified families, and
Bessenrodt--Bowman \cite[Theorem~7.11]{BB} gives the stronger two-row
square splitting used in the earlier project analysis. The corner value
$m-1$ is classical and already explicit in this literature. The independent
contribution comparison found the full uniform estimate new to this project,
after deducting those operations. Neither that bounded comparison nor this
article demonstrates new-to-literature priority. No campaign acceptance
or publication decision is asserted here.
