                        The sharp minimum multiplicity
                          in a staircase tensor square
                                         Myeongjin Kang

                                        October 18, 2026


                                              Abstract
          Let ρm = (m, m − 1, . . . , 1) and Nm = m(m + 1)/2. Assuming the pinned cyclic-
      polytabloid source theorems speciﬁed here, we prove that every irreducible complex rep-
      resentation of SNm , other than the trivial and sign representations, occurs in S ρm ⊗ S ρm
      with multiplicity at least m − 1, for every m ≥ 2. The bound is sharp at (Nm − 1, 1).
      The proof quantitatively evaluates an inherited recursion for a speciﬁed cyclic submodule.
      An asymmetric invariant has four exceptional shapes. Two nonexceptional predecessors
      handle diagrams with large ﬁrst-four-row capacity; explicit rigged conﬁgurations give a
      uniform Kostka bound in the complementary range. Exact ﬁnite calculations supply the
      starts through order eight. The uniform estimate is presented as new to this project;
      new-to-literature priority is not claimed.

1      Statement, notation, and dependencies
All representations in this article are ﬁnite-dimensional complex representations. Tensor
products of representations of one symmetric group carry its diagonal action. For partitions
α, β, λ ⊢ n, write
                           g(α, β, λ) = dim HomSn (S λ , S α ⊗ S β ).
Partitions are padded by zero parts when necessary; λt denotes conjugation and ℓ(λ) denotes
the number of positive parts. Put ρm = (m, m − 1, . . . , 1) and Nm = m(m + 1)/2.

Theorem 1.1. For every integer m ≥ 2 and every λ ⊢ Nm with λ ∈
                                                             / {(Nm ), (1Nm )},

                                     g(ρm , ρm , λ) ≥ m − 1.                                (1.1)

Moreover g(ρm , ρm , (Nm − 1, 1)) = m − 1.

Their truth is an explicit premise of this work. We do not reprove or audit them. Section 2
states exactly which module-level conclusions are used; ordinary Saxl positivity by itself
would not suﬃce. The other non-elementary combinatorial input is the standard positive
rigged-conﬁguration formula for Kostka numbers, in the convention established in Section 5.1
from Kirillov–Schilling–Shimozono [4]. The ﬁnitely many starts are exact computations
described in Section 7. Every remaining all-order estimate is proved below.
     The band multiplicity recursion, including the rule of taking the maximum of separate
quotient bounds, was developed in I19, Section 4 [8]. The dominance-module and Kostka
reﬁnement was developed in I21, Section 2.1 [9], using the source’s explicit maps. Neither is
a new construction here. The residual result is the uniform evaluation of this recursion: a
ﬁxed exceptional set, a predecessor estimate, and a quantitative low-capacity Kostka bound.
The high-capacity predecessor argument was supplied by parent author assistance and is
incorporated in full in Section 4; that assistance is part of the author development, not an
independent review.
     Our claim concerns the ordinary tensor square. It does not give the multiplicity separately
in its symmetric and exterior squares, in the cyclic submodule, over the integers, or in positive
characteristic. In particular it does not resolve the earlier signed-square target of I19 and I21.
The familiar corner formula supplies sharpness, not a new representation-theoretic input.

Indeed a horizontal strip is precisely the interlacing λi ≥ νi ≥ λi+1 . There are at least two
positive gaps, and 0 < m < λ1 . A product of at least two nonconstant initial geometric sums

The next lemma incorporates the parent author-side construction [10], with its full proof.
Let C(λ) = λ1 + λ2 + λ3 + λ4 .
Lemma 4.1. Let m ≥ 7, h = m−2, n = Nh , and λ ⊢ Nm satisfy λ1 ≤ m and C(λ) ≥ 2m+1.
There are two distinct ν ⊢ n, both outside Eh , such that

                                  λi ≥ νi ≥ λi+4      for every i.                            (4.1)

Proof. Set µ = (λ5 , λ6 , . . .) and consider the skew diagram S = λ/µ with coordinate order:
(i, j) ≤ (i′ , j ′ ) if i ≤ i′ and j ≤ j ′ . Partitions µ ⊆ ν ⊆ λ correspond exactly to order ideals
of S. An interval between comparable cells lies inside the skew diagram, so this is also the
order generated by row and column adjacencies. The desired rank is

               k = n − |µ| = C − (2m − 1),           k ≥ 2,   C − k = 2m − 1 ≥ 13.

Write Hj = λtj and Lj = max(1, Hj − 3). Column j of S consists of rows Lj , . . . , Hj , so has
at most four cells; both endpoint sequences are nonincreasing. Put w = λ1 .
    We ﬁrst prove that every rank r with 2 ≤ r ≤ C − 2 has at least two ideals. Suppose A
were the unique rank-r ideal, and write B = S \ A. Every a ∈ A and b ∈ B must satisfy
a ≤ b. The alternative b < a contradicts downward closure. If they were incomparable,
adjoining the relation b < a would still give a partial order. A linear extension of that order
has a ﬁrst-r ideal of the original order diﬀerent from A, a contradiction.
    Let c be the greatest column meeting A and d the least column meeting B. Cross-
comparability forces c ≤ d. If c < d, column one lies wholly in A and column w wholly in
B, whence H1 ≤ Lw . Since H1 ≥ Hw ≥ Lw , this forces H1 = Hw = 1 and C = w ≤ m,
impossible. The same argument applies if 1 < c = d < w. If c = d = 1, all of A is a preﬁx of
column one and column w lies in B, so L1 + r − 1 ≤ Lw ≤ L1 , giving r ≤ 1. If c = d = w, all
of B is a suﬃx of column w and column one lies in A, so Hw − |B| + 1 ≥ H1 ≥ Hw , giving
|B| ≤ 1. Finally w = 1 would give C ≤ 4. Every case is impossible.
    If no exceptional shape is an interval predecessor, this rank count at k ﬁnishes the proof.
Suppose an exception ﬁts. The row (n) cannot ﬁt, since n > m ≥ λ1 . If the column ﬁts,
then λ5 ≤ 1; if Hh ﬁts, λ6 ≤ 1; if Jh ﬁts, λ7 ≤ 1. Thus every column after the ﬁrst has
height at most six. Also

                            ℓ(λ) ≥ n − h = (m − 2)(m − 3)/2 ≥ 10.

The four cells of S in column one consequently lie in rows at least seven. They are incom-
parable with all other cells of S, which lie in rows at most six and columns at least two.
Therefore S is the disjoint union of a four-cell chain and a poset S ′ of size C − 4.
    A ﬁnite poset has an ideal of every size from zero to its cardinality, by taking preﬁxes
of a linear extension. Since k ≤ C − 4, for each a = 0, . . . , min(4, k) combine a length-a
preﬁx of the separated chain with a rank-(k − a) ideal of S ′ . This gives at least min(4, k) + 1
predecessors. At most two exceptions ﬁt simultaneously: their lengths are n, n − h + 1, n − h,
whereas every predecessor has length in the interval [H1 − 4, H1 ], of width four, and h ≥ 5.
For k ≥ 3, at least four predecessors therefore leave two usable ones.
    It remains that k = 2, or C = 2m + 1. The construction gives three predecessors, whose
lengths diﬀer by at most two. The column cannot coexist with either Hh or Jh , because the
corresponding length diﬀerences are h − 1 ≥ 4 and h ≥ 5. If both Hh , Jh ﬁt, then λ6 ≤ 1
and λ5 ≥ 1. The number of cells of µ outside its ﬁrst column is λ5 − 1. Since Jh has h cells
outside its ﬁrst column and |Jh /µ| = 2, we get λ5 ≥ h − 1 = m − 3. This contradicts

                          C ≥ 4λ5 ≥ 4(m − 3) > 2m + 1            (m ≥ 7).

6    Closing the uniform induction
Let m ≥ 9 and assume (2.6) at m − 1, m − 2. The wide case was proved in Section 3. For
λ1 ≤ m and C(λ) ≥ 2m + 1, Lemma 4.1 and its LR consequence prove the desired step. It
remains that λ1 ≤ m and C(λ) ≤ 2m.
     If λ1 ≤ m − 1, Lemma 5.1 gives Kλt ,ρm ≥ m − 1 except at the column, already in Em . If
λ1 = m, the ﬁrst column of any semistandard tableau of shape λt and content ρm is forced to
be 1, . . . , m. Deleting it subtracts one from every content multiplicity and removes the last

8     Conclusion and attribution
The ﬁnite starts and Sections 3–6 prove (2.6) for every h ≥ 5 by strong induction. For m ≥ 5,
a label other than row and column is either outside Em or is Hm or Jm . In the latter cases
its transpose has ﬁrst row Nm − m + 1 or Nm − m, respectively, greater than m, and is not
a row. It is therefore outside Em . Equation (2.4) proves (1.1). The cases m = 2, 3, 4 are
supplied by Section 7.
    For sharpness, S (N −1,1) is the nontrivial summand of the natural permutation module.
Branching and Frobenius reciprocity give

              g(ρm , ρm , (Nm − 1, 1)) = dim EndSNm −1 (Res S ρm ) − 1 = m − 1,

because the staircase has m distinct removable corners and branching is multiplicity-free.
This ﬁnishes Theorem 1.1.
