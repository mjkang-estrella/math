\begin{abstract}
We deduce the advanced generalized Mukai classification from two stipulated
inputs in a pinned source: the generalized Mukai inequality with its equality
case, and an internal proposition giving a nonzero product of quantum
correspondences in independent numerical curve degrees. The second input is
essential and is stronger than the inequality alone. Retaining the dimension
of the supported composed cycle gives a closed rational-chain relation of
dimension at least $2\dim X-1$. A saturated quotient and a divisor argument
then give chain connectedness. The resulting bounded collection supplies
competitors at every minimal horizontal step of Fujita's construction,
establishing its unsplitness hypothesis without restrictions on dimension or
Picard number. Fujita's existing induction and bundle classifications finish
the proof. The source inputs are assumed, not proved or audited here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, dependencies, and contribution}
All varieties are over $\CC$. A Fano manifold is a smooth connected projective
variety $X$ with $-K_X$ ample. Write $n=\dim X>0$, $r=\rho(X)$ for its
\emph{actual} Picard number, and
\[
 i=\ii(X)=\min\{-K_X\cdot C:C\subset X\text{ an irreducible rational curve}\}.
\]
Projectivizations parametrize one-dimensional quotients. A product with zero
factors is a point. Numerical curve spaces have real coefficients.

The argument uses the following two assertions of the source
\cite{source}, at the fixed revision recorded in Section~\ref{sec:sources}.
They are stipulated throughout, including when applied to a Fano base of
smaller Picard number.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.8em,style=nextline]
\item[S1.] Every positive-dimensional Fano manifold satisfies
$r(i-1)\le n$, with equality precisely for $(\PP^{i-1})^r$.
\item[S2.] If $i\ge2$, there are $r$ linearly independent numerical
stable-map degrees $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r$, with
$d_j=-K_X\cdot\gamma_j\ge i$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:word}
 S_{\gamma_1}\cdots S_{\gamma_r}\ne0,
 \qquad \sum_{j=1}^r(d_j-1)\le n.
\end{equation}
Here $S_\gamma$ acts on even cohomology and lowers complex codimension by
$d_\gamma-1$. It is induced by the algebraic two-evaluation pushforward of
the genus-zero virtual class, of cycle dimension $n+d_\gamma-1$ on $X\times X$.
For a numerical degree the source sums the finitely many actual curve classes.
\end{description}
S1 is the source's main inequality and equality theorem. S2 is an internal
proposition about independent correspondences. We do not assert that it
follows from S1. Its spectral and descendant inputs are also stipulated. Thus the
following theorem is a conditional deduction with these exact dependencies.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Assume S1 and S2. If a positive-dimensional Fano manifold $X$ satisfies
$r(i-1)\ge n-1$, it is isomorphic to one of the following varieties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $(\PP^{i-1})^r$;
\item $Q^i\times(\PP^{i-1})^{r-1}$, where $i\ge3$;
\item $\PP_{\PP^i}(\OO^{\oplus(i-1)}\oplus\OO(1))
       \times(\PP^{i-1})^{r-2}$, where $r\ge2$ and $i\ge2$;
\item $\PP_{\PP^i}(T_{\PP^i})\times(\PP^{i-1})^{r-2}$,
       where $r\ge2$ and $i\ge2$;
\item $\PP^i\times(\PP^{i-1})^{r-1}$, where $r\ge2$ and $i\ge2$.
\end{enumerate}
Here $Q^i$ is a smooth quadric of dimension $i$. Every variety listed with
these restrictions is Fano with actual Picard number $r$ and pseudoindex $i$.
Type~\textup{(1)} has $n=r(i-1)$; types~\textup{(2)--(5)} have
$n=r(i-1)+1$.
\end{theorem}
The hypothesis forces $i\ge2$: otherwise $n\le1$, whereas a Fano curve is
$\PP^1$ and has pseudoindex two. There is no extra hypothesis about a
contraction, symmetry, or covering family. At $r=1$ the list consists of
$\PP^n$ and, for $n\ge3$, $Q^n$. The quadric $Q^2\simeq\PP^1\times\PP^1$
has actual Picard number two and lies in type~(1). In dimensions one and two
the possibilities are respectively $\PP^1$, and $\PP^2$ or
$\PP^1\times\PP^1$. When $i=2$, the defect-one list has just types~(3)--(5).
The restriction $r\ge2$ in type~(5) matters: $\PP^i$ alone has pseudoindex
$i+1$.

Fujita formulated this five-type classification and proved its pseudoindex
version for Picard parameter at most three or dimension at most five
\cite[Conjecture 1.4, Proposition 1.8, Remark 1.9]{fujita}, using the rank-one
input. We use Dedieu--H\"oring's established quadric characterization
\cite[Theorem 1.3]{dh} for that input. The latter paper addresses the gap in
the older rank-one argument. Fujita's minimal-family construction, quotient
theorem, extremal-ray criterion, induction, and bundle classifications remain
external ingredients here.

The addition is the integrated passage from the supported nonzero word to
full chain connectedness, and then to bounded degrees at \emph{every}
minimal horizontal step. The strict degree cutoff and the final induction
are supporting deductions. The equality case is S1 itself. Neither the full
named classification nor a literature search alone establishes novelty.
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} compares two recent primary proofs at the level
of their operations.

\section{Low degrees and closed incidence relations}\label{sec:low}
A family of rational curves means a full irreducible component of $\Rat(X)$,
as in \cite[Definition 2.1]{fujita}. It is \emph{unsplit} if it is proper.
Curves in the family are unparametrized irreducible rational images. We use
the associated proper Chow family for chains.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:low}
Let $X$ be Fano of pseudoindex $i$, and let $\gamma$ be a positive numerical
genus-zero stable-map degree with $d=-K_X\cdot\gamma<2i$. Every genus-zero stable map of degree
$\gamma$ has a single nonconstant component, birational onto its image.
Every full rational-curve family of that degree is unsplit. There are
finitely many such full families, and the two-evaluation correspondence of
degree $\gamma$ is supported on their closed two-point incidence relation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two nonconstant components would each contribute at least $i$ to the
anticanonical degree. A component covering its image with degree at least
two would likewise contribute at least $2i$. Both are impossible. Forget
all markings and stabilize. A contracted subtree would have a leaf with
fewer than three special points, so no contracted component remains in the
unmarked stable map. Its domain is $\PP^1$ and it is birational to its image.

Properness of the unmarked stable-map spaces therefore allows neither a
reducible limit nor a multiple-cover limit of curves of this degree. It
follows that every full component of $\Rat(X)$ in the degree is proper.
This is the source's minimum-degree incidence argument with its sufficient
strict bound $d<2i$ retained; it is not a new compactness theorem.

The fixed anticanonical bound gives bounded numerical coordinates, as in the
source's setup. Its finite actual-degree convention gives a finite union
of proper finite-type stable-map spaces. Each has finitely many components;
passing to unparametrized images and their normalizations still gives
finitely many full rational-curve components. Retain \emph{all} of them.
Their proper universal curves have proper two-fold fiber products, whose
images in $X\times X$ form a closed set $R_\gamma$. Both evaluation points
of a marked stable map lie on its unique rational image, even when a marking
lies on a contracted bubble. Its evaluation pushforward is thus supported
on $R_\gamma$. Every pair in $R_\gamma$ lies on one of the retained curves.
\end{proof}
The associated Chow families introduce no extra chain relation: every
cycle in these degrees is an irreducible reduced rational curve by the same
degree argument. We do not select one component of an incidence space to
represent a possibly signed virtual class.

\section{A large chain support forces connectedness}\label{sec:quotient}
For finitely many proper Chow families, we use the following precise
rational-chain quotient contract. There is a dense open $U\subset X$ and
a proper surjective morphism $\pi:U\to B$ such that each point fiber is
an \emph{entire} chain-equivalence class in $X$. In particular, $U$ is
saturated. After shrinking $B$ and taking its inverse image, the fibers
are pure of dimension $n-\dim B$. This is Koll\'ar's theorem
\cite[IV.4.16]{kollar}, in the formulation of
\cite[Theorem 3.2]{fujita}. The latter also gives a uniform chain bound
$2^{n-\dim B}-1$ within each fiber. Proper fibers are closed compact
subvarieties of $X$; the quotient itself need not extend to $X$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:connect}
Let $X$ be a smooth connected projective $n$-fold with $n\ge2$. Let
$\mathcal V$ be finitely many unsplit full families whose numerical classes
span $N_1(X)$. Suppose a closed set $Z\subset X\times X$ consists entirely
of pairs connected by $\mathcal V$-chains and satisfies $\dim Z\ge2n-1$.
Then $X$ is rational-chain connected by these families.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take the equidimensional saturated quotient just described, and put
$b=\dim B$ and $E=X\setminus U$. If a pair in $Z$ has one endpoint in $U$,
its other endpoint lies in the same entire equivalence class, hence in $U$
and in the same fiber. Consequently we have the actual containment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:saturated}
 Z\subset (U\times_B U)\ \cup\ (E\times E).
\end{equation}
The two constructible pieces have dimensions $2n-b$ and at most $2n-2$.
For $b\ge2$ their union cannot contain a closed set of dimension $2n-1$.
Thus $b\le1$. The exceptional piece is $E\times E$; a one-endpoint
exceptional locus would not give this conclusion.

Suppose $b=1$. Choose one curve $C_a$ from each family. Any $C_a$ meeting
$U$ is wholly contained in a single fiber, because every point of the curve
belongs to the same chain class. Choose a closed point $t\in B$ different
from the finitely many resulting fiber values. Curves outside $U$ also
avoid $F=\pi^{-1}(t)$. This nonempty proper fiber is closed in $X$ and pure
of dimension $n-1$. Any prime component $D$ of $F$ is a nonzero effective
Cartier divisor, since $X$ is smooth. It is disjoint from each $C_a$, so
$D\cdot C_a=0$ for all $a$. Spanning implies that $D$ is numerically
trivial. But for an ample divisor $H$ one has $D\cdot H^{n-1}>0$, a
contradiction. Only finitely many representatives were avoided; numerical
constancy does the remaining work.

Thus $b=0$. The dense open $U$, as the fiber of a proper morphism to a
point, is proper. Its inclusion in the separated variety $X$ has closed
image, so $U=X$. This fiber is a single entire chain class. The quotient
theorem's uniform bound, now $2^n-1$, also gives the bounded-chain
convention of rational-chain connectedness used by Fujita.
\end{proof}

\section{The composed virtual correspondence}\label{sec:cycle}
By S1 and the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
\[
 0\le\delta:=n-r(i-1)\le1.
\]
The case $i=1$ was excluded above. Put $s=\sum_j(d_j-1)$ for S2. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
 r(i-1)\le s\le n,\qquad
 \sum_j(d_j-i)\le\delta\le1.
\end{equation}
Every $d_j\le i+1<2i$. Lemma~\ref{lem:low} gives a finite collection
$\mathcal V$ of all full components in these degrees. Their numerical
classes span $N_1(X)$, because they include the $r$ independent degrees
of S2.

We specify the intersection-theory construction and its support. For a
cycle correspondence $A$ on $X\times X$, use the action
\[
 A_*\alpha=(p_2)_*(p_1^*\alpha\mathbin{\cap}\operatorname{cl}(A)).
\]
If a source uses the opposite evaluation order, transpose its correspondence
to adopt this convention. Composing $A$ followed by $B$ is the proper
pushforward of the refined intersection on the matching middle factors;
on a smooth triple product it is represented by
$(p_{13})_*(p_{12}^*A\cdot p_{23}^*B)$. Its action is $B_*A_*$.
Order the S2 factors accordingly (reading the operator word from right to
left). Reversing this order convention changes none of the degrees or chain
support arguments.

The standard refined Gysin construction is supported on the fiber product
of the original supports. Proper pushforward, refined intersection, and
the cycle-class map are compatible with this composition
\cite[\S\S6.2, 16.1, 19.1--19.2]{fulton}. More explicitly, the product of
the $r$ supported virtual classes has dimension $rn+s$. Imposing the
$r-1$ matching diagonals of $X$, each of codimension $n$, produces a
supported Chow class of dimension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-dim}
 rn+s-(r-1)n=n+s.
\end{equation}
Push it forward to the two remaining endpoint factors. The intermediate
support is the ordered incidence fiber product of the closed relations
$R_{\gamma_j}$. All maps to the endpoint product are proper. Its image
$Z\subset X\times X$ is therefore closed and consists entirely of pairs
joined by actual $\mathcal V$-chains, including points on its boundary.

The endpoint Chow class lies in the image of $A_{n+s}(Z)$ and its
cohomological action is the nonzero word in S2. It is therefore nonzero.
If $\dim Z<n+s$, the group of cycles in dimension $n+s$ on $Z$ is zero,
so this would be impossible. Hence
\[
 \dim Z\ge n+s\ge n+r(i-1)\ge2n-1.
\]
No effectiveness or positivity of virtual classes has been used. Nor have
we closed a relation that was known only on general points: the chain
support was a proper closed image from the start. Lemma~\ref{lem:connect}
now proves that $\mathcal V$ rational-chain connects all of $X$ when
$n\ge2$. The case $n=1$ is already $\PP^1$.

\section{Bounded horizontal competitors}\label{sec:horizontal}
Fujita's Construction 3.5 starts with a minimal dominating family and then
chooses a minimal horizontal dominating family for the quotient by the
families already chosen. Minimality here and below is with respect to
\emph{anticanonical degree}. The construction ends with numerically
independent families giving rational-chain connectedness
\cite[Definitions 2.1--2.2, Construction 3.5]{fujita}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:horizontal}
Suppose finitely many unsplit full families $V_a$ rational-chain connect a
positive-dimensional Fano manifold $X$, and each has anticanonical degree
at most $M$. The initial minimal dominating family and every minimal
horizontal dominating family in Fujita's construction have degree at most
$M$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every point lies on a curve from some $V_a$: connect it by a nonconstant
chain to a different point. Properness makes each $\Locus(V_a)$ closed.
Their finite union is $X$, so irreducibility makes one of them equal to
$X$. This supplies a competitor for the initial minimum.

Consider an intermediate proper saturated quotient $\psi:U\to B$ with
$\dim B>0$. For a full irreducible family $V_a$, the condition that two
points on a member lie in $U$ and have distinct $\psi$-images is open on
its two-fold universal curve. If nonempty it gives a dense constructible
image, hence a nonempty open subset, in the irreducible parameter space:
the general member is horizontal. If empty, $\psi$ is constant on
$C\cap U$ for every member $C$ meeting $U$. The proper fiber is closed in
$X$ and contains this dense open subset of $C$, hence contains all of $C$.
Thus a generically vertical family has no exceptional horizontal member.

For completeness, a vertical member meeting $U$ cannot coexist with general
horizontal members of the same proper family. That member lies wholly in
a compact fiber and hence wholly in $U$. Properness of the universal curve
makes the condition of lying wholly in $U$ open around it. On this open
family, the pullback of an ample divisor on the quasi-projective base has
degree zero on that member and constant degree in the family. The curves
therefore remain vertical. The two nonempty open conditions would
contradict irreducibility. This also agrees with the usual horizontal-family
convention in the cited construction.

Suppose none of the horizontal families dominates $B$. For each such family
the closure of $\psi(\Locus(V_a)\cap U)$ is a proper closed subset of $B$.
Choose $t\in B$ outside their finite union. No horizontal family meets
$F=\psi^{-1}(t)$. Every member of the other families that meets $F$ lies
wholly in $F$, by the preceding argument. Induction on the length of a
chain shows that every $\mathcal V$-chain starting in $F$ stays in $F$.
But $F$ is nonempty and proper in $X$, contradicting the given full chain
connectedness. Some $V_a$ is horizontal and dominant over $B$, providing a
competitor of degree at most $M$. Minimality gives the asserted bound.
\end{proof}
Apply the lemma to the collection from Section~\ref{sec:cycle}, with
$M=i+1$. Each family selected by Fujita has degree at most $i+1<2i$ and is
therefore unsplit by Lemma~\ref{lem:low}. This establishes the premise about
\emph{all} selected families in \cite[Corollary 6.4]{fujita}. We have not
asserted that the original correspondence families all dominate $X$, or
that they coincide with the newly selected families.

\section{Classification and exact invariants}\label{sec:classification}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main} by induction on actual Picard number. The
case $r=1$ has $i\ge n$. The rank-one consequence of
\cite[Theorem 1.3]{dh} gives $\PP^n$ or $Q^n$; $Q^2$ is excluded by its
Picard number, and dimension one is $\PP^1$. These are types~(1) and~(2).
The equality case for every $r$ is already S1.

Let $r\ge2$ and suppose the statement holds for all smaller actual Picard
numbers. Section~\ref{sec:horizontal} and Fujita's Corollary 6.4 give an
extremal ray whose contraction has every fiber of dimension at most $i-1$.
The ray length is at least $i$. Wi\'sniewski's inequality and the
maximal-length contraction theorem, as combined in
\cite[Theorems 5.1--5.2]{fujita}, show that the contraction is a
$\PP^{i-1}$-fibration. Its base $Y$ is smooth and Fano, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:base}
 \dim Y=n-i+1,\qquad \rho(Y)=r-1,\qquad j:=\ii(Y)\ge i
\end{equation}
by \cite[Lemma 4.2]{fujita}. S1 ensures
$n-i+1\ge(r-1)(i-1)\ge1$, and
\[
 (r-1)(j-1)\ge(r-1)(i-1)\ge (n-i+1)-1.
\]
The induction hypothesis applies at the actual invariants of $Y$.

These are precisely the base classifications used in the proof of
\cite[Proposition 5.3(b)]{fujita}. That proof observes that the listed bases
are rational, makes the projective-space fibration a projective bundle,
and applies its Corollaries 4.6--4.7. It yields the five displayed types for
$X$. This entire bundle classification is a cited result, not a new part
of the argument.

There is no assumption that pseudoindex remains constant. In defect one,
S1 on $Y$ gives
\[
 (r-1)(j-i)\le1.
\]
Thus $j=i$ for $r\ge3$. For $r=2$ the base may be $\PP^i$, of pseudoindex
$i+1$, or $Q^i$, of pseudoindex $i$; $Q^2$ is unavailable as a rank-one
base. Fujita's Corollary 4.7(d) treats the $\PP^i$ case and includes both
nontrivial bundles.

One small point about the formulation of Proposition 5.3(b) deserves to be
explicit. Its stated inductive premise is parametrized by a \emph{lower}
Picard bound. In its proof the needed base has actual Picard number $r-1$,
so the induction just given supplies the premise that is used. Alternatively,
it supplies the whole parametrized premise: if a base has actual Picard
number greater than the parameter, the arithmetic in the following paragraph
and S1 identify it as a product of projective lines. No classification at a
larger actual Picard number has been assumed in this induction.

To verify the equivalence with Fujita's parametrization, let $r_0\le r$ and
$r_0(i-1)\ge n-1$. If $r>r_0$, S1 implies
$(r-r_0)(i-1)\le1$. Since $i\ge2$, necessarily $i=2$ and $r=r_0+1$.
The two bounds then give $n=r$, so S1 gives $X=(\PP^1)^r$. In the
parametrized list this is $Q^2\times(\PP^1)^{r_0-1}$. Thus the actual-rank
formulation loses no case of the advanced generalized Mukai classification.

We finish by checking the converse and all invariants. Projective space
$\PP^m$ has pseudoindex $m+1$; $Q^i$ for $i\ge3$ has Picard number one
and pseudoindex $i$. On products, Picard numbers add and the pseudoindex
is the minimum of the factor pseudoindices: degrees of nonconstant
projections are positive and add, and a line in a factor attains the
minimum.

For $E=\OO^{\oplus(i-1)}\oplus\OO(1)$ on $\PP^i$, put
$\xi=c_1(\OO_{\PP(E)}(1))$ and let $H$ be the pullback hyperplane class.
The projective-bundle canonical formula gives
\[
 -K_{\PP(E)}=i\xi+iH.
\]
Both $\xi$ and $H$ are nef, and $\xi+H$ is ample. Each rational curve has
positive integral $(\xi+H)$-degree, and a fiber line has degree one.
This bundle is Fano of pseudoindex $i$, Picard number two, and dimension
$2i-1$. For $E=T_{\PP^i}$ the same formula gives
$-K_{\PP(E)}=i\xi$. The tangent bundle of projective space is ample;
$\xi$ is ample and has degree one on fiber lines. The invariants are the
same. Multiplication by $(\PP^{i-1})^{r-2}$ gives types~(3) and~(4).
Type~(5) has pseudoindex $i$ exactly because $r\ge2$ supplies a
$\PP^{i-1}$ factor. All four defect-one types have dimension
$r(i-1)+1$. Type~(1) has dimension $r(i-1)$. This proves the converse
and completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Comparison with related proofs}\label{sec:comparison}
The pinned source's equality argument uses minimum-degree proper families,
an ordered chain, and a chain-locus estimate. At equality the endpoint locus
fills $X$; cyclic rotation makes the families covering and a product
characterization finishes. With one unit of defect, that endpoint argument
alone can give only a divisor-sized locus. It does not establish full chain
connectedness or bound all the horizontal families in Fujita's construction.
Here the dimension of the \emph{composed cycle} is retained on the
two-endpoint product. The saturated inclusion~\eqref{eq:saturated}, the
compact divisor contradiction, and Lemma~\ref{lem:horizontal} supply those
missing conclusions.

Fujita's Theorem 6.1 assumes $r-1$ numerically independent dominating
unsplit families. Its Corollary 6.4 instead assumes that every family in
Construction 3.5 is unsplit. His Propositions 7.1--7.3 establish this in
specified numerical ranges. We use the corollary at that exact hypothesis;
neither the corollary nor its final induction is counted as an addition.

Watanabe's \emph{Fano varieties of middle pseudoindex}, version 2
\cite{watanabe-middle}, proves the classification for $2i=n+1$ and
$\rho(X)>1$. This includes the rank-two defect-one case of our statement.
Its proof treats birational and fiber-type contractions, uses one-point
unsplit loci and intersections of fibers of distinct contractions, obtains
a projective bundle, and applies existing bundle classifications. These
are already established cases; we make no claim to newly classify them.

The revised version 2 of \emph{Smooth Fano varieties with pseudoindex equal
to half of their dimension} \cite{watanabe-half} assumes $i=n/2\ge2$
\emph{and the existence of a birational extremal contraction}. It gives
eight types under that hypothesis. Its proof separates small and divisorial
contractions, uses unsplit loci and fiber-dimension inequalities to force
another contraction to be a projective bundle, and invokes explicit bundle
or blowup classifications. Its locus estimates include a needed
irreducibility qualification for the initial subvariety. None of these
arguments is an unrestricted replacement for the two-endpoint support and
saturated-quotient argument here. The birational-contraction assumption
must not be dropped when comparing that theorem.

These comparisons are based on reading the complete mathematical bodies
of both versioned papers, including their proofs and references. They
identify overlap and the different operation used here; they do not
certify worldwide priority or constitute external peer review.
