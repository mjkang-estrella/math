       Advanced Mukai classiﬁcation from independent quantum
                          correspondences
                                            Myeongjin Kang

                                            October 17, 2026


                                                 Abstract
          We deduce the advanced generalized Mukai classiﬁcation from two stipulated inputs in a
      pinned source: the generalized Mukai inequality with its equality case, and an internal propo-
      sition giving a nonzero product of quantum correspondences in independent numerical curve
      degrees. The second input is essential and is stronger than the inequality alone. Retaining the
      dimension of the supported composed cycle gives a closed rational-chain relation of dimension
      at least 2 dim X − 1. A saturated quotient and a divisor argument then give chain connected-
      ness. The resulting bounded collection supplies competitors at every minimal horizontal step of
      Fujita’s construction, establishing its unsplitness hypothesis without restrictions on dimension
      or Picard number. Fujita’s existing induction and bundle classiﬁcations ﬁnish the proof. The
      source inputs are assumed, not proved or audited here.


1     Statement, dependencies, and contribution
All varieties are over C. A Fano manifold is a smooth connected projective variety X with −KX
ample. Write n = dim X > 0, r = ρ(X) for its actual Picard number, and

                 i = ι(X) = min{−KX · C : C ⊂ X an irreducible rational curve}.

Projectivizations parametrize one-dimensional quotients. A product with zero factors is a point.
Numerical curve spaces have real coeﬃcients.
    The argument uses the following two assertions of the source [1], at the ﬁxed revision recorded
in Section 8. They are stipulated throughout, including when applied to a Fano base of smaller
Picard number.
S1.   Every positive-dimensional Fano manifold satisﬁes r(i − 1) ≤ n, with equality precisely for
      (Pi−1 )r .
S2.   If i ≥ 2, there are r linearly independent numerical stable-map degrees γ1 , . . . , γr , with
      dj = −KX · γj ≥ i, such that

                                                            ∑
                                                            r
                                     Sγ1 · · · Sγr ̸= 0,        (dj − 1) ≤ n.                            (1)
                                                            j=1

      Here Sγ acts on even cohomology and lowers complex codimension by dγ −1. It is induced by
      the algebraic two-evaluation pushforward of the genus-zero virtual class, of cycle dimension
      n + dγ − 1 on X × X. For a numerical degree the source sums the ﬁnitely many actual curve
      classes.

                                                       1
S1 is the source’s main inequality and equality theorem. S2 is an internal proposition about
independent correspondences. We do not assert that it follows from S1. Its spectral and descendant
inputs are also stipulated. Thus the following theorem is a conditional deduction with these exact
dependencies.

Theorem 1.1. Assume S1 and S2. If a positive-dimensional Fano manifold X satisﬁes r(i − 1) ≥
n − 1, it is isomorphic to one of the following varieties:
 (1) (Pi−1 )r ;
 (2) Qi × (Pi−1 )r−1 , where i ≥ 3;
 (3) PPi (O⊕(i−1) ⊕ O(1)) × (Pi−1 )r−2 , where r ≥ 2 and i ≥ 2;
 (4) PPi (TPi ) × (Pi−1 )r−2 , where r ≥ 2 and i ≥ 2;
 (5) Pi × (Pi−1 )r−1 , where r ≥ 2 and i ≥ 2.
Here Qi is a smooth quadric of dimension i. Every variety listed with these restrictions is Fano
with actual Picard number r and pseudoindex i. Type (1) has n = r(i − 1); types (2)–(5) have
n = r(i − 1) + 1.

     The hypothesis forces i ≥ 2: otherwise n ≤ 1, whereas a Fano curve is P1 and has pseudoindex
two. There is no extra hypothesis about a contraction, symmetry, or covering family. At r = 1 the
list consists of Pn and, for n ≥ 3, Qn . The quadric Q2 ≃ P1 × P1 has actual Picard number two and
lies in type (1). In dimensions one and two the possibilities are respectively P1 , and P2 or P1 × P1 .
When i = 2, the defect-one list has just types (3)–(5). The restriction r ≥ 2 in type (5) matters:
Pi alone has pseudoindex i + 1.
     Fujita formulated this ﬁve-type classiﬁcation and proved its pseudoindex version for Picard
parameter at most three or dimension at most ﬁve [2, Conjecture 1.4, Proposition 1.8, Remark
1.9], using the rank-one input. We use Dedieu–Höring’s established quadric characterization [3,
Theorem 1.3] for that input. The latter paper addresses the gap in the older rank-one argument.
Fujita’s minimal-family construction, quotient theorem, extremal-ray criterion, induction, and bun-
dle classiﬁcations remain external ingredients here.
     The addition is the integrated passage from the supported nonzero word to full chain connect-
edness, and then to bounded degrees at every minimal horizontal step. The strict degree cutoﬀ
and the ﬁnal induction are supporting deductions. The equality case is S1 itself. Neither the full
named classiﬁcation nor a literature search alone establishes novelty. Section 7 compares two recent
primary proofs at the level of their operations.


2    Low degrees and closed incidence relations
A family of rational curves means a full irreducible component of RatCurvesn (X), as in [2, Deﬁnition
2.1]. It is unsplit if it is proper. Curves in the family are unparametrized irreducible rational images.
We use the associated proper Chow family for chains.

Lemma 2.1. Let X be Fano of pseudoindex i, and let γ be a positive numerical genus-zero stable-
map degree with d = −KX ·γ < 2i. Every genus-zero stable map of degree γ has a single nonconstant
component, birational onto its image. Every full rational-curve family of that degree is unsplit.
There are ﬁnitely many such full families, and the two-evaluation correspondence of degree γ is
supported on their closed two-point incidence relation.


                                                   2
Proof. Two nonconstant components would each contribute at least i to the anticanonical degree. A
component covering its image with degree at least two would likewise contribute at least 2i. Both
are impossible. Forget all markings and stabilize. A contracted subtree would have a leaf with
fewer than three special points, so no contracted component remains in the unmarked stable map.
Its domain is P1 and it is birational to its image.
    Properness of the unmarked stable-map spaces therefore allows neither a reducible limit nor a
multiple-cover limit of curves of this degree. It follows that every full component of RatCurvesn (X)
in the degree is proper. This is the source’s minimum-degree incidence argument with its suﬃcient
strict bound d < 2i retained; it is not a new compactness theorem.
    The ﬁxed anticanonical bound gives bounded numerical coordinates, as in the source’s setup.
Its ﬁnite actual-degree convention gives a ﬁnite union of proper ﬁnite-type stable-map spaces. Each
has ﬁnitely many components; passing to unparametrized images and their normalizations still gives
ﬁnitely many full rational-curve components. Retain all of them. Their proper universal curves
have proper two-fold ﬁber products, whose images in X × X form a closed set Rγ . Both evaluation
points of a marked stable map lie on its unique rational image, even when a marking lies on a
contracted bubble. Its evaluation pushforward is thus supported on Rγ . Every pair in Rγ lies on
one of the retained curves.

    The associated Chow families introduce no extra chain relation: every cycle in these degrees is
an irreducible reduced rational curve by the same degree argument. We do not select one component
of an incidence space to represent a possibly signed virtual class.


3    A large chain support forces connectedness
For ﬁnitely many proper Chow families, we use the following precise rational-chain quotient contract.
There is a dense open U ⊂ X and a proper surjective morphism π : U → B such that each point
ﬁber is an entire chain-equivalence class in X. In particular, U is saturated. After shrinking B
and taking its inverse image, the ﬁbers are pure of dimension n − dim B. This is Kollár’s theorem
[4, IV.4.16], in the formulation of [2, Theorem 3.2]. The latter also gives a uniform chain bound
2n−dim B − 1 within each ﬁber. Proper ﬁbers are closed compact subvarieties of X; the quotient
itself need not extend to X.

Lemma 3.1. Let X be a smooth connected projective n-fold with n ≥ 2. Let V be ﬁnitely many
unsplit full families whose numerical classes span N1 (X). Suppose a closed set Z ⊂ X × X consists
entirely of pairs connected by V-chains and satisﬁes dim Z ≥ 2n − 1. Then X is rational-chain
connected by these families.

Proof. Take the equidimensional saturated quotient just described, and put b = dim B and E =
X \ U . If a pair in Z has one endpoint in U , its other endpoint lies in the same entire equivalence
class, hence in U and in the same ﬁber. Consequently we have the actual containment

                                    Z ⊂ (U ×B U ) ∪ (E × E).                                      (2)

The two constructible pieces have dimensions 2n − b and at most 2n − 2. For b ≥ 2 their union
cannot contain a closed set of dimension 2n − 1. Thus b ≤ 1. The exceptional piece is E × E; a
one-endpoint exceptional locus would not give this conclusion.
    Suppose b = 1. Choose one curve Ca from each family. Any Ca meeting U is wholly contained
in a single ﬁber, because every point of the curve belongs to the same chain class. Choose a closed
point t ∈ B diﬀerent from the ﬁnitely many resulting ﬁber values. Curves outside U also avoid

                                                 3
F = π −1 (t). This nonempty proper ﬁber is closed in X and pure of dimension n − 1. Any prime
component D of F is a nonzero eﬀective Cartier divisor, since X is smooth. It is disjoint from
each Ca , so D · Ca = 0 for all a. Spanning implies that D is numerically trivial. But for an ample
divisor H one has D · H n−1 > 0, a contradiction. Only ﬁnitely many representatives were avoided;
numerical constancy does the remaining work.
    Thus b = 0. The dense open U , as the ﬁber of a proper morphism to a point, is proper. Its
inclusion in the separated variety X has closed image, so U = X. This ﬁber is a single entire chain
class. The quotient theorem’s uniform bound, now 2n − 1, also gives the bounded-chain convention
of rational-chain connectedness used by Fujita.

Every dj ≤ i + 1 < 2i. Lemma 2.1 gives a ﬁnite collection V of all full components in these degrees.
Their numerical classes span N1 (X), because they include the r independent degrees of S2.
   We specify the intersection-theory construction and its support. For a cycle correspondence A
on X × X, use the action
                                    A∗ α = (p2 )∗ (p∗1 α ∩ cl(A)).
If a source uses the opposite evaluation order, transpose its correspondence to adopt this convention.
Composing A followed by B is the proper pushforward of the reﬁned intersection on the matching
middle factors; on a smooth triple product it is represented by (p13 )∗ (p∗12 A · p∗23 B). Its action is
B∗ A∗ . Order the S2 factors accordingly (reading the operator word from right to left). Reversing
this order convention changes none of the degrees or chain support arguments.
     The standard reﬁned Gysin construction is supported on the ﬁber product of the original sup-
ports. Proper pushforward, reﬁned intersection, and the cycle-class map are compatible with this
composition [5, §§6.2, 16.1, 19.1–19.2]. More explicitly, the product of the r supported virtual
classes has dimension rn + s. Imposing the r − 1 matching diagonals of X, each of codimension n,
produces a supported Chow class of dimension

                                     rn + s − (r − 1)n = n + s.                                     (4)

Push it forward to the two remaining endpoint factors. The intermediate support is the ordered
incidence ﬁber product of the closed relations Rγj . All maps to the endpoint product are proper.
Its image Z ⊂ X × X is therefore closed and consists entirely of pairs joined by actual V-chains,
including points on its boundary.
    The endpoint Chow class lies in the image of An+s (Z) and its cohomological action is the
nonzero word in S2. It is therefore nonzero. If dim Z < n + s, the group of cycles in dimension
n + s on Z is zero, so this would be impossible. Hence

                               dim Z ≥ n + s ≥ n + r(i − 1) ≥ 2n − 1.

No eﬀectiveness or positivity of virtual classes has been used. Nor have we closed a relation that
was known only on general points: the chain support was a proper closed image from the start.
Lemma 3.1 now proves that V rational-chain connects all of X when n ≥ 2. The case n = 1 is
already P1 .


5    Bounded horizontal competitors
Fujita’s Construction 3.5 starts with a minimal dominating family and then chooses a minimal
horizontal dominating family for the quotient by the families already chosen. Minimality here and
below is with respect to anticanonical degree. The construction ends with numerically independent
families giving rational-chain connectedness [2, Deﬁnitions 2.1–2.2, Construction 3.5].

Lemma 5.1. Suppose ﬁnitely many unsplit full families Va rational-chain connect a positive-
dimensional Fano manifold X, and each has anticanonical degree at most M . The initial minimal
dominating family and every minimal horizontal dominating family in Fujita’s construction have
degree at most M .

Proof. Every point lies on a curve from some Va : connect it by a nonconstant chain to a diﬀerent
point. Properness makes each Locus(Va ) closed. Their ﬁnite union is X, so irreducibility makes
one of them equal to X. This supplies a competitor for the initial minimum.
    Consider an intermediate proper saturated quotient ψ : U → B with dim B > 0. For a full
irreducible family Va , the condition that two points on a member lie in U and have distinct ψ-images
is open on its two-fold universal curve. If nonempty it gives a dense constructible image, hence a
nonempty open subset, in the irreducible parameter space: the general member is horizontal. If
empty, ψ is constant on C ∩ U for every member C meeting U . The proper ﬁber is closed in X and
contains this dense open subset of C, hence contains all of C. Thus a generically vertical family
has no exceptional horizontal member.
    For completeness, a vertical member meeting U cannot coexist with general horizontal members
of the same proper family. That member lies wholly in a compact ﬁber and hence wholly in U .
Properness of the universal curve makes the condition of lying wholly in U open around it. On this
open family, the pullback of an ample divisor on the quasi-projective base has degree zero on that
member and constant degree in the family. The curves therefore remain vertical. The two nonempty
open conditions would contradict irreducibility. This also agrees with the usual horizontal-family
convention in the cited construction.
    Suppose none of the horizontal families dominates B. For each such family the closure of
ψ(Locus(Va ) ∩ U ) is a proper closed subset of B. Choose t ∈ B outside their ﬁnite union. No
horizontal family meets F = ψ −1 (t). Every member of the other families that meets F lies wholly
in F , by the preceding argument. Induction on the length of a chain shows that every V-chain
starting in F stays in F . But F is nonempty and proper in X, contradicting the given full chain
connectedness. Some Va is horizontal and dominant over B, providing a competitor of degree at
most M . Minimality gives the asserted bound.

    Apply the lemma to the collection from Section 4, with M = i + 1. Each family selected by
Fujita has degree at most i + 1 < 2i and is therefore unsplit by Lemma 2.1. This establishes the
premise about all selected families in [2, Corollary 6.4]. We have not asserted that the original
correspondence families all dominate X, or that they coincide with the newly selected families.

6    Classiﬁcation and exact invariants
We prove Theorem 1.1 by induction on actual Picard number. The case r = 1 has i ≥ n. The
rank-one consequence of [3, Theorem 1.3] gives Pn or Qn ; Q2 is excluded by its Picard number, and
dimension one is P1 . These are types (1) and (2). The equality case for every r is already S1.
    Let r ≥ 2 and suppose the statement holds for all smaller actual Picard numbers. Section 5 and
Fujita’s Corollary 6.4 give an extremal ray whose contraction has every ﬁber of dimension at most
i − 1. The ray length is at least i. Wiśniewski’s inequality and the maximal-length contraction
theorem, as combined in [2, Theorems 5.1–5.2], show that the contraction is a Pi−1 -ﬁbration. Its
base Y is smooth and Fano, with

                      dim Y = n − i + 1,       ρ(Y ) = r − 1,     j := ι(Y ) ≥ i                   (5)

by [2, Lemma 4.2]. S1 ensures n − i + 1 ≥ (r − 1)(i − 1) ≥ 1, and

                          (r − 1)(j − 1) ≥ (r − 1)(i − 1) ≥ (n − i + 1) − 1.

The induction hypothesis applies at the actual invariants of Y .
   These are precisely the base classiﬁcations used in the proof of [2, Proposition 5.3(b)]. That
proof observes that the listed bases are rational, makes the projective-space ﬁbration a projective
bundle, and applies its Corollaries 4.6–4.7. It yields the ﬁve displayed types for X. This entire
bundle classiﬁcation is a cited result, not a new part of the argument.
   There is no assumption that pseudoindex remains constant. In defect one, S1 on Y gives

                                         (r − 1)(j − i) ≤ 1.

Thus j = i for r ≥ 3. For r = 2 the base may be Pi , of pseudoindex i + 1, or Qi , of pseudoindex i;
Q2 is unavailable as a rank-one base. Fujita’s Corollary 4.7(d) treats the Pi case and includes both
nontrivial bundles.
    One small point about the formulation of Proposition 5.3(b) deserves to be explicit. Its stated
inductive premise is parametrized by a lower Picard bound. In its proof the needed base has actual
Picard number r − 1, so the induction just given supplies the premise that is used. Alternatively,
it supplies the whole parametrized premise: if a base has actual Picard number greater than the
parameter, the arithmetic in the following paragraph and S1 identify it as a product of projective
lines. No classiﬁcation at a larger actual Picard number has been assumed in this induction.
    To verify the equivalence with Fujita’s parametrization, let r0 ≤ r and r0 (i − 1) ≥ n − 1. If
r > r0 , S1 implies (r − r0 )(i − 1) ≤ 1. Since i ≥ 2, necessarily i = 2 and r = r0 + 1. The two bounds
then give n = r, so S1 gives X = (P1 )r . In the parametrized list this is Q2 × (P1 )r0 −1 . Thus the
actual-rank formulation loses no case of the advanced generalized Mukai classiﬁcation.
    We ﬁnish by checking the converse and all invariants. Projective space Pm has pseudoindex
m + 1; Qi for i ≥ 3 has Picard number one and pseudoindex i. On products, Picard numbers add
and the pseudoindex is the minimum of the factor pseudoindices: degrees of nonconstant projections
are positive and add, and a line in a factor attains the minimum.
    For E = O⊕(i−1) ⊕ O(1) on Pi , put ξ = c1 (OP(E) (1)) and let H be the pullback hyperplane class.
The projective-bundle canonical formula gives

                                         −KP(E) = iξ + iH.

Both ξ and H are nef, and ξ + H is ample. Each rational curve has positive integral (ξ + H)-degree,
and a ﬁber line has degree one. This bundle is Fano of pseudoindex i, Picard number two, and

                                                  6
dimension 2i − 1. For E = TPi the same formula gives −KP(E) = iξ. The tangent bundle of
projective space is ample; ξ is ample and has degree one on ﬁber lines. The invariants are the same.
Multiplication by (Pi−1 )r−2 gives types (3) and (4). Type (5) has pseudoindex i exactly because
r ≥ 2 supplies a Pi−1 factor. All four defect-one types have dimension r(i − 1) + 1. Type (1) has
dimension r(i − 1). This proves the converse and completes the proof of Theorem 1.1.


7     Comparison with related proofs
The pinned source’s equality argument uses minimum-degree proper families, an ordered chain, and
a chain-locus estimate. At equality the endpoint locus ﬁlls X; cyclic rotation makes the families
covering and a product characterization ﬁnishes. With one unit of defect, that endpoint argument
alone can give only a divisor-sized locus. It does not establish full chain connectedness or bound all
the horizontal families in Fujita’s construction. Here the dimension of the composed cycle is retained
on the two-endpoint product. The saturated inclusion (2), the compact divisor contradiction, and
Lemma 5.1 supply those missing conclusions.
    Fujita’s Theorem 6.1 assumes r − 1 numerically independent dominating unsplit families. Its
Corollary 6.4 instead assumes that every family in Construction 3.5 is unsplit. His Propositions
7.1–7.3 establish this in speciﬁed numerical ranges. We use the corollary at that exact hypothesis;
neither the corollary nor its ﬁnal induction is counted as an addition.
    Watanabe’s Fano varieties of middle pseudoindex, version 2 [6], proves the classiﬁcation for
2i = n + 1 and ρ(X) > 1. This includes the rank-two defect-one case of our statement. Its proof
treats birational and ﬁber-type contractions, uses one-point unsplit loci and intersections of ﬁbers
of distinct contractions, obtains a projective bundle, and applies existing bundle classiﬁcations.
These are already established cases; we make no claim to newly classify them.
    The revised version 2 of Smooth Fano varieties with pseudoindex equal to half of their dimension
[7] assumes i = n/2 ≥ 2 and the existence of a birational extremal contraction. It gives eight
types under that hypothesis. Its proof separates small and divisorial contractions, uses unsplit
loci and ﬁber-dimension inequalities to force another contraction to be a projective bundle, and
invokes explicit bundle or blowup classiﬁcations. Its locus estimates include a needed irreducibility
qualiﬁcation for the initial subvariety. None of these arguments is an unrestricted replacement
for the two-endpoint support and saturated-quotient argument here. The birational-contraction
assumption must not be dropped when comparing that theorem.
    These comparisons are based on reading the complete mathematical bodies of both versioned
papers, including their proofs and references. They identify overlap and the diﬀerent operation
used here; they do not certify worldwide priority or constitute external peer review.
