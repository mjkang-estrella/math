       Abstract. Let L(x) = r=1 xr Xr , where the Xr are real symmetric m×m matrices and r Xr2 ⪯
       KI for a ﬁxed ﬁnite K. For the Gaussian inverse Mills ratio q, we prove that the mean squared
       row curl of q(L) controls both the lack of full symmetry of the coeﬃcient tensor and the sum of
       all squared coeﬃcient commutators. After division by m, the estimate has a modulus independent
       of the dimension, bounded by CK / log(e/t) when the normalized curl energy t is small. The proof
       compares divided diﬀerences at x and −x and controls a cutoﬀ on all tensor indices by Gaussian
       operator moments. We also construct a fully symmetric tensor with a controlled operator-energy
       bound and controlled algebraic residual.

   No normalization B ≈ I or full coeﬃcient symmetry is assumed. The ﬁxed bound on B is a
hypothesis throughout. The theorem estimates the deﬁning algebraic residual; it does not estimate
distance to a simultaneously diagonalizable family with constants uniform in m.
   The exact compatibility problem occurs in the entropy manuscript [1, section “From vanishing
slack to exponential products”]. There, if B = I and q(L) is a Jacobian, its ﬁrst two Taylor
terms at the origin imply full coeﬃcient symmetry and commutation. The present theorem gives a
quantitative implication from an integrated curl bound, with constants independent of dimension.
A pointwise Taylor argument does not give that estimate without additional control of the ﬁeld
near the origin. None of the entropy inequalities asserted in [1] is an assumption of Theorem 1.1.
   Spectral averaging of commutators appears in the linear equality argument of [2], and fourth-
moment spectral truncation appears in [3, section 10]. The comparison at the two reﬂected points
is the step that controls coeﬃcient symmetry before it is known. Results on almost commuting
matrices, such as [4], start from commutator bounds; their family estimates retain dependence on
the number of matrices. The algebraic characterization of symmetric orthogonally decomposable
tensors in [5] concerns the exact zero set. We do not use either result as a stability theorem for the
residual (1.3).
