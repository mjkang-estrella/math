                  A superquadratic sensitivity separation
                     for transitive Boolean functions
                                        Myeongjin Kang

                                             October 2026


                                               Abstract
         For every C > 0 we construct a nonconstant total Boolean function f , invariant
      under a group transitive on all input coordinates, such that f (0) = 0 and bs(f, 0) >
      C s(f )2 . Here s(f ) is ordinary sensitivity maximized over all inputs. We also obtain
      a ﬁxed α > 2 and a sequence of transitive functions with bs(Fm , 0) ≥ s(Fm )α and
      diverging block sensitivity. The construction equips the nested-predicate framework of
      the pinned OpenAI separation with a deterministic labelled tournament. First-diﬀerence
      labels on F4p admit simultaneous aﬃne covariance, have an alphabet of size (p − 1)/2, and
      exclude head-constant sets of more than 81 rows. These compatible properties preserve
      transitivity while keeping the alphabet cost small in the ordinary and joint sensitivity
      recurrences. The complete argument, including those recurrences and the composition
      endpoint, is given below.


1    Statement, conventions and antecedents
For a ﬁnite coordinate set Ω, an input x ∈ {0, 1}Ω , and B ⊆ Ω, write xB for the in-
put obtained by ﬂipping precisely the coordinates in B. For a total Boolean function
f : {0, 1}Ω → {0, 1}, deﬁne

               s(f, x) = |{i ∈ Ω : f (x{i} ) 6= f (x)}|,     s(f ) = max s(f, x).
                                                                     x∈{0,1}Ω

The block sensitivity bs(f, x) is the largest number of pairwise disjoint nonempty subsets
B1 , . . . , Bb such that f (xBj ) 6= f (x) for all j. A permutation σ of Ω acts on inputs by
(σx)σ(i) = xi . We call f coordinate-transitive if a subgroup of these permutations preserves
f and acts transitively on Ω.

Theorem 1.1. For every real C > 0 there exist a ﬁnite coordinate set Ω and a nonconstant
total coordinate-transitive function f : {0, 1}Ω → {0, 1} such that

                                f (0) = 0,       bs(f, 0) > C s(f )2 .

Moreover, there are a ﬁxed real α > 2 and nonconstant total coordinate-transitive functions
Fm , m ≥ 1, such that

                 Fm (0) = 0,       bs(Fm , 0) ≥ s(Fm )α ,        bs(Fm , 0) −→ ∞.

    All sensitivity upper bounds in this paper hold at every raw Boolean input. No promise
on the input, spectral substitute, row-only symmetry, or minterm representation is imposed.
The theorem makes no claim about cyclic invariance, graph properties, an optimal exponent,
or sensitivity below the cubic-root scale in the number of variables.

Section 2; it is bounded primary-literature comparison, not a worldwide priority assertion.
Familiar ingredients can form a routine assembly, so substantiality is not inferred merely
from the absence of a theorem with the same headline.

Lemma 3.1. For each l, the predicates Pl,q decrease as q increases. There is one coordinate-
transitive permutation group on their Nl coordinates that preserves every Pl,q simultaneously.

Proof. At level zero, nesting follows from the decreasing radii. Permutations of the M equal
blocks and independent permutations within each block preserve the set of centers, hence D
and every predicate. They act transitively on all LM bits.
    Inductively, increasing q decreases the gate index Q − q. By child nesting this makes the
gate predicate larger, so requiring it to be zero is a stronger condition. Thus parent nesting
holds. Take independent copies of the common child group in each child. They preserve
both indices used by every clause. Also let the aﬃne group in Lemma 2.3 permute the whole
children, retaining their identical internal coordinate indexing. It sends all targets in row u
to all targets in row λu + b. For a gate indexed by (v, a(u, v)), covariance sends its position
to
                        (λv + b, λa(u, v)) = (λv + b, a(λu + b, λv + b)),
the corresponding gate of that new clause. Every parent predicate is preserved.
    Given two raw coordinates, use the aﬃne group to move the ﬁrst coordinate’s child to
the destination child. Then use the independent group in that child to move its internal
coordinate to the requested bit. The generated group is transitive. The induction preserves
the entire nested family, which is necessary because clauses use two diﬀerent child indices.


4    Ordinary and joint sensitivity at every input
For b ∈ {0, 1}, let Sl,b be the maximum of s(Pl,q , x) over all indices q and inputs with
Pl,q (x) = b. Let Jl,b be the maximum number of coordinates i for which

                  Pl,q (x) = Pl,q0 (x) = b,   Pl,q (x{i} ) = Pl,q0 (x{i} ) = 1 − b,

over distinct indices q, q 0 and all such inputs. Empty maxima are zero. These are proﬁles of
the whole family; no structured-input condition is present.

Lemma 4.1. The base proﬁles satisfy

                        S0,0 ≤ L,       S0,1 ≤ LM,       J0,0 = J0,1 = 0.

Proof. At a rejecting input of a ball predicate with radius R = h0 − q ≤ d, every successful
ﬂip approaches a center at distance R + 1 ≤ d + 1. At most one center can be this close,
since distinct centers have distance 2L = 2d + 4 > 2d + 2. There are at most R + 1 ≤ L
coordinates approaching that center. The accepting-side bound is the total number LM of
coordinates. The integer-valued function D is 1-Lipschitz across a cube edge. A single ﬂip
cannot cross two distinct integer radii in the same direction, giving both joint bounds.

One to zero. At an accepting input ﬁx a satisﬁed clause. Every ﬂip that changes the

Joint one to zero. Let q < q 0 and ﬁx a clause initially satisﬁed for the stronger parent q 0 .

The clause is ﬁxed before the ﬂips are counted.

Zero to one. At a rejecting input and ﬁxed parent index q, form two lists of initially
almost-satisﬁed clauses. Let T consist of clauses with exactly one failed target and no failed
gate, and let G consist of clauses with exactly one failed gate and no failed target. A raw
bit belongs to only one child and aﬀects at most one condition of each clause. Hence every
clause made true by one ﬂip was already in T or G. The lists are ﬁxed independently of the
selected ﬂip.
    For a row v ∈ T , let mv be its unique failed target position. If u, v ∈ T and u → v,
the passing gate of u at (v, a(u, v)) says that the weaker child predicate there is zero. Its
strongest predicate is therefore zero too. The uniqueness of mv yields a(u, v) = mv . Thus
T is head-constant, and Lemma 2.2 gives |T | ≤ 81 < t. Each member has at most Sl−1,0
repairs, for a total at most tSl−1,0 .
    For each row in G, all its target predicates are true. Every internal edge u → v of G
therefore fails the gate of clause u in row v, because the strongest target there is one. Since
each clause in G has exactly one failed gate, its induced outdegree is at most one. Edge
counting gives |G| ≤ 3. At most one member has induced outdegree zero: an edge between
two such members would contradict this property. Every other member has its unique failed
gate in a row of G, whose strongest target index Q is initially one. Repairing the weaker
gate index Q − q from one to zero forces Q to zero by nesting. These are distinct indices,
so such a repair costs at most Jl−1,1 . The possible exceptional member costs at most Sl−1,1 .
The safe bound Sl−1,1 + 3Jl−1,1 , together with the T estimate, proves (9).

weaker parent q. A clause ﬁnally true for q 0 is also ﬁnally true for q, so it belongs to one
of these lists. The T repairs still cost at most tSl−1,0 . For a G witness, its failed gate at

    We recall the complete composition arguments. If g is a total Boolean function on an
outer coordinate set and h is a total Boolean function on an inner coordinate set, let g ◦ h
apply an identical independent copy of h in each outer position. At any input, a sensitive
raw bit must be sensitive for its child h, and that child’s output position must be sensitive
for g at the vector of child outputs. Hence

                                     s(g ◦ h) ≤ s(g)s(h).                                  (19)

If h(0) = 0, choose disjoint sensitive blocks Ci for g at zero and disjoint sensitive blocks Dj
for h at zero. The product blocks Ci × Dj are nonempty and pairwise disjoint. Flipping one
of them changes exactly the child outputs in Ci , so it changes g. Consequently

                               bs(g ◦ h, 0) ≥ bs(g, 0) bs(h, 0).                           (20)

For pairwise disjointness, two product blocks with diﬀerent outer indices use disjoint outer
sets, while those with the same outer index and diﬀerent inner indices use disjoint inner sets.
    If both functions are coordinate-transitive, so is their composition: an outer automor-
phism permutes the identical children, and independent inner automorphisms preserve their
outputs. To move a raw coordinate, move its outer position ﬁrst and then its inner position.
The generated group preserves the composition and is transitive on the full product coordi-
nate set. No automorphism is asserted to preserve a chosen witness family; only invariance
of the functions is needed.

8    Formal support and provenance
The accompanying TSLocal.lean package supplies scoped Lean support, using Lean 4.34.1
and the pinned mathlib revision recorded in its provenance. It does not formalize Theo-
rem 1.1. Its mathematical interfaces fall into three groups.
    First, it deﬁnes ﬁnite words recursively, an option-valued ﬁrst-diﬀerence edge label, and
the head-constant condition. Under an assumed irreﬂexive total base relation on an additive
group, it proves incoming-label uniqueness in the ﬁrst-coordinate quotient, inheritance in
each ﬁber, ﬁnite ﬁber counting, and the bound |I| ≤ 3h , including |I| < 82 when h = 4.
Thus this part includes an actual recursive exclusion argument, not merely the arithmetic
inequality 81 < 82. The base relation is not identiﬁed in Lean with the Paley-square relation
used in Section 2.
    Second, for function-valued vectors over a ﬁeld and a subgroup H of its unit group,
it proves aﬃne diﬀerence scaling, preservation of a speciﬁed ﬁrst diﬀering coordinate, and

                                              8
injectivity, transitivity, composition, identity and a left inverse for the row-position maps.
This algebraic model is separate from the recursive word representation. The identiﬁca-
tion of those representations, the square-subgroup orientation, and the resulting gate-label
covariance for the actual Paley tournament remain parts of the prose proof.
    Third, it proves the ﬁnite-depth coupled-proﬁle estimate from explicitly assumed scalar
recurrences, initial bounds u0 ≤ L, v0 ≤ 0, and L, ε ≥ 0, ε3d+1 ≤ 2. The conclusion includes
ul ≤ 2L and vl ≤ εL(3l − 1) for l ≤ d. Separate normalization lemmas assume the displayed
one-side and zero-side scalar inequalities. Exact seed support proves 220 · 1936 < 320 and
                     (44M 21 )2 < M 2 k 20   under M > 0 and 3M 2 ≤ 2k.
These assumptions are discharged mathematically in Sections 5–7, not by a formal derivation
of Boolean sensitivities.
    The implementation does not formalize the nested Boolean predicates, their ordi-
nary/joint proﬁle recurrences, the common permutation action on their raw bits, prime
existence and rounding choices, the disjoint zero blocks, OR balancing, Boolean composi-
tion, or the logarithmic exponent conclusion. Exact declaration signatures, source hashes,
compiler assumptions and validation records are supplied in the accompanying coverage
receipt. They must be read with these exclusions; local support is not a formal proof of the
full theorem.
    The proof above is a human-readable mathematical argument. Finite diagnostic compu-
tations are not premises for its asymptotic claims. The ﬁxed-depth tournament, arbitrary
depth of the nested predicates, suﬃciently large prime, and iterated compositions each have
separate roles; no ﬁnite experiment substitutes for their quantiﬁed proofs.
