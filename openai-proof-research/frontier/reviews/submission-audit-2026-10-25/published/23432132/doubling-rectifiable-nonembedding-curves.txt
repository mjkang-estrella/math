         Doubling rectiﬁable curves with no ﬁnite-dimensional
                        bi-Lipschitz embedding
                                          Myeongjin Kang

                                            October 2026


                                               Abstract
          Using the ﬁxed doubling Hilbert-space obstruction of OpenAI as an external premise, we
      construct in every inﬁnite-dimensional real Banach space a Lipschitz image of [0, 1] that is
      uniformly doubling in its ambient norm metric and admits no bi-Lipschitz embedding into
      any ﬁnite-dimensional real normed space at any ﬁnite distortion. The same compact, path-
      connected set of ﬁnite one-dimensional Hausdorﬀ measure works for all target dimensions
      and distortions. The additional construction is a dimension-independent ambient doubling
      thickening of a ﬁnite doubling Hilbert set: its dyadic nets are lifted to their own scales in
      one extra coordinate and joined by parent segments. Direct estimates control balls at edge
      interiors, including the bottom vertical edges and all scale regimes. Classical Dvoretzky em-
      beddings of the whole ﬁnite graph spans, shrinking placement along a spine, and summable
      closed tours then produce the curve. No intrinsic path metric, injective parametrization, or
      regularity beyond the stated conclusions is asserted.


1    Conventions, input, and main result
All balls in this paper are closed. If (M, d) is a metric space, write

                                 B M (x, r) = {y ∈ M : d(x, y) ≤ r}.

We call M D-doubling if every such ball with x ∈ M and r > 0 is covered by at most D balls
of radius r/2 with centers in M . Centers of a covering ball need not belong to the ball being
covered. For a subset of a normed space the metric is always the restricted ambient norm, never
the graph or path metric.
    An embedding f : M → N has distortion at most A if some scale a > 0 satisﬁes

                   a dM (x, y) ≤ dN (f (x), f (y)) ≤ Aa dM (x, y)       (x, y ∈ M ).                  (1.1)

The target dimension and the distortion are both allowed to be arbitrary ﬁnite numbers.
   Our external nonembedding input is the following theorem, used as stated, without a proof
here.
Input theorem 1 (OpenAI, Theorem 1). There is a ﬁxed subset S of real ℓ2 , with its induced
Hilbert metric, that is λ-doubling for λ = 76800 and admits no bi-Lipschitz embedding into Rk
for any positive integer k, at any ﬁnite distortion.

lary already supplies ﬁnite witnesses, Dvoretzky placement and a disconnected compact assem-
bly. We reproduce the parts of those deductions needed here, with attribution, and use Input
theorem 1 as the sole nonembedding premise.

                                                   1
Theorem A. Under Input theorem 1, every inﬁnite-dimensional real Banach space B contains
a set Γ = γ([0, 1]), for a Lipschitz map γ : [0, 1] → B, such that:

    1. Γ is Λ-doubling in its restricted ambient norm metric;

    2. Γ admits no ﬁnite-distortion bi-Lipschitz embedding into any ﬁnite-dimensional real
       normed space.

In particular, Γ is one compact, path-connected set with H1 (Γ) < ∞, chosen before the target
dimension and distortion are speciﬁed.

    We do not claim that γ is injective, that Γ is a simple arc, that its path metric is doubling,
or that it is uniformly quasiconvex or Ahlfors regular. The constants in (1.2) are not optimized,
and no quantitative cardinality bound for the ﬁnite witnesses is required.
    The main additional ingredient is the ﬁnite ambient thickening of Section 2. Net hierarchies
and scale separation are established tools, for example in Gupta–Talwar’s intrinsic doubling
completion [4]. Here the net scale is made into an actual extra Hilbert coordinate. This
controls ambient balls meeting long segment interiors, an issue not settled by doubling of a
graph’s shortest-path metric. Related distinctions are discussed in Section 7.


2     Finite ambient doubling thickening
For a real Hilbert space H, the sum H ⊕ R carries its Hilbert product norm.

Theorem 2.1. Let X ⊂ H be ﬁnite, with at least two points, diam X = 1, and doubling
constant at most an integer D ≥ 1. There is a ﬁnite abstract rooted tree whose straight-segment
realization G ⊂ H ⊕ R has the following properties:

    1. X × {0} ⊂ G, with all its original distances preserved;
                                               √
    2. G is connected, lies in a ball of radius 2 about its root o, and has ﬁnite abstract edge-
       length sum;

    3. G is 256D10 -doubling in its ambient Hilbert metric.

The doubling constant is independent of |X|, its minimum separation and the dimension of its
span.

   We separate the construction from the ball estimates. Segment crossings or overlaps are
permitted; the abstract tree is used for traversal and length accounting, not to deﬁne distances
on G.

2.1    The ﬁnite tree and its realization

with distinct points at distances strictly greater than sk . Maximality makes Nk an sk -net: every
point of X is within distance sk of Nk . Also Nm = X, and N0 is a 1-net since diam X = 1.
   For each x ∈ Nk choose a parent pk (x) ∈ Nk−1 with

                                   ∥x − pk (x)∥ ≤ sk−1 = 2sk .                               (2.1)

one parent, and every parent chain reaches (0, x0 ). This ﬁnite connected graph is a rooted tree.

3.1   Finite witnesses from the ﬁxed source obstruction
The following compactness deduction is the ﬁnite-witness argument in the proof of the source’s
compact Banach-space corollary [1].

Lemma 3.1. Under Input theorem 1, for every positive integer k and ﬁnite A ≥ 1 there is a
ﬁnite subset of S admitting no embedding into Rk with distortion at most A.

Proof. Suppose every ﬁnite subset admitted such an embedding. Fix o ∈ S and let

                         Qx = {u ∈ Rk : ∥u∥ ≤ A dS (o, x)}         (x ∈ S).
                         ∏
Each Qx is compact, so       x∈S Qx is compact. In this product impose the closed constraints

                              dS (x, y) ≤ ∥f (x) − f (y)∥ ≤ A dS (x, y).

Any ﬁnite list of constraints involves ﬁnitely many points. Embed those points together with
o, translate to send o to zero, and divide by the embedding’s lower scale in (1.1). The re-
sulting coordinates belong to their Qx ; set all remaining coordinates to zero. This proves the
ﬁnite-intersection property. Product compactness then produces an embedding of all of S with
distortion at most A, contradicting Input theorem 1.

    Choose for each j ≥ 1 a ﬁnite Xj ⊂ S with no embedding into Rj of distortion at most j.
These choices are made once, before any ﬁnal target is considered. Each Xj has at least two
points.
    Every Xj is λ2 -doubling in its induced metric. Indeed, an Xj -ball of radius r lies in the
corresponding S-ball. Two halvings cover the latter by λ2 balls of radius r/4. In each ball
meeting the original Xj -ball, choose a point of that intersection. Recenter there; the part in
question is within distance r/2 of the new center. This keeps all centers in Xj .
    Rescale each ﬁnite witness to diameter one, and retain the notation Xj . Rescaling preserves
both doubling and the obstruction because distortion permits a free scale. Apply Theorem 2.1
with D = λ2 to obtain Gj , root oj , and abstract edge-length sum Lj = L(Gj ). All Gj are
∆-doubling, with ∆ as in (1.2).

ﬁnite-dimensional form is used.

6    Excluding every ﬁnite-dimensional target
The construction of Γ used the entire sequence (Xj ) before any target space or distortion was
chosen. Suppose that f : Γ → V is a ﬁnite-distortion bi-Lipschitz embedding into a ﬁnite-
dimensional real normed space. The case dim V = 0 is impossible because I is nontrivial. If
dim V = k ≥ 1, choose a linear isomorphism V → Rk . Composing with f gives a bi-Lipschitz
map of some ﬁnite distortion A into Euclidean Rk .
   Choose an integer j ≥ k with j ≥ 2A. The bottom copy of Xj in Gj is isometric to Xj . Its
image in Cj under translation, scaling, and Tj has distortion at most two by (3.1); the scalar
ρj does not aﬀect distortion. Restrict the composite map to that copy and then include Rk
isometrically in Rj . This embeds Xj into Rj with distortion at most 2A ≤ j, contrary to its
choice. This completes the proof of Theorem A.


7    Relation to earlier constructions
The nonembedding input, extraction of ﬁnite witnesses, qualitative use of Dvoretzky’s theorem,
and separated accumulation of shrinking clusters are antecedents. In particular, the pinned
source [1] already proves a compact doubling nonembedding subset of every inﬁnite-dimensional
Banach space. The additional operation here is Theorem 2.1: a connected ﬁnite polygonal
thickening of each ﬁnite Hilbert witness, retaining its original distances and a uniform doubling
bound for the ambient Hilbert metric. Summable tree tours then supply a single rectiﬁable
curve.
    Gupta and Talwar [4, Theorem 13 and Section 4] construct low-dimensional completions
of doubling metrics using graph and shortest-path structures. Their net-tree and long-edge
modiﬁcations address the metric of a completion. They do not directly give the ambient Hilbert


                                                7
realization needed here: mapping an abstract graph into Hilbert space can bring interiors of
edges close together even when their endpoints are far apart in the graph metric. The height
coordinate and the four ambient-ball cases above control precisely this issue.
    Semmes [5, Section 6, especially Theorem 6.3] gives a closely related but diﬀerent construc-
tion. A snowﬂake set in Euclidean space is used to deﬁne a weight involving distance to that
set, and a weighted path metric produces a doubling metric space with curves. That metric is
not the inherited constant-coeﬃcient Hilbert metric. Consequently it does not directly provide
a ﬁnite-dimensional normed span to which the Dvoretzky step in our argument applies. This
comparison concerns the actual weighted-metric construction, not merely the common presence
of curves and doubling.
    Schul’s Hilbert-space traveling-salesman theorem [6, Theorem 1.5 and Section 4] supplies
rectiﬁable connected supersets from a multiscale beta-number estimate. Its stated conclusion
controls length; it does not assert a universal doubling bound for the ambient metric of the
output set in terms of a doubling constant of the input. The estimate in Theorem 2.1 supplies
that additional property directly. We make no claim of an optimal constant, an injective curve,
or an exhaustive classiﬁcation of earlier connected metric constructions. These comparisons are
bounded comparisons of the indicated primary results and proof operations, not a certiﬁcation
of worldwide priority.


8    Proof status and reproducibility
The accompanying Lean package, CDFLocal, checks selected metric-cover interfaces and exact
bounds. It deﬁnes closed-ball covers by actual ﬁnite sets of centers and proves cover composition,
iterated doubling, recentering onto subsets, separated-set packing, D2 doubling for metric sub-
sets, and D4 doubling under a surjective noncontracting 2-Lipschitz map. It also checks dyadic
scale counts, a ﬁve-center normed-segment cover, parent-edge projection and height inequalities,
metric grouping and cluster-separation estimates, and the polynomial count bounds used here.
The segment lemma is a suﬃcient ﬁve-center bound; the elementary two-center observation in
this manuscript is not its formal statement.
    These are conditional support results. The package does not construct the net tree, realize
the complete Hilbert graph, prove the whole all-regime graph-cover theorem, instantiate Dvoret-
zky, construct the inﬁnite tour, prove compactness, or derive the ﬁnal nonembedding theorem.
In particular, the geometric hypotheses connecting individual inequalities to the constructed
graph are supplied by the written proof. The source theorem is also external. The exact formal
source, declaration types, coverage limits, and validation records are separately hash-bound in
the accompanying provenance packet. No full formalization of Theorem A is claimed. The
mathematical proof in this article is a human-readable deduction from Input Theorem 1 and
the cited classical results. The source theorem is an explicitly accepted external premise; its
nonembedding proof is not re-proved or audited here. A separate source-and-proof map records
the exact pinned source interfaces, the reviewed scope, and the limits of the literature reading.
The accompanying build script reproduces this PDF from the original TeX source. Integrity
checks authenticate ﬁle contents and review dependencies; they are not mathematical proof
certiﬁcates for the full theorem.
