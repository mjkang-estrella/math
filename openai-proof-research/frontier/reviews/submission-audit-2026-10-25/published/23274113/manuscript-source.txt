\begin{abstract}
Let $H_G(x)$ be the maximum entropy of a perfect-matching law with
prescribed edge marginals $x$ in a finite loopless multigraph. We prove
that, for every vertex $v$,
\[
 H_G(x)\ge-\sum_e x_e\log x_e
       +\frac32\sum_{e\notin\delta(v)}(1-x_e)\log(1-x_e).
\]
On $2m$ vertices this gives a coefficient $\frac32(1-1/m)$ in the
unrooted complementary-entropy bound. The proof reserves the contribution
of one vertex star. This makes the entropy deficit exactly additive
under contraction of a tight odd cut and permits induction before a
covariance second-variation argument. Boundary marginals and labelled
parallel edges are included. We also obtain lower bounds for weighted
partition functions and for graphs containing edge-disjoint perfect
matchings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite undirected loopless multigraph with
$|V|=2m\ge2$ and at least one perfect matching. Parallel edges have
distinct labels. Write $\mathcal M(G)$ for the matching family and
$P(G)=\conv\{\mathbf1_M:M\in\mathcal M(G)\}$. For $x\in P(G)$, define
\begin{align*}
 H_G(x)&=\max\{\Ent(p):p\text{ is a law on }\mathcal M(G),\
                         \E_p\mathbf1_M=x\},\\
 F_G(x)&=-\sum_{e\in E}x_e\log x_e, &
 B_G(x)&=-\sum_{e\in E}(1-x_e)\log(1-x_e).
\end{align*}
All logarithms are natural, with $0\log0=0$. The maximization in
$H_G$ is essential: the conclusions do not concern every law with the
given marginals. For $v\in V$, set
\[
 b_v(x)=-\sum_{e\in\delta(v)}(1-x_e)\log(1-x_e),\qquad
 W_{G,v}(x)=B_G(x)-b_v(x).
\]
We omit the subscript $G$ when the graph is understood.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every such graph, every $x\in P(G)$ and every $v\in V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root}
                    F_G(x)-H_G(x)\le\frac32 W_{G,v}(x).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unrooted}
 H_G(x)\ge F_G(x)-\frac32\bigl(B_G(x)-\max_v b_v(x)\bigr)
       \ge F_G(x)-\frac32\left(1-\frac1m\right)B_G(x).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Indeed, $\sum_v b_v=2B$ implies $\max_v b_v\ge B/m$.
There is no restriction on degrees, connectivity or edge multiplicities.
When $m=1$, both sides of \eqref{eq:root} are zero.

The preprint \cite{OpenAI} proves
$H\ge F-2(1-1/m)B$ by combining face dimension with a covariance
trace. The coefficient in \eqref{eq:unrooted} is three quarters of
that coefficient for every $m>1$. The present argument uses the same
finite maximum-entropy and covariance framework, but changes the
quantity used in the induction. Across a tight odd cut with crossing
distribution $q$, the unrooted deficit $F-cB-H$ acquires a positive
term $c[-\sum_e(1-q_e)\log(1-q_e)]$. Reserving the star of a root
pays this term exactly. A positive global maximum then has no
nontrivial tight odd cut, so the degree equations alone give the
dimension estimate needed for $c>3/2$.

Edmonds' theorem \cite{Edmonds} supplies the polytope description.
Tight-cut contractions also underlie the dimension theory of Edmonds,
Lov\'asz and Pulleyblank \cite{ELP}. In the bipartite setting,
Gurvits' weighted Bethe inequality \cite[Theorem 2.2]{Gurvits},
together with entropy duality, gives $H\ge F-B$. That bound has a
smaller order-independent coefficient on its subclass. Our assertion
concerns arbitrary graphs and retains a root and the finite order.
The contraction and covariance ingredients are not new separately;
their combination through the additive quantity $W_{G,v}$ proves
\eqref{eq:root}.

We give the entropy and contraction arguments below. Apart from the
classical matching-polytope theorem, no result of \cite{OpenAI} is
needed as an unproved premise of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. We credit
that manuscript for the proof framework and the coefficient-two
comparison. Our theorem is an existence result for laws at prescribed
marginals; it does not supply an efficient sampler.

\section{Entropy on a matching face}
Edmonds' description, in perfect-matching form, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polytope}
 P(G)=\{x\ge0:x(\delta(v))=1\ (v\in V),\quad
                   x(\delta(S))\ge1\ (|S|\text{ odd})\}.
\end{equation}
It applies with distinct parallel-edge coordinates. It can also be
deduced from the simple-graph statement by adding coordinates in each
parallel class, decomposing the resulting simple-graph mean, and
choosing a label conditionally on the selected class.
At $x$, let $Q_x$ be the face obtained by retaining all constraints
tight at $x$, and let $T_x$ be its direction space. The point $x$ is
in the relative interior of $Q_x$: small two-sided perturbations
preserving the active constraints preserve every inactive inequality.
Every law with mean $x$ is supported on the matching vectors in $Q_x$,
since each active inequality has nonnegative slack on every matching.
In particular, if all $s$ edge coordinates are positive and no odd cut
with at least three vertices on each side is tight, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimension}
                         \dim T_x\ge s-2m.
\end{equation}
Singleton cuts and their complements are already degree equations.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:entropy}
The function $H$ is attained and continuous on $P(G)$. On the
matching vectors $\mathcal A_x$ of $Q_x$, its maximizing law is
positive and has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gibbs}
 p_a=\frac{e^{\theta\cdot a}}{\sum_{b\in\mathcal A_x}e^{\theta\cdot b}}.
\end{equation}
Its covariance $K$ has image $T_x$ and diagonal
$K_{ee}=x_e(1-x_e)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The fiber of laws with a given mean is a nonempty compact subset of a
finite simplex, so entropy attains its maximum. Mixing maximizing laws
shows that $H$ is concave. If $y_n\to x$, a convergent subsequence of
maximizing laws proves $\limsup H(y_n)\le H(x)$.
For the reverse inequality, put $t=\sqrt{\|y-x\|}$ and
$z=x+(y-x)/t$ for $y\ne x$ sufficiently close to $x$.
Then $z\in P(G)$. Active inequalities remain satisfied by linearity,
and all inactive inequalities remain satisfied because $z\to x$.
Since $y=(1-t)x+tz$ and $H\ge0$, concavity gives
$H(y)\ge(1-t)H(x)$. This proves continuity.

Relative interiority supplies a law $q$ of mean $x$ positive on all
of $\mathcal A_x$: mix a small amount of the uniform law on those
atoms with a law whose mean lies just beyond $x$ from their barycenter.
If a maximizer $p$ vanished on any face atom, mixing in $uq$ would
increase entropy by a positive multiple of $u\log(1/u)+O(u)$.
Thus $p$ is positive. The entropy gradient is orthogonal to the kernel
of the matrix with columns $(1,a)$. It follows that
$\log p_a$ is affine in $a$, proving \eqref{eq:gibbs}.

For any parameter in \eqref{eq:gibbs}, the resulting law maximizes
entropy at its own mean. Indeed, nonnegativity of relative entropy
gives $\Ent(q)\le\log\sum_a e^{\theta\cdot a}-\theta\cdot\E_q a$.
The mean remains in the relative interior of the same face. Finally,
$w^TKw=\sum_a p_a[w\cdot(a-x)]^2$, so its kernel is $T_x^\perp$.
The diagonal formula follows because each edge indicator is Boolean.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:trace}
Suppose $0<x_e<1$ for every edge. If $F-cW_v-H$ has a local maximum
at $x$, then, with $d=\dim T_x$ and $s=|E|$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trace}
                         0\ge d-s+m+c(m-1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $J=F-cW_v$. Its Hessian $A$ is diagonal, with
\[
 A_{ee}=-\frac1{x_e}+
          \frac{c\mathbf1_{\{e\notin\delta(v)\}}}{1-x_e}.
\]
Tilt the law in Lemma~\ref{lem:entropy} by $\theta+ta$, and denote
its mean by $y(t)$. Then $y'(0)=Ka$, $y''(0)\in T_x$, and
\[
 H(y(t))=\log\sum_{b\in\mathcal A_x}e^{(\theta+ta)\cdot b}
                                  -(\theta+ta)\cdot y(t).
\]
For $r(t)=J(y(t))-H(y(t))$, differentiation gives
\begin{align*}
 r'(0)&=(\nabla J+\theta)\cdot Ka,\\
 r''(0)&=(Ka)^TA(Ka)+a^TKa+(\nabla J+\theta)\cdot y''(0).
\end{align*}
Stationarity for every $a$ and $\operatorname{im}K=T_x$ cancel the
last term. For each unit eigenvector of $K$ with positive eigenvalue
$\lambda$, divide $r''(0)\le0$ by $\lambda$ and then sum. This yields
$0\ge d+\tr(KA)$. No commutation of the two matrices is needed.
The degree equations give $\sum_e x_e=m$ and
$\sum_{e\notin\delta(v)}x_e=m-1$, whence
\[
 \tr(KA)=\sum_e[-(1-x_e)+c\mathbf1_{\{e\notin\delta(v)\}}x_e]
         =-s+m+c(m-1).
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The rooted contraction identity}
Write $D_G=F_G-H_G$. Let $S$ be odd with both $S$ and its complement
of size at least three, and suppose $x(\delta(S))=1$. Choose the
side containing the root $v$ to be $S$. The graph $G_1$ retains $S$
and contracts its complement; $G_2$ retains the complement and
contracts $S$ to a vertex $z$. Discard edges internal to a contracted
side. Keep every crossing label separately in both graphs.
Let $x_1,x_2$ be the restricted means and put $q=x|_{\delta(S)}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gluing}
For the preceding contraction,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:additive}
 D_G(x)=D_{G_1}(x_1)+D_{G_2}(x_2),\qquad
 W_{G,v}(x)=W_{G_1,v}(x_1)+W_{G_2,z}(x_2).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A matching has a positive odd number of crossing edges. Under a law
of mean $x$, its expected crossing count is one, so it crosses exactly
once almost surely. Its two contractions are matchings sharing a
crossing label $L$ with law $q$. This also proves feasibility of
$x_1,x_2$. Define
\[
 h(q)=-\sum_e q_e\log q_e,\qquad
 b(q)=-\sum_e(1-q_e)\log(1-q_e).
\]
Conditional subadditivity gives
$\Ent(M_1,M_2)\le\Ent(M_1)+\Ent(M_2)-h(q)$.
Conversely, take maximizing child laws, draw $L\sim q$, and draw
the children independently conditional on $L$. Joining their common
crossing edge gives a parent matching with the required mean.
Zero-probability labels need no conditional law. Thus
\begin{align*}
 H_G&=H_{G_1}+H_{G_2}-h(q),\\
 F_G&=F_{G_1}+F_{G_2}-h(q), & B_G&=B_{G_1}+B_{G_2}-b(q).
\end{align*}
The first identity in \eqref{eq:additive} follows. The original root
star is unchanged in $G_1$, whereas $b_z(x_2)=b(q)$.
Subtracting these star contributions proves the second identity.
\end{proof}

\section{Small graphs and the induction}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ternary}
For a probability triple $p$, put
$h(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i$ and
$b(p)=-\sum_i(1-p_i)\log(1-p_i)$. Then $h(p)\le3b(p)/2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $f(u)=-u\log u+\frac32(1-u)\log(1-u)$.
On the boundary of the probability triangle, $\sum_i f(p_i)=-h(p)/2\le0$.
In the interior, $f''(u)=-1/u+3/[2(1-u)]$ changes sign at $2/5$.
At a maximum the three first derivatives agree. Two coordinates
above $2/5$, or one above and one equal to $2/5$, violate the
second-derivative test in their difference direction. Two equal to
$2/5$ violate stationarity with the third. If all are at most $2/5$,
strict decrease of $f'$ there forces the uniform triple, where the
sum is $\log(8/9)<0$.

The remaining possibility has two equal smaller coordinates and one
larger coordinate. Write it as $(t,t,1-2t)$, $0<t<1/3$, and set
$g(t)=2f(t)+f(1-2t)$. With $u=(1-2t)/t\ge1$,
\[
 \frac12 g'(t)=k(u)=\log u-\frac32\log\frac{u+1}{2},\qquad
 k'(u)=\frac{1-u/2}{u(u+1)}.
\]
The function $k$ increases from zero, then decreases to minus infinity,
with exactly one further zero. As $t$ increases, $g$ therefore first
decreases and then increases. Its maximum on $[0,1/3]$ is an endpoint
value, either $0$ or $\log(8/9)$. This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:parallel}
Collapse parallel classes to a simple graph, and let $y_a$ be the sum
of $x_e$ in class $a$. Then $D_G(x)=D_{\rm simple}(y)$ and
$W_{G,v}(x)\ge W_{{\rm simple},v}(y)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
When $y_a>0$, let $r^a_e=x_e/y_a$ be its conditional label law.
The chain rule and conditional subadditivity give
\[
 H_G(x)\le H_{\rm simple}(y)+\sum_a y_a h(r^a).
\]
For clarity, the entropy of the labels conditional on the collapsed
matching is at most the sum of their conditional entropies. Concavity
then bounds the contribution of class $a$ by $y_a h(r^a)$.
Choose a maximizing collapsed law and independently assign labels
with these conditional probabilities to attain equality. The same
identity holds for $F$, so the deficits agree. For
$\beta(u)=-(1-u)\log(1-u)$, concavity and $\beta(0)=0$ give
$\sum_{e\in a}\beta(x_e)\ge\beta(y_a)$. Each class is entirely
inside or outside the root star, proving the assertion for $W$.
Classes of zero mass contribute zero throughout.
\end{proof}

On two vertices $H=F$ and $W_v=0$. On four vertices use
Lemma~\ref{lem:parallel}. A simple graph has at most the three
perfect-matching types of $K_4$. Their probabilities $p_1,p_2,p_3$
are fixed by the mean, and each type contains two edges. Thus
$H=h(p)$, $F=2h(p)$, and $W_v=b(p)$ at every root. Missing types
have probability zero. Lemma~\ref{lem:ternary} proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at these orders.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Fix $c=3/2+\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon>0$. We prove $D_G\le cW_{G,v}$
simultaneously for all graphs, feasible means and roots by induction on
$m$. The two small orders were just proved. Suppose $m\ge3$ and a
counterexample exists. By continuity and compactness, $D_G-cW_{G,v}$
has a positive global maximum, attained at some $x$.

Delete zero-coordinate edges. The point remains a global maximum on
the resulting graph, since every mean there extends by zeros to the
original graph with the same objective. If $x_e=1$, that edge belongs
to every matching in the law and exhausts both endpoint degrees.
Delete it and its endpoints. This preserves $D$ and $B$. If the root
survives, it also preserves $W_v$, and induction contradicts positivity.
If the root is deleted, its star contribution was zero, so $W_v=B$.
The rooted bound at any vertex of the remaining graph gives
$D\le c(B-b_w)\le cB$, again a contradiction. If nothing remains,
$D=B=0$. Hence every retained coordinate is strictly between zero and one.

If a nontrivial odd cut is tight, both contracted graphs have smaller
even order. Induction and Lemma~\ref{lem:gluing} give
$D_G-cW_{G,v}\le0$, a contradiction. Thus no such cut is tight at
the positive maximum. Equations~\eqref{eq:dimension} and
\eqref{eq:trace} now imply
\[
 0\ge d-s+m+c(m-1)\ge(c-1)m-c
       =\frac{m-3}{2}+\varepsilon(m-1)>0.
\]
This contradiction completes the induction. For fixed $G,x,v$, let
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ to obtain \eqref{eq:root}. The limiting
step is needed at $m=3$, where the dimension estimate alone gives a
non-strict inequality at $c=3/2$. The consequence
\eqref{eq:unrooted} was established in the introduction.
\end{proof}

\section{Weighted and counting consequences}
For finite real edge weights $\theta_e$, put
\[
 Z_\theta=\sum_{M\in\mathcal M(G)}e^{\theta\cdot\mathbf1_M},\qquad
 Q_\theta=\max_{x\in P(G)}\{F(x)+\theta\cdot x\}.
\]
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:weighted}
For every graph in Theorem~\ref{thm:main},
\begin{align}
 \log Z_\theta&\ge\max_{x\in P(G)}
 \{F(x)+\theta\cdot x-\tfrac32(B(x)-\max_v b_v(x))\},\label{eq:weightedroot}\\
 Q_\theta-\tfrac32(m-1)&\le\log Z_\theta\le Q_\theta.\label{eq:weighted}
\end{align}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The Gibbs variational principle, obtained by comparing any matching law
with the law proportional to $e^{\theta\cdot\mathbf1_M}$, gives
$\log Z_\theta=\max_x(H(x)+\theta\cdot x)$.
For any matching law, order the edges and apply the entropy chain
rule to each vertex star. Conditioning on all earlier edges can only
decrease each term, and every edge lies in two stars. Thus
$2\Ent(M)\le\sum_v\Ent(M\cap\delta(v))=2F(x)$, proving $H\le F$.
This gives the upper bound; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives
\eqref{eq:weightedroot}. Finally,
$-(1-u)\log(1-u)\le u$ for $0\le u\le1$, so
$W_v\le\sum_{e\notin\delta(v)}x_e=m-1$.
Evaluate the rooted lower bound at a maximizer for $Q_\theta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:count}
If $G$ has $k\ge2$ edge-disjoint perfect matchings, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 |\mathcal M(G)|\ge k^m(1-1/k)^{\frac32(m-1)(k-1)}
                      \ge k^m e^{-\frac32(m-1)}.
\end{equation}
The same bounds hold if $G$ is $k$-regular and every odd cut has at
least $k$ edges.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The uniform mixture of the $k$ disjoint matchings has marginal $1/k$
on their union, with $km$ edges and $k$ edges incident to every vertex.
It therefore has $F=m\log k$ and
$W_v=(m-1)(k-1)\log(k/(k-1))$.
Use $\log|\mathcal M(G)|\ge H(x)$ and the rooted bound.
The second inequality follows from $\log(1+1/(k-1))\le1/(k-1)$.
In the regular case the constant vector $x_e=1/k$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:polytope}, so the same computation applies.
\end{proof}

After removing zero edges and forced matching edges, one may instead
apply the theorem to each remaining connected component. Entropy at
the prescribed mean is additive over components: subadditivity gives
one direction and the product of maximizing laws gives the other.
This yields the corresponding sum of rooted bounds and can improve
the estimate from the original vertex count.

We do not claim that $3/2$ is optimal. Already on $K_4$, the uniform
mixture of its three matchings has $D=\log3$ and
$W_v=2\log(3/2)$. Hence any universal coefficient in
\eqref{eq:root} is at least $\log3/[2\log(3/2)]\approx1.35476$.
The counting corollary is a consequence of the prescribed-mean theorem;
it need not improve bounds specialized to bipartite graphs or other
restricted families.
