  Edmonds’ theorem [1] supplies the polytope description. Tight-cut contractions also underlie
the dimension theory of Edmonds, Lovász and Pulleyblank [2]. In the bipartite setting, Gurvits’
weighted Bethe inequality [3, Theorem 2.2], together with entropy duality, gives H ≥ F − B. That
bound has a smaller order-independent coeﬃcient on its subclass. Our assertion concerns arbitrary
graphs and retains a root and the ﬁnite order. The contraction and covariance ingredients are not
new separately; their combination through the additive quantity WG,v proves (1.1).
  We give the entropy and contraction arguments below. Apart from the classical matching-
polytope theorem, no result of [4] is needed as an unproved premise of Theorem 1.1. We credit
that manuscript for the proof framework and the coeﬃcient-two comparison. Our theorem is an
existence result for laws at prescribed marginals; it does not supply an eﬃcient sampler.

                             3. The rooted contraction identity
   Write DG = FG − HG . Let S be odd with both S and its complement of size at least three, and
suppose x(δ(S)) = 1. Choose the side containing the root v to be S. The graph G1 retains S and
contracts its complement; G2 retains the complement and contracts S to a vertex z. Discard edges
internal to a contracted side. Keep every crossing label separately in both graphs. Let x1 , x2 be
the restricted means and put q = x|δ(S) .
Lemma 3.1. For the preceding contraction,
               DG (x) = DG1 (x1 ) + DG2 (x2 ),                 WG,v (x) = WG1 ,v (x1 ) + WG2 ,z (x2 ).   (3.1)
Proof. A matching has a positive odd number of crossing edges. Under a law of mean x, its expected
crossing count is one, so it crosses exactly once almost surely. Its two contractions are matchings
sharing a crossing label L with law q. This also proves feasibility of x1 , x2 . Deﬁne
                                ∑                                       ∑
                     h(q) = −           qe log qe ,          b(q) = −       (1 − qe ) log(1 − qe ).
                                    e                                   e

Conditional subadditivity gives Ent(M1 , M2 ) ≤ Ent(M1 ) + Ent(M2 ) − h(q). Conversely, take max-
imizing child laws, draw L ∼ q, and draw the children independently conditional on L. Joining
their common crossing edge gives a parent matching with the required mean. Zero-probability
labels need no conditional law. Thus
                 HG = HG1 + HG2 − h(q),
                 FG = FG1 + FG2 − h(q),                                     BG = BG1 + BG2 − b(q).
The ﬁrst identity in (3.1) follows. The original root star is unchanged in G1 , whereas bz (x2 ) = b(q).
Subtracting these star contributions proves the second identity.                                     □

most the three perfect-matching types of K4 . Their probabilities p1 , p2 , p3 are ﬁxed by the mean,

Proof of Theorem 1.1. Fix c = 3/2 + ε with ε > 0. We prove DG ≤ cWG,v simultaneously for
all graphs, feasible means and roots by induction on m. The two small orders were just proved.
Suppose m ≥ 3 and a counterexample exists. By continuity and compactness, DG − cWG,v has a
positive global maximum, attained at some x.
   Delete zero-coordinate edges. The point remains a global maximum on the resulting graph, since
every mean there extends by zeros to the original graph with the same objective. If xe = 1, that
edge belongs to every matching in the law and exhausts both endpoint degrees. Delete it and
its endpoints. This preserves D and B. If the root survives, it also preserves Wv , and induction
contradicts positivity. If the root is deleted, its star contribution was zero, so Wv = B. The rooted
bound at any vertex of the remaining graph gives D ≤ c(B − bw ) ≤ cB, again a contradiction. If
nothing remains, D = B = 0. Hence every retained coordinate is strictly between zero and one.
   If a nontrivial odd cut is tight, both contracted graphs have smaller even order. Induction and
Lemma 3.1 give DG − cWG,v ≤ 0, a contradiction. Thus no such cut is tight at the positive

   After removing zero edges and forced matching edges, one may instead apply the theorem to
each remaining connected component. Entropy at the prescribed mean is additive over components:
subadditivity gives one direction and the product of maximizing laws gives the other. This yields
the corresponding sum of rooted bounds and can improve the estimate from the original vertex
count.
   We do not claim that 3/2 is optimal. Already on K4 , the uniform mixture of its three match-
ings has D = log 3 and Wv = 2 log(3/2). Hence any universal coeﬃcient in (1.1) is at least
log 3/[2 log(3/2)] ≈ 1.35476. The counting corollary is a consequence of the prescribed-mean theo-
rem; it need not improve bounds specialized to bipartite graphs or other restricted families.
