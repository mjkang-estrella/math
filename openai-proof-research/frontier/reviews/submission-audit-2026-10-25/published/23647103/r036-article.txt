            One integral mapping-class orbit in every rank
                                         Myeongjin Kang

                                        October 18, 2026


                                              Abstract
          For every closed oriented surface of genus g ≥ 2 and every r ≥ 2, we construct a
      representation into SLr (OL ), for a number ﬁeld L ⊂ C, whose character has Zariski-
      dense orbit in the entire complex SLr character variety under the orientation-preserving
      mapping class group. The rank-two assertion is due to Coccia–Litt and has further an-
      tecedents. For r ≥ 3, two families of simple curves give a full cotangent certiﬁcate at
      a representation of the form (A, B, C, I, . . . , I) with A, B diagonal. Their joint resid-
      ual kernel is eliminated by a symmetric/skew-symmetric Cartan degeneration. Arith-
      metic parameters are chosen by ﬁnitely many open conditions; the countably many
      nonresonance conditions are imposed only on a complex torus already contained in one
      arithmetic orbit closure. Classical twist Hamiltonians then force that closure to be the
      whole variety. The higher-rank construction is presented as new to this project, without
      a claim of worldwide priority.

1     Statement, inputs, and antecedents
Let Σg be a closed connected oriented surface of genus g. Put

                  Rg,r = Hom(π1 (Σg ), SLr (C)),      Xg,r = Rg,r // SLr (C),

where the latter is the aﬃne character variety. The group Mod(Σg ) consists of orientation-
preserving mapping classes and acts on Xg,r by precomposition; inner automorphisms of
the surface group have no eﬀect on characters. We use [x, y] = xyx−1 y −1 throughout.
Theorem 1.1. For every pair of integers g ≥ 2 and r ≥ 2, there exist a number ﬁeld L ⊂ C
and a homomorphism
                               ρ : π1 (Σg ) −→ SLr (OL )
such that Mod(Σg ) · [ρ] is Zariski dense in Xg,r .
    Here OL is the full ring of integers. The conclusion concerns one orbit of an actual
integral representation and the entire complex character variety. There is no generic-input
condition and no restriction to a marked locus. No assertion for punctured surfaces is made.
For r ≥ 3 the ﬁeld in our construction depends only on r, although the selected matrices
may depend on g.

1.1   The credited rank-two case
Coccia–Litt [2, Propositions 3.1.10 and 5.0.1, Theorem 5.0.2] already prove the assertion
for r = 2, in a more general relative setting. Their Proposition 5.0.1 produces an actual
integral representation that is good for a pants decomposition, and Proposition 3.1.10 proves
                                                                                         √
density of its mapping-class
                √              orbit. To meet the unit hypothesis one can use K = Q( 5)
and v = (1 + 5)/2: both v and v − v −1 = 1 are units, and v is not a root of unity.
Their ﬁeld extension and construction therefore apply to every closed genus g ≥ 2. Density
over Q remains density after extending scalars to C: for each ﬁnite-dimensional space of
polynomials of bounded degree, the evaluation conditions have coeﬃcients in Q, so their
kernel commutes with extension of scalars. The remainder of the proof assumes r ≥ 3.
    Litt’s survey [6, Theorem 5.4.13] gives another rank-two single-orbit route, using a
Shimura-curve local system with a unitary Galois conjugate and Previte–Xia’s Theorem
1.4 [12]. Golseﬁdy–Tamam [4, Section 3.3 and Theorems G–J] identify exceptional loci that
must be excluded in the general formulation of that Previte–Xia theorem. We record both
the antecedent and this caveat; we do not use the uncaveated statement as an input to
our rank-two proof. Golseﬁdy–Tamam’s Theorem I gives a substantially stronger rank-two
orbit-closure theorem, under its suﬃciently-dense, nonexceptional, inﬁnite-orbit hypotheses.
Their detailed orbit-closure preprint [5] explicitly treats SL2 .

1.2   What the higher-rank argument adds
The pinned manuscript [10] proves potential density of the whole set of integral characters
in every rank, already over one number ﬁeld. Its closed-surface argument ﬁrst puts a whole
ﬁxed-spectrum ﬁber in that closure, then uses twist-torus saturation, Baire avoidance, and
a submersion. Those operations are antecedents here; density of a union of arithmetic orbits
does not itself choose one orbit.
    Goldman [3] supplies the symplectic and Hamiltonian twist machinery. Pickrell–Xia [11,
Proposition 2.1.24 and Lemma 2.1.34] provide a close antecedent to inﬁnitesimal spanning:
centralizers of products hg n , dense torus powers, and a BCH/root-component argument,
followed by transverse sewing in their Section 3. Their result concerns compact groups and

                                               2
generic ﬁbers in an almost-everywhere argument. The task here is to certify a particular
complex family with two commuting handle matrices inside the closure of one arithmetic
orbit.
   For comparison, the commuting twist tori associated to a pants decomposition have
dimension at most (3g − 3)(r − 1). For r ≥ 3,

                       (3g − 3)(r − 1) < (g − 1)(r2 − 1) = 12 dim Xg,r .

Thus the rank-two pants-torus ﬁber argument does not simply give the required higher-rank
cotangent certiﬁcate. We use two noncommuting families of simple curves and reduce their
common kernel to

                       (AdA − AdC )S = 0,             (I − AdC )S ∈ h + hC .             (1.1)

A symmetric/skew-symmetric degeneration proves this system generically injective. Arith-
metic parameters are then chosen in ﬁnitely many nonempty opens. A further complex
parameter, already inside the arithmetic orbit closure, is chosen outside countably many
resonance sets.
    Golseﬁdy–Tamam [4, Section 2.4, second remark] explicitly suggest extending their re-
sults to SLd and other Chevalley groups using Larsen–Pink. That extension is not proved
there. Our existence theorem is a special case of the anticipated broader higher-rank pic-
ture, rather than a claim that such a picture was unanticipated. The construction and
proof are new to this project; bounded searches found no higher-rank single-orbit integral
theorem, but do not establish worldwide novelty.
    The choice of point cannot be replaced by an unexamined high-rank dense-image asser-
tion. A MathOverﬂow comment by M. Kapovich reports non-dense mapping-class orbits of
Zariski-dense high-rank unitary representations [9]. We cite that as a discussion pointer, not
as a proved theorem used here. Independently, the actual paper of Biswas–Koberda–Mj–
Santharoubane [1, Proposition 1.2 and Section 5] constructs inﬁnite-image representations
whose GLn characters are ﬁxed by the entire mapping class group, using TQFT representa-
tions and point pushing. That is the precise published statement we use for comparison; it is
not the same as the stronger dense-image observation in the comment. Neither observation
is needed as a lemma in our proof.

We ﬁx the based-word convention, since the order of the factors matters both topologically

                                                    0 , c , d0 .                           (3.1)

Σg . A turn from a letter x to y is drawn as a chord joining half-edges x−1 and y in the
vertex disk.

                        {2, 8}, {4, 6};          {3, 4}, {2, 5}, {7, 8}, {1, 6}.

In each case the chords are noninterleaving. Each edge is used once, so these chords and

Proof. The one-by-one minors give Eii = 0. Set xij = Eij /Mij for i 6= j. The two-by-two

formula. Its diagonal term vanishes because Eii = 0; its transposition terms vanish by the
pair identities. The remaining two oriented cycles give

                     (Mij Mjk Mki − Mik Mkj Mji )(xij + xjk + xki ) = 0.

The ﬁrst factor is nonzero, so all oriented triangle sums vanish. Set h1 = 0 and hi = xi1
for i 6= 1. Antisymmetry and the triangle with reference index 1 give xij = hi − hj for
every i 6= j, including the cases involving index 1. Thus Eij = (hi − hj )Mij , including the
diagonal entries.

    This is an inﬁnitesimal form of classical principal-minor rigidity under diagonal sim-
ilarity. Loewy’s global theorem [7] and the pair-product and triangle-cycle identities of
Mantelos [8, Sections 3–4 and Lemma 6.2] are antecedents. We give the elementary proof
because its exact diﬀerential hypotheses are what is needed below; no new contribution is
claimed for this lemma alone. In rank three the three-by-three minor is the full determinant;
in both applications its derivative vanishes automatically.

Proposition 4.3. There is a nonempty Zariski open subset of T × SLr such that, for

them zero.

A.1    Limited algebraic formal support
A separate Lean artifact, t027o-formal-v1, supports a precisely bounded part of
Lemma 4.2. Its theorem triangle_coboundary, over an arbitrary ﬁeld and an in-
dex type with a chosen reference index, proves that a zero-diagonal antisymmetric
array with vanishing oriented triangle sums is of the form xij = hi − hj . Its theorem
principal_minor_rigidity assumes nonzero oﬀ-diagonal entries, zero diagonal variation,
the explicit pair stationarity polynomials, the six-term cycle derivative polynomials, and
unequal forward and reverse triangle products. It concludes Eij = (hi − hj )Mij .
    Those formal hypotheses are scalar polynomial identities. The pair identity becomes
the negative two-by-two minor derivative after the diagonal identities; the cycle identity be-
comes the three-by-three minor derivative only after the diagonal and pair identities. The
artifact does not prove that they are the derivatives of the corresponding determinant func-
tions. It does not formalize generic Cartan choices, Vandermonde interpolation, the surface
curves, the residual degeneration, arithmetic integrality, torus density, Goldman geometry,
the analytic ideal argument, or orbit density. It is not a formal proof of Theorem 1.1. Its
controls include a generic rational example and a counterexample to deleting the triangle-
genericity hypothesis. This counterexample shows that genericity cannot simply be deleted;
it does not prove necessity of every triple condition in larger index sets. An independent re-
view adds a formal derivative bridge and further controls in its own ﬁle, T027OReview.lean;
