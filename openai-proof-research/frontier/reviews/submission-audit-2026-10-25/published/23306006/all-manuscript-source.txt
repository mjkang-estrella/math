\begin{abstract}
Assuming the smooth variance and moment-coordinate identities specified
in this paper, we prove a power stability estimate for the logarithmic
Brunn--Minkowski inequality on origin-symmetric convex bodies. In each
dimension at least three, a small deficit forces the two bodies close
in Hausdorff distance to independent positive dilations of common
complementary factors. The exponent depends only on dimension; the
constant also depends on fixed inner and outer radii and on an interior
range of interpolation parameters. No boundary regularity, common
reflection group, facet bound, or lower bound on block angles is
required. The proof retains nonzero derivative energy in a compact-target
moment limit, converts a weak boundary identity to an absolute flux
estimate, and constructs one product body whose two dilations approximate
the original endpoints. The exponent is explicit but not sharp.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Write $B$ for the Euclidean unit ball, $\kappa_n=|B|$, and $h_K$ for the support function of
a convex body, and $|K|$ for its volume. For a positive continuous function
$f$ on $S^{n-1}$, let
\[
 \mathcal W[f]=\bigcap_{u\in S^{n-1}}\{x:\langle x,u\rangle\le f(u)\}.
\]
For origin-symmetric convex bodies $K,L$ with nonempty interior, put
\[
 C_t=\mathcal W[h_K^{1-t}h_L^t],\qquad
 \ell(t)=(1-t)\log|K|+t\log|L|,\qquad
 \Delta_\lambda=\log|C_\lambda|-\ell(\lambda).
\]
Let $\mathcal E$ be the set of pairs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality}
 \left(\sum_{i=1}^m D_i,\ \sum_{i=1}^m c_iD_i\right),\qquad
 \R^n=E_1\oplus\cdots\oplus E_m,\quad c_i>0,
\end{equation}
where the $E_i$ are nonzero complementary linear subspaces and each
$D_i\subset E_i$ is origin-symmetric with nonempty relative interior.
The subspaces need not be orthogonal. One factor is allowed. Define
\[
 d_{\mathcal E}(K,L)=\inf_{(K',L')\in\mathcal E}
                  \max\{d_H(K,K'),d_H(L,L')\}.
\]
No prescribed ball bounds are imposed on the comparison pair. We write
$b(u)=\log(h_L(u)/h_K(u))$ for the logarithmic support ratio.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:stability}
Assume the smooth inputs in Assumption~\ref{ass:input}. For each integer
$n\ge3$ there is a finite exponent $\alpha_n$, depending only on $n$,
such that the following holds. For every $0<r<R$ and
$0<\tau_0<1/2$ there is $c=c(n,r,R,\tau_0)>0$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stability}
 \Delta_\lambda\ge c\,d_{\mathcal E}(K,L)^{\alpha_n}
\end{equation}
for all origin-symmetric convex bodies with $rB\subset K,L\subset RB$
and all $\lambda\in[\tau_0,1-\tau_0]$.
\end{theorem}

The exponent may be taken to be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exponent}
 \theta_n=\frac{b_n}{8(a_n+b_n+1)},\qquad
 \alpha_n=\frac{8n(n+2)}{\theta_n},
\end{equation}
with the dimension-only constants $a_n,b_n$ specified in Section~4.
This choice is large and no optimality is claimed.

B\"or\"oczky and De \cite{BD} proved power stability under $n$ common
independent linear hyperplane symmetries. Their product reconstruction
uses those symmetries. The present conditional result treats arbitrary
origin-symmetric bodies. The smooth assumptions come from the pinned
logarithmic Brunn--Minkowski manuscript \cite{OpenAI}; they include
identities used in its proof, rather than only its final inequality.
We state them separately so that the dependence of
Theorem~\ref{thm:stability} is explicit.

Liu, Sun and Xiong \cite{LSX} retain the unnormalized tensor completion,
the positive regularizer and the derivative energy, and use moment
compactness and contact measures to study equality. We use these
operations with nonzero error and credit them throughout. Their proposed
centered nonsymmetric theorem is not an assumption here. The new
quantitative steps are the controlled weak limit, the boundary estimate,
the reconstruction of one compatible product, and its simultaneous
comparison with both original endpoints. Moment existence and
regularity, the Hessian bound, surface-area continuity and anisotropic
isoperimetric stability enter through the results cited below.

In the proof, $C_*$ denotes a constant depending only on
$n,r,R,\tau_0$, and $C_n,c_n$ denote positive dimensional constants.
Their values may change. The letter $dS$ on a boundary denotes
$\mathcal H^{n-1}$; $S_C$ is its pushforward under the outer normal map.
All assertions about outer normals are understood almost everywhere
with respect to boundary area. Matrix norms are Hilbert--Schmidt norms
unless stated otherwise.

\section{Smooth assumptions and the retained completion}
For $\varepsilon>0$, an even integer $q\ge2$, nonzero vectors $p_i$ and
positive numbers $h_i$, put
\[
 \psi_i(x)=\left(\frac{p_i\cdot x}{h_i}\right)^q,
 \quad V(x)=\frac\varepsilon2|x|^2+\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i(x),
 \quad d\mu=Z^{-1}e^{-V}dx.
\]
The Gaussian term makes all polynomial moments finite, even if the
vectors do not span. The hypotheses used below are the following
smooth statements.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:input}
For every potential just described, the following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For all real coefficients $\beta_i$,
\[
 \Var_\mu\left(\sum_i\beta_i\psi_i\right)
                  \le\sum_i\beta_i^2\E_\mu\psi_i.
\]
\item There is a smooth even convex function $\varphi$ on $\R^n$,
with $\int e^{-\varphi}=1$, such that $x=\nabla\varphi(z)$ is a
diffeomorphism onto $\R^n$ pushing $d\nu=e^{-\varphi}dz$ to $\mu$.
Writing $\tau=D_z^2\varphi$ and $M=\tau^{-1}$, one has
\[
 0<\tau\preceq(4/\varepsilon)I,
 \qquad \det\tau=Z\exp\{V(\nabla\varphi)-\varphi\}.
\]
\item For $v\in C_c^\infty(\R_x^n)$ set
$P=\tau D_x^2v$, $B_v=P\tau$, $W=Px-\operatorname{div}_zP$ and
$u=\delta_\mu W$, where
$\delta_\mu W=\nabla V\cdot W-\operatorname{div}_xW$.
Then $B_v,D_zW$ are symmetric, and the adjunctions are
\[
 \E_\mu ug=\E_\mu W\cdot\nabla g
              =\E_\mu\tr(B_vD_x^2g),\qquad
 \E_\mu x\cdot\nabla g=\E_\mu\tr(\tau D_x^2g).
\]
The first formula holds for compact smooth tests and for the
polynomials used below; the second holds with polynomial cutoffs.
As $v$ ranges over even compact smooth functions, $u$ is dense in the
centered even subspace of $L^2(\mu)$.
\item Put $H=D_x^2V$ and $\mathcal R=D_zW-B_v$. The tensor and Bochner
identities give
\begin{align*}
 \E_\mu u^2&=\E_\mu W^THW+\E_\mu\tr((D_xW)^2),\\
 \E_\mu\tr((D_xW)^2)-\E_\mu\tr(MB_vHB_v)
 &\ge\int\left(\|M^{1/2}\mathcal RM^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2
                    +\tfrac12\mathscr D(P)\right)d\nu,
\end{align*}
where
\[
 \mathscr D(P)=\sum_{a,b,k,r,\ell,s}
 M_{ab}\tau_{kr}M_{\ell s}(\partial_\ell P_{ak})(\partial_sP_{br}).
\]
The matrix square in the first trace is not a Hilbert--Schmidt square.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

The variance bound is Proposition~2.1 of \cite{OpenAI}. The moment,
adjunction and density statements are its moment-coordinate lemma,
compact Hessian identities and density lemma, in the source files
\texttt{moment.tex}, \texttt{identities.tex} and \texttt{density.tex}.
The final inequality is the retained form of the calculation in
\texttt{tensor.tex}. In a constant frame with $\tau=I$, that calculation
has remainder
\[
 \|M^{1/2}\mathcal RM^{1/2}\|^2+
 2|S+\Sym J|^2+\tfrac16|J-J^{(12)}|^2,
\]
where $S=D_x^3v$ and $J_{ijk}=(D_x^2v)_{ia}\varphi_{ajk}$.
For $K_{ak\ell}=\partial_\ell P_{ak}=S_{ak\ell}+J_{ka\ell}$,
the last two terms equal
$\tfrac12|K|^2+\tfrac32|\Sym K|^2$.
This explains the derivative contraction in the assumption. The same
completion and contraction are displayed in \cite[(3.10)--(3.16)]{LSX}.

For later use let $F=\sum_i\beta_i\psi_i$, $U=F-\E F$,
$\gamma_i=\beta_i/q$, $H_i=D_x^2\psi_i$, and
$D=\sum_i\beta_i^2\E\psi_i-\|U\|_2^2$. Homogeneity and adjunction give
\[
 \E\tr(\tau H_i)=q\E\psi_i,\quad
 \E\tr(B_vH_i)=\E u\psi_i,\quad
 \E x^TH_ix=q(q-1)\E\psi_i,\quad
 \E x^TH_iW=(q-1)\E u\psi_i.
\]
Completing the two squares therefore yields
\begin{align*}
 D+\|u-U\|_2^2={}&T(v)+\varepsilon\E\{\tr(MB_v^2)+|W|^2\}\\
 &+\sum_i\E\tr\{M(B_v-\gamma_i\tau)H_i(B_v-\gamma_i\tau)\}\\
 &+\sum_i\E(W-\gamma_ix)^TH_i(W-\gamma_ix),
\end{align*}
where $T(v)=\E\tr((D_xW)^2)-\E\tr(MB_vHB_v)$.
All terms on the right are nonnegative, and both remainder energies
in Assumption~\ref{ass:input} are retained simultaneously.

The variance bound makes the logarithm of each soft slab integral
concave along geometric interpolation of its widths. Letting $q$ tend
to infinity, then $\varepsilon$ to zero, and exhausting sphere
directions gives the logarithmic Brunn--Minkowski inequality for
symmetric bodies, as in \cite[section 2]{OpenAI}. It also makes
$t\mapsto\log|C_t|$ concave. Indeed, the Wulff interpolation of
$C_s,C_t$ is contained in $C_{(1-a)s+at}$, since their supports are
bounded by the defining interpolated widths. Applying the inequality
to $C_s,C_t$ proves the concavity.
\section{Moment fields at positive deficit}

\subsection{Approximation and time selection}

We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}. Put $\delta=\Delta_\lambda$ and initially use the original Euclidean coordinates. It suffices to treat \(0<\delta<\delta_*\), where the threshold depends only on the fixed parameters. Put \(\varepsilon=\delta\), further restricting \(\delta_*R^2\le1\).

Take nested symmetric direction nets with mesh \(a_j\downarrow0\), containing a fixed spanning net, and \(N_j\le C_na_j^{-(n-1)}\). For representative directions put
\[
h_i(t)=h_K(p_i)^{1-t}h_L(p_i)^t,\quad
b_i=\log(h_L(p_i)/h_K(p_i)),\quad
U_j(x,t)=\sum_i|p_i\cdot x/h_i(t)|^{q_j}.
\]
Choose even \(q_j\to\infty\) with
\[
a_j\le\delta/j,\qquad
(1+\log N_j)/q_j\le\delta/j,\qquad
q_j\ge j(1+\log N_j).
                                                                    \tag{3.1}
\]
These requirements are compatible. For example \(a_j=c_*\delta/(j+1)\) and an even \(q_j\ge C_*(j+1)^2(1+\log N_j)/\delta\) suffice. 

Let $\mathcal P_j(t)=\{x:|p_i\cdot x|\le h_i(t)\text{ for every }i\}$.
For these directions and widths, write $Z_{q,\varepsilon}(t)$ for the
integral with exponent $q$, and put
$G_j(t)=\log Z_{q_j,\varepsilon}(t)$.
Homogeneity gives
\[
|\log Z_{q,\varepsilon}(t)-\log|\mathcal P_j(t)||\le
C_*\varepsilon+C_n(1+\log N_j)/q.
\]
Taking $q=q_j$ and $N=N_j$, the identities used here are \(N^{-1/q}\mathcal P_j\subset\{U_j\le1\}\subset\mathcal P_j\),
\(\int e^{-U_j}=\Gamma(1+n/q)|\{U_j\le1\}|\), and a uniform second moment. The direction-net Wulff error is \(C_*a_j\). Concavity of the Wulff functional gives \(0\le\log|C_t|-\ell(t)\le\delta/\tau_0\). Hence
\[
|G_j(t)-\ell(t)|\le C_*\delta
\]
uniformly. Concavity and interior secants select \(t_j\in[1/3,2/3]\) with
\[
D_j=-G_j''(t_j)\le C_*\delta,\qquad
|G_j'(t_j)-\ell'|\le C_*\delta.                       \tag{3.2}
\]
No regularity or chamber selection for the Wulff path is needed.

At $t_j$, let $\mu_j$ be the normalized measure with density
$\exp[-U_j(x,t_j)-\varepsilon|x|^2/2]$, and let
$\nu_j=e^{-\varphi_j}dz$ be its source probability in moment coordinates
from Assumption~\ref{ass:input}. Put
$\psi_{i,j}(x)=|p_i\cdot x/h_i(t_j)|^{q_j}$ and take
\[
F_j=-q_j\sum_i b_i\psi_{i,j},\quad m_j=E_{\mu_j}F_j=-G_j'(t_j),
\quad U_j^{\rm score}=F_j-m_j.
\]
Parity removes the linear projection, not \(m_j\). Let $u_j=\delta_{\mu_j}W_j$ for the compact test fields defined in Assumption~\ref{ass:input}. Choose an even compact test \(v_j\) from the dense range in Assumption~\ref{ass:input} so that
\(\|u_j-U_j^{\rm score}\|_{L^2(\mu_j)}\le\eta_j\), with \(\eta_j\downarrow0\) and \(\eta_j^2\le\delta/j\).
There is no bound on the norm of \(v_j\), and none is required.

Write \(x_j=\nabla\varphi_j,\ \tau_j=D^2\varphi_j,\ M_j=\tau_j^{-1}\), and
\[
P_j=\tau_jD_x^2v_j,\quad B_j=P_j\tau_j,\quad
W_j=P_jx_j-\operatorname{div}_zP_j,\quad
R_j=D_zW_j-P_j\tau_j.
\]
The completion in Section~2 gives
\[
\int\left(\|M_j^{1/2}R_jM_j^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2+
\tfrac12\mathscr D_j(P_j)\right)d\nu_j
+\varepsilon\int|W_j|^2d\nu_j
\le D_j+\eta_j^2\le C_*\delta.                       \tag{3.3}
\]
Here
\[
\mathscr D(P)=M_{ab}\tau_{kr}M_{\ell s}
(\partial_\ell P_{ak})(\partial_sP_{br}).
\]
Both \(P_j\tau_j\) and \(D_zW_j\) are symmetric. This retains the \(R_j\) square as well as the derivative term. In particular \(\int|W_j|^2d\nu_j\le C_*\).

\subsection{Convergence of moment coordinates}

Pass to \(t_j\to t\in[1/3,2/3]\), and write \(C=C_t\). The limiting target is
\[
d\mu(x)=\rho(x)dx=Z^{-1}1_C(x)e^{-\varepsilon|x|^2/2}dx.                \tag{3.4}
\]
On every compact subset of \(\operatorname{int}C\), the slab ratios are at most some \(\zeta<1\). Every fixed derivative of the soft part is bounded by \(C_kN_jq_j^k\zeta^{q_j-k}\to0\). Outside \(C\), a fixed direction from the dense union eventually gives a strict violated constraint. The fixed spanning net supplies a common integrable superlinear tail bound, including every fixed polynomial weight. Thus the target densities converge in weighted \(L^1\), in \(W_1\), and in every fixed polynomial moment; \(Z_j\to Z\).

Berman--Berndtsson \cite[Theorem 1.1]{BB} applies to \(C\) and its positive globally smooth Gaussian density. It gives a probability-normalized smooth strictly convex \(\varphi\) on all \(\mathbb R_z^n\), with gradient diffeomorphism onto \(\operatorname{int}C\). Choose the unique minimum at zero; symmetry and uniqueness make \(\varphi\) even.

We verify convergence for the quadratic tilt. The linear-tilt case is treated in \cite[Lemma 3.4]{LSX}. Bonnet--Rubinstein \cite[Theorem 1.3]{BR} applies because the probabilities are centered, their first moments are bounded, and the limiting directional first moments are bounded below by the density on \(rB\). After translations it gives \(L^1\) source convergence. Cule--Samworth \cite[Proposition 2]{CS} gives local uniform density convergence, and convex difference quotients give local \(C^1\) convergence of potentials. The translations can be replaced by minimum-at-zero normalization: for the translated potentials, uniform convergence to a strictly convex potential on a ball places their minimizers inside that ball (use a positive tangent-plane gap on its boundary), and their minimizers tend to the unique limiting minimizer. Recentering therefore preserves the convergence.

On a fixed source ball, its limiting gradient image is compactly inside \(C\); the nearby images stay in a common target compact. The equations
\[
\det D^2\varphi_j=Z_j\exp[-\varphi_j+V_j(\nabla\varphi_j)]
\]
give positive two-sided determinant bounds there. The strict convex limit supplies nested compact sections and a common separation modulus at each positive scale:
\[
\varphi_j(w)-\varphi_j(z)-\nabla\varphi_j(z)\cdot(w-z)
\ge \tfrac m2|w-z|^2-2e_j|w-z|,\qquad e_j\to0.
\]
Interior section estimates give \(C^{1,\beta}\); composition in the equation gives a H\"older right side; Figalli--Jhaveri--Mooney \cite[Theorem 1.1]{FJM} gives \(C^{2,\beta}\). The determinant lower bound then gives local ellipticity, and differentiation plus Schauder estimates gives every fixed higher order. All derivatives of \(V_j\) on those target compacts converge to those of the fixed quadratic tilt, so the same bootstrap applies. Consequently
\[
\varphi_j\longrightarrow\varphi\quad\hbox{in every fixed }C^k_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^n).
                                                                    \tag{3.5}
\]
The inverse maps converge on compact subsets of \(\operatorname{int}C\).

Constants in this existence argument can depend on the fixed \(\delta,C,\varepsilon\) and core. They do not enter the final rate: after passage, all estimates used for the rate are rederived from the compact-target Hessian bound and ball bounds. No rate of (3.5), and no finite-\(q\) radius-only cap, is asserted.

\subsection{Weak compactness of the fields}

On every source ball the convergent coefficients and density are uniformly elliptic and positive. Equation (3.3) bounds \(D_zP_j\) in local \(L^2\). To bound its constant part, set \(A_j=\fint_{B_1}P_j\). Poincar\'e and the formula for \(W_j\) imply
\[
\|W_j-A_jx_j\|_{L^2(B_1)}
\le C\|D_zP_j\|_{L^2(B_1)}.
\]
The matrix \(\int_{B_1}x_jx_j^Tdz\) tends to a positive definite matrix, because \(\nabla\varphi(B_1)\) is open. The \(L^2\) bound on \(W_j\) therefore bounds \(A_j\). Comparing averages on larger balls bounds \(P_j\) in \(H^1_{\rm loc}\).

The retained \(R_j\) square now bounds \(D_zW_j=R_j+P_j\tau_j\) locally in \(L^2\). Diagonal Rellich compactness gives fields
\[
P,W\in H^1_{\rm loc}(\mathbb R^n_z),\qquad
P_j,W_j\to P,W\ \hbox{strongly in }L^2_{\rm loc},
\]
with weak convergence of their first derivatives. These are fields on the whole source space. In target coordinates \(W(x)=W((\nabla\varphi)^{-1}x)\) is defined on \(\operatorname{int}C\). Smooth coefficient convergence gives, distributionally,
\[
W=P\nabla\varphi-\operatorname{div}_zP,\quad
P\tau=\tau P^T,\quad D_zW=(D_zW)^T,\quad R=D_zW-P\tau.                 \tag{3.6}
\]
They inherit even \(P\) and odd \(W\). In particular \(W\) is a local Sobolev gradient in source coordinates. The argument does not identify it with a gradient in target coordinates.

For every fixed ball, quadratic lower semicontinuity with locally uniformly converging coefficients passes (3.3). Exhaustion by increasing balls yields
\[
\int\left(\|M^{1/2}RM^{1/2}\|_{\rm HS}^2+
\tfrac12\mathscr D(P)\right)d\nu\le C_*\delta,\qquad
\int|W|^2d\nu\le C_*.                                                \tag{3.7}
\]
The same reasoning retains any other nonnegative bulk completion term that has a defined limiting coefficient. No convergence of the divergent scores in bulk \(L^2\) is claimed.

Density completion precedes each limit, with error tending to zero; time selection precedes subselection; weighted lower semicontinuity follows coefficient convergence on each fixed core. None of these operations requires a bounded inverse for the dense range.

\subsection{The boundary identity}

Split each even power into its two signed one-sided powers solely for measure bookkeeping, and put
\[
\Lambda_j(dx,du)=\sum_iq_j\psi_{i,j}(x)\mu_j(dx)\delta_{p_{i,j}}(du).
\]
Radial integration gives
\[
\Lambda_j(1)=n-\varepsilon E_{\mu_j}|x|^2,\qquad
\Lambda_j(|x|^2)=(n+2)E_{\mu_j}|x|^2-\varepsilon E_{\mu_j}|x|^4.
\]
Hence the positive measures are tight. For smooth compact \(g(x)\),
\[
\int g\,d\Lambda_j
=\int(ng+x\cdot\nabla g-\varepsilon |x|^2g)d\mu_j
\longrightarrow \int_{\partial C}g\,h_C(\nu_C)\rho\,d\mathcal H^{n-1}.
\]
As in the contact-measure argument of \cite[section 3.3]{LSX}, any compact set with slack at least \(s>0\) has mass bounded by \(C N_jq_j(1-s/2)^{q_j}\to0\). All joint limits therefore lie on contact pairs. The position marginal is absolutely continuous relative to boundary area, and the normal is unique there almost everywhere. Thus
\[
\Lambda_j\rightharpoonup
h_C(\nu_C(x))\rho(x)d\mathcal H^{n-1}(x)\delta_{\nu_C(x)}(du).
\]
We may now multiply this positive measure limit by the continuous function $b$.

Subselect \(m_j\to m\), with \(|m+\ell'|\le C_*\delta\) by (3.2). For a compact subset of $\operatorname{int}C$, the inverse moment
maps have images in a common source compact. Approximate the limiting
fields there by smooth functions. The strong local $L^2$ convergence,
convergence of the maps, and uniform bounds on their Jacobians pass
the adjunction on that compact. The complementary target layer is bounded by
\[
C_*\|\nabla g\|_\infty\,\mu_j(\mathbb R^n\setminus C_s)^{1/2},
\qquad C_s=\{x\in C:\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial C)\ge s\}.
\]
First let \(j\to\infty\), then \(s\downarrow0\). This is zero, since \(\partial C\) has zero volume. The dense-test error is at most \(\eta_j\|g\|_2\). The resulting exact identity is
\[
\boxed{\ \int_C W\cdot\nabla g\,d\mu
=-\int_{\partial C}g\,b(\nu_C)h_C(\nu_C)\rho\,d\mathcal H^{n-1}
-m\int_Cg\,d\mu.\ }                                                   \tag{3.8}
\]
Here \(m\), not zero, is the limiting mean score. Taking a cutoff equal to one on \(C\) also verifies \(m=-\int_{\partial C}b h_C\rho\,dS\). No arbitrary direction-dependent test was inserted into radial integration.

\section{Quantitative coordinates and a constant matrix}

\subsection{Dimension-only core and tail exponents}

First apply a linear map sending the John ellipsoid of the selected \(C\) to \(B\). Then
\[
B\subset \widehat C\subset\sqrt n B.
\]
The operator norms of this map and its inverse are bounded in terms of the original \(r,R,n\). Writing $J$ for the inverse of the John normalization map, the transformed Gaussian is \(Q(x)=\varepsilon|Jx|^2/2\), with \(0\le Q\le1/2\) on \(\widehat C\). Thus its normalized density is between \(e^{-1/2}/|\widehat C|\) and \(e^{1/2}/|\widehat C|\). For a constant map $x'=Tx$, use $z=T^Tz'$ and
$\varphi'(z')=\varphi(T^Tz')-\log|\det T|$. Then
\[
 \tau'=T\tau T^T,\quad M'=T^{-T}MT^{-1},\quad
 P'=TPT^{-1},\quad W'=TW,\quad R'=TRT^T.
\]
The derivative tensor transforms as
$\partial_{z'_\ell}P'_{ak}=T_{ai}(T^{-1})_{jk}T_{\ell m}\partial_{z_m}P_{ij}$.
Substitution in $\mathscr D$ and $\tr(MRMR)$ cancels the coordinate
factors, so their integrals are unchanged. The $W$ norm changes by a
parameter-bounded factor. The boundary identity is covariant as well:
$u'=T^{-T}u/|T^{-T}u|$, while the boundary area changes by
$|\det T|\,|T^{-T}u|$ and the density by $|\det T|^{-1}$.
These factors cancel those in the transformed supports and flux.

This normalization is performed on the already existing limiting fields. Without it, balancing \(\exp(C(r,R)L)\) against \(\exp(-c(r,R)L)\) would give an aspect-ratio-dependent exponent, which would not prove the stated \(\alpha_n\) assertion.

For a normalized target \(B\subset C\subset\sqrt n B\), the source potential satisfies
\[
|\nabla\varphi|\le\sqrt n,\qquad
0<\tau\le2nI,\qquad
c_n|z|-C_n\le\varphi(z)\le C_n+\sqrt n|z|.             \tag{4.1}
\]
The cap is Klartag's compact-target bound \cite[Theorem 1]{Klartag}. A possible directional coercivity coefficient before reducing it is
\[
c_n^{(0)}=
\frac{e^{-1/2}}{\kappa_n n^{n/2}}\int_{B}|x_1|dx>0.
\]
Indeed the supporting planes at \(z,-z\), integrated against the normalized source, give
\(\varphi(y)-\varphi(0)\ge\int|x\cdot y|d\mu-n\).
The identity \(E[z\cdot\nabla\varphi]=n\) is justified first using any preliminary coercive bound from slopes in an inner ball. Normalization of \(\int e^{-\varphi}\) and the upper Lipschitz bound then bound \(\varphi(0)\) by dimensional constants.

The equation \(\det\tau=Z e^{-\varphi+Q(\nabla\varphi)}\), the cap and (4.1) give
\[
\tau\ge c_n e^{-C_nL}I,\quad e^{-\varphi}\ge c_ne^{-C_nL}
\quad\hbox{on }B_L,\qquad
\nu(B_L^c)\le C_n(1+L)^{n-1}e^{-c_nL}.                \tag{4.2}
\]
All constants in these exponents are dimensional; the bounded Gaussian on the target is sufficient, even after linear transformations.

Here is an explicit choice of the constants in \eqref{eq:exponent}. Write
\(I_n(c)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-c|z|}dz=n!\kappa_n/c^n\), take \(c_0=c_n^{(0)}\), and put
\[
h_*=\min\!\left\{1,\,
\frac{e^{-1/2}\kappa_n e^{-\sqrt n}}
{e^n I_n(c_0)(2n)^{\,n-1}}\right\},\quad
r_n=(2n)^{-1/2},\quad R_n=\sqrt{n/h_*},
\]
\[
c_1=\frac{e^{-1/2}}{\kappa_nR_n^n}
          \int_{r_nB}|x_1|dx,\qquad
a_n=10(n+1)(R_n+1),\qquad
b_n=\min\{1,c_1/(8n)\}.                               \tag{4.2a}
\]
Indeed \(\varphi(0)\le\log(e^nI_n(c_0))\) before the \(H\) transformation, so \(\tau\ge h_*I\) on \(B_1\), and \(h_*I\le H\le2nI\). In the final frame the body lies between \(r_nB,R_nB\), and its coercivity coefficient is at least \(c_1\). In the John frame, the derivative bound on $B_T$ grows at most as $e^{\sqrt n T}$, times a polynomial of degree at most $n/2+1$. A cube $[-L,L]^n$ in the final frame pulls back into $B_{R_nL}$. Since $10(n+1)(R_n+1)>\sqrt n R_n+n/2+3$, the displayed $a_n$ bounds the resulting exponential and polynomial factors. The displayed \(b_n\) absorbs the tail polynomial, its square root and its cap \(1/n\) power. Enlarging multiplicative dimensional constants handles \(L\ge2\). 

\subsection{Approximation by a constant matrix}

From (3.7), \(\mathscr D(P)\ge\lambda_{\min}(\tau)\lambda_{\max}(\tau)^{-2}|D_zP|^2\), and (4.2),
\[
\|D_zP\|_{L^2(B_L)}\le C_*e^{C_nL}\sqrt\delta.
\]
Set \(A=\fint_{B_1}P\). Poincar\'e and (3.6) give
\[
|A|\le C_*,\qquad
\|P-A\|_{L^2(B_L)}+
\|W-A\nabla\varphi\|_{L^2(B_L)}
\le C_*(1+L)^{n/2+1}e^{C_nL}\sqrt\delta.              \tag{4.3}
\]
To verify the bound on \(A\), (4.2) on \(B_1\) makes \(\nabla\varphi\) uniformly strongly monotone there; its image contains a dimensional ball, and the Hessian upper bound controls the Jacobian. Thus \(\int_{B_1}\nabla\varphi\nabla\varphi^Tdz\ge c_nI\). This and the global \(W\) bound control \(A\). Comparing averages on \(B_1\) and \(B_L\) controls the displayed polynomial factor.

Put \(H=\fint_{B_1}\tau\). Its eigenvalues lie in a dimensional compact interval. From \(P\tau=\tau P^T\),
\[
|AH-HA^T|\le C_*\sqrt\delta.
\]
In the constant dual frame \(x'=H^{-1/2}x\), replace \(H^{-1/2}AH^{1/2}\) by its symmetric part \(S\). This changes it by \(C_*\sqrt\delta\). The change of frame has dimensional condition number. The target still lies between dimensional balls, (4.1)--(4.2) hold with new dimensional constants, and
\[
|S|\le C_*,\qquad
\|S\tau-\tau S\|_{L^2(B_L)}
+\|W-S\nabla\varphi\|_{L^2(B_L)}
\le C_*e^{a_nL}\sqrt\delta.                          \tag{4.4}
\]
The values in (4.2a) suffice for these estimates on balls and on the cubes below.
The final-frame tail satisfies
$\nu(B_L^c)\le C_n(1+L)^{n-1}e^{-c_1L}$.
The choice $b_n\le c_1/(8n)$ bounds this tail, its square root and
its $1/n$ power by $C_ne^{-b_nL}$ after absorbing the polynomial.
From (4.4), the global \(W\) bound and the tail,
\[
\int_C|W-Sx|\,d\mu
\le C_*\left(e^{a_nL}\sqrt\delta+e^{-b_nL}\right).       \tag{4.5}
\]
The tail of \(Sx\) is harmless because \(C\) and \(S\) are bounded. 

\subsection{Spectral clusters and the trace}

Diagonalize \(S\). Merge consecutive eigenvalues whenever their gap is smaller than \(\gamma>0\). There are at most \(n\) clusters; each has diameter at most \((n-1)\gamma\), and distinct clusters have all eigenvalue gaps at least \(\gamma\). Let \(E_i\) be their orthogonal sums, and let \(A_\gamma\) equal a chosen eigenvalue \(a_i\) on \(E_i\). Then
\[
\|S-A_\gamma\|\le n\gamma,\quad |a_i|\le C_*,
\qquad
\|\Pi_i\tau\Pi_j\|_{L^2(B_L)}
\le C_*\gamma^{-1}e^{a_nL}\sqrt\delta\quad(i\ne j).     \tag{4.6}
\]
The last estimate is entrywise division of \([S,\tau]\) by the actual spectral gaps. There is no division by a within-cluster gap.

Set \(e=\delta^{\theta_n}\) and
\[
L=\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{8(a_n+b_n+1)},\qquad \gamma=e.
\]
For sufficiently small deficit \(L\ge2\). Equation~\eqref{eq:exponent} gives
\[
e^{a_nL}\sqrt\delta\le\delta^{3/8}\le e^2,\qquad
e^{-b_nL}=e.
\]
Consequently
\[
\int_C|W-A_\gamma x|d\mu\le C_*e.                    \tag{4.7}
\]
Test (3.8) against one fixed nonnegative smooth function supported in an inner dimensional ball. Weighted integration by parts for \(A_\gamma x\) has bulk coefficient
\(-\operatorname{tr}A_\gamma+(A_\gamma x)\cdot\nabla Q\).
The latter Gaussian term is \(O_*(\delta)\). It follows that
\[
|\operatorname{tr}A_\gamma-m|\le C_*e,\qquad
|\operatorname{tr}A_\gamma+\ell'|\le C_*e.             \tag{4.8}
\]
Subtracting this bulk identity from (3.8), for arbitrary smooth compact \(g\), gives
\[
\left|\int_{\partial C}g\,
\{A_\gamma x\cdot\nu+b(\nu)h_C(\nu)\}\rho\,dS\right|
\le C_*e(\|g\|_\infty+\|\nabla g\|_\infty).           \tag{4.9}
\]
The nonzero chord slope and Gaussian term have both been retained.

\section{Boundary flux and surface-area continuity}

The following lemma is independent of moment coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[Radial boundary flux]
Suppose \(r_1B\subset C\subset R_1B\), \(F(x,u)\) is bounded and Lipschitz on a fixed neighborhood of \(\partial C\times S^{n-1}\), and \(w\) is a boundary weight bounded above and below by positive constants. If
\[
\left|\int_{\partial C}g(x)F(x,\nu_C(x))w(x)dS(x)\right|
\le\eta(\|g\|_\infty+\|\nabla g\|_\infty)
\]
for all compact smooth \(g\), then
\[
\int_{\partial C}|F(x,\nu_C(x))|dS(x)\le C\eta^{1/4}                 \tag{5.1}
\]
for \(0<\eta\le1\). Constants depend only on the indicated bounds, dimension and the Lipschitz bound of \(F\). No derivative of the normal map is assumed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof} Let \(p=p_C=h_{C^\circ}\) be the gauge restricted to the sphere. It is bounded above and below and is uniformly Lipschitz. Its spherical distributional Hessian satisfies
\(D_S^2p+pI\ge0\). The total trace of this positive matrix measure is
\((n-1)\int_Sp\), since the spherical Laplacian integrates to zero. Hence \(D_S^2p\) has uniformly bounded total variation.

Write \(X(\theta)=\theta/p(\theta)\). This radial parameterization is uniformly bi-Lipschitz onto \(\partial C\), and almost everywhere
\[
N(\theta)=\nu_C(X(\theta))
=\frac{p(\theta)\theta+\nabla_Sp(\theta)}
       {|p(\theta)\theta+\nabla_Sp(\theta)|}.
\]
The denominator is at least \(\min p>0\). Thus \(N\) is a uniformly bounded BV function on the sphere. One can justify the chain rule directly by smoothing support functions in spherical charts or by rotation convolution: the positive Hessian-measure bound controls the derivatives of the smooth approximants, and lower semicontinuity gives the assertion. The radial area Jacobian
\[
J(\theta)=p(\theta)^{-n}|\nabla p_C(\theta)|
\]
is bounded above and below. Here $\nabla p_C$ is the full Euclidean gradient of the homogeneous gauge.

Therefore \(f(\theta)=F(X(\theta),N(\theta))\) has uniformly bounded BV norm and supremum. A finite chart partition and convolution at scale \(s\) give smooth \(f_s\) with
\[
\|f_s\|_\infty\le C,\quad \operatorname{Lip}(f_s)\le C/s,\quad
\int_S|f-f_s|\le Cs.
\]
The \(L^1\) estimate follows by integrating the derivative along translations for smooth approximants and passing to BV limits; the Lipschitz estimate is the \(L^1\) norm of the differentiated convolution kernel. These constants depend on the fixed sphere charts, not on facets.

Take \(g_s(x)=\chi(|x|)f_s(x/|x|)\), where \(\chi\) is a fixed compact smooth cutoff equal to one on \([r_1,R_1]\) and vanishing near zero. Then \(g_s(X(\theta))=f_s(\theta)\), and its test norm is at most \(C/s\). The area-Jacobian and weight bounds imply
\[
\int_{\partial C}F^2w\,dS
\le\left|\int_{\partial C}Fg_sw\,dS\right|+Cs
\le C(\eta/s+s).
\]
Choose \(s=\sqrt\eta\) and apply Cauchy--Schwarz on the uniformly bounded boundary area. This proves (5.1). Facet edges and nonsmooth contacts cause no loss beyond the BV bound. \end{proof}

Apply the lemma to
\[
F(x,u)=A_\gamma x\cdot u+b(u)h_C(u),\qquad w=\rho.
\]
The transformed endpoint supports are uniformly positive and Lipschitz, so \(b\) has a uniform \(C_*\) Lipschitz bound; \(h_C\) does too. Equations (4.9) and (5.1) give
\[
\int_{\partial C}|A_\gamma x\cdot\nu+b(\nu)h_C(\nu)|dS
\le C_*e^{1/4}.                                       \tag{5.2}
\]


We will also use
\[
d_{\rm BL}(S_C,S_Q)\le C_*d_H(C,Q)^{1/2}.               \tag{5.3}
\]
This is exactly Hug--Schneider \cite[(1.2)]{HS}. Here is an independent verification in the ball-bounded setting. For the two homogeneous gauges \(p,q\), Hausdorff error \(\zeta\) gives \(\|p-q\|_\infty\le C\zeta\) on a fixed annulus. With a cutoff equal to one on a smaller annulus, integration by parts and the positive Hessian measures give
\[
\int\chi|\nabla p-\nabla q|^2
=-\int(p-q)\{\chi\Delta(p-q)+\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla(p-q)\}
\le C\zeta.
\]
Each Laplacian measure has bounded mass there, by convexity and bounded gradients. Smooth approximation justifies the identity. Homogeneity transfers the estimate to the sphere. The normal and radial area-Jacobian formulas above are Lipschitz functions of \(p,\nabla p\) on their bounded range with positive denominator. Thus their \(L^1\) differences are \(O(\sqrt\zeta)\); testing a bounded Lipschitz function of the normal proves (5.3). This also clarifies why no total-variation convergence of the spherical measures is asserted.

\section{Construction of a common product body}

Use the orthogonal block coordinates of \(A_\gamma\), and take a cube \(Q_L=[-L,L]^n\) in the corresponding orthonormal basis. The definition of \(a_n\) in (4.4) includes replacement of \(B_L\) by \(B_{\sqrt n L}\).

For each block let
\[
F_i(z_i)=\fint_{[-L,L]^{n-\dim E_i}}
\Pi_i\nabla\varphi(z_i,z_{-i})\,dz_{-i},\qquad F=(F_1,\ldots,F_m).
\]
When there is one block, the transverse average is interpreted as the identity. Each \(F_i\) is a gradient of an averaged convex function, is Lipschitz with dimensional bound from the compact-target Hessian bound, and is odd: use the global evenness of \(\varphi\) and the symmetric averaging domain.

Poincar\'e only in the transverse variables, followed by integration in \(z_i\), yields
\[
\|\nabla\varphi-F\|_{L^2(Q_L)}
\le C_nL\left(\sum_{i\ne j}\|\Pi_i\tau\Pi_j\|_2^2\right)^{1/2}
\le C_*L\gamma^{-1}e^{a_nL}\sqrt\delta
\le C_*Le.                                             \tag{6.1}
\]
There is no restriction of an \(L^2\) Hessian to a coordinate line in this argument.

Both functions in (6.1) have uniformly bounded Lipschitz constants. If a Lipschitz function of bound \(M\) has maximum \(s\) on a cube, a ball of radius \(s/(2M)\), intersected with that cube, has volume at least a dimensional multiple of \((s/M)^n\), and its values are at least \(s/2\). This gives the usual \(L^2\)-to-sup estimate with exponent \(2/(n+2)\), including points on the cube boundary. Consequently
\[
\|\nabla\varphi-F\|_{L^\infty(Q_L)}
\le C_*(Le)^{2/(n+2)}
\le C_* e^{1/(n+2)}.                                   \tag{6.2}
\]
The last weakening uses \(L\le C_ne^{-1/2}\) for sufficiently small deficit, where here \(e=\delta^{\theta_n}\); the constant is dimensional.

Define relative convex bodies
\[
D_i=\operatorname{conv}\overline{F_i([-L,L]^{\dim E_i})},\qquad
D=\sum_iD_i.
\]
The Cartesian product identity
\(\operatorname{conv}\prod_i X_i=\prod_i\operatorname{conv}X_i\)
follows by taking products of the finite convex-combination coefficients. Thus (6.2) implies
\[
d_H\!\left(D,\operatorname{conv}\nabla\varphi(Q_L)\right)
\le C_* e^{1/(n+2)}.                                    \tag{6.3}
\]
All these sets are bounded and symmetric.

It remains to cover the part of \(C\) outside the core image. Let
\(C^{\rm core}=\operatorname{conv}\overline{\nabla\varphi(Q_L)}\subset C\).
The target density is bounded below on \(C\), and
\[
|C\setminus C^{\rm core}|
\le C_*\mu(C\setminus\nabla\varphi(Q_L))
=C_*\nu(\mathbb R^n\setminus Q_L)\le C_ne^{-c_nL}.
\]
The gradient image of \(B_1\) contains a dimensional ball, so \(C^{\rm core}\) has a uniform inner ball. If a convex body between fixed balls has a point with support excess \(s\) above a contained convex body, joining that point to the inner \((n-1)\)-disk through zero creates a cap of volume at least \(c_ns^n\). Applying this here gives
\[
d_H(C,C^{\rm core})\le C_ne^{-b_nL}\le C_ne.
\]
Combine with (6.3) and weaken the exponent once more:
\[
\boxed{\ d_H(C,D)\le C_*e^{1/(2(n+2))}=:\zeta.\ }       \tag{6.4}
\]
For small deficit \(D\) contains a fixed inner ball and lies in a fixed outer ball. Hence each \(D_i\) has nonempty relative interior and contains zero in it. This gives one compatible product body for both endpoints.

By (5.3),
\[
d_{\rm BL}(S_C,S_D)\le C_*\sqrt\zeta.                   \tag{6.5}
\]
The boundary of a product has, up to boundary area zero, exactly one boundary factor. Therefore \(S_D\) is supported on
\(U=\bigcup_i(E_i\cap S^{n-1})\).
The function \(\operatorname{dist}(u,U)\) is bounded Lipschitz, so
\[
\int\operatorname{dist}(u,U)dS_C(u)\le C_*\sqrt\zeta.   \tag{6.6}
\]
This step transports normal mass near the block union; it does not assert that any approximating facet lies exactly in a subspace.

\section{Comparison of the original endpoints}

Construct
\[
K^\sharp=e^{tA_\gamma}D=\sum_i e^{ta_i}D_i,\qquad
L^\sharp=e^{-(1-t)A_\gamma}D=\sum_i e^{-(1-t)a_i}D_i.    \tag{7.1}
\]
This pair belongs to \(\mathcal E\), with factors \(e^{ta_i}D_i\) and dilation ratios \(e^{-a_i}\). Its ball bounds depend only on the original fixed parameters, because \(|a_i|\le C_*\). These auxiliary bounds are a conclusion of the construction, not an imposed restriction on the comparison class.

\subsection{Boundary slope and support errors}

For \(S_C\)-almost every normal \(u\), the full Wulff bound contacts:
\[
h_C(u)=h_K(u)^{1-t}h_L(u)^t,\quad
h_K(u)=h_C(u)e^{-tb(u)},\quad h_L(u)=h_C(u)e^{(1-t)b(u)}.              \tag{7.2}
\]
To see contact, at a regular boundary point compactness of the sphere supplies an active constraint; its normal must be the unique normal there. Thus (7.2) holds regardless of inactive directions.

Choose a nearest block sphere to \(u\), say \(E_i\cap S^{n-1}\), and put \(d(u)=\operatorname{dist}(u,U)\). For its regular boundary position \(x\),
\[
|(A_\gamma x)\cdot u-a_i h_C(u)|\le C_*d(u).
\]
Indeed \(A_\gamma\) is scalar on that block and \(x\) is bounded. Dividing by \(h_C\ge c_n\), then using (5.2) and (6.6), gives
\[
\int|b(u)+a_i|\,dS_C(u)\le C_*(e^{1/4}+\sqrt\zeta).                  \tag{7.3}
\]
The nearest-block selection is only measurable bookkeeping; it is never used as a Lipschitz test.

The support formula for the actual product dilation gives
\[
|h_{K^\sharp}(u)-e^{ta_i}h_C(u)|\le C_*(d(u)+\zeta).
\]
The terms from the other blocks are bounded by their projection lengths, at most \(C_*d(u)\); the remaining difference uses (6.4). The analogous estimate holds for \(L^\sharp\).
The exponential is Lipschitz on the bounded range of slopes. Equations (7.2)--(7.3) therefore yield
\[
\int |h_K-h_{K^\sharp}|dS_C+
\int |h_L-h_{L^\sharp}|dS_C
\le C_*(e^{1/4}+\sqrt\zeta+\zeta)
\le C_*e^{1/(4(n+2))}=:\omega.                        \tag{7.4}
\]

Each absolute support difference in (7.4) is itself uniformly bounded and Lipschitz on the sphere. Apply (6.5) to transfer (7.4) from \(S_C\) to \(S_D\), at an additional cost \(C_*\sqrt\zeta\le\omega\). No discontinuous nearest-block test is transferred.

For a block diagonal positive map \(T\) with multiplier \(s_i\) on \(E_i\), the product boundary change-of-variables formula is
\[
dS_{TD}(u)=\frac{\det T}{s_i}\,dS_D(u)\quad(u\in E_i\cap S^{n-1}).   \tag{7.5}
\]
It follows either from the tangential Jacobian \(\det T\,|T^{-T}u|\), or directly by scaling the one boundary factor and all other interior factors. These coefficients are uniformly bounded above and below for the maps in (7.1). Thus
\[
\left|V_1(K^\sharp,K)-|K^\sharp|\right|
+\left|V_1(L^\sharp,L)-|L^\sharp|\right|\le C_*\omega,                \tag{7.6}
\]
where \(V_1(A,B)=n^{-1}\int h_B\,dS_A\).

\subsection{Endpoint volumes and centering}

Hausdorff closeness and an inner ball give
\(|\log|D|-\log|C||\le C_*\zeta\).
Equations (4.8), \(\log|C|=\ell(t)+O_*(\delta)\), and (7.1) imply
\[
|\log|K^\sharp|-\log|K||+
|\log|L^\sharp|-\log|L||\le C_*(\zeta+e+\delta)\le C_*\omega.          \tag{7.7}
\]
In particular the volume chord slope has supplied the determinant of the two comparison maps. Merely setting \(m=0\) would fail here for endpoints of different volume.

We use the following consequence of quantitative anisotropic isoperimetry. If symmetric \(A,B\) lie between fixed positive balls and
\[
|V_1(A,B)-|A||+||B|-|A||\le s,
\]
then
\[
d_H(A,B)\le C s^{1/(2n)}.                              \tag{7.8}
\]
Apply Figalli--Maggi--Pratelli \cite[Theorem 1.1]{FMP} with finite-perimeter set \(E=A\) and Wulff shape \(B\). Its anisotropic perimeter is \(nV_1(A,B)\). Ordinary Minkowski ensures the normalized deficit is nonnegative; the displayed hypotheses and positive volume bounds make it \(O(s)\). The theorem gives
\[
|A\mathbin\triangle(x+aB)|\le C\sqrt s,\qquad
a^n|B|=|A|,\quad a=1+O(s),
\]
for some translation \(x\), or a sequence approaching the infimum.

The optimal translation can be removed for symmetric convex bodies. The overlap \(f(x)=|A\cap(x+aB)|\) is even, and \(f^{1/n}\) is concave on its support: Minkowski interpolation of two intersections is contained in the intersection at the interpolated translation. Hence \(f(0)\ge f(x)\). Thus
\(|A\triangle aB|\le C\sqrt s\), and changing \(a\) to one costs \(O(s)\).
Finally a support excess \(h_A(u)-h_B(u)=\eta>0\) creates a cone cap inside \(A\setminus B\) of volume at least
\(\kappa_{n-1}r_1^{n-1}\eta^n/(nR_1^{n-1})\), by joining an exposed point to the inner disk perpendicular to \(u\). Interchange the bodies for the other sign. This proves (7.8), without a smoothness assumption.

Apply (7.8) to (7.6)--(7.7) for each endpoint:
\[
\max\{d_H(K,K^\sharp),d_H(L,L^\sharp)\}
\le C_*\omega^{1/(2n)}
\le C_*e^{1/(8n(n+2))}
=C_*\delta^{1/\alpha_n}.                              \tag{7.9}
\]
Undo the two constant linear transformations; their operator norms are bounded in terms of the original parameters. They preserve membership in the complementary-block class, allowing oblique subspaces. This proves \eqref{eq:stability} for small positive deficit.

For a larger deficit use the uniformly bounded distance to the fixed comparison pair \((rB,rB)\), and reduce the constant in \eqref{eq:stability}. If \(\delta=0\), repeat the construction with an arbitrary positive upper budget \(d\downarrow0\), taking \(\varepsilon=d\). Every estimate above used only \(\delta\le d\). Equation (7.9) gives \(d_{\mathcal E}=0\), sufficient for \eqref{eq:stability}, without invoking closedness or qualitative equality. The construction began directly with arbitrary bodies and direction exhaustion, so no facet-dependent approximation exponent remains. \qed
