    SCALAR EXTREMIZERS FOR COMPLETE CROUZEIX EQUALITY IN
                       DIMENSION FOUR

                                              MYEONGJIN KANG


       Abstract. Assume the complete Crouzeix bound and the analytic Faber coeﬃcient estimate stated
       below. Let A be a complex matrix of order at most four, and let P be a matrix-valued polynomial
       of arbitrary ﬁnite degree and coeﬃcient size. If ∥P [A]∥ = 2 maxW (A) ∥P ∥ > 0, we prove that
       there are constant unit coeﬃcient vectors u, v for which p = v ∗ P u attains the same equality. Jin’s
       scalar rigidity theorem then implies that W (A) is a disk, and the extracted scalar polynomial is
       a monomial after centering and scaling that disk. The main step converts zero slack in a positive
       boundary-ﬁeld inequality into a rank-one condition on the coeﬃcient factors of extremal singular
       vectors. An elementary Hermitian-pencil argument supplies the required regular boundary arc in
       dimension four.

   The conclusion extracts a scalar polynomial from the given matrix-valued polynomial by constant
coeﬃcient vectors. It does not classify the evaluated operator P [A]. The theorem concerns attained
polynomial equality; it makes no assertion about a supremum approached only by a sequence of
changing witnesses.
   Åhag, Czyż, and Virtanen [1] prove complete equality results through base dimension three.
Their ﬁnite-level reduction leaves two orthogonal coeﬃcient supports of dimensions (2, 2) as the
remaining possibility in dimension four. Their scalar polynomial classiﬁcation applies in every
ﬁnite dimension. In the current version, these results appear in Theorem 1.6 and Section 12. The
distinction is necessary: scalar equality theory does not by itself produce a scalar compression of a
speciﬁed matrix-valued extremizer.
   Our argument uses the positive resolvent ﬁeld for the original matrix. Zero slack makes its
range totally isotropic for the diﬀerence of two singular-vector Gram matrices. At a simple support
eigenvalue this ﬁeld has corank one, forcing both coeﬃcient supports to have dimension one. The
needed simple support direction exists for every unitarily irreducible matrix of order at most four.
A boundary limit allows us to use this direction on the numerical range itself.

                      4. A simple support direction in dimension four
Lemma 4.1. Every unitarily irreducible B ∈ Md (C) with 2 ≤ d ≤ 4 has interior spectrum and a
regular real-analytic boundary arc of W (B) with simple largest support eigenvalue.
Proof. If W (B) had empty interior, a translation and rotation would make B Hermitian, contra-
dicting irreducibility. If Bv = λv with λ ∈ ∂W (B), choose a supporting normal ν. The positive
matrix ℜ(νλ)I − ℜ(νB) has zero quadratic form at v, so it annihilates v. Combining this with the
eigenvector equation gives B ∗ v = λv. The line Cv would reduce B. Hence σ(B) ⊂ int W (B).

The complete bound and (5.1) give ψ(B) = 2. Jin’s scalar rigidity theorem [2, Theorem 1] implies
W (B) = a + ρD for some ρ > 0. It remains to control the other reducing blocks of A.
   Put T = (B − aI)/ρ and g(z) = p(a + ρz). Then σ(T ) ⊂ D, ∥g∥D = 1, and ∥g(T )∥ = 2. Prescribe
at each eigenvalue of T the contiguous jet of g through one less than the largest corresponding
Jordan block size. These ﬁnitely many data determine g(T ). Their least possible H ∞ (D) interpo-
lation norm is one. Indeed, if an interpolant f had norm c < 1, then for 0 < r < 1 the function
fr (z) = f (rz) is holomorphic near D and has norm at most c. Taylor approximation and the scalar
case of (1.2) give ∥fr (T )∥ ≤ 2c. As r ↑ 1, the interior spectral jets converge, so ∥f (T )∥ ≤ 2c < 2,
contradicting f (T ) = g(T ).
   The ﬁnite interpolation lemma [2, Lemma 4] says that such minimal-norm-one data have a unique
Schur-class interpolant and that it is a ﬁnite Blaschke product. Hence g is a ﬁnite Blaschke product.
Since g is a polynomial, all its Blaschke zeros are zero: a nonzero zero α ∈ D would give a pole at
1/α, which cannot cancel a numerator zero inside the disk. Therefore g(z) = ηz k with |η| = 1 and
1 ≤ k ≤ deg P . The exponent is positive because a constant cannot evaluate with norm two.
   This polynomial identity holds globally. The bound |p| ≤ 1 on W (A) therefore forces W (A) ⊂
a + ρD. The reverse inclusion follows from W (B) ⊂ W (A). Restoring M proves (1.6).                  □
