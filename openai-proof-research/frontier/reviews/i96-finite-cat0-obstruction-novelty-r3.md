### 3.2 Reverse median is an optimized four-point estimate

For any q>0 the classical Young–Reshetnyak inequality is

`d(p,j)^2+d(z,c')^2-d(p,c')^2-d(z,j)^2 ≤ q d(p,z)^2+q^-1 d(c',j)^2`.

Suppose `c'=mid(p,w)`, `j=mid(z,w)` and the squares P=d(p,w)^2, D=d(z,w)^2, A0=d(p,z)^2, Z=d(z,c')^2 differ from 3,2,1,3/4 by at most epsilon≤1/16. Put x=d(c',j)^2. CN at c' gives `d(p,j)^2≥2x-D/4+P/2`. Substitution in Young gives

`(q^-1-2)x≥Z+P/4-D/2-qA0`.

Take q=1/2-sqrt(epsilon), using a limit only for epsilon=0. Since q>0, direct rearrangement gives `x≥1/4-(17/16)sqrt(epsilon)`. Busemann convexity gives `x≤A0/4`. Therefore `|x-1/4|≤2sqrt(epsilon)`. This is a parameter optimization of the source flat-triangle equality data; no unproved quantitative splitting or general stability theorem is inserted.

Apply it with two full lines at i in a core hexagon, using the opposite midpoint c'. All required errors are at most `E0=17e+R0+3h`. Transfer c' to c and the line midpoint to J_i. Distances are bounded by 2 at these parameters; a conservative squared-distance transfer loss is 5(h+s0). Thus

`|d(c,J_i)^2-1/4|≤F=2sqrt(E0)+5(h+s0)`.
