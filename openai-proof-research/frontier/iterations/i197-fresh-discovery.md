## What the mathematical work establishes

The [complete analytic record](../experiments/i197_fresh_discovery/analytic-attempt.md) proves this **technical deduction** from the accepted 026 operations:

For each C>0 and integer ell>=1, some finite B>C and eta>0 satisfy, for all sufficiently large N,

`#{1<=n<=N-ell : C log p_{n+j}<p_{n+j+1}-p_{n+j}<=B log p_{n+j} for every 0<=j<ell} >= eta N`.

Thus an arbitrary fixed length of consecutive large gaps has positive ordinary lower index density, with a nonprescribed finite upper bound. The proof uses many small detector windows separated by suppressed blocks. A first/second-moment score charges both multiple primes in a detector window and primes in a separating block. Positive score forces a run of the required number of isolated, consecutive primes. The accepted fourth moment then converts weighted mass into ordinary starts, and strip-length multiplicity gives prime-index density.

This is not derived merely by intersecting positive-density sets, and it uses no joint prime lower estimate. The exact finite-union reduction, parameter order, constants, boundary handling and counterexample to the density-only inference are written out in the analytic record.

The distinct second route tries to put a prime in each prescribed small window and suppress everything between. It would yield arbitrary prescribed narrow normalized-gap boxes, but the source has only one genuine prime mark. Its pair and higher divisor majorants give upper bounds; they do not provide the needed lower mass for a product of prime counts. The record states sufficient estimates J1-J3 and supplies an explicit mutually exclusive-occupancy countermodel to the attempted marginal-to-joint inference. This route is incomplete, not a theorem.
