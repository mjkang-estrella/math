### 4.1 Reversible-clock lemma

Fix any finite geometry and any finite vector F of Boolean observables of its fair independent bits. Monotonicity is unnecessary. Let `I_v(omega)=1{F(omega^v)!=F(omega)}`, `N_F=sum_v I_v`, and let J_F[0,h] count times the vector changes. Refresh every bit at rate r. The flip generator is

`L f=(r/2)sum_v [f(omega^v)-f(omega)]`.

It is reversible for fair product measure pi. Crucially I_v(omega^v)=I_v(omega). If P_t is its semigroup and lambda=(r/2)N_F, then stationarity and the Markov property give exactly

`E_pi J_F[0,h] = h E_pi lambda`,

`E_pi[J_F[0,h](J_F[0,h]-1)] = 2 int_0^h (h-t) <lambda,P_t lambda>_pi dt`.  (D149.1)

Proof of the second identity: for the first counted transition, sum `(r/2) I_v(omega) (P_t lambda)(omega^v)` against pi. Change omega to omega^v separately in each summand. Invariance of pi and I_v turns the sum into `<lambda,P_t lambda>`. Integrate ordered pairs of jump times and multiply by two. The L2 contraction of P_t gives

`P_pi(J_F[0,h]>=2) <= r² h² E_pi N_F² /8`.  (D149.2)

This is an estimate for the actual reversible independent-resampling dynamics conditional on its actual geometry. It does not suppose that pivotal sets are independent at different times or after a pivotal flip. Replacing the post-flip state by an independent configuration would not prove the identity.
