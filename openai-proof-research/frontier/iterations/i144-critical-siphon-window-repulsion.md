`delta_r=min(delta_(r+1),min_(|Z|=r)b_Z)>0`.  (A10)

Then every size-r sum has that floor at all t≥tau_r. This completes the induction. At r=1, `{x in closure(P):x_i≥delta_1 for all i}` is a compact subset of P and contains every trajectory for all t≥tau_1. The floors depend only on graph,P,K,T,alpha; time additionally depends on Q through the u_Z. Every new stage starts at an arbitrary deterministic time tau, and all subsequent windows retain PE. No aligned phase, infinite scale recursion, finite-horizon compactness shortcut, or fresh-schedule invariant set is used. There are n−1 stages, and each stage controls **all future** excursions, explaining why finitely many steps suffice here.

### A5. Genuine higher-rank examples and restriction

The original bidirectional ring satisfies the criterion: in each critical pair both rare species have a direct first-order branching edge. Even the **one-direction** cycle `y0→y1→...→y5→y0` satisfies it. Near the A vertex, C first transfers through `y5→y0` to B, and B branches through `y0→y1`; the other vertices are cyclic copies. This tests the chronological path case, not only immediate growth.

For an explicit family of unbounded rank, take n≥3 species and all complexes `2e_i+e_j`, i≠j, with both directed edges between every pair of distinct complexes. The class is the mass-one simplex and rank is n−1: vectors `e_j−e_i` occur. A proper set with at least two species outside it is not a siphon, since a source supported on those two can reach a product meeting the set. Sets with exactly one outside species are siphons, because no complex is supported on that one species. They are accessible and critical: only the full-support total-mass conservation direction remains. If a is the outside species, each j in the siphon has the first-order branching edge `2e_a+e_j→e_a+2e_j`. Thus A144 applies in every n≥3. This is neither a scalar reduction nor a class without accessible siphons.

The criterion does not cover every weakly reversible network. For example, doubling every complex in the original ring leaves rank two and accessible critical pair siphons, but there are no first-order-in-pair sources at all. That example is a failure of this criterion, **not** a counterexample to permanence (its special higher-order symmetry may permit another direct return estimate). More generally, different rare monomials can have incompatible orders. Nothing in weak reversibility alone supplies A6 for them.

## 5. Route B: an endpoint-label window mechanism and its exact obstruction

### B1. A physical endpoint representation with paid effective bounds

Return to arbitrary compact P and its actual consumption C and degree d; no conservation normalization or siphon-transfer condition is imposed. Fix L≥1. Consider a physical window `[s,s+T]` satisfying the **coordinatewise endpoint condition**

`x_i(s+T)≤L x_i(s)` for every i.  (B1)

This condition is not inferred from nondoubling of one scalar. Write `z=x(s+T)`. For every u in the window, forward and backward use of consumption gives

`e^(−CT)L^(−1) z_i ≤ x_i(u) ≤ e^(CT)z_i`.

Define the actual, nonnegative, state-weighted coefficients

`q_e=(1/[T z^y_e]) integral_s^(s+T) k_e(u)x(u)^y_e du`.

Then exactly

`(z−x(s))/T=sum_e q_e z^y_e Delta_e`,

`a_L≤q_e≤b`, where `a_L=(alpha/T)(e^(−CT)/L)^d>0`, `b=K e^(CdT)`.  (B2)

The upper bound actually needs no B1: comparison backwards from the endpoint always gives it. B1 supplies the missing lower bound at the endpoint. For degree-zero sources the factors are one, consistent with the deliberately safe degree-d bounds. This retains each **physical monomial flux**; it is not a limiting averaged equation, an assumption of pointwise positive rates, or F1 incorrectly used as an upper bound at the initial monomial.

### B2. All endpoint labels and ties; no integrated-minimum exchange

`A(t)=a`, `B(t)=(epsilon−epsilon³)e^(−a²t)`,
