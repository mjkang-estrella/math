## 1. Proposed theorem and dependencies

Assume the pinned OpenAI corpus at `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb`. There is a polynomial-time uniform family of quantum circuits over one fixed finite gate set that, for a prime p, nonzero g,h modulo p with h in the subgroup generated by g, returns the unique integer a in [0,ord(g)) satisfying g^a=h with probability one. Gate count, qubit count and worst-case running time are polynomial in log p. The gate set is the one in the pinned exact-factoring theorem: NOT, CNOT, Toffoli, H, S=diag(1,i), inverses, and singly controlled versions. No input-dependent rotation, postselection, or expected-time retry is used.

The new core is the prime-order case with the order q supplied. The full theorem then uses the stipulated exact factoring algorithm and the classical prime-power digit reduction written below. Besides ordinary exact arithmetic, the external algorithmic input is fixed-dimensional lattice-point counting: for each fixed dimension d, integer points in a rational polytope can be counted deterministically in time polynomial in its binary encoding. The parent read Barvinok–Pommersheim, *An Algorithmic Theory of Lattice Points in Polyhedra*, Theorem 4.4 and Algorithm 5.2 discussion/procedure, particularly web extraction lines 887–1026, at https://library.slmath.org/books/Book38/files/barvinok.pdf. This is an external classical theorem, not an extra stipulated OpenAI premise. The weighted version used here is reduced explicitly to unweighted counts in dimension at most 13.

The pinned exact-factoring manuscript supplies two operational inputs: its Section 3, Lemma `lem:transform`, exactly implements the unnormalized matrix A_Q(k,x)=Q^(-1/2)g_0(kx/Q) on a flagged branch for Q a power of two, using polynomially many fixed-set gates; its Section 2 supplies exact dyadic coins and retained-history unitary compilation. Here g_0 is the complex polygonal phase interpolating exp(-2 pi i t) at quarter integers. The scalar base g in the theorem is unrelated to the phase function g_0. These source inputs are assumed true, with their actual branch probabilities retained.

## 2. Prime-order trial and exact output event

Let q>=3 be an odd prime, let g have order q, and first handle h=1 by returning zero. Otherwise the true logarithm a lies in {1,...,q-1}. Put b=ceil(log_2 q), B=2^b and Q=2^10 B². Prepare

    Q^(-1) sum_(0<=x,y<Q) |x,y>|g^x h^y mod p>.

All arithmetic has O(log p) bit lengths up to a fixed constant factor. Compute the group value by reversible compute-copy-uncompute, clearing all arithmetic scratch while retaining only x,y and the group-value output before the two interference transforms. Apply the source A_Q independently to the first two registers and retain both ancillary success flags. Do not copy either input register before this interference. Measure output integers k,l. A number k is an accepted q-bin if k=k_q(j):=floor(jQ/q+1/2) for some 0<=j<q. Decode j by rounding kq/Q to its nearest integer, then verify the displayed equality. Since q/Q<1, this recovers a bin label uniquely. Use an analogous check to obtain j' from l.

Require j!=0. Compute a'=j' j^(-1) mod q and verify g^(a')=h. A passing trial returns (j,a'); otherwise it fails. The verifier makes the logarithm output unique. For each j in {1,...,q-1}, let p_j be the actual probability that both flags and the decoder pass with that label and the true a. Its event is exactly

    k=k_q(j),  l=k_q(a j mod q),  both phase flags successful.

The main claims below are p_j>=1/(81q) and exact polynomial-time computability of p_j from q,j,a. The latter uses the found or guessed valid a, not an oracle for a before the trial.

## 3. Uniform Fourier lower bound

The polygonal phase has the absolutely convergent Fourier expansion

    g_0(t)=sum_(m congruent 1 mod 4) c_m exp(-2 pi i m t),
    c_m=8/(pi²m²)>0,                 sum_m c_m=1.       (F)

One obtains (F) by integrating each affine quarter-interval twice, or by convolving the four-point phase sequence with the periodic triangular interpolation kernel. The discrete four-point sum vanishes unless m=1 mod 4; on that class its triangular-kernel coefficient is (sin(pi m/4)/(pi m/4))²=8/(pi²m²). The zero coefficient vanishes. Absolute convergence and evaluation at zero give the stated sum.

For t in {1,...,q-1}, set k=k_q(t), eta=k-tQ/q and

    S(k,t)=sum_(x=0)^(Q-1) g_0(kx/Q) exp(2 pi i t x/q).

Write D_Q(v)=sum_(x=0)^(Q-1)exp(-2 pi i v x). Substitution of (F) gives

    S(k,t)=sum_m c_m D_Q((m-1)t/q+m eta/Q).

The m=1 term has magnitude at least (2c_1/pi)Q>Q/2: |eta|<=1/2 and the geometric-sum formula gives |D_Q(eta/Q)|>=2Q/pi, including eta=0 by continuity; 16/pi³>1/2.

Bound all other terms by the triangle inequality in three classes. First, aliases q|(m-1) have m=1+4qz, z!=0, since q is odd and m=1 mod 4. Their total coefficient is at most

    (8/pi²) sum_(z!=0) 1/(3q|z|)²
      =8/(27q²)<1/24.

Their D_Q values are at most Q. Second, nonaliases with |m|<=M:=Q/(2q) have distance at least 3/(4q) from an integer in the argument of D_Q: the unperturbed distance is at least 1/q and |m eta/Q|<=1/(4q). Thus |D_Q|<=2q/3, using |sin(pi v)|>=2 dist(v,Z). Their total coefficient is at most one. Third, the total coefficient of all |m|>M is at most 4/M for M>=2, by the integral bound on sum 1/m² and 8/pi²<1. Their contribution is at most 4Q/M=8q. Double counting some aliases in this last upper bound is harmless. Therefore

    |S(k,t)| >= Q/2-Q/24-(2q/3+8q)
               >= Q/2-Q/24-9q >= 5Q/12 > Q/3,       (S)

because Q>=216q. Our Q=1024B² satisfies that and M>=2 uniformly.

For the two-register trial, the unnormalized group-register vector at outputs k,l is

    Q^(-2) sum_(x,y<Q) g_0(kx/Q)g_0(ly/Q)|g^(x+a y)>.

For analysis only, project this vector onto the unit group-character vector

    chi_j=q^(-1/2) sum_(z=0)^(q-1) exp(-2 pi i jz/q)|g^z>.

The vectors |g^z> are distinct computational-basis states. The overlap factors exactly as

    [Q² sqrt(q)]^(-1) S(k,j)S(l,a j mod q).

For the accepted outputs attached to j, both arguments are nonzero modulo the prime q. Equation (S) proves p_j>=1/(81q). The character projection is only a lower bound in the analysis; the circuit never prepares or measures chi_j. No QFT of order q or normalization of a selected branch has been inserted.

## 4. Exact probability from a fixed-dimensional count

For fixed q,j,a, compute k=k_q(j), l=k_q(a j mod q). Tracing out the group register gives the exact formula

    p_j=Q^(-4) sum g_0(kx/Q)g_0(ly/Q)
                   conjugate(g_0(kx'/Q)g_0(ly'/Q)),   (P)

where x,y,x',y' range over [0,Q-1] integers with

    x+a y-x'-a y'=q z

for an integer z. It suffices to impose -Q<=z<=Q. The correct a is a fixed numerical coefficient in this classical probability computation, so this is a linear constraint, not a bilinear counting oracle.

For each of the four phase arguments introduce its integer quarter-bin b_v=floor(4c v/Q), with c=k or l, and write b_v=4n_v+sigma_v, sigma_v in {0,1,2,3}. There are only 4^4 residue cases. In each case enforce

    Q(4n_v+sigma_v) <= 4c v
      <= Q(4n_v+sigma_v+1)-1,
    0<=n_v<=Q.

These exact integer inequalities encode the floor without overlap. On this cell the phase is the affine Gaussian-rational function

    (-i)^sigma_v [1+(-i-1)(4c v/Q-4n_v-sigma_v)].

The conjugated factors use the conjugate coefficients. The product in (P) is a polynomial of total degree at most four in the four original variables and four n_v variables, with denominator dividing Q^4. All constraints define bounded rational polytopes in nine integer variables (including z); all coefficients have O(log Q+log q) bits and the number of constraints is bounded independently of the input. Equalities may be kept as two inequalities; fixed-dimensional counting allows lower-dimensional polytopes as well.

For completeness, weighted sums reduce to unweighted counts with at most four more variables. Expand the fixed-degree polynomial into monomials. For each nonnegative integer variable v use v^d=sum_(s=0)^d S(d,s)s! binom(v,s). A factor binom(v,s) counts integer tuples 0<=t_1<...<t_s<v, imposed by linear inequalities. The total number of new variables is at most the monomial's total degree, hence at most four. Signed real/imaginary rational coefficients are handled by exact linear combinations of these counts. No polynomial weight contains the possibly negative z. Thus a bounded number of calls to the classical unweighted counting theorem in dimension at most 13 computes (P) exactly.

This produces a Gaussian rational with denominator dividing Q^8. Symmetry under interchange of primed and unprimed variables makes it real; it is nonnegative because (P) is an actual branch probability. Its representation length is polynomial. Fixed-dimensional counting and all surrounding arithmetic run in deterministic polynomial bit time. In particular, exponentially many j labels are not enumerated.

## 5. Per-label dyadic completion

There are exactly q-1 valid label/output pairs (j,a). Let

    tau=2^(-b-8),        J=2b+4.

For every valid pair, tau<=p_j/2 by the lower bound in Section 3. Compute

    C_j=2^(-J) floor(2^J * 2tau/p_j),
    e_j=tau-p_j C_j/2.

Then 0<=C_j<=1 and 0<=e_j<p_j 2^(-J-1). With probability 1/2 run the ordinary trial, require its verified pair, and retain with probability C_j. With probability 1/2 guess two independent uniform b-bit strings (j,a'), require 1<=j<q, 0<=a'<q and g^(a')=h, then retain with probability 2^(2b+1)e_j, computed from that verified pair. This retention is less than 2^(2b-J)=1/16. Every sampled probability is dyadic; computing the floor uses exact integer division, not a nondyadic random coin.

For each j the ordinary branch contributes p_j C_j/2 and the guessing branch contributes exactly e_j. Their sum is tau. Hence total success is the initially known dyadic

    P_0=(q-1)tau >=1/512.

All successful branches return the same logarithm a. Repeat this preparation independently 1024 times and take the first verified success. The probability P=1-(1-P_0)^1024 is an exactly computable polynomial-bit dyadic at least 3/4: (1-1/512)^512<=1/2, as in the parent compiler lemma. Reuse the explicit unique-output quarter-success construction in `i322-parent-exactification-bridge.md`, or the pinned quarter-success arithmetic, to obtain a uniform unitary with success probability exactly 1/4. One ordinary Grover iteration then gives certainty.

All classical outputs used later have retained orthogonal history registers. The source's retained-record lemma compiles the measured/adaptive preparation into a uniform unitary and supplies its inverse over the same fixed gate set. The fixed-dimensional classical counting subroutine is simply a polynomially clocked reversible classical computation within that preparation. All bad guesses and failed histories have polynomial resource caps. The ordinary trial is never postselected or retried without a fixed cap.

## 6. Arbitrary order and prime-field logarithms

The prime q=2 case is solved by testing the two possible exponents, and g=1 is immediate. For general p and g, use the stipulated exact fixed-gate factorization algorithm on p-1. From its ordered prime-power factors compute ord(g)=m by repeatedly testing g^(m/ell)=1 and dividing when the test succeeds. Factor information for m is inherited from p-1. There are at most O(log p) such tests.

For ell^e dividing m, put g_ell=g^(m/ell^e), h_ell=h^(m/ell^e), and gamma=g_ell^(ell^(e-1)), of prime order ell. Starting x_0=0, if x_k agrees with a modulo ell^k, solve the prime-order logarithm

    gamma^(d_k)=(h_ell g_ell^(-x_k))^(ell^(e-1-k))

and set x_(k+1)=x_k+d_k ell^k. The exponent identity proves the right side equals gamma raised to the next base-ell digit. After e calls this gives a modulo ell^e. Chinese remaindering over the prime powers gives the unique a in [0,m). Total calls are bounded by sum e<=log_2 m, each with size polynomial in log p. Fresh histories or coherent copies followed by uncomputation retain exactness and polynomial worst-case bounds; all required inverse/control gates already belong to the fixed model.

This is the classical prime-power discrete-logarithm reduction. Its contribution is not claimed new. The new proposed bridge is the exact selected-bin probability count and the uniform per-label lower bound that enable fixed-gate completion. If a classical input fails the stated promise, the circuit may return a fixed default; the theorem is asserted on the promised inputs. Primality, nonzero residues and subgroup membership can also be verified with the exact factorization/primality interfaces and modular powering.

## 7. Validation and limits

This is a parent proof proposal, not an independent PASS. The Fourier coefficient normalization, all tail constants, the exact amplitude prefactors, bounded polytope representation, weighted-count reduction and uniform controlled compilation need adversarial checking. In particular, a purported reduction with input-dependent dimension, a hidden sum over exponentially many frequencies, a normalized postselection, or a non-dyadic retention would invalidate the proposed theorem.

The original factoring theorem is stipulated true and is not audited here. Classical fixed-dimensional counting and the classical prime-power group reduction are credited. The parent has not established literature novelty of this exact fixed-gate discrete-logarithm theorem. Mosca–Zalka's computed-rotation model, older exact hidden-subgroup algorithms and later fixed-gate work must be inspected independently. No worldwide priority, practical resource estimate, or claim about arbitrary nonabelian groups is made. A correct proof alone does not pass the contribution gate.
