## 5. Route two: renormalized finite periodic short-pair estimates

We choose a **finite periodic** statement for a fixed `0<=s<2`, with unit planar background and N distinct points on a torus of area N. No thermodynamic or stationary statement is silently identified with it.

### 5.1 Exact normalization and subtraction

Use U's full weighted-field normalization: `g_0=-log|x|`, `g_s=|x|^(-s)` for s>0, `kappa_0=2pi`, `kappa_s=4pi`, extension weight |y|^(s-1) for s>0. Let v_(s,L)=q_s G_(s,L) be the mean-zero periodic scalar Green kernel with singular part g_s, where

`q_0=2pi`, `q_s=2^(2-s) pi Gamma(1-s/2)/Gamma(s/2)`.

Write `r_(s,L)=lim_(x->0)[v_(s,L)(x)-g_s(x)]`. The scalar ordered energy is

`J_(s,L)(P)=N^(-1) sum_(i!=j) v_(s,L)(p_i-p_j)+r_(s,L)=V_s(P)/kappa_s`.

The periodic background is encoded by removing the zero Fourier mode; the regular part is the self subtraction. Define `delta_s=J_(s,L)(P)-J_(s,A)(A)`. At s=0 the Coulomb companion's half-field torus energy equals `pi N J_(0,L)`, so its per-site gap is pi delta_0. These conventions cannot be interchanged without that factor.

U's heat comparison, with `H_P(t)=N^(-1)sum_ij P_(t,L)(p_i-p_j)-(4pi t)^(-1)`, gives exactly

`delta_s = integral_0^infinity w_s(alpha) Delta_alpha(P) d alpha`,

`w_0(alpha)=1/(2alpha)`,

`w_s(alpha)=pi^(s/2) alpha^(s/2-1)/Gamma(s/2)` for 0<s<2. (R1)

To check the factors, `H_P-H_A=(4pi t)^(-1)Delta_(1/(4pi t))`; substitute t=1/(4pi alpha) into `q_s/Gamma(1-s/2) integral(H_P-H_A)t^(-s/2)dt`. Background 1 and the diagonal heat singularity cancel **before** integration. For each fixed simple finite periodic configuration the small-t remainder is O(t^(-1)exp(-c/t)) and the large-t remainder decays by the nonzero torus spectral gap. Constants here may depend on the configuration and shape. The source proves absolute convergence; Tonelli is then available for the nonnegative Delta_alpha, since Gaussian comparison holds for arbitrary period lattices. We use no uniform spectral gap as N or aspect ratio changes.

### 5.2 A cutoff-resolved singular defect budget

For T>=1 and d>0 define

`K_(0,T)(d) = (1/2) E1(pi T d²)`,

`K_(s,T)(d) = Gamma(s/2,pi T d²)/(Gamma(s/2)d^s)` for 0<s<2,

and `B_s(T)=sum_(v in A\{0}) K_(s,T)(|v|)`.

These are precisely `integral_T^infinity w_s(alpha) exp(-pi alpha d²)d alpha`. Let F be **any** subset of directed off-diagonal periodic pair incidences, including nonzero self-images if desired. Then

`N^(-1) sum_(i,j,lambda in F) K_(s,T)(|p_i-p_j+lambda|) <= delta_s+B_s(T)`. (R2)

To prove it, keep alpha>=T in (R1), write `Delta_alpha=E_alpha(P)-E_alpha(A)`, and discard the unselected positive pair terms in E_alpha(P). Integrate by Tonelli. The lattice subtraction is exactly B_s(T), not an unspecified renormalization constant. This proves a usable noncompact-parameter estimate which charges arbitrarily close pairs. It is not merely a compact Gaussian-mixture cell bound.

There is an explicit finite tail constant:

`B_s(T)<=C_s^tail exp(-pi a² T/2)` for T>=1,                 (R3)

where `C_s^tail=2^(s/2) B_s(1/2)` for s>0 and `C_0^tail=B_0(1/2)`. Indeed split `exp(-pi alpha |v|²)` into two equal factors, bound one by `exp(-pi T a²/2)`, and integrate the other with alpha=2u. For s=0 the remaining integral is B_0(T/2); for s>0 it is `2^(s/2)B_s(T/2)`. Monotonicity in T and Gaussian lattice summability prove (R3) with these finite constants. No T-dependent hidden constant is used.

For s>0 and `pi T d²<=1`,

`K_(s,T)(d)>=[Gamma(s/2,1)/Gamma(s/2)] d^(-s)`.

For s=0 on the same range, integration from pi T d² to 1 gives

`K_(0,T)(d)>=exp(-1) log(1/(sqrt(pi T)d))`.

Thus (R2) controls a weighted singular short-pair sum, not just pair counts. In particular, if 0<rho satisfies pi T rho²<1 and F consists of all pairs at distance <rho, deleting every incident point costs

`K/N <= [delta_s+B_s(T)]/K_(s,T)(rho)`.                      (R4)

Monotonicity of K supplies the denominator, and directed endpoint charging is the same exact inequality used in P134. The remaining points have periodic separation >=rho. Taking `T=max(1,(2/(pi a²))log(C_s^tail/delta_s))` for a positive small delta_s makes the numerator at most 2delta_s. The resulting entrance radius depends on T and the desired discarded mass. This is a paid near-minimizer entrance *after discard*, never an all-site separation theorem.

For completeness a compact-interval consequence is also available, but remains technical: integrate G134's explicit positive gap over alpha in [1,2] against w_s. The denominator D_alpha is continuous positive there, hence the resulting short-pair constant is positive. Repeat deletion/saturation to obtain the same cell estimates with delta_s in place of Delta; repeat A137's full construction for a finite periodic atlas at admissible scales. This composes already paid operations. It is not a new infinite-volume passage or a Coulomb interface law.

### 5.3 Exact near-collision family: separation does not transfer

Let L=mA, N=m² and m>=8. Start with A modulo mA. Remove the site 0 and insert `z=v+epsilon e`, where v is a fixed nearest neighbor of 0, e is a fixed unit vector and `0<epsilon<a/10`. The resulting configuration has exactly N distinct sites, area N, and a pair at distance epsilon. It has the same neutral periodic background. No grain-boundary or unscreened exterior construction is involved.

For the mean-zero scalar kernel, finite subgroup summation of Fourier characters gives

`sum_(x in A/mA) v_(s,mA)(z-x)=v_(s,A)(z)`.                (R5)

This equality holds distributionally and smoothly away from A: only dual-A modes survive, and N cancels the period-volume Fourier factor. Its regular part at z=0 gives `sum_(x!=0) v_(s,mA)(x)=r_(s,A)-r_(s,mA)`. Only pairs incident to the moved point change, so the exact ordered excess is

`delta_s= (2/N)[v_(s,A)(z)-v_(s,mA)(z)-r_(s,A)+r_(s,mA)]`. (R6)

There is no missing factor two. Period scaling gives

`v_(s,mA)(x)=m^(-s)v_(s,A)(x/m)`, `r_(s,mA)=m^(-s)r_(s,A)` for s>0;

`v_(0,mA)(x)=v_(0,A)(x/m)`, `r_(0,mA)=r_(0,A)+log m`.

Near zero, `v_(s,A)(x)=g_s(x)+r_(s,A)+O_s(|x|²)`. One way to justify this last expansion without a conditionally summed derivative is to split the periodic heat formula at t=1. The small-t nonzero image terms are smooth with summable derivatives, the large-t part is smooth by exponential spectral decay, and the kernel removed on R² has the stated singular part. Evenness removes the linear term. The constants are for the fixed lattice A only. Since v is in A,

`delta_s=(2/N)[g_s(epsilon)-g_s(z)+O_s(epsilon²)+O_s(m^(-s-2))]` for s>0,

`delta_0=(2/N)[log(1/epsilon)+log|z|+O(epsilon²)+O(m^(-2))]`. (R7)

These are asymptotics with uniform errors for 0<epsilon<a/10, m>=8; |z| stays bounded above and away from zero. Their lower bound delta_s>=0 follows independently from (R1).

Choose epsilon=exp(-sqrt(N)) in the logarithmic case. Then `delta_0=2N^(-1/2)+O(N^(-1))→0`. For 0<s<2 choose epsilon=N^(-p), any fixed 0<p<1/s. Then `delta_s=2N^(ps-1)+O_s(N^(-1))→0`. Thus arbitrary near minimizers can have arbitrarily small separation on perfectly regular compatible tori. The separation of an exact minimizer cannot be extended by an N-uniform continuity argument. This family is also consistent with (R2): the two directed collision incidences contribute their singular energy divided by N. It does not contradict bounds after discarding a small fraction, and it does not establish a failure of all possible coherent-domain theorems.

The Coulomb companion's retained decomposition is, on its compatible separated data,
