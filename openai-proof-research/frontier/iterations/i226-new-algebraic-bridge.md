## Strongest paid result: the specialized quadratic identity obstruction

Let F0 have characteristic zero, let f_1,...,f_c be a regular sequence of quadrics in F0[x_1,...,x_n], and assume every nonzero member of their span has rank at least rho after every field extension. For every k/F0, every central division k-algebra Delta of degree r, and every nonzero linear form t in Delta[x] satisfying

    t^2 = B_1 f_1 + ... + B_c f_c,   B_j in Delta,

there is an integer s>=rho such that

    2^{floor(s/2)} divides r.

In particular, `dim_k Delta >= 4^{floor(rho/2)}`. The B_j are allowed to be specialized and noncentral in Delta. The proof does not assume they are independent parameters.

For the broader Q226 statement the key bridge is concrete. Commuting t with its square yields a degree-three syzygy among the f_j. A regular sequence of quadrics has no syzygies with linear coefficients, so every B_j commutes with every coefficient of t. The B_j also belong to the algebra generated by those coefficients. They therefore generate a finite field F inside Delta. Over F the square identity is an ordinary Clifford relation for q=sum B_j f_j. Diagonalization yields anticommuting units. Their conjugation characters prove independence of all products in even rank, or even products in odd rank. This produces a division subalgebra of dimension `2^{2 floor(s/2)}` over F and proves actual divisibility of r. Neither matrix-splitting nor a generic-coefficient substitution is needed.

For each n define

    k_n = min{k>=1 : k(k+1)/2 >= n},   rho_n = n-k_n+1.

There is a nonempty Zariski-open set of ordered regular sequences of n complex quadrics such that **every** pinned-format division-coefficient construction for **every** tuple in that open set obeys

    2^{floor(rho_n/2)} divides r,
    dim_k Delta >= 4^{floor(rho_n/2)} = 2^{n-O(sqrt(n))}.

The open-set argument is an explicit symmetric-determinantal incidence dimension count intersected with the complete-intersection locus. It is not a compactness modulus. The last row of the pinned construction is exactly the square identity above. Hence polynomial auxiliary dimension in n is impossible for all quadratic inputs in that format.

In the pinned quadratic construction, the multipliers already lie in A_0=k because A is generated over k in degree one. Thus its application does not even require the broader coefficient-centrality lemma: scalar Clifford theory already applies. This is a material contribution limitation.

This is an obstruction to the size of that construction, not a lower bound on all proofs of EGH, all algorithms, arbitrary matrix algebras, or every regular sequence. Pure-square sequences admit degree one. No matching upper bound or explicit all-n family attaining the generic rank threshold is proved.

## The two routes and what they actually achieved

**Filtered resolution route.** The complete characteristic-zero cancellation statement was proved: for the pinned LPP ideal J of any homogeneous ideal I containing a regular sequence of any positive length, there are finitely supported nonnegative integers c_{p,j}, with c_{0,j}=c_{n+1,j}=0, such that

    beta_{p,j}(S/J)-beta_{p,j}(S/I)=c_{p,j}+c_{p+1,j}.

The missing integrality is paid by taking ranks over the division algebra before canceling its dimension. Mermin–Murai's prior pure-powers cancellation theorem completes the argument. High-degree completion and coefficient descent handle shorter sequences and all characteristic-zero fields. This is a standard filtered-complex transfer of accepted machinery, so it is explicitly excluded from substantial-finding status.

**Scalar degeneration route.** The desired coordinate-change/initial-ideal replacement fails already for

    (x^2+yz, y^2+xz, z^2+xy) in C[x,y,z].

It is a regular sequence but its quadratic span contains no nonzero square of a linear form. Every initial ideal containing the three pure squares would equal the pure-square ideal by its Hilbert function; its least-variable square would have to be the leading term of an actual square in the original ideal, a contradiction. Conca's prior theorem already places this no-square class among the exceptions to G-quadraticity. The diagnostic is not new literature and does not rule out arbitrary flat families or chains of deformations.

Continuing this route beyond the scalar failure produced the weaker matrix-corner estimate `rho<=2r-1` and then the stronger Clifford divisibility theorem above. The different routes address different structures: homology of a filtered module versus coefficient-algebra constraints on a square identity.

## Counterexamples and contribution limits

The proof was stressed by allowing specialized B_j, degenerate q, noncentral coefficient fields, odd rank, and arbitrary base extension. The exact proof handles them. Replacing Delta by a matrix algebra invalidates the theorem: the nonzero linear matrix `x_1 E_12` has square zero. Dropping regularity invalidates the crucial coefficient-centrality step. Pure-square regular sequences refute any proposed lower bound applying to every input. These are written algebraic checks, with no numerical experiments or scans.

The closest primary art is substantial. Eisenbud–Peeva–Schreyer (2021), Theorem 2.1, already construct division Clifford algebras for generic combinations and derive large matrix-factorization bounds. Hu–Mori (2023) formalize the quadratic-complete-intersection/Clifford-dual correspondence. The source companion itself cites generalized Clifford methods. The distinction here is the all-specializations statement and its explicit consequence for every auxiliary algebra in the pinned EGH format. In that format the quadratic multipliers are already scalar, so the broader centrality lemma is unnecessary for the application. That distinction does not, by itself, establish a substantial new result in the literature. A fresh proof of familiar Clifford representation constraints plus a generic rank incidence count can still be routine. No search miss is used to override this concern.
