# I201: corrected elliptic covers, but no substantial nomination

2026-10-12. **NO VIABLE SUBSTANTIAL TARGET COMPLETED IN THIS ROUND.** Eight findings remain accepted; two remain. This is an unsealed research record, not either independent acceptance gate.

The [analytic record](../experiments/i201_elliptic_covering/analytic-attempt.md) proves an actual corrected finite-cover theorem. For compact D, V C¹ near D, L,h>0, and

    tr DV=0,      det DV>-L^(-2) on D,

there is, for every epsilon>0, a finite cover of the physical ruled sweep F_h(E(D,V,L)) by cylinders with compact nondegenerate parallelogram perpendicular bases whose total area is at most

    h integral_D [1+2L²(det DV)_+] J_h(V) + epsilon.

This permits elliptic derivatives, arbitrary nilpotent shear, mixed regimes, and arbitrary compact label sets. The proof gives closed-set coverage, an explicit finite retention collar, and C¹ stability within the trace-free class. The determinant correction comes from two nested rectangular additions to a square, not from inflating squares by an operator norm.

This is nevertheless a technical combination of known/accepted operations. Kolountzakis's 1998 extended-square identity supplies the affine geometry, and the accepted ruled-set paper supplies the finite-retention method. The proof identifies precisely how to combine them, including the sign and transpose in the lattice formula. It is not nominated as a ninth finding.

A second geometric construction uses square and hexagonal label tilings after conjugating the elliptic differential to rotation. It proves explicit costs q_4(k), q_6(k), k=L sqrt(det A), and achieves the periodic affine density lower bound 1+k² at k=1/sqrt(3), 1, and sqrt(3). Its nonlinear finite realization includes a mesh choice that absorbs a C¹ Taylor remainder. This sharpness concerns a defined periodic affine model; it does not establish optimality among arbitrary finite cylinder covers or give a new convex-body covering bound.

The independent route sought an optimal common tile for the two endpoint lattices. The primary literature now supplies bounded polytopal common fundamental domains for any two equal-covolume lattices (Grepstad–Kolountzakis, 2025). The analytic record gives a compact polytopal counterexample to the proposed missing inference: a tile can cover both endpoint lattices exactly and still miss an intermediate, strictly denser lattice in the very same elliptic path. Thus endpoint tiling, finite diameter and the determinant inequality alone do not furnish an all-height cover. Existence of a specially chosen optimal all-height tile remains unresolved here.
