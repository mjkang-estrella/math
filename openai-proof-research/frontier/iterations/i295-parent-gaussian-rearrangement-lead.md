# Parent lead for I295 — one-dimensional Gaussian rearrangement

2026-10-13. This is an unreviewed route toward the unpaid G290 of I290, not a solved target or acceptance candidate.

Let v:R^N -> [-1,1], and let v* be its monotone one-dimensional Gaussian rearrangement, preserving its full distribution. Borell's two-set theorem suggests a direct convex-order proof:

1. Shift to f=(1+v)/2 in [0,1]. For an arbitrary output set A, layer-cake f to express integral_A Q_sigma f as integral_0^1 Pr(Y in A, f(X)>t) dt.
2. Borell bounds each integrand by parallel halfspaces of the same measures, attained simultaneously by f* and the appropriate halfspace A*.
3. Taking suprema over sets A of a fixed measure proves the integrated-quantile order Q_sigma f <=cx Q_sigma f*. In particular E psi(Q_sigma v) <= E psi(Q_sigma v*); means and initial information agree.

If justified, this reduces the Gaussian optimization to bounded monotone functions of one real variable. Helly selection, dominated convergence and OU L2 contraction then give compactness/attainment at fixed positive information and compact sigma range. Thus a strictly unattainable cube comparison in this one-dimensional Gaussian class would produce the uniform G290 gap. The exact strictness remains unpaid here.

Primary source inspected: Mossel–Neeman, arXiv:1210.4126v3, https://arxiv.org/html/1210.4126v3, served lines 80–110, especially Theorem 1.1 (two arbitrary sets, parallel halfspaces) and functional Theorem 1.2. Only these statement passages have been read by the parent; their proof must be independently read before reliance. Search hits on unrelated rearrangements of Gaussian fields were not used.

A scalar conditional entropy-power bound is another lead after rearrangement. For a binary variable B generated by v*(Z), scalar Gaussian Z and Z_sigma=sigma Z+sqrt(1-sigma²)Z', conditional EPI and Jensen suggest

I(B;Z_sigma) <= -1/2 log(1-sigma²+sigma² exp(-2 I(B;Z))).

This is stronger than the cube curve for a small-information region, but NOT throughout the target. The needed comparison is exp(-2 psi(sigma a)) < 1-sigma²+sigma² exp(-2 psi(a)). The function exp(-2 psi(sqrt x)) is not globally convex; its second derivative becomes negative near x=1. Hence do not claim the EPI argument proves all of G290. This observation was analytic, not a numerical scan. The multivariate EPI bound carries a dimension factor and is not a dimension-free substitute before rearrangement.

A possible way to extend low-information strictness is rigidity/analytic propagation along the one-dimensional OU flow: equality at one finite time forces the sharp comparison amplitude to remain exponential on an interval, and one would need a rigorous continuation argument to the small-information region. Real analyticity of the required binary entropy flow has NOT been proved here and must not be assumed. Alternatively derive strictness directly from the pinned scalar operations or known Gaussian equality theory.

The complete I290 proof and its marked-singleton bridge must remain intact. A small-amplitude theorem alone is a partial result, not full S128, and all old Borell/EPI machinery must be credited.

## A region where the scalar comparison is paid algebraically

For x=a² put E(x)=psi(sqrt(x)) and H(x)=exp(-2E(x)). With r=sqrt(x), differentiation gives

H''(x)/H(x) = atanh(r)²/r² - [r/(1-r²)-atanh(r)]/(2r³).

The positive series gives atanh(r)>=r and r/(1-r²)-atanh(r)<=r³/(1-r²). Hence H''/H >= 1-1/[2(1-x)]>0 for x<1/2. Thus on every b interval 0<b0<=b1<1/sqrt(2), the proposed scalar EPI bound is strictly below psi(sigma b), uniformly for sigma in a compact subinterval of (0,1). Strong convexity of H supplies an explicit chord gap, so this does not need a Gaussian equality classification. The formulas extend continuously at x=0.

If the rearrangement and EPI steps pass, G290 is therefore paid in this amplitude region. I290's transfer at an intermediate smoothing lambda in (rho,1) would then settle the full arbitrary-kernel S128 for compact parameter rectangles with rho1*a1<1/sqrt(2): choose lambda uniformly strictly between rho1 and min(1,1/(sqrt(2)*a1)), while keeping rho/lambda away from both endpoints. The original half-time choice is not essential, but every transfer and ODE estimate must be rechecked for this unequal split. This is a concrete proposed partial theorem, not a claim that all S128 rectangles are covered. Contribution against the old Borell/EPI tools and I290 must be assessed separately.
