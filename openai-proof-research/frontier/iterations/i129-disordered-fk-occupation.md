In a switch-revealing transcript, exposed edge factors and the remaining partition function occur in \(\phi_\xi(A)\), divided by the **full original** \(Z_\xi\). The remaining partition function cannot erase that denominator. Formula P1 also applies to hitting a disk or a specified strand segment, whether or not that event is a stopping history.

### P129 — one-coordinate posterior and an actual graph example

Fix all other signs. Write \(\phi_-\) for sign \(i=-1\), let \(t=e^{2\theta}-1\), \(p_i^-=\phi_-X_i\), and \(p_i^{-,A}=\phi_-(X_i\mid A)\). Bayes and the normalized single-edge tilt give
\[
\frac{\pi_A(\xi_i=+1\mid\xi_{-i})}{\pi_A(\xi_i=-1\mid\xi_{-i})}
=\frac{1+t p_i^{-,A}}{1+t p_i^-}.
\tag{P2}
\]
Thus the log odds have absolute value at most \(2\theta\), and more precisely at most \(t|p_i^{-,A}-p_i^-|\). Every scalar in P2 comes from separately normalizing the two environments. The same signs are used on repeated sheets; there \(1+tX_i\) is replaced by \(e^{2\theta Y_i}\), so the Bernoulli formula must not be reused.

On the exact \(n=1\) square, wiring top and left contracts their three vertices. The bottom-right vertex is joined to this wire by the two sampled edges. For their odds \(u,v\), the FK configuration weights, after division by a common factor two, are \(2,u,v,uv\). If the first edge is revealed open, then
\[
L(u,v)=\phi_{u,v}(X_1=1)=\frac{u(1+v)}{2+u+v+uv},
\qquad \partial_v L=\frac{u}{(2+u+v+uv)^2}>0.
\tag{P3}
\]
Taking \(u=\sqrt2e^{\pm\theta}\) and \(v=\sqrt2e^{\pm\theta}\), averaging over the still unobserved first sign shows \(\pi_A(\xi_2=+1)>1/2\) for every \(\theta>0\). Moreover \(L(u,v_+)/L(u,v_-)\) depends strictly on \(u\): after removing its positive constant factor, its derivative has numerator \(v_--v_+<0\). Hence the two latent signs are dependent under this posterior. This is an exact calculation in the prescribed physical model, not an auxiliary counterexample or an interface-limit claim. It is a single-edge reveal; no special stopping-line property is assumed.

The entropy cost of the posterior is also explicit:
\[
D(\pi_A\Vert\pi)=E_{\pi_A}\log\frac{\phi_\xi(A)}{Q(A)}
\le\log\frac1{Q(A)}.
\tag{P4}
\]
If signs are exposed successively, the chain rule writes this entropy as the sum of expected binary conditional divergences from a fair sign. For each prefix, its likelihood ratio is the quotient of sums over remaining signs of \(\phi_{+,\mathrm{rest}}(A)\) and \(\phi_{-,\mathrm{rest}}(A)\). The pointwise ratio lies in \([e^{-2\theta},e^{2\theta}]\), so the quotient of sums does too. For binary log odds \(\ell\), differentiation of its divergence from a fair sign gives \(\ell p_\ell(1-p_\ell)\); integrating yields \(D\le\ell^2/8\). Thus
\[
D(\pi_A\Vert\pi)\le \min\{\log(1/Q(A)),\ M\theta^2/2\}.
\tag{P5}
\]
P5 is a bound for biased dependent posteriors, derived from their likelihood, not E126 reapplied to them. It does not vanish in the required rare-hit regime. In particular it provides no uniform conditional Pinsker budget after normalizing a microscopic hit.

### P129-replacement — exact annealed expansion of the whole marked discrepancy

There is a more directed way to use the symmetry of each original sign. For any finite binary law \(P\), any bounded real readout \(F\), and one coordinate of marginal \(p=PX_i\), direct division of the two partition functions gives
\[
\frac12(P_{+\theta e_i}F+P_{-\theta e_i}F)-PF
=b(p,\theta)\operatorname{Cov}_P(F,X_i),
\tag{P6}
\]
\[
b(p,\theta)=
\frac{(\cosh\theta-1)(1-2p)}
{1+2p(1-p)(\cosh\theta-1)}.
\]
Indeed \(P_{t e_i}F=PF+(e^t-1)\operatorname{Cov}_P(F,X_i)/[1+p(e^t-1)]\); averaging the two signs and multiplying the denominators proves P6. This identity is exact at finite \(\theta\), for arbitrary dependence, and retains both normalization factors.

Order the sampled edges deterministically. Put \(s^{(i-1)}=(\theta\xi_1,\ldots,\theta\xi_{i-1},0,\ldots,0)\). Iterating P6 under the **original** independent signs yields
\[
E_\xi P_{\theta\xi}F-P_0F
=\sum_{i=1}^M E_{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{i-1}}
\left[b(P_{s^{(i-1)}}X_i,\theta)
\operatorname{Cov}_{P_{s^{(i-1)}}}(F,X_i)\right].
\tag{P7}
\]
There is no posterior-independence assumption: the signs here are added before sampling or conditioning the configuration. After some tilts, the next marginal need not be \(1/2\), even if all original pure marginals were. Therefore the first-coordinate cancellation does not telescope to zero.

Use the actual bounded readout
\[
F_{n,r,c}(w)=d_m\left(cn^{-5/3}\sum_{e\text{ traversed}}\delta_{\Pi z_e},
T_r\gamma_n(w)\right)\in[0,1].
\]
P7 is an exact expression for \(R_{n,r}(c)-P_0F_{n,r,c}\). It therefore retains the ordered curve, its own counting mark, the whole-plane tube and total mass. A sufficient new estimate would make its right side tend to the appropriate calibrated replacement error. For \(c=c_0\), making the absolute sum tend to zero in the R126 limit order would prove fixed G with that constant; for a different \(c\), the pure discrepancy is retained and must be calibrated too.

The elementary absolute bound still costs volume: \(|b(p,\theta)|\le\cosh\theta-1\) and \(|\operatorname{Cov}(F,X_i)|\le1/4\) for \(0\le F\le1\), giving \(M(\cosh\theta-1)/4\). P7 exposes the possible gain as a product of imbalance \(|1-2p_i|\) and the actual marked influence, but neither factor has a summable bound under the partly tilted laws or hit-conditioned laws in this investigation. The geometric squared-influence estimates in shrinking-window literature concern specific crossings and mixing rates, not \(F_{n,r,c}\)'s microscopic count under fixed disorder.
