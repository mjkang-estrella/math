Parent technical gate-ring check, to audit before use: entries of every source gate and its controlled version lie in R=Z[1/sqrt(2),i]. A bounded circuit on computational-basis input, with retained measurement histories, has every branch amplitude in R; computational-event probabilities lie in Z[1/2,sqrt(2)]. Its intersection with Q is Z[1/2], since rationality forces the sqrt(2) coefficient to vanish. Thus every rational computational-event probability is dyadic, even after summing finitely many ancillary outcomes or adaptive histories. In particular, exact uniform output on M labels is impossible whenever M is not a power of two in this model: a label would have probability 1/M. This rules out simply importing uniform multiplicative-group preparation when its order has an odd factor. It does not rule out a nonuniform distribution on primitive elements or an exact deterministic function output, and is not a new substantial nomination. The computation's bounded worst-case clock is essential; an unbounded rejection sampler escapes the finite-history argument.
