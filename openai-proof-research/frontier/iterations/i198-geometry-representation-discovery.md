## Result and contribution decision

The strongest quantitative deduction is:

> For every integer a≥2, every b≥(a−1)², and every finite-dimensional complex vector space V, the canonical multiplication map
> Sym^b(Sym^a V) → Sym^a(Sym^b V)
> is surjective.

The [analytic record](../experiments/i198_geometry_representation/analytic-attempt.md), Proposition F210, proves this with all endpoints and dependencies. At a=6 it gives canonical surjectivity from b=25 instead of b=30. The proof groups the last derivative transfers into powers and uses injectivity along a whole sl₂ string. Crucially, that exact whole-power lemma already appears in the sixth-power companion's `lem:polarization`; the rest of the induction is the quadratic-stabilization paper's accepted argument. Thus this is a technical combination of accepted lower operations. It is more precise than either displayed headline, but it does not supply a substantive unpaid bridge. Its contribution verdict does not rest on the proof being short or on a missing keyword in a literature search.

A genuinely different route through the Chow normalization, chart denominators and conductor was developed. Its available estimates stop at the already-paid cubic bound a(a−1)². A determinant polynomial gives a nonzero kernel for the attempted stronger ambient polarization identity when j<aL. The record distinguishes this obstruction to the shortcut from a counterexample to better canonical stabilization.

## 095: full nonliftability mechanism and the exact-lift companion

The nonliftability paper's complete sections 01–05 were read: the determinant construction; Hankel rank stratum and finite inverse limits; separation from the closure of Hankel images; the singular polynomial patch; positive moment extension; analytic Gram selection; and finite-order near-feasibility. Its finite-field seed certificate is accepted via the explicit seed lemma and was not rerun or audited. The complete four-part mathematical construction in the exact-lift companion was also read, including the degree-two angular matching identity and the range condition at singular X.

The distinction required by the brief is retained. The explicit 23-variable Choi–Lam example is nonspectrahedral in its original coordinates but has a 100×100 homogeneous lift with 307 auxiliaries. Its degree-20 polynomial has a degree-16 quotient with the same cone. The later high-dimensional Hankel examples exclude every finite affine lift. No result about the former is used as a nonliftability premise.

Three exact outcomes are recorded:

* An affine regularization of the source form, g_τ=g+τI and Q_τ=U^TGg_τGU, has an exact transition: no finite lift for 0≤τ<1, and a spectrahedral cone for τ≥1. The negative side repeats the source's strict residual sign; the positive side is its standard global-Gram block construction. This is a technical consequence, not an unpaid regularization theorem.
* For the more substantive perturbation Q^δ(y)=Q(y)+δ||y||²I, the old patch is infeasible for every δ>0: its A-coordinate has rank N<r while Q^δ(y_s) has rank r. A new patch or obstruction is needed. This target is neither proved nor refuted here.
* The source's moment deformation obeys dist(x_(c,ε),E_Q)≥d_c ε^12. This elementary inverse-matrix estimate does not give an approximation-error versus lift-size lower bound. Uniform control of lift selections and Taylor constants is missing.

Primary literature was checked early. Netzer–Sanyal already supply liftable approximations by smoothing, and Scheiderer proves second-order-cone representability under Nash-smooth boundary hypotheses. These rule out a blanket claim that smoothing preserves nonliftability. They do not answer the specified positive quadratic perturbation above.

## 100: companion-aware screen and a false extension

All four introductions and their actual theorem statements were read. The complete affine-grid and finite nonlinear approximation proofs in the ruled-set paper were then read. That theorem already handles arbitrary compact label sets, positive-area boundaries, measure-zero sets, square-zero derivatives with unrestricted shear, and mixed nilpotent/hyperbolic regimes. The radial companion already includes the apex, prescribed tags and one triangular cylinder per interval; the angular paper already states explicit counts, a controlled remainder, and the cubic-buffer improvement. These were not repackaged as discoveries.

The attempted extension to arbitrary trace-zero derivatives with the same integral cost is false. For V(p)=Jp on a radius-1/10 disk and |s|≤1, the swept slices have area πρ²(1+s²). Volume divided by diameter is strictly larger than πρ², whereas the proposed integral cost is smaller than πρ². Fubini makes this a lower bound for every finite cylinder cover, with arbitrary axes and measurable bases. This is a written counterexample to the extension, not a new tetrahedral-cover theorem. A corrected elliptic theory would need an expansion-dependent cost; no such construction was proved.
