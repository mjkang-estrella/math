## 3. First constructive approach to Q89: regularized energy descent

This approach constructs a canonical point in each action, propagates its exact error into A's sampling estimate, and proves a qualitative tail theorem. The final uniform constant is existential and is clearly separated from Q89's requested explicit value.

For a fixed finite symmetric alphabet S with multiplicities put

    E(x)=|S|^-1 sum_(s in S) d(x,sx)^2.

There is no freeness, properness, cocompactness, separability, local compactness, or finite-dimensional hypothesis in the following lemmas. X is a nonempty complete CAT(0) space.

**Lemma 3.1 (proximal point and tangent defect).** For lambda>0 and o∈X, the function F(y)=E(y)+lambda d(o,y)^2 has a unique minimizer p. Moreover

    E(p)+lambda d(o,p)^2 <= E(o),
    |S|^-1 sum_s <h,log_p(sp)> <= (lambda/2) d(o,p) ||h||           (3.1)

for every h in the completed tangent cone at p.

*Proof.* Along simultaneous geodesics y_t and sy_t, CAT(0) convexity of distance makes d(y_t,sy_t)^2 convex; hence E is continuous and convex. Squared distance to o is strongly convex. A minimizing sequence for F is bounded since F≥lambda d(o,·)^2. For any two terms, the midpoint inequality

    F(mid(y,z)) <= (F(y)+F(z))/2 - (lambda/4)d(y,z)^2

makes the sequence Cauchy. Completeness and continuity give its minimizer; the same inequality gives uniqueness. Comparison with o proves the first inequality.

For a represented tangent vector h take a short geodesic p_t from p with speed ||h||. A's crossed-geodesic convexity calculation and symmetry of S give

    E(p_t) <= |S|^-1 sum_s d(p_(2t),sp)^2.

Minimality of F and first variation imply

    0 <= -4 |S|^-1 sum_s <h,log_p(sp)> -2lambda<h,log_p(o)>.

Cauchy–Schwarz for cone scalar products gives (3.1). Represented directions are dense in the completed tangent cone, and the displayed scalar products are continuous, so the inequality extends to every h. This uses the complete first-variation operation in A, not a Hilbert-space gradient identity. ∎

When E(p)>0, rescale distances by E(p)^-1/2. The normalized vectors v_s now have average squared norm one and satisfy

    average_s <h,v_s> <= b ||h||,
    b = lambda d(o,p)/(2 sqrt(E(p))).                              (3.2)

If E(p)>E(o)/4 and lambda=k^-2, (3.1) gives b≤1/k. Every generator is bounded by A's six-letter estimate regardless of whether p minimizes E. Once the relative-count error is at most 1/10,

    ||v_s||² <=25(1+1/10)/(1-1/10)=275/9 <36;

we may take **M=6**, independent of |S| and the action.

**Lemma 3.2 (quantitative conditional test with a tangent defect).** Suppose average ||v_s||²=1, ||v_s||≤M, and average `<h,v_s>≤b||h||` for all h. Let t matches be coupled as in A's `sample:lem-coupling`, with both TV errors at most eta. For any common-I function h_I of norm at most B, and either fixed inversion convention on the additional field,

    E_match <h_I,v_e>
      <= B sqrt(1/t + b M (t-1)/t + 2M² eta) + 2 B M eta.          (3.3)

*Proof.* Cone cosine law gives

    average_s d(u,v_s)² >= ||u||²+1-2b||u||.

For independent uniform letters, form the inductive means m_1=v_1 and m_j=[m_(j-1),v_j]_(1/j). Every mean lies in the radius-M ball. Squared-distance convexity yields, with a_j=E||m_j||²,

    a_j <= ((j-1)/j)² a_(j-1) + 1/j²
             + 2bM(j-1)/j².

Multiplying by j² and summing gives `a_t≤1/t+bM(t-1)/t`, including t=1. A's cone scalar-product concavity gives pointwise

    t^-1 sum_j <h_I,v_(e_j)> <= <h_I,m_t> <= B||m_t||.

No independence of h_I from the e_j is assumed. The leaf-law TV comparison changes the squared norm expectation by at most 2M²eta, and each branch comparison changes the scalar-product expectation by at most 2BM eta. Cauchy–Schwarz gives (3.3). ∎

**Theorem F89 (qualitative, uniform along the actual source tail).** Fix any one deterministic sequence `(P_j,S_j)` satisfying A's `lem:completion-diagonal`, with its parameters, tables, fragment certificates and original symmetric alphabets fixed. There exists an integer K≥10, depending on this sequence, such that for every j≥K, every isometric action of G_j=π1(P_j) on every nonempty complete CAT(0) space X, and every o∈X,

    E_j(J_(K^-2)o) <= E_j(o)/4.                                  (3.4)

Consequently the iterates o_(m+1)=J_(K^-2)o_m converge to a point z fixed by G_j, and

    E_j(o_m) <= 4^-m E_j(o_0),
    d(o_m,z) <= 2K 2^-m sqrt(E_j(o_0)).                            (3.5)

In particular all sufficiently late **actual source groups** have the fixed-point property for all complete CAT(0) spaces, and admit no proper isometric action on any such space. They are infinite torsion-free hyperbolic groups with finite two-dimensional classifying complexes.

*Proof of (3.4).* Suppose no K works. For every integer k≥10 choose j_k≥k, an action and o_k with `E(p_k)>E(o_k)/4` for the minimizer with lambda=k^-2. Necessarily E(o_k)>0. Normalize at p_k. Lemma 3.1 gives b_k≤1/k; A's six-letter bound gives M=6 after discarding finitely many k. The source diagonal statistics still converge since j_k→∞.

For each *fixed* admissible test and *fixed* t, its TV errors tend to zero. Lemma 3.2 therefore gives limsup of its scalar-product expectation at most B/sqrt(t). Taking t→∞ **after** that limsup recovers exactly A's conditional-test conclusion. All functions needed there have B≤2M for paths of length at most three, or B≤M for the ternary midpoint test. There is no exchange of t with the alphabet-size limit and no dimension-dependent constant.

Apply A's product-of-match-configurations construction to these normalized actions. Its proof uses only complete CAT(0) geometry, the uniform displacement bound and the conditional-test conclusion, not properness, cocompactness, or a zero tangent defect at any finite stage. Single-field limits give unit edges. The path induction gives two-step squared distances at least two and admissible three-step distances at least three. Decoration quadrilaterals force equality for two-step distances. Hexagon midpoint equalities and flat triangles give the common center. The ternary test is passed for each fixed pair of fractions, with its angle computed afterwards in the limit. Thus every item of A's `prop:configuration` follows. A's complete `prop:no-configuration` excludes that configuration in an arbitrary complete CAT(0) space. Contradiction proves (3.4).

*Iteration.* From Lemma 3.1 and (3.4), `d(o_(m+1),o_m)≤K sqrt(E(o_m))≤K2^-m sqrt(E(o_0))`. The geometric tail sum gives (3.5). Completeness gives z. For each s in the **fixed finite** alphabet S_j, `d(o_m,so_m)≤sqrt(|S_j| E(o_m))→0`; continuity implies sz=z. Here |S_j| is fixed before m→∞, and is not asserted uniformly bounded over j. Proper actions have finite point stabilizers, so an infinite G_j cannot act properly with a global fixed point. The source supplies hyperbolicity, asphericity and infinitude. Torsion-freeness follows from the finite-dimensional contractible free G_j-CW model: a nontrivial finite-order cyclic subgroup would have a finite-length free resolution of Z over its group ring, contradicting its nonzero integral cohomology in arbitrarily high even degrees. ∎

**Constants and limits.** K is not computed. It can depend on the chosen sequence and convergence rates of its table statistics, and no rate in j, relator length, finite-index degree or dimension has been established. Formula (3.5) is a geometric iteration given access to the exact proximal map; it is not an algorithm on an arbitrary encoded metric space. For another finite alphabet T, conversion pays

    sqrt(E_(S_j)(x)) <= ( |T| |S_j|^-1 sum_(s in S_j) |s|_T² )^1/2
                         sqrt(E_T(x)).

There is no concealed generating-set independence in the numerical constant. Restriction to subgroups is not automatic for fixed-point properties; no subgroup classification is claimed.

## 4. Second constructive approach to Q89: finite metric-error propagation

This route works directly with one finite product of action configurations, retaining errors until the rigid geometry must be used. It supplies concrete obligations for a quantitative replacement of A's last geometric argument.

Fix a proximal point with normalized tangent defect b, M=6, and single-field TV error eta_0. Put a=2M²eta_0. Every edge squared length in the match product differs from one by at most a. If all needed conditional tests have upper bound tau in (3.3), induction gives

    d(u_0,u_k)² >= k-ka-2(k-1)tau,    1<=k<=3.                    (4.1)

For 0<tau<1 and B=12, a sufficient, completely explicit budget for (3.3)≤tau is

    t >= ceil(16 B²/tau²),
    b <= tau²/(16 B² M),
    eta <= min{ tau²/(32 B² M²), tau/(8 B M) }.                   (4.2)

Indeed the three terms inside the square root are at most `tau²/(16B²)` each, and the outside term at most tau/4, so the result is at most `(sqrt(3)+1)tau/4<tau`. These constants do not depend on a dimension or on the number of generators.

The CAT(0) quadrilateral inequality now gives, for each decoration square,

    2-2a-2tau <= each diagonal square <= 2+6a+2tau,
    distance² between diagonal midpoints <=2a+tau.                (4.3)

For the quadrilateral `(z_0,z_1,z_3,z_4)` inside a core hexagon, its side-square sum is at most `6+14a+4tau`, and each opposite square is at least `3-3a-4tau`. Hence

    opposite square <=3+17a+8tau,
    distance² between these opposite-pair midpoints <=5a+3tau.    (4.4)

*Proof of (4.1)–(4.4).* Translate the penultimate vertex to the basepoint and use A's log comparison. Each step adds an edge square at least 1−a and subtracts at most 2tau. This gives (4.1). In a decoration square both diagonals have the lower bound in (4.3), while the four side squares sum to at most 4+4a. Subtract the other diagonal lower bound for its upper bound; subtract both and divide by four for the midpoint bound. For the hexagon quadrilateral two sides are one-step and two are two-step distances. Use (4.3) to bound their sum; subtract the two opposite lower bounds from (4.1), and divide by four. These are genuine finite-action product estimates, not a hypothetical abstract distance table. ∎

**Unpaid step.** A's exact obstruction next passes to the tangent cone at the common hexagon center, uses exact flat triangles, obtains exact antipodality in a CAT(1) direction space, and splits the parallel set of an actual complete line. Equations (4.3)–(4.4) give nearby centers and approximate distances; they do not supply that line or an approximate splitting estimate. What is needed is an explicit modulus omega(a,tau)→0 that turns these finite errors and finitely many ternary fractional-distance tests into, simultaneously,

    <M_A,M_B> >= -omega(a,tau),
    <M_A,M_B> <= -1+omega(a,tau),

or a finite-distance substitute avoiding tangent vectors. A value with omega<1/2 would close the contradiction. No such proved modulus is supplied here. The obstruction involves the **actual** fixed Fano template, not arbitrary incompatible abstract data.

**Actual counterexample to a failed shortcut.** Let T_epsilon be the compact real tree made of a segment from o to b of length epsilon and two further segments from b to p,q, each of length 1−epsilon. It is a complete CAT(0) space, d(o,p)=d(o,q)=1 and d(p,q)=2−2epsilon. Nevertheless the Alexandrov angle at o between p and q is zero for every epsilon>0: sufficiently short initial segments coincide. In the limit epsilon→0 the pointed trees have opposite rays and angle pi. At fraction t on both segments, the distance is `2 max{t−epsilon,0}`. Thus approximate distances at radius one do **not** control the infinitesimal cone scalar product, even for trees. This refutes only the attempted direct angle-continuity step, not Q89 or a possible finite-distance proof. It explains why A passes fixed fractions through its ultralimit before taking an angle.

The finite counting stage is more accessible. For specified rational alpha,beta,delta put `kappa=min{beta−7alpha,alpha/5,delta}`. A's separator proof permits eta_sep=kappa/4 and, for example,

    K_frag = ceil((4*15*18/kappa)²),

because `2sqrt(2)/(1−sqrt(2/3))<18`. Its coincidence-rank condition for every collection of at most 8K_frag distinct occurrences is a finite test and yields a letter filling constant at most 60/kappa. For a specified T, taking alpha=delta=epsilon, beta=8epsilon with `0<epsilon<1/(196T)` preserves every sampling overlap margin at least 1/2. All these are explicit **after T and the metric tolerances are supplied**. They do not compute the missing geometric tolerance or produce an alphabet-size bound.

**Status of Q89.** The proximal approach proves that some tail constants exist. The finite metric approach pays the probabilistic-to-metric errors but stops at robust exceptional-incidence/parallel-set geometry. Q89 remains **unproved and unrefuted in this report**. We neither assert that an effective certificate search is impossible nor mistake a qualitative finite obstruction for a known uniform bound in presentation length.

## 5. Why F89 is technical despite its stronger statement

The source already contains uniform generator control, total-variation tests for actions selected after the tables, cone inductive averaging, products/ultralimits without properness, and the target-independent exact no-configuration proof. The only missing interface for arbitrary actions is approximate energy stationarity. Lemma 3.1 supplies it by classical strongly convex regularization; Lemma 3.2 is the source's own recurrence with the explicit extra term bM. Once that term tends to zero, the source proof runs unchanged. This is a concrete reduction to lower operations, not a claim that the printed theorem already says fixed points.

The primary comparison confirms the mechanism is old. Gromov's *Random walk in random groups*, §§3.8.C–D, discusses scaling-limit conversion between fixed points, almost fixed points and nonconstant harmonic orbits for classes stable under scaling limits, explicitly including all CAT(0) spaces. Kondo's Lemma 6.4 gives the finite-radius displacement-halving/Cauchy argument; Theorem 6.3 treats classes of complete spaces closed under scaling and ultralimits. These do not provide an effective value of K for A's tables. [Gromov primary](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/6106.pdf), [Kondo retained 2006 preprint](https://www.math.kyoto-u.ac.jp/preprint/2006/17kondo.pdf).

Izeki–Kondo–Nayatani, Proposition 2.1 and Theorem 2.9, turn an energy-gradient gap or an n-step energy deficit into a fixed point; their displayed constant is `2epsilon²/[n²(n−1)²]` in their normalization. Their random-group target classes have additional spectral hypotheses. We do not import their theorem as an all-CAT(0) random-group assertion. Our proximal proof is given in full above and the decisive uniform geometry remains A's stipulated operation. [Primary arXiv:1210.5829v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.5829v1).

Accordingly F89, its fixed-point-radius estimate and Lemma 3.2 are one technical package. They are not a ninth accepted finding. A new title or replacing “no geometric action” by “fixed-point property” does not by itself clear the contribution gate.

## 6. R89: a particular finite-residual word with a quadratic length bound

Fix one valid R triangle complex K and its full finite geometric/incidence data. Choose a label i_0. The subgraph L on V,W containing all r² edges x_(i_0,u,v) has free fundamental group of rank r²−1, and its inclusion in K is injective. Indeed any nonidentity graph loop can be cyclically reduced; at each of its turns it uses distinct same-i_0 directions. R's `lem:links` gives angle at least 14pi/5, and its complete triangle criterion says such a nonempty loop cannot be nullhomotopic. Conjugation back to the original basepoint does not affect injectivity.

Choose a maximal tree of K's one-skeleton containing one edge of L and an edge connecting O. Collapse that tree for the finite presentation of G. Two other L edges give two noncommuting generators u,v of literal length one. Every rectangular word has literal length at most four in this alphabet, even when a tree edge disappears. Keep the full **indexed list**, allowing repetitions of group elements:

    m = N r²(r−1)²,       R_list=(w_1,...,w_m).

Every w_i is nontrivial. For m≥1 put M=2^ceil(log_2 m), with M=1 if m=1. Padding the list to M entries by copies of its final entry adds no hypothesis.

**Lemma 6.1 (three choices suffice).** In a torsion-free hyperbolic group containing noncommuting u,v, for any a,b≠1 at least one of

    [a,b], [a,ubu^-1], [a,vbv^-1]

is nontrivial.

*Proof.* Use the accepted cyclic commensurator lemma `pre:cyclic-commensurator`, whose complete proof was read. For a≠1, C=Comm_G(<a>) is infinite cyclic and contains a. If a nontrivial b commutes with a, then b∈C. For every nontrivial c∈C, its cyclic subgroup is commensurable with <a>, so Comm_G(<c>)=C. If b and sbs^-1 both belong to C, they are commensurable; hence s∈Comm_G(<b>)=C. Therefore if all three displayed commutators were trivial, b∈C and both u,v∈C, contrary to their noncommutation. This proves the lemma without computing C or a boundary axis. ∎

**Theorem R89.** Given the chosen finite K and a total word-problem decider for its maximal-tree presentation, the following finite algorithm outputs a word W with

    W != 1 in G,   W in R_f(G),
    |W| <= (16 M²−4)/3 < (64/3)m²     when m>1,                  (6.1)

and |W|≤4 when m=1. It makes at most `3(M−1)` word-problem calls, each on a word of length at most the non-strict bound in (6.1).

*Construction and proof.* At every node of a balanced binary tree on the padded list, take the two current nontrivial words a,b. Test the conjugators `1,u,v` in that fixed order and take the first nontrivial commutator `[a,sbs^-1]`. Lemma 6.1 ensures success. Its normal closure is contained in each of the normal closures of a and b, since killing either entry kills the commutator. Inductively the root W is nontrivial and lies in the normal closure of **each** original w_i. R's rank/interpolation obstruction says every homomorphism from G to a finite group kills at least one original rectangle, hence kills W. Thus W∈R_f(G).

For level-zero length L_0=4, each node has literal length at most `L_(h+1)=4L_h+4`, since the conjugator has length at most one. Solving gives `L_h=(16*4^h−4)/3`. There are M−1 internal nodes and at most three tests at each; shorter levels have smaller bounds. With 4^h=M² this proves (6.1). No free reduction is needed for the upper bound. ∎

For an arbitrary supplied generating set T, specify words for the maximal-tree generators and let D be their maximum T-length; substitution gives |W|_T≤D(16M²−4)/3. The source parameters r,q,N, exceptional set, all cell data, the generating set and this D must be fixed before interpreting this bound. The bound is independent of finite-quotient order and representation dimension. It is not a bound in the length of an arbitrary input presentation.

**Algorithmic limits.** The total word-problem decider is an explicit input to this formulation. Its existence follows from source hyperbolicity, but the bound on the number and length of calls is not a bound on their time. No universal running-time function of presentation length is asserted. The source does not print a manageable finite K; this report does not claim to have computed its enormous table or the literal root W. The output specification is an algorithm on each actual supplied valid source object. An exhaustive finite search for incidence data is possible because the conditions are decidable and the source supplies existence; no useful numerical stopping bound is extracted here. Identifying an original w_i in R_f(G) remains a different problem: killing each rectangle in some finite quotient is positively searchable, while being killed in *every* finite quotient is not certified by waiting for that search to fail.

**Contribution.** Bou-Rabee–McReynolds, §3 and the complete proof of Proposition 4.1, already build common normal multiples by padding a list, conjugating pairs so they do not commute, and forming a balanced commutator tree, with quadratic length in the list size. Lemma 6.1 supplies the same operation in a torsion-free hyperbolic group using the stipulated cyclic-commensurator property and two fixed noncommuting generators. The source supplies the actual list and its obstruction. This is a concrete classical reduction, not a new classification of non-RF hyperbolic groups.
