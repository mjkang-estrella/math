## What I165 established

The universal square-step inequality (M),
$P_{ux}+P_{vy}+D_{11}\ge N_{00}+2$, is **false** in the exact original
class. A certified admissible square expansion takes a 50-vertex graph
with 576 Hamiltonian cycles to a 54-vertex graph with 572:
$N_{00}=138$ versus $P_{ux}+P_{vy}+D_{11}=52+38+44=134$. An explicit
prism-spliced family gives gain $76-8m$ for every even $m\ge4$,
negative from $m=10$ and unbounded below. Both decisive graphs carry
full certificates: simplicity, cubicity, bipartition, 3-vertex-connectivity
by exhaustive deletion checks, sphere rotation systems with Euler
characteristic two, and odd residual boundary paths. Two independent
enumerators agree on the complete cycle sets, and all six bijection
fibres of the I164 square-expansion identity check as exact sets.

This refutes the all-step induction, **not** Q164: the target
$h(G)\ge |V(G)|/2+2$ remains unproved and unrefuted, and the
counterexample graphs comfortably satisfy it.

Paid supporting results retained for reuse:

- A recoverable single-edge deletion/addition bijection proves the
  restricted inequality when either residual boundary path has length
  one.
- The affine splice formula $d(G_1\star G_2)=d(G_1)+d(G_2)-2$, with
  all three omitted-port classes nonempty (paid by the stipulated
  source $P_4$ contract), preserves maximal Hamiltonian-cycle affine
  dimension across splicing. With GSW tight-cut decomposition this
  reduces the full-affine-span route — and, by the original splice
  inequality, numerical Q164 itself — to cyclically 4-edge-connected
  pieces (cubic planar braces). The brace theorem itself was **not**
  obtained.
- The counterexample graphs have full affine dimensions (26 and 28),
  so the (M) obstruction does not obstruct the affine route.
