# Strict decrease of the squared Bessel-zero gap quotients

Ordinary proof candidate, 2026-11-06 UTC. No independent review, novelty clearance,
formal certificate, acceptance or submission is asserted.

## Literal target and source

Brix–Canuto–Dahmen, *Legendre-Gauss-Lobatto grids and associated nested dyadic
grids*, arXiv:1311.0028, Conjecture 3, printed pp17–18 (actual downloaded body):
with j_k the kth positive zero of J_1, and j_0=0, the quotients

    q_k = (j_(k+1)^2-j_k^2)/(j_k^2-j_(k-1)^2)

"decrease monotonically when k ∈ N increases." Here N={1,2,...}. We prove
q_(k+1)<q_k for EVERY positive integer k. This resolves this Bessel conjecture,
not the stronger finite-degree LGL-grid Conjecture 2 discussed before it.
The zero-order is fixed at 1 because that is the full source target, not a
selected parameter instance of a broader target.

## Classical inputs

Use the defining power series
J_1(x)=(x/2) sum_(m>=0) (-1)^m (x^2/4)^m/[m!(m+1)!], its Bessel ODE, and
existence, simplicity, ordering and leading asymptotics of its positive zeros:

    j_n = π(n+1/4)+o(1), n→∞.

These are classical facts, not new contributions. The actual DLMF §10.21(i)
and §10.21(vi), equation 10.21.19, were read; only the leading zero asymptotic
is needed. No *uniform* asymptotic error estimate is assumed from that formula.
The explicit error estimate below is proved for all zeros by an ODE identity.
All integrals below are ordinary improper real integrals, absolutely convergent
whenever absolute values are used. Also use 3<π<4 and 49/5<π²<10.

## 1. First zero exceeds 3

Put y=x². For 0<x≤3, the alternating tail of the power series after its cubic
term in y is nonnegative (successive magnitudes decrease from that point).
Consequently

    2J_1(x)/x ≥ P(y):=1-y/8+y²/192-y³/9216.

For 0≤y≤9, P'(y)=-1/8+y/96-y²/3072≤-1/32<0, whereas
P(9)=2007/9216>0. Thus J_1 has no positive zero ≤3, so j_1>3.

## 2. A uniform squared-zero estimate

Write a=3/4, u(x)=sqrt(x)J_1(x). Direct substitution into the Bessel ODE gives

    u''+(1-a/x²)u=0.

For x≥j_1 choose a continuous phase θ with
u=R sin θ, u'=R cos θ, R=sqrt(u²+u'²)>0, and θ(j_1)=π.
The phase initialization is compatible with R>0: J_1 is positive before its first zero, and simplicity gives J_1'(j_1)<0, hence u'(j_1)<0.
There is no simultaneous zero of u,u' by uniqueness for this regular ODE.
Differentiating the phase gives

    θ'=1-(a/x²)sin²θ>0.

Hence θ(j_n)=nπ for all n≥1. Since θ'-1 is integrable at infinity,
θ(x)-x has a limit L. Along j_n the classical leading zero asymptotic gives
L=-π/4. Integrating θ'-1 therefore yields the EXACT identity

    θ(x)=x-π/4+I(x),
    I(x)=a ∫_x^∞ sin²θ(t)/t² dt.

In particular, for j=j_n and β=π(n+1/4), β=j+I(j), and
0≤I(j)≤a/j. We now estimate I more accurately, without a nonuniform expansion.
Set C=∫_j^∞ cos(2θ(t))/t² dt. Then I(j)=a/(2j)-(a/2)C.
The phase equation gives the exact differential identity

    cos(2θ)= (sin(2θ))'/2 + (a/t²)sin²θ cos(2θ).

Integrating by parts, the endpoint term is zero because sin(2θ(j))=0 and
t^(-2)sin(2θ(t))→0. Thus

    C = ∫_j^∞ sin(2θ(t))/t³ dt
        + a ∫_j^∞ sin²θ(t) cos(2θ(t))/t⁴ dt,
    |C| ≤ 1/(2j²)+a/(3j³),
    |I(j)-a/(2j)| ≤ a/(4j²)+a²/(6j³).

As β=j+I(j),

    |j²-(β²-a)|
      = |a-2jI(j)-I(j)²|
      ≤ a/(2j)+4a²/(3j²)
      = 3/(8j)+3/(4j²) < 1/j.                    (A)

Here j>3. Moreover β-j=I(j)≤a/j<1/4<π/4, so j_n>nπ.
Consequently, putting s_n=j_n², we have FOR EVERY n≥1

    s_n = π²(n+1/4)²-3/4+e_n,
    |e_n| < 1/(π n).                             (B)

This step is the uniform analytic ingredient; the mere existence of an
asymptotic expansion would not justify the argument for all indices.

## 3. Strict log-concavity of all the gaps after the first

Let d_n=s_n-s_(n-1)>0. For n≥2, set

    L_n=π²(2n-1/2), δ_n=e_n-e_(n-1),
    d_n=L_n+δ_n, |δ_n|≤E_n:=2/[π(n-1)].

For m≥3 the unperturbed determinant is exactly

    L_m²-L_(m-1)L_(m+1)=4π⁴.

Expansion, retaining the nonnegative term δ_m², gives

    d_m²-d_(m-1)d_(m+1)
      ≥4π⁴-2L_m E_m-L_(m-1)E_(m+1)-L_(m+1)E_(m-1)
             -E_(m-1)E_(m+1).

Since L_n<2π²n, m/(m-1)≤3/2, and (m+1)/(m-2)≤4 for m≥3,
the three linear losses are at most 12π, 4π, 16π respectively.
The last loss is at most 4/[π²(m-2)m]≤4/(3π²). Therefore

    d_m²-d_(m-1)d_(m+1) ≥4π⁴-32π-4/(3π²)>0.       (C)

For the last strict sign, 3<π<4 gives the lower bound 324-128-4/27>0.
No finite scan or eventual-only assertion occurs here.

## 4. The origin convention and the first quotient comparison

Formula (B) is not imposed at n=0; the source stipulates s_0=0.
For n=1,2,3, use (B), 49/5<π²<10 and |e_n|<1/(3n) to obtain the
following deliberately coarse RATIONAL enclosures:

    13<s_1<16,    48<s_2<51,    102<s_3<105.

For example, the lower/upper expressions are
(49/5)(n+1/4)²-3/4-1/(3n) and
10(n+1/4)²-3/4+1/(3n); direct rational arithmetic gives the six displayed
inequalities. Hence d_1<16, d_2>32 and d_3<57, and

    d_2²-d_1 d_3 > 32²-16·57=112>0.              (D)

Combining (C) and (D), d_(k+1)²>d_k d_(k+2) for every k≥1.
Division by positive d_k d_(k+1) gives q_k>q_(k+1). QED.
