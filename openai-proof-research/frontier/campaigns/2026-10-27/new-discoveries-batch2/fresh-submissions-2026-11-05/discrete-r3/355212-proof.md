## Literal source and quantifiers

Barrett–Cameron–Evans–Hall–Kempton, *On the Laplacian spread of digraphs*, arXiv:2206.15410v1 (30 June 2022), Section 2 pp.2–3, Section 6 Conjecture 6.1 p.15. The current abstract retrieved 6 November 2026 UTC / 5 November Pacific still identifies v1. Journal reference located: Linear Algebra Appl. 666 (2023),126–146, DOI10.1016/j.laa.2023.01.016; journal full body has NOT been compared. The PDF is saved and hashed in sources/receipts.json.

For every finite unweighted simple loopless digraph Γ of order n>=2, with equal in- and out-degree at every vertex, its source spread is not strictly between 0 and 1. No connectedness, strong connectedness, regularity or absence of opposite arcs is assumed. The source defines L=D−A and Wr(L)={z*Lz:z in C^n, z*1=0, ||z||=1}; its spread is the maximum real part minus the minimum real part. It is NOT the diameter of the complex spectrum. At n=1 Wr is empty (explicit in the source); there is no defined positive spread to address.

We prove the stronger dichotomy: the spread is zero or at least one. We use no upper-spread theorem from the source, including its potentially problematic Section 4 equivalences.

## Real quadratic-form bridge

Write 1 for the all-ones vector, C=A+A^T, and H=(L+L^T)/2=D−C/2. Balance implies C1=2D1 and H1=0. H is real symmetric and H_ii=d_i, the integer out-degree. For every real x, x^TLx=x^THx. For complex z=x+iy,

    Re(z*Lz)=x^THx+y^THy.

If z is orthogonal to 1, both x,y are. If ||z||=1 the last expression is a convex combination of the Rayleigh values at x/||x|| and y/||y||, omitting zero parts. Conversely every real admissible vector is complex admissible. Thus the extrema of Re Wr(L) are exactly the extrema of x^THx on the real unit sphere of 1-perp. This sphere is nonempty and compact for n>=2, so both extrema exist. Let their difference be W. Every difference of two real admissible Rayleigh values has absolute value at most W.

## Unequal degree case

For every i, put p_i=e_i−1/n. Then p_i is in 1-perp, ||p_i||^2=(n−1)/n, and p_i^THp_i=H_ii=d_i because H1=0. Hence the Rayleigh value at p_i/||p_i|| is n*d_i/(n−1). If two degrees differ, their integer difference has absolute value at least one, giving

    W >= n/(n−1) > 1.

Consequently a possible W<1 must have all degrees equal to an integer d.

## Regular case: integer column differences

Now H=dI−C/2 and C is symmetric, integer, zero-diagonal, with constant row sum 2d. Its off-diagonal entries lie in {0,1,2}.

First suppose all off-diagonal entries of C equal c. Then C=c(J−I) and H acts as the scalar d+c/2 on 1-perp. All Rayleigh values are equal, so W=0. This also covers n=2, since there is only one off-diagonal pair.

Otherwise there exist distinct i,j and k outside {i,j} with C_ki != C_kj. Indeed, if no such triple exists, then for each k all off-diagonal entries in row k are equal. Symmetry equates these row constants, making every off-diagonal entry equal. For n=2 the nonconstant alternative is impossible.

Choose i,j with a nonzero difference outside those two positions. Define w=e_i−e_j and define the integer vector v by v_i=v_j=0 and

    v_k=C_ki−C_kj  (k outside {i,j}).

Since the i-th and j-th column sums of C coincide and C_ii=C_jj=0, C_ij=C_ji, we have sum_k v_k=0. Thus v lies in 1-perp. It is nonzero and integer with sum zero, so it has at least one positive coordinate >=1 and one negative coordinate <=−1. In particular s=||v||^2>=2. Also w is in 1-perp, ||w||^2=2 and w^Tv=0.

Coordinate by coordinate,

    Cw=v−C_ij*w.

By symmetry, w^TCv=(Cw)^Tv=s. Set u=w/sqrt(2) and t=v/sqrt(s). These are orthonormal real vectors in 1-perp, and

    u^TCt=s/sqrt(2s)=sqrt(s/2)>=1.

The two vectors z_plus=(u+t)/sqrt(2), z_minus=(u−t)/sqrt(2) are unit vectors in 1-perp. Expanding their quadratic forms gives

    z_plus^TCz_plus − z_minus^TCz_minus = 2*u^TCt >= 2.

Since H=dI−C/2, the corresponding H Rayleigh values differ in the reverse direction by at least one. Therefore W>=1.

Together the unequal-degree case and regular alternatives prove Conjecture6.1 for its entire stated domain. No finite enumeration, conjectural helper, unproved combinatorial classification or spectral-integrality assumption is used.

## Checks and objections

The first attempted generic integer-matrix shortcut would be invalid: compression to 1-perp need not have integer entries in an orthonormal basis, and eigenvalues need not be integers. The proof avoids it by constructing integer v in ambient coordinates and explicitly normalizing two orthogonal vectors. A different shortcut using only projected diagonal values gives no bound in the regular case; the column-difference argument supplies precisely that missing case.

Exact diagnostics in check_gap.py check every symmetric zero-diagonal {0,1,2} matrix through order5 having constant row sum. Among 140 nonconstant regular inputs, 1,128 nonzero pair checks verify sum(v)=0, s>=2, Cw=v−C_ij*w and w^TCv=s. Adversarial objections requiring parent review:

1. Source fidelity: retain the restricted numerical range and balance condition. Ordinary adjacency spectral spread or nonnormal eigenvalue spread would be a different theorem. The complex-to-real bridge above is essential.
2. Quantifiers: no regularity assumption can be silently added; the projected-coordinate case is essential. n=2 is scalar; n=1 has undefined source spread.
3. Significance: this uses elementary linear algebra and an integer zero-sum vector. It resolves a literal published-source conjecture if new, but shortness and possible subsumption by a general matrix-spread inequality are serious objections. An open label alone does not clear them.
