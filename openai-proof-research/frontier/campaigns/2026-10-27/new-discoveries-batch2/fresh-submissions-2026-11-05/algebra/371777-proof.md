# MathDB 371777: proof of the Arslan–Ahmia–Alemdar symmetry conjecture

Status: complete ordinary proof relative to two named classical theorems, MacMahon's q-multinomial theorem and the Garsia–Gessel/Stanley shuffle theorem for maj. Not independently reviewed. No Lean. Credit 0 until independent review and the root's acceptance.

## 1. Source statement and literal scope

Source: H. Arslan, M. Ahmia, N. Alemdar, *Signed Mahonian Polynomials on Colored Derangements*, arXiv:2512.02404v2 (10 Jan 2026), Conjecture 2.6, printed p. 8 (`sources/371777.pdf`, sha256 52da8c99…71ef). The definitions used are on pp. 2–4: eqs. (1)–(3) and (6), Theorem 1.1 (Adin–Roichman), and Table 2 on p. 7.

For integers c, n ≥ 1, G_{c,n} = Z_c ≀ S_n is the group of colored permutations π = π_1^{[t_1]} ⋯ π_n^{[t_n]}, with |π| = π_1⋯π_n ∈ S_n and t_i ∈ {0,…,c−1}. Let col(π) = Σ t_i. The source defines:

* inv~(π) = c·inv(|π|) + col(π)  (eq. (6));
* fmaj(π) = c·maj(π) + col(π)  (eq. (2)), where maj(π) = Σ{i ∈ [n−1] : π_i > π_{i+1}}, computed in the total order (3):
  1^{[c−1]} < ⋯ < n^{[c−1]} < ⋯ < 1^{[1]} < ⋯ < n^{[1]} < 1 < ⋯ < n.

**Conjecture 2.6.** For all c, n ≥ 1, Σ_{π∈G_{c,n}} t^{inv~(π)} q^{fmaj(π)} = Σ_{π∈G_{c,n}} q^{inv~(π)} t^{fmaj(π)}.

The source verified this by computer for n = 2 with c ≤ 7, and for G_{3,3} and G_{4,3}. It proves only the much easier variant fmaj_F = c·maj(|π|) + col (Proposition 2.7), in which colors do not affect descents.

**MathDB wording.** The MathDB record calls G_{c,n} "the group of colored derangements", but its displayed identity sums over G_{c,n}. Derangements do not form a group, and the derangement reading is false already for c = 1, n = 3: D_3 = {231, 312} has (inv, maj) = (2, 2) and (2, 1). See `diag/colored_derange.py`, which finds asymmetry in every tested (c, n). We prove the source's statement, which is also the MathDB displayed identity over G_{c,n}. The derangement misreading is a wording defect and earns no credit.

## 2. Encoding

Put u_i := c − t_i ∈ {1,…,c}. Color 0 gives u_i = c and color c−1 gives u_i = 1. Order (3) is exactly the lexicographic order on keys (u_i, π_i): a lower group number comes first, ties are broken by value. Write σ = |π|. The map π ↦ (σ, u) is a bijection G_{c,n} → S_n × [c]^n. Moreover

    col(π) = Σ(c − u_i) = cn − Σ_i u_i,
    inv~(π) = c·inv σ + col(π),
    fmaj(π) = c·maj_u(σ) + col(π),

where for a word u and σ ∈ S_n,

    maj_u(σ) := Σ { i ∈ [n−1] : (u_i, σ_i) >_lex (u_{i+1}, σ_{i+1}) }.

(The replay checks this encoding against all 18 rows of source Table 2.)

For a weak composition m = (m_1,…,m_c) of n, let R(m) be the set of words in [c]^n with exactly m_g letters equal to g. Then col depends only on m: col = Σ_g (c − g) m_g. Hence

    Z_{c,n}(t,q) := Σ_{π} t^{inv~ π} q^{fmaj π} = Σ_m (tq)^{Σ_g (c−g) m_g} · B_m(t^c, q^c),     (2.1)
    B_m(T,Q) := Σ_{u∈R(m)} Σ_{σ∈S_n} T^{inv σ} Q^{maj_u σ}.

Since (tq)^k is symmetric, Conjecture 2.6 follows once each B_m(T,Q) is shown to be symmetric. We prove a closed formula.

## 3. Notation and classical inputs

For a word w in a totally ordered alphabet, Des w = {i : w_i > w_{i+1}}, maj w = Σ_{i ∈ Des w} i, and inv w = #{i < j : w_i > w_j}. For σ ∈ S_n, imaj σ := maj(σ^{-1}), with σ^{-1} written as a word. [n; m]_q denotes the q-multinomial coefficient [n]_q! / ∏_g [m_g]_q!. For k ≥ 0 set

    A_k(T,Q) := Σ_{ω∈S_k} T^{imaj ω} Q^{maj ω}.

The involution ω ↦ ω^{-1} shows **A_k(T,Q) = A_k(Q,T)**.

**(M) MacMahon.** For every weak composition α of n, Σ_{w∈R(α)} q^{inv w} = Σ_{w∈R(α)} q^{maj w} = [n; α]_q. (MacMahon 1916; Andrews, *The Theory of Partitions*, Thm 3.6; Stanley, EC1, Prop. 1.7.1 and §1.7.)

**(GG) Shuffle theorem.** Let x^{(1)},…,x^{(c)} be words of lengths m_1,…,m_c whose n letters are pairwise distinct elements of a totally ordered set. For ν ∈ R(m), let sh_ν(x) be the word whose i-th letter is the next unused letter of x^{(ν_i)}. Then

    Σ_{ν∈R(m)} q^{maj sh_ν(x)} = q^{Σ_g maj x^{(g)}} · [n; m]_q.

References: Garsia–Gessel, Adv. Math. 31 (1979), Thm 3.1; Stanley, Mem. AMS 119 (1972), Prop. 12.6; bijective proof by Novick, Electron. J. Combin. 17 (2010) R64. The case c = 2 is the usual statement. The general case follows by induction: since all letters are distinct, ν ↦ sh_ν(x) is injective, and a shuffle of c words is uniquely a two-word shuffle of (a shuffle of the first c−1 words) with x^{(c)}. Then use [n; m] = [n; n−m_c, m_c]·[n−m_c; m_1,…,m_{c−1}].

**Lemma 1 (Foata–Schützenberger, descent-class form).** For every D ⊆ [n−1],

    Σ_{σ∈S_n, Des σ = D} T^{inv σ} = Σ_{σ∈S_n, Des σ = D} T^{imaj σ}.

*Proof (from (M)).* Fix S ⊆ [n−1] with elements s_1 < ⋯ < s_{k−1}, and let I_1,…,I_k be the consecutive position intervals cut at S, with lengths α = (α_1,…,α_k). If Des σ ⊆ S, then σ is increasing on each I_j. Define w ∈ R(α) by w_v = j iff σ^{-1}(v) ∈ I_j. Then σ ↦ w is a bijection from {Des σ ⊆ S} onto R(α).

(i) An inversion i < j, σ_i > σ_j has i, j in different intervals, I_a ∋ i and I_b ∋ j with a < b. With values v = σ_j < v' = σ_i this means w_v = b > a = w_{v'}, an inversion of w. Conversely every inversion of w arises this way. So inv σ = inv w.

(ii) σ^{-1}(v) > σ^{-1}(v+1) iff w_v > w_{v+1}: in the same interval the positions increase with the values, and positions in different intervals compare by interval index. So Des σ^{-1} = Des w, and imaj σ = maj w.

By (M), Σ_{Des σ ⊆ S} T^{inv σ} = [n; α]_T = Σ_{Des σ ⊆ S} T^{imaj σ} for every S. Möbius inversion on the Boolean lattice gives the exact classes. ∎

## 4. Main theorem

**Theorem.** For every weak composition m of n into c parts,

    B_m(T,Q) = [n; m]_T · [n; m]_Q · ∏_{g=1}^{c} A_{m_g}(T,Q).

Hence B_m(T,Q) = B_m(Q,T). By (2.1) Conjecture 2.6 holds for all c, n ≥ 1, with the closed form

    Σ_{π∈G_{c,n}} t^{inv~ π} q^{fmaj π} = Σ_m (tq)^{Σ_g (c−g) m_g} [n; m]_{t^c} [n; m]_{q^c} ∏_g A_{m_g}(t^c, q^c).

By Lemma 1, with maj ω a function of Des ω, A_k(T,Q) = Σ_{ω∈S_k} T^{inv ω} Q^{maj ω} as well.

*Proof.*

**Step 1 (replace inv by imaj).** Fix u and let E(u) = {i : u_i = u_{i+1}}. A lex descent at i occurs exactly when u_i > u_{i+1}, or when i ∈ E(u) and σ_i > σ_{i+1}. Thus

    maj_u(σ) = Σ_{i: u_i>u_{i+1}} i + Σ_{i ∈ E(u) ∩ Des σ} i

depends on σ only through Des σ. Grouping by Des σ and applying Lemma 1 class by class:

    B_m(T,Q) = Σ_{u∈R(m)} Σ_{σ∈S_n} T^{imaj σ} Q^{maj_u σ}.      (4.1)

**Step 2 (a bijection).** For u ∈ R(m) let P_g = {i : u_i = g}, with elements P_g[1] < ⋯ < P_g[m_g]. Define

    Θ(u, σ) := (u, ν, ω_1, …, ω_c),
    ν_v := g iff v ∈ σ(P_g)          (so ν ∈ R(m)),
    ω_g := std(σ_{P_g[1]}, …, σ_{P_g[m_g]}) ∈ S_{m_g}.

Θ is a bijection R(m) × S_n → R(m) × R(m) × ∏_g S_{m_g}. Its inverse sends P_g[k] to the ω_g(k)-th smallest element of V_g = {v : ν_v = g}. (Both sides have |R(m)|·n! elements.)

**Step 3 (maj_u in terms of (u, ω)).** Let M_g = m_1 + ⋯ + m_g. The rank of the key (u_i, σ_i) among all n keys is M_{g−1} + ω_g(k) when i = P_g[k]. So the word of key ranks is τ = sh_u(y), with y^{(g)} := (M_{g−1} + ω_g(1), …, M_{g−1} + ω_g(m_g)). Lex descents of the keys are exactly descents of τ, so maj_u(σ) = maj sh_u(y). Also maj y^{(g)} = maj ω_g.

**Step 4 (imaj σ in terms of (u, ν, ω)).** Read σ^{-1} as a word indexed by values v = 1,…,n. Its letters at the values in V_g, in increasing order of v, form

    x^{(g)} := (P_g[ω_g^{-1}(1)], …, P_g[ω_g^{-1}(m_g)]),

because the k-th smallest element of V_g is σ(P_g[ω_g^{-1}(k)]). Hence σ^{-1} = sh_ν(x), and imaj σ = maj sh_ν(x). The words x^{(g)} depend only on (u, ω), and their letter sets P_g partition [n]. Since x^{(g)} is order-isomorphic to ω_g^{-1}, maj x^{(g)} = maj ω_g^{-1} = imaj ω_g.

**Step 5 (two shuffle sums).** Transport (4.1) through Θ:

    B_m = Σ_{u} Σ_{ω} Q^{maj sh_u(y(ω))} Σ_{ν∈R(m)} T^{maj sh_ν(x(u,ω))}.

By (GG) the inner sum equals T^{Σ_g imaj ω_g} [n; m]_T, which is independent of u. Applying (GG) again to the words y^{(g)} (distinct letters) gives Σ_u Q^{maj sh_u(y(ω))} = Q^{Σ_g maj ω_g} [n; m]_Q. Therefore

    B_m = [n; m]_T [n; m]_Q Σ_{ω_1,…,ω_c} ∏_g T^{imaj ω_g} Q^{maj ω_g} = [n; m]_T [n; m]_Q ∏_g A_{m_g}(T,Q). ∎

## 5. Remarks on the mechanism and its scope

* Symmetry fails for a fixed colour assignment. With colours attached to values, Σ_σ T^{inv σ} Q^{maj(ρσ)} is symmetric only when ρ is the identity or the reversal (exhaustive for n ≤ 5, `diag/two_orders.py`). Symmetry also fails for fixed (u, ω), because the inv-version of the shuffle theorem is false for non-interval letter sets: shuffles of 13 and 2 give 1 + 2T, not 1 + T + T². The proof works only by aggregating over all colour words of a fixed content, and by moving inv to imaj with Lemma 1 before applying (GG).
* The theorem refines the conjecture: symmetry holds separately for every colour content m, not only after summing.
* c = 1 recovers the classical symmetry of (inv, maj) on S_n.
