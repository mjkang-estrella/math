19:                                                We assume our matroids are finite throughout the paper. A matroid is modular
20:                                            if every pair of flats of the matroid is modular, and hypermodular if every pair
21:                                            of maximal proper flats is modular. Modularity and hypermodularity of a matroid
22:                                            both are equal in rank 3, but different in higher ranks. Kantor made a conjecture
23:                                            about the modular extensions of hypermodular matroids [Kan74], and Bonin later
24:                                            rephrased it [Bon11].
25:                                            Conjecture 1.1 (Kantor’s Conjecture 1974). Every matroid that is hypermodular
26:                                            is strongly embeddable in a modular matroid.
27:                                            Conjecture 1.2 (Hypermodular Matroid Conjecture 2011). Every hypermodular
28:                                            matroid is a restriction of a modular matroid of the same rank.
29:                                               A matroid M is called sticky if for every two matroids M1 and M2 with M =
30:                                            M1 |E(M) = M2 |E(M) there is a matroid M̃ with M1 = M̃ |E(M1 ) and M2 = M̃ |E(M2 )
31:                                            where E(N ) denotes the ground set of N . In 1982, Poljak and Turzík [PT82] showed
32:                                            that every modular matroid is a sticky matroid and conjectured the converse holds.
33:                                            Conjecture 1.3 (Sticky Matroid Conjecture 1982). Stickiness of a matroid implies
34:                                            modularity. Thus, stickiness is equivalent to modularity.
35:                                              If Kantor’s conjecture holds in rank 4, the sticky matroid conjecture holds due to
36:                                            an argument of Bachem, Kern, and Bonin, [BK88, Bon11]. Recently, Hochstättler
37:                                            and Wilhelmi [HW19] showed the sticky matroid conjecture and Kantor’s conjecture
38:                                            both are equivalent. In this paper, we prove Kantor’s conjecture in rank 4 and hence
39:                                            both the sticky matroid conjecture and the whole Kantor’s conjecture.

570: 7.2    Antipalindromes
571: Another generalization concerns antipalindromes. Define a map from Σ∗2 to Σ∗2 by changing
572: every 0 to 1 and vice-versa; this is denoted w. Thus, for example, 010 = 101. An antipalin-
573: drome is a word w such that w = wR . It is easy to see that if a word w is an antipalindrome,
574: then it must be of even length. We call an integer n an antipalindrome if its canonical base-2
575: expansion is an antipalindrome. The first few antipalindromic integers are
576: 
577:                 2, 10, 12, 38, 42, 52, 56, 142, 150, 170, 178, 204, 212, 232, 240, . . . ;
578: 
579: they form sequence A035928 in the OEIS. Evidently every antipalindrome must be even.
580:     To use NWAs to prove results about antipalindromes, we slightly modify our machine
581: structure. While processing the second portion of the input string, we complement the
582: number of ones we guessed in the first portion. Assume we are working with n summands,
583: and let the input symbol be q ∈ {e, f }, and the state at the top of the stack be fx,y . The
584: state si has a transition to sj if i + (n − y) produces an output bit corresponding to q and a
585: new carry of j. We also set the initial state to be f0,0 , because the least significant bit of an
586: antipalindrome must be 0.
