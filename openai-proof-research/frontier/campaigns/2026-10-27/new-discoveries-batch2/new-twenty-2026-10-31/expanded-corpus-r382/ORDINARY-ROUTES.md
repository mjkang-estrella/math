## 336510 / 336511: canonical antipalindromes, multiple routes
Source1706.10206v3 §7.2 Conj13/14 matches both literal statements and the27 exceptions; §7.3 concerns leading-zero generalized antipalindromes and cannot substitute for canonical expansions. No mathematical code was used.

For a canonical antipalindrome of length2k with top-half integer u (2^(k-1)<=u<2^k), its exact value is P_k(u)=2^k u+(2^k-1)-rev_k(u). This follows because the low half is the bitwise complement of the reversal of the high half. The leading1 forces trailing0, as required. Consequently every permitted summand is positive and even. Zero is represented by the empty sum, not by silently calling its canonical digit0 an antipalindrome.

First proposed all-length induction: wrap a shorter word w of length2k by 1,w,0. Complement-of-reversal symmetry is preserved and the value becomes2^(2k+1)+2w. But wrapping r summands maps N=sum w_i to 2N+r*2^(2k+1), with the SAME middle length needed for all summands. This covers separated arithmetic bands and does not cover every even integer of the next lengths with r<=4. Arbitrarily padding w with leading zeroes destroys the canonical condition and is exactly the invalid generalized-antipalindrome shortcut.

Second mechanism: use the full P_k(u) freedom, split the desired number into high-half sums and low reversed-half sums. For r summands of common length2k, the exact equation is N=2^k sum u_i+r(2^k-1)-sum rev_k(u_i). Reversal is not an additive homomorphism. Matching high and low halves separately does not ensure simultaneous solutions: carries constrain them. A credible completion would specify finite carry/boundary states for the two-ended digit construction, prove every transition preserves complementary symmetric digits and canonical endpoints, and prove EVERY even input belongs to a state reachable with <=4 summands. For336511 it must additionally prove all nonlisted inputs reachable with<=3, not just that listed E fail. No such invariant/transition coverage proof was found here. ## 339332: sacrifice ordering in complete optimal play
Primary1805.09368v3 Definitions1–6,2-action Theorem12 proof portions, §7Conj17 read. The final EJC26(4)2019#P4.52 body has the same question as Conj37 and same example36; journal text source bindings in read inventory. This is not Stewart's already solved periodicity conjecture.

Let O(x)=max_(s in S,s<=x)(s-O(x-s)), terminal O=0, with the source opt convention choosing the LARGEST maximizing s. If first two optimal actions are a and b, and their remaining heap is z=x-a-b, then O(x)=a-b+O(z), O(x-a)=b-O(z). With greedy maximum M legal at both positions, the literal claim about these two sacrifices amounts to M-b<M-a, i.e. a<b. The exact competing-greedy inequalities needed for a counterexample a>=b are
 a-b+O(z)>=M-O(x-M),
 b-O(z)>=M-O(x-a-M),
with STRICT inequalities against a larger maximizing action whenever tie-breaking applies. These constrain different tail values. Bounds0<=O<=M alone do not give a contradiction; summing them loses exactly the tail dependence. This explains why the naive total-score argument fails.

A second route is rearrangement: source Lemma2 permits each player to put their own actions in non-increasing order. It does not compare one player's action to the other's, nor let one exchange actions across players. In particular it cannot turn a>=b into a profitable legal response; the available heap and stopping point change. In the 2-action class the conjecture's antecedent is impossible by source Observation4, so that class is vacuous evidence, not a full proof. Endgame greedy action is max{s<=x}, not always global M; using M at an illegal endgame position would be a false counterexample. A complete finite witness needs a chosen S,x, all relevant O-tail inequalities, the maximal-action tie convention and the exact meaning of multiple sacrifices paid.
