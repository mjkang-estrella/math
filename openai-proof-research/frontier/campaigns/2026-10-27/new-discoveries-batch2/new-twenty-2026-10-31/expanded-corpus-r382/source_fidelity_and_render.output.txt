77:                3. Marczinzik’s conjecture and its refinement
78:    The following notions on Dyck paths are due to Marczinzik and originate in
79: the representation theory of Nakayama algebras of a linearly oriented quiver on n
80: vertices. We refer to the MathOverflow discussion [3] for further background. For
81: a Dyck path D with row-area sequence (r0 , r1 , . . . , rn ) and column-area sequence
82: (c1 , . . . , cn+1 ),
83:        • D is the set of indices k with ck+1 = ck − 1,
84:        • F , is the set of indices k with rk+1+ck+1 = rk−1 + ck+1 + 2, and
85:        • N be the set of rows which do not contain a valley.
86:     We can now state Marczinzik’s conjecture. Recall that a Motzkin path of length n
87: is a lattice path from (0, 0) to (n, 0) consisting of up-steps (1, 1), down-steps (1, −1)
88: and level-steps (1, 0) that never goes below the x-axis.
89: Conjecture 1 (Marczinzik [3]). The number of Dyck paths such that N ∩ D is
90: contained in F equals the number of Motzkin paths of length n.
91:    Moreover, the number of Dyck paths such that N ∩ D is empty equals the number
92: of Riordan paths of length n, that is, Motzkin paths without level-steps at height
93: zero.

97: FindStat. Indeed, this approach was doubly successful: first, the result of the data-
98: base query, reproduced in the conjecture below, is a substantial common refinement
99: of both parts of Conjecture 1, and makes a proof strategy suggest itself. Second,

605:    Finally, we would like to discuss some algorithmic problems. For a fixed connected cubic
606: graph H consider a decision problem which we call the H-problem:
607: 
608: Problem 1. (H-problem) Given a connected cubic graph G, the goal is to decide whether
609: G admits an H-coloring.
610: 
611:     Observe that when H is 3-edge-colorable, we have that H-problem is equivalent to testing
612: 3-edge-colorability of the input graph G, which is NP-complete [6]. When H = S10 , we have
613: that all instances of H-problem have a trivial “yes” answer provided that Conjecture 5
614: is true. Thus, this problem is polynomial time solvable if Conjecture 5 is true. When
615: H = S12 , Conjecture 7 implies that H-problem is equivalent to testing the existence of a
616: perfect matching in the input graph G. This is known to be polynomial-time solvable. When
617: H = P10 , Conjecture 1 implies that H-problem is equivalent to testing bridgelessness of the
618: input graph G. This problem is also polynomial time solvable. Finally, when H = P12 ,
619: Conjecture 8 implies that H-problem is equivalent to testing whether the input graph G can
620: be covered with four perfect matchings. The latter problem is proved to be NP-complete in
621: [3]. Thus, depending on the choice of H, the H-problem may or may not be NP-complete.
622: Let CN P be the class of all connected cubic graphs H, for which the H-problem is NP-

434: the restriction of any line of M is a line in M . Furthermore:
435:       (i) M is finite if and only if M is finite.
436:      (ii) The simplification of M /p is isomorphic to the simplification of M/p for
437:            every p ∈ E(M ).
