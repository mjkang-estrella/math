303:    We also define a Young diagram and a skew Young diagram. We remark that all boundary
304: grids are skew Young diagrams.
305: Definition 3.3. A Young diagram is a finite, connected collection of boxes such that there
306: exists a bottom-left most box that we label as (1, 1). Moreover, if the box (k, ℓ) (which is in
307: the kth column from left and ℓth row from bottom) in the Young diagram, then every box
308: (k′ , ℓ′ ) with 1 ≤ k′ ≤ k, 1 ≤ ℓ′ ≤ ℓ is also in the diagram.
309: Definition 3.4. A skew Young diagram is a finite collection of boxes such that there exists
310: a Young diagram λ and another Young diagram µ ⊂ λ with the same bottom-left most box,
311: such that a box is in the skew Young diagram if and only if it is in λ but not in µ.
312: Remark. When dealing with a boundary grid of a permutation π or any skew Young diagram
313: generally, we will always use the convention of (x, y) to denote the box in the xth column
314: from left and y th row from bottom. This way, we correspond with the location of (i, πi ).
