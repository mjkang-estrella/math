828: prove Armstrong’s conjecture. We derive it as a consequence of:
829: Theorem 3.7. For fixed a, and b relatively prime to a, the average size of an
830: ( a, b)-core is a polynomial of degree 2 in b.
831: Proof. For fixed a, the number of a-cores is 1/a times the number of lattice points
832: in b∆ a−1 , which is a polynomial Fa (b) of degree a − 1. In the x-coordinates
833: Q = |core a | is invariant under Sa , and in particular rotation, so the sum of the
834: sizes of all ( a, b)-cores is 1/a times the sum of Q over the lattice points in b∆ a−1 .
835: By Euler-Maclaurin theory, the number of points in b∆ a−1 is a polynomial Ga (b)
836: of degree a + 1.
837:    Thus, the average value of an ( a, b)-core is Ga (b)/Fa (b), the quotient of a
838: polynomial of degree a + 1 in b by a polynomial of degree a − 1 in b. To show this
839: is a polynomial of degree two in b, we need to show that every root of Fa is a root
840: of Ga .
841:    Corollary 3.6 says that the roots of Fa are −1, −2, . . . , −( a − 1). We now give
842: another derivation of this fact, using Ehrhart reciprocity, that easily adapts to
843: shown these are also roots of Ga .
844:    Ehrhart reciprocity says that Fa (− x ) is, up to a sign, the number of points in
845: the interior of x∆ a−1 . The interior consists of the points in x∆ a−1 none of whose
846: coordinates are zero, and so the first interior point in x∆ a−1 is (1, 1, . . . , 1) ∈ a∆ a−1 .
847: Thus, Fa (b) vanishes at b = −1, . . . , −( a − 1), and as it has degree a − 1 it has no
848: other roots.
849:    Ehrhart reciprocity extends to Euler-Maclaurin theory, to say that up to a sign
850: Q a (− x ) is the sum of F of the interior points of x∆ a−1 . Thus Q a (− x ) also vanishes
851: at b = −1, . . . , −( a − 1), and so Pa /Q a is a polynomial of degree 2.
