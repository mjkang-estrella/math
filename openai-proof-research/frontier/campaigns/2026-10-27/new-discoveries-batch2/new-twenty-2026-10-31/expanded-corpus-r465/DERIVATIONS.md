## 330008: a finite hub obstruction, with a source-range hold

The complete two-page 1503.00410v1 is read. It assumes an order-n strongly connected digraph D, permits symmetric arcs, defines H[(i,j),(j,l)]=1 for l != i, and uses independent SITE occupation. Route A (zero spectral radius, full finite family). Let D_m be the bidirected star with hub h and m leaves. It is simple and strongly connected. A nonbacktracking walk can enter h from a leaf and leave to a different leaf, where it must stop; thus H^2=0 and rho(H)=0, algebraically, without a matrix computation. For p=1/2 site percolation, condition on h open. If L is the number of open leaves, E L=m/2 and Var L=m/4. Chebyshev gives Pr(L<m/4) <=4/m. Every open nonisolated vertex belongs to the same component; without h all components are singleton. For m>4, define giant as at least m/4 vertices. There is at most one giant in EVERY outcome. Given existence its uniqueness probability is exactly1, and its existence probability is at least (1/2)(1-4/m). This contradicts the unrestricted finite conditional formulation. It is not an infinite locally finite counterexample: the infinite star has infinite hub degree.

Route B (positive spectral radius, same family rather than a second result). Attach a triangle h,a,b to the hub, retain m pendant leaves, and put both arcs on each edge. D_m is again strongly connected. In its oriented line graph, incoming leaf-arcs have no predecessors and outgoing leaf-arcs no successors. The remaining recurrent arcs form exactly the two directed 3-cycles around the triangle. Ordering transient/recurrent blocks makes H block triangular, with nilpotent transient blocks and two 3-cycle permutation diagonal blocks. Therefore rho(H)=1, exactly. At p=1/2, p rho(H)=1/2<1. An open hub joins all L open leaves; a closed hub leaves only a,b possibly joined. If m/4>2 every giant must contain h, hence there is at most one. The same Chebyshev lower bound on existence applies, while conditional nonuniqueness probability is0. This avoids treating rho=0 alone as a discovery and confirms the obstruction is concentration of mass at a hub, not just a reciprocal-zero notation issue.

Route C (literal BOND model). For an undirected triangle-plus-leaves with each edge independently retained with p=1/2, L retained pendant edges is Binomial(m,1/2). The hub component has at least L+1 vertices; all other components are isolated leaves or a component among a,b. Again existence tends1 and at most one giant can exist. Under independent DIRECTED arc retention, a leaf is strongly connected to h exactly when both pendant arcs survive, independently with probability1/4. E L=m/4, Var L=3m/16, and Pr(L<m/8)<=12/m. Every strongly connected component with more than2 vertices contains h; so the same finite obstruction works with m/8 in place of m/4. These distinctions pay model cases rather than transferring site conclusions to bond without proof.

Joining many stars through a sparse backbone may raise rho(H) or lose positive-fraction concentration. Neither gives a locally finite infinite counterexample to the later theorem. No such counterexample is claimed. Requiring strong connectivity of the OLG would also exclude these graphs, though original Conjecture2 does not explicitly impose it; that condition is imposed for some DIFFERENT intermediate estimates.

## 334190: uniform bound, decomposition and equality routes

Route A: Suppose a NONTRIVIAL decomposition exists in some coordinate, with rectangles Ri x Si partitioning [r]x[s], Ti partitioning [t], and all triples in corresponding products. Assume inductively |T_i^actual|<=sqrt(|Ri||Si||Ti|) on smaller products. Put a_i=|Ri||Si| and b_i=|Ti|. Then
 |T| <= sum_i sqrt(a_i b_i) <= sqrt((sum_i a_i)(sum_i b_i))=sqrt(rst).
The second inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz. Its equality condition is that all positive a_i/b_i coincide; the first requires every nonempty block attain its own bound. This exactly explains the proportionality clause, but does not prove a decomposition exists. A single trivial block cannot be used inductively: its volume is unchanged. Nor do arbitrary rectangles become interval rectangles automatically. Unpaid global lemma: every equality example has a nontrivial compatible decomposition, after allowed property-preserving permutations, whose blocks can be recursively reduced. The source's nonextremal 15-triple discussion shows why a decomposition theorem for every sequence is too strong, but the material Figure4 incidence itself was NOT visually inspected or used as a new exact witness.

Route B: for each coordinate pair the projection of T is injective: if two distinct triples agree in two coordinates, neither can be strictly larger in two coordinates. Thus |T|<=min(rs,rt,st). This is true but in the balanced case only n^2, not n^(3/2). An entropy/Loomis–Whitney shortcut is invalid: the standard inequality bounds |T|^2 by the PRODUCT of projection sizes, giving at best (rst)^2, not rst. Injective projections alone allow Latin-square-size sets; it is the consistent ordering of every pair that must contribute the missing factor. Unpaid lemma: an ordering-sensitive projection inequality |T|^2<=rst, with sharpness forcing a separable interval structure.

For two product triples, if the outer entries differ, their first differing outer sequence order forces strict increase in at least two coordinate blocks, regardless of inner digits; if outer entries coincide the inner sequence supplies the two strict inequalities. Cardinalities multiply and r,s,t multiply. Consequently squared density efficiency eta=|T|^2/(rst) multiplies. ## 353432: equal-degree algebraic routes

Route A: try transfer a mixed conic-line counterexample. A generic degree-e projective covering pulls degrees1,2 to e,2e, still unequal. A quadratic Cremona transformation changes a degree-d curve to degree2d minus its three basepoint multiplicities. To turn a line and a conic both into conics, a line avoiding basepoints needs total multiplicity0, while a conic needs total multiplicity2. Thus basepoints must lie on the conics but avoid the lines, and all transformed components must remain smooth and ordinary with the same incidence graph. Exceptional curves and new basepoint intersections can alter the Levi graph; logarithmic freeness is not automatically preserved by this birational operation. Unpaid: one explicit pair of transformations paying smoothness, ordinariness, common degree, identical Levi graph, and the two opposite freeness certificates. None is obtained. Numerical Terao examples matching only multiplicity counts are also insufficient, since equal weak combinatorics need not give an isomorphic incidence graph.

Route B: use a pencil of k smooth transverse conics through four fixed basepoints. All intersections are ordinary k-fold points, hence the Levi graph is K_(4,k), independent of cross-ratio of pencil members. Writing f=product_j(q1-lambda_j q2), grad(q1) cross grad(q2) annihilates f and gives a polynomial syzygy of degree2. This is an exact formal differentiation argument, not a CAS calculation. A tempting hope is to vary lambda and change freeness. A conditional numerical obstruction defeats the simplest version: for a free plane curve of degree D=2k and minimal syzygy r<=2, the standard free Tjurina formula would give tau=(D-1)^2-r(D-1-r). For k>=3 this expression is minimized among r=0,1,2 at r=2 and equals4k^2-8k+7. The Milnor upper bound at four ordinary k-fold points is4(k-1)^2=4k^2-8k+4, smaller by3. Thus, CONDITIONAL on the standard free-curve formula and local tau<=mu and mu=(k-1)^2, no such pencil member arrangement is free at all. Those external formulas are not authenticated from a primary proof here, so this is a conditional failed mechanism, not an established theorem exclusion or new credited lemma. It does explain precisely why a pencil is not yet a counterexample pair.

Route C: deform arrangements with fixed incidence and test a Jacobian-syzygy rank jump by exact determinants. Freeness is controlled by the logarithmic derivation module, not the monomial edge ideal of the Levi graph studied in most of this manuscript. Isomorphic Levi graphs force those edge rings to agree, but do not force the Jacobian modules to agree. The desired parameterized rank jump therefore cannot be read from the manuscript's Cohen–Macaulay theorems.
