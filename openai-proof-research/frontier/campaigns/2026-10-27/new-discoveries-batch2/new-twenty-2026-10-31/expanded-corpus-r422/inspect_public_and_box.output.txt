Lemma 6.1. Let α be a partition.

   1. There is a unique maximal element X in the poset (Tα , ⩽box ). The SYT for X is
      given by writing the numbers 1, . . . , |α| in numerical order into the Young diagram
      for α.

   2. There is a unique minimal element Z in the poset (Tα , ⩽box ). The SYT for Z is the
      transpose of the Young diagram for α0 in which the numbers 1, . . . , |α| are entered
      in numerical order.

Proof. 1. If two numbers i, i + 1 do not occur in numerical order in the SYT for X, then
the box move exchanging i with i + 1 yields a SYT which is larger in the dominance order.
    2. By definition, taking transposes is an order-reversing bijection between the posets
(Tα , ⩽box ) and (Tα0 , ⩽box ).                                                         

Proposition 6.2. For a partition α, all saturated chains in the poset (Tα , ⩽box ) have the

Proof. Since covers in the Bruhat order increase the length by 1 (Lemma 4.1), the result
follows from Theorem 1.1, Proposition 4.4, Lemma 4.5 and Proposition 4.6.             

6.3. Open Question
We want to compare the two algorithms presented above. Suppose we have two SYTs
X, Z of shape α, such that X >dom Z. Say the first algorithm produces a SYT Ỹ , the
second a SYT Y , both of shape α. The tableaux satisfy

                            X >dom Ỹ ⩾dom Z,                   X >dom Y ⩾dom Z.

    The first algorithm depends on the choice of a factorization of z as a product of
adjacent transpositions. In this section, we describe a way to make the choice so that
(conjecturally) the two algorithms produce the same result, i.e., so that Y = Ỹ . Suppose
that to produce the reduced expression for z ∈ Sn , a version of the “bubble sort” algorithm
is used, as in [1, Example 3.4.3].

which transform z into the identity permutation. Start by moving n into the final position.
