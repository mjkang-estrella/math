Primary: Georgakopoulos, A Tractable Variant of Cover Time, arXiv1206.6605v1, Definition1.1, equation(1), Proposition2.1 and Conjecture5.4. Write N=|V|-1. All expectations used below are finite: on any connected finite graph, from any vertex there is a walk of bounded length visiting any prescribed vertex (or all vertices), with probability bounded below by a fixed positive number. Successive blocks therefore give a geometric tail for the hitting/cover times. First-step equations and nonnegative summation/tower arguments are justified. No limit-of-computation assumption occurs.

Before first hitting v, the walk is the reflected path walk. The expected hitting function h_i=L^2-i^2 satisfies h_L=0,h_0=1+h_1 and h_i=1+(h_{i-1}+h_{i+1})/2 for0<i<L. Uniqueness follows by the discrete maximum principle (a harmonic difference achieving a nonzero interior extremum propagates to absorbing L). Hence E_0 T_v=L^2. In particular every one of the m-1 other clique vertices has H_0(w)>=L^2. Consequently cc_0(B)>=(m-1)L^2/(L+m-1).

After first reaching v, the entire path has been visited. Suppress excursions from v into the tail, recording only transitions between different clique vertices. Let a be the expected duration until the next different clique vertex when starting at v. First-step conditioning gives
 a=(m-1)/m + (1/m)(2L+a),
so a=1+2L/(m-1). The successful clique neighbor is uniform among m-1 choices; all other clique vertices take exactly one step per embedded transition. Thus the embedded walk is simple random walk on K_m and every embedded transition has conditional expected physical duration at most a.

If k clique vertices remain unvisited, each embedded step hits one with conditional probability k/(m-1), since the current vertex is already visited. The waiting time has expectation(m-1)/k: induct on survival steps, or solve e=1+(1-k/(m-1))e. Summing stages gives (m-1)H_{m-1} expected embedded steps. Conditioning on the history before each transition and summing its expected duration therefore bounds the physical clique-cover time by a(m-1)H_{m-1}=(m-1+2L)H_{m-1}. It follows that
 CT_0(B)<=L^2+(m-1+2L)H_{m-1}.

## 323710

Scope: finite simple connected undirected rooted graphs, simple uniform-neighbor discrete-time random walk. Cover time is the expectation of the first time all vertices have been visited, with no return requirement. Write N=|V|-1. Definition1.1 charges each step before its destination is visited by the fraction of nonroot vertices still unvisited. On each sample path, the contribution from each nonroot vertex x is exactly its first hitting time divided by N. Summing finitely many contributions and taking expectations proves cc_r=sum_x H_r(x)/N, the source's equation(1) after division by N. This also fixes the cost convention and avoids substituting uppercase CC or roundtrip cover.

Finiteness: in a finite connected graph choose, from each possible state, a finite walk visiting every vertex, of length at most a common M. If maximum degree is D, the chance to follow that walk is at least D^(-M)>0 (the one-edge graph causes no difficulty). Conditional on any past history, the chance of covering during the next M steps is bounded below by this same positive constant. The cover-time tail at multiples of M is bounded by a geometric sequence. All cover and hitting times used below have finite expectation. First-step conditioning, strong Markov conditioning at first hits, and expectations of nonnegative sums are therefore legitimate. The chain need not be aperiodic.

Let B consist of vertices 0,...,64 forming a path and 255 additional vertices forming a clique together with vertex v=64. There are 65+255=320 vertices. Root is 0. There are only path edges and the clique edges; it is simple and connected. More generally write L=64,m=256. Until the first hit of v the process is reflected at 0 and otherwise has equal probabilities of moving either way along the path. For the expected time h_i from i to L, the candidate h_i=L^2-i^2 has h_L=0, h_0=1+h_1 and h_i=1+(h_(i-1)+h_(i+1))/2 for 0<i<L. It is the unique solution: a difference of two solutions is harmonic away from L; its maximum and minimum propagate along the finite connected interval to the zero boundary, including the reflecting endpoint. Hence E_0 T_v=L^2. Every additional clique vertex can be reached only after v, so each of its hitting expectations is at least L^2. Thus cc_0(B)>=255*4096/319>3072, since 4*255>3*319.

At T_v the entire tail has been visited. Starting there, record only successive arrivals at a different clique vertex, suppressing excursions down the tail. At v there are m neighbors: m-1 clique neighbors and the tail neighbor L-1. From L-1 the mean return time to v is L^2-(L-1)^2=2L-1; after including the departure from v, a failed trial costs mean 2L. Each return restarts the trial by the strong Markov property. Successful neighbor probabilities are all identical at each trial. Thus the eventual successful neighbor is uniform among m-1 possibilities, conditionally on all past history. From another clique vertex there are exactly m-1 neighbors, all in the clique, and the next physical step is the embedded step. Consequently the embedded chain is precisely the simple walk on K_m. No independence between holding time and destination is assumed.

For a start at v, let a be the expected physical duration until the next different clique vertex. It is finite by the finite-graph hitting argument (or the geometric number of trials with finite mean excursions). First-step conditioning gives a=(m-1)/m+(2L+a)/m, whence a=1+2L/(m-1). At any other embedded state the duration is 1. Every duration therefore has conditional mean at most a, given the entire history up to its starting embedded state.

If k clique vertices are still unvisited, the current state is visited. Exactly k of its m-1 possible embedded destinations are unvisited. Until a new vertex is found this conditional success probability stays k/(m-1). Iterated conditioning gives survival probability (1-k/(m-1))^t after t trials, and summing this geometric series gives mean (m-1)/k. There are stages k=m-1,...,1; their means add to E K=(m-1) H_(m-1), where K is the number of embedded steps to cover the clique.

For full stopping-time justification let F_j be the information at the start of embedded step j and D_j its physical duration. The event {K>=j} is F_j-measurable. Tonelli and the conditional bound give E sum_(j=1)^K D_j = sum_j E[1_{K>=j} E(D_j|F_j)] <= a sum_j P(K>=j)=a E K. E K is finite by the stage calculation, so this also proves integrability of the physical sum. Strong Markov at T_v now yields CT_0(B)<=L^2+(m-1+2L)H_(m-1). There is no omitted return from the final discovered vertex.

Partition 1,...,255 into the eight sets {2^j,...,2^(j+1)-1}, j=0,...,7. Each has 2^j terms at most 1/2^j, so H_255<=8. Therefore CT_0(B)<=4096+383*8<4096+384*8=7168. The denominator is strictly positive. Combining the strict lower bound for cc and strict upper bound for CT gives cc_0(B)/CT_0(B)>3072/7168=3/7.

For the path P on 320 vertices root an endpoint 0, put n=319 edges. To hit i the walk remains on 0,...,i; the preceding reflected-interval calculation with L=i gives H_0(i)=i^2. Reaching n visits every path vertex, so CT_0(P)=n^2. The identity sum_(i=1)^n i^2=n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 follows by induction (subtract successive cubic expressions); hence cc_0(P)/CT_0(P)=(n+1)(2n+1)/(6n^2). This is less than 3/8 iff 2n^2-24n-8>0. For n>=16, 2n(n-12)-8>=120>0, and n=319 qualifies. Finally 3/8<3/7, proving the strict equal-order comparison and negating the universal MathDB assertion. This is an ordinary full-literal refutation, not a proof of worldwide novelty.

## 323059

Use vertex set {0,1,2,3} x {0,...,8}; two distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their first coordinates agree. The graph is the disjoint union of four K9, has 36 vertices, is finite simple undirected and every vertex has exactly the other eight vertices in its component as neighbors. Thus d=8, d congruent 2 mod3, and 2(d-2)=12 divides 36. Disconnected graphs are allowed in the printed Conjecture5.2; its preceding proposed extremal construction itself uses disjoint unions. Neither the Section5 paragraph nor the introduction imposes connectedness, a nonclique condition, or a lower degree threshold excluding 8. These checks concern the actual copied v1, not the unread 2013 final.

An independent set contains at most one vertex in each component. If a component were missed, any of its vertices could be adjoined without creating an edge, so the set would not be maximal. Conversely one vertex per component is independent and every omitted vertex is adjacent to its component's chosen vertex, so the set is maximal. Taking the second coordinates of these four chosen vertices is a bijection from maximal independent sets to functions {0,1,2,3}->{0,...,8}. There are exactly 9^4=3^8. The proposed bound is 2^(36/12)*3^(36/6)=2^3*3^6. Since 9>8 and 3^6>0, 3^8>2^3*3^6, a strict violation in the nonsingular d=8 domain. Natural integer exponents agree with the real powers in the literal because both bases are positive.

Check the proposed source extremizer at d=8 separately. T is two disjoint triangles, with six vertices; H is the join of two copies of T, so it has twelve vertices and degree 2+6=8. In H an independent set lies wholly in one side. Maximality then requires exactly one vertex in each of that side's two triangles; this gives 9 choices in each of two sides, total18. The empty set is not maximal. Three disjoint copies of H have 36 vertices and 18^3=2^3*3^6 maximal independent sets. Hence the witness truly exceeds the source construction, rather than exploiting a wrong count of that construction. It does not use d=2, and it does not contradict Theorem1.2's asymptotic upper bound as d tends to infinity.
