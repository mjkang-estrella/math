## 331409 and 331410: Chocolate Numbers

Primary evidence: Ji–Khovanova–Park–Song, arXiv:1509.06093v1, Sections 2, 3.3, 5.1–5.3; final JIS 19 (2016), article 16.1.7, conjectures 16,22,23 and the displayed recurrence. B_n means A_{2,n}, not an unspecified sequence of all chocolate numbers. The recurrence is B_1=1 and
B_n=(2n-2)!+sum_{i=1}^{n-1} binom(2n-2,2i-1) B_i B_{n-i}.

### Route A: exact integral divided-power transfer; one direction obtained

Work first over Q[[t]]. Set C_0=1, C_n=product_{i=1}^n(4i^2-10i+5), and U(t)=sum C_n t^{2n}/(2n)!. This is exactly the paper's u(t^2), since its P_n=4^n C_n. Direct differentiation using C_{n+1}=(4n^2-2n-1)C_n gives (1-t^2)U''+U=0. Let b(t)=sum_{n>=1} B_n t^{2n-1}/(2n-1)!. The recurrence, coefficient by coefficient, says b'=b^2+1/(1-t^2), b(0)=0. On the other hand -U'/U has the same initial value and satisfies the same Riccati equation. Rational formal-series uniqueness follows recursively from (m+1)b_{m+1} being determined by lower coefficients. Thus b=-U'/U in characteristic zero.

All of U,U',b have integral exponential coefficients. Their products therefore belong to the integral Hurwitz/divided-power ring: (a*c)_m=sum_j binom(m,j)a_j c_{m-j}. Consequently U b=-U' is an integral coefficient identity and may be reduced modulo any prime. This reduction does not divide by a factorial in the residue field.

In the completed Hurwitz ring over F_p, every series A with constant coefficient 1 satisfies A^p=1. For a positive degree divided-power monomial e_m=t^{[m]}, e_m^p=((pm)!/(m!)^p)e_{pm}; the integer multiplier is divisible by p. Indeed its p-adic valuation is s_p(m)>0, by the factorial valuation formula. For a finite sum Frobenius applies; each fixed coefficient of an infinite sum depends on only finitely many terms. Thus all positive degree terms disappear in the pth power. It follows that U^{-1}=U^{p-1} and b=-U' U^{p-1} modulo p.

Suppose p is odd and 5 is a square modulo p (including p=5). Since 4 is invertible, there is a positive i for which (4i-5)^2=5 mod p. Thus C_i=0 mod p and C_n=0 for all n>=i. U is then a finite Hurwitz polynomial, so -U'U^{p-1} is a finite Hurwitz polynomial. Hence B_n is eventually zero modulo p. More explicitly if deg U<=2i-2, deg b<=p(2i-2)-1; this is only a theoretical bound, not a computed test. For p=2, the source's established v_2(B_n)>=n for n>1 gives eventual zero. Quadratic reciprocity gives (5/p)=(p/5) for odd p not 5, so these are exactly the primes on the sufficient side of 331409.

This pays the entire sufficient implication, including the factorial-denominator bridge. It does not pay the necessary implication. No novelty or significance claim is made for this half theorem.

### Route B: product dynamics, and why the converse does not follow

For nonsplit p, C_n is always nonzero mod p and C_{n+p}=R C_n, where R is the nonzero product of one p-block of the quadratic factors. Thus D^{2p}U=R U in the Hurwitz differential ring. This is a genuine identity, but it is not yet a periodicity theorem for B. Inversion under binomial convolution does not transport ordinary coefficient periods automatically. In particular U can have infinitely many nonzero divided-power coefficients while its logarithmic derivative has finite support (the formal exponential gives the general obstruction to that inference). Therefore 'C_n never vanishes, so B_n never eventually vanishes' is invalid. An unpaid lemma is a classification of finite-support solutions of b'=b^2+1/(1-t^2) mod p, with the initial data lifted from integral recurrence, proving that such a solution exists only in the split case. The rational function 1/(1-t^2) has finite divided-power support mod p, which prevents a simple highest-degree contradiction.

For 331410, one would need eventual ordinary periodicity of the logarithmic derivative coefficients in the nonsplit case, not merely periodicity of C_n. The source's p=3 theorem is already public and supplies no general-prime deduction. Also the literal says 'a period divisible by p(p-1)' rather than 'least period': once a period T exists, lcm(T,p(p-1)) is another period. Exploiting that phrasing would be a wording correction, not a new discovery. The main unpaid work is still eventual periodicity for all nonsplit primes.

### Route C: convolution induction

The source Theorem 9 propagates a long terminal zero block, but does not furnish such a block uniformly in p. Lucas-type reductions of the binomial recurrence keep contributions from indices spanning several p-blocks. The set of residues B_{n mod p} is not closed under that recurrence without an additional block-state theorem. No finite-state theorem or mathematical experiment was run. This route has a specific missing block-transfer lemma, not a proof from the auxiliary product alone.

## 332002: full ordinary route, but exact public prior theorem

Original: arXiv:1512.02844v2, definition (1), Conjecture 1 in the three-involution subsection. Current public Allocca–Trimmer arXiv:2506.19216v2 (20 September 2025), Theorem 4 and Corollary 2, states the exact conjectured bound and supplies its proof. This is earlier than this investigation and the record's August 2026 creation. The source proof body, not just its abstract, was read.

Here is a clean complete bridge and ordinary argument. Write reflections as r^a f. Pick one generator as f; the three generator exponents are A={0,a,b} in Z/nZ, with three distinct elements. S generates D_n iff a,b generate Z/nZ. The parity of a reflection word is odd, and multiplying on the right alternately adds and subtracts A in exponent coordinates. If W_l is the exponent set of words of exactly length l, then W_1=A and W_{l+1}=W_l+(-1)^l A. Since 0 belongs to A, these exponent sets are nested. They have size at most n.

Use Kneser's sumset inequality in its standard finite-group form. Let H stabilize W_{l+1}. If H=Z/nZ, then W_{l+1}=Z/nZ. Otherwise A is not contained in H, since A generates the whole group. If H is nontrivial, |A+H|>=2|H|, so Kneser gives |W_{l+1}|>=|W_l+H|+|A+H|-|H|>=|W_l|+|H|>=|W_l|+2. If H is trivial, the same inequality gives |W_{l+1}|>=|W_l|+3-1=|W_l|+2. Negating A does not change this argument. Hence |W_l|>=min(2l+1,n).

Choose the smallest odd l>=ceil((n-1)/2). Then l<=floor(n/2)+1 and W_l contains all n reflection exponents. Thus every reflection has word length <=floor(n/2)+1. Every conjugate gsg^{-1} of a reflection is a reflection; taking the maximum proves the literal lambda_1 inequality. The n=2 case cannot have three distinct reflections, so its premise is empty; all feasible n>=3 are covered. This is a full-scope mathematical deduction from Kneser, but the exact public Corollary 2 already resolves the target. No upstream theorem was re-audited and this is not new credit.

An earlier attempted shortcut through a pair of reflections fails: three reflections may generate while no selected pair generates, e.g. the differences can have separate common factors with n. A prime-modulus Cauchy–Davenport route proves growth but does not cover composite n. The stabilizer/quotient case above pays precisely that missing composite-order case. Strongest significance case: the all-composite growth argument is substantive and formalization would not be a mere numerical witness. Decisive contrary fact: the exact theorem and its proof are publicly present in the 2025 paper. Therefore exclusion is known public solution, irrespective of proof length.

The relation
product_i y_(ai,bi) - product_i y_(ai,b_{i+1})
is a nonzero cubic binomial in J, over every field (the distinct monomials remain distinct, including characteristic2).

To see it cannot be generated by quadrics: give each y_(q,q') its image monomial multidegree. A divisor of either cubic uses two distinct minima in row1 and two distinct maxima in row2. Consequently the degree3 fiber has two isolated vertices under all degree2 replacements.
