# Sustained mathematical routes — r46

No result here is proposed for discovery credit. The finite computations below are diagnostics, not certified mathematical endpoints.

## 1. MathDB 353241: exact product formula

Primary scope: Frankl–Kupavskii, arXiv:2201.03865v1, Definition 3 and Conjecture 2, equation (17). For positive integers t,s and n≥t+s, maximize cardinality of a family F⊆2^[n] satisfying |A∩B|≥t and |A∪B|≤n−s for every A,B∈F, including A=B. The conjectured maximum is max_{t≤u≤n−s} m(u,t)m(n−u,s). Source Theorem 11 and its induction through weighted bipartite matchings were read; they do not supply the missing quantitative t,s step. Source Theorem 8 proves t=s=1, and the closing discussion explicitly cites the s=1 result of Frankl (1975). These special cases receive no credit.

Attempt A: On a partition X⊔Y, take a maximum t-intersecting family on X and the complements of a maximum s-intersecting family on Y. Their Cartesian union family proves the conjectured lower bound. For the upper bound let U=F↑ and D=F↓. U is t-intersecting: witnesses A⊆A′ and B⊆B′ imply |A′∩B′|≥|A∩B|≥t. D is s-union by the dual inclusion argument. Thus Harris–Kleitman gives |F|≤|U∩D|≤|U||D|/2^n≤m(n,t)m(n,s)/2^n. This bound is insufficient: for n=6,t=s=2 the right side is 22²/64, while the proposed answer is 5. No equality/coordinate separation theorem has been proved. Source Theorem 11 disjoint matchings cannot be used directly after replacing disjointness by a t-threshold.

Attempt B: At n=t+s, self-pair constraints force every member to have size t. Pairwise t-intersection then forces all members equal. Hence m(n,t,s)=1, agreeing with the product expression. This boundary calculation is routine and not a discovery. It does not extend to n>t+s, where members can vary in size.

Attempt C: Six unrestricted binary integer models at n=6..9 were run with 6-second solver limits. Variables index subsets with t≤|A|≤n−s; each violating pair contributes x_A+x_B≤1. Full solver output, selected families and bounds are retained. Timeouts are explicitly retained and are not optimality claims. Some reported incumbents are smaller than the known product construction, indicating poor search, not mathematical evidence against that lower bound.

Attempt D: Fourteen second-model tests use orbits under S_u×S_v. An orbit consists of all subsets having a elements in the first block and b in the second. Two orbit types (a,b),(c,d) are compatible exactly when max(0,a+c−u)+max(0,b+d−v)≥t and min(u,a+c)+min(v,b+d)≤n−s. These follow by independently attaining the minimum overlap and maximum union inside each block. Self-compatibility is enforced. Orbit weights are binomial(u,a)binomial(v,b). The n=12..20 tests produced no incumbent larger than the product benchmark. A two-block family restriction excludes most unrestricted families; neither floating solver bounds nor this restriction settle the conjecture.

Remaining decisive lemma: an all-parameter upper bound by the best disjoint-coordinate product, or a specific finite family exceeding that product together with a rigorous one-sided-maximum certificate. Lean would need literal Finset families, pairwise cardinality semantics, maxima and all bridges. No such endpoint exists here. Katona's formula was used in diagnostics only; no source-body clearance or formalization of it is claimed.

## 2. MathDB 353049: square extremizers and recursive 2-extension

Primary scope: arXiv:2112.12716, Section 3, Lemma 15 and Conjecture 16. A 2-extension chooses two existing points and completes one of the three squares through them; one or both added points may already exist. That convention is essential: restricting to exactly two new points would produce a false parity objection. Similarities are permitted. The claim quantifies over every extremal n-point set, for every n≥6, not just the existence of one recursively generated optimizer.

Attempt A: Define closure of a seed square inside a fixed finite P by repeatedly adjoining every square of P having at least two vertices already reached. A finite P is obtainable by recursive 2-extension from some seed square if and only if one seed's closure equals P. Forward direction: every construction step uses a square of P, since the construction is increasing and ends at P, so each step lies in the closure. Reverse direction: realize each closure enlargement by completing its square; there are at most two unreached vertices at each step. Termination follows because P is finite. A similarity carries squares and this procedure to squares, so this criterion respects the source's similarity convention. This is an ordinary reachability observation, not a separately credited theorem or Lean surrogate.

Attempt B: Disconnected square configurations and configurations whose square blocks meet only in a single point can fail closure. They do not automatically refute extremality. The source's Figure 7 already discusses twisting at one vertex. Source Definition 36 and Lemma 37 bound square counts using a maximal extension subconfiguration C and an isosceles-right-triangle-minus-three-squares statistic on P\C. That proof was read: a square not contained in C has at most one vertex in C; its three/four exterior vertices charge a triangle, with four triangles corrected by subtracting three squares. This is a stronger source mechanism than mere component counting, but it still requires sharp bounds for all exterior configurations.

Attempt C: All subsets of 3×3 and 4×3 integer grids were tested for square counts and the exact closure algorithm. Squares were generated by rotating an ordered side vector by 90 degrees; all orientations and side lengths present in the grid are included. The diagnostic found no nonrecursive configuration attaining even the within-grid maximum for sizes≥6. This only concerns two finite grids. At n=12 the 4×3 grid maximum is 10, below the source's known global 11; it is particularly unsuitable as a global extremality certificate.

Remaining decisive lemma: either prove that every globally extremal configuration has a spanning seed closure, or exhibit a nonrecursive P and certify its global square optimum among all real planar point sets. The source's grid-embedding existence result preserves/increases the number of squares; it does not classify every optimizer up to similarity. An enumeration of one grid cannot bridge that quantifier. Lean would require Euclidean squares, similarities, the literal extension relation and global maximum semantics, not just the finite closure checker.

## 3. MathDB 353517: Drury's permitted partitions

Primary scope: Wanless arXiv:2202.01867, Conjecture 12; Drury, Mathematical Inequalities & Applications 21(2) (2018), 517–520, original Conjecture 1 on p519. Both actual bodies were read. The original question uses positive definite Hermitian matrices; the survey uses positive semidefinite matrices. A rigorous extension via A+epsilon I and continuity would be needed to identify these formulations. No certificate here depends on assuming that extension without proof.

Attempt A: Known counterexamples in the source live in diagrams (3,2) and (7,1), precisely those ruled out. Drury gives an explicit 2×8 integer-complex X, A=X*X, per(A)=2977257622144118400, and a larger eigenvalue of B_ij=A_ij per(A(i|j)). His Section3 computes the representation bridge: permutation matrices decompose into trivial plus (n−1,1), so the B spectrum is per(A) plus the corresponding Fourier-block spectrum. Using the published n=8 witness would duplicate an excluded-diagram known result, not solve the permitted-diagram assertion. The survey's broad 'cascade of counterexamples' sentence does not refute Conjecture12.

Attempt B: Diagram classification. An allowed partition either has first row at most2, or is a hook with all subsequent rows of length1 and first row at most6, or is a single row. This follows immediately from forbidding a first row≥3 together with second row≥2, and forbidding a first row≥7 together with a nonzero second row. In particular, at n=4 every partition is allowed: the full claim already includes the still-unresolved n=4 permanent-on-top case identified in the survey. Solving just a trace/immanant inequality would not bound every eigenvalue of these blocks. Branching the known witnesses upwards continues to contain a prohibited diagram, so that shortcut cannot generate a permitted one.

Attempt C: The standard representation (n−1,1) is allowed for n=5,6,7. Using the original exact bridge, 48 seeded random integer-complex Gram matrices of rank≤2 or≤3 were tested at these orders. The diagnostic compresses B to the orthogonal complement of the all-ones vector before comparing its largest eigenvalue with per(A). No positive gap was found. This is floating arithmetic and a tiny sample, not a proof of any matrix inequality. No exact candidate required certification. The script's first output failed to serialize a NumPy boolean; the failure log is retained and the corrected deterministic rerun completed.

Remaining decisive lemma: a permitted-block operator upper bound for all PSD Hermitian A, or a rational Gram witness and exact Rayleigh inequality in a permitted representation. No proof of the full spectral assertion is available. A formal endpoint would require actual symmetric-group Fourier/isotypic semantics, PSD, permanent and the block-spectrum bridge. Trace domination or a bounded numerical test is not an acceptable substitute.
