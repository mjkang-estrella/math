## Statement
Let G1,G2 be finite connected planar graphs, with sphere embeddings whose dual multigraphs are isomorphic. Then there is an integer matrix U with determinant ±1 such that U^t L(G1) U=L(G2). The source graphs are loopless with adjacency entries -1 on edges; duals may have loops and parallel edges. The proof below also works for loopless primal multigraphs using multiplicity in the usual incidence Laplacian. It does not reinterpret a loop-degree convention in the source.

## Integral lattice proof
Orient the edges of a connected graph G and let B be its vertex-by-edge signed incidence matrix. Thus L=BB^t: on an edge uv, the contribution to x^tLx is (x_u-x_v)^2. Write C(G)=im(B^t:Z^V→Z^E) with the standard dot product. Its kernel is precisely the integer constant vectors, since along every edge the endpoint coordinates agree and paths connect all vertices. Fix r in V. The rows b_v, v≠r, of B form a Z-basis of C(G). They generate because the sum of all rows is zero. They are independent because any zero linear combination extends to a constant kernel vector with r-coordinate zero. Their Gram matrix is the reduced Laplacian L_r.

Put compatible orientations on the dual edges: use the oriented sphere to rotate a primal edge direction by a fixed quarter-turn. Then the boundary vector of a dual face corresponding to primal vertex v is b_v, up to a global consistent sign. This assertion counts each side of each incident edge with its induced boundary orientation, so it remains valid at bridges, dual loops, parallel edges and repeated face-boundary vertices; contributions on the two sides cancel when both sides lie in the same face.

We pay the integral, rather than merely real, generation step. Let H be the embedded dual. Every integral circulation z in ker B_H is an integer sum of directed simple cycles. To see this, replace each nonzero edge coefficient by its absolute number of copies, oriented according to its sign. Conservation ensures that a walk following unused directed edges cannot stop at a vertex before closing a directed cycle. Truncate at the first repeated vertex, subtract one copy of that cycle, and repeat. The sum of absolute edge multiplicities decreases, so this process terminates. This is a mathematical existence argument, not a program or enumeration. Loops give cycles of length one; two parallel edges can give length two.

A simple cycle in a sphere embedding separates the sphere. Orient the disk on one side. Summing the oriented boundaries of all faces on that side cancels each interior edge and leaves precisely the oriented cycle vector. This also holds for an embedded loop and for a two-edge simple closed curve. For general facial boundaries, cancellation is on edge-sides, not on a supposition that each boundary walk has distinct vertices. The connected sphere embedding is cellular: start with a spanning tree, whose sphere complement is an open disk; adjoining each remaining embedded edge splits one face into two. Thus all the face regions used in this argument are the cellular faces. Consequently every integral circulation in H lies in the Z-span of its facial boundaries. Conversely every such boundary is a circulation because each local entry into a vertex is balanced by an exit. Hence

    ker(B_H: Z^E→Z^V(H)) = span_Z{dual facial boundary vectors} = C(G)

after the compatible signed edge identification. No finite-index or saturation assumption is being substituted for equality here: the cycle decomposition and disk-boundary cancellation prove the equality over Z.

Now identify H1 and H2 by their abstract multigraph isomorphism. Edge reorientation is multiplication by ±1 on each coordinate; the edge bijection is a coordinate permutation. Their composition is an integral Euclidean isometry of Z^E that identifies ker B_H1 and ker B_H2. The preceding identity therefore gives an isometry C(G1)≅C(G2) of integral lattices.

Their numbers of primal vertices agree. Indeed a connected sphere graph satisfies |V|-|E|+|F|=2: a spanning tree starts with one face and each remaining edge adds one face. Since |F(Gi)|=|V(Hi)| and the duals have equal vertex and edge counts, |V(G1)|=|V(G2)|=n. Equivalently both cut lattices have rank n-1. In the Z-bases of rows except their respective roots, the lattice isometry is a matrix A∈GL(n-1,Z); hence A^t L1,r A=L2,s, with direction of A chosen accordingly.

It remains to restore the degenerate full Laplacian, not just assert reduced equivalence. The coordinate map (y_v for v≠r,t)↦x with x_r=t and x_v=y_v+t is integral unimodular: its inverse is t=x_r and y_v=x_v-x_r. Every edge difference loses t, so its Gram matrix is diag(L_r,0). Apply this separately to G1 and G2 and insert diag(A,1) between these coordinate changes. The resulting integral unimodular U has U^tL1U=L2.
