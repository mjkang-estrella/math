                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                              The graph isomorphism problem is a long standing problem that is of both theoretical
                                          and practical importance, and much effort has been put into the research of this problem.
                                          For example, for many types of graphs, there are various known fast algorithms. Many
                                          graph invariants have been studied, and put into work. However, as stated in [15], it is clear
                                          that there lacks a uniform and deeper understanding of this problem, thus many issues are
                                          at an unclear stage.
                                              One main motivation of this paper is to try to initiate a new perspective to the study of
                                          graphs, and in particular the graph isomorphism problem, from ideas familiar in quantum
                                          field theory, with the hope of contributing toward a better understanding. On the other
                                          hand, another main point of the paper is to introduce another type of graph invariants,
                                          coming from the theory of quadratic forms over Z, which for the purpose of graph isomor-
                                          phism testing, seem to complement the physics idea in a certain sense that we will describe.
                                          Furthermore, it appears that some of these invariants capture important graph properties
                                          regarding graph embeddings, which we hope to be of independent interest, aside from the
                                          graph isomorphism problem.
                                              More specifically, in section 2, we study one of the simplest quantum field theories defined
                                          on a graph1, namely a real free scalar field theory, with a varying mass parameter. Its
                                          two-point correlation function gives us a version of the discrete Green’s function. This
                                          function showed up in [8] for different purposes. In section 3, we explain that this Green’s
                                          function directly gives rise to a particular easily computable graph invariant, which turns
                                          out to be not stronger than (but essentially very similar to) the 2-dim Weisfeiler-Lehman
                                          algorithm for graph isomorphism testing. Although this particular invariant is not sufficient,
                                          it indicates that simple ideas from physics may become useful for considerations on the graph
                                          isomorphism problem: e.g. there might be many variants of the physics idea, that give rise
                                          to more powerful graph invariants for isomorphism testing.
                                              In section 4, we introduce another type of graph invariants, by viewing graphs as quadratic
                                          forms over the integers: let A be the adjacency matrix of a finite simple graph G, and take any
                                          polynomial f with integral coefficients, f (A) defines a quadratic form over Z. It is obvious
                                          that the isomorphism type of the quadratic form, is an invariant of the graph isomorphism
                                          type. Note that a very different idea of quadratic forms on graphs was explored in [1]. For

   Let G be a connected graph other than the single-vertex graph P , we call G to be a
simple graph, iff G cannot be written as a sum G1 ∨X,Y G2 of two strictly smaller graphs.
Obviously the simple graphs, together with P , generate the submonoid of Gf consisting of
connected graphs. We have the following
Lemma 4.9. Any connected graph other than P has a unique decomposition into a wedge
sum of simple graphs, which we call its simple components.
Proof. First, an easy induction shows such a decomposition always exists.
   Next, to prove uniqueness, we consider the set of gluing vertices in the graph G: namely,
the set of vertices where the graph can be separated as a wedge sum of two components,
glued along the vertex. If there is no such vertex, then by definition the graph is simple,
and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, pick any such vertex V , then G is separated
into components, glued along V , which we call components of G, w.r.t. V . Obviously, any
connected subgraph of the connected graph G containing V cannot be a simple graph, so
the simple graphs in the decomposition of G, which are obviously connected, all appear as
subgraphs of components of G, w.r.t. V . Suppose there is another graph G0 isomorphic to
G as a graph, which is also written as a wedge sum of simple graphs. Then under this graph
isomorphism, V must be mapped to a gluing vertex V 0 of G0 . Next, the components of G
w.r.t. V must map bijectively to isomorphic components of G0 w.r.t. V 0 . Again, any simple
graph in the decomposition of G0 , must appear as a subgraph of components of G0 w.r.t. V 0 .
By induction, each such component has a unique decomposition into simple graphs, thus
the decomposition of G0 must be the same as that of G.                                    
Remark 4.10. Therefore, to understand the quadratic form of a connected graph, it suffices
to understand the forms of its simple components, and we do not need to worry about how
the simple components glue.
   To go further, from computer experiments that will be described below, we propose the
following
Conjecture 4.11. Let G1 and G2 be two connected planar graphs, and suppose there
exist embeddings of the two graphs into the 2-sphere, such that the dual graphs w.r.t. the
embeddings are isomorphic as multi-graphs, then the quadratic forms over Z, represented
by the combinatorial Laplacian of G1 and G2 are equivalent.
   Furthermore, one could try to remove the planar graph restriction: in general, any graph
can be embedding into a higher genus surface, and the minimal genus of such surfaces
is called the genus of the graph. Given any such embedding, the dual (multi-)graph can
obviously be defined in the same way, as that in the planar case. The above conjecture
could be extended to the following
Conjecture 4.12. Let G1 and G2 be two connected graphs of the same genus g, and
further suppose that G1 and G2 have embeddings into the genus g surface Σg , such that the
corresponding dual graphs are isomorphic as multi-graphs, then the quadratic forms over Z,
represented by the combinatorial Laplacian of G1 and G2 are equivalent..
Remark 4.13. Note that, if one drops the condition that G1 and G2 having the same genus,
the conjecture would become false: e.g. take the 3-path and the 3-cycle, both can be
embedded into a torus, such that the dual graph consists of a single vertex, and 3 self-edges.
However the quadratic forms of the 3-path and the 3-cycle are not equivalent.

GUARD """One explicit public GET plan per invocation; run inside the supplied runner.

The CLI has no batch/list traversal or retry mode. Every redirect is precharged.
This guards this client only, not other tools or arbitrary Python networking.
"""
import contextlib
import datetime
import fcntl
import hashlib
import ipaddress
import json
from pathlib import Path
import re
import sys
import urllib.error
import urllib.parse
import urllib.request

ROOTS = frozenset([
    "https://github.com/openai/math",
    "https://github.com/LeanPool/lean-pool",
    "https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti",
    "https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures",
])
PER_ID_CAP = 12
GLOBAL_CAP = 48
MAX_BYTES = 24 * 1024 * 1024


class PolicyError(RuntimeError):
    pass


def key(url):
    p = urllib.parse.urlsplit(url)
    if p.scheme != "https" or not p.hostname or p.username or p.password:
        raise PolicyError("Only explicit credential-free HTTPS URLs are allowed")
    host = p.hostname.lower()
    if p.port not in (None, 443) or host in ("localhost", "api.github.com") or host.endswith((".local", ".internal", ".localhost")):
        raise PolicyError("Disallowed host/port")
    try:
        if not ipaddress.ip_address(host).is_global:
            raise PolicyError("Nonpublic IP address")
    except ValueError:
        pass
    return host + urllib.parse.unquote(p.path).rstrip("/")


def artifacts(url):
    s = urllib.parse.unquote(url).lower()
    found = set(re.findall(r"(?<!\d)\d{4}\.\d{4,5}(?!\d)", s))
    for doi in re.findall(r"10\.\d{4,9}/[-._;()/:a-z0-9]+", s):
        while any(doi.endswith(t) for t in (".full", ".pdf", ".abstract", ".html", ".xml")):
            doi = doi.rsplit(".", 1)[0]
        found.add(doi)
    return found


class Guard:
    def __init__(self, lane, opener=None):
        self.lane = Path(lane).resolve()
        self.ids = set(map(str, json.loads((self.lane / "reservation.json").read_text())["ids"]))
        self.denied = json.loads((self.lane / "inherited-denials.json").read_text())
        self.opener = opener  # Tests inject a local fake; CLI always uses urllib.
        self.ledger = self.lane / "guard-ledger.jsonl"

    @contextlib.contextmanager
    def locked(self):
        with (self.lane / "guard.lock").open("a") as lock:
            fcntl.flock(lock, fcntl.LOCK_EX)
            records, previous = [], "0" * 64
            if self.ledger.exists():
                for raw in self.ledger.read_bytes().splitlines(keepends=True):
                    record = json.loads(raw)
                    if record.get("seq") != len(records) or record.get("previous") != previous:
                        raise PolicyError("Broken guard ledger chain; no request permitted")
                    records.append(record)
                    previous = hashlib.sha256(raw).hexdigest()
            yield records, previous

    def append(self, records, previous, event):
        record = dict(event, seq=len(records), previous=previous,
                      utc=datetime.datetime.now(datetime.timezone.utc).isoformat())
        raw = (json.dumps(record, sort_keys=True) + "\n").encode()
        with self.ledger.open("ab") as f:
            f.write(raw)
            f.flush()
        return record

    def note(self, event):
        with self.locked() as (records, previous):
            return self.append(records, previous, event)

    def reserve(self, url, routes, plan_sha):
        k = key(url)
        if url in ROOTS or k in {key(x) for x in ROOTS}:
            routes = sorted(self.ids)  # Shared roots can never be undercharged.
        if not routes or not set(routes) <= self.ids or len(routes) != len(set(routes)):
            raise PolicyError("Routes must be exact unique reserved IDs")
        with self.locked() as (records, previous):
            attempts = [r for r in records if r["event"] == "attempt"]
            failures = [r for r in records if r["event"] == "unavailable"]
            denied_keys = set(self.denied["blocked_keys"]) | {r["url_key"] for r in failures}
            denied_artifacts = set(self.denied["blocked_artifacts"])
            for r in failures:
                denied_artifacts.update(r.get("artifacts", []))
            if k in denied_keys or artifacts(url) & denied_artifacts:
                raise PolicyError("Inherited/current unavailable route or artifact: no retry")
            if any(r["url_key"] == k for r in attempts):
                raise PolicyError("Already attempted URL; use saved bytes/outcome, no refetch/query variant")
            if len(attempts) >= GLOBAL_CAP:
                raise PolicyError("Global GET-hop cap48 exhausted")
            for route in routes:
                if sum(route in r["routes"] for r in attempts) >= PER_ID_CAP:
                    raise PolicyError("Per-ID GET-hop cap12 exhausted: " + route)
            return self.append(records, previous, {
                "event": "attempt", "url": url, "url_key": k,
                "routes": routes, "plan_sha256": plan_sha,
                "ordinal": len(attempts) + 1,
            })

    def fetch(self, plan):
        if not isinstance(plan, dict) or set(plan) != {"url", "routes", "destination", "source_identity", "purpose"}:
            raise PolicyError("Exactly one explicit plan object required; no URL arrays/seed traversal")
        if not isinstance(plan["url"], str) or not isinstance(plan["routes"], list):
            raise PolicyError("Invalid URL/routes types")
        if not all(isinstance(x, str) for x in plan["routes"]):
            raise PolicyError("Route IDs must be strings")
        if not all(isinstance(plan[x], str) and len(plan[x].strip()) >= 12 for x in ["purpose", "source_identity"]):
            raise PolicyError("Concrete source identity and assigned-target purpose required")
        dest = (self.lane / plan["destination"]).resolve()
        if not dest.is_relative_to(self.lane / "sources") or dest == self.lane / "sources" or dest.exists():
            raise PolicyError("Destination must be a NEW file under own sources/")
        plan_sha = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(plan, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
        current = plan["url"]
        attempt = self.reserve(current, plan["routes"], plan_sha)
        guard = self

        class Redirect(urllib.request.HTTPRedirectHandler):
            max_redirections = 5

            def redirect_request(self, req, fp, code, msg, headers, newurl):
                nonlocal current, attempt
                guard.note({"event": "redirect", "url": current, "status": code, "location": newurl})
                current = newurl
                attempt = guard.reserve(current, plan["routes"], plan_sha)
                return super().redirect_request(req, fp, code, msg, headers, newurl)

        opener = self.opener or urllib.request.build_opener(Redirect())
        http_status = None
        observed_body = None
        response_content_type = None
        try:
            req = urllib.request.Request(current, headers={"User-Agent": "Research-source-reader/1.0", "Accept-Encoding": "identity"}, method="GET")
            with opener.open(req, timeout=20) as response:
                status = response.status
                http_status = status
                response_content_type = response.headers.get("Content-Type")
                if status != 200:
                    raise RuntimeError("Non-200 status " + str(status))
                body = response.read(MAX_BYTES + 1)
                observed_body = body
                if not body or len(body) > MAX_BYTES:
                    raise RuntimeError("Empty or oversized body; unavailable, no retry")
                if "html" in response.headers.get("Content-Type", "").lower() or body.lstrip().lower().startswith((b"<!doctype html", b"<html")):
                    start = body[:12000].lower()
                    if any(x in start for x in [b"verify you are human", b"cf-chl-", b"challenge-platform", b"robot check", b"access denied", b"incapsula", b"request unsuccessful"]):
                        raise RuntimeError("Challenge/denial body; unavailable, no retry")
                dest.parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
                with dest.open("xb") as f:
                    f.write(body)
                return self.note({"event": "success", "url": current, "status": status,
                                  "destination": str(dest.relative_to(self.lane)), "bytes": len(body),
                                  "sha256": hashlib.sha256(body).hexdigest(), "ordinal": attempt["ordinal"]})
        except Exception as error:
            self.note({"event": "unavailable", "url": current, "url_key": key(current),
                       "artifacts": sorted(artifacts(current)), "error": repr(error),
                       "status": getattr(error, "code", http_status), "ordinal": attempt["ordinal"],
                       "response_content_type": response_content_type,
                       "observed_bytes_read": len(observed_body) if observed_body is not None else None,
                       "observed_body_sha256": hashlib.sha256(observed_body).hexdigest() if observed_body is not None else None,
                       "body_may_be_truncated": len(observed_body) > MAX_BYTES if observed_body is not None else None})
            raise


def main():
    if len(sys.argv) != 2:
        raise PolicyError("Usage: source_get_guard.py OWN_EXPLICIT_PLAN.json")
    lane = Path(__file__).resolve().parent
    plan_path = Path(sys.argv[1]).resolve()
    if not plan_path.is_relative_to(lane):
        raise PolicyError("Plan must be an own-lane file")
    print(json.dumps(Guard(lane).fetch(json.loads(plan_path.read_text())), sort_keys=True))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
