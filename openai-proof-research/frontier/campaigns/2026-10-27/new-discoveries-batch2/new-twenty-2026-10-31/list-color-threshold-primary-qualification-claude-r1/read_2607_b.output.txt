3 Discussion
 We have proved Conjecture 1.4 posed in [12], thus showing that Question 1.3 has an
affirmative answer for edgeless graphs. Despite its deceptively simple appearance, this
result implies, as observed in Corollary 1.7, that if every connected component H of a
graph G has the property that Pℓu (H, k) = P u (H, k) whenever k ≥ N , then the graph G
itself also satisfies Pℓu (G, k) = P u (G, k) whenever k ≥ N . In particular, a graph G satisfies
the statement in Question 1.3 if each of its connected components does so.
 Despite its simplicity, we have not been able to locate Theorem 1.5 in the literature.
Given that our proof in Section 2, like many results in extremal set theory [9], uses shifting
as a crucial ingredient, it is natural to wonder whether one can conclude Theorem 1.5 from
a known more general result.
 Next, in the appendix, we give a cleaned-up version of the AI-produced proof of The-
orem 1.5. The key idea is in Lemma A.1 which proves a shadow inequality for a family
of multisets in the style of the probabilistic proof of the local LYM inequality (see [3])
by Frankl [8]. In some sense, our approach (shifting arguments) and the AI’s approach
(shadow inequalities) are the two natural classic approaches to problems in set systems like
Theorem 1.5.
 Note that the main question from [12] remains open. Gowers [10] asked ChatGPT 5.5
Pro to prove/disprove Question 1.3 for all graphs but it was unable to do so. Currently,
we do not know of any approach for tackling this question in full generality.
