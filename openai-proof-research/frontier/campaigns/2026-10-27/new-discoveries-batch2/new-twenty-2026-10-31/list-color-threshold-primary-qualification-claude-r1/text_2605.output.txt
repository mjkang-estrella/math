 Abstract
 Chromatic choosability is a notion of fundamental importance in list coloring. A
 graph G is chromatic-choosable when its chromatic number, χ(G), is equal to its list
 chromatic number χℓ (G). In 1990, Kostochka and Sidorenko introduced the list color
 function of a graph G, denoted Pℓ (G, m), which is the list analogue of the chromatic
 polynomial of G, P (G, m). A graph G is said to be enumeratively chromatic-choosable
 when Pℓ (G, m) = P (G, m) for every m ∈ N. Theta graphs and their generalizations have
 played an important role in graph coloring problems over the years; for example, they
 appear in the characterization of chromatic-choosable graphs with chromatic number
 2. In this paper we characterize the enumeratively chromatic-choosable theta graphs.
 Our proof utilizes ideas from DP-coloring (a.k.a. correspondence coloring), providing
 yet another example of how the more general setting of DP-coloring can be leveraged to
 attack a problem in list coloring.
 Keywords. list coloring, chromatic choosability, list color function, theta graphs, enu-
 merative chromatic choosability

1.1 List Coloring and Chromatic Choosability
 For classical vertex coloring of graphs, we wish to color the vertices of a graph G with
up to m colors from [m] so that adjacent vertices receive different colors, a so-called proper
m-coloring. The chromatic number of a graph G, denoted χ(G), is the smallest m such that
G has a proper m-coloring. List coloring is a well-known variation on classical vertex coloring
that was introduced independently by Vizing [25] and Erdős, Rubin, and Taylor [11] in the
1970s. For list coloring, we associate a list assignment L with a graph G such that each
vertex v ∈ V (G) is assigned a list of available colors L(v) (L is said to be a list assignment
for G). We say G is L-colorable if there is a proper coloring f of G such that f (v) ∈ L(v)
for each v ∈ V (G) (we refer to f as a proper L-coloring of G). A list assignment L for G is
called a k-assignment if |L(v)| = k for each v ∈ V (G). Graph G is said to be k-choosable if
it is L-colorable whenever L is a k-assignment for G. The list chromatic number of a graph
G, denoted χℓ (G), is the smallest m for which G is m-choosable. It is easy to show that for
any graph G, χ(G) ≤ χℓ (G).
 A graph G is called chromatic-choosable if χ(G) = χℓ (G) [21]. Determining whether
a graph is chromatic-choosable is, in general, a challenging problem. Perhaps the most
famous conjecture involving list coloring is about chromatic choosability. Indeed, the Edge
List Coloring Conjecture states that every line graph of a loopless multigraph is chromatic-
choosable (see [12]).

1.2 Enumeratively Chromatic-Choosable Graphs
 In 1912, Birkhoff [4] introduced the notion of the chromatic polynomial in hopes of using
it to make progress on the four color problem. For m ∈ N, the chromatic polynomial of a
graph G, denoted P (G, m), is the number of proper m-colorings of G. It is easy to show that
P (G, m) is a polynomial in m ofQdegree |V (G)| (see [4]). For example, whenever n ∈ N it
is well known that P (Kn , m) = n−1 n n
 i=0 (m − i), P (Cn , m) = (m − 1) + (−1) (m − 1), and
P (T, m) = m(m − 1) n−1 whenever T is a tree on n vertices, (see [3, 27]).
 The notion of chromatic polynomial was extended to list coloring in the early 1990s by
Kostochka and Sidorenko [17]. If L is a list assignment for a graph G, let P (G, L) denote
the number of proper L-colorings of G. For m ∈ N, the list color function of G, denoted
Pℓ (G, m), is the minimum value of P (G, L) where the minimum is taken over all possible
m-assignments L for G. Since an m-assignment could assign the same m colors to every
vertex in a graph, it is clear that Pℓ (G, m) ≤ P (G, m) for each m ∈ N. In general, the list
color function can differ significantly from the chromatic polynomial for small values of m.
For example, for any n ≥ 2, Pℓ (Kn,nn , m) = 0 and P (Kn,nn , m) > 1 whenever m ∈ {2, . . . , n}.
On the other hand, in 2023 Dong and Zhang [8], improving on earlier results (see [9, 24, 26]),
showed that for any graph G, Pℓ (G, m) = P (G, m) whenever m ≥ |E(G)| − 1.

 With this and chromatic choosability in mind, it is natural to try to determine which
graphs G satisfy Pℓ (G, m) = P (G, m) for every m ∈ N. Such graphs are said to be enumer-
atively chromatic-choosable. Enumerative chromatic choosability was first formally defined
in [14] even though it has been pursued since the introduction of the list color function in
the early 1990s [17]. One of the most important open questions on the list color function
illustrates a key challenge to proving a graph is enumeratively chromatic-choosable.
Question 1 ([16]). For every graph G, if Pℓ (G, m) = P (G, m) for some m ≥ χ(G), does it
follow that Pℓ (G, m + 1) = P (G, m + 1)?
 Indeed, since we don’t know the answer to Question 1, showing that Pℓ (G, χ(G)) =
P (G, χ(G)) doesn’t imply that G is enumeratively chromatic-choosable. Relatively little is
known about which graphs are enumeratively chromatic-choosable. Our next theorem gives
some examples of enumeratively chromatic-choosable graphs. However, before we state the
theorem we review some important terminology. Recall that a graph is chordal if it contains
no induced cycles of length greater than 3. For l1 , l2 , l3 ∈ N, theta graphs, denoted Θ(l1 , l2 , l3 ),
consist of a pair of end vertices joined by 3 internally disjoint paths of lengths l1 , l2 , and l3 .
We say that the core of a connected graph G is the graph obtained from G by successively
deleting vertices of degree 1.
Theorem 2 ([1, 6, 13, 16, 17]). The following statements hold.

 (i) Chordal graphs are enumeratively chromatic-choosable.

 (ii) Cycles are enumeratively chromatic-choosable.

(iii) If 1 ≤ l1 ≤ l2 ≤ l3 and the parity of l1 is different from both l2 and l3 , then Θ(l1 , l2 , l3 )
 is enumeratively chromatic-choosable.

 (iv) Suppose G is a connected graph with χ(G) = 2. Then, G is enumeratively chromatic-
 choosable if and only if the core of G is a copy of: K1 , C2k+2 for k ∈ N, or Θ(2, 2, 2)
 (i.e., K2,3 ).

 With Theorem 2 in mind, the motivation for this paper was the following question.
Question 3. Which theta graphs are enumeratively chromatic-choosable?
 Aside from the progress already made toward Question 3, theta graphs and their gener-
alizations have played an important role in graph coloring over the years. Indeed generalized
theta graphs have been widely studied (see e.g., [2, 6, 7, 11, 13, 18, 19, 20, 22]), and they are
the main subject of two classical papers on the chromatic polynomial [5, 23] which include
the celebrated result that the zeros of the chromatic polynomials of the generalized theta
graphs are dense in the whole complex plane with the possible exception of a unit disc.
 In this paper we use a generalization of list coloring called DP-coloring to answer Ques-
tion 3. Specifically, we prove the following.
Theorem 4. Suppose G = Θ(l1 , l2 , l3 ) where min{l1 , l2 , l3 } = l1 , l2 ≥ 2, and l3 ≥ 2. Then, G
is not enumeratively chromatic-choosable if and only if l1 , l2 , and l3 all have the same parity
and {l1 , l2 , l3 } ̸= {2}.
