350037 Let $G$ be a $t$-tough graph on $n\ge 3$ vertices. Define

$$
\sigma_2(G)=\min\{d_G(u)+d_G(v):u,v\in V(G),\ u\text{ and }v\text{ are nonadjacent}\}
$$

for noncomplete $G$, and set $\sigma_2(G)=\infty$ when $G$ is complete. A graph is **hamiltonian** if it contains a hamiltonian cycle.

**Ore-type toughness conjecture.** If

$$
\sigma_2(G)>\frac{2n}{t+1}-2,
$$

then $G$ is hamiltonian.

The statement strengthens the paper's proved Ore-type condition by removing the additive $t$ term. It is presented as a stronger statement that might be true and remains open.
350525 Let $G$ be a $4$-connected planar triangulation on $n$ vertices. The **Hakimi–Schmeichel–Thomassen conjecture.** $G$ has at least

$$
2(n-2)(n-4)
$$

hamiltonian cycles, with equality if and only if $G$ is the double-wheel graph on $n$ vertices, namely, the join of a cycle of length $n-2$ and an empty graph on two vertices.
This conjecture gives a sharp proposed lower bound for the number of Hamiltonian cycles in $4$-connected planar triangulations. The supplied paper proves exponential lower bounds for broad classes of such triangulations with few $4$-separators, but does not establish the conjectured exact quadratic bound and equality characterization in general.
350604 Fix $d\geq 2$, and let $H=\ovar{G}(n,d)$ be the sparse random digraph used in the paper. Write $\ovar{r_1}(H)$ for its one-color oriented Ramsey number. **Linear random-digraph conjecture.** With high probability,

$$
\ovar{r_1}(H)=O_d(n).
$$

The paper currently proves only an upper bound of $n(\log n)^{O_d(1)}$ for fixed $d$ and leaves the asserted linear bound open.

A matrix is a **slack matrix** if it is the slack matrix of a polytope. **Polynomial-time recognition conjecture.** There is an algorithm polynomial in $m,n$ that correctly determines whether $S$ is the slack matrix of a polytope. The conjecture concerns the unresolved complexity of recognizing slack matrices among 0/1-valued matrices, equivalently the recognition problem for 2-level polytopes.
353563 Let $n\geq 1$, and let $K_{2n+1}$ be the complete graph on $2n+1$ vertices. **Ringel–Kotzig decomposition conjecture.** The graph $K_{2n+1}$ can be decomposed into $2n+1$ subgraphs, all isomorphic to a given tree with $n$ edges. This is a tree-decomposition problem closely connected with graceful labelings. The supplied source presents it as a longstanding conjecture and gives no resolution evidence.

This conjecture would extend connectivity criteria for finding rigid, and potentially globally rigid, subgraphs in dimensions $d\geq 3$. The source says that it has remained open for over forty years.
353974 Let a **4MP** be a 4-connected maximal planar graph, and let a **linear Hamiltonian cycle** be a Hamiltonian cycle such that the regions on one side of the cycle share edges with at most two other regions on that side; equivalently, the dual of those regions is a path. **Linear Hamiltonian cycle conjecture.** Every 4MP has a linear Hamiltonian cycle. Hamiltonian cycles in 4-connected maximal planar graphs correspond to pairs of spanning 2-trees, and Whitney's theorem guarantees that every 4MP is Hamiltonian. The stronger linearity requirement is the assertion at issue here; the supplied text does not give a resolution, so its status remains open.
354102 A graph is **$(3,1)$-choosable** if every list assignment in which each list has size at least $3$ and the lists of adjacent vertices have intersection of size at most $1$ admits a proper list colouring. **The $(3,1)$-choosability conjecture.** Every planar graph is $(3,1)$-choosable. This conjecture concerns list colouring of planar graphs with bounded separation and remains open; it is presented as a conjecture from the literature.

A collection of perfect matchings is an **edge-covering collection** when every edge of $G$ belongs to at least one matching in the collection. **Berge's conjecture.** Every bridgeless cubic graph admits five perfect matchings forming an edge-covering collection. The source states that this conjecture is equivalent to Fulkerson's conjecture and remains open.

$$
\min\{d(v),3\}
$$

different colors in $N(v)$.

The conjecture is posed as a strengthening of the coloring method used in the paper: it would immediately imply the paper's theorem via the color-merger argument. The source gives supporting examples, including triangulations with acyclic chromatic number at most four, but does not resolve the general case.

This is the paper's main conjecture and is motivated by the question of whether minor- and union-closed families yield different homomorphism indistinguishability relations; its status is presented as open.

This is a special case of the $H$-Hadwiger conjecture. The paper presents it as an open conjecture concerning chromatic bounds for graphs excluding complete bipartite minors.

The source attributes this conjecture to Gnanajothi and gives no resolution, so it remains open.

The source says that Wang and Yao proved this conjecture equivalent to the graceful tree conjecture; no resolution of either conjecture is supplied here.

$$
\chi(L(G))=\chi_\ell(L(G)).
$$

The source notes that this conjecture is proved for bipartite graphs, while its general status is not resolved in the supplied material.

$$

The source derives this formulation from the preceding threshold conjecture using the Four Color Theorem; no resolution is supplied.

This would extend the known graphic and cographic cases, potentially using Seymour's regular matroid decomposition theorem. The supplied text presents it as a conjectural consequence that remains unproved.
