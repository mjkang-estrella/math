## Rank 1 — 386794, singular homology of cyclic closure graphs

Inherited objection: the c_k closure and singular theory had not been restored; clique homology alone is insufficient. The original AIM workshop section is still unread, and remains a material scope objection. No AIM retry was made. This pass restores the conventions in actual related primary sources, not in that workshop original.

Read Milićević, arXiv:2301.00660v4 (19 February 2025), sections 2.2, 2.7, the beginning of section 5, Lemmas 5.2–5.3 and proofs, Theorems 5.13, 5.15, 5.16 and their complete displayed proofs, and section 6. Relevant extraction lines: 195–222, 484–509, 1000–1098, 1138–1224, 1467–1518. Singular chains are free abelian on continuous maps from ordinary real standard simplices; continuity is f(c(A)) subset d(f(A)). This is not finite graph-map homology. The cyclic metric is minimum absolute representative distance modulo n, with closure generated by radius-m closed neighborhoods. The displayed threshold in the c_m formula has an apparent k/m variable inconsistency; no credit or theorem is based on repairing it. The prose and all subsequent constructions identify the nearest-neighbor convention.

Attempt A: two-piece interior covers. The earlier partial theorem n>=4m gives zero higher homology. Its proof uses a good-pair quotient to reduce to n>=6m and an explicit Mayer–Vietoris cover. An arbitrary cover is inadequate: for Z4,c1, the two three-point arcs have interiors {0} and {2}. The Z6,c2 bottleneck in section 6 is real for this route: each minimal point-neighborhood has five points; its neighborhood is the whole six-point space. Contractibility of a path subspace alone does not establish the required good pair. Extending this cover argument to all n,m leaves a genuine missing deformation-retraction/cover comparison. No small finite sweep was offered as a replacement for infinitely many singular simplices.

Attempt B: restore the singular-to-clique bridge. Read Milićević–Scoville arXiv:2409.01370v3 (26 September 2026), Example 1, Definition 20, Theorem 21 with its complete proof, and Theorem 24 with its complete concluding proof. Theorem 24 gives a weak equivalence from the realization of directed VR to every finite reflexive digraph, and Theorem 21 gives the homology isomorphism. For symmetric relations Definition 20 is the ordinary unordered clique complex. Theorem 24 uses the neighborhood interior cover, induction on the number of vertices, Lemma 12 (inverse-image deformation retract), and Corollary 3 (local weak-equivalence gluing). Theorem 21 uses mapping cylinders, relative exact sequences and finite delta-complex realization of cycles. These dependencies were identified, NOT all audited. The paper itself already computes H_i(Z6,c2)=Z in degrees 0,2 and zero otherwise, and explicitly proposes the general n,m computation using Adamaszek. Therefore even this attractive small exact example is already public; it cannot qualify.

Attempt C: all-parameter classification rather than a tiny example. Read Adamaszek arXiv:1104.0433v3, section 6 definitions, Propositions 6.1–6.2 with their complete concluding proofs, and Corollary 6.5 with proof. The complement of the radius-m cycle graph is T_{n,a}, a=n-2m-1. Its neighborhood consists of the a opposite vertices, as in equation (4). The two suspension reductions preserve a and reduce n by 2(a+1), until a+1<=n<=3a+2. Corollary 6.5 already determines the homotopy type.

Explicit all-scope bridge under these related-source conventions: for n>=1,m>=0 integers, m=0 is the discrete n-point case. If m>=floor(n/2), the graph is complete, hence its clique realization is contractible. Otherwise take q=n-2m, a=q-1>=1. The parity condition on n,a is automatic. If n=(2l+1)q, its reduced integral homology is Z^(q-1) in degree 2l and zero elsewhere. If (2l+1)q<n<(2l+3)q, it is Z in degree 2l+1 and zero elsewhere. H0 is Z in the connected m>=1 cases. This is a direct substitution into the published/preprint classification plus the later comparison theorem, NOT a discovery. The n=6,m=2 example has q=2,l=1 and gives S2, agreeing with the explicit successor example. No diagnostic computation, Lean, or new all-parameter proof is needed to exclude counting this route as new.

Limit: the exact original workshop convention, published-version equality with each fetched arXiv revision, and a complete proof-dependency audit are not established. The known-comparison route is sufficient to avoid investing in a rediscovery; it does not license changing the record to a fully source-cleared resolved status. The general closure-space axiomatic uniqueness question 386792 is not resolved by this finite-graph comparison.

## Rank 2 — 386886, a center-free FS-exponent computation

Inherited objection: a renamed center construction is not a center-free method; an input/computation model and direct indicator proof were missing. The original AIM fusion-category context is still unread. In particular, a spherical/pseudo-unitary hypothesis must not be silently inserted into an arbitrary C.

Read Ng–Schauenburg arXiv:math/0601012v3 (5 August 2006), section 2 pivotal/dual conventions, Definition 5.1, Remark 5.2, Proposition 5.3 and its complete proof, Example 5.4, Theorem 5.5 and its complete proof. Definition 5.1 uses least n with indicator equal to pivotal dimension; the category exponent is defined through objects. The proof of Theorem 5.5 uses the center twist and a weighted sum of root-of-unity phases. Real squared pivotal dimensions are nonnegative; equality of the weighted phase sum with its maximum forces all contributing phases to be one. The converse uses the indicator trace formula. External real-dimension/nonvanishing and center-dimension results, and the earlier indicator trace theorem, remain dependencies, not independently replayed here.

Attempt A: stop at the order of the center twist. Rejected: this computes precisely the excluded center object. Example 5.4 also prevents confusing Etingof's exponent with FS-exponent: the cocycle-twisted C2 example has the former 2 and the latter 4. A putative group-exponent shortcut would change the invariant.

Attempt B: compute indicators from explicit associator, duality, pivotal and fusion data. For a spherical fusion category over C with exact computable scalar input, enumerate n>=1, compute the pivotal cyclic-rotation trace on Hom(1,X_i^tensor n) for each simple X_i, and stop when every trace equals d(X_i). Additivity extends this test to all objects. Theorem 5.5 supplies termination and minimality via the common center-twist order; the algorithm itself need not construct the center. This is only a routine bridge to already defined indicators, with an exact-arithmetic input hypothesis. It is not a novel theorem or a uniform complexity improvement. For arbitrary uneffectively supplied complex F-symbols, decidable equality is an additional issue. The source workshop might intend something stronger than this basic enumeration, so no exact record resolution is asserted.

Attempt C: exploit a concrete direct formula rather than disguising a center computation. Read Schauenburg, author-hosted Computing higher Frobenius–Schur indicators in fusion categories constructed from inclusions of finite groups, section 4 equations (4.1)–(4.8), Theorem 4.1 and its complete proof. For G finite, H subgroup, d double-coset representative, S=Stab_H(dH), and stabilizer representation W of character eta, the indicator is (1/|S|) sum_{r in dH, r^n in S} eta(r^n). The proof reduces a conjugacy/double-coset sum to S-orbits and uses orbit-stabilizer. This gives genuinely center-free finite arithmetic at execution time, but it is already the author's theorem, and its category is a special group-inclusion family. It cannot be promoted to a new general fusion-category solution. No finite examples were computed because they would only instantiate the existing formula.

Remaining lemmas for any new proposal: restore the original meaning and hypotheses for C; specify exact input data; prove a nontrivial new general invariant/algorithm beyond indicator enumeration or known special-family formulas, with termination, equality, and no hidden center reconstruction; then formalize pivotal traces, category semantics and every bridge. No such new mechanism survives this pass. Lean would be substantial foundational work on an already-known/uncleared endpoint; none was started.
