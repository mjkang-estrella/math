Notes:
1. **"Rayless" is not defined in arXiv:2504.02425.** The word appears only at lines 747, 750 and 775. The lane's CASE line 17 attributes it to "Section 2 of the source", which is inaccurate. The standard meaning, "contains no ray", is stated explicitly by the same authors in arXiv:2506.03853 (Annals of Combinatorics 2026), `tmp/2506.03853.txt` line 210, and in arXiv:2510.01667 line 349. Any fidelity write-up should cite those. This changes no mathematics.
2. **Two readings of "completion".** Definition 2.6 allows an arbitrary isometric embedding. The lane, at CASE line 23 and in the Lean draft at lines 47–48, uses the inclusion reading: X complete and X₀ dense. The two readings are equivalent, by this argument, which the lane does not give:
   - Suppose φ : X₀ → X is an isometry onto a dense set. Then φ(X₀) is generated by the transported labeled ray and is totally bounded.
   - φ(X₀) ≠ X. Otherwise X₀ would be complete and totally bounded, hence compact. But every vertex of a ray is isolated (Fact 1), and an infinite discrete space is not compact.
   - So the inclusion form holds for X₀' = φ(X₀). The converse is trivial.

   The Lean endpoint should include this lemma, or at least a fidelity remark. It is a few dozen lines.
3. "Degree greater than 2" must include infinite degree. The draft's `(2 : ℕ∞) < (G.neighborSet c).encard` does this correctly.
4. Mathlib's `TotallyBounded` uses ambient centres. This matches Definition 2.4 literally.

- Step 2 (lines 42–48):

  - Take y ∈ X ∖ X₀, which exists because X₀ ⊊ X. Every approximating index sequence tends to ∞, so d(y, x_m) = M_m.
  - Two such points y, y′ are at distance ≤ limsup λ = 0.
  - Hence X = X₀ ∪ {p}. Correct.
  - The isolation sentence at line 44 is not used later. It is harmless.
- Step 3 (lines 50–53): K = the last index ≥ n where M_n is attained is a record. The least record r(n) ≥ n satisfies λ_{r(n)} = M_n. I checked both cases, r(n) = K and r(n) < K. A label-0 vertex is never a record, because M > 0. So every leg end joined to p has a positive label. Correct.

It is rayless: a ray can use p at most once, and every leg is finite. The three distance cases check out. In case (c), index r_i lies in [n, m], so the ray distance is M_n. The tree distance is max(M_n, 0, M_m) = M_n because M is non-increasing. Correct.
- Step 6 (line 72): the closure of a totally bounded set is totally bounded, and complete plus totally bounded gives compact. Correct.

**(ii) ⇒ (i)**

There are infinitely many groups because there are infinitely many legs. Each group is written as y_{s+1}, …, y_e, y_s. The ray edge condition holds because every boundary pair contains a leader. **Edge cases checked:**

**Is the conjecture's class just "compact with exactly one accumulation point"?** No. Take p plus points x_n with d(p, x_n) = 2⁻ⁿ, plus two points a, b with d(a, b) = 1, each at distance 2 from everything else. This space is compact and has one accumulation point. It is not generated by any starlike rayless tree. The only points with h ≥ 2 are a and b, and a group needs a leader at height 2 from which the other members are at distance 2. So the conjecture characterises a genuine proper subclass. Neither side reduces to the other trivially.

## C. Source-internal results and prior art: QUALIFIED_PASS

**Source and companion papers already cover the analytic half.** Nothing implies either direction outright.
- The source's Theorem 5.6 proof, part (ii) ⇒ (i) (`tmp/x1/arxiv.txt` lines 642–663), shows that the completion of a totally bounded ray space is X₀ plus one point. - Dovgoshey–Küçükaslan, Annals of Combinatorics 26 (2022), arXiv:2101.00626v3, which I downloaded (39b18f06…). Text in `tmp/dk.txt`:
  - Lemma 4.7 (lines 451–465): the ray sequence is Cauchy iff its labels tend to 0.
  - Theorem 4.15 (lines 838–842): totally bounded iff every V_ε is finite and δ(v) < ∞ whenever l(v) > 0.
  - Theorem 4.20 (lines 973–976): compact iff the tree is rayless, every V_ε is finite, and δ(v) < ∞ whenever l(v) > 0.

  These give Step 1 of (i) ⇒ (ii), Step 2 of (ii) ⇒ (i) (l(c) = 0, since δ(c) = ∞), the finiteness of {h ≥ ε}, and compactness in (i) ⇒ (ii) almost verbatim. The lane's screen did **not** cite DK 2022. It should be added to the prior-art record.

No source or companion result contains either, and no stated theorem implies either direction of the conjecture.
