Fix alpha=1/2. For each positive integer m let V be the vector space of functions {1,...,m}->F2. Give the carrier R_m=F2 x V componentwise addition and multiplication

(a,v)(b,w)=(ab,aw+bv).

This is a carrier notation, not the coordinatewise direct-product ring of fields. Closure follows from field operations and vector scalar multiplication. Componentwise addition is associative and commutative; zero is(0,0), and -(a,v)=(-a,-v)=(a,v). Thus all additive abelian-group laws hold. Multiplication is commutative since ab=ba and aw+bv=bv+aw. For x=(a,v), y=(b,w), z=(c,u), both (xy)z and x(yz) equal

(abc,ab u+ac w+bc v).

This follows by field associativity and the vector-action distributive laws, and proves associativity. The element(1,0) is a two-sided identity, and differs from zero because1!=0 in F2. Zero absorbs on either side. For left distributivity, x(y+z) has scalar a(b+c)=ab+ac and vector a(w+u)+(b+c)v=aw+au+bv+cv, which is the vector of xy+xz after reordering. Right distributivity follows by commutativity or the same expansion. These checks establish every ring law: an ordinary nontrivial unital finite commutative ring.

Let E_m={(0,v):v in V}. It contains zero and is closed under subtraction. Multiplication by any(a,w) sends(0,v) to(0,av); hence E_m is an ideal.
