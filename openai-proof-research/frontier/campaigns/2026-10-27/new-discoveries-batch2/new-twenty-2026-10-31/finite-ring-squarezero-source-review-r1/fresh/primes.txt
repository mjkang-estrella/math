                                                                                  Abstract
                                                     In a recent work [5], A. Fish proved that if E1 and E2 are two subsets
                                                 of Z of positive upper Banach density, then there exists k ∈ Z such that
                                                 k · Z ⊂ (E1 − E1 ) · (E2 − E2 ) . In this article we will show that a similar
                                                 result is true for the set of primes P (which has density 0). We will
                                                 prove that there exists k ∈ N such that k · N ⊂ (P − P) · (P − P) , where
                                                 P − P = {p − q : p > q and p, q ∈ P} .


                                           1     Introduction
                                           Estimating the size of sums and products of subets of Z are widely studied in
                                           Additive combinatorics. For any two sets A, B ⊂ Z (or R), define A + B and
                                           A · B as A + B = {a + b : a ∈ A, b ∈ B} and A · B = {a · b : a ∈ A, b ∈ B} . The
                                           following conjecture of Erdős and Szemerédi [4] is one of the central problem on
                                           sum-product estimates.
                                           Conjecture 1.1. [4] If A ⊂ Z(or R) is any finite set, then for every ǫ > 0, we
                                           should have
                                                                   |A + A| + |A · A| ≫ |A|2−ǫ .
                                              The best known upper bound till date is due to Konyagin and Shkredov [1],
                                           and the proof is based on a breakthrough paper of Solymosi [16], saying that

    In [2], M. Bjőrklund and A. Fish proved that for any three set A, B and
C with positive upper Banach density, there exists k ≥ 1 such that k · Z ⊆
                               2

will discuss in subsection 1.1.
    H. Furstenberg [6] found a connection between the difference of sets of pos-
itive upper Banach density and the return time of a set with positive measure
in measurable dynamical system. Before further discussion, let us recall some
preliminaries from measurable dynamical system.

1.1    Ergodic foundation
Let (X, B, µ, T ) be a measure preserving system and A ⊆ X be a measurable
set. Denote by R (A) the set of return time defined as
                      R (A) = n ∈ Z : µ A ∩ T −n A > 0 .
                                                  

For any A ⊆ X with µ (A) > 0, we know by Poincaré’s theorem that R (A) 6= ∅.
As a consequence of Furstenberg correspondence principle [6], we have, for any
subset E ⊆ Z, with d⋆ (E) > 0, there exists a measurable dynamical system
(X, B, µ, T ) such that A ∈ B such that d⋆ (E) = µ (A) and R (A) ⊆ E − E. In
[5], A. Fish proved the following theorem.
Theorem 1.4. [5, Theorem 1.1] Let (X, µ, T ) and (Y, ν, S) be two measure
preserving system and let A ⊂ X, B ⊂ Y be two measurable sets with µ (A) > 0
and ν (B) > 0. Then there exist k ∈ Z such that k · Z ⊆ R (A) · R (B).
   As a consequence of Theorem 1.4, and Furstenberg correspondence principle,
we have the following corollary.
Corollary 1.5. If E1 and E2 are two subsets of positive density of Z , then
there exists k ∈ Z such that k · Z ⊆ (E1 − E1 ) · (E2 − E2 ) .
   The Corollary 1.5 immediately improves the result of M. Bjőrklund and A.
Fish [2].

   Before proceed let us recall some conjectures and basic results on P − P.

1.2    A brief introduction to P − P
In 1905, Maillet [12] conjectured that the set of the difference of primes should
contain all even numbers.
Conjecture 1.8. [12] Every even number is the difference of two primes.
   Originally before Maillet, there were two stronger forms of this conjecture.
In 1901, Kronecker [11] made the following conjecture.

                                            3
Conjecture 1.9. [11] Every even number can be expressed in infinitely many
ways as the difference of two primes.
   In 1849, Polignac [14] conjectured the following which is the most general
one.
Conjecture 1.10. [14] Every even number can be written in infinitely many
ways as the difference of two consecutive primes.
    Based on [7], Zhang [17] made a recent breakthrough and proved that there
exists an even number not more than 7×107 which can be expressed in infinitely
many ways as the difference of two primes. Soon after, Maynard and Tao [15, 13]
reduced the limit of such an even number to not more than 600. The best known
result now is not more than 246; for details see [15]. The following theorem is due
to Huang and Sheng Wu [8], an outstanding application of Pigeonhole principle
and Zhang-Maynard-Tao theorem [8, Theorem 3.1].
Theorem 1.11. There exists r ∈ N such that P − P is an ∆⋆r set.
   Hence it is an IPr⋆ set. As the set of primes has density 0, we can’t say
nothing about (P − P) · (P − P) .
   We will use theorem 1.11 to deduce the following result.
Theorem 1.12. There exists k ∈ N such that k · N ⊆ (P − P) · (P − P) .
   Let us recall some basic preliminaries of algebra of ultrafilters, which will be
helpfull for us.

1.3    A brief review of topological algebra
In this subsection we will recall some basic preliminaries of the algebra of ul-
trafilters, which we will use to deduce some corollaries. For details the read-
ers can see the beautiful book on algebra of ultrafilters [9] and a short re-
view [1, Chapter 2]. Denote by βN, the Stone-Čech compactification of N. It
can be shown that βN is the set of all ultrafilters over N, where the points
of N are identified with the principle ultrafilters. The basis for the topology
is Ā : A ⊆ N , where Ā = {p ∈ βN : A ∈ p}. The operation of N can be ex-
tended to βN making (βN, +) a compact, right topological semigroup. For
p, q ∈ βN and A ⊆ N, A ∈ p + q if and only if {x ∈ N : −x + A ∈ q} ∈ p, where
−x + A = {y ∈ N : x + y ∈ A}. In [3], Ellis proved that every compact right
topological semigroup contains idempotents. Note if A ∈ p = p + p, there exists
a sequence hxn in∈N such that F S (hxn in∈N ) ⊆ A. In fact the converse is also
true. That means if A contains an IP set, then there exists an idempotent p
such that A ∈ p. So, a set A is IP ⋆ if and only if A ∈ p for all idempotents
p ∈ βN.
Remark 1.13. For any n ∈ N, let A1 , A2 , . . . , An be IP ⋆ sets. Then for each
idempotents p ∈ βN, Ai ∈ p for all i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , n} . So, ∩ni=1 Ai ∈ p for all
idempotents p ∈ βN. Hence ∩ni=1 Ai is an IP ⋆ set.
