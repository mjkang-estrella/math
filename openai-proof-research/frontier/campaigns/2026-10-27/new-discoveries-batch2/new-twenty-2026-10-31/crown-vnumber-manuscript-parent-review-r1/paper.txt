    Abstract. Let G = C_{n,n} be the crown graph, obtained from the complete bipartite graph K_{n,n} by
    deleting a perfect matching, and let J_{G} be its binomial edge ideal over a field K, in 4n variables. We prove
    that for every field K, every n ≥ 4, and every cut set T           C(G), the local v-number of J_{G} at the
    determinantal prime P_{T}(G) equals four: v_{P_{T}(G)}(J_{G}) = 4. The argument is uniform over all
    fields, including characteristic two. Lower bounds follow from a vertex-support analysis of
    multihomogeneous components and a Gauss-lemma divisibility by a product of two nonassociate irreducible
    2 x 2 minors; matching upper bounds are explicit quartic witnesses. This settles the v-number conjecture for
    crown graphs, Conjecture 5.6 of Kumar, Pomeroy and Tran, simultaneously for every cut-point prime. The
    full-side cases carry the substantive step; the remaining cases and all quartic witnesses follow known source
    constructions. Ordinary proof only: the formalization status is stated in the box above and in Section 11.


1. Introduction
Throughout, K is an arbitrary field and n ≥ 4 is an integer. The crown graph G = Cn,n has vertex set V = X ∪ Y
with X = {a1, ..., an} and Y = {b1, ..., bn}, and its edges are exactly aibj with i ≠ j; thus G is Kn,n with the perfect
matching {aibi} deleted. It is connected, has 2n vertices, and is (n − 1)-regular. On the polynomial ring S =
K[xv, yv : v ∈ V] in 4n variables over K sits the binomial edge ideal JG introduced in [1]; our conventions follow
[3] and are fixed in Section 2.
For a subset T ⊆ V, the determinantal prime PT(G) is recalled in Section 2; for the crown graph, the deleted sets
T that occur are classified in [3, Theorem 3.3]. Following the source literature, the local v-number of JG at
PT(G) is the minimum total degree of a homogeneous f ∈ S with the exact colon equality (JG : f) = PT(G); see
Definition 2.3. Section 3 relates this reading to the module formulation used in [3, Lemma 5.7].
The problem. Let C(G) be the family of cut sets of G (Definition 2.3). The source [3, Section 5] proves 3 ≤
v(JG) ≤ 4 for n ≥ 4, the upper bound vPT(G)(JG) ≤ 4 for every T ∈ C(G), and equality for T = ∅; the value 4 at
every cut-point prime is Conjecture 5.6 of [3], recorded as MathDB problem 369163 [6]. The mixed
two-component cuts are known over the complex numbers by [4, Theorem 4.15]. The following uniform
statement completes the conjecture for all cut primes and all fields at once.

Theorem 1.1 (main theorem).. Let K be a field, let n ≥ 4, let G = Cn,n be the crown graph, and let JG ⊂ S be its
binomial edge ideal. Then for every T ∈ C(G),
                                              vPT(G)(JG) = 4.                                               (1)
In particular the minimum in Definition 2.3 is attained, and no homogeneous polynomial of degree at most three
has exact colon PT(G) for any cut T. A stronger equality on low-degree pieces is recorded in Corollary 9.2.
Strategy and credit. Everything rests on two elementary lemmas: an exact-separator characterization for a finite
irredundant decomposition into pairwise incomparable primes (Lemma 3.1), and a contraction lemma that tests
membership of a multihomogeneous component on its support by setting all other coordinates to zero (Lemma
4.2). The lower bound at the full-side primes PX and PY (Propositions 5.1 and 5.2) is the substantive step: a
separator of degree at most three is divisible by a product of two nonassociate irreducible quadrics, hence has

2. The crown graph and its cut-point primes
Notation 2.1 (rings, minors, ideals).. Variables are indexed by vertices: the coordinate pair of ai is (xai, yai), and
that of bj is (xbj, ybj). For distinct u, v ∈ V put [u, v] = xuyv − xvyu, a 2 x 2 minor of the two-row matrix with
columns (xw, yw)w. For W ⊆ V set D(W) = ([u, v] : u, v ∈ W) and L(W) = (xw, yw : w ∈ W). The binomial edge
ideal of G is JG = ([ ai, bj ] : i ≠ j); it is quadratic and homogeneous. All degrees in this paper are ordinary total
degrees, except for the finer vertex grading introduced in Section 4.

Definition 2.2 (cut sets).. For T ⊆ V let GV∖T denote the induced subgraph on V ∖ T. Following [1, Corollary
3.9], T is a cut set, written T ∈ C(G), if T = ∅, or if every t ∈ T is a cut vertex of the induced subgraph on (V ∖
T) ∪ {t}.

Definition 2.3 (local v-number).. Let I ⊆ S be a homogeneous ideal and P a minimal prime of I. The local
v-number of I at P is vP(I) = min{ d ≥ 1 : some homogeneous f ∈ S of degree d satisfies (I : f) = P }, provided
this set is nonempty.

Theorem 2.4 (the cut-point primes; [1], Section 3 and Theorem 3.3 of [3]).. Let n ≥ 4. The binomial edge ideal
JG is radical, and its minimal prime ideals are exactly the following pairwise distinct, pairwise incomparable
primes, whose intersection is JG:
                                  P0 = D(V), PX = L(X), PY = L(Y),
                                Qi = L(X − {ai}) + D({ai} ∪ (Y − {bi})),
                                                                                                                  (2)
                         Ri = L(Y − {bi}) + D({bi} ∪ (X − {ai})) for 1 ≤ i ≤ n,
                      Mij = L(V − {ai, bi, aj, bj}) + ([ ai, bj ], [ aj, bi ]), 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n,
Proof. By [1, Corollary 2.2] the ideal JG is radical, and by [1, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.9] its minimal primes
are the ideals PT(G) as T ranges over C(G), where for T with GV∖T having connected components C1, ..., Cr one
takes PT(G) to be L(T) together with, for each connected component C of the induced subgraph on V − T, the
ideal of 2 x 2 minors supported on the completion of C to a clique. For the crown, every completion is a clique,
so the general form specializes to the displayed ideals once the cut sets are known. The classification of cut sets
is [3, Theorem 3.3]: for n ≥ 3 the nonempty T ∈ C(G) are exactly X; Y; X − {ai}; Y − {bi}; and the sets T = A
∪ { bc : ac ∈ A } with A ⊆ X, |A| = n − 2, whose complements are V − {ai, bi, aj, bj} for a pair i < j. The empty
set occurs because G is connected, giving P0 = D(V); the mixed cut with deleted pair {i, j} gives Mij. Each
displayed ideal is prime: quotienting the L-part leaves a polynomial ring in the surviving variables and the block
is the ideal of 2 x 2 minors of a generic two-row matrix, whose quotient embeds in a polynomial ring by the
rank-one parametrization xw = s tw, yw = u tw; see also [1, Section 3]. Distinctness of the six families follows
from their degree-one parts, which are L(T) for the six distinct deleted sets T (for P0 the degree-one part is zero,
and 2n − 4 > 0 since n ≥ 4, so no mixed prime has empty L-part). As minimal primes of a radical ideal they are
pairwise incomparable, and radicality identifies their intersection with JG itself, not merely with its radical.

Remark 2.5 (normalization of the mixed indices).. Mij is indexed by the unordered pair {i, j}: Mij = Mji is a
single ideal; distinct pairs {i, j} have distinct deleted sets, hence give distinct ideals, and the same holds across
the six families. Thus a statement about Mij for unspecified i, j is to be read with i < j, and references to Mij with
j < i denote Mji.

Remark 2.6 (the shape of Q_{i} and R_{i).. In Qi the completed component is the clique on {ai} ∪ (Y − {bi}):
the vertex ai lies in the same completed component as all of Y − {bi}, while bi is isolated. Omitting ai from the
determinantal block would give a different, wrong ideal. In particular, one cannot test nonmembership in Qi by
setting all X-coordinates to zero; the correct specializations are computed in Section 9. The description of Ri is
symmetric.

3. The exact-separator lemma
Lemma 3.1 (exact separators).. Let J = ∩λ Pλ be a finite irredundant decomposition into primes which are
pairwise incomparable, and let Pτ be one of them. Then for f ∈ S one has (J : f) = Pτ if and only if f ∉ Pτ and f ∈
Pλ for every λ ≠ τ.
Proof. For a prime P and f ∈ S one has (P : f) = P if f ∉ P, and (P : f) = S if f ∈ P: the first because P prime and
f, g ∉ P force fg ∉ P, so (P : f) ⊆ P; the second because fg ∈ P for all g when f ∈ P. Distributing the colon over
the decomposition, (J : f) = ∩ { Pλ : f ∉ Pλ }. If f ∉ Pτ and f ∈ Pλ for λ ≠ τ, the index set is {τ} and the equality
follows. Conversely suppose (J : f) = Pτ. The index set is nonempty, for otherwise (J : f) = S, while Pτ is proper.
If the index set contained no τ, then from each intersectand Pλ, λ ≠ τ, one may choose gλ ∈ Pλ ∖ Pτ, which is
possible because the primes are incomparable and distinct; their product lies in the intersection, hence in Pτ, but
outside Pτ since the latter is prime, a contradiction. Pairwise incomparability likewise excludes any additional
intersectand besides Pτ: if Pτ ∩ Pμ appeared with μ ≠ τ the intersection would equal Pτ only if Pτ ⊆ Pμ. Hence
the index set is exactly {τ}, that is, f ∉ Pτ and f ∈ Pλ for all other λ. □

Remark 3.2 (zero and constant witnesses; the module reading).. If f = 0 then (J : f) = S, and if f is a nonzero
constant then f lies in no member of the decomposition and (J : f) = J; neither is a proper cut prime, so every
exact-colon witness is nonconstant. Furthermore, distributing as in the proof and using incomparability exactly
as above gives (J : Pτ) = ∩λ≠τ Pλ, because (Pλ : Pτ) = Pλ for λ ≠ τ: if g ∉ Pλ and gPτ ⊆ Pλ, the image of Pτ in the
domain S/Pλ is annihilated by a nonzero class, hence vanishes, forcing Pτ ⊆ Pλ. Consequently a class g of
positive degree in (∩λ≠τPλ)/J lies outside Pτ, being outside J while inside every other factor, and Lemma 3.1
turns it into an exact-colon witness; and conversely, via the same two lemmas, a homogeneous f with (J : f) = Pτ
is the same datum as a nonzero class in (∩λ≠τPλ)/J of the same degree. This is the reading of the invariant used
in [3, Lemma 5.7]; here it is derived directly, and no colon inclusion is ever treated as an equality of ideals.
Throughout the paper, (2) denotes this six-family list; its members are the minimal primes of JG, and unindexed
references such as Pλ range over it. For the lower bound it suffices to prove the following implication, for every
Pτ in (2) and every homogeneous f of degree d ≤ 3:
                                       f ∈ ∩λ≠τ Pλ implies f ∈ Pτ.                                               (3)
Indeed, if (JG : f) = Pτ with d ≤ 3, Lemma 3.1 gives f ∉ Pτ and f ∈ Pλ for all other λ; (3) then forces f ∈ Pτ as
well. A homogeneous f of degree at most three with (JG : f) = Pτ would thus satisfy both f ∉ Pτ, by Lemma 3.1,
and f ∈ Pτ, by (3): a contradiction. Hence, once (3) is proved for a prime Pτ, no homogeneous polynomial of
degree at most three has exact colon Pτ, i.e. vPτ(JG) ≥ 4. Sections 5, 6 and 7 prove (3) for each family of (2).

and their intersection. In consequence:

Lemma 4.1 (multihomogeneous components).. Let I be one of the ideals in (2) or JG itself, and f ∈ S with
multihomogeneous components fα of multidegree α. Then f ∈ I if and only if fα ∈ I for every α. A nonzero
component of ordinary degree d has vertex support supp(α) = { v : αv > 0 } of size at most d; if the size equals 3
and d ≤ 3, then d = 3 and α is 1 at each supported vertex. Moreover, if supp(α) meets the deleted set T of a
prime PT from (2), then every monomial of fα is divisible by xw or yw for some w ∈ supp(α) ∩ T, so fα ∈ L(T) ⊆
PT automatically.
Proof. Homogeneity of I gives I = ⊕α Iα as a direct sum of its pieces, and a decomposition of f into
multihomogeneous parts is unique, so membership is componentwise. If fα ≠ 0 its ordinary degree is the sum
∑v αv (each monomial of multidegree α has total degree ∑αv), giving |supp(α)| ≤ d, with equality forcing α ≡
1 on its support when the support has three vertices and d ≤ 3. For the last claim, a monomial of multidegree α
involves the variable pair of v whenever αv > 0, since the total exponent of xv and yv in that monomial is exactly
αv. If some w ∈ supp(α) ∩ T, every monomial is therefore divisible by xw or yw ∈ L(T). □

Lemma 4.2 (retraction and contraction).. Let U ⊆ V and let π : S → SU = K[xv, yv : v ∈ U] be the retraction
setting all coordinates outside U to zero, so that π restricts to the identity on SU. For each ideal I of (2), the
contraction I ∩ SU is generated, as an ideal of SU, by exactly those displayed generators whose support lies in U:
the surviving linear coordinates xw, yw with w ∈ U ∩ (deleted set), and the minors [u, v] with u, v ∈ U from the
determinantal block. Consequently, a polynomial h supported in U (i.e. in SU) satisfies h ∈ I if and only if h lies
in that survivor ideal.
Proof. Each displayed generator maps under π either to itself, when both indices lie in U, or to 0, when some
index does not; this holds for linear generators and for minors alike. Hence π(I) is the survivor ideal. If h ∈ I ∩
SU then h = π(h) ∈ π(I), since π fixes SU pointwise; conversely π(g) ∈ I for every generator, being either a
generator or 0, so the survivor ideal is contained in I ∩ SU. This is a computation on ideals, not on monomials: it
applies to arbitrary coefficient combinations, repeated vertex degrees and all cancellations, and no elimination
theorem or determinantal-quotient structure is used. The characterization of membership of supported
polynomials follows since such h lies in SU. □

Remark 4.3.. Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2 together reduce every membership below to a statement about polynomial
rings in at most three pairs of variables. Characteristic two requires no separate treatment: no sign, no
determinant and no factoriality argument below depends on the characteristic, and the two spots where a sign
appears explicitly (the evaluations in Section 8) are computed with the sign retained.

5. The lower bound at the full-side primes
Proposition 5.1 (P_{X}).. Every homogeneous f of degree d ≤ 3 with f ∈ ∩λ≠PXPλ satisfies f ∈ PX; thus
implication (3) holds for PX.
Proof. Work componentwise (Lemma 4.1). A component whose support meets X lies in PX = L(X)
automatically. Consider a nonzero component h supported in U ⊆ Y. Since PY, all Qi, all Ri, all Mij and P0 are
among the primes of (2) different from PX, membership h ∈ Qi for every i is available. By Lemma 4.2, h ∈ Qi ∩
SU, and the survivor ideal of Qi in SU is generated by the minors of D(Y − {bi}) with both endpoints in U; there
are none unless |U − {bi}| ≥ 2. □

If |U| ≤ 2, choose i ∈ U when U is nonempty and any i otherwise. Fewer than two supported vertices survive in
Y − {bi}, so the survivor ideal is zero and h = 0.
Otherwise |U| = 3, so d = 3 and, by Lemma 4.1, α equals 1 at each vertex of U = {br, bs, bt}. The restrictions of
Qr and Qs give, in SU,

                                             h ∈ ([ bs, bt ]) and h ∈ ([ br, bt ]).

These two quadrics are irreducible and nonassociate in the polynomial unique factorization domain SU, over
any field. Indeed, as a polynomial in xbs, the minor [ bs, bt ] = xbsybt − xbtybs is linear and primitive: its
coefficients ybt and −xbtybs have no nonconstant common divisor, so Gauss’s lemma proves irreducibility.
Nonassociation follows from the distinct vertex multidegrees of the two minors. Irreducible elements of this
unique factorization domain are prime, and two nonassociate irreducibles are coprime; since both divide h, their
product divides h. A nonzero multiple of that product has degree at least 4 > 3 = d, so h = 0. The coefficient pair
is coprime in characteristic two as well, where − equals +; no sign or factoriality beyond the polynomial ring SU
is used, and in particular no unique factorization of a determinantal quotient. Hence every pure-Y component of
f vanishes, the remaining components lie in PX, and f ∈ PX.

Proposition 5.2 (P_{Y}).. Implication (3) holds for PY as well: every homogeneous f of degree ≤ 3 with f ∈
∩λ≠PYPλ satisfies f ∈ PY.
Proof. Apply the symmetry that exchanges x with y and ai with bi for each index. It fixes JG, P0 and each Mij,
exchanges PX and PY, and exchanges Qi with Ri; it preserves homogeneity and degree. The image of a preimage
f under this symmetry satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition 5.1 with PY in place of PX, and the conclusion
pulls back. □

Remark 5.3.. Propositions 5.1 and 5.2 are the obstructions that the earlier partial results do not supply: a
separator for PX of degree at most three would have to be a common multiple of two nonassociate irreducible
quadrics, hence of degree at least four. The argument uses unique factorization only in the polynomial ring SU,
never in a determinantal quotient, and is valid over every field including characteristic two.

6. The lower bound at the one-deleted-side primes
Proposition 6.1 (Q_{i}).. For every i, implication (3) holds for Qi: every homogeneous f of degree ≤ 3 with f ∈
∩λ≠QiPλ satisfies f ∈ Qi.
Proof. Work componentwise, and fix i. A component whose support meets X − {ai} lies in L(X − {ai})
automatically. So consider a nonzero component h with support U ⊆ {ai} ∪ Y. Since PX is a prime of (2)
different from Qi, h ∈ PX; the piece of PX = L(X) in the multidegree of h is nonzero only if some coordinate xaa
with aa ∈ X has positive degree, and the only vertex of X available is ai: thus αai > 0, i.e. h has positive
ai-degree. □

If bi ∉ U: membership in P0 = D(V) holds, and Lemma 4.2 gives h ∈ D(U), the survivor ideal being generated
by all pairs in U. Since U ⊆ {ai} ∪ (Y − {bi}) in this case, D(U) ⊆ D({ai} ∪ (Y − {bi})) ⊆ Qi, as required.
If bi ∈ U: the degree is at most three and both ai and bi occur, leaving room for at most one further vertex, which
would be some bj with j ≠ i (no further ac can occur, the support being contained in {ai} ∪ Y). If such a bj
occurs, then U = {ai, bi, bj} exactly. The prime Mij (its pair unordered, Remark 2.5) is a member of (2) different
from Qi, so h ∈ Mij; by Lemma 4.2 its survivor ideal in SU is generated solely by [ ai, bj ], the other generator [
aj, bi ] having the unsupported endpoint aj. Hence h ∈ ([ ai, bj ]) ⊆ Qi, since ai and bj ∈ Y − {bi} lie in the block.
If no such bj occurs, then U = {ai, bi}. Choose any j ≠ i and use Mij again: its contraction to the ring in xai, yai,
xbi, ybi is zero, because both defining minors have an endpoint outside the pair; hence h = 0. The argument
nowhere assumes squarefree exponents, so arbitrary powers supported on these two vertices are covered as
well.
Every component has now been treated, so f ∈ Qi. This is a symbolic support argument, uniform in n: h
throughout is an arbitrary homogeneous polynomial in the indicated multidegree, not a tested monomial.

Corollary 6.2 (R_{i}).. For every i, implication (3) holds for Ri as well.
Proof. This is Proposition 6.1 read through the symmetry of the proof of Proposition 5.2, which exchanges Qi
and Ri while preserving the list (2), homogeneity and degree. □

7. The lower bound at the empty and mixed primes
Proposition 7.1 (P_{0}).. Implication (3) holds for P0 = D(V).
Proof. Let h be a nonzero component of f, supported in U, not automatic in P0: since D(V) has empty L-part, no
support is automatic, and the argument below uses the other memberships. Membership in PX and PY (both
different from P0) forces, by Lemma 4.1, positive degree at some vertex of X and at some vertex of Y: the
support meets both sides. With degree at most three, one side is a singleton, say {ai} on the X-side (the other
case is symmetric). Then h ∈ Qi, and the survivor ideal of Qi in SU is generated by minors [ ai, bc ] with bc ∈ U
∩ (Y − {bi}), the L-part L(X − {ai}) contributing nothing as no other ac occurs. Each such minor has both
endpoints in U, so h ∈ D(U) ⊆ D(V) = P0. If instead the singleton is bj on the Y-side, the same argument with Rj
applies. This independently recovers the already known lower bound at the empty prime, where the exact value
4 is [3, Lemma 5.1] via connected domination [2]. □

Proposition 7.2 (the mixed primes M_{ij).. For every pair i < j, implication (3) holds for Mij.
Proof. Fix the pair and write W = {ai, bi, aj, bj}. A component that is not automatic in Mij has support U ⊆ W,
the deleted set of Mij being V − W. Membership in PX and PY again forces both sides in the support, and degree
at most three forces one side to be a singleton: one of ai, aj, bi, bj. If it is ai, h ∈ Qi, whose survivor ideal in SU is
generated, if at all, by [ ai, bj ]: the L-part contributes nothing (aj ∉ U), and among the block minors only [ ai, bj ]
has both endpoints in U. Likewise aj singleton gives [ aj, bi ] via Qj, and a singleton bi or bj gives, via Ri or Rj in
exactly the symmetric way, the minor [ aj, bi ] or [ ai, bj ], or nothing. All these testing primes differ from Mij —
they remain distinct even at n = 4, by Remark 2.5 and distinct deleted sets — and each displayed minor is a
generator of Mij. Hence h ∈ Mij or h = 0, and f ∈ Mij. This support route also avoids the informal comparison of
two representations modulo JG used in the proof of [3, Lemma 5.7]. □

Theorem 7.3 (the lower bound).. For every field K, every n ≥ 4, and every T ∈ C(G), no homogeneous
polynomial of degree at most three has exact colon PT(G); equivalently, vPT(G)(JG) ≥ 4.
Proof. By Theorem 2.4 the minimal primes of JG are exactly the members of (2), and PT(G) is one of them.
Propositions 5.1, 5.2 and 6.1, Corollary 6.2 and Propositions 7.1 and 7.2 establish implication (3) for every
member. The reduction after Remark 3.2 then rules out separators of degree at most three, as recorded there. □

8. Quartic witnesses
The witnesses of this section are homogeneous of degree four; their membership and nonmembership
verifications are direct. They are the constructions of [3, Proposition 5.2], whose published Q-case carries a
copied-subscript slip, corrected here by explicit verification; no witness is claimed as new. Distinct indices i, j, k
appear below; the full-side witnesses use four distinct vertices of one side, which is where n ≥ 4 is needed,
while the remaining witnesses need at most three distinct indices.

Construction 8.1 (a witness for P_{0}).. Put f = xa1xa3xb1xb2.
Proof. Every member of (2) other than P0 contains f: its deleted set meets {a1, a3, b1, b2}. The full sides do so
trivially; a one-deleted side retains all but one vertex of its side; and a mixed prime deletes only two indices,
whereas these four vertices involve the three indices 1, 2, 3, so some supported vertex survives in the L-part and
the corresponding linear generator divides f. For nonmembership in P0 = D(V), specialize xv = tv and yv = 0:
every generator [u, v] of D(V) maps to tu·0 − tv·0 = 0, so the image of P0 is zero, while f maps to the nonzero
monomial ta1ta3tb1tb2. Hence (JG : f) = P0 by Lemma 3.1. □

k the factor xbk lies in L(Y − {bl}), while if l = k the minor lies in L(Y − {bk}), its monomials being divisible
by coordinates of bi or bj, indices distinct from k. Finally let Mrs be a mixed prime, with deleted set T = V − {ar,
br, as, bs}. If ai ∈ T, every monomial of f is divisible by xai; if instead bk ∈ T, every monomial is divisible by xbk;
otherwise i, k ∈ {r, s}, i.e. {r, s} = {i, k}, and then bj ∈ T, so the two monomials xaixbkxbiybj and xaixbkxbjybi are
divisible by ybj and xbj respectively. In every case some divisor of each monomial lies in L(T), whence f ∈
Mrs. □

Nonmembership in Qi. Let φ be the ring homomorphism that keeps xai and every xbc, bc ∈ Y − {bi}, as
indeterminates, and sends to zero the following coordinates: both coordinates of every ac with c ≠ i; yai; ybc for
all bc ∈ Y − {bi}; and xbi, while ybi is sent to a new indeterminate s. Every generator of Qi maps to 0 — the
linear generators by construction, and each block minor because its monomials contain a vanishing
y-coordinate. Hence the image of Qi is zero, while the image of the witness is

                                         φ(f) = xaixbk·( xbi·0 − xbj·s ) = −xaixbkxbj·s,

the negative of a nonzero monomial, nonzero in every characteristic. Thus f ∉ Qi, and Lemma 3.1 gives (JG : f)
= Qi. The symmetric witness ybiyak[ ai, aj ] for Ri is obtained from the symmetry of Section 5, which exchanges
Qi and Ri, preserves the list (2) and degrees, and whose mirrored specialization proves nonmembership; we do
not spell the mirror out.

Construction 8.4 (a witness for M_{ij}).. For i < j put f = [ ai, aj ]·[ bi, bj ], a product of one X-pair minor and
one Y-pair minor.
Proof. Membership. Each factor is a generator of D(V), so f ∈ P0. The X-pair minor [ ai, aj ] lies in L(X), its
monomials xaiyaj and xajyai being divisible by X-coordinates; hence f ∈ PX, and symmetrically f ∈ PY through
the Y-pair minor. For Ql: the X-pair minor lies in L(X − {al}) for every l, since each of xaiyaj, xajyai is divisible
by a coordinate of ac with c ∈ {i,j} ∩ ([n] − {l}) with c ≠ l, which exists because i ≠ j. Hence f ∈ Ql for every l,
and, symmetrically through the Y-pair minor, f ∈ Rl for every l. Let Mrs ≠ Mij be a mixed prime, with deleted
set T. At least one of ai, aj lies in T, since otherwise {r, s} = {i, j}; write ac for it, c ∈ {i, j}. Both monomials of
the X-pair minor are then divisible by xac or yac, so that minor, and with it f, lies in L(T) ⊆ Mrs. □

Nonmembership in Mij. Evaluate the coordinate columns (xai, yai) and (xbj, ybj) at (1, 0), the columns (xaj, yaj)
and (xbi, ybi) at (0, 1), and all remaining columns at 0. The linear generators of the L-part vanish, and the two
block minors evaluate as 1·0 − 1·0 = 0 and 0·1 − 0·1 = 0, so the image of Mij is zero. The image of the witness
is

                                               ( 1·1 − 0·0 )·( 0·0 − 1·1 ) = −1,

nonzero in every characteristic, characteristic two included. Hence f ∉ Mij, and Lemma 3.1 gives (JG : f) = Mij.
This construction needs only two distinct indices; the full-side witnesses of Construction 8.2 are the ones
requiring four distinct vertices, hence n ≥ 4.

Theorem 9.1 (Theorem 1.1 restated).. For every field K, every n ≥ 4, and every T ∈ C(G), vPT(G)(JG) = 4.
Proof. By Theorem 2.4, PT(G) is one of the primes of (2). For the upper bound, take for that prime the witness
of Constructions 8.1 to 8.4 (P0: 8.1; PX and PY: 8.2; Qi and Ri: 8.3; Mij: 8.4). Each witness is homogeneous of
degree 4, lies in every other member of (2), and is outside its own prime; by Lemma 3.1 its exact colon is the
prime, so v ≤ 4. The lower bound is Theorem 7.3. □

Corollary 9.2 (the low-degree equality).. For every prime Pτ of (2) and every d ≤ 3,

                                                      (∩λ ≠ τ Pλ)d = (JG)d,

Proof. For the nontrivial inclusion take a homogeneous h of degree d in the left side. Implication (3) for the
prime Pτ, proved in Sections 5 to 7, gives h ∈ Pτ; combined with membership in the other factors, h lies in every
member of (2), i.e. in JG. The reverse inclusion is immediate since JG is contained in every Pλ. By Remark 3.2
the left side is also (JG : Pτ)d. □

Remark 9.3.. Corollary 9.2 packages the lower bounds in ideal-theoretic form and is an explicit target for
independent re-verification during the formalization effort. We also record the bookkeeping used throughout:
the zero polynomial is not a witness, since a proper prime is never the unit ideal; and each low-degree equality
is a statement about homogeneous pieces, to be read through the multigrading of Section 4.

10. Relation to the source and prior results
The source. Reference [3] proves vP∅(G)(JG) = 4 (its Lemma 5.1, via the connected-domination identity for the
empty prime in [2, Theorem 3.2] and γc(G) = 4), the bounds 3 ≤ v(JG) ≤ 4 and vPT(G)(JG) ≤ 4 (its Lemmas 5.4
and Proposition 5.2, respectively, the latter supplying the quartic witnesses used here), and poses the all-cuts
statement as its Conjecture 5.6. Its Lemma 5.7 treats the mixed cuts T = A ∪ { bc : ac ∈ A } with |A| = n − 2
through the identity vPT(JG) = α((JG : PT)/JG), cited there from the colon-formula literature in the lineage of [7];
its proof compares two representations modulo JG, a step the support argument of Section 7 avoids. Two
presentational defects of the published text are corrected here without changing the mathematics: the
mixed-prime indexing is normalized to unordered pairs (Remark 2.5), and the Q-case of [3, Proposition 5.2]
verifies its witness against the X-primes, a copied subscript slip whose intended statement is verified directly in
Construction 8.3.
Two-component cuts over the complex numbers. Reference [4] works over C and its localized results require a
minimal cut in the sense of its Definition 2.3: the deleted set leaves exactly two connected components. Its
Theorem 4.15 applies to the crown cuts Qi, Ri and Mij, whose complements have two components: for Qi a
connected dominating set in the star component must contain ai and two distinct bc, bd with c, d ≠ i, while the
isolated component contributes bi, giving 3 + 1 = 4; for Mij each edge component contributes both endpoints,
giving 2 + 2 = 4; Ri is symmetric. The full-side cuts PX and PY instead leave n ≥ 4 singleton components,
outside the two-component hypothesis; the general upper bound, Theorem 3.5 of [4], yields the localized value
only under a radicality hypothesis on the colon that its proof does not extract from the degree of a transversal,
and no transfer across fields is stated there. Thus [4] covers neither PX, PY nor uniform positive characteristic.
Combinatorial bounds. Reference [5] studies transversal bounds vS−(G) ≤ vPS(G)(JG) ≤ vS+(G). For the crown it
yields vX− = 3, uniformly for n ≥ 4: any two Y-vertices miss a hyperedge of the relevant outside-X cuts, while
every three-element set meets each. Its equality criterion requires every surviving component to be noncomplete
(hypothesis (H) there); for every nonempty crown cut some component is a singleton or a complete edge, so (H)
fails and the gap 3–4 is not closed there either.
Closed-graph formulas. Reference [9] computes local v-numbers for generalized binomial edge ideals; its
strongest formula requires the graph to be connected and closed, in which setting maximal cliques carry interval
labels. A crown with n ≥ 4 has an induced claw — a1 with three of its Y-neighbors — and an induced claw
cannot sit inside interval-labeled cliques, since two of its leaves force a leaf-leaf edge; the formula therefore

does not apply to crowns. Its remaining results (the empty prime, and the classification of values 1 and 2) do not
bear on the full-side degree-three obstruction.
Summary of provenance. The empty value is known ([3, Lemma 5.1] with [2]); the mixed value is known, in
the present generality of the lower bound re-derived in Section 7 and, over C, by [4]; the one-deleted-side
values are known over C by [4, Theorem 4.15], and Proposition 6.1 removes the field restriction by an
elementary argument; the full-side lower bounds of Section 5 are, per the bounded comparison above, not
supplied by the literature inspected, and they are what makes the uniform statement Theorem 1.1 possible. The
quartic witnesses are due to [3, Proposition 5.2]. No claim of worldwide novelty, priority, or exhaustive
coverage is made: the accompanying record keeps version, chronology and citing-coverage questions open as
bounded, not complete. No publisher text is reproduced in this draft; only statements, values and bibliographic
data appear, the latter checked against the retained sources.
