          A Four-Edge Obstruction to Submodular Separation
               of Quadratic Boolean Monomial Images
                               Research draft prepared with OpenAI Codex assistance
                                   No human authorship or institutional afﬁliation is asserted.

The low-degree Boolean monomial image at degree two in ﬁve variables cannot be the negative set of a submodular in-
teger-valued function. This refutes Conjecture 3.33 in the read 2019 preprints of Brakensiek, Gopi and Guruswami, even
after its efﬁcient-evaluation requirement is removed. The shortest proof is an explicit four-edge dependency cycle, apply-
ing Elbracht-Kneip-Teegen. An independent symmetric certiﬁcate has thirteen rows and is the argument formalized by the
supplied Lean development. We give both certiﬁcates and exact checks of smaller feasible cases. The 2022 journal version
of record remains unread; retention of the conjecture there is not certiﬁed. This is a draft, with AI assistance, not an ac-
ceptance or priority determination.

The shortest proof: a dependency cycle
  Theorem. There is no submodular function f : {0, 1}16 → Z satisfying f ≤ −1 on F 2,5 and f ≥ 0 outside it.
Consequently Conjecture 3.33 fails without its evaluation-time clause.
   Work on the face where the empty-monomial coordinate is one. Label the other coordinates, in order, by
                               0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 01, 02, 03, 04, 12, 13, 14, 23, 24, 34.
A label such as 01 denotes the pair {0, 1}. A decimal mask m encodes these ﬁfteen bits, with the ﬁrst coordi-
nate the least signiﬁcant bit. Write q(X) for the mask of all singleton and pair labels contained in
X⊆{0, 1, 2, 3, 4}. The thirty-two good masks are exactly the monomial image on this face. A mask outside that
image is bad. The restriction h of f remains submodular, since the face is a sublattice. Thus an obstruction
here is an obstruction on the full cube.
   Elbracht, Kneip and Teegen [2, Lemma 4.1 and Corollary 4.2] show that the dependency digraph of an
order-induced submodular partial lattice is acyclic. In the outer-edge case used here, a, b are bad, c is good,
and b is one of a∩c and a∪c while the other point is bad. Submodularity forces strict decrease from a to b;
our signs give a decrease of at least one.
   The independent review supplies the following four rows. All meets and joins are bitwise AND and OR.
                           a        b     good c     a AND c       a OR c     input X for c
                         103     5743      5646            6         5743     {1,2,3}
                        5743     1067     19899         1067        24575     {0,1,3,4}
                        1067     5359      4301            9         5359     {0,2,3}
                        5359      103     11127          103        16383     {0,1,2,4}

  In each row c = q(X). The starting points, endpoints, and the other meet/join points 6, 24575, 9, 16383 are
bad: each fails the test that its pair bits are exactly all pairs of its singleton support. The four submodular in-
equalities give, respectively,
                                h(5743) ≤ h(103) + h(5646) − h(6) ≤ h(103) − 1,

                           h(1067) ≤ h(5743) + h(19899) − h(24575) ≤ h(5743) − 1,

                               h(5359) ≤ h(1067) + h(4301) − h(9) ≤ h(1067) − 1,

                            h(103) ≤ h(5359) + h(11127) − h(16383) ≤ h(5359) − 1.
Adding cancels every function value and yields 0 ≤ −4, a contradiction. This is the shortest proof presented
here; no graph-girth minimality claim is needed. It even rules out real-valued separators with these signs. This
applies the published obstruction criterion; no new general obstruction is claimed.

An independent symmetric certiﬁcate
   The supplied Lean proof uses a different certiﬁcate. Sum over all permutations of the ﬁve input variables:
                                              g(m) =       Σ h(σ m).
                                                       σ ∈ S5

A permutation renames both singleton and pair labels. It preserves meet, join and the good family. Hence g is
submodular, constant on permutation orbits, at most −120 on good masks and nonnegative on bad ones. This is
a sum, not a normalized average; no division is used.

Here 615 = q({0, 1, 2}) and 5871 = q({0, 1, 2, 3}), whereas 3 and 16383 are bad. The sum of all thirteen
weighted left sides vanishes identically for orbit-invariant g. Thus
                                            0 ≤ −600 − 240 = −840,
again a contradiction.
   Check cancellation without trusting orbit labels or a numerical solver: expand every g as its sum of h values
over all 120 permutations. The resulting integer coefﬁcient of each actual mask is zero. Our fresh isolated
Python veriﬁer regenerates the coordinate action and good family from their deﬁnitions. It veriﬁes all meet/join
identities after renaming, all sign classiﬁcations, and every coefﬁcient. There are 1,080 submodular row in-
stances, touching 365 distinct masks across the complete certiﬁcate, and no residual coefﬁcient. Total good-
row weight seven gives the upper bound −120 × 7 = −840.
   The weights are exact integers. The historical search used linear programming to locate and compress the
obstruction, but no optimization oracle or ﬂoating-point assertion is a premise of either proof. Our fresh veriﬁ-
cation runs no solver. The cycle proof is shorter; this larger certiﬁcate is retained because it is the one proved
by the existing Lean development.

The exact Lean assertion
   A point in the packet is a ﬁnite set of ﬁnite subsets of Fin d. It is allowed at degree r if each label has cardi-
nality at most r. The allowed points form exactly the Boolean lattice on the low-degree labels, including the
empty label. The function phi is the ﬁltered powerset of the input support: the source monomial map in coor-
dinate-labeled form.
   Separator d r f requires submodularity for every two allowed points, negativity on every image
point, and nonnegativity on every allowed point outside the image. Values on disallowed points impose no con-
straint and can be extended arbitrarily. SeparationExistence universally quantiﬁes over natural numbers
d, r with r ≤ d, demanding an integer-valued separator for each pair.
   Separator.lean proves permutation and group-sum bridges. Certificate.lean inserts the empty
label in decode, proves ﬁnite permutation identities and the thirteen-row contradiction, and specializes the
universal assertion at d = 5, r = 2. Its endpoint is:
Separation.source_existence_refuted :
  not Separation.SeparationExistence
This refutes bare existence, hence the stronger efﬁcient-evaluation assertion. The packet does not formalize a
complexity model or a separate bijection to a numerically indexed cube; the coordinate-model identiﬁcation
above is a semantic correspondence checked in the supplied independent review.

Related work and scope
    Nägele, Sudakov and Zenklusen [3] develop submodular minimization under congruency constraints, in-
cluding constant prime-power moduli, and relate correctness to forbidden set systems. That supplies the algo-
rithmic context of the source question. The negative-set realization in Conjecture 3.33 is a separate assertion;
its failure does not invalidate the prime-power algorithms.
   Elbracht, Kneip and Teegen [2] provide the dependency-digraph obstruction used in our shortest proof. We
credit their Lemma 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 explicitly. Their partial-lattice formulation applies on our constant-
one face without requiring a symmetric order function. The concrete failing monomial instance is the content
here; no new general obstruction is claimed. We make no priority claim for identifying this instance or for any
part of its method.
   Later work on congruency-constrained totally unimodular problems [4] and strictly delta-modular integer
programs [5] develops related reductions and algorithms. The supplied bounded primary-body review found no
resolution of the monomial-image separator assertion in those texts. Its registry and citation screens have cov-
erage limits. They record what was checked, not exhaustive novelty clearance. No live search was performed
during this preparation.
   The signiﬁcance is modest: an interior-parameter counterexample blocks the universal separator premise of
a proposed hardness approach. Neither tractability nor hardness of modular submodular minimization for gen-
eral moduli follows. A replacement reduction or restricted separator hypothesis would need its own analysis.
