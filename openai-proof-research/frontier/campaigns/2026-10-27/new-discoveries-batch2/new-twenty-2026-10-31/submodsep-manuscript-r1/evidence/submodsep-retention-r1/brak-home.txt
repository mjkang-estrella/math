     Research 
    My primary research agenda is to tackle fundamental questions in theoretical computer science and mathematics, particularly questions inspired by modern challenges in computing. Towards this goal, I apply theoretical and computational techniques from many areas including combinatorics, algebra, convex optimization, and formal methods.
     
     
    Some of my recent works with various collaborators include:
     
        Formulating the theory of  higher order  MDS  codes  which unify a number of concepts in coding theory including connecting the recoverability of a real-world distributed storage architecture with fundamental questions in matroid theory. Of recent interest are connections to  subspace design codes . 
       
        Advancing the theory of  (promise)  constraint satisfaction problems , which has led to exciting connections between the theory of approximation algorithms and universal algebra. 
       
        Understanding the sparsifiability of constraint satisfaction problems. Many such questions can be unified under a common combinatorial framework known as  non-redundancy . 
       
        Ascertaining the structure of  edit distance , including finding improved approximation algorithms for computing edit distance and finding better error-correcting codes which can withstand edit distance errors.  
       
        Using SAT-solving/formal methods to resolve a 90-year-old question in mathematics ( Keller's conjecture  in seven dimensions), as featured in  Quanta ,  Wired , and  Popular Mechanics .
