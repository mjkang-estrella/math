## 353152: complete domain correction, excluded

Take n=3, p=2, q=1, u=1, t=1/2. Definition 4.1 explicitly permits these positive integer parameters with n>q. Each state is a position j on the three-cycle and one species s in {1,2}. At fixed species the particle moves one way with rate/probability 1/9 and the other with 1/18. It switches species at its position with probability 1/9 in either direction. Thus every row and every column has off-diagonal sum 5/18 and diagonal 13/18. The uniform six-state distribution is stationary. All states communicate (positive steps around the cycle and positive species switches), so finite irreducibility gives uniqueness; alternatively a maximal stationary-coordinate argument propagates equality backwards along every positive edge. All coordinates are therefore 1/6.

The two partitions allowed by q=1 are (1,0,0) and (2,0,0). Each ASEP has three states and has clockwise/counterclockwise probabilities 1/3 and 1/6, with diagonal 1/2. Again each column sum is one and the directed cycle is irreducible, giving stationary coordinates 1/3. Every within-partition ratio for both processes is 1. Hence the literal equivalence fails at t=1/2. This is the elementary one-particle degeneracy, a domain correction, and earns zero credit. No nondegenerate conjecture is solved. ## 323414: adjacent-level injection and a precise recurrence

Use the primary Definition 1.1: R_n is uniform on subsets of {0,...,n-1}; R'_n conditions on 0, and R''_n conditions on both 0 and n-1. The statistic counts missing sums only in [0,n-1], with repeated summands allowed. It is not the source's full [0,2n-2] statistic, whose tail blips are proved separately.

For the conditioned model R'_n, let B be a subset of [0,n-2] containing 0, let M(B) count its missing sums in [0,n-2], and let E(B) mean n-1 is not in B+B. Let c_n(m) count such B with M(B)=m and let a_n(m) count those additionally satisfying E. Adjoining the independent final bit n-1 changes no sum below n-1. If E holds, omission of the bit creates one additional missing sum, and inclusion fills it using 0+(n-1). If E fails, either bit leaves the count unchanged. Therefore the number of R'_n subsets with missing count m is exactly

    2 c_n(m) - a_n(m) + a_n(m-1).

In R''_n the last bit is forced present, so the count distribution is exactly that of M(B), with denominator 2^(n-2) for n>=2. This is an exact useful reduction, not an answer: the sequence c_n is itself the previous conditioned distribution, while a_n encodes a correlated no-pair-sum event. Monotonicity of c_n alone does not control the difference a_n(m)-a_n(m-1). The unconditional model has the further mixture according to whether 0 is present; its 0-absent summand does not obey this same final-bit formula.

First route: insert the least missing element into a subset to map level m+1 to m. This can create multiple previously missing sums simultaneously, so it need not land at m. Adding x creates every x+b as well as 2x. A marker could invert the insertion, but preserving exact level and obtaining the required injection remains unproved. Second route: reflect the set or complement it. Reflection sends the low interval to the high interval of the full sumset and does not preserve the required statistic; set complementation does not complement a sumset. Third route: prove shape preservation through the recurrence above. Missing lemma: an appropriate bound on successive differences of a_n relative to c_n, valid after the actual mode and through all three exceptional-n clauses. No such bound was proved. The source's conditional probabilities for individual consecutive sums do not supply the distribution of their total. Figure captions and tail discussion were read; printed figure data and complete companion coverage were not. No claimed refutation from the word decreasing versus strictly decreasing.

## 366601: finite path histories, three routes

The literal source is Appendix A.1, Conjecture A.19. A regeneration means an edge used exactly once over the entire n-step walk. It does not mean a last exit or a first visit. Both the event of any regeneration and the stronger count dominance must be distinguished.

For n=3, write o for the start and v for its first neighbor. The path o,v,o,v has zero singleton edges; o,v,o,w with w!=v has one; o,v,w,v with w!=o has one; every nonbacktracking path of length three has three. These are exhaustive by tree acyclicity. The no-regeneration probability is

    (1/deg(o)) sum_{v adjacent o} 1/(deg(v) deg(o)) <= 1/9.

More simply it is (1/deg(o)^2) sum_v 1/deg(v). The probability of three singletons is (1/deg(o)) sum_v ((deg(v)-1)/deg(v)) times an average over w!=o of (deg(w)-1)/deg(w), hence at least 4/9. The cubic values are respectively 1/9 and 4/9. Singleton counts are only 0,1,3, so these two bounds give all stochastic tails at n=3. This is only a helper/parameter instance and earns zero credit.

First full route: couple distances from the root using outward probability at least 2/3. Even a monotone radial path does not identify which old edge is selected at a revisit; radial height records no edge multiplicity. Singleton count can decrease when an old singleton is crossed again. Thus it is not the pure-birth process of source Lemma 8. Second route: split a degree-d neighbor choice into a cubic choice plus extra fresh branches. On return visits the extra branches are not fresh, and preservation of the original walk's conditional uniform law conflicts with the desired edge-history ordering. A coupling must handle those correlated revisits, not just a first-exit binomial comparison. Third route: transfer the source's time-reversed bus coupling. Bus assignments use one fixed alphabet with exchangeability; walk choices use vertex-dependent alphabets and are constrained by the same traversal history. The missing bridge is a monotone conditional law on singleton-edge histories, not the already proved occupancy lemma. No such bridge established. No claim that the bus theorem resolves Appendix A.1.
