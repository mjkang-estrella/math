First attempted mechanism: a bipartite imbalance obstruction, then isolates to repair ordinary equitable colourability while using private lists on isolates to keep the list obstruction. In K(3,N), N>=4, any acyclic colour class meeting both parts in at least two vertices contains a 4-cycle. Conversely a complete bipartite induced subgraph with one side of size<=1 is a star or independent set, so is acyclic. For two common colours, among the three left vertices one colour occurs at least twice. That colour covers at most one right vertex. If all three left vertices have that colour, its class has size at least3 and the other colour covers at least N-1 right vertices; if the split is2+1, the latter colour covers at least N-1 right vertices and one left vertex, so has size at least N. In fact choosing all three left vertices colour a and all right vertices colour b gives maximum class N, and choosing2+1 on left cannot reduce below N.
