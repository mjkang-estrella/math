## 342490: exact balanced reconstruction, two routes

Primary: Day–Fleischmann–Manea–Nowotka, arXiv1904.09125v2, definitions in section2 and section5, Proposition23, Conjecture24 and full Proposition25 proof read. Their weakly-0-balanced means exactly equal numbers of a and b; the corpus calls this strictly balanced. Multiplicities are ignored. Put h=floor(k/2)+1; the target is equality of sets of subsequences with h a's and h b's for two words with k of each letter. All k>=1 are required. Proposition25 only handles at most two b-blocks (or symmetrically a-blocks). Its case distinctions reconstruct five run exponents; that is an existing public subcase, not a full solution.

Route A: gap vectors. Write w=a^{x0} b a^{x1} b ... b a^{xk}, xi>=0 and sum xi=k. A balanced query u=a^{r0} b a^{r1} ... b a^{rh}, sum rj=h, is present precisely if some selected b-indices 1<=i1<...<ih<=k satisfy
 r0<=sum_{j=0}^{i1-1}xj,
 r_l<=sum_{j=i_l}^{i_{l+1}-1}xj (1<=l<h),
 rh<=sum_{j=ih}^{k}xj.
Necessity follows by locating the selected b's. Sufficiency follows by selecting the required a's separately in the disjoint intervening intervals. Thus the exact all-word task is injectivity of the union of these downward composition regions, not merely recovery of each region if its indexing were known. Overlapping regions erase which b's were selected. Knowing only coordinatewise maxima does not identify their joint feasible tuples. No injectivity theorem for arbitrary nonnegative x with sum k has been proved here. This is the exact unpaid lemma.

Every balanced subsequence with q of each letter, q<=h, extends to one with h of each: retain an embedding and add h-q unused occurrences of each symbol, then read their positions in order. Therefore the supplied spectrum determines all shorter balanced spectra. This useful reduction does not produce highly unbalanced queries a^i b a^{k-i} used in Proposition23: those have k a's and one b, exceeding h a's when k>2. Mere appeal to Dress–Erdos is not a complete bridge.

Route B: greedy prefix induction. If w starts ab, then ab u is a subsequence exactly when u is a subsequence of w after its first two letters: the greedy embedding of ab ends earliest. Consequently equal balanced h-spectra for two words both starting ab give equal balanced (h-1)-spectra of their balanced residual words. The same holds for ba. If k=2m, the residual parameter is 2m-1, whose required h is m; we have exactly h-1=m. If k=2m+1, the residual parameter is 2m, requiring m+1 of each, while we have only m. Thus the odd induction step fails by one of each letter. Worse, if the first b follows a^s with s>1, greedy ab consumes a^s b and leaves an unbalanced residual word, outside the induction hypothesis. An arbitrary first balanced prefix can be long and loses still more query length. Neither parity nor arbitrary initial runs have been repaired. These are proof gaps, not a counterexample.
