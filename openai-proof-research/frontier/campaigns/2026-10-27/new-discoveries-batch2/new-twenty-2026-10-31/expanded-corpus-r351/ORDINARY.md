## 368520: reverse oddtown, exact small dimensions and two failed general routes

Work over a finite field of odd cardinality q. The literal endpoint is for EVERY even positive n and EVERY family of distinct even-dimensional subspaces with odd-dimensional pairwise intersections: cardinality at most [n-1]_q. The family of all (n-2)-spaces inside a fixed hyperplane attains this for n>=4. Their pairwise intersection dimension is n-3 by the dimension formula; their number is [n-1]_q by duality with one-dimensional subspaces. For n=2 the optimal family is a singleton.

First route: projective incidence over F_2. Let v_A be the characteristic vector of the one-dimensional subspaces contained in A, on N=[n]_q coordinates. Exactly [dim A]_q entries are one. Since q is odd, [d]_q has parity d. Thus v_A dot v_A=0 and v_A dot v_B=1 for distinct family members. For m family members the Gram matrix is J_m-I_m. Solving (J-I)x=0 gives x=(sum x_i)1; hence its rank is m when m is even and m-1 when m is odd. Every v_A is orthogonal to 1, so their row span has dimension <=N-1. Combining these facts gives at most N-1 when n is even (if m odd and m<=N, then N even precludes m=N). This reconstructs Dey's Theorem 1.6, not Conjecture 1.8. The desired [n-1]_q is smaller by q^(n-1)-1. No justified family-dependent projection of the incidence space to [n-1]_q coordinates was found. Rank over F_2 alone supplies no such projection; it must preserve the particular off-diagonal Gram conditions, not just even row weights.

Second route: hyperplane restriction. Intersect every A with a fixed hyperplane H. Its dimension drops by either zero or one, so even dimensions need not stay even. Pairwise intersections can drop from odd to even. Moreover distinct A can have the same trace. Therefore induction on dimension cannot use this trace without both a collision bound and a parity-preserving transformation. Neither follows from the dimension formula. Counting hyperplanes and averaging trace sizes does not fix the parity defect.

Continue the geometric route rather than stop at these shortcuts: the full conjectured inequality holds for n=2 and n=4 by the following ordinary argument. For n=2, the only even-dimensional subspaces are 0 and V, and both cannot occur together, so m<=1=[1]_q. For n=4, if 0 or V belongs to the family, it cannot coexist with another even-dimensional member: intersection with 0 is zero and intersection with V has even dimension. Otherwise all members have dimension two. If m<=1 there is nothing to prove. Choose distinct U,W and put L=U intersect W, S=U+W. Then dim L=1 and dim S=3. If every member contains L, passing to the quotient V/L injects the family into the one-dimensional subspaces of a 3-dimensional space; m<=[3]_q. Otherwise choose T not containing L. Its intersections with U and W are distinct lines, so T is their sum and lies in S. Also U intersect W intersect T=0. Any further plane A not contained in S has dim(A intersect S)<=1. Its nonzero intersections with U,W,T must then be the same line A intersect S, forcing that line into their zero triple intersection, a contradiction. Hence every member is contained in S. There are exactly [3]_q planes in S, again m<=[3]_q. This is a full proof for n=2,4 only; it works for arbitrary prime powers in those dimensions. It is a classical intersecting-lines reduction and receives zero credit, not a candidate.

Actual remaining lemma: for n>=6, control mixed even dimensions and intersections 1,3,... without presuming a common point or hyperplane. The rank argument above and the planar-line classification do not do this. No general proof or counterexample is claimed. ## 368971: cubic Speyer–Tutte identity, beyond coefficient substitution

The endpoint is g_G''(0)=2n t_0,1(G)-4t_0,2(G) for every connected cubic graph. Panzer allows finite undirected multigraphs and self-loops, so a simple-graph surrogate would be insufficient. The exact conjecture is 1.22. Source Sections 1.2, 1.3, 2 definitions, 3.1–3.3 passages, worked examples 3.7 and 3.13, and companion Ferroni Theorem 4.3 and its proof were read. Relevant figures are available as preserved alt text and captions, not visually audited drawings.

Route A: exploit the known g'_M(0)=beta(M)=t_0,1(M) and the known formula for g'_M(-1). This fails to determine g''_M(0): derivatives at distinct points are different linear combinations of all coefficients. In the expansion g=t sum_i N_i(1+t)^i, g''(0)/2=sum_i i N_i, not N_2 and not g''(-1)/2. The source's 3-sum and twist conjectures for N_2 therefore do not automatically prove this endpoint. In particular substituting the already-proved first derivative identity would leave precisely the unknown weighted sum.

Route B: deletion–contraction on a cubic edge. The Tutte side satisfies deletion–contraction, but deletion creates degree-two vertices and contraction creates degree-four vertices. Applying the cubic inductive hypothesis to those minors is invalid. Suppressing degree-two vertices can help only on the deletion side and may introduce parallel edges; it does not repair the contracted degree-four vertex. The source does not supply a Tutte-like deletion–contraction identity for g at coefficient two. No universal such recurrence is assumed. A bridge is harmless: it is a coloop, g is zero, and the Tutte polynomial has an x factor, so both relevant x^0 coefficients vanish. This handles a boundary subfamily only.

Route C: exact cyclic-flat/Schubert expansion. Source (3.1) gives g_M=sum_C lambda_C g_SM(C), and Proposition 3.6 identifies lambda_C as a product of negative interval Möbius functions. The companion's Theorem 4.3 gives g_S=t sum_B (1+t)^alpha(B) for loopless coloopless Schubert S, with external activity one and internal activity zero. The proof groups Delannoy paths according to north-east corners. Differentiating this FINITE polynomial identity is legitimate and yields the exact reduction

sum_C lambda_C sum_{B: e(B)=1, i(B)=0 in SM(C)} alpha(B)
= n t_0,1(G)-2 t_0,2(G).

The left is exactly g_G''(0)/2 by the two published identities. What is unpaid is this weighted chain cancellation for all cubic cycle matroids. Cubicity constrains local degree, whereas cyclic flats may have many vertex boundaries; no proved reduction collapses their Möbius-weighted chain contributions to the two Tutte coefficients. Schubert basis corner counting cannot simply be transferred to general graphic matroids: Ferroni explicitly warns that his basis formula fails for non-Schubert matroids. Worked K4 source example gives g=2t+2t^2+t^3, but examples neither prove the chain cancellation nor establish a new result. No computation was run.

## 370387: complete literal C5 witness, publicly/source excluded

Let V=Z/5Z, with adjacency iff the difference is 1 or -1. This is a finite simple connected graph; every vertex has degree two. Every nonempty subgraph has all degrees at most two, hence it is 2-degenerate. Give every vertex the same list {1,2,3,4}. Its list size equals degree+2. In a proper conflict-free coloring, the two neighbors of every vertex must have distinct colors: if their colors agree, that color occurs twice and no color occurs exactly once; if distinct, each occurs once. Therefore vertices at distance two have distinct colors, and properness gives distinct colors at distance one. Every two different vertices of C5 have distance one or two. Thus the coloring would inject five vertices into four colors, impossible. This is a complete in-scope refutation of the exact literal record, requiring no finite search.

Check beyond the shortcut: the paper's Proposition 2 proves the weaker degree+d+1 bound by choosing a degeneracy order and protecting the earliest neighbor of each vertex. There are up to d properness exclusions and degree(v) protected witness exclusions. Removing its extra color is not justified just by overlapping these sets; the C5 witness shows that the bound is in fact tight on that graph. A recoloring argument for a non-C5 graph would need a new witness-maintenance invariant, and the paper's tree argument does not apply to arbitrary d-degenerate graphs.

The v2 introduction explicitly says C5 is 2-degenerate and not degree+2 choosable BEFORE stating its unqualified Conjecture 4; the literal follows that internally inconsistent formulation. The companion arXiv:2508.20521 also explicitly presents C5 as the known obstruction and restricts the corresponding connected-graph conjecture.
