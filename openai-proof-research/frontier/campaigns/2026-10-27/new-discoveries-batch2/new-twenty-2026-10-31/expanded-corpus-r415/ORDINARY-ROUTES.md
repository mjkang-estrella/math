## 336246: full-scope matrix identity; known-source exclusion
Source: Merca–Schmidt arXiv1706.00393v2,4 August2017. Actual coefficient definition at p.4, Conjecture3.1 p.9, Theorem3.2 pp.10–11 and its full proof read. For every n>=1 and1<=k<=n, the theorem states s_inverse(n,k)=sum_{d|n}p(d-k)mu(n/d), with p(negative)=0. Setting k=1 pays exactly every n of the target, using the same inverse matrix. This is a complete applicable pre-existing bridge, not a similarity of titles. Contrary assessment: MathDB describes this as unresolved experimental material, but its own cited v2 immediately proves the stronger all-column formula. No novel credit.

Independent ordinary reconstruction: let P(q)=product_{j>=1}(1-q^j), with inverse series sum p(m)q^m. The coefficient definition of S is s(n,j)=[q^n]P(q)q^j/(1-q^j). For fixed k, let a_j=(S inverse)_{j,k}, zero for j<k. Lower triangular inversion with unit diagonal says sum_j s(n,j)a_j=delta(n,k). Therefore P(q)sum_j a_j q^j/(1-q^j)=q^k coefficientwise. Every coefficient uses finitely many summands, so no analytic convergence assumption is needed. Multiplication by P(q) inverse gives sum_{d|n}a_d=p(n-k). Möbius inversion yields the stated expression. Conversely the Möbius expression satisfies the divisor sum and therefore the triangular equations, so uniqueness identifies the inverse column. k=1 is exactly the target. This extends beyond an observed sequence or one divisor test and resolves the entire identity, but the same argument is in Theorem3.2, so it is not a discovery.

The extracted text states unit diagonal and the coefficient definition fixes it unambiguously. Do not obtain credit by correcting these typos. Source target and theorem3.2 use the same symbols and all n,k. No reliance on experimental plots or their numerical tables. Companion-source audit is unnecessary for the zero-credit exclusion once the source's own stronger theorem is read; no positive novelty inference follows.

## 336801: ambient-edge counting, two sustained routes, unpaid global lemma
Read Faron–Postle arXiv1708.02264v1, pp.1–5, including Def1.5, Conj1.8, Thm1.7 and its full proof. The conjecture has H bipartite but G arbitrary. The proved theorem requires G bipartite. This distinction cannot be discarded by deleting ambient edges: two disjoint edges of H can be adjacent in L(G)^2 solely through a connector joining vertices on the same side of H. Removing that connector destroys the hypothesis. External vertices cannot be a length2 line-graph connector between disjoint H edges, so restriction to G[V(H)] is valid, but deletion of within-side edges is not.

Route A: let v maximize d_H(v)=Delta_H, A=N_H(v), C=N_G(v) minus A, and classify H edges by ambient distance from v. In the ambient bipartite case the paper defines S as distance2 endpoints of H edges whose other endpoint is at distance3. Strong-clique adjacency forces every u in S adjacent to every x in A. Counts are
 |E_C|<=(d_G(v)-|A|)Delta_H,
 |E_S|<=|S|Delta_H,
 |E_A|<=|A|(sigma-d_G(v)-|S|).
Since |A|=Delta_H they sum to Delta_H(sigma-Delta_H)<=sigma²/4. In arbitrary G a residual H edge can have both endpoints at distance2 from v; there is no forced complete S-to-A join. This is the exact failure, not an asserted counterexample. To repair it requires charging all residual distance2–distance2 edges to ambient degree consumed by connectors, without counting a connector more than once across the A-neighborhood constraints. That charging lemma is unpaid.

Route B: retain a fixed bipartition U,V of H and charge any same-side G connector to its endpoints' extra degrees e_z=d_G(z)-d_H(z). For disjoint H edges ab,cd (a,c in U), a strong-clique witness may be cross ad or cb, or same-side ac or bd. A connector zw can witness up to d_H(z)d_H(w) ordered endpoint choices; the pairwise strong-clique condition therefore gives a covering constraint with highly unequal multiplicities. The pointwise Ore restriction d_G(a)+d_G(b)<=sigma on every H edge controls these extra degrees only locally. Summing it gives sum_z d_H(z)d_G(z)<=m sigma, m=|E(H)|. This is valid by counting each endpoint over its incident H edges. It does not yield m<=sigma²/4: the number of active vertices and the connector multiplicities remain unbounded in this expression. An unweighted pair count or a matching-only witness misses shared endpoints and cannot replace the required weighted inequality.

Sanity families by hand, no enumeration: H=K_{a,b} inside G=H has m=ab, sigma=a+b and the target follows from(a-b)²>=0, with equality when a=b. H a matching of m edges, G adds a clique on its U endpoints; then degrees are m on U and1 on V, all H edges are strong-adjacent, sigma=m+1, and4m<=(m+1)² from(m-1)²>=0. The second family explicitly shows why ambient within-side edges matter while not refuting the bound. Neither family proves arbitrary H. Next decisive ordinary lemma is an extremal compression/weighted charge bound handling mixed cross and same-side connectors with this edgewise Ore budget. No such lemma proved here. Thm1.9 only reduces another conjecture to Conj1.8 and cannot be assumed to prove its hypothesis. Park rather than claim an incomplete proof.
