## 322399: a complete but excluded source-recipe refutation

The source is Johnson–Markström arXiv:1110.4283v1, Section 2, Lemma 2, Theorem 3 and Conjecture 5 (PDF pages 5–6). Its subcube families may contain repeats, but the following example uses ten distinct cubes, so that convention does not affect it.

Take d=4, r=3, n=10. The conjectured range includes this triple because 10=2*2^(4/2)+(3-2). Begin with the source's complete tripartite coordinate-partition construction with parts of sizes 4,4,1: all four cubes ab**, all four cubes **cd, and ****. Split 00** into 000* and 001*, exactly the operation in its Lemma 2. Thus the ten cubes are

    000*, 001*, 01**, 10**, 11**, **00, **01, **10, **11, ****.

The first five cubes are pairwise disjoint, the next four are pairwise disjoint, and the last cube meets all other nine. Each of 01**,10**,11** meets four of the second group, contributing twelve edges. Each of 000*,001* meets two of that group, contributing four more. There are exactly 16+9=25 edges. The intersection graph is tripartite and hence K4-free.

Every graph H allowed by Conjecture 5 is complete tripartite with class capacities 2^d1,2^d2,2^d3 where nonnegative integer di sum to four. Up to reordering the possibilities are (4,0,0),(3,1,0),(2,2,0),(2,1,1). Their capacities are (16,1,1),(8,2,1),(4,4,1),(4,2,2). The last two cannot contain ten vertices. In (16,1,1), a ten-vertex subgraph has at most 8*1+8*1+1=17 edges. In (8,2,1), the maximum occurs at (7,2,1), giving 14+7+2=23 edges; removing a vertex from the largest part minimizes lost edges in the eleven-vertex host. Allowing edge deletion cannot increase this maximum. Consequently 25 exceeds every proposed host's bound. If a partition requires nonempty parts, fewer hosts are allowed and the conclusion persists.

This closes the literal finite refutation, but it is precisely the printed coordinate construction plus a single printed split. The task explicitly excludes short augmentations and published recipe bridges. Zero discovery credit; no CANDIDATE.md and no execution referral. A broader family obtained by further splits would have the same exclusion. The important semantic bridge is that a single graph with more edges than every allowed ten-vertex host refutes the assertion about the maximum; the exact unrestricted maximum need not be known.

## 316508: early versus revised Stirling conjectures

The literal statement matches arXiv:0707.3104v1 Conjecture 1.5. Its m0 is ceil(log2(k)), and its predicted number of nonconstant residue classes is 2^(m0-2) at every m>=m0. The later author-hosted manuscript, `stirling-journal`, instead states Conjecture 1.6 with existential stabilization level m0(k) and count mu(k). Example 1.9 and its printed discussion give k=13, eight classes at level 3, only three constant, and eventual count four at level 5; the old formula requires FOUR nonconstant classes at every m>=4 (m0=4). The printed level-3 exception alone therefore does NOT refute the literal assertion. This is a source reformulation with exceptional behavior, not a certified refutation. The exact old threshold/splitting assertion needs further comparison; no new significant result is claimed. The later paper uses an existential stabilization threshold.

Route A was direct residue splitting using k! S(n,k)=sum_j (-1)^(k-j) binom(k,j) j^n. Reduction modulo powers of two can certify constant branches, but checking finitely many n cannot certify that an entire residue class has constant valuation. Route B was separating the even-j exponentially vanishing terms and interpolating odd-j terms 2-adically on parity classes. A simple-root/Hensel argument would give one surviving child once a root is simple; there is no proof here that every relevant root is simple or that the old count is correct. Route C was the 2013 Bennett–Mosteig finite-congruence criterion (Theorem 5 and Section 6); it certifies the revised assertion for k<=20, including more complicated k=7 and k=13 behavior. These are existing results, not an opportunity to recount a new finite parameter instance. The 2009 paper retrieved in `stirling-2009` is a further prior-art lead, not an audited proof of the literal conjecture. No finite computation was run.

## 320403: paired cycles, sustained but unresolved

The literal statement is Conjecture 3 in v1–v2, not simply the later connected Whitehead-graph wording. The latest v4 Section 3 defines loops/multiedges, cycles as connected 2-regular graphs, local edge connectivity, pairwise well-connectedness, connecting maps and balanced cycle lists. Section 4 Theorem 12 proves the regular case using perfect matching polytopes; it also covers subdivisions of regular graphs. Theorem 24 covers the four-vertex Whitehead case and its subdivisions. Example 27 explicitly shows that 2-connectedness cannot replace the pairwise connectivity hypothesis. These source families must be excluded before any finite obstruction is proposed.

Route A: express the question as a rational cone problem. For a fixed loop-free multigraph and involution/maps, index rows by (v,{e,f}); one column for each actual simple cycle C. Its entry is 1 if C uses e,f at v minus 1 if C uses sigma_v(e),sigma_v(f) at mu(v). Then a balanced list is exactly a nonzero nonnegative integral kernel vector. Rational feasibility plus scaling gives an integral list. Repeated rows from inverse maps are harmless. For the extra conclusion let b_C=1 for cycles of length at least three and zero for bigons; seek Ax=0, x>=0, b*x=1. This is exactly equivalent to a balanced integral list containing a long cycle, by rationality and rescaling. All cycles must be included for a negative certificate; a chosen cycle sample is insufficient.

A sufficient exact obstruction would be rational row weights y with A^T y>=b. Every hypothetical x would give 0=y^T Ax >= b*x=1. For failure of any nonempty balanced list use b_C=1 for all cycles. This is a certificate format, not a found certificate or a source-qualified execution task. A fixed graph must also carry source-correct inverse connecting bijections and deg(v) edge-disjoint v–mu(v) paths (or an exact min-cut proof). A witnessed failure of the strengthened clause suffices to refute the conjunction. No such graph or dual vector is supplied.

Route B: regularize unequal degrees by duplicating edges and invoke Theorem 12. This is not justified: independent multiplicities must commute with every connecting bijection and make every vertex degree equal; paired vertices have equal degrees but different pairs need not. More seriously, projecting a simple cycle of the enlarged graph can become a repeated-vertex closed walk, which is not a source cycle. A walk decomposition can change the prescribed pair counts. Thus a proof needs both compatible regularization and a pair-preserving projection, neither established.

Route C: put a stationary nonbacktracking transition law on incident edge pairs, invariant under the connecting maps. A circulation decomposes into closed walks in the directed-edge state graph. This does not imply decomposition into simple graph cycles with the same transitions: splitting at a repeated vertex replaces turns. The missing lemma is precisely a source-cycle decomposition preserving every paired turn count. Local balance and Menger connectivity alone have not supplied it.

This is the strongest unresolved route retained, with precise certificate semantics. Source examples, all later journal/citing bodies, and current complete registries have not been cleared, so there is no Pi implementation request. Root would first need that qualification and an explicit proposed graph/certificate scope.

Hales–Jewett only gives unrestricted active size; a diagonal-only coloring at n=HJ gives a finite lower bound, not infinity. No full route.
* 378961: do not conflate S(G)=R(G), already the source's Theorem 1.1, with a word sequence. A vanishing sequence chosen separately for one G is also weaker than the literal uniform statement. Wilson's primary proof remains unread, so this is a strong known-resolution lead, not an independently audited classification or new claim.
* 378975: source v1 omitted independence, v2 announces McDiarmid's counterexample, v3–v4 impose independence and match the literal record. * 319844: radical inversion for decomposable words is Lemma 1.3. * 318509: source Theorem 16 derives the assertion from unboundedness of the directed Poljak–Rödl function. A fixed counterexample to strong Hedetniemi equality does not establish boundedness of that function or negate this path assertion. No uniform threshold proof.
