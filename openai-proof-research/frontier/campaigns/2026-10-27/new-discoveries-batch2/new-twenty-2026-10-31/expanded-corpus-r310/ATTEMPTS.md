## 358193: a full refutation, excluded by a complete earlier bridge

The literal endpoint is universal over n by n metric matrices and bounds the number of positive eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, by floor((n+1)/3). The source Conjecture 4.1 has precisely this endpoint. The footnote has a misindexed triangle inequality; the object below satisfies the genuine triangle inequality, so the refutation does not exploit that typo.

Let V be the fifteen two-element subsets of {1,...,6}. Define M(S,S)=0; define M(S,T)=1 when S and T are disjoint; otherwise define M(S,T)=2 for distinct intersecting S,T. This is a genuine metric: positivity and symmetry are immediate. If the intermediate point equals an endpoint, the triangle inequality is equality; otherwise the two right-side distances are each at least 1, while the left side is at most 2. It is also the unweighted distance matrix of KG(6,2). Disjoint sets are adjacent. Distinct intersecting sets have union of size three, so a two-element subset of the three-element complement is a common neighbor and gives distance two.

Here is an independent full spectrum proof, with no reliance on a numerical eigenvalue routine. Let B be the six by fifteen incidence matrix, B(i,S)=1 iff i belongs to S. Then B^T B has diagonal 2 and off-diagonal entry |S intersect T|. Consequently M=J-3I+B^T B. Every i belongs to five pairs and every distinct i,j belongs to exactly one common pair, so BB^T=4I+J on R^6. This matrix is positive definite, with eigenvalue 10 on the all-ones line and 4 on its five-dimensional orthogonal complement. In particular B has rank six.

The all-ones vector on V is an eigenvector of M of eigenvalue 22: each S has six disjoint pairs and eight other intersecting pairs. For x in R^6 with coordinate sum zero, B^T x has coordinate sum five times that sum, hence zero. Also B^TB(B^Tx)=B^T(4I+J)x=4B^Tx. Thus M(B^Tx)=B^Tx. B^T is injective, so this supplies a five-dimensional eigenspace of eigenvalue 1. Finally ker(B) has dimension nine. Since B^T 1=2*1, every vector in ker(B) is orthogonal to 1; M is -3I there. These spaces are mutually orthogonal and dimensions 1+5+9=15, so the spectrum is exactly 22^1,1^5,(-3)^9. Six positive eigenvalues contradict floor(16/3)=5. This resolves the full universal claim by a finite in-scope object, not a helper or parameter-only affirmative result.

I then investigated subsumption rather than treating this as a novel discovery. The graph is strongly regular with parameters (15,6,1,3): a vertex has C(4,2)=6 neighbors; two disjoint pairs have one common neighboring pair, and two intersecting pairs have three. Aalipour et al., arXiv:1509.01196v2 Section 2, explicitly gives the full distance spectrum for every strongly regular graph (n,k,lambda,mu), with the proof mechanism in Remark 2.1, D=2(J-I)-A. Substituting (15,6,1,3) gives sqrt((1-3)^2+4(6-3))=4, adjacency eigenvalues 6,1,-3 of multiplicities 1,9,5; the distance eigenvalues are 22,-3,1. This is a complete earlier classical/source bridge to exactly the same counterexample. The primary formula body, not just a matching title or abstract, was read. Under the user's explicit exclusion standard this receives ZERO, even though it refutes the literal 2023 conjecture. The 2016 journal counterpart was identified, but its CoCalc route returned 403 and was not retried; no claim of reading that journal body is made. The independently available arXiv primary text suffices to demonstrate the bridge.

No CANDIDATE.md and no Pi execution request are appropriate for this excluded result. A finite semantic certificate would be possible, but its feasibility does not restore significance or novelty.

## 358393: full-domain rational five-cycles, not an integer exception table

Read the actual T definition: positive t congruent to 2 modulo 4 whose squarefree part has an odd prime factor congruent to 2 modulo 3. Read both listed arXiv versions and the 2024 Integers printed journal text for the relevant Section 4 obstruction and Section 5 question. The graph is on Q^3, not Z^3. Norm-squared t five-vectors summing to zero would give a closed five-walk. With nonzero edges and triangle exclusion, a closed odd five-walk necessarily contains a five-cycle: deleting a repeated-vertex closed subwalk would otherwise leave a triangle or an impossible loop. Thus the exact construction obligation is explicit; proving a lower bound of five alone is insufficient.

Route 1: transfer an integer exceptional parameter by scaling. A five-cycle for n^2 t in Z^3 scales to a rational five-cycle for t. Conversely clearing denominators of a rational cycle gives such an integer cycle. However one must prove existence of some n for EVERY t, not just the finite source list T1 or the statement that multiplying those listed exceptional t by nine happens to work. Square factors and powers of four must be normalized correctly in invoking the source T partition. The conjectured integer coverage is itself not proved, so it cannot be used as a bridge.

Route 2: build equal-norm vectors in symmetric pairs. One attempted ansatz is (a,b,c), (a,-b,-c), (-a/2,d,e), (-a/2,-d,e), (-a,0,-2e). Their sum is zero. Requiring all five squared norms equal t forces a^2+b^2+c^2=t, a^2+4e^2=t, and a^2/4+d^2+e^2=t. The last two imply d^2=3t/4. For t in T, 3t is not a rational square, since the odd prime congruent to 2 mod 3 occurs to odd exponent. Thus this seemingly flexible symmetric construction actually misses the entire intended domain. It is not a candidate.

Route 3: allow three freely varying equal-norm rational vectors with sum s, and close with u=-s/2+w and v=-s/2-w. The exact remaining equations are w dot s=0 and |w|^2=t-|s|^2/4, plus noncollisions of the partial sums. Rational sphere parametrization alone does not solve the resulting binary quadratic form in s-perp. There is no proved uniform local solubility statement here, much less a rational point construction for every t in T. The source Theorem 4.4 already proves that finitely many of its linear binary-form parameterizations cannot cover all T even via rational representations. A finite menu of those constructions is therefore structurally inadequate. I did not replace this missing arithmetic theorem by a generic solver proposal. Parked with exact missing lemma: existence, for every t in T, of five rational norm-squared-t vectors summing to zero, or equivalently a full rational pentagon construction with all semantic conditions. No full proof survives.

## 357881: arbitrary-position basis claim

Conjecture 1.11 is specific to k=2. Source Theorem 1.13 gives a printed k=3 cross-ratio obstruction and is out of scope. The 2025 journal article with Santos is a companion to a different earlier cofactor/general rigidity paper, not a journal resolution of this solo bipartite conjecture; its Remark 1.11 expressly points to the bipartite follow-up. Both available solo-paper arXiv versions were fetched and their relevant conjecture/definition text inspected. No all-version full-paper read is claimed.

The source reduced bipartization has n-3 vertices in each part and allowed positions S_2(n)={(a,b):a,b in [n-3], a+b<=n}; a graph comes from a maximal three-crossing-free diagonal set under the source relabeling. The row for (i,j) in polynomial two-rigidity has the opposite point's (1,t) coordinates in each endpoint block, with the source sign convention. Theorem 3.3 identifies polynomial/hyperconnectivity and moment-curve bar-and-joint matroids by invertible column changes and nonzero row rescaling. Thus a full endpoint requires linear independence of ALL rows for EVERY valid triangulation and EVERY allowed distinct parameter assignment, not a generic minor or a special equally spaced placement.

Route 1: stress equations. For edge weights c_ij, dependence requires sum_j c_ij=0 and sum_j c_ij*y_j=0 at each left vertex, and sum_i c_ij=0 and sum_i c_ij*x_i=0 at each right vertex (up to the fixed sign convention). At a degree-two vertex, distinct neighboring parameters force both edge stresses to vanish. Repeated elimination proves independence for graphs admitting such an elimination. But general reduced 2-triangulations need not admit an elimination certified by these conditions. Avoiding a K3,3 subgraph does not prove independence: larger circuits and cross-ratio effects exist in the source. Corollary 3.15 controls the K3,3 circuit signs; Theorem 3.16 explicitly shows how equal cross-ratios can cause a coefficient to vanish in a larger configuration. A bare count or local Vandermonde argument therefore cannot close the claim.

Route 2: ear induction/vertex splitting. The companion's cofactor two-triangulation theorem uses an ear/vertex-split induction, which suggests a real mechanism rather than a parameter sweep. But after bipartization the operation and rank target differ. A generic determinant nonzero polynomial can still vanish on a distinct ordered real parameter configuration. Rank semicontinuity supplies openness of good placements, not equality with the whole parameter chamber. A successful proof needs an exact bipartite ear reduction with a nonzero factor, or a subtraction-free expansion whose factors have fixed positive signs throughout each admissible chamber. Neither lemma was established. Source Theorem 4.7 is explicitly generic via the Jacobian of the matrix-product variety and does not remove the gap.

Theorem 5.1 and its complete inspected proof settle the smallest n=2k+2 case by the K_(k+1,k+1) circuit and ICoP signs. Those known smallest cases are not the requested arbitrary-n result. Unordered-label readings or collisions between the two classes need source-fidelity adjudication; they are not used to manufacture a scope-correction counterexample. Parked: no full all-position determinant/sign theorem and no exact valid finite singular triangulation was produced.
