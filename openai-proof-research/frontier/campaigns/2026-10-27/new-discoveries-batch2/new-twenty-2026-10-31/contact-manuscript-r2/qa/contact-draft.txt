 Abstract
 We exhibit a connected simple graph on five vertices whose two-colour Cauchy transform has contact order 6 at the
 boundary point corresponding to (infinity,0), although the root is adjacent to a w-coloured vertex. This contradicts
 Conjecture 6.1 of Tully-Doyle, Adlin, Thai and Tiscareno [1], which predicts twice the nearest-w distance. The two local
 branches have exact orders 6 and 2. We prove the result directly by the zero-set definition of contact, as well as by analytic
 level-curve expansions. The accompanying r6 Lean dossier proves the repaired local endpoint ContactPilot.refutationLocal,
 the literal negation of SourceConjectureLocal, including the matrix resolvent, polynomial reduction, stability and Cayley
 transfer. This draft makes no priority claim.

 1. Source conventions and the statement
 Let G be a finite simple undirected graph with adjacency matrix A. Colour each vertex z or w, let Y be the diagonal
 indicator of the z vertices, and choose a root i. The source representation is
 M(z,w) = A - zY - w(I-Y),                  f(z,w) = (M(z,w)^(-1))_(i,i).

 It is the diagonal entry of the inverse, not its reciprocal. On the bi-upper half-plane, M is invertible: if Mv=0 with v
 nonzero, the imaginary part of v*Mv is strictly negative. With D=diag(Im z or Im w), the inverse identity is
 Im(M^(-1)) = (M^(-1))* D M^(-1).

 Thus Im f is positive. Real coefficients give real values on the real boundary wherever the quotient is defined. The Cayley
 map beta(v)=(1+iv)/(1-iv), with inverse i(1-v)/(1+v), gives a rational inner function
 phi(Z,W) = beta(f(beta^(-1)(Z), beta^(-1)(W))).

 The boundary point (infinity,0) becomes (-1,1). All contact orders below are local there. The source's Section 4, Theorem
 4.6 proves contact order 2d when there is exactly one w vertex and its minimum path distance from the root is d. Conjecture
 6.1 extends this assertion to multiple w vertices. The adjacent paragraph additionally predicts equal contact orders on all
 branches. Our example has two w vertices; it violates both expectations without contradicting Theorem 4.6.

 For a reduced expression phi=q/p with p stable and q a unimodular multiple of its reflection, use the face |Z|=1, |W|<1. At a
 torus point (tau1,tau2) with q(tau1,tau2)=0, local contact K means the following exact quantifiers. First fix c>0, C>0 and
 rho0>0. There exists delta0>0 such that for every unit Z with 0<h=|Z-tau1|<delta0 and EVERY disk zero v of q(Z,v) with
 |v-tau2|<rho0, c h^K <= 1-|v|. Also, for EVERY 0<rho<=rho0 there exists delta>0 such that for every unit Z with 0<h<delta
 there EXISTS a disk zero v with |v-tau2|<rho and 1-|v|<=C h^K. The constants c,C,rho0 are fixed before both clauses; only
 the upper parameter interval may shrink with rho. In particular C cannot depend on rho, and an empty local zero set cannot
 supply the upper witness.

 This compares the local zero-height infimum with h^K in arbitrarily small neighborhoods. Here (tau1,tau2)=(-1,1). Section
 3.1 and Remark 3.2 of [2] retain the maximum branch convention in the ELA 2026 journal version: generic matched level
 curves yield the maximum of their vanishing orders. It is neither the minimum nor their sum.

 2. The graph and the reduced resolvent
 Use vertices 0,1,2,3,4, root 0, and the following data:
 E = {01,02,03,13,24,34},               z vertices = {0,3,4},      w vertices = {1,2}.

 The root has w neighbours 1 and 2. A path of length zero cannot reach a w vertex since the root is z. Consequently its
 nearest-w distance is exactly 1. The graph is connected: 1,2,3 are adjacent to 0, and 4 is adjacent to 2 (also to 3). In the
 indicated order its matrix is
 M = [ -z       1    1     1    0 ]
     [ 1       -w    0     1    0 ]
     [ 1        0   -w     0    1 ]
     [ 1        1    0    -z    1 ]
     [ 0        0    1     1   -z ].

 Expanding the determinant and the (0,0) cofactor gives f=N/Q, with
 N = (wz-w-1)(wz+w-1),
 Q = -w^2 z^3 + 2w^2 z + 4wz^2 + 2wz - w - 3z.

 This is an identity of rational functions; the pointwise matrix-inverse identity is used where Q is nonzero. In the bi-upper
 half-plane that condition follows from the strict imaginary-part argument. The determinant and cofactor are independently
 recoverable from the displayed matrix, so this quotient is tied to the actual graph.

 We verify reduction over C[z,w], not merely over the rational numbers. Each factor w(z-1)-1 and w(z+1)-1 is primitive as a
 polynomial in w over C[z]. It is linear and irreducible over C(z), hence irreducible by Gauss's lemma. They are
 nonassociate: their coefficients of w differ while their constant coefficients agree. Neither divides Q, since substitution
 yields
 Q(z,1/(z-1)) = (2z-1)^2/(z-1)^2,
 Q(z,1/(z+1)) = -1/(z+1)^2.

 Both are nonzero rational functions. There are no z-only factors of N because its factors are primitive. Every common
 divisor of N and Q is therefore a unit. The formal proof obtains irreducibility using a polynomial equivalence and checks
 nondivisibility already at (z,w)=(0,-1) and (0,1). It concludes a common-divisor statement in the original bivariate complex
 polynomial ring.

 The two distinct w factors matter: this is genuinely a degree-two representation in w after reduction. Removing a cancelled
 factor cannot turn it into the one-w case. Likewise, the distance calculation refers to the original graph, rather than an
 auxiliary quotient graph or an effective coupling parameter.

 3. Analytic level curves and exact differences
 For a finite nonzero real level L set t=1/z and w=tu. The cleared equation f=L is
 F(t,u,L) = a_L(t)u^2 + b_L(t)u + c_L(t) = 0,
 a_L = L(1-2t^2)+t-t^3,
 b_L = -4L-2Lt+Lt^2-2t,       c_L = 3L+t.

 Indeed F=t(N-LQ) after substitution. At t=0 it equals L(u-1)(u-3), with simple roots 1 and 3. The discriminant has value
 4L^2, so a local analytic square root with initial value 2L exists. The quadratic formula gives two convergent analytic
 branches, uniquely labelled by their slopes. The leading coefficient is nonzero near zero, so these are all roots; in particular
 all w roots are O(t).

 The identity N-LQ=0 away from t=0 is equivalent to f=L on these branches. If Q vanished there then N would vanish as
 well. The displayed substitutions in Section 2 show that common zeros cannot accumulate at z=infinity. Thus no level
 branch here is a spurious common-zero component.

 The branches have expansions
 w_(L,1)(t) = t-t^2+t^3-t^4/2-t^5
              +(17/4-1/(2L))t^6+O(t^7),
 w_(L,3)(t) = 3t+(3-2/L)t^2+O(t^3).

 These are convergent analytic expansions. For an explicit coefficient check for the longer jet, put
 p_L(t) = 1-t+t^2-t^3/2-t^4+(17/4-1/(2L))t^5.

 Substitution into F gives O(t^6). The identity F(t,u)-F(t,p)=(u-p)[a_L(t)(u+p)+b_L(t)] has bracket -2L at (t,u,p)=(0,1,1).
 Division by this analytic nonvanishing bracket shows u-p=O(t^6), proving the first expansion. For the slope-three branch,
 put p=3+(3-2/L)t; its residual is O(t^2) and the bracket has value 2L. This proves the second expansion without treating
 formal jets as analytic existence.

 For distinct finite nonzero real L,H, subtraction gives
 w_(L,1)-w_(H,1) = (1/(2H)-1/(2L))t^6+O(t^7),
 w_(L,3)-w_(H,3) = (2/H-2/L)t^2+O(t^3).

 The coefficients are nonzero; the orders are exactly 6 and 2. Cross-matching slopes gives order 1 and is not the local branch
 matching. The calculation works for every nonzero real level, not only positive levels. Consequently it is compatible with
 the generic-level convention of [2, Remark 3.2] and [3, Theorem 3.1]. The maximum order is 6. We next prove this
 conclusion directly from the zero-set definition, independently of the generic-level theorem.

 4. A direct zero-set proof of contact six
 For domain assertions use x=-1/z, which preserves the upper half-plane; t=1/z alone reverses it. Clearing the denominator
 of beta(f(-1/x,w)) gives
 R(x,w) = x^3[Q(-1/x,w)-iN(-1/x,w)]
   = (1-ix-2x^2+ix^3)w^2
     +(4x-(2+2i)x^2-x^3)w+3x^2-ix^3.

 Write A(x)=1-ix-2x^2+ix^3, B(x)=4-(2+2i)x-x^2 and C(x)=3-ix. For w=xu the equation is x^2[A(x)u^2+B(x)u+C(x)]=0.
 At zero the desingularized quadratic has roots -1,-3. Its normalized discriminant (B^2-4AC)/4 has value 1. The analytic
 inverse function theorem for s -> s^2 at 1 supplies a local analytic square root. The quadratic formula yields exhaustive
 analytic branches u_1,u_3. No global choice of square-root branch is required.

 Here is an analytic remainder proof of their imaginary orders. Put
 P(x) = -1-x-x^2-x^3/2+x^4.

 Direct polynomial multiplication gives A P^2+B P+C=x^5 H(x), where H is polynomial with H(0)=-17/2+i. Subtract the
 branch equation; the divided difference A(u_1+P)+B has value 2. Therefore, on a neighbourhood,
 u_1=P+x^5 g,              g=-H/[A(u_1+P)+B],
 g analytic,               g(0)=17/4-i/2.

 Similarly A(-3)^2+B(-3)+C=x H_3(x), with H_3(0)=6-4i. The divided difference A(u_3-3)+B has value -2. Thus
 u_3=-3+xh with h analytic and h(0)=3-2i. For real x the two pole roots w_j=xu_j satisfy
 Im w_1(x) = x^6 Im g(x) = -x^6/2+O(x^7),
 Im w_3(x) = x^2 Im h(x) = -2x^2+O(x^3).

 The nonzero analytic factors give exact real analytic orders 6 and 2. After shrinking the interval, both roots have negative
 imaginary part. They tend uniformly to zero, and every root is one of them for nonzero sufficiently small x. Continuity
 gives |Im w_1| between |x|^6/4 and |x|^6, while |Im w_3| is at least |x|^2. Restricting |x|<1, every root has imaginary
 distance at least |x|^6/4, and the first root witnesses an upper bound |x|^6 in any prescribed small neighbourhood. The
 stronger formal attribution is half_plane_contact_six_localized in RootLocalization.lean: for every epsilon>0, one positive
 interval works for an all-root lower constant 1/4 and a witness of norm below epsilon with upper constant 1. The weaker
 HasHalfPlaneContact R 6 is HISTORICAL and is not a general faithful local definition.

 There is no common component of R and its coefficientwise conjugate: one would divide the cleared N and Q, while x
 cannot divide R because R(0,w)=w^2. Thus the local boundary zero is not an artefact of common-factor cancellation.

 5. Reduction on the bidisk and the Cayley comparison
 For clarity the global certificate uses polynomial clearing rather than a merely local quotient. In C[Z,W], set a=i(1-Z),
 b=1+Z, c=i(1-W), e=1+W and define
 N_c = b(ca-cb-be)(ca+cb-be),
 Q_c = -c^2 a^3+2c^2 ab^2+4ca^2 be
       +2cab^2 e-cb^3 e-3ab^2 e^2,
 q_b = Q_c+iN_c,       p_b = Q_c-iN_c.

 Clearing gives N_c=b^3 e^2 N(a/b,c/e) and the analogous identity for Q_c. Hence phi=q_b/p_b in the bidisk. The proof of
 coprimality factors N_c into b and its two bilinear factors. Each is irreducible; exact evaluations at (1,-i), (1,i) and (-1,0)
 exclude their divisibility into Q_c. Invertible scalar linear combinations then give reduction of q_b,p_b. Thus reduction is
 checked after clearing, including its exceptional divisors; it is not inferred solely from rational coordinate substitution.

 The support of p_b is bounded by (3,2). With reflection in that bidegree, direct polynomial coefficient computation gives
 q_b=reflect(p_b), with phase 1. The denominator is stable: beta^(-1) takes the bidisk into the upper half-plane, Q is nonzero
 there by invertibility of the graph matrix, and Q-iN cannot vanish because Im(N/Q)>0. Indeed Q-iN=0 would imply f=-i.
 The clearing factors are nonzero in the open bidisk. This gives a reduced rational-inner certificate for the actual graph
 function. Both q_b and p_b vanish at (-1,1).

 For real nonzero x near zero let Z=-beta(x)=beta(-1/x). Coefficientwise conjugation of R converts lower-half-plane pole
 roots u to upper-half-plane roots conjugate(u), and W=beta(conjugate(u)) gives precisely the nearby numerator zeros. The
 clearing and quotient identities establish both directions; possible exceptional denominators are ruled out by localization.
 Every such image eventually lies inside the disk and arbitrarily close to 1.

 The transfer uses explicit estimates, not an assertion that contact is conformally invariant. If Im y>0 and |y|<1/2, then
 1-|beta(y)|^2 = 4 Im y / |1-iy|^2,
 Im y/2 <= 1-|beta(y)| <= 16 Im y.

 For |Z|=1 near -1 the circle chart x=i(1+Z)/(1-Z) is real and
 |Z+1|/2 <= |x| <= |Z+1|.

 Combining the half-plane lower constant 1/4, height lower factor 1/2, and parameter lower factor 1/2 in power six yields
 1/512. The upper constant is 16. Localization first places all witnesses in any proposed numerator neighbourhood. Thus
 bidisk_local_contact_six proves HasLocalZeroSetContact (eval2 bidiskNumerator) (-1) 1 6: fix rho0=1/2, c=1/512 and
 C=16. One delta0>0 bounds ALL disk zeros within rho0. For EACH rho in (0,rho0], a possibly smaller delta>0 supplies a
 zero within rho with the SAME C=16. These constants are read from BidiskContact.lean and LocalBidiskContact.lean; the
 lower estimate is inherited, while the upper localization is proved separately.

 Finally contact 2 is impossible, as formalized by local_contact_unique and bidisk_not_local_contact_two. Its positive
 quadratic lower bound, applied to the localized sixth-order witness, would imply c|Z+1|^2 <= 16|Z+1|^6 for arbitrarily
 small positive |Z+1|. Dividing and tending to zero contradicts c>0. The boundary point is therefore a genuine contact-six
 singularity, and 6 is unequal to twice the nearest-w distance 1.

 6.1. Existence, uniqueness and the source bridge
 For arbitrary functions q, arbitrary torus points and natural orders, local_contact_unique proves uniqueness. Combine the
 lower bound of one order and the upper witness of the other inside min(rho1,rho2), with a common sufficiently small
 parameter interval. If K1<K2, then c1 h^K1<=C2 h^K2 is impossible as h tends to zero through positive circle
 displacements. circle_punctured_small supplies those displacements at every unit point; the parameter domain is not
 vacuous. Swapping the orders gives equality. This theorem does not assert existence of any contact germ.

 The ordinary existence bridge uses the source's LOCAL infimum/maximum-branch convention. Isolate the finite local
 numerator-root cluster at tau2. Its branch heights have positive power comparisons. Their maximum exponent K_src gives
 a lower bound for every branch, after restricting h<1, and an upper bound on a branch attaining that maximum. This branch
 tends to tau2, so it enters every smaller neighborhood with the same comparison constant. Roots outside the cluster are
 excluded by first shrinking rho0 and then delta0. Thus whenever the source order is defined in this sense, it satisfies
 HasLocalZeroSetContact. Conversely its two clauses compare the local infimum with h^K, and uniqueness prevents a
 different exponent. Graphs without such a germ are outside this explicit hypothesis; no invented order is assigned.

 This is an ordinary statement-correspondence argument, not a general Lean analytic-contact existence theorem. Reduced
 representations of the same rational function differ by scalar units; enlarged reflection bidegrees allow monomial local units
 at (-1,1). These changes cannot create another local exponent. The input Cayley map sends (infinity,0) to (-1,1)
 independently of the graph; it does not assert a singularity for every graph.

 For the actual counterexample, the exhaustive analytic branches in Sections 4-5 independently establish existence and
 K_src=6. The source conjecture would give K_src=2 for this graph, a contradiction. More generally the source conjecture
 implies SourceConjectureLocal by the preceding ordinary existence bridge and uniqueness. Calling an arbitrary contact
 hypothesis a restriction would not by itself justify that implication.

 6.2. Diagnostic locality regression (ordinary, non-Lean)
 Let p6=p_b and q6=q_b be the pair of Section 5, and use the reviewer's diagnostic
 p*(Z,W) = p6(Z,-W)(2+Z-W),
 q*(Z,W) = q6(Z,-W)(2ZW+W-Z).

 The preserved exact regression verifies reduction and bidegree-(4,3) reflection. Stability follows from stability of p6 and
 |Z-W|<2 in the open bidisk. At Z=-1 the first numerator factor is -8i(W+1)^2, nonzero on the compact closed ball
 |W-1|<=1/2. Uniform continuity excludes that factor's zeros there for Z sufficiently near -1. Every local zero therefore
 comes from the linear factor, with unique root v(Z)=Z/(2Z+1). It tends to 1 and thus enters every positive neighborhood of
 1.

 For |Z|=1 and h=|Z+1|>0, |2Z+1|^2=1+2h^2, so
 1-|v(Z)| = 2h^2/[sqrt(1+2h^2)(sqrt(1+2h^2)+1)].

 For h<1 this lies between h^2/3 and h^2, since the denominator is at least 2 and at most sqrt(3)(sqrt(3)+1)<6. The root lies
 in the disk for h>0. Take rho0=1/2, c=1/3 and C=1, and shrink delta0 for separation and h<1. The upper witness enters each
 rho in (0,rho0] after shrinking delta, preserving C=1. Hence the diagnostic has local order exactly 2. An alleged order-6
 upper witness in the separation ball would force h^2/3<=C h^6 for arbitrarily small h, impossible. This also illustrates
 general uniqueness.

 This diagnostic is an ordinary analytic argument with exact symbolic algebra, not a Lean theorem. No graph-resolvent
 realization of the product is asserted. The old predicate could borrow the remote order-6 cluster at -1 through a large radius;
 the repaired predicate cannot. See REGRESSION.md and the preserved r6 regression log for the full provenance.

 8. Related work and access limits
 The single-w theorem in [1] uses a scalar Schur complement and path-generating functions. Bickel-Hong [2] develop graph
 modifications and contact computations while retaining the one-w setting in the relevant results. Their journal Remark 3.2
 explicitly preserves the maximum convention. Neither theorem applies to this graph's two w vertices. The possibility of
 unequal contact data for general stable polynomials in [4] does not itself realize the present unweighted adjacency graph or
 settle the nearest-colour conjecture.

 Body reads are targeted, not cover-to-cover proof audits: [1] Sections 2,4,6 in arXiv v1/v2; [2] Section 3, Remark 3.2, the
 graph-extension results and the ELA journal body; [3] Definition 2.5 and Theorem 3.1; [4] Theorems 2.12,2.19 and
