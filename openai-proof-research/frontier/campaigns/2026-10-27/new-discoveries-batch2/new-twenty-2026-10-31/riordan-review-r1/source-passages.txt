117: 2.1    Riordan graphs
118: Let κ[[z]] be the ring of formal power series over an integral domain κ. If there exists a pair of
119: generating functions (g, f ) ∈ κ[[z]] × κ[[z]], f (0) = 0 such that for j ≥ 0,
120:                                                   X

122:                                                 i≥0

124: then the matrix L = [ℓi,j ]i,j≥0 is called a Riordan matrix (or, a Riordan array) over κ generated
125: by g and f . Usually, we write L = (g, f ). Since f (0) = 0, every Riordan matrix (g, f ) is an infinite
126: lower triangular matrix. If a Riordan matrix is invertible, it is called proper. Note that (g, f ) is
127: invertible if and only if g(0) 6= 0, f (0) = 0 and f ′ (0) 6= 0.
128:    For a Riordan matrix (g, f ) over Z, the matrix L = [ℓi,j ]i,j≥0 defined by
129:                                      ℓi,j ≡ [z i ]gf j (mod 2),
130: is called a binary Riordan matrix, and it is denoted by B(g, f ). The leading principal matrix of
131: order n in B(g, f ) is denoted by B(g, f )n .
132:     The following result is well known as the Fundamental Theorem of Riordan matrices (FTRM).
133: Lemma 2.1 (FTRM, [17]). Let R = (g, f ) be a Riordan matrix and let R A = B where A and B
134: are two infinite vectors with the generating functions a(z) and b(z), respectively. Then b = g a(f ).
135:    The following definition gives the notion of a Riordan graph in both labelled and unlabelled
136: cases. We note that throughout this paper the graphs are assumed to be labelled unless otherwise
137: specified.

298: 2.4    The spectral graph theory
299: Let G be a graph and A(G) be its adjacency matrix. The eigenvalues λ1 ≥ · · · ≥ λn of A(G)
300: are said to be the eigenvalues of G, and the eigenvalues form the spectrum of this graph. The
301: largest eigenvalue λ1 is called the spectral radius of G. The determinant of G, denoted by det(G),
302: is the determinant of A(G). The number of positive and negative eigenvalues of a graph are called
303: positive and negative inertias of the graph, respectively. We denote the positive and negative
304: inertias of a Riordan graph Gn by n+ (Gn ) and n− (Gn ), respectively. If Gn is understood from
305: the context, we simply write n+ and n− for the inertias.
306:     Let M be an i × j matrix. The null space of M is the set of all j-dimensional column vectors
307: X such that M X = 0. The dimension of the null space of M is called the nullity of the matrix M ,
308: and is denoted by η(M ), or just by η when M is understood from the context. The nullity of G is
309: η(G) = η(A(G)). A graph G is called singular if its adjacency matrix is singular.
310: Definition 2.12. The Laplacian matrix L(G) and signless Laplacian matrix Q(G) of a graph G
311: are defined, respectively, as D(G) − A(G) and D(G) + A(G), where D(G) is the diagonal matrix
312: of vertex degrees of G.
313: Definition 2.13. The Laplacian spectrum of a graph G is the sequence of its Laplacian eigenvalues,
314: i.e. µ1 ≥ µ2 ≥ · · · ≥ µn−1 ≥ µn = 0. We let Lspec(G) denote the set {µ1 , µ2 , . . . , µn }. The signless
315: Laplacian spectrum of G is the sequence of its signless Laplacian eigenvalues, i.e. q1 ≥ q2 ≥ · · · ≥ qn .

117: 2.1    Riordan graphs
118: Let κ[[z]] be the ring of formal power series over an integral domain κ. If there exists a pair of
119: generating functions (g, f ) ∈ κ[[z]] × κ[[z]], f (0) = 0 such that for j ≥ 0,
120:                                                   X

122:                                                 i≥0

124: then the matrix L = [ℓi,j ]i,j≥0 is called a Riordan matrix (or, a Riordan array) over κ generated
125: by g and f . Usually, we write L = (g, f ). Since f (0) = 0, every Riordan matrix (g, f ) is an infinite
126: lower triangular matrix. If a Riordan matrix is invertible, it is called proper. Note that (g, f ) is
127: invertible if and only if g(0) 6= 0, f (0) = 0 and f ′ (0) 6= 0.
128:    For a Riordan matrix (g, f ) over Z, the matrix L = [ℓi,j ]i,j≥0 defined by
129:                                      ℓi,j ≡ [z i ]gf j (mod 2),
130: is called a binary Riordan matrix, and it is denoted by B(g, f ). The leading principal matrix of
131: order n in B(g, f ) is denoted by B(g, f )n .

133: Lemma 2.1 (FTRM, [17]). Let R = (g, f ) be a Riordan matrix and let R A = B where A and B
134: are two infinite vectors with the generating functions a(z) and b(z), respectively. Then b = g a(f ).

298: 2.4    The spectral graph theory
299: Let G be a graph and A(G) be its adjacency matrix. The eigenvalues λ1 ≥ · · · ≥ λn of A(G)
300: are said to be the eigenvalues of G, and the eigenvalues form the spectrum of this graph. The
301: largest eigenvalue λ1 is called the spectral radius of G. The determinant of G, denoted by det(G),
302: is the determinant of A(G). The number of positive and negative eigenvalues of a graph are called
303: positive and negative inertias of the graph, respectively. We denote the positive and negative
304: inertias of a Riordan graph Gn by n+ (Gn ) and n− (Gn ), respectively. If Gn is understood from
305: the context, we simply write n+ and n− for the inertias.
306:     Let M be an i × j matrix. The null space of M is the set of all j-dimensional column vectors
307: X such that M X = 0. The dimension of the null space of M is called the nullity of the matrix M ,
308: and is denoted by η(M ), or just by η when M is understood from the context. The nullity of G is
309: η(G) = η(A(G)). A graph G is called singular if its adjacency matrix is singular.
310: Definition 2.12. The Laplacian matrix L(G) and signless Laplacian matrix Q(G) of a graph G
311: are defined, respectively, as D(G) − A(G) and D(G) + A(G), where D(G) is the diagonal matrix
312: of vertex degrees of G.
313: Definition 2.13. The Laplacian spectrum of a graph G is the sequence of its Laplacian eigenvalues,
314: i.e. µ1 ≥ µ2 ≥ · · · ≥ µn−1 ≥ µn = 0. We let Lspec(G) denote the set {µ1 , µ2 , . . . , µn }. The signless
315: Laplacian spectrum of G is the sequence of its signless Laplacian eigenvalues, i.e. q1 ≥ q2 ≥ · · · ≥ qn .
