## Adversarial ordinary mathematical audit

**Coordinates and measure space: PASS.** With row-vector stationary law `pi=((1+t)/2,(1-t)/2)`, the displayed matrix has positive entries for `0<l<1`, `|t|<1`, rows summing to one, and `pi P=pi`. Write `J` for the matrix whose rows equal `pi`. Then `P=J+l(I-J)` and `J²=J`, giving `P^i=J+l^i(I-J)=P(l^i,t)`. The stated inverse chart `l=u+v-1`, `t=(u-v)/(2-u-v)` is correct. It covers an open neighborhood of the constructed symmetric point, which has `u=v=(1+l0)/2`. It need not cover negative eigenvalues far away to prove local maximality.

The stationary cylinder formula in source (6.1) gives a unique stationary two-sided Markov law here. Every finite contiguous cylinder has positive mass; every nonempty open cylinder contains such a cylinder. Conversely, near this law, one- and two-coordinate cylinder probabilities stay positive and recover `u` and `v` by conditional-probability ratios. On a finite-alphabet compact shift, cylinders are clopen and their probabilities determine weak convergence; finite-coordinate cylinders at arbitrary negative times reduce by stationarity to nonnegative-time cylinders. These observations give the asserted local correspondence in the relative weak topology. Boundary Markov laws, including nonergodic stationary mixtures at degenerate transitions, cannot invalidate the conclusion: positivity of these finitely many cylinder probabilities is an open condition around this point. This is a local chart argument, not a claim that all boundary laws have unique matrix coordinates.

**Entropy identity: PASS.** The conditional entropy in the proof is `H(r,t)=H(X_i|X_0)` when `r=l^i`. Stationarity makes it equal to `H(X_0|X_i)` since both one-coordinate entropies are `H(pi)`; no unjustified time-reversibility assumption is needed. For ordered selected indices, the marginal selected chain has successive transition matrices `P^gap`; the Markov entropy chain rule therefore gives exactly the displayed sum, including the initial `H(pi)` when the selected set is nonempty.

For a particular pair of endpoints at distance `i`, being consecutive selected points has probability `2^{-(i+1)}`: both endpoints selected, the `i-1` intervening points omitted, and unrestricted points outside. There are `N-i` such pairs. The probability of a nonempty selection is `1-2^-N`. Multiplying the resulting average entropy by `2/N` gives exactly the finite formula in the candidate. The full block entropy is `H(pi)+(N-1)H(l,t)`. Complementation permutes all subsets with equal weights, so source intricacy is twice average sample complexity minus block entropy per site. The initial term vanishes, and the series limit follows from `0<=H<=log 2` with a geometric tail (or dominated convergence on the positive integers). No missing factor two, gap-weight shift, empty-set entropy, or boundary correction remains. One-sided finite blocks suffice for this computation, but they must be connected to the stationary **two-sided** source law.

**Diagonal maximum: PASS.** Uniform geometric domination gives a continuous extension of `f(l)=F(l,0)` to `[0,1]`. Its two endpoints vanish. On `0<l<1`, `l^i<=l`, and binary entropy is strictly increasing on `(0,1/2)`; hence each weighted entropy difference is nonnegative and the `i=2` difference is positive. Thus `f>0` inside. Compactness gives an interior maximizer `l0`. No uniqueness, isolation, or strict longitudinal maximum is used.

**Transverse calculation: PASS.** Setting `x=(1-r)/2`, expansion of the *weighted* binary entropies gives the coefficient

`c(r)=-x h'(x)+(x²/2)h''(x)=-x log((1-x)/x)-x/(2(1-x))`.

Both the stationary weights and transition arguments must be differentiated; the candidate does so correctly. Direct differentiation gives

`c'(r)=0.5 log((1+r)/(1-r))-r/(1+r)^2`,

`c''(r)=1/(1-r²)-(1-r)/(1+r)^3=4r/((1-r)(1+r)^3)>0`.

Here `c'(0)=0`, so `c'>0` on `(0,1)` and `c` is strictly increasing on `[0,1)`. It is **not** necessary or claimed that `c` itself is positive; for example `c(0)=-1/2`. Therefore `c(l^i)-c(l)<=0`, strictly for `i=2`, and summing the positive geometric weights yields `F_tt(l,0)<0`. The inequality direction is correct.

**Infinite differentiation and full local promotion: PASS.** On the proposed rectangle `[a,b] × [-delta,delta]`, all arguments of `h` lie in `[(1-b)(1-delta)/2,(1+delta)/2]`, a compact subinterval of `(0,1)`. The functions `h,h',h''` and the elementary prefactors in the first two `t` derivatives are uniformly bounded there independently of `i`. This supplies geometric majorants for the function and both derivative series. Standard uniform differentiation in `t`, with joint continuity of the derivative series, establishes the stated continuous `F_tt`. Differentiation in `l` is unnecessary; there is no hidden need for a uniform `l`-derivative estimate.

Swapping the two labels gives evenness in `t` and hence `F_t(l,0)=0` for every nearby `l`. Continuity makes `F_tt` negative on a full rectangle about `(l0,0)`. Integration on either side of zero gives `F(l,t)<=F(l,0)` there (strict for `t!=0`), and diagonal maximality gives `F(l,0)<=F(l0,0)`. This is a genuine two-dimensional local maximum, even if the diagonal maximum is flat. It makes no assertion of local optimality among arbitrary invariant measures or higher-order Markov measures.
