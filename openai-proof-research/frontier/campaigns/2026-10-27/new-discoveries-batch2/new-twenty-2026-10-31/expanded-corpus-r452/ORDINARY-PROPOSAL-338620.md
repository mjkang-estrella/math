## Exact source and target
Original v2, Section1.6, Conjecture1.9, page6, requires only (i) A_ij=0 whenever j in S_i and (ii) every nonforced k-by-k minor nonsingular. It states random independent Bernoulli-half S_i, and a universal positive constant c in |F| >= c binom(n,k)^c with high probability. The source introduction uses k<=n. No balanced-rank hypothesis appears in the original conjecture. Extra zeros outside S_i are permitted. This matters: requiring nonzeros everywhere else would be a different target.

## Two failed shortcuts and replacement
1. A random permitted binary matrix does not automatically work: independent rows need not have the common parity relation required to make all cofactors nonzero. A positive probability for one minor cannot establish simultaneous nonsingularity.
2. The source GM-MDS theorem gives fields of size at least n+k-1 and does not contradict c*n^c for arbitrary small c. Its Reed-Solomon construction cannot supply the binary conclusion. Instead use a freely chosen basis of the even-parity hyperplane, respecting each row's support constraint.

## Lemma: independent representatives of vector subspaces
Let V_1,...,V_m be subspaces of a vector space over any field. Independent vectors v_i in V_i exist iff dim(sum_{i in I} V_i)>=|I| for every I subset [m]. Necessity follows because the chosen vectors in I are independent in that sum.
For sufficiency induct on m, with m=0 trivial. If a nonempty proper I is tight, let U=sum_I V_i of dimension |I|. The induction hypothesis chooses a basis of U with one vector in each V_i, i in I. For J in the complementary indices, dim((U+sum_J V_j)/U) >= |I union J|-|I|=|J|. Apply induction to the images in the quotient and lift the resulting independent representatives. Their union with the basis of U is independent. If no nonempty proper I is tight and m>1, every such sum has dimension at least |I|+1. Choose a nonzero v_m in V_m. For each nonempty J subset [m-1], quotient by span(v_m) reduces dimension by at most1, leaving at least |J|. Induction in that quotient and lifting finishes. For m=1 choose a nonzero vector using dim V_1>=1. This proves the lemma without invoking an unread external theorem.

## Deterministic support criterion
Put T_i=[n] minus S_i and W={x in F2^n: sum_j x_j=0}. Define V_i to be vectors supported on T_i whose coordinate sum is0. Assume:
(A) every nonempty I subset [n-1] has |union_{i in I}T_i|>=|I|+1;
(B) any two distinct T_i intersect.
For singleton I, (A) gives |T_i|>=2, and V_i is spanned by vectors e_a+e_b for pairs a,b in T_i. For arbitrary I the union of the complete graphs on T_i is connected on U=union_I T_i, since the cliques pairwise intersect by(B). Their edge vectors span exactly all even-parity vectors on U. Indeed a path from a to b sums to e_a+e_b, and fixing a root gives |U|-1 independent such vectors. Thus dim(sum_I V_i)=|U|-1>=|I|. The lemma supplies n-1 independent row vectors v_i in V_i. Since dim W=n-1 they form a basis of W.
Projection W to any n-1 coordinates is injective: a vector in its kernel is supported on the one omitted coordinate and has sum0, hence is zero. It is consequently an isomorphism. Projecting the row basis therefore leaves independent rows for EVERY choice of n-1 columns. The resulting A has all maximal minors nonsingular and satisfies A_ij=0 on S_i. Thus it satisfies even more than condition(ii), with no need to characterize forced minors.

## Probability of the deterministic criterion
For any fixed distinct i,j, P(T_i intersect T_j empty)=(3/4)^n. A union bound gives P(B fails)<=binom(n-1,2)*(3/4)^n=o(1).
Failure of(A) gives a row set I of size t between1 and n-1 with union size at most t. Therefore some J of n-t columns is excluded by every T_i in I. These t(n-t) independent Bernoulli conditions have probability 2^{-t(n-t)}. Hence
P(A fails)<=sum_{t=1}^{n-1} binom(n-1,t) binom(n,n-t) 2^{-t(n-t)}.
Let m=min(t,n-t). Each product of binomial coefficients is at most n^{2m}, and t(n-t)=m(n-m)>=mn/2. For all sufficiently large n, a_n=n^2*2^{-n/2}<1, so every summand is at most a_n^m<=a_n. The whole sum is at most n^3*2^{-n/2}=o(1). These are symbolic bounds proved by counting possible obstruction sets; no program, finite enumeration, numerical test or matrix generation was used.
Thus (A) and(B) hold jointly with probability1-o(1), proving the claimed binary construction and contradiction for every possible absolute c>0.
