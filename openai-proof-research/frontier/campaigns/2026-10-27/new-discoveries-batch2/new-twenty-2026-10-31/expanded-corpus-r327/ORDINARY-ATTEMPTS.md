## 370211: full ordinary proof, excluded by public resolution

Exact endpoint: every finite simple graph G on n vertices has E(G)/2 >= n - xi_f(complement G). Energy means sum of absolute adjacency eigenvalues. Definition 1.4 of Elphick–Tang–Zhang arXiv:2504.01295v5 defines xi_f(H) as the infimum of d/r over positive integer d,r and rank-r Hermitian orthogonal projectors P_v in C^d with P_v P_w=0 for edges vw of H. Complex projectors, not only real ones, are allowed.

Proof independent of the public resolution. Fix such a representation for complement G. Write A=A_+-A_- for its positive and negative spectral parts; tr A_+=tr A_-=E(G)/2. Put P=diag(P_v) on the direct sum of n copies of C^d, and restrict operators to W=im P, of dimension nr. Let C=P(J_n tensor I_d)P restricted to W and B=P(A tensor I_d)P restricted to W. Then C is positive semidefinite, rank C <= d, and B=C-I_W: diagonal blocks of B are zero; for a nonedge of G the corresponding P_v P_w vanishes, and for an edge it is precisely the corresponding off-diagonal block of C. Hence tr B=0. Every vector in ker C is a -1 eigenvector of B, so its negative spectral trace is at least nr-d (if nr<d this lower bound is harmless). Trace zero implies tr B_+=tr B_- >= nr-d.

For a Hermitian X=U-V with U,V positive semidefinite, tr X_+ <= tr U: take the spectral projector Q onto the positive eigenspaces and use tr QX=tr X_+, tr QV>=0, tr QU<=tr U. Apply this with U=P(A_+ tensor I)P and V=P(A_- tensor I)P restricted to W. Cyclicity of trace and the common rank-r diagonal blocks give tr U=sum_v (A_+)_{vv} tr P_v=r tr A_+. Thus r E(G)/2 >= nr-d, or E(G)/2 >= n-d/r. It follows that n-E(G)/2 is a lower bound for every admissible d/r and hence <= their infimum. The admissible set is nonempty (mutually orthogonal rank-one coordinate projectors suffice), and bounded below by 1 for nonempty G. For the empty graph the natural convention xi_f(empty)=0 makes the endpoint immediate; if the source leaves this convention unspecified, restrict its standard parameter to nonempty graphs and state the empty case separately. This is the exact energy inequality, not an inertia-only helper.

Failed shortcuts before closure: merely substituting xi_f for chi_f in the fractional-colouring theorem reverses the useful implication, since xi_f<=chi_f. Bounding the compression by d E(G) only yields E(G)/2 >= nr/d-1. The rank-r partial-trace calculation above repairs that loss and handles the complex representation directly.

Prior exclusion: Kumar–Pragada, arXiv:2607.19817v1, submitted 22 July 2026 06:51:31 UTC, Theorem 1.2 already proves E(G)>=2(n-alpha(G)). The complete Sections 2–3 proof was read (neighbourhood deletion inequality, Schur complements, edge inequality and induction). Their introduction explicitly lists alpha(G)<=xi_f(complement G) and explicitly says their theorem resolves the original Conjecture 6.1. Independently, if S is an independent set of G, its vertices form a clique of complement G; their rank-r projector ranges are pairwise orthogonal. Thus |S|r<=d, alpha(G)<=d/r for every representation, and alpha(G)<=xi_f(complement G). Consequently n-xi_f<=n-alpha<=E/2. No novelty claim follows from our alternative compression proof. ## 370372: finite-unital ordinary reconstruction closes; source qualification HOLD

Source: arXiv:2509.12004v1, introduction, Theorem 1, Corollary 1, Lemma 2, Theorem 3 and following conjecture. Finite unital rings are the intended setting. Vertices of Cl_2(R) are (e,u) with e nonzero idempotent and u a unit; distinct vertices are adjacent iff ef=fe=0 OR uv=vu=1. Product vertices include ((0,f),(u,v)) when f!=0. These cannot be omitted.

Attempt 1: lift a given graph isomorphism F coordinatewise through R x P. This fails as a proof: F preserves the union of the orthogonality and inverse predicates, whereas product adjacency requires each predicate separately, conjoined with the appropriate predicate in P. A graph isomorphism need not come furnished with maps on idempotents and units. Moreover the zero-idempotent slice is absent from Cl_2(R) but present in the product. These are logical gaps, not counterexamples.

Attempt 2: recover the two factors from intrinsic graph invariants. For k=|U(R)| and t=|Id(R)\{0}|, let O_e count nonzero idempotents orthogonal to e. The degree is t-1+O_e(k-1) for u self-inverse, and t+O_e(k-1) otherwise. Minimum degree t-1 is attained at (1,1), so t and then k follow from the graph order. With k>2 the residue modulo k-1 separates self-inverse and other vertices; k=2 has only self-inverse units, and k=1 reduces directly to the idempotent orthogonality graph. Also O_e=0 iff e=1, because 1-e is a nonzero orthogonal idempotent whenever e!=1. These invariants recover sizes and inversion type counts, but do not recover an isomorphism of the entire idempotent orthogonality relations.

Attempt 3: use the all-involution slice. Let T=U'(R), s=|T|. Its induced graph replaces every idempotent vertex by an s-clique, with complete cross adjacency exactly for orthogonal idempotents. Thus it is a clique blowup of the idempotent graph including 1. If s and the blowup graph are intrinsically recoverable, finite true-twin classes permit division of multiplicities by s and recovery of that base graph. Together with inversion-pair data this would construct the needed product isomorphism: extend the base map by 0, pair inversion orbits arbitrarily, use the given P-ring isomorphism, and check both adjacency disjuncts componentwise. The reconstruction can in fact be completed for finite nonzero unital rings, as follows. The degree residue argument already proves preservation of that slice for k>2. For k=2 the unit group has order two, so all units are involutions; for k=1 the same is immediate. Thus s is recovered as the size of the slice divided by t, and agrees in both rings. In a clique blowup X[K_s], two vertices have identical closed neighborhoods exactly when their base vertices have identical closed neighborhoods. Quotient by this equivalence; each class has size s times the size of the corresponding base true-twin class. An isomorphism preserves the quotient adjacency and these sizes. Divide by the common positive integer s and expand each quotient vertex into a clique of that size to recover X up to isomorphism. This proves cancellation, including X=K1 and s=1. Here X is the graph of nonzero idempotents with orthogonality adjacency, and has t vertices.

Choose a recovered base isomorphism a:Id(R1)\{0}->Id(R2)\{0}, extend by a(0)=0, and choose a bijection b on units intertwining inversion. The latter exists because the numbers k of units and s of fixed inversion points agree: pair the s fixed points and the (k-s)/2 two-cycles. Orthogonality on all idempotents, including 0, is preserved by a; a nonzero idempotent is never orthogonal to itself, so the graph relation suffices for equal arguments too. Inversion predicates, including equal self-inverse arguments, are preserved by b. A unital ring isomorphism h:P1->P2 preserves both predicates and zero. The map
((e,f),(u,v)) |-> ((a(e),h(f)),(b(u),h(v)))
is a bijection on nonzero-idempotent/unit product vertices. Orthogonality of the product idempotents is the conjunction of the two factor orthogonality predicates; inverse-unit adjacency is the conjunction of the two factor inverse predicates. Both are preserved in both directions, so their disjunction and distinctness are preserved. This proves the stated product isomorphism in the finite nonzero unital setting. If one factor is the zero ring, its product is canonically the other factor; if Cl2(R) is empty then R is the zero ring because every nonzero unital ring has vertex (1,1). These cases reduce directly. Thus the full intended finite ordinary route closes, subject to independent checking; an arbitrary infinite-ring version is NOT proved. No explicit finite counterexample was searched computationally.

## 370689: matching preorder, sustained but unresolved

Exact source definition (Section 5 of arXiv:2510.01110v1): N<=M iff EVERY nonincreasing labelled graphic sequence d_1>=...>=d_n realizable by a graph containing M is also realizable by a graph containing N. It is not containment between the matching edge sets and is not the unlabelled existence relation.

Attempt 1: exploit the source potential phi(M)=sum_{uv in M}2^(u+v). Lemma 10 makes phi strictly decrease along each directed elementary switch. If every preorder comparison were a switch chain, cycles would be impossible. But that switch-generation assertion is the source's separate Conjecture 11, not a proved lemma. Reusing the source implication only restates the open bridge. Also the potential is not injective: binary carries and repeated sums prevent deducing equality of matchings solely from equality of phi. No claim that a potential equality creates a preorder cycle.

Attempt 2: distinguish M and N by one nonincreasing degree sequence. If a sequence has a UNIQUE labelled realization H containing M but not N, it separates the two. Threshold graphs provide useful unique-realization sequences, but their forced nested-neighbourhood order cannot presently encode every prescribed perfect matching. The identical all-one sequence realizes every matching, so taking the matching graph itself provides no separation despite the unique edge set before relabelling. What is needed is a proof that for every distinct M,N some sorted graphic sequence realizes exactly one of them (in the membership sense), not necessarily that every matching is uniquely realizable.

Attempt 3: induction at the first differing partner or an alternating cycle of M symmetric-difference N. The source's switches allow reductions of individual four-endpoint patterns, but maintaining a sorted separating degree sequence while attaching the remaining vertices requires a padding lemma. Adding isolated or universal vertices changes the sorted label positions and can permit new matchings. No padding lemma was proved. The exact missing result is separation by sorted-realizability profiles, or the stronger source switch-generation theorem. The source n=4 calculation and its n=6 incomparable examples are evidence only, not the universal theorem. Its h=2 examples of nonantisymmetry concern 2-factors, outside the matching domain, and are not counterexamples to this task. No enumeration, solver or finite diagnostic was run.

Current arXiv history lists only v1 (1 October 2025). Bounded exact-title/author public searches found the original, not a complete later proof; this is not novelty clearance. Full predecessor/citing/journal coverage remains incomplete.

## 370263: correction-only subgroup obstruction

The source defines D(N,L) via gcd(d,L) of common differences d for L-distinct-element cyclic APs; its prose about minimal differences introduces additional representational ambiguity. Regardless of minimality, if g=gcd(d,L) and L distinct residues form an AP mod N, then L<=N/gcd(d,N). For N=mk and L=nk, gcd(d,L) does not automatically divide N, but taking a claimed g that divides both N and L gives the obstruction below.

Take m=3,n=2,k=2. The proposed RHS includes g=2. If gcd(d,4)=2 then d is even; modulo 6 its additive order is at most 3. It cannot generate a progression of four distinct residues. Hence 2 is not in D(6,4). This refutes the literal formula under the source's gcd definition. It is a parameter-extension correction, not an eligible significant discovery. No stronger false claim is substituted. The original Lemma 2.3 treats N=mL and uses g<=m; replacing L by nk while keeping modulus mk breaks that ratio. v1 and v2 were retrieved; v2 definitions and Lemmas 2.1–2.3 proofs and Conjecture 5.1 read, v1 not fully read.
