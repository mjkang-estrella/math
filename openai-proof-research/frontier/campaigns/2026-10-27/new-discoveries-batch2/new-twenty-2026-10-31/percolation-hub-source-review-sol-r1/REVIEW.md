## Independent proof

Let T_m have vertices h,a,b,l_1,...,l_m, triangle h-a-b-h, and pendant edges h-l_i. Replace every edge by its two opposite arcs. This is simple and strongly connected: every vertex can reach h and h can reach every vertex. It has n=m+3 vertices, m+3 undirected edges and 2(m+3) arcs. Use the original definition H[(i,j),(j,k)]=1 when k differs from i. We reason symbolically, with no matrix calculation by program.

Partition the arc vertices of its OLG into I={l_i -> h}, C=the six triangle arcs, and O={h -> l_i}. No arc can precede a member of I: its tail l_i has only neighbor h, and immediate reversal is forbidden. No member of O has a successor for the same reason. Inside C the only transitions are the two disjoint directed 3-cycles circling the triangle in opposite directions. Order I,C,O. The diagonal blocks of H are zero, P_3 direct-sum P_3, zero; off-diagonal blocks run only forwards in that order. Its characteristic polynomial is therefore x^(2m)(x^3-1)^2. The permutation blocks have spectral radius 1, so rho(H)=1. At p=1/2, p rho(H)=1/2<1. For comparison, the bidirected star has H^2=0: a nonbacktracking walk of two edges ends at a leaf and cannot extend. Its radius is zero; adding the triangle avoids any reciprocal-zero convention. These are one obstruction family, not separate discoveries.

For all sufficiently large m, define a giant to be a maximal strongly connected component with at least n/16 vertices; choose m>29 so this threshold exceeds 2. The threshold is a fixed positive fraction of the ORIGINAL vertex count. For site percolation the retained graph is bidirected, so connected, weak and strongly connected components coincide. If h is closed, remaining components have at most two vertices. If h is open, every open leaf belongs to its component; every component not containing h has at most two vertices. Thus in every configuration there is at most one giant.

Given h open, let X be the number of open leaves. Independence gives E X=m/2 and Var X=m/4. Chebyshev gives P(X<m/4)<=4/m. For m>=3, m/4>=n/8>n/16. Consequently, if E_m denotes existence of a giant, P(E_m)>= (1/2)(1-4/m), and P(at least two giants | E_m)=0 whenever the conditioning event has positive probability. In fact P(E_m)<=1/2 for m>29; this site family does NOT have giant existence whp. Given h open, existence is whp, and given existence uniqueness is exactly certain. This distinction is essential.

In undirected bond percolation, keep each undirected edge independently with probability 1/2 and retain all vertices. Now X counts retained pendant edges with the same mean, variance and bound. Hence P(E_m)>=1-4/m tends to one, and P(exactly one giant) tends to one. Each nonhub component has at most two vertices, in every outcome. Bond independence of opposite arcs is a different model: if every directed arc is independently retained with probability 1/2, leaf l_i is in the SCC of h exactly when both pendant arcs survive. These events are independent over i, with probability 1/4. Thus E X=m/4, Var X=3m/16, and P(X<m/8)<=12/m. Since m/8>=n/16 for m>=3, SCC giant existence and uniqueness are whp. Every SCC not containing h is contained in {a,b} or a singleton leaf, so at most one can be giant. Weak components also have at most one giant (and existence follows already from the SCC bound). We do not extend the claim to a count of distinct maximal in-/out-reachable sets, which are not a partition on a general directed graph.

The source's edge-fraction motivation does not avoid the obstruction: every leaf in the hub SCC contributes its two opposite arcs in the site/bidirected model and in the independent-arc SCC model. The lower bounds above yield a positive fraction of the original 2(m+3) arcs as well. Removal of h destroys macroscopic mass into singletons and a piece of size at most two; it does not produce two macroscopic fragments. The heuristic inference from fragility to coexistence of large clusters fails for such hubs.
