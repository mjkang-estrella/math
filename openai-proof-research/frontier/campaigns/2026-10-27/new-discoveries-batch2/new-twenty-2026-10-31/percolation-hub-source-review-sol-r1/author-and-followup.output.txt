## 330008: a finite hub obstruction, with a source-range hold

The complete two-page 1503.00410v1 is read. It assumes an order-n strongly connected digraph D, permits symmetric arcs, defines H[(i,j),(j,l)]=1 for l != i, and uses independent SITE occupation. Its Conjecture 2 says below p rho(H)=1 a percolating cluster, if present, is nonunique whp as n grows. The corpus instead says BOND probability. Neither specifies a graph-sequence class or formal meaning of its conditional whp. These cannot be silently repaired. Source 1505.03963v5 definitions pp2–3 define a finite giant as a positive fraction of all vertices. Its pp11–12 Theorems20/24 and Corollary25 concern an infinite LOCALLY FINITE graph and increasing finite subgraphs; the paragraph immediately before Corollary25 explicitly calls its weaker version a conjecture in reference66, namely 1503.00410. Read that proof and reference66. This is concrete contrary source evidence, not a generic novelty caveat.

Route A (zero spectral radius, full finite family). Let D_m be the bidirected star with hub h and m leaves. It is simple and strongly connected. A nonbacktracking walk can enter h from a leaf and leave to a different leaf, where it must stop; thus H^2=0 and rho(H)=0, algebraically, without a matrix computation. For p=1/2 site percolation, condition on h open. If L is the number of open leaves, E L=m/2 and Var L=m/4. Chebyshev gives Pr(L<m/4) <=4/m. Every open nonisolated vertex belongs to the same component; without h all components are singleton. For m>4, define giant as at least m/4 vertices. There is at most one giant in EVERY outcome. Given existence its uniqueness probability is exactly1, and its existence probability is at least (1/2)(1-4/m). This contradicts the unrestricted finite conditional formulation. It is not an infinite locally finite counterexample: the infinite star has infinite hub degree.

Route B (positive spectral radius, same family rather than a second result). Attach a triangle h,a,b to the hub, retain m pendant leaves, and put both arcs on each edge. D_m is again strongly connected. In its oriented line graph, incoming leaf-arcs have no predecessors and outgoing leaf-arcs no successors. The remaining recurrent arcs form exactly the two directed 3-cycles around the triangle. Ordering transient/recurrent blocks makes H block triangular, with nilpotent transient blocks and two 3-cycle permutation diagonal blocks. Therefore rho(H)=1, exactly. At p=1/2, p rho(H)=1/2<1. An open hub joins all L open leaves; a closed hub leaves only a,b possibly joined. If m/4>2 every giant must contain h, hence there is at most one. The same Chebyshev lower bound on existence applies, while conditional nonuniqueness probability is0. This avoids treating rho=0 alone as a discovery and confirms the obstruction is concentration of mass at a hub, not just a reciprocal-zero notation issue.

Route C (literal BOND model). For an undirected triangle-plus-leaves with each edge independently retained with p=1/2, L retained pendant edges is Binomial(m,1/2). The hub component has at least L+1 vertices; all other components are isolated leaves or a component among a,b. Again existence tends1 and at most one giant can exist. Under independent DIRECTED arc retention, a leaf is strongly connected to h exactly when both pendant arcs survive, independently with probability1/4. E L=m/4, Var L=3m/16, and Pr(L<m/8)<=12/m. Every strongly connected component with more than2 vertices contains h; so the same finite obstruction works with m/8 in place of m/4. These distinctions pay model cases rather than transferring site conclusions to bond without proof.

Failed mechanisms and boundary: bounded-degree finite trees avoid high-degree hubs, but with fixed p<1 their large-depth clusters need not occupy a fixed positive fraction of all vertices. Joining many stars through a sparse backbone may raise rho(H) or lose positive-fraction concentration. Neither gives a locally finite infinite counterexample to the later theorem. No such counterexample is claimed. Requiring strong connectivity of the OLG would also exclude these graphs, though original Conjecture2 does not explicitly impose it; that condition is imposed for some DIFFERENT intermediate estimates.

                                           Abstract
                                           We analyze site percolation on directed and undirected graphs with site-dependent open-site probabilities. We construct upper
                                           bounds on cluster susceptibilities, vertex connectivity functions, and the expected number of simple open cycles through a chosen
                                           arc; separate bounds are given on finite and infinite (di)graphs. These produce lower bounds for percolation and uniqueness

                                           transitions in infinite (di)graphs, and for the formation of a giant component in finite (di)graphs. The bounds are formulated in
                                           terms of appropriately weighted adjacency and non-backtracking (Hashimoto) matrices. It turns out to be the uniqueness criterion
                                           that is most closely associated with an asymptotically vanishing probability of forming a giant strongly-connected component on a
                                           large finite (di)graph.
