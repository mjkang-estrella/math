# Proof and remaining obligation

A plane of order q has indexed point and line sets of size q²+q+1, Boolean incidence, degree q+1 on both sides, exactly one joining line for each distinct pair of points, exactly one intersection point for each distinct pair of lines, and a quadrangle. Unique incidences are expressed as cardinality one of a filtered duplicate-free enumeration. With q ≥ 2 these are the usual finite projective-plane axioms together with its order. Indexing chooses labels, not extra geometry.

`step` takes a union with all lines having at least r infected points, always counting in the OLD state. `rounds` iterates it synchronously. `Percolates` quantifies over all natural rounds. `TimeIs` asserts fullness at t and failure at every smaller round. Strong induction establishes existence of a least time for a percolating set; `time` chooses it, and `time_unique` proves any `TimeIs` witness equals it. `percolates_iff_full_stable` connects the eventual-full formulation to a full stable closure. `Minimal` excludes every proper subset, not just subsets of one smaller cardinality.

The 31×31 incidence table is copied numeric data, but its defining rule and all plane axioms are independently checked by the kernel with `decide`. The normalized vector for index i is (1,⌊i/5⌋,i mod 5) for i < 25, (0,1,i−25) for 25 ≤ i < 30, and (0,0,1) for i = 30. `incidence_dot` proves incidence precisely when the integer dot product is zero modulo 5. These are the canonical representatives over the field of five elements. This development uses arithmetic modulo 5 directly, not an imported abstract ZMod projectivization or an isomorphism theorem to mathlib geometry.

`card_mono`, `step_mono`, and `rounds_mono` prove monotonicity. A fixed point B containing C and omitting v prevents C from ever becoming full: every round from C remains in B. For each initially infected v in a witness A, the certificate supplies a fixed B_v containing A without v and omitting v. Any proper subset C of A misses such a v, so the separator blocks C. `minimal_from_separators` proves this argument for arbitrary C and arbitrary natural percolation times.

The 26 deletion certificates are one module each (10 at threshold 4 and 16 at threshold 5). Each contains the literal semantic subset, fixed-point, and omitted-point assertions; each is proved by kernel reduction. Control modules combine those assertions with fullness and all earlier-round failures. They prove `control4` and `control5`, not just numerical diagnostics.

Bounded structural approach: a minimal threshold-5 set cannot contain an entire six-point line. Removing a point of that line leaves five infected points, restoring the removed point in the first synchronous step. Monotonicity then implies percolation after that deletion, contradicting minimality. This is formalized in `Structure.lean`; `line_deletion_count` checks the finite count, while `minimal_no_redundant` proves the general deletion argument. Consequently any first-round line in a minimal threshold-5 set has exactly five initial points. This does not constrain the interactions of those lines sufficiently to finish the nonexistence proof. In particular deleting a point can alter later triggering lines, so merely counting the first-round lines does not close the argument.

The conditional endpoint applies Conjecture20 at q = r = 5 and PG5, contradicting `NoMinimalTwoRound5`. Its single hypothesis is explicit. To remove it, a subsequent worker must either finish a mathematical proof or supply all of: a proved completeness bridge to an independently generated CNF; a proved projective normalization to any imposed fixed triple (or an unnormalized certificate); a proved sound checker; and bounded kernel replay of the certificate with theorem-level chunk links. No part of that chain is currently hidden behind a definition or claimed complete here.
