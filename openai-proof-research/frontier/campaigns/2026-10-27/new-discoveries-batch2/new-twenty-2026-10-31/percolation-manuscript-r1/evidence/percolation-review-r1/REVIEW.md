## A. Source statement and quantifiers

The authors are Dániel Gerbner, Balázs Keszegh, Gábor Mészáros, Balázs Patkós and Máté Vizer. Title: *Line Percolation in Finite Projective Planes*, arXiv:1608.00531v1, submitted 1 August 2016. The live abs page still lists only v1 (`downloads/arxiv-abs/body`, lines 164–166). The journal record is SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics 32(2) (2018), 864–881, DOI 10.1137/16M1087709, online 17 April 2018. Bibliography is confirmed by the publisher metadata and the later citing paper's reference [16] (`triangle.txt`, lines 1002–1003).

In the independently converted `source.txt`, lines 62–78 (PDF p. 2) define infection at **at least** r points, synchronous old-state updates, closure, eventual percolation, least percolation time, and inclusion minimality. This is not minimum cardinality. Section 5, lines 529–549 (p. 11), explicitly studies inclusion-minimal sets and proves Proposition 17, excluding time 1. Lines 580–599 (p. 12) discuss the r=2,3 cases and the three-round Proposition 18. Its hypothesis is 4 ≤ r ≤ (q+7)/3; its construction and deletion proof occupy lines 594–671 (pp. 12–14). At q=5 it covers r≤4, and says nothing prohibiting the proposed threshold-5 obstruction. I read the construction, Claim 19 and all three deletion cases, not just the proposition label.

Conjecture 20 is at `source.txt:674–675`, PDF p. 14. It is an existence assertion for each 4≤r≤q in an arbitrary plane of order q. The paper's general-plane convention is at lines 89–102 (pp. 2–3). There is no sufficiently-large-q qualifier, Desarguesian restriction, or exclusion of r=q in the section. The statement in `mathdb-entry.json` matches this quantification. Its final explanatory sentence about a nearby construction must not be mistaken for a construction proving the conjecture: the nearby proposition gives time 3, not time 2. Raw corpus provenance is line 3158 of the chunk recorded in `raw-input-receipt.json`; the exact line is preserved in `mathdb-raw-line.json`.

`extracted/src/Definitions.lean:8–17` describes an arbitrary labeled finite plane, with line and point degrees q+1, unique joins and meets, and a quadrangle. Labeling points and lines by Fin(q²+q+1) does not restrict to coordinatizable planes. Lines 19–31 implement subset, fullness, old-state threshold updates, iteration, eventual percolation, exclusion of **every proper subset**, and exact time. Lines 32–52 construct and characterize least time. Lines 59–66 relate eventual fullness to full stable closure. Lines 67–68 quantify over every q, every plane and every r in the stated range, with **∃ a**, not ∀ a. These match the literal conjecture.

## B. Independent mathematics and certificate

My own `mathcheck.py:3–21` constructs projective points from normalized vectors over F5, dot-product lines and verifies line sizes, unique pair joins and unique line intersections. For F4 it implements multiplication modulo X²+X+1 with XOR addition; it does not incorrectly use integers modulo 4. No discovery or formal Python script was imported or executed.

`mathcheck.py:62–89` writes a fresh encoding with 1,085 variables and 8,312 clauses. Variables are A (initial points), X (round 1 points), I and J (round 1 and round 2 line activation), and 31×31 separator variables B. Unlike the discovery's one-way line activation constraints, my encoding makes each activation **equivalent** to its threshold (lines 70–74). For six points, at least five is equivalent to requiring at least one point in every pair. Conversely every five-subset forces activation. X is exactly A union the I-lines (lines 75–78); every point lies in X or a J-line (line 79). A negative disjunction of all X variables excludes fullness at round 1 (line 80). Inflationarity also excludes fullness at round 0, so fullness at round 2 is exact time 2.

For each v∈A, B_v contains A minus v, excludes v, and is closed: whenever five points of a line are in B_v, so is the sixth (lines 81–86). Closure is enforced unconditionally, while containment and omission are gated by A_v. For v outside A one can choose the empty closed set. Crucially, this is **not** a two-round restriction on deleted configurations. In a genuine minimal percolating set the closure of A minus v is a closed separator omitting v. Otherwise it contains A, and monotonicity would make A minus v percolate. Thus every hypothetical target supplies a satisfying assignment to the unnormalized formula.

Conversely the separators block percolation of every one-point deletion, by closedness and monotonicity; every proper subset lies inside a one-point deletion. Hence this is exact inclusion minimality. Encoding X and I,J exactly makes the time constraints literal. The formula is not a test merely of minimum-cardinality sets.

Symmetry is sound: any set contained in a line remains in that line, since every different line has at most one infected point (<5). Thus a percolating set contains a noncollinear triple. Representatives of such a triple form a basis; inverse basis change sends it to indices 0,25,30. For point transformation M, lines transform by inverse transpose, preserving zero dot products. This is an incidence bijection, which commutes with each synchronous step, preserves least time, and transports proper subsets in both directions. Consequently minimality and percolation are invariant. `mathcheck.py:87` imposes the three normalized initial points without losing any target orbit. This normalization applies to PG(2,5), not arbitrary planes; only this one counterinstance needs it.

CaDiCaL returns UNSAT. My own `checkproof.py` checks every addition from the original CNF using only positive RUP hints. It negates the candidate clause, then requires every hinted input/previous clause to be unit or conflicting; an empty conclusion must occur. No solver output is trusted as a certificate without this check. Deletions are ignored soundly because all retained clauses were original or proved consequences. ## E. Significance and exclusion screen

The (5,5) result refutes the literal permitted existential statement in a genuine projective plane. It is at the allowed **upper boundary**, not an interior parameter as `CANDIDATE.md` informally calls it. The correction is descriptive only: the source explicitly includes that boundary. Source propositions about q-sized thresholds concern maximal nonpercolating sets, a different extremal parameter, and do not themselves supply this time-2 obstruction. Proposition 18 constructs time 3 in a restricted range and cannot be evaluated at (5,5) under its hypotheses. The candidate is therefore not merely the source's published construction run at its next parameter.

I tested the most obvious boundary shortcut. When r=q, an initially full line makes an initial point redundant; thus a minimal set has no full initial line (`Structure.lean:37–58`). In complement language, infection deletes a missing point when a line contains it as its unique missing point; a closed nonfull set has a missing set meeting each incident line in at least two points. These observations explain the separator encoding but do not force time≥3. In fact the verified (4,4) time-2 minimal set disproves any universal claim that the r=q boundary alone forbids two rounds. An odd-order obstruction remains conceivable, but is not a cited theorem or proved short deduction located in this review. No novelty claim is made for that possible stronger family.

Under the supplied precedents, a small SAT-certified counterexample can be substantive. I found no demonstrated known-result, routine-corollary, domain-correction, credited-family variant or source-procedure exclusion here. Gate E passes provisionally on the inspected mathematical evidence. It does not authorize acceptance, settle journal retention, prove absolute novelty, or supply the missing Lean endpoint.
