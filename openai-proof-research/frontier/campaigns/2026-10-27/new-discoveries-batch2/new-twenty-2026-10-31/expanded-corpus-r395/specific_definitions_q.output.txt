1.1    Matchings using elbows
In this subsection, we will deal with orthogonal geometric graphs whose edges are elbows. Let
S be a point set such that no two of its elements lie on the same vertical or horizontal line. We
now prove:

Theorem 1. S always admits a family of bn/4c vertex disjoint mutually crossing elbows. There
are sets of n points that do not contain families of crossing elbows with more than n/3 elements.


Proof. Suppose without loss of generality that S has n = 4m points. Let L be the horizontal
line that divides the plane in two regions each containing 2m elements of S. Sweep a vertical

imply that, in any crossing family of elbows, there is a subset of A, B or C such that all the
elbows of the crossing family have an endpoint in such a subset. Therefore, any crossing family
of elbows contains at most m = n/3 elements.
