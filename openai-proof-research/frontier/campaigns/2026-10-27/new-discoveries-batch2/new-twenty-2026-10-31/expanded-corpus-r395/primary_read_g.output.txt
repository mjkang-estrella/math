     Given an abelian group G, we define the group-annihilator graph to be the graph Γ(G) =
(V (Γ(G)), E(Γ(G))) with vertex set V (Γ(G)) = G and for two distinct a, b ∈ V (Γ(G)), the vertices
a and b are adjacent in Γ(G) if and only if [a : G][b : G]G = {0}, that is, E(G) = {(a, b) ∈ G × G :
[a : G][b : G]G = {0}}.

     By the definition of group-annihilator graph, it appears that the vertex 0 is adjacent to all
vertices of the graph and the structure of Γ(G) is simple, that is, Γ(G) is without self loops and
parallel edges. Furthermore, the eccentricity of 0 (distance between 0 and farthest verrtex from 0)
in Γ(G) is 1, which is in fact the minimum eccentricity of the graph. Thus 0 is the central vertex
of the graph. The maximum eccentricity of Γ(G) is atmost 2, since the distance between any two
