349290 A **Steiner triple system** ${\rm STS}(v)$ is a $2$-$(v,3,1)$ design. A **zero-sum $n$-flow** of a design $\mathcal{D}=(X,\mathcal{B})$ is a map

$$
f:\mathcal{B}\longrightarrow \{\pm1,\ldots,\pm(n-1)\}
$$

such that the sum of the values over all blocks incident with each point is zero. **The zero-sum 3-flow conjecture.** Every ${\rm STS}(v)$ with $v>7$ admits a zero-sum $3$-flow. This conjecture concerns the existence of bounded integer flows on the incidence structures of Steiner triple systems; the source gives no evidence of resolution.
349311 Let $p$ be a prime and let $\alpha$ be a positive integer. Consider the group-annihilator graph realised by the group $\mathbb{Z}/p^{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}$. **Hypoenergeticity conjecture.** For any positive integer $\alpha$, this graph is not hyperenergetic but hypoenergetic. This conjecture extends the preceding result for $\alpha=2$ to all positive integers and concerns the energy of threshold graphs arising from finite abelian groups.
349357 Let $G$ be an essentially $4$-connected planar graph on $n$ vertices, and let $\operatorname{circ}(G)$ denote the length of a longest cycle in $G$. **Circumference conjecture.** Every essentially $4$-connected planar graph on $n$ vertices satisfies

$$
\operatorname{circ}(G)\ge \frac{2}{3}(n+4).
$$

The bound is motivated by constructions of essentially $4$-connected maximal planar graphs attaining equality. The source states that it remains open whether an essentially $4$-connected planar graph can have smaller circumference; the paper proves the bound for essentially $4$-connected maximal planar graphs.
349451 Let $s$ be a positive integer, and let $\Delta$ be an $s$-Cohen–Macaulay complex of dimension $d-1$, with $h$-vector $(h_0,h_1,\ldots,h_d)$. **The stronger $s$-Cohen–Macaulay $M$-vector conjecture.** The vector

$$
(h_0,\ h_1-h_0,\ \ldots,\ h_{\lceil\frac{d+s}{2}\rceil}-h_{\lceil\frac{d+s}{2}\rceil-1})
$$

is an $M$-vector. This would strengthen the usual $g$-theorem-type conclusion for Cohen–Macaulay complexes, but the approach described in the paper gives no clue how to prove the asserted injection or this formulation.
356717 Let $G$ be a graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, let $b$ be the number of blocks of $G$, and let $span_0(G)$ denote the homological span of its degree-$0$ chromatic symmetric homology.

**Span bounds conjecture.** The homological span satisfies

$$
n-b \leq span_0(G) \leq n-1.
$$

The upper bound is proved later in the source, while the lower bound remains part of the conjectural statement from earlier work. Thus the full two-sided conjecture is not resolved by the displayed result.
356728 Let $\beta$ be the parameter, and let $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ and $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ be strict partitions. Let $GQ^{(\beta)}_{\lambda/\!/\mu}$ denote the corresponding skew shifted Grothendieck function, and let $\mathbf{GQ}^+$ and $\mathbf{gp}^+$ be the indicated positive spans of shifted Grothendieck functions.

**Shifted Grothendieck positivity conjecture.** One has

$$
GQ^{(\beta)}_{\lambda/\!/\mu} \in \mathbf{GQ}^+
$$

and

$$
gp^{(\beta)}_{\lambda}gp^{(\beta)}_{\mu} \in \mathbf{gp}^+.
$$

These are conjectural positivity properties analogous to the known positivity of products of the corresponding $GP^{(\beta)}$ and $GQ^{(\beta)}$ functions.
356778 Let 
$$
scr C$ and
$$
scr D$ be infinite classes of finite graphs. An **FO-transduction** interprets graphs using first-order formulas, while an **FOM-transduction** allows first-order formulas with modular counting. **The FO–FOM transduction conjecture.**

$$
\mathscr D \text{ is an FOM-transduction of } \mathscr C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathscr D \text{ is an FO-transduction of } \mathscr C.
$$

The conjecture asks whether modular counting increases the power of first-order transductions on infinite classes of finite graphs. The paper presents this as an unresolved question arising from the fact that it is not clear whether FOM-transductions are more powerful than FO-transductions in general.
356783 Let $N>6$ and consider a configuration of grasshoppers at the vertices of a regular $N$-gon, with legal moves given by the grasshopper-jumping game described in the paper. **Polynomial-move conjecture.** Every regular $N$-gon can be transformed to a similar but larger configuration in polynomially many moves in $N$. The main theorem establishes that a finite sequence of moves always exists, while the conjecture asks for a polynomial bound; the explicit bound obtained from the proof is unreasonably large and may be exponential.
356789 Let $I_H$ be the $H$-graph ideal, and let $\mathfrak{m}_\lambda$ be the corresponding noncommutative monomial symmetric-function element. $$
\mathfrak{m}_\lambda
$$

are diagram positive modulo $I_H$. The source presents this as an equivalent formulation of the preceding monomial-positivity conjecture, and gives no resolution status.
356804 Let $I$ be a monomial ideal, let $\mathcal{F}_L$ be a Lyubeznik resolution, and let Algorithm $1$ be the trimming algorithm defined in the source. The conjecture would explain why the algorithm systematically yields the rank inequality from the preceding conjecture and would extend the constructions known for certain edge ideals; the source gives this as an open direction rather than a proved result.
356831 A **polyomino** is a finite union of edge-connected unit squares in the plane. It is **convex** if every row and every column has at most one connected component. A set of queens is **non-attacking** when no two queens attack each other, and the maximal domination problem asks whether such a set dominates the polyomino.

**Convex-polyomino maximal queen domination conjecture.** The maximal domination problem for non-attacking queens on convex polyominoes is NP-complete.

The corresponding maximal non-attacking rook problem is known to be in P, but the queen problem has not been settled. The paper notes that existing gadgets are non-convex and leaves this conjecture open.
356917 Let $a_1\ge a_2$ and $b_1\ge b_2$ be positive integers satisfying

a_2\ge b_2>1,\qquad a_1\le b_1,\qquad b_1+b_2\ge a_1+a_2.

For graphs $G$ and $H$, let $\rho(G,H)$ denote the density domination exponent. In particular, write $K_{a_1,a_2}$ and $K_{b_1,b_2}$ for complete bipartite graphs.

**The density domination exponent conjecture.**

$$
\rho(K_{a_1,a_2},K_{b_1,b_2})=\max\left\{\frac{b_1b_2}{a_1a_2},\frac{b_1+b_2-1}{a_1+a_2-1}\right\}.
$$

This conjecture gives the missing case in the paper's determination of density domination exponents for complete bipartite graphs. The supplied text does not state whether it has been resolved.
356919 Let $S$ be a set of $n$ points in the plane in general position. An **elbow** is an orthogonal geometric-graph edge consisting of one horizontal and one vertical line segment, and a crossing family of elbows is a family in which every two elbows cross. **Elbow crossing-family conjecture.** There are point sets of $n$ elements that do not admit a crossing family of elbows with more than $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ elements. The proved lower bound is $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$, while the matching upper-bound construction remains conjectural.
356921 Let $S$ be a point set with $3n$ elements in the plane in general position. An **intersecting edge-disjoint triangle family** is a family of edge-disjoint triangles such that every two triangles have intersecting edges. **Intersecting-triangle upper-bound conjecture.** Every such point set has at most $n^2$ intersecting edge-disjoint triangles. The paper proves that $n^2$ triangles can be attained for point sets in convex position, so the conjecture asserts that this construction is extremal; it remains open in general.
356989 Let $G$ be a graph with vertex set $V(G)$, and let $d_i$ be the degree of node $i\in V(G)$. Suppose

d_i\sim X,

where $X$ is a regularly varying random variable. Let $PR_\infty(i)$ denote the infinity-PageRank value of node $i$.

**Scale-free infinity-PageRank conjecture.** If $d_i\sim X$ where $X$ is a regularly varying random variable, then $PR_\infty(i)$ will also be scale-free.

The conjecture concerns the transfer of heavy-tailed, regularly varying degree behavior to infinity-PageRank values. The source motivates it by similarity with known behavior of standard PageRank, but provides no proof or resolution.

**The cycle conjecture.** For any prime $p\equiv 1\bmod 8$ or $p\equiv 3\bmod 8$, the permutation $\phi_p$ is a $p$-cycle.

The conjecture is presented as an earlier conjecture that the paper aims to prove using link relation matrices. The source does not explicitly state the resolution status of this candidate.

\gamma_g(G) \leq \frac{3}{5}n.

The source mentions this as a central conjecture for the ordinary domination game, posed by Kinnersley, West and Zamani; the supplied text gives no resolution status.
368269 Let $\mathscr C$ be a hereditary class of graphs. A **transduction** is a first-order interpretation operation between classes of structures.

**Stable transduction conjecture.** A hereditary class of graphs is stable if and only if it is a transduction of a nowhere dense class of graphs.

The conjecture seeks a normal form for stable hereditary classes in terms of sparse classes. The supplied text says that it has been proposed as a characterization and gives no evidence of resolution.
371533 Let $\mathrm{M}$ be a matroid, let $\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{M})$ be its lattice of flats, and let $\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{M}))$ be the order complex of its proper nonempty flats, also called the Bergman complex. **Athanasiadis–Kalampogia-Evangelinou's conjecture.** The $h$-polynomial of $\Delta(\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{M}))$ has only nonpositive real roots.
