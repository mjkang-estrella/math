## Actual theorem and exact nonmatch

Use H for their graph to distinguish it from our arbitrary base G. H has vertices [n], partitioned into consecutive antichains C_1,...,C_k. Definition 2.2 requires C_1 to be a base and not a summit, C_k a summit and not a base; there are no edges within any C_i. A downward edge exists exactly when its source is in a summit and target in a lower base. Every non-summit vertex has an upward outgoing edge. Upward edges otherwise have flexibility. These restrictions do not describe an arbitrary digraph.

Definition 2.3 assumes 1 <= m < n-1 and that vertices 1,...,m are bases; m lies in C_t. The canonical functions have domain {m+1,...,n-1}; nonfixed function edges must belong to H. Fixed points are allowed only in base/summit blocks at or above C_t, at most one per such block. The starred class excludes fixed points in C_t. Forests have precisely root set [m]. The distinguished vertex n lies in the tree rooted at j. The theorem gives bijections from the unstarred class for j <= N_(t-1) and the starred class for N_(t-1)<j<=m, multiplying weights by q_n t_j. Summing these gives the forest weight polynomial with those prescribed roots.

Weights are W(i,j)=p_i s_j for i<j and q_i t_j for i>=j. They track source and destination separately by ascent/descent, not arbitrary independent weights on all directed edges. The theorem DOES concern rooted forests and fine multivariate weights; dismissing it as merely unrooted trees or threshold graphs would be incorrect. It does NOT state the local quotient identity or conditional joint law at issue.

There is a concrete graph obstruction. Every filtered H has a directed path from every vertex to vertex 1: a summit has a downward edge to 1, while a non-summit can follow strictly upward edges until a summit is reached. Thus two disjoint bidirected K_2 components are not filtered in any ordering. They occur within the required scope as G x K_2 for the edgeless two-vertex base G.

Paying the usual root reduction does not fix this obstruction. For any product graph D, adjoin a sink rho and an edge z->rho of weight t from each vertex z. A forest maps bijectively to a tree toward rho by adding one such edge at each forest root. Deleting rho and its incident edges is the inverse. Original horizontal x_a and vertical y_(u,j) weights are unchanged, so this reduction preserves the desired root and projection statistics exactly. But for the two-component example this augmented digraph is still not filtered under any ordering/filtration. Since all filtered vertices reach vertex 1 and rho is a sink, rho must be vertex 1. Each bidirected pair occupies different blocks; its lower endpoint must be a base and its upper endpoint a summit. Vertex n belongs to one pair and is a summit. The lower endpoint of the other pair is a lower base, so condition 3 forces an edge from n to that endpoint. That edge is absent. This is a symbolic structural argument, not a search or enumeration.

Summing theorem 2.4 over root sets is also unpaid: its root sets are initial base segments in a filtration that must remain valid; arbitrary relabelling need not preserve that structure. The authors explicitly leave arbitrary-root extensions with coarser weights unpursued at the end of section 2. Dense completion and setting weights to zero is not a supplied general solution either: descent weights obey W(i,j)W(i',j')=W(i,j')W(i',j) whenever all four pairs are descents, and analogous ascent identities. Independent horizontal arc markers need not obey these constraints. Local destination variables alone can fit endpoint weights; it is the simultaneous graph, arbitrary horizontal, root and local coefficient requirements that lack transport. No claim is made that every conceivable gadget/cancellation encoding is impossible.
