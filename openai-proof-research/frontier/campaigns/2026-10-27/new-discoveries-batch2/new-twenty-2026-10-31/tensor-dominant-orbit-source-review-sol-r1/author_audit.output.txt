Source personally read: Bremner–Hu, arXiv1206.5179v1, pp1–3 tensor rank and direct-product action, pp4–6 small orbits and Conjecture2. Page6 also visually inspected from the saved PDF through capped rendering. The target is not Conjecture1's full ten-orbit classification. For p≥4 use GL_p(F2)×GL_2(F2)×GL_2(F2), without permuting factors. The first factor alone suffices for transitivity below.

Let V=F2^p, W=F2^2, U=F2^2 with fixed standard bases. Write a tensor T uniquely as
T=sum_(j,k=1,2) a_jk ⊗ e_j ⊗ e_k.
Its flattening A has the four column vectors a_11,a_12,a_21,a_22 in V. Let O_p be the tensors whose four columns are independent.

1. O_p is a single orbit. Any two ordered independent four-frames extend to bases of V. The linear map sending the first completed basis to the second is invertible and sends each of the four columns to its counterpart. Consequently it sends the entire tensor to its counterpart, using the identity in the two GL2 factors. Conversely the rank of this flattening is invariant under the full product action: the GLp factor multiplies on the left and the other two factors induce an invertible linear map on W⊗U, hence on the four-column coordinate space. Thus no tensor outside O_p lies in this orbit.

2. Every T in O_p has tensor rank exactly4. The displayed expression is a sum of four simple tensors; all its first factors are nonzero. Any decomposition into r simple tensors has all four columns in the span of its r first factors. Since A has rank4, r≥4. This proves equality and supplies the source rank bridge, not just matrix rank.

3. In fact no tensor outside O_p has rank4. Identify W⊗U with M_2(F2), with simple tensors the nonzero rank-one matrices. If A has rank≤3, the span H0 of its p horizontal slices is a proper subspace of M2 and is contained in a hyperplane H. A nonzero functional defining H has a nonzero coefficient matrix B. Under invertible row/column operations B has rank-one normal form E11 or rank-two normal form I; applying the corresponding inverse transformations to the slices preserves rank-one matrices. Therefore H is equivalent either to {M:M11=0} or to {M:tr M=0}. The first hyperplane has rank-one basis E12,E21,E22. The second has rank-one basis E12,E21,J, where J is the all-ones matrix. In characteristic2, tr J=0, and these three matrices are independent (the diagonal entries force the J coefficient to vanish). Every slice of T is a linear combination of the chosen three rank-one matrices. Collecting each of their coefficients across the p slices writes T as a sum of at most three simple tensors. Zero first coefficient vectors are omitted. Thus tensor rank≤3. The relation between slice span and flattening rank is ordinary equality of row rank and column rank. This argument covers p<4 as well, so rank4 is impossible there; the source's asymptotic claim begins at p≥4.

4. Exact count and limit. The first column has 2^p−1 choices, the second 2^p−2, the third 2^p−4, and the fourth 2^p−8, because a j-dimensional binary subspace has 2^j elements. Thus
|O_p|=(2^p−1)(2^p−2)(2^p−4)(2^p−8).
There are 2^(4p) tensors. For p≥4,
|O_p|/2^(4p)=product_(j=0)^3 (1−2^(j−p)).
For numbers x_i in [0,1], induction on the number of factors gives 0≤1−product(1−x_i)≤sum x_i: adding a factor changes the defect from D to D+x−Dx≤D+x. Hence
0≤1−|O_p|/2^(4p)≤15·2^(−p),
which tends to0. This is the literal p→∞ statement, with a varying orbit O_p, not a single set in changing ambient spaces. All integer p≥4 are covered; there is no subsequence or computational evidence premise.

Two independently formulated ordinary mechanisms were pursued: direct frame counting above; and the dual obstruction bound. In the latter, rank-deficiency means a nonzero c∈F2^4 has Ac=0. For fixed c, one column with coefficient1 is forced by the remaining three columns, so exactly 2^(3p) tensors satisfy the equation. The union bound over 15 nonzero c gives the same upper bound 15·2^(−p) on the deficient fraction. This is symbolic counting, not finite enumeration or an executed mathematical program. Neither route needs the source's computational orbit tables or Conjecture1.

326578: actual1310.6008v1 Section2.3 defines fast as equality of shortest instruction lengths, not generation by linear instructions. Source Conj1 explicitly has prime q. A linear instruction updates one coordinate by a linear form with nonzero own-coordinate coefficient. Arbitrary instructions are reversible one-coordinate functions. Merely Gaussian-eliminating an invertible matrix does not compare the shortest nonlinear program.

Route A, one-update schedules. Suppose a program updates each used coordinate at most once and its final map is linear. At each update the updated value must already equal its final linear target coordinate, because that register will never be updated again. Before this update, the current registers are linear forms in the initial registers by induction. Their joint map is invertible because the preceding instructions are permutations. The desired output is therefore a linear form in the current registers, and its own-register coefficient is nonzero because the new joint map is invertible. Replace this update by that linear instruction. This proves schedule-preserving linearity for these schedules (identity steps may be omitted). It does not handle shortest programs with repeated registers.

Route B, affine shadow, pays q=2,n≤2 only. Every binary reversible instruction with two registers is x_i←x_i+h(x_j), and every function h:F2→F2 is affine. Thus all intermediates are affine bijections. Replacing each affine instruction by its linear part gives the same final linear map, at no increased length; translations compose separately. This establishes the small case, with n1 immediate, not the all-n conjecture. For n≥3 h can depend nonlinearly on two other bits. Finite-field function identities such as x²=x mean that discarding higher-degree terms is not compatible with composition in the Boolean function algebra.
