## Rank 1: 385263, effective coherence for arbitrary free-by-cyclic groups

Literal endpoint: given a finite set S of elements of a free-by-cyclic G, compute a presentation of <S>. Representation of G and its automorphism and the original AIM input conventions remain source gaps. In particular coherence alone is not an algorithm.

**Mechanism A, height-kernel closure.** Given G=F semidirect_alpha Z and H=<S>, compute the image of height using the gcd of heights and rewrite generators with a height-one (relative to the image) element u. When all heights vanish, Stallings folding solves the subgroup problem in F. Otherwise one might close a finite subgroup K_0 of F under conjugation by u and u^-1, then output K semidirect Z once stabilization is certified. Equality of two finitely generated subgroups of F is decidable by folded core graphs. Thus stabilization is checkable. Termination, however, is false even in a very elementary ambient example.

Let F=F(a,b), alpha(x)=b x b^-1. Identify G with F(a,b) x <z> by t=bz. Take H=<a,t>. Projection to F sends a to a and t to b and restricts to an isomorphism H -> F(a,b): its inverse on free generators is a->a, b->t, so both compositions fix generators. Height on H corresponds to exponent sum in b. Its kernel has free basis b^j a b^-j for j in Z. Here is an ordinary free-reduction proof of independence: combine consecutive powers having the same j in a reduced product in these proposed generators. A nonempty such product becomes b^{j_1} a^{e_1} b^{j_2-j_1} a^{e_2} ... a^{e_s} b^{-j_s}, where all e_i are nonzero and all internal b exponents are nonzero. This cannot freely reduce to the identity. Schreier rewriting of a word of b-exponent zero expresses it in these generators (track its b-height at every occurrence of a). Thus every K_n generated by |j|<=n has rank 2n+1 and K_n is strictly contained in K_{n+1}. Finite closure never terminates, although H itself has the trivial finite presentation <a,t | >. This is an obstruction to the attempted algorithm, not a counterexample to effective coherence.

**Mechanism B, enumeration of finite presentations.** Enumerate relators on S that evaluate to 1 using the ambient word problem. Coherence says the actual kernel is normally finitely generated. It does not provide a stopping certificate that all relations have appeared. Surjectivity of the candidate presented group onto H is immediate; injectivity requires showing every ambient-trivial word follows from finitely many selected relators. Ambient word problem plus enumeration only detects a missing relation relative to a candidate when non-derivability can be decided; this is not supplied uniformly. Dovetailing candidate presentations and valid relator derivations does not certify that universal condition.

**Mechanism C, invariant graph pairs / benign splitting.** Read Andrew–Bering–Kapovich–Vidussi definitions 3.2–3.3, 4.1; Lemma 4.2; Proposition 4.3 and its actual proof; Corollary 4.5 and Proposition 4.6 proofs. At minimal relative rank, a tight invariant pair provides the presentation. Proposition 4.3 explicitly refers existence to Feighn–Handel Proposition 5.4 and correctness to its Main Proposition, Section 6. Those original FH proof bodies were not read in this pass. Minimal relative rank is an infimum over all representing pairs: ordinary tightening with no further decrease is not proved to certify global minimality. The two-generator case distinguishes relative rank 0/1 by an existence argument; it does not supply this general stopping algorithm.

Read Dahmani–Touikan v2 Definition 4.2, Proposition 4.3 and its Nielsen/gcd proof, Theorem 4.4, Proposition 4.5 and its complete short proof, through Proposition 4.6. The algorithm is available for piecewise trivial suspensions globally fibered with finite-rank free fiber. The displayed benign splitting has vertex groups F(v) x Z and slender edge groups with computable coset intersections. It does not apply to arbitrary exponential monodromy by merely naming the ambient group a mapping torus: the natural HNN edge group is a nonabelian free group, not slender. No general benign splitting has been constructed. Known restricted effective coherence earns no credit.

**Remaining decisive lemmas:** a computable certificate that an invariant graph pair realizes minimal relative rank, or a different full-scope presentation/injectivity certificate with provable search termination; input and original workshop scope qualification. No literal Lean endpoint is justified. Formalizing the free-kernel obstruction would be a helper, not a campaign solution.

## Rank 2: 385371, uniformly bounded 2R-cluster stabilizers

The related primary paper supplies an exact version as Problem 5.1: for every 2R-regular Delone set in each d>=4, bound |S_x(2R)| solely by d. Read definitions: a cluster equivalence must take center to center; mere unpointed congruence differs. The original AIM body remains unread, so this is strong primary correspondence but not verified workshop-version identity.

**Mechanism A, short invariant spanning set.** Lemma 3.1 proves full dimension at 2R using the covering condition and a perpendicular empty-ball argument. Proposition 3.2 then bounds a stabilizer via its faithful action on a basis chosen in the 2r-cluster, if that smaller cluster is full-dimensional. The finite kissing-number bound gives a uniform order bound in that case. The hypothesis cannot be discarded: X_L=Z x (L Z)^{d-1}, L>1, is a regular lattice, with r=1/2 and R=sqrt(1+(d-1)L^2)/2. Translations give equivalence of all centered clusters at every radius. Its 2r=1 cluster and its entire unit-step reachable component span only the first coordinate direction. This verifies the old objection on genuine globally 2R-regular examples, not isolated finite clusters. The example does not refute Problem 5.1 (its group is small).

**Mechanism B, propagate rank along short chains.** Read the whole proof of Theorem 4.1. Provided the entire 2r-reachable component is full-dimensional and dr<=R, a plateau in reachable rank before step d propagates using overlap of neighboring reachable sets, contradicting full dimension. Then the d-step reachable set has <=(2d+1)^d points and its spanning basis bounds the group by (2d+1)^{d^2}. Read Theorem 4.2 and its complete proof, plus Remark 4.3: replacing r by ar introduces dependence on a. Taking a=R/r restores full scope but loses the required independence. For X_L above, every fixed a<L still leaves reachable rank one. Thus there can be no universal dimension-only choice of a forcing the hypothesis. A short-chain mechanism must handle lower-rank components and transverse symmetries, rather than assuming them away.

**Mechanism C, quotient induction or rational representation.** Try taking V to be the span of the shortest-step component. One first needs a local recognition/invariance lemma: the whole infinite component is not automatically visible to a symmetry of the finite 2R-cluster. In the short-scale regime where a finite invariant reachable set spans V, its orthogonal complement is invariant too and one can try controlling the restriction to V by a short-chain argument. One would still need a dimension-only bound on the kernel acting on V-perp. Projecting X into V-perp is not proved to be uniformly discrete, nor are congruent centered projected clusters or a covering/minimum-spacing ratio supplied. A quotient may identify many points; the needed Delone/equivalence bridge is missing. Alternatively, a finite subgroup of O(d) need not have bounded order: planar rotations of arbitrary order embed in O(d). To invoke rational finite-group bounds one must construct an invariant Q-form of rank d, not just take the Q-span of arbitrary real cluster vectors. Even regular polygons in a real plane can have Q-span rank >2 (a rotation by 2pi/5 has minimal polynomial of degree 4 over Q). These finite configurations are not Delone counterexamples; they show why real dimension alone does not justify the rational bridge. No global Delone construction realizing unbounded order is supplied.

**Remaining decisive lemmas:** a transverse stabilizer bound after lower-rank shortest-step decomposition, with all projected/global cluster bridges, or a d-dimensional invariant rational structure. The paper's subgroup tower/regularity-radius bounds are different endpoints. They cannot be substituted for an order bound. This route is genuinely held, not claimed solved or publicly excluded.

## Rank 3: 384974, quantum enumeration and its cost model

Read van Apeldoorn–Gribling–Nieuwboer, retained arXiv PDF showing Quantum acceptance 2024-02-27, not just its abstract. Read the oracle model and masking discussion, Theorems 2.4/2.5, Lemma 2.6 proof; Lemma 3.6 and Proposition 3.7 with its algorithm and full correctness proof; Theorem 3.9 and full proof through its final error/cost combination, Corollary 3.10. Internal foundational amplitude-amplification proofs and cited lower-bound bodies were not audited. No lower-bound originality assertion follows.

**Removal strategy.** With k marked inputs in an N-element oracle domain, after j distinct discoveries the masked oracle has k-j solutions. Query cost of m discoveries is bounded by the known sum C sqrt(N) sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(k-j)^(-1/2), ignoring specified amplification constants. For m<=k/2 this is between constants times m sqrt(N/k). Hence it does not offer a new asymptotic saving over m searches at the original density in that regime. For m=k the sum is O(sqrt(Nk)), already printed with the masking construction in the paper. Distinctness and oracle implementation are part of the argument, not an unproved slogan.

**Coupon strategy and intervals.** Proposition 3.7 certifies t distinct indices using uniform successful samples, geometric waiting-time tails and explicit verification of each returned index. Theorem 3.9 samples a subset, splits the original order into intervals, controls the largest remaining marked count by a union bound and uses exact enumeration within intervals. Cauchy–Schwarz sums query costs; the proof separately bounds gate costs using sum k_j^3. Thus this is an actual proof-body obstruction to claiming that the obvious sampling/enumeration improvement is new. Choosing m=k gives the familiar query result; other partial-output regimes retain their own density and gate comparisons.
