## 335147 — complete literal refutation from earlier example, zero credit
Let ζ be a primitive 105th root and α=ζ^35, β=ζ^21, γ=ζ^15. Set
S={α^i β^j:1≤i≤2,1≤j≤4} ∪ {γ^h:1≤h≤6}.
Multiplication identifies μ_105 with μ_3×μ_5×μ_7, by the elementary coprime-order/CRT uniqueness: if α^iβ^jγ^h=1, raising to 35,21,15 respectively forces the three coordinates trivial. Thus these fourteen elements are distinct. Their coordinate support is {(i,j,0):i≠0,j≠0} ∪ {(0,0,h):h≠0}.
Geometric-series identities give sum S=(α+α²)(β+β²+β³+β⁴)+(γ+...+γ⁶)=(-1)(-1)-1=0.
There is no 3-orbit contained in S: its varying first coordinate includes i=0; if its fixed second coordinate is nonzero this element is absent, while if that coordinate is zero only i=0 could occur in S. The same argument swaps the first two coordinates to exclude every 5-orbit. A 7-orbit varies h: if fixed (i,j)=(0,0), its h=0 element is absent; otherwise support at nonzero h is absent. Hence S contains no orbit of ANY prime divisor of 105. A disjoint union of prime orbits representing nonempty S would have a first orbit contained in S, contradiction. Here 2k+1=105 means k=52, within the literal universal claim. This proves its exact negation, not merely failure of a weight formula or of a restricted prime-power case.

Choosing 3,5,7 is exactly the S above. No extrapolation from their weight-semigroup theorem is needed. Our CRT proof above supplies the complete distinct-subset/no-orbit bridge even without invoking their minimality proof. ## 330333 — general reducedness helper; Buchberger gap remains

Write L(M) for the set of loop vertices. Lemma 3.2 gives f_M=det L(T[L(M)],X) and leading monomial x_L. Independently, since T[L] is a forest, a nonzero determinant permutation consists of fixed points and disjoint adjacent transpositions: any longer cycle would give a cycle in the forest. Hence
f_L=Σ_matching F in T[L] (-1)^|F| x_{L\V(F)}.
The empty matching gives the unique highest-degree monomial x_L with coefficient 1, and every other monomial is supported inside L. For fixed size j, distinct minimal loop sets form an antichain: L'⊊L would itself violate minimality of the j-matching with loop set L. Equal loop sets yield identical polynomials, identified in the set B_j. Therefore no monomial of one f_L is divisible by another's leading monomial. Monicity and the irreducibility condition in the source definition of reducedness hold for every j. This does NOT prove B_j is a Gröbner basis.

Attempt 1: generation alone does not control initial ideals, so Theorem 3.7 is insufficient. Attempt 2: for two supports L,K the S-polynomial is x_{K\L}f_L−x_{L\K}f_K. Disjoint L,K are covered by the coprime-leading-term product criterion; equal L,K cancel. For intersecting unequal supports, the determinant matching expansions cancel their top terms but give mixed residual supports. Minimality does not by itself show each residual leading support contains an allowed minimal j-loop set. Attempt 3: use leaf determinant recurrence f_L=x_v f_{L\v}−f_{L\{v,u}} (when v is a leaf with neighbor u). The two terms change loop counts; preserving a size-j 2-matching requires simultaneously rerouting the nonloop paths. A simple deletion may lose size j, so the induction does not close. Proposition 4.4 would transport a legitimate reduction through arbitrary size-j matchings, but does not construct it. The source n−1 proof reduces to two individual paths and their connected intersection; at general j each complement can have several paths and the same single-path construction is not established.
