## Independent ordinary proof

Let V={1,2,3,4,5,6}, A={1,3,5}, B={2,4,6}. Define G by adjacency between every A vertex and every B vertex, and no other adjacency. Define H by the cyclic order 1,2,3,4,5,6,1. Set f=id_V. These are finite simple undirected connected graphs on literally the same set. Opposite G parts are joined directly, and same-part vertices have a path of length two through the other part. H has paths supplied by the cycle. The identity is bijective. No equality G=H is a target hypothesis: in fact their degrees are respectively three and two. Both are regular and 2-connected separately, so adding either of those isolated conditions would not repair the claim.

Put r=(1 3 5)(2 4 6), alpha=(2 4 6). Thus alpha fixes A. It preserves G adjacency in both directions because it preserves each part. The permutation r is a two-step rotation of the displayed cycle, hence preserves H adjacency in both directions; id also does. Alpha is not an H automorphism: edge {1,2} maps to nonedge {1,4}.

Define beta_g=r for g in A and beta_g=id for g in B, and gamma(g,h)=(alpha(g),beta_g(h)). An explicit inverse sends (u,v) to (alpha^{-1}(u),beta_{alpha^{-1}(u)}^{-1}(v)). Since alpha preserves both parts, the latter beta can also be written beta_u. This proves bijectivity without relying on the source's compressed injectivity argument.

Inner edges map to inner edges because each beta_g is an H automorphism. Every connecting edge has the form {(a,b),(b,a)} with a in A,b in B. Its image is {(a,r(b)),(r(b),a)}, the connecting edge indexed by a and r(b). Conversely each such connecting edge has the unique preimage indexed by a and r^{-1}(b). On inner edges the inverse uses the inverse H automorphism. Thus both edge classes map onto themselves: this is adjacency iff, not merely a one-sided edge-preserving bijection argument. Gamma is in tilde A.

Independently, Proposition3.5's exact neighborhood condition is met. If g in A, N_G(g)=B, on which beta_g=r=alpha. If g in B, N_G(g)=A, on which beta_g=id=alpha. This confirms the edge calculation using the actual primary criterion.

For a general bijective f the source diagonal is d_delta(g,h)=(delta(g),f delta f^{-1}(h)), with delta in Aut(G), retained only if it is a product-graph automorphism. Here f=id, so it is (delta(g),delta(h)). Preservation of inner edges forces delta in Aut(H), since there is a whole induced copy of H in each fiber. Conversely delta in Aut(G) intersect Aut(H) yields an admissible diagonal: it preserves inner edges and maps the connecting edge for gg' to that for delta(g)delta(g'). Consequently bar A is exactly this common-automorphism diagonal subgroup. Closure and inversion follow either by diagonal multiplication or by closure of that intersection.

Every generator Phi(g,beta) of hat B fixes the first coordinate. The identity does too, and first-coordinate-fixing maps are closed under composition and inversion. Therefore EVERY element of the subgroup generated by these generators fixes the first coordinate; this is not an assertion confined to generators. Any product d_delta b, or b d_delta, has first-coordinate permutation delta. If gamma were such a product, delta=alpha. This contradicts alpha not in Aut(H). Hence gamma is outside bar A hat B, proving that this product is a proper subset of tilde A. The source's internal semidirect assertion necessarily implies equality of this product set, so the instance refutes its universal conjecture. No classification of the entire automorphism group is needed.

Here there is also a second check: hat B={1}. For g in A its stabilizer fixes B pointwise. In C6, 1 is the unique common neighbor of 2 and 6; 3 of 2 and 4; 5 of 4 and 6. Thus it fixes A too. For g in B the reverse argument uses 2 as the unique common neighbor of 1 and 3, 4 of 3 and 5, and 6 of 5 and 1. Every allowed beta is identity, every local generator is identity, and their generated group is trivial. Gamma still is not diagonal, e.g. its second-coordinate action at h=1 is 3 in A-fibers and 1 in B-fibers. This corroborates the projection obstruction without replacing it.
