The search turned up one paper that states the conjecture and a companion paper that appears to settle the bipartite case.

- **The conjecture:** In "Extremal Restraints for Graph Colourings" (arXiv 1611.08920), the authors observe that for complete graphs and trees, the simple restraints that maximize the restrained chromatic polynomial are minimal colourings. This does not always hold in general. They propose Conjecture 3.1: for a bipartite graph and large enough x, the restrained chromatic polynomial under any standard simple restraint is at most that under the alternating restraint. They say they verified that the conjecture above is correct for all such graphs of order at most 6.

- **Resolution for bipartite graphs:** "Restraints Permitting the Largest Number of Colourings" (Brown and Erey, arXiv 1611.09536) takes up the same question. Its abstract says it determines such extremal restraints for all bipartite graphs. The paper gives two necessary conditions for a restraint to maximize the count for any graph, and states that these conditions are sufficient for bipartite graphs. I did not confirm from the snippets that this result is explicitly labelled as a proof of Conjecture 3.1, so that link should be checked in the full text.

- **Open question:** The first paper also asks whether, for almost all graphs, the maximizing simple restraint is a minimal colouring. The snippets do not show whether this has been answered.
