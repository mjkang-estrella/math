The search turned up the main paper on this topic, but nothing that directly confirms a typical orbit size of m+n+1 or a noncrossing matching rotation result.

- **Main paper:** Lafrenière, Lewis, McNicholas, Striker, and Welch (arXiv 2505.04000) describe orbits of interval-closed set rowmotion on [2] × [n]. They prove a homomesy conjecture from an earlier Elder et al. paper, involving the signed cardinality statistic. They completely describe the orbits of interval-closed set rowmotion on products of two chains [2] × [n]. For [2] × [n] specifically, the orbits are of sizes dividing n+3 or n+5, with the exception of a single orbit of size 2 and the quadratic orbits described in Theorem 3.26 and Table 4. That is a statement about [2] × [n], not the general m+n+1 pattern in your query.
- **Original definition:** The interval-closed set framework comes from arXiv 2307.08520, which studies interval-closed sets of product of chains posets, proving further results about enumeration and homomesy.
- **Related rank-based results:** Patrias and Pechenik-style work on order-ideal rowmotion reports orbit sizes tied to rank plus a constant. One paper notes that for every graded poset Q, the ψ-orbit of the empty order ideal has size rank(Q) + 2. It also describes NRP ('not relatively prime') rowmotion for the product P = a × b of two chains, which concerns orbit sizes relative to rank plus c plus 1. This is for ordinary order ideals, not interval-closed sets, so it is only a loose analogy.
- **Not found:** Nothing in these results mentions a noncrossing matching rotation, and I found no source confirming an m+n+1 orbit-size conjecture for interval-closed sets of [m] × [n]. The 2505.04000 paper may address this in its later sections, so the full text is the place to check.
