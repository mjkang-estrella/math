  We now give precise definitions of the objects and actions in the title of the paper and discuss relevant
results of the prior paper [11]. We also state analogous dynamical results for order ideals, for the sake of
comparison.
  Let P be a partially ordered set (poset). All posets in this paper are finite. A chain of a poset P is a
totally ordered subset of P . An n-element chain poset has elements 1 < 2 < · · · < n and is denoted as [n].
The Cartesian product of two chains is given as [m] × [n] = {(i, j) | 1 ≤ i ≤ m, 1 ≤ j ≤ n} with partial order
(a, b) ≤ (c, d) if and only if a ≤ c and b ≤ d. An order ideal of P is a downward-closed subset, and an order
filter of P is an upward-closed subset. The set of order ideals of P is denoted J(P ). A linear extension of P
is a total order that respects (or extends) the partial order P . See [19, Ch. 3] for details and other standard
poset terminology.
Definition 1.4. Let P be a poset and I a subset of P . We say that I is an interval-closed set (ICS) of P
if for all x, y ∈ I and z ∈ P such that x ≤ z ≤ y, then z ∈ I. Let IC(P ) denote the set of all interval-closed
sets of P .
  The following definition contains notation that will be used in Section 2; see [11, Examples 2.2 and 2.15]
for examples.
Definition 1.5. Given I ∈ IC(P ), let ∆(I) denote the smallest order ideal (downward-closed set) containing
I and ∇(I) denote the smallest order filter (upward-closed set) containing I. Denote the minimal elements
of I as Min(I) and the maximal elements of I as Max(I). Let Inc(I) be the set of elements in P that are
incomparable with I and IncI (J) be the restriction of Inc(J) to the subset I. The ceiling of I, denoted Ceil(I),
is the set of minimal elements of the order filter of I, not including I. That is, Ceil(I) = Min(∇(I) − I).
