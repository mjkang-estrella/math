 Abstract
 We refute Conjecture 1 of Gurvich and Naumova in the game and with the payoff stated in arXiv:2311.03257v1. For
 every number n ≥ 3 of piles and every number l ≥ 3 of players, we give a positive initial position at which all-GM
 play makes the first player lose, whereas changing only that player's first move makes that player win. A
 block-invariant argument gives both exact lengths. An explicit finite witness is covered by the Lean endpoint
 SlowNim360653.conjecture1_false. Separate finite computations test the family, give a certificate independent of
 the no-multiple convention, and perform bounded best-response searches. MathDB 360653 is the target; 361128
 duplicates the conjecture. The DAM version of record of the sibling paper is unread, and the prior-art screen is
 bounded.

 1. The source game and the claim
 Exact slow NIM(n,k) has n nonnegative integer piles; a move reduces exactly k positive piles by one each. Here k =
 n − 1 and n ≥ 2. Players P0,…,Pl-1 move cyclically, with P0 first and l ≥ 2. A legal move keeps one coordinate
 unchanged and reduces every other coordinate by one; each reduced coordinate must have been positive. A position
 is terminal exactly when at least two coordinates are zero. The player due to move there loses; the other players win.
 Length L counts moves, not positions.

 Fix an initial position x0. A constant C is admissible if C exceeds the length of every legal play from x0. The loser
 receives −(C − L); each winner receives (C − L)/(l − 1). Thus a faster win is preferable to a slower win, and a slower
 loss is preferable to a faster loss. These are the source's monetary payoffs, not merely binary win/loss utilities.

 The GM rule keeps the smallest coordinate value divisible by l, choosing the largest index attaining that value. This
 is the pivot. If no coordinate is divisible by l, it keeps a largest entry, with the rightmost tie convention. All other
 entries decrease by one. At a nonterminal state with a zero, that zero is unique and is the pivot, so GM is legal. If
 every entry is positive, every kept-index choice is legal.

 Conjecture 1 reads verbatim: “The set of ℓ GM-strategies form a (uniform) Nash equilibrium.” [GN] The source

 2. A family for every n,l ≥ 3
 Theorem. Let n,l ≥ 3, put a = (n − 2)l + 1 and b = (n − 1)l − 1, and start from x0 = (a,…,a,b), with n − 1 copies of a.
 All-GM play has length T = (n − 1)l. If P0 instead keeps an a on the first move and uses GM thereafter, while every
 other player uses GM, the length is T − 1. Thus the respective losers are P0 and Pl-1, and the GM profile is not Nash
 for every admissible C.

 Proof. We use multisets only to describe values. Keeping either of two equal entries produces the same successor
 multiset. The least divisible value and the largest fallback value depend only on this multiset, so a rightmost tie
 cannot affect the value argument. This justifies the following calculation for the literal coordinate rule, without
 imposing an additional tie-breaking hypothesis.

 Initially b − a = l − 2 > 0. The entries have residues 1 and −1 modulo l, neither zero. GM therefore keeps b. Set ti = 1
 + (i − 1)l and qi = (n − 1 − i)l. At time ti the all-GM invariant is

 Ii = {qi repeated n − i times; qi + l repeated i − 1 times; qi + l − 1}, 1 ≤ i ≤ n − 1.

 The first move gives I1. Suppose i ≤ n − 2, write q = qi, and select a q-valued pile as a representative pivot. Here q ≥
 l. If that representative is kept for s moves, the other values are q − s (n − i − 1 copies), q + l − s (i − 1 copies), and q
 + l − 1 − s. For 1 ≤ s < l, none of these is divisible by l except the last value at s = l − 1, when it equals q. Hence GM
 keeps value q throughout the block. A possible change of representative at that equality has exactly the same
 multiset effect.

 Before each of the l reductions, the smallest potentially reduced entry is at least q − l + 1 ≥ 1. Thus every move is
 legal; no intermediate state is terminal. After l moves the values are q − l repeated n − i − 1 times, q repeated i times,
 and q − 1. These are precisely I . This proves the invariant by induction, including blocks with zero multiplicity in

 1 + (n − 2)l + (l − 1) = (n − 1)l = T.

 3. The deviating blocks and a literal witness
 Keep an a at the first move. The resulting multiset is {(n − 2)l repeated n − 2 times; (n − 2)l + 1; (n − 1)l − 2}. At the
 same times ti, the second invariant is

 Ji = {qi repeated n − 1 − i times; qi + 1; qi + l − 2; qi + l repeated i − 1 times}.

 This holds initially. For i ≤ n − 2 there is at least one fresh q-valued pile and q ≥ l. Keep such a representative. At s
 moves into a block, the two distinguished piles would have values q + 1 − s and q + l − 2 − s. For 1 ≤ s < l, the
 former is divisible only at s = 1 and the latter only at s = l − 2, in each case equalling q. When l = 3 these events
 coincide, so both piles tie the pivot simultaneously. They still have the same value q. Other fresh and previously kept
 piles have values q − s and q + l − s, respectively, and are not divisible within the block.

 It follows inductively that GM always keeps value q. Every tie can be represented as continuing to keep the original
 representative, since the successor multiset agrees. The positivity bound q − l + 1 ≥ 1 applies to all reduced entries
 before reduction. At the end of l moves the values are q − l repeated n − 2 − i times, q − l + 1, q − 2, and q repeated i
 times. This is Ji+1, proving the second invariant.

 The last boundary is J = {1; l − 2; l repeated n − 2 times}. GM keeps an l. One move yields {0; l − 3; l − 1

 exactly l − 3 more moves until the second distinguished entry vanishes; no other entry vanishes earlier. The length is

 1 + (n − 2)l + 1 + (l − 3) = (n − 1)l − 1 = T − 1.

 The dossier freshly executes isolated Python with exact rational rewards under resource caps. Computations are
 supplementary evidence; the preceding family proof is not an extrapolation from a scan. Sorted states are used only
 for finite searches: equal-valued choices have identical successor multisets, and any chosen value can be
 implemented by choosing a coordinate of that value. A separate unsorted trace check confirms the literal witness
 above.

 The robust certificate starts from (5,5,6), with n = l = 3. Its mass is 16, so every play has length at most 8 and C = 9
 is admissible. All-GM play has length 8 and loser P2; every nonterminal position on this baseline has a multiple of 3.
 Thus the baseline is independent of any completion of the no-multiple rule, and P0's payoff is 1/2.

 Here is the minimax specification used for the certificate. At a terminal state (x,t), assign reward −(9 − t) if t mod 3 =
 0, otherwise (9 − t)/2. At P0's turn maximize over all legal successors. At an opponent turn with a divisible entry use
 its GM successor. At any other opponent turn minimize over all legal successors. Strict mass decrease makes this a
 finite acyclic recursion. It grants the opponents more freedom than a single fixed completion, including dependence
 on the entire play.

 Fresh evaluation returns value 1. Extract one maximizing P0 action at each reached P0 state, retain every adversarial
 opponent branch, and traverse the whole resulting policy tree. Every leaf has (length,loser) = (7,1). Thus one policy

 5. What the formal certificate proves
 The reused archive proves the literal negation, not the full parameter family. In namespace SlowNim360653, Pos n
 is Fin n → Nat, with no sorting abstraction. Terminal means two distinct zero coordinates. Legal x i requires every
 coordinate other than i to be positive, and move keeps i and subtracts one elsewhere. Although natural subtraction is
 truncated, Legal prevents an illegal zero reduction. Strategy consists of a positional choice function and a proof of
 legality at every nonterminal position.

 The finite sets multiples, pivotValue and pivots define gm by the minimum divisible value, or the maximum value
 when there is no multiple, followed by maximum index. The lemmas gm_minimum, gm_rightmost and gm_fallback
 identify these rules; gm_legal makes GM a valid Strategy. GMProfile assigns it to every player. Thus legality is part
 of the formal strategy type, rather than a premise omitted from evaluation.

 The total run is defined with Nat.rec on fuel. A terminal state contributes zero further moves; otherwise the player at
 t mod l chooses and the recursor continues at t + 1. The mass-decrease theorem gives run_bound. The theorem
 run_independent identifies any two fuel values strictly exceeding the initial mass, and no_exhaustion certifies
 termination before fuel can run out. The function length uses fuel mass + 1 and initial time zero. Its value is therefore
 the legal play length, not an unsupported truncation. The terminal loser is length mod l. The rational payoff is
 precisely the source transfer, and Admissible quantifies over every legal profile at the same initial state.

 IsNash says that no player can increase its payoff by replacing its own strategy with any other valid positional
 strategy. This suffices for refutation of the unrestricted claim: positional deviations are also history-dependent
 deviations. Conversely, for admissibility, every legal play can be implemented by a positional profile, because mass
 strictly decreases and a position cannot recur. Specify the chosen move at each state on that play and use legal GM
 elsewhere. Hence the formal all-profile length bound covers every legal play, even if one initially permits history
 dependence.

 Conjecture quantifies over n ≥ 2, l ≥ 2, all initial states and every admissible rational C. Restricting C to rationals
 does not weaken the refutation of a claim allowing real constants, since the witness uses the integer 7. The endpoint
 is

 The literal_bridge certifies the two actual coordinate plays from (4,4,5), their lengths and payoffs; seven_admissible
 derives admissibility from length_bound. The control two_every_profile_nash concerns n = 2, not l = 2: every legal

 6. Context, provenance and limits
 [GN] studies GM dynamics and explicitly states the multiplayer conjecture. [SN] develops the related screw-system
 viewpoint and repeats the same conjecture in its arXiv text. These are one mathematical target, represented by
 MathDB 360653 and duplicate 361128; they are not two independently credited results. The elementary deviation
 analysis compares unilateral alternatives, whereas the source's dynamical formulas describe GM trajectories alone.
 The family changes a loss to a win for all n,l ≥ 3, so it does not rest on a marginal preference between two wins. Its
 significance is limited to resolving this precise proposed equilibrium property, not to a general classification of
 multiplayer impartial games.

 [GMMV] gives the two-player remoteness result: the M-rule wins as quickly as possible from winning positions and
 resists as long as possible from losing ones. With an admissible C, this is exactly the two-player preference ordering
 induced by the source payoff. The later Games paper [GMMN] addresses normal and misère remoteness; the
 Modern Mathematical Methods paper [GMN] treats other exact slow NIM parameters. The available source or
 abstract evidence concerns two-player play. [BGMV] concerns remoteness for Moore, Wythoff and Euclid games.
 Those results give context, not a multiplayer equilibrium proof.

 The frozen evidence includes primary TeX for both conjecture papers and [GMMV], exact raw MathDB lines,

 conjecture is located in the multiplayer subsection of [GN], original TeX lines 1106–1151, with its verbatim
 conjecture at lines 1139–1142; [SN] repeats it at lines 1777–1780. Hash manifests bind the original inputs before
