                                                   Abstract. Let P be a graph property. A P-coloring with at most k colors
                                                   is a coloring of the vertices of a simple graph G such that each color class
                                                   induces a graph in P. Harary polynomials are generalizations of the chromatic
                                                   polynomial for simple graphs based on conditional colorings. We denote by
                                                   χP (G; k) the number of P-colorings of G with at most k colors. χP (G; k) is a
                                                   polynomial in Z[k]. A first paper studying Harary polynomials systematically
                                                   was published in 2021 by O.Herscovici, J.A. Makowsky and V. Rakita. [28]. It
                                                   studies under which conditions on P is χP (G; k) definable in Monadic Second
                                                   Order Logic and under which conditions is χP (G; k) a chromatic invariant.
                                                       Let P, Q be two graph properties. Two graphs G, H are P-mates if
                                                   χP (G; k) = χP (H; k). χQ is at least as distinctive as χP , χP ≤ χQ , if for all
                                                   graphs G, H we have that χQ (G; k) = χQ (H; k) implies χP (G; k) = χP (H; k).
                                                   In this paper we study under which conditions on P are there any (many)
                                                   P-mates and under which conditions on P, Q is χQ is at least as distinctive
                                                   as χP .

2.1. Graph parameters. Recall that [n] = {1, 2, . . . , n}. We denote by G the
class of all simple finite graphs (V (G), E(G)). To make it into a set we assume
that V (G) = [n] for some n ∈ N+ . G(n) is the family of graphs of order n with
V (G) = [n].
    A graph parameter F is a function mapping finite graphs G into a domain D which
is invariant under graph isomorphism. If D = {0, 1} we call F a graph property and
denote it usually by P, Q possibly indexed by some suggestive letter. If D is a set
of numbers like N, Z, Q, R or C we speak of F as a numeric graph parameters. If
D is a polynomial ring in a fixed finite number of indeterminates x = (x1 , . . . , xk )
like N[x], Z[x], Q[x], R[x] or C[x] we speak of F as a graph polynomials. If F is a
graph polynomial with x = x we speak of univariate graph polynomials. Typical
examples are:
      Graph properties: The class of connected graphs, planar graphs, edge-less
        graphs, regular graphs, etc.
      Numeric graph parameters: The number of vertices n(G), the number
        of edges m(G), the number of connected components k(G), the maximal
        degree, the chromatic number χ(G), etc.
      Univariate graph polynomials: The chromatic polynomial χ(G; k), the
        matching polynomial, the independence polynomial IND(G; x),
        the characteristic polynomial char(G; x) are all univariate graph polynomials.
      Multivariate graph polynomials: The Tutte polynomial T (G; x, y), the
        edge elimination polynomial ξ(G; x, y, z)
