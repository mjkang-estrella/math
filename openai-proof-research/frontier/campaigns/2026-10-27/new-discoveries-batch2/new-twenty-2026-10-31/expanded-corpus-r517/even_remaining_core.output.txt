3    General Observations
Given an edge-colouring c : E(Kn ) → p(c) of Kn , a weakening c̃ of c is an edge-colouring of
Kn obtained by post-composing c with a function f , namely c̃ = f ◦ c. Conversely, we call c a

                                                     8
strengthening of c̃. When f is injective, the weakening c̃ = f ◦ c gives an equivalent colouring to c.
Otherwise, the effect of post-composing by f amounts to identifying colours k1 , k2 ∈ p(c) whenever
f (k1 ) = f (k2 ).
We make the following simple but important observation regarding the monotonicity of even-
chromaticity and unique-chromaticity with respect to the weakening of an edge-colouring.
Observation 3.1. Let c, c̃ be edge-colourings of Kn , where c̃ is a weakening of c. A copy of a graph
H in Kn that is unique-chromatic with respect to c̃ must also be unique-chromatic with respect
to c. Likewise, a copy of a graph H in Kn that is even-chromatic with respect to c must also be
even-chromatic with respect to c̃.
The key example of weakenings we consider is that of the amalgamation c ⊗ d into its components.
Observation 3.2. Let c ⊗ d be the amalgamation of edge-colourings c and d (as defined in Section
2). Then the components (c ⊗ d)1 , (c ⊗ d)2 , (c ⊗ d)3 , (c ⊗ d)4 of c ⊗ d are weakenings of c ⊗ d.
Consider an amalgamation c ⊗ d of a Kt -unique (resp. Kt -odd) edge-colouring c and any auxiliary
edge-colouring d. Let S be a copy of Kt in Knm which is not unique-chromatic (resp. is even-
chromatic) with respect to c ⊗ d. The memory of the edge-colouring c via (c ⊗ d)1 , combined with
the special colourings of ‘vertical’ edges via (c ⊗ d)4 , forces the existence of a ‘rectangular’ structure
within S when viewed as a subset of the grid [n] × [m] = V (Knm ).
Lemma 3.3. Let c be a Kt -unique ( resp. Kt -odd) edge-colouring of Kn and let d be any edge-
colouring of Km . Let S be a copy of Kt in Knm (on vertex set [n] × [m]) which is not unique-
chromatic ( resp. is even-chromatic) with respect to the amalgamation c ⊗ d. We have
  a) S must contain two vertices (x, y1 ), (x, y2 ) sharing the first coordinate.
  b) For any two vertices (x, y1 ), (x, y2 ) in S sharing the first coordinate, S must contain the vertices
     (x′ , y1 ), (x′ , y2 ) for some x′ ̸= x.
  c) In particular, S must contain four vertices (x, y1 ), (x, y2 ), (x′ , y1 ), (x′ , y2 ) forming a ‘rectangle’
     in the [n] × [m] grid.

Proof. By Observation 3.1 and 3.2, S is not unique-chromatic (resp. is even-chromatic) with respect
to any of (c ⊗ d)1 , (c ⊗ d)2 , (c ⊗ d)3 , (c ⊗ d)4 .
  a) Let S = {(x̃1 , ỹ1 ), · · · , (x̃t , ỹt )}. For the sake of contradiction, let the first coordinates
     x̃1 , · · · , x̃t be pairwise distinct. By our assumption that c is a Kt -unique (resp. Kt -odd)
     edge-colouring of Kn , the copy {x̃1 , · · · , x̃t } of Kt in Kn is unique-chromatic (resp. is not
     even-chromatic) with respect to c. Hence the copy S of Kt in Knm is unique-chromatic
     (resp. is not even-chromatic) with respect to (c ⊗ d)1 , a contradiction.
  b) We have (c ⊗ d)4 ((x, y1 ), (x, y2 )) = {y1 , y2 }. As S is not unique-chromatic (resp. is even-
     chromatic) with respect to (c ⊗ d)4 , there must be another edge in E(S) receiving the colour
     {y1 , y2 } under the edge-colouring (c ⊗ d)4 . This concludes the proof as all edges in Knm
     receiving the colour {y1 , y2 } under the edge-colouring (c ⊗ d)4 are of the form (x′ , y1 )(x′ , y2 )
     for some x′ ∈ [n].
  c) Immediate given (a), (b).

The proof of Lemma 3.3 captures our general strategy. Given a copy S of Kt in Knm which is not
unique-chromatic (resp. is even-chromatic), we iteratively build up more structure in S as a subset
of the grid [n] × [m] = V (Knm ), from which we then derive contradictions.
