## 372265: three full-scope routes pursued, none closes

Actual Makowsky 2512.22556v1 Definition 1.1 admits arbitrary isomorphism-invariant graph properties, not only hereditary or decidable properties. Conjecture 3 is exactly nonexistence of an almost-complete Harary polynomial for G(n,1/2). The random graph quantifier concerns uniqueness among all nonisomorphic graphs, not merely separating a prescribed finite list. The paper also states the opposed BPR conjecture for the chromatic polynomial. No repaired typography, partition-versus-colouring normalization or finite exceptional mate is offered as a result.

Route A: propagate small mates. For additive/component-closed P, disjoint union can propagate existing mates. This gives infinitely many mates but does not establish failure of almost completeness: graphs with a fixed separate component occur with probability tending to zero in G(n,1/2). For example, for any fixed nonempty vertex subset size a, probability of no crossing edges is 2^{-a(n-a)}; union over binom(n,a) such subsets still tends to zero. Even knowing infinitely many pairs therefore leaves the density-one uniqueness question entirely unpaid. Arbitrary P has no component factorization at all. Need a mate-producing operation on a positive fraction of dense connected random graphs which preserves EVERY partition coefficient.

Route B: random/adversarial property encoding to refute the conjecture. Write h_j^P(G) for the number of partitions into j allowed induced blocks. A random P assigns one bit per graph isomorphism type, so all coefficients are correlated polynomials in these bits. At large block orders a random G usually has many distinct induced types: this could supply randomness for a fingerprint. But h_1 is only one bit, and h_{j} aggregates many partitions; distinct induced types do not imply independent h_j or anti-concentration of equality between two graphs. Worse P must be ONE fixed property for every n, not a new property selected independently for each order of host graph. A diagonal choice at order n changes h_j at all larger orders. Needed exact lemma: existence of a single property P with Pr_G[exists nonisomorphic H: all h_j^P(G)=h_j^P(H)] ->0. No pair-collision bound or compatible diagonal construction proved. Not a full candidate.

Route C: counting all coefficient vectors to prove the conjecture. Always 0<=h_j<=S(n,j), hence at most product_j(S(n,j)+1) vectors. The naive logarithmic bound is O(n^2 log n), larger than the Theta(n^2) entropy of random graphs; it does not force collisions. Even a sub-exponential number of vectors within a restricted P family would not cover arbitrary P. The chromatic case alone cannot be settled by importing known weak distinguishability of the independence polynomial: those polynomials count different data. Need a genuinely sharper joint coefficient entropy theorem or universal mate operation.

Section8.13's displayed cycle-partition argument is not relied upon as a universal result: it concerns a special property and mixes fixed small blocks with large-subset estimates, so it cannot pay the arbitrary-P endpoint. ## 364944: exact dichotomy versus an unpaid general construction

Actual Yip 2409.16778v2 Definition1.6 and Conjecture1.8 agree with the literal. Definition uses nested sets, not the corpus's backslash typography. An even-chromatic copy is a (not necessarily induced) copy whose every colour count is even. Source Theorem1.7 attributes the already-known polynomial lower bound for even-decomposable H; that direction is not new. The exact converse is much stronger than K8 or any fixed clique.

Route A: parity peeling. An independent set I can be deleted while keeping even edge parity exactly when sum_{v in I} deg(v) is even. Therefore every even-degree vertex is a legal singleton step, and any two nonadjacent odd-degree vertices are a legal two-vertex step. If neither step exists and H is nonempty, all degrees are odd and all vertices are pairwise adjacent, so H is a complete graph of even order. Its even edge count forces its order divisible by four. This establishes that restricted greedy peeling can end only at an empty graph or a clique K_{4a}; it DOES NOT establish that ending at a clique certifies non-decomposability, because earlier choices may have been wrong and larger independent sets are allowed. Nor does it transform a colouring avoiding that residual clique into an H-odd colouring: the deleted cut can change each colour's parity, not merely the total parity. Needed lifting invariant is per-colour, not scalar edge parity.

Route B: extend source amalgamation. Read Definition2.2, Lemmas2.3–2.5, Observations3.1–3.2, Lemma3.3 and relevant proofs of Propositions2.6–2.8. Formula r_P(nm)<=(2r_Q(m)+1)r_P(n)+binom(m,2) yields subpolynomial growth only AFTER proving closure of P under amalgamation with a strictly easier Q. The source's K8 proof removes a rectangle of four vertices, then uses all cross pairs to cancel colour parity in the first coordinate, and uses K4-uniqueness on projections. For an arbitrary H the four rectangle positions need not contain all six edges, and external vertices need not connect in paired fashion. Thus both rectangle production and parity cancellation fail. No universal Q_H with subpolynomial colour count and this closure property was proved. Changing the scalar recurrence exponent would leave precisely this missing combinatorial closure untouched.

Route C: try a finite obstruction to the equivalence. A non-decomposable H containing a polynomial-forcing subgraph F is not enough: an even-chromatic copy of H need not have an even-chromatic F (colours can cancel between F and its complement). Conversely avoiding F-even-chromatic copies need not avoid H. Refining colours preserves oddness for a fixed graph, but multiplying finitely many known clique colourings does not make nonclique copies contain the required clique. No concrete H with a proof of polynomial r_H outside the even-decomposable class was obtained. Random-graph prevalence of even decomposability is a different theorem and is not a solution of the universal dichotomy. Search specifically found this tempting but irrelevant resolution; no API URL in those search results was fetched.

## 370089: a third sustained route, exact load inequality unpaid

Read Fox–Luo 2509.01766v2 Conjecture1.3, known bounds Theorems1.4–1.5, affine-plane upper construction, multipartite Theorem2.2/Proposition2.3 and selected random proof context. The random theorem has denominator r^2-r+5/4; taking p=1 does not delete 5/4. Merely changing its constant without a new structural inequality would repeat the excluded one-estimate pattern.

Route A: vertex incidence. Let C_i(v) be the component of colour i containing v, including isolated vertices. Every other vertex is in some C_i(v), because its edge to v has a colour. Thus sum_i(|C_i(v)|-1)>=n-1; summing gives sum_{i,C}|C|(|C|-1)>=n(n-1). But actual e_i(C) can be far below binom(|C|,2), so this is not the requested lower bound on one e_i(C). Reversing that inequality is invalid; large tree components show the obstruction.

Route B: balanced component counts. If there were at most r(r-1) positive-edge components in total, averaging their partition of E(K_n) would prove the target. There can be many more tiny components, so deleting them requires redistributing their edges without increasing the maximum load. No such component-compression theorem is proved; n-dependent counting errors cannot establish the exact all-n bound.

Route C: encode a colouring by r equivalence relations (component partitions). Every pair shares at least one class; its edge is assigned to one shared class. Thus a proposed refutation can be sought as pair-covering partitions with all block edge-loads below binom(n,2)/(r(r-1)). Conversely proving every such feasible load assignment has a block at least that large would settle the conjecture. This formulation retains overlapping coordinate classes and is stronger than a naive count of classes. The affine-plane construction shows why r(r-1) is the correct balanced equality count. I did not prove the necessary weighted cover/load inequality or construct a violating feasible family. Random multipartite density estimates do not supply it. PARK.
