Fix any l>=1 and any finite l-connected chordal K_{l+3}-free G, order n. Connectivity means n>=l+1 and deletion of fewer than l vertices leaves connected. Repeatedly delete a simplicial vertex until order l+1. Here are the complete structural obligations:

(A) A finite chordal graph has a simplicial vertex. More strongly, outside any proper clique Q it has one. Proof by induction on order. A complete graph is immediate. In a noncomplete graph choose a minimal separator S of two nonadjacent vertices, with corresponding components A,B. Minimality means each s in S has neighbors in both A and B. If s,t in S were nonadjacent, shortest s-t paths with interiors in A and B would make an induced cycle of length at least four (no edges between components and neither path has chords), contradicting chordality. Thus S is a clique. The clique Q has vertices outside S in at most one component. Choose another component C. In the smaller induced chordal graph on C union S, apply induction with specified clique S to get a simplicial vertex in C. All its whole-graph neighbors lie in C union S, so it is simplicial in G and outside Q. For a disconnected graph the empty separator and a component away from Q give the same induction. This also proves ordinary simplicial existence by taking Q empty.

(B) Removing a simplicial vertex v preserves l-connectivity while at least l+1 vertices remain. After deleting any further <l vertices, any two remaining vertices have a path in the original l-connected graph. A passage u-v-w on that path can be replaced by edge u-w because N(v) is a clique. Thus the smaller graph stays connected. Every graph in the process still has minimum degree at least l: a vertex with fewer neighbors would be isolated from at least one other remaining vertex by deleting fewer than l vertices. At order l+1, l-connectivity forces completeness: any nonadjacent pair would be separated by deleting the other l-1 vertices.

(C) Each deleted simplicial vertex has degree either l or l+1. The lower bound is (B). Its closed neighborhood is a clique, so exclusion of K_{l+3} bounds its degree by l+1. Let a,b be respectively the numbers deleted with these two degrees. Then a+b=n-l-1. Counting cliques containing v gives the CORRECT recurrence C(G,x)=C(G-v,x)+x(1+x)^deg(v); it follows directly by choosing any subset of its clique neighborhood. The missing x in formula(2.2) of 2112.09744 is not used. The companion and 2019 clique-pasting formula provide matching correct identities.

Telescoping yields, for N=n-l>=1,
C(G,x)=(1+x)^{l+1}+a x(1+x)^l+b x(1+x)^{l+1}
       =(1+x)^l[1+N x+b x^2],  0<=b<=N-1.
If b=0 the second factor is linear. If b>0 its discriminant is N^2-4b >= N^2-4(N-1)=(N-2)^2>=0. Hence all its complex zeros are real, as are the added roots -1. This proves the full literal for every l, not only fixed small l. The minimal graph K_{l+1} corresponds to a=b=0, N=1.

Counting cliques gives C(H,x)=(1+x)[1+N x+b x^2]. Therefore C(G,x)=(1+x)^{l-1} C(H,x).
