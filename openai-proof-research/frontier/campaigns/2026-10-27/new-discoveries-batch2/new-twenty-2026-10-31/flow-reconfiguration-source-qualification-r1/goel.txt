                                                                                               Abstract
                                                       Let A be a finite abelian group. A nowhere-zero A-flow of a graph gives every edge a direction
                                                   and a non-zero value in A, so that at every vertex the sum of the values on the entering edges
                                                   equals the sum on the leaving edges. Such a flow can be changed along a cycle: going around
                                                   the cycle, add a fixed non-zero element of A to the value of every edge that points forward and
                                                   subtract it from the value of every edge that points backward. The Cranston–Li–Su–Wang–Xu
                                                   (2026) conjecture states that if A has at least four elements, then in every graph in which all
                                                   degrees are even, any two nowhere-zero A-flows are joined by a chain of such changes through
                                                   nowhere-zero A-flows. It also states that, for every integer k ≥ 4, the same holds for integer
                                                   flows whose values are between 1 and k − 1 in absolute value, where each change adds a non-zero
                                                   integer. We prove this conjecture. The core of the proof is an induction on the number of edges:
                                                   if a flow enters a vertex v along an edge uv and leaves v along an edge vw with the same value,
                                                   the two edges can be replaced by one edge uw, and the induction hypothesis applies to the
                                                   smaller graph. So the main task is to change every flow into a flow with such a pair of edges. For
                                                   nowhere-zero A-flows with |A| ≥ 4, replacing vertices of degree more than 4 by triangles reduces
                                                   the problem to graphs with all degrees at most 4. For integer flows, a counting argument joins
                                                   any two nowhere-zero flows with values in the cyclic group Zk , k ≥ 4, by changes that add 1,
                                                   −1, 2 or −2; every such chain gives a chain of integer flows with values between 1 and k − 1 in
                                                   absolute value, and every integer flow of this chain reduces modulo k to a flow of the given chain.
                                                   All results of this paper are also proved in the Lean 4 proof assistant, from Lean’s standard
                                                   axioms alone.

    Esperet, Hendrey, Lagoutte, Marseloo, Norin and Steiner [2] introduced the graphs F(G, A) and
F(G, k) and asked for which graphs G, groups A and integers k they are connected. Cranston, Li,
Su, Wang and Xu [1, Conjecture 4.1] conjectured that every graph in which all degrees are even
is A-flow-connected for every finite abelian group A with |A| ≥ 4 and k-flow-connected for every
integer k ≥ 4. In [1], a graph in which all degrees are even is called Eulerian. Since “Eulerian” often
also requires the graph to be connected, we call a graph even if every vertex has even degree; an
even graph need not be connected.

    The number 4 in Theorem 1.1 cannot be replaced by 3. By [1, Theorem 2.1], for a graph with a
nowhere-zero 3-flow (an integer flow with values in {1, −1, 2, −2}), the following are equivalent: it is
Z3 -flow-connected; it is 3-flow-connected; all its degrees are even and no two of its vertices are joined
by more than four edge-disjoint paths. The even graph with two vertices and six parallel edges has a
nowhere-zero 3-flow (direct every edge from one vertex to the other, and give three edges the value 1
and three the value −1), and its two vertices are joined by six edge-disjoint paths. So it is neither
Z3 -flow-connected nor 3-flow-connected.

Further results. Every even graph in which all degrees are at most 4 is A-flow-connected for every
finite abelian group A, of any order (Theorem 2.2); for A = Z3 this also follows from [1, Theorem 2.1],
since an even graph has a nowhere-zero 3-flow (the value 1 on every edge, with the edges directed so
that at every vertex as many edges enter as leave, which is possible because all degrees are even) and,
when all degrees are at most 4, no two vertices are joined by more than four edge-disjoint paths. For
the cyclic groups Zm with m ≥ 4, any two nowhere-zero Zm -flows of an even graph are joined by a
chain of steps whose values are in {1, −1, 2, −2} (Theorem 2.4); for even m, Zm -flow-connectedness,
with steps of any value, was known [1, Theorem 4.3]. The restriction of the step values to ±1 and
±2 is what the proof of part (b) of Theorem 1.1 needs (see the outline below).

    An expanded version of this paper, with many other results, is posted on Zenodo [3]. The
two main questions of combinatorial reconfiguration, as listed in [2, Section 1], are whether the
reconfiguration graph (here F(G, A)) is connected and, if so, what its diameter is. For even graphs
and |A| ≥ 4, Theorem 1.1 answers the first. For the second, [3, Theorem 2.5] gives a polynomial upper
bound: if |A| ≥ 4, any two nowhere-zero A-flows of an even graph with M edges are joined by a chain
of at most 16M 3 +12M 2 +M steps. For integers q ≥ 1 and p > 2q, a modular (p, q)-flow [1, Section 2]
is a Zp -flow whose values are residues of q, q + 1, . . . , p − q, and a graph is (p, q)-flow-connected if
any two of its modular (p, q)-flows are joined by a chain of steps through modular (p, q)-flows. All
even graphs are (p, q)-flow-connected if and only if p ≥ 4q [3, Theorems 9.1 and 9.2]. None of these
results is used here.

Outline of the proof. Section 3 reduces the flow-connectedness of all even graphs to the statement
that, in every loopless even graph with at least one edge, every flow can be changed, by a chain of
steps, into a flow with a balanced pair (defined next) at some vertex. If a flow enters a vertex v
along an edge e = uv and leaves along another edge e′ = vw with the same value (a balanced pair,
Definition 2.1), the two edges can be replaced by a single edge uw, and the flows with this balanced
pair correspond to the flows of the smaller graph (Lemma 3.2). Assuming the statement above,
we use induction on the number of edges: the smaller graph is flow-connected by the induction
hypothesis, so any two flows with a balanced pair on the same two edges are joined by a chain of
steps. An Eulerian orientation directs every edge so that at every vertex as many edges enter as
leave; it exists because every degree is even. A constant flow with value a ̸= 0 has the value a on
every edge in the direction of an Eulerian orientation. For any two edges at a vertex of a loopless
even graph and any a ̸= 0, the two edges form a balanced pair at that vertex in some constant
flow with value a, and for a suitable a, all constant flows with value a are joined to each other
(Lemma 3.3). So all flows with a balanced pair are joined to each other, and it suffices to join every
flow to one of them; this is statement (LOCAL) of Proposition 3.4.
    Statement (LOCAL) is needed only for loopless graphs in which every degree is 0 or at least
4: loops can be deleted (Lemma 3.1), and at a vertex of degree 2 the two edges form a balanced
pair in every flow, by the flow condition. Section 4 proves it when every degree is 0 or 4; this
gives Theorem 2.2 (all degrees at most 4). In Section 5 a vertex of larger degree is replaced by a
triangle: if |A| ≥ 4 and the graph with the triangle is A-flow-connected, then so is the original graph
(Lemma 5.1). This implication is a special case of [1, Theorem 5.9(1)]; we include its short proof.
That theorem applies because a triangle is A-connected when |A| ≥ 4, in the sense of [4]. This notion
is different from A-flow-connectedness: a graphP  H is A-connected if, for a fixed reference direction of
every edge and every map β : V (H) → A with v β(v) = 0, there is a map f : E(H) → A \ {0} such
that at every vertex v the sum of f on the leaving edges minus the sum on the entering edges is β(v).
    Part (b) of Theorem 1.1 needs a further argument: the steps of Section 4 can have any value,
but Lemmas 7.5 and 7.6, which turn a step between two Zk -flows into a step between two integer
flows whose residues modulo k are these two Zk -flows, apply only to steps with values ±1 and ±2.
So, for the cyclic groups Zm with m ≥ 4, Section 6 proves (LOCAL) using only steps with values ±1
and ±2, by a counting argument that needs no bound on the degrees; this gives Theorem 2.4, which
also proves part (a) for cyclic groups (Remark 6.4 explains why the counting argument does not
extend to all groups). Section 7 lifts these chains to integer flows, using Tutte’s lifting theorem [5] in
the form of [2, Theorem 2.3]: every nowhere-zero Zk -flow is the residue modulo k of an integer flow
with values between 1 and k − 1 in absolute value.

2    Definitions and results
Graphs. All graphs are finite; loops and parallel edges are allowed. A graph is even if every vertex
has even degree, where a loop counts twice. A cycle is a connected subgraph in which every vertex
has degree 2; a loop is a cycle with one edge, and two parallel edges form a cycle with two edges. A
directed cycle is a cycle together with one of its two directions of traversal.

Flows. Let A be a finite abelian group, and let G be a graph. Fix a reference direction of every
edge. An A-flow of G is a map f : E(G) → A such that at every vertex the sum of f over the edges
that leave the vertex equals the sum over the edges that enter it. The value of f on an edge e is
f (e) in the reference direction of e and −f (e) in the other direction. From now on, a flow is a
nowhere-zero A-flow, that is, f (e) ̸= 0 for every edge e.
    For a directed cycle Q, let χQ (e) be 1 if Q traverses e in its reference direction, −1 if Q traverses
e against it, and 0 if e is not on Q. A step is a pair of flows f and f ′ = f + tχQ , where Q is a
directed cycle and t ̸= 0 is an element of A, the value of the step. The value is determined only
up to sign, as reversing Q or exchanging f and f ′ replaces t by −t. The graph F(G, A) has the
flows of G as its vertices, and two flows are adjacent if they form a step. In [1, 2] two different flows
are adjacent if they agree outside a cycle Q; this gives the same graph, because the difference of
two such flows is an A-flow that is zero outside Q, and such an A-flow is tχQ for some t ∈ A and a
direction of Q. The graph G is A-flow-connected if F(G, A) is connected; here F(G, A) counts as
connected when it has at most one vertex, in particular when G has no flow.
    Let T be a set of non-zero elements of A with T = −T . A T -step is a step whose value is in T ,
and two flows are T -joined if a chain of T -steps (possibly empty) leads from one to the other.
    A multi-step with value t ̸= 0 is a pair of flows f and f ′ = f + t(χQ1 + · · · + χQr ), where
r ≥ 1 and Q1 , . . . , Qr are directed cycles, no two of which have a common edge. Every partial sum
f + t(χQ1 + · · · + χQj ) is then a flow, as it agrees with f or f ′ on every edge; so a multi-step is a
chain of r steps with value t.

Ends of edges. Every edge has two ends, one at each endpoint; a loop at v has both ends at v.
The degree of v is the number of ends at v. If v is a vertex of a cycle Q, the ends of Q at v are the
two ends at v of the edges of Q. For an A-flow f and an end i at v of the edge e, the outward value
yi (f ) ∈ A is the value of f on e in the direction that leaves v through i; it is non-zero if f is a flow.

Definition 2.1. Let G have no loop. A balanced pair of a flow f at a vertex v is a pair of different

3      Reduction to flows with a balanced pair
This section proves the following (Proposition 3.4). Let a class of even graphs be closed under
deleting a loop and under splitting off, that is, replacing two edges uv and vw at a vertex v by one
edge uw (Lemma 3.2). If, in every loopless graph of the class with at least one edge, every flow is
joined by steps to a flow with a balanced pair, then in every graph of the class any two flows are
joined by steps. In this section A is a finite abelian group with at least two elements, and T is a
set of non-zero elements of A with T = −T . Here T is the set of allowed step values (A \ {0} for
Theorems 2.2 and 2.3, and {1, −1, 2, −2} for Theorem 2.4). We use two conditions on T :
(T1) any two non-zero elements of A are joined by a sequence of non-zero elements of A in which
     consecutive elements differ by an element of T ;
(T2) there is a non-zero a ∈ A with 2a = 0 or 2a ∈ T .
Both conditions hold for T = A \ {0}, and also for A = Zm with m ≥ 3 and T = {1, −1, 2, −2}:
the sequence 1, 2, . . . , m − 1 gives (T1), and a = 1 gives (T2). Condition (T1) is used for loops in
Lemma 3.1, and (T2) is used in Proposition 3.4 to join the constant flows of Lemma 3.3.
Lemma 3.1 (deleting a loop). Let T satisfy (T1), and let ℓ be a loop of G. If any two flows of
G − ℓ are T -joined, then any two flows of G are T -joined.
Proof. A loop adds the same term to both sides of the flow condition at its vertex. So a flow of
G is a flow of G − ℓ together with any non-zero value on ℓ. A step of G − ℓ, with the value on ℓ
kept, is a step of G, and a change of the value on ℓ by an element of T is a T -step along the cycle ℓ.
Given two flows of G, join their restrictions by T -steps of G − ℓ, then change the value on ℓ along a
sequence as in (T1).

Lemma 3.2 (splitting off). Let G have no loop, let v be a vertex, and let e = vu and e′ = vw be
two different edges at v (u = w is allowed). Let G′ be the graph G − e − e′ + g with a new edge
g = uw (a loop if u = w): the first end of g is at u and replaces the end of e at u, and its second
end is at w and replaces the end of e′ at w. The direction of g from u to w is the direction from its
first end to its second end; if u = w, this fixes one of the two directions of the loop g. For a map
f ′ : E(G′ ) → A, let x be the value of f ′ on g from u to w, and let Lf ′ : E(G) → A be the map that
agrees with f ′ on the edges other than e, e′ , g (the common edges of G and G′ ) and has the value x
on e in the direction from u to v and on e′ in the direction from v to w. Let B(v; e, e′ ) be the set of
flows f of G in which the ends of e and e′ at v are a balanced pair.
 (a) L is a bijection from the flows of G′ onto B(v; e, e′ ).
    (b) If f2′ comes from f1′ by a multi-step of G′ with value t, then Lf2′ comes from Lf1′ by a multi-step
        of G with value t. In particular, if two flows of G′ are T -joined, their images under L are
        T -joined.

Lemma 3.3 (constant flows). Let G be even and let a ̸= 0 be an element of A. An Eulerian
orientation D of G directs every edge so that at every vertex the in-degree equals the out-degree (one
exists because G is even); the directed edges are the arcs of D. Let a1D be the constant flow with
value a along every arc of D.

 (a) For two Eulerian orientations D, D2 of G, either a1D2 = a1D , or a1D2 comes from a1D by a
     multi-step with value −2a.

 (b) In the situation of Lemma 3.2, B(v; e, e′ ) contains a1D for some Eulerian orientation D of G.

Proof. (a) The arcs of D that are reversed in D2 form a directed graph with in-degree equal to
out-degree at every vertex, so they are the union of directed cycles Q1 , . . . , Qr of D, no two of
which have a common arc. The reversal of an arc changes its value along D from a to −a. So
a1D2 = a1D − 2a(χQ1 + · · · + χQr ). If 2a = 0 or r = 0, the two flows are equal; otherwise this is a
multi-step with value −2a.
   (b) The graph G′ is even. Take an Eulerian orientation D′ of G′ , and let D agree with D′ on
the common edges and direct e and e′ through v in the direction of g in D′ (from u to v to w if
D′ directs g from u to w, and the reverse otherwise). Then D is an Eulerian orientation of G and
a1D = L(a1D′ ).

Proposition 3.4 (reduction to a balanced pair). Let G be a class of even graphs such that G − ℓ ∈ G
for every G ∈ G and every loop ℓ of G, and G′ ∈ G for every G ∈ G with no loop and every graph
G′ obtained from G as in Lemma 3.2. Let T satisfy (T1) and (T2). Suppose that the following
statement holds.

(LOCAL) For every loopless G ∈ G in which every vertex has degree 0 or at least 4, and which has
        at least one edge, every flow of G is T -joined to a flow that has a balanced pair at some
        vertex.

Then in every G ∈ G any two flows are T -joined.

Proof. We use induction on the number of edges of G. A graph with no edge has exactly one flow. If
G has a loop ℓ, use Lemma 3.1 and the induction hypothesis for G − ℓ. From now on G has no loop.

4    Reaching a balanced pair when all degrees are 0 or 4
This section proves statement (LOCAL) of Proposition 3.4 for graphs in which every degree is 0
or 4 (Proposition 4.4). We fix generators of the cyclic summands of A and call an edge small if its
value is one of these generators or its negative (precise definitions follow). First, steps along cycles
change a flow into one in which the edges that are not small form a forest (Corollary 4.2); then, as
the graph has an edge and every vertex with an edge has degree 4, the small edges contain a cycle,
and one more step along that cycle gives a balanced pair (Lemma 4.3). Let A be a finite abelian
group with at least two elements. Write A as a direct sum of cyclic groups,

Direct Q so that it enters v through the end 1 and leaves through the end 2. For j ∈ {3, 4} let
tj = y1 + yj , and let j ′ be the other element of {3, 4}; tj is not 0 because f has no balanced pair.
By (2) and (1), in f + tj χQ the end 1 has the outward value y1 − tj = −yj and the end 2 has the
outward value y2 + tj = −yj ′ . Neither is 0, and the ends 1 and j are a balanced pair. An edge of
Q that is not at v gets the value 0 exactly if its value in the direction of Q was −tj . Hence the
following holds.

 (∗) If no edge of Q that is not at v has the value −tj in the direction of Q, then f + tj χQ is a flow
     with a balanced pair.

In particular, (∗) applies if tj is not small, because then −tj is not small either (S = −S), while all
values on Q are small; and (∗) applies if Q has only two edges, because then both edges of Q are at
v. Below we use the identity t3 + t4 = 2y1 + y3 + y4 = y1 − y2 , which holds by (1).
    Case 1: two consecutive edges of Q have different colors. Choose v to be a common vertex of
these two edges. Then y1 = a is small, of color h, and y2 = b is small, of color h′ ̸= h. Suppose
that t3 and t4 are both small. Their sum a − b has exactly two non-zero parts, a in ⟨sh ⟩ and −b
in ⟨sh′ ⟩. Two small elements of the same color have a sum with at most one non-zero part; so t3
and t4 have different colors; by the uniqueness of the parts, t3 and t4 are the two non-zero parts of
t3 + t4 = a − b, that is, {t3 , t4 } = {a, −b}. But t3 = a means y3 = 0, and t4 = a means y4 = 0. This
is impossible. So t3 or t4 is not small, and (∗) gives the step.
    Case 2: all edges of Q have the same color h. Here v may be any vertex of Q. Then y1 , y2 ∈
{sh , −sh } and y1 + y2 ̸= 0, so y1 = y2 = a and 2a ̸= 0. (If 2sh = 0 this case does not occur: the
ends 1, 2 would be a balanced pair.) The values of the edges of Q in the direction of Q are a or −a.
Now −t3 = a means y3 = −2a and then y4 = −2a − y3 = 0; and −t3 = −a means y3 = 0. Both are
impossible, so (∗) applies with j = 3 and gives a step with value t3 .

Proposition 4.4 (reaching a balanced pair when all degrees are 0 or 4). Let G be a loopless graph
in which every vertex has degree 0 or 4, and which has at least one edge. Every flow f of G is joined
by a chain of at most ρ(f ) + 1 steps to a flow with a balanced pair.

Proof. By Corollary 4.2, f is joined by a chain of at most ρ(f ) steps to a flow f ∗ in which the edges
that are not small form a forest. Let n ≥ 1 be the number of vertices of degree 4. Then G has 2n
edges, all on these n vertices; at most n − 1 of them are not small in f ∗ (a forest on n vertices has
at most n − 1 edges), so at least n + 1 are small, and the small edges contain a cycle Q. If f ∗ has no
balanced pair, Lemma 4.3 gives a further step to a flow with a balanced pair.

Lemma 5.1 (contracting a triangle). Let |A| ≥ 4. Let G+ be a graph and ∆ a triangle of G+ with
vertices x1 , x2 , x3 , such that the three edges of ∆ are the only edges of G+ whose two ends are both at
vertices of ∆. Let G = G+ /∆ be the graph obtained by deleting the three edges of ∆ and identifying
x1 , x2 , x3 to one vertex x. For a flow f of G+ , let pf be its restriction to E(G) = E(G+ ) \ E(∆).

 (a) pf is a flow of G, and every flow of G is pf for some flow f of G+ .

    (b) If f2 comes from f1 by a multi-step with value t, then pf2 = pf1 or pf2 comes from pf1 by a
        multi-step with value t.

So if G+ is A-flow-connected, then G is A-flow-connected.

Proof. (a) The flow condition of pf at x is the sum of the flow conditions of f at x1 , x2 , x3 , in which
the edges of ∆ cancel. Let φ be a flow of G. For i = 1, 2, 3, let βi be the sum of the outward values
of φ over the ends at x that are ends at xi in G+ ; then β1 + β2 + β3 = 0. Give the edges x1 x2 , x2 x3 ,
x3 x1 of ∆, in these directions, the values z, z − β2 , z + β1 . The flow conditions at x1 , x2 , x3 are
β1 + z − (z + β1 ) = 0, β2 + (z − β2 ) − z = 0 and β3 + (z + β1 ) − (z − β2 ) = 0; they hold for every z.
The three values are not 0 if z ∈  / {0, β2 , −β1 }, and |A| ≥ 4 leaves such a z.
    (b) Let f2 = f1 + t(χQ1 + · · · + χQr ) with directed cycles of G+ , no two of which have a common
edge. Let H be the directed graph on V (G) whose arcs are the edges of Q1 , . . . , Qr outside ∆, each
with the direction of its cycle. At every vertex other than x, the in-degree of H equals its out-degree,
because this holds for the union of the cycles. It also holds at x: in the union of the cycles, the total
in-degree of x1 , x2 , x3 equals their total out-degree, and every arc of the cycles inside ∆ adds one to
the total in-degree and one to the total out-degree. So H is the union of directed cycles of G, no
two of which have a common arc, and pf2 − pf1 is t times the sum of their maps χ. If H has no arc,
pf2 = pf1 ; otherwise this is a multi-step of G with value t.
    The last statement follows, because a multi-step is a chain of steps and p is onto by (a).

Proof of Theorem 2.3. By Lemma 3.1 (withPT = A \ {0}), deleting the loops one by one, we may
assume that G has no loop. Let Φ(G) = v max(deg v − 4, 0). We use induction on Φ(G). If
Φ(G) = 0, this is Theorem 2.2. Otherwise let v be a vertex of degree d ≥ 6. Let G+ be the graph
obtained from G by replacing v with a triangle ∆ with vertices x1 , x2 , x3 : two of the ends at v
become ends at x1 , two become ends at x2 , and the other d − 4 become ends at x3 . The new degrees
are 4, 4 and d − 2, so G+ is even and Φ(G+ ) = Φ(G) − 2. The graph G+ has no loop, and the edges
of ∆ are the only edges whose two ends are both at vertices of ∆, because G has no loop at v. Also
G = G+ /∆. By the induction hypothesis G+ is A-flow-connected, and Lemma 5.1 gives the same
for G.


6      Cyclic groups: steps with values ±1 and ±2
This section proves Theorem 2.4. Let A = Zm with m ≥ 4 and T = {1, −1, 2, −2}. An edge is small
for a flow f if its value is 1 or −1 (as in Section 4 with S = {1, −1}), and ρ(f ) is the number of
edges that are not small. The value of an edge that is not small is, in each direction, the residue of
an integer in {2, . . . , m − 2}.



                                                    9
Lemma 6.1 (a cycle with no small edge, in Zm ). Let f be a flow of a graph G and Q a cycle none
of whose edges is small. Then f is T -joined, by steps along Q with value −1, to a flow f ′ with
ρ(f ′ ) ≤ ρ(f ) − 1.

Proof. Fix a direction of Q. For an edge e of Q, let xe ∈ {2, . . . , m − 2} be the integer whose residue
is the value of e in this direction, and let x be the least of the xe . For j = 1, . . . , x − 1 the map
f − jχQ has on e the residue of xe − j, where 1 ≤ xe − j ≤ m − 3; so it is a flow. In f ′ = f − (x − 1)χQ ,
an edge with xe = x has the value 1; the edges outside Q did not change, and no edge of Q was
small in f . So ρ(f ′ ) ≤ ρ(f ) − 1.

Lemma 6.2 (a cycle of small edges, in Zm ). Let G have no loop, let f be a flow with no balanced
pair, Q a cycle all of whose edges are small, and v a vertex of Q.

 (a) The two ends of Q at v have the same outward value a ∈ {1, −1}.

  (b) If an end at v that is not an end of Q has an outward value c ̸= −2a, then f is T -joined, by
      steps along Q, to a flow with a balanced pair at v.

Proof. (a) Both values are in {1, −1} and their sum is not 0.
     (b) Call the two ends of Q at v the end 1 and the end 2, direct Q so that it enters v through the
end 1 and leaves through the end 2, and let t = a + c. Then t ̸= 0 (f has no balanced pair), t ̸= a
(c ̸= 0), and t ̸= −a (c ̸= −2a). So t is the residue of an integer τ with 2 ≤ τ ≤ m − 2. The values
of the edges of Q in the direction of Q are 1 or −1. For j = 2, 3, . . . , τ the map f + jχQ has on the
edges of Q the residues of 1 + j or −1 + j, integers between 1 and m − 1; so it is a flow. The chain
f , f + 2χQ , f + 3χQ , . . . , f + τ χQ consists of one step with value 2 and τ − 2 steps with value 1.
In f + τ χQ = f + tχQ the end 1 has the outward value a − t = −c by (2), and the end with the
outward value c did not change; so these two ends are a balanced pair at v.

Proposition 6.3 (reaching a balanced pair in Zm ). Let m ≥ 4, and let G be a loopless even graph
in which every vertex has degree 0 or at least 4, and which has at least one edge. Every flow of G is
T -joined to a flow with a balanced pair.

Proof. Let n ≥ 1 be the number of vertices of degree at least 4; all edges are on these vertices, and
|E(G)| ≥ 2n. By Lemma 6.1, used as long as the edges that are not small contain a cycle, a given
flow is T -joined to a flow f ∗ in which they form a forest; so at most n − 1 edges are not small, as
they lie on the n vertices. Suppose that f ∗ has no balanced pair (otherwise there is nothing to
prove), and that the hypothesis of Lemma 6.2 (b) fails for every cycle Q of small edges and every
vertex v of Q. Then at every vertex v of such a cycle, all ends at v that are not ends of the cycle
have the outward value −2a, where a ∈ {1, −1} is the common outward value at v of the two ends
of the cycle (Lemma 6.2 (a)). As m ≥ 4, −2a is neither 1 nor −1: −2a = a would mean 3 ≡ 0, and
−2a = −a would mean 1 ≡ 0, modulo m. So the edges of these ends are not small, and every vertex
of a cycle of small edges lies on exactly two small edges, the two edges of the cycle. Hence every
cycle of small edges is a whole component of the graph formed by the small edges on the n vertices.
So every component of this graph is a tree or a cycle (a component that contains a cycle is that
cycle), and there are at most n small edges. Then |E(G)| ≤ n + (n − 1) < 2n, a contradiction. So,
for some cycle Q of small edges and some vertex v of Q, Lemma 6.2 (b) joins f ∗ to a flow with a
balanced pair.

Proof of Theorem 2.4. Apply Proposition 3.4 to the class G of all even graphs and T = {1, −1, 2, −2}:
(T1) and (T2) hold, as noted at the start of Section 3, and (LOCAL) is Proposition 6.3.

7    Integer flows: proof of Theorem 1.1 (b)
This section turns a chain of Zk -flows from Theorem 2.4 into a walk in F(G, k): every Zk -flow of
the chain is replaced by a path of k-flows (integer flows with values between 1 and k − 1 in absolute
value, defined below) whose residues modulo k form that Zk -flow.

Definitions. An integer flow of a graph G is a map F : E(G) → Z that satisfies the flow condition
at every vertex. For a map x from E(G) to Z or to the real numbers, the boundary (∂x)(v) at a
vertex v is the sum of x over the edges that enter v minus the sum over the edges that leave v (a
loop at v is in both sums). So an integer map x is an integer flow if and only if ∂x = 0; also ∂χQ = 0
for every directed cycle Q.
    Let k ≥ 2. A k-flow of G is an integer flow F with 1 ≤ |F (e)| ≤ k − 1 for every edge e; for
k = 3, these are the nowhere-zero 3-flows of Section 1. The graph F(G, k) has the k-flows of G as its
vertices, and two k-flows are adjacent if they differ by λχQ , where Q is a directed cycle and λ ̸= 0 is
an integer. The graph G is k-flow-connected if F(G, k) is connected; as for F(G, A), it counts as
connected when it has at most one vertex. As with F(G, A) in Section 2, this graph is the same as
the graph F(G, k) of [1, 2]. In this section a Zk -flow means a nowhere-zero Zk -flow. An integer lift
of a Zk -flow f is a k-flow F with F (e) ≡ f (e) modulo k for every edge e. The residues modulo k of
a k-flow form a Zk -flow, because no value of a k-flow is a multiple of k.

Theorem 7.1. Let k ≥ 4. Every even graph is k-flow-connected.

    This is part (b) of Theorem 1.1. The proof lifts a chain from Theorem 2.4 step by step.
Lemma 7.3 is Tutte’s lifting theorem in the form of [2, Theorem 2.3], and Lemma 7.4 is contained
in [2, Lemma 5.5]. We include their short proofs to keep the paper self-contained; the rounding
lemma (Lemma 7.2) gives Lemma 7.3 and is used again in the proof of Lemma 7.6.

Lemma 7.2 (rounding). Let G be a graph and x a map from E(G) to the real interval [0, 1] such
that (∂x)(v) is an integer for every vertex v. Then there is a map z : E(G) → {0, 1} with ∂z = ∂x
and with z(e) = x(e) for every edge e with x(e) ∈ {0, 1}.

Proof. Call an edge e fractional if 0 < x(e) < 1. If no edge is fractional, take z = x. Otherwise
the fractional edges contain a cycle Q. If some loop is fractional, it is such a cycle. If no loop is
fractional, then no vertex v meets exactly one fractional edge, because the other edges at v have
integer values and (∂x)(v) is an integer; and a graph with no loop, at least one edge and no vertex of
degree 1 contains a cycle. Give Q a direction, and let δ > 0 be the least number such that x + δχQ
has the value 0 or 1 on some edge of Q. The map x + δχQ takes values in [0, 1], has the same
boundary as x, agrees with x on the edges that are not fractional, and has fewer fractional edges
than x. After at most |E(G)| such changes no edge is fractional; take z to be the final map.

of f on e in the reference direction. Then ∂rf ≡ 0 modulo k at every vertex, and an integer lift of f

Lemma 7.3 (integer lifts). Let k ≥ 2. Every Zk -flow f of a graph G has an integer lift.
Proof. The map x = rf /k takes values in (0, 1) and has integer boundary. Let z be as in Lemma 7.2,

rf (e) ∈ [1, k − 1] or rf (e) − k ∈ [−(k − 1), −1].

Lemma 7.4 (integer lifts of a given flow). Let k ≥ 2 and let f be a Zk -flow of a graph G. Any two
integer lifts of f are joined by a path in F(G, k) all of whose vertices are integer lifts of f .
Proof. Let F, F ′ be integer lifts of f . On every edge both have the value rf (e) or rf (e) − k, so
h = (F ′ − F )/k is an integer flow with values in {−1, 0, 1}. Direct every edge e with h(e) ̸= 0 so
that h is 1 in this direction. By the flow condition these arcs form a directed graph with in-degree
equal to out-degree at every vertex, so they are the union of directed cycles Q1 , . . . , Qj , no two of
which have a common arc, and h = χQ1 + · · · + χQj . Let Fi = F + k(χQ1 + · · · + χQi ). On every
edge Fi has the value of F or the value of F ′ , so Fi is an integer lift of f ; and Fi − Fi−1 = kχQi . So
F = F0 , F1 , . . . , Fj = F ′ is a path in F(G, k) of integer lifts of f .

Lemma 7.5 (a step with value 1). Let k ≥ 2, let Q be a directed cycle of a graph G, and let f and
f + χQ be Zk -flows of G. Then for every integer lift F of f , the map F + χQ is an integer lift of
f + χQ .
Proof. Choose the reference directions so that Q traverses all its edges in the reference direction.
(The statement does not depend on this choice: a change of the reference direction of an edge changes
the signs of F (e), of f (e) and of χQ (e).) The map F + χQ is an integer flow with the residues of
f + χQ . Let e be an edge of Q. Then rf (e) ̸= k − 1, because f + χQ is not 0 on e. The value F (e) is
rf (e) or rf (e) − k, so F (e) + 1 is in [2, k − 1] or in [2 − k, −1].

Lemma 7.6 (a step with value 2). Let k ≥ 3, let Q be a directed cycle of a graph G, and let f and
f + 2χQ be Zk -flows of G. Then there is an integer lift F of f such that F + 2χQ is an integer lift

Proof. Lemma 7.5 cannot simply be used twice, because f + χQ can have zeros. As in the proof of
Lemma 7.5, choose the reference directions so that Q traverses all its edges in the reference direction,

All values of x are in (0, 1], and x(e) = 1 exactly for e ∈ E1 . The boundary ∂x = (∂r)/k is an
