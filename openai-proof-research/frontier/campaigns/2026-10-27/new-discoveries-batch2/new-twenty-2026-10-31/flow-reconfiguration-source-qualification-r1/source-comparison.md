# Source comparison and exclusions

Literal target: for every finite graph G and positive integer k, F(G,k) is connected if and only if F(G,Z/k) is connected. No prescribed orientation or connectedness assumption belongs in the universal target. Fixing an orientation merely chooses coordinates. Integer states satisfy Bf=0 over Z and 0<|f_e|<k; modular states satisfy Br=0 modulo k and r_e != 0. Empty reconfiguration spaces count as connected. The MathDB raw provenance and exact statement hashes are inherited from r261 and separately verified in evidence.json.

Primary source https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24685v1: Cranston–Li–Su–Wang–Xu, Conjecture A, printed p.3. Theorem 2.1 on p.3–4 is the k=3 connectedness characterization, conditional on existence; empty cases are equivalent by Tutte. Lemma 2.5 on p.5 is Tutte's lift theorem, NOT fiber connectivity. Theorem 2.9 concerns the special circular/modular (2a+1,a) setting, not general k-flows for arbitrary odd k. Theorem 3.2 proves cubic bipartite 4-flow-connectedness. Theorem 3.3 proves a cubic triangle obstruction for Z4 and hence integer 4. Theorem 3.4 proves modular connectivity of prisms C_n square P2 for n>=4; its odd prisms start at ten vertices. Theorem 4.2 proves Eulerian even integer k; Theorem 4.3 gives even-order groups. These are known cases, not endpoints.

Esperet et al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17342 has v1 December 19, 2025; v2 April 21, 2026; v3 May 4, 2026; v4 July 3, 2026, per cached and fresh submission histories. Relevant statements and proof passages of each were read, not just their abstracts. In v1 the converse is Problem 2.4, forward implication Observation 2.3, and Tutte lift Theorem 2.1. In v2–v4 these are Problem 2.6, Observation 2.5, and Theorem 2.3. Lemma 5.5 is fiber connectivity in every version. The v4 lift statement allows zero-valued flows as auxiliary objects, with zero coordinates preserved; the reconfiguration vertices are still nowhere-zero. V2–v3 phrase the lift for nowhere-zero input. Metadata advertises v3/v4 corrections in Section 6.4/Theorem 6.22, not a withdrawal of the equivalence question. No equivalence withdrawal was found at the read passages. The displayed support-equality assertion in the forward projection proof requires stationary steps to be deleted; Cranston Lemma 6.4 explicitly handles that case. The theorem survives this correction.

Printed examples: Esperet Section 2.4, Figures 1–2, K4: the modular Z4 graph is three disjoint edges on six states, and the integer graph has three components of eight states. This is NOT connected-modular/disconnected-integer. It distinguishes Z4 from the nonisomorphic Klein group. Esperet Section 3, Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 3.5, Figure 3: the twenty-vertex frozen Z5 grid construction has a modular isolated state; sign negation gives a different state, so modular connectivity fails. The forward implication then also gives integer disconnection. It refutes the universal 5-flow-connectedness problems, not equivalence. Lemma 3.5's actual proof uses two grid-coordinate maps whose level sets are forests and whose other coordinates increase along edges of values 1/2. It is a printed mechanism, not a new discovery. The v1 5-flow questions changed status in v2; that change must not be transferred to the different converse question.

Other printed mechanisms read or located: Esperet Section 4 Kempe changes, uniquely 3-edge-colorable cubic planar/Klee graphs and triangle expansion (Figure 4); Section 5 duality (Figure 5), the two-vertex parallel-edge graphs D_m (Corollary 5.7), and planar dual-degeneracy Corollary 5.8. Cranston Figures 1–3 illustrate the cubic bipartite path, cubic triangle invariant and prism swaps; Figures 4–5 the 4-vertex/contractible-cycle mechanisms; Figure 6 the large-cyclic-factor integer bridge. Cranston Section 5 cycle-degeneracy/FRC contraction is a modular theorem and must not automatically be declared an integer lift theorem. The figure captions and associated proof text were read, but not every drawn edge visually reconstructed. No future witness has received full printed-object/twin/blowup/short-augmentation comparison.
