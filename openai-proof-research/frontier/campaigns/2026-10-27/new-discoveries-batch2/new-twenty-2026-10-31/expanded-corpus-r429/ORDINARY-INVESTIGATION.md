## 325291: balanced linear labels and cd positivity

Primary acquired: 1304.1169v2, September 2019, Sections 2, 4 and 8, Theorem 4.7, Definition 4.8, Conjecture 8.4, proofs of Theorem 4.7 and Lemmas 8.2–8.3. Graphs can have multiple edges; local finiteness means finitely many directed paths in every interval. For each path e1,...,ek the descent word has letter a when adjacent labels are related, b otherwise. Psi is the sum of these words over all source-to-sink paths. Balanced means equality of rising and falling path counts for EVERY interval and EVERY length. A linear labeling uses a linear order, not an arbitrary relation.

Route A, negative cd polynomial realization. Positivity of ab coefficients does not imply positivity in c=a+b,d=ab+ba: c²-d=a²+b² has nonnegative ab coefficients but a negative d coefficient. Thus a natural counterexample program is to realize such a polynomial using balanced interval data. The source itself supplies this polynomial in Example 4.9/Figure 2 with a non-linear relation. Its written comparison requirements include alpha~beta~alpha, with gamma not~beta not~gamma. For distinct alpha,beta, the first requirement cannot be a linear order: antisymmetry would force equality. Replacing labels on individual edges is not automatically valid: the same edge participates in several comparisons, and every proper interval must remain balanced. The source's comparison relation is therefore not a counterexample to the reserved statement. The figure caption and textual relations were read; the figure was not visually inspected and no graph-level fidelity claim is made from it.

An alternative realization by adding disjoint paths for selected ab words also fails to pay the local hypothesis. An internal two-edge segment with distinct endpoints and no alternative two-edge path has either one rising path and no falling path or the reverse. Such an interval is not balanced. Correcting this by attaching a second path introduces additional source-to-sink words; it does not preserve the proposed polynomial automatically. At low path lengths the constant coefficient and the coefficient of c are counts of edges/rising paths and are nonnegative. That observation does not control mixed cd words or supply a full refutation.

Route B, induction from the coalgebra proof. Theorem 4.7 proves membership in Z<c,d>, using the rising/falling polynomials, kappa(a)=a-b,kappa(b)=0, and its reversed counterpart. The interval coproduct splits paths at an internal vertex. This gives an inductive identity for 2 Psi in terms of smaller intervals and rising-path polynomials. It is tempting to induct positivity from smaller intervals. But powers of a-b become alternating cd expressions; already (a-b)²=c²-2d. The proof establishes integrality and cd membership, not a cone-preserving recurrence. An arbitrary pairing of rising and falling paths guaranteed by equality of counts is not a coherent local involution; changing one segment can change comparisons at both ends. Missing lemma: a globally compatible, linear-order-respecting path decomposition whose blocks contribute nonnegative cd monomials, or a different positivity-preserving recursion. Neither was constructed.

Route C, use the constructive converse. Theorem 8.1 constructs every nonzero nonnegative cd polynomial. Lemma 8.2 inserts extremally labeled parallel edges, giving Psi(G1)dPsi(G2), and Lemma 8.3 glues sources/sinks, giving a sum. Their proofs were read. These operations preserve the nonnegative cone. There is no proved reverse decomposition of an arbitrary balanced linear-labeled graph into these pieces. Reading the converse as the desired theorem would reverse its quantifiers. Search1 found additional expository mentions of the conjecture, not a body proving or refuting it. HOLD: no full ordinary proof, no authenticated full-scope counterexample, no novelty/significance clearance.

## 325530: unique prime factorization for non-thin hypergraphs

Primary acquired: 1305.1824, rendered header November 8, 2018. Definitions 2.1–2.2, Conjecture 1, Theorem 3.18, Lemmas 3.12–3.15 and their proofs, Proposition 3.17 proof context and algorithm discussion were read in selected passages. This is a finite hypergraph question. Simple means that all edges have size at least two and no edge is contained in another. It is stronger than absence of duplicate edges. Thin means distinct closed neighborhoods. The normal product's non-Cartesian edges project into edges of both factors with cardinality equal to the smaller factor-edge size and an injective projection in that coordinate. The strong product projects ONTO edges of both factors and has cardinality equal to the larger edge size. These are different constraints, so a witness for one product would not automatically certify the other, although a failure for one would refute the conjunction.

Route A, quotient and lift. The 2-section forgets higher edge incidence, and vertices with equal closed neighborhoods form twin classes. The graph argument factors a thin quotient and restores twin-class sizes. That restoration is insufficient here. On four vertices, the single four-vertex edge and the hypergraph consisting of all triples both have complete 2-section and one twin class of size four; both are connected, simple and non-thin. They are nonisomorphic (one edge versus four). This proves only that cardinalities and the quotient do not determine a hypergraph. It supplies NO pair of incompatible product decompositions of one hypergraph, and is not a counterexample to Conjecture 1. An unequal factor reconstruction from insufficient data is a failure of an algorithm, not a failure of uniqueness. Missing lemma: a canonical higher-incidence decoration of the thin quotient whose factorization both exists and refines all product decompositions.

Route B, force a collision in complete-neighborhood factors. Let U_m be the hypergraph on m>=2 vertices with one full edge. Each U_m is prime for either product. Indeed, a hypothetical nontrivial factorization has factors with strictly fewer than m vertices; connected nontrivial factors have edges, and a Cartesian factor-edge copy is a product edge of size strictly less than m. This contradicts the only edge of U_m having size m. Thus complete 2-section and non-thinness by themselves are compatible with primeness. In U_m product U_n the Cartesian edges retain sizes m,n; normal non-Cartesian edges have size min(m,n), strong non-Cartesian edges size max(m,n). This blocks the naive identification of products merely from their common complete 2-section: higher edge ranks remain visible. No edge-set equality between genuinely different multisets of prime factors was established.

A more flexible variant uses K2 as one normal factor and a hypergraph B of rank above two. Higher-rank Cartesian B edges stay within B-layers; non-Cartesian edges involving K2 have size two. For B consisting of all triples on four vertices, the size-two edges between the two B-layers form K4,4. Its bipartition recovers those layers up to exchange, so a proposed arbitrary shuffle of twins does not yield a different B. With mixed edge ranks the size-two graph can be more complicated, but no alternate coordinatization preserving ALL higher-rank edges was constructed. Simplicity prevents adding contained pair edges simply to erase the layer distinction.

Route C, extend common refinement. Theorem 3.18 uses Proposition 3.17 to refine A product B = C product D into AC,AD,BC,BD. Lemmas 3.12–3.15 then transport edges through the common coordinates. Their proofs explicitly assume the common coordinate system supplied for a thin hypergraph. Applying their edge arguments without establishing that system for non-thin H is circular. Missing lemma: a simultaneous refinement of vertex coordinates across twin classes, compatible with complete edge sets for the specified product. Neither a proof of that lemma nor an explicit incompatible pair was obtained. Search1 found the original thin result and a dissertation conjecture, not an authenticated newer theorem. No general novelty claim follows from these bounded search results. HOLD.

## 325636: sparse collapse and expected dimension

Primary acquired: 1305.5768v2, December 11, 2013. Definition 5.1 says expected dimension means dimension of the image of the double characteristic-polynomial map equals m+1 for n vertices and m off-diagonal directed edges. The section states that graphs are strongly connected throughout. Definition 5.2 defines an exchange at the distinguished input-output vertex. Definition 6.5 prints an asymmetric collapse: retain old i->j edges on the remaining vertices and add 1->j for old 0->j. It does not explicitly say to redirect every i->0 to i->1. The target is Conjecture 6.7, not Conjecture 6.6. Do not silently replace its n-1-edge condition by 2n-4.

Route A, minimal strong connectivity. Under a connectivity-preserving interpretation, G' has n-1 vertices and n-1 edges. Every vertex has positive indegree and outdegree; the sums force exactly one of each, so strong connectivity makes G' a directed cycle. Proposition 5.4 proves that a directed cycle has expected dimension by recovering its diagonal entries up to finite permutation from the deleted-root characteristic polynomial and then the cycle product from the full constant coefficient. Proposition 5.5 proves that adding a pendant exchange and moving the root preserves expected dimension, and its determinant proof was read. These settle the pendant subfamily. They do not prove the conjecture: extra edges incident to the split vertices can be erased or merged by the collapse, and the edge budget allows them. Under the printed asymmetric operation, even strong connectivity of G' requires a separate argument; strong connectivity of G alone does not authorize an unprinted symmetric contraction. Missing bridges: exact operation fidelity, preservation of strong connectivity where needed, and a classification of all lifts meeting the sparse edge count.

Route B, recover invariant parameters from determinant polynomials. Consider a labelled four-vertex lift with edges 0->1,1->0,1->2,2->3,3->1,2->0, root 0. It is strongly connected with six edges, so m=2n-2; under the printed rule its collapse is the directed 1,2,3 cycle. Write diagonal parameters a0,a1,a2,a3; A for the 0,1 exchange product; B for the 1,2,3 cycle product; C for the 0,1,2 cycle product. Put
D(t)=(t-a1)(t-a2)(t-a3)-B,
F(t)=(t-a0)D(t)-A(t-a2)(t-a3)-C(t-a3).
These determinant identities follow by the cycle terms in the determinant: the only nontrivial simple cycles are the three listed, and no pair of them is vertex-disjoint. No computer calculation was used.

The trace difference recovers a0. The leading coefficient of R=F-(t-a0)D recovers -A. Generically R is quadratic and R=-(t-a3)[A(t-a2)+C], so a3 is algebraic of degree at most two over the output field. From D and a3, its first two coefficients recover the sum and product of a1,a2, so these are algebraic with at most two choices. Its constant coefficient then recovers B, and the linear coefficient of R recovers C. Thus the four diagonal entries and three cycle products are algebraic over the coefficient field. They are seven algebraically independent invariant parameters: each cycle product has an edge variable absent from the other two (1->0,3->1,2->0 respectively), and diagonals are separate variables. This gives expected dimension seven for this subfamily, using the standard characteristic-zero dimension/transcendence-degree bridge. It is an ordinary parameter-recovery argument, not formal or independent verification.

The instance is NOT a campaign survivor. A complete applicable source bridge also covers it: start from the 0,1 exchange (Proposition 5.4); add vertex2 with 1->2,2->0 (Theorem 5.7, the triangle includes root0); add vertex3 with 2->3,3->1 (Theorem 5.7, the exchange at1 together with the 1,2,3 cycle is a chain containing root0 and new vertex3). No novel solution follows from re-proving an instance already covered by the source. The independent determinant calculation is useful only to test the recovery mechanism.

Route C, extend recovery to arbitrary lifts. In larger lifts, extra incident edges produce several overlapping cycle products. Subtracting (t-a0)D still packages cycles through the root, but the residual polynomial need not retain a single factor isolating the final diagonal parameter, and deleting root0 can leave several cycles. Counting output coefficients against proposed invariants does not establish algebraic independence or a finite generic fiber. A uniform triangular recovery, a nonzero symbolic Jacobian minor justified without computation, or a field-theoretic finite-fiber theorem for every permitted lift is unpaid. There is also the exact printed-collapse bridge above. Consequently this is not a full proof of Conjecture 6.7 and no FULLCANDIDATE or formal dispatch is justified.
