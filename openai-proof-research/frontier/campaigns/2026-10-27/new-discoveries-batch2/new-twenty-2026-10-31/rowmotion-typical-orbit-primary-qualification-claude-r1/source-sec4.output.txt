With the complete description of the orbits from Theorem 1.2, we can now prove Theorem 1.3.
Theorem 1.3. Suppose that n is odd. Then the signed cardinality statistic on IC([2] × [n]) is 0-mesic with
respect to rowmotion.
Proof. We verified with SageMath [20] that all the orbits of [2] × [n] have an average signed cardinality of 0
for n ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7, 9}. Now assume that n ≥ 11 and that n is odd. The proof of Theorem 1.2 gives a complete
description of all the orbits. Those are one orbit of size 2, orbits of size dividing n + 3 and n + 5 and orbits
of quadratic sizes. The orbit of size 2 contains two ICS for which the signed cardinality is 0, which are the
full poset and the empty poset. Following Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.11, the signed cardinality also has
an average of 0 over orbits of size dividing n + 3. Theorem 3.19 shows that the average value of signed
cardinality over the orbits of size dividing n + 5 is also 0. As for the quadratic orbits, all of them contain
singleton representatives in the lower chain, except when n ≡ 1 (mod 3), in which case there is one orbit
with representative {(1, n − 2), (1, n − 1)}. Theorem 3.27 says that the average value of signed cardinality
over the orbits with lower-chain singleton representatives is 0. Lemma 3.24 means that the signed cardinality
sums to 0 for the one orbit with no singleton when n ≡ 1 (mod 3). Together, the last two statements mean
that all the orbits of quadratic size also have an average signed cardinality of 0, from which we conclude that
all orbits of [2] × [n] under rowmotion have an average signed cardinality of 0. Hence, the signed cardinality
on IC([2] × [n] is 0-mesic with respect to rowmotion when n is odd.
It is worth noting that homomesy only occurs for the [2] × [n] poset when n is odd, as shown in the
proposition below.
Proposition 3.30. Let n be positive and even. Then, the signed cardinality statistic is not homomesic under
rowmotion for the poset [2] × [n].

every orbit. Under rowmotion, every nonempty poset has an orbit of size 2 containing the whole poset and
the empty ICS, which both have signed cardinality 0. However, the global average of signed cardinality is
not 0 for the poset [2] × [n] when n is even. Indeed, the average would be 0 only if the sum of the signed

4 Conjectures and future work
In this section, we discuss some conjectures and directions for future work.
4.1 Rowmotion orbits of interval-closed sets of [m] × [n]
Experimental evidence suggests that it will not be possible to fully catalogue rowmotion orbits in [m] × [n]
for m > 2 and arbitrary n. Nevertheless, some steps in this direction may be possible. It follows from
Theorem 1.2 that for large n, the vast majority of ICS of [2] × [n] belong to orbits of size n + 3. Experimental
evidence suggests that this extends to [m] × [n], in the following sense.
Conjecture 4.1. Fix any positive integer m. Let r(n) be the fraction of interval-closed sets of [m] × [n] that
belong to orbits of size m + n + 1. Then r(n) → 1 as n → ∞.
One may view this conjecture as asserting that, although the overall behavior of rowmotion on ICS is
complicated and of large order, its behavior on typical ICS is very nice. It would be possible in principle
to prove Conjecture 4.1 by identifying a sufficiently large family of orbits of size m + n + 1, and comparing
with the enumeration of all ICS. We outline a strategy in this direction here.

4.2 Max-minus-min homomesy
Our second main result, Theorem 1.3, proved a homomesy conjecture of [11]. We state below another
conjecture of that paper and give some remarks. The m = 2 case was proved in [11, Thm. 4.7].
Conjecture 4.2 ([11, Conj. 4.9]). The number of maximal elements minus the number of minimal elements
is 0-mesic under rowmotion on IC(P ), for P = [m] × [n].
Remark 4.3. The proof in [11, Thm. 4.7] of this statement in the case m = 2 proceeded by matching each
interval-closed set that contributes +1 with an interval-closed set in the same orbit contributing −1. In that

size 1 or 2 in [2] × [n]. For larger products of chains, it is not possible to do the same. For instance, in
[3] × [5], nonempty antichains have size 1, 2 or 3, so the max-minus-min statistic takes values between −2
and +2. However, it is not always possible to pair, over a given orbit, interval-closed sets with values i and
