2 A simpler global definition of rowmotion
In [11], rowmotion on interval-closed sets was defined in two ways. It was first defined as a composition of
toggles through which we add or remove individual elements in an interval-closed set, i.e., the local definition.
Rowmotion was then characterized globally ([11, Theorem 2.20]) and it was shown that the two definitions
are equivalent. The global definition sped up computations significantly, allowing the authors to search more
effectively for dynamical phenomena. We recall this result now; see Definition 1.5 for necessary notations.
Theorem 2.1 ([11, Theorem 2.20]). Given an interval-closed set I ∈ IC(P ), rowmotion on I is given by

3 Rowmotion orbits and signed cardinality homomesy of interval-
closed sets of [2] × [n]
Our goals in this section are to prove Theorem 1.2, characterizing the rowmotion orbits on interval-closed
sets of [2] × [n], and Theorem 1.3, a homomesy result on these orbits with respect to the signed cardinality
statistic, when n is odd.
We begin in Subsection 3.1 by giving a classification of the various types of ICS (Lemma 3.1) and showing
the effect of rowmotion on each (Lemma 3.2). In Subsection 3.2, we discuss some basic properties of signed
cardinality for these ICS. We then analyze orbits of size dividing n+3 in Subsection 3.3, proving Theorem 3.7
and Corollary 3.9, enumerating orbits of sizes dividing n + 3, and Theorem 3.10 and Corollary 3.11, showing
the average signed cardinality over each orbit is 0. We study orbits of size dividing n + 5 in Subsection
3.4, proving Theorems 3.18 and 3.19 on orbit enumeration and homomesy, respectively. We study all other
orbits in Subsection 3.5, proving Theorems 3.26 and 3.27 on orbit structure and homomesy. Subsection 3.6
shows that all the orbits were listed in the previous subsections, allowing us to describe precisely the orbit
structure and complete the proofs of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3.
3.1 Cataloging types of interval-closed sets of [2]×[n] and the effect of rowmotion
on each
In this subsection, we describe all the ICS of [2] × [n] and their image under rowmotion. We classify those
into six types. To do so, we start by introducing notation.
The elements of the poset [2] × [n] may be divided into two subposets, {1} × [n] and {2} × [n], which we
call the lower and upper chain, respectively. We parameterize each I ∈ IC([2] × [n]) by six numbers that
record how many elements lie below, in, and above I on these two chains. Precisely, we denote the ICS I by
the tuple
[b1 , i1 , a1 : b2 , i2 , a2 ]
where the parameters are given as follows:
• i1 and i2 are the number of elements of I that belong to the lower and upper chain, respectively,
• a1 and a2 are the number of elements in the lower and upper chains, respectively, above I,
• b1 and b2 are the number of elements in the lower and upper chains, respectively, below I, and
• we take the convention that if I does not intersect the lower chain then b1 = n and a1 = 0, while if I
does not intersect the upper chain then b2 = 0 and a2 = n.
