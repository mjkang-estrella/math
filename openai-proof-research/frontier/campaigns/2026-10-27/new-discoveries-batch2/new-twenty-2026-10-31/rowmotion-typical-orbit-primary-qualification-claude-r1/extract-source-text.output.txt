                                                             Interval-closed set rowmotion and homomesy
                                                                       on products of two chains
                                           Nadia Lafrenière, Joel Brewster Lewis, Erin McNicholas, Jessica Striker, Amanda Welch
                                                                                            May 8, 2025




arXiv:2505.04000v1 [math.CO] 6 May 2025
                                                                                               Abstract
                                                   We study rowmotion dynamics on interval-closed sets. Our first main result proves a simplification of
                                                the global definition of interval-closed set rowmotion from (Elder, Lafrenière, McNicholas, Striker, and
                                                Welch 2024). We then completely describe the orbits of interval-closed set rowmotion on products of
                                                two chains [2] × [n] and use this understanding to prove a homomesy conjecture from (ELMSW 2024)
                                                involving the signed cardinality statistic.


                                          1    Introduction
                                          Rowmotion is a natural action with surprisingly nice dynamical properties in certain cases. An interesting
                                          feature is that this action has both global and local definitions; globally, it may be seen as a convex closure
                                          operation, while locally, it is given as a composition of toggle operators. Rowmotion was originally studied
                                          on order ideals of a finite poset (equivalently, distributive lattices) [23] and has recently been extended to
                                          generalized realms [9, 3], other combinatorial objects [22, 15], and other types of lattices [2, 24, 7]. Rowmotion
                                          has even been categorified [14]. Many foundational results in dynamical algebraic combinatorics relate to
                                          this action; see the survey articles [17, 21, 13].
                                              Recently, Elder, Lafrenière, McNicholas, Striker, and Welch [11] defined rowmotion on the set IC(P )
                                          of interval-closed sets (or convex subsets) of a poset, a natural superset of order ideals, giving both local
                                          and global characterizations. The local characterization is a natural toggle action for interval-closed sets,
                                          analogous to the definition for order ideals: for x ∈ P , the toggle tx acts on an interval-closed set I by
                                          taking the symmetric difference of {x} and I, provided this is an interval-closed set, and as the identity
                                          otherwise. The local definition of rowmotion applies all toggles in the poset from top to bottom, that is, in
                                          the reverse order of any linear extension. The global characterization of ICS rowmotion, though, is much
                                          more complicated than the analogous definition for order ideals. Our first main theorem is a simplification
                                          of this global definition of rowmotion on interval-closed sets; see Section 2 for the notation.
                                          Theorem 1.1. Given an interval-closed set I ∈ IC(P ), rowmotion on I is given by

                                             The rest of our results focus on products of two chains [m] × [n]. Order ideals of [m] × [n] have many nice
                                          dynamical properties: the order of rowmotion is m + n [1, 23] and several statistics exhibit homomesy [16], in
                                          which the average value of a statistic f on each orbit of rowmotion equals the global average c. (In this case
                                          we also say f is c-mesic.) We prove some analogous theorems on IC([m] × [n]), though due to the increased
                                          complexity, we focus on the case m = 2. The second theorem below settles [11, Conjecture 4.12 (m = 2)].

Theorem 1.3. Suppose that n is odd. Then the signed cardinality statistic on IC([2] × [n]) is 0-mesic with
respect to rowmotion.
     This homomesy is surprising because the orbits of rowmotion on IC([2] × [n]) are much less well-behaved
than in the case of order ideals. Most homomesies appearing in the literature involve actions with a small
order, but this theorem proves homomesy on an action with wild orbits, like the homomesies found on
noncrossing partitions in [10].
     We now give precise definitions of the objects and actions in the title of the paper and discuss relevant
results of the prior paper [11]. We also state analogous dynamical results for order ideals, for the sake of
comparison.
     Let P be a partially ordered set (poset). All posets in this paper are finite. A chain of a poset P is a
totally ordered subset of P . An n-element chain poset has elements 1 < 2 < · · · < n and is denoted as [n].
The Cartesian product of two chains is given as [m] × [n] = {(i, j) | 1 ≤ i ≤ m, 1 ≤ j ≤ n} with partial order
(a, b) ≤ (c, d) if and only if a ≤ c and b ≤ d. An order ideal of P is a downward-closed subset, and an order
filter of P is an upward-closed subset. The set of order ideals of P is denoted J(P ). A linear extension of P
is a total order that respects (or extends) the partial order P . See [19, Ch. 3] for details and other standard
poset terminology.
Definition 1.4. Let P be a poset and I a subset of P . We say that I is an interval-closed set (ICS) of P
if for all x, y ∈ I and z ∈ P such that x ≤ z ≤ y, then z ∈ I. Let IC(P ) denote the set of all interval-closed
sets of P .
    The following definition contains notation that will be used in Section 2; see [11, Examples 2.2 and 2.15]
for examples.
Definition 1.5. Given I ∈ IC(P ), let ∆(I) denote the smallest order ideal (downward-closed set) containing
I and ∇(I) denote the smallest order filter (upward-closed set) containing I. Denote the minimal elements
of I as Min(I) and the maximal elements of I as Max(I). Let Inc(I) be the set of elements in P that are
incomparable with I and IncI (J) be the restriction of Inc(J) to the subset I. The ceiling of I, denoted Ceil(I),
is the set of minimal elements of the order filter of I, not including I. That is, Ceil(I) = Min(∇(I) − I).
    We now review the definition of generalized toggles from [22] where we define toggles relative to any set

results on order ideals, in which case we use L = J(P ).
