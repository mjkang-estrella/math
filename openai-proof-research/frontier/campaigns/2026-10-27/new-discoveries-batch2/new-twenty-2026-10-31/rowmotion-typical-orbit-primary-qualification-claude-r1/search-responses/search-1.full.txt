The search turned up the paper most likely behind your query, though it doesn't match the "size m+n+1" detail exactly.

- **Follow-up paper:** Lafrenière, Lewis, McNicholas, Striker, and Welch (arXiv 2505.04000, May 2025) study rowmotion on interval-closed sets. It proves a simplified global formula for the rowmotion action, from the 2024 Elder–Lafrenière–McNicholas–Striker–Welch paper, and completely describes the orbits of interval-closed set rowmotion on products of two chains [2] × [n]. It also proves a homomesy conjecture from that 2024 paper, involving the signed cardinality statistic.
- **Orbit sizes:** For [2] × [n], the paper states that the orbits are of sizes dividing n+3 or n+5, with the exception of a single orbit of size 2 and certain quadratic orbits. So the orbit sizes here are n+3 and n+5 rather than m+n+1. The general [m] × [n] case with orbit sizes m+n+1 does not appear in these results.
- **Original framework:** The 2023 paper on interval-closed sets of posets (arXiv 2307.08520) introduces the notion and includes a section on products of chains. It shows that the collection of interval-closed sets of [m] × [n] with at least one property is handled there, though the snippet cuts off before the statement.
- **Related background:** For order ideals (not interval-closed sets), Patrias and Pechenik proved a 1995 conjecture of Cameron and Fon-der-Flaass that rowmotion on a product of two chains has "not relatively prime" orbit behavior. The orbit-length theorem for order ideals of [n] × [k] is due to Fon-der-Flaass, and the length of any orbit is (n+k)/d for some d dividing both n and k.

I didn't find a proof of an m+n+1 orbit-size conjecture for interval-closed sets of [m] × [n]. If that is the specific statement you have in mind, the 2023 paper's section on products of chains or the 2025 paper's later sections are the places to check, and the search snippets don't confirm it.
