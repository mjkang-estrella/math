## 326561: Kneser treewidth, all n >= 3k, k >= 2

Put N=binom(n,k), alpha=binom(n-1,k-1), M=binom(n-1,k), and d=binom(n-k,k). Fix a ground-set point a. The family S of k-sets containing a is independent; its complement B has size M. Make a tree decomposition with central bag B and, for each s in S, a leaf bag {s} union N(s). Each edge has one endpoint in B or both in B: the former appears in its leaf bag, the latter centrally. Every B-vertex occurs centrally and in some incident leaves, a connected subtree. Each S-vertex occurs just in its own leaf. Thus this is a valid decomposition of width max(M-1,d). Since n-k <= n-2, k>=2, and the binomial counts strictly increase here, d < binom(n-1,k)=M. Hence tw(KG(n,k)) <= M-1 throughout the full requested parameter range. No Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem is needed for this upper bound.

First lower-bound route: every nonempty graph of treewidth w has a vertex of degree <=w. To prove this, repeatedly remove a leaf bag contained in its neighbor. If only one bag remains, every vertex has degree <=bag size-1<=w. Otherwise a leaf bag has a vertex absent from its neighbor; the connected-bag condition means that vertex occurs nowhere else. All its neighbors lie in the leaf bag. A Kneser graph is d-regular, since neighbors are exactly the k-subsets of its n-k point complement. Therefore d <= tw <= M-1. This is a proven bound, not the requested equality: d is generally smaller than M-1. Raising the minimum-degree bound to M-1 would be false as an argument, since the actual degree is d.

Second lower-bound route: a tree decomposition gives a bag whose removal leaves components of at most N/2 vertices. Assign each graph vertex to one bag containing it; choose a weighted centroid of the decomposition tree (move toward any branch of weight>N/2; such a move cannot immediately reverse, so a finite tree terminates). Removing that bag separates graph vertices assigned to different remaining branches, by edge coverage and connected occurrence subtrees. In KG(n,k), any two families belonging to different residual components are cross-intersecting. An exact separator bound strong enough to force a bag of size M would settle the target by this route. No such cross-intersection/separator inequality has been proved here; it is not assumed true. The ordinary maximum intersecting-family bound controls independent sets, not arbitrary components, so substituting it at this step is invalid. An alternative full route would require a bramble of order M, with a proof that every hitting set has size >=M; the family of all individual vertices is not a bramble since many pairs do not touch. The proposed star decomposition does not supply this lower-bound certificate.

## 373605: total 1–2 coloring of every finite simple graph

For an edge labeling h:E->{1,2}, let s(v)=sum of incident h(e). Write a vertex label as 1+b(v), b(v) in {0,1}. For an edge uv, the desired inequality is s(u)+b(u) != s(v)+b(v). If s(u)=s(v), this forces b(u)!=b(v). If s(v)=s(u)+1, the only forbidden pair is b(u)=1,b(v)=0, so b(u)<=b(v). If the difference has absolute value>=2 the edge imposes no constraint. This is an exact reduction, with the shared edge uv included once in each s and hence canceling in the difference.

Full-scope route A: choose h so this Boolean constraint system is feasible. Its clauses are explicit: equal sums give (b(u) or b(v)) and (not b(u) or not b(v)); a unit increase gives (not b(u) or b(v)). Thus a successful global edge-label construction must eliminate all implication contradictions, not merely odd cycles in the equal-sum graph. No solver is executed. The naive choice all h=1 fails on every odd regular graph, since then the equal-sum graph is the whole graph. Changing one edge changes both endpoint sums and changes constraints at every incident edge, so a local repair need not decrease a count of conflicts. The unpaid lemma is an edge-label assignment with no contradictory implication cycle, for every graph.

Concrete nontrivial tests by ordinary proof, not enumeration: every bipartite graph works with all h=1. For each vertex choose g(v) in {1,2} so deg(v)+g(v) is even on one bipartition side and odd on the other; exactly one of 1,2 has the needed parity. Every edge then has unequal totals. This includes isolated vertices without constraints. Even cycles are included. For an odd cycle v1,...,vm (m>=3), put h(v1v2)=2 and all other edge labels 1; choose g(v1)=1,g(v2)=2, and g(v3),...,g(vm)=1,2,1,...,1. Totals at v1,v2 are 4,5; along the remaining path totals alternate 3,4 with both ends 3. Both end edges therefore distinguish, and so does v1v2. Thus all cycles work. These proofs are not the all-graph statement and are not credited or declared novel.

Full-scope route B: form the polynomial product over edges uv of (x_u-x_v + sum_{e incident u}x_e - sum_{e incident v}x_e), with one orientation per edge. Evaluation on {1,2}^{V union E} is nonzero exactly when the total labeling works. On writing x=1+y, reduction by y_i^2=y_i preserves all Boolean evaluations. A nonzero reduced multilinear polynomial would prove existence: by induction on the variable count a multilinear polynomial vanishing on every Boolean point has all coefficients zero. Nonzero formal product before reduction is insufficient (y(y-1) illustrates why). Alternatively a nonzero squarefree degree-|E| coefficient of the original product would permit the standard grid coefficient argument, but no such coefficient has been established for arbitrary G. ## 381233: interval partitions and decision-tree activity

The full target is a converse for every graph and every strongly Tutte-descriptive activity. The literal does not give the precise Delta-activity algorithm. Here is a conditional combinatorial reduction using the usual edge-deletion/contraction interpretation; it does not authenticate that interpretation against Courtiel's body.

Write each spanning-tree interval as [L_T,U_T]=[T minus I_T,T union E_T], with internal active set I_T subset T and external active set E_T disjoint from T. For a decision-tree algorithm whose root queries an ordinary (nonloop, nonbridge) edge e, e is inactive in every resulting tree. The interval partition therefore splits on the coordinate e. On the e-absent side use trees not containing e (deletion); on the e-present side use trees containing e, then remove e from each set (contraction). If each projected activity is again strongly Tutte-descriptive, induction would reconstruct a decision tree. The necessary full-scope lemma is: every such partition on a loopless bridgeless graph has a globally inactive edge, AND the two induced activities satisfy the requisite Tutte identities. Neither is proved here. A partition of a cube alone does not automatically give a recursive splitting coordinate; assuming it does would beg the question.

Sustained restricted case: for the cycle matroid C_m (m>=2, allowing the parallel-edge C_2), every basis is E minus one edge. The interval containing the empty set has L_T=empty, so every edge of its tree T0 is internally active. Let e0 be its one missing edge. If e0 were externally active there, this interval would be the entire cube, leaving no interval for the other m-1 bases. Thus it is precisely [empty,T0]. In every other tree T, e0 is present; if e0 were internally active, L_T would be a subset of T0 and hence lie in the already occupied interval. This contradicts disjointness. Thus e0 is globally inactive. The remaining intervals all lie on the e0-present side; removal of e0 gives exactly the basis intervals of the contracted C_{m-1}. The deleted graph is a tree, whose only basis interval covers its cube and all its edges are internally active. The cycle Tutte polynomial is x^{m-1}+...+x+y, obtained recursively by deletion/contraction (delete -> tree x^{m-1}, contract -> smaller cycle; C_1 is a loop with polynomial y). Subtracting the deleted-tree term shows the projected contracted activity has the correct activity generating polynomial. Induction therefore gives a recursive partition of this restricted type, ending with the one-loop case. This proves a conditional cycle-matroid converse under the stated interpretation; it does not prove the graph-universal converse or source fidelity.

Obstruction route: a genuine counterexample could be a strongly Tutte-descriptive activity on some loopless bridgeless graph with every edge active somewhere, since no root coordinate could be chosen. But both interval disjointness/coverage and the exact bivariate Tutte polynomial must be proved, not inferred from interval sizes. No witness of this type has been constructed. Generalizing the cycle proof fails because a tree can contain several non-tree edges relative to T0: removing one active edge need not make its lower endpoint a subset of T0. That is the precise failed inference. No finite enumeration or program has been used to search for a witness.

Strongest contrary significance case: the recursive criterion/cycle case may already be in the source's decision-tree characterization results. Without the actual definitions, theorems, proof and later/final article, they have no discovery standing. A full nonrecursive partition would remain potentially significant regardless of smallness, but is absent here. Source and full ordinary gates remain HOLD.
