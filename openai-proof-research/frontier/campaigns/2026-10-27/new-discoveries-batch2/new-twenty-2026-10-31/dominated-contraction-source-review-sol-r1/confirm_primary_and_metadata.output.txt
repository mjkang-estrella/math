Theorem 2.6 If the graph Q(m, H) is obtained by identifying each vertex of Kn with
a fixed vertex of H, then
                      n(χdom (H) − 1) ≤ χdom (Q(m, H)) ≤ nχdom (H).
Proof. To obtain a dominated coloring for Q(m, H), we have to color the graph H
with χdom (H) colors and we shall use new colors for another copies of H. So we need
at most nχdom (H) colors. Thus χdom (Q(m, H)) ≤ nχdom (H).
Let V (Kn ) = {v1 , v2 , ..., vn }. Note that vi is adjacent with vi−1 and vi+1 . If we color
one of the adjacent vertex of vi in H, with the color of vi−1 and do this for another
vi ’s, then we have dom-coloring and obviously we need n(χdom (H) − 1) colors. So
χdom (Q(m, H)) ≥ n(χdom (H) − 1).                                                          

    Here we consider r-gluing of two graphs. Let G1 and G2 be two graphs and r ∈
N ∪ {0} with r ≤ min{ω(G1 ), ω(G2 )}, where ω(G) shows the clique number of G.
Choose a Kr from each Gi , i = 1, 2, and form a new graph G from the union of G1 and
G2 by identifying the two chosen Kr ’s in an arbitrary manners. The graph G is called
r-gluing of G1 and G2 and denoted by G1 ∪Kr G2 . If r = 0 then G1 ∪K0 G2 is just disjoint
union. The G1 ∪Ki G2 for i = 1, 2, is called vertex and edge gluing, respectively. The
following result is about the dominated chromatic number of r-gluing of two graphs.
Theorem 2.7 For any two connected graphs G1 and G2 ,
      max{χdom (G1 ), χdom (G2 )} ≤ χdom (G1 ∪Kr G2 ) ≤ χdom (G1 ) + χdom (G2 ) − r.
Proof. Since we need at least χdom (G1 ) colors to color G1 and χdom (G2 ) colors to
color G2 , so we need at least max {χdom (G1 ), χdom (G2 )} colors to color G1 ∪Kr G2 . So
we have max{χdom (G1 ), χdom (G2 )} ≤ χdom (G1 ∪Kr G2 ). On the other hand, first we
give colors a1 , a2 , . . . , ar to the vertices of Kr . Then we give ar+1 , . . . , aχdom (G1 ) to the
other vertices of G1 to have a dom-coloring for G1 . Also we give b1 , b2 , . . . , bχdom (G2 )−r
to the other vertices of G2 to have a dom-coloring for G2 . So every vertex of G1 ∪Kr G2
uses the color class which used before and this is a dom-coloring for G1 ∪Kr G2 . So
χdom (G1 ∪Kr G2 ) ≤ χdom (G1 ) + χdom (G2 ) − r.                                                     

Remark 2. The bounds in the Theorem 2.7 are sharp. For the lower bound it suffices
to consider G1 = K4 , G2 = K5 and r = 4. For the upper bound it suffices to consider
the complete graph G1 = K5 , G2 = K6 and r = 5.
