Construction. Let m >= 2. Take disjoint sets A={a_1,...,a_m} and B={b_1,...,b_m}, and vertices u,v. Make A union {u} and B union {v} complete graphs; add only the cross edge uv. Call this connected simple graph G_m, and contract e=uv to w, obtaining H_m. Thus H_m is two complete graphs of order m+1 sharing w and no other vertices or edges.

Exact original parameter: chi_dom(G_m)=2m.
Each of A and B is a clique, so its vertices all have distinct colors. Moreover no a in A and b in B can share a dominated color: a common dominator would have to lie in N(a) intersect N(b), but these sets are subsets of A union {u} and B union {v}, respectively, and are disjoint. This remains true even if domination were interpreted using closed neighborhoods. Thus all 2m vertices of A union B have distinct colors in every dominated coloring. To achieve 2m colors, use the classes {a_1,v}, {b_1,u}, and the singleton classes {a_i}, {b_i}, for i>=2. The first class is independent and dominated by u; the second is independent and dominated by v; the remaining A singletons are dominated by u and B singletons by v. These classes partition every vertex. Hence the exact value is 2m.

Exact contracted parameter: chi_dom(H_m)=m+1.
Its clique A union {w} forces m+1 colors in every proper coloring. Use {w} as one class and {a_i,b_i} for each 1<=i<=m. Each pair is independent and dominated by w; the singleton {w} is dominated by a_1. This gives exactly m+1 dominated colors.

At m=3 this gives eight vertices, thirteen edges, chi_dom(G_3)=6 and chi_dom(G_3/e)=4. The asserted inequality requires 5<=4, which is false. This is an exact negation witness for the universal MathDB statement, not a finite test or restricted positive theorem. No mathematical program was used.

Sustained mechanism and adversarial checks. The first shortcut of lifting a dominated coloring from a contraction fails because a class dominated by w can straddle the two old neighborhoods and have no old dominator. Merely recoloring u and v does not repair it. The two-clique construction isolates this failure: all A/B pairs share w as dominator after contraction but none before it. Enlarging the cliques sustains the obstruction for all m, giving a drop m-1, unbounded. For m=2 the drop is one and is not a refutation; m=3 is the first witness in this particular family, with no claim of global minimality. For m=4 the drop is three and even contradicts the source Theorem 3.1's claimed lower bound. The proof in that theorem says remaining classes retain dominators when splitting w; the paired A/B classes show the missing bridge explicitly. Universal-vertex coloring on H is proved directly above, not inferred from the source's Theorem 2.5. The graph is connected, has no loops, no isolated vertices, and the contracted graph has no parallel-edge ambiguity.
