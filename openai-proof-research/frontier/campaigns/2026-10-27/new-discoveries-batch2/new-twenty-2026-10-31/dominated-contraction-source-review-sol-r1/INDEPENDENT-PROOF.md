# Independent proof: MathDB 344699

The definitions here are for finite simple undirected graphs. A dominated coloring is a partition into nonempty independent sets C, each satisfying C subset N(d) for some vertex d. The quantifiers are “for every class, there exists a dominator,” not “for every vertex, there exists a class it dominates.” Neighborhoods in this proof are open. All singleton classes below have an explicit adjacent vertex distinct from their element; no self-domination or empty-fiber convention is used.

Fix an integer m >= 2. Use distinct vertices u,v,a_1,...,a_m,b_1,...,b_m. Put A={a_1,...,a_m}, B={b_1,...,b_m}. The edges of G_m are exactly all pairs in A union {u}, all pairs in B union {v}, and uv. Thus the two induced complete graphs have order m+1 and uv is their sole connecting edge. G_m is simple, connected, and has no isolated vertices. Removing uv leaves precisely those two connected cliques, so uv is a bridge. Every vertex in either clique reaches its root and then the other root and clique, which also explicitly proves connectivity.

Lower bound in G_m. Properness gives different colors to distinct vertices of A and to distinct vertices of B. For a in A and b in B, N(a)=(A minus {a}) union {u} and N(b)=(B minus {b}) union {v}. These sets are disjoint. If a and b shared a class C, its dominator would lie in both neighborhoods, an impossibility. Thus the 2m vertices of A union B have pairwise distinct colors in every dominated coloring, even if the endpoints use some of those colors. Consequently chi_dom(G_m) >= 2m. Using closed neighborhoods instead would leave the same cross-side disjointness, so that possible terminological convention cannot rescue the conjecture here.

Upper bound in G_m. Take classes {a_1,v} dominated by u, {b_1,u} dominated by v, and {a_i}, {b_i} for i=2,...,m, dominated by u and v respectively. The two pairs are independent because the only cross edge is uv. Every displayed dominator is adjacent to every element of its class. The 2+2(m-1)=2m classes are nonempty, pairwise disjoint and cover all vertices. Therefore chi_dom(G_m)=2m.

Actual contraction. Let q identify exactly u and v to a new vertex w and fix A union B. Distinct quotient vertices are adjacent precisely if some representatives were adjacent. The edge uv becomes a loop and is removed in the simple quotient; all other edges are the clique edges on A union {w} and B union {w}, with no A-B edges. There were no common neighbors of u and v, so no two non-loop edges are merged into a parallel edge. The map from quotient vertices to {w} union A union B is bijective and preserves adjacency in both directions by this list. Hence G_m/uv is exactly H_m, two K_(m+1)'s meeting in w, equivalently K_1 joined to two disjoint K_m's. It is connected, simple and has no isolates. It has 2m+1 vertices; the contraction removes exactly uv in this family, meeting even the source's imprecise general “one less edge” sentence literally.

Lower bound in H_m. A union {w} is a clique of size m+1, so any proper, hence any dominated, coloring needs at least m+1 nonempty colors.

Upper bound in H_m. Use {w}, dominated by a_1, and {a_i,b_i}, dominated by w, for i=1,...,m. All pairs are independent; all classes are nonempty and partition the vertices. The singleton has a genuine open-neighborhood dominator. Thus chi_dom(H_m)=m+1.

Consequences, personally derived without a mathematical program. At m=2 the values are 4 and 3 and the proposed inequality is an equality. At m=3 they are 6 and 4, and 6-1 > 4 refutes the universal MathDB claim, already within the connected no-isolate domain. The graph has eight vertices and thirteen edges by the two K_4 edge lists and bridge. No global minimality claim follows. At m=4 the values are 8 and 5, so 8-2 > 5 also refutes the printed lower bound in Theorem 3.1. In general the exact drop is m-1. For every positive integer D choose m=D+1 (and m>=2); the drop is D. These are one family and one target, with zero separate credit for other parameters or the neighboring theorem.

Failure of the printed lifting argument. Start in H_m with the optimal partition {w}, {a_i,b_i}. Theorem 3.1's lower-bound proof splits w and gives u and v new colors but leaves the other classes. Every pair {a_i,b_i} is still independent in G_m, yet has no common neighbor by the disjoint-neighborhood calculation above. Its old dominator w no longer exists, and neither u nor v dominates the pair. Recoloring just u and v does not repair any such pair. The alleged retention of all remaining dominators is therefore false. Failure of this one lifting procedure alone would not refute the numeric theorem; the independently proved exact parameters do refute its lower bound at m>=4. No use is made of that false theorem as an axiom.
