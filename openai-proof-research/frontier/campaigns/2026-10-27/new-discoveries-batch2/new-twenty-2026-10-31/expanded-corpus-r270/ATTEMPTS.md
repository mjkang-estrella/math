## 316253: Gaussian tree rings

Read Sullivant 0704.0918, Section 5, Theorem 5.1 and its proof, Corollaries 5.2–5.3 and their proofs, and Conjecture 5.4 in both versions. Crucial convention: a tree is an arbitrary polytree, but a **directed tree** in this conjecture has all edges pointing away from one source. An inward star with independent leaves therefore does not refute the target. The MathDB summary does not spell this out.

The source's homogeneous cone inequalities are x_i >= 0, y_e >= 0,
L_jk = x_j + sum_(i->j) y_ij - y_jk >= 0, and
N_j = 2x_j + sum_(i->j) y_ij - sum_(j->k) y_jk >= 0.
The polytope section has sum x_i = 1. The cone is normal by the source. The relevant lattice is generated by the trek exponent columns, not an arbitrarily chosen ambient lattice.

First failed shortcut: impose primitive distance one to *every listed inequality*. This is invalid because Theorem 5.1 describes a system, not an irredundant facet list. At a leaf, N_j = 2x_j + y_in is redundant. At a nonroot with one child, N_j = x_j + L_jk is redundant. At a root with two children, N_root is the sum of its two L inequalities. Requiring N=1 in these cases falsely produces contradictions even for directed paths.

More precise attempt: for a nontrivial rooted outward tree choose y_e=1 for every edge, x_root=2 and x_j=1 elsewhere. Every L_jk is 1. At a branching vertex the N slack is 4-deg(j), including at the root. Thus the putative facet-distance-one point naturally singles out valence three. At a nonroot vertex of degree three N=1; at a root of degree three N=1. In degrees at most two the larger N values might be harmless precisely because those inequalities are redundant. Vertices of degree at least four give N<=0 at this proposed point.

This is a credible ordinary route, but not a complete proof. Required lemmas: determine all facets in each root/leaf configuration; prove their primitive forms in the trek lattice; prove that distance-one equations force the displayed point (rather than merely ruling out this one choice); establish the canonical-module/interior-semigroup equivalence with the actual quotient ring. The one-vertex tree needs a separate polynomial-ring check. Source facets and the classical Gorenstein semigroup criterion must be credited; a routine application alone cannot establish significance. Journal, later commutative-algebra bodies, and full novelty clearance remain unread. No finite solver encoding is proposed.

## 316883: exact cycle lengths under a degree-sum bound

Read Adamus 0711.4394 v1/v2 Conjecture 1.1, Remark 1.2, Example 1.3, Theorem 1.4, and Section 2 through Proposition 2.1's proof. The source already proves k=1. A long cycle of length *at least* n-k is insufficient: the claim requires exactly n-k. Negative k in an unrestricted integer reading gives vacuous complete-graph defects; this is a domain correction and excluded, not a discovery.

Attempt 1: use a small separating clique. Let G=K_s joined to t disjoint copies of K_a. Then n=s+ta and nonadjacent vertices in different components have degree sum 2(s+a-1). When t>s, a cycle visits at most s components, so its length is at most s(a+1). For s=2 this maximum is exactly the degree-sum bound 2a+2. It therefore does not give a forbidden target length: choosing two component paths of independently variable lengths yields every length from 3 through 2a+2 (the triangle uses the two separator vertices and one component vertex). Any admissible target m=n-k satisfies m<=2a+2. Raising t may violate m>n/2 rather than creating a counterexample. This is related to the printed separator sharpness examples and receives no source-modification clearance.

Attempt 2: perturb an unbalanced complete bipartite graph. In K_(a,b), add one edge inside the larger class, with b>=a+1 and a>=2. If at least two untouched vertices remain in that class, their degree sum is 2a; hence an admissible target remains at most 2a. The added edge produces odd cycles of every length 3,5,...,2a+1: alternate j small vertices and j+1 large vertices and close using that edge, for 1<=j<=a. Even cycles of lengths 4,...,2a already exist. Thus this standard parity-breaking construction also fails. The source's Example 1.3 is a different sharpness graph and cannot simply be adjusted by one edge and counted as new.

Attempt 3: use high-girth regular graphs for missing lengths. A d-regular graph has nonadjacent degree sums 2d, so the conjecture's range requires n/2<m<=2d, hence n<4d. Large-girth families tend to exceed this order constraint. This is only an obstruction to that route, not a classification of all possible witnesses.

Remaining gap: either a genuine graph satisfying every nonedge degree-sum inequality with one missing eligible exact length, outside printed modification families, or a uniform cycle-shortening argument preserving length n-k. Closure preserving Hamiltonicity does not by itself preserve this exact shorter-cycle property. No witness, complete proof, or justified finite certificate bound is available. No numerical or exhaustive graph search was run.

## 317909: bounded prime-subsequence representations

Read Shevelev 0811.0290 latest Section 1 through Conjecture 2 and the preceding Moser-sequence proof; compare versions. v1/v2 Conjecture 2 is a *different* minimality question. The assigned bounded-representation question is present as Conjecture 2 in v3/v4/v5.

Let F(z)=sum_(p in T) z^p. The coefficient of z^m in F(z)F(z^2) is the number of ordered role assignments (p,q) with p+2q=m. The roles are distinct even when the same two primes can occur in different assignments. This identity is exact; it is not a new solution.

Failed extension 1: the source's unique-representation identity fixes coefficients to 1 and forces a Moser-type expansion containing infinitely many composites. Bounded coefficients 1<=r(m)<=C2 do not give that functional equation, so its digit-recursion proof does not transfer.

Failed extension 2: reduction modulo 3. Away from the exceptional prime 3, p,q are +/-1 mod 3, and p+2q is p-q. Equal residue classes produce 0; unequal classes produce +/-1. Using both residue classes can therefore cover all required residues. No contradiction follows from prime avoidance of 0. For any other fixed modulus, residue-support constraints alone do not bound the number of representations of an individual large integer.

Failed extension 3: counting. If A(X)=|T intersect [1,X]|, positivity of all large odd coefficients only forces A(X)A(X/2) to be at least a constant times X. This is compatible with square-root-scale sparse additive bases, so it does not contradict prime sparsity. A uniform multiplicity lower-growth theorem specific to prime subsets, or a structural theorem forcing composite entries from bounded multiplicity, remains missing. This infinite claim has no finite refutation certificate without an explicit infinite construction and proof.
