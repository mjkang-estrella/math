## 329448 — unweighted Tron, exact game, two constructive routes

Primary: Hoske–Rollin–Ueckerdt–Walzer, arXiv:1412.4100v1, definitions pp1–3, full weighted proof pp3–9 (text lines122–419), open problem p9 lines421–460. Alternating starts, Alice first; each player extends only its own last vertex, cannot revisit either player's path, and may continue after the other stops. Value is Bob's final weight minus Alice's, minimized by Alice and maximized by Bob. All weights 1/n in the literal target. The theorem on weighted trees gives 1/5, NOT 1/10. The final Discrete Mathematics article DOI10.1016/j.disc.2015.06.002 is identified; direct exact final-body GET returned403. Do not retry. Figures1–5 were not visually checked, so no claimed hand-certified source example is based on their geometry.

Route A: strengthen the existing response-edge/heaviest-path strategy using unit weights. For each starting u, select an optimal Bob response B(u). Direct each u toward the first edge on the path to B(u). A finite tree has a mutual directed edge: following arrows must repeat, and a cycle in a tree can only be the two opposite directions on one edge. Call it a_l a_r. Responses to these two starts lie on opposite sides. On each side take the heaviest path P from a, then a heaviest path Q from its far endpoint. Decompose into X=P\Q, Y=P∩Q, Z=Q\P, and residual R. Source Lemma2 and its construction yield x<=z<=y on either side. These are normalized path cardinalities in the unit-weight case. Avoid-Bob strategies starting at a_l,a_r give
 Δ <= y_r+z_r-x_l-y_l,
 Δ <= y_l+z_l-x_r-y_r.                  (A)
Adding gives 2Δ <= z_l+z_r-x_l-x_r. Together with x>=0, z<=y, total weight1, these give only Δ<=1/4. The source pays the additional timing constraints dist(start,d)<=dist(B(start),d) and interior starts e_l,e_r. Its Lemmas3–5 finally give Δ<=1/5 via 10Δ<=2. I read those cases, including the opposite-side version, not just the theorem heading.

Could integer cardinalities improve 1/5 to1/10? They imply nΔ is an integer and hence at best Δ<=floor(n/5)/n from this final inequality. That approaches1/5; it does not approach1/10. The basic constraints alone have the feasible rational assignment x_l=x_r=r_l=r_r=0, y_l=z_l=y_r=z_r=1/4. Each inequality(A) allows Δ=1/4. This is NOT a claimed realizable game/tree or counterexample; it shows the algebra has discarded essential timing/start information. Unit weights must be used in a genuinely new strategy or structural inequality before discarding that information. Exact missing all-parameter lemma: for every unit-weight tree there exists an Alice strategy with final Bob count minus Alice count <=n/10, including every Bob start and every subsequent response. No source inequality currently proves that. Simply replacing the weighted constants by integer roundings is insufficient.

Check beyond the first shortcut: for a path, Alice starts at a central vertex. After Bob's start she takes the half not containing Bob. Her final path has at least floor((n-1)/2)+1 vertices, while Bob, confined to the other half by the occupied central vertex, has at most ceil((n-1)/2). Thus Bob minus Alice is <=0. On a star with at least3 vertices, Alice takes the center, then an unclaimed leaf: Alice gets2, Bob gets1. These full strategies settle these subclasses, not the target. They demonstrate why a proposed positive witness needs several interacting branch sites; a long arm by itself is not enough. No parameter-instance credit.

Route B: try turning the source's weighted1/5 obstruction into a uniform-weight counterexample. Replacing a weight-k vertex by a path of k unit vertices is not a game-preserving reduction at a degree3 branch. A route between two attachment terminals in the replacement tree need not visit all the replacement vertices; a simple path cannot traverse all three branches of a tripod. Attaching all external edges at one end of a weight-path makes a traversal between two external edges bypass its mass. Attaching at both ends does not preserve all three pairwise transitions. Even where final path weights could be controlled, new internal starting vertices and longer travel times alter the minimax game; a source proof controlling only old starts would not suffice. Pendant copies likewise permit only one leaf per path. Exact missing reduction: an unweighted tree gadget with a uniform guarantee against every internal Alice start and whose path-extension timing simulates the weighted minimax instance, with error small enough to retain Δ>1/10. No such gadget is supplied. The source40-vertex example reaches1/10, so replicating it or padding with negligible branches is not a refutation.

Stop: HOLD ordinary, final-source and novelty/significance gates. This is neither known-excluded by the weighted theorem nor research-qualified. Formal route is dormant: finite rooted path-game states, termination and minimax, universal unit-weight strategy (not present), then rational bound for all trees. No Pi dispatch.

## 329562 — nice exponents, involution and polynomial route

Primary Katz–Langevin arXiv:1412.8530v3, 23April2015. Section3 lines253–256 defines equivalence s'≈s by s' congruent p^j s or p^j/s modulo q-1. Definition6.1 explicitly requires gcd(s,q-1)=1. Section6 including both paradigmatic propositions and Conjectures6.8–6.9, lines647–808, was read. The literal target is Conjecture6.8, with odd characteristic, s not≈1, and at most3 VALUES of the multiplicity function N(v). It does not mean three output values, a three-valued Fourier spectrum, arbitrary noninvertible exponents, or a standard derivative count without the source's x+y=1 normalization. Remark6.10 gives an actual implication to Helleseth's conjecture, so treating this as an elementary finite-table exercise would misrepresent its scope. No full final-journal/correction comparison is claimed.

Let q=|L| be odd and choose the exponent representative 1<=s<=q-2 coprime to q-1. It is odd. Define f(x)=x^s+(1-x)^s and N(v)=#{x∈L:f(x)=v}. This is exactly the ordered-pair count after y=1-x, not unordered pairs.

First complete structural reduction. The involution x↦1-x preserves f and has the unique fixed point1/2. Consequently exactly one output v0=f(1/2)=2^(1-s) has an odd fiber size r; every other fiber size is even. This does NOT show r=1: other involution pairs may lie in that same fiber. Also N(0)=0. Indeed f(x)=0 implies x^s=-(1-x)^s=(x-1)^s since s is odd; the power map is bijective, giving x=x-1, impossible. The fiber of1 contains x=0 and1. If v0=1 these occur in the odd fiber alongside1/2; otherwise they belong to a nonempty even fiber.

The excluded equivalence class≈1 consists exactly of p-power residues, because the inverse of p^j modulo p^m-1 is another p-power residue. For s not≈1, f is nonconstant as a function: write s in base p and choose its lowest nonzero digit position j=p^k. If s is not a p-power then j<s. Expanding (1-x)^s, its x^j coefficient is (-1)^j binom(s,j), nonzero modulo p. One can see this without invoking an unproved binomial criterion: in characteristic p, (1+X)^s=∏_i(1+X^(p^i))^(s_i), and the coefficient at its least positive digit position is s_k≠0. The x^s term contributes nothing to x^j. Since deg f<=s-1<q, this nonconstant polynomial cannot equal a constant at all q field elements (apply the root bound to f-c). Thus f has at least two output values.

N(v) therefore has zero, a unique positive odd value r, and at least one positive even value e. Niceness forces ALL nonempty even fibers to have that same size e. The odd value occurs at v0 only. If t counts the nonempty even fibers, then t>=1 and
 q = r+e t,     r odd>=1, e even>=2,      N(0)=0.       (B)
The target is now exactly e=2; r cannot equal2. This reduction handles the entire literal scope but does not prove its endpoint.

Second route: polynomial-degree control and higher moments. The expansion above bounds every fiber by deg f, and leading cancellation gives deg f<=s-1. Thus r,e<=s-1; it does not force e=2 for large s. (B), parity and the degree cap alone allow e>=4: the abstract frequency data r=1,e=4,t=3,q=13 satisfy the counting equations, with a degree cap at least4. This is an abstract integer table, NOT an actual power-permutation witness. Realizability is the missing issue. A collision/second-moment identity would read
 ∑_v N(v)(N(v)-1)=r(r-1)+t e(e-1).
The left side counts ordered distinct x,y with f(x)=f(y). The known involution supplies q-1 such pairs but does not preclude additional collisions, so it gives a lower bound, not the needed upper bound. Establishing an upper bound strong enough to force e=2 for every admissible power map is an unpaid structural theorem, not an arithmetic simplification of(B). Applying the three-valued Fourier theorem in reverse is invalid; source Theorem5.2 has stronger spectral hypotheses.

Third check of the reduction against actual proved families. Proposition6.5 gives s=3 (when invertible): f(x)=1-3x+3x², with multiplicities0,1,2 in odd characteristic other than3; characteristic3 is excluded by≈1. Proposition6.6 treats inverse exponent q-2 and derives fibers from quadratic equations, with the exceptional v=1 fiber separately handled. These are already proved parameter families, not full-scope candidates. Their proofs do not classify all nice exponents. No unsupported claim that all nice exponents are these two families is made; source Problem6.7 asks precisely a related prime-field classification.

Stop: HOLD. Exact remaining ordinary lemma is e=2 in(B) for the power-polynomial f, not for arbitrary symmetric functions. No full proof/counterexample, independent source/significance pass, final-version clearance, or Lean bridge. A dormant formal route would define finite fields, power-map invertibility, equivalence, exact fiber cardinalities, then prove that missing all-field lemma; formalizing the involution alone earns no solution credit.

## 322870 — signed arcs, hand-check of the endpoint and universal gap
