## Independent complete ordinary derivation

Everything in this section concerns finite simple graphs and simple tree paths. It does not assume a conjecture or an external existence theorem for cubes.

First establish the path/internal-vertex equivalence. A shared edge of two trees is their common unique path between its endpoints, so CIST implies edge disjointness. If v has degree at least two in both trees, components after deleting v give two partitions of the other vertices, each with at least two nonempty parts. Pick x,y separated by the first partition. If the second also separates them, use this pair. Otherwise choose z in another part of the second partition. At least one of x,z and y,z is separated by the first, because x,y were separated there. That pair is separated in both partitions, and both tree paths have v as a nonendpoint. Contradiction. Conversely, if trees have disjoint edge sets and disjoint degree-at-least-two sets, a common nonendpoint of two paths would have two incident path edges in each tree, contradicting those internal sets. This establishes exactly the openly-disjoint path definition, including its edge condition. For one vertex, both sides of this equivalence allow repeated empty trees unless distinctness is imposed separately; the proof does not conceal that fact.

In a tree on at least three vertices, its internal set S is nonempty: otherwise a connected graph of maximum degree one has at most two vertices. For two vertices of S, each intermediate vertex of their unique path has degree at least two, so the path remains in S. Thus S is connected in the tree, including the singleton case. Every vertex outside S is a leaf with a neighbor in S: adjacent leaves would be their entire connected component. Hence S dominates all other vertices. These assertions use the tree degree, not the ambient cube degree.

Now let n>=1 and k>=2, and suppose Q_n has the required trees T_i. Q_1=K_2 cannot carry two, because both trees would use its only edge. Therefore n>=2 and there are at least four vertices. Write A=(F_2)^n and S_i=Int(T_i). Each S_i is nonempty, connected in its tree and dominating; different S_i are disjoint, and the trees are edge-disjoint.

Fix i and a coordinate j. Suppose S_i contains no pair differing exactly in j. Any tree path within S_i cannot change the j bit, since doing so requires such an edge. Therefore S_i is contained in a half cube H={a:a_j=c}. A vertex z outside H must have a neighbor in S_i by domination. Its only cube neighbor in H is z+e_j. As z ranges over the other half, these neighbors cover H, so S_i=H. Each z outside H is a leaf of T_i and must attach to S_i; its leaf edge is forced to be {z,z+e_j}. Thus the entire j-coordinate matching lies in T_i.

Choose h different from i. Disjointness gives S_h contained in the other half. Every vertex of H must be dominated by S_h and has only one neighbor across the cut, forcing S_h=A\H. The same leaf argument forces the entire matching into T_h, contradicting edge disjointness. Therefore for each j there are s_j,t_j in S_i with s_j+t_j=e_j. The edge need only be an ambient cube edge; it need not itself be an edge of T_i. That distinction is sufficient and correct in the quotient argument.

Define H_i on the disjoint tagged copies {L_x:x in A} and {R_y:y in A}, with L_x adjacent to R_y exactly when x+y belongs to S_i. For any x and j, the walk L_x, R_(x+s_j), L_(x+s_j+t_j)=L_(x+e_j) is valid: the first edge has sum s_j and the second sum t_j. Successively flipping the coordinates of x+x' connects L_x to L_x'. This is a symbolic finite path argument, not a mathematical enumeration. For any fixed s in S_i, R_y is adjacent to L_(y+s), so every right vertex belongs to that same component. Hence H_i is connected. Moreover H_i and H_h are edge-disjoint because an edge's sum cannot belong to both disjoint S_i and S_h.

A finite connected graph has a spanning tree: choose a connected spanning subgraph with the least number of edges; if it contained a cycle, deleting a cycle edge preserves connectedness and contradicts minimality. Choose such a B_i within H_i. This is an existence proof, with no algorithm or search executed.

In L_x install T_i by the bijection a -> (0,x,a+x); in R_y install T_i by a -> (1,a+y,y). XOR with a fixed block is its own inverse and preserves the single differing coordinate in both directions. Thus these are genuine local spanning trees, degrees are preserved, and a local vertex (b,x,y) is internal exactly when x+y belongs to S_i. The same translation is applied to every tree index within a fixed cluster. We are not asserting that arbitrary independent translations of the two ends preserve cross-edges: instead, for each edge L_x R_y in B_i, select the actual source cross-edge (0,x,y)(1,x,y). Its endpoints are both already internal in their local copies by the membership condition.

Let U_i consist of all these local trees and selected cross-edges. It spans every source vertex. To connect any two vertices, follow a B_i path between their clusters and use the connected local tree to move between successive selected ports, including the start and end vertices. A walk suffices for connectivity and may be shortened to a path. Each selected cross-edge is a bridge: removing the corresponding B_i edge partitions the clusters into two sets, and it is the only U_i edge crossing that partition. Therefore no cycle uses a cross-edge. A cycle using only local edges stays in one cluster and contradicts the local tree property. Thus U_i is a genuine spanning tree.

Within a cluster, edge disjointness of the U_i follows from that of the T_i under their common translation; across different clusters their local edges cannot coincide. Selected cross-edges for different i cannot coincide because the B_i are edge-disjoint. No local edge is a cross-edge. Finally, all added edges touch vertices already locally internal, so they create no new internal vertex. Consequently Int(U_i)={(b,x,y):x+y in S_i}, with equality, not merely containment. The S_i are disjoint, and the proved equivalence gives k openly-disjoint spanning trees of F_(n+1).

For k=1, every cluster is connected because a cube is connected by coordinate flips. Every left cluster has exactly one cross-edge to every right cluster, so the cluster graph is the connected K_(2^n,2^n). The same path-lifting argument proves the whole graph connected and the minimal-edge argument supplies a spanning tree. For n=1 this handles the only nonvacuous premise; k>=2 is impossible. For n=0 use the distinct-set extension or the indexed-family obstruction described above. There is no unpaid gluing, acyclicity, internal-set, degree, or path-semantic lemma in the positive-n proof.
