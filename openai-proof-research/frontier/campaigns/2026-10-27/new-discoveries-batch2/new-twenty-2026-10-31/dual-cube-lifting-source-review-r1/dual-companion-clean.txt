 An explicit construction of two completely independent spanning trees in the four-dimensional dual-cube Jitendra Prajapati Affiliation: Independent Researcher Email: jitendraprajapati2603@gmail.com August 2026 Abstract Lalou, Mbarek, Skender and Togni (arXiv:2607.25917) proved that the n -dimensional dual-cube F_{n} admits two completely independent spanning trees for every n\geq 5 , observed that none exist for n\leq 3 , and identified F_{4} as the first unresolved case, reporting more than 700 hours of inconclusive computation. We settle this case affirmatively by an explicit construction, completing the classification: F_{n} admits two completely independent spanning trees if and only if n\geq 4 . The internal-vertex sets of the two trees are the level sets of a single ten-term cubic polynomial over \mathbb{F}_{2} in the seven vertex bits, and correctness reduces to finite connectivity checks that are machine-verified by a solver-free program distributed with the certificate. In F_{4} the two trees necessarily use 254 of the 256 edges. We also report exact infeasibility results for simpler rules of the same shape: within the search model, no affine or quadratic rule works, and ten terms is the fewest possible for a cubic rule. 1 Introduction Let G=(V,E) be a simple undirected graph. A spanning tree T of G is a connected acyclic subgraph with V(T)=V(G) ; a vertex x is an internal vertex of T if d_{T}(x)\geq 2 and a leaf otherwise. Two (x,y) -paths P_{1},P_{2} are openly disjoint if they are edge-disjoint and share no vertex other than x and y . Spanning trees T_{1},\dots,T_{k} of G are completely independent (CIST) if for every pair of vertices x,y , the (x,y) -paths in T_{1},\dots,T_{k} are pairwise openly disjoint. The study of CISTs began with Hasunuma [ 1 ] , who gave the following characterization. Theorem 1 ( [ 1 ] ) . Spanning trees T_{1},\dots,T_{k} of a graph G are completely independent if and only if they are edge-disjoint and, for every vertex x\in V(G) , there is at most one index i with d_{T_{i}}(x)>1 . Hasunuma also proved that deciding the existence of two CISTs is NP-complete [ 2 ] . Araki [ 3 ] reformulated existence in terms of vertex partitions; we use the following form, as restated in [ 8 ] . Theorem 2 ( [ 3 ] ) . A connected graph G has two completely independent spanning trees if and only if V(G) admits a partition \{V_{1},V_{2}\} such that G[V_{1}] and G[V_{2}] are connected and the bipartite subgraph of G induced by the edge set E(V_{1},V_{2}) has no tree component. In that case the trees can be chosen so that V_{i} is exactly the set of internal vertices of T_{i} . Constructions of two CISTs are known for hypercubes and several dense hypercube variants [ 5 ] ; see the survey [ 6 ] . The dual-cube F_{n} , introduced by Li and Peng [ 4 ] , is sparse by design, which places it near the edge-count threshold for two edge-disjoint spanning trees. Lalou, Mbarek, Skender and Togni [ 9 ] recently proved that F_{n} admits two CISTs for every n\geq 5 , observed that the edge count already obstructs existence for n\leq 3 , and reported that the case n=4 resisted more than 700 hours of computation and was left unresolved. In this note we settle the remaining case. Theorem 3 . F_{4} admits two completely independent spanning trees. Consequently, the dual-cube F_{n} admits two completely independent spanning trees if and only if n\geq 4 . The construction (Section 3 ) is compact: the internal-vertex partition is given by a ten-term cubic polynomial over \mathbb{F}_{2} in the seven vertex bits, and its correctness reduces via Theorem 2 to finite connectivity checks. A certificate pinning the two trees explicitly, together with a verifier that uses only the Python standard library and checks all \binom{128}{2}=8128 pairs of tree paths directly against the definition, is publicly available (Section 4 ). Section 5 reports exact infeasibility results for simpler rules of the same shape. 2 Preliminaries Following [ 9 ] , the dual-cube F_{n} is the n -regular n -connected graph on the vertex set \{0,1\}^{2n-1} in which two vertices u=(u_{2n-1},\dots,u_{1}) and v=(v_{2n-1},\dots,v_{1}) are adjacent if and only if they differ in exactly one bit position i and: 1. if 1\leq i\leq n-1 then u_{2n-1}=v_{2n-1}=0 ; 2. if n\leq i\leq 2n-2 then u_{2n-1}=v_{2n-1}=1 ; 3. i=2n-1 is always allowed. Thus F_{4} has 128 vertices, 256 edges, and degree 4 ; it consists of sixteen Q_{3} clusters, eight in each class, joined by a perfect matching of 128 cross-edges. For consistency with the machine-readable certificate we index bits from zero: a vertex v\in\{0,1\}^{7} of F_{4} has bits x_{0},\dots,x_{6} with x_{i}=u_{i+1} , so x_{6} is the class bit, x_{0},x_{1},x_{2} are the class- 0 cluster coordinates and x_{3},x_{4},x_{5} are the class- 1 cluster coordinates. Two edge-disjoint spanning trees of F_{4} use 2\cdot 127=254 of the 256 edges. By Theorem 1 , in any CIST pair no vertex is internal in both trees; since F_{4} is 4 -regular, every vertex is then either internal in exactly one tree or a leaf in both. 3 The construction Define z\colon V(F_{4})\to\mathbb{F}_{2} by z(v)\;=\;x_{0}x_{1}\oplus x_{2}\oplus x_{1}x_{2}\oplus x_{1}x_{3}\oplus x_{3}x_{4}\oplus x_{1}x_{5}\oplus x_{4}x_{5}\oplus x_{0}x_{6}\oplus x_{3}x_{6}\oplus x_{2}x_{4}x_{6}, (1) and set V_{1}=\{v:z(v)=1\} and V_{2}=\{v:z(v)=0\} . Proposition 1 . |V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=64 ; the induced subgraphs F_{4}[V_{1}] and F_{4}[V_{2}] are connected with exactly 64 edges each (hence unicyclic); and the bipartite subgraph induced by the 128 edges of E(V_{1},V_{2}) has exactly two components, each with 64 vertices and 64 edges (hence unicyclic). Proof. This is a finite computation on a 128 -vertex graph; it is performed independently of any solver by the verification program of Section 4 . ∎ Since a unicyclic component is not a tree, Proposition 1 and Theorem 2 prove Theorem 3 : \{V_{1},V_{2}\} is a CIST partition of F_{4} , and the classification follows from [ 9 ] . Every vertex is internal in exactly one tree, so the pair realizes the balanced 64/64 pattern. The certificate pins the two trees explicitly, so correctness does not depend on quoting Theorem 2 . The unique cycle of F_{4}[V_{2}] contains the edge \{0,64\} and the unique cycle of F_{4}[V_{1}] contains the edge \{21,85\} . Orient each unicyclic component of the cut graph functionally, so that every vertex acquires exactly one outgoing cut edge \{u,\mathrm{out}(u)\} . Then T_{2}^{\,\prime}=\bigl(E(F_{4}[V_{2}])\setminus\{\{0,64\}\}\bigr)\;\cup\;\bigl\{\{u,\mathrm{out}(u)\}:u\in V_{1}\bigr\}, and symmetrically T_{1}^{\,\prime} with \{21,85\} removed, are two edge-disjoint spanning trees, with internal-vertex sets exactly V_{1} and V_{2} ; the only edges of F_{4} unused by T_{1}^{\,\prime}\cup T_{2}^{\,\prime} are \{0,64\} and \{21,85\} . 4 Machine verification The construction is distributed as a short JSON certificate together with a verifier that uses only the Python standard library — no solver is involved. The verifier (i) rebuilds F_{4} from the bit definition of Section 2 , (ii) evaluates the polynomial ( 1 ) and checks every statement of Proposition 1 , (iii) reconstructs T_{1}^{\,\prime} and T_{2}^{\,\prime} and checks that they are edge-disjoint spanning trees with internal-vertex sets V_{1} and V_{2} , and (iv) for each of the 8128 unordered vertex pairs checks directly that the two tree paths are openly disjoint. Step (iv) is the definition of complete independence, so the verified claim does not rest on Theorem 2 or on any search software. A second, independently fixed edge-list witness passes a separate verifier. Both programs refuse to run under Python’s assertion-stripping -O mode. 5 Exact searches for simpler rules The rule ( 1 ) was found with a CP-SAT model (OR-Tools) whose Boolean variables encode two edge-disjoint spanning trees together with the coefficients of an algebraic normal form (ANF) for z , subject to the exact-complement pattern: every vertex is internal in exactly one tree, with 64 internal vertices in each. This pattern is a genuine model restriction — a general CIST pair may contain vertices that are leaves in both trees — and the results below are statements about this model only. Within it, the solver returned exact infeasibility (not timeouts) for: • all 127 nonconstant affine rules z(v)=\mathrm{parity}(v\wedge m) , m\in\{1,\dots,127\} ; • every ANF of degree at most 2 — so, with the degree- 1 case, degree 3 is the minimum ANF degree realizable in the model; • every ANF of degree at most 3 with at most nine nonconstant terms — so ten terms is the minimum term count among degree-at-most- 3 rules in the model (sparser rules of degree exceeding 3 were not excluded); • all rules of the separable form f(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\oplus g(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\oplus\alpha x_{6} , and eight relation-based rules h(\mathrm{op}(A,B))\oplus\alpha x_{6} for A=(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}) , B=(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}) , \mathrm{op}\in\{\oplus,\,+\bmod 8,\,-\bmod 8,\,\mathrm{Gray}(A)\oplus\mathrm{Gray}(B)\} and \alpha\in\{0,1\} . These are solver statuses for the stated finite models. CP-SAT does not emit solver-independent unsatisfiability certificates, so the negative results carry the solver’s usual trust assumptions (solver version and run parameters are recorded with the data); the positive result of Theorem 3 does not depend on them. Remark 1 . F_{4} has the compound form K_{8,8}(Q_{3}) : contracting its sixteen Q_{3} clusters yields K_{8,8} . CIST results for compound graphs [ 7 ] state corollaries for compound graphs with complete-graph clusters, as in L\text{-}HSDC_{m}(m)=Q_{m}(K_{m}) , and the constructions in this line of work proceed via edge-disjoint Hamiltonian cycles with clusters K_{2n} [ 6 ] . We have not been able to consult the full sufficient condition of [ 7 ] ; we note, however, that Q_{3} is not complete and its 12 edges cannot carry two edge-disjoint spanning trees on 8 vertices, so approaches requiring per-cluster tree pairs cannot apply to F_{4} . 6 Concluding remarks The polynomial ( 1 ) answers the existence question for F_{4} with a certificate short enough to print. Two natural questions remain. First, the minimality statements of Section 5 are relative to the exact-complement model and to the solver; a solver-independent proof that no quadratic rule works, or a simpler rule outside the model (allowing vertices that are leaves in both trees), would be of interest. Second, Lalou et al. [ 9 ] conjecture on the existence of k CISTs in higher-dimensional dual-cubes; whether algebraically defined partitions of the present kind can produce three or more CISTs in F_{n} for larger n is open.
