 Abstract 
    
 XOR-magic graph labelings form a special subclass of group distance magic labelings. A simple connected graph of order  2 n 2^{n} 
is called an  open  (respectively,  closed )  XOR-magic graph of power  n n  if its vertices can be labeled bijectively with vectors from  ( ⁡ Z 2 ) n (\mathop{\mathbb{}}{Z}\nolimits_{2})^{n}  such that the sum (over  ( ⁡ Z 2 ) n (\mathop{\mathbb{}}{Z}\nolimits_{2})^{n} ) of labels in each open (respectively, closed) neighborhood of every vertex is equal to the zero vector. 
 
    
 In  [ 2 ] , Batal asked whether there exists any odd-regular open XOR-magic graph or any even-regular closed XOR-magic graph. In this paper, with partial help of MILP solver, we answer this question in the affirmative. More precisely, we prove that for every integer  n > 3 n>3 , there exists an odd-regular open XOR-magic graph of power  n n  and an even-regular closed XOR-magic graph of power
 n n . We also show some applications of the spectra of graphs for an open XOR-magic labeling. 
 
  
 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  denote a graph with vertex set  V V  and edge set  E E . For  x ∈ V x\in V  we denote the set of all neighbors of  x x  by  N ⁡ ( x ) N(x)  and  N ⁡ [ x ] = N ⁡ ( x ) ∪ { x } N[x]=N(x)\cup\{x\} . The set  N ⁡ ( x ) N(x)  we call  open neighborhood set  of  x x , whereas  N ⁡ [ x ] N[x]  we call  closed neighborhood set  of  x x . If there exists an integer  k k  such that  | N ⁡ ( x ) | = k |N(x)|=k  for every  x ∈ V x\in V , then we say that  G G  is  regular with valency   k k , or  G G  is a  k k -regular graph. Furthermore, we say that  G G  is  odd  ( even )  regular , if it is regular with valency  k k , where  k k  is odd (even, respectively). 
 
 
 
 
 Let  ( Γ , + ) (\Gamma,+)  be a finite Abelian group. A  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling  of a graph  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  with  | V | = n |V|=n  is a bijection  ℓ \ell  from  V V  to an Abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  n n , for which there exists  μ ∈ Γ \mu\in\Gamma ,
such that the weight  w ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ y ∈ N ⁡ ( x ) ℓ ⁡ ( y ) w(x)=\sum_{y\in N(x)}\ell(y)  of every vertex  x ∈ V x\in V  is equal to  μ \mu   [ 9 ] . In this case, the element  μ \mu  is called the  magic constant of   G G  and the graph  G G  is called  Γ \Gamma -distance magic graph. Note that in a  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling, the weight of each vertex  v v  is computed over its open neighborhood  N ⁡ ( v ) N(v) . Analogously, in  [ 1 ] , the notion of a  Γ \Gamma -closed distance magic labeling is defined, where the weight is taken over the closed neighborhood  N ⁡ [ v ] N[v] . Formally, a  Γ \Gamma -closed distance magic labeling  is a bijection  ℓ ′ : V → Γ \ell^{\prime}\colon V\to\Gamma , such that the weight  w ′ ​ ( v ) = ∑ u ∈ N ⁡ [ v ] ℓ ⁡ ( u ) w^{\prime}(v)=\sum_{u\in N[v]}\ell(u)  of every vertex  v ∈ V v\in V  is equal to the same element  μ ′ ∈ Γ \mu^{\prime}\in\Gamma . 
 
 
 
 
 Independently, Siehler defined a closed XOR-magic graph of power  n n . Formally speaking, a simple connected graph of order  2 n 2^{n}  is defined as a  closed XOR-magic graph of power   n n  if its vertices can be labeled with vectors from  ( ⁡ Z 2 ) n (\mathop{\mathbb{}}{Z}\nolimits_{2})^{n}  in a one-a
to-one manner, such that the sum of labels in each closed neighborhood set
of vertices equals zero  [ 11 ] . Thus, a closed XOR-magic graph of power  n n  is a connected  ( ⁡ Z 2 ) n (\mathop{\mathbb{}}{Z}\nolimits_{2})^{n} -closed distance magic graph with the magic constant  0 0 . A labeling ensuring the fulfillment of the condition by definition will be called a  closed XOR-magic labeling . 
 
 
 
 
 This definition was recently adopted by Batal for an open neighborhood of vertices, an open XOR-magic graph of power  n n  is a connected  ( ⁡ Z 2 n ) (\mathop{\mathbb{}}{Z}\nolimits_{2}^{n}) -distance magic graph with the magic constant  0 0   [ 2 ] . Such labeling we call an  open XOR-magic labeling . Batal proved the
existence of  k k -regular closed XOR-magic graphs of power  n n  for all  n ≥ 2 n\geq 2  and for all
 k ∈ { 3 , 5 , 7 , … , 2 n ​ - ​ 5 } ∪ { 2 n ​ - ​  1 } k\in\{3,5,7,\dots,2^{n}\text{-}5\}\cup\{2^{n}\,\text{-}\,1\} . He showed that there is no
( 2 n ​ - ​  3 2^{n}\,\text{-}\,3 )-regular open XOR-magic graph of power  n n .

 Batal showed some connection for open XOR-magic and closed XOR-magic graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 Observation 1  ( [ 2 ] ) 
 
 Let  G G  be a connected graph on  m = 2 n m=2^{n}  vertices such that  G ¯ \overline{G}  is also connected. Then  G G  is an open XOR-magic graph if and only if  G ¯ \overline{G}  is a closed XOR-magic graph. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 While there are many papers on even regular  Γ \Gamma -distance magic graphs see  [ 5 ,  8 ]  for more references, there is not much known for odd regular: 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem 2  ( [ 5 ] ) 
 
 Let  G G  be an  r r -regular graph on  n n 
vertices, where  r r  is odd. If  Γ \Gamma  is an Abelian group of order  n n  with  | I ⁡ ( Γ ) | = 1 |I(\Gamma)|=1 , then  G G  is not  Γ \Gamma -distance magic 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Observation 3  ( [ 5 ] ) 
 
 Let  G G  be an  r r -regular graph on  n ≡ 2 ( mod 4 ) n\equiv 2\pmod{4}  vertices, where  r r  is odd. Then  G G  is not  Γ \Gamma -distance magic for any Abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  n n . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 However, there are some known examples of odd regular  Γ \Gamma -distance magic graphs  [ 4 ,  6 ] . Only three of them are connected, and there were no known ones for  Γ = ( ℤ 2 ) m \Gamma=(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{m} . Therefore, Batal posted the following open problem: 
 
 
 
 
 Open Problem 4 
 
 Is there any even regular closed XOR-magic or odd regular open
XOR-magic graph? 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively in Section  3 . Namely, we prove the following theorem. 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem 5 
 
 For any integer  n > 3 n>3  there exists an even closed XOR-magic graph of power  n n , and an odd open XOR-magic graph of power  n n . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Moreover, in Section  2  we show some applications of the spectra of graphs for open XOR-magic labeling. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 2  Algebraic approach and its application to Cayley graphs 

 
 In this section, we focus on the algebraic approach to the problem of the nonexistence of open XOR-magic labelings for graphs. Then, we will apply our approach to Cayley graphs.
Let  X X  be a (finite or infinite) group
and  S S  be a unit-free symmetric subset of  S S . The  Cayley graph   Cay ⁡ ( X , S ) \mathrm{Cay}(X,S)  is a graph with vertices labeled (bijectivelly) by the elements of the group  X X , and the adjacency relation is defined by the subset  S S  of the group  X X . In particular,  circulant graphs  are Cayley graphs defined over cyclic groups – the simplest and most fundamental family of groups. Formally, let  m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 ,  D = { 1 , 2 , … ​ ⌊ m / 2 ⌋ } D=\{1,2,\ldots\lfloor{m/2}\rfloor\}  and let  S = { s 1 , s 2 , … , s r } ⊆ D S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}\}\subseteq D .  In the following, for each  a , b ∈ ℤ a,b\in\mathbb{Z}  such that  a < b a<b , by  [ a , b ] [a,b]  we denote the set  { a , a + 1 , … , b } \{a,a+1,\ldots,b\} .

 Let  m = 2 n m=2^{n}  and  S = { s 1 , s 2 , … , s k } S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k}\}  be such that the circulant graph  G = C m ​ ( S ) G=C_{m}(S)  is connected and there exists an odd integer  l ∈ [ 1 , ⌊ m 2 ⌋ ] ∖ S l\in[1,\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor]\setminus S .  4  Conclusions 

 
 An interesting observation comes from another fact proven by Batal. He proved the following fact. 
 
 
 
 
 Proposition 24  ( [ 2 ] ) 
 
 There does not exist any even  k k -regular graph of order  2 n 2^{n}  admitting a closed XOR-magic labeling for  k ∈ { 0 , 2 , 2 n − 4 , 2 n − 2 } k\in\{0,2,2^{n}-4,2^{n}-2\} . Equivalently, there does not exist any odd  k k -regular graph of order  2 n 2^{n}  admitting an open XOR-magic labeling for  k ∈ { 1 , 3 , 2 n − 3 , 2 n − 1 } k\in\{1,3,2^{n}-3,2^{n}-1\} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Firstly, notice that the direct consequence of the Proposition  24  is that there does not exist any odd (even) regular open (closed) XOR-magic graph of order  8 8 . So, our constructed graphs of order  16 16  are the minimal possible graphs. However, we proved that for order  2 n 2^{n}  (where  n = 4 n=4 ), there exist odd regular open XOR-magic graphs for each possible odd degree  d d  such that  d ∉ { 1 , 3 , 2 n − 3 , 2 n − 1 } d\notin\{1,3,2^{n}-3,2^{n}-1\}  and there exist even regular closed XOR-magic graphs for each possible even degree  d d  such that  d ∉ { 0 , 2 , 2 n − 2 , 2 n } d\notin\{0,2,2^{n}-2,2^{n}\} . 
 
 
 
 
 Moreover, observe that by Theorems  3  and  18  and Corollary  20  we obtain that there exists an open odd XOR-magic graph of power  8 8  for any odd degree  d ∈ { 9 , 11 , … , 21 } d\in\{9,11,\ldots,21\}  as well as an even closed XOR-magic graph of power  8 8  for any odd degree  d ∈ { 8 , 10 , … , 24 , 34 , 44 , 48 , 54 , 62 , 76 , 80 , 98 , 120 } d\in\{8,10,\ldots,24,34,44,48,54,62,76,80,98,120\} . 
 
 
 
 
 Recall, if  δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ( m − 1 ) / 2 \delta(G)\geq(m-1)/2  for a graph  G G  of order  m m , then the graph  G G  is connected. Thus, by Observation  1  there exists odd  d d -regular open XOR-magic graph of power  8 8  for any odd  d ∈ { 135,157,175,179,193 , 201 , 207 , 211 , 221 , 231 , … , 247 } d\in\{135,157,175,179,193,201,207,211,\allowbreak 221,231,\ldots,247\}  as well as an even closed XOR-magic graph of power  8 8  for any even degree  d ∈ { 234,236 , … , 246 } d\in\{234,236,\ldots,246\} .
In connection with that, we pose the following conjecture. 
 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 25 
 
 For each degree  d ∈ { 5 , 7 , … ​ 2 n − 7 , 2 n − 5 } d\in\{5,7,\ldots 2^{n}-7,2^{n}-5\} , there exists  d d -regular open XOR-magic graph of order  2 n 2^{n} . For each degree  d ∈ { 4 , 6 , … ​ 2 n − 6 , 2 n − 4 } d\in\{4,6,\ldots 2^{n}-6,2^{n}-4\} , there exists  d d -regular closed XOR-magic graph of order  2 n 2^{n} .
