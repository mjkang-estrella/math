                                               THE ABU-KHZAM–LANGSTON CONJECTURE FOR GRAPHS WITH α(G) = 2


                                                                                         JONATHAN C. DAHLKE


                                                   Abstract. The Abu-Khzam–Langston conjecture—the weak-immersion analogue of Hadwiger’s con-
                                                   jecture and a weak version of an earlier conjecture of Lescure and Meyniel—asserts that every graph G
                                                   contains a weak immersion of Kχ(G) . We prove the conjecture for the class of graphs of independence
                                                   number two. Along the way, we introduce a notion of cycle-matching colouring of a graph—a relaxation
                                                   of edge-colouring in which colour classes induce vertex-disjoint unions of edges and odd cycles—and prove
                                                   a sharpening of Vizing’s theorem in this setting: every multigraph admits a cycle-matching colouring
                                                   with at most ∆(G) colours.

                                           1    Introduction

                                           A graph H is said to be a (weak) immersion of a graph G if there is an injection ι : V (H) → V (G) together
                                           with a family of pairwise edge-disjoint paths in G, one for each edge uv ∈ E(H), whose endpoints are
                                           ι(u) and ι(v). The immersion is a strong immersion if, in addition, the internal vertices of these paths
                                           avoid the image ι(V (H)). Note that throughout the paper, whenever we use the term immersion, we
                                           refer to a weak immersion.
                                              In 1989, Lescure and Meyniel [10] formulated the following analogue of Hadwiger’s famous conjecture [9]
                                           for strong immersions.
                                           Conjecture 1.1 (Lescure, Meyniel [10]). Every graph G contains a strong immersion of Kχ(G) .
                                              Independently, Abu-Khzam and Langston [1] proposed the corresponding statement for weak immer-
                                           sions, which is the conjecture we focus on in this paper.
                                           Conjecture 1.2 (Abu-Khzam, Langston [1]). Every graph G contains a weak immersion of Kχ(G) .
                                              Since every strong immersion is a weak immersion, Conjecture 1.1 clearly implies Conjecture 1.2; both
                                           remain open in general. While Hadwiger’s conjecture is currently settled only up to χ(G) ≤ 6 [15] and
                                           notoriously open beyond, the immersion analogues are a bit better understood: both conjectures are
                                           trivial for χ(G) ≤ 4 and were verified for χ(G) ∈ {5, 6} by Lescure and Meyniel themselves [10]; the
                                           case χ(G) = 7 was settled by DeVos, Kawarabayashi, Mohar, and Okamura [7]. For χ(G) ≥ 8, both
                                           conjectures remain open.
                                              A natural restricted setting for Hadwiger’s conjecture, Conjecture 1.1, and Conjecture 1.2 is the class
                                           of graphs of small independence number. The case α(G) = 2 has received particular attention. An initial
                                           investigation of Conjecture 1.2 restricted to this class of graphs was undertaken by Vergara [18], who,
                                           among other things, showed that for graphs with α(G) ≤ 2 it is equivalent to the assertion that every
                                           such graph contains a weak immersion of K⌈|V (G)|/2⌉ and proposed
                                           Conjecture 1.3 (Vergara [18]). Every graph G with α(G) = 2 contains a weak immersion of Kχ(G) .
                                              A number of partial results have followed. Quiroz [14] verified Conjecture 1.3 for H-free graphs with
                                           |V (H)| ≤ 4. Bustamante, Quiroz, Stein, and Zamora [4] obtained immersion-size lower bounds in terms
                                           of the independence number for graphs of arbitrary independence number. Botler, Jiménez, Lintzmayer,
                                           Pastine, Quiroz, and Sambinelli [3] proved a biclique analogue, showing that every graph with α(G) = 2
                                           contains an immersion of K⌈n/2⌉,⌈n/2⌉ ; a subsequent shorter proof was proposed by Chen and Deng [5].
                                           Most recently, Botler, Fernandes, Lintzmayer, Lopes, Mishra, Netto, and Sambinelli [2] proposed a proof
                                           of Conjecture 1.3 for graphs satisfying the maximum-degree bound ∆(G) < 19   29 n − 1 (with n ≥ 11).
                                              In this paper, we resolve Conjecture 1.3.
                                           Theorem 1.4. Let G be a graph with α(G) = 2. Then G contains a weak immersion of Kχ(G) .

Notation. We use χ(G), α(G), ∆(G), χ′ (G) for the chromatic number, independence number, maximum
degree, and chromatic index of G, respectively. For two vertex sets A, B ⊆ V (G), E(A, B) denotes the set
of edges with one endpoint in A and the other in B. We write E(v, A) := E({v}, A), E(A) := E(A, A),
and dG (v) = |E(v, V (G))| for the degree of v. For an edge set F ⊆ E(G), G[F ] is the graph on V (G)
with edge set F . For a vertex set A ⊆ V (G), G[A] is the graph on A with edge set E(A). A component
of G consisting of a single vertex is called trivial. A vertex set X is spanned by an edge set F , if every
x ∈ X has dG[F ] (x) > 0. For notation not defined here, we refer to [8]. Throughout, all graphs are finite
and loopless; the multigraphs appearing in section 2 are allowed to have parallel edges.


2    A Vizing-type theorem for cycle-matching colourings

In this section we develop the edge-colouring tool that lies at the heart of our argument. Throughout the
section, G may be a multigraph.
Definition 2.1. A map f : E(G) → C is called an r-bounded regular colouring if, for each c ∈ C, each
component of the subgraph G[f −1 (c)] is regular of degree at most r.
   For r = 2, we also call f a cycle-matching colouring, since each colour class is then a vertex-disjoint
union of isolated edges and odd cycles.
   The associated chromatic index χ′r (G) is the smallest number of colours admitting an r-bounded
regular colouring of G.
   For r = 1 this definition recovers the standard notion of chromatic index, and so χ′1 (G) = χ′ (G).
As every r-bounded regular colouring is also r′ -bounded regular for r′ ≥ r, the parameter χ′r (G) is
non-increasing in r; in particular it is well defined for every r ∈ N+ .
   By a theorem of Shannon [17], χ′r (G) ≤ χ′ (G) ≤ 23 ∆(G), where the upper bound can be sharpened to
∆(G) + 1 for simple graphs [19]. For r ≥ 2, in [6], the authors introduce the notion of ocm sets, which
are edge sets consisting of vertex disjoint odd cycles and isolated edges, and they prove that every graph
G is covered by exactly ∆(G) ocm sets and no fewer. For them, covered means that an edge is contained
in at least two odd cycles of different ocm sets of the covering or is an isolated edge in at least one of the
sets. This implies
Theorem 2.2 (Cornaz, Nguyen [6]). For every multigraph G, there exists an edge set F ⊆ E(G) that
spans every vertex of maximum degree in G.
    We obtain by an induction argument provided later in this paper:
Corollary 2.3. For every multigraph G and every integer r ≥ 2,
                                               χ′r (G) ≤ ∆(G).
    We shall give a different proof of Theorem 2.2, as we will—to give a specialised lemma needed for the
proof of Theorem 1.4—explore the structure of obstructions to the existence of spanning colour classes.
    Note that the bound in Corollary 2.3 is best possible for any r ≥ 2: for the star K1,s , one has
χ′r (K1,s ) = s for every r ∈ N+ . The lower bound of ∆(G) that applies to the covering by ocm sets does
not apply, as we do not require an edge contained in an odd cycle to be covered by more than one set in
our notion of colouring. Instead, we have χ′r (G) ≥ ∆(G)/r (forced by the edges at a vertex of maximum
degree). This is tight—it is attained, for example, when G decomposes into s edge-disjoint r-regular
spanning subgraphs, in which case χ′r (G) = s = ∆(G)/r.
   To prove Corollary 2.3 we use the classical f -factor theorem of Lovász [11] (see also [12, Theorem 10.2.18]
or [16, Chapter 31]; for a detailed write-up of how to get to the formulation used here, see [13]).

Proof of Corollary 2.3. Induction on ∆(G). The base case ∆(G) = 0 is trivial. For the inductive step,
let H be the multigraph obtained from G by doubling every edge, so that every edge of H has even mul-
tiplicity. By Corollary 2.6 applied to H, there is an f -bounded subgraph H ′ ≤ H (with f ≡ 2) consisting
of disjoint 2-cycles and odd cycles and covering every vertex of maximum degree in H (equivalently, in
G). Replacing each 2-cycle of H ′ by a single edge yields an edge set F ⊆ E(G) in which every component
of G[F ] is 1- or 2-regular and every vertex of maximum degree in G has positive degree in G[F ]. Setting
f (F ) to one colour and applying induction to G − F (whose maximum degree is strictly smaller) extends
f to a cycle-matching colouring of G using at most ∆(G) colours.                                       □
