                                                  Abstract. This paper is a continuation of our previous paper, where we studied non-semistable
                                                  exceptional objects in hereditary categories and introduced the notion of regularity preserving
                                                  category. To obtain examples of such categories we introduced certain conditions on the Ext-
                                                  nontrivial couples and found some examples where these conditions are satisﬁed.
                                                     In this paper we show that for any Dynkin quiver Q the category Repk (Q) does not have Ext-
                                                  nontrivial couples, which implies regularity preserving. We show also that for any two exceptional
                                                  objects X, Y in Repk (Q), where Q is a Dynkin quiver, one of the spaces Hom(X, Y ), Ext1 (X, Y)
                                                  vanishes. In the end we give some directions for future research.

                                             T. Bridgeland introduced in his seminal work [1] the definition of a locally finite stability condition
                                          on a triangulated category T, motivated by the work of Douglas on Π-stability for Dirichlet branes.
                                          He proved that the set of these stability conditions is a complex manifold, denoted by Stab(T).

                                          3.19]. The full σ-exceptional collections are exactly these exceptional collections which generate
                                          σ. In [8] we focus on constructing σ-exceptional collections from a given σ ∈ Stab(D b (A)), where

   1.4 The basic observation of the present paper is that if Q has the property that Hom(ρ, ρ′ ) = 0
or Ext1 (ρ, ρ′ ) = 0 for any two exceptional representations ρ, ρ′ , then the dimension vectors of any
Ext-nontrivial couple {ρ, ρ′ } satisfy hdim(ρ) + dim(ρ′ ), dim(ρ) + dim(ρ′ )i ≤ 0(Lemma 3.1). This

fails (see (17)). This example is obtained by changing the orientation of the quiver Q2 , for which
we showed in [8] that this property holds. In particular we see that the category of representations
changes by changing the orientation of the arrows and keeping the quiver acyclic (Lemma 3.7).

   Some notations. In these notes the letters T and A denote always a triangulated category and
an abelian category, respectively, linear over an algebraically closed field k, the shift functor in T is
designated by [1]. We write Homi (X, Y ) for Hom(X, Y [i]) and homi (X, Y ) for dimk (Hom(X, Y [i])),
where X, Y ∈ T. For X, Y ∈ A, writing Homi (X, Y ), we consider X, Y as elements in T = D b (A),
i.e. Homi (X, Y ) = Exti (X, Y ).
   An exceptional object is an object E ∈ T satisfying Homi (E, E) = 0 for i 6= 0 and Hom(E, E) = k.
We denote by Aexc , resp. D b (A)exc , the set of all exceptional objects of A, resp. of D b (A).
   An exceptional collection is a sequence E = (E1 , E2 , . . . , En ) ⊂ Texc satisfying hom∗ (Ei , Ej ) = 0
for i > j. If in addition we have hEi = T, then E will be called a full exceptional collection.
   An abelian category A is said to be hereditary, if Exti (X, Y ) = 0 for any X, Y ∈ A and i ≥ 2, it
is said to be of finite length, if it is artinian and noterian.
   For any quiver Q we write D b (Q) for D b (Repk (Q)).

                             7. Some directions of future research
     Motivated by Proposition 6.1 and Corollaries 6.2, 6.3 and the results in [8] we conjecture
Conjecture 7.1. For each Dynkin quiver Q and each σ ∈ Stab(D b (Q)) there exists a full σ-
exceptional collection.
  When there are Ext-nontrivial couples, RP property 1 and RP property 2 are our method to
prove regularity-preserving. The fact that they hold not only in Repk (Q1 ), but also in Repk (Q2 )
(Corollary 2.6) seems to be a trace of a larger unexplored picture. We expect that there are further
non-trivial examples of regularity-preserving categories. For example, we conjecture
Conjecture 7.2. For each quiver Q, whose underlying graph is a loop, there is a choice of the
orientation of the arrows such that category Repk (Q) is regularity preserving.
NON-SEMISTABLE EXCEPTIONAL OBJECTS IN HEREDITARY CATEGORIES: SOME REMARKS AND CONJECTURES
                                                                                       21

   We showed in [8] that Repk (Q2 ) is regularity-preserving, but did not answer the question: is
there a σ-exceptional quadruple for each σ ∈ Stab(D b (Q2 )) (the Q2 -analogue of Theorem 1.1). The
results of [8, Sections 7,8, Subsection 9.1] hold for Repk (Q2 ). These are clues for a positive answer.
In section [8, Section 2] are given the dimensions of Hom(X, Y ), Ext1 (X, Y ) for any two exceptional
objects X, Y in Q2 as well. This lays a ground for working on the Q2 -analogue of Theorem 1.1.
   Using the results in Section 2 and Theorem 1.1 we study recently further the topology of
Stab(D b (Repk (Q1 ))) and, in particular, we expect to prove
Conjecture 7.3. Stab(D b (Q1 )) is simply-connected.
Question 7.4. In [8] and in the present paper, the notion of regularity-preserving category was
defined and applied to categories of homological dimension 1. To define and study a relevant notion
for higher dimensional categories is another direction of future research.
