                                                                              Abstract
                                                    We prove that every word is strongly concise in the class of compact
                                                R-analytic groups.


                                          1     Introduction
                                          A word in k-variables is an element w(x1 , . . . , xk ) of the free group F (x1 , . . . , xk ),
                                          and, given any group G, it inherently defines a map w : G(k) → G. The image
                                          of that map, which will be denoted by w{G}, is the set of word-values of w,
                                          and it is typically not a subgroup of G. However, we can naturally associate
                                          two subgroups to w: the verbal subgroup defined as w(G) = hw{G}i, and the
                                          marginal subgroup, that is,
                                                   w∗ (G) = {g ∈ G | w(gx1 , x2 , . . . , xk ) = w(x1 , . . . , gxi , . . . , xk )
                                                                                                   = w(x1 , . . . , gxk )
                                                                                                   = w(x1 , . . . , xk ) ∀xi ∈ G}.
                                              A word is said to be concise in G if w{G} being finite implies that w(G) is
                                          finite. More generally, w is concise in a class C of groups if it is concise in all
                                          the groups G ∈ C, and w is simply called concise if it is concise in any group.
                                          P. Hall initially conjectured that all words were concise, but Ivanov [8] proved
                                          that conjecture to be false. Nevertheless, as Jaikin-Zapirain highlighted in [11],
                                          P. Hall’s conjecture is still open for profinite groups:
                                          Profinite Conciseness Conjecture. Every word is concise in the class of
                                          profinite groups.
                                              There are several articles analysing the conciseness of certain words, but
                                          we know a few classes of groups where all the words are concise. Indeed, it has
                                          been known since the 1960s that linear groups and virtually abelian-by-nilpotent
                                          groups are concise (cf. respectively [13] and [15]). This work provides another
                                          class of concise groups, namely the class of compact R-analytic groups. In other
                                          words, we have the following:

Main Theorem. Every word is concise in the class of compact R-analytic

    R-analytic groups are topological groups endowed with a manifold structure
over a pro-p domain R such that both structures are compatible; they are defined
and thoroughly studied in [6, Chapter 13], we also refer to [12] and [14]. Those
groups arise as a generalisation of p-adic analytic groups. Furthermore, every
pro-p group of finite rank is linear (cf. [6, Theorem 7.19]), so the conciseness of
compact p-adic analytic groups follows from Merzljakov’s result [13]. Compact
p-adic analytic groups being concise is a decisive step in Jaikin-Zapirain’s study
of the verbal width of finitely generated pro-p groups (cf. [11]).

Notation and conventions. Most of the notation is standard, except for X (n) ,
which denotes the nth Cartesian power of X. Besides, p is always a prime num-
ber, Fp is the field of p elements, Zp is the ring of p-adic integers, Q[[X]] is the
ring of power series with coefficients in the ring Q, and dimKrull (Q) and char Q
denote, respectively, the Krull dimension and the characteristic of Q. Finally,
in a topological space S ⊆o X means that S is an open subset.
    Moreover, we recall that a pro-p domain is a Noetherian complete integral
domain, whose residue field is finite of characteristic p. Throughout this paper,
R will always be a pro-p domain with maximal ideal m, which sometimes will
be denoted as (R, m) for briefness; additionally, this notation might be used for
local rings. According to Cohen’s Structure Theorem [2], any pro-p domain R
is a finite integral extension of Q[[t1 , . . . , tm ]], where m = dimKrull (R) − 1, and
Q = Zp when char R = 0 and Q = Fp [[t]] when char R = p.


2     Strong conciseness
In [3] and [4], the notion of conciseness is generalised in profinite groups. Indeed,
the word w is said to be strongly concise in the profinite group G, if |w{G}| < 2ℵ0
implies w(G) to be finite. Moreover, a word is strongly concise in a class C of
groups, if it is strongly concise in any G ∈ C.
Strong Conciseness Conjecture. Every word is strongly concise in the
class of profinite groups.
   However, in the class of compact R-analytic groups strong conciseness is
equivalent to conciseness. Indeed, the following proposition shows that whenever
|w{G}| < 2ℵ0 in a compact R-analytic group, then w{G} is finite.
Proposition 2.1. Let M and N be compact R-analytic manifolds and let
F : M → N be an R-analytic map such that | im F | < 2ℵ0 , then im F is finite.
Proof. Let m = dim M and n = dim N. Since F is analytic, for each x ∈ M
there exists an R-chart (Ux , ϕ) of x in M, an R-chart (V, ψ) of F (x) in N and
a formal power series G ∈ R[[X1 , . . . , Xm ]](n) such that
                     ψ ◦ F ◦ ϕ−1 |ϕ(Ux ) (y) = G(y) ∀y ∈ ϕ(Ux ).

3     R-standard groups
An R-standard group of level N and dimension d is an R-analytic group S with
a global atlas {(S, φ)} such that
                   (d)
  (i) φ : S → mN        is an homeomorphism,
 (ii) φ(1) = 0 and
 (iii) for all j ∈ {1, . . . , d} there exists a formal power series Fj ∈ R[[X1 , . . . , X2d ]],
       with constant term zero, such that

                  φ(xy) = (F1 (φ(x), φ(y)), . . . , Fd (φ(x), φ(y))) ∀x, y ∈ S.

Sometimes we will denote an R-standard group by (S, φ) to emphasise the rôle
of the homeomorphism. Any tuple of power series F = (F1 , . . . , Fd ) satisfying
condition (iii) of the above definition is a d-dimensional formal group law, and
there exists a formal inverse of it, i.e., a tuple of power series I = (I1 , . . . , Id ) ∈
R[[X1 , . . . , Xd ]](d) such that

                             F(I(X), X) = F(X, I(X)) = 0,

where X is a d-tuple of indeterminates (cf. [6, Proposition 13.16(ii)]).
Proposition 3.1. Let S be an R-standard group and let w be a word such that
w{S} is finite, then w(S) = {1}.

5      Proof of the Main Theorem
The demonstration technique consists in reducing the problem to a group that
is analytic over a pro-p domain of Krull dimension one, using the change of
rings described in the preceding section. Hence, we have first to prove the
1-dimensional case.

5.1        Pro-p domains of Krull dimension one
We shall start with a well-known but useful observation, whose proof can be
found, for instance, in [5, Lemma 4].
Lemma 5.1. Let G be a group and let w be a word such that w{G} is finite.
Then w(G)′ is finite, and w(G) is finite if and only if it has finite exponent.
    We have already pointed out that compact p-adic analytic groups are concise.
By Cohen’s Structure Theorem (loc. cit.) and [6, Theorem 13.23] the result
extends to analytic groups over Krull dimension one and characteristic zero
pro-p domains. Moreover, whenever R is a pro-p domain of Krull dimension
one and characteristic p, according to Cohen’s Structure Theorem (loc. cit.)
R is a finitely generated free Fp [[t]]-module, so by [6, Examples 13.9(iv)], any
R-analytic group is by restriction of scalars analytic over Fp [[t]]. Note that in
this case any R-analytic group has also analytic structure over the local field
Fp ((t)) (cf. [6, Section 13.1]).
Proposition 5.2. Let R be a pro-p domain Krull dimension 1. Then every
word is concise in the class of compact R-analytic groups.
Proof. We only have to deal with the case of positive characteristic. Let G be a
compact R-analytic group, let w be a word such that w{G} is finite, Z = Z(G)
and K = Fp ((t)). We can assume that G is K-analytic.
    Firstly, by [9, Proposition 5.1] G/Z is a linear group over the local field
K that, by Proposition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2, virtually satisfies an identity, so
by the Topological Tits Alternative (cf. [1, Theorem 1.3]) G/Z has an open
soluble subgroup. Let G be the Zariski closure of G/Z in GLn (K), which is also
virtually soluble (cf. [16, Theorem 5.11]). Let N be the largest normal soluble
subgroup of G and N ◦ its connected component, i.e., the soluble radical of the

                                                  9
algebraic group G. Then N has finite index in G, and so, by [7, Proposition 7.3],
N ◦ has finite index in G. Let N/Z be the intersection of G/Z with N ◦ , then N
has finite index in G.
    Since [N ◦ , G] is unipotent, (cf. [7, Lemma 19.5]) and as we are working
in characteristic p, then [N, G]Z/Z is nilpotent of finite exponent. Therefore,
[N, G]Z is nilpotent, and, according to [12, Lemma 5.1], H = [[N, G]Z, G] has
finite exponent. Finally,

                      H = [[N, G]Z, G] = [N, G, G] ≥ [N, N, N ],

and so G/H is virtually nilpotent of class at most 3.
    Furthermore, since w(G)′ is finite by Lemma 5.1, up to a quotient we can
assume that w(G) is a finitely generated abelian subgroup. Hence, since H has
finite exponent, w(G) ∩ H is finite.
    Finally, since |w{G/H}| ≤ |w{G}| and G/H is virtually nilpotent, then

is finite by [15, Corollary 2], so this yields the result.
   More generally, we have proved that any soluble R-analytic group, where R
has characteristic p, is virtually nilpotent of finite exponent-by-nilpotent.

5.2     General case
