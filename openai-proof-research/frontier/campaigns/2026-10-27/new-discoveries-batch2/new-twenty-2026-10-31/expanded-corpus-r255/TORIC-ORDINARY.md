# 327188: closed ordinary endpoint, routine/source-data exclusion risk

Figure 1.4 and Table 3.1 of 1402.0060v2 give exponent sets E4 = {(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(0,1),(0,−1)} and E5 with (0,−1) replaced by (−1,−1). The evaluation torus is (F8*)². On functions on this torus put T(f)(x,y) = x³ f(x⁻¹,x⁴y). The torus substitution is an involution and x³ is nonzero. Thus T is a monomial coordinate transformation. Since x⁷ = 1, its images on the six basis monomials 1,x,x²,x³,y,y⁻¹ are respectively x³,x²,x,1,y,x⁻¹y⁻¹. Hence T maps C(E4) onto C(E5). This proves the literal code equivalence, with no computational search and no unresolved algebraic bridge.

The concern is eligibility, not proof. The source's Table 3.1 already prints (6,−1) as an alternate representative for P6(4) and (−1,−1) for P6(5); these are identical modulo 7. A one-line use of the usual modular-exponent torus equivalence is therefore a source-data/corollary bridge, likely excluded under the user's express rule. Do not count it as a significant new campaign solution. The 2021 citing paper by Cairncross, Ford, Garcia and Jabbusch (actual body, Remark 3.5, pp.615–616) still reports the six-dimensional F8 pair as unresolved and unsuccessful computational attempts, but that does not override the campaign significance standard.
