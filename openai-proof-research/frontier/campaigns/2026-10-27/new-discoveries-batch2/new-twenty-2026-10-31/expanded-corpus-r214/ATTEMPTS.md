## 342385: literal Kasami design codes

The source is Tang, *Infinite families of 3-designs from APN functions*, formula (4), Theorem 13, Conjecture 7. For q = 2^n and admissible i, put s = 2^(2i) - 2^i + 1 and

B = GF(q) minus { ((x+1)^s + x^s + 1)^(inverse of 2^i+1 mod q-1) : x in GF(q) }.

The code is the binary span of the GA_1(q)-orbit of B. The source reports Magma checks at n = 5, 7. `kasami.py` and `kasami13.py` construct that literal block and its orbit span. Multiplication by x is checked to have order q-1 in each chosen polynomial representation. The affine group is generated by this multiplication and translations by an additive basis. Starting from B, row reduction and closure under those generators therefore compute the entire orbit span, without enumerating all q(q-1) blocks. All computation uses integer bitsets and polynomial arithmetic over GF(2).

Computed dimensions, in increasing admissible i:

- n = 5, i = 1,2: 11, 21.
- n = 7, i = 1,2,3: 15, 64, 36.
- n = 9, i = 1,2,4: 19, 184, 46.
- n = 11, i = 1,2,3,4,5: 23, 496, 276, 133, 56.
- n = 13, i = 1,2,3,4,5,6: 27, 1262, 339, 157, 677, 66.

They are pairwise distinct within each tested n. Dimension is invariant under coordinate permutations, so these computations support finite inequivalence, never a counterexample. The n = 13 run took about 90 s wall and sampled 36.2 MB RSS under the wrapper. Full bases and blocks are retained. No assertion that these finite checks are new to the literature is made. A uniform rank formula separating every admissible index remains missing. The primitive polynomial choice and arithmetic are computational diagnostics, not Lean-certified finite fields. Related Lean Pool Kasami cyclic-additive and APN/almost-bent projects concern adjacent claims; metadata does not establish a proof of this inequivalence target.

## 342736: covering hypergraphs

An Euler tour is a closed walk using each hyperedge exactly once, with consecutive anchor vertices distinct and both in that edge. The exact DFS state is (used-edge mask, current anchor); for a fixed starting anchor it exhausts every permitted next edge and anchor. All orientations of the first edge are tried, removing only cyclic duplication of a possible tour. Returning to the start is required after the final edge.

Every ell-covering k-uniform hypergraph with ell >= 2 and at least k vertices is 2-covering. Thus testing 2-covering is the strongest covering test for these finite orders.

- n = 6, k = 4: all 32,752 labelled simple edge subsets with at least two edges, of which 27,626 are 2-covering. Every covering instance passed.
- n = 7, k = 5: all 2,097,130 labelled simple edge subsets with at least two edges, of which 2,062,844 are 2-covering. Every covering instance passed.

The second run took about 76.9 s wall, sampled 16.7 MB RSS. Repeated edges were not included. The source proves quasi-eulerianity and eulerianity for (k-1)-covering. The later Sajna-Wagner paper *Covering hypergraphs are eulerian* also uses (k-1)-covering; it does not dispose of all ell. A possible full proof would need to merge all components of a chosen Euler family while preserving the even-degree selection in the incidence graph. No new merging lemma was established. The source already develops alternating-cycle switching, so repackaging that technique alone would not qualify.

## 344348: q-series signs

For each m, multiply truncated formal power series by 1/(1-q^j) for j congruent to 1 mod m, and by 1/(1+q^j) for j congruent to -1 mod m. The ascending update a[n] += a[n-j], respectively a[n] -= a[n-j], is exact for these inverses. Terms above the truncation cannot affect lower coefficients.

Two sweeps completed with no negative coefficient:

- all m = 4..100, degrees 0..1000;
- all m = 4..40, degrees 0..10000.

The larger run took about 18.1 s wall. Result files retain per-m exact-vector hashes, zero counts and any negative values; full arrays are reproducible from the script. A published proof of m = 4 by Craig, and Chern's preceding asymptotic work, do not settle all m. Elementary pairing can make 1/((1-q^a)(1+q^b)) positive when a divides b, but assigning distinct positive denominator factors this way does not cover the negative factors of the full product. No complete injection or uniform coefficient bound resulted.

## 344800: fixed-multiplicity semigroup counts

All numerical semigroups through genus 28 were generated by deleting effective minimal generators x in [conductor, conductor+m-1]. The multiplicity m is recomputed for every node. The bit representation extends through 2G+2, sufficient because Frobenius <= 2g-1 for positive genus. A genus-g child has a unique parent obtained by adjoining its Frobenius number; the enumeration therefore has no duplicate child-parent histories. The genus-0 conductor is treated as 1 for this implementation.

The genus-28 level has 2,091,030 semigroups. Fixed multiplicity counts were compared for every adjacent completed level, with no violation. The scope is only g <= 27 for inequalities N_m(g) <= N_m(g+1). These levels are below historical exhaustive ranges; this is not a discovery claim.

An initial implementation incorrectly used conductor 0 for the ordinary genus-0 semigroup, producing empty later levels. Its log is preserved and explicitly invalid. The corrected `semigroup20-fixed` and `semigroup28` runs produce the standard initial counts 1,1,2,4,7,12,23,... and the stated final level. The invalid preliminary output was replaced by the corrected output; no HTTP receipt was overwritten.

An injection obtained by selecting a child in the semigroup tree cannot work universally: e.g. <3,5> is a leaf with its minimal generators below conductor. In Kunz coordinates, increasing every coordinate preserves inequalities but adds m-1 to genus. Increasing a minimum coordinate can have collisions: for m = 3, (1,2) and (2,1) can both go to (2,2). Neither observation resolves the conjecture.

## 346262: short parity bridge, not nominated

Let A_n = |VHC(S_(n-1))|. Source Corollary 7.3 gives A(z) = -log Q(z). In exponential-series normalization q_0 = 1, q_(2j+1) = (-1)^(j+1) Catalan(j), and positive even coefficients q_(2j) = 0. From QA' = -Q' one gets

A_n = -q_n - sum_(i=1)^(n-1) binom(n-1,i) q_i A_(n-i).

The script verifies exact integers through n = 512, with no parity failure. Here is the short ordinary argument explaining why that route is excluded as a source-formula bridge. For odd n, n-1 is even and binom(n-1,i) is even at every odd i by Lucas, so A_n = q_n mod 2. Catalan(j) is odd exactly when j+1 is a power of two, giving precisely n+1 a power of two. For even n, substitute this already known odd-index parity into the recurrence and pair i with n-i. Paired coefficients sum to binom(n,i), which is even for odd i. The possible unpaired middle i=n/2 is odd; its Catalan factor vanishes unless i is a Mersenne number, and binom(2i-1,i) is even for every odd i>1 (the binary carry criterion). Only n=2 is exceptional, outside the conjecture's n>=3 range. Thus every even n>=4 has even A_n. This is a short deduction from the source's EGF and classical Catalan/binomial parity. It is not submitted as a mathematically significant campaign discovery, and no Lean packet was built.

## 340116: literal duplex diagnostics

Generated every reduced Latin square of orders 2 through 6. Counts are 1,1,4,56,9408. For each, choose two distinct cells in each row, with column and symbol counts bounded by 2. At completion their sums force every count to equal 2. Every square passed. Row/column/symbol relabelling preserves duplex existence, so reduction loses no such square at those orders. Earlier literature reports exhaustive checks through order 9; this is validation within an already known range.
