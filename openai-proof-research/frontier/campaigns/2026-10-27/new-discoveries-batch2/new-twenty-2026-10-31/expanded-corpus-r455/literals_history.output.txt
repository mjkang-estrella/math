325313 Let $(x_i)_{i\geq 1}$ be a sequence of positive integers with $1 \leq x_i \leq K$. Two consecutive intervals of positive integers $I,J$ have the same length if they contain the same number of integers.

**Brown–Freedman conjecture.** There are two consecutive intervals $I,J$ of the same length such that

$$
\sum_{i\in I}x_i=\sum_{j\in J}x_j.
$$

Equivalently, if $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ satisfy $a_{n+1}-a_n\leq K$ for all $n$, then there exist $i<j<k$ such that

$$
i+k=2j\quad\text{and}\quad a_i+a_k=2a_j.
$$

This is a well-known open problem in combinatorics of words and is equivalent to asking whether every infinite word on a finite alphabet of positive integers contains an additive square, namely two adjacent blocks of equal length and equal sum. A positive answer would also imply the stated special case of the Erdős conjecture on arithmetic progressions.
