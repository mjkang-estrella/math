30:                                                                                             1. Introduction
31:                                            1.1. Background. Let P be an n-element poset, whose order relation we denote by <P . A labeling
32:                                            of P is a bijection L : P → [n] (where [n] = {1, . . . , n}). A labeling L is called a linear extension if
33:                                            it preserves the order on P , i.e. if for all pairs x, y ∈ P with x <P y we have L(x) < L(y). Let Λ(P )
34:                                            be the set of all labelings of P ; let L(P ) ⊂ Λ(P ) be the subset consisting of all linear extensions.
35:                                                 In [Sch63, Sch72, Sch76], Schützenberger introduced an intriguing bijection on L(P ) called
36:                                            promotion. Promotion has connections with various topics in algebraic combinatorics and represen-
37:                                            tation theory, as seen in [EG87, Hua20, PPR09, Rho10, Sta09].
38:                                                In [DK22], Defant and Kravitz extended the promotion map to an operator ∂ : Λ(P ) → Λ(P ),
39:                                            not necessarily invertible, that is deﬁned on all of Λ(P ). When the poset is a chain, extended
40:                                            promotion is dynamically equivalent to the bubble-sort map studied in [Knu98]. Promotion can
41:                                            also be described in terms of Bender-Knuth involutions (ﬁrst introduced by Haiman [Hai92] as
42:                                            well as Malvenuto and Reutenauer [MR94]); in [DK22], Defant and Kravitz extended these Bender-
43:                                            Knuth involutions to arrive at an equivalent “toggle” deﬁnition of extended promotion. The following
44:                                            results of [DK22] are crucial properties of extended promotion:
45:                                            (1) When restricted to L(P ), ∂ agrees with Schützenberger’s promotion operator.
46:                                            (2) If L is a labeling of an n-element poset P , then ∂ n−1 (L) ∈ L(P ).
47:                                            Thus, (extended) promotion1 may be regarded as a sorting operator, where linear extensions are
48:                                            considered “sorted.” Property (2) shows that promotion sorts every labeling after at most n − 1
49:                                            applications.
50:                                                We deﬁne the sorting time of a labeling L to be the smallest k ∈ N such that ∂ k (L) ∈ L(P ).
51:                                            Defant and Kravitz mainly studied tangled labelings—those labelings with sorting time n − 1.
52:                                            1Henceforth, “promotion” always refers to ∂ rather than its restriction to L(P ).

97:        2. Preliminaries, Frozen Elements, and Some Special Classes of Posets
98: 2.1. Preliminary Definitions. A lower order ideal of a poset P is a subset Q ⊂ P such that for
99: every x ∈ Q and y ∈ P with y <P x we have y ∈ Q. Similarly, an upper order ideal of a poset P is
100: a subset Q ⊂ P such that for all x ∈ Q and y ∈ P with y >P x we have y ∈ Q. It is often useful
101: to note that Q is a lower order ideal of P if and only if P \ Q is an upper order ideal of P . For
102: x, y ∈ P , we say that y covers x and write x ⋖ y if x <P y and {z ∈ P | x <P z <P y} = ∅. In this
103: case, we say that y is a parent of x and that x is a child of y.
104:     The Hasse diagram of a poset P is a graphical illustration of its covering relations. Each element
105: of P is represented by a vertex, and if x <P y, then the vertex corresponding to x is drawn below
106: that corresponding to y; there exists an edge between these vertices if and only if x ⋖P y. We say
107: a poset is connected if its Hasse diagram is connected when regarded as a graph; the connected
108: components of P are the subposets induced by the connected components of the Hasse diagram of
109: P.
