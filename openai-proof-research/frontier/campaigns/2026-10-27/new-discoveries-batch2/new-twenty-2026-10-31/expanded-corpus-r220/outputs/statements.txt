366512 Guy's equality conjecture for complete graphs in specified genus ranges
Let $K_n$ be the complete graph, let $S_t$ be the orientable surface of genus $t$, and let $\delta_t(K_n)$ and $\mu_t(K_n)$ denote its Euler excess and skewness, respectively. **Guy's equality conjecture.** If $n=6m$ for $m\geq 1$, then

$$
m-1\leq t\leq 2m^2-3m+1
$$

implies

$$
\delta_t(K_n)=\mu_t(K_n).
$$

The claim is presented as a consequence of a construction based on pair-of-pants cobordisms and handles, and gives equality throughout the stated genus interval for this divisibility class.
SOURCE Paul C. Kainen, “Skewness, crossing number and Euler's bound for graphs on surfaces”, arXiv:2501.02400 (2025).

366515 Weighted-graph third-eigenvalue conjecture
For $n\geq 2$, let $\mathcal{S}_n$ be the set of $n\times n$ real symmetric matrices with entries in $[0,1]$, and let $\lambda_{n-1}(M)$ denote the penultimate eigenvalue of $M$. **Weighted-graph conjecture.** The function $\lambda_{n-1}:\mathcal{S}_n\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfies

$$
-\frac{n}{3} \le \inf_{M\in\mathcal{S}_n}\lambda_{n-1}(M).
$$

This is the weighted-matrix extension of the extremal third-eigenvalue problem; the supplied text presents it as a conjecture and gives no resolution.
SOURCE Giacomo Leonida and Sida Li, “On graphs with large third eigenvalue”, arXiv:2501.02563 (2026).

366563 MSO obstruction characterization for bounded linear cliquewidth
Let $\mathscr{C}$ be a class of graphs. A class is **bounded linear cliquewidth** when its linear cliquewidth is bounded, and $\mathsf{CMSO}$ denotes counting monadic second-order logic.

**Linear cliquewidth CMSO obstruction conjecture.** A class of graphs $\mathscr{C}$ has bounded linear cliquewidth if and only if the class of trees cannot be $\mathsf{CMSO}$-transduced from $\mathscr{C}$.

The analogous characterization for cliquewidth is known with the class of all graphs in place of trees, but the linear-cliquewidth version remains open according to the survey.
SOURCE Michał Pilipczuk, “Graph classes through the lens of logic”, arXiv:2501.04166 (2025).

366634 Baranyai–Katona wreath conjecture
Let $k\leq n$ be positive integers. Write $\mathbb{Z}_{n}$ for the integers modulo $n$, and let $\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{(k)}$ be the set of $k$-element subsets of $\mathbb{Z}_{n}$. A **wreath** is a set of the form $\mathcal{F}_{\pi}$ obtained from a permutation $\pi$ of $\mathbb{Z}_{n}$ by taking the cyclically indexed blocks of $k$ consecutive values, as defined in the paper.

**The wreath conjecture.** For any positive integers $k\leq n$, there is a decomposition of $\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{(k)}$ into disjoint wreaths.

This conjecture generalizes Baranyai's factorization theorem for complete uniform hypergraphs. It was posed by Baranyai and reformulated in terms of wreaths by Katona; the source does not state a resolution.
SOURCE Jan Petr and Pavel Turek, “Intervals in Dyck paths and the wreath conjecture”, arXiv:2501.07277 (2025).

366697 The AB-graph conjecture for maximal k-covers
Let $S\subseteq \mathbb{F}_2^n$ be a **$k$-cover**, and suppose that

$$
|S|=2^{n/2}.
$$

An **AB function** is a function $F\colon\mathbb{F}_2^{n/2}\to\mathbb{F}_2^{n/2}$ whose graph is denoted by $\mathcal{G}_F$; two sets are affinely equivalent when they are related by an affine transformation. **AB-graph conjecture.** If $S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_2^n$ is a $k$-cover with $|S|=2^{n/2}$, then there exists an AB function $F\colon\mathbb{F}_2^{n/2}\to\mathbb{F}_2^{n/2}$ such that $\mathcal{G}_F$ is affinely equivalent to $S$. The theorem preceding the conjecture shows that every such $k$-cover gives a bent function, while graphs of AB functions provide examples; the conjecture asserts that these are the only examples up to affine equivalence.
SOURCE Darrion Thornburgh, “On generalizing cryptographic results to Sidon sets in F_2^n”, arXiv:2501.11184 (2025).

366751 Submodular-cut extension conjecture for oriented matroids

366827 Permutation criterion for two families of pentanomials over finite fields
Let $m$ and $k$ be positive integers and set $q=2^m$. Define

$$
f_1(x)=x^{2^k+1}+x^{2^kq^2+1}+x^{q+2^k}+x^{q^2+2^k}+x^{2^kq+1}
$$

and

$$
f_2(x)=x^{2^k+1}+x^{(2^k+1)q}+x^{q+2^k}+x^{q^2+2^k}+x^{2^kq^2+q}.
$$

**Permutation criterion.** The polynomials $f_1$ and $f_2$ are permutation polynomials over $\mathbb{F}_{q^3}$ if and only if

$$
\operatorname{gcd}(2^k+1,q-1)=1.
$$

This conjecture is based on numerical results concerning permutation pentanomials over finite fields of characteristic two; the supplied text does not establish whether the criterion has been proved or disproved.
SOURCE Tongliang Zhang, Lijing Zheng, Hengtai Wang, Jie Peng and Yanjun Li, “Further results on permutation pentanomials over F_q^3 in characteristic two”, arXiv:2501.15741 (2025).

366899 Kruft's terminal-set conjecture
Let $G$ be a graph. A set $S\subseteq V(G)$ is a **terminal set** if $S$ is a general position set of $G$ and adding any vertex $u\in V(G)-S$ to $S$ creates three-in-a-line with $u$ as an endpoint. **Kruft's conjecture.** Every graph has a terminal set.

Terminal sets correspond to maximal general position sets contained in a layer of a Cartesian product. The conjecture is known for graphs of diameter at most three, cographs, and chordal graphs, but remains open in general.
SOURCE Ullas Chandran S. V., Sandi Klavžar and James Tuite, “The General Position Problem: A Survey”, arXiv:2501.19385 (2026).

366962 Strong contextuality conjecture for universally transversal code-switching protocols
Let $\mathcal{Q}_1$ and $\mathcal{Q}_2$ be subsystem stabilizer codes, and consider a fault-tolerant code-switching protocol between them. Let the **set of transversal gates** for each code be the gates implementable transversally on that code. **Strong contextuality conjecture.** If the union of the set of transversal gates for $\mathcal{Q}_1$ and that for $\mathcal{Q}_2$ forms a universal gate set, then the code-switching protocol must be strongly contextual in a partial closure. This is proposed as a no-go principle for noncontextual routes to transversal universality through code-switching; the supplied text gives no resolution, so the conjecture remains open.
SOURCE Derek Khu, Andrew Tanggara, Chao Jin and Kishor Bharti, “Contextuality of Quantum Error-Correcting Codes”, arXiv:2502.02553 (2026).

367074 Bensmail–Filasto–Hocquard–Marcille conjecture on mixed local irregularity
A sink-source path is an oriented path containing an arc directed into a vertex and an arc directed out of that vertex, as used in the source. For $a,b\in\{+,-\}$, define $(a,b)$-local irregularity by requiring $d^a(u)\neq d^b(v)$ on every arc $uv$, and let $\mathrm{lir}^{(+,-)}(D)$ be the corresponding chromatic index. **Bensmail–Filasto–Hocquard–Marcille conjecture.** Every digraph $D$ without a sink-source path satisfies

$$
\mathrm{lir}^{(+,-)}(D)\leq 3.

The source gives a constant upper bound of seven for digraphs without a sink-source path, but does not report that the conjectured bound three has been attained.

367093 Häggkvist's avoidability conjecture for $(m,m,m)$-arrays
An $(m,m,m)$-array is an $n\times n$ array of sets of size at most $m$, such that each number in $[n]$ occurs at most $m$ times among the sets in any row or column. It is **avoidable** if there exists an $n\times n$ Latin square $L$ such that no entry of $L$ is contained in the set in the corresponding cell of the array. **Häggkvist's avoidability conjecture.** There exists a constant $\gamma>0$ such that if $m<\gamma n$, then every $(m,m,m)$-array is avoidable. This conjecture asks for a uniform linear range in which all such arrays admit an avoiding Latin square; the source gives no resolution here.
SOURCE Candida Bowtell, Alice Devillers, André Kündgen, Padraig Ó Catháin and Ian M. Wanless, “Extendibility of Latin Hypercuboids”, arXiv:2502.08868 (2025).

367097 Positivity conjecture for permanents of polystochastic matrices
A $d$-dimensional polystochastic matrix of order $n$ is a nonnegative array whose sums over every line are equal to $1$. Its permanent is

$$
\operatorname{per} A=\sum_{\ell\in D(A)}\prod_{\alpha\in\ell}a_{\alpha},
$$

where $D(A)$ is the set of diagonals of $A$, and a diagonal is a collection of $n$ indices such that no two share the same hyperplane. **Positivity conjecture.** Every polystochastic matrix of even dimension or odd order has a positive permanent. For odd dimension at least $3$ and even order, examples of polystochastic matrices with zero permanent are known, while no such matrices are known for the remaining parameter values; the conjecture asserts positivity in those remaining cases.
SOURCE Anna A. Taranenko, “Enumeration and constructions of vertices of the polytope of polystochastic matrices”, arXiv:2502.09149 (2025).

367150 The double Hall path-cover conjecture
Let $G=(X,Y)$ be a dHp bigraph. Write $\mathsf{\Lambda}_G(X)$ for the neighborhood of $X$ in $Y$, and let a **$Y$-$Y$ path** be a path whose two endpoints lie in $Y$.

**Double Hall path-cover conjecture.** If

$$
|\mathsf{\Lambda}_G(X)|\geq |X|+1,
$$

then there exists a collection of disjoint $Y$-$Y$ paths whose union covers all of $X$.

The paper presents this as a conjecture weaker than Salia's cycle conjecture and proves that it is equivalent to another such weakening. Its general status is open.

367173 Ryser's maximum permanent conjecture for regular (0,1)-matrices

**Ryser's conjecture.** The permanent of $J^*$ is maximal in $\mathscr{U}(v,k)$.
