Turán densities for matroid basis hypergraphs
Jorn van der Pol
Note: University of Twente, Email: j.g.vanderpol@utwente.nl
Zach Walsh
Note: Auburn University, Email: zwalsh@auburn.edu
Michael C. Wigal
Note: University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign, Email: wigal@illinois.edu
August 24, 2026
Abstract
Let  $U$  be a uniform matroid.
For all positive integers  $n$  and  $r$  with  $n\geq r$ , what is the maximum number of bases of an  $n$ -element, rank- $r$  matroid without  $U$  as a minor?
We show that this question arises by restricting the problem of determining the Turán number of a daisy hypergraph to the family of matroid basis hypergraphs.
We then answer this question for several interesting choices of  $U$ .
1 Introduction
For a matroid  $M$ , we write  $b(M)$  for the number of bases of  $M$ . Classic results of Björner [9] and Purdy [37] were concerned with minimizing  $b(M)$  over all matroids  $M$ .
This note is concerned with maximizing the number of bases in matroids. Without any restrictions, this problem is trivial, as for any fixed size and rank, a uniform matroid uniquely maximizes the number of bases. To make this problem interesting, we place structural restrictions on the matroids considered. For a matroid  $N$ , we write
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,N)=\max\{b(M):\text{$M$ is an $n$-element, rank-$r$, $N$-minor-free matroid}\}$ 
and
 $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,N)}{\binom{n}{r}}.$ 
Standard averaging arguments show that  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  and  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  exist – see Propositions 3.1 and 3.2. We say that the numbers  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,N)$ ,  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$ , and  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  are the Turán basis number, Turán basis density, and link Turán basis density of the matroid  $N$ . We will focus on the case in which  $N$  is a uniform matroid  $U_{s,t}$ .
Problem 1.1.
Let  $s$  and  $t$  be positive integers with  $t\geq s$ .
 $(i)$  
For all integers  $n$  and  $r$  with  $n\geq r\geq s$ , determine  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,t})$ .
 $(ii)$  
For all integers  $r$  with  $r\geq s$ , determine  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{s,t})$ .
 $(iii)$  
Determine  $\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{s,t})$ .
While we believe that this problem is interesting from a purely matroidal perspective, it has close connections to several interesting related problems as well.
We comment that while several past works bound the number of bases of a matroid in terms of other parameters such as number of circuits [16] or sizes of corcircuits [17], we bound the number of bases in terms of rank and number of elements.
Our first main result is the following.
Theorem 1.2.
Let  $n$  and  $r$  be integers and let  $t$  be a prime power such that  $r\geq 2$  and  $n\geq\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ . Then
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})\leq\frac{n^{r}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(t^{r}-t^{i})}{r!\cdot(t^{r}-1)^{r}}$ 
with equality when  $n$  is a multiple of  $\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ .
In particular,
 $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{2,t+2})=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-t^{-i}).$ 
Theorem 1.2 was inspired by a recent breakthrough result of Ellis, Ivan, and Leader [18] regarding vertex Turán problems for the hypercube.
They found a construction that gives a lower bound for the limit of Theorem 1.2, which has had widereaching consequences throughout combinatorics, see e.g. [1, 2, 26]. Our matching upper bound of Theorem 1.2 shows that one must look beyond matroid basis hypergraphs to improve upon their work. We discuss this further in Section 2.
We also obtain bounds for forbidding  $U_{s,s+1}$ -minors.
The number  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,s+1})$  is particularly natural: it is the maximum number of bases among all  $n$ -element rank- $r$  matroids with circumference at most  $s$ , where circumference is the number of elements in a largest circuit of the matroid.
We determine  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})$  for all  $r\geq 3$ .
Theorem 1.3.
Let  $r$  be an integer with  $r\geq 3$ . Then  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})=\frac{r!\cdot 2^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}}{r^{r}}$ .
In particular,  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,4})=4/9$ . We can interpret  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(s,U_{s,t})$  as a specialization of the well-known hypergraph Turán density parameter  $\pi(K_{t}^{(s)})$ , where  $K_{t}^{(s)}$  is the  $s$ -uniform hypergraph on  $t$  vertices. This problem is particularly well-studied in the case  $t=s+1$  [4, 31, 38]. We discuss this further in Section 2.
Finally, in Section 6, we focus on  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,t})$ .
Our third main result is as follows.
Theorem 1.4.
 $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,5})=3/4$ .
This confirms a famous conjecture of Turán [41], that  $\pi(K_{5}^{(3)})=3/4$ , in the special case of matroid basis hypergraphs.
In addition to Theorem 1.4, we also prove two structural results (Theorems 6.2 and 6.3) for rank- $3$  matroids with no  $U_{3,t}$ -restriction. These may provide a future approach for finding  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,t})$ , and may be of independent interest to matroid theorists and finite geometers.
The rest of this note is organized as follows. In Section 2, we will outline preliminaries regarding matroids and hypergraphs. In Section 3 we will show that the limits of Problem 1.1 exist and discuss characterizations of matroid basis hypergraphs, including a characterization via induced Turán numbers as introduced by Loh, Tait, Timmons, and Zhou [30].
In Section 4 we will prove Theorem 1.2.
In Section 5 we will prove Theorem 1.3 and present a conjecture for  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,U_{s,s+1})$ . In Section 6 we will prove Theorem 1.4 along with the structural properties of Theorems 6.2 and 6.3. Finally, in Section 7, we will discuss some related problems and directions for future work.
2 Preliminaries
In this section we will give a brief overview of relevant matroid terminology and how it relates to hypergraphs. We refer the reader to [35] for any notation that remains undefined and for background information regarding matroids.
2.1 Matroids
A matroid  $M$  is a pair  $(E,\mathcal{B})$  where  $E$  is a finite set and  $\mathcal{B}$  is a non-empty family of subsets of  $E$  that satisfies the following property:
(B1) 
For all  $B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}$  and  $x\in B_{1}-B_{2}$ , there exists  $y\in B_{2}-B_{1}$  so that  $(B_{1}-\{x\})\cup\{y\}\in\mathcal{B}$ .
The set  $E$  is the ground set of  $M$ , the sets in  $\mathcal{B}$  are the bases of  $M$ , and property (B1) is the basis-exchange property.
We often write  $\mathcal{B}(M)$  for the set of bases of a matroid  $M$ .
We will take the standard matroid theory convention and write  $X-x$  for  $X-\{x\}$  for a set  $X$  with an element  $x$ .
It follows from (B1) that all sets in  $\mathcal{B}$  have the same cardinality, which is the rank of  $M$ .
So, every rank- $r$  matroid  $M=(E,\mathcal{B})$  can be viewed as an  $r$ -uniform hypergraph with vertex set  $E$  and edge set  $\mathcal{B}$ ; we say that this hypergraph is the basis hypergraph of  $M$ .
We write  $\mathsf{M}$  for the class of matroid basis hypergraphs.
Matroid basis hypergraphs are not particularly well-studied, although they do play a central role in several recent works [11, 14, 32].
Let  $M=(E,\mathcal{B})$  be a matroid.
We say that  $M$  is representable over a field  $\mathbb{F}$  is there is a matrix over  $\mathbb{F}$  with columns indexed by  $E$  so that the elements of  $\mathcal{B}$  are precisely those subsets of columns that form a basis of the column space.
While not every matroid is representable, much of the standard matroid terminology is inspired by this connection to linear algebra.
The rank  $r(X)$  of a set  $X\subseteq E$  is the cardinality of a maximum subset of  $X$  contained in a basis.
A loop of  $M$  is an element that is not in any basis,
and a coloop is an element that is in every basis.
A circuit of  $M$  is a minimal set of elements that does not appear in any basis.
A point of  $M$  is a maximal rank- $1$  set, and a line of  $M$  is a maximal rank- $2$  set.
The closure  $\cl_{M}(X)$  of a set  $X\subseteq E$  is the maximal set of rank  $r(X)$  that contains  $X$ .
Non-loop elements of  $M$  are parallel if they are not in a common basis, and the equivalence classes under this relation are the parallel classes of  $M$ .
Restricting the ground set of  $M$  to consist of one element from each parallel class gives a new matroid  $\si(M)$  called the simplification of  $M$ .
A matroid is simple if it is equal to its simplification.
The deletion of a non-coloop element  $e$  from  $M$  is the matroid  $M\!\setminus\!e=(E-e,\mathcal{B}^{\prime})$ , where  $\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\{B\in\mathcal{B}\colon e\notin B\}$ , and the contraction of a non-loop element  $e$  from  $M$  is the matroid  $M/e=(E-e,\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime})$ , where  $\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}=\{B-e\colon e\in B\in\mathcal{B}\}$ .
If  $e$  is a coloop, we define  $M\!\setminus\!e$  to be  $M/e$ , and if  $e$  is a loop, we define  $M/e$  to be  $M\!\setminus\!e$ .
A matroid  $N$  is a minor of  $M$  if it can be obtained from  $M$  by a sequence of deletions and contractions.
Reordering the sequence of deletions and contractions does not change the minor, so for disjoint sets  $C,D\subseteq E$  we write  $M/C\!\setminus\!D$  for the matroid obtained from  $M$  by contracting the elements in  $C$  and deleting the elements in  $D$ .
We write  $M|X$  for  $M\!\setminus\!(E-X)$ , the restriction of  $M$  to  $X$ .
The dual of  $M=(E,\mathcal{B})$  is the matroid  $M^{*}$  on ground set  $E$  whose bases are the complements of bases of  $M$ .
Finally, the direct sum of matroids  $M_{1}=(E_{1},\mathcal{B}_{1})$  and  $M_{2}=(E_{2},\mathcal{B}_{2})$  with disjoint ground sets is the matroid  $M_{1}\oplus M_{2}=(E_{1}\cup E_{2},\mathcal{B})$ , where  $\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1}\cup B_{2}\colon B_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}\textrm{ and }B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{2}\}$ .
2.2 Hypergraphs
We will also use some standard notation for hypergraphs.
Given a hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}=(V,F)$ , a subgraph of  $\mathcal{H}$  is a hypergraph obtained from  $\mathcal{H}$  by deleting vertices and edges, and an induced subgraph is a hypergraph obtained from  $\mathcal{H}$  by deleting vertices.
For a hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}=(V,F)$  and  $W\subseteq V$  we write  $\mathcal{H}\!\setminus\!W$  for the hypergraph obtained by deleting the vertices in  $W$ , and we write  $\mathcal{H}/W$  for the link of  $\mathcal{H}$  at  $W$ , which is the hypergraph with vertex set  $V-W$  and edge set  $\{X-W\colon W\subseteq X\in F\}$ .
Note that if  $\mathcal{H}$  is the basis hypergraph of a matroid  $M$ , then  $\mathcal{H}\!\setminus\!v$  is the basis hypergraph of  $M\!\setminus\!v$  (if  $v$  is a non-coloop of  $M$ ), and  $\mathcal{H}/v$  is the basis hypergraph of  $M/v$  (if  $v$  is a non-loop of  $M$ ).
We write  $|\mathcal{H}|$  for the number of edges of  $\mathcal{H}$ .
We will refer to  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs as  $r$ -graphs and to  $2$ -graphs as graphs.
2.3 Extremal problems for matroids and hypergraphs
We now explain how Problem 1.1 arises by restricting Turán parameters to matroid basis hypergraphs.
Given an  $r$ -graph  $\mathcal{H}$ , let  $\ex(n,\mathcal{H})$  be the maximum number of edges of an  $n$ -vertex  $r$ -graph with no subgraph isomorphic to  $\mathcal{H}$ .
Then let
 $\pi(\mathcal{H})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ex(n,\mathcal{H})/\binom{n}{r}$ ; it is well-known that this limit exists.
The parameters  $\ex(n,\mathcal{H})$  and  $\pi(\mathcal{H})$  are the Turán number and Turán density, respectively, of  $\mathcal{H}$ .
When  $r=2$ , Turán’s classical theorem [40] shows that  $\ex(n,K_{t})\leq(1-\frac{1}{t-1})\binom{n}{2}$  for all integers  $n$  and  $t$  with  $n,t\geq 3$ , with equality for balanced complete  $(t-1)$ -partite graphs, which implies that  $\pi(K_{t})=1-\frac{1}{t-1}$  for all  $t\geq 3$ .
When  $r\geq 3$ , determining Turán numbers and densities for cliques becomes much more difficult.
Amazingly,  $\pi(K^{(s)}_{t})$  is not known for any  $t>s\geq 3$ , where  $K^{(s)}_{t}$  is the complete  $s$ -uniform graph on  $t$  vertices.
We direct the reader to Keevash’s comprehensive survey [27] and the more recent survey of Balogh, Clemen, and Lidický [5] for details on past results for hypergraph Turán densities.
In 2011, Bollabás, Leader, and Malvenuto [10] defined a natural generalization of  $K_{t}^{(s)}\!\!\!\!$ .
For all integers  $r,s,t$  with  $t\geq r\geq s\geq 1$ , an  $(r,s,t)$ -daisy, denoted  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t)$ , is an  $r$ -uniform hypergraph with vertex set  $S\cup T$  and edge set  $\{S\cup X\colon X\in T^{(s)}\}$ , where  $S$  is an  $(r-s)$ -element set,  $T$  is a  $t$ -element set disjoint from  $S$ , and  $T^{(s)}$  is the set of all  $s$ -element subsets of  $T$ .
The set  $S$  is the stem and the sets in  $T^{(s)}$  are the petals.
Note that  $\mathcal{D}_{s}(s,t)$  is simply  $K^{(s)}_{t}$ , and as  $r$  grows with  $s$  and  $t$  fixed,  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t)$  becomes sparser because the number of edges remains constant.
Motivated by vertex Turán problems on the hypercube, Bollobás, Leader, and Malvenuto conjectured that  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t))=0$  for all integers  $s$  and  $t$  with  $t\geq s\geq 1$ .
(It is straightforward to show that  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t))$  exists; see Propositions 3.1 and 3.2.)
Ellis, Ivan, and Leader [18] recently disproved this conjecture by showing that  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{r}(2,4)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-2^{-i})\approx 0.29$ , which implies that  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t)\gtrsim 0.29$  whenever  $s\geq 2$  and  $t\geq s+2$ . In fact they proved something more general.
Theorem 2.1 (Ellis, Ivan, and Leader [18]).
If  $t$  is a prime power, then
 $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(2,t+2))\geq\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-t^{-i}).$ 
For all integers  $r$  and  $n$  with  $r\geq 2$  and  $n=k\cdot\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$  for a positive integer  $k$ , they consider the  $n$ -vertex  $r$ -uniform hypergraph whose vertices are points in  $r$ -dimensional projective space over the finite field  $\mathbb{F}_{t}$  with  $k$  copies of each vector, and whose edges are bases, and they show that this hypergraph has no  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(2,t+2)$ -subgraph.
We now present an attractive matroidal reinterpretation of the construction by Ellis, Ivan, and Leader. First, we relate matroid minors to hypergraph suspensions (also known as hypergraph augmentations), see e.g. [1, 6, 33].
For any  $s$ -uniform hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}$  and integer  $r\geq s$ , the rank- $r$  suspension of  $\mathcal{H}$ , denoted  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$ , is the  $r$ -uniform hypergraph with vertex set  $S\cup V(\mathcal{H})$  with  $|S|=r-s$  and edge set  $\{S\cup X\colon X\in E(\mathcal{H})\}$ . The set  $S$  is the stem of  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$ . We see that  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t)=\mathcal{S}_{r}(K_{t}^{(s)})$ .
For an  $n$ -element rank- $s$  matroid  $N$  with basis hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}$  and an integer  $r\geq s$ , define  $\mathsf{F}_{r}(N)$  to be the set of all  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs  $\mathcal{K}$  on  $n+r-s$  vertices that contain  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  with stem  $S$  as a subgraph, with the property that whenever an edge  $e$  of  $\mathcal{K}$  contains  $S$ , then  $e$  is an edge of  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  (this is equivalent to the link of  $\mathcal{K}$  at  $S$  being a copy of  $\mathcal{H}$ ).
Proposition 2.2.
Let  $r\geq s\geq 1$  be integers and let  $N$  be a rank- $s$  matroid. For every rank- $r$  matroid  $M$  with basis hypergraph  $\mathcal{F}$ ,  $M$  has an  $N$ -minor if and only if  $\mathcal{F}$  has an induced subgraph belonging to  $\mathsf{F}_{r}(N)$ . If, in addition,  $N$  is a uniform matroid, then  $\mathcal{F}$  contains  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  as a subgraph if and only if  $M$  has an  $N$ -minor.
Proof.
First, suppose that  $M$  has an  $N$ -minor. Then there are disjoint sets  $E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq E(M)$  so that  $M\!\setminus\!E_{1}/E_{2}=N$  and  $E_{2}$  is independent in  $M$  [35, Lemma 3.3.2].
Since  $E_{2}$  is independent, the bases of  $N$  are precisely the subsets of  $E(N)$  whose union with  $E_{2}$  is a basis of  $M$ .
Equivalently, the link of  $\mathcal{F}\!\setminus\!E_{1}$  at  $E_{2}$  is a copy of  $\mathcal{H}$ .
Therefore, the restriction of  $\mathcal{F}$  to the vertex set  $E(M)\!\setminus\!E_{1}$  contains a copy of  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  with stem  $E_{2}$ . Therefore the induced subgraph of  $\mathcal{F}$  corresponding to  $E(M)\!\setminus\!E_{1}$  belongs to  $\mathsf{F}_{r}(N)$ .
Now suppose  $\mathcal{F}$  has an induced subgraph  $\mathcal{D}$  belonging to  $\mathsf{F}_{r}(N)$ . Then  $\mathcal{D}$  contains  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  as a subgraph with a stem  $S$ . Deleting the vertices outside of  $\mathcal{D}$  and taking the link at  $S$  gives  $\mathcal{H}$ . These operations correspond to deleting elements of  $M$  outside of  $\mathcal{D}$  and contracting the elements in  $S$  to obtain a copy of  $N$ . That is,  $M$  has an  $N$ -minor.
When  $N$  is uniform, it suffices to argue that if  $\mathcal{F}$  has  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  as a subgraph, then  $M$  has  $N$  as a minor. Indeed, if  $S$  is the stem of  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$ , deleting the vertices outside  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})$  and taking the link at  $S$  yields  $\mathcal{H}$ .
∎
An application of Proposition 2.2 to uniform matroids shows that a rank- $r$  matroid is  $U_{s,t}$ -minor-free if and only its basis hypergraph does not have the daisy  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t)=\mathcal{S}_{r}(K_{t}^{(s)})$  as a subgraph.
Corollary 2.3.
Let  $r,s,t$  be integers with  $r,t\geq s\geq 1$ , and let  $\mathcal{H}$  be the basis hypergraph of a matroid  $M$ .
Then  $\mathcal{H}$  has  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t)$  as a subgraph if and only if  $M$  has a  $U_{s,t}$ -minor.
Therefore Problem 1.1 arises by restricting hypergraph Turán parameters to matroid basis hypergraphs.
From this perspective,  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(2,t+2)$ -subgraph-freeness of the hypergraph constructed by Ellis, Ivan, and Leader is immediate, as the associated matroid is  $\mathbb{F}_{t}$ -representable and therefore has no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor (see e.g. [35, Corollary 6.5.3]).
Moreover, Ellis, Ivan, and Leader show that the same hypergraph is  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(t,t+2)$ -subgraph-free in [19, Proposition 1].
From the matroid perspective, this follows immediately from the fact that  $U_{t,t+2}$  is the dual matroid of  $U_{2,t+2}$ , and the class of  $\mathbb{F}_{t}$ -representable matroids is closed under duality.
3 Matroid basis hypergraphs
In this section we will show that the limits of Problem 1.1 exist, and describe – in terms of forbidden substructures – how the class of rank- $r$  matroid basis hypergraph sits within the class of all  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs.
3.1 Density
Given families  $\mathsf{Q}$  and  $\mathsf{H}$  of hypergraphs, let  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})$  be the maximum number of edges of an  $r$ -uniform,  $n$ -vertex, hypergraph in  $\mathsf{Q}$  with no subgraph in  $\mathsf{H}$ .
We set  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})=0$  if no such hypergraph exists.
Furthermore, we allow  $\mathsf{H}$  to be empty; in this case,  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\varnothing)$  simply denotes the maximum number of edges of an  $r$ -uniform,  $n$ -vertex, hypergraph in  $\mathsf{Q}$ .
Let  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathsf{H})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})/\binom{n}{r}$ , if this limit exists.
Note that when  $\mathsf{Q}$  is the class of  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs and  $\mathsf{H}=\{\mathcal{H}\}$  for an  $r$ -graph  $\mathcal{H}$ , then  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})$  is equal to the classical Turán number  $\ex(n,\mathcal{H})$ .
We will show that  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathsf{H})$  exists whenever  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under vertex deletion.
The following argument is standard, but we include it for completeness.
Proposition 3.1.
Let  $\mathsf{Q}$  and  $\mathsf{H}$  be families of hypergraphs, and let  $r$  be an integer with  $r\geq 1$ .
If  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under vertex deletion, then  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathsf{H})$  exists.
Proof.
We will show that the sequence  $\{{\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})}/{\binom{n}{r}}\colon n\geq r\}$  is monotone decreasing.
Let  $n\geq r+1$ .
If  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})=0$ , then  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(m,r,\mathsf{H})=0$  for all  $m\geq n$  because  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under vertex deletion.
So let  $\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Q}$  be an  $n$ -vertex  $r$ -graph with no subgraph in  $\mathsf{H}$  and with  $|\mathcal{F}|=\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})$ .
By computing the average edge density of all single-vertex deletions of  $\mathcal{F}$ , we see that
 $\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{F})}\frac{|\mathcal{F}\!\setminus\!v|}{\binom{n-1}{r}}=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{(n-r)|\mathcal{F}|}{\binom{n-1}{r}}=\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{\binom{n}{r}}=\frac{\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})}{\binom{n}{r}}.$ 
Therefore there is some vertex  $v$  of  $\mathcal{F}$  so that  $|\mathcal{F}\!\setminus\!v|/\binom{n-1}{r}\geq\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})/\binom{n}{r}$ . As  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under vertex deletion,  $\mathcal{F}\!\setminus\!v\in\mathsf{Q}$ , thus  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n-1,r,\mathsf{H})/\binom{n-1}{r}\geq\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathsf{H})/\binom{n}{r}$ , as desired.
∎
The next result uses the notion of hypergraph suspension as defined in Section 2.
Given a family  $\mathsf{H}$  of  $s$ -graphs and an integer  $r\geq s$ , let  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H})=\{\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H})\colon\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{H}\}$ .
Proposition 3.2.
Let  $\mathsf{Q}$  be a family of hypergraphs and let  $\mathsf{H}$  be a family of  $s$ -graphs.
If  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under vertex deletion and taking links, then  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))$  exists.
Proof.
We will show that the sequence  $\{\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))\colon r\geq s\}$  is monotone decreasing.
Fix some  $r\geq s+1$  and  $n\geq r+1$ .
If  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))=0$ , then  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(t,\mathcal{S}_{t}(\mathsf{H}))=0$  for all  $t\geq r$  because  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under taking links.
So let  $\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Q}$  be an  $n$ -vertex  $r$ -graph with no subgraph in  $\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H})$  and with  $|\mathcal{F}|=\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))$ .
By computing the average basis density of all link graphs of  $\mathcal{F}$ , we see that
 $\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{F})}\frac{|\mathcal{F}/v|}{\binom{n-1}{r-1}}=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\frac{r|\mathcal{F}|}{\binom{n-1}{r-1}}=\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{\binom{n}{r}}=\frac{\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathcal{H}))}{\binom{n}{r}}.$ 
Therefore there is some vertex  $v$  of  $\mathcal{F}$  so that  $|\mathcal{F}/v|/\binom{n-1}{r-1}\geq\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))/\binom{n}{r}$ .
In particular,  $v$  is in at least one edge, so  $\mathcal{F}/v$  is a nonempty  $(r-1)$ -graph.
Since  $\mathcal{F}/v\in\mathsf{Q}$  because  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under taking links, it follows that  $\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n-1,r,\mathcal{S}_{r-1}(\mathsf{H}))/\binom{n-1}{r}\geq\ex_{\mathsf{Q}}(n,r,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))/\binom{n}{r}$ .
This implies that the sequence  $\{\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\mathcal{S}_{r}(\mathsf{H}))\colon r\geq s\}$  is monotone decreasing, as desired.
∎
We have the following corollary for matroid basis hypergraphs.
Corollary 3.3.
If  $N$  is a matroid, then  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  exists for all integers  $r\geq 1$ , and  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  exists.
Proof.
Let  $\mathcal{M}$  be the class of  $N$ -minor-free matroids, and let  $\mathsf{Q}$  be the class of basis hypergraphs of matroids in  $\mathcal{M}$ .
Then  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)=\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\varnothing)$ .
Note that  $\mathsf{Q}$  is closed under vertex deletion and taking links because  $\mathcal{M}$  is closed under deletion and contraction.
Therefore  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  exists for all  $r\geq 1$  by Proposition 3.1, and  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,N)$  exists by Proposition 3.2.
∎
Note that the same proof shows that if  $\mathcal{M}$  is any minor-closed class of matroids and  $\mathsf{Q}$  is the class of basis hypergraphs of matroids in  $\mathcal{M}$ , then  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\varnothing)$  and  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,\varnothing)$  both exist.
In this case we write  $\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(r)$  for  $\pi_{\mathsf{Q}}(r,\varnothing)$ .
3.2 Forbidden substructures
Since we will be specializing problems for  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs to the class  $\mathsf{M}$  of matroid basis hypergraphs, we will briefly discuss how  $\mathsf{M}$  fits into the class of all uniform hypergraphs.
Since  $\mathsf{M}$  is closed under vertex deletion and taking links, it is natural to ask for a characterization of  $\mathsf{M}$  via a list of hypergraphs that are not in  $\mathsf{M}$  but have every vertex deletion and every link in  $\mathsf{M}$ .
This list was found by Cordovil, Fukuda, and Moreira [15]; it consists of three  $4$ -vertex graphs, and the infinite family of  $2$ -edge  $r$ -graphs whose vertex set is the disjoint union of the two edges.
It is also natural to ask for a characterization of the class  $\mathsf{M}$  by forbidden induced subgraphs.
It is straightforward to show that for each  $r\geq 2$ , the class of  $r$ -uniform matroid basis hypergraphs can be characterized by a finite collection of forbidden induced subgraphs; we omit the proof.
Proposition 3.4.
An  $r$ -uniform hypergraph is a matroid basis hypergraph if and only if every induced subgraph with at most  $2r$  vertices is a matroid basis hypergraph.
Let  $\mathsf{F}$  and  $\mathsf{H}$  denote families of  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs, and let  $\ex(n,\{\mathsf{H},\mathsf{F}\text{-ind}\})$  denote the maximum number of edges in an  $n$ -vertex  $r$ -uniform hypergraph containing no subgraph isomorphic to a hypergraph belonging to  $\mathsf{H}$  and no induced subgraph isomorphic to a hypergraph belonging to  $\mathsf{F}$ . If  $N$  is a rank- $s$  uniform matroid on  $t$  elements, then its basis hypergraph is  $K_{t}^{(s)}$ . An immediate consequence of Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 3.4 is for  $r\geq s$ ,
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,N)=\ex(n,\{\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t),\mathsf{F}\text{-ind}\}),$ 
for an appropriate family  $\mathsf{F}$  of  $r$ -uniform hypergraphs. In the case when  $H$  and  $F$  are graphs, the problem of determining  $\ex(n,\{H,F\text{-ind}\})$  was introduced by Loh, Tait, Timmons, and Zhou [30] in 2018 and has received significant attention henceforth.
While the number of forbidden induced subgraphs for  $r$ -uniform matroid basis hypergraphs is finite for all  $r$ , it grows exponentially as a function of  $r$ .
Write  $B_{n}$  for the  $n$ -th Bell number, which counts the number of partitions of an  $n$ -element set, and write  $m(n,2)$  for the number of rank- $2$  matroids on an  $n$ -element set. While better asymptotics for  $B_{n}$  are known, we will use only the trivial bound  $B_{n}\leq n^{n}$ . Every rank-2 matroid on  $[n]$  can be described as a set partition of  $[n+1]$  (in which the class containing  $n+1$  denotes the set of loops), so  $m(n,2)\leq B_{n+1}\leq(n+1)^{n+1}$ .
Proposition 3.5.
The class of  $r$ -uniform matroid basis hypergraphs has at least  $2^{r^{2}/2-O(r\log(r))}$  non-isomorphic forbidden induced subgraphs on  $r+2$  vertices.
Proof.
We first show that every non-empty  $r$ -graph  $\mathcal{H}$  with at most  $r+1$  vertices is in  $\mathsf{M}$ .
If  $\mathcal{H}$  has at most  $r$  vertices, then it has at most one edge, so its edges trivially satisfy the basis exchange axiom.
So we may assume that  $\mathcal{H}$  has  $r+1$  vertices.
Let  $F_{1}$  and  $F_{2}$  be edges of  $\mathcal{H}$ .
Note that  $|F_{1}-F_{2}|=|F_{2}-F_{1}|=1$ .
Let  $v_{1}\in F_{1}-F_{2}$  and let  $v_{2}\in F_{2}-F_{1}$ .
Since  $(F_{1}-v_{1})\cup v_{2}$  and  $(F_{2}-v_{2})\cup v_{1}$  are both edges (the former is  $F_{2}$  and the latter is  $F_{1}$ ), the edges of  $\mathcal{H}$  satisfy the basis exchange axiom, so  $\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{M}$ .
Consequently, an  $r$ -graph  $\mathcal{H}$  on  $r+2$  vertices is a forbidden induced subgraph if and only if it is not in  $\mathsf{M}$ .
The number of  $r$ -graphs in  $\mathsf{M}$  on  $r+2$  vertices is equal to the number of  $(r+2)$ -element matroids of rank  $r$ , which, by matroid duality, is equal to the number of  $(r+2)$ -element matroids of rank  $2$ . It follows that the number  $f_{r}$  of non-isomorphic forbidden induced subgraphs on  $r+2$  vertices satisfies
 $f_{r}\geq\frac{1}{(r+2)!}\left(2^{\binom{r+2}{r}}-m(r+2,2)\right)\geq\frac{1}{(r+2)!}\left(2^{\binom{r+2}{r}}-(r+3)^{r+3}\right).$ 
The proposition then follows from Stirling’s approximation.
∎
The proof of Proposition 3.5 shows that, as  $r\to\infty$ , almost every  $r$ -graph on  $r+2$  vertices is a forbidden induced subgraph for the class of matroidal hypergraphs. The actual number of forbidden induced subgraphs is likely much larger than this lower bound, because the proof only considers  $r$ -graphs on  $r+2$  vertices, while forbidden induced subgraphs can have up to  $2r$  vertices.
Finally, we remark that a typical  $r$ -graph is not the basis hypergraph of a matroid, in the sense that
 $\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{m(n,r)}{2^{\binom{n}{r}}}=0\qquad\text{for all $r\geq 2$},$ 
where  $m(n,r)$  is the number of  $r$ -uniform matroid basis hypergraphs on a given set of  $n$  vertices. For  $r=2$ , this follows immediately from the above observation that  $m(n,2)\leq(n+1)^{n+1}$ , while for  $r\geq 3$  this follows from the bound  $\log m(n,r)=O\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\binom{n}{r}\right)$  as  $n\to\infty$ , see e.g. [8].
4  $U_{1,t}$  and  $U_{2,t}$ 
In this section we consider  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{s,t})$  with  $s\in\{1,2\}$ , and prove Theorem 1.2.
We begin with  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{1,t})$ , which is straightforward to calculate.
Proposition 4.1.
Let  $r\geq 1$  and  $t\geq 2$  be integers. Then
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{1,t})\leq(t-1)^{r},$ 
with equality when  $t-1$  divides  $n$ .
Proof.
We proceed by induction on  $r$ .
A matroid of rank  $1$  with no  $U_{1,t}$ -restriction has at most  $t-1$  non-loop elements, and therefore at most  $t-1$  bases. Thus the bound holds when  $r=1$ , establishing the base case of the induction.
Now let  $r>1$  and assume that the claim holds for all rank- $(r-1)$  matroids. Let  $M$  be a rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{1,t}$ -minor and let  $C^{*}$  be a largest cocircuit (a circuit of the dual) of  $M$ . As  $M$  has no  $U_{1,t}$ -minor, we have  $|C^{*}|\leq t-1$ . Furthermore, as  $C^{*}$  is a cocircuit, every basis of  $M$  contains an element of  $C^{*}$  (see [35, Proposition 2.1.19]) and  $C^{*}$  contains no loops of  $M$ .
For each element  $e\in C^{*}$ , as  $e$  is not a loop of  $M$ , the matroid  $M/e$  has rank  $r-1$  and therefore at most  $(t-1)^{r-1}$  bases, by induction.
Since  $|C^{*}|\leq t-1$ , it follows that  $M$  has at most  $(t-1)(t-1)^{r-1}=(t-1)^{r}$  bases, as desired.
Finally, we show that the bound is sharp when  $t-1$  divides  $n$ .
If  $t-1$  divides  $n$ , let  $M$  be the union of  $r$  parallel classes with cardinality  $t-1$  so that the bases of  $M$  are the transversals of these classes, i.e.,  $M$  is a parallel extension of an  $r$ -element independent set.
Then  $M$  has  $n$  elements, rank  $r$ , and exactly  $(t-1)^{r}$  bases.
∎
Proposition 4.1 implies that  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{1,t})=0$  for all  $t\geq 2$  and  $r\geq 1$ . We comment that this already follows from the stronger result that  $\ex(n,\mathcal{D}_{r}(1,t))\leq\frac{t-1}{n-r+1}\binom{n}{r}$ . To see why the latter result holds, note that if  $\mathcal{H}$  is an  $r$ -uniform hypergraph with  $n$  vertices that does not contain  $\mathcal{D}_{r}(1,t)$  as a subgraph, then every  $r-1$  vertices of  $\mathcal{H}$  are contained in at most  $t-1$  edges; it follows that  $\mathcal{H}$  has at most  $\binom{n}{r-1}\frac{t-1}{r}$  edges, from which the result follows.
We next turn our attention to  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{2,t})$ .
We will rely on the following theorem of Kung [29].
Theorem 4.2 (Kung [29]).
For all integers  $r$  and  $t$  with  $r,t\geq 2$ , every simple rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor has at most  $\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$  elements.
For general matroids, Theorem 4.2 implies that any matroid forbidding  $U_{2,t+2}$  as a minor has a bounded number of parallel classes.
In general,  $U_{s,t}$ -minor-free matroids need not have this property.
For example, one can take a direct sum of  $r/2$  copies of  $U_{2,\ell}$  to obtain a simple rank- $r$  matroid with  $r\cdot\ell$  points and no  $U_{s,t}$ -minor with  $s\geq 3$  and  $t\geq 5$ .
For  $r\geq 1$  and  $t\geq 2$ , define
 $b(r,t)=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(t^{r}-t^{i})}{r!\cdot(t-1)^{r}}=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}-\frac{t^{i}-1}{t-1})}{r!}.$ 
(1)
When  $t$  is a prime power,  $b(r,t)$  is the number of bases of the rank- $r$  projective geometry  $\text{PG}(r-1,t)$  (see e.g. [35, Proposition 6.1.4]).
Noting that  $b(1,t)=1$  for all  $t$ , the following recursion holds for all  $r\geq 2$  and  $t\geq 2$ :
 $b(r,t)=\frac{b(r-1,t)\cdot t^{r-1}(t^{r}-1)}{r\cdot(t-1)}.$ 
(2)
Our calculation of  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{2,t+2})$  will rely on the theory of hypergraph Lagrangians.
The Lagrangian polynomial of an  $n$ -vertex hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}$  is the polynomial
 $p_{\mathcal{H}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{H})}\prod_{i\in e}x_{i}.$ 
The Lagrangian of a hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}$  with vertex set  $[n]$ , denoted  $\lambda(\mathcal{H})$ , is the maximum value of  $p_{\mathcal{H}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})$  over the standard simplex  $\{x_{i}\geq 0:\sum_{i}x_{i}=1\}$ . By continuity, as the standard simplex is bounded and closed,  $\lambda(\mathcal{H})$  is well-defined.
For background and applications regarding Lagrangians, see [27].
For a matroid  $M$  with basis hypergraph  $\mathcal{H}$ , we write  $p_{M}$  for  $p_{\mathcal{H}}$  and  $\lambda(M)$  for  $\lambda(\mathcal{H})$ .
An immediate observation is that  $\lambda(M)=\lambda(\si(M))$  for any matroid  $M$  on ground set  $[s]$ . Clearly deleting a loop does not change  $\lambda(M)$ , because loops are not in any bases, and if elements  $1$  and  $2$  of  $M$  are parallel, then  $p_{M}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{s})=p_{M\!\setminus\!1}(x_{1}+x_{2},x_{3},\dots,x_{s})$  because  $1\cup J\in\mathcal{B}(M)$  if and only if  $2\cup J\in\mathcal{B}(M)$  for any  $J\subseteq[s]$ .
The following lemma relates derivatives of  $p_{M}$  to matroid contraction.
Lemma 4.3.
Let  $r\geq 2$ , let  $M$  be a rank- $r$  matroid on a ground set  $[s]$  with Lagrangian polynomial  $p_{M}$ , and let  $i\in[s]$ . Then
 $\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p_{M}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\right)\leq(1-x_{i})^{r-1}\cdot\lambda(M/i)$ 
for all nonnegative reals  $x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}$  such that  $\sum_{j}x_{j}=1$ .
Proof.
If  $i$  is a loop of  $M$ , then  $p_{M}$  does not depend on  $x_{i}$ , so the left-hand side is equal to 0 and the claim holds trivially.
So we may assume that  $i$  is not a loop of  $M$ .
In that case, the bases of  $M$  that contain  $i$  are in one-to-one correspondence with the bases of  $M/i$ .
We see that
 $\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p_{M}\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\right)$ 
 $\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}(M)}\prod_{j\in B}x_{j}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B\in\mathcal{B}(M)\\
i\in B\end{subarray}}\prod_{j\in B-i}x_{j}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(M/i)}\prod_{j\in B^{\prime}}x_{j}=(1-x_{i})^{r-1}\cdot\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(M/i)}\prod_{j\in B^{\prime}}\frac{x_{j}}{1-x_{i}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq(1-x_{i})^{r-1}\cdot\lambda(M/i).\qed$ 
Using Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.2, we bound the Lagrangian of a  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor-free matroid for  $t\geq 2$ . Our techniques for optimizing the Lagrangian are well-known, see for example Sidorenko’s classic work [39], see also [27]. We comment that the computation for the specific case of  $t=2$  was done implicitly by Alon [1] via probabilistic techniques in the context of bounding the size of erasure list-decodable codes.
Theorem 4.4.
Let  $t\geq 2$  and let  $r\geq 1$  be integers. Let  $M$  be a rank- $r$   $U_{2,t+2}$ -free matroid. Then
 $\lambda(M)\leq b(r,t)\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r}.$ 
Proof.
As  $\lambda(M)=\lambda(\si(M))$ , without loss of generality, we may suppose  $M$  is simple. Let  $s$  denote the number of elements of  $M$ . By Theorem 4.2,  $s\leq\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ . Let  $p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})$  be the Lagrangian polynomial of  $M$ . When  $r=1$ , this clearly implies  $\lambda(M)=1$ . If  $s=r$ , then by convexity we have  $\lambda(M)=(1/r)^{r}$ . Note then,
 $\displaystyle\frac{r!}{r^{r}}=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{i}{r}\right)\leq\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{t^{i}-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)$ 
where the inequality follows from the fact that the function  $(t^{x}-1)/x$  is increasing on  $(0,\infty)$ . By (1) and the above inequality, we have,
 $\displaystyle\lambda(M)=\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)^{r}\leq\frac{1}{r!}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{t^{i}-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)=\frac{1}{r!}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(\frac{t^{r}-t^{i}}{t^{r}-1}\right)=b(r,t)\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r}.$ 
We proceed with induction on  $r$  and  $s$ . Let  $\mathbf{x}^{*}=(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{s}^{*})$  maximize  $p(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})$  over the standard simplex. If  $x^{*}_{i}=0$ , then the claim holds by induction on  $s$ . So we may suppose  $x^{*}_{i}>0$  for all  $i\in[s]$ . Thus  $\mathbf{x}^{*}$  belongs to the relative interior of the standard simplex, and as a consequence, the gradient of  $p$  must be orthogonal to the hyperplane defined by  $\sum_{i}x_{i}=1$ . In particular, there exists a constant  $c\in\mathbb{R}$  such that  $\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p(\mathbf{x}^{*})=c$  for all  $i\in[s]$ . By Euler’s identity for homogeneous functions, we have
 $\displaystyle r\cdot p(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}^{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p(\mathbf{x}^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}x_{i}^{*}c=c.$ 
By Lemma 4.3, for all  $i\in[s]$ ,
 $c=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}p\left(\mathbf{x}^{*}\right)\leq(1-x_{i}^{*})^{r-1}\lambda(M/i)=(1-x_{i}^{*})^{r-1}\lambda(\si(M/i)).$ 
By the pigeonhole principle, there exists some  $j$  such that  $x_{j}\geq\frac{t-1}{t^{r}-1}$ . It follows by induction on  $r$  and (2) that
 $\displaystyle c$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{t-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r-1}b(r-1,t)\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r-1}-1}\right)^{r-1}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t(t^{r-1}-1)}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r-1}b(r-1,t)\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r-1}-1}\right)^{r-1}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t(t^{r-1}-1)}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r-1}b(r,t)\frac{r(t-1)}{t^{r-1}(t^{r}-1)}\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r-1}-1}\right)^{r-1}=r\cdot b(r,t)\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r}.$ 
We have  $r\cdot p(\mathbf{x}^{*})\leq r\cdot b(r,t)\left((t-1)/(t^{r}-1)\right)^{r}$ , which yields the claim.
∎
We have the following corollary for the maximum number of bases of an  $n$ -element, rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor.
Theorem 4.5.
Let  $n,r,t$  be integers with  $r\geq 1$  and  $t\geq 2$  and  $n\geq\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ . Then
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})\leq b(r,t)\cdot\left(\frac{n(t-1)}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r}.$ 
Furthermore, this bound is tight when  $t$  is a prime power and  $n$  is a multiple of  $\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ .
Proof.
Let  $M$  be an  $n$ -element rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor, and let  $N=\si(M)$ .
For each element  $i$  of  $N$ , let  $y_{i}$  be the number of elements of  $M$  parallel to  $i$ , and let  $x_{i}=y_{i}/n$ .
Then
 $\displaystyle b(M)=\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}(N)}\prod_{i\in B}y_{i}=n^{r}\cdot\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}(N)}\prod_{i\in B}x_{i}\leq n^{r}\cdot\lambda(N)\leq n^{r}\cdot b(r,t)\cdot\left(\frac{t-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)^{r}\!\!,$ 
where the last inequality holds by Theorem 4.4.
When  $n$  is a multiple of  $\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$  and  $t$  is a prime power, equality holds for the loopless  $n$ -element rank- $r$  matroid whose simplification is  $\text{PG}(r-1,t)$  and whose parallel classes all have equal size.
∎
Since Theorem 4.5 is asymptotically tight whenever  $t$  is a prime power, we have the following corollary.
Corollary 4.6.
For every integer  $r\geq 2$  and every prime power  $q$  we have
 $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{2,q+2})=b(r,q)\cdot\left(\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}\right)^{r}\cdot r!=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{q^{i}-1}{q^{r}-1}\right).$ 
In particular, this implies that the lower bound of Theorem 2.1 is tight when restricted to matroid basis hypergraphs.
Corollary 4.7.
For every prime power  $q$  we have
 $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{2,q+2})=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{-i}).$ 
While Theorem 4.5 is tight when  $t$  is a prime power, this is likely not the case when  $t$  is not a prime power because of the following result.
Theorem 4.8 (Geelen and Nelson [24]).
Let  $t\geq 2$  be an integer, and let  $q$  be the largest prime power at most  $t$ .
For all sufficiently large  $r$ , every simple rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor has at most  $\frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}$  elements.
In light of this theorem and Theorem 4.5, we make the following conjecture.
Conjecture 4.9.
Let  $t\geq 2$ , and let  $q$  be the largest prime power at most  $t$ .
For all sufficiently large integers  $r$  and  $n$  we have
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})\leq b(r,q)\cdot\left(\frac{n(q-1)}{(q^{r}-1)}\right)^{r}.$ 
If true, Conjecture 4.9 would be asymptotically tight. Conjecture 4.9 seems more difficult than Theorem 4.5.
The main difficulty is that we can no longer use direct induction on  $r$  because Theorem 4.8 only applies when  $r$  is sufficiently large.
In spite of this, we can use standard prime estimation techniques to prove an approximate version of Conjecture 4.9; a similar estimate was made in [18]. We use the following well-known prime estimation result.
Theorem 4.10 (Baker, Harman, and Pintz [3]).
For all sufficiently large real numbers  $x$ , there is a prime in the interval  $[x-x^{0.525},x]$ .
We can now approximate  $\ex_{M}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})$  using the largest prime at most  $t$ .
Theorem 4.11.
Let  $r$  be a fixed positive integer and let  $t$  be a sufficiently large integer. For all  $n\geq r$  we have
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})\leq\left(b(r,q)\left((q-1)/(q^{r}-1)\right)^{r}+O_{t}(t^{\theta-2})\right)n^{r}$ 
where  $\theta=0.525$  and  $q$  is the largest prime at most  $t$ .
Proof.
Let  $t_{0}$  be such that the interval  $[t-t^{\theta},t]$  contains a prime number for all  $t\geq t_{0}$  (such a  $t_{0}$  exists by Theorem 4.10), and assume that  $t\geq t_{0}$ .
As  $t\leq q+t^{\theta}$ , we have  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,U_{2,t+2})\leq\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,U_{2,q+t^{\theta}+2})$  because every matroid with no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor also has no  $U_{2,q+t^{\theta}+2}$ -minor. By (1),
 $\displaystyle b(r,t)\left((t-1)/(t^{r}-1)\right)^{r}=\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(\frac{t^{r}-t^{i}}{t^{r}-1}\right)=\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{t^{i}-1}{t^{r}-1}\right).$ 
As  $q\leq t$ , by the binomial theorem we have that
 $(q+t^{\theta})^{r}\leq q^{r}+2^{r}t^{r+\theta-1}.$ 
As  $t$  is sufficiently large, there exists a constant  $C=C(r)$ ,
 $\displaystyle-\frac{1}{(q+t^{\theta})^{r}-1}\leq-\frac{1}{q^{r}+2^{r}t^{r+\theta-1}-1}\leq-\frac{1}{q^{r}-1}+\frac{C}{t^{r+1-\theta}}.$ 
We have,
 $\displaystyle\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{t^{i}-1}{t^{r}-1}\right)$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{t^{i}-1}{(q+t^{\theta})^{r}-1}\right)\leq\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{t^{i}-1}{q^{r}-1}+\frac{C(t^{i}-1)}{t^{r+1-\theta}}\right)$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{q^{i}-1}{q^{r}-1}+\frac{C(t^{i}-1)}{t^{r+1-\theta}}\right)=\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left(1-\frac{q^{i}-1}{q^{r}-1}\right)+O_{t}\left(\frac{1}{t^{2-\theta}}\right).$ 
By Theorem 4.5, the claim follows.
∎
Once again, we have a corollary for Turán basis density.
Corollary 4.12.
Let  $r$  and  $t$  be integers with  $r\geq 2$  and  $t$  sufficiently large, and let  $q$  be the largest prime power at most  $t$ . Then
 $\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\Big(1-\frac{q^{i}-1}{q^{r}-1}\Big)\leq\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{2,t+2})\leq\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\Big(1-\frac{q^{i}-1}{q^{r}-1}\Big)+\frac{C\cdot r!}{t^{2-\theta}},$ 
where  $\theta=0.525$  and  $C$  is a positive constant depending only on  $r$ .
5  $U_{s,s+1}$ 
In this section we consider  $U_{s,s+1}$ .
The problem of finding  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,s+1})$  has a particularly interesting interpretation, which we briefly describe.
First, the circumference of a matroid with at least one circuit is the number of elements in a largest circuit, and a matroid has circumference at most  $s$  if and only if it has no  $U_{s,s+1}$ -minor.
To see this, let  $M$  be a matroid whose largest circuit has  $k\geq s+1$  elements; deleting all elements outside the circuit and then contracting  $k-s-1$  elements from the circuit results in a  $U_{s,s+1}$ -minor. In the other direction, note that the circumference cannot increase when deleting or contracting an element, so if  $M$  has a  $U_{s,s+1}$ -minor, then the circumference of  $M$  is at least the circumference of  $U_{s,s+1}$ , which is  $s+1$ .
So,  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,s+1})$  is the maximum number of bases over all matroids with  $n$  elements, rank  $r$ , and circumference at most  $s$ .
When  $s=1$  we have  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,s+1})=1$  by Proposition 4.1.
We can easily get a tight upper bound when  $s=2$ .
Proposition 5.1.
Let  $n\geq r\geq 2$ . Then
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,3})\leq\left(\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r},$ 
with equality when  $r$  divides  $n$ .
Proof.
Let  $n\geq r\geq 2$  be integers, and let  $M$  be an  $n$ -element rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{2,3}$ -minor. Without loss of generality, we may assume that  $M$  is loopless. Let  $B$  be a basis of  $M$ .
Every element of  $M$  is parallel to an element of  $B$ , or else  $M$  has a  $U_{2,3}$ -minor.
So  $M$  is a direct sum of parallel classes  $P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r}$  with  $\sum_{i}|P_{i}|=n$ .
The number of bases of  $M$  is  $\prod_{i}|P_{i}|$ , and since  $\sum_{i}|P_{i}|=n$ , by convexity, it follows that  $\prod_{i}|P_{i}|\leq(\frac{n}{r})^{r}$ , as desired.
When  $r$  divides  $n$ , equality holds when  $|P_{i}|=n/r$  for all  $i\in[r]$ .
∎
We next consider the case when  $s=3$ . The following lemma is a structural result for  $U_{3,4}$ -minor-free matroids.
Lemma 5.2.
If  $M$  be a  $U_{3,4}$ -minor-free matroid, then  $M$  is a direct direct sum of matroids of rank at most 2.
Proof.
The claim is trivial if  $M$  has rank at most  $2$ . Suppose  $M$  has rank  $r\geq 3$ , and without loss of generality, suppose  $M$  is simple.
Let  $B$  be a basis of  $M$ . Then each element not in  $B$  is spanned by a pair from  $B$ , or else  $M$  has a  $U_{3,4}$ -minor.
If  $e,f\in E(M)-B$  are spanned by the pairs  $\{b_{1},b_{2}\}$  and  $\{b_{2},b_{3}\}$ , respectively, with  $b_{1}\neq b_{3}$ , then by the circuit elimination axiom there is a circuit  $C$  contained in  $\{b_{1},b_{3},e,f\}$ .
Then  $C$  must be  $\{b_{1},b_{3},e,f\}$  or else  $M$  has two distinct lines that share two points, a contradiction.
Therefore there is a partition of  $B$  so that each part has size at most 2, each  $e\in E(M)-B$  is spanned by a part of size 2, and each part of size 2 spans some  $e\in E(M)-B$ . These parts span matroids whose direct sum is equal to  $M$ , so  $M$  is a direct sum of matroids of rank at most 2.
∎
For each real number  $x$  and positive integer  $k$  we write
 $\binom{x}{k}=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{x-i}{k-i}.$ 
Recall that for a matroid  $M$  we write  $b(M)$  for the number of bases of  $M$ .
Theorem 5.3.
Let  $n$  and  $r$  be positive integers with  $n>r\geq 2$ .
 $(i)$  
If  $r$  is even, then  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{3,4})\leq\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r}{2}}$ , and  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})=\frac{r!\cdot 2^{r/2}}{r^{r}}$ .
 $(ii)$  
If  $r$  is odd, then  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{3,4})\leq\big(1+o_{n}(1)\big)\frac{n}{r}\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}$  and  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})=\frac{r!\cdot 2^{\frac{r-1}{2}}}{r^{r}}$ .
Proof.
Let  $M$  be an  $n$ -element, rank- $r$ ,  $U_{3,4}$ -minor-free matroid such that  $b(M)=\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{3,4})$ . By Lemma 5.2, we may assume that  $M$  is a direct sum of matroids of rank at most two. We first show that the desired upper bounds hold by induction on  $r$ .
When  $r=2$ , clearly  $(i)$  holds, so we may assume that  $r\geq 3$ .
We consider two cases, depending on the parity of  $r$ .
First suppose that  $r$  is even.
Then  $M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}$  where  $r(M_{1})=2$  and  $r(M_{2})=r-2$ .
Let  $n_{i}=|M_{i}|$  for  $i=1,2$ , so  $n_{1}+n_{2}=n$ .
Then
 $b(M)=b(M_{1})\cdot b(M_{2})\leq\binom{n_{1}}{2}\cdot\binom{\frac{2n_{2}}{r-2}}{2}^{\frac{r-2}{2}},$ 
where the bound on  $b(M_{2})$  holds by induction on  $r$ .
Since  $n_{1}+n_{2}=n$ , this product is maximized when  $n_{1}=\frac{2n}{r}$  and  $n_{2}=n-\frac{2n}{r}$ , so  $n$  is distributed equally to each of the  $\frac{r}{2}$  factors in the product.
Since  $\frac{2n_{2}}{r-2}=\frac{2n}{r}$ , we see that  $b(M)\leq\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r}{2}}$ , as desired.
Next suppose  $r$  is odd.
Then  $M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}$ , where  $r(M_{1})=1$  and  $r(M_{2})=r-1$ .
Let  $n_{i}=|M_{i}|$  for  $i=1,2$ , so  $n_{1}+n_{2}=n$ .
Then
 $b(M)=b(M_{1})\cdot b(M_{2})\leq n_{1}\cdot\binom{\frac{2n_{2}}{r-1}}{2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}\leq\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}n_{1}n_{2}^{r-1},$ 
where the bound on  $b(M_{2})$  holds by induction on  $r$ .
Since  $n_{1}+n_{2}=n$ , this product is maximized when  $n_{1}=\frac{n}{r}$  and  $n_{2}=\frac{r-1}{r}n$ . It follows that  $b(M)\leq(\sqrt{2})^{r-1}(n/r)^{r}\leq\big(1+o_{n}(1)\big)\frac{n}{r}\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}$ , as desired.
We now combine the upper bounds with asymptotically matching lower bounds to find  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})$ .
If  $r$  is even and  $r/2$  divides  $n$ , let  $M$  be the direct sum of  $r/2$  copies of  $U_{2,\frac{n}{r/2}}$ .
Then  $b(M)=\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r}{2}}$  and  $M$  has no  $U_{3,4}$ -minor.
Combined with the upper bound from  $(i)$  we see that
 $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r}{2}}}{\binom{n}{r}}=\frac{r!\cdot 2^{r/2}}{r^{r}}.$ 
If  $r$  is odd and  $r$  divides  $n$ , let  $M$  be the direct sum of  $\frac{r-1}{2}$  copies of  $U_{2,\frac{2n}{r}}$  and one copy of  $U_{1,\frac{n}{r}}$ .
Then  $b(M)=\frac{n}{r}\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}$  and  $M$  has no  $U_{3,4}$ -minor.
Combined with the upper bound from  $(ii)$  we see that
 $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,4})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\frac{n}{r}\binom{\frac{2n}{r}}{2}^{\frac{r-1}{2}}}{\binom{n}{r}}=\frac{r!\cdot 2^{\frac{r-1}{2}}}{r^{r}}.\qed$ 
We have the following corollary for  $r=3$ .
Corollary 5.4.
 $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,4})=4/9$ .
The basis hypergraph of  $U_{3,4}$  is the 3-uniform hypergraph  $K_{4}^{(3)}$ , and while its Turán density is not known, a construction due to Turán [40] shows that  $\pi(K_{4}^{(3)})\geq 5/9$ .
So Corollary 5.4 is notable because it shows that the Turán density of  $K_{4}^{(3)}$  is significantly smaller when we restrict to matroid basis hypergraphs.
We next consider  $s\geq 4$ .
Matroids with circumference  $s$  are not as well-structured when  $s\geq 4$ , but we expect that  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,s+1})$  is achieved by a direct sum of uniform matroids of rank  $s-1$ , as it is for  $s=2$  and  $s=3$ .
Conjecture 5.5.
Let  $s,r$  be positive integers with  $s\geq 4$  and  $r\geq s-1$ .
Then  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{s,s+1})\leq\binom{\frac{n}{r/(s-1)}}{s-1}^{\frac{r}{s-1}}$  for all  $n\geq r$ , with equality when  $s-1$  divides  $r$  and  $\frac{r}{s-1}$  divides  $n$  for the direct sum of  $r/(s-1)$  copies of  $U_{s-1,\frac{n}{r/(s-1)}}$ .
This may not be difficult to prove, but the direct structural approach of Theorem 5.3 seemingly does not apply.
6  $U_{3,t}$ 
After considering  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{s,t})$  when  $s\in\{1,2\}$  or  $t=s+1$ , perhaps the next natural cases to consider are  $s=3$  or  $t=s+2$ .
While we believe that  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{s,s+2})$  and  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(r,U_{3,t})$  are interesting parameters for future work, we will next focus on the simpler parameter  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,t})$ , the Turán basis density for rank-3 matroids with no  $U_{3,t}$ -restriction.
We freely use the facts that any two lines in a matroid share at most one point, and that if  $\mathcal{L}$  is a collection of subsets of a set  $E$  so that each set in  $\mathcal{L}$  has at least three elements and each pair of sets in  $\mathcal{L}$  shares at most one common element, then  $\mathcal{L}$  is the set of long lines (i.e. lines with at least three points) of a simple, rank-3 matroid with ground set  $E$  [35, Proposition 1.5.6].
We first give lower bounds for  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,t})$ .
Proposition 6.1.
For all integers  $m\geq 2$ ,
 $(a)$  
 $\pi_{M}(3,U_{3,2m+1})\geq 1-\frac{1}{m^{2}}$  and
 $(b)$  
 $\pi_{M}(3,U_{3,2m+2})\geq\frac{4m^{4}}{(2m^{2}+1)^{2}}=1-\frac{2}{2m^{2}+1}+\frac{1}{(2m^{2}+1)^{2}}$ .
Proof.
For (a), it suffices to exhibit an  $n$ -element rank-3 matroid that does not have a  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction and has a large number of bases.
Suppose that  $m$  divides  $n$ , and let  $M$  be the simple rank-3 matroid on ground set  $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$  with long lines  $L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{m}$  where  $L_{i}=\{m(i-1)+1,m(i-1)+2,\ldots,mi\}$ . Any set of  $2m+1$  points intersects one of these lines in at least three points, so  $M$  does not have a  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction.
The bases of  $M$  are all 3-elements subsets that are not contained in one of the  $L_{i}$ , so
 $b(M)=\binom{n}{3}-m\binom{n/m}{3}=\binom{n}{3}\left(1-\frac{1}{m^{2}}\right)+o(n^{3}).$ 
For (b), it suffices to exhibit an  $n$ -element rank-3 matroid that does not have a  $U_{3,2m+2}$ -restriction and has a large number of bases.
Let  $\alpha=\frac{2m}{2m^{2}+1}$  and let  $\beta=1-m\alpha$ . Let  $E$  be an  $n$ -element set, and partition  $E=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\ldots\cup L_{m}\cup P$  such that  $||L_{i}|-\alpha n|\leq 1$  for all  $i$  and  $||P|-\beta n|\leq 1$ . Let  $M$  be the rank-3 matroid on  $E$  in which  $L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{m}$  are the long lines of  $M$ , the restriction  $M|L_{i}$  is simple for all  $i\in[m]$ , and  $P$  is a parallel class.
By the pigeonhole principle, each set of  $2m+2$  elements intersects  $P$  in more than one element or one of the sets  $L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{m}$  in more than two elements, so  $M$  does not have a  $U_{3,2m+2}$ -restriction.
The number of bases of  $M$  is
 $\displaystyle b(M)$ 
 $\displaystyle=|P|\binom{\sum_{i}|L_{i}|}{2}+\sum_{1\leq p<q<r\leq m}|L_{p}||L_{q}||L_{r}|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq p,q\leq m\\
p\neq q\end{subarray}}\binom{|L_{p}|}{2}|L_{q}|$ 
 $\displaystyle=\beta n\binom{m\alpha n}{2}+\binom{m}{3}(\alpha n)^{3}+m(m-1)\binom{\alpha n}{2}\alpha n+o(n^{3})$ 
 $\displaystyle=\binom{n}{3}\left(\frac{2m^{2}}{2m^{2}+1}\right)^{2}+o(n^{3}).\qed$ 
We next prove a general structural result for rank- $3$  matroids with no  $U_{3,t}$ -restriction (in two cases, depending on the parity of  $t$ ).
Roughly speaking, this theorem says that every matroid with no  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction is a union of at most  $m$  lines and a small leftover set.
Theorem 6.2.
Let  $m\geq 2$ .
If  $M$  is a rank- $3$  matroid with no  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction, then there is an integer  $k$  with  $0\leq k\leq m$  and disjoint sets  $X$  and  $Y$  with  $X\cup Y=E(M)$  such that
• 
 $M|X$  is a union of  $k$  lines, and
• 
 $M|Y$  has no  $U_{3,2(m-k)+1}$ -restriction (if  $k<m$ ), no  $U_{2,\binom{2(m-k)}{2}+2}$ -restriction, and at most  $\binom{2(m-k)}{2}\big(\binom{2(m-k)}{2}-1\big)+2(m-k)$  points.
Proof.
We may assume that  $M$  is simple.
Let  $(L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{k})$  be a maximal sequence of subsets of  $E(M)$  so that  $k\leq m$  and for each  $i\in[k]$  the set  $L_{i}$  is a line of  $M\!\setminus\!(L_{1}\cup\cdots\cup L_{i-1})$  with at least  $\binom{2(m-i)+1}{2}+2$  points (taking  $L_{0}=\varnothing$ ).
Let  $X=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\cdots\cup L_{k}$  and let  $Y=E(M)-X$ .
By maximality, each line of  $M|Y$  has at most  $\binom{2(m-(k+1))+1}{2}+1=\binom{2(m-k)}{2}+1$  points.
Suppose  $M|Y$  has a  $U_{3,2(m-k)+1}$ -restriction, where  $U_{3,1}$  refers to a single point. Then  $k\geq 1$  because  $M$  has no  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction. The lines  $L_{1},\dots,L_{k}$  are large enough that we can iteratively (in reverse order) add two points from each line to extend the  $U_{3,2(m-k)+1}$ -restriction of  $M|Y$  to a  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction of  $M$ .
We now make this idea precise.
Write  $Y=L_{k+1}$  for convenience. Let  $j$  be minimum so that  $M$  has a  $U_{3,2(m-j+1)+1}$ -restriction  $N$  contained in  $L_{j}~\cup~L_{j+1}~\cup~\cdots~\cup~L_{k+1}$ .
Note that  $j>1$  because  $M$  has no  $U_{3,2m+1}$ -restriction, and that  $j$  is well-defined because  $j=k+1$  is a valid option.
The points of  $N$  span  $\binom{2(m-j+1)+1}{2}$  lines, and each line contains at most one point of  $L_{j-1}$ .
Since  $L_{j-1}$  has at least  $\binom{2(m-(j-1))+1}{2}+2$  points, it contains points  $a$  and  $b$  not spanned by any line of  $N$ .
Also,  $\{a,b\}$  does not span any point  $e$  of  $N$ , because then  $e\in L_{j-1}$ , while  $N$  is disjoint from  $L_{j-1}$  by the definitions of  $(L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{k})$  and  $Y$ .
Therefore  $N\cup\{a,b\}$  is a  $U_{3,2(m-j+1)+3}$ -restriction of  $M$  contained in
 $L_{j-1}~\cup~L_{j}~\cup~\cdots~\cup~L_{k+1}$ , which contradicts the minimality of  $j$ .
Therefore  $M|Y$  has no  $U_{3,2(m-k)+1}$ -restriction.
Finally, we bound the number of points of  $M|Y$ . If  $Y$  is a set of rank at most 2, which is true in particular when  $k=m$  or  $k=m-1$ , then the bound follows by a straightforward computation. So we may assume that  $Y$  has rank 3. Let  $t$  be maximum so that  $M|Y$  has a  $U_{3,t}$ -restriction  $N$ .
By the maximality of  $t$ , each point of  $M|Y$  is spanned by a pair of elements of  $N$ .
So  $M|Y$  is a union of  $\binom{t}{2}$  lines, each with at most  $\binom{2(m-k)}{2}-1$  points not in  $E(N)$ .
Since  $t\leq 2(m-k)$ , it follows that  $M|Y$  has at most  $\binom{2(m-k)}{2}(\binom{2(m-k)}{2}-1)+2(m-k)$  points.
∎
Note that if  $k=m$  then  $M|Y$  has  $0$  points, and if  $k=m-1$  then  $M|Y$  has no  $U_{3,3}$ -restriction and therefore has rank at most  $2$ .
So if  $k\in\{m-1,m\}$ , then  $M$  is covered by  $m$  lines.
In particular, when  $m=2$ , Theorem 6.2 implies that every rank- $3$  matroid with no  $U_{3,5}$ -restriction is either the union of two lines, or has at most  $34$  points.
We have a similar result for matroids with no  $U_{3,2m+2}$ -restriction; we omit the proof as it is nearly identical to the proof of Theorem 6.2.
Theorem 6.3.
Let  $m\geq 2$ .
If  $M$  is a rank- $3$  matroid with no  $U_{3,2m+2}$ -restriction, then there is an integer  $k$  with  $0\leq k\leq m$  and disjoint sets  $X$  and  $Y$  with  $X\cup Y=E(M)$  so that
• 
 $M|X$  is a union of  $k$  lines, and
• 
 $M|Y$  has no  $U_{3,2(m-k)+2}$ -restriction (if  $k<m$ ), no  $U_{2,\binom{2(m-k)}{2}+2}$ -restriction, and at most  $\binom{2(m-k)+1}{2}\big(\binom{2(m-k)+1}{2}-1\big)+2(m-k)+1$  points.
Note that if  $k=m$  then  $M|Y$  has no  $U_{2,2}$ -restriction and therefore consists of at most one point, and if  $k=m-1$  then  $M|Y$  has no  $U_{3,4}$ -restriction and is therefore the direct sum of a line and a point by Lemma 5.2.
So if  $k\in\{m-1,m\}$ , then  $M$  is covered by  $m$  lines and a point.
Roughly speaking, Theorems 6.2 and 6.3 reduce the problem of finding  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,t})$  to analyzing matroids with a small number of points.
We do this for  $t=5$ .
Lemma 6.4.
Let  $M$  be a rank- $3$  matroid with no  $U_{3,5}$ -restriction. Either
 $(a)$  
 $M$  has no  $U_{2,5}$ -minor, or
 $(b)$  
 $M$  is the union of two lines.
Proof.
We may assume that  $M$  is simple, because the statement holds for  $M$  if and only if it holds for  $\si(M)$ .
An ordered pair  $(L_{1},L_{2})$  of lines of  $M$  is good if  $|L_{1}-L_{2}|\geq 4$  and  $|L_{2}-L_{1}|\geq 2$ .
First suppose that  $M$  has a good pair  $(L_{1},L_{2})$  of lines.
Suppose there is a point  $w$  of  $M$  outside of  $L_{1}\cup L_{2}$ .
Let  $x,y\in L_{2}-L_{1}$ .
The lines  $\cl_{M}(\{w,x\})$  and  $\cl_{M}(\{w,y\})$  each contain at most one point in  $L_{1}-L_{2}$ , and so there are points  $s,t\in L_{1}-L_{2}$  that are not spanned by any pair of  $\{w,x,y\}$ .
But then  $\{w,x,y,s,t\}$  forms a  $U_{3,5}$ -restriction of  $M$ , a contradiction.
So  $E(M)\subseteq L_{1}\cup L_{2}$ , and therefore  $(b)$  holds when  $M$  has a good pair of lines.
Next suppose that  $M$  has a line  $L_{1}$  with  $|L_{1}|\geq 5$ .
There are points  $x$  and  $y$  of  $M$  outside of  $L_{1}$ , or else  $(b)$  holds.
Let  $L_{2}=\cl_{M}(\{x,y\})$ .
Then  $(L_{1},L_{2})$  is a good pair of lines of  $M$ , and so  $(b)$  holds.
So we may assume that  $M$  has no good pairs and no lines with at least five points.
We will show that  $(a)$  holds. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that  $M$  has a  $U_{2,5}$ -minor.
Since  $M$  has a  $U_{2,5}$ -minor, there is a point  $e_{0}$  so that  $M/e_{0}$  has a  $U_{2,5}$ -restriction.
Then  $e_{0}$  is on at least five lines of  $M$ , because the lines of  $M$  through  $e_{0}$  correspond to the points of  $M/e_{0}$ .
Let  $T$  be a transversal of five of the lines through  $e_{0}$ .
Since  $M$  has no line with at least five points,  $M|T$  has rank  $3$ .
And since  $M|T$  is a simple rank- $3$  matroid with five points, it has a  $U_{3,4}$ -restriction or contains a line with four points. In the former case, adding  $e_{0}$  to the  $U_{3,4}$ -restriction gives  $U_{3,5}$ , because no pair from  $T$  spans  $e_{0}$ , and this is a contradiction. In the latter case,  $(L_{1},L_{2})$  is a good pair of lines, where  $L_{1}\subseteq T$  is the line with four points and  $L_{2}=\cl_{M}((T\setminus L_{1})\cup\{e_{0}\})$ , and this is again a contradiction.
∎
We can use the previous lemma to find  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(n,3,U_{3,5})$ .
Recall that  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,3,U_{2,5})\leq b(3,3)\cdot(\frac{n}{13})^{3}$ , by Theorem 4.5, where  $b(3,3)$  is the number of bases of the ternary projective plane, which is  $\frac{13\cdot 12\cdot 9}{3!}=234$ .
Theorem 6.5.
If  $n\geq 14$ , then
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,3,U_{3,5})\leq\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{3}-\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}.$ 
Furthermore, equality holds when  $n$  is even, so  $\pi_{\mathsf{M}}(3,U_{3,5})=3/4$ .
Proof.
Let  $M$  be an  $n$ -element, rank- $3$  matroid with no  $U_{3,5}$ -restriction and  $b(M)=\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{3,5})$ .
Either  $(a)$  or  $(b)$  holds from Lemma 6.4.
If  $(a)$  holds, then  $b(M)\leq\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,5})$  and therefore by Theorem 4.5,
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,3,U_{3,5})\leq\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,3,U_{2,5})=b(3,3)\Big(\frac{n}{13}\Big)^{3}=234\Big(\frac{n}{13}\Big)^{3}<\Big(\frac{n}{2}\Big)^{3}-\Big(\frac{n}{2}\Big)^{2},$ 
where the last inequality holds because  $n\geq 14$ .
So  $(b)$  holds, and  $M$  is the union of lines  $L_{1}$  and  $L_{2}$ .
It is straightforward to check that  $b(M)$  is maximized when  $L_{1}$  and  $L_{2}$  are disjoint lines and have  $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$  and  $\lceil n/2\rceil$  points, respectively.
Then  $M$  has at most  $(\frac{n}{2})^{3}-(\frac{n}{2})^{2}$  bases, as desired.
∎
By Proposition 2.2, the basis hypergraph of a  $U_{3,5}$ -free matroid is  $K^{(3)}_{5}$ -subgraph-free. Turán [41] conjectured that  $\pi(K^{(3)}_{5})=3/4$ , and Theorem 6.5 confirms this conjecture for matroid basis hypergraphs. The extremal construction of bases in the rank- $3$  matroid consisting of two lines is a special case of a family of examples due to Keevash and Mubayi, showing that  $\pi(K^{(3)}_{5})\geq 3/4$  – see [27, Section 9]. The actual value of  $\pi(K^{(3)}_{5})$  remains unknown.
7 Directions for future work
We close by discussing directions for future work relating to Problem 1.1.
7.1 Matroid truncations
As discussed in Section 2, some of the strongest constructions for daisy-free hypergraphs are naturally cast in matroidal terms.
We believe that this connection can be further explored.
Ellis, Ivan, and Leader proved the following lower bound on  $\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t))$ .
Theorem 7.1 (Ellis, Ivan, and Leader [18]).
Let  $q$  be a prime power,  $m$  a positive integer and  $s$  even, and  $t=\lfloor sq^{\frac{2m}{s}+1}+1\rfloor$ .
Then
 $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(s,t))\geq 1-q^{-(m+1)}-O(q^{-(m+2)}).$ 
In the special case that  $s=m=k$  and  $q=2$ , this shows that  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(k,8k+1))=1-O(2^{-k})$ .
Improving on this, Alon [1] showed that  $\lim_{r\to\infty}\pi(\mathcal{D}_{r}(k+2\log_{2}k,2k+3\log_{2}k))\geq 1-2^{-k}$  where  $k$  is some large fixed integer. Both of these results on link Turán densities are of interest due to applications to lower bounding vertex Turán densities of the hypercube, see [18] for further details. For recent progress regarding link Turán densities and vertex Turán problems in the hypercube, see [13, 19].
Just as in Theorem 2.1, both constructions are matroidal.
When  $n=k(q^{m+r}-1)$  for a positive integer  $k$ , they consider the  $\mathbb{F}_{q}$ -representable matroid whose elements are the nonzero vectors in  $\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m+r}$  with  $k$  copies of each vector.
(This matroid simplifies to the projective geometry  $\text{PG}(m+r-1,q)$ .)
They then take the  $m^{th}$  truncation of this matroid, where the  $m^{th}$  truncation of a rank- $(r+m)$  matroid  $M$  is the rank- $r$  matroid  $T^{(m)}(M)$  whose bases are the rank- $r$  independent sets in  $M$  [35, Proposition 7.3.10].
Then Theorem 7.1 follows by showing that truncations do not lead to  $U_{s,t}$ -minors with  $t$  arbitrarily large. Alon’s bound is obtained by improving the analysis in their proof utilizing the classic Plotkin bound [36] from the theory of error-correcting codes. We propose the following problem.
Problem 7.2.
Let  $q$  be a prime power and let  $r$  and  $m$  be integers with  $r\geq 2$  and  $m\geq 0$ .
For all  $s$ , what is the maximum  $t$  so that  $T^{(m)}(\text{PG}(r+m-1,q))$  has a  $U_{s,t}$ -minor?
It will certainly be difficult to give an exact answer. Even when  $m=0$ , the problem is equivalent to the question over which fields the uniform matroid  $U_{s,t}$  is representable [35, Problem 6.5.19], an open problem in projective geometry and coding theory. However, as shown by Theorem 7.1, even coarse bounds can have significant consequences for vertex Turán problems on the hypercube.
7.2 Discrete geometry
By restricting the parameter  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,s,U_{s,t})$  to affine matroids over  $\mathbb{R}$  (see [35, Proposition 1.5.1]), we obtain the following natural problem.
Problem 7.3.
Let  $n,s,t$  be positive integers with  $t\geq s\geq 3$ .
Among all sets of  $n$  vectors in  $\mathbb{R}^{s-1}$  with no  $t$  in general position, what is the maximum number of  $s$ -sets in general position?
One can ask this question for vectors over other fields as well, but we focus on  $\mathbb{R}$  for simplicity.
In the special case  $s=3$ , this problem is asking for the maximum number of non-colinear triples among  $n$  points in  $\mathbb{R}^{2}$  with no  $t$  in general position.
This is in some sense dual to a problem of Erdős [20] that asks for the minimum cardinality of a set of points in general position among  $n$  points in  $\mathbb{R}^{2}$  with no four on a line; see [7] for recent progress.
By noting that the lower bound of Proposition 6.1 also holds for  $\mathbb{R}$ -representable matroids, Theorem 6.5 solves Problem 7.3 in the case  $(t,3)=(5,3)$ .
We expect that the parameter  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,s,U_{s,t})$  can be used to solve Problem 7.3 in other cases as well.
We mention one other direction for future work relating to Section 6.
For a positive integer  $a$ , the  $a$ -covering number of a matroid  $M$ , denoted  $\tau_{a}(M)$ , is the minimum number of sets of rank at most  $a$  required to cover the ground set of  $M$ .
In particular,  $\tau_{1}(M)$  is the number of points of  $M$ , and  $\tau_{2}(M)$  is the number of lines needed to cover the ground set of  $M$ .
Theorems 6.2 and 6.3 imply that if  $M$  is a rank- $3$  matroid with no  $U_{3,t}$ -restriction, then  $\tau_{2}(M)=O(t^{2})$ .
On the other hand, the  $U_{3,t}$ -free matroids exhibiting the bounds of Proposition 6.1 have  $2$ -covering number  $\Omega(t)$ .
This leads to the following problem.
Problem 7.4.
Find the maximum  $2$ -covering number for rank- $3$  matroids with no  $U_{3,t}$ -restriction.
More generally, one could ask for the maximum  $(s-1)$ -covering number for rank- $s$  matroids with no  $U_{s,t}$ -restriction, but we will focus on  $s=3$  for simplicity.
Problem 7.4 has several interesting variants and extensions.
It is interesting even if we restrict to the class of  $\mathbb{R}$ -representable matroids: what is the maximum number of lines needed to cover  $n$  points in  $\mathbb{R}^{2}$  with no  $t$  in general position?
One could also allow rank to increase and ask for the maximum  $2$ -covering number for rank- $r$  matroids with no  $U_{3,t}$ -minor; this would be an analogue of Theorem 4.8.
Problem 7.4 also fits nicely with more general but coarser results of Geelen and Nelson [25, 34] which show that there are very few possibilities for the behavior of  $a$ -covering numbers for matroids in minor-closed classes.
We should also mention that while Problems 1.1 and 7.4 ask for specific properties of the class of matroids with no  $U_{s,t}$ -minor, there are a number of deep conjectures about the overall structure of matroids in this class; see [21, 22].
7.3 Exact Turán basis numbers
The bound on  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})$  in Theorem 4.5 is tight whenever  $t$  is a prime power and  $n$  is a multiple of  $\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ ; in this case the bound is attained by the matroid that is obtained from  $\text{PG}(r-1,t)$  by replacing each element by a parallel class containing  $n/\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$  elements. In this section, we speculate on the exact value of  $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}(n,r,U_{2,t+2})$  when  $n\leq\frac{t^{r}-1}{t-1}$ .
Let  $t$  be a prime power, and let  $r$  and  $c$  be integers such that  $1\leq c\leq r-1$ . Let  $F$  be a rank- $(r-c)$  flat of  $\text{PG}(r-1,t)$ , and let  $\text{BB}(r-1,t,c-1)$  be the matroid obtained by restricting  $\text{PG}(r-1,t)$  to the complement of  $F$ . Note that  $\text{BB}(r-1,t,c)$  has  $\frac{t^{r}-t^{r-c}}{t-1}$  points and rank  $r$ .
Problem 7.5.
Let  $t>2$  be prime power, and let  $r$  and  $c$  be integers such that  $1\leq c\leq r-1$ . Is it true that
 $\ex_{\mathsf{M}}\left(\frac{t^{r}-t^{r-c}}{t-1},r,U_{2,t+2}\right)=b(\text{BB}(r-1,t,c-1))?$ 
The statement holds when  $t=2$  and  $r\leq 3$ . This is immediate for  $r\leq 2$ ; for  $r=3$ , it can be shown that a matroid attaining the maximum number of bases is simple, after which the statement follows from a case analysis.
The matroids  $\text{BB}(r-1,t,c)$  play a role in extremal matroid theory similar to that of balanced complete multipartite graph in extremal graph theory: the matroid  $\text{BB}(r-1,t,c)$  does not contain a  $\text{PG}(c,t)$  as a submatroid, and Bose and Burton [12] showed that it is the densest submatroid of  $\text{PG}(r-1,t)$  with this property.
7.4 Minor-closed classes
For a minor-closed class  $\mathcal{M}$  of matroids, let  $\ex_{\mathcal{M}}(n,r)$  be the maximum number of bases of an  $n$ -element, rank- $r$  matroid in  $\mathcal{M}$ . Our proof of Theorem 4.5 relies heavily on Theorem 4.2, which provides an upper bound on the number of elements of a rank- $r$  matroid with no  $U_{2,t+2}$ -minor.
The function  $h_{\mathcal{M}}$  that maps a positive integer  $r$  to the maximum number of elements of a simple matroid in  $\mathcal{M}$  with rank at most  $r$  is the extremal function or growth rate function of  $\mathcal{M}$ .
Our proof of Theorem 4.5 leads to the following question: can  $h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)$  be used to find  $\ex_{\mathcal{M}}(n,r)$ ?
We give a concrete example in which this may be of interest.
Let  $\mathcal{N}$  be the class of binary matroids with no  $\text{PG}(2,2)$ -minor ( $\text{PG}(2,2)$  is also commonly called the Fano plane, and denoted by  $F_{7}$ ).
Kung [29, Corollary 6.4] proved that  $h_{\mathcal{N}}(r)=\binom{r+1}{2}$  with equality holding for the graphic matroid of a complete graph on  $r+1$  vertices.
This leads to the following natural problem.
Problem 7.6.
Let  $n,r$  be integers with  $n\geq r\geq 1$ . Determine  $\ex_{\mathcal{N}}(n,r)$  for the class  $\mathcal{N}$  of binary matroids with no  $F_{7}$ -minor.
If for every large  $n$  there is an  $n$ -element, rank- $r$  graphic matroid  $M$  with  $b(M)=\ex_{\mathcal{N}}(n,r)$ , a solution would generalize Kelmans’ result [28] on the maximum the number of spanning trees of an  $(r+1)$ -vertex graph with  $n$  edges.
More generally, the Growth Rate Theorem [23] shows that there are only four possibilities for the behavior of  $h_{\mathcal{M}}$  for a minor-closed class  $\mathcal{M}$ : linear, quadratic, exponential, or infinite.
We close with the following open-ended question: does the classification of extremal functions lead to a corresponding classification of Turán basis densities  $\pi_{\mathcal{M}}(r)$  for minor-closed classes of matroids?
