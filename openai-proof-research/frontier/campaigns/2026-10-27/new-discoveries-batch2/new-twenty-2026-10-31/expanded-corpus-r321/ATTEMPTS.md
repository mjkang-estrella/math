## A. 373447: co-diameter-two co-chordal classification

Source 2603.05100, preliminaries, Section 7 proof context, Section 7.1 and Section 8 conjecture read. Graphs are finite, simple and undirected. Non-trivial minimal toughness is positive finite toughness with every edge deletion strictly lowering it. The paper explicitly identifies the split subcase as already classified by Katona–Varga (reference 23); that companion proof has not been independently read here, so no novelty clearance rests on it.

Known direction, checked completely: let G have three mutually adjacent centers, each with l >= 1 private pendant vertices. G is split, hence co-chordal. Its complement has diameter two: every center-center pair has a common complement neighbor among the third center's leaves; a center and its private leaf have a common complement neighbor among another center's leaves. These are exactly the edges of G, so all nonadjacent complement pairs are covered.

For any cut S containing r centers and s leaves, if r=1 or 2, the remaining centers and their remaining leaves form one component and at most r*l leaves become isolated. Thus c(G-S) <= r*l+1 and |S| >= r, giving |S|/c >= r/(r*l+1) >= 1/(l+1). If r=0 the remainder is connected. If r=3, the remaining graph consists of 3*l-s leaves, and the ratio (3+s)/(3*l-s) >= 1/l whenever it is a disconnecting cut. Removing just one center realizes 1/(l+1). Thus toughness is exactly 1/(l+1). Deleting a pendant edge disconnects G. Deleting center edge ab and then removing the third center c leaves l pendant singletons plus the two star components, giving toughness at most 1/(l+2). Every edge is therefore essential. This is the known family direction only.

Full converse mechanism attempted: a non-split co-chordal graph contains an induced C4, since split graphs are exactly the graphs with no induced 2K2, C4, C5 and co-chordality excludes 2K2 and C5. Co-diameter two additionally says every edge uv has a vertex nonadjacent to both endpoints. Try to combine those witnesses with minimal-toughness cut witnesses to find a redundant edge of the induced C4. This needs a universal uncrossing lemma forcing one of its edges to retain toughness after deletion. I did not prove it. Simplicial-vertex arguments used in Section 7 for complement distance >=3 do not transfer directly: their separated neighborhoods overlap in the diameter-two case. Neither merely exhibiting a net nor verifying all S(l,l,l) proves the converse. No full endpoint and no Pi plan.

## B. 373703/373704: tensor perfect matching

Actual source 2603.18253 Definition 1.2, Conjectures 1–5, Theorems 1–6 and their relevant Hall/Tutte/inequality proof bodies were inspected in excerpts. A matching is an involution with adjacency to its image; loops and fixed points are allowed. The support graph on cells (x,y) has a loop exactly when M(x,y)>0. Thus odd vertex count is not an obstruction.

First full mechanism attempted: take a perfect matching of the bipartite double, viewed as a permutation of cells. Even cycles can be replaced by adjacent transpositions. Odd cycles cannot in general be paired internally, but one might absorb an odd cycle through a looped cell using the rectangular crossed-entry rule. The required simultaneous absorption theorem for all odd cycles is not proved. A concrete tempting obstruction fails: for the 3x3 zero-diagonal, off-diagonal-one matrix, the three diagonal cells form a triangle without loops. Nevertheless pair (1,1) with (2,3), pair (2,2) with (3,3), and fix each remaining off-diagonal cell. The crossed entries of each pair are one, so this is a valid involution. This is one hand object, not an enumeration.

Second full mechanism: use source Lemma 3.7 (Tutte with loops). In the row-k/column-n multigraph every cell has weighted degree nk. For every odd loop-free cell set U of size t, the sufficient cut condition is
sum_{i,j} M(x_i,y_j) M(x_j,y_i) <= (t-1)*n*k.
The source proves this when all x_i are distinct and all y_i are distinct, by spectral or multi-affine arguments. For repeated coordinates the selected-cell matrix duplicates original row/column mass, so its row/column norms need not stay bounded by those of M. This is precisely the missing bound, not a cosmetic index change. The repeated coordinates are required for the full literal claim. Hall on the double supplies only the initial permutation, not an involution. Source Theorem 6 relates the two assigned records; no double credit could arise.

## C. 374646: regularity of mutable lattices

Read latest 2605.09601v2 Definitions 3.1–3.2, Lemma 3.4 with proof, AC/mutation criterion and related Section 3 proofs, Definitions 5.1–5.2, Lemmas 5.3–5.5 and Theorem 5.6 with proof, Example 5.9, Conjecture 5.10 and Propositions 5.13–5.15 with proofs. The earlier v1 Conjecture 6.10 has the same regularity claim. The examples/figure captions in the appendix were read as text, not visually verified figures.

A flip keeps the undirected Hasse graph. For a mutable lattice all lattices reached by mutations are locally mutable. Local mutability yields a bijection between atoms and coatoms, so minimum and maximum vertices have the same degree. Source Proposition 5.15 gives an antipodal graph automorphism; this gives equality between opposite vertices, not between every pair.

First full mechanism attempted: transport the least element to any vertex by source Theorem 4.10, using mutability to keep each orientation a lattice, then propagate the number of atoms across adjacent mutations. Let a be an atom in a nontrivial lattice, with r atoms. Its original degree is 1 plus its number of upper covers. In the mutation with a as the new minimum, the number of atoms becomes exactly that degree. Therefore the missing local assertion is that every atom has exactly r-1 upper covers. This would propagate constant degree along the connected Hasse graph and prove the full claim. The source AC-correspondence partitions the lattice into a principal ideal and filter, but I did not derive that cover count.

Second mechanism attempted: match other atoms b to upper covers of a via a join b. This proposed map already fails to land in covers in the pentagon lattice: choose a to be the atom on the longer side and b the other atom. Their join is the top, with an intermediate element above a. The pentagon is a source mutable polygon, so the failure does not refute regularity; it refutes the proposed proof. Semidistributivity alone does not repair this map. The source's locally mutable non-mutable example cannot refute a claim quantified over mutable lattices. Known polygon, Boolean, Coxeter and small Cambrian examples are source cases, not new discoveries. Full degree transport remains unproved; no candidate.
