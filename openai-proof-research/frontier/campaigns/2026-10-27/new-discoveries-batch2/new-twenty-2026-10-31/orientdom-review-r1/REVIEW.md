## B. Independent mathematics — PASS

Here is an independent derivation of the substantive step. Let D,E be valid orientations of the same simple graph. Assume that each arc of D that E reverses is the only D-arc entering its head. Let S totally dominate D. For any reversed x→y, choose z→y in E. If z→y were present in D, uniqueness at y would give z=x, impossible. Thus y→z is in D and reversed in E. Uniqueness at z forces y into every total dominating set of D, in particular S. This proves that **each head of a disagreement arc lies in S**. Now take a vertex y with an S-predecessor x in D. If x→y survives, domination survives. Otherwise choose z→y in E again. The reversed D-arc y→z shows that z is a disagreement head and hence z∈S. This gives an S-predecessor in E. Thus TD(D,S) implies TD(E,S) under the forced-head hypothesis.

Consequently if S dominates D but not E, some reversed a→b has a second D-predecessor w≠a at b. Flip only a→b. The vertex b retains w→b; all other vertices retain their old incoming arcs. This gives validity and TD(F,S∪{w}), so γ_t(F)≤γ_t(D)+1 for minimum S. The new b→a agrees with E, no other arc changes, and the disagreement count decreases by exactly one. If γ_t(D)<c≤γ_t(E), a minimum S for D cannot dominate E. Repeatedly apply this step, using new minima each time. The count strictly decreases, while γ_t stays at most c; induction must reach equality. Monotone increase of γ_t is neither proved nor needed.

Adversarial points checked:

- The vertex w is a distinct predecessor of b, not necessarily a member of S. Inserting w repairs b. Any incoming domination w itself already had is retained because only b loses an arc. Loops and w=b are ruled out by asymmetry.
- The uniqueness hypothesis is essential. In the forced-head argument the reversed **tail** is forced into S by domination of its next vertex; this is why the first conclusion about heads is correct. The proof does not mistakenly infer membership from domination of the head itself.
- Valid orientations need not be connected under valid single-edge flips: the two directed orientations of a cycle are isolated. Therefore the ordinary full-hypercube continuity argument cannot simply be invoked. In this stalled example both values are equal. The lemma establishes the relevant stalled-case domination comparison in general.
- Parallel-edge identity and 2-cycles are not encoded or claimed. The source is an orientation of a simple graph, not an arbitrary digraph; accepting the proof does not justify a theorem for multigraph orientations with opposite parallel arcs.

It independently enumerates **every labeled simple graph and every orientation**, with no connectivity filter and no imported discovery code. Orders 0–6 have, respectively, 1,0,0,1,22,603,25418 admissible graphs and 1,0,0,2,122,16168,5545708 valid orientations. All spectra are intervals (`logs/exhaustive.log`). The n=6 difference from the lane's connected counts is exactly the 10 labeled disjoint pairs of triangles and their 40 orientations. This is diagnostic evidence, not the general proof.

## E. Significance and exclusion — QUALIFIED_PASS

The result would settle the full finite simple-graph conjecture, rather than a parameter value, an endpoint correction, a small instance or a formalization of a cited theorem. I found no defect, no duplication with a known accepted family, and no direct reduction to the 1999 vertex-deletion construction or 2023 bipartite construction.

The final strong induction and the fact that one safe flip changes a minimum by at most one are routine discrete continuity. The potentially new content is narrower and concrete: if all target-directed flips are blocked by an indegree-1 head, every total dominating set transfers to the target orientation. This overcomes a real obstruction, illustrated by isolated cycle orientations. It is not valid to call the entire proof an immediate hypercube-continuity corollary while ignoring that obstruction. Conversely, uniqueness forcing a vertex into every dominating set is elementary published machinery; using that idea twice is not, by itself, evidence of substantive novelty. The missing 1996 comparison is precisely what decides whether this is a new repair of the classical proof or a routine adaptation already present in it.

The inspected sources do not explain a technical barrier or establish how much expert effort the general case received. Its repeated conjectural status supports interest, not originality. I therefore provisionally favor eligibility if the listed comparisons clear the forced-head step, but cannot recommend acceptance packaging on the present evidence. I do **not** recommend EXCLUDE solely because the proof is short. Root should retain zero credit until D is cleared and E can be adjudicated on that evidence.
