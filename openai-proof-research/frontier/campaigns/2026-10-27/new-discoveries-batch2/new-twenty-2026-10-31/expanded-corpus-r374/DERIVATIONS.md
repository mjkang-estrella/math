Integral gaps follow from the parity conditions, including toward-facing gaps because vector lengths are integers. Define L=n-3k and y_i=2x_i+v_i-3i. Then y_(i+2k)=y_i+2L. Legal pairs imply strict increasing y; the source's 4k≤n ensures L≥k>0. The compressed velocity u(v)=sign(v)(2|v|-3) has values (-1,+1,-1,+1) on (-2,-1,+1,+2). Without a FIX, 2v+TURN(v)-v = v+TURN(v)=u(v). Thus y advances by its compressed unit velocity.

For the collision pair with old gap 1 and vectors (-1,+1), old compressed separation is 2+2-3=1; free motion would cross. JUMP changes gap to 3, TURN gives (+2,-2), and FIX gives (+1,-1). The old y positions (a,a+1), with velocities (+1,-1), become sorted free positions (a,a+1), with exchanged velocities (-1,+1). Adjacent collision pairs are disjoint because a stone cannot have both required signs. Remaining legal cases require the pair inequalities, not a blanket assumption that collisions never occur.

## 325944: double intervals; two developed routes and the exact unpaid step

Source 1307.5094 defines a(S) as the maximum NUMBER of approving voters, and a(S)/n as approval ratio. The record calls a(S) a ratio, an internal transcription inconsistency. We investigate the intended source inequality, not manufacture credit from dividing an already normalized ratio by n. Approval sets consist of two disjoint closed intervals on one common line. Pairwise intersecting means intersections of whole approval sets, not every individual component pair.

Route A: endpoint accounting, including the source's strengthened boundary correction. Write a for maximum depth; each voter contributes intervals A_i,A'_i. Define L,R,B,C of a component I as the numbers of other components covering just its left endpoint, just its right endpoint, both endpoints, or lying in its interior. Each voter meets every other voter, so the sum over its two components of L+R+B+C is at least n-1. Summing gives n(n-1)≤Σ(L+R+B+C). A containing pair contributes once to B at the inner interval and once to C at the outer, hence ΣB=ΣC.

No point is in more than a intervals: a voter's own components are disjoint. Initial endpoint capacities are Σ(L+B)≤2n(a-1) and Σ(R+B)≤2n(a-1). Order the 2n left endpoints. The first a positions have capacities 0,1,...,a-1 instead of all a-1, saving a(a-1)/2; order right endpoints from the right for the same saving. Thus
n(n-1) ≤ 4n(a-1)-a(a-1) = (4n-a)(a-1).
Solving this quadratic yields the source bound a≥ceil(2n+1/2-sqrt(3n²-n+1/4)), asymptotically (2-sqrt3)n. This is a complete known source deduction, not a full proof of one third. The defect is concrete: n=19,a=6 satisfies the relaxed inequality because 19·18=342≤(76-6)·5=350, although 6<19/3. This manual integer calculation is only a relaxation-feasible pair, not an actual society or a counterexample. No enumeration was run. The source's example n=8,a=3 and double-string constructions remain compatible with one third.

Route B: piercing rather than aggregate counts. If three points hit all approval sets, double-counting incidences gives n≤Σ_{j=1}^3 depth(p_j)≤3a. Repeated voters only increase the sum; no distinctness assumption is needed. Choose the component I with smallest right endpoint p, belonging to voter v, whose other component is J. Any component meeting I contains p: it ends no earlier than p and starts no later than p. Consequently every approval set avoiding p must intersect J, since it must intersect I∪J but cannot intersect I. This reduction is exact.

The tempting next claim that residual voters have a common component point, or can all be hit by two more points, has not been proved. Their restrictions to J can be TWO intervals, not one interval, and pairwise intersection of full approval sets need not be preserved under restriction to J. Helly's interval theorem cannot simply be invoked on them. The exact sufficient missing lemma is: for every pairwise-intersecting double-interval family F and the earliest-right-endpoint p above, F_p={A∈F:p∉A} has a transversal of size at most 2. Alternatively any correct three-point piercing theorem on this exact non-separated domain would subsume the desired n≤3a argument. That theorem's body has not been read here.

They are HOLD, not asserted applicable or novel. Scott's 2011 thesis, explicitly cited by the source, and a separate final/corrections body remain unread. ## 325430: symmetry, exponential rates, boundary obstruction, and the intended gap

The 2013 thesis 1304.6432, final Conjecture3, says the S-walks in the half-plane with inward normal along symmetry axis L have the same exponential growth as quarter-plane walks. Its standing walk convention starts at the origin. The later author-hosted journal article (Johnson–Mishna–Yeats, AAM92 (2018)144–163, accepted August1 2017) states general sharp bounds under hypotheses and names Garbit–Raschel (2016), On the exit time from a cone for random walks with drift, and Duraj (2014). Their actual relevant proofs/corollaries have not been acquired. They are specific HOLDs, not clearance from the journal's narrative.

Route A: inventory symmetry and exponential tilting. For finite S define P(z)=Σ_s exp(<z,s>), with z in the nonnegative dual quadrant. Reflection in L preserves S, hence P. Convexity makes averaging z with its reflected value decrease P. However reflection in a general first-quadrant axis need not preserve the nonnegative dual quadrant! Thus averaging over the entire feasible cone is not automatically legal. A symmetry-axis minimizer and the equality of the quadrant growth rate with the constrained inventory infimum both need hypotheses and proof. Even with a valid minimizer, the easy containment gives only Q_n≤H_n. For a tilted law p(s)=exp(<z,s>)/P(z), the lower bound still requires subexponential cost of survival in the quadrant (and endpoint tilt control). The journal's Theorem2 and Lemma7 concern half-plane projections and do not by themselves prove quadrant survival. A path from the origin into a suitable interior buffer is a real semantic requirement, not a harmless convenience.

Route B: constructive lifting/blocks. A would-be injection of half-plane walks into quadrant walks can add a buffer and arrange zero-sum or positive-drift blocks. To preserve exponential growth it must lose only exp(o(n)) possibilities and use o(n) added steps. Reflection of an entire path across L does not ensure both coordinates nonnegative at every prefix; independent reflections of prefixes need closure of the transformed step set and compatibility at the joining point. Those properties were not established. A buffer cannot be appended at the start unless it is itself S-reachable in the quadrant. Thus this is a precise failed full mechanism, not a proof from an upper bound.

There is a complete literal degeneracy witness: S={(-1,1),(1,-1)}, L={x=y}, H={x+y≥0}. Reflection exchanges the steps. L passes through the quadrant interior. Every unrestricted word stays on x+y=0, hence H_n=2^n. Neither first step is in the quadrant, so Q_n=0 for all n≥1. The exponential rates are respectively2 and0 (equivalently finite versus infinite generating-function radius). No numerical search or solver is involved. This meets the unqualified literal statement, but it concerns a trivial inaccessible boundary model of the sort the enumeration literature separates out. It is a domain correction with credit0; it does not refute a repaired nontrivial/accessibility version. We do not assert an unnoticed new substantive counterexample. Both the precise original model-domain passages and the later journal's nontriviality qualifications are retained. The intended theorem is HOLD for the exact Garbit–Raschel and Duraj bodies and the full reachable/nonsingular scope bridge. No Pi plan.

Finite C5 cannot be permuted with infinite R, so Aut(G)=Aut(C5), dihedral of order10. Nonidentity rotations move5 vertices. A reflection fixes one vertex and exchanges two pairs, hence moves4. Therefore m(G)=4 and 2^m=16≥10.
