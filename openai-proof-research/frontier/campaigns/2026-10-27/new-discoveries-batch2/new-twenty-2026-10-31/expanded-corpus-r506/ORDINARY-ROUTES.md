Pay the entire new bridge without Burnside: fix x with walk matrix W=[e_x,Ae_x,...,A^(n-1)e_x] invertible over R. Since A is symmetric, for all 0<=r,s<n the product A^r E_x A^s=(A^r e_x)(A^s e_x)^T belongs to T1. The n vectors A^r e_x form a basis over R and C. The n^2 outer products form a basis of M_n(C): their arbitrary linear combinations are W B W^T with B arbitrary, and any matrix M equals W(W^(-1) M W^(-T))W^T. Thus T1=M_n(C), and hence T2=M_n(C). This is simultaneous for all x on the controllability event. No confusion with controllability from the all-ones vector is allowed.

At p=1/2 the measure is uniform labelled simple graphs. A graph satisfying all-base controllability is connected for n>1: otherwise all A^r e_x vanish off its component. Thus this event itself implies connectedness. Consequently the fraction of bad graphs among connected labelled graphs is at most P(bad)/P(connected), and P(connected)>=P(all-base controllable)->1. It tends to zero. n=1 is full already. This pays conditioning without importing an unproved connectivity estimate. An unlabeled-uniform interpretation is not silently substituted; the source leaves almost-all measure unstated. To extend to unlabeled counting would require a separate asymmetry/counting bridge not asserted here.

This agrees with the direct walk-basis proof and is not an extra credited family. Just simple spectrum alone fails because e_x could miss an eigenspace.

## 348800: two definition-sensitive routes
Original2011.13163v1 pp4--6,13 and actual final AAMAS2022pp651--660 (definition passage and Conjecture1 p657) both print the probability of visiting i before absorbing at k, summed over j,k excluding i. They also call it current-flow betweenness and cite Newman. Actual Newman cond-mat0309045v1 Section2.2 pp7--9 instead cancels opposite traversals and uses the net flow, half the sum of absolute incident voltage differences. Those are different quantities. On a pendant vertex, an excursion can hit the vertex with positive probability but net current from source to target elsewhere is zero. This is a concrete primary-definition conflict, not an inference from simulations.

Route A, connected hitting-probability model. Let i,u be nonadjacent in a connected graph. For target k!=i define h(z)=P_z(tau_i<tau_k). On the finite graph h(i)=1,h(k)=0, and h is harmonic elsewhere. Adding edge i-u changes no harmonic equation except at u and adds a pull toward boundary value1. The maximum principle gives h_new>=h_old. More explicitly, subtract the killed graph Laplacian equations: L_new(h_new-h_old)=(1-h_old(u)) e_u on vertices other than i,k. L_new is positive definite, and its inverse has nonnegative entries by the minimum principle. Thus every ordered pair contribution to C_i weakly increases. To get strictness choose k as the predecessor of u on a shortest i-u path. The path has length>=2, so k!=i,u and P_u(tau_k<tau_i)>0, hence 1-h_old(u)>0. The inverse diagonal at u is positive, so the contribution starting at j=u strictly increases. The symmetric argument gives strict centrality gain for endpoint u. Finitely many positive gains let one choose epsilon less than both, and addition improves both utilities. Thus every connected noncomplete graph is unstable under the printed hitting-probability definition.

Deletion from K_n,n>=3 strictly decreases both endpoint hitting centralities by the reversed argument. For endpoint i and removed neighbour u choose a third k; h_old(u)<1 because u has a direct edge to k. The postdeletion graph stays connected; killed inverse diagonal positivity gives strict loss. Choose epsilon smaller than the finite positive losses for all endpoints/deletions: K_n is APSN. K2 has no internal source-target pairs, so its centrality is zero and deletion saves cost; it is not stable. That endpoint defect earns zero credit.

The extension from connected to disconnected networks is NOT silently solved: the printed sum does not specify treatment of unreachable absorbing targets. If contributions are restricted to connected source-target pairs and zero otherwise, any nonempty disconnected graph has an improving cross-component addition: choose endpoint u in a component A with >=2 vertices and endpoint i in another B. For i, ordered pair u,k with k another vertex in A has positive hit-i probability after bridging, whereas it had zero; all existing within-component pairs retain their old hitting probabilities until first hitting the endpoint and cannot decrease. For u, pair starting i targeting k in A hits u certainly. The symmetric existing contributions are nondecreasing. This gives strict gains and excludes disconnected nonempty graphs under THAT explicit convention. It does not authenticate that convention from the source.

Route B, actual Newman net-current definition. The preceding boundary-edge monotonicity proof concerns hitting probability and does not prove monotonicity of sums of absolute currents. On a connected graph where i,u are linked by a path, adding i-u redistributes individual edge currents with signs; Rayleigh's effective-resistance monotonicity alone does not prove the endpoint absolute-current centrality gains. Disjoint clique-plus-isolate ideas depend additionally on how disconnected normalization and endpoints are handled, so no full counterexample is asserted. Exact unpaid lemmas: simultaneous endpoint net-current gain or an exact stable noncomplete graph under authenticated disconnected convention, plus source-definition fidelity. A small K2 endpoint or the definition repair would not earn credit. This full target is PARK, despite a complete connected-model helper proof.
