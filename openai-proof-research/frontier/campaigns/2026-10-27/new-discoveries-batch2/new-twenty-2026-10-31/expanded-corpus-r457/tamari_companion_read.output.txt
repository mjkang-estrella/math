                                                                       PHILIPPE BIANE AND WENJIE FANG

                                                 Abstract. The greedy Tamari poset, inspired by the well-studied Tamari lattice, was
                                                 recently defined by Dermenjian in the more general setting of greedy ν-Tamari posets.
                                                 Bousquet-Mélou and Chapoton counted intervals of the greedy m-Tamari poset in 2024
                                                 by solving a functional equation, and found that they are equi-enumerous to planar
                                                 (m + 1)-constellations. In this work, we give a combinatorial proof of this fact for the
                                                 case m = 1, which also gives the refined enumeration conjectured by Bousquet-Mélou

                                                 and Chapoton. This is done by establishing a recursive decomposition of greedy Tamari
                                                 intervals isomorphic to that of bipartite planar maps. We also propose a more general
                                                 and refined conjecture for the case of general m.




                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                             The Tamari lattice is a much studied object in combinatorics and geometry, whose
                                          definition goes back to the work of Dov Tamari in 1962 [Tam62], see [MHPS12] for infor-
                                          mation on this subject. Later, generalizations of the Tamari lattice, such as the m-Tamari
                                          lattice [BPR12] and the ν-Tamari lattice [PRV17], were proposed with motivation from
                                          other domains of combinatorics, such as the study of diagonal coinvariant spaces [BPR12].
                                          It has been observed, since the seminal work of Chapoton [Cha06], that counting intervals
                                          in the Tamari lattice and its variants often yields formulas that also count objects related
                                          to planar maps, which are drawings of graphs on the plane. Such results are often first
                                          obtained by solving functional equations, as in [Cha06], then reproved bijectively, as in
                                          [BB09; Fan18; FFN25]. However, there is the exception of the intervals of m-Tamari
                                          lattices. Their counting formula obtained in [BMFPR12] looks close to those for the enu-
                                          meration of planar maps, but no combinatorial proof is known, nor any natural family of
                                          planar maps with the same counting formula. It is thus a surprising news that Bousquet-
                                          Mélou and Chapoton found and proved in [BMC24] that the number of intervals in the
                                          greedy m-Tamari poset, whose definition is inspired by that of the m-Tamari lattice, is
                                          equal to the number of certain planar maps.
                                             The greedy m-Tamari poset was considered recently by Dermenjian in [Der23] as a
                                          special case of the greedy ν-Tamari poset, defined on the same ground set as ν-Tamari
                                          lattices, but with cover relations produced by a “greedy” version of the rules for ν-
                                          Tamari lattices. When defined on m-ballot paths of the same size, we obtain the greedy
                                          m-Tamari poset, which was shown in [Der23, Theorem 34] to be isomorphic to the sub-
                                          poset of elements with maximal in-degree in the (m + 1)-Tamari lattice. Soon afterwards,
                                          Bousquet-Mélou and Chapoton enumerated in [BMC24] the number of intervals in the
                                          greedy m-Tamari poset by solving a functional equation based on a recursive decompo-
                                          sition of such intervals. They found that the counting formula is the same as that of
                                          planar (m + 1)-constellations, a family of planar maps first enumerated in [BMS00] using

   Our main contribution in this article is proving [BMC24, Conjecture 6.1] combina-
torially for m = 1 by exhibiting a recursive decomposition of greedy Tamari intervals
that is isomorphic to the classical recursive decomposition of planar bipartite maps from
Tutte [Tut62]. Such an isomorphism gives naturally a recursively-defined bijection be-
tween greedy Tamari intervals and planar bipartite maps. Furthermore, we show that
the statistics used in the refined counting on each side are transferred according to the
conjecture. The greedy Tamari poset was originally defined on lattice paths, but in our
proof we realize it on a certain family of plane trees, which provides a better under-
standing of the intervals we study. Unfortunately, we did not succeed in adapting our
approach to the general m case. However, we will propose a generalized and refined ver-
sion of [BMC24, Conjecture 6.1] related to the recursive decomposition of constellations
in [Fan16, Section 4.1].
   This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give the definitions of the greedy
Tamari poset and its intervals, using both Dyck paths and plane trees. We also define
bipartite planar maps and their recursive decomposition from Tutte. We then present
in Section 3 the recursive decomposition of greedy Tamari intervals, which leads to the
recursively-defined bijection between these intervals and bipartite maps. Finally, in Sec-
tion 4 we consider the m-versions of the previous objects (namely m-greedy Tamari
posets and (m + 1)-constellations) and we state a generalized and refined conjecture for
a bijection between these objects.


                  2. Greedy Tamari intervals and bipartite maps
2.1. The greedy Tamari poset on Dyck paths. We start by defining the greedy
Tamari poset over Dyck paths following [BMC24; Der23]. In the following, we denote by
|w|a the number of letters a in a word w, and by · the concatenation of words.
   A Dyck path of size n is a word w on the alphabet {0, 1} with |w|0 = |w|1 = n, such
that for each prefix w′ of w, we have |w′ |0 ≤ |w′ |1 . We denote by Dn the set of Dyck
paths with 2n letters. Such w ∈ Dn is usually represented as a lattice path on Z2 starting
from (0, 0), with up steps (1, −1) (resp. down steps (1, 1)) standing for the letter 1 (resp.
0), while staying in the upper half plane y ≥ 0 and ending at (2n, 0). An ascent (resp.
descent) of a Dyck path w is a maximal segment of consecutive 1’s (resp. 0’s) in w, while
a valley (resp. peak ) is an occurrence of the factor 01 (resp. 10).

Definition 2.1. For w ∈ Dn with a marked valley, we write w = A · 01 · A′ , where 01 is
the valley. Let w◦ be the longest prefix of 1 · A′ that is also a Dyck path, then w has a
factorization w = A · 0 · w◦ · A′′ . Let w′ = A · w◦ · 0 · A′′ , this is clearly a Dyck path and we
declare that w ⋖g w′ . The relation ⋖g thus obtained by considering all w ∈ Dn with all
possible marked valleys, is the cover relation of an order relation (Dn , ≤g ) the transitive
closure of ⋖g . The greedy Tamari poset is (Dn , ≤g ). The relation ≤g is also called the
greedy Tamari order. See Figure 1 for an example of the cover relation ⋖g and the greedy
Tamari poset of size 4.

   There is a unique maximal element in (Dn , ≤g ), namely the path 1n 0n , as it is the only
element without valley, thus never covered. However, unlike the classical Tamari lattice,
in general (Dn , ≤g ) does not have a unique minimal element, hence it is not a lattice.

2.2. Plane trees and Dyck paths. We denote by Tn the set of rooted plane trees with
n edges, which are always drawn with the root at the bottom. There is a well-known
bijection between Dyck paths in Dn and plane trees in Tn .
Definition 2.2. Given a plane tree T , its contour path, denoted by wT , is the Dyck
path obtained from the left-to-right contour walk of T , where the first traversal (resp. last
traversal) of an edge is transcribed into a letter 1 (resp. 0). It is clear that the leaves of
T correspond bijectively to the peaks of wT . See Figure 2 for an example.
    In the following, for a given plane tree T ∈ Tn , we denote its nodes by u0 , u1 , . . . , un
in the order of first visit in the left-to-right depth-first search order, that we call the
pre-order hereinafter. See Figure 2 for an illustration. In this context, we also say that ui
is the i-th node of T in the pre-order. In particular, the 0-th node is always the root. For
i > 0, we denote by ei the edge linking ui to its parent. It is clear that, in the left-to-right
contour walk of T , edges of T are visited for the first time in the order e1 , . . . , en .
    For ui a leaf of T , let j ≥ 0 be the smallest index such that, for all ℓ with j ≤ ℓ < i, the
node uℓ is the parent of uℓ+1 . We call the sequence of edges ({uℓ , uℓ+1 })j≤ℓ<i the ascent of
ui , which is of length i−j. It is clear from Definition 2.2 that each ascent of T corresponds
to an ascent of wT of the same length. We denote by fasc(T ) the length of the only ascent
of T containing the root u0 , which corresponds to the first ascent of wT . The ascents of

2.3. The greedy Tamari poset on trees. For a plane tree T ∈ Tn , an inner corner of
T at a node v is a corner at v between two consecutive children of v, namely v ′ and v ′′ .
It is clear from Definition 2.2 that the inner corners of T are in bijection with the valleys
of its contour path wT .
Definition 2.3. For a plane tree T ∈ Tn with a node v ′′ that is not the leftmost child of
its parent v, we define the slide-up operation at v ′′ as follows:
    (1) Take v ′ be the child of v preceding v ′′ , thus (v, v ′ , v ′′ ) form an inner corner;
    (2) Detach edges of v to subtrees rooted at v ′′ and all children of v to its right;
    (3) Slide these detached subtrees up along the edge {v, v ′ } and reattach them to v ′ .
See Figure 3 for an example of the slide-up operation.
   By abuse of notation, denote by ⋖g the relation on Tn such that T ⋖g T ′ if T ′ can be
                                                                  (i)
obtained from T by a slide-up. More precisely, write T ⋖g T ′ if T ′ is obtained from T ′ by
a slide-up at the i-th node ui of T in the pre-order. The relation ⋖g is the cover relation
of an order ≤g and we denote by (Tn , ≤g ) the corresponding poset. See Figure 3 for an
illustration of (T4 , ≤g ).
  We note that the slide-up at a vertex v is possible if and only if v is not the leftmost
child of its parent. Our use of the same notation ⋖g for the orders on Dn and Tn is
justified by the following proposition.
Proposition 2.4. The poset (Tn , ≤g ) is isomorphic to the greedy Tamari poset (Dn , ≤g )
by taking the contour path.

T ⋖g T ′ if and only if w ⋖g w′ . Indeed, let c = (v, v ′ , v ′′ ) be an inner corner of T . The
largest Dyck subpath w◦ obtained in Definition 2.1 from the valley of w corresponding
to c is given by the part of the contour walk of T on all the subtrees of v after the inner

4.1. Planar constellations and their decomposition. A planar m-constellation is a
special planar map where faces are colored black and white, with the outer face colored
white and each edge bordering a black and a white face. Furthermore, black faces are
called hyperedge and are all of degree m. White faces are called hyperfaces and are all of
degree divisible by m. See Figure 14 for an example of a planar constellation. The size
of an m-constellation is the number of its hyperedges. Note that we allow the unique
                                                                            (m)
m-constellation of size 0, which consists of a single vertex. We denote by Cn the set of m-
                                                              (m)
constellations of size n. One may also encode elements in Cn with certain (m +1)-tuples
of permutations in Sn , see [LZ04]. One may identify planar 2-constellations with rooted
planar bipartite maps by transforming each hyperedge into an edge, as every hyperedge
is of degree 2.

Remark 4.4. The definition of m-constellations given here is in fact what is usually
called m-hypermaps in the literature. For m-constellations, there is a further condition
on vertex coloring. However, in the planar case the two notions coincide, which allows us
to slightly simplify the definition. Readers are again referred to [LZ04] for further details.
