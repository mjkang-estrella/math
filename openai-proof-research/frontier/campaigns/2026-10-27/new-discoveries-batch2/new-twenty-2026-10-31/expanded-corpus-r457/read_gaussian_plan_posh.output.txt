One can associate a direct combinatorial meaning to Vessenes’ conjecture in the context
of representation theory of GL(V ). Indeed, if it holds true, it would signify that there
is an embedding of the composite of symmetric powers S a (S d (V )) inside S b (S c (V )), as
GL(V )-modules. It may however be more natural to state that there is a surjective
GL(V )-module morphism the other way around (which is also equivalent). Therefore
each GL(V )-irreducible occurs with smaller multiplicity in S a (S d (V )) than it does in
S b (S c (V )), and the conjecture reflects this at the level of the corresponding characters
(with Schur polynomials appearing as characters of irreducible representations).
The sole attempt [9] toward resolving Conjecture 1 was made by F. Zanello, who attends to
the special case a ≤ 3, including the property of symmetry and unimodality. A sequence
of numbers is unimodal if it does not increase strictly after a strict decrease. The author
in [9] offers a strengthening of Conjecture 1 to the effect that
Conjecture 2. Preserve the hypothesis in Conjecture 1. Then, the coefficients of the
symmetric polynomial
