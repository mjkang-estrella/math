Equation (4), given in [Tut62] by the name of “even slicing”, can be explained combina-
torially as follows. Taking out the edge next to the root in clockwise order of a non-empty
bipartite planar map, there are two cases (see Figure 5):
      • The map breaks into two, in which case we root the two pieces at the appropriate
        edge adjacent to the vertices adjacent to the original root. To reconstruct a map
        in this case, we only need to take any pair of bipartite planar maps, add an edge
        from the black vertex of the root of the first component to the white vertex of the
        root of the second, which becomes the root.
      • The map remains connected then, by planarity, the deleted edge must separate
        an inner face and the outer face, thus the deletion adds the degree of the inner
        face to that of the outer face. To reconstruct a map in this case, we only need
        to take a bipartite planar map, then draw a new edge from the root to a white
        vertex to form a new inner face whose degree “comes from” that of the original
        outer face. The operator Ω describes every way to do that.
In both cases, an extra factor xt accounts for the fact that there is a new edge on the
outer face. This implies Equation (4).

3.1. Properties of greedy Tamari intervals. We list a number of useful properties

greedy Tamari order.
Lemma 3.1. For T, T ′ ∈ Tn with T ⋖g T ′ , either asc(T ) = asc(T ′ ), or asc(T ′ ) is obtained
from asc(T ) by merging two consecutive ascents, i.e.,
          asc(T ) = (a1 , . . . , ar ),   asc(T ′ ) = (a1 , . . . , as−1 , as + as+1 , as+2 , . . . , ar ),
for some 1 ≤ s < r. The second case occurs exactly when the edge along which the
slide-up occurs is a leaf.
Proof. Suppose that T ′ is obtained from T by a slide-up at the inner corner (v, v ′ , v ′′ ).
Observe that the only ascent that may be affected is that of the leftmost leaf v̄ of the slid-
up subtree. When v ′ is not a leaf in T , ascents are not changed, thus asc(T ) = asc(T ′ ).
When v ′ is a leaf in T , the ascent of v ′ and v̄ are merged in T ′ , which gives the merging
of components in asc(T ′ ), since v̄ is the next leaf after v ′ in the pre-order of T .     □
Corollary 3.2. Let I = (T, T ′ ) ∈ In be a greedy Tamari interval, with u0 , . . . un (resp.
u′0 , . . . u′n ) the nodes of T (resp. T ′ ) in the pre-order. For any i, if ui+1 is the leftmost
child of ui in T , then u′i+1 is also the leftmost child of u′i in T ′ ; if u′i is a leaf in T ′ , then
ui is also a leaf in T .
Proof. We only need to prove the claim for T ⋖g T ′ . This follows from Lemma 3.1 and
the fact that, in a tree, ui+1 is the leftmost child of ui if and only if they belong to the
same ascent and ui is a leaf if and only if it is the last node in its ascent.            □

the backbone Pt(R, k) of R, then all its descendants are in Pt(R, k).                  □
