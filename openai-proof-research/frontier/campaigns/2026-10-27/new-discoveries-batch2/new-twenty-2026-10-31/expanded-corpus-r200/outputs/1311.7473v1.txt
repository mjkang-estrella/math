                                                                                                   Abstract


                                                         It is known that the Markov matrix of the asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) is invariant
                                                     under the Uq (sl2 ) algebra. This is the result of the fact that the Markov matrix of the ASEP coincides
                                                     with the generator of the Temperley-Lieb (TL) algebra, the dual algebra of the Uq (sl2 ) algebra.
                                                     Various types of algebraic extensions have been considered for the ASEP. In this paper, we considered
                                                     the multi-state extension of the ASEP, by allowing more than two particles to occupy the same box.
                                                     We constructed the Markov matrix by dimensionally extending the TL generators and derived explicit
                                                     forms of the particle densities and the currents on the steady states. Then we showed how decay
                                                     lengths differ from the original two-state ASEP under the closed boundary conditions.


                                                     1    Introduction
                                                     The asymmetric simple exclusion process (ASEP) is such a one-dimensional exclusion process
                                                     that describes discrete one-dimensional random walks. Among far-from-equilibrium systems,
                                                     the ASEP is one of few examples which allows us the exact analysis. This model is first
                                                     introduced in order to discuss the dynamics of ribosome translocation along mRNA [1]. Then
                                                     the model was applied in the context of traffic and transport systems [2]. As the exactly
                                                     solvable non-equilibrium systems, the ASEP has been attracted for decades; After used in
                                                     the context of a diffusion process [3, 4], the connection to the solvable two-dimensional lattice
                                                     system, the six-vertex model associated with the Uq (sl2 ) algebra, was pointed out in [5, 6].
                                                     Based on the Uq (sl2 )-invariance of the Markov matrix, the dynamical exponent was discussed
                                                     besides the particle-density profiles and the particle currents computed on the steady state
                                                     under the closed boundary conditions [7].
                                                         The standard procedure to analyze the ASEP with general boundary conditions is called
                                                     the matrix product ansatz (MPA) [8]. By assigning a matrix to each of the empty and
                                                     occupied state, the steady state is given by the combination of those matrices which satisfy
                                                     the quadratic relations. Employing this method, various physical quantities such as particle-
                                                     density profiles and particle currents, as well as steady states, were exactly computed. The
                                                     existence of the phase transition in the particle densities with respect to incoming and outgoing
                                                     hopping rates was obtained from the exact calculation [9, 10, 11, 12]. Matrices casted in a
                                                     matrix product state satisfies quadratic relations which are understood in the context of the


                                                                                                        1
q-boson [13] and later whose connection with the Askey-Wilson polynomials was pointed out
[14].
    The Bethe ansatz method, first introduced to solve many body problems [15], was also ap-
plied to the computation of physical quantities on the steady state of the ASEP. The periodic
boundary condition [6], the system with infinite length [16, 17], and the open boundary case
[18] were discussed in each literature. The relation to the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) univer-
sality classes was also discussed through the dynamical exponent derived from the analysis
of the Bethe-ansatz equations for the periodic boundary case [6] and through the asymptotic
behavior of correlation functions for the closed boundary case [7]. Even though the connec-
tion between the MPA and the Bethe vectors have not been clear for years, this question
was unveiled in [19], by expressing creation and annihilation operators of the algebraic Bethe
ansatz in terms of the operators in the matrix product states.
    The model was algebraically extended to be associated with various algebras. The pos-
sible realizations of the Hecke algebra were pointed out which describe the time evolution of
reaction-diffusion processes [20]. Among various algebraic extensions, the ASEP with multi-
species has been closely studied [21, 22, 23]. The steady state was constructed by the MPA,
whose matrix elements satisfy similar quadratic relations as in the one-species case [24]. The
spectrum of the Markov matrix was also studied on the periodic system, in which the dynam-
ical exponent was found to be the same as the one-species case [25]. It has been also pointed
out that various models are mapped onto the ASEP. The interesting example is the zero-range
process [26], which often appears as another traffic model with hopping rates depending on
the number of particles in each box.
    In this paper, another algebraic extension of the ASEP, the ASEP associated with the
higher-dimensional representation of the Uq (sl2 ) algebra is proposed. The original two-state
ASEP is characterized by the two-dimensional basis defined by the empty state and the
one-particle state. The idea is based on the dimensional extension of the Markov matrix,
which results in the multi-state extension of the ASEP. Although it is not straightforward
how to extend the Markov matrix itself to the higher-dimensional one by keeping integra-
bility of the model, we found the similarity transformation which maps the Markov matrix
onto the TL generators. The dimensional extension of the TL generators was proposed in
[27], by using the notion of fusion. Applying the projection operator q-symmetrizes the ℓ
spaces of the two-dimensional representations by allowing to take out the (ℓ + 1)-dimensional
representation, the highest weight representation. The virtue of this dimensional extension
employing the fusion procedure is that, be keeping the commutativity with the Uq (sl2 ) gen-
erators, the TL generator is dimensionally extended. Consequently, the Markov matrices
associated with higher-dimensional TL generators have the Uq (sl2 )-invariance but describe
multi-state stochastic processes. This construction of the multi-state ASEPs suggests the ex-
istence of a new family of integrable stochastic models associated with the higher-dimensional
representations.
    It is an interesting question how the number of states in the model affects on physical
behaviors. For example, we would expect that the different exponent in decay lengths for the
ASEPs with different number of states. Due to the possible variety of incoming and outgoing
hopping rates, the rich structure of phases in the particle-density profiles is expected under
the general boundary conditions. This paper focus on phenomena in the steady state under
the closed boundary conditions, in which the whole system is invariant under the Uq (sl2 )
algebra. We first derive the steady states of the multi-state ASEPs, which are given by
the basis of the Uq (sl2 ) algebra. Although the computation is cumbersome if one works

on the high-dimensional representations, we showed the computation of the high-dimensional
representations are reduced onto the two-dimensional representations by utilizing the property
of the projection operators and the comultiplication of the Uq (sl2 ) generators. We derive the

ASEP. The Section 3 is devoted to the model settings of the multi-state ASEPs, including the
construction of the higher-dimensional TL generators and the similarity transformations in
order for a Markov matrix to satisfy the principle of preservation of probability. In the Section
4, we show the exact calculation of the particle-density profiles and the particle currents as
the examples of physical quantities that can be computed on the multi-state ASEPs. Then
we give a concluding remarks and remaining problems in the Section 5.

3        Multi-state ASEP
As we reviewed in the previous section, the update operators of the ASEP satisfy the TL
relations. Based on this fact, the extension of the process to multi-state cases is, taking ac-
count that a state of each box is given by (4), achieved by constructing the higher-dimensional
update matrix which obeys the TL relations. The dimensional extension of the TL generators
have been discussed by P. Zinn-Justin in [27], in which they used the fusion procedure, which
is such a method to construct the (ℓ + 1)-dimensional representation from the ℓ spaces of the
two-dimensional representation. Although the higher-dimensional TL generators themselves
cannot be update operators, as they meet neither the probability conservation nor the posi-
tivity of probability in nature, we found such similarity transformations which make the TL
generators of an arbitrary dimensional representation describe stochastic processes.

the projection operator to the ℓth fold of the tensor product of two-dimensional represen-

highest weight representation.

In this section, we discuss the steady state of the multi-state processes constructed in the
previous section. The steady state |Pst i is such a state that is invariant under the time
development:
                                         d
                                            |Pst i = 0.                                 (30)
                                         dt
Due to the invariance of the TL algebra with respect to the transformation q ↔ q −1 , we
restrict our attention to the regime q > 1; more hopping to the right than to the left.
    The steady state and physical quantities of the closed system have been intensively studied
in [7] for the two-state ASEP based on the Uq (sl2 ) algebraic relations. However, the multi-
state extension requires, although the model still belong to the same algebra, drastically
cumbersome computation even for the norms on the steady states. We resolve this difficulties
by expressing all the calculations in terms of the original two-state system.
    Since we constructed the update matrix of the multi-state ASEPs in order to be invariant
under the Uq (sl2 ) algebra, the steady state is given by the zero eigenvectors of the Uq (sl2 )
generators.

zero-particle state) is then the steady state of the (ℓ + 1)-state ASEP and mapped onto the
vacuum state of the two-state ASEP with length ℓN :

6    Conclusions
In this paper, we have constructed the multi-state asymmetric simple exclusion processes
based on the fusion procedure of the Temperley-Lieb algebra, satisfied by the Markov matri-
ces. Motivated by the higher-spin extension of the integrable quantum spin chains, we have
discussed the new family of integrable stochastic processes. As is in the case of ASEP, the
multi-state processes given by this paper have the corresponding quantum spin chains, whose
wave functions are exactly discussed by the Bethe ansatz method. This fact implies that the
multi-state ASEP also admits the exact calculation of physical quantities through the Bethe
ansatz method.
    The existence of the similarity transformation which makes the Markov matrix to sat-
isfy the probability conservation law is significant, since otherwise the higher-dimensional
Temperley-Lieb generators do not describe any stochastic processes. It is also important to
find the proper combination of the twisted generators such that the positivity of probability
is satisfied. All these are possible due to the beautiful algebraic structure of the Temperley-
Lieb algebra, which still holds for the extended model. Based on this algebraic structure, we
computed the particle-density profiles and the particle currents on the steady state. Then we
obtained the existence of currents in the steady state, which is one of the interesting features
of nonequilibrity. The specific feature of the multi-state processes has been obtained in the
decay lengths which strongly depend on the number of states of the system. The decay lengths
were defined from the rising and falling exponent of the particle-density profiles obtained from
the asymptotic analysis.
    Although we focused on the closed model on which the Temperley-Lieb algebraic structure
is conserved for the whole system, it is more interesting to consider the general boundary
conditions. The open system would be solved via the matrix product ansatz, although we
did not find the matrix product steady state. We hope this attracting problem will be soon
resolved.
