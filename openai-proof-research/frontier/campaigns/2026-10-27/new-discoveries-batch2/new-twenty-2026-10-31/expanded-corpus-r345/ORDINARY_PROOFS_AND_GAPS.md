## 361991: full exact finite witness, already subsumed by published resolution

The literal endpoint quantifies over all n ≥ 4 and all collections of k n-edged graphs. One counterexample with n = 4 refutes it. Source Conjecture 3.20 explicitly means multisets, so repeated members are allowed. Edge deletion retains the vertex set. The v1 and v2 bodies, and the actual 2025 Homology, Homotopy and Applications journal body, Theorems 3.21–3.22, were read. This is not the single-graph edge-reconstruction conjecture assigned separately as 365666.

An initial shortcut was to quote the source's rank argument. That does not give an explicit object, and cardinality comparisons need care if isolated vertices are allowed. I therefore worked out an explicit fixed-vertex witness instead.

Use vertex set {0,1,2,3,4}. Let A have edges 01,02,03,04 (the 4-star), B have edges 01,12,23,34 (the 5-vertex path), C have edges 01,02,03,34 (the tree of degree sequence 3,2,1,1,1), and D have edges 01,12,23,30 (a 4-cycle and an isolated vertex). Each is simple and has exactly four edges.

Write S for K1,3 with one isolated vertex, P for the 4-vertex path with one isolated vertex, and Q for the disjoint union of the 3-vertex path and an edge. These are pairwise nonisomorphic: S and P have an isolated vertex but different maximum degrees, while Q has no isolated vertex. Their degrees are respectively (3,1,1,1,0), (2,2,1,1,0), and (2,1,1,1,1).

Deleting any edge of A leaves S, so ED(A)=4S. In B, deleting either end edge leaves P, while deleting either of its two internal edges leaves Q; hence ED(B)=2P+2Q. In C, deleting 34 leaves S; deleting 03 leaves Q; deleting either 01 or 02 leaves P. Thus ED(C)=S+2P+Q. Deleting any edge of D leaves P, so ED(D)=4P. These classifications specify every edge, rather than relying on a finite search.

Consequently 4 ED(C)=4S+8P+4Q=ED(A)+2 ED(B)+ED(D). The multisets {C,C,C,C} and {A,B,B,D} are distinct: C has maximum degree 3, while A has maximum degree 4, B maximum degree 2, and D has a cycle. They contain the same number k=4 of graphs, all on five vertices with n=4 edges, and have identical union decks including multiplicities. This proves the exact negation of the literal universal claim, with no semantic bridge left for the ordinary finite witness.

Sustained second route: the edge-deck map is a derivation on formal sums with disjoint-union multiplication: deleting one edge in X disjoint-union Y deletes it either in X or in Y. Thus d(XY)=d(X)Y+Xd(Y). A kernel element can be squared to make new kernel elements, with positive and negative coefficients separated into graph multisets; card count forces equal collection cardinalities. The source already develops exactly this route in Remark 3.25, Proposition 3.26 and Corollary 3.27, and even supplies k4 ≤ 2. Thus our k=4 witness is weaker than a published construction. No new novelty or significance is claimed from its explicit size. No Pi plan is appropriate.

## 371076: exact literal interpretation versus source definition

The corpus defines dim-boundedness using the indicated graph parameter; at fixed t its literal reading demands an absolute bound on dimension for all posets with K_t-minor-free cover graphs. The actual primary source defines it differently: dim(P) ≤ f(se(P)), where se(P) is the maximum standard-example order. Both available arXiv versions v1 and v2 contain this definition. The source explicitly says Kelly posets have treewidth at most 3 and arbitrarily large dimension, and its conjecture concerns bounding dimension by standard-example order even in minor-excluded classes. A literal correction is not a new mathematical result.

Here is the complete earlier-result bridge disproving the absolute-bound reading. Published Kelly construction gives, for every d ≥ 5, a poset P_d with planar cover graph containing the standard example S_d as an induced subposet in the order-theoretic sense (all order relations restricted to its ground set). This construction is stated and illustrated in the actual Combinatorica 36 (2016), pp. 431–450 journal copy, Figure 3 and discussion on p. 438; the present source also gives Figure 2 and this family. Planarity implies exclusion of K5 as a minor: edge deletion and contraction preserve planarity, whereas K5 is nonplanar. Restriction of any family of linear extensions realizing P_d gives a realizer of S_d, so dimension is monotone on subposets.

For completeness, the needed lower bound does not require quoting a dimension formula. In S_d there are incomparable pairs (a_i,b_i) and relations a_i < b_j for i ≠ j. Some realizing extension must put b_i before a_i for each i. A single linear extension cannot do this for two distinct i,j, since it would contain b_i < a_i < b_j < a_j < b_i, a strict cycle. At least d extensions are required. Therefore dim(P_d) ≥ d. If fixed-t dim-boundedness meant an absolute bound M at t=5, choose an integer d greater than M and at least 5, contradiction.

This bridge is complete relative to the explicitly published Kelly planarity/construction theorem. I have not reconstructed a crossing-free embedding of every P_d independently, and only the figure captions and extracted labels, not a rendered figure, were inspected. This distinction prevents representing the packet as an independent construction certificate. The original 1981 article body was not acquired. The 2016 journal body is a substantive source for the construction, not merely a catalogue record. No claim that the source's true se-bounded conjecture is disproved follows. The exact literal absolute-bound refutation is a classical/source bridge and a definition correction, so it earns zero regardless of size or ease.

Second attempted route: use the standard example alone as a small obstruction. This fails for arbitrarily large d because its own cover graph ceases to be planar. Passing to Kelly's containing poset is essential. Likewise a single fixed P_d cannot refute an unspecified finite bound; the entire published family is required. No candidate or implementation is proposed.

## 361848: sustained parity induction, averaging, and branching obstruction

Source Definition 1.3 and Section 5 use the asymptotic random-tournament benchmark 2^{-e(D)} for homomorphism density. The 2026 companion Definition 1.1 makes the exact finite-host inequality explicit: hom(D,T) ≤ 2^{-e(D)} n^{v(D)} for every finite tournament T. Homomorphisms need not be injective. Source Proposition 5.2 and its proof were read in full. The 2026 companion introduction, path/spider theorem statements, and final related-work paragraph were read; the latter corrects the primary source's assertion about the (2,2,2,2)-spider. Do not use that assertion as a counterexample. The December 2025 comparator's normalization involving t_K2 differs at finite n from the constant 1/2 benchmark; it was not silently substituted. Its claimed equivalences were not independently justified here. September 2026 Chen–Lin work gives long paths with bounded direction flips, not all trees.

A complete rooted partial theorem can be proved without any numerical test. Suppose a rooted tree R has an even number of children at every vertex. There is one orientation D, fixed independently of the host, such that for every tournament T on n ≥ 1 vertices and every prescribed image x of the root, the number of homomorphisms D→T taking the root to x is at most n^{v(R)-1}/2^{e(R)}. Induct on v(R). The single vertex has exactly one such map. Otherwise take a deepest vertex with children; all those children are leaves, and there are at least two. Remove two children u,w and apply induction to R'. Orient the removed path u→v→w. For each homomorphism R'→T with v-image y, the number of ways to replace u,w is d^-(y)d^+(y). Since d^-(y)+d^+(y)=n-1, this is at most (n-1)^2/4 ≤ n^2/4. Multiplying the bound for R' proves the claim; v increases by 2 and e by 2. Summing over root images gives the anti-Sidorenko bound. The orientation choice depends only on the tree and induction, so quantifiers are correct.

If an unrooted tree has exactly one even-degree vertex ρ, root there. Its root has even child count and every other vertex has deg-1 children, again even, so the partial theorem applies. If every vertex has odd degree, choose any edge and delete it. Root each resulting component at the endpoint of that edge; every child count in both components is even. Orient the two components by the rooted partial theorem and orient the deleted edge from the first root to the second. If their rooted counting functions are a(x) and b(y), they are bounded pointwise by n^{v1-1}/2^{e1} and n^{v2-1}/2^{e2}. The total homomorphism count is the sum of a(x)b(y) over the n(n-1)/2 arcs xy of T. It is therefore at most n^{v1+v2}/2^{e1+e2+1}. This is exactly the published parity family, not a new endpoint.

First failed extension: at a vertex with an odd number of remaining children, pair all but one. A single leftover edge contributes d^+(y) or d^-(y); neither is uniformly at most n/2 for a fixed orientation. A vertex at an end of a transitive tournament has one degree n-1. For n>2 that exceeds n/2. Thus the required rooted estimate is false even for the rooted single edge. This is a concrete obstruction to that strengthened invariant, not a refutation of the unrooted conjecture.

Second extension: average over all orientations of an undirected tree with m edges and m+1 vertices. For each proper vertex map into an n-vertex tournament, exactly one of the 2^m orientations fits. A tree has n(n-1)^m proper maps into the underlying complete graph, by choosing a root image then each child image different from its parent. Hence average homomorphism count is n(n-1)^m/2^m ≤ n^{m+1}/2^m. For each host some orientation works. However, the desired endpoint is one orientation that works for every host; ∀T∃D cannot be exchanged with ∃D∀T. A host-independent mixed orientation distribution is not a pure orientation satisfying the conjecture. No minimax conclusion closes this gap.

Third extension: decompose into directed paths or three-leg spiders and glue their roots. The available unrooted inequalities control sums over the image of the root. The count after gluing is a sum of products of rooted profiles; separately bounded sums do not bound this product sum by the product of the desired uniform averages. Root profiles can be positively correlated. The companion's full spider proofs contain additional inequalities; their conclusions alone give no arbitrary branching closure. The missing mathematical lemma is an orientation rule and rooted profile inequality stable under arbitrary nonisomorphic branch gluing, with sufficient control on these correlations. No such lemma was proved. It would be misleading to replace it by a solver architecture or generic certificate plan. The general exact endpoint remains unpaid, material related work is not exhaustively reviewed, and no Pi execution is requested.
