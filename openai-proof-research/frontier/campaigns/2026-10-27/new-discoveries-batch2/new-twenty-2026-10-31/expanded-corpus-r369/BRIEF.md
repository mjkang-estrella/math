## Sustained mechanism 1 — 335255, square-zero ideals

**Exact claim examined.** Fish, arXiv:1702.02544v2 (13 February 2017), Conjecture 1, PDF p.5, text lines 234–239: for every alpha>0 there are N,k depending only on alpha such that every sufficiently large finite commutative ring R and every E of density at least alpha have a subring R0 contained in (E−E)(E−E) with |R|/|R0|<=k. The whole six-page original text was displayed/read. The separately hosted author PDF repeats Conjecture 1 (text lines 239–243). This is not restricted to fields, reduced rings, cyclic additive groups, or characteristic tending to infinity.

**First failed shortcut.** Taking E to be a large additive subgroup does not by itself control products. For cyclic rings, Fish's Corollary 1.2 positively supplies a bounded-index ideal. That argument cannot be transferred simply to arbitrary finite commutative rings: additive recurrence produces scalar multiples, and a bounded integer scalar can annihilate the entire additive group in small characteristic. The missing structural premise is precisely multiplicative nondegeneracy, absent in the conjecture.

**Full ordinary counterexample.** Fix alpha=1/2. For each positive m let V=F_2^m and define R=F_2×V with componentwise addition and

(a,v)(b,w)=(ab, aw+bv).

The additive group is abelian; (1,0) is the multiplicative identity and (0,0) is zero. Multiplication is commutative. Distributivity follows from linearity of scalar multiplication. Associativity follows because both ((a,v)(b,w))(c,z) and (a,v)((b,w)(c,z)) equal

(abc, ab z+ac w+bc v).

Consequently R is an associative unital commutative finite ring, not a nonunital convention artifact. Its cardinality is 2^(m+1). Let E={0}×V, a proper ideal of cardinality 2^m. Then |E|=|R|/2. Subtraction preserves E, and every (0,v) equals (0,v)−(0,0), hence E−E=E. For all v,w, (0,v)(0,w)=(0,0), and zero occurs. Thus

(E−E)(E−E)={(0,0)}.

Every nonempty subring contained in that set is {0}, of cardinality 1. If subrings must contain the ambient identity, there is no such subring at all, since 1≠0. Either convention defeats the assertion. Under the more permissive convention the index is |R|, unbounded. Given proposed N,k, choose m with 2^(m+1)>max(N,k); this exists since powers of 2 are unbounded (inductively 2^j>=j+1). Then R meets the size and density hypotheses while every possible R0 has index>k. This negates the entire existential uniform-bound assertion, not merely a selected finite instance. No search or numerical experiment is needed.

**Second route and obstruction check.** The equivalent quotient presentation F_2[x_1,...,x_m]/(x_i x_j:all i,j) has unique normal form a+sum v_i x_i and the same multiplication. The direct-pair model above avoids depending on an unproved normal-form theorem. Its radical has fixed additive index 2 and arbitrarily large dimension, explaining why even increasing |R| cannot repair the conjecture. Taking a fixed ring or one failed bounded index alone would not refute the quantifiers; the whole family is essential.

**Actual prior/source check and precise hold.** Fish's integer Corollary 1.1 and cyclic Corollary 1.2 were read with their proofs. They do not subsume this family: its additive group has exponent 2 and rank m+1. Shkredov, *On some multiplicative properties of large difference sets*, journal-hosted PDF DOI 10.4153/S0008414X23000500, Proposition 5.2 and Corollary 5.3, explicitly require integers 1,...,floor(alpha^-1) to be units. At alpha=1/2 our ring has 2=0, so that premise fails. Its Theorem 5.4 concerns Z/qZ and an additional least-prime-factor premise; neither applies. This is a checked nonmatch, not absence of another result elsewhere in that paper or literature.

## Sustained mechanism 2 — 341196, binder overcount

Read Bodmann–King arXiv:1811.11513v2 (10 January 2020), Theorem 4.12 (triple-product simplex criterion), Lemma 5.2's proof/formula, Proposition 5.3's construction/proof, Conjecture 5.12, and its surrounding equivalence discussion. Its target parameter at p=3 and tuple length J=3 is (729,27,13).

**Failed extrapolation.** The correct low-dimensional values lambda=1 at J=1 and lambda=p+1 at J=2 do not determine higher dimensions. Diagonal graph constructions alone do not count the full binder; symmetric off-diagonal parameters give additional genuine blocks. Assuming only affine lines over F_(p^J) would omit many isotropic F_p-subspaces.

**Explicit ordinary obstruction.** Write index vectors as (x,y) in F_3^3×F_3^3. For each (a,b,c) in F_3^3 form the symmetric matrix

A=[[0,0,0],[0,a,b],[0,b,c]]

and the graph B_A={(x,Ax):x in F_3^3}. Projection to x is a bijection, so B_A has 27 points, and all contain the two distinct indices (0,0) and (e_1,0). Matrices with different triples give different graphs: equality of graphs forces equality of the unique y over every x, hence equality on basis vectors and equality of matrices. Thus there are 3^3=27 different such sets, by the direct parameter bijection, without enumeration.

For the canonical alternating form omega((x,y),(z,w))=x·w−z·y, the graph of a symmetric A is isotropic, since x·Az−z·Ax=0. Every difference of graph points remains in this graph. Hence omega(v_1−v_3,v_2−v_3)=0 for every triple of its points. Lemma 5.2's triple-product expression, with all m_i=p and the same primitive pth root, is −zeta raised to a fixed invertible scalar times this alternating expression. It is therefore −1 on every distinct triple in B_A. Theorem 4.12 plus |B_A|=27 proves each B_A is in the binder. One fixed pair is in at least 27 blocks, contradicting lambda=13. This suffices for the literal refutation, without a classification of all blocks.

**Exact earlier-result reduction, zero.** Fickus–Lake arXiv:2302.08879v2 (21 July 2025), Theorem 4.5 and proof, Lemma 4.3(b), Lemma 4.4, and Remark 4.7 were actually read. Remark 4.7 explicitly identifies and disproves Bodmann–King Conjecture 5.12 as stated. It gives the phase change and coordinate identification from Gabor–Steiner vectors to the dual symplectic ETF. Phase changes cancel in triple products, so the binder is preserved. Theorem 4.5 says its blocks are precisely affine Lagrangians, with lambda=product_(j=1)^(J−1)(p^j+1). At p=3,J=3 this is (4)(10)=40, not 13. Therefore the exact original binder fails the prescribed BIBD parameters. The direct 27-block proof above is an explicit subfamily of this prior public mechanism. No credit, variant or novel claim; there is no reason to dispatch it to formalization. ## Sustained mechanism 3 — 335320, weighted transfer and collapsed basis

Read Zaleski arXiv:1702.05634v2 (15 October 2017), Sections 1.3, 2.1–2.3 and 3.1–3.3. Beta sets are order ideals with no consecutive labels, pillar k having h_k beads (0..d, except last 0..d−1); adjacent occupied pillars are forbidden. The size weight is

S=sum_k [k h_k+n h_k(h_k−1)/2]−H(H−1)/2, H=sum_k h_k.

Pre-moment means sum S^r over all allowed beta sets, as follows from (q d/dq)^r G at q=1; it is not the normalized moment. The unweighted count N_d satisfies N(1)=1,N(2)=d,N(n)=N(n−1)+dN(n−2).

**Failed shortcut and sustained route.** Unweighted Fibonacci recurrence alone does not imply the same recurrence for weighted moments: adding a pillar changes both sum of labels and the quadratic H term. Keep joint moments of label sum and H instead. Expanding (W+increment)^a(H+h)^b closes at lower total degree, giving polynomially forced second-order recurrences. Repeated differentiation of a finite-state transfer construction has characteristic roots r_±=(1±sqrt(1+4d))/2 with polynomial multiplicities; polynomial coefficients multiplying the two independent count solutions are plausible. But independence of N(n),N(n+1) fails at d=2: N_2(n)=2^(n−1), so both proposed basis vectors have no (−1)^n component. A proof that ignores that loss is invalid, even when the two characteristic roots themselves are distinct.

**Actual source bridge.** Xiong–Zang arXiv:1804.09091, Theorem 1.5, Remark, identities (1.3)–(1.4), Theorem 4.9 and the proof of 1.5, and Theorem 1.13 with its proof were read. Their corrected independent basis M_d has M(−1)=0,M(0)=1,M(n)=M(n−1)+dM(n−2). They prove power sums are A(n,d)M(n)+B(n,d)M(n+1), with polynomial coefficients in n. For d≠2, substitute the explicitly proved identities

M_d(n)=[d(d−1)N_d(n)−N_d(n+1)]/[d(d−2)],
M_d(n−1)=[(d−1)N_d(n+1)−dN_d(n)]/[d(d−2)],

and M(n+1)=M(n)+dM(n−1). This supplies the exact requested a(n)N(n)+b(n)N(n+1) for every fixed positive d≠2 (and r=0 is the defining count). Thus that range is a complete routine public bridge, not a candidate.

**Exceptional d=2, explicit routine refutation.** At r=1, Theorem 1.13 reduces by rational substitution d=2 to

m_1(2,n)=A(n)M_2(n)+C(n)M_2(n−1),
A=7n^2/48−13n/48+1/8,
C=7n^2/48+n/16−5/24.

The recurrence/initial conditions give M_2(n)=(2^(n+1)+(−1)^n)/3, hence

m_1(2,n)=2^n(21n^2−23n+2)/144+(−1)^n(1−n)/9.

These are hand algebra substitutions, not CAS or numeric fitting. If the literal assertion held at d=2, then dividing by N_2(n)=2^(n−1) would give a polynomial a(n)+2b(n). Subtracting (21n^2−23n+2)/72 would give a polynomial equal to (−1)^n(1−n)/(9·2^(n−1)) for every n≥2. This tends to zero but is nonzero for every n≥2. A real/rational polynomial tending to zero on positive integers is identically zero: a nonzero polynomial of degree>=1 is unbounded in magnitude after dividing by its leading term, and a nonzero constant cannot tend to zero. Contradiction. Thus the literal all-d statement fails. Zaleski already explicitly flags d=2 as anomalous, and the later paper changes the basis specifically to fix it. Under the campaign's source-correction/variant rule, this full explicit reduction receives zero. It does not establish a new mathematical family.
