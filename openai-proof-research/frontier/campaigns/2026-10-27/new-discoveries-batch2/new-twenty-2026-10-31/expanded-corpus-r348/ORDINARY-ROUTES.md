## 369614: reduced graphs and negative inertia

Exact endpoint: every finite simple graph with distinct open neighborhoods and no isolated vertices, with k negative adjacency eigenvalues counted with multiplicity, has at most 2^(k+1)-2 vertices, for every integer k >= 0. This is the definition of reduced in source arXiv:2508.01163, Section 1, not the rank-deletion paraphrase found in the earlier 369613 reservation. Versions v1 Conjecture 2.2 and v2/v3 Conjecture 2.3 retain this endpoint. Theorem 2.4 in v3 is a lower-bound construction, not the missing upper bound.

Route A, exact edge elimination. For an edge uv, order the remaining vertices after u,v and write
A = [[0,1,a^T],[1,0,b^T],[a,b,C]], where a,b are binary column vectors. Congruence elimination of the invertible leading block B=[[0,1],[1,0]] gives B direct-sum S, where S=C-ab^T-ba^T. Thus n_-(S)=k-1 exactly, since B has one positive and one negative eigenvalue. This is stronger than interlacing alone and pays the desired one-unit inertia reduction. But S has diagonal entries -2 a_i b_i and off-diagonal entries in {-2,-1,0,1}. It is not an adjacency matrix in general. For a common neighbor of u,v its diagonal is -2. Deleting common neighbors does not give the required order control. Twin-freeness of A does not imply twin-freeness of S. The recurrence N(k)<=2N(k-1)+2 would yield precisely N(k)<=2^(k+1)-2 from N(0)=0, but no reduction with at most two preimages per residual vertex has been proved. This is the precise missing structural lemma. The Kotlov-Lovasz construction attains that recurrence from below; inverting its construction is unjustified.

Route B, negative spectral projection. Factor the real symmetric matrix as A=P^T P-Q^T Q, using positive and negative eigenvalues and omitting zero eigenvalues. Q has k rows. For columns p_i,q_i, zero diagonal gives ||p_i||=||q_i||. If q_i=q_j, those norms agree and p_i dot p_j=A_ij+||q_i||^2. Cauchy-Schwarz forces A_ij=0, then equality forces p_i=p_j. Consequently the whole i and j columns of A agree, contrary to the reduced condition. Also q_i=0 implies p_i=0 and an isolated vertex. Hence the n vertices inject into R^k minus {0}. Moreover ||p_i-p_j||^2-||q_i-q_j||^2=-2 A_ij. This is an exact geometric reduction, but injection into a continuous space has no cardinality bound. Encoding only coordinate signs loses the equality-of-projections argument: equal signs do not imply equal vectors. A discrete packing theorem sharp at 2^(k+1)-2, respecting these two squared-distance relations, remains unproved. No finite witness or proof of the upper bound follows.

Payable base cases, with full argument. k=0: a PSD zero-diagonal matrix has every entry zero, since its 2 by 2 principal minors give -A_ij^2>=0. No nonempty reduced graph remains. k=1: a triangle has eigenvalues 2,-1,-1; P4 has eigenvalues plus/minus sqrt((3+sqrt(5))/2) and plus/minus sqrt((3-sqrt(5))/2); two disjoint edges have eigenvalues 1,1,-1,-1. Interlacing excludes each as an induced subgraph. There is exactly one nontrivial component, because no isolates are allowed and two components would induce two disjoint edges. The graph is triangle-free and induced-P4-free. Fix an edge uv. Any vertex at distance at least three from u would give an induced shortest path P4, so every vertex is u, in N(u), or at distance two. N(u) is independent by triangle-freeness. Let y have distance two and choose a in N(u) adjacent to y. For any b in N(u) not adjacent to y, y,a,u,b is an induced P4, impossible. Hence all distance-two vertices join all N(u), and no two distance-two vertices are adjacent (they share a neighbor in N(u), which would create a triangle). Thus the graph is complete bipartite. Distinct open neighborhoods force both parts to be singletons, so it is K2. These cases are already in the source's table and receive zero credit.

## 374607: Frankl-Kupavskii exact matching-family bound

Exact quantifiers: m>=1, s>=2, 1<=ell<=ceil(s/2), n=(m+1)s-ell, all finite families F subset of the power set of [n] with matching number less than s. Endpoint: their largest size equals |P|, where P consists of A with |A|+|A intersect L|>=m+1, |L|=ell-1.

Full lower-bound proof. If A_1,...,A_s in P were pairwise disjoint, sum_i (|A_i|+|A_i intersect L|) >=s(m+1). But disjointness bounds that sum by n+|L|=(m+1)s-1, contradiction. So nu(P)<s. The size depends only on |L|: any permutation sending L to L' sends P(L) bijectively to P(L'). Explicitly it is sum over j=0,...,ell-1 of binom(ell-1,j) times sum over t=max(0,m+1-2j),...,n-ell+1 of binom(n-ell+1,t). This is a symbolic identity, not an executed enumeration.

First upper-bound route, complement pairing, actually closes s=2. Then ell=1, n=2m+1 and P consists of sets of size at least m+1, so |P|=2^(n-1) by complement pairing. Any intersecting family contains at most one member of each pair {A,[n] minus A}, since the pair is disjoint. Thus e(n,2)<=2^(n-1)=|P|, and the lower-bound construction attains it. For s>2, choosing one of each complementary pair does not prohibit an s-matching, so this cannot prove the full statement.

Second upper-bound route, upward closure and shifting. Replacing a set by an available superset preserves size and does not create a new s-matching: replacing the superset in a hypothetical new matching by its original subset yields the old matching. Iterating finitely gives an up-set. Standard ij-compression can likewise preserve size and the matching prohibition, but even granting it, a shifted up-set need not have a single weighted threshold of the P form. The missing assertion is a quantitative comparison bounding every shifted matching-free family by the exact binomial sum above, including all finite s and all m. This is not a consequence of the elementary lower-bound weight budget.

Actual source scope. In v3 Theorem 1.3, for every fixed m>=3 and epsilon>0 there is s0(m,epsilon), and the range ell<=((m+1)/(2m+1)-epsilon)s is solved for all s>=s0. Since (m+1)/(2m+1)-1/2=1/(4m+2)>0, choosing epsilon smaller than this difference covers ell<=ceil(s/2) for sufficiently large s, but cannot cover every finite s. No explicit universal small-s bridge has been proved. This is not an exclusion of the entire problem as already resolved.
