                                                  Abstract: Guided by the connections between hypergraphs and exterior algebras, we study
                                                  Turán and Ramsey type problems for alternating multilinear maps. This study lies at the
                                                  intersection of combinatorics, group theory, and algebraic geometry, and has origins in the
                                                  works of Lovász (Proc. Sixth British Combinatorial Conf., 1977), Buhler, Gupta, and Harris
                                                  (J. Algebra, 1987), and Feldman and Propp (Adv. Math., 1992).
                                                      Our main result is a Ramsey theorem for alternating bilinear maps. Given s,t ∈ N,
                                                  s,t ≥ 2, and an alternating bilinear map φ : V ×V → U with dim(V ) ≥ s · t 4 , we show that
                                                  there exists either a dimension-s subspace W ≤ V such that dim(span(φ  (W,W ))) = 0, or a

                                                  dimension-t subspace W ≤ V such that dim(span(φ (W,W ))) = 2 . This result has natural
                                                  group-theoretic (for finite p-groups) and geometric (for Grassmannians) implications, and
                                                  leads to new Ramsey-type questions for varieties of groups and Grassmannians.

                                           Key words and phrases: Ramsey and Turán problems, extremal combinatorics, alternating multilinear
                                           maps, exterior algebras, p-groups, Grassmannians

                                           1     Introduction
                                           The main result of this paper is a Ramsey theorem for alternating bilinear maps, or equivalently, for linear
                                           spaces of alternating bilinear forms.
                                                To state the result we need some definitions. Let F be a field. Let U be an n-dimensional vector space
                                           over F. We use W ≤ U to denote that W is a subspace of U. Recall that a bilinear form f : U ×U → F
                                           is alternating, if for any u ∈ U, f (u, u) = 0. For W ≤ U, the restriction of f to W is denoted as
                                            f |W : W ×W → F. Let Λ(U) be the linear space of alternating bilinear forms on U. For A ≤ Λ(U) and
                                           W ≤ U, A|W := { f |W | f ∈ A} ≤ Λ(W ).

Theorem 1.1. Let F be a field, s,t ∈ N, s,t ≥ 2, and n ≥ s · t 4 . Let U be an n-dimensional vector space
over F. Then for any A ≤ Λ(U), either there exists V ≤ U, dim(V ) = s, such that A|V is the zero space,
or there exists W ≤ U, dim(W ) = t, such that A|W = Λ(W ).

    We will prove Theorem 1.1 in Section 3, after presenting some preliminaries in Section 2. Theorem 1.1
has natural interpretations in group theory and geometry, and it can be naturally understood as a linear
Ramsey theorem in the context of Turán and Ramsey problems for alternating multilinear maps. These
will be explained in Section 4 and 5.
    A closely related result is Weaver’s “quantum” Ramsey theorem [Wea17]. That result concerns the
so-called operator systems from quantum information, which are actually linear spaces of matrices over
C satisfying certain conditions. Cliques and anticliques can be defined for such matrix spaces, which are
in close analogy with the totally-isotropic spaces and the complete spaces studied here. In [Wea17], it
was shown that when n ≥ 8s11 , any operator system has either an s-clique or an s-anticlique.
    The initial strategy for our proof (in particular Steps 1 and 2, see Section 3) follows closely some
ideas in Weaver’s proof in [Wea17] and Sims’ work on enumerating p-groups [Sim65, Sec. 2]. However,
new ideas are indeed required, as in the alternating matrix space setting, there are no diagonal matrices
which are crucial for Weaver’s proof in the operator system setting. Furthermore, our result works over
any field.

2.1   Some notation
                                                                                   [n]
For n ∈ N, [n] := {1, . . . , n}. The set of size-ℓ subsets of [n] is denoted as    ℓ .



2.2   Vector spaces
For a field F, Fn is the linear space consisting of length-n column vectors over F. We use ei to denote the
ith standard basis vector in Fn . The dual space of Fn is denoted as (Fn )∗ , and the dual vector of v ∈ Fn is
denoted as v∗ . For S ⊆ Fn , span(S) denotes the subspace spanned by vectors in S. For v ∈ Fn and i ∈ [n],
v(i) denotes the ith component of v. For S ⊆ Fn , S⊥ := {v ∈ Fn | ∀u ∈ S, vt u = 0}.


2.3   Matrices
We use M(ℓ × n, F) to denote the linear space of ℓ × n matrices over F, and set M(n, F) := M(n × n, F).
For A ∈ M(ℓ × n, F), A(i, j) is the (i, j)th entry of A. We shall use 0 to denote all-zero vectors or matrices
of appropriate sizes. A matrix A ∈ M(n, F) is alternating, if for any v ∈ Fn , vt Av = 0. For (i, j) ∈ [n] × [n],
Ei, j ∈ M(n, F) is the n × n matrix with the (i, j)th entry being 1, and the rest entries being 0. For

2.4    Alternating matrix spaces
Let Λ(n, F) be the linear space of n × n alternating matrices over F. Subspaces of Λ(n, F) are called
alternating matrix spaces.
    Two alternating matrix spaces A, B ≤ Λ(n, F) are isometric, if there exists T ∈ GL(n, F), such that
A = T t BT := {T t BT | B ∈ B}.
    Let A ≤ Λ(n, F). Suppose U ≤ Fn is of dimension d, and let T ∈ M(n × d, F) be a matrix whose
column vectors span U. The restriction of A to U via T is A|U,T := {T t AT | A ∈ A} ≤ Λ(d, F). Given
another T ′ ∈ M(n × d, F) whose columns also span U, A|U,T and A|U,T ′ are isometric. Therefore, we
may write A|U as the restriction of A to U via some T ∈ M(n × d, F) whose columns span U.
    We say that U is a totally-isotropic space1 for A, if dim(A|U ) = 0. That is, for any      ′

    Given v ∈ Fn , the degree of v in A, degA (v), is the dimension of {Av | A ∈ A} ≤ Fn . When A is clear
from the context, we may simply write deg(v) instead of degA (v). The minimum degree of A, denoted as

    Given S ⊆ Fn , the radical space of S in A is radA (S) := {u ∈ Fn | ∀A ∈ A, v ∈ S, ut Av = 0}. We
may simply write radA ({v}) as radA (v). When A is clear from the context, we may simply write rad(S)
instead of radA (S). We also define the radical space of A ≤ Λ(n, F) as rad(A) := radA (Fn ) = {v ∈ Fn |
∀A ∈ A, Av = 0}.


2.5    Alternating multilinear maps
An ℓ-linear map φ : (Fn )×ℓ → Fm is alternating, if for any (v1 , . . . , vℓ ) ∈ (Fn )×ℓ where vi = v j for some
i ̸= j, φ (v1 , . . . , vℓ ) = 0. Here, (Fn )×ℓ denotes the ℓ-fold Cartesian product of Fn . Two alternating ℓ-linear
maps φ , ψ : (Fn )×ℓ → Fm are isomorphic, if there exists (S, T ) ∈ GL(n, F) × GL(m, F), such that for any
v1 , . . . , vℓ ∈ Fn , φ (S(v1 ), . . . , S(vℓ )) = T (φ (v1 , . . . , vℓ )).


2.6    3-way arrays
Matrices are 2-way arrays, i.e. an array with two indices. We shall also need the notion of 3-way arrays,
namely arrays with three indices. We use M(ℓ × m × n, F) to denote the linear space of 3-way arrays with
the index set being [ℓ] × [m] × [n]. Let A ∈ M(ℓ × m × n, F) be a 3-way array. Following [KB09, GQ21],
we define the following. The frontal slices of A are A1 , . . . , An ∈ M(ℓ × m), where Ak (i, j) = A(i, j, k). The
tube fibres of A are vi, j ∈ Fn , i ∈ [ℓ], j ∈ [m], where vi, j (k) = A(i, j, k).
    3-way arrays are also referred to as 3-tensors in some literature. We adopt 3-way arrays, because
3-tensors are usually considered to be 3-way arrays together with the natural action of GL(ℓ, F) ×
GL(m, F) × GL(n, F). In this paper, as the reader will see soon, 3-way arrays are used to record the
structure constant of alternating bilinear maps. Therefore, the group action of relevance in this context is
by GL(n, F) × GL(m, F) where GL(n, F) acts covariantly on the first two indices.

the study of bilinear forms [MH73], and have been used in e.g. [BMW17].

2.7   Relations between 3-way arrays, bilinear maps, and matrix spaces
It is not hard to see that alternating bilinear maps, alternating matrix spaces, and 3-way arrays are closely
related. We spell out some details here.
     An alternating bilinear map φ : Fn × Fn → Fm can be represented as a tuple of alternating matrices
A = (A1 , . . . , Am ) ∈ Λ(n, F)m , such that for any u, v ∈ Fn , φ (u, v) = (ut A1 v, . . . , ut Am v)t .
     From an alternating matrix tuple A ∈ Λ(n, F)m , we can construct a 3-way array A ∈ M(n × n × m, F)
whose frontal slices are Ai ’s. We can also construct an alternating matrix space A = span{A1 , . . . , Am } ≤
Λ(n, F).
     Let A ≤ Λ(n, F) be an m-dimensional alternating matrix space. Let A = (A1 , . . . , Am ) ∈ Λ(n, F) be
an ordered linear basis of A. Then A gives rise to an alternating bilinear map φ and a 3-way array A as
above. Note that different ordered bases of A yield different but isomorphic alternating bilinear maps.

                                                                                        Abstract

                                           Using probabilistic methods, we establish universal cumulant lifting criteria for Stieltjes and
                                           Hamburger moment sequences. In the Stieltjes case, we characterize coefficientwise Hankel total
                                           positivity after convolution with every initial moment sequence by requiring each once-shifted
                                           cumulant sequence to be Stieltjes. We also prove equivalence with the numerical moment
                                           property at every nonnegative intensity. In the Hamburger case, we use twice-shifted cumulants
                                           to obtain coefficientwise-positive leading Hankel determinants, a degree bound, an explicit
                                           highest-degree component, and drift invariance. We work with formal series and require neither
                                           exponential moments nor moment determinacy. We establish composition, binomial-convolution,
                                           and substitution results, and apply them to implicit equations and autonomous differential
                                           equations, tree and forest families, and lognormal compound-Poisson moments in a unified
                                           approach. From known moment sequences, we obtain general coefficientwise Hankel-totally
                                           positive families containing the classical Bessel, Lah, and Laguerre polynomials. We not only
                                           give new proofs of coefficientwise Hankel-total positivity of many combinatorial polynomials, but
                                           also prove coefficientwise Hankel total positivity for the component polynomials of functional
                                           digraphs confirming a conjecture of Sokal and establish fixed-shape Schläfli-Gessel-Seo polynomials
                                           positivity supporting another conjecture of Sokal.

PAGE 3 1. Introduction
Total positivity of matrices has been deeply studied in various branches of mathematics.
For example, Brenti [4] developed total positivity to study unimodality and log-concavity
of sequences. Lusztig extended total positivity to reductive groups [24]. Fomin and Zelevin-
sky [11] studied totally nonnegative parts of double Bruhat cells and their parametrizations.
Rietsch related totally positive Toeplitz matrices to the quantum cohomology of partial flag
varieties [34]. Kodama and Williams [19] connected the totally nonnegative Grassmannian
with regular soliton solutions of the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation. Belton, Guillot,
Khare and Putinar [2] obtained a classification for entrywise polynomial preservers of posi-
tive semidefiniteness in fixed dimension. In their work on moment-sequence transforms [3],
they classified entrywise maps preserving moment sequences and totally nonnegative
Hankel matrices, and also treated multivariable transforms. We also refer the reader to
monographs [18, 32] for more details.
   Recent years, there many important progress on coefficientwise Hankel-total positivity.
For example, in [31], Pétréolle, Sokal and Zhu developed the theory for coefficientwise
Hankel-total positivity based on lattice paths and branched continued fractions. See Zhu
[48, 49] for methods from production matrices and Riordan arrays in combinatorics. There
are still many difficult open problems on coefficientwise total positivity. We also refer the
reader to [7, 31, 38, 30, 31, 44, 45, 46, 48, 49] for more details. In this present paper, we
will develop the probabilistic method to prove coefficientwise Hankel-total positivity. We
address the functional-digraph problem within this development by characterizing the
exponential cumulant families for which a universal coefficientwise lifting property holds.

1.5. Organization of the paper
In Section 2, we collect the common moment and Poisson tools and prove the Stieltjes
lifting criterion in Theorem 1.7. In Section 3, we prove the Hamburger lifting criterion by
using the real-line Vandermonde expansion. In Section 4, we establish binomial-convolution,
composition, and substitution results, discuss logarithms of ordinary Stieltjes series, and
collect moment inputs for the applications.
   In Section 5, we apply the lifting criteria to functional digraphs and their extensions.
In Section 6, we derive general results for implicit equations and autonomous differential
equations from Pólya-frequency series. In Section 7, we use known moment representations
for tree and forest sequences to verify the lifting hypotheses and obtain families that are
coefficientwise Hankel-totally positive in the lifting variables. We treat the Ward sequence
and several related tree families in this way.
   In Section 8, we obtain further general families from polynomial moment inputs,
including extensions of the Bessel, Lah, and Laguerre polynomials and a fixed-shape result
for the Schläfli-Gessel-Seo polynomials. We also consider set partitions, lognormal moments,
Poisson differences, and matrix consequences. For each application, we distinguish the
fixed parameters used to verify the moment condition from the variables in which we
prove coefficientwise positivity. We not only give new proofs of coefficientwise Hankel-
total positivity of many combinatorial polynomials in a unified approach, but also prove
coefficientwise Hankel-total positivity for the component polynomials of functional digraphs
confirming a conjecture of Sokal and establish fixed-shape Schläfli-Gessel-Seo polynomials
positivity supporting another coefficientwise Hankel-total positivity conjecture of Sokal.
In Section 9, we discuss the remaining questions about positivity jointly in the structural
parameters.

8. Polynomial moment inputs and further lifted families
We will obtain general coefficientwise Hankel-positive families from the shifted moment
conditions of concrete counting sequences. For the Schläfli-Gessel-Seo polynomials poly-

and Laguerre polynomials, we recover known Hankel positivity by a different proof and
allow arbitrary initial moments, independent colors, and independently chosen fixed shape
parameters. We specify these extensions below and attribute the earlier polynomial re-

moments; we use formal series even when exponential moments are infinite.

9. Some final remarks
For colored functional digraphs, we keep the cycle weights q1 , . . . , qm fixed and nonnegative.
In Theorem 1.5, we obtain joint coefficientwise positivity in the component intensities
y1 , . . . , ym . It remains to establish joint coefficientwise positivity in both q and y.
   We likewise keep the real equation coefficients fixed in Theorems 6.4 and 6.7. We
retain all independent component indeterminates in their conclusions. To retain the
equation parameters as well, we would need to extend the lifting argument beyond the
real moment-sequence hypotheses used here.
   For the Schläfli-Gessel-Seo family, we prove the shifted moment condition for every fixed
a, b ≥ 0 in Lemma 8.4. Applying lifting to those inputs, we obtain coefficientwise Hankel
total positivity in x, arbitrary initial moments, and independent colors in Theorem 8.5. We
leave the joint x, a, b assertion of Conjecture 8.1 unresolved. Nonnegative minors for every
numerical specialization of a, b do not imply nonnegative coefficients in those parameters.

   For the bivariate forest model in Corollary 7.7, we keep w ≥ −1 fixed. The correspond-
