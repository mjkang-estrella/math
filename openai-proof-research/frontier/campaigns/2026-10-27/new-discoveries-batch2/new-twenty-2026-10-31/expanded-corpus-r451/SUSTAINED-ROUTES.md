## 350679: root-descent forests, full conjunction (a),(b),(c)

The actual definition is the associated exponential Riordan array f[n,k]=n![t^n]B_w(t)^k/k!, B_w=(exp(wT)-1)/w, interpreted by its formal expansion at w=0; T=t exp(T). Row polynomials satisfy sum F_n(x,w)t^n/n!=exp(xB_w(t)). Sokal original v2 pp52–54 and published final PDF pp55–57 specify this. In particular the exponential, not ordinary, Riordan convention is essential. All minors of the coefficient matrix and all Hankel minors are required, jointly in the displayed indeterminates, not just principal blocks or numerical w.

Route 1: production matrices. Invert the formal equation s=B_w(t). Then T=log(1+ws)/w and t=T exp(-T). Differentiating gives B'_w(t)=exp((w+1)T)/(1-T), hence its A-series is (1+ws)^(1+1/w)/(1-log(1+ws)/w). This is an identity of formal series with a removable w=0 interpretation. Sokal (6.49) gives its cubic coefficient (16-2w^2)/6. The negative coefficient prevents using the sufficient coefficientwise-TP production-matrix criterion. It does NOT refute the output array or its Hankel matrix: inverse output multiplication need not preserve positivity. Applying a production theorem backwards would be an invalid inference.

Route 2: retain the parameter through cumulant lifting. Here the cumulants are kappa_n=(n+w)^(n-1), n>=1. For every finite minor of order r of the shifted row Hankel matrix, each entry F_{i+j+1} has smallest x-degree one. Therefore its coefficient of x^r is the determinant [kappa_{i+j+1}]. This explains why once-shifted cumulant positivity is necessary, but supplies no converse in a polynomial coefficient order. The cumulants' shifted sequence is (w+n+1)^n. Sokal Theorem6.13 already asserts coefficientwise positivity of the stronger shift (v+n)^n, with w=v-1; replacing v by w+1 has nonnegative coefficients and proves the weaker part(c). This part is public, not a new endpoint; the original/final announcement does not include its proof, and the companion's full proof is not audited here.

The concrete newer primary 2611.01172, Corollary7.7, gives F_n coefficientwise Hankel positivity in x for each fixed real w>=-1. Its Lemma7.6 proves B'_w=exp((w+1)T)/(1-T); Theorem7.5 writes B_w as an integrated tree kernel. I read these statements and proofs (pp55–56), the explicit scope limitation p77, and the central lifting sufficiency proof pp18–27 (with prerequisites only partly read). It uses a positive real jump measure depending on w. A square-integral kernel is nonnegative numerically; its coefficients in a structural parameter need not be nonnegative. Even (w-1)^2 illustrates that logical distinction. The proof's parameter-dependent measure and signed translation differences do not establish a coefficientwise-positive expression in w. Need an algebraic lift valid over the polynomial cone, or an explicit weight-preserving cancellation for every minor. This remains unpaid. Fixed-w positivity is a bounded nonmatch to joint positivity, not a reason to exclude the whole target as known or routine.

Route 3: planar-network/switching construction. Interpret each B_w component as a rooted tree with weight 1+w on root descents. Entry positivity follows from this interpretation, but determinant expansion is an alternating sum of tuples of forests. A Lindstrom-type cancellation would require an order-compatible encoding with sources and sinks and a first-intersection switch preserving the root-descent statistic. Switching tails may change which edge is incident to a root and hence its weight. No such network or involution has been constructed. The shifted matrix's published 2x2 minor -1+2v+2v^2 (final p56) is NOT a counterexample at unshifted w>=0: v=w+1 gives 3+6w+2w^2. Both the failed production route and the failed shifted shortcut therefore leave the actual claim open in this lane.

Significance contrary case: a new full joint minor formula or exact negative minor would resolve genuine remaining assertions; its being short or using determinants would not make it routine. Here no such formula/witness exists. Ordinary HOLD, novelty unestablished, no formal dispatch.

## 350677: Schlaefli–Gessel–Seo full three-part claim

Original pp41–44 and final pp45–46 give P_n(x;a,b)=x product_{i=1}^{n-1}(x+ia+(n-i)b), P_0=1 and P[n,k]=[x^k]P_n. The forest statistic has a for descents and b for ascents (or the alternative proper-vertex interpretation). The target is joint coefficientwise positivity in a,b and x, not fixed a,b.

Production route: the displayed output production matrix has a 2x2 minor with coefficient -2 on a^7 b, and symmetrically on ab^7 (original (6.13), final p46). This kills the naive sufficient production argument, not the conjecture. Entrywise positivity of the production matrix is also insufficient for positivity of all minors of the output Hankel matrix.

Inverse-series route: G is the compositional inverse of g(u)=(exp(-bu)-exp(-au))/(a-b), with the diagonal limit u exp(-au). Then sum P_n t^n/n!=exp(xG(t)); the shifted cumulants are product_{h=1}^n(ha+(n+1-h)b). Zhu Lemma8.4 and Theorem8.5 (pp63–65, read through their proofs) establish real shifted moment measures for each fixed a,b>=0 and coefficientwise positivity only in component intensities. Their proof uses symmetry a>=b, d=a-b, a geometric series with ratio b/a, completely monotone inverse derivatives, and Bernstein–Widder representation. These steps are legitimate numeric arguments including a=b and b=0 cases; subtraction a-b and divisions are not positivity-preserving substitutions in Z[a,b]. They furnish no polynomial-cone bridge. Zhu explicitly leaves joint positivity unresolved (p76).

Combinatorial route: the product formula gives entry coefficients, but a signed tuple-of-forests cancellation must preserve ascent/descent weights. A simple relabeling explains a/b symmetry, not determinant signs. A network with independent edge weights would be promising, but no compatible transfer graph for all n,k was obtained. Likewise, coefficientwise Hankel positivity for specializations a=b or a=0 cannot recover mixed coefficients. Need arbitrary-order sign cancellation, not a finite list of checks. All three parts remain unpaid here; the known fixed-shape proof is not a full applicable routine bridge. A concise full mixed-parameter proof would be significant, so no smallness exclusion is used.

## 347351: complete versus isotropic spaces, arbitrary ell

Qiao v4 is the journal-form primary (Discrete Analysis2023:12, DOI10.19086/da.84736); definitions in Sections2.5,5.8,5.9 are read. An alternating map factors through L: exterior^ell V -> U. W is complete when L restricted to exterior^ell W is injective; anisotropy only excludes nonzero decomposable wedges from the kernel. Complete implies anisotropic; the converse is false. Theorem1.1 proves the bilinear bound s*t^4 over any field; it does not settle general ell. Its proof outline and selected low-degree extraction/triangular-matrix lemmas were read, not every remaining normalization step or cited external proof. Section5.6 explicitly distinguishes the fixed-codomain Feldman–Propp Turan theorem from the required Ramsey theorem.

Route 1: coordinate hypergraph Ramsey. Color each basis ell-subset by whether its image is zero. A zero homogeneous coordinate subspace is isotropic by multilinearity. A nonzero homogeneous set does not give a complete subspace: distinct nonzero images can be linearly dependent. Already for ell=2, set all three pair images on a three-dimensional coordinate space equal to one nonzero scalar. The induced exterior-map rank is one, not three; moreover phi(e1,e2-e3)=0, so merely checking coordinate pairs does not even prove anisotropy. This is a symbolic example, no enumeration. Refining the color to image values requires unbounded/infinite colors as U and field vary. Thus ordinary finite-color Ramsey does not close the target.

Route 2: contraction induction to bilinear forms. Fix ell-2 independent vectors and contract phi to a bilinear map on a complementary space. The known bilinear result gives independence for wedges containing that fixed tuple. It does not give independence for every ell-wedge of the enlarged subspace. Nor does an isotropic contracted subspace imply isotropy for tuples missing one of the fixed vectors. Iterating contractions needs a common subspace and a simultaneous rank invariant; separate large subspaces may intersect in dimension zero. No dimension recurrence ensuring the joint rank was found. Thus the proposed induction fails at a precisely identified merge lemma, not simply at lack of effort.

Route 3: bound output rank then apply the fixed-codomain Turan bound. One may replace U by im L, but dim(im L) can grow as binomial(n,ell). Absence of a complete t-space does not imply dim(im L)<binomial(t,ell): rank may be spread across overlapping coordinate subsets without a single injective restriction. The required local-to-global rank theorem is unpaid. Applying a fixed-m bound with m growing in n supplies no explicit n-only threshold. The strong bilinear rank-normalization proof explains what a higher-exterior analogue would have to do; it is not supplied by the hypergraph analogy.

The full target is PARKED. No counterexample to its conclusion, no new upper bound in all ell, no exclusion as routine. The importance of a uniform explicit bound is not diminished by these elementary failed reductions. Full formalization would need an actual all-field exterior-power rank argument first; no dormant formal plan is warranted without it.
