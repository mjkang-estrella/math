## 355357 Continuity conjecture for the Rényi entropy rate at order one
Let $Z=\{Z_n\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ be a stationary ergodic process over a finite alphabet $\mathcal{Z}$. Its Shannon entropy rate is

$$
H(Z)\triangleq \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{H(Z_1^n)}{n},
$$

and, when the limit exists, its $\alpha$-th order Rényi entropy rate is

$$
H_{\alpha}(Z)\triangleq \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{H_{\alpha}(Z_1^n)}{n}.
$$

**Continuity conjecture.** For every stationary ergodic process $Z$,

$$
\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H_{\alpha}(Z)=H(Z).
$$

The conjecture is a natural rate-level analogue of the finite-block identity $\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H_{\alpha}(Z_1^n)=H(Z_1^n)$, but the existence of the Rényi entropy rate itself is not known for general stationary ergodic processes. The source reports this conjecture as neither proved nor disproved in the literature.

## 355972 Conjecture on convex functions and Busemann functions
Let $M$ be a symmetric space of noncompact type. A function $f:M\to\mathbb{R}$ is **convex** if it is convex in the sense used for functions on $M$, and a **Busemann function** is a function $\beta:M\to\mathbb{R}$ associated with a geodesic ray in $M$. **Convex-function/Busemann-function conjecture.** For every convex function $f$ on $M$, there is a Busemann function $\beta:M\to\mathbb{R}$ and constants $a>0$ and $C\in\mathbb{R}$ such that, for every $x\in M$,

$$
f(x)>a\beta(x)+C.
$$

The conjecture seeks a universal Busemann lower bound for convex functions on symmetric spaces; the source presents it as further research after proving such a bound for functions arising from representation-theoretic data. The supplied status evidence says that an analogous conjecture for trees is false, but does not disprove this stated conjecture.

Status: **refuted** according to the source (as of September 2022). “The anlagous conjecture for trees is false: Let $G$ be a leafless $d$-regular tree, $x_0\in G$ a vertex and $(x_i)_{i=0}^\infty$ an infinite ray.”

## 355985 2-torsion and 4-torsion conjecture for double coverings of arrangement complements
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a complex hyperplane arrangement, let $M(\mathcal{A})$ be its complement, and let $\omega\in H^1(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z}_2)$. Denote by $M(\mathcal{A})^\omega$ the associated double covering and by $\mathcal{L}_\omega$ the rank-one local system determined by $\omega$. **2-torsion and 4-torsion conjecture.** The integral homology $H_1(M(\mathcal{A})^\omega,\mathbb{Z})$ has nontrivial $2$-torsion if and only if the local-system homology $H_1(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathcal{L}_\omega)$ has a $4$-torsion summand $\mathbb{Z}_4$. Examples in the source exhibit this relationship for specific arrangements, but the general equivalence is presented as a conjecture and its resolution is not given.

## 356037 The extremal orbifold Betti-number and Euler-characteristic conjecture
Let $n$ be a positive integer, and consider projective $n$-dimensional varieties with quotient singularities and trivial canonical class. Write $H$ for the sum of their orbifold Betti numbers. For odd $n$, write $\chi_{\mathrm{orb}}$ for the orbifold Euler characteristic. **Extremal orbifold invariants conjecture.** In dimension $n$, the largest possible value of $H$ is

$$
H=2(s_0-1)\cdots(s_n-1).
$$

For odd $n$, the smallest possible orbifold Euler characteristic is

$$
-(s_0-1)\cdots(s_{n-1}-1)(2s_n-6),
$$

and the largest is

$$
(s_0-1)\cdots(s_{n-1}-1)(2s_n-6).
$$

The conjecture is motivated by three explicit examples whose common Betti-number sum is the largest known for Calabi-Yau threefolds. The source gives no resolution status.

## 356241 Morse-Novikov genus conjecture for oriented links
For an oriented link $L$ in $S^3$, let $g_{{\rm{MN}}}(L)$ denote the minimum genus among possibly disconnected Seifert surfaces for $L$ that realize the Morse-Novikov number, and let $g(L)$ be the genus of $L$.

**Morse-Novikov genus conjecture.** One expects

$$
g_{{\rm{MN}}}(L)=g(L).
$$

This asserts that the Morse-Novikov genus is realized by a minimal-genus Seifert surface. The statement is not known in general, although the paper notes that for knots, and also for oriented links allowing disconnected Seifert surfaces, the Morse-Novikov genus is realized by an incompressible Seifert surface.

## 356267 Endoscopic character identity for covers of reductive groups
Let $G$ be a reductive group with cover $G(F)_{x_G}$, let $(\dot s,\mathcal H)$ be a refined endoscopic datum for $G$, and let $H(F)_{x_H}$ be the corresponding endoscopic cover. Let $\varphi^G$ and $\varphi_H$ be tempered $L$-parameters related by the $L$-embedding $^L\xi_{H,G}$, and let $\Theta^{s,\mathsf{w}}_{\varphi_G}$ and $S\Theta_{\varphi_H}$ be the associated weighted and stable character distributions. If $f \in \mathcal C_c^\infty(G(F)_{x_G})$ and $f^H \in \mathcal C_c^\infty(H(F)_{x_H})$ are matching anti-genuine functions, then

$$
\Theta^{s,\mathsf{w}}_{\varphi_G}(f)=S\Theta_{\varphi_H}(f^H).
$$

Equivalently, for strongly regular semi-simple elements and the transfer factor $\Delta'_x[\mathsf{w}]$,

$$
\Theta^{s,\mathsf{w}}_{\varphi_G}(\delta_G)=\sum_\gamma \Delta'_x[\mathsf{w}](\gamma_H,\delta_G)S\Theta_{\varphi_H}(\gamma_H),
$$

where the sum runs over strongly regular semi-simple elements $\gamma$ of $H(F)$ up to stable conjugacy, and $\gamma_H\in H(F)_{x_H}$ is an arbitrary lift of $\gamma$. This is the expected character identity for refined endoscopic transfer in the setting of covers of reductive groups; the supplied text gives no evidence that it has been proved or disproved.

## 356287 Uniqueness conjecture for Markov fractions
Let $q$ be a Markov number, and call a rational number a Markov fraction when it is one of the Markov fractions defined in the paper; its denominator is the denominator in lowest terms. The interval under consideration is $[0,\frac{1}{2}]$. **Uniqueness conjecture for Markov fractions.** For each Markov number $q$, the interval $[0,\frac{1}{2}]$ contains at most one Markov fraction with denominator $q$. This is equivalent to the uniqueness conjecture for Markov numbers, which asserts that every Markov number is the maximum of exactly one ordered Markov triple; the proposition is stated as yet to be proved or refuted.
