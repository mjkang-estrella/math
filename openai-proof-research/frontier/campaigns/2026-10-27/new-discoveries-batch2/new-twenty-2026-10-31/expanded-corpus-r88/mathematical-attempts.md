## 1. 345244: Endo–Mimura–Sawada hypergroup conjecture (strongest finite route)

Source: arXiv 2001.07925, Definition 2.1 for (S1) and (S2), equation (2.1) for the constants p^k_{ij}, Definition 2.9, the remark "becomes a hypergroup iff associativity and commutativity", and Conjecture 4.7. The graphs are simple, connected, locally finite and countable, and satisfy (iii).

### 1.1 Exact reformulation (new to this lane; ordinary proof, not Lean-checked)

Let (Γ, v0) be a finite pointed graph satisfying (S1) and (S2). Write s_i = |S_i(v0)| (by (S1) this also equals |S_i(x)| for every x). Let P be the n × (D+1) characteristic matrix of the distance partition {S_0(v0), …, S_D(v0)}, and A_i the distance-i matrices.

* (S2) says exactly that A_i P = P B_i, where (B_i)_{kj} = |S_i(x) ∩ S_j(v0)| for x ∈ S_k(v0). So W = col(P) is invariant under every A_i.
* By (2.1) and (S1), p^k_{ij} = (B_j)_{ik} / s_j. Also B_i e_0 = e_i and e_0^T B_i = s_i e_i^T. With S1, B_i 1 = s_i 1. Since S B_i is symmetric, s^T B_i = s_i s^T.
* **Commutativity** p^k_{ij} = p^k_{ji} is equivalent to B_i e_j = B_j e_i for all i, j. Equivalently, the v0-column of [A_i, A_j] vanishes, and equivalently B_i B_j e_0 = B_j B_i e_0.
* **Lemma A.** Under (S1) and (S2), (Γ, v0) produces a hypergroup if and only if the matrices B_0, …, B_D commute pairwise.
  * Set T_j = B_j / s_j. Then x_i ∘ x_j = Σ_k p^k_{ij} x_k = e_i^T T_j (row-vector notation), and e_0^T T_l = e_l^T.
  * Associativity of ∘ is equivalent to T_i T_j = Σ_l (T_j)_{il} T_l for all i, j.
  * (⇒) Commutativity gives (T_j)_{il} = p^l_{ij} = p^l_{ji} = (T_i)_{jl}. So T_i T_j = Σ_l p^l_{ij} T_l = T_j T_i.
  * (⇐) Commutativity of the constants: B_i e_j = B_i B_j e_0 = B_j B_i e_0 = B_j e_i.
  * (⇐) Associativity: M = T_i T_j − Σ_l (T_j)_{il} T_l lies in the commutative algebra generated by the T's, and e_0^T M = e_i^T T_j − e_i^T T_j = 0. For every Q in the algebra, e_0^T Q M = e_0^T M Q = 0. The row vectors e_0^T Q span everything, since e_0^T T_l = e_l^T, so M = 0.
* **Corollary B (diameter at most 2).** If the eccentricity of v0 is at most 2, Conjecture 4.7 holds for (Γ, v0). Here B_2 = 1 s^T − I − B_1, and B_1 1 s^T = r 1 s^T = 1 s^T B_1 by (S1) and the symmetric edge count. So B_1 and B_2 commute.
* For eccentricity 3 the hypergroup property is equivalent to [B_1, B_2] = 0, because B_3 = 1 s^T − I − B_1 − B_2. The edge-level identities (the S1 sphere counts at the neighbours of v0, plus the double-counting identities) leave a one-parameter family. They do not force α_2 = a_2, where for x ∈ S_2(v0), α_2 is the number of neighbours of v0 at distance 2 from x, and a_2 is the number of neighbours of x inside S_2(v0). So a counterexample would need eccentricity at least 3, (S1), non-vertex-transitivity, and non-constant λ or μ.
* **Remark.** If Γ is vertex-transitive and satisfies (S2) at one vertex, it is distance-regular, by the same argument as the source's Lemma 2.4. So every counterexample is non-vertex-transitive.

Remaining lemma for a full proof: show [B_1, B_2] = 0 (and in general that all B_i commute) from (S1) and (S2) for eccentricity at least 3. Failing that, find a non-vertex-transitive, distance-degree-regular graph with eccentricity at least 3 at a vertex satisfying (S2), where α_2 ≠ a_2 or the B_i fail to commute.

## 2. 353625: García-Marco–Knauer retract-image conjecture for non-bipartite G(n,k)

Source: arXiv 2202.06785 (J. Algebraic Combin. 59 (2024)), Section 2 definitions, Theorem 2.1, and Conjecture 4.1. Cores satisfy the conjecture trivially, since every endomorphism is an automorphism.

* **Lemma C.** Let G(n,k) be non-bipartite and not a core, with odd girth g.
  * By Theorem 2.1(b) no shortest odd cycle uses a spoke. A spoke-free cycle is a whole rim, so the shortest odd cycles are exactly the rim cycles of length g.
  * Any endomorphism maps a shortest odd cycle bijectively onto a shortest odd cycle: an odd closed walk of length g with a repeated vertex would contain a shorter odd cycle. So it maps each inner rim isomorphically onto a rim g-cycle.
  * For prisms G(n,1) with n odd, both rims are mapped onto rims. If they go to different rims the map is a bijection, hence an automorphism. If they go to the same rim R, every spoke maps into R and the image is R, which is a retract (the projection).
  * So the conjecture holds for every prism G(n,1). This is an elementary argument recorded for completeness, not a new result claim.
* **Exact enumeration** (`scripts/gp_images_exact.py`, using Lemma C) of all endomorphism images of all non-core non-bipartite G(n,k), n ≤ 40, k ≠ 1. The non-core list was computed from Theorem 2.1(b) in `outputs/gp-noncore-list-40.json`.
  * Complete for every case except the G(3m,m) family (time-capped at 40 s) and (33,3), (35,5), (35,7), (35,14), (39,3), (40,8), (40,16), which were killed at the CPU cap.
  * Every image found is a retract (`outputs/gp-exact-summary.txt`).
  * Images are richer than single cycles. For example G(21,3) has 385 image classes, G(27,3) has 1537, and G(18,6) and G(24,8) have images of sizes 12 and 18.
* **Independent subset method** (`scripts/gp_retract_search.py`), which enumerates every connected vertex set containing a shortest odd cycle, gives no non-retract image for G(5,1), G(6,2), G(7,1), G(9,1), G(9,3), G(10,4), G(11,1). It reproduces the bipartite counterexample G(8,3) of the source.
* **Randomised endomorphism sampling** up to n = 24 was also negative.

Remaining lemma for a full proof: a classification of endomorphism images for the families of Theorem 2.1(c) that are not cores (inner rims of odd length g = n/gcd(n,k)), with an explicit retraction for each. No uniform argument was found.

## 3. 353943: Crenshaw–Oxley graphicness conjecture for 3-connected orderable binary matroids

Source: arXiv 2203.08305, Section 1 (consistent ordering), Proposition 8(i) (deletion-closed), Proposition 9 (orderable binary with a spanning circuit is graphic), and Conjecture 4.

* Orderability is decided exactly as a CSP: find a global "adjacent pairs" relation A whose restriction to every circuit is a Hamiltonian cycle.
  * Two solvers: `orderable` and `orderable_fast` with propagation. They agree on 6 446 random binary matroids.
  * Both agree with the source on F7, F7*, R10 (non-orderable) and on M(K4), M(K5), M(K3,3) and M(Petersen) (orderable).
* Every simple binary matroid of rank r contains a basis, which may be taken to be the standard one. Orderable matroids are deletion-closed (Prop. 8(i)), and a spanning circuit persists in full-rank supersets, so supersets with a spanning circuit are graphic if orderable (Prop. 9). Hence a level-by-level closure under coordinate permutations is exhaustive.
* **Result:** for rank ≤ 6 (any number of elements) every 3-connected orderable binary matroid is graphic.
  * Rank ≤ 5 gives no candidate at all.
  * Rank 6 gives 104 coordinate-permutation classes of 3-connected orderable matroids without a spanning circuit. All are graphic, for example M(K_{3,4}), by the non-separating-cocircuit reconstruction test in `scripts/graphic_test.py`, self-tested in `outputs/graphic-selftest.txt`.
  * Logs: `logs/oml-r4.log`, `logs/oml-r5.log`, `logs/oml-r6-*.log`, `logs/graphic-r6.log`.
* Rank 7 was not attempted, because the coordinate-permutation group has 5040 elements and the canonicalisation is too slow under the caps.

## 5. 342072: Chu's two-AP non-sum-dominance conjecture

* Equal common differences: proved by Chu (arXiv 1906.03793; Integers 2020, A87).
* Different common differences: Kumar–Mohan–Pandey (Integers 26 (2026) #A58, full PDF read for Theorems 1 and 2) prove the case max X1 < min X2. They prove the overlapping case except two explicit residual cases:
  * (i) gcd(q1, q2) > 1 and 0 < d2 < (m1 − 1) d1;
  * (ii) q1 = 2, gcd(2, q2) = 1 and 0 < d2 < (m1 − 1) d1.
* Searches:
  * General unions: 1 955 629 distinct sets (differences ≤ 10, lengths ≤ 20, offsets |b| ≤ 70).
  * Targeted residual cases: 5 118 901 distinct sets (d1 ≤ 12, d2 < 4 d1, lengths ≤ 14).
  * No sum-dominant set.
* A full Lean endpoint would need formalising Chu's and Kumar–Mohan–Pandey's case analyses as well as the residual cases, which is infeasible in this pass. Parked.
