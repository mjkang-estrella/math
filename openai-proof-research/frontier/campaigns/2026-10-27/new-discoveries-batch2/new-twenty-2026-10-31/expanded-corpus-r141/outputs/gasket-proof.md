# Weighted modular colouring route for 363306 — zero credit, routine-adaptation objection

Source: Attarzadeh–Abbasi–Behtoei, arXiv:2405.19172, Theorem 13 and its proof, and the preceding literal contraction construction. The source's Theorem 13 colours a two-letter word xy by f(x)+f(y) modulo k, and already proves the lower bound by retaining a base copy.

For t >= 2 put w_h = 2^(t-h-2) for zero-based h < t-1 and w_(t-1)=1. Thus w_h is exactly the sum of all later weights. For a proper k-colouring f of G, colour a word x_0...x_(t-1) by sum_h w_h f(x_h) modulo k. Any linking edge has endpoints p i j...j and p j i...i, so the two colours agree because the coefficient of f(i) at the first differing coordinate equals the total coefficient of the remaining suffix. Hence the colour is constant on every equivalence class generated by all the contracted linking edges. Every surviving edge comes from a base-copy edge, where only the final coordinate differs. Its coefficient is one, so the two colours remain different. This descends to a proper k-colouring of the contracted graph at every level t. The source's retained base copy gives the opposite chromatic inequality. t=1 is tautological; the empty base graph can be treated separately.
