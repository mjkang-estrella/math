## Exact target and source identification

For integers l>=0, n>k>=0 and l/(l+1)<k/n<(l+1)/(l+2), put r=l+1 and s=n-k. The inequalities become r*s<n<(r+1)*s. Hence s>=2, n>=2r+1, 1<=k<=n-2. The primary's cyclic polytope convention n>2r therefore applies without adding hypotheses or inventing negative-parameter refutations.

On V=Z/n, K=N(n,k) is the downward closure of F_i=[i,i+k]_n (Definition 3.2 in arXiv v1; Definition 3.1 in the journal). Define B as the downward closure of the unions of r pairwise vertex-disjoint cycle edges {a,a+1}. Gale evenness identifies this EXACT labeled B with boundary C_(2r)(n): a 2r-subset is a facet iff every two omitted ordered vertices have an even number of selected vertices between them (v1 Theorem 5.2; journal Theorem 7.1). The wraparound edge {n-1,0} is included. Face membership in B means containment in such a facet, not merely an evenness test on a face of arbitrary size.

For nonempty sigma, let its positive cyclic successive distances be d_1,...,d_t summing to n. Then sigma belongs to K iff max d_j>=s, equivalently its complement contains s-1 consecutive vertices. A singleton has the one distance n. The empty face belongs to both K and B and is never matched. This gap criterion follows directly by complementing an interval F_i, in both directions. Boundary inclusion follows from r disjoint pairs: some intervening empty gap has at least (n-2r)/r > s-2 vertices, hence at least s-1. This is the actual source's Lemma 5.8 / journal Lemma 7.4.

The requested endpoint is a finite chain of elementary simplicial collapses from THIS K to THIS B, all over V with its existing labels. A codimension-one pair (sigma,tau) is removable only when tau is maximal and is the unique proper coface of sigma at that stage. All of B must survive. For a finite acyclic face matching whose unmatched faces are exactly B, the standard matching-to-collapse theorem supplies such a chain; a list of sphere-like critical dimensions does not. Abstract isomorphisms of spheres, subdivisions, homology generators and simple homotopy equivalences allowing expansions do not establish this endpoint.

At n=2r+1 the inequalities force s=2 and k=n-2; K and B are the boundary of a simplex, so the identity chain suffices. There is no n<=2r convention gap. These observations receive no separate credit.

## Ordinary route A — short-arc fibers, complete only for l=0

Assume 1<=k<n/2. Every nonempty face with at least two vertices has a unique shortest clockwise containing arc [a,b]_n of length d<=k: its complementary successive gap has length n-d>n/2, so is the unique gap longer than n/2. Its endpoints are selected. The boundary B is exactly all vertices and adjacent cycle edges (and the empty face).

For each d>=2 and each anchor a, put b=a+d mod n. Faces of this fiber are exactly {a,b} union T for T contained in the d-1 internal vertices of [a,b]. Pair each face missing v=a+1 with that face union {v}. This is a total involution on the fiber, all pairs have codimension one, both sides belong to K, and neither side belongs to B. Toggling an internal vertex does not change endpoints or span, so the fiber and pivot are unchanged.

Here is an actual free-face order, not just a critical-cell count: process d from k down to 2; within an endpoint fiber process lower faces in decreasing cardinality, breaking ties arbitrarily; fibers of equal d can be processed in any order. A proper coface of a lower face has span at least d. Greater-span cofaces have already been removed. A same-span coface has the same endpoints (inclusion between equal-length short containing arcs forces equality). If it is not the paired upper face, it either is a larger lower face or is the upper mate of a larger lower face, already removed by cardinality order. Therefore the only surviving proper coface is its mate, which is maximal. Each removal is elementary; all nonboundary faces are removed and B remains literally fixed. There are finitely many pairs, so the order terminates. Equivalently span cannot increase on alternating downward steps and, at fixed span, a single fixed toggle cannot make a cycle.

This is a complete ordinary construction in the entire l=0 range, including the zero-step k=1 case. It is consistent with the source's elementary circle discussion and with the published restricted graph-power theorem. No new-to-literature or significant full-problem solution is claimed, and a parameter subrange receives zero credit. No formal work is requested for it.

Attempted extension: choose the largest complementary gap and break ties by starting label, then toggle a vertex in its complementary span. Insertion away from the gap's endpoints preserves the chosen gap, but deletion merges the two distances adjacent to the pivot. Their sum can exceed the chosen largest distance, or tie it with an earlier anchor. The new face may therefore change fiber, and the inverse rule can select another pivot. The l=0 proof uses n-d>d to prevent this; the higher-l inequalities do not. Furthermore deleting a pivot can produce a B face; adding cannot turn a non-B face into B because B is downward closed. Thus a boundary-aware involution needs a deletion-stability lemma as well as an anchor-stability lemma. Neither is proved. This is a specific failure of the attempted rule, not a counterexample to the conjecture.

## Ordinary route B — one-step parameter layers and saturated gaps

Keep n and l fixed and suppose BOTH k and k-1 lie strictly in the same admissible interval. Then B is contained in K'=N(n,k-1). The relative faces K\K' have exactly max successive distance s=n-k. Thus boundary fixing is automatic for any matching entirely in this layer. This improves on merely testing whether a local face lies in B.

Try to anchor at the earliest saturated distance s and toggle an internal vertex away from that gap. Inserting a vertex splits another distance and preserves the maximum because the anchored s remains. To invert the operation when the pivot is present, removal must merge its adjacent distances u,v with u+v<=s. If equality holds, the merged saturated gap must not precede the anchor. If u+v>s, the lower face lies in K', so pairing it would destroy the intended relative layer. A pivot function must also select the same vertex after toggling. These are exact local necessary conditions, derived directly from the membership test. The inequalities r*s<n<(r+1)*s alone do not supply them uniformly. The graph-power proof's unique-maximal-coface condition is absent here: multiple saturated gaps can give different containing intervals.

Even a proof of every such layer collapse would stop at k0=floor(n*l/(l+1))+1. The additional required statement would be N(n,k0) collapsing to B, not just to N(n,k0-1), whose homotopy regime changes or hits an even-sphere threshold. Consequently layer induction alone does not solve the base for higher l. Next substantive lemma would have to give an explicit anchor-stable pivot satisfying the merged-gap inequality on every layer, plus a separate relative matching at k0. No full lemma or proof is obtained. No all-face enumeration or finite parameter sweep was used.

## Ordinary route C — relative double-suspension induction

The source recurrence uses two cones, their intersection complex, a contractible subcomplex T, and a second suspension. In the v1 proof, T has facets tau', tau_(i,j); in induced T_h, vertex h is dominated by h+k. Iterated deletion proves T contractible; the remaining intersection has exactly the interval facets of N(k,2k-n). These identifications are genuine and were read, rather than assumed from a homotopy-type table.

But this procedure contains two explicit nonrelative operations: replace a union of contractible subcomplexes by a suspension of its intersection, and delete vertices from T. Neither gives a collapse of the original pair fixing B. In particular all B vertices must survive, and the proposed vertex deletions do not. The parameter map reduces the sphere dimension, but the natural labeled cyclic boundary on n vertices is not identified face-by-face with the double suspension of the smaller boundary. A homeomorphic sphere after retriangulation is insufficient.

The useful replacement would be an intermediate subcomplex L with B subset L subset K and explicit free pairs outside B taking K to L, followed by a relative reduction of L whose inductive boundary is identified with THIS B. That requires specifying the added/coned pieces and their boundary attachment maps, proving every intermediate free-coface condition, and giving the label-preserving boundary identification; none follows from contractibility of T or the two homotopy equivalences. No such L and attachment lemma were constructed. This route is parked for that exact gap, not rejected merely for a large anticipated certificate.

A lexicographic greedy vertex matching on K\B would at best give a standard acyclic partial matching; it does not prove that no non-B critical faces remain. Running it on all faces would be the forbidden generic enumeration approach and is not a substitute for any of the missing uniform lemmas above.
