## 380331: full classical reduction, excluded

Read Genest §§1.6–1.7, including Definitions 1.6.1–1.6.4, 1.7.1, Corollary 1.7.6 and Proposition 1.7.11/Theorem 1.7.12. This inverse is NOT the real inverse of the loopless adjacency matrix. Natural colours encode degree parity: white vertices have even degree. The binary matrix is M=A+diag(1+degree mod 2). Here all degrees are (n−1)/2, even, so M=A+I. The source's exceptional n=1 therefore has M=[1], not [0].

Vertex and edge transitivity and self-complementarity imply transitivity on ordered or unordered nonedges as needed for common-neighbour counts. Thus G is strongly regular. Put k=(n−1)/2. Self-complementarity gives μ=λ+1. Counting edges between the neighbours and nonneighbours of a fixed vertex gives k(k−1−λ)=kμ. For n>1, k>0, hence μ=(n−1)/4 and λ=(n−5)/4. Work now over F2 and let J be the all-ones matrix.

If n=5 mod 8, k is even, λ even and μ odd, so A²=A+I+J and AJ=0. Consequently (A+I)(A+J)=I. The off-diagonal part of A+J is the complement graph, and its diagonal is all ones. If n=1 mod 8 and n>1, A²=A, so (A+I)A=0 with A nonzero. Thus M is singular. At n=1 the inverse is [1] and the underlying graph is the empty singleton, its own complement.

The semantic bridge is ordinary symmetric Gaussian elimination with principal pivots. A diagonal 1 permits a one-vertex pivot; otherwise a nonzero off-diagonal entry permits the invertible block [[0,1],[1,0]]. Every nonsingular nonempty symmetric residual has one of these blocks. Schur complementation preserves nonsingularity, so induction exhausts the vertex set. These are precisely a white local complementation and a black-edge pivot in Genest's parity-word conventions. For a one-vertex block the external update is C→C+bbᵀ: off-diagonal edges between neighbours toggle, as do their colours; the pivot remains white. For a black-edge block the update is C→C+bcᵀ+cbᵀ, with no external diagonal change. This is the source's edge-complementation word [uv]. Pivots on disjoint blocks compose to pivot on their union, as follows directly by eliminating variables in Mx=y. A reduced full parity word therefore exists iff M is nonsingular, and its final matrix is M⁻¹. Conversely each allowed block is invertible, so a full such elimination implies nonsingularity by the determinant Schur identity. This completes the ordinary reduction in both directions and identifies the complement, rather than merely checking orders or spectra.

No classification of Paley/Peisert graphs is needed. This is a short standard binary-matrix bridge plus the source's strong-regularity mechanism, not a new significant family. Its full proof body has NOT been read and is NOT claimed as qualification. ## 318975: sustain the descent-preserving route, then exclude published proof

The source defines T_n^0 by replacing one entry of an n-cycle's one-line notation with 0; comparisons across this entry count normally. Deleting the marked entry would change the statement. The elementary map θ from words to cycles preserves neither the desired full descent set nor the comparison at the mark, so merely invoking the fundamental transformation is insufficient.

A stronger route inserts n+1 at the broken cyclic edge. For U_n (replace an entry with n+1), write the cycle as (t1,…,t_{n−1},k) when the replaced entry is at position k and form (t1,…,t_{n−1},k,n+1). This is a bijection U_n→C_{n+1}; its first n comparisons equal those in the broken word. One still needs a bijection C_{n+1}→S_n preserving comparisons on [n−1]. Elizalde's later *Descent sets of cyclic permutations*, Theorem 2.1 and §4, supplies exactly this nontrivial bijection. Corollary 4.2 proves the U_n version. Corollary 4.3 conjugates by reverse-complement to obtain the exact T_n^0 bijection, preserving every descent set (and sends the zero position to the position of 1). The proof body of these corollaries was read, not just the abstract. Thus the full assigned statement is already proved, not a fresh proof opportunity. ## 379622: exact coefficient mechanism survives only as a gap

Read Wagner Example 5.2 and Conjectures 5.3–5.4, with the partition-function/Rayleigh conventions. I K_n means independent sets of the graphic matroid, hence forests on EDGES, not stable vertex sets of K_n. Let F=Z(I K_n), e=uv and f=uw. Write F=F00+y_e F10+y_f F01+y_e y_f F11, with the four coefficients summing forests with those presences. The Rayleigh difference is exactly F10 F01−F00 F11, independent of y_e,y_f.

Fix one monomial in the other variables. No exponent exceeds 2. Let C be its squared edges and R its edges of exponent 1. In either product the two forests have precisely C in common. If C is cyclic, both contributions vanish. Otherwise contract C and keep edges of R individually labelled (parallel edges and loops after contraction must be retained). The positive coefficient counts partitions R=P⊔Q such that C∪P∪{e} and C∪Q∪{f} are forests. The negative coefficient counts partitions with C∪P∪{e,f} and C∪Q forests. This is an exact, finite combinatorial statement for each labelled multigraph contraction, not a generic solver encoding. Adjacent e and f remain adjacent after contraction, possibly becoming parallel or loops. A loop forced into either side kills the relevant terms; parallel e,f prevent them from sharing a forest, killing the negative term.

Attempt 1: move f from the first forest to the second. If Q+f is acyclic, this succeeds and preserves the monomial. If not, Q has a unique u–w path. Exchanging an edge on that path repairs Q+f, but the moved edge can close a cycle in P+e and the selected edge is not recoverable from the output. This is an actual missing injection, not an implemented algorithm.

Attempt 2: contract C, then use an alternating exchange trail beginning at the f-circuit. The first exchange can be chosen by an edge ordering, but subsequent exchanges can revisit the e-component. There is no proved termination rule preserving both forests or inverse encoding of the first defect. Ordinary matroid basis exchange does not fix this: forests here need not be bases, and the degree-by-degree coefficient statement is stronger than unweighted negative correlation. Source Conjecture 5.4 would imply the target but is itself conjectural and cannot be used as a lemma. The spanning-tree Rayleigh theorem counts bases, with different cancellation; adjoining a root counts rooted forests with component-size weights and does not remove those weights. These second-stage failures rule out a claimed proof.

Precise remaining lemma: for every C,R and incident e,f in the contracted labelled graph, inject the negative pairs above into the positive pairs, preserving the edge multiset and allowing loops/parallel edges.
