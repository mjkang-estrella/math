Problem
The question
Isobel Davies and Orlando Marigliano,
Coloured Graphical Models and their Symmetries
, arXiv:2012.01905v5, Section 4, Conjecture 4.4, PDF page 11:
If $G$ is an uncoloured circulant graph, then the number of orbits of the action of $\operatorname{Aut}(G)$ on the set $V*V$ of unordered pairs of vertices of $G$ coincides with the number of distinct eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix.
The preceding definition says: “By definition, these are graphs that have a symmetry which is a cyclic permutation of its vertices.” Corollary 3.3, page 7, uses the entrywise action of the full automorphism group; its count $\binom{n+1}{2}$ includes diagonal pairs. The source's adjacency matrix is the real symmetric zero-one matrix of the simple undirected graph. Preregistration:

Motivation
Our foothold
The settling declaration is
D5/S3/Combinatorics/Hamming/DaviesMariglianoCirculantOrbits.result
. The finite graph joins residues modulo $2$, $3$ and $5$ that disagree in exactly one coordinate. It is the thirty-vertex Cartesian product $K_2\square K_3\square K_5$ under the residue-coordinate bijection.
Gap
Missing bridges
A cyclic symmetry does not force full-automorphism pair-orbit counts to equal the number of distinct adjacency eigenvalues. Distinct coordinate disagreement patterns can remain different orbits while different tensor summands share an eigenvalue.
Route
Proposed approach
Translation by one is a cyclic graph symmetry. Edges of the three coordinate types have respectively $0$, $1$ and $3$ common neighbours. These intrinsic counts are invariant under every graph automorphism. Paths using the other two types recover equality of each coordinate, so automorphisms preserve all three coordinate equality relations. Eight representative disagreement patterns therefore lie in distinct unordered-pair orbits.
An exact integer circulant certificate gives the annihilating polynomial
$$p(X)=X(X-7)(X-5)(X-4)(X-2)(X+1)(X+3).$$
Transport to the real adjacency endomorphism and evaluation on an eigenvector put every eigenvalue in ${-3,-1,0,2,4,5,7}$. Thus the proof needs only $s\geq8$ and $r\leq7$, which contradict the asserted equality.
Falsifier
What would falsify this route
A failure of circulancy, a collapse of the eight representative orbits under a full graph automorphism, or a failure of the exact annihilator identity would invalidate this witness. These obligations are checked on the proof path to
result
. The formal assertion keeps diagonal pairs and the full automorphism group.

Computed — exact base counts and resonance.
The base has $1{,}440$ full graph automorphisms and exactly eight unordered-pair orbits of sizes $15,30,30,30,60,60,120,120$. Its characteristic polynomial is $x^4(x-7)(x-5)(x-4)^2(x-2)^6(x+1)^8(x+3)^8$, giving exactly seven distinct eigenvalues. The subset-sum equality $2+3=5$ merges two indexed tensor values. Evidence: the pinned exact experiment; these exact counts are not asserted as separate Lean theorems.
Computed — finite extensions.
Direct graph, cyclic-symmetry and characteristic-polynomial checks cover $b=3,5,7$ in $K_2\square K_b\square K_{b+2}$. All $50$ odd values $3\leq b\leq101$ have seven distinct subset sums. The five checked factor lists are $(2,3,5)$, $(2,3,5,11)$, $(2,3,5,11,23)$, $(2,3,5,11,23,47)$ and $(2,3,5,11,23,47,97)$, with $7,14,28,56,112$ distinct subset sums. The corresponding $8,16,32,64,128$ orbit counts are formula outputs outside the exhaustively enumerated base. Evidence: the pinned experiment and its exact tested scope above.
Open — general families.
The infinite odd-$b$ family, equality if and only if distinct subset sums for general distinct complete factors, and the unbounded-gap extension are not kernel-formalized in this delivery. Finite formula checks do not establish their infinite quantifiers.

1366: 8    Conclusion
1367: In this paper, we have derived the first-order asymptotics of the number of galled networks and proved
1368: limit laws for shape parameters of galled networks which are picked uniformly at random. This is the
1369: first time that such results are obtained for a widely-used class of phylogenetic networks of “large” size;
1370: compare with the discussion in Section 1.
1371:      We end the paper with some concluding remarks.
1372:      First, because of (2), Theorem 2 also implies corresponding limit distribution results for the number
1373: of tree nodes and for the total number of nodes of galled networks with n leaves. It would be interesting
1374: to study stochastic properties of the height of galled networks and other shape parameters, where the
1375: height is defined as the number of edges on each of the longest paths from the root to a leaf (see the
1376: conclusion of [12] where this parameter was called the depths).
1377:      Second, another question is to investigate further properties of the limit distribution Y from Theo-
1378: rem 2 since the expression given there (which is obtained by summing over j) is not particularly easy
1379: to handle. (However, as seen in Section 6, at least the computation of moments is feasible from this
1380: expression.)
1381:      Finally, the most immediate question raised by our study is how about other “large” classes of
1382: phylogenetic networks? Can they be (asymptotically) enumerated as well? Also, how to study the
1383: limit behavior of shape parameters for them? One natural class to consider next would be the class
1384: of reticulation-visible networks. In [11], the authors asked for (exact) enumeration results. We now
1385: broaden this question and ask for an asymptotic study similar to the one we carried out in this paper.
