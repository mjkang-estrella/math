The needed classical facts are precise. Every right option of a nonnegative numeric literal form is strictly positive. Check each auction against arbitrary opponent bids and subsequent choices:

* Left bids zero. If she wins, Right bid zero and Left owned the marker. On W this excludes the zero-value case; N is positive. Choose the nonnegative left option just described. The tie transfers the marker to Right, no chips change, and the successor is again in W, even when its classical value is exactly zero. The move exists.
* If Right wins a tied zero bid, he owned the marker and transfers it to Left. Every legal right option is strictly positive, so the successor is in W regardless of owner.
* If Right wins a positive bid with his own marker, he may retain it or explicitly transfer it; either result is strictly positive and the strategy covers both. If Left owned the marker, Right's positive bid leaves it with Left, again harmless. The payment changes the chip split within the same b and does not affect W.

At the literal empty form and Right's marker, Left's zero bid forces exactly this result (or Right bids positively and still cannot move). At any nonempty classical-zero form with Right's marker, any right move goes strictly positive.

Repeated positive bids cannot evade induction: every actual move decreases birthday; an auction winner unable to move loses immediately.
