## 331096 — full refutation by an earlier source example; routine bridge, zero credit

Literal source: Alvir, arXiv:1508.02432v1 (10 August 2015), Definition 2 and Conjecture 12, p.7; annihilator equivalence in Introduction and Lemmas 1–2. The formula is the simultaneous gcd of a and ALL principal-union generators, not a separate representative for each generator. Minimal union is not defined more precisely in the source. The construction below satisfies both pairwise divisor minimality and irredundancy (no summand can be removed), so it does not exploit a merely redundant presentation.

Earlier material example: Spiroff–Wickham, Communications in Algebra 39 (2011), 2338–2348, Introduction pp.2338–2339 and Definition 1.1. Actual author-hosted journal body read. It explicitly uses D/I with D=Z[X,Y], I=(X^3,XY). This predates Alvir. We do not claim that this paper explicitly states an Alvir refutation.

D is a UFD, by Gauss's lemma twice. Choose one associate representative of every nonzero element n of I which has no proper nonassociate divisor in I. Call this set N. Every nonzero f in I has a divisor in N: among its finitely many divisors up to associates which lie in I, choose one with minimal number of irreducible factors. Thus I is exactly the union of (n), n in N (and 0 belongs to every such ideal). If n belongs to (m) for m,n in N, minimality implies they are associates, hence m=n. In particular n itself witnesses that (n) cannot be removed from the union. This proves the required minimal-union condition, including for the infinite family.

X^3 and XY are in N up to associates. A proper divisor of X^3 is a unit, X or X^2, none of which is in I. Proper divisors of XY are units, X, Y, none of which is in I. Each element of I is divisible by X; since gcd(X^3,XY)=X, the gcd of ALL elements of N is X up to a unit. For a=Y, the simultaneous gcd of a and all n in N is therefore 1. Existence and value of this infinite gcd are proved by the two displayed members, not assumed for arbitrary infinite families.

In D/I, x=X+I and y=Y+I are nonzero (monomial membership in (X^3,XY)); xy=0. Hence y is a legitimate nonzero zero-divisor, and x is a nonzero member of ann(y). But ann(1)=0, so [y] differs from [1]. The right side is not even a vertex of the compressed graph; if brackets are extended to all annihilator classes as in Alvir Theorem 5, equality is still false. This is a refutation of the intended annihilator equality, beyond merely complaining about brackets on units.

This completes the reduction from the earlier explicit ring example, with minimal-union and gcd bridges fully paid. Proposed routine-source exclusion, zero credit; no exclusion based on ease or size. Whether anyone explicitly published this exact refutation later, any final Alvir publication/corrections, and exact registry coverage remain HOLD. Those unresolved questions do not turn an earlier-example reduction into a novel qualifying result. No inference of worldwide novelty.

Alternative checked mechanism (not a second credited family): D=k[X,Y], I=(X,Y)^2, a=X. The same irreducible-factor descent provides a minimal union containing X^2 and Y^2. Simultaneous gcd is 1, x is nonzero and x*x=0, so ann(x) differs from ann(1). This is a full independent algebraic witness, subsumed for campaign purposes by the preceding source reduction.

## 381265 — complete all-E equality through a source/classical bridge, zero credit

Wu, arXiv:1508.07337v4 (29 March 2017), Definition 1.1, Lemmas 2.5–2.7, Lemma 3.7, Proposition 1.12, Conjecture 1.22, Lemma 4.3 and Corollary 4.7. Actual text passages read. Let B=Z[x_e:e in E(G)], I the elementary-symmetric incidence ideal, and S=Z[x_e:e in E]. Lemma 3.7 identifies H_0=B/I. Its annihilator as an S-module is I intersect S: test multiplication on the class of 1, then use the ideal property. Thus the literal module Krull dimension is dim(S/(I intersect S)), without assuming that B/I is finite as S-module.

At each vertex pad the lists of incoming and outgoing edge variables with zeros to equal length. For each choice sigma of a bijection between the padded lists at every vertex, define L_sigma by all matched differences. There are finitely many choices. Every incidence generator lies in L_sigma, since matched lists have identical elementary symmetric functions. L_sigma is generated by variable equalities and variables forced to zero. Taking the equivalence relation generated by these equalities, marking any class containing a forced zero, gives an explicit isomorphism B/L_sigma = Z[t_C: C an unmarked class]. The map sends each unmarked edge variable to its class variable and each marked one to zero; choosing one edge in each unmarked class supplies its inverse. Therefore L_sigma is prime.

For EVERY prime P containing I, pass to the integral domain B/P. At a vertex equality of all elementary symmetric functions gives equality of the two monic products of (T-variable). The lists can be matched in this domain: substitute the first element of one list into the equal polynomial; a product of differences is zero, so one factor is zero. Cancel the common monic linear factor in the domain polynomial ring and induct. Zeros and repeated elements cause no exception. Choose these finitely many matchings vertexwise. Then L_sigma is contained in P. It follows directly from the intersection-of-primes description of radicals that radical(I) is the finite intersection of the L_sigma. This works integrally; it is not an inference from a complex point-set equality.

Contract to S. Radical commutes with contraction and contraction commutes with intersections. K_sigma=L_sigma intersect S is again generated by zeros and equalities among retained variables: the restriction of the explicit polynomial map above has image exactly Z[t_C:C unmarked and meeting E]. Distinct such class variables are algebraically independent, and the induced polynomial map has precisely the asserted kernel. Write r_sigma for their number. Thus S/K_sigma is a polynomial ring in r_sigma variables over Z.

For completeness dimension of S/(intersection K_sigma) is max dim(S/K_sigma). Any prime containing the intersection contains some K_sigma: the product of these finitely many ideals lies in their intersection, and primality otherwise supplies one element outside the prime from each ideal, a contradiction. Given a prime chain choose K_sigma in its bottom prime, so the entire chain lies over this same K_sigma. The reverse dimension inequality follows by quotient maps. Nilpotents do not change prime chains.

Over Q use the identical padded matchings and the identical argument. Equivalently localization is exact, so the rational annihilator is the rational extension of I intersect S. The corresponding quotients are Q[t_C:C meets E], with the SAME r_sigma. Wu's Lemma 4.3 gives dimensions r_sigma+1 over Z and r_sigma over Q. Taking maxima proves precisely
K-dim_(Q[E]) H_0^Q(G) = K-dim_(Z[E]) H_0(G)-1
for every finite directed graph and every subset E. Empty E and graphs with no edges give Z and Q, hence dimensions 1 and 0, as required. Loops are present in both lists and cause no change.

This is the full prime-quotient and contraction version of Wu's Lemmas 2.5–2.7, plus the polynomial dimension fact already stated as Lemma 4.3. All semantic bridges are supplied here; no restriction to E(G), a single vertex, characteristic zero fibers alone, or flatness inference is used. Recommend complete routine-source/classical bridge exclusion, zero credit. This does not authenticate an explicit earlier publication of this exact equality. ## 327031 — sustained matrix-cover route, incomplete

Dolzan–Oblak arXiv:1401.1941v1: total graph definition p.1; Lemma 4.2, Proposition 4.3, Lemma 4.4, Theorem 4.5 and final conjecture pp.7–8 read, including their proofs and M_2(F_2), M_3(F_2) examples. The record/source say 'arbitrary ring' at the conjecture, whereas the setup and total graph invariance proofs use finite rings. Preserve this fidelity gate; an infinite radical is not silently finite.

First route: modulo radical and product reductions lead to the matrix assertion gamma(tau(M_n(F_q)))=n(q-1)+1. The source gives the upper set {0} union {a E_1j:a nonzero,1<=j<=n}. A nonzero determinant has a nonzero cofactor along row 1; changing that entry kills the determinant. Signs of cofactors must be included (the extracted source suppresses them); choosing the actual signed cofactor fixes the construction. This proves the upper bound, not minimality.

Second route: interpret a dominating set D as a cover by translates of the singular-matrix locus, with each center also counted in its own closed neighborhood. To refute domination one must construct A outside D with det(A+d) nonzero for EVERY d in D. Avoiding a single translate or an intersection of kernels is inadequate because the singular vector may depend on d.

A rigorous partial obstruction: in characteristic two, if |D|=t<q then F(A)=product_(d in D) det(A+d) is a nonzero polynomial in n^2 variables, with individual variable degrees at most t<q. It is nonzero because the polynomial ring is a domain and each determinant factor is nonzero (top homogeneous part det(A)). By induction on variables, a nonzero polynomial of individual degrees below q cannot vanish on all F_q-points: the univariate fact is the root bound, and write in the last variable then apply induction to a nonzero coefficient. Therefore some A makes all determinants nonzero. In characteristic two this A cannot equal any d in D, since det(d+d)=0. Such A is undominated, proving gamma>=q in this case. This is a helper only: q<n(q-1)+1 for n>=2. In odd characteristic the outside-D step is additionally unpaid.

Third route: extending this polynomial argument to t=n(q-1) fails because individual degrees reach or exceed q and a nonzero polynomial may induce the zero function (X^q-X). Choosing just diagonal A or a one-parameter pencil also loses control of all centers. No coefficient argument preventing reduction to zero modulo (X_ij^q-X_ij) is supplied. The precise missing lemma is simultaneous invertibility outside D for every D of size <=n(q-1), in all n>=2 and all q. No solver, finite enumeration, numerical experiment or source-asserted small example was passed off as that lemma. PARK, no complete result and no Pi plan.
