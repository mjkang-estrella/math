                                                   A BSTRACT. A permutation is called block-wise simple if it contains no
                                                   interval of the form 𝑝1 ⊕ 𝑝2 or 𝑝1 ⊖ 𝑝2 . We present this new set of permu-
                                                   tations and explore some of its combinatorial properties. We present a
                                                   generating function for this set, as well as a recursive formula for count-
                                                   ing block-wise simple permutations. Following Tenner, who founded the
                                                   notion of interval posets, we characterize and count the interval posets
                                                   corresponding to block-wise simple permutations. We also present a bi-
                                                   jection between these interval posets and certain tiling’s of the 𝑛-gon.
                                                   Finally, we prove that the bi-variate distribution of the descent and in-
                                                   verse descent numbers are gamma-positive, provided the correctness of
                                                   our recent conjecture on simple permutations.

                                                                            1. INTRODUCTION
                                              This study focuses on a new set of permutations called block-wise simple
                                           permutations, which we define in two different but equivalent ways. The
                                           first definition is recursive and is stated here. The other, as well as the proof
                                           of the equivalence of the two definitions, is presented in Section 3. The
                                           notions and notations required are defined in Section 2.
                                           Definition 1.1. Let 𝑛 be the group of permutations of the set {1, … , 𝑛}.
                                              (1) The identity permutation 𝜋 = 1 is block-wise simple.
                                              (2) A permutation 𝜋 ∈ 𝑛 is block-wise simple if there is 𝜎 ∈ 𝑘 (𝑘 ≥ 4)
                                                   which is a simple permutation (see Definition 2.3), and there are

                                                   𝜎[𝛼1 , … , 𝛼𝑘 ], the inflation of 𝜎 by 𝛼1 , … , 𝛼𝑘 (see Definition 2.7).

Definition 2.1. Let 𝜋 = 𝑎1 ⋯ 𝑎𝑛 ∈ 𝑛 . An interval (or block) of 𝜋 is a non-
empty contiguous sequence of entries 𝑎𝑖 𝑎𝑖+1 ⋯ 𝑎𝑖+𝑘 whose values also form
a contiguous sequence of integers. For 𝑎 < 𝑏, [𝑎, 𝑏] denotes the interval of

are called proper.

        3. E NUMERATION OF BLOCK-WISE SIMPLE PERMUTATIONS
3.1. An alternate definition of block-wise simple permutations. Recall
Definition 1.1. We begin this section with another definition of block-wise
simple permutations.
Definition 3.1. A permutation 𝜋 ∈ 𝑛 is block-wise simple if and only if it
has no interval of the form 𝑝1 ⊕ 𝑝2 or 𝑝1 ⊖ 𝑝2 .
Theorem 3.2. The two definitions of block-wise simple permutations are
equivalent.
Proof. If 𝜋 ∈ 𝑛 is block-wise simple according to definition 1.1, it is clear
that 𝜋 contains no interval of the form 𝑝1 ⊕ 𝑝2 or 𝑝1 ⊖ 𝑝2 .
   On the other hand, let 𝜋 ∈ 𝑛 , 𝑛 > 1, be such that 𝜋 has no interval
of the form 𝑝1 ⊕ 𝑝2 or 𝑝1 ⊖ 𝑝2 . Based on theorem 2.9, we can write 𝜋 =
𝜎1 [𝛼11 , … , , 𝛼𝑘1 ] with 𝜎1 ∈ 𝑆𝑖𝑚𝑝𝑘 and assume to the contrary that 𝜋 is not
block-wise simple. Because 𝜋 has no intervals of the form 𝑝1 ⊕𝑝2 or 𝑝1 ⊖𝑝2 ,
we must have 𝑘 ≥ 4 and there is some 1 ≤ 𝑖 ≤ 𝑘 such that 𝛼𝑖1 is not block-
wise simple. Assuming without loss of generality that 𝑖 = 1, we can write
𝛼11 = 𝜎2 [𝛼12 , … 𝛼𝑙2 ] . As 𝜋 has no intervals of the form 𝑝1 ⊕ 𝑝2 or 𝑝1 ⊖ 𝑝2 , 𝜎2
is also simple of order 𝑙 ≥ 4. Without loss of generality, 𝛼12 is not block-wise
simple. Proceeding in this manner, we obtain a sequence of non-block-wise
simple permutations (𝛼1𝑗 ) which are inflation’s of simple permutations, such
that the sequence of the orders (|𝛼1𝑗 |) is strictly decreasing. Hence, some 𝑗
exists, such that |𝛼1𝑗 | = 1 whereas 𝛼1𝑗 is not block-wise simple. However,
this is a contradiction.                                                         

Definition 4.1. The interval poset of a permutation 𝜋 ∈ 𝑆𝑛 is the poset 𝑃 (𝜋)
whose elements are the non-empty intervals of 𝜋; the order is defined by set
inclusion (see for example Figure 3). The minimal elements are the intervals

direct descendants are increasingly ordered according to the minimum of
each interval from left to right. We note that in [11] another embedding of

dants. Hence, all simple permutations of a given order 𝑛 share the same
interval poset (see for example Figure 4).

  Following [15], we define a dual-claw poset as a poset that has a unique
maximal element and 𝑘 ≥ 4 minimal elements, which are all covered by the
maximal element. For our convenience, we omit the adjective ’dual’ and
call it here just claw poset .
  A claw poset with 𝑘 minimal elements is an interval poset of a permutation
𝜎 if and only if 𝜎 has no proper intervals; that is, 𝜎 is simple. Thus, its
generators are the simple permutations of order 𝑘.

Remark 4.3. A tree poset is a poset whose Hasse diagram is a tree. In [15]
(Theorem 6.1), the author claimed that 𝑃 (𝜎) is a tree interval poset if and
only if 𝜎 contains no interval of the form 𝑝1 ⊕ 𝑝2 ⊕ 𝑝3 or 𝑝1 ⊖ 𝑝2 ⊖ 𝑝3 .
Based on Theorem 3.2, it is evident that the interval poset of a block-wise
simple permutation is a tree. In fact, by Definition 1.1, the interval poset
of a block-wise simple permutation is a claw of claws. This means that the
root is a claw and every node is either a claw or a leaf (see Figure. 5 for an
example).

Example 4.5. Consider [𝑎, 𝑏] = [1, 17] in Figure 6. The corresponding
polygon is 𝐸 = {1, … , 18}. The descendants of [1, 17] are [1, 10], {11},
[12, 15], {16} and {17}.
   The interval [1, 10] creates the sub-polygon {1, … , 11} of order 11. The
interval [12, 15] creates the sub-polygon {12, … , 16}. Both are of size greater
than 4 by the condition on the size of intervals. The additional sub-polygon
{1, 11, 12, 16, 17, 18} is of size greater than 4 because each of the descen-
dants of [1, 17] contributes at least one vertex (its first vertex if it is an inter-
val, or itself if it is a singleton) and including the vertex 18 makes it greater
than 4.

simple permutation of order 𝑛 is equal to the number of ways to place non-
crossing diagonals in a convex (𝑛 + 1)-gon such that no triangles or quadri-
laterals are present.
