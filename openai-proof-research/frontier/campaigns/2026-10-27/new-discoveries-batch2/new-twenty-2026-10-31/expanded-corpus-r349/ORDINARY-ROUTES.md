## 366258: treewidth and connectivity partition

Exact intended endpoint: for each positive integer k and finite k-connected graph G of treewidth at most k, find an independent I with tw(G-I) <= k-1 and vertex connectivity of G-I >= k-1 (including the usual size requirements). The literal record says integer k without a lower bound; negative-width or empty-graph conventions are a scope issue, not a substantive refutation.

Read the source v1, v2 and journal endpoint: Conjecture 40 in v1 becomes Conjecture 36 in v2 and EJC 33(1), P1.27 (2026), final page of main text. The source itself gives the k-tree case. Horton’s counterexample mentioned in the record’s references concerns a different planar/Hamiltonian assertion and does not refute this partition conjecture.

First route, completion and coloring. A partial k-tree can be completed on its vertex set to a k-tree H (for sufficiently many vertices). Properly color H with k+1 colors by its recursive construction: start with distinct colors on the initial clique and give a new vertex the sole color missing on its k-clique neighborhood. Every maximal clique has all k+1 colors. For a color a, its class I_a is independent in G and H-I_a is a (k-1)-tree: initially it is K_k; a new vertex of color a disappears, and any other new vertex has exactly k-1 retained earlier neighbors forming a clique. Thus tw(G-I_a) <= k-1 by the inherited decomposition. This closes the width requirement for each color.

The same construction closes connectivity if G=H. A (k-1)-tree is (k-1)-connected: K_k has that property; adding a vertex with at least k-1 neighbors to a (k-1)-connected graph preserves it, since deletion of fewer than k-1 vertices leaves an old connected graph and a surviving neighbor for the new vertex. This supplies every bridge in the published special-case argument, and earns zero as a source adaptation.

Failure beyond the special case: deleting completion edges can disconnect H-I_a. G being k-connected controls deletion of fewer than k vertices, whereas I_a can contain arbitrarily many vertices. It supplies no immediate connectivity conclusion after deleting I_a. Even an individual color can fail: take C4, k=2, triangulated by diagonal 1--3 and colors 1:A, 2:B, 3:C, 4:B in cyclic order. Deleting color B leaves the two nonadjacent vertices 1 and 3 in the original graph, though it leaves an edge in the completion. This is a hand-specified obstruction to the claimed implication, not a counterexample to the conjecture (another color works).

Second route, track separators rather than merely bag sizes. For candidate I, failure of (k-1)-connectivity means there is S subset V(G)\I with |S| <= k-2 and two nonempty sets A,B partitioning V(G)\(I union S) with no A--B edge. Thus I union S is a separator of G. A proof must choose one width-reducing independent set that simultaneously avoids every such obstruction, not just hit all size-(k+1) bags. k-connectivity only implies |I union S| >= k and hence at least two deleted independent vertices can be implicated. Those two need not be adjacent, so independence is no contradiction.

A local exchange replacing a deleted vertex by a retained one could repair such a separator, but must also hit every saturated bag and keep the deleted set independent. The source k-tree greedy step does not provide this exchange invariant in a graph with missing completion edges. No simultaneous separator-avoidance lemma is proved here. This is the precise unpaid gap. General k-tree subclasses, k=1 and the source k=2 theorem are helpers/special cases, not the exact endpoint. No finite counterexample is asserted. No Pi execution plan is warranted.

## 365361: balanced tree decomposition

Exact endpoint: for every n>=1 and every tree T with n vertices on each side, partition E(K_{2n-1,2n-1}) into edge sets of copies of T. Let q=2n-1. T has q edges, so exactly q copies are needed. Divisibility is satisfied, and does not prove packing.

Read v1 Section 5.1, Conjecture 19 and Questions 20--21, and the journal version Discrete Mathematics 348 (2025), 114641, same numbered conjecture and examples. The smaller-host P6 example concerns K_{5,3}, not the assigned K_{5,5}. Its obstruction cannot be transferred.

First route, cyclic development, with a complete sufficient bridge. Suppose there are injective maps a:L(T)->Z/qZ and b:R(T)->Z/qZ for which the q oriented differences b(v)-a(u), uv in E(T), are all distinct. They therefore run through Z/qZ. For each s in Z/qZ embed u on the left as a(u)+s and v on the right as b(v)+s. Injection on each side makes each a genuine copy of T. Given host edge (x,y), its difference d=y-x identifies exactly one tree edge uv; then s=x-a(u) is the unique translate covering (x,y). Consequently these q copies are edge-disjoint and cover every host edge. No primality of q is used. This is a full exact decomposition conditional only on the stated labeling lemma.

Trying to establish the labels by rooting T and prescribing edge differences does establish consistency: on a tree the labels propagate uniquely from a root value, as there are no cycles. It does not establish injection on either side. A collision between same-side vertices imposes that an alternating signed sum of prescribed differences on their even-length path is zero modulo q. Distinct edge differences do not prevent this: on a length-four path, distinct residues 0,1,3,2 modulo 5 have alternating sum 0-1+3-2=0. Extending that path by a leaf gives the balanced P6, so the consistency shortcut already fails inside the intended class. P6 itself is not a refutation; one may choose other differences. The missing lemma is a global choice of all q distinct differences with every same-side path sum nonzero, for arbitrary balanced T. No proof or literature clearance for that lemma is supplied. One cannot call root propagation an exact ordinary proof.

Second route, pack q copies without assuming a common cyclic orbit. Define embeddings a_i:L->Z/qZ, b_i:R->Z/qZ for 1<=i<=q, each injective on its side. The exact necessary and sufficient certificate is that (i,uv) -> (a_i(u),b_i(v)) is bijective onto the q^2 host edges. A naive greedy leaf insertion must place one new vertex in each copy while avoiding both used host edges and within-copy collisions. Hall's condition for a single batch is insufficient: earlier placements change later available edge sets, and reassigning a center changes every incident edge already embedded. Neither Hall's inequalities nor a global exchange invariant is proved. This identifies a mathematical simultaneous-placement gap, not an invitation to run a solver.

The journal's approximate packing result leaves slack and handles far fewer than q copies at the scale required here; it is not an exact-cover theorem. Its introduction attributes an approximate version to Matthes, not a complete Graham--Haggkvist proof. The earlier general conjecture for any q-edge tree implies this statement by |V(T)|=q+1, but is a conjectural premise, so that reduction earns no solution. Classification restricted to paths/stars/double-stars would be a family instance. General exact labeling/packing remains unpaid. No Pi dispatch.

## 364636: full ordinary source proof and exact public bridge

This route is CLOSED as ordinary mathematics, but PUBLIC, so excluded from new credit. The source v1 Conjecture 9 matches the square existence-of-integer-coefficients endpoint. V2 Conjecture 2 specifies all signs and general rectangular dimensions. Colbrook's public manuscript, pinned revision 0e916df335209819b5bf9bb8ed8f65ea978c049d, Theorem 1 and Sections 1--4, proves this stronger identity. Its complete source hash is cc794d1fe11d5ae3bc5eaf1a635720ed0ad5c977cba4c7c7064dc7f29bff0c8f. The public record dates the ordinary resolution to 11 September 2026 and formal verification to 17 September. First-merge chronology was not independently reconstructed; the proof was encountered before this investment, so no priority/independent rediscovery claim is made.

Here is a verification of the square argument, beyond merely citing the degree bound. Let D be all pairs (R,C) of equal-cardinality subsets of {2,...,n}. Define Delta_RC=det A[{1} union R,{1} union C] and Gamma_RC=a11 det A[R,C], with increasing indices and empty determinant 1. Define the integer matrix B indexed by D with diagonal 2|R|-n, raising coefficient (-1)^(position of added row + position of added column), lowering the negative of the deletion sign, and row/column exchange coefficients given by the ordinary determinant reorder signs. These are exactly H/n in the public rectangular formula at m=n; all entries are integers. Put c_S=det B[S,S]. Then c_S is an integer depending only on n,S. The principal-minor expansion of K=diag(Gamma)+x B diag(Delta), by choosing the second summand in precisely the columns S, is det K=sum_S c_S M(S) x^|S|. Off-S columns are diagonal, so the sign and residual factor are exactly the principal minor and the complementary Gamma product.

It remains to prove det K=0. First let A already have all row/column sums 1, x=a11>0, and block form [x r^t; c E]. Set T=E-cr^t/x and f_RC=det T[R,C], b_RC=det E[R,C]. Then Delta=xf. Summing rows and columns gives T1=1-c/x, 1^tT=1^t-r^t/x, and sum(T)=n-1/x. Let L,U,C0,R0 denote the signed lowering, raising, column-exchange and row-exchange sums on the array f, respectively. For order k minors, cofactor expansion and replacing one selected column/row at a time give:

 Df[J]=Lf;
 Df[(T1)1^t]=k f+C0 f;
 Df[1(1^tT)]=k f+R0 f;
 Df[(T1)(1^tT)]=sum(T) f-Uf.

Here Df[Z] is the coefficient of t in det(T[R,C]+t Z[R,C]). These are polynomial identities and require no inverse of a selected minor. For the fourth, sum the bordered determinant identity T_iC adj(T_RC) T_Rj=T_ij det(T_RC)-det([T_RC T_Rj; T_iC T_ij]) over i,j. Borders with repeated selected row/column vanish; the others give the raising signs. For k=0 the last identity reads 0=sum(T)-sum(T), and the other empty-minor cases are also immediate.

Write u=1-T1 and v^t=1^t-1^tT. Then E=T+xuv^t. Terms with two rank-one replacement columns vanish, so b=f+x Df[uv^t]. Expand uv^t=J-(T1)1^t-1(1^tT)+(T1)(1^tT). Substitution yields b=x[(n-2k)f+Lf-C0f-R0f-Uf]. With Delta=xf this rearranges to b+(2k-n)Delta+U Delta-L Delta+C0 Delta+R0 Delta=0, which is exactly b+B Delta=0 coordinatewise. Since Gamma=x b, K1=0. The empty coordinate of 1 is 1, so this is a nonzero null vector and det K=0.

For general positive A, let A'=D_alpha A D_beta be its Sinkhorn scaling. Each Delta_RC and Gamma_RC acquires the same positive factor d_RC=alpha1 beta1 product_R alpha_i product_C beta_j. Thus K(A',x)=K(A,x)diag(d), at x=Sink(A)11. The balanced case supplies K(A',x)1=0, hence K(A,x)d=0 with d nonzero, proving the identity for all positive A. Only the standard existence of the positive Sinkhorn scaling is used in this ordinary bridge; the public formal contracts separately include existence and uniqueness.

Finally |D|=sum_k binom(n-1,k)^2=binom(2n-2,n-1) by counting (n-1)-subsets of two disjoint (n-1)-sets by intersection size. Therefore the degree bound in the literal formula is respected. n=1 is included: D contains the empty pair, B=[-1], K=[a11(1-x)] and x=1. Vanishing minors never required division. The literal omission of a nonzero-coefficient condition would also admit the all-zero choice, but that wording shortcut is not the reason for exclusion: the substantive nontrivial identity itself is already public. Fang's degree bound alone would not supply these structured coefficients.
