Abstract
    

A 2-factor of a graph GGG is a 2-regular spanning subgraph of GGG. We present a survey summarising results on the structure of 2-factors in regular graphs, as achieved by various researchers in recent years.

3.2 Perfect Matching Hamiltonian Graphs




A graph GGG admitting a 111–factor is said to have the Perfect-Matching-Hamiltonian property (for short, the PMH-property) if every 111–factor MMM of GGG can be extended to a Hamiltonian cycle of GGG, that is, there exists a 111–factor NNN of GGG such that M\cup NM∪NM\cup N induces a Hamiltonian cycle of GGG. This problem was first introduced by Las Vergnas [37] and Häggkvist [29] in the 1970s, and in recent years this concept was studied with particular focus on cubic graphs, the reader may find more details in [5] and [9] . For simplicity, a graph admitting the PMH-property is said to be PMH or a PMH-graph. The class of 222-factor Hamiltonian graphs are of course PMH-graphs.
