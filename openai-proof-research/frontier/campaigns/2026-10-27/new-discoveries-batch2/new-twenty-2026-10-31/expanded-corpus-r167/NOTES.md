## 1. 372835: Radtke–Keszegh–Lauff Conjecture 3 (blue k-gon with ≥ k − 4 red pseudolines)

Source arXiv:2601.20574v1 (only version). §3 has Conjecture 3; the tightness example is Figure 13.

### Reduction (proved here)

- **Red lines are chords.** Let P be a face of the blue sub-arrangement. Every red pseudoline meets P in a single segment: to re-enter P it would have to recross a blue line it already crossed, since P lies on one side of every blue line. The two endpoints of that segment lie on different sides of P.
- **The picture inside P.** So inside P we have m pseudo-chords, pairwise crossing at most once. The faces incident to ∂P are the "boundary faces".
- **Face sizes.** A boundary face f with a_f boundary arcs, r_f red edges and j_f polygon corners inside its arcs is supported by exactly j_f + a_f + r_f pseudolines.
- **Bichromatic is automatic.** If m ≥ 1, every boundary face contains a red edge and a blue edge.
- **Equivalence.** So for k ≥ 5 the conjecture holds if and only if no configuration has all boundary faces of size ≥ 5 together with k ≤ m + 4 corners.

### Literal defects (zero credit)

- **k ∈ {3, 4}.** With zero red lines, P itself is a monochromatic blue triangle or quadrangle, so the literal statement fails.
- **Unbounded P.** If P is allowed to be an unbounded k-sided region, red pseudolines that run to infinity inside P without crossing there leave no bounded face at all. Straight lines suffice: k lines tangent to a parabola, with nearly vertical red lines.

### Euler identity

For any configuration, Σ_{inner faces} (|f| − 4) = k − 4. Hence the conjecture is equivalent (for k ≥ 5) to the arrangement inequality

  (★) Σ_{boundary faces} (5 − a_f − r_f) ≥ m + 5,

equivalently c ≥ Σ_{interior faces} (5 − |f|), where c is the number of crossings.

Sufficiency: (★) gives k ≥ Σ_f max(0, 5 − a_f − r_f) ≥ m + 5.

### Exhaustive search (`scripts/chords.py`)

- **Method.** All pseudo-chord arrangements were generated by incremental insertion through the dual graph. Every arrangement arises this way, by deleting the last chord. Deduplication uses an exact planar-map canonical form under rotation and reflection.
- **Counts.** Distinct arrangements: m = 1: 1; m = 2: 2; m = 3: 5; m = 4: 19; m = 5: 130; m = 6: 1824.
- **Result.** For every arrangement with m ≤ 6, the exact minimum corner count is k_min = m + 5, and (★) holds even without the positive part. So the conjecture holds for all (k, m) with m ≤ 6.
- **Equality cases.** The non-crossing configurations (the tight family of Figure 13) and the pentagram (m = 5: five wedges and five faces of type (a, r) = (1, 4)).
- **Logs.** `logs/chords-m6*.log`, `logs/chords-removable.log`.

### Proof attempts (not closed)

- **Deleting a chord.** An exact formula for ΔΦ under chord deletion (Φ = LHS − RHS of (★)) gives a "removable chord" criterion. It holds in all but one arrangement per level (the pentagram family), so naive induction fails.
- **Pushing a wedge crossing out of P.** This changes Σ by −1 if the opposite face G at the crossing is a boundary face, and by 4 − |G| if G is interior. It fails when G is an interior triangle (the pentagram again).
- **Precise remaining lemma.** Prove c ≥ Σ_{interior f} (5 − |f|) for every arrangement of pseudo-chords in a disk.

### Status

Parked as **conjecture supported to m ≤ 6**. Even with a proof, the Lean cost is very high, because it needs a combinatorial model of faces of pseudoline arrangements.

## 2. 368246: Zhang–Wu–He Conjecture 1 (BJGL-type pancyclicity)

Source arXiv:2504.21628v1 (only version). The authors prove 3-cycles (Theorem 1.4) and (n − 1)-cycles (Theorem 1.5) for **strong** D. They report no computations.

### Literal defect

Conjecture 1 omits "strong". The transitive tournament T₃ is a vacuous LSD that is not pancyclic. This is a wording correction with zero credit.

### Search (`scripts/pancyc.py`)

- **Setup.** For strong D the condition implies the hypothesis of BJGL Theorem 1.1, so D is Hamiltonian. WLOG the Hamiltonian cycle is 0 → 1 → … → n−1 → 0.
- **Enumeration.** For each L, all supersets of the Hamiltonian cycle with no cycle of length L, using hereditary pruning plus a sound degree-condition pruning. Each leaf is tested for the condition and for being an LSD.
- **Exceptions.** The exceptional class D_L is, by Guo's Theorem 1.3, exactly the strong non-pancyclic LSDs. So a counterexample must be non-LSD and must not be the balanced complete bipartite digraph.
- **Coverage, exhaustive:**
  - n ≤ 8: all L;
  - n = 9: L = 3, …, 7 (L = 8 is Theorem 1.5; L = 3 is also Theorem 1.4).
- **Result.** The only non-LSD survivors are the balanced complete bipartite digraphs on 4, 6 and 8 vertices (class D_B). **No counterexample for n ≤ 9.**
- **n = 10** exceeded the cap (`logs/pancyc-pruned-10.log`). Other logs: `logs/pancyc-*.log`.

## 3. 363761: Conjecture 2.1 of arXiv:2407.01121 (well-dominated strong products)

### Reductions

- The conjecture reduces to connected G (components).
- H must itself be non-trivially WD (source Theorem 1.1).
- For WD G, the set I × {0, 2} is a maximal independent set of G ⊠ C₄ of size 2γ(G). So γ(G ⊠ C₄) < 2γ(G) already certifies non-WD.

### Exhaustive search over McKay connected graphs (`scripts/wd.py`, `scripts/wd_gamma.py`)

- **Coverage.** All connected graphs of order ≤ 9 (hashes in `outputs/graphdata.sha256`).
- **Counts.** WD and non-trivially WD graphs per order:

  | order | WD | non-trivially WD |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤ 7 | 118 | 73 |
  | 8 | 475 | 353 |
  | 9 | 3858 | 3250 |

  The source's counts on 4 and 5 vertices are reproduced.
- **Result.** For **every** non-trivially WD G, G ⊠ C₄ or G ⊠ C₅ is not WD. Each case is certified by two minimal dominating sets of different sizes.
- **Exceptions to the domination-drop mechanism.** For 18 graphs of order ≤ 8, neither C₄ nor C₅ lowers the domination number; non-WD there comes from oversized minimal dominating sets.
- **Status.** No uniform mechanism, and WD graphs are not characterized, so a full-scope proof is not in reach. Parked.

## 4. 362060: Conjecture 3.4 of arXiv:2402.17499

- **Part (i) is literally a misprint.** G is planar, hence R₃-independent (source Theorem 1.1), so G cannot be a 2-sum of circuits. The source's double banana DB − {ac, ac′} satisfies the hypothesis. Intended: "G + {e, f} is the 2-sum". Zero credit.
- **Part (ii), natural candidates.** These are 2-sums of rigid circuits with the double banana, which have |E| = 3n − 7. They are provably **not** 2-edge-apex: either a K₅-subdivision remains, or planarity with u, v cofacial would make DB edge-apex, contradicting source Example 5.1.
- **Status.** Generic-rigidity rank certificates are very Lean-expensive. Parked.

## 5. 354706: Conjecture 4.8 of arXiv:2205.10598

- **Definitions.** Egerváry graphs are the matchable graphs with no spanning subgraph made of matching edges plus at least one odd cycle. The decomposition is Deming's algorithm, which depends on the matching chosen.
- **Where a counterexample must live.** With at most one Deming-K₄ part, conditions (2) and (5) already give the Egerváry property, since G = G[V(K) ∪ V(R)]. So a counterexample needs at least two independent Deming-K₄ parts linked only through R.
- **Status.** Testing requires a faithful implementation of Deming's algorithm and of Deming-K₄ / Deming-BP subgraph recognition, which was not completed in this pass. Parked.
