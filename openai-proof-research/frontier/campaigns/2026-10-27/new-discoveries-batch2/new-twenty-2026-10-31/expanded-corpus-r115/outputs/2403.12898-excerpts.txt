357: In this article, we investigate plane Hamiltonian paths and cycles in the subclass of convex drawings.
358: Convex drawings were introduced by Arroyo, McQuillan, Richter and Salazar [10] as the most general class of the convexity hierarchy between geometric and simple drawings of  K_{n} .
359: Arroyo, Richter, and Sunohara [11] already showed Rafla’s conjecture for h-convex drawings, which are a subclass of convex drawings.
360: The notion of convexity in simple drawings generalizes the classic convexity and is based on triangles of a drawing, i.e., the subdrawings induced by three vertices.
361: Since in a simple drawing the edges of a triangle do not cross, a triangle partitions the plane (resp. the sphere) into exactly two connected components.
362: The closures of these components are the two sides of the triangle.
363: In particular, the three vertices forming the triangle lie in both sides of the triangle.
364: A side  S  of a triangle is convex if every edge that has its two incident vertices in  S  is fully contained inside  S .
