237: In 2019, Beisegel et al. [1] proposed a common generalization of all these results by introducing the concept of avoidable paths.
238: An induced path  P  in a graph  G  is said to be avoidable if every extension of  P  is contained in an induced cycle.
239: Here, an extension of  P  is any induced path in  G  that can be obtained by extending  P  by one edge from each endpoint.
240: Beisegel et al. conjectured that for every positive integer  k , every graph that contains an induced  k -vertex path also contains an avoidable induced  k -vertex path, and proved the statement for the case  k=2 .
241: The general conjecture was proved in 2020 by Bonamy et al. [5].
242: A further strengthening was given by Gurvich et al. [12], who showed that in every graph, every induced path can be transformed into an avoidable one via a sequence of shifts (where two induced  k -vertex paths are said to be shifts of each other if their union is an induced path with  k+1  vertices).
