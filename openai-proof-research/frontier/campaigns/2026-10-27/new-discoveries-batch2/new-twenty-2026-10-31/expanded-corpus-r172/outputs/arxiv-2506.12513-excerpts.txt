Theorems 2.1 and 2.3, together with Corollary 2.4, are motivated by their analogues in the continued–fraction setting. This naturally raises the question of whether the converse direction also holds; namely, whether Theorem 2.5 and Conjecture 2.6 admit counterparts for continued fractions. One may notice that for any k\in\mathbb{N}, if k\geq 2 then




\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}{\left(F_{\leq k}+F_{\geq k}\right)}=\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+4k}-k}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+4k}-k}{2k}+\frac{1}{k}<1;



in particular, F_{\leq k}+F_{\geq k}\not\equiv\mathbb{R}\mod 1, as the diameter of the sum is not sufficient to make it contain an interval of length at least 1. One can formulate the following alternative conjecture instead.
