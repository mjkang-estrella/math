Theorem 1.1 and the uniqueness of the Faber–Krahn position have several immediate consequences. A convex body whose symmetry group acts irreducibly on \mathbb{R}^{n} is automatically in Faber–Krahn position. In particular, the regular simplex minimizes \lambda_{1} among all simplices of fixed volume, in every dimension. This gives a new proof of
this fact, which is normally proved using the Steiner symmetrization. The same argument shows that a regular polygon minimizes \lambda_{1} among all of its area-normalized linear images. This gives a novel application: the affine-orbit part of the Pólya–Szegő polygonal conjecture, stating that the regular m-gon minimizes the first Dirichlet eigenvalue among all m-gons of the same area (see [37, Conjecture 5.1.19] and [7] for background and recent developments). We refer to section 2.5 for details.




In addition to Theorem 1.1 for the inverse inradius, we prove it as well for the planar Cheeger constant. The latter uses the planar characterization of the Cheeger set of a convex body due to Kawohl and Lachand-Robert [33].
