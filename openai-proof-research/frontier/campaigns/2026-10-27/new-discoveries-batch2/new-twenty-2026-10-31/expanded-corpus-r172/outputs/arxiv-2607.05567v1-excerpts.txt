The two obstructions are internal to the enumeration, yet the degree sequence
itself carries a direct mechanical reading. The flat-foldable mountain-valley
assignments of the Miura-ori are the states of a reconfigurable
metamaterial [ZWJ21], and a face flip is a single
reconfiguration between them [PLVP21]; mountain-valley
reassignment underlies the several stable states of a Miura
stack [LFXW21] and the multistable, reprogrammable devices built from
it [JMAP22, LFXW23]. A vertex degree is then the
number of single-flip reconfigurations available to a state, and the degree
sequence is the distribution of this local reconfigurability over the design
space. The states with d reconfigurations grow as a multiple of

Earlier work computed the Miura-ori flip-graph degree sequence by separate
arguments; the envelope construction gives instead a single one, valid for every
degree, rendering each degree-d count a lattice-point enumeration over a
polytope, piecewise quasi-polynomial in (m,n) and a single symmetric polynomial
on the high region. Through d=7 these polynomials are explicit and of total
degree d-2. Two questions remain at the edges of this description, whether the
total degree stays d-2 for every degree and where, below the high region, the
count first departs from p_{d}; the construction has recast both, from questions
about the flip graph into questions about the lattice-point count. That count is
an invariant of the Miura-ori design space, and one construction now reaches it
at every degree.
