      Sandpile Groups of Cayley Graphs over Fr2: Sylow-2 Structure,
      Isospectral Counterexamples, and Boolean Incidence Algebra
                                                 David N. Betzer

                                                   August 2026


                                                      Abstract
           We determine several 2-primary sandpile invariants for Cayley multigraphs of Fr2 . For the hyper-
       cube we close Conjecture 4.14 of Gao–Marx-Kuo–McDonald–Yuen by determining the first previously
       unknown Sylow-2 cyclic exponent. For arbitrary connected Cayley multigraphs, the number d(M ) of
       Sylow-2 cyclic factors is even exactly when every nonzero generator has odd multiplicity, in which case
       d(M ) = 2r − 2. We construct, for every r ≥ 3, primitive equal-degree isospectral pairs with noniso-
       morphic sandpile groups and exact factor counts dA = 3 · 2r−2 − 1 and dB = 2r − 2, disproving both
       the published eigenvalue-set conjecture and the stronger earlier unlabeled-multiset formulation. We also
       give a fixed-degree r = 3 counterexample to the proposed GLr (F2 ) classification and prove its degree-
       7 minimality. A coprime weighted-square family has a closed Smith form. The proofs use a Boolean
       incidence-algebra model that also yields exact zeta/Möbius transforms and product-of-chains spectral
       formulas.

1     Introduction
Indicator vectors, Boolean-lattice incidence matrices, and subset-indexed transforms identify finite-set op-
erations with exact coordinate, incidence-algebra, and transform representations. For critical groups and
chip-firing we use the standard Laplacian/Smith-form framework of Lorenzini and Biggs [5, 6]. We use the
classical incidence-algebra framework of Rota and the standard Boolean-lattice treatment of Stanley [7, 8],
together with the structured matrix conventions of the companion preprints [1, 2, 3, 4].

1.1    Results
The principal new results are the sandpile theorems in Section 11. Theorem 11.4 determines the previously
open nth Sylow-2 cyclic exponent of the hypercube. Theorem 11.5 gives the exact parity classification
for arbitrary connected Cayley multigraphs of Fr2 , replacing Conjecture 5.1 of Gao–Marx-Kuo–McDonald–
Yuen by a complete criterion. Theorem 11.7 then constructs an infinite family of pairs in F32 having the same
degree and the same complete Laplacian eigenvalue multiset but different numbers of Sylow-2 cyclic factors.
Theorem 11.8 lifts that mechanism to every r ≥ 3, with exact factor counts 3 · 2r−2 − 1 and 2r − 2. It
therefore refutes both published Conjecture 5.2, which is stated in terms of the set of eigenvalues [20], and
the stronger earlier REU Conjecture 10.1, which states that the sandpile group depends only on the collection
of eigenvalues without their labellings [19]. The all-odd member of the same family also provides a uniform
witness to the failure of published Conjecture 5.1 and its earlier REU form, Conjecture 9.2.

                                                          1
     Theorem 11.6 gives a closed Smith form for every coprime weighted square in F22 . Its specialization to
(1, 5, 0) and (2, 3, 0) yields equal critical groups Z/60Z from non-GL2 (F2 )-equivalent multiplicity triples.
That particular collision was already implicit in Section 7 together with Proposition 2.6 of the 2018 REU
report [19]; the new content of Theorem 11.6 is the exact Smith formula for the entire coprime family. Theo-
rem 11.9 supplies the principal counterexample to Conjecture 5.3: two degree-7, generic, isospectral Cayley
multigraphs of F32 have the same sandpile group but distinct multiplicity multisets, hence distinct GL3 (F2 )-
orbits. Proposition 11.10 proves by exact orbit enumeration that degree 7 is minimal for a same-degree
critical-group collision among connected r = 3 Cayley multigraphs.
     The Boolean incidence algebra developed in Sections 2–10 supplies the proof mechanism and has inde-
pendent transform consequences. We give exact coordinate formulas for Boolean set operations, recursive
and Kronecker constructions for the binary-ordered power-set matrix, tensor factorizations of the Boolean
zeta and Möbius matrices, matrix forms of inclusion–exclusion and exact-membership inversion, exact op-
eration counts for staged Boolean-lattice transforms, and a deterministic worst-case optimal Θ(nk) direct
algorithm for union cardinality from k explicit length-n indicators. Section 11 also extends the calculus to
bounded-multiset product posets and to sparse and quantum transform questions.


Principal sandpile results and proof roadmap
The sandpile results are the paper’s primary contribution. Their proofs are collected in Section 11 after the
Boolean-incidence machinery used to obtain them. The four structural conclusions are as follows.
   (i) The hypercube theorem determines the first previously unknown 2-primary cyclic exponent and closes
       Conjecture 4.14 of Gao–Marx-Kuo–McDonald–Yuen.
  (ii) Theorem 11.5 gives the exact parity classification of the number of Sylow-2 cyclic factors for every
       connected Cayley multigraph of Fr2 , replacing the proposed eigenvalue-valuation criterion.
 (iii) Theorem 11.8 constructs, for every r ≥ 3, an infinite family of primitive equal-degree isospectral
       Cayley multigraph pairs with nonisomorphic sandpile groups. It refutes the published eigenvalue-
       set conjecture and the stronger earlier unlabelled eigenvalue-multiset formulation in every dimension
       r ≥ 3.
 (iv) Theorem 11.9 gives a fixed-degree, isospectral, equal-critical-group counterexample to the proposed
       GL3 (F2 ) classification, and Proposition 11.10 proves degree 7 is minimal for such a same-degree
       collision in the connected r = 3 family.
The intervening Boolean-lattice results should therefore be read as the integral and mod-2 proof calculus
supporting these sandpile theorems, with independent transform and algorithmic consequences.

4     Hadamard Product Structure
Proposition 4.1 (Hadamard product on binary vectors). For u, v, w ∈ {0, 1}n , the Hadamard product u ◦ v
again lies in {0, 1}n and is the coordinatewise meet:

                                  (u ◦ v)i = ui vi = min{ui , vi },          1 ≤ i ≤ n.

Hence, with 0 = (0, . . . , 0)T and 1 = (1, . . . , 1)T ,

       u ◦ v = v ◦ u,         (u ◦ v) ◦ w = u ◦ (v ◦ w),           u ◦ 1 = u,        u ◦ 0 = 0,   u ◦ u = u.

Moreover, for the coordinatewise partial order,

                                             u◦v =u          ⇐⇒         u ≤ v,

and for every z ∈ {0, 1}n ,
                                       z ≤u◦v         ⇐⇒         z ≤ u and z ≤ v.

Proof. For each coordinate i, the numbers ui , vi ∈ {0, 1} satisfy ui vi ∈ {0, 1} and ui vi = min{ui , vi }.
This proves closure and the coordinatewise-meet formula. Commutativity, associativity, the identity and
annihilator laws, and idempotence follow coordinatewise. Also ui vi = ui if and only if ui ≤ vi , proving
u ◦ v = u ⇐⇒ u ≤ v. Finally, zi ≤ min{ui , vi } if and only if zi ≤ ui and zi ≤ vi for every i, so u ◦ v is
the greatest lower bound of u and v.

Corollary 4.2 (Binary Hadamard meet-semilattice). The triple

                                                     ({0, 1}n , ◦, 1)

is a commutative idempotent monoid. With the coordinatewise partial order,

                                  u ∧ v = u ◦ v,            0≤u≤1         (u ∈ {0, 1}n ),

so {0, 1}n is a bounded meet-semilattice with least element 0 and greatest element 1. Its order is recovered
algebraically by
                                       u ≤ v ⇐⇒ u ◦ v = u.
