## 320904: complete counterexample, completely excluded by its source

Take k=3, l=1, a=2·3·5−7=23. Let C=F_3∩[-6,23] and B=(C\{-5})∪{19}. The translated canonical set is the integers in [24,53] divisible by 2, 3, or 5. Its only elements not divisible by 2, 3, or 7 form {25}: among multiples of 5 in that interval, 30,40,50 are even, 45 is divisible by 3, and 35 by 7. Removing 25 and adding 49 therefore gives exactly the integers in [24,53] divisible by 2,3,or7. In particular every translated element of B has a divisor in the three-element set {2,3,7}. Four pairwise coprime integers are impossible by the pigeonhole principle. Since -5∈C and 19∉C, |B|=|C| and B≠C. This refutes the literal universal equality claim.

There is no credit: Chen–Zhou v2 §5, opening of the proof of Theorem 1, prints A(54,3)=(F_3\{5,25})∪{7,49}, with the interval [1,54] implicit in that notation. Intersect this printed example with [24,53] and subtract 30. The intersection removes 5 and 7, leaving exactly the B above. This is a full source-restriction/translation bridge, not a newly discovered mechanism. v1 does not state this equality conjecture; v2 Conjecture 2 is a cardinality inequality and Conjecture 3 is eventual uniqueness for initial intervals. The database blends scopes. r263's 320903 is the same source family; its metadata refutation flag alone did not prove this equality case.

## 377206: rooted trees, two sustained routes, unresolved

Definitions: rooted labelled trees on [n], k improper parent-child edges (x,y), where improper means x>β(T_y), and β is the minimum descendant label. Degree is the number of children, not undirected degree. If n has children, λ is the first vertex u≠n on the n-to-β(T_n) path satisfying u<β(T_n\T_u). In particular λ need not be the minimum descendant. The target is Conjecture 4.2 (4.1), all n≥3, all 1≤i≤n−2, with the admissible k range inherited from the tree definition. v1 says n≥2, but the i range makes n=2 vacuous; v2 retains the refined recurrence; the author manuscript link returned404 and its body was not read. Printed table checked on p16, not treated as evidence of an all-n proof.

Route A: remove the second-largest label n−1, then relabel old n to n−1. If n−1 is a leaf not below n, removing it leaves every minimum on T_n unchanged, so λ=i is preserved. This suggests the n−2 insertion choices in the first summand. The naive leaf-only decomposition is incomplete: n−1 can have children, can lie above n, or lie on the critical path inside T_n. Deleting such a vertex changes ancestor relations and can change the first critical vertex even when the global minimum is preserved. A complete bijection must partition nonleaf cases and explain exactly n+k−3 inverse choices, with no overlaps. That lemma has not been established. The source's lifting/lowering bijections (Theorem 3.2 and following discussion) specifically track critical nodes; using them without a proof that the refinement is preserved would assume the missing result.

Route B: let H_{n,i}(z)=Σ_k |R_{n,k}[λ=i]|z^k. The exact desired identity is
H_{n,i}(z)=(n−2)(1+z)H_{n−1,i}(z)+z² H'_{n−1,i}(z).
This follows formally from the proposed recurrence because Σ_k(n+k−3)h_{k−1}z^k=z[(n−2)H+zH']. Conversely coefficient comparison recovers the full recurrence. A marked-improper-edge construction would explain z²H', and ordinary attachment to one of n−2 sites would explain the first part. No such disjoint exhaustive construction is proved: marked edges can occur inside or above the critical subtree, and contractions can move λ. The two source special cases k=1 and i=n−1 do not determine all polynomials H_{n,i}; the unrefined Shor recurrence cannot imply the refined one by summing. This is a precise combinatorial gap, not a counterexample.

Before any Pi task: complete citing-body/latest-journal review; either supply the bijection or ask root for a small, exact tree-enumeration diagnostic with parent arrays, exact improper-edge and λ definitions. A bounded table alone cannot resolve the universal claim. No certificate or execution recommendation is made now.

## 377613: finite interval optimization, structural bounds and exact chamber route

The primary object is not an additive set on a finite group. For measurable E⊆[0,1), D(E)=max_c μ(E∩(2c−E)); this is the maximum measure of a symmetric subset. Δ_k(ε) is the infimum of D(E) over unions of k intervals of total length ε. Both k=3 and k=4 entire polygonal graphs must be established. Lemma 7.6 and Figure 8 were checked, together with both arXiv versions and the author manuscript target passage. The source explicitly already reduces the problem to parametric linear programs. Merely repeating that reduction receives no credit.

Route A (ordinary lower bound): sort interval lengths l1≥l2≥…≥lk. The largest interval alone gives D≥l1. Centering any two intervals at the midpoint of their centers gives symmetric paired pieces of total length 2 min(li,lj), so D≥2l2. Consequently ε≤D+(k−1)D/2 and D≥2ε/(k+1). Thus the initial slope is bounded below by 1/2 for k=3 and 2/5 for k=4. This is not the whole polygonal formula.

For k=3, an explicit initial-segment construction is E_t=(0,t)∪(2t,3t)∪(5t,7t), 0≤t≤1/7. The self-overlaps are tents on centers c in [0,t],[2t,3t],[5t,7t], with peaks t,t,2t. The unordered-pair contributions are trapezoids of height 2t, on c supports [t,2t], [5t/2,4t], [7t/2,5t]. On the overlaps [5t/2,3t] the second self-tent and the first long-pair rising edge total at most 2t; on [7t/2,4t] the two long-pair edges total 2t. Everywhere else at most one nonzero relevant contribution has height at most 2t. Hence D(E_t)=2t, |E_t|=4t. Combined with the lower bound, Δ_3(ε)=ε/2 on [0,4/7]. This is partial and falls within the source's finite-interval recipe/initial-line discussion. It is not a full MathDB solution and is not offered for credit.

Route B (exact full-scope certificate specification): write ordered endpoints 0≤x1≤…≤x_{2k}≤1 and Σ_j(x_{2j}−x_{2j−1})=ε, allowing zero lengths for boundary closure. Let s=2c. Overlap between interval i and the reflection of j has length
max(0,min(x_{2i},s−x_{2j−1})−max(x_{2i−1},s−x_{2j})).
Sum over ordered i,j. As a function of s this is continuous and piecewise affine; every breakpoint is a sum xp+xq. Therefore its maximum is among these finitely many sums. Decompose endpoint space by every comparison of such pair sums and every comparison used in the min/max expressions at those sums. On each closed chamber D is the maximum of explicit rational linear forms in endpoints. Minimize an epigraph variable d subject to d≥each form and all chamber, ordering and measure constraints. Include all faces/ties. A complete all-ε lower certificate requires exact nonnegative dual multipliers proving d≥the relevant claimed affine segment on every feasible chamber, and exact primal endpoint families for all segments. A witness against the conjecture instead needs rational endpoints, all breakpoint overlaps below the claimed polygon at the rational ε, and comparisons to all printed constructions. No multipliers, chamber census, or full list of endpoint families has been obtained. This is a sound finite semantic reduction and a remaining certificate task, not a solved LP. Runtime/size may be prohibitive; no blind sweep or solver was run. Pi execution requires root assignment after source and registry gaps are closed.

## 378417: orbit-count route parked for source/formulation reasons

For flag involutions a,b,c, the source identifies vertices, edges, faces with orbits of <b,c>, <a,c>, <a,b>. Replacing (a,b,c) with (ac,b,c) leaves vertices and edges unchanged and replaces face-orbits by <ac,b> zigzag orbits. Thus χ_s=V−E+Z. Replacing with (a,b,ac) instead leaves faces and edges unchanged, so χ_p=Z−E+F. The prism has (V,E,F)=(2m,3m,m+2), giving χ_s=Z−m and χ_p=Z−2m+2. The antiprism has (2m,4m,2m+2), giving χ_s=Z−2m and χ_p=Z−2m+2. Hence the numeric claims reduce exactly to Z_prism=gcd(m,4) and Z_antiprism=1+gcd(m,3), plus separate orientability proofs. The figure/formula page visibly calls skew(APrism_m) both oriented and non-oriented. Exploiting this is an excluded typo correction. A correct intended full result would require resolving which operation the last clause meant, proving both zigzag orbit counts and checking later map literature. No full eligible route survives these gaps; source family already appears in r720, and its Deza–Pankov successor is not automatic subsumption.
