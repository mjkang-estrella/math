## 344477 — exact tableaux endpoint, complete routine bridge

Source arXiv:1911.05049v2, Section2.3: the edge S -> s_i S is permitted when i is in the lower row and i+1 is in the upper row. Section3.2 Conjecture3.7 asks exactly whether sorted upper-row entries a_j>=b_j imply S<=T. This is not the positive-matrix conjecture Theorem3.9 addresses.

Encode a standard (n,n) tableau by a word of length2n, writing U for upper-row membership and D for lower-row membership. Row increasingness gives unique rows from the word. The column condition is equivalent to each prefix having at least as many U as D: the jth U occurs before the jth D. Thus standard tableaux correspond exactly to Dyck words. An allowed edge replaces adjacent DU by UD; it raises one intermediate prefix height by2 and leaves all other prefix heights fixed, so standardness is preserved.

Given current upper entries c_j>=b_j, if not equal choose the FIRST j with c_j>b_j. Entries with lower indices equal their targets. Then c_j-1 is not an upper entry: the previous upper entry, if present, is b_{j-1}<b_j<=c_j-1. Consequently labels c_j-1,c_j occupy lower,upper rows respectively. Their swap is an allowed edge. It changes only c_j to c_j-1, retains increasing row order, and retains c_j>=b_j. The nonnegative integer sum_j(c_j-b_j) decreases by1. After finitely many swaps all entries equal those of T. Hence S<=T. Conversely every edge decreases exactly one sorted top entry, so any directed path implies all coordinate inequalities. This proves the exact endpoint including n=0 if empty tableaux are allowed.

Coordinate containment is generated by adding single admissible cells. The proof above explicitly implements that standard cover-generation construction. ## 344067 — exact facial lattice equivalence, complete source reduction

Source arXiv:1910.03511v1 Proposition2.5: every face F determines an interval [m_F,M_F] of the region poset P, and a region R corresponds to [R,R]. Definition2.9: F<=G iff m_F<=m_G AND M_F<=M_G. Suppose FW is a lattice. For any regions R,S let J be their join in FW and write [a,b] for its facial interval. Since [R,R],[S,S]<=J, both R,S<=a. The region [a,a] is therefore an upper bound. Since J is the least upper bound, J<=[a,a], forcing b<=a. With a<=b we have a=b, so J is a region. For any region upper bound T of R,S, [R,R],[S,S]<=[T,T]; hence J<=[T,T], or a<=T. Thus a is the least upper bound in P. Dually for the FW meet K=[c,d], R,S>=d, so [d,d] is a lower bound and [d,d]<=K forces d<=c. Therefore c=d is their greatest lower bound in P. Every pair in P has join and meet. Combined with Theorem4.1 this proves the literal equivalence.

Equivalently P is a monotone retract under the minimum-endpoint map; the proof explicitly verifies preservation of bounds instead of assuming it. A second approach using sign-vector closure is unnecessary and would require proving closure under coordinatewise min/max, which is generally false. This is the general standard interval-poset retraction argument specialized using the source's explicit definitions, so it is a complete routine bridge, zero credit, not an exclusion by familiarity alone.
