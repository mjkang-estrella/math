10. The Global Configuration and a Toy Problem




In light of Theorem 8.3, the  \lambda_{k} -minimizer  A\subset\mathcal{R}^{n}_{k}  is a disjoint union of connected components for  k>2 . We wish to study how translating the disconnected components of  A  affect  \lambda_{k} .




Let  u_{k}  be the eigenfunction corresponding to the minimizing  \lambda_{k} . Let  u=u_{k}|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{i}}  for any  1\leq i\leq k , choose  \|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}=1 . Let  A=\{u\neq 0\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n} , then we can write



(10.1)

 A=A_{1}\cup A_{2},\text{ with }A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\varnothing. 



Let  C={\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{A_{1}\cup A_{2}\} . We investigate how translating  A_{1}  and  A_{2}  affects  \lambda_{k} . We decompose the region of integration  {\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}  in the following way.



(10.2)

 \displaystyle\lambda_{k}(A)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}=\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{1}} 
 \displaystyle+\int_{A_{2}}\int_{A_{2}}+\int_{C}\int_{C} 


(10.3)


 \displaystyle+2\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{2}}+2\int_{A_{1}}\int_{C}+2\int_{A_{2}}\int_{C}. 



We first note that the integrals on  A_{1}\times A_{1} ,  A_{2}\times A_{2} , and  C\times C  (which is  0 ), are invariant under translation. To study the cross terms, we have



(10.4)

 \displaystyle\int_{A_{1}}\int_{C}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{A_{1}}\int_{C}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 


(10.5)


 \displaystyle=\int_{A_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{1}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 


(10.6)

 \displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}\int_{C}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{A_{2}}\int_{C}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 




 \displaystyle=\int_{A_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\int_{A_{2}}\int_{A_{1}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}-\int_{A_{2}}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 



where the only non-invariant pieces are the integrals on  A_{1}\times A_{2} . Combining these two integrals with the the integral on  A_{1}\times A_{2}  in (10.2) gives



(10.7)

 \displaystyle\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{(u(x)^{2}-2u(x)u(y)+u(y)^{2})}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 
 \displaystyle-\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}} 


(10.8)


 \displaystyle-\int_{A_{2}}\int_{A_{1}}\frac{u(y)^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}=\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{2}}\frac{-2u(x)u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}. 



This is the only non-invariant term under translation of  A_{1} ,  A_{2} . If  u  has the same sign on  A_{1},A_{2} ,  u(x)u(y)>0  then  \lambda_{k}  decreases by moving  A_{1},A_{2}  closer, and if  u  has different signs,  u(x)u(y)<0  then  \lambda_{k}  decreases by moving  A_{1},A_{2}  further.




If  A  is connected, we expect  A  to be a ball by symmetric decreasing rearrangements. If  A\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{n}  has more than one disjoint connected components, by Theorem 8.3,  u  has a sign on each connected component. We can choose  A_{1}  to be any union of the the connected components with the same sign, and  A_{2}=A\setminus A_{1} . Since  A  is a  \lambda_{k} -minimizer,  \lambda_{k}  cannot be reduced by translating any union of connected components. We believe this is false and one can always reduce the energy in this case. Thus, we expect that a  \lambda_{k} -minimizer is a union of balls in different copies of  {\mathbb{R}}^{n} .




Conjecture 10.9.



For  k\geq 2 , let  A  be a  \lambda_{k} -minimizer, and  u_{k}  the corresponding eigenfunction. If  \lambda_{k}  is simple, then  \{u_{k}|_{{\mathbb{R}}_{i}^{n}}\neq 0\}  is a ball for all  i .





We observe that since  u  is not sign-changing on each of the balls, then  u  must be the first eigenfunction on each ball. Since  \lambda_{k}  is a minimizing eigenvalue,  A  has to consists of  k  disjoint balls of the same size. In particular, it follows that




 \lambda_{1}(A)=\cdots=\lambda_{k}(A), 



which contradicts that  \lambda_{k}  is simple. Therefore, Conjecture 10.9 implies the following.




Corollary of Conjecture 10.10.



For  k\geq 2 , let  A  be a  \lambda_{k} -minimizer, then  \lambda_{k}(A)  is not simple.





Remark 10.11.



(1) 



Corollary of Conjecture 10.10 is the nonlocal analogy of the conjecture made in [20] which states that in the local case, such minimizing  \lambda_{k}  is not simple.

(2) 



Conjecture 10.9 is true if  A  consists of  m=2,3  disjoint connected sets. When there are two disjoint connected sets, if  u  has the same sign on them,  \lambda_{k}(A)  decreases by translating them close together, and if  u  has different signs on each of them,  \lambda_{k}(A)  decreases by translating them further away in the direction opposite of each other. If  m=3 , either they all have the same sign, in which case moving them closer together decreases  \lambda_{k}(A) , or exactly one of them has a different sign, in which case, moving this one away from the other two decreases the energy. We note that we can always do above translations smoothly when  m=2,3 , and these perturbations keep the order of the eigenvalues.

(3) 



If  A  consists of  m>3  disjoint connected sets, let  A^{+}=\{u>0\} ,  A^{-}=\{u<0\} . We note that we can put  A^{+} ,  A^{-}  in different copies of  {\mathbb{R}}^{n} , or equivalently, put  A^{+}  and  A^{-}  infinitely away from each other, which reduces the energy in (10.2). However, it is not clear that this reduced energy corresponds to the  k-th  eigenvalue.

(4) 



As  s\rightarrow 1 , our  (-\Delta)^{s}\varphi=-\Delta\varphi  for any  \varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}) . Thus, this nonlocal problem (1.1) offers a new perspective in the study of the minimizer of the  k-th  eigenvalue of  -\Delta .







In (10.7), if we replace  A_{1},A_{2}  by their enter of mass, we have the following discrete toy problem.




Let  x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}) ,  x_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n} , with signed mass  m(x_{i}) , such that  \sum_{i=1}^{d}|m(x_{i})|^{2}=1 . Let the energy of the system be



(10.12)

 E(m(x))=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{-2m(x_{i})m(x_{j})}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{n+2s}}. 



We say that the configuration  x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}  is stationary and stable if for every  v=(v_{1},...,v_{d}),v_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n} 



(10.13)

 \lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t}(E(x+tv)-E(x))=0,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}(E(x+tv)+E(x-tv)-2E(x))\geq 0. 



An associated conjecture to Conjecture 10.9 for the toy problem is the following.




Conjecture 10.14.



(10.12) doesn’t have any local minimum for  d\geq 2 .





Remark 10.15.



(1) 



The discrete toy problem is a simplified version of the minimization problem (1.1), and understanding this discrete problem would help us in understanding the global configuration of the  \lambda_{k} -minimizers.

(2) 



If we formally take  s=-1 , (10.12) coincide with the energy in classical electrostatics




 \sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\frac{-2m(x_{i})m(x_{j})}{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{n-2}}. 



Earnshaw’s theorem states that there is no stable and stationary configurations. However, the proof doesn’t translate to the case  s\in(0,1) .

(3) 



For any  x_{i},x_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n} , let



(10.16)

 \displaystyle d_{ij} 
 \displaystyle=\text{dist}\textit{Proj}_{\mathbf{v}}{x_{j}}, 



 \displaystyle r_{ij} 
 \displaystyle=\sqrt{|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{2}-d{ij}^{2}}. 






If  x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})  with mass  m(x_{1}),\cdots,m(x_{d})  achieves a local minimum of  E  in (10.12), then (10.13) gives the following explicit expression.



(10.17)


 \displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{m(x_{i})m(x_{j})(n+2s)d_{ij}}{\sqrt{r_{ij}^{2}+d_{ij}^{2}}^{n+2s+2}}=0, 




 \displaystyle\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{m(x_{i})m_{(}x_{j})[r_{ij}^{2}-d_{ij}^{2}(n+2s+1)]}{\sqrt{r_{ij}^{2}+d_{ij}^{2}}^{n+2s+4}}\geq 0.
