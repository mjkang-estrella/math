# Equitable partitions of finite rectangular grids are symmetry orbits

DRAFT · MathDB 343536 · 3 November 2026

## Abstract

For every pair of positive integers m and n, every equitable partition of the finite grid P_m □ P_n is an orbit partition for a subgroup of its graph automorphism group. We prove this by propagating colour-preserving symmetries from the boundary, using multiset cancellation and the first arrival of side walks at corners. We also prove the two lattice assertions in Neuberger, Sieben and Swift's Conjecture 5.6: the equitable-partition lattice is isomorphic to the full automorphism subgroup lattice precisely when there is a free vertex, and the only exceptions are the squares of sides 2 and 3. The supplied Lean endpoint is unconditional. This draft does not replace the separate root replay. The substantive contribution is modest and concerns the first assertion for m,n ≥ 2; paths are already covered by reported tree-network results.

## 1. Source, definitions and theorem

Write [m] = {1,…,m}. The path P_m has edges between consecutive integers; its box (Cartesian) product with P_n has vertex set V = [m] × [n], with adjacency |i−i′| + |j−j′| = 1. There are no periodic boundary edges. Let Aut(G) be the full group of adjacency-preserving bijections of V. Its natural action is evaluation, g·v = g(v). For H ≤ Aut(G), its orbit partition is {Hv : v ∈ V}.

Definition 5.1 of [1] calls a partition π equitable when |N(u) ∩ C| = |N(v) ∩ C| for every cell C and every pair u,v in one cell of π. Write EqPart(G) for these partitions. Order partitions by refinement: π ≤ σ means every π-cell lies inside a σ-cell. Order Sub(Aut(G)) by inclusion. A free vertex x has trivial stabilizer in the full group: gx = x implies g = 1.

Conjecture 5.6 [1], verbatim (with the box-product symbol typeset): “The equitable partitions of P_m □ P_n are the orbit partitions of natural actions of the subgroups of the automorphism group of the graph. The lattice of equitable partitions is isomorphic to the lattice of subgroups exactly when there is a vertex with trivial stabilizer subgroup. This happens unless 2 ≤ m = n ≤ 3.” The source reports computations through m = n = 20 and some larger graphs. These are evidence, not a proof.

Theorem 1. For all integers m,n ≥ 1: (i) π is equitable if and only if π is the orbit partition of some H ≤ Aut(G); (ii) EqPart(G) and Sub(Aut(G)) are isomorphic lattices if and only if G has a free vertex; (iii) G has a free vertex if and only if (m,n) is neither (2,2) nor (3,3). In the free cases the natural map Φ(H) = {Hv} itself is an order and lattice isomorphism. All lattice assertions use the refinement convention above.


## 2. Colours, determinism and positive layers

For a fixed equitable partition, let χ(v) be the cell of v. There is a multiset M(c) of neighbour colours for every cell c, satisfying M(χ(v)) = ⟦χ(w) : w ∈ N(v)⟧. Equality of these multisets is exactly Definition 5.1: the multiplicity of a colour C is |N(v) ∩ C|. Repeated colours are retained throughout. If g is an automorphism, χ∘g satisfies the same rule M, because g bijects N(v) onto N(gv).

Let R(i,j) = (m+1−i,j) and C(i,j) = (i,n+1−j). Let A₀ be the subgroup generated by R,C and, only in a square, T(i,j) = (j,i). These are explicit automorphisms. Define Hπ = {g ∈ A₀ : χ(gv) = χ(v) for every v}. It is a subgroup, and every Hπ-orbit lies in a π-cell. We prove the stronger assertion MainOrbit: any related u,v are joined by an element of Hπ.

Lemma 2 (saturation). Degree is constant on cells. If S is a union of cells, so is {v : N(v) ∩ S ≠ ∅}. Indeed degree is the sum of all neighbour-colour multiplicities. The count into S is the sum over its cells, so is constant on each cell; being positive is consequently cell-invariant.

Lemma 3 (row determinism). If χ and ψ obey the same rule M and agree on row 1, then χ = ψ. Suppose they agree through row i < m. At (i,j), every neighbour except (i+1,j) lies in rows at most i. Cancel the multiset of these known neighbour colours from M(χ(i,j)) = M(ψ(i,j)). The remaining singleton gives χ(i+1,j) = ψ(i+1,j). Induction completes the proof. Reflections and transposition give the same statement for any outer row or column. This argument also applies to one-vertex rows and to equality colours.

Lemma 4 (positive-layer propagation). Suppose λ : V → {1,2,…} is constant on cells and invariant under A₀. Suppose each w with λ(w) ≥ 2 has a neighbour a with λ(a) = λ(w)−1 for which w is the unique neighbour of a of layer at least λ(w). If related vertices of layer 1 are joined by Hπ, then MainOrbit holds.

Proof. Induct on λ(w), which is positive. Given w ≡ w′ of layer k ≥ 2, choose a as stated. Equitability at w,w′ gives a neighbour a′ of w′ with a ≡ a′. By induction some g ∈ Hπ sends a to a′. Transport the unique-neighbour property of a through g. The vertex gw is the unique neighbour of a′ of layer at least k, since g preserves λ. But w′ is such a neighbour too, so gw = w′. The converse inclusion of orbits in cells was already noted. Positivity is essential for the induction base.

Orbit partitions of any automorphism subgroup are equitable: if h maps u to v, then w ↦ hw bijects N(u) ∩ B with N(v) ∩ B for each orbit B. Thus only MainOrbit remains for the first assertion of Theorem 1.


## 3. The saturated border and corner timing

Assume m,n ≥ 3. Define ℓ(i,j) = min(i,j,m+1−i,n+1−j), and B = {ℓ = 1}. Corners have degree 2, other border vertices degree 3, and interior vertices degree 4. Thus B is a union of cells. Every edge changes ℓ by at most 1. The sublevel sets satisfy

{ℓ ≤ k+1} = {ℓ ≤ k} ∪ {v : N(v) ∩ {ℓ ≤ k} ≠ ∅},   k ≥ 1.

To check the nontrivial inclusion, if ℓ(v) = k+1 is attained by i, then (i−1,j) has layer k. The three other minimum coordinates give the other directions. Lemma 2 inductively makes every sublevel set saturated, hence makes ℓ constant on cells. Reflections and square transposition preserve ℓ.

The same coordinate observation proves the uniqueness hypothesis of Lemma 4. If ℓ(w) = k+1 is attained by i, choose a = (i−1,j). Its other neighbours, when present, are (i−2,j) and (i−1,j±1), all of layer at most k. The target w is its unique neighbour of layer at least k+1. The other attaining directions follow by reflection or exchange of coordinates. Ties between minimum coordinates cause no problem.

Saturation of B gives a restricted neighbour-colour rule M_B(c) = ⟦χ(w) : w ∈ N(v) ∩ B⟧ for v ∈ B of colour c. An internal vertex of any side has exactly two border neighbours, the preceding and following side vertices. Its third neighbour is interior, also when a dimension is 3. Corners have their two side neighbours in B. Thus the induced border graph is a cycle whose marked vertices are the four corners.

Lemma 5 (non-backtracking walks). Let x₀,…,x_d and y₀,…,y_d be side walks with non-corner internal vertices and x_{t+1} ≠ x_{t−1}, y_{t+1} ≠ y_{t−1} at every internal step. If χ(x₀) = χ(y₀) and χ(x₁) = χ(y₁), their colours agree at all indices. At an internal step, the two distinct border neighbours are precisely the preceding and following vertices. Hence

M_B(χ(x_t)) = ⟦χ(x_{t−1}), χ(x_{t+1})⟧.

Equality of the rules and cancellation of the preceding colour forces equality of the following colour. Induction proves the lemma, even if all these colours coincide.

Corollary 6 (corner timing). Two monotone side walks with matching first two colours reach their next corners at the same step. Compare their common prefix up to the earlier corner arrival using Lemma 5. If arrival times differed, equal colours would join a degree-2 corner and a degree-3 non-corner, contrary to Lemma 2. For walks starting at a corner, “next” means the opposite endpoint of that side. For walks starting internally, it means the first endpoint in the chosen direction. This convention includes arrival at step 1 on sides of length 3.


## 4. Border transitivity by explicit symmetries

We describe every symmetry used in the proof. From the upper left corner (1,1), RᵃCᵇ, with a,b ∈ {0,1}, maps the flag ((1,1),(1,2)) to the horizontal flag at the chosen corner, directed into its row. In a square, RᵃCᵇT maps it to the vertical flag directed into its column. These eight maps realize all directed corner flags in a square. In a nonsquare only a horizontal side has n vertices, so a flag with that side length is realized by one of the first four maps.

Proposition 7. Related border vertices u ≡ u′ are joined by some g ∈ Hπ.

First suppose u = (1,1). Its two neighbours are p = (1,2) and q = (2,1). At u′ the equal neighbour multiset permits a choice of its two neighbours p′,q′ with matching colours, including χ(p′) = χ(p). The walk along row 1 from u through p has its next corner at step n−1. The side walk from u′ through p′ has next corner at step s−1, where s is the number of vertices of that side. Corner timing gives s = n. The preceding explicit flag maps therefore supply g ∈ A₀ taking u to u′ and p to p′. The two walks match along the whole first row by Lemma 5. Thus χ∘g and χ agree there, and Lemma 3 implies χ∘g = χ everywhere. Hence g ∈ Hπ.

Next let u = (1,j) with 2 ≤ j ≤ n−1. Equal restricted border rules at u,u′ match its forward and backward neighbours to distinct border neighbours a′,b′ of u′, respectively. These are vertices, so their distinctness does not require distinct colours. The walks from u in the two directions first reach corners at distances n−j and j−1. Apply corner timing to the matched walks from u′. Their endpoint distances are the same. Consequently u′ lies on a side of n vertices, at distance j−1 from the corner c′ reached through b′. Choose the explicit flag map g taking (1,1) to c′ and (1,2) to the next vertex into that side. Then g(1,j) = u′ and g(1,j+1) = a′. Lemma 5 in both directions gives χ∘g = χ on row 1, and row determinism gives it everywhere.

For completeness, the coordinate normalizations above are justified as follows. If f : G → G′ is a graph isomorphism, transport colours by χ′(z) = χ(f⁻¹z). A witness g′ for χ′ returns to g = f⁻¹g′f. Direct substitution gives gu = u′ and χ(gv) = χ(v). Row and column reflections normalize A₀. Transposition between rectangular grids conjugates R to C and C to R; in a square it also conjugates T to T. Thus these transports preserve the required subgroup membership, rather than assuming it. They move any chosen side to the first row and any corner to (1,1).

Lemma 4 with λ = ℓ now proves MainOrbit for every m,n ≥ 3, including 3×3. Nothing in the border argument excludes a side of length 3.


## 5. Paths, ladders and exceptional square witnesses

Paths. For m = 1,n = 1 there is one vertex and the assertion is immediate. For m = 1,n ≥ 2 put λ(j) = min(j,n+1−j). Layer 1 is exactly the degree-1 endpoints. The sublevel recurrence of Section 3 and Lemma 2 make λ cell-invariant. Reversal preserves it. A target on the nearer left side has predecessor j−1, whose only possible other neighbour j−2 has smaller layer; reflect for the other side. If the endpoints are related, reversal preserves the endpoint colour. Neighbour-rule cancellation along the path then preserves every colour: at each step cancel the preceding colour to obtain the next. If the endpoints are unrelated, each endpoint cell is already a singleton. In either case layer-1 transitivity holds, and Lemma 4 proves MainOrbit. Transposition handles all n = 1 grids.

Ladders. Suppose m = 2,n ≥ 3 and λ(i,j) = min(j,n+1−j). Layer 1 consists of the four degree-2 corners; other vertices have degree 3. Again sublevel saturation gives cell invariance. If λ(i,j) = j ≥ 2, choose a = (i,j−1). The other neighbours (i,j−2) and (3−i,j−1), if present, have layer at most j−1. Reflection gives the corresponding right-side choice. Hence Lemma 4 applies after checking corners.

Given related corners u,u′, let u* denote the other vertex in u's end column. Of u's two neighbours, u* has degree 2 and the neighbour towards the next column has degree 3. Degree separation and equitability force u* ≡ u′*. Select R exactly when the row coordinates of u,u′ differ, and C exactly when their end columns differ. Their product g ∈ A₀ sends u to u′ and u* to u′*. Therefore χ∘g agrees with χ on the entire end column of u. Column determinism (Lemma 3 after transposition and, if needed, an end reflection) makes g colour-preserving globally. This proves the base case and then MainOrbit for all ladders. Transposition gives n = 2,m ≥ 3.

The 2×2 square. The graph is the four-cycle. Choose related u,u′ and one neighbour p of u. Equitability gives a neighbour p′ of u′ of matching colour. A map among RᵃCᵇ and RᵃCᵇT takes the directed edge (u,p) to (u′,p′). Follow the cycle in that direction. At each step cancel the known previous neighbour colour from the two-neighbour rule. All four colours agree under the map, which therefore lies in Hπ. This supplies MainOrbit without a computation.

The 3×3 square is already covered by Sections 3–4. In the supplied formal architecture it is also handled by a finite witness certificate: classify the four corners and four edge midpoints, keep the centre separate by degree, and check the eight displayed symmetries on every equitable candidate. These are alternative proofs for that branch. The ordinary argument here and the formal finite certificate both concern every equitable partition, rather than selected examples.

Combining these cases proves assertion (i) of Theorem 1 for all positive dimensions. The path proof is included for a self-contained endpoint, without a novelty claim for paths.


## 6. Free vertices in the full automorphism group

The distinction between A₀ and the full group matters for stabilizers. Here is a deduction from MainOrbit that avoids assuming the classical automorphism-group classification.

Lemma 8. If x has trivial A₀-stabilizer, it has trivial Aut(G)-stabilizer, and Aut(G) = A₀.

Let S = Stab_{Aut(G)}(x). Its orbit partition is equitable, hence by MainOrbit is the orbit partition of some K ≤ A₀. The S-orbit of x is {x}, so every element of K fixes x. Triviality of the A₀-stabilizer gives K = 1. Every S-orbit is therefore a singleton. The natural action of graph automorphisms is faithful, so S = 1. Apply MainOrbit again to the orbit partition of Aut(G). For g ∈ Aut(G), there is k ∈ A₀ with gx = kx. Thus k⁻¹g fixes x and is the identity, proving g = k ∈ A₀.

For m ≠ n with m,n ≥ 2, A₀ consists of 1,R,C,RC: R and C commute and are involutions. Their four images of (1,1) are distinct corners, so this vertex has trivial A₀-stabilizer. When exactly one dimension is 1, the reflection in that coordinate is the identity and the other reflection interchanges the two endpoints. An endpoint has trivial stabilizer. When m = n = 1, the full group is trivial and the unique vertex is free.

For m = n = k ≥ 4, the relations T² = R² = C² = 1, RC = CR, and TRT = C put every element of A₀ into the form RᵃCᵇ or RᵃCᵇT. The images of x = (1,2) under these eight maps are

(1,2), (k,2), (1,k−1), (k,k−1),
(2,1), (k−1,1), (2,k), (k−1,k).

They are pairwise distinct for k ≥ 4. Hence x has trivial A₀-stabilizer. Lemma 8 gives a free vertex in the full group in every claimed positive case.

At k = 2 every diagonal vertex is fixed by T and each off-diagonal vertex by the anti-diagonal reflection (i,j) ↦ (3−j,3−i). Both are nonidentity automorphisms. At k = 3, the corners (1,1),(3,3) and the centre are fixed by T; the other corners (1,3),(3,1) are fixed by (i,j) ↦ (4−j,4−i). The two middle-row edge vertices are fixed by R, and the two middle-column edge vertices by C. Thus every vertex in either exceptional square has a nontrivial full-group stabilizer. This proves assertion (iii).

The supplied formal proof proves the positive classification independently of MainOrbit. Its oriented-flag rigidity follows a side because the inward neighbour has degree 4, the next side neighbour has degree at most 3, and injectivity excludes the preceding vertex. Unequal side lengths contradict corner timing. A fixed nonsquare corner cannot interchange directions; a fixed near-corner in a square of side at least 4 fixes its unique adjacent corner. Row determinism then fixes the whole grid. The path and ladder cases use their endpoint and degree-separated end-column versions. These differences in dependency order do not change the theorem being proved.


## 7. Lattices and the two cardinality obstructions

The equitable partitions form a complete lattice in refinement order. Here is an argument valid for any finite graph. Given equitable setoids r in a family S, let s be their join in the lattice of all setoids. For each r ≤ s, projecting the r-neighbour-colour multiset onto s-classes makes the s-neighbour multiset equal at r-related vertices. The kernel t of the map sending a vertex to its s-neighbour multiset therefore contains every r. The universal property of the join gives s ≤ t, which is exactly equitability of s. This includes the empty join, the discrete relation. Meets within EqPart are joins of equitable common refinements. One must not identify them with ordinary setoid intersections without proof. The subgroup lattice has intersections as meets and generated unions as joins.

By assertion (i), Φ : Sub(Aut(G)) → EqPart(G) is surjective. It is monotone since H ≤ K implies Hv ⊆ Kv. If x is free and Φ(H) ≤ Φ(K), then for every h ∈ H some k ∈ K satisfies hx = kx. The element k⁻¹h fixes x, hence h = k ∈ K. Thus Φ reflects order and is an order isomorphism. Order isomorphisms preserve all existing suprema and infima. This proves the free direction of assertion (ii), including its natural-map strengthening.

For the converse we give finite cardinality bounds. At each k = 2,3 the eight displayed symmetries form a dihedral subgroup D of the full group. Write r(i,j) = (j,k+1−i) and s = T; then r⁴ = s² = 1 and srs = r⁻¹. Ten distinct subgroups are {1}, ⟨r²⟩, the four ⟨rᵃs⟩ (0 ≤ a < 4), ⟨r⟩, ⟨r²,s⟩, ⟨r²,rs⟩, and D. Distinctness is checked by their elements in these normal forms. Thus the full subgroup lattice has at least ten elements.

An economical upper bound on partitions uses the proved MainOrbit. D is exactly A₀ here and has exactly those ten subgroups: a subgroup's intersection with ⟨r⟩ is 1, ⟨r²⟩ or ⟨r⟩; if it contains a reflection, all its reflections constitute one coset of that intersection. At 2×2, ⟨r⟩, ⟨R,C⟩ and D have the same one-cell orbit partition, saving two images; moreover ⟨r²⟩ and ⟨r²,T⟩ have the same opposite-corner-pair partition, saving another. Hence |EqPart| ≤ 7. At 3×3, ⟨r⟩ and D share the centre/corners/edge-midpoints partition, so |EqPart| ≤ 9. Since 7,9 < 10, neither exceptional lattice is even equinumerous with the subgroup lattice. This proves (ii).
