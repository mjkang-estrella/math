                                                                                          1. Introduction

                                           Definition 1.1. A family of graphs F , whose members are subgraphs of the complete graph Kn , is
                                           said to be H-intersecting if for any two graphs F, F ′ ∈ F, their edge intersection F ∩ F ′ contains
                                           a subgraph isomorphic to H.

                                           One generalization of a classic result in extremal combinatorics, the Erdős-Ko-Rado Theorem, is
                                           to determine max |F |, the maximum possible size of an H-intersecting family, for different choices
                                           of H. A trivial construction for an H-intersecting family involves fixing a specific copy of H within
                                                                n                                                                    n
                                           Kn and taking all 2( 2 )−e(H) supergraphs. This establishes a baseline lower bound of 2( 2 )−e(H) .
                                           For some graphs, this bound is sharp. A notable example is H = K3 , for which a conjecture by
                                           Simonovits and Sós stating this optimality was proven by Ellis, Filmus, and Friedgut [5]. However,
                                           this trivial bound is not always optimal. For the path on four vertices, P4 , the trivial bound is

                         2. A Construction via Multipartite Graphs

We present a construction that improves upon the trivial bound for intersecting families where H is
a complete multipartite graph. An alternative construction for non-trivial H-intersecting families,
where H = Ks1 ,...,sk−1 ,t , was recently proved in [2] by Balogh and Linz. We do a detailed comparison
between the two results in Section 3.
Here is our result:
Theorem 2.1. Let k ≥ 2 be an integer, and let s1 , . . . , sk−1 be positive integers. Consider an
integer t ≥ 2s1 +···+sk−1 , and let H = Ks1 ,...,sk−1 ,t be a complete k-partite graph with parts of sizes
s1 , . . . , sk−1 , and t. Denote by N = |E(H)| the number of edges in H. Then, for any integer
n ≥ s1 + · · · + sk−1 + t + 2, there exists an H-intersecting family of subgraphs with size at least
                              (t + 2)(2s1 +···+sk−1 − 1) + 1 (n)    1 (n)

Proof. Let the host graph be G = Ks1 ,...,sk−1 ,t+2 . The vertex set of G is partitioned into sets
V1 , . . . , Vk−1 , W with |Vi | = si and |W | = t + 2. The graph H is an induced subgraph of G.
Consider the t + 2 subgraphs of G of the form Gw = G[V (G) \ {w}] for each w ∈ W . Each
Gw is isomorphic to Ks1 ,...,sk−1 ,t+1 . The intersection of any two distinct subgraphs, Gw ∩ Gw′ , is
isomorphic to Ks1 ,...,sk−1 ,t , which is precisely H.
We construct an H-intersecting family G of subgraphs of G as follows. Let m = s1 + · · · + sk−1 .
For each of the t + 2 subgraphs Gw , we take all supergraphs of Gw within G except for G itself.
This gives 2e(G)−e(Gw ) − 1 = 2m − 1 distinct subgraphs for each w. Adding the complete graph G
to this collection gives a family of size |G| = (t + 2)(2m − 1) + 1. Since the intersection of any two
chosen subgraphs must contain some Gw ∩ Gw′ or Gw itself, this family G is H-intersecting.
To obtain the family F on Kn , we take all supergraphs of the members of G. The measure of this
family is |G| · 2−e(G) . We must show this improves the trivial bound:
                             |G| · 2−e(G) > 2−e(H) ⇐⇒ |G| > 2e(G)−e(H)
The difference in edges is e(G) − e(H) = m(t + 2) − mt = 2m. The inequality to verify is
(t + 2)(2m − 1) + 1 > 22m . Given the condition t ≥ 2m , we have:
         (t + 2)(2m − 1) + 1 ≥ (2m + 2)(2m − 1) + 1 = (22m + 2m − 2) + 1 = 22m + 2m − 1
Since m ≥ 1, this is strictly greater than 22m , which completes the proof.                             □

                            5. On the Optimality of the P4 Bound

While the Christofides construction improved the known bound for P4 -intersecting families from
                   17
the trivial 81 to 128 , its optimality remains an open question. It is believed among some experts in
the field that this bound is indeed sharp, though this conjecture does not appear to be formally
stated in the literature. We state it here.
                                                                                         n
Conjecture 5.1. For n ≥ 6, the maximum size of a P -intersecting family is 17 · 2( 2 ) . Further-

more, any extremal family is isomorphic to one generated by the Christofides construction.

This conjecture is particularly significant due to its connection to a more general conjecture pro-
posed by Alon and Spencer [1]. They conjectured that for any graph H that is not a star-forest (a
graph where each connected component is a star), the trivial H-intersecting family is not extremal.
Conjecture 5.2 (Alon-Spencer [1]). There exists some constant ϵ > 0 such that if H is a fixed
graph that is not a star-forest and F is an H-intersecting family on Kt , then

A proof of Conjecture 5.1 would completely resolve this conjecture. The reasoning is that any
graph H that is not a star-forest must contain K3 or P4 as a subgraph.
     • If H contains K3 , any H-intersecting family F is also a K3 -intersecting family. Thus, |F | is
                                                                      n
       bounded by the known maximum size for K3 , which is 81 · 2( 2 ) , as proved by Ellis, Filmus,

     • If H is triangle-free but contains P4 , any H-intersecting family F is also a P4 -intersecting

As partial evidence supporting Conjecture 5.1, we performed a computational search for denser con-
structions on small host graphs. This search yielded no constructions outperforming the Christofides
bound. We summarize our findings as follows.

Theorem 5.3. The Christofides density of 128  is optimal for all P4 -intersecting families generated
from a connected host graph G on 6 vertices with 7 or 8 edges.

Proof. Our method involved an exhaustive search using Python (our implementation is available
in Appendix A). For a given host graph G, we generated its subgraphs with at least 3 edges
(the number of edges in P4 ) and constructed an auxiliary graph whose vertices represented these
subgraphs. An edge was placed between two vertices in the auxiliary graph if their corresponding
subgraphs had a P4 in their intersection. The size of the largest P4 -intersecting family supported
by G is then the size of the maximum clique in this auxiliary graph, a problem known to be
NP-complete [6].                                                                                  □

While our search was limited to small graphs, its results support the conjecture that the Christofides
density is optimal.
