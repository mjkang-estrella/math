                                                                                         Abstract

                                                     Determining the complexity of colouring (4K1 , C4 )-free graph is a long open
                                                 problem. Recently Penev showed that there is a polynomial-time algorithm to
                                                 colour a (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-free graph. In this paper, we will prove that if G is a
                                                 (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graph that contains a C6 , then G has bounded clique-width.
                                                 To this purpose, we use a new method to bound the clique-width, that is of
                                                 independent interest. As a consequence, there is a polynomial-time algorithm
                                                 to colour (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs.

                                         Keywords: clique-width, graph algorithm, graph colouring


                                         1       Introduction
                                         Let L be a list (class) of graphs. A graph G is L-free if G does not contain an
                                         induced subgraph isomorphic to a graph in L. There has been much interest in
                                         finding polynomial-time algorithms to colour L-free graphs for several well-defined
                                         classes L. In this context, perhaps one of the most well-known open problems is to
                                         colour “even hole-free” graphs in polynomial time (definitions will be given later).
                                         Recently, many researchers studied graph classes whose forbidden list L contains
                                         graphs with four vertices. Figure 1 shows all 11 four-vertex graphs. Recent papers
                                         of Lozin and Malyshev [12], and of Fraser, Hamel, Hoàng, Holmes and LaMantia [9]
                                         discuss the state of the art on this problem, identifying three outstanding classes:
                                         L = (4K1 , claw), L = (4K1 , claw, co-diamond), and L = (4K1 , C4 ). For now, the
                                         solutions to these three problems seem elusive. A natural way to make progress is to
                                         enlarge the forbidden list L. Then the question is, which L is an interesting list to be
                                         considered? In this paper, we study the class L = {4K1 , C4 }. Before introducing our
                                         problem, we will need to introduce a few definitions.

    By Ck (respectively, Pk ), we denote the chordless cycle (respectively, path) on k
vertices. A hole is a Ck with k ≥ 4. A hole is even if it has an even number of vertices.
The problem of colouring even-hole-free graphs has been extensively studied. The
combined results of Addario-Berry, Chudnovsky, Havet, Reed, and Seymour [2] and
Chudnovky and Seymour [5] shows that for an even-hole-free graph G, the chromatic
number of G is at most two times its clique number minus one1 (the clique number
of a graph is the number of vertices in a largest clique). It is currently not known
whether even-hole-free graphs can be coloured in polynomial time. However, there
are polynomial-time algorithms for colouring some subclasses of even hole-free graphs
[10, 3, 4, 13]. See [18] for a survey on even hole-free graphs. Until now, we have
discussed four colouring problems which are summarized as follows.

Problem 1.1 What is the complexity of colouring (4K1 , claw)-free graphs?

Problem 1.2 What is the complexity of colouring (4K1 , claw, co-diamond)-free
graphs?

Problem 1.3 What is the complexity of colouring (4K1 , C4 )-free graphs?

Problem 1.4 What is the complexity of colouring even-hole-free graphs?

   Problems 1.1 and 1.2 have been studied by many researchers (see [1, 6, 7, 10, 17]).
This paper is concerned with Problem 1.3.

    In [8], Foley et al. proposed the intersection of Problems 1.3 and 1.4. They asked,
what is the complexity of colouring (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-free graphs. Penev ([14, 15]) an-
swered this question by providing a polynomial time algorithm to colour (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-
free graphs.

Theorem 1.1 ([14]) There is a polynomial-time algorithm to colour (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-
free graphs.

   Inspired by this result, we asked whether the C6 in the list L may be replaced by
another graph. The Ck is usually studied in conjunction with Pk . Thus, we chose P6
(the chordless path on six vertices). That is, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2 There is a polynomial-time algorithm to color (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free
graphs.

   Our approach to prove Theorem 1.2 is by bounding the clique-width of
(4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs that contain a C6 . We use a new method for bounding
the clique-width that is of independent interest.


Outline of the paper
In Section 2, we will provide the necessary definitions and discuss the context of
our problem (in particular we explain why to prove Theorem 1.2 it is enough to
bound the clique-width of (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs that contain a C6 and explain
our new upper bound on the clique-width). In Section 3, we state and prove several
observations about how a C6 sees the rest of the graph in some (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free
graph. In Section 4, we prove that a (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graph that contains a C6 has
bounded clique-width. Finally, in Section 5, we discuss further different methods to
bound the clique-width and propose some related open problems.


2    Background and method
If a vertex a is adjacent to a vertex b in some graph G, then we write a ∼ b. By N (x),
we denote the set vertices adjacent to x and for any set X, NX (v) stands for the set
N (v) ∩ X. The colouring problem is the problem of assigning a minimum number of
colours to the vertices of an input graph so that two vertices receive different colours
if they are adjacent. It is well known that the colouring problem for a general graph
is NP-hard.
    Consider the following operations to build a labelled graph, starting from an empty
graph.


                                           3
   • Create a vertex u with label i.

   • Take the disjoint union of two previously constructed labelled graphs.

   • Join by an edge all vertices with label i to all vertices with label j for i ̸= j.

   • Relabel all vertices with label i by label j.

   The clique-width of an unlabelled graph G, is the minimum number of labels
needed to build the graph with the above four operations. For instance, the reader
may verify that for any n ≥ 2, the clique on n vertices has clique-width 2, and for
any n ≥ 3, Pn has clique-width 3. Rao [16] proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1 ([16]) The colouring problem is polynomial-time solvable for graphs
with bounded clique-width.

   A vertex in a graph is simplicial if its neighbourhood is a clique. If x is a simplicial
vertex, then χ(G) = max(χ(G − x), |N (x)| + 1); thus if we know χ(G − x), then we
know χ(G). Penev proved the following.

Theorem 2.2 ([14]) The class of (4K1 , C4 , C6 , C7 )-free graphs that do not contain
a simplicial vertex has bounded clique-width.

   Foley, Fraser, Hoàng, Holmes, and LaMantia proved the following.

Theorem 2.3 ([8]) The class of (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-free graphs that contains a C7 has
bounded clique-width.

   Theorems 2.3, 2.2 and 2.1 imply that the colouring problem for (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-free
graphs can be solved in polynomial time as already stated in Theorem 1.1. We will
prove:

Theorem 2.4 The class of (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs that contains a C6 has bounded
clique-width (at most 27).

    It is easily seen that Theorems 1.1, 2.4 and 2.1 imply Theorem 1.2. In Theorem 2.4,
it is essential to assume that G contains a C6 because there exist (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free
graphs with arbitrarily large clique-width (we postpone their description to the end
of the section because we need some terminology to describe them).
    Let us now explain our tool to bound the clique-width.




                                            4
Bounding the clique-width
Given disjoint sets X, Y of vertices in some graph G, we say that X is complete (resp.
anticomplete) to Y if there are all possible edges (resp. no edge) between vertices in
X and vertices in Y ; we denote this relationship by X 1 Y (resp. X 0 Y ). Also,
we say that X is uniform to Y if X 1 Y or X 0 Y . We say that X is monotone
to Y if for all u, v ∈ X, either NY (u) ⊆ NY (v) or NY (v) ⊆ NY (u). Observe that the
empty set is complete, anticomplete, uniform and monotone to any set of vertices of
any graph. The following well-known observation is easy and we omit its proof.

Theorem 2.5 For any graph G and every disjoint sets X, Y ⊂ V (G), the following
are equivalent.

   • X is monotone to Y .

   • Y is monotone to X.

   • There does not exist x, x′ ∈ X and y, y ′ ∈ Y such that x ∼ y, x′ ∼ y ′ , x ̸∼ y ′
     and x′ ̸∼ y.

   • The vertices of X (resp. Y ) are linearly ordered by the inclusion of their neigh-
     bourhood in Y (resp. X).

  In particular, if X and Y are two disjoint cliques in a C4 -free graph, then X is
monotone to Y .

    Let G be a graph and P = (A1 , . . . , Ak ) be a partition of V (G). We say that P
is monotone if for all i ∈ {1, . . . , k}, Ai is monotone to V (G) \ Ai . In a monotone
partition, every set Ai is linearly ordered with respect to the inclusion of neighbour-
hoods outside of Ai (see Theorem 2.5). So, in each non-empty set Ai , there exists at
least one maximal (resp. minimal ) vertex, that is a vertex v such that NV (G)\Ai (v)
is inclusion-wise maximal (resp. minimal). A vertex in Ai that is either maximal or
minimal is extreme. We say that a monotone partition P has the extreme vertex prop-
erty if there exists i ∈ {1, . . . , k} such that some extreme vertex v of Ai is uniform
to all sets Aj for all j ∈ {1, . . . , k} \ {i}. We say that P has the hereditary extreme
vertex property (hev-property for short) if for every set X ⊆ V (G), the partition P[X]
has the extreme vertex property in G[X] (where P[X] = (A1 ∩ X, . . . , Ak ∩ X) and
G[X] is the subgraph of G induced by X).

Theorem 2.6 Let G be a graph and P = (A1 , . . . , Ak ) be a monotone partition of
V (G) into cliques A1 , . . . , Ak . If P has the hev-property, then G has clique-width at
most k + 1.

that is uniform to all Aj with j ̸= i. By the heredity property, P[V (G) \ {v}] has the
hev-property for G − v. So, by the induction hypothesis G − v can be constructed as
stated above. Now give the label L to v (L is a special label used only for v).

    The following is a convenient way to prove the hev-property.

Lemma 2.7 Let G be a graph and P = (A1 , . . . , Ak ) be a monotone partition of
V (G) such that A1 is uniform to all sets Aj (j ∈ {2, . . . , k}) except possibly one. If
(A2 , . . . , Ak ) has the hev-property (for G[A2 ∪ · · · ∪ Ak ]), then P has the hev-property
(for G).

Proof. If k = 1, then the conclusion is clear, so suppose k ≥ 2. Let us prove that
some extreme vertex of some set Ai (i ∈ {1, . . . , k}) is uniform to all sets Aj , j ̸= i.
    From the assumption, there exists some set Ai (with i ∈ {2, . . . , k)) such that
some extreme vertex v in Ai is uniform to all sets Aj (j ≥ 2, j ̸= i). If v is uniform
to A1 in G, we are done, so we may assume it is not. So, v has neighbours and
non-neighbours in A1 . By the assumption, A1 is uniform to all sets Aj , j ≥ 2, j ̸= i.
If v is a maximal vertex of Ai , then a minimal vertex of A1 must be anticomplete
to Ai , because otherwise, by the maximality of v, v should be complete to A1 , a
contradiction. If v is a minimal vertex of Ai , then a symmetric argument shows that
a maximal vertex of A1 is complete to Ai . So we have found in A1 an extreme vertex
w that is uniform to all sets Ai (i ∈ {2, . . . , k}).
    The same proof can be done in G[X] with the partition P[X] for any set X ⊆
V (G), so G has the hev-property.                                                         2

Construction of (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs of large clique-width
As announced above, let us describe (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs of arbitrarily large
clique-width. We rely on a construction of the first and last author of the present
paper, see [11]. They construct graphs of unbounded clique-width that admit a
monotone partition into three cliques, called 3-rings. This is enough, because it is
easy to check that none of 4K1 , C4 and P6 admits a monotone partition into three
cliques. Hence, the 3-rings are (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free.

5       Conclusion
In this paper, we consider the complexity of colouring (4K1 , C4 )-free graphs. Lozin
and Malyshev [12] conjectured that the problem can be solved in polynomial time.
Even though the problem is still open, we show there is a polynomial-time algorithm
for colouring (4K1 , C4 , P6 )-free graphs. We note that the following classes of graphs
also admit polynomial-time colouring algorithms: (4K1 , C4 , C5 )-free graphs as given
in [10], (4K1 , C4 , C6 )-free graphs as given in [14].
    Our method to bound the clique-width generalizes some previous works. The box
graph b(G; P) of the graph G with respect to a partition P = (A1 , . . . , Ak ) is the
graph whose vertices are the sets Ai , and two vertices Ai , Aj of b(G; P) are adjacent
if and only if Ai is not uniform to Aj .
    The partition P is k-near-uniform if for all i ∈ {1, . . . , k}, Ai is a clique2 , and
b(G; P) is a graph with maximum degree one (so a matching plus some isolated
vertices). The key to prove Theorem 2.3 in [9] is the following:

Theorem 5.1 ([9]) Let G be a graph admitting a k-near-uniform partition P =
(A1 , . . . , Ak ) such that if Ai is not uniform to Aj , then G[Ai ∪ Aj ] is C4 -free. Then,
the clique-width of G is at most 2k.

    We observe that by applying Lemma 2.8, a k-near-uniform partition as in the
theorem is in fact monotone (because any set of the partition is uniform to all other
sets except possibly one, and in this case, we use the fact pointed out in Theorem 2.5:
two cliques in a C4 -free graph are monotone to each other). Then, by applying
Lemma 2.7 repeatedly, we can see that such a partition has the hev-property. Hence,
Theorem 2.6 shows that the bound 2k in Theorem 5.1 can be improved to k + 1.
    Also, our approach allows some generalizations. For instance, to prove the hev-
property, it is enough that b(G; P) is a forest (instead of a matching):

Theorem 5.2 Let G be a graph and P = (A1 , . . . , Ak ) be a monotone partition of
V (G). If b(G; P) is a forest, then P has the hev-property (in particular, G has clique-
width at most k + 1 whenever the sets Ai are all cliques).
