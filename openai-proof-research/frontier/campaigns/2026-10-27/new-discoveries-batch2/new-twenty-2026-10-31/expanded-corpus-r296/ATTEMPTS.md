## 350412: sum index versus difference index

Exact endpoint: for every finite simple graph G, minimize the number of integer edge sums and absolute integer edge differences over injective integer vertex labels independently; prove dif(G)<=sum(G). Empty edge sets cause no problem (both indices zero). Definitions were read in Haslegrave v3 and the journal body. The original equality and its later lower inequality are not the assigned upper inequality. r43's public odd-cycle chain counterexamples therefore do not exclude this target. v1 and v2 retain Conjecture5.3; v3 does too; the journal renumbers it Conjecture4.

Route A: transfer an optimal sum labeling. Shift an injective sum-optimal labeling f so all its values are positive. For a bipartition A,B define h(v)=f(v) on A and h(v)=-f(v) on B. Then h is injective, including across the two parts, and every absolute edge difference is exactly f(u)+f(v). This proves the bipartite case with the exact same number of labels. It is a complete routine bridge, not a discovery claim.

The obstacle for general graphs is precise: the sign transfer needs opposite signs on both ends of every edge, which is impossible around an odd cycle. Assigning signs along a spanning tree leaves same-sign chord edges with differences not controlled by the sum alphabet. Merely bounding the number of chord edges gives an additive error, which does not prove the literal inequality.

Route B: use the bipartite double cover and descend. Its vertices (v,+),(v,-) admit labels +f(v),-f(v); every lifted edge has a difference from the original sum alphabet. Thus the double cover has a representation with <=sum(G) difference values. However identifying the two lifts of v does not preserve differences. A section choosing one lift at each vertex puts every edge across signs exactly when G is bipartite. Thus double-cover descent has recreated the odd-cycle obstruction instead of solving it. The missing lemma is a relabeling descent theorem with no increase in difference count, and this is not known from the source.

Route C: a nonlinear pointwise relabeling avoids the need to pick signs, but fails to preserve equal sums. Squaring gives h(u)-h(v)=(f(u)+f(v))(f(u)-f(v)). On three disjoint edges whose positive endpoint labels are (1,6),(2,5),(3,4), all sums are7 but the squared differences are35,21,7. This disproves that proposed transfer mechanism, not the graph conjecture: a matching itself has difference index1. Similarly taking absolute values about a common origin collapses some distinct vertices and only controls opposite-side edges. No injectivity-preserving universal repair was found.

Counterexample direction: source Section3 supplies a degree-sequence lower bound dif(G)>=max_j(delta_{2j}-j+1) and sum(G)>=max_j(delta_j+delta_{j+1}-j). The near-complete subdivided-clique examples increase dif beyond ceil(sum/2), not beyond sum. Their dense branch vertices also force large sum index, so transplanting them does not produce the needed gap. A real counterexample would need an explicit sum labeling using s labels and a proof that every injective labeling has at least s+1 absolute differences. No such graph was produced. The next substantive lemma is the general transfer/descent lemma or a graph-specific unavoidable-differences bound meeting that strict gap; this is a full-result obstruction, not an execution ticket.

Literature limit: current journal body was accessible and read for definitions, Section3 proofs and the Section5 endpoint; no correction was identified in this read. All three arXiv version target passages were read. The Harrington et al. 2023 companion body and later citing papers were not read here. This remains HOLD, with no novelty clearance.

## 360917: tree recognition from partial decks

Exact endpoint: for each n>=44, any submultiset of floor(n/2)+2 vertex cards determines treehood. Equivalent obstruction: a tree T and a non-tree H on the same n vertices have a common-deck multiset of at least that size. Multiplicities must be counted as the sum over isomorphism types of min(m_T(C),m_H(C)); distinct card types alone are insufficient.

Route A: reduce a hypothetical obstruction structurally before trying to recover edges. Source v2 Section3 uses Myrvold's connectivity result to reduce H to connected. Lemma8 reduces further to unicyclic H: a graph with two cycles has at most two forest cards. Consequently every common card deletes a vertex of the unique cycle, whose length L>=floor(n/2)+2. Comparing edge counts in a common card T-v=H-w gives deg_H(w)=deg_T(v)+1. If every such v had degree>=2, at least floor(n/2)+2 vertices of H would have degree>=3, contradicting its degree sum2n. Thus at least one common card deletes a leaf of T and a cycle vertex of degree2 in H.

This gives actual information beyond an edge-count guess, but does not determine the whole deck. A leaf-deletion common card is connected and allows one to regard T as reattaching one leaf to a cycle-cut tree. The naive next step, recovering H's cycle endpoints from that path, fails because branches can extend a longest path and the card is unlabeled. A choice of cut and an isomorphism can change which visible path is the original cycle minus a vertex. Neither a single common leaf card nor a count of leaves supplies the all-isomorphism multiplicity bound.

Route B: follow the source's branch and longest-path mechanism into its exact quantitative failure. Read v2 Lemmas11,12, the latter part of14, Lemma15, Corollaries16-18 and the actual proof of Theorem4. The source derives L<=n/2+19.5, at most18 cycle vertices failing to give common cards, branch height<=14, and off-main-component paths of size<=27. It marks green cycle vertices maximizing the total size K of attached branches. At most3 green vertices yield common cards, hence there are at most21 green vertices altogether. For n>=5000, pigeonhole gives a gap with at least118 consecutive nongreen vertices. Removing29 vertices at each end leaves60 positions, at least42 common. From cards cut there, green vertices are intrinsically recognizable using two path components of size>=29 and K other vertices. The two distances to the green extremes in T force a window of width10. Two common cuts at distances in[30,39] and[50,59] contradict that window. This is a genuine proof for its stated range.

The failed extrapolation is exactly the long-gap step. For n>=44 the inequalities L>=floor(n/2)+2 and number of green vertices<=21 do not force any gap of length118. Decreasing29 also invalidates the source's recognizability criterion because branch paths can have size27. It is therefore incorrect to replace5000 by44, cite the abstract without the qualifier, or claim the finite leftover is a solved implementation detail.

A viable next lemma would bound the common-card multiplicity by floor(n/2)+1 for every tree/unicyclic pair in44<=n<5000, using short-gap branch structure; alternatively a new recognizable statistic with bounds depending on each actual branch rather than27 could remove the long-gap loss uniformly. No such lemma was proved. Enumerating these orders is prohibited and would be enormous; none was attempted. Source v1 also explicitly has the5000 threshold. Historical r154 ID360916 asks the stronger2(n+1)/5 bound/classification; that unproved stronger statement is not silently assumed here.

## 371728: logarithmic clique-width of cyclic-box graphs

Read the primary definitions and Section2 proof tools, Section5 Theorem5.2 and Conjecture5.2. A monotone partition means the outside neighborhoods of all vertices of EACH clique are totally ordered by inclusion globally. It is stronger than separate pairwise chain conditions. The box graph joins exactly pairs of partition classes whose bipartite adjacency is nonuniform; a nonedge can mean complete or anticomplete, not necessarily anticomplete. The endpoint requires a function g of k alone with cwd(G)<=g(k)log|V(G)| for all such graphs.

Route A: extend hereditary extreme-vertex elimination. The source's Lemma2.7 extends a hereditary extreme-vertex partition when a new class has at most one nonuniform neighbor, and Theorem2.6 converts that property to k+1 labels by induction. In a forest box graph leaf deletion supplies exactly this condition. A chordless cycle has no leaf. Deleting one entire clique makes a path but reattaching it can require many distinct neighborhoods into the constructed graph. Giving the entire deleted clique one label loses those distinctions. Giving it one label per vertex costs its size, and yields no logarithmic bound. The source's unbounded-width rings explicitly prevent simply asserting the same constant-width conclusion.

Route B: balanced threshold recursion. Choose a median vertex x of a clique A_i in its global outside-neighborhood order. Split A_i into lower and upper intervals around x. Each other class splits into neighbors/nonneighbors of x. Since vertices below x have outside neighborhoods contained in N(x), lower vertices are anticomplete to outside nonneighbors of x; upper vertices are complete to outside neighbors of x. These are exact uniform cross-pairs useful for a recursive construction, not yet a complete decomposition.

The remaining cross-pairs between the two cyclic neighbor classes can stay nonuniform. Propagating compatible thresholds through consecutive classes may avoid that locally, but on returning around the cycle the propagated threshold need not match the initial split. Also median balance in one clique does not guarantee a balanced total vertex split. Finally a low-rank cut by itself would give exponential rather than logarithmic clique-width bounds via standard rank-width comparison; that shortcut does not prove the desired endpoint.

The missing lemma must supply balanced splits (say each child has at most a fixed fraction of n), while permitting a label construction in which only C(k) additional persistent labels are reserved per recursion level. Uniform cross-pairs must be certified, and inherited boundary classes must remain compatible with existing labels rather than requiring their exponential refinement. Such a lemma would give at most base(k)+C(k)ceil(log n) labels by recursive construction; no such label-compatible cyclic threshold lemma was established. The source's forest mechanism and the median deductions above do not imply it. This is an ordinary mathematical HOLD, not a generic solver plan and not a candidate for Pi. The cited ring-construction paper, other versions, journal and citing bodies remain unread qualification gaps.
