9:                                                                                              Abstract
10:                                                       We conjecture a new lower bound on the algebraic connectivity of a graph that involves
11:                                                   the number of vertices of high eccentricity in a graph. We prove that this lower bound implies
12:                                                   a strengthening of the Laplacian Spread Conjecture. We discuss further conjectures, also
13:                                                   strengthening the Laplacian Spread Conjecture, that include a conjecture for simple graphs
14:                                                   and a conjecture for weighted graphs.

17:                                            1      Introduction
18:                                            The algebraic connectivity of a graph is one of the most well-studied parameters in spectral graph
19:                                            theory. It is defined as the second smallest eigenvalue of the combinatorial Laplacian matrix of a
20:                                            graph. That is, if the Laplacian matrix L of a graph G has eigenvalues 0 = λ1 (G) ≤ λ2 (G) ≤ · · · ≤
21:                                            λn (G), the algebraic connectivity is λ2 (G). It is well known that this eigenvalue gives a measure
22:                                            for how well-connected a graph is. Famously, λ2 (G) > 0 if and only if G is connected. Good
23:                                            lower bounds on λ2 (G) have been difficult to obtain. For example in Tables 4.1 and 4.2 of [3] we
24:                                            find twelve upper bounds for λ2 (G) but only four lower bounds. Moreover, these lower bounds are
25:                                            typically far from sharp.
26:                                                For a graph G on n vertices with Laplacian eigenvalues 0 = λ1 (G) ≤ λ2 (G) ≤ · · · ≤ λn (G), the
27:                                            Laplacian Spread Conjecture states that
28:                                                                                      λn (G) − λ2 (G) ≤ n − 1.                                      (1)
29:                                            Because of the straightforward relationship that λi (G) + λn+2−i (Gc ) = n for i = 2, ..., n, where Gc
30:                                            denotes the complement of G, this conjecture can be reformulated as stating
31:                                                                                     λn (G) + λn (Gc ) ≤ 2n − 1                                     (2)
32:                                            or equivalently
33:                                                                                        λ2 (G) + λ2 (Gc ) ≥ 1.                                      (3)
34:                                               In light of the connection between λ2 and how well-connected the graph is, the symmetric
35:                                            formulation of the Laplacian spread conjecture in (3) can be interpreted as stating that a graph
103: implies (3) and (6) above. We will also see how it can strengthen the Laplacian Spread Conjecture,
104: and compare its bound to other known bounds on λ2 . In Section 3, we will prove the conjecture is
105: true for several different families of graphs. These families will include all graphs of diameter other
106: than 3 and 4, all trees, and some families of graphs with diameter 3 and 4 that have particularly
107: small λ2 . These families will also explain the motivation for Conjecture 1. Finally, in Section 4,
108: we will explore some further related conjectures, including a conjecture analogous to the Laplacian
109: Spread Conjecture for weighted graphs, and a conjecture on the optimal spread of all diameter 3
110: graphs.

114: Proposition 2.1. If G is a graph on n vertices, then
115:                                           |D(G)| + |D(Gc )| ≤ n.
116: Proof. We claim that any vertex v of G that has eccentricity 3 or more in G will have eccentricity
117: at most 2 in Gc . To this end, suppose v has eccentricity 3 in G. We will show that v is distance at
118: most 2 from any vertex in Gc . Let x be an arbitrary vertex of G. If v and x are not adjacent in G,
119: then they are adjacent in Gc , so assume that v and x are adjacent in G. Let y be a vertex in G at
120: distance 3 or more from v in G. Note that y cannot be adjacent to x, since if it was, then since x
121: is adjacent to v in G, then y would be distance only 2 from v. Thus, in Gc , y is adjacent to both v
122: and x. Thus v and x are within distance 2 of each other in Gc . This completes the proof.

956: 4.3     Conjectures on weighted graphs
957: For a given labeling of n graph vertices, the set of Laplacians of simple graphs are the geometric
958: extreme points of a cube of dimension n2 each point of which is the Laplacian of a weighted
959: graph G in which all weights lie in the interval [0, 1]. For any such weighted graph G the algebraic
960: connectivity λ2 (G) and spectral radius λn (G) can also be defined, and it is a natural question
961: whether the Spread Conjecture also holds for such weighted graphs. Investigations on families of
962: such graphs and on small examples of random graphs appear so far to satisfy the Spread Conjecture.

981:     Two other conjectured bounds have been introduced. The symmetric formulation of Conjecture
982: 3 is represented by the dashed (green) curve, and the symmetric formulation of Conjecture 1 is
983: represented by the solid (red) curve of Figure 7. It is natural to ask whether these inequalities
984: might be satisfied also for weighted graphs, but the answer is that, for n ≥ 5 vertices, there are
985: weighted graphs that fail these inequalities. Investigations have identified a family of weighted
986: graphs that appear to be spectrally extreme with respect to the Laplacian spread. The graphs in
987: this family depend on a parameter s, and the values of λ2 (G) and λ2 (Gc ) that are achieved as
988: s varies for this family delineate what appears, in our investigations to date, to be the strongest
989: possible inequality over weighted graphs.

991: Definition 4.7. An SE(s) graph is a weighted graph G with all edge weights in the interval [0, 1]
992: and with vertices u and v such that:
993:    • the edge weight between u and any vertex in V (G) \ {u, v} is 0,
994:    • the edge weight between v and any vertex in V (G) \ {u, v} is 1, and
995: 
996:    • the edge weight between u and v is s ∈ (0, 1).
997:     Observe that the complement of an SE(s) graph is an SE(t) graph, for s + t = 1, with the
998: roles of u and v exchanged. The SE(s) graphs are, in retrospect, a natural family of weighted
999: graphs to consider when trying to disconnect, as much as possible, both the weighted graph and its
1000: complement. A simple graph G is most easily disconnected by having any one vertex of degree 0;
1001: symmetrically, it is easiest to disconnect Gc by having any one vertex with degree n − 1 in G. A
1002: graph and its complement cannot be simultaneously disconnected, of course, but they could be if it
1003: were possible to have both a vertex u of degree 0 and a vertex v of degree n − 1. The impediment
1004: to simultaneous disconnection comes from the fact that u and v cannot agree on the number of
1005: edges between them, a number that cannot be simultaneously 0 and 1. In the class of weighted
1006: graphs, u and v can compromise on this number using any edge weight wuv = s in the range [0, 1]
1007: (and edge weight t = 1 − s in the complement), giving a one-parameter family of graphs that are
1008: poorly connected simultaneously with their complements. In fact we will show the full SE(s) family
1009: consists of a great many such one-parameter families, because the non-parametrized weight of any
1010: edge involving neither u nor v is arbitrary within its legal range of [0, 1].
1011:     We first establish a relationship between the extremal Laplacian eigenvalues, or equivalently
1012: between the algebraic connectivity and complementary algebraic connectivity, of weighted graphs
1013: within the SE(s) family.
