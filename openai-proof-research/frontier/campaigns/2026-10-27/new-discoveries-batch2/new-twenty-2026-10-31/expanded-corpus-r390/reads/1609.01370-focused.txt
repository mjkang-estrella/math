12:                                                   The Mallows distribution behaves like a q-analogue of the uniform distribution by weighting
13:                                                   each permutation π by q inv(π) , where inv(π) is the number of inversions in π and q is a positive,
14:                                                   real-valued parameter. We prove that the growth rate exists for all patterns and all q > 0, and
15:                                                   we generalize Goulden and Jackson’s cluster method to keep track of the number of inversions
16:                                                   in permutations avoiding a given consecutive pattern. Using singularity analysis, we approxi-
17:                                                   mate the growth rates for length-3 patterns, monotone patterns, and non-overlapping patterns
18:                                                   starting with 1, and we compare growth rates between different patterns. We also use Stein’s
19:                                                   method to show that, under certain assumptions on q, the length of σ, and inv(σ), the number
20:                                                   of occurrences of a given pattern σ is well approximated by the normal distribution.
