11:                                                    two of the three letters are required to be adjacent. They established many connections

19:                                                                                     1. Introduction
20:                                               Since the publications of Duncan and Steingrímsson [15], Corteel, Martinez, Savage and
21:                                            Weselcouch [14] and Mansour and Shattuck [24], there has been increasing interest in
22:                                            counting pattern avoiding ascent/inversion sequences [1, 2, 6, 8, 10–12, 19–23, 25, 28, 29]. In
23:                                            particular, motivated by the study of generalized patterns in permutations [4,13], Yan and
24:                                            the first named author [23] carried out the systematic study of ascent/inversion sequences
25:                                            avoiding vincular patterns of length 3. They reported many nice connections with familiar
26:                                            combinatorial families and posed several challenging enumeration conjectures. The ob-
27:                                            jective of this paper is to address two of their conjectures concerning the pattern 120 in
28:                                            ascent/inversion sequences. It turns out that 120-avoiding ascent and inversion sequences
29:                                            possess attractive enumeration results albeit having elusive structure.
30:                                               Before stating our results, we need to review some definitions on inversion sequences.
31:                                            An integer sequence e = e1 e2 . . . en of length n is an inversion sequence if 0 ≤ ei < i for
32:                                            all 1 ≤ i ≤ n. Inversion sequences of length n, denoted In , are in natural bijection with
33:                                            permutations Sn of [n] := {1, 2, . . . , n} via the famous Lehmer code (see [14, 20]). The set
34:                                            An of ascent sequences of length n consists of e ∈ In such that
35:                                                                                  ei+1 ≤ asc(e1 e2 . . . ei ) + 1
36:                                            for all 1 ≤ i < n, where asc(e1 e2 . . . ei ) := |{ℓ ∈ [i − 1] : eℓ < eℓ+1 }| is the number of ascents
37:                                            of e1 e2 . . . ei . As one of the most important subsets of inversion sequences, ascent sequences
38:                                            were introduced by Bousquet-Mélou, Claesson, Dukes and Kitaev [9] to encode the (2+2)-
39:                                            free posets. Many remarkable connections between inversion (resp. ascent) sequences and
40:                                            permutations (resp. restricted permutations) with unexpected applications have been found
41:                                            in the literature; see [17, 20, 27] and the references therein.

443: 3.2. The last entry statistic. In this subsection, we study the last entry statistic of
444: inversion sequences and obtain a new succession rule for powered Catalan numbers. For an
445: inversion sequence e ∈ In , let last(e) = en be the last entry of e. The last entry statistic has
446: been found to be useful in solving two enumeration conjectures in [19]. By comparing the
447: construction of the rule ΩpCat for 120-avoiding inversion sequences in the proof of Conj. 1.2
448: and that for 110-avoiding inversion sequences in the proof of [8, Prop. 19], we have the
449: following equidistribution.
450: Proposition 3.4. The tripe (last, zero, rmin) has the same distribution over In (120) and
451: In (110), where rmin(e) is the number of right-to-left minima of an inversion sequence e.
452:    Lin and Yan [23, Lem. 2.19] showed that Baril and Vajnovszki’s b-code [5] restricts to a
453: bijection between Sn (3214) and In (120). For a permutation π = π1 π2 · · · πn ∈ Sn , define
454: the encoding Θ by
455:                Θ(π) = (e1 , e2 , . . . , en ),   where ei := |{j : j < i and πj > πi }|.
456: The encoding Θ, known as invcode of permutations, is a variation of the famous Lehmer
457: code. One interesting feature of Θ that the b-code does not possess is last(π) = last(Θ(π)),
458: where last(π) := n − πn .
459: Proposition 3.5. The invcode Θ restricts to a bijection between Sn (3214) and In (120).
460: Consequently, the triple (last, zero, rmin) over In (120) (or In (110)) is equidistributed with
461: (last, lmax, rmax) over Sn (3214), where lmax(π) (resp. rmax(π)) denotes the number of
462: left-to-right maxima (resp. right-to-left maxima) of a permutation π.

511:   The classification of Wilf equivalences for vincular patterns of length 3 in inversion se-
512: quences has been completed, thanks to Auli and Elizalde’s recent work1 [3]. Towards the

519: In their attempt to prove this conjecture, Beaton, Bouvel, Guerrini and Rinaldi [8, Conj. 23]
520: found the following refinement.
521: 
522: Conjecture 4.1. The number of permutations of Sn (2314) with k right-to-left minima is
523: cn,k .
524: 
525:    Conjecture 4.1 is equivalent to the assertion that the statistic ‘zero’ over In (120) or
526: In (110) has the same distribution as ‘rmin’ over Sn (2314), where rmin(π) denotes the
527: number of right-to-left minima of a permutation π. Using Maple program, we find the
528: following refinement of Conjecture 4.1.
529: 
530: Conjecture 4.2. The quadruple (rmin, lmin, rmax, asc) on Sn (2314) has the same distri-
531: bution as (zero, max, rmin, rep) on In (110).
532: 
533:   Here we use lmin(π) (resp. asc(π)) to denote the number of left-to-right minima (resp. as-
534: cents) of a permutation π. And for an inversion sequence e ∈ In , the two statistics involved
535: are

537:          max(e) := |{i ∈ [n] : ei = i − 1}| and     rep(e) := n − |{e1 , e2 , . . . , en }|.

539: Conjecture 4.2 has been verified for 1 ≤ n ≤ 9.
540:   Finally, the consideration of the last entry statistic leads to another refinement of Baxter
541: and Shattuck’s enumeration conjecture.
542: 
543: Conjecture 4.3. The pair (last, rmax) on Sn (2314) has the same distribution as (last, rmin)
544: on In (120).

546:   Conjecture 4.3 has also been verified for 1 ≤ n ≤ 9. In view of Proposition 3.6, it would

548: One remarkable special case of Conjecture 4.3 is that
549: 
550:                |{π ∈ Sn (2314) : πn = n}| = Bn−1 = |{e ∈ In (120) : en = 0}|,
551: 
552: which follows from the enumeration results in [13, 23]. Here Bn is the n-th Bell number.
