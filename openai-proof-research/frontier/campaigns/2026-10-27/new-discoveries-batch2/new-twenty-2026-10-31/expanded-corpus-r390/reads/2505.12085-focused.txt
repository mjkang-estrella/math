829: 4. Conclusion
830: In this work, we explored the computation of 3-colour Rado numbers for certain 3-term linear equa-
831: tions. Our SAT-based approach enabled us to extend the known values of 𝑅3 (ℰ(3, 0; 𝑎, 𝑏, 𝑏)) and
832: 𝑅3 (ℰ(3, 0; 𝑎, 𝑎, 𝑏)), computing previously unknown Rado numbers and formulating new conjectures
833: based on observed patterns. We proved the lower bounds 𝑅3 (ℰ(3, 0; 𝑎, 𝑏, 𝑏)) ≥ 𝑎3 + 𝑎2 + (2𝑏 + 1)𝑎 + 1
834: and 𝑅3 (ℰ(3, 0; 𝑎, 𝑎, 𝑎 + 1)) ≥ 𝑎3 (𝑎 + 1) for certain ranges of 𝑎 and 𝑏. The case-based proofs were
835: automatically verified using a combination of symbolic computation and SMT solvers in a tool we call
836: AutoCase.
837:    We did not attempt to compute Rado numbers involving more than three colours due to the rapid
838: growth in the SAT instances, and for simplicity we focused on linear equations with a constant term of
839: zero. In principle, our methods should be applicable to nonhomogeneous linear equations and to Rado
840: numbers involving four or more colours, though they may require more computational resources or
841: more advanced combinatorial optimization techniques. We leave such cases for future work.
