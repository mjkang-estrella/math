A shortest path can be chosen without repeated vertices by deleting a closed subwalk. In a cycle, once its first edge and direction are chosen, a simple path must continue in that direction until reaching the endpoint: taking the other edge would repeat the preceding vertex. Thus it is one of the two arcs, giving the matching lower bound. This argument is a proof, not an automated distance table.

    <h1 class="title mathjax"><span class="descriptor">Title:</span>Distinguishing number and distinguishing index of natural and fractional powers of graphs</h1>
    <div class="authors"><span class="descriptor">Authors:</span><a href="https://arxiv.org/search/math?searchtype=author&amp;query=Alikhani,+S" rel="nofollow">Saeid Alikhani</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/search/math?searchtype=author&amp;query=Soltani,+S" rel="nofollow">Samaneh Soltani</a></div>            <div id="download-button-info" hidden>View a PDF of the paper titled Distinguishing number and distinguishing index of natural and fractional powers of graphs, by Saeid Alikhani and Samaneh Soltani</div>
    <a class="mobile-submission-download" href="/pdf/1604.03839">View PDF</a>
    <a class="mobile-submission-download" href="https://arxiv.org/html/1604.03839v1">HTML (experimental)</a>



    <blockquote class="abstract mathjax">
            <span class="descriptor">Abstract:</span>The distinguishing number (index) $D(G)$ ($D&#39;(G)$) of a graph $G$ is the least integer $d$ such that $G$ has an vertex labeling (edge labeling) with $d$ labels that is preserved only by a trivial automorphism. For any $n \in \mathbb{N}$, the $n$-subdivision of $G$ is a simple graph $G^{\frac{1}{n}}$ which is constructed by replacing each edge of $G$ with a path of length $n$. The $m^{th}$ power of $G$, is a graph with same set of vertices of $G$ and an edge between two vertices if and only if there is a path of length at most $m$ between them. The fractional power of $G$, denoted by $G^{\frac{m}{n}}$ is $m^{th}$ power of the $n$-subdivision of $G$ or $n$-subdivision of $m$-th power of $G$. In this paper we study the distinguishing number and distinguishing index of natural and fractional powers of $G$. We show that the natural powers more than two of a graph distinguished by three edge labels. Also we show that for a connected graph $G$ of order $n \geqslant 3$ with maximum degree $\Delta (G)$, $D(G^{\frac{1}{k}})\leqslant min\{s: 2^k+\sum^s_{n=3}n^{k-1}\geqslant \Delta (G)\}$ and for $m\geqslant 3$, $D&#39;(G^{\frac{m}{k}})\leqslant 3$.
    </blockquote>
