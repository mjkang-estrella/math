324157 Mu3 conjecture for hypohamiltonian snarks https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.4510 
 Let $G$ be a **snark**, and let $\mu_3(G)$ be the number of edges not covered by the union of three 1-factors, minimized over all choices of three 1-factors. A graph is **hypohamiltonian** if it is non-Hamiltonian but deleting any vertex produces a Hamiltonian graph. **Hypohamiltonian-snark conjecture.** If $G$ is hypohamiltonian, then

$$
\mu_3(G)=3.
$$

The source motivates this by noting that hypohamiltonian snarks have cyclic cores and that known examples have $\mu_3(G)=3$. Its status is not resolved in the source.
324245 The antichain-sequence conjecture for naturally labeled posets https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2412 
 Let $(P,\omega)$ and $(Q,\tau)$ be naturally labeled posets, meaning that the labeling is compatible with the poset order. Write $(P,\omega)\sim(Q,\tau)$ when their $P$-partition generating functions are equal. For a poset $P$, let

$$
\mathrm{anti}(P)=(a_1,\ldots,a_w),
$$

where $a_i$ is the number of antichains in $P$ of size $i$ and $w$ is the width of $P$. **Antichain-sequence conjecture.** If $(P,\omega)\sim(Q,\tau)$, then

$$
\mathrm{anti}(P)=\mathrm{anti}(Q).
$$

This would strengthen the preceding result that equality of $P$-partition generating functions preserves the width of naturally labeled posets. The conjecture is presented as an extension of that corollary; the source gives no resolution.
324246 Conjecture on eventual equality of card-visit counts https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.2419 
 Let $c_i(t)$ denote the number of times card $i$ has been seen by time $t$. **Eventual equality conjecture.** For any card $i$, there exists $t$ such that

$$
c_i(t)=c_1(t).
$$

This would make precise the observation that the numbers of times any two cards have been seen eventually converge. The preceding proposition shows that once two cards have been seen the same positive number of times, their counts remain within $1$; it remains open whether equality with card $1$ must always occur.
324279 Burnham–Rosen–Sidman–Vermeire conjecture on graph curves and Betti tables https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3064 
 Let $G$ be a graph with $d$ vertices, genus $g$, and girth $b3$. Let $\overline{G}$ be the corresponding graph curve, with Betti numbers $b_{i,j}(\overline{G})$. **Burnham–Rosen–Sidman–Vermeire conjecture.** If $d=2g+1+p$ and $\gamma-2\leq p$, then

$$
b_{\gamma-2,\gamma}(\overline{G})
$$

is equal to the number of $\gamma$-cycles in $G$. This predicts that a graph's cycle structure is reflected in the Betti table of its graph curve; the paper proves a specific case for trees of cycles, while the stated general form is not resolved here.
324280 Bridge-count conjecture for graph curves https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3064 
 Let $G$ be a graph and let $\overline{G}\subset \mathbb{P}^{n}$ be its graph curve. Write $b_{i,j}(\overline{G})$ for the graded Betti numbers, and call an edge of $G$ a **bridge** if removing it disconnects $G$. **Bridge-count conjecture.** The Betti number

$$
b_{n-1,n}(\overline{G})
$$

is equal to the number of bridges in $G$. This connects a graph-theoretic connectivity invariant with the Betti table of the associated graph curve; the paper proves the assertion for trees of cycles, but the general statement is presented as a conjecture.
324281 Betti-table symmetry conjecture for graphs formed from glued four-cycles https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3064 
 Let $G$ be a graph formed by gluing $k$ copies of the four-cycle $C_{4}$ together along one edge. Write $b_{i,j}(\overline{G})$ for the graded Betti numbers of its graph curve. **Betti-table symmetry conjecture.** Then

$$
b_{2,4}(\overline{G})=b_{1,2}(\overline{G})-1,
$$

$$
b_{i,i+1}(\overline{G})=b_{i+2,i+4}(\overline{G}),\qquad i\geq 2,
$$

and

$$
b_{k,k+2}(\overline{G})=\left(b_{k+1,k+3}(\overline{G})\right)^{2}-1.
$$

These experimentally observed identities describe patterns between the quadratic and cubic strands for this family of graph curves; the source gives them as conjectural future work, with no resolution stated.
324286 Joint equidistribution conjecture for statistics on beta(0,1)-trees https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3219 
 Let $\operatorname{root}$, $\operatorname{rzero}$, $\operatorname{rmod}$, and $\operatorname{sub}$ be the statistics on beta$(0,1)$-trees defined in the paper. Two pairs of statistics are jointly equidistributed when they have the same joint distribution over beta$(0,1)$-trees.

**Joint equidistribution conjecture.** The pairs

$$
(\operatorname{root},\operatorname{rzero})
$$

and

$$
(\operatorname{rmod},\operatorname{sub})

are jointly equidistributed on beta$(0,1)$-trees.

The claim extends the paper's established equidistribution results for other pairs of statistics. It is stated without a proof, and the supplied text gives no evidence that it has been resolved.
324556 Dual-rank characterization of graphicness for binary matroids https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5823 
 Let $M$ be a $3$-connected binary matroid and let $S\subset E(M)$ satisfy $r^*_M(S)\ge 3$. A non-separating cocircuit is a cocircuit whose deletion leaves a connected matroid.

**Graphicness characterization conjecture.** The following assertions are equivalent:

1. $M$ is graphic.
2. Each element of $S$ avoids at most $r^*(M)-1$ non-separating cocircuits of $M$.
3. Each element of $S$ avoids no linearly dependent set of non-separating cocircuits of $M$.

The source presents this as a consequence of the main conjecture, whose proof was reduced to a computational verification. Thus the characterization remains conditional on that unresolved conjecture in the supplied paper.
324561 Mullen's conjecture on value-set bounds for even-degree polynomials https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6044 
 Let $n$ be an even integer, let $q$ be odd with $q>n(n-2)$, and let $ffbbf fbbf_q[x]$ have degree $n$. Write $V_f=f(fbbf_q)$ for the value set of $f$ on $fbbf_q$.

**Mullen's conjecture.** The value set satisfies

$$
|V_f|\leqslant q-\left\lceil\frac{q-1}{n}\right\rceil.
$$

This conjecture is a generalisation of Carlitz's conjecture concerning polynomials that are close to being permutation polynomials. The source says that it had remained unresolved in published literature at the time of writing.
324571 Equal-distribution pair discrepancy bound https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6486 
 Let $D({\bf p},{\bf q})$ denote the discrepancy between the color distributions of the matching left-right pair when the left and right shoes have distributions ${\bf p}$ and ${\bf q}$. In the equal-distribution case, write $D({\bf p},{\bf p})$. **Equal-distribution discrepancy bound.** One should have

$$
\sup_{\bf p}D({\bf p},{\bf p})<1.
$$

This is the analogue of the universal discrepancy bound under the constraint that the two shoe distributions agree. The paper describes it as plausible and leaves it open.
