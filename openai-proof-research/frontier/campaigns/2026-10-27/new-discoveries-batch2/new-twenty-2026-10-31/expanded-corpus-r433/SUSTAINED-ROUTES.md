## Flashcard synchronization, 324246
The initial deck is1,2,...; at its kth viewing a card is reinserted at position k+1. At time1 card1 is already counted once. For i<j, source Proposition3.1 gives c_i>=c_j always. Proposition3.4 says an achieved positive equality is stable to within1, but does not establish an equality is achieved. Theorem3.6 supplies T1(n)<=n²-n+1. Corollary3.3 gives T_i(k+1)<T1(i+k), while Theorem3.10 improves an early-view lower comparison in an index-dependent range. None gives an eventual zero deficit.

First mechanism: fix i and study d(t)=c1(t)-ci(t)>=0. Each viewing of1 increments d, each viewing of i decrements it, and other cards leave it fixed. If no equality occurs, d stays positive. The source comparison bounds the lag for fixed i but allows a persistent positive lag: two count sequences can both have the same quadratic-scale viewing times and differ by a constant forever. Thus a contradiction cannot come from leading growth rates or a uniform bounded deficit alone. Need an actual strict decreasing potential over selected return blocks, not a non-strict asymptotic inequality.

Second mechanism: reduce to adjacent cards, hoping the smaller reinsertion depth of the lagging card forces it to be seen twice between some consecutive visits of the leading card. Immediately after viewing the leading card with count a, it is at position a+1. A trailing card viewed with count b<a goes to b+1; this suggests a relative speed advantage. But the number of intervening cards removed and reinserted before each front encounter changes the relative distance. Both cards can overtake the other in different return blocks. The difference a-b in insertion lengths alone does not bound the relative distance before the next leap. A needed lemma is: if adjacent-index counts remain unequal indefinitely in the actual slow-game orbit, then some block forces two visits of the lagging card without an intervening visit of the leading card, with enough net repeats eventually to erase the deficit. I have not proved this statement; no invariant eliminating the competing intervening-card motion was found.

Third mechanism: for a finite prefix1,...,i, consider total deficits sum_(j<=i)(c1-cj), and group returns between consecutive views of1. Source ordering gives each prefix card many repeat visits, but a prefix potential may increase whenever1 is seen and gains no uniform strict decrease from interactions with cards>i. Truncating the infinite deck changes reinsertion destinations and removes precisely these interactions. A finite-deck synchronization proof would therefore need a uniform comparison to the infinite process; no such comparison is justified. This route is parked, not refuted.

No finite enumeration, numerical test or simulation was performed. Historical r78 numerical data about a different bounded-increment conjecture is documentary only, neither a proof nor a new experiment.

## Antichain multiplicities, 324245
Original Definition2.1 makes natural labels weak/order-preserving. The strict-label involution lets greatest monomial exponents detect width (Proposition4.5/Corollary4.6). Trying to count antichains by counting equal-value level sets overcounts them by the choices of order maps on the rest of the poset. Knowing only which powers occur therefore cannot give the desired multiplicities.

A more structural route would cut K_P along ideals via the quasisymmetric coproduct, then apply linear functionals recording numbers of minimal/maximal elements, since antichains biject with ideals via their maximal sets. The missing mathematical step is an explicit, justified functional on K_I extracting the requisite maximal-count distribution without retaining the ideal itself. I did not assume this functional exists or turn the coproduct heuristic into a proof.
