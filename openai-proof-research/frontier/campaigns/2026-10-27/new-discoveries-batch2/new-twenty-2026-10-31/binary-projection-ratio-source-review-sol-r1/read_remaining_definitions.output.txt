349177 Nonexistence conjecture for dendrimer nanostars of at least four stages A **dendrimer nanostar** is the nanostar structure obtained by growing a dendrimer through a specified number of stages; a graph is a **$G_\phi$-graph** if it is realized as $G_\phi(A)$ for an appropriate set $A$, using the Euler $\phi$ function as in the paper. **Nonexistence conjecture for dendrimer nanostars.** There is no dendrimer nanostar which has grown at least four stages, as a $G_\phi$-graph. 349441 Estrada–Mugnolo's Kirchhoff-index ordering conjecture for hubs-biased resistance distances Let $G$ be a connected graph. Write $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=1}}}(G)$ for the Kirchhoff index based on the hubs-repelling resistance distance, $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}}_{\alpha=0}(G)$ for the standard Kirchhoff index, and $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=-1}}}(G)$ for the Kirchhoff index based on the hubs-attracting resistance distance.

**Kirchhoff-index ordering conjecture.** For $\alpha\in\{-1,1\}$,

$$
\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=1}}}(G)\geq\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}}_{\alpha=0}(G)\geq\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=-1}}}(G),
$$

with equality if and only if $G$ is regular.

The conjecture was formulated after computational experiments on more than 12,000 connected graphs with $5\leq n\leq 8$, and predicts that hubs-repelling navigation gives the largest Kirchhoff index while hubs-attracting navigation gives the smallest. Its general validity is left open in the source.
350452 Non-IR conjecture for paths and cycles Let $P_n$ denote the path on $n$ vertices and $C_n$ the cycle on $n$ vertices. An **IR-graph** is a graph arising as an irredundance graph.

**Non-IR conjecture for paths and cycles.** The path $P_n$ is not an IR-graph for each $n\geq 3$, and the cycle $C_n$ is not an IR-graph for each $n\geq 5$.

These are open problems concerning which graphs can occur as irredundance graphs. They are stated together in the paper's list of conjectures and open problems and are attributed there to Mynhardt (2019).

2. Background

   To the best of our knowledge, there is no method in the literature that directly addresses the binary

sequence design problem. Nonetheless, we summarize in this section two existing approaches for the problem

of sequence design with unit modulus. We include those two approaches since they can be easily modified

to design binary sequences by changing the optimization constraints. For other approaches to unimodular

sequence design, such a change is not straightforward [34–38]. We will use the original and proposed modified

algorithms for sequence design in the numerical simulations presented in Section 5.
