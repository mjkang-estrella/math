## Endpoint and hypotheses

There exist two disjoint nonempty subsets of the normalized eight-column complex DFT basis, a positive tolerance, and a real globally optimal solution of source equation25 such that Gaussian projection followed by coordinate signs produces a feasible binary output violating the conjectured bound on an event of positive probability. For every fixed positive finite search length, independent repetitions have positive probability of selecting a violating feasible output. The statement is existential in the optimum, not universal in all optima. It refutes a guarantee asserted without a restriction on which optimum is returned. It does not refute an existential choice of a specially selected optimum.

Interpret band matrices as column synthesis matrices F_B and band energies as ||F_B^H x||²; equivalently write the literal MathDB analysis matrix as A_B=F_B^H. Thus its denominator tr(A_M^H A_M S) is tr(F_M F_M^H S). This is the same mathematical function, not a change of hypotheses. ## Fourier and real-matrix bridges

Index coordinates and frequencies by 0,...,7. Put e=(1,...,1)^T, J=ee^T, Q=J/8 and P=I-Q. Let f_k(j)=exp(2πijk/8)/sqrt(8). For k=l the Hermitian inner product is 1. For k≠l, z=exp(2πi(l-k)/8) is not 1 and z^8=1: the integer l-k lies strictly between -8 and 8 and is nonzero, so its phase is not an integer multiple of 2π. Multiplying 1+z+...+z^7 by 1-z gives 1-z^8=0. Thus the sum vanishes. The eight columns are orthonormal and therefore a basis in complex dimension eight. The square column matrix is unitary: its adjoint is its inverse, so its column projectors sum to I.

Take Omega_I={0}, Omega_M={1,...,7}. In source indexing these are {1} and {2,...,8}. Both are nonempty and disjoint; Section4.1 expressly imposes no other band restrictions. Each is also an interval in that indexing; no implicit contiguity requirement obstructs the example. The message set includes every conjugate pair and the real Nyquist column. Because f_0=e/sqrt(8), F_I F_I^H=Q and F_M F_M^H=P. Both matrices are real symmetric positive semidefinite projectors. For a real vector x, squared band norm is x^TQx=(e^Tx)^2/8 or x^TPx=||x||²-(e^Tx)^2/8. These follow by multiplying the complex analysis map by its adjoint; all imaginary parts cancel exactly. We require no general conversion theorem for arbitrary complex PSD data.

## Exact global SDP optimality

Q²=Q, Q^T=Q, Pe=0, P²=P=P^T. Decompose x=Px+Qx into orthogonal real summands. Consequently x^TPx=||Px||²≥0. Let S=(8/7)P and alpha=5. Its diagonal entries are (8/7)(1-1/8)=1. Its eigenvalues are 8/7 on H={x:e^Tx=0} and 0 on span(e); rank is seven. Moreover QS=0, so its SDP interferer trace is 0≤5/2. This pays the source's HALVED tolerance, not merely the original tolerance. The objective is tr(PS)=(8/7)tr(P)=8 since tr(P)=7.

For every feasible real PSD X with diagonal1, tr(X)=8 and e^TXe≥0. Expanding the finite trace sum gives tr(QX)=e^TXe/8 and tr(PX)=8-e^TXe/8≤8. This upper bound applies to every feasible X, regardless of its constraint value. S attains it and is therefore a genuine global optimum. No solver, numerical optimum, uniqueness assumption or author certificate is used. A balanced binary vector t with four +1 and four -1 also gives a rank-one optimal matrix tt^T; the existence of that alternative does not remove S from the optimal set. A claim only about a solver forced to return such a rank-one optimum is different and not refuted here.

## Gaussian support, eigenbasis freedom and strict signs

Let a=(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,-7). Then e^Ta=0 and a∈H. For ANY real orthonormal eigenbasis U of H, put c=sqrt(8/7), T=cU, and v0=c^(-1)U^Ta in R^7. U U^T=P, so Tv0=a. For any v, ||T(v-v0)||_2=c||v-v0||_2. A ball of radius r=1/(2c) about v0 maps to vectors with every coordinate differing from a by less than 1/2, since each coordinate modulus is bounded by the Euclidean norm. The first seven coordinates are strictly positive and the last strictly negative. Hence sign(Tv)=s=(1,1,1,1,1,1,1,-1) throughout that open ball; no convention at zero is used.

The source's Gaussian has independent standard coordinates. Its joint Lebesgue density in R^7 is (2π)^(-7/2) exp(-||v||²/2). On the closed ball of radius r around v0 this density is bounded below by the strictly positive constant (2π)^(-7/2) exp(-(||v0||+r)^2/2). The open ball contains a Cartesian cube of positive side length: choose coordinate deviations strictly less than r/(2sqrt(7)). Its product Lebesgue measure is positive and the same density lower bound applies. Integrating over this cube proves positive Gaussian probability. It also proves measurability: the cube and ball are open and the sign event is an intersection of open linear halfspaces. Thus singular covariance in R^8 causes no zero-probability obstruction: we integrate in the seven-dimensional parameter domain where the density is nondegenerate.

All positive eigenvalues equal 8/7. Any choice, rotation, permutation or sign of their orthonormal eigenvectors satisfies the same construction; the center v0 changes but its norm and the existence of the ball do not. An eight-dimensional implementation with one zero eigenvalue uses the same event in the seven active coordinates and any open interval in the independent null coordinate; its probability is again positive. More generally the argument only uses the actual factor's surjection onto H. The SVD in Algorithm1 is written U Lambda U^T for this symmetric PSD matrix, hence is such an eigenfactor. Replacing it by a non-corresponding pair of singular vectors would not implement the stated algorithm.

## Feasible violation and probability quantifiers

For s, ||s||²=8 and e^Ts=6. Thus g(s)=36/8=9/2<5 and f(s)=8-9/2=7/2. The denominator is the attained positive SDP objective8, and gamma=(7/2)/8=7/16. Since π>3, π/2-1>1/2>7/16. This is a strict violation on the whole positive-probability ball, not a single prescribed Gaussian seed. Let E be that event and F the event g(sign(Tv))≤5. E⊆F and P(E)>0, so P(F)>0 and P(E|F)=P(E)/P(F)>0. Consequently even an almost-sure conditional guarantee fails. The assertion that every feasible realized projection has the stated ratio also fails directly.

For L=1 the event E yields the selected feasible output. For any fixed integer L≥1 under independent fresh Gaussian draws, the intersection of L copies of E has probability P(E)^L>0. All candidates are then the identical s, all feasible, and every tie-breaking rule in Algorithm1 returns s. Selection by the alternative score of Section4.3 also returns the same vector on this event. The source does not impose a positive lower bound on L; the L=1 refutation therefore needs no further independence convention. For L>1 the conclusion explicitly uses independence, the ordinary intended repeated-search law. No statement about infinitely many draws or a forced stopping rule until a particular quality threshold is claimed.
