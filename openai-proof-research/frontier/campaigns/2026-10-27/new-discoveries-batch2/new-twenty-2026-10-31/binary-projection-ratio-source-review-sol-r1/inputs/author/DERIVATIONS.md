Route A, closure by local sorting. The operator pi_i changes only row i to Hmin(T_(i-1),T_(i+1)); it decreases that row componentwise. Every action path decreases entries. If A<=_W B<=_W T, then support_T(B)={i:B_i!=T_i} is a subset of support_T(A): once a row drops it cannot recover. In particular every nontrivial step on a path from T to T' has its index in J=support_T(T'). Deleting trivial steps gives a word in the parabolic generators J. This is an actual reachability consequence, not just componentwise order.

Parabolic normalization r_J(U)=U pi_w0(J) shares all rows outside J and is the unique componentwise minimum among triangles with those fixed rows (source Proposition4.1). For U in [T',T], all those rows agree, hence r_J(U)=r_J(T) is constant. Since T' is obtained using only J, longest-element absorption shows r_J(T')=r_J(T); and r_J(T')<=_W T'. Thus the common normalization lies BELOW the lower endpoint. It is not an interior operator on the open interval unless the image happens to lie there, which it does not. A naive closure-operator proof therefore fails. It would be invalid to reverse that inequality to manufacture a contraction.

Route B, Boolean support fibers. The map U->support_T(U) is order-reversing into nonempty subsets of J on the open interval. For K subset J, triangles whose support lies in K fix every row outside K and have the common parabolic minimum T pi_w0(K). But the lower cutoff T'<=_W U may remove that minimum; the fact that a triangle lies componentwise between T' and T does not establish weak-order reachability. Unpaid precise lemma: for every nonempty K subset J, determine the homotopy type of {U:T'<_W U<_W T, support_T(U) subset K}, with a natural map compatible under K-inclusion, and show that the induced fiber/nerve pattern is a simplex or its boundary in exactly the parabolic endpoint cases of Conjecture8.2. No claim that all fibers have a least element has been established. Proposition4.1 alone is insufficient.

Route C, coatom nerve. For each i in Des(T), T pi_i differs from T only in row i. It is a coatom: a nontrivial intermediate action path would have to change only row i; idempotence leaves no intermediate triangle. Distinct such triangles are componentwise incomparable, so these give all coatoms. Cover the open interval by the subposets U<=_W T pi_i, retaining only those above T'. Each nonempty single piece has a top and hence a contractible order complex. Intersections of pieces are the difficulty. To identify a Boolean nerve one needs to prove the appropriate intersection is empty or contractible and characterize it exactly. The tempting claim that the meet of these coatoms is T pi_w0(K) is UNPROVED; Figure1 and the explicit nonlattice example forbid importing general lattice crosscut arguments. A common lower bound of two coatoms need not have a known greatest weak-order representative. This is the same unpaid lifting bridge in a more focused form.

Route D, discrete Morse. One could attempt a matching of chains by inserting/removing the first available sorted row. To be a proof it must (i) keep every matched chain strictly above T', (ii) prove no directed cycles, (iii) classify every unmatched chain and its dimension. No such matching is currently supplied. The source's shelling of Phi_n does not pay these requirements. There is no new ordinary full endpoint and no formal plan for this target. This is a sustained full-scope attempt with a concrete common-lower-bound/fiber gap, not a claim that a permutation subcase resolves the triangle conjecture.
