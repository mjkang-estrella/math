245: Definitions and notation. Let G = (V, E) be a graph with vertex set V and
246: edge set E. The degree of a vertex v will be denoted dv , and the size of a cycle c
247: will be sc . Graph order is the number of vertices, and graph size is the number of
248: edges. A cut vertex or articulation point is a vertex whose removal increases the
249: number of connected components of a graph, and a cut edge or bridge is an edge
250: whose removal does the same. A leaf is a vertex of degree 1, and a pendant is an
251: edge with a leaf at one end. If required for intelligibility, we will write R(G) for R,
252: dv (G) for dv , and so on.
253:     Common graphs we use are: the path on n vertices, Pn ; the cycle on n vertices,
254: Cn ; the star on n + 1 vertices Sn . A graph is starlike with r > 2 arms if it has one
255: vertex of degree r called its root to which are attached r paths. We may refer to
256: a path or cycle as even (respectively, odd ) when it has an even (respectively, odd )
257: number of vertices. A tree is a connected graph where every vertex is either a leaf
258: or an articulation point; equivalently, every edge is a bridge. It is the connected
259: graph with the fewest edges for a given number of vertices. A cactus is a connected
260: graph where any two cycles intersect in at most one point, and a nontrivial cactus
261: is a cactus without bridges; in other words, a nontrivial cactus may be constructed
262: by iteratively adding cycles to one another (glued together by identifying a vertex
263: from each), and a cactus by adding cycles and bridges.
264:     We may sometimes divide more complicated graphs up into smaller subgraphs
265: for convenience. A block is a maximal biconnected subgraph, that is, a subgraph
266: without any articulation points; equivalently, the articulation points of a graph
267: divide it into connected subgraphs which we call blocks. We may draw a graph of
268: these blocks as in Figure 1, called the block cut tree or BC-tree: assign a vertex in
269: the BC-tree for each articulation point and for each block in the graph, and assign
270: an edge in the BC-tree for each pair of an articulation point and a block which
271: contains it. Note that it is indeed a tree.
272:     We study these graphs by use of two standard invariants, radius and diameter,
273: and a degree-based topological index, the Randić index. The distance dist(u, v)
274: between two vertices is the minimal length of a path between them, and the eccen-
275: tricity ecc(v) of a vertex is the maximum distance from that vertex to any other
276: vertex in its component,
