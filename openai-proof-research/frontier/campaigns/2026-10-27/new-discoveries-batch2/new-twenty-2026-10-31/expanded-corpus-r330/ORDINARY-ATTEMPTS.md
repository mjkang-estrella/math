## 362023 — every connected graph with D'(G)=2 has a rigid orientation

Primary definitions read: arXiv:2402.16169v2, introduction, Theorems 3, 7, 9–11, 15 and Conjecture 17. All graphs in this source are connected and exclude K2; colorings are not required proper. OD'^- is the minimum arc distinguishing index over orientations, and equals 1 exactly when an orientation has trivial automorphism group. The earlier v1 is saved; v2 is dated 10 September 2026, rather than the stale date suggested by search metadata. Meslem–Sopena arXiv:1910.12738 companion body was inspected at Proposition 2 and the paths/cycles/complete-graph results. This is not a full companion-proof audit. No general resolution appears in the inspected source passages. Remaining citing literature and all journal/correction versions are not cleared.

Route A: encode a distinguishing two-edge-coloring c by reversing a reference orientation on the c=1 edges. If there is an Aut(G)-invariant reference orientation O, then an automorphism preserving the resulting orientation also preserves c: it preserves both O and the reversed-edge subset. Thus it is the identity. This proves the desired conclusion for that hypothesis. In particular, a bipartition whose two classes are individually invariant gives O with all edges from the first class to the second. This is precisely the known source Theorem 3/Corollary 4 specialization, not a new result.

Failure at full scope: a reference orientation need not be invariant. Even if the colored edge set has trivial stabilizer, an automorphism may reverse the reference directions at exactly the edges where it changes c, preserving the encoded orientation. Treating a chosen vertex ordering as invariant would be circular. The balanced bipartite case admits class-swapping automorphisms, and graphs with odd cycles do not even supply the two-class encoding.

Continuation A1: for a balanced bipartite graph, encode color 0 as X to Y and color 1 as Y to X. An orientation-preserving class-preserving automorphism must be identity by distinction. A remaining class-swapping automorphism must interchange the two color classes globally. Consequently its square is class-preserving and color-preserving, hence identity. There can be at most one such remaining automorphism: the quotient of any two would preserve classes and colors. This isolates a genuine residual involution, but does not eliminate it. Flipping one edge kills that particular involution if the edge is moved or reversed appropriately; it may create new orientation-preserving automorphisms that did not preserve the old orientation. There is no proof that one flip retains the trivial class-preserving stabilizer. No flip-search was run.

Route B: use the signed permutation action on edge directions. Fix a reference direction on each edge and let x in F2^E encode reversals. For each graph automorphism phi, let T_phi act on reference-oriented edges by transport; it has a permutation part P_phi and a reversal vector b_phi. Fixed orientations satisfy (I+P_phi)x=b_phi over F2. Twisted automorphisms (a power reverses an edge) have no fixed orientation. For a non-twisted phi, the cycle parity conditions hold and there are 2^{number_of_edge_orbits(phi)} fixed orientations: each edge orbit has one free starting direction. This follows by transporting a direction around every orbit; consistency is exactly the absence of reversal on return.

A sufficient general condition is sum over nonidentity non-twisted phi of 2^{edge_orbits(phi)-|E|}<1. The union bound then leaves a free orientation. But D'=2 supplies a regular subset for the unsigned edge action; it does not establish this numerical inequality. No argument relates that regular subset to a free vector for the signed action. Replacing this implication by an arbitrary group assertion would need its own proof and might be stronger than the graph problem. No mathematical group enumeration or probabilistic simulation was performed.

Route C: construct an acyclic orientation with vertices distinguished by their reachable sets. For a Hamiltonian ordering, orient every edge forward. A vertex at position i reaches exactly the later vertices, using the Hamiltonian path, so its number of reachable vertices is n-i and rigidity follows. This is the actual source Theorem 10. For a general connected graph, ordering a rooted spanning tree does not give this reachability count: siblings can have equal descendants, and chords add reachability. The two-edge-coloring does not automatically give a Hamiltonian path or invariant rooted decomposition. Source claw-free extension uses its specific path-cover and longest-cycle lemmas, not merely D'=2.

Precise missing endpoint: prove existence of a free point in the signed orientation action for every finite connected simple G with a distinguishing two-edge-coloring, including balanced bipartite class swaps and nontraceable graphs with claws; or give an explicit in-scope G and a proof that every orientation is nonrigid while D'=2. No complete endpoint, no execution request, no Pi plan.

## 362857 — dominant identity coefficient in the universal Drinfeld–Yetter algebra

Primary: arXiv:2404.16786v1 (only version listed in current metadata). Definition 3.8 defines the algebra as End_DY([V1]); basis Proposition 3.9 and Corollary 3.10 identify its graded vector space with the direct sum of K[S_n], for characteristic-zero K. The product is composition, not the separate star product. Degree n+m coefficients are those in the r_{n+m}^pi basis. Theorem 5.9 gives a signed sum over n by m looms. Corollary 5.14 groups it into positive and negative fibers. Appendix Proposition A.3 gives identity coefficient binomial(n+m,n); Conjecture A.7 asks that its absolute value dominate EVERY coefficient for arbitrary n,m, not just n=1. The thesis [28] is explicitly where proofs of Appendix A computations are located. Its landing GET returned 403; no alternate protected route was attempted. Thus even a successful new inequality would retain a material companion-body gate. Tile figures have not received complete graphical verification: extracted prose contains several empty pictorial symbols and raw TikZ fragments. This limitation prevents claiming a fully authenticated combinatorial implementation.

Route A: let Gamma_pi be the looms routing to pi with the two outer permutations identity, and let a_pi=P_pi-N_pi be its signed cardinality. Then the exact full target is |a_pi|<=binomial(n+m,n) for every pi in S_{n+m}. Bounding the total number of looms or summing absolute values over all fibers is insufficient. The theorem supplies |a_pi|<=P_pi+N_pi, but not a bound on each of these quantities by the identity coefficient.

Continuation A1: source Definitions 5.10–5.13 cancel opposite-sign looms in each fiber. Pairing min(P_pi,N_pi) leaves |a_pi| unpaired looms of one sign. This cardinality statement follows by separating the two finite sign classes; it is not itself an injection to the identity fiber. To prove the conjecture by this route one needs a uniform assignment from the surviving looms of EACH pi to (n,m)-shuffles, with an inverse uniqueness argument. Reading off a binary boundary word is a possible candidate, but different interior routings can share the same boundary word, and neither a sign-reversing involution preserving pi nor recoverability from that word was constructed. A raw total-loom bound loses exactly the cancellation needed. Source Example 5.11 already shows cancellation-pair choices need not be disjoint; independently canceling every displayed pair would double-use a loom.

Route B: Proposition A.2 supplies r_n^id composed with r_1^id = (n+1)r_{n+1}^id - sum_i r_{n+1}^{(i,i+1)}, and the analogous reversed product. For m=1, the identity coefficient n+1 dominates the n coefficients -1 (for n>=1); n=0 is the unit. This completely proves that boundary case, but it is already the printed proposition. It earns no new credit.

Continuation B1: associativity moves a factor r_1 across r_n^id composed with r_m^id. Solving the displayed recurrence for r_{m+1}^id introduces terms r_n^id composed with r_{m+1}^{(i,i+1)}. The induction hypothesis about identity-by-identity products does not bound these mixed-permutation products. A triangle inequality additionally discards their cancellations, so it does not yield the Pascal binomial bound. Enlarging induction to all mixed products would require a new coefficient inequality for permutations, which is not supplied by the conjecture. No unjustified positivity is assumed: the source expansion has negative signs.

Precise missing lemma: either an explicit sign-preserving fiber cancellation/injection into the binomial shuffle set for all n,m, with all tile and permutation bridges, or a simultaneous coefficient recurrence with a proved invariant implying the same bound. No such lemma, counterexample, or complete ordinary proof obtained. No solver, loom enumeration, CAS, or formalization was run.

## 362355 — perfect PackIt! games in the prime-gap range

Primary: arXiv:2403.12195v1 and v2; latest listed v2 is 15 May 2024. Definitions, Lemmas 1–2, prime obstructions Theorems 1–2 and Conjecture 1 were inspected. Final publication landing identifies LIPIcs.FUN.2024.14, published 29 May 2024. The final PDF body and later informal stronger-packing claim located by search remain unread; neither is clearance. Figures 1–2 are textually present with labels, but the geometry was not visually authenticated.

A legal turn t places one axis-aligned grid rectangle of area t or t+1, wholly inside the m by n board and disjoint from earlier rectangles. A perfect game covers all cells. Let T_k=k(k+1)/2, K=max{k:T_k<=mn}, gamma=mn-T_K, P={primes p:n<p<=K}, and delta be 1 exactly when K+1 is prime, as the source states. Literal MathDB denotes the final indicator as 1_{K_p}; the source's exact convention must be preserved in any future formal endpoint. Do not replace perfect tiling by area arithmetic.

Route A: choose expansion flags e_t in {0,1} with sum e_t=gamma and area a_t=t+e_t. Every p in P forces e_p=1 because area p cannot fit in either dimension. If a_t is a prime exceeding n, that turn is forbidden. Thus each p in P forbids e_{p-1}=1, and a final large prime K+1 similarly forbids e_K=1. Distinct odd primes have disjoint forced indices; primes in this range exceed n, so the small exceptional p=2 must be treated separately rather than silently absorbed. The lower and upper bounds count forced expansions and nonexpansions. This explains the source obstructions, but supplies no geometric packing. Composite a_t can also lack a factor pair bounded by m,n; absence of a prime obstruction alone is not an argument for factorability. No claim that the source bounds are sufficient for even the rectangle-selection stage has been smuggled into the proof.

Continuation A1: even if for every t one chooses h_t v_t=a_t with h_t<=m and v_t<=n and the total is mn, arbitrary such rectangles need not tile a board. Exact full endpoint would need integral coordinates (x_t,y_t), inside-board inequalities and pairwise disjoint cell sets, plus their union equals the board. Those are the missing geometric conditions; an area-sum certificate is not a counterexample or proof.

Route B: restrict to horizontal bars (height 1) whenever a_t<=n. It suffices to partition the turns into m row-bins whose area sums are each n. Given such bins, place bars consecutively in their assigned rows. They are disjoint by the consecutive placement, cover each row by the sum condition, and may be played in turn order because no occupied cell intersects a future rectangle. This is a complete sufficient construction, not the conjecture: when K>n, several a_t exceed n and bars cannot be used at all, even in the admissible prime-gap range. When K<=n, a universal equal-bin partition with a permitted expansion count is still unproved. For m=1, K<=n and any gamma expansion choices summing to n do concatenate into a perfect packing; this easy special case gives no campaign credit.

Continuation B1: induction by adding one column fails directly. The old turn sequence uses K(m,n) rectangles; new turns have areas roughly K+1, which may exceed the entire added column's area m. Reusing the old tiling without rearrangement cannot absorb them. A multirow rearrangement would have to preserve the turn areas of every old rectangle while making a new large rectangle fit; no invariant supporting that operation was found. Checkerboard imbalance might obstruct a fixed sequence of rectangles, but choices of parity, position and expansion flags remain free, so a parity objection must hold for all those choices. No such universal obstruction was established. No finite board search was run.

Precise missing endpoint: either a general constructive tiling algorithm with an ordinary proof for every m<=n satisfying the literal prime-gap inequalities, or one concrete board in that range with a full impossibility argument covering every legal expansion/shape/placement choice. None supplied. Park as unresolved, with no Pi diagnostic or generic solver plan.
