## 357792 — rank 1, exact Conjecture 10

New primary reading: matching.txt lines 38–78 (definitions), 93–140 (convex minimax strategy), 209–245 and 280–331 (actual Theorem 2 proof mechanism), 545–568 (Problem 9/Conjecture 10). Lemmas 5/6 proof bodies at 348–445 were NOT fully read; no whole-paper audit. Published Discrete Mathematics page located, not a full published-body comparison. Historical Fingerhut result is a different planar ellipse assertion, not an inherited exclusion of this quantitative all-dimensional question.

The required universal claim is independent of cardinality: each d has epsilon_d>0 working for every finite even separated point set and EVERY maximum-total-Euclidean-length matching. For centers c_e=(a+b)/2 and radii R_e=|a-b|/2 set F(z)=max_e(|z-c_e|-R_e). A ball B(z,rho) is contained in every diameter ball precisely when rho<=-F(z); hence the desired inequality is -min F >= epsilon_d min_pair_distance. Maximum matching concerns length, not squared length; the source explicitly distinguishes these objectives.

Attempt 1: minimize the convex coercive F. At a minimizer, its subgradient convex hull contains zero. Caratheodory retains at most d+1 active subgradients. For an active center equal to z, the norm subdifferential is a unit ball; otherwise it is the radial unit vector. The selected subfamily has the SAME minimizer and SAME minimum value: the weighted active supporting inequalities bound its maximum below by F(z). Retaining the matched endpoints retains a maximum matching on those endpoints, since any improving rematching would improve the original. Thus a uniform assertion for at most d+1 matched pairs would suffice for all cardinalities. This is an ordinary reduction, not a new credited result and not a Lean endpoint.

Attempt 2: normalize min separation to 1 and translate one endpoint to zero. On a bounded-diameter stratum of fixed cardinality, configurations form a compact set and finitely many matching patterns have closed optimality conditions. If qualitative open-ball intersection is known there, continuity of the minimax value gives a strictly positive minimum inradius on that stratum. This fails to prove the claim because diameter is unbounded; in d>2 even the qualitative input is not supplied by the source. Required remaining lemma: rule out a sequence of optimal matchings with <=d+1 pairs, separation>=1, diverging diameter and inradius tending to zero. A multiscale cluster decomposition must preserve the length exchange inequalities; a squared-distance substitute is not valid.

Attempt 3: seeded floating diagnostics exhaust all pairings for 35 samples of each (d,n)=(2,4),(2,6),(3,6),(3,8), anisotropy factors up to 10^4; then minimize F numerically. No negative computed inradius. Nelder–Mead is uncertified and its value gives a LOWER bound on the attainable inradius, not a certified global optimum; the legacy key 'inradius_upper_estimate' in diagnostics is misleading and denotes only this numerical estimate. Solver success is not a proof. No general lower bound or novelty clearance follows. Small sweeps stopped.

Formal feasibility: finite endpoint and exact exchange certificate could be modest, but a full positive solution needs Euclidean minimax/subdifferential bridges and the missing escape lemma. No formal certificate started.

## 357475 — rank 2, all coprime a,b of different residues

New primary reading: triangle.txt lines 65–102, including complete Proposition 1 proof and Conjecture 1; Lemma 1 and its full proof lines 120–146. The paper's full remaining proof is not audited. The database sentence claiming that three colors are already known insufficient in the entire residue case is unsupported: source verifies only a+b<=7. Journal EJC120(2024)103977 identified; full published body unread. Scope is metric isometric triples, including bent degenerate triangles, NOT only Euclidean collinear triples.

Attempt 1: reuse the source's axis-projection reduction. The extreme pair forces one coordinate to differ by a+b; the middle point then has that coordinate at distance a or b from an endpoint. Thus one-dimensional forbidden translated/reflected triples are necessary coordinate patterns, but a color class's two projections are insufficient to encode the coupled middle point. The source's three-color construction is confined to a=b mod3. Extending it across residues is the missing mechanism, not an available bridge.

Attempt 2: affine three-coloring floor(x)+floor(y) mod3 fails even on integer points. Exact witnesses: (a,b)=(1,2): (0,0),(0,3),(1,2); (1,3): (0,0),(2,4),(3,3); (1,8): (0,0),(0,9),(1,8); (2,7): (0,0),(0,9),(1,2). All three sums have equal residue; sorted max-norm distances are exactly a,b,a+b. This kills the natural diagonal shortcut, not the conjecture.

Attempt 3: enumerate every integer triple on grids, solve three-color feasibility, and independently check each returned assignment against ALL enumerated hyperedges. Finite valid assignments found on 5x5 for (1,2) and 10x10 for (1,8),(2,7). They do not refute the real-plane assertion and cannot be repeated periodically without wraparound checks. Required remaining lemma: a uniform finite four-chromatic hypergraph for every coprime different-residue pair, or a periodic coloring verified on ALL translated integer triples plus a valid real-plane lifting. Neither supplied. Increasing grids would not fix the all-parameter gap, so stopped.

Formal feasibility: an exact universal geometric projection lemma is feasible in Lean; a finite UNSAT certificate for one source-known instance would earn no new credit. No significant full-scope endpoint survives.

## 357592 — rank 3, list-packing Dinitz

New primary reading: packing.txt lines 25–52 (list packing definitions); 864–925 (actual Proposition 25 proof and Conjecture 26 with product-graph bridge). Published Cambridge version found at Delft, retrieval timed out; no publisher-body clearance. List packing means k proper list colorings with distinct colors at EACH vertex; many colorings alone do not suffice.

Attempt 1: try iteration of Galvin's ordinary list edge coloring. Removing a color used by the first coloring leaves lists one shorter, while the underlying rook graph still needs n colors. After two extractions from n+1 lists, n-1 lists remain, so Galvin's n-list theorem cannot be iterated to close the packing. This is an explicit obstruction to a proposed routine bridge, not an impossibility theorem.

Attempt 2: replace packing by one list coloring on (K_n square K_n) square K_(n+1), copying a cell's list into its n+1 replicas. A proper coloring yields a packing because replicas form a clique, and each layer is proper on the rook graph. This is EXACTLY the source's proposed bridge, already known; no discovery credit. Ordinary product maximum degree is 3n-2 and greedy degree+1 is 3n-1, not n+1. A Combinatorial Nullstellensatz orientation would require an appropriate nonvanishing coefficient; no such coefficient proved. The choice from lists must be arbitrary, so a single cyclic Latin array or a random-list sweep cannot certify the universal problem.

Remaining lemma: every (n+1)-list assignment on the rook graph has n+1 disjoint proper list colorings, or the stronger product list-coloring statement, for all n>=3. A finite counterexample would need exact lists and a sound exhaustive nonpacking certificate. No concrete list assignment defeating n+1 was identified. The lower bound >n is explicitly author-proved Proposition 25, not a new result.
