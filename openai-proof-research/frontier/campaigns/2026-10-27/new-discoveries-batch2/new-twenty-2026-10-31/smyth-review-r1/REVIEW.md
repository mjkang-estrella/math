## A. Statement fidelity: PASS

**Source definitions in the arXiv TeX:**

* **Partitions and dominance (line 24, line 26).** "λ is dominated by μ (or μ dominates λ) iff |λ| = |μ| and Σ_{i≤j} λ(i) ≤ Σ_{i≤j} μ(i) for all positive integers j", written λ ⊴ μ or μ ⊵ λ. The sum condition is part of the definition.
* **Majorization (line 28).** Majorization on ℝ^m via the non-increasing rearrangement x_p. The line states that dominance on 𝒫^m and 𝒰^m is majorization.
* **Compositions (line 73).** λ : [m] → ℕ, with 𝒰^m_n = {λ ∈ 𝒰^m : |λ| = n}. The line has two harmless typos: "with k non-negative parts" should be m, and "we say λ is a partition of n" should refer to 𝒫^m.
* **Dominance on 𝒰^m (line 75).** λ ⊴ μ iff λ_p ⊴ μ_p.
* **Concatenation (line 77).** (λμ)(i) = λ(i) for i ∈ [A], and μ(i − A) after that.
* **Conjecture 1.5 (line 79).** "For all integer A,a,B,b with A ≥ B ≥ 0 and a ≥ b ≥ 0 there is an injection γ : 𝒰^A_b × 𝒰^B_a ↪ 𝒰^A_a × 𝒰^B_b such that for all (λ1,μ1) … (λ2,μ2) = γ((λ1,μ1)) … satisfies λ1μ1 ⊵ λ2μ2."

**MathDB literal** (`raw/332037.jsonl`, the single line). It states the same quantifier ranges, the same domain and codomain order, "compositions of n with m non-negative parts", concatenation, and "dominance order induced by the non-increasing rearrangements" with λ1μ1 ⊵ λ2μ2. It matches the source exactly. The MathDB title says "on compositions"; that is a label only.

**Orientation, confirmed independently:**

* Lemma 2.1(iii) (lines 91–103) says that if λ ⊵ μ then p_λ ≤ p_μ for TN₂ sequences p.
* Theorem 3.1 (lines 193–199) needs p_{λ1μ1} ≤ p_{λ2μ2}, to conclude (p^A)_b(p^B)_a ≤ (p^A)_a(p^B)_b. So the source must dominate the image, which is the orientation used.
* Line 198 contains a sign typo ("this means that p_{λ1μ1} ≥ p_{λ2μ2}"), immediately followed by the correct "≤". The author-hosted Order manuscript has fixed it: its text lines 376–377 read "Lemma 3 (iii) implies p_{λ1μ1} ≤ p_{λ2μ2}".
* As a contrast check, under the *reversed* orientation the maximum matching at (3, 20, 2, 19) is only 4050 of 4410 (`logs/consistency.log`). The orientation therefore matters, and the candidate uses the source's orientation.

**Do the source's own results settle or contradict the conjecture? No.**

* Theorem 1.4 (line 68) is proved independently by a Cauchy–Binet-type induction (lines 169–191). Line 231 calls the conjecture something that "would also follow … if it were true".
* The source proves no special case of the conjecture.
* There is no contradiction. The refutation's Hall violator is the indicator of a down-set, which is not a product weight p_λ. I checked directly that the k = 2 inequality of Theorem 1.4 still holds at (3, 20, 2, 19) for Poisson 1/n!, Binomial C(40, n), geometric 2^{-n}, constant and C(5, n) weights (`logs/consistency.log`). Ratios include 0.667, 0.604 and 0.955; for C(5, n) both sides are 0.

**Edge cases.** For B = 0 or A = 0, the sets 𝒰^0_n are {()} or ∅, so those cases hold trivially. The counterexample has A = 3 ≥ B = 2 ≥ 0 and a = 20 ≥ b = 19 ≥ 0, which is in scope.

## E. Significance and exclusion screen: QUALIFIED_PASS

**Not an excluded type:**

* **Not a source procedure** and not a first-catalogue-entry find. The source gives no procedure or catalogue. The counterexample required an exact Hall or max-flow search, and the conjecture survives every case with A ≤ 10, a + b ≤ 16 and every (3, 2) case with a + b ≤ 38.
* **Not a source-ingredient bridge or routine corollary.** It contradicts no source theorem: Theorem 1.4 and the product inequality remain true, as checked numerically above.
* **Not a reading artefact.** The orientation and quantifiers are verified, and the reversed reading fails much more grossly.

**Family check.**

* 366371 (Keller–Petrov ordinal spaces embedding in ℝ) shares only the word "majorization". It has a different object, different authors and a different mechanism.
* The 338258 Riordan family is about Riordan *graphs*. Smyth's published Theorem 2 concerns Riordan matrices, but the refuted statement is a combinatorial injection on compositions, not a Riordan-graph property.
* So it is not a variant of a credited family.

**Strengths.**

* It is an exact, small, mechanically checkable certificate: 8 generators, two counts.
* It decides an explicit conjecture from a refereed journal paper, with the retention caveat in §D.
* It kills the source's proposed combinatorial route to Theorem 1.4 (k = 2). The infinite family pattern (computed, not proved) adds interest.

**Weaknesses.**

* The conjecture is low-profile: no citing work discusses it.
* It is not load-bearing, because the theorem it would imply is proved by other means.
* The witness was found by computer search.

These match earlier credited small-counterexample refutations, so I judge the standard met with modest significance.
