## 320294: complete source-based reduction, excluded as routine

The source's identity is a(bc)=a(cb). The algebra is not associative; the objects are multilinear bracketed words modulo the congruence generated by that identity. Do not confuse this with the different convention (ab)c=(ac)b sometimes also called right commutativity.

Lemma 16 of Bremner–Peresi, with its proof, says that every right factor may be treated as a fully commutative nonassociative monomial. Algorithm 17 then decomposes a word uniquely as a right-commutative left factor times a commutative right factor. Examples 22, 25, and 29 were read, including the degree-five total 525 and the displayed types and symmetries.

A full counting argument is as follows. Forget left/right order at every binary node of a word and distinguish its leftmost leaf. This unordered leaf-labelled full binary tree together with its distinguished leaf is unchanged by a(bc)=a(cb): the swap occurs strictly off the path to the leftmost leaf. Conversely, orient the path from the root to the distinguished leaf so that its next edge is always the left edge. All the branches attached on the right can be oriented arbitrarily, and Lemma 16 identifies every such choice. Recursing along the distinguished path proves that the fibres are exactly the right-commutativity classes. Alternatively the inverse is the source's recursive left factor/right commutative factor normal form. No extra associativity or rotations are being imposed.

Let c_n count unordered rooted full binary trees with n distinct labelled leaves. The one-leaf tree gives c_1=1. Delete the leaf labelled n and suppress its parent. Conversely insert that parent on one of the edges of a tree with n-1 leaves, including the edge above its root, and attach the new leaf. There are 2n-3 slots: a full binary tree with n-1 leaves has 2n-4 ordinary edges and one stem. These operations are inverse even when the parent was the root. Thus c_n=(2n-3)c_(n-1) and c_n=(2n-3)!!. Marking any of the n leaves gives

    dim FRC_n = n (2n-3)!! = n (2n-2)! / (2^(n-1) (n-1)!).

The free vector space modulo differences of equivalent words has the classes as a basis: mapping each word to its class gives one direction, and choosing one representative per class gives an inverse; this includes the algebraic semantic bridge. It holds in every field for the right-commutativity quotient itself.

This closes the literal formula, including n=1, but is an explicit application of the printed normal form and the classical binary-tree insertion count. I judge it excluded by the instruction against routine source/classical bridges. Shortness is not the reason. No independent significance review or worldwide novelty clearance has occurred, and there is no formalization referral. ## 318976: complete arithmetic mechanism already public in 2012

The source counts permutations whose *minimum* alphabet size is N, not all permutations realizable on an N-letter alphabet. The difference is material. The source's construction of canonical realizing words, examples at n=3,4, and the minimum-alphabet interpretation were read. The body is the SIAM 2009 preprint; the version-of-record body was not separately obtained.

The initial idea of using value complementation proves divisibility by two only. Inverting or cyclically moving a permutation does not automatically preserve its minimum shift alphabet, so a guessed free S_3 action would leave an unproved semantic bridge. Passing instead through the printed formula yields a complete proof, but it is precisely the proof published by Michael Wijaya on 17 September 2012, crediting the workshop participants and Sergi Elizalde. The public article's theorem and both lemma proofs were read in the HTML, including formula image alt text. URL and content hash are in sources/shift-prior.html.receipt.json.

For clarity the complete arithmetic is recorded here. Put psi_M(t)=sum_(d|t) mu(d) M^(t/d). For t>=3, 6 divides psi_M(t). Modulo 2, M is 0 or 1, giving zero by sum_(d|t) mu(d)=0. Modulo 3, M is 0,1,-1. Only -1 needs care. For odd t>1, psi_(-1)(t)=-sum mu(d)=0. For 4|t, squarefree divisors leave even exponents, so it equals sum mu(d)=0. For t=2u with u odd>1, pairing d|u with 2d gives 2 sum_(d|u) mu(d)=0. The exceptional t=2 is excluded.

The source formula is

    a_(n,N) = sum_(i=0)^(N-2) (-1)^i binom(n,i) B_(n,N-i),
    B_(n,M) = (M-2) M^(n-2) + sum_(t=1)^(n-1) psi_M(t) M^(n-t-1).

Terms t>=3 vanish modulo 6. The remaining expression, using psi_M(1)=M and psi_M(2)=M^2-M, is

    (M-2)M^(n-2) + M^(n-1) + (M^2-M)M^(n-3)
      = 3(M-1)M^(n-2).

For n>=3 this is divisible by 6 because M(M-1) is even. Every B term is divisible by 6, hence so is the outer integer linear combination. N>n causes no exception to the formula's interpretation (there are no permutations requiring that many letters). This proof is known, not a candidate. Even an independently invented bijection would not earn credit for resolving an already resolved question.

## 320412: genuine remaining full-scope gap, parked

Source conventions are F_0=F_1=1, F_(j+2)=F_(j+1)+F_j, A_i=floor(phi i), B_i=A_i+i. A_i,B_i are the Wythoff losing pairs, NOT the eight families in Theorem 2. W* subtracts either (A_j,B_j) or its reversal, for j>=1, when both coordinates remain nonnegative. Every axis position is terminal and losing. The source's Conjecture 1 (pp.9–10), definitions, Theorem 1, Theorem 2, Lemma 4 and the case-by-case proof pp.10–12 were read. Table 1 p.30 was rendered and visually inspected. Figure 1 itself was not visually inspected; its caption was read. No complete figure-family exclusion is claimed.

First attempt: show that every legal option leaves at least one coordinate outside the lower Wythoff sequence, so Theorem 1(ii) makes it winning. The source uses Fibonacci zero-parity for several small offsets. It fails as a uniform proof: its other cases need explicit second moves to known losing positions, so zero-parity alone does not cover all options.

Second attempt: use Lemma 4 to discard options with coordinate ratio exceeding phi, then match the remaining options by a second legal subtraction to an earlier Fibonacci-family losing position. The source's offset-4 proof uses the exceptional move (2,1) followed by a move to (3,3); offset-9 needs three explicit targets, including (6,6), (3,3), and an earlier Fibonacci point. The offsets in Conjecture 1 grow with n, so the number of exceptional options is not uniformly bounded by these printed cases. No exhaustive carry-template recurrence was proved. A claim that all remainders have a terminal move would be false in the source's own nonterminal cases.

Third attempt: induct on n while allowing both conjectured families as targets. To close it one needs, for EVERY admissible (n,i) and EVERY legal j and orientation, either (a) a source-proved direct winning criterion for the option, or (b) a second move to a source-proved losing point or a strictly earlier induction point. The target must be nonnegative, the second difference exactly a Wythoff pair, and the induction measure strictly decrease. Merely matching digit suffixes establishes none of the missing totality claim. Those are the precise remaining lemmas, not a ready finite certificate.

At n=5 the first-family points (55,85),(55,80),(55,77) are rows 69,68,67 respectively; these are printed examples, not new witnesses. The other source theorem already includes offset 9. No claim is made about a first unprinted case, and no finite search was executed.
