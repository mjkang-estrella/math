## Complete ordinary proof and hypothesis audit

Let V = {a,b,c,A,B,C} and E = {ab,bc,ca,aA,bB,cC}. All labels are distinct. Edges are unordered two-element sets; there are no loops or multiple edges. The triangle connects a,b,c, and each uppercase leaf is joined to its corresponding lowercase vertex. Thus G is connected, has no isolated vertices, n=6, q=6, and E is nonempty. A leaf of degree one cannot lie on a simple cycle. Consequently its only simple cycle, up to starting point and orientation, is abc. In particular there is no pair of vertex-disjoint odd cycles.

Every maximal independent set contains exactly one element of each of {a,A}, {b,B}, {c,C}: both cannot occur because of the pendant edge, and if neither occurred the corresponding leaf could be added. At most one lowercase vertex can occur because a,b,c form a clique. Thus the complete list of maximal independent sets is {A,B,C}, {a,B,C}, {A,b,C}, {A,B,c}. Each listed set is independent and maximal, because every excluded vertex has its paired neighbor in the set. Taking complements gives all inclusion-minimal vertex covers: {a,b,c}, {A,b,c}, {a,B,c}, {a,b,C}. These all have cardinality three. The complement correspondence is exact: failure to cover an edge is precisely containment of that edge in the complement, and deleting a cover vertex corresponds to adding an independent-set vertex. Hence G is unmixed, alpha(G)=3, tau(G)=3, and is very well-covered. This is a proof and explicit list, not a computed enumeration.

Let G1,G2,G3 be the induced subgraphs on {a,A}, {b,B}, {c,C}. Each is exactly an edge. Their vertex sets partition V, and tau(G)=3=1+1+1=sum tau(Gi). Section 2 therefore makes them a tau-reduction. Definition 3.16 requires the first s-1 parts to be edges and the last part to be an edge or C3,C5,C7. Here s=3=ceiling(6/2) and the last part is an edge. The definition's strong condition explicitly has this edge case as a disjunct; the neighborhood/odd-cycle condition belongs to the other branch. Nothing requires the whole graph to be bipartite or to have odd order.

Property (P), although not an extra conjunct of Definition 3.16, is also satisfied by every pendant edge. For aA, an arbitrary edge Az' forces z'=a. If az is any edge, then zz'=za is that same edge and belongs to E. Reversing the orientation gives the same argument. This applies identically to bB and cC. In particular it checks the premise needed if Proposition 2.3 or 3.20 is used; it does not incorrectly assert (P) for triangle edges.

For normality, the 2010 source defines incidence columns v_e=e_u+e_v for graph edges; it assumes no isolated vertices globally and a connected graph in section 3 and Theorems 3.2/3.3. All hold here. Theorem 3.2 says the system x>=0, xA<=1 has the integer rounding property iff the subgraph induced by the vertices of every pair of vertex-disjoint odd cycles is connected. The pair condition is vacuous here. Theorem 3.3(d) then gives normality of K[t,x_1t,...,x_nt,x^{v_1}t,...,x^{v_q}t]. The exact generator identification is t; at,bt,ct,At,Bt,Ct; abt,bct,cat,aAt,bBt,cCt, with labels interpreted as independent polynomial variables. Thus it is normality of precisely S, not of the edge ring alone. In Theorem 2.1's notation, the integer vectors below an edge incidence vector are 0, its two unit vectors, and that edge vector; no isolated vertices ensures all six units occur. This independently checks the down-set ring identification. Both sources take K to be a field with no characteristic, algebraic-closure or infinitude condition. Theorem 3.2's proof and 3.3's proof explicitly pass through the extended Rees algebra and Proposition 3.1 to the same S; no unjustified transfer between unrelated rings is used.

Theorem 3.14 (2021, printed pages 6-7), read with its proof and preceding propositions, states: if S is normal and n is even, S is Gorenstein iff G is an unmixed bipartite graph. Bipartiteness is a conclusion on the necessary side, not an ambient hypothesis. The proof supposes a nonbipartite graph, chooses an odd k-cycle, obtains canonical generator degree n/2+1 and a matching reduction from Gorensteinness, and produces (1_C,(k-1)/2) in NB, contradicting the degree bound |u|<=2d in Lemma 3.2. The reverse implication uses Proposition 2.7 for bipartite graphs. This proof context confirms the scope of the statement. Our S is normal and n=6 is even. G cannot be bipartite because alternating colors around abc force a and c to have both the same and opposite colors. The necessary implication of 3.14 therefore gives S not Gorenstein. We have every right-side conjunct of Conjecture 3.24 and the negation of its left side. This disproves the converse and hence the exact biconditional over every field K.

## Independent check of the conjectural bridge

An explicit obstruction also identifies where 3.23 fails. Write M for the sum of the three pendant-edge exponent generators; M=(1,1,1,1,1,1;3), in coordinate order a,b,c,A,B,C;t. Put w=M+(e_a+e_b+e_c;2)=(2,2,2,1,1,1;5). The last summand equals the triangle edge bc plus the singleton a, so w is in NB. It also equals half the sum of the three triangle-edge generators plus half the t-generator. All six spatial coordinates of w are positive and this latter representation has strictly positive t-generator coefficient, so source Lemma 3.3 makes w interior.

In any integer representation of w, each pendant coefficient is at most one, from the leaf coordinate. Their sum has maximum three, attained by the displayed representation. A representation maximizing that sum must therefore use each pendant edge once. After subtracting M its remaining spatial coordinates are exactly (1,1,1,0,0,0) at degree two. If lambda>0, the non-t generators would have total degree at most one and could supply at most two units of spatial degree, whereas these coordinates have spatial degree three. This is impossible. Thus every maximizing representation has lambda=0 and none is principal under Definition 3.21. This explicitly disproves 3.23 for the same valid hypotheses. It is ordinary reasoning, with no enumeration program or formal API.

The corresponding canonical-module check is w-(1,1,1,1,1,1;4)=(1,1,1,0,0,0;1), outside NB by Lemma 3.2's degree bound. Proposition 3.12 forces that putative generator if S were Gorenstein, since this G is nonbipartite and S normal. This reproduces the obstruction underlying Theorem 3.14, rather than assuming an unexplained non-Gorenstein conclusion. It is an explanatory verification of the existing source mechanism, not a separately credited result.
