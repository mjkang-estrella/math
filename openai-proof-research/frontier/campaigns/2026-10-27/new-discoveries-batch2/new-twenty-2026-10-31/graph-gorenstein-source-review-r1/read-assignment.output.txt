Let V={a,b,c,A,B,C} and E={ab,bc,ca,aA,bB,cC}. This is the triangle with one pendant leaf on each vertex. It is a finite connected simple graph with six vertices and nonempty edge set. Its only cycle is abc: a degree-one vertex cannot be in a cycle, and the subgraph on a,b,c is exactly a triangle. Thus there are no two vertex-disjoint odd cycles.

Normality bridge (fully paid, no analogy between rings): Dupont–Rentería-Márquez–Villarreal, arXiv:0803.1208v2 (29 April 2010), Theorem 3.2 gives the integer rounding property for the connected graph incidence system x>=0,xA<=1 iff every pair of vertex-disjoint odd cycles has connected induced union. Here the universal pair condition is vacuous. Theorem 3.3(d) says precisely that K[t,x_1t,...,x_nt,x^{v_1}t,...,x^{v_q}t] is normal. Its generators agree term by term with the S above: the six singleton generators, six listed squarefree edge generators, and t. This is normality of the required ring over the field of the sources, not normality of an unrelated edge/cover ideal.

Unmixedness bridge: In every maximal independent set F, exactly one of a,A is present. At most one is possible because aA is an edge. If neither were present, A could be added since its only neighbor is a, contradicting maximality. The same argument applies to b,B and c,C. Consequently every maximal independent set has exactly three vertices; at least one exists, e.g. {A,B,C}. Complements give a bijection between maximal independent sets and inclusion-minimal vertex covers: a complement is a cover exactly when its complementary set is independent, and minimality is exactly maximality. Therefore every minimal vertex cover has size three and tau(G)=3. This proves unmixedness in the source's exact sense.

Reduction bridge: the induced subgraphs on {a,A},{b,B},{c,C} are edges; their vertex sets partition V, and tau(G)=3=1+1+1 is the sum of their cover numbers. They are a tau-reduction. Definition 3.16 calls it a ceil(n/2)-tau-reduction when all but the last part are edges and the last is an edge or C3,C5,C7. Here all are edges and ceil(6/2)=3. Its definition of strong is a disjunction: it holds automatically if the last part is an edge. There is no unfulfilled odd-cycle-neighborhood condition in this branch. The same proof applies to the other two pendant edges.

G contains abc and cannot be bipartite: assigning opposite colors along ab and bc gives a,c the same color, contradicting ca. Thus S is not Gorenstein.
