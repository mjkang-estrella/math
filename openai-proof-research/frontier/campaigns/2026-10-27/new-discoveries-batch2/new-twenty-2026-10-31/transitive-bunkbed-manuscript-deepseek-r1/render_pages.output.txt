Abstract

We prove the bunkbed conjecture in Hollom’s E7 model for the transitive tournament T(n): for every n and all ver-
tices u, v, the probability that (u,0) reaches (v,0) is at least the probability that (u,0) reaches (v,1). The proof reveals
the random tokens column by column, compresses the revealed preﬁx to the pair of reachable-copy counts, and pre-
serves a reﬂected mass dominance by a paired-sum induction. Transitivity is used through the total order, which
prevents a path from returning to a smaller index, and through completeness, which makes every earlier vertex a
predecessor of the new one. The endpoint is kernel checked in Lean 4.34.1 with mathlib revision
d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612; the reported axioms are exactly propext, Classical.choice and
Quot.sound. No acceptance, peer review, priority or worldwide novelty is claimed.

1. Introduction

The bunkbed conjecture concerns two copies, called layers, of a graph, joined by vertical edges. In the undirected
form, with independent retention of edges, it asserts that for all vertices u and v the probability that the layer-0
copies of u and v are connected is at least the probability that the layer-0 copy of u is connected to the layer-1 copy
of v. The conjecture was introduced by Kasteleyn in 1985 and is now known to be false in general.

Hollom studied directed versions. In the model E7 of the arXiv version, renamed D7 in the published journal ver-
sion at the same numbered locations, a directed graph is doubled; each horizontal arc is retained independently in
each layer, while each vertex carries one vertical token that enables both directions at once. Hollom’s Theorem 1.6
shows that BBC(D7(D)) fails for suitable directed multigraphs. He then asks, in Question 6.3, whether
BBC(D7(D)) holds for all acyclic digraphs, and writes that it "seems unclear ... whether the answer ... should be
positive or negative", adding that the problem is "far more amenable to inductive techniques than the case of graphs
with cycles, but actually performing this induction seems far from trivial".

Conjecture 6.4 then singles out the transitive tournament: it asserts that BBC(D7(T(n))) holds for T(n), the transitive
tournament on n vertices. Hollom calls this "one case that does seem approachable, though was found by the author
to be more difﬁcult than expected", and remarks that the techniques used for the undirected complete graph "do not
directly apply, in short because Tn does not have the same symmetries as Kn". The undirected complete graph case
is due to van Hintum and Lammers.

The general acyclic question was later answered negatively by Przybylowski: he constructs a simple acyclic di-
rected graph with distinguished vertices u and v such that P(u- → v-) < P(u- → v+) (Theorem 1.2), resolving the
conjectures of Leander and Hollom. His graph has missing adjacencies and is not a tournament, so it does not con-
tradict Conjecture 6.4. Tournaments are singled out because the order is total: every earlier vertex is a predecessor
of every later one. In the proof below, this completeness is exactly what makes the pair of reachable-copy counts a
sufﬁcient statistic. The MathDB record 382297 states the same conjecture for T(n) in the E7 model and records it as
open.

2. Model and theorem

Let T(n) be the transitive tournament with vertex set {0,...,n-1}, in which i → j is an arc exactly when i < j. The
bunkbed has vertices (i,l) with i in {0,...,n-1} and layer l in {0,1}. There are two kinds of random token:
     • For every arc i → j and every layer l there is one fair horizontal token h(i,j,l). If h(i,j,l) is retained, the arc (i,l)
     → (j,l) is present.
     • For every index i there is one fair vertical token z(i). If z(i) is retained, both arcs (i,0) → (i,1) and (i,1) → (i,0)
     are present. The two directions are enabled together by the single token.
An outcome is an assignment of 0 or 1 to every token, and all 2ˆ(n(n-1)+n) = 2ˆ(n*n) outcomes are equally likely.
Reachability is the existence of a directed path; formally it is the reﬂexive-transitive closure of the enabled-arc rela-
tion, so the zero-length path counts and every vertex reaches itself. We write (u,0) → (v,s) when (v,s) is reachable
from (u,0).

Theorem 2.1. For every n ≥ 0 and all u, v in {0,...,n-1},
P((u,0) → (v,0)) ≥ P((u,0) → (v,1)).
Equivalently, in the ﬁnite uniform model,
|{ω : Reach n ω (u,false) (v,true)}| ≤ |{ω : Reach n ω (u,false) (v,false)}|,
where both sets are counted among the 2ˆ(n*n) outcomes; the common denominator cancels. For n = 0 there are no
vertices u and v, so the statement is vacuous.

3. Proof

Fix u. Since every arc weakly increases the index, a directed path from (u,0) never visits an index below u; in partic-
ular, if v < u then both events are impossible. We therefore reveal the tokens in increasing index order and track
reachability of the revealed preﬁx.
