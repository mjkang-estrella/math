## Independent mathematical audit and complete ordinary proof

1. Diameter and children. Every non-singleton totally bounded ultrametric subspace B has positive ATTAINED finite diameter R. Choose u,v in B with a=d(u,v)>0. Partition B into finitely many disjoint open a/2-balls, using total boundedness and recentering in B. For distinct classes every cross distance is the distance of their representatives, by the strong triangle inequality. Within one class all distances are <a/2. Since the chosen pair is in different classes and has distance a, the maximum over finitely many representative distances dominates all within-class distances and equals diam B. This also proves boundedness without any compactness assumption. The relation d(x,y)<R has finitely many classes, at least two: representatives of distinct classes are R-separated and a finite R/2-net bounds their number. These classes are B_R(x) inside B. If B is an open ball of X, this is an ambient open ball contained in B, by ultrametric recentering. Each non-singleton child has its own attained diameter strictly below R; singletons have diameter zero. No supremum is silently assumed to be attained because of total boundedness in a general metric space.

2. Hierarchy and finite depth. X is bounded, hence is an open ball of sufficiently large radius. Starting from X, split each non-singleton into its d<R classes. Finite branching gives finitely many nodes at each finite level. Nested children strictly decrease and have unique parents, so these finite levels form a rooted acyclic graph H. Fix x and epsilon>0 with B_epsilon(x)={x}. Every non-singleton ancestor containing x has diameter >=epsilon: it contains some y different from x, and d(x,y)>=epsilon. At each strict split choose a point from a discarded child. Points discarded at different stages have mutual distance equal to the diameter at the earlier split, hence >=epsilon. A finite epsilon/2-net forbids more than finitely many such stages. Therefore {x} is reached at finite depth. There is no assertion of a uniform depth bound over X. Infinite descending branches with empty intersection in X cause no difficulty and add no vertices.

Every ambient open ball A is in H. Choose x in A. At a hierarchy node B containing A, if A meets two children, recenter A at a point of A intersect B. Its radius must exceed R=diam B, since it contains a cross-child pair at distance R. It then contains all of B, so A=B. Otherwise A lies in a unique child. Continuing along x's FINITE chain ends at {x}, so equality must occur by then. Conversely every constructed node is an ambient open ball. Thus H has precisely the open balls as vertices, leaves precisely the singleton balls, and every vertex has a finite path to X. No completion points are inserted. For a singleton X this hierarchy is one vertex and the argument below is replaced by the one-vertex tree labeled zero.

3. Centers and simultaneous contractions. Assume every open ball is a centered sphere in X. For a non-singleton B write B={c} union {z in X:d(c,z)=r}. Some z differs from c, so r<=diam B; the ultrametric inequality applied through c gives diam B<=r. Hence r=R>0, and c's d<R class is exactly {c}. Conversely a singleton child {c} of B implies B is the centered sphere of radius R in the AMBIENT X: distances from c to other children equal R, and any point outside B has distance at least the defining open-ball radius, which is >R by attainment. Thus there is no ambient-versus-subspace gap.

Choose one singleton child {c(B)} at every internal node B. An individual leaf has exactly one parent, so the chosen edges B--{c(B)} are pairwise vertex-disjoint. Internal and leaf vertices cannot coincide in this non-singleton case. Contract ALL these edges. Each resulting fiber is either {B,{c(B)}} or an unselected singleton-leaf vertex. Every internal node was matched, so fibers are in bijection with points of X. Define q({x})=x and q(B)=c(B). Use the noncollapsed images of H-edges as the edges of T on the set X itself, eliminating loop images.

Fibers are connected and of size at most two. Projection of a finite H-path gives a finite T-walk, hence connectivity. In a tree, intersection of a simple path with a connected fiber is an interval; otherwise the unique path between two fiber vertices would leave that fiber. Consequently projecting a simple path and deleting consecutive repetitions gives a simple quotient path. A finite quotient cycle would lift, using the internal fiber edges, to a cycle in a finite subtree of H. Equivalently contracting the finitely many relevant fiber edges in that finite subtree preserves acyclicity. Parallel edges cannot occur: two different H-edges joining the same connected fibers would themselves yield a finite cycle. T is a simple ordinary tree on exactly X. Infinite simultaneous contraction introduces neither an infinite-path notion nor end vertices.

4. Labels and maxima, including endpoint labels. Assign l(c(B))=diam B; assign zero to unselected points. The assignment is single-valued by the matching property. For x!=y the two finite leaf-to-root paths have a least common ancestor B. Their first distinct children are separated by diam B, so d(x,y)=diam B. The H-path between {x} and {y} passes through B. Its projected path contains q(B), with label diam B, even if q(B) is an endpoint or the selected singleton child lies off the original path. Every nonzero quotient label encountered is diam D for an internal D in a fiber meeting this path. If the meeting vertex is a leaf endpoint selected by D, its unique parent D also lies on the original path. Thus every such D is B or a descendant of B, and diam D<=diam B. This explicitly closes the possible endpoint-label objection. Therefore max of l over the finite T-path is exactly diam B=d(x,y). Distinct endpoints have positive distance, so edge-label nondegeneracy follows as well. Diagonal equality is stipulated zero, not l(x).

5. Converse. Suppose a labeled ordinary tree on X generates d. If u,v are in A=B_r(a) and w is on their path, its u--w subpath has maximum at most d(u,v), with the case w=u trivial. Ultrametricity gives d(u,v)<r and then d(a,w)<=max(d(a,u),d(u,w))<r. Thus A is graph-convex. If A is non-singleton, step 1 supplies u,v in A realizing R=diam A. Their finite path has some vertex c with label R. Convexity puts c in A. For every z in A other than c the c--z path contains c, so R<=d(c,z)<=R. Since u,v lie in A, ultrametric recentering gives R=d(u,v)<r. Any z outside A with d(c,z)=R would satisfy d(a,z)<r, a contradiction. Therefore A={c} union {z in X:d(c,z)=R}. Singleton {c} is a centered sphere of any positive radius strictly below an isolation radius; if X itself is singleton any positive radius works. This completes the full equivalence.

Choice and degeneracies: all statements above are ordinary classical mathematics. Finite nets, discarded representatives and centers can be chosen using choice. In fact H has finite levels and is countable, and X injects into its singleton leaves; hence the hypotheses already force X at most countable. One may use countable choice among finite center sets, or ordinary AC; no enumeration of X is an extra hypothesis. The unique one-point tree, a two-point space, diameter-zero nodes, empty edge sets, nonuniform isolation radii and unbounded hierarchy depth are all covered. Nonempty is essential for the formulation; the proof does not silently add an empty case.

Corrections to r342's exposition: make the finite cover disjoint by the ultrametric ball equivalence; explicitly note all non-singleton ancestors of x have diameter >=epsilon; recenter an arbitrary ball before the two-child argument; justify quotient simple paths by connected fibers; and check endpoint labels as above. These are ordinary clarifications/repairs, not counterexamples or changes of hypotheses. No residual ordinary correctness gap was found.

The tempting local-finiteness bridge is false. On positive integers set d(i,j)=max(1/i,1/j) for i!=j. Every i is isolated, the tail is contained in arbitrarily small balls and a finite prefix completes a finite net, but X itself is an infinite bounded open ball. Thus metric local finiteness would imply finite X here and cannot be inferred. The representing GRAPH H being locally finite is a different fact.
