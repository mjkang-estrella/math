ID 323010 Linear singular-index conjecture for unsatisfiable hitting clause-sets 
 Let $\mathcal{U}\mathcal{HIT}_{\!\delta=k}$ be the class of unsatisfiable hitting clause-sets of deficiency $k$. Let $\operatorname{singind}(F)$ denote the singular index of $F$, and let $\operatorname{varsing}(F)$ be the set of singular variables of $F$. **Linear singular-index conjecture.** For every $k \in \mathbb{N}$ there are $a \in \mathbb{N}$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}$ such that, for all $F \in \mathcal{U}\mathcal{HIT}_{\!\delta=k}$ with $\operatorname{singind}(F) \geq a$,

$$
\lvert\operatorname{varsing}(F)\rvert \geq \alpha \cdot \operatorname{singind}(F).
$$

The conjecture asks for a quantitative relation between the singular index and the number of singular variables. The source presents it as an open question in its discussion of singular tuples; no resolution is supplied.

ID 323537 Murty–Simon conjecture on diameter-two edge-critical graphs 
 Let $G$ be a simple graph on $n$ vertices. Write $E(G)$ for its edge set, and call $G$ **diameter-$2$ edge-critical** when $\operatorname{diam}(G)=2$ and $\operatorname{diam}(G-e)>\operatorname{diam}(G)$ for every edge $e\in E(G)$. Let $K_{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor,\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}$ denote the complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes $\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor$ and $\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil$. **Murty–Simon conjecture.** If $G$ is a diameter-$2$ edge-critical graph on $n$ vertices, then

$$
|E(G)|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
$$

Moreover, equality holds if and only if

$$
G\cong K_{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor,\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}.
$$

The conjecture asserts both the extremal edge bound and the uniqueness of the extremal graph, building on the observation that known minimal graphs of diameter two have at most $\left\lfloor n^2/4\right\rfloor$ edges. Its resolution status is not specified in the supplied text.

ID 323999 The weak virtual periodicity conjecture for maximal coclass trees 
 Let $r\in\mathbb N_0$ and let $K$ be an arbitrary field. For an algebra $A$ in $\mathcal G_{r,K}$, write $\mathcal T(A)$ for the rooted subgraph consisting of all paths starting at $A$. A maximal coclass tree is a coclass tree containing a unique maximal infinite path and satisfying the maximality condition from the source. Let $\mathcal T$ be such a tree, with maximal infinite path $A_1\to A_2\to\cdots$, and let $\overline{\mathcal T}(A)$ denote its unlabelled version rooted at $A$. **Weak virtual periodicity conjecture.** There exist positive integers $l$ and $k$ such that

$$
\overline{\mathcal T}(A_l)\cong\overline{\mathcal T}(A_{l+k}).
$$

This conjecture predicts eventual periodicity of the unlabelled maximal coclass trees and would imply finite width. Its resolution is not supplied here.

ID 324375 The algebraic-extension characterization via Stallings graph coverings 
 Let $\mathbf{F}$ be a free group, and let $H\leq J\leq\mathbf{F}$. For a basis $X$ of $\mathbf{F}$, write $H\leq_{{\scriptscriptstyle \overset{\twoheadrightarrow}{X}}}J$ when the canonical morphism from the Stallings core graph $\Gamma_X(H)$ to $\Gamma_X(J)$ is onto. An extension $H\leq J$ is **algebraic** when $J$ is not contained in any proper free factor of $\mathbf{F}$ containing $H$. **Algebraic-extension conjecture.** If $H\leq J\leq\mathbf{F}$ and $H\leq_{{\scriptscriptstyle \overset{\twoheadrightarrow}{X}}}J$ for every basis $X$ of $\mathbf{F}$, then $J$ is an algebraic extension of $H$. The converse was known: every algebraic extension has the covering property for every basis. The conjecture was raised by Martino, Valiunas and Ventura, but the paper explains that the original conjecture fails; this candidate is therefore refuted.

ID 324805 The Newton polygon conjecture for tree coefficients 
 Let $T$ be a rooted tree, let $\sympawn_T$ be its coefficient, and let $\square_T$ be the Newton polygon of the numerator of $\sympawn_T$. Write $\#T$ for the number of vertices of $T$, and call the greatest distance from the root the height of $T$; the number of vertices at height $i$ is the number of vertices of height $i$ in $T$. **The Newton polygon conjecture.** The upper boundary of $\square_T$ is a horizontal line corresponding to the coefficient of degree $\#T$ in $x$. The lower boundary is a horizontal line corresponding to the constant coefficient in $x$. The right boundary is a line of slope $1$. The left boundary is a sequence of segments of slope $1/i$ for every $i$ between $1$ and the height of $T$. The segment of slope $1/i$ has vertical height equal to the number of vertices of height $i$ in $T$.

ID 325072 The one-third–two-thirds conjecture for antimatroids 
 An **antimatroid** is a set system whose basic words are the orderings generated by its feasible sets. For an antimatroid $\mathcal{A}$ with more than one basic word, choose a basic word uniformly at random and, for elements $x,y\in\mathcal{A}$, let $\Pr[x<y]$ denote the probability that $x$ occurs earlier than $y$. Define

$$
\delta(\mathcal{A})=\max_{x,y\in\mathcal{A}}\min\left\{\Pr[x<y],\Pr[y<x]\right\}.
$$

**The one-third–two-thirds conjecture for antimatroids.** Every antimatroid $\mathcal{A}$ with more than one basic word satisfies

$$
\delta(\mathcal{A})\ge\frac13.
$$

Equivalently, every such antimatroid contains a pair $x,y$ with $\frac13\le\Pr[x<y]\le\frac23$. This generalizes the classical one-third–two-thirds conjecture for finite non-total partial orders. The claim has no counterexamples among antimatroids on six or fewer elements, but remains open in general.

ID 325100 The complete-partite graph cycle-space conjecture for K^{s,s-1} 
 Let $\mathrm{K}^{\widehat{s},s-1}$ denote the graph described in the paper, and let $\mathrm{Z}_1(\mathrm{K}^{\widehat{s},s-1};\mathbb{F}_2)$ be its cycle space over $\mathbb{F}_2$. Let $\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{K}^{\widehat{s},s-1})$ denote the set of Hamilton circuits of this graph, and let $\langle\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{K}^{\widehat{s},s-1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_2}$ be their $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear span.

**The complete-partite graph cycle-space conjecture.** For every $s\geq 4$,

$$
\mathrm{Z}_1(\mathrm{K}^{\widehat{s},s-1};\mathbb{F}_2) = \langle\mathcal{H}(\mathrm{K}^{\widehat{s},s-1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_2}.
$$

The claim would provide an infinite family of graphs with a degree-$2$ vertex in which every cycle is a symmetric difference of Hamilton circuits, showing that the corresponding implication in the paper does not hold without additional hypotheses. The source presents this as seeming to hold based on examples and calculations, rather than as an established result.

ID 325429 The higher-dimensional bootstrapping conjecture for lattice walks 
 Let ${\mathfrak{S}}$ be a set of small steps in $\mathbb{Z}^d$ for some dimension $d$, and let $R$ be an open region in $\mathbb{Z}^d$ bounded by rational hyperplanes. Define

$$
{\mathfrak{T}}={\mathfrak{S}}\cup\{s_0\},\qquad {\mathfrak{U}}={\mathfrak{S}}\cup\{s_1,s_2\}.
$$

Here $s_0$ is a step away from any boundary of $R$, and $s_1,s_2$ is a pair of steps without drift towards any boundary. Let $\beta_{\mathfrak{S}}$, $\beta_{\mathfrak{T}}$, and $\beta_{\mathfrak{U}}$ denote the exponential growth of walks in $R$ on the respective step sets. **The higher-dimensional bootstrapping conjecture.** The lower bounds

$$
\beta_{\mathfrak{S}}+1\leq\beta_{\mathfrak{T}},\qquad \beta_{\mathfrak{S}}+2\leq\beta_{\mathfrak{U}}
$$

should hold, as suggested by Lemmas onestep and twostep. This proposes that the planar bootstrapping arguments extend to lattice walks in regions of arbitrary dimension bounded by rational hyperplanes.

ID 325449 The regular-word existence and uniqueness conjecture for generalized Thue–Morse sequences 
 Let $r\geqslant 2$ and let $n$ be the length of a word over a two-letter alphabet. An $r$-regular word is a word satisfying the corresponding regularity condition, and $\operatorname{PTE}(2^{r+1},2,r)$ denotes the set of words representing Prouhet–Tarry–Escott solutions with these parameters. **Regular-word existence and uniqueness conjecture.** On a two-letter alphabet, there are $r$-regular words of length $n$ if and only if

$$
n=k\cdot 2^r,\qquad k\geqslant 2.
$$

Moreover, $\operatorname{PTE}(2^{r+1},2,r)$ contains just one word: the initial segment of the Thue–Morse sequence of length $2^{r+1}$. The preceding theorem establishes existence whenever $n=k\cdot 2^r$ with $k\geqslant 2$; the conjectural content is the converse for all $r\geqslant 2$ and the asserted uniqueness of the corresponding Prouhet–Tarry–Escott word.

ID 325572 Cui–Hou switching-equivalence conjecture for skew spectra of bipartite graphs 
 Let $G$ be a bipartite graph with an orientation $\tau$, and let $S(G^\tau)$ be its skew-adjacency matrix. Write $Sp_S(G^\tau)$ for the spectrum of $S(G^\tau)$ and $Sp(G)$ for the adjacency spectrum of $G$. If $G=G(X,Y)$, its **elementary orientation** directs every edge from $X$ to $Y$. Two orientations are **switching-equivalent** if one can be obtained from the other by repeatedly reversing all arcs between a vertex subset and its complement.

**Cui–Hou conjecture.** For every orientation $\sigma$ of $G$,

$$
Sp_S(G^\sigma)=iSp(G)
$$

if and only if $\sigma$ is switching-equivalent to the elementary orientation of $G$.

The conjecture characterizes the orientations of a bipartite graph whose skew-spectrum is $i$ times its ordinary spectrum. The supplied text attributes it to Cui and Hou; no resolution is given here.

ID 325706 Signless Laplacian power-sum conjecture for bipartite graphs 
 Let $G$ be a bipartite graph with $n$ vertices. Write $Q(G)$ for its signless Laplacian matrix, let $q_1,dots,q_n$ be the eigenvalues of $Q(G)$, and define

$$
S_\alpha(G)=\sum_{i=1}^n q_i^\alpha.
$$

**Bipartite signless Laplacian power-sum conjecture.** If $\alpha>1$, then

$$
S_{\alpha}(G)\leq n^\alpha+(\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-1)\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil^\alpha+(\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil-1)\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor^\alpha,
$$

with equality if and only if $G=K_{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil}$.

This extends the established extremal result for the corresponding range of exponents in related graph classes to bipartite graphs. The conjecture concerns the sharp upper bound and its equality case for all $\alpha>1$.

ID 327560 Characterization of Kyle graphs among symmetric directed graphs 
 Let $D$ be a symmetric directed graph on $m$ vertices, with corresponding adjacency matrix

$$
\begin{pmatrix} e_{11} & e_{12} & \dots & e_{1m}\\ e_{21} & e_{21} & \dots & e_{2m}\\ \hdotsfor{4}\\ e_{m1} & e_{m2} & \ \dots & e_{mm} \end{pmatrix}.
$$

**Kyle graph characterization conjecture.** The graph $D$ is a Kyle graph if and only if all of the following conditions hold: (i) $m$ is odd and $m=2a_l+1$, where $a_l$ is determined by Ratanang's Theorem; (ii) $e_{ii}=0$ for every $i$; (iii) $e_{i(i+1)}+e_{(i+1)i}=e_{j(j+1)}+e_{(j+1)j}$ for $1\leq i,j\leq m-1$; (iv) in row $(e_{1j})$, $e_{12}\geq e_{13}\geq\cdots\geq e_{1\lfloor m/2\rfloor}=1$ and $e_{1j}=0$ for $\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\leq j\leq m$; and (v) in column $(e_{j1})$, $e_{21}=e_{31}=\cdots=e_{(\lfloor m/2\rfloor-1)1}=1$ and $e_{j1}=0$ for $\lfloor m/2\rfloor\leq j\leq m$. This is presented as an open problem concerning the characterization of Kyle graphs within the class of symmetric directed graphs; the notation $a_l$ and Ratanang's Theorem are not defined in the supplied context.

ID 328297 The Taylor-to-SD(wedge) conjecture for oriented trees 
 Let $\mathbb H$ be an oriented tree, and let $\operatorname{\mathbf{alg}}\mathbb H$ denote its associated algebra. An algebra is **Taylor** if it has a Taylor term operation, and it is $\mathrm{SD}(\wedge)$ when it has the corresponding meet-semidistributive term condition. If $\mathbb H$ is a core, then $\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbb H)$ is the associated homomorphism problem.

**The Taylor-to-SD(wedge) conjecture.** For every oriented tree $\mathbb H$, either $\operatorname{\mathbf{alg}}\mathbb H$ is not Taylor or it is $\mathrm{SD}(\wedge)$. In particular, if $\mathbb H$ is a core, then $\mathbb H$ has bounded width or $\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbb H)$ is **NP**-complete.

The paper establishes the analogous assertion for special trees, while presenting this statement as a proposed generalization to all oriented trees. Its status is therefore open.

ID 329350 Generalized M-polynomial product conjecture for graded Lie algebra components 
 Let $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z}\mathfrak g(i)$ be a $\mathbb Z$-grading of a simple Lie algebra, and let $\Delta(1)$ be the corresponding weight poset with height function ${\mathsf{ht}}$. Let $\mathfrak{An}(\Delta(1))$ denote its antichains and let $\EuScript M_{\Delta(1)}(t)$ be its M-polynomial.

**Generalized M-polynomial product conjecture.** One has

$$
\EuScript M_{\Delta(1)}(t)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta(1)}\frac{1-t^{{\mathsf{ht}}(\gamma)+1}}{1-t^{{\mathsf{ht}}(\gamma)}}.
$$

In particular,

$$
\#\mathfrak{An}(\Delta(1))=\EuScript M_{\Delta(1)}(1)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Delta(1)}\frac{{\mathsf{ht}}(\gamma)+1}{{\mathsf{ht}}(\gamma)}.
$$

The formula is proved in the abelian and extra-special cases and in some additional small product cases, but remains open for arbitrary gradings.

ID 336647 The classification conjecture for groups admitting oriented regular representations 
 An **oriented regular representation (ORR)** of a finite group $G$ is an oriented Cayley digraph on $G$ whose automorphism group is the right-regular representation of $G$. A group is **generalized dihedral** if it is a generalized dihedral group in the usual sense. Let $C_n$ denote the cyclic group of order $n$, let $Q_8$ denote the quaternion group of order $8$, and let $D_4\circ D_4$ denote the central product of two dihedral groups of order $8$.

**The ORR classification conjecture.** Every finite group $G$ admits an ORR, unless one of the following occurs: 

1. $G$ is generalized dihedral with $|G|>2$; or
2. $G$ is isomorphic to one of the eleven groups

$$
Q_8,\,C_4\times C_2,\, C_4\times C_2^2,\, C_4\times C_2^3,\, C_4\times C_2^4,\,C_3^2,\,C_3\times C_2^3,
$$

$$
\langle a,b\mid a^4=b^4=(ab)^2=(ab^{-1})^2=1\rangle,
$$

where the group has order $16$;

$$
\langle a,b,c\mid a^4=b^4=c^4=(ba)^2=(ba^{-1})^2=(bc)^2=(bc^{-1})^2=1,
$$

$$
a^2=c^2,a^c=a^{-1}, a^2=b^2\rangle,
$$

where the group has order $16$;

$$
\langle a,b,c\mid a^4=b^4=c^4=(ab)^2=(ab^{-1})^2=1,
$$

$$
(ac)^2=(ac^{-1})^2=(bc)^2=(bc^{-1})^2=a^2b^2c^2=1\rangle,
$$

where the group has order $32$; or $D_4\circ D_4$, the extraspecial group of order $32$ of plus type.

The conjecture would classify precisely the finite groups that do not admit ORRs. The paper establishes the result for all finite non-solvable groups and analyzes broad classes of groups generated by at most three elements, while the proposed classification for all finite groups remains open.

ID 349858 The even-stretch lower-bound conjecture for fault-tolerant spanners 
 Let $n$ and $f$ be positive integers, and let $k$ be an even integer. An **$f$-EFT $(2k-1)$-spanner** of a graph is an edge fault-tolerant spanner resilient to any set of at most $f$ failed edges, with stretch at most $2k-1$. **Even-stretch lower-bound conjecture.** There exist $n$-node graphs in which every $f$-EFT $(2k-1)$-spanner has

$$
\Omega\left(f^{1/2} n^{1+1/k} \right)
$$

edges. This would show that the paper's upper bound has the correct dependence on $f$ for even $k$, closing the remaining $f^{1/(2k)}$ gap to the known lower bound.

ID 351285 The local-distance conjecture for autoencoder generalization 
 Let $\mathcal{D}_{train}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{test}$ be the training and test subsets of the dataset, and let $D_{model}\circ E_{model}$ be a trained autoencoder. Near each training point, compare this map with a projection map, equivalently measuring its local distance from the identity through its Jacobian. **Local-distance conjecture.** The further the map $D_{model}\circ E_{model}$ is from a projection near the training points, the worse the mean squared error will be for the autoencoder on the test points. This proposes a relationship between local geometric deviation from a projection on training data and test-set reconstruction error; the supplied text gives the relationship as a conjecture rather than an established result.

ID 351481 Conjectural formulas for cluster variables of star graphs 
 Let $S_n$ be the star graph on $n$ vertices, with central vertex labeled by $1$, and let

$$
\mathcal{C}=\{\{3\},\{4\},\dots,\{n\},\{1,3,4,\dots,n\}\}.
$$

For a vertex subset $S$, write $Y_S$ for the associated cluster-algebra element and $[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}$. The cluster is $\mathcal{C}$, and the formulas below conjecturally describe $Y_S$ for every vertex subset $S$. **Star-graph cluster formula conjecture.** If $1\in S$, then

$$
Y_S=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{i\in S\backslash\{1,2\}}Y_i^2}{Y_{[n]\backslash\{1,2\}}}\left(\sum\limits_{i\notin S}\prod\limits_{j\ne1,2,i}Y_j\right)+\frac{Y_{[n]}}{\prod\limits_{i\in[n]\backslash S}Y_i}+\frac{Y_{[n]}}{Y_{[n]\backslash\{2\}}}\left(\sum\limits_{i\in[n]\backslash S}\frac{1}{Y_i}\right),&2\notin S,\\[2mm]
\displaystyle\frac{Y_{[n]\backslash\{2\}}}{\prod\limits_{i\in[n\backslash(S\cup\{2\})]}Y_i}+\left(\prod\limits_{i\in S\backslash\{1\}}Y_i\right)\left(\sum\limits_{j\in[n]\backslash(S\cup\{2\})}\frac{1}{Y_i}\right),&2\in S.
\end{cases}
$$

If $1\notin S$, then $S$ consists either of disconnected leaves or of a single leaf, and

$$
Y_S=\begin{cases}Y_i,&S=\{i\},\\prod\limits_{i\in S}Y_i,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$

Here the source uses $\operatorname{textrm}{if}$ and contains an apparent index mismatch in the second case, with $Y_i$ occurring inside a sum indexed by $j$; these expressions are retained mathematically as stated. The conjecture proposes explicit formulas for cluster-algebra elements associated with subsets of a star graph and is presented as a future direction for extending rooted-cluster results to other clusters. No resolution is supplied in the source.

ID 355434 The inverse conjugation conjecture for bidding games 
 Let $G$ be a bidding game, and let $\overline G$ denote its conjugate. An **inverse** of $G$ is a game $H$ such that $G+H$ is equivalent to the identity element.

**Inverse conjugation conjecture.** If a bidding game $G$ has an inverse, $H$, then

$$
H=\overline G.
$$

The claim would follow immediately if every invertible game were a number, as the source notes. Its status is unresolved in the supplied text.
