406                                       4. Concluding remarks
407    It seems very likely that the condition ppnq ě n´1{2`ε in Theorem 1 can be significantly improved:
408 apparently (work in progress), by adapting available embedding technology to the present purposes
409 one can improve ´1{2 to ´2{3 in Theorem 1. It also seems as if ppnq ě n´2{3`ε is the utmost
410 of what can be achieved with the technique of proving the existence of some spanning subgraph
411 pre-selected from the monotone property Mt|¨|u,0 : the graph C2n is a non-minimal element of the
412 monotone property M|¨|,0 (resp., for even n, of M|¨|,1 ), and it is possible to descend quite far by
413 deleting edges to construct sparser ‘rebar’, and yet ppnq ě n´2{3`ε seems to be required even when
414 using a minimal element of M|¨|,0 .
415    It nevertheless still seems plausible that one may even significantly improve the conjectured
416 bound n´2{3`ε , but this will require several new ideas: some customary arguments for showing
417 that a set of vectors is a generating system carry with them an ‘instability’ unusual for arguments
418 
419 8                                                PETER HEINIG

422 about Gn,pn . For example, an argument which were to randomly choose a dimension-sized subset
423 of distinct Hamilton-circuits and then go on to prove this subset to be a.a.s. linearly independent
424 over F2 would have to be so sensitive to the size of the subset so as to break down if only 1 `
425 dimF2 Z1 pG; F2 q circuits would have been selected instead. Nevertheless, it is not inconceivable that
426 with the right ideas one can improve the threshold n´1{2`ε all the way down to what Theorem 8
427 allows, i.e. to ppnq ě log n`2 logn log n`ωn , the threshold for minimum degree ě 3.

                                                                                           Abstract

                                                  Let G be a simple graph and Q(G) be the signless Laplacian matrix of G. Let Sα (G) be
                                               the sum of the α-th powers of the nonzero eigenvalues of Q(G). We disprove two conjectures
                                               by You and Yang on the extremal values of Sα (G) among bipartite graphs and among graphs
                                               with bounded connectivity.

                                               AMS Classification: 05C50
                                               Keywords: Signless Laplacian eigenvalues of graph



                                         1     Introduction

                                         Let G be a simple graph with vertex set V (G) = {v1 , . . . , vn }. The degree of a vertex v ∈ V (G),
                                         denoted by d(v), is the number of neighbors of v. The adjacency matrix of G is an n×n matrix A(G)
                                         whose (i, j) entry is 1 if vi and vj are adjacent and zero otherwise. The signless Laplacian matrix
                                         of G is the matrx Q(G) = A(G) + D(G), where D(G) is the diagonal matrix with d(v1 ), . . . , d(vn )
                                         on its main diagonal. It is well-known that Q(G) are positive semidefinite and so its eigenvalues
                                         are nonnegative real numbers. The multiplicity of zero eigenvalue for Q(S) is equal to the number
                                         of bipartite connected components of G. The eigenvalues of Q(G) are called the signless Laplacian
                                         eigenvalues of G and are denoted by q1 (G), . . . , qn (G). We drop G from the notation when there
                                         is no danger of confusion. We denote the complete graph on n vertices by Kn and the complete
                                         bipartite graph with parts with r and s vertices by Kr,s . The (vertex) connectivity κ(G) of a

to every vertex of H. We also denote the number of edges of G by e(G).
