114: 1.3. Previous results on the Hall ratio. Since the Hall ratio is the hereditary version of the
115: inverse of the independence ratio (deﬁned as the independence number divided by the number of
116: vertices), any result on the independence ratio in a hereditary class of graphs can be extended to the
117: Hall ratio. The Hall ratio of a graph has often been studied in relation with the girth, which is the
118: length of a smallest cycle in the graph. A ﬁrst result in this direction is the celebrated introduction of
119: the so-called “deletion method” in graph theory by Erdős, who used it to demonstrate the existence
120: of graphs with arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number. The latter is actually established by
121: proving that the Hall ratio of the graph is arbitrarily large. As a large girth is not strong enough a
122: requirement to imply a constant upper bound on the chromatic number, a way to pursue this line
123: of research is to express the upper bound in terms of the maximum degree ∆(G) of the graph G
124: considered. This also applies to the Hall ratio.
