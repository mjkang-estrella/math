## 375976 and 375977: complete small objects, then latest-source failure

Use K = Z semidirect Z with (x,y)(u,v)=(x+(-1)^y u,y+v). The action y -> (u -> (-1)^y u) is a homomorphism into Aut(Z), proving associativity; the identity is (0,0) and inverse is (-(-1)^y x,-y). K is torsion-free: if (x,y)^r=e for r>0, then ry=0 implies y=0, and then rx=0 implies x=0. This is an exact group, not a finite quotient.

First attempted route: disprove two-block abelianity by equal squares in the Klein-bottle group. Put e=(0,0), a=(0,1), b=(1,1), S={e,a,b}, A={e,a}, B={b}. A and B are disjoint nonempty abelian sets; their generated subgroups are abelian too. Products are exactly
S^2={(0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(0,2),(-1,2),(1,2)}.
Indeed a^2=b^2=(0,2), ab=(-1,2), ba=(1,2), and identity products give the first three elements. All six are distinct. Thus |S|=3, |S^2|=6=3|S|-3, and <S> is nonabelian. This refutes the literal unqualified 375976 when k means |S|. It is the SAME six-element product-set mechanism already recorded for 375978 in completed r314; there is no new family credit.

Second route, rather than stopping at the first object: three singleton blocks allow a smaller product set. Put S={(1,1),(2,1),(3,1)} and take A,B,C to be its three singleton subsets. Each block generates an abelian subgroup. Products (i,1)(j,1)=(i-j,2) give exactly S^2={(-2,2),(-1,2),(0,2),(1,2),(2,2)}: every difference -2 through 2 is attained by endpoints or equal indices, and none outside that interval is possible. Thus |S^2|=5=3|S|-4, whereas (1,1)(2,1)=(-1,2) differs from (2,1)(1,1)=(1,2). This is exactly source Example 6.2 at k=3 with the defining singleton partition. It is a complete earlier-source reduction, not a novel discovery.

Chronology is decisive. v1 was posted July 13 2026; v2 September 30; v3 October 3, before this research. v1 Conjecture 6.1 omits block cardinality restrictions; v2/v3 add |A|,|B|>=3. v2 Conjecture 6.2 still omits block sizes; v3 adds that EACH of A,B,C has size at least three. v3 also changes Conjecture 6.3 to 3|S|-4, agreeing with the completed r314 objection. The corpus omits those current substantive hypotheses. Hence both assigned short refutations are corrections, not solutions of current intended conjectures. No dormant Pi plan.

Third mechanism tested symbolically: enlarge the blocks without losing the small-doubling budget. In K the commutation equation is x(1-(-1)^v)=u(1-(-1)^y). Two odd-height elements commute exactly when x=u; an even-height element commutes with an odd-height one exactly when its first coordinate is zero. Consequently distinct odd-height elements in Example 6.2 cannot be merged into commuting blocks, and adjoining distinct even elements commuting with them imposes x=0 and creates new products on new height levels. One cannot simply declare the three singleton blocks large. The source's own large three-block Example 6.1 has |S^2|=3|S|-3, one ABOVE the required 3|S|-4. No uniform enlargement construction meeting the stricter threshold was obtained. The ordered separation assumptions of Theorem 2.10 are not available in an arbitrary torsion-free group. ## 376001: genuine directed-square mechanism, but unspecified source scope

Read source v1 (the only advertised version), Cayley definition (2.1), GAP generator selection in Section 3, coefficient formula (4.7), Conjecture 4.1 and surrounding empirical interpretation. The text uses GeneratorsOfGroup, explicitly not guaranteed to give a minimum generating set. Conjecture 4.1 itself says “under appropriate restrictions to S”; it does not define those restrictions. Figures were read only through captions and surrounding text, not visually inspected. No journal/correction or companion-wide clearance is claimed.

Initial mechanism: taking all nonidentity elements in any nontrivial finite perfect group makes many squares. That is inadequate because it uses a very redundant generating set, and unknown source restrictions might exclude it.

A stronger ordinary object removes redundancy. In A5 let a=(12)(34), b=(13)(24), c=(12)(45), and S={a,b,c}. These are distinct involutions, none identity; S is inverse-closed and irredundant. a,b generate the four-element Klein group fixing 5. This Klein group acts transitively on {1,2,3,4}; c connects 5 to that orbit, so H=<S> acts transitively on five letters. Hence 5 divides |H|, while 4 divides |H|. The product ac is the 3-cycle (345), so 3 divides |H|. As H<=A5 and |A5|=60, Lagrange gives |H|=60, so H=A5. Each pair of involutions generates a dihedral group, with order twice their product order; here ab has order 2 and ac,bc have orders 3,5 respectively. Thus all three pairs generate proper subgroups and S is irredundant. These are direct permutation identities, not results of an enumeration.

A5 is perfect: [a,c]=(ac)^2 is a 3-cycle. The derived subgroup is normal in A5 and contains all A5-conjugates of this 3-cycle. All 3-cycles are conjugate in A5 (adjust the parity of any conjugating permutation by swapping the two fixed letters), and 3-cycles generate A5, since every even permutation decomposes into paired transpositions and each such pair is a product of 3-cycles. Therefore [A5,A5]=A5.

Since a,b commute and are distinct involutions, e -> a -> ab -> b -> e is a directed cycle of length four with four distinct vertices in Cay(A5,S). The coefficient formula needs more than a picture: at v=e, every u,w in S with u!=w has e in N_out(u) intersect N_in(w), because S is inverse-closed. Thus every numerator summand in (4.7) is nonnegative. For u=a,w=b, that intersection also contains ab, so its summand is at least 1. Degrees are 3. For theta_uw=0, denominator summand is 5-common>=2. For theta_uw=1, common<=3 and denominator is 3-common>=0. The selected pair a,b has theta_ab=0, since ab is the third nontrivial element of the Klein group and is none of a,b,c. Thus the denominator is positive. Vertex transitivity gives strictly positive coefficient at every vertex. This defeats the unrestricted vanishing claim even with an irredundant, inverse-closed, identity-free S.

Precise hold: irredundant is not minimum-cardinality. A5 is 2-generated, and the source may intend minimum generators or a particular GAP choice, or other still unstated restrictions. No object can be certified as satisfying unknown restrictions. The calculation is a conditional diagnostic, not a literal qualified MathDB solution. No candidate or Pi implementation plan is warranted. Do not relabel an unspecified generating-set correction as discovery credit.

## 367034: two sustained attempts at the exact tree endpoint; unresolved

Read definitions of ensnaring (all toric trajectories contractible), n-vertex complement, the discrete Theta dynamics, winding-vector criterion, Theorems 8.2, 8.4 and complete 8.6 proof, and Conjecture 9.1 in latest v2. v1 and v2 were retrieved; selected corresponding sections checked, not every line of both manuscripts. The advertised chronology is February 9 and April 28 2025. Companion [1] and final journal/corrections were not fully read, so source/novelty holds remain.

The actual complement is the complement of T plus n-m isolated vertices, so the added vertices become UNIVERSAL vertices; they cannot be ignored as isolated vertices of the billiard graph. The orbit endpoint is every winding coordinate zero for every orbit, not merely existence of a short or periodic orbit. Finite-state periodicity alone does not imply zero winding in the universal cover.

Route 1: a leaf induction. Deleting a leaf from T changes one missing edge and removes a vertex that was adjacent to almost every other vertex in compl_n(T). Its interactions with the stone create reversals and swaps, so projecting a Theta orbit by deleting that replica is not known to yield an orbit of the smaller complement with the same winding vector. Keeping n fixed instead changes a missing-edge endpoint into a universal vertex and also changes the proposed parity conclusion, so ordinary edge monotonicity cannot justify induction. Exact missing lemma: a winding-preserving (or parity-controlled) return-map correspondence for adding a pendant vertex to the missing-edge tree, with all orbit and coordinate quantifiers. No such lemma was proved.

Route 2: extend source's bridged-edge reversal mechanism. Theorem 8.6 treats K_(ell,r), whose vertices in each part are interchangeable; post-pivotal swaps of twins, combined with time-reversal conjugacy, force zero winding. For stars T=K_(1,m-1), m>=3, its criterion says ensnaring iff n-m odd. For even n this is exactly m odd. For m=2, Theorem 8.4 gives non-ensnaring, as desired. These are already proved source cases and receive no credit. For an arbitrary tree the needed twin symmetry is absent. For example in P5 there are no open twins, so the particular post-pivotal identification cannot be copied. A pivot to a third tree vertex changes its neighbors; identifying those states is not a dynamical conjugacy. A tree's bipartite coloring only controls the sides of a bridged edge, not winding coordinates of all replicas. Missing lemma: every orbit in the odd-m case either has a suitable reversible pivot forcing all coordinate windings zero, or admits another complete cancellation mechanism. In the even-m case, a uniform construction of one orbit with nonzero winding is also missing. Theorem 8.2 splits CONNECTED COMPONENTS of the missing-edge graph; a tree is one component, so it cannot split along its edges. Neither attempt yields a full proof or counterexample. Park, without a fabricated finite-search plan.
