                                                           SETS IN DICYCLIC GROUPS

                                                                                 SAGAR MANDAL AND NEETU∗


                                                 Abstract. We investigate the relationship between the sizes of the sum and difference sets of the
                                                 Dicyclic Group Dic4n . We first determine the exact numbers of MSTD (more sums than differences),




arXiv:2602.09073v1 [math.GM] 9 Feb 2026
                                                 MDTS (more differences than sums), and balanced subsets of size two. As a consequence, we show
                                                 that the numbers of MSTD and balanced subsets of size two are asymptotically equal as n → ∞.
                                                 For odd n, we then obtain exact counts of MSTD, MDTS, and balanced subsets of size three, with
                                                 the results depending on whether n is divisible by 3. In this case, we establish that asymptotically
                                                 the number of MSTD subsets of size three is six times the number of MDTS subsets and also six
                                                 times the number of balanced subsets. Finally, we establish a lower bound for the number of MSTD,
                                                 MDTS, and balanced subsets of Dic4n corresponding to the boundary case of size 2n.

                                                                                       1. Introduction
                                             If A ⊂ Z, where Z denotes the group of integers, the sumset and difference set of A are defined
                                          as follows.
                                                                   A + A := {x ∈ Z : x = a1 + a2 for some a1 , a2 ∈ A},
                                                                   A − A := {x ∈ Z : x = a1 − a2 for some a1 , a2 ∈ A}.
                                          These elementary operations are fundamental in additive number theory. For integers a and b, we
                                          define [a, b] = {x ∈ Z : a ≤ x ≤ b}. A natural problem of recent interest has been to understand
                                          the relative sizes of the sum and difference sets of a set A.
                                          Definition 1.1. If |A + A| > |A − A|, A is called a more-sums-than-differences (MSTD) set or a
                                          sum-dominant set , while if |A + A| = |A − A|, A is said to be balanced, and if |A + A| < |A − A|,
                                          then A is called a more-differences-than-sums (MDTS) set or a difference-dominant set.
                                            2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 11A07, 11B75, 11B99, 11P70.
                                            Key words and phrases. Balanced sets, Combinatorics, Dicyclic Groups, MDTS sets, MSTD sets.

   Since addition is commutative and subtraction is not, we usually expect most sets to be MDTS.
However, MSTD sets do exist. It is believed that Conway in 1969 gave the first example of an MSTD
set, which is {0, 2, 3, 4, 7, 11, 12, 14}. Martin and O’Bryant [11] proved that for all n ≥ 15, a positive
proportion of the 2n subsets of {0, 1, . . . , n − 1} are MSTD. Zhao [20] later gave a deterministic
algorithm to compute the proportion of MSTD sets as n goes to infinity and proved that this
proportion is at least 4.28 · 10−4 .
   The last few years have seen an explosion of papers examining the properties of sum-dominant
sets of integers, one can see ( [1, 4–12, 14, 19]).
   Much of the study of sum-dominant sets has concerned subsets of the integers; however, the
phenomenon in finite groups has received some attention, notably from [5], [15], [18] and [16]. One
key motivation for this line of research is the close connection between MSTD sets in finite abelian
groups and those in the integers. Nathanson in [15] showed that families of MSTD sets of integers
can be constructed from MSTD sets in finite abelian groups. More precisely, if A ⊂ Z/nZ satisfies
|A + A| > |A − A|, then {a ∈ Z : a mod n ∈ A, 0 ≤ a ≤ kn} is an MSTD set of integers for
sufficiently large k.
   In this direction, Nathanson in [15] established lower bounds on the number of MSTD subsets in
certain finite abelian groups, showing that

In the context of non-abelian groups, Miller and Vissuet in [13] began a systematic investigation of
sumsets and difference sets, started with dihedral group
                                D2n := ⟨r, s : rn = s2 = 1, r−1 s = sr⟩.
    Based on both theoretical and computational evidence, they proposed the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.2. [13] Let n ≥ 3 be an integer. There are more MSTD subsets of D2n than MDTS
subsets of D2n .
  Further progress toward this conjecture was made by Ascoli et al. in [2], who studied subsets of
D2n according to their cardinality.
  In the finite groups, although the operation is usually written multiplicatively, we follow the
notation from [13] and define the sumset and difference set as
                                     A + A = {a1 a2 : a1 , a2 ∈ A},
and
                                   A − A = {a1 a−12 : a1 , a2 ∈ A}.
In this paper we focus on dicyclic groups. For a positive integer n ≥ 1, the dicyclic group, denoted
as Dic4n , is defined as
                           Dic4n = ⟨a, b : a2n = 1, an = b2 , b−1 ab = a−1 ⟩.
                                                                                                      3

The following properties of the dicyclic group Dic4n can be derived from the relations a2n = 1,
an = b2 , and ab = ba−1 , as shown in [3].
Lemma 1.3. [3, Lemma 2.6] For the dicyclic group Dic4n , we have
   (1) bak = a−k b, ak b = ba−k ;
   (2) ak bam b = ak−m+n ;
   (3) (bak )−1 = ban+k , (ak b)−1 = an+k b,
where k ∈ [0, 2n − 1].
  Consequently, the group Dic4n admits the following explicit representation:
                         Dic4n = {1, a, a2 , . . . , a2n−1 , b, ab, a2 b, . . . , a2n−1 b}.
We now compare MSTD and MDTS sets in the dihedral groups D2n and the dicyclic groups Dic4n
for small values of n. When n = 1, both groups are isomorphic to Z/4Z and all subsets of Z/4Z are
balanced. For larger values of n, the structures begin to diverge, and interesting behaviors emerge.

   From these tables, it is clear that dihedral groups always contain more MSTD sets than MDTS
sets, which supports Conjecture 1.2. In fact, Ascoli et al. [2] proved that, for subsets of sizes 2 and
3, the dihedral group D2n contains strictly more MSTD sets than MDTS sets. However, this does
not hold for dicyclic groups, which display different behavior.
   For m = 2, we can easily observe from Table 1 and Table 2 that, dicyclic groups also contain
strictly more MSTD sets than MDTS sets. In contrast, for m = 3, we observe that when 2 ≤ n ≤ 4,
dicyclic groups have more MDTS sets than MSTD sets, whereas for n = 5 the situation reverses
and MSTD sets become more numerous.

                           6. Concluding remarks and future research
   In this paper, we count the exact numbers of MSTD, MDTS, and balanced subsets of size two.
For odd values of n, we also obtain exact counts of these sets for subsets of size three, where the
results depend on whether n is divisible by 3. Moreover, using the same techniques, one can also
derive exact formulas for subsets of size three when n is even. A natural direction for future work
is to investigate subsets of size four and larger. However, the method used in this paper involves
many detailed case analyses and quickly becomes difficult to handle for larger sets. Finding new
and simpler approaches may help to understand these cases more effectively.
The following problems and conjectures  naturally arise from our study. The results of Theorems 1.4
and 1.6 imply that, for m = 2, 3, gcd SDic4n (m) : n ≥ 3 is odd is divisible by 4. This observation
motivates the following question.
                                                                               
Question 6.1. For any fixed integer m with 2 ≤ m ≤ 2n, is 4 a divisor of gcd SDic4n (m) : n ≥ 3 ?

  Furthermore,
                Theorems 1.4 and 1.6 show that for any fixed odd integer n ≥ 3, 4n is a divisor of
gcd SDic4n (m) : m = 2, 3 . This observation naturally leads to the following question.
                                                                    
Question 6.2. For any fixed integer n ≥ 3 , is 4n a divisor of gcd SDic4n (m) : m ≥ 2 ?

  In addition, Theorems 1.4 and 1.6, imply that SDic4n (m), DDic4n (m) and BDic4n (m) are even inte-
gers for any odd integer n ≥ 3 and m = 2, 3. This observation suggests the following more general
question.

Question 6.3. Is it true that SDic4n (m), DDic4n (m) and BDic4n (m) are even integers for any positive
integers n, m with n ≥ 3 and 2 ≤ m ≤ 2n?

     In light of the results established in this paper, we are led to propose the following two conjectures.

Conjecture 6.1. For a given integer m ≥ 2, there exists a positive integer Km such that if n ≥ Km ,
then Dic4n has more MSTD sets than MDTS sets of size m.

Conjecture 6.2. For given integer m ≥ 2, there exists a positive integer Km such that if n ≥ Km ,
then SDic4n (m) > 2m/2 .
