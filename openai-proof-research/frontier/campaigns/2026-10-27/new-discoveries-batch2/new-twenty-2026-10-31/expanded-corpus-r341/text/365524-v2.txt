Abstract.
 
Hu and Ye conjectured that for a $k$ -th order and $n$ -dimensional tensor $\mathcal{A}$ with an eigenvalue $\lambda$ and the corresponding eigenvariety $\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ ,
 
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})(k-1)^{\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})-1},$ 
 
where $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)$ is the algebraic multiplicity of $\lambda$ , and $V_{1},\ldots,V_{\kappa}$ are all irreducible components of $\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ .
In this paper, we prove that if $\mathcal{A}$ is a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor with spectral radius $\rho$ , then $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})|$ for all eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ with modulus $\rho$ , where $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ is the projective eigenvariety of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\lambda$ .
Consequently we confirm Hu-Ye’s conjecture for the above eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ and also the least H-eigenvalue of a weakly irreducible $Z$ -tensor.
We prove several equality cases in Hu-Ye’s conjecture for the eigenvalues of the adjacency tensor or Laplacian tensor of uniform hypergraphs.
 
Key words and phrases: Tensor; eigenvalue; eigenvector; spectral radius; multiplicity; hypergraph

1. Introduction
A tensor (also called hypermatrix) $\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})$ of order $k$ and dimension $n$ over a field $\mathbb{F}$ refers to a multi-array of entries $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\in\mathbb{F}$ for all $i_{j}\in[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $j\in[k]$ .
Surely a square matrix is a tensor of order $2$ .
It is known that the algebraic multiplicity of an eigenvalue of a square matrix over complex field $\mathbb{C}$ is always greater than or equal to its geometric multiplicity.
People wish to know the relationship between the algebraic multiplicity of an eigenvalue of a tensor and the geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue.
We start this topic with some basic notions.
Given a vector $x\in\mathbb{F}^{n}$ , $\mathcal{A}x^{k-1}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}$ , which is defined as follows:
 $\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}x^{k-1})_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\in[n]}a_{ii_{2}\ldots i_{k}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}},i\in[n].$ 
A tensor $\mathcal{I}=(i_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{k}})$ of order $k$ and dimension $n$ is called an identity tensor, if $i_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{k}}=1$ for
 $i_{1}=i_{2}=\cdots=i_{k}\in[n]$ and $i_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{k}}=0$ otherwise.
In 2005, Lim [26] and Qi [31] independently introduced the eigenvalues of the tensors.
Definition 1.1 ([26, 31]).
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a $k$ -th order $n$ -dimensional tensor.
For some $\lambda\in\mathbb{F}$ , if the polynomial system $(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A})x^{k-1}=0$ ,
or equivalently $\mathcal{A}x^{k-1}=\lambda x^{[k-1]}$ , has a solution $x\in\mathbb{F}^{n}\backslash\{0\}$ ,
then $\lambda$ is called an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ and $x$ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\lambda$ ,
where $x^{[k-1]}:=(x_{1}^{k-1},x_{2}^{k-1},\ldots,x_{n}^{k-1})$ .
The characteristic polynomial $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$ of $\mathcal{A}$ is defined as the resultant of the polynomials $(\lambda\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A})x^{k-1}$ (see [31, 3]).
It is known that $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ if and only if it is a root of $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$ , and the algebraic mulitplicity of $\lambda$ is defined as the multiplicity of $\lambda$ as a root of $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$ , denoted by $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)$ .
To avoid confusions, sometimes we use $\mathrm{am}(\lambda,\mathcal{A})$ instead of $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)$ to stress the algebraic multiplicity of $\lambda$ as an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ .
The spectrum of $\mathcal{A}$ , denoted by $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A})$ , is the multiset of roots of $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)$ .
Usually we take $\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}$ , the complex field, and call the largest modulus of the elements in $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A})$ the spectral radius of $\mathcal{A}$ , denoted by $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ .
Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ .
The eigenvariety of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ is defined to be the affine variety
 $\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\mathcal{A}x^{k-1}=\lambda x^{[k-1]}\},$ 
namely, the eigenvector of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\lambda$ together with zero.
The geometric multiplicity of $\lambda$ is defined as the dimension of $\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ , which is the maximum dimension of the irreducible components of $\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ , denoted by $\mathrm{gm}(\lambda)$ .
The projective eigenvariety of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\lambda$ is defined to be the projective variety ([14])
 $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})=\{x\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}:\mathcal{A}x^{k-1}=\lambda x^{[k-1]}\},$ 
where $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is the complex projective spaces over $\mathbb{C}$ of dimension $n-1$ ,
In 2016 Hu and Ye [23] proposed the following conjecture and showed that it is true in several cases.
Conjecture 1.2 ([23]).
(Hu-Ye’s conjecture)
Suppose that a $k$ -th order $n$ -dimensional tensor $\mathcal{A}$ has an eigenvalue $\lambda$ with the eigenvariety $\mathcal{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ possessing $\kappa$ irreducible components $V_{1},\ldots,V_{\kappa}$ . Then
(1.1)
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})(k-1)^{\mathrm{dim}(V_{i})-1}.$ 
In particular,
(1.2)
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq\mathrm{gm}(\lambda)(k-1)^{\mathrm{gm}(\lambda)-1}.$ 
To verify the Hu-Ye conjecture, one usually considers the adjacency tensor of a $k$ -uniform hypergraph.
Cooper and Fickes [9] fully described the irreducible components of the zero-eigenvariety of a $3$ -uniform (loose) hyperpath $P_{n}^{(3)}$ , and used Bao-Fan-Wang-Zhu’s result [1] to compute the algebraic multiplicity of zero eigenvalue, and finally confirmed (1.1) in Hu-Ye’s conjecture for the zero eigenvalue of $P_{n}^{(3)}$ .
Zheng [38] confirmed (1.2) in Hu-Ye’s conjecture for all nonzero eigenvalues of a $k$ -uniform hyperpath $P_{n}^{(k)}$ , and
all nonzero eigenvalues of a $k$ -uniform hyperstar $S_{n}^{(k)}$ , by using results of Chen-Bu [6] and Bao-Fan-Wang-Zhu [1] on the characteristic polynomials of hyperpaths and hyperstars.
In this paper, we will confirm Hu-Ye’s conjecture for the eigenvalues of a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor with modulus equal to its spectral radius and the least H-eigenvalue of a weakly irreducible $Z$ -tensor.
We also prove the equality cases of (1.1) Hu-Ye’s conjecture for several classes of hypergraphs.
The paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we will introduce some basic results on nonnegative tensors and hypergraphs, including the Perron-Frobenius theorem, the cyclic index and stabilizing index, and the adjacency tensor, Laplacian tensor and signless Laplacian tensor of uniform hypergraphs.
In Section 3 we introduce local ring and multiplicity of zeros of a polynomial ideal, characterize the simple zeros of the polynomial ideal by using the rank of Jacobian matrix of polynomials, and prove some results on the simple eigenvectors of the adjacency, Laplacian or signless Laplacian tensor of uniform hypergraphs.
In Section 4, for a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor $\mathcal{A}$ with spectral radius $\rho$ , by using Poisson formula we prove that $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})|$ for all eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ with modulus $\rho$ , and hence confirm Hu-Ye’s conjecture for the above eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ and also the least H-eigenvalue of a weakly irreducible $Z$ -tensor.
In the last section, we prove the equality case in Hu-Ye’s conjecture for some eigenvalues of the adjacency or Laplacian tensor of several classes of hypergraphs.
2. Preliminaries
2.1. Perron-Frobenius theorem for nonnegative tensors
Let $\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})$ be a tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ .
Associate $\mathcal{A}$ with a directed graph $D(\mathcal{A})$ on vertex set $[n]$ such that $(i,j)$ is an arc of $D(\mathcal{A})$ if and only if there exists a nonzero entry $a_{ii_{2}\ldots i_{k}}$ such that $j\in\{i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}\}$ .
Then $\mathcal{A}$ is called weakly irreducible if $D(\mathcal{A})$ is strongly connected; otherwise, it is called weakly reducible.
We say $\mathcal{A}$ is nonnegative if its all entries are nonnegative, and $\mathcal{A}$ is symmetric if all entries $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}$ are invariant under any permutation of its indices.
The support (or the zero-nonzero patter [32]) of $\mathcal{A}$ , denoted by $\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})=(s_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})$ , is defined as a tensor with same order and dimension of $\mathcal{A}$ , such that $s_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}=1$ if $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\neq 0$ , and $s_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}=0$ otherwise.
 $\mathcal{A}$ is called combinatorial symmetric if $\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{A})$ is symmetric [14].
Surely combinatorial symmetric tensors are generalizations of symmetric tensors.
For a combinatorial symmetric tensor $\mathcal{A}$ of order $k$ and dimension $n$ . Set
 $E(\mathcal{A})=\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in[n]^{k}:a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\neq 0,i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\},$ 
and for each $e=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in E(\mathcal{A})$ and $j\in[n]$ , define
 $b_{e,j}=|\{t:i_{t}=j,t\in[k]\}|.$ 
The matrix $B_{\mathcal{A}}=(b_{e,j})$ is called the incidence matrix of $\mathcal{A}$ [14].
Chang et al. [4], Yang and Yang [36, 35, 34], and Friedland et al. [19] generalized the Perron-Frobenius theorem from nonnegative matrices to nonnegative tensors.
Below we list some of the results of the theorem,
where an eigenvalue is called an $H^{++}$ -eigenvalue if it is associated with a positive eigenvector.
Theorem 2.1.
[19, 34]
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ with spectral radius $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ . Then the following results hold.
(1) 
 $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ is a unique $H^{++}$ -eigenvalue, with a unique positive eigenvector up to a positive scalar, called the Perron vector of $\mathcal{A}$ .
(2) 
If $x$ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with an eigenvalue with modulus $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ , then $|x|$ is the Perron vector of $\mathcal{A}$ .
(3) 
If $\mathcal{A}$ has $m$ distinct eigenvalues with modulus equal to $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ , then those eigenvalues are $\rho(\mathcal{A})e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi j/m},j=0,1,\ldots,m-1$ . Furthermore, there exists a diagonal matrix $D$ with unit diagonal entries such that $\mathcal{A}=e^{-\mathbf{i}2\pi/m}D^{-(k-1)}\mathcal{A}D$ , and $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A})=e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi/m}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A})$ .
In Theorem 2.1, $\mathbf{i}=\sqrt{-1}$ , and the product $P\mathcal{A}Q$ with $P,Q$ being $n\times n$ diagonal matrices is defined by Shao [32] as
 $(P\mathcal{A}Q)_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}=P_{i_{1}i_{1}}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}Q_{i_{2}i_{2}}\cdots Q_{i_{k}i_{k}}.$ 
If $D$ is an invertible diagonal matrix, then $D^{-(k-1)}\mathcal{A}D$ is called diagonal similar to $\mathcal{A}$ , and has the same spectrum as $\mathcal{A}$ [32].
2.2. Cyclic index and stabilizing index
The value $m$ in Theorem 2.1(3) is called the cyclic index of $\mathcal{A}$ [2], and is related to the spectral symmetry of $\mathcal{A}$ .
Definition 2.2 ([16]).
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a general tensor, and let $\ell$ be a positive integer. The tensor
is called spectral $\ell$ -symmetric if
(2.1)
 $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A})=e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi/\ell}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{A}).$ 
The maximum $\ell$ such that (2.1) holds is the cyclic index of $\mathcal{A}$ , denoted by $c(\mathcal{A})$ .
If $\mathcal{A}$ is nonnegative and weakly irreducible, then $c(\mathcal{A})$ in Definition 2.2 is consistent with that given in [3]; see more details in [16].
By using the following result,
Fan et al. [16] proved that
 $c(\mathcal{A})=\gcd\{d:\mathrm{Tr}_{d}(\mathcal{A})\neq 0\},$ 
where $\mathrm{Tr}_{d}(\mathcal{A})$ is the generalized $d$ -th order trace of $\mathcal{A}$ (see [8, 28, 33]).
Theorem 2.3.
[16]
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ .
Let $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\lambda^{N-i}$ be the characteristic polynomial of $\mathcal{A}$ , where $N=n(k-1)^{n-1}$ . Then the following conditions are equivalent.
(1) 
 $\mathcal{A}$ is spectral $\ell$ -symmetric.
(2) 
If $\ell\nmid d$ , then $a_{d}=0$ ; that is, $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{t}f(\lambda^{\ell})$ for some nonnegative integer $t$ and some polynomial $f$ .
(3) 
If $\ell\nmid d$ , then $\mathrm{Tr}_{d}(\mathcal{A})=0$ .
Corollary 2.4.
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor.
Then for any eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ with modulus $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ ,
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)=\mathrm{am}(\rho(\mathcal{A})).$ 
Proof.
Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ with modulus $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ .
By Theorem 2.1(4), $\lambda=\rho(\mathcal{A})e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi j/m}$ , where $j\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}$ and $m=c(\mathcal{A})$ .
Furthermore, $\mathcal{A}$ is spectral $m$ -symmetric.
By Theorem 2.3,
the characteristic polynomial of $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{t}f(\lambda^{m})$ for some nonnegative integer $t$ and some polynomial $f$ .
So, $\lambda$ and $\rho(\mathcal{A})$ have the same algebraic multiplicity.

Next, we will introduce the stabilizing index of a tensor, which is related to the projective eigenvariety associated with the spectral radius.
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an $k$ -th order $n$ -dimensional nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor, which is also spectral $\ell$ -symmetric.
Let
 $\lambda_{j}=\rho(\mathcal{A})e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi j/\ell},j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1$ 
be the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{A}$ , where $\lambda_{0}=\rho(\mathcal{A})$ .
Define
 $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{D}_{j}(\mathcal{A}),~\mathbb{D}_{j}(\mathcal{A})=\{D:\mathcal{A}=e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi j/\ell}D^{-(k-1)}\mathcal{A}D,d_{11}=1\},j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1,$ 
where $D$ is an $n\times n$ invertible diagonal matrix in the above definition.
Note that $\mathbb{D}_{0}(\mathcal{A})\neq\emptyset$ as it contains the identity matrix.
It is easy to verify $\mathbb{D}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ is an abelian group under the usual matrix multiplication.
Definition 2.5.
[14]
The stabilizing index of a tensor $\mathcal{A}$ , denoted by $s(\mathcal{A})$ , is defined to be the cardinality of the group $\mathbb{D}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ .
Let
 $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A}):=\cup_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})$ .
by Theorem 2.1(2), each eigenvector $x\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})$ has no zero entries so that we can assume that each point $x\in\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})$ holds $x_{1}=1$ .
Write $x=D_{x}\mathfrak{p}$ , where $\mathfrak{p}:=|x|$ is the unique Perron vector of $\mathcal{A}$ also by Theorem 2.1(2), and
 $D_{x}=\mathrm{diag}(x_{1}/|x_{1}|,\ldots,x_{n}/|x_{n}|).$ 
Now define a product $\circ$ in $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})$ as
 $x\circ x^{\prime}:=D_{x}D_{x^{\prime}}\mathfrak{p},$ 
and a map
(2.2)
 $\Psi:\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A}),x\mapsto D_{x}.$ 
Lemma 2.6.
[14, Lemma 2.5, Lemma 3.1]
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a $k$ -th order and $n$ -dimensional nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor that is also spectral $\ell$ -symmetric. Then the following results hold.
(1) 
 $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ is an abelian group the usual matrix multiplication, where $\mathbb{D}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ , and $\mathbb{D}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ is a coset of $\mathbb{D}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ in $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ for $j\in[\ell-1]$ .
(2) 
 $(\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A}),\circ)$ is an abelian group that contains $\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{A})$ as a subgroup and $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})$ as a coset of $\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{A})$ for each $j\in[\ell-1]$ , and $\Psi$ is a group isomorphism from $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})$ to $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ which sends $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})$ to $\mathbb{D}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ for each $j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1$ .
(3) 
If $\mathcal{A}$ is further combinatorial symmetric, then $\ell\mid k$ and $D^{k}=I$ for any $D\in\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ .
It was proved that in [15] that $\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{A})$ is finite for a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor $\mathcal{A}$ . So we have the following result by Lemma 2.6.
Corollary 2.7.
[15]
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a $k$ -th order and $n$ -dimensional nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor which is also spectral $\ell$ -symmetric.
Then $|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})|=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{A})|=s(\mathcal{A})$ for all $j\in[\ell-1]$ ,
where $\lambda_{j}=\rho(\mathcal{A})e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi j/\ell},j\in[\ell-1]$ .
Moreover, assume that $\mathcal{A}$ is combinatorial symmetric.
By Lemma 2.6(3), $y^{\circ k}=(D_{y})^{k}\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}$ , so $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ both admit $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -modules, and $\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})$ is $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -isomorphic to $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ sending $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})$ to $\mathbb{D}^{(j)}(\mathcal{A})$ for each $j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1$ by Lemma 2.6(2).
Also,
for each $D\in\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ , as $D^{k}=I$ , we can write
 $D=\mathrm{diag}(e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi\phi_{1}(D)/k},\ldots,e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi\phi_{n}(D)/k}),$ 
where $\phi_{i}(D)\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ for $i\in[n]$ , and $\phi_{1}(D)=0$ as $D_{11}=1$ by definition.
So we have a map
(2.3)
 $\Phi:\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n},D\mapsto(\phi_{1}(D),\ldots,\phi_{n}(D)).$ 
Define
(2.4)
 $\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}:B_{\mathcal{A}}x=\frac{kj}{\ell}\mathbf{1}\mbox{~over~}\mathbb{Z}_{k},x_{1}=0\},j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1,$ 
and $\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{A})=\cup_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ , where $\mathbf{1}$ is an all-ones vector of dimension $n$ .
Obviously $\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{A})$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -module which contains $\mathbb{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ as a $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -submodule, and $\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A})+y^{(j)}$ for $y^{(j)}\in\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ for $j\in[\ell-1]$ .
Lemma 2.8.
[14]
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a nonnegative combinatorial symmetric weakly irreducible tensor that is spectral $\ell$ -symmetric. Then $\Phi$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -isomorphism from $\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A})$ to $\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{A})$ which sends $\mathbb{D}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ to $\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ for each $j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1$ .
By Lemma 2.6 and Lemma 2.8, $\Phi\Psi:\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{A})$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -isomorphism sending $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})$ to $\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ for $j=0,1,\ldots,\ell-1$ .
So, for each $x\in\mathbb{V}_{\lambda_{j}}(\mathcal{A})$ , writing
 $x=(|x_{1}|e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi\phi_{1}(x)/k},\ldots,|x_{n}|e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi\phi_{n}(x)/k}),$ 
where $\phi_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ for $i\in[n]$ , we have
 $(\phi_{1}(x),\ldots,\phi_{n}(x))\in\mathbb{S}_{j}(\mathcal{A})$ , namely,
for each entry $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\neq 0$ ,
(2.5)
 $\phi_{i_{1}}(x)+\cdots+\phi_{i_{k}}(x)\equiv\frac{kj}{\ell}\mod k.$ 
The property (2.5) is called uniform angle property.
The following theorem gives the decomposition of the $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -module $\mathbb{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A})$ , and hence the stabilizing index $s(\mathcal{A})$ of $\mathcal{A}$ .
Theorem 2.9.
[14, Theorem 3.6]
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a nonnegative combinatorial symmetric weakly irreducible tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ . Suppose that $B_{\mathcal{A}}$ has a Smith normal form over $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ with invariant divisors $d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}$ . Then $1\leq r\leq n-1$ , and
 $\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathbb{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A})\cong\oplus_{i,d_{i}\neq 1}\mathbb{Z}_{d_{i}}\oplus\underbrace{\mathbb{Z}_{k}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{k}}_{n-r-1\mbox{~copies}}.$ 
Consequently, $s(\mathcal{A})=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{A})|=k^{n-r-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}$ .
2.3. Hypergraphs
A hypergraph $H=(V(H),E(H))$ consists a vertex set $V(H)$ and an edge set $E(G)$ whose elements are the subsets of $V(H)$ .
The degree of a vertex $v$ of $H$ , denoted by $d_{v}$ , is the number of edges of $H$ containing $v$ .
If for each edge $e\in E(H)$ , $|e|=k$ , then $H$ is called $k$ -uniform.
Let $H$ be a $k$ -uniform hypergraph on vertices $v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}$ .
Cooper and Dutle [8] introduced the adjacency tensor $\mathcal{A}(H)$ of $H$ , which is defined to be an order $k$ dimension $n$ tensor
whose $(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k})$ -entry is given by
 $a_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{k}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(k-1)!},&\text{ if }\{v_{i_{1}},v_{i_{2}},\dots v_{i_{k}}\}\in E(H),\\
~~0,&\text{ otherwise }.\end{cases}$ 
The Laplacian tensor of $H$ is defined as $\mathcal{L}(H)=\mathcal{D}(H)-\mathcal{A}(H)$ and the signless Laplacian tensor of $H$ is defined as $\mathcal{Q}(H)=\mathcal{D}(H)+\mathcal{A}(H)$ [30], where $\mathcal{D}(H)$ is a $k$ -th order and $n$ -dimensional diagonal tensor such that $d_{v\cdots v}=d_{v}$ , the degree of the vertex $v$ for each $v\in V(H)$ .
It is known that $\mathcal{A}(H),\mathcal{Q}(H)$ are nonnegative, $\mathcal{A}(H),\mathcal{L}(H),\mathcal{Q}(H)$ are all symmetric each of which is weakly irreducible if and only if $H$ is connected [34].
The incidence matrix of $H$ , denoted by $B_{H}=(B_{e,v})$ , coincides with that of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ , that is, $b_{e,v}=1$ if $v\in e$ , and $b_{e,v}=0$ otherwise.
If viewing $B_{H}$ is a matrix over $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ , then the incidence matrices of $\mathcal{L}(H),\mathcal{Q}(H)$ are both $B_{H}$ .
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices.
Define
 $\mathbb{S}_{0}(H)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}:B_{H}x=0,x_{1}=0\}.$ 
By Theorem 2.9,
(2.6)
 $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{A}(H))}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{Q}(H))}(\mathcal{Q}(H))|=|\mathbb{S}_{0}(H)|.$ 
It is known that $0$ is always an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{L}(H)$ associated with an all-one vector $\mathbf{1}$ .
Hu and Qi [21] proved that for any eigenvector $x$ of $\mathcal{L}(H)$ associated with zero eigenvalue, $x$ contains no zero entries, and if normalizing $x$ such that $x_{1}=1$ , then $x$ has the form
(2.7)
 $x=(e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi\phi_{1}(x)/k},\ldots,e^{\mathbf{i}2\pi\phi_{n}(x)/k}),$ 
where $\phi_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ and $\phi_{1}(x)=0$ .
Furthermore, $x$ has the uniform angle property, namely, for each edge $e\in E(H)$ ,
(2.8)
 $\sum_{i\in e}\phi_{i}(x)=0\mod k.$ 
Assume each point of $\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))$ has the form (2.7).
By introducing the Hadamard product in $\mathbb{V}_{0}^{\mathcal{L}}(H)$ , Fan et al. [18] proved that $\mathbb{V}_{0}^{\mathcal{L}}(H)$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ -module that is isomorphic to $\mathbb{S}_{0}(H)$ .
So,
(2.9)
 $|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))|=|\mathbb{S}_{0}(H)|.$ 
Finally, we discuss the zero eigenvalue of $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ .
It was proved that zero is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ if and only if $k$ is even and $H$ is odd-colorable [29, 21, 18].
If $x$ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ associated with the zero eigenvalue, then by normalizing $x$ such that $x_{1}=1$ , $x$ also has the form (2.7) and the uniform angle property ([21]): for each $e\in E(H)$ ,
(2.10)
 $\sum_{i\in e}\phi_{i}(x)=\frac{k}{2}\mod k.$ 
Fan et al. [18] proved that if zero is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ , then
(2.11)
 $|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{Q}(H))|=|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))|=|\mathbb{S}_{0}(H)|.$ 
3. Local ring and multiplicity
Let $\mathbb{K}$ be an algebraic closed field.
For $p=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in\mathbb{K}^{n}$ , denote $M_{p}=\langle x_{1}-p_{1},\ldots,x_{n}-p_{n}\rangle$ , the maximal ideal in $\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ .
Define
 $\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}=\{f/g:f,g\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],g(p)\neq 0\}.$ 
It is well-known that $\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}$ is the so-called local ring.
Let $J$ be a zero-dimensional ideal in $\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ , and let
 $J\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}$ be the ideal generated by $J$ in the larger ring $\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}$ .
Let $\mathcal{V}(J)$ denote the zero set of $J$ and let $p\in\mathcal{V}(J)$ .
The local (intersection) multiplicity (simply called multiplicity) of $p$ is defined to be
 $m(p):=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}/J\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}},$ 
the dimension of the quotient $\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}/J\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{M_{p}}$ as a vector space over $\mathbb{K}$ .
There is another equivalent definition of multiplicity using dual space.
For an index array $j=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}$ and $x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})$ , write
 $x^{j}=x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{j_{n}}$ , $(x-y)^{j}=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{j_{1}}\cdots(x_{n}-y_{n})^{j_{n}}$ .
The order of $j$ is $|j|=j_{1}+\cdots+j_{n}$ .
Define
 $\partial_{j}=\partial_{j_{1}\ldots j_{n}}=\frac{1}{j_{1}!\ldots j_{n}!}\frac{\partial^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{n}}}{\partial x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{n}^{j_{n}}},$ 
and a functional at $p$ :
 $\partial_{j}[p]:\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\to\mathbb{C},f\mapsto(\partial_{j}f)(p).$ 
The dual space of $J$ at $p$ is defined to be
 $\mathcal{D}_{p}(J)=\{c=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}c_{j}\partial_{j}[p]:c(f)=0\mbox{~for all ~}f\in J\}.$ 
For $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}$ , let $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{p}(J)$ denote the set of functionals in $\mathcal{D}_{p}(J)$ with differential orders bounded by $\alpha$ .
The Hilbert function is defined as
 $H(0)=\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}^{0}_{p}(I)=1,H(\alpha)=\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{p}(I)-\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha-1}_{p}(I)\mbox{~for~}\alpha=1,2,\ldots.$ 
Let $\sigma$ be the smallest integer such that $\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{p}(I)=\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha+1}_{p}(I)$ (or equivalently, $H(\alpha+1)=0)$ ).
Then $\mathcal{D}_{p}(I)=\mathcal{D}^{\sigma}_{p}(I)$ .
Dayton and Zeng [10] proved that the multiplicity of $p\in\mathcal{V}(I)$ is $m$ if and only if the dimension of $\mathcal{D}_{p}(I)$ is $m$ .
Furthermore, they used the multiplicity matrices to characterize the Hilbert functions.
Theorem 3.1.
[10]
Let $p$ be an isolated zero of $f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ , where $t\geq n$ . For $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}$ , a functional $\sum_{|j|\leq\alpha}c_{j}\partial_{j}[p]$ is in
 $\mathcal{D}_{p}(\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}\rangle)$ if and only if the coefficient vector $c=[c_{j}:|j|\leq\alpha]\in\mathbb{K}^{n_{\alpha}}$ satisfies
 $\sum_{|j|\leq\alpha}c_{j}\partial_{j}((x-p)^{k}f_{i})(p)=0,k\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|k|<\alpha,i\in[t],$ 
which corresponds to a homogeneous linear system $S_{\alpha}c=0$ , where $S_{\alpha}$ is an $m_{\alpha}\times n_{\alpha}$ matrix with rows indexed by $(k,i)$ and columns indexed by $j$ , and is called the $\alpha$ -th order multiplicity matrix.
Here $m_{\alpha}=\binom{\alpha-1+n}{\alpha-1}\cdot t$ and $n_{\alpha}=\binom{\alpha+n}{\alpha}$ .
Consequently,
 $H(\alpha)=\mbox{nullity}(S_{\alpha})-\mbox{nullity}(S_{\alpha-1})$ 
for $\alpha=1,2,\ldots$ , with $S_{0}=[f_{1}(p),\ldots,f_{t}(p)]^{\top}=O_{t\times 1}$ .
By Theorem 3.1, we immediately get the following result on simple zeros (i.e. zeros with multiplicity $1$ ).
Corollary 3.2.
Let $p$ be an isolated zero of $f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ .
Then $p$ is a simple zero if and only if the Jacobian matrix
 $J(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})=\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{t\times n}$ 
has rank $n$ at $p$ .
Proof.
The multiplicity matrix $S_{1}$ in Theorem 3.1 can be written as
 $S_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}f_{1}(p)&\\
\vdots&J(p)\\
f_{t}(p)&\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&\\
\vdots&J(p)\\
0&\end{array}\right],$ 
where $J(p)$ is the Jacobian matrix $J(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})$ at $p$ .
So, $p$ is a simple zero of $f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}$ if and only if $H(1)=0$ , namely,
 $\mbox{nullity}(S_{1})=\mbox{nullity}(S_{0})=1$ , which is equivalent to say $J(p)$ 
has rank $n$ .
∎
Lemma 3.3.
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a combinatorial symmetric weakly irreducible tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ such that $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\neq 0$ only if $i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}$ are equal or pairwise distinct.
Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ and let $p\in\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ , which satisfy the following properties:
(1) 
 $\lambda\neq a_{i,\ldots,i}$ for any $i\in[n]$ ,
(2) 
 $p$ has no zero entries,
(3) 
there exists a real number $\alpha$ such that for each set $e=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}$ with $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\neq 0$ , $p^{e}=|p^{e}|e^{\mathbf{i}\alpha}$ , where $p^{e}:=\prod_{i\in e}p_{i}$ .
Then $p$ has multiplicity $1$ as a point of $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})$ .
Proof.
Noting that $\mathcal{A}$ is combinatorial symmetric, we denote
 $E=\{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}:a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\neq 0,i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\mbox{~are pairwise distinct}\},$ 
and associate $\mathcal{A}$ with a hypergraph $H$ with vertex set $[n]$ and edge set $E$ .
As $\mathcal{A}$ is weakly irreducible, $H$ is connected.
Let $E_{i}$ denote the set of edges of $H$ containing the vertex $i$ for $i\in[n]$ , and
 $E_{i,j}$ denote the set of edges of $H$ containing both $i$ and $j$ for $i\neq j$ and $i,j\in[n]$ .
Let
 $F_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\lambda-a_{i,\ldots,i})x_{i}^{k-1}-\sum_{e\in E_{i}}x^{e\setminus\{i\}},i\in[n].$ 
By definition, $p$ is a zero of $F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}$ .
By assumption, $p$ has no zero entries so that we can assume $p_{n}=1$ .
Let
 $f_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=F_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},1),i\in[n].$ 
Then $\tilde{p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n-1})$ is a zero of $f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}$ .
Consider the Jacobian matrix $J(\tilde{p})=J(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1})|_{\tilde{p}}$ :
 $J(\tilde{p})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}(k-1)(\lambda-a_{1,\ldots,1})p_{1}^{k-2}&-\sum_{e\in E_{1,2}}p^{e\setminus\{1,2,n\}}&\cdots&-\sum_{e\in E_{1,n-1}}p^{e\setminus\{1,n-1,n\}}\\
-\sum_{e\in E_{1,2}}p^{e\setminus\{1,2,n\}}&(k-1)(\lambda-a_{2,\ldots,2})p_{2}^{k-2}&\cdots&-\sum_{e\in E_{2,n-1}}p^{e\setminus\{2,n-1,n\}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
-\sum_{e\in E_{1,n-1}}p^{e\setminus\{1,n-1,n\}}&-\sum_{e\in E_{2,n-1}}p^{e\setminus\{2,n-1,n\}}&\cdots&(k-1)(\lambda-a_{n-1,\ldots,n-1})p_{n-1}^{k-2}\end{array}\right].$ 
Let $H_{n}$ be the hypergraph obtained from $H$ which has vertex set $[n-1]$ and edge set $\{e\setminus\{n\}:e\in E(H)\}$ .
Suppose $H_{n}$ has connected components $C_{1},\ldots,C_{s}$ , $s\geq 1$ .
Then after a relabeling of the vertices, we can write $J(\tilde{p})$ as the following form:
 $J(\tilde{p})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}J_{1}(\tilde{p})&O&\cdots&O\\
O&J_{2}(\tilde{p})&\cdots&O\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
O&O&\cdots&J_{s}(\tilde{p})\end{array}\right],$ 
where $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ is indexed by the vertices of $C_{i}$ , and for $v,w\in V(C_{i})$ ,
 $J_{i}(\tilde{p})_{vv}=(k-1)(\lambda-a_{v,\ldots,v})p_{v}^{k-2},J_{i}(\tilde{p})_{vw}=-\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}p^{e\setminus\{v,w,n\}}\mbox{~for~}v\neq w.$ 
We first show each $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ is irreducible.
By assumption and the fact $p_{n}=1$ ,
 $J_{i}(\tilde{p})_{vw}=-\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}p^{e\setminus\{v,w,n\}}=-\frac{1}{p_{v}p_{w}}\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}p^{e}=-\frac{1}{p_{v}p_{w}}e^{\mathbf{i}\alpha}\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}|p^{e}|,$ 
so $J_{i}(\tilde{p})_{vw}\neq 0$ if and only if $E_{v,w}\neq\emptyset$ .
Noting that $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ is symmetric, we can define a graph $G_{i}$ associated with $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ on the vertex of $C_{i}$ such that $\{v,w\}$ is an edge of $G_{i}$ if and only if $E_{v,w}\neq\emptyset$ .
As $C_{i}$ is connected, $G_{i}$ is connected and hence $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ is irreducible.
Next we will show that $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ is nonsingular.
Let $D=\mathrm{diag}\{p_{v}:v\in V(C_{i})\}$ such that its rows are arranged conformal with the rows of $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ .
Surely $D$ is nonsingular as $p$ has no zero entries.
For the matrix $J_{i}(\tilde{p})D$ and each $v\in V(C_{i})$ ,
 $\displaystyle\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v\}}\left|(J_{i}(\tilde{p})D)_{vw}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v\}}\left|\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}p^{e\setminus\{v,w,n\}}p_{w}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v\}}\left|\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}p^{e\setminus\{v,n\}}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v\}}\frac{1}{|p_{v}|}\left|\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}p^{e\setminus\{n\}}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v\}}\frac{1}{|p_{v}|}\sum_{e\in E_{v,w}}\left|p^{e\setminus\{n\}}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E_{v}}\frac{1}{|p_{v}|}\left|p^{e\setminus\{n\}}\right|\sum_{w\in e\backslash\{v,n\}}1,$ 
where the fourth equality holds as $p_{n}=1$ and $p^{e\backslash\{n\}}=p^{e}=|p^{e}|e^{\mathbf{i}\alpha}$ for all edges $e$ of $H$ under assumption.
Note that $\sum_{w\in e\backslash\{v,n\}}1$ equals $k-2$ or $k-1$ depending on whether $n$ lies in $e$ or not.
So,
 $\displaystyle\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v\}}\left|(J_{i}(\tilde{p})D)_{vw}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq(k-1)\frac{1}{|p_{v}|}\sum_{e\in E_{v}}\left|p^{e\setminus\{n\}}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=(k-1)\frac{1}{|p_{v}|}\left|\sum_{e\in E_{v}}p^{e\setminus\{n\}}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=(k-1)\left|\sum_{e\in E_{v}}p^{e\setminus\{v,n\}}\right|$ 
 $\displaystyle=(k-1)|\lambda-a_{v,\ldots,v}||p_{v}^{k-1}|$ 
 $\displaystyle=|(J_{i}(\tilde{p})D)_{vv}|,$ 
where the fourth equality follows by the eigenvector equation of $\mathcal{A}$ .
So, $J_{i}(\tilde{p})D$ is diagonal dominant.
Furthermore, by the definition of $H_{n}$ , there exists an edge $e_{0}$ of $H$ such that $e_{0}\backslash\{n\}$ is contained in $C_{i}$ .
Taking a vertex $v_{0}\in e_{0}\backslash\{n\}$ , and noting $\sum_{w\in e_{0}\backslash\{v_{0},n\}}1=k-2$ , by the above discussion we will have
 $\sum_{w\in V(C_{i})\backslash\{v_{0}\}}\left|(J_{i}(\tilde{p})D)_{v_{0}w}\right|<|(J_{i}(\tilde{p})D)_{v_{0}v_{0}}|.$ 
So by the knowledge of matrix theory ([20, Corollary 6.2.9]), $J_{i}(\tilde{p})D$ is nonsingular, and hence $J_{i}(\tilde{p})$ is nonsingular.
The result now follows by Corollary 3.2.
∎
Corollary 3.4.
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H$ .
Then for any eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ with $|\lambda|=\rho(\mathcal{A}(H))$ , each point $p\in\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}(H))$ has multiplicity $1$ .
Proof.
It suffices to check the conditions in Lemma 3.3 for the adjacency tensor $\mathcal{A}(H)$ .
As $H$ is connected, surely $\mathcal{A}(H)$ is combinatorial symmetric and weakly irreducible.
Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ with $|\lambda|=\rho(\mathcal{A}(H))$ .
Then $\lambda\neq 0$ , which satisfies (1) of Lemma 3.3.
Each point $p\in\mathbb{V}^{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}(H)$ has no zero entries by Theorem 2.1, satisfying (2) of Lemma 3.3.
By (2.5) of the uniform angle property, the condition (3) of Lemma 3.3 also holds for $p$ .
The result now follows.
∎
Similarly, we have the following result for the signless Laplacian tensor $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ of a connected hypergraph $H$ .
Note that the cyclic index of $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ is $1$ by Theorem 2.3 as $\mathrm{Tr}_{1}(\mathcal{Q}(H))>0$ , which implies that $\rho(\mathcal{Q}(H))$ is the unique eigenvalue with modulus $\rho(\mathcal{Q}(H))$ .
Corollary 3.5.
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H$ , and let $\rho$ be the spectral radius of $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ .
Then each point $p\in\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{Q}(H))$ has multiplicity $1$ .
Proof.
Let $\Delta$ be the maximum degree of $H$ .
By [22, Theorem 4.6], $\rho>\Delta\geq d_{v}$ for all vertices $v$ of $H$ , implying that (1) of Lemma 3.3 holds.
The conditions (2) and (3) of Lemma 3.3 hold for $p\in\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{Q}(H))$ by Theorem 2.1 and (2.5) respectively.
∎
Corollary 3.6.
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H$ .
Then each point $p\in\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))$ has multiplicity $1$ .
Proof.
As $H$ is connected, $d_{v}\neq 0$ for each vertex $v$ of $H$ , implying that (1) of Lemma 3.3 holds.
The conditions (2) and (3) of Lemma 3.3 hold for $p\in\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))$ by (2.7) and (2.8) respectively.
∎
Similarly to (2.10) and the discussion therein, we get the following result.
Corollary 3.7.
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H$ .
If $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ has a zero eigenvalue, then each point $p\in\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{Q}(H))$ has multiplicity $1$ .
4. Hu-Ye’s conjecture on spectral radius of tensors
Given homogeneous polynomials $F_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\ldots,F_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ of degrees $d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}$ respectively, where $\mathbb{K}$ is an algebraic closed field.
Let
(4.1)
 $\bar{F}_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}):=F_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},0),i\in[n],$ 
(4.2)
 $f_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}):=F_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},1),i\in[n].$ 
Theorem 4.1.
(Poisson formula)
If $\mathrm{Res}(\bar{F}_{1},\ldots,\bar{F}_{n-1})\neq 0$ , then the quotient ring
 $A=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]/\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\rangle$ has dimension $d_{1}\cdots d_{n-1}$ as a vector space, and
 $\mathrm{Res}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n})=\mathrm{Res}(\bar{F}_{1},\ldots,\bar{F}_{n-1})^{d_{n}}\det(m_{f_{n}}:A\to A),$ 
where $m_{f_{n}}:A\to A$ is a linear map given by multiplication by $f_{n}$ .
It is known that for each $p\in\mathcal{V}(\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\rangle)$ , $f_{n}(p)$ is an eigenvalue of $m_{f_{n}}$ with multiplicity $m(p)$ , and
(4.3)
 $\det m_{f_{n}}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{V}(\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\rangle)}f_{n}(p)^{m(p)}.$ 
Let $\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})$ be a tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ , and let
(4.4)
 $F_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\lambda x_{i}^{k-1}-\sum_{i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}a_{ii_{2}\ldots i_{k}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}},i\in[n].$ 
Then the characteristic polynomial of $\mathcal{A}$ is the resultant
 $\varphi_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda)=\mathrm{Res}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}).$ 
Let $\mathbb{K}=\overline{\mathbb{C}[\lambda]}$ be the algebraic closure of $\mathbb{C}[\lambda]$ .
Then $\mathrm{Res}(\bar{F}_{1},\ldots,\bar{F}_{n-1})\neq 0$ , so that we can apply Poisson Formula to $\mathrm{Res}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n})$ .
Theorem 4.2.
Let $\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})$ be a nonnegative weakly irreducible tensor of order $k$ and dimension $n$ , and let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ with modulus equal to the spectral radius $\rho$ .
Then
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})|,$ 
and Hu-Ye’s conjecture holds for the eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ .
Proof.
By Corollary 2.4, $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)=\mathrm{am}(\rho)$ ; and
by Corollary 2.7, $|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})|=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})|$ .
So it suffices to consider the eigenvalue $\rho$ .
For $i\in[n]$ , let $F_{i}$ be defined in (4.4), and $\bar{F}_{i},f_{i}$ be defined as in (4.1) and (4.2) respectively.
By Theorem 4.1 and (4.3), we have
 $\mathrm{Res}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n})=\mathrm{Res}(\bar{F}_{1},\ldots,\bar{F}_{n-1})^{k-1}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{V}(J)}f_{n}(p)^{m(p)},$ 
where $J=\langle f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}\rangle$ .
Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the sub-tensor of $\mathcal{A}$ indexed by $[n-1]$ , i.e.,
 $\mathcal{B}$ consists of the entries $a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}$ of $\mathcal{A}$ for all $i_{j}\in[n-1]$ and $j\in[k]$ .
Then the characteristic polynomial of $\mathcal{B}$ is
 $\varphi_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)=\mathrm{Res}(\bar{F}_{1},\ldots,\bar{F}_{n-1}).$ 
It is known that $\rho(\mathcal{B})<\rho(\mathcal{A})=\rho$ as $\mathcal{A}$ is nonnegative and weakly irreducible (see [24, Corollary 3.4].
So, $\rho$ is not a root of $\mathrm{Res}(\bar{F}_{1},\ldots,\bar{F}_{n-1})$ , which implies that $\rho$ is a root of $\prod_{p\in\mathcal{V}(I)}f_{n}(p)^{m(p)}$ .
Let
 $\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)=\{p\in\mathcal{V}(J):f_{n}(p)|_{\lambda=\rho}=0\}.$ 
For each $p\in\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)$ , write
 $p=p(\lambda)=(p_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,p_{n-1}(\lambda)).$ 
Then
 $p(\rho)$ is a root of $f_{1}(\rho),\ldots,f_{n-1}(\rho)$ .
Let $\bar{p}(\lambda)=(p_{1}(\lambda),\ldots,p_{n-1}(\lambda),1)$ .
By definition,
 $\bar{p}(\rho)$ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\rho$ .
So we can define a map
(4.5)
 $\psi:\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)\to\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}),p(\lambda)\mapsto\bar{p}(\rho).$ 
We show that $\psi$ is surjective.
For each $x\in\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})$ , by Theorem 2.1(2), $x$ has all entries nonzero.
So we can assume $x_{n}=1$ by normalization.
Let $\tilde{x}$ be the restriction of $x$ on $[n-1]$ .
Then, by taking $\lambda=\rho$ in $f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}$ , we have
 $\tilde{x}\in\mathcal{V}(J)|_{\lambda=\rho},f_{n}(\tilde{x})|_{\lambda=\rho}=0,$ 
which implies that there exists a point $p=p(\lambda)\in\mathcal{V}(J)$ such that
 $\tilde{x}=p(\rho)$ and $f_{n}(p)|_{\lambda=\rho}=0$ .
So $\psi$ is a surjective map.
For each $p\in\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)$ , denote by $m_{\rho}(p)$ the multiplicity of $\rho$ as a root of $f_{n}(p)$ .
We have
(4.6)
 $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)}m_{\rho}(p)m(p)\geq\sum_{\bar{p}(\rho)\in\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})}m(\bar{p}(\rho))=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})}m(x)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})|.$ 
As $\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})$ is finite with elements $x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(\kappa)}$ , where $\kappa=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})|$ , the eigenvariety $\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})$ possesses $\kappa$ irreducible components $\mathbb{C}x^{(1)},\ldots,\mathbb{C}x^{(\kappa)}$ each with dimension $1$ .
 $\mathrm{am}(\rho)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})|=\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}\mathrm{dim}(\mathbb{C}x^{(i)})(k-1)^{\mathrm{dim}(\mathbb{C}x^{(i)})-1},$ 
which implies that (1.1) holds.
By the above discussion, $\mathrm{gm}(\rho)=1$ , so that
 $\mathrm{am}(\rho)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A})|\geq\mathrm{gm}(\rho)(k-1)^{\mathrm{gm}(\rho)-1}=1.$ 
The discussion for other eigenvalues $\lambda$ with modulus $\rho$ is similar.
∎
Lastly, we will show that Hu-Ye’s conjecture also holds for the least H-eigenvalue of a $Z$ -tensor, where an eigenvalue is called an H-eigenvalue if it is associated with a real eigenvector.
Recall a real tensor $\mathcal{A}$ is called a $Z$ -tensor if all of its off-diagonal entries are non-positive, or equivalently, it can be written as
 $\mathcal{A}=s\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{B},$ 
where $s>0$ and $\mathcal{B}$ is nonnegative; see [11, 37].
The least H-eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ is exactly $s-\rho(B)$ , which is also the eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ with the least real part; see [37] for a similar discussion.
Corollary 4.3.
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a weakly irreducible $Z$ -tensor, and let $\lambda$ be the least H-eigenvalue of $\mathcal{A}$ .
Then
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)\geq|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})|,$ 
and Hu-Ye’s conjecture holds for the eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}$ .
Proof.
Write $\mathcal{A}$ as $\mathcal{A}=s\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{B},$ 
where $s>0$ and $\mathcal{B}$ is nonnegative and weakly irreducible.
Then $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)$ equals the algebraic multiplicity of $\rho(\mathcal{B})$ as an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{B}$ .
Observe that $\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{V}_{\rho(\mathcal{B})}(\mathcal{B})$ as $x$ is an eigenvector of $\mathcal{A}$ associated with $\lambda$ if and only if it is an
eigenvector of $\mathcal{B}$ associated with $\rho(\mathcal{B})$ ;
or see [17, Lemma 3.1].
The result follows by Theorem 4.2.
∎
5. Hu-Ye’s conjecture on eigenvalues of hypergraphs
We first introduce some classes of hypergraphs.
A hypertree is a connected hypergraph without cycles.
Let $k\geq 3$ be an integer, and let $G$ be a simple graph.
The $k$ -th power of $G$ , denoted by $G^{(k)}$ , is a $k$ -uniform hypergraph obtained from $G$ by replacing each edge (a $2$ -set) with an $k$ -set by adding $k-2$ additional vertices.
Let $P_{m},C_{m},S_{m}$ be a path, cycle and a star all of $m$ edges (all as simple graphs), respectively.
Then $P_{m}^{(k)}$ is called a (loose) hyperpath, $C_{m}^{(k)}$ is called a hypercycle, and $S_{m}^{(k)}$ is called a hyperstar.
A complete $k$ -uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices, denoted by $K_{n}^{[k]}$ , is a hypergraph with any $k$ -subsets of the vertex set being an edge.
5.1. The spectral radius of adjacency tensor of a hypergraph
In this section, the eigenvalues,spectral radius of a hypergraph $H$ always refers to its adjacency tensor $\mathcal{A}(H)$ .
The following results on the algebraic multiplicity are based on Poisson formula.
Combining with chip-firing game on graphs, Bao and Fan et al. [1] give a recursive and explicit formula for the characteristic polynomials of a class of starlike hypertrees, including hyperpaths and hyperstars.
Their result implies that the spectral radius of a $k$ -uniform starlike hypertree with $m$ edges has algebraic multiplicity $k^{m(k-2)}$ .
Duan, van Dam and Wang [12] gives an explicit formula of the characteristic polynomial of a $k$ -uniform double hyperstar with $m$ edges, which implies that the
algebraic multiplicity of its spectral radius is also $k^{m(k-2)}$ .
Chen and Bu [5] proved a general result as follows.
Theorem 5.1.
[5]
The algebraic multiplicity of the spectral radius of a $k$ -uniform hypertree with
 $m$ edges is $k^{m(k-2)}$ .
Recently, Li, Su and Fallat [25] expressed the characteristic polynomial of a uniform hypertree in terms of the matching polynomials of its subhypertrees, and provided a new insight for the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalues of hypertrees.
Zheng [40] gives an explicit formula of the characteristic polynomial of a complete $3$ -uniform hypergraph $K_{n}^{[3]}$ on $n$ vertices, which is a product of polynomials of degree at most $3$ .
His result implies that the spectral radius of $K_{n}^{[3]}$ has algebraic multiplicity $1$ if $n>3$ .
The following results on the algebraic multiplicity are based on traces.
By using the eigenvalues of signed graph, Duan, van Dam and Wang [12] gives an explicit formula of the characteristic polynomial of $C_{3}^{(k)}$ (also called hypertriangle), which implies that the
algebraic multiplicity of its spectral radius is $k^{|V(C_{3}^{(k)})|-|E(C_{3}^{(k)})|-1}=k^{3k-7}.$ 
In a sequent paper [13], Duan, Wang and Wei gives an explicit formula of the characteristic polynomial of $C_{4}^{(k)}$ , which implies that the
algebraic multiplicity of its spectral radius is also $k^{|V(C_{3}^{(k)})|-|E(C_{3}^{(k)})|-1}=k^{4k-9}.$ 
Recently, Chen, van Dam and Bu [7] express the entire spectrum (including multiplicities and characteristic polynomial) of $G^{(k)}$ in terms of parity-closed walk of $G$ , and get a general result as follows.
Theorem 5.2.
[7]
For a connected graph $G$ ,
the algebraic multiplicity of the spectral radius of $G^{(k)}$ is
 $k^{|V(G^{(k)})|-|E(G^{(k)})|-1}=k^{|V(G)|+|E(G)|(k-3)-1}.$ 
Lemma 5.3.
[14]
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph with spectral radius $\rho$ . Then the following results hold.
(1) 
If $H$ is a hypertree with $m$ edges, then $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=k^{m(k-2)}$ .
(2) 
If $H=G^{(k)}$ for a connected graph $G$ , then $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=k^{|V(G)|+|E(G)|(k-3)-1}$ .
(3) 
If $H=K_{n}^{[k]}$ with $n>k$ , then $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=1$ .
Proof.
The results (2) and (3) follow from [14, Corollary 4.4] and [14, Example 4.4] respectively.
In fact, they can be obtained from Theorem 2.9 by considering the incidence matrix.
If $H=G^{(k)}$ as in (2), then the invariant divisors $B_{H}$ over $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ are $1$ ’s with multiplicity $|E(H)|$ .
So by Theorem 2.9,
 $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=k^{|V(H)|-|E(H)|-1}=k^{|V(G)|+|E(H)|(k-3)-1}$ 
as $|V(H)|=|V(G)|+|E(G)|(k-2)$ and $|E(H)|=|E(G)|$ .
If $H=K_{n}^{[k]}$ , consider (2.4) for $\mathcal{A}(H)$ :
 $\mathbb{S}_{0}(\mathcal{A}(H))=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{k}^{n}:B_{H}x=0\mbox{~over~}\mathbb{Z}_{k},x_{1}=0\}.$ 
It only has zero element so that $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(H)|=1$ .
If $H$ is a $k$ -uniform hypertree with $m$ edges, let $e$ be a pendent edge of $H$ and let $H-e$ be the sub-hypertree of $H$ by deleting the edge $e$ together with the vertices of $e$ of degree $1$ .
By elementary transformations, $B_{H}\cong 1\oplus B_{H-e}$ .
So, by induction $B_{H}$ has invariant divisors $1$ ’s over $\mathbb{Z}_{k}$ with multiplicity $m$ .
Noting that $|V(H)|=m(k-1)+1$ , so
 $|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=k^{|V(H)|-m-1}=k^{m(k-2)}$ by Theorem 2.9.
∎
Theorem 5.4.
If $H$ is one of the following hypergraphs:
(1) 
 $k$ -uniform hypertrees,
(2) 
 $k$ -th power of connected simple graphs,
(3) 
complete $3$ -uniform hypergraphs on at least $4$ vertices,
then for any eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ with modulus equal to the spectral radius,
 $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)=|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}(H))|.$ 
Consequently, (1.1) in Hu-Ye’s conjecture holds equality for the eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ .
Proof.
Let $\rho$ be the spectral radius of $H$ .
If $H$ is a $k$ -uniform hypertree or a $k$ -th power of connected simple graph, by Theorem 5.1, Theorem 5.2 and Lemma 5.3, we have $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|.$ 
If $H$ is a $3$ -uniform hypergraphs on at least $4$ vertices, then $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=1$ ([40]), and hence by Lemma 5.3, $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|.$ 
For any eigenvalue $\lambda$ with $|\lambda|=\rho$ , by Corollary 2.4 and Corollary 2.7, $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)=\mathrm{am}(\rho)$ and $|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}(H))|=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|$ , which implies that $\mathrm{am}(\lambda)=|\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{A}(H))|.$ 
By a similar discussion as the final part of the proof of Theorem 4.2,
we easily get the equality in (1.1) for the eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathcal{A}(H)$ .
The result follows.
∎
Finally, we pose the following conjecture.
Conjecture 5.5.
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph with spectral radius $\rho$ .
Then
 $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|.$ 
Surely Conjecture 5.5 holds for the hypergraphs in Theorem 5.4.
From (4.6) in the proof of Theorem 4.2, if $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|$ , then the following conditions hold:
(1) 
 $\rho$ is a simple root of $f_{n}(p)$ for each $p\in\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)$ ,
(2) 
the map $\psi:\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(J)\to\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))$ in (4.5) is injective (and hence bijective).
The above conditions (1) and (2) are also sufficient for the equality $\mathrm{am}(\rho)=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|$ as $m(x)=1$ for each $x\in\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))$ by Corollary 3.4.
Here are some concrete examples to support the condition (1).
In [5] it was proved that for a $k$ -uniform uniform hypertree $T$ with a vertex $u$ , then for each $p\in\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(\langle f_{v}:v\in V(T)\setminus u\rangle)$ 
 $f_{u}(p)=\frac{\mathfrak{m}_{T}(\lambda)}{\mathfrak{m}_{T-u}(\lambda)},$ 
where $\mathfrak{m}_{T}(\lambda)$ is the matching polynomial of $T$ , and $T-u$ is obtained from $T$ by deleting the vertex $u$ together with all edges containing $u$ .
The function $f_{u}(p)$ has degree $1$ in $\lambda$ and $\rho$ is a simple root of $f_{n}(p)$ or $\mathfrak{m}_{T}(\lambda)$ .
In [6] it was proved that for a $k$ -uniform hyperpath $P_{m}^{(k)}$ with a pendent vertex $u$ , then for each $p\in\mathcal{V}_{\rho}(\langle f_{v}:v\in V(P_{m}^{(k)})\setminus u\rangle)$ 
 $f_{u}(p)=\frac{\varphi_{P_{m}}(\lambda^{k/2})}{\lambda^{k-1}\varphi_{P_{m-2}}(\lambda^{k/2})},$ 
where $\varphi_{P_{m}}(\lambda)$ is the characteristic polynomial of a path $P_{m}$ .
We also find that $f_{u}(p)$ has the degree $1$ in $\lambda$ and $\rho$ is a simple root of $f_{n}(p)$ or $\varphi_{P_{m}}(\lambda^{k/2})$ .
5.2. The zero eigenvalue of Laplacian or signless Laplaican of a hypergraph
Recently, Zheng [39] proved that the algebraic multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian tensor of a $k$ -uniform hyperpath or hyperstar with $m$ edges is $k^{m(k-2)}$ .
Lin and Bu [27] generalized the above result for a general $k$ -uniform hypertree.
Theorem 5.6.
Let $T$ be a $k$ -uniform hypertree with $m$ edges.
Then the algebraic multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of $\mathcal{L}(T)$ is $k^{m(k-2)}$ .
For a $k$ -uniform hypertree $T$ , by Theorem 5.1 and Theorem 5.6, we have
(5.1)
 $\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{L}(T))=\mathrm{am}(\rho,\mathcal{A}(T)).$ 
For a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H$ , by (2.6) and (2.9), we have
(5.2)
 $|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))|=|\mathbb{V}_{\rho}(\mathcal{A}(H))|.$ 
So, by Theorem 5.4, we immediately get the following result.
Theorem 5.7.
Let $T$ be a $k$ -uniform hypertree. Then
 $\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{L}(T))=|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(T))|.$ 
Consequently, (1.1) in Hu-Ye’s conjecture holds equality for the zero eigenvalue of $\mathcal{L}(T)$ .
We think (5.1) should hold for all connected $k$ -uniform hypergraphs.
Conjecture 5.8.
Let $H$ be a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph. Then
(5.3)
 $\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{L}(H))=\mathrm{am}(\rho,\mathcal{A}(H)).$ 
Consequently,
(5.4)
 $\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{L}(H))=|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))|.$ 
Finally, we consider the signless Laplacian tensor of a connected $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H$ .
Fan et al. [18] proved that $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ has a zero eigenvalue if and only if there exists a nonsingular diagonal matrix $D$ such that $\mathcal{Q}(H)=D^{-(m-1)}\mathcal{L}(H)D$ .
So $\mathcal{Q}(H)$ and $\mathcal{L}(H)$ have the same spectrum [32] in this case, which implies that
 $\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{Q}(H))=\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{L}(H)).$ 
By (2.11), $|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{Q}(H))|=|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{L}(H))|$ .
So, if (5.4) holds, we will have
 $\mathrm{am}(0,\mathcal{Q}(H))=|\mathbb{V}_{0}(\mathcal{Q}(H))|.$
