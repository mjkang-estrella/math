Abstract
 
We show that for any integer $k\geq 1$ there exists an integer $t_{0}(k)$ 
such that for integers $t,k_{1},\ldots,k_{t+1},n$ with $t>t_{0}(k)$ ,
 $\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t+1}\}\leq k$ , and $n>2k(t+1)$ , the following
holds:
If $F_{i}\subseteq{[n]\choose k_{i}}$ and $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k\choose k_{i}-1}+1$ 
for all $i\in[t+1]$ , then either $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ admits a
rainbow matching of size $t+1$ or there exists $W\in{[n]\choose t}$ 
such that $W$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ .
This may be viewed as a rainbow non-uniform extension of the classical Hilton-Milner theorem.
We also show that the same holds for every $t$ and $n>2k^{3}t$ ,
generalizing a recent stability result of Frankl and Kupavskii on matchings to
rainbow matchings.
 
1 Introduction
For any positive integer $n$ , let $[n]$ denote the set $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ .
For any nonnegative integer $k$ and any set $S$ , let ${S\choose k}=\{e\subseteq S:|e|=k\}$ .
Let $k\geq 2$ be an integer.
A $k$ -uniform hypergraph or $k$ -graph is a pair
 $H=(V,E)$ , where $V=V(H)$ is the set of vertices of $H$ and
 $E=E(H)\subseteq{V\choose k}$ is the set of edges of $H$ , and
we often identify $H$ with $E(H)$ and use $e(H)$ or $|H|$ to denote the number of edges in $H$ .
For any $S\subseteq V(H)$ , let $H[S]$ denote the
subgraph of $H$ with $V(H[S])=S$ and $E(H[S])=\{e\in E(H):e\subseteq S\}$ , and let $H-S:=H[V(H)\setminus S]$ .
Let $\bar{H}$ be the $k$ -uniform complement of $H$ , i.e.,
 $V(\bar{H})=V(H)$ and $E(\bar{H})={V(H)\choose k}\setminus E(H)$ .
A matching in a hypergraph $H$ is a subset of $E(H)$ consisting
of pairwise disjoint edges. The maximum size of a matching in a hypergraph $H$ 
is denoted by $\nu(H)$ . A vertex cover of $H$ is a set of vertices of $H$ that meets all edges of $H$ , and we use $\tau(H)$ to denote the minimum size of a vertex cover of $H$ .
Clearly, $\nu(H)\leq\tau(H)$ .
A classical problem in extremal set
theory is to determine $\max e(H)$ with $\nu(H)$ fixed.
Erdős
[6] in 1965 made the following conjecture:
For positive integers $k,n,t$ with $n\geq kt$ , every $k$ -graph $H$ on $n$ vertices with $\nu(H)<t$ satisfies $e(H)\leq\max\left\{{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k},{kt-1\choose k}\right\}.$ 
This bound is tight for the complete $k$ -graph on $kt-1$ vertices and for the $k$ -graph on $n$ vertices in which every edge intersects a fixed set of $t-1$ vertices.
There have been recent activities on this conjecture, see [2, 3, 7, 9, 10, 15, 19].
In particular, Frankl [9]
proved that if $n\geq(2t-1)k-(t-1)$ and $\nu(H)<t$ then $e(H)\leq{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}$ , with further improvement by Frankl
and Kupavskii [11].
The work in this paper was motivated by a recent result of Frankl and Kupavskii [12] on a stability version of the Erdős Matching Conjecture.
Let $n,k,s$ be positive integers. For $i\in[k]$ define
 ${\cal A}_{i}^{(k)}(n,s):=\left\{A\in{[n]\choose k}:\left|A\cap[(s+1)i-1]\right|\geq i\right\},$ 
and let ${\cal H}^{(k)}(n,s)$ denote the family
 $\left\{e\in{[n]\choose k}:e\cap[s-1]\neq\emptyset\right\}\cup\left\{[s+k]\setminus[s]\right\}\cup\left\{e\in{[n]\choose k}:e\cap[s]=\{s\},e\cap([s+k]\setminus[s])\neq\emptyset\right\}.$ 
Note that $e({\cal A}_{i}^{(k)}(n,s))\leq{n\choose k}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}{(s+1)i-1\choose j}$ , $\nu({\cal H}^{(k)}(n,s))=s$ , and $e({\cal H}^{(k)}(n,s))={n\choose k}-{n-s\choose k}-{n-s-k\choose k-1}+1$ .
Frankl and Kupavskii [12] proposed the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.1
Let $n,k,t$ be three positive integers such that $n>kt$ . Let $F$ be a
 $k$ -graph of order $n$ . If $\nu(F)\leq t$ , then $\tau(F)\leq t$ or
 $e(F)\leq\max\{e({\cal A}_{2}^{(k)}(n,t)),\dots,e({\cal A}_{k}^{(k)}(n,t)),e({\cal H}^{(k)}(n,t))\}.$ 
The case $t=1$ is the Hilton-Milner theorem in [14].
In [12], Frankl and Kupavskii confirms Conjecture
1.1 for $n\geq(2+o(1))kt$ . We are interested in
extending results like this to rainbow matchings.
Let $\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\}$ be a family
of hypergraphs. A set of pairwise disjoint edges, one from
each $F_{i}$ , is called a rainbow matching for $\mathcal{F}$ , and we say that ${\cal F}$ or
 $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\}$ admits a rainbow matching. Aharoni and
Howard [1] (also see Huang, Loh and Sudakov [15])
conjectured the following rainbow version of the Erdős Matching
Conjecture: If $F_{i}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}$ and $|F_{i}|>\max\left\{{n\choose k}-{n-q+1\choose k},{kq-1\choose k}\right\}$ for $i\in[q]$ then
 $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\}$ admits a rainbow matching. This conjecture was confirmed for $n>3k^{2}q$ by Huang, Loh and Sudakov [15]. The bound $3k^{2}q$ on $n$ was improved to $12kq\log(e^{2}q)$ 
by Frankl and Kupavskii [13]. Lu, Wang and Yu [18] further improved this bound to $n>2kq$ for $q$ sufficiently large.
Recently, Keevash, Lifshitz, Long and Minzer [17] proved a more general result with $n=\Omega(kq)$ using sharp threshold techniques developed in [16].
Our main result in this paper is the following, which extends the result of Frankl and
Kupavskii [12] to rainbow matchings.
Theorem 1.2
Let $k\geq 1$ be any integer. There exists $t_{0}=t_{0}(k)$ such that, for
positive integers $t,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{t+1},n$ with $t>t_{0}$ ,
 $\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t+1}\}\leq k$ , and $n>2k(t+1)$ , the following
holds: If
 $F_{i}\subseteq{[n]\choose k_{i}}$ and
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k\choose k_{i}-1}+1$ 
for all $i\in[t+1]$ , then
(i) 
 $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ admits a rainbow matching of size
 $t+1$ ; or
(ii) 
there exists $W\in{[n]\choose t}$ such that $W$ is a
vertex cover of $F_{i}$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ .
To prove Theorem 1.2, we need to establish the following
result, which may be viewed as a stability version(as well as a
generalization) of the above mentioned result of Huang, Loh and
Sudakov [15] as well as an earlier result of Bollobás, Daykin and Erdős [5].
Theorem 1.3
Let $n,t,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{t+1}$ be positive integers such that $k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq\ldots\geq k_{t+1}\geq 2$ and $n\geq 2k_{1}^{2}k_{2}t$ .
Let $F_{i}\in{[n]\choose k_{i}}$ , $i\in[t+1]$ , such that, for $i\in[2]$ ,
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k_{3-i}\choose k_{i}-1}+1,$ 
and for $i\in[t+1]\setminus[2]$ ,
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k_{3}\choose k_{i}-1}+1.$ 
Then one of the following holds:
(i) 
 $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ admits a rainbow matching of size $t+1$ ; or
(ii) 
there exists $W\in{[n]\choose t}$ such that $W$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ .
In Section 2, we first reduce the problem for finding rainbow matchings for non-uniform hypergraphs to one for uniform hypergraphs (through an operation called “expansion”), and then reduce the problem to
a matching problem for ${\cal F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ , a special class of
uniform hypergraphs. We will see that the extremal hypergraphs of Theorem 1.2 naturally corresponds to a special class of
 $(k+1)$ -graphs, denoted by ${\cal F}_{t+1}(n,k;t)$ and defined later.
This matchings problem for ${\cal F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ will be further reduced to a near perfect matching problem.
In Section 3, we prove Theorem 1.3 by an inductive argument.
We prove Theorem 1.2 in Section 4 when ${\cal F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is close to ${\cal F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ , in the sense that most edges of ${\cal F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ are also edges of ${\cal F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ .
In Section 5, we deal with the case when ${\cal F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is not close to ${\cal F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ . We conclude with a rainbow version of Conjecture 1.1 in Section 6.
2 Reductions
The goal of this section is to reduce the problem for finding rainbow matchings for
non-uniform hypergraphs to a near perfect matching problem for
uniform hypergraphs.
We need to use shadows of hypergraphs.
Let $k,m$ be positive integers.
The $k$ -cascade representation of $m$ is
 $m={a_{k}\choose k}+{a_{k-1}\choose k-1}+\cdots+{a_{s}\choose s}$ 
where $a_{k}>a_{k-1}>\dots>a_{s}\geq s\geq 1$ are integers.
Given a family ${\cal F}$ of sets, the shadow $\partial{\cal F}$ of
 ${\cal F}$ is defined as
 $\partial{\cal F}=\{E:E=F\setminus\{x\}\text{ for some }F\in{\cal F}\text{ and }x\in F\}.$ 
For $i\geq 1$ , we define $\partial^{i+1}{\cal F}=\partial(\partial^{i}{\cal F})$ .
The following result is known as the Kruskal-Katona theorem (see Theorem 1 in [8]).
Theorem 2.1 (Kruskal and Katona)
Let $n,k$ be positive integers and ${\cal F}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}$ .
If $|{\cal F}|={a_{k}\choose k}+{a_{k-1}\choose k-1}+\cdots+{a_{s}\choose s}$ where $a_{k}>a_{k-1}>\dots>a_{s}\geq s\geq 1$ are integers.
Then
 $|\partial{\cal F}|\geq{a_{k}\choose k-1}+{a_{k-1}\choose k-2}+\cdots+{a_{s}\choose s-1}.$ 
The first step of our reduction needs the following connection between the number of edges in an $r$ -graph and its $k$ -uniform “expansion”.
Lemma 2.2
Let $n,k,r$ be three positive integers with $n\geq k>r$ . Let $F\subseteq{[n]\choose r}$ and let $G=\{e\cup f:e\in F,f\in{[n]\setminus e\choose k-r}\}.$ 
If $|F|>{n\choose r}-{n-t\choose r}-{n-t-k\choose r-1}+1$ , then $|G|>{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-k\choose k-1}+1$ .
Proof. Suppose the conclusion is false. Then the number of edges in $\overline{G}$ , the complement of $G$ , satisfies $|\overline{G}|\geq{n-t\choose k}+{n-t-k\choose k-1}-1$ . Applying the
formula ${x\choose y}={x-1\choose y}+{x-1\choose y-1}$ to ${n-t-k\choose k-1}$ and repeat, we obtain
 $\begin{split}|\overline{G}|&\geq{n-t\choose k}+{n-t-k-1\choose k-1}+{n-t-k-1\choose k-2}-1\\
&={n-t\choose k}+{n-t-k-1\choose k-1}+{n-t-k-2\choose k-2}+{n-t-k-2\choose k-3}-1\\
&={n-t\choose k}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{n-t-k-i\choose k-i}+1\right)-1\\
&={n-t\choose k}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{n-t-k-i\choose k-i}\end{split}$ 
Thus, by Theorem 2.1, we have
 $\begin{split}|\partial^{k-r}(\overline{G})|&\geq{n-t\choose r}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}{n-t-k-i\choose r-i}-1\\
&={n-t\choose r}+{n-t-k\choose r-1}-1\end{split}$ 
Since $\partial^{k-r}(\overline{G})\cap F=\emptyset$ (by definition), $|F|\leq{n\choose r}-{n-t\choose r}-{n-t-k\choose r-1}+1$ , a contradiction.
 $\Box$ 
By Lemma 2.2, Theorem 1.2 follows from the following result.
Theorem 2.3
Let $k\geq 3$ be a positive integer. There exists $t_{0}=t_{0}(k)$ such
that, for integers $t,n$ with $t>t_{0}$ and $n>2k(t+1)$ , if $F_{i}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}$ and
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-k\choose k-1}+1,$ 
for $i\in[t+1]$ , then
(i) 
 $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ admits a rainbow matching; or
(ii) 
there exists $W\in{[n]\choose t}$ such that $W$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ .
To prove Theorem 2.3, we convert
this rainbow matching problem on $k$ -graphs to a matching problem for a special class of $(k+1)$ -graphs.
Let $F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}$ be a family of subsets of ${[n]\choose k}$ and $X:=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{q}\}$ be a set of $q$ vertices.
We use $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ to denote the $(k+1)$ -graph with vertex
set $X\cup[n]$ and edge set
 $E(\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}\{\{x_{i}\}\cup e\ :\ e\in F_{i}\}.$ 
If $F_{1}=\cdots=F_{q}=H_{k}(s,n)$ , where $1\leq s<n$ and $H_{k}(s,n)$ denotes the $k$ -graph with vertex set $[n]$ 
and edge set ${[n]\choose k}\setminus{[n]\setminus[s]\choose k}$ , then
we denote such $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ by $\mathcal{F}_{q}(k,n;s)$ .
Observation 1: $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\}$ admits a rainbow matching if, and only if, $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ has a matching of size $q$ .
Hence, to prove Theorem 2.3, we need to see when
 $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ has a matching of size $t+1$ . We further
reduce this problem to a near perfect matching problem.
Write $n-kq=km+r$ , where $0\leq r\leq k-1$ . Let $F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}$ , and let $F_{i}={[n]\choose k}$ for $i=q+1,\ldots,q+m$ .
Let $Q=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+q}\}$ and let $\mathcal{H}^{q}(k,n)$ be
the $(k+1)$ -graph with vertex set $Q\cup[n]$ and edge set
 $\displaystyle E(\mathcal{H}^{q}(k,n))=\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{q}\{\{x_{i}\}\cup e\ :\ e\in F_{i}\}\right)\bigcup\left(\bigcup_{i=q+1}^{q+m}\{\{x_{i}\}\cup e\ :\ e\in{[n]\choose k}\}\right).$ 
When $F_{1}=\cdots=F_{q}=H_{k}(s,n)$ ,
we denote $\mathcal{H}^{q}(k,n)$ by $\mathcal{H}_{q}(k,n;s)$ . Note that $\nu(\mathcal{H}_{q}(k,n;q))=q+m=(n-r)/k$ , i.e., ${\cal H}_{q}(k,n;q)$ has a matching covering
all but at most $r<k$ vertices (and such a matching is said to be near perfect).
The following lemma provides an equivalent condition on matchings in $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ and matchings in $\mathcal{H}_{q}(k,n;q)$ . See Lemma 2.1 in [18].
Lemma 2.4
Let $n,k,q$ be positive integers and let $F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\subseteq{[n]\choose k}$ . Then
 $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ has a matching of size $q$ if, and only if, $\mathcal{H}_{q}(k,n;q)$ has a matching of size $m+q$ , where $m+q=\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ .
For the proof of Theorem 2.3, we need to tell how far
a hypergraph is close to another hypergraph.
Given two $k$ -hypergraphs $H_{1},H_{2}$ with $V(H_{1})=V(H_{2})$ , let $c(H_{1},H_{2})$ be the minimum of $|E(H_{1})\backslash E(H^{\prime})|$ 
taken over all isomorphic copies $H^{\prime}$ of $H_{2}$ with $V(H^{\prime})=V(H_{2})$ .
For a real number $\varepsilon>0$ ,
we say that $H_{2}$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $H_{1}$ if $V(H_{1})=V(H_{2})$ and $c(H_{1},H_{2})\leq\varepsilon|V(H_{1})|^{k}$ .
The following is striaghtforward to verify.
Observation 2: (i) If $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{q}(k,n;s)$ then
 $\mathcal{H}^{q}(k,n)$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{H}_{q}(k,n;s)$ ; (ii) for $n\leq 2k^{3}\min\{q,s\}$ , if $\mathcal{H}^{q}(k,n)$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{H}_{q}(k,n;s)$ then
 $\mathcal{F}^{q}(k,n)$ is $4k^{5}\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{q}(k,n;s)$ .
Our proof of Theorem 2.3 will be divided into two parts, according to whether or
not $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(n,k;t)$ .
If $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(n,k;t)$ , we will apply greedy argument to construct a matching of size $t$ .
If $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is not close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(n,k;t)$ , then by Observation 2, $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is not close to $\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(n,k;t)$ (see proof of Theorem 2.3), and
we will show that $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n;t)$ has a small
matching $M_{1}$ with nice absorbing properties and
 $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n;t)-V(M_{1})$ has a spanning subgraph
in which we can find a large matching $M_{2}$ that can be extended to a
near perfect matching by using $M_{1}$ .
3 Stability results on small matchings
We begin with the result of Huang, Loh and Sudakov [15]
mentioned previously, and state a corollary which generalizes it to
non-uniform hypergraphs.
Theorem 3.1 (Huang, Loh and Sudakov)
Let $n,k,t$ be three positive integers such that $n>3k^{2}t$ .
Let ${\cal F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\}$ be a family of subsets of ${[n]\choose k}$ . If
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}$ 
for all $1\leq i\leq t$ , then ${\cal F}$ admits a rainbow matching.
Corollary 3.2
Let $t,n,k_{1},\ldots,k_{t},k$ be positive integers such that $k=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t}\}$ and $n>3k^{2}t$ .
Let ${\cal F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\}$ be a family where $F_{i}\in{[n]\choose k_{i}}$ for $i\in[t]$ . If
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t+1\choose k_{i}}$ 
for all $1\leq i\leq t$ , then ${\cal F}$ admits a rainbow matching.
Proof. For $i\in[t]$ , we define the expansion of $F_{i}$ as
 $G_{i}=\{e\cup f:e\in F_{i},f\in{[n]\setminus V(e)\choose k-k_{i}}\}.$ 
Note that each $G_{i}$ is a $k$ -graph.
We claim that $|\bar{G_{i}}|<{n-t+1\choose k}$ for all $i\in[t]$ . For, otherwise, there exists some $i\in[t]$ such that $|\bar{G_{i}}|\geq{n-t+1\choose k}$ .
Then by Theorem 2.1, $|\partial^{k-k_{i}}(\bar{G_{i}})|\geq{n-t+1\choose k_{i}}$ .
Since $\partial^{k-k_{i}}(\bar{G_{i}})\cap F_{i}=\emptyset$ (by definition), $|F_{i}|\leq{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t+1\choose k_{i}}$ , a contradiction.
Therefore, $|G_{i}|>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}$ . Hence, by Theorem 3.1, $\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{t}\}$ admits a rainbow matching, which implies that ${\cal F}$ admits a rainbow matching.
 $\Box$ 
We also need a classical theorem of Mörs, which is Theorem 6 in [20].
Theorem 3.3 (Mörs, [20])
Let $n,k,l$ be three positive integers such that $n\geq k+l$ .
Let $\mathcal{A}\in{[n]\choose k}$ and $\mathcal{B}\in{[n]\choose l}$ .
Suppose $\cap_{C\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}}C=\emptyset$ and
 $A\cap B\neq\emptyset$ for all $A\in\mathcal{A}$ and $B\in\mathcal{B}$ .
Then either
 $|\mathcal{A}|\leq{n-1\choose k-1}-{n-1-l\choose k-1}+1$ 
or
 $|\mathcal{B}|\leq{n-1\choose l-1}-{n-1-k\choose l-1}+1.$ 
Proof of Theorem 1.3.
We apply induction on $t$ . For the base case, suppose $t=1$ . Then for
 $i\in[2]$ ,
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-1\choose k_{i}}-{n-1-k_{3-i}\choose k_{i}-1}+1={n-1\choose k_{i}-1}-{n-1-k_{3-i}\choose k_{i}-1}+1;$ 
so (i) of Theorem 1.3 follows from Theorem
3.3.
Now suppose $t\geq 2$ and the conclusion holds with $t$ sets.
Moreover,
we may assume that $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ does not admit a rainbow matching, as otherwise (i) holds.
Since $k_{1}\geq k_{i}$ for $i\in[t+1]$ , we have from the assumption of Theorem 1.3 that $|F_{i}|\geq{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k_{1}\choose k_{i}-1}+1$ for $i\in[t+1]$ . Hence, since ${n-(t-1)\choose k_{i}}={n-t\choose k_{i}}+{n-t\choose k_{i}-1}$ , $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-(t-1)\choose k_{i}}$ for $i\in[t]$ . Thus by Corollary
3.2,
 $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\}$ admits a rainbow matching, say $M_{1}$ .
Therefore, since $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ does not admit a rainbow matching,
every edge in $F_{t+1}$ must intersect $V(M_{1})$ .
Thus the maximum degree $\Delta(F_{t+1})\geq|F_{t+1}|/|V(M_{1})|$ .
Let $v\in[n]$ such that $d_{F_{t+1}}(v)=\Delta(F_{t+1})$ .
Then, since
 ${n\choose k_{t+1}}={n-1\choose k_{t+1}-1}+{n-1\choose k_{t+1}}=\ldots=\sum_{i=1}^{t}{n-i\choose k_{t+1}-1}+{n-t\choose k_{t+1}}\geq t{n-t\choose k_{t+1}-1}+{n-t\choose k_{t+1}},$ 
we have
 $d_{F_{t+1}}(v)\geq\frac{|F_{t+1}|}{|V(M_{1})|}>\frac{(t-1){n-t\choose k_{t+1}-1}}{\sum_{i=1}^{t}k_{i}}.$ 
Hence, since $n\geq 2k_{1}^{2}k_{2}t$ ,
 $d_{F_{t+1}}(v)>\frac{(t-1){n-t\choose k_{t+1}-1}}{\sum_{i=1}^{t}k_{i}}>\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t}k_{i}\right){n-2\choose k_{t+1}-2}.$ 
If $\{F_{1}-v,\ldots,F_{t}-v\}$ admits a rainbow matching,
say $M_{2}$ , then $d_{F_{t+1}}(v)>|V(M_{2})|{n-2\choose k_{t+1}-2}$ as $|V(M_{2})|=\sum_{i=1}^{t}k_{i}$ .
So there exists an edge $e\in F_{t+1}$ such that $v\in e$ and $e\cap V(M_{2})=\emptyset$ .
Now $M_{2}\cup\{e\}$ is a desired rainbow matching for $\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t},F_{t+1}\}$ , a contradiction.
So $\{F_{1}-v,\ldots,F_{t}-v\}$ does not admit any rainbow
matching. Note that, for $i\in[2]$ ,
 $|F_{i}-v|\geq|F_{i}|-{n-1\choose k_{i}-1}>{n-1\choose k_{i}}-{(n-1)-(t-1)\choose k_{i}}-{(n-1)-(t-1)-k_{3-i}\choose k_{i}-1}+1,$ 
and, for $i\in[t]\setminus[2]$ ,
 $|F_{i}-v|\geq|F_{i}|-{n-1\choose k_{i}-1}>{n-1\choose k_{i}}-{(n-1)-(t-1)\choose k_{i}}-{(n-1)-(t-1)-k_{3}\choose k_{i}-1}+1.$ 
Thus, by inductive hypothesis (applied to $\{F_{1}-v,\ldots,F_{t}-v\}$ ),
there exists $W^{\prime}\in{[n]\setminus\{v\}\choose t-1}$ such that $W^{\prime}$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}-v$ for all $i\in[t]$ .
Write $W=W^{\prime}\cup\{v\}$ . We may assume that there exists $f\in F_{t+1}$ such that $f\cap W=\emptyset$ ; for otherwise (ii) holds.
Note that the number of edges in $F_{i}$ (for each $i\in[t]$ ) intersecting both $f$ and $W$ 
is
 $e_{i}(f,W)\leq{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-k_{t+1}\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}+{n-t-k_{t+1}\choose k_{i}}.$ 
Hence, for each $i\in[t]$ ,
since every edge of $F_{i}$ intersects $W$ ,
the number of edges in $F_{i}$ disjoint
from $f$ is
 $\begin{split}|F_{i}-f|&=|F_{i}|-e_{i}(f,W)\\
&>\left({n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k_{3}\choose k_{i}-1}+1\right)-e(f,W)\\
&\geq{n-k_{t+1}\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k_{3}\choose k_{i}-1}-{n-t-k_{t+1}\choose k_{i}}+1\\
&>{n-k_{t+1}\choose k_{i}}-{(n-k_{t+1})-(t-1)\choose k_{i}}\end{split}$ 
Hence by Corollary 3.2, $\{F_{1}-f,F_{2}-f,\ldots,F_{t}-f\}$ admits a rainbow matching $M_{3}$ of size $t$ .
Therefore, $M_{3}\cup\{f\}$ is a rainbow matching of size $t+1$ which satisfies (i).
 $\Box$ 
As a consequence of Theorem 1.3, we have the following conclusion.
Corollary 3.4
Let $t,k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{t+1},n$ be positive integers, let $k=\max\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{t+1}\}$ , and let $F_{i}\subseteq{[n]\choose k_{i}}$ for $i\in[t+1]$ .
Suppose $n\geq 2k^{3}t$ and $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k_{i}}-{n-t\choose k_{i}}-{n-t-k\choose k_{i}-1}+1$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ .
Then
 $(i)$ 
 $\{F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{t+1}\}$ admits a rainbow matching
of size $t+1$ ; or
 $(ii)$ 
there exists $W\in{[n]\choose t}$ such that $W$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ .
4 Extremal case
In this section, we prove Theorem 1.2 for the case when $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}({k},n)$ 
is $\varepsilon$ -close to the extremal configuration $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}({k},n;t)$ for some $\varepsilon\ll 1/k$ .
We write $0<a\ll b$ to mean that there exists an increasing function
 $f$ such that our result holds whenever $a\leq f(b)<b$ .
Let $H$ be a $(k+1)$ -graph and $v\in V(H)$ . We define the neighborhood $N_{H}(v)$ of $v$ in $H$ to be the set $\{S\in{V(H)\choose k}\ :\ S\cup\{v\}\in E(H)\}$ .
Let $H$ be a $(k+1)$ -graph with the same vertex set as $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Given real number $\alpha$ with $0<\alpha<1$ , a vertex $v$ in $H$ is called
 $\alpha$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ if
 $\left|N_{\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)}(v)\setminus N_{H}(v)\right|\leq\alpha n^{k}.$ 
Clearly, if $H$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ ,
then at most $(k+1)(1+1/k)^{k+1}\varepsilon n/\alpha$ vertices of $H$ are not
 $\alpha$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
First we deal with the case when all vertices of $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}({k},n)$ 
are $\alpha$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Let $Q,V$ be two disjoint sets. A $(k+1)$ -graph $H$ with vertex $Q\cup V$ 
is called $(1,k)$ -partite with partition classes $Q,V$ 
if, for each edge $e\in E(H)$ , $|e\cap Q|=1$ and $|e\cap V|=k$ . Clearly, $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}({k},n)$ and $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}({k},n;t)$ are $(1,k)$ -partite graphs
with partition classes $X,[n]$ .
Lemma 4.1
Let $\zeta,\alpha$ be real numbers and $n,k,t$ be positive integers such that $0<\alpha\ll\zeta<1$ , $\alpha\ll 1/k$ , $n\geq 8k^{4}$ , $t\geq n/(2k^{3})$ and $t+1<(1-\zeta)n/k$ .
Let $H$ be a $(1,k)$ -partite $(k+1)$ -graph with the same partition
classes as $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
If every vertex of $H$ is $\alpha$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ , then $\nu(H)\geq t$ with equality only if $H$ is a subgraph of $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Proof. Let $X:=\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{t+1}\}$ , $W:=[t]$ , and $U:=[n]\setminus[t]$ , such that $X,[n]$ are the partition classes of $H$ and
 $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
If $H$ is a subgraph of $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ , then we define $e_{0}=\emptyset$ .
Otherwise, there exists an edge $e_{0}$ in $H$ such that $|e_{0}\cap X|=1$ and $e_{0}\cap W=e_{0}\cap[t]=\emptyset$ .
Without loss of generality, we may assume $e_{0}\cap X=\{x_{t+1}\}$ .
Write $H^{\prime}=H-V(e_{0})$ , i.e. deleting all vertices in $e_{0}$ . Let $M$ be a maximum matching in $H^{\prime}$ such that $|e\cap X|=|e\cap W|=1$ for all $e\in M$ . Thus $|M|\leq|W|=t$ . Let $X^{\prime}=X\setminus(V(M)\cup V(e_{0}))$ , $W^{\prime}=W\setminus(V(M)\cup V(e_{0}))$ , and $U^{\prime}=U\setminus(V(M)\cup V(e_{0}))$ . Thus $|W^{\prime}|=|W|-|M|$ and $|U^{\prime}|\geq|U|-|M|(k-1)-k$ .
We claim that $|M|\geq n/(4k^{3})$ . For,
suppose $|M|<n/(4k^{3})$ .
Consider any vertex $x\in X^{\prime}$ .
Since $x$ is $\alpha$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ , we have
 $\left|\left(W\times{U\choose k-1}\right)\setminus N_{H}(x)\right|\leq\alpha n^{k}.$ 
Since $t+1<(1-\zeta)n/k$ , $|U^{\prime}|\geq|U|-|M|(k-1)-k\geq(n-t)-t(k-1)-k>\zeta n$ . Hence, since $t\geq n/2k^{3}$ ,
 $\left|W^{\prime}\times{U^{\prime}\choose k-1}\right|\geq(|W|-|M|){|U|-|M|(k-1)-k\choose k-1}>\frac{n}{4k^{3}}{\zeta n\choose k-1}>\frac{n}{4k^{3}}\frac{(\zeta n/2)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!},$ 
Therefore, $\left|W^{\prime}\times{U^{\prime}\choose k-1}\right|>\alpha n^{k}$ , as $\alpha<\zeta^{k-1}(k^{3}2^{k+1}(k-1)!)^{-1}$ .
Hence, there exists $f\in N_{H}(x)\cap\left(W^{\prime}\times{U^{\prime}\choose k-1}\right)$ . Let $f^{\prime}=\{x\}\cup f$ , Then $f^{\prime}\in E(H)$ , $|f^{\prime}\cap X|=|f^{\prime}\cap W|=1$ , and $f^{\prime}\cap V(M)=\emptyset$ . Now $M^{\prime}=M\cup\{f^{\prime}\}$ is a matching of size $|M|+1$ in $H$ , and
 $|e\cap X|=|e\cap W|=1$ for all $e\in M^{\prime}$ . Thus, $M^{\prime}$ contradicts the choice of $M$ , completing the proof of the claim.
Let $\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k+1}\}\subseteq V(H)\setminus V(M)$ , where $u_{1}\in X^{\prime}$ , $u_{k+1}\in W^{\prime}$ and $u_{i}\in U^{\prime}$ for $i\in[k]\setminus\{1\}$ .
Since $|M|\geq n/(4k^{3})\geq 2k$ ,
let $\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}$ be an arbitrary $k$ -subset of $M$ , and let
 $e_{i}:=\{v_{i,1},v_{i,2},\ldots,v_{i,k+1}\}$ with $v_{i,1}\in X$ , $v_{i,k+1}\in W$ , and $v_{i,j}\in U$ for $i\in[k]$ and $j\in[k]\setminus\{1\}$ .
For $j\in[k+1]$ , let $f_{j}:=\{u_{j},v_{1,j+1},v_{2,j+2},\ldots,$ $v_{k,j+k}\}$ with addition in the subscripts
modulo $k+1$ (except we write $k+1$ instead of $0$ ). Note that $f_{1},\ldots,f_{k+1}$ are pairwise disjoint.
If $f_{j}\in E(H)$ for all $j\in[k+1]$ then $M^{\prime}:=(M\cup\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k+1}\})\setminus\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}$ is a matching in $H$ 
such that $|M^{\prime}|=|M|+1>|M|$ and $|f\cap X|=|f\cap W|=1$ for all $f\in M^{\prime}$ , contradicting the choice of $M$ .
Hence, $f_{j}\not\in E(H)$ for some $j\in[k+1]$ .
Note that there are $\binom{|M|}{k}k!$ choices of ordered $k$ -tuples $(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})\in M^{k}$ and that any two different such choices correspond to different $f_{j}$ .
Hence,
 $\displaystyle|\{e\in E(\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n))\setminus E(H):|e\cap\{u_{i}:i\in[k+1]\}|=1\}|$ 
 $\displaystyle\geq$ 
 $\displaystyle|M|(|M|-1)\cdots(|M|-k+1)$ 
 $\displaystyle>$ 
 $\displaystyle\left(n/(4k^{3})-k\right)^{k}$ 
 $\displaystyle>$ 
 $\displaystyle\left(n/(8k^{3})\right)^{k}\quad\mbox{ (since $n\geq 8k^{4}$)}$ 
 $\displaystyle>$ 
 $\displaystyle(k+1)\alpha n^{k}\quad\mbox{ (since $\alpha<((k+1)8^{k}k^{3k}))^{-1}$}.$ 
This implies that there exists $i\in[k+1]$ such that
 $|N_{\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n)}(u_{i})\setminus N_{H}(u_{i})|>\alpha n^{k}$ ,
contradicting the fact that all $u_{i}$ are $\alpha$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n)$ .
Therefore, $H$ has a matching $M$ of size $t$ .
Moreover, $M\cup\{e_{0}\}$ is a matching of size $t+1$ unless $e_{0}=\emptyset$ in which case $H$ is a subgraph of $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
 $\Box$ 
We can now prove Theorem 1.2 when $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Lemma 4.2
Let $k\geq 3$ , $t\geq 1$ and $n$ be integers, and let $\varepsilon,\zeta$ be real numbers such that
 $0<\varepsilon\ll\zeta<1$ and $\varepsilon\ll 1/k$ ,
 $t\geq 48k^{2}$ and $t+1<(1-\zeta)(1-k(k+1)\sqrt{\varepsilon})n/k$ .
Let ${\cal F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ be a family of subsets of ${[n]\choose k}$ such that
 $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-k\choose k-1}+1$ for
 $i\in[t+1]$ .
Suppose $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Then $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ has a matching of size $t+1$ or $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is a subgraph of $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Proof. We may assume $n\leq 2k^{3}t$ as otherwise the assertion follows from Corollary 3.4.
Let $X,[n]$ be the partition classes of ${\cal F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ , and
let $X:=\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{t+1}\}$ .
Note that each edge of ${\cal F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ intersects $[t]$ .
Let $B$ denote the set of vertices in $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ that
are not $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ . Since $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is
 $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ , $|B|\leq(k+1)(1+1/k)^{k+1}\sqrt{\varepsilon}n\leq 4(k+1)\sqrt{\varepsilon}n$ .
Let $b:=\max\{|B\cap X|,|B\cap[t]|\}$ ; so $b\leq 4(k+1)\sqrt{\varepsilon}n$ .
We choose $X_{1}\subseteq X,W_{1}\subseteq[t]$ such that $B\cap X\subseteq X_{1}$ , $B\cap[t]\subseteq W_{1}$ , and $|X_{1}|=|W_{1}|=b$ .
For each $x_{i}\in X\setminus X_{1}$ , $x_{i}$ is
 $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ -good with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ 
and we let
 $\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)[X_{1}\cup W_{1}\cup U\cup\{x_{i}\}]$ .
For every $x\in X_{1}\cup\{x_{i}\}$ , there exists $j\in[t+1]$ such
that $x=x_{j}$ , and we have
 $\begin{split}|N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}(x)|&\geq{|N_{\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)}(x_{j})|}-\left({n\choose k}-{n-|[t]\setminus W_{1}|\choose k}\right)\\
&=|F_{j}|-\left({n\choose k}-{n-(t-b)\choose k}\right)\\
&>{n-(t-b)\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-k\choose k-1}+1\\
&={n-(t-b)\choose k}-{(n-(t-b))-b\choose k}-{(n-(t-b))-b-k\choose k-1}+1.\end{split}$ 
Since $n-(t-b)>n/2\geq 2k^{3}(k+1)\sqrt{\varepsilon}n>2k^{3}b$ , it follows from
Corollary 3.4 that the family $\{N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}(x):x\in X_{1}\cup\{x_{i}\}\}$ admits a rainbow matching of size $b+1$ ,
or there exists $W^{i}\in{[n]\setminus([t]\setminus W_{1})\choose b}$ 
such that $W^{i}$ is a vertex cover of $N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}(x)$ for $x\in X_{1}\cup\{x_{i}\}$ .
Suppose there exists $x_{i}\in X\setminus X_{1}$ such that $\{N_{\mathcal{F}_{i}}(x):x\in X_{1}\cup\{x_{i}\}\}$ admits a rainbow matching $M_{i}$ of size $b+1$ .
Write $H:=\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)[X\cup[n]\setminus(V(M_{i})\cup B)]$ .
Let $n^{\prime}=|V(H)\cap[n]|$ .
Since $b\leq 4(k+1)\sqrt{\varepsilon}n$ and $n>48k^{2}$ , every vertex
in $H$ is $\varepsilon^{1/3}$ -good with respect to
 $\mathcal{F}_{t-b}(k,n^{\prime};t-b)$ (with relabeling of vertices so that
 $[t]\setminus W_{1}$ corresponds to $[t-b]$ ) .
By Lemma 4.1, $H$ has a matching $M^{\prime}$ of size $t-b$ . Therefore, $M_{1}\cup M^{\prime}$ is a matching in ${\cal F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ of size $t+1$ .
Hence, we may assume that for each $x_{i}\in X\setminus X_{1}$ , there
exists $W^{i}\in{[n]\setminus([t]\setminus W_{1})\choose b}$ such that $W^{i}$ is a vertex cover of $\mathcal{F}_{i}$ .
If $W:=W^{i}=W^{j}$ for all $x_{i},x_{j}\in X\setminus X_{1}$ then $([t]\setminus W_{1})\cup W$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}$ for all $i\in[t+1]$ ; so $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is a subgraph of $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ .
Hence, we may assume that there exist $i\neq j$ such that $W^{i}\setminus W^{j}\neq\emptyset$ .
Now fix $x\in X_{1}$ . Note that each edge in
 $N_{\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)}(x)$ either intersects $([t]\setminus W_{1})\cup(W^{i}\cap W^{j})$ or intersects both $W^{i}\setminus W^{j}$ and $W^{j}\setminus W^{i}$ .
Let $r:=|W^{i}\setminus W^{j}|=|W^{j}\setminus W^{i}|$ ; then
 $|([t]\setminus W_{1})\cup(W^{i}\cap W^{j})|=(t-b)+(b-r)=t-r$ . Hence,
 $N_{\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)}(x)\leq\left({n\choose k}-{n-(t-r)\choose k}\right)+{n-t-r\choose k-2}r^{2}.$ 
Note that
 ${n-(t-r)\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}\geq r{n-t\choose k-1}=\frac{(r-1)(n-t)}{k-1}{n-t-1\choose k-2}+{n-t\choose k-1}.$ 
Hence,
 $\begin{split}N_{\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)}(x)&\leq{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t\choose k-1}+{n-t-1\choose k-2}r^{2}-\frac{(r-1)(n-t)}{k-1}{n-t-1\choose k-2}\\
&<{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-1\choose k-1}\text{\quad(as $n>10kb\geq 10kr$ and $t\leq n/k$)}\\
&<{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-k\choose k-1}.\end{split}$ 
This leads to a contradiction as $|F_{i}|>{n\choose k}-{n-t\choose k}-{n-t-k\choose k-1}+1$ for $i\in[t+1]$ . $\Box$ 
5 Non-extremal case
To deal with the case when $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}({k},n)$ is not
 $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}({k},n;t)$ , we need two
lemmas proved by the present authors in [18], both with a slight
variation but same proof.
The first is Lemma 4.2 in [18].
The only difference between that lemma and the statement below is that we replace the assumption
“ $d_{H}(x_{i})>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}$ for $i\in[t]$ ” in Lemma
4.2 of [18] by “ $d_{H}(x_{i})>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}-\rho n^{k}$ for $i\in[t]$ ”. We omit the proof as it is almost
identical to the proof of Lemma 4.2 in [18].
Lemma 5.1
Let $n,k,t$ be integers and $\zeta,\rho$ be real numbers,
such that $1/n\ll\rho\ll\zeta\ll 1/k$ and $n/(2k^{3})\leq t\leq(1-\zeta)n/k$ .
Let $H$ be a $(1,k)$ -partite $(k+1)$ -graph with partition classes $\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\},[n]$ such that
 $d_{H}(x_{i})>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}-\rho n^{k}$ for $i\in[t]$ and $d_{H}(x_{i})={n\choose k}$ for $i=t+1,\ldots,\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ .
Then for any $c$ with $0<c\ll\zeta$ ,
there exists a matching $M$ in $H$ such that $|M|\leq 2kcn$ and, for any balanced subset $S\subseteq V(H)$ with $|S|\leq(k+1)c^{1.5}n/2$ , $H[V(M)\cup S]$ has a perfect matching.
The second result we need is Lemma 6.4 in [18], with the
assumption $n/(3k^{2})\leq t$ replaced by $n/(2k^{3})\leq t$ in the
statement below. Again, the same proof in [18] works. Recall
the definition of $\mathcal{H}_{t}(k,n;t)$ .
Lemma 5.2
Let $k\geq 3$ be an integer, $0<c\ll\rho\ll\varepsilon\ll 1$ be real numbers, $n\in k\mathbb{Z}$ sufficiently large, and let $t$ be an integer with $n/(2k^{3})\leq t\leq(\frac{1}{2}+c)n/k$ .
Let $H$ be a $(1,k)$ -partite $(k+1)$ -graph with partition classes $X,[n]$ such that $k|X|=n$ .
Let $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ be a partition of $A$ such that $|A_{1}|=t$ and $|A_{2}|=n/k-t$ . Suppose that $d_{H}(x)>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}-\rho n^{k}$ for all $x\in A_{1}$ and $d_{H}(x)={n\choose k}$ for all $x\in A_{2}$ . If $H$ is not $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{H}_{t}(k,n;t)$ , then there exists a spanning subgraph $H^{\prime}$ of $H$ 
such that the following conditions hold:
 $(1)$ 
For all $x\in V(H^{\prime})$ , with at most $n^{0.99}$ exceptions,
 $d_{H^{\prime}}(x)=(1\pm n^{-0.01})n^{0.2}$ .
 $(2)$ 
For all $x\in V(H^{\prime})$ , $d_{H^{\prime}}(x)<2n^{0.2}$ .
 $(3)$ 
For any two distinct $x,y\in V(H^{\prime})$ , $d_{H^{\prime}}(\{x,y\})<n^{0.19}$ .
Lemma 5.2 allows us to apply the following result attributed to Pippenger [21]
(see Theorem 4.7.1 in [4]). An edge cover in a hypergraph $H$ 
is a set of edges whose union is $V(H)$ .
Theorem 5.3 (Pippenger)
For every integer $k\geq 2$ and reals $r\geq 1$ and $a>0$ , there are $\gamma=\gamma(k,r,a)>0$ and $d_{0}=d_{0}(k,r,a)$ such that for every $n$ and $D\geq d_{0}$ the following holds: Every $k$ -uniform hypergraph $H=(V,E)$ on a set $V$ of $n$ vertices in which all vertices have positive degrees and which satisfies the following conditions:
 $(1)$ 
for all vertices $x\in V$ but at most $\gamma n$ of them, $d_{H}(x)=(1\pm\gamma)D$ ;
 $(2)$ 
for all $x\in V$ , $d_{H}(x)<rD$ ;
 $(3)$ 
for any two distinct $x,y\in V$ , $d_{H}(\{x,y\})<\gamma D$ ;
contains an edge cover of at most $(1+a)(n/k)$ edges.
Proof of Theorem 2.3.
By Corollary 3.4, we may assume that $2k(t+1)<n<2k^{3}(t+1)$ .
Let $0<\varepsilon\ll{1/k}<1$ be sufficiently small and
 $t>t_{0}(k)=\max\{48k^{2},n/(2k)^{3}\}$ be sufficiently large.
By Observation 1, it suffices to show $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ has a matching of size $t+1$ .
Applying Lemma 4.2 to $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ (by
choosing $\zeta<1/3$ for instance),
we may assume that $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is not $4k^{5}\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{F}_{t+1}(k,n;t)$ . That is,
 $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is not $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(k,n;t+1)$ by Observation 2 as $n<2k^{3}(t+1)$ .
Now we apply Lemma 5.1 to $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ 
with $t\leq n/(2k)$ and sufficiently small $\zeta$ .
Thus there exists some constant $0<c\ll\varepsilon$ such that
 $n-2k^{2}cn\geq(2k-2k^{4}c)(t+1)$ , and $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ contains an absorbing matching $M_{1}$ with $m_{1}:=|M_{1}|\leq 2kcn$ and for any balanced subset $S$ of vertices with $|S|\leq(k+1)c^{1.5}n$ , $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)[V(M_{1})\cup S]$ has a perfect matching. Let $H:=\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)-V(M_{1})$ and $n_{1}:=n-km_{1}$ .
Next, we see that $H$ is not $(\varepsilon/2)$ -close to $\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(k,n-km_{1};t+1)$ . For, suppose otherwise.
Then
 $\displaystyle|E(\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(k,n;t+1))\setminus E(\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n))|$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq|E(\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(k,n-km_{1};t+1))\setminus E(H)|+|e\in E(\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(k,n;t+1)):e\cap V(M_{1})\neq\emptyset|$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon/2)(n+n/k)^{k+1}+2k(k+1)cn\cdot n^{k}$ 
 $\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon(n+n/k)^{k+1}.}$ 
This is a contradiction as $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ is not $\varepsilon$ -close to $\mathcal{H}_{t+1}(k,n;t+1)$ .
Let $Q,[n]$ denote the partition classes of $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ ,
and let $X$ consist of all vertices of $Q$ contained in
 $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ . Let $A_{1}=X\cap V(H)$ and $A_{2}=(Q\setminus X)\cap V(H)$ . Then we have $d_{H}(x)>{n\choose k}-{n-t+1\choose k}-\rho n^{k}$ for all $x\in A_{1}$ and $d_{H}(x)={n\choose k}$ for all $x\in A_{2}$ .
Since $n_{1}=n-km_{1}\geq n-2k^{2}cn\geq(2k-2k^{4}c)(t+1)$ , by Lemma 5.2 $H$ has a spanning subgraph $H_{1}$ such that
(1) 
for all but at most $n_{1}^{0.99}$ vertices $x\in V(H_{1})$ ,
 $d_{H_{1}}(x)=(1\pm n_{1}^{-0.01})n_{1}^{0.2}$ ;
(2) 
for all $x\in V(H_{1})$ , $d_{H_{1}}(x)<2n_{1}^{0.2}$ ;
(3) 
for any two distinct $x,y\in V(H_{1})$ , $d_{H_{1}}(\{x,y\})<n_{1}^{0.19}$ .
Hence by applying Lemma 5.3 to $H_{1}$ by choosing $a$ with $0<a\ll c^{1.5}$ ,
 $H_{1}$ contains an edge cover of size at most $(1+a)((n_{1}/k+n_{1})/(k+1))$ .
Thus, at most $a(n_{1}/k+n_{1})$ vertices are each covered by more than one edge
in the cover. Hence,
after removing at most $a(n_{1}/k+n_{1})$ edges from the edge cover, we obtain a matching $M_{2}$ covering all but at most $(k+1)a(n_{1}/k+n_{1})\leq 3kan_{1}\leq 3kan$ vertices.
Now we may choose a subset $S$ of $V(H)\setminus V(M_{2})$ such that
 $|V(H)\setminus(V(M_{2})\cup S)|\leq k$ and if $S\neq\emptyset$ then
 $|S\cap[n]|={k|S\cap Q|}$ .
Since $|S|\leq 3kan\leq(k+1)c^{1.5}n$ , $\mathcal{H}^{t+1}(k,n)[V(M_{1})\cup S]$ has a perfect matching, say $M_{3}$ . Thus,
 $M_{2}\cup M_{3}$ is matching of ${\cal H}^{t+1}(k,n)$ covering all but at most $k$ vertices, and, hence, has size $\lfloor n/k\rfloor$ .
Therefore, by Lemma 2.4, $\mathcal{F}^{t+1}(k,n)$ has a matching of size $t+1$ . $\Box$ 
6 Conclusion
We proved stability results for rainbow matchings for families of
non-uniform hypergraphs. In the uniform case, our result generalizes
the result of Frankl and Kupavskii [13] on Conjecture 1.1.
We think the following could be true:
Conjecture 6.1
Let $n,k,t$ be three positive integers such that $n>kt$ . Let ${\cal F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t+1}\}$ be a family of subsets of ${[n]\choose k}$ , such that, for $1\leq i\leq t+1$ ,
 $|F_{i}|>\max\{e(H_{k}(n,t,2)),\dots,e(H_{k}(n,t,k)),e(HM^{(k)}(t,n))\}.$ 
Then ${\cal F}$ admits a rainbow matching of size $t+1$ or there exists $W\in{[n]\choose t}$ such that for all $i\in[t+1]$ , $W$ is a vertex cover of $F_{i}$ .
