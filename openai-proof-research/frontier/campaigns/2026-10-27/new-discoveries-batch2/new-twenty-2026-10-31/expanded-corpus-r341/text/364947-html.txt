Abstract.
 
We derive an explicit formula for the quasi–shuffle product satisfied by Schlesinger–Zudilin Multiple $q$ -Zeta Values, expressed in terms of partition data. To achieve this, we interpret Schlesinger–Zudilin Multiple $q$ -Zeta Values as generating series of distinguished marked partitions, which are partitions whose Young diagrams have certain rows and columns marked. Together with the description of duality using marked partitions in [Bri24], and Bachmann’s conjecture ([Bac14]) that all linear relations among Multiple $q$ -Zeta Values are implied by duality and the stuffle product, this paper completes the description of the conjectural structure of Multiple $q$ -Zeta Values using marked partitions.
 
Key words and phrases: Multiple zeta values, partitions.
2020 Mathematics Subject Classification05A17, 11M32.
1. Introduction
Multiple $q$ -Zeta Values, or $q$ MZVs for short, can be regarded as generalizations of Multiple Zeta Values (MZVs) as well as (quasi-)modular forms or as generating functions of specific types of partitions. They are $q$ -series that reduce to a Multiple Zeta Value (or a $\mathbb{Q}$ -linear combination of them) in the limit $q\to 1$ , often after modifying the series by multiplying it with a suitable power of $1-q$ . In this paper, we focus on the $q$ MZVs introduced by Schlesinger [Sch01] and Zudilin [Zud03]. For an overview of $q$ MZVs, see, for instance, [Bri24].
In the following, let us consider the set $\mathcal{U}:=\{u_{j}\mid j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}$ . We refer to $\mathcal{U}$ as an alphabet, and its elements are called letters. Furthermore, monomials composed of elements of $\mathcal{U}$ (with respect to concatenation) are called words. The neutral element under concatenation is denoted by ${\mathbf{1}}$ , which we call the empty word. Let $\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ denote the set of words formed from letters in $\mathcal{U}$ . We define $\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle$ as the $\mathbb{Q}$ -vector space $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ , which is provided with the non-commutative, but associative multiplication given by concatenation. The Schlesinger–Zudilin stuffle product $\ast\colon\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle\times\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle$ is defined via $\mathbb{Q}$ -bilinearity and the recursion
 $\displaystyle u_{j_{1}}\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast u_{j_{2}}\mathtt{W}_{2}:=u_{j_{1}}\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast u_{j_{2}}\mathtt{W}_{2}\right)+u_{j_{2}}\left(u_{j_{1}}\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}\right)+u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}\right)$ 
for all $j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ and $\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ , with initial condition $\boldsymbol{1}\ast\mathtt{W}=\mathtt{W}\ast\boldsymbol{1}=\mathtt{W}$ for any $\mathtt{W}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ . Note that the usual stuffle product is defined on $\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\backslash\{u_{0}\}\rangle\times\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\backslash\{u_{0}\}\rangle$ only, using the same recursion. Nevertheless, throughout this paper, we refer to the Schlesinger–Zudilin stuffle product whenever we use the term “stuffle product”  for consistency and better readability. By a result of Hoffman ([Hof00]), it is known that $(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle,\ast)$ is an associative and commutative $\mathbb{Q}$ -algebra. Given a word $\mathtt{W}=u_{k_{1}}\cdots u_{k_{r}}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ , we call $\operatorname{len}(\mathtt{W}):=r$ its length and $\operatorname{depth}(\mathtt{W}):=\#\{k_{j}\neq 0\mid 1\leq j\leq r\}$ its depth. Let $\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ}:=\mathcal{U}^{\ast}\backslash u_{0}\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ be the set of words in $\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ that do not start with $u_{0}$ . Furthermore, denote by $\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ}$ the corresponding subspace of $\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle$ , i.e., the $\mathbb{Q}$ -vector space generated by words not starting with $u_{0}$ . Note that $\ast$ restricts to a map $\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ}\times\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ}\to\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ}$ which causes a commutative $\mathbb{Q}$ -algebra $(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ},\ast)$ , as shown in [Hof00]. Furthermore, the map $\zeta_{q}^{\mathrm{SZ}}\colon(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ},\ast)\to(\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket,\cdot)$ is the $\mathbb{Q}$ -algebra homomorphism (see [HI17]) defined by $\zeta_{q}^{\mathrm{SZ}}({\mathbf{1}})=1$ , $\mathbb{Q}$ -linearity, and, with $m_{d+1}:=0$ ,
(1.1)
 $\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\mathrm{SZ}}\left(u_{k_{1}}u_{0}^{z_{1}}\cdots u_{k_{d}}u_{0}^{z_{d}}\right):=\sum\limits_{m_{1}>\cdots>m_{d}>0}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\binom{m_{j}-m_{j+1}-1}{z_{j}}\frac{q^{m_{j}k_{j}}}{(1-q^{m_{j}})^{k_{j}}},$ 
for any $k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ and $z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ where $d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ (note that this definition is not the usual one, like in [Sin15], but equivalent to it; this equivalence can be deduced from, for example, [Bri24, Theorem 2.18]). We denote by $\mathcal{Z}_{q}$ the image of $\zeta_{q}^{\mathrm{SZ}}$ and call elements in $\mathcal{Z}_{q}$ (Schlesinger–Zudilin)- $q$ MZVs, abbreviated as (SZ-) $q$ MZVs. A remarkable property of SZ- $q$ MZVs is their invariance under the involution $\tau:\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle^{\circ}$ , defined by $\mathbb{Q}$ -linearity, $\tau({\mathbf{1}}):={\mathbf{1}}$ , and
 $\tau\left(u_{k_{1}}u_{0}^{z_{1}}\cdots u_{k_{d}}u_{0}^{z_{d}}\right):=u_{z_{d}+1}u_{0}^{k_{d}-1}\cdots u_{z_{1}+1}u_{0}^{k_{1}-1}$ 
for all $d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ , $k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\text{ and }z_{1},\dots,z_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ (see [Zha20, Theorem 8.3]; $\tau$ is often referred to as duality). At this point, we highlight the following conjecture by Bachmann regarding the structure of $\mathcal{Z}_{q}$ (see [Bac14]; a published version can be found in [Zud15, Conjecture 1]).
Conjecture 1.1 (Bachmann).
All $\mathbb{Q}$ -linear relations among elements in $\mathcal{Z}_{q}$ are obtained by the stuffle product $\ast$ and duality $\tau$ .
Furthermore, the space $\mathcal{Z}_{q}$ contains the Fourier expansion of all quasi-modular forms via the $q$ -expansion of SZ- $q$ MZVs,
 $\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\mathrm{SZ}}(\mathtt{W})=\sum\limits_{N\geq 0}\psi_{N}(\mathtt{W})q^{N},$ 
where $\psi_{N}(\mathtt{W})$ denotes the $N$ -th Fourier coefficient of $\zeta_{q}^{\mathrm{SZ}}(\mathtt{W})$ for any $\mathtt{W}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ}$ (see [Bac15]). This paper provides a combinatorial framework for studying the Fourier coefficients of $q$ MZVs. Specifically, we interpret each $\psi_{N}(\mathtt{W})$ as a finite sum over so-called marked partitions, as introduced in [Bri24]. Moreover, we describe the stuffle product in terms of a pairing on marked partitions.
Let us now introduce marked partitions as in [Bri24]. Consider a partition $p$ of $N$ with $d$ distinct parts $m_{j}$ having multiplicities $n_{j}$ . This means we have
 $\displaystyle m_{1}>\cdots>m_{d}>0,\quad n_{1},\dots,n_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\quad N=m_{1}n_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}n_{d}.$ 
We use dots to mark rows and columns in the Young diagram corresponding to $p$ . If $k_{j}$ rows of length $m_{j}$ are marked, we call $(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})$ the type of the row marking. A row marking is said to be distinct if the lowest row of each length $m_{j}$ is marked. A distinct column marking of type $(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})$ for $p$ is defined as a distinct row marking of type $(z_{d},\dots,z_{1})$ for the conjugate partition of $p$ . A Young diagram, together with both a distinct row marking and a distinct column marking, is called a marked partition. Let $\widehat{p}$ denote a marked partition with $(k_{1},\dots,k_{d})$ as the type of its row marking, and $(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})$ as the type of its column marking. We then associate to the marked partition $\widehat{p}$ the word $u_{k_{1}}u_{0}^{z_{1}-1}\cdots u_{k_{d}}u_{0}^{z_{d}-1}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ},$ which we call the type of the marked partition $\widehat{p}$ . Given a marked partition $\widehat{p}$ , we will often denote the corresponding partition simply by $p$ .

Statement of results.
The main result of this paper provides a combinatorial interpretation of the stuffle product in terms of marked partitions.
Theorem 1.9.
Let $\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2},\mathtt{W}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ}$ be words. For all $\widehat{p}\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}}$ , we have
(1.72)
 $\displaystyle m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\widehat{p}}=m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}.$ 
In particular, given $\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2}$ , $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\widehat{p}}$ only depends on the word $\mathtt{W}$ but not on the marked partition $\widehat{p}\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}}$ .
A remarkable aspect of Theorem 1.9 is that, conjecturally, all linear relations among Multiple $q$ -Zeta Values can now be described combinatorially using marked partitions. This follows from Conjecture 1.1 and the fact that duality is already expressible in terms of marked partitions (see [Bri24]). Theorem 1.9 completes this picture by providing a combinatorial interpretation of the stuffle product using marked partitions.

2. About the stuffle product
The following characterization of the stuffle product is equivalent to its definition.
Proposition 2.1.
Consider $\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ and $j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ . Then, we have
 $\displaystyle\mathtt{W}_{1}u_{j_{1}}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}u_{j_{2}}=\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}u_{j_{2}}\right)u_{j_{1}}+\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}u_{j_{1}}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}\right)u_{j_{2}}+\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}\right)u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}.$ 
Proof.
The proof proceeds by induction on $\operatorname{len}(\mathtt{W}_{1})+\operatorname{len}(\mathtt{W}_{2})$ , where the induction step involves the recursive definition of the stuffle product.
∎
To prepare for the proof of Theorem 1.9, the following recursion for the stuffle product is needed.
Lemma 2.2.
Let $\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\,\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}$ , $j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ be words, and $n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ . Consider
 $\displaystyle\mathtt{W}_{1}=\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}},\quad\mathtt{W}_{2}=\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}}.$ 
We have
 $\displaystyle\mathtt{W}_{1}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}=$ 
 $\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq j\leq n_{2}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{2}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{1}+k}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{j-k}\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}-j-\varepsilon}\right)u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}+k}$ 
 $\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq j\leq n_{1}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{1}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{2}+k}{n_{2}}\binom{n_{2}}{j-k}\left(\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}-j-\varepsilon}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}\right)u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}+k}$ 
 $\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n_{2}}\binom{n_{1}+k}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}-k}(\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}\ast\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime})u_{j_{1}+j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{1}+k}.$ 
Proof.
For $n_{1}=0$ , the proof is by induction on $n_{2}$ . The base case $n_{1}=n_{2}=0$ corresponds to Proposition 2.1. In the induction step, we first use Proposition 2.1 and then the induction hypothesis to establish the claim. The case $n_{2}=0$ is handled analogously. For $n_{1},n_{2}>0$ , the proof follows by induction on $n_{1}+n_{2}$ , using Proposition 2.1 and appropriately rearranging sums and applying identities for binomial coefficients.
∎
We write

0,\quad\text{if }\bullet\text{ is false}\end{cases}$ 
for the Kronecker Delta, as usual. From Lemma 2.2, we derive the following recursion for the numbers $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}$ .
Proposition 2.3.
Let $\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2},\mathtt{W}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ}$ be words.
(i) 
If $\mathtt{W}_{1}={\mathbf{1}}$ , we have $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}=\delta_{\mathtt{W}=\mathtt{W}_{2}}$ .
(ii) 
If $\mathtt{W}_{2}={\mathbf{1}}$ , we have $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}=\delta_{\mathtt{W}=\mathtt{W}_{1}}$ .
(iii) 
If $\mathtt{W}={\mathbf{1}}$ , we have $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}=\delta_{\mathtt{W}_{1}=\mathtt{W}_{2}={\mathbf{1}}}$ .
(iv) 
If $\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2},\mathtt{W}\neq{\mathbf{1}}$ , write
 $\displaystyle\mathtt{W}_{1}=\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}},\quad\mathtt{W}_{2}=\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}},\quad\mathtt{W}=\mathtt{W}^{\prime}u_{j_{3}}u_{0}^{n_{3}}$ 
with unique $\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ}$ , $j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ , and $n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$ . Then,
 $\displaystyle m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}=\,$ 
 $\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq j\leq n_{2}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{2}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{1}+k}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{j-k}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}-j-\varepsilon};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}\delta_{n_{1}+k=n_{3}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\,+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq j\leq n_{1}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{1}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{2}+k}{n_{2}}\binom{n_{2}}{j-k}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}-j-\varepsilon},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}\delta_{n_{2}+k=n_{3}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\,+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n_{2}}\binom{n_{1}+k}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}-k}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}\delta_{n_{1}+k=n_{3}}.$ 
Proof.
While (i), (ii), and (iii) are evident following the definition of the stuffle product, (iv) is an immediate consequence of Lemma 2.2.
∎
3. Proof of our main theorem
Having established the recursion for the stuffle product in Lemma 2.2, we can now proceed to prove our main theorem. The underlying idea is straightforward: We demon- strate, in a combinatorial manner, that the numbers $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\widehat{p}}$ satisfy the same recursion as the numbers $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}}$ (where $\mathtt{W}$ represents the type of $\widehat{p}$ ). This allows the claim to follow by induction on $\operatorname{depth}(\mathtt{W})$ . To facilitate our combinatorial arguments in the proof of the main theorem, we first introduce some necessary notions.
Definition 3.1.
Let $\mathtt{W}\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast,\circ}$ and $\widehat{p}\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}}$ .
(i) 
We denote by $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ the minimal length of the parts in $\widehat{p}$ .
(ii) 
We denote by $\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{p}\right)\subset\{1,\dots,\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p}\right)\}$ the column markings of $\widehat{p}$ that occur in the horizontal block of minimal length in $\widehat{p}$ . More precisely, $j\in\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ if and only if the $j$ -th leftmost column in $\widehat{p}$ has a marking and $j\leq\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ .
(iii) 
We denote by $(\widehat{p})_{-1}$ the marked partition obtained from $\widehat{p}$ by removing its horizontal block of minimal length $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ , along with the corresponding row markings. Additionally, any column markings in the $j$ -th leftmost column are removed if $j\in\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ .
(iv) 
We denote by $(\widehat{p})_{1}$ the marked partition consisting of the horizontal block of minimal length $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ from $\widehat{p}$ . This includes preserving the row markings, and column markings are retained in the $j$ -th leftmost column if and only if $j\in\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ .
Note that we will sometimes use the phrase “ $m$ is a column marking of $\widehat{p}$ ”to mean that the $m$ -th leftmost column of $\widehat{p}$ is marked.

Analogously, we obtain
(3.15)
 $\displaystyle\,\#\left\{(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}\mid\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)>\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right),\ \Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}\right\}$ 
(3.16)
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle\,\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq n_{1}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{1}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{3}}{n_{2}}\binom{n_{2}}{j-(n_{3}-n_{2})}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}-j-\varepsilon},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}.$ 
Case $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right)$ .
We aim to determine the number
 $\displaystyle\#\left\{(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}\mid\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right),\ \Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}\right\}.$ 
Note that for $(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}$ with $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right)$ and $\Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}$ , the following holds:
 $\displaystyle(\widehat{p})_{-1}=\Phi((\widehat{p_{1}})_{-1},(\widehat{p_{2}})_{-1})\quad\text{and}\quad(\widehat{p})_{1}=\Phi((\widehat{p_{1}})_{1},(\widehat{p_{2}})_{1}).$ 
Furthermore, for $h\in\{1,2\}$ , we have
 $\displaystyle(\widehat{p_{h}})_{-1}\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{h}^{\prime}},\quad(\widehat{p_{h}})_{1}\in\mathcal{MP}_{u_{j_{h}}u_{0}^{n_{h}}},\quad(\widehat{p})_{-1}\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}^{\prime}},\quad\text{and}\quad(\widehat{p})_{1}\in\mathcal{MP}_{u_{j_{3}}u_{0}^{n_{3}}}.$ 
Now, for $h\in\{1,2\}$ , fix marked partitions $\widehat{q_{h}}\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{h}^{\prime}}$ such that
 $\displaystyle\Phi(\widehat{q_{1}},\widehat{q_{2}})=\,(\widehat{p})_{-1}.$ 
We are interested in the number of pairs $(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime},\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{MP}_{u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}}}$ such that
 $\displaystyle\Phi(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime},\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime})=(\widehat{p})_{1}.$ 
Note that the underlying Young diagram of both $\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}$ and $\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}$ are uniquely determined by $(\widehat{p})_{1}$ . Also the row markings of $\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}$ and $\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}$ are determined by $(\widehat{p})_{1}$ . In particular, this is possible only if $j_{3}=j_{1}+j_{2}$ , which implies $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p}\right)$ .
Now, since
 $\displaystyle\#(\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}\right)\backslash\{\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}\right)\})=n_{1},\quad\#(\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{p}\right)\backslash\{\operatorname{sm}\left(p\right)\})=n_{3},\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}\right)\subset\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{p}\right),$ 
we have $\binom{n_{3}}{n_{1}}$ ways to determine the column markings of $\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}$ which determines $\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}$ . Furthermore, since $\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}\right)\cup\operatorname{C}\left(\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}\right)=\operatorname{C}\left((\widehat{p})_{1}\right)$ , it follows that $n_{2}-(n_{3}-n_{1})$ column markings of $\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}$ , distinct from $\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}\right)$ , also belong to $\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}$ . This is possible in $\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}-(n_{3}-n_{1})}$ ways when $\widehat{q_{1}}^{\prime}$ is already determined, thereby determining $\widehat{q_{2}}^{\prime}$ .
Hence, by using $\widehat{p}=\Phi((\widehat{p})_{-1},(\widehat{p})_{1})$ , we have proven
(3.17)
 $\displaystyle\,\#\left\{(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}\mid\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right),\ \Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}\right\}$ 
(3.18)
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle\,\binom{n_{3}}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}-(n_{3}-n_{1})}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};(\widehat{p})_{-1}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{3}=j_{1}+j_{2}\end{subarray}}$ 
(3.19)
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle\,\binom{n_{3}}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}-(n_{3}-n_{1})}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{3}=j_{1}+j_{2}\end{subarray}},$ 
where the last step follows from the induction hypothesis since $\operatorname{depth}(\mathtt{W}^{\prime})=\operatorname{depth}(\mathtt{W})-1$ .
Conclusion.
Now, (3.14), (3.16), and (3.19) imply by definition of $m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\widehat{p}}$ that
 $\displaystyle\,m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\widehat{p}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle\,\#\left\{(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}\mid\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)<\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right),\ \Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}\right\}$ 
 $\displaystyle\,+\#\left\{(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}\mid\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)>\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right),\ \Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}\right\}$ 
 $\displaystyle\,+\#\left\{(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})\in\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{1}}\times\mathcal{MP}_{\mathtt{W}_{2}}\mid\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{1}}\right)=\operatorname{sm}\left(\widehat{p_{2}}\right),\ \Phi(\widehat{p_{1}},\widehat{p_{2}})=\widehat{p}\right\}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle\,\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq j\leq n_{2}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{2}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{1}+k}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{j-k}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime}u_{j_{2}}u_{0}^{n_{2}-j-\varepsilon};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}\delta_{n_{1}+k=n_{3}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\,+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq j\leq n_{1}\\
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\min\{1,n_{1}-j\}\end{subarray}}\binom{n_{2}+k}{n_{2}}\binom{n_{2}}{j-k}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime}u_{j_{1}}u_{0}^{n_{1}-j},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{2}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}\delta_{n_{2}+k=n_{3}}$ 
 $\displaystyle\,+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n_{2}}\binom{n_{1}+k}{n_{1}}\binom{n_{1}}{n_{2}-k}m_{\mathtt{W}_{1}^{\prime},\mathtt{W}_{2}^{\prime};\mathtt{W}^{\prime}}\delta_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+j_{2}=j_{3}\end{subarray}}\delta_{n_{1}+k=n_{3}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle\,m_{\mathtt{W}_{1},\mathtt{W}_{2};\mathtt{W}},$ 
where the last step immediately follows from Proposition 2.3(iv). This completes the induction step, so the theorem is proven.
∎
Remark 3.4.
Marked partitions appear to be a powerful tool for studying the coefficients in the $q$ -expansion of $q$ MZVs. They provide a combinatorial interpretation of the (algebraic) behavior of $q$ MZVs. In this paper, we used them to describe the stuffle product. In [Bri24], we previously applied them to study duality in the Schlesinger–Zudilin model of $q$ MZVs. Consequently, by Conjecture 1.1, we can now conjecturally describe all $\mathbb{Q}$ -linear relations among $q$ MZVs at the level of marked partitions.
For future work, it would be interesting to make further progress on proving problems related to the algebraic structure of $q$ MZVs using marked partitions. One notable open problem is a conjecture by Bachmann ([Bac19, Conjecture 4.3]), which asserts that the space $\mathcal{Z}_{q}$ is spanned by $\left\{u_{k_{1}}\cdots u_{k_{d}}\,|\,k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\right\}.$
