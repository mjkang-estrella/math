## 365299: toroidal K3,3 — sustained rigidity route, HOLD

The primary is Lorand, arXiv:2410.10673v1, sections 3–5, Table 3, Conjecture 1. The source fixes the unit square torus. Its number 5 sqrt(2)/18 is the center distance (the disk diameter), notwithstanding a radius/diameter wording inconsistency. Changing the torus shape is outside this source endpoint. Figures were reviewed through captions and exact tables, not raster images.

First attempted shortcut: nine equal-length constraints on six two-dimensional vertices suggest a flex after quotienting translations. This dimension count is insufficient over the reals at a singular framework. There is an explicit positive equilibrium stress.

Use the table's numbering. The bipartition is A={1,3,5}, B={2,4,6}. At each A vertex the three uniquely shortest lifted edge vectors are a permutation of (-2,14)/36, (10,-10)/36, (-14,2)/36. At each B vertex they are the negatives. Every vector has squared norm 200/1296. The three edges with vector parallel to (1,-1) are 1–4, 3–2, 5–6. Assign these weight 8/5 and all other edges weight 1. At every vertex the weighted sum is zero, because (-2,14)+(-14,2)+(8/5)(10,-10)=0. This checks all vertices by the displayed bipartition and vector pattern, without a computational sweep.

A complete local helper: fix these nine integer lift offsets, and retain the same common edge length d=5 sqrt(2)/18. Write q_i=p_i+u_i. For each edge with original lifted vector r_ij, its new squared length is |r_ij|²+2r_ij·(u_j-u_i)+|u_j-u_i|². Sum with the positive weights above. The linear term is zero by equilibrium. Equal old and new lengths force sum w_ij|u_j-u_i|²=0. Each term is nonnegative, so u_i=u_j on every edge. K3,3 is connected; all u_i agree. Thus every sufficiently close realization with the same diameter is a translation. Shortest lift choices are locally stable because the coordinate differences are strictly away from half-integers. This is a local fixed-diameter result only. It is a standard stress-energy argument, receives zero credit, and does not prove the literal global uniqueness assertion.

Second route, after that shortcut failed: try a reflection-symmetric family with p1=(s,-s), p2=(t,u), p3=(u,t) and the remaining points their negatives. The four squared edge-length types near the source point are 2(1-2s)², 2(t-u)², (s-t)²+(s+u)², and 4u²+(1-2t)². The local positive branch gives t-u=1-2s. Substituting into the other two equalities gives
2s²+2s-1+(-4s+2)u+2u²=0 and
8s²-1+(-16s+4)u+8u²=0.
Subtracting four times the first from the second yields u=3/4-2s. The resulting quadratic is 18s²-11s+13/8=0, with roots s=13/36 and s=1/4. Hence this symmetric ansatz has no continuous branch through the proposed source realization; the second root is not a nearby realization. This rules out this ansatz, not all asymmetric or disconnected branches.

Remaining full endpoint: rule out (or construct) another equal-disk realization with different edge lift offsets, different common diameter, or an asymmetric branch. Even with offsets fixed, allowing a new diameter d' gives sum w|u_j-u_i|²=(sum w)(d'²-d²); it no longer forces u constant. No global reduction to the symmetric ansatz or a fixed diameter was established. No classification/enumeration program was executed or substituted for this gap.

## 365660: tree reconfiguration — sustained structural route, HOLD

Beaton arXiv:2411.02300v1, Definition 1.1: vertices of R(G) are inclusion-minimal dominating sets. Distinct M,N are adjacent when one difference is a singleton {v} and the opposite difference is contained in the open neighborhood N_G(v). Sizes may change. Minimum dominating sets and inclusion-minimal sets cannot be interchanged.

First full-direction reduction: source Theorem 2.1 gives R(G disjoint-union H)=R(G) Cartesian-product R(H). Isolated vertices occur in every minimal dominating set and contribute a one-vertex factor. For a star with at least one leaf the only minimal dominating sets are its center and its whole leaf set: the center alone dominates; any set missing the center must contain every leaf, and any set including it and another vertex is not minimal. The two sets are adjacent by the contraction rule. Thus a star plus isolates has R=K2. A graph consisting of isolates has R=K1. This proves the entire stated sufficiency direction, including the degenerate star convention, but it is already the source's Theorem 2.5 plus Theorem 2.1. Zero credit.

Necessity route A: two components with nontrivial reconfiguration edges give a four-cycle in their Cartesian product, with other factor coordinates fixed. A tree R(G) therefore cannot have two such factors. This does not imply that every nontrivial connected G has connected R(G), and that unsupported implication was not used. To finish, one still needs a connected-component classification of acyclic R, not simply source connectivity for trees and split graphs.

Necessity route B: seek cycles from small structural obstructions. For P4 labeled 1–2–3–4, the minimal dominating sets are {1,3},{1,4},{2,4},{2,3}. Every singleton fails to dominate; every dominating pair in this list is minimal; the two other pairs {1,2} and {3,4} fail; any three-set contains one listed pair. Under Definition 1.1 the four sets give a four-cycle in the listed order (closing last to first), and neither diagonal is adjacent. This is a complete hand proof for this particular graph, not an executed enumeration. K3 has R=K3, while the source's K2,2 construction has R=K2,4 and hence a cycle. The obstruction argument does not extend automatically to an induced P4/K3/K2,2 in an arbitrary graph: a dominating set in an induced subgraph need not dominate the original graph, and extension may destroy minimality or the single-vertex difference condition.

Source Lemma 2.10 supplies a valid embedding only for the special residual graph G-N[S] with S independent. Every residual minimal dominating set extends by S, retaining adjacency. This advances the obstruction route: if one can find such S whose residual has a cycle in its R, then R(G) has a cycle. The missing structural lemma is that every connected nonstar has a suitable residual obstruction or another directly certified R-cycle. No such lemma was proved, and no universal claim that arbitrary induced subgraphs inherit R was made.

A further tempting degree argument also remains unpaid: removing v from M and adding neighbors can make another member of M redundant, so minimalizing need not retain M minus {v}. Thus one cannot assert every member of M gives a distinct expansion and deduce minimum degree R>=2. A private-neighbor invariant preserving every retained token is the precise missing ingredient for that alternative.

Read primary Definition 1.1, Theorems 1.3, 2.1, 2.3–2.5, 2.9, Lemma 2.10 and their selected proof bodies, and section 4's Conjecture 4.1. Current history lists v1 only. No newer journal proof/correction located by the bounded title search. A later pancyclicity article appeared as a citing lead but its body was not acquired/reviewed; no literature clearance is claimed. HOLD, not a candidate.
