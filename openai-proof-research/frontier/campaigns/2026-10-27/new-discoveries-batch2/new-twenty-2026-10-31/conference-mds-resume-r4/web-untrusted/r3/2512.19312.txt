                                                   Paley graphs and Paley tournaments where the (out-)degree of each vertex has the same parity.
                                                   For Paley graphs, we establish a lower bound for the number of large even induced subgraphs,
                                                   particularly those containing a constant proportion of vertices. We determine the number of

                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                              Let q be an odd prime power and let Fq be the finite field with q elements. If q ≡ 1 (mod 4),
                                           we define the Paley graph Pq on vertex set Fq by joining two vertices x and y with an edge if and
                                           only if x − y ∈ □q , where □q stands for all square elements in F∗q . If q ≡ 3 (mod 4), then the Paley
                                           tournament P Tq of order q is the digraph on vertex set Fq where (x, y) is an arc if and only if
                                           x − y ∈ □q ; this is a well-defined tournament since −1 ∈ / □q . Behaving in many ways like random
                                           (di)graphs with edge probability p = 1/2, the Paley graphs (tournaments) are a famous family
                                           of quasi-random graphs (tournaments), which are commonly seen as explicit constructions of the
                                           random structures. The quasi-randomness of graphs and tournaments on n vertices are rigorously
                                           defined by a sequence of conditions that are all equivalent [CGW88, CG91], among which that the
                                           second-largest eigenvalue is o(n) is sometimes the easiest to be verified. We refer to the book [AS16]
                                           for more details.
                                              In the literature, various particular induced subgraphs of Paley graphs Pq have been investigated,
                                           for example, the chromatic number and the Turán number of Pq was studied in [BDR88] and
                                           [FNP25], respectively. The cliques of Pq have also been studied extensively [Coh88, BEHW96,
                                           BMR13, GKSV18, HP21, GMS22, Yip22a, Yip22b], partially due to their role in the determination
                                           of Ramsey numbers [GG55, EPS81, Mat87, ABL+ 14].
                                              The existence of any small induced subgraph of constant order in Pq and P Tq is guaranteed by
                                           the definition of quasi-randomness, namely, for every quasi-random graph (resp. tournament) G on
                                           n vertices, the asymptotic number ind(H, G) of labeled occurrences of any graph (resp. tournament)
                                           H of constant order r in G is given by

   In this paper, we direct our focus on even and odd induced sub(di)graphs of Paley graphs and
Paley tournaments. We say that an induced subgraph H of a graph G is even (resp. odd) if
the degree of each vertex v ∈ V (H) in H, denoted by degH (v), is even (resp. odd). Even (resp.
odd) induced subdigraphs F of a digraph D are defined likewise by considering the parity of the
out-degree deg−  F (v) of each vertex v ∈ V (F ).
   For any undirected graph G, a classical theorem of Gallai (see Theorem 2.1) shows that there
exists a partition of V (G) such that each part induces an even subgraph, ensuring the existence of
even induced subgraphs of G with at least |V (G)|/2 vertices. We call such partition V (G) = V1 ⊔ V2
an even-even partition and call G[V1 ] and G[V2 ] a pair of co-even induced subgraphs of G. Assume
that |V1 | ≥ |V (G)|/2, then we say that G[V1 ] is a primary co-even induced subgraph of G.
   Recently, the number of such partition is determined asymptotically for the random graphs with
more general degree congruence conditions [BPST23]. For the odd induced subgraphs, it was proved
by Scott in [Sco01, Theorem 1] that V (G) has a partition V1 , V2 , ..., Vk such that G[Vi ] are all odd
induced subgraphs if and only if |V (G)| is even. A famous conjecture, which was described by Caro
[Car94] as “part of the graph theory folklore”, goes that for all graphs G (without isolated vertices)
of order n, there exists an absolute constant c > 0 such that the largest order of the odd induced
subgraph of G is at least cn. This was ultimately confirmed by Ferber and Krivelevich [FK22] in
2022, showing the statement holds with constant c = 1/10000.
   Nevertheless, for Paley graphs Pq , Gallai’s theorem only ensures the existence of its primary
even induced subgraph, which would be trivial since Pq itself has all degrees even. In Section 3,
we concentrate on the number of the giant even induced subgraph of arbitrary undirected simple
graph G, viz. the induced subgraph that contains a constant proportion of vertices in G. As a
consequence, we have the following theorem.

   As an application of these results, we find a correspondence between the sub(di)graph and a
large family of MDS self-dual codes which are constructed via (extended) GRS codes from subsets
of finite fields. The construction of these codes has been a thriving research area in the past decade,
with numerous explicit constructions based on the arithmetic properties of the subsets of Fq (e.g.,
[JX17, Yan19, FF19, LL22, WZL23, MFF+ 26]). Wide ranges of possible length n of MDS self-dual
codes (which corresponds to the order of induced sub(di)graphs) were provided, while restrictions
on the field size q and other parameters are imposed and there is a lack of global existence or
enumeration results. Our contribution on the induced sub(di)graphs leads to new existence and
counting results on MDS self-dual codes, which are summarized in Section 6.
   Finally, we note that for induced subgraphs with the degree parity conditions in Paley graph
Pq , studying the even induced subgraphs will suffice. This is because an odd induced subgraph H
of Pq must be of even order, hence the subgraph of the complement of Pq induced by V (H) must
have all degrees even. Therefore the number of odd induced subgraphs of Pq equals the number of
the even induced subgraphs of Pq of even order, given that Pq is self-complementary. Meanwhile, a
similar argument applies to the odd induced subdigraphs of even order in P Tq , noting that P Tq is
self-converse.

                                         2. Preliminaries
2.1. Even-even partition and induced subgraphs with degree parity conditions. The
even and odd induced subgraphs in arbitrary graphs have been studied extensively. The following
theorem guarantees the existence for even-even partition in any graph, see [Lov07, Problem 5.17]
for a proof.
Theorem 2.1 (Gallai’s Theorem). Let G be any graph. Then there exists a partition V (G) = V1 ⊔V2
such that both G[V1 ] and G[V2 ] are even induced subgraphs of G.
    An interpretation of Theorem 2.1 using linear algebra was given by Caro [Car96]. Let G be
any undirected simple graph (i.e. without loops and multiple edges). Let NG [v] denote the closed
neighborhood of v for each vertex v ∈ V (G), namely the set {w ∈ V (G) | v ∼ w} ∪ {v}. A subset
Q ⊆ V (G) is called an odd-parity cover if for every vertex v ∈ V (G), |NG [v] ∩ Q| ≡ 1 (mod 2).
Let A denote the adjacency matrix of G and write the characteristic vector of vertex set Q as 1Q .
Then it is straightforward to see that Q is an odd-parity cover if and only if (A + I)1Q = 1 over
F2 , where 1 stands for the all-ones vector.

Theorem 2.2 ([Car96]). Let G be any graph and suppose that W is an odd-parity cover of G.

2.2. Motivation from coding theory and related problems. For the prime power q, a q-ary
[n, k, d]-linear code C is a k-dimensional subspace of Fnq with minimum Hamming distance d. It
is well-known that parameters n, k and d must be subject to the Singleton bound d ≤ n − k + 1.
The code C is called a maximum distance separable code, or simply an MDS code if the equality is
attained. For two vectors u = (u1 , u2 , ..., un ) and w = (w1 , w2 , ..., wn ) in Fnq , the Euclidean inner
                                               Pn
product of u and w is defined by ⟨u, w⟩ = i=1 ui wi ∈ Fq . The (Euclidean) dual code C ⊥ of C is
then defined by
                              C ⊥ := u ∈ Fnq | ⟨u, w⟩ = 0 for all w ∈ C .
                                    

If C = C ⊥ , we say that C is (Euclidean) self-dual. If C is both self-dual and MDS, we say that C is
an MDS self-dual code.
    It is clear that for each MDS self-dual [n, k, d]-linear code C we have n = 2k and therefore
d = n/2 + 1, so the parameters of C are completely determined by its length n. The study of MDS
self-dual codes usually focuses on the possible length n. This was completely solved by Grassl and
Gulliver in [GG08] if q is even, who showed that in this case there exist MDS self-dual codes of
length n for every n ≤ q; while the existence and the quantity of MDS self-dual codes over the finite
fields with odd characteristic remain open.
    In the literature, one of the most commonly used tool to construct explicit families of MDS
self-dual codes is the generalized Reed-Solomon codes, or simply the GRS codes, see for instance
[LLL19, FF19, Yan19, ZF20, FLLL20]. We note that a GRS code over Fq associated with vectors
α = (α1 , α2 , ..., αn ) and v = (v1 , v2 , ..., vn ) in Fnq , where the αi ’s are distinct and the vi ’s are
non-zero, is defined by
         GRSk (α, v) := {(v1 f (α1 ), v2 f (α2 ), ..., vn f (αn )) | f (x) ∈ Fq [x], deg (f (x)) ≤ k − 1} .
It is well-known that the code GRSk (α, v) is a q-ary [n, k, n − k + 1]-MDS code and that its dual
is also a GRS code. Furthermore, consider the extended GRS code of GRSk (α, v) given by
    GRSk (α, v, ∞) := {(v1 f (α1 ), v2 f (α2 ), ..., vn f (αn ), fk−1 ) | f (x) ∈ Fq [x], deg (f (x)) ≤ k − 1} ,

1, k, n − k + 2]-MDS code. The following result provides unified criteria for (extended) GRS codes
to be MDS self-dual (see [ZF20, Theorem 1]).

4.1. Characterization of co-even induced subgraphs. As a complement to Theorem 2.2, for
each graph G we show the one-to-one correspondence between co-even induced subgraphs of G and
odd-parity covers of G.
Proposition 4.1. Let G be a graph. Then the number of co-even induced subgraphs of G equals
the number of odd-parity covers of the odd-extension G.
Proof. Let Γe be the set of the vertex sets of all co-even induced subgraphs of G and let Γo be the
set of all odd-parity covers of G. Then by Theorem 2.2 we have a well-defined mapping

   For a non-zero square integral matrix M of order m, its Smith normal form S(M ) is a diagonal
matrix S(M ) = P M Q = diag(s1 , ..., sm ), where P and Q are integral with determinant ±1 and
s1 | s2 | · · · | sm . The integers si are called the elementary divisors of M . The Smith normal form
S(M ) exists and is uniquely determined up to the signs of the si . Since the integral matrix M
represents a Z-homomorphism, we can define the Smith group of M to be its cokernel Zm /M Zm ,
the computation of which is equivalent to finding S(M ). By the fundamental theorem of finitely
generated abelian groups, we have group isomorphisms
                        Zm /M Zm ∼= Zm /S(M )Zm ∼   = (Z/s1 Z) ⊕ · · · ⊕ (Z/sℓ Z) ⊕ Zm−ℓ
for some 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ m, and it follows that S(M ) = diag {s1 , s2 , ..., sℓ , 0}.

M over the finite field of order p. It is straightforward to see that rankp (M ) is exactly the number
of the elementary divisors si that are not divisible by p. We refer to Chapter 13 of the book [BH12]
for more details.

(q − 1)/2 is also prime (there are 229567 such primes q within 108 ), then q ≡ 3 (mod 4) Type I-1,
I-2 and Type III-1 admit only possibly n = 4, n = (q + 1)/2 and n = 2, q − 1, respectively. Likewise,
if q ≥ 13 and (q − 1)/4 are both prime (there are 119718 such q within 108 ), then q ≡ 5 (mod 8)
and Type I-1, I-2 and Type III-1 admit only possibly n = 4, 6, (q + 3)/2, n = 2, (q + 3)/4 and
n = 2, 4, (q − 1)/2, q − 1, respectively.
    Therefore, we present our results in the language of coding theory as a complement to the results
that are listed in Table 1, showing the existence and the quantity of all the MDS self-dual codes
that could be constructed via (extended) GRS codes.
    For every number n, if n is even, let Ω(n, q) be the set of all subsets S ⊆ Fq with cardinality

S ⊆ Fq with cardinality |S| = n that satisfy item (ii) in Theorem 2.4. Then |Ω(n, q)| is the number
