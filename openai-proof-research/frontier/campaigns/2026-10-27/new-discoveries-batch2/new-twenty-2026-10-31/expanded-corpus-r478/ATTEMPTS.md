## 321098: reflection, distribution, and digit-factorization routes
Define S(n,r)={m:0<=m<=n, binomial(n,m)=r mod16}, with r odd. The involution m -> n-m preserves the coefficient. If n is odd it has no fixed point. If n=2h>0 its only possible fixed point is h, but binomial(2h,h)=2 binomial(2h-1,h-1) is even, so h is not in S. Thus every a16,r(n) is even for n>0. For n=0 the only nonzero count is 1, which is not divisible by3. Zero counts satisfy both divisibilities. Consequently the first of the seven implications holds for every n. This is already explicitly explained in Rowland Section2; no new helper credit.

The same involution gives only one factor2; an additional orbit action would have to preserve the actual residue modulo16. Lucas describes which positions are odd (m binary-subordinate to n), but does not determine their odd residues modulo16. Toggling an allowed binary digit therefore is not proved residue-preserving. This defeats an attempted free action on blocks of four or eight positions.

A second route aggregates N_r and N_{r+8}, obtaining the modulo8 count. The source recalls Granville's theorem that this sum is zero or a power2. Together with evenness this is insufficient: abstract counts10 and6 sum to16 and are even, but10 violates5=>4. This is an abstract obstruction to this proposed inference, NOT an actual binomial counterexample and NOT the assertion these counts occur for a row. To prove all seven implications, one needs the actual admissible splits, not merely their total. Fourier inversion on U16 (isomorphic C4 x C2) would express each count in terms of all eight character sums over binary-subordinate m. No formula for those sums with overlapping binary blocks was established. Independence of digit contributions is an unpaid hypothesis, not a consequence of Lucas.

A third approach separates widely spaced nonzero binary digits to obtain multiplicative distribution recurrences, then hopes to reduce arbitrary n to a bounded list of local patterns. This would require an exact modulo16 transition theorem and a proof that all low-2-adic-valuation outputs lie in a finite classified family even when the binary length is unbounded. Neither transition completeness nor that finiteness is proved here. No mathematical automaton or enumeration was executed. The full target remains PARK at the six higher divisibility implications, not a proposed refutation or full proof. Each Xp in LP is fundamental, since 1-Xp=X(1-p). Setting Q=LP, define s:Q->LQ by Xp -> Y_[Xp]. Every defining additive pair, inverse pair and triple product for P remains such a relation between fundamental elements in Q; the prescribed sign relation also survives. Thus this assignment descends, and canonical evaluation epsilon_Q composed with s is the identity on Q. This proves a split epimorphism LQ->Q and a section, not an isomorphism.

For the opposite composite, an arbitrary generator Yq of LQ is indexed by an arbitrary fundamental q of Q. Such q is a signed monomial in Xp, but its partial products need not be fundamental. The triple-product relations therefore do not automatically reduce Yq to that monomial in Y_[Xp]. Exact unpaid direct-presentation lemma: all fundamental monomial identities q=product Xp^e in Q must lift through s to Yq=s(q). A split epimorphism by itself cannot pay this lemma.
