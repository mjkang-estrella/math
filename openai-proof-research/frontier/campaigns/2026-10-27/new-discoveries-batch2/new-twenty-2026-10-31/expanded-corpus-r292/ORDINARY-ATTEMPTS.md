## 369163: crown local v-number — sustained, incomplete

Source: Kumar–Pomeroy–Tran, 2507.03889v2, definitions in §§1–3, Theorem 3.3, §5.1 through Lemma 5.7. The graph is K(n,n) minus paired edges, over an arbitrary field. Its binomial edge ideal J is the radical intersection of the cut-set primes. v_P(J) is the least degree of a homogeneous f with (J:f)=P; this is NOT merely finding f in a colon ideal. Theorem 3.3 lists exactly: empty set, X, Y, X minus one vertex, Y minus one vertex, and paired mixed sets leaving two matching indices. Source Lemma 5.1 settles empty T by connected domination=4; Proposition 5.2 gives every local upper bound 4; Lemma 5.4 gives lower bound 3; Lemma 5.7 settles all mixed sets. Read its full cubic bidegree contradiction, not just its title. Its two opposite color classes are essential; repeating it for one-sided T does not work. Source v1 has the same conjecture and the relevant construction; the earlier NMSU manuscript was located in public search but its full body was not downloaded. No journal or citing completeness claim.

Mechanism A: characterize degree-three separators using the radical decomposition. If (J:f)=P_T, then f is outside P_T and inside EVERY other minimal prime. Testing a single monomial cannot establish this for arbitrary homogeneous f. Cut-set classification reduces the genuinely missing cases to X, Y, X\{a_i}, Y\{b_i}.

Partial deduction for T=X (symmetric for Y): set all X variables to zero, obtaining nonzero g of degree ≤3 solely in the Y variables. Because f belongs to P_(X\{a_i}) for every i, g belongs to J(K_(Y\{b_i})) for every i. These ideals are homogeneous for the multigrading assigning one coordinate per Y vertex. A nonzero multihomogeneous component of g has support at most three. Support size at most two is killed by choosing an omitted vertex in that support, leaving no quadratic generator supported on what remains. Support three forces degree exactly three and multidegree (1,1,1). Omitting the first or second supported vertex forces divisibility by two distinct 2×2 minors, each degree two. In the polynomial UFD these minors are irreducible and nonassociate, hence their product divides that component; degree four contradicts degree three. Thus no such g exists. This gives a plausible complete lower-four argument for the two full-side primes, conditional on explicitly transporting the standard radical decomposition. It is a partial case, earns zero, and is NOT offered for Pi.

Mechanism B: use Ohtani neighborhoods for T=X\{a_i}. The prime P_T contains all T coordinates plus the complete-graph ideal on Y\{b_i}; the remaining b_i is isolated. Here f may involve a_i, so setting every X variable to zero can annihilate f even when f is outside P_T. The previous argument therefore fails precisely at this specialization. The required missing lemma is: for every degree-three f in the intersection of all other cut-set primes, its image in S/P_(X\{a_i}) is zero. This must cover arbitrary coefficients, characteristic two, and cancellation modulo the rank-one determinantal relations. No proof of that lemma has been supplied. A product-of-minors degree-four separator is consistent with the known upper bound and gives no lower bound. Multigrading may reduce the lemma to support on three vertices, but the componentwise annihilator condition still needs a rigorous argument. Do not replace this with connected domination, which only settles T=empty.

## 365628: Hamming polynomial system — sustained, incomplete

Source 2411.00250v2, generic-matrix definition near Remark 7/Lemma 8, §4.2.1 and Figure 1 textual construction, §6 Theorem 40/Corollary 41/Conjecture 1. v1 has the same conjecture, with Theorem 39 renumbered. Variables are real edge weights in a symmetric generic matrix; the distance-d entries of M^d are sums of products along ALL shortest paths, not merely signed adjacency matrices or a characteristic-polynomial surrogate. In H(d,3) each antipodal pair lies on a binary d-cube and its polynomial has d! square-free monomials. Every edge weight must stay nonzero.

Mechanism A: extend the source d=2 odd-cycle parity obstruction. In H(2,3) each of nine square equations has exactly two products. A vanishing equation forces those products to have opposite signs; multiplying the nine equations contradicts each edge appearing twice. At d≥3 a zero sum of d! nonzero terms does not impose a fixed product-sign relation. There is no valid implication from the d=2 system to the distance-d system by taking a two-dimensional face: the latter imposes equations on antipodes at distance d, not on faces at distance two. Need a genuinely new elimination or positivity identity for all antipodal equations. None obtained.

Mechanism B: try Clifford-type direction operators that anticommute, as in the binary cube construction. Two invertible anticommuting real matrices in odd dimension cannot exist: det(AB)=det(-BA)=(-1)^N det(BA) contradicts invertibility when N is odd. H(d,3) has odd dimension 3^d, so this convenient ansatz cannot furnish invertible anticommuting direction matrices. This excludes that particular construction only. Direction matrices could be singular, or a solution could cancel without pairwise anticommutation. It proves neither the conjecture nor a counterexample.

Mechanism C: positive edge weights make every path product positive and cannot solve the equations. Allowing signs or unrestricted real magnitudes is indispensable; testing only positive or ±1 weights would not settle the literal real system. No solver or sign enumeration was run. Exact gap: a real all-nonzero assignment for some d≥3, or a uniform contradiction from the full shortest-path sums. No dormant execution plan is justified.

## 362897: cyclic designs — full routine weak-literal bridge, zero credit

MathDB's definition is a finite orbit of a unitary on states whose DECOHERED probability vectors form a simplex t-design. Paper Definition 5 instead requires the columns of I,U,...,U^k to constitute a complex projective t-design; it concerns whole orthonormal bases. Definition 4 gives the flat-simplex moments. Theorem 9 and Appendix E require an eigenbasis whose squared moduli form a d-point simplex 2-design, plus suitable distinct phase differences. Necessary decoherence is not equivalent to the full basis condition.

For the weak printed MathDB definition there is a complete elementary construction in every d≥2. Put s=1/sqrt(d+1), b=(1-s)/d, a=b+s. Then a+(d-1)b=1 and a,b>0. Take the unit state ψ=(sqrt(a),sqrt(b),...,sqrt(b)) and the unitary cyclic coordinate permutation U. Its orbit has exactly d states. Their decohered probability vectors are the permutations with one coordinate a and all others b. Each first moment is 1/d. The diagonal second moment is [a²+(d-1)b²]/d = 2/[d(d+1)], since a²+(d-1)b² = 1/d + (d-1)s²/d = 2/(d+1). For distinct coordinates the second moment is [2ab+(d-2)b²]/d = 1/[d(d+1)], also following by squaring the coordinate sum and symmetry. These are exactly all degree≤2 simplex moments, so this is simultaneously a 1- and 2-design under the printed weak definition. No computation or search is needed.

It does NOT supply a full cyclic projective design: the states of this single orbit are not orthogonal; for different orbit points their inner product is 2sqrt(ab)+(d-2)b>0. Thus they cannot be the proposed eigenbasis columns. Completing from the paper's construction still requires a unitary V with the specified squared-modulus moment matrix. The flat Fourier basis is not such a V (all probability vectors coincide). Robust Hadamard constructions settle only an infinite subfamily, already source Theorem 11 and separately excluded as record 362898 in r116. An intermediate-value argument for one row norm does not control all cross moments and unitarity simultaneously. The source Appendix E phase-sum proof was read in part; its terminal fourth-moment calculation and latest journal PDF were NOT fully read. v1–v4 were retrieved, but earlier bodies were not exhaustively reviewed. The journal landing page identifies Quantum 9,1763, 4 June 2025 and v4. No correction/citing clearance. The only complete literal construction is a routine simplex-moment construction exploiting the record's weakened definition: ZERO by semantic/domain correction and classical bridge, not by shortness.
