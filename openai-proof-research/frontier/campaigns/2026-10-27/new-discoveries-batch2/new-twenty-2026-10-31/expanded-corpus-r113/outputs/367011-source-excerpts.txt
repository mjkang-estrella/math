296: Our contribution
297: In this paper we improve the existing lower bounds on the maximal number of realizations for a given vertex count for the complex plane, sphere and three dimensional space by further exploiting the methods from [22]. In higher dimensions we also show upper bounds for the minimal such number.
298: Furthermore, we show observations and results from extensive computational experiments. As such we work on an open problem from [22] to see how certain rigid graph constructions influence the number of realizations. This is interesting because these constructions can be used to generate every minimally rigid graph.

303: Organization of the paper
304: We repeat basic notions of rigidity in Section 2.
305: One main part of this paper is to obtain better lower bounds for the maximal number of realizations for graphs with a given number of vertices.
306: To do so we construct large graphs by gluing them on minimally rigid subgraphs (Section 3).
307: In Sections 4, 5 and 6 we present new bounds in the plane, the sphere, and higher dimensional spaces, respectively.
308: Having all the computational data, we analyze thoroughly the impact of certain rigidity preserving constructions on the number of realizations (Section 7).

1075: In [17] a combinatorial algorithm was presented to compute the number of complex realizations on the sphere.
1076: This algorithm is faster than using Gröbner bases but still exponential.
1077: Implementations can be found in [18, 19, 8].
1078: Now we present first major computational results from this algorithm.
1079: For instance we were able to compute  \mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}  for all minimally rigid graphs with minimum degree 3 and at most 13 vertices.
1080: Hence, with  n\leqslant 13  we know the value of  \mathbf{M}^{\circ}_{2}(n)  precisely.
1081: For higher number of vertices we have experimental results using a large data set on minimally rigid graphs.
1082: Unlike in the plane the graphs with maximal realization count seem to have less properties in common.
1083: In [17] it was already shown that a graph  G  with  \mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}(G)=\mathbf{M}^{\circ}_{2}(n)  is not unique, i. e.,  \mathcal{H}^{\circ}_{2}(n)  may have more than one element.
1084: For instance there are 5 graphs in  \mathcal{H}^{\circ}_{2}(7) , where  \mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}(G)=64=\mathbf{M}^{\circ}_{2}(7) .

1365: Since it is the same class of graphs that are minimally rigid on the sphere and in the plane, it is interesting to compare the results of realization counting.
1366: In general for a minimally rigid graph  \mathbf{c}_{2}(G)  and  \mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}(G)  might be different.
1367: It was proven in [12] that we always have  \mathbf{c}_{2}(G)\leqslant\mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}(G) .
1368: Also a basic computational analysis has been done there.
1369: For instance the distribution of ratios  \mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}/\mathbf{c}_{2}  has been illustrated and the percentage of graphs with  \mathbf{c}^{\circ}_{2}(G)\neq\mathbf{c}_{2}(G) .
1370: Here we present the results of two more experiments: the comparison of graphs with high number of realizations in the plane and on the sphere and the differences in the distribution of realization numbers.
