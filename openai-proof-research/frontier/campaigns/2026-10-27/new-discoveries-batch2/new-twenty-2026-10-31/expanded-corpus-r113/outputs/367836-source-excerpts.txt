257: We present a  \mathsf{SAT}  framework which allows to investigate properties of simple drawings of the complete graph  K_{n}  using the power of AI.
258: In contrast to classic imperative programming, where a program is operated step by step, our framework models mathematical questions as Boolean formulas which are then solved using modern  \mathsf{SAT}  solvers.
259: Our framework for simple drawings is based on a characterization via rotation systems and finite forbidden substructures.
260: We showcase its universality by addressing various open problems, reproving previous computational results and deriving several new computational results.
261: In particular, we test and progress on several unavoidable configurations such as variants of
262: Rafla’s conjecture on plane Hamiltonian cycles,
263: Harborth’s conjecture on empty triangles, and
264: crossing families
265: for general simple drawings as well as for various subclasses.
266: Moreover, based our computational results we propose some new challenging conjectures.

324: A crucial ingredient of our framework is a theorem by Kynčl [37]
325: together with the computational result by Ábrego et al. [1].
326: It gives a compact characterization which systems of cyclic permutations are in fact a rotation system of a simple drawing.
327: We give the precise definitions and the characterization in Section 2.
328: In Section 3 we describe the encoding of the combinatorial structures such that the solutions are in correspondence with rotation systems. Since the default input format of  \mathsf{SAT}  solvers are Boolean formulas in conjunctive normal form (CNF),
329: we present a list of clauses which are then conjugated (logical “AND”) to one formula.
330: A clause is a disjunction (logical “OR”) of literals,
331: where a literal is a Boolean variable or its negation.
332: For more background see the standard textbook [18].
333: In Appendix A we moreover develop a SAT encoding for checking whether a system comes from a drawing and give an alternative proof of the characterization of Ábrego et al. [1].

362: The previously best computational results were obtained using enumerative and brute-force approaches [1].
363: However, as there exist approximately 7 billion rotation systems of  K_{9}  and the number of rotation systems of  K_{n}  grows as  2^{\Theta({n^{4}})}  [36],
364: an enumerative approach for  n=10  will not be possible in reasonable time with contemporary computers.

377: Various properties of a simple drawing
378: only depend on the combinatorics of the drawing.
379: For example, to determine the existence of plane substructures, it is sufficient to know which pairs of edges cross – the actual routing of edges does not play a role.
380: More generally, we will make extensive use of the cyclic orders of edges around the vertices, which in particular capture the information about crossing edge pairs.
381: In this article we only consider properties which do not depend on the choice of the unbounded cell.
382: Hence, in the following we often do not distinguish between drawings on the sphere and drawings in the plane.

560: A pre-rotation system on  [n]  consists of  n  cyclic permutations  \pi_{a}  on  [n]\setminus\{a\} .
561: One way to encode the cyclic permutations in terms of a CNF is to use ordinary permutations.

564: introduce clauses such that for every  i\in[n-1]  there is exactly one  b\in[n]\setminus\{a\}  for which  X_{aib}  is  \mathsf{True} .

1641: 6 Discussion
1642: 
1643: 
1644: 
1645: 
1646: To tackle a comprehensive list of conjectures on simple drawings of  K_{n} ,
1647: we developed SAT encodings for various properties
1648: and subclasses.
1649: Our highly parameterized
1650: python framework
1651: allows to create a CNF instance such that the solutions correspond to drawings with specified properties.
1652: Modern SAT solvers
1653: are used to decide whether there exist such drawings.
1654: 
1655: 
1656: 
1657: 
1658: To optimize the overall performance,
1659: we experimented with the optimization tool SBVA [25].
1660: It introduces auxiliary variables into a CNF to reduce the number of clauses while preserving equisatisfiablity.
1661: Even though this approach does not always lead to a better performance of the SAT solver,
1662: we analyzed all instances where SBVA lead to a speedup and incorporated the learned patterns into our encoding.
