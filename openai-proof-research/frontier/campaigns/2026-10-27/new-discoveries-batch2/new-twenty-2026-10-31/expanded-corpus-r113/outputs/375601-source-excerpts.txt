197: Abstract
198:     
199: 
200: In the pinwheel scheduling problem, each task  i  is associated with a positive integer  a_{i}  called its period, and we want to (perpetually) schedule one task per day so that each task  i  is performed at least once every  a_{i}  days. An obvious necessary condition for schedulability is that the density, defined as the sum of execution rates  1/a_{i} , does not exceed  1 . We prove that all instances with density not exceeding  5/6  are schedulable, as was conjectured by Chan and Chin in 1993. Like some of the known partial progress towards the conjecture, our proof involves computer search for schedules for a large but finite set of instances. A key idea in our reduction to these finite cases is to generalize the problem to fractional (non-integer) periods in an appropriate way. As byproducts of our ideas, we obtain a simple proof that every instance with two distinct periods and density at most  1  is schedulable, as well as a fast algorithm for the bamboo garden trimming problem with approximation ratio  4/3 .

210: In many systems—like factory machines, sensor networks, or digital communication—tasks must be performed regularly, without long gaps.
211: The pinwheel scheduling (PS) problem [20] models this need:
212: each task has a time limit within which it must be repeated,
213: and the goal is to build a schedule that
214: performs one task per unit time (or “day” herein)
215: while satisfying all these limits.
216: Specifically,
217: each task  i\in[k]=\{1,\dots,k\} 
218: must be scheduled at least once every  a_{i} 
219: days, where  a_{i}  is the period of task  i .
