188: Abstract
189:     
190: 
191: Given a graph  H , a graph  G  is  H -free if  G  does not contain  H  as an induced subgraph. Shi and Shan conjectured that every  1 -tough  2k -connected  (P_{2}\cup kP_{1}) -free graph is hamiltonian for  k\geq 4 . This conjecture has been independently confirmed by Xu, Li, and Zhou, as well as by Ota and Sanka.
192: Inspired by this, we prove that every  2k -connected  (P_{2}\cup kP_{1}) -free graph with toughness greater than one is hamiltonian-connected.

203: 1 Introduction
204:

208: We consider finite simple graphs, and use standard terminology and notations from [4, 23] throughout this article. We denote by  V(G)  and  E(G)  the vertex set and edge set of a graph  G,  respectively, and denote by  |G|  and  e(G)  the order and size of  G,  respectively. For a vertex  x\in V(G)  and a subgraph  H  of  G ,  N_{H}(x)  denotes the set of neighbors of  x  that are contained in  V(H) . For a vertex subset  S\subseteq V(G) , define  N_{G}(S)=\cup_{x\in S}N_{G}(x)\setminus S  and  N_{H}(S)=N_{G}(S)\cap V(H) . If  F  is a subgraph of  G , we write  N_{F}(H)  for  N_{F}(V(H)) . We use  G[S]  to denote the subgraph of  G  induced by  S , and let  G-S=G[V(G)\setminus S] . Furthermore, we denote the number of components in a graph  G  by  c(G) . The symbol  [k]  used in this article represents the set  \{1,2,\ldots,k\} . The subscript  G  will be omitted in all the notation above if no confusion may arise.

243: The concept of toughness of a graph was introduced by Chvátal [8].
244: Clearly, every hamiltonian graph is  1 -tough, but the converse is not true.
245: Chvátal [8] proposed the following conjecture, which is known as Chvátal’s toughness conjecture.
246: 
247: 
248: 
249: Conjecture 1.1 (Chvátal [8]).
250: 
251: 
252: 
253: There exists a constant  t_{0}  such that every  t_{0} -tough graph
254: with at least three vertices is hamiltonian.
255: 
256: 
257: 
258: 
259: 
260: Bauer, Broersma and Veldman [2] showed that  t_{0}\geq\frac{9}{4}  if it exists.
261: Conjecture 1.1 has been confirmed for a number of special classes of graphs
262: [1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22].
263: For example, it has been confirmed for graphs with forbidden (small) linear forests, such as  1 -tough  R -free graphs with  R=P_{3}\cup P_{1},P_{2}\cup 2P_{1}  [15],
264:  2 -tough  2P_{2} -free graphs [5, 19, 16],  3 -tough  (P_{2}\cup 3P_{1}) -free graphs [13],  7 -tough  (P_{3}\cup 2P_{1}) -free graphs [11] and  15 -tough  (P_{3}\cup P_{2}) -free graphs [20]. Though great efforts have been made, it remains open. In 2022, Shi and Shan [21] proposed the following conjecture.
265: 
266: 
267: 
268: Conjecture 1.2 (Shi-Shan [21]).
269: 
270: 
271: 
272: Let  k\geq 4  be an integer and let  G  be a
273:  1 -tough and  2k -connected  (P_{2}\cup kP_{1}) -free graph. Then  G  is hamiltonian.
274: 
275: 
276: 
277: 
278: 
279: Recently, Conjecture 1.2 has been independently confirmed by Xu, Li, and Zhou, as well as by Ota and Sanka.
280: It is well known that a hamiltonian-connected graph has toughness strictly larger than one.
281: Suppose  R  is a graph such that
282: every  1 -tough  R -free graph is hamiltonian. Is then every  R -free graph  G  with  \tau(G)>1  hamiltonian-connected?
283: In 1978, Jung [12] obtained the following result, in which he showed that for
284:  P_{4} -free graphs, the necessary condition  \tau(G)>1  is also a sufficient condition for hamiltonian-connectivity.
285: 
286: 
287: 
288: Theorem 1.3 (Jung [12]).
289: 
290: 
291: 
292: Let  G  be a  P_{4} -free graph. Then  G  is hamiltonian-connected if and only if  \tau(G)>1 .
293: 
294: 
295: 
296: 
297: 
298: In 2022, Zheng, Broersma and Wang [25] provided some sufficient conditions for hamiltonian-connectivity by replacing other graphs to  P_{4} .
