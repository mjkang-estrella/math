For an input 2-(v,k,lambda) design with b blocks and e fibres, a pair in one fibre lies in lambda v^(e-1) output blocks. A pair in distinct fibres lies in b v^(e-3)(2k+e-2) blocks (the two special-fibre contributions and e-2 remaining contributions). These are the source's equations (9) and (10).

If the two numbers differ, their multiplicities distinguish the same-fibre equivalence relation. Every automorphism preserves the fibres. Its induced action on each fibre preserves the input blocks, since an output block has k >= 2 points in a unique fibre. Thus Aut(output) equals the asserted wreath product in this regime. The source's Proposition 4.3 says exactly that coincidence of the two values is the regime where the output is a 2-design. Therefore this observation leaves the source's central 2-design regime unresolved and is a routine combination of its own displayed counts. It is not a new campaign result.

For a general 1-design input, same-fibre pair counts need not be constant and may overlap the cross-fibre value, so the preceding argument cannot simply be extended without a new reconstruction invariant. The brute-force search covers all positive regular uniform simple input hypergraphs on 2 through 5 labelled points with k >= 2, e in {2,3}, ve <= 10, and (k >= 3 or e >= 3). It verifies fibre preservation using all subset incidence counts under complete point bijections, not merely checking a subgroup order. There are 21 such labelled input/e cases. No fibre-breaking automorphism was found. This does not extend the authors' unspecified small-example coverage.
