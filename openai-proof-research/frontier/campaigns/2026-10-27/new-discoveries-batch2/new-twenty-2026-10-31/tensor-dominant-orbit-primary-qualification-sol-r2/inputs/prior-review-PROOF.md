Let F=F_2, V=F^p, W=U=F^2 with ordered bases. Work with G_p=GL(V) x GL(W) x GL(U), without permutations. Put T=sum_{j,k} a_{jk} tensor e_j tensor e_k and let A be the p by 4 matrix of these columns, in order 11,12,21,22. This is a matrix flattening, not the source's lexicographic row-vector encoding flat(X).

For p>=4 let O_p be the tensors for which the four columns of A are independent. This is nonempty: use the first four standard vectors. Any two ordered independent four-frames extend to bases of V: successively choose a vector outside the span until dimension p (a proper subspace cannot exhaust V). The linear map sending the resulting first basis to the second is invertible and sends the columns in their given order. Thus GL(V) alone is transitive on O_p. The action of (g,b,c) sends A to g A (b tensor c)^t under the selected column convention. The right matrix is invertible, with inverse (b^{-1} tensor c^{-1})^t. Consequently O_p is invariant under the entire actual product group, so it is exactly one G_p orbit. No arbitrary GL_4 action is used for transitivity.

A sum of r simple three-factor tensors has every first-mode column in the span of the r first factors; hence matrix rank(A)<=r. Conversely the displayed four-column expansion has at most four nonzero simple summands; zero columns are omitted. Thus tensor rank is at most four, and on O_p is exactly four. Invertible factor actions preserve tensor rank by applying the action to a minimal decomposition and then applying its inverse.

Here is the additional bridge needed to prove uniqueness among ALL rank-four tensors. Write T=sum_i f_i tensor M_i with M_i in M_2(F). If A has rank less than four, its row span S is proper in F^4=M_2(F). To justify the row/column passage without a rank assumption, elementary invertible row and column operations reduce any matrix to diag(I_r,0): choose a nonzero pivot, exchange its row/column into the first position, clear its row and column by elementary additions/scalings, and induct on the remaining submatrix; if none exists it is zero. Both row rank and column rank are unchanged by these operations and equal r on the reduced matrix. These auxiliary column operations prove equality of ranks only; they are not asserted to be tensor symmetries.

A proper subspace S lies in a hyperplane: extend a basis of S to a basis of F^4 and choose a nonzero linear functional vanishing on S. Every such functional has the form <C,M>=tr(C^t M)=sum C_jk M_jk for a unique nonzero 2 by 2 matrix C. Such C has rank one or two. The last two actual factor actions send M to B M D^t, where B,D are invertible 2 by 2 matrices. If N=B M D^t, its hyperplane coefficient is C'=B^{-t} C D^{-1}, since <C,M>=<B^{-t} C D^{-1},N>. Rank-one C=u v^t with u,v nonzero can be reduced to E11 by invertible left and right maps (extend u,v to bases); full-rank C can be reduced to I by left multiplication by C^{-1}. Every desired invertible left L and right R occurs here by B=L^{-t}, D=R^{-1}. Therefore actual allowed tensor actions carry S into either H1={M:M11=0} or H2={M:tr M=0}. This is a dual transformation, with the inverses and transposes retained.

H1 has the rank-one basis E12,E21,E22. H2 consists in characteristic two of [[a,b],[c,a]], and has rank-one basis E12,E21,J, with J=[[1,1],[1,1]]=(e1+e2)(e1+e2)^t. Explicitly [[a,b],[c,a]]=a J+(b+a)E12+(c+a)E21. Independence follows from the diagonal entry first, then entries 12 and 21. In particular tr J=1+1=0. After the chosen action, express every M_i as sum_{l=1}^3 lambda_il R_l in the appropriate fixed rank-one basis. Then T'=sum_{l=1}^3 (sum_i lambda_il f_i) tensor R_l. Each R_l=w_l u_l^t is nonzero and rank one, giving at most three simple three-factor tensors; discard any zero first coefficients. Inverting the action shows rank(T)<=3. This covers every proper slice span, including zero, with no enumeration and no missing deficient case.

Hence rank(T)=4 if and only if rank(A)=4, for all positive p. For p<4 no such tensor exists. For every p>=4 O_p is the unique orbit of rank four, not merely one orbit with a large proportion. No classification of lower-rank orbits or Conjecture 1 is claimed.

Counting ordered four-frames: after j independent columns, their span contains exactly 2^j vectors, since its basis gives a bijection from F^j to that span. Thus there are 2^p-2^j choices for the next column, independently of the chosen earlier frame. By the finite multiplication principle, |O_p|=product_{j=0}^3(2^p-2^j). Each array corresponds bijectively to one tuple of four columns, and all p by 2 by 2 arrays are the 2^(4p) choices of binary entries. Therefore the uniform proportion is product_{j=0}^3(1-2^(j-p)).

For numbers x_j in [0,1], 0<=1-product(1-x_j)<=sum x_j: adjoining a factor changes the defect d to d+x(1-d)<=d+x, starting at d=0. For p>=4 apply this to x_j=2^(j-p). It gives 0<=1-|O_p|/2^(4p)<=15*2^(-p). The latter tends to zero along ALL integers: 2^p>=p+1 by induction, so choose p large enough that 15/(p+1)<epsilon. Thus the required full integer limit is 1.
