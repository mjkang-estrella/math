## 323877: full combinatorial route, but source-formula error earns zero

The immutable literal and the visually inspected source page 15, Conjecture 4.14, both display
(1/(n-1)) binom(2n-2,n) binom(2n,n-2). This is not merely a PDF-to-text extraction error. Source definition in section 3 says Rec-star is the INCREASING recurrent configurations, rather than all labelled recurrent configurations. This standing definition is essential. Work below is for n>=2; n=1 makes the printed denominator undefined and supplies no eligible endpoint correction.

Definitions 3.4–3.5 encode an increasing stable configuration by a nondecreasing list of column heights (1+a_1,...,1+a_(n-1),n) and row widths (1+b_1,...,1+b_n). Theorem 3.7, whose proof was read through the Dhar/block-partition bridge, says recurrence is equivalent to the intersection being a parallelogram polyomino. For an increasing recurrent configuration the two boundary profiles recover its heights uniquely. Conversely a parallelogram polyomino in an n by n square determines these nondecreasing profiles, each in [1,n], hence a stable increasing configuration, and Theorem 3.7 returns recurrence. No labelled permutation factor belongs here.

Positivity of every a_i says the upper boundary begins NN. Positivity of every b_i says the lower boundary begins EE. The upper boundary always ends E and the lower always ends N; disjointness is at all intermediate vertices. Delete these prescribed prefixes and suffixes. The remaining two NE paths run
U:(0,2) -> (n-1,n), L:(2,0) -> (n,n-1),
each with length N=2n-3, and must be vertex disjoint. There is no extra forbidden contact with a removed prefix or suffix: antidiagonal times separate those pieces. This also covers n=2, with remaining U=E,L=N.

Here is a full bijection to the walks in the literal (including the horizontal endpoint condition). At common antidiagonal time t=0,...,N in these remaining paths put d(t)=y_U(t)-y_L(t). Initially d=2, finally d=1. Disjointness and planar ordering are exactly d(t)>=1. Pair steps at each time. NN and EE preserve d, NE increases d, EN decreases d. Reverse the pair sequence and set the walk height at time j to d(N-j)-1. Its initial height is 0, final height 1, and it stays nonnegative. Encode a pair NN by an east step and EE by a west step; the two unequal pairs give the uniquely determined north/south step through this reversed height rule. The total height increase of the original two paths is 2n-3=N. Consequently the numbers of NN and EE pairs are equal: the resulting walk ends at horizontal coordinate 0. Conversely in any N-step walk from (0,0) to (0,1), horizontal balance gives equal numbers of those two equal pairs. Invert the encoding and reverse; total pair height increase is N and gap goes from 2 to 1, forcing each individual path endpoint to be the U,L endpoints above. Nonnegative walk height gives disjointness. Attach the deleted pieces and recover the positive increasing recurrent configuration. These two operations are inverse. This settles the intended configuration/walk correspondence at ordinary level, with the cited recurrence bridge, but it does not validate the displayed binomial formula.

An independent counting route explains the discrepancy for every n. Count the two disjoint NE paths by the first-common-vertex tail-swap involution. There are A^2 unrestricted pairs with A=binom(2n-3,n-1). Swapping tails at the first intersection pairs the intersecting ones bijectively with pairs having swapped endpoints. Every swapped-endpoint pair must intersect: the gap on common antidiagonals starts positive and ends negative, changes by at most one and therefore hits zero. Each swapped path has binom(2n-3,n) choices (the complementary n-3 count is equal), giving B^2 bad pairs, where B=binom(2n-3,n). Thus the count is A^2-B^2. Since B/A=(n-2)/n, this equals
(1/(n-1)) binom(2n-2,n) binom(2n-2,n-2).
This is the formula with the second upper index 2n-2. The argument is an ordinary symbolic bijection/involution, not a finite enumeration.

A separate literal disproof needs no recurrence conventions: when n=3 the displayed number is 12. A 3-step half-plane walk from (0,0) to (0,1) has either three vertical steps (NNS or NSN) or one N and two opposite horizontal steps (three choices of N-position and two horizontal orders), so exactly 8. These are two exhaustive structural cases, personally counted, not a computer sweep. The literal identifies its displayed number with this walk count, so the equality is false. The source PDF itself visibly contains the same erroneous formula.

Strongest significance case: the all-n bijection answers the source's express request for a combinatorial explanation and is stronger than the single n=3 mismatch. Nevertheless the only exact-literal refutation offered here is a source numerical/formula correction, explicitly excluded by the user's credit rule. ## 324271: two sustained mechanisms, global hypothesis remains unpaid

Read original definitions, algorithms, Lemma 2.13 and its proof, Remark 2.16, Theorem 3.2 and proof, and Conjecture 4.1. Route A, local root-count obstruction. For a rooted component at x, the number of subtrees containing x is the product of (1+f(B_i)) over its off-path branches. Degree is 2 plus the number of off-path branches. Product size does not by itself order number of factors. For example use adjacent x,y, a leaf v attached to x, a leaf w attached to y, a length-four pendant path attached to x, and two additional leaves attached to y. On v-x-y-w the off-path counts are 5 at x and 4 at y, yet degrees are 3 and 4. This rigorously defeats the inference from one sorted leaf path to its degree ordering.

It is NOT a counterexample to the source conjecture. On the path from the end of the length-four pendant path to w the five internal off-path counts, in path order, are 1,1,1,2,4. They are not centrally unimodal, hence cannot satisfy the required central alternating monotonicity. This is an explicit failed mechanism, not an authenticated earlier full counterexample claim.

Route B, try to exploit the universal quantifier. Rerouting a leaf path into an off-path branch changes f_x by deleting that branch factor and including a previously excluded path tail. An inversion in degrees could perhaps be forced to create a bad rerouted path. But knowing only f_x>=f_y compares products, not individual branch factors. Pigeonholing the larger degree at y only produces a smaller factor somewhere, with no proved control of the included tail at x. The unpaid lemma is: under alternating root-count monotonicity on EVERY leaf path, any adjacent centrally compared pair with f_x>=f_y has at least as many off-path branches at x as at y. No induction proving this, including ties and unequal leaf depths, was obtained.

A tempting variant is to invoke greedy-tree extremality. Source Theorem 3.2 proves the greedy tree is extremal using all three orderings in Remark 2.16, including the degree ordering at issue. It does not say that Phase I count ordering alone characterizes greedy trees. Calling that theorem here would be circular. Phase-II tail ordering also cannot simply be imported: Conjecture 4.1 displays only the component-count condition. Even if Phase-II were established from that universal condition, Lemma 2.13 only says a degree switch increases subtree count; the hypotheses do not assert that H is a maximum. Neither strictness nor the equality case repairs this implication. PARKED, exact global lemma unpaid, no proof/refutation or routine-adaptation exclusion asserted.

## 332117: row-scaling/CRT route and direct reduction route, exact Pre-RGB scope unpaid

Route A: normalize the full Kronecker rows modulo M=X^m-1. Temporarily give every product-order off-diagonal its natural Theorem10 value. Let T be its upper-triangular unshifted matrix, t_i its diagonal, d_i=gcd(M,t_i), and choose u_i M+v_i t_i=d_i. Define H by scaling row i of T by v_i modulo M and giving the diagonal d_i. H's rows lie in span(T) modulo M. At any root z of M in a splitting field, if t_i(z)!=0, Bezout gives v_i(z)=d_i(z)/t_i(z)!=0, so the corresponding pivot row is retained.

The following local rank fact supplies more than a heuristic. If G_A is the genuine RGB generator lattice, the lattice contains M_A I. Localize at a simple root alpha of M_A. In the resulting discrete valuation ring with parameter t, the quotient of the ambient free module by the lattice is killed by t. Therefore its Smith factors have exponent zero or one. Their exponent sum is val(det G_A), which, because each diagonal divides squarefree M_A, is precisely the number of diagonals zero at alpha. Reducing mod t consequently gives rank equal to the number of nonzero diagonal entries. The nonzero-diagonal rows are visibly independent by triangularity, hence span G_A(alpha)'s rowspace. The same holds for G_B(beta). This is a personal elementary local-lattice deduction, not an executed Smith-form computation.

For a root z of M, alpha=z^(b n_B), beta=z^(a n_A) have the required orders dividing m_A and m_B; negative exponents are legitimate since z is nonzero, and substitutions are understood in the quotient where X is a unit. The tensor rowspace is spanned by pairs of the selected rows. These pairs have nonzero t_i(z) and survive the Bezout scaling. Thus T(z) and H(z) have equal rowspaces. Squarefreeness and the Chinese remainder theorem identify the quotient algebra with a product of its root fields after extension, so equality at all roots gives equality of the row modules after extension. Faithfulness of field extension descends equality to F_q: a nonzero quotient vector cannot tensor to zero. Restore the common monomial column shifts, which are invertible modulo M. This proves generation of the same quotient code for the fully specified natural matrix, all finite fields and single-root permitted lengths, without finite testing.

The CRT generation proof permits such rows to be dependent on later pivot rows, whereas C4 demands they be zero. One may choose v_i=0 when d_i=M, yet the literal must first establish the intended existential versus arbitrary-choice quantifier and the omitted same-block entries.
