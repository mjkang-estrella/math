## Literal construction

Take a clique C = {c_i : i in Z/9Z} and an independent set I = {u_i : i in Z/9Z}. Join c_i to u_j exactly when j-i modulo 9 belongs to B = {0,1,2,3,5}. There are 18 vertices and 81 edges. Every u_j has degree 5. Every c_i has eight clique neighbours and five independent neighbours, hence degree 13. The exact edge list is outputs/split-search.json, where vertices 0 through 8 are c_i and vertices 9 through 17 are u_i.

Write N_i = i+B for the independent neighbours of c_i. Every N_i has size five. For shifts 1 through 8 the intersection sizes |B intersect (B+t)| are 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3. In particular, any two distinct clique vertices have at least two common independent neighbours. These counts can also be checked directly from the nine neighbour sets in the saved certificate.

## Complete ordinary proof of the graph properties

The graph is split by its displayed partition. After deleting at most four vertices, at least five clique vertices remain. Every remaining independent vertex retains a clique neighbour, since it originally had five different clique neighbours. The surviving clique is connected and every other surviving vertex attaches to it. Thus the graph is 5-connected. Deleting the five neighbours of any independent vertex isolates it while other vertices remain, so its connectivity is exactly five. Its order satisfies 18 >= 2*5+7 = 17.

Suppose an induced K_(1,6) or K_(1,6)+e occurs, with centre x and six other vertices L. The graph on L has respectively zero or one edge. The centre cannot be independent because every independent vertex has degree five. Thus x=c_i. Put q=|L intersect C|.

If q=0, the six leaves would be six members of N_i, which has size five, impossible. If q=1, all five independent leaves are N_i. The clique leaf c_j has at least two neighbours in N_i, giving at least two edges inside L, impossible. If q=2, the four independent leaves form a four-element subset of N_i. Each of the two clique leaves has at least two neighbours in N_i, and therefore at least one neighbour among these four independent leaves. Together with the edge between the two clique leaves this gives at least three edges in L. If q>=3, the clique leaves alone give at least three edges. All cases contradict the required zero or one edge. Both forbidden induced subgraphs are absent.

Finally there is no spanning path from c_0 to c_1. Such a path would contain all nine independent vertices, none at an endpoint. Consecutive independent vertices are impossible, so the nine independent vertices require at least ten clique vertices to occur before, between and after them. Only nine clique vertices exist. Therefore the graph is not Hamilton-connected. This proves an ordinary refutation of the literal current-preprint conjecture at r=5.
