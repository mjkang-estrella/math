## 345851 — subgroup closure of finite BCI-groups

Literal semantics: for every finite BCI-group G and every subgroup H, every isomorphism BCay(H,S) ≅ BCay(H,T), S,T arbitrary subsets, must imply T=h S^β with h∈H and β∈Aut(H). BCay uses vertices (x,1),(x,2) and edges (x,1)—(sx,2). This is Conjecture 2 of Sattari–Arezoomand–Iranmanesh 2003.06624v1. Its Lemma 1 only treats characteristic H; its proof passage was read. Theorem 1 claiming BCI implies CI is NOT used to infer BCI subgroup closure, and contrary literature snippets about CI versus BCI are not conflated with this question.

First route, disjoint-copy induction: any H-graph isomorphism extends to G by choosing right-coset representatives: (x r,i) maps to (f_i(x) r,i) for a part-preserving isomorphism; component bipartition swaps can first be aligned. Edges stay in Hr because S⊂H. BCI(G) yields T=g S^α in G. Normalize nonempty S,T by elements so 1∈S,T; empty sets are immediate. If both normalized sets generate H, connectedness forces α(H)=H and g∈H: the component through identity in BCay is controlled by <SS^{-1}>=<S>. Restriction then works. But for disconnected graphs the subsets generate only proper K,L≤H. Mapping K to L does not imply α(H)=H. This is the exact missing extension step; characteristic-subgroup reasoning alone fails.

Second route, complement: attempt to make the bi-Cayley graph or its bipartite complement connected. This succeeds except the two equal complete-bipartite-component case. In that case normalized S and T are index-two subgroups of H; Aut(G)-equivalence does not in general give Aut(H)-equivalence. Thus the exceptional case cannot be discarded. The 2025 paper pays this obstruction explicitly in Lemma 3.7, whose proof and exceptional-case argument were read.

A complete earlier-result bridge is now available. Zhang–Feng–Zhou–Yin, arXiv:2506.12306v1 (14 June 2025), Theorem 1.7, Corollaries 3.4 and 3.6, and Lemma 3.5 give the following proof of the literal target.

1. If G is abelian BCI, it is 2PCI by Corollary 3.4. Corollary 3.6 says each Sylow subgroup is elementary abelian or cyclic, and BCay(<S>,S) is 2PCI for every S⊂G. For H≤G, each Sylow subgroup of H is a subgroup of the corresponding Sylow subgroup of G, hence again elementary abelian or cyclic. For every S⊂H the group <S> and graph BCay(<S>,S) are the same ones as in G. Both hypotheses of Corollary 3.6 therefore hold for H, so H is 2PCI, and Corollary 3.4 makes H BCI. The proof of Corollary 3.6 and its use of homogeneity were read; this is not the invalid inference that arbitrary 2PCI-groups have hereditary 2PCI.
2. If G is nonabelian BCI, Theorem 1.7(2) restricts it to D6, D10, Q8. Every proper subgroup of D_{2p}, p=3 or 5, is trivial, C2, or Cp: a subgroup containing two different reflections contains their nonidentity rotation and hence all of D_{2p}; one with no reflection is cyclic; one with a reflection but no nontrivial rotation has order two. Every proper subgroup of Q8 is trivial, C2 or C4, since two elements from different order-four cyclic subgroups generate Q8. The whole subgroup G is BCI by hypothesis. C1 is immediate; C2,C3,C4,C5 are BCI by Theorem 4 of 2003.06624 (the prime and C4 cases are in its stated list). Thus every subgroup is BCI.
3. These cases exhaust all finite G, so the bridge pays the full universal conclusion and every subset, including disconnected and empty graphs.

## 349015 — binary projective three-weight middle weight

Literal target is all projective binary linear [n,k] codes with exactly three nonzero weights w1<w2<w3, their sum 3n/2, and conclusion w2=n/2. Projective means the generator columns form a set S of distinct nonzero vectors in F2^k spanning F2^k. The authentic journal file (s10623-022-01118-z, published 15 November 2022, Designs Codes Cryptography 91 (2023), 645–675) retains exact Conjecture 3.3. Definitions, Conjecture 3.3/Table 2, Lemma 3.1, and the proofs of Lemmas 5.10–5.11 were inspected. The latest arXiv body is v3, 5 October 2022.

First route, moments: put N=2^k, A_i=#words of weight wi. Projectivity gives sum A_i=N-1, sum wi A_i=Nn/2, and sum wi(wi-1) A_i=Nn(n-1)/4. For the third identity, two distinct nonzero binary generator columns are independent, so each ordered pair of coordinates is simultaneously one in exactly N/4 words. These are exact necessary equalities, not sufficient code constructions. The source exhibits feasible parameter tuples excluded by further divisibility restrictions and other tuples not excluded. Therefore solving these equations cannot settle the conjecture.

Second route, Fourier/group-algebra strengthening: let f=1_S on V=F2^k, with unnormalized convolution. Its Fourier transform is λ_u=sum_{s∈S}(-1)^{u·s}=n-2 wt(uG). For u≠0 the three possible values a,b,c have a+b+c=0. Let e2=ab+ac+bc and e3=abc. Thus λ_u^3+e2 λ_u-e3=0. Fourier inversion gives the exact identity

f*f*f + e2 f - e3 δ_0 = h 1_V,   h=(n^3+e2 n-e3)/N.

At 0, repeated entries cannot sum to 0 since 0∉S, so (f*f*f)(0)=6T, where T counts unordered distinct triples summing to 0. At x∈S, triples with a repeated entry contribute 3(n-1)+1=3n-2. The remaining triples are six times unordered distinct triples. At x∉S, x≠0, every contributing triple has distinct entries. Consequently h+e3 is a nonnegative multiple of 6; h-e2-(3n-2) is a nonnegative multiple of 6; h is a nonnegative multiple of 6 if there is an outside nonzero point. These are genuine extra incidence constraints; none forces e3=0 by itself.

Sustain the smallest displayed surviving source tuple rather than pretending a feasible table is a witness: n=112,k=9,(w1,w2,w3)=(50,54,64), multiplicities (48,336,127). Then (a,b,c)=(12,4,-16), e2=-208,e3=-768,N=512,h=2700. The identity becomes f*f*f-208 f+768δ0=2700·1. It would require 322 unordered zero-sum triples, 429 unordered distinct triples summing to each x∈S, and 450 summing to each nonzero x∉S. All are integral and nonnegative. Conversely, an actual 112-point subset of F2^9\{0} satisfying this convolution identity would have all nontrivial Fourier values among 12,4,-16; the first two moments and total count fix the displayed positive multiplicities, so it would span, produce exactly three weights, and refute the literal conjecture. This specifies an exact unpaid finite existence lemma, not a claimed construction. No set S or generator matrix has been found here.

## 348538 — balanced matching equals two

Definition 1.2 and Notation 1.4 of arXiv:2011.01053v2: d given sides of cardinalities nt; nonnegative nonzero edge weights with constant weighted degree on each side. bm is the minimum guaranteed integral matching size over all such hypergraphs. Section 5.2 Conjecture 5.6, Observation 5.7 and its actual construction discussion were read, as was the preceding end of Theorem 5.1 proof. Final journal PDF GET timed out; no alternate endpoint or retry was used. Thus final-version contents remain a precise HOLD.

The lower-bound direction is supplied by source Theorem 1.1: normalize the balanced weighting of an n-partite n-by-...-by-n hypergraph so vertex degree is one, giving fractional matching total n. The partite matching lower bound n/(n-1)>1 yields an integral matching at least two for n≥2. Reproving only this is not the target. n=1 would give one, a domain convention correction only, and is not used for credit.

First construction route: use a standard projective plane of order q. Delete a point P and the q+1 lines through it. The q+1 sides are the deleted lines minus P, each of size q. Every retained line meets every side once. Two retained lines meet away from P, so the hypergraph is intersecting; each remaining point belongs to q retained lines. Giving each edge weight 1/q gives degree one everywhere. Duplicate a side, copying its vertices in every edge, to raise the number of sides without changing balance or intersectingness.

Take two such hypergraphs of orders q and r. Set n=q+r. Pad each to n sides (q+1≤n and r+1≤n when q,r≥1). For each side take the disjoint union of the q and r vertices; union the edge families on these disjoint supports. Every vertex still has weighted degree one, so the union is fractionally balanced, with n sides of size n. It has matching number exactly two: at most one edge from each intersecting component, and one edge from each gives two disjoint edges. This pays balance, uniformity, side size, and the exact matching maximum, rather than just an abstract incidence table. It proves the upper bound on a family, not all n. The source describes a closely related two-truncated-plane construction in its own p,q/p+q-2 convention; the standard order-q convention here is explicitly kept separate, not silently equated to that offset.

Second route, universal padding: appending an isolated vertex to each side destroys balance because old weighted degrees are positive and new ones zero. Appending a separate diagonal edge to restore degree gives one more disjoint edge and increases the possible matching size from two to three. Duplicating vertices within sides also risks splitting a common intersection; an arbitrary blow-up can destroy pairwise intersectingness. Thus neither padding nor generic regularization supplies the missing induction n→n+1.

Third continuation, two-component strategy beyond prime powers: one needs for every n a partition n=u+v into side sizes of intersecting fractionally balanced hypergraphs, each having at most n sides. The source's Conjecture 5.8 isolates a related missing all-parameter construction. Invoking a Goldbach-type representation covers conditional subfamilies, not every integer. Exact unpaid lemma is this balanced intersecting construction for all remaining sizes, or another explicit n-partite n-by-...-by-n balanced hypergraph with matching number exactly two. Park; no universal endpoint, no original finite-instance progress claim, no Pi.
