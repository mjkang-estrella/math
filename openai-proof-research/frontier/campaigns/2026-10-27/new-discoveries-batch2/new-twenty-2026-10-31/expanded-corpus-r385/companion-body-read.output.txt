                                               On kernel isomorphisms of m-Cayley digraphs and finite
                                                                   2PCI-groups

                                                             Xing Zhang, Yan-Quan Feng∗, Jin-Xin Zhou, Fu-Gang Yin
                                                   School of mathematics and statistics, Beijing Jiaotong University, Beijing, 100044, P.R. China




arXiv:2506.12306v1 [math.CO] 14 Jun 2025
                                                                                             Abstract
                                                        The isomorphism problem for digraphs is a fundamental problem in graph theory. In this
                                                    paper, we consider this problem for m-Cayley digraphs which are generalization of Cayley
                                                    digraphs. Let m be a positive integer. A digraph admitting a group G of automorphisms
                                                    acting semiregularly on the vertices with exactly m orbits is called an m-Cayley digraph of
                                                    G. In our previous paper, we developed a theory for m-Cayley isomorphisms of m-Cayley
                                                    digraphs, and classified finite mCI-groups for each m ≥ 2, and finite mPCI-groups for each
                                                    m ≥ 4. The next natural step is to classify finite mPCI-groups for m = 2 or 3. Note that
                                                    BCI-groups form an important subclass of the 2PCI-groups, which were introduced in 2008
                                                    by Xu et al.. Despite much effort having been made on the study of BCI-groups, the problem
                                                    of classifying finite BCI-groups is still widely open.
                                                        In this paper, we prove that every finite 2PCI-group is solvable, and its Sylow 3-subgroup
                                                    is isomorphic to Z3 , Z3 × Z3 or Z9 , and Sylow p-subgroup with p ̸= 3 is either elementary
                                                    abelian, or isomorphic to Z4 or Q8 . We also introduce the kernel isomorphisms of m-Cayley
                                                    digraphs, and establish some useful theory for studying this kind of isomorphisms. Using
                                                    the results of kernel isomorphisms of m-Cayley digraphs together with the results on 2PCI-
                                                    groups, we give a proper description of finite BCI-groups, and in particular, we obtain a
                                                    complete classification of finite non-abelian BCI-groups.

                                                    Keywords: m-Cayley digraph, m-PCayley digraph, Cayley isomorphism, m-Cayley isomor-
                                                    phism, semiregular group.
                                                    2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 20B25, 05C25.

                                           arc set of Γ respectively, while E(Γ) denotes the edge set of Γ when Γ is a graph. For convenience,

                                           arc set {(u, v) : {u, v} ∈ E(Γ)}, and this makes no confusion in this paper. Thus, a graph is a
                                           special case of a digraph.

1.2    m-Cayley isomorphisms of m-Cayley (di)graphs
In graph theory, one of the fundamental problems is to decide whether or not two given graphs
are isomorphic. This paper is an attempt to investigate this problem for m-Cayley digraphs of the
same group. Let Γ = Cay(G, Si,j : 1 ≤ i, j ≤ m) be an m-Cayley digraph of G. Let NSV (Γ) (R(G))
be the normalizer of R(G) in the symmetric group SV (Γ) on V (Γ). Take α ∈ NSV (Γ) (R(G)). It is
easy to see that α is an isomorphism from Γ to Γα = Cay(G, Ti,j : 1 ≤ i, j ≤ m), where Ti,j = Si,j
                                                                                               α

for 1 ≤ i, j ≤ m. We say that such an isomorphism α is an m-Cayley isomorphism. It is natural to
investigate the conditions under which two m-Cayley (di)graphs of the same group are isomorphic
if and only if there is an m-Cayley isomorphism between them.

Definition 1.1. Let G be a group.

  • An m-Cayley (di)graph Γ of a group G is said to be mCI (mCI stands for m-Cayley
    isomorphism) if, for any m-Cayley (di)graph Σ of G isomorphic to Γ, there exists some
    α ∈ NSV (Γ) (R(G)) such that Γα = Σ.

  • If every m-Cayley digraph (m-Cayley graph, resp.) of G is mCI, then G is called an mDCI-
    group (mCI-group, resp.).

    In case m = 1, 1-Cayley (di)graph is just the Cayley (di)graph, and 1CI Cayley (di)graph is
just the CI Cayley (di)graph. Furthermore, the 1DCI-group and 1CI-group are just DCI-group

and CI-group, respectively. There are two long standing open questions on Cayley (di)graphs: (1)
Which Cayley (di)graphs of a group G are CI? (2) Which groups are DCI-groups or CI-groups?
There has been quite a lot of research on these two questions, for which we refer the reader to the
nice survey paper [26] and some recent papers [11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 25, 29, 32, 31, 33, 34, 35].
    Note that it is possible that an m-PCayley digraph of a group G may be isomorphic to an
m-Cayley digraph of a group G which is not m-PCayley. So an interesting problem is to consider
the isomorphisms between two m-PCayley (di)graphs of G. This motivates us to introduce the
following concepts.
Definition 1.2. Let G be a group and let m > 1.
  • An m-PCayley (di)graph Γ of G is said to be mPCI if, for any m-PCayley (di)graph Σ of G
    such that there is an isomorphism from Γ to Σ keeping {G1 , G2 , · · · , Gm } invariant, there is
    some n ∈ NSV (Γ) (R(G)) such that Γn = Σ.
  • If every m-PCayley digraph (m-PCayley graph, resp.) of G is mPCI, then G is called an
    mPDCI-group (mPCI-group, resp.).
    The 2CI-(di)graphs and 2PCI-(di)graphs have also been received considerable attention (see,
for example, [2, 9]). In our previous paper [38], we developed some general theory for mCI-
(di)graphs and mPCI-(di)graphs. With these, we obtained, somewhat to our surprise, a complete
classification of finite mCI- and mDCI-groups for each m ≥ 2, and a complete classification of
finite mPCI- and mPDCI-groups for each m ≥ 4. This suggests that the essential difficult case
in the study of mCI- and mDCI-groups is m = 1, namely, the ordinary CI- and DCI-groups. For
mPCI- and mPDCI-groups, the remaining cases are m = 2 and m = 3. In this paper, we shall
deal with 2PCI-groups. The following is our first main theorem.
Theorem 1.3. Let G be a 2PCI-group. Then G is solvable, and every Sylow 3-subgroup of G is
isomorphic to Z3 , Z3 × Z3 or Z9 , and every Sylow p-subgroup of G with p ̸= 3 is either elementary
abelian, or is isomorphic to Z4 or Q8 .
    This theorem implies that every finite 2PCI-group is solvable and has very restrictive subgroup
structure. So we would like to propose the following problem.
Problem 1.4. Classify finite 2PCI-groups.

1.3    Kernel m-Cayley isomorphisms of m-Cayley (di)graphs
Xu et al. in [36] initiated the study of BCI-groups, which form a special class of 2PCI-groups
defined below. Let Γ be a 2-PCayley graph of a group G. Let N = NSV (Γ) (R(G)) and let K be the
kernel of N acting on {G1 , G2 }. Define K(1G )1 as the stabilizer of the vertex (1G )1 in K. Then Γ is
said to be BCI if, for any 2-PCayley graph Σ of G isomorphic to Γ, there exists some α ∈ K(1G )1
such that Γα = Σ. As R(G) ≤ Aut(Γ), according to Proposition 2.1, it is not difficult to see that
Γ is BCI if and only if for any 2-PCayley graph Σ of G isomorphic to Γ, there exists some α ∈ K
such that Γα = Σ.
    In general, let Γ be an m-PCayley (di)graph of a group G with m ≥ 2. Let K be the kernel
of NSV (Γ) (R(G)) acting on {G1 , G2 , . . . , Gm }. For any β ∈ K, it is easy to see that Γβ is also an
m-PCayley (di)graph of G. Clearly, β is an isomorphism from Γ to Γβ . Such an isomorphism
β will be called a kernel m-Cayley isomorphism. Motivated by this, we introduce the following
concepts which generalize the BCI-graphs.

                                                   3
Definition 1.5. Let Γ be an m-Cayley (di)graph of a group G. Let N = NSV (Γ) (R(G)) and let K
be the kernel of N acting on the orbits set {G1 , G2 , . . . , Gm } of R(G) on V (Γ).
  • Γ is said to be KmCI (stands for kernel m-Cayley isomorphism) if, for any m-Cayley
    (di)graph Σ of G isomorphic to Γ, there exists k ∈ K such that Γk = Σ.

  • If every m-Cayley digraph (m-Cayley graph, resp.) of G is KmCI, then G is called a KmDCI-
    group (KmCI-group, resp.).
Assume that Γ is an m-PCayley (di)graph of a group G. Then
  • Γ is said to be KmPCI if, for any m-PCayley (di)graph Σ of G such that there is an
    isomorphism from Γ to Σ keeping {G1 , G2 , · · · , Gm } invariant, there is some k ∈ K such
    that Γk = Σ.

  • If every m-Cayley digraph (m-Cayley graph, resp.) of G is KmPCI, then G is called an
    KmPDCI-group (KmPCI-group, resp.).
    By definition, every KmPCI-(di)graph is also an mPCI-(di)graph. In case m = 2, the K2PCI-
graphs (namely the BCI-graphs) have been extensively studied in the literature. For example, a cri-
terion for a 2-PCayley graph being K2PCI was given in [2, Theorem C] (see also [23, Lemma 2.21]).
In [3], it was proved that every K2PCI-group (namely, BCI-group) is solvable. In [2], it was proved
that every cyclic group of order 9 or a prime order is K2PCI, and moreover, it was proved that
every Sylow p-subgroup of a K2PCI-group is elementary abelian for p > 3. There is also some
work on K2PCI-graphs with valency at most 3 (see, for example, [17, 22, 23]). For more work
about K2PCI-graphs, we refer the reader to [1, 18, 19, 20, 21, 24].
    Motivated by the facts listed above, in this paper we shall investigate the kernel isomorphisms
of m-Cayley (di)graphs over the same group. We first give a criterion for an m-Cayley (di)graph
being KmCI as well as a criterion for an m-PCayley (di)graph being KmPCI. With these, we
prove the following theorem which gives a classification of KmCI-groups and KmDCI-groups for
all m ≥ 2, and a classification of KmPCI-groups and KmPDCI-groups for all m ≥ 3.
Theorem 1.6. Let m be a positive integer and let G be a finite group. Then
 (1) For m ≥ 2, G is KmCI if and only if m = 2 and G = 1;

 (2) For m ≥ 2, G cannot be KmDCI.

 (3) For m ≥ 3, G cannot be KmPCI and KmPDCI.
   This theorem reveals that the main difficult in the study of KmPCI-groups and KmPDCI-
groups is the case m = 2. Our third main result gives a characterization of abelian K2PCI-groups,
and in particular, a complete classification is given of non-abelian K2PCI-groups.
Theorem 1.7. Let G be a group. Then the following hold.
(1) If G is abelian, then G is K2PCI (namely, BCI) if and only if every Sylow 3-subgroup of G is
    isomorphic to Z3 , Z3 × Z3 or Z9 , every Sylow p-subgroup of G with p ̸= 3 is either elementary
    abelian, or is isomorphic to Z4 , and for any S ⊆ G, Cay(⟨S⟩, S, ∅, ∅, S −1 ) is K2PCI.

(2) If G is non-abelian, then G is K2PCI (namely, BCI) if and only if G is isomorphic to one of
    the following groups: D6 , Q8 or D10 .

   A linear q-ary code C of length n and dimension k is called an [n, k]q code. The number
of positions which are not all-zero is called the effective length of C. If the length equals
the effective length, C is called full-length. Two positions i, j ∈ {1, . . . , n} of C are called
projectively equivalent if there is a λ ∈ Fq∗ with ci = λc j for all codewords c ∈ C. The code
C is called projective if it is full-length and there are no projectively equivalent positions.
For a general full-length code, the position multiplicity type is the sequence (m i ) where m i
denotes the number of projective equivalence classes of size i.
