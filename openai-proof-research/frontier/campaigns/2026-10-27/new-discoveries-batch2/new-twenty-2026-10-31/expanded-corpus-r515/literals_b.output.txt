365567 Let $b\in(0,1)$ and let $c=2^{bn}$. For a cover of $\mathcal{O}\mathcal{I}^n$ of size $c$, write $\rho_{n,n-1,c}$ for the relevant minimum maximum $(n-1)$-coordinate projection volume, and for a part $K$ and a coordinate set $A\subset[n]$ write $K_A$ for its projection onto $A$.

**Product of Tribes optimality conjecture.** There exists a universal constant $\delta$ such that

$$
\rho_{n,n-1,c}\geq\frac{1}{c}\left(1+\delta b\log(1/b)\right).
$$

Equivalently, for any cover of $\mathcal{O}\mathcal{I}^n$ of size $2^{bn}$, some part $K$ and some coordinate set $A\subset[n]$ with $|A|=n-1$ satisfy

$$
|K_A|\geq\frac{1}{c}\left(1+\delta b\log(1/b)\right).
$$

This formalizes the claim that the Product of Tribes construction cannot be substantially improved. The construction gives an upper bound of $\frac{1}{c}(1+O(b\log(1/b)))$, while the general lower bound is only $\frac{1}{c}(1+\Omega(b))$; the conjectured logarithmic factor remains unproved.
365679 Let $B_n$ be the Boolean lattice on $[n]$, let $N$ denote the size of a largest layer of $B_n$, and call the layers closest to the middle the **central layers**. For a function $\beta=\beta(n)$, consider antichains of size $\beta N$ in $B_n$.

**Sparse Sperner conjecture.** There exists a function $\beta^*(n)=\tilde\Theta(1/n)$ such that if $\beta\geq\beta^*(n)$, then almost all antichains of size $\beta N$ in $B_n$ are contained in three central layers.

This strengthens the proved result that the same conclusion holds for $\beta>C\log^2 n/\sqrt n$ for an absolute constant $C$. The conjectured threshold is motivated by the expected appearance of vertices two layers outside the middle, and the claim would follow from a suitable extension of the relevant container result to $\lambda=\tilde\Omega(1/n)$.
365976 Let $\mathcal F$ be a finite graph family, let $\chi(F)$ denote the chromatic number of a graph $F$, let $\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal F)$ be the maximum number of edges in an $\mathcal F$-free graph on $n$ vertices, let $T_{n,2}$ be the Turán graph with two parts, and let $\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal F)$ and $\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal F)$ denote, respectively, the spectrally extremal and extremal $\mathcal F$-free graphs. For sufficiently large $n$, suppose that

$$
\chi(F)=3,\qquad \mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal F)\le e(T_{n,2})+Qn,
$$

where $Q<\frac{1}{3}$. **The spectral extremal conjecture.** For $n$ large enough,

$$
\mathrm{SPEX}(n,\mathcal F)\subseteq\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal F).
$$

This is the proposed weaker analogue, for $r=2$, of the paper's spectral extremal result for non-bipartite graph families with $r\geq 3$. The preceding proposition shows that the conclusion fails at the boundary value $Q=\frac{1}{3}$ for an infinite sequence of $n$; the conjectured range below that threshold remains open.
367192 Let $G$ be a finite simple connected graph with vertex set $V(G)$, adjacency matrix $A_G$, and principal eigenvector $\underline{x}=(x_v)_{v\in V(G)}$ corresponding to the largest eigenvalue, with $x_v>0$. Define

$$
\Gamma_G:=\frac{\|\underline{x}\|_1^2}{\|\underline{x}\|_2^2}=\frac{\left(\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_v\right)^2}{\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_v^2}.
$$

Here $P_k$ denotes the path graph with $k$ vertices, and $K_4+P_{n-4}$ is obtained by attaching an endpoint of $P_{n-4}$ to the unique largest-degree vertex of $K_4$. **Rücker, Rücker and Gutman's conjecture.** If $n\geq 6$, then among $n$-vertex connected graphs, $\Gamma_G$ is minimized by

$$
G=K_4+P_{n-4}.
$$

The quantity $\Gamma_G$ measures how concentrated the principal eigenvector is; it satisfies $\Gamma_G\leq |V(G)|$, with equality precisely for regular graphs. The conjecture was presented as an extremal problem for connected graphs and is also related to questions about principal ratios and other measures of graph irregularity. The supplied source gives no evidence of resolution, so its status remains open.
368393 Let $G$ be a connected graph with $n$ vertices, and let $L$ be a list-assignment of $G$. Write $\mathcal{C}(G,L)$ for the $L$-recolouring graph and $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(G,L)$ for the corresponding relaxed recolouring graph. **Linear-diameter conjectures.** (a) If, in addition to the conditions in the Key Lemma, $G$ has minimum degree at least $3$, then $\mathcal{C}(G,L)$ is connected and has diameter $O(n)$. (b) If, in addition to the conditions in the Main Theorem, $G$ has minimum degree at least $3$, then $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(G,L)$ is connected and has diameter $O(n)$. (c) If, in addition to the conditions in the Main Theorem, $G$ has no path of degree-$2$ vertices of length more than $t$ for some $t\geq0$, then $\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(G,L)$ is connected and has diameter $O((t+1)n)$. These conjectures seek linear, or controlled near-linear, diameter bounds beyond the quadratic diameter forced by long induced paths of degree-$2$ vertices. 371594 Let $G$ be a connected graph. A **PSD fast join** is a graph of order $n\geq 2$ such that $G\cong K_n$ or

$$
G\cong (K_{n_1}\mathbin{\,\dot{\cup}\,} K_{m_1})\vee \ldots \vee (K_{n_t}\mathbin{\,\dot{\cup}\,} K_{m_t}),
$$

for $t\geq 2$ and positive integers $n_i,m_i$ with $n=\sum_{i=1}^t n_i+m_i$. Here $\overline{\operatorname{pt}_+}(G)$ denotes the maximum positive semidefinite propagation time over minimum positive semidefinite forcing sets. **PSD fixed-propagation-time conjecture.** If $G$ is a connected graph and

$$
\overline{\operatorname{pt}_+}(G)=1,
$$

then $G$ is a PSD fast join. The theorem establishing the converse shows that PSD fast joins have fixed PSD propagation time equal to one; the conjecture is proved for graphs that are joins or have sufficiently large forcing numbers, while the general case remains open.
371817 Let $q>r\geq 3$ be integers. An $r$-graph is an $r$-uniform hypergraph, and it is **$K_q^r$-divisible** when it satisfies the divisibility conditions necessary for a decomposition into copies of the complete $r$-graph $K_q^r$. Write $\delta(G)$ for the minimum $(r-1)$-degree and $v(G)$ for the number of vertices. **Hypergraph Nash-Williams conjecture.** If $G$ is a sufficiently large $K_q^r$-divisible $r$-graph such that

$$
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\Theta_r\left(\frac{1}{q^{r-1}}\right)\right)\cdot v(G),
$$

then $G$ admits a $K_q^r$-decomposition. This conjecture generalizes Nash-Williams' triangle-decomposition conjecture to uniform hypergraphs. The source states that the paper proves a bound of order $1-c/q^{r-1+o(1)}$, approximately confirming the conjectured dependence on $q$, but does not establish the conjecture in its full stated form.
375877 Let $k_0\geq 1$ be an integer. For a graph $H$, write $\operatorname{ex}(n,H)$ for its Turán number, and call a rational number $r\in[1,2]$ **realizable** as a Turán exponent if there is a graph $H$ such that

$$
\operatorname{ex}(n,H)=\Theta(n^r).
$$

The minimum feedback vertex number (FVN) of a graph is the minimum number of vertices whose removal makes the graph acyclic. **Feedback-Exponent Conjecture.** There exists a rational number $p/q\in[1,2]$ that cannot be realized as the Turán exponent of any bipartite graph whose minimum FVN is at most $k_0$.

This conjecture asks whether bounded minimum feedback vertex number restricts the rational exponents realizable by bipartite graphs. It is motivated by the Rational Exponent Conjecture of Erdős and Simonovits and by examples showing that, for bipartite graphs with minimum FVN $2$, the Turán exponent is not determined solely by the minimum cycle length; the status of the proposed restriction is not established in the supplied text.
375966 For a graph $G$, let $N(C_q,G)$ denote the number of copies of the cycle $C_q$ in $G$, and let $\operatorname{ex}(n,C_q,\mathcal C_{\ge L+1})$ be the maximum possible value of $N(C_q,G)$ over all $n$-vertex graphs $G$ containing no cycle of length at least $L+1$. Let $H(n,L)$ be the Kopylov-type extremal graph on $n$ vertices with circumference at most $L$. **Zhu et al.'s conjecture.** For fixed integers $q\ge4$ and $L>q$, and all sufficiently large $n$, 

$$
\operatorname{ex}\bigl(n,C_q,\mathcal C_{\ge L+1}\bigr) =N\bigl(C_q,H(n,L)\bigr).
$$

This conjecture proposes that the Kopylov-type construction is asymptotically the exact extremal graph for counting copies of $C_q$ under a bounded-circumference restriction. The source indicates that the result is known for $q=4$ and $q=5$, while the stated general case remains open.
376018 Let $k\geq 4$, let $\binom{[n]}{k}_{s\text{-stable}}$ be the family of $s$-stable $k$-subsets of $[n]$, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a family in this set. An intersecting family is one whose members pairwise intersect; it is a **non-star** family if it is not contained in a family of all sets containing a fixed element. Let $\tau(\mathcal{F})$ denote its covering number. For $x\in[n]$ and $Y\in\binom{[n]}{k}_{s\text{-stable}}$ with $x\notin Y$, define

$$
\mathcal{F}_{s,k}(x,Y)=\{Y\}\cup\{F\in\binom{[n]}{k}_{s\text{-stable}}\mid x\in F,\ F\cap Y\neq\emptyset\},
$$

and let $\mathcal{G}_{s,k}$ be the collection of all such families.

**Extremal-family conjecture.** If $\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}_{s\text{-stable}}$ is a non-star intersecting family with $\tau(\mathcal{F})\geq 2$, then, for $n$ large enough—suggested in the source as $n\geq k(2s-1)+1$—

$$
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\max\{|F|\mid F\in\mathcal{G}_{s,k}\}.
$$

This is an extremal set-theoretic conjecture intended to identify the largest non-star intersecting families of $s$-stable sets. The source gives only a suggested threshold and does not provide a resolution.
