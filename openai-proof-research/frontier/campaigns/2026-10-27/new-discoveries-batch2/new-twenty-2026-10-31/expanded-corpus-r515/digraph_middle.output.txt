Proof. Suppose to the contrary and without loss of generality that |W ∩ V (Gi )| ≥ 1 for i ∈ {1, 2, 3},
and choose vi ∈ W ∩ V (Gi ) for each i. Then G[{v1 , v2 , v3 }] ∼ = K3 , and any b ∈ B is adjacent to
at least two vertices in {v1 , v2 , v3 }. Thus no forces (standard or PSD) are possible, so B is not a
(standard or PSD) forcing set.
Theorem 4.4. If G is a PSD fast join, then pt+ (G) = 1 and Z+ (G) ≥ |V (G)| − 2. If G is a
standard fast join, then pt(G) = 1 and Z(G) ≥ |V (G)| − 2.



                                                     11
Proof. Assume G is a PSD fast join or a standard fast join of order n. If G is a complete graph of
order at least two, then Z+ (G) = Z(G) = n − 1, and so pt+ (G) = pt(G) = 1. So assume G ∼    ̸= Kn ,
which implies Z+ (G), Z(G) ≤ n − 2. Let B be a minimal (standard or PSD) forcing set of G and let
W = V (G) \ B, so that |B| ≤ n − 2 and |W | ≥ 2.
    In light of Lemma 4.3, W can contain vertices from at most two components of G. By Observation
4.2, we may assume G has no white adjacent twins. Thus, W can contain at most one vertex from
each partite set of a component of G that is (complete) bipartite. Observation 4.2 also allows us to
assume no white independent twins if G is a standard fast join; thus W can contain at most one
vertex from a component of G that is a complete graph (K2 = K1,1 is treated as a complete graph
for a standard fast join). We are thus left with the following possibilities for W :
    1. W consists of one vertex from each of two components of G;
    2. W consists of one vertex from each partite set of a single component of G; or
    3. W consists of one vertex from each partite set of one component of G and at least one vertex
       from another component of G.
    First we show that the set B in case (3) is neither a PSD nor a standard forcing set. Note that
in this case, the white vertices induce a connected graph in G, so only the standard zero forcing rule
applies initially.
    • Any blue vertex in a component of G with two white vertices is adjacent in G to the white
      vertex in its partite set and a white vertex in the other component of G with white vertices,
    • Any blue vertex in a component of G with one white vertex is adjacent in G to both of the
      white vertices in the component with two white vertices, and
    • Any other blue vertex is adjacent to all of the white vertices.
Because B cannot perform any forces, it is not a (standard or PSD) forcing set. Since case (3)
cannot occur for PSD or standard forcing and B is any minimal forcing set, the (standard or PSD)
forcing number is at least n − 2.
    For cases (1) and (2), we consider PSD forcing and standard forcing separately, beginning with
PSD forcing. For case (2), both white vertices can be PSD forced in the first round by any vertex in
any of the other components, because the two white vertices are in separate components of G − B.
Now consider case (1). For i = 1, 2, let Ai (containing white vertex wi ) and Bi (all vertices blue)
denote the partite sets of the two components of G containing white vertices. Then in the first
round, w1 can be forced by a vertex in B2 and w2 can be forced by a vertex in B1 . In both cases,
pt+ (G, B) = 1. Thus, for all PSD forcing sets B ⊊ V (G) of G, we have pt+ (G, B) = 1 and therefore
pt+ (G) = 1.
    Finally we consider standard forcing. For case (2), both white vertices can be standard forced in
the first round, each by a blue vertex in the same partite set of the component in G, because each
partite set contains at least two vertices. For case (1), denote the vertices of the components of G
that contain white vertices by U1 and U2 and the white vertices by wi with wi ∈ Ui for i = 1, 2.
Then w1 can be forced in round one by a blue vertex in U2 that is not adjacent in G to w2 , and
similarly for w2 . In both cases, pt(G, B) = 1. Thus, for all standard zero forcing sets B ⊊ V (G) of
G, we have pt(G, B) = 1 and therefore pt(G) = 1.

Lemma 5.1. Let G be a graph and let u and v be adjacent vertices of G such that NG [u] ⊊ NG [v].
Then pt(G, V (G) \ {u, v}) = pt+ (G, V (G) \ {u, v}) = 2.
Proof. Start with exactly the vertices in B = V (G) \ {u, v} blue. Then no vertex can force u in the
first round but there is a vertex x adjacent to v and not to u, so x → v in the first round and v → u
in the second round. Thus pt(G, B) = pt+ (G, B) = 2.
   The next two corollaries are immediate from the previous lemma.
Corollary 5.2. If G is a connected graph of order n ≥ 3 with pt+ (G) = 1 or pt(G) = 1, then G
has no leaves.

Corollary 5.3. If G ∼
                    ̸= Kn and pt+ (G) = 1 or pt(G) = 1, then G cannot have a universal vertex.
   A set B ⊊ V (G) is a slow standard (respectively, PSD) forcing set if pt(G, B) ≥ 2 (respectively,
pt+ (G, B) ≥ 2).
Lemma 5.4. If G is a join of two graphs and at least one of the two conditions pt+ (G) = 1 or
pt(G) = 1 holds, then none of the graphs P4 , P3 ∪˙ K1 , or K2 ∪˙ 2K1 is an induced subgraph of G.
Proof. Suppose that G = G1 ∨ G2 and at least one of the conditions pt+ (G) = 1 or pt(G) = 1 holds.
The proof proceeds by assuming that G has an induced subgraph H isomorphic to one of the three
listed graphs, and showing that this implies a slow (standard or PSD) forcing set exists. Figure 5.1
illustrates the proof.
    In all cases, the four vertices of H must all lie within a single side of the join, without loss of
generality in G1 , because H does not contain a 4-cycle and has no vertex of degree greater than 2.
In the case that H = P4 , label its vertices (w, x, y, z) in order. In the case that H = P3 ∪˙ K1 , let the
vertices of the P3 be labeled (w, x, y) in order and let the vertex of the K1 be labeled z. In the case
that H = K2 ∪˙ 2K1 , let the vertices of the induced K2 be labeled x and y and let the remaining
vertices be labeled w and z. Let p be any vertex of G2 . and let B = V (G) \ {p, y, z}.
    No blue vertex can force y or z in the first round, because the set of white vertices is connected
and every blue vertex is either in G1 , and also adjacent to the white vertex p, or is in G2 , and is
adjacent to both white vertices y and z. The blue vertex w is adjacent to p and to no other white
vertex, so w forces p in first round, then in the second round the vertex x forces y. The vertex z
can be forced by p in the third round if it was not already forced in the second round. Thus B is a
slow forcing set of G.

Lemma 5.7. If pt+ (G) = 1 and G is a join of two graphs, then α(G) ≤ 2 and therefore G has no
induced subgraphs isomorphic to K3 .
Proof. Suppose G = G1 ∨ G2 and there are three independent vertices u, v, and w. These must all
lie in one side of the join, without loss of generality in G1 . Let p be any vertex of G2 . We show
that the complement of the vertex set {p, u, v} is a PSD forcing set that forces in time greater than
one. No blue vertex can force u in the first round, because the set of white vertices is connected and
every blue vertex that is adjacent to u is either in G1 , and is also adjacent to p, or is in G2 and is
also adjacent to v. However, the blue vertex w is adjacent to p and no other white vertex, and so
in the first round it forces p. Now the set of white vertices {u, v} is disconnected, and in the second
round, p can force both u and v.

Theorem 5.8. Let pt+ (G) = 1 and let G be a join of two graphs. Then G is a PSD fast join.

Proof. Since G is the join of two graphs, G has at least two connected components. By Corollary
5.6 and Lemma 5.7, every component of G is a complete bipartite graph. Consider a component of
G and let A and B denote the partite sets. If A (or B) is empty, then B (or A) contains an isolated
vertex of G and therefore a universal vertex of G, implying that G ∼
                                                                   = Kn (by Corollary 5.3).
   Next we consider standard forcing.

Lemma 5.9. Let G be a join of two graphs G1 and G2 , and G1 ∼
                                                            = K1,r with r ≥ 2. Then pt(G) ≥ 2.
Proof. Let c be the center of the star G1 , let u and v be leaves of G1 , let w ∈ V (G2 ), and define
B = V (G) \ {c, v}. No vertex in G2 can perform a force in the first round since such a vertex is
adjacent to both v and c in G. Since u is adjacent in G to v but not to c, u can force v in the first
round. However, since NG (c) = V (G2 ), c cannot be forced in the first round. After round one, c is
the only white vertex, so any of its neighbors (e.g., w) can then force c.
Theorem 5.10. If pt(G) = 1 and G is a join of two graphs, then G is a standard fast join.
Proof. Assume pt(G) = 1 and G is a join of two graphs, so G has at least two connected components.
If G is not only a join but a complete graph, then G is indeed a standard fast join. Otherwise it
suffices to show, for any component H of G, that H is either complete of order at least two, or is
complete bipartite with each partite set containing at least two vertices.

    By Theorem 5.5, H is either complete or complete bipartite. If H is complete, it must be of
order at least two so as to avoid the universal vertices that are forbidden by Corollary 5.3. This
leaves only the case where H is complete bipartite. The case where one partite set has exactly one
vertex and the other has more than one makes H a star graph of the sort forbidden by Lemma 5.9.
Thus A and B each contain at least two vertices.
