# MathDB 362436: ordinary counterexample, not an accepted solution

Source read: Andrew Steane, arXiv:2403.16197, Sections 2, 3, 4.1.1 and 5, including definitions, local rerouting explanation, conjecture, and tensor-product proof. The cited older arXiv:1709.03974 was retrieved but is an unrelated monoid paper; do not use that metadata link as a predecessor body. No source-access clearance follows from this mismatch.

Let V = (Z/12Z) × {0,1,2,3}. Join (i,a) and (j,b) exactly when j-i = ±1 modulo 12 and a ≠ b. This is a simple undirected connected graph with 48 vertices and 144 edges. It is the categorical product C_12 × K_4.

Every vertex is distance redundant. Fix v=(i,t). For any two neighbors x=(j,a), y=(k,b), choose a label c outside {a,b,t}; four labels suffice. Then w=(i,c) differs from v and is adjacent to both x and y. In any walk whose endpoints differ from v, replace every passage x,v,y by x,w,y. If x=y, remove that two-step excursion instead. Each operation reduces the number of appearances of v and never increases length. It gives a walk in G-v of length at most the original. Inclusion gives the reverse inequality on shortest lengths. Thus deletion preserves all distances, with precisely the source definition, not merely bounded reachability.

No vertex is surrounded by a distinct vertex. Write S_i={i-1,i+1}. If u=(i,a) and v=(i,b) are distinct, the vertex (i+1,b) is a neighbor of u but belongs to neither N(v) nor {v}. If their layers i,j differ, S_i and S_j are distinct two-element sets on the 12-cycle. Choose k in S_i\S_j and choose c distinct from a and, if k=j, also distinct from b. Then (k,c) belongs to N(u) but not N[v]. This proves N(u) is not a subset of N[v] for every ordered pair u≠v; proper versus nonproper subset interpretations make no difference.

Projection onto the 12-cycle sends every edge to a cycle edge. Consequently the distance between (0,0) and (6,0) is at least 6. A six-edge path exists: take layers 0,1,2,3,4,5,6 with labels 0,1,0,1,0,1,0. Thus the diameter is at least 6. Since 32≤48<64, floor(log_2 48)=5. This already refutes the proposed universal upper bound, without touching a small-order or domain endpoint.

For completeness, the diameter is exactly 6. Let δ be the cycle distance between the layers. If δ≥2, use a shortest layer path and a length-δ walk in K_4 with the prescribed endpoint labels. Such a label walk exists for every length ≥2: length 2 uses a label different from both endpoints; length 3 uses two suitable intermediate labels; append a two-edge excursion for larger lengths. If δ=0 and labels differ, a two-edge excursion through an adjacent layer works. If δ=1 and labels agree, take a three-edge layer walk i,j,i,j and three distinct labels along the first three vertices, returning to the initial label at the fourth. The remaining cases have distance 0 or 1. The literal distance table in Candidate.lean records these values.

The source Theorem 5.1(ii) assumes both tensor factors are strong. C_12 is not strong, so that theorem alone does not give this construction.
