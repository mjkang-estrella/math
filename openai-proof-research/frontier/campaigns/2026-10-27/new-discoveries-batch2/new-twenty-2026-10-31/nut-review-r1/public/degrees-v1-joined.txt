single zero eigenvalue such that all non-zero kernel eigenvectors have no

It is known that infinitely many d d -regular nut graphs exist for 3 ≤ d ≤ 12 3\leq d\leq 12 and for d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 such that d ≡ 0 ( mod 4 ) d\equiv 0\pmod{4} .
Here it is shown that infinitely many d d -regular nut graphs exist for each degree d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . Keywords: Nut graph, regular graph, circulant graph, nullity, graph spectra. Math. Subj. Class. (2020): 05C25, 05C50. 1 Introduction Nut graphs are graphs that have a one-dimensional nullspace (i.e., η ⁡ ( G ) = 1 \eta(G)=1 ),
where the non-trivial kernel eigenvector 𝐱 = [ x 1 ⋯ x n ] ⊺ ∈ ker 𝐀 ( G ) \mathbf{x}=[x_{1}\ \cdots\ x_{n}]^{\intercal}\in\ker\mathbf{A}(G) is full (i.e., it has no zero entries).
Early work established that non-trivial nut graphs are connected, non-bipartite and have no
leaves, and that the
smallest non-trivial
nut graphs have seven or more vertices [ 25 ] . If G G is a d d -regular nut graph, then the degree d ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 d(G)\geq 3 .
Regular nut graphs with d ⁡ ( G ) = 2 d(G)=2 do not exist, as the nullity of a cycle is either 0 0 or 2 2 .
The case d ⁡ ( G ) = 3 d(G)=3 is of interest in chemical applications of nut graphs [ 24 ] .
It has been shown that there exist infinitely many d d -regular nut graphs for
each degree 3 ≤ d ≤ 11 3\leq d\leq 11 [ 19 ] .
In the same paper, for every 3 ≤ d ≤ 11 3\leq d\leq 11 , the authors determined the orders for which d d -regular nut graphs exist.
These results were extended to degree d = 12 d=12 in [ 4 ] . A special class of regular graphs is the class of vertex-transitive graphs [ 5 , 18 , 21 ] .
For results on highly symmetric nut graphs see [ 2 , 3 , 16 ] and references therein.
A well known class of vertex-transitive graphs is the class of circulant graphs [ 21 , Section 1.5] .
By Circ ⁡ ( n , S ) \Circ(n,S) , where S ⊆ { 1 , 2 , … , ⌊ n 2 ⌋ } S\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\} , we denote the graph on
the vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) = ℤ n V(G)=\mathbb{Z}_{n} , where vertices u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u,v\in V(G) are adjacent if and only if u − v ∈ S u-v\in S or v − u ∈ S v-u\in S (where addition is done in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} ).
Circulant nut graphs have been extensively studied [ 13 , 14 , 15 , 17 ] .
The paper [ 14 ] determined all degree-order pairs for which circulant nut graphs exist, thereby
extending the results of [ 19 ] and [ 4 ] to cover all degrees d ≡ 0 ( mod 4 ) d\equiv 0\pmod{4} .
The present work completes the story by resolving the outstanding degrees d d , where d > 12 d>12 such that d ≢ 0 ( mod 4 ) d\not\equiv 0\pmod{4} .
