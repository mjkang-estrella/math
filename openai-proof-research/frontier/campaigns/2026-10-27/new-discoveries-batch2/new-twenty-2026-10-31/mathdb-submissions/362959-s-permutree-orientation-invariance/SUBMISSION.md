Proved. For fixed s, changing an up decoration to a down decoration changes neither the cardinality of the delta-permutree lattice nor the f-vector of its canonical join complex. ## Problem statement as recorded on MathDB

Fix an $n$-tuple ${\boldsymbol{s}}$ of non-negative integers and a decoration map ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ assigning one of the four local orientations—none, up, down, or up-down—to each index $i$ with $s_i\ne0$. Let $W_{\boldsymbol{s}}/\equiv_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ be the ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$-permutree lattice, and let its canonical join complex be the simplicial complex formed by canonical join representations.

**Orientation invariance conjecture for s-permutree lattices.** For fixed ${\boldsymbol{s}}$, changing any up decoration to a down decoration in ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$ does not affect the cardinality of the ${\boldsymbol{\delta}}$-permutree lattice or the $f$-vector of its canonical join complex.

The conjecture concerns invariants that remain unchanged under the indicated decoration change, while the source notes that other structures, such as the undirected cover graph and face lattices, can depend on the positions of the up and down decorations. No resolution is supplied.

## Summary of the result

For every n, every tuple s of non-negative integers (zero entries allowed), every decoration of the other positions and every position k decorated up, changing the decoration at k from up to down changes neither the number of elements of the delta-permutree lattice (the quotient of the s-weak order by the lattice congruence generated by the decoration) nor, for any m, the number of faces of size m of its canonical join complex. This is Conjecture 76 of Philippe and Pilaud. Lean formalizes the objects literally: s-trees and their order with a proved lattice structure, s-arcs, the generated congruence (proved to contract exactly the forbidden arcs), the quotient lattice and canonical join representations defined lattice-theoretically; the proof identifies faces with marked arc diagrams by a sweep bijection and proves the forward direction of the source's Theorem 72 by a direct congruence construction.
