## Summary (paste into the MathDB summary field)

Proved for every binary polynomial with at least two nonzero terms. Garbe (arXiv:1304.4635, Conjecture 3.7) conjectured that for p = 2 the growth rate λ is at least 3, with λ = 3 only for polynomials similar to 1+x, and that λ > 3 forces λ ≥ 1+√5, with equality only for polynomials similar to 1+x+x^2. All four parts hold, and both 'only' clauses hold as equivalences. As printed, the first sentence fails for monomials (λ = 2) and for 0. The proof is formally verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib and kernel-checked with only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. ## Problem statement as recorded on MathDB

Let $f(x)$ be a polynomial and let $\lambda=\lambda(f)$ be the associated growth rate. Two polynomials are **similar** when they are related by the transformations described in the paper. **Eigenvalue-bound conjecture.** When $p=2$, $\lambda\geq 3$. Moreover, $\lambda=3$ only for polynomials similar to $1+x$. If $p=2$ and $\lambda>3$, then $\lambda\geq 1+\sqrt{5}$; moreover, $\lambda=1+\sqrt{5}$ only if $f(x)$ is similar to $1+x+x^2$. These numerical bounds are inferred from computations and are presented as conjectural classifications of the first possible values of $\lambda$.

## Summary of the result

The conjecture holds for every polynomial over the field with two elements that has at least two nonzero terms: the growth rate λ of the number of nonzero coefficients in the first 2^k powers of f is at least 3, it equals 3 exactly when f is similar to 1+x, there is no value strictly between 3 and 1+√5, and it equals 1+√5 exactly when f is similar to 1+x+x^2. The bound λ ≥ 3 is short: after removing common powers every state of the section recursion has at least three children. The gap and the two classifications need more: multiplicities are projected modulo the direction of f, each three-way choice is deferred for a fixed number of steps, and states are weighted by (1+√5) to the number of pending choices; a count to any fixed depth cannot give 1+√5, as the polynomials 1+x+x^(2^m) show. For monomials the rate is 2 and for the zero polynomial it is 1, so the first sentence of the conjecture as printed fails there; the source's own tables and remarks concern non-monomials only. Lean defines the cumulative count, the growth rate as the limit of its k-th root along 2^k, and similarity as the equivalence relation generated by shift, dilation, reversal and powers, and proves the full statement for polynomials with at least two terms, as well as the values 2 and 1 in the degenerate cases.
