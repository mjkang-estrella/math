## Problem statement as recorded on MathDB

Let $n\geq 1$, and let $(k,n)$ be a valid pair for Littlewood polynomials. A pair is **Littlewood-admissible** if some Littlewood polynomial of degree $n$ has exactly $k$ zeros in the open unit disk and no zeros on the unit circle. **Littlewood-admissibility conjecture.** The pairs $(2,n)$ and $(n-2,n)$ with $n\equiv 1\pmod 6$ and $n\geq 13$ are the only non-trivial Littlewood-inadmissible pairs. Computations establish this classification for $n\leq31$, while the assertion for all larger degrees remains open.

## Summary of the result

The conjecture holds: for 1 ≤ k ≤ n−1, the pair (k, n) is inadmissible exactly when n ≥ 13, n ≡ 1 (mod 6) and k = 2 or k = n−2. There are two halves. Exclusion, for every such n (the source had it only up to n = 31, by exhaustive computation): a Littlewood polynomial with no zero on the circle and exactly two zeros inside is coprime to its reciprocal, so its Schur–Cohn form is nonsingular with exactly two negative directions; the tree of sign prefixes that survive this test is small and repeats with period 12, which is carried to every depth by stationary submatrices and 60 kernel vectors, and every completed word is excluded by an exact certificate. This half is computer-assisted: about 32,000 certificate cases, all checked in the Lean kernel together with proofs that they cover every case. Existence, for every other pair: most cases are theorems of the source; the remaining cases (even k ≥ 12 in two ranges) are filled by a new family of polynomials, whose zero counts are proved in Lean by a root-counting argument on arcs and a strict-dominance comparison. Lean defines Littlewood polynomials (all coefficients ±1), the count of zeros in the open disc with multiplicity, and admissibility literally, proves the Schur–Cohn root count from scratch, and proves the classification from five results of the source taken as explicit hypotheses.
