# A Frobenius-twisted Kummer counterexample to Gow–McGuire Conjecture 1

MathDB 354868. Formalization and manuscript lane, 20 November 2026.

## Abstract

We construct a counterexample to Conjecture 1 of Rod Gow and Gary McGuire,
*Linearized Polynomials, Galois Groups and Symmetric Power Modules*,
arXiv:2206.01551v1. Over the subfield generated by the prime field and
`t = u^80` in `F₈₁(u)`, the polynomial

\[
L(X)=X^{81}+(t+t^2)X^{27}-(t+t^2)X^9+(t+t^2)X^3
       -t(t^2+t+1)X
\]

has 3-degree four, is not a `3^s`-polynomial for any integer `s>1`, and
`L(X)/X` is irreducible. Nevertheless its degree-two evaluation map on homogeneous
forms over `F₃` has a nonzero kernel. The roots are obtained by applying
`x ↦ x+x³` to the Kummer root space `u F₈₁`. The same change preserves the
transitive nonzero-root orbit while removing the excluded polynomial form.
The packet includes Lean proofs for this concrete field construction and the
negation of the universal conjecture at `q=3`.

## 1. Source statement and literal definitions

The source's Conjecture 1, printed page 26, reads:

> Conjecture 1. Let \(L=L(x)\) be a \(q\)-polynomial in \(F[x]\) of \(q\)-degree
> \(n\), where \(F\) is a field of characteristic \(p\) that contains \(\mathbb F_q\).
> Assume that \(L\) is not a \(q^s\)-polynomial for \(s>1\). Assume that
> \(L(x)/x\) is irreducible over \(F\).
>
> If \(1\le r\le q-1\) then \(\epsilon_r\) is injective.

Here and below the source's mathematical typography has been rendered in LaTeX;
its wording is retained. We now place its definitions next to their Lean forms.

In Section 1 the source writes:

> Let \(p\) be a prime number, and let \(q=p^a\) where \(a\) is a positive integer.
> Let \(\mathbb F_q\) denote the finite field with \(q\) elements. Let \(F\) be a
> field of characteristic \(p\), and assume that \(F\) contains \(\mathbb F_q\).
> A \(q\)-linearized polynomial over \(F\) is a polynomial of the form
> \[
> L=a_0x+a_1x^q+a_2x^{q^2}+\cdots+a_nx^{q^n}\in F[x].
> \]
> If \(a_n\ne0\) we say that \(n\) is the \(q\)-degree of \(f\). We will usually
> say \(q\)-polynomial instead of \(q\)-linearized polynomial.

The source uses the letter `f` in that last sentence. The corresponding Lean
predicate and degree condition are:

```lean
def IsQPoly {A : Type*} [Semiring A] (q : ℕ) (L : Polynomial A) : Prop :=
  ∀ k ∈ L.support, ∃ i : ℕ, k = q ^ i
-- With IsQPoly 3 L, q-degree n is expressed as:
-- L.natDegree = 3 ^ n
```

For `q=3`, powers of three are strictly increasing. A nonzero polynomial with
this support has a unique highest exponent `3^n`, so the degree condition is
exactly the source's nonzero highest coefficient condition. In particular it
excludes the zero polynomial.

Section 2 says:

> We next consider a slight variation on the theme of \(q\)-polynomials.
> Let \(s\ge1\) be an integer and let
> \[
> L(x)=a_0x+a_1x^{q^s}+a_2x^{q^{2s}}+\cdots+a_mx^{q^{ms}}\in F[x],
> \]
> where we assume that \(a_m\ne0\). Then \(L\) is a \(q\)-polynomial of
> \(q\)-degree \(ms\) and it is also a \(q^s\)-polynomial. We will not always
> require that \(\mathbb F_{q^s}\) is a subfield of \(F\) when \(s>1\).

We use its support condition literally:

```lean
def IsQsPoly {A : Type*} [Semiring A] (q s : ℕ) (L : Polynomial A) : Prop :=
  ∀ k ∈ L.support, ∃ i : ℕ, k = q ^ (s * i)
```

Our polynomial has positive degree, so no issue concerning a leading coefficient
of the zero polynomial enters this predicate.

Section 4, printed page 9, gives the domain and map. Here `A(𝔽_q)` is the
polynomial ring in the n formal variables `x_i`:

> Definition For each positive integer \(r\), let \(H_{n,r}(\mathbb F_q)\)
> denote the subspace of \(A(\mathbb F_q)\) consisting of all homogeneous
> polynomials of degree \(r\) in the \(x_i\), along with the zero polynomial.

> As before, let \(E\) denote a splitting field for \(L\) over \(F\).
> Let \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\in E\) be an \(\mathbb F_q\)-basis for \(V\).
> Consider the mapping \(\epsilon_r:H_{n,r}(\mathbb F_q)\longrightarrow E\)
> defined by
> \[
> \epsilon_r P(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=P(v_1,\ldots,v_n).
> \]
> Thus \(\epsilon_r\) evaluates a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(r\) on
> the basis of \(V\) and hence determines an element of \(E\).
> Note that \(\epsilon_r\) is an \(\mathbb F_q\)-linear mapping.

The formal definitions are the actual homogeneous submodule and its evaluation
linear map, with coefficients in `ZMod 3`, not in `F`:

```lean
abbrev H (n r : ℕ) :=
  MvPolynomial.homogeneousSubmodule (Fin n) (ZMod 3) r

def eps {E : Type*} [CommRing E] [Algebra (ZMod 3) E]
    {n : ℕ} (v : Fin n → E) (r : ℕ) : H n r →ₗ[ZMod 3] E :=
  (MvPolynomial.aeval v).toLinearMap.comp (H n r).subtype
```

For transparency the complete universal Lean proposition is:

```lean
def Conjecture : Prop :=
  ∀ (F E : Type) [Field F] [Field E] [CharP F 3]
    [Algebra (ZMod 3) F] [Algebra F E] [Algebra (ZMod 3) E]
    [IsScalarTower (ZMod 3) F E]
    (n r : ℕ) (L : F[X]) (v : Fin n → E),
    IsQPoly 3 L → L.natDegree = 3 ^ n →
    (∀ s, 1 < s → ¬ IsQsPoly 3 s L) →
    Irreducible L.divX → Polynomial.IsSplittingField F E L →
    LinearIndependent (ZMod 3) v →
    (∀ x : E, aeval x L = 0 ↔ x ∈ Submodule.span (ZMod 3) (Set.range v)) →
    1 ≤ r → r ≤ 3 - 1 → Function.Injective (eps v r)
```

Only `p=q=3` is fixed. All fields of that characteristic, all polynomials,
all splitting fields and root-space bases, and both integer parameters stay
universally quantified. The conclusion `¬ Conjecture` is the literal negation
of this universal instance. It refutes the printed conjecture, which in
particular asserts this instance. Splitting and basis hypotheses encode the
source's definitions of `E` and `v`, rather than a surrogate tensor model.

The source identifies the symmetric power with homogeneous polynomials in a
basis in Section 1 and defines the map using precisely this polynomial model
in Section 4. This identification holds in every degree. If symmetric tensors
are instead presented as permutation-invariant tensors, averaging identifies
them with the symmetric quotient when `r!` is invertible; here `r=2` in
characteristic three satisfies that condition.

Although Conjecture 1 follows a subsection on q-degree two, it explicitly
quantifies over q-degree `n`. The preceding Theorem 21 explicitly drops the
regular-extension and special-linear-group assumptions of Theorem 20.
Neither assumption is imposed in the printed conjecture. The present base
field is in fact a rational function field over `F_3`, so it also satisfies
the regular-extension condition.

## 2. The explicit field and root space

Let
\[
k=\mathbb F_3,\quad f(Z)=Z^4+Z+2,\quad K=k[Z]/(f),\quad z=Z\bmod f.
\]
The quartic has no root in `k`. The monic irreducible quadratics over `k` are
`Z²+1`, `Z²+Z+2`, and `Z²+2Z+2`; the respective remainders of `f` are `Z`,
`Z+1`, and `Z+1`. Thus `f` is irreducible, `K` is a field with 81 elements,
and `(1,z,z²,z³)` is a `k`-basis. The Lean proof takes a slightly more direct
finite route: it gives Bézout certificates for all nine monic quadratics, in
addition to excluding the three linear factors. No probabilistic or external
finite-field oracle is used.

Set `E=K(u)`, with `u` transcendental, and let `F` be the subfield of `E`
generated by `k` and `t=u^80`. In Lean these are exactly:

```lean
abbrev K := AdjoinRoot modulus
abbrev E := RatFunc K
def u : E := RatFunc.X
def tE : E := u^80
def F : IntermediateField k E := IntermediateField.adjoin k {tE}
def t : F := ⟨tE, IntermediateField.subset_adjoin k {tE}
  (Set.mem_singleton tE)⟩
def L : F[X] := poly t
```

There is no replacement of `F` by a different field with a paper-only
isomorphism. The formal polynomial is over this very subfield of `E`.

For `a∈K`, put
\[
w_a=au+a^3u^3.
\]
The map `a ↦ w_a` is `k`-linear: Frobenius is additive, and the elements of
`k` satisfy `c³=c`. It is injective, since equality of two such rational
functions gives equality of polynomials in the transcendental `u`, whose
linear coefficients are the two values of `a`. Consequently
\[
W=\{w_a:a\in K\}
\]
has 81 elements and dimension four over `k`; a basis is
`v_i=w_(z^i)` for `0≤i≤3`.

Every `a∈K` satisfies `a^81=a`. Write `v=au` and `w=v+v³`. Then
`v^81=tv`, and characteristic three gives
\[
S(w):=w-w^3+w^9-w^{27}=v-v^{81}=(1-t)v. \tag{1}
\]
Similarly,
\[
w^{81}=t^3w+(t-t^3)v. \tag{2}
\]
The identity `(t+t²)(1-t)=t-t³` combines (1) and (2) to give
\[
w^{81}-t^3w-(t+t^2)(w-w^3+w^9-w^{27})=0,
\]
which expands to `L(w)=0`. Thus the 81 distinct elements of `W` are roots
of the monic degree-81 polynomial `L`, and they are all its roots. This
proves the claimed basis and span description, not just inclusion in the
root set.

The coefficient `t+t²` is nonzero: its image in `K(u)` is the polynomial
`u^80+u^160`, with nonzero coefficient at degree 80. Hence the exponent three
occurs in `L`. If `L` were a `3^s`-polynomial with `s>1`, we would have
`3=3^(s i)` for some integer `i≥0`, forcing `s i=1`, a contradiction.
The highest exponent is `81=3^4`, so `L` has 3-degree four. Its linear
coefficient is nonzero as well: its image in `K(u)` is
`-(u^240+u^160+u^80)`, whose degree-240 coefficient is `-1`.

## 3. Irreducibility and the splitting field

For each `c∈K*`, substitution `u ↦ cu` defines an automorphism `σ_c` of
`E=K(u)` fixing `K`. It fixes `t` because `c^80=1`, and hence fixes the
subfield `F` generated by `k` and `t`. Moreover
\[
\sigma_c(w_1)=cu+c^3u^3=w_c.
\]
These give 80 distinct conjugates of `w_1`. The element `w_1` is integral
over `F`, since it is a root of the monic polynomial `L`. Its minimal
polynomial therefore has degree at least 80. On the other hand `w_1≠0`,
so `L(X)/X` is a monic polynomial of degree 80 vanishing at `w_1`. The
minimal polynomial divides it and the degrees force equality. Thus
`L(X)/X` is irreducible over `F`.

This argument is implemented using genuine polynomial-ring scaling
automorphisms lifted to fraction rings, followed by a proof that they fix
every element of `IntermediateField.adjoin k {t}`. There is no assumption
about a Galois group or a paper-only appeal to Eisenstein.

It remains to check that `E` is the splitting field as used in the definition
of `ε_r`. Splitting already follows from the 81 roots. Since `t≠1`, equation
(1) recovers
\[
au=\frac{w_a-w_a^3+w_a^9-w_a^{27}}{1-t}. \tag{3}
\]
Let `S` be the intermediate field generated over `F` by the roots. It
contains every `au`; taking `a=1` yields `u`, which is nonzero. Division
by `u` yields every constant `a∈K`. Finally every rational function in
`K(u)` is a quotient of polynomials in `u` with coefficients in `K`, so
`S=E`. Lean proves both the splitting assertion and this generation
assertion, and then obtains `Polynomial.IsSplittingField F E L`.

## 4. A nonzero homogeneous quadratic in the kernel

In four formal variables over `k`, define
\[
Q=-X_0^2+X_0X_1-X_0X_2-X_0X_3-X_1X_2+X_2^2+X_3^2.
\]
All terms have degree two. The form is nonzero, for example because
`Q(0,0,0,1)=1`.

For coefficients `c_ij` of this quadratic and `a_i=z^i`, set
\[
B=\sum_{i\le j}c_{ij}a_ia_j,\qquad
C=\sum_{i\le j}c_{ij}(a_i a_j^3+a_i^3 a_j).
\]
Expansion of products of twisted roots gives
\[
Q(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)=Bu^2+Cu^4+B^3u^6, \tag{4}
\]
because all `c_ij` are in `k` and satisfy `c_ij³=c_ij`.
For this particular `Q`,
\[
\begin{aligned}
B&=-1+z-z^2-2z^3+z^4+z^6,\\
C&=-2+z-(z^6+z^2)-z^9-(z^7+z^5)+2z^8+2z^{12}.
\end{aligned}
\]
In characteristic three these satisfy the polynomial identities
\[
B=(1+z^2)(z^4+z+2),\qquad
C=(-1+z-z^2-z^3+z^4-z^8)(z^4+z+2).
\]
Thus `B=C=0` in `K`, so (4) vanishes. Equivalently, the literal nonzero
member `Q∈H_(4,2)(k)` and the zero polynomial have the same image under
`ε₂`. The Lean proof certifies the expanded quadratic identity in an
arbitrary characteristic-three ring satisfying `z⁴+z+2=0`, and then
specializes it to `E`. No computational axiom or unchecked evaluation is
needed.

We have now verified every source hypothesis for `q=3`, `n=4`, and `r=2`,
including `1≤2≤3-1`, while `ε₂` is not injective. This proves the claimed
refutation. ∎

## 5. Significance, prior art, and verification boundaries

The construction starts from the excluded Kummer root space of
`X^81-tX`, then applies the additive twist `x↦x+x³`. The twist keeps the
transitive orbit needed for irreducibility while making the coefficient
of `X³` nonzero. Quadratic products still have linked outer coefficient
blocks, so a relation survives. This explains the example structurally;
the finite arithmetic supplies a compact witness. We make no claim here
about minimality of the counterexample or about a formally proved family
for arbitrary `q` and `n`.

The independent Opus review of 20 November 2026 reported: “Not public, as
far as an honest search can tell.” Its bounded checks found only arXiv v1,
no journal version of this precise paper, and no matching resolution in
the inspected public registries or follow-up papers. In particular the
Müller paper arXiv:2311.14953 concerns a different Gow–McGuire conjecture,
and the inspected citing papers did not discuss `ε_r` or Conjecture 1.
The source's Theorem 12 treats the base field `F = F_q`, Theorem 15 treats the
excluded `q^s` case, Theorem 17 constrains relations involving two
independent roots, and Theorems 20–21 concern dimension two. None directly
yields this admissible counterexample. The review nevertheless records
moderate routine-risk: a specialist might regard additive twisting as
standard once suggested. This lane inherits that bounded prior-art
assessment; it has not performed a new exhaustive literature or registry
search and makes no unconditional priority claim.

The complete formal statement is the negation of `Gow.Conjecture`, not
