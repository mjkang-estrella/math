> Suppose n is even and k is odd with n/2 ≤ k < n − 3. Let h = (n − k − 1)/2 and p = gcd(n, h). If n/2 ∈ ⟨h⟩, then

The source's label and span conventions (p. 2) are:

> For a positive integer k, a function f : V(G) → {0, 1, 2, 3, · · · } is a radio-k-labeling of a graph G, if for all u, v ∈ V(G), u ≠ v,
>
> |f(u) − f(v)| ≥ k − d(u, v) + 1.

> The span of a radio-k-labeling f of G is defined as span(f) := max{|f(u) − f(v)| : u, v ∈ V(G)}. We follow the convention of labeling at least one vertex 0, so the span of any radio-k-labeling is the largest label assigned to a vertex v ∈ V(G).

## Theorem and complete written proof

**Theorem (written proof and kernel-checked Lean endpoint).** Under exactly the hypotheses of Conjecture 1 above, rn_k(C_n)=LB(n,k)+p−1.

For n≥3 the cycle consists of consecutive residues around a ring. A shortest path between two residues follows one of the two arcs: a path with no repeated vertices has no branching choice after its first edge, since each vertex has exactly two neighbors. The arc lengths are |i−j| and n−|i−j|. This proves the metric formula used in the written argument. Splitting any walk gives the triangle inequality. Translating all residues preserves distance. All modular equalities in the following proof refer to this cycle.

## An elementary path lemma

If z_0,...,z_m are real numbers, let L=sum_(i<m)|z_(i+1)-z_i| and R=max_i z_i-min_i z_i. Then

2R <= L+|z_m-z_0|.                                                  (P)

Proof. Choose occurrences of the minimum and maximum. If a minimum precedes a maximum, the path length is at least

(z_0-min)+(max-min)+(max-z_m)=2R+z_0-z_m.

If a maximum precedes a minimum, the analogous bound is 2R+z_m-z_0. In either case L>=2R-|z_m-z_0|. This also covers R=0. QED.

## Proof of the new lower bound

Write

n=2d, k+1=2d-2h, a=d-2h, Phi=2d-3h.

The parameter hypotheses give h>=2 and 2h<=d-1, hence 4h<n. Also a>=1. Since p=gcd(n,h), we have p<=h. The subgroup hypothesis d in <h> is equivalent to p|d; we use only this divisibility.

Fix any radio-k labeling and subtract its minimum. Because k>=d, its labels are distinct. Order the vertices x_0,...,x_(2d-1) by increasing label, and write g_i=f(x_(i+1))-f(x_i). Every g_i>=a.

For any three vertices of a cycle, their three pairwise distances sum to at most n: put them in cyclic order and let the three intervening arc lengths be alpha,beta,gamma. They sum to n, and each of the corresponding shortest distances is at most its arc length. Applying the radio inequalities to x_i,x_(i+1),x_(i+2), and adding, gives

2(g_i+g_(i+1)) >= 3(k+1)-n = 2Phi.

Thus g_i+g_(i+1)>=Phi. This also proves span>=LB by grouping the gaps into d-1 consecutive pairs and one last gap.

If p=1, span>=LB is already the desired lower bound. Suppose p>=2, and assume for contradiction that the excess

s=span-LB

satisfies 0<=s<=p-2. In particular s<h.

For 0<=t<d put v_t=x_(2t), w_t=x_(2t+1), and

delta_t=g_(2t)-a>=0.

For 0<=t<d-1 put

epsilon_t=g_(2t)+g_(2t+1)-Phi>=0,
eta_t=g_(2t+1)+g_(2t+2)-Phi>=0.

Let E=sum epsilon_t. Directly from these definitions,

s=E+delta_(d-1),
eta_t=epsilon_t+delta_(t+1)-delta_t.                          (1)

Since eta_t>=0, backward summation gives

delta_t <= delta_(d-1)+sum_(u=t)^(d-2)epsilon_u <= s.          (2)

Moreover sum eta_t=E+delta_(d-1)-delta_0=s-delta_0, so every eta_t<=s. Every epsilon_t<=s as well. An estimate needed below is

epsilon_t+delta_(t+1)<=s.                                    (3)

It follows from (2) at t+1, retaining the epsilon_t term.

### Near-antipodal pairs and short two-step displacements

The radio inequality for v_t,w_t gives D(v_t,w_t)>=d-delta_t. Therefore there is an integer e_t, |e_t|<=delta_t, such that

w_t = v_t+d+e_t (mod n).                                     (4)

Since delta_t<=s<h<d, the representative e_t is unambiguous.

The radio inequality for v_t,v_(t+1) gives

D(v_t,v_(t+1))>=k+1-(Phi+epsilon_t)=h-epsilon_t.

On the other hand g_(2t+1)=d-h+epsilon_t-delta_t, so the inequality for w_t,v_(t+1) gives

D(w_t,v_(t+1))>=d-h-epsilon_t+delta_t.

The antipodal identity D(y+d,z)=d-D(y,z) on C_(2d) follows by choosing a separation r in [0,2d]: min(r,2d-r)+|d-r|=d. Together with (4), it implies

D(v_t+e_t,v_(t+1))<=h+epsilon_t-delta_t.

The triangle inequality and |e_t|<=delta_t now give the upper bound. Thus

h-epsilon_t <= D(v_t,v_(t+1)) <= h+epsilon_t.                 (5)

Similarly, the label difference from w_t to w_(t+1) is Phi+eta_t, and

g_(2t+1)=d-h+eta_t-delta_(t+1).

The radio inequality for w_t,v_(t+1) therefore gives D(w_t,v_(t+1))>=d-h-eta_t+delta_(t+1). By |e_(t+1)|<=delta_(t+1) and the triangle inequality,

D(w_t,v_(t+1)+e_(t+1))>=d-h-eta_t.

Since w_(t+1)=v_(t+1)+e_(t+1)+d modulo n, the antipodal identity gives D(w_t,w_(t+1))<=h+eta_t. The label difference Phi+eta_t gives the matching lower bound. Thus

h-eta_t <= D(w_t,w_(t+1)) <= h+eta_t.                         (6)

### The two tracks choose the same direction

Because epsilon_t,eta_t<=s<h and h+s<2h<d, the signed shortest displacements q_t from v_t to v_(t+1), and r_t from w_t to w_(t+1), are nonzero and have absolute value strictly less than d. By (5)–(6) write

q_t=sigma_t h+u_t,       |u_t|<=epsilon_t,
r_t=tau_t h+b_t,         |b_t|<=eta_t,

where sigma_t,tau_t belong to {-1,1}.

Equation (4) at t and t+1 implies

r_t-q_t-e_(t+1)+e_t = 0 (mod n).

The absolute value of its left side is at most

2h+epsilon_t+eta_t+delta_t+delta_(t+1)
=2h+2(epsilon_t+delta_(t+1))
<=2h+2s<4h<n.                                                (7)

Here we used (1) and (3). Consequently the displayed congruence is equality over the integers. If tau_t and sigma_t were different, that equality would imply

2h <= |b_t|+|u_t|+|e_(t+1)|+|e_t|
<=epsilon_t+eta_t+delta_(t+1)+delta_t
=2(epsilon_t+delta_(t+1))<=2s<2h,

a contradiction. Therefore tau_t=sigma_t, and the integer equality reduces to

b_t-u_t=e_(t+1)-e_t.                                        (8)

The common sign may vary with t; no globally fixed direction is assumed. This within-step direction agreement is the additional ingredient beyond the source's zero-slack coset argument.

### Integer lifts and their range

Define integer sequences V_t,W_t by

V_0=0, W_0=e_0,
V_(t+1)=V_t+u_t, W_(t+1)=W_t+b_t.

By (8), W_t-V_t=e_t for every t. Since p divides h and d, the congruences defining q_t,r_t and (4) show inductively that

V_t = v_t-v_0 (mod p),
W_t = w_t-v_0 (mod p).                                      (9)

Form a single integer path by following V backwards from V_(d-1) to V_0, then taking the segment from V_0 to W_0, then following W forward to W_(d-1). Its total variation is at most

sum |u_t|+|e_0|+sum |b_t|
<=E+delta_0+sum eta_t
=2E+delta_(d-1).                                            (10)

Its endpoints differ by |e_(d-1)|<=delta_(d-1). By (P), its range R, which is exactly the range of all V_t and W_t, therefore satisfies

2R <= 2E+2delta_(d-1)=2s,

so R<=s. All the lifts are integers in an interval containing at most s+1<=p-1 integers.

But v_0,w_0,...,v_(d-1),w_(d-1) run through every vertex of Z/nZ. Because p|n, their residues modulo p run through all p residue classes. By (9), the same is true of the lifts. An interval with at most p-1 integers cannot represent p distinct residue classes. This is the required contradiction.

Hence every labeling has span>=LB+p-1.

## Matching upper bound, with complete written verification

The matching construction is already Theorem 11 of the source. We give the complete written verification so that the mathematical proof does not invoke Theorem 11 as a black box. The verification is formalized in `RadioConstructionArithmetic`, `RadioConstructionPermutation`, `RadioConstructionDistances`, `RadioConstructionLabels`, and `RadioSharpUpper`.

Write d=pq, h=pr. Since gcd(n,h)=p and n=2pq, gcd(2q,r)=1. In particular r is odd, h has order 2q modulo n, and qh=dr=d (mod n). For 0<=t<d define

B_t=floor(t/q),
v_t=-th-B_t (mod n), w_t=v_t+d (mod n),
F(v_t)=tPhi+B_t, F(w_t)=tPhi+B_t+a.                          (11)

These formulas give every vertex exactly once. Indeed write t=ell q+j with 0<=ell<p and 0<=j<q. Both v_t and w_t have residue -ell modulo p, so different ell blocks cannot overlap. In a fixed block put c=-ell qh-ell. Its vertices are

c-jh and c-jh+d=c+(q-j)h,  0<=j<q.

The exponents -j and q-j together run through 2q consecutive integers, hence all exponents modulo 2q. Because h has order 2q, all 2q vertices in this block are distinct. There are p disjoint blocks of size 2q, as required.

For consecutive t, write m=B_(t+1)-B_t, which is 0 or 1. Put b=d-h=Phi-a. In the order v_0,w_0,v_1,w_1,...,v_(d-1),w_(d-1), the consecutive label gaps are a and b+m, all positive. It remains to verify the radio inequalities.

- Within one pair, D(v_t,w_t)=d and the label gap is a=k+1-d, so the inequality is equality.
- Between two consecutive pairs, the same-track distances are D(v_t,v_(t+1))=D(w_t,w_(t+1))=h+m. This is indeed the shortest distance because h+1<d. Their label gaps are Phi+m, and
  k+1-(h+m)=Phi-m<=Phi+m.
- For the pair w_t,v_(t+1), the distance is d-h-m=b-m, and the label gap is b+m. The required gap is
  k+1-(b-m)=b+m,
  so equality holds.
- For v_t,w_(t+1), the distance is again b-m, while the label gap is Phi+a+m=b+2a+m>=b+m. Thus the inequality holds as well.

The distance b-m is positive and at most d: h>=2 and 2h<=d-1 imply d-h-1>0. Finally consider pairs whose pair indices differ by j>=2. The smallest possible label difference between such pairs is

jPhi+(B_(t+j)-B_t)-a>=2Phi-a
=3d-4h=(k+1)+a>=k+1.

Since a cycle distance is nonnegative, all these radio inequalities follow immediately. These cases exhaust every pair of distinct vertices. Thus (11) defines a radio-k labeling.

Its minimum label is zero and its maximum label is

F(w_(d-1))=(d-1)Phi+B_(d-1)+a
=(d-1)Phi+(p-1)+a=LB+p-1,

because d=pq implies B_(d-1)=p-1. This proves the upper bound. Together with the new lower bound it proves the entire literal conjecture. QED.
