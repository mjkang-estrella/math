## Problem statement as recorded on MathDB

Let $n$ be even and $k$ be odd with

$$
\frac{n}{2} \leqslant k < n-3.
$$

Set

$$
h=\frac{n-k-1}{2},\qquad p=\gcd(n,h).
$$

Write $\langle h\rangle$ for the subgroup generated by $h$ in the relevant cyclic group. **Sharpness conjecture.** If $n/2\in\langle h\rangle$, then

$$
\operatorname{rn}_k(C_n)=\operatorname{LB}(n,k)+p-1.
$$

The paper notes that the values of the radio $k$-number are known for most parameters in the stated range, but cases with different parities remain open. The conjecture asserts that the upper bound from the preceding theorem is sharp whenever $n/2\in\langle h\rangle$.

## Summary of the result

For every even n and odd k with n/2 <= k < n-3, with h = (n-k-1)/2 and p = gcd(n,h), if n/2 lies in the subgroup of Z_n generated by h then the radio-k-number of the cycle C_n equals LB(n,k) + p - 1. This is Conjecture 1 of Bloomfield, Liu and Ramirez. The upper bound is the source's Theorem 11; the new part is the matching lower bound, where the source proves only LB + ceil(p/2) - 1. The proof makes the source's gap lemma quantitative, maps vertices to residues modulo p, and rules out the case where the resulting closed walk wraps around, which is exactly the factor of two the source left open. Lean states the conjecture literally: the graph distance of Mathlib's cycle graph, the radio inequality over the integers, the radio-k-number as a minimum span, and LB proved equal to the source's formula.
