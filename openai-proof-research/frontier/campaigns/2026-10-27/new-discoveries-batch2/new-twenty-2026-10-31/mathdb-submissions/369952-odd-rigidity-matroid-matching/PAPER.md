# Matroid matching in odd-dimensional generic rigidity matroids

## Abstract

For every odd dimension $d\ge 5$ and every $n\ge 2(d+2)^2+2(d+2)-1$, we construct a family of $h$ two-edge sets in the complete graph whose maximum rigidity matroid matching has size $h-2$, while the minimum in the Dress–Lovász bound is $h-1$. This proves Conjecture 3.20 of arXiv:2508.18838v1 and Conjecture 3.22 of arXiv:2508.18838v2. The construction and both extrema also hold for every real placement with algebraically independent coordinates.

## 1. Statement and upper bound

Let $E$ be the set of unordered distinct-vertex pairs of $K_n$. Use the field $F=\mathbb{Q}(p_{v,a}: v\in V,1\le a\le d)$ with independent indeterminates. The rigidity row $R_{uv}$ has the vector $p_u-p_v$ in block $u$, its negative in block $v$, and zero elsewhere. The rank $r(S)$ of $S\subseteq E$ is the dimension of the span of those rows. For any real placement whose coordinates are algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$, all these ranks agree with their real counterparts. Indeed, each minor is a polynomial with rational coefficients, and specialization is injective on the polynomial ring. Such placements exist for each finite $n,d$. At each step the field generated by the finitely many preceding choices is countable, and the reals algebraic over it are countable. Choosing a real transcendental over that field extends the algebraically independent list.

A pair family $\mathcal H$ is a set of two-element subsets of $E$. Its members may overlap. A subfamily $\mathcal J$ is a matching when $r(\bigcup\mathcal J)=2|\mathcal J|$. The equality implies that the selected pairs are disjoint and their union is independent, by $r(\bigcup\mathcal J)\le |\bigcup\mathcal J|\le 2|\mathcal J|$. For a flat $Z$ of the complete-graph matroid and a partition $\pi$ of $\mathcal H$ into nonempty blocks, put $U_B=\bigcup_{P\in B}P$ and
$$
  A(Z,\pi)=r(Z)+\sum_{B\in\pi}
  \left\lfloor\frac{r(Z\cup U_B)-r(Z)}2\right\rfloor .
$$
A flat contains exactly the edges whose rows are in its span. The matching property asks for equality of the maximum matching size and the minimum of $A(Z,\pi)$ for every pair family.

We use the general upper bound $|\mathcal J|\le A(Z,\pi)$. For a matching $\mathcal J$, let $W_B$ be the span of its selected rows in block $B$, and let $L_Z$ be the span of $Z$. The subspaces $W_B$ form a direct sum because all selected rows are independent. Set $a_B=\dim(W_B\cap L_Z)$. Then $\sum_B a_B\le r(Z)$, and the rank increment for $U_B$ is at least $2|\mathcal J\cap B|-a_B$, the dimension of the image of $W_B$ in the quotient by $L_Z$. Its half-floor is at least $|\mathcal J\cap B|-\lceil a_B/2\rceil\ge |\mathcal J\cap B|-a_B$. Summing proves the bound, including for overlapping families. It holds for any $Z\subseteq E$.

## 2. Bipartite stresses and same-side edges

Write $s=d+2$ and let $t$ be $s$ or $s+1$. In a copy of $K_{s,t}$ write $X_i=(1,p_{x_i})$ and $Y_j=(1,p_{y_j})$. Every $d+1$ augmented positions in either part are independent, by specializing the corresponding points to $0,e_1,\ldots,e_d$ to exhibit a nonzero determinant.

We also need independence of the augmented outer products $XX^{\mathsf T}$ over all $s+t$ vertices. The space of polynomials of degree at most two in $d$ variables has dimension $Q=(d+1)(d+2)/2$. At the $Q$ points $0,e_a,2e_a,e_a+e_b$ for $a<b$, the evaluations are independent. The first gives the constant, the two axis values determine each linear and square term, and the sums determine each mixed term. Any subset of rows of this evaluation matrix is independent. Since $s+t\le 2d+5\le Q-1$ for $d\ge 5$, specialization proves independence of evaluation at our generic points. Via the pairing of quadratics with augmented outer products, this is the asserted outer-product independence.

Let $\mu_{ij}$ be coefficients of a row dependence and write $a_i=\sum_j\mu_{ij}$ and $b_j=\sum_i\mu_{ij}$. Equilibrium at the left and right vertices is equivalent to $a_iX_i=\sum_j\mu_{ij}Y_j$ and $b_jY_j=\sum_i\mu_{ij}X_i$. Multiplying by $X_i^{\mathsf T}$ and $Y_j^{\mathsf T}$ on the appropriate sides and summing gives
$$
\sum_i a_i X_iX_i^{\mathsf T}
=\sum_{i,j}\mu_{ij}X_iY_j^{\mathsf T}
=\sum_j b_jY_jY_j^{\mathsf T}.
$$
Thus every $a_i$ and $b_j$ is zero. The left affine-dependence space is one-dimensional, generated by a vector $\lambda$ with all entries nonzero, since deleting any one vertex leaves $d+1$ independent augmented positions. The right-vertex equations now show each column is a multiple of $\lambda$. The left-vertex equations then give exactly
$$
 \mu_{ij}=\lambda_i c_j,\qquad \sum_j c_jY_j=0.
$$
Conversely these formulas imply equilibrium. For $t=s$ the stress space is one-dimensional with full support, so the edge set is a circuit. For $t=s+1$ it is two-dimensional, each $c_j$ is a nonzero functional, and setting two different $c_j$ to zero forces the zero stress. In fact, deleting one augmented position on the right leaves rank $d+1$ and a one-dimensional dependence space, while deleting two leaves an independent set.

A same-side edge of $K_{s,s}$ lies outside the span of its bipartite rows. For such an edge $uv$, consider the $2s$ quadratic evaluations at the vertices and add the functional $q\mapsto(p_u-p_v)^{\mathsf T}A(p_u-p_v)$, where $q(x)=x^{\mathsf T}Ax+b^{\mathsf T}x+c$ and $A$ is symmetric. Specialize $p_u=e_1$, $p_v=e_2$, and use distinct points of the preceding interpolation set other than $e_1+e_2$ for the others. The evaluations stay independent and vanish on $x_1x_2$, on which the extra functional is nonzero. Hence the augmented rank holds generically. There is therefore a quadratic $q$ vanishing at every vertex and having nonzero extra functional. Give the two sides velocities $Ap+b/2$ and $-(Ap+b/2)$ respectively. Each bipartite row evaluates to a difference of $q$ at its two endpoints, hence vanishes. The row of $uv$ evaluates, up to sign, to the nonzero extra functional.

## 3. The witness and its ranks

Start with a base $B=K_{s,s+1}$, with vertices $x_0,\ldots,x_{s-1}$ and $y_0,\ldots,y_s$. For each $j$ attach a fresh $A_j=K_{s,s}$ whose left and right vertices with local index zero are respectively $x_0$ and $y_j$. All positive-index local vertices are fresh and distinct from all other pieces. Remove the seam $e_j=x_0y_j$ from the union. Let $S_j=E(A_j)\setminus\{e_j\}$. The retained ground $D$ of the construction has $m=s+1$ classes $D_j$, each consisting of $S_j$ and the retained base edges $x_i y_j$ for $i>0$. Each class has size $L=s^2+s-2$, and the vertex count is
$$
 s+(s+1)+2(s+1)(s-1)=2s^2+2s-1.
$$
Embed these vertices injectively in $K_n$. All ranks in what follows refer to the full $E$.

In class $j$, pair base edge $x_i y_j$ for $1\le i<s$ with the local edge $(i,0)$. For each $0\le i<s$, pair the local edges $(i,2r+1)$ and $(i,2r+2)$ for $0\le r<(s-1)/2$. The odd dimension makes $s-1$ even. These rules partition $D$ into a family $\mathcal H$ of $h=mL/2$ pairs.

For a linear combination of rigidity rows the forces $f_v$ in the vertex blocks satisfy $\sum_v f_v=0$ and $\sum_v p_v\wedge f_v=0$. Both identities hold on each row, by cancellation and alternation. If the forces vanish except at two vertices $u,v$, then $f_v=-f_u$ and $(p_u-p_v)\wedge f_u=0$. Since $p_u-p_v\ne 0$, the combination is a scalar multiple of $R_{uv}$.

Each $A_j$ is a circuit. Thus $S_j$ is independent and the unique expansion of $R_{e_j}$ in its rows has all coefficients $\beta_{j,g}\ne0$. In a dependence of $D$, the internal vertex forces of $S_j$ vanish, so its combination is $z_jR_{e_j}$. Compressing all pieces gives a base stress whose seam coefficients are $z_j$. Conversely, expanding the seams in a base stress gives a stress on $D$ and recovers the original coefficients under compression. The class $j$ coefficients are $\lambda_i c_j$ on retained base edges and $\beta_{j,g}\lambda_0 c_j$ on gadget edges. All multipliers are nonzero.

In particular the stress space of $D$ has dimension two. The space of stresses supported after a deletion has dimension two for the empty deletion, one for a nonempty deletion contained in one class, and zero for a deletion meeting two classes. These dimensions give the ranks by subtracting the dimension from the number of retained edges. The circuits of $D$ are precisely the supports $D\setminus D_j$, because a nonzero stress can vanish on at most one class and each single class can be made to vanish. Their common size is $C=(m-1)L$. Keeping $h$ or $h-1$ pairs leaves a dependent union, while deleting a pair in each of two classes gives an independent union. Hence the matching maximum is attained at $h-2$.

## 4. Separation in the complete ambient graph

For a representation of a row in the span of $D$, divide each coefficient by its nonzero class multiplier from Section 3. Changing representation adds a stress, shifting all normalized coefficients in class $j$ by the same value $c_j$. For a pair $P$ in that class, equality of its two normalized coefficients is thus independent of representation. The row belongs to the span of $D\setminus P$ exactly when these coefficients agree. One implication uses a representation zero on $P$. For the other, subtract a stress with the prescribed common value of $c_j$, which exists since that functional is nonzero.

We show that for every $e\in E\setminus D$ there is a pair $P$ with $R_e\notin\operatorname{span}(D\setminus P)$. An edge touching a vertex outside the construction has a nonzero block at such a vertex and lies outside the span of $D$. The remaining edges are covered by the following cases.

**Two base endpoints.** Suppose a representation on $D$ is consistent on every pair. Each gadget compresses to $z_j R_{e_j}$, since its internal force is zero. Its unique expansion on $S_j$ gives normalized coefficient $z_j/\lambda_0$ on every gadget edge. Each base edge has a gadget partner, so its coefficient is $\lambda_i z_j/\lambda_0$. With seams restored the full column has coefficients $\lambda_i t_j$ with $t_j=z_j/\lambda_0$. This combination gives zero force at every $y_j$ by the affine dependence of the $X_i$. At $x_i$ its force is $\lambda_i(Ap_{x_i}-b)$ with $A=\sum_j t_j$ and $b=\sum_j t_j p_{y_j}$. An edge involving any $y_j$, including a deleted seam, has a nonzero force there. For an edge with two left endpoints there are at least two other left vertices, with distinct positions. Their zero forces imply $A=0$ and $b=0$, making the entire force zero. Both possibilities contradict representation of $R_e$.

**One internal endpoint in $A_j$ and the other outside $A_j$.** Let $u=p_{x_0}$, $v=p_{y_j}$ and $w=v-u$. Project both endpoint positions minus $u$ orthogonally to $w^\perp$, getting $a$ and $b$. The denominator $w^{\mathsf T}w$ and the Gram determinant $(a^{\mathsf T}a)(b^{\mathsf T}b)-(a^{\mathsf T}b)^2$ are nonzero in $F$. Specializing the four distinct positions to $0,e_1,e_2,e_3$ gives denominator and determinant equal to one. On $A_j$ give a vertex at $p$ velocity $(ab^{\mathsf T}-ba^{\mathsf T})(p-u)$ and give all other vertices zero velocity. The map is skew and kills $w$, so it vanishes at both seam vertices. Consequently every row of $D$ evaluates to zero. The row of $e$ evaluates to the Gram determinant up to sign, using the projected endpoints and the zero velocity at the outside endpoint. It lies outside the span of $D$.

**An internal endpoint and both endpoints in the same $A_j$.** A missing edge with these endpoints is same-side. If its row were represented on $D$, the contribution from $D\setminus S_j$ would have zero forces off the seam. It has no internal support in $A_j$, and outside $A_j$ it equals the row of $e$ minus the contribution from $S_j$, which is zero there. The two-vertex force identity makes the contribution a seam multiple. This puts the same-side row in the span of $E(A_j)$, contradicting Section 2. These cases establish separation for the entire ambient ground.

## 5. The minimum over all flats and partitions

Fix a flat $Z\subseteq E$ and a partition $\pi$ of $\mathcal H$.

If $e\in Z\setminus D$, choose $P$ with $R_e$ outside the span of $D\setminus P$. Choose $Q=\{f,g\}$ in a different class. Add the pair $\{e,f\}$ to the family and as a singleton block of the partition. Its increment above $r(Z)$ is at most one because $e\in Z$, so the half-floor is zero. The augmented family has a matching of size $h-1$ consisting of all original pairs except $P,Q$ and the new pair. Its union is $(D\setminus(P\cup\{g\}))\cup\{e\}$. The old rows here are independent by the two classes hit, and $e$ is independent of them by separation. The upper bound from Section 1 applied to the augmented family yields $A(Z,\pi)\ge h-1$.

For $Z\subseteq D$, assume $A(Z,\pi)\le h-2$. Nonnegative increments give $r(Z)\le h-2<C-1$, so $Z$ is independent since every circuit has rank $C-1$. Put $z=|Z|$ and $t_B=|Z\cap U_B|$. The $U_B$ partition $D$ and each has even size, so $\sum_B t_B=z$. Consider three cases.

- If every $Z\cup U_B$ is independent, the half-floors sum to $h-\sum_B\lceil t_B/2\rceil$. Consequently $A(Z,\pi)\ge h$.
- If exactly one is dependent, its nullity is at most two by Section 3. Its half-floor decreases by at most one from the independent cardinality calculation, giving $A(Z,\pi)\ge h-1$.
- If at least two are dependent, each of those two contains a circuit and has rank at least $C-1$. Their two increments give $A(Z,\pi)\ge z+2\lfloor(C-1-z)/2\rfloor\ge C-2\ge h-1$. The last inequality follows from $C=(m-1)L$, $h=mL/2$, $m\ge8$ and $L\ge2$.

All three cases contradict the assumption. Finally, every edge of the complete graph has a nonzero row, so the empty set is a flat. With this flat and the partition into one block, $A$ equals $\lfloor r(D)/2\rfloor=\lfloor(2h-2)/2\rfloor=h-1$. Thus the minimum is attained and strictly exceeds the matching maximum. Taking $d=2k+1$ gives, for every $k\ge2$, the threshold $N(k)=2(2k+3)^2+2(2k+3)-1$ for all $n\ge N(k)$. This supplies a sufficient threshold. The rank agreement in Section 1 gives the same family and extrema for every algebraically independent real placement.
