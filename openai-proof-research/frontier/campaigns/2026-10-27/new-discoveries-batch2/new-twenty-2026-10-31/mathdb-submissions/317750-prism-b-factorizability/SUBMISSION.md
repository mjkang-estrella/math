## Problem statement as recorded on MathDB

For a regular graph $G=(V,E)$, call $G$ **B-factorizable** if every saturated monoid element with edge component equal to the graph's characteristic vector belongs to the monoid generated by compatible perfect matchings. A $k$-gonal prism is the graph formed by two $k$-cycles together with corresponding matching edges.

**Prism B-factorizability conjecture.** The graph of every $k$-gonal prism is B-factorizable.

The source reports verification for the 3-dimensional cube and the pentagonal prism by Hilbert-basis calculations, but does not establish the claim for every $k$.

## Summary of the result

The conjecture holds for every k ≥ 3: whenever a home-away pattern table for the k-gonal prism (a round-robin style schedule in which only the pairs of teams joined by a prism edge play) admits a fractional compatible schedule, it admits an integral one. The proof starts from Kashiwabara's own Corollary 9, which reduces the question to finding a compatible proper 3-edge-colouring, and then uses three prism-specific steps: the differences along the two rails alternate, the one-turn digraph of the pattern can be spliced into cycles with the right cut parity, and the exclusions on the rungs propagate around the prism to give the colouring. Kashiwabara had verified the cube (k = 4) and the pentagonal prism (k = 5) by Hilbert-basis computation. Lean states Definition 4 of the source literally (the monoid generated by compatible perfect matchings, its saturation, B-factorizability) and proves it for prism k for all k ≥ 3, together with the equivalent statement that the feasible polytope has an integral point when it is non-empty.
