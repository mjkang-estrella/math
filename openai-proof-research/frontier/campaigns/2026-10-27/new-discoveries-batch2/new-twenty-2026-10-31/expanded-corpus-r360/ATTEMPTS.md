## 353335: full character-sum divisibility, then public-proof exclusion

Exact endpoint: for every finite group Gamma, y of order divisible by 3 for which S_y^2 exists, and every irreducible complex character chi, T_y(chi)/3 is an integer. In v2 (29 March 2022), Section 5 defines S_y^2 as the smallest skew-symmetric subset containing y and closed under conjugacy and the relation consisting of relatively prime powers congruent to 1 modulo 3. Conjecture 5.4 is exactly the reserved assertion. In v3 (16 February 2023), the equivalent smallest closure is S_y^3, defined after Lemma 5.7 for tolerable y. Existence of the former closure is equivalent to skew-symmetry of the union of the conjugates of the power class, which is exactly tolerability by Lemma 5.7. Thus the change of superscript changes no objects or quantifiers. Lemmas 5.14 and 5.15 explicitly prove divisibility, splitting ord(y)=3^t m into t=1 and t>=2. This is a public source resolution, not a new theorem.

An independent complete classical reduction gives the same endpoint. Put S=S_y^2, d=chi(1), and a=d^{-1} sum_{s in S} chi(s). Let N be the exponent of Gamma, so 3 divides N and character values belong to K=Q(zeta_N). Conjugacy invariance makes z=sum_{s in S}s central in C[Gamma]. Schur's lemma says its action on the irreducible representation is the scalar a. This scalar is an eigenvalue of the integer matrix of multiplication by z on the regular representation, hence an algebraic integer. Equivalently one can use the monic characteristic polynomial of that multiplication operator; the irreducible representation occurs in the regular representation.

For any unit u modulo N with u=1 mod 3, sigma_u(chi(s))=chi(s^u), because the eigenvalues of a finite-order representation matrix are N-th roots of unity. The map s -> s^u permutes S: closure gives containment, and a unit inverse modulo N again equals 1 mod 3 and gives the inverse map. Thus sigma_u(a)=a. The fixed field of these automorphisms is Q(omega), omega=exp(2 pi i/3). Therefore a is an algebraic integer in Q(omega), so a=A+B omega with integers A,B. For completeness, write a=A+B omega first with rational A,B. Its trace 2A-B and norm A^2-AB+B^2 are integers. Their discriminant is -3B^2, so writing B=p/q in lowest terms forces q^2 to divide 3 and hence q=1. Now 2A is integral, and the norm excludes odd numerator for A with denominator 2, so A is integral as well. Finally chi(s^{-1}) is the complex conjugate of chi(s), whence T=i sqrt(3)(a-conj(a))=-3B. This proves T/3 integral for every j, with no enumeration or restriction on d.

Failed shortcut: integrality of T alone does not imply divisibility by 3. Reducing traces modulo 3 before dividing by d also fails when 3 divides d. The central algebraic-integer eigenvalue and its quadratic fixed field remove both problems. This proof depends only on standard finite-group representation and cyclotomic-field facts; no missing new mathematical lemma remains. The novelty gate fails independently because the precise claim is already proved in v3. No dormant implementation request is warranted.

## 353689: arbitrary abelian vertex groups — necessity and sufficiency separated

Definitions read: a finite simplicial graph; graph product is the free product of its nontrivial vertex groups modulo edge commutators; IA is the kernel of Aut(G)->Aut(G^ab); semicomplete means IA=Inn. A separating star means that the induced complement of st(v) has at least two components. The nontriviality convention is explicit in the primary introduction. The reserved text omits it.

Necessity in the intended convention is source Lemma 2.13 and remains valid without finite generation. Choose nonidentity x in G_v, one complement component C, and distinct vertices a in C, b in another component. Partial conjugation by x on the factors in C preserves each defining edge relation, and its inverse uses x^{-1}. It acts trivially on abelianization. It is not inner: retract to the graph product on {v,a,b}, a free product of three nontrivial groups, by killing all other factors. A hypothetical conjugator there fixes every element in G_v and G_b; free-product reduced normal forms imply that the centralizers of nonidentity elements of these two distinct factors have trivial intersection. That conjugator is therefore 1. But it would conjugate nonidentity elements of G_a by x, which has a reduced word of length three rather than one. Contradiction. Retraction commutes with conjugation; this argument uses no assumption that an automorphism descends to a quotient. Only the images of a hypothetical global conjugation are retracted.

Sufficiency attempt 1: follow Theorem D. For finitely generated abelian factors, split each into a finite clique of cyclic factors of infinite or prime-power order. Lemma 2.17 preserves the separating-star condition. The source imports [SS21, Theorem 5.1], generation of IA by partial conjugations and commutator transvections. No separating star makes the former inner; Proposition 2.9 gives no SIL; Lemma 2.19 excludes the latter. This closes the finitely generated case, already public. It does not close arbitrary factors: arbitrary abelian groups need not be direct sums of cyclic groups, and an infinite splitting does not supply the same finite-graph automorphism-generation theorem.

Sufficiency attempt 2: restrict a hypothetical IA automorphism to finitely generated subgroups of the factors and invoke the finite case. Each finite set of image words involves finitely many syllables, but closing a finite subgroup system under both the automorphism and its inverse can require infinitely many stages. There is no proved invariant finitely generated graph-product subgroup. Without invariance the restriction is not an automorphism, so Theorem D is unavailable. Nor does a collection of local conjugators prove existence of one common conjugator on the entire group: a compatibility or rigidity lemma is missing. The finite number of vertices does not bound the number of generators in each vertex group.

Literal correction: take three isolated vertices, all labeled by the trivial abelian group. Every star complement has two isolated vertices and is disconnected. The graph product is trivial, so Aut, IA and Inn are trivial and the product is semicomplete. This refutes the unrestricted literal wording if trivial factors are permitted. Removing trivial factors is the explicit original convention; this correction earns zero credit and does not solve the intended arbitrary-nontrivial-abelian conjecture.

Missing full-scope lemma: an IA-generation/rigidity theorem for finite graph products of arbitrary nontrivial abelian groups, sufficient to force global conjugation when there is no separating star. No proof of that lemma is supplied. The journal abstract, source v1 and v2 all retain finitely generated scope for the theorem. The arbitrary version remains HOLD, with no literature-absence claim and no Pi plan.

## 354588: Rayleigh SOS versus compatible determinantal factors

The source defines Delta_ij(f)=partial_i f partial_j f - f partial_i partial_j f, initially for i,j in [n]. Corollary 5.4 assumes total degree n<=m, multiaffinity in the first n variables, and top coefficient 1. Its criterion is one Hermitian square g_ij conjugate(g_ij), not an arbitrary sum of squares. Conjecture 5.5 instead asks about a power and arbitrary SOS. The NSF journal manuscript preserves that conjecture. The Oeding resolution mentioned in the introduction concerns symmetric principal-minor hyperdeterminantal equations, not Conjecture 5.5.

Constructive attempt: write each Delta as a sum of squares and interpret that sum as a norm. Realification of a single compatible Hermitian representation indeed gives a symmetric representation of f^2, via the real block matrix [Re M, -Im M; Im M, Re M], whose determinant is det(M) conjugate(det(M)). But separate SOS expressions need not be norms of single complex polynomials: a complex norm has the restricted form u^2+v^2. Even when each difference has this form, one must choose factors satisfying the source's resultant identities res_{x_k}(g_ij,f)=g_ik g_kj. The source constructs compatible factors under its stronger hypotheses. No simultaneous matrix-valued replacement or finite power satisfying these identities was constructed here. Multiplying independent norm certificates does not identify their determinant with a common power of f.

Counterexample attempt on the wording: n=1,m=2 and f=x(1+y^2). In the first-variable-only interpretation Delta_11=(1+y^2)^2 is SOS; for distinct pairs from [n] the test is empty. The coefficient of x as a monomial is 1, whereas as a polynomial coefficient in R[y][x] it is 1+y^2. Also Delta_xy=0 but Delta_yy=2x^2(y^2-1) is not SOS. Thus the claimed hypotheses depend materially on whether all i,j range over [m] or [n] and whether top coefficient means a scalar leading coefficient. This object is not promoted as an unambiguous exact counterexample.

The no-definite-pencil obstruction for this object is complete: suppose det M(x,y)=f(x,y)^q, q>0, for a real symmetric affine pencil, positive definite at (a,b). Then a!=0, and t -> det M(a,b+t) is a nonzero constant times det(I+t C), C real symmetric after congruence by the positive square root of M(a,b). Every root is real. But f(a,b+t)^q=a^q(1+(b+t)^2)^q is nonconstant with only nonreal roots. Contradiction. This supports a possible missing-hypothesis correction only; it does not refute the fully multiaffine, normalized, intended total-degree version. The broad SOS-to-power converse is not proved. No new credit or dormant Pi plan.
