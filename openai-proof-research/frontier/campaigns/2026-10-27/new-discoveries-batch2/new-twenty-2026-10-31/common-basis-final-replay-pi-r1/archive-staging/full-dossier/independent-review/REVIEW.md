## Exact mathematics

Put E={0,...,8}, x=1, y=6, r=4, k=2. Interpret [9,6,4,11,6,14,5,1,12] as four-coordinate binary column vectors. In `check.py` a four-subset is a base precisely when its determinant modulo two is one. The determinant is computed by the 24-term Leibniz formula (signs coincide modulo two), using integer division, multiplication/Boolean conjunction and parity. This uses neither Gaussian elimination nor sums/XORs of column combinations nor author routines.

All 126 four-subsets were evaluated. Exactly 76 are bases. They agree entry by entry with both `matroid-certificate.json` and `lean/Bases.lean`; `analyze.py` asserts this equality. For every ordered base pair A,B and every e in A\B the code independently finds f in B\A with A-e+f a base: 12,736 obligations, none failing. Nonempty equal-cardinality bases and this exchange axiom define a genuine rank-four matroid on E. The explicit determinant-nonzero base establishes rank four directly as well.

For z=1,6 define D_z={B base: z notin B and E\({z} union B) is a base}. The enumeration retains every member, not a sample: |D_1|=32 and |D_6|=24, with empty intersection. Each unordered partition is counted twice, giving 16 and 12 partitions. Nonvacuity is witnessed by

- E−1 = {0,2,3,5} disjoint-union {4,6,7,8};
- E−6 = {0,1,2,7} disjoint-union {3,4,5,8}.

If partitions share B, then B belongs to both D_1 and D_6, contradiction. This proves the full impossibility, not just incompatibility of the exhibited partitions. `independent-math.json` retains the bases, D lists, all circuits and component computation. All elements lie in bases (no loops), the circuit-connectivity computation yields one component, and the only two-circuit is {1,4}. Thus labels 1 and 4 are parallel, while x=1 and y=6 are distinct and are not that parallel pair. Connectedness is supplemental ordinary computation, not claimed as part of the Lean endpoint.

The families D_z themselves are not matroid base families. `nonmatroid-extendible-families.json` gives explicit failed base exchanges: in D_1, A={2,5,6,8}, B={3,5,7,8}, e=2; in D_6, A={0,4,7,8}, B={0,3,4,5}, e=8. Neither eligible replacement works. Accordingly one cannot just invoke the two-matroid intersection theorem on D_1,D_6. On F=E\{x,y}, the desired B must simultaneously be a base of M|F, ((M\x)* )\y, and ((M\y)* )\x (the latter restrictions have rank four here). This involves three base conditions, and the two-matroid common-base results inspected do not settle it. This observation is an explanation of a failed shortcut, not a proof of global non-reducibility.
