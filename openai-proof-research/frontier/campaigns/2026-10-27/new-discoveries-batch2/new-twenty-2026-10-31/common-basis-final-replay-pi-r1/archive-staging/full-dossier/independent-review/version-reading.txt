                                                      Degree bounds for the toric ideal of a matroid

                                                                                   Michal Lasoń

                                                            Institute of Mathematics of the Polish Academy of Sciences,
                                                                     ul.Śniadeckich 8, 00-656 Warszawa, Poland
                                                                                michalason@gmail.com


                                                    Abstract. Describing minimal generating set of a toric ideal, or the minimum
                                                    degree in which it is generated, is a well-studied and diﬃcult problem. In 1980
                                                    White conjectured that the toric ideal of a matroid is generated by quadratic
                                                    binomials corresponding to symmetric exchanges. In particular, that it is
                                                    generated in degree 2.
                                                         We prove that the toric ideal associated to a matroid of rank r is generated
                                                    in degree at most (r + 3)!. As a corollary we obtain that checking if White’s
                                                    conjecture is true for matroids of a ﬁxed rank is a decidable problem.

                                               Neil White made three conjectures of growing diﬃculty that describe generators
                                           of the ideal IM .
                                               Conjecture 1 (White 1980, [11]). The toric ideal of a matroid is generated
                                           in degree 2.

                                           is referred to [9] for background of matroid theory, and to [7] for other exchange
                                           properties). That is, for every bases B1 , B2 and e ∈ B1 \B2 there exists f ∈ B2 \B1 ,

                                           case we say that the quadratic binomial yB1 yB2 − yB1′ yB2′ corresponds to symmetric
                                           exchange. It is clear that such binomials belong to the ideal IM .

    Conjecture 2 (White 1980, [11]). The toric ideal of a matroid is generated
by quadratic binomials corresponding to symmetric exchanges.
    The third and the strongest among White’s conjectures describing generators
of the ideal IM turned out to be equivalent to Conjecture 2 (see the discussion in
Section 4 of [6]).
    The original conjectures due to White were expressed in the combinatorial
language. Conjecture 1 asserts that if two multisets of bases of a matroid have
equal union (as a multiset), then one can pass between them by a sequence of
steps, in each step exchanging two bases for another two bases of the same union
(as a multiset). In Conjecture 2 additionally each step corresponds to a symmetric
exchange. Therefore White’s conjectures do not depend on the ﬁeld K.
    Conjectures 1 and 2 are known to be true for many special classes of matroids:
graphic matroids [1], strongly base orderable matroids [6] (so also for transversal
matroids), sparse paving matroids [2], and for matroids of rank at most 3 [5] (see
also other related papers [3, 4, 10]).
    The ﬁrst result valid for arbitrary matroids was the conﬁrmation of White’s
conjecture up to saturation. Let JM be the ideal generated by quadratic binomials
corresponding to symmetric exchanges. Clearly, JM ⊂ IM and Conjecture 2 asserts
that the ideals JM and IM are equal. Recall that two homogeneous ideals are equal
up saturation with respect to the ideal generated by all variables if and only if they
are equal starting from some degree.
    Theorem 3 (Lasoń, Michalek, [6]). The toric ideal of a matroid and the ideal
generated by quadratic binomials corresponding to symmetric exchanges are equal
starting from some degree.
     Here we prove another result towards White’s conjecture which is valid for
arbitrary matroids.
     Consider a single matroid M , then by Hilbert’s basis theorem the ideal IM is
ﬁnitely generated. In particular it is generated in some degree, however so far we
did not have any eﬀective bound. If we ﬁx the size of the ground set, then there
are only ﬁnitely many matroids on it. Hence in this case there is a common bound
on degree in which the corresponding toric ideals are generated. But, when we ﬁx
only the rank, then the number of matroids of that rank is inﬁnite. We prove that
in this case there is also a common bound on degree in which the corresponding
toric ideals are generated. Our bound is explicit.
    Theorem 4. The toric ideal associated to a matroid of rank r is generated in
degree at most (r + 3)!.
    In the combinatorial language the meaning of Theorems 3 and 4 is the following.
Let M be a matroid of rank r. Let A, B be two multisets of k bases of M with equal
union (as a multiset). Theorem 3 asserts that if k ≥ f (M ) (for some function f ),
then one can pass between A and B by a sequence of steps, in each step exchanging
two bases for two bases created by a symmetric exchange. While Theorem 3 asserts
that if k ≥ (r + 3)!, then one can pass between A and B by a sequence of steps,
in each step exchanging (r + 3)! bases for another (r + 3)! bases of the same union
(as a multiset). Thus in Theorem 4 the steps between multisets of bases are less
precise (consist of exchanging (r + 3)! instead of 2 bases), but the lower bound on
k (the size of multisets of bases) is better ((r + 3)! instead of f (M )).

    Corollary 5. Checking if Conjecture 2 is true for matroids of a fixed rank is
a decidable problem (it is enough to check connectivity of a finite number of graphs).
     Equality of saturations of the ideals JM and IM from Theorem 3 implies equal-
ity of their radicals. Since the ideal IM is prime, it is also radical. Thus the radical
of JM equals to IM . In other words, for every element a of the toric ideal of a
matroid there exists a power n, such that an is generated by quadratic binomials
corresponding to symmetric exchanges. We can make this statement more explicit.
    Corollary 6. Suppose the characteristic of the field K is 2. Then for every
matroid of rank r, the 2r(r + 3)!-th power of every element of its toric ideal is
generated by quadratic binomials corresponding to symmetric exchanges.

                                     2. Preliminaries
     Blasiak [1] proposed a very nice and simple translation of the problem of gen-
erating the ideal IM to the problem of connectivity of some graphs naturally con-
structed from matroids. We are going to use this approach.
     Suppose M is a matroid whose ground set can be partitioned into k (for k ≥ 3)
disjoint bases (so the ground set is of size r(E)k). We call such a matroid a k-
matroid, and deﬁne for it a k-base graph denoted by Bk (M ). Vertices of Bk (M )
correspond to sets of k pairwise disjoint bases of M (partitions into bases of the
ground set). An edge joins vertices with nonempty intersection. That is, sets of
bases {B1 , . . . , Bk } and {B1′ , . . . , Bk′ } are connected if for some i, j equality Bi = Bj′
holds. A simple corollary of the proof of Proposition 2.1 from [1] gives the following.
    Proposition 7 (Blasiak, [1]). Let C be a class of matroids that is closed under
deletions (that do not lower the rank of a matroid) and adding parallel elements.
Suppose that for every k > n and for every k-matroid M in C the k-base graph
Bk (M ) is connected. Then for every matroid M in C, the toric ideal IM is generated
in degree at most n.
    In particular, in order to prove Conjecture 1 it is enough to show that for every
k > 2 and for every k-matroid M the k-base graph Bk (M ) is connected.

lower the rank of a matroid) and adding parallel elements. The upcoming Theorem

                         2. Graphs on bases of a matroid
     We say that two bases of a matroid are neighbouring if one is obtained from
another by a symmetric exchange. That is, if their symmetric diﬀerence has two
elements. A graph on bases of a matroid M with edges between neighbouring bases
is called the basis graph of a matroid, and denoted by B(M ). Basis graphs have
been studied in 1960s and 1970s, and they are well understand. In particular, basis
graphs are Hamiltonian (with two trivial exceptions), even a characterization is
known (see [13, 14] and references within).
     For k ≥ 1, a k-matroid is a matroid whose ground set can be partitioned into
k pairwise disjoint bases. We call a basis of a k-matroid complementary if its
complement can be partitioned into k − 1 pairwise disjoint bases. That is, when it
is an element of some partition of the ground set into bases. When B is the set of
bases of a k-matroid, then the set of complementary bases we denote by Bc .
     Blasiak [1] proposed a very nice and simple translation of the problem of gen-
erating the ideal IM to the problem of connectivity of some graphs naturally asso-
ciated to k-matroids. We are going to use this approach for the proof of Theorem 5
and Corollary 6. For k ≥ 3, the k-base graph of a k-matroid M , denoted by Bk (M ),
is a graph on sets of k pairwise disjoint bases of M (partitions of the ground set into
bases), where edges join vertices with nonempty intersection. That is, sets of bases
{B1 , . . . , Bk } and {B1′ , . . . , Bk′ } are connected in Bk (M ) if for some i, j equality
Bi = Bj′ holds. A simple corollary of the proof of Proposition 2.1 from [1] gives the
following.

    Proposition 10 (Blasiak, [1]). Let C be a class of matroids that is closed under
deletions that do not lower the rank of a matroid, and adding parallel elements.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
      (1) for every k > n and for every k-matroid M in C the k-base graph Bk (M )
          is connected,
      (2) for every matroid M in C the ideal IM is generated in degree at most n.

    In particular, in order to prove Conjecture 1 it is enough to show that for every
k > 2 and for every k-matroid M the k-base graph Bk (M ) is connected.
     Here we propose another approach to White’s conjecture. Consider other
graphs that can be naturally associated to k-matroids. The complementary ba-
sis graph of a k-matroid, denoted by Bc (M ), is a graph on complementary bases
of M with edges between neighbouring bases. That is, the complementary basis
graph of a k-matroid is the restriction of its basis graph to complementary bases
Bc (M ) = B(M )|Bc .
     Graphs Bc (M ) have been already studied for 2-matroids. In 1985 Farber,
Richter and Shank [7] proved that for a graphic 2-matroid M the graph Bc (M ) is
connected, they also conjectured connectivity for arbitrary 2-matroids. In [1] after
the proof of Proposition 2.1 Blasiak observes the following easy equivalence.

    Proposition 11 (Blasiak, [1]). Let C be a class of matroids that is closed under
deletions that do not lower the rank of a matroid, and adding parallel elements.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
      (1) for every 2-matroid M in C the complementary basis graph Bc (M ) is
          connected,

    We recall one of the versions of the matroid union theorem, which will be used
several times in this paper. It characterizes k-matroids in terms of rank function.
     Theorem 2.1 (Nash-Williams [25], Edmonds [9]). A matroid M is a k-matroid,
if and only if for every A ⊂ E the inequality k · r(A) ≥ |A| holds, and k · r(E) = |E|.
     Blasiak [1] proposed a very nice and simple translation of the problem of gen-
erating the toric ideal of a matroid to the problem of connectivity of some graphs
naturally associated to k-matroids. We are going to use this approach for the proof
of Corollary 3.3. Following Blasiak, for k ≥ 3 the k-base graph of a k-matroid M ,
denoted by Bk (M ), is a graph on sets of k pairwise disjoint bases of M (partitions
of the ground set into bases), where edges join vertices with nonempty intersection.
That is, sets of bases {B1 , . . . , Bk } and {B1′ , . . . , Bk′ } are connected in Bk (M ) if
for some i, j the equality Bi = Bj′ holds. Recall that if {e, f } is a circuit in a
matroid M , then elements e and f are said to be parallel. In this case B is a basis
of M containing e if and only if (B ∪ f ) \ e is a basis of M containing f . Via this
property one can add to a matroid elements parallel to a ﬁxed element (enlarging
its ground set), or remove them. Notice that the reﬂexive closure of being parallel
is an equivalence relation. A simple corollary of the proof of Proposition 2.1 from
[1] gives the following.
    Proposition 2.2 (Blasiak, [1]). Let C be a class of matroids that is closed under
deletions that do not lower the rank of a matroid, and adding parallel elements.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
      (1) for every k > d and for every k-matroid M in C the k-base graph Bk (M )
          is connected,
      (2) for every matroid M in C the ideal IM is generated in degree at most d.
     In particular, in order to prove Conjecture 1.1 it is enough to show that for
every k > 2 and for every k-matroid M the k-base graph Bk (M ) is connected.
     Here we propose another approach to White’s conjecture. Consider other
graphs that can be naturally associated to k-matroids. The complementary ba-
sis graph of a k-matroid, denoted by Bc (M ), is a graph on complementary bases
of M with edges between neighboring bases. That is, the complementary basis
graph of a k-matroid is the restriction of its basis graph to complementary bases
Bc (M ) = B(M )|Bc .
     Graphs Bc (M ) have been already studied for 2-matroids M . In 1985 Farber,
Richter and Shank [11] proved that for a graphic 2-matroid M the graph Bc (M ) is
connected, they also conjectured connectivity for arbitrary 2-matroids. In [1] after
the proof of Proposition 2.1 Blasiak observes the following easy equivalence:
    Proposition 2.3 (Blasiak, [1]). Let C be a class of matroids that is closed under
deletions that do not lower the rank of a matroid, and adding parallel elements.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
      (1) for every 2-matroid M in C the complementary basis graph Bc (M ) is
          connected,
      (2) for every matroid M in C, elements of degree 2 in IM considered in the
          noncommutative polynomial ring KhyB : B ∈ Bi are generated by qua-
          dratic binomials corresponding to symmetric exchanges.
    We state the following two conjectures strongly related to White’s conjectures:

          2.5 implies the strongest White’s Conjecture 1.3,

     Proof. We begin with implication (1). Let M be a k-matroid in C, and let
B1 , B1′ be complementary bases in M . So, there exist bases B2 , . . . , Bk , B2′ , . . . , Bk′
such that entries of the sequences A = (B1 , . . . , Bk ) and A′ = (B1′ , . . . , Bk′ ) form

alently sequences of bases A and A′ have equal union (as a multiset). By the
assumption, we can generate b using quadratic binomials corresponding to sym-
metric exchanges, or equivalently we can pass between A and A′ by a sequence of
steps, in each step making a symmetric exchange. Notice that all bases appearing
during this process are complementary bases of M . Observe that the ﬁrst bases of
two sequences joined by a single step are either the same or neighboring. Thus we
get a path in Bc (M ) between B1 and B1′ .
     For the implication (2), by Propositions 2.2 and 2.3 it is enough to show that for
every k ≥ 3 and for every k-matroid M in C the k-base graph Bk (M ) is connected,
and for every 2-matroid M in C the complementary basis graph Bc (M ) is connected.
The second part we get directly from complementary basis graph Conjecture 2.4.
For the ﬁrst part, let M be a k-matroid in C (for k ≥ 3) and let {B1 , . . . , Bk },
{B1′ , . . . , Bk′ } be two vertices in Bk (M ). Since Bc (M ) is connected, it is enough to
show that vertices of Bk (M ) containing neighboring bases are connected in Bk (M ).
If the symmetric diﬀerence of B1 and B1′ is {x, y}, then consider the restriction of M
to the set E \ (B1 ∩ B1′ ). This matroid satisﬁes assumptions of Conjecture 2.5 with

connected by an edge. The ﬁrst one is connected by an edge with {B1 , . . . , Bk },
while the second with {B1′ , . . . , Bk′ }. We get that any two vertices {B1 , . . . , Bk },
{B1′ , . . . , Bk′ } in Bk (M ) containing neighboring bases are connected by a path. 

                      3. Degree bounds and Gröbner bases
     By Hilbert’s basis theorem the ideal IM is ﬁnitely generated. However, it is not
easy to give any explicit bound on degree in which it is generated. A bound follows
from a more general theorem about toric ideals. Theorem 13.14 from [29] asserts
that if a graded set A ⊂ Zd generates a normal semigroup, then the corresponding
toric ideal IA is generated in degree at most d. ForPa matroid M we consider the
set of indicator vectors of all bases, that is {eB = i∈B ei : B ∈ B} ⊂ Z|E| , and
denote it also by B. By [30, Theorem 1] the semigroup NB generated by B is
normal (it is also an easy consequence of the matroid union theorem – Theorem
2.1). The toric ideal corresponding to B is the ideal IM . Hence, the toric ideal of a
matroid is generated in degree at most the size of its ground set. If we ﬁx the size
of the ground set, then there are only ﬁnitely many matroids on it, so a common
bound is not surprising.
     When we ﬁx only the rank, then the number of matroids of that rank is inﬁnite.
Theorem 3.2 asserts that in this case there is also a common bound on the degree
in which the corresponding toric ideals are generated. Furthermore, there is a
common bound on the degree of their Gröbner bases. In order to prove it we need
the following structural result:

     Theorem 3.1. Let M be a k-matroid of rank r. Among every k − s pairwise
disjoint bases in M at most r(r + 2)! + s(r + 1)! bases are not complementary.
