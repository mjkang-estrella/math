## 376946: three sustained routes

Deza–Rosenberg math/0005270v2 pp2–3 defines alpha(P) as the 0/1 indicator of (m+1)-subsets transversal to every nonempty block of an (m+1)-partition; P_n^m is their conic hull. Section9 Conjecture5 is the exact target, not a metric-cone adjacency conjecture.

Route A, explicit decompositions. Suppose S=(A,B,C union D,R), T=(A union B,C,D,R), where A,B,C,D are pairwise disjoint nonempty sets and R is the SAME list of m-2 residual blocks partitioning the complement. Put U=(A,C,B union D,R), V=(A union C,B,D,R). Then alpha(S)+alpha(T)=alpha(U)+alpha(V). To verify every coordinate, a transversal must use one element of every residual block and exactly three elements from the four local atoms. Repeated use of an atom makes all four indicators zero. For three distinct atoms, the four possible types ABC, ABD, ACD, BCD occur exactly once on each side: S accounts for ABC,ABD; T for ACD,BCD; U for ABC,ACD; V for ABD,BCD. Other tuples vanish on both sides. U and V are genuinely different rays from S,T. If S,T generated a two-dimensional face, positivity and the face property force U,V into that face; its only extreme rays would be S,T, contradiction. This proves nonadjacency for this common-residual case. It does not settle Conjecture5: the six specified subsets need not force the remaining m-2 blocks to agree when m is large. Reusing this identity with unmatched residual partitions is invalid. Even when m=2 or3 pays this direction, necessity is still unpaid. No claimed new small-case credit; source already knows several such cases.

Route B, minimal support face. Since every ray is nonnegative, any decomposition of alpha(S)+alpha(T) uses only partitions U whose transversal support lies in supp(S) union supp(T). This follows coordinatewise: a zero coordinate of the sum cannot contain a positive summand. One might classify these U from the bipartite intersection matrix of S-blocks and T-blocks. The gap is an exact combinatorial lemma: either this support face has only the two prescribed rays, or its extra rays force precisely the six-block unions in the target. Neither implication was proved. A support face can contain extra rays without proving that the smallest face through S,T has dimension>2, so support enumeration alone would not suffice even if permitted.

Route C, exposing functional. Seek weights on (m+1)-subsets, nonnegative on every partition ray, vanishing exactly on S and T. Coordinate-zero penalties handle all rays with outside support but fail to separate possible extra rays entirely inside the union support. Need a signed correction simultaneously nonnegative on all such partitions; no all-parameter weight construction or classification established. Park, not a counterexample or a full criterion.

## 377975: complete applicable public bridge and independent algebra check

Williams math/0307271v2 Sections5–6 defines Ehat=q^(k-n)E and counts regular permutations with k weak excedences by (k-1)(n-k)-alignments. Conjecture5.10 states the assigned product. Corteel math/0505031v1 Proposition1 uses precisely this normalization; Theorem1 has b_h=y[h+1]_q+[h]_q and lambda_h=y[h]_q^2. Propositions2–3 and Section2 give the Motzkin weight construction and crossings+alignments=(k-1)(n-k), so this is not a different Carlitz/major-index q-Eulerian polynomial.

The exact ProbXiv public post, dated August17,2026, already gives the following proof. Its machine review labels are not evidence; here is the checked algebra. Let F_h be the continued-fraction tail. At q=0 all F_h for h>=1 equal M, where M=1/(1-(1+y)x-yx^2 M). Formal x-adic uniqueness justifies equal tails; each coefficient sees finitely many heights. Put A_h=[q]F_h. For h>=2, A_h=A and A=M^2((1+y)x+2yx^2 M+yx^2 A). At height1, A1=M^2(yx+2yx^2 M+yx^2 A). Subtracting gives A-A1=M^2 x. Solving yields A1=M^2 xy(1+xM)^2/(1-yx^2 M^2). Denominators have constant1, so inversion is legitimate in formal power series, with no analytic convergence assumption. F0(0)=M/(1+xM), b0=y and lambda1=y have no linear q term. Hence A0=F0(0)^2 yx^2 A1=x^3 y^2 M^4/(1-yx^2 M^2).

Set T=xM. The quadratic gives T=x phi(T), phi(t)=(1+t)(1+yt). Since phi(T)-T phi'(T)=1-yT^2, A0=y^2 T^3/(1-x phi'(T)). Lagrange's coefficient identity [x^n] H(T)/(1-x phi'(T))=[t^n]H(t)phi(t)^n, applied to H=t^3, gives [x^n y^k]A0=[t^(n-3)y^(k-2)](1+t)^n(1+yt)^n=binom(n,k-2)binom(n,n-k-1). This is the desired product. The identity follows, for example, by formal substitution x=t/phi(t): dx/x=(1-t phi'/phi)dt/t, which cancels the denominator; this is a formal residue change of variables, not a numeric test. For n<3 the left series begins at x^3 and the product vanishes for every 1<=k<=n; k=1 and k=n also vanish, consistent with the coefficient extraction. Other out-of-range binomials use zero convention.

Route B considered direct Le-diagrams: minimal regular rank n-k uses one1 per nonempty column; rank n-k+1 requires exactly one additional1, but the Le condition couples its row/column to the shape boundary. A direct pair-of-subsets bijection with sizes k+1 and k-2 was not constructed.
