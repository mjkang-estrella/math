    As previously mentioned, the 2006 AIM workshop was, in many ways, a springboard for numerous

    The degree of a vertex v in G, denoted by degG (v), is defined as the number of neighbors of v in G,
while the minimum degree δ(G) represents the smallest degree among all vertices of G. It is easily seen that
the parameter δ is not minor monotone. This fact suggests the introduction of two additional parameters,
namely, the degeneracy of G, defined as

                                   l(G) = max {δ(H) : H is a subgraph of G},

and the minor monotone ceiling of δ(G), is given by

                                   ⌈δ⌉(G) = max {δ(H) : H is a minor of G}.

    The degeneracy of a graph is a classical topic in graph theory and can be seen as a measure of the sparsity
of a graph. In [18], a graph was said to be k-degenerate if for every induced subgraph H of G, δ(H) ≤ k.
By definition, l(G) is monotone under edge and vertex deletions, while ⌈δ⌉(G) is minor monotone. Since a
subgraph can always be regarded as a minor, we have δ(G) ≤ l(G) ≤ ⌈δ⌉(G).
    The δ-conjecture asks whether M(G) ≥ δ(G), or equivalently mr(G) ≤ |G| − δ(G) holds for all graphs
G. After the original δ-conjecture was conceived and observed, additional analysis has led to stronger
conjectures about the minimum rank restricting to certain subclasses of positive semidefinite matrices in
