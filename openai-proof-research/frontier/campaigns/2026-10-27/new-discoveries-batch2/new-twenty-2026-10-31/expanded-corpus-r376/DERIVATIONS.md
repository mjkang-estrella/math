Let n>1 be odd, m=(n-1)/2, z primitive. Define A_jk=1/(1-z^(j-k)) for j!=k and A_jj=0 on Z/n. Define B_jk=(1+z^(j-k))/(1-z^(j-k)) off diagonal and zero on diagonal.

Fourier eigenvalues of A are lambda_k=sum_(d=1)^(n-1) z^(-kd)/(1-z^d). Pair d and n-d to get lambda_0=(n-1)/2=m. For 0<=k<n-1,
lambda_(k+1)-lambda_k=sum_(d=1)^(n-1) z^(-(k+1)d)=-1,
since (z^(-d)-1)/(1-z^d)=z^(-d). Hence lambda_k=m-k. These Fourier vectors form a basis (geometric-sum orthogonality), giving one zero eigenvalue at k=m, and product of nonzero eigenvalues (-1)^m(m!)^2. For a diagonalizable matrix with a simple zero eigenvalue and normalized Fourier eigenvector v, adj(A) is the product of the nonzero eigenvalues times vv*/n. This follows by applying adj to a diagonal form, or by multiplying det(A+tI)(A+tI)^(-1) and taking t=0. Every principal cofactor is therefore (-1)^m(m!)^2/n. The determinant expansion of a principal submatrix has only derangements because its diagonal is zero. Deleting index n proves exactly the third displayed identity in record 351646.

The zero mode is constant, so each principal cofactor is that product divided by n. Its determinant expansion proves exactly the fourth displayed identity, including its sign and literal factor n. No numerical calculations or solver were used.

Define the multiaffine polynomial F_n(y)=sum_(pi in S_n) product_(i in Pin(pi)) y_i. Then F_1=1, and for n>=2,
F_n=[2+(n-2)y_n] F_(n-1)+2 y_n sum_(i=1)^(n-1)(1-y_i) partial_i F_(n-1).
Proof: insert the new maximum n in a permutation of length n-1. Its two end positions preserve old peaks and do not create a new peak, giving 2F. The n-2 internal gaps create peak n. In a provisional y_n(n-2)F, an internal gap adjacent to an old peak i incorrectly retains y_i; exactly two gaps touch that peak, and replace its factor y_i by 1. Two old peaks cannot touch the same gap, since adjacent positions cannot both be peaks. The correction for each peak is therefore exactly 2y_n(1-y_i)partial_i F. This accounts for every insertion once. For n=2 there are no peaks/derivatives and F_2=2, as required. No permutation enumeration program was run.

Multiaffinity yields partial_i F evaluated at y(T) equal to [q_(n-1)(T)-q_(n-1)(T minus {i})]/2 if i in T, and [q_(n-1)(T union {i})-q_(n-1)(T)]/2 otherwise. Thus the exact recurrence at a permitted maximum couples T to sets with formerly forbidden peaks. It is not a scalar recurrence in q(T). A proposed induction keeping only q(T) silently discards the latter derivative contributions.
