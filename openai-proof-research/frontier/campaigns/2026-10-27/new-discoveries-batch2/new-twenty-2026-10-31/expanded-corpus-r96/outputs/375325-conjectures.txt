 2.2. Delta operators and the valley
conjecture 

 
 
 
 Let \Lambda be the algebra of symmetric functions over
 \mathbb{Q}(q,t) and \{\tilde{H}_{\mu}\} the modified Macdonald basis;
see [ 6 ] . For a symmetric function f , the operators

 \Delta^{\prime}_{f}\tilde{H}_{\mu}=f[B_{\mu}-1]\tilde{H}_{\mu} , where
 B_{\mu}=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mu}q^{j-1}t^{i-1} .

 For 0\leq k<n ,

 [ 4 , 1 ] . For the valley version, the cases
 t=0 and q=0 of the extended form are proved in [ 12 ] 
(see also [ 13 , 8 ] for the underlying ordered–set–partition combinatorics), the Schröder case in

square valley versions in [ 10 , 11 ] . Since
Conjecture  2.4 would identify \operatorname{Val}_{n,k} with a
symmetric function, the symmetry of \operatorname{Val}_{n,k} is a necessary
consistency property; it is open in general [ 3 ] .
Symmetry of \operatorname{Val}_{n,k} amounts to the symmetry of the coefficient
of each power of t , i.e. of the sum of all slices
 \operatorname{V}_{n,k,m} over the multisets m of a given area; the symmetry
of every individual slice is an a priori stronger property. It is
the stronger property that is established here for \operatorname{area}(m)\leq 1 , where, the multiset of each area being unique
(Remark  2.2 ), the two properties in fact coincide.

 8.1. No letter–level intertwiner 

 
 
 
 The classical route to exchange identities, as in [ 7 ] , is a
local involution: an R –matrix at the level of single letters. The
following observation shows that in the present calculus no such
operator exists, already in the flat undecorated case; the burden of
symmetry is genuinely carried by the closed series identities of
Theorem  4.2 and Lemma  6.3 . 
 
 
 
 Proposition 8.1 . 
 
 
 
 Let A_{1}:=M_{u}\,\mathfrak{d} and A_{2}:=M_{v} , the letter operators of the
model, so that T=A_{1}+A_{2} . If an operator \Psi on
 R=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}][u,v,z] , linear over \mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}] ,
satisfies the intertwining relations 
 

 
 
 \Psi\,A_{1}=A_{2}\,\Psi,\qquad\Psi\,A_{2}=A_{1}\,\Psi,

 A_{1}=u\,\sigma_{v} , A_{2}=M_{v} acting on
 \mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}][u,v] . 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 
 By Lemma  4.1 (2),
 A_{1}A_{2}=M_{u}\mathfrak{d}M_{v}=q\,M_{u}M_{v}\mathfrak{d}=q\,A_{2}A_{1} : the letter operators
satisfy the quantum–plane relation. Hence 
 

 
 
 A_{2}A_{1}\Psi\;=\;\Psi\,A_{1}A_{2}\;=\;q\,\Psi\,A_{2}A_{1}\;=\;q\,A_{1}A_{2}\,\Psi\;=\;q^{2}\,A_{2}A_{1}\Psi, 
 
 
 
 so (1-q^{2})\,A_{2}A_{1}\Psi=0 . The ring R is a free
 \mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}] –module and 1-q^{2}\neq 0 , so
 A_{2}A_{1}\Psi=0 ; and A_{2}A_{1}=uv\,\mathfrak{d} is injective ( \sigma_{v} is
bijective, and (1+qzv) , u , v are non–zero–divisors), whence
 \Psi=0 . The specialization z=0 is identical with
 A_{2}A_{1}=uv\,\sigma_{v} .
∎

 8.4. Open problems 

 
 
 
 (1) 
 
 
 
 Area two. Prove Conjecture  8.2 (or
only Conjecture  2.6 ) for m=0^{n-2}1^{2} ,
 n\geq 3 . As
explained in Section  8.2 , the missing ingredient is a
closed family of symmetric series generalizing Lemma  6.3 to
two interacting \bar{\mathfrak{d}} –insertions. 
 
 (2) 
 
 
 
 Schur positivity. Corollary  7.4 upgrades
the t^{0} and t^{1} slices from quasisymmetric to symmetric;
their monomial expansions are manifestly positive. The valley Delta
conjecture predicts that these slices are Schur positive (indeed equal to the corresponding slices of
 \Delta^{\prime}_{e_{n-k-1}}e_{n} ), and it would be very interesting to
establish Schur positivity directly, e.g. through an LLT–type
expansion of the refined classes. 
 
 (3) 
 
 
 
 A bijective witness. Proposition  8.1 rules
out a letter–local involution, not a global one. Does the refined
exchange of Theorem  7.1 admit a weight–preserving
involution on objects? The recursion ( 9 ), whose
correction term is a difference of manifestly symmetric pieces
weighted by the factor 1-q^{-1} , not a nonnegative integral
combination, suggests that any such involution must
act non–locally. 
 
 (4) 
 
 
 
 Propagation. The implications of Iraci and Vanden
Wyngaerd [ 10 , 11 ] transport the full valley Delta
conjecture to its generalized and square versions. Do the
slice symmetries of Corollary  7.4 , or the refined
exchange itself, propagate along the same schedules? Relatedly,
D’Adderio and Iraci [ 3 ] derive consequences from a
symmetry hypothesis on (a refinement of) the extended valley
side; extending Theorem  7.1 to the partially labelled
setting would connect the two threads.
