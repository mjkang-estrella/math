result of Rassart [ 26 ] that the map
 k\mapsto K_{k\lambda,k\mu} is a polynomial function of k . Viewing

 GT_{\lambda\mu} , McAllister [ 23 ] computed its degree,
resolving a conjecture of
King–Tollu–Toumazet [ 17 ] . 
 
 
 
 
 Continuous Gelfand–Tsetlin geometry provides another way to pass from
discrete tableau sums to real-valued objects, by replacing summation over
integral patterns with integration over real or geometric Gelfand–Tsetlin
patterns. O’Connell [ 24 ] represents
 J_{\lambda}(x)=h(\lambda)^{-1}\det(e^{\lambda_{i}x_{j}}) , where
 h(\lambda)=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}) , as an integral
over the real Gelfand–Tsetlin polytope, and further relates
this integral to Whittaker functions and Givental’s formula. Tao [ 33 ] discusses continuous analogues of Schur and skew Schur polynomials obtained by replacing sums over integral Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns with integrals over real Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns. More combinatorial, Prasad [ 25 ] develops timed tableaux and a timed plactic monoid extending classical tableau combinatorics to real-valued settings. Our operator \rowMap_{k} is related to these in that it also connects classical tableau combinatorics with scaled or fractional structures. Rather than passing directly to continuous objects, \rowMap_{k} produces symmetric functions governed by stretched Kostka coefficients and tableaux with fractional weights, while retaining positivity properties and explicit tableau models. 
 
 
 
 
 A fundamental problem in algebraic combinatorics is to understand positivity
phenomena in expansions between different bases. For instance, the Schur
positivity of LLT polynomials, introduced by Lascoux, Leclerc, and
Thibon [ 18 ] , was established by Grojnowski and
Haiman [ 13 ] and plays an important role in the study of
Macdonald polynomials [ 13 ] . Similar positivity
phenomena arise in the the

nald polynomials [ 13 ] . Similar positivity
phenomena arise in the theory of chromatic symmetric functions, introduced by
Stanley [ 29 ] . In particular, for incomparability graphs of
 (3+1) -free posets, Gasharov [ 11 ] proved Schur positivity,
providing strong evidence for the Stanley–Stembridge conjecture asserting
 \mathrm{e} -positivity. Guay-Paquet [ 14 ] showed that this
conjecture reduces to the case of unit interval graphs, and it was proved by
Hikita [ 15 ] . 
 
 
 
 
 Our construction of \rowMap_{k} is also closely related to classical operations
on symmetric functions. In particular, the map \rowMap_{k} is reminiscent of
the Verschiebung operator, which is adjoint to the Adams operator with respect
to the Hall inner product [ 22 , 31 ] . From this
perspective, the map \rowMap_{k} can be regarded as a close cousin of these
classical constructions, while exhibiting distinct positivity and
combinatorial features.
