# Independent ordinary proof: MathDB 368088

Let P* be the primes greater than 3. A prime in P* is divisible by neither 2 nor 3: otherwise its positive divisor 2 or 3 would equal it. Division with remainder by 6 therefore gives residue 1 or 5. Define f(p)=p for residue 1 and f(p)=p-1 for residue 5, and Q=f(P*), a set (not a multiset) of positive natural numbers.

The images of the two branches have residues 1 and 4 modulo 6 respectively. Equality across branches is impossible; within a branch equality cancels the same shift and implies equality of primes. Thus f is injective. Its outputs are positive (p>=5 implies p-1>=4) and all have residue 1 modulo 3. In particular Q has no elements at most 3.

Define both counting functions on integer cutoffs using finite sets of natural numbers embedded in the integers. For n>=3 let A={p prime:3<p<=n}. Let E be the singleton {n+1} precisely when n+1 is prime and has residue 5 modulo 6, and empty otherwise. Then f restricts to a bijection A disjoint-union E -> {q in Q:q<=n}. For forward inclusion: p in A has f(p)<=p<=n; for p in E, f(p)=p-1=n. For surjectivity take the unique preimage p of q<=n. If p<=n then p belongs to A. Otherwise p>n; the fixed branch would give q=p>n, impossible. On the other branch p-1<=n<p implies, by integrality, p=n+1, hence p belongs to E. Injectivity was already proved; A and E are disjoint. These are finite sets since A is bounded by n and E has at most one element.

The primes at most n are the disjoint union {2,3} and A, so pi(n)=2+|A|, while pi_Q(n)=|A|+epsilon(n), epsilon=|E| in {0,1}. Consequently, as integers, pi_Q(n)-pi(n)=-2+epsilon(n), with absolute value at most 2. At n<0 both sets are empty because their members are positive; at n=0,1 both counts vanish; at n=2 prime count is 1 and Q count is 0. Thus every integer n satisfies |pi_Q(n)-pi(n)|<=2<3. The fixed natural constant c=3 works with the strict inequality; there is no truncated natural subtraction in this identity.

If infinitude is requested, P* is infinite by Euclid: if all primes were 2,3,p1,...,pk, the integer M=6*p1*...*pk+1>1 would have a prime divisor, obtained by choosing its least divisor >1, and this divisor divides none of the listed primes' product, a contradiction. Removing 2,3 leaves infinitely many; injectivity of f makes Q infinite. Neither arithmetic-progression infinitude nor a prime-pattern theorem is needed.

For every natural threshold N choose n=3(N+1). The integer 2n=6(N+1) is even and exceeds N, since 6(N+1)-N=5N+6>0. Every sum of two Q elements has residue 2 modulo 3, whereas this 2n has residue 0. Therefore no q1,q2 in Q sum to it. This applies also when q1=q2, and remains a refutation if distinct summands were required. It proves the full negation: there exists one qualifying Q such that for every natural N there is an even integer 2n>N without a Q representation.
