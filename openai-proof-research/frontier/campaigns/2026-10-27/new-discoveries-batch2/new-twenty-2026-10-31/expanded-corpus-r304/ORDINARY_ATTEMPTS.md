# Ordinary investigation; no surviving endpoint

All arguments here are ordinary reasoning. No mathematical enumeration, solver, numerical experiment or Lean execution was performed. The three principal routes below aim at the whole statement. None establishes it. A source lemma or a special case is not a candidate.

## 361271: two irreducible Laurent-series summands

Read the actual definitions and Section 4 of arXiv:2312.14888v2, compared the conjecture in v1 (4.5) with v2 (4.7), and checked the NYJM 31 (2025), 1409–1426 typeset statement and supporting lemmas. The source has already proved the three-summand equivalence (Theorem 4.4). Thus the only new direction needed is (1) implies (2), with TWO summands, for every additively reduced, additively atomic semidomain S and every nonmonomial Laurent series. The other implications must not be counted as new results.

The support is a bounded-below subset of Z. Additive reduction and the embedding into a domain imply that supports of products are Minkowski sums: a sum of nonzero products cannot cancel to zero. A nonmonomial series is irreducible if it is monolithic (one factor of every product is a monomial) and its coefficients have no common nonunit divisor (Lemma 4.1). A unit coefficient suffices for the latter condition. Atomicity and A_+(S)=S^× allow a coefficient s to be split as u+v, with u a unit and v possibly zero; expressions involving subtraction in the source are allocations of these summands, not an assumption that S is a ring.

First mechanism: allocate the support between two series, each beginning with a uniquely shortest consecutive gap. Lemma 4.2 supplies monolithicity. Its proof is substantive: if g,h have first support gaps d<=e, then the product first gap is d. Translating the first two terms of g by the second support term of h gives a later product gap <=d, contradicting uniqueness of the initial minimum. Seed each allocated series with a unit coefficient by splitting source coefficients where possible.

This closes the source's finite-minimum-gap case by its own construction, hence earns no credit. Let Δ be the minimum consecutive support gap and J the positions attaining it. When J is finite, keep its first pair together in the dense summand and move the remaining offending pairs into an eventually increasingly spaced second support. The source's Lemma 4.3 then makes the sparse summand monolithic. The two unit allocations at later terms ensure both summands have a unit coefficient. This is exactly the two-summand part of the proof of Theorem 4.4, not a solution of the infinite-J case.

Second mechanism for infinite J: view pairs at distance Δ as edges on the support. These form paths, since every exponent has at most one successor and predecessor at this distance. Alternating colors suppresses all same-color Δ gaps. Introduce one monochromatic seed edge of each color and alternate thereafter. On consecutive support this recovers Example 4.6: red support {0,1,4,6,8,...}, blue support {2,3,5,7,9,...}, both with initial gap 1 and every later gap at least 2. The construction is valid for this example and is already printed in the source.

The obstacle is the prefix before the first useful minimum-gap pair. Its terms must still be allocated. Their initial gaps may exceed Δ; a later monochromatic seed gap then destroys the hypothesis of Lemma 4.2. Making one color eventually sparse does not help the other color when infinitely many Δ pairs remain. It is not enough to say that an initial finite prefix can be ignored: multiplying by a monomial shifts all exponents but cannot delete that prefix.

Third repair attempted: replace the initial-gap criterion by uniqueness of a smallest gap ANYWHERE. That criterion is false. Over N, (1+x^2)(1+x^3)=1+x^2+x^3+x^5 has consecutive gaps 2,1,2, with a unique interior minimum and a genuine nonmonomial factorization. Its coefficients include units, so primitivity does not repair this failure. Nor does merging the source's three irreducible summands help: a sum of two irreducibles can be reducible, for example (1+x)+(x^2+x^3)=(1+x)(1+x^2). This refutes the proposed merge lemma, not Conjecture 4.7.

The exact unpaid lemma is a support-and-coefficient allocation theorem for arbitrary infinite J with an arbitrary finite prefix, guaranteeing two monolithic primitive summands, or a different irreducibility criterion that accommodates the prefix and infinitely repeated shortest gaps. No such theorem was obtained. A finite polynomial witness cannot be asserted from these failures; the source already handles polynomials. Park, credit 0. Companion literature and all citing bodies are not cleared.

## 375393: lexicographic blow-up and simultaneous Gram completion

Read Section 5, Lemmas 5.2–5.4 and proof of Theorem 1.10 of arXiv:2606.17974. The literal endpoint concerns ALL finite graphs G and all positive t, not just forests, cycles or one orientation difference. The source already handles forests and cycles. A purported repeat of those constructions receives zero credit.

Let d=diam(I(G)). An orientation difference is an arbitrary edge labeling π:E(G)->F_2. A list of d inversions X_s supplies a vector a(v) in F_2^d at every vertex, with π(uv)=a(u)·a(v). Conversely such vectors prescribe the inversions by their coordinates. Thus the definition gives universal solvability of every scalar edge-labeling Gram-completion problem in dimension d. In a t-fold independent blow-up an edge uv carries an arbitrary t by t matrix M_uv of orientation differences. The exact desired theorem is universal solvability of U(u)^T U(v)=M_uv with U(v) in F_2^(td by t). Its coordinate rows give td inversions and prove the literal diameter bound. This bridge is complete; existence of U is the missing mathematics.

First lift attempted: independently solve each matrix entry using scalar dimension d. The direct sum gives t^2 d coordinates, not td. For ordered indices (i,j), put scalar labels M_uv(i,j) on a base edge and solve them, but the endpoint coordinates must remember whether a clone is the i-end or the j-end. Without a globally compatible side choice, these scalar solutions also toggle unwanted clone pairs. Even on bipartite G, orienting all edges from one part to the other only repairs this side ambiguity; it still leaves t^2 blocks. Counting t^2 systems as t systems would be the whole unjustified step.

Second lift: solve over F_(2^t), then expand each coordinate through a trace pairing. This handles edge matrices arising as multiplication/trace bilinear forms. An arbitrary t by t F_2 matrix is an arbitrary linear operator, not necessarily multiplication by a field element: the operator space has dimension t^2, whereas the multiplication operators have dimension t. Additionally scalar universal solvability over F_2 does not imply universal solvability over an extension field. There are two distinct gaps, so a field-extension slogan does not prove the bound.

Third lift: symmetric matrix completion. For scalar labels d is the maximum minimal Gram dimension over free diagonal/nonedge entries. For blocks, one can choose within-fiber blocks and nonedge blocks freely, but prescribed cross-edge blocks remain arbitrary. A rank bound alone is insufficient over F_2: a symmetric alternating matrix has a different bilinear-form type from the ordinary dot product, and embedding may require an extra dimension. Any rank-completion argument must control both rank <=td and form type. The source instead constructs 2t-dimensional blocks on cycles using P=(I,0)^T, Q=(0,I)^T, R=(I,I)^T and invertible adjacent concatenations. At a vertex with two prescribed incident matrices, solving B^T Z=X and C^T Z=Y is legitimate because (B|C) is invertible. At three or more simultaneous neighbors this is overdetermined unless a new dependence invariant is supplied. A spanning-tree construction therefore leaves chord equations unpaid; its success on a tree is already public.

No exact in-scope counterexample is supplied. Failure of the three lifts is not evidence the conjecture is false. The full unpaid lemma is the scalar-to-block universal Gram-completion implication with no additive dimension loss, or an explicit graph and block labeling whose Gram dimension exceeds td together with a proven scalar diameter d. The latter would require a universal lower-bound argument, not just one orientation distance. Park, credit 0. The original and 2026 companion were fetched for bounded source comparison; exhaustive citing/journal/corrections review remains incomplete.

## 360957: marked primitive decomposition and binomial differences

Read arXiv:2311.18227v1, v2, v3 statement locations; actual primitive-factor decomposition and proof of Theorems 2.6 and 3.2 in v3; the DMTCS journal download is byte-identical to v3. Here a is left of n at positional distance k, all labels <a are right of n, f=T_(1,1)/x, T_(1,0)=x and T_(a,0)=|S_(a-1)(1324)|x^a. The claim is the entire family of formal-power-series identities for k>=a. Proving the three displayed expressions equivalent is only algebra, not the combinatorial conjecture.

In a fraction field set H_k=T_(a,k)/f^k and D_j=sum_(i=0)^j (-1)^i binom(j,i) H_(j-i). Newton inversion gives H_k=sum_(j=0)^k binom(k,j)D_j. Hence the first displayed formula is D_k=0 for all k>=a; it yields the second formula by truncation, and the third follows by collecting coefficients of H_j. The coefficient is sum_(r=j)^(a-1) (-1)^(r-j) binom(k,r)binom(r,j)=(-1)^(a-j-1)binom(k,j)binom(k-j-1,a-j-1). Denominators can be cleared, so this does not depend on treating f as a unit in Z[[x]]. This complete bridge is routine and uncredited.

The proposed full mechanism is to remove the labels 1,...,a-1, factor the remaining permutation uniquely into k primitives using the source's dot product, and mark the primitive positions containing the entries immediately following the deleted small labels. There are at most a-1 marked positions; several deleted labels can have the same mark, and terminal deleted labels have a separate suffix state. If inserting an unmarked primitive preserved all allowable marked reconstructions independently of its position, there would be fixed series C_j with T_(a,k)=sum_(j<a)binom(k,j)f^(k-j)C_j. The initial k<a values would force C_j=sum_i(-1)^i binom(j,i)f^i T_(a,j-i), exactly the target coefficients. For a=2 this is the source's genuine one-mark bijection, not merely a heuristic.

I pursued the required stability beyond the one-mark case. Delete small labels in increasing order while marking their right neighbors. To reconstruct, process deletions in reverse order, restoring each label before its marked entry. This makes multiple marks ordered and prevents loss of data when small labels are adjacent. Terminal strings of small labels must be stored separately. These repairs solve encoding uniqueness, but do not prove that every insertion is 1324-avoiding. A new small label can be the first element of a forbidden pattern spanning several primitives. Concretely, 3142 avoids 1324 (it has length four and is a different permutation), whereas inserting a new minimum at its front after incrementing its entries gives 14253, which contains 1,4,2,5 in order, a 1324 pattern. Thus removal preserving avoidance does not make reinsertion automatic. This illustration is not a counterexample to the conjecture's stricter domain.

Second attempt: collect all factors intersecting a small-label obstruction into a single marked block. Then unmarked factors would be freely inserted only between obstruction blocks. But a block may contain an unbounded number of primitive factors, and binom(k,j) counts chosen individual positions, not arbitrary interval lengths. Without proving that each obstruction can be localized to one marked primitive, grouping changes the generating function and loses the claimed denominator order a.

Third attempt: use reverse-complement averaging as in Theorem 3.1. For one deleted label the numbers of legal positions are j+1 and i+1, summing to n-k. For several deleted labels legal positions are correlated; averaging a product does not equal the product of the averages. The simple derivative recurrence therefore has no proved iteration. The unpaid lemma is a weight-preserving, insertion-stable decomposition with at most a-1 freely placeable marked factors, including shared marks and suffix states. No such lemma or exact counterexample was obtained. Park, credit 0; no dormant Pi plan is justified.
