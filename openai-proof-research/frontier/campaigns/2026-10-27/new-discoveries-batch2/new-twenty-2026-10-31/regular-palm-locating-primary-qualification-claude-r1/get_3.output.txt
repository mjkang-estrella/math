Abstract
Finding the partition dimension of a graph is one of the interesting (and uncompletely solved) prob-
lems of graph theory. For instance, the values of the partition dimensions for most kind of trees are
still unknown. Although for several classes of trees such as paths, stars, caterpillars, homogeneous
firecrackers and others, we do know their partition dimensions. In this paper, we determine the
partition dimension of a subdivision of a particular tree, namely homogeneous firecrackers. Let
G be any graph. For any positive integer k and e ∈ E(G), a subdivision of a graph G, denoted
by S(G(e; k)), is the graph obtained from G by replacing an edge e with a (k + 1)-path. We
show that the partition dimension of S(G(e; k)) is equal to the partition dimension of G if G is a
homogeneous firecracker.
Keywords: partition dimension, resolving partition, subdivision, homogeneous firecracker

   Let u, v be two vertices of a connected graph G(V, E). We define the distance between vertices
u and v as the minimum length of a path connecting them. This distance is denoted by d(u, v).
For a set A ⊆ V , the distance from vertex u to set A, denoted by d(u, A), is min{d(u, x)|x ∈ A}.
