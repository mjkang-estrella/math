## 364187: full endpoint, classical reduction and exact public duplication

Definitions 1–3 of Bonetto–Jardón Kojakhmetov give finite simple undirected graphs with disjoint spanning edge classes G,H and M(e)=L_G+e A_H. Definitions 7–8 count positive and negative eigenvalues with multiplicity and set signature=Nplus−Nminus. The primary positive-axis domain is e>0. Empty graphs, disconnected G, zero eigenvalues and repeated eigenvalues are included.

First attempted route: track individual eigenvalue derivatives. At a simple zero, v^T M'(e)v=−v^T L_G v/e<=0. This is suggestive but not a complete proof: persistent kernels, multiplicities and nonsmooth eigenvalue choices require work. A second attempt to assert M(t)<=M(s) fails because A_H need not be positive semidefinite.

Complete repair using the classical spectral theorem and positive subspaces: for real symmetric Q, the greatest dimension of a subspace on which x^TQx>0 for nonzero x is Nplus(Q). The positive-eigenvector span attains that dimension. Any larger subspace intersects the span of nonpositive eigenvectors nontrivially by the dimension formula, contradicting positivity. Replacing Q by −Q gives the negative version. This proves the exact inertia bridge without assuming nonsingularity.

L=L_G is positive semidefinite because x^T L x is the sum over unordered edges {u,v} in G of (x_u−x_v)^2. A=A_H is symmetric. For 0<s<=t, write Q(e)=A+L/e. Then Q(s)−Q(t)=(1/s−1/t)L is positive semidefinite. Every positive subspace for Q(t) is positive for Q(s), and every negative subspace for Q(s) is negative for Q(t). Hence Nplus(Q(t))<=Nplus(Q(s)) and Nminus(Q(s))<=Nminus(Q(t)). Positive scalar multiplication preserves every eigenvalue sign, so these are exactly the indices of M(t),M(s). Subtract to obtain signature(M(t))<=signature(M(s)). Equality of parameters is included; zeros contribute zero at crossings. This supplies the COMPLETE classical/source bridge, not merely a reference to matrix familiarity.

## 365244: full metric dimension target, interval and construction routes

Type I is the odd-parity sublattice in the rectangle 0<=x<=2n, 0<=y<=2m: vertices (2i,2j+1),0<=i<=n,0<=j<m, and (2i+1,2j),0<=i<n,0<=j<=m. Type II is the even-parity sublattice 0<=x<=2n−2,0<=y<=2m. In either graph, adjacency means BOTH coordinate differences have absolute value1. Metric dimension is the least cardinality of a vertex subset distinguishing every pair by graph distances. For every 1<=m and n>2m+1, the target is equality to ceil((n−1)/m)+1 for BOTH types.

Distance bridge: for distinct vertices in either full parity rectangle, d(u,v)=max(|dx|,|dy|). Every diagonal edge changes each coordinate by1, proving the lower bound. Both coordinate differences have the same parity. Set D to their maximum. In a coordinate with displacement D take D monotone steps. In the other coordinate take unit steps with the prescribed displacement, using an even number of extra steps as backtracks along an available neighboring coordinate, staying within the interval. Since each rectangle has positive width and height, boundary positions have a neighbor. One can first use a neighboring backtrack then follow a monotone path, choosing the neighbor inward at endpoints. The parity condition ensures the walk stays on the chosen vertex sublattice, hence is a graph path. This establishes the formula without executing shortest paths.

Strategy 1: full lower bound by distinguishing vertical pairs. If u=(x,y),v=(x,y+2) are legal and r=(a,b), put c=|x−a|,alpha=|y−b|,beta=|y+2−b|. Its distances differ exactly when alpha!=beta and c<max(alpha,beta). This exact criterion turns each necessary distinction into a horizontal interval constraint for a landmark. Both bottom and top vertical pairs must be distinguished. Landmarks near one boundary cover a longer interval on the opposite boundary and a shorter interval near their own; middle landmarks split this budget. The coarse interval total produces the right order n/m but DOES NOT give the exact ceil((n−1)/m)+1: parity, endpoint coverage, strict inequality and simultaneous saturation matter. Need a sharp joint covering lemma for all legal landmark coordinates and both row families. We do not assert it or pretend a growth estimate proves equality.

Strategy 2: prove the proposed upper set from the source. For q=ceil((n−1)/m), the source alternates boundary stations at x=1+2mt for TypeI, x=2mt for TypeII,0<=t<q, plus a parity-dependent terminal landmark. Plugging the L-infinity distance formula reduces injectivity to intersection of piecewise affine distance profiles on consecutive station slabs. Interior horizontal separation is visible to distant stations, while equal-x pairs need the vertical contributions. At station endpoints and the residual terminal slab the branches change, so a local slab separation argument must also compare vertices in distinct slabs. The source asserts these sets are resolving; we have not supplied a full proof across every parity and residual class. Even accepting that upper bound as a source theorem would leave the lower bound above unproved. No finite computation, fabricated certificate, or generic solver plan.
