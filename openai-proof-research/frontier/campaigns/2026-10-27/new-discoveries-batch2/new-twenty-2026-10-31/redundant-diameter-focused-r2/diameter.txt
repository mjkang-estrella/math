                                                        Abstract: Let G1 and G2 be two undirected nontrivial graphs. The
                                                    Kronecker product of G1 and G2 denoted by G1 ⊗G2 with vertex set V (G1 )×
                                                    V (G2 ), two vertices x1 x2 and y1 y2 are adjacent if and only if (x1 , y1) ∈ E(G1 )
                                                    and (x2 , y2 ) ∈ E(G2 ). This paper presents a formula for computing the
                                                    diameter of G1 ⊗ G2 by means of the diameters and primitive exponents of
                                                    factor graphs.
                                                    Keywords: diameter, Kronecker product, primitive exponent
                                                    AMS Subject Classification: 05C12, 05C50


                                          1         Introduction
                                          For notation and graph-theoretical terminology not defined here we follow [18]. Specif-
                                          ically, let G = (V, E) be a nontrivial graph with no parallel edges, but loops allowed,
                                          where V = V (G) is the vertex-set and E = E(G) is the edge-set.
                                              For two graphs G and H, Kronecker product G ⊗ H is a graph with vertex set
                                          V (G) × V (H) and two vertices x1 x2 and y1 y2 are adjacent when (x1 , y1 ) ∈ E(G) and
                                          (x2 , y2) ∈ E(G).
                                              As an operation of graphs, Kronecker product G ⊗ H was introduced first by Weich-
                                          esel [15] in 1962. It has been shown that the Kronecker product is a good method to
                                          construct lager networks that can generate many good properties of the factor graphs
                                          (see [9]), and has received much research attention recently. Some properties and
                                          graphic parameters have been investigated [1, 2, 5, 8, 11]. The connectivity and diam-
                                          eter are two important parameters to measure reliability and efficiency of a network.
                                          Very recently, the connectivity of Kronecker product graph has been deeply studied
                                          (see, [3, 6, 7, 11, 12, 14, 16, 17]). However, the diameter of Kronecker product graph has
                                          been not investigated yet.

2     Some Lemmas
Let G be a graph. Denote γ(G; x, y) to be the minimum integer such that there exists
an (x, y)-walk of length k for any k ≥ γ(G; x, y) and γ(G) be the minimum integer γ for
which, for any two vertices x and y in G, there exists an (x, y)-walk of length k for any
integer k ≥ γ. Let γ(G) = max{γ(G; x, y) : x, y ∈ V (G)}.
    If γ(G) is well-defined, then G is said to be primitive, and γ(G) is called the primitive
exponent, exponent for short, of G. If γ(G) does not exist, then denote γ(G) = ∞.
    Let Kn+ be a graph obtained from a complete graph Kn by appending a loop on each
vertex. It is clear that for a graph G without parallel edges of order n, γ(G) = 1 if and
only if G ∼
          = Kn+ .
    Let A be the adjacency matrix of G. Equivalently, the exponent of G is the minimum
integer γ for which Aγ > 0 and Ak ≯ 0 for any positive integer k < γ. Let Ai be the
adjacent matrix of Gi for i = 1, 2. Since for any positive integer k, (A1 ⊗A2 )k = Ak1 ⊗Ak2 ,
by definition, we have the following result immediately.

Proposition 2.1 Let Gi be a primitive graph with exponent γi for i = 1, 2, and G =
G1 ⊗ G2 . Then γ(G) = max{γ1 , γ2}.

    The following lemmas will be used in proofs of our main results.

Lemma 2.1 (Liu et al. [10]) A graph G is primitive if and only if G is connected and
contains odd cycles.

Lemma 2.2 (Liu et al. [10]) Let G be a primitive graph, and let x and y be any pair
of vertices in V (G). If there are two (x, y)-walks P1 and P2 with lengths k1 and k2 ,
respectively, where k1 and k2 have different parity, then γ(G; x, y) ≤ max{k1 , k2 } − 1.

Lemma 2.3 (Delorme and Solé [4]) If G is a primitive graph with diameter d, then
γ(G) ≤ 2d.

Lemma 2.4 (Weichesel [15]) Let G1 and G2 be two connected graphs and G = G1 ⊗ G2 .
Then G is connected if and only if either G1 or G2 contains an odd cycle.

Lemma 2.5 Let G = G1 ⊗ G2 , xi and yi be any two vertices in Gi , Pi be an (xi , yi )-
walk of length ℓi in Gi for i = 1, 2. If ℓ1 and ℓ2 have same parity, then there is an
(x1 x2 , y1y2 )-walk of length max{ℓ1 , ℓ2 } in G.

Proof. Without loss of generality, suppose ℓ1 ≥ ℓ2 . Let k = ℓ1 − ℓ2 . Then k is

Lemma 2.6 Let G be a primitive graph with exponent γ and order n ≥ 2. We have
(i) if γ is odd, then there exist two vertices x and y, and two different vertices u and v,
such that the shortest odd (x, y)-walk and the shortest even (u, v)-walk are of length γ
and γ + 1, respectively;
(ii) if γ is even, then there exist two different vertices p and q, and two vertices w and
s, such that the shortest even (p, q)-walk and the shortest odd (w, s)-walk are of length γ
and γ + 1, respectively.

Proof. (i) Assume that γ is odd. If γ = 1, then G ∼   = Kn+ . Let u and v be two different
vertices in G. Then the shortest odd (u, v)-walk and the shortest even (u, v)-walk are of
length 1 and 2, respectively. Suppose now γ ≥ 3.
    Let A be the adjacency matrix of G. By definition of γ, Aγ−1 ≯ 0 and Aγ−2 ≯ 0.
These imply that there exist four vertices x, y, u and v such that there are no odd
(x, y)-walk and even (u, v)-walk with length γ − 2 and γ − 1, respectively. Hence there
are no odd (x, y)-walk and even (u, v)-walk with length no more than γ − 2 and γ − 1,
respectively. Therefore, the shortest odd (x, y)-walk and the shortest even (u, v)-walk
are of length γ and γ + 1, respectively.
    We now show u 6= v. If u = v, then (u, w, u) is an even (u, v)-walk of length 2 for
any vertex w adjacent to u in G, a contradiction with γ + 1 ≥ 3.
    (ii) Assume γ is even. If γ = 2, then d = d(G) = 1 or 2 since d ≤ γ. If d = 1, then
G is isomorphic to a complete graph Kn with m vertices having loops and m < n. Let
p be a vertex with no loop and q 6= p be another vertex in G. Then the shortest even
(p, q)-walk and odd (p, p)-walk are of length 2 and 3, respectively. If d = 2, then there
exist two different vertices p and q such that dG (p, q) = 2, and hence the shortest even
(p, q)-walk and odd (p, q)-walk are of length 2 and 3, respectively.
    The case when γ > 2 can be proved by applying the similar discussion as in (i).

Lemma 2.7 Let Gi be a primitive graph with exponent γi for i = 1, 2, G = G1 ⊗G2 , and
x = x1 x2 and y = y1 y2 be two different vertices in G. If the shortest odd (resp. even)
(x1 , y1)-walk in G1 and the shortest even (resp. odd) (x2 , y2)-walk in G2 are of length m
and n, respectively, then dG (x, y) ≥ min{m, n}.

Proof. Without loss of generality, assume that m is odd and n is even. Let
                                    P = (x1 x2 , . . . , u1 u2 , . . . , y1 y2 )
be a minimum (x, y)-path with length s in G. Then
                           (x1 , . . . , u1, . . . , y1 ) and (x2 , . . . , u2, . . . , y2 )
be an (x1 , y1 )-walk in G1 and an (x2 , y2 )-walk in G2 , respectively, and both of them are
of length s.
    If s is odd, then s ≥ m since the shortest odd (x1 , y1)-walk in G1 is of length m; If s
is even, then s ≥ n since the shortest even (x2 , y2)-walk in G2 is of length n. Therefore
dG (x, y) = s ≥ min{m, n}.

3     Main results
Let G be a connected graph with odd cycles and C o (G) be the set of all odd cycles in
G. For C ∈ C o (G) and x ∈ V (G), let
                           dG (x, C) = min{dG (x, y) : y ∈ V (C)},
and let
                 doG (C) = max{dG (x, C) : x ∈ V (G − C)} for C ∈ C o (G),
                 lo (G) = min{2doG (C) + |V (C)| − 1 : C ∈ C o (G)}.
We define lo (G) = ∞ if G is bipartite.
Theorem 3.1 γ(G) ≤ lo (G) for any connected graph G.
Proof. If G contains no odd cycles, then lo (G) = ∞, and so the conclusion holds.
Suppose that G contains odd cycles. By Lemma 2.1, G is primitive. We only need to
prove that for any two vertices x and y in G, γ(G; x, y) ≤ lo (G).
    By definition, there exists an odd cycle C such that lo (G) = 2doG (C) + |V (C)| −
1. Let d1 = dG (x, C) and d2 = dG (y, C). Then d1 ≤ doG (C) and d2 ≤ doG (C). Let
Px = (x, x1 , . . . , xd1 ) and Py = (y, y1, . . . , yd2 ) be two shortest paths from x and y to
C, respectively, where xd1 , yd2 ∈ V (C) (maybe xd1 = yd2 ). Two vertices xd1 and yd2
partition C into two paths P1 and P2 with lengths p1 and p2 , respectively. Then p1 and
p2 have different parity, say p1 ≥ p2 . Thus, Px ∪ P1 ∪ Py and Px ∪ P2 ∪ Py are two
(x, y)-walks with length of different parity and at most
                       d1 + d2 + p1 ≤ 2doG (C) + |V (C)| = lo (G) + 1.
By Lemma 2.2, γ(G; x, y) ≤ lo (G).
Corollary 3.1 If G is a connected graph with loops and diameter d, then γ(G) ≤ 2d.
   Let Hn,p and Fn,p (p ≥ 1) be two graphs, which are obtained by joining a complete
graph Kp and a cycle Cp to the end-vertex xn−p of a path Pn−p = (x1 , x2 , . . . , xn−p ) with
an edge, respectively.
   The following result can be deduced by Theorem 3.1.
Corollary 3.2 (Wang and Wang [13]) Let G be a primitive graph with order n and odd
girth p ≥ 3. Then γ(G) ≤ 2n − p − 1 with equality if and only if G is isomorphic to Fn,p .
Proof. By Lemma 2.1, G is connected and contains an odd cycle C with lo (G) =
2doG (C) + |V (C)| − 1. Since doG (C) ≤ n − |V (C)| and |V (C)| ≥ p, by Theorem 3.1, we
have that
                          γ(G) ≤ lo (G) = 2doG (C) + |V (C)| − 1
                                 ≤ 2(n − |V (C)|) + |V (C)| − 1                   (3.1)
                                 ≤ 2n − p − 1.
The equality implies that all equalities in (3.1) hold, in particular, doG (C) = n − |V (C)|
and |V (C)| = p. Thus, there is a vertex x1 such that dG (x1 , C) = n − p in G. Suppose
P = (x1 , x2 , . . . , xn−p , xn−p+1 ) is a shortest path from x1 to C, where xn−p+1 is in C. By
the minimality of P and primitivity of G, it is easy to see that G is isomorphic to Fn,p .
Also, if G is isomorphic to Fn,p , then the shortest odd closed (x1 , x1 )-walk is of length
2(n − p) + p = 2n − p. This implies there is no closed (x1 , x1 )-walk of length 2n − p − 2.
Hence, γ(Fn,p ) ≥ 2n − p − 1.

Corollary 3.3 If p ≥ 3, then γ(Hn,p ) = 2n − 2p + 2.

Proof. Let G = Hn,p . Since G contains Kp and p ≥ 3, G is primitive by Lemma 2.1,
and so doG (C) = dG (x1 , C) = n − p for any C ∈ C o (G). Let C be a cycle of length 3 in
G. By Theorem 3.1,

      γ(G) ≤ lo (G) ≤ 2doG (C) + |V (C)| − 1 = 2(n − p) + |V (C)| − 1 ≤ 2n − 2p + 2.

   It is clear that the shortest odd closed (x1 , x1 )-walk is of length 2(n − p) + 3. This
implies there is no closed (x1 , x1 )-walk of length 2(n − p) + 1. Hence, γ(G) ≥ 2n − 2p + 2.
The conclusion follows.
