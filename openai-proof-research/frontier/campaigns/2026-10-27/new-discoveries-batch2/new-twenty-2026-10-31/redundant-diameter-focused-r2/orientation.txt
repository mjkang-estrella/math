1    Introduction

Let G be a simple undirected graph with vertex set V (G) and edge set E(G).
For v ∈ V (G), the eccentricity, denoted by eG (v), of v is deﬁned as eG (v) =
max {dG (v, x) | x ∈ V (G) }, where dG (v, x) denotes the distance from v to x in G.
The diameter of G, denoted by d(G), is deﬁned as d(G) = max{ eG (v) | v ∈ V (G) }.
   Let D be a digraph with vertex set V (D) and arc set A(D) which has neither loops
nor multiple arcs (that is, arcs with same tail and same head). For v ∈ V (D), the
notions eD (v) and d(D) are deﬁned as in the undirected graph. For x, y ∈ V (D),
we write x → y or y ← x if (x, y) ∈ A(D). For sets X, Y ⊆ V (D), X → Y
denotes {(x, y) ∈ A(D) : x ∈ X and y ∈ Y }. For distinct vertices v1 , v2 , . . . , vk ,
v1 → v2 → · · · → vk represents the directed path in D with arcs v1 → v2 , v2 →
v3 , . . . , vk−1 → vk . For subsets V1 , V2 , . . . , Vk of V, we write V1 → V2 → · · · → Vk
for the set of all directed paths of length k − 1 whose ith vertex is in Vi , 1 ≤ i ≤ k.
For x ∈ V (D) and V  ⊆ V (D), by dD (x, V  ) ≤ k, we mean dD (x, v  ) ≤ k, for all
v ∈ V .

V (G × H) = V (G) × V (H) and E(G × H) = {(u, v)(x, y) : ux ∈ E(G) and vy ∈

G × H, and similarly, for y ∈ V (H), the H-layer Hy of G × H is {(x, y) : x ∈ V (G)}.
  An orientation of a graph G is a digraph D obtained from G by assigning a
direction to each of its edges. By abuse of notation, by D we mean an orientation of
G and also the digraph arising out of the orientation of G.
   A vertex v is reachable from a vertex u of a digraph D if there is a directed
path in D from u to v. An orientation D of G is strong if any pair of vertices
in D are mutually reachable in D. Robbins’ celebrated one-way street theorem [7]
states that a connected graph G has a strong orientation if and only if G is 2-
edge-connected. Throughout this paper, whenever an orientation of a graph G is
considered, we assume that G is 2-edge-connected. For a 2-edge-connected graph G,
let D(G) denote the set of all strong orientations of G. The orientation number of

problem of deciding whether a graph admits an orientation of diameter 2 is NP-hard.
For results on orientations of graphs, see a survey by Koh and Tay [3].
   Let Pn , Cn and Kn denote the path, cycle and complete graph of order n, respec-
tively. Notation and terminology not deﬁned here can be seen in [1].

Theorem 2.1. Let G be a graph and let F be a Z2n -family containing χ (G) sets,
where χ (G) is the edge-chromatic number of G. If d(G) ∈ {2, 3}, then d(G
                                                                        × Kn ) =
d(G × Kn ), and hence ρ(G × Kn ) = 0.


Proof. Let χ (G) = r, F = {A1 , A2 , . . . , Ar } be a Z2n -family and let (E1 , E2 , . . . , Er )
be an r-edge colouring of G. Orient G × Kn so that for every edge uv of G, (u, i) →
{(v, i + ) :  ∈ Aj } whenever uv ∈ Ej . Let D be the resulting digraph. Observe
that, for n ≥ 3:

  (i) if d(G) = 3, then d(G × Kn ) = 3;

 (ii) if d(G) = 2 and there is an edge of G which is not in a triangle of G, then
      d(G × Kn ) = 3;

 (iii) if d(G) = 2 and every edge of G is in a triangle of G, then d(G × Kn ) = 2.

We shall show that d(D) = d(G × Kn ). This implies that ρ(G × Kn ) = 0. To show
that d(D) = d(G × Kn ), it is enough to determine the eccentricity eD ((x, i)) for each
(x, i) in V (D). By the nature of the orientation, we need only consider (x, 0).
  First we claim that, for i ∈ Zn \{0}, dD ((x, 0), (x, i)) ≤ 2.
   Let xy ∈ E(G). Now xy ∈ Ej for some j ∈ {1, 2, . . . , r}. Since Aj is a Z2n -set,
i = (a + b) (mod n) for some a, b ∈ Aj . The existence of the path (x, 0) → (y, a) →
(x, i) in D proves the claim.
  Next, we consider dD ((x, 0), (z, i)), for z ∈ V (G), z = x, and i ∈ Zn .
   Since d(G) ∈ {2, 3}, there exists an (x, z)-path x = v0 , v1 , . . . , vk−1 , vk = z of
length k (≤ 3) in G.
Case 1. k = 2.
  Since xv1 and v1 z are adjacent edges, xv1 ∈ Es and v1 z ∈ Et for some s, t ∈
{1, 2, . . . , r} with s = t. Since {As, At } is a Z2n -pair, any element of Zn can be
expressed as c + d for some c ∈ As and d ∈ At . The existence of the path (x, 0) →
(v1 , c) → (z, c + d) in D shows that dD ((x, 0), Gz ) ≤ 2.
Case 2. k = 2 and d(G) = 3 or d(G) = 2 and there is an edge of G which is not in
a triangle of G.
   If k = 1, then by the above claim, dD ((x, 0), Gx ) ≤ 2 and hence dD ((x, 0), Gz ) ≤ 3.
If k = 3, then by Case 1, dD ((x, 0), Gv2 ) ≤ 2 and hence dD ((x, 0), Gz ) ≤ 3.
Case 3. k = 2, d(G) = 2 and every edge of G is in a triangle of G.
  In this case k = 1, and so xz is an edge of G. Then, by hypothesis, there is a
vertex y such that xyzx is a triangle of G. By Case 1, dD ((x, 0), Gz ) ≤ 2.
  Thus d(D) = d(G × Kn ).

Corollary 2.1. Let G be a graph and let d(G) ∈ {2, 3}. If n ≥ max{2χ (G)+ 5, 15},
then ρ(G × Kn ) = 0.

Corollary 2.2. Let G be a graph and let d(G) ∈ {2, 3}. If χ (G) = 3, then ρ(G ×
Kn ) = 0 for n ≥ 11 or n = 9. If χ (G) = 4, then ρ(G × Kn ) = 0 for n ≥ 13.

Corollary 2.3. Let n ≥ 11 or n = 9. Then ρ(Q3 × Kn ) = 0, where Q3 denotes the
3-cube.
