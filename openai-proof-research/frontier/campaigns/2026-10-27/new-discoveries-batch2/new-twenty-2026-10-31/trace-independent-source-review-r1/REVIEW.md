## 2. Ordinary mathematics: PASS at this review's stated scope

The following is my assessment of the argument, not just the producer's PASS label.

**Admissibility and termination.** Alternating four-factor trace identities vanish on three-dimensional matrices; polarization preserves this property. Full multilinearity and absence of a length-seven factor imply that after substituting a suffix of length k−6, every resulting trace containing it has length at most k−1; every other trace has length at most six. Recursion terminates and preserves multidegree and matrix evaluation. The low-degree step is justified by the source generation theorem and independently checked injectivity, rather than chosen arbitrarily.

**Embedding and low-degree-to-full-CE bridge.** Centering is a triangular invertible change of formal trace coordinates, with `[empty]=3`. All centered E are linear combinations of primitive centered trace coordinates; their linear independence follows from injectivity in their degrees (at most six). Extending independent linear forms to a basis gives polynomial independence in the formal trace algebra; this does not claim matrix invariants are algebraically independent. The exact evaluation/inverse certificates cover all 308 positive-weight monomials through weight six in 80 components, including the ten degree-six E generators. The scalar-zero elimination packet alone covers only E1–35, which suffices at centered weight seven because no centered weight-one generators exist.

For full matrices, the scalar parts and traceless coordinates are independent linear coordinates. Expanding a full-CE weight-seven relation in scalar variables gives centered coefficients of weight at most six whenever scalar degree is positive. Their vanishing follows from the injectivity just checked. Thus such a relation is scalar independent, and the scalar-zero equalities in the packet do lift to full CE. This bridge is valid over C as well as Q: the evaluation matrices have rational inverses and remain invertible after scalar extension.

**Nonzero relation and cancellation.** The ten-term p is nonzero in polynomial CE: its abstract assignment T1=6,T35=1, all other coordinates zero, gives 1. This is a valid separator in CE, not a proposed matrix tuple. The q-to-p equality is an explicit identity modulo products of degree-at-most-six fundamental identities. Those lower identities are killed by the low-degree retraction, so p is an actual matrix relation. Neither a rank computation nor completeness of an elimination ideal is needed. Both stabilizer cases give `R0(F) = -47p/12` and the deformation `-72tp`; t=−47/864 cancels exactly. The frozen verifier re-expands those equalities, including canonical top substitutions.

**Failure of generation.** All selected images are homogeneous of total degree at least seven. In total degree seven only constants may multiply degree-seven generators, and constants cannot change multidegree. The two selected images of weight (3,2,2) vanish. Higher-degree images cannot produce p. Thus the fixed-list ideal has zero component at that weight while the actual kernel contains p≠0. This proves non-generation, a stronger obstruction than redundant generators, for the intended arXiv-v1 statement.

## 3. Novelty and subsumption: HOLD, no demonstrated overlap refutation

I read [Benanti–Drensky, math/0701609v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0701609v1), Proposition 2.4 (especially weight (3,2,2)), Theorem 3.1 and its proof, and Corollary 3.2. They already provide the degree-seven relation module, no relations through degree six, and explicit highest-weight relations. For d=3 the only surviving module is W3(3,2,2); its highest-weight space is one-dimensional. Consequently the surviving weight-(3,2,2) relation is not a new trace relation. Its expression in Hoge coordinates and low-degree independence certificates do not earn discovery credit.

More seriously, **Hoge's own paragraph following Lemma 2.4 already explains erasing a chosen relation by changing an arbitrary retraction**. The new candidate must be distinguished from that public observation. It does make a substantive additional compatibility check: the erasure is effected by one normalized multilinear reduction followed by the prescribed recursion, and both same-weight permutations vanish at one parameter. Neither that paragraph nor Lemma 2.8 proves this constraint automatically. Genericity means the successful locus contains a dense open set; it does not imply its closed complement is nonempty. Conversely the explicit nonzero deformation slope proves nonemptiness here.

There is still a strict-credit concern: adding a polarized known minimal syzygy to a reduction and solving one affine cancellation equation uses standard algebra. Once the slope −72 and stabilizer compatibility are established, the remaining proof is elementary. I did not find a published theorem that automatically supplies these two facts for this fixed list, but I have also not ruled out a short standard representation/polarization argument that makes them routine. **The existence of a named conjecture and 713-term source data does not settle that question.** This review does not clear the candidate as a genuinely new, nonroutine discovery.
