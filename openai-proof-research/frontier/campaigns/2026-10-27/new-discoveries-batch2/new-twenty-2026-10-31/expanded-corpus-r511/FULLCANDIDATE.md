## Elementary lower bound proof
Fix m>=1. Let A be the finite-dimensional real associative algebra with basis the empty word 1 and all words on [m] having no repeated letter. Multiply basis words by concatenation if supports are disjoint, and by zero otherwise; bilinearly extend. Associativity follows because a concatenation of three words is zero exactly when some two supports overlap. Let a_i denote the one-letter basis vector. We have a_i^2=0, so 1+t a_i is invertible with inverse 1-t a_i, for every real t. Let G be the group generated by these units.

Let V be the real span of all conjugates g a_i g^{-1}, g in G. V is invariant under conjugation by G, since Ad_h Ad_g(a_i)=Ad_{hg}(a_i). For v in V,
 Ad_{1+a_i}(v)-Ad_{1-a_i}(v)=2[a_i,v].
This follows by expanding the two products; their quadratic terms cancel (indeed a_i v a_i=0 in this algebra). Hence V is closed under v -> [a_i,v] and contains every right-nested bracket in the a_i.

For each nonempty subset S of size k choose one fixed anchor j in S. The (k-1)! elements
 [a_{i_1},[a_{i_2},...,[a_{i_{k-1}},a_j]...]]
indexed by all orderings of S\{j}, are independent. In the expansion of such a bracket, the only word ending in j is i_1 i_2 ... i_{k-1} j, with coefficient1. Induction proves this: choosing the right term -U a_i at any bracket stage makes the terminal letter i, and further outer brackets cannot change it back to j because no letter repeats. Reading coefficients of words ending j gives an identity matrix. Different S have disjoint homogeneous support components. Thus V contains at least
 D=sum_{k=1}^m binom(m,k)(k-1)!=m+C(m)
independent elements. As V is a span of conjugates, we can select D independent individual conjugates q_l=g_l a_{i_l} g_l^{-1}. This uses ordinary finite linear algebra only, no enumerative computation.

Write each g_l as a finite product of elementary units 1+t a_i. Its inverse is the reverse product with negated coefficients. Form the elementary-product expression
 Q(s)=product_{l=1}^D g_l (1+s_l a_{i_l}) g_l^{-1}.
At s=0 every block is1, so Q(0)=1 and partial Q/partial s_l=q_l. Expand this into a single fixed word W of elementary factors, and now allow EVERY factor weight to vary independently. The polynomial coefficient map F_W:R^r -> A therefore has rank at leastD at the signed parameter vector used above. The s_l weights are zero there; this causes no problem because F_W is polynomial on all real space.

Some D-by-D Jacobian minor is a nonzero real polynomial. A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on the positive orthant: induction on the number of variables reduces this to the univariate finite-root property by considering its coefficients on an open box. Consequently there exists a strictly positive weight vector alpha with rank(dF_W)_alpha >=D. This is an existence argument, not a numerical search. The word contains every symbol: the spanning selection includes singleton directions, and otherwise those directions could not occur in V's selected span.

For positive weights let r_i be the sum of weights at positions labeled i, so every r_i>0. Normalize weights of symbol i by r_i. Selecting one occurrence of each symbol independently gives a probability p_sigma of a permutation sigma. Put atoms at the distinct integer positions of W. Their supports are disjoint across different variables, so the independent variables are noncolliding and p is an actual member of P_m (source Definition5.2).

For each distinct-letter word u with support S, its coordinate in F_W(alpha) is the total weight of all subsequences realizing u. Independent selection of only the symbols in S proves the identity
 [u]F_W(alpha)=(product_{i in S}r_i) sum_{sigma: sigma restricted to S=u}p_sigma(alpha).
Every choice of an occurrence for each i in S has a unique ordering u. Every full choice extends it, and the total normalized weight of choices for missing symbols is1. This pays the marginalization bridge, for every subset including singletons and the empty word. For empty support the identity is1=1.

Thus the full polynomial coefficient map factors, on the positive orthant, as F_W=H(r,p), with H explicitly given by these formulas. By the derivative chain rule,
 rank(dF_W) <= rank(d(r,p)) <= m+rank(dp).
At the chosen positive point rank(dp)>=D-m=C(m). This step subtracts m individual mass parameters, not merely the single scalar multiplying full-length coordinates; overlooking the other marginal coordinates would be invalid.

A nonzero C-by-C minor of dp, fixing the other input coordinates, gives by the inverse function theorem a C-dimensional locally parametrized subset in its image: project to the selected output coordinates and use their local inverse. The domain remains positive in a neighborhood. Since this image lies in P_m, dim(P_m)>=C(m). Combine with actual source Theorem5.13 dim(P_m)<=C(m). This proves the exact all-m endpoint, conditional only on that explicitly imported upper theorem and standard real differential/semialgebraic dimension facts.
