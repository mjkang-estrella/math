375351 Let $k\geq 1$ be an integer. A family $\mathcal F\subseteq\binom{[2n]}{n}$ is **balanced** if every element of $[2n]$ belongs to the same number of members of $\mathcal F$; a balanced subfamily of size $2$ is a complementary pair. 

**Moss–Pedersen conjecture.** For all sufficiently large $n$, there exists a family $\mathcal F\subseteq\binom{[2n]}{n}$ such that

$$
|\mathcal F|=\frac{1}{2}\binom{2n}{n},
$$

$\mathcal F$ contains no balanced subfamily of size $2,4,\ldots,2k$, and $\mathcal F$ contains a balanced subfamily of size $2k+2$.

The first condition makes $\mathcal F$ as large as possible among families containing no complementary pair. The paper’s abstract states that the result is proved constructively, so the conjecture is resolved by the work presented here. SOURCE Sa'ul A. Blanco, “On balanced subfamilies of maximum complement-free families in the middle layer of the Boolean lattice”, arXiv:2606.16172 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16172 

369089 Let $d\geq 1$, let $G$ be a $d$-vertex-connected graph, and let $r$ be a vertex of $G$. A family of spanning trees $T_1,\ldots,T_d$ is said to provide independent paths from $r$ if, for every vertex $v\in V(G)\setminus\\{r\\}$, the paths from $v$ to $r$ in the trees are pairwise internally vertex-disjoint. **Itai–Zehavi conjecture.** For every such $G$ and $r$, there exists a family of $d$ spanning trees $T_1,\ldots,T_d$ providing independent paths from $r$. This conjecture asks for a global strengthening of Menger's theorem, replacing separately chosen disjoint paths by paths organized into spanning trees. The paper proves the conjecture asymptotically with high probability for suitable Erdős–Rényi and random regular graphs, while the general deterministic statement remains open. SOURCE Lawrence Hollom, Lyuben Lichev, Adva Mond, Julien Portier and Yiting Wang, “Approximate Itai-Zehavi conjecture for random graphs”, arXiv:2506.23970 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23970 

369952 For integers $k\geq 2$ and sufficiently large $n$, consider the rigidity matroid ${\mathcal R}_{2k+1}(K_n)$ of the complete graph $K_n$ in dimension $2k+1$. **The matroid matching conjecture.** The matroid ${\mathcal R}_{2k+1}(K_n)$ does not have the matroid matching property for all $k\geq 2$ and sufficiently large $n$. The preceding proof establishes the corresponding even-dimensional result, while the odd-dimensional case is suspected to fail in this way for dimensions at least five and large complete graphs. SOURCE John Hewetson, Bill Jackson, Anthony Nixon and Ben Smith, “k-fold circuits and coning in rigidity matroids”, arXiv:2508.18838 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18838 

371348 Let an **equitable $k$-coloring** be a proper $k$-coloring whose color classes have sizes differing by at most one, and let $d(x)$ denote the degree of a vertex $x$. **Kierstead-Kostochka conjecture.** For $k≥3$, if $G$ is a graph satisfying $d(x)+d(y)≤2k$ for every edge $xy$, and $G$ has no equitable $k$-coloring, then $G$ contains either $K_{k+1}$ or $K_{m,2k-m}$ for some odd $m$.

This is the Ore-type analogue of the Chen-Lih-Wu Conjecture. The paper proves it when $k$ is at least a fixed positive proportion of the number of vertices, while the case $k=o(n)$ remains a stated direction for future work. SOURCE Yangyang Cheng, Zhenyu Li, Wanting Sun and Guanghui Wang, “A step toward Chen-Lih-Wu conjecture”, arXiv:2511.03957 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03957 

371577 Let $P_n$ be the path graph on $n$ vertices, and let $C_n$ be the cycle graph on $n$ vertices. The incompatibility robustness of a graph is denoted by $\eta(\cdot)$.

**Path-graph robustness conjecture.** For all $n\geq 1$,

$$
\eta(P_{2n})=\eta(P_{2n+1})=\eta(C_{2n+2})=\frac{2}{2n+2}\csc\Big(\frac{\pi}{2n+2}\Big).
$$

The prediction is motivated by comparisons with exact values obtained by semidefinite programming and by the asymptotically tight bounds previously established for paths. It remains open whether these equalities hold for every $n\geq 1$. SOURCE Daniel McNulty, “A Graph-Theoretic Approach to Quantum Measurement Incompatibility”, arXiv:2511.15954 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15954 

363627 For $k\geq3$, let a $k$-uniform tight cycle be a cyclic sequence of vertices whose edges are the sets of $k$ consecutive vertices. Consider a red-blue edge-colouring of the complete $k$-graph on $n$ vertices.

**Approximate Lehel conjecture.** There exist constants $c_k\geq1$ and $n_0(k)\in\mathbb N$ such that, for every $n\geq n_0(k)$, the coloured complete $k$-graph contains vertex-disjoint red and blue tight cycles whose union contains at least $n-c_k$ vertices.

The paper proves the weaker bound $n-o(n)$ and notes that the error cannot be zero. A constant error is known for $k=3$, while the conjecture remains open for every $k\geq4$. SOURCE Vincent Pfenninger, “On k-uniform tight cycles: the Ramsey number for C_kn^(k) and an approximate Lehel's conjecture”, arXiv:2406.14468 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14468 

364140 Let $S_m$ be the symmetric group, let $\Delta(S_m)$ be the simplex of probability distributions on $S_m$, and let $P_m\subseteq\Delta(S_m)$ be the locus hit by product measures. A **pure cycle** is a permutation with exactly one nontrivial cycle and all other points fixed. Write

$$
C(m)=\sum_{k=2}^m \frac{m!}{k\cdot (m-k)!}
$$

for the number of pure cycles in $S_m$.

**Dimension conjecture.** For all $m$, the dimension of $P_m$ is exactly $C(m)$.

The paper proves the upper bound $\dim P_m\leq C(m)$ and verifies equality for $m\leq 7$, but the equality is conjectural for general $m$. SOURCE Eric Babson, Moon Duchin, Annina Iseli, Pietro Poggi-Corradini, Dylan Thurston and Jamie Tucker-Foltz, “Models of random spanning trees”, arXiv:2407.20226 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20226 

364945 For a graph $H$, let $r_H(n)$ be the least number of colours in an edge-colouring of $K_n$ with no even-chromatic copy of $H$. For a clique $K_t$, having an even number of edges is equivalent to $t\equiv 0,1\pmod 4$.

**Ge–Xu–Zhang conjecture.** For every positive integer $t\geq 4$ with $t\equiv 0,1\pmod 4$,

$$
r_{K_t}(n)=n^{o(1)}.
$$

For these values of $t$, $K_t$ is not even-decomposable, so this is the clique case predicted by Versteegen's conjecture. The cases $t=4$ and $t=5$ are established in the paper, while the general statement remains open. SOURCE Fredy Yip, “A variant of the Erdős-Gyárfás problem for K_8”, arXiv:2409.16778 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16778 

364946 Given an edge-colouring of $K_n$, a copy of $H$ is **unique-chromatic** if some colour occupies exactly one edge of it. An edge-colouring is $H$-unique if every copy of $H$ is unique-chromatic, and $u_H(n)$ is the least number of colours in an $H$-unique edge-colouring of $K_n$.

**Clique uniqueness conjecture.** For every positive integer $t\geq 2$,

$$
u_{K_t}(n)=n^{o(1)}.
$$

The paper proves a polynomial lower bound for every non-complete graph without isolated vertices, so cliques are conjectured to be precisely the graphs admitting sub-polynomially many colours. The cases $t=4$ and $t=5$ are proved; the remaining clique cases are open. SOURCE Fredy Yip, “A variant of the Erdős-Gyárfás problem for K_8”, arXiv:2409.16778 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16778 

357323 Let $g\in\mathbb{N}$. An intersection graph of lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ has one vertex for each line and edges joining intersecting lines; its girth is the length of its shortest cycle. **Polynomial chromatic-number conjecture for high-girth line graphs.** For every $g\in\mathbb{N}$ there exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that, for every sufficiently large $n$, there exists an intersection graph of $n$ lines in $\mathbb{R}^3$ of girth at least $g$ and chromatic number at least $n^{\varepsilon}$. Davies's result gives arbitrarily large chromatic number at fixed girth, while this conjecture asks for polynomial growth in the number of lines. SOURCE István Tomon, “Coloring lines and Delaunay graphs with respect to boxes”, arXiv:2301.10129 (2023). https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10129
