357324 An intersection graph of lines in $\mathbb{PR}^3$ has one vertex for each projective line and edges joining intersecting lines; it is triangle-free when it has no $3$-cycle. **Polynomial chromatic-number conjecture for projective line graphs.** There exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that, for every sufficiently large $n$, there exists a triangle-free intersection graph of $n$ lines in $\mathbb{PR}^3$ of chromatic number at least $n^{\varepsilon}$. Triangle-free projective-line intersection graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number are known, but the asserted polynomial dependence on $n$ remains open; the source notes that affine constructions rely on parallel lines. SOURCE István Tomon, “Coloring lines and Delaunay graphs with respect to boxes”, arXiv:2301.10129 (2023). https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10129 

358528 Let $G$ be an $N$-vertex graph and $T$ a tournament on the same vertex set. For a vertex $v$, write $N_T^+(v)$ for its out-neighbourhood in $T$.

**Sublogarithmic out-neighbourhood conjecture.** There exists a function $f(N)$ satisfying

$$
f(N)=o(\log N)
$$

such that, for every $N$-vertex graph $G$ with $\chi(G)\geqslant f(N)$ and every tournament $T$ on the same vertex set, there is a vertex $v$ for which

$$
\chi(G[N_T^+(v)])\geqslant 3.
$$

This asks whether a chromatic-number threshold growing strictly slower than $\log N$ suffices to force a non-bipartite out-neighbourhood; the source leaves it open. SOURCE António Girão, Kevin Hendrey, Freddie Illingworth, Florian Lehner, Lukas Michel, Michael Savery and Raphael Steiner, “Chromatic number is not tournament-local”, arXiv:2305.15585 (2023). https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15585 

359140 Let $k\geq4$ be even. A symmetrically colored $k$-AP is a nontrivial progression $n,n+d,\ldots,n+(k-1)d$ whose terms symmetric about the center receive equal colors. **Avoiding symmetrically colored $k$-APs.** For every $N$, there exists an $N^{o(1)}$-coloring of $[N]$ avoiding symmetrically colored $k$-APs. The $k=4$ case would imply the corresponding conjecture for every even $k$, and the source proves that this coloring conjecture would imply the extended uniformity conjecture; it remains open. SOURCE Mingyang Deng, Jonathan Tidor and Yufei Zhao, “Uniform sets with few progressions via colorings”, arXiv:2307.06914 (2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06914 

360248 For a tournament $T$ and an ordering $\prec$ of $V(T)$, let $T^{\prec}$ be its backedge graph. A **BST-ordering** is the ordering associated with a binary search tree satisfying the tournament-neighbourhood conditions described in the paper. **The good ordering conjecture.** There exists a function $f$ such that, for every tournament $T$, there exists an ordering $\prec^*$ of $V(T)$ such that

$$
\omega(T^{\prec^*})\leq f(\operatorname{\overrightarrow{\omega}}(T))\quad\text{and}\quad tww(T,\prec^*)\leq f(tww(T)).
$$

If true, this would imply the bounded twin-width conjecture; the paper notes that BST-orderings are natural candidates because they already provide the required twin-width bound, leaving the backedge-graph clique bound open. SOURCE Pierre Aboulker, Guillaume Aubian, Pierre Charbit and Raul Lopes, “Clique number of tournaments”, arXiv:2310.04265 (2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04265 

361174 Fix integers $m\geq4$ and $n\geq m$. Consider the $m-1$ coordinate hyperplanes in $\mathbb R^{m-1}$ and add $n$ further hyperplanes. **Bounded-region realization conjecture.** For every integer $k$ satisfying

$$
1\leq k\leq\binom{n+m-2}{m-1},
$$

there exist $n$ hyperplanes such that the arrangement of all $m+n-1$ hyperplanes has exactly $k$ bounded regions. The preceding argument establishes the asserted realization for sufficiently large parameters, while the stated uniform range for all $m\geq4$ and $n\geq m$ remains the conjectural part. SOURCE Oliver Clarke, Serkan Hoşten, Nataliia Kushnerchuk and Janike Oldekop, “Matroid Stratification of ML Degrees of Independence Models”, arXiv:2312.10010 (2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10010 

354067 Let $d^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}$ be defined by

$$
\log\left(\tau_b(t,\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{r})\right)=\sum_{n\geq1}\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n}t^n \frac{d^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}}{z_\lambda(1+b)^{\ell(\lambda)}}p_\lambda q_\mu r_\nu.
$$

The coefficients $h^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}$ are related to them by

$$
h^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}=\frac{n d^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}}{z_\lambda(1+b)^{\ell(\lambda)-1}}.
$$

**b-conjecture.** For all partitions $\lambda,\mu,\nu\vdash n\geq 1$, the coefficient $h^\lambda_{\mu,\nu}$ is a polynomial in $b$ with non-negative integer coefficients. The $b$-conjecture is a connected analogue of the Matching-Jack conjecture and concerns the integrality and positivity of the associated coefficients. SOURCE Houcine Ben Dali, “Integrality in the Matching-Jack conjecture and the Farahat-Higman algebra”, arXiv:2203.14879 (2022). https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14879 

354147 Let $d_1\leq\dots\leq d_n$ be the degree sequence of a graph $G$. **The degree-sequence conjecture.** There is a function $f$ such that, for every $\beta>0$, every integer $r\geq 2$, and all sufficiently large $n$, if

$$
d_i\geq i+\beta n
$$

for every $i\leq n/2$, then every $r$-edge-colouring of $G$ admits a partition of $V(G)$ into at most $f(r)$ monochromatic cycles. This conjecture extends Pósa-type Hamiltonicity conditions to coloured cycle partitions; an approximate solution is known for $r=2$, but the general assertion remains open. SOURCE Peter Allen, Julia Böttcher, Richard Lang, Jozef Skokan and Maya Stein, “Partitioning a 2-edge-coloured graph of minimum degree 2n/3 + o(n) into three monochromatic cycles”, arXiv:2204.00496 (2022). https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00496 

356297 A **binary word** is a finite word over the alphabet $\{\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}\}$. For a binary word $\mathfrak{w}$, let $\mathsf{P}(\mathfrak{w})$ denote its number of nonempty subsequences.

**Logarithmic-length conjecture.** For every integer $n\geqslant 1$, there exists a binary word $\mathfrak{w}$ such that

$$
|\mathfrak{w}|=O(\log n)
$$

and

$$
\mathsf{P}(\mathfrak{w})=n.
$$

The Fibonacci-type alternating words show that any binary word with exactly $n$ subsequences must have length $\Omega(\log n)$, so the conjecture asks whether this lower bound is sharp up to a constant factor. The source does not provide a resolution. SOURCE Radosław Żak, “Finding binary words with a given number of subsequences”, arXiv:2210.00342 (2022). https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00342 

349872 Let $g_n$ satisfy

$$
\frac{g_n}{n}\to\theta\in(0,1/2),
$$

and let $\mathbf{T_{n,g_n}}$ be a uniform triangulation of genus $g_n$ with $3n$ edges. An induced subgraph is a **$\kappa$-expander** if every set of vertices has at least $\kappa$ times its size in edges leaving it. **Large expander conjecture.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists a $\kappa>0$ depending only on $\varepsilon$ and $\theta$ such that, with high probability, $\mathbf{T_{n,g_n}}$ contains an induced subgraph $G_n$ with at least $(1-\varepsilon)3n$ edges that is a $\kappa$-expander. This conjecture extends the paper's theorem for uniform unicellular maps to uniform triangulations, and concerns the global geometric structure of high-genus maps. It is presented as an ambitious open problem. SOURCE Baptiste Louf, “Large expanders in high genus unicellular maps”, arXiv:2102.11680 (2021). https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11680 

351449 A **square-free word** is a finite word over a finite alphabet that contains no factor of the form $XX$ for a nonempty word $X$. An **extremal square-free word** is a square-free word none of whose extensions, obtained by inserting one letter at any position, is square-free.

**Grytczuk–Kordulewski–Niewiadomski conjecture.** There exists no extremal square-free word over a finite alphabet of size at least $4$.

This conjecture concerns the possible existence of extremal square-free words over larger alphabets. Infinitely many ternary extremal square-free words are known, while the conjecture was open for every finite alphabet according to the authors. SOURCE Letong Hong and Shengtong Zhang, “No extremal square-free words over large alphabets”, arXiv:2107.13123 (2021). https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13123
