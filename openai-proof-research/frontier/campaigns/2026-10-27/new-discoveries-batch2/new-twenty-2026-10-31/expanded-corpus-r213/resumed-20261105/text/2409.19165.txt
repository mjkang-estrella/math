                                                   Four-coloring Eulerian triangulations of the torus




arXiv:2409.19165v1 [math.CO] 27 Sep 2024

                                                                                         Abstract
                                                       Hutchinson, Richter and Seymour [J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 84 (2002), 225–
                                                   239] showed that every Eulerian triangulation of an orientable surface that has a
                                                   sufficiently high representativity is 4-colorable. We give an explicit bound on the
                                                   representativity in the case of the torus by proving that every Eulerian triangulation
                                                   of the torus with representativity at least 10 is 4-colorable. We also observe that the
                                                   bound on the representativity cannot be decreased to less than 8 as there exists a
                                                   non-4-colorable Eulerian triangulation of the torus with representativity 7.


                                           1       Introduction
                                           Graph coloring is one of the most classical and oldest concepts in graph theory. Recall that
                                           the chromatic number of a graph G, denoted by χ(G), is the smallest k such that G is k-
                                           colorable, i.e., it is possible to assign k colors to the vertices of G so that any pair of adjacent
                                           vertices receive distinct colors; graphs with chromatic number equal to k are referred to as
                                           k-chromatic. The Four Color Theorem [2,13] asserts that every planar graph is 4-colorable.
                                           It is well-known that every Eulerian triangulation of the plane, i.e., a triangulation with
                                           all degrees even, is 3-colorable [5], and in fact, a planar graph is 3-colorable if and only if
                                           it is a subgraph of an Eulerian triangulation of the plane.
                                                We are interested in graphs that can be embedded in surfaces of higher genera in
                                           a locally planar way deﬁned further; we refer the reader to the monograph [11] for the
                                           comprehensive introduction to graphs embeddable in surfaces. The representativity of an
                                           embedded graph G, denoted by r(G), is the smallest number of intersections of a non-
                                           contractible curve with the embedding of G. Thomassen [18] proved that for every surface

   A similar phenomenon as in the case of plane triangulations where Eulerian trian-

Hutchinson [3] to hold for locally planar triangulations of orientable surfaces of higher
genera. The conjecture of Collins and Hutchinson was proven by Hutchinson, Richter and
Seymour [6] who showed that Eulerian triangulations of orientable surfaces that have large
representativity are 4-colorable.

Theorem 1 (Hutchinson, Richter and Seymour [6]). For every orientable surface Σ, there
exists rΣ such that every Eulerian triangulation of Σ with representativity at least rΣ is
4-colorable.

    Since it is not hard to construct 4-chromatic Eulerian triangulations of the torus with
large representativity, the bound on the chromatic number given in Theorem 1 is the best
possible. Note that there are 5-chromatic Eulerian triangulations of the projective plane
with large representativity [6], which follows from the characterization of the chromatic
number of Eulerian triangulations of the projective plane by Mohar [10]; in particular,
any Eulerian triangulation of the projective plane obtained by adding a vertex to each
face of a 4-chromatic quadrangulation of the projective plane is 5-chromatic. Hence, the
condition that the surface Σ is orientable cannot be omitted in Theorem 1. We remark that
Nakamoto [12] showed that every 5-chromatic Eulerian triangulation of a non-orientable
surface with large representativity contains an independent set whose removal results in an
even-faced non-3-colorable graph. We also remark that there are Eulerian triangulations of
the Klein bottle that are 6-chromatic, however, every 6-chromatic Eulerian triangulation
of the Klein bottle contains K6 as a subgraph [7].
    In their paper [6], Hutchinson, Richter and Seymour did not provide any explicit bound
on rΣ and the bounds that follow from their proof would be enormous for surfaces with
large genera as the arguments are based on results from the graph minor project. Our
main result is a reasonably small bound on rΣ in Theorem 1 in the case when Σ is the
torus; as discussed further, the bound of 10 in the statement of the next theorem cannot
be improved to less than 8.

Theorem 2. Every Eulerian triangulation of the torus with representativity at least 10 is
4-colorable.

We remark that in the case of the torus, combining proof techniques used in [6] with
results of Schrijver from [14,15] would likely give a two-digit bound in the case of the torus
although we have not computed the bound exactly (the exact value would also depend on
the amount of ﬁne tuning of the arguments).
    We now brieﬂy overview the above mentioned lower bound of 8 on rΣ from Theorem 1
in the case when Σ is the torus while deferring more detailed discussion to Section 4.

Thomassen [19] classiﬁed 6-critical toroidal graphs and derived as a corollary [19, Theorem
3.3] that a 6-regular triangulation of the torus is 5-colorable unless the triangulation is
K7 or the Cayley graph Γ(Z11 , 4, 5). Using Altshuler’s classiﬁcation of 6-regular toroidal
graphs [1], Yeh and Zhu [21] classiﬁed all 6-regular toroidal graphs with chromatic number
larger than three. In particular, the 6-regular Cayley graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}), which is
depicted in Figure 1, is 5-chromatic. As we discuss in Section 4, the representativity of the
(unique) Eulerian triangulation of the torus given by this graph is 7 and so the bound in
Theorem 2 cannot be decreased to less than 8.
    We next present the overview of the proof of Theorem 2 and discuss how the paper is
structured. Similarly to [6], we analyze cycles with speciﬁc free homotopies and extensions
of their precolorings with three colors. Rather than introducing necessary concepts from [6],
we decided to cast the results needed for our proof in the language of vertex-colorings
and included short proofs for completeness in Section 3; we would like to remark that
extendibility of 3-colorings to partial Eulerian triangulations of the plane has been studied
using various notations [4, 8, 9], also see the survey [16]. In Section 3, we also prove our
main result—Theorem 2. To prove Theorem 2, we need to understand conditions on the
existence of vertex-disjoint cycles with speciﬁc free homotopies. In Section 2, we obtain
such understanding using classical results of Schrijver [14, 15] on the existence of vertex-
disjoint cycles with prescribed free homotopies in graphs embedded in the torus. As in
each of Section 2 and Section 3 we need concepts and notation speciﬁc for that section
only, we introduce these brieﬂy at the beginning of each of the two sections. In Section 4,
we show that the representativity of the (unique) Eulerian triangulation of the torus given

2     Disjoint cycles on the torus
We start with recalling results from [14, 15] on the existence of disjoint homotopic cycles
in graphs embedded in the torus; we refer to [14] for particular results presented below.
We view the torus as R2 /Z2 and let Cm,n for (m, n) ∈ Z2 be the closed curve in the torus
parameterized as (m · t mod 1, n · t mod 1) for t ∈ [0, 1]. Every closed curve in the torus
is freely homotopic to Cm,n for some (m, n) ∈ Z2 , see e.g. [17].
    Fix a graph G embedded in the torus for our presentation until Theorem 4, and let
cG (m, n) be the minimum number of intersections of a closed curve freely homotopic to Cm,n
with the graph G. Note that cG (0, 0) = 0 and the representativity of G is the minimum
value of cG (m, n) taken over all non-zero pairs (m, n) ∈ Z2 . Deﬁne a convex set P (G) as

                   P (G) = {(x, y) ∈ R2 such that mx + ny ≤ cG (m, n)}.

Note that the set P (G) is symmetric and bounded; the former follows from that cG (m, n) =
cG (−m, −n) for all (m, n) ∈ Z2 , and the latter from ﬁniteness of cG (0, 1) = cG (0, −1) and
cG (1, 0) = cG (−1, 0). It can be shown [14, Section 4] that

                    max{mx + ny such that (x, y) ∈ P (G)} = cG (m, n)                      (1)

for any (m, n) ∈ Z2 . The following theorem of Schrijver [14] relates the existence of disjoint
cycles with a given free homotopy to the set P (G).
Theorem 3 (Schrijver [14, Theorem 2]). Let G be a graph embedded in the torus. For
any non-zero (m, n) ∈ Z2 such that m and n are coprime and any k ∈ N, it holds that
(kn, −km) ∈ P (G) if and only if the graph G contains k pairwise disjoint cycles freely
homotopic to Cm,n .
    Recall that the polar of the set P (G) is the set P (G)∗ deﬁned as

          P (G)∗ = {(x′ , y ′) ∈ R2 such that xx′ + yy ′ ≤ 1 for all (x, y) ∈ P (G)}.

If X is a compact symmetric subset of R2 , we deﬁne λ(X) as

          λ(X) = min{t ≥ 0 such that t · X contains a non-zero integer point}.

It can be shown [14, Section 5] that λ(P (G)∗ ) is equal to the representativity r(G) of the
graph G. Since it holds for any compact symmetric subset of R2 that λ(X)λ(X ∗) ≤ 4/3 [14,
Theorem 4], we obtain that λ(P (G))−1 ≥ 3r(G)/4, which is equivalent to the following
theorem.

Theorem 6. Let G be a graph embedded in the torus with representativity at least 10.
There exist two free homotopy classes h1 and h2 such that each of the following holds:

   • the graph G contains four disjoint cycles each from h1 ,

   • the graph G contains four disjoint cycles each from h2 ,

   • the graph G contains four disjoint cycles each from h1 + h2 , and

   • the graph G contains four disjoint cycles each from h1 − h2 .

Proof. Fix a graph G as in the statement of the theorem. By Theorem 4, there exist non-
zero (a, b) ∈ Z2 such that (7.5a, 7.5b) ∈ P (G). By choosing a suitable parameterization
of the torus, we may assume that a = 0. It follows that the set P (G) contains the point
(0, 7.5) (note that if b ≥ 2, the point (0.7.5) belongs to P (G) as it is a convex combination
combination of the point (0, 0) and (0, 7.5b)) and so the set P (G) also contains the point
(0, −7.5).
     We now argue that we may assume that P (G) contains the point (10, −y0) for some
y0 ∈ [0, 5]. Since the representativity of G is at least 10, it holds by (1) that cG (m, n) ≥ 10
for every non-zero (m, n) ∈ Z2 . In particular, applying (1) with (m, n) = (1, 0) yields that
the set P (G) contains a point (10, z) for some z ∈ R. Since reparameterizing the torus
using the mapping (x, y) 7→ (x, y + α · x mod 1) for α ∈ Z transforms the set P (G) by
the mapping (x, y) 7→ (x, y − α · x), we can assume that z ∈ [−5, +5]. Finally, if z ≥ 0, we
reparameterize the torus using the mapping (x, y) 7→ (x, −y). We conclude that we may
assume that the set P (G) contains both points (0.7.5) and (10, z) where z ∈ [−5, 0] and se
we can set y0 = −z.

some x0 ∈ R, and applying (1) with (m, n) = (1, 1) yields that P (G) contains a point
(t0 , 10 − t0 ) for some t0 ∈ R. Lemma 5 now implies that the set P (G) contains one of the

   We next verify that the type of a closed walk in an Eulerian triangulation of the torus
depends only on its free homotopy.

Proposition 7. Let G be an Eulerian triangulation of the torus. If two closed walks W1
and W2 are freely homotopic, then the types of W1 and W2 are the same.

Proof. Recall that any two freely homotopic closed walks can be transformed to each other
by a sequence of the following two types of operations on subwalks or the reverses of these
two operations: replacing a with aba where b is a neighbor of a, and replacing ab with
acb where abc is a triangular face. Since the former operation preserves the value assigned
to the vertex preceding a, the vertex a itself, and the vertex following a in the walk (see
Figure 13 for illustration of possible cases) and the latter operation preserves the value
assigned to the vertex preceding a, the vertices a and b, and the vertex following b (see
Figure 14 for illustration of possible cases), the type of a closed walk is not altered by any
of these two operations. It follows that the types of any two freely homotopic closed walks
are the same.
   Proposition 7 yields that we can deﬁne a function τ from free homotopy classes of closed
curves to S3 by setting τ (h) to be the common type of all closed walks of G that belong to
h. Consider two closed walks W1 = v1 , . . . , vk and W2 = w1 , . . . , wℓ such that v1 = w1 and
vk = vℓ . Since the type of the closed v1 , . . . , vk w1 , . . . , wℓ is the composition of the types of
W1 and W2 , we obtain that τ is a homomorphism from the group of free homotopy classes
to S3 .

   We next focus on completing a graph obtained from an Eulerian triangulation on the
torus to an Eulerian triangulation of the plane. Recall that a graph obtained by cutting
along a non-contractible cycle is the graph embedded in the surface obtained by cutting
the surface along the curve corresponding to the cycle while duplicating all vertices and
edges of the cycle and embedding the two copies of the cycle along the boundaries created
by the cutting. We remark that the implication stated in Proposition 8 is actually an
equivalence but we only present the argument for the implication, which we need in the
proof of Theorem 2.

Proposition 8. Let G be an Eulerian triangulation of the torus and let C be a non-
contractible cycle of G. If the type of the cycle C is the identity, the graph obtained from
the Eulerian triangulation G by cutting along C, which is embedded in the cylinder, can be
completed to an Eulerian triangulation of the plane.

Proof. Fix an Eulerian triangulation G of the torus and a non-contractible cycle C of G
as in the statement, and consider the graph obtained from G by cutting along C. Paste
two copies of a disk to obtain a plane graph, let C1 and C1′ be the two copies of the cycle
C, and let f1 and f1′ be the two faces bounded by C1 and C1′ , respectively. Observe that
all vertices except those on C1 and C1′ have even degrees.
    We now show that it is possible to add new vertices and edges inside the face f1 in a
way that all vertices inside the face f1 and all vertices on C1 have even degrees and all
newly created faces inside f1 are triangles. If the length of C1 is three and its type is the
identity, then none of the three vertices of C1 is bad, i.e., their degrees are even and no
vertices and edges need to be added. Consider now the mapping ϕC1 from the deﬁnition
of a type of a closed walk; since the type of C1 is identity, the mapping is the same (up to
permutation of colors) regardless the choice of a starting vertex. If the mapping ϕC1 uses
only two colors, each vertex of C1 is bad, i.e., its degree is odd, and the length of C1 is
even as its type is the identity. In this case, we add a single new vertex w and join it to
each vertex of C1 .

uses three colors. The latter implies that there exist three vertices v, v ′ and v ′′ consecutive
on the cycle C1 such that the colors ϕC1 (v), ϕC1 (v ′ ) and ϕC1 (v ′′ ) are distinct; in particular,

Proof of Theorem 2. Fix an Eulerian triangulation G of the torus that has representativity
at least ten. We show that there exists a free homotopy class h0 such that G contains four
disjoint cycles each from h0 and τ (h0 ) is the identity. Apply Theorem 6 to G, and let h1
and h2 be the two free homotopy classes from the statement of the theorem. If τ (h1 ) or
τ (h2 ) is the identity, then we have established the existence of the sought free homotopy
class h0 . Since the group of free homotopy classes is abelian and τ is its homomorphism
to S3 , the subgroup of S3 that is the image of τ has either two or three elements. In the
former case, τ (h1 + h2 ) is the identity and so we can set h0 = h1 + h2 . In the latter case,
τ (h1 − h2 ), τ (h1 ) and τ (h1 + h2 ) are diﬀerent elements of this 3-element subgroup and so
one of them is the identity, which yields the choice of h0 .
    Fix four vertex-disjoint cycles C1 , C2 , C3 and C4 of G that belong to the free homotopy
class h0 . Let G′ be the graph obtained from G by cutting along the cycle C1 ; note that
the graph G′ is embedded in the cylinder. Let C1′ and C1′′ be the two copies of C1 on the
boundary of the cylinder. By Proposition 8, the graph G′ can be completed to an Eulerian
triangulation of the plane and so G′ is 3-colorable. Fix a vertex-coloring c of G′ using the
colors 0, 1 and 2. Observe that the coloring c restricted to C1′ or to C1′′ is the function
ϕC1 with the colors 0, 1 and 2 permuted, however, possibly by a diﬀerent permutation for

     Let V0 be the set of the vertices of the cycle C1′ , V1 the set of all vertices of G′ between

4     Lower bound
In this section, we show that the graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}) is a non-4-colorable Eulerian
triangulation of the torus with representativity seven. As mentioned in Section 1, the
chromatic number of the graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}) was determined in [21], however, we
prefer including a short proof for completeness. In addition, we believe that the proof also
highlights particular symmetries that the graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}) possesses.
    We formulate and prove the bounds on the representativity and the chromatic number
of the graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}) as propositions.

Proposition 9. The representativity of the unique embedding of the graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11})

±1, ±10 and ±11 such that ai = vi+1 − vi mod 37. Since v1 , . . . , vℓ is a cycle in the graph
Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}), it holds that

is at least seven (this is again tight as 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 11 + 11 + 11 = 37). The proof of the

Proof. We will prove that the graph Γ(Z37 , {1, 10, 11}) has no independent set of size ten,

5    Conclusion
Unfortunately, the techniques used in Sections 2 and 3 do not seem to suﬃce for improving
the bound on the representativity in Theorem 2 unless a new way of getting insights linking
the structure of a graph G and the set P (G) is found. Indeed, consider the set X ⊆ R2
given by the following four linear constraints:

                                            4x + 3y ≤ 27
                                           −2x + 3y ≤ 27
                                           −4x − 3y ≤ 27
                                            2x − 3y ≤ 27

The set X is visualized in Figure 20. It is not clear how to rule out that this set is the
set P (G) for an Eulerian triangulation G of the torus with representativity 9 as the value
of (1) for all non-zero (m, n) ∈ Z2 is at least 9. On the other hand, the set X does not
contain any four integer points that would imply the conclusion of Theorem 6. At the
same time, it seems tricky to relate the structure of a possible Eulerian triangulation G to
the information encoded by the shape of P (G) in a way that would establish 4-colorability
of G without cutting along four disjoint cycles as in the proof of Theorem 2.
