                                                                                            Abstract
                                                     An involution in a Coxeter group has an associated set of involution words, a variation on
                                                 reduced words. These words are saturated chains in a partial order first considered by Richardson
                                                 and Springer in their study of symmetric varieties. In the symmetric group, involution words
                                                 can be enumerated in terms of tableaux using appropriate analogues of the symmetric functions
                                                 introduced by Stanley to accomplish the same task for reduced words. We adapt this approach
                                                 to the group of signed permutations. We show that involution words for the longest element in
                                                 the Coxeter group Cn are in bijection with reduced words for the longest element in An = Sn+1 ,
                                                 which are known to be in bijection with standard tableaux of shape (n, n − 1, . . . , 2, 1).

2.1         Symmetric functions
Fix a partition λ = (λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ · · · ≥ λk > 0). The Young diagram of λ is the set of pairs
      def
Dλ = {(i, j) : i ∈ [k] and j ∈ [λi ]}, which we envision as a collection of left-justified boxes oriented
as in a matrix. A semistandard tableau of shape λ is a filling of the boxes of the Young diagram
Dλ by positive integers, such that each row is weakly increasing from left to right and each column
is (strictly) increasing from top to bottom. Such a tableau is standard if its boxes contain exactly
the numbers 1, 2, . . . , |λ|.
     Similarly, a marked semistandard tableau of shape λ is a filling of the Young diagram of λ by
numbers from the alphabet of primed and unprimed positive integers {1, 2, 3, . . . } ⊔ {1′ , 2′ , 3′ , . . . }
such that (i) the rows and columns are weakly increasing under the order 1′ < 1 < 2′ < 2 < · · · ,
(ii) no unprimed letter i appears twice in the same column, and (iii) no primed letter i′ appears
twice in the same row.
     Assume λ is a strict partition, i.e., has all distinct parts. A marked semistandard shifted tableau
of shape λ is a filling of the shifted Young diagram {(i, i + j − 1) : (i, j) ∈ Dλ } with primed and
unprimed positive integers satisfying properties (i)-(iii) from the previous paragraph. A semistan-
dard marked (shifted) tableau T of shape λ is standard if exactly one of i or i′ appears in T for
each i = 1, 2, . . . , |λ|.
     Given a (marked) semistandard (shifted) tableau T , write xT for the monomial formed by
replacing the boxes in T containing i or i′ by xi and then multiplying the resulting variables.

2.3   Transition formulas
We use the term word to refer to a finite sequence of nonzero integers. The one-line representation
of a signed permutation w ∈ Cn is the word w1 w2 · · · wn where we set wi = w(i). We usually write
m in place of −m so that, for example, the eight elements of C2 are 12, 12, 12, 1 2, 21, 21, 21, and
2 1. In this notation, the longest element of Cn is

                                                     wnC = 1 2 3 · · · n.

The map w1 w2 · · · wn 7→ w1 w2 · · · wn (n+1) is an inclusion Cn ֒→ Cn+1 . We do not distinguish

1)(n + 2) · · · (n + m) represent the same signed permutation for all m ∈ N.

6     Future directions
6.1     Geometry
There are geometric connections in type A for which we do not know type C analogues. The
type A involution Stanley symmetric function F̂w is a limit of involution Schubert polynomials,
which are known to represent the cohomology classes of the orbit closures of On (C) acting on the
type A complete flag variety. One can also define type C involution Schubert polynomials, which
represent cohomology classes on the type C isotropic flag variety insofar as they are positive integer
combinations of type C Schubert polynomials, but we do not know a more interesting description
of these classes.

6.2     Positivity
As mentioned in Section 2.2, F̂y is not only Schur-positive but Schur-Q-positive, with integral
coefficients up to a predictable scalar [12]. It would be interesting to find a similar expression for
Ĝy as a positive combination of some Schur-Q-positive symmetric functions in a nontrivial way.
Theorem 1.6 accomplishes this for ĜwnC , because the Schur S-functions are Schur-Q-positive (they
are in fact skew Schur Q-functions), but usually Ĝy is not Schur-S-positive.

6.3     Type D analogues
