                                                                                                     Abstract
                                                     Given a digraph D together with an ordering ≺ of its vertices, the backedge graph of D with respect to ≺
                                                 is the undirected graph D≺ with the same vertex set as D, where xy ∈ E(D≺ ) if xy ∈ A(D) and y ≺ x.
                                                 We introduce the notion of the clique number of a digraph D, defined as the minimum clique number over all
                                                 backedge graphs of D. We investigate its relationship with the dichromatic number. In particular, this concept
                                                 allows us to define −→
                                                                      χ -bounded classes of digraphs, which constitute the main topic of this paper, with a primary
                                                 focus on tournaments. A class of tournaments is −→
                                                                                                  χ -bounded if, for every tournament in the class, its dichromatic
                                                 number is bounded by a function of its clique number. We study for which tournaments H the class of H-free
                                                 tournaments is − →χ -bounded, and prove in particular that H must have a backedge graph that is a forest. We
                                                 prove that if a class of tournaments is −
                                                                                         →
                                                                                         χ -bounded, then so is its closure under substitution. We also explore the
                                                                        −
                                                                        →
                                                 relationship between χ -bounded classes of tournaments and certain conjectures on tournaments. We prove that
                                                 a−→χ -bounded class of tournaments satisfies the BIG ⇒ BIG Conjecture, and that a polynomially −       →
                                                                                                                                                        χ -bounded
                                                 class of tournaments satisfies the (tournament) Erdős-Hajnal Conjecture.

                                           1    Introduction
                                           In this paper, we only consider graphs or directed graphs (digraphs in short) with no loops, no parallel edges or
                                           arcs nor anti-parallel arcs (in particular our digraphs contain no cycle of length 2).
                                               Given an undirected graph G, we denote by ω(G) the size of a maximum clique of G and by χ(G) its chromatic
                                           number. Given a digraph D, we denote by −     →
                                                                                         χ (D) its dichromatic number, that is the minimum integer k such that
                                           the set of vertices of D can be partitioned into k acyclic subdigraphs.

2    Definitions and Notations
Definitions and notations of this paper that are not explained in this section follow from classical textbooks such
as [9], [12] or [19].
    A k-dicolouring of a digraph is a partition of its vertex set into k subsets inducing acyclic subdigraphs.
Alternatively, it is a function ϕ : V (D) → [k] such that D[ϕ−1 (c)] is acyclic for every colour c ∈ [k]. A digraph
is k-dicolourable if it has a k-dicolouring. The dichromatic number of a digraph D, denoted by ⃗        χ(D), is the
least integer k such that D is k-dicolourable.
    If xy ∈ A(D), we say that x is an in inneighbour of x and y an outneighbour of x, and that x and y are
adjacent. The set of outneighbours (resp. inneighbours) of x is denoted by N + (x) (respectively N − (x)) and we
denote by N (x) the set N + (x) ∪ N − (x). For a set of vertices X, N + (X) = ∪x∈X N + (x) \ X, N − (X) =
∪x∈X N − (x) \ X, N (X) = N + (X) ∪ N − (X), N + [X] = N + (X) ∪ X and N − [X] = N − (X) ∪ X.
    Given two disjoint sets of vertices X, Y of a digraph D, we write X ⇒ Y to say that for every x ∈ X and for
every y ∈ Y , xy ∈ A(D), and we write X → Y to say that every arc with one end in X and the other one in Y is
oriented from X to Y (but some vertices of X might be non-adjacent to some vertices of Y ). When X = {x} we
write x ⇒ Y and x → Y .
    We also use the symbol ⇒ to denote a composition operation on digraphs: for two digraphs D1 and D2 ,
D1 ⇒ D2 is the digraph obtained from the disjoint union of D1 and D2 by adding all arcs from V (D1 ) to V (D2 ).
    A dominating set of a digraph D is a set of vertices X such that N + [X] = V (D). The dominating number
dom(D) of D is the size of a smallest dominating set of D.
    A tournament is an orientation of a complete graph. A transitive tournament is an acyclic tournament and we
denote by T Tn the unique acyclic tournament on n vertices.
    Given three tournaments T1 , T2 , T3 , we denote by ∆(T1 , T2 , T3 ) the tournament obtained from disjoint copies
of T1 , T2 , T3 by adding arcs in such a way that T1 ⇒ T2 , T2 ⇒ T3 and T3 ⇒ T1 . If one or more of the
tournaments Ti is a transitive tournament T Tk , we simplify the notation by using its size k instead of writing
T Tk in the ∆ construction: for example, ∆(1, k, T ) corresponds to ∆(T T1 , T Tk , T ) and ∆(1, 1, 1) is simply the
directed triangle, which we also denote by C  ⃗ 3.
    A class of graphs (resp. digraphs) is a set of graphs (resp. digraphs) that is closed under induced subgraphs,
meaning that if G belongs to the set, then any induced subgraph of G also belongs to the set. Given a set of
(di)graphs C the hereditary closure of C is the class of all induced sub(di)graphs of elements of C.
    Given a set of tournaments F, we say that a tournament T is F -free if it contains no member of F as a
subtournament. We denote by F orb(F ) the class of F-free tournaments. We write F orb(F1 , . . . , Fk ) instead of
F orb({F1 , . . . , Fk }) for simplicity.

Twin-width is a parameter introduced in [14] to measure the complexity of binary relational structures. We refer to
Sections 2.2 and 2.6 of [21] for the general definitions of twin-width in the context of binary relational structures.
    Since this paper focuses on specific structures, we reformulate these definitions for the cases where the struc-
ture, denoted by S, is a graph, an ordered graph, a tournament or an ordered tournament. A contraction sequence
for S is a sequence Pn , Pn−1 . . . , P1 of partitions of the set of vertices of S where Pn is the partition into single-
tons, P1 is the partition into a single part, and Pi−1 is obtained by merging two parts of Pi . For a given part X
of Pi , the error degree of X in (S, Pi ) is the number of parts Y ∈ Pi \ X that are not homogeneous to X. Let
us detail the meaning of not being homogeneous in the cases of graphs, tournaments, ordered graphs and ordered
tournaments. We say that X ̸≺ Y if there is x ∈ X and y ∈ Y such that y ≺ x.
      • If S is a graph, then both (X × Y ) ∩ E(S) and (X × Y ) \ E(S) are non-empty.

      • If S is a tournament, then both (X × Y ) ∩ A(S) and (Y × X) ∩ A(S) are non-empty.
      • If S = (G, ≺) is an ordered graph, then either:
           – (X × Y ) ∩ E(G) and (X × Y ) \ E(G) are both non-empty, or
           – X ̸≺ Y and Y ̸≺ X.

      • If S = (T, ≺) is an ordered tournament, then either:
           – (X × Y ) ∩ E(S) and (X × Y ) \ E(S) are both non-empty, or
           – X ̸≺ Y and Y ̸≺ X.

The width of the contraction sequence is the maximum error degree of (S, Pi ) over all choices of i ∈ {1, . . . , n}.
Finally, the twinwidth of S, denoted by tww(S), is the minimum width of a contraction sequence for S.
   Classes of graphs of bounded twin-width have been shown to be χ-bounded [13, 42], and even polynomially
χ-bounded [15].
Theorem 3.12 ([15])
      For every k ≥ 1, the class of undirected graphs with twin-width at most k is polynomially χ-bounded.

We did not give much thought yet to the clique number of digraphs. On this matter, it is to be noted that Theo-
rem 3.3 does not hold for digraphs. Indeed, take a triangle-free (undirected) graph G with large chromatic number,
say χ(G) = k. Let ≺ be an ordering of V (G) and let G    ⃗ be the digraph obtained from G by orienting each edge
from right to left with respect to ≺. Then χ(T )/ω(T ≺ ) = χ(G)/ω(G) = k/2 > −
                                                 ≺                                       →χ(⃗G) = 1. Since most of
the results we have proven for tournaments rely on Theorem 3.3, we believe that studying the clique number of
digraphs may not be as fruitful as studying the clique number of tournaments.
     On the positive side, we can extend Theorem 3.3 to digraphs with bounded independence number, where the
independence number of a digraph D is the size of a maximum independent set of (the underlying graph) of D.
Since tournaments are precisely oriented graphs with independence number 1, it is indeed a generalisation of
Theorem 3.3. Note that many results on tournaments extend to digraphs with bounded independence number. See
for example [25, 33].

all integers i, j by Ramsey’s Theorem.

    An obvious topic that we did not investigate is the complexity of computing the clique number. Nguyen,
Scott and Seymour ask for the complexity of computing the clique number of a tournament in Section 9 of [41].
Between the time we submitted the paper and the time we receive the reviews, the second author proved [7] that
for any k ≥ 3 fixed, deciding if the clique number of a tournament is at most k is NP-complete. The question for
k = 2 remains open.
