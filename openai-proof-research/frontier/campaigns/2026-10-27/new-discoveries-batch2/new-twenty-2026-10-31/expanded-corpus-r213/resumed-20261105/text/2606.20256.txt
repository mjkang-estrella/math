                                                                                                     Abstract
                                                     In this paper, we investigate the tree-independence number of graph classes that do not contain K1,d
                                                 as an induced subgraph. Dallard et al. conjectured that for any positive integer d and any planar graph H,
                                                 the class of all K1,d -free graphs without H as an induced minor has bounded tree-independence number.
                                                 Our main contribution towards this conjecture is showing that the conjecture holds for outerstring graphs.
                                                 Additionally we give linear and quadratic bounds for the tree-independence number of various K1,d -free
                                                 graph classes, sharpening previous bounds. Finally, we bound the tree-independence number of K2,d -free
                                                 graphs additionally forbidding holes of length at least 5.


                                           1 Introduction
                                           In this paper we study a relatively recent graph width parameter called the tree-independence number , denoted
                                           by α-tw in certain graph classes excluding a star (i.e., a graph K1,d for some integer d) as an induced
                                           subgraph. Tree-independence number was introduced independently by Yolov [Yol18] and Dallard, Milanič, and
                                           Štorgel [DMŠ24a]. Roughly speaking, it measures how similar a graph is to being chordal. It is defined similarly
                                           as treewidth, except that instead of measuring the cardinality of each bag of a tree decomposition (minus 1),
                                           we measure the independence number of the graph induced by the set of vertices contained in each bag. It is
                                           known that boundedness of tree-independence number gives a sufficient condition for polynomial-time solvability
                                           of many algorithmic problems related to independent sets, including the Maximum Weight Independent
                                           Packing problem and, as a consequence, the Maximum Weight Independent Set and the Maximum
                                           Weight Induced Matching problems (see [dMM+ 26, DMŠ24a, Yol18]).
                                           Given two graphs G and H, we say that G is H-free if it does not contain an induced subgraph isomorphic
                                           to H. Moreover, for a class F of graphs, we say that G is F-free if for every graph F ∈ F, G is F -free.
                                           Similarly, we say that a graph class G is H-free if every graph G ∈ G is H-free, and G is F-free if every graph
                                           G ∈ G is F -free.
                                           Recently, a result giving a complete characterisation of graph classes with bounded tree-independence number,
                                           defined by forbidding a finite set of graphs as an induced subgraph, was shown by Hajebi and Spirkl [HS26].
                                           Let S be the family of all graphs whose every connected component is a tree with at most three leaves.

                                             Theorem 1 (Hajebi and Spirkl [HS26]). Let F be a finite set of graphs. Then the class of F-free graphs
                                             has bounded tree-independence number if and only if F contains a complete bipartite graph, a graph
                                             from S and a line graph of a graph from S.

Note that the above results do not give much insight in terms of the upper bound on the tree-independence num-

The aim of this paper is to focus on specific upper bounds for various graph classes. Specifically, we will
consider graph classes forbidding K1,d as an induced subgraph. The importance of K1,d -free graph classes can
be seen as follows. Korhonen [Kor23] showed that an induced variant of the celebrated Grid-Minor Theorem by
Robertson and Seymour [RS86] holds for graphs with bounded maximum degree.

  Theorem 2 ([Kor23]). There exists a function f : N2 → N such that for every positive integer k and
  every graph G, if tw(G) > f (∆(G), k), then G contains the (k × k)-grid as an induced minor.

In their paper, Dallard et al. [DKK+ 24] posed a conjecture that, if true, would generalise Theorem 2.

  Conjecture 1 ([DKK+ 24]). Let d be a positive integer and let G be a hereditary K1,d -free graph class.
  Then G has bounded tree-independence number if and only if there exists a positive integer k such that G
  excludes the (k × k)-grid as an induced minor.

Chudnovsky et al. [CCPR25] showed that for any d and every planar graph H, the class G of H-induced-minor-
free K1,d -free graphs has polylogarithmic tree-independence number. Additionally, it is known that the broader
class of H-induced-minor-free graphs has a bounded tree-independence number for the following cases.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we present some important definitions and notation. Then, in
Section 3 we investigate several K1,d -free graph classes, namely the classes of tolerance, co-comparability, and
AT-free graphs, as well as a particular case of bisplit graphs, and show that they have bounded tree-independence
number with a linear upper bound in terms of d. In Section 4 we then prove that classes of K1,d -free outerstring
and, more generally, k-outerstring graphs have bounded tree-independence number by showing some explicit
upper bounds. We continue in Section 5 by showing that classes of K1,d -free circle containment graphs and
rook graphs have unbounded tree-independence number. Several results from Sections 3, 4, and 5 are captured
in Figure 1. In Section 6 we then turn our attention to K2,d -free graphs and generalise some results from
Section 3 by showing that when excluding all cycles of length at least 5 such graph classes have bounded
tree-independence number, again with a linear upper bound in terms of d. Finally, in Section 7 we present a
couple of open problems.

2 Preliminaries
All graphs considered in this paper are finite, simple (i.e., without loops or parallel edges), and undirected. We
write V (G) and E(G) for the vertex set and edge set of a graph G, respectively.
For v ∈ V (G), the set NG (v) = {u ∈ V (G) : vu ∈ E(G)} is the neighbourhood of S  v, and NG [v] = N (v)∪{v}
is the closed neighbourhood of v. Similarly, for S ⊆ V (G), we write NG [S] = v∈S NG [v] and NG (S) =
NG [S] \ S. The degree of a vertex v ∈ V (G), denoted by dG (v), is the number of edges incident with v, or
equivalently, dG (v) = |N (v)|. If the graph is clear from the context, we may just write N (v), N [v], N (S),
N [S] and d(v) instead of NG (v), NG [v], NG (S), NG [S] and dG (v), respectively.
An independent set in a graph G is a set of pairwise nonadjacent vertices. The independence number of a
graph G, denoted by α(G), is the cardinality of a largest independent set in G. A clique in a graph G is a set
of vertices such that every two distinct vertices are adjacent. The clique number of a graph G, denoted by
ω(G), is the size of a largest clique in G.
A graph H is an induced subgraph of a graph G if a graph isomorphic to H can be obtained from G by a
sequence of vertex deletions. Given a set S ⊆ V (G), we denote by G[S] the subgraph of G induced by S. For
every set S ⊆ V (G), we denote by G − S the graph G[V (G) \ S], and similarly, for every u ∈ V (G), G − u
corresponds to the graph G[V (G) \ {u}]. Given a graph G and an edge uv in G, contracting the edge uv
means replacing u and v with a single new vertex w so that the vertices adjacent to w are exactly the vertices
of G that are adjacent to u or v. A graph H is an induced minor of a graph G if a graph isomorphic to H can
be obtained from G by a sequence of vertex deletions and edge contractions. If a graph G does not contain a
graph H as an induced minor, then we say that G is H-induced-minor-free.
A complete graph of order n is denoted by Kn . Given two integers m, n ≥ 0, the complete bipartite graph
Km,n is a bipartite graph with parts of size m and n, such that two vertices are adjacent if and only if they
belong to different parts. The path graph and the cycle graph with n vertices are denoted by Pn and Cn ,
respectively. Given a graph G and an integer k ≥ 0, a path of length k in G is a sequence P = v1 . . . vk+1 of
pairwise distinct vertices of G such that vi vi+1 ∈ E(G) for all i = 1, . . . , k. A path v1 . . . vk with k ≥ 3 and
vk v1 ∈ E(G) is a cycle of length k in G. An edge vi vj ∈ E(G) with |i − j| > 1 is a chord of the cycle; the
cycle is said to be chordless if it has no chords. Given two disjoint subsets of vertices A and B, a set S is a
separator between A and B in a graph G if there is no path between a vertex from A and a vertex from B in
the graph G − S. We remark that possibly S ∩ A ̸= ∅ or S ∩ B ̸= ∅.
A tree decomposition of a graph G is a pair T = (T, {Xt }t∈V (T ) ) where T is a tree and every node t of T
is assigned a vertex subset Xt ⊆ V (G) called a bag such that the following conditions are satisfied: every
vertex v is in at least one bag, for every edge uv ∈ E(G) there exists a node t ∈ V (T ) such that Xt contains

3 Graph classes without long induced cycles
In this section we consider the question of establishing an upper bound on the tree-independence number for
specific K1,d -free graph classes excluding long induced cycles. One particular result in this direction is the
following result by Dallard et al. [DKK+ 24].

   Theorem 5 ([DKK+ 24]). Let k ≥ 3 and d ≥ 2 be integers and let G be a {Pk , K1,d }-free graph. Then
   α-tw(G) ≤ (d − 1)(k − 2).

Moreover, one can easily obtain the following slight generalisation of Theorem 5 (see also [DKK+ 24,
CHMW26]).

   Theorem 6. Let ℓ ≥ 3 and d ≥ 2 be integers and let G be a K1,d -free graph with no hole of length more
   than ℓ. Then α-tw(G) ≤ (d − 1)(ℓ − 2).

Note that in the above theorem, if ℓ = 3, then G is a chordal graph and the tree-independence number of
chordal graphs is equal to 1.
On another note, we already mentioned that Theorem 1 yields boundedness of the class of {Pk , Kd,d }-free
graphs. Although not much is known in terms of the upper in the general case, a recent result by Blažej et
al.[BGH+ 26] shows that {P5 , Kd,d }-free graphs have tree-independence number at most 4d.
We now explore the tree-independence number of certain graph classes without long induced cycles. First, we
present several examples of K1,d -free graph classes for which the tree-independence number is bounded as an
immediate consequence of Theorem 6.

3.1   Tolerance, co-comparability, and AT-free graphs
An interval graph is a graph where each vertex v can be assigned a closed interval Iv on the real line, such that
x and y are adjacent if and only if |Ix ∩ Iy | ̸= ∅. Moreover, if every vertex v of an interval graph can be assigned
a positive real value tv (called a tolerance) such that x and y are adjacent if and only if |Ix ∩ Iy | ≥ min{tx , ty },
then the graph is said to be a tolerance graph.

   Corollary 7. Let G be a K1,d -free tolerance graph with d ≥ 2 an integer. Then α-tw(G) ≤ 2d − 2.

    Proof. As shown by Golumbic, Monma, and Trotter [GMT84, Theorem 2], tolerance graphs do not
   contain holes of length at least 5. Thus, by Theorem 6, α-tw(G) ≤ 2d − 2.                 ■

A graph is comparability if its edges can be oriented such that if (a, b) and (b, c) are directed edges, then there
is a directed edge (a, c). A graph G is co-comparability if G is comparability.

   Corollary 8. Let G be a K1,d -free co-comparability graph with d ≥ 2 an integer. Then α-tw(G) ≤ 2d − 2.

    Proof. As shown by Gallai [Gal67], comparability graphs contain no complements of holes of length at
   least 5. Hence co-comparability graphs do not contain holes of length at least 5. Thus, by Theorem 6,
   α-tw(G) ≤ 2d − 2.                                                                                  ■

A triple x, y, z of vertices in a graph G is called an asteroidal triple if between each pair of them there exists a
path in G that avoids the closed neighbourhood of the third one. A graph is AT-free if it does not contain an
asteroidal triple.

   Corollary 9. Let G be a K1,d -free AT-free graph with d ≥ 2 an integer. Then α-tw(G) ≤ 3d − 3.

    Proof. AT-free graphs do not contain holes of length at least 6, since three mutually non-adjacent
   vertices in holes of length at least 6 form an asteroidal triple. Thus, by Theorem 6, α-tw(G) ≤ 3d − 3. ■


3.2   Bisplit graphs
A graph is bisplit if there exists a partition (X, Y, Z) of its vertices such that X, Y , and Z are independent
sets and Y ∪ Z induces a complete bipartite graph. We call such a partition a bisplit partition. Since walls
are bipartite and the class of bisplit graphs contains all bipartite graphs, it follows that the class of bisplit
graphs contains K1,4 -free graphs of arbitrarily large tree-independence number. However, adding one extra
requirement that both sides of the biclique must be non-empty dramatically decreases the tree-independence
number of the class.
First, we prove that adding the above mentioned requirement results in a bisplit graph whose every induced
cycle has bounded length.

  Lemma 10. Let d ≥ 3 be a positive integer and let G be a K1,d -free bisplit graph with a bisplit partition
  (X, Y, Z) of V (G) such that neither Y nor Z is empty. Then, G contains no induced cycle of length at
  least 2d − 1.

    Proof. Let d ≥ 3 and let G be a K1,d -free bisplit graph. Let (X, Y, Z) be a bisplit partition of V (G)
   such that neither Y nor Z are empty. Since G is K1,d -free, it follows that both Y and Z have size at
   most d − 1. Now fix C to be any induced cycle in G.
   First, suppose that C does not contain both a vertex from Y and a vertex from Z. Without loss of
   generality, we assume that C contains no vertex from Z. Since both Y and X are independent sets, it
   follows that C is an even cycle with exactly half of its vertices belonging to Y and the other half belonging
   to X. Now, since |Y | < d, C has length at most 2d − 2.
   Second, we consider the case when C contains both a vertex y ∈ Y and a vertex z ∈ Z. Since Y ∪ Z
   induces a complete bipartite graph, yz ∈ E(G). If y and z are the unique vertices of C contained in
   Y ∪ Z, then, since X is independent, C is of length at most 3 < 2d − 1 for all d ≥ 3. Therefore, without
   loss of generality, we may assume that C contains another vertex from Y distinct from y, say y ′ . In that
   case, C contains an induced path yzy ′ . It follows that either C contains another vertex from Z, in which
   case C is a cycle of length 4 < 2d − 1 for all d ≥ 3, or z is the only vertex of C from Z. In that case,
   since Y ∪ Z induces a complete bipartite graph, C cannot contain vertices from Y \ {y, y ′ } and Z \ {z}.
   Therefore, C has length at most 4. This concludes the proof that G contains no cycle of length at least
   2d − 1.                                                                                                 ■

Note that in the above lemma if we set d = 1, then G is edgeless, and if we set d = 2, then 2d − 1 = 3.
However, it is easy to observe that G can contain a C3 while not containing any induced cycle of length at
least 4.
For a graph class C, the hereditary closure of C is the minimal graph class containing all graphs in C that is
closed under taking induced subgraphs.

   Lemma 11. Let d ≥ 2 be a positive integer and let G be the class of K1,d -free bisplit graphs whose bisplit
   partition (X, Y, Z) of V (G) is such that neither Y nor Z are empty. Let Ĝ be the hereditary closure of G.
   Then, for every G ∈ Ĝ, α-tw(G) ≤ d − 1.

    Proof. Let G be any graph from Ĝ. Then G is an induced subgraph of a bisplit graph G′ with bisplit
   partition (X, Y, Z) such that neither Y nor Z is empty. Furthermore, since G′ is K1,d -free, we have that
   |Y |, |Z| ≤ d − 1. Then it suffices to show that α-tw(G′ ) ≤ d − 1.

4 Outerstring Graphs
In this section, we prove that the class of K1,d -free outerstring graphs has bounded tree-independence number.
A graph G is called outerstring if G may be represented as an intersection graph of curves, called grounded
curves, lying inside a unit disc such that for each such curve, one of the endpoints, called grounded point, is
on the boundary of the disk.
Throughout this section, for a given outerstring graph, without loss of generality, we assume that its intersection
model satisfies the following conditions:
   1. no grounded curve intersects itself,
   2. all grounded points are distinct,
   3. grounded curves intersect the boundary of the disk only at their respective grounded points, and
   4. no three grounded curves intersect a single point.
Now observe that for two grounded curves that do not intersect each other, we can find a curve that bisects
the disk, that does not intersect with both grounded curves. This observation can be extended for arbitrary
non-adjacent induced subgraphs of an outerstring graph.

   Observation 12. Let G be an outerstring graph with a given intersection model of grounded curves in a
   disk D. Let η be a curve contained in D that does not intersect itself and both endpoints are on the
   boundary circle of D. Let S be the set of vertices whose corresponding grounded curves intersect with η,
   and let A and B be the set of vertices such that their corresponding grounded curves are contained in
   two different components of D − η, respectively. Then S is a separator between A and B.

Another main tool for this section is the theorem from [CW25].

   Theorem 13 ([CW25]). Let d ≥ 2 and η ≥ d be positive integers and let G be a K1,d -free graph with
   α-tw(G) ≥ 16dη. Then G contains an induced path P and an induced cycle C such that
      • P and C are non-adjacent, and
      • if S is a separator between P and C in G, then α(S) ≥ η.

Finally, we show that K1,d -free outerstring graphs have bounded tree-independence number by using Observa-
tion 12.

  Theorem 15. Let G be a K1,d -free outerstring graph. Then α-tw(G) < 16d(6d − 5).

   Proof. Suppose that α-tw(G) ≥ 16d(6d − 5). Then by Theorem 13, we can find an induced path P
  and an induced cycle C such that they are non-adjacent and any separator S between P and C satisfies
  α(S) ≥ 6d − 5. Applying Lemma 14, we can find Y ⊆ V (C) with |Y | ≤ 6 such that N [Y ] is a separator
  between P and C. However, α(N [Y ]) ≤ (d − 1) · |Y | ≤ 6d − 6, which is a contradiction. Therefore, we
  have α-tw(G) < 16d(6d − 5).                                                                         ■

Note that An, Oh, and Xue [AOX24] showed that if an outerstring graph is planar, then it has treewidth
O(log n). Since n × n-grid has treewidth n, a sufficiently large grid cannot be an outerstring graph. Therefore,
since the class of outerstring graphs is closed under taking induced subgraphs, Theorem 15 is a special case for
Conjecture 1.
Next, we consider two related graph classes. A circle graph is a graph whose vertices can be associated with
a finite set of chords of a circle such that two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding chords
cross. A circular permutation graph is a graph which corresponds to an intersection model consisting of paths
between two concentric circles, such that no two paths intersect in more than one point.

Now we consider a generalisation of outerstring graphs such that grounded points could lie on several circles. A
graph G is said to be a k-outerstring graph if it is an intersection graph of curves γ ∈ Γ such that there exists

intersects exactly once one of Ci at one of the endpoints of γ. Note that the definition of 1-outerstring graphs
is equal to the definition of outerstring graphs. Observe that we may change the ‘circles’ in the definition to
‘closed curves’ without affecting the graph class defined.

   be the set of vertices whose grounded points are on Ci . If Vk is anticomplete to V (G) \ Vk , then
   G − Vk is a (k − 1)-outerstring graph and G[Vk ] is 1-outerstring graph. Thus we have α-tw(G) =
   max{α-tw(G[Vk ]), α-tw(G \ Vk )} ≤ 16d(6d − 5) + 2(d − 1)(k − 2).
   Now without loss of generality, suppose that there is an edge between Vk and Vk−1 . Then we can find
   vertices u ∈ Vk and v ∈ Vk−1 , whose grounded curves are γu and γv respectively, such that there is a
   curve η with one endpoint on Ck and the other on Ck−1 , and η ⊆ γu ∪ γv (see Figure 3). We may
   assume that η does not cross itself. Notice that the grounded curve corresponding to a vertex w ∈ V (G)
   intersects with η if and only if w is adjacent to u or v.
   We can construct a new closed curve C ′ that encloses Ck , Ck−1 , and η by adding two curves between Ck
   and Ck−1 slightly offset from η on either side (see Figure 3). Then G−(N [u]∪N [v]) is a (k−1)-outerstring
   graph, with the closed curves C1 , C2 , . . . , Ck−2 , C ′ . Therefore, we have

          α-tw(G) ≤ α(N [u] ∪ N [v]) + α-tw(G − (N [u] ∪ N [v])) ≤ 16d(6d − 5) + 2(d − 1)(k − 1)

   and the proof is complete.                                                                                      ■

grid.

5 Graph classes with unbounded tree-independence number
In this section we discuss a couple of K1,d -free graph classes that have unbounded tree-independence number.
Firstly, we note that by Theorem 1, the only interesting hereditary classes to consider are those that have
an infinite number of forbidden induced subgraphs. Also observe that grids and walls have unbounded
tree-independence number [DMŠ24a] (see Observation 18). Moreover, grids are K1,5 -free and walls are
K1,4 -free. Grids and walls are contained in many significant sparse graph classes, such as the classes of planar
graphs.
However, they are also contained in some denser graph classes. For instance, like we show in Section 5.1, the
class of circle containment graphs contains all grids. Observe that a natural subclass of circle containment
graphs, the class of interval containment graphs, in fact has bounded tree-independence number when forbidding
K1,d . Namely, this is exactly the class of permutation graphs, a subclass of the class of outerstring graphs, and
so the result follows from Theorem 15.
Moreover, since subdividing edges does not decrease the tree-independence number (since having tree-
independence number at most k is closed under taking induced minors [DMŠ24a]), classes containing subdivisions
of arbitrarily large grids or walls also have unbounded tree-independence number. For instance, this gives an
easy proof that the class of {C4 , K1,5 }-free graphs has unbounded tree-independence number. We will use the
induced minor relation to show that the class of rook graphs has unbounded tree-independence number in
Section 5.2.

5.1    Circle containment graphs
A circle containment graph is a graph whose vertex set corresponds to circles (disks) in 2 dimensional space and
two vertices are adjacent if and only if one of the corresponding circles is contained in the other one.

   Lemma 20. The class of circle containment graphs contains all grids.

   Proof. Consider an m × n grid Gm×n with V (Gm×n ) := {(a, b) : a ∈ [m], b ∈ [n]} such that
  (a1 , b1 )(a2 , b2 ) ∈ E(Gm×n ) if and only if |a1 − a2 | + |b1 − b2 | = 1. Then, for ε a fixed small constant,
  the following is a circle containment representation of Gm×n . If for a vertex (a, b) ∈ V (Gm×n ) the sum
  a + b is odd, we represent it by a circle of radius 1 + 2ε with centre point (a, b). Otherwise, if a + b is
  even, we represent (a, b) by a circle with radius ε and centre point (a, b).                                ■

See Figure 4 for an illustration of the construction of the proof above. Given that grids are K1,5 -free and have
unbounded tree-independence number [DMŠ24a], we obtain the following corollary.

   Corollary 21. The class of K1,5 -free circle containment graphs has unbounded tree-independence number.


5.2    Rook graphs
For a final example, we consider the class of rook graphs. The n × m rook graph is a graph Rn,m with vertex
set V (Rn,m ) := {vi,j : i ∈ [n], j ∈ [m]} and two vertices vi,j and vi′ ,j ′ are adjacent if and only if i = i′ or
j = j ′ . Intuitively, it is the graph given by legal moves for rooks on a n × m chessboard. As such, they are
significantly denser supergraphs of grids. Note that rook graphs are K1,3 -free.

   Lemma 22. For any graph H there exists an nH ∈ N+ such that RnH ,nH contains H as an induced
   minor.
      Proof. We will prove the following more precise statement:

                  Figure 4: G3×4 and its corresponding circle containment representation.


     Claim 22.1. For any graph H there exists an nH ∈ N+ such that RnH ,nH contains an induced minor
     model X of H where for each u ∈ V (H) the branch set Xu ∈ X is given by {vi,j : i ∈ Iu , j ∈ Ju }
     for some subsets Iu , Ju ⊆ [nH ].

  We will proceed by induction on |V (H)|. For |V (H)| = 1, the claim trivially holds with nH = 1. Now
  suppose that |V (H)| ≥ 2. Let v ∈ V (H) be an arbitrary vertex and let H ′ := H − v. By induction,
  the claim holds for H ′ . That is, there exists an nH ′ ∈ N+ and sets Iu′ , Ju′ ⊆ [nH ′ ] such that RnH ′ ,nH ′
  contains an induced minor model X′ of H ′ where for each u ∈ V (H ′ ) the branch set Xu′ ∈ X′ is given by
  {vi,j : i ∈ Iu′ , j ∈ Ju′ }. If v is an isolated vertex in H, it suffices to set nH := nH ′ + 1 and to consider
  the induced minor model X′ ∪ {Xv } where Xv = {vnH ,nH }. Hence, we may assume that degH (v) ≥ 1.
  Then let v1 , . . . , vdegH (v) be the neighbours of v in H. Let nH := nH ′ + degH (v) + 1 and consider the
  induced minor model X = {Xu }u∈V (H) of H in RnH ,nH defined by sets Iu , Ju ⊆ [nH ] as follows:
     • Iu := Iu′ and Ju := Ju′ for u ∈ V (H) \ NH [v];
     • Ivℓ := Iv′ ℓ ∪ {nH ′ + ℓ} for ℓ ∈ [degH (v)] and Jvℓ := Jv′ ℓ ; and
     • Iv := {nH ′ + 1, nH ′ + 2, . . . , nH ′ + degH (v)} and Jv := {nH }.
  Since two vertices u, u′ ∈ V (H) are adjacent according to the induced minor model if and only if
  Iu ∩ Iu′ ̸= ∅ or Ju ∩ Ju′ ̸= ∅, the correctness of this induced minor model follows from the correctness of
  the induced minor model X′ of H ′ and the observation that the extension of X′ to X is exactly responsible
  for the edges incident to v in H.                                                                        ■

Given that having tree-independence number at most k is closed under taking induced minors [DMŠ24a] and
that graph classes of unbound tree-independence number exist, Lemma 22 implies the following:

  Corollary 23. The class of rook graphs has unbounded tree-independence number.


6 A step towards K2,d -free graph classes
Finally, we now show that, in fact, when excluding all holes of length at least 5, one can prove a slightly
stronger result than the one from Theorem 6. We start with some definitions following terminology from
[BDD+ 26].
Let G and H be two graphs such that H is an induced minor of G. An induced minor model of H in G is a
collection X = {Xu : u ∈ V (H)} of subsets of V (G), called branch sets of X, such that
  • for every u, v ∈ V (H), u ̸= v, Xu ∩ Xv = ∅,



                                                                                                    Page 10 of 14
                                                          Tree-independence number of K1,d -free graph classes


   • for every u ∈ V (H), the graph G[Xu ] is connected, and
   • for every u, v ∈ V (H), u ̸= v, there exists an edge in G between Xu and Xv if and only if uv ∈ E(H).
The subgraph of G induced by X is the subgraph of G induced by the union of the branch sets of X. An
induced minor model X = {Xu : u ∈ V (H)} of H in G is said to be minimal if X induced a subgraph G′ of G
such that no proper induced subgraph of G′ contains H as an induced minor.
Before we state our results we present the following structural lemma for minimal induced minor models.

  Lemma 24 ([BDD+ 26]). Let G and H be two graphs such that H is an induced minor of G. Then, there
  exists a minimal induced minor model X = {Xu : u ∈ V (H)} of H in G such that the following holds.
     1. |Xu | = 1 for every vertex u ∈ V (H) with degree at most 2 such that u belongs to a connected
        component of H that is not a cycle.
     2. For every connected component C of H that is a cycle, there exists a vertex v of C such that
        G[Xv ] is a path and |Xu | = 1 for every vertex u in V (C − v).

Using Lemma 24, we can now prove the following.

  Theorem 25. Let G be a graph with no holes of length at least 5. Let p, q ≥ 2 be integers. Then G
  contains Kp,q as an induced minor if and only if G contains Kp,q as an induced subgraph.

   Proof. One direction is trivial: if G contains Kp,q as an induced subgraph, then G contains Kp,q as an
  induced minor.
  To prove the other direction, let G be a graph with no holes of length at least 5 containing Kp,q as
  an induced minor. Let X = {A1 , . . . , Ap , B1 , . . . , Bq } be a minimal induced minor model of Kp,q in G
  with a bipartition (A, B) where A := {A1 , . . . , Ap } and B := {B1 , . . . , Bq }. If every bag of X contains
  a single vertex, then we are done, so there is a bag of X containing at least two vertices. Without loss
  of generality, suppose that |A1 | ≥ 2. For each u ∈ A1 , let ϕ(u) = |{B ∈ B | N (u) ∩ B ̸= ∅}| be the
  number of bags of B that contain a vertex adjacent to u. Let u be a vertex of A1 maximizing ϕ(u).
  We now prove that we can find an induced cycle of G of length at least 5, which is a contradiction.
  By the minimality of X, there must be a bag Bi ∈ B such that N (u) ∩ Bi = ∅. Without loss of generality,
  let i = q. It follows that there exist a vertex in A1 , distinct from u, adjacent to Bq . Choose a vertex
  v ∈ A1 ∩ N (Bq ) such that the distance between u and v in A1 is minimized, and let P be a shortest u-v
  path in A1 . Note that v is the only vertex of P adjacent to Bq .
  By the maximality of ϕ(u), there exists j ∈ {1, . . . , q − 1} such that u is adjacent to Bj while v is not
  adjacent to Bj . Let w ̸= v be the vertex on P adjacent to Bj that minimizes the distance to v along P .
  Note that u = w is also possible. Then we can find a v-w path Q whose internal vertices are contained
  in Bj ∪ A2 ∪ Bq . Concatenating Q with the subpath of P between v and w yields an induced cycle of
  length at least 5, which is contradiction. Therefore, we conclude that each bag of X contains exactly
  one element, so the vertices contained in the induced minor model of Kp,q in G also induces Kp,q as an
  induced subgraph.                                                                                        ■

Theorem 25 immediately implies the following.

  Corollary 26. Let G be a K1,d -free graph containing no holes of length at least 5. Then G is a
  K2,d -induced-minor-free graph.

   Proof. Suppose to the contrary that G contains a K2,d as an induced minor. Then, by Theorem 25 G
  contains a K2,d as an induced subgraph, a contradiction since G is K1,d -free.                  ■

Before we prove our final result of this section, we need to state the following result by Dallard, Milanič, and
Štorgel [DMŠ24b].

                                                        Tree-independence number of K1,d -free graph classes



  Theorem 27 ([DMŠ24b]). For every integer q ≥ 2 and every K2,q -induced-minor-free graph G, the
  tree-independence number of G is at most 2q − 2.

Using Theorem 25 together with Theorem 27, we are now ready to state out result which is a generalisation of
Theorem 6 in the case when ℓ = 4.

  Theorem 28. Let d ≥ 2 be an integer. Let G be a K2,d -free graph containing no holes of length at least
  5. Then α-tw(G) ≤ 2d − 2.

   Proof. Let G be a K2,d -free graph containing no holes of length at least 5. By Theorem 25, since G
  is K2,d -free, G contains no K2,d as an induced-minor. It follows, by Theorem 27, that G has tree-
  independence number at most 2d − 2 as claimed.                                                     ■

Note that Theorem 28 generalises both Corollary 7 and Corollary 8, and more generally, it generalises Theorem 6
in the case when ℓ = 4.

7 Open problems
We conclude by presenting several open questions. First, observe that the bounds on tree-independence number
shown in Section 3 and Section 4 are given in polynomial in d. Thus we may ask the following strengthening
of Conjecture 1 that whether the bound on tree-independence number of a K1,d -free graph class without a
grid as an induced minor is always given by a polynomial.

  Conjecture 2. Is there a polynomial f such that for any integer d ≥ 2, if a K1,d -free graph G does not
  contain a d × d grid as an induced minor, then α-tw(G) ≤ f (d)?

Comparing width-parameters in various graph classes play an important role both in structural and algorithmic
graph theory. In [BMPY25], authors verified relationships between width parameters of several graph classes,
where the only open case is relation between tree-independence number and sim-width in the Kt,t -free graph
class. One may ask a weaker version of above question by forbidding K1,d instead of Kt,t .

  Conjecture 3. For each d ≥ 2, does a K1,d -free graph class with bounded sim-width have bounded
  tree-independence number?

Another interesting width parameter to compare is twin-width. In [AJK+ 25], authors proved that the graphs
with twin-width 1 are permutation graphs. Hence by Theorem 15, the class of K1,d -free graphs with twin-width 1
has bounded tree-independence number. However, an n × n grid where n ≥ 7 has twin-width 4, so the class of
graphs with twin-width at most 4 has unbounded tree-independence number. Therefore, one may ask what
happens in the case of classes of graphs of twin-width 2 or 3.

  Conjecture 4. For each d ≥ 2, does a K1,d -free graph class with twin-width at most 3 have bounded
  tree-independence number?
