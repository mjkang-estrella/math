A wedge-complement domain with a nonstarlike Cassinian ball

DRAFT - 1 November 2026. Prepared with AI assistance (Codex); no human authorship or affiliation asserted. No priority claim. MathDB 331705.

Abstract

A thin closed wedge removed from the complex plane gives an open, connected, proper domain U containing 0. It is starlike about 0 both in the segment sense and in the finite-boundary-point strict sense. Nevertheless its strict Cassinian ball of radius 7/4 contains y = 4+i and omits z = y/2. We give rational estimates sufficient for this conclusion and an exact evaluation at y on each of the two boundary rays. Two immutable Lean modules certify the geometric construction and the negations of the universal claims for the two domain hypotheses. The sharper evaluation and motivating slit computation are ordinary mathematical supplements. A bounded literature check found no explicit antecedent in the inspected bodies, but several important bodies remain unavailable; novelty is not established.

1. Definitions and the assertion being tested

Throughout, C is the Euclidean plane with modulus |.|, and a domain is a nonempty connected open subset of C. For a proper domain D and finite a,b in D, define

c_D(a,b) = sup { |a-b| / (|a-p| |b-p|) : p in boundary D },
B_c,D(a,r) = { b in D : c_D(a,b) < r },  r > 0.                         (1)

Boundary means the finite Euclidean frontier. Distances in (1) are Euclidean, not chordal. For the witnesses used below the boundary is nonempty, and the ratio set is bounded. The one-point compactification convention in the source adds infinity to an unbounded domain's boundary. For finite a,b its contribution is 0: the quotient tends to 0 as |p| tends to infinity. Adjoining 0 does not change a nonempty bounded supremum of nonnegative numbers. The formal certificate proves this insertion-of-zero fact, rather than constructing the compactification itself.

A set D containing a is ordinarily starlike about a if (1-t)a+tb belongs to D for every b in D and 0 <= t <= 1. Here 'strictly starlike' means that (1-t)a+tp belongs to D for every finite p in boundary D and 0 <= t < 1. This is the boundary-point convention of [2, Section 2]. Another convention requires boundedness and exactly one boundary intersection on every ray; it is not the convention asserted for our unbounded U. The distinction matters: a slit complement is ordinarily starlike but fails the boundary-point condition.

The assertion in [1, Conjecture 4.7] says: for every proper starlike domain D, every starlike center a in D, and every r > 0, B_c,D(a,r) is starlike about a. We test both the ordinary and the boundary-point strict domain hypothesis, retaining the ordinary conclusion. Failure of the ordinary conclusion also prevents the usual stronger strict conclusion for an open ball. A planar counterexample refutes an assertion posed for all dimensions n >= 2; no transport to every dimension is needed.

2. The wedge and its actual frontier

Put

K = { u+iv : u >= 1, |v| <= (u-1)/20 },       U = C minus K.              (2)

y = 4+i,             z = 2+i/2,              r = 7/4.                  (3)

Theorem. U is an open connected proper domain containing 0. It satisfies both starlikeness conditions of Section 1, but B_c,U(0,7/4) is not starlike about 0.

Proof of the domain assertions. K is the intersection of the three closed half-planes u >= 1, 20v <= u-1 and -20v <= u-1, so U is open. It contains 0 and excludes 1. Suppose w is in U and bw is in K, where 0 <= b <= 1. Then b Re(w) >= 1 implies b > 0. Also

20b |Im(w)| <= b Re(w)-1 <= b(Re(w)-1).

Division by b shows w is in K, a contradiction. Thus [0,w] lies in U. These segments connect each point to 0, proving path connectedness and hence connectedness. Direct substitution shows y,z are in U.

We claim that the finite frontier is exactly

boundary U = { 1+t(1+i/20) : t >= 0 }
             union { 1+t(1-i/20) : t >= 0 }.                            (4)

A frontier point belongs to K since K is closed. If u > 1 and both oblique inequalities are strict, the point is interior to K and cannot be in the frontier. At u = 1 the inequalities force v = 0. The remaining points satisfy u >= 1 and 20v = u-1 or 20v = -(u-1), precisely (4). Conversely, each such point p belongs to K, and p-delta belongs to U for every positive real delta: either its real part is below 1, or an oblique inequality fails. Taking delta down to 0 proves frontier membership. This verifies both inclusions for the actual frontier, rather than substituting a convenient boundary subset.

For strict starlikeness, let p be on a ray in (4) and let 0 <= b < 1. The ray equality gives 20|Im(bp)| = b(Re(p)-1). If bp were in K, it would also give 20|Im(bp)| <= b Re(p)-1, forcing b >= 1. Thus bp belongs to U, including the case p = 1. This proves the finite-boundary-point strict condition.

It remains to show that y belongs to the ball while its midpoint z does not. The next two sections do this with a uniform margin. All domain and frontier arguments above occur in Wedge.lean. In particular, the proof does not rely on a numerical picture of the removed wedge.

3. Bounds and exact ray suprema at y

For p = s+iv on either boundary ray, s >= 1 and 20v <= s-1. The denominator squared in (1) at a = 0 and b = y is

F(s,v) = (s²+v²)((4-s)²+(1-v)²).                                       (5)

The following elementary lower bounds are sufficient for the formal proof. Each row applies to its closed interval, so overlaps at endpoints cause no gap.

s interval          lower bound for F(s,v)
[1,5/4]             121/16
[5/4,2]             (25/16)(4+(19/20)²)
[2,3]               4(1+(9/10)²)
[3,4]               9(17/20)²
[4,5]               16(4/5)²
[5,infinity)        25

For example, on [2,3], s² >= 4, (4-s)² >= 1, and v <= 1/10 implies (1-v)² >= (9/10)². The other finite intervals use the same factorwise argument. On the final interval use s² >= 25 and (4-s)² >= 1. Every displayed lower bound exceeds 153/25. Since |y|² = 17, every boundary ratio is at most 5/3. The ratio set is nonempty (p = 1), so

c_U(0,y) <= 5/3 < 7/4.                                                 (6)

This uniform bound justifies passing to the supremum. Pointwise strict inequalities without a uniform margin would not justify (6).

Ordinary exact supplement. In fact, on each ray separately the supremum is sqrt(17/10), attained at the apex p = 1. Here is an exact positivity certificate avoiding numerical root selection. For e = +1 or -1 and p = 1+t(1+ei/20), write F_e(t) = |p|² |y-p|². Expansion gives

F_+(t)-10 = t Q(t)/160000,
Q(t) = 160801t³ - 657640t² - 187600t + 2224000,
F_-(t)-F_+(t) = t(32080t²+64000t+32000)/160000.                        (7)

To check Q > 0, its cubic Bernstein coefficients on [0,2], [2,3], [3,4] are, respectively,

(2224000, 6296800/3, 3291040/3, 504648),
(504648, 625396/3, 44014/3, 84067),
(84067, 460388/3, 486048, 1242624).

These mean Q(a+(b-a)u) = sum from j=0 to 3 of B_j binom(3,j)u^j(1-u)^(3-j). All coefficients are positive. For t = 4+h, h >= 0, Q = 160801h³+1271972h²+2269728h+1242624 > 0. Thus (7) proves F_e(t) >= 10, with equality exactly at t = 0. The apex quotient is |y|/(|1||y-1|) = sqrt(17)/sqrt(10). This sharper equality is not an endpoint claimed by the Lean modules; their bound (6) suffices.

4. Excluding the midpoint; the slit limit

For a valid use of a boundary witness in a real supremum, first check boundedness at z. On the frontier, |p| >= 1. If s <= 3, then v <= 1/10, so |z-p| >= |1/2-v| >= 2/5. If s > 3, the real distance |2-s| exceeds 1. Thus all ratios at z are bounded above by (5/2)|z|. The point

p_0 = 2+i/20

lies on the upper ray, with

|p_0|² = 1601/400,      |z-p_0| = 9/20,      |z|² = 17/4.

Its boundary ratio has square

|z|² / (|p_0|² |z-p_0|²) = 680000/129681 > 49/16.                    (8)

The final inequality is the integer inequality 10880000 > 6354369. All quantities are positive, hence the ratio itself exceeds 7/4. Boundedness and the definition of supremum give c_U(0,z) > 7/4. Consequently y belongs to the strict ball and z = y/2 does not. The ordinary starlikeness conclusion fails. Together with Section 2 this proves the theorem under both domain hypotheses.

Slit motivation (ordinary supplement). Let G = C minus [1,infinity). This is the zero-angle version of (2); it is open and ordinarily starlike about 0. Its frontier is [1,infinity). A point 2 of that frontier has midpoint 1 outside G, so G fails the strict boundary-point condition. This explains why a positive wedge angle is useful.

For b = y or z and real p = s >= 1, minimize s² |b-s|². At y the polynomial is s²((s-4)²+1). Its derivative is 2s(2s²-12s+17), with critical points 3 +/- sqrt(2)/2. On [1,infinity), the minimum is 10 at s = 1: the other candidate minimum has value 71/4-2sqrt(2) > 10, since 31 > 8sqrt(2). Thus c_G(0,y) = sqrt(17/10).

At z the polynomial is s²((s-2)²+1/4). Its derivative is s(4s²-12s+17/2); the interior local minimum is at s = (6+sqrt(2))/4. Its value is (71-8sqrt(2))/64, which is less than the endpoint value 5/4 because 71-8sqrt(2) < 80. Therefore

c_G(0,z) = 4sqrt(17)/sqrt(71-8sqrt(2)) > 7/4.                            (9)

The exact slit evaluations in this paragraph were independently checked in the frozen review. The original Cassinian.lean certifies sufficient inequalities for the slit, rather than these exact optimizer formulas. Equations (7) and (9) are ordinary supplements, not additional formalized theorems. No bounded-domain extension is claimed in this draft.

6. Related work, access limits and status

The target is the unqualified global assertion in the 2015 preprint [1]. Its journal version is Math. Nachr. 290 (2017), 1531-1543, DOI 10.1002/mana.201600117. The actual journal body was not obtained: the already denied Wiley route was not retried, and a repository record did not supply an accessible body. We do not assert that numbering or wording is unchanged in the journal version.

Klén [2, Sections 2, 7 and 11] explicitly discusses the boundary-point convention, gives growth bounds for the Cassinian distance near the center and compares monotonicity properties of other metrics. Those local derivative and growth bounds do not imply global Cassinian ball starlikeness. The comb example there concerns the distance-to-boundary function, rather than an explicit Cassinian ball counterexample.

The foundational 2009 paper [3] was located bibliographically through primary references and the author's CV, but no lawful primary body was obtained in this bounded check. The later coauthored paper [4] was inspected in its arXiv body: it concerns metric comparisons, Möbius distortion and the inner Cassinian metric. The scale-invariant and Möbius-invariant Cassinian metrics are different metrics; their existence and abstracts cannot resolve the assertion for (1). Their original bodies remain an access limitation here. Hariri et al. [5] compare the ordinary Cassinian and triangular ratio metrics in the unit ball. Their triangular-ratio results must not be substituted for Cassinian ball starlikeness. Similarly, the universal triangular ratio ball result in [6] is about the sum denominator, not the product in (1).

The 2020 monograph [7] was checked through its public book and chapter pages (Chapters 11 and 13), but relevant chapter bodies remained subscription content. The OpenAlex citing-work listing returned nine records for the source DOI. Their accessible arXiv bodies were screened where present. In particular, the 2025 preprint [8] studies average scale-invariant Cassinian metrics and local convexity in punctured spaces. The 2021 paper [9] and, especially, Cao-Zhang [10] remain body-unread: [10]'s abstract expressly mentions starlikeness, so this is a material unresolved prior-art gap, not clearance from an abstract. The full dated reading and access ledger is PRIOR-ART.json/md. No explicit prior counterexample or full resolution was found in the inspected bodies; that bounded negative result permits drafting but does not establish novelty.

Public proof-registry heads were checked on 1 November 2026. The retained prior review and new receipts distinguish head observations from actual body coverage; registry metadata alone is not novelty evidence. Credit for this lane remains zero.
