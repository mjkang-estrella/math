                                                                                                        Abstract
                                              A circular nowhere-zero r-flow on a bridgeless graph G is an orientation of the edges and an assignment
                                          of real values from [1, r − 1] to the edges in such a way that the sum of incoming values equals the sum
                                          of outgoing values for every vertex. The circular flow number of G is the inﬁmum over all values r such
                                          that G admits a nowhere-zero r-ﬂow. We prove that the circular glow number of Goldberg snark G2k+1
                                          is 4 + 1/(k + 1), proving a conjecture of Goedgebeur, Mattiolo, and Mazzuoccolo.
                                          Keywords. ciruclar ﬂow number, snarks.



                                          1      Introduction
                                          A circular nowhere-zero r-flow on a bridgeless graph G is an orientation of the edges and an assignment
                                          of real values from [1, r − 1] to the edges in such a way that the sum of incoming values equals the sum
                                          of outgoing values for every vertex. The circular flow number, we denote it simply by ϕ(G), of G is the
                                          inﬁmum over all values r such that G admits a nowhere-zero r-ﬂow. The inﬁmum from the deﬁnition is
                                          attained and is rational [2]. The circular ﬂow number is a natural reﬁnement of the ﬂow number of a
                                          graph, ⌈ϕ(G)⌉ equals the ﬂow number of G [2].
                                              Snarks are bridgeless cubic graphs that are not 3-edge-colourable. Snarks are intensively studied
                                          mostly because many important conjectures and statements can be easily reduced to snarks, e.g. Four
                                          Colour Theorem, 5-ﬂow Conjecture, Cycle Double Cover Conjecture. The circular ﬂow number of a cubic
                                          graph equals 3 if and only if the graph is bipartite and equals 4 if and only if the graph is non-bipartite
                                          and 3-edge-colourable [9, 1]. Thus, among cubic graphs, only snarks are of interest with respect to the
                                          circular ﬂow number and their circular ﬂow number is more than 4. Tutte’s 5-ﬂow Conjecture [10] asserts
                                          that the circular ﬂow number of any graph is at most 5. On the other hand any rational value between
                                          4 and 5 is attainable as a circular ﬂow number of a snark [6].
                                              There are three widely studied inﬁnite classes of snarks that represent various methods of construc-
                                          tions: Isaac’s snarks, Generalised Blanuša snarks, and Goldberg snarks. For Isaacs snark I2k+1 the upper
                                          bound ϕ(I2k+1 ) ≤ 4 + 1/k was proved by Steﬀen [9] and the corresponding lower bound was obtained
                                          by Lukoťka and Škoviera [5]. The circular ﬂow number of Generalized Blanuša snarks is 4.5 [7]. The
                                          Goldberg snarks turned out to be the most challenging among these three classes.
                                              Goldberg snark G2k+1 is a graph with vertex set {ai , bi , ci , di , ei , fi , gi , hi : i ∈ Z2k+1 } and edge
                                          set {ai bi , bi ai+1 , ai ei , bi ci , ci di , di ei , ci fi , ei gi , fi gi , gi fi+1 , di hi , hi hi+1 : i ∈ Z2k+1 } (see Figure 1). The-
                                          orem 2.4 from [5] implies ϕ(G2k+1 ) ≥ 4 + 1/(2k + 1). Lukoťka [7] proved that ϕ(G2k+1 ) ≤ 4 + 1/k.
                                          Recently, Goedgebeur, Mattiolo, and Mazzuoccolo [3] proved ϕ(G2k+1 ) ≤ 4 + 1/(k + 1) and, based on
                                          computational evidence, conjectured that this is the correct value. In this paper we prove this conjecture.
                                              Instead of considering Goldberg snark G2k+1 we will consider Goldberg graph H2k+1 obtained by
                                          contracting all vertices in {hi : i ∈ Z2k+1 } into a single vertex h of degree 2k + 1 (see Figure 1). We will
                                          show that ϕ(H2k+1 ) ≥ 4 + 1/(k + 1) and thus also ϕ(G2k+1 ) ≥ 4 + 1/(k + 1), completing the bound of
                                          Goedgebeur, Mattiolo, and Mazzuoccolo.

2     Balanced valuations
We use balanced valuations introduced by Jaeger [4], but to avoid fractions use a deﬁnition from [8]. Let
∂(S) denote the number of edges with exactly one end in S. Let G be a bridgeless graph. A balanced
valuation is a mapping b : V (G) → Z such that
    • b(S) ≤ ∂(S) for each S ⊆ V (G) and
    • b(v) ≡ ∂(v)(mod 2) for each v ∈ V (G),
where b(S) denotes the sum of b(v) for all v ∈ S. To avoid zeroes in denominators we will mostly consider
only subsets S ⊆ V (G) with ∂(S) 6= 0, to write that S is such subset we use S ⊏ G. Note that for sets S
with ∂(S) = 0 the deﬁnition of balanced valuation implies b(S) = 0. A balanced valuation is orientable
if for each S ⊏ G, we have b(S) < ∂(S). Note that if G has an orientable balanced valuation, then G is
bridgeless.
     Given a circular nowhere-zero r-ﬂow on G, we can create an orientable balanced valuation b on G by
taking b(v) to be equal to the number of incoming minus number of outgoing edges of v. As the maximal
ﬂow value is r − 1 one can bound the ratio of incoming and outgoing edges to S and which in turn can
be expressed using b(S) and ∂(S). The resulting condition is not only necessary, but also suﬃcient.

balanced valuation b of G such that for each S ⊏ G,

Lemma 7. Let G be a graph, let b be an orientable balanced valuation of G, and let S ⊏ G. If

Proof. Assume p = ∂(S) + b(S) and q = ∂(S) − b(S). Due to the deﬁnition of balanced valuation both p
