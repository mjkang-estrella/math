## Scope and hypotheses

Let V be finite, T a subset, and B_c a partition of U=V\T into nonempty blocks, each finitely ordered. In S=A[x_v,y_v:v in V], let I_T be generated by x_v,y_v for v in T and all x_u y_v-x_v y_u with u,v in a common B_c. The target is the formal polynomial ring on mutually disjoint labels s_c,t_c,z_v. ## 1. Single block: legal steps, descent, existence and uniqueness

For a source monomial use natural exponents a_i,b_i, column capacities d_i=a_i+b_i, total x count r=sum a_i and D=sum d_i. A reducible pair is exactly i<j with b_i>=1 and a_j>=1. Subtract one from b_i and a_j to obtain a monomial H; all its exponents remain natural. The old monomial is H y_i x_j, the new one H x_i y_j. Thus old minus new equals -H(x_i y_j-x_j y_i). No division in A or cancellation of coefficients is involved. Higher powers simply permit further legal unit steps.

Every d_i and r are invariant. The integer weight w=sum i*a_i changes by i-j<0. It is nonnegative, so successive available steps cannot continue more than the initial w times. To make existence explicit, choose the least available pair in the finite ordered pair set at each step; stop precisely when no pair remains. This supplies an actual finite sequence, rather than an assumption that a terminal monomial exists. There is no executed search here.

Let p_i=sum_{h<i}d_h and g_i=min(d_i,max(0,r-p_i)). Natural truncated subtraction is intended. The prefix identity sum_{h<=i}g_h=min(r,sum_{h<=i}d_h) follows by cases: p_i>=r gives previous sum r and g_i=0; p_i<r gives previous sum p_i and adding min(d_i,r-p_i) gives the stated minimum. In particular 0<=g_i<=d_i and sum g_i=r for 0<=r<=D. This constructs N(d,r)=product x_i^{g_i}y_i^{d_i-g_i} directly.

If g_i<d_i, then necessarily r<p_i+d_i, so the x count r is exhausted by this index and all later g_j are zero. Hence N has no reducible pair. Conversely take a terminal monomial. If no y exponent is positive, a_i=d_i throughout and r=D, which is the formula. Otherwise take the least k with b_k>0. Then a_i=d_i for i<k; every a_j=0 for j>k, since (k,j) would otherwise be reducible. Consequently a_k=r-p_k, with p_k<=r<p_k+d_k. For i<k the greedy formula saturates d_i, at k it yields a_k, and thereafter it gives zero. This proves uniqueness of the terminal monomial independently of the map. It also proves all reduction orders terminate at the same N; confluence is a conclusion.

Zero-capacity columns do not affect the reasoning. At r=0 all g_i=0; at r=D all g_i=d_i. The empty block has D=r=0 and N=1. The one-column case has no moves and N=x_1^r y_1^{D-r}. These cases pay saturation endpoints and existence as well as uniqueness.

## 2. Telescoping, arbitrary coefficients, and the hard inclusion

For an actual sequence M=M_0,...,M_l=N, adding the identities M_h-M_{h+1}=-H_h minor(i_h,j_h) gives M-N=-sum H_h minor(i_h,j_h). All multipliers are genuine monomials with nonnegative exponents. This is a finite certificate in the original generated ideal, with the correct sign.

For an arbitrary polynomial f=sum c_M M over its finite monomial support, multiply each certificate by its original coefficient c_M and sum. Partition this finite support by (d,r); let C_{d,r} be the coefficient sum in each fiber. Then f-sum C_{d,r}N(d,r) is a finite sum of polynomial multiples of original generators. Arbitrary signs, repeated exponents, zero divisors and cancellations are included, rather than restricted to monomial inputs.

Substitution sends M to s^r t^{D-r} z^d. Equality of two such target exponent vectors forces equality of all d from z and of r from s; conversely those invariants determine the target monomial. Hence these fibers account for ALL image collisions. The coefficient at that target monomial is exactly C_{d,r}, even if this sum happens to be zero. Formal polynomial equality is coefficientwise over every A. Thus phi(f)=0 forces every C_{d,r}=0, the normal sum vanishes, and the certificate yields f in I. This is the reverse inclusion, proved without assuming determinantal primality. For the forward inclusion, each generator maps to st z_i z_j-st z_j z_i=0, and the kernel contains every finite ideal combination. Together these prove equality.

If two combinations of distinct N represent the same quotient class, their difference lies in I, so its image is zero; distinct image monomials force equality of each coefficient. The N therefore span and are A-linearly independent in the quotient. This is freeness over A. It does not say the additive group is torsion-free: any torsion already present in A remains in a free A-module. The proof works unchanged for polynomial coefficient rings A=B[w]. In characteristic two, minus equals plus, but every displayed identity is still valid and coefficient extraction never divides by two. There is no localization, point evaluation, density, algebraic closure or finite-field function argument.

## 3. Simultaneous blocks and killed coordinates

Every monomial involving a killed variable factors as that variable times a monomial by subtracting one positive exponent. Including its original coefficient yields a finite coordinate-ideal certificate. Deleting all such terms leaves f_surv, with f-f_surv in the coordinate ideal and Phi(f)=Phi(f_surv). The surviving monomials retain distinct exponent vectors under zero-coordinate substitution; its kernel is exactly the coordinate ideal over any A.

For a surviving monomial record d_v=a_v+b_v and each separate r_c=sum_{v in B_c}a_v. Normalize each block by the preceding argument. During a block certificate the other block factors multiply every term. For clarity, if the local factors are M_1,...,M_q and normal factors N_1,...,N_q, the identity
product M_c - product N_c = sum_c (product_{h<c}N_h)(M_c-N_c)(product_{h>c}M_h)
is a finite telescoping certificate in the union of original block ideals. Alternatively the sum of local weights decreases at every legal move. Each terminal block is uniquely prescribed, so their product is the unique terminal monomial. Disjointness of variables makes this a statement about every surviving exponent.

Group finite coefficients of f_surv by (d,(r_c)). Their images are precisely product s_c^{r_c}t_c^{D_c-r_c} product z_v^{d_v}. Each z_v recovers d_v, and each distinct s_c recovers its own r_c. Thus there are no additional image collisions between tuples. Combining deleted-term certificates and normalized-term certificates gives f-NF(f) in the ORIGINAL I_T. If Phi(f)=0, every grouped coefficient is zero, hence f belongs to I_T. Conversely all original coordinates and within-block minors map to zero. This establishes both ideal inclusions and quotient-basis uniqueness simultaneously, without using a tensor-product-of-domains assertion.

For T=V there are no blocks, the target is A, NF is the constant coefficient and the kernel is the all-coordinate ideal. If V is empty this is the identity map on A. Singletons have no minors and impose no relation on their two surviving coordinates; their parametrizations are injective by the one-block argument. Empty blocks can be omitted; unused target parameters do not change coefficients or kernels.

Separate row pairs are essential. For distinct components c,e and vertices u,v in them, the cross minor has image z_u z_v(s_c t_e-s_e t_c). The two displayed monomials have distinct exponent vectors and nonzero coefficients 1,-1 over every field, also in characteristic two. It is therefore not in the kernel. ## 4. Domain, properness and primality

For a nontrivial integral domain A, a polynomial ring in finitely many variables is a domain. Independently check the printed lexicographic argument: each finite nonempty support has a greatest exponent alpha or beta. For any other pair alpha'<=alpha, beta'<=beta with at least one strict, translation invariance gives alpha'+beta'<alpha+beta. Thus only the leading pair contributes at alpha+beta and its coefficient is the nonzero product of the leading coefficients. Zero target variables reduce to A itself. This proof is valid for finite support; a well-order property of lex order is unnecessary.

The target therefore has 1!=0. Phi fixes A, so 1 cannot lie in its kernel. If fg belongs to I_T=ker(Phi), the domain product condition forces Phi(f)=0 or Phi(g)=0, giving primality. The induced quotient map is injective because of the reverse inclusion proved above. No claim that its image equals the whole target is used. Primality is deliberately not extended to coefficient rings with zero divisors or to the zero ring.

## 6. Crown families, checked directly for n>=4

Use disjoint a_i,b_i with edges exactly a_i b_j for i!=j. With T empty, two distinct a vertices share b_k for k outside their indices, and symmetrically for b vertices. Opposite unequal indices are adjacent. For a_i,b_i choose k!=i and l outside {i,k}; a_i,b_k,a_l,b_i is a path whose three edges all have unequal indices. Thus the whole graph is one component and Pc(empty)=D(V), with one row pair.

For T=X\{a_i}, survivors are a_i and all Y. Each b_j with j!=i is adjacent to a_i and b_i has no surviving neighbor. The two blocks are exactly {a_i} union (Y\{b_i}) and {b_i}; the central a_i must be included. This gives Q_i=L(T)+D({a_i} union (Y\{b_i})), with independent row pairs for the star and singleton. Exchanging sides gives R_i and isolated a_i. No minor connects the isolate to the star.

For distinct i,j and T=V\{a_i,a_j,b_i,b_j}, the only surviving edges are a_i b_j and a_j b_i. There are exactly two blocks, each of size two, yielding M_ij=L(T)+([a_i,b_j],[a_j,b_i]). Each block's reverse minor is its negative, so these two listed minors generate all its completed-component minors. Independent row pairs are mandatory. Reversing i,j gives the same cut and ideal. At i=j the survivors would instead be a_i,b_i with no edge, so the displayed two-edge assertion cannot be extended to equal indices. The Fam datatype admits equal mix indices, but the ordinary family claim explicitly excludes them.

Validity can also be checked without importing classification exhaustiveness. The empty cut is allowed by definition. For delX i, restoring a deleted a_j joins b_i to the star and connects all survivors: choose k outside {i,j} as a common leaf, while b_j connects to a_i. Before restoration there are two components, after restoration one, so the restored vertex is a cut vertex. This holds for each deleted a_j; delY is symmetric. For a mixed cut, restoring any deleted a_k connects b_i to b_j and hence joins the two edge components; restoring a deleted b_k is symmetric. Thus every deleted vertex is a cut vertex in the required induced graph. Equal-index mix is not justified by this argument: restoring a_k leaves the isolated a_i disconnected and does not increase the component count when a_k is removed. Full-side cuts leave n isolated opposite vertices; restoring one vertex joins n-1 of them, leaving two components, and deleting it gives n>2. They are valid as well. No exhaustive classification is newly asserted here.

Full-side deletions have singleton blocks and coordinate-only ideals. In every displayed noncoordinate case there is a block of size at least two; a minor on distinct vertices there is a nonzero formal polynomial with two distinct monomials over a field. Zeroing T fixes it, so it cannot lie in the coordinate ideal alone. This excludes replacing these actual primes by coordinate ideals. All checks remain valid at n=4 and in characteristic two.
