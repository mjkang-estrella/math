 Abstract 
 
 We prove that the standard zero forcing number Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) and the positive semidefinite zero forcing number Z + ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G) are equal for all claw-free graphs G G . This result resolves a conjecture proposed by the computer program TxGraffiti and highlights a connection between these graph invariants in claw-free structures. As a corollary, we show that a graph G G is claw-free if and only if every induced subgraph H ⊆ G H\subseteq G satisfies Z ​ ( H ) = Z + ​ ( H ) Z(H)=Z_{+}(H) . 
 
 
 
 
 
 “Try to use the claw, but you don’t need to use the claw” 
 
 
 – Baloo the Bear, The Jungle Book 
 
 
 
 
 Keywords: claw-free graphs ; positive semidefinite zero forcing number ; TxGraffiti ; standard zero forcing number 
 AMS subject classification: 68R10 
 
 
 1 INTRODUCTION 

 
 Let G G be an undirected, simple, and finite graph with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) , and let V ⁡ ( G ) = B ∪ W V(G)=B\cup W represent a partition of the vertices into a set of blue vertices B B and a set of white vertices W W , with time t = 0 t=0 initialized. A forcing game on a graph is a process in which a player strategically applies a color change rule to propagate blue, or white, vertices through the graph. A color change rule specifies a condition under which a vertex v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) can be forced to change color (either from white to blue, or from blue to white). In this paper we only consider those rules allowing blue vertices to force color changes on white vertices, denoted ( u → v ) (u\rightarrow v) , where u u is a blue vertex and v v is a white vertex changing to be blue. A valid force under a given rule is any pair ( u → v ) (u\rightarrow v) where u ∈ B u\in B and v ∈ W v\in W satisfies the condition specified by the rule. At each time step t t , a player selects a subset of valid forces, ensuring that no white vertex is forced by more than one vertex. If Δ ​ B t \Delta B_{t} denotes the set of newly blue vertices resulting from these forces, the sets are then updated as B ← B ∪ Δ ​ B t B\leftarrow B\cup\Delta B_{t} and W ← W ∖ Δ ​ B t W\leftarrow W\setminus\Delta B_{t} , and the time is incremented as t ← t + 1 t\leftarrow t+1 . This process is repeated until no further changes occur, resulting in a terminal time step , denoted by τ \tau . If all vertices in V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) are blue at this terminal time step, the initial set B B is called a forcing set of G G under the given rule. The minimum cardinality of such a forcing set defines a graph invariant associated with the rule. 
 
 
 Two of the most studied color change rules are the standard zero forcing rule and the positive semidefinite zero forcing rule (or simply standard forcing rule and positive semidefinite forcing rule for brevity). The standard forcing rule allows a vertex u ∈ B u\in B with exactly one neighbor v ∈ W v\in W to standard force v v , denoted ( u → v ) z (u\rightarrow v)_{z} . Forcing sets under this rule are called standard forcing sets , and the minimum cardinality of such a set is the standard forcing number , Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) . The positive semidefinite forcing rule generalizes the standard rule by allowing a blue vertex u ∈ B u\in B to force a white vertex v ∈ W v\in W if v v is the only neighbor of u u in its component of the induced subgraph G ⁡ [ W ] G[W] , denoted ( u → v ) + (u\rightarrow v)_{+} . Forcing sets under this rule are called positive semidefinite forcing sets and the minimum cardinality of such a set is the positive semidefinite forcing number , Z + ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G) . 
 
 
 Both Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) and Z + ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G) were initially motivated by applications in linear algebra, particularly in the problem of determining the maximum nullity (equivalently, the minimum rank ) over symmetric matrices described by a graph. Specifically, Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) provides the upper bound M ⁡ ( G ) ≤ Z ⁡ ( G ) M(G)\leq Z(G) for the maximum nullity M ⁡ ( G ) M(G) of symmetric matrices whose off-diagonal zero-nonzero pattern corresponds to the adjacency structure of G G (see  [ 1 ] ). Similarly, Z + ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G) bounds the maximum positive semidefinite nullity , M + ​ ( G ) M^{+}(G) , which is the largest nullity among symmetric positive semidefinite matrices whose off-diagonal zero-nonzero pattern matches G G (see  [ 2 ] ). 
 
 
 It is straightforward to observe that Z + ​ ( G ) ≤ Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)\leq Z(G) for any graph G G , as the positive semidefinite forcing rule has more flexible criteria for valid forces than the standard forcing rule. Furthermore, the gap between these parameters can be arbitrarily large: for any constant C > 0 C>0 , there exists a graph G G such that Z ⁡ ( G ) − Z + ​ ( G ) > C Z(G)-Z_{+}(G)>C . For example, in any tree T T , the positive semidefinite forcing number satisfies Z + ​ ( T ) = 1 Z_{+}(T)=1 regardless of the structure of T T , while the standard forcing number Z ⁡ ( T ) Z(T) can grow arbitrarily large with the maximum degree or the number of strong support vertices in T T . Conversely, certain graphs, such as paths and diamond-necklaces, satisfy Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) (see  [ 6 ] ). Indeed, the conjecturing program TxGraffiti (see  [ 3 ] for a description of the program) conjectured the following: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1 ( TxGraffiti – confirmed) . 
 
 If G G is a claw-free graph, then Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) . 
 
 
 
 Motivated by Conjecture  1 , which we confirm in this paper, we consider a broader question: for which families of graphs does the equality Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) hold universally, even for all induced subgraphs? This question aligns with classic themes in graph theory, such as the study of perfect graphs, where the equality of clique number and chromatic number holds for every induced subgraph  [ 8 ] . To formalize this, we define a graph G G to be ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) –perfect if Z + ​ ( H ) = Z ​ ( H ) Z_{+}(H)=Z(H) for every induced subgraph H ⊆ G H\subseteq G . As we demonstrate, claw-free graphs offer a complete characterization of ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) –perfect graphs, unifying and extending prior results on the relationship between Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) and Z + ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G) in claw-free graphs, such as Z ​ ( G ) = Z + ​ ( G ) Z(G)=Z_{+}(G) for line graphs (see  [ 4 ] ) and the bounds Z ⁡ ( G ) ≤ Z + ​ ( G ) ≤ 2 ​ Z ​ ( G ) Z(G)\leq Z_{+}(G)\leq 2Z(G) (see  [ 7 ] ). 
 
 
 As part of proving Conjecture  1 , we also establish in this paper an interesting result about positive semidefinite forcing sets. A central focus in [ 5 ] is what can be said when forcing sets are vertex cuts. Utilizing the concept of path bundles introduced in [ 5 ] , we explore the opposite and show in Lemma  4 that for any graph, there is a minimum positive semidefinite forcing set of that graph whose complement is connected. This connectivity plays a key role in the proof of Conjecture  1 . 
 
 
 The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section  2 , we provide the necessary definitions and preliminaries, including relaxed chronologies and path bundles . Section  3 provides lemmas regarding positive semidefinite forcing needed to prove our main result. Section  4 contains the proof of our main theorem and its corollary. Finally, in Section  5 , we give concluding remarks.

 3 THE LEMMAS 

 
 The following result connects path bundles and positive semidefinite forcing sets. 
 
 
 Lemma 2 ( [ 5 ] ) . 
 
 Let G G be a graph, and let B B be a positive semidefinite forcing set of G G . If ℱ \mathcal{F} is a relaxed chronology of forces for B B on G G , and 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} is the path bundle of ℱ \mathcal{F} induced by some vertex x ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) x\in V(G) , then the set Term ⁡ ( ℱ | 𝒬 ⁡ ( ℱ , x ) ) \Term(\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{F};x)}) is a positive semidefinite forcing set of G G . 
 
 
 
 The next lemma shows that any minimum positive semidefinite forcing set of a connected graph can be modified to maximize the size of a component in its complement. 
 
 
 Lemma 3 .

 Lemma 4 . 
 
 If G G is a connected graph, then there exists a minimum positive semidefinite forcing set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) such that G − S G-S is connected. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) be an arbitrary minimum positive semidefinite forcing set of G G . If G − S G-S is connected, then S S satisfies the conclusion of the lemma, and we are done. Suppose instead that G − S G-S is disconnected. By Lemma  3 , given a component C C of G − S G-S , there exists another minimum positive semidefinite forcing set S ′ ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S^{\prime}\subseteq V(G) such that G − S ′ G-S^{\prime} has a strictly larger component than C C . Specifically, there exists a component C ′ C^{\prime} of G − S ′ G-S^{\prime} that contains C C with V ⁡ ( C ) ⊊ V ⁡ ( C ′ ) V(C)\subsetneq V(C^{\prime}) . 
 
 
 Replace S S with S ′ S^{\prime} and C C with C ′ C^{\prime} , and repeat the process. At each step, the size of the component C C in G − S G-S strictly increases. Since G G is a finite graph, this process must terminate after finitely many steps. When the process terminates, we obtain a minimum positive semidefinite forcing set S S such that G − S G-S is connected, completing the proof.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 4 THE MAIN THEOREM AND COROLLARY 

 
 This section presents our main result, Theorem  5 , and its corollary, Corollary  6 . 
 
 
 Theorem 5 . 
 
 If G G is a connected and claw-free graph, then Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let G G be a connected and claw-free graph. Since every standard forcing set is also a positive semidefinite forcing set, it follows that Z + ​ ( G ) ≤ Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)\leq Z(G) . To prove the theorem, it suffices to show that Z + ​ ( G ) ≥ Z ⁡ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)\geq Z(G) . By Lemma  4 , there exists a minimum positive semidefinite forcing set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) such that the induced subgraph G ⁡ [ W ] G[W] is connected, where W = V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ S W=V(G)\setminus S . Fix such a set S S , and color the vertices in S S blue and the vertices in W W white. 
 
 
 We aim to show that every valid positive semidefinite force during a chronological list of forces beginning at S S is also a valid standard force, which will, in turn, imply that S S is a standard forcing set of G G . Along the way we will need to track that the white colored vertices always induce a connected subgraph; that is, the set of white colored vertices at time t t induces a connected subgraph of G G , for each time step 0 ≤ t < τ 0\leq t<\tau . 
 
 
 Base Case ( t = 0 t=0 ): At time t = 0 t=0 , the set of white vertices is W [ 0 ] = W W^{[0]}=W , and by the choice of S S , the induced subgraph G ⁡ [ W [ 0 ] ] G[W^{[0]}] is connected. Also, since no forces occur prior to t = 1 t=1 , it is vacuously true that each force prior to that point is a valid standard force. Next consider the first positive semidefinite force ( u → v ) + (u\rightarrow v)_{+} that occurs at time t = 1 t=1 . By the positive semidefinite forcing rule, u u is a blue vertex with v v as its unique white neighbor in the component of G ⁡ [ W [ 0 ] ] G[W^{[0]}] containing v v . Since G ⁡ [ W [ 0 ] ] G[W^{[0]}] is connected, this implies that v v is the unique white neighbor of u u in G G . Thus, G ⁡ [ W [ 0 ] ] G[W^{[0]}] is connected and any positive semidefinite force at time t = 1 t=1 is also a valid standard force at time t = 1 t=1 . 
 
 
 Inductive Step: For our inductive step, we assume that for some t > 0 t>0 , we have that 
 
 (a) 
 
 The induced subgraph G ⁡ [ W [ t − 1 ] ] G[W^{[t-1]}] is connected, where W [ t − 1 ] W^{[t-1]} is the set of white vertices at time t − 1 t-1 . 
 
 (b) 
 
 Every positive semidefinite force up to and including time t t corresponds to a valid standard force in G G . 
 
 
 
 
 Let v v be the vertex that was forced to become blue at time t t by the vertex u u . We first verify that the induced subgraph of the remaining white vertices, G ⁡ [ W [ t ] ] G[W^{[t]}] , remains connected after the force at time t t has been applied, where 
 

 
 
 W [ t ] = W [ t − 1 ] ∖ { v } . W^{[t]}=W^{[t-1]}\setminus\{v\}. 
 
 
 Suppose, for contradiction, that G ⁡ [ W [ t ] ] G[W^{[t]}] is disconnected. This implies that the removal of v v from G ⁡ [ W [ t − 1 ] ] G[W^{[t-1]}] disconnects the white colored subgraph. Thus, v v is a cut-vertex of G ⁡ [ W [ t − 1 ] ] G[W^{[t-1]}] . Consequently, v v has at least two white neighbors in G ⁡ [ W [ t − 1 ] ] G[W^{[t-1]}] that belong to different components of G ⁡ [ W [ t ] ] G[W^{[t]}] , say w w and z z . Since ( u → v ) z (u\rightarrow v)_{z} is a valid standard force, v v is the unique white neighbor of u u in G G at time t − 1 t-1 . Therefore, u u is not adjacent to any of the white neighbors of v v , and in particular, the nonadjacent vertices w w and z z . Thus, the set { u , v , w , z } \{u,v,w,z\} induces a claw centered at v v , contradicting the assumption that G G is claw-free. Therefore, G ⁡ [ W [ t ] ] G[W^{[t]}] must be connected. 
 
 
 Next suppose that ( u ′ → v ′ ) + (u^{\prime}\rightarrow v^{\prime})_{+} is a valid positive semidefinite force at time t + 1 t+1 . By the positive semidefinite forcing rule, u ′ u^{\prime} is a blue vertex at time t t , and v ′ v^{\prime} is the unique white neighbor of u ′ u^{\prime} in the component of G ⁡ [ W [ t ] ] G[W^{[t]}] containing v ′ v^{\prime} . Since G ⁡ [ W [ t ] ] G[W^{[t]}] is connected, it follows that v ′ v^{\prime} is the unique white neighbor of u ′ u^{\prime} in G G . Therefore, ( u ′ → v ′ ) z (u^{\prime}\rightarrow v^{\prime})_{z} is a valid standard force in G G at time t + 1 t+1 . 
 
 
 By induction, we have established that at each time step t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 , the positive semidefinite force ( u → v ) + (u\rightarrow v)_{+} occurring during the chronological list of forces starting from S S is also a valid standard force. Therefore, the chronological list of positive semidefinite forces starting from S S effectively mirrors a standard forcing process. Since S S is a positive semidefinite forcing set, this process eventually colors all vertices of G G blue, which implies that S S is a standard forcing set of G G . Thus, we have 
 

 
 
 Z ⁡ ( G ) ≤ | S | = Z + ​ ( G ) , Z(G)\leq|S|=Z_{+}(G), 
 
 
 which when combined with the earlier inequality Z + ​ ( G ) ≤ Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)\leq Z(G) , implies Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) , our desired result.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Since the standard and positive semidefinite forcing numbers are additive with respect to components, it is clear that any disconnected and claw-free graph G G satisfies Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) . Using this fact, together with induced subgraphs of claw-free graphs being claw-free, we now give a characterization of claw-free graphs in terms of standard and positive semidefinite forcing. 
 
 
 Corollary 6 . 
 
 A graph is ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) -perfect if and only if it is claw-free. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let G G be a graph. If G G is claw-free, then any induced subgraph H H of G G is also claw-free. Thus, by Theorem  5 , Z + ​ ( H ) = Z ​ ( H ) Z_{+}(H)=Z(H) for every induced subgraph H H of G G , and G G is ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) -perfect. 
 
 
 Conversely, and proceeding by way of contraposition, suppose G G is a graph containing at least one induced subgraph H ⊆ G H\subseteq G with H ≅ K 1 , 3 H\cong K_{1,3} . Then, 
 

 
 
 1 = Z + ​ ( H ) < Z ⁡ ( H ) = 2 , 1=Z_{+}(H)<Z(H)=2, 
 
 
 implying that G G is not ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) -perfect. Thus,
 G G is ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) -perfect if and only if G G is claw-free.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 5 CONCLUSION 

 
 In this paper, we proved that Z + ​ ( G ) = Z ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G)=Z(G) for all claw-free graphs (Theorem  5 ), thereby confirming Conjecture  1 proposed by TxGraffiti . As a consequence, we established that a graph G G is ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) –perfect if and only if G G is claw-free (Corollary  6 ). This result not only characterizes ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) –perfect graphs but also unifies and extends prior work on the relationship between Z ⁡ ( G ) Z(G) and Z + ​ ( G ) Z_{+}(G) . 
 
 
 Our characterization naturally leads to several questions for future research. Can similar equivalences be identified for other graph classes or for other variants of zero forcing? What are the algorithmic consequences of ( Z + , Z ) (Z_{+},Z) –perfectness, particularly for claw-free graphs? Moreover, how might these results generalize to directed graphs or other settings? Investigating these questions may provide further insights into the connections between structural graph theory and zero forcing concepts.
