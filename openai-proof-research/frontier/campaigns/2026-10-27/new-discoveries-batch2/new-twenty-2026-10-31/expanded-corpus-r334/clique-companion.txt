 Abstract 
 
 Recently Chase determined the maximum possible number of cliques of size t t in a graph on n n vertices with given maximum degree. Soon afterward, Chakraborti and Chen answered the version of this question in which we ask that the graph have m m edges and fixed maximum degree (without imposing any constraint on the number of vertices). In this paper we address these problems on hypergraphs. For s s -graphs with s ≥ 3 s\geq 3 a number of issues arise that do not appear in the graph case. For instance, for general s s -graphs we can assign degrees to any i i -subset of the vertex set with 1 ≤ i ≤ s − 1 1\leq i\leq s-1 . 
 
 We establish bounds on the number of t t -cliques in an s s -graph ℋ \mathcal{H} with i i -degree bounded by Δ \Delta in three contexts:
 ℋ \mathcal{H} has n n vertices; ℋ \mathcal{H} has m m (hyper)edges; and (generalizing the previous case) ℋ \mathcal{H} has a fixed number p p of u u -cliques for some u u with s ≤ u ≤ t s\leq u\leq t . When Δ \Delta is of a special form we characterize the extremal s s -graphs and prove that the bounds are tight. These extremal examples are the shadows of either Steiner systems or partial Steiner systems. On the way to proving our uniqueness results, we extend results of Füredi and Griggs on uniqueness in Kruskal-Katona from the shadow case to the clique case. 
 
 
 
 1 Introduction 

 
 There has been recent interest in generalized Turán problems: determining the maximum (or minimum) number of copies of a fixed graph T T that a graph G G can contain, subject to a variety of constraints. The roots of this problem go back to Turán’s theorem [ 23 ] and its extension by Zykov [ 25 ] which determine, respectively, the maximum number of copies of K 2 K_{2} and K t K_{t} in a graph on n n vertices containing no K r + 1 K_{r+1} . The paper of Alon and Shikhelman [ 1 ] proved many foundational results and introduced the general problem to a wider audience. 
 
 
 1.1 Many cliques in bounded-degree graphs 

 
 We will focus on hypergraph versions of three generalized Turán problems: determining the maximum number of cliques in graphs of bounded degree, using either vertices, edges, or cliques as a “resource.” We discuss the graph problems below; for a more complete history see [ 2 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 12 , 17 , 18 ] . The first phase of progress in these problems consisted of “signpost” results: estimates that are best possible infinitely often, but not for all values of the parameters. 
 
 
 We write k t ​ ( G ) k^{t}(G) for the number of cliques of size t t (and always insist that t ≥ 1 t\geq 1 ). Similarly k ≥ t ​ ( G ) k^{\geq t}(G) is the number of cliques of size at least t t in G G . The next two theorems are versions of results due to Wood, phrased to match the hypergraph results we prove later. 
 
 
 Theorem 1 (Wood [ 24 ] ) . 
 
 If G G is a graph on n n vertices with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ r − 1 \Delta(G)\leq r-1 then 
 

 
 
 k t ​ ( G ) ≤ n r ​ ( r t ) and k ≥ 1 ​ ( G ) ≤ n r ​ ( 2 r − 1 ) k^{t}(G)\leq\frac{n}{r}\,\binom{r}{t}\quad\text{and}\quad k^{\geq 1}(G)\leq\frac{n}{r}\,\big(2^{r}-1\big) 
 
 
 with equality when G = a ​ K r G=aK_{r} . 
 
 
 
 Theorem 2 (Wood [ 24 ] ) . 
 
 If G G is a graph having m m edges with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ r − 1 \Delta(G)\leq r-1 then 
 

 
 
 k t ​ ( G ) ≤ m ( r 2 ) ​ ( r t ) and k ≥ 2 ​ ( G ) ≤ m ( r 2 ) ​ ( 2 r − r − 1 ) , k^{t}(G)\leq\frac{m}{\binom{r}{2}}\,\binom{r}{t}\quad\text{and}\quad k^{\geq 2}(G)\leq\frac{m}{\binom{r}{2}}\,\big(2^{r}-r-1\big), 
 
 
 with equality when G = a ​ K r G=aK_{r} . 
 
 
 
 Quite recently results in this direction were proved that are best possible for all values of the parameters. The vertex problem was solved by Chase [ 4 ] . He proved a conjecture of Gan, Loh, and Sudakov [ 12 ] using their reduction of the problem to the case t = 3 t=3 . Later Chao and Dong [ 3 ] gave a new proof of Theorem   3 that proves the result for all t t simultaneously. 
 
 
 Theorem 3 (Chase [ 4 ] , Chao and Dong [ 3 ] ) . 
 
 Let G G be a graph with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ r − 1 \Delta(G)\leq r-1 on n n vertices. Let a a and b b satisfy n = a ​ r + b n=ar+b with 0 ≤ b < r 0\leq b<r . Then 
 

 
 
 k t ​ ( G ) ≤ a ​ ( r t ) + ( b t ) , k^{t}(G)\leq a\binom{r}{t}+\binom{b}{t}, 
 
 
 with equality for the graph G = a ​ K r ∪ K b G=aK_{r}\cup K_{b} , the disjoint union of a a copies of K r K_{r} and one copy of K b K_{b} . 
 
 
 
 Using Theorem   3 , Chakraborti and Chen [ 2 ] solved the edge problem. 
 
 
 Theorem 4 (Chakraborti and Chen [ 2 ] ) . 
 
 Let G G be a graph with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ r − 1 \Delta(G)\leq r-1 having m m edges. Let a a and b b satisfy m = a ​ ( r 2 ) + b m=a\binom{r}{2}+b with 0 ≤ b < ( r 2 ) 0\leq b<\binom{r}{2} . Then 
 

 
 
 k t ​ ( G ) ≤ a ​ ( r t ) + k t ​ ( 𝒞 2 ​ ( b ) ) , k^{t}(G)\leq a\binom{r}{t}+k^{t}(\mathcal{C}_{2}(b)), 
 
 
 with equality for the graph G = a ​ K r ∪ 𝒞 2 ​ ( b ) G=aK_{r}\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}(b) . Here, 𝒞 2 ​ ( b ) \mathcal{C}_{2}(b) is the colex graph having b b edges: the graph on vertex set ℕ \mathbb{N} whose edges are the first b b pairs in colexicographic order. 
 
 
 
 In this paper we are concerned with hypergraph versions of these problems. To state the questions we need to introduce our notation for hypergraphs and discuss the issue of degrees in hypergraphs. This we do next. 
 
 
 In Section   2 we discuss various versions of the Kruskal-Katona theorem, which is central in this area. In Section   3 we prove general results for arbitrary degree bounds. In Section   4 we introduce constructions which, in some cases, give optimal examples, and prove some results about optimality and asymptotic optimality. Finally, in Section   5 we mention some open problems.

 1.3 Related extremal problems 

 
 The area of extremal problems for hypergraphs is rich and deep. The Kruskal-Katona theorem, which we discuss in Section   2 , is an upper bound on the number of t t -cliques in an s s -graph with a given number of edges. Moreover, it implies a bound on the number of t t -cliques in an s s -graph having a given number of u u -cliques for some s < u ≤ t s<u\leq t . In [ 9 ] , Frohmader improved this bound in the case s = 2 s=2 . 
 
 
 The Kruskal-Katona theorem puts few restrictions on the s s -graphs involved. A substantial amount of work has been done when we forbid large cliques in our s s -graphs. The earliest such result is by Zykov [ 25 ] . He proved the following result for graphs. 
 
 
 Theorem 7 (Zykov [ 25 ] ) . 
 
 If ℋ \mathcal{H} is a graph on n n vertices containing no ( r + 1 ) (r+1) -clique then k t ​ ( ℋ ) ≤ k t ​ ( T r ​ ( n ) ) k^{t}(\mathcal{H})\leq k^{t}(T_{r}(n)) . Here T r ​ ( n ) T_{r}(n) is the Turán graph , that is to say it is the complete r r -partite graph on n n vertices whose parts are of sizes as equal as possible. 
 
 
 
 The analogous result where we constrain G G to have m m edges is much more recent. The following result is due to Frohmader [ 8 ] . To describe the result we need to define the r r -partite colex Turán graph. Let r r be a positive integer. The r r -partite colex order is the restriction of the colex order on ( ℕ 2 ) \binom{\mathbb{N}}{2} to { i ​ j : i ≢ j ( mod r ) } \{ij\,:\,i\not\equiv j\pmod{r}\} . The r r -partite colex Turán graph with m m edges , C ​ T r ​ ( m ) CT_{r}(m) , is the graph on vertex set ℕ \mathbb{N} whose edge set consists of the first m m edges in r r -partite colex order. (Note that if m = t r ​ ( n ) m=t_{r}(n) , then the unique non-trivial component of C ​ T r ​ ( m ) CT_{r}(m) is isomorphic to T r ​ ( n ) T_{r}(n) .) 
 
 
 Theorem 8 (Frohmader [ 8 ] ) . 
 
 If G G is a K r + 1 K_{r+1} -free graph with m m edges and 2 ≤ t ≤ r 2\leq t\leq r , then k t ​ ( G ) ≤ k t ​ ( C ​ T r ​ ( m ) ) k^{t}(G)\leq k^{t}\bigl(CT_{r}(m)\bigr) . 
 
 
 
 In stark contrast to these positive results about graphs, even the Turán problem for s s -graphs with s > 2 s>2 is apparently intractable.
For no r > s ≥ 3 r>s\geq 3 is the problem of determining 
 

 
 
 max ⁡ { | ℋ | : ℋ  is an  s -graph on vertex set  [ n ]  not containing an  ( r + 1 ) -clique } \max\{|\mathcal{H}|\,:\,\text{$\mathcal{H}$ is an $s$-graph on vertex set $[n]$ not containing an $(r+1)$-clique}\} 
 
 
 solved for all n n , even asymptotically. (See Keevash’s survey [ 15 ] for extensive discussion of this problem.) The hypergraph analogue of Theorem   8 seems no easier. 
 
 
 In a recent paper, Liu and Wang [ 20 ] determined the maximum number of t t -cliques in an s s -graph on n n vertices containing at most k k disjoint edges (for n n sufficiently large). 
 
 
 In the context of hypergraphs with bounded degree, Jung [ 13 ] considered the question of minimizing the ratio | ∂ s − 1 ( ℋ ) | / | ℋ | |\partial_{s-1}(\mathcal{H})|/|\mathcal{H}| for s s -graphs ℋ \mathcal{H} having bounded 1 1 -degree. Jung’s results have a similar spirit to ours, but are not directly comparable. In an opposite direction Füredi and Zhao [ 11 ] considered 3 3 -graphs ℋ \mathcal{H} with large minimum degree and gave asymptotically best possible lower bounds on the size of ∂ 2 ( ℋ ) \partial_{2}(\mathcal{H}) . 
 
 
 
 
 2 The Kruskal-Katona Theorem 

 
 The fundamental theorem given in Theorem   10 below was proved independently by Kruskal [ 19 ] and Katona [ 14 ] . It shows that for a given number of edges m m , the s s -graph with the most t t -cliques and the smallest q q -shadow is the colex hypergraph , denoted 𝒞 s ​ ( m ) \mathcal{C}_{s}(m) , whose edges form an initial segment in the colexicographic (or colex ) order . Colex order is defined on finite subsets of ℕ \mathbb{N} by A < B A<B iff max ⁡ ( A △ B ) ∈ B \max(A\bigtriangleup B)\in B . The original version of the Kruskal-Katona theorem discussed only shadows, but the version below describes also a closely related version, giving bounds on the number of cliques in s s -graphs. For completeness we prove these versions (and slightly more) in Appendix   A . 
 
 
 Definition 9 . 
 
 We define the following functions mapping a number of edges m m to the the size of the q q -shadow and the number of t t -cliques of 𝒞 s ​ ( m ) \mathcal{C}_{s}(m) . 
 

 
 
 ∂ q s ( m ) = | ∂ q ( 𝒞 s ​ ( m ) ) | and k s t ​ ( m ) = k t ​ ( 𝒞 s ​ ( m ) ) . \partial_{q}^{s}(m)=|\partial_{q}(\mathcal{C}_{s}(m))|\quad\text{and}\quad k^{t}_{s}(m)=k^{t}(\mathcal{C}_{s}(m)).

 For all 0 ≤ q < s < t ≤ n 0\leq q<s<t\leq n , if 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is an s s -graph on vertex set V V with | V | = n |V|=n then we have 
 

 
 
 | ∂ q ( 𝒜 ) | ≥ ∂ q s ( m ) , and k t ​ ( 𝒜 ) ≤ k s t ​ ( m ) , |\partial_{q}(\mathcal{A})|\geq\partial_{q}^{s}(m),\qquad\text{and}\qquad k^{t}(\mathcal{A})\leq k^{t}_{s}(m),

 2.2 Lovász Kruskal-Katona 

 
 Cascades have the merit of giving the precise values of ∂ q s ( m ) \partial_{q}^{s}(m) and k s t ​ ( m ) k^{t}_{s}(m) , but are somewhat unwieldy to work with. There is a simpler form of the Kruskal-Katona theorem, due to Lovász [ 21 ] , that is often strong enough.  4.2 Steiner shadows and packing shadows 

 
 Here we define and discuss some important hypergraphs that turn out to be optimal examples in some cases of our problem. 
 
 
 Definition 38 . 
 
 A Steiner system with parameters i , r , n i,r,n (abbreviated as an S ⁡ ( i , r , n ) S(i,r,n) ) is a collection of r r -sets of some n n -set V V that covers each i i -set of V V exactly once.
That is to say, it is an r r -graph 𝒜 \mathcal{A} on vertex set V V such that for all I ∈ ( V i ) I\in\binom{V}{i} there exists a unique A ∈ 𝒜 A\in\mathcal{A} such that I ⊆ A I\subseteq A . 
 
 
 
 It has been known for a long time (by straightforward counting arguments) that in order for a Steiner system with parameters i , r , n i,r,n to exist it must be the case that certain divisibility conditions are satisfied. In groundbreaking work Peter Keevash [ 16 ] showed (among other things) that for sufficiently large n n these conditions are also sufficient. 
 
 
 Theorem 39 (Keevash [ 16 ] ) . 
 
 For fixed i ≤ r i\leq r and for n n sufficiently large, an S ⁡ ( i , r , n ) S(i,r,n) exists if and only if for all 0 ≤ j < i 0\leq j<i we have that ( r − j ) ( i − j ) (r-j)_{(i-j)} divides ( n − j ) ( i − j ) (n-j)_{(i-j)} . 
 
 
 
 Corollary 40 . 
 
 For fixed i ≤ r i\leq r , the set of n n for which an S ⁡ ( i , r , n ) S(i,r,n) exists has positive lower density. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The divisibility conditions are certainly satisfied if n − i + 1 n-i+1 is divisible by r ( i ) r_{(i)} , so the lower density of { n : an  S ⁡ ( i , r , n )  exists } \{n\,:\,\text{an $S(i,r,n)$ exists}\} is at least 1 / r ( i ) 1/r_{(i)} .
∎ 
 
 
 
 We can weaken the definition of a Steiner system to require only that each i i -set is covered at most once (rather than exactly once), giving the following definition. 
 
 
 Definition 41 . 
 
 An i i -packing of r r -sets (abbreviated as a P ⁡ ( i , r ) P(i,r) ), also called a partial Steiner system , is a collection of r r -sets of some set V V that covers each i i -set of V V at most once. That is to say, it is an r r -graph 𝒜 \mathcal{A} on vertex set V V such that for all I ∈ ( V i ) I\in\binom{V}{i} there exists at most one A ∈ 𝒜 A\in\mathcal{A} such that I ⊆ A I\subseteq A . Equivalently, any distinct r r -sets A , B ∈ 𝒜 A,B\in\mathcal{A} have | A ∩ B | < i |A\cap B|<i .
