 Abstract 
 
 Negami’s famous planar cover conjecture is equivalent to the statement that a connected graph can be embedded in the projective plane if and only if it has a projective planar cover. In 1999, Hliněnỳ proposed extending this conjecture to higher genus non-orientable surfaces. In this paper, we put forward a natural extension that encompasses orientable surfaces as well; for every compact surface Σ \Sigma , a connected graph G G has a finite cover embeddable in Σ \Sigma if and only if G G is embeddable in a surface covered by Σ \Sigma . 
 
 As evidence toward this, we prove that for every surface Σ \Sigma , the connected graphs with a finite cover embeddable in Σ \Sigma have bounded Euler genus.
Moreover, we show that these extensions of Negami’s conjecture are decidable for every compact surface of sufficiently large Euler genus, surpassing what is known for Negami’s original conjecture.
We also prove the natural analogue for countable graphs embeddable into a compact (orientable) surface.
More precisely, we prove that a connected countable graph G G has a finite ply cover that embeds into a compact (orientable) surface if and only if G G embeds into a compact (orientable) surface. 
 
 Our most general theorem, from which these results are derived, is that there is a constant c > 0 c>0 such that for every surface Σ \Sigma , there exists a decreasing function p Σ : ℕ → ℕ p_{\Sigma}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N} with lim g → ∞ p Σ ​ ( g ) = 0 \lim_{g\to\infty}p_{\Sigma}(g)=0 such that every finite cover embeddable in Σ \Sigma of any connected graph with Euler genus g ⩾ c g\geqslant c has ply at most p Σ ​ ( g ) p_{\Sigma}(g) . 
 
 
 
 1 Introduction 

 
 A graph G ^ \widehat{G} is a cover of a graph G G if there exists a surjective map φ : V ⁡ ( G ^ ) → V ⁡ ( G ) \varphi:V(\widehat{G})\to V(G) such that, for every v ^ ∈ V ⁡ ( G ^ ) \widehat{v}\in V(\widehat{G}) , the neighbours of v ^ \widehat{v} are mapped bijectively to the neighbours of φ ⁡ ( v ^ ) \varphi(\widehat{v}) . Given a subgraph S ⊂ G S\subset G , the lift S ^ \widehat{S} of S S (into G ^ \widehat{G} ) is the preimage φ − 1 \varphi^{-1} . When G G is connected and G ^ \widehat{G} is finite, there is a natural number p p such that | φ − 1 ​ ( v ) | = p |\varphi^{-1}(v)|=p for every v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) . We call this common preimage size p p the ply of the cover. If G ^ \widehat{G} is planar, then we say that G G has a planar cover . 
 
 
 In 1988, Negami [ 21 ] made the beautiful conjecture that a connected graph has a finite planar cover if and only if it can be embedded on the projective plane. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1 (Negami [ 21 ] , 1988) . 
 
 A connected graph has a finite planar cover if and only if it is projective-planar. 
 
 
 
 One direction is easy: since the sphere is a double cover of the projective plane, every projective-planar graph has a finite planar cover. For the converse, the main approach has been based on the observation that if a graph H H has a finite planar cover, then so does every minor of H H . Building on work of Glover, Huneke and Wang [ 7 ] , Archdeacon [ 2 ] proved a Kuratowski theorem for projective-planar graphs in 1981, characterizing embeddability on the projective plane by a list of 35 forbidden minors. To prove Negami’s planar cover conjecture, it would suffice to show that none of the connected forbidden minors in this list have a planar cover. By 1998, the combined work of Archdeacon [ 3 ] , Hliněnỳ [ 9 ] , Fellows [ 5 ] , and Negami [ 20 ] had settled all but one case, reducing Negami’s conjecture to whether K 1 , 2 , 2 , 2 K_{1,2,2,2} has a finite planar cover. For an excellent survey on this progress, we refer to [ 13 ] . Notably, this last case has remained open even after another 25 years. 
 
 
 This is not to say that there have been no further advancements. Most relevantly to the present paper, Annor, Nikolayevsky and Payne [ 1 ] recently proved that K 1 , 2 , 2 , 2 K_{1,2,2,2} has no finite planar cover with ply at most 13. This means that any cover in a counterexample to Negami’s conjecture must have ply at least 14. However, there is no known upper bound on the ply of a smallest finite planar cover of K 1 , 2 , 2 , 2 K_{1,2,2,2} , and consequently no upper bound on the ply of a counterexample. Such a result would be very attractive as it would render the problem finite. 
 
 
 In 1999, Hliněnỳ [ 10 ] considered extensions of Negami’s conjecture to higher genus surfaces. The surfaces in this paper are connected compact 2-manifolds without boundary, and hence specified by their (non)-orientability and genus as per the classification of surfaces (see [ 19 , Chapter 3.1] ). Generalising planar covers, if a graph G G has a cover G ^ \widehat{G} that is embeddable in a surface Σ \Sigma , then we call this a Σ \Sigma -cover . An equivalent statement to Conjecture   1.1 is then that a connected graph can be embedded in the projective plane if and only if it has a projective cover.
Hliněnỳ [ 10 ] gave this formulation and proposed the following generalisation, where the projective plane is replaced by non-orientable surfaces of any genus. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.2 (Hliněnỳ [ 10 , 11 ] , 1999) . 
 
 Let Σ \Sigma be a non-orientable surface.
Then, a connected graph has a finite Σ \Sigma -cover if and only if it is embeddable in Σ \Sigma . 
 
 
 
 The preceding statement cannot be true for orientable surfaces since there are graphs with planar covers that have arbitrarily high orientable genus [ 4 ] . Nonetheless, the fact that the orientable surface of Euler genus g g covers itself and the non-orientable surface of Euler genus g / 2 + 1 g/2+1 (see, for instance, [ 8 ] ) leads to a natural analogue for orientable surfaces. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.3 . 
 
 For every orientable surface Σ \Sigma of Euler genus g g , a connected graph has a finite Σ \Sigma -cover if and only if it is embeddable in Σ \Sigma or embeddable in the non-orientable surface of genus g / 2 + 1 g/2+1 . 
 
 
 
 We also propose the following conjecture that unifies Conjecture   1.2 and Conjecture   1.3 . 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.4 . 
 
 For every surface Σ \Sigma of Euler genus g g ,
a connected graph has a finite Σ \Sigma -cover if and only if it is embeddable in a surface finitely covered by Σ \Sigma . 
 
 
 
 In this paper, we provide evidence toward Conjecture   1.4 and a theoretical framework for verifying it. Our main theorem, from which will derive several key consequences, does what has not yet been achieved for Negami’s conjecture and provides an upper bound on the ply of a Σ \Sigma -cover for graphs with sufficiently high Euler genus.

 For every surface Σ \Sigma , the connected graphs with a finite Σ \Sigma -cover have bounded Euler genus. 
 
 
 
 The second significant implication of Theorem   1.5 is that Conjecture   1.4 is decidable for each surface of Euler genus at least c c . To see this, let us fix such a surface Σ \Sigma . A result of Seymour [ 26 ] gives an explicit upper bound on the size of a minimal forbidden minor of a graph embeddable in any fixed surface.
Since there are algorithms for testing if a given graph is embeddable in a given surface (see [ 18 ] ), one can find in finite time the minimal forbidden minors for any surface covered by Σ \Sigma .
Given the forbidden minors for two classes of graphs (the classes of graphs embeddable in two surfaces covered by Σ \Sigma respectively, say), one can also find in finite time the minimal forbidden minors for the union of the two classes [ 23 ] . Putting this together, we can find in finite time the (finite list of) minimal forbidden minors ℋ Σ \mathcal{H}_{\Sigma} that characterise the class of graphs embeddable in some surface covered by Σ \Sigma . 
 
 
 To prove (or disprove) Conjecture   1.4 for Σ \Sigma , it is then enough to consider the graphs in ℋ Σ \mathcal{H}_{\Sigma} .
Suppose that all graphs in ℋ Σ \mathcal{H}_{\Sigma} have at most n Σ n_{\Sigma} vertices.
Then, one can enumerate all graphs with at most n Σ ​ p Σ n_{\Sigma}p_{\Sigma} vertices and test one by one whether they are embeddable in Σ \Sigma and whether they cover a graph in ℋ Σ \mathcal{H}_{\Sigma} .
If such a graph exists, then it (along with its embedding in Σ \Sigma ) provides a counter-example to Conjecture   1.4 for the surface Σ \Sigma .
Otherwise, by Theorem   1.5 , if no such graph exists then this algorithm provides a proof of Conjecture   1.4 for the surface Σ \Sigma .
Thus, we obtain: 
 
 
 Theorem 1.7 . 
 
 For each surface Σ \Sigma of Euler genus at least c c , there is a finite time algorithm that determines whether or not Conjecture   1.4 is true for the surface Σ \Sigma , and produces a counter-example if it its not true. 
 
 
 
 The caveat here is that the algorithm described above will in general require a huge number of steps, so it does not directly give a practical answer to our question.
It may be possible, however, to extend the ideas of the present paper to improve Theorem   1.5 and settle Conjecture   1.4 for at least some surfaces. In addition, the preceding theorem is already in contrast to Negami’s conjecture, where the question of decidability is still open. It is interesting that this result brings us closer in some sense to proving high genus analogues of Negami’s conjecture than proving Negami’s conjecture itself, despite the latter being reduced to essentially just one remaining case.
One other famous open conjecture in graph minor theory which is known to be decidable (for each k k ) is Hadwiger’s conjecture [ 15 ] . 
 
 
 The proof of Theorem   1.5 crucially uses a structural theorem of Robertson and Seymour [ 25 ] (see Theorem   2.2 ) concerning excluded minors, which similarly to Archdeacon’s theorem [ 2 ] for projective-planar graphs allows us to reduce to studying covers of a very specific family of graphs. While we have so far worked with finite graphs, these tools all have counterparts for infinite (countable) graphs that are actually even stronger. Hence, the same outline proves an analogue of Negami’s conjecture for countable graphs embeddable in a compact surface.
For this, we use a recent forbidden minor theorem of Georgakopoulos [ 6 ] (see Theorem   2.6 ). 
 
 
 Theorem 1.8 . 
 
 A connected countable graph G G has a finite ply cover that embeds into a compact (orientable) surface if and only if G G embeds into a compact (orientable) surface. 
 
 
 
 Note that the surfaces in Theorem   1.8 can be restricted to orientable ones since every countable graph embeddable in a compact surface is embeddable in a compact orientable surface [ 6 ] . 
 
 
 In Section   2 , we discuss the aforementioned structural results for graphs on surfaces in both the finite and countable cases. At the end of that section, we state the remaining result connecting structure to ply that we need to prove Theorem   1.5 and Theorem   1.8 and give these arguments. The purpose of Section   3 is then fill in this last result, which entails verifying that all Σ \Sigma -covers of the excluded graphs from our structural results have bounded ply. For basic concepts of topological graph theory we follow [ 19 ] , and for standard facts about surfaces we refer to [ 8 ] . 
 
 
 
 2 Surfaces and forbidden minors 

 
 When dealing with embedded graphs, a useful operation that preserves many topological properties is the Y ​ Δ Y\Delta -transformation, which is performed by deleting a vertex v v of degree 3 and adding three edges between the vertices of N ⁡ ( v ) N(v) to form a triangle. We will study a slightly more general relation that still works well with covers. Say that a graph G G is a Y-minor of a graph H H if G G can be obtained from H H by any sequence of the following operations: adding an edge between two neighbours of a degree 3 vertex, deleting a vertex, deleting an edge, and contracting an edge. This is slightly more general than the usual notion of minors (in particular, note that if H H is a minor of G G , then H H is also a Y-minor of G G ). 
 
 
 Proposition 2.1 . 
 
 If a graph H H has a p p -ply cover in a surface Σ \Sigma , then so does every Y-minor of H H .

This is clear for the graph minor operations using the fact that φ \varphi is a bijection on neighbourhoods, and that the class of graphs embeddable in Σ \Sigma is minor-closed:

 3 Bounding ply for excluded Y-minors 

 
 The goal of this section is to prove Lemma   2.13 . This boils down to showing that if H H is one of the excluded Y-minors in Theorem   2.11 or Theorem   2.12 , then the ply of any Σ \Sigma -cover H ^ \widehat{H} of H H is suitably bounded in terms of the Euler genus of Σ \Sigma . In all cases, the basic idea is that, since covers are bijective on neighbourhoods, one can easily relate the ply to the number of edges in H ^ \widehat{H} and number of vertices in H ^ \widehat{H} . Euler’s formula provides a means to bound these quantities, but to apply it effectively, we will need to consider embedded multigraphs and their minors. 
 
 
 A face of a multigraph G G embedded in a surface Σ \Sigma is a component of Σ − G \Sigma-G homeomorphic to an open disk. A 2-face is a face bounded by just 2 edges.
We say that two edges in an embedded multigraph are parallel if they form a 2-face in the embedding.
In this section, when taking minors of embedded graphs, we only delete multiedges in the (multi)graph after an edge contraction so that there is no pair of parallel edges (in other words, so that the embedded (multigraph) has no 2-face).
This is to ensure that good bounds can be obtained from Euler’s formula, as below (see Section 3.1 of [ 19 ] ). 
 
 
 Theorem 3.1 . 
 
 Let G G be a n n -vertex multigraph embedded on a surface of Euler genus g g without 2-faces.
Then | E ⁡ ( G ) | ⩽ 3 ​ n − 6 + 3 ​ g |E(G)|\leqslant 3n-6+3g . 
 
 
 
 Theorem 3.2 . 
 
 Let G G be a n n -vertex bipartite multigraph embedded on a surface of Euler genus g g without 2-faces.
Then | E ⁡ ( G ) | ⩽ 2 ​ n − 4 + 2 ​ g |E(G)|\leqslant 2n-4+2g . 
 
 
 
 This is already enough to handle the easiest graph in our collection; K 3 , k K_{3,k} .

 We are now ready to handle the last two sum-Kuratowski graphs; Θ 1 , k \Theta_{1,k} and then Π 1 , k \Pi_{1,k} . 
 
 
 Lemma 3.9 . 
 
 Let Σ \Sigma be a surface of Euler genus g g and let k ⩾ 9 k\geqslant 9 .
If Θ 1 , k \Theta_{1,k} has a finite p p -ply Σ \Sigma -cover, then p ⩽ 28 ​ g k − 8 p\leqslant\frac{28g}{k-8} . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let H ^ \widehat{H} be a p p -ply cover of Θ 1 , k \Theta_{1,k} embedded in Σ \Sigma .
Let 𝒞 \mathcal{C} be the hanging triangles of Θ 1 , k \Theta_{1,k} .
By Lemma   3.8 , we may assume that for every hanging triangle C C of H H , every contractible component of C ^ \widehat{C} is a facial triangle.
By Lemma   3.6 , we may assume that at least half of the vertices of 𝒞 ^ \widehat{\mathcal{C}} are contained in contractible components. 
 
 
 Let x x and y y be the joining vertices of Θ 1 , k \Theta_{1,k} .
Let x 1 , … , x p x_{1},\ldots,x_{p} be the vertices of H ^ \widehat{H} that project onto x x and let y 1 , … , y p y_{1},\ldots,y_{p} be the vertices of H ^ \widehat{H} that project onto y y .
Let C 1 , … , C r C_{1},\ldots,C_{r} be the contractible (facial triangle) components of 𝒞 ^ \widehat{\mathcal{C}} .
Then k ​ p / 2 ⩽ r kp/2\leqslant r .
Now, let G G be the embedded graph obtained from H ^ \widehat{H} by deleting all non-contractible components of 𝒞 ^ \widehat{\mathcal{C}} , deleting each edge that projects onto x ​ y xy , and then contracting each C i C_{i} to a vertex c i c_{i} .
Then G G is bipartite with | V ⁡ ( G ) | = 2 ​ p + r |V(G)|=2p+r . 
 
 
 Each c i c_{i} is adjacent to vertices of the form x j x_{j} and y k y_{k} , and so certainly has minimum degree at least 2.
If c i c_{i} has degree exactly 2 for some i i , then H ^ \widehat{H} would contain an embedding of K 5 − e K_{5}-e on vertex set C i ∪ { x j , y k } C_{i}\cup\{x_{j},y_{k}\} where C i C_{i} is a facial triangle, x i ​ y k x_{i}y_{k} is not an edge, and there is no non-contractible cycle.
But there is no such planar embedding of K 5 − e K_{5}-e , so we may conclude that every c i c_{i} has minimum degree at least 3. This implies that | E ⁡ ( G ) | ⩾ 3 ​ r |E(G)|\geqslant 3r . 
 
 
 Plugging into Theorem   3.2 , we obtain 3 ​ r ⩽ 2 ​ ( 2 ​ p + r ) − 4 + 2 ​ g 3r\leqslant 2(2p+r)-4+2g , so r ⩽ 4 ​ p + 2 ​ g − 4 r\leqslant 4p+2g-4 , and therefore k ​ p / 2 ⩽ 4 ​ p + 2 ​ g − 4 ⩽ 4 ​ p + 2 ​ g kp/2\leqslant 4p+2g-4\leqslant 4p+2g .
Since k > 8 k>8 , this gives p ⩽ 4 ​ g k − 8 ⩽ 28 ​ g k − 8 p\leqslant\frac{4g}{k-8}\leqslant\frac{28g}{k-8} , as desired.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Lemma 3.10 . 
 
 Let Σ \Sigma be a surface of Euler genus g g and let k ⩾ 25 k\geqslant 25 .
If Π 1 , k \Pi_{1,k} has a finite p p -ply Σ \Sigma -cover, then p ⩽ 28 ​ g k − 24 p\leqslant\frac{28g}{k-24} . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let H ^ \widehat{H} be a p p -ply cover of Π 1 , k \Pi_{1,k} embedded in Σ \Sigma .
Let 𝒞 \mathcal{C} be the hanging triangles of Π 1 , k \Pi_{1,k} .

 Let P P be the joining path of Π 1 , k \Pi_{1,k} .
Then, P ^ \widehat{P} consists of p p paths P 1 , … , P p P_{1},\ldots,P_{p} isomorphic to P P .
Let C 1 , … , C r C_{1},\ldots,C_{r} be the contractible (facial triangle) components of 𝒞 ^ \widehat{\mathcal{C}} .
Then k ​ p / 2 ⩽ r kp/2\leqslant r .
Consider the minor G 1 G_{1} of H ^ \widehat{H} , obtained from H ^ \widehat{H} by performing the following operations:

 3.

 It is clear that G 1 G_{1} is bipartite and | V ⁡ ( G 1 ) | = p + r |V(G_{1})|=p+r .
Since K 5 K_{5} is non-planar, it follows that each c i c_{i} has degree at least two in G 1 G_{1} .
Partition { c 1 , … , c r } \{c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\} into two sets: X X consisting of those with degree at least three, and Y Y consisting of those with degree two.

Hence, we have p ⩽ 8 ​ g k − 8 ⩽ 28 ​ g k − 24 p\leqslant\frac{8g}{k-8}\leqslant\frac{28g}{k-24} as desired. 
 
 
 Otherwise, we may assume that | Y | ⩾ r / 2 ⩾ k ​ p / 4 |Y|\geqslant r/2\geqslant kp/4 .

Then V ⁡ ( G 2 ) = { p 1 , … , p p } V(G_{2})=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{p}\} is a set of size p p .

Then there exist vertices c 1 ′ , c 2 ′ , c 3 ′ ∈ Y c_{1}^{\prime},c_{2}^{\prime},c_{3}^{\prime}\in Y and p 1 ′ , p 2 ′ ∈ { p 1 , … , p p } p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}\in\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{p}\} of G 1 G_{1} such that the three paths p 1 ′ ​ c 1 ′ ​ p 2 ′ p_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime} , p 1 ′ ​ c 2 ′ ​ p 2 ′ p_{1}^{\prime}c_{2}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime} , and p 1 ′ ​ c 3 ′ ​ p 2 ′ p_{1}^{\prime}c_{3}^{\prime}p_{2}^{\prime} all have the same homotopy type.

 Observe that in J J , there is a path Q 1 Q_{1} between x a 2 x_{a_{2}} and y a 2 y_{a_{2}} on the vertex set T 1 ∪ { x 1 , … , x a 2 } ∪ { y 1 , … , y a 2 } T_{1}\cup\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{a_{2}}\}\cup\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{a_{2}}\} , and another path Q 3 Q_{3} between x a 2 + 1 x_{a_{2}+1} and y a 2 + 1 y_{a_{2}+1} on the vertex set T 3 ∪ { x a 2 + 1 , … , x k + 1 } ∪ { y a 2 + 1 , … , y k + 1 } T_{3}\cup\{x_{a_{2}+1},\ldots,x_{k+1}\}\cup\{y_{a_{2}+1},\ldots,y_{k+1}\} .

Thus, | E ⁡ ( G 2 ) | ⩾ | Y | / 2 ⩾ r / 4 ⩾ k ​ p / 8 |E(G_{2})|\geqslant|Y|/2\geqslant r/4\geqslant kp/8 .
By Theorem   3.1 we then have k ​ p / 8 ⩽ 3 ​ p + 3 ​ g − 6 ⩽ 3 ​ p + 3 ​ g kp/8\leqslant 3p+3g-6\leqslant 3p+3g . This gives p ⩽ 24 ​ g k − 24 ⩽ 28 ​ g k − 24 p\leqslant\frac{24g}{k-24}\leqslant\frac{28g}{k-24} , as desired.
∎
