 Abstract 
 
 The Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is the only infinite sequence over the alphabet { 1 , 2 } \{1,2\} that starts with 1 and is its own run-length encoding.
In the present work, we take a step back from this largely known and studied sequence by introducing some randomness in the choice of the letters written. This enables us to provide some results on the convergence of the density of 1 1 ’s in the resulting sequence. When the choice of the letters is given by an infinite sequence of i.i.d. random variables or by a Markov chain, the average densities of letters converge. Moreover, in the case of i.i.d. random variables, we are able to prove that the densities even almost surely converge. 
 
 
 
 1 Introduction 

 
 The Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence 122112122122112 ​ … 122112122122112\dots introduced by R. Oldenburger [ 12 ] and lately mentioned by W.  In [ 12 ] , R. Oldenburger refers to sequences over an alphabet Σ \Sigma as trajectories and refers to the sequence Δ ⁡ ( w ) \Delta(w) as the exponent trajectory of the trajectory w w . He stated that << a periodic trajectory is distinct from its exponent trajectory >> (Theorem 2, [ 12 ] ) and, therefore, the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is not periodic. 
 
 
 The Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is also connected to differentiable words, C ∞ C^{\infty} -words and smooth words [ 2 , 4 , 8 ] . A sequence w w over the alphabet Σ ⊂ ℕ ⋆ \Sigma\subset\mathbb{N}^{\star} is differentiable if and only if Δ ⁡ ( w ) \Delta(w) is also defined over the same alphabet Σ \Sigma . The sequence Δ ⁡ ( w ) \Delta(w) is called the derivative sequence of w w   [ 8 ] . A C ∞ C^{\infty} -word , or smooth word , is an infinitely differentiable sequence. Obviously, the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is a C ∞ C^{\infty} -word since it is a fixed-point of the run-length encoding operator Δ \Delta . 
 
 
 Although not answering Keane’s question fully, F.M. Dekking established connections between possible combinatorial properties of the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence [ 8 ] : if the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is closed by complementation (that is, if w w occurs in 𝒪 \mathcal{O} then so does w ~ \widetilde{w} with 1 ~ = 2 \widetilde{1}=2 and 2 ~ = 1 \widetilde{2}=1 ) then it is recurrent (any word that occurs in 𝒪 \mathcal{O} does so infinitely often) (Prop. 1, [ 8 ] ). Moreover, the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is closed by complementation if and only if it contains every finite C ∞ C^{\infty} -word (Prop. 2, [ 8 ] ). 
 
 
 A few years later, A. Carpi stated that the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence contains only a finite set of squares (words of the form x ​ x xx where x x is not empty) and does not contain any cube (word of the form x ​ x ​ x xxx where x x is not empty) [ 6 ] . Hence, since 𝒪 \mathcal{O} contains only squares of bounded length then it cannot be the fixed point of a non degenerated morphism: the image of a square w = x ​ x w=xx by such a morphism is still a square longer than w w . 
 
 
 There exist several ways to extend the definition of the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence, depending on whether one wants to preserve the fixed point property or to follow the construction scheme without requiring the resulting sequence to be a fixed point for the run-length encoding operator Δ \Delta . For instance, one can deal with other alphabets and thus construct Generalized Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence (GOK-sequence for short) as follows: for any pair ( a , b ) (a,b) of non-zero natural numbers, there exists a unique fixed point 𝒪 a , b \mathcal{O}_{a,b} of Δ \Delta over the alphabet { a , b } \{a,b\} starting with a a . Also, according to this notation, the original Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence is 𝒪 1 , 2 \mathcal{O}_{1,2} .

 A significant result is, unlike the case of the original Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence, that the densities of 1 1 ’s in 𝒪 1 , 3 \mathcal{O}_{1,3} and 𝒪 3 , 1 \mathcal{O}_{3,1} are known and approximately 0.3972 [ 1 ] . 
 
 
 Generalized Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequences are also connected with smooth words over arbitrary alphabets [ 3 , 5 ] .
As for the (Generalized) Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequences, the properties of smooth words are better known for alphabets with letters of the same parity: for instance, while the frequency of letters in an infinite smooth word over { 1 , 2 } \{1,2\} is still unsolved, in [ 5 ] the authors showed that the frequency of letters for extremal smooth words (for the lexicographic order) over the alphabet { a , b } \{a,b\} , where a a and b b are both even, is 0.5. They also computed the frequency for extremal smooth words over alphabets of type { 1 , b } \{1,b\} , where b b is odd.
Moreover, if a a and b b have the same parity, then every infinite smooth word over the alphabet { a , b } \{a,b\} is recurrent [ 5 ] . Also, if a a and b b are both odd, then every infinite smooth word is closed under reversal but not under complementation [ 5 ] . On the other hand, if a a and b b are both even, then the extremal smooth words over the alphabet { a , b } \{a,b\} are neither closed under reversal nor closed under complementation [ 5 ] . 
 
 
 For a more detailed survey on the Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence and on generalizations over arbitrary two letter alphabets see [ 13 ] .

 2.3 Extension of the definition to infinite directing sequences 

 
 Now that we have started studying the notions of directing and directed sequences, a natural question arises: << Can one extend the definition of 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} sequences to infinite (possibly not periodic) sequences T T ? >>
Let 𝒜 ⊆ ℕ ⋆ \mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\star} be an alphabet and let T = ( t n ) n ∈ ℕ ⋆ T=(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}} be an infinite sequence over 𝒜 \mathcal{A} .
By construction, 𝒪 t 1 ​ … ​ t n \mathcal{O}_{t_{1}\dots t_{n}} is a prefix of 𝒪 t 1 ​ … ​ t n + 1 \mathcal{O}_{t_{1}\dots t_{n+1}} for each n ∈ ℕ ⋆ n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star} .  The present work deals with the densities of letters in 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} when T = ( t n ) n ∈ ℕ ⋆ T=(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}} is an infinite sequence over 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . Do these densities exist? If so, how much are they value? 
 
 
 We are especially interested in the case where the directing sequence is random. Let 𝕋 = ( T n ) n ∈ ℕ ⋆ \mathbb{T}=(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}} be a sequence of random variables. In section 3 , we consider the case where the directing sequence is made of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables over a two-letter alphabet. In section 4 , we treat the case where the random sequence 𝕏 \mathbb{X} is directed by a Markov chain.

 5 Non conservation of the density 

 
 In previous sections, we have studied different cases where the directing sequences are random. In all the cases we considered (sequences of independent and identically distributed random variables, Markovian sequences), the densities of letters of the directed sequence obtained are the same as those in the directing sequence, almost surely. 
 
 
 Simulations also suggest that for any (infinite) periodic sequence T T , the density of 1 1 ’s in directed sequence 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} is well-defined and is equal to the density of 1 1 ’s in T T , see Figure  1 .

 On Figure  1 , we have chosen to represent only the data on short prefixes of 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} so that it remains usable, especially to distinguish the densities in the very first terms of the sequence 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} . However, further experiments have been carried out on a large number of periodic sequences T T and they seem to corroborate our first impression, namely that if the sequence T T is periodic then the densities in 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} would be the same as those in T T . This leads us to state the following conjecture, that extends Keane’s conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 2 . 
 
 For any periodic sequence T T over the alphabet { 1 , 2 } \{1,2\} , the density of 1 1 ’s in the directed sequence 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} is well-defined and is equal to the density of 1 1 ’s in T T .

Obviously, because of Conjecture 2 , we do not expect to find such a candidate of directing sequence among the periodic ones.

 6 Conclusion and discussion 

 
 Over the alphabet { 1 , a } \{1,a\} , with a ∈ { 2 , 3 } a\in\{2,3\} , we have shown that in almost all the sequences directed by an infinite sequence 𝕋 = ( T n ) n ∈ ℕ ⋆ \mathbb{T}=(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}} of i.i.d. random variables with ℙ ⁡ ( T n = 1 ) = p ∈ ] 0 , 1 [ \mathbb{P}(T_{n}=1)=p\in]0,1[ and ℙ ⁡ ( T n = a ) = 1 − p \mathbb{P}(T_{n}=a)=1-p , the density of 1 1 ’s is equal to p p .
We have also shown that the average density of 1 1 ’s among all sequences directed by a Markov chain with transition probability p ∈ ] 0 , 1 [ p\in]0,1[ from 1 1 to a a and from a a to 1 is equal to 1 / 2 1/2 . 
 
 
 Keane’s conjecture [ 9 ] states that this result can be extended to the deterministic case, namely when p = 1 p=1 , over the alphabet { 1 , 2 } \{1,2\} . On the other hand, over the alphabet { 1 , 3 } \{1,3\} this result is not extendable to the deterministic case since the density of 1 1 ’s in 𝒪 1 , 3 \mathcal{O}_{1,3} is close to 0.3972. [ 1 ] . 
 
 
 When 𝕋 \mathbb{T} is a Markov chain, the closer its transition probability p p is to 1 1 , the more likely the sequence 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} is to share a long prefix with 𝒪 1 , 3 \mathcal{O}_{1,3} . Therefore, the closer the transition probability p p is to 1, the closer the density of 1’s in the sequence 𝒪 T \mathcal{O}_{T} is to that in the sequence 𝒪 1 , 3 \mathcal{O}_{1,3} on a long prefix. However, computer experiments suggest that when the first perturbations in the alternation of 1’s and 3’s appear in 𝕋 \mathbb{T} , the density of 1 1 ’s in the prefix of 𝒪 𝕋 \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{T}} eventually approaches 0.5 as this prefix gets longer. See Figure  3 for an illustration with p = 0.99 p=0.99 . 
 
 
 Figure 3: Evolution of the frequency of 1 1 ’s for a markovian directing sequence on the alphabet { 1 , 3 } \{1,3\} of parameter p = 0.99 p=0.99 : the frequency is first close to the one of 𝒪 1 , 3 \mathcal{O}_{1,3} then moves away from it to converge to 1 / 2 1/2 . 
 
 
 This implies it seems difficult to derive information about the original Oldenburger-Kolakoski sequence 𝒪 1 , 2 \mathcal{O}_{1,2} by letting p p tend to 1 1 in the Markovian case over the alphabet { 1 , 2 } \{1,2\} . 
 
 
 Finally, the study of sequences directed by random sequences on alphabets of more than 2 letters or by random sequences constructed from other distributions also seems interesting.
