 Abstract 
 
 In this paper, we discuss matching extendability
of optimal 1 1 -projective plane graphs (abbreviated as O1PPG),
which are drawn on the projective plane P 2 P^{2} so that
every edge crosses another edge at most once, and has n n vertices and exactly 4 ​ n − 4 4n-4 edges.
We first show that every O1PPG of even order is 1 1 -extendable.
Next, we characterize 2 2 -extendable O1PPG’s in terms of
a separating cycle consisting of only non-crossing edges.
Moreover, we characterize O1PPG’s having connectivity exactly 5 5 .
Using the characterization, we further identify three independent edges in those graphs
that are not extendable. 
 
 
 
 Keywords: 1-embeddable graph, projective plane, perfect matching 
 
 
 2020 Mathematical Subject Classification: 05C10 
 
 
 1 Introduction 

 
 Our graphs dealt in this paper are all finite, simple and connected.
We denote the vertex set and the edge set of a graph G G 
by V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) and E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) , respectively.
The order of G G means the number of vetices of G G .
A cycle of length k k is a k k -cycle .
For a cycle C C , an edge e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G) such that
 V ⁡ ( e ) ⊂ V ⁡ ( C ) V(e)\subset V(C) and e ∉ E ⁡ ( C ) e\notin E(C) is called a chord of C C .
A cycle C C in G G is separating 
if G − V ⁡ ( C ) G-V(C) is a disconnected graph.
We denote the induced subgraph of a graph G G by S ⊂ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subset V(G) by G ⁡ [ S ] G[S] .
A set M M of edges of a graph G G is a matching 
if no two edges of M M share a vertex.
Let M M be a matching of a graph G G .
Each vertex incident with
an edge of M M is covered by M M .
The set of vertices covered by M M is denoted by V ⁡ ( M ) V(M) .
In particular,
 M M is perfect if M M covers all vertices of G G ; that is, V ⁡ ( G ) = V ⁡ ( M ) V(G)=V(M) .
A matching M M of G G is extendable 
if G G has a perfect matching containing M M .
Moreover,
a graph G G with at least 2 ​ k + 2 2k+2 vertices is k k -extendable 
if any matching M M in G G with | M | = k |M|=k is extendable.
Matching extendability has been widely studied in literature (e.g., see [ 13 ] ).
In particular, matching extendability of graphs on closed surfaces was investigated in
 [ 1 , 2 , 4 , 11 ] ; for example,
it was proven as a basic result that no planar graph is 3 3 -extendable. 
 
 
 A graph G G is 1 1 -embeddable on a closed surface F 2 F^{2} 
if it can be drawn on F 2 F^{2} 
so that every edge of G G crosses another edge at most once.
The drawn image of G G on F 2 F^{2} is a 1 1 - embedded graph on F 2 F^{2} .
(We implicitly consider good drawings , that is, (i) vertices are on different
points on the surface, (ii) no adjacent edges cross, (iii) no three edges cross
at the same point, and (iv) any non-adjacent edges do not touch tangently.)
The study of 1 1 - planar graphs, which are
 1 1 -embeddable graphs on the plane or the sphere,
was first introduced by Ringel [ 14 ] ,
and recently developed in various points of view (see e.g., [ 6 , 16 ] );
the drawn image is called a 1 1 - plane graph .
It is known that if G G is a 1 1 -embedded graph on F 2 F^{2} 
with at least three vertices,
then | E ⁡ ( G ) | ≤ 4 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 4 ​ χ ​ ( F 2 ) |E(G)|\leq 4|V(G)|-4\chi(F^{2}) holds,
where χ ⁡ ( F 2 ) \chi(F^{2}) stands for the Euler characteristic of F 2 F^{2} 
(see [ 7 ] for example).
In particular, a 1 1 -embedded graph G G on F 2 F^{2} that
satisfies the equality, that is | E ⁡ ( G ) | = 4 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 4 ​ χ ​ ( F 2 ) |E(G)|=4|V(G)|-4\chi(F^{2}) ,
is optimal .
An edge in a 1 1 -embedded graph G G is crossing 
if it crosses another edge,
and non-crossing otherwise.
Let G G be an optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on F 2 F^{2} , and let W W 
be a closed walk consisting of only non-crossing edges
that bounds a 2 2 -cell D D ; where a 2 2 -cell is homeomorphic to
an open disc.
If D D contains an odd number of vertices, then
we call D D an odd weighted region .
In particular, if W W is a cycle, then W W is a barrier cycle .
A barrier cycle of length k k is called a barrier k k -cycle . 
 
 
 The matching extendability of 1 1 -embedded graphs on F 2 F^{2} was first addressed
in [ 3 ] , and the authors proved that
every optimal 1 1 -plane graph (abbreviated as O1PG) of even order is 1 1 -extendable.
Further in the same paper, they discussed 2 2 -extendability of O1PG’s, and established the following theorem. 
 
 
 T HEOREM 1 (Fujisawa et al. [ 3 ] ) 
 
 An O1PG G G of even order is 2 2 -extendable
unless G G contains a barrier 4 4 -cycle. 
 
 
 
 Furthermore, they discussed extendable three edges in O1PG’s and obtained the following result. 
 
 
 T HEOREM 2 (Fujisawa et al. [ 3 ] ) 
 
 Let G G be a 5 5 -connected O1PG of even order,
and M M be a matching of G G with | M | = 3 |M|=3 .
Then M M is extendable unless
 G G contains a barrier 6 6 -cycle C C such that V ⁡ ( M ) = V ⁡ ( C ) V(M)=V(C) . 
 
 
 
 We extend the topic to graphs on non-spherical closed surfaces.
In this paper, we especially discuss matching extendability of
optimal 1 1 -embedded graphs on the projective plane;
we denote the projective plane by P 2 P^{2} briefly.
An optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on P 2 P^{2} is also
called an optimal 1-projective plane graph ,
and is abbreviated as O1PPG.
Note that every O1PPG has exactly 4 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | − 4 4|V(G)|-4 edges by
the equality above with χ ⁡ ( P 2 ) = 1 \chi(P^{2})=1 .
First, we discuss 1 1 -extendability of O1PPG’s, and
show the following theorem, using Hamiltonian paths
contained in those graphs. 
 
 
 T HEOREM 3 
 
 Every O1PPG of even order
is 1 1 -extendable. 
 
 
 
 Next, we discuss 2 2 -extendability, and prove the following theorem.
The statement looks similar to the spherical case,
but we need to establish some lemmas specific to the case of
the projective plane. 
 
 
 T HEOREM 4 
 
 An O1PPG G G of even order
is 2 2 -extendable if and only if
 G G contains a barrier 4 4 -cycle. 
 
 
 
 The following corollary easily follows from Theorem 4 . 
 
 
 C OROLLARY 5 
 
 Any 5 5 -connected O1PPG of even order is 2 2 -extendable. 
 
 
 
 Every O1PPG G G has a vertex with degree 6 6 ,
since the average degree of G G is less than 8 8 .
(And since the minimum degree of G G is at least 6 6 ,
and every optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on a closed surface
is Eulerian. We mention these facts in Section  2 .)
Therefore, no O1PPG is 3 3 -extendable; take three edges on the 6 6 -cycle
induced by neighbors of a vertex of degree 6 6 .
The following theorem characterizes three mutually independent edges that are not extendable in those graphs. 
 
 
 T HEOREM 6

Then M M is not extendable if and only if
 G G has either

 2 Preliminaries and basic results 

 
 A vertex set S S of a connected graph G G is a cut if
 G − S G-S has at least two connected components.
A cut S S of G G is minimal if any proper subset of S S 
is not a cut of G G .
For a cut S S of G G , if | S | = k |S|=k , then we call S S a k k -cut 
of G G .
We denote the number of connected components of
 G − S G-S for S ⊂ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subset V(G) by C ⁡ ( G − S ) C(G-S) .
In particular, the number of odd components (resp., even components),
i.e., connected components having odd (resp., even) number of vertices,
is denoted by C o ​ ( G − S ) C_{o}(G-S) (resp., C e ​ ( G − S ) C_{e}(G-S) ).
That is, we have C ⁡ ( G − S ) = C o ​ ( G − S ) + C e ​ ( G − S ) C(G-S)=C_{o}(G-S)+C_{e}(G-S) . 
 
 
 Let G G be a graph embedded on a closed surface F 2 F^{2} .
Then a connected component of F 2 − G F^{2}-G ,
which is as a topological space,
is a face of G G ,
and we denote the face set of G G by F ⁡ ( G ) F(G) ;
that is, “a face” in this paper is not necessarily
homeomorphic to an (open) 2 2 -cell.
A boundary closed walk W W of a face f f is a closed walk bounding f f 
in G G .
(Actually, under our definition, the boundary of a face
might be a union of closed walks.) A k k - gonal face or simply a k k - face means a face with
boundary closed walk of length exactly k k .
If every face of G G is homeomorphic to a 2 2 -cell, then
 G G is a 2 2 -cell embedding or 2 2 -cell embedded graph on F 2 F^{2} .
Furthermore, a region bounded by a closed walk might contains
some vertices and edges in its interior in our latter argument;
that is, a face is always a region, but the converse
does not hold in general. 
 
 
 A simple closed curve γ \gamma on a closed surface F 2 F^{2} is
 trivial if γ \gamma bounds a 2 2 -cell on F 2 F^{2} , and
 essential otherwise.
We apply these definition to cycles of graphs embedded on F 2 F^{2} ,
regarding them as simple closed curves.
A simple closed curve γ \gamma on a closed surface F 2 F^{2} 
is surface separating if F 2 − γ F^{2}-\gamma is disconnected as a topological space.
We also apply the definition to cycles of graphs on F 2 F^{2} .
Note that every trivial closed curve on a closed surface
is surface separating.
In addition, it is well-known that every surface separating simple closed curve
on the sphere or the projective plane is trivial.
The following proposition is known in
topological graph theory, and is commonly used. 
 
 
 P ROPOSITION 7 (Nakamoto [ 8 ] ) 
 
 Let G G be a graph embedded on a closed surface F 2 F^{2} so that each face is bounded
by a closed walk of even length. Then the length of two cycles in G G have the same parity
if they are homotopic to each other on F 2 F^{2} .
Furthermore, there is no surface separating odd cycle in G G . 
 
 
 
 The representativity r ⁡ ( G ) r(G) of
a graph G G embedded on a non-spherical closed surface F 2 F^{2} is
the minimum number of crossing points of G G and
 γ \gamma ,
where γ \gamma ranges over
all essential simple closed curves on F 2 F^{2} .
A graph G G embedded on F 2 F^{2} is k-representative if
 r ⁡ ( G ) ≥ k r(G)\geq k .
A graph G G embedded on a non-spherical closed surface F 2 F^{2} is polyhedral 
if G G is 3 3 -connected and 3 3 -representative.
In particular, a graph G G embedded on the sphere is polyhedral
if G G is just 3 3 -connected. 
 
 
 A quadrangulation (resp., triangulation ) is a simple 2 2 -cell embedded
graph on a closed surface
such that every face is a 4 4 -face (resp., 3 3 -face).
It was shown in [ 7 ] that
every simple optimal 1 1 -embedded graph G G on F 2 F^{2} is obtained from a polyhedral
quadrangulation H H by adding a pair of crossing edges in each face of H H .
We call the quadrangulation H H , which consists of all the non-crossing edges of G G ,
the quadrangular subgraph of G G ,
and denote it by Q ⁡ ( G ) ( = H ) Q(G)(=H) .
By the property above, deg G ⁡ ( v ) = 2 ​ deg H ⁡ ( v ) \deg_{G}(v)=2\deg_{H}(v) for any v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) ,
that is,
 G G is Eulerian.
For a vertex v v of an optimal 1 1 -embedded graph G G on F 2 F^{2} ,
the union of all the faces (with boundaries) of Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) incident to v v 
forms a disc D D containing the unique vertex v v .
We call the boundary cycle of D D the link of v v and
denote it by L G ​ ( v ) L_{G}(v) ; observe that the boundary corresponds to
a cycle since Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) is polyhedral. 
 
 
 Let F 2 F^{2} be a closed surface.
An arc in F 2 F^{2} is the image of a continuous map
 α : [ 0 , 1 ] → F 2 \alpha:[0,1]\rightarrow F^{2} ; we denote the image α ⁡ ( [ 0 , 1 ] ) \alpha([0,1]) by α \alpha for brevity,
if there is no misunderstanding.
The arc α \alpha joins its endpoints α ⁡ ( 0 ) \alpha(0) and α ⁡ ( 1 ) \alpha(1) .
Let G G be an optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on F 2 F^{2} and let H 1 H_{1} and H 2 H_{2} be
connected subgraphs of G G .
Then a subgraph K K of Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) separates H 1 H_{1} and H 2 H_{2} on F 2 F^{2} 
if V ⁡ ( K ) ∩ V ⁡ ( H i ) = ∅ V(K)\cap V(H_{i})=\emptyset for each i ∈ { 1 , 2 } i\in\{1,2\} , and any arc α \alpha on F 2 F^{2} that
joins x 1 ∈ V ⁡ ( H 1 ) x_{1}\in V(H_{1}) and x 2 ∈ V ⁡ ( H 2 ) x_{2}\in V(H_{2}) has an intersection with K K ; i.e.,
 α ∩ K ≠ ∅ \alpha\cap K\neq\emptyset .
Note that F 2 ∖ K F^{2}\setminus K is disconnected as a
topological space. 
 
 
 Let G G be an optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on F 2 F^{2} and
let 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{C}(G) be the set of all the crossing points of G G .
We obtain the associated graph G × G^{\times} , which is
embedded on F 2 F^{2} , from G G by regarding every crossing point as a vertex of degree 4 4 .
(That is, G × G^{\times} has V ⁡ ( G × ) = V ⁡ ( G ) ∪ 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) V(G^{\times})=V(G)\cup\mathcal{C}(G)

 3 Minimal cuts and subgraphs in Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) 

 
 In this section, we describe properties of induced subgraphs by minimal
cuts in O1PPG’s.
First of all, we show 4 4 -connectedness of O1PPG’s as follows. 
 
 
 T HEOREM 12 
 
 Every O1PPG G G is 4 4 -connected.
Furthermore, if G G has a 4 4 -cut S S ,
then Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] contains a separating trivial 4 4 -cycle of G G . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
In [ 10 ] ,
it was proven that
every quadrangulation G G on a closed surface with | V ⁡ ( G ) | ≥ 6 |V(G)|\geq 6 
can be extended to a 4 4 -connected triangulation by adding
a diagonal edge in every face of G G .
Furthermore,
it was shown in [ 15 ] that
every O1PPG has at least nine vertices.
Combining the results above, we obtain the former half of the statement of
the theorem. 
 
 
 Next, we discuss the latter half of the statement.
Let S S be a 4 4 -cut of G G , and first
assume that Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] is not a 2 2 -cell embedding;
note that Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] is bipartite.
Since Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] has at least two faces by Lemma  8 ,
 Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] contains a cycle C C .
Then | C | = 4 |C|=4 and C C does not have any chord; otherwise Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] would have a
trivial cycle of length 3 3 , a contradiction to that Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] is bipartite.
In this case, Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] is just a 4 4 -cycle, and has exactly two faces, one of which
is a 2 2 -cell and the other of which contains a cross cap.
Each face contains exactly one connected component by Lemma  8 ,
and we have our desired trivial 4 4 -cycle in Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] . 
 
 
 Secondly, we assume that Q ⁡ [ S ] Q[S] is a 2 2 -cell embedding.
By Euler’s formula 4 − | E ⁡ ( Q ⁡ ( S ) ) | + | F ⁡ ( Q ⁡ [ S ] ) | = 1 4-|E(Q(S))|+|F(Q[S])|=1 ,
 | E ⁡ ( Q ⁡ [ S ] ) | |E(Q[S])| is either 5 5 or 6 6 since | F ⁡ ( Q ⁡ [ S ] ) | ≥ 2 |F(Q[S])|\geq 2 by Lemma  8 .
In the case when | E ⁡ ( Q ⁡ [ S ] ) | = 6 |E(Q[S])|=6 , Q ⁡ [ S ] ≅ K 4 Q[S]\cong K_{4} ,
and it is known that
 K 4 K_{4} is uniquely embedded on P 2 P^{2} such that each
face is bounded by a closed walk of even length, which is actually
a cycle of length 4 4 .
On the other hand, if | E ⁡ ( Q ⁡ [ S ] ) | = 5 |E(Q[S])|=5 , then

 5 Remarks 

 
 In this paper, we have discussed matching extendability of
O1PPG’s.
Then, how about for optimal 1 1 -embedded graphs on the
torus (or the Klein bottle)?
We are aware that the situation changes significantly,
particularly in contrast to the discussion on the sphere and the projective plane.
At least, there exist optimal
 1 1 -embedded graphs on the torus that are not 1 1 -extendable.
(Consider an optimal 1 1 -embedded graph G G on the torus
such that Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) has a subgraph H H that is also a quadrangulation of the torus,
and that every face of H H is an odd weighted region of G G .
Then every non-crossing edge of H H is not extendable;
observe that | V ⁡ ( H ) | = C o ​ ( G − V ⁡ ( H ) ) |V(H)|=C_{o}(G-V(H)) .) 
 
 
 Moreover, every O1PG and every O1PPG is not 3 3 -extendable since
the graph has a vertex of degree exactly 6 6 .
However, the property does not hold for optimal 1 1 -embedded graphs
on the torus (or the Klein bottle); there exists infinitely many 8 8 -regular graphs whose quadrangular
subgraphs are 4 4 -regular.
At the end of the paper, we establish the following conjectures for those graphs: 
 
 
 C ONJECTURE 19 
 
 Let G G be an optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on the torus or the Klein bottle.
If Q ⁡ ( G ) Q(G) has no quadrangulation as a subgraph each of whose face corresponds
to an odd weighted region of G G , then G G is 1 1 -extendable. 
 
 
 
 C ONJECTURE 20 
 
 Every 8 8 -regular optimal 1 1 -embedded graph on the torus or the Klein bottle is
 3 3 -extendable. 
 
 
 
 Note that for 2 2 -extendability, the statement for optimal 1 1 -embedded graphs on the
torus and that for optimal 1 1 -embedded graphs on the Klein bottle might be substantially different;
since the Klein bottle admits a separating simple closed curve that is not trivial,
which is known as an equator of the surface.
