 Abstract. 
 
 We are interested in conjecturing the sign of the pizza quantity
 P ⁡ ( ℋ , 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , R ) ) P(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{B}(a,R)) for the irreducible Coxeter arrangements ℋ \mathcal{H} of
type A n A_{n} , where n ≡ 2 n\equiv 2 or 3 mod 4 3\bmod 4 , and
type D n D_{n} , where n n is odd.
Our approach is to express the pizza quantity in terms of
the pizza quantity of subarrangements known as 2 2 -structures,
and we obtain the first non-zero term in the multivariate Taylor expansion. 
 
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  
Coxeter arrangements,
 2 2 -structures,
Pizza Theorem,
reflection groups

20F55, 51F15; Secondary
51M20, 51M25.
 
 
 1. Introduction 

 
 For an hyperplane arrangement ℋ \mathcal{H} in an inner product space V V 
let 𝒯 \mathscr{T} denote the set of chambers of the arrangement  ℋ \mathcal{H} .
Let T 0 T_{0} be a base chamber
and let the sign ( − 1 ) T (-1)^{T} be − 1 -1 to the number of hyperplanes that separate
the chamber T T from the base chamber T 0 T_{0} .
The pizza quantity 
for a measurable set K K in the space V V 
is defined in  [ 5 ] 
to be the alternating sum 
 

 
 (1.1) 
 
 P ⁡ ( ℋ , K ) \displaystyle P(\mathcal{H},K) 
 = ∑ T ∈ 𝒯 ( − 1 ) T ⋅ Vol ⁡ ( K ∩ T ) . \displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{T}}(-1)^{T}\cdot\Vol(K\cap T). 
 
 
 The notion to have in mind is that two friends are sharing a pizza.
They cut the pizza with the hyperplanes in ℋ \mathcal{H} and then alternate
taking slices. The pizza quantity measures the difference in the amount
of pizza they each receive. 
 
 
 Recall that a Coxeter arrangement is an hyperplane arrangement which is preserved under
reflections in each of its hyperplanes, and the group generated is finite.
For balls and for Coxeter arrangements satisfying the parity condition,
the following result states that the two friends can share the pizza equally
as long as the ball contains the origin. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1 (Ehrenborg–Morel–Readdy  [ 5 ] ) . 
 
 Let ℋ \mathcal{H} be a Coxeter arrangement
on a finite-dimensional
inner product space V V such that | ℋ | ≥ dim ( V ) |\mathcal{H}|\geq\dim(V) .
Assume that the ball 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , R ) \mathbb{B}(a,R) contains the origin,
that is, R ≥ ‖ a ‖ R\geq\|a\| . 
 
 (i) 
 
 If the number of hyperplanes is strictly greater than the dimension of  V V 
and has the same parity as that dimension
then the pizza quantity P ⁡ ( ℋ , 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , R ) ) P(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{B}(a,R)) vanishes. 
 
 (ii) 
 
 If the number of hyperplanes is equal to the dimension of V V 
then
 P ⁡ ( ℋ , 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , R ) ) P(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{B}(a,R)) is independent of the radius  R R . 
 
 
 
 
 
 This result was known in a few cases:
For the type B 2 B_{2} it was stated as a problem by Upton  [ 11 ] .
For the dihedral arrangement I 2 ​ ( 2 ​ k ) I_{2}(2k) , where k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 ,
this result was first proved by Goldberg  [ 8 ] .
For a product arrangement of type A 1 × I 2 ​ ( 2 ​ k ) A_{1}\times I_{2}(2k) , again k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 ,
it was proved by Frederickson  [ 7 ] .
Furthermore, Brailov had proved the result independently
for an arrangement of type B n B_{n} in  [ 2 ] . 
 
 
 Note that Theorem  1.1 does not
yield a satisfactory answer for the arrangements
where the parities of the number of hyperplanes and the dimension differ.
The irreducible types of Coxeter arrangements that do not satisfy the parity condition are
 I 2 ​ ( 2 ​ k + 1 ) I_{2}(2k+1) where k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 ,
 A n A_{n} where n ≡ 2 n\equiv 2 or 3 mod 4 3\bmod 4 , and
 D n D_{n} where n n is odd.
For the two-dimensional dihedral arrangements
Mabry and Deiermann  [ 10 ] 
obtained the following result. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.2 (Mabry–Deiermann  [ 10 ] ) . 
 
 Let ℋ \mathcal{H} be an arrangement of type I 2 ​ ( m ) I_{2}(m) where m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 and m m is odd.
Let a a be a point in the interior of a chamber T T of the arrangement
such that ‖ a ‖ ≤ R \|a\|\leq R .
Then the sign of the pizza quantity is given by 
 

 
 
 ( − 1 ) ( m − 3 ) / 2 ⋅ ( − 1 ) T ⋅ P ⁡ ( ℋ , 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , R ) ) \displaystyle(-1)^{(m-3)/2}\cdot(-1)^{T}\cdot P(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{B}(a,R)) 
 > 0 . \displaystyle>0. 
 
 
 
 
 
 In terms of pizza, assume that you and your friend are sharing a two-dimensional pizza
and you would like to maximize the amount of pizza you receive. If you cut the pizza with
 m ≡ 3 mod 4 m\equiv 3\bmod 4 lines in a dihedral arrangement
you should make sure you take the slice containing the center of the pizza.
On the other hand, if
 m ≡ 1 mod 4 m\equiv 1\bmod 4 you should avoid the slice containing the center. 
 
 
 Hence it is natural to conjecture the following: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.3 (Ehrenborg–Morel–Readdy  [ 5 ] ) . 
 
 Let ℋ \mathcal{H} be a Coxeter arrangement and let a ∈ V a\in V such that ‖ a ‖ ≤ R \|a\|\leq R .
Assume that the number of hyperplanes of ℋ \mathcal{H} is greater than the dimension of  V V 
and that these two quantities differ in their parities.
Then the pizza quantity  P ⁡ ( ℋ , 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , R ) ) P(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{B}(a,R)) 
is zero if and only if the center  a a lies
on one of the hyperplanes of  ℋ \mathcal{H} . 
 
 
 
 This conjecture is basically
Conjecture 9.5 in  [ 5 ] .
Our purpose in this paper is to generate data such that
we can predict the sign of the pizza quantity given the sign of the
open chamber that contains the center a a , in the case when
the Coxeter arrangement has type A n A_{n} or D n D_{n} .
Our approach for obtaining data follows the idea
of  [ 6 ] of expressing the pizza quantity
in terms of pizza quantities of smaller subarrangements,
known as 2 2 -structures.
These subarrangements were introduced by Herb
in the study of the characters of discrete series representations  [ 9 ] .
In the cases we are interested in, the 2 2 -structures all have
the type A 1 k A_{1}^{k} . For the type A 1 k A_{1}^{k} we obtain the multivariate Taylor series
for the pizza quantity, yielding the Taylor series for the desired
pizza quantity of types  A n A_{n} and  D n D_{n} .
We were able to do the (computer) calculations up to and including
dimension  7 7 , before the polynomials became too unwieldy.
Lastly, we return to type D n D_{n} , where we make a conjecture
that would imply the sign for arrangements of type D n D_{n} 
where n n  is odd. 
 
 
 
 2. Coxeter arrangements, pseudo-root systems and 2 2 -structures 

 
 Given two hyperplane arrangements ℋ 1 \mathcal{H}_{1} and ℋ 2 \mathcal{H}_{2} in the two spaces V 1 V_{1} 
and V 2 V_{2} , respectively.
The product arrangement ℋ 1 × ℋ 2 \mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2} in V 1 × V 2 V_{1}\times V_{2} is defined to be 
 

 
 
 ℋ 1 × ℋ 2 \displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2} 
 = { H × V 2 : H ∈ ℋ 1 } ∪ { V 1 × H : H ∈ ℋ 2 } . \displaystyle=\{H\times V_{2}:H\in\mathcal{H}_{1}\}\cup\{V_{1}\times H:H\in\mathcal{H}_{2}\}. 
 
 
 A Coxeter arrangement ℋ \mathcal{H} is a finite set of hyperplanes
such that the arrangement is invariant under the orthogonal reflections
in any of its hyperplanes, and the group (known as the Coxeter group)
generated by these reflections is finite.
Note that the class of Coxeter arrangements is closed under
Cartesian products. Furthermore, call an arrangement
 irreducible if it cannot be written as a Cartesian product.
The irreducible Coxeter arrangements have been classified;
see for instance  [ 1 ] .
Their types are
 A n A_{n} for n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 ;
 B n B_{n} for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 (also known as type B / C B/C );
 D n D_{n} for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 (note that D 3 D_{3} is isomorphic to A 3 A_{3} );
 E 6 E_{6} , E 7 E_{7} and E 8 E_{8} ;
 F 4 F_{4} ;
 H 3 H_{3} and H 4 H_{4} ;
and
the dihedral arrangement  I 2 ​ ( m ) I_{2}(m) where m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 
(observe that I 2 ​ ( 2 ) ≅ A 1 2 I_{2}(2)\cong A_{1}^{2} , I 2 ​ ( 3 ) ≅ A 2 I_{2}(3)\cong A_{2} and I 2 ​ ( 4 ) ≅ B 2 I_{2}(4)\cong B_{2} ). 
 
 
 Let V V be a finite-dimensional real vector space with an inner product
 ( ⋅ , ⋅ ) (\cdot,\cdot) .
For every non-zero vector α ∈ V \alpha\in V ,
let H α = { x ∈ V : ( α , x ) = 0 } H_{\alpha}=\{x\in V:(\alpha,x)=0\} 
denote the hyperplane orthogonal to α \alpha ,
and let s α s_{\alpha} be the orthogonal reflection in
the hyperplane H α H_{\alpha} . 
 
 
 Definition 2.1 . 
 
 A subset Φ \Phi of V V is a
 pseudo-root system if: 
 
 (a) 
 
 Φ \Phi is a finite set of unit vectors; 
 
 (b) 
 
 for all α , β ∈ Φ \alpha,\beta\in\Phi , we have s β ​ ( α ) ∈ Φ s_{\beta}(\alpha)\in\Phi . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Note that a consequence of condition (b) is that α ∈ Φ \alpha\in\Phi implies − α ∈ Φ -\alpha\in\Phi 
by setting α = β \alpha=\beta .
Elements of Φ \Phi are called pseudo-roots .
Let the Coxeter group W W be the group generated by all the reflections
 s α s_{\alpha} for α \alpha in the pseudo-root system Φ \Phi .
For a group element  w w in W W the sign of the element w w 
is the sign of the chamber w ⁡ ( T 0 ) w(T_{0}) , that is, ( − 1 ) w = ( − 1 ) w ⁡ ( T 0 ) (-1)^{w}=(-1)^{w(T_{0})} .
Note that this definition is same as defining the sign of w w 
as ( − 1 ) ℓ ⁡ ( w ) (-1)^{\ell(w)} where ℓ \ell is the length function in the Coxeter system. 
 
 
 The elements of a pseudo-root system Φ \Phi are naturally divided
into positive and negative roots,
that is, the following disjoint union holds: Φ = Φ + ⊔ Φ − \Phi=\Phi^{+}\sqcup\Phi^{-} .
We use the characterization given in  [ 4 , Lemma B.1.9] .
Define the lexicographic order on ℝ r \mathbb{R}^{r} as follows:
 ( x 1 , … , x r ) < lex ( y 1 , … , y r ) (x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})<_{\text{lex}}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}) 
if there is an index i i such that
 x 1 = y 1 , … , x i − 1 = y i − 1 x_{1}=y_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}=y_{i-1} and x i < y i x_{i}<y_{i} .
Then Lemma B.1.9 is the following statement. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.2 . 
 
 Let Φ \Phi be a pseudo-root system in  V V 
and assume that v 1 v_{1} through  v r v_{r} are linearly independent elements of  V V 
such that no pseudo-root of  Φ \Phi is orthogonal to every  v i v_{i} .
Then the set  Φ + \Phi^{+} defined by 
 

 
 
 Φ + \displaystyle\Phi^{+} 
 = { α ∈ Φ : ( ( α , v 1 ) , … , ( α , v r ) ) > lex ( 0 , … , 0 ) } \displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\Phi:((\alpha,v_{1}),\ldots,(\alpha,v_{r}))>_{\text{lex}}(0,\ldots,0)\}

 Given a pseudo-root system Φ \Phi .
We obtain a Coxeter arrangement by considering
the collection ℋ Φ = { H α : α ∈ Φ + } \mathcal{H}_{\Phi}=\{H_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\} .
Moreover, the base chamber T 0 T_{0} is
on the positive side on each hyperplane, that is,
 T 0 = ⋂ α ∈ Φ + { x ∈ V : ( α , x ) ≥ 0 } T_{0}=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\{x\in V:(\alpha,x)\geq 0\} .
Similarly, given a Coxeter arrangement
we obtain a pseudo-root system by considering the unit
vectors orthogonal to the hyperplanes.
The positive pseudo-roots are on the same side as the base chamber,
that is,
 Φ + = { α ∈ Φ : ∀ x ∈ T 0 ​ ( α , x ) ≥ 0 } \Phi^{+}=\{\alpha\in\Phi:\forall x\in T_{0}\>\>(\alpha,x)\geq 0\} . 
 
 
 For a root system Φ \Phi with positive roots Φ + \Phi^{+} 
we define the Jacobian to be the product 
 

 
 
 J ⁡ ( a ) \displaystyle J(a) 
 = ∏ α ∈ Φ + ( α , a ) . \displaystyle=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(\alpha,a). 
 
 
 Note that the Jacobian is the polynomial which is zero on
all the hyperplanes in the arrangement associated with Φ \Phi .
Moreover, any skew symmetric polynomial on V V factors
as the Jacobian times a polynomial invariant under W W . 
 
 
 The two types of
irreducible Coxeter arrangements, equivalently pseudo-root systems,
we consider in this paper are the types A n A_{n} and D n D_{n} .
For each type we present the positive pseudo-roots and the Jacobian. 
 
 ( A n ) (A_{n}) 
 
 Let V V be the n n -dimensional space

We use Definition B.2.1 of  [ 4 ] ,

 3. Multivariate Taylor expansion of the pizza quantity 

 
 We begin to consider the pizza quantity of an arrangement
of type A 1 k A_{1}^{k} in an n n -dimensional space.
Let β m \beta_{m} be the volume of the m m -dimensional unit ball,
that is, 
 

 
 
 β m \displaystyle\beta_{m} 
 = π m / 2 Γ ⁡ ( m / 2 + 1 ) . \displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{m/2}}{\Gamma(m/2+1)}. 
 
 
 
 
 Lemma 3.1 . 
 
 Let V V be an n n -dimensional space and
let ℋ \mathcal{H} be the Coxeter arrangement of type A 1 k A_{1}^{k} .
That is, ℋ = { H f } f ∈ E \mathcal{H}=\{H_{f}\}_{f\in E} 
where E = { f 1 , … , f k } E=\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\} is a set of k k orthogonal unit vectors.
The base chamber T 0 T_{0} is the cone generated by the set E E and all vectors
orthogonal to the set  E E .
Let a a be a point in V V such that 0 ∈ 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , 1 ) 0\in\mathbb{B}(a,1) .
Then the pizza quantity is given by the k k -dimensional integral 
 

 
 
 P ⁡ ( ℋ , 𝔹 ⁡ ( a , 1 ) ) \displaystyle P(\mathcal{H},\mathbb{B}(a,1)) 
 = 2 k ⋅ β n − k ⋅ ∫ 0 ( f 1 , a ) ⋯ ∫ 0 ( f k , a ) ( 1 − t 1 2 − ⋯ − t k 2 ) ( n − k ) / 2 d t 1 ⋯ d t k . \displaystyle=2^{k}\cdot\beta_{n-k}\cdot\int_{0}^{(f_{1},a)}\cdots\int_{0}^{(f_{k},a)}\left(1-t_{1}^{2}-\cdots-t_{k}^{2}\right)^{(n-k)/2}\>dt_{1}\cdots dt_{k}. 
 
 
 If n n and k k have the same parity,
then
the pizza quantity is a polynomial in a a of degree at most  n n . 
 
 
 
 Proof.

 6. Concluding remarks 

 
 The other pizza theorem proved in  [ 5 ] 
states that for a Coxeter arrangement ℋ \mathcal{H} , different from  A 1 n A_{1}^{n} , such that
the negative of the identity map belongs to the associated Coxeter group W W ,
and for a measurable set K K , stable under the action of W W , such that
the convex hull of { w ⁡ ( a ) : w ∈ W } \{w(a):w\in W\} is contained in K K , then
the pizza quantity vanishes, that is,
 P ⁡ ( ℋ , K + a ) = 0 P(\mathcal{H},K+a)=0 ;
see  [ 5 , Theorem 1.2] .
For the case when the Coxeter group W W does not contain the negative map,
can we obtain a multivariate Taylor series for the pizza quantity?
In the irreducible types, these cases would be A n A_{n} for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ,
 D n D_{n} for n n odd, the sporadic type  E 6 E_{6} 
and the dihedral type I 2 ​ ( m ) I_{2}(m) where m m is odd. 
 
 
 Note that Proposition  3.2 
uses analysis in its proof.
There are two pizza theorems in  [ 5 , Theorems 1.1 and 1.2] .
The first result is Theorem  1.1 
and the second one is discussed in the previous paragraph.
The paper  [ 6 ] provides a dissection proof
of the second theorem.
Hence it would be interesting if
the usage of analysis could be removed from
the proof of Proposition  3.2 
such that we have a completely non-analytic proof of the result.
