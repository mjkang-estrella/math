 Abstract. 
 
 We prove that the groups Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) satisfy the Boone–Higman conjecture for all n n , meaning each Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) embeds in a finitely presented simple group. In fact, we prove that each Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) satisfies the “permutational” Boone–Higman conjecture, which means the simple group in question can be taken to be a twisted Brin–Thompson group. A far-reaching consequence of our approach is that finitely presented twisted Brin–Thompson groups are universal among finitely presented simple groups that are highly transitive. This is evidence toward the Boone–Higman conjecture being equivalent to its permutational version. Proving the conjecture for Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) also confirms the conjecture for all groups (virtually) embedding into some Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) , such as mapping class groups of non-closed surfaces, braid groups, loop braid groups, ribbon braid groups and certain Artin groups. This answers several questions of the first and fourth authors with Bleak and Matucci. Yet another consequence of our approach is that satisfying the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture is closed under free products and regular wreath products, which answers a question of the fourth author, confirms the conjecture for free solvable groups, and reduces the conjecture for finitely generated 3 3 -manifold groups to the case of non-Seifert fibered closed graph manifolds. 
 
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  Thompson group, simple group, finitely presented, word problem, Boone–Higman conjecture, automorphism group, braid group, mapping class group

 Introduction 

 
 The Boone–Higman conjecture , posed by Boone and Higman over 50 years ago [ BH74 ] , predicts the following: 
 
 
 Boone–Higman conjecture . 
 
 Every finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in a finitely presented simple group. 
 
 
 
 Here a group has solvable word problem if there is an algorithm to tell whether or not a given word in the generators represents the identity. The converse of the conjecture is easily seen to be true [ Kuz58 ] . The Boone–Higman–Thompson theorem [ Tho80 ] states that every finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in a finitely generated simple subgroup of a finitely presented group, so the conjecture is that this two-step embedding process can be reduced to an embedding in a single group. Some examples of groups for which the conjecture is known to hold include GL n ⁡ ( ℤ ) \GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) [ Sco84 ] , hyperbolic groups [ BBMZ26 ] , Baumslag–Solitar groups and (finitely generated free)-by-cyclic groups [ BLIW ] , contracting or finitely presented self-similar groups [ BBMZ26 , Zar25a ] , and all subgroups thereof. See [ BBMZ ] for more on the history and progress around this conjecture. 
 
 
 Our first main result adds to this list a very prominent and important family of groups in geometric group theory, namely the group Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) of automorphisms of the free group F n F_{n} , for each n n . 
 
 
 Theorem A . 
 
 For each n n , the group Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) embeds in a finitely presented simple group, and hence satisfies the Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 This quickly leads to the following corollary, which in particular settles [ BBMZ , Problem 5.3(1)] , and partially settles [ BBMZ , Problem 5.3(2, 3)] . 
 
 
 Corollary B . 
 
 The following groups satisfy the Boone–Higman conjecture: 
 
 • 
 
 The (extended) mapping class group of a surface Σ \Sigma of finite type, where Σ \Sigma satisfies any of the following: has non-empty boundary, has at least one puncture, is orientable of genus at most two, is non-orientable of demigenus at most three. 
 
 • 
 
 The braid groups ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} for all n n . 
 
 • 
 
 Artin groups of types B n = C n B_{n}=C_{n} , D n D_{n} , I 2 ​ ( m ) I_{2}(m) , A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} , C ~ n \widetilde{C}_{n} , for all n n (braid groups are Artin groups of type A n A_{n} ), and H 3 H_{3} . 
 
 • 
 
 Loop braid groups ℒ ​ ℬ n \mathcal{LB}_{n} , extended loop braid groups ℒ ​ ℬ n e ​ x ​ t \mathcal{LB}_{n}^{ext} , ribbon braid groups ℛ ​ ℬ n \mathcal{RB}_{n} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 For spherical and Euclidean Artin groups, the Boone–Higman conjecture remains open for the exceptional type spherical Artin groups other than type H 3 H_{3} , and the Euclidean Artin groups other than type A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} and C ~ n \widetilde{C}_{n} ; in rank 3 3 it is also known for type G ~ 2 \widetilde{G}_{2} (and A ~ 2 \widetilde{A}_{2} and C ~ 2 \widetilde{C}_{2} , which we recover) [ BLIW ] . Note that for general Artin groups it is a famous open problem whether they even have solvable word problem [ Cha , Problem 10] , but for all spherical and Euclidean types they do [ Del72 , MS17 ] . Also note that all Coxeter groups satisfy the Boone–Higman conjecture [ BBM20 ] . 
 
 
 For mapping class groups, the conjecture remains open for closed surfaces of genus at least three. Note that the Boone–Higman conjecture is known for many finitely generated linear groups [ Sco84 , Zar25a ] , but it is an open problem whether mapping class groups of closed surfaces of genus at least three are linear. The case of genus two is special, thanks to an exceptional relationship with braid groups, which is behind both the proof of linearity in [ BB01 ] and our proof of the Boone–Higman conjecture for these groups. 
 
 
 As a further application of Theorem  A , we also recover the conjecture for some important classes of groups for which it was already known, such as virtually compact special groups and finitely generated virtually free-by-cyclic groups [ BBMZ , Problem 5.3(8)] ; see Remark  5.1 . Our approach to finitely generated free-by-cyclic groups also easily adapts to more general extensions: see Proposition  5.2 . In particular it covers finitely generated surface-by-cyclic groups, which reduces the (still open) conjecture for finitely generated 3 3 -manifold groups to the non-Seifert fibered closed graph manifold case; see Corollary  5.4 . 
 
 
 In fact we prove a more precise version of Theorem  A that is stronger in two ways. Before stating the strong version we need some background. First, we work with the much larger group Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) of G G -automorphisms of the free product G ∗ F n G*F_{n} , for G G a group with certain properties. Here a 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -automorphism of G ∗ F n G*F_{n} is one that restricts to the identity on G G , so clearly Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) embeds into Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) for any G G . Second, the finitely presented simple groups we obtain in Theorem  A always come from the family of twisted Brin–Thompson groups, thus proving that the groups in question satisfy the a priori stronger “permutational” form of the Boone–Higman conjecture, which will be explained shortly. Twisted Brin–Thompson groups are defined as follows. Given a group Γ \Gamma acting faithfully on a set S S , we can form the permutational wreath product V ≀ S Γ V\wr_{S}\Gamma , where V V is the classical Thompson group. This admits a natural action on ( { 0 , 1 } ℕ ) S (\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}})^{S} , and the twisted Brin–Thompson group S ​ V Γ SV_{\Gamma} is the corresponding topological full group. These groups were introduced by the first and fourth authors in [ BZ22 ] , following the “untwisted” case due to Brin [ Bri04 ] ; also see [ Zar25b , BBMZ ] for more details. 
 
 
 Theorem A (precise version) . 
 
 For each n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , the group Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) (and hence all of its subgroups) embeds in a finitely presented simple group, namely the twisted Brin–Thompson group S ​ V Γ SV_{\Gamma} where Γ = Aut V ⁡ ( V ∗ F n ) \Gamma=\Aut_{V}(V*F_{n}) and S = Hom V ⁡ ( V ∗ F n , V ) S=\Hom_{V}(V*F_{n},V) , and hence satisfies the Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 The choice of Thompson’s group V V for the group G G is just a convenient one for a concise statement, however, as we will see below, any finitely presented simple MIF group will do. Here Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) denotes the set of G G -homomorphisms from G ∗ F n G*F_{n} to G G , that is, homomorphisms restricting to the identity on G G . Equivalently, it is the set of retractions of G ∗ F n G*F_{n} onto the free factor G G . The action of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) is by precomposition. Note that elements of Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) are in one-to-one correspondence with ordered n n -tuples of elements of  G G , so the set S S in Theorem  A can alternatively be viewed as  V n V^{n} , where again V V is the classical Thompson group. 
 
 
 As we mentioned, this shows that our groups satisfy not only the Boone–Higman conjecture but even the “permutational” Boone–Higman conjecture. Let us recall what this means. Given a group Γ \Gamma acting faithfully on a set S S , we say that the action is of type (A) if: 
 
 (i) 
 
 The group Γ \Gamma is finitely presented. 
 
 (ii) 
 
 The stabilizer Stab Γ ⁡ ( s ) \Stab_{\Gamma}(s) is finitely generated for all s ∈ S s\in S . 
 
 (iii) 
 
 The action has finitely many orbits of pairs, i.e., the diagonal action of Γ \Gamma on S × S S\times S has finitely many orbits. 
 
 
 Twisted Brin–Thompson groups are always simple [ BZ22 ] , and it turns out that S ​ V Γ SV_{\Gamma} is finitely presented if and only if the action of Γ \Gamma on S S is of type (A) [ Zar ] ; this is analogous to the fact that V ≀ S Γ V\wr_{S}\Gamma is finitely presented if and only if the action is of type (A) [ dC06 ] . Now the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture [ Zar ] predicts: 
 
 
 Permutational Boone–Higman conjecture . 
 
 Every finitely generated group with solvable word problem embeds in a group admitting an action of type (A), and hence in a finitely presented (simple) twisted Brin–Thompson group. 
 
 
 
 At this point we should clarify some terminology. When we say that a finitely generated group “satisfies the Boone–Higman conjecture” we are implicitly saying it has solvable word problem, and explicitly saying it embeds in a finitely presented simple group. Similarly, when we say it “satisfies the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture” we are implicitly saying it has solvable word problem, and explicitly saying it embeds in a group admitting an action of type (A). For brevity, we will often abbreviate “permutational Boone–Higman” by “PBH”, and “Boone–Higman” by “BH”, and write things like, “satisfies PBH,” for, “satisfies the PBH conjecture.” Writing “(P)” in parentheses emphasizes that even satisfying BH is a new result. By a Boone–Higman embedding , we mean an embedding of a group as a subgroup of a finitely presented simple group. 
 
 
 In addition to proving (P)BH for Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) and all its subgroups, our approach has some interesting consequences concerning the relationship between BH and PBH. A fundamental question in this context is whether BH and PBH are equivalent [ BBMZ26 , Question 1.6] , which amounts to determining whether every finitely presented simple group satisfies PBH, and our work here is a step in the direction of a positive answer. Namely, we establish that at least this holds provided the finitely presented simple group is highly transitive or, more generally, mixed identity-free. These conditions are defined as follows: 
 
 • 
 
 A group G G is highly transitive if it admits a faithful action on a set that is k k -transitive for every k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . For example, Thompson’s group V V is highly transitive because of its action on the orbit of 0 ¯ \overline{0} in { 0 , 1 } ℕ \{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}} . Similarly, any twisted Brin–Thompson group is highly transitive [ BBMZ26 , Proposition 5.4] . 
 
 • 
 
 If G G is a group, a non-trivial word w ⁡ ( x 1 , … , x n ) ∈ G ∗ F n w(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in G*F_{n} is called a mixed identity in  G G if w ⁡ ( g 1 , … , g n ) = 1 w(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=1 for all g 1 , … , g n ∈ G g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G . That is, w ∈ G ∗ F n w\in G*F_{n} is a mixed identity if it belongs to the kernel of every element of Hom G ​ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \mathrm{Hom}_{G}(G*F_{n},G) , which explains the relevance to our construction. A group G G is mixed identity-free (MIF) if it has no mixed identities.
MIF groups are always lawless, but the converse does not hold; for example, Thompson’s group T T is lawless but not MIF [ LBMB22b , Proposition 4.7] . 
 
 
 See [ Ana86 ] for background on mixed identities, and [ HO16 , Theorem 5.9] or [ LBMB22b , Proposition A.1] for a proof that every finitely generated, highly transitive simple group is MIF. 
 
 
 Our main result in this context is the following. 
 
 
 Theorem C . 
 
 For a finitely generated group G G (with solvable word problem) the following are equivalent: 
 
 (i) 
 
 G G satisfies the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture, i.e., it embeds in a group admitting an action of type (A). 
 
 (ii) 
 
 G G embeds in a finitely presented (simple) twisted Brin–Thompson group. 
 
 (iii) 
 
 G G embeds in a finitely presented simple group that is highly transitive. 
 
 (iv) 
 
 G G embeds in a finitely presented simple group that is MIF. 
 
 
 In particular, every finitely presented simple group that is highly transitive, or more generally MIF, satisfies the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture, as does every subgroup thereof. 
 
 
 
 The new part of Theorem  C is the implication (iv) ⇒ \Rightarrow (i). In fact, twisted Brin–Thompson groups are themselves highly transitive and thus MIF, so Theorem  C has the following immediate consequence. 
 
 
 Corollary D . 
 
 Finitely presented (simple) twisted Brin–Thompson groups are universal among finitely presented simple highly transitive groups, and more generally among finitely presented simple MIF groups.∎ 
 
 
 
 What we mean by “universal” is that within the class of finitely presented simple highly transitive groups, every member of this class embeds in a member of the subclass of finitely presented (simple) twisted Brin–Thompson groups, and similarly for MIF. This emphasizes the outsized importance of twisted Brin–Thompson groups in the study of finitely presented simple groups. See Remark  3.4 for a discussion of which known finitely presented simple groups are highly transitive and MIF, including the interesting case of Burger–Mozes groups. 
 
 
 The bulk of the work in proving Theorems  A and  C amounts to proving the (P)BH conjecture for certain groups of the form Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) , detailed in the following: 
 
 
 Theorem E . 
 
 For any finitely presented simple MIF group G G , the group Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) ( n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ) admits an action of type (A), and hence satisfies the (permutational) Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 Theorem  E is the key to proving that (iv) implies (i) in Theorem  C , and also to proving Theorem  A . It also has other important consequences, relating to permanence properties of the PBH conjecture. Indeed, the main advantage of knowing PBH for a group, instead of just BH, is that PBH is stable under several group-theoretic constructions. For instance, PBH is stable under commensurability and direct products [ Zar ] . Now Theorem  E adds two more such properties to the list, namely free products and regular wreath products, answering [ Zar , Question 5.7] . 
 
 
 Corollary F . 
 
 If A A and B B satisfy the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture, then the free product A ∗ B A*B also satisfies the (permutational) Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 For example the free product V ∗ V V*V of Thompson’s group V V with itself satisfies (P)BH, which was not known before. Much more generally, any free product of twisted Brin–Thompson groups satisfies the conjecture. This can also be seen as a step towards establishing the conjecture for finitely generated 3 3 -manifold groups: indeed, thanks to powerful structural results on such groups [ AFW15 ] , we can reduce the problem to non-Seifert fibered closed graph manifolds (see Corollary  5.4 ). 
 
 
 Corollary G . 
 
 If A A and B B satisfy the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture, then the regular wreath product A ≀ B A\wr B also satisfies the (permutational) Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 This applies to the following important class. 
 
 
 Corollary H . 
 
 Finitely generated free solvable groups satisfy the (permutational) Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 Note that not all finitely generated solvable groups have solvable word problem [ Hc81 ] , and even for those that do, the Boone–Higman conjecture remains open in general, for example for finitely generated metabelian groups [ BBMZ , Problem 5.3(7)] . A further stability property that covers some extensions is recorded in Proposition  5.2 . 
 
 
 The key to all of this is Theorem  E . To prove this, we consider the action of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) , for G G a finitely presented simple MIF group, and prove it is of type (A). We should mention that in the course of the proof that the action is of type (A), the only part that actually requires G G to be MIF is the proof of faithfulness (indeed, this construction is used in [ FFWZ ] to show that every finitely presented simple group embeds into a finitely presented “relatively simple” group from the Thompson world). If the MIF requirement could be dropped, for example by proving that the quotient of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) by the kernel of the action on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) is finitely presented, or by proving that every finitely presented simple group embeds in one that is MIF, then we would conclude that finitely presented (simple) twisted Brin–Thompson groups are universal among all finitely presented simple groups, and that BH is equivalent to PBH. We do not know whether every finitely presented simple group embeds in a finitely presented simple MIF group; there do exist finitely presented simple groups that are not MIF, for example among groups acting on the circle, see [ LBMB22b , Proposition 4.7] , such as Thompson’s group T T and certain variants, including torsion-free examples [ HL25 ] . Note however that T T embeds in Thompson’s group V V , which is MIF, so it is unclear what to expect in general. 
 
 
 This paper is organized as follows. In Section  1 we set up the group Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) and prove some basic properties of it. In Section  2 we look at the action of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) and prove that if G G is nice then the action is nice. In Section  3 we prove our main results. Section  4 is devoted to permanence properties, and in Section  5 we prove Corollary  B .

 1. Groups of G G -automorphisms 

 
 We introduce some notions from algebraic geometry on groups, as developed in [ BMR00 ] ; these will be at the heart of our constructions.
Let G G be a group. A 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -group is a group containing a designated copy of G G , which we usually identify with G G . A 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -homomorphism from a G G -group H H to another G G -group H ′ H^{\prime} is a group homomorphism ϕ : H → H ′ \phi\colon H\to H^{\prime} such that the restriction ϕ | G \phi|_{G} is the identity; if ϕ \phi is bijective then it is a 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -isomorphism . A 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -automorphism is a G G -isomorphism from a G G -group to itself; the G G -automorphisms of a G G -group H H form the group of G \boldsymbol{G} -automorphisms of H H , denoted Aut G ⁡ ( H ) \Aut_{G}(H) . The free G \boldsymbol{G} -group of rank n n is the free product G ∗ F n G*F_{n} , where F n F_{n} is the free group of rank  n n . Write Hom G ⁡ ( H , H ′ ) \Hom_{G}(H,H^{\prime}) for the set of all G G -homomorphisms from a G G -group H H to another G G -group  H ′ H^{\prime} . A 𝑮 \boldsymbol{G} -presentation of a G G -group H H is a surjective G G -homomorphism from G ∗ F n G*F_{n} to  H H . In particular, Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) is the set of all G G -presentations of G G on n n generators. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.1 . 
 
 If G G is finitely presented and has trivial center, then Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) is finitely presented. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 If G = { 1 } G=\{1\} then Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) = Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n})=\Aut(F_{n}) is finitely presented. Now suppose G ≠ { 1 } G\neq\{1\} . Since G G has trivial center, Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) contains no inner automorphisms of G ∗ F n G*F_{n} except the trivial one, and so the subgroup Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⋅ Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Inn(G*F_{n})\cdot\Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) of Aut ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut(G*F_{n}) splits as a semidirect product Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⋊ Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Inn(G*F_{n})\rtimes\Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) . Because Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Inn(G*F_{n}) is finitely generated, in order to prove that Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) is finitely presented, it now suffices to prove that Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⋊ Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Inn(G*F_{n})\rtimes\Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) is finitely presented. This is the group of all automorphisms of G ∗ F n G*F_{n} that agree with an inner automorphism on G G , and this group is shown to be finitely presented in [ Car11 , Theorem 4.1] (where it is denoted Aut i ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{i}(G*F_{n}) ), again using the fact that G G has trivial center.
∎ 
 
 
 
 It will be convenient later to have an explicit generating set X X for Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) pinned down. At this point we assume G G is freely indecomposable with trivial center (for example our main case of interest: G G is non-abelian simple). The specific generating set we will use is far from optimal, but the way we lay it out here will be useful. First let Y Y be the set of all elements of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) consisting of the following automorphisms (where in all cases, of course, G G is fixed): 
 
 (i) 
 
 Those automorphisms induced by permutations of the basis { x 1 , … , x n } \{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\} . 
 
 (ii) 
 
 The automorphism fixing x 2 , … , x n x_{2},\dots,x_{n} and sending x 1 x_{1} to x 1 − 1 x_{1}^{-1} . 
 
 (iii) 
 
 The automorphisms fixing x 2 , … , x n x_{2},\dots,x_{n} and sending x 1 x_{1} to g − 1 ​ x 1 ​ g g^{-1}x_{1}g for some g ∈ G g\in G . 
 
 (iv) 
 
 The automorphisms fixing x 2 , … , x n x_{2},\dots,x_{n} and sending x 1 x_{1} to x 1 ​ x 2 x_{1}x_{2} or x 1 ​ x 2 − 1 x_{1}x_{2}^{-1} . 
 
 
 Write X X for the subset of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) that consists of Y Y along with: 
 
 (v) 
 
 The automorphisms fixing x 2 , … , x n x_{2},\dots,x_{n} and sending x 1 x_{1} to x 1 ​ g x_{1}g for some g ∈ G g\in G . 
 
 
 Note that X X and Y Y are symmetric, i.e., inverse-closed. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.2 . 
 
 If G G is freely indecomposable with trivial center, then X X generates Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Since all elements of X X belong to Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) , it suffices to show that ⟨ X ∪ Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⟩ \langle X\cup\Inn(G*F_{n})\rangle equals A ≔ Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⋊ Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) A\coloneqq\Inn(G*F_{n})\rtimes\Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) (recall from the proof of Proposition  1.1 that this is indeed a semidirect product, because G G has trivial center). In [ Car11 , Section 4] a generating set for A A is given, consisting of two types of automorphisms, so it suffices to show that each of these is contained in ⟨ X ∪ Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⟩ \langle X\cup\Inn(G*F_{n})\rangle . Let us start by noticing that ⟨ X ⟩ \langle X\rangle contains Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) : indeed the generators of type (i), (ii) and (iv) are the standard Nielsen generators of Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) . 
 
 
 The first type is the set of Whitehead automorphisms , in the sense of [ Gil87 , Section 1] . These are those automorphisms α ∈ Aut ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \alpha\in\Aut(G*F_{n}) with the following property: there exists an element y ∈ G ∪ { x 1 ± , … , x n ± } y\in G\cup\{x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm}\} such that α | G \alpha|_{G} is either the identity or conjugation by y y , and each x i x_{i} is mapped to one of x i x_{i} , x i ​ y x_{i}y , y − 1 ​ x i y^{-1}x_{i} , or y − 1 ​ x i ​ y y^{-1}x_{i}y . In case α | G \alpha|_{G} is the identity, this is either a composition of generators of types (i), (iii), and (v) (if y ∈ G y\in G ) or an element of Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) (otherwise), so it belongs to ⟨ X ⟩ \langle X\rangle . In case α | G \alpha|_{G} is conjugation by y y , it is a composition of the inner automorphism defined by y y with either a composition of generators of types (i), (iii), and (v) (if y ∈ G y\in G ) or an element of Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) (otherwise). In all cases, we see that every Whitehead automorphism belongs to ⟨ X ∪ Inn ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) ⟩ \langle X\cup\Inn(G*F_{n})\rangle . 
 
 
 The second type is the set of automorphisms α \alpha that act on G G as conjugation by an element of G G , and send each x i x_{i} to x i ± 1 x_{i}^{\pm 1} .  Proof.

 Remark 2.5 . 
 
 Our focus here is the action of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) . This is, at least in spirit, closely related to the action of Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) on the subset of Hom ⁡ ( F n , G ) \Hom(F_{n},G) consisting of surjective homomorphisms (in words, the action of Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) on presentations of G G with n n generators), which we denote by P n ​ ( G ) P_{n}(G) . This is a very important action, whose study was popularized by Wiegold especially in the case of finite simple groups, see e.g., the excellent surveys [ Pak01 , Lub11 ] ; but it has interesting features also in the case of finitely generated infinite simple groups [ WW78 , Wie88 , CFF ] . 
 
 
 The action of Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) on P n ​ ( G ) P_{n}(G) is by precomposition. Quotienting out P n ​ ( G ) P_{n}(G) by the natural Aut ⁡ ( G ) \Aut(G) action by postcomposition defines a set P ¯ n ​ ( G ) \overline{P}_{n}(G) on which Inn ⁡ ( F n ) \Inn(F_{n}) acts trivially, and hence on which Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) acts. It turns out that a sufficient condition for this action to be highly transitive is that G G be a lawless Tarski monster (i.e., a lawless group all of whose proper subgroups are cyclic) [ GG13 ] . While being lawless is much less restrictive than being MIF (for example every group with a non-abelian free subgroup is lawless), the requirement that G G be a Tarski monster is extremely restrictive, so much so that no finitely presented example is known. Therefore it is unclear whether the stabilizers of such actions can ever be finitely generated, and so this does not seem to produce type (A) actions for Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) . It is more likely that one could produce an embedding of Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) in a group with a type (A) action, but this remains open  [ BBMZ , Problem 5.3] . 
 
 
 It is now known that all acylindrically hyperbolic groups admit faithful highly transitive actions [ HO16 ] , and this applies in particular to Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) [ BF10 ] (and, for that matter, to Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) [ GH21 ] ). But in this more general result the action is built using small cancellation techniques, and so it is even less likely that the methods could lead to a type (A) action for Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) .

 3. Proof of the main results 

 
 Now we can use the results from the previous sections to prove Theorem  E , after which most of the other main results will follow quickly. We will save the proofs of some of the main corollaries for the next two sections. 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  E . 
 
 Let G G be a finitely presented simple MIF group and let n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . Consider Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) acting on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) , and we claim this action is of type (A). It is faithful by Lemma  2.2 . The group Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) is finitely presented by Proposition  1.1 . The stabilizer of ϕ 0 ∈ Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \phi_{0}\in\Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) is finitely generated by Proposition  2.6 , and the action is transitive by Lemma  2.1 so every point stabilizer is finitely generated. Finally, there are finitely many orbits of pairs by Proposition  2.3 . We conclude that Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) admits a type (A) action, and hence satisfies the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture as desired. It therefore also satisfies the Boone–Higman conjecture by [ Zar ] .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  A . 
 
 For n = 1 n=1 we have Aut ⁡ ( F 1 ) ≅ ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \Aut(F_{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} so there is nothing to prove. Now assume n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . Fix some finitely presented simple MIF group, for instance Thompson’s group V V . Note that Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) embeds in Aut V ⁡ ( V ∗ F n ) \Aut_{V}(V*F_{n}) , which admits an action of type (A) by Theorem  E . Thus, Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) satisfies PBH for all n n .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  C . 
 
 First note that (i) implies (ii) by [ Zar , Theorem A] . By [ BBMZ26 , Proposition 5.4] , any twisted Brin–Thompson group admits a faithful highly transitive action, so (ii) implies (iii). Any finitely presented simple group admitting a highly transitive action is MIF thanks to [ HO16 , Theorem 5.9] (see also [ LBMB22b , Proposition A.1] ), so (iii) implies (iv). It remains to prove that (iv) implies (i). Let G G be a finitely presented simple MIF group. Since G G embeds in Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F 2 ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{2}) , for example by sending g g to the G G -automorphism that takes x 1 x_{1} to x 1 ​ g x_{1}g and fixes x 2 x_{2} , and Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F 2 ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{2}) admits an action of type (A) by Theorem  E , we conclude that G G satisfies PBH.
∎ 
 
 
 
 The main outstanding general question is the following. 
 
 
 Question 3.1 . 
 
 For G G a finitely presented simple group and n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , is the quotient of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) by the kernel of its action on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) finitely presented? 
 
 
 
 Just to recall the context, Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) itself is finitely presented, and we showed that if G G is MIF then this kernel is trivial, so the result holds in this case. By Observation  2.4 , if n = 1 n=1 then the kernel is trivial regardless, but for G G non-MIF (even for the easiest example we are aware of: Thompson’s group T T [ LBMB22b , Proposition 4.7] ) and n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 it is definitely non-trivial, so it is unclear what to expect. 
 
 
 If the answer to Question  3.1 is yes, then BH is equivalent to PBH, and finitely presented twisted Brin–Thompson groups are universal among all finitely presented simple groups. To see why this follows note that, on the one hand, if a group admits an action that satisfies all the aspects of being of type (A) except faithfulness, then modding out the kernel of the action yields a faithful action that satisfies all the aspects of being of type (A) except possibly finite presentability. Also, the copy of G G inside Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) coming from translating x 1 x_{1} by elements of G G intersects the kernel of the action on Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) \Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) trivially, so G G still embeds in the quotient. 
 
 
 A related question, that seems to be of interest in its own right, is the following. 
 
 
 Question 3.2 . 
 
 For G G a finitely presented simple group, is the quotient of G ∗ F n G*F_{n} by the Jacobson radical J G ​ ( G ∗ F n ) J_{G}(G*F_{n}) finitely presented? That is, is J G ​ ( G ∗ F n ) J_{G}(G*F_{n}) finitely normally generated in G ∗ F n G*F_{n} ? 
 
 
 
 While it is not immediately clear to us whether Questions  3.1 and  3.2 are equivalent for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , the latter feels like the more natural first step. Indeed, even for Thompson’s group T T this is unclear to us. 
 
 
 Yet another relevant question, which is more succinct than Question  3.1 but probably more difficult to prove directly, is the following. 
 
 
 Question 3.3 . 
 
 Does every finitely presented simple group embed in a finitely presented simple MIF group? 
 
 
 
 If the answer is yes, then BH is equivalent to PBH, and finitely presented twisted Brin–Thompson groups are universal among finitely presented simple groups. If the answer is no, then PBH is false. Thus either answer would be very interesting. 
 
 
 Remark 3.4 . 
 
 Now that we know that all finitely presented simple groups that are highly transitive, or more generally MIF, satisfy PBH, it is worth recording which finitely presented simple groups are known to have these properties. Note that for finitely generated simple groups, highly transitive implies MIF [ HO16 , Theorem 5.9] [ LBMB22b , Proposition A.1] , and in fact we are not aware of any finitely presented simple groups that are MIF but not highly transitive (there exist finitely generated examples [ LBMB22a , Theorem 4.13] ). 
 
 
 First there are the ones that are “obviously” highly transitive, hence MIF. This includes any finitely presented simple groups of homeomorphisms of the Cantor space { 1 , … , n } ℕ \{1,\dots,n\}^{\mathbb{N}} that contain the commutator subgroup of the Higman–Thompson group V n V_{n} . In particular this includes Thompson’s group V V itself, along with all finitely presented commutator subgroups of Röver–Nekrashevych groups V n ​ ( G ) V_{n}(G) [ Nek18 , Theorem 4.7] . Moreover, all twisted Brin–Thompson groups themselves are highly transitive [ BBMZ26 , Proposition 5.4] , hence MIF. 
 
 
 The finitely presented simple Burger–Mozes groups Λ \Lambda from [ BM97 ] are not “obviously” highly transitive and MIF, but it turns out that they do have these properties; we thank Adrien Le Boudec for this argument. By construction, Λ \Lambda is dense in a subgroup of the automorphism group of a locally finite tree T T that is 2 2 -transitive on the boundary [ BM97 , Remark 5] . It follows from density that the action of Λ \Lambda on ∂ T \partial T is extremely proximal. By [ FLMMS22 , Theorem B] it suffices to show that the action of Λ \Lambda on ∂ T \partial T is topologically free. Suppose that this is not the case; this means that there exists a proper open set U ⊂ ∂ T U\subset\partial T such that the subgroup Λ U \Lambda_{U} of elements of Λ \Lambda supported on U U is non-trivial. By extreme proximality, there exist g 1 , g 2 , g 3 ∈ Λ g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in\Lambda such that the g i ​ U g_{i}U are pairwise disjoint. Then the g i g_{i} -conjugates of Λ U \Lambda_{U} generate a direct product. However, Λ \Lambda has cohomological dimension 2 2 , so it cannot contain a direct product of three non-trivial groups. This shows that Burger–Mozes groups are highly transitive, hence MIF, and thus satisfy PBH. In fact, this latter fact was recently established, in a completely different way, in [ BLIW ] . 
 
 
 
 To the best of our knowledge, when this paper was first written the only existing source of finitely presented infinite simple groups for which PBH remained open was non-affine Kac–Moody groups over finite fields [ CR09 ] . These are closely related to Burger–Mozes groups in some aspects, but with key differences. One of these differences is the behavior of the action on the boundary, which in turn is responsible for the difference in their second bounded cohomology [ CF10 , Theorem 1.8] . In the above proof that Burger–Mozes groups are highly transitive, this was the main input, so a different argument would be needed. 
 
 
 Question 3.5 . 
 
 Are finitely presented simple Kac–Moody groups highly transitive? Are they MIF? Do they satisfy PBH? 
 
 
 
 Ekaterina Rybak [ Ryb ] recently answered Question  3.5 by showing that such groups are MIF, and hence they satisfy PBH (although it remains open if they are highly transitive). 
 
 
 Remark 3.6 . 
 
 Since the first version of this paper, two new examples of finitely presented simple groups have been constructed. The first construction, by Amir–Lazarovich, consists of lattices in products of a tree and a Davis complex [ AL ] . Projecting onto the tree factor, one can run the same argument as the one above for Burger–Mozes groups, to prove that they are MIF (this was already noticed in [ Ryb ] ). The second construction is in the realm of lattices in exotic two-dimensional irreducible Euclidean buildings. For type A ~ 2 \widetilde{A}_{2} , these are conjectured to be all virtually simple [ BCL19 ] , although no example has appeared to date. For type C ~ 2 \widetilde{C}_{2} , Titz Mite–Witzel [ TMW ] constructed non-residually finite examples, and the subsequent normal subgroup theorem of Lécureux–Witzel [ LW ] implies that they are virtually simple. They were also recently shown to be MIF, by Le Bars–Leibtag–Vigdorovich [ LBLV ] . 
 
 
 
 Remark 3.7 . 
 
 We proved that if G G is finitely presented, simple, and MIF, then the twisted Brin–Thompson group S ​ V Γ SV_{\Gamma} is also finitely presented, where Γ = Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Gamma=\Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) and S = Hom G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n , G ) S=\Hom_{G}(G*F_{n},G) . It is natural to conjecture that if G G has type ℱ m \F_{m} then so does S ​ V Γ SV_{\Gamma} . Here recall that a group has type ℱ m \boldsymbol{\F}_{m} if it has a classifying space with finite m m -skeleton, so type ℱ 2 \F_{2} means finitely presented. 
 
 
 However this is not the case in general. It is shown in [ FFKLZ , Corollary 1.6] that when G G is a finitely presented simple Burger–Mozes group, which is of type ℱ ∞ \F_{\infty} and even of type ℱ \F , the corresponding S ​ V Γ SV_{\Gamma} is not of type ℱ ∞ \F_{\infty} . 
 
 
 
 
 4. Permanence properties 

 
 In this section we use our construction to prove that the PBH conjecture is preserved under two important group-theoretic constructions, namely free products and regular wreath products. We start with free products; the main idea of the proof is due to Xiaolei Wu. 
 
 
 Proof of Corollary  F . 
 
 Let A A and B B be groups that satisfy PBH. By [ Zar , Proposition 5.5] , the direct product A × B A\times B also satisfies PBH, so A × B A\times B embeds in a finitely presented twisted Brin–Thompson group, which we will just denote by G G . Since G G is finitely presented, simple, and MIF, Theorem  E says that Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) satisfies PBH. Now observe that (for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ) Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) contains G ∗ G G*G , and hence A ∗ B A*B , for example as the subgroup of G G -automorphisms generated by the copy of G G fixing x 2 , … , x n x_{2},\dots,x_{n} and sending x 1 x_{1} to x 1 ​ g x_{1}g for g ∈ G g\in G , and the copy of G G fixing x 2 , … , x n x_{2},\dots,x_{n} and sending x 1 x_{1} to x 1 ​ ( x 2 − 1 ​ g ​ x 2 ) x_{1}(x_{2}^{-1}gx_{2}) for g ∈ G g\in G .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Given that satisfying PBH is preserved under direct and free products, the following is the natural next step: 
 
 
 Question 4.1 . 
 
 Let G G be a graph product over a finite graph such that each vertex group satisfies PBH. Does G G satisfy PBH? 
 
 
 
 Another natural question one might have is whether satisfying PBH is preserved under amalgamated products, or more general graph of groups constructions. When the corresponding Bass–Serre tree is locally finite, some results are contained in [ BLIW ] , but in the general case this is not true. Indeed, solvability of the word problem for an amalgamated product is tied to solvability of the membership problem for the amalgamated subgroup, and so it is easy to construct examples of groups satisfying PBH but whose amalgamated product does not even have solvable word problem. 
 
 
 Now we move to wreath products. We first prove a general criterion for permutational wreath products, and then use Theorem  E to apply it to regular wreath products. 
 
 
 Proposition 4.2 . 
 
 Let A A be a group satisfying the permutational Boone–Higman conjecture. Let B ↷ X B\curvearrowright X be an action of type (A). Then the permutational wreath product A ≀ X B A\wr_{X}B satisfies the (permutational) Boone–Higman conjecture. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Up to embedding, we may assume that A A itself admits an action of type (A), say A ↷ Y A\curvearrowright Y . We consider the natural imprimitive action A ≀ X B ↷ Y × X A\wr_{X}B\curvearrowright Y\times X , where we see Y × X Y\times X as a disjoint union of copies of Y Y indexed by X X , with each summand of ⊕ X A \oplus_{X}A acting on the corresponding copy of Y Y , and B B shifting the copies according to its action on X X . We claim that this action is of type (A). 
 
 
 First, A ≀ X B A\wr_{X}B is finitely presented by [ dC06 ] . Now let ( y , x ) ∈ Y × X (y,x)\in Y\times X ; an element ( ( a x ) x ∈ X , b ) ((a_{x})_{x\in X},b) fixes it if and only if b b fixes x x and a x a_{x} fixes y y . Now let x 1 , … , x n x_{1},\ldots,x_{n} be a set of orbit representatives for the action of Stab B ⁡ ( x ) \Stab_{B}(x) on X ∖ { x } X\setminus\{x\} ; note that this is finite because B ↷ X B\curvearrowright X is of type (A). Then Stab A ≀ X B ⁡ ( y , x ) \Stab_{A\wr_{X}B}(y,x) is generated by Stab B ⁡ ( x ) \Stab_{B}(x) together with the subgroup of ⊕ X A \oplus_{X}A with Stab A ⁡ ( y ) \Stab_{A}(y) at the x x coordinate, A A at the x i x_{i} coordinate for i = 1 , … , n i=1,\ldots,n , and the trivial group at all other coordinates. Since both actions are of type (A), these are both finitely generated groups, so Stab A ≀ X B ⁡ ( y , x ) \Stab_{A\wr_{X}B}(y,x) is finitely generated. 
 
 
 Finally, we show that the action has finitely many orbits of pairs. Using the action of B B , any pair ( y 1 , x 1 ) , ( y 2 , x 2 ) (y_{1},x_{1}),(y_{2},x_{2}) can be moved so that { x 1 , x 2 } \{x_{1},x_{2}\} is mapped to a representative of the finitely many orbits of pairs for the action of B B on X X . Now if x 1 ≠ x 2 x_{1}\neq x_{2} , we can use the action of the corresponding copies of A A to move y 1 , y 2 y_{1},y_{2} to representatives of the finitely many orbits for the action of A A on Y Y . ∎

 Let A , B A,B be two groups satisfying PBH. Then B B embeds in a finitely presented twisted Brin–Thompson group, which we denote by G G , and it suffices to show that A ≀ G A\wr G satisfies PBH.  5. Boone–Higman for specific groups 

 
 In this section we prove Corollary  B , showing that many specific groups of interest satisfy the (P)BH conjecture. 
 
 
 Proof of Corollary  B . 
 
 By Theorem  A , we know that Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) satisfies PBH for all n n . This immediately implies that ((extended) loop) braid groups satisfy PBH, since we have embeddings ℬ n → ℒ ​ ℬ n → ℒ ​ ℬ n e ​ x ​ t → Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{LB}_{n}\to\mathcal{LB}_{n}^{ext}\to\Aut(F_{n}) ; see, e.g., [ Dam17 , Section 4] . Moreover, the pure ribbon braid group and the pure loop braid group are isomorphic [ Dam17 , Proposition 5.10] , which implies that ℛ ​ ℬ n \mathcal{RB}_{n} is commensurable to a group satisfying PBH and thus satisfies PBH [ Zar , Proposition 5.6] . 
 
 
 For Artin groups, those of type B n = C n B_{n}=C_{n} are the “annular braid groups”, i.e., the mapping class groups of punctured annuli, and the Artin groups of type  A ~ n \widetilde{A}_{n} embed into these  [ KP02 ] . The Artin groups of type  D n D_{n} embed into the mapping class groups of certain finite type surfaces with nonempty boundary  [ PV92 , Theorem 1] . The Artin groups of type I 2 ​ ( m ) I_{2}(m) are all commensurable to the braid group on three strands  [ CP22 , Theorem 5] and thus satisfy PBH  [ Zar , Proposition 5.6] . The Artin groups of type  C ~ n \widetilde{C}_{n} embed into braid groups [ All02 , CC05 ] . Finally, the Artin group of type H 3 H_{3} embeds into the Artin group of type D 6 D_{6} [ Cri99 ] . 
 
 
 We are left to prove the results on mapping class groups. Surfaces Σ \Sigma are always assumed to be of finite type. If Σ \Sigma is non-orientable, then its mapping class group embeds into the mapping class group of its orientable double cover Σ ~ \widetilde{\Sigma} [ Par14 , Proposition 26] . Moreover, if Σ \Sigma has punctures or boundary, so does Σ ~ \widetilde{\Sigma} , and if Σ \Sigma has demigenus at most three, then Σ ~ \widetilde{\Sigma} has genus at most two. Hence from now on we may assume that all surfaces are orientable . We will use the fact that satisfying PBH is invariant under commensurability [ Zar , Proposition 5.6] . In particular, it suffices to consider mapping class groups, and the results for extended mapping class groups will follow. 
 
 
 First we will prove the result for surfaces with at least two punctures. Let Σ \Sigma be a closed surface with at least one puncture, and let ∗ * be a marked point on Σ \Sigma , so MCG ⁡ ( Σ , ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma,*) is the group of all self-homeomorphisms of Σ \Sigma that preserve the basepoint ∗ * , up to pointed isotopy. Then MCG ⁡ ( Σ , ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma,*) embeds into Aut ⁡ ( π 1 ​ ( Σ ) ) \Aut(\pi_{1}(\Sigma)) by the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer Theorem (see [ FM12 , Theorem 8.8] and the paragraph following it), which is just Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) for some n n , and therefore satisfies PBH. Moreover MCG ⁡ ( Σ , ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma,*) is the finite index subgroup of MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∖ { ∗ } ) \MCG(\Sigma\setminus\{*\}) consisting of mapping classes that fix the puncture ∗ * , so MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∖ { ∗ } ) \MCG(\Sigma\setminus\{*\}) satisfies PBH. This shows that MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) \MCG(\Sigma) satisfies PBH whenever Σ \Sigma is a closed surface with at least two punctures. 
 
 
 For the case of one puncture, first let Σ \Sigma be a closed surface, which we may assume to be of genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 , and let Σ ∗ ≔ Σ ∖ { ∗ } \Sigma_{*}\coloneqq\Sigma\setminus\{*\} , for some ∗ ∈ Σ *\in\Sigma . Then Σ ∗ \Sigma_{*} has a double cover Σ ~ \widetilde{\Sigma} , which must have genus 2 ​ g − 1 2g-1 and two punctures. Let L ≤ MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∗ ) L\leq\MCG(\Sigma_{*}) denote the subgroup of mapping classes that can be lifted to Σ ~ \widetilde{\Sigma} . Identifying MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma_{*}) with Aut ⁡ ( π 1 ​ ( Σ ) ) \Aut(\pi_{1}(\Sigma)) via the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer Theorem [ FM12 , Theorem 8.8] , L L corresponds to the subgroup of automorphisms that preserve H ≤ π 1 ​ ( Σ ) H\leq\pi_{1}(\Sigma) where H H is the index- 2 2 subgroup defined by the double cover. It follows that L L has finite index in MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma_{*}) . Finally, we note that each element in L L has a unique lift that fixes the two punctures in  Σ ~ \widetilde{\Sigma} . This defines a homomorphism L → MCG ⁡ ( Σ ~ ) L\to\MCG(\widetilde{\Sigma}) , which is injective by a version of the Birman–Hilden Theorem [ ALS09 , Corollary 4] . Since MCG ⁡ ( Σ ~ ) \MCG(\widetilde{\Sigma}) satisfies PBH by the previous paragraph, this proves PBH for L L . Since L L has finite index in MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma_{*}) , this proves PBH for MCG ⁡ ( Σ ∗ ) \MCG(\Sigma_{*}) . 
 
 
 Next let Σ \Sigma be a surface with non-empty boundary. Let Σ ′ \Sigma^{\prime} be the surface obtained from Σ \Sigma by attaching a twice-punctured disk to every boundary component of Σ \Sigma . Then MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) \MCG(\Sigma) embeds into MCG ⁡ ( Σ ′ ) \MCG(\Sigma^{\prime}) [ FM12 , Theorem 3.18] , which is handled by the previous paragraph. 
 
 
 The only remaining case to prove is when Σ \Sigma is the closed surface of genus 2 2 . Let h ∈ MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) h\in\MCG(\Sigma) be the hyperelliptic involution. The subgroup ⟨ h ⟩ \langle h\rangle is central and of order 2 2 , and MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) / ⟨ h ⟩ ≅ MCG ⁡ ( Σ ′ ) \MCG(\Sigma)/\langle h\rangle\cong\MCG(\Sigma^{\prime}) , where Σ ′ \Sigma^{\prime} is a sphere with six punctures [ BH73 ] . Moreover, MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) \MCG(\Sigma) is residually finite [ FM12 , Theorem 6.11] , and so there exists a finite-index subgroup H < MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) H<\MCG(\Sigma) intersecting ⟨ h ⟩ \langle h\rangle trivially, hence mapping injectively into MCG ⁡ ( Σ ′ ) \MCG(\Sigma^{\prime}) . This shows that MCG ⁡ ( Σ ) \MCG(\Sigma) is commensurable to MCG ⁡ ( Σ ′ ) \MCG(\Sigma^{\prime}) , which satisfies PBH.
∎ 
 
 
 
 A natural question is whether a similar approach could prove the (P)BH conjecture for Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) , and for mapping class groups of closed surfaces. As indicated in Remark  2.5 , it seems very difficult to find an action of type (A) for Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) itself, so we would want to embed it in some analog of Aut G ⁡ ( G ∗ F n ) \Aut_{G}(G*F_{n}) . However, inner automorphisms of F n F_{n} do not interact nicely with G G -automorphisms, so it is not clear whether this is possible. If a version for Out ⁡ ( F n ) \Out(F_{n}) were possible, it is likely that a similar argument would also cover the mapping class group of a closed surface Σ \Sigma , since this is an index- 2 2 subgroup of Out ⁡ ( π 1 ​ ( Σ ) ) \Out(\pi_{1}(\Sigma)) . 
 
 
 Remark 5.1 . 
 
 Knowing that every Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) satisfies (P)BH not only establishes the (P)BH conjecture for the above groups, for which it was not previously known, but also recovers the (P)BH conjecture for some important classes of groups for which it was previously known. 
 
 
 First, we recover the fact that virtually compact special groups satisfy PBH. Indeed, by commensurability invariance it suffices to show this for RAAGs [ HW08 ] , and RAAGs embed into Aut ⁡ ( F n ) \Aut(F_{n}) [ Hum94 ] (in fact they even embed into braid groups [ KK15 ] ), so we conclude from Theorem  A that they satisfy PBH.
It was already known that RAAGs satisfy PBH, in fact for various reasons. First, they embed into GL n ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) [ DJ00 ] , which embeds in a highly transitive finitely presented simple group thanks to work of Scott [ Sco84 ] , and hence satisfies PBH by Theorem  C . More directly, every RAAG embeds in some n ​ V nV [ BBM20 ] , in fact into 2 ​ V 2V [ Sal ] , and thus satisfies PBH. 
 
 
 Next, we recover the fact that (finitely generated free)-by-cyclic groups satisfy PBH, which was recently established, in a completely different way, in [ BLIW ] . In fact we will prove something more general in Proposition  5.2 below. Thanks to [ Lin26 ] , we deduce that, more generally, finitely generated free-by-cyclic groups satisfy the PBH conjecture, and hence also finitely generated virtually free-by-cyclic groups. This applies to many groups of geometric interest, including large families of one-relator groups [ KL24b , Fis26 ] . 
 
 
 
 Proposition 5.2 . 
 
 Let A A be a centerless group such that Aut ⁡ ( A ) \Aut(A) satisfies PBH, (for instance, a finitely generated free group of rank at least 2 2 , or the fundamental group of a closed surface of genus at least 2 2 ). Let G G be a group containing A A as a normal subgroup such that G / A G/A satisfies PBH. Then G G satisfies PBH. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The action of G G on A A by conjugation induces a homomorphism G → Aut ⁡ ( A ) G\to\Aut(A) . Consider the product G → Aut ⁡ ( A ) × G / A G\to\Aut(A)\times G/A with the quotient map. The kernel of the right component is precisely A A , but since A A is centerless, the restriction of the left component to A A is injective. Hence the product G → Aut ⁡ ( A ) × G / A G\to\Aut(A)\times G/A is injective. Now by assumption Aut ⁡ ( A ) \Aut(A) and G / A G/A satisfy PBH, hence so does their product by [ Zar , Proposition 5.5] .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Finally, let us discuss finitely generated 3 3 -manifold groups. 
 
 
 Question 5.3 . 
 
 Let M M be a 3 3 -manifold with finitely generated fundamental group. Does π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) satisfy the Boone–Higman conjecture? 
 
 
 
 The results in this paper allow us to reduce to the case of non-Seifert fibered closed graph manifolds. 
 
 
 Corollary 5.4 . 
 
 Suppose that all fundamental groups of non-Seifert fibered closed graph manifolds satisfy PBH. Then all finitely generated 3 3 -manifold groups satisfy PBH. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let M M be a (connected) 3 3 -manifold such that π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) is finitely generated. By [ KL24a , Proposition 2.2] , there exist compact orientable aspherical 3 3 -manifolds M 1 , … , M n M_{1},\ldots,M_{n} and a free group F F such that a finite-index subgroup of π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) is isomorphic to the free product of F F and the π 1 ​ ( M i ) \pi_{1}(M_{i}) . By commensurability [ Zar , Proposition 5.6] and Corollary  F , we may now assume that M M itself is compact orientable and aspherical. 
 
 
 If M M has non-empty boundary, then it is homotopy equivalent to its spine (see e.g., [ Cas65 , page 560] ), which is an aspherical 2 2 -complex, hence π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) has rational cohomological dimension at most 2 2 . Now [ KL24a , Theorem 1.1] applies and gives that π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) is virtually free-by-cyclic, hence it satisfies PBH by Remark  5.1 (or by [ BLIW ] and [ Lin26 ] ). 
 
 
 If M M is closed, and not a graph manifold, then by one of the virtual fibering theorems of Agol, Wise and Przytycki–Wise [ Ago08 , Ago13 , PW18 , Wis21 ] , M M virtually fibers. Again by commensurability [ Zar , Proposition 5.6] we may assume M M is fibered, so there exists a closed surface Σ \Sigma such that π 1 ​ ( M ) ≅ π 1 ​ ( Σ ) ⋊ α ℤ \pi_{1}(M)\cong\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z} , for α ∈ Aut ⁡ ( π 1 ​ ( Σ ) ) \alpha\in\Aut(\pi_{1}(\Sigma)) . If Σ \Sigma has genus at least 2 2 , then π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) satisfies PBH by Proposition  5.2 . If Σ \Sigma is the torus, then π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) embeds into GL n ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) for some n n , hence satisfies PBH. 
 
 
 It remains to consider closed Seifert fibered manifolds. For such a manifold M M , we may assume that π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) is infinite, in which case up to finite index it is a bundle S 1 → M → Σ S^{1}\to M\to\Sigma , where Σ \Sigma is a closed surface with χ ⁡ ( Σ ) ≤ 0 \chi(\Sigma)\leq 0 . Now by [ AF13 , Proof of Lemma 4.17] , there exists a finite-index subgroup G e < π 1 ​ ( M ) G_{e}<\pi_{1}(M) that embeds into H × π 1 ​ ( Σ ) H\times\pi_{1}(\Sigma) , where H H is a finitely generated nilpotent group. Hence H H embeds into some GL n ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}) (in fact, the H H appearing in the proof is just the integral Heisenberg group H 3 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathrm{H}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) ) and so it satisfies PBH, and π 1 ​ ( Σ ) \pi_{1}(\Sigma) satisfies PBH as we have seen above. By [ Zar , Proposition 5.5] , the direct product H × π 1 ​ ( Σ ) H\times\pi_{1}(\Sigma) satisfies PBH, hence so does G e G_{e} , hence π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) by commensurability [ Zar , Proposition 5.6] .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Remark 5.5 . 
 
 Note that the proof above also covers closed graph manifolds that virtually fiber over the circle (which includes all non-positively curved graph manifolds [ Sve02 ] ), so the problem is actually reduced to proving PBH for fundamental groups of non-fibered closed graph manifolds. Up to finite index, these are fundamental groups of graphs of groups with F n × ℤ F_{n}\times\mathbb{Z} vertex groups and ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} edge groups [ Sun , Lemma 2.1] , and we already mentioned that the question of whether graphs of groups with vertex groups satisfying PBH satisfy PBH is a delicate one. On the other hand, these groups have algebraic features that could make them amenable to arguments using closure properties for PBH (indeed a recent example can be found in [ Sun ] ); the main issue is that infinitely generated free groups often play a role in these descriptions.
