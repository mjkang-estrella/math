315996 For a finite simple undirected graph $G$, the degeneracy of $G$ is the least
integer $d$ such that every non-empty subgraph of $G$ has a vertex of degree at
most $d$. For a positive integer $k$, a proper $k$-colouring of $G$ is a map
$\gamma : V(G) \to \{1,\dots,k\}$ with $\gamma(u) \neq \gamma(v)$ for every edge
$uv \in E(G)$. Two proper $k$-colourings $\gamma, \gamma'$ of $G$ are adjacent
if there is exactly one vertex $v \in V(G)$ with $\gamma(v) \neq \gamma'(v)$;
the reconfiguration graph of proper $k$-colourings of $G$ has as its vertices
all proper $k$-colourings of $G$, with this adjacency.

For every integer $d \ge 1$ there exists a constant $C = C(d)$ such that for
every graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with degeneracy at most
$d$ and every two proper $(d+2)$-colourings $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of
$G$, there is a sequence of proper $(d+2)$-colourings

$$
\alpha = \gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_\ell = \beta
$$

such that $\gamma_{i-1}$ and $\gamma_i$ differ in the colour of exactly one
vertex for each $i \in \{1,\dots,\ell\}$, and

$$
\ell \le C n^2 .
$$

Equivalently, the reconfiguration graph of proper
$(d+2)$-colourings of an $n$-vertex graph of degeneracy at most
$d$ is connected and has diameter $O(n^2)$, where the implied constant depends
only on $d$.
