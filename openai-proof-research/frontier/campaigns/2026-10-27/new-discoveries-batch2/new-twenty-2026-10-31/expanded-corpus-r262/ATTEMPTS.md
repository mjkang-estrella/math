## 320200: certify a graph with no good vertex

The literal target is existence of a good vertex in every nonempty graph, not just the independent-set bound. The original source defines the exact product and Conjectures 1.4 and 1.5 on pages 2–4. Its Figure 1 gives one bad distinguished vertex, not a graph with every vertex bad. Copying that example, joining copies, or attaching leaves is not a solution: every new vertex must also be bad, and the product depends on the changed degrees. Multiplication by disconnected components preserves the sign of the good-vertex inequality at a vertex, so taking disjoint copies of the printed example cannot remove any good vertices it contains.

Sah–Sawhney–Stoner–Zhao, author PDF 1805.04021, Section 2, equation (2), and Section 4, equation (8), prove the maximum-degree inequality on bipartite graphs. The proof cancels the factors outside the first three distance layers and uses analytic inequalities in the appendix. This eliminates the entire bipartite witness route, not just regular bicliques. Their proof of the independent-set bound for arbitrary graphs uses the bipartite double cover. It does not transfer a good vertex to the original graph: removing one lifted vertex is different from removing both lifts, and the products of the two deleted graphs do not simply square. Thus the already proved independent-set bound is not a proof of the literal target. A proof extension must handle edges inside distance layers; no sign-compatible correction has been established here.

Conditional finite certificate plan: root could authorize a bounded search among connected nonbipartite graphs, with every vertex tested. Do not assume a minimum counterexample exists or that a small one is likely. For a fixed proposed graph, let F consist of G and all G-x and G-N[x]. Choose a positive common multiple D of all nonzero degree products d_H(u)d_H(v), for H in F. Define the integer

    A_H = 2^(D iso(H)) product_(uv in E(H)) (2^d_H(u)+2^d_H(v)-1)^(D/(d_H(u)d_H(v))).

Then Pi(H)^D = A_H. Choose positive rational bounds L_H <= Pi(H) <= U_H, certified by L_H^D <= A_H <= U_H^D; clearing denominators leaves integer inequalities. For every x require U_G < L_(G-x)+L_(G-N[x]). Monotonicity of positive integer powers proves the bounds, hence every vertex is strictly bad. Rational approximations suffice whenever a true strict counterexample exists, by density and its finite positive minimum margin. No logarithmic floating-point comparison belongs in the proof certificate. Recompute degrees and isolated vertices separately in every H.

Pi implementation, if root authorizes it: explicit adjacency on Fin n; induced deletion and reindexing; exact iso/degree/product definitions over real positive roots; common exponent identity; rational interval data; integer inequalities by kernel reduction; final exists G, nonempty G and forall x, not Good G x. No native_decide, no axioms, no unproved equivalence to a surrogate objective. Certificate size is O(n^3) raw degree/edge data plus O(n) integer-power comparisons; bit sizes depend on D and the unknown strict margin. There is no actual adjacency matrix or margin in this lane. Current journal/correction and complete registry clearance remain open, so this plan is not execution-ready research qualification.

## 318385: two definitions give different questions

All four arXiv versions were fetched. Versions 1 and 2 define c(G)=c(U_G), where U_G is the clutter of maximal independent sets, but later change to matching arguments without consistently changing notation. Versions 3 and 4 state Conjecture 1 with c(M_G), the maximal-matching clutter, and n>=1 rather than n>=2. The journal-body attempt returned a captcha page; it was retained as an access failure, not read or retried. These changes matter more than a finite graph search.

Under the early independent-set definition there is a complete ordinary refutation of the printed classification: take K_(2,2,2). Its maximal independent sets are exactly its three parts of size two. A singleton in a part identifies that part, while the empty set identifies none, so every edge of the clutter has hardness 1/2. The graph is connected and 4-regular, is not C7, is not complete, and is not bipartite. This is a routine complete multipartite observation under a superseded definition, and receives zero credit. It does not refute the corrected matching conjecture.

Indeed, in K_(2,2,2), match the two vertices of the first part bijectively to the two of the second part, leaving the third part uncovered. This matching H is maximal. Deleting either matching edge permits an alternative maximal matching using an uncovered vertex, so no proper subset recognizes H. Thus c(M_G)=1. The proposed six-vertex witness fails for the intended later conjecture.

For a general matching H, write U=V(G) minus its covered vertices. H is maximal exactly when U is independent. H without edge uv recognizes H exactly when neither u nor v has a neighbor in U. Proof: with H-uv fixed the only free vertices are U,u,v. If its only edge is uv, the unique maximal completion is uv. If another edge exists it joins u or v to U, and extending that edge yields another maximal completion. Consequently c(M_G)<1 exactly when every maximal matching has at least one edge whose endpoints have no neighbors in U. If a proper recognizing subset exists, it lies in H-e for some e; supersets of recognizing sets also recognize, proving the converse reduction.

This supplies a precise finite certificate format: enumerate all maximal matchings, supply for each an edge with the stated property, and prove enumeration completeness; also certify regularity, connectivity and exclusion of C7, complete and complete bipartite families. Merely testing perfect matchings is vacuous, since U is empty. The source's Lemma 4 already supplies related structural restrictions, so the reduction itself is not a new mechanism or credit. No graph survives here, and current journal and later-classification clearance is unresolved.

## 321378: parity reduction killed by later classification

For a cut W, a switch can erase its crossing edges exactly when every crossing 2-by-2 rectangle has even parity. Necessity follows because switching toggles zero, two, or four edges in each rectangle. For sufficiency choose a in W and c outside W and solve switching bits s_b=A_bc, s_d=A_ad+A_ac; rectangle parity gives A_bd=s_b+s_d. This is exactly the original source's Lemma 1, not new work.

A finite even-order critical graph would admit a compact certificate: for every admissible W give an odd rectangle; for each deleted vertex give an admissible cut and switching bits erasing it. Fixing one isolated vertex by switching removes a gauge degree of freedom. Neither the odd cyclic examples nor duplicating a vertex gives a witness: twins create an isolable pair. This was a plausible exact finite route before the later-body check.

Bespalov–Krotov, arXiv:1412.2947, Theorem 1 on page 8, states that if all one-vertex deletions are separable then the graph is separable or q is even, n is odd, and the graph is switching-isomorphic to G_(n,gamma). At q=2 additive switching is exactly Seidel switching. For even n the exception is impossible. Contraposition proves the intended even-order assertion. Proposition 3 gives the exceptional odd family and explicit isolable pairs after deletion. No even-order search or re-formalization deserves new credit. Only target passages and the proof setup were read, not a line-by-line verification of the whole Russian proof; the journal translation remains unread.

## 319218: exact honeycomb constraints, but no finite witness

The source's Section 1 requires one point in every occupied consecutive row in each of three lattice directions and distinct ordered vector differences. Its Theorem 1 reduces to 2r+1 dots in a Lee sphere. In axial integer coordinates this becomes points (x,y) with |x|,|y|,|x+y|<=r, each projection x,y,x+y a bijection onto [-r,r], with all differences p_i-p_j for i!=j distinct. A certificate for a thirteenth orbit must list these integer coordinates and prove that no dihedral symmetry followed by translation maps it to any of the twelve printed arrays. Compare actual point sets, not degree sequences or only radius.

The source reports r<=643 from a separate bound, symmetric searches to 31 and Costas-derived constructions to 325, and prints twelve objects in Figures 7–11, including its own two new radius-seven examples. Coordinates of every figure were not transcribed or visually reviewed here. Thus neither a new search range nor a new example is qualified. The general exact-cover formulation has projection equalities plus difference-uniqueness constraints; the printed Costas recipes cover only part of that feasible set. A blanket claim that all radii have been excluded would require an exhaustive certificate and the external radius bound, far beyond a bounded ideation task.
