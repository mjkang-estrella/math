378319 Separation of survival and root-reinfection thresholds on strongly exponentially growing trees
Let $T$ be an infinite tree. Call a vertex $v$ **essential** if $T\setminus\{v\}$ has at least two infinite components. Say that $T$ has **strongly exponential growth** if there is some integer $r\geq1$ such that, for every $v\in T$, there are at least three essential vertices $w$ at graph distance $r$ from $v$. Let $\lambda_1$ be the survival threshold and let $\lambda_a$ be the threshold above which the root is reinfected at arbitrarily large times; together with $\lambda_b$, $\lambda_2$, and $\lambda_c$, these are the critical values defined for the contact process above. **Separation conjecture.** For any tree with strongly exponential growth, either all the above critical values are zero, or

$$
\lambda_1\neq\lambda_a.
$$

The claim concerns whether survival can begin strictly before recurrent reinfection of the root on sufficiently branching trees. The source gives no resolution.

378274 Small-trade conjecture for subsets of Latin squares
Let $L$ be a Latin square of order $n\geq 3$, and let $S$ be a subset of its entries with $|S|\leq \left\lfloor n^2/4\right\rfloor$. For a Latin trade $T$ in $L$, let $R(T)$, $C(T)$, and $E(T)$ denote respectively the numbers of rows, columns, and elements involved in $T$. **Small-trade conjecture.** There exists a Latin trade $T\subseteq L$ such that

$$
T\cap S=\varnothing
$$

and at least one of $R(T)\leq 3$, $C(T)\leq 3$, or $E(T)\leq 3$ holds. This conjecture is motivated by the use of small trades to rule out small uniquely completable or critical sets. It is stated as a conjecture in the paper; no resolution beyond the discussion and the verified order-seven case is supplied here.

320954 McCuaig–Ota conjecture on connected subgraph deletion
A graph $G$ is **3-connected** if deleting fewer than three vertices leaves it connected. For a subgraph $H$, write $G-V(H)$ for the graph obtained by deleting all vertices of $H$.

**McCuaig–Ota conjecture.** For every $ellgeq1$ there exists an $f(ell)$ such that every $3$-connected graph on at least $f(ell)$ vertices contains a connected subgraph $H$ on $ell$ vertices such that $G-V(H)$ is $2$-connected.

The conjecture is known for $ellleq4$ with sharp values of the threshold, but is wide open in general. Even the existence of a corresponding threshold for higher connectivity is not known.

321317 The list-version of Thue's theorem for nonrepetitive sequences
Let $n\geqslant 1$ and let $L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}$ be sets of symbols, each of size $3$. A sequence $s_{1}\ldots s_{n}$ is **chosen from lists** $L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}$ if $s_i\in L_i$ for every $i=1,\ldots,n$; it is nonrepetitive if it contains no adjacent repeated block. **The list-version of Thue's theorem.** For every $n\geqslant 1$ and every sequence of sets $L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}$, each of size $3$, there is a nonrepetitive sequence chosen from $L_{1},\ldots,L_{n}$. This is the list analogue of Thue's theorem that three symbols suffice for arbitrarily long nonrepetitive sequences; the paper's preceding argument establishes only that lists of size at least $4$ suffice, so the assertion for lists of size $3$ remains open.

321378 The even-order non-separable subgraph conjecture
Let $G$ be a non-separable graph of even order, meaning that no subset $W$ of its vertices with $2\leq |W|\leq |V(G)|-2$ becomes disconnected from its complement after any switching. A subgraph obtained by removing one vertex is an induced subgraph, as only induced subgraphs are considered. **Even-order non-separable subgraph conjecture.** From every non-separable graph of even order one can obtain a non-separable subgraph by removing one vertex. The question concerns the even-order counterpart to the known odd-order examples: for every odd $n\geq 5$, there is a non-separable graph of order $n$ whose subgraphs of order $n-1$ are all separable, while exhaustive searches found no analogous examples of orders $6$ or $8$.

319306 The Big-Line-Big-Chromatic-Number Conjecture for planar visibility graphs
Let $P$ be a finite set of points in the plane, and let $\mathcal{V}(P)$ be its visibility graph. A $(k-1)$-colouring assigns one of $k-1$ colours to each point. **Big-Line-Big-Chromatic-Number Conjecture.** For all integers $k\geq2$ and $\ell\geq1$ there is an integer $n$ such that if $P$ has at least $n$ points and is assigned one of $k-1$ colours, then $P$ contains $\ell$ collinear points or some visible pair receives the same colour; equivalently, $\chi(\mathcal{V}(P))\geq k$. This weakening follows from the Big-Line-Big-Clique Conjecture and is known for $k\leq5$ or $\ell\leq3$; the cases beyond these ranges remain open.

317293 Finite-field list conjecture for matroid representability
Let $k$ be a prime power. The claim concerns matroids and representability over fields and finite fields $\operatorname{GF}(q)$. **Finite-field list conjecture.** There exists a number $n_k$ such that, for all matroids $M$, $M$ is representable over all fields with at least $k$ elements if and only if it is representable over all finite fields $\operatorname{GF}(q)$ with $k \leq q \leq n_k$. This generalizes the characterization of near-regular matroids; the paper states that the conjecture remains open, including for $k=4$.

379698 The Stability Conjecture for generic relator tuples in free groups
Fix $k\ge 2$ and $m\ge 1$, and let $F=F(a_1,\dots,a_k)$ be the free group of rank $k$. For an $m$-tuple $\sigma$ of elements of $F$, write $R(\sigma)$ for the corresponding set of relators. **The Stability Conjecture.** There exists an algorithmically recognizable generic class $\mathcal{Y}$ of $m$-tuples of elements of $F$ such that, whenever $\sigma,\tau\in\mathcal{Y}$ and $\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(F)$ satisfy that $R(\sigma)$ and $R(\alpha(\tau))$ have the same normal closure in $F$, one has

$$
R(\sigma)=R(\alpha(\tau)).
$$

The conjecture would extend the paper's rigidity arguments from generic quotients of the modular group to generic quotients of free groups of arbitrary finite rank. The authors report that their computer experiments support it, but no proof or resolution is given here.

377492 The Wide Partition Conjecture
Let $M$ be a matroid. An $M$-tableau of shape $\lambda$ is a Young diagram of shape $\lambda$ with an element of $M$ in each cell. A partition $\lambda$ **satisfies Rota's conjecture** if, for every matroid $M$ and every sequence $(I_i)$ of independent sets of $M$ with $|I_i|=\lambda_i$ for all $i$, there is an $M$-tableau $T$ of shape $\lambda$ such that the elements in row $i$ are exactly $I_i$ and the elements in column $j$ form an independent set of $M$.

**The Wide Partition Conjecture.** A partition $\lambda$ satisfies Rota's conjecture if and only if it is wide.

The conjecture generalizes Rota's basis conjecture and was formulated as a possible route to proving it by induction. The paper explains that necessity is straightforward, while sufficiency is the substantive unresolved direction; the free-matroid case is treated separately.

320189 Conjecture PC5 on hereditary maximum parsimony trees
Let $S:=f_1f_2\ldots f_n$ be a sequence of characters (an alignment) on a set $X$ of taxa, with $|X|=m$, and let $\mathcal{T}$ be a maximum parsimony tree for $S$. For a subset $Y\subseteq X$, write $S|_Y$ for the sequence restricted to the taxa in $Y$, and $\mathcal{T}|_Y$ for the tree restricted to $Y$. **Conjecture PC5.** For each $k=4,\ldots,m-1$, there exists a subset $Y$ of $X$ with $|Y|=k$ such that $\mathcal{T}|_Y$ is a maximum parsimony tree for $S|_Y$. This conjecture asks whether every maximum parsimony tree has most-parsimonious restrictions at every intermediate number of taxa; the cases of one, two, and three taxa are excluded because their unrooted trees are unique. The paper investigates this heredity property through examples of maximum parsimony trees and presents the conjecture as an open question.

320373 The minimum vertex-degree conjecture for perfect matchings in uniform hypergraphs
Let $r\geq 3$ be an integer. An **$r$-uniform hypergraph** $H$ is a hypergraph whose edges have size $r$, and its **minimum vertex degree** $\delta_1(H)$ is the minimum number of edges containing any vertex. A **perfect matching** is a collection of pairwise disjoint edges covering all vertices.

**Minimum vertex-degree conjecture.** For each integer $r\geq 3$ there exists an integer $n_0=n_0(r)$ such that the following holds. Suppose that $H$ is an $r$-uniform hypergraph whose order $n\geq n_0$ is divisible by $r$. If

$$
\delta_1(H)>\binom{n-1}{r-1}-\binom{(r-1)n/r}{r-1},
$$
\nthen $H$ has a perfect matching.

The conjecture generalizes the paper's exact result for $3$-uniform hypergraphs and is motivated by the extremal construction in which the edges are precisely those meeting a smaller vertex class. Partial results were known from work of Hàn, Person and Schacht and of Markström and Ruciński; the general case is left open in the source.

319218 Completeness conjecture for honeycomb arrays
A **honeycomb array** is an arrangement of points on the triangular lattice satisfying the defining conditions described in the paper; two arrays are considered equivalent up to the symmetries of the hexagonal sphere. **Completeness conjecture.** The list of known honeycomb arrays is complete. Thus there are exactly $12$ honeycomb arrays, up to symmetry. Extensive searches found no additional examples with radius $r\leq 31$ having six-fold symmetry, nor among the constructions from Costas arrays checked for $r\leq 325$, but completeness beyond these searches remains open.

317860 The deck characterization of switching equivalence
Let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be graphs on $n$ vertices, and let $\mathcal{D}(X_i)$ denote the deck of vertex-deleted subgraphs of $X_i$. The decks are **switching equivalent** when there is a bijection between their cards that maps each card to a switching-equivalent graph. **The deck characterization conjecture.** The graphs $X_1$ and $X_2$ are switching equivalent if and only if

$$
\mathcal{D}(X_1) \sim \mathcal{D}(X_2).
$$

This is presented as an equivalent reformulation of the switching-equivalent reconstruction conjecture and is computationally verified in the supplied source for representatives with $4\leq n\leq 10$ vertices; the general statement is left open.

321211 A quadratic upper-growth conjecture for the van der Waerden numbers $w(2;3,t)$
Let $w(2;3,t)$ denote the least integer $N$ such that every 2-colouring of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length $3$ in one colour or length $t$ in the other. **Quadratic upper-growth conjecture.** There exists a constant $c>1$ such that

$$
w(2;3,t)\leqslant ct^2.
$$

The precise bound $w(2;r,t)\leqslant t^2$ has been invalidated in the paper, but the authors state that quadratic growth still seems appropriate; the conjecture remains open in the supplied text.

318385 Classification conjecture for regular graphs of complexity less than one
Let $G$ be a connected regular graph, and let $c(G)$ denote its complexity. Let $C_7$ be the cycle of length seven, and let $K_{n,n}$ and $K_{2n}$ denote the complete bipartite and complete graphs, respectively.

**Regular-graph complexity conjecture.** If $c(G)<1$, then $G$ is either isomorphic to $C_7$, or there is an integer $n\geq2$ such that $G$ is isomorphic to either $K_{n,n}$ or $K_{2n}$.

The conjecture proposes that, apart from these specified cases, connected regular graphs have complexity at least one; the source gives no resolution status.

316625 Stable-degree conjecture for permutation representations
Let $G$ be a finite group, let $\rho$ be a permutation representation of $G$, and let $P(\rho)$ be its permutation polytope. Set

$$
d:={\rm dim \,} P(\rho).
$$

Two permutation representations are stably equivalent when they differ by the stabilization relation used in the paper.

**Stable-degree conjecture.** There exists a stably equivalent permutation representation $\rho'\colon G\to S_n$ such that

$$
n\leq 2d.
$$

The statement was checked for $d\leq 4$ in the paper and would imply the weak part of the strong embedding conjecture. Its general validity remains open.

317862 Kerov's positivity conjecture for character polynomials
For each permutation $\pi$, let $K_{\pi}$ be the universal Kerov character polynomial defined by

$$
\Sigma^{\lambda}_{\pi}=K_{\pi}(R_2^{\lambda},R_3^{\lambda},\dots).
$$

For a cycle of length $k$, write $K_k$ for the corresponding polynomial. **Kerov's conjecture.** The coefficients of $K_k$, for $k\geq 1$, are non-negative integers.

This conjecture predicts a positivity property for the exact character formulas expressed through free cumulants. The source reports that it was formulated from numerical evidence; no resolution is supplied here.

377729 Symmetry-group conjecture for graphs of type $3_n$
Let $3_n(G)$ denote a graph of type $3_n$ with point group $G$, where the groups considered are $D_{2h}$, $D_{2d}$, and $D_2$. A graph is **tight** when it satisfies the source's tightness condition.

**Symmetry-group conjecture.** (i) $3_n(D_{2h})$ exists if and only if $\frac{n}{4}$ is even and $n\geq 16$; there are no tight $3_n(D_{2h})$. (ii) $3_n(D_{2d})$ exists if and only if either $\frac{n}{4}$ is odd and $n\geq 20$, or $\frac{n}{8}$ is even and $n\geq 24$; the graph defined in Theorem Possible-forms-for-3nS (iii) is the unique tight $3_n(D_{2d})$ graph if $\frac{n}{4}$ is odd and $n\geq 20$. (iii) $3_n(D_2)$ exists if and only if $n\geq 24$ and $n\ne 28,32$; tight $3_n(D_2)$ exists if and only if $\frac{n}{4}$ is odd, $n\geq 44$, and $n\ne 60,84$; there are $i$ tight $3_n(D_2)$ starting with $\frac{n}{4}=6i+5$ for $1\leq i\leq 9$, except the case $i=5$ starting with $\frac{n}{4}=37$.

The authors say that they checked this conjecture for $n\leq 500$; its general validity is not resolved in the supplied text.

376550 The complex-fugacity Glauber spectral-radius conjecture
Let $G$ be a finite graph of maximum degree $\Delta$, let $P$ be the Glauber transition matrix at complex fugacity $w$, and let $\operatorname{spr}(P)$ denote its spectral radius on the quotient by constant functions. **Complex-fugacity mixing conjecture.** There exists a complex domain $D_\Delta$ containing

$$
[0,1/(\Delta-1))
$$

such that $\operatorname{spr}(P)<1$ for every graph $G$ of maximum degree $\Delta$ whenever $w\in D_\Delta$. There are separate equal- and unequal-fugacity versions; the conjecture would provide a uniform complex-domain criterion for nonvanishing of $Z(w)$, but it remains open in the source.

320200 The good-vertex conjecture for the independent-set bound
For a graph $G$, let ${\rm ind}(G)$ be its number of independent sets, let $d(u)$ be the degree of $u$, let ${\rm iso}(G)$ be the number of isolated vertices, and define

$$
\Pi(G)=2^{{\rm iso}(G)}\prod_{uv\in E(G)}\left(2^{d(u)}+2^{d(v)}-1\right)^\frac{1}{d(u)d(v)}.
$$

A vertex $x\in V(G)$ is **good** for $G$ if

$$
\Pi(G)\geq \Pi(G-x)+\Pi(G-x-N(x)),
$$

where $N(x)$ is the set of neighbours of $x$. **Good-vertex conjecture.** For every graph $G$ with at least one vertex, there is a vertex $x\in V(G)$ that is good for $G$. This conjecture would imply Kahn's independent-set bound.
