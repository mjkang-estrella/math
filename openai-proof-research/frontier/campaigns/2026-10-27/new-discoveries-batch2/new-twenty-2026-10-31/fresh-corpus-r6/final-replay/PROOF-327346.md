## Witness and dimensions

Take p=4, q=7, e=22. Three left vertices join all seven right vertices; the fourth joins only the first right vertex. This is a connected, simple bipartite graph with no isolated vertices, equivalently K_{3,7} with one pendant left vertex. It satisfies the substantive hypotheses; no endpoint, empty-graph, or transcription loophole is used.

Any admissible `(s,t)` has `s≤4,t≤7,22≤st`. If s≤3, then st≤21, contradiction. Hence s=4. Then t≥6. The choice t=7 would require st-e=6≤min(4,7)=4, impossible, so t=6. The sole proposed graph deletes two edges incident with one vertex in the part of size six. A representative 4×6 incidence matrix has left row degrees (6,6,5,5), and its last column meets the first two left vertices.

## Lean semantics

`lean/SpectralData.lean` uses finite 0/1 incidence matrices to represent labeled simple bipartite graphs. `adjacency B` is the full symmetric block matrix `[0 B;Bᵀ 0]`. `rho B` is the supremum of the set of real adjacency eigenvalues, each defined by an actual nonzero eigenvector. `rho_of_positive` proves this supremum is an attained greatest eigenvalue in every application here. Thus there is no assumption that a Gram/quotient eigenvalue is maximal. `block_eigenvector` supplies the full adjacency bridge. `refined s t e` implements the required deletion at a vertex in the larger part, with a canonical choice of deleted neighbors; such choices differ only by relabeling. `Conjecture` quantifies arbitrary p,q,e and all 0/1 incidence matrices, with exact edge count and feasible deletion conditions. `counterexample : ¬ Conjecture` instantiates and refutes that statement. The extra `1<e<pq` guards do not weaken this witness.

This is a coordinate encoding, not a MathDB-provided formal definition or an independently reviewed equivalence theorem for an external graph API. The source's spectral radius is explicitly its largest adjacency eigenvalue, matching `rho` for these certified symmetric matrices.
