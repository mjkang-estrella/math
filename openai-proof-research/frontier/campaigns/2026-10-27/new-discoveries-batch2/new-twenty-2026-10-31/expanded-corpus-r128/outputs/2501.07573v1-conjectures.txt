1418: 8.2    A possible rowmotion map on rectangular tableaux
1419: Promotion and rowmotion are important maps on the set of order ideals of a poset [22]. In the
1420: special case of the type A root poset An−1 , its order ideals are in natural correspondence with
1421: the set Dn of Dyck paths, which in turn are in bijection with SYT(n2 ) via the map ∆ from
1422: equation (2). Under these bijections, promotion of order ideals of An−1 translates to promotion on
1423: SYT(n2 ), an extensively studied map defined not only on standard Young tableaux of any shape,
1424: including SYT(nk ), but on linear extensions of any poset [20]. On the other hand, finding a natural
1425:

1429: rowmotion operation on standard Young tableaux remains an open question in dynamical algebraic
1430: combinatorics.
1431:     To define a rowmotion operation on SYT(n2 ), one can simply translate rowmotion on order
1432: ideals of An−1 via the above bijections. This map, which we denote by row, has the property that
1433: Asc(T ) = HDes(row(T )) for all T ∈ SYT(n2 ). It can be shown that the bijection ρ : SYT(nk ) →
1434: SYT(nk ) described in Proposition 5.3 coincides with row when k = 2, and hence it is a potential
1435: candidate for a more general rowmotion operation on rectangular tableaux. This bijection satisfies
1436: asc(T ) = hdes(ρ(T )) for all T ∈ SYT(nk ), although, for k ≥ 3, it is not true that Asc(T ) =
1437: HDes(ρ(T )) in general. We propose to study the orbits that are obtained when iterating this
1438: map ρ.
1439:     The recursive bijection from Corollary 7.3 provides another candidate for a rowmotion operation
1440: on SYT(λ). This bijection has the advantage of being defined for all λ, and of satisfying Asc(T ) =
1441: HDes(f (T )) for all T ∈ SYT(λ). However, our definition is recursive, and it does not coincide with

1446: 8.3   Generalizations to other posets
1447: As discussed in Section 7, Theorems 2.2 and 2.4 provide direct bijections proving Theorem 7.1 in
1448: special cases. It is natural to ask whether our involution ψ can be generalized to other shapes λ
1449: for which the poset Pλ is graded, in order to give a direct bijective proof of the symmetry of the
1450: distribution of asc on SYT(λ). It would also be interesting to find a generalization of ψ to other
1451: graded posets, such as the product of three chains k × n × m.
1452:     It follows from Theorem 7.1 that the partitions λ for which the distribution of asc on SYT(λ)
1453: is symmetric are precisely those for which the poset Pλ is graded. One could ask if there is
1454: an analogous characterization of the partitions λ for which the distribution of des on SYT(λ) is
1455: symmetric. We know this is the case for rectangles λ = (nk ) (by Theorem 2.1), for self-conjugate
1456: shapes λ = λ′ (as discussed in Section 3.1), and for hooks λ = (n, 1k−1 ) (since all standard Young
1457: tableaux of this shape have exactly k descents). However, there are other shapes for which the
1458: distribution of des on SYT(λ) is symmetric as well, the smallest ones being λ = (4, 3, 1) and its
1459: conjugate.
