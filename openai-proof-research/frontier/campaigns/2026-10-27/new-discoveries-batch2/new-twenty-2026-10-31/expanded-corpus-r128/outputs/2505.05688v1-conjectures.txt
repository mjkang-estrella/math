691:    In this section, for dual simple 3–connected planar lattice graphs G and G ∗ , we define
692: another geometric invariant, which we call the right-angled volume vol⊥ (G). We prove

695:    An orthogonal circle pattern is a pair of circle packings whose contact graphs are planar
696: dual graphs, such that dual edges are perpendicular and no other edges intersect; i.e.,
697: the tangent points of the dual circle packings coincide, and the two tangent lines at
698: each point are perpendicular. The Koebe-Andreev-Thurston Circle Packing Theorem has
699: been generalized to orthogonal circle patterns (see [14] and references therein): Every 3–
700: connected plane graph admits an orthogonal circle pattern representation, which is unique
701: up to Möbius transformations.
702:    An orthogonally dual embedding of G and G ∗ is a planar embedding such that the
703: inscribed circles in their faces form an orthogonal circle pattern. We will say that G and
704: G ∗ are orthogonally dual lattice graphs, or that G and G∗ are orthogonally dual graphs
705: on the torus, if they admit an orthogonally dual embedding. Equivalently, the associated
706: Temperleyan graph Gb is a quadrangulation embedded such that its faces are right kites,
707: which are kites with at least two right angles, given by radii of the orthogonal circle
708: pattern. Many well-known planar lattice graphs satisfy orthogonal duality; see Figure 5.

710: orthogonal circle pattern defines a right-angled biperiodic ideal hyperbolic polyhedron P
711: in H3 whose vertices are the white vertices of G b . Let P (G) = P/Λ, which is a hyperbolic
