# Counterexample to the uniqueness assertion in Lindzey's Conjecture 16

Source: Nathan Lindzey, *An Eventown Result for Permutations*, arXiv:2601.12613v1, Section 6, Conjecture 16. The preceding prose says that Sylow 2-subgroups are **precisely** the extremal families. Distinct permutations are required in the pairwise condition, as in the source's p-regular Cayley graph definition. This is not an assertion about a permutation's sign. An even cycle means an orbit of positive even exact length.

The eight permutations below are in zero-based one-line notation on {0,1,2,3,4}:

```
01234  02143  03412  04321
01243  10234  34210  43201
```

Call this family F. Its elements are distinct; it contains the identity. Every quotient of distinct elements has an even cycle. `formal/Eventown.lean` checks this with kernel `decide`: for every pair, it finds a point and a cycle length between 1 and 5, checks return at that length and checks nonreturn at each positive smaller length. The lemma `bounded_cycle_sound` converts these certificates to the unbounded exact-orbit definition used in the conjecture.

F is not closed under multiplication. For example w1*w5 is [2,0,1,4,3], which is not listed. If a double-translate aPb of a subgroup contains 1, choose h0 in P with ah0b=1. Then b=(ah0)^(-1), and

(ah1b)(ah2b) = a(h1 h0^(-1) h2)b.

Thus it is closed under multiplication. F cannot be a double-translate of any subgroup, hence cannot be a double-translate of a Sylow subgroup.

Every Sylow 2-subgroup of S5 has size 2^v2(120)=8. Suppose the full source classification were true. Such a Sylow subgroup would then be a maximum even-cycle-intersecting family, so every intersecting family would have size at most 8. Since F has size 8, F would also be maximum, and the asserted classification would force F to be a Sylow double-translate, a contradiction.

This proves the negation of the universal classification, without needing an independent theorem computing the maximum at n=5. The Python exhaustive search additionally reports that the actual maximum at n=5 is 8; that report is a diagnostic, not a premise of the Lean proof. No claim is made to refute the weaker conjecture that a Sylow subgroup has maximum cardinality.
