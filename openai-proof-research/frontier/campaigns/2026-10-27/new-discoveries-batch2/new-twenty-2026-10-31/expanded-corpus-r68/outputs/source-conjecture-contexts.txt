305: Let  G  be a graph, and let  C_{1},\dots,C_{n}  be a sequence of circuits of  G  such that non-consecutive circuits are vertex-disjoint and consecutive circuits intersect in exactly one vertex. Then, a circuit chain of  G  is the union of  C_{1},\dots,C_{n} , where  n\geq 1 . Two edges of a circuit chain are consecutive if they are adjacent and belong to the same circuit  C_{i} . Given a set of vertex-disjoint circuit chains, two edges are consecutive if they are consecutive on one of the circuit chains in the set. A vertex of a circuit in a circuit chain is internal if it belongs only to that circuit of the chain. A circuit chain connects two vertices  u  and  v  of  G  if  u  and  v  are internal vertices of  C_{1}  and  C_{n} , respectively, or vice versa.

293: In 1999, Katona and Kierstead [19] proved that if an  n -vertex  k -graph  {\mathcal{H}}  satisfies  \delta_{k-1}({\mathcal{H}})\geq(1-\frac{1}{2k})n+4-k-\frac{5}{2k}  then  {\mathcal{H}}  contains a Hamilton  (k-1) -cycle (also called tight Hamilton cycle),
294: and further conjectured that the best possible bound should be
295:  \delta_{k-1}({\mathcal{H}})\geq\frac{n-k+3}{2} .
296: 
297: 
298: 
299: 
300: Major progress towards this conjecture was made by Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi, who [29, 30] proved an asymptotic version of this conjecture in [19] which asserted that for  k\geq 3,\gamma>0  and sufficiently large  n , every  k -graph  {\mathcal{H}}  on  n  vertices with  \delta_{k-1}({\mathcal{H}})\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\gamma)n  contains a tight Hamilton cycle.
301: For  k=3 , they [31] further improved the minimum co-degree condition to a tight one, e.g.  \delta_{2}({\mathcal{H}})\geq n/2 .
302: Later Markström and Ruciński [26] showed that this indeed provides asymptotically tight minimum co-degree conditions for all  \ell\in[k-1]  such that  (k-\ell)\mid k .
303: For other  \ell , that is, when  (k-\ell)\nmid k , this threshold can be lowered significantly.
304: Hàn and Schacht [14] proved that for  k\geq 3,\gamma>0  and  1\leq\ell<k/2 , every  n -vertex  k -graph  {\mathcal{H}}  with  \delta_{k-1}({\mathcal{H}})\geq(\frac{1}{2(k-\ell)}+\gamma)n  contains a Hamilton  \ell -cycle.
305: This full threshold was established by Kühn, Mycroft and Osthus [21], that is,
306: assuming minimum co-degree at least  \frac{n}{\lceil\tfrac{k}{k-\ell}\rceil(k-\ell)}+\gamma n  contains a Hamilton  \ell -cycle.

333: 1.3 Conjectured Gaussian limit
334: 
335: 
336: 
337: 
338: We make some general remarks about the conjectured large  n  behavior of  C_{n} . In Section 4.2 we continue the discussion in the light of our current results.
339: 
340: 
341: 
342: 
343: In the  n  by  n  complete bipartite graph  K_{n,n}  each vertex has  n  edges, of which exactly one will participate in the minimum cost perfect matching. We expect that most often the participating edge will be one of the cheaper. The cost of the very cheapest of the  n  edges at a given vertex is exponential of mean  1/n , and the  \zeta(2)  limit shows that the average cost of an edge in the minimum perfect matching is only about  1.64  times this minimum (what Talagrand called the “cost of monogamy” in [20]). Moreover, although not easy to justify even heuristically, it seems that the costs of matching vertices that are distant in the geometry induced by the edge costs are close to independent. We therefore expect  C_{n}  to behave qualitatively like a sum of  n  independent random variables with mean of order  1/n .

236:           We use the Vinogradov notation  f\gg g , equivalent to  g=O(f) , to denote the existence of some absolute constant  c>0  such that  \left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert\geqslant c\left\lvert g(x)\right\rvert  for all sufficiently large  x .  \|\widehat{1_{A}}\|_{1}\gg\log N  for any set  A  of  N  integers, which is achieved for example by an arithmetic progression of length  N . This was proved in 1981 independently by Konyagin [11] and McGehee, Pigno, and Smith [12]. A discussion of the problem and its history may be found in [3, Chapter 10].

241: This lower bound is universal, yet rarely achieved – in fact generically we expect  \|\widehat{1_{A}}\|_{1}  to be closer to the trivial upper bound  N^{1/2} , which is an immediate consequence of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Parseval’s identity. This is achieved when  A  is lacunary, for example, or a random positive density subset of an interval (with high probability). In fact, the only examples we know that achieve  O(\log N)  are arithmetic progressions and simple variations, for example the union of  O(1)  arithmetic progressions and some small unstructured set.

360: Most recently, S. Jung, I. Kim, M. Saito, and Y. Yoon defined Hodge ideal spectrum and Tjurina spectrum for isolated singularities (see [JKSY22]). Those new spectra come from Hodge ideals and are highly related to the original object. It is rather hard to describe Hodge ideals in a few lines. Roughly speaking, let  Z=\mathrm{div}(f)  be principal divisor defined by  f , then Hodge ideal  I_{k}(\alpha Z)  is an ideal of  \mathcal{O}_{n+1}  indexed by  k\in\mathbb{N}  and  \alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1] . Hodge ideals induce a filtration on  \mathcal{O}_{n+1}  by
361: 
362: 
363: 
364: 
365:  V_{HI}^{\beta}:=\sum_{\alpha+p\geq\beta}I_{p}(\alpha Z). 
366: 
367: 
368: 
369: Naturally, we have induced filtrations on Milnor algebra  \mathcal{O}_{n+1}/(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{0}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}})  and Tjurina algebra  \mathcal{O}_{n+1}/(f,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{0}},...,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}})  by canonical surjections. 277: Reinforcement learning (RL) is a subfield of machine learning (ML) that deals with developing models that automatically learn optimal decisions over time [33]. At a high level, an RL system comprises an agent and an environment, with the agent iteratively interacting with the environment by performing actions on it, and the environment providing feedback in return through observations and rewards. In an RL setting, the agent aims to discover a strategy, called the policy, which should maximize the long-term success of its actions with respect to the rewards returned by the environment. Since the agent learns purely by interacting with the environment without having any additional information on the problem being solved, RL is considered to be much more focused on goal-directed learning through interaction than other ML paradigms [50].

398: 1.1. Graph Reconstruction Conjecture
399: 
400: 
401: 
402: 
403: Let  G  be a finite simple undirected graph with vertex set  V(G)  of size  n .
404: For  v\in V(G)  we write  G-v  for the induced subgraph obtained by deleting  v  and all incident edges.
405: The deck of  G  is the multiset of vertex-deleted subgraphs
406: 
407: 
408: 
409: 
410:  \mathcal{D}(G)=\{G-v:v\in V(G)\}. 
411: 
412: 
413: 
414: Two graphs  G,H  on  n  vertices have the same deck if their decks agree as multisets of unlabeled graphs.
415: A graph  G  is reconstructible if every graph with the same deck is isomorphic to  G .
416: 
417: 
418: 
419: Conjecture 1 (Graph Reconstruction Conjecture (Ulam-Kelly) [4, 5]).
420: 
421: 
422: 
423: Every finite simple graph on at least three vertices is reconstructible from its deck.
424: 
425: 
426: 
427: 
428: 
429: The conjecture is classical and has generated a large literature; survey-level entry points include [6].
430: Many special families are known to be reconstructible (for example trees and regular graphs), but no general proof is known.
431: At the opposite extreme, calculations have verified reconstructibility for all graphs up to modest size; see McKay’s tabulations and notes [7]. The deck forgets the labeling of vertices, so the challenge is not merely to recover an adjacency relation but to recover it up to isomorphism from unlabeled local views.
432: 
433: 
434: 
435: 
436: The experiments in this paper focus on two regimes in which the deck retains nontrivial combinatorial rigidity while still admitting explicit computational checks. First, we study bipartite matrix reconstruction.
437: A bipartite graph  G=(U\sqcup V,E)  with  |U|=u  and  |V|=v  can be encoded by its  0-1  incidence matrix  A\in\{0,1\}^{u\times v} .

457: For a row  r , define the permutation  \rho_{r}\in S_{n}  in one-line notation by

570: 1.3. Rota Basis Conjecture
571: 
572: 
573: 
574: 
575: Let  M  be a matroid of rank  n  on ground set  E .
576: Let  B_{1},\dots,B_{n}  be pairwise disjoint bases of  M .
577: A transversal of  (B_{1},\dots,B_{n})  is a set  T\subseteq\bigcup_{i}B_{i}  that meets each  B_{i}  in exactly one element.
578: Equivalently, it is the image of a system of distinct representatives for the partition  (B_{1},\dots,B_{n}) .
579: 
580: 
581: 
582: Conjecture 3 (Rota Basis Conjecture (matroid form)).
583: 
584: 
585: 
586: Given  n  disjoint bases  B_{1},\dots,B_{n}  in a rank- n  matroid  M , there exist  n  pairwise disjoint transversals  T_{1},\dots,T_{n}  of  (B_{1},\dots,B_{n})  such that each  T_{j}  is a basis of  M .
587: 
588: 
589: 
590: 
591: 
592: In the linear case, one may take  M  to be the matroid of column-independence of vectors in an  n -dimensional vector space  V .
593: Then each  B_{i}  is a basis of  V , and a transversal basis is a rainbow basis containing exactly one vector from each  B_{i} .
594: The conjecture asserts that one can partition the multiset  \bigsqcup_{i}B_{i}  into  n  disjoint rainbow bases.
595: 
596: 
597: 
598: 
599: Rota formulated this conjecture in the late 1980s, and it has since become a central open problem in matroid theory.
600: Already in early work one sees strong links to determinant identities and to Latin-square phenomena; for example, Onn exhibited a colorful determinantal identity that ties the conjecture to Latin squares [20], and Huang-Rota connected the conjecture to Latin-square straightening coefficients [19].
605: 
606: 
607: A number of matroid classes are known to satisfy the conjecture or strong variants.
608: For example, Geelen and Humphries proved Rota’s conjecture for paving matroids [23], and Geelen-Webb obtained systematic packing and exchange results that guarantee many disjoint transversal bases under additional hypotheses [24].
609: In the linear setting, substantial quantitative progress has been made in recent years: Bucić, Kwan, Pokrovskiy, and Sudakov proved a result guaranteeing about  n/2  disjoint rainbow bases [25], Sauermann established the conjecture for random bases in a natural probabilistic model [27], and Pokrovskiy proved an asymptotic form of the conjecture [28].
610: Montgomery and Sauermann obtained asymptotically tight packing and covering bounds in related rainbow-base problems [29].
611: Algorithmic and computer-assisted approaches have also entered the picture; for example, Kirchweger, Scheucher, and Szeider used SAT-based methods to verify Rota-type statements in small ranks [26].
612: 
613: 
614: 
615: 
616: Our experiments focus on a small but instructive linear regime rank  \leq 13  over  \mathbb{F}_{2} . The goal is not to resolve the conjecture, but to use scored local-exchange search to find explicit policies that reliably repair invalid columns and reach a full set of transversal bases on adversarially structured test benchmarks.

1885: 5. Rota’s Basis Conjecture
1886: 
1887: 
1888: 
1889: 
1890: Rota’s Basis Conjecture (RBC) asks whether  n  disjoint bases of a rank- n  matroid can always be rearranged into an  n\times n  grid whose columns are also bases.
1891: The experiments in this part restrict to linear matroids over  \mathbb{F}_{2}  and aim to construct  n  disjoint transversal bases by a sequence of local moves.
1892: The evolvable block in these experiments is not a direct output construction but a policy for a greedy local-exchange engine: at each step, given the current partially filled grid, the policy scores all legal insert and repair moves and the engine executes a move of maximal score.
1893: 
1894: 
1895: 
1896: 
1897: The evaluation harness converts the qualitative goal ”find a full transversal-basis grid” into a scalar fitness that both rewards success and shapes the intermediate dynamics.
1898: For a rollout producing a trajectory  (A_{t})_{t=0}^{T}  of assignment matrices, the primary term is the success indicator  \mathbf{1}[\text{all columns are full bases at termination}] .
1899: To avoid sparse all-or-nothing feedback, the harness also tracks progress and damage: it rewards completing full columns, penalizes breaking a column that was already full, penalizes the use of explicit repair moves, and grants partial credit for the number of valid (independent) columns at the end of the rollout.
1900: A small time penalty proportional to the number of steps discourages policies that solve instances only by wandering for long trajectories.
1901: The concrete fitness scalar in the experiments below has the form displayed in Experiment A, with fixed weights chosen so that success dominates all secondary terms.

194: In this paper we prove Bialy’s conjecture, which states that if two ellipses  \mathscr{E}  and  \mathscr{E}^{\prime}  satisfy
195:  \beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{0})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho_{0})  and  \beta_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{1})=\beta_{\mathscr{E}^{\prime}}(\rho_{1}) 
196: for two distinct rotation numbers  \rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in(0,1/2] , then they must coincide up to isometries.
197: We also prove that the same conclusion holds when only one rotation number is prescribed, provided the two ellipses have the same perimeter.
198: Finally, we discuss consequences for local extremizers of Mather’s beta function, building on a recent result of Baranzini, Bialy, and Sorrentino.
