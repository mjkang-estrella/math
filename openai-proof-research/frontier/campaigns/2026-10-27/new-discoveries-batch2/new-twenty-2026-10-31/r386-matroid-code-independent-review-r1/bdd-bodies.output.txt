298: 2.2    BDDs and ZDDs
299: Binary decision diagrams (BDDs) [8] and zero-suppressed binary decision diagrams (ZDDs) [19]
300: are directed acyclic graphs under specific conditions and represent Boolean functions and set
301: families, respectively. Note that a Boolean function can be viewed as a set family if the set of
302: true variables in an assignment of input variables for which the function evaluates to true belongs
303: to the family.
304:     Let E be a finite set,  be a total order on E, and S ⊆ 2E be a subset family of E. The
305: BDD B(S, (E, )) and ZDD Z(S, (E, )) for S with respect to (E, ) are defined as follows.
306: Given the family S and the totally ordered set (E, ), a binary decision tree corresponding to
307: them is uniquely determined. For example, when S is {∅, {1}, {1, 2}, {1, 2, 3}} and (E, ) is
308: ({1, 2, 3}, ≤), Figure 2.1 is the corresponding binary decision tree. The directed acyclic graph
309: obtained by applying the following reduction rules to the binary decision tree is a BDD or ZDD.
310: The applied rules differentiate BDDs and ZDDs.
311:     As a common condition, the directed acyclic graph has at most two nodes with outdegree
312: zero, called terminals. One of two terminals is called the 0-terminal ⊥ and the other the 1-
313: terminal ⊤. Each non-terminal node ν has a label label(ν) representing an element of E and
314: exactly two arcs whose tails are ν. These arcs are called the 0-arc and 1-arc, and their heads
315: are denoted by ν0 and ν1 , respectively. Since there is a unique 1-arc (resp. 0-arc) whose tail is
316: the same as a given 0-arc (resp. 1-arc), in this paper, it is referred to as the corresponding 1-arc
317: (resp. 0-arc). The size of a BDD/ZDD is the number of non-terminal nodes in it.
318:     The reduction rules are as follows. (NS) is the common rule as shown in Figure 2.2(a):
319: (NS) Non-terminal nodes ν, ν ′ whose labels are the same and which satisfy both ν0 = ν0′ and
320:      ν1 = ν1′ must be merged into a single node.
321: Additionally, BDDs (resp. ZDDs) must satisfy the following rule (B-ND) (resp. (Z-ND)) as
322: illustrated in Figure 2.2(b) (resp. Figure 2.2(c)):
323: (B-ND) A non-terminal node ν with ν0 = ν1 must be removed, and the arcs whose head is ν
324:        must be redirected to ν0 (equal to ν1 ).
325: (Z-ND) A non-terminal node ν with ν1 = ⊥ must be removed, and the arcs whose head is ν
326:        must be redirected to ν0 .

328: Removed nodes are referred to as redundant nodes. The resulting BDD/ZDD is uniquely de-
329: termined regardless of the order in which these rules are applied1 , though a BDD and ZDD

340:   Since we would like to consider natural classes of matroids that have
341: unbounded branch-width, we are motivated to make the next definition.
342: Definition 2.9. Let M be a class of matroids. Then M is pigeonhole if,
343: for every positive integer, λ, there is an integer ρ(λ) such that bw(M ) ≤ λ
344: implies dw(M ) ≤ ρ(λ), for every M ∈ M.
345:    Thus a class of matroids is pigeonhole if every subclass with bounded
346: branch-width also has bounded decomposition-width. The class of F-repre-
347: sentable matroids is pigeonhole when F is a finite field [10, Theorem 5.1].
348: Note that the class of F-representable matroids certainly has unbounded
349: decomposition-width, since it has unbounded branch-width. Some natural
350: classes possess a stronger property than the pigeonhole property:
351: Definition 2.10. Let M be a class of matroids. Assume that for every
352: positive integer λ, there is a positive integer π(λ), such that whenever M ∈
353: M and U ⊆ E(M ) satisfies λM (U ) ≤ λ, there are at most π(λ) equivalence
354: classes under ∼U . In this case we say that M is strongly pigeonhole.
355: Proposition 2.11. If a class of matroids is strongly pigeonhole, then it is
356: pigeonhole.
357: Proof. Let M be a strongly pigeonhole class, and let π be the function from
358: Definition 2.10. We may as well assume that π is non-decreasing. Let λ be
359: any positive integer, and let M be a matroid in M with branch-width at
360: most λ. Let (T, ϕ) be a decomposition of M such that λM (U )+1 ≤ λ for any
361: set U displayed by an edge of T . Then there are at most π(λ−1)-equivalence
362: classes under ∼U . Thus (T, ϕ) demonstrates that dw(M ) ≤ π(λ − 1). So
363: bw(M ) ≤ λ implies dw(M ) ≤ π(λ − 1) for each M ∈ M, and the result
364: follows.                                                                  
