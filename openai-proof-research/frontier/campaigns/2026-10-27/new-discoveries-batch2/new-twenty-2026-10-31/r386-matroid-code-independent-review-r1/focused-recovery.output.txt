632: 4.1     Upper bounds on widths depending only on connectivity functions
633: A trivial upper bound for the number of minors on E \E,i is 2i . In this section, we provide more
634: precise upper bounds in terms of connectivity functions of matroids by proving Theorem 1.4.
635: Recall from Section 1 that the connectivity function λM : 2E → N of a matroid M is defined by
636: λM (X) := r M (X) + r M (E \ X) − r (M ) for X ⊆ E. We write λM as λ if M is clear from the
637: context. This section finds upper bounds on the number of minors that depends only on λ(E,i ).

639: 4.1.1    Related work
640: Sekine et al. [26] provided an upper bound for cycle matroids. In the BDD for the family of
641: spanning trees for a simple connected undirected graph G with the edge set E, each node with
642: label ei corresponds to a certain minor of G on E \ E,i . Moreover, limited 2-isomorphic minors
643: correspond to the same node. Thus the ith width of the BDD for all spanning trees of G is equal
644: to the number of minors of G on E \ E,i . This result can be interpreted regarding to BDDs for
645: the collection of bases of the cycle matroid M (G). Eventually, the number of minors of M (G) on
646: E \ E,i is bounded by the (V (G[E,i ]) ∩ V (G[E \ E,i ]))-th Bell number, where G[X] denotes
647: the induced subgraph of G on X ⊆ E.
648:     The class of matroids known as the strongly pigeonhole class is closely related to this study.
649: For a matroid M and S ⊆ E(M ), define the equivalence relation ∼M,S on 2S as follows. For
650: X1 , X2 ⊆ S, the sets X1 and X2 are equivalent, written X1 ∼M,S X2 , if and only if the indepen-
651: dence of X1 ∪ Y and X2 ∪ Y are equivalent in M for all Y ⊆ E(M ) \ S. A class M of matroids
652: is called strongly pigeonhole [13, Definition 2.10] if there is a function f : N \ {0} → N \ {0} such
653: that, for any matroid M ∈ M, positive integer λ′ , and S ⊆ E(M ) with λM (S) ≤ λ′ , the size
654: of the quotient set by ∼M,S is always at most f (λ′ ). Thus, if a class M of matroids is strongly
655: pigeonhole, then the width of BDDs/ZDDs for a matroid M ∈ M can be bounded by a value
656: depending only on the connectivity function of M . Funk et al. [14, Proposition 10 and Theorems
657: 18, 22, and 34] showed that several classes of matroids are strongly pigeonhole such as funda-
658: mental transversal matroids, 3-connected bicircular matroids, and 3-connected H-gain-graphic
659: matroids for a finite group H.
