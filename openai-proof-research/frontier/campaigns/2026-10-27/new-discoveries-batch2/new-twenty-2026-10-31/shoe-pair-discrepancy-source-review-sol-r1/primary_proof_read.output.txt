932:    Example 2.1. (Birthday Problem) The classical birthday problem asks
933: for the minimum number of people in a room such that two of them have the
934: same birthday with probability at least 50%. It is well known that the min-
935: imum number people for which this holds is approximately 23. In fact, the
936: expected number of samples, chosen uniformly with replacement, p         required
937: from a set of size N until some value is repeated, is asymptotically πN/2.
938: A generalization of this considers birthday coincidences among individuals of
939: different types, that is, in a room with equal numbers of boys and girls, when
940: can one expect a boy and girl to share the same birthday. Finding matches
941: among different types can also be stated in terms of sampling colored balls
942: and placing them in urns: Suppose there are N urns and two colors, and
943: the balls are colored independently with probability 1/2 and placed in the
944: urns uniformly with probability 1/N . The number of draws needed to have 2
945: balls with different colors in the same urn is the first time when a boy and a
946: girl share the same birthday, when boys and girls sequentially enter a room
947: independently with probability 1/2. In this case, Corollary 2.1 for q = 2
948: shows that the limiting distribution
949:                                  p      of the first collision time is a Rayleigh
950: distribution with parameter
951:                        √          1/2. This implies that the expected time of
952: the first collision is πN . When N = 365 and the birthdays are assumed
953: to be uniformly distributed over the year, the expected time before there is
954: a boy and a girl with the same birthday is 34. For a detailed discussion on
955: the birthday problem and its various generalizations and applications, refer
