12:                                                         Consider an urn model where at each step one of q colors is sam-
13:                                                     pled according to some probability distribution and a ball of that
14:                                                     color is placed in an urn. The distribution of assigning balls to urns
15:                                                     may depend on the color of the ball. Collisions occur when a ball
16:                                                     is placed in an urn which already contains a ball of different color.
17:                                                     Equivalently, this can be viewed as sequentially coloring a complete
18:                                                     q-partite graph wherein a collision corresponds to the appearance of
19:                                                     a monochromatic edge. Using a Poisson embedding technique, the
20:                                                     limiting distribution of the first collision time is determined and the
21:                                                     possible limits are explicitly described. Joint distribution of succes-
22:                                                     sive collision times and multi-fold collision times are also derived.
23:                                                     The results can be used to obtain the limiting distributions of run-
24:                                                     ning times in various birthday problem based algorithms for solving
25:                                                     the discrete logarithm problem, generalizing previous results which
26:                                                     only consider expected running times. Asymptotic distributions of
27:                                                     the time of appearance of a monochromatic edge are also obtained
28:                                                     for other graphs.

32:                                           with |V | = N , are colored independently and uniformly at random with c
33:                                           colors. The probability that the resulting coloring has no monochromatic
34:                                           edge, that is, it is a proper coloring is χG (c)/cN , where χG (c) denotes the
35:                                           number of proper colorings of G using c-colors. The function χG is the
36:                                           chromatic polynomial of G, which is a central object in graph theory [18,
37:                                           30]. A natural extension is to consider a general coloring distribution p =
38:                                           (p1 , p2 , . . . , pc ), where the probability that a vertex is colored with color a ∈
39:                                           [c] isPpa which is independent of the colors of the other vertices, where pa ≥ 0,
40:                                           and ca=1 pa = 1. Then the probability that G is properly colored is related
41:                                           to Stanley’s generalized chromatic polynomial [19, 43]. Limit theorems for
42:                                           the number of monochromatic edges under the uniform coloring distribution,
43:                                           that is, pa = 1/c for all a ∈ [c], was derived recently by Bhattacharya et al.

53: well-known birthday problem: by replacing the colors by birthdays, occurring
54: with possibly non-uniform probabilities, the birthday problem can be seen as
55: coloring the vertices of a complete graph independently with c = 365 colors.

111:     1.1. Collision Times in a Multicolor Urn Model. A natural generaliza-
112: tion of the birthday problem is to consider coincidences among individuals
113: of different types, that is, in a room occupied with an equal number of boys
114: and girls, when can one expect a boy and girl to share the same birthday.
115: This can be viewed as an urn model with two colors, where balls are colored
116: independently with probability 1/2 and placed in the urns uniformly. The
117: event of having a matching birthday is same as having an urn with balls
118: of both the colors. This event is often referred to as a collision. For exact
119: expressions of the number of collisions, factorial moments and other related
120: problems, refer to Nakata [37] and the references therein. The number of col-
121: lisions between two discrete distributions was also used by Batu et al. [7] for
122: distributional property testing. Wendl [44] studied a very related problem
123: and referred to some applications in collisions of airborne planes, celestial
124: objects, and transportations.
125:     In this paper we consider the sequential version of this problem, for general
126: urn selection distributions.
