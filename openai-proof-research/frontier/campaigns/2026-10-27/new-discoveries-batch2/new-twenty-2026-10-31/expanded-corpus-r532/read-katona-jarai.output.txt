2     Preliminaries
   A family of k internally disjoint (v, Y )-paths whose terminal vertices are distinct in
Y is referred to as a k-fan from v to Y .

Lemma 2.1. (Bondy and Murty [3]) Let G be a k-connected graph, let v be a vertex
of G and Y ⊆ V (G) \ {v} be a set of at least k vertices of G. Then there exists a k-fan
in G from v to Y .

Lemma 2.2. (Erdős and Gallai [5]) Let G be a 2-connected graph and x, y be two
given vertices. If every vertex other than x, y has degree at least k in G, then there is
an (x, y)-path of length at least k.

    For a vertex v ∈ V (G) and a subgraph H ⊆ G, let G − H denote the subgraph
induced by V (G) \ V (H), and NH (v) denote the set of vertices in H which are adjacent
to v and dH (v) = |NH (v)|. We simply write N (v) = NG (v) and use δ(G) to denote
the minimum degree of G. Let p(G) be the order of a longest path in G and P is
hamiltonian if |P | = |G|. We call P a strong dominating path if N (v) ⊆ V (P ) for any
v ∈ V (G − P ) and a strong dominating cycle is defined similarly.
    Let σ3 (G) = max{d(u) + d(v) + d(w) | {u, v, w} is an independent set in G}.

Lemma 2.3. (Saito [11]) Suppose G is a 2-connected graph of order n. Then either
G contains a strong dominating cycle or p(G) ≥ min{n, σ3 (G) − 1}.

1.2   Main results
Theorems 1 and 2 below state our main results. Figure 1 illustrates these when G is a path of
length three, that is k = 4.
Theorem 1. Let G be a finite connected simple graph with |E| ≥ 2.
(i) If x ∈ IG , and n = nx + O(1), then pG (n) → 0 exponentially fast, as n → ∞, at a rate
depending on x. The rate of decay is bounded away from 0 on subsets bounded away from RG .
(ii) There exists a constant cG > 0, such that if x ∈ RG , and n = nx + O(1), then pG (n) → cG ,
as n → ∞.
