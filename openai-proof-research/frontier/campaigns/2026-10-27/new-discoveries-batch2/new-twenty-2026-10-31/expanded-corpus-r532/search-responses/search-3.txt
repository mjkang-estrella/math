The search turned up consistent support for the asymptotic result, mostly from survey papers and one arXiv paper.

- **The result itself:** A 2026 arXiv survey says Gargano, Körner, and Vaccaro showed that the ratio of CAN(2, k, v) to log k approaches v/2. It frames this as a solution to Rényi's 1971 problem on the largest family of 2-independent k-partitions of an n-set. In [34] Gargano et al. determine the asymptotics of the largest size of a family of 2-independent k-partitions of an n-set, which at the time was a longstanding problem in combinatorics posed in its generality by Rényi in 1971. In the context of covering arrays, their result implies that the ratio of CAN(2, k, v) to log(k) is asymptotic to v/2.
- **Logarithm base:** Another arXiv paper (1503.08876) uses base-2 logarithms and states the theorem as d(2, v) = v/2, where d is the limsup of CAN(t,k,v)/log₂k. It also notes that the exact value of d(t, v) is only known when t = 2.
- **Equivalent terminology:** Covering arrays of strength t are equivalent to t-qualitatively independent families of partitions. One source defines this so that for any t of the partitions, choosing one class from each always gives a nonempty intersection. Another notes that covering arrays have been studied using different nomenclature, as qualitatively independent partitions [13], t-surjective arrays [5].
- **Constructions and the caveat:** The 1503.08876 paper also notes that matching constructions are hard to find. The only family which we currently know how to construct which attains this size is the already mentioned family of CAs with t=2 and v=2. So the v/2 constant is established as a limit, but optimal arrays for v > 2 are not known in general.
- **Related general bound:** For general t, a known asymptotic upper bound is of order (t−1)v^t log k, which the same paper describes as not too far from being asymptotically optimal.
