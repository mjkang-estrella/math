A Note on the Complexity of Directed Clique
Grzegorz Gutowski #
Institute of Theoretical Computer Science, Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science,
Jagiellonian University, Kraków, Poland
Mikołaj Rams #
Institute of Theoretical Computer Science, Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science,
Jagiellonian University, Kraków, Poland

      Abstract
For a directed graph G, and a linear order ≪ on the vertices of G, we define backedge graph G≪ to
be the undirected graph on the same vertex set with edge {u, w} in G≪ if and only if (u, w) is an
arc in G and w ≪ u. The directed clique number of a directed graph G is defined as the minimum
size of the maximum clique in the backedge graph G≪ taken over all linear orders ≪ on the vertices
of G. A natural computational problem is to decide for a given directed graph G and a positive
integer t, if the directed clique number of G is at most t. This problem has polynomial algorithm
for t = 1 and is known to be NP-complete for every fixed t ≥ 3, even for tournaments. In this note
we prove that this problem is ΣP2 -complete when t is given on the input.


Keywords and phrases Directed Clique, Computational Complexity, Polynomial Hierarchy

 1      Introduction
All graphs discussed in this paper are finite, simple, and loopless. For directed graphs we
allow for parallel arcs in opposite directions. We do not allow for parallel arcs in the same
direction. The vertex set and the edge set of an undirected graph G are denoted by V (G)
and E(G). The vertex set and the arc set of a directed graph G are denoted by V (G)
and A(G). For a subset U ⊆ V (G), G[U ] denotes the subgraph of G induced by U . A
directed graph T is a tournament when for each pair u, w ∈ V (G) of vertices, exactly one of
the (u, w) or (w, u) is an arc in A(G). For a directed graph G, and a linear order ≪ on the
vertex set V (G) we define the set of backedges to be:

     BE(G, ≪) = {{u, w} : (u, w) ∈ A(G) ∧ w ≪ u} .

We define the backedge graph G≪ to be the undirected graph with vertex set V (G) and edge
set BE(G, ≪). We say that a directed graph G is transitive if it does not contain a directed
cycle. The transitive tournament T Tn with n vertices is the only tournament with n vertices
that is transitive.
    The complete directed graph Qn with n vertices is the directed graph with n vertices    and
all possible n(n − 1) arcs. An undirected graph with t vertices and all possible 2t edges is

        Other graph parameters can also be defined for directed graphs using the backedge graph
    similarly as for the definition of the directed clique number. See the discussion of other
    intersting parameters in a paper on degree-width of directed graphs by Aboulker, Oijid, Petit,
    Rocton and Simon [2]. The classical notion of dichromatic number, defined first by Neumann-
    Lara [6] can equivalently be defined for a directed graph G as the minimum chromatic
    number of G≪ taken over all linear orders ≪ on V (G). Therefore, out of these similarly
    defined parameters, the dichromatic number attracts the most attention. Not surprisingly,
    the dichromatic number and the directed clique number are related, see [7, 1, 4, 2].
        Nguyen, Seymour and Scott [7] asked if TournamentClique is in P or co-NP. We
    are unable to answer this questions, but we provide some strong evidence that the answer
    should be negative. We do that by settling the complexity of the more general problem on
    directed graphs. Observe that the problem DirectedClique on instance ⟨G, t⟩ is naturally
    expressed as:

introduction, ΣP2 is defined as NPNP – a class of languages decidable in polynomial time by

to test any language in NP in a single step of execution. The canonical complete problem for
ΣP2 is the following.

 Problem: Existential-2-Level-SAT
 Input:    Boolean formula φ(x1 , . . . , xa , y1 , . . . , yb ) with variables in two disjoint sets
           {x1 , . . . , xa } and {y1 , . . . , yb }
 Question: Yes if and only if the following Boolean formula is true.
           ∃x1 ,x2 ,...,xa : ∀y1 ,y2 ,...,yb : φ(x1 , . . . , xa , y1 , . . . , yb )

   It was independently proved by Stockmeyer [11] and Wrathall [12] that the class ΣP2 is
exactly the class of languages reducible to Existential-2-Level-SAT via polynomial-time
many-one reductions. This fact makes ΣP2 a natural complexity class for DirectedClique
and TournamentClique problems. We make the following conjecture that would settle
the complexity of both problems.

▶ Conjecture 1. TournamentClique is ΣP2 -complete.
