162: Remarks.
163:   1. In the study of s-core partitions, s is typically assumed to be greater than 1. However,
164:      one can meaningfully consider the cases s “ 0 and s “ 1. First take s “ 1; according
165:      to the definition using hooks, the only 1-core partition is ∅. However, the partition p1q
166:      is the unique partition with its 1-content (which is why we need to assume s ‰ 1 in
167:      Theorem 2.1). Nevertheless, most of the theory of core partitions applies (in a trivial
168:      way) with s “ 1, if we take C1 “ t∅u. Similarly for multipartitions, we take Cp1 | cq “ t∅l u
169:      (which is consistent with the definition of core multipartitions given above provided
170:      l ⩾ 2), and the results we prove below will apply in this case.
171:       Now consider the case s “ 0. In this case we should regard the residue of a node pa, bq as
172:       the integer b ´ a, and correspondingly define the 0-content to be a multiset of integers.
173:       It is then not hard to prove that any partition is determined by its 0-content, so every
174:       partition is a 0-core.
175:       The situation with multipartitions is less straightforward when s “ 0. Given c P Zl
176:       we define the p0 | cq-residue of the node pa, b, kq to be the integer b ´ a ` ck , so that the
177:       p0 | cq-content is again a multiset of integers. But now not every multipartition is a p0 | cq-
178:       core. In fact this is easily seen: if c j “ ck for some j ‰ k, then a multipartition λ has the
179:       same p0 | cq-content as the multipartition obtained by switching the components λpjq and
180:       λpkq , so cannot be a p0 | cq-core if these components are unequal. So the study of p0 | cq-
181:       core multipartitions is certainly non-trivial, and we will include the case s “ 0 in our
182:       considerations in this paper. Given two integers a, b, the condition a ” b pmod sq should
183:       be read as a “ b in the case s “ 0.
184:       The case s “ 0 can be regarded as the limiting case as s gets very large (in fact, this
185:       situation is often described as s “ 8 rather than s “ 0): given a multipartition λ and
186:       c P Zl , it is easily seen that we have λ P Cp0 | cq if and only if λ P Cps | cq for all sufficiently
187:       large s.
188:     2. Our definition of core multipartitions is not completely arbitrary, but has representation-
189:        theoretic significance. Associated to a pair ps | cq as above and a positive integer n is an
190:        Ariki–Koike algebra (a Hecke algebra of the complex reflection group of type Gpl, 1, nq).
191:        This algebra has an important family of modules (the Specht modules) labelled by l-multi-
192:        partitions of size n. Lyle & Mathas [LM] showed that two multipartitions having the
193:        same ps | cq-content is equivalent to the corresponding Specht modules lying in the same
194:        block of the Ariki–Koike algebra, and the author [F1] showed that a multipartition being
195:        an ps | cq-core is equivalent to the corresponding Specht module being contained in a sim-
196:        ple block. This is analogous to the significance of s-cores in the s-modular representation
197:        theory of the symmetric group (or more generally the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of type A).
198:        In fact, this analogy goes further: in [F1] the author defines a “weight” function on mul-
199:        tipartitions (depending on s, c) which is an analogue of the s-weight of a partition λ (i.e.
200:        the number of rim s-hooks that need to be removed to reach the s-core of λ). ps | cq-cores
201:        are then simply multipartitions of weight 0. We will use some of the results from [F1]
202:        below.
