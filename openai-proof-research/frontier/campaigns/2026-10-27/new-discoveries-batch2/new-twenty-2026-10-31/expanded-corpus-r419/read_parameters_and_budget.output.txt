46: maximizing closeness centralization in two-mode data, where the number of data of
47: each type is fixed. Intuitively, our result states that among all networks obtainable
48: via two-mode data, the largest closeness is achieved by simply locally maximizing
49: the closeness of a node. Mathematically, our study concerns bipartite graphs with
50: fixed size bipartitions, and we show that the extremal configuration is a rooted tree
51: of depth 2, where neighbors of the root have an equal or almost equal number of
59: persons and edges represent the relationships between pairs of individuals. Our work
60: focuses on simple unweighted graphs: our graph only tells us, for a given (binary)
61: relation R, which pairs of individual are in relation according to R.
62:     Centrality is a crucial concept in studying social networks [8, 12]. It can be seen
63: as a measure of how central is the position of an individual in a social network.
64: Various node-based measures of the centrality have been proposed to determine the
65: relative importance of a node within a graph (the reader is referred to the work
66: of Koschützki et al. [9] for an overview). Some widely used centrality measures are
67: the degree centrality, the betweenness centrality, the closeness centrality and the
68: eigenvector centrality (definitions and extended discussions are found in the book
69: edited by Brandes and Erlebach [5]).
