H is a depth-two tree rooted in P0, adjacent to every P1 vertex, with their numbers of P0 children differing by at most one. Ce is called eigenvalue centrality at Conjecture7, whereas the motivating prose calls it eigenvector centralization. No normalization for a Perron eigenvector is supplied in these read passages. An arbitrary Perron vector scale makes intergraph maximization meaningless. The literal MathDB scope includes disconnected bipartite graphs, where selecting a Perron vector can itself be nonunique. Neither defect licenses a credited endpoint correction.

A full-scope optimization route would first fix the intended normalization and whether Ce(v,G) is the coordinate itself or sum over P0 of differences. For a bipartite adjacency block B, the P0 coordinates satisfy BB^T x=rho^2 x. In H, letting y_j be the P1 coordinate and m_j its leaf count, the leaf coordinates are y_j/rho, and the P1 equation gives (rho^2-m_j)y_j=rho*x_root. Together with rho*x_root=sum_j y_j, this gives sum_j 1/(rho^2-m_j)=1, provided root is positive. This exact scalar reduction is valid for connected H; it is not an inequality for arbitrary B. To complete a proof one needs a sharp comparison of the intended normalized objective for arbitrary connected B against this balanced configuration, or a connected graph witness beating it. Simple edge addition increases rho but need not increase a normalized coordinate or centralization, so Perron monotonicity alone does not close the route.
