The source defines Delta(s;z)=(z/s,(z+1)/s,...,(z+s-1)/s), and a hypergeometric coefficient as the product of the rising factorials of all numerator parameters divided by j! (there are no denominator parameters here). The printed conjecture concatenates k such blocks but labels its hypergeometric function _{r+1}F_0. For k>1 it therefore supplies k(r+1) numerator entries to an (r+1)-entry function. That is a real syntax defect. We distinguish the undefined strict-arity reading from the natural concatenated-list reading, repairing only the subscript to k(r+1). We do not claim a proof of a proposition with undefined notation.

All-odd-r refutation under the concatenated-list reading. Let r>=1 be odd, s=r+1, k=s, and every part have size one. This is the connected simple graph K_s. Every block Delta(s;-1) contains zero (the entry indexed by1). Thus every positive-index hypergeometric coefficient is zero, by (0)_j=0 for j>=1. The displayed right side is x^s. In K_s a path of length r is a spanning path. Any ordering of the s distinct vertices gives one, and the only two orderings giving the same undirected path are its two orientations: from either endpoint the next vertex is determined successively. There are s!/2 such paths. No two can be vertex-disjoint. The defining higher matching polynomial is therefore x^s-s!/2. Since s>=2, this differs from x^s. This is a full family for arbitrarily large odd r, not only r=1 or a computed finite search. At r=1 the concrete witness K2 already has x^2-1 rather than x^2.

Positive parts larger than one also expose the issue: for r=1, n1=n2=n3=2, each Delta(2;-2)=(-1,-1/2), so its product is1/2. The displayed argument is -1/x^2. The coefficient of x^(N-2) predicted by all six numerator entries is -1/8. In K_{2,2,2} the same coefficient is minus the number of edges, namely -(2*2+2*2+2*2)=-12. Changing the hypergeometric arity alone does not repair this. This is an exact hand calculation, not a numerical experiment. No factorial/path source theorem is needed beyond the source definition and the elementary counting proof above.

Beyond the shortcut, a correct full-scope combinatorial representation. For positive part sizes n1,...,nk and path length r>=1, let s=r+1. Let W_s be the finite set of color words of length s with adjacent letters different. For an ordered j-tuple of such words let d_i be the total number of occurrences of color i. Write (n)_down_d for the number of injections from d labeled slots into n vertices, zero if d>n. Then
p_r(K_(n),j) = 1/(2^j j!) * sum_{(w1,...,wj) in W_s^j} product_i (n_i)_down_{d_i}.
Proof: fill the labeled slots of the ordered oriented paths injectively within each color. Adjacent colors differ exactly when successive vertices are adjacent in the complete multipartite graph. Global injectivity makes all paths simple and mutually disjoint. Conversely an ordered orientation of any packing determines exactly one such word tuple and slot filling. Forgetting the j path order and j orientations has exactly 2^j j! preimages because r>=1, all vertices are distinct, and different paths have disjoint vertex sets. Thus the formula follows, with zero terms handled by the injection convention. This supplies all coefficients, all r and all part sizes, without asserting that this repackaging is a novel result.

At r=1 this specializes to a useful alternative expression: sum over nonnegative integers a_ih (i<h), sum a_ih=j, d_i=sum_{h!=i}a_min(i,h),max(i,h), of product_i(n_i)_down_{d_i}/product_{i<h}a_ih!. For each part allocate its incident vertices to partner parts, then pair the allocated endpoints for each part pair. The allocation factorials give two copies of a_ih!, and the bijections restore one, leaving the displayed denominator. This confirms that a single product over all parts cannot count general pairwise part interactions merely by adjusting the arity.
