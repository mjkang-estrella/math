# 333995: exact weighted-path conditioning route; interpretation HOLD

This is a complete ordinary proof for trace-preserving condition-number minimization on positive weighted graphs. It is NOT yet an unconditional resolution of the source's ambiguously specified GraphCondition operation. No mathematical program, eigenvalue computation software, or formal checker was used.

Let P4 have successive edge conductances a,b,c. Its Laplacian is the sum of w_e(e_u-e_v)(e_u-e_v)^T. Restrict to the zero-sum subspace. Define condition number as largest divided by smallest eigenvalue there. A common scale does not affect condition number. At fixed total conductance, equivalently fixed Laplacian trace, consider an optimal reweighting on the same support.

For a=c, reflection decomposes the space. On reflection-symmetric vectors (x,y,y,x) with x+y=0 the nonzero eigenvalue is 2a. On reflection-antisymmetric vectors (x,y,-y,-x) the matrix is [[a,-a],[-a,a+2b]], with eigenvalues a+b +/- sqrt(a^2+b^2). Its characteristic polynomial follows by expanding (a-t)(a+2b-t)-a^2. Both are positive for a,b>0; 2a lies between them because sqrt(a^2+b^2)>=|a-b|. Hence the condition number is (a+b+d)/(a+b-d), d=sqrt(a^2+b^2). The inequality 2(a^2+b^2)>=(a+b)^2 gives d/(a+b)>=1/sqrt(2), with equality iff a=b. Thus among symmetric paths uniform weights attain the minimum 3+2sqrt(2).

This is global, not merely symmetric optimization. Given any positive a,b,c, rescale its restricted Laplacian A by its smallest eigenvalue. Then I<=A<=kappa I. Reflection conjugation preserves these inequalities. Their average also satisfies them and is the restricted Laplacian with equal outer weights (after the same scaling). Consequently the averaged path has condition number at most kappa. The preceding bound applies to it, proving that every positive path has condition number >=3+2sqrt(2). Uniform weights achieve the global minimum. A zero edge disconnects a path and cannot be a finite-condition minimizer. We require existence of the displayed uniform optimizer only; uniqueness of all optimizers is not used.

Take the initial weights (7,8,7), total 22, and the globally optimal output (22/3,22/3,22/3). Both Laplacians have trace 44. The initial extreme eigenvalues are 15 +/- sqrt(113); its remaining nonzero eigenvalue is 14. Its condition number is strictly larger than the optimal one because 113/225>1/2 (226>225) and (1+t)/(1-t) is strictly increasing for 0<t<1. All weights are positive and the underlying graph remains connected.

On a tree, unit current between two vertices travels only along their unique path: every off-path branch has zero net flow by eliminating leaves, and each on-path edge has current one. Ohm's law therefore gives resistance as the sum of reciprocals of conductances along that path. Summing over unordered vertex pairs counts an edge separating s and 4-s vertices exactly s(4-s) times. For P4, total unordered resistance is 3/a+4/b+3/c. Initially this is 6/7+1/2=19/14. For the uniform optimizer it is 10/(22/3)=15/11. The difference is 1/154>0. Dividing by six gives average increase 1/924. Ordered-distinct-pair averaging gives the same average.

This defeats both the tempting spectral-spread shortcut and the stronger claim that every globally best-conditioned trace-preserving weighted output reduces average resistance. It is not just an arbitrary improvement in condition number. Averaging the spectral resistance formula over pairs also removes eigenvector dependence: for a unit zero-mean eigenvector f, sum_{u<v}(f(u)-f(v))^2=4. Thus total resistance=4 sum 1/lambda. Better condition number with fixed trace does not imply a smaller reciprocal sum; the explicit path proves this within graph Laplacians, not just abstract spectra.
