## Full independent ordinary proof

Use additive notation. Let C3=Z/3Z and C2=Z/2Z, with coordinatewise addition, and G=C3×C2. Each factor is an abelian group, so G is abelian. Its cardinality is 3·2=6. Put H0=C3×{0} and A=B=C3×{1}. The map u↦(u,1) is a bijection C3→A and likewise to B, so both sets are finite, nonempty and of cardinality three. Their elements have second coordinate 1, whereas the identity (0,0) has second coordinate 0. Therefore 0∉B (and also 0∉A).

Every sum of an element of A with one of B has second coordinate 1+1=0; thus A+B⊆H0. Conversely (u,0)=(u,1)+(0,1), so H0⊆A+B. Therefore A+B=H0. The residues 0 and 1 in C2 differ, so A∩H0=∅. All source hypotheses hold. Every bijection A→B is a matching because its assigned sums lie in H0 outside A.

Fix ANY matching f:A→B with unit acyclicity sequence. Write s_f(a)=a+f(a). If distinct a,a' had the same image x, then m_f(x)≥2, contrary to the unit condition. Conversely an injective s_f gives every positive fibre cardinality one. Its image is contained in H0; an injection from a three-element set into the three-element H0 is surjective. Hence for each x∈G, the fibre of s_f over x has cardinality one if x∈H0, and zero otherwise. In particular m_f=1_H0 as functions on all G, including every point outside the support. This is stronger than equality of supports or sorted sequences.

Let t=(1,0). This is nonzero since 1≠0 in C3. Translation T_t:b↦b+t maps B to B, with inverse b↦b−t; the second coordinate stays 1. Define g=T_t∘f, namely g(a)=f(a)+t. Composition of bijections gives a bijection A→B. Its sums lie in H0, so it is admissible. For any a, equality g(a)=f(a) would cancel f(a) and force t=0. Since A is nonempty, g≠f as functions (indeed they differ everywhere).

Associativity gives a+g(a)=a+f(a)+t. For every x∈G this equals x exactly when a+f(a)=x−t. The two defining fibres are therefore the SAME subset of A, and m_g(x)=m_f(x−t). Subtracting t leaves the second coordinate unchanged, so x−t∈H0 iff x∈H0. Using the already proved indicator formula gives m_g(x)=1_H0(x−t)=1_H0(x)=m_f(x) for EVERY x∈G. By function extensionality, m_g=m_f. Acyclicity of f would force g=f, contradicting the preceding paragraph. Thus every unit matching is nonacyclic, so no matching satisfies both requested conclusions. This refutes the exact source conjecture, and hence also the copied literal claim. There is no permutation enumeration.

Unit existence is nonvacuous: f=id_A, using A=B, is a bijection and hence a matching. Its sums are (2u,0) for u∈C3. Multiplication by 2 is invertible in C3 because 2·2=1 modulo 3, so these sums are distinct and cover H0. Therefore id has unit acyclicity sequence. Existence of bijections or distinct-sum matchings is not the obstruction.

For comparison, for any fixed c∈C3 set h_c((u,1))=(c−u,1). This is a bijection A→B (its own inverse) and is admissible. Every sum equals (c,0), so m_hc is three at that point and zero elsewhere. If another matching k has this same labelled multiplicity, every a∈A must satisfy a+k(a)=(c,0); cancellation gives k(a)=(c,0)−a=h_c(a). Thus k=h_c. These are acyclic matchings with sequence (3), not unit matchings. The weaker acyclic-existence assertion holds for this pair.

A valid broader wording is: for any nontrivial finite ABELIAN H, A=B=H×{1} in H×C2 admits no acyclic unit matching. If a unit matching exists, its |H| distinct sums exhaust H×{0}, and any nonzero output translation from H gives the same argument. If none exists, the unit-existence conclusion already fails. General unit existence via id additionally requires doubling on H to be bijective, for example H of odd order; it is not automatic for every H. Only the finite C3 witness is needed here, and no family receives extra credit. The author's phrase 'any nontrivial finite H' needs 'abelian' for fidelity to the source; this is a nonfatal wording repair to the optional generalization.

## Prior bridge audit and strongest objections

Read actual cached 2103.11432v2 (30 January 2022), especially Definitions 1.1/2.1, Theorems 1.2/1.3 and their complete proofs, Examples 2.2/2.3, Theorems 2.4/2.5 and discussion, Proposition 2.6 and its complete proof, Example 2.7 and Remark 2.8. The paper is Journal of Algebra 598 (2022), 85–104. The primary body, not snippets, informs the comparisons below.

1. Theorem 2.5 discusses earlier inverse-matching counterexamples in prime groups. For A=B, f and f⁻¹ have the same multiplicity by reindexing and commutativity, so an acyclic f must be an involution; on an odd-sized A it has a fixed point. Their chosen A is invariant under doubling, which makes a fixed point inadmissible. But invariance under doubling forces 2A⊆A∩(A+A), nonempty. Hence these earlier examples fail the exact additional sumset-disjointness assumption. One cannot import their acyclic-matching-property failure into this target. Translating or lifting them would require a new full bridge preserving their obstruction; none is established here.

2. Proposition 2.6 gives an acyclic matching under a Sidon condition on B, with no unit conclusion. Its proof uses minimal counterexamples and equal labelled multiplicities to obtain a forbidden additive relation in B. Our B fails its condition: (0,1)+(2,1)=(1,1)+(1,1), with disjoint sets of summands. Even if the existence theorem applied, its conclusion would not imply a unit sequence. Theorem 1.2 concerns identity self-matchings in prime cyclic groups subject to a size/characteristic bound; its proof uses the rank of a permutation-matrix combination. Our G has composite order, and Remark 2.8's prime-exponent extension also does not contain C3×C2. Theorem 1.3 is another existence result with size and prime-cyclic assumptions. 3. Read all six pages of cached s91alia.pdf (Aliabadi–Taylor, SLC 91 (2025), B91b), especially the entire construction and uniqueness induction in Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.3. The construction orders the image sums, assigns all currently available pairs realizing the first sum, removes those arguments and outputs, and continues. For group addition, cancellation in both coordinates makes each equal-sum layer a collection of nonconflicting pairs; equality of multiplicity at successive labels forces equality of the assignments. Theorem 2.3 concludes acyclic existence for every admissible pair in every abelian group, not existence with a unit sequence.
