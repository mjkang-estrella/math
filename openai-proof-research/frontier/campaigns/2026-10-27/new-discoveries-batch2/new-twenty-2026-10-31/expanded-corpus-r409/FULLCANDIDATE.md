## Factor cores, paid rather than inferred
Let C5 have vertices Z/5Z and adjacency differences ±1. Its independence number is2: three mutually nonadjacent selected vertices would require at least one unselected vertex in each of three cyclic gaps, requiring at least6 vertices. Its chromatic number is3: alternating two colors around an odd cycle is impossible; the sequence0,1,0,1,2 is a3-coloring. Every proper induced subgraph is a disjoint union of paths and isolated vertices, hence2-colorable by alternating on each path. Thus C5 itself is its unique chromatic core. K2 is its unique core, because any proper induced subgraph has chromatic number at most1. These are finite connected undirected simple graphs as required by the paper.

## Route A: strong C5 square, sustained after the weak product-bound shortcut
Take G=H=C5 and G'=G,H'=H. Let X=C5 strong-product C5. A proof using only max chi <= chi(product) <= product chi would leave chi(X) undetermined and cannot prove core minimality. Instead establish exact color and independent-set certificates.

Use coordinates(i,j) in (Z/5Z)^2. Adjacent vertices differ by(a,b) in {0,1,-1}^2 minus{(0,0)}. The map c(i,j)=i+2j mod5 is proper: if b=0 the change is±1; if b=1 the possible changes are1,2,3; if b=-1 they are-1,-2,-3, all nonzero mod5. Thus chi(X)<=5.

For any independent set S, put S_i={j:(i,j) in S}. A row is a C5, so |S_i|<=2. If some S_i has2 elements, cyclic symmetry lets them be{0,2}; their closed neighborhoods in C5 cover all five columns. Each neighboring row must then be empty, since strong adjacency includes same-column as well as neighboring-column edges. The two remaining rows are adjacent to one another. If either contains2 vertices the other is empty by the same argument, while if neither contains2 their total is at most2. Therefore in this case |S|<=4. If no row contains2, |S|<=5. Consequently alpha(X)<=5. This proof covers every independent set, not a sampled list.

Let Y=X minus vertex(0,0), an induced proper subgraph. Any independent set of Y is also independent in X, so it has at most5 elements. Four colors cover at most20 vertices, whereas Y has24. Hence chi(Y)>=5. Restrict the displayed5-coloring to get chi(Y)=5. Since Y is induced, chi(X)>=5, so chi(X)=5 too. Each vertex of X has8 distinct neighbors (the eight allowed nonzero displacement pairs), so |E(X)|=25*8/2=100; deleting one vertex leaves92 edges. Thus si(X)=125 and si(Y)=116. In fact strict decrease alone suffices. The proposed product of factor cores is X itself, which cannot minimize si because Y has the same chromatic number and smaller si. ## Route B: a smaller strong witness and the lexicographic source claim
Take G=C5,H=K2, again each its own unique core. Because K2 is complete, their strong product and lexicographic product are identical: for different first coordinates adjacency occurs exactly when the first coordinates are adjacent; within one fiber the two vertices are adjacent. Call this graph Z=C5[K2]. This equality is checked directly from both primary definitions, rather than imported from a product theorem.

Any independent set in Z contains at most one vertex per K2 fiber. Its first-coordinate projection is independent in C5, so it has at most2 vertices. Every vertex(i,0) receives color i, and vertex(i,1) color i+2 mod5. The two colors in each fiber differ. Consecutive fibers carry the disjoint sets{i,i+2} and{i+1,i+3}; the same statement wraps modulo5, so this is a proper5-coloring.

Let W=Z minus(0,0). It has9 vertices and independent sets of size at most2. Four colors cover at most8 vertices, so chi(W)>=5, while restriction of the displayed coloring proves chi(W)<=5. Thus chi(W)=chi(Z)=5. A vertex of Z has its one fiber neighbor and four vertices in the two neighboring fibers, so degree5. Z has10 vertices and25 edges, W has9 vertices and20 edges, and their structor indices are35 and29. Therefore Z is not a chromatic core of itself.
