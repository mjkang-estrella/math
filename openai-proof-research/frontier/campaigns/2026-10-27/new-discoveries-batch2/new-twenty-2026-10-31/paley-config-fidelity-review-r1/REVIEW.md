## 355956: exact source, semantics, ordinary proof

The source quantifies over prime powers Q congruent to 1 modulo 2d, d>1, and the largest positive r giving an integral quotient (Q−1)/(p^r−1) divisible by d. The formal admissibleR includes positivity and denominator divisibility before using natural division. This avoids truncated-quotient artifacts and r=0. These conditions are the ordinary integer-divisibility interpretation, not arbitrary rational divisibility. Denominator positivity and its bound give r≤8 in this instance. Direct exact calculation gives integral possibilities r=1,2,4,8; their quotients are 97656,16276,626,1, respectively. Only r=1 has quotient divisible by 3. Q=390625 and Q−1=6·65104.

In any field K of that cardinality, additive torsion gives 5^8·1=0; field nontriviality and absence of nilpotents give 5·1=0. Nonzero constants 1,2,3,4 then show characteristic exactly five. The theorem mem_subfieldSet_iff proves that z^5=z means exactly z∈{0,1,2,3,4}; this is the actual prime subfield, not an unrelated five-element surrogate. In general p^r−1 dividing p^m−1 is equivalent to r dividing m, consistent with the source subfield notation. No unsupported subfield-existence premise is used in the instantiated endpoint.

Let f=T^8+T^7+2T^6+2 and x=2θ+4θ²+θ³. The eight Frobenius identities prove f divides T^(5^8)−T through AdjoinRoot. The latter polynomial splits in K and is nonzero. Monicity and degree eight ensure f is nonzero and nonconstant, so it has a root in K. This argument does not require f irreducible, or infer the existence of a root merely from a potentially trivial quotient. The identities are universal polynomial equalities in characteristic five, checked by ring/linear_combination; root existence is then a separate splitting argument.

For each a=0,...,4 there is an explicit cube root y_a and inverse h_a with y_a³=x−a and (x−a)h_a=1. Thus x−a and y_a are nonzero and x differs from every element of F5. Every nonzero w∈F5 has w^4=1, hence (w³)³=w. F5 is a clique, and adjoining x gives a strictly larger clique. The cube sign identity (−y)³=−y³ supplies the reverse adjacencies. There is no zero-cube or one-sided-adjacency loophole.

The universal theorem is nonvacuous: both negation endpoints explicitly instantiate GaloisField 5 8, with the proved cardinality. The custom Set clique/maximality endpoint and Mathlib SimpleGraph.IsClique/Maximal endpoint both close. SimpleGraph.fromRel symmetrizes its relation; for d=3 the nonzero-cube relation is already symmetric, and is irreflexive. Although the source does not separately prove a general all-d graph-equality bridge, no such bridge is needed for the d=3 refutation: the extension proof explicitly supplies both directions, and fromRel has exactly the literal adjacency here.

Independent.py uses a separate polynomial-arithmetic implementation, rather than the author's field/search code. It checks T^(5^8)=T mod f, all five cube identities and five inverse identities, x^5≠x, and the admissible-r list. It additionally checks irreducibility by Rabin (degree eight, the sole prime divisor of eight is two), independently furnishing a concrete field of size 5^8. This supplemental irreducibility check is not attributed to the author's Lean theorem. No mass search was rerun.

**Scope:** this refutes the unrestricted maximal-subfield-clique conjecture. Theorem 1.2 assumes base-field size p^r>(n−1)². Here p^r=5 and n=8, so 5≤49; that valid conditional theorem is not contradicted. The source abstract/introduction's “confirms” language must be qualified by the theorem's explicit hypothesis. Neither the diagnostic count 960 nor the separate conjecture about dropping the theorem's hypothesis is a certified endpoint or separate credited result here.

## 355701: exact source, semantics, ordinary proof

The source explicitly treats configurations combinatorially, so no straight-line geometric realization is required. The nine lines are 023,245,358,278,136,068,014,467,157. The independent checker verifies distinctness, three points per line, degree three at each point, and at most one line containing each pair of distinct points. Nine points equal nine lines; these are consistent hypotheses, not a contradictory configuration premise.

A triangle has three distinct pair-intersection points. The source's inclusion/exclusion definition excludes concurrent triples; it also forces the points and lines to be distinct. IsTriangle reproduces those clauses literally. The own checker enumerates line triples and requires all three singleton intersections to be different, finding 21 triangles. It examines all 512 subsets and all 512 line colorings. The maximum pairwise-intersecting set has size four, attained by indices 0,1,2,3; every coloring has at least one monochromatic triangle. Its computed minimum exactly one is diagnostic only.

The Boolean incidence tests are connected to the Finset objects by mem_L, meets_iff, L_inj, mem_lines9, filter_image and the configuration/cardinality lemmas. Injectivity prevents silently collapsed duplicate lines. The indexed clique bound transfers to arbitrary subfamilies through preimage/image equality and cardinality. The triangle list need not be complete to prove the endpoint: every listed tuple satisfies the literal definition and coloring_core finds a listed monochromatic one in every coloring. minMonoNonzero9 accepts an arbitrary coloring of all Finset point sets and restricts it to L; every coloring on the actual lines extends to this domain, so this does not omit colorings. There are finitely many nonnegative triangle counts, so universal positivity is equivalent to positive minimum.

S4 has cardinality four and is inclusion-maximal by the universal upper bound. Thus both endpoints refute their intended readings: conjectureInclusion_false refutes the inclusion-maximal reading, and conjectureMaximum_false refutes even existence of any intersecting family of size greater than four. No surrogate endpoint is left without a bridge. Automorphism order nine, exact minimum one, and the source's defective second example are not additional certified discoveries.
