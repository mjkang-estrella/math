## 338010: exact degree-seven triangle density

Primary body: Kirsch–Radcliffe arXiv:1712.07769v2, Definitions 2.1 and 6.2, Lemma 6.1 with its double-counting proof, Theorem 6.3 with its counting proof, Conjecture 6.4, and full Theorem 6.5 proof (cached text lines 607–695). The actual objective is triangles per vertex, not triangles per possible triple. All finite simple graphs with maximum degree at most seven and no K4 must satisfy 11 k3(G) <= 40 |V(G)|. The explicit BT(3) has eleven vertices and forty triangles. Disjoint unions do not change the density.

Let t(v)=e(G[N(v)]). Mantel gives t(v)<=12 and sum t(v)=3 k3(G). Thus the target is sum_v (11 t(v)-120)<=0. Vertices with t=12 have excess12, t=11 excess1, and t<=10 have deficit at least10. If every t were <=10 the result would follow, so the hard cases are precisely neighborhoods with eleven or twelve edges. The bounded exact enumeration in experiments.py shows the degree patterns of all labeled triangle-free seven-vertex neighborhoods with these edge counts. It supports the expected K3,4 and K3,4-minus-edge types, but degree-pattern enumeration alone is not a proof of isomorphism classification.

First sustained route: extend the source's Theorem 6.5 discharging. Its degree-five argument excludes adjacent optimal vertices by forcing excessive degrees. That exclusion fails at degree seven. In the admissible graph K3,3,4, its six vertices in size3 parts have degree7 and t=12, and four vertices in the size4 part have degree6 and t=9. Optimal vertices are adjacent across the size3 parts. Its excess total is 6*12-4*21=-12. This is a concrete obstruction to that extension.

Lemma 6.1 applied naively with maximum weight12 and a single low neighbor gives at best (12-1/8)/3=95/24, larger than40/11. Even forcing all seven neighbors below12 yields (12-1/2)/3=23/6, still larger. A one-level high-versus-not-high count therefore cannot reach the target. It must use actual deficits, overlap constraints, and the t=11 class. In BT(3), independent part4 has t=10; in the modified K3,3 part, four untouched vertices have t=12, two edge-endpoints have t=12, and the inserted vertex has t=8. Thus distribution is six t12, four t10, one t8, sum120; total excess 72-40-32=0. This gives a necessary tightness test for any discharging rule.

Second sustained route: G=H vee I_s, where H is triangle-free. Necessary and sufficient degree restrictions are |H|<=7 and Delta(H)+s<=7. Every triangle has one vertex in I_s, so density is s e(H)/(|H|+s). experiments.py enumerates every labeled triangle-free H of orders1..7 using edge recursion pruning a new edge exactly when its endpoints already have a common neighbor. All 356 aggregated (order, edges, maximum degree, s) feasible cases have density<=40/11. This rules out every graph in this join subclass, not arbitrary graphs, and is not a Lean proof. The analytic restriction for fixed s is e(H)<=min(floor(h^2/4),floor(h(7-s)/2)); these coarse bounds are not always attainable, motivating the exact local enumeration.

Exact next lemma: construct a charge transfer on edges or on maximal triangle-free neighborhood configurations proving sum(11 t(v)-120)<=0 for arbitrary K4-free maximum-degree-seven graphs, tight on BT(3). No transfer with proven overlap bounds is supplied. No ordinary full-scope proof, source-object Lean endpoint, semantic bridge, or independent review exists. Park.

## 337625: interval-code semantic obstruction

Primary Franke–Muthiah arXiv:1711.03185v2, Definition2 (stimulus space X, U_empty=X), Definition5, Conjectures1/2 and proposed endpoint perturbation pp8–9 read. Conj2 is the reserved statement: C is OPEN CONVEX, odim(C)=2 => arbitrary convex dim(C)=2. Its cited equivalence to Conj1 does not license dropping the open-convex hypothesis for a counterexample. The source supplies a proposed construction, not a proved equivalence argument or full proof of perturbation.

Attempt A: perturb each closed endpoint outward and each open endpoint inward by epsilon/3. At a shared endpoint with intervals ending and starting, the new overlap/gap can create a missing codeword. Exact example on X=[-1,1]: U1=X, U2=[-1,0], U3=(0,1] realizes {12,13}. Perturb U2 rightward and U3's left endpoint rightward creates a gap carrying word1. This is a concrete failure of the proposed general construction, not an exact refutation of Conj2: {12,13} cannot have ANY open convex realization. Indeed U1 is the disjoint union of the two nonempty relatively open sets U1 intersect U2 and U1 intersect U3, contradicting convex connectedness.

Attempt B: take three consecutive codeword strata L,P,R at a tied endpoint, partition neurons into left-ending, right-starting, persistent, and singleton intervals; replace the point stratum by a small open interval. A singleton interval can be expanded, but simultaneous mixed endpoint choices may insert persistent-only or union codewords. An open realization rules out the two-stratum disconnection example above, but no argument was found that it rules out every multi-neuron transition obstruction or supplies a consistent global endpoint order. Merely proving connectedness of each U_sigma does not yet construct such an order.

Closest later primary Gupta–Karthika–Prathiba2020, DOI10.26637/MJM0S20/1153: read web-extracted full Theorem4.3 proof and Example4.4. Theorem4.3 treats open versus closed interval realizations; Example4.4 uses {12,13}. This is the same failed shortcut, and is already public. Do not credit it or call it a counterexample to the OPEN-CONVEX reserved target. Direct publisher PDF retrieval returned404 once; web indexed primary text was accessible and read. No denied-route retry.

Exact next lemma: characterize mixed interval codes that admit an open convex realization in any dimension by an order of their endpoint events admitting open-interval perturbations without adding/deleting codewords, including singleton sets, unbounded intervals, empty intervals, tied endpoints, and the source's X convention. Alternatively give an open plane realization of a mixed-interval code lacking every open-line realization. Neither delivered. Full ordinary proof and Lean semantic library (code atoms, Euclidean convex/open realization, minimum dimensions, lower-bound obstruction) remain absent. Park.
