Route A, finite-state proof: a finite group has finitely many subgroups, but after each ascendent the new family is a subset of the power set of the OLD family. If |A_j|=m, the immediate bound is |A_{j+1}|<=2^m, not an invariant bound. Therefore there is no pigeonhole argument for repeated isomorphism states; even a repeated state would at most prove eventual periodicity, whereas the literal request says periodic. Needed: a uniform finite set of decorated restriction-system types for ALL ascendants and, for pure periodicity, a return to the initial fusion data or a proof transient values repeat from index zero. Neither is proved.

Route B, closure-system duality: Theorem 5 gives FS={chi_<R>: R subset S}. One can prove it directly: <H intersect S> agrees with H on S and lies in H, forcing equality if H is Occam; conversely, a subgroup generated by R subset S lies in every subgroup agreeing on S. One might hope the ascendent remains a meet-closed system so that closure duality yields a two-cycle. It does not. Let G=C2 x C2 and name its three nontrivial cyclic subgroups L1,L2,L3 and top V. Choose S consisting of generators of L1,L2 and T consisting of generators of L1,L3. Then FS={0,L1,L2,V}, FT={0,L1,L3,V}. Their intersection is {0,L1,V}. It is not FU for any subset U of G: to generate V, U must include nonidentity elements from at least two different lines, forcing both such lines into FU. This proves the proposed meet-closure invariant false by hand, without a mathematical search program. It is not a counterexample to periodicity. A valid replacement must track the actual embedding/evaluation incidence, not just the abstract order type. This unpaid incidence-transform lemma is precise; no solver plan or candidate is fabricated.

336680, source arXiv:1707.06696v4, pp.1–4 definitions, Lemmas 2.1–2.3 and Theorem 2.4 proofs; section 4 proof passage and discussion examined. The source assumes integer a>=2; the MathDB phrase 'every a' inherits that convention. Each functional graph component has one directed cycle. An element g of order d is periodic iff gcd(a,d)=1: periodicity gives d | a^k-1, and conversely Euler's theorem supplies a k. Its cycle has length ord_d(a), since g^(a^k)=g iff a^k=1 mod d. Thus N(a,G)=sum_{d|rho} w_G(d)/ord_d(a), with identity term one, rho the largest divisor of |G| coprime to a. This is a complete reconstruction of Lemma 2.1, not the conjecture.

Route A, direct-product extension: on a product, cycles of lengths u,v produce gcd(u,v) cycles, each length lcm(u,v), because the product cycle has uv points. Hence N(a,G x H)>=N(a,G)N(a,H), exactly the source's Theorem 2.4. The nilpotent reduction uses direct products of Sylow subgroups. General groups are not such products: in S3 the three transpositions have order 2 and the two 3-cycles have order 3, whereas a cyclic Sylow product C2 x C3 has elements of order 6; no group isomorphism exists. Therefore the source's nilpotent theorem is a proper-scope result, not a full exclusion or a new endpoint.

Route B, order-divisibility transport: suppose there is a bijection b:C_n -> G with |b(c)| dividing |c|. For every periodic c, b(c) is periodic and ord_|b(c)|(a) divides ord_|c|(a). Therefore 1/ord_|b(c)|(a)>=1/ord_|c|(a). Summing over periodic c, injecting their images into all periodic G elements and adding nonnegative remaining terms proves N(a,G)>=N(a,C_n). This is a complete conditional bridge. No all-group such bijection is proved here or claimed known. Its matching formulation requires Hall inequalities for unions of element-order divisibility classes; individual equations x^d=1 or prime-power counts do not by themselves prove all those union inequalities. Needed: for every subset S of C_n, at least |S| group elements whose order divides the order of some c in S. An authenticated theorem satisfying exactly this and all finite groups would be a material possible subsumer and require full read/chronology review. None identified/cleared.
