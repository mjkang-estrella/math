Exact hub decomposition repairs this. If h belongs to S, each opposite vertex b_i must be dominated within {a_i,b_i,c_i}. This is equivalent to choosing a nonempty subset of that triple; h already dominates a_i and c_i. Its local weight is 3x+3x^2+x^3. Thus these sets contribute x(3x+3x^2+x^3)^n. If h is absent, each triple is a path on three vertices, whose dominating subsets are {b_i}, its three two-element subsets, and the full triple. Their local weight is x+3x^2+x^3. Among these product choices, precisely one selection fails to dominate h: all triples choose {b_i}. Subtract its weight x^n. Choices on different triples are independent and the two hub cases are disjoint. Therefore, as a polynomial identity,

D(F_{4,n},x)=x(3x+3x^2+x^3)^n+(x+3x^2+x^3)^n-x^n
=x^n [ x p(x)^n + q(x)^n - 1 ],
p(x)=x^2+3x+3=(x+3/2)^2+3/4>0, q(x)=p(x)-2.

Complete sign proof: let n be positive odd. For x>0, q(x)=1+x(x+3)>1. Thus q(x)^n>1 and x p(x)^n>0, so the bracket is positive. For x<0, if q(x)<=0 then q(x)^n<=0 and x p(x)^n<0, so the bracket is negative. Otherwise q(x)>0. If x<=-1, then 0<q(x)<p(x); hence q(x)^n<p(x)^n<=(-x)p(x)^n and x p(x)^n+q(x)^n<0, so the bracket is negative. If -1<x<0, then x+3>0 and 0<q(x)=1+x(x+3)<1. Hence q(x)^n<1 and the bracket is negative again. In every nonzero case x^n and the bracket have the same strict sign (n odd), giving D(F_{4,n},x)>0. No nonzero real root exists.
