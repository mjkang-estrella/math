## 318479: degree of translated LR coefficients

The acquired current original remains Conjecture 5.5, not an eventual-polynomiality conjecture alone. Lemma 5.3 proves the latter from a finite chamber complex. An affine ray crosses only finitely many chamber walls: for every defining linear form ell, ell(na+b)=n ell(a)+ell(b) has eventually fixed sign, or is identically constant. Thus even when a lies on a wall, the translating ray eventually lies in one fixed relatively open face. This supplies a clean boundary-safe explanation of eventual polynomiality. It does not by itself compare degrees on different faces.

Route A, monotone sandwich: the source tableau addition injection gives f(n)<=P(n). If for some K the direction Ka dominates the translation b in the LR semigroup, it also gives P(n)<=f(n+K). Since f has positive leading coefficient and degree d, these bounds force the eventual polynomial degree to be d. This is exactly source Lemma 5.6, and the dominance condition is not implied by b having positive LR coefficient: a can lie on a supporting face with ell(a)=0 but ell(b)>0. Then ell(Ka-b)<0 for every K. No universal domination was inferred.

Route B, rational-polytope thickening (conditional bridge, worked out personally): suppose a fixed-dimensional rational inequality model P_n={x:A x<=n a+b} is bounded, and P_0^dir={x:A x<=a} is nonempty of affine dimension D. For sufficiently large n, every feasible vertex of P_n is n v_I+w_I from one of finitely many nonsingular active bases I. Its limiting v_I is in P_0^dir, because every limiting inequality is satisfied. Every w_I belongs to a fixed finite set. Therefore P_n lies in n P_0^dir plus a fixed bounded ball. This includes cases where P_0^dir has smaller dimension than P_n.

Because the affine span of a rational polytope is rational, choose an invertible rational change of coordinates carrying its direction subspace into the first D coordinates. Images of integer lattice points lie in a fixed denominator grid. The last coordinates of the thickening are within a bounded distance of n times a rational point, so they have only O(1) grid choices independently of n. The first D coordinates have extent O(n), yielding O(n^D) total lattice points. A fixed lattice translate of nP_0^dir, if known to lie in P_n, supplies the matching lower growth. In a genuine lattice-polytope counting model of the stretched coefficient, positive integer dilates and relative-volume lower bounds would identify D=d. Combined with eventual polynomiality and the source injection, the upper growth bound would force degree d even on a boundary direction.

Unpaid exact bridge: an actual primary hive/tableau model with fixed inequalities, boundedness after all boundary normalization, and a lattice-point bijection for all six arbitrary partitions was NOT acquired/read here. In particular, rational vertices need not be integral, and a declaration that the model is a hive does not prove dimension equals the stretched polynomial degree; that needs a rational-lattice relative-volume argument and nonempty affine lattice compatibility. Those facts are plausible but are not silently credited. A full literal theorem would also need the zero/empty partition and same-size conventions. The source f(n) being a polynomial of degree d excludes the zero polynomial only once that convention is fixed.

Route C, representation multiplication: the source addition injection might be strengthened to finite generation over the graded algebra of multiples of a. Finite generation would bound the Hilbert polynomial degree of the translated module. Boundary directions make finite generation/nonzero rank the central issue, so writing 'Hilbert polynomial' is not a proof. The 2017 quiver/KTT search hit is navigation to a potentially relevant but not authenticated theorem, not evidence that it settles Conjecture 5.5.

                                                   same shape and content to itself.




                                                                               1. Introduction
                                             The Littlewood-Richardson (LR) coefficients c(λ; µ, ν) appear in many branches
                                          of mathematics. For example, they appear in the representation theory of the
                                          symmetric groups, in the theory of symmetric functions, in the Schubert calculus
                                          and in problems regarding the existence of matrices with certain eigenvalues or
                                          invariant factors (see [Ful]).
                                             Some recent research has been concerned with the behaviour of the stretched
                                          LR coefficients. More precisely, fix partitions λ, µ, ν and investigate the function
                                          f (n) = c(nλ; nµ, nν) as a function of n, where nλ is the partition obtained from λ
                                          by multiplying every part by n. King et al. [KTT] conjectured that the stretched
                                          LR coefficient is a polynomial in n. Derksen and Weyman [DW] as well as Rassart
                                          [Ras] proved King’s conjecture to hold true, using semi-invariants of quivers and
                                          partition functions, respectively. In fact, Rassart [Ras] proved even more: fix a
                                          positive integer k and let the partitions λ, µ, ν have length at most k, then the
                                          triples (λ, µ, ν) of partitions with positive LR coefficient c(λ; µ, ν) form a cone in
                                          R3k . This cone decays into a finite number of cones in which the LR coefficient is
                                          given by a polynomial in (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , µ1 , . . . , νk ). Rassart remarks in his paper that
                                          Knutson also has an unpublished proof for this property using symplectic geometric
                                          techniques.
                                             In [KT] (see also [Bu1]) Knutson and Tao proved the saturation conjecture which
                                          is, that f (n) = c(nλ; nµ, nν) 6= 0 for some n ≥ 1 implies c(λ; µ, ν) 6= 0. In
                                          [KTW] Knutson, Tao and Woodward proved that f (n) is constant if and only if
                                          c(λ; µ, ν) = 1. Furthermore, if the polynomial f (n) 6= 0 has an integer root −t ∈ Z
