332021 Let $\mathbb G^*$ be the compactification of rooted countable graphs introduced in the paper, with metric $d$. Let $\mathcal S$ be a finite set of finite connected graphs, and let $Ex^{\bullet}(\mathcal S)$ be the class of rooted, labelled or unlabelled, connected graphs with no minor in $\mathcal S$. Let $R_n$ be a uniformly random element of $Ex^{\bullet}(\mathcal S)$ with $n$ vertices.

**Compactified rooted convergence conjecture.** The sequence $R_n$ converges weakly with respect to $d$.

The compactification allows infinite-degree vertices and is compact, so every sequence has a convergent subsequence. The conjecture asserts convergence of the entire sequence for minor-excluding classes; it is closely related to the earlier rooted weak-convergence formulation but is not merely ordinary weak convergence.
332084 Let $0<s\leq k\leq n$ be positive integers. Let $L=\{\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s\}$ be a set of $s$ positive integers such that $0<\ell_1<\cdots<\ell_s$. Suppose that $n>k^2-k+1$. Let $\mathcal F=\{F_1,\ldots,F_m\}$ be an **$L$-intersecting, $k$-uniform family** of subsets of $[n]$, meaning that $|F_i\cap F_j|\in L$ for every distinct $F_i,F_j\in\mathcal F$. **The Erdős–Ko–Rado-type conjecture for $L$-intersecting families.** Then

$$
m\leq {n-\ell_1\choose s}.
$$

Moreover, if

$$
|\mathcal F|={n-\ell_1\choose s},
$$

then there exists a set $T\in {[n]\choose \ell_1}$ such that $T\subseteq F$ for every $F\in\mathcal F$. This conjecture extends the preceding bound and its equality characterization from the proven range to the stated condition $n>k^2-k+1$; the projective-plane example motivating it shows why the threshold is natural. Its resolution is not indicated in the supplied text.
335093 A **binary tree** is a tree in which every vertex has degree $1$ or $3$. A **rooted binary tree** is a rooted tree whose root has degree $2$ and whose other vertices have degree $1$ or $3$. A **rgood binary tree** is a rooted greedy binary tree with root degree $2$ and degree sequence consisting of vertices of degrees $3$, $2$, and $1$. A **binary caterpillar** consists of a path with pendant vertices such that every internal vertex has degree $3$. For a tree $T$, let $C(T)$, $CT(T)$, and $Core(T)$ denote its middle parts, and let $d(A,B)$ denote the distance between middle parts $A$ and $B$. The **eccentricity** of a vertex is its maximum distance from any vertex.

**Extremal middle-parts conjecture.** Among binary trees of order $n$, the tree $T$ that maximizes $d(CT(T), C(T))$ is formed by identifying the root of an rgood binary tree with a vertex of maximum eccentricity in a binary caterpillar. The same tree structure maximizes $d(Core(T), CT(T))$ as well as $d(Core(T), C(T))$.

The conjecture proposes a common extremal structure for three distances between different middle parts of a binary tree. The source gives no resolution, so its status remains open.
340875 Let $G=G_{n,p}$ be a random graph, and let $(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{\delta})$-Chvátal-resilience mean resilience with respect to subgraphs satisfying the shifted Chvátal degree condition: there is an ordering $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ with $d_H(v_1)\geq\cdots\geq d_H(v_n)$ such that, for every $i<n/2$, either

$$
d_H(v_i)\leq (n-i)p-\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}np
$$

or

$$
d_H(v_{n-i-\boldsymbol{\delta} n})\leq ip-\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}np.
$$

**Shifted Chvátal-resilience conjecture.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, there exists $C>0$ such that, for $p\geq C\log n/n$, a.a.s. the random graph $G_{n,p}$ is $(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$-Chvátal-resilient with respect to Hamiltonicity.

This conjecture proposes the Hamilton-cycle analogue of the preceding theorem for perfect matchings, extending Chvátal-type resilience to Hamiltonicity in sparse random graphs. Its resolution status is not specified in the supplied text.
