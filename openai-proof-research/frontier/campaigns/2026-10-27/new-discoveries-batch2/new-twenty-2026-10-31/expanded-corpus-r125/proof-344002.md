## 1. Source statement and definitions

Source: C. C. Graham, *A beastiary of sets having extremal Sidon constant, or, there must be more than one theorem somewhere here*, arXiv:1910.00924v1 (the only version). The raw e-print is at `sources/eprint/1910.00924v1/src.bin`, and its SHA-256 is listed in `sources/eprint-sha256.txt`. The conjecture is `conjexceptionalZprime`, at `Bestiary.tex` line 1479:

> **Conjecture.** If p is prime and 1 < n < p, all the extreme n-element subsets of Z_p are equivalent.

The MathDB record 344002 (chunk 0006, line 3382, raw SHA-256 `f049c024d0fc6753…`) states exactly this.

The definitions come from the source.

- **Sidon constant and extreme measure (Definition 1.1).** A nonzero measure μ on the discrete abelian group G is *extreme* if ‖μ̂‖_∞ = ‖μ‖ / √(# supp μ). Here ‖μ‖ is the total variation and μ̂ is evaluated on the whole dual group Γ.
- **Extreme set.** A finite set E is extreme if it is the support of an extreme measure.
- **Equivalence (Subsection "Automorphisms and equivalent sets").** E and F are equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of group automorphisms and translations. In Z_p this is exactly F = aE + b with a ∈ Z_p^×.

The source's Theorem 2.2 (`thmExtrMsConstant`) says μ is extreme if and only if |μ| is constant on supp μ and |μ̂| is constant on Γ. The proof below does not need this theorem: it checks Definition 1.1 directly.

## 2. The objects

Both have |E_i| = 25.

## 3. Proof that E1 and E2 are extreme

**Autocorrelation (exact integer check).** For each i and each g ∈ Z_71,

A_i(g) := Σ_x c_i(x) c_i(x+g) = 25 if g = 0, and 0 otherwise.

This is checked exactly in `scripts/cert344002.py` and printed in `outputs/cert344002.json`. In other words, c1 and c2 are first rows of circulant weighing matrices CW(71, 25).

**Flat Fourier transform.** Write χ_k(x) = e^{−2πikx/71} and μ_i = Σ_x c_i(x) δ_x. Then

|μ̂_i(k)|² = Σ_x Σ_y c_i(x) c_i(y) χ_k(x − y) = Σ_g χ_k(−g) A_i(g) = 25

for every k. So ‖μ̂_i‖_∞ = 5.

**Total variation.** ‖μ_i‖ = Σ_x |c_i(x)| = 25, and # supp μ_i = 25. Hence

‖μ_i‖ / √(# supp μ_i) = 25 / 5 = 5 = ‖μ̂_i‖_∞.

So μ_i is extreme and supp μ_i = E_i, which means E_i is an extreme set. The script also checks numerically that |μ̂_i(k)| differs from 5 by at most 2·10^{−13} for every k.

## 4. Proof that E1 and E2 are inequivalent

Define I(E) = #{(x, y) ∈ E × E : 2y − x ∈ E}.

- **Invariance under automorphisms.** Every automorphism φ of the additive group Z_71 is additive, so φ(2y − x) = 2φ(y) − φ(x), and the map (x, y) ↦ (φx, φy) is a bijection between the counted pairs.
- **Invariance under translations.** (x + b, y + b) gives 2(y + b) − (x + b) = (2y − x) + b, so translations also preserve I.

Hence I is constant on equivalence classes. The script computes I(E1) = 225 and I(E2) = 205 (I(E3) = 225). Therefore E1 and E2 are not equivalent.

As an independent check, the canonical forms of E1, E2 and E3 under all 70·71 = 4970 affine maps are pairwise distinct.

Since 71 is prime and 1 < 25 < 71, this negates the conjecture exactly as stated.

**Risk.** Y. Strassler, *New circulant weighing matrices of prime order in CW(31,16), CW(71,25), CW(127,64)*, J. Statist. Plann. Inference 73 (1998), reports **four inequivalent CW(71,25)**. Its body is behind ScienceDirect, which was not accessed under the access rules, so it is **unread**. Equivalence of weighing matrices (w ↦ ± x^s w(x^t)) is the affine action on the sequence, so inequivalent matrices need not have inequivalent supports. Still, Strassler's printed first rows almost certainly include supports that are affinely inequivalent; our three support classes are presumably among his four matrices. If so, the objects contradicting Graham's conjecture were published in 1998, and the only new step is the bridge "CW support ⇒ Graham-extreme set" (Section 3). That is the 355525 pattern: explicit-example literature whose stated properties, after a one-line bridge, contradict a structural conjecture.

Arasu and Ma (JACO 14, 2001; full text fetched, SHA-256 `e74ecd72…`) does not print CW(71,25) rows.

Adin, Epstein and Strassler (arXiv:math/9910164) classify CW(31,16): there are two inequivalent matrices, but their supports are **affinely equivalent** (checked). So Z_31 gives no counterexample.

**Exhaustive context.** Numerical search over all affine classes of n-subsets of Z_p for p ≤ 17 (`logs/ext-*.log`) found at most one extreme class per (p, n). This is a numerical diagnostic only, and local minima are possible.

**Significance.** The conjecture is a speculative remark in an informal note, and the counterexample comes from classical design-theoretic objects. Root should weigh this against the exclusion rules for explicit-example literature.
