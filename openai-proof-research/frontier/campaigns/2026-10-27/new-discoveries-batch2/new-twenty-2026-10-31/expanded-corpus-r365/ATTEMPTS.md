## 347257: exponential diagonals, sustained Jordan mechanism

Literal: every non-diagonalizable directed unweighted adjacency matrix A, every pair i,j and every algebraic β, equality of the two exponential diagonals implies equality of all positive power diagonals. Ballini–Deniskin 2007.08956v2 explicitly assumes β≠0 at lines 95–98. Its Proposition 4 (357–408) allows only one defective eigenvalue with blocks of size at most two; Conjecture 3 (411–418) asks for the full extension. Deniskin–Benzi, Journal of Combinatorics 14(4), 2023, Problem 1 (journal extraction 620–638), still asks the nonnegative algebraic weighted version with β≠0. This later weighted problem does not imply a public proof or refutation of the unweighted question.

**Complete literal endpoint correction, not discovery.** Take A to be the direct sum of [[0,1],[1,0]], [[0,1],[0,0]], and [0]. This is a directed unweighted loopless graph. The second summand has a nonzero nilpotent Jordan block, so A is not diagonalizable (its minimal polynomial has a repeated factor x²). For β=0, exp(βA)=I, so the diagonal at vertex 1 equals that at vertex 5. But (A²)11=1 and (A²)55=0. All literal hypotheses hold and the conclusion fails at r=2. The source's explicit β≠0 removes this witness. This is solely an endpoint correction, hence zero credit; the intended nonzero problem remains HOLD.

**Route 1, full Jordan reduction.** Over the algebraic closure, choose Jordan form A=QJQ⁻¹ with algebraic Q. For each eigenvalue λ and 0≤k<sλ define dλk as the i-minus-j diagonal of Q times the k-th nilpotent part in the λ-blocks times Q⁻¹, divided by k!. Then
F(t)=(exp(tA))ii−(exp(tA))jj=Σλ exp(λt)Pλ(t), Pλ(t)=Σk dλk t^k.
For algebraic nonzero β, all λβ are distinct algebraic numbers. Source Theorem 2 (185–189) therefore implies Pλ(β)=0 for each λ. It does NOT imply Pλ≡0: evaluation at one algebraic point is not injective on algebraic polynomials. For example t(t−β) vanishes both at 0 and β but is not zero. Equality of all power diagonals would follow from F≡0 by differentiating at zero, but the current reduction stops strictly before that conclusion. This is the precise missing lemma, not a vague difficulty with diagonalization.

**Route 2, reproduce the exact restricted proof rather than overextend it.** If just one eigenvalue λ* is defective and has index≤2, every other Pλ is constant, so Pλ(β)=0 makes those constants zero. Since F(0)=Iii−Ijj=0, the constant term of Pλ* is also zero. Pλ*(β)=0 and β≠0 then kill its remaining linear coefficient. Hence F≡0. This is exactly the complete Proposition 4 mechanism; it is a routine bridge with zero credit. With index≥3, Pλ*(0)=Pλ*(β)=0 permits t(t−β); with two defective eigenvalues even their constant terms need not separately vanish. The proof cannot simply be iterated over blocks because different blocks at the same λ share the same exponential.

**Route 3, positivity/realization.** Powers of an adjacency matrix count closed walks, so each diagonal power sequence is nonnegative. Their difference is signed; positivity does not make the dλk nonnegative. A rational/algebraic matrix realizing a polynomial cancellation is insufficient: it must be realized with 0/1 entries, and the selected vertex diagonals must retain the cancellation at the exact β. Nilpotent acyclic directed graphs do not help: every positive power diagonal is already zero. Appending a nilpotent block to a diagonalizable graph gives a defective matrix but does not bypass its old algebraic-β theorem on the old vertices. No exact nonzero unweighted witness or all-Jordan proof was found.

Gates: literal fidelity exposes correction; nonzero ordinary mathematics OPEN; public weighted Problem 1 is not a solution; novelty/significance fail for β=0 and remain uncleared for nonzero; no formal endpoint. DORMANT Pi plan withheld because there is no qualified candidate. A finite β=0 certificate would formalize only an expressly ineligible correction.

## 346307: matching augmentation, two concrete mechanisms and their limits

Literal G admits a perfect matching M; if G+M is Class II then every G+tM is Class II. Source 2004.14049v1, §4 lines 579–606, Conjecture 4.1, works in the bridgeless cubic framework. Its latest arXiv history has only v1 (29 April 2020). The published 2023 journal version and Zerafa thesis are specific material comparison sources; search snippets are routing only and their full bodies were NOT read here. They remain HOLD, not cleared by the preprint.

**Route 1: reverse a factorization.** In the cubic setting, G+tM is (t+3)-regular. A Class I coloring is precisely a partition into t+3 perfect matchings N1,…,N(t+3), because each color must meet each vertex exactly once. Every non-M edge appears once; each M edge appears t+1 times. A proof would compress this integral factorization to four perfect matchings with multiplicity two on M and one off M, giving a 4-coloring of G+M and the desired contrapositive. Merely deleting t−1 colors is invalid: a color class need not equal M, and deleting other colors removes original non-M edges. Nor can the copies of M each automatically be assigned an exclusive color after recoloring; that is the missing integral compression statement itself.

There is genuine forward monotonicity in the other direction: if G+tM has a Δ(G)+t coloring, add one fresh color on another whole copy of M to color G+(t+1)M with Δ(G)+t+1 colors. Thus the feasible t form an upward-closed set. Upward closure does NOT show its first element is at most one. Fractional matching decomposition also does not prove an integral factorization with the required exact multiplicities.

**Route 2: odd-cut obstruction.** In any Class I r-regular graph H on even order, each color class is a perfect matching, and every odd vertex set S is crossed by at least one edge of each color; hence |δH(S)|≥r. For H=G+tM, let a=|δG(S)| and b=|M∩δG(S)|. The obstruction is a+tb<t+3, equivalently a−3+t(b−1)<0. Because M is perfect, b is odd and at least one. If b=1 and a<3, the obstruction persists for all t. In a bridgeless cubic graph every odd cut has a≥3, so this elementary obstruction cannot explain its Class II behavior. If b≥3 the deficit can increase with t and cease to obstruct; one cannot claim all Class II colorings have a persistent odd-cut witness. No general obstruction was produced.

**Route 3: Kempe exchanges.** Union of two color classes consists of even alternating circuits. Exchanges can change where copies of M lie, but local exchanges preserving some copies do not guarantee a color that is exactly M. A minimum-intersection potential would need a proof that every noncanonical factorization admits a decreasing exchange, with an obstruction precisely corresponding to no Class I augmentation at any t. That lemma is absent. Flower-snark colorings in §4.1 do not settle arbitrary (G,M), and their already public instances cannot earn new credit. The broader literal nonregular setting requires separate source-fidelity analysis as well.

Gates: source definition partly read; mathematical full endpoint missing; journal/thesis comparison unread and material; no novelty/significance clearance and no formal work. HOLD, not an exclusion based on familiarity.

## 348506: regular versus balanced staged trees, sustained source-semantic mechanism

The literal wording risks conflating two identities. Actual v3 (28 Jan 2022), Proposition 2 and §4, and the author-hosted EJS 16 (2022), 2607–2620 journal body distinguish them. With a distinguished last edge κ at each internal vertex, Nij is ONE downward-path label monomial, whereas tij is the formal SUM of all root-to-leaf path monomials in the relevant subtree. Regularity requires Nij Nsκ=Nsj Niκ; balance requires tij tsκ=tsj tiκ for all same-stage i,s and corresponding j. The journal, lines 507–569, still states the converse as Conjecture 1. A proof treating Nij=tij would be a semantic error, not a discovery.

**Route 1: reverse the source's coefficient argument.** Write tij=Nij+t'ij, and similarly for the three other subtrees. Then
F=tij tsκ−tiκ tsj=Q+R,
Q=Nij Nsκ−Nsj Niκ,
R=t'ij t'sκ−t'sj t'iκ+t'ij Nsκ−t'sj Niκ+t'sκ Nij−t'iκ Nsj.
The source proves balance⇒regularity: separate downward labels A from other labels B; Q is the B-constant coefficient, so F=0 implies Q=0. Reversing the implication is invalid: Q=0 does not kill R. This gives an exact residual polynomial whose vanishing is unpaid.

**Route 2: recursive induction.** The obvious strengthening is to use all regularity equations strictly downstream to pair monomials in R. A useful inductive assertion would be that the two composite trees with polynomials tij tsκ and tiκ tsj have a weight-preserving bijection between ALL pairs of paths, not just their selected downward paths. Ordinary height induction gives balanced identities only for pairs of vertices already sharing a stage; roots of the four child subtrees need not share stages. Thus there is no available induction hypothesis comparing these four t-polynomials. Same-stage equality at the parent supplies only equality of N-products. The missing cross-subtree pairing lemma is precise and must be proved or refuted, not assumed from the recursive terminology.

**Route 3: vary the distinguished paths.** If one could independently vary all distinguished choices while retaining the regularity hypothesis, one could hope to recover all path coefficients. But the hypothesis in the actual criterion is about the chosen natural-coordinate representation. Invariance under all such changes, including independent changes in subtrees tied by stage identifications, was not proved. Even collecting attainable monomial-product equalities needs multiplicity accounting to imply equality of formal sums. Setting outgoing labels to probabilities and using Σθ=1 is also invalid: it makes each tij=1 and loses precisely the formal polynomial identity defining balance. This route cannot be advertised as a proof.

The EJS journal proof of Proposition 3 was read in full; its converse remains open there. Relevant Duarte–Ananiadi/Duarte–Solus/Görgen–Maraj–Nicklasson companion bodies and possible later corrections are NOT reviewed. Those specific sources are material HOLD. Literal apparent tautology is not accepted as a resolution. No full candidate or Pi plan.

## 348394: metric dimension, additional substantive route

The actual definitions use d(s,uv)=min(d(s,u),d(s,v)); vertex and edge resolving sets minimize different families of constraints. Source Conjecture 14 is exactly |dim−edim|≤c. The 2025 published Chakraborty–Foucaud–Hakanen author copy explicitly restates that SAME conjecture at lines 509–516 and obtains only the bound 2c. Its Theorem 8/Proposition 9 (356–402, actual edge-proof read) construct resolving sets from a root and endpoints of a good edge set. This is not a proof of the requested factor-one difference bound.

Route A: spanning-tree deletion leaves c extra edges. A hoped-for one-landmark repair per added edge would yield a transfer between vertex and edge bases. Deleting an edge changes shortest paths globally, so a resolving set in the tree cannot simply be reused in G. Endpoints of all extra edges give at best a 2c-sized repair and are not automatically a transfer from an OPTIMAL basis. The precise needed statement is: from each minimum vertex resolving set construct an edge resolving set by ≤c additions, and vice versa. Neither implication follows from the cited common upper bounds.

Route B: pruning pendant trees forces common branch-resolving costs. Even if both dimensions equal a shared forced cost plus residual core minima, independent upper bounds 2c on the residual minima imply only a 2c difference. Cactus arguments do not extend automatically when cycles share edges and their distance obstructions interact. No graph or formula establishing a violation was found by hand; no enumeration was run. The newer mixed-dimension papers 2607.27573 and 2608.17944 are specific unread possible comparisons, not proof of clearance. ## 347254: complete public-result subsumption bridge (zero credit)

Use Horton–Kloster–Sullivan 1804.05966v3, graph definition at 282–288, Theorem 4.1/Corollary 4.2 at 408–419, Appendix B proof at 876–1188. Choose an integer c≥max(C,3), where C is the theorem's existential threshold, and put m=c+1. Let the vertices be an independent set U={u1,…,uc} and m disjoint cliques Cq={vq1,…,vqc}. Inside each Cq join every distinct pair; join ui to vqi for each q,i, and add no other edges. This is a finite simple undirected connected graph. The permutations of the coordinate indices, simultaneously in U and every clique, and the permutations of the cliques act transitively on U and on its complement separately. Thus there are at most two exponential-diagonal values. Every ui has degree m=c+1 and every vqi has degree (c−1)+1=c. Hence (A²)ui,ui=c+1 and (A²)vqi,vqi=c, so the graph is NOT walk-regular. Theorem 4.1 supplies β in (0,1/(c−2)) making the two walk-class exponential-diagonal values identical. Transitivity then makes ALL vertex β-subgraph centralities identical. β>0 and all literal graph hypotheses hold; the proposed implication to walk-regularity is false. This is the exact unrestricted-β assertion, not an inference from closeness/betweenness centrality counterexamples.

The proof passage was read through its continuity step, explicit spectral formulas and its two eventually strict inequalities (limits e+1>e and e−2>1/2, with final vanishing remainder).
