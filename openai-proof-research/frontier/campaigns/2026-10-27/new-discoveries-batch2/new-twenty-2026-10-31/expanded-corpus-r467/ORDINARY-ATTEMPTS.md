## 377054: Tutte polynomial versus critical group

Actual Wagner original math/0010241v1: §1 defines finite possibly directed multigraphs, Q=Delta-A, full group coker Q^t and reduced group its torsion. For connected undirected graphs the full group is reduced group plus Z, so distinguishing either group distinguishes the other. §7 Conjecture7.9 is precisely the assigned existential assertion. Example7.8 proves the REVERSE non-determination, and is not a witness for this target: the triangle-square vertex sum has the same cyclic group of order12 as C12 but different Tutte polynomials.

Route1, Whitney switching, pursued through its obstruction. In a one-vertex sum, choose the shared vertex as sink. The reduced Laplacian is block diagonal; its cokernel is the direct sum of block groups. The Tutte polynomial is multiplicative because the graphic matroid is a direct sum. Moving the articulation location therefore preserves BOTH invariants. For two-terminal switching, Wagner Proposition7.3 and Corollary7.6 explicitly supply integer row/column equivalences, and Corollary7.7 says isomorphic graphic matroids give the same reduced group. Thus no Whitney-twist pair can be a target witness. The actual proof has been read: weighted rows from the activity vector and ordinary column sums exchange terminal blocks, followed by sign changes. In the undirected case every activity is1. Dropping the simple-vertex hypothesis yields source Example7.4, but those graphs are directed and their different group orders prevent their use here. Neither this example nor Example7.8 is a new solution.

Route2, invariant matching without matroid isomorphism. Equal Tutte polynomials force equal spanning-tree counts by evaluation at(1,1). They do not logically force equal Smith invariant factors. A viable witness would have, for some prime p, different dimensions of K/pK, while retaining the complete Tutte polynomial. For an undirected connected graph with Laplacian L, dim_Fp ker L = 1 + dim_Fp(K/pK), since full cokernel is Z plus K and tensoring presents the cokernel over Fp. Consequently a parity-nullity distinction could prove nonisomorphism without full Smith forms. This is an exact criterion, not a computed example. Rotor gluing with a cyclic three-terminal rotor is a credible Tutte-preserving operation beyond Whitney twisting, but an actual terminal graph and its full subset-rank matching have not been produced. The unpaid lemma is the complete equality of the two subset-rank distributions together with a displayed prime-nullity distinction. Symmetry alone does not pay either requirement.

Route3, algebraic construction through two-terminal networks. Over Q, eliminating internal vertices gives an effective terminal Laplacian (Schur complement). Matching these rational responses controls spanning-tree quantities, but not the integral cokernel: elimination may divide by a nonunit. Conversely matching one determinant does not match the full Tutte polynomial. A proposed construction needs integer unimodular reductions AND a deletion-contraction identity for every state of the network. Those two requirements remain unpaid. There is no chosen finite pair and no FULLCANDIDATE. Contrary significance case: an explicit finite witness to the exact existential statement could be significant despite short Smith computations; absence of a witness, not shortness, is the present obstruction. A search excerpt mentions an already published diagram of same-Tutte/different-critical graphs, but no authenticated primary identity or proof was acquired; that excerpt is navigation only.

## 379627: Narayana preservation, exact public comparison

Actual Liu-Wang math/0602672v3 §1 uses NONNEGATIVE real sequences with x_k^2<=x_{k-1}x_{k+1}. §5 defines peak-count N(n,k)=binom(n,k)binom(n,k-1)/n for n>=1, and Conjecture5.2 is the target. Its unspecified MathDB input condition must be recovered from that standing definition, not expanded to arbitrary signed numbers.

Route1, symmetrized minors. For each total index t, set p_k=x_k x_{t-k}, k<=floor(t/2), and let c_k be the symmetrized coefficient of p_k in z_{n-1}z_{n+1}-z_n^2, counting the central term only once. For positive input the adjacent-ratio inequalities give p_k>=p_{k+1}. If c_k changes once from nonnegative to nonpositive and its total sum is nonnegative, then sum c_k p_k >= p_r sum c_k>=0. This is exactly Liu-Wang Theorem4.8, whose proof was read. Row q-log-convexity alone pays only the total coefficient sum; it does not control the weighting by arbitrary log-convex inputs. In particular one cannot reuse the historical379626 polynomial result as if it solved this target.

Route2, shifted kernel, completed as an attributed public theorem bridge. Actual Zeng2610.19548v3 Theorem1.3 has kernel N_theta(n,k), with theta=1 equal to N(n+1,k+1), and preserves strong coefficientwise log-convexity. Put g_k=x_{k+1}, regarded as constant polynomials. A positive log-convex scalar sequence is strongly log-convex by multiplying its nondecreasing adjacent ratios. Then z_{n+1}=sum_{k=0}^n N(n+1,k+1)g_k exactly, so the shifted public theorem proves all adjacent inequalities centered at n>=2 for the ordinary output. With the usual N(0,0)=1 empty-Dyck-path convention, z0=x0,z1=x1,z2=x1+x2; the remaining inequality is x0*x1+(x0*x2-x1^2)>=0. This is why simultaneous reindexing alone would not discharge index0.

Zero cases: an infinite nonnegative log-convex sequence with x_j>0 for j>=1 forces x_{j-1}>0 and x_{j+1}>0; propagating gives positivity at every index. Otherwise its entire positive-index tail is zero, and the transform tail is zero. Thus no division by zero is hidden in the scalar strong-log-convexity bridge. A convention N(0,0)=0 instead changes the boundary claim and is not silently substituted.

Route3, moment methods and its limitation. For fixed nonnegative q the row polynomials have moment representations, yielding row log-convexity by Cauchy-Schwarz. If the input itself were a Stieltjes moment sequence, integrating a positive kernel would be promising. ## 379629: Eulerian transform — precise remaining all-n obstacle

Actual Liu-Wang §4.1 uses A(n,k) for permutations with k-1 DESCENTS, so rows n=1,2,3,4 are respectively (x1), (x1+x2), (x1+4x2+x3), (x1+11x2+11x3+x4) after transformation. These rows follow by ordinary insertion recurrence A(n,k)=k A(n-1,k)+(n-k+1)A(n-1,k-1); no enumeration was run. Descents k versus k-1 cannot be interchanged without an input shift and boundary check.

Route1, full coefficient reduction. Define c_k(n,t)=A(n-1,k)A(n+1,t-k)+A(n+1,k)A(n-1,t-k)-2 A(n,k)A(n,t-k), with the central half convention. The all-n theorem would follow from one-sign-change (or the weaker condition that all prefix sums of c_k are nonnegative) and the source row q-log-convexity. The latter is source Proposition4.6; the former is NOT supplied by it. Abel summation gives the sharper exact desired bridge: if C_j=sum_{k<=j}c_k, then sum c_k p_k = C_s p_s + sum_{j<s} C_j(p_j-p_{j+1}). Thus nonnegative prefixes would suffice. There is no proof here of all these Eulerian prefix inequalities. The source recurrence couples coefficient levels k and k-1 in products; substituting it leaves mixed-row terms with uncontrolled signs, rather than a closed positive recurrence for C_j.

Route2, direct insertion and ratio induction. The first nontrivial checks can be done symbolically: D1=x0*x1+(x0*x2-x1^2); D2=2*x1*x2+(x1*x3-x2^2). Also D3=4*x1*x2+4*x1*x3+3*x2*x3+x1*x4+5*(x1*x3-x2^2)+(x2*x4-x3^2), all nonnegative under the standing hypotheses. This is a hand derivation of small symbolic identities, not a program or experiment, and supplies no all-n claim. In general insertion weights depend on the current number of descents; a coupling that controls the two products must also preserve these multiplicities. A map between permutation pairs that only shifts a descent is insufficient. The unpaid lemma is a multiplicity-respecting injection/weighted coupling producing a positive combination of input log-convexity minors for every n.

Route3, geometric and moment input. For x_k=q^k, output is precisely the Eulerian polynomial at q, and source Proposition4.6 handles it. Positive sums of such geometric inputs are Stieltjes moment inputs, not all log-convex inputs. The cone of all log-convex sequences cannot simply be replaced by their linear span or by moment sequences. Hence these special cases do not pay the universal conjecture. No counterexample or full all-n mechanism was found. Search results showing the current MathDB open record are not novelty evidence, and the bounded search did not establish worldwide absence of a solution. Park at the explicit prefix-minor/coupling lemma; no formal dispatch.
