In this paper we consider a variant of the general position problem called the monophonic position problem , obtained by replacing ‘shortest path’ by ‘induced path’. We prove some basic properties and bounds for the monophonic position number of a graph and determine the monophonic position number of some graph families, including unicyclic graphs, complements of bipartite graphs and split graphs. We show that the monophonic position number of triangle-free graphs is bounded above by the independence number. We present realisation results for the general position number, monophonic position number and monophonic hull number. Finally we discuss the complexity of the monophonic position problem. Keywords: general position set , general position number , monophonic position set , monophonic position number. MSC 05C12 , 05C69 1 Introduction In 1900 Dudeney, famous for his mathematical puzzles, posed the following question [ 15 ] : what is the largest number of pawns that can be placed on an n × n n\times n chessboard such that no three pawns are on a straight line? This problem was generalised to the setting of graph theory independently at least three times in [ 6 , 26 , 27 ] as follows: a set of vertices S S in a graph G G is in general position if no shortest path of G G contains more than two vertices of S S . The problem then consists of finding the largest set of vertices in general position for a given graph G G . This has been shown to be an NP-complete problem [ 27 ] . The general position problem has been the subject of intensive research; for some recent developments see [ 17 , 25 , 28 , 29 , 30 , 32 ] . Some interesting variants of the general position problem have been considered in the literature. In [ 23 ] the authors consider the general position problem using the Steiner distance instead of the normal graph distance. In [ 24 ] the authors set a limit on the length of the shortest paths considered. For a fixed integer d d , they define a set S S of vertices of a graph G G to be a general d d -position set if for any three vertices u , v , w ∈ S u,v,w\in S that lie on a common geodesic P P , the length of P P is greater than d d ; the number of vertices in a largest general d d -position set is the general d d -position number gp d ​ ( G ) {\rm gp}_{d}(G) of G G . The paper [ 33 ] discusses the largest general position sets that are also independent sets. A further recent variant of the general position problem can be found in [ 11 ] , which discusses mutual visibility sets ; a set M M of vertices of a graph G G is mutually visible if for any u , v ∈ M u,v\in M there exists at least one shortest u , v u,v -path in G G that intersects M M only in the vertices u , v u,v . In this paper, we consider a variation of the general position problem, which we call the monophonic position problem , obtained by replacing ‘shortest path’ by ‘induced path’. The number of vertices in a largest general position set of G G is called the general position number of G G and is denoted by gp ⁡ ( G ) {\rm gp}(G) . A graph G G is distance-hereditary if for any connected induced subgraph H H of G G and vertices u , v ∈ H u,v\in H we have d H ​ ( u , v ) = d G ​ ( u , v ) d_{H}(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v) , where d G ​ ( u , v ) d_{G}(u,v) is the distance between u u and v v in G G and d H ​ ( u , v ) d_{H}(u,v) is the distance between u u and v v in the subgraph H H .
