Keywords: graph convexity, general position number, iteration time. 1 Introduction Convexity is a classical topic, studied in many different branches of mathematics. A rich source is the book “Theory of Convex Structures” by van de Vel [ 61 ] . The study of convexities applied to graphs has started recently, about 50 years ago. Accordingly to Duchet [ 24 ] , the first paper on general graphs, published in english, is the 1981 paper “Convexity in graphs” from Frank Harary and Juhani Nieminem [ 35 ] , where it was introduced the iteration time of a graph in the geodesic convexity. Other important graph convexity parameter is the general position number, which is related to the 1917 Dudeney’s No-three-in-line problem [ 26 ] . The explicit definition of it to the graph geodesic convexity was done by Manuel and Klavžar [ 47 ] . To the best of our knowledge, these parameters were defined and studied only in the geodesic convexity. Our first contribution in this paper is the definition of them to any graph convexity. We also prove computational complexity results on these parameters in three important graph convexities (geodesic, monophonic and P 3 P_{3} ), including the NP-hardness of the geodesic iteration number, a long standing open question. In order to prove this, we also had to prove the NP-hardness of the P 3 P_{3} iteration number. For this, we need some terminology. A convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} [ 61 ] on a finite set V ≠ ∅ V\neq\emptyset is a family of subsets of V V such that ∅ , V ∈ 𝒞 \emptyset,V\in\mathcal{C} and 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is closed under intersections. That is, S 1 , S 2 ∈ 𝒞 S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{C} implies S 1 ∩ S 2 ∈ 𝒞 S_{1}\cap S_{2}\in\mathcal{C} . A member of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is said to be a 𝒞 \mathcal{C} - convex set . Given S ⊆ V S\subseteq V , the 𝒞 \mathcal{C} - convex hull of S S is the smallest 𝒞 \mathcal{C} -convex set conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S) containing S S . It is easy to see that conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \conv_{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot) is a closure operator , that is, for every S , S ′ ⊆ V S,S^{\prime}\subseteq V : (a) S ⊆ conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) S\subseteq\conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S) (extensivity), (b) S ⊆ S ′ ⇒ conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) ⊆ conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ′ ) S\subseteq S^{\prime}\Rightarrow\conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S)\subseteq\conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S^{\prime}) (monotonicity), (c) conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( ∅ ) = ∅ \conv_{\mathcal{C}}(\emptyset)=\emptyset (normalization 1 1 1 Here we follow the definition of closure operator from van de Vel [ 61 ] , which includes the normalization property. ) and (d) conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) ) = conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \conv_{\mathcal{C}}(\conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S))=\conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S) (idempotence). We say that I : 2 V → 2 V \I:2^{V}\to 2^{V} is an interval function on V V if, for every S , S ′ ⊆ V S,S^{\prime}\subseteq V , (a) S ⊆ I ⁡ ( S ) S\subseteq\I(S) [extensivity], (b) S ⊆ S ′ ⇒ I ⁡ ( S ) ⊆ I ⁡ ( S ′ ) S\subseteq S^{\prime}\ \Rightarrow\ \I(S)\subseteq\I(S^{\prime}) [monotonicity] and (c) I ⁡ ( ∅ ) = ∅ \I(\emptyset)\ =\ \emptyset [normalization]. It is not difficult to prove that every interval function induces a unique convexity, containing each set S ⊆ V S\subseteq V such that I ⁡ ( S ) = S \I(S)=S . Moreover, every convexity is induced by an interval function. In the rest of the paper, we assume that every convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} on V V is defined by an explicitly given interval function I 𝒞 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \I_{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot) on V V . It is well known that the convex hull of a set S S in a convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} can be obtained by exhaustively applying the corresponding interval function I 𝒞 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \I_{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot) until obtaining a convex set. Given a finite graph G G , a graph convexity on G G is simply a convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} on V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) with a given interval function I 𝒞 ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \I_{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot) on V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . A standard way to define a graph convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} on a graph G G is by fixing a family 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of paths of G G and taking the interval function I 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \I_{\mathcal{C}}(S) as the set with all vertices lying on some path of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} whose endpoints are in S S . The most studied graph convexities are path convexities, such as the geodesic convexity [ 29 , 30 , 35 ] , the monophonic convexity [ 25 , 37 ] , the P 3 P_{3} convexity [ 11 ] and the P 3 ∗ P_{3}^{*} convexity [ 3 ] , where 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is, respectively, the family of all geodesics (shortest paths) of the graph, of all induced paths, of all paths of order three and of all induced paths of order three. We use the subscripts g \gc , m \mc , p3 \pc and p3* \psc to refer to the the geodesic convexity, the monophonic convexity, the P 3 P_{3} convexity and the P 3 ∗ P_{3}^{*} convexity, respectively. In the following, when we say that some numerical parameter is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard, we mean that the determination of its value is an 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard problem. From these definitions, we can define the two parameters investigated in this paper for any graph convexity. The iteration time of a set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) in a graph convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , denoted by ti 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \ti_{\mathcal{C}}(S) , is the minimum k k such that I 𝒞 k ⁡ ( S ) = conv 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \I^{k}_{\mathcal{C}}(S)=\conv_{\mathcal{C}}(S) , that is, k k applications of the interval function are necessary in order to obtain the convex hull of S S . Also let the iteration time of a graph G G in a graph convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , denoted by ti 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \ti_{\mathcal{C}}(G) , be the maximum value of ti 𝒞 ⁡ ( S ) \ti_{\mathcal{C}}(S) among the subsets S S of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . The iteration time is one of the first graph convexity parameters, introduced in 1981 by Harary and Nieminem [ 35 ] in the geodesic convexity. In [ 54 ] , bounds were obtained for the geodesic iteration time. In 2016, Dourado et al. [ 20 ] obtained a polynomial time algorithm to determine the geodesic iteration time on distance hereditary graphs, which was improved in 2020 by Moscarini [ 51 ] . However, the computational complexity of the iteration time was so far open for any graph convexity, including the geodesic convexity. The general position number of a graph G G in a graph convexity 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , denoted by gp 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \gp_{\mathcal{C}}(G) , is the size of a maximum subset of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) in general position, where we say that a subset S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) is in general position if z ∉ I 𝒞 ⁡ ( { x , y } ) z\not\in\I_{\mathcal{C}}(\{x,y\}) for every distinct x , y , z ∈ S x,y,z\in S . In the P 3 P_{3} convexity, curiously any subset in general position induces a subgraph with maximum degree 1 and then gp p3 ⁡ ( G ) \gp_{\pc}(G) is equivalent to the dissociation number diss ⁡ ( G ) \diss(G) of the graph G G , a parameter introduced in 1981 by Yannakakis [ 63 ] , who also proved 𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{NP}} -hardness even for bipartite graphs and planar graphs with maximum degree four. In the P 3 ∗ P_{3}^{*} convexity, any subset in general position induces a subgraph whose connected components are cliques and, then, gp p3* ⁡ ( G ) \gp_{\psc}(G) is equivalent to the IUC number ( independent union of cliques ) of the graph G G , a parameter introduced in 2020 by Ertem et al. [ 28 ] and proved 𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{NP}} -hard even in planar graphs. The geodesic general position number is a generalization of the No-three-in-line problem in the n × n n\times n grid from discrete geometry, which can be traced to the famous Dudeney’s “Puzzle with Pawns” of his book “Amusements in Mathematics” [ 26 ] from 1917. All the following results are for the geodesic convexity. In 1995, Korner [ 44 ] investigated the general position number on hypercubes, while in [ 12 ] it was considered for the first time on general graphs. However, the formalization of the problem as we know it today and the notation that is in use have been introduced in [ 47 , 48 ] . Also see [ 31 , 45 , 50 , 56 ] for the related general position subset selection problem in computational geometry. In 2018, it was proved that determining the general position number is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard [ 47 ] . In 2019, general position sets in graphs were characterized [ 1 ] and, after this, several additional papers on the general position problem were published, many of them with bounds on the maximum size of a general position set and exact values in graph products [ 13 , 40 , 41 , 42 , 43 , 55 , 58 , 59 ] . In this paper, we prove that the iteration time is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard in the P 3 P_{3} convexity. This result is the basis to prove that the iteration time is also 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard in the geodesic convexity even in graphs with diameter two. Surprisingly, even though this problem has been defined in 1981, its computational complexity had not been settled yet. In addition to these points, we consider these results even more important due to the fact that they are the last two missing proofs of 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hardness among the ten main graph convexity parameters in the geodesic and P 3 P_{3} convexities. In order to justify this, notice that the following nine graph convexity parameters are 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard in the geodesic and P 3 P_{3} convexities: hull number and convexity number [ 4 , 3 , 21 ] , interval number [ 11 , 34 ] , Carathéodory number [ 5 , 15 ] , Radon number [ 2 , 22 ] , Helly number [ 10 , 19 ] , general position number [ 47 , 63 ] , rank [ 38 , 57 ] and percolation time [ 2 , 49 ] . Regarding the monophonic convexity, we prove that the monophonic general position number is W ⁡ [ 1 ] W[1] -hard (parameterized by the size of the solution) and n 1 − ε n^{1-\varepsilon} -inapproximable in polynomial time for any ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 unless 𝖯 = 𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{P}}={\mathsf{NP}} , even in graphs with diameter two. Finally, we also prove that the general position number in the P 3 P_{3} convexity is W ⁡ [ 1 ] W[1] -hard (parameterized by the size k k of the solution) and we obtain fixed parameter tractable results regarding the neighborhood diversity, the vertex convex number and the cliquewidth of the graph. 2 Preliminary results Given a positive integer n n , let [ n ] = { 1 , … , n } [n]=\{1,\ldots,n\} .
