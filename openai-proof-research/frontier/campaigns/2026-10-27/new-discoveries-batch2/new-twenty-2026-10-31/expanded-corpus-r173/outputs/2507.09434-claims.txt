Proposition 2.9 . We have d(n)=0 if and only if n is a power of 3 or the sum or difference of two powers of 3. Proof. We deduce directly from Lemma 2.8 that d(n)=0 if and only if there is no integer t\geq 0 such that \lfloor n/3^{t}\rfloor or \lceil n/3^{t}\rceil is at least 5 and congruent to \pm 1\pmod{6}. Write n in balanced ternary form n=3^{a_{1}}\pm 3^{a_{2}}\pm\cdots where a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots\geq 0. If the representation stops after at most 2 terms, then by checking each t\in\{0,1,\ldots,a_{1}-1\} we see that no such t exists. Otherwise, if t=a_{3}, then either \lfloor n/3^{t}\rfloor or \lceil n/3^{t}\rceil is 3^{a_{1}-a_{3}}\pm 3^{a2-a3}\pm 1. Since 3^{a_{1}-a_{3}}\pm 3^{a_{2}-a_{3}} is divisible by 6, either \lfloor n/3^{t}\rfloor or \lceil n/3^{t}\rceil is \pm 1\pmod{6}. ∎ When n is not nice, the function d(n) will still be essential for our proof, as we now explain.
