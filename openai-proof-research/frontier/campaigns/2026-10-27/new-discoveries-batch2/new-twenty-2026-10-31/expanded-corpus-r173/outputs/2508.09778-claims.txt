The necessity of the Rado triple condition follows from the fact that partition regularity of ( 1.2 ) implies partition regularity of its linear counterpart ( 1.1 ). The sufficiency, however, is far less understood: no instance of a Rado triple (a,b,c) is currently known for which partition regularity of ( 1.2 ) has been established. Depending on the coefficients, the problem exhibits a range of distinct and often deeply challenging behaviors. In [ 12 ] , among other results, the authors prove partition regularity with respect to the pair (x,y) for the equation 16x^{2}+9y^{2}=z^{2}; that is, for every finite partition of \mathbb{N} there exist distinct x,y in the same cell and z\in\mathbb{N} such that the equation is satisfied. In [ 14 ] , partition regularity with respect to (x,y) and with respect to (y,z) is established for the Pythagorean equation x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2}. For every pretentious multiplicative action (X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu,T_{n}) and every set A\in{\mathcal{X}} with \mu(A)>0, there exist distinct x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}} such that \mu\bigl(T^{-1}_{x}A\cap T^{-1}_{y}A\cap T^{-1}_{z}A\bigr)>0\quad\text{and}\quad ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2}. Remark 1 . Our proofs give in fact something stronger: we show that the conclusion holds for many triples x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}}, and we provide lower bounds for the measure of the intersection. If a=c or b=c, then for any \varepsilon>0, we show there exist distinct x,y,z\in{\mathbb{N}} such that \mu(T^{-1}_{x}A\cap T^{-1}_{y}A\cap T^{-1}_{z}A)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon, while in case a+b=c, the exponent is either 3 or 4, depending on whether all of the quadratic forms appearing in the parametrization of the solutions of ax^{2}+by^{2}=cz^{2} are irreducible or not.
