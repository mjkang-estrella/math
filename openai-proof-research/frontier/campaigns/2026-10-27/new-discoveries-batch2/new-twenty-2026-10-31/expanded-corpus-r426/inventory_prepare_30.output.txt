Lemma 17. Let G be a weakly connected finite simple digraph such that for all
vertices d± (v) ≤ 1 then G is either a directed path or a directed cycle.
Proof. Let n is the number of elements in G. If n = 1 then G is an isolated
vertex.
     When n > 1 we divide into two cases.
     Case 1. There is a vertex v1 with d− (v1 ) = 0. Then d+ (v) = 1 which
means that there is a unique element v2 such that (v1 , v2 ) is an edge, if n > 2
then d+ (v) = 1 since, if this is false, we will have that {v1 , v2 } is a weakly
connected component of G because there cannot be other edges pointing v2 by
the fact that d− (v2 ) ≤ 1. So there is a unique element v3 such that (v2 , v3 ) is
an edge. Inductively, there should be a sequence of vertices v1 , . . . , vn such that
(vi , vi+1 ) ∈ E(G) for all i ∈ {1, . . . , n − 1}. This means G is a directed path.
     Case 2. There is a vertex v1 with d− (v1 ) = 0. The same proof as case 1
works with the obvious modifications.
     Case 3. For every vertex v ∈ V , d+ (v) = d− (v) = 1. Take now any vertex
v1 ∈ V then since for every vertex d+ (v) = 1 we can form unique sequence of
vertices {vi }i∈N such that (vi , vi+1 ) ∈ E(G) since the graph is finite this sequence
should repeat. That is, there is some k and l such that vk+l = vl . Now, consider
the directed path vl → vl+1 → vl+k = vl . For each vi , since d+ (v) = d− (v) = 1,
there is a unique w such that w, vi ∈ E(G), which should be vi−1 and similarly,
vi+1 is the unique vertex after vi . This means that {vl , . . . , vk+1 } is directed
cycle and a weakly connected component. Thus k = n and G must be this
directed cycle.
