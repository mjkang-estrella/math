                                           1     Introduction
                                           The energy of graph and the Randic index are well known graph invariants
                                           defined from considerations in chemical graph theory. This two quantities are
                                           known to be good descriptors of any graph and their properties have been
                                           explored in networks. Since for many networks the relation between nodes is
                                           non-symmetric it is natural to study these descriptors for directed graphs, where,
                                           for example, the inner and outer degrees play very different roles in terms of the
                                           “flow” structure of the network.
                                               The following inequalities have been proven in [1] and [14]:

                                           where G is an (undirected) simple graph, R(G) the Randic index of G, E(G)
                                           the energy of G and ∆(G) the maximum degree of G. A notion of energy of a
                                           digraph was defined in [11]. Recently, the definition of Randic index has been
                                           extended and studied for digraphs [5, 12]. The main purpose of this paper is to
                                           extend the inequality in (1) to directed graphs. This is proved in Theorem 6 and
                                           Theorem 9. In this case, since the adjacency matrix of a directed graph is not

    In order to describe the graphs for which the equalities in (1) hold we use,

    Apart from this introduction, the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2, we introduce the energy of a digraph as defined by Nikiforov. We also define
the outer energy of a vertex E + (v) and inner energy of a vertex E − (v) and prove
that for adjacent vertices E + (vi )E − (vj ) ≥ 1. In Section 3, we prove the main

Hermitianization trick. We use this technique to give another proof of the main
theorems of the paper and to describe the graphs in which the equalities in (1)
are fullfilled.


2     Digraph energy and vertex energy
A (finite) directed graph or digraph is a pair G = (V, E) where V is a finite set
and E ⊂ V × V , the elements in V are called vertices and the elements in E are
called edges. We assume that G is simple, i.e. (v, v) ∈ / E for all v ∈ V . For a
digraph G with n vertices, the adjacency matrix, denoted by A = A(G), is the
n × n matrix with entries Aij = 1 if (vi , vj ) ∈ E and Aij = 0 otherwise.
    The energy of a graph is given by the trace of the absolute value of its
adjacency matrix [6, 9, 10]. In analogy to graphs, the energy of a digraph is
defined as follows. Let us consider a digraph G = (V, E) with adjacency matrix
A ∈ Mn (R). One can define the absolute value of A in two ways:

    Given a digraph G = (V, G), a vertex v ∈ G is called a sink if d+ (v) = 0 and
is called a source if d− (v) = 0. We say that G is a sink-source digraph if every
vertex of G is either a sink or a source.

the set of vertices V into two subsets V1 , V2 and such that ij ∈ E if and only if
i ∈ V1 and j ∈ V2 , moreover if G there is a two to one correspondence between
weakly connected sink-source digraphs and connected bipartite graphs given by
choosing the sink and the source sets.
