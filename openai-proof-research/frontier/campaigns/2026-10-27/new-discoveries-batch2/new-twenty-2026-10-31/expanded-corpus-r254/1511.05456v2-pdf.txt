                                          Abstract. Laborde-Zubieta conjectured a formula to enumerate corners in symmetric tree-
                                          like tableaux. By exploring the relationship among symmetric tree-like tableaux, symmetric
                                          alternative tableaux, permutation tableaux of type B and signed permutations, we reduce L.-
                                          Z.’s conjecture to counting the number of signed descents and ascents in signed permutation, and
                                          thus confirm this conjecture. Moreover, we give a conjectural x-analogue of this enumeration.
                                          AMS Classification 2010: 05A05, 05A19
                                          Keywords: Signed Permutations; Permutation tableaux of type B; Symmetric Tree-like tableaux;
                                          Symmetric Alternative tableaux.


                                          1    Introduction

                                          The main objective of this paper is to prove Laborde-Zubieta’s conjecture on the enumeration of
                                          corners in symmetric tree-like tableaux. Tree-like tableaux were introduced by Aval, Boussicault
                                          and Nadeau in [3], and they are related to many combinatorial objects such as permutations,
                                          permutation tableaux, non-ambiguous trees, alternative tableaux, Dyck tableaux and so on. For
                                          more information, we refer the reader to [2, 1, 3, 8, 10, 11].
                                              L.-Z. [12] introduced a kind of minor structure called corner in a symmetric tree-like tableau.
                                          He studied the number of occupied corners in symmetric tree-like tableaux and obtained the
                                          following result.

                                          Theorem 1.1 ([12]). The number of occupied corners in the set of symmetric tree-like tableaux
                                          of size 2n + 1 is 2n n!.

                                             Moreover, L.-Z. also considered the number of corners in symmetric tree-like tableaux and
                                          proposed the following conjecture.

                                          Conjecture 1.2. ([12, Conjecture 4.2]) The number of corners in the set of symmetric tree-like
                                          tableaux of size 2n + 1 is 2n × n! × 4n+13
                                                                                 12 .

                                          of type B and symmetric alternative tableaux. In this paper, we similarly define corners in
                                          permutation tableaux of type B and symmetric alternative tableaux according to L.-Z.’s defini-
                                          tion of corner in [12]. To our surprise, on the basis of the bijection established by Corteel and

    By employing the bijection between signed permutations and permutation tableaux of type
B due to Corteel and Kim [4], we can count the number of corners in type B permutation
tableaux. Then via establishing a bijection between symmetric alternative tableaux and per-
mutation tableaux of type B, we can give an enumerative result on the number of corners in
symmetric alternative tableaux. And in the end we confirm Conjecture 1.2 by invoking a bijec-
tion between symmetric tree-like tableaux and symmetric alternative tableaux. We would like
to note that Hetczenko and Lohss [7] independently proved L.-Z.’s conjecture.
    The present paper is organized as follows. We will recall some necessary concepts and no-
tations associated with the above mentioned combinatorial objects in Section 2. In Section
3, we will introduce three bijections: the first one is established between symmetric alterna-
tive tableaux and symmetric tree-like tableaux; the second one is between symmetric alterna-
tive tableaux and type B permutation tableaux; the last one is between type B permutation

and symmetric tree-like tableaux. Finally, we conjecture an x-analogue of the enumeration of
corners in symmetric tree-like tableaux.

    Note that a (k, n)-diagram D is uniquely determined by its southeast border which is the
lattice path labeled orderly by [n] = {1, . . . , n} from the top right corner of D to its bottom left
corner; it is the path labeled by the integers 1, 2, · · · , 8 on the left of Figure 1. That is to say, a
shifted (k, n)-diagram is uniquely determined by the southeast border of its (k, n)-subdiagram.
Thus, given a shifted (k, n)-diagram D ∗ , we call an edge a border edge if it lies on the southeast
border of its (k, n)-subdiagram D. A border edge is called a south(resp. west) step if it goes
down(resp. left) along the southeast border.
    Let D ∗ be a shifted (k, n)-diagram, rows and columns of D ∗ will be labeled by integers in the
following manner. Since the border edges of D ∗ are labeled by [n], if a south(resp. west) step
is labeled by i(resp. j), then the corresponding row(resp. column) is labeled with i(resp. j).
And then we label the added rows as follows: if the diagonal cell of an added row is in column i,
then the row is labeled by −i. We will say row i or column j when we actually refer to the row
with the label i or the column with the label j. Then the cell locating at the intersection of row
i and column j can be denoted by (i, j). Note that we will actually write all the column(resp.
row) labels on the top(resp. left side) of the corresponding columns(resp. rows) for an improved
readability, as shown in the right of Figure 1.

   In what follows, we shall introduce three kinds of tableaux, which are the main combinatorial

Definition 2.3. A symmetric tree-like tableau is a filling of a (k, n)−diagram(without empty
rows or empty columns) with points inside some cells, such that the resulting tableau satisfies
the following three rules,

 (1) the top left cell of the diagram contains a point, called the root point;
 (2) for every non-root pointed cell c, there exists either a pointed cell above c in the same
     column, or a pointed cell to its left in the same row, but not both;
 (3) every column and every row possess at least one pointed cell;
 (4) the tableau is invariant with respect to the reflection across the main diagonal of its
     underlying diagram.

is defined to be the number of its points. It is not difficult to see that the length of a symmetric
tree-like tableau equals to its size plus one [3]. Thus the size of a symmetric tree-like tableau is

3     Enumeration of Corners in Symmetric Tree-like Tableaux
                                                                 sym
In this section, we will calculate the number of elements in C(T2n+1 ). We first describe a bijection
α from tree-like tableaux to alternative tableaux due to Aval et al [3] and then we restrict it
to symmetric tree-like tableaux and symmetric alternative tableaux. Additionally, we need a
bijection ζ between permutation tableaux of type B and symmetric alternative tableaux via
the alternative representation of type B permutation tableaux defined by Corteel and Nadeau
[5]. Another bijection ξ established by Corteel and Kim [4] from permutation tableaux of type
B to signed permutations is also indispensable. Based on these bijections, we will give some
enumerative results on the number of corners in these kinds of tableaux. We finish by giving
a conjectural x-analogue of the enumeration of corners in symmetric tree-like tableaux of fixed
size.

3.1.1    A Bijection between Symmetric Tree-like Tableaux and Symmetric Alterna-
         tive Tableaux

We call the tableau that satisfies the first three items in Definition 2.3 a tree-like tableau. More-
over, the tableau satisfying the first item in Definition 2.4 is called an alternative tableau. Let
Tn denote the set of tree-like tableaux of size n and AT n denote the set of alternative tableaux
of size n.

Theorem 3.1 ([3]). There is a bijection α : Tn → AT n−1 such that for any T ∈ Tn , the
underlying diagram of α(T ) is obtained from D by deleting the topmost row and the leftmost
column of D, where D is the underlying diagram of T .

   First of all, we give a description of the bijection α and its inverse α−1 in detail without
proof. Given a tree-like tableau T of size n, replace every non-root point p with a left arrow
“←” if there is no point in the left of p in the same row and an up arrow “↑” if there is no point
above of p in the same column. Then simply deleting the topmost row and the leftmost column.
One can verify that the obtained tableau, denoted by α(T ), is an alternative tableau of length
n − 1.
    As to α−1 , given an alternative tableau T ′ of length n − 1 with underlying diagram D ′ .
Suppose D ′ has k rows and n − 1 − k columns. We first add a column of k cells at the left of the
leftmost column of D ′ and a row of n − 1 − k cells above the topmost row of D ′ . Then adding
one cell at the top left corner of D ′ . Now we denote the resulting diagram by D. Note that D ′
is a (k, n − 1)-diagram and D is a (k + 1, n + 1)-diagram. Next, for any cell c = (i, j) in D ′ , if
there is an up arrow “ ↑ ” in c and there is no arrows(both “ ← ” and “ ↑ ” ) in the left of c
in the same row, add a point in the cell (i + 1, 1) in D. If there is a left arrow “ ← ” in c and
there is no arrows above c in the same column, add a point in the cell (1, j + 1) in D. Then add
a point in (1, 1) and (1, j + 1) or (i + 1, 1) in D if column j or row i has no arrows in D ′ . Lastly,
add a point in (i + 1, j + 1) in D if there is an arrow in (i, j) in D ′ , the resulting tableau is a
tree-like tableau of size n, denoted by T = α−1 (T ′ ).
   As a matter of fact, the bijection α can be restricted to symmetric tree-like tableaux and
symmetric alternative tableaux.
