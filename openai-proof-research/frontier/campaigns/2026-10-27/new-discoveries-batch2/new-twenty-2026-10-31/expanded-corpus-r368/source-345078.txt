                                                                                       1. Introduction

                                           In our note we study the systems of Diophantine equations and connect this problem to Shnirelman’s
                                           theorem: “Every integer n > 7 is a sum at most s0 (Shnirelman’s constant) odd primes” [1]. The
                                           problem of the representation of Gaussian integers by a sum of odd Gaussian primes is explored
                                           from an angle of Shnirelman’s theorem.

        3. Gaussian Integers, Odd Gaussian Primes and Shnirelman’s Theorem

Let us introduce the notion of “odd” and “even” Gaussian integers, where z is odd if z ≡ 1 (mod 1+i)
and even if z ≡ 0 (mod 1 + i).
Our global experiment was to see whenever any element from the set ΓG = {z ∈ Z[i]| Re(z) >
0 ∧ − Re(z) < Im z ≤ Re(z)} could be represented as a sum of at most k0 elements from the set
Γπ = {z ∈ πZ[i] | Re(z) > 0 ∧ − Re(z) < Im(z) ≤ Re(z)}. Some of our computations and results are
presented in appendix.
Our experimental work has allowed us to observe some interesting facts about Gaussian integers,
notably given z ∈ ΓG , and either Im(z) = Re(z) − 1 or Im(z) = Re(z), z cannot be represented as
a sum of odd primes from Γπ . This has led us to consider a different choice for our set ΓG = {z ∈
Z[i]| Re(z) > 0 ∧ Im(z) ≥ 0} and the following set Kπ = {z ∈ πZ[i] | Re(z) ≥ 0 ∧ Im(z) ≥ 0}. It is
worth noting that in both cases ΓG is a maximal set of non-associated Gaussian integers.
Studying the results from [2] and our experimental data we have formulated the following conjecture:

Proof. The proof is by induction. Let the proposition be true for some k0 . Let it be false for

is analogous.                                                                                 

If we assume that every odd Gaussian integer z where Re(z) > 0, Im(z) > 0 and max(Re(z), Im(z)) ≥
c0 is a sum of 3 odd Gaussian primes with non-negative real and imaginary parts, then we can use
proposition 1 to get the following theorem:
Theorem 3. Any element a ∈ {z ∈ Z[i]| Re(z) > 0 ∧ Im(z) > 0 ∧ max(Re(z), Im(z)) > c1 } is a
sum of at most 4 odd Gaussian primes belonging to the set Kπ = {z ∈ πZ[i] | Re(z) ≥ 0 ∧ Im(z) ≥ 0}

If we consider Z as a subset of Z[i], there are even elements of Z which can not be represented as a
sum of 2 elements from the set πZ ∩ πZ[i] . However, if one of the hypotheses below is true then the
elements of Z as a subset of Z[i] could be represented as a sum of at most s0 primes from πZ ∩ πZ[i] .
     • Hypothesis 1: There exists c0 > 0 such that every integer n ≥ c0 , n ≡ 2 (mod 4) is a sum
       of 2 primes where pi ≡ 3 (mod 4)
     • Hypothesis 2: There exists c0 > 0 such that every integer n ≥ c0 , n ≡ 1 (mod 4) is a sum
       of 3 primes where pi ≡ 3 (mod 4)
     • Hypothesis 3: There exists c0 > 0 such that every integer n ≥ c0 , n ≡ 0 (mod 4) is a sum
       of 4 primes where pi ≡ 3 (mod 4)
     • Hypothesis 4: There exists c0 > 0 such that every integer n ≥ c0 , n ≡ 3 (mod 4) is a sum
       of 5 primes where pi ≡ 3 (mod 4)
Indeed if at least one of the above hypotheses is true, then the following theorem is also true:

                                   4. Discussion and Conclusion

In the present note we have constructed a solution of a system of Diophantine equations of the
first degree in several variables over a set of non-negative integers by the Shnirelman theorem.
We have demonstrated this approach on Gaussian integers. Based on experimental data on the
representation of Gaussian integers by a sum of Gaussian primes we have shown that there is an
infinite number of elements of a maximal set of non-associated Gaussian integers ΓG which cannot
be represented as a sum of two or three Gaussian primes belonging to a set Kπ (ΓG ⊂ Kπ ⊂ Z[i]).
In this case we have formulated the Goldbach conjecture for Z[i] as some analogy of the Shnirelman
theorem. However, according to our experimental evidence we expect that any Gaussian integer of
ΓG , excepting a finite number, can represent by a sum at most three odd Gaussian primes belonging
to a set Sπ (ΓG ⊂ Kπ ⊂ Sπ ⊂ Z[i]) where Sπ is a minimal subset of Z[i] to satisfy such conditions.
