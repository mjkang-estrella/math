function of size n 1 if its nondecreasing rearrangement (b1 ≤ · · · ≤ bn ) satisfies bi ≤ i for all i.

                                           2. Background
   We begin with background related to classical noncrossing partitions, intersection lattices, abso-
lute order and W -noncrossing partitions, Fuss analogs of W -noncrossing partitions, the definition
of W -noncrossing parking functions in [ARR], and homogeneous systems of parameters.

2.1. Notation. Given any poset P , a k-multichain is a sequence of k elements x1 , . . . , xk of P
satisfying x1 ≤ · · · ≤ xk .
   We let W denote an irreducible finite real reflection group and we let V denote the reflection
representation of W . Except in Section 9, we extend scalars to C and consider V to be a complex
vector space. We let n = dim(V ) be the rank of W .
   We let S ⊂ W be a choice of simple reflections in W and we let T = w∈W wSw−1 be the set
                                                                               S
of reflections in W . We let c ∈ W denote a choice of (standard) Coxeter element. That is c is an
element of W of the form c = s1 . . . sn , where S = {s1 , . . . , sn }. Any two Coxeter elements of W
are conjugate. We denote by h their common order, also called the Coxeter number of W .
   We denote by k ∈ Z>0 a Fuss parameter. We let g be a distinguished choice of generator for the
cyclic group Zkh and we let ω be a primitive khth root of unity.

2.2. Set partitions. Set partitions of [n] are partially ordered by refinement: π ≤ π ′ if each block
of π ′ is a union of blocks of π. The poset of set partitions of [n] has the structure of a geometric
lattice with minimal element 0̂ = {1/2/ . . . /n} and maximal element 1̂ = {1, 2, . . . , n}.
   A partition π of the set [n] is called noncrossing if whenever there are indices 1 ≤ a < b < c <
d ≤ n such that a ∼ c and b ∼ d in π, we necessarily have a ∼ b ∼ c ∼ d in π. Equivalently, if
one labels the boundary of the disc D2 clockwise with the points 1, 2, . . . , n, a partition π of [n]
is noncrossing if and only if the convex hulls of its blocks do not intersect. The left of Figure 3
shows the noncrossing partition {1, 2, 5/3, 4/6} of [6] drawn on D2 . The right of Figure 3 shows
the noncrossing partition {1, 6/2/3, 5/4} drawn on D2 . The cyclic group Zn acts on noncrossing
partitions of [n] by clockwise rotation.
   A set partition π is called k-divisible if each block of π has size divisible by k. Refinement order on
set partitions restricts to give a poset structures on the sets of noncrossing partitions and k-divisible
noncrossing partitions.

    The poset of noncrossing partitions of [n] has the structure of a complemented lattice. An
explicit complementation K was introduced by Kreweras [Kre]. Label the boundary of the disc D2
clockwise with 1′ , 1, 2′ , 2, . . . , n′ , n. Given a noncrossing partition π of [n], draw the convex hulls of
the blocks of π on D2 using the unprimed vertices. The Kreweras complement K(π) is the unique
maximal partition of [n] under refinement obtained using the primed vertices whose blocks do not
intersect the blocks of π in D2 . The partition K(π) is automatically noncrossing.
    Figure 3 shows an example of Kreweras complementation when n = 6. The Kreweras complement
of {1, 2, 5/3, 4/6} is {1, 6/2/3, 5/4}.
    There is a natural injection from the set of noncrossing partitions of [n] to Sn . If π is a non-
crossing partition of [n], the corresponding permutation Ω(π) ∈ Sn of [n] has cycles given by the
blocks of π, read in clockwise order on D2 . In particular, the maximal noncrossing partition has
permutation Ω({1, 2, . . . , n}) = (1, 2, . . . , n) and the minimal noncrossing partition has permutation
Ω({1/2/ . . . /n}) = (1)(2) . . . (n) = (). The permutation of the partition π on the left of Figure 3 is
Ω(π) = (1, 2, 5)(3, 4)(6) ∈ S6 . Let Π denote the inverse of the injection Ω. For example, we have
Π((1, 2, 5)(3, 4)(6)) = {1, 2, 5/3, 4/6}.
    There is a bijective correspondence between k-divisible noncrossing partitions of [kn] and k-
multichains of noncrossing partitions of [n] under refinement. To explain this bijection, we will
need the notion of a shuffle of partitions. Given two sets of integers A and B satisfying |A| = |B|
and a partition π of A, let π(B) be the unique partition of B which is order isomorphic to π. For

2.3. Reflection groups and intersection lattices. A standard parabolic subgroup of W is a

2.4. Reflection length and W -noncrossing partitions. For any w ∈ W , a T -reduced expression

ℓT (w) = n − dim(V w ) for any w ∈ W .

A partition π of ±[kn] is said to be k-D-noncrossing if the following five conditions are satisfied.
    (1) If B ∈ π, then −B ∈ π (i.e., π is centrally symmetric).
    (2) The vertices on A2 corresponding to the blocks of π can be connected by simple closed
         curves contained in A2 such that the curves corresponding to distinct blocks do not cross.
    (3) If π contains a block Z satisfying Z = −Z (i.e., a centrally symmetric block), then Z must
         contain all of the vertices on the inner boundary of A2 together with at least 2k additional
         vertices on the outer boundary of A2 .
    (4) When the vertices of any block of π are read in the cyclic order coming from the embed-
         ding in A2 , the absolute values of consecutive vertices represent consecutive residue classes
         modulo k.
    (5) If every block of π consists entirely of vertices on the outer boundary of A2 or entirely of
         vertices on the inner boundary of A2 , then the inner blocks of π are determined by the
         outer blocks of π in the following way. A block B ∈ π on the outer boundary is called
         visible if it can be connected to the inner boundary by a curve which does not cross any
         other blocks on the outer boundary. Let B be a visible block and let b ∈ B be the last
         vertex read in clockwise order. Then the two inner blocks of π are the unique pair of two
         consecutive k-element subsets of the inner boundary such that one of the subsets ends in a
         (in counterclockwise order) where a > 0 and a ≡ b (mod k).
Condition 4 implies and is stronger than the condition that π is k-divisible. Condition 5 was
originally missed by Krattenthaler and Müller; its necessity was observed by Kim.

the inner boundary of A2 , outer if B only contains vertices on the outer boundary of A2 , zero if B
satisfies B = −B, and annular if none of the preceding conditions hold. If π is k-D-noncrossing, we
observe the following trichotomy.
       • The partition π contains a zero block and any other blocks of π are outer.
       • The partition π contains inner blocks and all other blocks of π are outer.
       • The partition π contains annular blocks and any other blocks of π are outer.
