## 343702: a full-scope counterexample and its general mechanism

Source: Pourhadi–Faghani, arXiv:1909.02634v1, 5 September 2019, introduction and Definition 1.1, Conjecture 2.9 (PDF p4). Graphs are finite, undirected and connected. For every edge uv, W_uv consists of vertices strictly closer to u than v. The required rational lambda>1 is fixed for the graph; the orientation of its ratio may change with the edge. The conjecture requires exactly two degree values AND exactly two transmission values, for every such graph. It does not impose edge transitivity, diameter three, primeness under graph products, or the special H(m,G,k) construction.

Let S have vertices {o,a,b} and edges oa,ob. Let G=S square S be the Cartesian product: (x,y) is adjacent to (x',y') precisely when one coordinate agrees and the other pair is an edge of S. This is a simple undirected finite graph with nine vertices. It is connected: first move the first coordinate through S and then the second. It is bipartite by parity of the number of leaf coordinates, so it satisfies even the bipartiteness asserted by the source.

For arbitrary connected graphs A,B,
 d_AdotB((x,y),(u,v))=d_A(x,u)+d_B(y,v).
Indeed every product edge changes one coordinate; projection of a path therefore requires at least the two coordinate distances in total. Concatenating shortest coordinate paths attains the sum. Consequently, for a product edge (u,v)(u',v), the common d_B(y,v) cancels in comparing distances from (x,y). Thus
 W^G_((u,v),(u',v))=W^A_(u,u') times V(B).
The reverse set has the same factor. The identical argument applies to edges changing the second coordinate. Therefore the Cartesian product of two quasi-lambda-DB graphs with the same lambda is again quasi-lambda-DB, without any orientation uniformity assumption. This is proved here, not inferred from the unread 2017 paper.

In S, along oa the vertices closer to o are {o,b} and those closer to a are {a}; along ob interchange a,b. Hence S is quasi-2-DB and every edge of G has closer-side cardinalities 6 and 3. Lambda=2 is rational, strictly greater than one, and works for ALL product edges. There are no equal-distance vertices on those edges.

Degree is additive in a Cartesian product because the two types of neighbors are disjoint. The degrees in S are 2 at o and 1 at a,b. Hence G has degree 4 at (o,o), 3 at a center-leaf pair, and 2 at a leaf-leaf pair. These exhaust all vertices and each occurs. Its degree-value set is exactly {2,3,4}.

For any product vertex,
 D_G(x,y)=|V(B)|D_A(x)+|V(A)|D_B(y),
by summing the proved distance formula over all (u,v). In S, D(o)=2 and D(a)=D(b)=3. Thus the transmissions in G are 12 at (o,o), 15 at a center-leaf pair, and 18 at a leaf-leaf pair. These exhaust all vertices and occur. Its transmission-value set is exactly {12,15,18}. Both asserted cardinalities are three, not two. This pays both conjuncts of the literal/source conjecture, not merely a helper statement or an endpoint convention.

Beyond the initial witness: take S_m=K_(1,m), m>=2, and its Cartesian d-th power, d>=2. Coordinate distances add by the same projection/concatenation argument. Every edge has closer-side sizes m(m+1)^(d-1) and (m+1)^(d-1); the ratio is m>1. A vertex with r leaf coordinates has degree dm-r(m-1) and transmission (m+1)^(d-1)[dm+r(m-1)], for each integer 0<=r<=d. There exist vertices for every r, and the difference between successive values is nonzero. Thus both value sets have exactly d+1 elements. This symbolic argument is not a programmed sweep. It shows that the failed inference from two edge-side counts to two global levels has arbitrarily large failure. The source's separate direct/tensor product discussion (PDF pp5–8) uses adjacency in BOTH coordinates and cannot prohibit this Cartesian example.

## 343496: full entropic counterexample, structural diagnosis, and complete pre-target bridge

Source: Nowak, arXiv:1908.04966v1, 14 August 2019. Definitions2.4/Remark2.5 use xy=xR+y(1-R), R an additive automorphism, and the Mendelsohn condition R^2-R+I=0. Definitions5.6–5.7 identify converse with opposite multiplication. Conjecture5.10, PDF p25, says self-converse iff no prime dividing |M| is 1 mod3. It does not assume indecomposability of the linear MTS. Lemma5.8 and Theorem5.9 on PDF pp24–25 are read, including their proof bodies.

Take M=F_7 direct-sum F_7, an abelian group of order49, and R(x1,x2)=(3x1,5x2). Both3 and5 are units, 1-3=5 and1-5=3 mod7, and 3^2-3+1=7, 5^2-5+1=21. Thus R and I-R are automorphisms and R^2-R+I=0. Define
 x*y=(3x1+5y1,5x2+3y2) mod7.
Each left and right translation is bijective since its variable coefficients are3,5, with inverses5,3. The operation is idempotent since3+5=1 mod7. In general for commuting A=R and B=I-R, AB=I and A+B^2=0; hence y*(x*y)=(A+B^2)y+BAx=x. The other cyclic form (x*y)*x=y follows from A^2+B=0 and AB=I. Mediality follows by expanding both (x*y)*(z*w) and (x*z)*(y*w): their four coefficients are A^2,AB,BA,B^2, with AB=BA. Therefore the structure is entropic and Mendelsohn under the actual definitions.

For distinct x,y the point z=x*y is distinct from both. For if z=x then B(y-x)=0, and if z=y then A(x-y)=0; invertibility contradicts distinctness. The identities show y*z=x and z*x=y, so the cyclic triples (x,y,x*y) supply a Mendelsohn triple system: every ordered distinct pair has precisely its prescribed third point. No unproved quasigroup-to-design bridge is needed.

Let J(x1,x2)=(x2,x1), an additive involutive bijection. Direct substitution gives J(x*y)=J(y)*J(x), since J R=(I-R)J. Thus J is an isomorphism to the converse; it also respects divisions, which are opposite multiplication in a Mendelsohn quasigroup. Reversing a cyclic block maps to a cyclic block by this same identity. The system is self-converse, while7 divides49 and7=1 mod3. This is an admissible finite counterexample to the necessity direction of the full conjecture.

The initially tempting inference that a non-self-converse factor forces its product non-self-converse fails. More generally, for any quasigroup Q, Q times Q^op is self-converse by swapping the two coordinates: the product multiplication is (a,b)*(c,d)=(a*c,d*b), and swapping gives the opposite product of the swapped arguments. Here Q has multiplication3x+5y on F7 and is not self-converse: a converse isomorphism would conjugate multiplication3 to multiplication5 in the abelian group Aut(C7), impossible. The product nevertheless is self-converse. In Lemma5.8 necessity, restricting an isomorphism to a coordinate factor need not map it into THAT same factor. J exchanges them. This explicitly locates the failure; no upstream OpenAI theorem is challenged.

The deeper split-primary mechanism is that if R is diagonalizable over a split field, a conjugator from R to I-R exchanges the eigenspaces for roots r and1-r. Their multiplicities must agree, since J maps the r-eigenspace bijectively onto the (1-r)-eigenspace. Conversely paired bases give such an involution. Thus balanced pairs can be self-converse even at primes1 mod3; presence of such a prime alone cannot be the obstruction. This is a mechanism, not a claim of a full classification over all finite abelian groups or ramified modules.
