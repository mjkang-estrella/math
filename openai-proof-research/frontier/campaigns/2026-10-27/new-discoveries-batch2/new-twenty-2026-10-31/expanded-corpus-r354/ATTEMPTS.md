## 346958 — cylinder extension, with the base kept separate

Primary: Basu–Jiang, arXiv:2006.11587, Section 4, Conjecture 2. Read the definition of H_k as the intersection of integer hulls of ALL rational k-halfspace relaxations, and P_k using projected lattices, not ambient integral points in an arbitrary irrational subspace. v1/v2 conjecture passages and v3 lines 412–465 read. v2 and v3 explicitly assert the n=k case follows from the preceding discussion. Bell RM-74-014 acquired; selected Theorems 3.3–3.5 and the parity/descent proof of 3.4 read. The extraction has substantial OCR degradation; this is NOT verification of a witness for all rational relaxations.

Exact conditional bridge. Fix k and a nonempty rational R in R^k satisfying H_k(R) != R_I, and put P=R x R^(n-k). A valid halfspace a.x+b.y<=c containing P must have b=0, since every y is free. Consequently every k-halfspace relaxation of P is Q x R^(n-k), where Q is a k-halfspace relaxation of R, and conversely. The integer hull of this product is Q_I x R^(n-k): one inclusion is immediate; for the other write a point in Q_I as a finite convex combination of integral points of Q, and write any free real vector as a convex combination of integer vertices of its surrounding unit cube. Products of the two sets of weights give the required convex combination. Hence H_k(P)=H_k(R) x R^(n-k).

For any rational k-dimensional projection L, projecting P's integer points gives points of the projected lattice inside its projection. Convexity therefore gives P_I inside every lifted projected integer hull, so P_I subset P_k(P). The coordinate projection onto the first k coordinates is among those tested and its lifted integer hull is R_I x R^(n-k). Since P_I is exactly that product, P_k(P)=R_I x R^(n-k). Any point in H_k(R) minus R_I lifts to a point in H_k(P) minus P_k(P). This proves the entire n>=k extension, conditional on the exact k-dimensional base.

Failed shortcut and repair: padding R by zero coordinates would introduce new valid halfspaces; unrestricted product coordinates avoid that problem. Restricting only to facet-based corner relaxations does NOT establish membership in all arbitrary k-halfspace relaxation hulls. That base quantifier is not silently paid by the Bell title or source assertion. Thus the extension is a routine bridge, zero credit, and the stronger base source gate remains open. A formal cylinder lemma alone would be a helper and is not dispatched.

## 348057 — complete elementary face-convention obstruction

Primary: Engstrom arXiv:2009.11077v1, definitions in introduction and Conjecture 1.6, lines 98–123 read, with surrounding independence-complex material. The source explicitly says the edge polytope of C5 is a simplex whereas a pentagon can work. Merely showing that the edge polytope fails is therefore no refutation.

Take the path on vertices 1,2,3,4 and edges 12,23,34. It has no cycles, hence no forbidden induced cycle. Its independence complex consists of four vertices and the three edges 13,14,24 and has no higher simplex: every three-vertex subset contains an edge of the original path. This complex is the tree 3–1–4–2, hence contractible, with reduced Euler characteristic 4-3-1=0.

If P has exactly one vertex for each of the three graph edges and satisfies the incidence-respecting face correspondence including the whole polytope as a face, its full face contains all three labels. The corresponding induced subgraph must contain both endpoints of every edge, thus all four vertices; it is P4 itself. Its reduced independence Euler characteristic is zero, contradicting the required +/-1. This is a complete literal obstruction under the usual full-face convention, not a difficult topological result.

Sustained alternatives: (1) A proper-face-only interpretation cannot rescue the printed C5 example: the full C5 also has nonzero reduced independence Euler characteristic (its independence complex is a 5-cycle), whereas the proper faces of the pentagon account only for edges and three-vertex paths. (2) A regular cell complex consisting of two segments joined at a vertex can accommodate the P4 incidence pattern: its three vertices carry the graph's edges and its two segments correspond to the two induced three-vertex paths. Thus replacing a single polytope by a suitable cell complex changes the endpoint materially. (3) Isolated vertices also create empty-edge-label ambiguities, so cardinality-only matchings must not replace the printed incidence condition.

Gate: this elementary face/endpoint correction receives zero credit under the task. No candidate, Pi plan or claim of literature novelty. The source's full homotopy conjectures and arbitrary regular-cell realizability remain unresolved here.

## 347887 — exact integral extension constraints and failed routes

Primary: Cavenagh–Mammoliti–Wanless arXiv:2009.03475v1, Conjecture 3; Section 5 definitions and Lemmas 20–22 read, including the printed nonextendable triples (10). The 2021 journal body was actually read at Conjecture 3, Theorem 19 and its nearby example. These agree that Theorem 19 covers n=2m with m even. A nonextendable triple whose inputs are not pairwise orthogonal is not an in-scope maximal pair. Later citing/companion 2511.00921, definitions and Section 2.2 with proof of Theorems 2.4/2.5 read. Journal 2026 Theorems 3.2/3.3 and proofs read; numbering and construction differ from preprint.

Let A,B be the two binary squares of order 2m. A third binary square is exactly a 0/1 matrix T with every row sum and column sum m, and scalar products <T,A>=<T,B>=m^2. Since A,B each have 2m^2 ones, these two equalities ensure all four pair counts with each input are m^2. Conversely orthogonality implies the scalar-product equalities. Therefore this is the full exact existence endpoint, not a proxy.

Route 1: XOR. For T=A xor B, pairwise orthogonality makes each of the four joint classes have m^2 cells. Within A=1, XOR is 1 exactly on the (1,0) class, giving m^2, and similarly for B; hence the two GLOBAL orthogonality equations hold. However if row r has h simultaneous ones, XOR has 2(m-h) ones in that row. Row balance would require h=m/2, impossible when m is odd. Thus XOR fails exactly on the outstanding parity. Complementing it cannot repair that failure. Replacing XOR by a single Boolean function of (A,B) cannot bypass local balance without further structure.

Route 2: balanced-row gluing. A partition of the rows into sets on which a binary frequency rectangle orthogonal to both restrictions exists yields T by stacking. Each rectangle has half ones in each column, so column totals add to m; every row is balanced; the scalar products add to m^2. This is the full proof of the source Lemma 20 bridge. For pairs, choose m columns C, put ones in the first row on C and in the second on its complement. Orthogonality to A is equivalent to sum_{c in C}(A[r,c]-A[s,c])=0; the analogous B difference must also vanish. Thus an exact sufficient lemma is a row pairing admitting such m-element column subsets for every pair, or larger groups whose signed discrepancy vectors cancel as in Lemma 22. No general such pairing/cancellation theorem for odd m was proved. The source expressly imposes n=0 mod4 for its subsequent classification, so transferring that theorem is unjustified.

Route 3: transportation/integrality. The fractional matrix T=1/2 satisfies all displayed equations. The row/column-only transportation polytope is integral, but adding the two arbitrary weighted equations does not preserve that theorem automatically. Decomposing the fractional point into balanced 0/1 matrices gives only an average pair of scalar products (m^2,m^2); it does not imply one member realizes both. Missing lemma: integral feasibility of this constrained transportation slice for every pair of orthogonal balanced A,B. No total-unimodularity or exchange argument was established. This is the substantive remaining problem, not a solver plan.

Comparator check on the new DK examples: their displayed construction has d-1 squares and n=d*lambda. A pair would force d=3; half frequency would force lambda=n/2 and hence d=2. These cannot both hold. The square-free lambda=1 theorem likewise gives no general pair at half frequency. This rules out that explicit construction as a subsumer/counterexample; it does not clear all later literature.

## 381916 — source-algebra bridge and sign conflict (supplement)

Read 2212.10586v2 Theorem 1.2, Theorem 5.11 with coefficient definitions (5.2)–(5.5), and Conjecture 5.13; v1 Conjecture 6.13 has the same negative last formula. Source coefficients w_(2k,k,i)=C_k binom(k-1,i-1) binom(k,i). For k>=2, write N_(k-1)(t) with coefficient binom(k-1,i)binom(k-1,i-1)/(k-1) at t^i, matching the source's peak convention. The candidate plus coefficient is C_k binom(k-1,i)binom(k-1,i-1); the coefficient of t times the candidate minus polynomial is C_k binom(k-1,i-1)^2. Their sum is the displayed w coefficient by Pascal's identity. Endpoint coefficients vanish with the usual binomial-zero convention. The plus coefficients are symmetric relative to degree k, and minus relative to k-1, by binomial symmetry. Such a decomposition is unique: a difference A=-tB with A k-symmetric and B (k-1)-symmetric gives A=-B by reversal, hence (1-t)B=0 in Z[t] and B=A=0. Thus the first two formulas are a routine complete source bridge. For k=1, W_(2,1)=t gives plus=0, minus=1 directly from the unique Dyck path UUDD.

The last formula has -t/2 times positive binomial and generalized Narayana polynomial for k>=3, while Theorem 5.11 defines W+ as a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients. Its t coefficient is strictly negative: the generalized Narayana constant is (2/(k-1))*binom(k-1,2)>0. Therefore the literal conjunction contains a sign conflict already in source, rather than a substantive new theorem. Formula's k=2 generalized Narayana index also needs a boundary convention.
