1081: 6.1. Double Sixes on Cubics. Every double six on a cubic surface—whether extremal or not—
1082: is a union of two quadric configurations. For an arbitrary double six A ∪ B on a cubic surface X,
1083: there is a choice of coordinates making X the blowup of six points on P2 (no three on a line, not
1084: all on a conic), and so that A consists of the six lines of exceptional divisors {E1 , . . . , E6 } and
1085: B consists of the proper transforms {C̃1 , . . . , C̃6 } of the six conics in P2 through five of the six
1086: points [Nag60, Thm 8, pg. 366]. Here, C̃i denotes the proper transform of the conic that misses
1087: the point blown up to get Ei .
