## Independent proof from definitions, without mathematical code

Let E={a,b,c,d}, and let the independent sets be exactly those of cardinality at most one. Heredity holds. For augmentation, unequal independent cardinalities force the smaller set to be empty and the larger to be a singleton, whose element augments it. Thus this is a matroid; its maximal independent sets are precisely the four singletons, so its rank is one. It is U1,4. It is loopless and connected, though not simple; simplicity is not a source hypothesis.

Take B=({a},{b},{c},{d}) and C=({b},{a},{d},{c}). All entries are bases. Every ground element occurs exactly once in each multiset union, so compatibility holds. The bases within either tuple are even pairwise disjoint and exhaust E. B and C differ at all four indexed positions; hence zero moves do not suffice.

Suppose one legal move gives C. Every changed position must be among the selected distinct positions, so all four positions must be selected, and q=4. Each outgoing element is forced by its singleton source. At selected position i_j the new singleton contains the original element at position i_(j+1). Thus the map from a receiving position to the original supplying position is a four-cycle. But the desired map is (1 2)(3 4). Its square fixes each position, whereas the square of a four-cycle fixes none of its four positions. This is impossible. Reversing the orientation convention merely inverts the four-cycle and does not change the contradiction. There is no freedom to select repeated elements here, even though the general definition need not forbid them.

First exchange positions 1,2 and then positions 3,4. Each is a permitted q=2 cyclic exchange and each resulting entry is still a singleton basis. This gives C in two moves. Hence the distance is exactly 2>1. A path of length at most rank one is either length zero or length one, both already excluded; there is no unexamined longer path within the bound.

This refutes precisely the unweighted arbitrary ordered-sequence single-cycle bound. It does not refute Hamidoune's k=2 conjecture, White's existence conjecture, the split-matroid ordering theorem, the weighted conjecture, or the precursor's parallel-round interpretation. In particular, do not infer anything about weighted total cost merely from needing two moves.

## Complete earlier-theorem adaptation: the exclusion bridge

The following dictionary works for every k and every distinct-singleton ordering, not just this four-element list.

1. Put the rank-one uniform matroid on a finite k-element set E. Label its elements x_1,...,x_k and use the ordered starting tuple A_i={x_i}.
2. All compatible target tuples are exactly A^p_i={x_(p(i))}, for p in S_k: compatibility gives each distinct label once. Conversely every permutation gives a compatible target. No matroid feasibility test remains: every singleton is a basis.
3. A single source move selects a list of distinct positions. Because the outgoing labels are forced, its effect on the receiving-to-supplying map is exactly the cycle on that list, fixing the complement. Conversely each such cycle is a legal move. This correspondence is both directions and allows every orientation. Allowing a length-one cycle only adds the identity.
4. Therefore reachability in at most one source move is equivalent to the relative permutation being identity or having exactly one nontrivial cycle in its disjoint-cycle decomposition. This proves the entire one-move relation on this subfamily, with no residual hypothesis or case search.
5. Apply Goodman's Theorem 1.5.3 (or Wildon's Lemma 14.4): a product of two disjoint nontrivial cycles has two uniquely determined nontrivial cycle factors, and cannot also be a single cycle. Such permutations exist for every k>=4, by taking two disjoint transpositions and fixing the rest. Thus the conjectured rank-one bound fails generically throughout that subfamily. The two disjoint cycle factors themselves yield two legal moves for the displayed witness.
6. Rank=1 makes the conjectured conclusion exactly the one-move relation of step 4. No separate diameter theorem, unknown matroid property, or experimental invariant is being added to the old permutation theorem. It would be incorrect to claim that the number of disjoint cycles always equals the minimum number of arbitrary cycle moves; only the zero/one characterization and this explicit two-factor upper bound are used.

The bridge is a routine replacement of distinct labels by singleton sets, plus an exact reading of the source formula. It gives a generic obstruction before any numerical datum or special object is investigated. I recommend applying the written “routine corollary” exclusion on this basis. I am NOT claiming Goodman or Wildon discussed this conjecture, nor that their theorem's publication is itself a direct prior refutation.
