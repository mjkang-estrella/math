## 375477: complete routine bridge, excluded

Exact endpoint: for every finite non-empty graph F and n >= |V(F)|+1, equality sat(n,K1 join F)=n-1+sat(n-1,F) implies existence of an extremal saturated graph with a vertex of degree n-1. Subgraphs are ordinary, not induced; isolated vertices of F are allowed. Source: Hua–Peng, arXiv:2606.22006 v1 and v2, Conjecture 5.1, definitions in Section 1. Companion/earlier theorem: Cameron–Puleo arXiv:2004.05410v2, Lemma 5 and its actual construction proof, journal Discrete Math. 345 (2022), 112867 as cited by Hua–Peng.

Existence of an F-saturated graph on n-1 vertices follows by taking an inclusion-maximal F-free graph in the finite set of graphs on that vertex set. The empty graph is F-free because F has an edge. Choose S minimizing its edge count. Form T by adjoining a new universal vertex u.

Here is the nontrivial freeness check, rather than merely invoking the upper-bound inequality. Suppose an injective edge-preserving embedding f of K1 join F into T exists, and let c be the join vertex in its domain. If u is absent from the image, restricting to F embeds F in S. If f(c)=u, the same restriction embeds F in S. In the remaining case u=f(v) for v in F. Define an embedding g of F into S by g(v)=f(c), and g(w)=f(w) for all w != v. It is injective since f is injective and c is outside F. An F-edge not incident with v is preserved by f. If vw is an F-edge, c is adjacent to w in the join, so f(c)f(w) is an edge of T with both endpoints in S, hence an edge of S. This again embeds F in S, a contradiction. This argument includes isolated vertices without any extra assumption.

Every missing edge xy of T lies within S. Saturation of S produces an F-copy in S+xy. Together with u this gives a K1 join F-copy in T+xy. Thus T is saturated and has exactly n-1+sat(n-1,F) edges. Under the literal equality assumption it is extremal, and u has full degree. This proves the entire exact implication.

A second route through deleting a universal vertex gives no stronger statement: if an extremal T has universal u, then T-u is F-free. For every missing xy, a K1 join F-copy in T+xy can be turned into an F-copy in (T-u)+xy by the same center-replacement argument (or restricted if u is absent). Thus T-u is F-saturated and e(T)>=n-1+sat(n-1,F). With the general upper bound this gives equality. It explains why the literal claim is exactly the equality case of the pre-existing construction, rather than new structure forced on every extremizer. It does NOT assert every extremizer contains a universal vertex.

Qualification: source and literal scope agree. Novelty/significance fail by the full explicit Cameron–Puleo construction bridge above, not because the proof is short. The source's own open label does not change this. No Pi plan.

## 366852: radius-controlled obstruction route, incomplete

Exact endpoint is equivalence of all three clauses of source Conjecture 4.3, including possibly infinite graphs and every integer r>=3. The source ball is not simply the induced closed neighborhood: vertices have distance <=r/2, and an edge xy is included when d(v,x)+d(v,y)<r. The strict edge condition matters for even r. Actual latest-source definition and conjecture were read. Original v1 was fetched, but its selected series-parallel search did not yield a reviewed corresponding passage. A later companion, 2512.19044, is about chordal global representations; only its introductory body was reviewed, not its full proofs. All-version and citing-body qualification remains incomplete.

Route A: prove (i) implies (ii). If H is an r-local wheel or K4 subdivision centered at v, any H-path is also a G-path. Thus distances from v in G are no greater than in H. The vertex and strict edge bounds placing all of H in B_H(v,r/2) also place H in B_G(v,r/2). A wheel has a K4 minor: choose three rim vertices, contract the three intervening rim arcs to a triangle and retain the hub and three spokes. Subdivision does not remove that minor. K4 subdivisions likewise have a K4 minor. Consequently that ball is not series-parallel. This is a complete necessary-direction deduction only, receiving no credit.

Route B: try to reverse this using a K4 subdivision inside a non-series-parallel ball. Since K4 has maximum degree three, a K4 minor can be converted to a topological K4 model by choosing minimal branch trees and their junctions. However ambient small radius does NOT imply small intrinsic radius in the model. Shortest paths to the ambient center may leave the model. Adding those paths can create intersecting spokes and branch sets. One must prove a reduction that yields either an intrinsically r-local K4 subdivision or an intrinsically r-local wheel subdivision while preserving the strict edge bound. No such reduction was obtained. Choosing a minimal model does not itself guarantee distance preservation, and suppressing degree-two vertices changes the relevant lengths. This is the first precise missing lemma.

Route C: use the r-local cover. A local isomorphism on the specified balls transports K4 minors in those balls, so a series-parallel cover implies local series-parallelity (source also notes this for ball-closed classes). The hard reverse asks that every finite K4 model in the cover can be localized or ruled out from local constraints. Ordinary universal-cover arguments cannot be used: the r-local cover need not be a tree, and shortening a closed walk in the ambient graph need not preserve the branch model. For infinite graphs any eventual treewidth argument must include a valid compactness/decomposition bridge. No complete cover-to-obstruction lemma survives. Park exact full equivalence; no finite proxy or Pi plan.

## 382260: weighted complement route, incomplete

Exact claim concerns all low-betweenness BUGs, not just those with betweenness <=9/10. Read arXiv:2401.00347v1, definitions and actual Lemma 8, Corollary 9, Lemma 15 and Theorem 17 proof bodies. The live journal preview (Discrete Applied Mathematics 395, 19–34, DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2026.07.018, issue dated 31 December 2026) still describes <=9/10 as the theorem and <1 as the conjecture; its full body was denied 403. The issue date is future relative to this run; it is not evidence of a new future resolution. Current theorem numbering differs from v1. Journal proof and correction comparison remains a material HOLD.

Let H be the complement on n vertices, c_e=|N_H(x) union N_H(y)| for e=xy. At diameter two, nonedges xy of G have n-c_e common neighbors, all shortest xy-paths have length two, and vertex v contributes 1/(n-c_e) exactly when v is not close to e. Thus B_G(v)=sum_e 1/(n-c_e)-sum_{e close to v}1/(n-c_e). Uniformity is precisely constancy of the latter weighted sum. Summing all betweennesses gives |E(H)|, since every nonedge contributes total one over its internal vertices. Hence the common value is |E(H)|/n; low betweenness gives |E(H)|<n. There is a tree component. The source's positive-weight strict-inclusion lemma forces each tree component to be a star. For a star K1,l every vertex is close to all l edges and all those edges have c_e=l+1, giving coB=l/(n-l-1). Equality between two star components forces their l to agree by cross-multiplication (n>1).

Route A beyond that shortcut: combine a star with a non-star (m,t)-uniform component. Equating coB yields l/(n-l-1)=m/(n-t), hence n=l+1+l(l+1-t)/(m-l). The source Lemma 15 supplies m>l, t<l+1. Theorem 17 sums component excesses and uses e(H_component)/|V(H_component)|=m/t to derive total e-n>=0, contradicting low betweenness. This completes only the uniform-component obstruction and is already public. It does not justify treating an arbitrary component as uniform. In particular, the source explicitly requires non-uniform components for any exotic coBUG.

Route B: attempt to remove uniformity by averaging. For a non-star component C the exact identity obtained by summing coB over its vertices is sum_{e in E(C)} c_e/(n-c_e)=|V(C)| l/(n-l-1). This scalar identity retains the entire distribution of c_e. Replacing c_e by an average inside the reciprocal loses equality information; Jensen gives one side only, without a bound connecting the required excess to the number of star components. The missing inequality must use vertexwise weighted balance, not merely the edge mean, and must force total excess >=0 whenever a non-star component is present.

Route C: expand 1/(n-c_e) as a positive geometric series. Then equal coB at vertices implies equality of sums of all weighted moments at this one value n. It does NOT imply equality of each moment or unweighted close-edge counts. Cancellation of differences between vertices is still possible across moment orders, despite individual positive summands. Varying n would change the graph and the equations. Thus coefficient comparison is invalid. This blocks a tempting reduction of the general case to Route A. Source's finite small-star exclusions cannot settle unbounded l; no enumeration was performed. No full endpoint, novelty clearance or Pi plan.
