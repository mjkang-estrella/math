Abstract
We show that the polar degrees of the coordinate-wise inverse of a linear subspace are given by the coefficients of a substitution of the reduced characteristic polynomial of the associated matroid . Our proof connects the geometry of conormal varieties of reciprocal linear spaces to the combinatorial conormal fan of . As a corollary, we settle two open conjectures regarding matroid discriminants.

1 Introduction
Let be a linear subspace of dimension not contained in any coordinate hyperplane and consider the rational Cremona map
The closure of the image of under this map is denoted and is called the reciprocal linear space associated to . This a projective variety whose geometric properties are closely related to the realisable matroid represented by . Reciprocal linear spaces have been widely studied both from the viewpoint of likelihood geometry in algebraic statistics and scattering equations [20, 15, 9, 21] and from the viewpoint of matroid theory [27, 31, 19, 16]. In this article we study through the lens of projective duality. The central object in our work is the conormal variety whose general points are pairs where is a smooth point and is a hyperplanes tangent to at . The projectively dual variety of is the image of under the projection to the second factor .
Our initial motivation for studying these varieties stems from a collection of recent conjectures by Matsubara-Heo and Telen in their work on principal matroid determinants [26]. They introduce an analogue of the theory of principal -determinants for toric varieties by Gelfand, Kapranov and Zelevinsky [18] to the setting of reciprocal linear spaces, thereby defining the principal matroid determinant . The key idea is that the stratification of toric varieties into orbits is replaced by the stratification of reciprocal linear spaces into strata corresponding to flats of the corresponding matroid . The main theorem [26, Theorem 1.2] shows how the principal matroid determinant decomposes into a union of dual varieties of reciprocal linear spaces of minors of . Especially in recent years, expressing combinatorial invariants as geometric and tropical intersection numbers turned out to be a powerful tool for resolving longstanding conjectures in matroid theory [19, 1, 10, 5]. The theorem above can be seen as another work in this line of tradition as it expresses the coefficients of a substitution of as intersection numbers on the conormal variety. Following this theme, Theorem A could be used to show the log-concavity of the coefficients of for realizable matroids. We note however that this log-concavity follows more directly from the log-concavity of the coefficients of which was proven in the groundbreaking work of Huh and Katz for realizable matroids [19] and Adiprasito, Huh and Katz for all matroids [1].
Our proof of Theorem A uses a rational parametrization of described in [26], akin to the Horn–Kapranov uniformization. We resolve the base locus of this map by passing from a product of projective spaces to the bipermutohedral variety. The latter is a smooth projective toric variety whose corresponding fan hosts the conormal fan of every matroid, introduced in [5]. We prove Theorem A in three steps that can be summarized as follows:
1.
Using tropical methods we pass from the rational parametrization of to a similar, but slightly simpler map with a different image while tracking how this affects polar degrees (Section 3.1).
2.
We show that these new degrees can be computed as intersection numbers on the bipermutohedral variety that involve the class of the conormal fan of . From this we derive a first combinatorial formula which sums over all decreasing flags of flats of (Section 3.2).
3.
Finally, we extract a recursion for the complicated combinatorial formula obtained in the previous step to relate the polar degrees to a substitution of the reduced characteristic polynomial (Section 3.3).
The structure of this article is as follows. Section 2 serves as an introduction to the tools that we will use in proving Theorem A. We also provide direct proofs of Conjectures 1.1 and 1.2. Especially the latter showcases our technique in a slightly simpler setting before we apply it to the conormal variety. The detailed proof of Theorem A is then presented in Section 3, which is subdivided further into the three parts mentioned above. We finish the article providing several applications of Theorem A in Section 4. This section contains examples, a discussion of Chern–Mather degrees of and a generalization of Theorem A to arbitrary negative powers of linear spaces , .
