On conjectures concerning the graph grabbing game
Lawrence Hollom
Address: Department of Pure Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics (DPMMS), University of Cambridge, Wilberforce Road, Cambridge, CB3 0WA, United Kingdom
Email address: lh569@cam.ac.uk
Abstract.
We consider two conjectures made in regard to the graph grabbing game, played on a vertex weighted graph.
Seacrest and Seacrest conjectured in 2012 that the first player can win the graph grabbing game on any even-order bipartite graph.
Eoh and Choi conjectured a strengthening of this in 2019, namely that the first player can win on any graph with no induced corona product of an odd cycle and a point.
We provide a family of counterexamples to the latter conjecture, and propose a weaker conjecture in its place.
We also show that the above two conjectures are equivalent when the vertex weights are all or .
1. Introduction
The graph-grabbing game has two players, referred to as Alice and Bob.
They play on a graph with a weight function , alternately removing vertices of so that the remaining vertices induce a connected subgraph of , with Alice going first.
Once all vertices are removed, the players compute their score, equal to the total weight of all vertices they removed.
Alice and Bob both aim to maximise their score, and we will say that Alice has a winning strategy, or simply that Alice wins if she has a strategy guaranteeing that she collects at least half the total weight.
The game was introduced in Winkler’s puzzle book [6], in which he asked for a proof that Alice could win when is an even-order path.
Micek and Walczak [4] then considered general trees of even order, proving that Alice can secure at least a quarter of the total weight.
This was in turn improved by Seacrest and Seacrest [5], who supplied an elegant proof that Alice can always win on any tree of even order.
They furthermore made the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.1 ([5] Conjecture 5).
Alice can win the graph-grabbing game played on a bipartite graph of even order.
The conjecture remains open, but there has been partial progress; Egawa, Enomoto, and Matsumoto [2] resolved the case of graphs they called -trees (a copy of with trees attached to its vertices).
Eoh and Choi [3] considered the situation where the weight function has codomain .
In this case, they were able to show that Alice wins whenever has no induced subgraph isomorphic to the corona product of a cycle and a point.
They also made the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.2 ([3]).
If is the family of corona products of an odd cycle and a point, then Alice wins the graph grabbing game if and only if is -free.
More recently, Doki, Egawa, and Matsumoto [1] found, among other results, an example of a graph containing an induced subgraph, yet on which Alice could still always win, ruling out one direction of Conjecture 1.2.
We give a family of graphs which are not -free, yet there is some weight function for which Alice loses, resolving the other direction of Conjecture 1.2 in the negative.
However, in Section 3 we consider Conjecture 1.2 in the special case of -weighted graphs, and show that it is equivalent to Conjecture 1.1 in this case.
Finally, we give a new conjecture on when Alice can guarantee to win at least half of the weight.

Having demonstrated that Conjecture 1.2 does not hold in general, we propose the following weaker conjecture in its place.

If a graph of even order has no induced subgraph isomorphic to a member of the family (as shown in Figure 2), then Alice wins the graph grabbing game on .
