Abstract
A multiloop with strands is a generic immersion of the union of circles into a surface , considered up to homeomorphisms.
A pinning set of is a set of points , such that in the punctured surface , the immersion has the minimal number of double points in its homotopy class.
Its pinning number is the minimum cardinal of its pinning sets.
In any fixed orientable surface , the pinning problem which given a multiloop and decides whether has been show to be NP-complete, even in restrictions to loops (with strand).
In this work we study the complexity of the pinning problem in restriction to multiloops whose strands are simple (embedded circles).
We show that in any fixed oriented surface , the problem is in P when and NP-complete when , and present some follow-up questions and conjectures.

We say that is a pinning set for when , namely when is taut in .
The collection of pinning sets of forms the pinning ideal , that is a poset under inclusion which is absorbing under union.

0.3.3 SAT attacks
The observations in Section 1.2 show that when is orientable there is a polynomial time algorithm which converts a loop or simple multiloop to a positive boolean formula in conjunctive normal form (CNF) called its mobidisc formula (Definition 1.10), and that the pinning sets of correspond to the satisfying assignments of . The problem of computing all minimal pinning sets of a multiloop is thus reduced to the problem of dualizing by converting it from a CNF to a logically equivalent positive disjunctive normal form (DNF).
Thus, one may ask whether SAT-solvers suited for such positive CNFs may be used to compute the minimum cardinal of their solutions more efficiently than expected. In practice, the current SAT-solvers for these problems are surprisingly fast (see for instance [18, 20]).
Question 0.13 (SAT-solvers heuristics).
The positive CNFs arising from multiloops are not arbitrary as they come from loops in surfaces: this adds constraints given by (the genus of) the surface. Can one formulate a precise structural result in this direction?
Can one tune a SAT-solver to find heuristics exploiting that surface structure of these motonone CNFs to solve them faster?
0.3.4 Random models: average parameters and complexity
Question 0.14 (average complexity).
Given a random model of multiloops in surfaces, what is the “average” complexity of the pinning problems as measured by the aforementioned SAT solvers?
Let us sketch a few random models of simple multiloops in a fixed or variable surface.
The random state model.
Our first random model constructs, given a surface and integers , a random simple multiloop of length (in a sense that will be clear) bounded by .
For simplicity assume that is orientable and add punctures to obtain a new surface whose fundamental group is free (a sphere with punctures, a torus with puncture or any surface of genus ), and choose a negatively curved (say hyperbolic) metric on .
Choose a free presentation of its fundamental group, and choose a random word of length in the generators: there is a unique closed geodesic for the associated hyperbolic geodesic for any chosen hyperbolic metric on .
Finally, label the intersections and resolve them according to the following procedure so as to obtain a union of simple loops: while some strands have some self-intersections, resolve their self-intersection with the smallest label in the unique way that increases the number of strands.
The random spin model.
Our second random model first constructs for given a size parameter , a random surface of size : choose a random -valent multigraph with vertices and a random cyclic order on the vertices yielding a banded surface to which you may glue discs to obtain a closed surface.
Then construct a simple multiloop by choosing a random Euler multicycle of the embedded multigraph (for instance by taking a random walk on the edges and burning edges as they are used), and finally resolve self-intersections between strands as in the previous model.
The random braid model.
Our third model constructs in any surface , for any parameter , a random simple multiloop with strands using the mapping class group action.
First choose a simple loop in , then add several punctures in each region and apply a random element of the pure braid group of the punctured surface. Continue the process times, starting with the initial simple loop with different punctures and applying a random pure braid. Finally erase all punctures.

1 General results for simple multiloops
In this section, we first recall some terminology about Boolean formulae in conjunctive normal form that are positive.
This will serve to set up a correspondence between the pinning sets of loops or simple multiloops and the satisfying assignments of such a formula.
After reviewing the Vertex Cover problem and a few of its variants, we use these ideas to prove a preliminary result (Theorem 1.19) that pinning simple multiloops with arbitrary many strands is NP-complete.
1.1 Boolean formulae in conjunctive normal form
Definition 1.1 (formulae and satisfying assignments).
Consider a set of variables which may take boolean values .
A formula is an expression in the variables involving negations , disjunctions , and conjunctions (and parentheses).
Evaluating the variables at yields a value for the formula .
Such an assignment of the variables is said to satisfy when .
Two formulae on the set of variables are called logically equivalent when their evaluations on every assignment of the variables are equal .
It is a classical observation that every formula is equivalent to a conjunctive normal form, which we now define.
Definition 1.2 (positive conjunctive normal forms).
Consider a set of boolean variables .
A clause is a formula of the form where each is a literal, namely a variable or its negation .
A conjunctive normal form (abbreviated CNF) is a formula which is conjunction of clauses .
A conjunctive normal form is called positive (or monotone) when none of the clauses feature negated variables.
Definition 1.3 (solution ideal).
Consider a positive conjunctive normal form over a set of boolean variables .
For an assignment of satisfying , we call a solution of .
The solutions of form an ideal : a sub-poset which is absorbing under union, and it contains the whole set of all elements .
Remark 1.4 (pruning).
By definition if two positive CNF are equivalent then their solution ideals are isomorphic.
Thus to compute the solution ideal, we may first “prune” it by retaining only those innermost clauses which are implied by no other clause.
Remark 1.5 (hypergraph vertex cover).
The solutions of a positive conjunctive normal form correspond to the vertex covers of the hypergraph whose vertices are indexed by the variables and hyperedges correspond to the clauses.
