Saturation numbers of some joins of graphs
Xinying Hua Yuejian Peng
††thanks: E-mail addresses: xyhuamath@163.com (X. Hua), ypeng1@hnu.edu.cn (Y. Peng, corresponding author), supported in part by National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 12571363) and Natural Science Foundation of Hunan Province (Grant No. 2025JJ30003).
Affiliation: School of Mathematics, Hunan University, Changsha, Hunan, 410082, P.R. China
Abstract
Let H be a graph. A graph G is H -saturated if G is H -free, but adding any edge between two non-adjacent vertices of G yields an H -copy as a subgraph. The saturation number \mathrm{sat}(n,H) is the minimum number of edges in an H -saturated graph on n vertices.
The saturation number for the join of a vertex and a graph F , denoted by K_{1}\vee F , has attracted considerable attention.
Cameron and Puleo [Discrete Math. 345 (2022), 112867] proved that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F)\leq n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,F) for n>|V(F)| .
A natural question is when the above equality holds. Most existing results impose conditions on F and assume that F has no isolated vertices.
Let K_{p}^{-} be the graph obtained by deleting one edge from the complete graph K_{p} .
In this paper, we investigate the saturation number of K_{1}\vee F when F contains isolated vertices, and determine the exact value of \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F) when F=K^{-}_{3}\cup sK_{1}(s\geq 1) or F=K^{-}_{p-1}\cup K_{1}(p\geq 5) . In our results, \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F)=n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,F) holds when F=K^{-}_{3}\cup sK_{1} for any s\geq 1 , but fails when F=K^{-}_{p-1}\cup K_{1} for p\geq 5 .
1 Introduction
All graphs considered in this paper are simple and undirected. Let G=(V(G),E(G)) be a graph.
We use |V(G)| to denote its order and e(G)=|E(G)| to denote its number of edges. For a fixed graph F , a graph G is called F -saturated if it contains no subgraph isomorphic to F , while for every edge e\notin E(G) , adding e to G produces a copy of F as a subgraph. The saturation number of F , denoted by \mathrm{sat}(n,F) , is the minimum number of edges among all F -saturated graphs of order n . Let
\mathrm{SAT}(n,\,F):=\{G:|V(G)|=n,e(G)=\mathrm{sat}(n,F),\mbox{ and }G\mbox{ is }F\mbox{-saturated}\},
and each graph in \mathrm{SAT}(n,\,F) is called an extremal graph for F .
For any two vertex-disjoint graphs G_{1} and G_{2} , their join graph G_{1}\vee G_{2} is defined as the graph formed from the disjoint union G_{1}\cup G_{2} by adding all edges between V(G_{1}) and V(G_{2}) .
We denote the path, cycle, star, complete graph, and empty graph on n vertices by P_{n} , C_{n} , S_{n} , K_{n} , and \overline{K_{n}} , respectively. We also let K_{p}^{-} denote the graph obtained by removing one edge from the complete graph K_{p} .
The study of the saturation number of a graph was initiated by Erdős, Hajnal and Moon. In [10], they proved that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{k+1})=(k-1)n-\binom{k}{2} , with
K_{k-1}\vee\overline{K_{n-k+1}} being the unique minimum-size K_{k+1} -saturated graph of order n .
Kászonyi and Tuza [21] gave a general upper bound on \mathrm{sat}(n,H) for any graph H ,
and determined \mathrm{sat}(n,P_{k}) , \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1,k}) , and \mathrm{sat}(n,kP_{2}) .
In subsequent work, the saturation number has been investigated for a wide variety of graph families.
Examples include small cycles [5, 6, 22, 24, 25, 27], complete multipartite graphs [7, 15, 20], forests [2, 8, 11, 12, 16, 21, 23], books [4], and disjoint unions of complete graphs [3, 14, 29]. Nevertheless, results providing the exact saturation number for all n are still quite limited.
See [9] and [13] for comprehensive surveys.
The join operation is a fundamental and widely used construction in graph theory.
Existing work on the saturation number for join graphs has focused on determining \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F) from the saturation number of F .
Cameron and Puleo [1] showed that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F)\leq n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,F) for n\geq|V(F)|+1 . It is an interesting problem to determine for which graphs the above inequality is tight.
In 2024, Hu, Luo and Peng [17] proved the following theorem.
Theorem 1.1.
[17] Let s and n be positive integers, and let F be a graph without isolated vertices. Then for n\geq 3s^{2}-s+2\,\mathrm{sat}(n-s,F)+1 , we have
\displaystyle\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s}\vee F)=\binom{s}{2}+s(n-s)+\mathrm{sat}(n-s,F)\,.
In [28], Qiu, He, Lu and Xu noted that the condition on F in Theorem 1.1 implies that F must contain an isolated edge. Subsequently,
Hu, Ji and Cui [18] proved that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee P_{t})=n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,P_{t}) holds for t\geq 5 and sufficiently large n .
Song, Hu, Ji and Cui [26] proved that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee C_{4})=\lfloor\frac{5n-10}{2}\rfloor holds for all n\geq 6 .
In [28], Qiu, He, Lu and Xu further showed that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee C_{t})=n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,C_{t}) holds if t\geq 8 and n\geq 56t^{3} .
Among these results, almost all graphs F under consideration are connected graphs or disconnected graphs without isolated vertices. Motivated by this, Hua and Peng [19] considered the case in which F consists of complete graphs and some isolated vertices and obtained the exact value of \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee(K_{p-1}\cup K_{1})) for all p\geq 4 . Moreover, when p=3 , the authors extended the result to any number of isolated vertices and determined the saturation number for K_{1}\vee F when F=K_{2}\cup qK_{1} , or F=2K_{2}\cup qK_{1} for any q\geq 1 . In addition, all extremal graphs in [19] are fully characterized.
In this paper, we investigate the saturation number of K_{1}\vee F when F is the vertex-disjoint union of the nearly complete graph K_{p}^{-} and some isolated vertices.
Let
K_{p^{-}}^{+s}:=K_{1}\vee(K^{-}_{p-1}\cup sK_{1}).
Our results are as follows.
Theorem 1.2.
For n\geq s+4 , \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{4^{-}}^{+s})=\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
For s=1 , we obtain the saturation number of K_{p^{-}}^{+1} for every p\geq 5 .
Theorem 1.3.
Let p\geq 5 , n\geq p+1 and n\equiv k\pmod{p} , where k\in[0,p-1] . Then \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{p^{-}}^{+1})=\frac{(p-1)n}{2}+\frac{k(k-p)}{2} .
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some necessary definitions and notations. In Section 3, we prove Theorem 1.2. In Section 4, we prove Theorem 1.3.
We conclude with remarks in the final section.
2 Definitions and Notation
Given two disjoint sets A,B\subseteq V(G) , let e(A,B) be the number of edges in G with one endpoint in A and the other in B . Given two vertices u,v\in V(G) , the distance between u and v , denoted by d(u,v) , is the length of a shortest (u,v) -path in G . We write [n]=\{1,2,\cdots,n\} .
For each vertex v\in V(G) , let N_{G}(v) be the open neighborhood of v ,
and N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\} be its closed neighborhood. The degree of v is given by d_{G}(v):=|N_{G}(v)| , and the minimum degree of G is defined as \delta(G):=\min\limits_{v\in V(G)}d_{G}(v) .
For a vertex subset D\subseteq V(G) , we use the notation N_{D}(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap D , N_{G}(D)=\bigcup_{v\in D}N_{G}(v) , and N_{G}[D]=D\cup N_{G}(D) .
For U\subseteq V(G) , let G-U be the graph obtained by deleting the vertex set U and all edges incident to U . Also, we let G[U] be the induced subgraph of G on U .
A cut vertex of a graph is a vertex whose deletion increases the number of components.
A vertex of degree one is said to be a leaf, and the edge incident to a leaf is called a pendant edge.
3 Saturation number for K_{4^{-}}^{+s}
In this section, we consider the saturation number of K_{4^{-}}^{+s} .
Recall the statement of Theorem 1.2.
Theorem 1.2.
For n\geq s+4 , \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{4^{-}}^{+s})=\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Proof of Theorem 1.2.
If n is odd, K_{1}\vee\frac{n-1}{2}K_{2} is a K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -saturated graph with \lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil edges.
If n is even, then K_{1}\vee(K_{1}\cup\frac{n-2}{2}K_{2}) is a K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -saturated graph with \lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil edges.
Thus, \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{4^{-}}^{+s})\leq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Now, we prove the lower bound.
Let G be a minimum K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -saturated graph of order n .
Claim 3.1.
If \sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq 3n-4 , e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Proof.
Since 2e(G)=\sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u) , if \sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)=3n-4 , then 3n-4 is even and e(G)=\frac{3n-4}{2}=\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil . If \sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq 3n-3 , then e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil . This completes the proof.
∎
We divide the proof into the following two cases.
Case 1.
G is connected.
Let A_{i}=\{x\in V(G)|d_{G}(x)=i\} and a_{i}=|A_{i}| . If A_{1}\cup A_{2}=\emptyset ,
then e(G)\geq\frac{3n}{2}>\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil . So we assume that A_{1}\cup A_{2}\neq\emptyset .
Claim 3.2.
If x and y are two vertices in A_{1}\cup A_{2} , then d(x,y)\leq 2 .
Proof.
If d(x,y)\geq 3 , then G+xy contains a K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -copy containing xy , and xy must be the pendant edge of this copy, say H . Then d_{H}(x)=s+3 or d_{H}(y)=s+3 .
Furthermore, d_{G}(x)=s+2 or d_{G}(y)=s+2 .
As s\geq 1 , d_{G}(x)\geq 3 or d_{G}(y)\geq 3 , a contradiction.
∎
Claim 3.3.
a_{1}\leq 1 .
Proof.
Otherwise, assume that x and y are two leaves in G .
Since n\geq s+4 and G is connected, xy\notin E(G) .
Then G+xy has a K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -copy containing xy .
Since each edge in K_{4^{-}}^{+s} is incident with a vertex of degree at least 3, and d_{G+xy}(x)=d_{G+xy}(y)=2 ,
xy cannot be any edge in a K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -copy, a contradiction.
∎
Thus, we consider the following two cases.
Subcase 1.1.
a_{1}=1 .
If a_{2}\leq 2 , then \sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq 1+2a_{2}+3(n-1-a_{2})=3n-2-a_{2}\geq 3n-4 .
By Claim 3.1, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
If a_{2}\geq 3 , let v^{\prime} be the unique leaf in G and v be the neighbour of v^{\prime} .
By Claim 3.2, d(v^{\prime},x)\leq 2 for every x\in A_{2} .
Thus, each vertex in A_{2} is adjacent to v .
Thus, d_{G}(v)\geq a_{2}+1
and
\sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq d_{G}(v)+1+2a_{2}+3(n-2-a_{2})\geq(a_{2}+1)+1+2a_{2}+3(n-2-a_{2})=3n-4.
By Claim 3.1, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Subcase 1.2.
a_{1}=0 .
First we assume that a_{2}\leq 4 . Then \sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq 2a_{2}+3(n-a_{2})=3n-a_{2}\geq 3n-4 .
Together with Claim 3.1, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Next we consider the case when a_{2}\geq 5 .
If \cap_{u\in A_{2}}N_{G}(u)\neq\emptyset , then
G contains a vertex, say x , such that A_{2}\subseteq N_{G}(x) . So, d_{G}(x)\geq a_{2} and \sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq d_{G}(x)+2a_{2}+3(n-1-a_{2})\geq 3n-3 .
Thus, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Now we assume that \cap_{u\in A_{2}}N_{G}(u)=\emptyset .
If there exist two vertices x and y in A_{2} such that xy\in E(G) , then
N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(y)=\emptyset .
Otherwise, assume that N_{G}(x)\cap N_{G}(y)=\{z\} . Then for any v\in A_{2}\backslash\{x,y\} , since d(x,v)\leq 2 and d(y,v)\leq 2 by Claim 3.2, vz\in E(G) .
It follows that \cap_{u\in A_{2}}N_{G}(u)\neq\emptyset , a contradiction.
Let N_{G}(x)\backslash\{y\}=\{x_{1}\} and N_{G}(y)\backslash\{x\}=\{y_{1}\} , see Fig. 1 (a).
Since for any z\in A_{2}\backslash\{x,y\} , d(x,z)\leq 2 and d(y,z)\leq 2 , we have N_{G}(z)=\{x_{1},y_{1}\} .
Thus, d_{G}(x_{1})+d_{G}(y_{1})\geq 2(a_{2}-2)+1+1=2a_{2}-2 .
Hence,
\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)
\displaystyle\geq d_{G}(x_{1})+d_{G}(y_{1})+2a_{2}+3(n-2-a_{2})
\displaystyle\geq(2a_{2}-2)+2a_{2}+(3n-6-3a_{2})
\displaystyle=3n+a_{2}-8
\displaystyle\geq 3n-3,
which gives e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Figure 1: Two graphs occurred in the proof of Subcase 1.2.
Now, we assume that G has no adjacent vertices of degree 2.
This means for any u\in A_{2} , each neighbour of u is of degree at least 3.
Also, by Claim 3.2, any two vertices in A_{2} share a common neighbour.
Combining this fact with \cap_{u\in A_{2}}N_{G}(u)=\emptyset , there exist two vertices x,y\in A_{2} such that x and y have a unique common neighbour, say a .
Let N_{G}(x)\backslash\{a\}=\{x_{1}\} and N_{G}(y)\backslash\{a\}=\{y_{1}\} , see Fig. 1 (b).
Since there exists a vertex z\in A_{2} such that za\notin E(G) , we have zx_{1}\in E(G) and zy_{1}\in E(G) .
Then for any w\in A_{2}\backslash\{x,y,z\} , |N_{G}(w)\cap\{a,x_{1},y_{1}\}|=2 .
Otherwise, d(w,x)>2 or d(w,y)>2 or d(w,z)>2 , a contradiction.
So, d_{G}(a)+d_{G}(x_{1})+d_{G}(y_{1})\geq 2a_{2} . By our assumption that a_{2}\geq 5 ,
we have
\sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq d_{G}(a)+d_{G}(x_{1})+d_{G}(y_{1})+2a_{2}+3(n-3-a_{2})\geq 3n+a_{2}-9\geq 3n-4.
Thus, by Claim 3.1, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
Combining the above arguments, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil if G is connected.
Case 2.
G is disconnected.
Suppose that G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{t}\,(t\geq 2) are the components of G . If \delta(G)\geq 3 , then e(G)>\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil . So, we may assume that there exists a vertex u\in V(G_{1}) with d_{G}(u)\leq 2 . Take v\in V(G)\backslash V(G_{1}) . Then G+uv has a K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -copy, say H , containing uv . Since uv is not contained in any triangle of G+uv , uv must be the pendant edge in H . As d_{G+uv}(u)\leq 3 , we have d_{H}(u)=1 and d_{H}(v)=s+3 . Thus, every vertex v\in V(G)\backslash V(G_{1}) satisfies d_{G}(v)=s+2\geq 3 .
Let |V(G_{1})|=n_{1} . If n_{1}\leq s+3 , then G_{1}\cong K_{n_{1}} .
Since n\geq 5 ,
\sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)\geq 3(n-n_{1})+n_{1}(n_{1}-1)=3n+(n_{1}-2)^{2}-4\geq 3n-4.
By Claim 3.1, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
If n_{1}\geq s+4 , then G_{1} is a connected K_{4^{-}}^{+s} -saturated graph.
From the connected case, we have \sum\limits_{u\in V(G_{1})}d_{G}(u)\geq 3n_{1}-4 . Thus,
\sum\limits_{u\in V(G)}d_{G}(u)=\sum\limits_{u\in V(G_{1})}d_{G}(u)+\sum\limits_{u\in V(G)\backslash V(G_{1})}d_{G}(u)\geq(3n_{1}-4)+3(n-n_{1})=3n-4.
By Claim 3.1, e(G)\geq\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\rceil .
This completes the proof.
∎
4 Saturation number for K_{p^{-}}^{+1}\,(p\geq 5)
In this section, we prove Theorem 1.3. We recall the statement of Theorem 1.3.
Theorem 1.3.
Let p\geq 5 , n\geq p+1 and n\equiv k\pmod{p} . Then
\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{p^{-}}^{+1})=\frac{(p-1)n}{2}+\frac{k(k-p)}{2}.
4.1 Preliminary results
We first prove several results that are used repeatedly in the proof of Theorem 1.3.
Observation 4.1.
Let G be a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph and G^{\prime} be any component of G .
If |V(G^{\prime})|\geq p+1 , then G^{\prime} contains no K_{p} -copy.
In particular, if G is connected and |V(G)|\geq p+1 , then G contains no K_{p} -copy.
Lemma 4.1.
Let G be a connected K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph with |V(G)|\geq p+1 . If u is a vertex in G such that G[N_{G}[u]] contains a K_{p}^{-} -copy, then d_{G}(u)=p-1 and G[N_{G}[u]]\cong K_{p}^{-} .
Proof.
Assume that M^{\prime} is a K_{p}^{-} -copy in G[N_{G}[u]] . Clearly, u\in V(M^{\prime}) , for otherwise, G[V(M^{\prime})\cup\{u\}] contains a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -copy, a contradiction.
If d_{M^{\prime}}(u)=p-2 , since M^{\prime} is a subgraph of G[N_{G}[u]] , G[N_{G}[u]] contains a K_{p} -copy, a contradiction by Observation 4.1. So, d_{M^{\prime}}(u)=p-1 .
If d_{G}(u)\geq p , there exists a vertex w\in N_{G}(u)\backslash V(M^{\prime}) such that M^{\prime}+uw is a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -copy in G , a contradiction. So, d_{G}(u)=d_{M^{\prime}}(u)=p-1 , and thus, N_{G}[u]=V(M^{\prime}) .
It follows that G[N_{G}[u]]\cong K_{p}^{-} or K_{p} . Since G is K_{p} -free by Observation 4.1, we have G[N_{G}[u]]\cong K_{p}^{-} .
∎
Lemma 4.2.
Let G be a graph and M^{\prime} be a copy of K_{p}^{-} in G . If u,v\in V(M^{\prime}) , then |N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)|\geq p-3 and G[N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)] contains a K_{p-3} -copy.
Proof.
Suppose that uv\in E(G) and, contrary to the claim, that G[N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)] contains no K_{p-3} -copy. Then uv does not belong to any K_{p-1} -copy, and thus, uv cannot be an edge of a K_{p}^{-} -copy, a contradiction. So, u and v are the unique pair of non-adjacent vertices in M^{\prime} . This directly implies that |N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)|\geq p-3 and G[N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)] contains a K_{p-3} -copy.
∎
Lemma 4.3.
Let v be a vertex in a graph G such that G[N_{G}(v)] contains no K_{p-2} -copy. Then for each v^{\prime}\in N_{G}(v) , we have G[N_{G}[v^{\prime}]]\ncong K_{p}^{-} .
Proof.
Assume that G[N_{G}[v^{\prime}]]\cong K_{p}^{-} , say M^{\prime} . Since vv^{\prime}\in E(G) , v\in V(M^{\prime}) .
Since G[N_{G}(v)] contains no K_{p-2} -copy, v does not belong to any K_{p-1} -copy.
So, v cannot be a vertex of any K_{p}^{-} -copy, a contradiction.
∎
Lemma 4.4.
Let G be a connected K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph with |V(G)|\geq p+1 , and let M_{1},M_{2} be two distinct K_{p}^{-} -copies in G . If x\in V(M_{1})\cap V(M_{2}) , then d_{M_{1}}(x)=d_{M_{2}}(x)=p-2 .
Proof.
Otherwise, assume that d_{M_{1}}(x)=p-1 . Then M_{1} is a K_{p}^{-} -copy in G[N_{G}[x]] .
By Lemma 4.1, d_{G}(x)=p-1 and G[N_{G}[x]]\cong K_{p}^{-} . So, G[N_{G}[x]]=M_{1} .
If d_{M_{2}}(x)=p-1 , then d_{M_{2}}(x)=d_{G}(x) and V(M_{2})=N_{G}[x]=V(M_{1}) . Note that G[N_{G}[x]]\cong K_{p}^{-} . Then M_{1}=M_{2} , a contradiction.
So, d_{M_{2}}(x)=p-2 .

Lemma 4.5.
Let G be a disconnected K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph of order n\geq p+1 . Then all but at most one component of G are K_{p} -copies.
Proof.
Assume that G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{t}\,(t\geq 2) are the components of G and that there exist 1\leq i<j\leq t such that G_{i} and G_{j} are both K_{p} -free. We claim that
if G_{i}\,(i\in[t]) is K_{p} -free, then G_{i} contains a vertex u such that G[N_{G}[u]] contains no K_{p}^{-} -copy.
Suppose on the contrary that for any u\in V(G_{i}) , G[N_{G}[u]] contains a K_{p}^{-} -copy.
Since G_{i} is K_{p} -free, it follows that G_{i} must be a connected K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph with |V(G_{i})|\geq p+1 . By Lemma 4.1, G[N_{G}[u]]\cong K_{p}^{-} , say M_{1} .
Let V(M_{1})=\{u,u_{1},\ldots,u_{p-1}\} and d_{M_{1}}(u_{p-2})=d_{M_{1}}(u_{p-1})=p-2 .
Since u_{p-2}\in V(G_{i}) , by the assumption, we also have G[N_{G}[u_{p-2}]]\cong K_{p}^{-} , say M_{2} . Clearly, d_{M_{2}}(u_{p-2})=p-1 .
Then u_{p-2}\in V(M_{1})\cap V(M_{2}) , but d_{M_{2}}(u_{p-2})=p-1 , a contradiction by Lemma 4.4.
Thus, we can take u\in V(G_{i}) and v\in V(G_{j}) such that G[N_{G}[u]] and G[N_{G}[v]] are both K_{p}^{-} -free.
By the definition of K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph, G+uv has a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -copy M containing uv .
Since uv is not contained in any K_{p}^{-} -copy of G+uv , uv must be the pendant edge of M . Thus, G[N_{G}[u]] or G[N_{G}[v]] contains a K_{p}^{-} -copy, a contradiction.
So, there is at most one component of G that is K_{p} -free.
Moreover, each component containing a K_{p} has exactly p vertices, for otherwise G contains a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -copy, a contradiction.
Therefore, all but at most one component of G are K_{p} -copies.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.3.
Since n\equiv k\pmod{p} , the graph \frac{n-k}{p}K_{p}\cup K_{k} is a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph of order n with h(n,p) edges. So, \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{p^{-}}^{+1})\leq e(\frac{n-k}{p}K_{p}\cup K_{k})=h(n,p) .
Now we prove the lower bound.
Let G be a minimum K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph of order n . If G is connected, then by Lemma 4.6, e(G)\geq h(n,p) . Now suppose that G is disconnected, and G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{s}\,(s\geq 2) are the components of G .
By Lemma 4.5, we may assume that G_{i}\cong K_{p} for each i\in[s-1] .
For the remaining component G_{s} of order n_{s} , if n_{s}\leq p , since n\equiv k\pmod{p} , we have n_{s}=k . Then G_{s}\cong K_{k} , for otherwise, adding edges in G_{s} produces no copy of K_{p^{-}}^{+1} , a contradiction. So, e(G)=\frac{(p-1)(n-k)}{2}+\binom{k}{2}=h(n,p) .
If n_{s}\geq p+1 , then G_{s} is a connected K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -saturated graph. Since G_{i}\cong K_{p} for each i\in[s-1] , we have n_{s}\equiv k\pmod{p} . By Lemma 4.6, e(G)\geq\frac{(p-1)(n-n_{s})}{2}+h(n_{s},p)=\frac{(p-1)(n-n_{s})}{2}+\frac{(p-1)(n_{s}-k)}{2}+\binom{k}{2}=h(n,p) .
Summarizing the above, if p\geq 5 , we have e(G)\geq h(n,p) .
Thus, \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{p^{-}}^{+1})=h(n,p) .
∎
5 Concluding remark
This work builds on the inequality proved by Cameron and Puleo [1], which states that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F)\leq n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,F) for all n\geq|V(F)|+1 . This bound immediately raises the question of when equality holds, and much of the subsequent work has focused on a non-empty graph F with no isolated vertex.
In this paper, we investigate the case in which F is a non-empty graph with isolated vertices.
We determine the exact saturation numbers for K_{1}\vee F when F=K^{-}_{3}\cup sK_{1}(s\geq 1) and F=K^{-}_{p-1}\cup K_{1}(p\geq 5) and verify whether
\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F)=n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,F)
(7)
holds for these cases.
In fact, both graphs F we investigate here are the union of a nearly complete graph K_{p-1}^{-} and some isolated vertices.
Interestingly, despite the very similar structures of these two families of graphs, we obtain two completely opposite conclusions. For F=K^{-}_{3}\cup sK_{1}(s\geq 1) , it can be easily checked that \mathrm{sat}(n-1,K_{3}^{-}\cup sK_{1})=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor for any n\geq s+4 . Together with Theorem 1.2,
\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee(K_{3}^{-}\cup sK_{1}))=\Big\lceil\frac{3n-4}{2}\Big\rceil=n-1+\Big\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\Big\rfloor=n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,K_{3}^{-}\cup sK_{1}).
Moreover, as shown in our proof, there exists an extremal graph of K_{1}\vee(K_{3}^{-}\cup sK_{1}) that contains a full-degree vertex, just like the construction in Eq. (7).
For F=K^{-}_{p-1}\cup K_{1}(p\geq 5) , however, the equality (7) does not hold.
From the saturation number of generalized books given in [4], we immediately obtain that if n\geq 4(2p-4)^{p-3} , then \mathrm{sat}(n-1,K_{p-1}^{-}\cup K_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\big((2p-7)n-p^{2}+4p-2+\theta\big) , where \theta=0 if n-p\equiv 0\pmod{2} , and \theta=1 otherwise. It can be checked that \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee(K_{p-1}^{-}\cup K_{1}))\neq n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,K_{p-1}^{-}\cup K_{1}) . Moreover, we claim that any extremal graph for K_{1}\vee(K_{p-1}^{-}\cup K_{1}) contains no full-degree vertex; this is proved in the following theorem.
Theorem 5.1.
Let n\geq p+1 .
If p\geq 5 , then any extremal graph of K_{1}\vee(K_{p-1}^{-}\cup K_{1}) contains no full-degree vertex.

For convenience, we proceed directly using the same notation, definitions, constructions, and intermediate results established in the proof of Lemma 4.6.

e(G)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{x\in V(G)}d_{G}(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(n-1+(p-1)(n-1)\big)=\frac{pn-p}{2}>\frac{(p-1)n}{2}\geq h(n,p),
a contradiction.
Take w\in V_{2} . Then G+vw contains a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -copy M_{w} with vw\in E(M_{w}) .
Recall that V_{2} is partitioned into two disjoint subsets A and B ,
where, for w\in A , vw is the pendant edge in M_{w} , and for w\in B , vw is an edge in the K_{p}^{-} -copy of M_{w} .
Also, A\cap B=\emptyset by Claim 4.2.

By Lemma 4.1, G[N_{G}[u]]\cong K_{p}^{-} and d_{G}(u)=p-1 . This contradicts the fact that d_{G}(u)=n-1 .
So, d_{M^{\prime}}(u)=p-2 .
Let u^{\prime} be a vertex in V(M^{\prime})\backslash\{u\} with d_{M^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})=p-2 .
Since G is K_{p} -free, uu^{\prime}\notin E(G) . This contradicts d_{G}(u)=n-1 .
So, A=\emptyset , which implies b=n-1-d_{G}(v)\geq n-1-(p-2)\geq 2 . By Claim 4.8, G[V_{1}] contains a K_{p-3} -copy.
From the proof of Case 2 in Lemma 4.6,
we consider the following cases.
1.
G[V_{1}]\cong K_{p-2} . By Claim 4.9, we have e(G)>h(n,p) , a contradiction.
2.
G[V_{1}]\cong K_{p-3} , or \delta(G)=p-2 and \frac{p^{2}-3p}{2}\leq e(G[N_{G}[v]])<\frac{p^{2}-3p+2}{2} .
Recall that A=\emptyset . By our proof of Claim 4.13, if e(G)=h(n,p) , then
all intermediate steps in (4) hold with exact equality.
Thus, e(G)=e(G[N_{G}[v]])+\frac{p+1}{2}a+\frac{2p-5}{2}b , \delta(G)=p-2 , e(G[N_{G}[v]])=\frac{p^{2}-3p}{2} and
e(G)=\frac{2p-5}{2}n+\frac{-p^{2}+4p-5}{2}=\frac{(p-1)n}{2}+\frac{k(k-p)}{2}=h(n,p).
Thus, n=p+\frac{5+k^{2}-pk}{p-4} . Then by the proof of Claim 4.7, we have p=5 .
Since d_{G}(v)=\delta(G)=p-2 and e(G[N_{G}[v]])=\frac{p^{2}-3p}{2} , we have G[N_{G}[v]]\cong K_{p-1}^{-} .
Together with p=5 , we have G[N_{G}[v]]\cong K_{4}^{-} .
Let V_{1}\backslash\{u\}=\{v_{1},v_{2}\} . As d_{G}(u)=n-1 , v_{1}v_{2} must be the one edge not in G[N_{G}[v]] . Then G+v_{1}v_{2} has a K_{p^{-}}^{+1} -copy containing v_{1}v_{2} , say M .
Since G[V_{1}]\ncong K_{p-2} , by Lemma 4.3, G[N_{G}[v_{i}]]\ncong K_{p}^{-} for each i\in[2] . Thus, v_{1}v_{2} must be an edge in the K_{p}^{-} -copy of M .
Recall we have shown that e(G)=e(G[N_{G}[v]])+\frac{p+1}{2}a+\frac{2p-5}{2}b and A=\emptyset .
By our proof of Claim 4.11, one can deduce that d_{G}(y)=p-2 and d_{V_{1}}(y)=p-3 for any y\in B .
Thus, u,v_{1} , and v_{2} are all vertices in G+v_{1}v_{2} of degree at least p-1 . So, all three vertices are in M and have degree at least p-1 in M . Furthermore, by the construction of a K_{p}^{-} -copy, two (p-2) -degree vertices in M must be adjacent to u,v_{1} and v_{2} . Since V(G)\backslash\{u,v_{1},v_{2}\}=B\cup\{v\} , there exists a vertex in B , say y^{\prime} , such that d_{V_{1}}(y^{\prime})=3=p-2 . This contradicts the property d_{V_{1}}(y^{\prime})=p-3 proved above.
3.
d_{G}(v)=p-2 and e(G[N_{G}[v]])<\frac{p^{2}-3p}{2} . Recall our assumption that G[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{p-3}\}]\cong K_{p-3} . Then d_{V_{1}}(v_{p-2})\leq p-5 .
Since e(G)=h(n,p) and A=\emptyset , by our proof in Claim 4.20, we have d_{V_{1}}(v_{p-2})=p-5 and p=5 .
This implies that v_{p-2} has no neighbour in V_{1} . This contradicts the fact that u\in V_{1} and d_{G}(u)=n-1 .
Summarizing the above, any extremal graph of K_{1}\vee(K_{p-1}^{-}\cup K_{1}) contains no full-degree vertex.
∎
From the above discussion, we reasonably conjecture that the presence of a full-degree vertex in some extremal graph of K_{1}\vee F is a necessary condition for Eq. (7) to hold. Our precise conjecture is as follows.
Conjecture 5.1.
Let F be a non-empty graph and n\geq|V(F)|+1 .
Then \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee F)=n-1+\mathrm{sat}(n-1,F) holds only if there exists an extremal graph of K_{1}\vee F containing a full-degree vertex.
Furthermore, it would also be interesting to investigate the conditions under which an extremal graph has a full-degree vertex.
While this problem is perhaps too general to be meaningful for arbitrary graphs, the situation becomes entirely different when considered for joins of graphs.
Turning to the results in our paper, it is natural to pose the following problem.
Problem 5.1.
Determine the exact value of \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{p^{-}}^{+s}) for all p\geq 5 and s\geq 2 .
The house graph is a five-vertex graph formed by adding a chord to a 5-cycle.
In this paper, we determine the saturation number for K_{4^{-}}^{+1} . Together with previous results, this leaves the house graph as the only connected graph on 5 vertices whose saturation number is still unknown.
We pose the following problem.
Problem 5.2.
Determine the saturation number for the house graph.
