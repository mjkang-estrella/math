Abstract
This work deals with undirected graphs that have the same betweenness centrality for each vertex,
so-called betweenness uniform graphs (or BUGs). The class of these graphs is not trivial and its
classification is still an open problem. Recently, Gago, Coroničová-Hurajová and Madaras conjectured
that for every rational \alpha\geq 3/4 there exists a BUG having betweenness
centrality \alpha . We disprove this conjecture, and provide an alternative view of the
structure of betweenness-uniform graphs from the point of view of their complement. This allows
us to characterise all the BUGs with betweennes centrality at most 9/10, and show that their
betweenness centrality is equal to \frac{\ell}{\ell+1} for some integer \ell\leq 9 . We
conjecture that this characterization extends to all the BUGs with betweenness centrality smaller
than 1.
Keywords:betweenness-uniform, betweenness centrality, shortest path
1 Introduction
Complex networks represent a model for many complicated real-world phenomena. Vertices of complex networks
usually represent some objects of interest, such as actors in social networks, nodes in computer
networks, proteins in their interaction structure, parts of the brain in connectome networks, etc.
For other examples, see [1] and references therein. One of the aims of the study of
complex networks is to identify interesting objects and evaluate their importance. This evaluation
can be done using so-called centrality measures originating from social networks [4]. These
measures rate vertices’ importance in their role in the corresponding network. Historically,
the primary approach, called degree centrality, is based on the number of neighbors of the
corresponding vertex. This local approach has been improved many times leading to more elaborate
measures accounting for broader neighborhoods, such as eigenvector
centrality [2]. As an alternative to the measures based on the size and importance of the neighborhood of vertices, centrality can also be defined as a mediator of communication in the network. Communication
is expressed as the amount of shortest paths passing through the given vertex. This
approach is formalized by the notion of betweenness centrality, which counts the overall fraction of shortest paths connecting pairs of vertices passing through the given
vertex [3].
To use any centrality measure, it is beneficial to know its typical behavior. Motivated by degree centrality and degree distribution studies resulting in concepts such as scale-freeness, one can ask about the typical behavior of other centrality measures, such as betweenness centrality. A natural starting point is the limiting case when betweenness centrality is the same for all vertices. We will call such structures betweenness-uniform graphs (shortly BUGs). Note that the value of the betweenness centrality of any vertex in a BUG is thus equal to the average betweenness centrality in the graph.
Characterizing the structure of BUGs is not a trivial task. The class of BUGs includes all vertex-transitive graphs, but not all BUGs are vertex-transitive; indeed, some BUGs are not even regular. We can go even further. Based on the correspondence with strongly regular graphs, we can construct a BUG for any automorphism group [5]. There are several approaches to characterize the betweenness-uniform graphs. One possibility is to find bounds for different characteristics, such as vertex connectivity; see a recent result of Hartman, Pokorná and Valtr [7, 6]. Another approach is to identify all the possible rational numbers that can occur as the betweenness value of a BUG. It has been conjectured that all rational numbers greater than \frac{3}{4} have this property.
Former conjecture 1 (Gago, Coroničová-Hurajová, Madaras 2013 [5]).
For any rational value \alpha in the interval (\frac{3}{4},\infty) , there exists a
betweenness-uniform graph with betweenness11
1
This conjecture is mentioned
in the text [5] after Lemma 2.3. Note that the definition of betweenness

It has already been shown that the only BUGs with the betweenness value of zero are complete graphs
and that there are no BUGs with betweenness in the interval (0,\frac{1}{2}) [5]. In this work, we examine the structure of BUGs with
betweenness below one, which we call low-betweenness BUGs. These graphs can be conveniently
described by the structure of their complements, which we abbreviate as coBUGs. We show that the
complement of a low-betweenness BUG needs to have fewer edges than vertices, and in fact the
complement must be disconnected, implying that a low-betweenness BUG has diameter at most 2.
An easy way to construct a low betweenness BUG is to fix integers k\geq 2 and \ell\geq 0 and
consider the complement of the graph obtained as a disjoint union of k copies of the star
K_{1,\ell} . This yields a BUG with betweenness value \frac{\ell}{\ell+1} . The question remains
whether there are other examples of low-betweenness BUGs, or even other possible values of
betweenness smaller than 1. We conjecture that this is not the case.
Conjecture 2.
If G is a low-betweenness BUG, then its complement \overline{G} is a disjoint union of stars of the same size.
The study of the structure of coBUGs can be viewed from the perspective of combining various types
of graphs as coBUG components, e.g. Corollary 16 shows that a star cannot
be combined with a cycle. For a subinterval (0,9/10\rangle , we show that stars K_{1,\ell} for
\ell\in\{1,\dots,8\} cannot be combined with other graphs as connectivity components in a
coBUG that correspond to a low-betweenness BUG. This implies the next theorem, which is our main
result.
Theorem 3.
Let G be a BUG with betweenness \alpha\leq 9/10 . Then \alpha=\ell/(\ell+1) for some integer \ell\geq 0 , and the complement of G is a graph whose every connected component is isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} .
This disproves Conjecture 1, and provides partial support for Conjecture 2 by showing that any counterexample for the conjecture must have betweenness within the interval (9/10,1) . As further support for Conjecture 2, we also show
that the complement of any counterexample to the conjecture must contain a component which is a star on at least 10 vertices together with a component of a very irregular structure; see Subsection 3.1 and Theorem 17 for the precise statement.
It appears that the structure of BUGs with betweenness less than one is relatively simple to describe in
terms of their complements, and in particular, Conjecture 2 implies that for a given
\alpha<1 and a given number of vertices n , there is up to isomorphism only one BUG on n
vertices with betweenness \alpha . The picture becomes much more complicated as soon as the value
\alpha=1 is reached. For instance, it can be easily verified that any graph whose complement is a
disjoint union of cycles of length at least 4 (not necessarily all of the same length) is a BUG
with betweenness 1 (see Example 2). But these are not the only BUGs of betweenness 1;
in fact, for any complete multipartite graph H with more edges than vertices, we can construct a
BUG G of betweenness 1 whose complement \overline{G} contains H as a connected component, with all
the other components of \overline{G} being stars (see Example 3). Moreover, we also
construct a sequence of BUGs whose betweenness values converge to 1 from above
(Example 4).
2 Preliminaries
At the beginning of this section, we introduce all the definitions and notation together with basic notions, ideas, and observations leading to deeper insight into the problem and into the motivation behind the key concepts. These basic insights will be also frequently used in the following sections, which contain our main results.
2.1 Definitions and notation
Throughout this article, a network is represented by an unoriented graph G with a set of vertices
V(G) and a set of edges E(G)\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{2} . For simplicity, in most cases,
we will denote the edge between the vertices x,y\in V(G) simply as xy\in E(G) instead of the
more accurate \{x,y\}\in E(G) . The complement \overline{G} of a graph G is the graph with vertex
set V(G) and edge set \binom{V(G)}{2}\setminus E(G) .
The symbol \cong denotes graph isomorphism.
The number of vertices of the currently discussed graph is denoted by n .
The set of vertices adjacent to a vertex v is denoted by N(v) , and N[v] denotes the set N(v)\cup\{v\} . The degree of a vertex v , denoted by \deg(v) , is equal to |N(v)| .
The maximum degree of G , denoted \Delta(G) , is \max_{v\in V(G)}\deg(v) .
The betweenness centrality (or just betweenness)
of a vertex x\in V(G) is defined as22
2
Note that some authors use an alternative
definition of betweenness that considers ordered pairs. In that case, the summation is across
all (v,w)\in V(G)\times V(G) such that u\neq v . The resulting value of betweenness is two
times larger than the value corresponding to the definition we use.
B_{G}(x)=\sum_{\{v,w\}\subseteq V(G)}\frac{\sigma_{vw}(x)}{\sigma_{vw}},
where \sigma_{vw} denotes the number of shortest paths between v and w and \sigma_{vw}(x)
is the number of such paths containing x as an internal
vertex [3]. In particular, \sigma_{vw}(x)=0 whenever x=v
or x=w . If the graph G is clear from context, we write B(x) instead of B_{G}(x) .
Betweenness centrality can be also defined for the whole graph G by taking the average betweenness of its vertices, i.e.,
B(G)=\frac{\sum_{x\in V(G)}B(x)}{|V(G)|}.
We say that a graph G is a betweenness-uniform graph (or shortly a BUG), if all its
vertices have the same betweenness, that is, B(x)=B(y) for all x,y\in V(G) . Note that in such
a case, the betweenness of the whole graph is identical to the betweenness of any vertex.
The complement \overline{G} of a BUG G will be referred to as a coBUG.
In this text, we will focus on betweenness-uniform graphs with values of betweenness smaller
than 1. We call such graphs low-betweenness BUGs. The complement of a low-betweenness BUG is
called a low-betweenness coBUG.
2.2 Bounds for betweenness via edge-vertex ratio
When dealing with BUGs, especially BUGs with low betweenness, it is often more natural to focus
on the properties of the corresponding coBUG rather than the BUG itself. This is illustrated by the
next lemma, which we will frequently use throughout the rest of the paper.
Lemma 4.
For a connected graph G on n vertices, we have B(G)\geq\frac{|E(\overline{G})|}{n} , with
equality if and only if G has diameter at most 2.
Proof.
Let G=(V,E) be a graph, let \overline{G}=(V,\overline{E}) be its complement, and let
n:=|V| . For two distinct vertices x,y\in V , let d_{G}(x,y) be the distance of x and y in
G , i.e., the number of edges on the shortest xy path in G . Recall that \sigma_{xy} is the
number of shortest xy -paths, and \sigma_{xy}(u) is the number of those shortest xy -paths that
contain u as an internal vertex. Notice that for any two distinct vertices x,y\in V ,
\sum_{u\in V}\sigma_{xy}(u)=(d_{G}(x,y)-1)\sigma_{xy},
since both sides of the equation count the number of pairs (u,P) where P is a shortest
xy -path and u is an internal vertex of P . We then have
\displaystyle B(G)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in V}B(u)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{u\in V}\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\binom{V}{2}}\frac{\sigma_{xy}(u)}{\sigma_{xy}}
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\binom{V}{2}}\sum_{u\in V}\frac{\sigma_{xy}(u)}{\sigma_{xy}}
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\binom{V}{2}}(d_{G}(x,y)-1)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\overline{E}}(d_{G}(x,y)-1)
\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\overline{E}|}{n},
where the last inequality holds with equality if and only if any two distinct nonadjacent vertices
of G have distance two, i.e., if and only if G has diameter at most two.
∎
For a graph with m edges and n vertices, the ratio \frac{m}{n} is referred to as the
edge-vertex ratio. It is precisely half of the average degree of the graph.
Lemma 4 then shows that the betweenness of a BUG G is lower-bounded by the
edge-vertex ratio of its complement, with equality for BUGs of diameter two.
Corollary 5.
If G is a low-betweenness BUG, then \overline{G} has fewer edges than vertices.
This corollary means that for a low-betweenness BUG, the only possible corresponding coBUG is either a tree or a disconnected graph. The informal message of this section is that low-betweenness BUGs are always relatively dense.
2.3 Low-betweenness BUGs with diameter at least three
In this section, we show that any low-betweenness BUG is of diameter at most two, thus
proving Theorem 7.
This result, together with Lemma 4, simplifies our study of low-betweenness BUGs.
The key part is realizing that complements of graphs with diameter at least three have a specific form.
Lemma 6.
A connected graph G satisfies \text{diam}(G)\geq 3 if and only if \overline{G} is connected and
contains a spanning tree which is a double star.
Proof.
We prove a stronger version of this lemma saying that two vertices a and b have a mutual distance of at least three in G if and only if \overline{G} contains a spanning tree which is a double star with the two centers a and b .
First, suppose that a and b have mutual distance at least three in G . Then ab is not an edge in G and thus it is an edge of \overline{G} . Similarly, for any vertex c\neq a,b , the graph G does not contain the path acb and therefore \overline{G} contains at least one of the edges ac and cb .
It follows that \overline{G} contains a spanning double star with the two centers a and b .
Suppose now that \overline{G} contains a spanning double star with the two centers a and b .
Then G contains neither an edge ab nor any path acb , since in the latter case c would not be connected by an edge to any of the two centers of the spanning double star in \overline{G} , which is impossible. It follows that a and b have distance at least three in G .
This completes the proof.
∎
The spanning double star is inevitable in the complement of any graph of diameter at least three. This property allows us to show that any coBUG containing it must have the edge-vertex ratio at least one. Using this corollary together with Lemma 4 we can show that there are no low-betweenness BUGs of diameter at least three.
Theorem 7.
If G is a connected BUG of diameter at least 3, then B(G)\geq 1 .
Proof.
Suppose for contradiction that G=(V,E) is a BUG of diameter at least 3 with B(G)<1 . By Lemma 6, the complement \overline{G}=(V,\overline{E}) is connected and contains a spanning double star. By Lemma 4, we know that \frac{|\overline{E}|}{|V|}\leq B(G)<1 , and in particular, |\overline{E}|<|V| . It follows that \overline{G} is a tree, hence \overline{G} itself is a double star. Let a and b be the two centers of the double star \overline{G} . We may easily check that G does not contain any shortest path whose internal vertex is a or b . Consequently, B(a)=B(b)=0 , and since G is a BUG, we have B(G)=0 . However, the only connected BUGs of betweenness equal to 0 are complete graphs, whose diameter is equal to 1. This contradiction completes the proof.
∎
2.4 BUGs with diameter two
Lemma 4 implies that if a BUG G has low betweenness, then its complement
\overline{G} has fewer edges than vertices. This is our motivation for defining the edge excess of
a graph H as \ex(H):=|E(H)|-|V(H)| . Thus, complements of low-betweenness BUGs have negative
edge excess.
This shows, in particular, that complements of low-betweenness BUGs are generally rather sparse,
and therefore the BUGs themselves rather dense. It is therefore easier and more natural, when
dealing with low-betweenness BUGs, to describe the structure of the corresponding coBUGs.
Theorem 7 shows that in our search for low-betweenness BUGs, we may restrict our
attention to graphs of diameter at most 2. Graphs of diameter 1 are trivial: they are the complete graphs, which as we know are betweenness-uniform with betweenness equal to 0. Henceforth, we will therefore focus on graphs of diameter 2.
Fortunately, it turns out that for a graph G of diameter 2, betweenness uniformity can be easily
characterised in terms of the complement \overline{G} . We now introduce the required terminology and
state the characterisation.
Let H=(V,E) be a graph. We say that
a vertex v\in V is close to an edge e=xy\in E , if v is adjacent to at least one
endpoint of e ; in particular, the two endpoints x and y of e are close to e . Let
C_{H}(e) be the set of vertices close to the edge e , and let C_{H}(v) be the set of
edges that are close to the vertex v . Notice that C_{H}(e) is equal to N_{H}(x)\cup N_{H}(y) , and that C_{H}(v) contains precisely those edges that are incident to at least
one vertex in N_{H}(v) ,
Example 1.
Let H\cong C_{k} be a graph isomorphic to a cycle of length k\geq 4 . For any edge e of
the cycle, we can compute |C_{H}(e)|=4 , since we have two end vertices of the edge and
each of them has one neighbor. Similarly, for any vertex v , |C_{H}(v)|=4 , since each of
the two neighbors has one edge back to vertex v and one to the next neighbor.
Let H=(V,E) be a graph on n vertices. For an edge e\in E , we define its weight as
w_{H}(e)=\frac{1}{n-|C_{H}(e)|}.
In case C_{H}(e)=n , i.e., if the edge e is close to all the vertices, we leave w_{H}(e)
undefined. Note that this only happens when H contains as a subgraph a double-star with central
edge e , and in view of Lemma 6, the complement of H then has diameter at
least 3 . Such situation will not occur in our applications.
We further define the co-betweenness of a vertex x as
coB_{H}(x)=\sum_{e\in C_{H}(x)}w_{H}(e),
i.e., the co-betweenness of a vertex is the total weight of the edges close to it.
If the graph H being considered is clear from the context, we write C(e) , w(e) and
coB(x) , instead of C_{H}(e) , w_{H}(e) and coB_{H}(x) . In all our applications, the role of
H will be played by the complement \overline{G} of a (potential) BUG G . The relevance of these
notions is explained by the next lemma.
Lemma 8.
Let G=(V,E) be a graph of diameter at most 2, and let x be a vertex of G . Let \overline{G} be the
complement of G . The betweenness B_{G}(x) of the vertex x satisfies
B_{G}(x)=\left(\sum_{e\in E(\overline{G})}w_{\overline{G}}(e)\right)-coB_{\overline{G}}(x).
(1)
Proof.
Since G has diameter at most 2, for any pair of distinct vertices e=\{v,w\} , there can be at
most one shortest path from v to w passing through the vertex x , and such a path exists
if and only if the following two conditions hold:
(a)
e is not an edge of G , i.e., e\in E(\overline{G}) , and
(b)
both edges xv and xw are in G , or equivalently, the vertex x
is not close to the edge e in \overline{G} .
It follows that for a pair of distinct vertices e=\{v,w\} , the number of shortest
paths from v to w in G is equal to 1 if e is an edge of G , and is equal to
n-|C_{\overline{G}}(e)| if e is an edge of \overline{G} .
We may then compute the betweenness of x as follows:
\displaystyle B_{G}(x)
\displaystyle=\sum_{\{v,w\}\subseteq V}\frac{\sigma_{vw}(x)}{\sigma_{vw}}
\displaystyle=\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E(\overline{G})}\frac{\sigma_{vw}(x)}{\sigma_{vw}}
\displaystyle=\sum_{e=\{v,w\}\in E(\overline{G})}\frac{\sigma_{vw}(x)}{n-|C_{\overline{G}}(e)|}
\displaystyle=\sum_{e=\{v,w\}\in E(\overline{G})}w_{\overline{G}}(e)\sigma_{vw}(x)
\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(\overline{G})\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(x)}w_{\overline{G}}(e)
by conditions (a) and (b) above
\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{e\in E(\overline{G})}w_{\overline{G}}(e)\right)-\sum_{e\in C_{\overline{G}}(x)}w_{\overline{G}}(e)
\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{e\in E(\overline{G})}w_{\overline{G}}(e)\right)-coB_{\overline{G}}(x)
as claimed.
∎
Corollary 9.
A graph G of diameter at most 2 is betweenness-uniform if and only if in its complement \overline{G} ,
all the vertices have the same co-betweenness.
Note that to determine the co-betweenness of a vertex x , we only need to know the weights of the
edges close to x , and to determine the weight of an edge e , we only need to know the total
number of vertices in the graph and the number of vertices close to e . Thus, to determine the
betweenness-uniformity of a graph G by inspecting its complement \overline{G} , we only need to look
at the local structure in \overline{G} , with the only ‘global’ property needed being the total number of
vertices. This of course only works when G has diameter at most 2; however, in the examples we
encounter, the complement \overline{G} will typically be a disconnected graph, which automatically
guarantees that G has diameter at most 2.
Corollary 9 allows us to restrict the possible structure of graphs that may appear
as connected components of a disconnected coBUG.
Lemma 10.
Let H be a graph with two vertices x,y such that C_{H}(x)\subsetneq C_{H}(y) . Then
H cannot be a connected component of any disconnected coBUG.
Proof.
Suppose \overline{G} is a disconnected coBUG with a component H . The strict inclusion
C_{H}(x)\subsetneq C_{H}(y) implies that coB_{\overline{G}}(x)<coB_{\overline{G}}(y) , which is
impossible in a coBUG.
∎
Example 2.
Let G be a graph on n vertices. Suppose that its complement \overline{G} is a disconnected graph
whose every component is a cycle of length at least 4. Then G is a BUG of betweenness 1. Indeed,
in \overline{G} , each edge is close to four vertices, and hence has weight \frac{1}{n-4} , and each
vertex is close to four edges, and hence has co-betweenness \frac{4}{n-4} . Since \overline{G} is
disconnected, G has diameter at most 2, and since all the
vertices of \overline{G} have the same co-betweenness, G is a BUG by Corollary 9. To
see that G has betweenness 1, we may either use Lemma 8, or better yet,
Lemma 4.
An easy way to obtain graphs where all the vertices have the same co-betweenness is to ensure that
each vertex is close to the same number of edges, and each edge is close to the same number of
vertices. A graph H is (m,t) -uniform if for any vertex v\in V(H) , |C_{H}(v)|=m , and for any edge e\in E(H) , |C_{H}(e)|=t . Clearly, a graph is (m,t) -uniform if
and only if each of its components is (m,t) -uniform. We say that a graph is uniform, if it
is (m,t) -uniform for some values of m and t .
Observation 11.
In an (m,t) -uniform graph on n vertices, every vertex has co-betweenness \frac{m}{n-t} .
In particular, every disconnected uniform graph is a coBUG by Corollary 9.
For future use, we remark that (m,t) -uniformity determines the edge-vertex ratio:
Lemma 12.
Any (m,t) -uniform graph H satisfies \frac{m}{t}=\frac{|E(H)|}{|V(H)|} .
Proof.
Count in two ways the number of pairs (v,e)\in V(H)\times E(H) such that e\in C_{H}(v) to
get |V(H)|m=|E(H)|t .
∎
A special case of uniform graphs are graphs in which every edge is close to every vertex.
Note that such graphs are precisely the complete multipartite graphs:
Observation 13.
For a graph H=(V,E) the following properties are equivalent:
•
H is (m,t) -uniform with m=|E| and t=|V| ,
•
every edge of H is close to every vertex of H ,
•
H has no induced subgraph isomorphic to K_{2}+K_{1} (i.e., the disjoint union of K_{2} and
K_{1} ),
•
H is complete multipartite (i.e., its complement is a disjoint union of cliques).
Note that a connected complete multipartite graph with at least two vertices cannot be a coBUG,
despite being uniform, since its complement is disconnected and therefore not a BUG;
however, complete multipartite graphs, and connected uniform graphs in general, are natural
candidates for connected components of coBUGs. We will later see, however, that to obtain new
examples of low-betweenness BUGs, we must consider coBUGs with components that are not
uniform.
3 Low-betweenness BUGs
We will now focus on the specific properties of BUGs with betweenness less than 1. We know from
Theorem 7 that such BUGs have diameter at most 2, and therefore the results of
Subsection 2.4, in particular Lemma 8 and Corollary 9,
apply to them.
By Lemma 4, the complement of a low-betweenness BUG has fewer edges than vertices,
and in particular, at least one of its components must be a tree. We show that the only trees that
can occur are stars.
Lemma 14.
Let \overline{G} be a low-betweenness coBUG on n vertices. Then
•
at least one connected component of \overline{G} is a tree,
•
any component of \overline{G} containing a vertex of degree at most 1 (in particular any tree
component) is isomorphic to a star K_{1,\ell} for some \ell , and
•
all the star components of \overline{G} are pairwise isomorphic, i.e., they are all stars of the
same size.
Proof.
Since \overline{G} has fewer edges than vertices, at least one of its components must be a tree. In
particular, at least one component of \overline{G} contains a vertex of degre at most 1. Let
H be such a component, and let x be a vertex of degree at most 1 in H . If x has degree 0,
then coB(x)=0 , and by Corollary 9, all the vertices of \overline{G} have co-betweenness
0, which implies that \overline{G} has no edges and the lemma holds with \ell=0 .
Suppose now that x has degree 1, and let y be
the vertex adjacent to x . Note that the set C_{\overline{G}}(x) contains precisely the edges incident
to y . If H is not a star, then H contains edges not belonging to C_{\overline{G}}(x) , and at least
one such edge is close to y . It follows that C_{\overline{G}}(x)\subsetneq C_{\overline{G}}(y) , which is impossible
by Lemma 10. Therefore H must be a star, i.e., isomorphic to K_{1,\ell}
for some \ell .
It follows that any vertex in H has co-betweenness \frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)} , and therefore all
the vertices of \overline{G} have this co-betweenness as well. Suppose that \overline{G}
contains another tree component H^{\prime} , necessarily isomorphic to a star K_{1,m} for some m . Then
\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}=\frac{m}{n-(m+1)} , since all the vertices in \overline{G} have the same
co-betweenness, and this implies \ell=m . We conclude that all the star components of \overline{G} are
pairwise isomorphic.
∎
Observe that for any k\geq 2 and \ell\geq 0 , the graph obtained by taking k disjoint copies of
the star K_{1,\ell} is a coBUG, whose complement is a BUG of betweenness \frac{\ell}{\ell+1} .
Our Conjecture 2 states that this is the only way to construct a low-betweenness BUG.
Let us call a BUG G exotic if it is a low-betweenness BUG whose complement is not a
disjoint union of stars of the same size; thus, Conjecture 2 equivalently states that
there are no exotic BUGs. The complement of an exotic BUG is referred to as an exotic
coBUG.
In the two following subsections, we prove two results which represent partial progress
towards Conjecture 2. First, in Subsection 3.1, we show that no exotic coBUG
may contain a uniform component other than a star. Thus, any construction of an exotic coBUG must
combine some number of star components of the same size (due to Lemma 14), together
with at least one component which is not uniform.
Next, in Subsection 3.2, we proceed towards Conjecture 2 by showing that
no exotic coBUG may contain a star component K_{1,\ell} for \ell\leq 8 . This implies that any
component in a potential exotic coBUG is either a star with at least 10 vertices, or a non-uniform
connected graph which is not a tree, and therefore has at least as many edges as vertices. Overall,
such an exotic coBUG has edge-vertex ratio greater than 9/10. By Lemma 4, this
means that any exotic BUG has betweenness more than 9/10. This line of reasoning is an outline of
the proof of Theorem 3, which is presented in more detail at the end of the section.
The results of Subsection 3.2 are proven by means of a case analysis, where we
separately consider stars K_{1,\ell} for values of \ell=0,\dotsc,8 , and show that they cannot
be combined with non-uniform graphs to form a coBUG. There is no reason why this approach could not
be pushed further to values of \ell greater than 8, thus improving the bound 9/10 from
Theorem 3. However, the arguments become more tedious and lengthy as \ell increases,
so it might be perhaps more worthwhile to look for a wholly different approach, with the aim of
solving the full Conjecture 2.
3.1 No exotic coBUGs with uniform components
Our next goal is to show that there can be no exotic coBUG with a non-star uniform component. We
first derive a collection of equalities and inequalities restricting the possible ranges of
parameters n,m,t,\ell for which one may have a coBUG on n vertices combining a component
K_{1,\ell} with a non-star (m,t) -uniform component.
Lemma 15.
Let \overline{G} be a coBUG on n vertices with a component K_{1,\ell} for some \ell , and another
component H not isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} . Suppose H is (m,t) -uniform. Then the following
holds:
1.
n=\ell+1+\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell} ,
2.
\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}\geq t ,
3.
m>\ell and t<\ell+1 , and
4.
\ell(\ell+1)\geq mt .
In particular, if H is a complete multipartite graph, which implies that m=|E(H)| and
t=|V(H)| , then the third item above says that K_{1,\ell} has fewer edges but more
vertices than H .
Proof.
Recall that co-betweenness of a vertex is coB(v)=\sum_{e\in C_{\overline{G}}(v)}w(e) and that in
disconnected coBUGs, the co-betweenness of any two vertices must be the same by
Corollary 9.
In the component of \overline{G} isomorphic to a star K_{1,\ell} , each vertex has co-betweenness
\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)} , while in the component H , each vertex has co-betweenness
\frac{m}{n-t} by Observation 11. We thus get the identity
\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}=\frac{m}{n-t}.
(2)
We first claim that m\neq\ell . Indeed, if we had m=\ell , then the above equality would imply
t=\ell+1 , and by Lemma 12, this would mean that H has fewer edges than vertices
and is therefore a tree. But by Lemma 14, all the tree components of \overline{G} must be
isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} , contradicting our assumptions about H .
Knowing that m\neq\ell , we can manipulate the identity (2) further:
\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}
\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n-t}
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle\ell(n-t)
\displaystyle=m(n-(\ell+1))
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle n(\ell-m)
\displaystyle=\ell t-m(\ell+1)
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle n
\displaystyle=\frac{m(\ell+1)-\ell t}{m-\ell}=\frac{m(\ell+1)-\ell(\ell+1)+\ell(\ell+1)-\ell t}{m-\ell}
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle n
\displaystyle=\ell+1+\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}.
This proves the first claim of the lemma. Since \overline{G} has at least one component isomorphic to
K_{1,\ell} and another isomorphic to H , we know that n\geq\ell+1+|V(H)| . Since H has at
least t vertices, this yields n=\ell+1+\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}\geq\ell+1+t , from
which we may deduce the second claim.
From the second claim, we know in particular that \ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}>0 . This
means that we have either (\ell+1)<t and m<\ell , or (\ell+1)>t and m>\ell . But the first
alternative would again mean that t\geq m+2 , i.e. H has more vertices than edges, so H is a
tree, but this is again impossible by Lemma 14. So the second alternative must be
correct, proving the third claim.
The final claim is deduced from the second and third one:
\displaystyle\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}
\displaystyle\geq t
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}-t
\displaystyle\geq 0
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle\frac{\ell(\ell+1)-\ell t-t(m-\ell)}{m-\ell}
\displaystyle\geq 0
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle\frac{\ell(\ell+1)-mt}{m-\ell}
\displaystyle\geq 0
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle\ell(\ell+1)-mt
\displaystyle\geq 0
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle\ell(\ell+1)
\displaystyle\geq mt.
∎
As an example of a simple application of the previous lemma, let us derive the following useful
consequence.
Corollary 16.
If a coBUG contains a component isomorphic to a star K_{1,\ell} then it cannot contain a
component isomorphic to a cycle. This is because a cycle is either (3,3) -uniform (for C_{3} ) or
(4,4) -uniform (for C_{k} with k\geq 4 ), and the third part of Lemma 15 would imply
either \ell<3<\ell+1 or \ell<4<\ell+1 , which is impossible.
Now we can proceed to the main result of this subsection.
Theorem 17.
An exotic coBUG cannot contain any uniform connected component other than a star.
Proof.
For a contradiction, let \overline{G} be an exotic coBUG on n vertices that contains an (m,t) -uniform
connected component H which is not a star.
By Lemma 14, \overline{G} contains a component isomorphic to a star K_{1,\ell} for some
\ell , and all the tree components of \overline{G} are isomorphic to the same star K_{1,\ell} .
From Lemma 15, we know that
n=\ell+1+\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}.
(3)
The same lemma also shows that m>\ell and t<\ell+1 , and in particular m>t .
Let e denote the number of edges of \overline{G} . Recall that the excess \ex(\overline{G}) of \overline{G} is
defined as e-n . To reach contradiction, we will now show that \overline{G} ’s excess is non-negative,
which by Lemma 4 implies that the BUG G has betweenness at least 1, and therefore
is not an exotic BUG.
We will estimate the excess of \overline{G} componentwise. Let
\mathcal{C} be the set of connected components of \overline{G} , let c_{H}\in\mathcal{C} be a connected component
isomorphic to H , \mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{C} be the set of the components of \overline{G} isomorphic to
K_{1,\ell} , and let \mathcal{C}_{2} be the set \mathcal{C}\setminus(\{c_{H}\}\cup\mathcal{C}_{1}) of all the remaining
components. For a component c\in\mathcal{C} , let e(c) and n(c) denote the number of edges and the
number of vertices of c , respectively.
Note that |\mathcal{C}_{1}| is at most \frac{n-t}{\ell+1} , since the component c_{H} has at least t
vertices and each component in \mathcal{C}_{1} has \ell+1 vertices.
We then have
\displaystyle e-n
\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\big(e(c)-n(c)\big)
\displaystyle=e(c_{H})-n(c_{H})+\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}(-1)+\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_{2}}\big(e(c)-n(c)\big)
\displaystyle\geq e(c_{H})-n(c_{H})-\frac{n-t}{\ell+1}.
(4)
Define \alpha:=\frac{e(c_{H})}{m} , and
note that \alpha\geq 1 . Recall from Lemma 12 that
\frac{e(c_{H})}{n(c_{H})}=\frac{m}{t} , and hence \frac{n(c_{H})}{t}=\frac{e(c_{H})}{m}=\alpha . Hence
e(c_{H})-n(c_{H})=\alpha(m-t)\geq m-t . Combining this with (3) and (4), and using
the notation \Delta:=m-\ell , we obtain
\displaystyle e-n
\displaystyle\geq m-t-\frac{n-t}{\ell+1}
\displaystyle=m-t-\frac{1}{\ell+1}\left(\ell+1+\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{m-\ell}-t\right)
\displaystyle=\ell+\Delta-t-\frac{1}{\ell+1}\left(\ell+1+\ell\cdot\frac{\ell+1-t}{\Delta}-t\right)
\displaystyle=\ell+\Delta-t-1-\frac{\ell}{\Delta}+\frac{\ell t}{(\ell+1)\Delta}+\frac{t}{\ell+1}
\displaystyle=\Delta-1+\ell\cdot\frac{\Delta-1}{\Delta}-t\left(1-\frac{\ell}{(\ell+1)\Delta}-\frac{1}{\ell+1}\right)
\displaystyle=\Delta-1+\ell\cdot\frac{\Delta-1}{\Delta}-t\cdot\frac{\ell(\Delta-1)}{(\ell+1)\Delta}
\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta-1}{\Delta}\left(\Delta+\ell-t\cdot\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}\right)
\displaystyle\geq 0,
(5)
where (5) follows from
\Delta+\ell-t\cdot\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}>\Delta+\ell-t=m-t>0 .
We conclude that e-n\geq 0 , showing that \overline{G} is not an exotic coBUG, a contradiction.
∎
Theorem 17 only applies to BUGs with betweenness smaller than 1. This bound is
sharp in the sense that there is an infinite family of BUGs of betweenness 1 whose complements
consist of a non-star uniform component combined with a suitable number of star
components. This is shown by the next example.
Example 3.
Let H be a complete multipartite graph with t vertices and m edges, and suppose that m>t .
Recall from Observation 13 that H is (m,t) -uniform. Define \ell:=m-1 . Let
\overline{G} be the graph obtained as a disjoint union of H with m-t disjoint copies of
K_{1,\ell} . We claim that \overline{G} is a coBUG whose complement is a BUG of betweenness 1. To see
this, note first that \overline{G} has the same number of vertices and edges: indeed, the number of
vertices is n:=(m-t)(\ell+1)+t=(m-t)m+t=m^{2}-mt+t , while the number of edges is
(m-t)(m-1)+m=m^{2}-mt+t . It then remains to verify that all the vertices in \overline{G} have the same
co-betweenness and apply Lemma 4. For a vertex x belonging to a copy of
K_{1,\ell} , the co-betweenness is
\displaystyle coB(x)
\displaystyle=\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}=\frac{m-1}{m^{2}-mt+t-m}=\frac{1}{m-t},
while for a vertex y from H , Observation 11 yields
\displaystyle coB(y)
\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n-t}=\frac{m}{m^{2}-mt+t-t}=\frac{1}{m-t}.
Since all the vertices have the same co-betweenness, \overline{G} is a coBUG, and since \overline{G} has the
same number of vertices and edges, its complement is a BUG of betweenness 1 by
Lemma 4.
We are also able to construct an example of a sequence of coBUGs combining star components and
non-star components, whose betweenness converges to 1 from above.
Example 4.
Fix a natural number t\geq 2 , and define the following parameters:
\displaystyle k
\displaystyle=4t^{2}-4t-1
\displaystyle\ell
\displaystyle=4t^{2}-1
\displaystyle b
\displaystyle=t+1
\displaystyle c
\displaystyle=2t.
Let \overline{G} be the disjoint union of k copies of the star K_{1,\ell} and b copies of the
complete bipartite graph K_{c,c} . We will show that \overline{G} is a coBUG with betweenness value
1+\frac{1}{4t} . The number of vertices of \overline{G} is
n:=k(\ell+1)+2bc=4t^{2}(4t^{2}-4t-1)+4t(t+1)=16t^{4}-16t^{3}+4t.
A vertex x in a component isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} has co-betweenness
coB(x)=\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}=\frac{4t^{2}-1}{16t^{4}-16t^{3}-4t^{2}+4t}=\frac{1}{4t^{2}-4t},
while a vertex y in a component isomorphic to K_{c,c} has co-betweenness
coB(y)=\frac{c^{2}}{n-2c}=\frac{4t^{2}}{16t^{4}-16t^{3}}=\frac{1}{4t^{2}-4t},
showing that \overline{G} is indeed a coBUG. To determine the betweenness, we first compute the number
of edges
e:=k\ell+bc^{2}=16t^{4}-12t^{3}-4t^{2}+4t+1,
and then use Lemma 4 to determine that the betweenness is
\frac{e}{n}=\frac{16t^{4}-12t^{3}-4t^{2}+4t+1}{16t^{4}-16t^{3}+4t}=\frac{4t+1}{4t}.
By Lemma 14 and Theorem 17, any exotic coBUG must consist of some number of
star components isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} together with some number of non-uniform components.
Getting a valid coBUG by combining non-uniform components with star components is quite tricky,
even when we do not require low betweenness. However, with the help of a computer-assisted search,
we can show that, at least without the low betweenness restriction, such coBUGs exist.
Our construction is based on the concept of inflation. For a graph H and a number k , the
k -fold inflation of H is the graph H[k] obtained by replacing each vertex x of H by
a k -tuple of new vertices x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k} forming a clique, and replacing every edge xy of H
by the k^{2} edges x_{i}y_{j} for all 1\leq i,j\leq k . In particular, if H has n vertices and e
edges, then H[k] has kn vertices and n\binom{k}{2}+ek^{2} edges.
We can now present the promised example of a coBUG combining tree components with non-uniform
components.
Example 5.
Let H be the 27-fold inflation of the cycle of length 721. Note that H is not
a uniform graph. Although every vertex of H has the same number of edges close to it (namely
4\cdot 27^{2}+3\cdot\binom{27}{2}=3969 ), there are two types of edges: the first type are the edges
connecting a pair of vertices x_{i}x_{j} obtained by inflating a single vertex x of the original
cycle (which have 3\cdot 27=81 vertices close to them), and the second type are the edges
connecting a pair of the form x_{i}y_{j} with xy an edge of the original cycle (which have
4\cdot 27=108 vertices close to them).
Let \overline{G} be the graph obtained as a disjoint union of H and 81 copies of the star K_{1,3924} .
In particular, \overline{G} has n:=27\cdot 721+81\cdot 3925=337392 vertices and e:=721\cdot\binom{27}{2}+721\cdot 27^{2}+81\cdot 3924=1096524 edges.
To verify that \overline{G} is a coBUG, we compute the co-betweenness of every vertex. For a vertex w
of a star component, we clearly have coB(w)=\frac{3924}{n-3925}=\frac{3924}{333467} . For a vertex
v of H , we first observe that an edge of the first type has weight
\frac{1}{n-81}=\frac{1}{337311} , while an edge of the second type has weight
\frac{1}{n-108}=\frac{1}{337284} . This gives
coB(v)=\frac{3\cdot\binom{27}{2}}{337311}+\frac{4\cdot 27^{2}}{337284}=\frac{3924}{333467},
showing that all the vertices have the same co-betweenness, and \overline{G} is indeed a coBUG. It is not
exotic, however, since the betweenness of the
corresponding BUG G is B(G)=\frac{e}{n}=\frac{13}{4}\geq 1 by Lemma 4.
Note that this construction also works when we replace the original cycle of length 721 by any
graph on 721 vertices formed by a disjoint union of cycles of length at least 4.
3.2 No exotic BUGs with betweenness up to \frac{9}{10}
In this section, we show that there are no exotic BUGs whose betweenness value is at most
\frac{9}{10} . To reach this result, we will show that any coBUG containing a connected
component isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} for some \ell\leq 8 must only consist of a disjoint union of
some number of copies of K_{1,\ell} . Since any potential exotic coBUG must contain a star
component, as well as at least one non-star component, this means that the star components in
exotic coBUGs are of the form K_{1,m} for some m\geq 9 , implying that the coBUG has
edge-to-vertex ratio greater than \frac{m}{m+1}\geq\frac{9}{10} .
We begin by collecting general facts about the possible structure of a non-star component in an
exotic coBUG.
Lemma 18.
Let \overline{G} be an exotic coBUG on n vertices, and let H be a non-star component of \overline{G} .
Then H has the following properties.
1.
H has no vertex of degree 1.
2.
H has no vertex of degree 2 whose neighbours are adjacent; in other words, there
is no triangle in H containing a vertex of degree 2.
3.
Suppose that x and y are two adjacent vertices of H which both have degree 2.
Then H does not contain any cycle of length 4 or 5 containing both x and y .
4.
H has no triple of vertices x,y,z , all of degree 2, such that xy and yz are
both edges of H .
Proof.
The first claim follows from Lemma 14.
For the rest of the proof, recall from Theorem 17 that H is not a uniform graph, and in
particular H is neither a cycle nor a complete multipartite graph.
To prove the second claim, let x\in V(\overline{G}) be a vertex of degree two adjacent to vertices y
and z such that yz\in E(\overline{G}) . Note that C_{\overline{G}}(x) is a subset of C_{\overline{G}}(y) as
well as of C_{\overline{G}}(z) . In view of Lemma 10, this means that
C_{\overline{G}}(x)=C_{\overline{G}}(y)=C_{\overline{G}}(z) . Since H is not a cycle, at least one of the two vertices y,z
must have degree greater than 2. Suppose that \deg(y)>2 and let w be any vertex adjacent to y .
Since any edge incident to w is close to y , it must be also close to x , and therefore any
edge incident to w must connect it to y or to z . Since H has no vertices of degree 1, any
vertex of H different from y or z must have degree two and be adjacent to both y
and z . It follows that H is isomorphic to the complete multipartite graph K_{1,1,\ell} for
some \ell\geq 1 , which is impossible since H is not uniform. This proves the second claim.
To prove the third claim, let x and y be two adjacent vertices of degree 2 in H , let u be
the neighbor of x different from y and let v be the neighbor of y different from x .
From the second claim, it follows that u\neq v . To prove the third claim, we need to show that
u and v are not adjacent and that they do not share any common neighbor.
Suppose first that uv is an edge of H . Then C_{\overline{G}}(x)\subseteq C_{\overline{G}}(u) and
C_{\overline{G}}(y)\subseteq C_{\overline{G}}(v) . By Lemma 10, these inclusions must in fact be
equalities. However, any edge incident to u other than ux and uv would belong to
C_{\overline{G}}(v)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(y) , hence \deg(u)=2 , and by the same argument, \deg(v)=2 , showing
that H is isomorphic to C_{4} , which is impossible.
Now suppose that u and v have a common neighbor w . Observe that each of the five edges xy ,
yv , vw , wu , and ux has weight at least \frac{1}{n-4} , with equality if and only if both
vertices of the edge have degree 2. Since coB(x)=coB(w) , it follows that the total weight of
edges in C_{\overline{G}}(x)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(w) is the same as the total weight of edges in
C_{\overline{G}}(w)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(x) . The set C_{\overline{G}}(x)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(w) contains only the edge xy ,
which has weight \frac{1}{n-4} , while C_{\overline{G}}(w)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(x) contain the edge wv , as
well as all the other edges incident to w or v , other than yv and wu . It follows that wv
has weight \frac{1}{n-4} , and C_{\overline{G}}(w)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(x) does not contain any other edge.
Symmetrically, C_{\overline{G}}(w)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(y) only contains the edge wu of weight \frac{1}{n-4} .
It follows that the vertices u , v and w all have degree 2, and H\cong C_{5} , which is
impossible. This proves the third claim.
To prove the fourth claim, note that since H is not a cycle, it must contain a vertex w of
degree at least 3. We may assume without loss of generality that this vertex is adjacent to z .
From the third claim of the lemma, we know that w is not adjacent to x and has no common
neighbors with x . The only edge in C_{\overline{G}}(z)\setminus C_{\overline{G}}(w) is the edge xy , which has
the smallest possible weight \frac{1}{n-4} . We will now show that there are at least two edges
close to w which are not close to z . Let u and v be any two neighbors of w different
from z . Note that u is incident to at least one edge e_{u} which does not connect it to w or
to v : indeed, if u were only incident to the two edges uw and uv , we would get a
contradiction with the second claim. Similarly, v is incident to an edge e_{v} which does not
connect it to u or w . The two edges e_{u} and e_{v} are close to w but not to z , and their
combined weight is at least \frac{2}{n-4} . This shows that coB(w)>coB(z) , a contradiction.
∎
We will now deduce several simple but useful consequences of Lemma 18.
Lemma 19.
Let H be a non-star component of an exotic coBUG \overline{G} on n vertices. Then any edge
of H has at least four vertices close to it, and therefore its weight is at least \frac{1}{n-4} .
Proof.
Let e=xy be an edge of H . By Lemma 18, both x and y have degree
at least 2, and if both have degree exactly two, they do not share a common neighbor. It follows
that e is close to at least four vertices and has weight
w(e)=\frac{1}{n-|C_{\overline{G}}(e)|}\geq\frac{1}{n-4} .
∎
Lemma 20.
Any non-star component of an exotic coBUG has at least six vertices.
Proof.
Let H be a non-star component of an exotic coBUG on n vertices, and suppose that H has at
most five vertices. Since H is not uniform, and therefore in particular not complete multipartite,
in contains an induced copy of K_{1}+K_{2} by Observation 13. In other words, it
contains three vertices x,y,z where yz is an edge of H but xy and xz are not. Since x
has degree at least two by Lemma 18, it is adjacent to two other
vertices u and v . It follows that x has degree exactly two and V(H)=\{x,y,z,u,v\} . Each of
the two vertices u and v must have at least one neighbor other than x . By the second claim of
Lemma 18, uv is not an edge of H .
If one of the two vertices u and v has degree 2, we will obtain a cycle of length 4 or 5 with
two adjacent vertices of degree two, which is impossible. If both u and v have degree 3, then
H is isomorphic to the graph K_{5}^{-} , obtained from K_{5} by the removal of one edge.
Then C_{\overline{G}}(x)=E(H)\setminus\{yz\} , while C_{\overline{G}}(y)=E(H) , contradicting
Lemma 10.
∎
Many of our arguments are based on the notion of excess \ex(H) of a graph H . We now collect some
of the salient facts related to this notion.
Observation 21.
For any graph H=(V,E) , its excess can be expressed as
\ex(H)=|E|-|V|=\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\deg(x)\right)-|V|=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\left(\deg(x)-2\right).
In particular, if H has minimum degree at least 3, then \ex(H)\geq\frac{|V|}{2} .
Observation 22.
Let \overline{G} be an exotic coBUG on n vertices with a non-star component H on t vertices and a
star component K_{1,\ell} . Then \overline{G} must have negative excess, and in particular, it must have
more than \ex(H) components isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} . Since t\geq 6 by
Lemma 20, we get
n\geq(\ex(H)+1)(\ell+1)+t\geq(\ex(H)+1)(\ell+1)+6.
Lemma 23.
Any non-star component H of an exotic coBUG has excess at least 2.
Proof.
Since H is not a star, it has non-negative excess. Since it is not uniform, and therefore not a
cycle, it has excess at least 1. By Observation 21, a connected graph with excess
equal to 1 and minimum degree 2 either contains two vertices of degree 3, or one vertex of degree 4,
while all the remaining vertices have degree 2. However, any such graph is in contradiction with
Lemma 18, since it contains a vertex of degree 2 in a triangle, two
adjacent vertices of degree 2 on a cycle of length 4 or 5, or three vertices of degree 2 forming a
path.
∎
Corollary 24.
Any exotic coBUG has at least three star components. In particular, with the notation of
Observation 22, we have n\geq 3(\ell+1)+t\geq 3\ell+9 .
Another useful fact is the following relation between the degree of a vertex and the number of edges close to it in a non-star component of a complement of low-betweenness BUG.
Lemma 25.
If H is a non-star component of an exotic coBUG \overline{G} , then any vertex v\in V(H) of
degree d has at least 2d edges close to it.
Proof.
Consider any w\in N(v) . We want to show that any w\in N(v) contributes on average by at least two edges to C_{\overline{G}}(v) .
Clearly, every w contributes at least by the edge vw .
By claim 1 of Lemma 18, either \deg(w)\geq 3 or w has a
neighbour x outside of N(v) . In the latter case, wx\in C_{\overline{G}}(v) is the second edge which
uniquely contributes to the cardinality of C_{\overline{G}}(v) .
In the case of \deg(w)\geq 3 , we have that w is adjacent to at least two vertices different from v .
If at least one of these vertices is outside of N(v) , then the corresponding edge uniquely contributes one to C_{\overline{G}}(v) . Otherwise, each such edge contributes by one half to |C_{\overline{G}}(v)| and as there are at least two such edges, the resulting contribution to |C_{\overline{G}}(v)| for w is at least two.
∎
Lemma 26.
In an exotic coBUG on n vertices, every vertex has co-betweenness at least \frac{6}{n-4} .
Proof.
Let H be a non-star component of an exotic coBUG \overline{G} on n vertices. It follows from
Lemma 18 that every edge is close to at least four vertices, and
therefore has weight at least \frac{1}{n-4} . Moreover, at least one vertex x of H has degree
at least 3, and by Lemma 25, such a vertex is close to at least six edges. In
particular the co-betweenness of such a vertex (and therefore of any vertex in \overline{G} ) is at least
\frac{6}{n-4} .
∎
3.2.1 Ruling out small stars in exotic coBUGs
Here we show that stars K_{1,\ell} for \ell\in\{0,1,2,\dots,8\} cannot appear as
components of exotic coBUGs. We start with small stars and proceed to larger ones. The arguments
become steadily more and more complicated as the size of the stars considered grows.
For small stars, Corollary 24 by itself is sufficient.
Lemma 27.
For \ell\in\{0,1,2,3,4\} , there is no exotic coBUG containing K_{1,\ell} as component.
Proof.
Suppose that an exotic coBUG on n vertices contains a component K_{1,\ell} , for some \ell\leq 4 . The vertices in such a star component have co-betweenness \frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}\leq\frac{4}{n-5} . At the same time, the vertices of \overline{G} have co-betweenness at least
\frac{6}{n-4} by Lemma 26. This yields
\displaystyle\frac{4}{n-5}
\displaystyle\geq\frac{6}{n-4}
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle 4(n-4)
\displaystyle\geq 6(n-5)
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle 7
\displaystyle\geq n.
This is impossible by Corollary 24.
∎
Remark 1.
Let K_{4}^{-} be the graph obtained by removing an edge from K_{4} . We may easily verify that the
disjoint union of K_{4}^{-} with K_{1,4} is a coBUG. It is not a counterexample to
Lemma 27, because its betweenness value is 1, and hence it is not exotic. This is in
fact a special case of the construction described in Example 3.
The argument from the proof of Lemma 27 can be extended to \ell=5 .
Lemma 28.
There is no exotic coBUG with a component isomorphic to K_{1,5} .
Proof.
If such an exotic coBUG existed, its vertices would have a betweenness of \frac{5}{n-6} , and by
Lemma 26,
\frac{5}{n-6}\geq\frac{6}{n-4},
which gives n\leq 16 , contradicting Corollary 24.
∎
For \ell\geq 6 , slightly more elaborate arguments are needed. For a graph H , we define the
closeness of H , denoted c(H) , as the maximum number of edges that are close to a single
vertex, i.e., c(H)=\max_{x\in V(H)}|C_{H}(x)| . We collect the main relevant properties of
this parameter in our next lemma.
Lemma 29.
Let H be a non-star component of an exotic coBUG on n vertices which contains a star component
isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} . Let c be the closeness of H . Then
1.
H has maximum degree at most \lfloor c/2\rfloor ,
2.
c>\ell ,
3.
n\leq\lfloor\frac{c(\ell+1)-4\ell}{c-\ell}\rfloor .
Proof.
The first part follows from Lemma 25. To prove the second part, assume for
contradiction that every vertex of H has at most \ell edges close to it. From the first part,
we know that all the vertices of H have degree at most \ell/2 , which means that every edge of
H has at most \ell vertices close to it, and therefore its weight is at most
\frac{1}{n-\ell} . It follows that the co-betweenness of any vertex in H is at most
\frac{\ell}{n-\ell} . However, \overline{G} contains star components isomorphic to K_{1,\ell} , whose
vertices have co-betweenness \frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}>\frac{\ell}{n-\ell} , showing that \overline{G} is
not a coBUG.
To prove the last part, fix a vertex x of H that has c edges close to it. Since every edge
has weight at least \frac{1}{n-4} by Lemma 19, we conclude that
coB(x) is at least \frac{c}{n-4} . Simultaneously, all the vertices of \overline{G} have
co-betweenness \frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)} , since \overline{G} contains K_{1,\ell} as a component.
Combining these estimates, while recalling that c>\ell from the second part of this lemma and
that n\geq 3\ell+9 from Corollary 24, yields
\displaystyle\frac{c}{n-4}
\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell}{n-(\ell+1)}
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle c(n-(\ell+1))
\displaystyle\leq\ell(n-4)
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle n(c-\ell)
\displaystyle\leq c(\ell+1)-4\ell
\displaystyle\iff
\displaystyle n
\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(\ell+1)-4\ell}{c-\ell}.
The lemma follows.
∎
Corollary 30.
Observe that for a fixed integer \ell\geq 0 , the expression \frac{c(\ell+1)-4\ell}{c-\ell} is a
decreasing function of c for c\in(\ell,+\infty) . Thus, for c an integer greater than \ell ,
the expression is maximised when c=\ell+1 , and Lemma 29 then yields n\leq(\ell+1)^{2}-4\ell .
Lemma 31.
There is no exotic coBUG with a component isomorphic to K_{1,6} .
Proof.
Corollary 30 yields n\leq 25 , while Corollary 24 yields n\geq 27 , a
contradiction.
∎
For \ell=7 , we again need a more involved argument.
Lemma 32.
There is no exotic coBUG with a component isomorphic to K_{1,7} .
Proof.
Suppose that \overline{G} is such a coBUG on n vertices, and H its non-star component.
Corollary 24 yields n\geq 30 .
Let c be the closeness of H . Part 2 of
Lemma 29 shows that c\geq 8 . If c\geq 9 , then part 3 of the same lemma implies
that n\leq\frac{9\cdot 8-4\cdot 7}{2}=22 , a contradiction (note that we use here the monotonicity
that we pointed out in Corollary 30).
We therefore conclude that c=8 , and Corollary 30 yields the
bound n\leq 36 . Combining this with the inequality n\geq 8(\ex(H)+1)+6 from
Observation 22, we get \ex(H)\leq 2 , and in view of Lemma 23,
\ex(H)=2 .
By part 1 of Lemma 29, all the vertices of H have degree at
most 4. Therefore, every edge of H is close to at most eight vertices and has weight at most
\frac{1}{n-8} . Note that due to the presence of the K_{1,7} component, all the vertices of
\overline{G} have co-betweenness \frac{7}{n-8} . In particular, every vertex of H must be close to at
least seven edges, and if it is close to exactly seven edges, all of them must have weight
\frac{1}{n-8} .
Note that H must contain a vertex of degree 2: if all its vertices had degree at least 3, then
from Lemma 20 and Observation 21, we would get \ex(H)\geq 3 , which we have already excluded. Let x then be a vertex of H of degree 2, and let y and
z be its two neighbors. Note that each of the two edges xy and xz is close to at most 6
vertices, therefore it has weight at most \frac{1}{n-6} . By the argument of the previous
paragraph, this means that x must be close to eight edges, otherwise its co-betweenness would be
too low. Consequently, the two vertices y and z both have degree 4 and they are not adjacent to
each other.
Since \ex(H)=2 , Observation 21 implies that y and z are the only vertices of
degree 4 in H , and all the remaining vertices have degree 2. Since x was chosen as an arbitrary
vertex of degree 2, it follows that all the vertices of degree 2 are adjacent to y and z ,
showing that H is the complete bipartite graph K_{2,4} . This, however, is impossible, since
K_{2,4} is uniform and cannot be a component of an exotic coBUG.
∎
Lemma 33.
There is no exotic coBUG with a component isomorphic to K_{1,8} .
Proof.
We again fix an exotic coBUG \overline{G} on n vertices, with star-components isomorphic to K_{1,8}
and a non-star component H . Corollary 24 yields n\geq 33 . Let c be the closeness
of H , which is at least 9 by part 2 of Lemma 29, and in fact it is exactly 9, since for
c\geq 10 the third part of the same lemma would imply n\leq 29 , while for c=9 , the lemma gives
n\leq 49 . It follows that the excess of H is at most 3, by Observation 22.
From c=9 , we conclude that the maximum degree of H is at most 4 by part 1 of Lemma 29, and therefore any edge of H
is close to at most eight vertices and has weight at most \frac{1}{n-8} . All the vertices of H
then must be close to exactly 9 edges, because a vertex close to at most eight edges would have
co-betweenness at most \frac{8}{n-8} , less than the actual value of \frac{8}{n-9} .
Every vertex of H therefore has degree at least 3, since a vertex of degree 2 would be close to
at most eight edges. It follows by Observation 21 that \ex(H)\geq|V(H)|/2 . This, in
view of the bound |V(H)|\geq 6 from Lemma 20 and the inequality \ex(H)\leq 3 derived above, tells us that \ex(H)=3 , |V(H)|=6 , and every vertex of H has degree 3.
Since H is a 3-regular graph on six vertices, it has nine edges. Since each of its vertices is
close to nine edges, it follows that every vertex is close to every edge, therefore H is a
complete multipartite graph by Observation 13, and therefore cannot be a component
of a coBUG.
∎
We are finally ready to combine the ingredients into the proof of our main result.
Proof of Theorem 3.
For contradiction, let G be an exotic BUG on n vertices with betweenness at most 9/10, and let
\overline{G} be its complement. By Lemma 14, \overline{G} must contain a star component
K_{1,\ell} for some integer \ell , and all its star components are of the same size. From the
previous lemmas of this section, we know that \ell\geq 9 . Furthermore, \overline{G} must also contain at
least one non-star component, and all such non-star components have more edges than vertices by
Lemma 23. Let t be the total number of vertices in the non-star components of \overline{G} ,
and let m be their total number of edges; in particular, m is greater than t . Let S be the
number of star components in \overline{G} , and let e be the number of edges of \overline{G} . The
betweenness of G is equal to e/n by Lemma 4. We calculate:
\displaystyle\frac{9}{10}\geq B(G)
\displaystyle=\frac{e}{n}
\displaystyle=\frac{\ell S+m}{(\ell+1)S+t}
\displaystyle=\frac{\ell(S+(m/\ell))}{(\ell+1)(S+t/(\ell+1))}
\displaystyle>\frac{\ell}{\ell+1}\geq 9/10,
a contradiction.
∎
4 Conclusion and open questions
Our results provide a complete characterization of betweenness-uniform graphs with betweenness at most 9/10, by showing that they are complements of graphs that consist of disjoint copies of K_{1,\ell} for some integer \ell , in which case their betweenness is equal to \ell/(\ell+1) . Our main open problem is to settle Conjecture 2, which states that this characterization can be extended to all BUGs of betweenness less than 1.
For BUGs of betweenness equal to 1, such simple characterization is no longer available, and indeed, we have provided several different constructions of BUGs that have betweenness equal to 1. It is an open problem to provide a full description of all the BUGs with betweenness equal to 1.
For betweenness values larger than 1, even less is known. In particular, we do not know which values \alpha>1 are equal to the betweenness of a BUG. Clearly, any such \alpha must be rational, but are there any other restrictions? Is there a rational number greater than 1 that is not equal to the betweenness of any BUG? Is the set of such rational numbers infinite? Is it unbounded? Is it even dense inside the interval (1,+\infty) ? All these questions, and many others like them, remain open.
