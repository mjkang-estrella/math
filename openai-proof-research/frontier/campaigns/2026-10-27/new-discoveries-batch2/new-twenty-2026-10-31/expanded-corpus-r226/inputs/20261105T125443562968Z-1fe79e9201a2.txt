 Abstract. 
    
 It was conjectured by Edoukou in 2008 that a non-degenerate Hermitian threefold in  ℙ 4 ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})  has at most  d ⁡ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1  points in common with a threefold of degree  d d  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . He proved the conjecture for  d = 2 d=2 . In this paper, we show that the conjecture is true for  d = 3 d=3  and  q ≥ 7 q\geq 7 . 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  Hermitian threefolds, cubic threefolds, rational points, Edoukou’s Conjecture
 
 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification Primary 14G05, 14G15, 05B25
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 Hermitian varieties over finite fields are fascinating objects in the theory of algebraic geometry, finite geometry, coding theory, and combinatorics. Bose and Chakravarti first studied these varieties in  [ 2 ] . The popularity of Hermitian varieties grew among coding theorists since the non-degenerate Hermitian varieties admit a very high number of rational points. Furthermore, Hermitian varieties come with fascinating point-line incidence structures, which are attractive to mathematicians working in finite geometry and combinatorics. From a coding theoretic perspective, the following question has been studied in  [ 14 ,  7 ,  8 ,  3 ,  4 ]  among other articles. 
 
 
 Question 1.1 . 
 
 Let  V m − 1 V_{m-1}  be a non-degenerate Hermitian variety in  ℙ m ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}) . Determine the maximum possible number of  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points common to a hypersurface of degree  d d  in  ℙ m {\mathbb{P}}^{m}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  and  V m − 1 V_{m-1} . 
 
 
 
 The case when  d = 1 d=1  is completely settled in  [ 2 ,  5 ] . When  m = 2 m=2 , one could derive from using Bézout’s Theorem that a curve of degree  d d  and a non-degenerate Hermitian curve intersect at a maximum of  d ⁡ ( q + 1 ) d(q+1)  many  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points. It is fairly easy to produce a plane curve of degree  d d  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  that intersects the Hermitian curve at  d ⁡ ( q + 1 ) d(q+1)  points. However, the question becomes much more difficult as  m m  increases. The case  m = 3 m=3 , to our knowledge, was first studied by Sørensen, in his Ph.D. thesis  [ 14 ] . He conjectured that a non-degenerate Hermitian surface in  ℙ 3 ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})  has at most  d ⁡ ( q 3 + q 2 − q ) + q + 1 d(q^{3}+q^{2}-q)+q+1  many  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points in common with a surface in  ℙ 3 {\mathbb{P}}^{3}  of degree  d d  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . Even though the conjecture was formulated in the early 1990s, the first breakthrough was made by Edoukou in 2007. In  [ 7 ] , he proved that the conjecture is true for  d = 2 d=2 . Sørensen’s conjecture was finally proved in a series of two papers  [ 3 ,  4 ] . It is now natural to look at the case when  m = 4 m=4 . Edoukou, in  [ 8 ] , determined the answer to Question  1.1  for  d = 2 d=2  and  m = 4 m=4 . Besides, Edoukou proposed the following conjecture: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1 . 
 
 [ 8 , Conjecture 1] 
Let  F ∈ 𝔽 q 2 ​ [ x 0 , … , x 4 ] F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{4}]  be a homogeneous polynomial of degree  d d  and  V 3 V_{3}  be a non-degenerate Hermitian threefold in  ℙ 4 ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}) . If  d ≤ q d\leq q , then 
 

 
 
 | V ⁡ ( F ) ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) ∩ V 3 | ≤ d ⁡ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 . |V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq d(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1. 
 
 
 Moreover, this bound is attained by a threefold  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4} , if and only if  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  is a union of  d d  non-tangent hyperplanes  Σ 1 , … , Σ d \Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{d}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , such that  Σ 1 , … , Σ d \Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{d}  contains a common plane  Π \Pi  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  and  Π \Pi  intersects  V 3 V_{3}  at a non-degenerate plane Hermitian curve. 
 
 
 
 As mentioned above, he proved Conjecture  1  for  d = 2 d=2 . The conjecture remained open to date. In this paper, we will prove the conjecture for  d = 3 d=3  and  q ≥ 7 q\geq 7 . It is worth noting that Edoukou’s proof of the conjecture for  d = 2 d=2  depends on the well-known classification of quadrics in  ℙ 4 ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}) . However, our approach is more algebro-geometric. We will in fact provide a proof of Edoukou’s result using our method in Corollary  4.6 . 
 
 
 This paper is organized as follows. In Section  2 , we recall various well-known properties of Hermitian varieties, and in particular non-degenerate Hermitian threefolds, revisit some preliminary notions from algebraic geometry and some bounds on the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field.
In Section  3  we take a step back to derive an upper bound for the number of  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points common to a degenerate Hermitian surface and a surface of degree  d d  in  ℙ 3 ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{3}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}) . In Section  4  we present some results for general values of  d ≤ q d\leq q  towards proving Edoukou’s conjecture. Finally, in Section  5 , we prove our main result. 
 
 
 
 2.  Preliminaries 

 
 Let us fix  q q , a prime power. As usual,  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q}  and  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  denote the finite fields with  q q  and  q 2 q^{2}  elements, respectively. For  m ≥ 0 m\geq 0 , we denote by  ℙ m {\mathbb{P}}^{m} , the projective space of dimension  m m  over the algebraic closure  𝔽 ¯ q \overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q} , while  ℙ m ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})  will denote the set of all  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points on  ℙ m {\mathbb{P}}^{m} .
Similarly,  𝔸 m {\mathbb{A}}^{m}  and  𝔸 m ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{A}}^{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})  will denote the affine space of dimension  m m  over  𝔽 ¯ q \overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}  and the set of all  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points of  𝔸 m {\mathbb{A}}^{m}  respectively.
Further, for a homogeneous polynomial  F ∈ 𝔽 q 2 ​ [ x 0 , … , x m ] F\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},\dots,x_{m}] , we denote by  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F) , the set of zeroes of  F F  in  ℙ m {\mathbb{P}}^{m}  and by  𝖵 ⁡ ( F ) = V ⁡ ( F ) ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathsf{V}}(F)=V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})  the set of all  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points of  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F) . By an algebraic variety, we mean a set of zeroes of a certain set of polynomials in the affine space or projective space, depending on the context. In particular, an algebraic variety need not be irreducible. We remark that whenever we say that a variety is nonsingular, we will mean that the variety is nonsingular over  𝔽 ¯ q \overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q} . However, when we say that a hypersurface  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  is irreducible, we will mean that the polynomial  F F  is irreducible in the field of its definition. This section is divided into three subsections: in the first subsection, we recall several known facts about Hermitian varieties over finite fields; the second subsection is dedicated to revisiting some useful notions from basic algebraic geometry, while the third subsection concerns some known bounds on the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field. None of the results mentioned in this section is new, and their proof can be found in the indicated references. 
 
 
 2.1.  Geometry of Hermitian threefolds over finite fields 

 
 To begin with, let us revisit the definition and several properties of Hermitian varieties (cf.  [ 2 ,  5 ] ) that will be used in the latter part of this paper.

We denote by  A ( q ) A^{(q)}  the matrix whose  ( i , j ) (i,j) -th entry is given by  a i ​ j q a_{ij}^{q} . The matrix  A A  is said to be a  Hermitian matrix  if  A ≠ 0 A\neq 0  and  A T = A ( q ) A^{T}=A^{(q)} . Strictly speaking, from an algebro-geometric point of view, one should denote this set by  V m − 1 ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) V_{m-1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}) . But to maintain the classical notation, we will stick to the symbol  V m − 1 V_{m-1}  to denote the set of all  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points on  V m − 1 V_{m-1} . The Hermitian variety is said to be  non-degenerate  if  rank ​ A = m + 1 \mathrm{rank}\ A=m+1  and  degenerate  otherwise. 
 
 
 
 It was established in  [ 2 , Equation 5.6]  that if the rank of a Hermitian matrix is  r r , then by a suitable change of coordinates over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , one can describe the corresponding Hermitian variety by the zero set of the polynomial 
 

 
 (1)

 2.2.  Preliminaries from algebraic geometry 

 
 Here we recall various basic results from algebraic geometry that will be instrumental for proving our main theorem. We will make use of the notions of dimension, degree and singularity of a variety, as can be found in standard textbooks of Algebraic Geometry, for example, the book of Harris  [ 9 ] . We begin with the following definitions. A variety is said to be of  pure dimension  or  equidimensional  if all the irreducible components of the variety have equal dimension. Further, two equidimensional varieties  X , Y ⊂ ℙ m X,Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{m}  are said to  intersect properly  if  codim ⁡ ( X ∩ Y ) = codim ​ X + codim ​ Y . \mathrm{codim}(X\cap Y)=\mathrm{codim}\ X+\mathrm{codim}\ Y.  It turns out that good upper bounds for the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field, depend on the degree and dimension of the variety. Because of this reason, the following proposition from  [ 9 ]  will be indispensable for us. 
 
 
 Proposition 2.10 . 
 
 [ 9 , Cor. 18.5] 
Let  X X  and  Y Y  be equidimensional varieties that intersect properly in  ℙ m {\mathbb{P}}^{m} . Then
 deg ⁡ ( X ∩ Y ) ≤ deg ⁡ X ​ deg ⁡ Y \deg(X\cap Y)\leq\deg X\deg Y . 
 
 
 
 Proposition 2.11 . 
 
 Let  F , G ∈ 𝔽 q 2 ​ [ x 0 , x 1 , … , x m ] F,G\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]  be nonconstant homogeneous polynomials having no common factors. Then 
 
 (a)  
 
 V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  and  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  intersect properly. 
 
 (b)  
 
 V ⁡ ( F ) ∩ V ⁡ ( G ) V(F)\cap V(G)  is equidimensional of dimension  m − 2 m-2 . 
 
 (c)  
 
 deg ⁡ ( V ⁡ ( F ) ∩ V ⁡ ( G ) ) ≤ deg ⁡ F ​ deg ​ G \deg(V(F)\cap V(G))\leq\deg F\deg G . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Part (a) is well-known in Algebraic Geometry and is generally proved using Krull’s principal ideal theorem. Part (b) is proved using Macaulay’s unmixedness theorem (See  [ 15 , Chapter 7, Theorem 26] ). Part (c) follows straightaway from Proposition  2.10 .
∎

 Suppose  V V  is a Hermitian variety in  ℙ m ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) {\mathbb{P}}^{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})  of rank at least  3 3 .  2.3.  Basic upper bounds 

 
 In this subsection, we recall some well-known upper bounds on the number of rational points on varieties defined over a finite field  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q}  with given degrees and dimensions. We begin with a result by J. -P. Serre  [ 12 ]  that is undoubtedly one of the fundamental results. This was independently proved by Sørensen  [ 13 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 2.13   (Serre’s inequality) . 
 
 Let  F ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ x 0 , x 1 , … , x m ] F\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]  be a nonconstant homogeneous polynomial of degree  d ≤ q d\leq q . Then 
 

 
 
 | 𝖵 ⁡ ( F ) | ≤ d ​ q m − 1 + p m − 2 , |{\mathsf{V}}(F)|\leq dq^{m-1}+p_{m-2}, 
 
 
 where  p m − 2 = 1 + q + ⋯ + q m − 2 p_{m-2}=1+q+\dots+q^{m-2} . Moreover,
equality holds if and only if  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  is a union of  d d  hyperplanes defined over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q}  all containing a common linear subspace of codimension  2 2 . 
 
 
 
 In particular, any plane curve defined over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q}  of degree  d ≤ q d\leq q  has at most  d ​ q + 1 dq+1  many  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -rational points. Furthermore, a plane curve of degree  d d  that admits exactly  d ​ q + 1 dq+1  many  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -rational points is a union of  d d  lines passing through a common point. Among various other known bounds for plane curves defined over finite fields, we would make use of the following result  [ 1 , Corollary 2.5]  by Aubry and Perret. 
 
 
 Theorem 2.14 . 
 
 [ 1 , Corollary 2.5] 
Let  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  be a plane absolutely irreducible algebraic curve defined over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} . Then  | # ​ C ​ ( 𝔽 q ) − ( q + 1 ) | ≤ ( d − 1 ) ​ ( d − 2 ) ​ q |\#C({\mathbb{F}}_{q})-(q+1)|\leq(d-1)(d-2)\sqrt{q} , where  d = deg ⁡ 𝒞 d=\deg\mathcal{C} . 
 
 
 
 For nonsingular curves, the above bound is known as  Hasse-Weil bound . However, the result by Aubry and Perret applies to a larger family of curves. To solve our problem, it is imperative that we consider bounds on varieties that are not necessarily hypersurfaces. To this end, we have the following result by Lachaud and Rolland. 
 
 
 Proposition 2.15 . 
 
 [ 11 , Prop. 2.3] 
Let  X X  be an equidimensional projective (resp. affine) variety defined over a finite field  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} . Further assume that  dim X = δ \dim X=\delta  and  deg ⁡ X = d \deg X=d . Then 
 

 
 
 | X ( 𝔽 q ) | ≤ d p δ ( resp . | X ( 𝔽 q ) | ≤ d q δ ) , |X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\leq dp_{\delta}\ \ \ \ \ \ (\mathrm{resp.}\ \ |X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})|\leq dq^{\delta}), 
 
 
 where  p δ = 1 + q + ⋯ + q δ p_{\delta}=1+q+\dots+q^{\delta} . 
 
 
 
 The following result from  [ 11 ]  on varieties that are irreducible over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} , but not absolutely irreducible, will be very important. 
 
 
 Proposition 2.16 . 
 
 [ 11 , Prop. 3.8] 
Let  X X  be a projective variety defined over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q}  that is irreducible over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} , but not absolutely irreducible. Then  X ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) = Sing ⁡ ( X ) ​ ( 𝔽 q ) X({\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\mathrm{Sing}(X)({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) , where  Sing ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{Sing}(X)  is the subset of  X X  consisting of all the singular points of  X X  in  𝔽 ¯ q \overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q} . 
 
 
 
 The above result shows, in particular, that if  X X  is a projective variety defined over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} , that is irreducible over  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q}  but not absolutely irreducible, then any  𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} -rational point on  X X  is a singular point of  X X . We conclude this section by stating a recent result that will be useful later.

 [ 6 , Theorem 3.1]

 Lemma 4.5 .

 5.  Intersection of cubic threefolds with Hermitian threefold 

 
 The main goal of this section is to determine the maximum number of  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} -rational points on the intersection of a cubic threefold and Hermitian threefolds in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4} . To this end, we will denote by  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  a cubic threefold in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  while we keep using  V 3 V_{3}  for denoting the non-degenerate Hermitian threefold in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4} . Note that, we have dealt with threefolds that 
 
 (a)  
 
 contains no generators of  V 3 V_{3}  and 
 
 (b)  
 
 contains a plane defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , but no hyperplanes in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} 
 
 
 in the previous section. Thus, we will need to study the cases
when 
 
 (i)  
 
 𝒞 \mathcal{C}  contains generators of  V 3 V_{3}  but no planes defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4}  and 
 
 (ii)  
 
 𝒞 \mathcal{C}  contains a hyperplane of  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} 
 
 
 in this section. For ease of reading, we will divide the section into two subsections.

 Case 1:   𝒞 \mathcal{C}  is reducible over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . In this case, the threefold  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  contains a hyperplane defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . From Proposition  5.1  we see that  | V ⁡ ( F ) ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) ∩ V 3 | ≤ 3 ​ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 |V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|\leq 3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1 . Also it follows from Remark  5.2  that the bound is attained in this case if and only if  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  is a union of three non-tangent hyperplanes  Σ 1 , Σ 2 \Sigma_{1},\Sigma_{2}  and  Σ 3 \Sigma_{3}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , such that each of the hyperplanes contains a common plane  Π \Pi  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  and  Π \Pi  intersects  V 3 V_{3}  at a non-degenerate plane Hermitian curve. 
 
 
 Case 2:   𝒞 \mathcal{C}  is irreducible over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} .  In this case,  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  does not contain any hyperplane defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . If  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  does not contain any generator of  V 3 V_{3} , then Proposition  4.1  shows that  | 𝒞 ⁡ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) ∩ V 3 | < 3 ​ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 |\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1 . If  V ⁡ ( F ) V(F)  contains a plane defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , then Proposition  4.2  applies and shows that  | 𝒞 ⁡ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) ∩ V 3 | < 3 ​ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 |\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1 . So, we may assume that  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  contains a generator of  V 3 V_{3}  but no plane defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} . It follows that for any plane  Π \Pi  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , the plane curve  V ⁡ ( F ) ∩ Π V(F)\cap\Pi  can contain at most three generators of  V 3 V_{3} . If there exists a plane  Π 0 \Pi_{0}  in  ℙ 4 {\mathbb{P}}^{4}  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}  such that  V ⁡ ( F ) ∩ Π 0 V(F)\cap\Pi_{0}  has exactly three generators of  V 3 V_{3} , then Proposition  4.3  implies that  | V ⁡ ( F ) ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) ∩ V 3 | < 3 ​ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 |V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1 . Furthermore, if for any plane  Π \Pi  defined over  𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} , the intersection  V ⁡ ( F ) ∩ Π V(F)\cap\Pi  contains at most two generators of  V 3 V_{3} , then Proposition  5.6  shows that  | V ⁡ ( F ) ​ ( 𝔽 q 2 ) ∩ V 3 | < 3 ​ ( q 5 + q 2 ) + q 3 + 1 |V(F)(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}})\cap V_{3}|<3(q^{5}+q^{2})+q^{3}+1 . Thus, we see that a strict bound always holds in this case. This completes the proof.
∎
