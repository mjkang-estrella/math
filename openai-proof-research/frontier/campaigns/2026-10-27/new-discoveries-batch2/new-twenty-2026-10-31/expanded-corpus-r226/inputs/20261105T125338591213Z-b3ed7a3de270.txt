 Abstract 
    
 A graph  G G  with  m m  edges is said to be a Nosal graph if  ρ ⁡ ( G ) > m \rho(G)>\sqrt{m} . For a graph  G G , we write  b ​ k ​ ( G ) bk(G)  for its maximum book size and  τ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G)  for the number of edges contained in triangles. Li, Liu and Zhang [J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 179 (2026) 219–249] proved that every  m m -edge Nosal graph satisfies  b ​ k ​ ( G ) > 1 24 ​ m bk(G)>\frac{1}{24}\sqrt{m}  and  τ ⁡ ( G ) > 1 12 ​ m \tau(G)>\frac{1}{12}\sqrt{m} . Recently, two results on the booksize constant are proved:  1 9 \frac{1}{9}  by Zhai, Li and Lou [arXiv:2601.10163v2], and  1 4 \frac{1}{4}  by Chen, Li and Tang [arXiv:2607.16746v1]. 
    
 In this paper, we establish the following result: Every  m m -edge graph  G G  with no isolated vertices and  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{m}  that is not isomorphic to any complete bipartite graph satisfies  b ​ k ​ ( G ) ≥ ρ ⁡ ( G ) 3 bk(G)\geq\frac{\rho(G)}{3}  and  τ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ρ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G)\geq\rho(G) . As direct consequences, we answer a question of Li, Liu and Zhang [J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 179 (2026) 219–249] and confirm a conjecture of Li, Feng and Peng [J. Graph Theory 110 (4) (2025) 408–425]. 
  
 
 
 Keywords:   Booksize; Triangular edges; Nosal graphs; Spectral radius

 1  Introduction 

 
 A central theme in extremal graph theory is to determine how many copies of a prescribed substructure must occur under given global constraints. In spectral extremal graph theory, one is often interested in how lower bounds on the spectral radius, together with a prescribed number of edges, guarantee the occurrence of many copies of certain subgraphs. A classical result of Nosal  [ 26 ] , also known as Nosal’s theorem, states that every triangle-free graph  G G  with  m m  edges satisfies  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ m \rho(G)\leq\sqrt{m} , where  ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G)  denotes the spectral radius of  G G . In 2007, Bollobás and Nikiforov  [ 3 ]  proved that  t ⁡ ( G ) ≥ n 2 12 ​ ( ρ ⁡ ( G ) − n 2 ) t(G)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{12}\left(\rho(G)-\frac{n}{2}\right)  and  t ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ρ ⁡ ( G ) 3 ​ ( ρ 2 ​ ( G ) − m ) t(G)\geq\frac{\rho(G)}{3}\left(\rho^{2}(G)-m\right) , where  t ⁡ ( G ) t(G)  is the number of triangles of  G G  and  n = | V ⁡ ( G ) | n=|V(G)| . The latter inequality was independently obtained by Cioabă, Feng, Tait and Zhang  [ 7 ] .
Ning and Zhai  [ 25 ]  later characterized the equality case, showing that equality holds if and only if  G G  is a complete bipartite graph possibly together with isolated vertices.
They also proved that if  ρ ⁡ ( G ) > m \rho(G)>\sqrt{m} , then  t ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ⌊ m − 1 2 ⌋ t(G)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{m}-1}{2}\right\rfloor , and this bound is best possible. These inequalities can be viewed as spectral supersaturation results: once the spectral radius exceeds the corresponding extremal threshold, the graph must contain many triangles. As noted in  [ 14 ] , supersaturation refers to the phenomenon that exceeding an extremal threshold forces the appearance of many forbidden structures, rather than just a single one. Related results on supersaturation were obtained in  [ 19 ,  20 ,  21 ,  27 ,  10 ,  18 ] . 
 
 
 Following  [ 14 ] , we say an  m m -edge graph  G G  is  Nosal  if  ρ ⁡ ( G ) > m \rho(G)>\sqrt{m} . Several extensions of Nosal’s theorem to other forbidden subgraphs  F F  have been studied, including cliques  [ 22 ,  3 ,  17 ,  31 ] , complete bipartite graphs  [ 1 ,  23 ] , cycles  [ 6 ,  11 ,  12 ,  32 ] , trees  [ 5 ,  9 ] , friendship graphs  [ 7 ,  30 ] ; see also  [ 13 ]  for related spectral results on triangular edges. For a survey on spectral conditions for extremal graph problems, we refer the reader to  [ 15 ] . Nosal’s theorem guarantees that every  m m -edge graph  G G  with  ρ ⁡ ( G ) > m \rho(G)>\sqrt{m}  contains a triangle. It is then natural to ask whether this spectral condition forces many triangles to share a common edge, that is, whether it forces a book of large size.
A book of size  k k  is a collection of  k k  triangles sharing a common edge, and we denote by  b ​ k ​ ( G ) bk(G)  the maximum size of a book contained in  G G . Zhai, Lin and Shu  [ 29 ]  conjectured that every  m m -edge Nosal graph  G G  should have large booksize.
Nikiforov  [ 24 ]  first proved that every  m m -edge Nosal graph  G G  satisfies  b ​ k ​ ( G ) > 1 12 ​ m 4 bk(G)>\frac{1}{12}\sqrt[4]{m} , and suggested that the exponent  1 4 \frac{1}{4}  might be improved. Motivated by this, Li and Peng  [ 16 ]  proposed the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1 . 
 
 (​​ [ 24 ,  16 ] ) 
For every  m m -edge Nosal graph  G G , we have  b ​ k ​ ( G ) = Ω ⁡ ( m ) bk(G)=\Omega(\sqrt{m}) . 
 
 
 
 The conjecture was recently confirmed by Li, Liu and Zhang  [ 14 ] .
More precisely, they proved the following lower bound and showed that the order  m \sqrt{m}  is best possible. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.2 . 
 
 (​​ [ 14 ] ) 
If  G G  is an  m m -edge Nosal graph, then  b ​ k ​ ( G ) > 1 24 ​ m bk(G)>\frac{1}{24}\sqrt{m} . Furthermore, there
exist  m m -edge Nosal graphs with no book of size larger than  ( 1 3 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ m (\frac{1}{3}+o(1))\sqrt{m} . 
 
 
 
 Recall that an edge is called triangular if it is contained in a triangle.
While the booksize measures how many triangles can be forced to share a common edge, the number of triangular edges measures how many edges are involved in triangles. Li, Feng and Peng  [ 13 ]  studied spectral supersaturation problems involving triangular edges and established a spectral version of the Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau theorem. They also posed the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.3 . 
 
 (​​ [ 13 ] ) 
If  G G  is a graph with  m m  edges and  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{m} , then  G G  has at least  m \sqrt{m}  triangular edges, unless  G G  is a complete bipartite graph with possibly some isolated vertices. 
 
 
 
 The corresponding version of this conjecture for Nosal graphs was recorded in  [ 14 ]  as follows. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.4 . 
 
 (​​ [ 13 ,  14 ] ) 
Every  m m -edge Nosal graph has more than  m \sqrt{m}  triangular edges. 
 
 
 
 In fact, since a book of size  k k  contains  2 ​ k + 1 2k+1  triangular edges, Theorem  1.2  implies the following lower bound. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.5 . 
 
 (​​ [ 14 ] ) 
Every  m m -edge Nosal graph has at least  1 12 ​ m \frac{1}{12}\sqrt{m}  triangular edges. 
 
 
 
 Theorem  1.2  shows that no lower bound of the form  b ​ k ​ ( G ) ≥ c ​ m bk(G)\geq c\sqrt{m}  can hold for all  m m -edge Nosal graphs with  c > 1 3 c>\frac{1}{3} . It is therefore natural to ask whether  1 3 \frac{1}{3}  is the best possible constant. Li, Liu and Zhang  [ 14 ]  posed the following problem. 
 
 
 Problem 1.6 . 
 
 (​​ [ 14 ] ) 
Does every  m m -edge Nosal graph contain a book of size  1 3 ​ m \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{m} ? 
 
 
 
 Let  B r + 1 B_{r+1}  denote the book of size  r + 1 r+1 . Zhai, Li and Lou  [ 28 ]  made further progress on this problem by proving a spectral extremal result for  B r + 1 B_{r+1} -free graphs. As an immediate consequence, their result implies that every  m m -edge Nosal graph satisfies  b ​ k ​ ( G ) > 1 9 ​ m bk(G)>\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{m} . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.7 . 
 
 (​​ [ 28 ] ) 
Let  r r  be a positive integer, and let  G G  be a  B r + 1 B_{r+1} -free graph with  m m  edges, where  m ≥ ( 9 ​ r ) 2 m\geq(9r)^{2} . Then  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ m \rho(G)\leq\sqrt{m} , with equality if and only if  G G  is a complete bipartite graph with possibly some isolated vertices. 
 
 
 
 Corollary 1.8 . 
 
 (​​ [ 28 ] ) 
Every  m m -edge Nosal graph  G G  satisfies  b ​ k ​ ( G ) > 1 9 ​ m bk(G)>\frac{1}{9}\sqrt{m} . 
 
 
 
 Recently, Chen, Li and Tang  [ 4 ]  further improved this result. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.9 . 
 
 (​​ [ 4 ] ) 
Every  m m -edge Nosal graph  G G  satisfies  b ​ k ​ ( G ) > 1 4 ​ m bk(G)>\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{m} . 
 
 
 
 However, the optimal constant problem posed in Problem  1.6  remained open. Moreover, Conjecture  1.3  on triangular edges was still unresolved. In this paper, we prove Conjecture  1.3  and answer Problem  1.6  in the affirmative. 
 
 
 1.1  Main results 

 
 Let  T ⁡ ( G ) T(G)  denote the set of all triangular edges of  G G , and let  τ ⁡ ( G ) = | T ⁡ ( G ) | \tau(G)=|T(G)| . Our main results are the following two theorems. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.10 . 
 
 Let  G G  be an  m m -edge graph with no isolated vertices such that  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{m}  and  G G  is not isomorphic to any complete bipartite graph. Then  b ​ k ​ ( G ) ≥ ρ ⁡ ( G ) 3 bk(G)\geq\frac{\rho(G)}{3} . 
 
 
 
 Theorem 1.11 . 
 
 Let  G G  be an  m m -edge graph with no isolated vertices such that  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{m}  and  G G  is not isomorphic to any complete bipartite graph. Then  τ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ρ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G)\geq\rho(G) . 
 
 
 
 Theorem  1.10  answers Problem  1.6  in the affirmative.
In fact, it proves a stronger statement under the condition  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{m} , with the only exceptions being complete bipartite graphs, possibly with some isolated vertices. Theorem  1.11  confirms Conjecture  1.3 , and hence also confirms Conjecture  1.4 . 
 
 
 The lower bounds in Theorems  1.10  and  1.11  are stated in terms of
 ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) , not just  m \sqrt{m} . This stronger spectral form is essential to the proof, as retaining  ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G)  throughout the arguments is what allows us to reach the final conclusion.

To prove Theorem  1.11 , we localize the graph  G G  around a vertex  u ∗ u^{*}  at which the Perron vector attains its maximum component, and express  ρ 2 ​ ( G ) − e ​ ( G ) \rho^{2}(G)-e(G)  as the difference between the excess contributed by internal edges in the neighborhood of  u ∗ u^{*}  and the deficit contributed by the remaining vertices. Since  ρ 2 ​ ( G ) ≥ e ⁡ ( G ) \rho^{2}(G)\geq e(G) , the excess must be at least the deficit. We define two significant vertex sets  U 1 U_{1}  and  U 2 U_{2} . If  U 2 = U 1 U_{2}=U_{1} , then a straightforward spectral analysis gives  τ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ρ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G)\geq\rho(G) . If  U 2 ⊊ U 1 U_{2}\subsetneq U_{1} , we argue by contradiction. Moreover, we prove that if the local triangular edge-counting inequality is not tight, then under the assumption  τ ⁡ ( G ) < ρ ⁡ ( G ) \tau(G)<\rho(G) , the deficit strictly exceeds the excess, yielding a contradiction. If the local triangular edge-counting inequality is tight, we analyze the structural properties of  G G . Subsequently, via a vector comparison argument, we control the total Perron weight of all triangular edges. Combining this with inequalities for non-triangular edges, we obtain  ρ 2 ​ ( G ) < e ​ ( G ) \rho^{2}(G)<e(G) , again a contradiction. 
 
 
 In fact, the proofs of Theorems  1.10  and  1.11  are both based on the same principle: by assigning weights to all vertices via the Perron vector of the graph  G G , the global requirement  ρ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ e ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{e(G)}  is transformed into local information about triangles.
