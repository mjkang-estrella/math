 Abstract 
    
 Let  s s  be a positive integer. Our goal is to find all finite abelian groups  G G  that contain a  2 2 -subset  A A  for which the undirected Cayley graph  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A)  has diameter at most  s s . We provide a complete answer when  G G  is cyclic, and a conjecture and some partial answers when  G G  is noncyclic. 
    
 2020 AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary: 11B13; Secondary: 05B10, 05C35, 11P70, 11B75, 20K01. 
    
 Key words: Abelian group, Cayley graph, diameter, sumset, signed sumset. 
  
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Throughout this paper we let  G G  be an additively written finite abelian group. When  G G  is cyclic and of order  n n , we identify it with  ℤ n = ℤ / n ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}_{n}=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} ; we consider  0 , 1 , … , n − 1 0,1,\dots,n-1  interchangeably as integers and as elements of  ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} . Below we use standard terminology and notation of additive combinatorics (see  [ 1 ] ). 
 
 
 Given a finite abelian group  G G  and a subset  A ⊆ G A\subseteq G , the  undirected Cayley graph   Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A)  has its vertex set formed by the elements of  G G , and has an edge between two vertices whenever their difference is an element of  A A , that is,  g 1 ∈ G g_{1}\in G  and  g 2 ∈ G g_{2}\in G  are connected when  g 2 = g 1 + a g_{2}=g_{1}+a  or  g 1 = g 2 + a g_{1}=g_{2}+a  for some  a ∈ A a\in A . If  A A  is a generating set of  G G , then  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A)  is connected; in particular, if  A A  is an  s s -spanning set  of  G G , meaning that every element of  G G  can be written as the signed sum of at most  s s  (not-necessarily distinct) elements of  A A , then  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A)  has diameter at most  s s . 
 
 
 Suppose now that  A A  is an  m m -subset of  G G . It is easy to compute that there are at most  ∑ i ≥ 0 ( m i ) ​ ( h − 1 i − 1 ) ​ 2 i \sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{h-1\choose i-1}2^{i}  vertices of  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A)  that are of distance  h h  away from any given vertex, and thus if  A A  is an  s s -spanning set in  G G , then the order of  G G  can be at most 
 

 
 
 ∑ h = 0 s ∑ i ≥ 0 ( m i ) ​ ( h − 1 i − 1 ) ​ 2 i = ∑ i ≥ 0 ( m i ) ​ ( s i ) ​ 2 i . \sum_{h=0}^{s}\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{h-1\choose i-1}2^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{s\choose i}2^{i}. 
 
 
 Therefore, we have the following bound. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.1 
 
 For positive integers  m m  and  s s , define 
 

 
 
 a ⁡ ( m , s ) = ∑ i ≥ 0 ( m i ) ​ ( s i ) ​ 2 i . \displaystyle a(m,s)=\sum_{i\geq 0}{m\choose i}{s\choose i}2^{i}. 
 
 (1) 
 
 If an abelian group  G G  has an  m m -subset  A A  for which the diameter of  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) \Gamma(G,A)  is  s s , then the order of  G G  is at most  a ⁡ ( m , s ) a(m,s) . 
 
 
 
 We note, in passing, that the values of  a ⁡ ( m , s ) a(m,s)  are known as  Delannoy numbers  (see  [ 3 ] ), and that they satisfy the recursion 
 

 
 
 a ⁡ ( m , s ) = a ⁡ ( m , s − 1 ) + a ⁡ ( m − 1 , s ) + a ⁡ ( m − 1 , s − 1 ) . a(m,s)=a(m,s-1)+a(m-1,s)+a(m-1,s-1). 
 
 
 
 
 It is a natural question to ask for the largest possible order of a group  G G  with an  m m -subset  A A  for which the diameter of  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) {\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s . Before we discuss our results on this question, let us review the analogous question in the directed case. 
 
 
 Given a finite abelian group  G G  and a subset  A ⊆ G A\subseteq G , the  directed Cayley graph   Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  has its vertex set formed by the elements of  G G , and has a directed edge from  g 1 ∈ G g_{1}\in G  to  g 2 ∈ G g_{2}\in G  when  g 2 = g 1 + a g_{2}=g_{1}+a  for some  a ∈ A a\in A . We say that  A A  is an  s s -basis  of  G G  if every element of  G G  can be written as the sum of at most  s s  (not-necessarily distinct) elements of  A A ; in this case, the diameter of  Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s . 
 
 
 For an  m m -subset  A A  of  G G , we see that there are at most  ( m + h − 1 h ) {m+h-1\choose h}  vertices of  Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  that are of distance  h h  away from any given vertex, so if  A A  is an  s s -basis of  G G , then we must have 
 

 
 
 | G | ≤ ∑ h = 0 s ( m + h − 1 h ) = ( m + s s ) . |G|\leq\sum_{h=0}^{s}{m+h-1\choose h}={m+s\choose s}. 
 
 
 
 
 When equality holds, we say that  A A  is a  perfect  s s -basis  of  G G : in this case, every element of  G G  arises as the sum of at most  s s  (not-necessarily distinct) elements of  A A  in a unique way. Trivially, the set of nonzero elements is a perfect  1 1 -basis in any
group  G G , and the  1 1 -element set consisting of a generator of  G G  is a perfect  s s -basis
in the cyclic group of order  s + 1 s+1 . It turns out that there are no others: 
 
 
 Theorem 1.2  (Bajnok, Berson, and Just; cf.  [ 2 ] ) 
 
 If a subset  A A  of a finite abelian group  G G  is a perfect  s s -basis, then  s = 1 s=1 
and  A = G ∖ { 0 } A=G\setminus\{0\} , or  G ≅ ℤ s + 1 G\cong\mathbb{Z}_{s+1}  and  | A | = 1 |A|=1 . 
 
 
 
 Given the scarcity of perfect bases, it is natural to ask for the largest groups with a given diameter and size of generating set. The paper  [ 9 ]  asks the following questions: 
 
 •  
 
 What is the largest possible order of an abelian group  G G  with an  m m -subset  A A  for which the diameter of  Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s ? 
 
 •  
 
 What is the largest possible order of a cyclic group  G G  with an  m m -subset  A A  for which the diameter of  Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s ? 
 
 
 
 
 After a series of papers  [ 10 ,  5 ,  8 ,  9 ,  4 ] , these two questions have been completely solved for the case of  m = 2 m=2 . We have the following two results. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3  (Morillo, Fiol, and Fàbrega; cf.  [ 10 ] ) 
 
 Let  s s  be a positive integer. The largest possible order of a cyclic group  G G  with a  2 2 -subset for which the diameter of  Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s  is given by  ⌊ ( s 2 + 4 ​ s + 3 ) / 3 ⌋ \left\lfloor(s^{2}+4s+3)/3\right\rfloor . 
 
 
 
 Theorem 1.4  (Fiol; cf.  [ 4 ] ) 
 
 Let  s s  be any positive integer. The largest possible order of an abelian group  G G  with a  2 2 -subset for which the diameter of  Γ → ​ ( G , A ) \vec{\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s  is given by  ⌊ ( s 2 + 4 ​ s + 4 ) / 3 ⌋ \left\lfloor(s^{2}+4s+4)/3\right\rfloor . 
 
 
 
 Observe that the quantities in Theorems  1.3  and  1.4  agree, unless  s ≡ 1 s\equiv 1  mod 3; for that case one can verify that the group  G = ℤ k × ℤ 3 ​ k G=\mathbb{Z}_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3k}  has the  s s -basis  { ( 0 , 1 ) , ( 1 , 3 ​ k − 1 ) } \{(0,1),(1,3k-1)\} , where  k = ( s + 2 ) / 3 k=(s+2)/3 . However, the question specifically for noncyclic groups is still open when  s ≢ 1 s\not\equiv 1  mod 3. 
 
 
 Let us now return to the undirected case. When equality occurs in Proposition  1.1 , then  A A  is called a  perfect  s s -spanning set  in  G G . Perfect spanning sets can be easily characterized for  m = 1 m=1  and  s = 1 s=1 .
Note that  a ⁡ ( 1 , s ) = 2 ​ s + 1 a(1,s)=2s+1  and, clearly, for any  a ∈ ℤ 2 ​ s + 1 a\in\mathbb{Z}_{2s+1}  with  gcd ⁡ ( a , 2 ​ s + 1 ) = 1 \gcd(a,2s+1)=1 , the set  { a } \{a\}  is a perfect  s s -spanning set in  ℤ 2 ​ s + 1 \mathbb{Z}_{2s+1} . Also,  a ⁡ ( m , 1 ) = 2 ​ m + 1 a(m,1)=2m+1 , and for any group  G G  of order  2 ​ m + 1 2m+1 , every asymmetric subset of size  m m  (that is, a set containing exactly one of any nonzero element or its inverse) is a perfect  1 1 -spanning set in  G G . 
 
 
 From ( 1 ) we get  a ⁡ ( 2 , s ) = 2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s + 1 a(2,s)=2s^{2}+2s+1 , and below we establish the following result. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.5 
 
 Let  s s  be a positive integer. The largest possible order of a cyclic group  G G  with a  2 2 -subset for which the diameter of  Γ ⁡ ( G , A ) {\Gamma}(G,A)  is at most  s s  is given by  2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s + 1 2s^{2}+2s+1 . 
 
 
 
 For noncyclic groups the situation seems a bit more nuanced. We have the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.6 
 
 Let  s ≥ 3 s\geq 3  be an integer. 
 
 •  
 
 If  2 ​ s + 1 2s+1  is prime, then the largest noncyclic group with an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2  has order
 2 ​ s 2 2s^{2} . 
 
 •  
 
 If  2 ​ s + 1 2s+1  is composite and  p p  is its smallest prime divisor, then the largest noncyclic group with an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2  has order
 2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s − ( p 2 − 1 ) / 2 2s^{2}+2s-(p^{2}-1)/2 . 
 
 
 In particular, for any noncyclic group  G G  with an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2  we have  | G | ≤ 2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s − 4 . |G|\leq 2s^{2}+2s-4. 
 
 
 
 (We add that the largest noncyclic group with a  2 2 -spanning set of size  2 2  is  ℤ 3 × ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3} .) 
 
 
 Below we prove that the quantities in Conjecture  1.6  are lower bounds. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.7 
 
 Let  s s  be a positive integer. 
 
 •  
 
 The largest noncyclic group with an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2  has order at least  2 ​ s 2 2s^{2} . 
 
 •  
 
 If  2 ​ s + 1 2s+1  is composite and  p p  is any prime divisor of  2 ​ s + 1 2s+1 , then the largest noncyclic group with an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2  has order at least  2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s − ( p 2 − 1 ) / 2 2s^{2}+2s-(p^{2}-1)/2 . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Establishing upper bounds appears to be more difficult, and we only have the partial results that we summarize here. We first note that a noncyclic group that has a spanning set of size  2 2  must have rank  2 2  and thus is isomorphic to  ℤ c × ℤ k ​ c \mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}  for some integers  c ≥ 2 c\geq 2  and  k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . 
 
 
 We say that the group  ℤ c × ℤ k ​ c \mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}  is  s s -regular  for some positive integer  s s , if it possesses an  s s -spanning set of the form  { ( 1 , u ) , ( 1 , v ) } \{(1,u),(1,v)\}  for some  u , v ∈ ℤ k ​ c u,v\in\mathbb{Z}_{kc} . While we have no precise count on the number of  s s -regular groups, it appears that most groups of rank  2 2  that have an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2  are  s s -regular. For example, for  s = 10 s=10 , of the  105 105  groups of rank  2 2  that have an  s s -spanning set of size  2 2 ,  103 103  are  s s -regular. (The two exceptions are  ℤ 8 × ℤ 16 \mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{16}  and  ℤ 10 × ℤ 20 \mathbb{Z}_{10}\times\mathbb{Z}_{20} .) In Theorem  3.1  below we prove for every odd prime  c c  and positive integer  k k  that if  ℤ c × ℤ k ​ c \mathbb{Z}_{c}\times\mathbb{Z}_{kc}  has an  s s -spanning set at all, then it is  s s -regular.

 For  s ≥ 3 s\geq 3 , every  s s -regular abelian group of rank 2 has order at most  2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s − 4 2s^{2}+2s-4 , with equality if, and only if, it is isomorphic to  ℤ 3 × ℤ ( 2 ​ s 2 + 2 ​ s − 4 ) / 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{(2s^{2}+2s-4)/3}  for some  s ≡ 1 s\equiv 1  mod  3 3 .
