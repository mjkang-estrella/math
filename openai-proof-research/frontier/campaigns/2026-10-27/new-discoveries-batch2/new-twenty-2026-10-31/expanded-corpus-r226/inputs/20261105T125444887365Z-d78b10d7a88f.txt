 Abstract. 
    
 Consider an eigenfunction of the Laplacian on a torus. How small can its  L 2 L^{2} -norm be on small balls?
We provide partial answers to this question by exploiting the distribution of integer points on spheres, basic properties of polynomials, and Nazarov–Turán type estimates for exponential polynomials. Applications to quantum limits and control theory are given.

Being able to distinguish these two different situations is at the heart of the present article. 
 
 
 For generic eigenfunctions,  it is expected that some form of quantum ergodicity should hold. Indeed, this dependence on the global geometry of the manifold makes the study of  m M m_{M}  very appealing.
We will explore in § 3  the behavior of  m M m_{M}  on various geometries.

 2.  Results and perspectives 

 
 2.1.  Main results and organization of the article 

 
 In § 4 , we investigate the possible order of vanishing of an eigenfunction.
This is a simpler question than that of the behavior of local  L 2 L^{2} -norms which define  m d ​ ( λ , r ) m_{d}(\lambda,r) , but it is closely related.
For  u ∈ C ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 d ) u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d}) , we denote by  Γ ⁡ ( u ) \Gamma(u)  its order of vanishing at the origin.
Precisely  Γ ⁡ ( u ) \Gamma(u)  is the maximum of the set of all  N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N}  such that  ∂ x α u ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 \partial_{x}^{\alpha}u(0)=0  for all  α ∈ ℕ d \alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}  with  | α | ≤ N |\alpha|\leq N .

 2.2.  Application to quantum limits 

 
 The measure  μ \mu  is called a quantum limit if it is the weak limit of the measures  μ j = | u j | 2 ⁡ d ​ x \mu_{j}=|u_{j}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x , where  u j u_{j}  is a normalized eigenfunction associated to the eigenvalue  λ j 2 → ∞ \lambda_{j}^{2}\to\infty .
Denote by  𝒬 \mathcal{Q}  the set of all quantum limits.
It follows from Connes  [ 7 ]  that the Lebesgue measure is absolutely continuous with respect to all quantum limits; and it was proved by Bourgain, cited in  [ 20 ] , that all quantum limits are absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure.
See  [ 20 ,  1 ]  for further results on quantum limits.
We will denote

We easily verify the following relation for all  δ ∈ ( 0 , r / 2 ) \delta\in(0,r/2) :

Whether the equality  m d ​ ( r ) = lim inf λ → ∞ m d ​ ( λ , r ) m_{d}(r)=\liminf\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}m_{d}(\lambda,r)  holds remains unclear to us.

 It is interesting to compare the behavior of quantum limits on tori with their behavior on the sphere or on hyperbolic surfaces.
Note that these are respectively manifolds of zero, positive, and negative curvature.
Roughly speaking, the corollary above expresses the fact that, near any point of a torus, quantum limits can vanish at most polynomially or exponentially.
This is intermediary between the case of the sphere, where quantum limits of highest weight spherical harmonics may vanish completely on some nonempty open sets, and the case of hyperbolic surfaces, where the quantum unique ergodicity is expected (and indeed has been proved by Lindenstrauss  [ 24 ]  in the arithmetic case). 
 
 
 
 2.3.  Application to control theory 

 
 The controllability of the linear Schrödinger group  e i ​ t ​ Δ e^{it\Delta}  without potential on tori has been established by Jaffard  [ 19 ]  when  d ≥ 2 d\geq 2  and by Komornik  [ 22 ]  and Macià  [ 28 ]  using different methods for all  d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 .

 2.4.  Open questions 

 
 The results which have been stated so far give a very lacunary picture, and much remains to be understood.
We start with two conjectures, for which strong evidence is available.
First, we conjecture that the examples constructed in § 4  give the highest possible order of vanishing.
See Remark  4.5  for an heuristic discussion. 
 
 
 Conjecture A . 
 
 For all  d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , there holds  Γ d ( λ ) ∼ d 𝒩 d ( λ ) 1 d − 1 \Gamma_{d}(\lambda)\sim_{d}\mathcal{N}_{d}(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{d-1}} . 
 
 
 
 Second, we conjecture that the non-optimal lower bound for  m d ​ ( r ) m_{d}(r)  when  d ≥ 4 d\geq 4  matches the upper bound given in Corollary  2.5 . 
 
 
 Conjecture B . 
 
 If  d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 , then
 exp ⁡ { − C d ​ r − d − 3 2 } ≲ m d ​ ( r ) ≲ exp ⁡ { − C d ′ ​ r − d − 3 2 } \exp\bigl\{-C_{d}r^{-\frac{d-3}{2}}\bigr\}\lesssim m_{d}(r)\lesssim\exp\bigl\{-C^{\prime}_{d}r^{-\frac{d-3}{2}}\bigr\} . 
 
 
 
 Beyond these two conjectures, it is tempting to ask if the upper bounds for  m d ​ ( λ , r ) m_{d}(\lambda,r)  given in § 5  are are optimal and if matching lower bounds exist, at least when  d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 .
However, our construction of the examples might not be sharp.
The distribution of integer points in low dimensions ( d = 2 , 3 , 4 d=2,3,4 ) is more irregular, and the upper bounds we are able to prove might be far off the mark.

 3.4.  Irrational tori and spectral bands 

 
 We consider now a general torus  𝕋 Λ d = ℝ d / Λ \mathbb{T}^{d}_{\Lambda}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda , where  Λ \Lambda  is a lattice of full dimension.
The definition ( 3.1 ) should be modified in this case since, for a generic choice of  Λ \Lambda , the eigenspaces have bounded dimension (equal to  2 2 ).
Instead, projection on eigenspaces should be replaced by projection on narrow spectral bands.
Precisely, we propose the definition 
 

 
 (3.3)

 With the definition ( 3.3 ) above, do the theorems proved in the present paper extend to general  Λ \Lambda ? Only partly. Roughly speaking, the arguments relying on Jarnik’s lemma (Lemma  C.1 ) or more generally Connes’ result (Lemmas  C.5  and  C.4 ) still apply.
However, many counting estimates (e.g., number of lattice points on spheres in low dimension) for general  Λ \Lambda  is not yet well understood. 
 
 
 Finally, it would certainly be of interest to consider spectral bands of arbitrary width, on tori but also on other compact manifolds, but this goes well beyond the scope of the present paper.

 5.  Examples and upper bounds 

 
 The aim of this section is to construct examples of eigenfunctions with eigenvalue  λ 2 \lambda^{2}  achieving small  L 2 L^{2} -norms on balls of radius  r r .
This is of course equivalent to bounding  m ⁡ ( λ , r ) m(\lambda,r)  from above. 
 
 
 Three main classes of examples are constructed as follows: 
 
 
 
 (1)  
 
 The first assertion of Theorem  5.3  concerns with functions with Fourier coefficients supported on (the integer points of) the whole sphere  λ ​ 𝕊 d − 1 \lambda\mathbb{S}^{d-1} , i.e., a general eigenfunction.
Such functions are the same as the ones achieving maximal orders of pointwise vanishing as per Theorem  4.1  but we are now looking more carefully at their  L 2 L^{2} -norms. 
 
 (2)  
 
 The second assertion of Theorem  5.3  concerns with functions with Fourier coefficients supported on (the integer points of) spherical caps on  λ ​ 𝕊 d − 1 \lambda\mathbb{S}^{d-1} .
This turns out to be interesting only if these caps violate the equidistribution integer points in the sense that the density of integer points on these caps is higher than the density on the whole sphere. 
 
 (3)  
 
 Finally, in Theorems  5.6  and  5.7 , we consider functions with Fourier coefficients supported on (the integer points of) the intersection of  λ ​ 𝕊 d − 1 \lambda\mathbb{S}^{d-1}  and a hyperplane, which is a subset of a lower-dimensional lattice.
This is a particular example of the Case ( 2 ) above.
