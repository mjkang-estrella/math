 Abstract. 
    
 We establish the  e e -positivity of spider graphs of the form  S ⁡ ( 4 ​ m + 2 ,  2 ​ m ,  1 ) S(4m+2,\,2m,\,1) , which was conjectured by Aliniaeifard, Wang and van Willigenburg. A key to our proof is the  e I e_{I} -expansion formula of the chromatic symmetric function of paths due to Shareshian and Wachs, where the symbol  I I  indicates integer compositions rather than partitions. Following the strategy of the divide-and-conquer technique, we pick out one or two positive  e J e_{J} -terms for each negative  e I e_{I} -term in an  e e -expression for the spiders, where  J J  are selected to be distinct compositions obtained by rearranging the parts of  I I . 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  chromatic symmetric function;  e e -positivity; Stanley and Stembridge’s conjecture

 1.  Introduction 

 
 An original motivation of this work
is  Stanley and Stembridge ’s  ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -free conjecture  [ 7 ] ,
which asserts that the incomparability graph of every  ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -free poset is  e e -positive.
A natrual generalization of the conjecture is to characterize all  e e -positive graphs.
 Wolfgang III [13]  provided a powerful criterion that
any connected  e e -positive graph has a connected partition of every type.
Some popular graphs such like complete graphs, paths and cycles are  e e -positive.
Recently confirmed  e e -positive graphs are melting lollipops, hats and cycle-chords, see  Wang [10] ,  Wang and Zhou [12]  and references therein.
The  e e -positivity of  K K -chains that is due to  Gebhard and Sagan [2] 
are rediscovered by  Tom [9]  and  McDonough et al. [3]  via new approaches. 
 
 
 Pioneering this generalization approach
was the  e e -positivity study of trees.
 Dahlberg et al. [1]  conjectured that the maximum degree of any  e e -positive tree is  3 3 ,
which was attacked by  Zheng [14]  who proved
this muximum degree is at most  5 5 . 
 
 
 A particular class of trees, the spiders,
plays an essential role along this study.
For any partition
 λ = λ 1 ​ ⋯ ​ λ d \lambda=\lambda_{1}\dotsm\lambda_{d}  of  n − 1 n-1 
with  d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 ,
the  spider   S ⁡ ( λ ) S(\lambda)  is the  n n -vertex tree consisting
of paths of lengths  λ 1 , … , λ d \lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}  with a common end.
For instance,
the spider  S ⁡ ( 4 ​ m + 2 ,  2 ​ m ,  1 ) S(4m+2,\,2m,\,1)  is obtained by adding a pendent vertex to the path of order  6 ​ m + 3 6m+3 , see  Fig.   1.1 .

 Figure 1.1.  The spider  S ⁡ ( 4 ​ m + 2 ,  2 ​ m ,  1 ) S(4m+2,\,2m,\,1) . 
 
 
 Dahlberg et al. [1, Lemma 13]  showed that
if a connected graph  G G  has a connected partition of type  μ \mu ,
then so does the spider  S ⁡ ( λ ) S(\lambda) ,
where  λ \lambda  is the partition consisting
of the sizes of connected components that are obtained by removing a vertex of degree at least 3 from  G G .
Therefore, the  e e -positivity of a tree implies
the  e e -positivity of certain spider
in view of  Wolfgang III ’s criterion. 
 
 
 Wang and Wang [11]  conjectured that a spider  S ⁡ ( a , b , c ) S(a,b,c)  with  c ≥ 3 c\geq 3  is  e e -positive
if and only if it is  S ⁡ ( 8 , 5 , 3 ) S(8,5,3)  or  S ⁡ ( 14 , 9 , 5 ) S(14,9,5) .
In contrast, in view of their results on the  e e -positivity or non- e e -positivity
of spiders of the form  S ⁡ ( a , b , c ) S(a,b,c)  with  c ∈ { 1 , 2 } c\in\{1,2\} ,
it is quite unpredictable that such a spider
is  e e -positive or not. Among this disorders,
however,  Zheng [14, Conjecture 6.2]  presented  Conjecture   1.1 
and attribute it to Aliniaeifard, Wang and van Willigenburg. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1   (Aliniaeifard, Wang and van Willigenburg) . 
 
 For any integer  m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 ,
the spider  S ⁡ ( 4 ​ m + 2 ,  2 ​ m ,  1 ) S(4m+2,\,2m,\,1)  is  e e -positive. 
 
 
 
 For example, for  m = 1 m=1 , the conjectured  e e -positivity is clear from the  e e -expasion

 Thibon and Wang [8, Theorems 4.3 and 4.6]  proved the Schur positivity of the spiders  S ⁡ ( a , 2 , 1 ) S(a,2,1)  and  S ⁡ ( a , 4 , 1 ) S(a,4,1)  for all  a ≥ 4 a\geq 4  by using noncommutative symmetric functions. 
 
 
 This paper is devoted to the confirmation of  Conjecture   1.1 .
In  Section   2 , we give a quick view of necessary notion and notation,
as well as two preliminary lemmas.
Our proof starts from  Section   3 .

 A  partition  of  n n  is a composition of  n n 
in non-increasing order, commonly denoted as

For an introduction on symmetric functions, see  Stanley [6, Chapter 7] .
For any partition  λ = λ 1 ​ λ 2 ​ ⋯ \lambda=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dotsm ,
the  elementary symmetric function  with respect to  λ \lambda 
is  e λ = e λ 1 e λ 2 ⋯ e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda_{1}}e_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots ,
where  e r e_{r}  is the sum of all products of  r r  distinct variables  x i x_{i} .
Elementary symmetric functions forms a basis of the algebra of symmetric functions.
A symmetric function is  e e -positive if all its  e e -coefficients are nonnegative.
We will use the extended symmetric function  e I e_{I}  for compositions indices  I I ,
which is defined as  e I = e λ e_{I}=e_{\lambda} , where  λ \lambda  is the partition obtained by
arranging the parts of  I I  from large to small.

has chromatic symmetric function  X K n = n ! ​ e n X_{K_{n}}=n!e_{n} .
