 Abstract. 
    
 In this paper, we fully resolve two major
conjectures on odd edge-colorings and odd edge-coverings of graphs,
proposed by Petruševski and Škrekovski ( European Journal of Combinatorics,  91:103225, 2021).
The first conjecture states that for any loopless and connected
graph  G G  with  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4 , there exists an edge  e e  such that
 G \ { e } G\backslash\{e\}  is odd  3 3 -edge-colorable.
The second conjecture
states that any simple graph  G G  with  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4  admits an odd  3 3 -edge-covering in which
only one edge receives two or three colors. In addition, we strongly confirm the second conjecture by demonstrating that there exists an odd  3 3 -edge-covering in which only one edge receives two colors. 
  
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 In this paper, we consider only undirected and loopless graphs, which may contain multiple edges unless specified otherwise as simple graphs. Throughout this paper, we employ the graph notation specified in  [ 3 ] . 
 
 
 For a graph  G G , an  edge-coloring  of  G G  is an assignment of colors from the set  [ k ] = { 1 , 2 , ⋯ , k } [k]=\{1,2,\cdots,k\}  to the edges of  G G ,
which provides an edge decomposition of  G G  into  k k  color classes.
Similarly, an  edge-covering  of  G G  is an assignment of colors from  [ k ] [k]  to the edges of  G G , allowing for multiple colors per edge.
Equivalently, we can denote an edge-coloring by a mapping  φ : E ⁡ ( G ) → [ k ] \varphi:E(G)\to[k] . In the case of an edge-covering, we denote it by a set-valued mapping  ψ : E ⁡ ( G ) → 2 [ k ] \ ∅ \psi:E(G)\to 2^{[k]}\backslash\emptyset .
We will frequently talk about vertices with odd degrees or even degrees.
For simplicity, we call these vertices  odd vertices  and  even vertices , respectively.
A graph is called an  odd graph  if every vertex is an odd vertex.
We say  G G  is  odd  k k -edge-colorable 
(respectively,  odd  k k -edge-coverable ) if there is an edge-coloring (respectively, an edge-covering)
of  G G  by  k k  odd subgraphs, which we call an odd  k k -edge-coloring (respectively, an odd  k k -edge-covering).
In other words, the coloring map or the covering map
 φ \varphi  is required to satisfy that
for any  j = 1 , ⋯ , k j=1,\cdots,k , the edge set
 E j := { e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) | j ∈ φ ⁡ ( e ) } E_{j}:=\{e\in E(G)\big|j\in\varphi(e)\}  forms an odd graph. We also define  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  (respectively,  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G) ) to be the least integer  k 0 k_{0}  such that  G G  is odd
 k 0 k_{0} -colorable (respectively, odd  k 0 k_{0} -coverable). It is reasonable to call  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  the
 odd chromatic index  of the graph  G G .
Alternatively, we understand  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  as the least integer
 k 0 k_{0}  such that  G G  can be decomposed into  k 0 k_{0}  (edge) disjoint odd subgraphs. 
 
 
 The topics of determining both  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  and  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)  for a graph  G G 
have been very active in recent years, and we refer to the recent survey paper  [ 6 ]  for the history, references, and discussions on many related problems, ranging from theoretical problems on graphs and multigraphs to problems involving algorithms. 
 
 
 As an initiating result in this area, Pyber  [ 8 ]  proved the following theorem. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1 . 
 
 ( [ 8 ] ) For any connected
simple graph  G G ,  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 4 .
If  G G  is a connected graph (not necessarily a simple graph) and it has even order,
then  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3 . 
 
 
 
 It was also observed in  [ 8 ]  that the upper bound of  4 4  in the previous result can be achieved by
the wheel graph  W 4 W_{4} . Lužar, Petruševski, and Škrekovski   [ 1 ]  showed that connected loopless multigraphs are odd 5-edge-colorable, with one particular exception that is odd 6-edge-colorable. Before proceeding, we define a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2) 
as one of the graphs on three pairwise adjacent vertices,
 v 1 , v 2 , v 3 v_{1},v_{2},v_{3} , where
any two of them are connected by multiple edges with even sizes.
Similarly, we define a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1)  for any graph on three pairwise adjacent vertices  v 1 , v 2 , v 3 v_{1},v_{2},v_{3} ,
where both edge sets between the pairs  v 1 ​ v 2 v_{1}v_{2}  and  v 1 ​ v 3 v_{1}v_{3}  have even sizes,
and the edge set between  v 2 ​ v 3 v_{2}v_{3}  has an odd size.
It can be verified that if  S 2 , 2 , 2 S_{2,2,2}  is a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2) , then
 χ odd ′ ​ ( S 2 , 2 , 2 ) = 6 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{2,2,2})=6 ,
and if  S 2 , 2 , 1 S_{2,2,1}  is a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1) , then  χ odd ′ ​ ( S 2 , 2 , 1 ) = 5 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{2,2,1})=5 . In 2017, Petruševski  [ 4 ]  proved that the above two families of graphs are the only exceptions;
all other graphs are odd  4 4 -edge-colorable. Furthermore, Petruševski  [ 4 ]  also proved that when  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4 , a color class can be reduced to a size of at most 2. In the end of the paper, the author raised a natural question regarding the existence of an edge-analogue of the second part of Theorem   1.1 . That is, for a graph that
is odd  4 4 -edge-colorable, is it possible to delete a particular edge (instead of a vertex), such that the resulting graph is odd  3 3 -edge-colorable?
Additionally, the following conjecture was formally raised later in  [ 6 ] . 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.2 . 
 
 ( Conjecture 2.14 of  [ 6 ] )
For any connected graph which is not a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2)  or type  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1) ,
either  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3 ,
or there exists a special edge  e e  whose removal will make the graph odd  3 3 -edge-colorable. 
 
 
 
 In  [ 4 ] , an effort was already made towards the above conjecture. Note that an equivalent way to express the conjecture is that, assuming  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4 , there exits an odd
 4 4 -edge-coloring in which color  4 4  is used only once.
Another related result, present in  [ 7 ] , obtained a partial result by
confirming the conclusion
for the special class of subdivisions of odd graphs.
Now we are ready to state the first result of this paper, which fully resolves the above conjecture positively. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3 . 
 
 For any connected graph  G G  that is not a Shannon graph of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2)  or  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1) ,
either  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3  or  G G  admits an odd  4 4 -edge-coloring such that one of the four colors is used only once for some edge  e e . Clearly, in the latter case,
 χ odd ′ ​ ( G \ { e } ) = 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G\backslash\{e\})=3 . 
 
 
 
 Now we move to graph coverability. Pyber also inquired in  [ 8 ] 
whether three colors are sufficient to give an odd edge-covering for any simple graph  G G , a question later confirmed by Mátrai  [ 2 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.4 . 
 
 ( [ 2 ] )
For any connected simple graph  G G ,  cov odd ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3 . 
 
 
 
 This result was later partially generalized by Petruševski and Škrekovski  [ 5 ]  to multigraphs. Moreover, as a connection between  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  and  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G) , as well as a tool to obtain more structural
information about edge-colorings and edge-coverings, Petruševski and Škrekovski  [ 5 ]  defined the overlapping function. More precisely,
for an edge-covering  ϕ : E ⁡ ( G ) → 2 [ k ] \ ∅ \phi:E(G)\to 2^{[k]}\backslash\emptyset ,
let  ovl ​ ( ϕ ) \text{ovl}(\phi)  be the sum of sizes of all images under  ϕ \phi  that are not singletons. The following are some examples of the values of the function  ovl ​ ( ϕ ) \text{ovl}(\phi) : 
 

 
 
 ovl ​ ( ϕ ) = { 0 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  is an edge-coloring. 2 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  has exactly one edge receiving two colors, with others receiving one color. 3 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  has exactly one edge receiving three colors, with others receiving one color.  4 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  has exactly two edges receiving two colors each, with others receiving one color. \text{ovl}(\phi)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ is an edge-coloring.}\\
2,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly one edge receiving two colors, with others receiving one color.}\\
3,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly one edge receiving three colors, with others receiving one color. }\\
4,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly two edges receiving two colors each, with others receiving one color.}\end{cases} 
 
 
 
 
 Given a graph  G G  with  cov odd ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3 ,
define 
 

 
 (1.1) 
 
 min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) = min ϕ ⁡ ovl ​ ( ϕ ) , \min\text{ovl}(G)=\min_{\phi}\text{ovl}(\phi), 
 
 
 where the minimum is taken over all odd 3-edge-coverings  ϕ \phi  of  G G . Immediately after the definitions,
if  G G  is odd 3-edge-colorable, then  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) = 0 \min\text{ovl}(G)=0 .
As the main result in  [ 5 ] , it was proved that except for a list of multigraphs with exactly  3 3  vertices, for any other connected graph  G G , one has  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) ∈ { 0 , 2 , 3 , 4 } \min\text{ovl}(G)\in\{0,2,3,4\} . In the same paper, it was also shown that there do exist multigraphs  G G  for which
 min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) \min\text{ovl}(G)  is at least  4 4 . So this leads to the following natural conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.5 . 
 
 ( Conjecture 2.5 of  [ 6 ] )
There is no simple graph  G G  such that  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) = 4 \min\text{ovl}(G)=4 . 
 
 
 
 Our second result of this paper strongly confirms the above conjecture. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.6 . 
 
 For any connected simple graph  G G , we have  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) ≤ 2 \min\text{ovl}(G)\leq 2 . 
 
 
 
 Below, in Section 2, we start with a brief overview of notation and preliminary results. Subsequently, in Section 3, we will address some important special cases, obtaining certain partial results.
We then proceed to prove Theorem  1.3  in Section 4 and Theorem  1.6  in Section 5. 
 
 
 
 2.  Notation and Preliminary Results 

 
 In this section, we introduce some notation that we will frequently use in this work. Given a graph  G G , we define a bouquet  ℬ \mathcal{B}  as the set of parallel edges on a pair of adjacent vertices.
For any two adjacent vertices  v v  and  u u , let  ℬ v ​ u \mathcal{B}_{vu}  denote the edge set incident to  v v  and  u u .
Given a loopless graph  G G , define a reduction graph  red ​ ( G ) \text{red}(G)  to be the spanning subgraph of  G G  where each bouquet  ℬ \mathcal{B}  is reduced to size  1 1  if it has an odd size,
and to size  2 2  if it has an even size.
It was proved in  [ 1 ] 
that the odd chromatic index of
the reduction graph  red ​ ( G ) \text{red}(G)  is greater than or equal to that of  G G .
It follows that for any loopless graph  G G , if  red ​ ( G ) \text{red}(G)  satisfies the conclusion of Theorem  1.3 , then the same is true for  G G .
Therefore, below we will only consider reduced graphs, that is, graphs satisfying  G = red ​ ( G ) G=\text{red}(G) . 
 
 
 A vertex  v v  is a  cut vertex  if  G − v G-v  is disconnected; otherwise, it is called an  internal vertex .
Let  v v  be a cut vertex of  G G , and let  C 1 , ⋯ , C k C_{1},\cdots,C_{k}  be the connected components of  G − v G-v .
Then each  G ⁡ [ V ⁡ ( C i ) ∪ { v } ] G[V(C_{i})\cup\{v\}] , for  i = 1 , ⋯ , k i=1,\cdots,k , is called a  v v -lobe  of  G G .
A  block  of a graph  G G  is a maximal connected subgraph of  G G  that contains no cut vertices. For a block  B B  of a graph  G G , the collection of internal vertices in  B B  is denoted by  I ​ n ​ t G ​ ( B ) Int_{G}(B) .
If a block of  G G  contains at most one cut vertex of  G G , we say it is
an  end-block  of  G G .
For each vertex  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , let  E G ​ ( v ) E_{G}(v)  be the set of edges that are incident to  v v ,
and  N G ​ ( v ) N_{G}(v)  be the set of neighbors of  v v  in  G G . In a reduced graph  G G ,  N G ′ ​ ( v ) N_{G}^{\prime}(v)  denotes the set of neighbors of  v v  in a bouquet of size 1, and  N G ′′ ​ ( v ) N_{G}^{\prime\prime}(v)  denotes the set of neighbors of  v v  in a bouquet of size 2. Let  T T  be an even-sized subset of  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . We define a spanning subgraph  H H  of  G G  to be a  T T -join  if  d H ​ ( v ) d_{H}(v)  is odd for all  v ∈ T v\in T  and even for all  v ∉ T v\notin T . 
 
 
 The following lemmas contain some long-known facts. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.1 . 
 
 (Proposition 2.5 of   [ 4 ] )
If  G G  is a connected graph that contains a bridge then  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3 . 
 
 
 
 Lemma 2.2 . 
 
 ( [ 2 ]  and  [ 6 ] ) Any forest  F F  is odd  2 2 -edge-colorable. 
 
 
 Moreover, for a fixed vertex  v v  in  F F , any local coloring of  E F ​ ( v ) E_{F}(v)  that uses at most two colors extends to a 2-edge-coloring of  F F  that is odd away from  v v . 
 
 
 
 For the first statement above,
we can start by considering any vertex  v ∈ F v\in F .
If  d F ​ ( v ) d_{F}(v)  is odd, we can assign color  1 1  to  E F ​ ( v ) E_{F}(v) .
If  d F ​ ( v ) d_{F}(v)  is even, we can assign colors  1 1  and  2 2  to  E F ​ ( v ) E_{F}(v)  arbitrarily, ensuring that both colors are used an odd number of times.
Then, one can greedily extend the above coloring to an odd  2 2 -edge-coloring of  F F . 
 
 
 Lemma 2.3 . 
 
 ( [ 9 ] ) Let  G G  be a connected graph and let  T ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) T\subseteq V(G)  be an even-sized subset. Then there exists a  T T -join of  G G  that can be either a forest or a coforest. 
 
 
 
 Let us remark that the second part of Theorem  1.1  follows directly from the above lemma. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.4 . 
 
 (Proposition 2.6 of   [ 4 ] )
Let  G G  be a connected graph and  v v  be an internal vertex. Let  T ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) T\subseteq V(G)  be an even-sized set. Then there exists a  T T -join  H H  of  G G  which is a coforest, such that the following additional property holds for  v v  in  H ^ \hat{H} , where  H ^ = G \ E ⁡ ( H ) \hat{H}=G\backslash E(H) . 
 
 (a)  
 
 If either  d G ​ ( v ) d_{G}(v)  is odd and  v ∈ T v\in T , or  d G ​ ( v ) d_{G}(v)  is even and  v ∉ T v\notin T , then  d H ^ ​ ( v ) = 0 d_{\hat{H}}(v)=0 . 
 
 (b)  
 
 If either  d G ​ ( v ) d_{G}(v)  is odd and  v ∉ T v\notin T , or  d G ​ ( v ) d_{G}(v)  is even and  v ∈ T v\in T , then  d H ^ ​ ( v ) = 1 d_{\hat{H}}(v)=1 . 
 
 
 Moreover, in Case (b), we can indicate a specific edge  e e  incident to  v v  such that  e ∈ E ⁡ ( H ^ ) e\in E(\hat{H}) . 
 
 
 
 Recall that a block graph is a connected graph admitting no cut vertex.
The following lemma is elementary and we omit its proof. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.5 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a block graph with at least three vertices.
Then for any three distinct vertices of  G G , there is a path which joins any two of them and contains the third vertex. 
 
 
 
 
 3.  Some Special Cases 

 
 Aiming to prove our two main theorems, in this section, we present some preliminary results dealing with special cases.
Note that some important partial results will already be obtained in this section. For example,
Theorem  1.3  will be proved under the condition that the graph  G G  admits at least one cut vertex.
We divide this section into two subsections for edge-colorings and edge-coverings, respectively.
Certain arguments are based on the various coloring and covering schemes provided in  [ 4 ]  and  [ 5 ] , respectively. 
 
 
 3.1.  Odd-Edge-Colorings 

 
 Let  S ℓ S_{\ell}  be a star graph with  ℓ \ell  leaves. When  ℓ = 1 \ell=1 , we regard one vertex as a leaf and
the other vertex as the center, instead of two leaves. We write  c ⁡ ( G ) c(G)  to denote the number of connected components of  G G .
The following technical lemma assists in identifying an edge of interest in a fixed tree. 
 
 
 Lemma 3.1 . 
 
 Let  T T  be a tree with  | V ⁡ ( T ) | ≥ 3 |V(T)|\geq 3 .
Let  M M  be a subgraph of  T T , satisfying the following conditions. 
 
 (1)  
 
 each connected component of  M M  is either a copy of  S 1 S_{1}  or a copy of  S 2 S_{2} ,
whose leaves are also leaves of  T T . 
 
 (2)  
 
 the total number of leaves of all the connected components of  M M  is even. 
 
 (3)  
 
 c ⁡ ( M ) ≥ 2 c(M)\geq 2 , and at least two connected components of  M M  are  S 1 S_{1} ’s. 
 
 
 Then there is an edge  e e  of  T ∖ E ⁡ ( M ) T\setminus E(M)  such that  T \ { e } = L ∪ R T\backslash\{e\}=L\cup R 
is a union of two subtrees,  L L  and  R R ,
and the number of leaves in both connected components  M ∩ L M\cap L  and  M ∩ R M\cap R  is odd. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Assume for contradiction that
 T T  is a tree with the smallest number of edges that admits some subgraph  M M  satisfying the three conditions, and the pair  ( T , M ) (T,M)  fails to satisfy the intended conclusion.
By assumption, we can find two components of  M M  which are  S 1 S_{1} ’s.
Let  u u  and  v v  denote their center vertices, respectively.
Let  P u ​ v P_{uv}  be the path in the tree  T T  joining vertices  u u  and  v v , and let  e e  be any edge in this path.
Let us write  T \ { e } = L ∪ R T\backslash\{e\}=L\cup R , a union of two subtrees.
By the assumption for  T T , the number of leaves in all the connected components of  M ∩ L M\cap L  is even, and  M ∩ L M\cap L  has at least one  S 1 S_{1} . This implies that there are at least two  S 1 S_{1} ’s in  M ∩ L M\cap L  and confirms condition (3).
By the minimality of  T T , in  L L  we can find an edge  f f  such that  L \ { f } L\backslash\{f\}  is a union of two subtrees  T 1 ∪ T 2 T_{1}\cup T_{2} , each
of which intersects  M M  with an odd number of leaves.
Observe that exactly one of these two subtrees, say  T 2 T_{2} , contains an end-vertex of  e e . Therefore,
 T \ { f } T\backslash\{f\}  is a union of two trees  T 1 ∪ ( T 2 ∪ { e } ∪ R ) T_{1}\cup(T_{2}\cup\{e\}\cup R) , and
the number of leaves of all the connected components of  T 1 ∩ M T_{1}\cap M  is odd, which is absurd.
∎ 
 
 
 
 We define that a graph  H H  has a  minimum feedback vertex set  (abbreviated as  min \min  FVS) of size  1 1  if  H H  contains a cycle and there exists a vertex  v v  such that  H − v H-v  is acyclic. We can refer to  v v  as a root of  H H . The following lemma is the same with Proposition 2.3 in  [ 6 ] . 
 
 
 Lemma 3.2 . 
 
 Let  H H  be a graph with a  min \min  FVS of size  1 1 , with a root  v v .
Then there exits an edge-coloring  φ : E ⁡ ( H ) → { 1 , 2 } \varphi:E(H)\to\{1,2\}  such that for each  w ≠ v w\neq v , both colors 1 and 2 are odd at  w w .
Moreover,
if  d H ​ ( v ) d_{H}(v)  is odd, then color  1 1  (respectively, color  2 2 ) is odd (respectively, even) at  v v .
If  d H ​ ( v ) d_{H}(v)  is even, then either both color degrees of  v v  are even, or both are odd. 
 
 
 
 Lemma 3.3 . 
 
 Let  H H  be a connected graph with a  min \min  FVS of size  1 1 , with a root  v v .
Assume  χ odd ′ ​ ( H ) = 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(H)=3 ,  d H ​ ( v ) d_{H}(v) 
is even, and  N H ′ ​ ( v ) ≥ 2 N_{H}^{\prime}(v)\geq 2 . Then there exists an edge  e e  of  H H  such that  H \ { e } H\backslash\{e\}  is odd  2 2 -edge-colorable. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Suppose  d H ​ ( v ) = 2 ​ k d_{H}(v)=2k , for some integer  k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 .
We obtain a new graph  H ′ H^{\prime}  by blowing up the vertex  v v  into  2 ​ k 2k  distinct vertices,  v 1 , v 2 , ⋯ , v 2 ​ k v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{2k} .
Therefore  H ′ H^{\prime}  is a forest and the original edge set  E H ​ ( v ) E_{H}(v)  becomes now
 E ⁡ ( M ) = { e 1 , ⋯ , e 2 ​ k } E(M)=\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{2k}\} , where  M = H ′ ​ [ { v , v 1 , ⋯ , v 2 ​ k } ] M=H^{\prime}[\{v,v_{1},\cdots,v_{2k}\}] .
The components of  M M  are  S 1 S_{1} ’s and  S 2 S_{2} ’s, where there are at least two  S 1 S_{1} ’s since  N H ′ ​ ( v ) ≥ 2 N_{H}^{\prime}(v)\geq 2 . Clearly, by Lemma  2.2 ,  H ′ H^{\prime}  admits an odd  2 2 -edge-coloring  ψ : E ⁡ ( H ′ ) → { 1 , 2 } \psi:E(H^{\prime})\to\{1,2\} .
The number of edges
in  { e 1 , e 2 , ⋯ , e 2 ​ k } \{e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{2k}\}  that are assigned with color  1 1  (or color  2 2 ) is even;
otherwise,  ψ \psi  induces
an odd  2 2 -edge-coloring for  H H , contradicting our assumption that  χ odd ′ ​ ( H ) = 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(H)=3 .
Now we consider the following cases. 
 
 
 Case 1.   There is a component of  H ′ H^{\prime}  containing an odd number of leaves of  M M . 
 
 
 Let  H 1 H_{1}  be such a component, where there is an even number of edges of  M M  with one color, without loss of generality, color  1 1 , and an odd number of edges of  M M  with color  2 2 . Then, in  H ′ − H 1 H^{\prime}-H_{1} , there are also an even number of edges with color  1 1  and an odd number of edges with color  2 2 . We can switch colors  1 1  and  2 2  in  H 1 H_{1}  such that there are an even number of edges with color  2 2  and an odd number of edges with color  1 1 . However, this leads to an odd  2 2 -edge-coloring for  H H , a contradiction with  χ odd ′ ​ ( H ) = 3 \chi^{\prime}_{\text{odd}}(H)=3 . 
   Case 2.   Every component of  H ′ H^{\prime}  has an even number of leaves of  M M . 
 
 
 Since  N H ′ ​ ( v ) ≥ 2 N_{H}^{\prime}(v)\geq 2 , there is a component  H 1 H_{1}  that has at least two  S 1 S_{1}  stars in  M M .
By Lemma  3.1 , there is an edge  e e  of  H 1 \ { e 1 , ⋯ , e 2 ​ k } H_{1}\backslash\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{2k}\}  such that  H 1 \ { e } H_{1}\backslash\{e\}  has one component  H 1 ′ H_{1}^{\prime}  with an odd number of leaves in  M M . We can switch the edge colors in  H 1 ′ H_{1}^{\prime} , as in Case 1, and obtain an odd 2-edge-coloring of  H \ { e } H\backslash\{e\} . Hence,  H \ { e } H\backslash\{e\}  is odd 2-edge-colorable.
∎ 
 
 
 
 The following two technical lemmas deal with graphs admitting cut vertices. 
 
 
 Lemma 3.4 .

 4.  Proof of Theorem  1.3 

 
 This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem  1.3 .
The main strategy of the proof involves searching for different coloring schemes that use only one of the four colors once, given that
 χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4 .
For example, we have already seen that this strategy works for the special case when the graph admits a cut vertex, as discussed in the previous section.
There are two additional ingredients in the proof.
The first ingredient is the method of removing one edge from a certain tree or a graph with a  min \min  FVS of size  1 1 , as demonstrated in Lemma  3.3 .
This removal gives us some freedom to color the distinct components of the resulting forest separately.
The second ingredient is a dichotomous approach to two coloring schemes for a specific graph, as demonstrated in Lemma  3.9 .
This flexibility is crucial for addressing the various cases that emerge in subsequent arguments. 
 
 
 Now we begin the proof
by considering a connected graph  G G  which is not a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2)  or type  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1) .
By Theorem  1.1 , if  G G  has even order, then  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3 .
If  v ⁡ ( G ) = 3 v(G)=3 , it is also not hard to conclude.
Therefore, below we can safely
assume that  G G  has odd order and  v ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 5 v(G)\geq 5 .
By comparing the parities, we observe that there exists at least one even vertex in  G G .
Let  ℰ \mathcal{E}  (respectively,  𝒪 \mathcal{O} ) be the set of even (respectively, odd) vertices in  G G .
Moreover, according to Lemmas  3.4  and  3.5 , we can assume that  G G  is a block graph. 
 
 
 We then divide into two cases.
In the first case, where there exists an even vertex  v v  satisfying  | N G ′ ​ ( v ) | > 0 |N_{G}^{\prime}(v)|>0 ,
the proof will be concise since most of the groundwork has been covered in Section  3 .
In the second case, where every even vertex  v v  satisfies  | N G ′ ​ ( v ) | = 0 |N_{G}^{\prime}(v)|=0 , we will follow some ideas from  [ 4 ] . In particular, we will split the main argument into a sequence of six claims to organize various coloring schemes for different specific cases.
