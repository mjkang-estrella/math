 Abstract 
    
 A well-known theorem of Mantel states that every
 n n -vertex graph with more than  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor  edges contains a triangle. An interesting problem in extremal graph theory
studies the minimum number of edges
contained in triangles among
graphs with a prescribed number of vertices and edges.
Erdős, Faudree and Rousseau (1992) showed that
a graph on  n n  vertices with more than  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor  edges
contains at least  2 ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ + 1 2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1  edges in triangles.
Such edges are called triangular edges.
In this paper, we present a spectral version of
the result of Erdős, Faudree and Rousseau.
Using the supersaturation-stability and the spectral technique,
we prove that every  n n -vertex graph
 G G  with  λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor}  contains at least  2 ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ − 1 2\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor-1  triangular edges, unless
 G G  is a balanced complete bipartite graph.
The method in our paper has some interesting applications.
Firstly, the supersaturation-stability can
be used to revisit a conjecture of Erdős concerning with
the booksize of a graph, which was initially proved by
Edwards (unpublished),
and independently by Khadžiivanov and Nikiforov (1979).
Secondly, our method can improve the bound on the order  n n  of a graph by
dropping the condition on  n n  being sufficiently large,
which is obtained
from the triangle removal lemma.
Thirdly, the supersaturation-stability can be applied to
deal with the spectral extremal graph problems on counting triangles,
which was recently studied by Ning and Zhai (2023). 
  
 
 
 Key words:  Extremal graph theory; triangular edges; spectral radius. 
 
 
 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification.  05C35; 05C50. 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Extremal combinatorics is increasingly becoming
a fascinating mathematical discipline as well as an essential
component of many mathematical areas, and it has experienced an impressive
growth in recent years.
Extremal combinatorics concerns the problems
of determining the maximal or the minimal size of a combinatorial object that satisfies certain properties.
One of the most important problems is the
so-called Turán-type problem, which has played an important role in the development
of extremal combinatorics.
More precisely,
the Turán-type questions usually
study the maximum possible number of edges in
a graph that does not contain a specific subgraph.
Such kind of questions could be
viewed as the cornerstone of extremal graph theory and have been studied
extensively in the literature. 
 
 
 A graph  G G  is  F F -free  if it does not contain a subgraph isomorphic to  F F .
For example, every bipartite graph is triangle-free.
A classical result in extremal graph theory is Mantel’s theorem  [ 5 ] ,
which asserts that every triangle-free graph on  n n 
vertices contains at most
 ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor  edges.
This result is tight
by considering the bipartite Turán graph  T n , 2 T_{n,2} , where
 T n , 2 T_{n,2}  is a complete bipartite graph whose
two vertex parts have sizes as equal as possible.
Equivalently, each graph on  n n  vertices
with more than  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor  edges must contain a triangle. 
 
 
 There are several results in the literature that guarantee something stronger than just one triangle.
For example, in 1941,
Rademacher (unpublished paper, see Erdős  [ 15 ,  18 ] ) showed that such graphs contain at least  ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ \lfloor n/2\rfloor  triangles.
After this result,
Erdős  [ 16 ,  17 ] 
showed that there exists a small constant
 c > 0 c>0  such that if  n n  is large enough and  1 ≤ q < c ​ n 1\leq q<cn , then
every  n n -vertex graph with  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + q \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+q 
edges has at least  q ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor  triangles.
Furthermore, Erdős conjectured the constant  c = 1 / 2 c={1}/{2} , which was finally confirmed by
Lovász and Simonovits  [ 39 ,  40 ] 
in 1975, they proved that
if  1 ≤ q < n / 2 1\leq q<{n}/{2}  is a positive integer and  G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with
 e ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + q e(G)\geq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor+q ,
then  G G  contains at least  q ​ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ q\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor 
triangles.
We refer the readers to  [ 62 ,  38 ,  4 ] 
for recent generalizations on the Erdős–Rademacher problem.
Moreover, Lovász and Simonovits  [ 40 ] 
also studied the supersaturation problem
for cliques in the case  q = o ⁡ ( n 2 ) q=o(n^{2}) .
While for  q = Ω ⁡ ( n 2 ) q=\Omega(n^{2}) ,
this problem turns out to be notoriously difficult.
Some recent progresses were presented by
Razborov  [ 54 ] , Nikiforov  [ 49 ] , Reiher  [ 55 ] , Liu, Pikhurko and Staden
 [ 37 ] . In addition, the supersaturation
problems for color-critical graphs were studied by Mubayi  [ 42 ] , and Pikhurko and Yilma  [ 53 ] . 
 
 
 1.1  Minimizing the number of triangular edges 

 
 In this paper, we shall consider the supersaturation problem
from a different point of view.
An edge is called  triangular  if it is contained in a triangle.
We shall consider the problem on counting
the number of triangular edges, rather than the number of triangles.
The first result in this topic was obtained by
Erdős, Faudree and Rousseau  [ 19 ] , who
provided a tight bound on
the number of triangular edges in any
 n n -vertex graph
with more than  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor  edges. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1   (Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau, 1992) . 
 
 Let  G G  be a graph with  n n  vertices and 
 

 
 
 e ⁡ ( G ) > e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) . e(G)>e(T_{n,2}). 
 
 
 Then  G G  has at least  2 ​ ⌊ n 2 ⌋ + 1 2\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1  triangular edges. 
 
 
 
 This bound is the best possible simply by adding an edge to the larger vertex part of
the balanced complete bipartite graph.
Motivated by the problem about the number of triangles,
it is natural to ask how many triangular edges an  n n -vertex
graph with  m m  edges must have, where
 m m  is an integer satisfying  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ < m ≤ ( n 2 ) \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor<m\leq{n\choose 2} .
Indeed, this problem was recently studied by
Füredi and Maleki  [ 23 ] 
as well as Gruslys and Letzter  [ 24 ] .
Given integers  a , b , c a,b,c , let
 G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c)  denote the graph on
 n = a + b + c n=a+b+c  vertices, which consists of a clique  A A 
of size  a a  and two independent sets  B B  and  C C 
of sizes  b b  and  c c  respectively, such that
all edges between  B B  and  A ∪ C A\cup C  induces a complete bipartite graph  K b , a + c K_{b,a+c} .
In other words, the graph  G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c)  can be obtained from
 K b , a + c K_{b,a+c}  by embedding a clique of order  a a  into the part of size  a + c a+c .
Note that  G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c)  has  ( a 2 ) + ( a + c ) ​ b {a\choose 2}+(a+c)b  edges and
it has  ( a 2 ) + a ​ b = m − b ​ c {a\choose 2}+ab=m-bc  triangular edges.
In 2017, Füredi and Maleki  [ 23 ] 
conjectured that the minimizers of the number of triangular
edges are graphs of the form  G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c)  or
subgraphs of such graphs. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.2   (Füredi–Maleki, 2017) . 
 
 Let  m > n 2 / 4 m>n^{2}/4  and  G G  be an  n n -vertex graph with  m m  edges that minimizes the number of triangular edges.
Then  G G  is isomorphic to a subgraph of  G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c) 
for some  a , b , c a,b,c . 
 
 
 
 Particularly, Füredi and Maleki  [ 23 ] 
proposed a numerical conjecture, which states that
every  n n -vertex graph with  m m  edges has at least
 g ⁡ ( n , m ) g(n,m)  triangular edges, where 
 

 
 
 g ( n , m ) = min { m − b c : a + b + c = n , ( a 2 ) + b ( a + c ) ≥ m } . g(n,m)=\min\left\{m-bc:a+b+c=n,{a\choose 2}+b(a+c)\geq m\right\}. 
 
 
 We remark that Conjecture  1.2 
characterizes the structures of the minimizers,
while the latter conjecture gives a lower bound only.
By using a generalization of Zykov’s symmetrization
method,
Füredi and Maleki  [ 23 ] 
showed an asymptotic formula:
if  G G  is a graph on  n n  vertices with  m > n 2 / 4 m>n^{2}/4  edges,
then  G G  has at least
 g ⁡ ( n , m ) − 3 ​ n / 2 g(n,m)-3n/2 
triangular edges.
Soon after, Gruslys and Letzter  [ 24 ] 
proved an exact version of the result of Füredi and Maleki.
Let  𝐍𝐓 ⁡ ( G ) \mathbf{NT}(G)  be the set of non-triangular edges of  G G .
The following result was established in  [ 24 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3   (Gruslys–Letzter, 2018) . 
 
 There is  n 0 n_{0}  such that
for any graph  G G  on  n ≥ n 0 n\geq n_{0}  vertices, there exists
a graph  H = G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) H=G(a,b,c)  on  n n  vertices such that  e ⁡ ( H ) ≥ e ⁡ ( G ) e(H)\geq e(G)  and  | 𝐍𝐓 ⁡ ( H ) | ≥ | 𝐍𝐓 ⁡ ( G ) | |\mathbf{NT}(H)|\geq|\mathbf{NT}(G)| . 
 
 
 
 Theorem  1.3  shows that for sufficient large  n n ,
the minimum number of triangular edges among all
 n n -vertex graphs with at least
 m m  edges is attained by the graph  G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c)  or its subgraph
for some  a , b , c a,b,c .
We refer the readers to  [ 24 ] 
for more details and  [ 25 ] 
for the study on the minimum number of edges that occur in odd cycles. 
 
 
 
 1.2  Spectral extremal graph problems 

 
 Spectral graph theory aims to apply the eigenvalues of matrices associated
with graphs to find the structural information of graphs.
Let  G G  be a simple graph on vertex set  { v 1 , v 2 , … , v n } \{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\} . The adjacency matrix of
 G G  is defined as  A ⁡ ( G ) = [ a i , j ] i , j = 1 n A(G)=[a_{i,j}]_{i,j=1}^{n} ,
where  a i , j = 1 a_{i,j}=1  if  v i v_{i}  and  v j v_{j}  are adjacent, and  a i , j = 0 a_{i,j}=0  otherwise.
Let  λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(G)  be the spectral radius
of  G G , which is defined as the maximum of modulus
of eigenvalues of  A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) .
The study in this article mainly concentrates on the adjacency spectral radius. 
 
 
 As mentioned before,
the Turán type problem studies the maximal size of
a graph forbidding certain subgraphs.
In particular, one could wish to investigate the maximum possible spectral radius of the associated adjacency matrix of a graph which does not contain certain subgraphs.
The interplay between these two areas above
is called the spectral Turán-type problem.
One of the famous results of this type was obtained
in 1986 by Wilf  [ 61 ]  who showed that
every graph  G G  on  n n  vertices with
 λ ⁡ ( G ) > ( 1 − 1 / r ) ​ n \lambda(G)>(1-{1}/{r})n  contains
a clique  K r + 1 K_{r+1} .
This spectral version generalized the classical Turán theorem
by invoking the fact  λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ m / n \lambda(G)\geq 2m/n .
It is worth emphasizing that spectral Turán problems have been receiving considerable
attention in the last two decades
and it is still an attractive topic; see, e.g.,
 [ 61 ,  45 ,  46 ,  30 ]  for graphs with no cliques,  [ 7 ,  35 ,  67 ,  14 ]  for a conjecture of Bollobás and Nikiforov,
 [ 35 ,  64 ,  33 ]  for non-bipartite triangle-free graphs,
 [ 58 ,  34 ]  for planar graphs and outplanar graphs,
 [ 47 ]  for a spectral Erdős–Stone–Bollobás theorem,
 [ 48 ]  for the spectral stability theorem,
 [ 10 ,  28 ] 
for spectral problems on cycles,
 [ 9 ]  for a spectral Erdős–Sós theorem,
 [ 20 ]  for some specific trees,
 [ 63 ]  for a spectral Erdős–Pósa theorem,
 [ 66 ,  50 ]  for books and theta graphs,
 [ 27 ,  68 ]  for cycles of consecutive lengths,
 [ 60 ]  for a spectral conjecture
on a class of graphs, and
 [ 59 ,  65 ]  for graphs without
 K t K_{t} -minors or  K s , t K_{s,t} -minors. 
 
 
 Although there has been a wealth of research
on the spectral extremal problems in recent years,
there are  very few  conclusions on problems
of counting substructures in terms of the spectral radius.
The first result in this topic
can even be traced back to a
result of Bollobás
and Nikiforov  [ 7 ]  who
showed that for every  n n -vertex graph  G G  and  r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 ,
the number of cliques of order  r + 1 r+1  satisfies 
 

 
 
 k r + 1 ​ ( G ) ≥ ( λ ⁡ ( G ) n − 1 + 1 r ) ​ r ⁡ ( r − 1 ) r + 1 ​ ( n r ) r + 1 . k_{r+1}(G)\geq\left(\frac{\lambda(G)}{n}-1+\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{r(r-1)}{r+1}\left(\frac{n}{r}\right)^{r+1}. 
 
 
 This result provides a spectral version of
supersaturation on cliques. 
 
 
 In 2023, Ning and Zhai  [ 52 ] 
studied the spectral saturation on triangles.
A result of Erdős and Rademacher states that
every  n n -vertex graph  G G  with  e ⁡ ( G ) > e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) e(G)>e(T_{n,2})  contains at least
 ⌊ n 2 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor  triangles. Correspondingly,
it is natural to consider the spectral version:
if  G G  is a graph with
 λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2}) ,
whether  G G  has at least  ⌊ n 2 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor  triangles.
Unfortunately, this result is not true.
Let  K a , b + K_{a,b}^{+}  be the graph obtained from the complete bipartite graph  K a , b K_{a,b}  by adding an edge to
the vertex set of size  a a .
For even  n n , we take  a = n 2 + 1 a=\frac{n}{2}+1  and  b = n 2 − 1 b=\frac{n}{2}-1 .
One can verify that  λ ⁡ ( K n 2 + 1 , n 2 − 1 + ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}-1}^{+})>\lambda(T_{n,2}) , while  K n 2 + 1 , n 2 − 1 + K_{\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}-1}^{+}  has exactly  n 2 − 1 \frac{n}{2}-1  triangles.
Recently, Ning and Zhai  [ 52 ]  provided
the following tight bound. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.4   (Ning–Zhai, 2023) . 
 
 If  G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with 
 

 
 
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) , \lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2}), 
 
 
 then  G G  has at least
 ⌊ n 2 ⌋ − 1 \left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-1  triangles,
unless  G G  is the bipartite Turán graph  T n , 2 T_{n,2} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 2  Main results 

 
 In the sequel, we shall put our attention on the extremal graph
problems
concerning the spectral supersaturation.
Specifically,
we shall present a tight bound on
the number of triangular edges
in a graph with spectral radius larger than that of  T n , 2 T_{n,2} . Hence, we prove a spectral version of
the result of Erdős, Faudree and Rousseau. 
 
 
 Theorem 2.1 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a graph with  n ≥ 5432 n\geq 5432  vertices and 
 

 
 
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) . \lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,2}). 
 
 
 Then  G G  has at least  2 ​ ⌊ n 2 ⌋ − 1 2\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1 
triangular edges, unless  G = T n , 2 G=T_{n,2} . 
 
 
 
 The spectral condition in Theorem  2.1 
is easier to satisfy than the edge-condition in Theorem  1.1 .
Namely, if a graph  G G  satisfies  e ⁡ ( G ) > e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) e(G)>e(T_{n,2}) ,
then  λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2}) .
This observation can be guaranteed by
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ e ​ ( G ) / n \lambda(G)\geq 2e(G)/n . Nevertheless,
there are many graphs with  λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2}) 
but  e ⁡ ( G ) < e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) e(G)<e(T_{n,2}) . Let  S n , k S_{n,k}  be the split graph, which is the join of a clique of size  k k  with an independent set of size  n − k n-k . Taking  k = n / 5 k=n/5 , we can verify that
 S n , k S_{n,k}  is a required example.
A few words regarding the tightness of Theorem  2.1 
are due.
We show in next section that
there exist three graphs  G G  such that  λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2})  and  G G  has exactly
 2 ​ ⌊ n 2 ⌋ − 1 2\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1  triangular edges,
which implies the bound in Theorem  2.1  is tight. 
 
 
 It is reasonable to reach
such a difference between the results on the spectral radius and the size of a graph. Note that if  e ⁡ ( G ) > e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) e(G)>e(T_{n,2}) , then  e ⁡ ( G ) ≥ e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) + 1 e(G)\geq e(T_{n,2})+1  holds immediately.
While, if  λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2})  holds, then there are many graphs with
 λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(G)  very closing to  λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(T_{n,2})  and  e ⁡ ( G ) = e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) e(G)=e(T_{n,2}) .
Roughly speaking, the spectral radii of graphs
are distributed more compactly.
Motivated by this observation,
Li, Lu and Peng  [ 32 ]  proposed a spectral conjecture on Mubayi’s result  [ 42 ]  and showed a spectral version of Erdős–Rademacher’s theorem.
Next, we are going to provide a variant of Theorem  2.1 . We shall establish a spectral condition
corresponding to the edge condition
 e ⁡ ( G ) ≥ e ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) + 1 e(G)\geq e(T_{n,2})+1 .
Recall that  K ⌈ n 2 ⌉ , ⌊ n 2 ⌋ + K_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{+}  is the graph obtained from the complete bipartite graph
 K ⌈ n 2 ⌉ , ⌊ n 2 ⌋ K_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}  by adding an edge to the vertex part of size  ⌈ n 2 ⌉ \lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil . 
 
 
 Theorem 2.2 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a graph on  n ≥ 5432 n\geq 5432  vertices with 
 

 
 
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ λ ⁡ ( K ⌈ n 2 ⌉ , ⌊ n 2 ⌋ + ) . \lambda(G)\geq\lambda(K_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{+}). 
 
 
 Then  G G  has at least  2 ​ ⌊ n 2 ⌋ + 1 2\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+1 
triangular edges, with equality if and only if
 G = K ⌈ n 2 ⌉ , ⌊ n 2 ⌋ + G=K_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{+} . 
 
 
 
 Our proofs of Theorems  2.1 
and  2.2  are quite different from
that of Theorem  1.4 .
It is a classical spectral method
to use the Perron eigenvector together with the walks of length two
to deduce the structural properties of spectral extremal graphs; see, e.g.,  [ 58 ,  59 ,  9 ,  34 ,  52 ,  65 ] .
However, applying this spectral method turns out to be difficult for graphs with much more triangles or triangular edges.
The key ingredient in our proof attributes to
a supersaturation-stability result (Theorem  4.5 ), which
says roughly that if a graph is far from being bipartite,
then it contains a large number of triangles.
This result may be of independent interest.
Although we used the stability method,
we only need a weak bound  n ≥ 5432 n\geq 5432  exactly * * 
             * 
            
            
            
          It seems possible to obtain a slightly better bound. To avoid unnecessary and tedious calculations, we did not attempt to get the best bound on the order of the graph in our proof.  ,
instead of the strong condition that  n n  is sufficiently large.
Apart from the supersaturation-stability,
another technique used in this paper is
a spectral technique developed by
Cioabă, Feng, Tait and Zhang  [ 8 ] ;
see, e.g.,  [ 29 ,  13 ,  60 ]  for recent results. Furthermore, we will obtain some approximately
structural results that describe the almost-extremal graphs with
large spectral radius and few triangular edges. 
 
 
 We anticipate that with additional efforts,
this method used in the proof of Theorems  2.1 
and  2.2  could be possibly applied to treat some spectral extremal graph problems in which the desired extremal graph contains a small number of triangles.
Incidentally, an upper bound on the number of triangular edges eventually leads to a restriction on the number of triangles.
In particular,
we shall present three quick applications of our method in Subsection  4.3 .
The first application gives a short proof of a conjecture of Erdős, which asserts that every  n n -vertex graph with more than  n 2 / 4 n^{2}/4  edges contains more than  n / 6 n/6  triangles sharing a common edge.
The second application allows us to simplify the proof of the main result
in  [ 8 ] , and it can also improve the bound on the order of graphs,
which was previously obtained from the celebrated triangle removal lemma.
The last application provides an alternative proof for
the elegant result of Ning and Zhai stated in Theorem
 1.4 .

One of the key ideas in this paper, i.e.,
the supersaturation-stability (Theorems  4.3  and  4.5 ),
would be introduced in Section  4 .
As indicated above, some quick applications
of the supersaturation-stability method will be provided in Subsection  4.3 .
In Sections  5  and  6 ,
we will present the detailed proofs of Theorems  2.1  and
 2.2 , respectively.
After proving our results, we propose some related spectral extremal problems involving
the edges that occur in cliques or odd cycles.

 3  Some auxiliary lemmas

 4  The supersaturation-stability results 

 
 4.1  The Lovász–Simonovits stability 

 
 To prove and generalize Erdős’ conjecture on
triangle-supersaturated graphs, Lovász and Simonovits  [ 39 ] 
proved a stability result,
and a much more general theorem in  [ 40 ] ,
the simplest form of which is the following: 
 
 
 Theorem 4.1   (Lovász–Simonovits stability) . 
 
 For any constant  C > 0 C>0 , there exists an  ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 
such that if  | k | < ε ​ n 2 |k|<\varepsilon n^{2}  and  G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with
 ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + k \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+k  edges and fewer than
 C ​ | k | ​ n C|k|n  triangles, then one can remove  O ⁡ ( | k | ) O(|k|)  edges from
 G G  to get a bipartite graph. 
 
 
 
 It was shown in  [ 40 ]  that if  G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with
 e ⁡ ( G ) = ( 1 − 1 x ) ​ n 2 2 e(G)=(1-\frac{1}{x})\frac{n^{2}}{2}  edges, where  x > 1 x>1  is a real number,
then for any integer  p ≤ x + 1 p\leq x+1 ,
the number of  p p -cliques satisfies  k p ​ ( G ) ≥ ( x p ) ​ ( n x ) p k_{p}(G)\geq{x\choose p}(\frac{n}{x})^{p} ;
see, e.g.,  [ 41 , p. 449]  for a detailed proof.
In the next, we introduce a more general theorem on stability.
Let  T n , p T_{n,p}  denote the  p p -partite Turán graph on  n n  vertices, that is,  T n , p T_{n,p}  is a complete  p p -partite graph
whose parts have sizes as equal as possible. 
 
 
 Theorem 4.2   (Lovász–Simonovits, 1983) . 
 
 Let  C > 0 C>0  be an arbitrary constant.
There exist constants  δ > 0 \delta>0  and  C ′ > 0 C^{\prime}>0  such that
if  1 ≤ k < δ ​ n 2 1\leq k<\delta n^{2}  and  G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with
 e ⁡ ( G ) = ( 1 − 1 x ) ​ n 2 2 e(G)=(1-\frac{1}{x})\frac{n^{2}}{2} , and  p ≤ x + 1 p\leq x+1  is an integer
satisfying  e ⁡ ( G ) = e ⁡ ( T n , p ) + k e(G)=e(T_{n,p})+k  and 
 

 
 
 k p ​ ( G ) < ( x p ) ​ ( n x ) p + C ​ k ​ n p − 2 , k_{p}(G)<{x\choose p}\left(\frac{n}{x}\right)^{p}+Ckn^{p-2}, 
 
 
 then  G G  can be made  ⌊ x ⌋ \lfloor x\rfloor -partite
by removing at most  C ′ ​ k C^{\prime}k  edges. 
 
 
 
 The application of the Lovász–Simonovits stability can be replaced here by an easy application
of the graph removal lemma  [ 11 ] 
and the Erdős–Simonovits stability  [ 56 ] .
The former result was initially proved using the Szemerédi Regularity Lemma, i.e., the graph regularity method.
For completeness, we present the following supersaturation-stability theorem.
In addition, we refer the readers to  [ 1 ,  12 ,  21 ] 
for some similar applications
on extremal set theory and Ramsey theory. 
 
 
 Theorem 4.3 . 
 
 For any  ε > 0 \varepsilon>0  and  r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 ,
there exist  η > 0 , δ > 0 \eta>0,\delta>0  and  n 0 ∈ ℕ n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}  such that if  G G 
is a graph on  n ≥ n 0 n\geq n_{0}  vertices with at most
 η ​ n r + 1 \eta n^{r+1}  copies of  K r + 1 K_{r+1}  and 
 

 
 
 e ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ( 1 − 1 r − δ ) ​ n 2 2 , e(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2},

 ε ​ n 2 \varepsilon n^{2}  edges. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The graph removal lemma
allows us to pass to a  K r + 1 K_{r+1} -free subgraph
 G ′ G^{\prime}  of  G G  which still has very many edges. At this point,
we can apply the standard stability theorem
to deduce that  G ′ G^{\prime}  is nearly  r r -partite.
Since we deleted few edges to go from  G G  to  G ′ G^{\prime} ,
we must also have that  G G  is nearly  r r -partite.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Although such an analogue can easily be obtained via the graph removal lemma, this gives
bounds which are far from sufficient for our purposes.
However, this approach would be less elementary and effective.
In next subsection, we shall give a more efficient stability result
so that we can calculate some explicit constants. 
 
 
 
 4.2  A generalized Moon–Moser’s inequality 

 
 First of all,
we shall present a result of Moon and Moser  [ 43 ] ,
which counts the minimum number of triangles in a graph with given order and size.
The alternative proofs can also be found in  [ 5 , p. 297]  and  [ 41 , p. 443] . 
 
 
 Theorem 4.4   (Moon–Moser, 1962) . 
 
 Let  G G  be a graph on  n n  vertices with  m m  edges.
Then 
 

 
 
 t ​ ( G ) ≥ 4 ​ m 3 ​ n ​ ( m − n 2 4 ) , t(G)\geq\frac{4m}{3n}\left(m-\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right), 
 
 
 where the equality holds if and only if  m = e ⁡ ( T n , r ) m=e(T_{n,r})  with  r r  dividing  n n . 
 
 
 
 We illustrate that Moon–Moser’s theorem
implies a supersaturation on triangles for graph with more than
 n 2 / 4 n^{2}/4  edges.
For example, if  G G  has at least  n 2 / 4 + 1 {n^{2}}/{4}+1  edges,
then it contains at least  n / 3 {n}/{3}  triangles.
This result is slightly weaker than
Erdős–Rademacher’s theorem.
Moreover, Moon–Moser’s theorem yields that
if  ε > 0 \varepsilon>0  and  G G  has at least
 n 2 / 4 + ε ​ n 2 {n^{2}}/{4}+\varepsilon n^{2}  edges,
then  G G  contains more than  ε ​ n 3 / 3 {\varepsilon}n^{3}/3  triangles.
In what follows, we shall show a generalization
for graphs with less than  n 2 / 4 n^{2}/4  edges. 
 
 
 We say that a graph  G G  is  t t -far from being bipartite 
if  G ′ G^{\prime}  is not bipartite
for every subgraph  G ′ G^{\prime}  of  G G  with
 e ⁡ ( G ′ ) > e ⁡ ( G ) − t e(G^{\prime})>e(G)-t .
In other words, if  G G  is  t t -far from being bipartite,
then no matter how we delete less than  t t  edges from  G G ,
the resulting graph is not bipartite.
Equivalently, we must remove at least  t t  edges from
 G G  to make it being bipartite.
It is well-known that every graph  G G  contains a bipartite subgraph  H H 
with  e ⁡ ( H ) ≥ e ⁡ ( G ) / 2 e(H)\geq e(G)/2 .
From this observation, we know that if  G G  is said to be  t t -far from being bipartite,
then we always admit the natural condition  t ≤ e ⁡ ( G ) / 2 t\leq e(G)/2 . 
 
 
 Next, we present a counting result,
which comes from the work of Balogh, Bushaw, Collares, Liu, Morris
and Sharifzadeh  [ 3 ]  during the study on the typical structure of graphs with no large cliques.
This result allows us to avoid the use of the triangle removal lemma,
so that we could obtain a better bound on the order of the extremal graphs.
This will be explained in the forthcoming Subsection  4.3 .

 ①Recall that a book of size  t t  consists of  t t  triangles sharing a common edge.
The study of bounding the largest size of a book in a graph was initially investigated by

Here, we show that Theorem  4.5 
can easily confirm the Erdős conjecture.

 7  Concluding remarks 

 
 In this paper,
we have bounded the minimum number of triangular edges
of a graph in terms of the spectral radius,
and we have established a spectral Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau theorem.
The main ideas in our proof
attribute to the supersaturation-stability (Theorem  4.5 ) and
some additional spectral techniques.
Particularly,
we stated in Subsection  4.3  that
Theorem  4.5  can deduce a conjecture
of Erdős involving the booksize of a graph.
It is worth mentioning that an interesting spectral problem
of Zhai and Lin  [ 66 , Problem 1.2]  conjectured that
every  n n -vertex graph  G G  with
 λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2})  has booksize greater than  n / 6 n/6  as well.
To solve this problem,
it is sufficient to show a spectral version of
Theorem  4.5 .
To be formal, we propose the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 7.1 . 
 
 If  G G  is  t t -far from being bipartite, then 
 

 
 
 t ⁡ ( G ) ≥ n 6 ​ ( λ ⁡ ( G ) + t − λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) ) . t(G)\geq\frac{n}{6}\Bigl(\lambda(G)+t-\lambda(T_{n,2})\Bigr). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Remark. 
Conjecture  7.1  is stronger than the aforementioned problem of Zhai and Lin.
Indeed, if  G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with  λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2}) , and
 G G  has exactly  t t  triangles,
then assuming Conjecture  7.1 , we know that
 G G  is not  6 ​ t / n {6t}/{n} -far from being bipartite.
So we can remove less than  6 ​ t / n {6t}/{n}  edges from  G G  to destroy all  t t  triangles.
Thus, one of these edges is contained in more than  n / 6 n/6  triangles, as expected. 
 
 
 A well-known result of Nosal  [ 44 ] 
(see, e.g.,  [ 45 ,  51 ] )
implies that if  G G  is a graph with
 m m  edges and  λ ⁡ ( G ) > m \lambda(G)>\sqrt{m} ,
then it contains a triangle.
In 2023, Ning and Zhai  [ 52 ] 
proved a counting result, which asserts that if
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m \lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{m} ,
then  G G  has at least  ⌊ m − 1 2 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{\sqrt{m}-1}{2}\rfloor  triangles, unless  G G  is a complete bipartite graph
(possibly with some isolated vertices).
Inspired by this result and Theorem  3.7 ,
we propose the following problem. 
 
 
 Conjecture 7.2 . 
 
 If  G G  is a graph with  m m  edges and 
 

 
 
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ m , \lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{m}, 
 
 
 then  G G  has at least  m \sqrt{m}  triangular edges, unless
 G G  is a complete bipartite graph. 
 
 
 
 As mentioned in the introduction,
we shall conclude some spectral extremal problems
concerning the minimum number of edges that occur in cliques or odd cycles.
Motivated by the study on the minimum number of
triangular edges
among graphs with  n n  vertices and  m ≥ n 2 / 4 + q m\geq n^{2}/4+q  edges,
we firstly propose the following conjecture,
which provides a spectral version of
Theorem  1.3 . 
 
 
 Conjecture 7.3 . 
 
 For any graph  G G  on  n n  vertices, there exists
an  n n -vertex graph  H = G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) H=G(a,b,c)  for some integers  a , b , c a,b,c  such that  λ ⁡ ( H ) ≥ λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(H)\geq\lambda(G)  and  | 𝐍𝐓 ⁡ ( H ) | ≥ | 𝐍𝐓 ⁡ ( G ) | |\mathbf{NT}(H)|\geq|\mathbf{NT}(G)| . 
 
 
 
 Recall that  T n , r T_{n,r}  is the  r r -partite Turán graph on  n n  vertices.
The famous Turán theorem  [ 5 , p. 294] 
states that an  n n -vertex graph  G G  with
 e ⁡ ( G ) ≥ e ⁡ ( T n , r ) e(G)\geq e(T_{n,r})  has a copy of  K r + 1 K_{r+1} , unless  G = T n , r G=T_{n,r} . Correspondingly,
Nikiforov  [ 46 ]  showed that if
 λ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ λ ⁡ ( T n , r ) \lambda(G)\geq\lambda(T_{n,r}) , then
 G G  contains a copy of  K r + 1 K_{r+1} , unless  G = T n , r G=T_{n,r} .
So it is natural to consider the following extension
by minimizing the number of edges that occur in  K r + 1 K_{r+1} . 
 
 
 Problem 7.4 . 
 
 Suppose that  r ≥ 3 r\geq 3  and
 G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with
 λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , r ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,r}) .
What is the smallest number of edges of  G G  that
are contained in  K r + 1 K_{r+1} ? 
 
 
 
 Remark. 
Inspired by Conjecture  1.2  and Theorem
 1.3 ,
we believe intuitively that the spectral extremal graphs
in Problem  7.4 
are possibly analogues of graphs of the form  G ⁡ ( a , b , c ) G(a,b,c) , i.e.,
they are perhaps constructed from
a complete  r r -partite graph of order  n n  by
adding an almost complete graph to one of the parts. 
 
 
 Apart from the number of triangular edges,
Erdős, Faudree and Rousseau  [ 19 ] 
also considered the analogous problems
for longer odd cycles in graph of order  n n  with
more than  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor  edges.
They proved that for any  k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , every
graph on  n n  vertices with  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + 1 \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+1 
edges contains at least  11 144 ​ n 2 − O ⁡ ( n ) \frac{11}{144}n^{2}-O(n) 
edges that are contained in an odd cycle  C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} .
It turns out that the case  k ≥ 2 k\geq 2  is quite different from
the triangle case.
Furthermore,
Erdős, Faudree and Rousseau  [ 19 ]  made
a stronger conjecture,
which asserts that every such graphs
contains at least  2 9 ​ n 2 − O ⁡ ( n ) \frac{2}{9}n^{2}-O(n)  edges that occur in  C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} .
We remark that adding an extra edge into the complete balanced bipartite graph is not optimal.
However, Füredi and Maleki  [ 23 ] 
showed that this conjecture is false for  k = 2 k=2 , and they
constructed  n n -vertex graphs with  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + 1 \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+1 
edges and with only  2 + 2 16 ​ n 2 + O ⁡ ( n ) ≈ 0.213 ​ n 2 \frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{16}n^{2}+O(n)\approx 0.213n^{2}  edges in  C 5 C_{5} .
In 2019, Grzesik, Hu and Volec
 [ 25 ]  obtained
asymptotically sharp bounds for the smallest possible
number of edges in  C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} . By using Razborov’s flag algebras method, they proved that if
 G G  is an  n n -vertex graph with  ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + 1 \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor+1  edges,
then it contains at least  2 + 2 16 ​ n 2 − O ⁡ ( n 15 / 8 ) \frac{2+\sqrt{2}}{16}n^{2}-O(n^{15/8}) 
edges that occur in  C 5 C_{5} ,
and for  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , it contains at least  2 9 ​ n 2 − O ⁡ ( n ) \frac{2}{9}n^{2}-O(n) 
edges in  C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} .
Motivated by the aforementioned results,
we may propose the following spectral problem. 
 
 
 Problem 7.5 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a graph of order  n n  with  λ ⁡ ( G ) > λ ⁡ ( T n , 2 ) \lambda(G)>\lambda(T_{n,2}) .
For each  k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , what is the smallest number of edges of  G G  that
occur in  C 2 ​ k + 1 C_{2k+1} ?
