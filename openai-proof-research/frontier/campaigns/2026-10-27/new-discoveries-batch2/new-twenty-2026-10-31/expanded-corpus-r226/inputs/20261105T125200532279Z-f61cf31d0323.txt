 Abstract. 
    
 We continue the study of the  k k -cut complex  Δ k ​ ( G ) \Delta_{k}(G)  of a graph  G G  initiated in the paper of Bayer, Denker, Jelić Milutinović, Rowlands, Sundaram and Xue [Topology of cut complexes of graphs, SIAM J. on Discrete Math. 38(2): 1630–1675 (2024)].
We give explicit formulas for the  f f - and  h h -polynomials of the cut complex  Δ k ​ ( G 1 + G 2 ) \Delta_{k}(G_{1}+G_{2})  of the disjoint union of two graphs  G 1 G_{1}  and  G 2 G_{2} , and for the homology representation of  Δ k ​ ( K m + K n ) \Delta_{k}(K_{m}+K_{n}) .
We also study the cut complex of the squared path and the grid graph. Our techniques include tools from combinatorial topology, discrete Morse theory and equivariant poset topology. 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  graph complex, grid graph, squared path graph, disconnected set, homology representation, homotopy, Morse matching, shellability

 1.  Introduction 

 
 This paper continues the investigations begun in  [ 2 ]  and  [ 3 ] . We study the  k k -cut complex   Δ k ​ ( G ) \Delta_{k}(G)  of a graph  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) . This is the simplicial complex whose facets  σ \sigma  are subsets of the vertex set  V V  of  G G , with the property that the complement  V ∖ σ V\setminus\sigma  is a  k k -subset of  V V  inducing a disconnected subgraph of  G G . Our definition is inspired by a celebrated result of Fröberg  [ 10 ]  for the case  k = 2 k=2 , which characterizes the Stanley-Reisner ideal generated by monomials of degree two in terms of chordal graphs. Consideration of higher degree monomials led us to
the above generalization of the simplicial complex to arbitrary  k k . For more details see  [ 2 ]  and  [ 3 ] . 
 
 
 The behavior of the cut complex in relation to some well-known graph operations was established in  [ 3 ] , and some particular families of graphs were studied. Here we study in more detail the effect of one particular graph operation, namely, the disjoint union  G 1 + G 2 G_{1}+G_{2}  of two graphs  G 1 , G 2 G_{1},G_{2} . We show that in this case the topology of the  k k -cut complex of the disjoint union is determined by the join  G 1 ∗ G 2 G_{1}*G_{2}  and the disjoint union of the complete graphs on the vertex sets of  G 1 G_{1}  and  G 2 G_{2} . This observation then allows us to give precise formulas for the  f f - and  h h -polynomials of  Δ k ​ ( G 1 + G 2 ) \Delta_{k}(G_{1}+G_{2}) .
We also study two additional families of graphs, the squared path and the grid graph. 
 
 
 The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the necessary definitions from  [ 3 ] , background information from topology, and facts about the face lattice of the  k k -cut complex from  [ 3 ] , establishing further results that will be necessary for the study of the grid graph in Section  5 . 
 
 
 In Section 3 we present an in-depth discussion of the effect of the disjoint union  G 1 + G 2 G_{1}+G_{2}  of two graphs  G 1 , G 2 G_{1},G_{2}  on the cut complex  Δ k \Delta_{k} . It had already been shown in  [ 3 ]  that
 Δ k ​ ( G 1 + G 2 ) \Delta_{k}(G_{1}+G_{2})  is shellable if and only if  Δ k ​ ( G i ) \Delta_{k}(G_{i})  is shellable for  i = 1 , 2 i=1,2 . We give simple explicit formulas for the  f f -polynomial and the  h h -polynomial of  Δ k ​ ( G 1 + G 2 ) \Delta_{k}(G_{1}+G_{2})  in terms of the respective polynomials for  Δ k ​ ( G i ) \Delta_{k}(G_{i}) ,  i = 1 , 2 i=1,2 ; see Theorem  3.8  and Corollary  3.14 . The  k k -cut complex  Δ k ​ ( K n 1 + K n 2 ) \Delta_{k}(K_{n_{1}}+K_{n_{2}}) , where  n i n_{i}  is the number of vertices of the graph  G i G_{i}  and  K n i K_{n_{i}}  is the complete graph, plays a crucial role in determining the topology of  Δ k ​ ( G 1 + G 2 ) \Delta_{k}(G_{1}+G_{2}) . 
 
 
 Section 4 studies the  k k -cut complex of the squared path, showing that it is always shellable. We give an enumeratively interesting conjecture about the Betti numbers. 
 
 
 Section  5  examines the grid graph. The homotopy type of the  2 2 -cut complex was determined in  [ 2 ] . Here we show that for  k = 3 , 4 k=3,4 , the  k k -cut complex has the homotopy type of a wedge of spheres in the top dimension, and we determine the Betti numbers. There is a considerable literature on independence complexes of grid graphs, and the topology in general is difficult to determine. The case of the cut complex seems to be similarly challenging. We prove shellability of the 3-cut complex, and determine the homotopy type of the 4-cut complex by a discrete Morse matching. For the 6-cut complex we show that homology is torsion-free and occurs in at most the top two dimensions. 
 
 
 Section  6  studies group actions on the homology of the cut complexes. We present a formula for the representation of the symmetric group on the unique nonvanishing homology of  Δ k ​ ( K n 1 + K n 2 ) \Delta_{k}(K_{n_{1}}+K_{n_{2}}) . As shown in  [ 3 ]  for the edgeless graph and the complete multipartite graph, the Specht modules indexed by hooks play a prominent role in the representation. Also included here are descriptions of the homology representation on the cut complexes of the path and the cycle for their respective symmetry groups. 
 
 
 The paper concludes with ideas for further exploration. 
 
 
 
 2.  Definitions 

 
 
General references for simplicial complexes, shellability and topology are  [ 4 ] ,  [ 11 ] 
and  [ 20 ] , and  [ 21 ]  for graph theory. All graphs in this paper are simple (no loops and no multiple edges). 
 
 
 Definition 2.1 . 
 
 A  simplicial complex   Δ \Delta  on a set  A A  is a collection of subsets of  A A 
 

 
 
 σ ∈ Δ ​  and  ​ τ ⊆ σ ⇒ τ ∈ Δ . \sigma\in\Delta\text{ and }\tau\subseteq\sigma\Rightarrow\tau\in\Delta. 
 
 
 The elements of  Δ \Delta  are called its  faces  or  simplices .
If the collection of subsets is empty, i.e.,  Δ \Delta  has no faces, we call  Δ \Delta  the  void complex . Otherwise  Δ \Delta  always contains the empty set as a face. 
 
 
 The  dimension of a face   σ \sigma ,  dim ( σ ) \dim(\sigma) , is one less than its cardinality; thus the dimension of the empty face is  ( − 1 ) (-1) , and the 0-dimensional faces are the  vertices  of  Δ \Delta . A  d d -face  or  d d -simplex  is a face of dimension  d d . The maximal faces of  Δ \Delta  are called its  facets , and the maximum dimension of a facet is the  dimension   dim ( Δ ) \dim(\Delta)  of the nonvoid simplicial complex  Δ \Delta . We write  Δ = ⟨ ℱ ⟩ \Delta=\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle  for the simplicial complex  Δ \Delta  whose set of facets is  ℱ \mathcal{F} . 
 
 
 In this paper all simplicial complexes will be  finite , that is, the vertex set is finite. 
 
 
 A (nonvoid) simplicial complex is  pure  if all its facets have the same dimension, which is then the dimension of the complex. 
 
 
 The  join  of two simplicial complexes  Δ 1 \Delta_{1}  and  Δ 2 \Delta_{2}  with disjoint vertex sets is the complex 
 Δ 1 ∗ Δ 2 = { σ ∪ τ : σ ∈ Δ 1 , τ ∈ Δ 2 } . \Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}=\{\sigma\cup\tau:\sigma\in\Delta_{1},\tau\in\Delta_{2}\}. 
 Thus the join  Δ 1 ∗ Δ 2 \Delta_{1}*\Delta_{2}  contains  Δ 1 \Delta_{1}  and  Δ 2 \Delta_{2}  as subcomplexes. 
 
 
 The  cone  over  Δ \Delta  and the  suspension  of  Δ \Delta  are the complexes 
 cone ⁡ ( Δ ) = Δ ∗ Γ 1 , susp ⁡ ( Δ ) = Δ ∗ Γ 2 = Δ ∗ { u } ∪ Δ ∗ { v } , \mathrm{cone}(\Delta)=\Delta*\Gamma_{1},\ \mathrm{susp}(\Delta)=\Delta*\Gamma_{2}=\Delta*\{u\}\cup\Delta*\{v\}, 
 where  Γ 1 \Gamma_{1}  is the 0-dimensional simplicial complex with one vertex, and  Γ 2 \Gamma_{2}  is the 0-dimensional complex with two vertices  u , v u,v . 
 
 
 The  j j -skeleton  of  Δ \Delta  is the simplicial complex formed by the faces of  Δ \Delta  of dimension at most  j j . 
 
 
 
 Definition 2.2   ( [ 16 , Chapter III, Section 2] ,  [ 4 , Section 11.2] ) . 
 
 An ordering  F 1 , F 2 , … , F t F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{t}  of the facets of a simplicial complex  Δ \Delta  is a  shelling  if, for every  j j  with  1 < j ≤ t 1<j\leq t ,  ( ⋃ i = 1 j − 1 ⟨ F i ⟩ ) ∩ ⟨ F j ⟩ \left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1}\langle F_{i}\rangle\right)\cap\langle F_{j}\rangle 
is a simplicial complex whose facets all have cardinality  | F j | − 1 |F_{j}|-1 , where  ⟨ F i ⟩ \langle F_{i}\rangle  is the simplex generated by the face  F i F_{i} .
The simplicial complex  Δ \Delta  is  shellable  if it admits a shelling order of its facets. 
 
 
 Equivalently, an ordering  F 1 , F 2 , … , F t F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{t}  of the facets of  Δ \Delta  is a shelling if and only if for all  i , j i,j  such that  1 ≤ i < j ≤ t 1\leq i<j\leq t , there exists  k < j k<j  such that 
 

 
 
 F i ∩ F j ⊂ F k ∩ F j and | F k ∩ F j | = | F j | − 1 . F_{i}\cap F_{j}\subset F_{k}\cap F_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad|F_{k}\cap F_{j}|=|F_{j}|-1. 
 
 
 
 
 Given a shelling order  F 1 , F 2 , … , F t F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{t}  of  Δ \Delta , the  restriction   r ⁡ ( F i ) r(F_{i})  of the facet  F i F_{i}  is defined to be the unique minimal face of  ( ⋃ j = 1 i ⟨ F j ⟩ ) ∖ ( ⋃ j = 1 i − 1 ⟨ F j ⟩ ) \left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}\langle F_{j}\rangle\right)\setminus\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}\langle F_{j}\rangle\right) . Hence the shelling order gives a partition into disjoint Boolean intervals
 Δ = ⊔ j = 1 t [ r ( F j ) , F j ] . \Delta=\sqcup_{j=1}^{t}[r(F_{j}),F_{j}].  The facet  F i F_{i}  is called a  full-restriction facet  if  r ⁡ ( F i ) = F i r(F_{i})=F_{i} . 
 
 
 
 Remark 2.3 . 
 
 By convention, the void complex is shellable. The complex whose only face is the empty set
is vacuously shellable.
The complex with a unique nonempty facet is (also vacuously) shellable, and contractible. 
 
 
 
 See Figures  1 ( b ) and  2 ( b ) for examples of shellable and nonshellable complexes. 
 
 
 In combinatorial topology, shellability is an important tool for determining the homotopy type of simplicial complexes, thanks to the following theorem of Björner. 
 
 
 Theorem 2.4   ( [ 4 , (9.19) and Sec. 11] ) . 
 
 A pure shellable simplicial complex of dimension  d d  has the homotopy type of a wedge of spheres, all of dimension  d d . (We include in this the wedge of no spheres, that is, when the complex is contractible.)
The number of spheres in the wedge is the number of full restriction facets in the shelling. 
 
 
 
 Definition 2.5 . 
 
 Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  be a graph.
If  S S  is a subset of the vertex set  V V , write  G ⁡ [ S ] G[S]  to denote the  induced subgraph  of  G G  whose vertex set is  S S . 
 
 
 
 Definition 2.6 . 
 
 Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  be a graph on  | V | = n |V|=n  vertices, and let  k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . Define the  k k -cut complex  of the graph  G G  to be the  ( n − k − 1 ) (n-k-1) -dimensional simplicial complex

Thus  σ \sigma  is a face of the cut complex  Δ k ​ ( G ) \Delta_{k}(G)  if and only if its complement  V ∖ σ V\setminus\sigma  contains a subset  S S  of size  k k  such that the induced subgraph  G ⁡ [ S ] G[S]  is disconnected.  Recall that the Betti numbers of a topological space  X X  are the ranks of the homology groups of  X X . In this paper we always consider reduced homology  H ~ ​ ( X ) \tilde{H}(X) , with coefficients in the rationals.

 2.1.  Some background from combinatorial topology 

 
 
 
 
 The following propositions are useful in analyzing cut complexes. 
 
 
 Proposition 2.10   ( [ 19 , Chapter 21, (21.3)] ,  [ 2 , Proposition 2.7] ) . 
 
 Let  X X  be a topological space with subspaces  A , B ⊆ X A,B\subseteq X  such that  X = A ∪ B X=A\cup B ,  A ∩ B ≠ ∅ A\cap B\neq\emptyset , and  A , B A,B  are both closed subspaces or both open subspaces. Then the quotient map  A / ( A ∩ B ) → X / B A/(A\cap B)\rightarrow X/B  of the inclusion  A ↪ X A\hookrightarrow X  is a homeomorphism. 
 
 
 
 Proposition 2.11   ( [ 11 , Proposition 0.17, Example 0.14] ) . 
 
 Let  ( X , A ) (X,A)  be a CW pair consisting of a CW complex  X X  and a subcomplex  A A . 
 
 (1)  
 
 If the subcomplex  A A  is contractible, then the quotient map  X → X / A X\rightarrow X/A  is a homotopy equivalence. 
 
 (2)  
 
 If  A A  is contractible in the complex  X X , then there is a homotopy equivalence 
 

 
 
 X / A ≃ X ∨ susp ⁡ ( A ) . X/A\simeq X\vee\susp(A). 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proposition 2.12 . 
 
 Let  X X  be a simplicial complex such that its 2-skeleton is complete. Then  X X  is simply connected. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The proof follows directly from Proposition 4.12 in  [ 11 ] . This proposition claims that for arbitrary  n n , the skeleton  X n X^{n}  and the entire complex  X X  have isomorphic homotopy groups  π i \pi_{i}  for all  i < n i<n . Therefore  π 1 ​ ( X ) ≅ π 1 ​ ( X 2 ) \pi_{1}(X)\cong\pi_{1}(X^{2}) . Further, since  X 2 X^{2}  contains all triangles on the vertex set  V ⁡ ( X ) V(X)  of  X X , we know that  X 2 X^{2}  is the same as  Y 2 Y^{2} , where  Y Y  is the full simplex on  V ⁡ ( X ) V(X)  vertices. We apply Proposition 4.12 from  [ 11 ]  to  Y Y  and we obtain: 
 

 
 
 π 1 ​ ( X 2 ) ≅ π 1 ​ ( Y 2 ) ≅ π 1 ​ ( Y ) ≅ 0 . \pi_{1}(X^{2})\cong\pi_{1}(Y^{2})\cong\pi_{1}(Y)\cong 0. 
 
 
 The triviality of the last group follows from the fact that every simplex is contractible, hence it has all trivial homotopy groups.
The connectivity of  X X  follows from the connectivity of  X 2 X^{2} , so we conclude that the complex  X X  is simply connected.
∎ 
 
 
 
 The converse of  Proposition   2.12  does not hold, as shown by the example in Figure  1 . Figure  3  gives a simple example of a 2-cut complex that does not contain a complete 2-skeleton, and is not simply connected. 
 
 
 As in  [ 3 ] , it will often be helpful to examine the face lattice of a simplicial complex. We record some facts from poset topology below. For more details see  [ 7 ] ,  [ 17 ] . 
 
 
 A poset  Q Q  is bounded if it has a unique minimal element  0 ^ \hat{0}  and a unique maximal element  1 ^ \hat{1} . Its proper part is the poset  Q ¯ = Q ∖ { 0 ^ , 1 ^ } \bar{Q}=Q\setminus\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\} . The face lattice  ℒ ⁡ ( Δ ) \mathcal{L}(\Delta)  of a simplicial complex  Δ \Delta  is defined to be the poset of faces ordered by inclusion, with the empty face as the minimal element, and an artificially appended maximal element.
The order complex of  Q Q  is defined to be the simplicial complex  Δ ⁡ ( Q ¯ ) \Delta(\bar{Q})  of chains in the proper part  Q ¯ \bar{Q}  of  Q Q .
The order complex of  ℒ ⁡ ( Δ ) \mathcal{L}(\Delta)  is the barycentric subdivision of  Δ \Delta  and hence is homeomorphic to  Δ \Delta , and therefore has the same homotopy type.
Recall (see  [ 17 ] ) that the reduced Euler characteristic of a simplicial complex is precisely the Möbius number of its face lattice. Thus, when the simplicial complex  Δ \Delta  is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of equidimensional spheres, the Möbius number of the face lattice coincides, up to sign, with the number of spheres in the wedge. In addition, when the complex  Δ \Delta  is not contractible, this number is also the unique nonzero Betti number.

 Our cut complexes  Δ k ​ ( G ) \Delta_{k}(G)  often have interesting symmetries arising from the automorphism group  Aut ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{Aut}(G)  of the graph  G G . These symmetries can be studied by examining the induced action on the homology. In many cases the automorphism group  Aut ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{Aut}(G)  acts simplicially on the cut complex  Δ k ​ ( G ) \Delta_{k}(G) , and hence on the rational homology, when the homotopy type is a wedge of spheres. It is well known that the face lattice  ℒ ⁡ ( Δ ) \mathcal{L}(\Delta)  of the simplicial complex  Δ \Delta  captures information about the homotopy type

 7.  Conclusion 

 
 In this paper we described the  f f - and  h h -polynomials for the cut complexes of the disjoint union of graphs in terms of the polynomials for the components. We showed that cut complexes of squared paths are shellable, and hence homotopy equivalent to a wedge of equidimensional spheres. We studied the cut complexes of grid graphs, showing that for  k = 3 , 4 k=3,4  the homotopy type is a wedge of spheres, in fact even shellable for  k = 3 k=3 , and computing the Betti numbers, as well as giving partial information for  k = 6 k=6 . Finally we completely determined the group representation on the rational homology of the cut complex for the disjoint union of complete graphs, and for paths and cycles first considered in  [ 3 ] . 
 
 
 In  Conjecture   4.10  we propose an enumeratively intriguing recurrence for the Betti numbers of the (shellable)  k k -cut complexes of squared paths for  4 ≤ k ≤ n − 4 4\leq k\leq n-4 .
Another open problem, see  Conjecture   5.9 , is whether the  k k -cut complex of the grid graph  G ⁡ ( m , n ) G(m,n)  is shellable for all  3 ≤ k ≤ m ​ n − 3 3\leq k\leq mn-3 . We would also like to have more information on the Betti numbers of cut complexes for grid graphs.
