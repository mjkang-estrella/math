 Abstract. 
    
 We prove three results about semialgebraic hypergraphs. First, we prove an optimal and oblivious regularity lemma. Fox–Pach–Suk proved that the class of  k k -uniform semialgebraic hypergraphs satisfies a very strong regularity lemma where the vertex set can be partitioned into  poly ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) \mathrm{poly}(1/\varepsilon)  pieces so that all but an  ε \varepsilon -fraction of  k k -tuples of parts are homogeneous (either complete or empty). Our result improves the number of parts in the partition to  O d , k ​ ( ( D / ε ) d ) O_{d,k}((D/\varepsilon)^{d})  where  d d  is the dimension of the ambient space and  D D  is a measure of the complexity of the hypergraph; additionally, the partition is oblivious to the edge set of the hypergraph. We give examples that show that the dependence on both  ε \varepsilon  and  D D  is optimal. 
    
 From this regularity lemma we deduce the best-known Turán-type result for semialgebraic hypergraphs. We show that any such hypergraph with  N N  vertices and  ε ​ N k \varepsilon N^{k}  edges contains a complete  k k -uniform  k k -partite subhypergraph with  Ω d , k ​ ( D − d ⁡ ( k − 1 ) ​ ε d ⁡ ( k − 1 ) + 1 ​ N k ) \Omega_{d,k}(D^{-d(k-1)}\varepsilon^{d(k-1)+1}N^{k})  edges. 
    
 Third, we prove a Zarankiewicz-type result for semialgebraic hypergraphs. Previously, Fox–Pach–Sheffer–Suk–Zahl showed that a  K u , u K_{u,u} -free semialgebraic graph on  N N  vertices has at most  O d , D , u ​ ( N 2 ​ d / ( d + 1 ) + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) O_{d,D,u}(N^{2d/(d+1)+o(1)})  edges and Do extended this result to  K u , … , u ( k ) K_{u,\ldots,u}^{(k)} -free semialgebraic hypergraphs. We improve upon both of these results by removing the  o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1)  in the exponent and making the dependence on  D D  and  u u  explicit and polynomial. 
    
 All three of these results follow from a novel “multilevel polynomial partitioning scheme” that efficiently partitions a point set  P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}  by low-complexity semialgebraic pieces. We prove this result using the polynomial method over varieties as developed by Walsh which extends the real polynomial partitioning technique of Guth–Katz. 
  
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 Semialgebraic graphs and hypergraphs appear ubiquitously in discrete geometry and beyond. These are graphs whose vertex set is a set of points in  ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}  and whose edges are defined by a finite number of polynomial inequalities. They can represent incidences between points and arbitrary varieties, crossings between curves, and many other configurations arising in discrete geometry. 
 
 
 Definition 1.1 . 
 
 A  semialgebraic hypergraph  in  ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}  is a  k k -uniform  k k -partite hypergraph  H = ( P 1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ P k , E ) H=(P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{k},E)  where  P 1 , … , P k P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}  are each finite subsets of  ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}  such that the following holds. There are polynomials  g 1 , … , g t ∈ ℝ ⁡ [ x 11 , … , x k ​ d ] g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{11},\ldots,x_{kd}]  and a function  Φ : { − 1 , 0 , 1 } t → { 0 , 1 } \Phi\colon\{-1,0,1\}^{t}\to\{0,1\}  so that  ( ( x 11 , … , x 1 ​ d ) , … , ( x k ​ 1 , … , x k ​ d ) ) ∈ P 1 × ⋯ × P k ((x_{11},\ldots,x_{1d}),\ldots,(x_{k1},\ldots,x_{kd}))\in P_{1}\times\cdots\times P_{k}  is an edge if and only if 
 

 
 
 Φ ⁡ ( sgn ⁡ ( g 1 ​ ( x 11 , … , x k ​ d ) ) , … , sgn ⁡ ( g t ​ ( x 11 , … , x k ​ d ) ) ) = 1 . \Phi(\sgn(g_{1}(x_{11},\ldots,x_{kd})),\ldots,\sgn(g_{t}(x_{11},\ldots,x_{kd})))=1. 
 
 
 We call  ∑ r = 1 t deg ⁡ g r \sum_{r=1}^{t}\deg g_{r}  the  total degree  of  H H . 
 
 
 
 Remark . 
 
 Previous works use slightly different definitions of the complexity of a hypergraph. For example,  [ 5 ]  gives a semialgebraic hypergraph a complexity  ( t , D ) (t,D)  where  t t  is the number of polynomials used and  D D  is an upper bound on the degree of each polynomial as a function of  x i ​ 1 , … , x i ​ d x_{i1},\ldots,x_{id}  for each  i ∈ [ k ] i\in[k] . A semialgebraic hypergraph of complexity  ( t , D ) (t,D)  thus has total degree at most  t ​ k ​ D tkD . 
 
 
 
 In this paper, we prove three main results about semialgebraic hypergraphs. The first concerns the regularity lemma for semialgebraic hypergraphs. 
 
 
 Definition 1.2 . 
 
 For a  k k -partite  k k -uniform hypergraph  H = ( P 1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ P k , E ) H=(P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{k},E)  we say that partitions  Π 1 , … , Π k \Pi_{1},\ldots,\Pi_{k}  of  P 1 , … , P k P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}  respectively form a  homogeneous partition  with error  ε \varepsilon  if 
 

 
 
 ∑ | π 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ π k | | P 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ P k | ≤ ε \sum\frac{|\pi_{1}|\cdots|\pi_{k}|}{|P_{1}|\cdots|P_{k}|}\leq\varepsilon

 Fox, Pach, and Suk  [ 5 ]  proved that every semialgebraic hypergraph has a homogeneous partition with  O ⁡ ( ε − C ) O(\varepsilon^{-C})  parts where  C C  only depends on the uniformity, dimension of the ambient space, and complexity of the hypergraph. Our main theorem gives the optimal dependence of the number of parts on the error  ε \varepsilon  and the total degree  D D . Furthermore, the partition produced will be essentially oblivious to the hypergraph itself, only depending on the vertex sets as well as  ε \varepsilon  and  D D .

In other words, there exist semialgebraic hypergraphs for which any homogeneous partition with error  ε \varepsilon  must have  Ω d , k ​ ( ( D / ε ) d ) \Omega_{d,k}((D/\varepsilon)^{d})  parts.  1.1.  Proof ideas and comparison to previous work 

 
 Our results are proved using what we call a “multilevel polynomial partitioning scheme”. We construct a partition of a point set  P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}  as follows. Using the real polynomial partitioning technique first developed by Guth and Katz  [ 6 ] , we find a low degree polynomial  f f  that cuts  ℝ d ∖ Z ⁡ ( f ) \mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z(f)  into a number of open cells each with few points from  P P . For each open cell  C C  (i.e., connected component of  ℝ d ∖ Z ⁡ ( f ) \mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus Z(f) ) we add  P ∩ C P\cap C  as a part of our partition. It then remains to partition  P ∩ Z ⁡ ( f ) P\cap Z(f) . To handle these points we use polynomial partitioning again inside the variety  Z ⁡ ( f ) Z(f) . We iterate this a total of  d d  times, reducing the dimension of the variety by one each iteration. To produce an efficient quantitative dependence, it is important that we use the method developed by Walsh  [ 10 ]  for real polynomial partitioning inside a given variety. Our multilevel polynomial partitioning scheme is qualitatively similar to previous work of Matoušek and Patáková  [ 7 ]  though our work has significantly stronger quantitative dependence. 
 
 
 To prove the semialgebraic regularity lemma ( Theorem   1.3 ) we apply this partitioning technique to the vertex sets  P 1 , … , P k P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}  with a parameter  A A  chosen in terms of  d , k , ε , D d,k,\varepsilon,D . It then remains to prove that this gives a homogeneous partition of any semialgebraic hypergraph  H H  on vertex set  P 1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ P k P_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup P_{k}  with total degree at most  D D . To do this we use an argument that is inspired by recent work of Bukh and Vasileuski  [ 2 ]  on the same-type lemma. We view our  k k  partitions of  ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d}  as giving a single partition of  ℝ d ​ k \mathbb{R}^{dk} . Then a  k k -tuple of cells is non-homogeneous only if it is properly crossed by one of the defining polynomials  g 1 , … , g t g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}  of the hypergraph  H H . We bound the number of cells crossed by the zero set of a polynomial using a Milnor–Thom-type result proved by Barone and Basu  [ 1 ] . 
 
 
 While this paper was in preparation, Rubin  [ 8 ]  independently proved a version of  Theorem   1.3  where the number of parts is bounded by  O d , k , D ​ ( ( 1 / ε ) d + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) O_{d,k,D}((1/\varepsilon)^{d+o(1)}) . His regularity lemma is also oblivious to the edge set of the hypergraph and has the additional advantage that the partition can be computed by an algorithm that runs in near linear time. However, his result has the disadvantage that the dependence of the number of parts on  D D  is not stated explicitly and is likely quite large, and the bound loses a factor of  ε − o ⁡ ( 1 ) \varepsilon^{-o(1)} . Rubin also constructs his partitions via iterated applications of polynomial partitioning though the technical details differ quite a lot from this paper. In addition, he proves the correctness of this partition using an analysis in  ℝ d ​ k \mathbb{R}^{dk}  that also is inspired by the recent work of Bukh and Vasileuski  [ 2 ] . 
 
 
 Our Turán result ( Corollary   1.5 ) is a straightforward corollary of the semialgebraic regularity lemma. To prove our Zarankiewicz result ( Theorem   1.6 ) we combine the Turán result with a second application of the multilevel polynomial partitioning scheme.

 3.  Constructing the partition 

 
 In this section, we explain how to construct the multilevel polynomial partitioning scheme in  Theorem   2.1 . The key tool is a quantitatively efficient real polynomial partitioning result over irreducible varieties introduced by Walsh  [ 10 ] . This allows us to afford to do real polynomial partitioning with polynomials of super-constant degree and lets us avoid the  o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) -loss in the exponent. Other discrete geometry results have also been established by similar techniques in a later paper of Walsh  [ 11 ] . The argument there is considerably more involved due to numerous technical difficulties in that setting. 
 
 
 To introduce Walsh’s real polynomial partitioning result, we need to define a more refined notion of degree that gives more precise quantitative control over a variety. This will be used extensively throughout our proof. 
 
 
 Definition 3.1   (Partial degrees) .

 5.  The Zarankiewicz problem 

 
 We prove the graph case ( k = 2 k=2 ) of  Theorem   1.6  first. The proof is essentially a specialization of the general proof, but the calculations are significantly simpler so we think it is valuable to read this case first. 
 
 
 The graph case is proved in two steps. First we deduce a weak Zarankiewicz result from our Turán result ( Corollary   1.5 ). Then we upgrade the weak Zarankiewicz result to the strong one via our multilevel polynomial partitioning scheme ( Theorem   2.1 ). These are the same two steps used by Fox, Pach, Sheffer, Suk, and Zahl in the proof of their semialgebraic Zarankiewicz result. They prove a similar weak Zarankiewicz result  [ 4 , Theorem 2.1]  using VC dimension techniques and then upgrade it using constant-degree polynomial partitioning. Note in contrast, both halves of our proof use only polynomial partitioning and we avoid losing  N o ⁡ ( 1 ) N^{o(1)}  factors by using polynomial partitioning using polynomials of fairly high degree.
