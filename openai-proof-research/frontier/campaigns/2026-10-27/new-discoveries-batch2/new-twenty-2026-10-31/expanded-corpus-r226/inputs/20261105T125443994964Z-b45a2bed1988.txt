 Abstract. 
    
 An odd  k k -edge-coloring of a graph  G G  is a (not necessarily proper) edge-coloring with at most  k k  colors such that each non-empty color class induces a graph in which every vertex is of odd degree; similarly, if more than one color per edge is allowed, we speak of an odd  k k -edge-covering of  G G .
In this paper, we fully resolve two major
conjectures on odd edge-colorings and odd edge-coverings of graphs,
proposed by Petruševski and Škrekovski ( European Journal of Combinatorics,  91:103225, 2021).
The first conjecture states that, apart from two particular exceptions which are respectively odd  5 5 - and odd- 6 6 -edge-colorable, for any other loopless and connected
graph  G G  there exists an edge  e e  such that
 G \ { e } G\backslash\{e\}  is odd  3 3 -edge-colorable.
The second conjecture
states that any simple graph  G G  admits an odd  3 3 -edge-covering in which
at most one edge receives more than one color. In addition, we strongly confirm the second conjecture by demonstrating that there exists an odd  3 3 -edge-covering in which at most one edge receives two colors and the rest of the edges receive unique colors. 
  
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 In this paper, we consider only finite undirected loopless graphs, which may contain multiple edges unless specified otherwise as simple graphs. Throughout this paper, we employ the graph notation from  [ 3 ] . 
 
 
 For a graph  G G , an  edge-coloring  of  G G  is an assignment of colors from the set  [ k ] = { 1 , 2 , … , k } [k]=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}  to the edges of  G G ,
which provides an edge decomposition of  G G  into  k k  color classes.
Similarly, an  edge-covering  of  G G  is an assignment of colors from  [ k ] [k]  to the edges of  G G , allowing for multiple colors per edge.
Equivalently, we can denote an edge-coloring by a mapping  φ : E ⁡ ( G ) → [ k ] \varphi:E(G)\to[k] . In the case of an edge-covering, we denote it by a set-valued mapping  ψ : E ⁡ ( G ) → 2 [ k ] \ ∅ \psi:E(G)\to 2^{[k]}\backslash\emptyset .
If  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) ,  E G ​ ( v ) E_{G}(v)  is the set of edges incident with  v v . Each  v v  with an even (resp. odd) degree  d G ​ ( v ) d_{G}(v)  is an  even  (resp.  odd )  vertex  of  G G . A graph is  even  (resp.  odd )
if all its vertices are even (resp. odd).
We say  G G  is  odd  k k -edge-colorable 
(respectively,  odd  k k -edge-coverable ) if there is an edge-coloring (respectively, an edge-covering)
of  G G  by at most  k k  odd subgraphs, which we call an odd  k k -edge-coloring (respectively, an odd  k k -edge-covering).
In other words, the coloring map or the covering map
 φ \varphi  is required to satisfy that
any non-empty edge set
 E j := { e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) : e ∈ φ − 1 ​ ( j ) } E_{j}:=\{e\in E(G):e\in\varphi^{-1}(j)\} ,  j = 1 , … , k j=1,\ldots,k , forms an odd graph. We also define  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  (respectively,  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G) ) to be the least integer  k 0 k_{0}  such that  G G  is odd
 k 0 k_{0} -edge-colorable (respectively, odd  k 0 k_{0} -edge-coverable).
Equivalently,  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  (respectively,  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G) ) is the least integer
 k 0 k_{0}  such that the edge set of  G G  admits a decomposition (respectively, cover) comprised of  k 0 k_{0}  odd subgraphs.
An obvious necessary and sufficient condition for odd edge-colorability or odd edge-coverability of  G G  is the absence of vertices incident only to loops. Apart from this, the presence of loops
does not influence the existence nor changes the values of the parameters  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  and  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G) . Therefore, while studying odd edge-colorability and odd edge-coverability, we may without loss of generality restrict ourselves to loopless connected graphs. 
 
 
 The topics of determining  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  and  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)  for a graph  G G 
have been very active in recent years, and we refer to the recent survey paper  [ 6 ]  for the history, references, and discussions on many related problems, ranging from theoretical problems on graphs and multigraphs to problems involving algorithms. 
 
 
 As an initiating result in this area, Pyber  [ 8 ]  proved the following theorem. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1 . 
 
 ( [ 8 ] ) For any
simple graph  G G ,  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 4 .
If  G G  is a connected graph (not necessarily a simple graph) and it has an even order,
then  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3 . 
 
 
 
 It was also observed in  [ 8 ]  that the upper bound of  4 4  in the previous result can be achieved by
the wheel graph  W 4 W_{4} . Lužar, Petruševski, and Škrekovski  [ 1 ]  showed that connected loopless (multi)graphs are odd 5-edge-colorable, with one particular exception that is odd 6-edge-colorable. Before proceeding, we describe the exception(s). Given a loopless graph  G G , a  bouquet   ℬ \mathcal{B}  is the set of parallel edges on a pair of adjacent vertices. In particular, for adjacent vertices  v , w ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v,w\in V(G) , the  v ​ w vw -bouquet   ℬ v ​ w \mathcal{B}_{vw}  is the intersection  E G ​ ( v ) ∩ E G ​ ( w ) E_{G}(v)\cap E_{G}(w) . A  Shannon triangle  is a loopless graph on three pairwise adjacent vertices. If  p , q , r p,q,r  are parities of the sizes of its bouquets in non-increasing order, with  2 2  (resp.  1 1 ) denoting an even-sized (resp. odd-sized) bouquet, then  S S  is a Shannon triangle of  type  ( p , q , r ) (p,q,r) , denoted  S p , q , r S_{p,q,r} . Examples can be found in Figure  1 . 
 
 
 

 
 
 
 
 
 Figure 1.  Examples of Shannon triangles (the smallest one of each type). 
 
 
 Noting that the edge set of every odd subgraph of a Shannon triangle is fully contained in a single bouquet, it can be verified that
 χ odd ′ ​ ( S p , q , r ) = p + q + r = cov odd ​ ( S p , q , r ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{p,q,r})=p+q+r=\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(S_{p,q,r}) . In particular,  χ odd ′ ​ ( S 2 , 2 , 2 ) = 6 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{2,2,2})=6  and  χ odd ′ ​ ( S 2 , 2 , 1 ) = 5 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{2,2,1})=5 . In 2017, Petruševski  [ 4 ]  proved that the above two families of graphs are the only exceptions -
all other connected loopless graphs are odd  4 4 -edge-colorable. Furthermore, Petruševski  [ 4 ]  also proved that when  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4 , a color class can be reduced to a size of at most 2. In the end of the paper, the author raised a natural question regarding the existence of an edge-analogue of the second part of Theorem   1.1 . That is, for a connected graph that
is odd  4 4 -edge-chromatic, is it always possible to delete a particular edge (instead of a vertex), such that the resulting graph is odd  3 3 -edge-colorable?
Additionally, the following conjecture was formally raised later in  [ 6 ] . 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.2 . 
 
 ( Conjecture 2.14 of  [ 6 ] )
For any connected loopless graph which is not a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2)  or type  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1) ,
either  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3 
or there exists a special edge  e e  whose removal will make the graph odd  3 3 -edge-colorable. 
 
 
 
 A considerable effort towards the above conjecture was already made in  [ 4 ] . Note that an equivalent way to express the conjecture is that, assuming  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4 , there exists an odd
 4 4 -edge-coloring in which the color  4 4  is used only once.
Another related result, presented in  [ 7 ] , provided a partial answer by
confirming the conclusion
for the special class of subdivisions of odd graphs.
Now we are ready to state the first result of this paper, which fully resolves the above conjecture positively. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3 . 
 
 Any connected loopless graph  G G  that is not a Shannon triangle of type  ( 2 , 2 , 2 ) (2,2,2)  or  ( 2 , 2 , 1 ) (2,2,1)  is either odd  3 3 -edge-colorable or admits an odd  4 4 -edge-coloring with color set  { 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 } \{1,2,3,4\}  such that the color  4 4  is used only on some edge  e e  whose endvertices are incident with a common color  c ∈ { 1 , 2 , 3 } c\in\{1,2,3\} . Clearly, in the latter case,
 χ odd ′ ​ ( G \ { e } ) = 3 \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G\backslash\{e\})=3 . 
 
 
 
 Now we move to graph coverability. Pyber also inquired in  [ 8 ] 
whether three colors are sufficient to give an odd edge-covering for any simple graph  G G , a question later confirmed by Mátrai  [ 2 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.4 . 
 
 ( [ 2 ] )
For any connected simple graph  G G ,  cov odd ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3 . 
 
 
 
 This result was later generalized by Petruševski and Škrekovski  [ 5 ]  to multigraphs, by characterizing which multigraphs are odd  3 3 -edge-coverable. In addition, as a connection between  χ odd ′ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)  and  cov odd ​ ( G ) \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G) , as well as a tool for obtaining more structural information about edge colors and edges, Petruševski and Škrekovski  [ 5 ]  defined the overlapping function. In more detail,
for a  3 3 -edge-covering  ϕ : E ⁡ ( G ) → 2 [ k ] \ ∅ \phi:E(G)\to 2^{[k]}\backslash\emptyset ,
let  ovl ​ ( ϕ ) \text{ovl}(\phi)  be the sum of sizes of all images under  ϕ \phi  that are not singletons. The following are some examples of the values of the function  ovl ​ ( ϕ ) \text{ovl}(\phi) : 
 

 
 
 ovl ​ ( ϕ ) = { 0 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  is an edge-coloring. 2 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  has exactly one edge receiving two colors, with others receiving one color. 3 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  has exactly one edge receiving three colors, with others receiving one color.  4 ,  if  ​ ϕ ​  has exactly two edges receiving two colors each, with others receiving one color. \text{ovl}(\phi)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ is an edge-coloring.}\\
2,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly one edge receiving two colors, with others receiving one color.}\\
3,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly one edge receiving three colors, with others receiving one color. }\\
4,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly two edges receiving two colors each, with others receiving one color.}\end{cases} 
 
 
 
 
 Given a graph  G G  with  cov odd ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3 ,
define 
 

 
 (1.1) 
 
 min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) = min ϕ ⁡ ovl ​ ( ϕ ) , \min\text{ovl}(G)=\min_{\phi}\text{ovl}(\phi), 
 
 
 where the minimum is taken over all odd 3-edge-coverings  ϕ \phi  of  G G . Immediately from the above definitions,
if  G G  is odd 3-edge-colorable, then  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) = 0 \min\text{ovl}(G)=0 .
As the main result in  [ 5 ] , it was proved that except for a list of multigraphs with exactly  3 3  vertices, for any other connected loopless graph  G G , one has  cov odd ​ ( G ) ≤ 3 \text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3  and  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) ∈ { 0 , 2 , 3 , 4 } \min\text{ovl}(G)\in\{0,2,3,4\} . In the same paper, it was also shown that there do exist multigraphs  G G  for which
 min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) \min\text{ovl}(G)  is at least  4 4 . So this leads to the following natural conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.5 . 
 
 ( Conjecture 2.5 of  [ 6 ] )
There is no simple graph  G G  such that  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) = 4 \min\text{ovl}(G)=4 . 
 
 
 
 Our second result of this paper strongly confirms the above conjecture. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.6 . 
 
 For any simple connected graph  G G , we have  min ⁡ ovl ​ ( G ) ≤ 2 \min\text{ovl}(G)\leq 2 . 
 
 
 
 The article is organized as follows. Section 2 is a brief overview of the notation and preliminary results. Subsequently, Section 3 addresses some important special cases in obtaining certain partial results.
We then proceed to prove Theorem  1.3  in Section 4 and Theorem  1.6  in Section 5. In Section 6, we present some concluding remarks. 
 
 
 
 2.  Notation and Preliminary Results 

 
 In this section, we introduce some notation that we will frequently use in this work. We mention only those points of relevance that are liable to variations. The parameters  v ⁡ ( G ) = | V ⁡ ( G ) | v(G)=|V(G)|  and  e ⁡ ( G ) = | E ⁡ ( G ) | e(G)=|E(G)|  are  order  and  size  of  G G , respectively. A vertex  v v  is a  cut vertex  of  G G  if  G − v G-v  has more (connected) components than  G G ; otherwise,  v v  is an  internal vertex  of  G G .
Let  v v  be a cut vertex of  G G , and let  C 1 , … , C k C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}  be the components of  G − v G-v .
Then each  G ⁡ [ V ⁡ ( C i ) ∪ { v } ] G[V(C_{i})\cup\{v\}] , for  i = 1 , … , k i=1,\ldots,k , is called a  v v -lobe  of  G G .
A  block graph  is a connected graph without cut vertices. Given a graph  G G , any maximal block subgraph  B B  is a  block  of  G G . For a block  B B  of  G G , each vertex  v ∈ V ⁡ ( B ) v\in V(B)  that is not a cut vertex of  G G  is an  internal vertex  of  B B  (and of  G G ); the collection of internal vertices of  B B  is denoted by  Int G ​ ( B ) \mathrm{Int}_{G}(B) .
Any block of  G G  that contains at most one cut vertex of  G G  is
an  end-block  of  G G . Every vertex  v v  of a block graph  G G  has a neighbor among the internal vertices of each end-block of  G − v G-v . 
 
 
 Let  T T  be an even-sized subset of  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . A spanning subgraph  H H  of  G G  is a  T T -join  if  d H ​ ( v ) d_{H}(v)  is odd for all  v ∈ T v\in T  and even for all  v ∉ T v\notin T .

 3.  Some Special Cases 

 
 Aiming to prove our two main theorems, in this section, we present some preliminary results dealing with special cases.
Note that some important partial results will already be obtained in this section. For example,
Theorem  1.3  will be proved under the condition that the graph  G G  admits at least one cut vertex.
We divide this section into two subsections for edge-colorings and edge-coverings, respectively.
Certain arguments are based on the various coloring and covering schemes provided in  [ 4 ]  and  [ 5 ] , respectively. 
 
 
 3.1.  Odd-Edge-Colorings 

 
 Let us first discuss graphs all of whose cycles (if any) share a common vertex. We begin with a particular one of multiplicity  2 2  - the so-called ‘double join’ of a path and a vertex  v v . Its special feature is the flexibility regarding odd  2 2 -edge-colorings that are odd away from  v v . This flexibility in regard to the parity of color degrees at the vertex  v v  will be important at some point in the proof of Theorem  1.3 . 
 
 
 Lemma 3.1 . 
 
 Let  H H  be a graph of order  v ⁡ ( H ) ≥ 4 v(H)\geq 4 , and let  v v  be an even vertex such that  N H ​ ( v ) = N H ′′ ​ ( v ) = V ⁡ ( H − v ) N_{H}(v)=N_{H}^{\prime\prime}(v)=V(H-v) . Moreover, let  H − v H-v  be a simple path. Then we can obtain
two distinct  2 2 -edge-colorings  φ 1 , φ 2 : E ⁡ ( H ) → { 1 , 2 } \varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}:E(H)\to\{1,2\}  with the following features. 
 
 (1)  
 
 The coloring  φ 1 \varphi_{1}  is an odd  2 2 -edge-coloring of  H H ; 
 
 (2)  
 
 The coloring  φ 2 \varphi_{2}  is a
 2 2 -edge-coloring of  H H  which is odd away from  v v ,
and both colors  1 1  and  2 2  are even at  v v . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let  { e 1 , e 2 } \{e_{1},e_{2}\} , { e 3 , e 4 } \{e_{3},e_{4}\} , … , ,\ldots, { e 2 ​ k − 1 , e 2 ​ k } \{e_{2k-1},e_{2k}\}  denote the bouquets that are incident to  v v , and let  P k − 1 = H − v P_{k-1}=H-v  be a path whose edge set is denoted by  { f 1 , f 2 , … , f k − 1 } \{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{k-1}\} , such that  e 2 ​ i − 1 , e 2 ​ i , e 2 ​ i + 1 , e 2 ​ i + 2 , f i e_{2i-1},e_{2i},e_{2i+1},e_{2i+2},f_{i}  form a Shannon triangle  S 2 , 2 , 1 S_{2,2,1}  for every  i = 1 , … , k − 1 i=1,\ldots,k-1 . 
 
 
 We define the first coloring  φ 1 \varphi_{1}  as follows.

 6.  Concluding remarks 

 
 For a pair  ( G , e ) (G,e)  consisting of a loopless graph  G G  and an edge  e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G) , let  G + e ′ G+e^{\prime}  denote the spannning supergraph of  G G , obtained by doubling the edge  e e ; in other words, we enlarge  G G  with a new edge  e ′ e^{\prime}  which is parallel to  e e . For example, by doubling any edge of  K 3 K_{3}  it becomes the reduced Shannon triangle  S 2 , 1 , 1 S_{2,1,1} . An equivalent formulation of Theorem  1.3  is the following. 
 
 
 Every loopless connected graph that is not  S 2 , 2 , 2 S_{2,2,2}  or  S 2 , 2 , 1 S_{2,2,1}  is either odd  3 3 -edge-colorable or there exists an edge  e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G)  such that the graph  G + e ′ G+e^{\prime}  admits an odd  3 3 -edge-coloring in which  e e  and  e ′ e^{\prime}  receive the same color. 
 
 
 Similarly, an equivalent formulation of Theorem  1.6  is the following. 
 
 
 Every simple connected graph is either odd  3 3 -edge-colorable, or there exists an edge  e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G)  such that the graph  G + e ′ G+e^{\prime}  admits an odd  3 3 -edge-coloring in which  e e  and  e ′ e^{\prime}  receive different colors. 
 
 
 Consequently, every simple connected graph  G G  with  χ odd ​ ( G ) = 4 \chi_{\text{odd}}(G)=4  contains edges  e e  and  f f  (not necessarily distinct) such that: 
 
 ( 1 ) (1)  
 
 the graph  G + e ′ G+e^{\prime}  admits an odd  3 3 -edge-coloring under which  e e  and  e ′ e^{\prime}  are colored the same; 
 
 ( 2 ) (2)  
 
 the graph  G + f ′ G+f^{\prime}  admits an odd  3 3 -edge-coloring under which  f f  and  f ′ f^{\prime}  are colored differently. 
 
 
 
 
 Figure  5  depicts the wheel  W 4 W_{4} , a simple connected graph with odd chromatic index  4 4 . Any of its four spokes satisfies both  ( 1 ) (1)  and  ( 2 ) (2) . One wonders whether every simple connected graph  G G  with odd chromatic index  4 4  contains an edge  e = f e=f  for which both  ( 1 ) (1)  and  ( 2 ) (2)  hold. We are tempted to end this article with the following.
