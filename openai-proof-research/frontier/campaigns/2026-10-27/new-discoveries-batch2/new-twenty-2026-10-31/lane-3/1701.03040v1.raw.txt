Abstract
Let GG be a finite simple graph. For X⊂V⁡(G)X\subset V(G), the difference of XX, d(X):=|X|−|N⁡(X)|\mathop{\mathrm{d}}(X):=|X|-|N(X)| where
N⁡(X)N(X) is the neighbourhood of XX and max⁡{d(X):X⊂V⁡(G)}\max\,\{\mathop{\mathrm{d}}(X):X\subset V(G)\} is called the
critical difference of GG.
XX is called a critical set if d(X)\mathop{\mathrm{d}}(X) equals the critical difference
and Ker(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(G) is the intersection of all critical sets. It is known that Ker(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(G) is an independent (vertex) set of GG. Diadem(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Diadem}}(G)
is the union of all critical independent sets. An independent set SS is an inclusion minimal set with d(S)>0\mathop{\mathrm{d}}(S)>0 if no proper subset
of SS has positive difference.
A graph GG is called König-Egerváry if the sum of its independence number (α⁡(G)\alpha(G)) and matching number
(μ⁡(G)\mu(G)) equals the order of GG. It is known that bipartite graphs are König-Egerváry.
In this paper, we study independent sets with positive difference for which every proper subset has a smaller difference and prove a
result conjectured by Levit and Mandrescu in 2013. The conjecture states that for any graph, the number of inclusion minimal sets SS with d(S)>0\mathop{\mathrm{d}}(S)>0
is at least the critical difference of the graph. We also give a short proof of the inequality
|Ker(G)|+|Diadem(G)|≤2​α​(G)|\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(G)|+|\mathop{\mathrm{Diadem}}(G)|\leq 2\alpha(G) (proved by Short in 2016).
A characterisation of unicyclic non-König-Egerváry graphs is also presented and a conjecture which states that for
such a graph GG, the critical difference equals α⁡(G)−μ⁡(G)\alpha(G)-\mu(G), is proved.
We also make an observation about Ker(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(G) using Edmonds-Gallai Structure Theorem as a concluding remark.

1 Introduction

A set SS of vertices is independent if no two vertices from SS are

An independent set SS is called inclusion minimal set with d(S)>0\mathop{\mathrm{d}}(S)>0

A matching in a graph GG is a set MM of edges
such that no two edges in MM share a
common vertex.
Size of a largest possible matching (maximum matching) is denoted by μ⁡(G)\mu(G). A vertex is matched

there is a matching from AA into BB if there is a matching MM such that any edge in MM joins a vertex in AA and a vertex in BB
and all the vertices in AA are matched by MM.

3 A Ker-Diadem Inequality
It was conjectured in [3] that the sum of sizes of Ker\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}} and Diadem\mathop{\mathrm{Diadem}} is at most twice
the independence number. Taylor Short proved this inequality in [12]
using structural results by Larson in [4]. Here a short and direct proof for this “Ker\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}-Diadem\mathop{\mathrm{Diadem}} Inequality” is presented.
Lemma 3.1.
If XX and YY are two critical independent sets of a graph GG then |N⁡(X)∩Y|=|N⁡(Y)∩X||N(X)\cap Y|=|N(Y)\cap X|.
Proof.
Let S=N⁡(X)∩YS=N(X)\cap Y. As S⊂Y,N⁡(S)⊂N⁡(Y)S\subset Y,N(S)\subset N(Y), and hence N⁡(S)∩X⊂N⁡(Y)∩XN(S)\cap X\subset N(Y)\cap X. Using Hall’s
Theorem it can be verified that if XX is a critical independent set then there is a matching from
N⁡(X)N(X) into XX (Theorem 2.1).
As S⊂N⁡(X)S\subset N(X), by Hall’s Theorem, |S|≤|N⁡(S)∩X|≤|N⁡(Y)∩X||S|\leq|N(S)\cap X|\leq|N(Y)\cap X|,
i.e., |N⁡(X)∩Y|≤|N⁡(Y)∩X||N(X)\cap Y|\leq|N(Y)\cap X|.
By a similar argument, |N⁡(Y)∩X|≤|N⁡(X)∩Y||N(Y)\cap X|\leq|N(X)\cap Y|.
∎

4 Characterisation of Unicyclic non-KE Graphs
For any graph GG, α⁡(G)+μ⁡(G)≤|V⁡(G)|\alpha(G)+\mu(G)\leq|V(G)| and
it is well known that for bipartite graphs equality holds [1, 13]. It turned out that many interesting
properties can be proved for graphs GG which satisfy α⁡(G)+μ⁡(G)=|V⁡(G)|\alpha(G)+\mu(G)=|V(G)|. This motivated the study of graphs for which this equality holds.
A graph is called König-Egerváry (KE) if α⁡(G)+μ⁡(G)=|V⁡(G)|\alpha(G)+\mu(G)=|V(G)|. KE graphs have been studied extensively [3].
This motivated researchers to consider graphs that are close to KE graphs. One of these classes is unicyclic graphs. For any
unicyclic graph GG, |V⁡(G)|−1≤α⁡(G)+μ⁡(G)≤|V⁡(G)||V(G)|-1\leq\alpha(G)+\mu(G)\leq|V(G)|.
In this section we characterise the unicyclic non-KE graphs and prove some properties. These properties also
lead to a proof of the Conjecture 1.3.

If GG is a disconnected unicyclic non-KE graph, then G=G′⊕FG=G^{\prime}\oplus F,
(⊕\oplus is used to denote disjoint union of graphs), where G′G^{\prime}
is the connected component of GG containing the unique (odd) cycle and FF is a forest.
Observe that G′G^{\prime} is a connected unicyclic non-KE graph.
Conversely, if G′G^{\prime} is any connected unicyclic non-KE graph and FF is an arbitrary forest, then
G=G′⊕FG=G^{\prime}\oplus F is a unicyclic non-KE graph.
By the corollary above, dc⁡(G′)=α⁡(G′)−μ⁡(G′)\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(G^{\prime})=\alpha(G^{\prime})-\mu(G^{\prime}). It is known that for KE graphs
the critical difference equals the difference between independence number and matching number [6].
Hence dc⁡(F)=α⁡(F)−μ⁡(F)\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(F)=\alpha(F)-\mu(F).
It may be verified that dc⁡(G)=dc⁡(G′)+dc⁡(F)\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(G^{\prime})+\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(F), α⁡(G)=α⁡(G′)+α⁡(F)\alpha(G)=\alpha(G^{\prime})+\alpha(F) and
μ⁡(G)=μ⁡(G′)+μ⁡(F)\mu(G)=\mu(G^{\prime})+\mu(F).
Thus dc⁡(G)=α⁡(G′)−μ⁡(G′)+α⁡(F)−μ⁡(F)=α⁡(G)−μ⁡(G)\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(G)=\alpha(G^{\prime})-\mu(G^{\prime})+\alpha(F)-\mu(F)=\alpha(G)-\mu(G).
Hence dc⁡(G)=α⁡(G)−μ⁡(G)\mathop{\mathrm{d_{c}}}(G)=\alpha(G)-\mu(G) holds for any disconnected unicyclic non-KE graph GG too and thus Conjecture 1.3 is proved.
Note that in the Procedure 4.1, if GG doesn’t contain a red vertex (i.e., Step (3) is never applied and hence G=CG=C), applying Step (4)
doesn’t change GG. We call such a Step (4) trivial.
Corollary 4.16.
Critical difference of a connected unicyclic non-KE graph GG is the
number of times a nontrivial Step (4) is applied in the construction of GG by Procedure 4.1.
Proof.
As the red vertices in the construction of a graph GG are in a one-to-one correspondence with the black vertices added by Step (3),
the result follows.
∎
This also shows that for any connected unicyclic non-KE graph GG the number of times each Step (excluding any trivial Step (4)) is chosen in
Procedure 4.1 is fixed even though the order may not be unique.
5 Concluding Observations
Ker\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}, Core\mathop{\mathrm{Core}}, Diadem\mathop{\mathrm{Diadem}} and other related notions have been well studied. Some basic questions still remain. Two of the
least understood problems are:
Problem 5.1.
[3, 9]
Characterise graphs such that Core(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Core}}(G) is critical.
Problem 5.2.
[3, 9]
Characterise graphs with Ker(G)=Core(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(G)=\mathop{\mathrm{Core}}(G).
We tried to use Edmonds-Gallai decomposition to understand Ker(G)\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(G) better. The observations in this Section seem to be insufficient
to address the above problems but may be useful in further work.
For the completeness and to fix the notation Edmonds-Gallai decomposition is stated below.
