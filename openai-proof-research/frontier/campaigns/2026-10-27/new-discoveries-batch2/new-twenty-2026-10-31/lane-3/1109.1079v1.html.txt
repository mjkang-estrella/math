Abstract
To study how balanced or unbalanced a maximal intersecting family ℱ⊆([n]r){\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r} is we consider the ratio ℛ⁡(ℱ)=Δ⁡(ℱ)δ⁡(ℱ){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}})=\frac{\Delta({\mathcal{F}})}{\delta({\mathcal{F}})} of its maximum and minimum degree. We determine the order of magnitude of the function m⁡(n,r)m(n,r), the minimum possible value of ℛ⁡(ℱ){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}), and establish some lower and upper bounds on the function M⁡(n,r)M(n,r), the maximum possible value of ℛ⁡(ℱ){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}). To obtain constructions that show the bounds on m⁡(n,r)m(n,r) we use a theorem of Blokhuis on the minimum size of a non-trivial blocking set in projective planes.
Keywords: Intersecting families, maximum and minimum degree, blocking sets
AMS subject classification: 05D05, 05B25
1 Introduction
A family ℱ{\mathcal{F}} of sets is said to be intersecting if F1∩F2≠∅F_{1}\cap F_{2}\neq\emptyset holds for any F1,F2∈ℱF_{1},F_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}. In their
seminal paper, Erdős, Ko and Rado showed [3] that if ℱ{\mathcal{F}} is an intersecting family of rr-subsets of an nn-element set XX (we denote this by ℱ⊆(Xr){\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\binom{X}{r}), then |ℱ|≤(n−1r−1)|{\mathcal{F}}|\leq\binom{n-1}{r-1} provided that 2​r≤n2r\leq n. Lots of generalizations of the above theorem have been considered ever since and lots of researchers have been interested in describing how intersecting families may look like. One of the quantities concerning intersecting families that has been studied [2, 4] is the unbalance 𝒰⁡(ℱ)=|ℱ|−Δ⁡(ℱ){\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{F}})=|{\mathcal{F}}|-\Delta({\mathcal{F}}) where Δ⁡(ℱ)\Delta({\mathcal{F}}) denotes the maximum degree in ℱ{\mathcal{F}}. In this paper we define another notion to measure the balancedness or unbalancedness of ℱ{\mathcal{F}}: if δ⁡(ℱ)\delta({\mathcal{F}}) denotes the minimum degree in ℱ{\mathcal{F}}, then our aim is to determine how small and how large ℛ⁡(ℱ)=Δ⁡(ℱ)δ⁡(ℱ){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}})=\frac{\Delta({\mathcal{F}})}{\delta({\mathcal{F}})} can be. To avoid δ⁡(ℱ)=0\delta({\mathcal{F}})=0 we will always assume that ∪ℱ=X\cup{\mathcal{F}}=X. Also, as by considering appropriate subfamilies one could modify the value ℛ⁡(ℱ){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}) easily, we will restrict our attention to maximal intersecting families, i.e. families with the property G∈(Xr)∖ℱ⇒∃F∈ℱ​F∩G=∅G\in\binom{X}{r}\setminus{\mathcal{F}}\Rightarrow\exists F\in{\mathcal{F}}\hskip 5.69046ptF\cap G=\emptyset. For sake of simplicity we will also assume that the underlying set XX of our families is [n]={1,2,…,n}[n]=\{1,2,...,n\}.
With the above notation and motivation we define our two main functions as follows:
M⁡(n,r)=max⁡{ℛ⁡(ℱ):ℱ⊆([n]r)​is maximal intersecting with∪ℱ=[n]},M(n,r)=\max\left\{{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}):\hskip 5.69046pt{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}\text{is maximal intersecting with}\ \cup{\mathcal{F}}=[n]\right\},
m⁡(n,r)=min⁡{ℛ⁡(ℱ):ℱ⊆([n]r)​is maximal intersecting with∪ℱ=[n]}.m(n,r)=\min\left\{{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}):\hskip 5.69046pt{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}\text{is maximal intersecting with}\ \cup{\mathcal{F}}=[n]\right\}.
The family giving the extremal size in the theorem of Erdős, Ko and Rado seems to be a natural candidate for
achieving the value of M⁡(n,r)M(n,r). In fact, most families ℱ{\mathcal{F}} that occur in the literature have ℛ⁡(ℱ)=Θ⁡(nr){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}})=\Theta(\frac{n}{r}). In Section 2 we will prove the following theorems showing that both M⁡(n,r)M(n,r) and m⁡(n,r)m(n,r) have different order of magnitude.
Theorem 1.1.
(i) For any values of nn and rr, we have M⁡(n,r)≤n+rrM(n,r)\leq n+r^{r}. In particular, if r<log⁡nlog⁡log⁡nr<\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}, then M⁡(n,r)≤(1−o⁡(1))​nM(n,r)\leq(1-o(1))n holds,
(ii) if 2​r+2<n2r+2<n, then
M⁡(n,r)≥n−2​r+3−n−2​r+2(2​r−3r−2)M(n,r)\geq n-2r+3-\frac{n-2r+2}{\binom{2r-3}{r-2}}
holds, in particular if ω⁡(1)=r<log⁡nlog⁡log⁡n\omega(1)=r<\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}, then
we obtain M⁡(n,r)∼nM(n,r)\sim n.
At first sight, the bound rrr^{r} seems to be very weak, but we will show in Section 3 that it cannot be strengthened too much in general.
A trivial lower bound on m⁡(n,r)m(n,r) is 1. The next theorem states that nr2\frac{n}{r^{2}} is another general lower and we construct families showing that this is the order of magnitude of m⁡(n,r)m(n,r) as long as r=O⁡(n1/2)r=O(n^{1/2}). For larger values of rr we obtain regular maximal families showing the tightness of the trivial lower bound.
Theorem 1.2.
(i) For any nn and rr, the inequality m⁡(n,r)≥nr2m(n,r)\geq\frac{n}{r^{2}} holds,
(ii) if r≤n1/2r\leq n^{1/2}, then m⁡(n,r)=Θ⁡(nr2)m(n,r)=\Theta(\frac{n}{r^{2}}) holds,
(iii) if ω⁡(n1/2)=r=o⁡(n)\omega(n^{1/2})=r=o(n) and r⁡(n)/nr(n)/n is monotone, then there exist infinitely many values n′n^{\prime} and r′=r′​(n′)r^{\prime}=r^{\prime}(n^{\prime}) such that m⁡(n′,r′​(n′))=1m(n^{\prime},r^{\prime}(n^{\prime}))=1 and r∼r′r\sim r^{\prime} holds.

Now we turn our attention to the function m⁡(n,r)m(n,r). In the proof of Theorem 1.2 we will use the following theorem by Blokhuis on blocking sets of projective planes (for a short survey on the topic see [6]).
Theorem 2.4 (Blokhuis, [1]).
Let QQ be a projective plane of order qq and BB be a blocking set (a set that meets all lines of the projective plane) of size less than 32​(q+1)\frac{3}{2}(q+1). If qq is prime, then BB contains a line of the projective plane.
We will also need the following strengthening of Chebyshev’s theorem.
Theorem 2.5 (Nagura, [5]).
For every integer n≥25n\geq 25 there exists a prime pp with n≤p≤(1+1/5)​nn\leq p\leq(1+1/5)n.
Proof of Theorem 1.2.
To prove (i) we make the following two easy observations: for any intersecting family ℱ{\mathcal{F}} we have Δ⁡(ℱ)≥|ℱ|/r\Delta({\mathcal{F}})\geq|{\mathcal{F}}|/r as for any set F∈ℱF\in{\mathcal{F}} the inequality ∑x∈Fd⁡(x)≥|ℱ|\sum_{x\in F}d(x)\geq|{\mathcal{F}}| holds. Also, the average degree in ℱ{\mathcal{F}} equals r​|ℱ|n\frac{r|{\mathcal{F}}|}{n}. As the average degree is at least as large as the minimum degree, we obtain
ℛ⁡(ℱ)=Δ⁡(ℱ)δ⁡(ℱ)≥|ℱ|rr​|ℱ|n=nr2.{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}})=\frac{\Delta({\mathcal{F}})}{\delta({\mathcal{F}})}\geq\frac{\frac{|{\mathcal{F}}|}{r}}{\frac{r|{\mathcal{F}}|}{n}}=\frac{n}{r^{2}}.
Note that the proof does not use the fact that ℱ{\mathcal{F}} is maximal.
To prove (ii) and (iii) we need constructions. Suppose first that r≤n1/2r\leq n^{1/2} holds. By Theorem 2.5 we can pick a prime pp such that 23​r<p<23​(1+15)​r=45​r\frac{2}{3}r<p<\frac{2}{3}(1+\frac{1}{5})r=\frac{4}{5}r. ℱn,r,p={F∈([n]r):l⊂F​for some line​l∈P}.{\mathcal{F}}_{n,r,p}=\left\{F\in\binom{[n]}{r}:l\subset F\hskip 8.5359pt\text{for some line}\ l\in P\right\}.

Fl⊃lF_{l}\supset l such that Fl∩G=∅F_{l}\cap G=\emptyset holds. 3 Concluding remarks
As we mentioned in the Introduction, the bound of Theorem 1.1 (i) cannot be strengthened in general as the following example shows. If n=2​rn=2r, then a maximal intersecting family ℱ{\mathcal{F}} contains one set from every pair of complement sets. Thus the family ℱ∗={F∈([n]r):1∉F,F≠[r+1,n]}∪{[r]}{\mathcal{F}}^{*}=\{F\in\binom{[n]}{r}:1\notin F,F\neq[r+1,n]\}\cup\{[r]\} is maximal intersecting and ℛ⁡(ℱ∗)=Θ⁡((nr))=eΘ⁡(n){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}^{*})=\Theta(\binom{n}{r})=e^{\Theta(n)} holds while rr=eΘ⁡(n​log⁡n)r^{r}=e^{\Theta(n\log n)}.
In Theorem 1.2 (iii), we could show regular maximal intersecting families only for special values of nn and rr. There are two ways to generalize our construction. First, one needs not insist that all projective planes should be of the same order, but for the maximality one still needs that they should be of the same asymptotic order (one will have to choose ss a bit more carefully). This will ruin the regularity, but for families ℱ{\mathcal{F}} obtained this way ℛ⁡(ℱ)=1+o⁡(1){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}})=1+o(1) would still hold. The other possibility is to add extra vertices that do not belong to ∪Pi\cup P_{i}, similarly to the construction used for Theorem 1.2 (iii). This will enable us to obtain constructions for arbitrary values of nn and rr (provided nn is large enough) but for these families ℱ′{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime} we will have ℛ⁡(ℱ′)=Θ⁡(r2n){\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})=\Theta(\frac{r^{2}}{n}).
It remains open whether one can construct maximal intersecting families with ℛ{\mathcal{R}}-value 1+o⁡(1)1+o(1) for any r⁡(n)r(n).
