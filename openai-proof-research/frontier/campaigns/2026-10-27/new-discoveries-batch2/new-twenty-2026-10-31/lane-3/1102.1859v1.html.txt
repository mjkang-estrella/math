Abstract
Let G=(V,E)G=\left(V,E\right). A set S⊆VS\subseteq V is independent if
no two vertices from SS are adjacent, and by Ind⁡(G)\mathrm{Ind}(G) we mean the
set of all independent sets of GG. The number d⁡(X)=d\left(X\right)=
|X|−|N⁡(X)|\left|X\right|-\left|N(X)\right| is the
difference of X⊆VX\subseteq V, and A∈Ind⁡(G)A\in\mathrm{Ind}(G) is
critical if
d⁡(A)=max⁡{d⁡(I):I∈Ind⁡(G)}​ [7].d(A)=\max\{d\left(I\right):I\in\mathrm{Ind}(G)\}\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Zhang}{}{}]}}.
Let us recall the following definitions:
ker(G)=∩{S:S is a critical independent
set} [5],\mathrm{\ker}(G)=\cap\left\{S:S\text{{ is a critical independent
set}}\right\}\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Levman2011a}{}{}]},}
core(G)=∩{S:S is a maximum
independent set} [4].\mathrm{core}\left(G\right)=\cap\left\{S:S\text{{ is a maximum
independent set}}\right\}\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{LevMan2002a}{}{}]}}.
Recently, it was established that ker⁡(G)⊆core⁡(G)\mathrm{\ker}(G)\subseteq\mathrm{core}(G) is true for every graph [5], while the corresponding
equality holds for bipartite graphs [6].
In this paper we present various structural properties of ker⁡(G)\mathrm{\ker}(G).
The main finding claims that
ker(G)=∪{S0:S0 is an inclusion minimal
independent set with d(S0)>0}.\mathrm{\ker}(G)=\cup\left\{S_{0}:S_{0}\text{ is an inclusion minimal
independent set with }d\left(S_{0}\right)>0\right\}.
.
Keywords: independent set, critical set, ker, core, matching
