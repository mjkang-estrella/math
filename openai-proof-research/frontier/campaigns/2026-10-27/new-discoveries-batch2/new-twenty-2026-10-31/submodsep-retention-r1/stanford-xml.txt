     In the field of coding theory, maximum-distance separable (MDS) codes are a class of codes which have optimal guarantees for correcting worst-case Hamming errors. Geometrically, such codes correspond to configurations of vectors in k dimensions such that no k vectors are linearly dependent. By imposing more geometric conditions, one can define a novel family of codes known as higher order MDS codes. These codes unify a number of concepts in coding theory -- such as connecting the recoverability guarantees of a real-world distributed storage architecture with fundamental questions in list-decoding. One application of this theory is that Reed-Solomon codes, as well as many other polynomial codes, generically achieve list-decoding capacity
