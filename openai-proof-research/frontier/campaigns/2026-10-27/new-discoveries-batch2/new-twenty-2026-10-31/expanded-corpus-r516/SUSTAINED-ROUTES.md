## 354942: circular defects and occupation rewards

Actual source: Lewis–Rai, arXiv2206.04614v1, Definitions1.1–1.2,2.2, Theorems2.3,4.2,5.1,6.2 and Conjecture6.1. Two colors, n of each, uniform choice among all 2n insertion positions; deterministic rotation when the end colors differ. Color complementation only folds the presentation. Reaching either alternating linear deck is the stopping event.

Route A: exact embedded-chain reduction. In a nonalternating circular deck the counts of cyclic 00 and 11 edges agree: writing b for the number of circular 01 edges gives n-b of each repeated edge. With the source convention that the first card is 0, the tier is k=n-b. At a random deck, removing the first card removes a repeated edge of its color. Insertion between two cards of the other color destroys one repeated edge of that color, decreasing k by one; every other insertion preserves k. There are exactly k opposing repeated gaps among 2n slots, so the conditional drop probability at EVERY random visit is k/(2n), independent of the current deck. Deterministic rotations preserve k.

Let M_k be the number of random visits spent in tier k after first entering it, counting the successful departure. Conditional on every preceding failure the next success probability remains k/(2n); hence P(M_k>j)=(1-k/(2n))^j, and E M_k=2n/k. No independence between tiers or from gap lengths is asserted. Starting in tier K, the expected number of random steps is 2n H_K. This reproduces source Theorem6.2 and earns no novelty.

If L_{k,j} is the number of deterministic moves after the jth random insertion in tier k and before the next random deck (or absorption), then the total time is initial deterministic prefix plus sum_k M_k plus sum_{k,j} L_{k,j}. This identity makes explicit what is missing. The source worst-case chain length gives L<=2n-2k-1 before a next random deck remaining in tier k, and source Theorem5.1 couples successive runs: their sum is <=2n-2k-1 on a same-tier transition, <=2n-2k+2 on a departure. Pairing runs reduces the factor two but does not supply a 1/k factor. It leaves a harmonic sum of order n^2 log n. Source Section5 already implements that refinement; it is not a discovery route by itself.

A sufficient NEW lemma would be a uniform occupation-reward estimate, for every entry state and 2<=k<=n-1,
 E[sum of deterministic runs charged to tier k] <= 2n^2/(k(k+1)) + O(n/k),
with one absolute implied constant and a consistent convention for the departure run. The tier1 formula/source Cor3.7 bounds its contribution by n^2+O(n). Since sum_{k=2}^infty 1/(k(k+1))=1/2, this lemma would imply 2n^2+O(n log n), satisfying the literal constant C<=2. It is a sufficient lemma, NOT proved. The initial prefix is O(n). Reassigning the departure run must not count it twice.

Route B: uniform-spacing heuristic tested logically. In a circular deck with 2k defects, their spacings total 2n. If the seam were a uniformly chosen defect, average next spacing would be n/k. But the actual seam advances by deterministic rotation, and the moved card is inserted uniformly into slots, not uniformly among defects. Long alternating arcs have more insertion slots and can be size-biased. There is no demonstrated uniform seam distribution, no stationary assumption before killing, and no permissible exchangeability argument turning n/k into the needed n/(k+1). Thus neither uniform slots nor transitive color symmetry pays the occupation lemma. Treating runs as independent geometric gaps would be invalid.

Route C: rotation-invariant energy. For the circular +/-1 walk with zero total increment, an energy such as sum_j(S_j-mean S)^2 is invariant under a complete rotation of the deck (the heights are cyclically shifted and translated). Consequently it has zero drift on EVERY deterministic move; it cannot be a one-step potential paying unit cost there. It could still pay embedded rewards, but an inequality E[E(R)-E(R_next)]>=c L(R) with appropriate bounded range has not been proved. Linear inversion count is also ineffective: rotation changes it by +/-n and two alternating rotations cancel, so it does not pay arbitrary long alternating runs monotonically. This blocks two shortcuts, not the conjecture. Precise PARK: prove a nonstationary occupation estimate or a reward-paying embedded potential; actual final Involve17(2024)603–632 and any correction/citing proof remain unread.

## 330531: direct sums, tensor codes and sharp-threshold obstruction

Actual source: Kumar–Pfister arXiv1505.05123v2 (15 June2015), SectionVIB Conjecture28 and preceding SectionVIA boundary discussion. The fetched PDF is v2, not an assumed original v1 or authenticated final. Conjecture28 matches the literal sequence with fixed limiting rate in (0,1), binary linear codes, transitive full permutation group, and BOTH distances tending to infinity. Target is bit-MAP, not block-MAP or an iterative decoder.

Route A: direct-product attempted counterexample. If C=A direct-sum B on disjoint coordinates, then erasure recovery of a coordinate in A depends only on received A coordinates; B supplies no parity constraints involving that coordinate. Thus the bit EXIT function at that coordinate is exactly its constituent EXIT function. Full coordinate transitivity of C forces all bit EXIT functions equal, including across A/B. The area integral then forces the constituent rates equal (the source explicitly gives this reduction). Therefore joining a rate-r1 capacity family and a rate-r2 capacity family to manufacture two transition locations destroys transitivity unless the EXIT curves coincide; merely giving equal block sizes does not repair this. Taking many identical constituents preserves their bit decoding curve and minimum distances, so it does not create noncapacity from capacity. PARK of this route is structural, not a counterexample.

Route B: genuinely transitive tensor families. Let A<=F2^a and B<=F2^b have dimensions u,v. The tensor code A tensor B has length ab and dimension uv; its rate is (u/a)(v/b). The product of transitive coordinate actions is transitive on the rectangle. Each codeword is a matrix with columns in A and rows in B. Its minimum weight is d(A)d(B): any nonzero column has at least d(A) nonzero rows, and each such row has weight at least d(B); equality is achieved by a pure tensor of minimum words.

There is also an exact dual-distance calculation, not merely an upper bound:
 d((A tensor B)^perp)=min(d(A^perp),d(B^perp))
for nonzero proper constituent codes. Upper bound: put a minimum dual word of A in one column, or one of B in one row. Lower bound: let W be a dual matrix of total weight t smaller than both constituent dual distances. With generator matrices G_A (u by a) and G_B (v by b), duality says G_A W G_B^T=0. Let I and J be the sets of nonzero rows and columns of W. Each has size <=t. Columns of G_A indexed by I are independent, or else their dependence is a nonzero A-dual word of weight <=t. Likewise for G_B,J. Taking left inverses of these two full-column-rank matrices yields W_{I,J}=0, contradiction. This proves the formula by elementary linear algebra in all lengths, no enumeration.

Hence tensoring two positive-rate transitive families with both distances diverging really does retain all distance and symmetry assumptions. But a failure of row-by-row or column-by-column decoding is NOT a bit-MAP failure. A random erased rectangle does not automatically carry a nonzero tensor-codeword, and pure tensor supports have probability p^{d(A)d(B)} tending to zero. A finite local stopping set for an iterative algorithm is not a kernel vector of the entire restricted generator system. No nonvanishing bit ambiguity probability at some fixed p<1-r_A r_B has been proved. This route therefore constructs legitimate test families but no refutation.

Route C: threshold proof. Fix coordinate i. Extrinsic nonrecovery is a monotone event Omega_i on erasures of the other N-1 coordinates. Global transitivity of the code does not make the stabilizer of i transitive on those N-1 coordinates, so the source double-transitivity influence argument cannot simply be reapplied. Diverging primal/dual distances do not imply a large pointwise number of pivotal coordinates: choose a minimum dual word of weight d>=3 containing i, receive exactly d-2 of its other coordinates, and erase all others. Revealing the single missing word coordinate recovers i. Any second pivotal coordinate would give a second weight-d dual relation on the same d-1 received positions with a different final coordinate; adding the relations gives a dual word of weight2, impossible. This is the source SectionVIA obstruction, not a new disproof. Rare boundary configurations need not dominate the biased measure, so this pointwise failure does not rule out an averaged sharp threshold. Unpaid lemma: derive a diverging averaged influence/entropy differential inequality from both distance hypotheses and coordinate transitivity, uniformly at every fixed p in (0,1). Neither distances alone nor the area identity localizes a threshold without such an inequality. Full conjecture PARK.
