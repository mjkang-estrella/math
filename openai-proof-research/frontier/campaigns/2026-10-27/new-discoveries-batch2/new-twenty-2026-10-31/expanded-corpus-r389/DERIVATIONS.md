## 326994: shifted Kinser dual containment

Use Definition3.1 in Cameron1401.0500. Write L_n(r;X) for the sum of ranks on the left and R_n(r;X) for the right; each has 2n−3 terms. First route: substitute r_N*(A)=|A|+r_N(E\A)−r_N(E). The constant rank terms cancel exactly since both sides have 2n−3 terms. Define D_n(f;X)=R_n(f;X)−L_n(f;X). The desired quantity becomes D_n(cardinality;X)+D_n(A↦r_N(E\A);X). This is a precise reformulation, with no assumption that the Xi are disjoint. A tempting cancellation of the cardinality part is invalid merely from balanced variable occurrences: cardinalities of overlapping unions are not sums of the variable cardinalities. Even for disjoint Xi, the remaining ranks involve INTERSECTIONS of complements. The supplied n+1 inequality concerns ranks of UNIONS of subsets, so complementing Xi is not the claimed transfer certificate.

Second route: introduce an extra subset for the common complement/intersection, then telescope submodularity. Source Lemma3.2 genuinely uses intersections in a modular lattice of vector subspaces: W is the intersection of the spans of X3,...,Xn, and the nested chain V2∩V3∩... gives dimension differences. In a general matroid the flats need not be modular; replacing these dimensions by matroid ranks does not preserve the equality used for dimension drops. Representable N has representable N*, so this pays a large subclass already known, but N in K_(n+1) need not be representable. A new proof must control the modular defects using the extra Kinser level, rather than assuming them zero.

A valid counterexample must certify N in K_(n+1) for every tuple, and display one n-tuple with negative dual slack. A single violating tuple in N* is insufficient without the global membership certificate. No enumeration performed. Exact unpaid lemma: an explicit nonnegative combination of (n+1)-Kinser slacks and rank submodularity slacks equal to D_n(r_N*;X), with all constructed subsets and cardinal residuals specified, OR a matroid satisfying the global hypothesis and violating the conclusion. ## 327022: planar subcubic median spectrum

The primary definition is R(G)=max(|lambda_floor((n+1)/2)|,|lambda_ceil((n+1)/2)|). Planarity and maximum degree3 are independent conditions; a Perron bound does not control the median sharply. Source2311.01884 supplies useful actual mechanisms, not the whole target.

Route1, unbalanced cut: choose a cut maximizing crossing edges. Moving a vertex would otherwise improve the cut, so each vertex has at least as many opposite-side neighbors as same-side neighbors. Maximum degree3 implies both induced sides have maximum degree1, hence all their adjacency eigenvalues lie in [−1,1]. If a side A has size strictly greater than n/2, interlacing bounds both middle eigenvalues by the extreme eigenvalues of G[A], thus in [−1,1]. For odd n every partition is unbalanced, so this route covers odd order. For even n it fails exactly when all relevant maximizing cuts are balanced; asserting an unbalanced cut without proof discards the hard case.

Route2, retain balanced cut: write adjacency as B+M, B bipartite across the cut and M the matching inside sides. Operator norm M<=1, so Weyl reduces the desired bounds to a zero central pair for B. If G contains K2,3, its two degree3 twin vertices must lie on the same side of an unfriendly cut: putting them opposite would require at least two common neighbors on each side, impossible with only three. All three common neighbors then lie opposite them. Consequently B has identical rows, is singular and bipartite; its symmetric spectrum forces both central eigenvalues zero (even order). Weyl gives the result. This is the full source Theorem1.2 mechanism; it does not create twins in arbitrary planar graphs.

Route3, separator induction: source Theorem1.3 uses K4-minor-freeness to disconnect a graph after a carefully chosen two-vertex deletion, then gains strict eigenvalue-count deficits on even pieces before interlacing. Planarity alone allows K4 minors, so the disconnection assertion is unavailable. Even a generic separator of size s only gives median-index uncertainty s; induction with non-strict half-order bounds can lose all the needed margin. Exact unpaid lemma: an even-order planar subcubic graph in the balanced-cut/no-K2,3/K4-minor regime admits either a cut giving a singular central bipartite part with norm<=1 remainder, or a separator with enough STRICT positive- and negative-eigenvalue count deficits to survive interlacing.
