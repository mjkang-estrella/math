31:                                           1    Introduction
32:                                           Let G = (V (G), E(G)) be a simple graph with vertex set V (G) and edge set E(G). Denote
33:                                           by |G| the order of graph G and NG (v) the neighborhood of a vertex v of G, and write N (v)
34:                                           when G is clear. For a subset A ⊆ V (G), denote by G[A] the subgraph of G induced by A,
35:                                           and write G − A for G[V (G) − A] sometimes.
36:                                               A graph is called subcubic if its maximum degree is at most 3. In mathematical chemistry,
37:                                           every subcubic graph is regarded as a chemical graph. It is known that the HOMO–LUMO
38:                                           separation, which is the gap between the Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital(HOMO) and
39:                                           Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital (LUMO), is related linearly to median eigenvalues of
40:                                           a graph [6, 7]. Therefore, it is worthwhile to estimate the median eigenvalues. Fowler and
41:                                           Pisanski [6,7] introduced the notion of HL–index of a graph (see also Jaklic̆ et al. [8]). For a
42:                                           simple graph G of order n, let λi (G) be the i-th largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
43:                                           of G (counting multiplicities).The HL-index of G is defined as
44:                                                                        R(G) = max {|λh (G)|, |λl (G)|} ,
45:                                                       n+1         n+1 
46:                                           where h = 2 and l = 2 .

49:                                           proposed the following conjecture.

62: Conjecture 1.1. [11] For every subcubic planar graph G, R(G) ≤ 1.
63:     Mohar [10] confirmed that Conjecture 1.1 holds for bipartite subcubic planar graphs.
64: Later, Mohar [12] proved that R(G) ≤ 1 holds for every√bipartite subcubic graph G except
65: the Heawood graph, whose median eigenvalues are ± 2. In addition, Benediktovich [2]
66: confirmed Conjecture 1.1 for subcubic outerplanar graphs. For more results on median
67: eigenvalues, see [9, 13–15].
68:     A graph H is a minor (or H-minor) of a graph G, if a copy of H can be obtained from
69: G by deleting and/or contracting edges of G. A graph is H-minor-free if H is not a minor
70: of it for every H ∈ H. When H = {H}, we simply write H-minor-free. In this note, we
71: prove the following result.
72: Theorem 1.2. For every subcubic graph G that contains a subgraph K2,3 , we have R(G) ≤
73: 1.
74:     In electronic engineering and computer science, K4 -minor-free graphs, which are also
75: called series–parallel graphs, are of great interest, because these graphs can be applied to
76: model series and parallel electric circuits. More results on series–parallel graphs may be
77: referred to [4, 5] and references therein. Another main result of this note is as follows.
78: Theorem 1.3. For every K4 -minor-free subcubic graph G, we have R(G) ≤ 1.
79: Remark 1. On one hand, Theorem 1.3 extends the result for subcubic outplanar graphs
80: in [2], since G is outerplanar if and only if neither K4 nor K2,3 is a minor of G. On
81: the other hand, Theorem 1.3 confirms that Conjecture 1.1 holds for K4 -minor-free subcubic
82: graphs, as every K4 -minor-free graph is planar, equivalently, {K5 , K3,3 }-minor-free.
83:    The rest of this note is organized as follows. In Section 2, some notations and lemmas
84: are presented. In Section 3, we prove Theorems 1.2 and 1.3.

87: 2     Preliminaries
88: First, we need the following two well-known theorems in spectral graph theory (for example,
89: see [3, pp. 17–20]).
90: Theorem 2.1 (Eigenvalue interlacing theorem). Let G be a simple graph G of order n. Let
91: A ⊆ V (G) be a vertex set of size k. Then for i ∈ {1, 2, · · · , n − k},
92: 
93:                               λi (G) ≥ λi (G − A) ≥ λi+k (G).
94: 
95: Theorem 2.2. Let G be a simple graph of order n. Suppose {E1 , E2 } is a partition of
96: E(G). For i ∈ {1, 2}, let Gi = (V (G), Ei ) be the spanning graph of G. Then
97: 
98:                       λi (G) ≤ λj (G1 ) + λi−j+1 (G2 ) (n ≥ i ≥ j ≥ 1),
99: 
100:                       λi (G) ≥ λj (G1 ) + λi−j+n (G2 ) (1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ n).
101:     We also need a simple fact.
102: Lemma 2.3. Let G be a simple graph with two distinct vertices u and v such that N (u) =
103: N (v). Then zero is an adjacency eigenvalue of G. Furthermore, if G is bipartite, then
104: R(G) = 0.
105: 
106: 
107:                                              2
108: 
109: Proof. The determinant of the adjacency matrix A of G is equal to 0, so zero is an eigenvalue
110: of A. Furthermore, if G is bipartite, then the adjacency eigenvalues of A is symmetric with
111: respect to origin 0; so R(G) = 0.
112:     Let G be a simple graph of order n. A partition {A, B} of vertex set of G is called
113: unfriendly if every vertex in A has at least as many neighbors in B as in A, and every vertex
114: in B has at least as many neighbors in A as in B. A partition {A, B} of vertex set of G
115: is called unbalanced if |A| 6= |B|; balanced, otherwise. We include Lemma 2.4 which was
116: initially presented in [1], and later applied to median eigenvalues by Mohar in [10, 11].
117: Lemma 2.4. [1] Every graph has an unfriendly partition.
118:     By applying Theorem 2.1, Mohar [10, Lemma 2.4] proved the following lemma.
119: Lemma 2.5. [10] If G is a subcubic graph with an unbalanced unfriendly partition, then
120: R(G) ≤ 1.
121:    Finally, we need the following lemma, which was verified by Mohar in the proof of [10,
122: Lemma 2.1].
123: Lemma 2.6. [10] For every subcubic graph G of odd order, R(G) ≤ 1.
