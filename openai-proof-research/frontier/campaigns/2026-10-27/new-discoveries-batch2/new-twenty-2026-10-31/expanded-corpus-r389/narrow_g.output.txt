285: 
286: 
287:    There are two natural “next steps” after finding the condition for colorability of K1,m,n . First one is
288: to find the exact number of colors needed to color K1,m,n and the second is to generalize the statement to
289: colorability of Kk,m,n for k > 1. Past work on those problems led us to the following conjectures.
290: 
291: Conjecture 2. Graph K1,m,n has an interval t-coloring if and only if t = m + n and gcd(m + 1, n + 1) = 1.
292: 
293: Conjecture 3. Graph Kk,m,n , where k ≤ m ≤ n and n > k + m is interval colorable if and only if graph
294: Kk,m,n−k−m is interval colorable.
295:

1241:                                     4. Conclusions
1242:    We have introduced some new methods for proving the log-concavity of a linear
1243: combination of log-concave sequences and of log-concave sequences that have been
1244: transformed by convolutions. We have used these methods to show that linear chains
1245: of graphs that satisfy certain conditions known to be true of many graphs, and which
1246: are possibly true for all graphs, have log-concave genus distributions. This motivates
1247: further study and development of these new methods for application to proving the
1248: log-concavity of the genus distributions of larger classes of graphs.
1249:    We have proved that, given a collection of graphs that are doubly vertex-rooted or
1250: doubly edge-rooted, whose partitioned genus distributions satisfy conditions given in
1251: Corollary 3.3 or Corollary 3.6, respectively, a linear chain formed from those graphs
1252: by iterative amalgamation has a log-concave genus distribution and log-concave partial
1253: genus distributions, as well.
1254:    We offer two restricted forms of the log-concavity conjecture for genus distributions of
1255: graphs. For Conjecture 4.1, the productions in Table 2.1 of [20] lead to an expression for
1256: Γ(X) as a linear combination of double-root partials and some of their offset sequences.
1257: For Conjecture 4.2, Theorems 2.6 and 2.7 of [51] give productions for the two ways to
1258: self-amalgamate a doubly edge-rooted graph.
1259: Conjecture 4.1. Let (H, u, v) be a doubly vertex-rooted graph with 2-valent roots.
1260: Then the genus distribution of the graph X formed by amalgamating the vertex-roots u
1261: and v is log-concave.
1262: Conjecture 4.2. Let (H, e, f ) be a doubly edge-rooted graph with 2-valent roots. Then
1263: the genus distributions of both the graphs that can formed by amalgamating the edge-
1264: roots e and f are log-concave.
1265: 
1266:   To the best of our knowledge, no one has found a graph whose genus distribution or
1267: partial genus distribution has internal zeros. We have not encountered any graph with
1268: a non-log-concave partial genus distribution. Based on these observations, we pose a
1269: third conjecture.
1270: 
1271: Conjecture 4.3. Partial genus distributions for singly or doubly vertex-rooted or edge-
1272: rooted graphs are log-concave and have no internal zeros.
