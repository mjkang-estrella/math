29:                                              From the 1930s, this conjecture has attracted a wide interest among geometers.
30:                                           The frequent attempts to prove it led to results in a number of special cases: for
31:                                           sets in R2 and R3 , for smooth bodies or sets with certain symmetries, etc., but the
32:                                           conjecture in general remained open till 1993, when it was disproved by Kahn and
33:                                           Kalai [17]. Their result did not mean that research on this problem stopped: the
34:                                           investigation of the so-called Borsuk number of a bounded set; that is, the minimum
35:                                           number of pieces of smaller diameters that it can be partitioned into, is still one of
36:                                           the fundamental problems of discrete geometry.
37:                                              Since 1933, a large number of generalizations of Borsuk’s problem has been
38:                                           introduced. Without completeness, we list only a few. The generalized Borsuk
39:                                           problem asks to find, for a fixed value of 0 < r < 1, the minimum number m such
40:                                           that any set of diameter one in Rn can be partitioned into m pieces of diameters at
41:                                           most r (cf., for example, [10]). The cylindrical Borsuk problem makes restrictions
42:                                           on the method of partition (cf. [15]). Clearly, the original problem is meaningful
43:                                           for sets in any metric space, for instance, for finite dimensional normed spaces (cf.
44:                                           [3]) or for binary codes equipped with Hamming distance. The latter one is called
45:                                           the (0, 1)-Borsuk problem, and is investigated, for example, in [21], [33] and [22].
46:                                           For more information on this problem and its generalizations, the reader is referred
47:                                           to the survey [24].
