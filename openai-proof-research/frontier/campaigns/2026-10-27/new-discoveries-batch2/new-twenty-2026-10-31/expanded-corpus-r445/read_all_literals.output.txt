333485 A **heroic** family of graphs is one for which there is a constant $d(\mathcal{H})>0$ such that every $\mathcal{H}$-free graph has cochromatic number less than $d(\mathcal{H})$. A complete graph is denoted by $K$, and a tree by $T$.

**Gyárfás–Sumner conjecture.** For every complete graph $K$ and every tree $T$, the family $\{K,T\}$ is heroic.

The source says this conjecture was made independently by Gyárfás and Sumner and is equivalent to the broader finite heroic-family conjecture above. It remains open in the generality stated.
333486 A **heroic** family of graphs is one for which there is a constant $d(\mathcal{H})>0$ such that every $\mathcal{H}$-free graph has cochromatic number less than $d(\mathcal{H})$. Let $K$ be a complete graph and let $t$ be a positive integer; the family in question consists of $K$ and all cycles of length at least $t$.

**Gyárfás's infinite-family conjecture.** For every complete graph $K$, and every integer $t>0$, the family consisting of $K$ and all cycles of length at least $t$ is heroic.

Unlike the finite-family conjecture, this concerns an infinite family and the source notes that the situation is more complicated. It is stated as an open conjecture of Gyárfás.
342054 Let $G$ be a bipartite graph with an arbitrary perfect matching $M$, and let $f0aMchromatic{G}{M}$ denote its $M$-chromatic number. A **matching minor** is obtained by the matching-minor operations defined in the paper.

**Matching-minor Hadwiger conjecture.** If

$$
\chi(G,M)\geq k,
$$

then $G$ contains $K_{k,k}$ as a matching minor.

This is proposed as an analogue of Hadwiger's Conjecture for $M$-colourings. The case $k=2$ is related to the Pfaffian/matching-minor characterization, while the general statement remains open.
342075 Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a hypergraph and let $I=I(\mathcal{H})$ be its edge ideal. Write $I^{(n)}$ for the $n$th symbolic power and $I^n$ for the $n$th ordinary power. A hypergraph satisfies the **packing property** if all of its minors satisfy the König property.

**Packing-property conjecture.** The following conditions are equivalent:

1. $I^{(n)}=I^n$ for all $n\geq 1$.
2. $\mathcal{H}$ satisfies the packing property.

The first condition is already known to be equivalent to the max-flow min-cut property, while the asserted equivalence with the packing property is proposed using the Conforti–Cornuéjols conjecture. The supplied text does not specify whether this conjecture has been resolved.
345508 A **hole** is an induced cycle of length at least $4$. For every integer $\ell$, consider triangle-free graphs having no hole of length greater than $\ell$. **Gyárfás's conjecture.** For every integer $\ell$, there exists $c$ such that every triangle-free graph with no hole of length greater than $\ell$ has chromatic number at most $c$. Seymour proved the corresponding statement with chromatic number replaced by tree-chromatic number, and Chudnovsky, Scott, and Seymour subsequently proved the original conjecture. Thus this conjecture is solved.
345746 Let $G$ be a graph, and let $\chi(G)$ denote its chromatic number. For a vertex decoration $f$, define $S(v)=\sum_{x\in N(v)}f(x)$, and call $f$ **cool** if $S(u)\ne S(v)$ for every adjacent pair $u,v$. **The additive 1-2 Conjecture.** Every graph $G$ has a cool decoration of vertices from the set $\{1,2,\dots,\chi(G)\}$. The assertion is tight for cliques and remains open even for bipartite graphs.
347428 Let $G$ be a graph with a list assignment $L$ to the edges of $G$. For every edge $e=uv$, define its permissible colour list by

$$
L(e)=\max\{1,\dots,\max\{\deg(u),\deg(v)\}+1\}.
$$

An $L$-edge-colouring assigns to each edge a colour from its list so that incident edges receive distinct colours.

**Local Vizing's theorem.** There is an $L$-edge-colouring of $G$.

Vizing's theorem gives an edge-colouring from a common list of size $\Delta(G)+1$; this conjecture proposes the corresponding locally varying bound. The source presents it as a belief and an open question, with no resolution supplied.
347464 Let $T$ be a tree. A graph is **$T$-vertex-minor-free** if it does not contain $T$ as a vertex-minor, and linear rank-width is the graph width parameter used in the claim.

**Kanté and Kwon's conjecture.** For every tree $T$, the class of $T$-vertex-minor-free graphs has bounded linear rank-width.

The conjecture is known for graph classes whose prime graphs under split decompositions have bounded linear rank-width, including distance-hereditary graphs, and for every tree $T$ that is a path. The full assertion remains open.
347470 Let $G$ be a graph, and let $L$ be a **$(k+1)$-list assignment** of $G$, meaning that each vertex $v$ has a list $L(v)$ of $k+1$ available colours. Suppose that for every vertex $v$ of $G$ and every colour $c\in L(v)$, at most $k$ neighbours $w\in N_G(v)$ satisfy $c\in L(w)$. **Reed's constrained colouring conjecture.** There exists a proper $L$-colouring of $G$. This conjecture strengthens ordinary list-colourability conditions by controlling the number of neighbours sharing each available colour; its status is not resolved in the supplied source.
349527 Let $T$ be a tournament. A **nebula** is a tournament admitting an ordering whose vertices are partitioned into the vertex sets of stars and singleton components, with no restriction on whether a star is left or right and no condition on the locations of star centers. **Nebula conjecture.** Every nebula satisfies the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture; equivalently, for every nebula $H$, there exists $\epsilon(H)>0$ such that every $H$-free tournament on $n$ vertices contains a transitive subtournament of size at least $n^{\epsilon(H)}$. Galaxies are known to satisfy the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture, but it is not known whether the result extends to nebulae after abandoning the galaxy condition on star centers.
350143 Let $K_t$ denote the complete graph on $t$ vertices. A **quasi-$K_t$-minor** in a graph $G$ consists of $t$ nonempty, pairwise disjoint vertex sets $B_1,\ldots,B_t$ such that each union $B_i\cup B_j$ induces a connected subgraph for every distinct $i,j$; a $K_t$-minor additionally requires each individual $B_i$ to induce a connected subgraph. **Las Vergnas–Meyniel's Conjecture C.** For any $t$, any graph that admits a quasi-$K_t$-minor admits a $K_t$-minor. The paper presents this conjecture among the claims disproved by its construction.
350146 For each integer $n\geqslant 2$, let $P_n$ be the Pancake graph, let $\chi(P_n)$ denote its chromatic number, and let $\chi_{=}(P_n)$ denote its equitable chromatic number, the least number of colors in a proper coloring whose color-class sizes differ pairwise by at most one.

**Pancake graph equitable coloring conjecture.** For every $n\geqslant 2$,

$$
\chi(P_n)=\chi_{=}(P_n).
$$

Since equitable colorings are proper colorings with an additional balance condition, one always has $\chi(P_n)\leqslant\chi_{=}(P_n)$. The paper notes that the equality holds for the optimal colorings known there, while the assertion for all $n\geqslant2$ remains conjectural.
350378 For positive integers $k$ and $\ell$, let $K_{\ell,\ell}$ denote the complete bipartite graph with parts of size $\ell$. A **$k$-wheel** is a graph consisting of an induced cycle of length at least $4$ and one additional vertex with at least $k$ neighbours on the cycle. **Biclique conjecture.** For every pair of positive integers $k,\ell$, the class of graphs with no induced $k$-wheel and no induced $K_{\ell,\ell}$ is $\chi$-bounded. The source presents this as a further expected strengthening; no resolution is given.
350463 Let $r\geq 2$ and $1\leq i\leq r$. Let $\mathcal{B}_{r,i}$ be the set of partitions whose $(i-1)$-bottom dissection consists of $i-1$ successive bottom squares followed by bottom rectangles, with all bottom rectangles above $B'_{r-1}$ empty. Let $B_{r,i}(n)$ be the number of partitions of $n$ in $\mathcal{B}_{r,i}$, and let $A_{r,i}(n)$ and $E_{r,i}(n)$ denote the associated quantities from the paper. **Bottom-dissection reformulation.** For every nonnegative integer $n$,

$$
B_{r,i}(n)=A_{r,i}(n)=E_{r,i}(n).
$$

The reformulation uses the equality $\mathcal{B}_{r,i}=\mathcal{C}_{r,i}$ to express the same companion identities through successive bottom squares and rectangles. The supplied source does not establish whether the conjecture has been resolved.
351988 Let $T$ and $T'$ be trees with the same number of vertices, and let $T\succcurlyeq T'$ mean that $\operatorname{hom}(T,G)\geq \operatorname{hom}(T',G)$ for every simple graph $G$, where $\operatorname{hom}(T,G)$ is the number of graph homomorphisms from $T$ into $G$. Let $\alpha(T)$ denote the average hitting time of $T$. **Sidorenko-order conjecture.** If

$$
T\succcurlyeq T',
$$

then

$$
\alpha(T')\geq \alpha(T).
$$

The authors report empirical evidence that distinct trees do not satisfy both the Sidorenko order and their previously defined order based on average hitting time. The conjecture proposes that the Sidorenko homomorphism order reverses the ordering by the average hitting-time index.
354287 Let $G$ be a connected graph without loops or bridges. Write $\tau(G)$ for the number of spanning trees of $G$, $b1(G)$ for the number of acyclic orientations of $G$, and $b1^*(G)$ for the number of totally cyclic orientations of $G$.

**Merino–Welsh conjecture.** The inequality

$$
\max(\alpha(G),\alpha^*(G))geq\tau(G)
$$

should hold.

The three quantities are evaluations of the Tutte polynomial: $\tau(G)=T_G(1,1)$, $\alpha(G)=T_G(2,0)$, and $\alpha^*(G)=T_G(0,2)$. The conjecture is known for several graph classes, including sparse or dense graphs, but is not resolved for all connected graphs without loops and bridges.
354432 Let $G$ be a nice graph and let $w:V(G)\cup E(G)\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,k\}$ be a total integer weighting. For each vertex $v$, define

$$
\sigma^{ven}(v):=w(v)+\sum_{e\ni v}w(e)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}w(u),
$$

where $N(v)$ is the open neighborhood of $v$. The weighting is a neighbor full sum distinguishing total coloring when adjacent vertices receive distinct values of $\sigma^{ven}$, and $\chi^{ven}(G)$ is the least such $k$. **Baudon's conjecture.** For every nice graph $G$, $\chi^{ven}(G)\leq 3$.
This extends total neighbor sum distinguishing colorings by also including the weights of neighboring vertices. The source presents the assertion as open.
355094 Let $\mathcal{F}_1$ and $\mathcal{F}_2$ be minor- and union-closed families of graphs. Write $\cong_{\mathcal{F}_1}\Rightarrow\cong_{\mathcal{F}_2}$ when the first homomorphism indistinguishability relation implies the second for all graphs. **Inclusion conjecture.**

$$
\cong_{\mathcal{F}_1}\Rightarrow\cong_{\mathcal{F}_2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{F}_1\supseteq\mathcal{F}_2.
$$

One direction is immediate from inclusion of the testing families, and the conjecture asserts the converse for minor- and union-closed families. The paper states that this conjecture is open and equivalent to the distinctness conjecture.
356100 Let $K_t$ be the complete graph on $t$ vertices, and call a graph $K_t$-minor-free if it has no $K_t$ minor. A list assignment $L$ assigns a set of permissible colours $L(v)$ to each vertex $v$; a graph is $k$-choosable if it has a proper $L$-colouring for every list assignment with $|L(v)|\geqslant k$. **List Linear Hadwiger's conjecture.** There exists a constant $C>0$ such that for every integer $t\geqslant1$, every $K_t$-minor-free graph is $Ct$-choosable. The ordinary list version of Hadwiger's conjecture is false, but this linear list version remains open.
381812 Let $\mathcal F$ be the class of graphs $G$ such that no component of $G$ contains every finite graph as a minor. A graph $U$ is **$<$-universal** for a class if every graph in the class is a minor of $U$. **Universal-element conjecture.** The class $\mathcal F$ has a $<$-universal element.
