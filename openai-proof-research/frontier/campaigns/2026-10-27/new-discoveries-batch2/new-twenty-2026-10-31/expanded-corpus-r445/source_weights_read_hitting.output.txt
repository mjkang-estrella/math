                                           On average hitting time and Kemeny’s constant for weighted trees
                                                                                             Ji Zeng∗



                                                                                            Abstract




arXiv:2109.09249v4 [math.CO] 2 Apr 2026
                                                      For a connected graph G, the average hitting time α(G) and the Kemeny’s constant κ(G)
                                                  are two similar quantities, both measuring the time for the random walk on G to travel between
                                                  two randomly chosen vertices. We prove that, among all weighted trees whose edge weights
                                                  form a fixed multiset, α is maximized by a special type of “polarized” paths and is minimized
                                                  by a unique weighted star graph. We also obtain a similar characterization of the κ-maximizing
                                                  and κ-minimizing elements among such a collection of weighted trees. Our proofs are based on
                                                  the forest formulas for α and κ.


                                          1       Introduction
                                          In this paper, we consider random walks on graphs whose edges are assigned positive weights.
                                          When the random walk on a connected weighted graph G = (V, E) is at vertex u, it will go to
                                          vertex v with probability proportional to the weight of the edge uv (see Section 1.4 in [10]). The
                                          hitting time HG (u, v) is the expected number of steps to walk from vertex u to vertex v. We note
                                          that HG (v, v) = 0 trivially for all vertex v. We shall study the average hitting time, defined as the
                                          arithmetic mean of all hitting times of G,

                                          In words, κ(G) is the expected time to travel from a fixed starting vertex u to a random destination
                                          sampled from the stationary distribution. Formally, this definition of κ(G) depends on the choice
                                          of u, but Kemeny and Snell [6] observed the surprising fact that the value κ(G) is independent
                                          of u (see Exercise 2.48 in [10]). The Kemeny’s constant has applications in network theory [8],

                                          Theorem 1.1. Among all unweighted trees of a fixed size, the path has the largest α and κ, and
                                          the star graph has the smallest α and κ.

    We note that, among all unweighted trees of a fixed size, Ciardo et al. [4] previously proved
the star graph minimizes κ; and recently, Faught et al. [5] proved the path maximizes κ. Our
approach will be somewhat different from theirs, and it generalizes to a weighted setting: For a
multiset (i.e. set with multiplicities) W of positive numbers, we let TW be the collection of all
weighted trees (up to isomorphism) T such that W equals {ω(e)|e ∈ E(T )} as a multiset. Here,
ω(e) denotes the weight of the edge e. See Figure 1 for examples of elements in TW . Notice that
when W only consists of multiple 1’s, TW is the collection of all unweighted trees of a fixed size.
We will investigate which elements in TW maximize or minimize α and κ for a general W .
    Clearly, there is a unique element SW in TW whose underlying graph structure is a star graph.
On the other hand, there are many weighted paths in TW due to various ways to assign the edge
weights. We care about a special type of “polarized” paths: For a weighted path P = v1 v2 . . . vn ,
we denote its edges as ei := vi vi+1 and define c(ei ) := min{i, n − i} for i = 1, . . . , n − 1, then we
say P is polarized provided ω(ei ) ≥ ω(ej ) whenever c(ei ) ≤ c(ej ).
    Intuitively, c(ei ) measures how centrally ei is located in P : We regard e1 and en−1 , the edges
containing the leaves of P , as the least central edges hence they have the smallest c(e1 ) = c(en−1 ) =
1; We regard e2 and en−2 as the second least central edges hence they have the second smallest
c(e2 ) = c(en−2 ) = 2 and so on; We regard the middle edge e⌈ n−1 ⌉ (as well as e⌈ n−1 ⌉+1 if n is odd)

    The characterization in Theorem 1.3 is not as clean as that in Theorem 1.2. The major con-
tribution in the proof of Theorem 1.3 is to show that a κ-maximizing element in TW has to be a
path. Nevertheless, there is only one unweighted path with a given size, so Theorem 1.1 is implied
by Theorems 1.2 and 1.3.
    Inside a general TW , the α-maximizing elements are quite different from the κ-maximizing
elements. For example, when W = {10, 8, 1, 1, 0.1}, we can check through Theorem 1.3 that κ is
maximized by a path with edge weight assignment ω1 = 10, ω2 = 0.1, ω3 = 1, ω4 = 1 and ω5 = 8,
which is not polarized, hence not α-maximizing by Theorem 1.2.
    The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents definitions, notations, and the
forest formulas for α and κ; Sections 3 and 4 are devoted to the proof of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3
respectively; Section 5 lists some remarks.

   Sidorenko [12] introduced a partial order ≽ before: For two unweighted trees T and T ′ , we write
T ≽ T ′ if and only if hom(T, G) ≥ hom(T ′ , G) for any unweighted graph G. Here, hom(T, G) is the
number of graph homomorphisms from T into G. The Hasse diagrams of ≽ on unweighted trees
with sizes 6 and 7 are determined in [12]. For unweighted trees of size at most 8, we observed that
there do not exist distinct trees T and T ′ satisfying both T ≽ T ′ and T ⪰ T ′ . In fact, for two trees
T and T ′ with |T | = |T ′ | ≤ 8, if T ≽ T ′ , then α(T ′ ) ≥ α(T ). We wonder if these properties are
true in general for trees of arbitrary sizes.
