                                                                                                          Abstract
                                                                In 2004, Karoński, Łuczak and Thomason proposed 1-2-3-conjecture: For every nice graph G
                                                            there is an edge weighting function w : E(G) → {1, 2, 3} such that the induced vertex coloring is
                                                            proper. After that, the total versions of this conjecture were suggested in the literature and recently,
                                                            Kalkowski et al. have generalized this conjecture to hypergraphs. In this paper, some previously
                                                            known results on the total versions are improved. Moreover, an affirmative answer is given to the
                                                            conjecture for some well-known families of hypergraphs like complete n-partite hypergraphs, paths,
                                                            cycles, theta hypergraphs and some geometric planes. Also, these hypergraphs are characterized
                                                            based on the corresponding parameter.

                                                     Keywords: vertex coloring, edge weighting, hypergraphs

Conjecture 1. [8] For every nice graph G, χe (G) ≤ 3.

    This conjecture has been explored in two ways. The first is to improve general upper bounds
on χe (G). The latter is to confirm it for some well-known families of graphs.
    The chromatic number of a graph is the smallest number of colors required to give a proper
vertex coloring for the graph. A graph is called k-colorable, if its chromatic number is at most
k. In [8], it is shown that if G is k-colorable, where k is odd, then χe (G) ≤ k. Consequently, the
conjecture is true for 3-colorable graphs. In the literature, the first constant bound was obtained
by Addario-Berry et al. [2] who showed that χe (G) ≤ 30 for every nice graph G. This result
was improved to χe (G) ≤ 16 by Addario-Berry, Dalal, and Reed [1] and then to χe (G) ≤ 13
by Wang and Yu [15]. Currently, the best upper bound is 5 due to Kalkowski, Karoński and
Pfender [10].
    Parameter χe has been studied for several classes of graphs including paths, cycles, complete
graphs, bipartite graphs, theta graphs and so on [5, 6, 12]. However, few number of graph classes
are characterized by the invariant χe . To see these results in details we refer the reader to the
survey written by Seamone [14].
    The cycle of length six and the complete graph on three vertices are simple instances of nice
graphs for which weights 1 and 2 do not suffice to give a neighbor sum distinguishing. Hence,
the bound claimed in Conjecture 1 is best possible. On the other hand, the following theorem
gives some intuitions for the correctness of Conjecture 1.
The random graph Gn,p is the graph on n vertices such that there is an edge between any two
vertices randomly and independently, with probability p.

Theorem 1.1. [1] Assume that G = Gn,p is a random graph for any constant p ∈ (0, 1).
Asymptotically almost surely, χe (G) ≤ 2.

    Clearly, χe (G) = 1, if and only if G contains no adjacent vertices with the same degree.
Therefore, determining the exact value of χe seems to be a more difficult problem for graphs
with more regular structures. This observation motivates researchers to focus on some regular
graphs and hypergraphs.

the total versions of χe , introduced in [13] and [4], respectively. Let w : V (G) ∪ E(G) →
{1, 2, . . . , k} be a total integer weighting function of G. For every vertex v, σ ve (v) is defined by

    A hypergraph H is an ordered pair H = (V, E), where V is a finite and non-empty set of
vertices and E is a collection of distinct non-empty subsets of V . The set of vertices and the
set of edges of H are denoted by V (H) and E(H), respectively. An r-uniform hypergraph is
a hypergraph such that all of its edges have size r. For a vertex v ∈ V (H), N(v) := {u :
∃ e ∈ E(H), {v, u} ⊆ e} stands for the neighborhood of v. A hypergraph vertex coloring is
called proper when each edge of H contains at least two vertices with distinct colors. Similar
to the graph case, χe (H) is the minimum integer k for which there exists a weighting function
w : E(H) → {1, 2, . . . , k} inducing a proper vertex coloring. Also, parameters χve and χven
are defined for hypergraphs, in a similar manner.
    Conjecture 1 has been generalized to 3-uniform hypergraphs by Kalkowski, Karonśki, and
Pfender in the following form [9].
Conjecture 4. [9] For every 3-uniform hypergraph H with no isolated edge, χe (H) ≤ 3.
    Let r ≥ 3 be an integer. In [9], it is shown that for any r-uniform hypergraph H with no
isolated edge, χe (H) ≤ max{5, r + 1}. Also, the authors in [9] have proved that χve (H) ≤ 3
for every hypergraph H with no edges of size 0 or 1.

    It is inferred from Theorem 1.2 that every edge in an r-uniform random hypergraph with
r ≥ 4 contains two vertices of different degrees with high probability. This fact motivates
researchers to explore regular r-uniform hypergrphs in Conjecture 5 as the most challenging

    In section 3, we determine the exact values of χe , χve and χven for the well-known families
of hypergraphs. Indeed, we will see that some r-uniform hypergraphs can not be weighted by 1
and 2 and hence, three colors are required. Moreover, we show that three colors are sufficient for
them. Briefly, main results of section 3 are as follows. Theorem 3.1 determines the parameter
χe for the complete n-partite r-uniform hypergraph with parts of equal sizes. This theorem is
a generalization of the result in [6] to hypergraphs. Moreover, as a straightforward corollary,
we get χe (Knr ) = 2 where Knr denotes the complete r-uniform hypergraph of order n. Also,
correctness of Conjecture 5 is confirmed for t-tight paths and t-tight cycles in Theorems 3.3 and
3.5, respectively. Consequently, the exact value of χe is determined for loose paths, loose cycles,
tight paths and tight cycles. The theta hypergraph HΘ is considered as a generalization of t-tight
cycle and the value of χe (HΘ ) is determined by Theorem 3.6. Another regular structure that we
explore is the family of geometric planes. In Theorem 3.7, the parameter χe is determined for
affine planes and projective planes which are known as the most applicable geometric planes.

3 Main results for hypergraphs
In this section, first some preliminary definitions will be given and then a number of results will
be presented for hypergraphs.
    Let H be a hypergraph. For a vertex v ∈ V (H), degree of v in H, denoted by degH (v), is the
number of edges in H containing v. We say that H′ is a subhypergraph of H, if V (H′ ) ⊆ V (H)
and E(H′ ) ⊆ E(H). A subhypergraph H′ of H is called spaning, whenever V (H′ ) = V (H).

3.1 Complete hypergraphs
An r-uniform hypergraph is called n-partite if there is a partition of the vertex set into n parts
such that each edge has at most one vertex in each part. A complete r-uniform hypergraph
of order n, denoted by Knr , is a hypergraph consisting of all the r-subsets of vertex set V of
cardinality n.
