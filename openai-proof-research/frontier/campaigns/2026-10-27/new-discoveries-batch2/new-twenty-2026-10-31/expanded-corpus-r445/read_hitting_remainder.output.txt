2. The two partial orders ⪰s and ⪰v are identical for unweighted trees. Indeed, if T is unweighted
in the definitions of ⪰s and ⪰v , we have volT1 (T1 ) = 2|T1 | − 1 and volT2 (T2 ) = 2|T2 | by handshaking
lemma, so |T1 | > |T2 | if and only if volT1 (T1 ) > volT2 (T2 ). Hence, we can write ⪰:=⪰s =⪰v if we
only consider unweighted trees. Figure 4 illustrates the Hasse diagram of ⪰ on unweighted trees of

   Sidorenko [12] introduced a partial order ≽ before: For two unweighted trees T and T ′ , we write
T ≽ T ′ if and only if hom(T, G) ≥ hom(T ′ , G) for any unweighted graph G. Here, hom(T, G) is the
number of graph homomorphisms from T into G. The Hasse diagrams of ≽ on unweighted trees
with sizes 6 and 7 are determined in [12]. For unweighted trees of size at most 8, we observed that
there do not exist distinct trees T and T ′ satisfying both T ≽ T ′ and T ⪰ T ′ . In fact, for two trees
T and T ′ with |T | = |T ′ | ≤ 8, if T ≽ T ′ , then α(T ′ ) ≥ α(T ). We wonder if these properties are
true in general for trees of arbitrary sizes.
