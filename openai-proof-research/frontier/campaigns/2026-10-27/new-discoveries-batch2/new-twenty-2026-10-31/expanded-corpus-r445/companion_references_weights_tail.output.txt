  The main achievement of this paper is the inequality
                hom(Pm , Pn ) ≤ hom(Tm , Pn ) for any m, n.

4 Concluding Remark
In Theorem 2.1, some complete n-partite graphs with parts of arbitrary sizes are considered
and the parameter χe is obtained for them. Theorem 2.2 determines χven for complete 3-partite
graphs with parts of arbitrary sizes. Now, one may raise the following question.
Problem 4.1. Let G be the complete n-partite graph with parts of arbitrary sizes. What are the
values of χe (G) and χven (G)?
    It was proved that if G is a 3-colorable graph, then χe (G) ≤ 3, [8]. Consequently, the 1-2-
3-conjecture is true for bipartite graphs. The only known bipartite graphs for which χe reaches
3 was an special subclass of theta graphs. This observation motivated Khatirinejad et al. to ask
the following question [11].
Problem 4.2. [11] Is it true that for every bipartite graph except an special subclass of theta
graphs, we have χe (G) ≤ 2?
    Davoodi and Omoomi answered the problem in negative by extending this family to bipartite
generalized polygon trees as a generalization of theta graphs (Theorem 4.4. in [6]). Note that
although there are many sufficient conditions for χe (G) ≤ 2, the problem of classifying graphs
with χe (G) ≤ 2 is still an open problem, even for graphs where correctness of 1-2-3-conjecture
was confirmed for them.
    A similar observation can be considered for hypergraphs, as well. Clearly, for a hypergraph
H, χe (H) = 1 if and only if every edge of H contains two vertices with different degrees. A pair
of vertices u and v are called twins, whenever the set of edges containing u is the same as the
set of edges containing v. A hypergraph is called twin-free if it contains no twins. For a graph
G, let Hr (G) be the family of r-uniform hypergraphs where every hypergraph H in Hr (G) is
constructed as follow. Every vertex v ∈ V (G) is replaced with a set Sv of new vertices such
that two vertices u and v are adjacent in G if and only if the set Su ∪ Sv is an edge of size r in
H. For example, a cycle of length 4k + 2 can be transformed to an r-uniform hypergraph by
setting |Sv | = r −1 for every other vertex on the cycle [3]. This procedure can be generalized to
every bipartite graph. It is sufficient to consider a breadth first search(BFS) tree of G and then
replace the vertices in odd levels with Sv ’s of size p and the vertices in even levels with Sv ’s of
cardinality r − p. Also, it is clear that a simple way to reach H, for an even r, is to transform
every vertex v to a set Sv of size 2r . Note that |E(G)| = |E(H)| and χe (G) = χe (H) for any
H ∈ Hr (G). Also, every member of Hr (G) has twins. Define
                   Ar = {H : H is an r-uniform hypergraph andχe (H) ≤ 2},

                                                 14
                 Br = {H : H is an r-uniform hypergraph and χe (H) > 2}.
Bennett and Dudek proved that Br 6= ∅ (Theorem 1 (ii) in [3]). Moreover, we presented
examples of cycles and their generalizations to some theta hypergraphs H for which χe (H) = 3
in Theorems 3.5 and 3.6. In [9] Kalkowski et al. proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6. [9] There is no twin-free hypergraph in Br .

   It can be seen that all known r-uniform hypergraphs in Br (obtained in [3] and this paper),
