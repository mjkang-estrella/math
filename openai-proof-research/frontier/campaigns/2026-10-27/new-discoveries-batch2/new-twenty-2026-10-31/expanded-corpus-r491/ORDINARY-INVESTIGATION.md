## 378914: apex deformation, type graph, and actual public obstruction

Source Block–Yu math0503279v3 defines generic input by its Proposition4 (unique tropical minors), shades (r,i) exactly when generator r lies in the apex-i sector, and states Conjecture14 on p9. Its assertion about all minimal apices being vertices relies on Joswig; it is not a fact that can be imported unquestioningly here.

Initial route: at a putative vertex, the bipartite generator/sector type graph is a tree. I considered proving the selected-sector sets disjoint by shrinking a halfspace whenever a generator has a tie between selected sectors. The valid inclusion condition for a fixed selection A, apex change h, is h_i<=h_j for all i in A,j outside A (with the min convention). Boundary witnesses impose reverse inequalities. Merely knowing one boundary witness for each selected sector does not ensure the same outside sector witnesses all of them. A tree with row connections {1,2},{1,3},{2,4} already defeats that proposed cycle argument: the two selected vertices can be linked to different unselected vertices. Thus the naive uniqueness-of-minimum proof fails; coordinatewise perturbations cannot be assumed independent. This is a precise failed proof mechanism, not a certificate by itself.

Next route: allow an apex to move along a type cell. A halfspace can remain inclusion-minimal even when the cell has positive dimension. Actual Gaubert–Katz0908.1586v2 Theorem4 supplies exact necessary and sufficient type conditions and its proof was read; actual Example2 supplies an explicit obstruction. In max-plus convention take four generators v^r=(r,2r,3r,4r), r=1,2,3,4, and coefficients a=(8,6,7/2,-1/2). The type of apex -a is ({1,2},{2},{3,4},{4}). For the displayed halfspace max(6+x2,-1/2+x4)<=max(8+x1,7/2+x3), every left sector meets a right sector, and each right sector has a generator shared with the left but with no other right sector. All four generators are covered by the right types. These are exactly conditions(i)-(iii), so the halfspace is minimal. Its type graph has two connected components, giving a one-dimensional type cell, not a vertex. The source supplies the varying-apex interval explicitly.

The min-plus convention is obtained by x->-x and v^r->-v^r. The apex becomes a, selected sectors are {1,3}, and the same failure of the vertex clause persists. Genericity holds because for any square submatrix of the matrix r*i the rearrangement inequality gives a unique extremizing permutation: an inversion swap changes the sum strictly when row and column indices are distinct. The signs just exchange minima/maxima. Negation and quotient by the diagonal preserve cells and their dimensions.

An alternative vertex apex cannot represent this same halfspace. Its selected and unselected sectors are determined by its unbounded directions; for each cross pair their two-coordinate boundary determines a_i-a_j. Hence two representations with nonempty complementary support sets have equal cross differences; connectedness of the complete bipartite cross graph forces their apices to differ only by a constant. This also follows from the source inclusion lemma. Thus the nonvertex apex cannot be repaired by choosing another vertex. The partition part need not fail in this example: failure of the required vertex clause already refutes the full assertion.

This is the actual publicly identified2009/2011 refutation, not a new discovery. The plotted Figure2 was not visually inspected; the exact coordinates/type conditions, not the plot, supply the comparison. No new formalization or family credit.

## 380014: parity construction and why the printed ancestor bound is source-own

The actual source0609264v2 defines discrete generations, labelled extants, restrictions by removing vertices without retained extant descendants, and constructs a pair via even/odd binary parity founder sets. Its Theorem1 proof, including binary-tree lifting and bit-toggle restriction isomorphisms, was read. Its Remark2 explicitly permits deleting the isolated all-zero vector, but Conjecture1 still prints at least2^(n-1) ancestors at depth n-2.

For a precise nonendpoint case set n=4. Founders for T are the nonzero even parity four-bit strings, seven in total; founders for U are the eight odd parity strings. For each labelled extant xi, its four founders are the parity strings whose ith bit is1. Connect xi to these four founders by a balanced depth-two binary tree, splitting according to one fixed other bit; use the same choice for Ti and Ui. Internal vertices of different trees are disjoint, while founders with identical strings are shared.

Deleting xj permits the founder bijection b->b xor ej on the surviving founder sets. It preserves all incidences with extants xi for i!=j. Within the tree for xi it either swaps the two first branches (if the splitting bit is j), or preserves that split (if not); at the remaining leaf pair it preserves unordered parent sets. This extends to a full isomorphism of every labelled three-extant restriction. The source proves the same construction for every n using consecutive coordinate splits; for n4 this argument includes all levels explicitly.

T and U cannot be congruent: T has4 extants+8 private internal vertices+7 founders=19 vertices, U has20. Every founder is an ancestor of some extant because the all-zero even string was deleted; every nonfounder has two distinct parents, and both pedigrees have depth2. Hence T is not3-reconstructible and has seven ancestors at depth n-2=2, fewer than2^3=8. No gender labelling is assumed by Conjecture1; source Remark1 treats that separate modification.

This is already forced by the source's own displayed construction, not a newly significant counterexample. The alternative positive route from hypergraph reconstruction only proves that each fixed-level ancestor incidence hypergraph agrees below depth n-2. It does not align isomorphisms coherently between levels: a pedigree is more than the extant-to-founder incidence hypergraph. Thus no proof of the corrected full minimal-depth conjecture is obtained. The exact corrected threshold, nonsynchronous generations and possible graph gluing obstructions remain unpaid. No claim all intended pedigree minimality is settled.

## 377306: full-scope attack through projection, not a finite sweep

Use ordered partitions P=(P1,...,Pk), all blocks nonempty, with delta(P)_uv=1 iff u lies in an earlier block. Actual source0111145v3 Theorem5 establishes these are extreme rays of QMET; its proof by transitive-order ray and vertex splitting was read. Their cone OMCUT is generated by these finite rays. Nonnegative decompositions of a point in the relative interior of a two-ray cone test adjacency: any additional ray in such a decomposition obstructs a two-dimensional face. Conversely, if every decomposition uses only those two rays, their cone is a face. The latter follows because a face of a finitely generated cone is generated by exactly the generators occurring in decompositions of its relative-interior points.

First route: reverse refinements. If B properly refines A^T, keep B's internal order in each A-block but permute the A-blocks. For any permutation pi of the coarse blocks,
 delta(A)+delta(B)=delta(A_pi)+delta(B_reverse_pi).
Across different coarse blocks both sides have entries1 in both directions; within one coarse block only B's refinement contributes. Choose a nontrivial pi. The new coarse partition ray and refined partition ray are distinct from the two original rays (for two coarse blocks choose reversal; proper refinement ensures the refined ray remains distinct). This proves reverse-refinement nonadjacency. It does not classify all nonneighbors.

Second route: fix vertex a and c=delta({a},V\{a}); project away all coordinates involving a. For B other than c,c^T the projection b on V\{a} is a nonzero oriented multicut, hence an extreme QMET ray. Every nonnegative decomposition of c+delta(B) projects to b. Therefore each contributing generator projects either to b or0, and the coefficients of generators projecting to b sum to1. A generator projecting to b is exactly obtained by inserting a as a singleton at a gap in B|V\{a}, or by attaching a to one of its blocks. Generators with zero projection are c and c^T. If B does not end in singleton {a}, some x!=a satisfies B_xa=0. The zero coordinate (c+B)_xa=0 excludes c^T.

Let t be the coefficient of c and average the remaining insertion/tie rays with weights summing to1. If a is tied to a nonsingleton block R in B, the coordinates x->a for x in R are zero, so contributing rays put a at or before R. Earlier blocks E satisfy B_Ea=1, forcing all contributing rays to put E before a. If a has a later block L then (c+B)_aL=2 forces t>=1, whereas (c+B)_aR=1 forces t<=1; consequently t=1 and all contributing rays tie a to R. If no later block exists, there is an earlier block E (otherwise B would have one block), and (c+B)_aE=1 with every E before a forces t=1; the R coordinates again force the tie. Thus only B remains.

If a is a singleton but not last, there is a later block L; coordinates a->L have total2 while L->a=0, so t>=1. When earlier blocks exist, E->a=1 forces E before a in every contributing ray, then a->E=1 gives t=1, and all later L must follow a. When no earlier block exists, all other blocks follow a. There are at least two such blocks because B!=c. No contributing ray may place a after the first block (reverse coordinate0). Thus it is either strictly first or tied to the first block. On any subsequent block a->L=2 forces t=1, and a->first=2 excludes any tie. Again only B remains. The case B=c^T has support only between a and its complement, so only c and c^T can occur. This proves adjacency except when B ends in singleton{a} with at least two preceding blocks.

For that exceptional B=(R1,...,Rs,{a}), s>=2, set Bfront=({a},R1,...,Rs). Then
 c+delta(B)=c^T+delta(Bfront),
entrywise; both new rays differ from the two old ones. Hence this is exactly the nonneighbor family for c. If F(k) counts all ordered partitions including the one-block partition, it has F(n-1)-1 elements. There are F(n)-1 nonzero rays; deleting c itself gives degree F(n)-F(n-1)-1. The source calls F=p-prime; the literal instead calls p-prime the number of extreme rays, an off-by-one mismatch. This correction is not a new result.

Third route: for any two rays A,B, at most one singleton cut c_a is forbidden by each (its last singleton block, if present). For n>=3 choose a avoiding the two forbidden labels. It is adjacent to each, unless it equals one endpoint, in which case those endpoints are already adjacent. Thus the skeleton diameter is at most2. It is at least2: choose B ending in {a} with two preceding nonempty blocks and apply the preceding decomposition. This pays diameter2 for n>=3 as an author ordinary helper argument.

Why this still parks the full target: part(ii) also asserts a UNIQUE maximal-degree orbit. Counting the reverse refinements of a general ordered partition does not prove its degree is smaller: the number of reverse refinements is a product of ordered Bell numbers of its block sizes, which need not exceed F(n-1)-1. Part(iii) requires a complete nonneighbor classification for a TWO-element first block. Projection now deletes two vertices, and additional ways of distributing their attachment positions and relative order survive; the one-vertex convex-average argument does not classify those simultaneous choices. No universal exposing functional or full decomposition criterion was produced for that case. The diameter helper is neither the full bundled literal nor another credited family. Current live search did not authenticate an earlier exact diameter theorem; that bounded nonmatch is not worldwide novelty. Author proof not independently reviewed.
