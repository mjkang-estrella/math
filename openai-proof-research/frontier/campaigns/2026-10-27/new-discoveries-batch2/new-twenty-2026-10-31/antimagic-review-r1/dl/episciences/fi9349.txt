                                                   bijection f : E → {1, 2, · · · , |E|} is called a local antimagic labeling if for any two adjacent
                                                   vertices uPand v, they have different vertex sums, i.e., w(u) 6= w(v), where the vertex sum

                                                   sum) w(v). The local antimagic chromatic number χla (G) is the minimum number of colors
                                                   taken over all colorings induced by local antimagic labelings of G. It was conjectured [6] that

1.    Background and introduction
By a graph G = (V, E) we mean a finite simple undirected graph without K2 components. For graph
theoretic terminology we refer to Chartrand and Lesniak [1]. Hartsfield and Ringel [2] introduced
the concept of antimagic labeling of a graph, and conjectured that every connected graph except K2
admits such an antimagic labeling, which remains unsettled till today.

Definition 1.1. Let G = (V, E) be a finite simple undirected graph without isolated vertices. Let
fP: E(G) → {1, 2, · · · , |E(G)|} be a bijection. For each vertex u ∈ V (G), the weight w(u) =
    e∈E(u) f (e), where E(u) is the set of edges incident to u. If w(u) 6= w(v) for any two distinct
 vertices u and v ∈ V (G), then f is called an antimagic labeling of G. A graph G is called antimagic
 if G has an antimagic labeling. Furthermore f is called a local antimagic labeling if w(u) 6= w(v)
 for any two adjacent vertices u and v ∈ V (G).
    Thus it is clear that any antimagic labeling must be local antimagic. Moreover, a local antimagic
labeling induces a proper vertex coloring of G where the vertex v is assigned the color (vertex sum)
w(v). This notation local antimagic labeling was raised in 2017 by the following two sets of authors
independently: Arumugam, Premalatha, Bača, Semaničová-Feňovčı́ková [3], and Bensmail, Senhaji
and Lyngsie [4]. Both groups also raised the following conjecture: Every connected graph other than
K2 is local antimagic. Bensmail et al. [4] propose the slightly stronger form of the previous conjecture
that every graph without component isomorphic to K2 has a local antimagic labeling and proved that
this conjecture is true for trees. However this conjecture has been proved by Haslegrave, using the
probabilistic method, in [5] more recently.
    The local antimagic chromatic number χla (G) of a graph G is the minimum number of colors
taken over all colorings induced by local antimagic labelings of G. Let χ(G) be the usual chromatic
number of a graph G. For any graph G, χla (G) ≥ χ(G). It was noted by Arumugam et al. [3] that
the difference χla (G) − χ(G) can be arbitrarily large as shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2. ([3], 2017) For any tree T with l leaves, χla (T ) ≥ l + 1.
    Among others in [3] the local antimagic chromatic number of paths, cycles, friendship graphs,
wheels, complete bipartite graphs were studied. In this article we study the local antimagic vertex
coloring of the trees. The local antimagic chromatic numbers for various corona products of G with
Km , when the graph G is Pn , Cn , Kn , were reported in [6]. It was furthermore conjectured in [6] that
for any tree T with l leaves, the local antimagic chromatic number χla (T ) is either l + 1 or l + 2. In the
following sections we verify the above conjecture for complete full t-ary trees, t ≥ 2. The definition
of complete full t-ary trees is given below.

Definition 1.3. Let t ≥ 2 be a positive integer. A complete full t-ary tree is a rooted tree in which
all nodes have exactly t children except leaves and every leaf is of the same depth.

Definition 1.4. The edge set of a complete full t-ary tree, t ≥ 2, can be partitioned into n levels,
where the edges of level l, 1 ≤ l ≤ n, are the l-th edges from the root vertex. Note that there are tl
such edges of level l.

2.    Key observations
In literature Skolem [7] remarked that Steiner triple systems could be constructed from a sequence
of integers 1, 2, · · · , 2n if these integers could be arranged in disjoint pairs (n of them) such that the
differences are 1, 2, · · · , n. He showed that such sequence exists if and only if n ≡ 0, 1 (mod 4).
For the purpose of arrangement for labeling, we study an additive analogue and extensions for this
sequence as following. Note that we denote the set of consecutive integers {m, m + 1, · · · , n} by
[m, n].

Problem 2.1. For the first 2n positive integers [1, 2n], can one partition them into n pairs so that the
corresponding n pair sums are consecutive?
Note that the answer is positive if n is odd and is negative if n is even. Let us see the case n is odd
first. Assume n = 2k + 1. Then we have the following.

Lemma 2.2. Let [1, 4k + 2] be the set of consecutive 4k + 2 integers, where k is a positive integer.
Then there exists a partition for [1, 4k + 2] into 2k + 1 pairs for which the sums of these pairs form a
set of 2k + 1 consecutive integers [3k + 3, 5k + 3].

Proof:
We consider the partition for [1, 4k +2] into 2k +1 pairs as follows: (1, 3k +2), (3, 3k +1), · · · , (2k +
1, 2k + 2) and (2, 4k + 2), (4, 4k + 1), (6, 4k), · · · , (2k, 3k + 3). Therefore the set of pair sums are
[3k + 3, 4k + 3] ∪ [4k + 4, 5k + 3], which is a set of 2k + 1 consecutive integers [3k + 3, 5k + 3]. This
completes the proof.                                                                                      t

Lemma 2.3. Let [1, 4k] be the set of consecutive 4k integers, where k is a positive integer. Then
there exists a partition for [1, 4k] into 2k pairs for which the sums of these pairs form a set of 2k − 1
consecutive integers [3k + 1, 5k − 1] with one isolated number 6k. Moreover this result is optimal in
the sense that for [1, 4k] there does not exist any partition into 2k pairs with consecutive pair sums.

Proof:
We use the following partition for [1, 4k] into 2k pairs: (1, 3k), (3, 3k − 1), · · · , (2k − 1, 2k + 1) and
(2, 4k − 1), (4, 4k − 2), (6, 4k − 3), · · · , (2k − 2, 3k + 1). The remaining two integers are (2k, 4k).
Therefore the set of pair sums are [3k + 1, 4k] ∪ [4k + 1, 5k − 1] ∪ {6k}, which is a set of 2k − 1
consecutive integers with one isolated number 6k.
     As to see the result is optimal, one can conclude from the parity check as follows. Suppose
[1, 4k] can be partitioned into pairs with consecutive sums, say a, a + 1, · · · , a + (2k − 1). Then
1 + 2 + · · · + 4k = a + (a + 1) + · · · + (a + 2k − 1), and it follows that 2(4k + 1) = 2a + 2k − 1,
which leads to a contradiction by parity. This completes the proof.                                        t

      To summarize with results of above lemmas, we have the following.

Theorem 2.11. For any tn consecutive integers, one can partition them into t-tuples (n of them) so
that the sums of t-tuples are n consecutive integers either for odd t ≥ 3, or for even t ≥ 2 and

3.     Labeling scheme
For a complete full t-ary tree T , t ≥ 2, we use the following labeling scheme to show the local
antimagic chromatic number is l + 1 ≤ χla (T ) ≤ l + 2, where l is the number of leaves of T .
     Let n be the number of levels of the complete full t-ary trees T . Note that if n = 1, the local
antimagic chromatic number is obviously l + 1. In case n = 2, it can be seen that the local antimagic
chromatic number is at most l + 2 since one may arrange the edge labels 1, 2, · · · , t for the level one
and t + 1, t + 2, · · · , t + t2 in such a way that grouping t2 into t t-tuples with t − 1 consecutive sums
and one isolate by Lemma 2.3, then adding them reversely with 1, 2, · · · , t to make t − 1 constant
sums and another isolate, which give rise to two new colors other than that on leaves. The top vertex
receives the color 1 + 2 + · · · + t = t(t + 1)/2 which is among t + 1, t + 2, · · · , t + t2 due to the
inequality t + 1 ≤ t(t + 1)/2 ≤ t + t2 . Therefore the local antimagic chromatic number is either l + 1
or l + 2.
     The labeling scheme for n ≥ 3 and the sketch of the proof are given as follows.
Step 1: When n is even, arrange labeling of edges of levels by sequential order 2, 4, · · · , n − 2, n −
1, n − 3, · · · , 3, 1, n. Whenever n is odd, arrange labeling of edges of levels by sequential order
2, 4, · · · , n−1, n−2, n−4, · · · , 3, 1, n. Note that the edge labels are used sequentially by consecutive
integers, and will be fixed in later steps by obtaining appropriate constant colors with possible isolated
numbers.
Step 2: We fix the edge labels from the top level to the bottom level sequentially according to the
requirement for colors. Start fixing the t edge labels for the level 1 arbitrarily. Hence the color for the
top vertex is done. Then in order to make constant colors (vertex sums) for vertices in the second row,
we may use Lemma 2.8 to arrange the t2 edge labels in level 2. Inductively we can assign edge labels
for levels one by one from the top to the bottom, so that we have the constant colors for vertices of the
same row until the row right above the row of leaves.

for leaves. Therefore only one new color other than that of leaves and the result is obtained.

4.      Complete full t-ary trees for odd t

odd, is shown to be l + 1, where l is the number of leaves of T . In particular, for the class of complete
full t-ary trees, where t ≥ 3 odd, the conjecture that for a tree T the local antimagic chromatic number
l + 1 ≤ χla (T ) ≤ l + 2 is verified as a byproduct.

5.    Complete full t-ary trees for even t
In this section, using the labeling scheme mentioned above, we verify the conjecture for the class of
complete full t-ary trees for even t ≥ 2.

Theorem 5.1. For complete full t-ary trees T , t ≥ 2 even, the local antimagic chromatic number
χla (T ) is either l + 1 or l + 2, where l is the number of leaves of T .

Proof:

trees T , t ≥ 2 even, since we have the lower bound l + 1 from Theorem 1.2 in [3]. Without loss of
generality we use the case n is even for the sketch of the proof.

N (resulted from adding the consecutive t-tuple-sums and reversely consecutive numbers) and the
isolated jump color J (resulted from adding the smallest consecutive t-tuple-sums and reversely the
largest of consecutive numbers) for the vertices between the level h−1 and level h are (2tn+1 +(−t3 +
t2 − t + 1) · th−1 − t2 − 3t + 2)/(2t − 2) and (2tn+1 + (−t3 + t2 + 2t − 2) · th−1 − t2 − 3t + 2)/(2t − 2)
respectively for 1 ≤ h ≤ n − 1.

vertices between the level h and level h + 1 are (2tn + (t3 − t2 + t − 1) · th − t2 − 3t + 2)/(2t − 2)
and (2tn + (t3 − t2 + 4t − 4) · th − t2 − 3t + 2)/(2t − 2) respectively for 1 ≤ h ≤ n − 3.

and (tn+1 + (t2 + 2t − 2) · tn−2 − t2 − 3t + 2)/(2t − 2) respectively.

6.     Future studies
In this article we are concerned with the local antimagic coloring of trees. It is interesting to observe
that, one may use the properties regarding the additive version of the Skolem sequence to give rise
to the arrangement of the labeling. Again this shows the intimate relationship between the integer
sequences and graph labeling and coloring.
    Among others, we obtain the upper bound l + 2 for the local antimagic chromatic numbers of a
complete full t-ary tree T for all t ≥ 2, and in particular we obtain that the exact value for the local
antimagic chromatic number of a complete full t-ary tree T is l + 1 for odd t, where l is the number
of leaves of T . However the following conjectures are still open and need further exploration.

Conjecture 6.1. For any complete full t-ary tree T , t ≥ 2 even, χla (T ) = l +1, where l is the number
of degree one vertices (leaves) of T .

Conjecture 6.2. For any tree T on at least three vertices, l + 1 ≤ χla (T ) ≤ l + 2, where l is the
number of degree one vertices (leaves) of T .
   Note that the Conjecture 6.2 was mentioned in [6]. It will be interesting to characterize trees T
such that χla (T ) = l + 1, where l is the number of degree one vertices (leaves) of T .
