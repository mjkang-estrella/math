603: As a first step toward extending the results in this paper to generic cycle types  (\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})  where  r>2 , we point out a special case in which we can describe  \mathcal{S}_{\alpha}  explicitly, namely when  \alpha_{1}\leq 2 :
604: 
605: 
606: 
607: 
608:  \mathcal{S}_{(2^{r-k},\>1^{k})}=\left\{\lambda\vdash n:\text{$\lambda$ has exactly $k$ many columns of odd length}\right\}. 
609: 
610: 
611: 
612: (See Proposition 6.4, following from a result of Schützenberger relating to involutions.)
613: Outside of this special case described above, however, for  r>2 , an explicit and comprehensive description of  \mathcal{S}_{\alpha}  quickly becomes quite complicated.
614: Somewhat surprisingly, the source of these complications lies entirely in the presence of repeated values among the  \alpha_{i} ’s.
615: In fact (see Conjecture 6.1), it appears that for  r>2 , we have  \mathcal{S}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}  whenever  \alpha_{1}>\cdots>\alpha_{r}>1 .
616: (It would then follow that for a fixed  r , as  n\rightarrow\infty , the proportion of cycle types satisfying  \mathcal{S}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}  approaches 100%.)
617: See Section 6 for additional details, along with further conjectures and open problems concerning cycle types and  \alpha -colorings.

3165: 6. Final remarks and conjectures
3166:

3170: In this section, we record several conjectures and avenues for further research, regarding the general case where  r>2 .
3171: We begin by treating cycle types corresponding to strict partitions (i.e., partitions  \alpha  with distinct parts  \alpha_{1}>\cdots>\alpha_{r} ).
3172: Somewhat surprisingly, these cycle types are far easier to handle than those corresponding to non-strict partitions.

3252: It seems likely that the converse of Conjecture 6.1 is also true.

3359: A sketch of a proof of Conjecture 6.6 — at least in the case where  Q=T_{\mu} , which is almost always possible — can be given as follows.
3360: Consider an  \alpha -coloring of  T_{\mu}^{\uparrow} , with associated permutation  \sigma .
3361: To obtain an  \widetilde{\alpha} -coloring of  T^{\uparrow}_{\lambda} , we take the  \alpha -coloring of  T_{\mu}^{\uparrow}  and insert one box (a fixed point of the new associated permutation  \widetilde{\sigma} ) into each of the the  k  many columns required to obtain the shape  \lambda ; in particular, we insert this box at the “axis of symmetry” in that column (as in case (2) of Construction 4.18), i.e., the unique location about which  \sigma  reflects the entries in that column.
3362: We then give each of these  k  boxes a unique color.
3363: Of course, special care must be taken in columns where  \sigma  leaves no original entries remaining, but in general every column has a location at which a fixed point can be inserted so as to preserve the standardness of  \widetilde{\sigma}\cdot T^{\uparrow}_{\lambda} .
