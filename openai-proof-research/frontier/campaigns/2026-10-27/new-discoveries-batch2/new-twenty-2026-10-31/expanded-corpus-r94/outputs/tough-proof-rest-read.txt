Claim 2.4.



For all positive integer  \alpha<\frac{n}{2}  such that  d_{\alpha}\leq\alpha , we have  |U^{\alpha}|\geq\alpha-t .




Proof of Claim 2.4.



Suppose  v_{\alpha}\in V(G)  such that  d_{\alpha}\leq\alpha<\frac{n}{2} . By the hypothesis,  d_{n-\alpha+t}\geq n-\alpha . That is, there are at least  n-(n-\alpha+t)+1=\alpha-t+1  vertices of degree at least  n-\alpha , indicating  |U^{\alpha}|\geq\alpha-t .
∎



Claim 2.5.



We have  d_{\alpha}>\alpha  for all integer  \alpha  with  k+t-1\leq\alpha<\frac{n}{2} .




Proof of Claim 2.5.



Assume there exists  \alpha  such that  k+t-1\leq\alpha<\frac{n}{2}  and  d_{\alpha}\leq\alpha . Choose such an  \alpha  to be minimum.
It suffices to show that  U^{\alpha}  is a universal clique: by Claims 2.3 and  2.4, we have  k-2\geq|\Omega|\geq|U^{\alpha}|\geq\alpha-t . Rearranging gives  k+t-2\geq\alpha\geq k+t-1 , a contradiction.
Thus we show that  U^{\alpha}  is a universal clique in the following.
By Claim 2.1,  U^{\alpha}  is a clique complete to  \{v_{i}:d_{i}\geq\alpha-t,i\in[1,n]\} .
Therefore, combining Claim 2.2, we show that every vertex  v_{j}  for  j\in[1,n]  belongs to the set  \{v_{i}:d_{i}\geq\alpha-t,i\in[1,n]\} 
or satisfies  d_{j}<\alpha-t  but  d_{j}\geq j-t+1 .




We first show that  d_{j}\geq\alpha-t  for all  j\in[\alpha,n] .
Consider for now that  j=\alpha .
If  \alpha>k+t-1 , then  \alpha-1\geq k+t-1 . By the minimality of  \alpha , we get  \alpha-1<d_{\alpha-1}\leq d_{\alpha}\leq\alpha . Thus  d_{\alpha}=\alpha>\alpha-t . If  \alpha=k+t-1 , then  d_{\alpha}\geq d_{k}=k>\alpha-t . In either case, we have shown  d_{\alpha}\geq\alpha-t . For any  j\in[\alpha+1,n] , we have  d_{j}\geq d_{\alpha}\geq\alpha-t .
Now for  j\in[1,\alpha-1] , suppose  d_{j}<\alpha-t .
By the minimality of  k , we have  d_{j}\geq j\geq j-t+1  if  j\in[1,k] . We have  d_{j}\geq d_{k}=k>k-1\geq j-t+1  if  j\in[k+1,k+t-2] .
By the minimality of  \alpha , we have  d_{j}>j>j-t+1  for all  j\in[k+t-1,\alpha-1] . This completes the proof.
∎



Claim 2.6.



We have  k\geq\frac{n}{2}-t .




Proof of Claim 2.6.



We suppose to the contrary that  k<\frac{n}{2}-t . Let  p=\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor .
Then  k+t-1\leq p<n/2 . By Claim 2.5, we have  d_{p}>p .
If  d_{p}=n-1 , then all vertices from  \{v_{p},\ldots,v_{n}\}  are contained in a universal clique of  G  and so we have  |\Omega|>\frac{n}{2} . This gives  k\geq|\Omega|>\frac{n}{2} , a contradiction to the assumption that  k<\frac{n}{2}-t .
Thus there exists  i\in[1,n]  such that  v_{p}\not\sim v_{i} . We choose such a maximum  i .
Since  v_{i}\nsim v_{p} , we have  d_{i}<n-t-d_{p}<n-t-(\frac{n-1}{2}-1)=\frac{n+1}{2}-t+1\leq d_{p} , which gives  i<p .
Then by Claim 2.5 and the argument in the second paragraph in the proof of Claim 2.5, we have  d_{i}\geq i-t+1 .
By the maximality of  i , we have  v_{p}\sim v_{j}  for all  j\in[i+1,n]  and so  d_{p}\geq n-i-1 . This gives  d_{i}+d_{p}\geq n-i-1+i-t+1=n-t , which contradicts that  v_{p}\nsim v_{i} .
∎



Claim 2.7.



We have  \delta(G)>\frac{n}{t+1}-1 .




Proof of Claim 2.7.



Assume  \delta(G)\leq\frac{n}{t+1}-1 . Then, as  2t\leq\delta(G) , we have  (2t+1)(t+1)\leq n . By Claim 2.2 and the choice of  k , we know that  U^{k}  is a universal clique. Therefore, by Claims 2.4 and 2.6, we get  \delta(G)\geq|U^{k}|\geq k-t\geq\frac{n}{2}-2t . Observe that for  t\geq 3 , we have




 \displaystyle\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{t+1} 
 \displaystyle= 
 \displaystyle\frac{n(t-1)}{2(t+1)}\geq\frac{(2t+1)(t+1)(t-1)}{2(t+1)} 




 \displaystyle= 
 \displaystyle(t+0.5)(t-1)>2t-1. 



This gives  \frac{n}{2}-2t>\frac{n}{t+1}-1 . Thus  \delta(G)\geq k-t>\frac{n}{t+1}-1 , a contradiction.
∎




As  \delta(G)>\frac{n}{t+1}-1 , Theorem 7 implies that  G  is Hamiltonian, a contradiction to our assumption that  G  is not Hamiltonian. This completes the proof.
  \blacksquare
