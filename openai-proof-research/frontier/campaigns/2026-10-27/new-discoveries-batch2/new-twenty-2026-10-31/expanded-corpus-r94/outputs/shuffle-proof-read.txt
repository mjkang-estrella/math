Proof of Theorem 1.2.



Let  n=3^{s}t  where  s>0 ,  t>1  and  3\nmid t .
Let  H  be the subgroup of  G_{3,3n}  generated by  \sigma  and  \rho_{(01)} . Then  H  is contained in the stabilizer of  3n-1  in  G_{3,3n} . Note that  G_{3,3n}  is  2 -transitive if and only if  H  is transitive on  [3n-1] . It suffices to prove that  [3n-1]\subseteq 0^{H} . Let




 x=(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{1},x_{0};X)\in[3n-1] 



where  X\in[t]  and  x_{i}\in[3]  for all  i\in[s+1] . We will complete the proof in three steps as follows.




Step 1. Show that  [t]\subseteq 0^{H} .




Let  x\in[t]^{*} . Then  x=X  and  x_{i}=0  for every  i\in[s+1] .
If  [x]_{3}^{0}=0 , then
 x^{\sigma^{-1}}=\frac{x}{3} .
If  [x]_{3}^{0}=1 , then  x^{\beta}=x-1 .
If  [x]_{3}^{0}=2  and  [t]_{3}^{0}=2  , then  [t+x]_{3}^{0}=1  and therefore




 \displaystyle x^{\alpha_{s}\beta\alpha_{s}}=(t+x)^{\beta\alpha_{s}}=(t+x-1)^{\alpha_{s}}=x-1. 



If  [x]_{3}^{0}=2  and  [t]_{3}^{0}=1  , then  [t+x]_{3}^{0}=0  and therefore




 \displaystyle x^{\alpha_{s}\beta\alpha_{s}\sigma^{-1}}=(t+x)^{\beta\alpha_{s}\sigma^{-1}}=(t+x+1)^{\alpha_{s}\sigma^{-1}}=(x+1)^{\sigma^{-1}}=\frac{x+1}{3}. 



Note that we have proved that  x^{H}  always contains an integer less than  x  for all  x\in[t]^{*} . Therefore  [t]\subseteq 0^{H} .




Step 2. Show that  x\in 0^{H}  if  x\notin[t]  and
 x_{i}\neq 2  for all  i\in[s+1] .




Assume that  x\notin[t]  and  x_{i}\neq 2  for all  i\in[s+1] .
Then there exists a nonempty subset  I  of  [s+1]  such that  x_{i}=1  if  i\in I  and  x_{i}=0  if  i\in[s+1]\setminus I . Since  [t]\subseteq 0^{H}  and
 x^{\prod_{i\in I}\alpha_{s-i}}=X\in[t] ,
we have  x\in 0^{H} .




Step 3. Show that  x\in 0^{H}  for all  x\in[3n-1] .




Recall that  T(x)=|\{i\in[s+1]\mid x_{i}=2\}| . We proceed by induction on  T(x)  to show that  x\in 0^{H}  for all  x\in[3n-1] . It has been proved that  x\in 0^{H}  if  T(x)=0  in the the first two steps. Now let  T(x)\geq 1  and suppose that  y\in 0^{H}  for all  y\in[3n-1]  satisfying  T(y)<T(x) .
We will complete the proof by verifying that there exists  b\in x^{H}  such that  T(b)<T(x) .




If  T(x)=s+1 , then  x_{i}=2  for every  i\in[s+1] . Therefore




 x=2(3^{s}+\cdots+3+1)t+X=3^{s+1}t-t+X=3n-(t-X). 



By Eq. (4), we have




 \displaystyle x^{\sigma^{s+1}} 
 \displaystyle=3^{s+1}X+3^{s}x_{s}+3^{s-1}x_{s-1}+\cdots+3x_{1}+x_{0} 




 \displaystyle=3^{s+1}X+2(3^{s}+3^{s-1}+\cdots+3+1) 




 \displaystyle=3^{s+1}(X+1)-1. 



Since  x=3n-(t-X)<3n-1 , we have  X<t-1  and it follows that




 x-x^{\sigma^{s+1}}=3^{s+1}t-t+X-3^{s+1}(X+1)+1=(3^{s+1}-1)(t-X-1)>0. 



Thus  x>x^{\sigma^{s+1}} .
Similarly, if  T(x^{\sigma^{i(s+1)}})=s+1  for some positive integer  i ,
then  x^{\sigma^{i(s+1)}}>x^{\sigma^{(i+1)(s+1)}} . Since  x  is finite, there exists a positive integer  \ell  such that  T(x^{\sigma^{\ell(s+1)}})<s+1 . Set  b=x^{\sigma^{\ell(s+1)}} . Then  b\in x^{H}  and  T(b)<T(x) .




Now we assume that  1\leq T(x)<s+1 . By Lemma 3.1, we can further assume  x_{0}=0  and  x_{1}=2 . Then  x=(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,0;X) . Since  3\nmid t , we have  [t]_{3}^{0}=1~\mbox{or}~2 . So we can proceed with the proof in two cases.




Case 1.  [t]_{3}^{0}=2 .




First we have the following observations.


(a) 



If  [X]_{3}^{0}=0 , then




 x=(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,0;X)\equiv 0\pmod{3}, 



and




 \displaystyle x^{\beta\alpha_{s}}= 
 \displaystyle(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,0;X)^{\beta\alpha_{s}}

 \displaystyle(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,0;X+1)^{\alpha_{s}} 



 \displaystyle= 
 \displaystyle(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,1;X+1)\equiv 0\pmod{3}. 




(b) 



If  [X]_{3}^{0}=1 , then




 \displaystyle x^{\alpha_{s}}= 
 \displaystyle(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,1;X)\equiv 0\pmod{3}

 \displaystyle x^{\beta}= 
 \displaystyle(x_{s},x_{s-1},\ldots,x_{2},2,0;X-1)\equiv 0\pmod{3}.
