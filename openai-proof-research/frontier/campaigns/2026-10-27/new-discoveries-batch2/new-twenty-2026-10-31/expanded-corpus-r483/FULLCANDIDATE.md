Let S be the triples (x,y,z) in {0,1,2}^3 satisfying
x=2 implies y>=1; y=2 implies z>=1; z=2 implies x>=1.

Define a poset on x1,x2,y1,y2,z1,z2. Its lower level is x1,y1,z1, its upper level x2,y2,z2, with relations x1<x2, y1<x2; y1<y2, z1<y2; z1<z2, x1<z2 and no further nontrivial relations. This is a poset because every edge runs from the lower level to the upper level. An ideal selects an initial segment from each named two-element chain. Its three initial-segment lengths belong to S, and every point of S gives an ideal. These operations are inverse.

S is finite and nonempty, with origin. For a nonempty ideal remove a maximal element; its triple decreases one positive coordinate by one, remains feasible, and proves accessibility A1. For ideals I not contained in J, choose a minimal element u of I minus J. Every predecessor of u belongs to I and, by minimality, to J. Thus J union {u} is an ideal; u is the next missing element on its coordinate chain. Its triple is obtained from J by adding one occurrence of a coordinate in I minus J. This proves the actual augmentation axiom A2, not merely accessibility plus an unjustified multiset-union assertion. Intersections and unions of ideals correspond respectively to coordinatewise minimum and maximum. Hence S is closed under intersection and is a poset poly-antimatroid under Definition3.9. It is genuinely three-dimensional: 000,100,010,001 all belong to S and are affinely independent. All points have nonnegative integral coordinates.

Suppose S were the union of a regular sequence of cuboids D1,...,Dm. Let li be the coordinatewise lower corner of Di. Definition3.2(b) implies li<=lj whenever i<=j. Every Di is a subset of S because its union is exactly S.

The three points a=(2,1,0), b=(0,2,1), c=(1,0,2) satisfy all three implications defining S. Choose indices i,j,k of cuboids containing a,b,c respectively. Since Di contains a, its lower z-coordinate is 0. Its lower y-coordinate must be 1: it is at most 1, and if it were 0 then the interval cuboid would contain (2,0,0), which violates x=2 implies y>=1. Similarly the lower corner of Dj has x=0 and z=1, since otherwise Dj contains forbidden (0,2,0). The lower corner of Dk has y=0 and x=1, since otherwise Dk contains forbidden (0,0,2).

Comparing z in the monotone sequence of lower corners gives i<j: j<=i would imply 1<=0. Comparing x gives j<k. Comparing y gives k<i. Contradiction. This rules out every number of boxes, overlapping or redundant boxes, degenerate coordinate intervals, and every choice of representation. It does not use the adjacent-overlap condition or increasing upper corners, so it proves a stronger obstruction. It also makes the six-order case split unnecessary.
