Own route1: character evaluation row matroids MA and MB have rank k by character-table invertibility. Multiplication of every character by a fixed character multiplies evaluation columns by nonzero scalars; hence acts transitively on the ground set preserving independence. Translate any basis by all N characters. Each character occurs exactly k times. Therefore for any subset U of characters, rank_A(U)>=k|U|/N: each translated basis contributes at most rank_A(U), while total intersection size is k|U|. Likewise rank_B(complement U)>=k(N-|U|)/N. The sum is >=k. Finite matroid intersection then gives a common independent set of size k, proving both determinants nonzero. This uses the finite matroid intersection theorem as a standard input, not an independently source-audited proof of that theorem; no claim that its application is novel. Trivial group and k=0 follow from the empty determinant convention; otherwise k<=N and the argument applies. The rank inequalities are over real/rational counts, so division is not performed in the field K and positive characteristic causes no problem.

Route2, actual public proof independently checked: use the nonsingular full character table to expand an arbitrary function phi:G->K(t_g) in characters. If all determinant products vanish, Cauchy–Binet implies det(phi(a_i+b_j))=0 for every phi. But the generic function phi(g)=t_g has nonzero determinant. To see this without a finite search, select g=a1+b1, take all rows admitting an entry g and force every such row to its unique g-column. Those columns are distinct since the a_i are distinct. Any assignment with the same multiset of sums must realize the maximum possible number of g's and therefore has those same forced positions. Apply induction to the remaining rows and columns. The resulting permutation gives a monomial appearing exactly once. Its coefficient is +1 or -1, nonzero in every characteristic. Thus generic determinant cannot vanish, contradiction. Extension to rational function fields creates no new character values: all e-th roots were already in K; nonzero original determinant products remain nonzero upon extension.
