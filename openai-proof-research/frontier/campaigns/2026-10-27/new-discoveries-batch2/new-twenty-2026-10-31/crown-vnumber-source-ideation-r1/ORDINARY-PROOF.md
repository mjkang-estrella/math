# MathDB 369163: an ordinary proof candidate for all local v-numbers

Status: complete ordinary argument, self-reviewed only; zero accepted credit. Source, novelty and independent-review gates remain HOLD as specified in REVIEW.md. No formal execution is proposed. This proof uses the source's radical minimal-prime decomposition and cut-set classification (Kumar–Pomeroy–Tran, arXiv:2507.03889v2, §3 and Theorem 3.3). It does not use a general local v-number formula, a computation, or a characteristic restriction.

Let K be any field, n ≥ 4, X={a_1,...,a_n}, Y={b_1,...,b_n}, and V=X∪Y. The edges of G are a_i b_j for i≠j. Work in S=K[x_v,y_v : v∈V], with ordinary total degree. Write [u,v]=x_u y_v-x_v y_u, and D(W)=([u,v]:u,v∈W). Write L(W)=(x_v,y_v:v∈W). The actual binomial edge ideal is J=([a_i,b_j]:i≠j).

The source's irredundant prime decomposition consists of the following primes, which we name by their cut sets:

* P_0=D(V).
* P_X=L(X), P_Y=L(Y).
* Q_i=P_(X minus a_i)=L(X minus a_i)+D({a_i}∪(Y minus b_i)).
* R_i=P_(Y minus b_i)=L(Y minus b_i)+D({b_i}∪(X minus a_i)).
* M_ij=P_(V minus {a_i,a_j,b_i,b_j})=L(V minus {a_i,a_j,b_i,b_j})+([a_i,b_j],[a_j,b_i]), for i≠j.

Thus J is the intersection of this finite family of distinct, pairwise incomparable prime ideals. In Q_i the vertex a_i belongs to the same completed connected component as Y minus b_i; b_i is isolated. Omitting a_i from that determinantal block would give the wrong prime. In particular, all X coordinates cannot be set to zero to test nonmembership in Q_i.

## 1. Exact separator equivalence

For any finite irredundant prime decomposition J=intersection(P_lambda),

(J:f)=P_tau if and only if f∉P_tau and f∈P_lambda for every lambda≠tau.

Indeed (J:f) is the intersection of those P_lambda not containing f. A finite intersection of prime ideals can equal the prime P_tau only if one of the intersectands equals P_tau: otherwise choose, from each intersectand, an element outside P_tau and multiply them. Pairwise incomparability then excludes any additional intersectand. The converse follows directly by distributing colon over intersection and using primality. Also (J:P_tau)=intersection_(lambda≠tau) P_lambda, by the same incomparability and distribution argument. A nonzero class in (J:P_tau)/J is outside P_tau and therefore has exact colon P_tau. This proves all colon equivalences used here, without treating a colon inclusion as equality.

It suffices for the lower bound to prove, for every tau and every homogeneous f of degree d≤3,

f∈intersection_(lambda≠tau)P_lambda implies f∈P_tau.   (L)

## 2. Multigrading and restriction lemma

Give both x_v and y_v degree e_v in N^V. Every displayed prime is homogeneous for this grading. Hence membership in each prime, and in their intersection, holds component by component. A component h of vertex multidegree alpha has vertex support U={v:alpha_v>0} with |U|≤d. If U meets the cut set of the target prime, every monomial of h is divisible by a coordinate killed by that prime, so h belongs to it automatically.

For a component supported in U disjoint from a prime's cut set, membership in that prime can be tested in S_U=K[x_v,y_v:v∈U]. Its contraction to S_U is generated by exactly those displayed minors with both endpoints in U. To prove this without an elimination assumption, set all coordinates outside U to zero. A killed-coordinate generator maps to zero because its vertex is outside U. Each minor maps either to itself (both endpoints in U) or to zero. The specialization fixes S_U and proves the claimed contraction; the reverse inclusion is immediate. Consequently this lemma applies to arbitrary coefficient combinations, including repeated vertex degrees and all cancellations.

## 3. Full-side primes: completing the r292 argument

Take tau=X. Components with X support already lie in P_X. A remaining component h lies in S_Y and, by membership in every Q_i, lies in D(Y minus b_i) for every i.

If its support U has at most two vertices, choose i in U when U is nonempty (choose any i when U is empty). The restriction of D(Y minus b_i) to S_U is zero: there are fewer than two surviving supported vertices. Thus h=0.

Otherwise |U|=3, d=3, and alpha has value one at each vertex of U. Write U={b_r,b_s,b_t}. The restrictions of Q_r and Q_s imply

h∈([b_s,b_t]) and h∈([b_r,b_t]) in S_U.

These two quadratic polynomials are irreducible and nonassociate in the polynomial UFD S_U over any field. For example [b_s,b_t], as a polynomial in x_(b_s), is primitive linear over the polynomial ring in the other variables: its coefficients y_(b_t) and -x_(b_t)y_(b_s) are relatively prime. Gauss's lemma proves irreducibility; the differing vertex degrees prove nonassociation. The argument remains valid in characteristic two. Thus their product divides h. A nonzero h has degree at least four, a contradiction. This proves (L) for X and, by swapping the sides, Y. No primeness or UFD property of a determinantal quotient has been assumed.

## 4. One-deleted-side primes: the missing family

Take tau=Q_i. Reduce to a component h supported in U⊆{a_i}∪Y. Since P_X is another minimal prime, h∈P_X. A nonzero such h must have positive a_i degree, so a_i∈U.

If b_i∉U, membership in P_0 gives h∈D(U), by the restriction lemma. Since U⊆{a_i}∪(Y minus b_i), D(U)⊆Q_i, as required.

If b_i∈U, degree at most three leaves room for at most one further vertex b_j with j≠i. If there is one, use M_ij, which is a different minimal prime from Q_i. On S_U its contraction is generated solely by [a_i,b_j]: the other generator [a_j,b_i] has an unsupported endpoint. Therefore h∈([a_i,b_j])⊆Q_i.

If there is no such b_j, choose any j≠i and use M_ij again. Its contraction to S_{ {a_i,b_i} } is zero, so h=0. This also handles arbitrary powers supported on those two vertices.

Every component has now been treated, so (L) holds for Q_i. Swapping X and Y proves it for every R_i. This is a symbolic support argument for all n, not a test of selected monomials: h throughout is an arbitrary polynomial in the indicated multidegree.

## 5. Source-proved families, independently checked by support

For tau=0, membership in P_X and P_Y forces a nonzero component to have support on both sides. With degree at most three, at least one side has a single supported vertex. If the sole X vertex is a_i, restrict Q_i to S_U. Its surviving generators are minors, all of which belong to P_0, so h∈P_0. If the sole Y vertex is b_i, use R_i instead. This independently recovers the already known empty-prime lower bound.

For tau=M_ij, a surviving component is supported in W={a_i,a_j,b_i,b_j}. Membership in P_X and P_Y again forces both sides in its support, and degree at most three forces one side to have a single supported vertex. If that is a_i, use Q_i. The only possible surviving minor is [a_i,b_j], a generator of M_ij. If it is a_j, use Q_j and [a_j,b_i]. If the Y side is singleton, use R_i or R_j in exactly the symmetric way. All these testing primes differ from M_ij. Thus (L) holds here too. This avoids relying on an informal comparison of two representations modulo J in the source's Lemma 5.7.

Sections 3–5 prove (L) for every minimal prime and every d≤3.

## 6. Explicit degree-four upper bounds and exact colons

The following constructions are those of source Proposition 5.2, with its copied X-prime subscripts in the Q_i paragraph corrected by direct membership verification. They are not claimed as new.

For P_0 use f=x_(a_1)x_(a_3)x_(b_1)x_(b_2). Every other cut set meets these four vertices. The full sides do; deleting one vertex from a side cannot remove both supported vertices on that side; and a mixed prime leaves only two index pairs, whereas these supported vertices involve three indices. Hence f belongs to every other prime. It is outside D(V), since specialization x_v=t_v,y_v=0 annihilates D(V) and sends f to a nonzero monomial.

For P_X use f=[b_1,b_2][b_3,b_4]. It is a nonzero polynomial in the Y coordinates, hence outside P_X. It belongs to P_0. It belongs to every Q_i since one of these disjoint pairs avoids b_i, and its minor is in the block D({a_i}∪(Y minus b_i)). It belongs to P_Y and every R_i since each two-vertex Y minor has a killed endpoint. Every mixed prime kills at least one of the four distinct Y vertices, so at least one factor belongs to it. Use the symmetric construction for P_Y.

For Q_i choose distinct i,j,k and put f=x_(a_i)x_(b_k)[b_i,b_j]. Membership in P_X and every Q_l with l≠i follows from x_(a_i). Membership in P_0 follows from its minor. For P_Y and R_l: if l≠k, x_(b_k) is killed; if l=k, both b_i and b_j are killed. For a mixed prime retaining index pair {r,s}, the factor x_(a_i) is killed unless i∈{r,s}; the factor x_(b_k) is killed unless k∈{r,s}. If both survive then {r,s}={i,k}, and b_j is killed, so the minor belongs to that prime. Finally f∉Q_i: set killed X coordinates to zero, set all y coordinates of {a_i}∪(Y minus b_i) to zero, and retain their x coordinates as independent indeterminates; set x_(b_i)=0 and y_(b_i)=s independent. This annihilates Q_i and sends f to -x_(a_i)x_(b_k)x_(b_j)s≠0, including in characteristic two. Use the symmetric construction for R_i.

For M_ij use f=[a_i,a_j][b_i,b_j]. It lies in P_0 and both full-side primes. In every Q_l at least one of a_i,a_j is killed; in every R_l at least one of b_i,b_j is killed. Every other mixed prime kills at least one of a_i,a_j, so its first factor belongs to that prime. To see nonmembership in M_ij, set (x_(a_i),y_(a_i))=(x_(b_j),y_(b_j))=(1,0) and (x_(a_j),y_(a_j))=(x_(b_i),y_(b_i))=(0,1), with all other columns zero. This kills M_ij and gives f=-1≠0 in any field.

Every displayed f is homogeneous of degree four. Section 1 now proves (J:f)=P_tau exactly for its respective prime. Combining these witnesses with (L),

for every field K, every integer n≥4, and every T∈C(G), v_(P_T)(J_G)=4.

In fact (intersection_(lambda≠tau)P_lambda)_d=J_d for all d≤3: (L) gives containment in every prime, and the reverse containment is immediate. This stronger low-degree equality is an explicit audit target for an independent reviewer.
