## Explicit subsumption bridges and their limits

The older Liwski Theorem 4.15 does apply over C to Q_i and M_ij: “minimal cut” means a cut-point set leaving exactly two components, not an inclusion-minimal cut in some different sense. For Q_i the components are V_1={a_i}∪(Y minus b_i) and V_2={b_i}. The V_2 connected dominating subset is {b_i}, size one. A connected dominating subset of G[V_1∪T] constrained to lie in V_1 must contain a_i and at least two distinct b_j,b_k with j,k≠i: without a_i, a connected subset of Y is a singleton and misses other Y vertices; with only b_j, the deleted vertex a_j is undominated. Those three vertices suffice, since j≠k. Thus the theorem gives 3+1=4. For M_ij each component is an edge. Its constrained connected dominating subset must contain both endpoints: one endpoint misses the deleted vertices on its own side. Both endpoints suffice, giving 2+2=4. These cases over C are therefore routine consequences of that actual theorem and must not be claimed as new in isolation. No all-characteristic extension of all its proof dependencies is claimed as already established literature.

That theorem has no full-side application: P_X leaves n≥4 components. The newer universal lower bound does not close this gap either. For S=X, its nice other cutsets are Y, each Y minus b_i, and all mixed cuts; X minus a_i is not nice because its star joins n−1 original Y components. The transversal hyperedges outside X are respectively Y, Y minus b_i, and Y minus {b_i,b_j}. A transversal has at least three Y vertices, and any three suffice, so v^-_X=3. Its upper bound is at most four from the two disjoint minor witness. Hence the stated general lower theorem leaves the original 3..4 gap for the full-side primes. This is a uniform set argument, not enumeration. No complete existing-theorem-plus-bridge subsumption of our all-prime, all-field endpoint was established. Shortness alone does not justify routine exclusion; conversely the still unread journal/history bodies preclude novelty clearance. Decision: HOLD, not a novelty claim and not an unsubstantiated routine-exclusion verdict.
