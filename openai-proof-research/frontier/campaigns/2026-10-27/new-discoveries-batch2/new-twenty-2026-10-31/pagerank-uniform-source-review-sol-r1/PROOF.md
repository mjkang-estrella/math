Constructed personally before reading FULLCANDIDATE.md. The task itself disclosed the proposed transposition mechanism, so this is independent verification and reconstruction, not independent discovery of that mechanism.

## Exact domain
Let N be a finite set of n vertices; E is a subset of N×N (directed edges, no parallel copies, loops permitted). Every row has positive outdegree. Fix a subset I and its complement J, fixed E_in⊆J×I nonempty and E_J⊆J×J. Vary all edges whose origin is in I, namely E_I⊆I×I and E_out⊆I×J, subject to every a∈I having a directed path to J. All outside rows are fixed. There are no fixed labeled inside degrees. These are the baseline source constraints; extra variants are separate. Nonempty E_in implies both I,J nonempty and n≥2. Let 0<c<1, P_ab=1/d_a on edges and zero elsewhere, e=1_I, z=1/n, and v=(Id-cP)^{-1}e. Suppose E maximizes π^T e over this entire admissible domain.

## Resolvent and objective, without irreducibility assumptions
For any row stochastic P, every P^m is nonnegative row stochastic. Each entry of R=Σ_{m≥0}c^m P^m converges absolutely, bounded by Σc^m. Finite geometric sums give (Id-cP)Σ_{m=0}^M c^mP^m=Id-c^{M+1}P^{M+1}; the remainder tends entrywise to zero. Multiplication in the reverse order gives the same identity. Thus R is the two-sided inverse, R≥Id entrywise, and R1=(1-c)^{-1}1. Define π^T=(1-c)z^TR. It is nonnegative, normalized, and π^T(Id-cP)=(1-c)z^T. Consequently π^TG=π^T for G=cP+(1-c)1z^T. Conversely any normalized invariant row satisfies this linear equation, hence equals π^T. Therefore π^Te=(1-c)z^Tv=(1-c)/n Σ_a v_a. Also v=e+cPv and v≥e.

## Maximum and contradiction
Let M=max_N v>0. An outside vertex attaining M would satisfy M=cΣ_b P_ab v_b≤cM<M. Thus every global maximizer lies inside I. Suppose no such maximizer receives an edge from J. Choose a maximizer x∈I and a fixed inlink (j,y)∈E_in. Then y∈I, v_y<M=v_x, x≠y, and P_ax=0 for every a∈J. If |I|=1 the supposition is already impossible; the following construction handles every remaining case.

Let τ exchange x,y and fix all other vertices (in particular every vertex of J). Define E' on origins in I by (a,b)∈E' iff (τa,τb)∈E; on origins in J retain E exactly. This is not a global graph isomorphism. On inside rows the destination map is a bijection, so d'_a=d_{τa}>0, there are no duplicated edges, and loops map to loops. All outside rows/degrees, E_in and E_J remain identical. E_I and E_out may change, which is authorized. To prove accessibility, take an old path from τa to J and stop at its first outside vertex. Apply τ to every vertex in that path. Every edge originates inside I, hence belongs to E'; its endpoint in J is fixed. Thus a still reaches J. E' is admissible even if the old graph is reducible. It need not retain each labeled inside degree, a constraint absent in the baseline problem.

Put u_a=v_{τa}. For a∈I, P'_ab=P_{τa,τb}, so (P'u)_a=(Pv)_{τa}; also e_a=e_{τa}. Thus r=e+cP'u-u is zero inside. For a∈J, u_a=v_a and the old Bellman equation gives
r_a=c(P_ay-P_ax)(v_x-v_y)=cP_ay(v_x-v_y)≥0.
The only changed terms in this outside row are columns x and y. At the chosen j, P_jy>0 and hence r_j>0. For v'=(Id-cP')^{-1}e, subtraction gives (Id-cP')(v'-u)=r; the proved resolvent identity implies v'-u=R'r≥r≥0. In particular v'_j>u_j, so Σv'>Σu=Σv. The last equality uses the fact τ is a permutation. Uniform personalization now gives π'^Te>π^Te, a contradiction. Therefore there exist i∈argmax_N v and j∈J with (j,i)∈E_in. The argument rules out the simultaneous absence of inlinks to all maximizers; it does not assert that every maximizer is targeted without further structure.

## Full literal chain endpoint
Source Theorem 12 (pp.19–20, statement and proof read) says every optimizer under the same baseline domain can be relabeled I={1,…,n_I} with v_1>…>v_{n_I}>v_{n_I+1}≥…≥v_n, E_I={(a,b):b≤a or b=a+1} and E_out={(n_I,n_I+1)}. Thus the first node in its forward chain is the unique global maximizer. Applying the proved existential formula to this unique node proves it receives an outside inlink. The statement covers any optimal structure and any c∈(0,1); no choice of a special optimizer is made. This bridge cites the actual source classification; the new existential proof does not require that classification. For singleton I the unique first node receives an inlink directly. The literal prose is precisely the explanatory source p.24 question followed by the p.25 formula, not an additional optimization of the external parent j. The following source question whether j belongs to the outside argmax set is not proved here.

## Dependence on uniformity
For arbitrary positive z, although z^T(v'-u)>0, generally z^Tu≠z^Tv, so this comparison does not show improvement. Uniformity is essential to this argument's preservation of the baseline sum. No claim of a nonuniform theorem follows. The improvement is exact and strict in an outside component, even though the target objective sums stationary masses inside I: the resolvent objective identity above is the required bridge.
