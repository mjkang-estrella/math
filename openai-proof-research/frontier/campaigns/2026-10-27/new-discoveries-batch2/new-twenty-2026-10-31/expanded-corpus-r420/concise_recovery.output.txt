3. Multislant matrices and p-shifted n-staircases
3.1. p-shifted n-staircases
The first type of partitions that we will study are the p-shifted n-staircases.
These are precisely the partitions whose entries form a non-constant arithmetic
progression. Explicitly, they are defined as follows:

  Definition 3.1. Let p > 0, n > 0 and k ⩾ 0 be integers. The p-shifted n-staircase
  of length k is the partition

                   λ = ( p + (k − 1)n, p + (k − 2)n, . . . , p + n, p).

  When p ⩽ n, we call this partition an inward-shifted n-staircase; when p > n,
  we call it an outward-shifted n-staircase.

row).

it appears to require a looser notion of “multislant matrices”, which no longer
lends itself to an easy computation of the vanishing probability.
