98: 1     Introduction
99: The arithmetic of non-associative indices (shapes or patterns of bracketing with a binary operation ∗)
100: has been defined as "Logarithmetic" by Etherington in the ’40s of the 20th century. One of the most
101: interesting properties that have been overlooked by modern cryptography is the Discrete Logarithm
102: problem and the Diffie-Hellman key exchange protocol in the non-commutative and non-associative
103: logarithmetic of power indices. In light of the latest developments in quantum computing, Shor’s
104: quantum algorithm that can solve DL problem in polynomial time if the underlying algebraic structures
105: are commutative groups, and the post-quantum cryptography, it seems that there is an opening for a
106: rediscovery of Logarithmetic and its applications in Cryptography.
107:     Etherington himself, as well as other authors later ([1], [2]), noticed that the notation of shapes
108: introduced in [3] quickly gets complicated (and from our point of view for using them for cryptographic
109: purposes, incapable of handling exponentially large indices).
110:     Inspired by the work of Etherington, in a series of works in ’50s, ’60s and ’70s of the last century,
111: many authors such as Robinson [4], Popova [5], Evans [6], Minc [7], Bollman [8], Harding [1], Dacey [2],
112: Bunder [9], Trappmann [10], developed axiomatic number systems for non-associative algebras which
113: in many aspects resemble the axiomatics of ordinary number theory. While the results from that de-
114: velopment are quite impressive such as the fundamental theorem of non-associative arithmetic (prime
115: factorization of indices [6]), or the analogue of the last Fermat Theorem [5, 6, 7], the construction of
116: the shapes was essentially sequential. In cryptography, we need to operate with power indices of ex-
117: ponential sizes. Thus, we have to define shapes over non-associative and non-commutative groupoids
118: that allow fast exponentiation, similarly as it is done with the consecutive rising to the powers of two
119: in the standard modular arithmetic, while keeping the variety of possible outcomes of the calculations,
120: as the flagship aspect of the non-commutative and non-associative structures.
121: 
122: 1.1   Our Contribution
123: We first define a general class of groupoids (G, ∗) (sets G with a binary operation ∗) over direct
124: products of finite fields with prime characteristics (Fp )L that are "Entropic" (for every four elements
125: x, y, z and w, if x ∗ y = z ∗ w then x ∗ z = y ∗ w). Then, for L = 2 we find instances of those
126: operations ∗ that are nonlinear in (Fp )2 , non-commutative and non-associative. In order to compute
127: the powers xa where a ∈ Z+ , of elements x ∈ G, due to the non-associativity, we need to know
128: some exact bracketing shape as , and we denote the power indices as pairs A = (a, as ). Etherington
129: defined the Logarithmetic of indices A, B, by defining their addition A + B and multiplication AB as
130: xA+B = xA ∗ xB and xAB = (xA )B . He further showed that the power indices of entropic groupoids,
131: satisfy the "Palintropic" property i.e., xAB = (xA )B = (xB )A = xBA which is the exact form of the
132: Diffie-Hellman key exchange protocol.
133:     We further analyze the chosen instances of entropic groupoids for how to find left unit elements, how
134: to compute the multiplicative inverses, how to define addition in those groupoids, and how to find gen-
135: erators g ∈ G that generate subgroupoids with a maximal size of (p−1)2 elements. We show that these
136: maximal multiplicative subgroupoids are quasigroups. We also define Sylow q-subquasigroups. Hav-
137: ing all this, we define several hard problems such as Discrete Entropoid Logarithm Problem (DELP),
138: Computational Entropoid Diffie-Hellman problem (CEDHP), and Decisional Entropoid Diffie-Hellman
139: Problem (DEDHP). We post a conjecture that DEDHP is hard in Sylow q-subquasigroups. Next, we
140: propose a new hard problem specific for the Entropoid algebraic structures: Computational Discrete
141: Entropoid Root Problem (CDERP).
142:     We propose instances of Diffie-Hellman key exchange protocol in those entropic, non-abelian and
143: non-associative groupoids. Due to the hidden nature of the bracketing pattern of the power index
144: (if chosen randomly from an exponentially large set of possible patterns), it seems that the current
145: quantum algorithms for finding the discrete logarithms, but also all classical algorithms for solving
146: DLP (such as Pollard’s rho and kangaroo, Pohlig-Hellman, Baby-step giant-step, and others) are not
147: suitable to address the CLDLP.
148:     Based on CDERP, we define two post-quantum signature schemes.
149:     Our notation for power indices that we introduce in this paper differs from the notation that
