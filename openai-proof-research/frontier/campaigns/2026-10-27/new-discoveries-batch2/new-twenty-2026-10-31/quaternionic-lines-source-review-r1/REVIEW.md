## Independent ordinary certificate

Write the quaternion algebra as H, the subgroup as H0, and the right vector space as V=H². Quaternion multiplication satisfies i²=j²=k²=-1, ij=k, ji=-k and their cyclic counterparts. Q8 consists of the eight distinct signed basis units. Those relations prove closure: multiplying two signed basis units produces another; each has norm one and inverse its conjugate. In particular Q8 is a group, without enumeration by a program.

Let D(q)=diag(q,q), c=-1/2, s=sqrt(3)/2>0, and

R=((c,-s),(s,c)),  H0={D(q):q in Q8},
G={D(q)R^a:q in Q8, a=0,1,2}.

Each displayed matrix acts right-linearly: A(v alpha)=(Av)alpha by associativity. Real entries commute with quaternion entries, so D(q)R=RD(q). Direct multiplication uses c²+s²=1, c²-s²=c and 2cs=-s, yielding R²=((c,s),(-s,c)) and R³=I. Neither R nor R² is diagonal. The map q to D(q) is injective and multiplicative. Therefore

(D(q)R^a)(D(r)R^b)=D(qr)R^(a+b modulo 3),
(D(q)R^a)^(-1)=D(q^(-1))R^(-a modulo 3).

This proves actual matrix subgroup closure, identity, inverses and invertibility. For distinct exponents modulo three, equality D(q)R^a=D(r)R^b would imply R^(a-b)=D(q^(-1)r), contradicting the nonzero off-diagonal entries of R and R². Equal exponents force q=r. Thus |G|=24, |H0|=8 and H0 is normal. These are counts of distinct matrices, not merely formal pairs. Conjugate transpose gives D(q)*D(q)=I; R*R=R^t R=I. Products are unitary, so G is a finite subgroup of U₂(H).

Here is an elementary dimension-two argument covering ALL actual right subspaces. Given nonzero w=(x,y), its right span wH is a nonzero subspace. If x is nonzero, right multiplication by x^(-1) gives w'=(1,t), t=y x^(-1). If a subspace containing w' also contains z=(u,v) outside w'H, subtract w'u to get (0,v-tu) with v-tu nonzero. Multiplying on the right by its inverse gives e₂, and then w'-e₂t=e₁. Such a subspace is V. If x=0, then y is nonzero and wH=e₂H; any vector outside it has nonzero first coordinate and the same elimination proves the whole space is obtained. Consequently every nonzero proper right subspace of V is exactly a right quaternionic line. No real-plane or complex-subspace proxy is involved.

Classify the H0-invariant lines. The vertical line e₂H is invariant. Every other line has a unique normalized generator (1,t), t in H. Invariance under D(i) says (i,it)=(1,t)a, so a=i and it=ti. Invariance under D(j) similarly gives jt=tj. For t=a+bi+cj+dk with real coefficients,

it-ti=2c k-2d j,
jt-tj=-2b k+2d i.

Linear independence over R implies c=d=b=0. The intersection of the two centralizers is exactly R. Conversely real t commutes with every q, so D(q)(1,t)=(1,t)q; hence the entire line is invariant by right linearity, and equality rather than only inclusion follows using inverses. It follows that

FixedLines(H0) = {L_t=(1,t)H:t in R} union {e₂H}.

These are nonzero proper subspaces: e₂ is outside every L_t, and e₁ is outside the vertical line. If L_t=L_u, then (1,t)=(1,u)a for some a, so a=1 and t=u. Thus n to L_n is an injection from the natural numbers into the ACTUAL invariant right subspaces. H0 fixes infinitely many lines; H0 is reducible, since L_0 is invariant. It fixes no common nonzero vector pointwise, because -I belongs to H0 and -v=v implies v=0 in characteristic zero.

If G were reducible, the dimension-two argument would give an invariant right line, hence one in the displayed H0 classification. But R e₂=(-s,c) is outside e₂H. For real t,

R(1,t)=(c-st,s+ct).

Membership in L_t forces the right coefficient to equal c-st and then forces s+ct=t(c-st). Since c,s,t are real this is s(1+t²)=0, impossible. There is no division by c-st, so its possible vanishing is covered. Therefore G has no nonzero proper invariant right quaternionic subspace: G is quaternionically irreducible. No irreducibility assertion about the underlying complexification or real restriction is being substituted.

Now take ANY subgroup K with H0<K<=G. Choose D(q)R^a in K outside H0, so a is 1 or 2. Multiplication by D(q)^(-1) in H0 shows R^a is in K. For a=2, squaring yields R; thus in either case K contains R. Since it also contains every D(q), it contains every displayed element of G. Hence K=G. The complete interval of subgroups containing H0 is {H0,G}; no normal-only or selected-list assumption occurs. As H0 is reducible and G irreducible, H0 is maximal among ALL reducible subgroups of G.

The triple (V,G,H0) therefore refutes exactly the broad finite irreducible statement of MathDB 370092. This certificate uses elementary quaternion identities, real positivity and explicit right-subspace arguments only. It is independent of any upstream theorem's truth and of any computed table.

The narrower reflection-only conjecture is not resolved. Indeed this G contains no nonidentity element with a nonzero pointwise fixed vector. For a=0 this follows from (q-1)x=0 in a division ring. For a=1 or 2, if D(q)R^a v=v then R^a v=D(q^(-1))v; commuting and the identity (R^a)²+R^a+I=0 give D(q^(-2)+q^(-1)+1)v=0. For q=1, -1, or q²=-1, the equivalent scalar q²+q+1 is respectively 3, 1, or q, all nonzero. Thus v=0. In dimension two a reflection fixes a line pointwise, so G is not generated by reflections. This does not undermine its membership in the expressly broader source alternative.
