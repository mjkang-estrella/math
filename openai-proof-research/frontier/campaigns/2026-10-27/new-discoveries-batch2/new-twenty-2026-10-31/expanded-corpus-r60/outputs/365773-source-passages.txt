This has become known as the Erdős Sum-Product Conjecture11 1 It is more commonly described as a conjecture of Erdős and Szemerédi, but this is not correct. In 1983, Erdős and Szemerédi [5] made the first substantial progress on the conjecture, proving the n^{1+\epsilon} bound for positive integers with an unspecified positive \epsilon .. A more formal statement of the conjecture follows. Let A+A\coloneqq\{x+y:x,y\in A\} be the sumset and AA\coloneqq\{xy:x,y\in A\} be the product set. Sum-Product Conjecture. For all \epsilon>0 there is an n_{0} such that if A is any set of positive integers with |A|\geq n_{0} , then \big|(A+A)\cup(AA)\big|\geq|A|^{2-\epsilon}. Erdős offered $100 for a proof or disproof, and offered $250 for a more exact bound (see Problem F18 [8]).
