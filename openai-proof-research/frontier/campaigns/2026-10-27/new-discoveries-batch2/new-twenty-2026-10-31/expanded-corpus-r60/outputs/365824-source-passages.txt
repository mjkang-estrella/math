Diameter graphs Borsuk’s classical problem asks whether any bounded set in {\mathbb{E}}^{d} may be partitioned into d+1 subsets, each of smaller diameter than the set. Borsuk confirmed it on the plane, and, to great surprise, it was later proven to be false in high dimensions by Khan and Kalai [68]. The answer is still positive for d=3 as shown independently by Grünbaum [66], Heppes [67] and Straszewicz [102] by means of ball polytopes. They considered Vázsonyi’s conjecture as well, and proved that in any set of n points in {\mathbb{E}}^{3} , the diameter of the set is attained at most 2n-2 times. For a new proof, see Swanepoel’s [103].
