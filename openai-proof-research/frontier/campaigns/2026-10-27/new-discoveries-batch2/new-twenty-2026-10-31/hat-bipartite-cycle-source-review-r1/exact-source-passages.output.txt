25:                                                                                          1. Introduction
26:                                             In the face of the apparent intractability of efficiently describing the set of all finite hamiltonian
27:                                          graphs, one realistic strategy to acquire more knowledge is the study of slices of that complicated
28:                                          set: under the assumption that a known sufficient condition for hamiltonicity holds, one tries to
29:                                          find proofs that such a graph has extra properties, such as having many Hamilton circuits, or having
30:                                          many well-distributed Hamilton circuits; examples in the deterministic setting (with the sufficient
31:                                          condition being a mininum-degree-condition) are to be found in [6] and [13]. In [9, Theorem 1],
32:                                          another extra property, a sort of ‘richness-’ or ‘universality-’ property (the set of all Hamilton
33:                                          circuits generates the cycle space), previously known for certain Cayley-graphs only [1], was proved
34:                                          to hold in graphs of minimum degree slightly above Dirac’s bound for hamiltonicity.
35:                                             There are known examples for the above paradigm in a random setting as well (e.g., [3] [4] [5]
36:                                          [11]), and in this note we give another variation on that theme: we will combine some recent results
37:                                          in order to prove a new result, Theorem 1, teaching us that sufficiently dense binomial random
38:                                          graphs a.a.s. have the above universality-property (and for a vastly smaller edge-probability than
39:                                          what would a.a.s. force the minimum-degree-condition from [9, Theorem 1] to hold).
40:                                             We write Z1 pG; F2 q for the cycle space of a graph G in the standard graph-theoretical sense, and
41:                                          HpGq for the set of all Hamilton circuits in G. The word ‘circuit’ means ‘2-regular connected graph’,
42:                                          while ‘cycle’ means ‘element of Z1 pG; F2 q’. For any graph G, the notation Z1 pG; F2 q “ xHpGqyF2 ,
43:                                          in which xHpGqyF2 denotes the F2 -span of the (vectors w.r.t the standard basis of the edge-space
44:                                          describing the) Hamilton circuits of G, is equivalent to saying ‘every cycle of G is a symmetric
45:                                          difference of Hamilton circuits of G’.
46:                                             We will phrase Theorem 1 in linear-algebraic language, to be able to efficiently use auxiliary
47:                                          results from [9]. However, the results can easily be translated into more constructive phrasings: (2)
48:                                          and (3) are equivalent to the statement that, a.a.s., the set of cycles which can be constructed as
49:                                          a symmetric difference of Hamilton circuits is as large as parity alone permits. I.e., all cycles if n
50:                                          is odd, and all even cycles (i.e., elements of the cycle space having a support of even size, of which

54:                                          (2) is not deterministically equivalent to saying that for even n, every even cycle is a symmetric

32:                                                                                           1. Introduction
33:                                               There exist investigations in which the set underlying a finite-dimensional vector space is not
34:                                           forgotten, but made to play a central part. One such investigation was begun thirty years ago
35:                                           by I. B.-A. Hartman and concerns the cycle space Z1 pX; Z{2q of a finite graph X (whose vectors
36:                                           are the Eulerian subgraphs of X): under what conditions does Z1 pX; Z{2q admit a basis over Z{2
37:                                           consisting of long graph-theoretical circuits only? Hartman proved [37, Theorem 1] a theorem which
38:                                           guarantees that—barring the sole exception of X being a complete graph with an even number of
39:                                           vertices—for every 2-connected finite graph X, the set of all circuits of length at least δpXq ` 1
40:                                           generates Z1 pX; Z{2q.
41:                                               The lower the minimum degree δpXq, the larger the set of cycle-lengths one has to allow in order
42:                                           to be guaranteed a generating system by Hartman’s theorem. In particular, statements guaranteeing
43:                                           a generating system consisting entirely of Hamilton circuits (a natural thing to ask for once the
44:                                           topic of long circuits has been broached) remain almost inaccessible via this theorem: one has to
45:                                           set δpXq :“ f0 pXq ´ 1, hence X – Kf0 pXq , and what remains of Hartman’s general theorem is a
46:                                           rather special (albeit still non-obvious) statement about the complete graph.
47:                                               The property of Z1 pX; Z{2q being generated by the Hamilton circuits of X seems to have been
48:                                           first studied by B. Alspach, S. C. Locke and D. Witte [5]. They proved that X has the property if
49:                                           X is a connected Cayley graph on a finite abelian group and is either bipartite or has odd order
50:                                           (these hypotheses being mutually exclusive for connected Cayley graphs on finite abelian groups).

Title:
When Hamilton circuits generate the cycle space of a random graph
Authors:
Peter C. Heinig
View a PDF of the paper titled When Hamilton circuits generate the cycle space of a random graph, by Peter C. Heinig
View PDF
HTML (experimental)
Abstract:
If eps > 0 and p >= n^{-1/2 + eps}, in a binomial random graph G(n,p) a.a.s. the set of cycles which can be constructed as a symmetric difference of Hamilton circuits is as large as parity by itself permits (all cycles if n is odd, all even cycles if n is even). Moreover, every p which ensures the above property a.a.s. must necessarily be such that for any constant c>0, eventually p >= (log n + 2 log log n + c)/n. So, whatever the smallest sufficient p for an a.a.s. Hamilton-generated cycle space might be, it does not coincide with the threshold for hamiltonicity of G(n,p).
Comments:
8 pages, 1 figure; added proof that any p which a.a.s. guarantees a non-trivial cycle space generated by Hamilton circuits must be significantly larger than the threshold for hamiltonicity of G(n,p)
