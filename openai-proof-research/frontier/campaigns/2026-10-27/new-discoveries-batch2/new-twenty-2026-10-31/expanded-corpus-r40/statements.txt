$$

The conjecture asserts that singularity is asymptotically caused by these elementary local obstructions. The paper presents it as a natural extension of a folklore conjecture and notes that the available result of Tikhomirov does not determine the required sharp error term.
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06553 Vishesh Jain, Ashwin Sah and Mehtaab Sawhney, “Sharp invertibility of random Bernoulli matrices”, arXiv:2010.06553 (2021).

348300 Linial's universal surface extremal-number conjecture
Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a $3$-uniform hypergraph, and let $\operatorname{ex}_{hom}(n,\mathcal{S})$ denote the maximum number of edges in a $3$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices containing no hypergraph homeomorphic to $\mathcal{S}$. **Linial's conjecture.** There exists a constant $c=c(\mathcal{H})>0$ such that

$$
\operatorname{ex}_{hom}(n,\mathcal{S})<cn^{5/2}.
$$

The conjecture is the general form of the proposed $O(n^{5/2})$ bound for avoiding a fixed topological hypergraph; the paper has established the bound for triangulations of orientable surfaces, while the general assertion remains open.
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07191 Andrey Kupavskii, Alexandr Polyanskii, István Tomon and Dmitriy Zakharov, “The extremal number of surfaces”, arXiv:2010.07191 (2020).

348336 Optimal Gonality Conjecture for optimal curves
Let $C$ be an optimal curve over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ of genus $g$, meaning that it has $N_q(g)$ rational points. The **geometric gonality** of a curve is the minimum degree of a morphism from its base change to $\mathbb{P}^1$ over the algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$.

**Optimal Gonality Conjecture.** Every optimal curve $C$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ of genus $g$ has gonality at most $\operatorname{geomgon}$.

The conjecture predicts that optimal curves have especially low gonality. It holds for $q=2,3,4$ and $g\leq 5$, and also for $q=2$ and $g=6$; the source gives further examples in genera $7$, $8$, and $9$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$.
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07992 Xander Faber and Jon Grantham, “Ternary and Quaternary Curves of Small Fixed Genus and Gonality with Many Rational Points”, arXiv:2010.07992 (2022).

348348 The semibrick-pair completion conjecture for tau-tilting finite algebras
Let $\Lambda$ be a $\tau$-tilting finite algebra, and let $\mathcal{D} \sqcup \mathcal{U}[1]$ be a **semibrick pair**, meaning a collection of bricks in the indicated degrees satisfying the relevant hom-orthogonality conditions. Let the smallest wide subcategory containing $\mathcal{D}\sqcup \mathcal{U}[1]$ be the wide subcategory generated by this pair. **Semibrick-pair completion conjecture.** The pair $\mathcal{D} \sqcup \mathcal{U}[1]$ is completable if and only if the smallest wide subcategory containing it has $|\mathcal{D}| + |\mathcal{U}|$ simple objects. This concerns when semibrick pairs extend to 2-term simple minded collections in the $\tau$-tilting finite setting; the supplied text does not state whether the conjecture has been resolved.
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08645 Emily Barnard and Eric J. Hanson, “Pairwise Compatibility for 2-Simple Minded Collections II: Preprojective Algebras and Semibrick Pairs of Full Rank”, arXiv:2010.08645 (2023).

348361 Ellipse-locus conjecture for triangle centers on focus-mounted triangles
Let $\mathcal{E}$ be an ellipse with foci $f_1$ and $f_2$, and let $P(t)$ sweep $\mathcal{E}$. For the triangle $\mathcal{T}_f(t)=f_1f_2P(t)$, denote by $X_k$ the $k$th triangle center, and let $X_1$ and $X_2$ be the first and second triangle centers. A point $X_k$ is a **fixed affine combination** of $X_1$ and $X_2$ if there are constants independent of $t$ such that $X_k=\lambda X_1+(1-\lambda)X_2$. **Ellipse-locus conjecture.** The locus of $X_k$ over $\mathcal{T}_f(t)=f_1f_2P(t)$ is an ellipse if and only if $X_k$ is a fixed affine combination of $X_1$ and $X_2$. Numerical analysis found no counterexample among the more than 38,000 listed centers that are not on the $X_1X_2$ line, while the proposition preceding the conjecture identifies several centers whose loci are ellipses. The claim remains unproved in the supplied text.
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09408 Mark Helman, Ronaldo Garcia and Dan Reznik, “Intriguing Invariants of Centers of Ellipse-Inscribed Triangles”, arXiv:2010.09408 (2021).
