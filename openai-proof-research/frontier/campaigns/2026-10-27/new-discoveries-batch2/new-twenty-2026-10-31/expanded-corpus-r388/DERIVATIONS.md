## 330791: bounded Sharing Nim rows

Source: Ho, arXiv:1506.06961v2, pp.3–7 and Section 4, Conjecture 1. Legal transfer moves are positive k from a larger pile x to a smaller pile y with 2k<=x-y; token removal is not a legal move. Common translation and permutation preserve the game tree. For a sorted triple, the sum of pairwise squared differences strictly decreases under a legal transfer (equivalently the sum of squares of pile sizes decreases by 2k(x-y-k)>0). This gives a genuine well-founded recursion, but a bound depending on the total pile sizes does not prove a uniform row bound.

Write g(a,n)=G(0,a,n), n>=a. The required assertion is: for every fixed a>=1 there exists a finite B_a such that g(a,n)<=B_a for every n>=a. It is not a claim about P/N status, eventual periodicity, or a fixed finite table. Normalize each follower after transfer:

* middle to bottom, 1<=k<=floor(a/2): (k,a-k,n) becomes (0,a-2k,n-k).
* top to middle, 1<=k<=floor((n-a)/2): (0,a+k,n-k) is already sorted.
* top to bottom, 1<=k<=floor(n/2): (k,a,n-k). If k<=a, translation yields (0,a-k,n-2k); if k>=a, it yields (0,k-a,n-k-a). The two formulas agree at k=a. These bounds include exactly the legal transfers, including equality when two piles meet; k=0 is excluded.

The first route was induction on the small row parameter a. The first follower family decreases a, and part of the third does too. But the entire second family and the k>a portion of the third reach unboundedly many larger row parameters as n grows. Source symmetry g(a,n)=g(n-a,n) replaces a row parameter by the smaller of the two complementary values; this still does not make these families uniformly less than a. For example, a move from (0,1,n) with k near n/3 in the top-to-middle family has both possible row parameters of order n. Thus this is a structural obstruction, not just one bad endpoint in the induction.

A second route was a finite-state/dyadic description. The source gives the zero-value classification and exact one-values, and shows that even the bottom row is not ultimately periodic. Neither statement controls all higher mex values. Nonperiodicity by itself does not disprove a bounded or automatic description. To use a finite-state recursion one must supply a finite collection of state functions closed under ALL three follower families and prove that their mex outputs lie in a fixed finite alphabet, uniformly in n. No such closed state system has been derived here. A residue-class guess covering zero and one omits arbitrarily high candidate mex values. No table or published finite maximum is used as proof.

Third, the option-count bound mex(S)<=|S| is valid but the top-to-middle family alone has floor((n-a)/2) moves. It cannot give a bound independent of n. To salvage a counting argument one needs a uniform bound on the number of DISTINCT follower values (not moves), including those coming from the interior rows above. This is a precise unpaid compression lemma. A weaker boundary-row compression result would not suffice.

The 2020 Kim paper 2008.01075, Definition 5.1, was checked as a concrete companion. It allows removal and transfers in the opposite direction, ending when the heaps are empty. It is a different game; its Nim-sum statements do not transfer. Its code was not executed. The completed r372 disposition for 330792 concerns nonperiodicity, not this boundedness target, and had no primary-body proof clearance. No inheritance of a solved status is justified. Outcome: HOLD, exact whole-row boundedness unpaid.

## 330284 and 330286: orthogonal representation mechanisms

Source: Solís-Encina–Portillo, arXiv:1504.03662v2, Definitions 1.1–1.3, 3.1–3.2 and Conjectures 3.1 and 3.3. The literal 330284 says merely no orthogonal representation in dimension k, whereas the banned-graph discussion uses faithful representations. Definition 1.2 requires adjacency iff inner product zero, and unequal assigned vectors; the prose on p.4 excludes proportional vectors, a stronger condition. Zero-vector treatment is also not consistently stated. Definition 3.2 and the later banned discussion do not provide a clean disjoint class of critical-but-not-banned graphs. We preserve these source problems rather than choose convenient semantics and claim a new refutation.

For a credible intended reading, take faithful, nonzero, pairwise projectively distinct vectors. A tempting doubling argument has this form: if twins x and x' in dimension k+1 are independent, their common neighbors lie in span(x,x')-perp, of dimension k-1. This is a valid local rank bound. It gives a contradiction only if those neighbors themselves force rank k. Valence k does not say their k vectors are independent; faithful nonadjacent neighbors can have linear dependences. If x,x' are allowed to be proportional, even the rank-two conclusion fails. The ordinary-OR version has still fewer constraints. Thus every witness condition matters.

I pursued global projection after this failed local shortcut. Projecting all vectors into x-perp would lower ambient dimension, but for formerly orthogonal u,w the new product is
<u,w>-<u,x><w,x>/<x,x>.
This need not vanish. Projection can therefore destroy required graph edges, and can also create forbidden zeros and vector coincidences. A proof needs a simultaneous projection or deformation preserving the full zero/nonzero pattern, not just the twin neighborhood. No such deformation is available. Projecting only neighbors does not define a representation of the original graph because it leaves mixed pairs unchecked.

A second global mechanism is the faithful Gram matrix: a real positive semidefinite matrix of rank d, with positive diagonal and off-diagonal zero set exactly the edge set, together with any required projective distinction. Duplicating a vertex gives constrained rows, but the zero pattern and row count alone do not force the desired rank increment. An ordinary minimum-rank theorem for one-direction orthogonality does not resolve this faithful condition. No solver was used.

For matching deletion, edge-criticality says something about each single-edge deletion separately. It does not imply a simultaneous dimensional reduction after deleting a maximal matching: faithful dimension is not generally monotone under deleting further edges, because every newly deleted edge requires a NONZERO inner product. Maximal and maximum matchings are different. Even a representation constructed for G-e cannot simply be reused for G-M. The missing lemma is a single rank-(d-1) faithful Gram construction with zero set E(G) minus M for every source-admissible critical G and every maximal M, proving all new nonzero entries and distinction conditions. Criticality supplies no common kernel vector or simultaneous perturbation. Conversely a refutation needs an explicit graph that is critical in precisely the source sense, not a familiar graph whose dimension stays unchanged after some edge deletions. No such fully verified witness survives here. Outcome: both HOLD, with source fidelity and mathematics separate.

## 334396 and 334397: Cheeger minimizers

Source: Kozlov, arXiv:1610.07136v2, Definitions 2.3 and 2.6, Proposition 2.7, Definition 4.4, Conjectures 5.2–5.3, and Appendix 6.3. The variational class is nonempty cut-minimal simple graphs: every cut has at most half its potential edges, and h(G)=T/m, where m>0 is the number of edges and T counts unordered vertex triples with an odd number of graph edges. It is not the ordinary conductance of a graph. The displayed use of ordered incidences in the source must be interpreted with its one-third triangle weights so each triangle contributes once. The source has h(n)>=n/3, equality for n not a power of two, and different upper-bound analysis for powers of two. We did not silently assume equality in all orders.

First attempt: removing a triangle should improve the quotient. Removing edges preserves cut-minimality, so this is a legitimate variation. For an edge uv set d_u,d_v for degrees, c_uv for common neighbors, and t_uv for odd triples containing uv before deletion. Exactly
 t_uv=n-d_u-d_v+2c_uv
third vertices give odd triples. Deleting uv changes T by n-2-2t_uv, while m changes by -1. If m>1 and G is a minimizer of ratio h, optimality implies
 n-2-2t_uv+h>=0.
Equivalently 2d_u+2d_v-4c_uv>=n+2-h. A triangle increases c_uv, suggesting an obstruction, but this inequality alone does not rule out c_uv>=1. For a triangle abc, deleting all its three edges changes T by
 3(n-2)-2(t_ab+t_ac+t_bc)+2.
The +2 correction is essential: that triple flips once, whereas three separate flips each counted it. If m>3, optimality yields this expression plus 3h>=0. Neither this nor the single-edge inequalities establishes triangle-freeness. The m<=3 cases require their own quotient comparison; no conclusion about them is being promoted. Missing: a strict improving deletion or other legal variation for every triangle-containing minimizer, with all denominator and small-order conditions paid.

Second attempt: switch a cut, preserving the coboundary class and T. At a cut-minimal graph, a perfect cut keeps m unchanged and hence also h. This can move between minimizers; it is not a strict improvement. It also does not automatically preserve absence of triangles or produce a bipartition. Choosing the cut N(v) isolates v after switching. Its remaining edge count equals the number T_v of odd triples containing v, so T_v>=m by cut-minimality. Sum_v T_v=3T gives nm<=3T. When h=n/3, equality forces T_v=m for EVERY v, which is useful additional rigidity. It does not yet classify the resulting two-graph, and for powers of two with h>n/3 there is no equality conclusion. Unpaid lemma: classify equality cut-minimal representatives sufficiently to force no triangles, and separately handle every power-of-two minimizer. A blow-up or staircase example only constructs some optimal graphs and cannot show all are bipartite.

Third attempt: use a shortest odd cycle to prove the stronger bipartiteness target directly. Absence of chords can simplify the induced cycle but not edges from its vertices to the rest of G, which enter every cut inequality and T. Switching alternating vertices of the odd cycle controls internal edges but uncontrolled outside incidences can erase the anticipated improvement. No universal outside-neighborhood inequality or strict ratio decrease has been proved. Unpaid: a cut/deletion inequality using arbitrary outside neighborhoods of every shortest odd cycle, while keeping cut-minimality and achieving a strict objective improvement. The stronger conjecture implies the triangle one, but the triangle one would not imply bipartiteness. Source Figure 4.1 is only a small-order catalogue; no new figure-based or finite-case classification is claimed. Outcome: HOLD both; these are not solved by the successful derivation of the known n/3 lower bound.
