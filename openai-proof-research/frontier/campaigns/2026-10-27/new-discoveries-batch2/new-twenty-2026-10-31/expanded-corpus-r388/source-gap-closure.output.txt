Q ) ∪ fR−1 (Q− \ J), which satisfies the condition in Lemma 3.8. Clearly, this defines
  +

the inverse of the map (1). Therefore, it follows that the map (1) is a one-to-one
correspondence.                                                                     

Theorem 3.15. The map
                     J 7→ P = {(p, hJ (p) − hJ (0)) | 0 ≤ p ≤ 2k + 1}
is a one-to-one correspondence between balanced order ideals J of Q and lattice
paths P from (0, 0) to (2k + 1, d), where d varies over positive integers.
Proof. Proposition 3.12 implies that this map is well-defined. Take any path P
from (0, 0) to (2k + 1, d) for some positive d (it follows that d is odd). Let pi be the
y-coordinate of the ith point in path P , that is, (i, pi ) ∈ P . Let JP be a subset of
Q so that hJP (i) = pi − 2b(d − 1)/4c − 1. One can see JP is indeed an order ideal
by the criterion in Proposition 3.12. Moreover, it is straightforward to check that
JP is balanced and the correspondence takes JP to P . Hence the map is onto.
   Conversely, if a balanced order ideal J maps to P , then hJ (i) − hJ (0) = pi .
Since |hJ (0) + hJ (2k + 1)| = 1, it follows that hJ (i) = hJP (i) for all 0 ≤ i ≤ 2k + 1.
Proposition 3.12 implies that J = JP , completing the proof.                            
