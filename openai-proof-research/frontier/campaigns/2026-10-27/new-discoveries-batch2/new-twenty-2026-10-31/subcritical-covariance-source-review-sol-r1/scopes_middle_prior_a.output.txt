350979 Let $n$ be an integer, and let a **circulant graph** mean a graph whose vertices are arranged cyclically with adjacency determined by a fixed set of cyclic differences. The monophonic position number is denoted by $a$, and the diameter by $D$. **Circulant graph conjecture.** For any $n\geq 11$, there is a circulant graph with order $n$, monophonic position number $a=2$, and diameter $D=2$. The preceding theorem establishes existence of some graph with these parameters if and only if $n\in\{3,4,5,8\}$ or $n\geq 11$; the conjecture strengthens the large-order existence assertion by requiring the graph to be circulant, based on computational evidence.
352253 Let $G$ be a doubled 3-regular graph. Perform the YY-$\Delta$ transformation on all wyes in all possible ways to obtain a graph $G'$ of smallest size. A **chain of 3-cycles** is a chain of one or more 3-cycles, each connected by a vertex that is also doubly adjacent to one more vertex, with the 3-cycles at either end having one vertex with exactly 4 neighbours and no connections of size greater than 2.

**Minimality criterion conjecture.** The graph $G'$ is a minimal graph if and only if it does not contain such a chain of 3-cycles.

The conjecture proposes that, for doubled 3-regular graphs, checking the more restricted subgraphs illustrated in the first column of the paper's figure suffices to determine minimality. The source gives examples and discusses related reducible subgraphs, but provides no proof or resolution.
352496 Let $M_n$ be the monoid of rook diagrams and let $\mathbf{0}$ denote its zero element. Let $\psi(n)$ be the number of nonzero products arising from $M_n\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$.

**Polynexus conjecture.** For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $\psi(n)$ equals the $(n+1)$th Polynexus number of order $7$, namely

$$
\psi(n)=\frac{(n+1)^7-n^7-(n+1)^3+n^3}{120}.
$$

The formula was suggested by numerical evaluation of the sextuple sum defining $\psi(n)$ and comparison with the Polynexus numbers. An analytic evaluation of that sum was not available in the source, so the conjecture remains open here.
352827 Let $k$ and $r$ be integers with $2\leq r\leq k-2$, and let $D(G)$ denote the diameter of a graph $G$. For $n=2k+r$, write $\operatorname{SG}(n,k)$ for the Schrijver graph. **Unit-gap conjecture.** If $2\leq r\leq k-2$, then

$$
D\left(\operatorname{SG}(2k+r,k)\right)-D\left(\operatorname{SG}(2k+r+1,k)\right)\in\\{0,1\\}.
$$

The conjecture concerns the possibility of larger drops in the diameter as $r$ increases. It is based on computations and is open; the paper notes that the analogous differences are in $\\{0,1\\}$ for $k\leq 5$, while a larger gap occurs at the transition from $r=1$ to $r=2$ when $k\geq 6$.
352972 Let $G$ be a flat graph, and let $c_i(G)$ denote the number of $i$-cliques in $G$. Define

$$
D_{clique}(G)=\left|\sum_{i:\ i\ \mathrm{is\ odd}}c_i(G)-\sum_{i:\ i\ \mathrm{is\ even}}c_i(G)\right|.
$$

Call $G$ **balanced** when $D_{clique}(G)\leq 1$.

**Balanced-graph conjecture.** Any flat graph is a balanced graph.

The source notes that related subclasses of flat graphs satisfy $D_{clique}(G)=1$. The supplied text gives no resolution of the conjecture, so it remains open.
353203 Let $K_{s,t}$ be the complete bipartite graph with part sizes $s$ and $t$, and let $\mathscr{PD}^{TAR}(K_{s,t})$ denote its power domination reconfiguration graph under token addition/removal reconfiguration. **Uniqueness conjecture.** $\mathscr{PD}^{TAR}(K_{s,t})$ is unique if and only if $s\ne 2$ and $t\ne 2$. This is supported by the uniqueness results for $K_{1,t}$ with $t\ge 3$ and $K_{3,3}$, together with Sage computations for $s\in\{3,4\}$ and $t\in\{3,4,5\}$; the conjecture proposes the complete characterization, including the cases not covered by those results.
354228 Let $T$ be a complete full $t$-ary tree, meaning a rooted tree in which every nonleaf has exactly $t$ children and all leaves have the same depth. Let $l$ be the number of leaves and let $\chi_{la}(T)$ be the local antimagic chromatic number. **Even-arity complete full tree conjecture.** For every even integer $t\geq2$,

$$
\chi_{la}(T)=l+1.
$$

The paper explicitly states this conjecture as open; it would sharpen the established upper bound $\chi_{la}(T)\leq l+2$, while the equality is proved in the paper for odd $t$.
355140 Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices, with minimum degree $\delta(G)$. The S-game and D-game parameters are denoted by $\gamma_{\rm SMB}'(G)$ and $\gamma_{\rm SMB}(G)$, respectively.

**Improved SMBD-number bounds.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(i) If }\gamma_{\rm SMB}'(G)<\infty,\text{ then }\gamma_{\rm SMB}'(G)\le \left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil-\delta(G)+1;\\
&\text{(ii) If }\gamma_{\rm SMB}(G)<\infty,\text{ then }\gamma_{\rm SMB}(G)\le \left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-\delta(G)+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

These bounds would improve the known sharp general upper bounds when the minimum degree is at least two; the authors note that sharp examples under this degree condition are not known.
355217 Let $G$ be a graph of order $n\ge 2$. The **strict middle-graph increase conjecture** asserts that

$$
\gamma{\tilde{}} _{c}(G) < \gamma{\tilde{}} _{c}(M(G)).
$$

The paper notes that this inequality holds for trees and for several standard graph families, including complete graphs, wheels, friendship graphs, complete bipartite graphs, and cycles. Whether it holds for every graph of order at least two remains open.
355956 Let $d>1$ be an integer. Let $q \equiv 1 \pmod {2d}$ be a power of a prime $p$, and let $r$ be the largest integer such that $d \mid \frac{q-1}{p^r-1}$. The subfield $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$ is a clique in the generalized Paley graph $GP(q,d)=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb{F}_q^+;(\mathbb{F}_q^*)^d)$. **Yip's maximal subfield clique conjecture.** The subfield $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$ forms a maximal clique in $GP(q,d)$. This conjecture asserts that the largest subfield producing a subfield clique cannot be extended to a larger clique. The paper resolves it in a stronger form under a sufficient-size hypothesis, while the unrestricted statement is the conjecture being addressed.
356266 Let $S_n$ be the set of permutations of $[n]$, and let $\mathcal{O}(n)$ be the clustered graph of overlapping $n$-permutations: its edges are the permutations in $S_n$, and consecutive permutations overlap in their last and first $n-1$ entries up to reduction. **Hurlbert's ordering conjecture.** Given any Euler tour of the clustered graph of overlapping permutations (that is, an ordering of $S_n$ such that each consecutive pair of permutations overlaps), there is a universal cycle for permutations that covers $S_n$ in the same order. Universal cycles correspond to Euler tours in this graph, but it remains open whether every such ordering can be realized by a universal cycle.
356756 Let $p\geq 13$ be a prime with $p\equiv 1\bmod 4$, and let $n$ be an integer divisible by $(p+3)/2$. For a digit set $D\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_p$, write $S(D,n)$ for the set of vectors in $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$ in which every digit of $D$ occurs the same number of times. Define

$$
D=\left\{0,1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p+3}{2}\right\}.
$$

**Prime-modulus progression-free-set conjecture.** The set $S(D,n)$ does not contain any arithmetic progression of length $4$.

This conjecture extends the progression-free constructions for odd progression lengths to a case not covered by the stated theorem, motivated by computations with small primes. Its status is unresolved in the source.
