# Independent ordinary proof: paid fourth-moment helper, target 317413

This proof was reconstructed before opening the author's full proposal; see independent-checkpoint.txt. It proves a helper and a conditional covariance bridge, not a cleared literal theorem. No numerical experiment, solver or formal tool is used.

For every fixed integer k>=1 there is C_k<infinity such that for every n>=1 and 0<=p<=1/(2n),
E|S_k(G(n,p))-E S_k(G(n,p))|^4 <= C_k n^2.
Here S_k is exactly sum over components of their sizes to power k, source (1.2). Indices in the covariance conjecture are k,l>=2, with no upper bound and no uniformity in k,l asserted.

Partition edges into independent rows R_i={(i,j):j>i}, i=1,...,n. In G^i replace only R_i by an independent copy. In H_i=G union G^i let C_i be the component containing i and s=|C_i|. Every changed edge touches i; outside C_i the graphs and their components agree. Both graphs partition C_i. For any partition of a set of size s and integer k>=1, the sum of kth powers of block sizes is between s and s^k: the upper bound follows repeatedly from a^k+b^k<=(a+b)^k. Thus |S_k(G)-S_k(G^i)|<=s^k. There is no factor two: both numbers belong to the SAME interval. For k=1 they are equal.

All distinct H_i edge indicators remain independent; edges not touching i have probability p, and root edges have probability p or 2p-p^2<=2p. Explore i first, then breadth first. At root, root-child count is dominated by Bin(n,2p). When a nonroot is processed, its edges to i are already tested, and every still untested edge to an unseen vertex is a distinct independent Bernoulli(p), even conditioned on all exploration so far. Never retest a tested failed edge. Pad each fresh list to n with new independent fictitious Bernoulli trials, assigned at this step only. At root, a uniform coupling first increases its individual probabilities to 2p, then pads. Conditional on the past each padded count has the same binomial law; hence successive padded counts are independent. Discovered collisions and edges to active vertices cannot increase the real queue. Coupling queue exploration gives domination by a tree with root offspring Bin(n,2p), all other offspring independently Bin(n,p). This pays adaptive conditioning, rather than assuming a random explored set is independent of its edges.

Let a=11/10 and theta=1/100. For ordinary progeny truncated at depth h, induction gives E exp(theta T_h)<=a: base e^theta<a, and
 e^theta E a^Bin(n,p) <= exp(theta+np(a-1)) <= exp(3/50)<a.
Indeed log(a)=integral_1^a du/u >=1/11>3/50. Monotone convergence bounds total progeny, including extinction, by the same exponential moment. With exceptional root, E exp(theta T)<=exp(theta+2np(a-1))<=exp(11/100)<2 (use log2>=1/2). Therefore E s^m <=2 m! theta^(-m), using the mth term of the exponential series. Put K_k=(2(4k)!100^(4k))^(1/4). This bounds every row-difference L4 norm. The empty last row, n=1 and p=0 cause no exception; when k=1 the statistic is deterministic.

Set F_i=sigma(R_1,...,R_i), M_i=E[Z|F_i]-EZ, D_i=M_i-M_(i-1). Conditional on F_i, integrating the independent replacement row and the future rows gives E[Z^i|F_i]=E[Z|F_(i-1)]. Thus D_i=E[Z-Z^i|F_i]. Conditional Jensen bounds ||D_i||4<=K_k. All variables are bounded for each n, so no integrability premise is omitted.

Writing A_i=E M_i^4, expansion gives
 A_i<=A_(i-1)+6 A_(i-1)^(1/2)K^2+4 A_(i-1)^(1/4)K^3+K^4.
Only E[M_(i-1)^3 D_i] vanishes by the martingale property. E[M_(i-1)D_i^3] need not vanish; its absolute value is bounded by Holder. The other mixed term uses Holder too, without a conditional variance assumption. If A_m<=100K^4 m^2, the next increment is <=K^4(60m+4sqrt(10)sqrt(m)+1). At m=0 the bound is immediate. For integer m>=1 use sqrt(m)<=m and sqrt(10)<4 to bound it by K^4(76m+1)<=100K^4(2m+1). Induction yields A_n<=100K^4 n^2. This establishes the stated theorem with C_k=100K_k^4. K=0 is also immediate. Row grouping, rather than individual edges, is essential to this estimate.

# Exact extent of the covariance bridge

Fix arbitrary c in (0,1/2), t=c/n, x=1/(1-c). Then p=1-exp(-c/n)<=c/n<=1/(2n), n^2t=cn tends to infinity and n(1-nt)^3=n(1-c)^3 tends to infinity. These are the actual conditions of Lemma5.8/Theorem5.9, not merely a near-critical nt->1 regime. Source3.4 gives ES_k=np_k(x)+O(1); source6.1 gives Cov(S_k,S_l)/n=P_(k,l)(x)+O(1/n).

Define W_(n,k)=(S_k-ES_k)/sqrt(n). The proved bound gives sup E|W_(n,k)|^4<infinity for each fixed k. Any finite vector W_n converging in distribution to a Gaussian V consequently has mixed moments converging: E(W_k W_l)^2<=sqrt(E W_k^4 E W_l^4), so product tails in L1 are bounded by a constant/R. Apply distributional convergence to the bounded continuous clipped product max(-R,min(R,z_k z_l)), then let R tend to infinity; the Gaussian tails are integrable. First moments pass similarly. Centering instead by np_k(x) changes W by O(n^(-1/2)) and preserves all these bounds. Thus EVERY such actual Gaussian limit has E V_k V_l=P_(k,l)(x). No equality of the two covariance polynomials was assumed to prove UI or the covariance calculation.

For clarity, the source's intended joint limit construction can be tracked without computing its covariances: the cross version of (5.1) has drift
 B_(k,l)(s)=1/2 sum_(a=1..k-1,b=1..l-1) binom(k,a)binom(l,b) p_(a+b+1)(x_s) p_(k+l+1-a-b)(x_s), x_s=(1-s)^(-1).
For the tilde martingales, multiply this by (1-s)^(k+l). Lemmas5.2-5.4 extended to a fixed finite vector, with scaled time s=nu, give the bracket limit integral_0^s (1-v)^(k+l)B_(k,l)(v)dv after dividing each tilde martingale by sqrt(n). The fourth-jump/bracket-error estimate (5.7)-(5.9) tends to zero on a fixed compact s<=c<1. Proposition5.5 and Remark5.6 then supply the continuous joint Gaussian martingale M_k. Its external proof [9] was not read; it is an attributed source dependency, not a newly independently verified probability theorem.

Use (5.21) successively on that finite vector: the limiting centered coordinate V_k(s) is
 (1-s)^(-k) M_k(s) + (1-s)^(-k) sum_(j=2..k-1) binom(k,j-1) integral_0^s (1-v)^k p_(k+2-j)(x_v)V_j(v)dv.
At k=2 the sum is empty. On s<=c<1 all coefficients are bounded; the source remainder is O_L1(1)/sqrt(n), and the recursive integrations preserve vanishing L1 errors. A finite recursive collection of continuous linear integral maps of a continuous Gaussian martingale is jointly Gaussian; no diagonal-only inference or assumption that individually Gaussian variables are jointly Gaussian is used. This identifies the OPERATIONAL limit covariance. It does not by itself authenticate every displayed definition of the named hat polynomials in5.10. The source's all-index computation there is sketchy, and I do not adopt its conflicting displays as consistent axioms.

If Q_(k,l)(x) are independently bound polynomials describing those very sqrt(n)-normalized covariances for every c in (0,1/2), the preceding UI argument proves Q_(k,l)(x)=P_(k,l)(x) throughout (1,2). Polynomial division shows a nonzero polynomial has finitely many roots, so the equality holds identically for every fixed pair. This proves the full all-index implication, conditional ONLY on that source binding, and not merely diagonal equality or a single c.
