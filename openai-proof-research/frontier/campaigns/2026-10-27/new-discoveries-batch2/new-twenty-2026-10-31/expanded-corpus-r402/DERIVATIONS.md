## 347363: two independent exact refutation mechanisms, publicly resolved

Source: Torres, arXiv:2007.13611v2, pp.5–6,23. Graphs are finite, simple, undirected, connected, and have at least two vertices. The 1-shell is the induced graph outside the 2-core. His matrix convention is B_{k→l,i→j}=δ_{jk}(1−δ_{il}). The conjecture is diagonalizability iff empty 1-shell. It is enough to exhibit a connected minimum-degree-two graph with a nonzero Jordan chain. Repeated characteristic roots alone would not suffice: a repeated eigenvalue can be semisimple. That shortcut was rejected.

First, an ordinary construction derived here without enumeration: let G=C6 □ K4. Vertices are pairs (i,j), i mod 6 and j in {1,2,3,4}. Edges either join cyclic neighbors with the same j, or distinct j with the same i. These edge types are disjoint, with two and three neighbors respectively. Thus G is simple, connected, 5-regular, has 24 vertices and 60 edges, and equals its 2-core. Define f(i,j)=c_i with c=(2,1,−1,−2,−1,1). The two cyclic neighbor values sum to c_i at every index: at successive indices these sums are 2,1,−1,−2,−1,1. The three clique neighbors contribute 3c_i, hence Af=4f. This six-entry verification is ordinary mathematics, not a program or numerical search.

On directed edges define a_{u→v}=f(u) and b_{u→v}=f(v). Directly from Torres's convention,
(Ba)_{k→l}=Σ_{i∼k,i≠l} f(i)=4f(k)−f(l),
(Bb)_{k→l}=Σ_{i∼k,i≠l} f(k)=4f(k).
Consequently Ba=4a−b and Bb=4a. Put v=−2a+b and g=−a. Then Bv=2v and (B−2I)g=v. On a clique edge at cyclic index 0, f(u)=f(v)=2, so v_{u→v}=−2≠0. Thus g is a generalized eigenvector of rank two. If B were diagonalizable over C, in a diagonalizing basis (B−2I)^2g=0 would force (B−2I)g=0 coordinatewise, a contradiction. This pays the empty-shell and genuine defectiveness conditions, and refutes the literal equivalence in its source domain. The 120 directed edges need not be enumerated.

## 347549: exact all-n telescoping route; already public

Source definition: La Croix–Roby arXiv:2008.03292v1 pp.1–3,14. F deletes parentheses from cycles whose maxima occur first and increase from cycle to cycle. R reverses one-line order. Q² sends w(i) to n+1−w(n+1−i). The action is τ=Q² F^{-1} R F. The source asserts Fix is 1-mesic on each τ-orbit in S_n, n≥1. Replacing this by the global uniform average over S_n would not establish orbit homomesy, and importing the reversal-inversion heap recurrence would change the action. Both shortcuts were rejected.

An exact route works through a coboundary, not orbit-size formulas. Write e(w),a(w),h(w) for excedances, antiexcedances, and fixed points. For any permutation, e+a+h=n. In the canonical cycles every antiexcedance u maps to a smaller successor inside its cycle, since the closing successor is the cycle maximum and cannot be smaller. Deleting parentheses sends these antiexcedances bijectively to descents of F(w). A cycle boundary is an ascent because the next cycle begins with a new maximum. Hence des(F(w))=a(w). Therefore asc(F(w))=n−1−a(w)=e(w)+h(w)−1.

Rotation exchanges excedances with antiexcedances via index i↔n+1−i. Reversal exchanges descents with ascents via adjacent-position reversal. Thus
 e(τw)=a(F^{-1}RF(w))=des(RF(w))=asc(F(w))=e(w)+h(w)−1.
Rearrange h(w)−1=e(τw)−e(w). Summing around each finite orbit cancels every excedance term, so Σ h=|orbit|. This proves the precise all-n orbit average. n=1 is included: both excedance counts are zero and h=1.

This is already Peter Mao, Foatic Homomesy, arXiv:2610.40882v1 (header: October 29 2026, manuscript date November 2 2026), Theorem 3.1(3). Read its exact statement p.7, telescoping setup p.11 and full relevant lemmas/proofs pp.12–14. The identity above is its Equation (2), with Lemmas 5.1–5.4. The earlier abstract's number of conjectural cases is immaterial; exact operators match. The current public proof predates this lane, even though it postdates the 2020 target. This is a known public resolution, zero credit. No worldwide novelty clearance is inferred.
