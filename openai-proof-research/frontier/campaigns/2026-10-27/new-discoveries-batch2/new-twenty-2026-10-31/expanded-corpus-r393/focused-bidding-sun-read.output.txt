    So, extending any permutation of length (k − 1)(ℓ − 1) that avoid in-
creasing and decreasing subsequences of lengths k and ℓ, respectively, by an
extra element to the left, or to the right, results in an occurrence of a mono-
tone subsequence in question. However, depending on k and ℓ, there may
be permutations of smaller lengths that have the same properties, namely,
that avoid the monotone subsequences in question but that do not admit
extensions to the right and/or to the left without introducing an unwanted
monotone subsequence. Such permutations, being of significant combinato-
rial interest, have not received any attention in the literature, and the main
goal of this paper is to fill in this gap.
    In fact, our studies in this paper belong to a much wider context. The
idea of a crucial object appearing, for example, in Combinatorics on Words
and Graph Theory, can be described as follows. Given a set of prohibitions
P , we consider the set S of all objects that avoid P , namely, the set of all
objects that do not contain elements of P as subobjects. Then, a crucial
object s ∈ S is an object that cannot be extended, in a specified way, to a
larger object in S. In other words, any attempt to enlarge s in the specified
way will necessarily introduce a prohibited subobject in P .

of prohibitions is increasing subsequences of length k and decreasing subse-
