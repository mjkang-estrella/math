                                                                                             Abstract

                                                         A path (cycle) is properly-colored if consecutive edges are of distinct colors. In 1997,
                                                     Bang-Jensen and Gutin conjectured a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence
                                                     of a Hamilton path in an edge-colored complete graph. This conjecture, confirmed by
                                                     Feng, Giesen, Guo, Gutin, Jensen and Rafley in 2006, was laterly playing an important
                                                     role in Lo’s asymptotical proof of Bollobás-Erdős’ conjecture on properly-colored Hamil-
                                                     ton cycles. In 1997, Yeo obtained a structural characterization of edge-colored graphs
                                                     that containing no properly colored cycles. This result is a fundamental tool in the study
                                                     of edge-colored graphs. In this paper, we first give a much shorter proof of the Bang-
                                                     Jensen-Gutin Conjecture by two novel absorbing lemmas. We also prove a new sufficient
                                                     condition for the existence of a properly-colored cycle and then deduce Yeo’s theorem
                                                     from this result and a closure concept in edge-colored graphs.



                                           1        Introduction
                                                   A graph G is called an edge-colored graph if each edge e is assigned a color colG (e) (not
                                           necessarily distinct). Denote by col(G) the set of colors assigned on E(G). For a vertex v and
                                           a vertex set S, we use colG (v, S) to denote the colors assigned to edges between v and S − v in
                                           G. In particular, define NGc (v) = colG (v, V (G) \ {v}). The subscript G is sometimes omitted
                                           when there is no ambiguity. An edge-colored graph G is called k-colored if |col(G)| = k and
                                           properly colored (or PC) if each pair of adjacent edges are of distinct colors. For terminology
                                           and notations not defined here, we refer the reader to [5].
                                                   Hamilton problem is one of the most attractive problem in graph theory, not only on
                                           structural analysis but also on algorithmic complexity. For undirected graphs, Hamiltonian
                                           property and related properties, such as traceability and pancyclicity, are widely studied. As a
                                           result, many classical methods are found or well used. For instance, Posá’s rotation-extension
                                           method, closure method and Menger’s theorem.

      For Hamilton problem in digraphs, even refining to multipartite tournaments, there are
many unsolved questions (see the survey paper of Volkmann [25]). One could naturally ask
what can be said about the Hamiltonian property of a highly connected multipartite tourna-
ment containing a cycle factor. Bang-Jensen, Gutin and Huang [2] showed that there is no
constant k such that every k-connected multipartite tournament with a cycle factor contains
a Hamilton cycle. However, for Hamilton path, Gutin [16] proved that every multipartite
tournament with a directed 1-path-cycle factor, i.e. a spanning subgraph which is a union of
a directed path and a number of directed cycles, contains a directed Hamilton path.
      Given a multipartite tournament Tp with partite sets V1 , V2 , . . . , Vp , define an edge-colored
complete graph K with V (K) = V (Tp ) and for each edge e = uv, if u, v ∈ Vi or u ∈ Vi , v ∈ Vj
and uv ∈ A(Tp ), then assign color i to e. Li et al. [20] showed that each PC cycle in K is a
directed cycle in Tp and vice versa. In this sense, the class of edge-colored complete graphs
generalizes the class of multipartite tournaments. Therefore, the relation between PC cycle
factors and PC Hamilton properties was studied. Bang-Jensen, Gutin and Yeo [3] proved
that a PC cycle factor in an edge-colored Kn guarantees a PC Hamilton path. Bang-Jensen
and Gutin [1] further conjectured that a PC 1-path-cycle factor, i.e. a PC spanning subgraph
which is a union of a PC path and a number of PC cycles, in an edge-colored Kn can also
guarantee a PC Hamilton path1 . This conjecture was confirmed by Feng et al. [11] in 2006.

Theorem 1 (Feng, Giesen, Guo, Gutin, Jensen and Rafiey [11]). Let {P, C1 , C2 , . . . , Ck } be
a PC 1-path-cycle factor of an edge-colored complete graph G such that P is a PC path and
Ci is a PC cycle for 1 ≤ i ≤ k. Then G contains a PC Hamilton path.

      One important open problem on PC cycles is the Bollobás-Erdős Conjecture. Let Knc be
an edge-colored Kn and ∆mon (Knc ) be the maximum number of edges with the same color
which are incident to the same vertex. Confirming a conjecture proposed by Daykin [10],
                                                      n
Bollobás and Erdős [6] showed that if ∆mon (Knc ) ≤ 69 then Knc contains a PC Hamilton
cycle. In the same paper, Bollobás and Erdős [6] conjectured that the weaker condition that
∆mon (Knc ) < ⌊ n2 ⌋ suffices for the existence of PC Hamilton cycle. In 2016, Lo [23] confirmed
the conjecture asymptotically by proving that for any ε > 0, every Knc with ∆mon (Knc ) ≤
( 21 − ε)n contains a PC Hamilton cycle, provided n is sufficiently large. One main tool of Lo’s
proof is Theorem 1. For problems and results related to Theorem 1, see [7, 9].
      In this article, we shall present a much shorter proof of Bang-Jensen-Gutin conjecture by
proving the following theorem, which is Claim A in Feng et al.’s proof [11]. Note that to
extend a PC path, the end-vertices, starting and ending colors are important. Hence we define
the character of a PC path P = u1 u2 · · · um as ch(P ) = [u1 , col(u1 u2 ), col(um−1 um ), um ].
We regard [u1 , col(u1 u2 ), col(um−1 um ), um ] and [um , col(um−1 um ), col(u1 u2 ), u1 ] as the same
object. So each PC path has a unique character.

Theorem 2 (Claim A in [11]). Let {P, C} be a 1-path-cycle factor of an edge-colored Knc such
that P is a PC path and C is a PC cycle. If ch(P ) = [u1 , x, y, um ] (m ≥ 2), col(u1 , V (C)) =
{x} and col(um , V (C)) = {y}. Then G contains a PC Hamilton path Q with ch(Q) = ch(P ).

    Another natural problem is to find the sufficient sharp color degree condition for the
existence of a PC cycle in an edge-colored graph. In 1997, Yeo [27] proved a local character-
ization, which extended a previous theorem of Grossman and Häggkvist [15] to edge-colored
graphs of arbitrary number of colors by a quite technical method.

Theorem 3 (Yeo [27]). Let G be an edge-colored graph containing no PC cycle. Then there
exists a vertex z ∈ V (G) such that no component of G − z is joint to z with edges of more
than one color.

   With aid of Yeo’s theorem, Fujita, the first author, and Zhang [13] proved that for an
edge-colored graph G with minv∈V (G) |NGc (v)| ≥ D, if D! D    1
                                                         P
                                                           i=0 i! > n then G contains a PC
cycle. Furthermore, the bound is best possible. Besides, Yeo’s theorem is frequently used not
only on the existence of PC cycles (see the survey paper [1] and recent results [8, 13, 17, 19])
but also on other topics, such as PC trees [7], decomposition of edge-colored graphs [12],
Ramsey numbers [24] and matchings [26].
    We present a new sufficient condition for the existence of a PC cycle in an edge-colored
graph.

Theorem 4. Let G be an edge-colored graph. If for each vertex v, there exists a vertex
v ′ and two internally vertex-disjoint PC paths P and Q such that ch(P ) = [v, α1 , β1 , v ′ ],
ch(Q) = [v, α2 , β2 , v ′ ] and α1 6= α2 , then G contains a PC cycle.

    By introducing a new concept of closure for properly-colored cycles in edge-colored graphs,
we will give a more intuitive and slightly shorter proof of Theorem 3 from Theorem 4.

   The original proof of Theorem 2 in [11] is by induction on 2|C| + |P | and reducing the
cases into |C| ∈ {3, 5}. Our proof adheres the proof structure and many notations, reducing
the cases into |C| = 3. New lemmas dramatically shorten the induction process. Now we
give the new proof of Theorem 2 as following.

Proof of Theorem 2. Let P = u1 u2 · · · um and C = v1 v2 · · · vn v1 . Then col(u1 u2 ) = x and
col(um−1 um ) = y. Suppose that (P, C) is a counterexample such that 2|C| + |P | is as small
as possible.

Claim 1. For each edge vv ′ ∈ E(C), the following statements hold:
(a) there is a PC path Q with V (P ) = V (Q) ∪ {v, v ′ } and ch(Q) = ch(P );
(b) G[V (C) \ {v, v ′ }] contains no spanning PC cycle.

Proof. Lemma 2 implies the existence of Q in (i). Suppose to the contrary that H is a
spanning PC cycle of G[V (C) \ {v, v ′ }]. Since 2|H| + |Q| < 2|C| + |P |, there must be a PC
path Q′ on V (H) ∪ V (Q) = V (C) ∪ V (P ) with ch(Q′ ) = ch(Q) = ch(P ), a contradiction.

Claim 2. If col(vi vi+1 ) = col(vj vj+1 ) = a, then col(vi vj+1 ) 6= a and col(vj vi+1 ) 6= a.

Proof. If vi vj+1 ∈ E(C) or vj vi+1 ∈ E(C), then Claim 1 and the fact that C is a PC cycle
imply the statement. Now assume that vi vj+1 6∈ E(C) and vj vi+1 6∈ E(C). By symmetry, it
suffices to prove that col(vj vi+1 ) 6= a. Suppose not. Then C ′ = vi+1 vi+2 · · · vj vi+1 is a PC
cycle. Let H be the cycle vj+1 vj+2 · · · vi vj+1 with colors of edges first adhered from that in G
and then recoloring the edge vi vj+1 by color a. Obviously, H is a PC cycle and P can absorb
V (H) with Q as a resulting PC path satisfying that ch(Q) = ch(P ). If this new colored
edge vi vj+1 is contained in Q, then replace the segment vi vj+1 in Q by vi vi+1 · · · vj vj+1 , we
obtain a desired PC path, a contradiction. Hence Q must be an edge-colored subgraph of
G. Consider the pair (C ′ , Q). Since 2|C ′ | + |Q| < 2|C| + P , there must be a PC path Q′ on
V (Q) ∪ V (C ′ ) = V (G) with ch(Q′ ) = ch(Q) = ch(P ), a contradiction.

Definition 1 (A closure for the existence of PC cycles). Let G be an edge-colored graph
with no PC cycle. If joining two nonadjacent vertices u and v in G by an edge e of a certain
color will not produce any PC cycle in G + e, then replace G by G + e. Repeat this process
until no more edges can be added. Then we say the resulting edge-colored graph is a closure
of G.

Proof of Theorem 3. Without loss of generality, assume that G contains no loops or mul-
tiple edges. Let H be a closure of G and let Nvi = {u ∈ NH (v) : colH (uv) = i}.
    If two vertices u and v are not adjacent in H, then by Definition 1, no matter what color
we assign to uv, the obtained edge-colored graph H + uv would contain a PC cycle. Thus
there are two PC paths P and Q from u to v with distinct starting colors. Let u′ be the vertex
in V (P ) ∩ V (Q) closest to u on P . Then uP u′ and uQu′ are two internally vertex-disjoint
PC paths from u to u′ with distinct starting colors in H.
    Since H contains no PC cycle, by Theorem 4 and the above analysis, there is a vertex
z ∈ V (H) such that z is adjacent to all the other vertices in H, and for each vertex w ∈
V (H) \ {z} and each PC path P from z to w, the starting color must be the same, namely
colH (zw). This implies that there is no edge between Nzi and Nzj for any distinct colors i
and j. Thus each component of H − z is joint to z with only one color. Recall that G is a
subgraph of H. So each component of G − z is joint to z with at most one color. The proof
is complete.


4       Concluding remarks
    As an analogue to Theorem 1, Cheng, Kano and Wang [9] conjectured the following.

Conjecture 1 (Cheng et al. [9]). Each edge-colored complete balanced bipartite graph Kn,n
contains a PC Hamilton path if it contains a PC 1-path-cycle factor.


                                                    10
    Note that Lemma 1 still holds when the host edge-colored graph is complete bipartite.
However, Lemma 2 is not applicable to edge-colored Kn,n in the present form.
    Let C1 and C2 be vertex-disjoint PC cycles in an edge-colored Kn . Looking at Theorem 1,
a natural question would be whether C1 and C2 can be emerged into one PC cycle. Assume
that there exists a coloring f : V (C1 ) → col(C1 ) such that col(uv) = f (u) or f (v) for all
distinct vertices u, v ∈ V (C1 ) and col(uw) = f (u) for all u ∈ V (C1 ) and w ∈ V (C2 ). It is
easy to check that there is no PC cycle C with V (C) = V (C1 ) ∪ V (C2 ). Such a vertex set
V (C1 ) was called “degenerate set” by the first author when studying the relationship between
edge-colored graphs and digraph (see [21, 22]). Bánkfalvi and Bánkfalvi [4] gave a necessary
and sufficient condition for the existence of a PC Hamilton cycle in a 2-colored Kn . Their
proof implies that the above construction is the only barrier for emerging C1 and C2 in a
2-colored Kn . For cases with at least 3 colors, the following small example containing no PC
cycle on V (C1 ) ∪ V (C2 ) implies the existence of other kinds of barriers.

Construction 1. C1 = x1 x2 x3 x1 , C2 = y1 y2 y3 y1 ; col(xi xj ) = i, col(yi yj ) = i and col(xi yj ) =
i for all i, j ∈ {1, 2, 3}; recolor the edge x2 y3 by color 1.

    An interesting problem might be stated as following.

Problem 1. Let C1 and C2 be vertex-disjoint PC cycles in a k-colored Kn (k ≥ 3). Charac-
terize the coloring structure when there is no PC cycle C satisfying V (C) = V (C1 ) ∪ V (C2 ).

    The characterization given by Bánkfalvi and Bánkfalvi [4] implies a polynomial algorithm
to check whether a 2-colored Kn contains a PC Hamilton cycle. In general, Gutin and Kim
[18] made the following conjecture. Solving Problem 1 would be a big step to studying
Conjecture 2.

Conjecture 2 (Gutin and Kim [18]). It is polynomial time checkable whether an edge-colored
complete graph contains a PC Hamilton cycle.

    Recently, Galeana-Sánchez, Rojas-Monroy, Sánchez-López and Imelda Villarreal-Valdés
[14] generalized Yeo’s Theorem to H-Cycles. Let G be an edge-colored graph and H be a
loopless graph with V (H) = col(G). A cycle C in G is called an H-cycle if for each pair of
consecutive edges e and f in C, the colors col(e) and col(f ) are adjacent in H. Particularly,
a PC cycle in G is an H-cycle with H being a complete graph on V (H) = col(G). By slightly
modifying Theorem 4 and the definition of closure, one can obtain a one-page proof of the
main theorem in [14].
