## Explicit subsumption tests and substantive significance

The strongest obvious reductions were tested against actual source proofs:

1. **Replication/complete-graph reduction fails to refute.** If each part has one constant vector, its vertex defect is exactly twice the corresponding K4 defect. Proposition 1 gives D(K4,3)=1, so every such coloring has maximum at least 2, and a replicated optimum attains 2. The witness necessarily leaves this entire ansatz. This is an exact separation, not merely the observation that k differs.
2. **Total-defect averaging does not furnish the witness.** Corollary 1 gives TD(K4,3)=2. Proposition 4(a) then gives total defect at least 8 for the eight-vertex blow-up, hence only maximum at least 1. Proposition 4(b) gives maximum at most 2. Neither inequality supplies a strict improvement. Minimizing total defect by recoloring to monochromatic vertices (Lemma 1) cannot be applied to minimize the maximum: one vertex's defect can increase while the total decreases.
3. **Two colors and divisible part counts do not transfer the endpoint.** Proposition 5 assumes 3 divides m, false here. Taking a subgraph of a known divisible example gives only that example's upper bound; the smallest larger multiple m=6 gives 2 at a=2, without strictness. Proposition 6's proof uses one scalar r, the total ordering r(u)≤r(v), complement 1−r, and the identity overlap=1−|r(u)−r(v)|. Three coordinates have no corresponding common scalar ordering. Reserving a third color for whole parts and applying the two-color theorem to three remaining parts again gives defect 2. No strict bound follows.
4. **Classical monochromatic balancing does not refute.** With eight vertices and three pure colors, some color appears at least three times. Since a part holds at most two vertices, among those three some vertex has at least two same-color neighbors. Every pure coloring therefore has maximum at least 2. The new effect is fractional balancing across heterogeneous independent twins, not a better ordinary coloring.
5. **Other author constructions do not supply the missing bridge.** Fan/wheel constructions and the rooks-graph upper bounds are graph-specific and do not automatically extend across the additional edges of this complete multipartite graph. The series-parallel companion counts neighbors with any intersection of q-color sets, rather than their normalized overlaps; moreover it excludes K4 minors, while this graph contains a K4 by taking one vertex from each part. Its Theorems 3, 5, 7–8 and explicit constructions give no full-scope reduction to the present refutation.
