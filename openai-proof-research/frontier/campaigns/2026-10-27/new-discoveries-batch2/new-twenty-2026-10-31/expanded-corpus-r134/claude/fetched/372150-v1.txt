An Induced A-Path Theorem

arXiv:2512.17232v1 [math.CO] 19 Dec 2025

Robert Hickingbotham ∗

Gwenaël Joret ∗

Abstract
Given a graph G and A ⊆ V (G), a classical theorem of Gallai (1964) states that
for every positive integer k, the graph G contains k pairwise vertex-disjoint A-paths,
or a set Z ⊆ V (G) of size at most 2(k − 1) such that G − Z contains no A-paths.
We generalise Gallai’s theorem to the induced setting: We prove that G contains k
pairwise anti-complete A-paths, or a set Z of size at most 78(k − 1) such that, after
removing the closed neighbourhood of Z, the resulting graph has no A-path. Here,
two paths are anti-complete if they are vertex disjoint and there is no edge in G
having one endpoint in each of them.
We further show that the bound 78(k − 1) on the size of Z can be reduced to
4(k − 1) if one removes the balls of radius 4 around the vertices of Z (instead of
radius 1), which is within a factor 2 of optimal. We also establish analogous results
for long induced A-paths.

Introduction

Let G be a graph and A ⊆ V (G). An A-path in G is a path with at least one edge whose
endpoints belong to A. The classic A-path theorem of Gallai [12] states that for every
k ∈ N, G contains k pairwise vertex-disjoint A-paths, or there exists a set Z ⊆ V (G) with
size at most 2k − 2 such that G − Z contains no A-paths. This min-max theorem was a
key ingredient in the original proof of the celebrated Erdős-Pósa theorem [11]: for every
k ∈ N, every graph G contains k vertex disjoint cycles or a set X with |X| ∈ O(k log(k))
such that X intersect all cycles in G. This result initiated a long line of research into other
so-called Erdős-Pósa type theorems; see [22] for a survey.
In recent years, attention has turned to coarse Erdős-Pósa type theorems, where ‘disjoint’
is replaced with ‘far apart’ [1, 10]. This direction is motivated by the emerging field
of coarse graph theory, which seeks to understand the large-scale geometry of graphs.

One of the central questions in this area is the Coarse Menger Conjecture, independently
posed by Albrechtsen, Huynh, Jacobs, Knappe, and Wollan [3] and Georgakopoulos
and Papasoglu [14]. For a graph G, X ⊆ V (G), and r ∈ N, let NG [X, r] := {v ∈
V (G) : distG (X, v) ⩽ r} and NG [X] := NG [X, 1].
Conjecture 1 ([3, 14]). For all k, d ∈ N, there exists c ∈ N such that every graph G and
X, Y ⊆ V (G) contains one of the following:
(i) k (X, Y )-paths P1 , . . . , Pk such that distG (Pi , Pj ) ⩾ d for all distinct i, j ∈ [k]; or
(ii) a set Z ⊆ V (G) with |Z| ⩽ k − 1 such that G − NG [Z, cd] has no (X, Y )-path.
Unfortunately, Nguyen, Scott, and Seymour [21] showed that Conjecture 1 is false for
all d ⩾ 3. Furthermore, the same set of authors more recently showed that it remains
false even when the number of balls is allowed to depend on a function of k [18]. See
[3, 13–15, 19, 20] for positive resolution of special cases of Conjecture 1.
In many settings, when a Menger-type theorem fails, the corresponding A-path version
holds. This phenomenon has been particularly fruitful in the study of group-labelled
graphs [4–6, 25, 26]. Motivated by this, Geelen proposed a Coarse Gallai Conjecture.1
Conjecture 2 (Geelen 2024). There exist functions f, g such that, for all k, d ∈ N, every
graph G and vertex set A ⊆ V (G) contains one of the following:
(i) k A-paths P1 , . . . , Pk such that distG (Pi , Pj ) ⩾ d for all distinct i, j ∈ [k]; or
(ii) a set Z ⊆ V (G) with |Z| ⩽ f (k) such that G − NG [Z, g(d)] has no A-path.
This paper resolves the d = 2 case of Geelen’s conjecture. For a graph G, two sets
X, Y ⊆ V (G) are anti-complete if X ∩ Y = ∅ and there is no edge in G with one end in
X, the other in Y .
Theorem 3. For every k ∈ N, every graph G and A ⊆ V (G), G contains k pairwise
anti-complete A-paths, or there exists a set Z ⊆ V (G) with |Z| ⩽ 78(k − 1) such that
G − NG [Z] has no A-paths.
Theorem 3 marks a positive development for coarse graph theory, offsetting the recent
trend of counterexamples in the area [2, 8, 18, 21]. The use of radius-1 balls is best possible:
the graph Kn with A = V (Kn ) contains neither 2 pairwise anti-complete A-path nor a set
Z ⊆ V (Kn ) with |Z| ⩽ n − 2 such that Kn − Z has no A-path.
In addition to Theorem 3, we prove the following induced A-path theorem, which reduces
the number of balls to nearly optimal at the expense of larger ball radii.

The number of balls in Theorem 4 is at most a factor of two from optimal. To see this,
let r ∈ N and G be the graph obtained from the complete graph on 2k − 1 vertices by
replacing each edge with a path of length 3r. Let A be the set of high-degree vertices of
G. Then G neither contains k pairwise anti-complete A-paths, nor a set Z ⊆ V (G) of size
at most 2k − 3 such that G − NG [Z, r] has no A-path.
We further extend Theorems 3 and 4 to long induced A-paths. For a graph G, an induced
path is a path that is an induced subgraph of G. The length of a path is the number of
edges it contains.
Theorem 5. For all k, ℓ ∈ N, every graph G and A ⊆ V (G) contains one of the following:
(i) k pairwise anti-complete induced A-paths of length at least ℓ; or
(ii) sets Z1 , Z2 ⊆ V (G) with |Z1 | ⩽ (12 max{ℓ, 3} + 42)(k − 1) and |Z2 | ⩽ 4(k − 1) such
that neither G − NG [Z1 ] nor G − NG [Z2 , max{ℓ + 1, 4}] contains an induced A-path
of length at least ℓ.
Setting ℓ = 1 recovers Theorems 3 and 4.
Beyond coarse graph theory, our motivation for studying induced A-paths stems from the
study of induced minors. A graph H is an induced minor of a graph G if it can be obtained
from an induced subgraph of G by contracting edges. In recent years, considerable progress
has been made in understanding the global structure of H-induced-minor-free graphs
when H is planar, yet comparatively little is known when H is non-planar. We hope
that Theorem 3 may prove useful in establishing an ‘Induced Minor Structure Theorem’,
one that is akin to the celebrated Graph Minor Structure Theorem [23]. See [16] for an
application of A-paths to induced minors.
Theorem 3 also provides strong evidence for the following Induced Menger Conjecture,
which corresponds to the d = 2 of Conjecture 1.
Conjecture 6. For every k ∈ N, every graph G and X, Y ⊆ V (G) satisfies one of the
following:
(i) G contains k pairwise anti-complete (X, Y )-paths; or
(ii) there exists a set Z ⊆ V (G) with |Z| ⩽ k −1 such that G−NG [Z] has no (X, Y )-path.
If true, Conjecture 6 would have immediate application to the study of induced minors. In
particular, it would imply an Induced Grid Minor Theorem for K1,t -induced-subgraph-free
graphs that was conjectured by Dallard, Krnc, Kwon, Milanič, Munaro, Štorgel, and
Wiederrecht [7]; see [17] for further discussion on this connection.

Proof

Let G be a graph and A ⊆ V (G). Our proof of Theorem 5 employs the well-known
frame technique by Simonovits [24]. An A-frame in G is roughly an induced subcubic
tree whose leaves correspond to vertices in A. We construct our A-frame iteratively by
taking geodesics from unprocessed A-vertices to the frame while protecting leaves and
4

degree-3 vertices. If we can construct an A-frame with many leaves, we extract from it
our collection of anti-complete A-paths. If not, then we can separate the A-frame from
the remainder of the graph using a small number of balls. By applying induction on the
number of A-paths in the remainder of the graph, we obtain one of our desired outcomes.
Note that Ahn et al. [1] used a similar technique to prove an induced Erdős-Pósa theorem.
The following lemma is used for extracting A-paths from the frame.
Lemma 8 ([4]). For every p ∈ N, every subcubic tree with p leaves contain ⌊ p2 ⌋ pairwise
vertex-disjoint leaf-to-leaf paths.
Before applying Lemma 8, we must overcome two obstacles. First, the leaf-to-leaf paths are
not necessarily anti-complete and long. Second, the A-frame may not induce a subcubic
tree since non-tree edges may be present. To address these difficulties, we introduce the
following structure.
For ℓ ∈ N, an ℓ-hub-tree is a tuple (F, T, A, X) satisfying the following properties:
(H1) F is a graph;
(H2) T ⊆ F is a subcubic tree with V (T ) = V (F );
(H3) A is the set of degree-1 vertices of T which is equal to the set of degree-1 vertices of
F;
(H4) X is the set of degree-3 vertices in T ;
(H5) for all distinct x, y ∈ A, we have distF (x, y) ⩾ ℓ;
(H6) for all distinct x, y ∈ X, we have distF (x, y) ⩾ 3; and
(H7) for every uv ∈ E(F )\E(T ), there exists x ∈ X such that max{distT (u, x), distT (v, x)} ⩽
2.
Lemma 9. For all p, ℓ ∈ N, if (F, T, A, X) is an ℓ-hub-tree with |A| = p, then F contains
⌊ p2 ⌋ pairwise anti-complete induced A-paths, each of length at least ℓ.
Proof. Let k := ⌊ p2 ⌋. By Lemma 8, the tree T contains k pairwise vertex-disjoint A-paths
P1′ , . . . , Pk′ .
Suppose for contradiction that there exists an edge uv ∈ E(F ) such that u ∈ V (Pi′ ),
v ∈ V (Pj′ ), and i ̸= j. Since Pi′ and Pj′ are vertex-disjoint paths with length at least 1,
both u and v have degree at least 2 in F . Since Pi′ and Pj′ are A-paths, (H3) implies that
neither u nor v is an endpoint of its respective path, so both u and v have degree at least 3 in
F . If uv ∈ E(T ), then by (H4), u, v ∈ X which contradicts (H6). Thus uv ∈ E(F ) \ E(T ).
By (H7), there exists x ∈ X such that max{distT (u, x), distT (v, x)} ⩽ 2. Since Pi′ and
Pj′ are vertex-disjoint, we may assume without loss of generality that x ̸∈ V (Pi′ ). Let
x′ ∈ V (Pi′ ) be the vertex in Pi′ that is closest to x in T . Since x′ lies on Pi′ and has a
neighbour closer to x, it has degree 3 in T , again contradicting (H6). Hence, no such edge
uv exists. Thus the paths P1′ , . . . , Pk′ are pairwise anti-complete in F .
