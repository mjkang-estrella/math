`ChainPrefix.no_chain` proves `¬ Literal.HasChain Concrete.g7` for the unchanged capacity-based chain definition. `ChainPrefix.fastFits_iff` proves the sparse capacity evaluator equivalent to the full inequality for every unordered vertex pair: pairs not represented in the schematic edge list have zero demand, while represented pairs are checked with the graph's symmetry. This is a proved optimization, not a different chain notion.

The chain obstruction uses the complete first-triangle reservations and a necessary second-triangle prefix. Kernel checks show that the only end vertices are 3 and 6. Two double attachments at 3 must go to 2 and 6, in either order; at 6 they must go to 3 and 4. Checking the first triangle with those attachments forces its other two vertices to be 1 and 8, in either order. A one-triangle chain would require the next base (1 or 8) to be another end, which is impossible. Otherwise the internal double attachment of that next base must use the remaining two copies of the 1–8 connection. At a second triangle, the next base must be 4 or 6 when starting at 3, and neither can have even one remaining double attachment. Starting at 6 permits no second triangle with distinct bases. The proof permits all apex and attachment coincidences allowed by the literal capacities.

`chain0_first`, `chain1_prefix` and `chain_long_prefix` derive the checked reservation prefixes directly from `Literal.Chain.capacity`; they do not assume an internal row type. The long-prefix lemma bounds the chain length by injectivity of its bases into the eleven vertices, and checks the finitely many lengths to normalize the prefix indices. The final obstruction therefore covers every natural chain length.
