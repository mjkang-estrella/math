## 321798: EPS arrays, full quasipolynomial route
Put A=product of the positive n_l, N=lcm(n_l), and D=A-1-sum_l(n_l-1). Coordinates are indexed by tuples i in the Cartesian product. By actual original1106.5068 Lemmas40–42 and Definitions43–44/Lemma45 (pp16–18), the monomials of degree d in the zero weight space are precisely nonnegative integral arrays e with total d and equal parallel one-coordinate slice sums. The diagonal Cartan action gives the difference of consecutive slice sums as eigenvalue, and monomials form a simultaneous eigenbasis, so cancellation between monomials creates no additional zero vectors. Consequently their count is the dimension over C. This is not the kernel of the raising operator E of Theorem46: that smaller space counts invariants, not the present target.

Define P by x_i>=0, sum x_i=1, and sum_{i_l=j}x_i=1/n_l for every l,j. Then e is EPS of degree d exactly when e is an integral point in dP (d=0 gives the zero array separately). P is bounded and rational. Its positive uniform point x_i=1/A proves the inequalities do not reduce the affine dimension. The affine equations have rank 1+sum(n_l-1): use the total functional and the slice indicators for j<n_l. A linear dependence has the form c+sum_l a_l(i_l)=0 on all tuples, with a_l(n_l)=0. Varying just i_l forces a_l(j)=0, then c=0. Hence dim P=D, including n_l=1, k=1 and D=0.

Here is a direct ordinary proof of the exact quasipolynomial assertion, rather than an unexplained application of a named theorem. A rational polytope admits a triangulation by rational simplices meeting face-to-face: induct on dimension and the number of vertices, triangulate visible boundary facets when inserting a vertex and cone their simplices to that vertex; all cones use existing rational vertices. Finite inclusion-exclusion expresses its indicator as an integer linear combination of the indicators of its simplices and their common faces. Homogenize each simplex F to the pointed cone generated by (v,1) at its vertices. Choose one positive M, also a multiple of N, clearing all vertex denominators in this finite triangulation. A cone over an e-dimensional simplex has linearly independent integral ray generators u_0,...,u_e all with last coordinate M. Every lattice point y of this cone has unique real coefficients lambda_i>=0. Subtract sum floor(lambda_i)u_i. The remainder is a lattice point in the bounded half-open parallelepiped sum [0,1)u_i. Conversely such a remainder plus arbitrary nonnegative integral multiples of the u_i is a lattice point, uniquely. There are finitely many remainders; their integral heights a satisfy 0<=a<(e+1)M. Thus its height generating function is a finite sum z^a/(1-z^M)^(e+1).

For d=r+mM with 0<=r<M, a=r+bM contributes binomial(m-b+e,e). Here 0<=b<=e. This polynomial has the required value for ALL m>=0: when m<b its top argument is one of 0,...,e-1 and the polynomial vanishes, as does the generating-function coefficient. Inclusion-exclusion now gives a rational polynomial on each residue, degree at most D, valid from d=0, not merely eventually. Intersections at the apex are handled by the same face inclusion-exclusion, so the constant count is one.

The degree is exactly D on every admissible residue and inadmissible residues have polynomial zero. Necessity of N|d follows from the integral slice sums d/n_l. For sufficiency, take N tuple occurrences indexed by h=0,...,N-1 and put one unit at tuple (h mod n_1,...,h mod n_k); repetitions add. Each slice has N/n_l units. Scaling supplies an EPS array of every degree divisible by N. Choose D+1 affinely independent rational points in P, scale by a common multiple M to integral arrays v_0,...,v_D of degree M (enlarge the preceding M if necessary). For sum a_i<=m, the arrays (m-sum a_i)v_0+sum a_i v_i are distinct, nonnegative and EPS of degree mM. Their number is binomial(m+D,D), by the ordinary stars-and-bars separator bijection. Add a fixed EPS array of degree r for each residue r divisible by N to get the same lower bound in degree r+mM. A degree<=D polynomial bounded below by that expression must have degree D and positive leading coefficient. This includes D=0. No integral-vertex or total-unimodularity claim was used.

This proves the precise quasipolynomial reading of 'family of polynomials'. It does NOT prove one polynomial on all multiples of N, or period exactly N. Clearing vertex denominators can require a larger period. On inadmissible residues the zero polynomial does not have exact degree D. These distinctions must be part of any faithful formal endpoint; a semantic strengthening is not silently paid.
