# Independent ordinary proof for 321798

This is a personal mathematical argument, with no mathematical computation, finite enumeration, CAS, solver or formal tool. It proves an exact quasipolynomial theorem, not a bound on the minimum period. All integers k,n_1,...,n_k are positive; d ranges over all nonnegative integers. Put A=product n_l, N=lcm(n_l), D=A-1-sum(n_l-1).

## Monomials and the actual zero weight space

Index variables by I=product_l {1,...,n_l}. In the complex polynomial ring the monomials x^e, e:I->nonnegative integers with sum e=d, are a basis of the degree d component. Define H_(l,j) on a generator x_i by (1_(i_l=j)-1_(i_l=j+1))x_i, and extend by the derivation rule. Repeated Leibniz gives H_(l,j)x^e=(S_(l,j)(e)-S_(l,j+1)(e))x^e, where S is the sum over the indicated slice. Thus this is a simultaneous eigenbasis. If f=sum c_e x^e is annihilated by every H, linear independence implies c_e times each corresponding eigenvalue is zero. For any e with c_e nonzero, all slice differences vanish; conversely such monomials are annihilated. Consequently the common zero eigenspace has precisely the EPS monomials as basis, and its complex dimension f(d) is their number. This proves the source Lemmas40-42/Definitions43-44/Lemma45 bridge directly; no cancellation between distinct monomials supplies extra vectors. The raising operators E are different: the source Theorem46 takes a further kernel inside this space to obtain invariants. That further kernel is not counted here. When n_l=1 there is no H in that factor, exactly as there is no equality between different parallel slices to impose. sl_1 contributes zero.

## The polytope and its dimension

Let P={x in R^I: x_i>=0, sum x_i=1, S_(l,j)(x)=1/n_l for all l,j}. It is nonempty (the uniform array x_i=1/A), closed, bounded by the unit cube, and defined over Q. For d>0, multiplication by d identifies its integral points with the EPS arrays of degree d; for d=0 both counts are one (the zero array). Necessity N|d follows because the equal integral slice sums must equal d/n_l.

The equations have rank c=1+sum(n_l-1). To see this use total plus the indicators of slices j<n_l. A relation is a function c_0+sum_l a_l(i_l)=0 on I, with a_l(n_l)=0. Set every coordinate to its last index to get c_0=0, then vary one coordinate to get each a_l(j)=0. The omitted last slice follows from total and the other slices, so these equations describe exactly the affine hull. Uniform positivity gives a relatively open neighborhood inside P in that affine space. Hence dim P=A-c=D. This also proves D>=0, including k=1 and factors n_l=1. No independence of inequalities is asserted.

For completeness, rationality here is vertex rationality as well as rational equations. At a vertex the affine equalities together with the active coordinate equalities have full rank in R^I: otherwise a nonzero null direction permits a small segment through that point, contradicting extremality. A full-rank subsystem has rational coefficients, so Gaussian elimination over Q shows its unique solution rational. A bounded polyhedron is the convex hull of its finitely many vertices: a nonvertex can be moved in a null direction until a new coordinate becomes zero in each direction and written as a convex combination of the two endpoints; induction on the number of nonzero coordinates terminates. This also establishes the required finite vertex description without an integral-vertex assertion.

## A compatible rational triangulation that repairs the apex issue

The following construction is useful precisely at d=0. In every nonempty proper face choose the barycenter of its vertices. These points are rational and relatively interior. Triangulate faces inductively by coning their already triangulated boundaries to these barycenters. This is compatible on common faces because every boundary subdivision was already fixed. The elementary reason it is a triangulation is that every ray from a relative interior point meets the boundary once, so radial cones cover; intersections are cones over common subdivided boundary faces. In dimension zero the subdivision is the singleton itself.

Now triangulate the boundary of P this way and cone it to the uniform rational point q in the relative interior of P. If D=0 take P itself. Every maximal simplex T_i of this triangulation contains q as a vertex. Each nonempty subfamily therefore has a nonempty common face F_J containing q. All these faces are rational simplices, of dimensions at most D. Finite inclusion-exclusion gives 1_P=sum_(nonempty J)(-1)^(|J|+1)1_(F_J). Crucially this same identity works after dilation even at zero: every F_J is nonempty and 0F_J={0}; the alternating sum of all nonempty subsets of the finite set of maximal simplices is one.

This does not conflate an empty face with a nonempty face. For an arbitrary triangulation disjoint simplices would produce homogenized cones intersecting only at {0}; that zero cone is not the cone over an ordinary nonempty face and contributes a lone constant to the generating series. Ignoring it is a real proof gap in an unrestricted assertion. Our common-q triangulation prevents such intersections altogether. Empty faces make no contributions. Thus no unproved Euler-characteristic cancellation is needed to repair zero dilation.

## Exact counting of each simplex, in its actual ambient lattice

Let F have dimension e and rational affinely independent vertices v_0,...,v_e. Enlarge a common positive integer M so that N|M and every M v_i in every face used above is integral. In R^(I+1) the u_i=(M v_i,M) are linearly independent integral vectors. The cone C_F={sum lambda_i u_i: lambda_i>=0} has height-d section dF for d>0 and singleton origin at height zero.

For any y in C_F intersect Z^(I+1), its real coefficients lambda_i are unique. Subtract sum floor(lambda_i)u_i. The remainder b is integral and lies in B={sum theta_i u_i: 0<=theta_i<1}. Conversely every integral b in B and arbitrary nonnegative integer coefficients t_i give a unique integral y=b+sum t_i u_i in the cone. Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of real coefficients and integer/fractional parts. B is bounded and so has finitely many integral points. This statement uses the ambient lattice intersected with the linear span; it does not assume the u_i generate that lattice, nor that the cone is full dimensional, unimodular or primitive. In particular all sublattice indices are paid by the multiple possible remainders.

Let a be the integral height of a remainder. Then 0<=a<(e+1)M. The generating series of lattice points by height is sum_(b in B intersect Z^(I+1)) z^a/(1-z^M)^(e+1). This is a formal power series identity: each fixed height has finitely many points and the decomposition is bijective. Expanding the denominator counts (e+1)-tuples of nonnegative integers by their sum, giving coefficient binomial(t+e,e) at height Mt. The usual separator bijection proves this coefficient formula.

Write d=r+mM, 0<=r<M, m>=0. Only remainders with a congruent r mod M contribute. Such an a is r+bM with an integer 0<=b<=e. Its contribution for m>=b is binomial(m-b+e,e). The polynomial binomial(X-b+e,e)=(X-b+1)...(X-b+e)/e! agrees even when 0<=m<b: then m-b+e is an integer between zero and e-1, so one factor is zero. Thus the same polynomial gives the correct coefficient for every m>=0, including small m, not merely eventually. For e=0 one has b=0 and the binomial polynomial is one. Inclusion-exclusion gives a rational polynomial in m, hence in d, of degree at most D for every residue r. This proves exact quasipolynomiality on all nonnegative degrees, with f(0)=1.

Any positive multiple of an available period is also available: restrict each constituent polynomial to the finer progression. All enlargement of M below is therefore harmless; the simplex proof can equivalently be repeated with enlarged ray height. Nothing implies M=N or minimum period M.

## Nonzero residues and exact degree

An EPS array of degree N is obtained by taking one occurrence of the tuple (h mod n_1,...,h mod n_k) for each h=0,...,N-1 (translate indices by one if using {1,...,n_l}). Equal tuples add their occurrences. For fixed l each residue occurs N/n_l times, so all slice sums agree. Multiplying this array supplies an EPS array of every nonnegative degree divisible by N, including zero. This is a symbolic construction, not an enumeration or numerical test.

Choose D+1 affinely independent rational points q_0,...,q_D of P: take the uniform q and sufficiently small rational steps in a rational basis of the nullspace of the affine equations. Their entries remain nonnegative by strict uniform positivity. Enlarge M to clear their denominators too; set v_i=M q_i. These are nonnegative integer EPS arrays, each of degree M, with differences v_i-v_0 linearly independent. For each nonnegative integer m and (a_1,...,a_D) with sum a_i<=m the array (m-sum a_i)v_0+sum a_i v_i is EPS of degree mM. Distinct tuples give distinct arrays by that linear independence. There are binomial(m+D,D) such tuples, again by separators. For any r in {0,...,M-1} divisible by N fix the constructed EPS array B_r of degree r. Adding B_r is injective and supplies at least binomial(m+D,D) arrays of degree r+mM, for EVERY m>=0.

The polynomial already obtained on that residue has degree at most D. The displayed lower bound forces degree D and a positive leading coefficient: otherwise its eventual growth would be smaller or negative. For N not dividing r every count is zero, so its constituent is the zero polynomial. No exact degree is assigned to that zero polynomial. D=0 gives positive constant constituents; for k=1 the unique possible EPS array has entries d/n_1 on admissible degrees and the count is exactly one. All-one factors cause no difficulty, and when all n_l=1 the count is one for all d. This completes the all-parameter theorem.

## What this theorem does and does not say

For each fixed positive tuple of sizes there is a positive period M divisible by N and rational polynomials p_0,...,p_(M-1) with f(d)=p_(d mod M)(d) for all d>=0; admissible constituents have exact degree D and positive leading coefficient, and inadmissible constituents are zero. It does not assert that M=N or that all admissible constituents coincide.

The source itself supplies a contrary case to that strengthening. In its Theorem31 (p9, visually read), for n_1=n_2=n_3=2 it prints p_0=(d+4)^2(d^2+8d+24)/384 and p_2=(d+2)(d+6)(d^2+8d+28)/384 on residues zero and two modulo four. Expanding by hand gives identical coefficients except constants 384/384 and 336/384, so p_0-p_2=1/8. A single polynomial agreeing with all even degrees would agree identically with p_0 on infinitely many multiples of four and identically with p_2 on infinitely many degrees two modulo four, an impossibility. This comparison uses the printed source theorem as prior, not a new independent proof of its special-case enumeration. It is a source-interpretation correction with zero credit.
