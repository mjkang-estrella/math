# Sustained ordinary attempts

## 323940: three-connectivity completion for Pirates and Treasure

Both arXiv versions, journal paper and 2017 follow-up were collected. Source Problem in Section 2 has positive integer weights, two specified starting vertices, and winning means strictly greater final score. Under the stated game, play stops when the player to move cannot move. Ordinary hardness needs a polynomial reduction and ALL possible deviations, not a few evaluated positions. The 2017 PSPACE-completeness result uses a different unrestricted construction; it does not by itself settle cubic or 3-connected restrictions.

Attempt: put an n-vertex Hamiltonian-path input G next to a path t0,...,t_(n-1). Add three universal hubs L,R,H, pairwise joined. Start Left at L and Right at R, assign H and t0 the same large weight M, other vertices weight 1. The graph is 3-connected: after fewer than three deletions an undeleted universal hub connects all remaining vertices. Proposed forced opening Left H, Right t0 blocks all hubs and leaves the independent G/path race. After that opening Left wins iff it can visit all n vertices of G: Right collects n-1 ordinary path vertices, while Left needs strictly more than n-1. If Right declines t0 after Left H, Left can take t0 next and win by the M weight gap, for M larger than the sum of ordinary weights.

The fatal unresolved direction is Left's initial choice of t0 rather than H. Right can respond H but does not automatically draw: if its path inside G runs out early, Left may already lead. Hence this is NOT a valid reduction. Increasing H's weight to force it also creates a positive initial score bias in the intended branch, allowing false positives before a Hamiltonian traversal. A hub at zero or negative weight would violate the source domain. Adding tail shortcuts preserves connectivity but changes the race. No endpoint or Pi search is authorized by these observations. Needed lemma: a positive-weight, 3-connected opening gadget whose deviations cannot give Left a winning score and whose canonical continuation has exactly the original threshold. Cubicness needs a separate degree-three gadget even if this were fixed.

Source example inventory for this route: original figures 1-2 game trees; Fig.3 sample position; Fig.4 Hamiltonian path reduction; Fig.5 greedy-play failure; Figs.6-9 scoring/misere examples. Follow-up Figs.1-2 PSPACE gadgets and later weight-space example. Their text/edge discussions have been inspected for role, but figures have not all been visually reconstructed. Thus the full figure gate remains open before proposing any finite gadget as new.

## 323797 and 323802: cancellation and rational refined ASM counts

Source 1207.4437 defines GMTs by three local conditions: interlacing between neighbors, strictness above weakly increasing triples, and forced equal neighbors above descending edges at endpoint equality. Signs count newcomers and sign-changing pairs. This prevents replacing the enumeration by ordinary interlacing arrays or counting unsigned triangles.

For 323797 all three clauses matter: block repetition for every i=0..n, odd doubled-row signed identity, and W reflection. The source has reflection/translation and a shifted antisymmetry V_(x,y) alpha. For the first two identities, repeatedly straightening the boundary descent between the two increasing blocks produces extra shifted terms, not a single cancellation. Equality of some boundary entries forces rows, but the descent itself permits newcomers and equal pairs. No sign-reversing involution was found that preserves these endpoint constraints and reduces every case to an ordinary n-row triangle. Reflection of the entire GMT sends the two-block bottom row to a reversed order rather than the required W boundary, so symmetry alone does not prove the W identity. Missing lemma: a boundary straightening identity with its cancellation signs, uniform in n and i, plus the W reflection argument. Checking i=0 is the definition and gives no credit.

For 323802 the source gives explicit k=4,5,6 rational examples. They cannot earn credit. A general approach would express the repeated-pair evaluation as a finite weighted sum of refined ASM boundary counts, then use refined enumeration to get rationality. The missing step is a uniform signed expansion for arbitrary k, and exact leading degree k-3 with no cancellation. Interpolating finitely many n values cannot certify rationality or that degree. No numerical fitting was performed. Treat q_k(n) as a nonvanishing denominator on the claimed range; do not exploit undefined rational evaluations.

Its final displayed minus sign corrects an intermediate sign in the longer version; no credit is sought for that. A later dissertation repeats the general rational-ratio conjecture; its body was search-located but not acquired/read fully, so later proof clearance is incomplete.
