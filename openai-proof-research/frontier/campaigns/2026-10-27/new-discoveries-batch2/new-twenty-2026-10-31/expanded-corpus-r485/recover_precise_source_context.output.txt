2     Background on symmetric functions
Throughout this paper we will adopt notation and terminology on parti-
tions and symmetric functions in Stanley [13]. Recall that a partition λ of
a nonnegative integer n is aPweakly decreasing sequence (λ1 , λ2 , . . .) of non-
negative integers satisfying i λi = n, denoted λ ⊢ n. We usually omit the
parts λi = 0. We also denote a partition λ ⊢ n by (nmn , . . . , 2m2 , 1m1 ) if λ
has mi i’s for 1 ≤ i ≤ n. Let Par(n) denote the set of all partitions of n.
    If λ ⊢ n, we draw a left-justified array of n squares with λi squares in the
i-th row. This array is called the Young diagram of λ. By transposing the
diagram of λ, we get the conjugate partition of λ, denoted λ′ . We use µ ⊆ λ
to denote that the Young diagram of µ is contained in the diagram of λ.
