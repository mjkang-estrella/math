Let $G$ be a graph. A graph is **hereditarily normal** if every induced subgraph of $G$ is normal, where a graph is normal when it has coverings by cliques and stable sets such that every clique in one covering intersects every stable set in the other. An induced subgraph is a **5-cycle** or **7-cycle** when it is isomorphic to the corresponding cycle graph.

**De Simone–Körner conjecture.** A graph $G$ is hereditarily normal if and only if neither $G$ nor its complement contains a 5-cycle or a 7-cycle as an induced subgraph.

The conjecture aims to characterize hereditary normality by the two known minimal obstructions, the 5-cycle, the 7-cycle, and the complement of the 7-cycle. The source gives no resolution status.
