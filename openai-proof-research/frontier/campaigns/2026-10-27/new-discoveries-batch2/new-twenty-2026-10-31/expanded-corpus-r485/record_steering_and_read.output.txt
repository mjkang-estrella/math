                                               Abstract
            Let Φ(x, y) be a bivariate polynomial with complex coefficients. The zeroes of Φ(x, y)
        are given a combinatorial structure by considering them as arcs of a directed graph G(Φ).
        This paper –the first of a series of two– studies the relationship between the polynomial
        Φ(x, y) and the structure of G(Φ).


1       Introduction
Let Φ(x, y) ∈ C[x, y] be a bivariate polynomial with complex coefficients and let I be the
ideal generated by Φ(x, y). The variety V(I) of I is the set of ordered pairs (u, v) ∈ C 2 such
that Φ(u, v) = 0. We give a combinatorial structure to V(I) by taking its elements as arcs
of a digraph G(Φ), and we explore the relationship between the polynomial Φ(x, y) and the
digraph G(Φ). This paper is the first one of a series of two. In it we give a general overview
of the topic and some concrete families of polynomials are studied. In the sequel [3] we
adopt an algebraic approach to the problem of deciding which polynomials produce a given
graph as a connected component of G(Φ).
    The digraphs G(Φ) were introduced in [2] under the name of Galois graphs. Lengths of
walks, distances and cycles were described in terms of Φ(x, y). Also, when the coefficients
of Φ(x, y) belong to a field k and k is the algebraic closure of k, the action of the Galois
group G(k/k) on G(Φ) was studied (and this was the motivation for the name of Galois
graphs).
    Let us fix some notation. In this paper digraphs are allowed to be infinite, and to have
multiple arcs and loops. A graph is a digraph without loops nor multiple arcs such that
for each arc (u, v) there exists the arc (v, u). The pair of arcs (u, v), (v, u) are called an
edge of the graph and it is denoted by uv. Let u be a vertex of a digraph D. The strong
(connected) component, or component, of u is the subdigraph of D induced by u and the
set of vertices v such that there exist a directed path from u to v. The underlying graph of
a digraph D is the graph obtained by taking as edges the set of uv with (u, v) an arc of D
and u 6= v. The weakly (connected) component of u in a digraph D is the component of u
in the underlying graph of D. Note that, in a graph, components and weakly components

In this section we analyze the components of G(Φ) for a symmetric polynomial Φ(x, y) of
total degree two. In particular, it is shown that all components of G(Φ) ∗ are isomorphic.
As G(Φ)∗ is a 2-regular graph, a component of G(Φ)∗ is isomorphic to a (undirected)
cycle Cn = Cay(Zn , {1, −1}) or to the double ray graph R = Cay(Z, {1, −1}). From
Theorem 5.2 we can assume that the polynomial is not of partial degree one, so it is of
the form Φ(x, y) = x2 + y 2 + axy + b(x + y) + c. As a consequence of the discussion, the
following Theorem is obtained.
Theorem 6.1. Let Φ(x, y) = x2 + y 2 + axy + b(x + y) + c be a standard polynomial. If the
equation λ2 + aλ + 1 = 0 has two different roots ω1 and ω2 which are primitive n-th roots
of the unity, then all components of G(Φ)∗ are isomorphic to Cn , the cycle of length n.
Otherwise, they are isomorphic to the double ray graph R.

recurrently as follows: the polynomial Φ(vn−1 , y) has two roots; one of them is vn−2 ; the

y − vn (and the remainder is 0). The division algorithm gives y − vn = y + avn−1 + vn−2 + b,

7     A conjecture
If Φ(x, y) is an homogeneous standard polynomial, then G(Φ)∗ is a Cayley digraph (Theo-
rem 4.2). Therefore if a component of G(Φ)∗ is finite, all of them are finite and isomorphic.
In Section 5 we have seen that if Φ(x, y) is a polynomial of partial degree 1, then if a
component of G(Φ)∗ is a directed n-cycle, then all of them are directed n-cycles. Also, for
a symmetric polynomials Φ(x, y) of partial and total degree 2, if a component of G(Φ) ∗
is a n-cycle, then all of them are n-cycles. In all these examples components and weakly
components have the same set of vertices.
