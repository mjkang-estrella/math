## 325145: complete three-rider route, then exact public exclusion

The standing source hypothesis is a nonempty finite set M of pairwise nonparallel primitive nonzero integer move vectors. A move line is unoriented; changing the orientation only renames signs. Put m=|M|. Configuration types are the left/right signs of each pair displacement against each move normal, modulo relabelling the three pieces. The board is a full-dimensional rational convex polygon with integral dilation permitted. These are the actual source definitions, not an arbitrary discrete fixed-board count.

Translate the first point to zero, and write the other displacements x,y in R². For each move normal a_s, the attack arrangement in R⁴ consists of A_s:a_s x=0, B_s:a_s y=0, C_s:a_s(y-x)=0. The discarded translation factor R² changes neither chambers nor their count. Each chamber is an open convex sign cone. Every chamber can be shrunk and translated into the interior of any such board, then approximated by a rational configuration without changing signs and dilated to integral coordinates. Thus chamber count really counts labelled types with all source hypotheses paid.

For m>=2 the arrangement has rank four. Its intersection lattice can be classified without using the numerical slopes. Here rank means codimension and mu means the lattice Möbius value from the ambient flat.

Rank one: 3m hyperplanes, mu=-1 each.

Rank two: (i) three collapsed-pair flats x=0, y=0, y=x, each lying in exactly m hyperplanes and having mu=m-1; (ii) m same-direction flats A_s intersect B_s intersect C_s, each containing three hyperplanes and having mu=2; (iii) 3m(m-1) intersections with two different directions on two different pairs, each containing exactly two hyperplanes and having mu=1. Completeness follows by choosing two independent equations: on one pair they force that pair to coincide; on distinct pairs a third equation can follow only when all three normals are parallel. The total mu at rank two is 3m²+2m-3.

Rank three: (i) a chosen collapsed pair plus a chosen direction for the remaining displacement: 3m flats, with atoms consisting of m equations on the collapsed pair and two equations of the chosen direction on the other pairs; (ii) one equation on each pair, with three pairwise distinct directions: m(m-1)(m-2) flats. In (ii), x and y lie on distinct move lines and y-x on a third distinct line. The three equations are independent, their solution space is one-dimensional, and each of x,y,y-x is nonzero on a nonzero solution. Consequently no further hyperplane contains that line. Distinct triples give distinct flats. For completeness, any rank-three flat has an independent triple. Two equations on the same pair give (i). One on each pair either has all directions equal (rank two), exactly two equal (the third pair collapses, case (i)), or all three distinct (case (ii)). This also proves that there are no slope-specific hidden rank-three flats.

For (i), the rank-two mu sum below it is (m-1)+2+2(m-1)=3m-1. Therefore its mu is -[1-(m+2)+(3m-1)]=2-2m. For (ii), three atoms and three rank-two flats give mu=-[1-3+3]=-1. The total rank-three mu is -m(m-1)(m+4).

The unique rank-four flat is the origin; the Möbius recursion gives mu=m³-3m+2. The standard real central arrangement region formula (equivalently deletion/restriction induction for characteristic polynomials) gives the sum of absolute Möbius values. Here that sum is
1+3m+(3m²+2m-3)+m(m-1)(m+4)+(m³-3m+2)
=2m(m²+3m-1).

The relabelling action is free on types: any chosen move normal puts the three distinct projections in a strict total order, and a label permutation preserving the type preserves this order and is the identity. Divide the labelled count by 3!=6. The unlabelled count is m(m²+3m-1)/3. For m=1, sorting the three projections directly gives six labelled types and one unlabelled type, agreeing with the formula. The source excludes m=0.

Two shortcut checks were insufficient by themselves. Merely applying a linear coordinate change handles two or three directions, but does not handle arbitrary m because four directions have projective moduli. Also, placing the third piece into the two-pencil planar arrangement gives the right number of regions for fixed first two pieces, but still requires a proof that the type descriptions cannot change while the second point moves within its cone. The full lattice classification above avoids this unpaid continuity shortcut.

## 324986: full fifteen-bit refutation mechanism, then public-adaptation exclusion

Use f=1_{b=0}, so output complementation does not change conditional entropy. Let n=15 and A be the sixteen strings of Hamming weight at most one. Let L be the first sixteen strings in lex order; L fixes the first eleven bits to zero. Both have mean p=16/32768=2^-11. Write rho=1-2alpha and use characters chi_S(x)=(-1)^{sum_{i in S}x_i}. The uniform binary symmetric channel has posterior E[f(X)|Y=y]=T_rho f(y)=sum_S rho^{|S|} fhat(S)chi_S(y). This follows directly by taking the product of the one-coordinate conditional expectations, without approximation.

For A, each singleton coefficient is (15-1)/32768=14/32768: among its sixteen points, fourteen contribute +1 net in the chosen coordinate. Thus W1(A)=15*14²/32768²=2940/32768². For L, eleven singleton coefficients equal16/32768 and four vanish, so W1(L)=11*16²/32768²=2816/32768². The difference is 124/32768²>0. These exact arithmetic expressions are handwritten deductions, not program output.

Let h be binary entropy in bits and F_f(rho)=2^-15 sum_y h(T_rho f(y)). Near zero every posterior lies in the interior of (0,1), since its value at zero is p>0. Finite sums permit termwise differentiation. Orthogonality gives F'_f(0)=0 and F''_f(0)=h''(p)W1(f), because the mean of the second-order character term vanishes. Here h''(p)=-1/(ln(2)p(1-p))<0. Consequently D=F_A-F_L has D(0)=D'(0)=0 and D''(0)<0. By continuity D'' remains negative on some (0,epsilon); two integrations imply D(rho)<0 there. Choose any rho in (0,min(epsilon,1)), giving alpha=(1-rho)/2 in (0,1/2). This is a strict counterexample in the full literal domain, though no particular rational alpha is certified. Endpoint alpha=1/2 is equality and causes no conflict with a strict interior witness.

Failed alternatives: the seven-bit radius-one ball gives the wrong singleton-weight comparison and is not a witness; low-noise edge-boundary intuition concerns alpha near zero and does not imply the high-noise statement. No enumeration was performed.

## 329472: sustained parity routes; no full endpoint

Source 1412.5242v1, Definition1.4 defines h by log of the disconnected covering series with reciprocal-automorphism weights; r=2g-2+ell(mu)+|mu|. Conjecture3.3 is exactly the three-clause target. Theorem3.1 proves integrality for |mu|>=3; this is a standing input, not the parity conjecture. Actual §3 Proposition3.2 proof and exceptional-half discussion were read.

Route one: transposition monodromy. Connected covers have transitive transposition monodromy, hence full S_d. For d>=3 its centralizer in S_d is trivial: an element commuting with every transposition fixes each unordered pair and hence each point. Thus simultaneous conjugation on labelled transitive factorizations is free and division by d! counts isomorphism classes. This repairs the tempting but invalid inference 'labelled count is even, therefore h is even': d! is itself even. Inverting or conjugating a tuple is not automatically a free involution on its quotient, and reversing factors changes the target product to its inverse. A quotient-level involution with a controlled fixed locus is still required. The sign identity sign(sigma)=(-1)^r only says r ≡ d-ell(mu) mod2; it imposes no parity on h and cannot prove the desired evenness.

Route two: cut-and-join induction modulo two. The actual recurrence groups terms by multiplicities. Joining distinct parts a,b has grouped coefficient (a+b)(m_{a+b}+1); joining equal parts has coefficient a(m_{2a}+1). Splitting an unequal pair has coefficient ab(m_a+1)(m_b+1); the equal split has coefficient a²(m_a+1)(m_a+2)/2. In disconnected splitting the factor is epsilon(m_a(l)+1)(m_b(n)+1)ab/2 times binomial(r-1,r1), with epsilon=2 off the diagonal and epsilon=1 on it. These integers explain integrality but do not make all forbidden-profile terms separately even. In particular odd joined a+b can arise from one even input part; proving odd profiles vanish cannot ignore these terms or the equal split's divided factor. The exceptional h_{g,(2)}=h_{g,(1,1)}=1/2 values mean reducing the ungrouped rational recurrence modulo two is illegitimate. A strengthened induction carrying 2-adic valuations and grouped cancellations, including those exceptional profiles, is unpaid. I do not assert that the displayed term families cancel.

Route three considered: complex-conjugation pairing of covers with real branch locations. Odd count implies some real fixed classes, but real covers can have multiple cycles over the distinguished point. A classification/count modulo two of the fixed classes is needed; existence of a real cover alone gives neither all odd parts nor ell<=2. This did not close the gap.

The three native searches located no applicable parity proof body in the returned navigation, but that bounded nonmatch is not novelty clearance. No full ordinary proof or counterexample; PARK, formal endpoint absent.

## 324908: general invariant graph probability route; unpaid global step

Cameron1301.7544 §1.8.3 Conjecture1 specifies independent fair bits on G-orbits of unordered vertex pairs. Positive probability implies existence immediately. For the converse, fix finite disjoint U,V. A candidate new vertex w realizes the extension pattern by prescribing at most |U|+|V| orbital bits, provided no orbit receives conflicting prescriptions. If there are infinitely many such candidates whose relevant orbit sets are pairwise disjoint, independence gives failure probability at most (1-2^{-(|U|+|V|)})^N for every N, hence zero. Countably many finite U,V then imply the random graph extension property almost surely. This proves a genuine sufficient condition but not the conjecture.

The missing implication is from existence of one invariant R to this disjoint-orbit-witness condition, or to a finite conditioning of positive probability that makes it hold. That implication is false without allowing conditioning: the source gives one fixed point and two infinite cycles, for which R has positive probability but not probability one. A finite obstruction of orbital bits can matter. Iteratively fixing a helpful finite assignment for each U,V can consume infinitely many bits; the resulting event may have probability zero, so compactness/Baire arguments do not supply the needed positive measure.

Counterexample effort: infinitely many fixed points plus only finitely many infinite vertex orbits initially looks like it could impose infinitely many independent positive-probability conditions. It fails already at existence. Choose two vertices a,b in one infinite orbit. A fixed point cannot distinguish them. For k fixed points, each of the finitely many nontrivial vertex orbits has only one adjacency vector to those k points, since they are fixed by G. Choose k so 2^k exceeds that finite number. An absent vector, together with the requirement 'adjacent a, nonadjacent b', has no witness in any nontrivial orbit or fixed point. Hence no invariant R exists. This disposes of the attempted construction without a finite search. The general orbital overlap case remains unpaid. Source Proposition17 supplies a regular-action sufficient condition, not the arbitrary permutation-group implication. PARK.
