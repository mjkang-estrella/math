                                           1. Introduction

                                                All graphs considered in the paper are simple and undirected. Given a graph G, its Laplacian matrix
                                           is defiened as L(G) = D(G) − A(G), where A(G) and D(G) are the adjacency matrix and the diagonal
                                           matrix of degrees of G, respectively. The Laplacian matrix is also known as the Kirchhoff matrix. Research
                                           on Laplacian matrix has a long history. For Laplacian matrix of graphs, a classical result is the famous
                                           Matrix-tree Theorem, which is implicit in the work of Kirchhoff in 1847 [18]. The reader can find more
                                           information on the Laplacian matrix in survey papers [29, 30] by Merris and [31] by Mohar.
                                                The Laplacian matrix is obviously symmetric, positive semidefinite, and its row sum is zero. The eigen-
                                           values of L(G) are called the Laplacian eigenvalues of G. The Laplacian eigenvlaues are related to several
                                           structural properties of a graph (see, e.g., [4, 13, 23, 24, 37, 40]). Among all Laplacian eigenvalues of G, the
                                           second smallest Laplacian eigenvalue is popularly known as the algebraic connectivity of G, and is usually
                                           denoted by a(G). The algebraic connectivity is an important parameter in spectral graph theory, and has
                                           received much attention (see, e.g. [7, 15, 22, 35, 39]). Fiedler [8] proved that a graph is connected if and only
                                           if its algebraic connectivity is positive. Moreover, the algebraic connectivity provides a lower bound of the
                                           vertex (edge) connectivity of a graph [8, 19]. On the other hand, the algebraic connectivity has important

ity plays a crucial role in convergence analysis of consensus and alignment algorithms, and it quantifies the
speed of convergence of consensus algorithms [32, 33].
    A classical topic on algebraic connectivity is to determine the minimum (maximum) algebraic connectiv-
ity of graphs under some constraints. For trees, it is easy to see that the minimum and maximum algebraic
connectivity are attained on the path and the star, respectively. More researches for the algebraic con-
nectivity of trees were seen in [12, 21, 34]. The minimum algebraic connectivity of graphs with cycles is
considered in many papers. In [10], Fallat and Kirkland conjectured that lollipop graph is the unique graph
minimizing the algebraic connectivity among all graphs with given girth. This conjecture was verified in
[11] for some special cases and was completely confirmed by Guo [14]. The minimum algebraic connectivity
of Hamiltonian graphs was determined by Guo, Zhang and Yu [16]. More generally, Xue, Lin and Shu [38]
studied the minimum algebraic connectivity of graphs with given circumference. It is worth mentioning that
the eigenvector plays a crucial role in the characterization of the minimum (maximum) algebraic connectiv-
ity. An eigenvector corresponding to the algebraic connectivity is called a Fiedler vector. Some important
properties for the Fiedler vector were established by Fiedler [9] and Kirkland, Rocha and Trevisan [20].
    A graph is k-regular if the degree of each vertex is equal to k. In general, 3-regular graphs are also
called cubic graphs, which play a prominent role in graph theory. During recent years, many investigations
concerning cubic graphs were reported (see, e.g., in [25, 27, 28, 6, 26, 5]).
    The cubic graph with the minimum algebraic connectivity was discussed in [2, 17]. On this topic, the
graphs are always connected since the algebraic connectivity equals 0 for disconnected graphs. The unique
extremal graph was determined by Brand, Guiduli and Imrich [2]. Recently, Abdi, Ghorbani and Imrich [1]
obtained the asymptotic value of the minimum algebraic connectivity of cubic graphs.
Theorem 1.1. ([1]) The minimum algebraic connectivity of connected cubic graphs on 2n vertices is (1 +
       π2
o(1)) 2n 2.

cubic graph. Hence, it is very interesting to consider the minimum algebraic connectivity of cubic bipartite
graphs. Our theorem 1.2 indicates that the minimum algebraic connectivity of cubic bipartite graphs is
twice that of cubic graphs.

Lemma 2.2. ([9]) Let G be a connected graph with a Fiedler vector x. For r ≥ 0, let

    Let B(2n, 3) be the set of all connected cubic bipartite graphs on 2n vertices. A graph in B(2n, 3) is
called extremal if it has the minimum algebraic connectivity. The aim of this section is to determine the
extremal graph in B(2n, 3). Suppose that G is an extremal graph in B(2n, 3) with bipartition (U, V ). Let
{uv, u0 v 0 } be a pair of independent edges in G with {u, u0 } ⊆ U and {v, v 0 } ⊆ V . We define four vertex sets
N̂ (u) = NG (u)\{v}, N̂ (u0 ) = NG (u0 )\{v 0 }, N̂ (v) = NG (v)\{u} and N̂ (v 0 ) = NG (v 0 )\{u0 }.
    The following lemmas present some properties on the Fiedler vector of the extremal graph G.

Lemma 2.3. Let G be an extremal graph in B(2n, 3). Suppose that {uv, u0 v 0 } is a pair of independent edges
in G as defined above. Let x be a unit Fiedler vector of G. If xu > xu0 and xv ≤ xv0 , then the following
statements hold.
(P1) G contains no cut edge.
(P2) {uv, u0 v 0 } is an edge cut.
(P3) NG (u) ∩ NG (u0 ) = ∅ and NG (v) ∩ NG (v 0 ) = ∅.
(P4) min{xw : w ∈ N̂ (u)} > xv0 ≥ xv > max{xw : w ∈ N̂ (v)}.
(P5) min{xw : w ∈ N̂ (v 0 )} ≥ xu > xu0 ≥ max{xw : w ∈ N̂ (u0 )}.

Proof. If there exists a cut edge e in G, then a component of G − e has the degree sequence (2, 3, . . . , 3).
Note that such a component is also a bipartite graph. However, the sequence (2, 3, . . . , 3) cannot be the
degree sequence of any bipartite graph, hence there is no cut edge in G.
     We claim that the graph G − {uv, u0 v 0 } + {uv 0 , u0 v} must be disconnected. Otherwise, if G − {uv, u0 v 0 } +
{uv 0 , u0 v} is connected, then Lemma 2.1 shows that the algebraic connectivity of G − {uv, u0 v 0 } + {uv 0 , u0 v}
is less than the algebraic connectivity of G, a contradiction.
     If G − {uv, u0 v 0 } is connected, then the graph G − {uv, u0 v 0 } + {uv 0 , u0 v} is clearly connected, a contra-
diction. Therefore, {uv, u0 v 0 } is an edge cut.
     According to (P1) and (P2), there are exactly two components in G − {uv, u0 v 0 }, say H1 and H2 . If H1
(or H2 ) contains only one vertex of {u, u0 , v, v 0 }, then there is a cut edge in G, contradicting (P1). Thus,

Lemma 3.2. The Fiedler vector of Pn is skew symmetry.

Proof. Let x be a unit Fiedler vector of Pn . Suppose that Pn = v1 v2 · · · vn . Without loss of generality, we
may assume that xv1 ≥ 0.
    We claim that xv1 > 0. Suppose that xv1 = 0. Since a(Pn )xv1 = xv1 − xv2 , then we have xv2 = 0.
Similarly, one can see that xv3 = 0 as a(Pn )xv2 = 2xv2 − xv1 − xv3 . Repeating this process, we will finally
obtain a zero vector, a contradiction.
    Let us consider another vector y on the vertices of Pn , where yvi = xvn+1−i for 1 ≤ i ≤ n. Clearly, the
vector y is obtained from x by exchanging the entries corresponding to vertices vi and vn+1−i . According
to the symmetry of Pn , y is also a Fiedler vector of Pn . Recall that a(Pn ) is a simple eigenvalue of L(G).
Hence y = ±x.
    If y = x, then xv1 = xvn . Since x⊥1, the vector contains a negative entry. Suppose that xvi < 0
with 1 < i < n. Let [V (Pn )]≥0 = {v ∈ V (Pn ) : xv ≥ 0}. Lemma 2.2 shows that the subgraph induced
by [V (Pn )]≥0 is connected. But this is impossible since vi ∈/ [V (Pn )]≥0 . It follows that y = −x. Hence
xvi = −xvn+1−i for 1 ≤ i ≤ n, as required.

4. Concluding remarks

    In this paper, we determine the unique graph with minimum algebraic connectivity among all connected
cubic bipartite graphs, and obtain the asymptotic value of the minimum algebraic connectivity. For any
k-regular graph G, its diagonal martix of degrees is D(G) = kI, where I is the identity matrix. According to
the definition of Laplacian matrix, one can see that the Laplacian matrix and adjacency matrix of G satisfy

                                             L(G) = kI − A(G).

The difference between the two largest eigenvalues of A(G) is called the spectral gap of G. According to the
above equation, one can see that the spectral gap of G is equal to its algebraic connectivity. Using this fact,
Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 imply the following results directly.
                                                                                                             2
Theorem 4.1. The minimum spectral gap of connected cubic bipartite graphs on 2n vertices is (1 + o(1)) nπ2 .

Theorem 4.2. Among all connected cubic bipartite graphs with at least 12 vertices, H2n is the unique graph
with the minimum spectral gap.

    Lemma 2.1 establishes a transformation which strictly decreases the algebraic connectivity. We remark
that this transformation also works for general k-regular bipartite graphs. Then it is natural to investigate
the minimum algebraic connectivity (spectral gap) of k-regular bipartite graphs for k ≥ 4. Based on our
observations, we think that the extremal graph will be path-like when n is sufficiently large. In Theorem
1.5, we present a spectral characterization for cubic bipartite graphs with maximum number of perfect
matchings. For general k-regular bipartite graphs, it is also interesting to find the spectral characterization
for the extremal graphs with maximum number of perfect matchings.
