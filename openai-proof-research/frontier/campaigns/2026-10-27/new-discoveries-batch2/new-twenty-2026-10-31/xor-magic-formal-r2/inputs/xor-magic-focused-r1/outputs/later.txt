 Algebraic characterizations of generating and affinely generating  Γ \Gamma -magic maps and  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labelings on regular graphs

 Abstract. 
    
 A graph  G G  of order  n n  is  distance magic  if it admits a bijection from  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  to  { 1 , … , n } \{1,\ldots,n\}  whose open-neighborhood sums are constant. Given an abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  n n , the graph  G G  is  Γ \Gamma -distance magic  if it admits a bijection  f : V ⁡ ( G ) → Γ f\colon V(G)\to\Gamma  whose open-neighborhood sums are constant in  Γ \Gamma . The graph  G G  is  group distance magic  if it is  Γ \Gamma -distance magic for every abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  n n . 
 
    
 More generally, for an arbitrary finite abelian group  Γ \Gamma , a  Γ \Gamma -magic map is a map  f : V ⁡ ( G ) → Γ f\colon V(G)\to\Gamma  whose open-neighborhood sums are constant. We introduce two generation conditions for such maps. We call  f f   generating  if its labels generate  Γ \Gamma , and  affinely generating  if its pairwise differences generate  Γ \Gamma . 
 
    
 Let  G G  be regular, and write the invariant factor decomposition of  Γ \Gamma  as 
 
    
 

 
 
 Γ ≅ ℤ / d 1 ​ ℤ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ℤ / d r ​ ℤ , d 1 ​ ∣ ⋯ ∣ ​ d r , \Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}/d_{1}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/d_{r}\mathbb{Z},\qquad d_{1}\mid\cdots\mid d_{r}, 
 
 
    
 and put  B Γ = ⨁ i = 1 r − 1 ℤ / d i ​ ℤ B_{\Gamma}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r-1}\mathbb{Z}/d_{i}\mathbb{Z} , with  B Γ = { 0 } B_{\Gamma}=\{0\}  when  r = 1 r=1 . Let  A ¯ m \overline{A}_{m}  denote the endomorphism induced by the adjacency operator on the quotient of  ( ℤ / m ​ ℤ ) V ⁡ ( G ) (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{V(G)}  by the constant vectors. We prove that  G G  admits a generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map if and only if  B Γ ↪ ker ⁡ A ¯ d r B_{\Gamma}\hookrightarrow\ker\overline{A}_{d_{r}} , whereas it admits an affinely generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map if and only if  Γ ↪ ker ⁡ A ¯ d r \Gamma\hookrightarrow\ker\overline{A}_{d_{r}} . When  | V ⁡ ( G ) | = | Γ | |V(G)|=|\Gamma| , the graph is  Γ \Gamma -distance magic if and only if the latter embedding can be chosen with vertex-separating image. If the reduced adjacency operator is nonsingular over  ℚ \mathbb{Q} , the first two conditions become subgroup conditions in the reduced adjacency Smith group. 
 
    
 Cichacz and Froncek conjectured that every distance magic graph is group distance magic. Using the affine criterion, we construct a  6 6 -regular distance magic graph of order  27 27  that admits no affinely generating  ( ℤ / 3 ​ ℤ ) 3 (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^{3} -magic map. Thus their conjecture fails even after bijectivity is weakened to affine generation. We propose the generating group distance magic conjecture that every distance magic graph of order  n n  admits a generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map for every abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  n n . We prove this conjecture for regular distance magic graphs when  Γ \Gamma  is generated by at most two elements, and consequently for regular distance magic graphs of cube-free order. For Cayley graphs on elementary abelian  p p -groups, our nonexistence result when the regularity is not divisible by  p p , combined with a known sufficient condition when the regularity is divisible by  p p , yields an exact criterion for the existence of a  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling over some abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of the graph order. Further applications concern the Hamming relation graphs, strongly regular graphs, and incidence graphs of symmetric designs.

 1.  Introduction 

 
 Let  G G  be a finite simple graph and let  Γ \Gamma  be a finite abelian group written additively. For  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , let  N G ​ ( v ) N_{G}(v)  denote the neighborhood of  v v  in  G G . A map 
 
 

 
 
 f : V ⁡ ( G ) ⟶ Γ f\colon V(G)\longrightarrow\Gamma

 ∑ u ∈ N G ​ ( v ) f ⁡ ( u ) = γ for every  ​ v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) . \sum_{u\in N_{G}(v)}f(u)=\gamma\qquad\text{for every }v\in V(G). 
 
 
 A  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling is a bijective  Γ \Gamma -magic map with  | V ⁡ ( G ) | = | Γ | |V(G)|=|\Gamma|   [ 12 ,  9 ] . An  ordinary distance magic labeling  of a graph of order  N N  is a bijection  ℓ : V ⁡ ( G ) → { 1 , … , N } \ell\colon V(G)\to\{1,\ldots,N\}  whose open-neighborhood sums are constant. A graph admitting such a labeling is called  distance magic . A graph of order  N N  is  group distance magic  if it is  Γ \Gamma -distance magic for every abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  N N . 
 
 
 
 
 For a family  ( x i ) i ∈ I (x_{i})_{i\in I}  of elements of  Γ \Gamma , write  ⟨ x i ∣ i ∈ I ⟩ \langle x_{i}\mid i\in I\rangle  for the subgroup of  Γ \Gamma  generated by this family. 
 
 
 
 
 To separate the algebraic content of bijectivity from the requirement that all labels be distinct, we consider two generation conditions on  Γ \Gamma -magic maps. Both are implied by bijectivity when  | V ⁡ ( G ) | = | Γ | |V(G)|=|\Gamma| .

 Let  f : V ⁡ ( G ) → Γ f\colon V(G)\to\Gamma  be a  Γ \Gamma -magic map. We say that  f f  is  generating  if

 We say that  f f  is  affinely generating  if  D ⁡ ( f ) = Γ D(f)=\Gamma .

 These implications are recorded in Proposition  2.1 .

 Thus, in the reduced-Smith criterion, ordinary generation discards one largest invariant factor of the labeling group, whereas affine generation retains the full group. A  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling is subject to the same subgroup condition as affine generation, together with the vertex-separation requirement in the modular kernel. 
 
 
 
 
 The contrast between the reduced-Smith criteria for generating and affinely generating magic maps is central to our analysis of the conjecture of Cichacz and Froncek that every distance magic graph is group distance magic, originally formulated in  [ 8 , Conjecture 29]  and later restated in  [ 7 , Conjecture 1.7] . We show that the conjecture fails even after bijectivity is weakened to affine generation. More precisely, writing  𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q}  for the field with  q q  elements when  q q  is a prime power, we construct a  6 6 -regular distance magic graph  G G  of order  27 27  for which 
 
 

 
 
 dim 𝔽 3 ker ⁡ A ¯ 3 = 2 . \dim_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}\ker\overline{A}_{3}=2. 
 
 
 Proposition  4.1 (a)  then shows that  G G  admits a generating  ( ℤ / 3 ​ ℤ ) 3 (\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^{3} -magic map but no affinely generating one. We therefore propose the generating group distance magic conjecture, in which affine generation is replaced by ordinary generation. The conjecture is stated without a regularity assumption, as in the original group distance magic conjecture. For regular distance magic graphs we prove it whenever the labeling group is generated by at most two elements. In particular, it holds for regular distance magic graphs of cube-free order. 
 
 
 
 
 Combining our nonexistence result with the known sufficient direction gives an exact distance-magic existence criterion for Cayley graphs on elementary abelian  p p -groups. If  P ≅ ( ℤ / p ​ ℤ ) n P\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{n} , then 
 
 

 
 
 Cay ⁡ ( P , S ) ​  admits a  Γ -distance magic labeling for some abelian group  Γ  of order  | P | ⟺ p ∣ | S | . \begin{aligned} &\operatorname{Cay}(P,S)\text{ admits a $\Gamma$-distance magic labeling}\\
&\text{for some abelian group $\Gamma$ of order $|P|$}\end{aligned}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid|S|. 
 
 
 This criterion yields exact existence results for the Hamming relation graphs  H j ​ ( d , q ) H_{j}(d,q)  and their special cases. In each feasible case, the elementary abelian group underlying the Cayley graph can be used as the labeling group. These results do not classify an arbitrary prescribed labeling group. For comparison, Anholcer et al. proved that  Q d Q_{d}  is  Γ \Gamma -distance magic for every abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  2 d 2^{d}  if and only if  d d  is even  [ 1 ] . 
 
 
 
 
 A closely related nonbijective framework is that of group vertex magic labelings introduced by Kamatchi et al.  [ 13 ] . In that setting a labeling takes values in  Γ ∖ { 0 } \Gamma\setminus\{0\} , whereas the  Γ \Gamma -magic maps considered here allow the zero element. Our emphasis is instead on generation by the labels and by their pairwise differences. 
 
 
 
 
 The vertex-separation viewpoint in Theorem  1.2 (c)  has a binary predecessor in the theory of XOR-magic graphs. For a graph of order  2 n 2^{n} , an open XOR-magic labeling is precisely a  ( ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ ) n (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n} -distance magic labeling with magic constant  0 0 . A closed XOR-magic labeling is the corresponding closed-neighborhood analogue  [ 2 ] . For closed XOR-magic graphs, Siehler  [ 17 , Theorem 2]  obtained nullity and row-distinctness tests from the nullspace of the closed-neighborhood matrix. Batal  [ 2 , Proposition 8.1]  gave a matrix characterization for closed and open XOR-magic labelings with zero magic constant in which suitable null vectors are assembled as columns and the resulting rows are pairwise distinct. In the open case the relevant neighborhood operator is the adjacency matrix. Theorem  1.2 (c)  extends this row-separation framework from elementary abelian  2 2 -groups to arbitrary finite abelian labeling groups and allows arbitrary magic constants through the reduced modular kernel. 
 
 
 
 
 Section  2  records some finite-abelian-group facts and proves the modular characterizations including Theorem  1.2 . Section  3  connects the modular operators with the integral reduced adjacency operator, gives the reduced-Smith formulation, and gives an example showing that affine generation need not imply bijectivity. Section  4  records modular criteria for group distance magic and constructs a counterexample to the conjecture of Cichacz and Froncek, even with bijectivity weakened to affine generation. It then states the generating group distance magic conjecture and proves it for regular distance magic graphs when the labeling group has at most two generators, and consequently for regular distance magic graphs of cube-free order. Section  5  uses the modular and reduced-Smith criteria to study generating and affinely generating  Γ \Gamma -magic maps and  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labelings across several graph families. These include complete graphs, Cayley graphs on finite  p p -groups, Hamming relation graphs, classical forms graphs, strongly regular graphs, and incidence graphs of symmetric designs. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 2.  Modular characterizations 

 
 We first record elementary consequences of the affine difference group on regular graphs.

 The next lemma describes how ordinary generation differs from affine generation. 
 
 
 
 
 Lemma 2.4 . 
 
 Let  G G  be regular and let  Γ \Gamma  be a finite abelian group. The graph  G G  admits a generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map if and only if there exists a subgroup  H ≤ Γ H\leq\Gamma  such that  Γ / H \Gamma/H  is cyclic and  G G  admits an affinely generating  H H -magic map.

 5.3.  Strongly regular graphs

 Let  G G  be the incidence graph of a nontrivial symmetric  2 2 - ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda)  design. If  k k  is odd and  λ \lambda  is even, then, for every finite abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of even order, there is no affinely generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map. Hence there is no  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling for any abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of order  2 ​ v 2v . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Both  k k  and  k − λ k-\lambda  are odd, so  k ⁡ ( k − λ ) k(k-\lambda)  is odd. If  | Γ | |\Gamma|  is even, then  exp ⁡ ( Γ ) \ex(\Gamma)  is even and therefore cannot divide  k ⁡ ( k − λ ) k(k-\lambda) . Theorem  5.13 (a)  excludes affine generation, and the distance-magic assertion follows.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 We recall the design families used below. A finite projective plane of order  q q  is a symmetric  2 2 - ( q 2 + q + 1 , q + 1 , 1 ) (q^{2}+q+1,q+1,1)  design. For a prime power  q q  and  m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , let  P ​ G ​ ( m , q ) PG(m,q)  denote the  m m -dimensional projective space over  𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . Its points and hyperplanes, with incidence given by containment, form a symmetric  2 2 - ( ∑ i = 0 m q i , ∑ i = 0 m − 1 q i , ∑ i = 0 m − 2 q i ) \bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{m}q^{i},\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}q^{i},\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}q^{i}\bigr)  design.
A  Hadamard symmetric design  has parameters  2 2 - ( 4 ​ u − 1 , 2 ​ u − 1 , u − 1 ) (4u-1,2u-1,u-1) . Its complementary design has parameters  2 2 - ( 4 ​ u − 1 , 2 ​ u , u ) (4u-1,2u,u) . See  [ 4 ]  for background on these families. 
 
 
 
 
 Corollary 5.16 . 
 
 For each of the following graphs and every abelian group  Γ \Gamma  of the same order, there is no affinely generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map and hence no  Γ \Gamma -distance magic labeling, while a generating  Γ \Gamma -magic map exists if and only if  Γ \Gamma  is cyclic.

 The incidence graph of a finite projective plane. 
 
 
 
 (b)  
 
 The incidence graph of the point-hyperplane design of  P ​ G ​ ( m , q ) PG(m,q)  with  m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . 
 
 
 
 (c)  
 
 The incidence graph of a Hadamard symmetric design  2 2 - ( 4 ​ u − 1 , 2 ​ u − 1 , u − 1 ) (4u-1,2u-1,u-1)  with  u ≥ 2 u\geq 2 . 
 
 
 
 (d)  
 
 The incidence graph of the complementary Hadamard symmetric design  2 2 - ( 4 ​ u − 1 , 2 ​ u , u ) (4u-1,2u,u)  with  u ≥ 2 u\geq 2 . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 For a projective plane,  v = q 2 + q + 1 v=q^{2}+q+1  and  k = q + 1 k=q+1 , so  v − q ​ k = 1 v-qk=1 . For the point-hyperplane design of  P ​ G ​ ( m , q ) PG(m,q) , 
 
 

 
 
 v = 1 + q + ⋯ + q m and k = 1 + q + ⋯ + q m − 1 , v=1+q+\cdots+q^{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad k=1+q+\cdots+q^{m-1}, 
 
 
 so again  v − q ​ k = 1 v-qk=1 . For the two Hadamard parameter sets, one has 
 
 

 
 
 gcd ⁡ ( 4 ​ u − 1 , 2 ​ u − 1 ) = 1 and gcd ⁡ ( 4 ​ u − 1 , 2 ​ u ) = 1 . \gcd(4u-1,2u-1)=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gcd(4u-1,2u)=1. 
 
 
 Thus  gcd ⁡ ( v , k ) = 1 \gcd(v,k)=1  in every case, and Corollary  5.14  gives the stated conclusions in every case.
∎
