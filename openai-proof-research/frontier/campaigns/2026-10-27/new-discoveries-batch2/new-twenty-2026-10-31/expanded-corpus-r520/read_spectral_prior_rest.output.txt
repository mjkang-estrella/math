4.4    Common-neighbour counts inside one positive layer
    Fix 0 ≤ j ≤ i ≤ ⌊q/2⌋. For x ∈ P define N− (x+ ) := {z ∈ P : z − ∈ NΓi,j (x+ )}.

Proposition 4.6. For any distinct x, y ∈ P , the following hold in Γi,j .

  (i) If x ≁ y in G, then x+ ≁ y + and µΓi,j (x+ , y + ) = 2µG (x, y).

 (ii) If x ∼ y in G, then x+ ∼ y + and λΓi,j (x+ , y + ) = 2λG (x, y) + 2.

Proof. The positive layer is a copy of the point graph G. Therefore x+ ∼ y + if and only if x ∼ y
in G, and the positive-layer contribution to the number of common neighbours is |NG (x) ∩ NG (y)|.
We prove that the negative-layer contribution is

                         |N− (x+ ) ∩ N− (y + )| = |NG (x) ∩ NG (y)| + 2 · 1{x∼y} .                        (4)

   First suppose that x, y ∈ PΠ . Then x ∼ y in G. By Lemma 4.2(i), all vertices of PΠ− are
adjacent to both x+ and y + . Thus the contribution from PΠ− is |PΠ |, whereas NG (x) ∩ NG (y)
contains |PΠ | − 2 vertices from PΠ . Outside PΠ , the relevant line-label intersections are unchanged
by the twists, because outside parts of all line labels are unchanged. Hence (4) holds.

                                                       9
    Next suppose that x, y ∈ P \ PΠ . In the negative layer, the lines through x− and the lines
through y − have the same outside parts as the corresponding original line labels. By Lemma 4.2 (ii),
their pairwise intersection sizes, including their intersections inside PΠ− , are the same as before the
twist. Thus all common neighbours other than possibly x− and y − are counted exactly as in G.
The vertices x− and y − are common neighbours of x+ and y + precisely when x and y lie on a
common line of S, that is, precisely when x ∼ y in G. This proves (4) in this case.
    Finally suppose, without loss of generality, that x ∈ P \ PΠ and y ∈ PΠ . The outside common
neighbours other than possibly x− are again counted as in G, because outside parts of line labels
are unchanged by Lemma 4.2 (ii). The vertex x− is a common neighbour precisely when x ∼ y in
G. For the contribution from PΠ− , Lemma 4.2 (i) gives PΠ− ⊆ NΓi,j (y + ). Hence

                               N− (x+ ) ∩ N− (y + ) ∩ PΠ− = N− (x+ ) ∩ PΠ− .

The latter set is the union of the traces in PΠ− of the negative-layer realizations of the line labels
through x. By Lemma 4.2 (ii), its cardinality is equal to the corresponding untwisted cardinality.
In the original point graph, however, NG (x) ∩ NG (y) ∩ PΠ excludes the point y, while the trace
union through x contains y exactly when x ∼ y. Thus the PΠ− contribution is larger by 1{x∼y} ,
and together with the possible contribution of x− this gives (4).
    Adding the positive-layer contribution proves both assertions.
