## 365999: maximal completion at bounded multiplicative edge cost
Actual source: Calbet 2411.19267v1, Definitions 1–5, Conjecture 1, Lemma 1, Theorem 6, and conical-vertex reductions. The original (r,t)-system has a Kr-free underlying simple graph H, distinct t-sets F, each maximally K(r-1)-free in H, with every pair of sets intersecting in a K(r-2). Maximality adds the requirement that every missing underlying edge creates either Kr or a K(r-1) within one prescribed set. This is not maximality of the family F. The target compares minima over all such systems with exactly s family members, for each fixed r>=3,t>=r.

Route A, complete to maximal on the same vertices. Success: add any admissible edge until none remains. An existing set remains K(r-1)-free by admissibility; its maximality persists since the old K(r-1) witness for each outside vertex persists. Pair-intersection cliques persist, distinctness and sizes persist, and Kr-freeness is imposed at each step. Thus this gives a maximal system with the SAME s. Failure: it controls vertex count only, not edge cost. Given S in F, every vertex outside S has a neighbor in S: otherwise adjoining it cannot create K(r-1) (this also covers r=3). Selecting one such edge per outside vertex gives m-t<=e(H), exactly the source Lemma 1. Hence this completion gives only e'(s)<=binom(e(s)+t,2). Quadratic versus linear loss is the decisive gap. This argument is already source-owned, not a solution.

Route B, compress by membership types before completion. Set P(v)={S in F:v in S}. Vertices of the same type have the same constraints from F, but their neighborhoods outside the sets are not determined by P(v). Identifying them may destroy the witness clique showing that S is maximal, or may identify distinct members of F. Since s is variable, the naive upper bound 2^s on types is useless. A bounded-loss quotient must preserve all t-set witnesses and pair-intersection cliques with O(e(H)) total new edges; no such quotient was proved. In r=3, an edge xy can be inserted only if it creates no triangle and x,y do not occur together in any S. Thus even the independent-set case requires a sparse triangle-free completion with simultaneous prohibitions, not ordinary maximal triangle-free completion.

Route C, replace rather than complete. One can use the source explicit maximal constructions, so the task becomes comparing their cost to EVERY arbitrary system. Source Theorem 6 already pays t=r, t=r+1 and (r,t)=(3,5). For general t the displayed lower and upper exponents differ: s^(1/(t-r+2)) versus s^(4/(t-r+2)), and for r=3 the improved lower is s^(2/(t-3)). These bounds do not imply the conjecture. Adding conical vertices shifts r,t together, preserving t-r; it therefore cannot reduce all cases to the three paid cases. Missing lemma: for every fixed r,t, a maximal replacement with exactly s sets and cost <=C(r,t)e(H). No full route survives beyond this gap; PARK, no helper or paid-parameter credit.

## 352007: exact geometric saturation, including uniqueness
Actual source: O'Neill–Spiro 2109.09931v1, Eq.(1), Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.8 (proof read), Corollary 3.12, Proposition 3.13 (proof read), Lemmas 3.16–3.17, final proof and Conjecture 1. Material figures were not visually inspected. The exact target is all r>=3 for sufficiently large n(r), BOTH equality and uniqueness up to cyclic-order-preserving isomorphism.

Route A, constrained descent. The source classification encodes every saturated family by an odd cyclic tuple C of length 2l+1, with |[w_i,w_(i-1)]|>=r. The objective counts r-subsets meeting every interval. The source local move translates a consecutive run, giving strict concavity whenever both moves are semi-valid. Its counting proof partitions lost and gained edges and evaluates binomial sums, so it is a real tool, not just a stated heuristic. But semi-validity does not imply r-validity for l<r-1: a move may shorten an already-tight interval from r to r-1. Such a move leaves the feasible set, even when it improves the objective. Precisely these boundary configurations are not covered by the unconstrained minimizer. An extension needs a feasible descent or a classification of ALL boundary minima, not a scalar strengthening of the strictness estimate.

Route B, separate long and short tuples. For l>=r-1, Lemma 3.16 makes every semi-valid tuple r-valid. Corollary 3.12 puts its minimum at a consecutive tuple. Proposition 3.13 makes these minima increase with l, so this branch is minimized at l=r-1 and reproduces H_n^(r). Thus the unpaid competitors have 1<=l<=r-2, a bounded number of tuple lengths for fixed r, though their gap lengths are unbounded with n. This is a genuine useful reduction but already follows from the source. For l=1, the intervals are the three arcs between anchors, with overlap at their endpoints. Making two gaps as short as permitted suggests an objective exceeding H_n^(r) in its n^(r-2) term; a sign statement for the leading term alone is insufficient because minimizers may have several growing gaps and coefficients depend on them. Need a uniform inequality for the exact binomial expressions over every feasible gap vector, strict except at the desired family. No such inequality proved.

Route C, boundary splitting. At a tight interval of r vertices, insert two anchors to increase l while preserving all old hitting sets, then try to inject new edges into old edges that are lost. Merely splitting intervals goes in the wrong direction: it can add constraints and lose arbitrary hitting r-sets, and saturation may not persist unless the resulting tuple is r-valid. No global cardinal injection or equality classification was obtained. The source r=3 comparison uses a single exceptional triple versus n-5 triples; for r>=4 the affected classes have multiple interior choices, so substituting a binomial coefficient without a disjoint-class proof would be unjustified. Precise gap remains the short-tuple comparison above. Deliberate full conjecture is a strong significance case; no completed routine adaptation or prior exclusion is asserted. PARK.

## 368090: exact optimizer phase sequence
Actual source: Nagy 2504.14389v2 (24 Nov 2025 header), Theorem 2.3, proof via Claim 3.20, Conjecture 3.23, Claim 3.25 and surrounding remarks. It optimizes B_j(n,k,l)=sum_(a=j)^k binom(f(j,l),a)binom(n-f(j,l),k-a), with j>=ceil(l/3), f=2j-ceil(l/3) below 2l/3 and f=3j-l above. The literal suppresses that formula and the admissible j range. For l>=4, the literal phase numbered j=1 starts outside this range. That observation is at most a formulation/index correction, zero credit, not a substantive discovery.

Route A, Bernoulli reduction. For each FIXED admissible j, dividing by binom(n,k) gives a hypergeometric tail. If k/n tends to p in (0,1), each fixed membership pattern on f coordinates has probability (k)_a(n-k)_(f-a)/(n)_f tending to p^a(1-p)^(f-a). Summing finitely many patterns gives P(Bin(f,p)>=j). This is an ordinary finite-product limit, no experimental evidence. It does NOT justify interchanging maximum over 1<=j<=k with limit; the tail of growing j must be controlled uniformly, especially as p approaches 1/3.

Route B, consecutive differences in the upper branch. Put N=3j-l and X~Bin(N,p), Y~Bin(3,p) independently. Then the difference between the j+1 and j tails is exactly
 P(X=j-2)p^3 + P(X=j-1)(3p^2-2p^3) - P(X=j)(1-p)^3.
Reason: for X<=j-3 neither threshold is reached; X=j-2 needs Y=3; X=j-1 needs Y>=2; X=j loses only when Y=0; X>=j+1 succeeds for both. For j>=2 and N>=j this can be divided by P(X=j)>0 and the ratios are j/(N-j+1)*(1-p)/p and j(j-1)/((N-j+1)(N-j+2))*((1-p)/p)^2. Hence the sign is that of
 p*[j(j-1)+j(3-2p)(N-j+2)/(1-p)] - (N-j+1)(N-j+2)(1-p)
after a positive rescaling; more safely retain the preceding exact probability-ratio expression as the audited formula. This reduces adjacent comparisons to low-degree rational expressions. It does not establish ordered thresholds, rule out nonadjacent overtakings, or join the piecewise lower branch for general l. The source's Claim 3.25 already does the first j=1 versus j=2 comparison for l=2, so repeating that computation has zero credit.

Route C, large-j exclusion. For p<1/3 bounded away from 1/3, the threshold j exceeds the mean of Bin(3j-l,p) by a positive linear fraction for large j, suggesting an exponential tail. Even granting a standard bound, uniform hypergeometric versions and the finite initial phase comparisons are needed to prove an actual optimal finite j along EVERY sequence n,k in a phase interval. A finite list of polynomial crossings is not all-parameter control because the phase sequence is infinite. Exact missing lemmas: globally ordered unique consecutive crossings, no skipped optimizer, uniform escape exclusion, and correct treatment of admissible initial indices. No full endpoint, no parameter/helper credit. PARK.
