Personally derived elementary family. Let m>=2 and take the join J_m of m disjoint pentagons. Vertices in distinct pentagons are adjacent; each pentagon retains its cycle edges. This is connected, simple, and (5m-3)-regular on 5m vertices. Nonadjacent pairs occur only in one pentagon and have one internal common neighbor and all 5(m-1) external vertices as common neighbors. Thus mu=5m-4. The all-constant subspace gives eigenvalue 5m-3. Vectors constant on each pentagon with total block-constant sum zero give eigenvalue 2-5=-3, multiplicity m-1. The direct sum of the five-cycle zero-sum subspaces gives the two roots of x^2+x-1, each with multiplicity 2m. One way to verify this last assertion without numerics is that on the zero-sum subspace the C5 adjacency B satisfies B^2+B-I=0: B^2=2I+(J-I-B)=I+J-B. Both roots occur, since trace(B)=0 and the constant eigenvalue is 2; either single-root alternative on its four-dimensional complement would have trace 4r, which cannot equal -2 for a root of x^2+x-1. The two real roots lie respectively in (0,1) and (-2,-1). These invariant spaces exhaust dimension 1+(m-1)+4m=5m, so there are exactly four distinct eigenvalues and smallest -3.

There are no distinct equal closed neighborhoods. Within one pentagon the internal closed neighborhoods are different three-element cyclic intervals. For vertices in different pentagons, the closed neighborhood of one omits two vertices of its own pentagon whereas the other includes that pentagon in full. An s-clique extension with s>=2 has equal closed neighborhoods within each replacement clique. Therefore J_m is no such extension, even of an arbitrary graph. Fix t=3; the orders 5m are unbounded and refute the printed universal threshold conclusion.
