 Estrada and Mugnolo conjectured that the hubs-repelling Kirchhoff index dominates the ordinary Kirchhoff
 index, which in turn dominates the hubs-attracting index [1, Conjecture 4.13]. For the spectral quantity in
 their Lemma 4.6, the graph consisting of K₆ with a pendant path of five edges has R₁ = 1696684/14035 <
 370/3 = R₀. We give exact rational inverse certificates and a Lean dossier with uniqueness proofs over both
 the rationals and the reals. The independently reviewed experiment has no spectral failures on all 12,103
 connected graphs of orders 5 through 8, consistent with the source's reported computation. Global minimality
 remains open: the smallest spectral counterexample has order between 9 and 11.
 The source's potential, Moore-Penrose, and spectral descriptions are not mutually equivalent on irregular
 graphs. We explain the distinction without claiming credit for these observations. The spectral
 counterexample persists after that distinction is made; it is the substantive result reported here.

 1. Source objects and the precise question
 Let G be a finite simple connected unweighted graph on n ≥ 2 vertices. Write A for its adjacency matrix, dᵢ
 for its positive degrees, K = diag(dᵢ), and 1 for the all-ones column. The biased Laplacian in [1, (3.8)] is
 defined by the following entries (we also allow α = 0):
                               cα(i) = Σ     Aᵢ (dᵢ/d )^α, Lα = diag(cα) − K^α A K^(−α).

 Thus Lα1 = 0 and L₀ is the ordinary combinatorial Laplacian. The source uses α = 1 for hubs-repelling and α
 = −1 for hubs-attracting. Definition 4.1 defines resistance as a potential drop divided by the imposed current.
 Lemma 4.2, equation (4.3), instead specifies a Moore-Penrose matrix expression. For X = Lα it is
                                Ωα(i,j) = Xᵢᵢ + X         − Xᵢ   − X ᵢ; Rαᴹᴾ = Σᵢ<     Ωα(i,j).

 The sum is Definition 4.5, equation (4.15). Both off-diagonal entries are essential, since X need not be
 symmetric. Lemma 4.6, equation (4.16), asserts the different spectral expression
                                             Rαˢᵖ = n Σ      ₂ⁿ 1/ρα,   = n tr(Lα#).

 Here 0 = ρα,₁ < ρα,₂ ≤ ≤ ρα, are the eigenvalues and # denotes the group inverse. Section 3 below
 justifies this spectral interpretation independently of the source's Moore-Penrose argument. Conjecture 4.13,
 equation (4.25), asserts R₁ ≥ R₀ ≥ R ₁, with equality if and only if G is regular. Violating the first inequality
 already refutes that universal assertion, regardless of the interpretation of its equality clause.

 2. Why the three source descriptions separate
 The physical equation has a compatibility obstruction
 Set wᵢ = dᵢ^(−2α). For adjacent i,j the identity wᵢ(dᵢ/d )^α = (dᵢd )^(−α) is symmetric in i,j. Consequently
 wᵀLα = 0. The potential equation in the proof of Lemma 4.2, namely LαV = I(eᵢ − e ) [1, (4.7)], requires I(wᵢ
 − w ) = 0. For nonzero I and α = ±1 this requires equal degrees at the source and sink. An irregular graph has
 unequal-degree pairs, so this equation does not define an all-pairs physical resistance index.
 This is a necessary compatibility condition for the source's equation, rather than a proposed repair of its
 directional Ohm-law or antisymmetry assumptions. In particular, we do not claim that every possible
 corrected physical resistance model is refuted by our graph. For the witness below, w₀ − w₁₀ = −35/36 when α
 = 1, giving an explicit incompatible pair.

 The non-orthogonal similarity does not preserve Penrose inversion
 The source diagonalizes Lα using a non-orthogonal change of basis and treats the correspondingly
 transformed reciprocal-eigenvalue matrix as its Moore-Penrose inverse in the proof of Lemma 4.2; Lemma
 4.6 then uses its trace. Similarity preserves the group-inverse equations, but in general it does not preserve the
 two transpose equations defining the Moore-Penrose inverse. Orthogonal similarity would preserve those
 equations; the degree scaling used here is not orthogonal on irregular graphs.
 Accordingly, (4.3) and Lemma 4.6's formula (4.16) give different numbers. The source's equation numbered
 (4.6) is the vertex potential-balance equation, not the spectral formula; this numbering distinction matters
 when describing the inconsistency. For our witness at α = 1 the true Moore-Penrose pair sum is
 515538204/7137235, whereas the spectral value is 1696684/14035. The dossier contains proofs that the
 group inverse is not a Penrose inverse at this witness. These observations receive no separate discovery
 credit.

 The reported computation diagnoses the intended numerical quantity
 Before Conjecture 4.13, the authors report computations on more than 12,000 connected graphs with 5 ≤ n ≤
 8. The independent frozen review [R] checked the connected graph datasets exactly and obtained the
 following counts. No equality-versus-regularity failures were found in either reading.

 At n = 5 the graph K₄ with a pendant vertex already has R₁ᴹᴾ = 33856/4317 < 17/2 = R₀. The Moore-Penrose

 statement that the spectral ordering holds through n = 8. This finite enumeration is supporting computational
 evidence, not a Lean theorem or a proof of the conjecture.

 3. The eleven-vertex counterexample
 Theorem and graph
 Let H have vertices 0,…,10. Join every pair in {0,…,5}, and add edges {0,6}, {6,7}, {7,8}, {8,9}, {9,10}.
 This is K₆ with a pendant five-edge path, and it is simple and connected. Its degree sequence is
 (6,5,5,5,5,5,2,2,2,2,1). Then
                                        R₁ˢᵖ(H) = 1696684/14035 < 370/3 = R₀(H).

 Hence the spectral reading of Conjecture 4.13 is false. Independently, R₁ᴹᴾ(H) = 515538204/7137235 <
 370/3, so its literal Moore-Penrose reading is false as well. This is one spectral-ordering counterexample,
 with a secondary source inconsistency; it is not two claimed results.

 Ordinary proof of the spectral correspondence
 Let W = diag(w). The matrix WLα is a symmetric weighted Laplacian whose edge conductances are
 (dᵢd )^(−α). For any real x, xᵀWLαx is the sum over edges of (dᵢd )^(−α)(xᵢ − x )². It is nonnegative, and
 connectedness makes its kernel precisely the constants. Since W is positive diagonal, S =
 W^(1/2)LαW^(−1/2) = K^(−α)LαK^α is symmetric positive semidefinite with one-dimensional kernel. Thus
 Lα is diagonalizable, zero is simple, and every other eigenvalue is positive.
 In a diagonalization of Lα, replace the simple zero eigenvalue by zero and every other eigenvalue by its
 reciprocal. The resulting matrix satisfies LGL = L, GLG = G, and LG = GL. Uniqueness of the group inverse
 (proved below and in Lean) identifies it with Lα#. Trace is similarity invariant, so tr(Lα#) = Σ ₂ⁿ 1/ρα, .
 This argument justifies the spectral reading without using any similarity law for the Moore-Penrose inverse.
 The eigenvalue argument in this paragraph is ordinary mathematics, not part of the Lean bridge.

 Exact values and the decisive inequality
 The rational certificates give the following values; the α = −1 row entries come from the independent exact
 review, while the formal refutation requires only α = 0 and 1.

 Reading                          α = −1                     α=0                   α=1

 MP pair sum                      21930927/278132            370/3                 515538204/7137235

 Spectral trace                   1096887/11036              370/3                 1696684/14035

 Cross multiplication gives R₀ − R₁ˢᵖ = 102898/42105 > 0. No floating-point eigenvalue estimate is involved.
 As an independent check on the ordinary value, each clique pair has resistance 1/3, yielding 5 in total. Among
 vertices 0,6,…,10 the path contributes 35. For a clique vertex i ≠ 0 and a path vertex at distance t from 0,
 resistance is 1/3 + t. The 25 such pairs contribute 5(5/3 + 15) = 250/3. Their sum is 370/3. Section 4 explains
 how the biased value is certified by finite rational identities.

 4. Exact certificates and uniqueness
 A compact description of all matrix entries
 The full 11 × 11 rational Laplacians, Moore-Penrose inverses, group inverses, and intermediate products for α
 = 0,1 appear explicitly in formal/Certificates.lean. The independent review's witness-exact.json also supplies
 all three α values as arrays of rational strings. The following formulas describe these large matrices without
 printing hundreds of entries in the paper. They specify every entry once the displayed graph and degree
 sequence are fixed.
 Put P = 1wᵀ/(wᵀ1), U = 11ᵀ/n, and T = wwᵀ/(wᵀw). Then define
                                         G = (Lα + P) ¹ − P, M = (I − U)G(I − T).

 The rank-one P is the spectral projection onto ker Lα along im Lα: P² = P and LαP = PLα = 0. On the zero
 eigenspace, Lα + P acts as the identity; on every other eigenspace it acts as Lα. It is therefore invertible. This
 gives LG = GL = I − P and the three group-inverse identities. Since im L = w and ker L = span{1},
 projection yields LM = I − T and ML = I − U. Both are orthogonal projections; hence LML = L and MLM =
 M, with both LM and ML symmetric. This proves all four Penrose conditions for M.

 Finite checking and a sample of the construction
 Every construction step can use exact rational Gaussian elimination. For α = 1, w =
 (1/36,1/25,1/25,1/25,1/25,1/25,1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4,1), and wᵀ1 = 401/180. The first row of L₁ is
 (9,−6/5,−6/5,−6/5,−6/5,−6/5,−3,0,0,0,0); an ordinary clique vertex has diagonal 29/6, entry −5/6 to vertex 0,
 and four entries −1. Vertex 6 has row entries −1/3 at 0, 4/3 at 6, and −1 at 7. These rules and the edge formula
 in Section 1 determine L₁ completely.
 The formal certificate does not trust a Gaussian-elimination program. It checks the resulting matrix equations
 and index sums in the kernel using rational arithmetic. Separate certificates verify the four Penrose equations
 for M₀,M₁ and the three group-inverse equations for G₀,G₁. The graph module proves that its Laplacians
 equal the displayed L₀,L₁ and that the witness is connected. The index equalities then reduce to exact rational
 identities.

 Why the inverse cannot be chosen differently
 Here is an algebraic uniqueness proof, valid over the reals or rationals. If X,Y are Penrose inverses of A, then
 (AX)(AY) = AY and (AY)(AX) = AX. Transposing the first identity, using symmetry of AX and AY,
 identifies AX = AY. Similarly the symmetric products XA and YA agree. Thus X = XAX = YAX = YAY =
 Y. If X,Y are group inverses, commutation gives AX = (AYA)X = Y(AXA) = YA = AY; then X = XAX =
 XAY = AXY = AYY = YAY = Y. This removes all inverse-choice ambiguity. In particular, existential
 inverse data in the conjecture cannot avoid the certified strict inequality. Both uniqueness arguments are
 formalized over the rationals in Basic.lean and repeated over the reals in RealBridge.lean.

 6. Minimality, related work, and access limits
 Minimality and scope
 The independent exact review [R] enumerated connected graphs through order 8 and found no spectral
 ordering violation. The witness gives an upper bound of 11, so the smallest spectral counterexample order lies
 in [9,11]. Orders 9 and 10 were not exhaustively tested. Within the clique-plus-pendant family K with a
 path of n−k edges (2 ≤ k < n), the review searched 3 ≤ n ≤ 11; its first failure is n = 11, k = 6. This establishes
 the first failing order in that family, not global minimality. The graph datasets' exhaustiveness at orders 5–8 is
 inherited from their published coverage and the review's parser/count checks, rather than formally certified
 enumeration.
 Only the universal ordering is refuted. No replacement classification, extremal bound, asymptotic claim, or
 statement about arbitrary repaired physical resistance is established. The equality condition need not be used.
 In particular, the secondary MP defect within the authors' computational range receives no separate credit.
