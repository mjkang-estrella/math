The search didn't turn up a proof of a Θ(n^(k-1)) treewidth result for token graphs of general trees. The closest match is a 2024 arXiv paper, "On the Treewidth of Token and Johnson Graphs" by Fabila-Monroy, Gómez-Galicia, Hernández-Cruz, and Trujillo-Negrete, which proves the Θ(n^(k-1)) bound only for stars. For Theorem 1 concerns the star graph on n+1 vertices, with 1 ≤ k ≤ n fixed, and gives tw(F_k(S_n)) = Θ(n^(k-1)). The paper also treats paths and gives an upper bound for the Johnson graph J(n,k), which is isomorphic to F_k(K_n). The authors conjecture that their upper bound for the treewidth of F_k(K_n) is tight. So the Θ(n^(k-1)) statement for complete graphs is still a conjecture in that source, not a proof.

The paper also shows the treewidth of a tree is one (the treewidth of a tree is one, while the treewidth of the complete graph on n vertices is n − 1), which is relevant context for why the question is about bounds on token graphs of trees rather than trees themselves. Another related paper, "On the Connectivity of Token Graphs of Trees" by Fabila-Monroy, Leaños, and Trujillo-Negrete (DMTCS, 2022), addresses a different parameter, connectivity, not treewidth.

If you need the exact statement for general trees, I'd check the full text of arXiv 2402.17962 directly, since my search only returned excerpts and didn't show whether the paper addresses general trees.
