The open question is still open. The best known bound for the general case is O(n²/log n), and the subquadratic result that exists applies only to special classes of matchings.

- **The conjecture:** Conlon, Fox, Lee, and Sudakov conjectured that some fixed ε > 0 gives r<(M<, K<₃) = O(n^(2−ε)) for every ordered matching M< on n vertices. The best-known upper bound is O(n²/log n), whereas the best-known lower bound is Ω((n/log n)^(4/3)), and Conlon et al. hypothesize that there is some fixed ε > 0 such that r<(M,K3) = O(n^(2−ε)) for every ordered matching M.

- **Why the interval chromatic number matters:** In order to get a subquadratic upper bound on r<(M<, K<3) one has to be able to employ the sparsity of M<, which is why matchings with interval chromatic number 2 are the natural target.

- **Rohatgi's result:** Rohatgi showed a subquadratic bound for almost all ordered matchings with interval chromatic number 2, with exponent 24/13. The upper bound was improved from O(n^(24/13)) to O(n^(7/4)).

- **Balko and Poljak (2023/2024):** For a uniformly random ordered matching with interval chromatic number 2, they show that with high probability r<(M<, K<₃) ≤ c·n^(7/4). The difference between the exponent in the lower bound and the exponent in the upper bound is exactly 1/2. Their lower bound for this random case is Ω((n/log n)^(5/4)).

- **Open problem:** Problem 1 still remains wide open, meaning the worst-case question of whether every n-vertex ordered matching has r<(M<, K<₃) ∈ O(n^(2−ε)) remains unresolved. The 2023 paper's abstract mentions these results are for almost all or random matchings, not all matchings.

Sources are inconsistent on one detail: the arXiv and journal versions state the random χ=2 lower bound at (n/log n)^(5/4), while the Eurocomb extended abstract lists the lower bound at (n/log n)^(5/4) for that case. The main sources agree on the upper bound of O(n^(7/4)).
