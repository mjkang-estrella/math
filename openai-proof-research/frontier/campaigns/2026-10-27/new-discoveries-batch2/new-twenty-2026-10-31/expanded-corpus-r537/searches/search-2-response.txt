The search turned up related work on token graphs and treewidth, but nothing that directly matches the full query, especially the "separator congestion" and "trees" conjecture parts.

**Treewidth of token graphs.** The most relevant paper is by Fabila-Monroy and coauthors, "On the Treewidth of Token and Johnson Graphs." It defines the k-token graph as the graph whose vertex set consists of all k-subsets of vertices of G, where two vertices A and B are adjacent in Fk(G) whenever their symmetric difference A△B is an edge of G. The paper gives exact or order-of-magnitude results only for specific base graphs. For stars, tw(Fk(Sn)) = Θ(n^(k−1)). For complete graphs, the result is tw(Fk(Kn)) ∈ Θ(n^k), and the authors conjecture that our upper bound for the treewidth of Fk(Kn) is tight. Their lower bounds rely on a border-based argument and a spectral gap result, which is a special case of Aldous' spectral gap conjecture. This conjecture was proved by Caputo, Liggett and Richthammer.

**Related connectivity bounds.** Leaños and Trujillo-Negrete showed that if G is t-connected and t ≥ k, then F_k(G) is at least k(t-k+1)-connected. A later paper extended this to edge-connectivity, and a further paper on trees found that if G is a tree, then the connectivity of Fk(G) is equal to the minimum degree of Fk(G).

**Spanning tree congestion.** The congestion search returned a 2024 paper showing that Spanning Tree Congestion is not FPT parameterized by treewidth (under standard assumptions). This concerns spanning trees of a graph, not token graphs, so it doesn't establish a separator-congestion lower bound on treewidth of token graphs.

**Gaps.** I found no source for a "Fabila-Monroy conjecture" about trees, nor any result linking separator congestion to treewidth lower bounds for k-token graphs of general connected graphs. The original paper's conjecture concerns K_n only. Checking the paper's full text or the authors' later work would be the next step.
