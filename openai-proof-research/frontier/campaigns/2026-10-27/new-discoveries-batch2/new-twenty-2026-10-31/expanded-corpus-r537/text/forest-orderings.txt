                                           Given a class of (undirected) graphs C, we say that a Feedback Arc Set (FAS for short) F is a C-FAS if the graph
                                           induced by the edges of F (forgetting their orientations) belongs to C. We show that deciding if a tournament has
                                           a C-FAS is NP-complete when C is the class of all forests. We are motivated by connections between C-FAS and
                                           structural parameters of tournaments, such as the dichromatic number, the clique number of tournaments, and the
                                           strong Erds-Hajnal property.

                                           Keywords: Forests, Tournaments, Feedback Arc Set



                                           1        Introduction
                                           Given a tournament T , a Feedback Arc Set (FAS for short) is a set of arcs F of T such that T \ F is
                                           acyclic. Feedback arc sets in tournaments are well studied from the combinatorial [16, 17, 22, 23, 30, 34],
                                           statistical [24], and algorithmic [3, 4, 10, 15, 12, 20, 32, 33] points of view.
                                              Given a class of (undirected) graph C, we say that a FAS is a C-FAS if the graph induced by the edges
                                           of F (forgetting their orientations) belongs to C, and we call C-FAS Problem the associated decision
                                           problem, that is deciding if a tournament has a C-FAS. The goal of this paper is to prove the following:
                                           Theorem 1.1. The C-FAS Problem is NP-complete when C is the set of forests.
                                           Motivations and related works Let us motivate the C-FAS Problem by showing its link with several
                                           studied problems in tournament theory. First, let us abuse notation and refer to a FAS F of a tournament
                                           as the undirected graph induced by the edges of F (forgetting their orientations).
                                              Given a tournament T , and a total order ≺ on V (T ), we denote by T ≺ the (undirected) graph with
                                           vertex set V (T ) and edge uv if u ≺ v and vu ∈ A(D). We call it the backedge graph of T with respect
                                           to ≺. Observe that F is a FAS of T if and only if there is an ordering ≺ such that F = T ≺ .

   A tournament H has the strong Erdős-Hajnal property if there exists ϵH > 0 such that every H-free
tournament T contains two disjoint subset of vertices A and B, such that |A|, |B| ≥ ϵH |V (T )| and all
arcs between A and B are oriented from A to B. Chudnovsky et al. [14] conjectured that a tournament H
has the strong Erdős-Hajnal property if and only if H admits a FAS inducing a forest, showing that these
tournaments might play an important role in tournament theory.

Structure of the paper Definitions and some preliminaries results are given in section 2. The proof of
Theorem 1.1 is a reduction from 3-SAT. Given an instance I of 3-SAT, we construct a tournament TI
such that TI has polynomial size (in the size of I) and has a forest-ordering if and only if I is a satisfiable
instance. We explain how to construct TI in Section 3.1. We prove that if TI has a forest-ordering, then
I is a satisfiable instance in Section 3.2, and finally, we prove that if I is a satisfiable instance, then TI
has a forest-ordering in Section 3.3.


2    Definitions and preliminaries
We refer the reader to [7] for snoteAtandard definitions.
  An orientation of a graph G is the digraph obtained by assigning an orientation to every edge of G. A
tournament is an orientation of a complete graph. If D is a digraph, we denote by V (D) and A(D) the
Finding forest-orderings of tournaments is NP-complete                                                               3

set of vertices and arcs of D, respectively. The transitive tournament on n vertices is the unique acyclic
tournament on n vertices.
   An ordered tournament is a pair (T, ≺) where T is a tournament and ≺ is a total ordering of V (T ). If
uv ∈ A(T ) with v ≺ u we say that uv is a back-arc of (T, ≺) and a forward-arc otherwise. We denote
by T ≺ the (undirected) graph with vertex set V (T ) and edge set {uv | v ≺ u and uv ∈ A(T )}, and call it
the backedge graph of T with respect to ≺. A topological-ordering of T is an ordering of V (T ) such that
every arc is a forward arc. Note that only transitive tournaments admit a topological ordering. A (tree)
forest-ordering of T is an ordering ≺ of V (T ) such that T ≺ is a (tree) forest.
   For an ordered tournament (T, ≺) and X, Y ⊆ V (T ), we write X ≺ Y to say that every vertex of X
precedes every vertex of Y . If X = {v} we drop the brackets from the notation and simply write v ≺ Y
or Y ≺ v. If V (T ′ ) ≺ v or v ≺ V (T ′ ) for a subtournament T ′ of T , we shorten the notation to T ′ ≺ v
and v ≺ T ′ .
   If x, y are vertices of a digraph D, we may also write x → y to say that there is an arc from x to y.
Given two disjoint sets of vertices X, Y of D, we write X ⇒ Y to say that for every x ∈ X and for every
y ∈ Y , xy ∈ A(D). When X = {x} or Y = {y}, we simply write x ⇒ Y or X ⇒ {y}, respectively. We
also use the symbol ⇒ to denote a composition operation on tournaments: for two tournaments T1 and
T2 , T1 ⇒ T2 is the digraph obtained from the disjoint union of T1 and T2 by adding all arcs from V (T1 )
to V (T2 ).
   For a positive integer k, we denote by [k] the set {1, . . . , k}.

3.1     The construction of TI

that for i ∈ [k], each clause Ci is formed by an ordered trio of literals. This naturally implies an ordering
ℓ1 , ℓ2 , ℓ3 , . . ., ℓ3k−2 , ℓ3k−1 , ℓ3k of the literals as they occur in the clauses, i.e. for j ∈ [k] we have
Cj = (ℓ3j−2 ∨ ℓ3j−1 ∨ ℓ3j ).
    This subsection is dedicated to the construction of the tournament TI from I. We construct an ordered
tournament (TI , ≺∗ ) where the order ≺∗ is used to simplify the description of TI , as many arcs of TI are
forward-arcs with respect to ≺∗ . The order ≺∗ is also used in Section 3.3 where, given an assignment of
