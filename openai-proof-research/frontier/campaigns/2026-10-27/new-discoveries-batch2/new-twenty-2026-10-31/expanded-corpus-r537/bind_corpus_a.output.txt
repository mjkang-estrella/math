Let $F$ be a bipartite graph containing a cycle. Suppose that, for some exponent $alpha$ and constant $c_F$,

$$
\textup{ex}(n,F)=c_F n^{\alpha}+o(n^{\alpha}).
$$

**Infinite Turán strict-inequality conjecture.** The infinite Turán number satisfies

$$
\textup{ex}(\infty,F)<c_F.
$$

The claim predicts that replacing the finite extremal asymptotic's limit superior by the limit inferior in the infinite setting always causes a strict loss for bipartite graphs containing a cycle. The paper establishes related bounds for complete bipartite graphs, but does not resolve this general statement.

Let $X(\infty)$ be the limiting estimator produced by the memoryless iterative process on an interval graph of length $n$, and let $Z(\infty)$ be the benchmark estimator used in the paper. **Variance-growth conjecture.**

$$
\operatorname{Var}[X(\infty)]\propto \sqrt{n}\operatorname{Var}[Z(\infty)].
$$

The paper motivates this claim from the conjectured Gaussian coefficient profile: although the mean-square error tends to zero, the iterative estimator becomes increasingly inefficient relative to $Z(\infty)$ as the interval length grows. Its precise asymptotic meaning and proof remain open.

Let $r$ and $n$ be integers with $r\geq 5$ and $n\geq r+3$, and let $G$ be a Hamiltonian $r$-regular graph on $n$ vertices. **Haythorpe's conjecture.** The graph $G$ has at least

$$
(r-1)^2 ((r-2)!)^{\frac{n}{r+1}}
$$

Hamiltonian cycles. The conjecture proposes an asymptotic lower bound matching the construction discussed in the source, while the paper explains that its upper-bound results disprove the conjecture in a strong asymptotic sense.

----- 337405 1827 True 1856
Let $W_t$ denote the wheel on $t+1$ vertices, and let $t\geq 3$. For a graph $G$, a wheel model is a model of $W_t$ in $G$.

**Uniform wheel-model Erdős–Pósa conjecture.** There are a constant $c>0$ and a function $g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ such that, for every integer $t\geq 3$, wheel models in $G$ have the Erdős–Pósa property with bounding function

$$
c k\log k+g(t)k.
$$

The paper proves an $O(k\log k)$ bound for each fixed wheel, while this conjecture asks that the coefficient of the logarithmic term be independent of the wheel size $t$. It is motivated by the analogous result for cycle models and remains open in the source.

$$
R(T_n,F_m)=2n-1.
$$

This conjecture extends the known exact result for stars versus fans and asserts that the Burr lower bound is attained for all sufficiently large trees in this range. Its resolution is not established in the supplied source context.

Let $G$ be a $k$-regular matching-covered graph, meaning that every edge of $G$ belongs to a perfect matching. **Lovász–Plummer regular-graph conjecture.** For every $k\geq 3$, there exist constants $c_1(k),c_2(k)>0$ such that $G$ contains at least

$$
c_2(k)c_1(k)^{|V(G)|}
$$

perfect matchings. Furthermore,

$$
c_1(k)\to\infty\quad\text{as }k\to\infty.
$$

This is presented as a generalization of the cubic-graph conjecture; the supplied text gives no resolution of the general statement.

----- 327681 2173 True 1896
Let $P_m$ denote the path on $m$ vertices, and let $\tilde{r}(P_a,P_b)$ denote the online Ramsey number for the pair of graphs $P_a$ and $P_b$.

**Path Ramsey conjecture.** For $k\ge 4$,

$$
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\tilde{r}(P_{k+1},P_{\ell+1})}{\ell}=\frac{3}{2}.
$$

Moreover, for all integers $\ell\ge k\ge 4$,

$$
\tilde{r}(P_{k+1},P_{\ell+1})=\left\lceil\frac{3\ell}{2}\right\rceil+k-3.
$$

In particular,

$$
\tilde{r}(P_{k+1})=\left\lceil\frac{5k}{2}\right\rceil-3\quad\text{for }k\ge 4.
$$

For fixed $k\ge 4$, the paper gives only bounds between $3/2$ and $2$ for the limiting ratio, so both the asymptotic value and the proposed exact formula remain open.
