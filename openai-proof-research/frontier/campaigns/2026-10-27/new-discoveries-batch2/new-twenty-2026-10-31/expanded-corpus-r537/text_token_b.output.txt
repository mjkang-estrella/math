  We say that two subsets of V (G) touch if either they have a vertex in common, or there is an edge
between them in G. A bramble, B, is a family of pairwise touching subsets of vertices of G, each of which
induces a connected subgraph. A subset S of vertices of G is said to cover B, if every set of B contains a
vertex in S; we call S a hitting set for B. The order of B, denoted ||B||, is the least number of vertices in
a hitting set of B. The bramble number of G, denoted by bn(G), is the maximum order of a bramble of G.
Seymour and Thomas found the following duality theorem, which relates brambles and treewidth.
