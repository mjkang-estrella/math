## 338791: explicit four-column central-algebra collision, intended rectangular reading

Source: Nenashev, arXiv:1803.09966v1 (27 March 2018), pp.1–2 definition of the facet power ideal, p.5 Definition 1 (z-equivalence), p.6 Conjecture 2. Actual source passages are retained as text with PDF-byte hashes in HTTP receipts. Let

A(t) = [[1,0,1,1],[0,1,1,t]], t in R minus {0,1}.

All columns are nonzero. Every pair is independent except none: determinants are 1,1,t,-1,-1,t-1, up to ordering. Rank is two. After deletion of any column there remain two independent columns, so there are NO bridge-columns. Thus the prescribed bridge deletion changes neither matrix, under the natural 2 by 4 interpretation of A'.

The four column lines are 0,infinity,1,t in the real projective line. A supporting face of a Minkowski sum of segments is the sum of their maximizing faces. For a nonzero covector in dimension two, its maximizing face is an edge exactly when it annihilates one of the column lines: none are parallel, so at most one segment is free. Conversely each column has a nonzero annihilator and gives an edge. Consequently all facet normals, up to nonzero scalar and sign, are x,y,x-y,t*x-y. Exactly three columns have nonzero pairing with each such normal. The source CENTRAL (k=0) ideal is therefore

I(t)=(x^3,y^3,(x-y)^3,(t*x-y)^3).

There are no lower-degree generators. Put X=x^2*y, Y=x*y^2, and

u=((x-y)^3-x^3+y^3)/3=-X+Y,
v=((t*x-y)^3-t^3*x^3+y^3)/(3*t)=-t*X+Y.

Then X=(u-v)/(t-1), Y=u+X belong to I(t). This pays BOTH inclusions I(t)=(x,y)^3: all four cubics are in the latter, and x^3,X,Y,y^3 generate that monomial ideal. In particular both central algebras for t=2 and t=3 are literally R[x,y]/(x,y)^3, as graded and ungraded algebras. A basis is 1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2; this basis is a consequence, not an inference from equal Hilbert series.

They are not z-equivalent. Equality of row spaces up to invertible column scalings and permutation, for two full-row-rank matrices, would give an invertible 2 by 2 row transformation relating the scaled/permuted matrices. This induces a projectivity matching the unordered four column lines. Define the cross-ratio invariant

J(t)=(t^2-t+1)^3/(t^2*(t-1)^2).

A projectivity leaves the ordered cross-ratio invariant. Relabeling four points changes its value by transformations generated by t -> 1-t and t -> 1/t: this follows by interchanging adjacent entries in the determinant formula for cross-ratio. Direct substitution leaves J invariant under each generator, hence under every relabeling. The invariant values are J(2)=27/4=243/36 and J(3)=343/36, unequal. Thus no such projectivity, and no z-equivalence, exists. This is a complete ordinary refutation of the intended rectangular matrix Conjecture 2, subject to authenticating that this remains the source's intended endpoint.

A second route was checked beyond equal Hilbert series: adapting source Theorem 8's external-algebra reconstruction. Its proof pp.6–8 uses square-free embedding and nilpotency lengths to recover projective column multiplicities. Here every nonzero degree-one element has nonzero square and zero cube. This length is constantly two and cannot distinguish any of the four column directions; the central quotient calculation above explains precisely why the external reconstruction does not transfer. Theorem 8 is not a subsumer, and this is not a routine-exclusion finding based on ease.

## 339366: FKM, three concrete mechanisms and remaining gap

DiMuro arXiv:1805.11641v1, §5 pp.15–16, Conjecture 2 is exactly the necklace alpha-suffix equivalence. A necklace is the lexicographically least rotation; FKM concatenates aperiodic prefixes in lexicographic necklace order. Source Theorem 1 controls Greedy through increasability, not the required FKM ordering.

Route 1: replace the known k-suffix theorem's coordinate target k^n by alpha. This fails to give a reduction: alphabet relabeling acts uniformly on every position, while alpha has position-dependent symbols; rotation would move those bounds. Need an actual order-preserving conjugacy respecting rotation AND aperiodic-prefix concatenation. No such conjugacy was proved.

Route 2: use DiMuro's game proof Theorem 1 and the §5 proposed prisoner strategy to force necklaces to occur in lexicographic order. Theorem 1 only ensures increasability of all rotations to the target; it does not order all necklace encounters or prove that intervening words equal the aperiodic-prefix blocks. Required lemma: under alpha-suffix closure of the NECKLACE SET, the winning-game cycle between successive necklaces is exactly the FKM block, for arbitrary periods, including the wrap from the maximum to minimum. Neither direction of the full equivalence follows from the actual game theorem without this lemma.

Route 3: try the source's explicit 18-word example from Sawada–Williams–Wong 2016. Its necklaces are 1112,1122,1212,1222,1322 with maximum alpha=1322. Each is coordinatewise <=1322. Every suffix replacement is again in this five-element list: replacing positions 1,2,3,4 onward yields, respectively,1322;1322; either1122 or1222 or1322; or the unchanged word (last entry already2). These are necklaces. Thus the known failure of the stronger 3-suffix condition is NOT a counterexample to alpha-suffix necessity. No new witness is claimed. General necessity would require deriving every coordinatewise bound and suffix-closure from the actual cyclic sliding-window condition, still unpaid. Companion theorem body comparison is separately recorded; source examples alone do not settle general necessity. PARK/HOLD, no Pi.
