                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                             The invariant now known as the Davenport constant D(G) of a finite abelian group G
                                          was already studied by Rogers [49] in 1963 and was later popularized by H. Davenport.
                                          It has its origins in the arithmetic of ideal class groups: if G is the ideal class group of a
                                          number field, then D(G) is the maximal number of prime ideals, counted with multiplicity,
                                          occurring in the prime ideal factorization of a principal ideal generated by an irreducible
                                          element. It has since developed deep connections with the factorization theory of Krull
                                          monoids, algebraic and classical number theory, coding theory; see, for instance, [1, 15,
                                          21, 22, 24, 25, 29, 42].
                                             Determining its exact value, or even obtaining sharp general bounds, is therefore a fun-
                                          damental problem with implications for several major areas of mathematics. A striking
                                          example of its number-theoretic significance is the proof by Alford, Granville, and Pomer-
                                          ance [1] that there exist infinitely many Carmichael numbers, in which an upper bound
                                          for the Davenport constant plays a crucial role.

For a long time, it was a prevailing expectation that the difference D(G) − D∗ (G) should
admit an upper bound depending only on the rank of G. This expectation was formulated
explicitly in the following conjecture of Gao and Geroldinger.
Conjecture 1 (Gao–Geroldinger, [15, Conjecture 3.7]). Every nontrivial finite abelian
group G satisfies
                          D(G) − D∗ (G) ≤ r(G) − 1.
   This conjecture is closely connected with the arithmetic of algebraic number fields.
In fact, it is implied by the following conjecture of Narkiewicz and Śliwa, proposed in
1982, concerning the first Narkiewicz constant, which arises in the asymptotic counting
of algebraic integers admitting unique factorization into irreducibles. We introduce this
constant in Section 4.
Conjecture 2 (Narkiewicz–Śliwa, [46]). Let G = CnP      1 ⊕ · · · ⊕ Cnr be a nontrivial finite
abelian group, where 1 < n1 | · · · | nr . Then N1 (G) = ri=1 ni .
   The inequality D(G) ≤ N1 (G), established in Section 4, shows that the Narkiewicz–Śliwa
conjecture would imply Conjecture 1. Hence, a possible rank-dependent upper bound for
this difference D(G) − D∗ (G) is therefore of particular importance, with implications for
problems in algebraic and classical number theory. Geroldinger, Grynkiewicz, and Zhong
also highlighted this question as a challenging open problem in their monograph published
in 2026.
Problem 1.1 (Geroldinger–Grynkiewicz–Zhong, [24, Appendix B, Problem 4(a)]). Does
there exist a function f : N → [0, ∞) such that
                                 D(G) − D∗ (G) ≤ f (r(G))
for every nontrivial finite abelian group G? In particular, can f be chosen linear?
   In this paper, we prove that, for every integer r ≥ 5, the difference D(G) − D∗ (G) is
unbounded among finite abelian groups G of rank r. Consequently, there is no finite-
valued function f : N → [0, ∞) such that D(G) ≤ D∗ (G) + f (r(G)) for every nontrivial
finite abelian group G, giving a negative answer to Problem 1.1. As a corollary, our result
disproves Conjectures 1 and 2, thereby refuting a long-standing expectation in the study
of the Davenport constant and the arithmetic of ideal class groups. Furthermore, we
apply our construction to disprove a conjecture of Girard in 2008 concerning cross number
(Conjecture 4 in Section 5), an invariant deeply rooted in the arithmetic of number fields
and Krull monoids.
