## 325400: connected fair sets

Exact definitions: finite simple connected undirected G; d is shortest-path distance; for nonempty S, f(v,S)=max_{s in S}d(v,s)-min_{s in S}d(v,s); FC(S) is the entire set of global minimizers, not a selected minimizer. The source calls FC(S) a fair set for |S|>1. Singletons S give V(G) anyway. The conjecture is the equivalence with being a block graph. A counterexample must be a nonblock graph with EVERY fair set connected. Producing a disconnected fair set in a nonblock graph supports the conjecture and is not a refutation.

Reduction proved here: suppose B is a block of G and S is a subset of B. B is isometric. Each component outside B attaches to B at exactly one vertex c; otherwise the block would not be maximal. For x in that component, d_G(x,s)=d_G(x,c)+d_B(c,s) for every s in S. Thus f_G(x,S)=f_B(c,S). The minimum over G equals the minimum over B. Map each outside component to its attachment c. A path inside FC_G(S) projects to a walk inside FC_B(S): adjacent projections agree or are adjacent. Consequently disconnectedness of FC_B(S) persists in G. Thus a putative counterexample can have no block which itself admits a disconnected fair center. This proves the soundness of restricting witness search to 2-connected noncliques; it does NOT prove that every such graph is obstructed.

A further complete restricted lemma: every triangle-free 2-connected diameter-two graph on at least five vertices has a disconnected fair set. Choose nonadjacent u,v and S=V minus {u,v}. For x in S, f(x,S) is 1 or 2, and equals 1 exactly when x is adjacent to all other vertices of S. There is at most one such x, since two such vertices together with a third S vertex form a triangle. If u has f(u,S)=0, u is adjacent to all S. Those vertices form an independent set. Since G is 2-connected each has degree at least two and therefore is also adjacent to v. Thus G=K_{2,n-2}, and FC(S)={u,v} is disconnected. The same applies to v. Otherwise u,v both have partiality one. Any x in S of partiality one cannot be adjacent to u: u has degree at least two, uv is absent, and another neighbor y of u belongs to S; the edge xy would create triangle uxy. Likewise x is not adjacent to v. Therefore FC(S) consists of u,v and at most one further vertex, with no edges, and is disconnected. Four-vertex case C4 is already covered by the printed even-cycle mechanism. This lemma is only a search reduction, not a significant solution or candidate for credit.

Failed generalization: induced chordless cycles alone do not transfer their fair centers to G; external vertices change distances and may join components. Isometric embedding alone also does not give the attachment projection used for blocks. A proof for all graphs still needs a mechanism producing S in an arbitrary 2-connected nonclique with triangles and/or diameter at least three. No such lemma has been established.

## 326140 / 326141: intended nonempty zero-sum mechanisms

The actual v2 definition is Sigma(S)={sigma(T): T divides S}, including the empty subsequence. Therefore 0 belongs to every Sigma(S). Conjecture 23 as printed asks H intersect Sigma(T/TH) empty, impossible for every subgroup H. Conjecture 22 does not require T0 nonempty, so the empty subsequence is already a uniquely represented sum of length zero for every zero-sum-free T. These issues persist in v1 (Conjectures 24/25). Counting either observation would be a prohibited wording/endpoint correction. No Lean task is proposed for them.

For intended nonempty semantics, a useful encoding is a multiplicity vector m_g on nonzero vectors of F_p^k. Zero-sum-freeness means sum a_g g !=0 for all nonzero integer vectors 0<=a_g<=m_g. A short nonempty indexed subsequence can have unique sum only if each repeated value g is either wholly selected or wholly omitted: a partially selected repeat has another selection of indices with the same sum. Among such unions of full value classes, uniqueness still requires exactly one bounded coefficient vector with that sum. This reduces the intended unique-sum test to coefficient sums plus binomial multiplicities; it does not settle the conjecture.

One sustained family check, by reasoning: T=e1^a e2^b (e1+e2)^c, 1<=a,b,c<p. It is zero-sum-free exactly when min(a,b)+c<p. Indeed a zero sum would require x=y=p-z with 1<=z<=c. The diagonal line is hit by the other two value classes, but for BOTH coordinate lines to be hit by the remaining classes requires b+c>=p AND a+c>=p, contradicting zero-sum-freeness. Thus this whole natural three-direction family cannot refute the intended separation conjecture. Moving to a fourth projective direction is the first missing mechanism, not a completed obstruction.

## 325648: a full ordinary counterexample, rejected as publicly resolved

A simple parity construction gives a literal endpoint: let each of three sides be {0,1} x {0,1,2}; include all triples whose three first coordinates have even sum. Every vertex has 2*3*3=18 incident edges; edges are distinct; each side has n=6 vertices. Any matching of size six covers all vertices. It would use exactly nine first-coordinate-1 vertices, whereas every edge uses an even number. Contradiction. The conjectured lower bound is ceil(17*6/18)=6. Hence this 18-vertex, 108-edge hypergraph refutes the literal statement. This construction is the standard parity barrier with three twins per base vertex. It is not proposed as new credit.

The source's full example inventory is Examples 1.4 (repeated shifted matching), 1.5 (Wanless even-order augmentation), 4.9 (repeated-edge hypergraph obstruction), plus the printed cyclic sharpness family following Theorem 4.21. The later primary manuscript Ramsey-nice families of graphs (Aharoni et al., dated August 22, 2017), Section 5, explicitly refutes source Conjecture 4.23 in a stronger way for every d>=4. It uses m=floor(3d/2), A=[d], B=[m] minus A, triples (xi,yi,zj),(xi,yj,zi),(xj,yi,zi) for i in A,j in B, and diagonal triples for odd d. Each edge is assigned its repeated A-index i; edges assigned the same index intersect, so a matching has at most d edges. Degree is d at every vertex. For even d the matching ratio is at most 2/3. At d=4,m=6 the bound sought is five but matching size is at most four. The actual construction and proof were read, not only its abstract. This is decisive public resolution and excludes ALL further formalization for campaign credit.
