Fix an integer k>=2 and a finite vector alpha with every alpha_i>0, fixed as n grows. Let theta^1,...,theta^n be independent Dirichlet(alpha) vectors. All coordinates lie strictly between zero and one almost surely. Their pairwise inner products lie in (0,1), so every off-diagonal logit is a finite real; the diagonal is defined to be zero. Work on that probability-one event for each n (or one countable infinite iid sequence).

Set a=2/3, b=9/10, c=log((ab)/(1-ab))=log(3/2)>0. For each j let p_j=P(theta_j>=b)>0 and p=min_j p_j>0. To justify this even for alpha_i<1, choose a small relatively open box in simplex coordinates whose closure is inside the simplex interior and theta_j>b. Its (k-1)-dimensional Lebesgue measure is positive. The Dirichlet density, a positive constant times product x_i^(alpha_i-1), is continuous and strictly positive on that compact closure, so its integral there is positive. Boundary singularities play no role. The standard Dirichlet law gives boundary probability zero.

Write h(z)=log(z/(1-z)); h is increasing on (0,1). For t<1/sqrt(2) set d(t)=-h(t^2)>0. As t increases to 1/sqrt(2), d(t) tends to zero. Choose once and for all t in (a,1/sqrt(2)) with 2d(t)<pc/2. This t depends only on k and alpha and is common to all j, all n and all samples.

For a given sample set H_j={v:theta^v_j>=t}, S_j={s:theta^s_j>=b}. Then S_j is a subset of H_j. For distinct s in S_j and v in H_j, the nonnegative simplex coordinates give z_sv>=theta^s_j theta^v_j>=bt>ba=3/5. Thus W_sv>c. In particular every vertex of S_j has nonnegative edges to every vertex of H_j, including its zero diagonal, so S_j misses U_j, the set of endpoints of negative edges in G[H_j]. Edges inside S_j are positive off the diagonal; edges to H_j minus (S_j union U_j) are positive as well. No condition on edges from S_j to deleted nodes is needed.

For any distinct u,v in H_j, z_uv>=t^2, hence W_uv>=-d(t). For u in U_j, all its negative neighbors lie in U_j by definition and there are at most |H_j|-1<=n-1 of them. S_j is nonempty there because its cardinality is an integer bounded below by a strictly positive real when n>=1. If U_j is empty the inequalities are vacuous and the Laplacian already has nonnegative edge weights; the same cap event still provides a nonempty S_j.

Let N_j=|S_j|. Its indicator summands are independent Bernoulli(p_j), so E N_j=np_j and E[(N_j-np_j)^2]=np_j(1-p_j). Applying Markov to the squared deviation gives P(N_j<np_j/2)<=4(1-p_j)/(np_j). A union bound, requiring no independence between different j, gives simultaneous success probability at least 1-(4/n) sum_j (1-p_j)/p_j, tending to one. This is stronger than the literal per-j nonvanishing probability. Small n where this lower bound is negative cause no issue.
