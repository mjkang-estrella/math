27:                                                                               1. Introduction
28:                                              Gilbreath’s conjecture occupies a distinctive place in the theory of prime gaps
29:                                           because it converts the irregularity of the primes into a rigid recursive pattern of
30:                                           first entries under repeated differencing. Starting from the ordered primes p1 , p2 , . . . ,
31:                                           one forms the first gap sequence d1n = pn+1 − pn and then iterates the unsigned for-
32:                                           ward difference operator by setting dkn = |dk−1        k−1
33:                                                                                          n+1 − dn | for k ≥ 2. The conjecture
34:                                           asserts that the leading entry of every row remains equal to 1. This phenomenon
35:                                           was already observed in the nineteenth-century work of Proth and later reappeared
36:                                           independently in Gilbreath’s note; modern discussion of the conjecture emphasizes
37:                                           both its elementary formulation and its surprisingly deep resistance to proof. The
38:                                           best-known computational verification is due to Odlyzko, who confirmed the con-
39:                                           jecture far beyond the range accessible to hand calculations, while the more recent
40:                                           work of Chase shows that the same differencing paradigm admits a rigorous proba-
41:                                           bilistic analog for sufficiently random sequences with controlled gaps [1, 3, 4, 5].
