11:                                                                                     Abstract
12:                                                         We study the geometry of some proper 4-colorings of the vertices
13:                                                     of sphere triangulations with degree sequence 6, ..., 6, 2, 2, 2. Such tri-
14:                                                     angulations are the simplest examples which have non-negative com-
15:                                                     binatorial curvature. The examples we construct, which are roughly
16:                                                     extremal in some sense, are based on a novel geometric interpreta-
17:                                                     tion of continued fractions. We will also present a conjectural sharp
18:                                                     “isoperimetric inequality” for colorings of this kind of triangulation.

22:                                           1.1        Background
23:                                           The Four Color Theorem, ﬁrst proved (with the assistance of a computer)
24:                                           by Wolfgang Haken and Kenneth Appel in 1976, is one of the most famous
25:                                           results in mathematics. See [W] for a thorough discussion. Here is one
26:                                           formulation. If you have any triangulation of the 2-sphere, it is possible to
27:                                           color the vertices using 4 colors such that no two adjacent vertices have the
28:                                           same coloring. This is called a proper vertex 4-coloring.
29:                                               Certainly one can properly 4-color the vertices of a tetrahedron. A proper
30:                                           vertex 4-coloring of a triangulation Z (with the same colors) canonically
31:                                           deﬁnes a simplicial map f from the sphere to the tetrahedron: Just map
32:                                           each vertex of Z to the like-colored vertex of the tetrahedron and then extend
33:                                           linearly to the faces. The map f in turn deﬁnes a 2-coloring of the faces of
