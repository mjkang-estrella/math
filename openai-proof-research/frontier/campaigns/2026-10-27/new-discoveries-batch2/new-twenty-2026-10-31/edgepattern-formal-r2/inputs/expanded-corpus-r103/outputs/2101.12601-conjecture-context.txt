23:                                            1       Introduction
24:                                            Over the last decade, several works have established a precise picture for the statistical and algorithmic
25:                                            behavior of the stochastic block model (see an account in [Abb18]). In particular, the questions of weak
26:                                            and exact recovery, i.e., whether it is possible (or not) to recover the communities in the extremal cases of
27:                                            weak and exact accuracy, have been fully closed in the two-community symmetric SBM by establishing
28:                                            sharp threshold phenomena in terms of appropriate signal-to-noise (SNR) ratios [Mas14, MNS15, MNS18,
29:                                            ABH16]. Yet, despite significant progress, the more nuanced question of proving how much information or
30:                                            agreement can be recovered about the communities at any given value of the SNR has remained open even
31:                                            in this simplest case.
32:                                                 More specifically, for two symmetric communities and in the sparse regime, the expression of the limit-
33:                                            ing entropy of the SBM is characterized1 at all SNR for the special case of disassortative communities (i.e.,
34:                                            communities that connect more outside than inside) [COKPZ17]. The problem for assortative communi-
35:                                            ties is closed but in the denser regimes, where the vertex degrees diverge while maintaining a finite SNR
36:                                            [DAM16]. However, for the classical case of assortative communities and in the sparse regime, a complete
37:                                            characterization remains open, despite significant progress [KMS16, MNS16, MX15]. The expression of
38:                                            the optimal agreement (rather than the entropy) is known in this case for SNR large enough, and is related
39:                                            to the problem of robust reconstruction on a tree [MNS16]. This result is conjectured to hold all the way
