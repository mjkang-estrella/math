377606 — positive trace route. Source Conjecture1.2 genuinely concerns ordinary positive integral words, though surrounding symmetric word equations allow real exponents. A palindrome evaluates to a positive definite matrix: for even length it is U U^T; for odd length U C U^T with C=A or B positive definite. A product S T of two such matrices has trace tr(S^{1/2} T S^{1/2})>0. This pays the easy direction in every positive dimension. It does not characterize all universally trace-positive words.

Route1: cyclic trace reduction. Cyclic rotation preserves trace. A word is a concatenation of two palindromes precisely when it is a reflection-symmetric necklace: if W=UV with U,V palindromic, reversal is VU, a rotation; conversely if reversal equals a rotation, the associated reflection on cyclic positions cuts at two symmetry axes, with the possible fixed letters forming centers of the two palindromic blocks. This is a combinatorial normalization, not a proof that all other necklaces attain negative trace.

Route2: low-dimensional and rank-one obstructions. For real PD2x2 A,B, orthogonally diagonalize A; then conjugate by diag(1,-1) if necessary so B's off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. All entries of A,B are nonnegative with positive diagonals, so every word has a strictly positive diagonal contribution and nonnegative trace contributions. Therefore NO 2x2 counterexample to universal trace positivity can establish the hard direction for arbitrary non-nearly-symmetric words. For A diagonal positive and B=I+t vv^T with t>=0, expansion by B occurrences reduces each selected term to products of positive moments v^T A^s v (or tr A^s for the empty choice); these are nonnegative and the empty term strictly positive. This entire natural rank-one perturbation mechanism cannot produce negative trace. It does not cover general PD B or negative t. A genuine 3x3 mechanism must control signed off-diagonal cycles against diagonal contributions while preserving every principal minor. No all-word choice of exponents/scalings was established. The source's solved symmetric equations generate certain infinite negative-trace families; those are not the full necessity theorem. Source-only cases/helper/2x2 obstruction earn zero.

382537 — intended strictness, complete algebraic check but correction0. Put n=m-1 and A_ij=[i,j]_n for i,j=0,...,n. With Bernstein polynomials b_i(t)=binom(n,i)t^i(1-t)^{n-i}, direct beta integration gives integral b_i b_j = n!^2/(2n+1)! times A_ij. The b_i are linearly independent: dividing a zero linear combination by (1-t)^n on (0,1) gives a polynomial in t/(1-t) with infinitely many zeros. Thus A is positive definite. Since sum_i b_i=1 and integral b_i=1/(n+1), A has constant row sum c=binom(2n+1,n). Decompose A=(c/m)J+C; C kills the all-ones vector and is positive definite on its orthogonal complement. For any permutation matrix P, tr(A P A P^T)=c^2+tr(C P C P^T). When m>=2, both C and P C P^T are positive definite on the same nonzero complement, so their product has positive trace (conjugate by the positive square root of C on that complement). Hence strictness holds for all m>=2 and every permutation. For m=1, A=(1), c=1 and equality holds.
