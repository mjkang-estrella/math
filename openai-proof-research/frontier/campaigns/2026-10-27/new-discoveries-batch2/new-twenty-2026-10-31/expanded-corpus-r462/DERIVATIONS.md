## 331192: entangled threads and the Motzkin product

Actual source: Egge–Rubin, arXiv1508.05310v3, DMTCS18:2(2016)#5. Definitions2.1–2.4 and3.5–3.6, Theorem3.1, Corollary3.3, Theorem3.8, Propositions3.11–3.13, Lemma3.14, Theorem3.15 and Section5 were read. Conjecture5.8 is exactly the two-sided cardinality assertion. Section5 proves layered cases, not the general assertion. The antipermutation @ has length−1 and is an even thread; the empty permutation is an odd thread. These bases cannot silently be discarded.

Write O(a) for the number of odd partners of an even thread a, and E(b) for the number of even partners of an odd thread b. An entangled pair determines its parity-preserving snow leopard permutation uniquely: at even positions use twice the even-thread values and at odd positions use twice the odd-thread values minus one. Thus counts of snow leopards with a fixed thread equal the partner counts, with no multiplicity from repeated descriptions of a partner.

Route A, exact coupled recursion. For even a of length n, every snow leopard has the UNIQUE Theorem3.1 decomposition (1⊕cπ1⊕1) ⊖1⊖π2. Its even thread is c(b)⊖1⊖a1 where b=π1^o and a1=π2^e. Group by the ACTUAL split, not merely any eligible connector. Conversely every admissible split and every independently chosen partner of b and a1 yields such a snow leopard. Distinct splits have different unique top decompositions. Consequently
O(a)=Σ E(b)O(a1),
where the sum ranges over actual decompositions a=c(b)⊖1⊖a1 with b odd and a1 even; their lengths satisfy |b|+|a1|+1=n. The bases include E(empty)=O(@)=1. This is a symbolic counting argument, not executed enumeration.
For odd b, its decomposition b=(1⊕c(a2)⊕1)⊖b2 is unique (Theorem3.8). The same argument gives E(b)=O(a2)E(b2). Sizes decrease in this second equation. This pays an exact recursion for every input, but a sum of products of Motzkin numbers need not itself be a product of Motzkin numbers.

Route B, forced connectors. Lemma3.14 says a nonleftmost ACTUAL connector requires a to begin with its largest element. Hence when a does not begin with its maximum, Theorem3.15 and Lemma3.14 force a single split. O(a)=E(b)O(a1); the b-factor can then be reduced by the odd recursion to smaller even factors. Repeated application reduces the problem to even threads beginning with their maximum. For those, several connectors can coexist; choosing the canonical leftmost connector loses other snow leopards. In particular Proposition3.13 gives only one inclusion: for a1=empty and a2=1, a1⊖1⊖a2=21 also has partner123, although the products from that prescribed split give312 and321. This is the source's explicit obstruction, not an invented counterexample to the conjecture.

Route C, Motzkin block factorization. Proposition3.11 gives an exact Cartesian factorization for skew sums of odd threads; Theorem5.1 identifies increasing odd and decreasing even fibers with3412-avoiding involutions. Their recursion is the Motzkin recursion. Corollaries5.4 and5.7 already solve the layered cases, so those earn zero. A credible full extension would canonically partition arbitrary fibers into independent involution blocks. The precise unpaid lemma is that the competing-connector sum above, for every maximum-initial even thread, admits such a Cartesian product decomposition (or an exact formula proving the same cardinality). The leftmost-decomposition bijection to restricted Catalan paths counts THREADS, not the sizes of individual entanglement fibers; the Janus bijection counts the intersection of two sets, also not these fibers. Neither supplies this lemma. Fig6 on printedp11 was visually inspected and agrees with the positional argument in Lemma3.14. No finite trials were performed. No full proof/refutation, no novelty/significance clearance.

## 325688: vertex-contraction ideal

Actual final Perrot–Pham EJC23(1)(2016)P1.57, printedpp28–32: Proposition9 and its proof, Conjecture2, Fig12 read; figure visually inspected. Original1306.0294 not acquired and no version-equivalence claim made. The final literally specifies connected undirected G and nonempty W contained in the neighbor set N, contracts s∪W, then removes loops, and asks for membership in Q[x,y]. Fig12 shows that distinct contractions can have repeated polynomials; it is an example, not a universal certificate.

Route A, lift the source recurrence. Proposition9 uses the burning condition to make the empty-neighbor fiber vanish, applies Boolean inclusion-exclusion, then identifies nonempty fibers with contraction recurrent configurations and explicit chip intervals. On undirected graphs this controls T_G(1,y). Even if this is expressed as a polynomial combination of the specialized loopless-contraction polynomials, the strongest conclusion is T_G∈I+(x−1) for I generated by the requested two-variable polynomials. It does NOT imply T_G∈I: the residual (x−1)K needs its own certificate. Vanishing after specialization does not kill a class in Q[x,y]/I. Repeated division has no supplied finite termination rule. The unpaid lemma is a rank/activity-refined fiber bijection yielding polynomial coefficients before setting x=1, including all loop-removal factors. The source itself flags extending internal/external activities as hard; intermediate burning/contraction lemmas were not fully audited here, so even the specialized theorem is source reliance, not a new independently proved result.

Route B, deletion–contraction at the star of s. Branching on incident edges gives minors in which some of those edges are deleted and some contracted. The target generators instead retain every uncontracted incident edge in the whole quotient, except loops. The branch with no retained star edge isolates s and contributes a G−s term. No signed regrouping into the prescribed generators was found. Simply replacing each partially deleted quotient by the full quotient is invalid. Need a universal star-deletion elimination identity with coefficients in Q[x,y], not rational functions. Over Q(x,y), one nonzero generator trivially spans; that says nothing about the polynomial ideal. Likewise common-zero arguments would at best address the radical without a radicality proof.

Boundary self-checks, not new results: if s has a single neighbor and one simple incident bridge, deletion–contraction gives T_G=x T_{G/s}; loops add their usual factors. If s is adjacent to all other vertices, W=N contracts everything and the loopless generator is1, so the ideal is the whole ring. Cycles also illustrate why specialized intuition can succeed: for C_m,m≥4, single-neighbor and double-neighbor contractions give consecutive cycle polynomials f_{m−1} and f_{m−2} (for m=4 the latter is the two-edge parallel cycle). Their difference is x^{m−2}, and f_m=f_{m−1}+x^{m−1} belongs to their ideal. This is a hand algebraic illustration, not computed enumeration and not a full-graph proof. A one-vertex graph has N empty, hence an endpoint/domain issue, not campaign credit. General graphs with nonneighbors remain unpaid. No routine full-target exclusion asserted.

## 326360: specified Cayley minor

Actual author revision On planar right groups dated2015-01-07: definitions in Section2, Lemmas2.1–2.2, Sections4–5, Lemma5.2 proof and Proposition5.3, conjecture on printedpp9,14 read. The paper works with finite right groups and underlying SIMPLE undirected graphs obtained by discarding directions, parallel edges and loops. The literal final sentence about failure is misleading: the source failure concerns replacing the right-zero factor by a DIFFERENT semigroup, even Z2. It does not refute the reserved right-zero conjecture. Repairing this sentence earns zero.

Route A, explicit branch stars. Fix (g,r_j)∈C and use one branch B_x consisting of (x,r_j) and (xg^{-1},r_i) for each i≠j. Every leaf has an edge to its center via (g,r_j); the branches are connected, pairwise disjoint, and cover all vertices. Retain same-layer edges and the anchor edges, deleting other cross-layer edges before contracting. A layer-j generator h stays h. A layer-i generator h, i≠j, joins branches x and xg^{-1}hg. Thus the quotient contains the Cayley graph with generating union C_j ∪ ⋃_{i≠j}g^{-1}C_i g. When conjugation fixes or inverts the appropriate part, the desired underlying graph is obtained, exactly the source Lemma5.2. In particular abelian groups are source-covered, not a new result.

Route B, variable leaf labels. Allow the leaf in layer i of B_x to be (xg_i^{-1},r_i), where every g_i∈C_j, so an anchor edge still joins it to the center. The quotient has generating union C_j ∪ ⋃g_i^{-1}C_i g_i. This is a legitimate enlarged family of explicit branch models. But no choice rule was proved that makes this union contain a graph isomorphic to Cay(G,π_G(C)) for arbitrary C. Changing labels independently by layer does not mean the different conjugations induce ONE common vertex relabeling. Need an all-generator simultaneous compatibility lemma, or a genuinely different branch geometry. If C_j is singleton, there is no freedom in this improvement.

Route C, equivariant spanning-tree contraction. Lemmas2.1–2.2 and5.1 yield a contraction Cayley graph of G for SOME generating set. That is sufficient for the genus comparison in the source. It does not preserve the specified projected generating set. Adding back the missing projected edges requires branch adjacencies not supplied by the lemma. Strong connectivity likewise gives paths between fibers, not disjoint connected branch sets for all x. The precise unpaid endpoint is a branch-set certificate for every finite group, every k and every generating C, with adjacency for EACH projected generator. No group enumeration or experiments were run. Partial abelian/Coxeter constructions are already printed in the source. General significance is not rejected because the construction is short; the full construction is absent.

## 331491: authenticated public exclusion, complete bridge

Beke–Csáji–Csikvári–Pituk, Advances in Mathematics446(2024)109674, final ten-page body acquired/read in full. Theorem1.1 and Section2 prove infinitely many loopless coloopless matroids M with A B<C², where A=T_M(2,0), B=T_M(0,2), C=T_M(1,1). They use the two-thickening of a uniform matroid of rank2n/3, giving both an explicit66-element example and the asymptotic proof. No numeric claims were recomputed in this lane; the actual proof was read. The matroid is loopless because every parallel element belongs to a basis, and coloopless because its parallel mate can replace it.

For completeness, the paper's p3 direct-sum bridge pays ALL THREE target assertions, not just multiplicative. Put Q=M⊕M*. Tutte multiplicativity and duality give T_Q(2,0)=T_Q(0,2)=AB and T_Q(1,1)=C². Therefore max=AB<C², sum=2AB<2C², product=(AB)²<C⁴. Q is still loopless and coloopless; the literal imposes no simplicity or connectedness. Thus it is exactly a counterexample to every displayed universal inequality. The strongest contrary significance case is that a genuine discovery of such a witness would settle the broad matroidal conjecture; however this construction, its proof and its direct-sum bridge were publicly printed in2024, before this investigation. This is a complete applicable public exclusion, not an inference from smallness or standard tools. Zero new credit. The source-neighbor graphic conjecture remains a different question and is not declared refuted.
