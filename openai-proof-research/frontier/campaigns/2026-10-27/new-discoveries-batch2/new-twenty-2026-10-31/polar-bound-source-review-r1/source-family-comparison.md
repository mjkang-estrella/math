## Decisive explicit reduction

Use the example immediately after Conjecture 49 at its source parameter k=3. To avoid confusing this with the candidate's k=4 call this parameter t=3. It has C={c1,c2}, I={i1,i2,i3}, S=2K2, with C-I edges c1i2,c1i3,c2i1,c2i3. The source says it is a minimal (infinity,3)-polar obstruction; that assertion is not needed for the reduction.

1. Delete i3, the I vertex complete to C.
2. Replace c1 by the adjacent true-twin pair {0,1} and c2 by the adjacent true-twin pair {2,3}. Inherited edges between different fibres are all present or all absent according to the seed.
3. Replace i1 by the nonadjacent false-twin pair {8,9}, and i2 by the nonadjacent false-twin pair {10,11}.
4. Keep the four S vertices and name their edges 45,67.

The resulting C is K4, S is 2K2, I is independent, C is complete to S, I anticomplete to S, and the C-I edges are exactly {2,3}×{8,9} and {0,1}×{10,11}. No additional edge toggles or gluing choices remain. check_and_inventory.py independently reconstructs the printed seed and proves equality with all 32 certificate edges by finite set equality. The seed has 9 vertices, deletion leaves 8, and four duplications produce 12. This is a one-vertex deletion and uniform twin expansion of a printed example, not merely a resemblance or degree argument.

This reduction does not assert that minimal polarity obstructions are preserved by arbitrary twin expansion. The new minimality conclusion needs verification; ordinary-proof-notes.md provides it. Nevertheless its proof is very short and follows the source Lemma 39 mechanism. This is a campaign eligibility judgment, not a claim that the source already prints this twelve-vertex counterexample.

Here I′0=I′1={8,9} and I′2=I′3={10,11}; their two missing C pairs partition C. Thus the candidate uses the source proof's paired missing-neighborhood pattern twice with multiplicity two.
