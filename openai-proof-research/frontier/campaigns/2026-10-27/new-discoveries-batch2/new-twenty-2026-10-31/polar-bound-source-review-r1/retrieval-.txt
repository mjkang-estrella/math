L98: A partition (A,B) of the vertex set of a graph G is called a polar partition of G when G[A]
L99: 
L100: and G[B] are complete multipartite graphs. If (A,B) is a polar partition of G in which G[A]
L101: and G[B] have at most s and k parts, respectively, then (A,B) is an (s,k)-polar partition
L102: 
L103: of G, and G is said to be an (s,k)-polar graph. A graph is said to be unipolar (monopolar)
L104: 
L105: if its vertex set admits a polar partition (A,B) such that A is a clique (an independent set,
L106: 
L107: respectively). A graph admitting a (1,1)-polar partition is usually called a split graph.
L108: 
L109: Naturally, most problems related to polar partitions are trivial on split graphs, even
L110: when some of them are very hard in general. In this work, we present some results
L111: 
L112: related to polar partitions on two graph classes generalizing split graphs. Our main
L113: 
L114: results include efficient algorithms to decide whether graphs in these classes admit
L115: 
L116: such partitions. We also establish upper bounds on the order of minimal (s,k)-polar
L117: 
L118: obstructions for these families, for any s and k (possibly s= ∞ or k= ∞).

L123: 1. Introduction
L124: 
L125: All graphs in this work are finite and simple. In general, we follow [1], although some notation may differ slightly.
L126: 
L127: As usual, we use K n to denote the complete graph on n vertices, and H≤G to denote that the graph H is an induced
L128: 
L129: subgraph of the graph G. We denote by G+H and G⊕H the disjoint union and the join of the graphs G and H, respectively.
L130: Congruently, we use nG to denote the disjoint union of n copies of a graph G. Two subsets U and W of the vertex set of
L131: 
L132: a graph G=(V,E) are said to be complete (respectively, anticomplete) if u w∈E (respectively, if u w /∈E) for every pair
L133: 
L134: of distinct vertices u∈U and w∈W. For a family F of graphs, we say that a graph G is F-free if G contains no graph
L135: 
L136: in F as an induced subgraph; if F= {F} we say that G is F-free instead of {F}-free. A property of graphs is said to be
L137: hereditary if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs. A minimal P-obstruction for a hereditary property P of graphs
L138: 
L139: is a graph that does not have the property P, but such that every vertex-deleted subgraph does.
L140: 
L141: Given two nonnegative integers, s and k, a partition (A,B) of the vertex set of a graph G is called an (s,k)-polar partition
L142: 
L143: of G if A induces a complete multipartite graph with at most s parts (i.e. a complete s-partite graph) and B induces the
L144: disjoint union of at most k complete graphs (which is called a k-cluster); an (s,k)-polar graph is a graph admitting an (s,k)-
L145: 
L146: polar partition. If either s or k (or both) are set to ∞, it means that there is no restriction on the number of parts of G[A],
L147: 
L148: G[B], or both, respectively. A polar partition is an (∞,∞)-polar partition. A monopolar partition (respectively, a unipolar
L149: 
L150: partition) is a polar partition (A,B) such that A is an independent set (respectively, a clique); notice that a monopolar
L151: partition is exactly a (1,∞)-polar partition. Naturally, graphs admitting polar, monopolar, or unipolar partitions are called

L325: 2. Polarity on pseudo-split graphs
L326: 
L327: Split graphs are trivially polar, monopolar, unipolar, and (s,k)-polar graphs for any positive integers s and k. Similarly,
L328: 
L329: if G=(C,S,I) is an imperfect pseudo-split graph and S′ is a maximum clique of G[S], then a polar partition of G is
L330: given by (C∪(S\S′),I∪S′); hence, pseudo-split graphs are polar graphs. In this section we study polarity properties on
L331: 
L332: pseudo-split graphs. As our main results, we provide O(|V|)-time algorithms to decide whether a pseudo-split graph is
L333: 
L334: (s,k)-polar from its degree sequence, even when one of s or k is ∞, and we prove tight upper bounds for the order of
L335: 
L336: pseudo-split minimal (s,k)-polar obstructions.
L337: It is worth noticing that C 5 admits only two essentially different polar partitions (A,B). The first is a (2,1)-polar partition
L338: 
L339: with A inducing P 3 and B inducing K 2; the second is a (1,2)-polar partition with A inducing K 2 and B inducing P 3. By
L340: 
L341: using the previous observation, in the next proposition we summarize some facts about the general structure of any
L342: 
L343: polar partition of an arbitrary imperfect pseudo-split graph.
L344: Lemma 3. Let G=(C,S,I) be an imperfect pseudo-split graph, and let (A,B) be a polar partition of G. Let M C be the set of
L345: 
L346: vertices in C that are anticomplete to I, and let M I be the set of vertices in I that are complete to C.
L347: 
L348: 1. If G[S] inherits a (2,1)-polar partition from (A,B), then I⊆B. Otherwise, if G[S] inherits a (1,2)-polar partition from
L349: 
L350: (A,B), then C⊆A.
L351: 
L352: 2. If C∩B= ∅, then G[S] inherits a (2,1)-polar partition from (A,B). Similarly, if I∩A= ∅, then G[S] inherits a (1,2)-polar
L353: partition from (A,B)
L354: 
L355: 3. C\M C⊆A and I\M I⊆B.
L356: 
L357: Proof.
L358: 
L359: 1. If (A∩S,B∩S) is a (2,1)-polar partition of G[S], then, since I is anticomplete to S and A induces a P 3-free graph,
L360: 
L361: we have that I⊆B. The other implication can be proved similarly.
L362: 
L363: 2. Let u∈C∩B. Since G[B] is a cluster, B induces a P 3-free graph, so B∩S is a clique and A∩S induces P 3. Therefore,
L364: 
L365: G[S] inherits a (2,1)-polar partition from (A,B). The other implication admits an analogous proof.
L366: 3. Let u∈C\M C, and let v∈I∩N(u). If u/∈A, then u∈B, so C∩B= ∅ and we have from items 1 and 2 that v∈B.
L367: 
L368: But then, for any w∈B∩S, {u, v, w} induces P 3, which is impossible because B induces a cluster. Therefore, u∈A,
L369: 
L370: so we conclude that C\M C⊆A. That I\M I⊆B can be proved analogously. □

L372: 2.1. Recognition of pseudo-split (s,k)-polar graphs
L373: 
L374: Lemma 3 reveals that the sets M C and M I are the key to determine polar partitions of an imperfect pseudo-split graph.
L375: In this section, we use such a property to completely characterize pseudo-split graphs admitting unipolar, monopolar,
L376: 
L377: and (s,k)-polar partitions. From these characterizations we derive efficient algorithms to recognize pseudo-split graphs
L378: 
L379: having the mentioned partitions.
L380: 
L381: We begin by pointing out a direct consequence of Theorem 2. Notice that, in the notation of Lemma 3, this observation
L382: implies that, for any pseudo-split graph G=(C,S,I), the cardinalities of C, I, M C, and M I, can be computed in O(|V|) time
L383: 
L384: from the degree sequence of G.

L390: Remark 4. Let G=(C,S,I) be an imperfect pseudo-split graph. For each u∈V G, d(u)= |C| + 4 if and only if u is a vertex
L391: 
L392: in C that is anticomplete to I, and d(u)= |C| if and only if u is a vertex in I that is complete to C.
L393: It can be easily verified that C 5 is a minimal unipolar obstruction and, since split graphs are unipolar, it follows that
L394: 
L395: a pseudo-split graph is unipolar if and only if it is a split graph, which can be checked in O(|V|) time from its degree
L396: 
L397: sequence. Note that a perfect pseudo-split graph is a split graph and hence an (s,k)-polar graph for any fixed positive
L398: 
L399: integers s and k. Now, we present a necessary and sufficient condition for a pseudo-split graph to be (s,k)-polar for any
L400: fixed nonnegative integers s and k with s+k≥1.
L401: 
L402: Theorem 5. Let G=(C,S,I) be an imperfect pseudo-split graph, and let s and k be nonnegative integers. Let M C be the set
L403: 
L404: of vertices in C that are anticomplete to I, and M I be the set of vertices in I that are complete to C . Then, G is an (s,k)-polar
L405: 
L406: graph if and only if either
L407: 
L408: 1. k ≥ |I| + 1 and s≥ |C|−|M C| + 2, or
L409: 
L410: 2. s ≥ |C| + 1 and k≥ |I|−|M I| + 2.
L411: Proof. Let c= |C|,i= |I|,m c= |M C|, and m i= |M I|. First, let us suppose that G admits an (s,k)-polar partition (A,B). If
L412: 
L413: G[S] inherits a (2,1)-polar partition from (A,B), we have from Lemma 3 that I⊆B and C∩B⊆M C. Thus,
L414: 
L415: |C∩A| = |C|−|C∩B| ≥ c−m C,
L416: 
L417: where we conclude that s≥c−m C+2. Furthermore, in this case G[B] is a cluster with exactly i+1 components, so
L418: 
L419: k≥i+1. Hence, we have proved that, if G[S] inherits a (2,1)-polar partition from (A,B), then k≥i+1 and s≥c−m C+2.
L420: If G[S] does not inherit a (2,1)-polar partition form (A,B), then it inherits a (1,2)-polar partition, and it can be proved
L421: 
L422: using analogous arguments that in such a case s≥c+1 and k≥i−m I+2.
L423: 
L424: Conversely, let us assume that k≥i+1 and s≥c−m C+2. Let B 1 be a maximum clique of G[S], and let A 1=S\B 1.
L425: 
L426: Then, we have that (A 1∪(C\M C),B 1∪M C∪I) is a (c−m C+2,i+1)-polar partition of G, so G is an (s,k)-polar graph, as
L427: we had to prove. The result follows analogously if we assume that s≥c+1 and k≥i−m I+2, only taking a (1,2)-polar
L428: 
L429: partition (A 1,B 1) of G[S] instead of a (2,1)-polar partition. □
L430: 
L431: Notice that, by Remark 4, the conditions in Theorem 5 can be verified in O(|V|) time from the degree sequence of the
L432: 
L433: input graph, so (s,k)-polarity can be decided in linear time on this family of graphs, and an (s,k)-polar partition can be
L434: 
L435: found with the same time complexity.
L436: Next, we characterize pseudo-split graphs admitting an (s,∞)-polar partition, where s is any fixed nonnegative integer.
L437: 
L438: Once again, it follows from Remark 4 that the conditions in Theorem 6 can be verified in O(|V|) time from the degree
L439: 
L440: sequence of any pseudo-split graph, so (s,∞)-polarity can be decided in linear time on pseudo-split graphs, and an (s,∞)-
L441: 
L442: polar partition can be found with the same complexity. Since monopolar graphs are precisely the (1,∞)-polar graphs,
L443: the same is true for monopolarity on pseudo-split graphs.
L444: 
L445: Theorem 6. Let s be a nonnegative integer. If G=(C,S,I) is an imperfect pseudo-split graph, then G is an (s,∞)-polar graph
L446: 
L447: if and only if either s≥ |C| + 1, or |C| ≥ s≥2 and there are at least |C| − s+2 vertices in C that are anticomplete to I.
L448: 
L449: Proof. Let us denote |C| by c. Suppose that (A,B) is an (s,∞)-polar partition of G such that s≤c. Observe that, by Lemma
L450: 3, if the restriction of (A,B) to S is a (1,2)-polar partition, then C⊆A, but that is impossible since G[A] would have K c+1
L451: 
L452: as an induced subgraph and s≤c. Thus, G[S] is covered by a (2,1)-polar partition, and we have from Lemma 3 that I⊆B.
L453: 
L454: Since A induces a complete s-partite graph and G[A∩S] is a complete bipartite graph, at most s−2 vertices of C belong
L455: 
L456: to A. Then, at least c−s+2 vertices of C belong to B, and we have from Lemma 3 that any such vertex is anticomplete
