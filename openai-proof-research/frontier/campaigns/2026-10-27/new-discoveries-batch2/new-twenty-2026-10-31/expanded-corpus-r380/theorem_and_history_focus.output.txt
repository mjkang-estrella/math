289          Outerplanar graphs and 2-trees. We start by considering undirected graphs. A graph 𝐺 is
290          outerplanar if it admits a plane drawing with all vertices incident to the outer face. Further,
291          𝐺 is maximal outerplanar if no edge 𝑒 can be added to 𝐺 such that 𝐺 + 𝑒 remains outerplanar.

482           3.      Outerplanar DAGs have Constant Stack Number
483 
484           In this section we prove Theorem 1.3, our first main result. One key ingredient is a recent
485           result by Nöllenburg and Pupyrev [51] stating that the stack number of monotone outerplanar
486           graphs is bounded. The idea behind our approach is to partition a given outerplanar DAG 𝐺
487           into “transitive parts”, such that the contraction of each part into a single vertex yields a block-
488           monotone DAG 𝐻 . Then the result from [51] can be applied to the blocks of 𝐻 individually.
489           Two things are left to do: First we show that the many stack layouts for the blocks of 𝐻 can be
490           combined into a single stack layout of 𝐻 without requiring too many additional stacks. Then we
491           show that each transitive part can be “decontracted” to yield a stack layout of 𝐺 , again without
492           requiring too many additional stacks.
493                 We formalize all this by introducing a novel structural tool called directed 𝐻 -partitions.
494

499           DAGs. One of their results is that monotone outerplanar DAGs with at most one vertex stacked
500           on the base edge 7 have bounded twist number (and therefore also bounded stack number).

1445           5.     Conclusion and Open Problems
1446 
1447           We proved that outerplanar DAGs have bounded stack number (Theorem 1.3) and that mono-
1448           tone 2-trees with at most two vertices stacked on every edge have unbounded stack number
1449           (Theorem 1.4). In both cases we solved long-standing open problems or conjectures. In doing
1450           so we got pretty close to pinpointing the boundary between bounded and unbounded stack
1451           number of directed acyclic 2-trees, hence DAGs of treewidth 2. However, several interesting
1452           questions worth to be considered remain open.
