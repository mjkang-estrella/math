71 An independent set I of G is nested if the vertices of I can be linearly ordered so that v ≤ u

77 Definition 2.2. Let G be a finite simple graph, and let C be a proper vertex k-colouring
78 C1 ·∪ · · · ·∪ Ck of G. If every colour class of C is nested, then C is a nested colouring of G.
79 The nested chromatic number χN (G) is the least cardinality of a nested colouring of G.
80 Moreover, the graph G is colour-nested if χN (G) = χ(G).

98 Remark 2.4. Isolated vertices are exactly those vertices that have an empty open neigh-
99 bourhood. Since the empty set is a subset of every set, isolated vertices are weak duplicates
100 of every vertex of a graph. Thus isolated vertices can be put in to any colour class without
101 modifying the nesting of the colour class.
102   We also have a pair of immediate bounds on the nested chromatic number.
103 Remark 2.5. Since every nested colouring of a finite simple graph G is a proper colouring
104 of G, we clearly have χ(G) ≤ χN (G). Moreover, χN (G) ≤ #V (G) as the singleton colouring
105 ·∪ v∈V (G) {v} is nested.
106   Graphs with very small or very large chromatic number are colour-nested.
107 Lemma 2.6. Let G be a finite simple graph on n vertices. If χ(G) ∈ {1, n − 1, n}, where
108 n ≥ 2, then χN (G) = χ(G), i.e., G is colour-nested.
109 Proof. Suppose that χ(G) = 1. Hence E(G) = ∅ and every vertex is a duplicate of every
110 other vertex by Remark 2.4. Thus the set V (G) is a nested colouring of G and χN (G) = χ(G).
111   Suppose that χ(G) = n − 1, where n ≥ 2. Thus G is Kn with a nonempty subset of the
112 edges connected to some vertex, say, v, removed. Let u be a vertex nonadjacent to v. Thus
113 NG (u) = V (G) \ {u, v} contains NG (v), and v is a weak duplicate of u. Hence {u, v} is a
114 nested independent set of G and so χN (G) ≤ n − 1. By the preceding remark we thus have
115 χN (G) = χ(G).

895 5.8. Monotonicity.
896    Recall that a graph property is monotone decreasing (monotone increasing, respectively)
897 if it is preserved under deletion (respectively, addition) of edges. For example, removing an
898 edge can only decrease the chromatic number of a graph, so being k-colourable is a mono-
899 tone decreasing graph property. However, having a nested k-colouring is neither monotone
900 decreasing or increasing. To see this, we use three of the graph products discussed above.
901    Let G and H be finite simple graphs, and suppose χN (H) < #V (H). By construction,
902 we know that G × H is G ⊠ H with edges removed and G ⊠ H is likewise G[H] with edges
903 removed. That is,
904                    E(G × H) ⊂ E(G ⊠ H) ⊂ E(G[H]) ⊂ E(K#V (G)·#V (H) ).
905 However, by Propositions 5.10 and 5.20, both χN (G×H) and χN (G[H]) are at most #V (G)·
906 χN (H) < #V (G) · #V (H). Hence using Proposition 5.17 we have that
907                  χN (G × H) < χN (G ⊠ H) > χN (G[H]) < χN (K#V (G)·#V (H) ).

910    Given integers c and n such that 1 ≤ c ≤ n, it is known that there exists a finite simple
911 graph G on n vertices with χ(G) = c. We show that if we are also given an integer s such
912 that 1 ≤ c ≤ s ≤ n, then G can be chosen so that χN (G) = s for all but a few specific cases.
913    For fixed n ≥ 2, the case when c ∈ {1, n − 1, n} was handled in Lemma 2.6. The one other
914 infinite case is that there does not exist a bipartite graph with nested chromatic number 3.
915 Lemma 6.1. If G is a bipartite graph, then χN (G) 6= 3.
916 Proof. Let G be a bipartite graph, and suppose, without loss of generality (see Remark 2.4),
917 that G has no isolated vertices. Suppose χN (G) ≤ 3. Hence by Corollary 5.5 we may assume
918 G is connected, and so G has a unique proper 2-colouring B ∪· W .

920 w1 , . . . , wt be the elements of W ordered such that NG (wi+1 ) ⊂ NG (wi ). Thus for each b ∈ B
921 there is a k such that b ∈ NG (wi ) if and only if 1 ≤ i ≤ k, i.e., NG (b) = {w1 , . . . , wk }. Hence
922 B is also nested, and χN (G) = 2.                                                                   
