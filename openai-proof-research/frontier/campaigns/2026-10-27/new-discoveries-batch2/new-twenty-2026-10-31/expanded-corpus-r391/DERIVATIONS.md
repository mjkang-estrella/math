## 353626 — semigroup Cayley representations

Literal scope: every generalized Petersen G(n,k), n>=3 and 1<=k<n/2, must be the simple underlying graph on the semigroup elements themselves, not a subgraph or component of a larger Cayley graph. Section3 of García-Marco–Knauer defines arcs s->sc for c in C, with loops suppressed, directions forgotten and parallel edges merged. Thus a witness must give exactly2n elements, associative multiplication, C subset S, and an explicit adjacency bijection onto the outer n-cycle, n spokes and inner k-edges; no extra nonloop edge is allowed. C need not generate S.

First mechanism: use a non-group core without4cycles to force impossibility. Lemma3.2 proves left multiplication is an endomorphism of the directed Cayley graph by associativity. Lemma3.3 obtains a group or a4cycle only when that graph is loopless. Suppressing a loop invalidates the inference that left multiplication acts as an endomorphism of the simple graph: endpoints of a nonloop edge may collapse to a looped vertex. Hence a core cannot exclude the literal target. This is a genuine missing witness condition, not a refutation. Petersen itself has source monoid representations (Prop3.6), despite not being a group graph.

Second mechanism, pursued beyond this failure: extend the explicit orthogroup in Theorem3.11. Put d=gcd(n,k), r=n/d; take outer A=Z_n and inner A'=Z_r times a left-zero band L_d. Source products are x(i,l_j)=(x+i mod r,l_(x+j mod d)) and (i,l_j)x=(i+x mod r,l_j), with their group/left-group internal products. Source Lemma3.10 supplies associativity for these homomorphism actions. C={1,(1,l_0)} gives outer edges x to x+1 and spoke x to (x+1,l_x). On A' both right actions advance the Z_r coordinate by1. Spokes carry x+k to (x+k+1,l_x) since d divides k. To turn inner adjacency into exactly the desired k-step adjacency, this source labeling requires k=+1 or -1 modulo r. This is exactly k²=+k or -k modulo n. Changing the inner coordinate by an invertible scaling c changes both steps by c, so compatibility remains ck=+c or -c mod r, equivalently the same condition. Thus rescaling does not extend the construction.

For the missing G(7,2) case, d=1,r=7 and2 is neither1 nor-1 modulo7; the source construction's vertex corresponding to x+2 is not adjacent by its unit inner step. Theorem3.1's group criterion k²=1 mod n also fails (4 differs from1 mod7). Prop3.20's size2 monoid classification has n/d odd and excludes this pair. This does NOT exclude unrestricted semigroups or larger connection sets. A right-zero attempt s*c=c is associative but makes every c adjacent to every other vertex, incompatible with a cubic graph on14 vertices whenever C is nonempty. A left-zero attempt creates loops only, so has no edges. Neither is a complete new obstruction.

Exact remaining problem: permit loops and more complicated multiplication while preserving exactly2n elements and all3n intended edges, without any extra edges; prove associativity for all triples and exact adjacency in both directions uniformly in n,k. No witness or impossibility theorem with these conditions was obtained. Journal Conj4.2 and section3 definitions were checked directly; the arxiv construction proofs above were read, but illustration-dependent multiplication tables/figures were not independently visually audited. ## 353990 — paired Cayley paths

Scope: finite abelian G, distinct nonzero a,b generating G, arcs (v,v+a),(v,v+b). A witness requires two lists of exactly|G| distinct vertices, exhaustive coverage, permitted consecutive increments and no shared directed arc. Translation can change starts but cannot excuse repeats or extra cycle components.

First mechanism: lift two paths from a Cartesian product of directed cycles via Z_ord(a) times Z_ord(b) -> G, (i,j)->ia+jb. The map is onto; unless the cyclic subgroups are independent it has a nontrivial kernel. Image paths then repeat vertices (domain cardinality exceeds |G|). Deleting repetitions need not preserve permitted increments or arc-disjointness. Thus Theorem4.4 is not a full-scope proof. The special case in which a and b separately generate cyclic G is easy: delete one arc from each constant-generator Hamiltonian cycle; the two arc sets are disjoint since a!=b. This is only a subclass and earns no credit.

Second mechanism, pursued after lifting fails: source arc-forcing subgroup F=<a-b>. A spanning quasi-path has one path component and possibly cycles (Definition3.6). In every nonterminal coset all vertices travel by the same generator (Lemma3.9). In the terminal coset, with terminal tau and h=ord(a-b), the vertices tau+i(a-b),1<=i<=d travel by b and those d<i<h by a, with initial tau+a+d(a-b). The absent terminal outgoing edge and unique missing incoming edge are essential. The rule follows because choosing an a-edge at v precludes a b-edge at v+(a-b), as they would have the same head. These degree conditions alone allow disjoint cycles and do not prove Hamiltonicity.

Source Proposition4.3, proof read, gives a concrete completion target: find Hamiltonian P,P' with b-edge counts summing to |G|-2, |G|-1 or |G|. Lemma4.1 rearranges quasi-paths to be disjoint and Cor3.12 preserves Hamiltonicity at equal b-count, so the proposition pays the disjointness bridge once the two genuine paths are supplied. Complementing all nonterminal coset choices naturally targets complementary counts, but it gives no proof that either resulting quasi-path has no cycle components. A general cyclic-permutation connectivity lemma is still needed.

Proposition6.1 handles even index |G:F| by matched counts t*h+d and (t-1)*h+h-1-d. Their sum is2th-1=|G|-1. Its proof was read. For odd index, Proposition6.2 reduces to Cay(Z_k;a,a+1) with both generators nonzero, and Cay(Z_k;-a,a+1) with (2a+1)|k and both generators nonzero. The reduction proof was read: c=t(a+b), shifted generators c+a,c+b land in F; in the exceptional zero-generator case the presentation identifies G with the second cyclic family. This is a reduction to full missing families, not a bounded list of small group instances. Proposition6.6 covers large generator-subgroup indices via lattice estimates, not every cyclic case. The abstract's >600 and the displayed proposition's >=600 threshold differ; no novelty is sought in that endpoint.

Remaining all-scope lemma: on both cyclic residual families produce Hamiltonian quasi-path parameters with complementary counts within1, or supply a pair-independent obstruction satisfying all finite group assumptions. ## 353401 — stopped by an earlier full proof, with all gcd conditions paid

The game starts at(0,0), leaves it by one of(a,b),(b,a), and stops at a zero coordinate; Alice has priority at(0,0). Winning states in the conjecture mean reachable terminal states, not all formal states on the two axes. The source explicitly formulates Conj1.2 and proves Theorem1.3. Its necessity proof section3 was read: m=a+b is immediately divisible into b²-a²; otherwise consecutive reachable points on a layer cannot both be terminal on the Alice axis, and the width-expansion argument reaches every algebraically representable position after periodic wrapping. In particular i=lcm(m,n)-a and j=b yield (b²-a²,0). This uses stopped reachability, unlike simply solving an unrestricted two-generator congruence.

For arbitrary original parameters put g=gcd(a,b,m,n). The maps Z_(m/g)->Z_m, u->gu and Z_(n/g)->Z_n, v->gv inject into the reachable coordinate subgroups. Dividing a,b,m,n by g bijects move words and their reachable states; gu=0 mod m iff u=0 mod(m/g), and analogously for n. Thus the stopping time and Alice-priority rule are preserved. The divided parameters are positive with0<a/g<b/g<min(m/g,n/g), have gcd1 and retain m/g | n/g. Applying the published necessity theorem gives m/g | (b²-a²)/g². Multiplication implies m*g | b²-a², hence m | b²-a². Every condition needed for the literal arbitrary-gcd conjecture has been paid. This is a full adaptation of the source's already proved result, not a new theorem. The journal's actual Theorem1.3 confirms the same scope. Zero credit.
