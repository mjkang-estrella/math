AMS code: 51E20 Keywords: unital, Buekenhout-Metz unital, O’Nan configuration 1 Introduction A unital of order n n is a 2– ( n 3 + 1 , n + 1 , 1 ) (n^{3}+1,n+1,1) design. Unitals of prime power order q q exist in the Desarguesian projective planes PG ​ ( 2 , q 2 ) {\textup{PG}}(2,q^{2}) , as the absolute points and lines of a hermitian polarity. Such unitals are called classical unitals. Buekenhout [ 4 ] constructed unitals in 2–dimensional translation planes. This was generalised by Metz [ 8 ] to construct non-classical unitals in PG ​ ( 2 , q 2 ) {\textup{PG}}(2,q^{2}) . These unitals are called Buekenhout-Metz (BM) unitals. They have the property that every line of PG ​ ( 2 , q ) {\textup{PG}}(2,q) meets the unital in 1 or q + 1 q+1 points. Further, the unital has exactly one point on the line at infinity, called the special point. For a comprehensive treatise on unitals, see Barwick and Ebert [ 2 ] . An O’Nan configuration in a unital is a set of four distinct lines forming a quadrilateral, whose six points of intersection lie on the unital. In 1972, O’Nan [ 9 ] noted that these configurations do not occur in the classical unital. It was conjectured by Piper [ 10 ] that this property characterizes the classical unital within the class of all unitals.
