The notion of locally irregular graphs is also strongly related with yet another well-known descendant of research on irregular graphs [ 12 ] and so-called irregularity strength of graphs, see e.g. [ 13 ] , which is the 1–2–3 Conjecture . This was posed in 2004 by Karoński, Łuczak and Thomason [ 16 ] and recently confirmed by Keusch [ 17 ] . It may be phrased as follows. Given any connected graph with at least 3 3 vertices, one may multiply every its edge e e to at most 3 3 copies of e e so that the resulting multigraph is locally irregular, i.e. its adjacent vertices have pairwise distinct degrees. See e.g. [ 8 ] for a more comprehensive discussion on relations between these concepts. Let us remark that our approach is significantly different from the one used in [ 8 ] to prove Theorem 2 . It benefits in particular, but not exclusively, from the allowed one more locally irregular subgraph, which admitted an entirely new design of a desired decomposition. Its first phase exploits the probabilistic method in order to initially decompose a given regular graph to 2 2 subgraphs with relatively small chromatic numbers, where we put some effort to optimize our approach with regard to minimizing the lower bound for d d in Theorem 4 .
