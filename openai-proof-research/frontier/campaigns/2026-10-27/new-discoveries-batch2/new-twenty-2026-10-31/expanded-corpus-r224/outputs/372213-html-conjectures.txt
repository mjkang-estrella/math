Introduction A proper q q –coloring of a finite undirected graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) is an assignment φ : V → [ q ] \varphi:V\to[q] such that adjacent vertices receive different colors. When q ∈ ℤ ≥ 1 q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1} , the number of proper q q –colorings is denoted P G ​ ( q ) P_{G}(q) , and it is a classical theorem of Birkhoff and Whitney that there is a unique polynomial P G ​ ( x ) ∈ ℤ ​ [ x ] P_{G}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x] which encodes the number of proper q q -colorings of G G when evaluated at x = q x=q . This is the so-called chromatic polynomial of graph G G , a polynomial of degree | V | |V| and with leading coefficient equal to 1 1 . This object was first introduced by Birkhoff [ 5 ] in 1912 as part of an algebraic approach for the Four Color Problem. Birkhoff’s original motivation was to study colorings of planar maps by encoding them in a polynomial and analyzing its behavior at small integer values, particularly at x = 4 x=4 . Although this approach did not yield a proof of the Four Color Theorem, it initiated a rich line of research and established the chromatic polynomial as a fundamental graph invariant. Whitney [ 15 ] subsequently extended Birkhoff’s ideas to general graphs and introduced many of the structural properties of chromatic polynomials that are now standard.
