Conjecture 2 . The following properties of an amoeba A A are equivalent: • A A is mortal. • A A is not immortal. • A A is mortal w.r.t. any tree. Deciding mortality looks hard (undecidable); perhaps one can model a general Turing machine by amoebas. For this purpose a more general notion of an amoeba colony might be useful. An amoeba colony is a set of amoebas 𝒞 = { A 1 , … , A n } \mathcal{C}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\} . An ℓ \ell -growth G ′ G^{\prime} of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} in G G is an ℓ \ell -growth of some A i ∈ 𝒞 A_{i}\in\mathcal{C} . The remaining definitions are the same as for the case of amoebas. So, in construction of an ℓ \ell -growth sequence for an amoeba colony, one can choose an alive amoeba to be extended. It gives more freedom and we conjecture that such a process can simulate a universal computation. Conjecture 3 . The following algorithmic problem is undecidable. Input: an integer ℓ \ell , a tree T T and an amoeba colony 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . Question: is there an infinite growth sequence for 𝒞 \mathcal{C} starting from T T ? The following generalizations might be of independent interest. A more general type of an extension of an amoeba is to attach to each vertex a specified tree. A general-type amoeba is ( T , π ) (T,\pi) , where π \pi is a root function from V ⁡ ( T ) V(T) to rooted trees (the empty tree is allowed). All other definitions are naturally extended to the case of general-type amoebas. A general-type amoeba is called absolutely mortal if it is mortal for any root function π \pi . Characterization of absolutely mortal amoebas is an interesting open question in this area. Another way of generalization is to substitute trees by arbitrary graphs, possibly disconnected. We know a little about this general problem. But it also seems to be interesting. As already mentioned in Remark 1 , we can extend the definition of confining trees to general graphs.
