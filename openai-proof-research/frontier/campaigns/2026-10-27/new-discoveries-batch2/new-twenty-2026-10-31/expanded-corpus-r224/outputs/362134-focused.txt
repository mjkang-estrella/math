Introduction In 1974 in the seminal paper [ 7 ] George Bergman introduced machinery whereby by adding sufficient generators and relations to an algebra, one can lift any pair of non-zero finitely generated projective modules to become isomorphic. To be precise, let R R be an algebra over a field 𝗄 \mathsf{k} , and let ( P i , Q i ) i ∈ I (P_{i},Q_{i})_{i\in I} be pairs of non-zero finitely generated projective left R R -modules. Bergman’s machinery adjoins certain generators and relations to R R , so that the resulted extension algebra S S obtained provides universal isomorphisms of modules, S ⊗ R P i ≅ S ⊗ R Q i S\otimes_{R}P_{i}\cong S\otimes_{R}Q_{i} , where i ∈ I i\in I . Furthermore, the non-stable K K -theory of the algebra S S can be obtained from that of R R by identifying the pairs ( P i , Q i ) i ∈ I (P_{i},Q_{i})_{i\in I} in the structure. Starting from a field 𝗄 \mathsf{k} and choosing the pair of 𝗄 \mathsf{k} -modules ( 𝗄 n , 𝗄 m ) (\mathsf{k}^{n},\mathsf{k}^{m}) , n , m ∈ ℕ + n,m\in\mathbb{N}^{+} , Bergman observed that his machinery retrieves the celebrated Leavitt algebra R = L 𝗄 ​ ( n , m ) R=L_{\mathsf{k}}(n,m) , where R n ≅ 𝗄 n ⊗ 𝗄 R ≅ 𝗄 m ⊗ 𝗄 R ≅ R m R^{n}\cong\mathsf{k}^{n}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}R\cong\mathsf{k}^{m}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}R\cong R^{m} as R R -modules.
