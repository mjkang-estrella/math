Circular-arc graphs are graphs that can be represented as intersection graphs of subpaths of a cycle. Interval graphs are graphs that can be represented as intersection graphs of subpaths of a path. Since cycles are locally paths, every circular-arc graph is locally interval. In this paper, we prove that the converse holds as well: every locally interval graph is a circular-arc graph. This result and its proofs are connected to a recent broader study of structural local-global theory and build on previous work on locally chordal graphs. Connections to local-global theory In addition to addressing an intuitive and visually appealing idea, our study of locally interval graphs connects to a broader study of local-global graph theory. This line of study began with the work of Diestel, Jacobs, Knappe, and Kurkofka [ 4 ] , which introduced an idea called the r r -local covering to study the “ r r -local” structure of a graph. Given a graph G G and an integer r ⩾ 1 r\geqslant 1 , the r r -local covering of G G , denoted p r : G r → G p_{r}:G_{r}\to G , is the universal covering of G G that preserves its r / 2 r/2 -balls. (See Section 2.3 for the formal definition.) The graph G r G_{r} is called the r r -local cover of G G . In [ 1 , 2 ] , the first and fourth authors and Kobler studied locally chordal graphs . A graph is chordal if each of its cycles of length at least four has a chord. Equivalently, a graph G G is chordal if and only if G G admits a region representation over a tree. Since interval graphs are the graphs that admit region representations over paths, interval graphs are a subclass of chordal graphs. A graph is r r -locally chordal if each of its r / 2 r/2 -balls is chordal. A graph is r r -chordal if each of its cycles of length at least four and at most r r has a chord.
