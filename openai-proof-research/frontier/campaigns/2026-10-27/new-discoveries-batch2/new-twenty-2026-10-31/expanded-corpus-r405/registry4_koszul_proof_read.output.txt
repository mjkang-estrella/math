Proof. Let M be a matroid with lattice of flats L. As previously noted at the beginning of this
subsection, we may assume without loss of generality that M is simple and rk M ≥ 3. By Theorem
3.3, we also know that the lattice L admits a total coatom order ≺. In what follows, all ideals are
considered with respect to the atom-free presentation of A = CH(M ), and all initial segments of
flats are with respect to the chosen total coatom ordering. However, all up-sets refer to the natural
partial order of L. Consider the collections of ideals:
        F0 = {(xG | G ∈ G) | G ⊆ L≥2 an up-set}
        F1 = {(xG | G * F ) + (xG | G ∈ G) | F ∈ L≥2 , G an initial segment covered by F },
and set F = F0 ∪ F1 . Note that (0) ∈ F0 when G = ∅ and that A+ ∈ F0 when G = L≥2 . We claim
that F is the desired Koszul filtration.
   Let 0 6= I ∈ F0 so that I = (xG | G ∈ G) for some nonempty up-set G. We then choose any
flat G′ ∈ G minimal in G with respect to the partial order of L and set G ′ = G r {G′ }. It is easy
to see that G ′ is also an up-set since we are removing a minimal element of G. Consider the ideal
J = (xG | G ∈ G ′ ) ∈ F0 . Clearly J ⊆ I and I/J is cyclic. Since G ′ contains all flats properly
containing G′ and none of the flats contained in G′ , it follows from Corollary 4.9 that
                     (J : I) = (J : xG′ ) = (xG | G * G′ ) + (xG | G ∈ coat(G′ ))
if rk G′ ≥ 3 and (J : I) = A+ if rk G′ = 2. In the former case, it follows that (J : I) ∈ F1 , and we
have (J : I) ∈ F0 in the latter case.
   Let 0 6= I ∈ F1 so that I = (xG | G * F ) + (xG | G ∈ G) for some flat F and G an initial segment
of flats covered by F . If G 6= ∅, then rk G ≥ 3, and we can take G′ to be the largest element of G with
respect to the total coatom order and G ′ = G r {G′ }. Then J = (xG | G * F ) + (xG | G ∈ G ′ ) ∈ F1 .
Again, J ⊆ I with I/J cyclic. Moreover

other hand, if G = ∅, then I = (xG | G * F ) ∈ F0 , and so, we have already seen in the preceding
paragraph how to choose an ideal contained in I with the desired properties. Thus, F is a Koszul

Augmented Chow rings of matroids were introduced in [BHM+ 22] and employed in [BHM+ 20]
as a key ingredient in the resolution of the Top-Heavy Conjecture that interpolates between the
combinatorics of the lattice of flats of a matroid (as encoded by its graded Möbius algebra) and the
good algebraic properties of the Chow ring of the matroid. In particular, it is shown [BHM+ 20,
Theorem 2.19] that the augmented Chow ring of a matroid is also a quadratic Artinian Gorenstein
graded algebra. It was pointed out to us by Chris Eur that the augmented Chow ring of M could
be viewed as the Feichtner-Yuzvinsky Chow ring D(L, G) of the lattice of flats of a related matroid,
the free coextension of M , with respect to a certain building set. Using this observation, we show
how to obtain an atom-free presentation for the augmented Chow ring of a matroid and that all
of the results of the preceding section carry over with only minor modifications to show that the
augmented Chow ring of a matroid is also Koszul.
   We begin by sketching the details of Eur’s observation about the augmented Chow ring. Let M
be a simple matroid on the ground set E. The free coextension of M is the matroid (M ∗ + e)∗ ; it
is the dual of the free single element extension of the dual matroid of M . We refer the interested
reader to [Oxl11, Sections 2.1, 7.2] for further details on duality and free extensions of matroids.
For our purposes, it suffices to note that the free coextension (M ∗ + e)∗ is a matroid on the ground
set E ∪ {e}, where e is any element not in E, with rank function defined as follows for any set
F ⊆ E:

Using the description of the rank function of the dual of M , it is easily checked that the condition
clM ∗ (E r F ) = E is satisfied if and only if rkM F = |F |, which is equivalent to F being an
independent set of M . From this, it can be seen that a set of the form F ∪ e is a flat of the free
coextension of M if and only if F is a flat of M , and a set F is a flat of the free coextension if
and only if F is an independent set of M . Thus, in a way, the free coextension treats independent
sets and flats of M on equal footing. By identifying the flat F of M with the flat F ∪ e of the free
coextension, we see that the lattice of flats of the free coextension contains an isomorphic copy of
the lattice of flats of M , just shifted upward in rank by one, in addition to all independent sets of
M with their usual rank.
