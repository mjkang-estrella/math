## 359473: full ordinary equivalence, with a complete hierarchy bridge

Scope: every nonempty totally bounded ultrametric space X, all of whose points are isolated. UGVL means there is a graph-theoretic tree on exactly X and a labeling l:X→[0,∞), with d(x,y) equal to the largest vertex label on the unique finite tree path from x to y for x≠y, and d(x,x)=0. A centered sphere is {c}∪{z:d(c,z)=r} for some c and r>0. Open balls have positive radius.

The local-finiteness shortcut fails: a discrete totally bounded metric space need not be finite or locally finite. For example, positive integers with d(i,j)=max(1/i,1/j) for i≠j are totally bounded and isolated, but an appropriate bounded ball is infinite. Thus Theorem 4.6 of 2308.06626 cannot be applied just by substituting the corpus hypotheses.

Here is a full construction instead. We spell out its supporting elementary facts rather than leaving an infinite-limit bridge unpaid.

A non-singleton totally bounded ultrametric space B has a positive attained diameter R. To see this, choose distinct u,v with d(u,v)=a>0 and cover B by finitely many balls of radius a/2, choosing representatives in B. Distances between different covering classes equal the distances between representatives, by the ultrametric inequality; distances within a class are <a/2. Consequently the supremum of all distances in B is the maximum of finitely many interclass distances, and is attained. Partition B by x∼y iff d(x,y)<R. This is a partition into at least two and finitely many classes: distinct classes are R-separated, contradicting total boundedness if there were infinitely many. The classes are balls B_R(x). If B was an open ball in X, these are also open balls of X contained in B. Each class has diameter strictly less than R: apply the attained-diameter fact to the class if it is non-singleton. All its pair distances are <R, so its attained maximum is <R.

Starting at X (which is itself an open ball since X is bounded), make these classes the children of every non-singleton node. The resulting inclusion hierarchy is a rooted tree H. Each point x reaches the singleton {x} at finite depth. Indeed, choose ε>0 with B_ε(x)={x}, and choose a finite cover of X by balls of radius ε/2. Along the chain containing x, every split at diameter ≥ε separates off a child not containing x. Different such discarded children contain points pairwise ≥ε apart: at their first separating node their mutual distance is that node's diameter. There can be only finitely many such discarded children under the fixed finite ε/2 cover. An infinite chain containing x is therefore impossible. Every open ball occurs in H: if a proper open ball A meets two children of a node B, the radius defining A exceeds diam B and hence contains all of B; thus any proper A is contained in one child. Follow that child until A is reached; an infinite descent would contain an isolated point of A and contradict its finite depth. Children have positive separation from each other, so the same argument identifies A exactly at some node. Singleton balls are precisely the leaves, and every node connects to the root by a finite path.

Assume every open ball is a centered sphere. At a non-singleton node B with diameter R, write B={c}∪{z∈X:d(c,z)=r}. Since B has another point, r≤diam B; the ultrametric inequality gives diam B≤r, so r=R. The class of c for d<R is therefore the singleton {c}. Choose one such singleton child for each internal node B, writing its point as c(B). Distinct nodes choose distinct leaves, since each leaf has a unique parent. Contract in H all edges B—{c(B)}. These contraction edges are pairwise vertex-disjoint. Every remaining vertex is naturally a singleton leaf {x}, either uncontracted or merged with its parent, so the quotient graph has vertex set exactly X. It is connected because paths in H are finite, and acyclic because any finite quotient cycle would lift to a cycle in the tree H using the finitely many disjoint contracted edges. Thus the quotient is an ordinary tree T, not an end-space or completion with extra points.

Set l(c(B))=diam B; give the unselected leaves label zero. For distinct x,y, let B be the least common ancestor of {x},{y} in H. They are in different children, so d(x,y)=diam B. The quotient path passes through the vertex c(B), possibly as an endpoint; its other labels are diameters of descendant nodes or zero, all ≤diam B. Contracting a singleton-child edge never erases this maximum, because its label is transferred to that singleton. Hence max(path labels)=diam B=d(x,y). This proves UGVL with every semantic bridge specified, including infinite graph connectedness and the original underlying set.

Conversely assume d=d_l for a tree T on X. Every open ball B is convex in the graph-theoretic sense: if endpoints are in B_r(a), every vertex on their T-path is also in B_r(a), since paths to the endpoints and their label maxima bound its distance to an endpoint by the endpoint distance, which is <r. If B is non-singleton, its attained diameter R is realized by u,v. The finite path u—v contains a vertex c with l(c)=R, and c∈B. For each z∈B\{c}, the path c—z contains c, so d(c,z)≥l(c)=R, while diameter gives d(c,z)≤R. Thus all these distances equal R. No z outside B can have d(c,z)=R: choose an original ball radius r for B; attainment gives R<r, and d(c,z)=R would place z in B. Therefore B is the centered sphere at c of radius R. A singleton {c} is a centered sphere using any positive radius smaller than its isolation radius. This proves the other implication.

## 358888: two sustained routes; neither closes the exact curvature conjecture

Use the source's degree-weighted inner product, not the unannotated corpus brackets. Put a=δ_x−δ_y and d=d(x,y)>0. Normalize the weak 1-Lipschitz polytope K by one additive constant; let F={f∈K:⟨f,a⟩=d}. Let A=the multivalued hypergraph Laplacian, A^0 its minimum norm selection, J_λ=(I+λA)^{-1}, A_λ=(I−J_λ)/λ. The source's canonical restriction is precisely the minimum weighted norm element of Af, not an arbitrary endpoint of that set. The diagonal x=y wording is undefined and earns no correction credit.

For each fixed f∈F, A_λ f→A^0 f. Since KD_λ=sup_{g∈K}⟨J_λg,a⟩, we have (d−KD_λ)/λ≤(d−⟨J_λf,a⟩)/λ=⟨A_λf,a⟩. Taking the curvature limit and then the infimum over f∈F proves κ≤C exactly as Proposition 3.6. It does not prove the reverse inequality.

Route 1: compactness and maximal monotonicity. For maximizers f_λ of KD_λ, write the exact loss as (d−⟨f_λ,a⟩)/λ+⟨A_λf_λ,a⟩. Compactness gives subsequential limits in F if the normalized losses stay bounded, but pointwise convergence of A_λ does not justify replacing its value at moving f_λ by A^0 at a limit. A generic monotone-operator obstruction is A=∂|t|: J_λ(t)=sgn(t)max(|t|−λ,0), A^0(t)=sgn(t) for t≠0 and 0 at zero. At t=λ/2, A_λ(t)=1/2 while A^0(t)=1, so uniform convergence fails on a compact interval. This is an exact symbolic example, not a hypergraph counterexample or executed search. Missing lemma: for this particular hypergraph operator and every maximizing sequence, liminf of the displayed total loss is at least inf_{f∈F}⟨A^0f,a⟩. Generic resolvent convergence does not supply it.

Route 2: polyhedral faces. The finite hypergraph energy has finitely many extremizer patterns, so one can analyze J_λ separately on chambers. This still does not permit freezing a chamber: maximizing f_λ can lie at distance O(λ) from a tie face. One needs a boundary compatibility inequality for every adjacent active pattern, including the nonnegative tightness-defect term (d−⟨f_λ,a⟩)/λ. Alternatively one must prove KD_λ equals its supremum over F for all sufficiently small λ, together with the source's Conjecture 3.11 error control. Neither has been proved here. Source Theorem 3.14 expressly assumes both ingredients. Replacing KD by weak KD imposes F by definition and proves a different endpoint; source Theorem 3.21 remains conditional on Conjecture 3.11. This is not the original conjecture.

## 362008: failed local route followed through to exact public subsumption

The tempting local assertion that an arbitrary bag with large independence number directly yields a large induced matching is false: an edgeless graph has an arbitrarily large bag but has induced matching treewidth zero, and another decomposition has singleton bags. Thus any proof needs a bag transformation.

Actual public proof: Abrishami et al., arXiv:2405.04617v2, Theorem 1.1 and Section 3 (Lemmas 3.1–3.2 and Claims 3.3–3.7 read) prove exactly the required bound. Their K_{t,t}-free means induced-subgraph-free, explicitly in Section 2. Their induced matching touches a bag when every matching edge has at least one endpoint in it, exactly the corpus convention. Both minima range over tree decompositions.

For clarity, the complete reduction is simply μ=k and r=K(k,t), since their conclusion tree-α(G)<K(k,t) implies ≤r. If a convention includes k=0, such graphs are edgeless (an edge forces a one-edge matching touching a bag), hence have tree-α≤1. For t=0 with empty K_{0,0} as an induced subgraph, the class is empty; under the source's positive-integer convention no extension is needed. No hereditary-class hypothesis is inserted on the individual graph theorem.

The source mechanism was checked rather than inferred from its abstract. It picks a maximum independent S, bounds independent subsets of β(x)\S using a bipartite matching and Ramsey extraction, calls s∈S light when α(N(s))<C and heavy otherwise, and bounds heavy vertices per bag. It replaces the light vertices of each old bag by their neighborhoods, adding a leaf bag N[s] for each light s. Edge coverage and connected occurrence subtrees survive: each added T_s meets T_v for a neighbor v, and its new leaf is attached on T_s. The new bag bound is 2M+μC. The matched Ramsey lemmas ensure finite M and C. These are the nontrivial ingredients missing from the local shortcut, already present in the public proof.

The 2025 quantitative companion 2511.03864 Theorem 1.2 and Section 4 were also read; its proof reuses the original structural claims and replaces Ramsey bounds by polynomial estimates. The 2026 citing paper 2609.18648 Theorem 1.2 strengthens this further; its full proof was not reviewed. This is known/public, zero credit, without needing a global literature-clearance claim.
