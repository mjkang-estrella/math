Bartosz Walczak 2 2 footnotemark: 2 Abstract For a tree decomposition 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of a graph G G , let μ ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \mu(\mathcal{T}) denote the maximum size of an induced matching in G G with the property that some bag of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} contains at least one endpoint of every edge of the matching. The induced matching treewidth of a graph G G is the minimum value of μ ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \mu(\mathcal{T}) over all tree decompositions 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of G G . Classes of graphs with bounded induced matching treewidth admit polynomial-time algorithms for a number of problems, including Independent Set , k k -Coloring , Odd Cycle Transversal , and Feedback Vertex Set . In this paper, we focus on combinatorial properties of such classes. First, we show that graphs with bounded induced matching treewidth that exclude a fixed biclique as an induced subgraph have bounded tree-independence number , which is another well-studied parameter defined in terms of tree decompositions. This sufficient condition about excluding a biclique is also necessary, as bicliques have unbounded tree-independence number. Second, we show that graphs with bounded induced matching treewidth that exclude a fixed clique have bounded chromatic number, that is, classes of graphs with bounded induced matching treewidth are χ \chi -bounded. The two results confirm two conjectures due to Lima et al. [ESA 2024]. 1 Introduction The notions of tree decompositions and treewidth are among the most successful concepts in graph theory. They were discovered by several groups of authors with various motivations [ 2 , 4 , 14 , 19 ] . They proved to be essential in the study of graph minors [ 19 , 20 , 21 ] and in the design of efficient algorithms on graph classes [ 8 , Chapters 7 and 11] . A tree decomposition describes the structure of a graph G G in terms of a tree whose nodes correspond to subsets of the vertices of G G called bags (see Section 2 for a precise definition) in such a way that imposing local conditions on the bags leads to strong and interesting properties of the entire graph G G . For instance, bounding the size of every bag leads to the definition of treewidth. As another example, chordal graphs are the graphs that admit a tree decomposition where all bags are cliques. Such bags may be of arbitrary size, but their structure is heavily restricted. A common generalization of both the above-mentioned restrictions is the concept of tree-independence number , introduced independently by Yolov [ 23 ] and by Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel [ 11 ] ; we follow the notation of the latter paper. The independence number of a tree decomposition 𝒯 \mathcal{T} , denoted by α ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \alpha(\mathcal{T}) , is the maximum size of an independent set contained in a single bag of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . The tree-independence number of a graph G G , denoted by tree ​ - ​ α ⁡ ( G ) \treealpha(G) , is the minimum independence number of a tree decomposition of G G . Using this terminology, chordal graphs can be described as the graphs with tree-independence number equal to 1 1 . Classes of graphs with bounded tree-independence number have received some attention, both from the structural [ 1 , 7 , 12 ] and from the algorithmic [ 10 , 11 , 17 , 18 , 23 ] points of view. However, as pointed out already by Yolov [ 23 ] , tree-independence number is in some ways unsatisfactory. For example, such a simple graph as K t , t K_{t,t} (the complete bipartite graph with t t vertices on each side) has large tree-independence number. Specifically, tree ​ - ​ α ⁡ ( K t , t ) = t \treealpha(K_{t,t})=t , because every tree decomposition of K t , t K_{t,t} has a bag that contains one of the two parts of the bipartition. As a remedy, Yolov [ 23 ] defined a relaxed variant of tree ​ - ​ α \treealpha ; we use the notation introduced later in [ 17 , 18 ] . For a tree decomposition 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of a graph G G , we say that an induced matching (i.e., a set of edges that are pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent in G G ) M M in G G touches a bag β \beta of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} if every edge in M M has at least one end in β \beta , and we let μ ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \mu(\mathcal{T}) denote the maximum size of an induced matching that touches some bag of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . The induced matching treewidth of G G , denoted by tree ​ - ​ μ ⁡ ( G ) \yolov(G) , is the minimum value of μ ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \mu(\mathcal{T}) over all tree decompositions 𝒯 \mathcal{T} of G G . While this definition may seem somewhat arbitrary, Yolov [ 23 ] showed that some classical NP -hard problems, including Independent Set and k k - Coloring , can be solved in polynomial time in classes of graphs with bounded tree ​ - ​ μ \yolov . Further algorithmic applications and structural results were later provided by Lima, Milanič, Muršič, Okrasa, Rzążewski, and Štorgel [ 17 , 18 ] . There is a straightforward relationship between tree-independence number and induced matching treewidth: tree ​ - ​ μ ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ tree ​ - ​ α ⁡ ( G ) \yolov(G)\leqslant\treealpha(G) for every graph G G . Indeed, if 𝒯 \mathcal{T} is a tree decomposition of G G and M M is an induced matching that touches a bag β \beta of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} , then β \beta includes an independent set of size | M | \lvert M\rvert containing one end of every edge from M M , witnessing that μ ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) ⩽ α ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \mu(\mathcal{T})\leqslant\alpha(\mathcal{T}) . On the other hand, tree-independence number cannot be bounded from above by any function of induced matching treewidth. For example, tree ​ - ​ μ ⁡ ( K t , t ) = 1 \yolov(K_{t,t})=1 (as K t , t K_{t,t} contains no two-edge induced matching) while tree ​ - ​ α ⁡ ( K t , t ) = t \treealpha(K_{t,t})=t , as we have observed above. Lima et al. [ 17 , Conjecture 8.2] (see also [ 18 , Conjecture 17] ) conjectured that the presence of large complete bipartite graphs (also called bicliques ) is the only reason why graphs of small tree ​ - ​ μ \yolov might have large tree ​ - ​ α \treealpha . More formally, they suggested that graphs of bounded induced matching treewidth that exclude some fixed biclique as an induced subgraph actually have bounded tree-independence number. As our first result, we confirm this conjecture. Theorem 1.1 . For any positive integers μ \mu and t t , there is an integer 𝖪 ⁡ ( μ , t ) \mathsf{K}(\mu,t) such that the following holds. This result, combined with the discussion above, immediately yields the following classification of tree-independence number and induced matching treewidth in hereditary classes of graphs, i.e., classes that are closed under vertex deletion. (Here, by functionally equivalent, we mean that one parameter can be bounded by a function of the other, and vice versa.) Corollary 1.2 . The parameters tree ​ - ​ α \treealpha and tree ​ - ​ μ \yolov are functionally equivalent in a hereditary graph class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} if and only if 𝒢 \mathcal{G} does not contain all complete bipartite graphs. Theorem 1.1 strengthens the following known result on graphs with K t , t K_{t,t} excluded as a subgraph rather than an induced subgraph: for any μ \mu and t t , there exists 𝖪 ′ ​ ( μ , t ) \mathsf{K}^{\prime}(\mu,t) such that every graph G G with tree ​ - ​ μ ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ μ \yolov(G)\leqslant\mu and with no K t , t K_{t,t} subgraph has treewidth less than 𝖪 ′ ​ ( μ , t ) \mathsf{K}^{\prime}(\mu,t) [ 17 , Theorem 7.5] . This follows from Theorem 1.1 , because, by Ramsey’s theorem, treewidth is bounded by a function of tree ​ - ​ α \treealpha and the maximum size of a clique, and the latter is less than 2 ​ t 2t in considered graphs. Graphs with bounded tree ​ - ​ μ \yolov and with no K t , t K_{t,t} subgraph are known to have bounded minimum degree. This can be seen as a corollary to the above-mentioned result from [ 17 ] (and the fact that minimum degree is bounded by treewidth), but it also follows from a well-known theorem of Kühn and Osthus [ 16 ] that graphs with no induced subdivisions of a fixed graph and with no K t , t K_{t,t} subgraph have bounded minimum degree; this is because every proper subdivision of K s , s K_{s,s} (with all edges subdivided at least once) has induced matching treewidth at least s s . In hereditary graph classes, bounded minimum degree implies bounded chromatic number. If we exclude a large clique rather than a large K t , t K_{t,t} , we cannot hope for a property as strong as bounded minimum degree (as it does not hold for bicliques), but it turns out that we can still bound the chromatic number. A graph class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is χ \chi -bounded if there is a function f : ℕ → ℕ f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N} such that every graph G ∈ 𝒢 G\in\mathcal{G} satisfies χ ⁡ ( G ) ⩽ f ⁡ ( ω ⁡ ( G ) ) \chi(G)\leqslant f(\omega(G)) . Here, χ ⁡ ( G ) \chi(G) is the chromatic number of G G , and ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) is the maximum size of a clique in G G . The study of χ \chi -boundedness is a very fruitful topic in graph theory; see [ 22 ] for a survey. Since chromatic number is bounded by treewidth (plus one), which is in turn bounded by a function of tree ​ - ​ α \treealpha and ω \omega , classes of graphs with bounded tree-independence number are χ \chi -bounded. Another conjecture of Lima et al. [ 17 , Conjecture 8.3] (see also [ 18 , Conjecture 18] ) asserts that classes of graphs with bounded tree ​ - ​ μ \yolov are also χ \chi -bounded. Our second result confirms this conjecture. Theorem 1.3 . Every class of graphs with bounded tree ​ - ​ μ \yolov is χ \chi -bounded. The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
