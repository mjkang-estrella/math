## Author theorem
For every positive integer g there is a finite simple bipartite graph G of maximum degree at most100, girth at least g, and nu_ur(G)<(3/4)nu(G). Consequently epsilon=1/4, Delta=100 negates the full target.

## Lemma1: a uniquely restricted bipartite matching forces an empty rectangle
Let M={x_i y_i:1<=i<=m} in a bipartite graph with fixed sides X,Y. Form a directed graph on its matched edges, putting i->j precisely when i!=j and x_i y_j is an edge. A directed simple cycle i1->i2->...->ir->i1 would replace the r matching edges x_it y_it by x_it y_i(t+1), producing a distinct matching covering exactly the same vertices. Thus, if M is uniquely restricted, this directed graph is acyclic. Every finite acyclic directed graph has a topological order: repeatedly take a vertex of indegree zero; such a vertex exists since otherwise repeatedly following a predecessor in a finite set yields a directed cycle. Relabel so every directed edge has i<j. For s=floor(m/2), take the last s matched X vertices and the first s matched Y vertices. These sets have no edge between them: every such edge would run from a strictly later to an earlier index; no diagonal matching edge is between these disjoint index ranges. Thus an empty s-by-s rectangle exists. This proves the needed implication directly, without importing the source's alternating-cycle characterization.

## Lemma2: bounded-degree high-girth graphs with no large bipartite holes
Fix g, put h=max(1,ceil(g/2)), and set d=100. Choose an integer N divisible by4, large enough for the inequalities below, and take d independent uniformly random bijections pi_j:X->Y, where |X|=|Y|=N. Retain each edge x pi_j(x) with its colour j, initially as a bipartite multigraph. No loops are possible. Every vertex has multidegree d. The first colour is a specified perfect matching.

Let a=N/4. For fixed A subset X, B subset Y with |A|=|B|=a, the probability that a single random bijection avoids all A-B edges is
 (N-a)_a/(N)_a = product_{i=0}^{a-1}(N-a-i)/(N-i)
 <=(1-a/N)^a <=exp(-a*a/N)=exp(-N/16).
The first equality counts injective images of A in Y\B; remaining bijections cancel. If a>N-a the probability is zero, so the inequality still holds (here a=N/4). Independence of the colours gives exp(-100N/16) for the union graph. There are at most 2^N*2^N=4^N choices of A,B. Hence the probability of any empty a-by-a rectangle is at most exp((log4-100/16)N)<1/4 for all sufficiently large N. No numerical test is used: log4<2 and 100/16>6 imply an exponent less than -4N.

It remains to pay simplicity and girth without damaging this rectangle condition or the matching. For 1<=l<=h, count coloured closed cycle witnesses of length2l. For l>=2 require distinct X vertices x1,...,xl and distinct Y vertices y1,...,yl, and the 2l distinct coloured edges x_i y_i, x_(i+1) y_i (cyclic indices). For l=1 require two DISTINCT colours on the same X-Y pair, witnessing a parallel pair. Overcounting by orders and colours is harmless: there are at most N^(2l)d^(2l) witnesses. In one colour, if its specified pairs conflict, their probability is zero. Otherwise r specified distinct input-output pairs have probability 1/(N)_r. Independence of colours, total r=2l, and N>=4h imply that any witness has probability at most (N-2l)^(-2l). Therefore expected witness count W_l is at most (2d)^(2l). The l=1 convention also satisfies this bound: its two distinct colours have probability N^(-2). Ordinary simple cycles of every length at most2h and every parallel pair are included in these witness lists.

Let S=sum_{l=1}^h 2l W_l and B_h=sum_{l=1}^h 2l(200)^(2l). These are finite expressions, not computed enumerations. E S<=B_h. Choose N>400 B_h, also large enough for the preceding rectangle bound and N>=4h. Markov's inequality gives Pr(S>=N/100)<=100 B_h/N<1/4. The union of the two bad events has probability less than1/2. Thus at least one tuple of permutations has no empty a-by-a rectangle and S<N/100.

Delete every vertex on any of these cycle witnesses. At most S vertices in total are deleted. All parallel pairs lose their endpoints and all cycles of length at most2h are destroyed. The remaining induced graph G is therefore simple, bipartite, has maximum degree at most100 and girth greater than2h, hence at least g. An acyclic remaining graph also satisfies the no-short-cycle convention. Deletion cannot introduce an empty rectangle of surviving vertices that was not already empty before deletion. The first-colour perfect matching loses at most one edge per deleted vertex, so nu(G)>=N-S>99N/100.

## Finish
If G had a uniquely restricted matching of size m>=N/2, then floor(m/2)>=N/4 because 4 divides N. Lemma1 gives an empty rectangle with each side at least N/4. Selecting exactly N/4 surviving vertices on each side contradicts Lemma2's inherited no-hole property. Thus nu_ur(G)<N/2. Since nu(G)>99N/100, we have nu_ur(G)/nu(G)<50/99<3/4 (200<297). This proves the author theorem for every g and the exact quantified negation. The graph need not have equal surviving side sizes, and no perfect matching of the surviving graph is claimed or required. A large retained matching suffices. Original graphs, not subdivisions, are used, so subdivision dilution does not arise.
