## 367030: even white holes, modulo four

The actual arXiv body served is 2502.05918v2, 3 June 2025. Its Conjecture 2 is the target. The actual BICA107 published reprint preserves it as Conjecture 3.7, printed page42. Source definitions on printed page37 call the majority color white; odd white vertices have odd row and column, while even white vertices have both coordinates even. Consequently r=1 or c=1 provides no even white hole, and cannot refute the statement. The clause “except sometimes” is not an exact classification of the exceptional residues. The unambiguous substantive direction is divisibility by four when at least one side is 1 modulo4.

Route A, cycle-flip extension. Form Ho on odd white vertices at distance two along rows or columns. A matching directs every Ho vertex towards the other odd white neighbor of its matched black vertex. Because the hole is even white, every Ho vertex has outdegree one. A finite functional digraph has a directed cycle. The source's planar parity argument makes every such cycle surround the hole, and the innermost-cycle reversal is a fixed-point-free involution on matchings. The same innermost geometric cycle remains selected after reversal. This pays evenness (source Lemma7(a), final Lemma3.4(a)), not four-divisibility. Flipping the very same cycle twice returns the original matching; it is not an independent second involution. Selecting a second Ho cycle cannot work uniformly because the source construction guarantees at least one, not two. Using the even-white web Se also fails at this stage: the hole is now its root, with outdegree zero, so a cycle-free rooted component is permitted. In the source's opposite case of an odd white hole, Se has no hole, explaining why its following mod-four observation for ODD holes does not solve this target. Figure5/arXiv printed page9 was visually inspected: it depicts one enclosing directed cycle and the complementary pair, not a guaranteed free four-action.

Route B, pair the two-element orbits rather than the matchings. Forget orientation of the selected cycle. A second pairing would need to act without fixed points on these unoriented configurations whenever a side is1mod4. The dimensional hypothesis has not entered RouteA at all. Boundary reflections move a general specified hole, so they do not act on the required fixed-hole matching set. Diagonal reflection is unavailable on a nonsquare rectangle. Thus the square proof's factorization cannot simply be transplanted. Precise missing lemma: the set of selected-cycle reversal orbits has even cardinality for every specified even white hole in every odd rectangle with at least one side1mod4, including off-axis holes. No parity proof of that orbit set is supplied here.

Route C, determinant lifting. A signed bipartite matching matrix for the punctured grid, IF its determinant is proved to count matchings with a common sign, could reduce the question to a determinant modulo4. Merely proving a mod2 singularity recovers evenness. Two independent mod2 kernel vectors would imply four-divisibility by integer row/column operations, but there is no proof of that stronger nullity statement; the source's parity theorem does not assert it. Even a corank-one integer matrix can have determinant2 rather than0mod4. A first-order mod4 lifting/cancellation for the particular grid signing is required, as is the signing-to-matching bridge. This conditional route is not represented as a paid theorem or a numerical experiment.

No full proof or refutation. This is a genuinely precise unpaid all-rectangle lemma, not an exclusion based on proof length. The final source itself leaves the claim as a conjecture. A current bounded search found that final and a secondary open-problem restatement; neither no-hits nor the open label clears novelty. No applicable new public full proof was authenticated.

## 371370: definition correction and the genuine residual question

Actual 2511.05285v3, 23 September2026, Section3.1 printed page5 defines (ρ,σ)-boundedness on EVERY induced subgraph of EVERY class member, explicitly to avoid needing the class hereditary. Question6.1 on printed page28 is exactly the pathwidth-to-α-treewidth question. Theorem6.3 proves the TREEDPTH analogue using exclusion of arbitrarily long induced paths plus bicliques and Theorem6.2; paths themselves have pathwidth at most2 in the source convention, so bounded pathwidth at clique2 cannot substitute in that proof.

The literal's weaker class-level reading admits the following full obstruction. For n>=2 let Gn be disjoint union of Kn and Kn,n, and close this family under isomorphism. Then ω(Gn)=n. A path decomposition of Kn,n holds all n vertices in one part and introduces vertices of the other part one at a time, so pw(Gn)<=n+1 using the source's maximum-bag-size convention. Thus the literal weak class-level bound f(k)=k+1 holds.

But every tree decomposition of Kn,n contains one entire part in a bag. Proof: let Ai,Bj be the connected subtrees of decomposition nodes carrying vertices of the two independent parts. Each Ai meets each Bj because every cross edge is present. If all Ai pairwise meet, the Helly property for subtrees of a tree gives one bag containing the entire A part. Otherwise choose two disjoint Ai subtrees. Every Bj contains the path between these two subtrees, so there is one bag carrying every B vertex. In either case a bag contains an independent n-set. Hence α-treewidth(Gn)>=n by restricting its bags to the induced Kn,n. The weak literal is false.

This does not refute the source question: the same class contains Kn,n as induced subgraphs of members, all with clique2 and pathwidth>=n (by the preceding bag argument). Therefore it is NOT (pw,ω)-bounded in the source definition. This is a complete source-to-literal correction bridge, zero significance credit; no appeal to shortness. Strongest contrary significance case: a new counterexample satisfying induced-subgraph boundedness, or a proof for all such classes, would remain a substantive result. This construction supplies neither.

The alternative positive route pays exclusion of sufficiently large bicliques, since those have fixed clique2 and unbounded pathwidth. It cannot pay exclusion of long paths, which have bounded pathwidth. The source's finite-forbidden-family theorem and treedepth result do not fill this gap. Another attempted counterexample route from the published treewidth counterexamples also lacks a pathwidth binding function; bounded treewidth in terms of clique number does not bound pathwidth in terms of clique number.
