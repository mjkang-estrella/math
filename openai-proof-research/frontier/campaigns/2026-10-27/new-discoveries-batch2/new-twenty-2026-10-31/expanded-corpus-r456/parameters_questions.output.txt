6    Discussion and open problems
Recall that Chudnovsky and Trotignon (see [30]) constructed graph classes with clique-bounded treewidth
and unbounded α-treewidth, thus disproving a conjecture of Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel [39] that
treewidth is awesome. However, their construction says nothing about the following weaker implication.

Question 6.1. Is it true that every (pw, ω)-bounded graph class has bounded α-treewidth?

    As we explain next, the analogue of Question 6.1 obtained by replacing pathwidth with treedepth
has a positive answer. This is a consequence of a recent result due to Hajebi and Spirkl [50] stating
that for any finite set F of graphs, the class of F-free graphs has bounded tree-independence number
if and only if F contains a complete bipartite graph, a graph each component of which is a path or a
subdivided claw, and the line graph of such a graph. Combined with the analogous characterisation
for bounded treewidth due to Lozin and Razgon [65], the result of Hajebi and Spirkl implies that
treewidth is awesome in a finite sense, that is, every hereditary graph class defined by finitely many
forbidden induced subgraphs that is (tw, ω)-bounded has bounded α-treewidth. It also implies the
following sufficient condition for bounded α-treewidth (see also [69, Theorem 1.2] for a recent further
strengthening).

Theorem 6.2. For every positive integer n, the class of {Pn , Kn,n }-free graphs has bounded α-treewidth.

    The announced result now follows easily.

Theorem 6.3. Every (td, ω)-bounded graph class has bounded α-treewidth.

Proof. By Theorem 6.2, it suffices to show that every hereditary (td, ω)-bounded graph class excludes
some path and some complete bipartite graph. Let G be a hereditary (td, ω)-bounded graph class,
and let f : N → N be a nondecreasing function such that td(G) ≤ f (ω(G)) for all G ∈ G. Let Pn be
an n-vertex path that belongs to G. Then td(Pn ) ≤ f (2), since ω(Pn ) ≤ 2 and f is nondecreasing.
However, td(Pn ) = ⌈log2 (n + 1)⌉ (see, e.g., [68]) and therefore n ≤ 2f (2) − 1. Hence G excludes the path
of order 2f (2) . The argument for complete bipartite graphs Kn,n is similar, using the fact that they
have bounded clique number but unbounded treedepth (note that td(Kn,n ) ≥ td(P2n ), since P2n is a
subgraph of Kn,n ).

    As a side remark, let us note that the conclusion of Theorem 6.2 cannot be strengthened to bounded
α-pathwidth. Indeed, our construction of (td, ω)-bounded graph classes with unbounded α-pathwidth
given in Section 4.2 results in {P6 , K2,2 }-free graphs. In particular, this shows that treedepth and
pathwidth are not awesome even in the aforementioned finite sense. On the other hand, a strengthening
to bounded α-treedepth can be obtained if, instead of excluding a complete bipartite graph, we exclude
a star K1,n . Indeed, the proof of [35, Theorem 1.5], stating that the class of {Pn , K1,n }-free graphs
has bounded α-treewidth, can be easily adapted to show that the class has bounded α-treedepth (see
also [22] for a different proof).

    We conclude the paper with several further questions left open by this work.
    A natural direction is to develop a better understanding of the landscape of awesome graph
(hyper)parameters, and in particular to identify which parameters beyond those captured by Theorem 5.7
are awesome. For instance, it would be interesting to determine whether degeneracy is awesome or not,
with respect to its canonical hyperparameterisation. A similarly defined max-min parameter, which we
deem worthy of investigation with respect to awesomeness, is symmetric difference of graphs. Symmetric
difference was implicitly introduced in [7], and studied in [5, 16, 36]. It can be viewed as a dense
analogue of degeneracy, and is formally defined for graphs G with at least two vertices as the maximum,
over all induced subgraphs H of G with at least two vertices, of the minimum, over all pairs of distinct
vertices x, y in H, of the number of vertices distinct from x and y that are adjacent to exactly one of x
and y.
    In Section 4, we introduced a graph transformation that increases α-pathwidth by exactly one, and
the same for α-treedepth. We are not aware of any such transformation for α-treewidth.

Question 6.4. Is there an efficiently computable graph transformation that increases α-treewidth by a
fixed constant?

    Recall that by Proposition 3.4, any graph class G that has bounded α-ρ, where ρ is a basic
hyperparameter, has clique-bounded ρ with a polynomial binding function. This motivates the study
of the following variant of awesomeness. Let us say that a basic hyperparameter ρ is weakly awesome
if polynomial clique-boundedness of ρ is equivalent to boundedness of α-ρ. Of course, every awesome
basic hyperparameter is also weakly awesome. But there may be more.

Question 6.5. Which basic hyperparameters are weakly awesome?

    At the moment, we are in fact not aware of any weakly awesome basic hyperparameter that is not
awesome: all the awful basic hyperparameters we are aware of (treewidth, pathwidth, treedepth, and
chromatic number) turn out not to be weakly awesome, as we explain next.
    In [30], Chudnovsky and Trotignon constructed graph classes with polynomially clique-bounded
treewidth but unbounded α-treewidth. This shows that treewidth is not weakly awesome. Furthermore,
our proof of Theorem 4.5, being based on a graph class in which the treedepth is bounded by a linear
function of the clique number, shows that pathwidth and treedepth are not weakly awesome.
    The argument based on Proposition 3.4 and [19] (recall the discussion following Question 3.5 on p. 11)
ruling out awesomeness of any hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number does not say anything
about weak awesomeness. Nonetheless, weak awesomeness of the canonical hyperparameterisation of
the chromatic number given in Section 3.3 can be ruled out with the following much simpler argument.
The family of disjoint unions of complete graphs forms a χ-bounded graph class G that has unbounded
α-chromatic number, since for every n ∈ N and every proper colouring of the graph nKn ∈ G, there
exists an independent set of n vertices with pairwise different colours.
    We leave for future research the study of various other hyperparameterisations of the chromatic num-
ber, including the question of whether the parameter admits a weakly awesome hyperparameterisation.

5.3     Some applications of awesomeness
We now derive some consequences of Corollary 5.8, giving sufficient conditions for awesomeness.

5.3.1    Modulators to treewidth
We start with the case when µ is a modulator to bounded treewidth, that is, µ = µtw,c for some c ∈ Z.
In this case, we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.24. Let c ∈ Z, let µ be the (tw, c)-modulator number, and let G be a (µ, ω)-bounded graph
class. Then G has bounded α-treewidth.

Proof. Corollary 5.8 implies that G has bounded α-µ, that is, there exists an integer k such that for each
graph G ∈ G and each induced subgraph G′ of G, there exists a set S ⊆ V (G′ ) such that α(G′ [S]) ≤ k
and tw(G′ − S) ≤ c. We can apply Lemma 5.14 with ρ = tw, since tw is strongly inheritable by
Proposition 5.11, and λ = α. This implies that α-tw(G) ≤ tw(G − S) + α(G[S]) ≤ c + k, and thus
shows that G has bounded α-treewidth, as claimed.

    Theorem 5.24 implies that any (µtw,c , ω)-bounded graph class enjoys the good algorithmic properties
of graph classes with bounded α-treewidth. In particular, Lima et al. [61] showed that for any such
graph class G, the following problems are solvable in polynomial time when restricted to graphs G ∈ G:



                                                        24

PAGE 25    • For every k, r and a CMSO2 formula ψ, given a graph G and a weight function on the vertices
     of G, find a maximum weight set X ⊆ V (G) such that G[X] |= ψ and ω(G[X]) ≤ r, or conclude
     that no such set exists.

   • For a fixed positive even integer d, given a collection of connected subgraphs of G, each equipped
     with a real weight, find a maximum weight subcollection of the subgraphs any two of which are at
     distance at least d from each other.

Both of the above results capture the Maximum Weight Independent Set and the Maximum
Weight Induced Matching problems (also studied for graphs with bounded α-treewidth in [38,
77]). Furthermore, the CMSO2 -based problem listed above encompasses many other problems, including
Graph Homomorphism to a fixed target graph, Maximum Induced Forest (which is equivalent
to Minimum Feedback Vertex Set), Maximum Induced Bipartite Subgraph (which is equivalent
to Minimum Odd Cycle Transversal), Maximum Induced Planar Subgraph, and Maximum
Induced Cycle Packing.
    In particular, Theorem 5.24 and the polynomial-time solvability of the Maximum Weight Inde-
pendent Set problem for graph classes with bounded α-treewidth provide a partial answer to the
following question.

Question 5.25 (Dallard et al. [39, Question 8.3]; see also [37, Question 9.8]). Is it true that for every
(tw, ω)-bounded graph class G, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for the Maximum Weight
Independent Set problem when restricted to graphs in G?

Indeed, since treewidth is inheritable due to Corollary 5.15, we derive from Theorem 5.18 (1) that,
for any integer c, any graph class with bounded (tw, c)-modulator number has bounded treewidth.
Therefore, the answer to Question 5.25 is affirmative if the treewidth is replaced by any (tw, c)-modulator
number. Formally, we have the following result, providing an infinite sequence of graph parameters
whose clique-boundedness implies polynomial-time solvability of the Maximum Weight Independent
Set problem.

Theorem 5.26. For every integer c and every (µ, ω)-bounded graph class G, where µ is the (tw, c)-
modulator number, there exists a polynomial-time algorithm for the Maximum Weight Independent
Set problem when restricted to graphs in G.

    Recall that for c ∈ {1, 2}, the (tw, c)-modulator number coincides with the vertex cover number and
the feedback vertex set number, respectively. The result of Theorem 5.26 for c = 1 is not new, as for
every (vc, ω)-bounded graph class G there exists an integer n such that every graph in G is nK2 -free
(see, e.g., Theorem 5.29), and this is known to imply polynomial-time solvability of the Maximum
Weight Independent Set problem (see, e.g., [63]). On the other hand, to the best of our knowledge,
the result of Theorem 5.26 is new for all c ≥ 2. In particular, for the case c = 2, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.27. For every (fvs, ω)-bounded graph class G, the Maximum Weight Independent Set
problem can be solved in polynomial time when restricted to graphs in G.

   We next complement the case c = 1 of Theorem 5.24 with a characterisation in terms of forbidden
induced subgraphs.

5.3.2   Vertex cover number
The fact that the vertex cover number is awesome leads to a characterisation of hereditary graph classes
with bounded α-vertex cover number. To derive this result, we recall the following Ramsey-type result.



                                                    25

PAGE 26 Theorem 5.28 (Curticapean and Marx [32], Lozin [64]). For any three positive integers p, q, and r
there exists an integer s such that any graph containing a matching of size s contains either Kp or Kq,q
or rK2 as an induced subgraph.

Theorem 5.29. Let G be a hereditary graph class. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

  1. G is (vc, ω)-bounded.

  2. G has bounded α-vertex cover number.

  3. There exists a positive integer n such that G excludes the graphs nK2 and Kn,n .

Proof. Corollary 5.9 implies the equivalence of the first two conditions.
    Assume now that the first condition holds, that is, G is (vc, ω)-bounded, and let f : N → N be a
nondecreasing function such that for every graph G in the class G, every induced subgraph G′ of G
satisfies vc(G′ ) ≤ f (ω(G′ )). For every positive integer n, the graphs nK2 and Kn,n have clique number 2,
and vertex cover number n. Therefore, G excludes the graphs nK2 and Kn,n for all n ≥ f (2) + 1. Hence,
the third condition holds.
    Finally, assume that the third condition holds, that is, that there exists a positive integer n such
that G excludes the graphs nK2 and Kn,n . By Theorem 5.28, for any three integers p, q, and r, there
exists a smallest integer s(p, q, r) such that every graph containing a matching of size s(p, q, r) contains
either Kp or Kq,q or rK2 as an induced subgraph. We show that G is (vc, ω)-bounded, more precisely,
that every graph G ∈ G has a vertex cover with cardinality at most f (ω(G)) where f : N → N is the
function defined by f (k) = 2(s(k + 1, n, n) − 1), for all k ∈ N. Let G be a graph in G and let k be the
clique number of G. Since G has no clique of size k + 1 nor an induced subgraph isomorphic to Kn,n
or nK2 , Theorem 5.28 implies that G does not contain a matching of size s(k + 1, n, n). Let M be a
maximum matching in G and let S be the set of vertices of G saturated by M . Then S is a vertex cover
in G with cardinality at most 2(s(k + 1, n, n) − 1). Therefore, vc(G) ≤ f (k), as claimed.

    Since the independence number α is weakly submodular and tame, Propositions 3.3 and 5.13 imply
that bounded α-vertex cover number implies bounded α-treewidth. Hence, Theorem 5.29 provides
a strengthening of the fact that, for any positive integer n, the class of {nK2 , Kn,n }-free graphs has
bounded α-treewidth. The latter is a consequence of a more general result due to Abrishami et al. [2,
Theorem 1.1], as well as of a result of Hajebi and Spirkl [50, Theorem 1.4].

satisfying this property, a witnessing vertex partition can be computed in O(|V (G)|6k+2 ).

problems that are NP-hard in general.


6    Discussion and open problems
Recall that Chudnovsky and Trotignon (see [30]) constructed graph classes with clique-bounded treewidth
and unbounded α-treewidth, thus disproving a conjecture of Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel [39] that
treewidth is awesome. However, their construction says nothing about the following weaker implication.

Question 6.1. Is it true that every (pw, ω)-bounded graph class has bounded α-treewidth?

    As we explain next, the analogue of Question 6.1 obtained by replacing pathwidth with treedepth
has a positive answer. This is a consequence of a recent result due to Hajebi and Spirkl [50] stating
that for any finite set F of graphs, the class of F-free graphs has bounded tree-independence number
if and only if F contains a complete bipartite graph, a graph each component of which is a path or a
subdivided claw, and the line graph of such a graph. Combined with the analogous characterisation
for bounded treewidth due to Lozin and Razgon [65], the result of Hajebi and Spirkl implies that
treewidth is awesome in a finite sense, that is, every hereditary graph class defined by finitely many
forbidden induced subgraphs that is (tw, ω)-bounded has bounded α-treewidth. It also implies the
following sufficient condition for bounded α-treewidth (see also [69, Theorem 1.2] for a recent further
strengthening).

Theorem 6.2. For every positive integer n, the class of {Pn , Kn,n }-free graphs has bounded α-treewidth.

    The announced result now follows easily.

Theorem 6.3. Every (td, ω)-bounded graph class has bounded α-treewidth.

Proof. By Theorem 6.2, it suffices to show that every hereditary (td, ω)-bounded graph class excludes
some path and some complete bipartite graph. Let G be a hereditary (td, ω)-bounded graph class,
and let f : N → N be a nondecreasing function such that td(G) ≤ f (ω(G)) for all G ∈ G. Let Pn be
an n-vertex path that belongs to G. Then td(Pn ) ≤ f (2), since ω(Pn ) ≤ 2 and f is nondecreasing.
However, td(Pn ) = ⌈log2 (n + 1)⌉ (see, e.g., [68]) and therefore n ≤ 2f (2) − 1. Hence G excludes the path
of order 2f (2) . The argument for complete bipartite graphs Kn,n is similar, using the fact that they
have bounded clique number but unbounded treedepth (note that td(Kn,n ) ≥ td(P2n ), since P2n is a
subgraph of Kn,n ).

    As a side remark, let us note that the conclusion of Theorem 6.2 cannot be strengthened to bounded
α-pathwidth. Indeed, our construction of (td, ω)-bounded graph classes with unbounded α-pathwidth
given in Section 4.2 results in {P6 , K2,2 }-free graphs. In particular, this shows that treedepth and
pathwidth are not awesome even in the aforementioned finite sense. On the other hand, a strengthening
to bounded α-treedepth can be obtained if, instead of excluding a complete bipartite graph, we exclude
a star K1,n . Indeed, the proof of [35, Theorem 1.5], stating that the class of {Pn , K1,n }-free graphs
has bounded α-treewidth, can be easily adapted to show that the class has bounded α-treedepth (see
also [22] for a different proof).

PAGE 29     We conclude the paper with several further questions left open by this work.
    A natural direction is to develop a better understanding of the landscape of awesome graph
(hyper)parameters, and in particular to identify which parameters beyond those captured by Theorem 5.7
are awesome. For instance, it would be interesting to determine whether degeneracy is awesome or not,
with respect to its canonical hyperparameterisation. A similarly defined max-min parameter, which we
deem worthy of investigation with respect to awesomeness, is symmetric difference of graphs. Symmetric
difference was implicitly introduced in [7], and studied in [5, 16, 36]. It can be viewed as a dense
analogue of degeneracy, and is formally defined for graphs G with at least two vertices as the maximum,
over all induced subgraphs H of G with at least two vertices, of the minimum, over all pairs of distinct
vertices x, y in H, of the number of vertices distinct from x and y that are adjacent to exactly one of x
and y.
    In Section 4, we introduced a graph transformation that increases α-pathwidth by exactly one, and
the same for α-treedepth. We are not aware of any such transformation for α-treewidth.

Question 6.4. Is there an efficiently computable graph transformation that increases α-treewidth by a
fixed constant?

    Recall that by Proposition 3.4, any graph class G that has bounded α-ρ, where ρ is a basic
hyperparameter, has clique-bounded ρ with a polynomial binding function. This motivates the study
of the following variant of awesomeness. Let us say that a basic hyperparameter ρ is weakly awesome
if polynomial clique-boundedness of ρ is equivalent to boundedness of α-ρ. Of course, every awesome
basic hyperparameter is also weakly awesome. But there may be more.

Question 6.5. Which basic hyperparameters are weakly awesome?

    At the moment, we are in fact not aware of any weakly awesome basic hyperparameter that is not
awesome: all the awful basic hyperparameters we are aware of (treewidth, pathwidth, treedepth, and
chromatic number) turn out not to be weakly awesome, as we explain next.
    In [30], Chudnovsky and Trotignon constructed graph classes with polynomially clique-bounded
treewidth but unbounded α-treewidth. This shows that treewidth is not weakly awesome. Furthermore,
our proof of Theorem 4.5, being based on a graph class in which the treedepth is bounded by a linear
function of the clique number, shows that pathwidth and treedepth are not weakly awesome.
    The argument based on Proposition 3.4 and [19] (recall the discussion following Question 3.5 on p. 11)
ruling out awesomeness of any hyperparameterisation of the chromatic number does not say anything
about weak awesomeness. Nonetheless, weak awesomeness of the canonical hyperparameterisation of
the chromatic number given in Section 3.3 can be ruled out with the following much simpler argument.
The family of disjoint unions of complete graphs forms a χ-bounded graph class G that has unbounded
α-chromatic number, since for every n ∈ N and every proper colouring of the graph nKn ∈ G, there
exists an independent set of n vertices with pairwise different colours.
    We leave for future research the study of various other hyperparameterisations of the chromatic num-
ber, including the question of whether the parameter admits a weakly awesome hyperparameterisation.
