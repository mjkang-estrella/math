## 371828: complete earlier-public theorem bridge, excluded

Write p for the cycle-power parameter and h for the positive integer called r in the assigned statement. The exact graph is the simple graph C_n^p on Z/nZ, adjacent at cyclic distance at most p. Its total 2-cut complex D has faces sigma whose complement contains a nonadjacent pair. Thus D=Cl(C_n^p)^*, directly from the Alexander-dual definition; this identifies the source objects, not merely their names.

The source range is 2p+3<=n<=3p, hence p>=3. Set t=n-2p, so 3<=t<=p. The assigned interval says p/(h+1)<t<=p/h. If equality holds, p=h*t and p/n=h/(2h+1). If the inequality is strict on both sides, h<p/t<h+1 and therefore h/(2h+1)<p/n<(h+1)/(2h+3). All multiplications use positive denominators. These are precisely the l=h cases of Bravo Proposition 47 (Adamaszek Corollary 6.7).

That public result gives Cl(C_n^p) a wedge of n-2p-1 spheres of dimension 2h at equality, and a single sphere of dimension 2h+1 otherwise. Integer Alexander duality gives the reduced homology of D in dimensions n-2h-3 and n-2h-4 respectively, with the stated multiplicity. There is no torsion, so the cohomology shift introduces no additional group.

Here is an independent connectivity bridge, so homology is not silently substituted for homotopy. The complement H of C_n^p is regular of positive degree n-1-2p. An independent set S in any positive-degree regular graph has |S|<=n/2: count the degree incidences from S into its complement to get d|S|<=d(n-|S|). For every set sigma of at most three vertices, |V\sigma|>=n-3>n/2, since n>=9. Thus V\sigma is not independent in H and contains a nonedge of C_n^p. Every such sigma lies in D. Consequently D contains the full 2-skeleton and is simply connected. The finite complex with free reduced homology concentrated in a single positive degree is a wedge of spheres: Hurewicz gives maps representing a basis in the first nonzero degree, their wedge induces a homology isomorphism, and simply connected Whitehead gives a homotopy equivalence. The dimensions here are at least 2. This proves the exact claimed homotopy formula from the complete earlier classical clique theorem.

For n<3p the substitution is Bravo Theorem 48(b). At n=3p the assigned integer is h=1 and Theorem 48(c) gives p-1 spheres of dimension 3p-5, exactly n-2p-1 and n-2h-3. ## 372043: minimal-fort rigidity route, not closed

A fort is a nonempty vertex subset F with |N(v) intersect F| !=1 for every v outside F. Minimality is inclusion-minimality among forts. If a forcing process starting from S ever first entered a fort disjoint from S, its forcing vertex would be outside the fort and have exactly one neighbor in it, impossible. Conversely, a process that stops with nonempty white set W leaves every gray vertex with zero or at least two neighbors in W; W is a fort and contains a minimal fort by finiteness. Hence S zero forces iff it intersects every minimal fort, proving Z(G)=tau(F_G).

Let m=|F_G|. Selecting one vertex per fort proves tau<=m. If two minimal forts intersect, select one vertex in their intersection and one vertex from each of the remaining m-2 forts: this gives tau<=m-1. If all minimal forts are pairwise disjoint, every transversal has at least m vertices, so tau=m. Thus tau=m iff all minimal forts are pairwise disjoint. This reduction already appears in substance in v1 Propositions 5.1, 5.2 and 5.4; it earns no separate credit.

At m=n/3, the exact desired claim is therefore extremal rigidity: every pair of minimal forts is disjoint. A putative proof by fort packing assumes this conclusion. A putative lower bound Z>=n/3 has no general justification: long paths have Z=1. The source's tree proof obtains structural star centers and H corona E2; cycles destroy the cut-edge decomposition used in that proof. Latest v3 Theorem 5.4 establishes the equality characterization for trees only. v1 Conjecture 6.3 was the actual all-graph question. v2/v3 no longer printing it does not establish a solution.

A useful exact subcase is m=1,n=3: tau=1 by the argument above, without classifying the graph. Extending it to arbitrary m cannot be obtained by adding components unless every component separately has its tight fort count. The exact missing lemma is: a finite simple graph with 3|F_G|=|V(G)| cannot have intersecting minimal forts. A global strict abundance inequality for intersecting forts would be sufficient but is stronger and is not being substituted for the assigned target. No proof or counterexample to the exact equality-rigidity lemma was obtained. The 2026 companion gives abundance for block graphs and counts compatible forts; abundance alone does not force pairwise disjointness at equality. Only its displayed relevant lemmas were reviewed. Figures in v1 were not visually inspected, so no illustrated graph is claimed as a certificate.

## 371659: one-rule-per-root Horn systems, not closed

For each root v there is at most one implication A_v -> v with A_v subset V\{v}. The closed family C consists exactly of F satisfying A_v subset F implies v in F. Empty-set membership excludes an empty stem. The desired endpoint is NDS(C)=sum over F of (2|F|-|V|)<=0, not merely one rare element. The source Conjecture 6.1 explicitly leaves even the rare-element assertion open in the general stem setting; the corpus explanatory claim that it is already known cannot be used as a proof.

For a chosen v put W=V\{v}, C0={F in C:v notin F}, and C1={F\{v}:F in C,v in F}. For any family A on W write NDS_W(A)=sum (2|F|-|W|). Directly separating the two disjoint classes gives
NDS_V(C)=NDS_W(C0)+NDS_W(C1)+|C1|-|C0|.
This identity is exact and keeps multiplicities separated. It supplies a concrete induction target.

The first attempted induction would apply the conjecture separately to C0,C1. It fails its hypotheses. C0 forbids containing A_v; generally W is absent. C1 has the rule A_v removed but all occurrences of v removed from other stems, possibly producing empty stems and forced elements, so empty-set membership can fail. Even hypothetical nonpositive NDS for both terms does not settle the count difference. A second attempt projects C onto W and invokes a functional-preorder theorem. This loses whether each trace has one or two preimages, and arbitrary stems do not become singleton stems. Eliminating a root by substituting its rule into several other rules can create multiple constraints, so one cannot assume the original hypothesis is preserved under all needed deletions.

The source forest proof has a genuine special mechanism: for a connected rooted forest, the only ideal containing its top root is V, yielding NDS(I)=NDS(I without top)+|V|-|I|+1. Distinct principal ideals and the empty ideal give |I|>=|V|+1. Disconnected forests use the exact product identity NDS(A x B)=|B|NDS(A)+|A|NDS(B). Larger stems supply neither unique top nor that ideal count. These are complete reductions for the source special case, not a full proof of the assigned question. Missing: a trace/conditioning inequality strong enough to bound the displayed three terms while preserving one-rule-per-root semantics.
