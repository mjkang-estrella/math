                                                   Abstract. In this paper, we investigate the homotopy type and combinatorial properties
                                                   of total cut complexes of squared cycle graphs. The total cut complexes are a new type
                                                   of graphical complexes introduced by Bayer et al in [1] to extend Fröberg’s theorem. In
                                                   [3], the authors made a conjecture on the homotopy type of total cut complexes of squared
                                                   cycle graphs for k ≥ 3. We proved this conjecture in the case k = 3. For general k ≥ 3, we
                                                   confirmed the cases when n = 3k + 1 and 3k + 2.

                                       2. Preliminaries
  In this section, we give some basic definitions and useful results related to total cut
complexes and discrete Morse matching. General references for simplicial complexes and
topology are [4], [11] and [10].

2.1. Simplicial Complexes. A simplicial complex ∆ on a set A is a collection of subsets of
A such that
                                  σ ∈ ∆ and τ ⊆ σ ⇒ τ ∈ ∆.
The elements of ∆ are its faces or simplices. If the collection of subsets is empty, we call
∆ the void complex. The dimension of a face σ, dim(σ), is one less than its cardinality. A
facet of a simplicial complex is a maximal face. The dimension of a simplicial complex is the
maximum of the dimensions of its simplices. The complexes we will be considering are pure,
meaning that all facets are of the same dimension.
   We will be using the fowlling operations.
Definition 2.1. [11][12] Let ∆ be a simplicial complex and σ a face of ∆.
    • The link of σ in ∆ is
                        lk∆ σ = {τ ∈ ∆ | σ ∩ τ = ∅, and σ ∪ τ ∈ ∆}.
      • The (closed) star of σ in ∆ is
                                st∆ σ = {τ ∈ ∆ | σ ∪ τ ∈ ∆}.
      • The deletion of σ in ∆ is
                                    del∆ σ = {τ ∈ ∆ | σ ̸⊆ τ }.
2.2. Discrete Morse Theory. We present the essential definitions and key tools from
discrete Morse Theory that are utilized in this paper. For more details, refer to [8].
Definition 2.2. [8] A partial matching in a poset (P, <) is a subset M ⊆ P × P , such that:
     (i) If (a, b) ∈ M , then b ≻ a, meaning a < b and there is no c ∈ P satisfying a < c < b;
         and
    (ii) Every a ∈ P is contained in at most one pair in M .
   If (a, b) ∈ M , we denote a by d(b) and b by u(a). A partial matching on P is called acyclic
if there do not exist distinct ai ∈ P , 1 ≤ i ≤ m, m ≥ 2 such that
(4)          a1 ≺ u(a1 ) ≻ a2 ≺ u(a2 ) ≻ . . . ≺ u(am ) ≻ a1 is a cycle.
  After applying an acyclic partial matching M on P , those elements which not belong to
the matching are called unmatched or critical cells with respect to the matching M.
  We use the following type of matchings as the main tool in computing the homotopy types.

Theorem 2.5 ([12],Thm.11.13). Let ∆ be a simplicial complex and M be an acyclic matching
on the face poset of ∆. Denote by ci the number of critical i-dimensional cells of ∆ with
respect to the matching M. Then ∆ is homotopy equivalent to a cell complex ∆c that has
exactly ci cells in each dimension i ≥ 0, with the addition of a single 0-dimensional cell, when
the empty set is also paired.
   In particular, if an acyclic matching has critical cells in only one fixed dimension i ≥ 0,
then ∆ is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of i-dimensional spheres.

                       3. 3-total cut complex of squred cycles
   In this section, we prove the first main result (Theorem 1.5) of this article. Throughout the
section, we use the discrete Morse theory as our main tool. We aim to construct an efficient
element matching. First, we recall the following definitons.
Definition 3.1. The squared cycle graph Wn is the graph with vertex set [n], and edge set
{{i, i + 1 (mod n)}, {i, i + 2 (mod n)}}, i = 1, 2, · · · , n.
   We label the vertices of Wn by 1, 2, · · · , n in cyclic order. Note if n ≤ 5, Wn is the complete
graph Kn .
   To study the k-total cut complex of Wn , we need to determine when a subset A of vertices
forms a face of ∆tk (Wn ). At this point we have
Lemma 3.2. For n ≥ 3k + 1, any subset A of 3k − 2 vertices must contain a k-independent
set in Wn . Therefore, every subset of [n] with cardinality at most n − 3k + 2 is a face of
∆tk (Wn ).
Proof. Let A be a subset of 3k −2 vertices. For n > 3k +1, we can remove n−(3k +1) vertices
that are not in A from Wn without increasing the independent number of A. Therefore, it
suffices to show that any subset of [3k + 1] with cardinality 3k − 2 contains a k-independent
set in Wn .
   If [3k + 1] \ A is a set of three consecutive vertices, then A is a squared path and contains
a k-independent set.
   If [3k + 1] \ A contains two adjacent vertices and one vertex not adjacent to either, then
A is divided into two squared paths of cardinality c1 and c2 , where c1 + c2 = 3k − 2. If
c1 ≡ c2 ≡ 2 (mod 3), then A contains a c13+1 + c23+1 = k-independent set. If c1 ≡ 0 (mod 3)
or c2 ≡ 0 (mod 3), then A contains a c31 + c23+2 = k-independent set.
            HOMOTOPY TYPE OF TOTAL CUT COMPLEXES OF SQUARED CYCLE GRAPHS                          5

   If [3k + 1] \ A are three nonadjacent vertices, then A is divided into three squared paths of
cardinality c1 , c2 , c3 , where c1 + c2 + c3 = 3k − 2. Then there are three cases (considering the
symmetry of the three parts):
       • If c1 ≡ c2 ≡ 0 (mod 3), then A contains a c31 + c32 + c33+2 = k-independent set.
       • If c1 ≡ c2 ≡ 1 (mod 3), then A contains a c13+2 + c23−1 + c33+1 = k-independent set.
       • If c1 ≡ c2 ≡ 2 (mod 3), then A contains a c13+1 + c23+1 + c33 = k-independent set.
Therefore, we have shown that any subset of [3k + 1] with cardinality 3k − 2 contains a
k-independent set, and thus every subset of [n] with cardinality at most n − 3k + 2 is a face
of ∆tk (Wn ).                                                                                    □

                             5. Conclusions and Further Directions
   In [3] and [1], the authors determined the homotopy type of the complex ∆tk (Cn ) and
∆k (Cn ) for all k. They also proved that the complex ∆t2 (Wn ) (n ≥ 7) is homotopy equivalent
to a single sphere of dimension n − 4. Later in [6], the authors proved that the complex
∆3 (Wn ) of the squared cycle graphs is shellable for all n ≥ 9, by constructing a shelling order.
In this paper, we proved that the complex ∆t3 (Wn ) (n ≥ 9) is homotopy equivalent to a
single sphere of dimension n − 6. Moreover, we construct a new approach to describe the
faces of ∆tk (Wn ), and solve the condition when n = 3k + 1 and n = 3k + 2. We believe that
this method can be generalized to prove the homotopy type of ∆tk (W3k+i ) for all k ≥ 3 and
1 ≤ i ≤ k − 1.
   Furthermore, by using SageMath, we calculated the homology groups of ∆tk (Cnp ), namely
k-total cut complexes of powers of cycles (with coefficient Z), and conjectured general cases
for total cut complexes of powers of cycles. We have data for some small values of k, p
and n, which is illustrated in Tables 1-3. Missing entries in these tables indicate that the
corresponding complex ∆tk (Cnp ) is void. An entry of the form i : Zβ denotes that the i-th
homology group is Zβ . Through these tables, we propose the fowlling conjectures.
