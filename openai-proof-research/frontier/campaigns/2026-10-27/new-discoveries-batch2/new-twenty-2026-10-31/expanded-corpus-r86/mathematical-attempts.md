## 354079: complete classical bridge, excluded as routine

The source algebra is the quotient by the squarefree quadratic **edge ideal**, not the subalgebra generated by edge monomials. This distinction is essential. Read Milas §2.1–2.2, Theorem 2.3 and Conjecture 2.4 (`sources/354079.txt`, lines 354–535). Read Trung, *On Gorenstein graphs*, Theorem 4.4 and its whole proof (`sources/gorenstein-prior.txt`, lines 607–631). The latter paper is published in Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics 54 (2021), 673–688, DOI 10.1007/s10801-021-01051-9. The primary manuscript was retrieved from its author's institute; the DOI page independently identifies the same statement and publication.

For k >= 2 put H = Gamma_(3k-1), and let x = 3k-1 be its last vertex. From the source edge lists,

N_H(x) = {1,4} union {3s+1 : 1 <= s <= k-2}.

The set indexed by s is empty when k = 2. The graph Gamma_(3k+2) keeps every edge of H and adds exactly

(x,3k), (3k,3k+1), (3k+1,3k+2), and (3k+2,v) for v in N_H(x).

To verify the last assertion directly, the extra new edge to 3k-2 is the s = ell = k-1 instance of the final source edge rule when k >= 3; the remaining vertices in N_H(x) are already listed by the source special-vertex rule and earlier instances of that final rule. No other new edge is allowed by the source list. The old terminal x is non-isolated (it is adjacent to 1 and 4).

This is Trung's construction with a = 3k+2, b = 3k+1, c = 3k. Its hypotheses and ring convention match exactly. The base Gamma_5 is C5, which is the result of the same theorem applied to K2 (also explicitly discussed in Trung's proof). K2 is Gorenstein: its ring is a polynomial ring modulo the nonzero quadratic xy, a hypersurface. Induction proves the full source assertion over every field. The source author does not need to know or cite this application for the classical bridge to be decisive under this campaign's exclusion rule. This is a routine direct application of the already published preservation theorem, not a qualifying new discovery.

An independent topological explanation: join Ind(H) with a 0-sphere on vertices u,v, then stellar-subdivide the edge {x,u}. In the complement graph, subdivision adds edges x-u, u-v, v-w and connects w to N_H(x), exactly the same operation. Thus these independence complexes are iterated stellar subdivisions of suspensions of a pentagon. This route was developed before the explicit published graph theorem was found and is superseded by that sharper prior bridge.

Computational checks: all independent sets for k = 1,...,8; purity and h-vectors; reduced F2 homology of every link for k <= 5 (11 + 39 + 135 + 459 + 1539 = 2183 links). All are sphere-compatible. These are diagnostics over one field, not an all-field ring certificate. `scripts/structural_verify.py` also verifies the edge-set identification for k = 2,...,100. No Lean investment is justified after the classical exclusion.

## 348288: full finite rule universe, bounded modular orbit tests

Source §§2, 4.1, 5.1 and 5.4 define Q as connected, cardinally unbounded, diagonally unbounded, non-glued rules. Connectivity is strong connectivity of a four-vertex directed graph with loops. Cardinal unboundedness means that each cardinal direction can occur twice without its opposite intervening; the script tests the corresponding directed cycle in the induced three-vertex digraph. Diagonal and glued predicates are literal equations (5.3)–(5.5) and (5.8).

Enumerating all 65536 matrices reproduces the source counts: connected = 25696, U = 14978, U intersect D = 14209, Q = 13749. This checks definition recovery, not the conjecture.

Let M = I - T diag(tx,ty,t/x,t/y). Put D = det M and D_theta = the principal complementary minor. The renewal decomposition gives 1-B_theta = D/D_theta. This can also be obtained directly by the Schur complement: remove theta, sum arbitrary intervening paths with (I-M_removed)^(-1), and return to theta. The source involutions preserve D/D_theta. Each determinant has Laurent degree at most 1 in x and y, since each weighted column occurs at most once. Multiplying the equality in one variable by that variable gives a quadratic with one known root. The product-of-roots formula supplies the other root, exactly as in source Lemma 20.

The script evaluates that formula over F_1000003, at two starts (x,y,t) = (7,11,5) and (13,17,19). It checks powers 1 through 24 of the product of the two involutions, separately for all four theta. All 13749 rules were evaluated. First-direction first/second-point counts: 2138 have return 2 at both, 872 return 3, 286 return 4, 132 return 5, 12 return 6; 10309 have no return through 24. These are product return lengths, **not certified group orders**. Multiplying a genuine product order by two would give the dihedral group order only after proving both generators have the required distinct nontrivial behavior.

Eight rows encounter a singular evaluation in at least one direction. Two nonsingular rows have a return at 24 at one point in the north direction but no return at the other point. These are not consistent evidence of rational finite order and do not refute the conjecture. No row has a stable unequal finite return across its two points. Full matrices and all exceptions are retained in `outputs/groups-analysis.json` and full logs. A single modular non-return can disprove a proposed small rational order at a good specialization, but it cannot prove infinite order. Conversely a modular return at two points proves no rational identity.

Second route considered: all four maps restrict to the same two root swaps on the spectral curve D=0, because the equation B_theta=1 is independent of theta. This alone is insufficient: it is only a common invariant **fiber**, whereas group order is an identity on the two-variable rational function field. A proof needs a faithful restriction theorem or explicit conjugacies between all four rational actions; neither is established. Restriction to one elliptic curve cannot silently replace the full source endpoint. An exhaustive exact rational finite-order classification plus certified infinite-order witnesses is another remaining route. No such certificate is provided.

## 351203: determinant tests and the all-n reducedness gap

Read thesis §2.2.2, Cayley's criterion and the determinant construction (2.5)–(2.6), the degree proof in Propositions 2.21–2.22, and Conjecture 2.26. Read the published article's Remark 1.5 and corresponding Cayley/degree discussion (`sources/billiard-journal.txt`). Odd n = 2m+1 uses det(B_(i+j)), indices i,j = 1,...,m. Even n = 2m uses det(B_(i+j+1)), i,j = 1,...,m-1. The B_j are coefficients of

sqrt((1+(1-lambda/a)t)(1+(1-lambda/b)t)(1+t)).

The recurrence 2B_j = P_j - sum_(i=1)^(j-1) B_i B_(j-i) computes these coefficients over any odd-characteristic field. This avoids ambiguous OCR of the even determinant. At a=2, b=1 modulo 1009, for every n=3,...,24 the determinant has exactly the generic upper-bound degree and its gcd with its derivative is 1. Degrees range from 2 to 143. Full outputs and the recurrence/determinant code are preserved.

These finite modular identities imply nonzero discriminants over the characteristic-zero parameter field for these individual n if the obvious denominator and reduction bridges are supplied. They do not prove the universal claim for all n, nor constitute Lean certificates. Exceptional a=b cases do not refute generic simplicity: the source expressly excludes finitely many parameter ratios in its generic convention.

A possible all-n mechanism uses the elliptic curve y^2=(1+t)(1+rt)(1+st), the marked point (0,1), and its n-torsion divisor. Here r=1-lambda/a, s=1-lambda/b; changing lambda moves along lines through (1,1) in the (r,s)-plane. If the Cayley determinant defines the reduced n-torsion divisor with multiplicity one, characteristic-zero generic transversality for this pencil would yield simplicity away from its base locus. The missing steps are substantial: the scheme-theoretic multiplicity-one determinant/torsion comparison, exclusion of pencil components contained in a radial line, and treatment of degeneracies and composite periods. Merely identifying the determinant's zero set with a torsion locus does not prove reducedness. No claim of a full proof or novelty is made.

## 348638: coefficient search only

Source class: f is analytic and normalized on the disk and z f'(z)/f(z)-1 is subordinate to sinh z. A Schwarz function w gives q=sinh(w) and f=z exp(integral q(t)/t dt). A finite Blaschke family w=z^s(a+z^d)/(1+a z^d), with real |a|<1, belongs to the Schwarz class. The script tests d,s = 1,...,11 and 101 equally spaced a in [-0.99,0.99], total 12221 functions, coefficients a_2,...,a_33. It uses floating complex arithmetic, so is only exploratory. There is no violation beyond 1e-9 tolerance. It neither optimizes the full Schur parameter space nor proves the all-coefficient bound. Its coefficients are obtained from n A_n = sum_(j=1)^n q_j A_(n-j), with A_0=1 and f=z sum A_n z^n.

### Additional 351203 primary-body comparison: the determinant/EDS bridge

A further retrieved primary source is A. Yura, *Hankel Determinant Solution for Elliptic Sequence*, arXiv:1411.6972 (`sources/billiard-division-prior.pdf`; see Theorem 1, equations (19)–(22), and the appendix proof). The whole theorem statement, recurrence setup and relevant determinant identities were read. This is not recorded as a full subsumer of generic simplicity.

There is an exact useful conversion. Write sqrt(1+S t+B t^2+A t^3)=sum C_j t^j, C_0=1, and set u_j=-C_(j+1)/2. Comparing coefficients of the square gives

u_(n+1)=sum_(i=0)^n u_i u_(n-i) for n>=2,

with

u_0=-S/4, u_1=-(4B-S^2)/16, u_2=-(S^3-4BS+8A)/32.

These are precisely the three-parameter Catalan coefficients in Yura (19). Put H_k^(r)=det(u_(i+j+r))_(0<=i,j<k). The source Cayley determinants are (-2)^k H_k^(1) for n=2k+1, and (-2)^k H_k^(2) for n=2k+2. In Yura's normalization choose sigma=-1 and W_2^4=-A/4; then the same determinants are 2^k W_(2k+1), and 2^k W_(2k+2)/W_2 respectively. This follows directly from equation (20), since 2u_0 u_1-u_2=A/4. It supplies an exact all-index recurrence bridge on the generic nondegenerate locus (possibly after adjoining a fourth root), much stronger than a numerical resemblance.

However Yura's general elliptic-sequence parametrization does not by itself show that this particular one-parameter pencil crosses each torsion divisor with multiplicity one. To apply characteristic-zero separability, one still needs a rigorous smooth/etale moduli-coordinate comparison for the (r,s)-family, and a unit-times-division-polynomial identification including bad/base fibers. Those gaps remain. No full all-n proof, routine-exclusion ruling, Lean endpoint or discovery credit is claimed from this additional comparison.
