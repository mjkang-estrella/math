In this section, we investigate the feedback game on toroidal grid graphs. The undirected

solved [11], and the directed edge geography on a directed toroidal grid graph is also
investigated in [13].



                                                    9
Definition 14 (Toroidal grid graph). A toroidal grid graph Q(m, n) is the Cartesian
product of two cycles Cm = u0 u1 . . . um−1 and Cn = v0 v1 . . . vn−1 with m ≥ 2 and n ≥ 2,
that is,
    • V (Q(m, n)) = {(ui , vj ) : i ∈ {0, 1, . . . , m − 1}, j ∈ {0, 1, . . . , n − 1}}.

    • (ui , vj )(ui′ , vj ′ ) ∈ E(Q(m, n)) if and only if

           – i = i′ and vj vj ′ ∈ E(Cn ) or
           – j = j ′ and ui ui′ ∈ E(Cm ).

    In other words, Q(m, n) is a 4-regular quadrangulation embedded on the torus, which
is a graph on a surface with each face quadrangular. For example, see Figure 7; by
identifying the top and bottom (resp., right and left) sides along the direction of arrows,
we have the toroidal grid graph Q(3, 4). Note that Q(m, n) is vertex-transitive, that is,
there exists an automorphism of the graph mapping a vertex into any other vertex. Thus
the feedback game on Q(m, n) does not depend on the choice of a starting vertex, and
hence, toroidal grid graphs seem to be a reasonable subclass of connected Eulerian graphs
with maximum degree at most 4 for considering the feedback game.
