   Our motivation for asking this question comes from the study of the semigroup tree. The
semigroup tree is a rooted tree with root N0 where the vertices at level g are exactly the
numerical semigroups of genus g. The easiest way to describe the tree is by explaining how
to find the path from a numerical semigroup S back up to N0 . If S has genus g, then
S ′ = S ∪ {F (S)} is a numerical semigroup of genus g − 1. Repeating this process, we get a
numerical semigroup of genus g − 2, followed by one of genus g − 3, all the way back up to
N0 , the unique numerical semigroup of genus 0.
   This description does not directly explain how to produce level g + 1 of the tree given the
first g levels of the tree. Let S be a numerical semigroup. We say that S ′ is a child of S in
the semigroup tree if S = S ′ ∪ {F (S ′ )}. We now explain how to describe the set of children
of S. Minimal generators of S larger than F (S) are called effective generators, or sometimes
right generators, of S. Let eg(S) denote the set of effective generators of S. The number
of effective generators of S, | eg(S)|, is sometimes called the effectivity of S and is denoted

                 3. Semigroups with Fixed Ordinarization Number
  The main motivation for this section comes from Conjecture 1.5.
Question 3.1. Let r be a fixed positive integer. How does ng,r behave as a function of g?
   Proposition 2.1 answers this question when r = 1. There is one quadratic polynomial that
gives the formula for ng,1 when g is even, and a different quadratic polynomial that gives the
formula for ng,1 when g is odd. We show that this kind of behavior holds for any value of r.
Definition 3.2. A quasipolynomial Q is an expression of the form
                              Q(t) = cn (t)tn + · · · + c1 (t)t + c0 (t)
where c0 , . . . , cn are periodic functions in t. Assume that cn is not the zero function. The
degree of Q is n, and the least common period of c0 , . . . , cn is the period of Q.
  A function f : N → N is eventually quasipolynomial if there exists a quasipolynomial Q
such that f (t) = Q(t) for all sufficiently large t.
Example 3.3. The formula for ng,1 from Proposition 2.1 is a quasipolynomial of degree 2
and period 2. We see that c2 is the constant function 83 and each of c1 and c0 have period 2.
