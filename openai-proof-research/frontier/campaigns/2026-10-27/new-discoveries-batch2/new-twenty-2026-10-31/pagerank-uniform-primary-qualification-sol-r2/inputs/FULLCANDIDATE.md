Exact setup. N is a finite set of n nodes, E a subset of N×N, with loops allowed, no repeated edges, and every node has positive outdegree d_a. Let I be a subset. All rows of E outside I are fixed (both incoming-to-I and outside-to-outside edges); all rows originating in I may be chosen. Admissibility A says every node of I reaches a node of J=N\I. Fix 0<c<1 and uniform z=1/n. P_ab=1/d_a for edges and zero otherwise. Let e indicate I and v=(Id-cP)^(-1)e. The objective is π(I)=(1-c)/n Σ_a v_a. Suppose E maximizes this objective over all admissible choices, and there is at least one edge from J into I. Claim: some global maximizer of v is the target of such an edge.

Resolvent lemma proved here. For any finite nonnegative row stochastic P, the series R=Σ_{m≥0}c^m P^m converges entrywise (every entry of every P^m lies in [0,1]). Telescoping partial sums gives (Id-cP)R=R(Id-cP)=Id, since every entry of c^(m+1)P^(m+1) tends to zero. R is nonnegative and R≥Id entrywise. Thus v=Re≥e and v=e+cPv. The row vector (1-c)z^T R is normalized, since R1=1/(1-c)1, and satisfies the PageRank stationarity equation. Uniqueness follows by invertibility of Id-cP. This proves the objective identity, not just citing a Markov theorem.

A maximum is inside I. The inlink assumption makes I and J nonempty and n≥2. Since v≥e, its global maximum M≥1. If a maximizer a lay in J, its Bellman equation would give M=cΣ_b P_ab v_b≤cM<M, contradiction. Thus global argmax(v) is nonempty and contained in I.

Assume for contradiction that no global maximizer receives an edge from J. Choose x in global argmax(v). Choose an edge (j,y) with j in J, y in I. Then x≠y, v_x>v_y, and P_ax=0 for every a in J. Let τ exchange x,y and fix all other nodes, including J. Define E' by leaving every row originating in J exactly fixed, and replacing each edge (a,b) with a in I by (τ(a),τ(b)). This is a legal set of edges, not a multigraph operation. The outdegree of a in I becomes d_{τ(a)}, and every outside outdegree is unchanged; hence all outdegrees remain positive. For a in I, P'_{a,b}=P_{τ(a),τ(b)}, while P'_{a,b}=P_{a,b} for a in J.

Admissibility paid. For any a in I, take an old path from τ(a) to J and stop it at its first outside node. All preceding edges originate in I, so applying τ gives an E' path from a to the same first outside node. Thus every node in I still reaches J. Outside rows are unchanged even when they point to x or y; this is not a global graph isomorphism and does not alter the prescribed external inlinks.

Define u_a=v_{τ(a)}. For a in I, e_a+cΣ_b P'_{ab}u_b=u_a, by change of index and the old Bellman equation. For a in J, u_a=v_a, and the residual is
r_a=e_a+cΣ_b P'_{ab}u_b-u_a
   =c(P_ay-P_ax)(v_x-v_y)
   =cP_ay(v_x-v_y)≥0.
For the chosen j this is strictly positive, since P_jy=1/d_j>0. Residuals inside I are zero. Therefore r≥0 and r_j>0. Let v' be the true value vector for E'. Subtracting equations gives
v'-u=(Id-cP')^(-1)r≥r.
Consequently Σ_a v'_a>Σ_a u_a=Σ_a v_a. The equality is exactly permutation invariance of the uniform sum; it would fail for the nonuniform personalization in the source's exception. Multiplication by (1-c)/n>0 contradicts optimality. The existential target follows.
