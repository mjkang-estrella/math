                                                                               Abstract
                                                     Let G = (V, E) be a graph and Γ an Abelian group both of or-
                                                 der n. A Γ-distance magic labeling P of G is a bijection ℓ : V → Γ for
                                                 which there exists µ ∈ Γ such that x∈N (v) ℓ(x) = µ for all v ∈ V ,
                                                 where N (v) is the neighborhood of v. Froncek showed that the Carte-
                                                 sian product Cm Cn , m, n ≥ 3 is a Zmn -distance magic graph if and
                                                 only if mn is even. It is also known that if mn is even then Cm Cn
                                                 has Zα × A-magic labeling for any α ≡ 0 (mod lcm(m, n)) and any
                                                 Abelian group A of order mn/α. However, the full characterization of
                                                 group distance magic Cartesian product of two cycles is still unknown.

                                                     In the paper we make progress towards the complete solution this
                                                 problem by proving some necessary conditions. We further prove that
                                                 for n even the graph Cn Cn has a Γ-distance magic labeling for any
                                                 Abelian group Γ of order n2 . Moreover we show that if m 6= n,
                                                 then there does not exist a (Z2 )m+n -distance magic labeling of the
                                                 Cartesian product C2m C2n . We also give necessary and sufficient
                                                 condition for Cm Cn with gcd(m, n) = 1 to be Γ-distance magic.

1     Introduction
All graphs G = (V, E) are ﬁnite undirected simple graphs. For standard
graph theoretic notation and deﬁnitions we refer to Diestel [4].
    In 1963 Sedláček [8] noticed the following connection between a magic
square M of size n × n and an edge labeling of the complete bipartite graph
Kn,n . Namely, assigning the entry in row i and column j of the magic square
to the edge connecting the i-th vertex in one partite set to the j-th vertex in
the other set, we obtain the sum of labels of edges incident with each vertex
equal to the magic square constant. This type of labeling became known as
magic labeling, supermagic labeling or vertex-magic edge labeling. Another
concept in graph labeling that was motivated by the construction of magic
squares labels vertices instead. A distance magic labeling of a graph G of
order n is a bijection ℓ : V → {1, 2, . . . , n} with the property that there
exists a positive integer µ such that

the weight of the vertex v. The constant µ is called the magic constant of
the labeling f . Any graph admitting a distance magic labeling is called a
distance magic graph.
     We recall one out of four standard graph products (see [6]). Let G and
H be two graphs. The Cartesian product GH is a graph with vertex set
V (G) × V (H). Two vertices (g, h) and (g ′ , h′ ) are adjacent if and only if
either g = g ′ and h is adjacent with h′ in H, or h = h′ and g is adjacent with
g ′ in G.
     Rao at al. proved the following result for Cartesian product of cycles in
[7].

Theorem 1.1 ([7]). The Cartesian product Cm Cn , m, n ≥ 3 is a distance
magic graph if and only if m, n ≡ 2 (mod 4) and m = n.

set S. The identity element of Γ will be denoted by 0. Recall that any group
element ι ∈ Γ of order 2 (i.e., ι 6= 0 and 2ι = 0) is called an involution.
    The magic labeling (in the classical point of view) with labels being the
elements of an Abelian group has been studied for a long time (see papers
by Stanley [9, 10]). Therefore, it was a natural step to label the vertices of a
graph G with elements of an Abelian group also in the case of distance magic
labeling. This concept was introduced by Froncek in [5].

    A Γ-distance magic labeling of a graph G = (V, E) with |V | = n is a
bijectionPℓ from V to an Abelian group Γ of order n such that the weight
w(v) =      u∈N (v) ℓ(u) of every vertex v ∈ V is equal to the same element
µ ∈ Γ, called the magic constant. A graph G is called a group distance magic
graph if there exists a Γ-distance magic labeling for every Abelian group Γ
of order |V (G)|.

   First result on Γ-distance magic labeling of Cartesian product of cycles
was proved by [5]:

Theorem 1.2. ([5]) The Cartesian product Cm Cn , m, n ≥ 3 is Zmn -
distance magic graph if and only if mn is even.

   The result was later improved by Cichacz [2].

Theorem 1.3. ([2]) Let m or n be even and l = lcm(m, n). Then Cm Cn
has a Zα × A-magic labeling for any α ≡ 0 (mod l) and any Abelian group
A of order mn/α.

   The following related results were also proved in the respective papers.

Theorem 1.4. ([5]) The graph C2n C2n has a (Z2 )2n -distance magic labeling
for n ≥ 2 and µ = (0, 0, . . . , 0).

Theorem 1.5. ([2]) If m, n are odd, then Cm Cn is not Γ-distance magic
graph for any Abelian group Γ of order mn.

   The following general problem is still widely open.

Problem 1.6. ([5]) For a given graph Cm Cn , determine all Abelian groups
Γ such that the graph Cm Cn admits a Γ-distance magic labeling.



                                       3
   Note that if a graph G of order n is distance magic, then it is Zn -distance
magic. Moreover there are inﬁnitely many distance magic graphs that at the
same time are group distance magic [1]. Hence Cichacz and Froncek stated
the following conjecture.
Conjecture 1.7 ([3]). If G is a distance magic graph, then G is group dis-
tance magic.
    In the paper we make some progress towards solution of Problem 1.6
by proving some necessary conditions as well as some new existence results.
In particular, we prove that for n even the graph Cn Cn has a Γ-distance
magic labeling for any Abelian group Γ of order n2 . Moreover we show that
if m 6= n, then there does not exist a (Z2 )m+n -distance magic labeling of the
Cartesian product C2m C2n . We prove a necessary and suﬃcient condition
for Cm Cn with gcd(m, n) = 1 to be Γ-distance magic. Observe that the
Cartesian product C2m C2n is Z2m+n -distance magic by Theorem 1.3 but is
not distance magic by Theorem 1.1. Therefore, this result is the ﬁrst example
that shows that assumptions in Conjecture 1.7 cannot be relaxed, that is,
the statement that if a graph G of order n is Zn -distance magic then it is
group distance magic is not true.


2     Sufficient conditions
Recall that the exponent exp (Γ) of a group Γ of order q with elements
a1 , a2 , . . . , aq is the smallest possible r such that rai = 0 for any ai ∈ Γ.
It is well known that in Abelian groups, r = lcm(o1 , o2 , . . . , oq ) where oi is
the order of ai for i = 1, 2, . . . , q.
     It also well known that if Γ has an even order, then there is an element ai
of even order, and hence exp (Γ) = r must be even. Because the non-existence
of Γ-labelings of Cm ✷Cn for |Γ| = mn odd follows from Theorem 1.5, we will
from now on only consider the case where |Γ| = mn is even. Consequently,
we will always have exp (Γ) = r even.

Observation 3.4. For any positive integer m there exists n for which the
Cartesian product Cm Cn is not group distance magic. That is, there exists
an Abelian group Γ such that Cm Cn is not Γ-distance magic.

Proof. Let n = 2m3 and Γ ≡ (Zm )4 ×Z2 . Then exp(Γ) ≤ 2m and gcd(m, n) =
m. Hence we have 2r gcd(m, n) ≤ 4m2 < 2m3 , because m ≥ 3. But
lcm(m, n) = n = 2m3 , and the product Cm Cn is not Γ-distance magic
by Theorem 3.2.
   The following extreme case is worth mentioning.

Observation 3.5. Let gcd(m, n) = 1. There exists a Γ-distance magic la-
beling of the Cartesian product Cm Cn if and only if mn is even and either
Γ∼= Z2 × Zmn/2 or Γ ∼ = Zmn .
Proof. For Γ ∼ = Zmn and Γ ∼= Z2 × Zmn/2 the labelings exist by Theorem 1.7.
    Because gcd(m, n) = 1, we cannot have both m and n even, so without
loss of generality m is odd. If n is odd, then Cm Cn is not Γ-distance magic
graph for any Abelian group Γ of order mn by Theorem 1.5.
    It is well known that for an Abelian group Γ of order 2k, exp(Γ) = k if
and only if k is even and Γ ∼= Z2 × Zk .
    Therefore, if Γ ≇ Zmn and Γ ≇ Z2 ×Zmn/2 , we must have exp(Γ) < mn/2.
On the other hand, we have lcm(m, n) = mn, because gcd(m, n) = 1. Hence
2r gcd(m, n) < mn = lcm(m, n) and there does not exist a Γ-distance magic
labeling of Cm Cn by Theorem 3.2.
    Finally, we complement Theorem 1.4 and Observation 3.6 by the following
related result.

Theorem 3.6. There exists a (Z2 )m+n -distance magic labeling of the Carte-
sian product C2m C2n if and only if 2 ≤ m = n.

Proof. When m = n, then we are done by Theorem 1.4. Suppose that
m 6= n. Without loss of generality we can assume that 2 ≤ m < n; then
min{f (2m ), f (2n ), gcd(2m , 2n )} = 2m−2 , whereas lcm(2m , 2n ) = 2n . Observe
that the exponent of (Z2 )m+n is r = 2. Therefore 2r min{f (m), f (n)} =
2m < 2n = lcm(m, n).




                                       15
4    Conclusion
We made some progress towards the full characterization of Abelian groups Γ
such that |Γ| = mn and there exists a Γ-distance magic labeling of Cm Cn .
    We improved a previous bound for existence of such labeling, showing that
if Γ has a cyclic subgroup of order lcm(m, n)/2, then Cm Cn is Γ-distance
magic, lowering the bound from lcm(m, n).
    On the other hand, we have shown that groups with an exponent exp(Γ)
that is relatively small compared with lcm(m, n) do not admit such labeling.
    Since we found necessary conditions for existence of such labeling (Theo-
rem 3.1 and 3.2), which in some cases were also suﬃcient (Observation 3.5),
we post now the following conjecture.
Conjecture 4.1. Let Γ be an Abelian group of an even order mn with ex-
ponent r. There exists a Γ-distance magic labeling of the Cartesian product
Cm Cn if and only if 2r min{f (m), f (n), gcd(m, n)} ≥ lcm(m, n).
