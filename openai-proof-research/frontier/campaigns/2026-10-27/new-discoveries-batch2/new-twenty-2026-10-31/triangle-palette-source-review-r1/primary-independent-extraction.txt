                                                                                  Abstract
                                                     We consider edge colorings of a graph in such a way that each two dif-
                                                 ferent triangles have distinct colorings. It is an extension of the well-known
                                                 idea of distinguishing all maximal stars in a graph. It was introduced in
                                                 literature in 1985 and studied by many authors in various variants, but al-
                                                 ways for stars. We estimate new invariants regarding triangles for proper
                                                 and general colorings.


                                           1    Introduction
                                           Let G = (V, E) be a ﬁnite graph with a given edge coloring. Consider a maximal
                                           star K1,deg(v) at every vertex v. Two stars S1 and S2 with |V (S1 )| ≥ |V (S2 )| are
                                           distinct if either there exists an edge of S1 with color α and there is no edge of
                                           S2 with color α or there exists a color β such that the number of edges of S1
                                           with β is diﬀerent from the number of edges with β in S2 (the coloring does need
                                           not be proper). An edge coloring is called a coloring distinguishing stars (in the
                                           literature called also vertex distinguishing) if for any two diﬀerent vertices v and
                                           w the stars K1,deg(v) and K1,deg(w) are distinct.
                                               In 1996, Burris and Shelp [3], and independently Černý, Horňák and Soták
                                           [4] posed a question: How many colors are needed to distinguish stars in a proper
                                           coloring of a graph?. It was conjectured by Burris and Schelp and proved in [2],
                                           that if G is a graph of order n, without isolated edges and with at most one
                                           isolated vertex, then n + 1 colors enough to distinguish all maximal stars in G.
                                           This topic was spanned by hundreds of papers and was in the main spectrum of
                                           interest of many mathematicians. It was based on a similar concept introduced
                                           ten years earlier by Harary and Plantholt [5] for general vertex colorings (not
                                           necessary proper) and later by Aigner and Triesch [1].

    This note is the ﬁrst paper where another graph is investigated instead of a
star. More precisely, we will consider triangles K3 . Let f : E 7→ {1, 2, . . . , k} be
an edge coloring of a graph G = (V, E). Then, each triangle T ⊂ G deﬁnes a set
(or a multiset) of colors F (T ) := {f (e) : e ∈ E(T )} called a palette.
    We say that a coloring f distinguishes triangles if for every two diﬀerent
triangles T1 , T2 we have F (T1 ) 6= F (T2 ).
    We will only consider the case G = Kn , n ≥ 3. However, observe that the
obtained results also give estimates of the number of colors needed for any graphs.
Indeed, if a coloring distinguishes triangles in a complete graph, then this property
is preserved when any number of edges is removed.
    In Section 2 we investigate a proper coloring f and determine the smallest
number τ ′ (n) of colors such that the coloring f distinguishes triangles in Kn .
    In Section 3 we investigate a general (not necessary proper) coloring f . Then
the palettes are multisets, and by τm (n) we denote the smallest number k of colors
in an edge coloring f distinguishing triangles in Kn . We estimate τm (n) and we
discuss a modiﬁcation τs (n), when we consider sets of colors of edges instead of
multisets, despite the fact that colorings need not be proper.

3    General coloring
If the coloring is general, then the palettes of three colors may be multisets. Then
color palettes do not have to be rainbow, they can be of the form [ααβ] or [αββ]
or [ααα]. Obviously, by the result for proper colorings, τm (G) ≤ n + 1. Let us
count how many diﬀerent triples we can get with k colors. Simple calculations
(or using Pascal’s triangle property) give

to obtain a coloring that distinguishes all triangles of the complete graph Kn . for
n = k + 2. This is the case, for example, for n = 3, where of course one color is
enough. The next theorem shows that this is the only exception.
Theorem 3. For n ≥ 4, we have τm (n) ≥ n − 1.

Perhaps the following conjecture holds.

Conjecture 4. For n ≥ 4 we have τm (n) = n − 1.


    Another possibility is to distinguish triangles by sets in a general coloring.
There are fewer 3-element sets than multisets, but more than 3-element rainbow
sets. Hence, we have an obvious inequality

                              τm (n) ≤ τs (n) ≤ τ ′ (n).

   The following conjecture would imply Conjecture 4.

Conjecture 5. For n ≥ 4 we have τs (n) = n − 1.
