# 329080: chord diagrams with genus range {1, 2}

Status: candidate full-scope proof of the intended statement (n ≥ 3, n ≠ 4). It is computer-assisted in one finite base lemma, and nothing here is compiled in Lean. Zero credit unless root accepts.

## 1. Source and literal statement

Source: B. Burns, N. Jonoska, M. Saito et al., *Genus Ranges of Chord Diagrams*, arXiv:1410.6148 (only v1 exists, checked 2026-11-03). Published in J. Knot Theory Ramifications 24 (2015); the PMC version PMC4607078 (fetched 2026-11-03, `sources/329080-pmc.html`) keeps the conjecture unchanged.

**Conjecture 3.4 (source, printed verbatim in MathDB 329080).** For any n ≠ 4, there is a unique (up to equivalence) chord diagram with genus range {1, 2}, and it is (123123)(44 ⋯ nn).

Source definitions (Section 2):

- A chord diagram with n chords is given by a double-occurrence word. Two words are equivalent when they are related by cyclic rotation, reversal and renaming.
- A thickened chord diagram thickens the backbone circle to an annulus and attaches each end of each band to either the inner or the outer boundary circle. That gives 4^n orientable surfaces F.
- The genus range is gr(D) = { g(F) }.
- Lemma 3.1 (source): g(F) = (n − b(F) + 2)/2, where b(F) is the number of boundary components.

**Literal defect (zero credit, recorded only).** For n = 1 and n = 2 no diagram has genus range {1, 2}. The source's own table (Section 3.2) gives GR₁ = GR₂ = {{0, 1}}. So the printed "for any n ≠ 4" is false at n = 1, 2 by the source's own data. The intended statement is n ≥ 3, n ≠ 4, and that is what is proved below.

## 2. Combinatorial model of b(F) (dart model)

Number the 2n endpoints 0, …, 2n − 1 in backbone order. Let τ be the fixed-point-free involution pairing the two endpoints of each chord. A side assignment c gives each endpoint i a side c(i) ∈ {in, out}.

Define σ_c(i) as the next endpoint on the same side as i:

- in increasing cyclic order if c(i) = in;
- in decreasing cyclic order if c(i) = out.

The two boundary circles of the annulus carry opposite induced orientations, which is why the directions differ.

Then:

b(F) = #cycles(σ_c ∘ τ) + [no endpoint is in] + [no endpoint is out].

Justification: follow a boundary curve. It runs along the boundary circle from the attachment at i to the next attachment σ_c(i) on that circle, crosses the band to τ(σ_c(i)), and continues. Orientability makes every band untwisted, so the continuation direction is the induced one. A circle with no attachments is a boundary component by itself.

Checks (scripts in `scripts/`):

- (a) The model equals the standard 6n half-edge ribbon-graph face count on all 995,572 (word, side assignment) pairs with n ≤ 5 (`chord_dart.py`, log `logs/chord-dart.log`).
- (b) The resulting sets GR_n for n ≤ 6 coincide with the source's computed table (`chord_full.py`, `outputs/chord-full-verification.json`, `GR_matches_source: true`).
- (c) Sanity values agree with the source: gr(123123) = [1, 2], and gr(12312345674675) = [2, 4] (source Lemma 5.6).

## 3. Lemmas

**Lemma 1 (deleting a chord).** Let D' = D − x and let F' be the thickening of D' with the same side assignment on the remaining endpoints. Then b(F') = b(F) ± 1, and hence g(F') ∈ {g(F), g(F) − 1}.

Proof:

1. Removing the band of x changes χ by +1. The core graph loses one chord and two backbone vertices, and two backbone edges merge into one.
2. Only the boundary curves running along the two long sides of the band are affected. These are one or two curves, and they are reconnected into two or one. So |b(F') − b(F)| ≤ 1.
3. Integrality of the genus forces b(F') ≢ b(F) (mod 2).

So b(F') − b(F) = ±1. Lemma 3.1 then gives g(F') = g(F) or g(F) − 1.

The source states the same band-removal fact inside its proof of Lemma 4.2. It was also checked exhaustively on all 4,948,896 (word, chord, assignment) triples with n ≤ 5 (`outputs/chord-full-verification.json`, `deletion_lemma`).

In the dart model the proof is the classical transposition fact. Inserting the two darts p, q of a chord replaces the transitions τℓ_p → a_p and τℓ_q → a_q of π = σ_c τ by τℓ_p → p → a_q and τℓ_q → q → a_p. This splits one cycle or merges two, with the obvious corrections for empty circles and for consecutive insertions.

**Corollary 2 (heredity).** If D' is a sub-diagram of D (obtained by deleting chords), then:

- min gr(D') ≤ min gr(D): restrict a minimum-genus thickening of D.
- max gr(D') ≤ max gr(D): extend a maximum-genus thickening of D' by re-adding the deleted bands with arbitrary sides. Each re-addition does not lower the genus.

**Lemma 3 (bipartite interlacement ⇒ genus 0).** Let G(D) be the interlacement graph: chords are vertices, and two chords are adjacent when their endpoints alternate. If G(D) is bipartite with parts A and B, attach both ends of every chord of A inside and both ends of every chord of B outside.

The chords of A are pairwise non-crossing, so they cut the inner disc into |A| + 1 regions; likewise B cuts the outer disc into |B| + 1 regions. The surface embeds in the sphere, b = n + 2 and g = 0. So min gr(D) = 0.

Checked: for all diagrams with n ≤ 6, min gr(D) = 0 if and only if G(D) is bipartite (`bipartite_iff_min0_failures = 0`). Only the direction stated in Lemma 3 is used.

**Lemma 4 (five-chord base lemma; finite, computer-verified).** Let D₅ be any chord diagram with 5 chords and max gr(D₅) ≤ 2. Then:

- (a) some chord of D₅ crosses no other chord;
- (b) if D₅ has three pairwise crossing chords a, b, c, then each of the other two chords has cyclically adjacent endpoints, and all four of their endpoints lie in one of the six arcs cut out by the endpoints of a, b, c. That is, D₅ ≅ (123123)(4455).

Verification, by full enumeration that does not rely on heredity:

- Among the 79 equivalence classes of 5-chord diagrams, exactly 12 have max genus ≤ 2, and all 12 satisfy (a) and (b) (`outputs/chord-full-verification.json`, key `F5`).
- Kernel-style certificate over all 945 labelled standard words: 492 satisfy the predicate P = (a) ∧ (b). Each of the other 453 has an explicit side assignment with exactly one boundary component, hence genus (2 + 5 − 1)/2 = 3 (`outputs/chord-F5-certificate.json`, generator `scripts/chord_cert.py`).

**Lemma 5 (R3 is not planar).** The diagram 123123 has no thickening with 5 boundary components, so min gr(123123) ≥ 1. This is a finite check of 2⁶ assignments (certificate key `R3_configs_with_5_faces = 0`). It is also the source's K₃,₃ remark in the proof of Lemma 5.1.

**Lemma 6 (existence: transfer lemma).** Let D_m = 123123 a₁a₁ ⋯ a_ma_m, with the isolated chords appended at the end edge, i.e. between the last endpoint and the base point. For an assignment c define:

- d = b − m;
- s = [the inner side and the outer side of the end edge lie on the same boundary component].

In the dart model, s compares the cycle containing the smallest inner endpoint with the cycle containing the largest outer endpoint; an empty circle counts as its own component.

Appending one more isolated chord at the end edge acts as follows:

- in-in or out-out: (d, s) ↦ (d, s);
- one end in and one end out, in either order: (d, true) ↦ (d, true) and (d, false) ↦ (d − 2, true).

Proof (dart algebra). Let p < q be the new endpoints, placed after all old ones.

*In-in.*
- σ'(ℓ) = p, σ'(p) = q and σ'(q) = a, where ℓ is the largest and a the smallest old inner endpoint.
- Then π'(q) = q is a new fixed-point face, π'(p) = a and π'(τℓ) = p.
- So p joins the old cycle through τℓ → a, b increases by exactly 1, and s is unchanged.

*Out-out* is symmetric.

*In-out.*
- Here τℓ → a and τv → u (v the smallest and u the largest old outer endpoint) become τℓ → p → u and τv → q → a.
- If the two old cycles are distinct they merge: b − 1, so d drops by 2.
- If they coincide the cycle splits: b + 1, so d is unchanged.
- In both cases a and q end up on one cycle, so s becomes true.

The empty-circle cases are the same with the extra component counted. The rules were checked on all 87,296 (assignment, extension) pairs for m ≤ 4 (`logs/chord-transfer.log`, 0 violations).

For D₀ = 123123 the set of attained states is {(1, true), (3, false), (3, true)}, a finite check of 64 assignments. This set is closed under the rules. Hence for every m:

- every assignment has d ∈ {1, 3}, i.e. g = (2 + 3 + m − b)/2 = (5 − d)/2 ∈ {2, 1};
- both values occur (extend an assignment of each type by in-in chords).

So gr(D_m) = {1, 2} for all m ≥ 0. This agrees with source Lemma 5.2(2), which the source proves via its concatenation Lemma 4.3. Here it is re-derived without that lemma.

## 4. Theorem and proof

**Theorem.**

- (i) For n ∈ {1, 2} no chord diagram has genus range {1, 2}.
- (ii) For n = 3 the unique such diagram is 123123.
- (iii) For n = 4 there are exactly four: 11234234 (≅ (123123)(44)), 12134234, 12314234 and 12341234.
- (iv) For every n ≥ 5 the unique such diagram, up to equivalence, is (123123)(44 ⋯ nn).

So Conjecture 3.4 holds for every n ≥ 3, n ≠ 4, and fails only at the degenerate values n = 1, 2.

*Proof.* Parts (i)–(iii) are finite checks: the hereditary and full enumerations for n ≤ 6 (`outputs/chord-full-verification.json`, `outputs/chord-gr12-n14.json`). Existence for n ≥ 3 is Lemma 6.

Uniqueness for n ≥ 5. Let D have n ≥ 5 chords and gr(D) = {1, 2}.

1. **No planar thickening.** min gr(D) = 1, so by Lemma 3 the graph G(D) is not bipartite.
2. **Shortest odd cycle.** Let C be a shortest odd cycle of G(D). Then C is induced: a chord of C would split it into two cycles, one odd and shorter.
3. **C is a triangle.** Suppose |C| ≥ 5. Let S be five consecutive vertices of C. Interlacement of a sub-diagram is the induced subgraph, since crossing depends only on the four endpoints involved. So G(D[S]) is C₅ or P₅, and every chord of D[S] crosses another chord of D[S]. By Corollary 2, max gr(D[S]) ≤ 2, contradicting Lemma 4(a). Hence |C| = 3. The three chords a, b, c pairwise cross, so D[{a, b, c}] ≅ 123123.
4. **Pairs of extra chords.** There are n − 3 ≥ 2 further chords. For any two of them, x and y, the sub-diagram D[{a, b, c, x, y}] has max genus ≤ 2 by Corollary 2 and contains the triangle {a, b, c}. By Lemma 4(b):
   - x and y each have cyclically adjacent endpoints within that sub-diagram;
   - all four endpoints lie in one arc of the six arcs cut out by a, b, c.

   In particular x and y neither cross each other, nor are nested, nor cross a, b or c.
5. **All extras share one arc.** Each extra chord lies in a single arc, and any two lie in the same arc. So all n − 3 extra chords lie in one arc I.
6. **Extras are adjacent pairs.** Let x be an extra chord with its endpoints at positions u < v of I. Any endpoint strictly between u and v would belong to another extra chord y, since no endpoint of a, b or c lies inside I. Then y would cross x or be nested in x, which step 4 forbids. So each extra chord occupies two consecutive positions, and the content of I is a sequence of adjacent pairs.
7. **Conclusion.** The six arcs of 123123 are permuted transitively by rotation: rotating 123123 by one letter and renaming gives 123123 again. Rotating the whole word to put I at the end and renaming gives D ≅ (123123)(44 ⋯ nn). ∎

## 6. Source-ingredient audit (lessons 347138, 371637, 374241)

What the source contains:

- the definitions;
- Lemma 3.1;
- the band-removal ±1 observation (inside its proof of Lemma 4.2);
- "R3 ⊂ D ⇒ 0 ∉ gr(D)" (K₃,₃ remark);
- existence via Lemma 5.2(2).

What the source does not contain:

- any uniqueness argument;
- the bipartite-interlacement criterion (Lemma 3);
- the odd-cycle reduction;
- any classification of small diagrams of maximum genus ≤ 2 (Lemma 4);
- the transfer lemma.

Its Section 6 lists the realizability questions as open, and the conjecture is stated as supported only by computed examples (n ≤ 7).

The proof is short once Lemma 4 is known, but Lemma 4 is a new finite classification (79 classes, 453 genus-3 witnesses). The reduction to it, by heredity plus a shortest-odd-cycle argument, is not printed anywhere I could find.
