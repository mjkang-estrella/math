2.6. The Main Conjecture

   Our Main Conjecture is a stronger and more direct geometric connection between the
ﬁrst and last terms in (2.6), eliminating the crystallographic hypothesis. Here we consider
the subvariety/zero locus V Θ cut out in V by the ideal (Θ − x). The notation V Θ is
meant to be suggestive of the fact that this subvariety is the subset of V ﬁxed pointwise
under the map Θ : V → V that sends the element of V having coordinates (x1 , . . . , xn )
to the element of V having coordinates (θ1 , . . . , θn ). Note that Proposition 2.11 implies
C[V ]/(Θ − x) is a C-vector space of dimension (h + 1)n , since C[V ]/(Θ) is. In particular,
this subvariety V Θ ⊆ V can contain at most (h + 1)n points, and contains exactly this
many points when counted with multiplicity. Note that the W × C-action on V , in which
an element cd in C scales V by the root-of-unity ω d , restricts to one on V Θ .

  We close this section with two remarks on the formulas in Proposition 9.2.

Remark 9.3. One can readily check that the formula for Kirk(W, k; q) when W is of
type An−1 given above is, up to a power of q, the same as the q-hook-formula f λ (q) or
fake degree polynomial for λ = (n − k, n − k, 1k ) which q-counts standard Young tableaux
of shape λ by their major index; see [51, Corollary 7.21.5].
   At q = 1, this coincidence between type A Kirkman numbers and tableaux numbers is
an observation of K. O’Hara and A.V. Zelevinsky; see Stanley [48] for a bijective proof.
We do not have a good algebraic explanation for the coincidence when the variable q is
unspecialized, even though both polynomials in q have Hilbert series interpretations.

Remark 9.4. Other versions of q-Kirkman numbers Kirk(W, k; q) have been considered
recently in the literature in types A, B/C, D and I2 (m), but deﬁned in ad hoc ways.
They have appeared in conjunction with cyclic sieving phenomena (CSP’s) involving the
set X of clusters for W of a ﬁxed cardinality – recall from Corollary 3.3 that Kirk(W, k)
counts the clusters of cardinality n − k. This set X carries a natural action of a cyclic
group C = τ of order h + 2 introduced by Fomin and Zelevinsky [24], generated by
their deformed Coxeter element τ .
   We compare here this type-by-type with the other version of q-Kirkman numbers
with a renormalized version of our Kirk(W, k; q) deﬁned in (9.2), where one divides by
the smallest power of q to make the constant term 1; call this renormalized polynomial
Kirk0 (W, k; q).
