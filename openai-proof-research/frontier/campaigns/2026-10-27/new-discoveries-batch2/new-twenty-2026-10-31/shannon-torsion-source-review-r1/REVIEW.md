## Ordinary obligation audit and complete details

O1. Product and all simplices: PASS for stated domain. Each fiber F_i is K_m; neighboring fibers are completely joined; other fibers have no edges. A clique projects to one vertex or one edge of C_n, because C_n has no triangles. Thus every simplex lies in F_i or F_i union F_(i+1); the latter includes dimension 2m-1. This remains true at n=4, where the two neighbors of i are distinct and nonadjacent.

O2. Free-face deletion: PASS with the following explicit justification. Fix twins v,w in a fiber. Order all faces sigma containing v but not w by decreasing cardinality and pair sigma with tau=sigma union {w}. Twin adjacency guarantees tau exists. A proper coface gamma of sigma without w is paired and removed earlier, since it is larger. A coface gamma with w other than tau has gamma\{w} properly containing sigma, and its pair was also removed earlier. Consequently tau is the unique surviving proper coface when the pair is removed; sigma is codimension one in tau. No equal-size tie can obstruct this argument. Pairs exhaust exactly the faces containing v; what remains is the induced complex deleting v. Iterating m-1 times in each fiber leaves exactly the unfilled n-cycle. This pays every high-dimensional face.

O3. Integral transport and primitive generator: PASS. An elementary pair has incidence +/-1, so eliminating it is an integral chain contraction: solve the paired boundary with that unit pivot and subtract its multiples from the other boundaries. Equivalently, send v to w and fix the other vertices. This is a simplicial retraction r; each face union its image is a clique. The usual oriented prism sum over the vertex list, with repeated-vertex terms zero, has integer coefficients and telescoping boundary dP+Pd=id-ir. Thus both chains and their dual cochains are integral chain-homotopy equivalent; no rational-only Betti argument is used. Composing leaves p:X->C_n and a section i with pi=id. The cycle c=sum_i [a_i,a_(i+1)] maps to the cycle of C_n. On the cycle, the boundary equations say every integral edge coefficient of a cycle is equal, hence H1=Z generated by that loop; H0=Z by vertex augmentation, no higher groups. Retraction isomorphisms make c primitive, not merely nonzero. All integral homology and cohomology groups are free. In cohomology the seam cochain beta (value 1 on every edge from F_(n-1) to F_0, 0 on all other crossing/internal edges, antisymmetric on reversals) is p* of a primitive integral cohomology generator.

O4. Lattice identity: PASS; specify that harmonic homology lattice means orthogonal projection of Z_j/B_j, not intersection of the integer chain lattice with the harmonic space. The latter would generally be the wrong lattice. Kernel Z_j is primitive in C_j(Z), because C_j/Z_j is isomorphic to the free image B_(j-1). Extend its basis integrally. The complementary basis projected to Z_j-perp has covolume 1/z_j, since a unimodular full basis has volume 1. Boundary multiplies that volume by s_j, the product of nonzero singular values, giving b_(j-1)=s_j/z_j. Since H_j is free, B_j is primitive in Z_j. Extend its basis by homology lifts and factor the orthogonal block volume to get z_j=b_j h_j. Neither statement assumes B_j primitive in all of C_j. Rank-zero factors are 1. In degree zero d_0=0 (chain convention), z_0=1, and b_0 h_0=1. At the top B_d=0, b_d=1, with s_(d+1)=1, and no residual top factor.

O5. All-degree determinant/sign telescope: PASS. In degree k the real orthogonal decomposition is B_k direct-sum harmonic_k direct-sum Z_k-perp. Boundary squared contributes nonzero spectrum s_k² on the coexact part; incoming boundary contributes s_(k+1)² on the exact part. Hence pdet L_k=s_k² s_(k+1)². The exponent of s_j² in A is (j-1)(-1)^j+j(-1)^(j+1)=(-1)^(j+1). Substitution s_j=b_(j-1)b_j h_j cancels internal b_j; leftover b_0² becomes h_0^-2. Therefore A=product_j h_j^[2(-1)^(j+1)]. The calculation also applies in zero ranks/end degrees and uses no continuum comparison or homotopy invariance of A.

O6. Triangle cocycle: PASS. Orient crossing edges forward cyclically and alpha=1/n there; alpha is 0 on internal edges and negates under reversal. Every triangle lies in one or two adjacent fibers. On those fibers choose a local potential t equal to 0 on the first and 1/n on the next, also across the seam using its cyclic order. Alpha restricted to that triangle is dt. Thus alpha([y,z])-alpha([x,z])+alpha([x,y])=0 for every ordered triangle; an internal triangle is identically zero. Higher cochain degrees require no additional test for d_1 alpha=0; their effects are already in O2–O5. No global potential is being claimed.

O7. Divergence, integral cohomology and primitive period: PASS. At a vertex there are exactly m incoming and m outgoing crossing edges, canceling in d_0* alpha; internal edges contribute zero. At n=4 these are disjoint neighbor fibers. Hence alpha lies in ker L1. Its pairing with c is n/n=1. More explicitly set f(i,a)=i/n for i=0,...,n-1. Then alpha-beta=df on every edge, including the seam where df=-(n-1)/n. Thus alpha is the harmonic representative of the actual integral primitive class beta, although its coefficients are not integral. This removes any possible integral-normalization gap.

O8. Norms and H0: PASS. There are n m² unoriented crossing edges, each used once in an orthonormal oriented-edge basis. Alpha norm²=n m²/n²=m²/n.
