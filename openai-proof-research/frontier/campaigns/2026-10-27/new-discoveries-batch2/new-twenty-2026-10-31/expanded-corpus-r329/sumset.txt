Abstract For a prime p ≡ 2 ( mod 3 ) , it is well known that the largest sum-free subsets of 𝔽 p n have size p + 1 3 ​ p n − 1 , and the extremal sets must be a cuboid of the form { p + 1 3 , p + 1 3 + 1 , … , 2 ​ p − 1 3 } × 𝔽 p n − 1 up to isomorphism. Recently, Reiner and Zotova proved a Hilton-Milner type stability result showing that for large p , any sum-free set not contained in the extremal cuboid has size at most p − 2 3 ​ p n − 1 , and all possible structures attaining this bound were classified. In this paper, we develop a general Hilton-Milner theory for ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free sets in 𝔽 p n for k > ℓ ≥ 1 . We determine the maximum size of such sets for all p ≡ μ ( mod k + ℓ ) with 2 ≤ μ ≤ k + ℓ − 1 , and show that the extremal configurations are precisely ⌈ μ − 1 2 ⌉ non-isomorphic cuboids. Beyond the extremal regime, we prove sharp Hilton–Milner type stability results showing that, for all sufficiently large p , a ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free set not contained in any of these extremal cuboids is uniformly bounded away from the maximum by a gap p n − 1 , and we determine the full structure of all sets achieving this second-best bound in several broad parameter ranges. In particular, when 2 ≤ μ ≤ k + ℓ − 3 (which is tight), only two structural types occur for all k + ℓ ≥ 5 ; and when μ = 2 or 3 , we obtain a complete classification for all k > ℓ ≥ 1 . Our arguments combine additive combinatorics and Fourier-analytic methods, and make use of recent progress toward the long-standing 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture, highlighting new connections between inverse additive number theory and extremal problems over finite vector spaces. Index Terms: ( 𝒌 , ℓ ) -sum-free sets, Hilton-Milner type theorem, arithmetic progressions, additive combinatorics. I Introduction Let G be a finite abelian group. A subset A of G is called sum-free if the equation x + y = z has no solutions with x , y , z ∈ A . Equivalently, if we take the standard notation A + B := { x + y : x ∈ A , y ∈ B } for subsets A and B in G , then A is sum-free if and only if A ∩ ( A + A ) = ∅ . How large can a sum-free subset be in a finite abelian group? Although the first work dates back to 1969 by Yap [ 1 ] , the question was only completely resolved by Green and Ruzsa in 2005 for all finite abelian groups [ 2 ] . For sum-free sets over integers, research goes back to the pioneering work of Schur [ 3 ] when attacking the Fermat’s last theorem, and it remains at the forefront of current research (see [ 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 ] ). When G is a vector space over finite fields, sum-free sets have connections to projective geometry and coding theory. Let G = 𝔽 p n with n ≥ 1 and prime p ≡ 2 ( mod 3 ) , Yap [ 1 ] described the unique structure of a maximum sum-free subset up to additive group isomorphisms as below. Here, two subsets of 𝔽 p n are isomorphic to each other if there is an (additive) isomorphism of 𝔽 p n sending one to the other, i.e. considering 𝔽 p n as an additive group. Theorem I.1 ( [ 1 ] ) . Let p = 3 ​ m + 2 be a prime number with m ≥ 1 . If A ⊂ 𝔽 p n is a sum-free set, then | A | ≤ ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 . Moreover, if | A | = ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 , A is isomorphic to the following unique form, A 2 , 1 ≜ [ m + 1 , 2 ​ m + 1 ] × 𝔽 p n − 1 . Here, the notation [ a , b ] with a , b ∈ 𝔽 p means the set { a , a + 1 , … , b } in 𝔽 p . The subset [ a , b ] ⊂ 𝔽 p is called an interval in 𝔽 p . A subset of 𝔽 p n formed by an interval multiplied by a subspace of dimension n − 1 is called a cuboid . Theorem I.1 shows that, the structure of a sum-free set of maximum size must be a cuboid with the unique parameters. Once the extremal structure is known, it is natural to ask the corresponding stability problem: what is the structure of sum-free sets in 𝔽 p n of size close to the maximum size? Recently Reiher and Zotova [ 8 ] proved a Hilton-Milner type result of Theorem I.1 , which is a strong stability result. They showed that if a sum-free set is nontrivial , i.e., not contained in the extremal cuboid, then there is a large gap between its size and the maximum size, which means a sum-free set of size close to the maximum size must be contained in the extremal cuboid. They also characterized the possible structures for the nontrivial optimal sum-free sets. Theorem I.2 ( [ 8 ] ) . Let p = 3 ​ m + 2 be a prime number with m ≥ 1 . If a sum-free set A ⊂ 𝔽 p n is not isomorphic to a subset of A 2 , 1 , then | A | ≤ m ​ p n − 1 . Moreover, if | A | = m ​ p n − 1 , the structures of A are determined up to isomorphism. More specifically, there exists an integer s ∈ [ n − 1 ] , and a subset P ⊂ 𝔽 p s satisfying 0 ∉ P + P ( P can be empty), such that A = A 0 × 𝔽 p n − 1 − s , where A 0 ⊂ 𝔽 p × 𝔽 p s is defined by A 0 = { ( m , 0 ) } ⊔ ( { m + 1 } × ( 𝔽 p s \ P ) ) ⊔ ( [ m + 2 , 2 ​ m − 1 ] × 𝔽 p s ) ⊔ ( { 2 ​ m } × ( 𝔽 p s \ { 0 } ) ) ⊔ ( { 2 ​ m + 1 } × P ) . Here the symbol “ ⊔ ” means disjoint unions. The classical Hilton-Milner theorem [ 9 ] is a strong stability result for the famous Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem [ 10 ] . There has been a surge in Hilton-Milner type stability results for various extremal configurations, including cross-intersecting families [ 11 ] , intersecting chains [ 12 ] , and generalized Turán problems [ 13 ] . In this paper, we generalize Theorem I.1 and Theorem I.2 from sum-free sets to ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free sets. For any positive integer h ≥ 2 , by denoting 2 ​ A := A + A , the set of all h -term sums is defined by h ​ A := ( h − 1 ) ​ A + A . This notation should be distinguished from h ⋅ A , which means { h ​ a : a ∈ A } in our paper. Given positive integers k > ℓ ≥ 1 , we say a nonempty set A is ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free if k ​ A ∩ ℓ ​ A = ∅ . Equivalently, this is to say, the equation x 1 + x 2 + ⋯ + x k = x 1 ′ + ⋯ + x ℓ ′ has no solution with all unknowns x 1 , … , x k , x 1 ′ , … , x ℓ ′ in A . The maximum size of ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free sets over abelian groups or positive integers has also been widely studied, see for example [ 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 ] . Further, many cornerstone results in additive combinatorics study structures or relationships of h -term sums for varying h . For example, Freiman-Ruzsa theorem and the recently solved polynomial Freiman-Ruzsa conjecture [ 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 ] describe the structure of sets with small h -term sum sets; Roth’s theorem determines the maximum density of subsets of integers avoiding nontrivial 3 -term arithmetic progressions [ 22 , 23 , 24 ] ; and the sum-product phenomenon concerns the non-coexistence of small sum sets and small product sets within certain rings [ 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 ] . I-A Main results In the following, we always assume that k > ℓ ≥ 1 and n , m ≥ 1 unless otherwise stated. Analogous to Theorem I.1 , we first establish the following result. Note that the upper bound in Theorem I.3 can also be deduced from [ 14 , Theorem 5] . Theorem I.3 . Let p = ( k + ℓ ) ​ m + 2 + λ be a prime number with λ ∈ [ 0 , k + ℓ − 3 ] . If A ⊂ 𝔽 p n is a ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subset, then | A | ≤ ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 . Moreover, there are in total ⌈ λ + 1 2 ⌉ mutually non-isomorphic structures of A with | A | = ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 . More specifically, for any j ∈ [ 0 , ⌈ λ + 1 2 ⌉ − 1 ] ⊂ 𝔽 p , denote a j := − ( k ​ m + 1 + j ) ​ ( k − ℓ ) − 1 . Then the j -th structure of A is a cuboid A k , ℓ , p , j ≜ [ a j , a j + m ] × 𝔽 p n − 1 . For convenience, we name them extremal cuboids . Especially, if λ = 0 , 1 , the extremal cuboid is unique up to isomorphism. If A is isomorphic to a subset of an extremal cuboid, A is said to be trivial ; otherwise, A is nontrivial . Next, we state our main results Theorems I.4 - I.6 , which are the corresponding Hilton-Milner type results of Theorem I.3 . All of them state that when p is large enough (depending on k and ℓ ), if A is a nontrivial ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subset, then | A | ≤ m ​ p n − 1 , and if the equality holds, the structures of A can be fully characterized. To lower bound p , we need a function T over the rational field whose definition is more involved. For a smoother reading experience, at this moment, we only mention that T is related to the Freiman’s ( 3 ​ k − 4 ) conjecture, for which the formal definition will be introduced in Subsection I-B . I-B Function T and 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture Now we define the function T in the lower bounds of m in Theorems I.4 - I.6 . Before that, we need to introduce some results about the 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture, which studies under what condition an additive set can be covered by a short arithmetic progression. If an additive set A can be covered by an arithmetic progression of length | 2 ​ A | − | A | + 1 , we say A has the covering property . In the integer ring Freiman proved the following 3 ​ k − 4 theorem, see [ 30 ] and [ 31 , Theorem 7.1] . Theorem I.7 (Freiman 3 ​ k − 4 Theorem) . Let A be a finite set of integers satisfying | 2 ​ A | ≤ 3 ​ | A | − 4 . Then A has the covering property. However, the analog of Theorem I.7 over 𝔽 p seems much harder, which leads to the following famous 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture, see for example [ 32 ] . For behaviors of arithmetic progressions in 𝔽 p , see Section II-A for more explanations. Conjecture I.1 ( 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture) . If p is a large prime number and A ⊂ 𝔽 p satisfies | A + A | ≤ min ⁡ { 3 ​ | A | − 4 , p − 1 } , then A has the covering property. Although many efforts have been taken during the decades [ 33 , 34 , 32 , 35 , 36 ] , the 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture is still open. Significant progress does emerge, though, when considering a “sharper” A . There are mainly two ways to explain the sharpness. On the one hand, one can require a smaller extension ratio of A , which means | A + A | / | A | is smaller; on the other hand, one can require A to be a smaller portion in 𝔽 p , which means the density | A | / p is limited. The original 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture says that the covering property holds for any density if the extension ratio is roughly 3 . Known results reveal a trade-off between the extension ratio and the density. See the followings. Theorem I.8 ( [ 37 ] , Theorem 1.4) . Let A , B ⊂ 𝔽 p be nonempty subsets satisfying A + B ≠ 𝔽 p . Set C = − 𝔽 p \ ( A + B ) . If we have | A + B | = | A | + | B | + r for some r satisfying | A + B | ≤ min ⁡ { | A | + 1.0527 ​ | B | − 3 , p − 9 ​ ( r + 3 ) } , there are arithmetic progressions P A , P B , P C ⊂ 𝔽 p of the same common difference such that for all X ∈ { A , B , C } , X ⊂ P X and | P X | ≤ | X | + r + 1 . Setting B = A in Theorem I.8 , we know that the covering property holds for any density if the extension ratio | A + A | / | A | is roughly at most 2.0527. Theorem I.9 ( [ 32 ] ) . Let A ⊂ 𝔽 p with | A + A | ≤ 2.4 ​ | A | − 3 . If further | A | ≤ p / 10.7 , then A has the covering property. By Theorem I.9 , if we reduce the density | A | / p to 1 / 10.7 , we only need the extension ratio | A + A | / | A | to be at most 2.4. Based on this observation, we introduce the function T as follows. Definition I.5 . For a constant c ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) , we say that τ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] is feasible for c if any A ⊆ 𝔽 p with | A | ≤ c ​ p has the covering property provided that | A + A | ≤ ( 2 + τ ) ​ | A | − 3 . Define the supremum of feasible τ for c as τ ⁡ ( c ) , and denote T ⁡ ( c ) = 1 τ ⁡ ( c ) . By Definition I.5 , for any 0 ≤ c ≤ c ′ ≤ 1 , we must have τ ⁡ ( c ) ≥ τ ⁡ ( c ′ ) . From Theorem I.8 we know that for any 0 ≤ c < 1 , τ ⁡ ( c ) ≥ 0.0527 and hence T ⁡ ( c ) < 20 . From Theorem I.9 we know that T ⁡ ( 1 10.7 ) ≤ 2.5 . When c goes down, T ⁡ ( c ) does not increase. So in Theorems I.4 - I.6 , we can replace all the values of T by 20 if we are not committed to finding the best possible lower bound of m . I-C Main idea and organization Our idea of proving Theorems I.4 - I.6 is motivated from [ 8 ] which uses a projection method. Given a pair ( v , K ) such that K ⊂ 𝔽 p n is a subspace of dimension n − 1 and v ∈ 𝔽 p n \ K , we can naturally write any element in 𝔽 p n as x = i ​ v + x ′ with i ∈ 𝔽 p and x ′ ∈ K . For any set A ⊂ 𝔽 p n , writing each element in this form, we can partition A into p parts based on different i ∈ 𝔽 p , and project each part into a subset of K . First, we show that if A is ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free and a little “unbalanced” based on ( v , K ) , that is, most parts of A in this partition are empty except for a few parts, then by some additive combinatorial analysis, A is forced to be extremely unbalanced, which means almost all parts of A are either very close to K or empty. Then considering the set of those few indices i with nonempty parts (i.e. support of A ), the problem is reduced to consider structures of ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subsets of the 1 -dimensional space 𝔽 p . By Fourier analysis we show that, if A is ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free, the spectral radius of A is big, leading to that for at least one choice of ( v , K ) , the corresponding distribution of A is unbalanced. The main contribution of this paper is mainly two-folded. First, we give a combining method of additive combinatorial analysis and Fourier analysis for the structures of large ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free sets in 𝔽 p n , which yield Hilton-Milner type results for a wide range of parameters. We believe that this method can be extended to incorporate more parameters that have not yet been considered. Second, we find a new application of the 3 ​ k − 4 conjecture in bounding the field size in our main results. Further improvements on this conjecture can give better lower bound of p applicable to our results. The article is organized as follows. In Section II we give some notations and preliminary results frequently used in this paper. The proofs of Theorem I.3 and the non-overlapping among the five types are also included. Section III is devoted to analyze the structures of the support of a large ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subset by using additive combinatorial tools. Theorem I.4 is proved in Section IV , while the cases when A is of small weight and large weight are treated differently, and the latter one applies Fourier analysis. By following the same proving process but with extra criterion and techniques, Theorems I.5 and I.6 are proved in Section V . Section VI concludes our paper and presents several open problems. II Preliminaries We begin with some basic tools widely used in additive combinatorics. Given a finite nonempty subset A of an abelian group G , we write S ​ y ​ m ​ ( A ) ≜ { g ∈ G : { g } + A = A } for the symmetric group of A . Then A is a union of some cosets of S ​ y ​ m ​ ( A ) and thus A = A + S ​ y ​ m ​ ( A ) . We call two sets of G isomorphic if there is an automorphism of G sending one to the other. Theorem II.1 (Kneser [ 38 , 39 ] ) . If A 1 , … , A k are finite nonempty subsets of an abelian group G , and H = S ​ y ​ m ​ ( A 1 + ⋯ + A k ) , then | A 1 + ⋯ + A k | ≥ | A 1 + H | + ⋯ + | A k + H | − ( k − 1 ) ​ | H | . Consequently, | A 1 | + ⋯ + | A k | ≤ | A 1 + ⋯ + A k | + ( k − 1 ) ​ | H | . When G = 𝔽 p and k = 2 , instead we have Theorem II.2 (Cauchy-Davenport [ 40 , 41 ] ) . If p is a prime number and A , B ⊂ 𝔽 p are nonempty, then | A + B | ≥ min ⁡ { p , | A | + | B | − 1 } . The extremal case of Theorem II.2 was completely determined by [ 42 ] . The following characterization is the main case when p is big. Theorem II.3 (Vosper [ 42 ] ) . Let p be a prime and A , B ⊂ 𝔽 p satisfying | A | , | B | ≥ 2 and | A + B | ≤ p − 2 . Then | A + B | = | A | + | B | − 1 if and only if A and B are representable as arithmetic progressions (APs) in 𝔽 p with the same common difference. II-A Arithmetic progressions in 𝔽 p In Theorem II.3 , we use “representable” to mean that we can arrange all elements of the set such that any two consecutive elements have a common difference. For APs over ℤ , the common difference is unique up to sign , meaning that if A is an AP with common difference d , then the only other possible common difference is − d by reversing the order of elements in A . However, APs in 𝔽 p can wrap around. If A ⊂ 𝔽 p is representable by an AP, we may wonder whether the common difference is unique. It seems that there is no conclusion about this question in the literature. A counterexample is that A = 𝔽 p \ { x } with any fixed point x can be representable by an AP of length p − 1 with any common difference in 𝔽 p \ { 0 } . We will show that besides this example, the common difference of an AP in 𝔽 p is unique up to sign, see Lemma II.1 . Before proving Lemma II.1 , we introduce the following useful notations. For integers a ≤ b , we use the same notation [ a , b ] to denote the set { a , a + 1 , … , b } and write [ b ] = [ 1 , b ] for short. Let A be a subset of 𝔽 p with | A | ∈ [ 2 , p − 2 ] . For any d ∈ [ ( p − 1 ) / 2 ] , define E d ​ ( A ) = { ( x , y ) ∈ A × ( 𝔽 p ∖ A ) : | x − y | = d } and e d ​ ( A ) := | E d ​ ( A ) | , that is, the number of pairs of difference d with exactly one element in A . Given A , let ℰ ⁡ ( A ) denote the multiset by collecting all e d ​ ( A ) s for d ∈ [ ( p − 1 ) / 2 ] . We have the following two facts: • A is an AP of common difference d if and only if e d ​ ( A ) = 2 ; • If A , A ′ ⊂ 𝔽 p are isomorphic, then ℰ ⁡ ( A ) = ℰ ⁡ ( A ′ ) . Lemma II.1 . VI Conclusion We studied the Hilton-Milner type problem of ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subsets in 𝔽 p n for general k > ℓ ≥ 1 . Under certain parameter conditions, we completely classified all such sets with the largest size but not contained in any extremal cuboid A k , ℓ , p , j , i.e., optimal nontrivial structures. More specifically, for large p , we solved the problem when p ≡ 2 + λ ( mod k + ℓ ) for any λ ∈ [ 0 , k + ℓ − 5 ] , and for λ ∈ { 0 , 1 } with any k > ℓ ≥ 1 . The former one was classified into only two types of structures, and special cases in the latter one gives more: when ( k + ℓ , λ ) = ( 5 , 1 ) we have four types, and when ( k + ℓ , λ ) = ( 4 , 1 ) we have three types of structures. All our results show that when p = ( k + ℓ ) ​ m + 2 + λ , if A cannot be covered by any extremal structure A k , ℓ , p , j which is of size ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 , i.e., A is a nontrivial ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subset, then | A | ≤ m ​ p n − 1 . In other words, there is a gap of p n − 1 between the sizes of the “best structure” and the “best nontrivial structure”. However, this phenomenon does not always happen for all ( k , ℓ , λ ) and large enough prime p . For example, when ( k , ℓ ) = ( 2 , 1 ) and p ≡ 1 mod 3 (i.e., λ = k + ℓ − 1 = 2 which does not fall in our assumption), the largest sum-free set in 𝔽 p n has size ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 , but there are nontrivial examples of size ( m + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 − 1 [ 46 ] . Note that when λ = k + ℓ − 2 , p = ( k + ℓ ) ​ m + 2 + λ = ( m + 1 ) ​ ( k + ℓ ) , which is not a prime. So the first question we raise is the following. Problem VI.1 . For any k > ℓ ≥ 1 and λ = k + ℓ − 1 , if p ≡ λ + 2 ≡ 1 ( mod k + ℓ ) is a large prime, determine the largest size M k , ℓ , λ of a ( k , ℓ ) -sum-free subset in 𝔽 p n for any n ≥ 1 and characterize all extremal structures. Theorem I.3 solved all the remainant cases of Problem VI.1 when λ ∈ [ 0 , k + ℓ − 3 ] .
