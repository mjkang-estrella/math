After the discovery of Khovanov homology, which categorifies the Jones polynomial, an analogous categorification of the chromatic polynomial, known as chromatic homology, was introduced. Its graded Euler characteristic recovers the chromatic polynomial. In this paper, we present a spanning tree model for the chromatic complex, i.e., we describe a chain complex generated by certain spanning trees of the graph that is chain homotopy equivalent to the chromatic complex. We employ the spanning tree model over 𝒜 m := ℤ ⁡ [ x ] / ⟨ x m ⟩ algebra to answer two open questions. First, we establish the conjecture posed by Sazdanovic and Scofield regarding the homological span of chromatic homology over the 𝒜 m algebra, demonstrating that for any graph G with v vertices and b blocks, the homological span is v − b . Additionally, we prove a conjecture of Helme-Guizon, Przytycki, and Rong concerning the existence of torsion of order dividing m in chromatic homology over the 𝒜 m algebra. 1. Introduction It is a fundamental problem in graph theory to determine the number of ways a graph can be colored using λ colors. The chromatic polynomial P ⁡ ( G , λ ) counts the number of such colorings when evaluated at λ . It was first defined in 1912 by George David Birkhoff in an attempt to solve the four-color theorem for planar graphs, indeed proving that P ⁡ ( G , 4 ) > 0 for all planar graphs would have implied the result. Let G be a connected simple graph with N vertices, and fix an ordering on its edges. A spanning tree T of G is called an NBC spanning tree if, for every edge e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) ∖ E ⁡ ( T ) , e is not the smallest ordered edge in the unique cycle of the subgraph T ∪ { e } of G . There are several expansions of the chromatic polynomial in terms of different graph-theoretic objects. One such expansion expresses the chromatic polynomial in terms of NBC spanning trees. This formulation enables a purely algebraic computation of the chromatic polynomial. Helme-Guizon and Rong [ HGR05a ] introduced a Khovanov-type bi-graded homology theory which categorifies the chromatic polynomial. Let 𝒜 m denote the algebra defined by 𝒜 m := ℤ ⁡ [ x ] / ⟨ x m ⟩ . Throughout the paper, the chromatic complex over the algebra 𝒜 m will be denoted by C ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) for a given graph G . The chromatic complex of G is generated by the enhanced spanning subgraphs of G . It should be noted that, although the original definition of chromatic homology was given for the algebra 𝒜 2 , it can be generalized to any graded commutative algebra. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, it will always be assumed that we are working over 𝒜 2 . However, in Section 4, we will explore chromatic homology over general 𝒜 m . Spanning-tree models were introduced independently by Champanerkar-Kofman [ CK09 ] and by Wehrli [ Weh08 ] in the context of Khovanov homology, by constructing chain complexes generated by spanning trees of the Tait (checkerboard) graph associated to a knot diagram [ CK09 , Weh08 ] . Subsequently, related spanning-tree descriptions appeared in δ -graded knot Floer homology [ BL12 ] and in Heegaard Floer theory for the branched double cover of a link [ Gre13 ] . We introduce a spanning-tree model for chromatic homology in which the differential can be computed explicitly from combinatorial data. We define a bi-graded chain complex CST ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ⁡ ( G ) generated by signed NBC spanning trees whose differential can be easily combinatorially computed from the graph. We call it the spanning tree complex . Its homology will be denoted by HST ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ⁡ ( G ) . Our main theorem states that the spanning tree complex is chain homotopy equivalent to the chromatic complex C ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) . {theorem} [Spanning tree model for chromatic homology] The spanning tree complex CST ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ⁡ ( G ) and the chromatic complex C ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) are chain homotopy equivalent and therefore, HST i , j 𝒜 m ⁡ ( G ) ≅ H i , j 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) . The graded Euler characteristic χ q ​ ( CST ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 2 ⁡ ( G ) ) is the alternating sum of the graded ranks of its cohomology groups; in other words, χ q ​ ( CST ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 2 ⁡ ( G ) ) = ∑ i , j ( − 1 ) i ⋅ q j ​ dim ⁡ ( HST i , j 𝒜 2 ⁡ ( G ) ) . As a corollary of \Autoref main, we obtain the spanning tree expansion of the chromatic polynomial. {corollary} Let G be a graph with N vertices. The graded Euler characteristic of CST ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 2 ⁡ ( G ) coincides with the chromatic polynomial of the graph G when evaluated at λ = 1 + q . Hence, we obtain P ⁡ ( G , λ ) = ( − 1 ) N − 1 ​ λ ​ ∑ i = 1 N − 1 t i ​ ( 1 − λ ) i where t i is the number of NBC spanning trees of G having internal activity i (with respect to any fixed ordering of E ⁡ ( G ) ). \Autoref main can be regarded as a categorification of the spanning tree expansion formula of the chromatic polynomial. Our theorem builds on the work of Chandler and Sazdanovic [ CS22 ] . Using methods of algebraic Morse theory, they proved that the chromatic complex (over any commutative graded algebra) deformation retracts to a complex generated by enhanced NBC subgraphs. This can be viewed as a categorification of Whitney’s Broken Circuit Theorem. In a similar vein, using methods from algebraic Morse theory, we prove that the chromatic complex is chain homotopy equivalent to a chain complex generated by the signed NBC spanning trees of the graph. This results in a significant reduction in the number of generators. To emphasize this point, we provide some computations of chromatic homology using the spanning tree model without computer assistance. Moreover, the spanning tree model perspective enables us to explore the chromatic complex using graph-theoretic properties. The spanning tree model can be extended over the 𝒜 m algebra. We prove the following conjecture on the homological span of chromatic homology over the 𝒜 m algebra posed by Sazdanovic and Scofield [ SS18 ] . {theorem} [Conjecture 43 [ SS18 ] ] The homological span of chromatic homology over algebra 𝒜 m of any graph G with v vertices and b blocks is equal to h ​ s ​ p ​ a ​ n ​ ( H ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) ) = v − b . We also prove the following conjecture posed by Helme-Guizon, Przytycki, and Rong on the existence of torsion of order dividing m in chromatic homology over the 𝒜 m algebra. {theorem} [Conjecture 25 [ HGPR06 ] ] The cohomology H ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) of a graph G contains a torsion part if and only if G has no loops and contains a cycle of order greater than or equal to 3 . In this case, H ∗ , ∗ 𝒜 m ​ ( G ) has a torsion of order dividing m . The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some background material. A short review of graph co-homology In this subsection, we recall the construction of the chromatic homology associated with a graph. There are multiple formulations of chromatic homology in the literature. We define chromatic homology following Viro’s interpretation of Khovanov homology via enhanced states, adapted to the setting of chromatic homology [ Vir04 , Section 4.3] . This enhanced-state viewpoint was already provided in the original categorification of the chromatic polynomial [ HGR05a ] and is used throughout subsequent work on chromatic homology and its variants, for example [ HGPR06 , CCR08 , PPS09 , LS17 ] . In this paper, we shall keep using the enhanced state formulation of chromatic homology as it will be the most convenient for describing alternating-path counts in the chromatic chain complex. We shall only be concerned with the algebra 𝒜 m = ℤ ⁡ [ x ] / ⟨ x m ⟩ , but the constructions can also be done with a more general commutative algebra. Given a graph G = ( V , E ) , an enhanced state of G is an ordered pair ( H , ϵ ) where H is a spanning subgraph of G and ϵ is a coloring of the connected components of H by the colors { 1 , x , … , x m − 1 } . More precisely, let H ⊆ G be a subgraph with V ⁡ ( H ) = V ⁡ ( G ) and E ⁡ ( H ) ⊆ E ⁡ ( G ) . If π 0 ​ ( H ) denotes the collection of connected components of H , then ϵ is a map ϵ : π 0 ​ ( H ) → { 1 , x , … , x m − 1 } . 2.3.1. Spanning Tree Model for Chromatic Homology We will construct a spanning tree model for the chromatic complex, namely, a complex generated by signed spanning trees that is chain-homotopy equivalent to the chromatic complex. Our approach is to decompose the NBC ⁡ ( G ) part of the chromatic complex into interval pieces indexed by NBC spanning trees. This interval decomposition is inspired by Björner’s work on homology and shellability; in fact, the interval pieces arise from a shelling of NBC ⁡ ( G ) [ Bjö82 , Bjö92 ] . Since the NBC ⁡ ( G ) part of the chromatic complex is generated by the enhanced NBC subgraphs, it admits a similar decomposition into interval pieces. On each interval piece, we construct an acyclic matching with exactly two critical points. Applying algebraic Morse theory then produces a chain complex generated by signed spanning trees, where the sign ± records the two critical points associated with the interval piece of a given NBC spanning tree. Finally, we obtain an explicit combinatorial description of the differential by counting alternating paths between critical points induced by the matching.
