In [ 15 ] , we investigate the pair ( P , Supp ⁡ ( P ) ) of minimal path P and its supporting sub-digraph Supp ⁡ ( P ) in the path complex of a digraph G under the strongly regular condition. In this paper, first, we consider the special minimal path P specified by the admissible condition (Definition 3.8 ), which means that ( P , Supp ⁡ ( P ) ) admits a singular cubical realization. Based on such a subset, we systematically introduce the definitions of cellular chain complex associated to G and prove the well-definedness. Then we study several properties of such cellular homologies. Finally, we present several intriguing examples as well as some important observations. Introduction Since 2012, Grigoryan, Lin, Muranov and Yau develop a homotopy-homology approach to digraphs and initiate several studies of the relationship between discrete topology (where the discrete topology is played by objects of various categories of digraphs or graphs) and continuous topology, which is now known as GLMY-theory. Compared with other previously studied notions of graph homologies (e.g. via cliques [ 1 ] , or via Hochschild (co)homology [ 13 ] ), it boasts many commendable properties ( [ 2 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 ] ): (1) It “generalises” the homology of simplicial complex. (2) It satisfies the properties that are analogous to Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms. (3) It is well linked to graph-theoretical operations: • it respects the digraph suspension; • the Künneth formula holds for both Cartesian product of two digraphs and join of two digraphs. (4) There is an appropriate notion of fundamental group and covering digraphs. (5) There is a dual cohomology theory of digraphs which coincides with the one developed by Dimakis and Müller-Hoissen in [ 3 , 4 ] as a reduction of the “universal differential algebra”. In [ 15 ] , we study the structure of (a sub-complex of) path complex of a digraph G . More explicitly, we focus on the ℤ -coefficient generators of the path complex under the strongly regular condition 1 1 1 The “strongly regular condition” and “minimal path” are originally proposed in the Huang-Yau paper [ 12 ] . They also give the definition of supporting digraph, but it is a little different from our definition 2.5 . (that is, we stipulate that each elementary path must comprise distinct vertices, see Definition 2.1 ), study the so-called minimal path P (see, Definition 2.4 ) and its supporting digraph Supp ⁡ ( P ) (see, Definition 2.5 ). Question 1.1 . Why do we consider the strongly regular condition? On the one hand, given a finite simple digraph G , the GLMY’s regular condition allows the path of the form e i 0 i 1 ⋯ i n , i j ≠ i j + 1 , j = 0 , 1 , … , n − 1 . Under such a condition, the path complex as well as the path homology may be of infinite degree. For example, (1) For the circulant digraph C 5 1 , 2 in Subsection 4.1 : dim Ω n GLMY ​ ( C 5 1 , 2 ) > 0 , n ∈ ℕ . See Remark 4.4 for an explanation; (2) For the digraph G in Appendix A of [ 15 ] : dim H 3 ​ n + 1 GLMY ​ ( G ) > 0 , n ∈ ℕ . Thus, by definition, the strongly regular requirement gives rise to a finite sub-complex of GLMY’s path complex. This sub-complex, by virtue of its finiteness, becomes more amenable to computational analysis and theoretical exploration. But it also presents several limitations and challenges. For example, in general, ∂ f ∗ ≠ f ∗ ∂ , where f is the digraph map and the push-forward f ∗ is defined in 3.6 . Of course, when we consider the category of acyclic digraphs, the distinction between the strongly regular condition and the GLMY’s regular condition disappears, then the functoriality holds in such a narrow case. On the other hand, it is based on the topological/geometric consideration. We hope the minimal n -path P and its supporting digraph Supp ⁡ ( P ) could play the role of the regular n -cell in the topological space. To propel this idea, we study many examples and dissect the structure of P and Supp ⁡ ( P ) in [ 15 ] . The key results in [ 15 ] are the structure theorem (see, Theorem 2.10 ) and acyclic result for H ~ ∗ ​ ( Supp ⁡ ( P ) ) . Immediately, we obtain several applications based on the standard acyclic model theorem. However, it is indeed meritorious to highlight that, in the course of our investigations, we have also made the following noteworthy observations: • For any integer n ≥ 3 , there are infinitely many kinds of minimal n -paths. • The digraph Supp ⁡ ( P ) may not be contractible (Definition 3.36 ), such as the exotic cube 2 2 2 GJM [ 5 ] show that the exotic cube possesses a nontrivial singular cubical cycle in degree 2, and they also prove the homotopy invariance of singular cubical homology. Thus, the exotic cube is not contractible. (see, Example 3.15 ). More explicitly, for each n ≥ 3 , only finite kinds of Supp ⁡ ( P ) exhibit the property of contractibility. Specifically, as the number of elementary path components within the minimal path P increases, the contractibility of Supp ⁡ ( P ) will disappear. Contractibility stands as a cornerstone property in the topological spaces, significantly influencing further constructions and analyses. For example, the fiber bundle is trivial over the contractible space. Due to the aforementioned reasons, this paper endeavors to establish a robust and nuanced connection between digraphs and topological spaces by studying special minimal paths under an additional condition and by examining the CW complex and cellular homology of the digraph G . 1.1. The Cellular Homology of Digraphs In this section, we will consider the CW complex associated to a digraph by specifying certain pairs (which we call admissible pairs) of minimal path and its supporting digraph. The admissible paths may be linearly dependent as paths. Our definition of CW complex depends on the choice of maximal linearly independent subset of admissible paths. To circumvent the intricacies involved in the choice of a basis, we define the cellular chain group of the digraph as the quotient of the free group generated by so-called admissible pairs, modulo the linear relations imposed thereupon. 3.1. Functoriality and Homotopy invariance Let us recall basic homotopy theory of digraphs developed in [ 8 ] . Fix n ≥ 0 . Denote by I n = ( V ⁡ ( I n ) , E ⁡ ( I n ) ) any digraph, with V ⁡ ( I n ) = { 0 , 1 , … , n } and E ⁡ ( I n ) containing exactly one of the edges i → i + 1 , i + 1 → i for any i = 0 , 1 , … , n − 1 . We call it the line digraph. Definition 3.36 (Homotopy) . Let G , H be two digraphs. (1) Two digraph maps f , g : G → H are called homotopic if there exists a line digraph I n for some n ≥ 1 and a digraph map F : G ⊡ I n → H , such that F | G ⊡ { 0 } = f and F | G ⊡ { n } = g . We shall write f ​ ≃ 𝐹 ​ g (or simply f ≃ g ) and call F a n -step homotopy between f and g . (2) Two digraphs G and H are called homotopy equivalent if there exist digraph maps f : G → H , g : H → G such that f ∘ g ≃ Id H , g ∘ f ≃ Id G . Discussion and Outlook 5.1. The digraphs from geometry There are several ways to discrete a manifold M and get a graph which captures some topological structure of M . For example, one can choose a simplicial complex K which triangulates M , and its one-skeleton gives us a graph. Such a discretization has a wide range of applications, such as Cheeger-Müller theorem, combinatorial quantum field theory. CMY [ 10 ] prove that after twice barycentric subdivisions, the corresponding path homology of the resulting digraph is isomorphic to the simplicial homology of K . Instead, we can consider the digraph from geometry in another way. For simplicity, let ( M , g ) be an oriented complete Riemannian manifold with metric g and injectivity radius bounded below by a positive constant b . First, choose a discrete collection of points { x i } = V ⊂ M satisfying (1) dist g ⁡ ( x i , x j ) ≥ b 4 , for any x i ≠ x j ; (2) for every x ∈ M there is some x i ∈ V such that dist g ⁡ ( x i , x ) < b 2 . Next, define an (undirected) edge structure on V by the relationship x i ∼ x j , if ​ dist g ⁡ ( x i , x j ) < b ​ and ​ i ≠ j . Then G = ( V , E ) is a connected graph. Moreover, we consider the direction of each edge which is induced by the orientation of M : Given an undirected edge x i ∼ x j , we say x i → x j , if the tangent vector at x i to a distance minimizing geodesic connecting x i to x j is given by the orientation on M . Example 5.1 (The integer lattice ℤ n ) . Let ℝ n be the Euclidean space. Take V = ℤ n and b = 2 , then we get the standard nearest-neighbor digraph on the given lattice. We will study the path homology and cellular homology of the digraph from geometry and compare them to the homology of the manifold in a separation paper. 5.2.
