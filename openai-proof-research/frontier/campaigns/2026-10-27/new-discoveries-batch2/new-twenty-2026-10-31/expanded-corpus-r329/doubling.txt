We prove an asymptotically sharp bound for the minimal doubling of a small measurable subset of a compact connected Lie group, settling a conjecture of Breuillard and Green. Namely, let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension d G with Haar probability measure μ G , and let ϵ > 0 . We show that for all compact subsets A of sufficiently small measure we have μ G ​ ( A 2 ) ≥ ( 2 d G − d M − ϵ ) ​ μ G ​ ( A ) , where d M is the maximal dimension of a proper closed subgroup. This recovers, by a different method, the small-measure doubling theorem of Jing–Tran–Zhang for G = SO 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) . More generally, for every ϵ > 0 and any pair of non-empty compact subsets A , B of sufficiently small measure, we prove the Brunn–Minkowski-type inequality: μ G ​ ( A ​ B ) 1 d G − d M ≥ ( 1 − ϵ ) ​ ( μ G ​ ( A ) 1 d G − d M + μ G ​ ( B ) 1 d G − d M ) . We also prove an inverse theorem in the semi-simple case: for any fixed K ≥ 1 and δ > 0 , every compact K -approximate subgroup of sufficiently small positive Haar measure is covered by C ⁡ ( d G , K ) translates of the δ -neighbourhood of a proper closed connected subgroup. Our approach combines this inverse theorem with optimal transport and its recent applications to the Brunn–Minkowski inequality. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification Primary 22E30; Secondary 43A75, 20N99 1. Introduction Let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension d G and let μ G be the Haar probability measure, i.e. the unique G -invariant Borel probability measure on G . The doubling constant of a measurable subset A ⊂ G of positive measure is defined as the ratio, with inner Haar measure used if the product is not measurable, μ G ​ ( A 2 ) μ G ​ ( A ) , where A 2 := { a 1 a 2 | a 1 , a 2 ∈ A } is the Minkowski product. The doubling constant is an important object of study in both additive combinatorics [ 5 ] and geometric analysis [ 21 ] . The minimal value it can take in a given group G has been a long-established subject of research. A foundational general lower bound for connected locally compact groups is due to Kemperman [ 32 ] . For sets of finite positive Haar measure in Euclidean spaces and simply connected nilpotent Lie groups of dimension d G , the doubling constant is at least 2 d G [ 28 ] . Recent work of Jing, Tran and Zhang [ 31 ] yields similar inequalities in many non-compact Lie groups. This paper is concerned with doubling inequalities in compact Lie groups. Taking A = G gives doubling constant 1 , so a lower bound greater than 1 requires a restriction on the size of A . Small measure, however, does not imply that A is contained in a small ball: a thin neighbourhood of a proper subgroup has small measure while extending along the whole subgroup. The local dimension of G therefore does not by itself determine the least possible doubling. Breuillard and Green conjectured that doubling for small sets in SO 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) must be at least 4 − ϵ . In higher dimensions, their conjecture becomes: Conjecture 1.1 (Breuillard–Green conjecture for compact Lie groups, [ 23 , 30 ] ) . Let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension d G and let d M be the maximal dimension of a proper closed subgroup. Then lim α ↓ 0 inf { μ G ​ ( A 2 ) μ G ​ ( A ) | A ⊂ G compact , 0 < μ G ( A ) ≤ α } = 2 d G − d M . The exponent d G − d M counts the directions transverse to a subgroup of maximal dimension. A thin neighbourhood of such a subgroup already fills the subgroup directions, so doubling acts to first order in the remaining directions. This explains why these neighbourhoods are the natural model for small doubling. For example, in G = SO 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) let H ≃ SO 2 ⁡ ( ℝ ) be the subgroup of rotations fixing a given axis. The neighbourhood H δ has two transverse directions, so its measure is asymptotic to a positive constant times δ 2 . Since ( H δ ) 2 = H 2 ​ δ , its doubling tends to 4 as δ tends to zero, whereas the doubling of a small ball in G tends to 8 . Table 1 in Appendix A lists the corresponding codimensions for compact almost simple Lie groups. When G admits a non-trivial torus quotient, the conjecture follows from Kemperman’s inequality [ 32 ] . Little progress was made on the Breuillard–Green conjecture beyond that case until a recent breakthrough of Jing, Tran and Zhang [ 30 ] building upon previous work and model-theoretic tools. They solved the original Breuillard–Green conjecture (corresponding to G = SO 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) above), but the sharp bound for general G remained open. The main result of this paper is a proof of the general case of this conjecture: Theorem 1.2 (Minimal doubling in compact Lie groups) . Let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension and let ϵ > 0 . There is α > 0 such that for all compact A ⊂ G with μ G ​ ( A ) ≤ α we have μ G ​ ( A 2 ) ≥ ( 2 d G − d M − ϵ ) ​ μ G ​ ( A ) , where d G denotes the dimension of G and d M denotes the maximal dimension of a proper closed subgroup. The proof also gives a bound for the error in terms of the measure of A . Suppose that G is semi-simple and that its Lie algebra has n simple factors, counted with multiplicity. There are constants c , C > 0 , depending only on d G , such that every compact subset A of sufficiently small positive measure satisfies μ G ​ ( A 2 ) ≥ ( 2 d G − d M − C ( log ∘ ( n + 1 ) ⁡ ( 1 / μ G ​ ( A ) ) ) c ) ​ μ G ​ ( A ) , where log ∘ j denotes the j -fold iterate of the natural logarithm, and the measure is small enough that all these logarithms are defined and positive. In particular, for an almost simple group the error is O d G ​ ( ( log ⁡ log ⁡ ( 1 / μ G ​ ( A ) ) ) − c ) . The repeated logarithms come from the measure threshold in the inverse theorem, while the estimate near a subgroup has a power error in the neighbourhood radius. We record the deduction at the end of § 4 . When G is not semi-simple, Kemperman’s inequality gives μ G ​ ( A 2 ) ≥ 2 ​ μ G ​ ( A ) for μ G ​ ( A ) ≤ 1 / 2 , so no error is needed. Some assumption of small measure is necessary, since μ G ​ ( G 2 ) = μ G ​ ( G ) . One expects more precisely a correcting term of order μ G ​ ( A ) 2 d G − d M , and in the semi-simple case that order would be optimal: if H has maximal dimension among proper closed subgroups, the subsets H δ satisfy μ G ​ ( H δ ) ≍ δ d G − d M and have doubling 2 d G − d M − Θ ⁡ ( δ 2 ) (by the small-ball volume formula on the normal homogeneous space G / H , whose scalar curvature is positive in this case). An error bound of this order remains open in the present argument. As is often the case, the doubling inequality (Theorem 1.2 ) stems from a much more general lower bound on the measure of product-sets. The lower bound we obtain takes the form of a Brunn–Minkowski-type inequality: Theorem 1.3 (Brunn–Minkowski in compact Lie groups) . Let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension. Let ϵ > 0 . There is α > 0 such that if A , B are non-empty compact subsets of G with μ G ​ ( A ) , μ G ​ ( B ) ≤ α , then μ G ​ ( A ​ B ) 1 d G − d M ≥ ( 1 − ϵ ) ​ ( μ G ​ ( A ) 1 d G − d M + μ G ​ ( B ) 1 d G − d M ) , where d G denotes the dimension of G and d M denotes the maximal dimension of a proper closed subgroup. By inner regularity, the two theorems also hold for Haar-measurable sets, with the same thresholds and with inner Haar measure for products that are not known to be measurable. Indeed, approximate each set from within by compact subsets, using a singleton when a non-empty input has measure zero. The Brunn–Minkowski-type inequality is asymptotically sharp, and in the semi-simple case the presence of the correcting term ϵ is also necessary. The quantitative dependence on the measures, especially when μ G ​ ( B ) becomes small relative to μ G ​ ( A ) , raises further questions. We do not pursue these here. The asymmetric problem is developed further in [ 35 ] . We also establish an inverse theorem for approximate subgroups of small measure, which is the main technical contribution of this paper. Recall that a K -approximate subgroup is a symmetric subset Λ containing e such that Λ 2 is covered by at most K left translates of Λ . We write H δ for the δ -neighbourhood of a subgroup H . Theorem 1.4 (An inverse theorem for approximate subgroups of small measure) . Let G be a compact connected semi-simple Lie group of positive dimension, equipped with the metric fixed in § 1.4 , and let K ≥ 1 . For all δ > 0 there is ϵ > 0 such that if Λ ⊂ G is a compact K -approximate subgroup with e ∈ Λ , μ G ​ ( Λ ) > 0 and μ G ​ ( Λ ) ≤ ϵ then there is a proper closed connected subgroup H such that Λ is covered by C ⁡ ( d G , K ) translates of H δ . Besides its use in the proof of Theorem 1.3 , this result is of independent interest: it applies for every fixed K , with a covering bound independent of δ . The measure threshold may depend on δ . Theorem 1.4 is a continuous analogue of Jordan’s theorem: in a fixed compact connected Lie group, every finite subgroup is covered by a bounded number of cosets of a torus, with the bound depending only on the ambient group. In the semi-simple case the torus is proper. Breuillard and Green established a version for finite approximate subgroups of unitary groups [ 6 ] . Here we consider compact approximate subgroups of small positive Haar measure. Our method relies on the shrinking commutator argument recalled in § 3.4 , which we can exploit by analysing discretisations of approximate subgroups at various scales. Even when specialised to SO 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) , our proof provides a new approach to the result of [ 30 ] based on geometric ideas related to Jordan’s theorem, the structure of approximate groups and the optimal transport approach to the Brunn–Minkowski inequality. This new approach provides further insight into the structure of sets with doubling close to the optimum. Recall that the relevance of the constant 2 d G − d M was explained in [ 23 , 30 ] by the fact that neighbourhoods of proper subgroups of maximal dimension have doubling asymptotic to 2 d G − d M . This suggests a converse: sets with doubling close to the optimum should concentrate near a coset of a proper subgroup of maximal dimension. We address this stability question in companion work, the methods from the present paper being the main engine there as well. 1.1. Historical background The first results on the doubling constant can be traced back to fundamental papers of Brunn [ 8 ] , Minkowski [ 40 ] and Lusternik [ 34 ] regarding Minkowski sums in ℝ d G . Their works unveiled a natural relationship between minimal doubling and dimension: the base- 2 logarithm of the minimal doubling constant equals the dimension d G . This tells us that minimal doubling is intimately linked to the local behaviour of the set considered. These inequalities and their generalisations have been a fundamental tool of geometric analysis in relation to isoperimetric inequalities. We refer to the survey [ 21 ] for this and more. As a counterpart, the seminal work of Freiman [ 19 ] showed that doubling is also linked to global phenomena. Freiman’s work showed quantitatively that finite subsets of ℤ exhibiting small doubling are contained in generalised arithmetic progressions of bounded rank and controlled size, again relating small doubling to a dimensional constant. Many works have improved upon Freiman’s results. They were extended to other abelian and non-abelian groups (e.g. [ 24 , 27 ] ) culminating in a general classification result for finite approximate subgroups due to Breuillard, Green and Tao [ 7 ] . The quantitative aspects of Freiman’s results were also improved towards the polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture [ 41 , 22 ] . The interaction between these two fields recently gained traction following the seminal work of Figalli and Jerison on the quantitative stability problem of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality [ 15 ] . They observed there that the one-dimensional stability could be seen as a consequence of Freiman’s 3 ​ k − 4 theorem [ 18 ] and used it as the starting point of their proof. Following this, further quantitative stability results were obtained [ 14 , 3 ] culminating in the recent sharp bounds obtained by Figalli, van Hintum and Tiba [ 17 ] . Van Hintum, Keevash and Tiba [ 44 ] proved a discrete Brunn–Minkowski inequality in ℤ n , resolving a conjecture of Ruzsa. We show in this article that techniques from the structure theory of approximate groups and the optimal transport proof of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality can be combined to prove the Breuillard–Green conjecture for compact Lie groups (Theorem 1.2 ). Ellis, Kindler, Lifshitz and Minzer [ 13 ] study product-set estimates for large subsets of compact Lie groups, complementing the small-measure regime considered here. 1.2. A look at the methods The proof of Theorem 1.3 , and hence of Theorem 1.2 , has to account for sets that are small in measure but extend along a proper subgroup. Kemperman’s inequality handles groups with a non-trivial torus quotient, so we may assume that G is semi-simple. The local result is an extension of the Brunn–Minkowski inequality to subsets in small enough neighbourhoods of the identity: Theorem 1.5 (Local Brunn–Minkowski inequality) . Let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension d G , equipped with a bi-invariant Riemannian distance d . There are C ⁡ ( G , d ) > 0 and ρ 0 > 0 such that for all 0 < ρ < ρ 0 and all non-empty compact subsets A , B ⊂ B G ​ ( e , ρ ) , μ G ​ ( A ​ B ) 1 d G ≥ ( 1 − C ⁡ ( G , d ) ​ ρ 2 ) ​ ( μ G ​ ( A ) 1 d G + μ G ​ ( B ) 1 d G ) . In the semi-simple case, with the metric fixed in § 1.4 , both C and ρ 0 can be chosen to depend only on d G . To establish this result we adapt a strategy described in particular in [ 14 , pp. 242–244] proving Brunn–Minkowski-type inequalities thanks to optimal transport. To prove Theorem 1.3 , and hence the main doubling theorem, we must turn the local inequality into a bound for arbitrary sets A , B of small measure. For pairs that do not already satisfy the required bound, Tao’s product-set inverse theorem (Proposition 3.2 ) covers A and B by left and right translates of a common approximate subgroup Λ of small measure. Our inverse theorem (Theorem 1.4 ) then gives covers by boundedly many translates of a thin neighbourhood H δ of a proper closed connected subgroup H . There are two issues in using these covers: obtaining the sharp inequality near H , where the sets need not lie in a small ball, and combining the estimates without losing a factor depending on the number of translates. For the first, we average the local Brunn–Minkowski inequality (Theorem 1.5 ) along H using double counting. The volume growth of balls in H enters through this averaging and yields the codimension exponent 1 / ( d G − dim H ) in Proposition 4.5 . For the second, we refine the covers so that products of a fixed piece of one set with distinct pieces of the other are disjoint. A comparison of the proportions of A and B contained in their respective pieces then allows the estimates near H to be summed with no loss in the constant. Since dim H ≤ d M , this gives the required inequality. The proofs also give quantitative bounds, although their dependence on the parameters is far from optimal. 1.3. Outline of the paper We start in § 2 by establishing the local Brunn–Minkowski inequality (Theorem 1.5 ) thanks to an optimal transport argument. We follow up in § 3 by showing that small approximate subgroups are covered by boundedly many translates of neighbourhoods of proper subgroups (Theorem 1.4 ). We conclude in § 4 the proof of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 by relying on the previous two sections and a double counting argument. We end this paper in § 5 with questions about minimisers and the error close to a subgroup, followed by an application to the growth of small subsets. 1.4. Notation Given two subsets X , Y of a group G we denote by X ​ Y the set of products { x y | x ∈ X , y ∈ Y } and we write X − 1 := { x − 1 | x ∈ X } . For all n ∈ ℕ define inductively X 0 := { e } , X 1 := X , X 2 := X ​ X and X n + 1 := X ​ X n . Whenever a product is not known to be measurable, its measure is understood as inner Haar measure, the supremum of the measures of its compact subsets. Unless a result explicitly states otherwise, G denotes a compact connected semi-simple Lie group of positive dimension d G . We say a compact connected Lie group is semi-simple if it has no non-trivial connected normal closed abelian subgroup, equivalently if the identity component of its centre is trivial. Here almost simple means that the Lie algebra is simple, so an almost simple group may have non-trivial finite centre. The word connected in the condition on the normal subgroup cannot be dropped: SU 2 ⁡ ( ℂ ) has centre { ± 1 } . Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 are stated for compact connected G . Lemma 4.6 below reduces them to the semi-simple case, so we lose nothing by this convention. Compact Lie groups can be equipped with a bi-invariant (i.e. invariant under right and left translations) Riemannian metric as well as a Haar probability measure μ G which is a bi-invariant Borel probability measure, see [ 42 ] for this and more. We fix the metric as follows. Write G = G 1 ⋯ G n as an almost direct product of its compact almost simple factors. On each G i choose the bi-invariant Riemannian metric whose distance has diameter 1 / n , take the orthogonal sum of these metrics on ∏ i G i , and give G the quotient metric under the finite central multiplication map. Thus the product distance is ( ∑ i d i 2 ) 1 / 2 , and G has diameter at most 1 / n ≤ 1 . There are only finitely many compact semi-simple groups in bounded dimension up to isomorphism. Since the scale on each almost simple factor is now fixed, the geometric constants used below depend only on d G . The quotient metrics on factor groups are comparable, with constants depending only on d G , to the same normalisation on those groups. We use that comparison whenever we apply a result to a factor quotient. Write ⟨ ⋅ , ⋅ ⟩ for the resulting Ad ⁡ ( G ) -invariant scalar product on the Lie algebra 𝔤 . We write ∥ ⋅ ∥ for the associated norm and d for the bi-invariant Riemannian distance it induces on G . All orthogonal complements in 𝔤 are taken with respect to ⟨ ⋅ , ⋅ ⟩ . We will often use the fact that balls centred at e for the distance d are normal, and that d is geodesic: the one-parameter subgroups of G are the geodesics through e and are minimising below the injectivity radius, so that ∥ X ∥ = d ⁡ ( e , exp ⁡ ( X ) ) for ∥ X ∥ smaller than the injectivity radius of G at e , and B G ​ ( e , r + s ) ⊂ B G ​ ( e , r ) ​ B G ​ ( e , s ) for all r , s > 0 . A maximal proper closed connected subgroup means one that is maximal among proper closed connected subgroups: if H ⊂ L ⊂ G and L is closed and connected, then L = H or L = G . This is distinct from having maximal dimension. Given a subgroup H and δ > 0 write H δ for the δ -neighbourhood of H . It is defined by H δ := { g ∈ G | ∃ h ∈ H , d ( h , g ) < δ } = B G ( e , δ ) H = H B G ( e , δ ) , where B G ​ ( e , δ ) denotes the ball of radius δ centred at e . The identity component of H is written H ∘ . A subscript on B naming a space is followed by a centre and a radius, as in B G ​ ( e , r ) , B H ​ ( h , ρ ) and B G / H ​ ( g ​ H , δ ) . For a normed vector space W , we use B W ​ ( 0 , r ) for the norm ball. We also use the abbreviation B r := B G ​ ( e , r ) and write B ¯ r for the corresponding closed ball. We write d M for the maximal dimension of a proper closed subgroup of G , and, whenever a specific proper closed subgroup H has been fixed, d H := dim H . Thus d H ≤ d M , with equality exactly when H has maximal dimension among proper closed subgroups. Throughout the paper, we will deal with several parameters and constants that depend on one another. When needed, we indicate the dependence in parentheses: a constant C ⁡ ( d G ) > 0 is a constant depending on the parameter d G . We will usually not keep track of the exact value of each constant. In many steps of our proofs constants depending exclusively on the dimension of the ambient Lie group G will appear. We will denote all these constants by C ⁡ ( d G ) even though they might differ. We do however keep reference to the dimension to emphasise the dependence. The same convention applies to the lowercase c ⁡ ( d G ) . As a rule of thumb, α and ϵ will denote small quantities, and δ and ρ will denote radii of certain balls. In § 2 , λ denotes Lebesgue measure in exponential coordinates. 2. We first prove the inverse theorem (Theorem 1.4 ) when G is almost simple. We follow the shrinking commutator argument used by Breuillard and Green for finite approximate subgroups [ 6 ] , recalled in § 3.4 . The difficulty here is that Λ 2 need not have a shortest nonidentity element. Discretisation allows us to recover the argument whenever Λ 2 has a sufficiently wide gap in its distances from the identity. For a fixed power of Λ , the approximate subgroup property supplies a controlled number of exceptional scales r 1 > ⋯ > r k . Between consecutive scales, bounded products can be replaced by elements of Λ 2 with an error at the smaller scale. In other words, Λ 2 is (almost) closed under product in between these exceptional scales. This lets us take products repeatedly without continually increasing the number of factors. Suppose two consecutive exceptional scales are far apart. If Λ 2 has a wide empty annulus between them, we choose a shortest element λ ∈ Λ 2 beyond the annulus and proceed with the shrinking commutator argument essentially as in the discrete case. This gives a substantial subset of Λ whose quotients almost centralise λ , and from it we obtain the required covering near a proper connected subgroup. If there is no such gap, one can find an element at each scale. Arguments from the theory of growth in groups show that each such element and its conjugates can be used to generate a ball of comparable radius in boundedly many products. In between these exceptional scales, this implies that Λ 2 already (approximately) contains this ball. Passing through all the exceptional scales then covers a ball of fixed radius by a controlled number of translates of Λ , contradicting the small measure assumption. The covering number in this contradiction may depend on the requested radius δ , whereas the covering near a subgroup must be independent of δ . 3.3. Closing remarks 5.1. The quest for minimisers Our results corroborate the following semi-simple version of the minimiser conjecture raised for almost simple groups in [ 30 , Conj. 3.13] : Conjecture 5.1 . Let G be a compact connected semi-simple Lie group of positive dimension, equipped with the metric fixed in § 1.4 . For all sufficiently small α > 0 there are a proper closed subgroup H of dimension d M and δ > 0 , with 2 ​ δ below the injectivity radius of the induced metric on G / H , such that μ G ​ ( H δ ) = α and μ G ​ ( A 2 ) ≥ μ G ​ ( ( H δ ) 2 ) for every measurable subset A ⊂ G with μ G ​ ( A ) = α . The subgroup H is chosen as part of the minimisation and may depend on α . If true, this conjecture would pin down the exact rate at which the doubling constant approaches 2 d G − d M , and in particular the correcting term discussed after Theorem 1.2 . Inverse results can describe sets with small doubling without identifying exact minimisers, as in Freiman-type structure theorems [ 41 ] . The existence and structure of minimisers therefore remain separate questions, even when an inverse theorem is available: Problem 5.2 . Let G be a connected Lie group with a fixed left Haar measure μ G , and let 0 < α < ∞ with α ≤ μ G ​ ( G ) . Is the infimum of μ G ​ ( A 2 ) / μ G ​ ( A ) over measurable subsets A ⊂ G with μ G ​ ( A ) = α attained? When it is, what is the structure of the minimisers? Concrete groups G of particular interest are SO 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) and SL 2 ⁡ ( ℝ ) . Note however that such minimisers never exist in the Heisenberg group ℋ 3 ​ ( ℝ ) [ 38 ] . A related quantitative question concerns Proposition 4.4 . For a fixed proper closed subgroup H and all sufficiently small δ > 0 , can one replace the loss C ⁡ ( d G ) ​ δ 4 3 by C ⁡ ( d G ) ​ δ 2 , under the same hypotheses on A , B , A ​ B ? At scales ρ ≥ δ , the present proof loses O ⁡ ( ρ 2 + δ 2 / ρ ) . The term δ 2 / ρ comes from the tangential enlargement of the output rectangle to radius 2 ​ ρ + c ⁡ ( d G ) ​ δ 2 , and the choice ρ = δ 2 3 balances the two errors. A quadratic bound would require going beyond this balance, perhaps by estimating the effect of the tangential enlargement more precisely. 5.2. Growth of small subsets Lower bounds on the growth of products are one route to diameter bounds for Cayley graphs, as in the questions surrounding Babai’s conjecture [ 1 ] . In the compact setting, the following proposition bounds the number of products needed for a small set to reach a fixed positive measure. The size of this threshold is relevant to connecting our estimates with the large-set regime studied in [ 13 ] . Proposition 5.3 . Let G be a compact connected Lie group of positive dimension and let 0 < δ < 1 . There is α = α ⁡ ( d G , δ ) > 0 such that for every compact subset A ⊂ G with 0 < μ G ​ ( A ) ≤ α we have μ G ​ ( A m ) ≥ α for some m ≤ 2 ​ μ G ​ ( A ) − 1 ( 1 − δ ) ​ ( d G − d M ) . Proof. Apply Theorem 1.2 with ϵ := 2 d G − d M − 2 ( 1 − δ ) ​ ( d G − d M ) , which is positive since δ > 0 , and let α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] be the resulting threshold. It may be chosen to depend only on d G and δ : Lemma 4.6 gives a uniform threshold when G has an abelian factor, and there are only finitely many semi-simple groups of dimension d G up to isomorphism. That theorem then gives μ G ​ ( B 2 ) ≥ 2 ( 1 − δ ) ​ ( d G − d M ) ​ μ G ​ ( B ) for every compact subset B ⊂ G with μ G ​ ( B ) ≤ α . Write ν := μ G ​ ( A ) and let k ≥ 0 be least such that μ G ​ ( A 2 k ) ≥ α . Such a k is well defined since the measures μ G ​ ( A 2 j ) increase by a factor at least 2 ( 1 − δ ) ​ ( d G − d M ) > 1 as long as they stay below α , while remaining at most 1 . If k = 0 the conclusion holds with m = 1 , so assume k ≥ 1 . For every j < k we have μ G ​ ( A 2 j ) < α by minimality of k , so the displayed inequality applies to B = A 2 j and, by induction, μ G ​ ( A 2 j ) ≥ 2 j ⁡ ( 1 − δ ) ​ ( d G − d M ) ​ ν for all j ≤ k .
