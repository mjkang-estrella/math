## 361400: full refutation, excluded by a complete earlier theorem bridge

The literal endpoint is a conjunction of two domination inequalities for every finite simple graph G such that G and its complement have radius and diameter 2. To refute it, one of the two inequalities must fail on a graph in that exact class. Refuting only a diameter-two variant would be insufficient.

Primary definitions and Conjecture 2.8 were read in arXiv:2401.02765v2; the v1 text was also fetched and its conjecture context checked. Arumugam et al. use ordinary closed-neighborhood domination. Their Theorem 1.7 states the familiar complement-diameter implication, but the following direct proof removes any dependence on that cited theorem.

Bridge. If gamma(G)>2, every distinct pair {u,v} fails to dominate G. Therefore there is w outside that pair adjacent to neither u nor v in G. In the complement u-w-v is a path. Hence the complement has diameter at most 2. If G is connected with diameter 2 it has no isolated vertex, so its complement has no universal vertex. Thus every vertex of the complement has eccentricity exactly 2. Also gamma(G)>2 rules out a universal vertex of G, so every vertex of G has eccentricity exactly 2. Consequently every diameter-two graph with gamma(G)>2 lies in SCC(2).

The actual primary lower-bound proof in Al-Yakoob and Tuza, JCMCC 40 (2002), 183–191, Theorem 1, printed pp.186–187, was read visually from the public publisher scan (PDF pages 4–5). The theorem supplies arbitrarily large n and graphs of diameter 2 with gamma(G) at least (1/(2 sqrt(2))+o(1)) sqrt(n log n). Its definition gamma(n,2) and the theorem statement were read on pp.184–185. The proof chooses p=sqrt((2+epsilon)log n/n), proves all vertex pairs have common neighbors with probability tending to 1, then union bounds over all small proposed dominating sets. Since that lower bound eventually exceeds ceil(sqrt(n)), the bridge gives precisely an in-scope violation of the literal conjunction. This is a complete prior-source reduction, not an assertion that the source used the name SCC(2). The 2002 theorem precedes the January 2024 conjecture.

For an independently explicit finite ordinary route, without executing or enumerating anything: set N=2^256, n=N^2, p=32/N, and sample independent edges. The probability some pair has no common neighbor is at most n^2 exp(-p^2(n-2)) < 2^1024 exp(-1023) < 1/8. The last inequality follows from e>5/2 and (4/5)^4<1/2. For a fixed N-element set D, domination has probability (1-(1-p)^N)^(n-N). Since p<1/2, log(1-p)>=-2p, so (1-p)^N>=exp(-64). Union bounding over at most n^N sets gives probability at most exp(N log n -(n-N)exp(-64)). Here log n<512, n-N>=N^2/2, and exp(64)<3^64<2^128; thus the exponent is less than N(512-2^127)<-N. This probability too is less than 1/8. Therefore a finite graph has every pair joined by a two-edge walk and no dominating N-set. A dominating smaller set could be enlarged to N, so gamma(G)>N=ceil(sqrt(n)). Such a graph is not complete and has diameter exactly 2; the bridge proves SCC(2). This finite probabilistic route is fully subsumed by the earlier theorem and bridge. No explicit adjacency list or finite certificate was constructed. No claim of an eligible discovery or Pi plan follows.

Failed first approaches: gamma(G) gamma(complement(G))<=n bounds only the smaller parameter and cannot prove both requested inequalities. A maximum-degree bound is likewise not an upper bound on gamma. Requiring both graphs to have diameter two does not repair either inference. The random construction survived those objections, but failed the substantive novelty gate for the explicit reason above.

## 361539 and 361540: residue and division-slice mechanisms; unresolved

Source 2401.09497 has only v1 (17 January 2024) in the live submission history. Definitions 1.1 and 2.1, Lemmas 2.2–2.4 and their proofs, starting-sequence/rule context, and Conjectures 5.1–5.3 were inspected. Searches did not supply a later proof body; that is not novelty clearance. Earlier completed r95 work concerns the stronger generation conjecture 361538 and a construction omission; it does not supply a proof or disproof of these endpoint claims. No earlier enumeration was rerun or treated as a certificate.

Write a_m=position(m). The definition propagates divisibility along each residue class, and Lemma 2.2 identifies {i:m divides sigma(i)} with the single full residue class a_m modulo m in [1,k]. Its size is floor(k/m). Thus if k=q m+r, the least representative s_m in [1,m] satisfies s_m>r. This cardinality constraint is necessary but does not locate a_m at an end of its class: any of the q positions has the same residue. For m=1 the condition is wholly vacuous. Therefore the tempting argument that the unique hole (position of 1) must be an endpoint does not follow from the individual class counts.

A second mechanism combines classes. Membership in classes for m and d intersects as dictated by simultaneous divisibility of sigma(i), equivalently lcm(m,d) divides sigma(i). When lcm<=k that is the corresponding full residue class; when lcm>k the intersection must be empty within [1,k]. In particular overlapping prime classes must reserve positions for their common multiples; choosing each class independently is invalid. Pairwise the WCS definition is equivalent to gcd(sigma(i),|i-j|)=gcd(sigma(j),|i-j|): every divisor of either gcd divides the other, using the symmetric implication. This is an exact reformulation but not the missing global compatibility theorem. No proof that these intersection constraints force the sole non-multiple-of-any-prime position to lie in {1,k} was obtained.

A third step uses Lemma 2.4 beyond raw residue counting. Its division slice D_m is a WCS of length floor(k/m); the entry 1 in that slice corresponds to the original entry m. If the endpoint-1 conjecture holds for all positive lengths, then m is at the first or last position of its m-residue class. That puts position(m) in [1,m] or [k-m+1,k], proving 361540 in the requested range m<k/2 (indeed whenever the slice is nonempty). This is exactly the dependency already stated in the primary paper. It is neither an unconditional solution nor separately creditable. Applying induction to the m=1 slice is circular because it has the original length. Induction on proper slices only controls entries m>=2 and has not located the remaining hole. Precise gap: prove or refute endpoint-1 from simultaneous full-class compatibility for every k; no valid interior-hole witness or structural proof is supplied. Both IDs remain held.

## 361479: chromatic-algebra minimality; unresolved

Primary 2401.06095v1 (11 January 2024), Sections 3–5, was inspected at definition/proof contexts. The scalar field is C((Q)), multiplication is vertical stacking, and the quotient imposes contraction minus deletion on inner non-loop edges, loop evaluation Q-1, and removal of bivalent vertices. Definition 5.1 specifies e^n_{i,j}, with the entire contiguous top-and-bottom interval attached to one inner vertex; the identity is allowed implicitly (Remark 5.2). Theorem 2 proves generation, by reduction to the noncrossing partition basis B_n. Conjecture 5.11 asks precisely that each generator remain necessary, not that arbitrary smaller generating sets be impossible (the separate stronger Conjecture 5.12).

First mechanism: use independence of B_n to separate each e_{i,j} from the linear span of the other generators. This only excludes degree-one linear expressions. An algebra generating set allows arbitrarily long noncommutative words, so linear independence does not prove the endpoint.

Second mechanism: filter diagrams by interval size or connected boundary support and isolate a leading coefficient for the missing generator. Multiplying generators supported on overlapping adjacent intervals can create a connected internal graph spanning the union. Contraction of the inner edge then produces the union connectivity, while deletion produces other terms. Thus support of a word is not confined to the support of any single factor, and a coefficient functional vanishing on the other individual generators need not vanish on their generated algebra. The required multiplication-stable proper subspace was not constructed. No unverified equality among three diagrams is being asserted here.

Third mechanism: a representation or specialization could distinguish the deleted generator from every word in the rest. One needs a character or invariant subspace respected by every remaining generator and violated by that generator, with the representation satisfying all three quotient relations over the symbolic field. Specializing Q at a numerical value without an injectivity argument can lose exactly the distinguishing information; a numerical rank diagnostic alone would not prove generic necessity. No such representation/character was proved. Exact gap: for each n and interval [i,j], construct an algebraic separator for the unital C((Q))-subalgebra generated by E_n minus e_{i,j}, or provide a symbolic expression refuting necessity. No formal or solver execution is proposed.
