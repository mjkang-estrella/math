Given a non-decreasing sequence S = ( s 1 , s 2 , … , s k ) of positive integers, an S -packing coloring of a graph G is a partition of its vertex set into k subsets { V 1 , V 2 , … , V k } such that for each 1 ≤ i ≤ k , the distance between any two distinct vertices u , v ∈ V i is at least s i + 1 . In this paper, we study the S -packing coloring problem for Halin graphs with maximum degree Δ ≤ 5 . Specifically, we present a linear-time algorithm that constructs a ( 1 , 1 , 2 , 2 , 2 ) -packing coloring for any Halin graph satisfying Δ ≤ 5 . It is worth noting that there are Halin graphs that are not ( 1 , 2 , 2 , 2 ) -packing colorable. Keywords : vertex coloring; packing coloring; Halin graph. 1 Introduction Let S = ( s 1 , s 2 , … , s k ) be a non-decreasing sequence of positive integers (i.e., s 1 ≤ s 2 ≤ ⋯ ≤ s k ). An S -packing coloring of a simple graph G = ( V ⁡ ( G ) , E ⁡ ( G ) ) is a partition of V ⁡ ( G ) into k disjoint subsets V 1 , V 2 , … , V k such that for each i ∈ [ k ] := { 1 , 2 , … , k } , any two distinct vertices u , v ∈ V i satisfy d G ​ ( u , v ) ≥ s i + 1 , where d G ​ ( u , v ) denotes the distance between u and v in G . The packing chromatic number of G , denoted χ p ​ ( G ) , is the smallest integer k for which G admits a ( 1 , 2 , … , k ) -packing coloring (also called a k -packing coloring ). The concept of packing chromatic number was introduced by Goddard, Hedetniemi, Hedetniemi, Harris, and Rall [ 15 ] in 2008 under the original name broadcast chromatic number , motivated by frequency assignment problems in wireless broadcast networks. A proper k -coloring of G is a mapping φ : V ⁡ ( G ) → [ k ] such that for any adjacent vertices u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) , φ ⁡ ( u ) ≠ φ ⁡ ( v ) ; if such a mapping exists, then G is said to be k -colorable . In contrast, a 2-distance coloring of G is a vertex coloring φ : V ⁡ ( G ) → [ k ] such that for any two distinct vertices u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) with d G ​ ( u , v ) ≤ 2 , φ ⁡ ( u ) ≠ φ ⁡ ( v ) ; the minimum k for which this is possible is the 2-distance chromatic number χ 2 ​ ( G ) . Notably, these concepts relate to packing colorings: a ( 1 , 1 , … , 1 ) -packing coloring corresponds to an ordinary proper coloring, while a ( 2 , 2 , … , 2 ) -packing coloring aligns with 2-distance coloring. Furthermore, the ( 2 , 2 , … , 2 ) -packing coloring is closely related to, but strictly stronger than, the concept of exact 2-distance coloring [ 26 , Section 11.9] : while an exact 2-distance coloring requires only that vertices at distance exactly 2 receive distinct colors (allowing adjacent vertices to share colors), a ( 2 , 2 , … , 2 ) -packing coloring enforces that all pairs of vertices within each color class are at distance at least 3, thereby implying a valid exact 2-distance coloring but not vice versa. If graphs are restricted to be triangle-free, then exact 2-distance coloring becomes equivalent to an injective coloring —a coloring where any two vertices sharing a common neighbor receive distinct colors [ 9 , 10 ] . It follows that even in triangle-free graphs, ( 2 , 2 , … , 2 ) -packing coloring remains strictly stronger than injective coloring. It is worth noting that Bu, Qi and Zhu [ 11 ] proved that Halin graphs with maximum degree at most five has an injective 7-coloring. We refer the readers to further reading on these relative topics [ 31 , 28 , 27 , 13 ] . We define S ⁡ ( G ) as the graph obtained by subdividing each edge of G , i.e., replacing each edge u ​ v ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) with a path u - w - v through a new vertex w . The problem of bounding χ p ​ ( G ) and χ p ​ ( S ​ ( G ) ) for subcubic graphs (where each vertex has degree at most 3) has been investigated in several studies [ 8 , 14 ] . Gastineau and Togni [ 14 ] were the first to ask whether χ p ​ ( S ⁡ ( G ) ) ≤ 5 holds for all subcubic graphs G . This question was later formalized as a conjecture by Brešar et al. [ 8 ] , who proposed the following: Conjecture 1.1 . If G is a subcubic graph, then χ p ​ ( S ⁡ ( G ) ) ≤ 5 . For a graph G and its subdivision S ⁡ ( G ) , Gastineau and Togni [ 14 ] established a fundamental relationship between their packing colorability: if G is ( s 1 , s 2 , … , s k ) -packing colorable, then S ⁡ ( G ) is ( 1 , 2 ​ s 1 + 1 , 2 ​ s 2 + 1 , … , 2 ​ s k + 1 ) -packing colorable. They further observed that when G is ( 1 2 , 2 2 ) -packing colorable ( p r in a sequence means p is repeated r times), this immediately implies χ p ​ ( S ⁡ ( G ) ) ≤ 5 . Consequently, to verify Conjecture 1.1 for any subclass of subcubic graphs, it suffices to demonstrate ( 1 2 , 2 2 ) -packing colorability within that subclass. Toward this goal, Liu, Zhang, and Zhang [ 20 ] proved that every subcubic graph G is ( 1 2 , 2 2 , 3 ) -packing colorable, thus establishing χ p ​ ( S ⁡ ( G ) ) ≤ 6 . This result is just one parameter shy of the conjectured bound and significantly improved an earlier result by Balogh, Kostochka, and Liu [ 1 ] , who showed that the packing chromatic number of the 1-subdivision of subcubic graphs is bounded by 8. Several studies have investigated diverse packing S -colorings of subcubic graphs [ 6 , 5 , 17 , 19 , 25 , 23 , 2 , 4 , 7 , 18 ] . In particular, Gastineau and Togni [ 14 ] demonstrated that every subcubic graph admits both ( 1 2 , 2 3 ) -packing colorings and ( 1 , 2 6 ) -packing colorings. Balogh, Kostochka, and Liu [ 1 ] further showed that any subcubic graph has a ( 1 2 , 2 2 , 3 2 , k ) -packing coloring where color k ≥ 4 is used at most once, and that every 2-degenerate subcubic graph is ( 1 2 , 2 2 , 3 2 ) -packing colorable. For cubic graphs specifically, Cranston and Kim [ 12 ] proved that all cubic graphs except the Petersen graph admit ( 2 8 ) -packing colorings. This result was complemented by Thomassen [ 30 ] and independently by Hartke, Jahanbekam, and Thomas [ 16 ] , who established that cubic planar graphs are ( 2 7 ) -packing colorable. To systematically refine these results, researchers have focused on i -saturated subcubic graphs , a family defined by structural constraints on high-degree vertices. A k -degree graph is a graph G satisfying Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ k , and an i -saturated subcubic graph is one where each vertex of degree 3 is adjacent to at most i other degree-3 vertices. This classification has yielded significant progress: Yang and Wu [ 33 ] proved that all 0-saturated subcubic graphs admit ( 1 2 , 3 ) -packing colorings; Bazzal [ 3 ] extended this to 1-saturated graphs with ( 1 2 , 2 ) -colorings; Mortada and Togni [ 22 ] further generalized to 2-saturated graphs with ( 1 2 , 2 , 3 ) -colorings; and their subsequent work [ 21 ] established the ( 1 2 , 2 2 ) -colorability for ( 3 , 0 ) -saturated variants (a refined subclass where ( 3 , 0 ) -saturated denotes 3-saturated graphs with non-adjacent heavy vertices, defined as degree-3 vertices whose neighbors are all degree-3 vertices). Despite the limited exploration of S -packing coloring in graphs with larger maximum degrees, Mortada and Togni [ 24 ] recently advanced this field by analyzing k -degree graphs under varying saturation constraints, where a k -degree graph G is t -saturated if every vertex of degree k has at most t neighbors of degree k . Their results are as follows: for 0 -saturated k -degree graphs, G admits a ( 1 k − 1 , 3 ) -packing coloring; when saturation increases to 1 ≤ t ≤ k − 2 , it transitions to ( 1 k − 1 , 2 ) -packing colorability. Further, ( k − 1 ) -saturated k -degree graphs ( k ≥ 4 ) require a ( 1 k − 1 , 2 k − 1 ) -packing coloring, while all k -degree graphs ( k ≥ 3 ) universally satisfy ( 1 k − 1 , 2 k ) -packing colorability. These findings not only generalize prior work on subcubic graphs but also offer a unified framework for analyzing degree-constrained graph colorability. A Halin graph is a plane graph constructed from a tree T (called characteristic tree ) without vertices of degree two by connecting all leaves through a cycle C (called adjoint cycle ). Halin graphs exhibit distinct S -packing colorability patterns. Tarhini and Togni [ 29 ] proved cubic Halin graphs are ( 1 2 , 2 , 3 ) -packing colorable, while Wang, Hu, and Wang [ 32 ] showed 5-degree Halin graphs require ( 2 6 ) -packing colorings due to their square graphs’ chromatic number 6. We strengthen this bound in this paper, proving that 5-degree Halin graphs actually admit ( 1 2 , 2 3 ) -packing colorings, improving the state-of-the-art for this graph class. It is worth noting that the wheel graph W 5 (with maximum degree 5 ) is a Halin graph that does not admit a ( 1 , 2 3 ) -packing coloring. Theorem 1.2 . Halin graphs with maximum degree at most 5 are ( 1 2 , 2 3 ) -packing colorable. The proof of Theorem 1.2 is algorithmic: it provides an explicit procedure to construct the desired ( 1 2 , 2 3 ) -packing coloring in O ⁡ ( n ) time, where n denotes the number of vertices in the Halin graph. The algorithm is introduced in Section 2 and verified in Section 3 . The PACKING-COLORING algorithm begins by properly coloring the characteristic tree T using two colors, 1 and 1 ′ . Upon reaching line 5 , the algorithm proceeds to recolor all vertices on the adjoint cycles according to two distinct cases. In each case, when a vertex a k from the set { 1 , 1 ′ } is recolored using either the CASE-1 or CASE-2 algorithm, the color chosen is distinct from the one currently assigned to b k . Throughout the execution of the algorithms CASE-1 , CASE-2 , and RECOLORING , no two consecutive vertices in the adjoint cycle C are assigned the same color. It is noteworthy that, in the CASE-1 algorithm, vertex a 1 is reassigned the color 1 1 , while vertex a n is always reassigned the color 1 n . Given that the original colors of a 1 and a n , as determined by lines 5 – 5 of the PACKING-COLORING algorithm and lines 1 – 1 of the RECOLORING algorithm, are distinct, it follows that φ ⁡ ( b 1 ) ≠ φ ⁡ ( b n ) and thus 1 1 ≠ 1 n . Consequently, the vertices colored with 1 (or 1 ′ ) induce an independent set. Given that the CONFLICTS-RESOLVING algorithm does not introduce any new vertices colored with 1 or 1 ′ , the conclusion that vertices colored with 1 (or 1 ′ ) induce an independent set remains valid even after executing line 5 . ∎ A 2-independent set is defined as a vertex subset in which the distance between any pair of vertices is at least 3. The PACKING-COLORING algorithm operates in linear time, i.e., it has a time complexity of O ⁡ ( | G | ) . First, the 2-coloring process applied to the characteristic tree T using a Depth-First Search (BFS) traversal operates in O ⁡ ( t ) time, where t represents the number of vertices in T . The RECOLORING algorithm, which encompasses the CASE-1 and CASE-2 subroutines, processes each case in O ⁡ ( n ) time, with n being the number of vertices in the adjoint cycle C . The CONFLICTS-RESOLVING algorithm, crucial for resolving color conflicts, also operates within O ⁡ ( n ) time. This is because constructing the set ℒ and the dictionary 𝒩 each take O ⁡ ( n ) time. The conflict resolution loop iterates over each even index i from 1 to n . For each even i , the algorithm computes the set J . Given that the size of J is roughly bounded by 4 (note that 𝒩 ⁡ [ b j ] has at most 5 vertices because the maximum degree of G is at most 5), the inner loop iterating over J runs in constant time O ⁡ ( 1 ) , as it performs at most 4 iterations. Therefore, the entire conflict resolution loop retains a linear time complexity of O ⁡ ( n ) , since the outer loop is O ⁡ ( n ) and the inner loop is O ⁡ ( 1 ) . Consequently, the overall time complexity of the PACKING-COLORING algorithm is O ⁡ ( t + n ) = O ⁡ ( | G | ) . ∎ 4 Open questions When applying the PACKING-COLORING algorithm to color a Halin graph G with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 5 , we observe that color 2 c is assigned to relatively few vertices. This observation leads us to investigate whether the algorithm can be modified to ensure that the minimum distance between any two vertices colored with 2 c exceeds the current constraint. We formalize this inquiry as follows: Question 4.1 . Are Halin graphs with maximum degree at most 5 ( 1 2 , 2 2 , 3 ) -packing colorable? In the PACKING-COLORING algorithm, all non-leaf vertices of the characteristic tree of a Halin graph G are colored 1 or 1 ′ . This actually gives a special ( 1 2 , 2 3 ) -packing coloring of G . Perhaps the following question is interesting: Question 4.2 . Let G be a Halin graph with maximum degree Δ , where T is the characteristic tree and C is the adjoint cycle. What is the minimum ℓ := ℓ ⁡ ( Δ ) such that G admits a ( 1 2 , 2 ℓ ) -packing coloring where all vertices in V ⁡ ( T ) ∖ V ⁡ ( C ) (the internal vertices of T not on C ) are colored with the first two colors labeled by 1 in the sequence? For Halin graphs with maximum degree Δ ≥ 6 , we pose the following fundamental problem in packing coloring theory: Question 4.3 . Determine the optimal parameters ( k , ℓ ) such that every Halin graph G with maximum degree Δ admits a ( 1 k , 2 ℓ ) -packing coloring. Specifically, how does the feasible region of ( k , ℓ ) expand as Δ increases?
