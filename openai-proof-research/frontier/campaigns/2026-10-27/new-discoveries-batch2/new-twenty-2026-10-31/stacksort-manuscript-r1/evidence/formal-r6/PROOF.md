The tagged deletion construction uses an explicit finite set. Its outer tag contains the two child arities, each in range 3. The next component is a literal reduced collision with arities r-1+t and s-1+u. Its fibre is the product of childChoices on the last t and u words. Erasing the tags takes the first r-1 and s-1 words and retains the oriented children. Tuple membership proves that these prefixes have the required lengths. Conversely a ReducedPairs element determines both arities, both full reduced tuples, and the two child constructors. The take/drop identities give inverse maps. The 1,2,1 cardinality theorem for childChoices then identifies the sum with the exact frozen deletion XI expression. No irreducibility condition is imposed on deletion.

The fixed-maximum construction reuses the complete 5A pieces/glue bijection. A piece contributes precisely when its suffix label subset contains M-1. If the maximum belongs to the suffix, the prescribed labels bound every suffix entry by M-1, and irreducible_maximum_last puts it at the final output position. Disjoint prefix labels exclude an earlier occurrence. Conversely, if the glued output ends at the maximum and the suffix omitted it, it would occur in the prefix. Its index would then be smaller than the prefix output length, contradicting the positive suffix output length. Thus restriction of the frozen bijection gives the exact maximum-final reducible family.

The maximum-final collision family partitions into all irreducibles and the maximum-final reducibles. T2 shows every full irreducible belongs to the maximum-final family. Summing the restricted pieces first by their three natural indices and then by their label subset gives choose(M-1,b-1): subsets of range M with size b containing {M-1}. The prescribed-label fibre cardinalities are the inherited XI and II counts. The frozen clipping proofs apply termwise in exactly the same dependency cone as 5A; only the shuffle coefficient changes.
