3. **Routine.** Write down the shortest proof you can find from published theorems and from the source's own lemmas, formulas and tables. 4. **Correctness of the written proof.** Check every step of the proof in `PAPER.md` (and the proof lane's file where the paper is terse). Test each lemma by your own exact computation where cheap. Check the small and boundary cases of the stated range.
5. **Lean definitions against the source.** Compare every Lean definition used by the final endpoint, one by one, with the source's definition, and the endpoint statement with the conjecture as printed. Look for: a simplified object standing in for the source's, a missing or extra hypothesis, a statement that holds vacuously, default values doing the work (0 for an empty infimum, division by zero, truncated subtraction on naturals), a quantifier in the wrong place, a bridge to the source's object that is proved only on paper. Where cheap, transcribe the Lean definitions into a short script and confirm on small cases that they compute what the source's definitions compute.

Every step must follow, every number must be reproducible, nothing may be claimed that the Lean endpoint or the written proof does not support, and the significance paragraph must be honest about how modest the result is. Fix what is fixable by writing `PAPER-<id>-revised.md` in your folder: keep the mathematics and the Lean statements unchanged, do not touch the appendix, and state in the provenance paragraph that the proof, the Lean code and the first review are by Claude Opus 5.5 and the cross-model review is by you (name your model).

## Verdict
One of:
- `CONFIRMED_TIER1`: nothing public, the literal conjecture, correct, the Lean statement faithful, and your not-routine probability.
- `NOT_COUNTED`: public, routine, trivial, or not the stated problem. Give the citation or the short route.
- `FAIL`: first gap in the proof, or a Lean definition that does not match the source. Say whether it looks repairable and how.
- `BLOCKED`: name the paper and the exact question.

An unreadable journal version or citing paper (paywall, 403, bot challenge) is **not** a reason for BLOCKED and not a reason to stop. Record it under `caveats_for_public_wording` as "journal text unread" or "citing paper unread", with your probability that it settles the question (root lists it for the user to check by hand), use the latest arXiv version, the abstract and metadata of the journal record, and the citing papers, and carry on with every step. Use BLOCKED only when you have positive evidence that an unreadable text settles the question. The one-line synopsis in your task is root's shorthand and may be imprecise: the MathDB record and the source decide what the conjecture is.

## Root rulings (added 2026-11-23 after the first reviews came back): when to stop early and when not
Stop early only in two cases: the result is **public** (give the citation), or it is **routine** (write the short route out in full). In every other case finish all six steps, so that root can decide with everything in hand.
- **Unreadable journal version or citing paper**: a caveat, as said above. Finish all steps.
- **The printed statement fails at a degenerate or boundary case** (an isolated vertex, an empty word, n = 1 or 2, an equality clause, a missing "m != 0"), and the packet proves the statement outside that case. This is not by itself NOT_COUNTED. Root has counted such results before with an explicit caveat (for example "proved for trees with at least three vertices; the printed statement fails for K1 and K2, as does the source's own theorem") when all of these hold: (a) the authors plainly did not intend the degenerate case (their own examples, tables and theorems ignore it or need the same restriction); (b) the corrected statement is the real content of the conjecture, not a weakening chosen to make a proof possible; (c) the packet states the defect openly and, ideally, proves the failure of the printed statement at the degenerate case in Lean as well. So: report the defect, say whether (a), (b), (c) hold, and then assess the corrected statement fully (routine, correctness, Lean definitions, paper), giving the not-routine probability of the corrected statement. Give the verdict CONFIRMED_TIER1 with the caveat spelled out if you would defend "resolved, with this caveat" before the source's authors, and NOT_COUNTED if the degenerate failure is all there is, or if the corrected statement is not what the authors meant.
- **A link outside Lean** (the Lean endpoint is a combinatorial statement and the conjecture as printed follows from it by a theorem the source itself proves and prints): report it as a definition issue, check that the cited theorem is really proved in the source and really gives the printed statement, and say whether you accept "Lean endpoint plus the source's own theorem". Root has accepted that when the cited theorem is the source's own and the public wording names it.
