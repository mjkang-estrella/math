## Complete ordinary proof, checked independently

Take six distinct vertices a,b,p1,p2,p3,p4 and exactly four edges e1={a,p1}, e2={a,b,p2}, e3={a,b,p3}, e4={b,p4}. They are distinct, nonempty, of sizes 2,3,3,2. Each contains its own private pi, which belongs to no other edge. In fact no edge contains another, so even an additional clutter convention would be satisfied. No graph-only or uniform hypothesis is assumed.

For connectivity, a is adjacent in the incidence graph to e1,e2,e3; b is adjacent to e2,e3,e4. Thus b has a path to a via e2, all edge nodes have paths to a, and every pi has a path through ei. Every incidence node lies in this component. This is connectivity of the exact source graph, not just a drawing.

Let H'=(V',E') be ANY subhypergraph with V' nonempty, V' subset V and E' subset E, with each retained edge contained in V'. If E' is empty, any vertex in V' has degree zero. Otherwise select ei in E'. Then pi lies in V' and its only possible incident edge in E' is ei, which is present. Its degree is exactly one. This proves the universal degeneracy quantifier, including subhypergraphs with isolated vertices, not merely a leaf in H. An empty-edge, empty-vertex object is handled by the conventional nonempty qualifier stated above. Restricting subhypergraphs to connected ones would not invalidate this stronger assertion.

The degrees in H are d(a)=d(b)=3 and d(pi)=1. Suppose f:V→Z2 were cordial. The two vertex counts sum to six and differ by at most one, so both are three. Hence the sum of all vertex labels is three copies of 1, or 1 in Z2. The two edge counts sum to four and differ by at most one, so both are two. Hence the sum of all induced edge labels is 0 in Z2.

But finite incidence double counting gives sum_e sum_{v in e} f(v) = sum_v d(v) f(v). Every degree is odd, so the right side equals sum_v f(v)=1. The left side is the sum of induced edge labels, already 0. This contradiction excludes every labeling, without enumeration. Equivalently the expanded four edge sums contain a and b three times each and every pi once.

The candidate has the incidence cycle a,e2,b,e3,a, consisting of four distinct incidence nodes. Thus the two triples sharing {a,b} are allowed for Conjecture 20 and make it ineligible for the hypertree theorem. This is the source's Berge two-edge cycle. Its private pi in e2/e3 are not source-defined leaves: those edges each have two vertices of degree above one. The proof uses private degree-one vertices, not the stronger leaf-edge notion.

Adversarial convention checks: under induced traces, deleting all pi would leave the edge {a,b} from the triples, potentially with duplicate traces; other trace conventions change the target. No trace is used in this proof. Simplicity, min edge size, finiteness and even a clutter requirement are satisfied. A newly imposed linearity restriction would reject this witness, but that restriction is absent in the actual conjecture. No repair for a different linear conjecture is claimed. Neither disconnected-forest counterexamples nor optional isolated vertices establish the present connected endpoint by themselves.
