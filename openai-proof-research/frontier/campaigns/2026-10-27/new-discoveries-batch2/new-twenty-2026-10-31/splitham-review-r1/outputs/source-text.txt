Abstract
A graph G is \{F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{k}\}-free if G contains no induced subgraph isomorphic to any F_{i} (1\leq i\leq k). A connected graph G is a split graph if its vertex set can be partitioned into a clique and an independent set. Ryjáček et al. [J. Comb. Theory, Ser. B 134 (2019) 239–263] conjectured that every 4-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graph with minimum degree at least 6 is Hamiltonian and they confirmed the case with connectivity at least 5, where K_{1,4}+e is the graph obtained from K_{1,4} by adding a new edge.
In this paper, we show that
every 3-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph of order at least 13 is Hamilton-connected.
It implies that Ryjáček et al.’s conjecture holds
for split graphs of order at least 13.
Keywords: \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graphs, Hamilton-connected, Split graphs, I-cover
1 Introduction 
In this paper, we consider only connected simple graphs and refer the reader to [1] for undefined notation and terminology.
For any graph G,
let V(G) and E(G) denote
its vertex set and edge set,
respectively.
A graph G is \{F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{k}\}-free if G contains no induced subgraph isomorphic to any F_{i} (i\in[k]),
where [k]=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}.
We call a graph G Hamiltonian if it contains a spanning cycle.
A path in a graph G
with endpoints u and v is called a (u,v)-path.
A path in a graph G is called a Hamiltonian (u,v)-path if it is a spanning (u,v)-path.
A graph G is Hamilton-connected, if for
each pair of vertices u,v\in V(G), there exists a
Hamiltonian (u,v)-path in G.
Obviously, a Hamilton-connected
graph is Hamiltonian.
A graph G is pancyclic if it contains a cycle of all lengths from 3 to |V(G)|. A connected graph G is a split graph if its vertex set can be partitioned into a clique and an independent set (either of which may be empty). Split graphs were introduced by Foldes and Hammer [6] in 1977 and have been further studied in [12, 15, 3, 14, 19, 8, 7].
Theorem 1.1.
(Renjith and Sadagopan [15])
Let G be a K_{1,3}-free split graph. Then G is Hamiltonian if and only if G is 2-connected.
Dai et al. [4]
further extended the above result.
Theorem 1.2.
(Dai et al. [4])
Let G be a K_{1,3}-free split graph. Then G is pancyclic if and only if G is 2-connected.
Theorem 1.3.
(Dai et al. [4])
If G is a 3-connected K_{1,4}-free split graph, then G is Hamiltonian.
Recently, Liu et al. [12] explored the Hamilton-connectedness of r-connected K_{1,r}-free split graphs, where r\in\{3,4\}.
Theorem 1.4.
(Liu et al. [12]) 
For any r\in\{3,4\}, if
G is a r-connected K_{1,r}-free split graph,
then G is Hamilton-connected.
(a)          (b)
          (c)      
Fig. 1: The graphs K_{1,3},K_{1,4}~\text{and}~K_{1,4}+e.
Let r\geq 3 be an integer and let K_{1,r+1}+e be the graph obtained from K_{1,r+1} by
adding an edge joining
two nonadjacent vertices in K_{1,r+1}.
An example is shown in
Fig. 1 (c). The purpose of this paper is to study the Hamilton-connectedness of \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graphs. Obviously, a K_{1,3}-free graph is also a \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graph, but the reverse is not true. The Hamiltonicity of \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graphs was first studied by Li and Schelp [10] in 2002.
Theorem 1.5.
(Li and Schelp [10]) 
Let G be a 3-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graph of order n\geq 30. If \delta(G)\geq(n+5)/5, then G is Hamiltonian.
Later, Li [9] considered the Hamiltonicity of 2-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graphs.
Theorem 1.6.
(Li [9]) 
Let G be a 2-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graph of order n\geq 13. If \delta(G)\geq n/4, then G is Hamiltonian or G\in\mathcal{F}, where \mathcal{F} is a family of non-Hamiltonian graphs of connectivity 2.
Ryjáček et al. [17] proved that the minimum degree condition can be significantly decreased to 6 if the connectivity is increased to at least 5 when considering the Hamiltonicity of \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graphs.
Theorem 1.7.
(Ryjáček et al. [17]) 
Every 5-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graph with minimum degree at least 6 is Hamiltonian.
Ryjáček et al. [17] also posed the following two equivalent conjectures.
Conjecture 1.8.
(Ryjáček et al. [17]) 
Every 4-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graph with minimum degree at least 6 is Hamiltonian.
Conjecture 1.9.
(Ryjáček et al. [17]) 
Every 4-connected line graph with minimum degree at least 5 is Hamiltonian.
Obviously, Conjecture 1.9 is a weaker version of  Thomassen’s conjecture [18], which states that every 4-connected line graph is Hamiltonian. Ryjáček [16] showed that Thomassen’s conjecture is equivalent to the Matthews-Sumner’s conjecture [13], which asserts that every 4-connected K_{1,3}-free graph is Hamiltonian. For more information on equivalent conjectures, we refer the reader to [2].
For the traceability of \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free graphs, we refer the reader to [5, 11, 20].
There exists an infinite family of
r-connected K_{1,r+1}-free split graphs, each of which is not
Hamilton-connected [12].
In this article, we focus on
\{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free
split graphs and establish
the following result on their
Hamilton-connectedness.
Theorem 1.10.
If G is a 3-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph of order at least 13, then G is Hamilton-connected.
Obviously, Theorem 1.10 also extends Theorem 1.4
due to Liu, Song, Zhan and Lai.
It is because there exist infinite many 3-connected
K_{1,4}-free
split graphs that are not Hamilton-connected.
The remainder of this manuscript is organized as follows. In Section 2,
we mainly introduce some
known results
due to Liu et al. [12].
In Section 3, we provide
two forbidden subgraphs
of a split graph G
which is \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free.
In Section 4, we show that
a 3-connected split graph G which is \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free contains
an I-cover
for any split partition (S,I).
The proof of Theorem 1.10 is given in Section 5.
Finally, in Section 6, we pose an open problem.
2 Preliminary
In this section, we present some useful lemmas.
For two graphs G_{1} and G_{2}, let G_{1}\cup G_{2} be a graph with vertex set V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2}) and edge set E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2}).
For any X\subseteq V(G),
let G[X] denote
the subgraph
of G induced by X,
where X\neq\emptyset,
and let G-X=G[V(G)\setminus X], where X\neq V(G).
Similarly,
for any Y\subseteq E(G),
let G[Y] denote the spanning
subgraph of G with edge set Y, and let
G-Y=G[E(G)\setminus Y].
For a subgraph H of G with E(H)\cap Y=\emptyset,
we write H+Y for H\cup G[Y].
Let G be a split graph with V(G)=S\cup I, where S
is a maximum clique of G and I is an independent set of G. Following Liu et al. [12], we call such an ordered pair (S,I) a split partition of G. Denote s=|S|.
Then, G[S]\cong K_{s} and G[I]\cong|I|K_{1}.
Since any complete graph of order at least three is Hamilton-connected, we may assume that |I|>0. Let I=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{|I|}\}. Then N_{G}(v)\subseteq S for any v\in I. If \kappa(G)\geq k, then s\geq k. By the maximality of s, s\geq k+1.
A path P=a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{2t+1} in G is called
an (S,I)-alternating path
if a_{1}\neq a_{2t+1}
and V(P)\cap S=\{a_{1},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2t+1}\}.
Let End(P)=\{a_{1},a_{2t+1}\} and
P^{o}=V(P)\setminus End(P) be the interior of the path P.
Similarly,
a cycle C=a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{2t+1} in G is called
an (S,I)-alternating cycle
where a_{1}=a_{2t+1},
if V(P)\cap S=\{a_{1},a_{3},\ldots,a_{2t+1}\}.
We call a collection \mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{h}\} of (S,I)-alternating paths in G an I-cover if the following conditions hold:
(A) 
V(P_{i})\cap V(P_{j})=\emptyset for any i,j\in[h] and i\neq j; and
(B) 
I\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}P_{i}^{o}.
For a collection {\mathcal{P}}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{h}\} of (S,I)-alternating paths,
denote by End(\mathcal{P}) the union of End(P_{i})’s
over all path P_{i} in {\mathcal{P}}.
Let Inn({\mathcal{P}})=(\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}P_{i}^{o})\cap S. For convenience, we also use \mathcal{P} to denote the subgraph \bigcup_{i=1}^{h}P_{i}.
Lemma 2.1.
(Liu et al. [12]) 
Let G be a split graph with a split partition (S,I) and an I-cover \mathcal{P}, and let u,v be any two distinct vertices in
V(G)\setminus Inn(\mathcal{P}). If u,v are not the endpoints of an
(S,I)-alternating path in \mathcal{P}, then G has a Hamiltonian
(u,v)-path.
Let G be a split graph with a split partition (S,I).
We call a collection \mathcal{Q}=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{h_{1}},C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{h_{2}}\} of (S,I)-alternating subgraphs in G a pseudo I-cover if the following conditions hold:
(C) 
each P_{i} is an (S,I)-alternating path and each C_{j} is an (S,I)-alternating cycle; and
(D) 
I\subseteq(\bigcup_{i=1}^{h_{1}}P_{i}^{o})\cup(\bigcup_{j=1}^{h_{2}}V(C_{j})).
For any pseudo I-cover {\mathcal{Q}}, let
End(\mathcal{Q}) denote the collection of the endpoints of all alternating paths in {\mathcal{Q}}, and let Inn(\mathcal{Q})=(\bigcup_{i=1}^{h_{1}}P_{i}^{o})\cap S.
For convenience, we also use \mathcal{Q} to denote the subgraph (\bigcup_{i=1}^{h_{1}}P_{i})\cup(\bigcup_{j=1}^{h_{2}}C_{j}).
Lemma 2.2.
(Liu et al. [12]) 
Let r\geq 2 be an integer, and let G be an r-connected K_{1,r+1}-free split graph with a split partition (S,I). Then G contains a pseudo I-cover.
3 Forbidden subgraphs
Let G be a split graph
with a split partition (S,I).
The next two lemmas show that
if G is \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free,
then G does not contain
any subgraph H
with |V(H)\cap S|=i+1
and H\cong G_{i},
where 1\leq i\leq 2
and
G_{1} are G_{2} are the graphs shown in Fig. 2.
These two conclusions
will be used in later proofs.
Fig. 2: 
V(G_{1})\cap S=\{u,v\}
and V(G_{2})\cap S=\{u,v,w\}.
For any two graphs H_{1} and H_{2}, let H_{1}\cup_{0}H_{2}
denote the vertex-disjoint union of H_{1} and H_{2}.
For any integer k, let kH_{1}
denote the vertex-disjoint union of k copies of H_{1}.
Lemma 3.1.
Let G be a \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph with a split partition (S,I).
Then, G does not have
a subgraph H
such that |V(H)\cap S|=2
and H\cong K_{1,3}\cup_{0}K_{1,2}.
Proof. Suppose that G contains
an induced subgraph H
such that |V(H)\cap S|=2
and H\cong K_{1,3}\cup_{0}K_{1,2}.
We may assume that
V(H)=\{u,v,a,b,c,x,y\},
with V(H)\cap S=\{u,v\},
and
E(H)=\{ua,ub,uc,vx,vy\},
as shown in Fig. 2 (a).
Since u,v\in S, we have uv\in E(G).
Then, no matter whether
N(v)\cap\{a,b,c\}=\emptyset
or N(v)\cap\{a,b,c\}\neq\emptyset,
G[V(H)] contains an induced
subgraph
which is isomorphic to
K_{1,4} or K_{1,4}+e,
a contradiction to the assumption.
Hence the result holds.
 \Box
Lemma 3.2.
Let G be a \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph with a split partition (S,I).
Then, G does not have
a subgraph H
such that |V(H)\cap S|=3
and H\cong 3K_{1,2}.
Proof. Suppose that G contains
an induced subgraph H
such that |V(H)\cap S|=3
and H\cong 3K_{1,2}.
We may assume that
V(H)=\{u,v,w,a,b,c,d,x,y\},
with V(H)\cap S=\{u,v,w\},
and
E(H)=\{ua,ub,vc,vd,wx,wy\},
as shown in Fig. 2 (b).
By Lemma 3.1,
we have N(u)\cap\{c,d,x,y\}=\emptyset.
Similarly, N(v)\cap\{a,b,x,y\}=\emptyset
and
N(w)\cap\{a,b,c,d\}=\emptyset.
Since u,v,w\in S, we have
G[\{u,v,w\}]\cong K_{3}.
Thus, G[V(H)] has an induced
subgraph isomorphic to K_{1,4}+e,
a contradiction to the assumption.
Hence the result holds.
 \Box
4 Existence of an I-cover
Let G be a split graph
with a split partition (S,I).
By Lemma 2.2, G has a pseudo I-cover
\mathcal{Q}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{h_{1}},C_{1},\ldots,C_{h_{2}}\}.
By Lemma 3.2, the number of (S,I)-alternating cycles
in {\mathcal{Q}}
is at most two, i.e., h_{2}\leq 2.
In the section, we will further show that if |V(G)|\geq 9,
such a pseudo I-cover Q
with h_{2}=0 exists,
i.e., an I-cover exists.
Lemma 4.1.
Let G be a 3-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph with |V(G)|\geq 9
and a split partition (S,I).
Then G contains an I-cover.
Proof. The proof is by contradiction. Suppose that G has no I-cover. Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{h_{1}},C_{1},\ldots,C_{h_{2}}\} be a pseudo I-cover such that
(1) 
h_{2} is minimized; and
(2) 
subject to (1), h_{1} is minimized.
Let {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{h_{1}}\}
and {\mathcal{Q}}_{2}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{h_{2}}\}.
By the assumption on G, h_{2}\geq 1. Then, by Lemma 3.2,
we have h_{2}\leq 2;
otherwise, G contains a
subgraph H with the properties that
|V(H)\cap S|=3 and H\cong 3K_{1,2},
contradicting Lemma 3.2.
Let S_{1}=S\cap V(\mathcal{Q}) and S_{2}=S\setminus S_{1}.
We first prove the following claim.
Claim 1.
For any 1\leq i\leq h_{2} and
x\in I\cap V(C_{i}), N_{G}(x)\cap(S_{2}\cup End(\mathcal{Q}))=\emptyset. So, N_{G}(x)\subseteq S_{1}\setminus End(\mathcal{Q}).
Fig. 3: For the proof Claim 1.
Proof. If
y\in N(x)\cap S_{2},
then C_{i} can be replaced by
a new alternating path P^{\prime}
with the property that V(P^{\prime})=V(C_{i})\cup\{y\},
as shown in Fig. 3 (a),
and a new pseudo I-cover
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime} can be obtained by
replacing C_{i} by P^{\prime},
a contradiction to
the minimality of h_{2}.
Similarly, if
y\in N(x)\cap End(P_{j}),
where 1\leq j\leq h_{1},
then C_{i} and P_{j}
can be replaced by
a new alternating path P^{\prime}
with the property that V(P^{\prime})=V(C_{i})\cup V(P_{j}),
as shown in Fig. 3 (b),
and a new pseudo I-cover
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime} can be obtained by
replacing C_{i} and P_{j}
by P^{\prime},
a contradiction to
the minimality of h_{2}.
Hence Claim 1 holds.

Applying the conclusion of
Claim 1,
the following claim can be proved similarly as Claim 2 of Lemma 4.2

Claim 2.
S_{2}=\emptyset.
Claim 3.
If {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\neq\emptyset, then
for each x\in I\cap V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2}),
|N_{G}(x)\cap(S\cap V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2}))|=2,
implying that
N_{G}(x)\cap(P^{o}_{j}\cap S)\neq\emptyset for some P_{j}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}.
Proof. Assume that {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}\neq\emptyset.
Suppose there exists
x\in I\cap V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2}),
such that |N_{G}(x)\cap(S\cap V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2}))|\neq 2.
Then,
x is adjacent to some
vertex y\in S which is on some cycle C_{r} in {\mathcal{Q}}_{2},
but xy is not an edge in
C_{r}.
Let u\in End(P_{1}).
As y,u\in S,
we have yu\in E(G).
Let x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime} be the two neighbors of y on cycle C_{r}.
By Claim 1,
\{x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\}\cap N_{G}(u)=\emptyset.
Then, the subgraph induced by
\{y,x,x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime},u\} is isomorphic to K_{1,4}, contradicting the assumption of G.
Thus, |N_{G}(x)\cap(S\cap V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2}))|=2.
Since \delta(G)\geq 3,
we have N_{G}(x)\cap(V({\mathcal{Q}}_{1})\cap S)\neq\emptyset.
By Claim 1,
N_{G}(x)\cap(P^{o}_{j}\cap S)\neq\emptyset for some P_{j}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}.
Hence Claim 3 holds.

It is known that 1\leq h_{2}\leq 2.
Now we are going to prove the following claim.
Claim 4.
h_{2}=1.
Proof. Suppose that h_{2}=2.
By Lemma 3.2,
P_{i} has length 2 for
each P_{i}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1},
i.e., P^{o}_{i}=\emptyset
for each P_{i}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}.
Since \delta(G)\geq 3, by Claim 3,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}=\emptyset.
For j=1,2,
let C_{j} be the alternating cycle a_{j,1}b_{j,1}\ldots a_{j,k_{j}}b_{j,k_{j}}a_{j,1},
where a_{j,1},a_{j,2},
\ldots,a_{j,k_{j}}\in S.
Without loss of generality, we assume that k_{1}\geq k_{2}\geq 2.
Then, by Lemma 3.2, k_{1}\leq 3.
Suppose that k_{1}=3.
Since d_{G}(b_{1,1})\geq 3,
b_{1,1} is adjacent to some
vertex a_{i,j} in V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2})\cap S, where
i\in[2], but b_{1,1}a_{i,j}
is not an edge on cycle C_{i}.
Then, it is noticed that G has
a subgraph H with the property that |V(H)\cap S|=2
and H\cong K_{1,3}\cup_{0}K_{1,2}, a contradiction to Lemma 3.1.
Hence k_{1}=2.
By Claim 2, S_{2}=\emptyset.
Since {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}=\emptyset,
we have V(G)=V({\mathcal{Q}}_{2}),
implying that |V(G)|=|V(C_{1})|+|V(C_{2})|=8,
contradicting the assumption that |V(G)|\geq 9.
Hence Claim 4 holds.

a_{i}\in S and b_{i}\in I

Let’s now prove that {\mathcal{Q}}_{1}=\emptyset.
Claim 5.
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}=\emptyset.

We first show that k3,
N_{G}(b_{1})\cap\{a_{i}:i\in[k]\}=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}

By Lemma 4.1,
we may now assume that
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}\} is an I-cover of a 3-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph of order at least 9. Denote by t_{i} the length of path P_{i}.
Clearly, each t_{i} is even and t_{i}\geq 2.
In the following, we further show that each t_{i} is no more than 6 if the order of G is at least 11.
Lemma 4.2.
Let G be a 3-connected \{K_{1,4},K_{1,4}+e\}-free split graph with a split partition (S,I). If |V(G)|\geq 11, then G has an I-cover
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}\}
such that each path is of length
at most 6.
Proof. By Lemma 4.1, we may choose an I-cover \mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{h}\}
whose lengths are t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{h}, respectively, such that
t_{1}\geq t_{2}\geq\cdots\geq t_{h}\geq 2 and
(1) 
h is maximized;
(2) 
subject to (1), t_{1} is minimized;
(3) 
subject to (1) and (2), |\{j|t_{j}=t_{1},2\leq j\leq h\}| is minimized, i.e., the number of paths in \mathcal{P} with length t_{1} is minimized.
Let S_{1}=(\bigcup_{i=1}^{h}V(P_{i}))\cap S and S_{2}=S\setminus S_{1}.
Claim 1.
For i\in[h],
if t_{i}\geq 4, then
