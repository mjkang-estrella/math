648:     The class of matroids known as the strongly pigeonhole class is closely related to this study.
649: For a matroid M and S ⊆ E(M ), define the equivalence relation ∼M,S on 2S as follows. For
650: X1 , X2 ⊆ S, the sets X1 and X2 are equivalent, written X1 ∼M,S X2 , if and only if the indepen-
651: dence of X1 ∪ Y and X2 ∪ Y are equivalent in M for all Y ⊆ E(M ) \ S. A class M of matroids
652: is called strongly pigeonhole [13, Definition 2.10] if there is a function f : N \ {0} → N \ {0} such
653: that, for any matroid M ∈ M, positive integer λ′ , and S ⊆ E(M ) with λM (S) ≤ λ′ , the size
654: of the quotient set by ∼M,S is always at most f (λ′ ). Thus, if a class M of matroids is strongly
655: pigeonhole, then the width of BDDs/ZDDs for a matroid M ∈ M can be bounded by a value
656: depending only on the connectivity function of M . Funk et al. [14, Proposition 10 and Theorems
657: 18, 22, and 34] showed that several classes of matroids are strongly pigeonhole such as funda-
658: mental transversal matroids, 3-connected bicircular matroids, and 3-connected H-gain-graphic
659: matroids for a finite group H.
