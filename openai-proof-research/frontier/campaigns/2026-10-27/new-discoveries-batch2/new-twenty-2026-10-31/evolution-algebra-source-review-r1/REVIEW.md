## 337518-MATH — PASS, adversarial ordinary verification

1. **Positive forms and zero propagation.** Let L be coordinate sum in the target. L(xy) is sum_j d_j x_j y_j for target A, and sum_j x_j y_j for target B. Both are positive definite on real vectors since every d_j>0. Images t_i of distinct source basis vectors have product zero and are therefore orthogonal for this form. If t_i=0, multiplicativity gives 0=sum_{j~i} c_i t_j, with c_i=1 or 1/d_i according to the source. Pairing with t_j gives c_i||t_j||²=0; every neighbor has zero image. Propagation along finite paths proves all images vanish. A nonzero map thus has n nonzero pairwise orthogonal images in dimension n and is bijective. If x=f(u), y=f(v), f^{-1}(xy)=uv, so its inverse is multiplicative. No inverse adjacency matrix, determinant assumption, entrywise sign restriction or cancellation claim is used. This checks both homomorphism directions directly.

2. **Diagonal orientation and all-coordinate support.** For an isomorphism f:A→B put T_ij=(f(e_i))_j, r_i=||t_i||_2>0, R=diag(r_i) and O=R^{-1}T. Orthogonality gives OO^t=I; finite squareness gives O^tO=I. For every output coordinate k, D_k=diag_j(a_jk/d_j). Off-diagonal entries of T D_k T^t vanish because they are the k-coordinates of t_i t_h for i≠h. Hence O D_k O^t=Lambda_k is diagonal, and right multiplication by O gives O D_k=Lambda_k O. Entry (i,j), for every k, implies O_ij≠0 ⇒ a_jk/d_j=(Lambda_k)_ii. This uses rows of normalized adjacency, not columns or a single eigenvalue. Two support indices have identical entire normalized rows; nonempty equal supports imply equal neighborhoods and equal degrees. They belong to one twin class. This is valid even if adjacency is singular. It does not assert individual products T_ij T_hj vanish.

3. **Both directions of twin correspondence.** Every image row is nonzero, so its target class C(i) exists. Its square is (r_i²/d_C(i))1_N(C(i)). If C(i)=C(h), applying f^{-1} to normalized squares gives e_i²/r_i²=e_h²/r_h². Nonempty 0/1 adjacency vectors then force equal neighborhoods and r_i=r_h. Conversely source twins have equal squares; equality of the displayed positive multiples forces equal target neighborhoods. By the definition of maximal twin classes this means C(i)=C(h), with equal degree and row norm. Invertibility also forbids an unused target coordinate class: otherwise those columns are zero. Thus this is a bijection of whole twin classes, not just a map on some supports.

4. **Class cardinalities.** All rows mapped to a target class C′ are exactly the rows from its corresponding source class C. They are independent and supported in R^{C′}, so |C|≤|C′|. Because all other rows are supported outside C′, projection of their total span onto R^{C′} shows these rows span R^{C′}, giving the reverse inequality. Hence |C|=|C′|. No equality of total numbers of classes substitutes for this dimension argument.

5. **Quotient graph and degrees.** Distinct twins cannot be adjacent in a simple loopless graph, and an edge between two twin classes implies all interclass edges. For i∈C the square equation is sum_{j~i}t_j=(r_C²/d_C′)1_N(C′). Project onto D′. If C is adjacent to D, the left side is sum_{j∈D}t_j; otherwise it is zero. This row sum cannot vanish: its squared Euclidean norm is |D|r_D²>0. The right projection is nonzero iff C′ is adjacent to D′. This proves quotient adjacency in both directions. The quotient alone does not preserve degrees; use the just-proved cardinalities: d_C=sum_{D~C}|D|=sum_{D′~C′}|D′|=d_C′.

6. **Block norms and signs.** On an adjacent D′ the equation becomes sum_{j∈D}t_j=(r_C²/d_C)1_D′. The squared norm identity gives |D|r_D²=|D′|r_C^4/d_C². The nonzero equal cardinalities cancel, and positivity gives r_D=r_C²/d_C, not its negative. Apply the same identity with C,D reversed: r_C=r_D²/d_D. Thus all neighbor classes D of a fixed C have the same norm and the same degree d_D=r_D²/r_C. Coordinates of individual rows may be negative; only orthogonal squared norms and positive scalar coefficients were used.

7. **Even walks and sufficiency.** Any length-two vertex walk has endpoints of the same degree; if endpoints are twins this already follows from their identical neighborhoods. Iteration proves equality along all even walks, including repeated vertices. In a connected bipartite graph the two sides are the even-walk classes. In a connected graph with an odd cycle, travel to the cycle, traverse it and return to obtain an odd closed walk; insert it to change the parity of a walk between any two vertices. Every pair is then joined by an even walk, hence all degrees are equal. The constructions in the held proof have the correct orientation A→B: r=d in the regular case, r_1³=d_1²d_2, r_2³=d_1d_2² in the biregular case. Strictly positive real cube roots satisfy r_2=r_1²/d_1 and r_1=r_2²/d_2. Diagonal scaling verifies both diagonal and off-diagonal basis products and is invertible. Its inverse gives B→A scaling by 1/r_s, satisfying (1/r_D)/d_C=(1/r_C)² on edges. Any nonzero B→A map is invertible by item 1, so applying items 2–7 to its inverse proves necessity for that direction as well. No independent unproved reverse-direction twin argument is needed.

No substantive ordinary gap was found in these seven steps. This is a mathematical review, not a kernel check or formal certificate.
