                                                                                                1. Introduction
                                             A subset B of a group G is called a difference basis for a subset A ⊂ G if each element a ∈ A can be written
                                          as a = xy −1 for some x, y ∈ B. If the group operation of G is denoted by +, then the element xy −1 is written
                                          as the difference x − y (which justifies the choice of the terminology).
                                             The smallest cardinality of a difference basis for A ⊂ G is called the difference size of A and is denoted
                                          by ∆[A]. For example, the set {0, 1, 4, 6} is a difference basis for the interval A = [−6, 6] ∩ Z witnessing
                                          that ∆[A] ≤ 4. In Proposition 2.2(4) we shall prove that the difference size is subadditive in the sense that
                                          ∆[A ∪ B] < ∆[A] + ∆[B] for any non-empty subsets A, B of a group G.
                                             The definition of a difference basis B for a set A in a group G implies that |A| ≤ |B|2 and hence ∆[A] ≥ |A|.
