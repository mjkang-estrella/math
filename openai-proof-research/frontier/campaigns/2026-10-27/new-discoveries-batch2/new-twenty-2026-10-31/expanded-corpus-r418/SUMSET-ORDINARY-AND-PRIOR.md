For k=1, F_1(q) is exactly the minimum size of a cyclic difference basis. Here is a complete personally derived proof F_1(20)=6>5=F_1(21).

If a set A modulo20 has at most four elements, it gives at most |A|(|A|-1)<=12 nonzero ordered differences, less than19. For five elements let a be the number of even representatives. There are exactly 2a(5-a) ordered pairs with odd difference. The ten odd residues each need a representation. For a=0,1,4,5 this count is <=8, impossible. For a=2 or3 it is12; since the total number of distinct ordered non-diagonal pairs is20, only eight even nonzero differences can then be represented. There are nine even nonzero residues, again impossible. Therefore size five is impossible. This is a parity counting proof of nonexistence for ALL five-element sets, not an exhaustive computation.

The six-element set {0,1,4,6,9,11} modulo20 covers all differences. From the five-element subset {0,1,4,9,11}, differences up to sign have least absolute representatives 1,4,9,9,3,8,10,5,7,2, coming from the pairs in increasing lexicographic order (0,1),(0,4),(0,9),(0,11),(1,4),(1,9),(1,11),(4,9),(4,11),(9,11). These cover 1,2,3,4,5,7,8,9,10 and their negatives; adding6 supplies6 and-6. Zero is a self-difference. Hence F_1(20)=6.

Modulo21 use {0,1,4,14,16}. The ten positive differences in the same pair order are 1,4,14,16,3,13,15,10,12,2. Up to sign modulo21 their least positive magnitudes are 1,4,7,5,3,8,6,10,9,2, precisely1 through10. Their negatives plus zero cover all21 residues. Four elements give at most13 total differences, so F_1(21)=5. This pays the literal universal negation without assuming a computational minimum.
