## 322552: arbitrary unicyclic G and EVERY spanning tree T

Source 1111.3513v2 pp1-2 defines finite simple connected graphs, distance to a nonempty part as its minimum distance, and partition dimension as the minimum number of parts with injective distance vectors. Conjecture10 p11 is exactly pd(G)<=pd(T)+1. The actual final Bulletin57(105)(2014) pp381-391 retains it as Conjecture1 p389 and attributes a +3 substitute to Proposition1 pp389-390. This is actual final-body comparison, not a search excerpt. No claim here that the source's full +3 argument has been independently repaired or certified.

Route A, refine a minimum T-partition: Write G as a cycle C with rooted attached trees U_i at cycle vertices c_i; T deletes one cycle edge. For x in U_i, write h(x)=d(x,c_i). For y in U_j, i!=j,
 d_G(x,y)=h(x)+d_C(c_i,c_j)+h(y).
Within U_i the unique tree path is still shortest: leaving and returning through c_i adds a positive cycle circuit, so no shortening occurs internally. For a part A, separate A into its U_i portion and outside portion. Its distance from x is the minimum of the internal tree distance and h(x)+min_{a outside U_i}(d_C(c_i,c(a))+h(a)). Thus adding the edge changes a COMMON cutoff for vertices at equal height in U_i, but does not preserve arbitrary old resolving coordinates. The inference 'internal distances unchanged, hence every old distinguishing part remains distinguishing' is not proved: a newly shorter outside minimum may mask the internal difference. A valid +1 refinement must pay this cutoff issue as well as the cross-root issue. Merely copying the source's three-singleton recipe does not yield +1.

A concrete failure of a naive choice (not a target counterexample): take C6 with vertices0,...,5 and T the path0-1-2-3-4-5. The two parts {0} and {1,...,5} resolve T because distance to0 is its path coordinate. In C6, splitting off {3} gives three parts {0},{3},{1,2,4,5}, yet1 and5 both have vector(1,2,0). Hence even in a cycle the added singleton cannot be chosen without a positional argument. Adjacent landmarks0,5 do resolve this particular cycle; this failure does not refute the conjecture.

Route B, cycle landmarks plus rooted local partitions: Two cycle landmarks do not automatically encode both root and height. In C6, choose adjacent landmarks0,5. The vertex1 and a pendant vertex attached at0 both have distances(1,2) to them. The equalities h+d_C(i,z)=h'+d_C(j,z) permit a nonzero height offset. Three well-placed landmarks can rule out these offsets, as the source's +3 strategy seeks to do, but charging those markers to only one new part requires coordinated reuse of the old partition. Unpaid lemma: every minimum T-partition can be replaced, without increasing its number of parts, by a rooted-distance robust partition that remains resolving after the cycle edge is restored, apart from a single controlled class of collisions separable by one new part. Neither an arbitrary-part proof nor an optimization argument establishing this lemma was found.

Route C, counterexample structure: true twins force different parts, but a unicyclic graph cannot gain a large new twin class solely by restoring one edge. Leaves with a common support remain twins in T and G, so their lower bound is already charged to pd(T). Starting from a path yields only a cycle (known pd3 versus pd2), not a violating gap. Attached equal-height branches offer possible cycle collisions but these are not a lower bound for EVERY partition of G. No explicit full witness nor all-partitions certificate is present. This is a substantive combinatorial proof gap, not a novelty veto or exclusion based on proof shortness.

Formal feasibility: finite simple graphs, graph distance and finite partitions are plausible definitions, but an arbitrary-graph refinement theorem is missing. Formalizing the displayed identities would only formalize helpers. No full candidate or dispatch.

## 322416: all r>0 polynomial, convex on the real line, alternating coefficients

The original1110.4932 Conjecture3.1 and Th3.2 pp6-7 explicitly distinguish all-r conjecture from formulas for fixed r. Read actual source coefficients r=0,...,4. O'Sullivan's actual author manuscript 'On the partial fraction decomposition of the restricted partition generating function', Section7 pp12-15, supplies Theorem7.3, formulas7.1-7.11 and Lemma7.6. It proves polynomiality, degree2r, monicity, and roots0,1. It does NOT prove full convexity, alternation, or absence of other roots. Theorem7.4's negativity proof includes an attributed finite check below110; no such check was run or independently reproduced here. Theorem7.5's positivity statement is attributed, not a new proof. The separate search hit Integers16(2016)q78 says these remaining properties are conjectural; that distinct body is unread and is navigation, not a paid proof gate.

Route A, coefficient extraction: put t=1-x. For integers N>r the pole expansion gives
 Q_N(t)=product_{j=1}^N jt/(1-(1-t)^j),
 P_r(N)=4^r r! [t^r]Q_N(t).
Indeed F_N(1-t)=Q_N(t)/(N!t^N), while the pole term C_{01,N-r}(N)(x-1)^(-(N-r)) contributes (-1)^(N-r)C to the coefficient of t^(r-N). The two (-1) powers cancel in the stated normalization. This derivation only gives values at positive integers; extending by the unique polynomial is required before differentiating with respect to real N.

Hand expansion through t2: one factor equals1+(j-1)t/2+(j²-1)t²/12+O(t³). Thus the coefficient of t in log Q is N(N-1)/4, and that of t² is N(-2N²+15N-13)/144. Exponentiating gives
 P_2(x)=x4-(22/9)x3+(13/3)x2-(26/9)x
       =x(x-1)(x²-(13/9)x+26/9).
The quadratic factor is positive on R because its leading coefficient is positive and its discriminant is -767/81. Its only real roots are0,1. Moreover P_2''(x)=12x²-(44/3)x+26/3 has discriminant -1808/9<0, hence is strictly positive. P_1=x(x-1) is also strictly convex. These are source-known cases, not credited results. Validity at each fixed r does not prove an arbitrary-r assertion.

Route B, O'Sullivan principal subsum: let y=x(x-1) and M_r(x)=product_{j=0}^{r-1}(y+4j). Then M_r(-x) has nonnegative coefficients, so this entire subsum alternates. At x=1/2, y=-1/4, y'=0, y''=2. Differentiating the finite product by hand gives
 M_r''(1/2)=2 product_{j=1}^{r-1}(4j-1/4) [1-sum_{a=1}^{r-1}1/(16a-1)].
The sum is unbounded, so this subsum eventually loses convexity. This is precisely a source-known obstruction, NOT a counterexample for P_r. The omitted Bernoulli/power-sum terms can change second derivatives in either direction. To refute P_r using this route requires an exact r and real x with a proved remainder bound, not an experiment. To prove P_r, it requires a global nonnegative second-derivative decomposition despite the negative M_r contribution. No such bound or decomposition was obtained.

Route C, alternation through power sums: polynomial continuation in formula7.9 introduces Bernoulli numbers with changing signs. Positivity of factor coefficients at positive integer N would not imply that P_r(-x) has nonnegative coefficients, and even that would not imply convexity on R. O'Sullivan's monicity/root proof uses the unique degree-maximal term and vanishing s_m(0)=0,s_m(1)=1; these facts do not control intermediate coefficients. Unpaid lemmas: all-degree coefficient domination of P_r-M_r, and a global second-derivative sign certificate. No small-r counterexample, no source error claim, no all-r solution. Strong contrary significance: source authors explicitly leave these uniform sign questions open; a complete proof could be meaningful despite elementary coefficient tools. No routine exclusion asserted.

## 319715: exact already-public endpoint

Original1003.4728 p14 Conjecture23 and note added in proof explicitly name Levande1006.3013. Read actual latter Claim6.1 and full proof pp13-14. Here is the checked bridge, including reversal of the Eulerian index. Remove the arc ending at2n and relabel the remaining endpoints, giving a unique matching on2n-2; conversely insert its opener in one of2n-1 slots. A predecessor with j left-nestings has n slots immediately before closers (including the new last closer), which preserve j. Of its n-1 opener slots, j interrupt an existing left-nesting and create exactly one replacement left-nesting, also preserving j. The remaining n-1-j slots create one new nesting. No other adjacency of old openers changes. Consequently
 L(n,j)=(n+j)L(n-1,j)+(n-j)L(n-1,j-1).
Set T(n,k)=L(n,n-k); then
 T(n,k)=kT(n-1,k)+(2n-k)T(n-1,k-1), T(1,1)=1,
with out-of-range terms zero. These are exactly Claim6.1's second-order Eulerian recurrence and boundary values. The empty matching has L(0,0)=1 if n0 is included. This is a full public proof-to-target bridge, not a new theorem or an earlier exact counterexample assertion. ## 322380: source-own assertion, monoid mechanism only

Read White1110.3077v1 graphical species and quotient definition pp4-7, acyclic-orientation products pp8-9, Conjecture24 p17 and its immediate sentence 'This conjecture is actually true'. Do not report this as a newly solved open question. A natural underlying MONOID proof uses block-decorated words modulo swapping adjacent blocks with no edge between them. Each word induces an acyclic orientation of the quotient graph by directing earlier to later. Any two linear extensions of a finite poset are related by adjacent swaps of incomparable elements: move the first element of the desired extension leftwards, passing only incomparable elements, then induct on the tail. Incomparable vertices cannot have an oriented edge. Thus the quotient of words has precisely the orientation indexing in A-circle-g. For a disjoint-commutative target monoid, iterated multiplication of the decorations is independent of extension, and every such map is forced by the generating one-block elements. This pays an underlying ordinary monoid mechanism.

It does not by itself settle the categorical Hopf statement as literally written: one must specify the generator map's primitive condition, coproduct on decorated blocks, braiding convention, and universal target category. Arbitrary graphical species have no supplied coproduct. The standard candidate extends generators primitively and coproducts by cuts that do not split blocks, but that construction and its intended universal category are not fully established here. Source-own truth assertion already removes prospective novelty; no new categorical theorem claimed. Strong contrary case: an actual missing full published proof could be mathematically useful, but supplying exposition of the source's own announced fact does not qualify under this task.
