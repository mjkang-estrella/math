Here is the complete group-specific argument, with a small weakness of the lemma's more general wording avoided.
Order the finite sumset A+B arbitrarily as c1,...,ct. Maintain equal-size unused subsets A_j,B_j. At step j assign simultaneously EVERY available pair a,b with a+b=c_j, remove their endpoints, and proceed. For a fixed sum these pairs are disjoint: a+b=a+b' implies b=b' and a+b=a'+b implies a=a'. Thus both cancellation properties of a GROUP are used. The paper's abstract left-cancellation-only formulation does not by itself justify distinct images; the actual group application has both and is valid. If nonempty unused sets remained after all sums, any unused pair would have occurred at its sum's step, contradiction. This produces a bijection f.
Now let g have the same multiplicities as f. Inductively g agrees on all endpoints assigned before step j. On the residual sets, the complete set of edges labelled c_j is precisely the disjoint set chosen at step j. To attain f's multiplicity for c_j, g must choose ALL those edges; prior fixed edges have identical contributions in both functions. This establishes agreement at step j. Finally g=f. The disjointness A intersect(A+B)=empty ensures every bijection is a matching, so this is precisely acyclicity. Empty A=B has the unique empty bijection. Finite A,B in infinite groups cause no change because only A+B is ordered. Prime groups are abelian, hence no prime witness exists. No field, ordering of the whole group, bound on p, or Sidon hypothesis is imported.
