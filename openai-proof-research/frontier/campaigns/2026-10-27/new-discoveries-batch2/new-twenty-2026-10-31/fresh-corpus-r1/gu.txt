                                    1. Introduction
1.1. Overview. Adder MAC is a simple noiseless multiple-access channel (MAC),
where if users send messages X1 , . . . , Xh ∈ {0, 1}n, then the receiver receives Y =
X1 + · · · + Xh with addition over Z.
    Communication over the noiseless adder MAC has been studied for more than
fifty years. In the most well-studied version, each user i (1 ≤ i ≤ h) has their
own codebook Ci ⊆ {0, 1}n , and Xi is picked from Ci . We insist our protocol to be
zero-error, i.e., we can uniquely determine X1 , . . . , Xk given Y . More formally, if
we have u1 , . . . , uh , v1 , . . . , vh with ui , vi ∈ Ci (1 ≤ i ≤ h) and
                              u1 + · · · + uh = v1 + · · · + vh ,
then we must have ui = vi for all 1 ≤ i ≤ h. A code tuple (C1 , . . . , Ch ) satisfying
the above property is called uniquely decodable. The quantity we would like to
optimize is the sum rate, defined as

1.2. Related work. In this section we review previous works on uniquely decod-
able code tuples and Bh -codes.
   In both settings, the case h = 2 is studied most. A uniquely decodable code tuple
with h = 2 is called uniquely decodable code pair (UDCP) in literature. Lindström
[Lin69] prove that 12 log 6 ≤ R2 ≤ 32 . Since then, a lot of constructions of UDCPs
have been given, improving the lower bound on R2 , including 1.30366 by Coebergh
van den Braak and van Tilborg [CvdBvT85], 1.30369 by Ahlswede and Balakirsky
[AB99], 1.30565 by Coebergh van den Braak [CvdB83], 1.30999 by Urbanke and
Li [UL98], 1.31782 by Mattas and Östergård [MÖ05]. These constructions are all
explicit constructions, and usually have small n. There has been no upper bound
of R2 better than the entropy bound 23 .
   For UDCPs, people have also considered the following question: when α = logn|C1 |
is close to one, how large can β = logn|C2 | be? Kasami et al. [KLWY83] gave a
construction where α ≥ 1 − ǫ and β ≥ 0.25. This is recently improved by Wiman
[Wim17] to α ≥ 1 − ǫ and β ≥ 0.2563. Urbanke and Li [UL98] proved that when
α ≥ 1 − ǫ, we have β ≤ 0.4921. It is improved by Ordentlich and Shayevitz [OS16]
to β ≤ 0.4798 when α ≥ 1 − ǫ and by Austrin et al. [AKKN17] to β ≤ 0.4228 when
α ≥ 1 − ǫ.
   Let us discuss Rh for h ≥ 3. The special case where all |Ci | = 2 is studied
under the name detecting matrix. Cantor and Mills [CM66] and Lindström [Lin65]
constructed codes with sum rate increases as (1 + o(1)) log2 h as h → ∞. For the
general case, Khachatrian and Martirossian [KM98] gave a combinatorial construc-
tion for all h. Kiviluoto and Östergård [KÖ07] gave better explicit constructions
for 3 ≤ h ≤ 5. For k ≥ 3, it has been proven by Bross and Blake [BB98] that Rh is
strictly smaller than H(B(h, 21 )).
   Now let us turn to R2∗ . A B2 -code is also called a Sidon code, in analogy with
Sidon sequences in number theory. Lindström [Lin69] gave a construction of a B2
code of rate 21 that actually works with addition over Z/2Z. There has been several
nontrivial upper bounds for R2∗ . Lindström proved in [Lin69] that R2∗ ≤ 23 , and in
[Lin72] that R2∗ ≤ 0.6. Cohen et al. [CLZ01] improved this to R2∗ ≤ 0.5753.
   The number theoretic analogy of Bh -codes are called Bh -sequences. Any con-
struction of Bk -sequences can be directly translated into Bk -codes with the same

                                       2. Preliminaries
2.1. Definitions. In this chapter we give necessary definitions and study existing
constructions of Bh -codes.
Definition 2.1. Let A be an abelian group and C ⊆ A be a subset. We say C is
a Bh -set (or C satisfies the Bh -property) if for any a ∈ A, there exists at most one
multiset {u1 , . . . , uh } with u1 , . . . , uh ∈ C such that a = u1 + · · · + uh . In other
words, if u1 , . . . , uh , v1 , . . . , vh ∈ C satisfies u1 + · · · + uh = v1 + · · · + vh , then the
multisets {u1 , . . . , uh } and {v1 , . . . , vh } are equal.
Definition 2.2. A Bh -code is a Bh -set C ⊆ {0, 1}n ⊆ Zn . The rate of a Bh -code
is defined as logn|C| .
Remark 2.3. We are most interested in the asymptotic growth of rate of Bh -
codes as n goes to ∞. Therefore, when we say “there exist Bh -codes of rate f ” we
actually mean “there exist a family of Bh -codes C1 , C2 , · · · with Ci ⊆ {0, 1}ni such
that ni → ∞ and log logn|C

desired Bh -codes. However, this idea does not work, because the expectation could
be large. For example, if v1 = v2 , then we have Θ(th−1 ) violations of the form

Definition 2.8. A configuration C of shape (k, 2) is a k × 2 matrix of random
variables (Ci,j )1≤i≤k,1≤j≤2 taking values in {0, 1} with the property that
    (1) For each i, j, P(Ci,j = 0) = P(Ci,j = 1) = 12 .
    (2) Some (or no) variables are identified, i.e., P(Ci,j = Ci′ ,j ′ ) = 1 for some
        i, j, i′ , j ′ . We treat identified variables as the same variable. Variables that
        are not identified are mutually independent.
    (3) No variable appears in two columns, i.e., if P(Ci,j = Ci′ ,j ′ ) = 1, then j = j ′ .
Two configurations of the same shape are equivalent if they have the same law after
repeatedly (1) swapping columns and (2) swapping entries in the same column. Let
Conf(k, 2) denote the     S set of equivalence classes of configurations of shape (k, 2).

4.4. Another kind of list-decoding. There is another natural list-decoding ver-

in configurations.
                                                   19
Definition 4.15. A Bh# [d]-code is a set C ⊆ {0, 1}n satisfying the property that
