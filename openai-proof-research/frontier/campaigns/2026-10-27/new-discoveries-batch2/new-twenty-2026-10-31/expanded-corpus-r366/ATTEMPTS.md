## 348817: biased measures, a complete public proof, not a new result

Frankl's actual Conjecture 11.4 in arXiv:2011.14252 is exactly the reserved weighted sum. The circle proof (Theorem 11.3, read in full) bounds the number of IU arcs of each size by min(k,n-k), yielding floor(n/2)ceil(n/2)/n after averaging. Summing those separate size constraints cannot reach the requested constant 1/6. A second route replaces the discrete circle by a continuous product measure.

For a finite IU-family F on [n], let U be its upward closure, D its downward closure, and C={ [n]\\S:S in D}. U and C are intersecting increasing families; D is decreasing. These assertions follow by enlarging witnesses in F for U and taking complements of subsets of witnesses for C. For 0<=p<=1/2, q=1-p, set a=mu_p(U), b=mu_q(U), c=mu_p(D), d=mu_q(D). Because neither U nor D contains a set together with its complement, a+b<=1 and c+d<=1. The biased intersecting-family theorem gives a<=p and d=mu_p(C)<=p. The increasing/decreasing Harris inequality and F subset U intersect D give
mu_p(F)+mu_q(F)<=ac+bd<=a(1-d)+(1-a)d=a+d-2ad<=2p(1-p).
The final inequality follows since the two partial derivatives 1-2d and 1-2a are nonnegative on [0,p]^2. Integrating p from 0 to 1/2 gives integral_0^1 mu_p(F) dp<=1/6. For each S of size j, integration by parts gives integral_0^1 p^j(1-p)^(n-j)dp=j!(n-j)!/(n+1)!=1/((n+1)binom(n,j)). Finite summation therefore proves the full literal bound. The star excluding another coordinate has mu_p=p(1-p), hence equality. No endpoint or asymptotic weakening is used.

This is the actual route in Wu–Feng, current arXiv:2607.07564v3, Theorem 1.2 and Sections 2–3 (read through the proof, including the equality argument). It was posted July 8, revised July 14 and July 22, 2026, before this investigation. The current title is *The binomial norm of intersecting-union families*; the first-version title found by search was *The Lubell bound for intersecting-union families*. We do not claim independent novelty for rediscovering its mechanism. The displayed ordinary proof relies on the named biased EKR and Harris theorems as ordinary published lemmas, not on Lean bridges. Their original proof bodies were not read here. The endpoint reduction from the public Theorem 1.2 to MathDB is identity: same ground set, both pairwise conditions, every subset counted once, same binomial weight, same constant. PUBLIC EXCLUSION, zero credit.

## 350696: exact deletion/contraction reduction and two failed continuations

Use the source's intended distinct edges e and f. The literal record omits distinctness. Taking e=f on a single-edge graph gives 1/2<=1/4, merely a wording correction and ineligible for credit.

For distinct e,f let H=G-e-f. Write A,B,C,D for the numbers of forests of G with respective inclusion states 00,10,01,11 after deleting the marked edges from each forest. Thus A=#forests(H), B counts forests of H to which e may be added, C similarly for f, and D counts forests to which both may be added. The total Z=A+B+C+D. The desired inequality is DZ<=(B+D)(C+D); cancellation gives exactly AD<=BC. This also follows from the multiaffine expansion Z=A+z_e B+z_f C+z_e z_f D and the Rayleigh difference BC-AD.

Give H's forests the uniform law. Let E be the event that e's endpoints are disconnected, F the analogous event for f, and T the event that both edges are individually addable but together create a cycle. Then B=A P(E), C=A P(F), D=A(P(E intersect F)-P(T)). Consequently
BC-AD=A^2(P(T)-Cov(1_E,1_F)).
T consists exactly of two distinct H-components pairing the four endpoints in one of the two crossing pairings (with the natural identifications if endpoints coincide). This is a complete finite combinatorial identity, not a sign proof. Since the covariance of disconnectedness indicators equals the covariance of their complementary connectivity indicators, the unpaid inequality is P(T)>=Cov(connectivity_e,connectivity_f). Neither term may simply be discarded. A forest-independent injection from the AD pairs to BC pairs would need to handle overlapping path exchanges while retaining invertibility; no such injection has been supplied.

Continuation 1: the matrix-forest determinant counts rooted forests. For an unrooted forest with components C_i it multiplies the forest's weight by product |C_i|, the choices of roots. That factor varies with the forest. Determinantal negative correlation therefore cannot be transferred to the uniform unrooted measure by normalization. Conditioning on component number also fails to close the argument: total covariance contains Cov(E[X_e|K],E[X_f|K]), whose sign is not controlled by conditional negative correlation. Bridges and edges in separate graph components give easy equality cases only: the measure factors across components and a bridge may be toggled freely, so its indicator is independent Bernoulli(1/2).

Continuation 2: the current primary paper arXiv:2610.00549 explicitly keeps this conjecture open. Its introduction and precise Rayleigh formulas were read, through Theorem 1.4 and the setup of 1.5; later proofs were not read. It controls the highest two fugacity coefficients using transfer currents and an additional sum-of-squares identity. Positivity of leading coefficients proves positivity for sufficiently large fugacity, not at lambda=1: lower coefficients remain uncontrolled. Its complete-graph connected-subgraph statements concern another measure. The 2026 wheel theorem found by search is also only a graph family; its body was not accessed because the inherited ScienceDirect route is unavailable. The full lambda=1 sign identity for arbitrary G remains the precise mathematical gap. HOLD, zero credit.

The pinned OpenAI manuscript *The free uniform spanning forest is a factor of IID* (current registry head 4eaee875..., paper September 25, 2026) was read through its definition, Theorems 1.1/1.3 and construction outline. It uses infinite-volume limits of finite spanning TREE laws. Those measures do not equal the uniform law on all finite forests: even a single-edge graph has deterministic spanning tree and two equally likely forests. Thus the theorem cannot be substituted for the missing sign proof. No upstream truth was audited.

## 348853: signed cut albums, cancellation, and the missing universal structure

The actual source arXiv:2011.14955v2 and current author manuscript retain Conjecture 6.1. Read Section 6's definitions and (6.1)–(6.3), the facet-initial proof in Theorem 6.3(1), and the Scrope induction Proposition 7.2. The source uses prefix-pure ordered simplicial complexes, initial restriction/contraction, and ordered join with shuffle product; substituting arbitrary unordered simplicial complexes would change the problem.

Fix the input order w, a prefix-pure complex Gamma, an output order u and an output complex Omega. Every nonzero Takeuchi summand is indexed by a composition A into consecutive intervals. Its complex is Re_A(Gamma), the join of the interval minors. Its order is one shuffle of the restricted block orders. Thus the coefficient is exactly
 sum_{A: D(w,u)<=A<=E, Re_A(Gamma)=Omega} (-1)^(number of blocks A).
Encode A by its subset of the n-1 possible cuts. If the admissible cuts form one Boolean interval [L,U], its contribution vanishes whenever U\\L is nonempty: choose one optional cut j and pair each subset S with S symmetric-difference {j}; the output and the descent condition are preserved and signs reverse. If no optional cut exists the contribution is a single sign. This is an actual involution for an interval, not an invocation of cancellation by assertion.

First attempted extension: toggle the first cut whose adjacent interval minors have the same join as their merged minor. For any fixed surrounding cuts and an equality of those two minors, this pairs the corresponding terms. But after toggling, the first available cut need not remain the same; the contractions defining later minors can change. No invariant ordering of admissible cuts, nor a proof that this first-cut rule squares to the identity, has been found. One can partition into maximal Boolean intervals only after addressing overlapping intervals; a sum of individually 0/±1 contributions need not itself have absolute value at most one. The source's facet-initial hypothesis supplies a special interval description and then a further grouping argument. Prefix purity alone has not supplied that description.

Second attempted extension: represent the whole coefficient album as a Scrope complex. Proposition 7.2 proves the required 0/±1 reduced Euler characteristic by induction: remove the last facet; the remainder is a cone; its intersection with that facet is again a Scrope complex; gluing two contractible pieces suspends the intersection. This really proves the statement for Scrope complexes. However, the required generators must be complements of intervals with the endpoint ordering specified in Section 7.1. For a general prefix-pure complex, equality Re_A(Gamma)=Omega has not been converted to such generators. There is no established embedding of every prefix-pure complex into the source's generalized-permutohedron Hopf submonoid either. The missing lemma is a structure theorem for every constrained reassembly album, or an explicit admissible album with Euler characteristic outside {0,±1}. No finite enumeration was run and no such witness has been asserted. HOLD. Material companion and journal-body coverage remains incomplete, so novelty is also HOLD independently of this mathematical gap.
