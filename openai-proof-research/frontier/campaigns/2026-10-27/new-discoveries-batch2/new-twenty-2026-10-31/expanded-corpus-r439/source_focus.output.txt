             4. Lattices which hold a 2-design on every layer
   As opposed to the case of 4-designs, it is well known that lattices with 2-designs
on every layer do exist in every dimension n. Indeed, for instance all irreducible
root lattices have this property, as was already noticed by Ryškov [23], although
in a different language. To see why this is true, we first define the automorphism
group Aut(S) of a finite set S of vectors of the same norm in Rn as
                          Aut(S) = {g ∈ O(n) | g(S) ⊆ S}.
   With this definition, the automorphism group Aut(Λ) of a lattice Λ is obviously
a subgroup of the automorphism group of each of its layers.
   Then we have the following
Theorem 12 (cfr. [17], Theorem 3.6.6). Let S be a configuration of vectors of
the same norm in Rn , and G a subgroup of Aut(S), with the property that G acts
irreducibly on Rn . Then S is a 2-design.
   Now, if Λ is an irreducible root lattice in Rn , then its Weyl group W (Λ) (the
subgroup of Aut(Λ) generated by the orthogonal reflections in the minimal vectors)
acts irreducibly on Rn (see [12], Lemma 1.9), hence to conclude it suffices to apply
Theorem 12 to each layer of Λ. As a consequence of this, and of Lemma 11 we have
this
Corollary 13. The irreducible root lattices An , Dn , E6 , E7 and E8 , as well as
their dual lattices, are critical points for the height function.
   Since E6 , E7 and E8 actually have 4-designs on every shell, it was already known,
from [8], that these lattices are local minima of the height function.
   Once established that such lattices, which we will call fully critical, exist in
every dimension, one would naturally like to ask for a complete classification in
every given dimension, and for a test which allows to determine whether a given
lattice is fully critical or not. The classification is intended up to similarity: we
recall that a similarity of Rn of ratio λ 6= 0 is an endomorphism σ of Rn which
satisfies the identity
(59)                  σ(x) · σ(y) = λ2 (x · y) for all x, y ∈ Rn .
   For strongly eutactic lattices, the classification up to similarity is complete in
dimension up to 6, and it has been done successively by several mathematicians,
namely Štogrin, Bergé and Martinet for dimensions 2 to 4 (see [17], Sections 9.3 and
14.3), Batut for dimension 5 (in [3]), Elbaz-Vincent, Gangl and Soulé for dimension
6.
   We recall two important facts:
Proposition 14 ([17], Theorem 9.4.3). Up to similarity, there are only finitely
many strongly eutactic lattices in a given dimension.
Proposition 15 ([18], Proposition 1.8). A strongly eutactic lattice is proportional
to an integral lattice.
   In the web page on lattices [2] created by Martinet and Batut, one can find a
complete list of strongly eutactic lattices up to dimension 6, together with many
other known strongly eutactic lattices in higher dimension. Lattices there are given
by an (integral) Gram matrix. Since fully critical lattices are in particular strongly
eutactic, the representatives of the classes of equivalence of fully critical lattices up
to similarity are to be found in this list. We are lead to distinguish the case of even
dimension from that of odd dimension.

          5. Comparison with known results and final remarks
  We conclude with some remarks and open questions.

5.1. Theorem 1 only gives a sufficient condition for a lattice Λ to be a stationary
point for the height. We do not know yet whether having a 2-design on every layer
is a necessary condition for a lattice to be critical. Moreover, as our first aim is to
study the minima of the height function, if the 2-design condition is not necessary,
we would like to know whether the minimum of h is to be found among the fully
critical lattices. This is true for the hexagonal lattice A2 in dimension 2, and for
the face-centred cubic lattice A∗3 in dimension 3; this is also true for D4 , which
achieves a strict local minimum of h in dimension 4.

5.2. Moreover, Theorem 1 does not tell whether a fully critical lattice is a local
maximum or minimum, or a saddle point for h.

5.3. We noticed a quite intriguing property of strongly eutactic lattices: up to
dimension 6, if a strong eutactic lattice Λ is not fully critical, then the sequence of
2-designs already stops at the second layer, i.e. M2 (Λ) is not a 2 design. As we
found no counterexample among the strongly eutactic lattices at our disposal in
dimension 7 and 8, we are lead to the following

Conjecture 21. If the first two layers M1 (Λ) and M2 (Λ) of a lattice Λ are spherical
2-designs, then all the other layers are 2-designs, in particular Λ is fully critical for
h.

   The conjecture is perhaps hazardous in high dimension, but thanks to the com-
plete classification of strongly eutactic lattices in small dimension, we can state it
as a theorem in dimension up to 6:

Theorem 22. Let Λ be a lattice of dimension 2 ≤ n ≤ 6. If its first two layers
M1 (Λ) and M2 (Λ) are spherical 2-designs, then all the other layers are 2-designs,
in particular Λ is fully critical for h.
