r each set S of M we define
Q⟂​(S)={q∈Q⟂:supp⁡(q)⊆S},
and for each circuit C of M we let qC be a vector in Q⟂ with supp⁡(qC)=C, noting that qC is unique up to scalar multiple.
For any collection A⊆𝒞 of circuits, we have
span{qC:C∈A}⊆Q⟂(∪C∈AC),
where span denotes the linear span over 𝔽. Hence, if
dim(Q⟂(∪C∈AC))<|A|,
(1)
then the dual codewords qC:C∈A are linearly dependent regardless of the chosen representation Q, and so we would like them to be dependent in the combinatorial derived matroid.
Now by standard duality arguments,
Q⟂​(S)=(Q|S)⟂,
where Q|S is the projection of Q to the coordinates in S. Thus
dim(Q⟂​(S))=|S|−dim(Q|S)=n⁡(S),
so the condition (1), which is sufficient for dependence of {qC:C∈A} can be rewritten as
n(∪C∈AC)<|A|.
(2)
For an arbitrary, not necessarily representable matroid, we build the definition of dependence of A⊆𝒞 in the combinatorial derived matroid on condition (2). First we define two operations on sets of circuits that we will later use to construct the dependent sets of the combinatorial derived matroid.
Definition 4.1.
Let 𝒞 be the set of circuits of some matroid, and let 𝒜⊆𝒞. Then we define the collection
ϵ(𝒜)=𝒜∪{(A1∪A2)∖{C}:A1,A2∈𝒜,A1∩A2∉𝒜,C∈A1∩A2}.
Moreover, we define
↑𝒜={A⊆𝒞:∃A′∈𝒜:A′⊆A},
and denote min⁡𝒜⊆𝒞 the collection of inclusion minimal sets in 𝒜.
For any matroid with ground set 𝒞, circuit set ℬ and collection of dependent sets 𝒜, we must have min⁡𝒜=ℬ and ↑ℬ=𝒜. Observe that, by definition, 𝒜⊆↑𝒜 and 𝒜⊆ϵ⁡(𝒜) for every 𝒜⊆2𝒞.
The operation ϵ:2𝒞→2𝒞 is designed to guarantee properties (C3) and (D3) in the matroid axioms.
Definition 4.2.
Let M be a matroid, and 𝒞=𝒞⁡(M) its collection of circuits. Define the collection
𝒜0:={A⊆𝒞:|A|>n(∪C∈AC)}.
(3)
Inductively, we let 𝒜i+1=↑ϵ⁡(𝒜i) for i≥1, and
𝒜=⋃i≥0𝒜i.
Analogously, we let ℬ0=min⁡𝒜0, ℬi+1=ϵ⁡(ℬi) for i≥1, and
ℬ=min⋃i≥0ℬi.
For a set A∈𝒜i+1∖𝒜i, respectively B∈ℬi+1∖ℬi, we say that it has depth i+1 in 𝒜 or ℬ respectively.
Note that the sequences 𝒜i and ℬi are both increasing and contained in the finite set 2𝒞. Hence, we have that 𝒜0⊆𝒜 and 𝒜=𝒜n for some n≥0, and analogously ℬ0⊆ℬ and ℬ=ℬn for some n≥0.
Definition 4.3.
Let M be a matroid with circuits 𝒞. Then the combinatorial derived matroid δ​M is a matroid with ground set 𝒞⁡(M) and dependent sets 𝒜. Alternatively, we can define δ​M by its circuits ℬ.
We will prove, in Propositions 4.7 and 4.9, that these definitions gives indeed a matroid and that the two definitions are the same. After this, we will define yet another way to construct the circuit set ℬ, in Proposition 4.10.
Remark 4.4.
Rewriting axiom (D3) in the logically equivalent form:
if ​D1,D2∈𝒟,
then ​D1∩D2∈𝒟​ or ​(D1∪D2)∖{e}∈𝒟​ for all ​e∈D1∩D2,
we could in principle for every A1,A2∈𝒜i such that A1∩A2∉𝒜i, have chosen to include either A1∩A2 or (A1∪A2)∖{C} into 𝒜i+1. By defining 𝒜i+1 via the operation ϵ, as in Definition 4.2, we guarantee to not construct any small dependent sets when not necessary. This appears as a desirable property for the constructed derived matroid to be “generic”.
Indeed, consequently choosing to include D1∩D2 in 𝒜 for all prescribed dependent sets D1,D2∈𝒜 would result in a rather less interesting matroid — often even with rank 0.
