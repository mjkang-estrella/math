## 344593: complete ordinary refutation, complete routine-adaptation bridge, zero credit
The actual original 1911.09799v1 Section 6 Conjecture 2 quantifies over finite undirected simple triangle-free factors. It does not require connectedness or an explicit vertex bound. Shitov 1905.02167v2 gives finite simple A,B and an integer c with chi(A)>c, chi(B)>c and a proper c-coloring f of A times B. Its full three-page body was read: Observation 1 normalizes exponential colorings; Claim 2 extracts robust colors; Claim 3 uses a girth-six base and the strong-product blowup to contradict a suited coloring; the final paragraph supplies the two factors. Its base graph is not itself the desired triangle-free pair: replacing A by the girth-six base loses the strong-product chromatic lower bound. That shortcut fails.

Route 1, exact sparse transfer. Kun 0706.1701v2 Theorem 1 supplies, for every finite relational S, t,k, an S' of girth>k with S'->S and S'->T iff S->T for all |T|<t. Actual definitions, Lemma 13 and proof of Theorem 1 were read, along with the expander/twisted-product machinery. Do not simply regard a symmetric graph as a relation and assume its notion of girth: the paper notes degenerate two-cycles for symmetric edges. Instead choose an arbitrary orientation of each edge of A and put only that ordered pair in a binary relation R. Apply the theorem with t=c+1 and k=4. Let T be the symmetric loopless complete c-vertex relation. R->T is exactly a proper c-coloring of A. Thus R' does not map to T. Define A' to have an undirected edge xy whenever R'(x,y) or R'(y,x). There are no loops by girth; a triangle would give three distinct relational tuples through three distinct points, a relational cycle of length 3, contrary to girth>4. The projection R'->R induces an ordinary homomorphism a:A'->A. If A' were c-colorable that coloring would be R'->T, impossible. Consequently A' is finite simple triangle-free and chi(A')>c. Repeat independently for B to obtain b:B'->B. The map (x,y)->f(a(x),b(y)) properly c-colors A' times B': each product edge maps to a product edge. Hence chi(A' times B')<=c<min(chi(A'),chi(B')). This is the exact negation of the assigned universal statement.

Route 2, an elementary proof of the needed special transfer, independent of Kun's relational encoding. Fix any finite simple A not c-colorable, c>=1. Replace each vertex by an independent fiber of size N. For each edge of A choose each possible cross edge independently with probability N^(-3/4). All other pairs are nonedges. Write v=|A|. The expected number X of triangles is at most (vN)^3 N^(-9/4)=v^3 N^(3/4). By Markov, P(X>N/2)<=2v^3 N^(-1/4), tending to zero. For each base edge uv and subsets U,V of its fibers of size s=ceil(N/(2c)), the probability of no cross edge is at most exp(-N^(-3/4)s^2)<=exp(-N^(5/4)/(4c^2)). At most |E(A)|4^N pairs of subsets need consideration. The union bound therefore tends to zero. For sufficiently large N both desired events occur. Remove one vertex from every triangle, at most N/2 vertices total. Every fiber retains at least N/2 vertices. If the remaining graph had a c-coloring, choose a majority color in each fiber; its color class has at least ceil(N/(2c)) vertices. Adjacent base fibers cannot choose the same color by the cross-edge property. This would c-color A, contradiction. Projection is a homomorphism to A and the retained graph is triangle-free. Applying this to both Shitov factors proves the same exact refutation. These are symbolic probability inequalities, not a random experiment or a program. Floors, vertex deletion and the global/fiber distinction are explicit.

Route 3, direct exponential construction, fails to bypass prior transfer: Shitov's second factor contains the constant-map c-clique, so it is not triangle-free. Deleting those constants invalidates normalization of suited colors and the robust-color contradiction. One needs either a new chromatic lower-bound proof for a triangle-free subgraph or the transfer just given.

Prior/significance judgment: this is a COMPLETE applicable primary-result-to-target bridge, not a claim based on proof length. The inputs already assert a counterexample and a homomorphic sparse replacement preserving each finite coloring obstruction; the only new step is composition of two homomorphisms with a given product coloring. Strongest contrary case: the 2019 source explicitly conjectures the triangle-free statement despite the general 2013 sparse theorem, so stating this consequence could correct a real literature oversight and is stronger than a finite instance. Nevertheless no new sparse theorem, product obstruction or counterexample mechanism is required; under this assignment's routine-adaptation exclusion it earns zero. No assertion that an exact earlier paper explicitly printed this corollary. No worldwide priority claim. He-Wigderson was independently identified but DNS-unavailable; that work is not needed for this bridge and was not retrieved elsewhere. Shitov is a distinct original work, not its alternate delivery. Current registry proof/chronology clearance unavailable. No FULLCANDIDATE or formal dispatch.

## 339365: sustained three routes, unresolved full scope
Read DiMuro 1805.11641v1 fully, including the Warden-game proof of Theorem 1. Read final 2019 EJC26(1) P1.35 Section 5.6 Conjecture 10; it retains the assigned gamma containing a<=k-2 and n>=m. The theorem reduces the exact conjecture to: every legal cyclic word beta can reach k^n by increasing one coordinate at a time while remaining legal. The empty language is not at issue: gamma contains a symbol below k so k^n avoids it.

Route A, eliminate a low symbol using an absent larger symbol. If gamma contains i but omits j>i, replace each occurrence of i in beta by j. Any new occurrence of gamma would have to use the changed coordinate, whose value j is absent from gamma; impossible. After all i are gone, raise the remaining coordinates to k. No i is reintroduced, hence gamma remains absent. This proves the whole n>=m subfamily, but is explicitly the source's own Section 4.6/5.6 argument and earns zero. The target allows gamma to use every larger letter, including k-1,k, so it does not supply an absent j.

Route B, local-maximal legal words. The finite poset of legal words ordered by componentwise increase has a maximal element above any beta. The desired statement is exactly that k^n is its only maximal element, because any sequence of strict legal increases terminates, and termination elsewhere is an obstruction. In a putative other maximum w, for each coordinate i with w_i<k and each b>w_i, changing w_i to b creates gamma. Since w initially avoids gamma, the created occurrence must contain i, with i aligned to a position of gamma whose symbol is b, and every other position of that occurrence already matching gamma. In particular the two potential replacements k-1 and k for a coordinate <=k-2 give two almost-occurrences with different alignments. They cannot have identical alignment. Their overlap forces equalities gamma_r=gamma_(r+d) except at the two changed alignments, on a cyclic window of length m. Unpaid lemma: show these overlap constraints either force gamma to use only k-1,k or yield a coordinate admitting a legal increase. A claimed ordinary period argument is insufficient: windows can wrap around n, their overlaps need not be an interval in a chosen linear representative, and letters can repeat. No contradiction from the constraints alone was proved.

Route C, anchored k-block growth. Starting from beta, attempt to create a block of k's then grow it until all positions are k. If gamma has no k the source route applies. Otherwise a boundary increment can create gamma using a terminal or internal k-run, so length of the current k-block alone is not an invariant. A repair using k-1 as a temporary guard can itself complete another occurrence with a different shift. The precise missing invariant is a choice of boundary plus guard ensuring no gamma-occurrence at every intermediate single-coordinate increase, for arbitrary overlap/border structure of gamma and every n>=m. Simultaneous replacement is not allowed by the theorem. The source examples over only the top two symbols show that arbitrary monotonicity is false, but are not counterexamples to this low-symbol hypothesis. No computational sweep was performed. Park exact monotone-move/overlap lemma; no novelty clearance.

## 364348: two constructive routes investigated; actual public full solution, zero
Original 2408.07411v1 Definition 1.4 and Conjecture 1.10 match all entries occurring once and common row/column constants across arrays. Its G is odd-order groups or groups with more than one involution. Its Theorem 4.7 treats restricted exponent not divisible by 8, not every group.
Route A, complete mapping partitions: use a bijection phi with phi+id bijective and blocks on which both sums vanish to assemble zero-sum arrays. Mere existence of a complete mapping gives no control of block sums. Original Theorem 3.3 block size may be exp(Delta) or 2exp(Delta), too large to create width-four arrays for arbitrary 2-groups. Minimal simultaneous zero-sum block size is exactly the unpaid lemma in that route.
Route B, subgroup extension: take a zero-sum starter on Z_p plus a small noncyclic 2-group; fill cosets with translates and combine arrays. Translation by x alters row sum by b*x and column sum by a*x. For width 4 and odd a, arbitrary high-exponent cosets do not preserve these constants; a naive lift fails. This is the obstacle beyond the exponent-four source subcase.

Actual Yu-Feng-Liu 2410.23662v1, dated 31 October 2024, Theorem 1.4 and complete 14-page extracted text read. It resolves the full criterion. Proof chain: Lemma1.1 gives necessity (unique involution and impossible odd-by-two); Lemma1.2 supplies nonexceptional cases; Lemma2.1 reduces remaining odd-by-2^n to Z_p plus noncyclic Sylow2 at width4; Lemma3.4 constructs exponent<=4 starters; Constructions4.1/4.2 fill subgroup holes; Construction4.3 raises a cyclic factor's exponent by three using paired columns and a p-by-six coefficient array; Lemma4.4 inducts on group order to cover all higher exponents. The displayed starter matrices were read in extracted text; no visual matrix audit or independent recalculation of all tables claimed.
Complete statement bridge: a finite abelian group's Sylow2 is trivial iff its order is odd. If its Sylow2 is nontrivial with r cyclic factors then it has 2^r-1 involutions, so it is noncyclic iff there is more than one involution. Thus their trivial-or-noncyclic condition is precisely original G. When a dimension is 2, their ab divisible by4 is equivalent to the other dimension being even, exactly excluding {2,2k+1}. Both-even cases never trigger that exception. Definitions and all a,b>1 and c>=1 match. Therefore the assigned statement is the public theorem, not a new contribution. Strongest contrary significance case: the result is substantial and solves the high-exponent obstruction; its substance belongs to the published authors. No new credit.
