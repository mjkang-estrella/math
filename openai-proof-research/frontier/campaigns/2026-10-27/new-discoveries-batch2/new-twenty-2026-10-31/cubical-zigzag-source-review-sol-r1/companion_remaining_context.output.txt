   In this section, we suppose that ∆ is a thin chamber complex of rank n. Then

there is a zigzag such that X and Y are contained in some flags of this zigzag.

4.1. Z-connectedness of facets and distance normal geodesics. If ∆ is αn
or βn , then
(6)                               d(X, Y ) = n − |X ∩ Y |
for any two facets X, Y . Recall that d(X, Y ) is the path distance between X and Y
in Γn−1 (∆). For the general case this distance formula fails. Consider, for example,
an n-gonal bipyramid; if n ≥ 6, then it contains two faces X, Y with a common
vertex and such that d(X, Y ) > 2. In the general case, we have
                                  d(X, Y ) ≥ n − |X ∩ Y |
for any two facets X, Y in ∆.
    We say that facets X and Y satisfying d(X, Y ) ≤ n form a distance normal pair
if the equality (6) holds. Any two adjacent facets form a distance normal pair. If
the path distance between two facets is equal to 2, then they form a distance normal
pair. It was noted above that any two facets in αn or βn form a distance normal
pair. Every geodesic joining distance normal pair X, Y will be called distance
normal geodesics if d(X, Y ) ≤ n.
Example 12. Let (W, S) be a Coxeter system and |S| = n. Consider the associated
Coxeter complex Σ(W, S). By Subsection 2.2, the graph Γn−1 (Σ(W, S)) can be
naturally identified with the Cayley graph C(W, S). Let si and sj be non-com-
muting elements of S. The path distance between the identity element e and
w = si sj si in C(W, S) is equal to 3. On the other hand, e and w correspond
to the facets {W 1 , . . . , W n } and {wW 1 , . . . , wW n }, respectively. Since we have
wW k = W k for every k 6= i, j, the intersection of these facets contains precisely
n − 2 vertices and they do not form a distance normal pair.
     The latter example can be generalized as follows.
Example 13. Let w and v be elements of W such that l(w−1 v) ≤ n. Then
d(w, v) ≤ n. The facets of Σ(W, S) corresponding to w and v form a distance
normal pair if and only if there is a reduced expression for w−1 v whose elements
are mutually distinct (the existence of such an expression implies that all reduced
expressions of w−1 v satisfy the same condition).

Theorem 1. Every distance normal geodesic of Γn−1 (∆) is contained in the (n−1)-

4.3. Z-connectedness of non-maximal faces. Two faces X and Y of the same
non-maximal rank k ≥ 1 are said to be weakly adjacent if their intersection is a
(k − 1)-face and there is no face containing both X and Y . This is a modification
of the relation defined for non-maximal singular subspaces of polar spaces [13,
Subsection 4.6.2]. Note that the non-maximal faces of βn considered in Example
11 are weakly adjacent.
Lemma 5. For every k ∈ [n − 2] any pair of weakly adjacent k-faces in ∆ cannot
be connected by a simple zigzag.
Proof. Let {xi }i∈N be the 0-shadow of a zigzag connecting weakly adjacent k-faces
X and Y . If the zigzag is simple, then there exists i such that
                                 X ∩ Y = {xi+1 , . . . , xi+k },
one of X, Y is {xi , . . . , xi+k } and the other is {xi+1 , . . . , xi+k+1 }. Since the rank k
is not maximal, we have k + 2 ≤ n and
                                 X ∪ Y = {xi , . . . , xi+k+1 }
is a face which contradicts the fact that X and Y are weakly adjacent.                       
