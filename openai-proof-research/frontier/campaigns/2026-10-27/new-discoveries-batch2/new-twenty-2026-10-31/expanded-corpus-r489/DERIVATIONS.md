## 320916: oriented-cut face proof, and the weighted-lift obstruction

Use actual 1101.0517v3 definitions pp3–5, 11–14 and Conjecture1 p18. An oriented cut q^S has q_ij=1 for i in S,j outside S, and zero otherwise. Here is a full ordinary proof of part(i), for the intended n>=3 (n=2 gives the same two-ray argument). Set A=S intersect T, B=S minus T, C=T minus S, D=V minus(S union T). Every within-class arc is zero in both cuts. In any point q of the common face these arcs stay zero. Triangle inequalities in both directions then imply that every ordered pair of nonempty classes has one common value: replacing an endpoint by another vertex in its class changes q by at most a zero arc. All class arcs zero in both cuts remain zero.

If all four classes occur, the only possibly nonzero arcs are AB,AC,AD,BC,CB,BD,CD. The triangle forms on B,D,C and C,D,B vanish on both cuts and give BC=BD and CB=CD. Those on B,A,C and C,A,B give AC=BC and AB=CB. The triangle on A,B,D gives AD=AB+BD. Consequently the whole vector is determined by x=AC=BC=BD and y=AB=CB=CD, with AD=x+y. It equals x q^S+y q^T. Nonnegativity gives x,y>=0.

With three nonempty classes, there are two patterns up to interchanging S,T and reversing every arc (both preserve the cone). For A,B,D, the only nonzero arcs are AB,BD,AD and the common triangle gives AD=AB+BD. For B,C,D the only nonzero arcs are BC,CB,BD,CD and the two common triangles give BC=BD and CB=CD. Again the face is exactly the nonnegative span of the two cuts. With two classes, distinct nonempty proper S,T must be complementary; the two possible arcs are unconstrained nonnegative coordinates. Equal cuts are not a pair of vertices. Thus every pair of distinct oriented cuts spans a two-dimensional face of WQMET. The same supporting equalities restrict to OCUT, so its skeleton is complete and is a subgraph of Sk(WQMET). The argument also establishes extremality: a decomposition of either cut in the larger cone lies in this two-dimensional face, where that cut is a boundary ray.
