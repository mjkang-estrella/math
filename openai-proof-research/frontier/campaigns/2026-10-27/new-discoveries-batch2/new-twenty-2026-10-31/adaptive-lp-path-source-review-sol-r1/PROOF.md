# Independent ordinary proof: auxiliary ensembles only

MathDB 376636. Personal mathematical review, no mathematical computation, enumeration, solver, or formal tool. This proves the following precise auxiliary results; it does not select an ensemble on behalf of the source.

## 1. Algorithm and the path

Use Algorithm 2 of cs/0703123v1, p6: minimize gamma^T x with initial constraints x_i>=0 for gamma_i>0 and x_i<=1 for gamma_i<0; solve; add every currently violated original parity inequality; repeat until none exists. On a check with neighbors {i,i+1}, the only odd subsets are the two singletons, giving x_i<=x_(i+1) and x_(i+1)<=x_i. No redundant parity checks are added in this algorithm. RPC generation in Section IV is a different algorithm.

Let L>=3, variables 1,...,L, and checks {i,i+1}, 1<=i<L. Their common binary kernel is precisely the repetition code. Rows are independent over F2: in any vanishing combination, the first coordinate forces the first coefficient to vanish, and induction forces all the others. Thus its rate is 1/L.

Suppose -3L<gamma_1<-2L and 1<gamma_i<3/2 for 2<=i<=L. For every 1<=r<=L the sum S_r=sum_(i<=r) gamma_i is strictly negative: S_r<-2L+3(r-1)/2<0. At the r-th solve (initial solve is r=1), the added inequalities on this path are exactly x_1<=...<=x_r. Its unique optimizer has r initial ones and L-r trailing zeros.

Proof of this assertion with the ACTUAL one-sided bounds: suffix coordinates have positive coefficient, lower bound zero, and no added incident inequality, so uniquely minimize at zero. For r=1, gamma_1<0 and x_1<=1 give x_1=1. For r>=2, put t=x_1. All other prefix coordinates are >=max(t,0), and setting each to max(t,0) is feasible and minimizes their strictly positive contributions for fixed t. For t<=0 the prefix cost is gamma_1*t>=0. For 0<=t<=1 it is S_r*t, uniquely minimized at t=1 because S_r<0. The feasible point with all prefix entries one has strictly negative cost and beats the t<=0 region. This proves existence and uniqueness, including the unbounded feasible directions. In particular gamma_1 alone is not mistakenly used to dominate an unconstrained negative variable. The bounds make every recession direction have nonnegative objective increment, strictly positive if nonzero; no unbounded-below ray exists.

At this optimizer, for r<L precisely the forward inequality x_r<=x_(r+1) is violated. Its reverse has values 0<=1. Every other edge has equal endpoint values, and both its inequalities hold at equality. Hence adding ALL cuts adds precisely this single forward inequality, proving induction. At r=L every edge has equal values, so no cut remains. Exactly L solves and L-1 nonterminal cut-addition rounds are required. Counting the final no-cut test as an iteration gives L executions; counting only successful additions gives L-1. Take L>alpha+2 to avoid any one-step convention issue. No reverse or additional original parity inequality can fire. All optima in the proof lie in the box, so adding both initial box bounds, or using final2008 Algorithm 2 with box-cut checks, changes nothing. The claim is not about arbitrary numerical tolerances or inexact solves.

The proof extends to L=2 with the same strict intervals; L=1 has no check and terminates immediately, and is not used. Open endpoints and gamma_i=0 events are excluded. No tie or degeneracy assumption is needed for the special path: its optimum is proved unique directly, though active constraints can be redundant in a larger problem.

## 2. Other components and global synchronization

For ANY nonzero objective coordinates and any subset of original cuts, the initial one-bound feasible region has compact objective sublevel sets: each term gamma_i*x_i is bounded below by min(0,gamma_i), and grows strictly in its single allowed unbounded direction. Added closed constraints preserve this property, and every partial LP contains a codeword, so the optimum exists. For degree-two checks, clipping each coordinate to [0,1] preserves every added order inequality. If any coordinate lies outside the box its objective contribution strictly improves upon clipping, while none worsens. Consequently every optimum lies in the box. There are only finitely many original cuts, and a retained cut cannot later be violated. Every nonterminal round adds a new cut, so the process terminates, irrespective of uniqueness.

On disjoint components the feasible region is a Cartesian product and the objective is a sum. Every global optimum projects to a local optimum; conversely a tuple of local optima is globally optimal. All-cut rounds act simultaneously on the components. A completed component cannot become incomplete while the other components continue: no local constraint changes, and every local optimum has the same cost; a.s. uniqueness suffices here, but the selected slow component alone already prevents early global termination for every possible global optimum. Thus the lower bound requires no favorable choices in other blocks.

For completeness, uniqueness holds a.s. under the Gaussian channel for every intermediate LP at fixed n. For each of finitely many objective sign patterns and finitely many subsets of original cuts, the feasible polyhedron has finitely many vertices, independent of objective magnitudes. A nonunique compact optimal face has two distinct vertices. Their equal-cost event lies on a proper linear hyperplane in objective space. A finite union of such hyperplanes, together with zero-coordinate events, has probability zero under a joint density. This argument handles adaptive selection of the constraints without conditioning incorrectly on a selected LP.

## 3. Fixed-L randomly relabeled blocks

Fix L first, then take b disjoint L-paths, with independently randomized variable/check relabeling if desired. Set n=bL, m=b(L-1). Blockwise row independence gives full row rank and actual rate R=1/L. All rows have weight two; endpoint columns have weight one, other columns weight two. The zero word is a valid transmitted word.

Use a fixed nondegenerate AWGN channel with antipodal symbols 0 mapped to +1, 1 to -1, independent noise N(0,sigma^2), 0<sigma<infinity. Equation (2) gives gamma_i=2r_i/sigma^2, independent Gaussians with everywhere positive density. Every prescribed nonempty bounded open interval has positive probability. Orient each path from its smaller-labeled endpoint (a measurable deterministic rule). Conditional on the entire matrix/relabeling, different blocks use disjoint independent channel coordinates, and each selected path event has the same strictly positive probability
p_L=Pr(-3L<gamma<-2L) * Pr(1<gamma<3/2)^(L-1).
Therefore conditional no-event probability is exactly (1-p_L)^b, independent of the matrix labels. Averaging gives the same unconditional probability. In fact the event indicators are unconditionally independent too: every joint conditional Bernoulli law is the same product law. No orientation choices depend on channel samples. An invariant alternative is to permit either endpoint as the negative endpoint; for L>=2 the two events are disjoint, also giving positive probability.

By section 2, Pr(T_b>alpha)>=1-(1-p_L)^b ->1 when L>alpha+2. This is an actual probability amplification, not the probability of one adversarial long-vector objective. Both n and m increase at fixed positive rate. Failure of the iteration conjunct defeats any joint iteration/constraint assertion for this ensemble, whatever beta is. Degree-two checks have only two inequalities, so this mechanism does not refute a standalone constant beta claim.

Quantifier limitation: this construction chooses L after alpha, changing rate and ensemble. It contradicts a bound uniform in the stated degree distributions and rates if these block ensembles are admissible. Alone it would not contradict a formulation allowing alpha to depend on an already fixed ensemble or confidence in an unrestricted way.

## 4. A SINGLE uniform fixed-degree FULL-RANK matrix ensemble

The following closes a more standard degree-sequence route without relying on the author's unpaid configuration-model concentration sketch. For each b>=1 let n=3b and m=2b. Fix 2b labeled columns to have weight one and b labeled columns to have weight two. Let H be UNIFORM among all binary m-by-n matrices with these column sums, every row sum two, and full row rank over F2. This set is nonempty: b disjoint 3-paths provide examples. Labels of the degree classes can additionally be randomized independently; nothing below changes. This is a precise auxiliary ensemble, not a claim that the source specifies it.

Interpret each row as an undirected edge on its two distinct column labels. Repeated edges would give equal rows, and any cycle gives a vanishing sum of its edge rows over F2; both are impossible at full row rank. Conversely any forest has independent edge rows, by leaf elimination. Thus the rank condition is equivalent here to being a forest. Every vertex has degree one or two, so every component is a path with two distinct degree-one endpoints. There are exactly b components (2b endpoints). There are no isolated vertices, loops, parallel edges, or cycle components. This addresses simplicity and actual rank directly instead of asserting that conditioning preserves an unconditioned whp statement.

Here is an exact bijective count. Pair the 2b labeled endpoints, orient each pair by the smaller endpoint label, and order pairs by their smaller endpoints. Choose a weak composition (K_1,...,K_b) of b into b parts. Take a permutation of the b labeled internal vertices; split its list into consecutive lists of lengths K_1,...,K_b; insert the j-th list on the oriented j-th endpoint pair. Finally label the 2b edges by the row indices. Every forest matrix has exactly one such representation. There are (2b-1)!! endpoint pairings, binom(2b-1,b-1) compositions, b! internal permutations, and (2b)! row labelings. Every factor except the composition is constant across compositions. Therefore (K_1,...,K_b) is uniform on these weak compositions. The j-th path has K_j+2 variables. This distribution is exchangeable in the b slot indices, even though the endpoint-pair ordering is deterministic.

Fix k>=1 and let N_k be the number of slots with K_j=k. For all sufficiently large b, stars-and-bars gives
q_b=Pr(K_1=k)=binom(2b-k-2,b-2)/binom(2b-1,b-1),
r_b=Pr(K_1=k,K_2=k)=binom(2b-2k-3,b-3)/binom(2b-1,b-1).
Only b>=3 and b>=2k are needed for these displayed ordinary binomial counts; finitely many smaller b are irrelevant to the limit. For fixed k, cancel factorials, obtaining
q_b=(b-1)(b)_k/(2b-1)_(k+1) -> q=2^(-k-1),
r_b=(b-1)(b-2)(b)_(2k)/(2b-1)_(2k+2) -> q^2,
where (a)_j is a falling factorial, with (a)_0=1. These limits follow by dividing the finitely many factors by b. No claimed concentration is inferred from expectation alone: exchangeability gives
Var(N_k)/b^2 = q_b/b + (1-1/b)r_b - q_b^2 ->0.
Chebyshev then gives N_k/b -> q in probability, in particular N_k>=qb/2 with probability tending to one.

Choose L=k+2>alpha+2 (k>=1). Conditional on H, its N_k length-L path events are independent with common probability p_L from section 3. Hence
Pr(no such slow path) <= Pr(N_k<qb/2) + (1-p_L)^(qb/2) ->0.
The path proof and global factorization imply Pr(T_b>alpha)->1. This holds FOR EVERY finite alpha in the SAME ensemble sequence H_b, with fixed rate 1/3 and fixed column-degree proportions 2/3 degree one and 1/3 degree two. The ensemble, rate, degree distribution, and channel are selected before alpha. Only the diagnostic finite length L and the positive small constant p_L depend on alpha. Thus no finite alpha depending on ensemble, fixed confidence 1-epsilon (0<epsilon<1), rate or this degree distribution rescues a size-independent high-probability iteration bound for THIS ensemble. Beta cannot rescue the conjunction.

Equivalently, a stub configuration matching conditioned to give a binary full-row-rank matrix has this law: each admissible labeled matrix is realized by the same product of column-stub and row-stub factorials. The bijective forest proof already supplies the conditioned law and needs no lower bound on the conditioning probability. The UNCONDITIONED configuration model with cycles is not asserted to have actual rate 1/3. The proof makes no claim for minimum variable degree >=2, connected ensembles, uniform unrestricted dense matrices, or positive-distance capacity-approaching code families.

## 5. Endpoint and scope

This is an independently checked ordinary proof of slow convergence for expressly defined ensembles, including a uniform full-rank prescribed-degree model. It proves divergence in probability of iteration counts for the latter. It does not prove a logarithmic growth rate or a sharp typical count. The remaining source issue is whether the historical informal phrase 'random parity-check code ... arbitrary degree distributions' is intended to include this ensemble and its degree-one disconnected support. Without that source/scientific bridge this auxiliary theorem is not a full-literal discovery, and supplies no acceptance credit or formal dispatch.
