# Sustained ordinary routes

All three routes below were investigated without programs for mathematics. Source/hash/text/PDF processing only was executed.

Sequenceability — 354107 / 354737. The source's word “sequenceable” is the union of linear and rotational sequencing, not linear alone. The record omits that union in its definition and omits finite size explicitly. The spurious counterexample {a,-a} merely exploits that omission and earns zero. Work on the intended finite statement. Strong sequenceability implies t-weak sequenceability for k>t: every pair except possibly (0,k) is distinct; (0,k) is not tested because k>t. Conversely, the universal weak conjecture, applied with t=k-1 for each k>=2, tests every pair except (0,k), exactly giving the intended strong conjecture; k=1 is immediate since its sole element is nonzero, and the empty ordering is vacuous. If the total sum is nonzero even (0,k) differs; if zero the only permitted equality is rotational. Thus the all-t pair is equivalent on the finite intended domain, not two independent discoveries. A fixed t assertion is strictly less than the entire quantified family.

Exact completed history: r360 354108 is only total-zero and inverse-pair-free; 354738 / 354739 are inverse-pair-free and equivalent via the same t=k-1 reduction. Our new pair allows inverse pairs and both sums, so those holds are relevant narrower obstructions, not blanket duplication. r360 arbitrary-size/splitting-collision holds remain unpaid.

Route S1: greedy extension. After a valid prefix of length h, appending x fails a local test only if x=s_i-s_h for one of at most t previous relevant sums. Thus more than t available elements permits a step. This proves a long prefix, not the last t positions. The obstruction is exhaustion, not existence of a bad small test case. The original weak paper §3 repairs bounded tails for t<=3 in cyclic groups and uses the uniqueness of the involution of a cyclic group; an arbitrary abelian group may have many involutions. The explicit branch uses 2u=0 => u in {0,n/2}; carrying it to elementary 2-groups is unjustified. A universal tail-repair theorem for arbitrary t and arbitrary abelian groups is missing.

Route S2: merge distinct a,b to c=a+b outside A union {0}; recursively order the smaller set, then split c. All old partial sums survive, but the inserted sum P+a (or P+b in reversed split order) can collide with an old partial sum. The existence of a legal merge does not force either split orientation to avoid every old sum. The 2026 companion Corollary2.5 supplies legal merges unless a whole subgroup-minus-zero case is already sequenceable; its proof was read. That lemma does not pay the split-collision gap. Its Theorems1.4–1.6 cover sizes20,22,23 with respective assumptions, not unbounded sizes. The 2026 cyclic least-prime-divisor bounds are restricted to cyclic groups and parameter ranges. Its weak definition also needs comparison to the original s0 convention before importing it. No universal proof results from either route. No numerical/source truth audit was performed.

Degree sequences — 354226 / 354475. In both sources degree sequence means already graphic, simple loopless undirected realizations and even n; k is a nonnegative integer (positive in nontrivial source bounds). The “d70” and free pi in354475 are transcription defects, not extra scope. Sorting an unsorted degree list is a simultaneous permutation of vertex labels. A realization with k disjoint perfect matchings relabels to one for the sorted list; subtracting k commutes with the same permutation, and graphicness is invariant under relabeling. Conversely undo the permutation. Together with identifying d70=pi this proves equivalence of the two records' intended endpoints, not merely family overlap. The literal transcription defects are not a significant refutation. Necessity: delete all k perfect matchings from the realizing graph; each degree drops exactly k and simplicity is preserved, yielding a realization of D_k(pi). Sufficiency is the unpaid direction.

Route D1: Kundu obtains a k-factor F in a realization, but a regular graph need not be 1-factorizable. In particular an odd cycle has no perfect matching (although the full realization has even n, even-order regular factors can have odd components). Repeatedly deleting matchings requires showing each residual factor admits another matching. Connectivity alone is not preserved on deletion. The 2023 source Theorem8 proof repairs connectivity while protecting an existing factor only under k>=d1/2+r or k>=n-1-dn+2r. These inequalities cannot be dropped at late r. The source explicitly notes regular highly connected counterexamples to a desired partition into a 2-factor and k-2 matchings, which blocks this shortcut, not the degree-sequence existential conjecture. This is a precise residual realization-choice problem.

Route D2: maximize the number r of extracted matchings and minimize deficiency of the residual (k-r)-factor. A switch that joins exposed vertices might destroy a previously extracted matching, so swaps must respect edge colours/factor classes. The 2025 source §7 opening and Claims7.1–7.7 were read: Gallai–Edmonds components and restricted multiswitches give a partial r bound. Need a colour-preserving exchange forcing a larger matching at every r<k, across all odd residual components; the available counting estimate does not imply r=k. Theorem2 gives min(ceil((k+5)/3),k) matchings inside a k-factor, not all k. Further results giving more matchings outside that factor do not make it factorable. The introduction calls k>=6 unresolved; this is contextual evidence, not a current worldwide status claim. The institutional journal file returned403 and was not retried.
