size is equal to k. Let ω = (a1 , a2 , . . . , ak ) be an ordering of the elements of A and we
define its partial sums s = (s0 , s1 , . . . , sk ) by s0 = 0 and si = a1 + · · · + ai for i > 0. We
denote the sum of the elements of A by ΣA. As G is abelian, for any ordering of the
elements of A the final partial sum sk is equal to ΣA. Then, as done in [4] and [9], we
give the following definitions.

Definition 1.1.      • The ordering ω is said to be a sequencing (or a linear sequencing)
     of A if the elements of s are distinct;

   • the ordering ω is said to be an R-sequencing (or a rotational sequencing) of A if the
     elements of s are distinct with the exception that s0 = 0 = sk ;

   • a subset A of an abelian group is said to be sequenceable if it admits a sequencing
     or an R-sequencing;

   • an abelian group G is said to be strongly sequenceable if every subset A of G \ {0}
     is sequenceable.

    We remark that a set A can have a linear sequencing only if ΣA 6= 0. On the other
hand, A can have a rotational sequencing only when ΣA = 0.

several questions and conjectures (see also [6, 7, 9, 10, 14]).

Conjecture 1.2 (Alspach-Liversidge [4]). Every abelian group is strongly sequenceable.

to strengthen Theorem 1 so that the realization preserved a subgraph found in the original graph.

Theorem 2. If there is a graph G0 = (V, E) with edge-disjoint spanning subgraphs F and Z0 with
δ(Z0 ) > ∆(F ) such that |E(Z0 )| ≥ |V | − 1 when δ(G0 ) = 1, then there is a G ∈ R(G0 , G0 − E(Z0 ))

the second part follows from Theorem 2 by letting F be empty and Z0 = G0 .

realization of π(G0 ) by at most |V (G0 )| − 1 edges. For δ(G0 ) ≥ 2, if we let Z0 be a spanning forest

identify finite subsets of Rd with integer points inside d-dimensional cube [n]d ,
where [n] = {1, . . . , n}. We establish the following estimation for the number

Theorem 1.1. If M ⊂ [n]d is a basic subset, then
                              |M| ⩽ dn − (d − 1).
This boundary cannot be improved.

subsets that are minimal in the sense of inclusion (to be precise, we call a non-

an element of a subset, where by layers we mean hyperplanes orthogonal to

Theorem 1.2. If M ⊂ [n]d is a minimal non-basic subset such that every

                           2n ⩽ |M| ⩽ dn − (d − 2).
     The condition for a subset M ⊂ [n]d to be basic is equivalent to the
consistency of the corresponding system of linear equations with integer co-
efficients. In particular, there exists an algorithm, polynomial in |M|, for
determining whether the subset M is basic. For d = 2, however, one can
indicate a simpler algorithm based on the following criterion: a finite subset
M ⊂ [n]2 is basic if and only if M does not contain a closed array, that is,
a non-trivial finite array with the coinciding first and last points [4]. The
reader can think about this criterion in the following way. Let us color the
points of a closed array in two colors, say, color odd points in red and even
points in blue respectively. Then every layer contains the same number of red
and blue points. Extending the idea of coloring onto Rd , we get the following
theorem.

in every layer. Then M is non-basic if and only if there exists a non-empty
subset K ⊂ M and a coloring of K in two colors such that every layer
contains the same number of elements of each color.
