Since an edgeless graph has inversion diameter 0, Lemma 2.1 implies diam(I(G)) ⩽ |V (G)| − α(G) where α(G) denotes the maximum size of an independent set in G. Since every non-complete graph has an independent set of size 2, it follows that a graph G has inversion diameter at most |V (G)| − 1 if and only if it is complete. Thus 1-Dual-Inversion-Diameter is polynomialtime solvable. An interesting variant of the inversion graph is the unlabelled inversion graph, denoted by U(G), which is obtained from I(G) by identifying vertices corresponding to isomorphic orientations. Clearly, diam(U(G)) ⩽ diam(I(G)). Hence it would be interesting to investigate whether all the upper bounds on diam(I(G)) obtained in this paper can be improved for diam(U(G)). For example, the bound diam(I(G)) ⩽ n − 1 is tight, but we believe it is not tight for diam(U(G)). Note that inv(n) is the eccentricity of the transitive tournament in U(Kn ). It has been proved in [APS+ 24, AHH+ 22] that √ n − 2 n log n ⩽ inv(n) ⩽ n − ⌈log(n + 1)⌉. Together with Theorem 2.3, this implies the following. √ Corollary 10.11. n − O( n log n) ⩽ diam(U(Kn )) ⩽ diam(I(Kn )) = n − 1. However, we believe that the upper bound on inv(n) also holds for diam(U(Kn )). Conjecture 10.12. diam(U(Kn )) ⩽ n − ⌈log(n + 1)⌉.
