Dvořák, Morrison, Noel, Norin and Postle proved the bound you're asking about, and the proof uses Brègman's theorem on permanents.

- **The result:** A simple graph with n vertices and average degree d has at most ((1 + O(log d / d)) · d/e)^n cycles. This proves Conjecture 6.1 of Arman and Tsaturian, and is asymptotically tight as seen by considering random graphs.
- **The method:** The paper's summary states that Theorem 5, which is proven using Brègman's Theorem, gives an upper bound on the number of cycles in a simple graph with n vertices and average degree d. The proof uses Brègman's Theorem on the permanent of a matrix.
- **Earlier bounds:** Arman and Tsaturian's thesis reports that, for graphs with the most cycles among those with n vertices and average degree d tending to infinity, the count is between roughly (d/e)^n and (d/2)^n, up to (1+o(1)) factors. The (d/2)^n upper bound is the weaker one that this result sharpens. The snippet is garbled, so check the thesis directly for the exact statement.
- **Related edge-count results:** The same authors' Király-style question about graphs with m edges was answered by Dvořák et al., who show a simple graph with m edges has at most O(γ^m) cycles for γ ≈ 1.384. The best known lower bound is Ω(κ1^m) for κ1 = (2+2√2)^{1/5} ≈ 1.3701 by Arman and Tsaturian. The paper's Conjecture 2 proposes that the true upper bound matches that construction.
