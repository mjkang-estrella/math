The search found the Galvin–Thanh work and its follow-up, but nothing that directly addresses variance or the "graphical Stirling numbers" angle in your query.

- **Original result (2013):** Galvin and Thanh showed that for forests with n vertices and c(n) components, the number of classes in a uniformly chosen partition into independent sets is asymptotically normal, provided c(n) = o(√(n/log n)) ([2-3]). The same paper proves that the generating functions have all real zeroes ([2-3]). The arXiv rendering of the abstract gives a different growth bound, so check the published version.
- **Conjecture:** They conjectured that for any acyclic graph on n vertices, the graphical Stirling numbers are asymptotically normal, with no restriction on the number of components ([7-2], [7-7]). Their proof needed the component bound because it was the only route they had to show the variance tends to infinity ([7-9]).
- **Resolution (Galvin, 2016):** Galvin settled the conjecture in the affirmative. He extended it to graphs that are co-chromatic with a quasi-threshold graph and have negligible chromatic number ([8-5]). The proof uses Navon's work and Engbers–Galvin–Hilyard on the Weyl algebra normal order problem, plus Kahn's work on matching polynomials ([8-6]). The source is Graphs and Combinatorics 32 (2016), 639–647.
