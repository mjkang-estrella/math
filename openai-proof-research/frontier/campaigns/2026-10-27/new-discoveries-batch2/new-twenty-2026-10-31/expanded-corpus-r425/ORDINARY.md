## 327153: uniformity, subfields, and the bounded-degree bridge

Read 1401.6992v1 Remark 1.1, Lemma 2.1 proof, Definition 2.2, Lemma 2.3 proof, Theorem 3.2 proof, Theorem 3.9 proof and Conjecture 3.10. Characteristic is greater than two; d>=2. Remark 1.1 makes constants depend only on dimension and not field size. The conjecture states an arbitrary polynomial, with no displayed degree bound. Latest/final/correction bodies have not been checked. These observations concern the actually acquired version only.

Route A, attempted direct proof: for nonzero x, P_x(t)=Q(tx) has nonzero constant Q(0), so is not the zero polynomial. If deg Q<=D, it has at most D roots. Consequently |V intersection l_x|<=D, and also |E intersection l_x|<=D. To spell out the source bridge rather than merely naming it: its second-moment argument gives
  sum_t nu(t)^2 <= |E|^2 |F|^2/q + q^(2d-1)|E| B(E,F),
and Plancherel gives B(E,F)<=D |F|/q^d. Cauchy-Schwarz therefore yields
  |Pi(E,F)| >= q |E||F| / (|E||F|+D q^d).
Every ingredient is paid by the source's actual Lemma 2.1 proof and ordinary polynomial root bound. If |E||F|>=C q^d, this is >= Cq/(C+D). This is uniform in field size for fixed D, but not uniform over D. In particular, if D is bounded in terms of d, this is a complete source-lemma adaptation, not an eligible new result. Q constant nonzero gives empty V and vacuous antecedent; Q zero is excluded by Q(0)!=0. No characteristic-two shortcut was used.

Route B, adversarial unrestricted-degree family: fix d=2. Let p range over odd primes, K=F_p and L=F_(p^2); q=p^2. Choose r=floor(sqrt(p)) distinct multiplicative cosets alpha_j K^* in L^*. There are p+1 cosets, so the choice exists. Put
  F=K^2,
  E=union_(j=1..r) alpha_j(K^2 minus {0}).
The r sets in E are disjoint. Indeed, alpha_i x=alpha_j y!=0 with x,y in K^2 forces alpha_i/alpha_j in K^* by a nonzero coordinate. Hence |E|=r(p^2-1), |F|=p^2, and |E||F|/q^2=r(1-p^-2), tending to infinity. For a nonzero x in K^2, the linear functional y -> x dot y maps K^2 onto K (choose a nonzero coordinate and solve for it), so Pi(E,F)=union_j alpha_j K, with size 1+r(p-1). Its ratio to q tends to zero, since r<=sqrt(p). Thus for every proposed dimension-only C>1 and c>0, an odd prime p sufficiently large makes the size antecedent true and the conclusion false.

A single explicit polynomial realizes the required ambient variety:
  Q(X_1,X_2)=(1-X_1^(q-1))(1-X_2^(q-1)).
Fermat in L shows Q(0,0)=1 and Q(x)=0 at every other vector. Thus V=L^2 minus {0}, and E subset V. Its degree grows with q. The literal's polynomial hypothesis, origin exclusion, field range and asymptotic constants all enter the construction; this is not a small-field computation. Existence of arbitrarily large odd primes and the quadratic field extension is ordinary mathematics, not executed code.

Route C, trying to avoid the degree issue: replace V by a fixed bounded-degree hypersurface excluding zero and retain a large family of origin rays. This fails structurally: the nonzero constant polynomial on every ray has at most D roots. The second-moment bound above already proves a positive density conclusion once constants may depend on D. A bounded-degree counterexample to that conclusion cannot be supplied by this mechanism. Allowing Q to vary while keeping degree bounded does not change the root bound.

Significance adjudication is deliberately conservative. Strongest case for significance: Route B negates the actual displayed dimension-uniform universal statement in every sufficiently large field of a family, not just an endpoint, and shows origin exclusion alone supplies no uniform radial sparsity. Strongest contrary case: its decisive freedom is precisely unbounded polynomial degree; the intended bounded-complexity version is immediately settled by the paper's own Lemma 2.1/2.3 and root-count proof. This lane does not promote a degree/uniformity formulation defect into campaign credit. Disposition: HOLD/INELIGIBLE_FORMULATION for discovery purposes, no FULLCANDIDATE and no formal dispatch. ## 327063: all-list tree ranking, three routes

Read Definition 1.1/list-ranking definition, Proposition 1.5 with proof, Conjecture 1.7, Propositions 6.1 and 6.2 proofs, Theorem 6.3 proof including its two cases. The displayed exponential threshold is 2^(p+2)-2p-4; superscripts are damaged in plain text, so this exponent is interpreted from the surrounding induction, not a linear expression. Figure 3 is not visually inspected; no figure-dependent proof certification claimed. No current final journal or companion-body clearance.

First pay the lower bound: assign all q leaves lists {q,...,2q-2} of length q-1 and all internal vertices lists {1,...,q-1}. Two leaves must repeat a label, and the unique path between them has only internal vertices, all with smaller labels. No ranking exists. This is exactly source Proposition 1.5 and receives zero credit. The task is the upper bound for every q-element list when p<q.

Route A, global maximum root: let a vertex v have the largest label M appearing anywhere. Assign M to v. Remove M from every remaining list; each has at least q-1 choices, and if every component of T-v has at most q-1 vertices, distinct-label choices in each component complete a ranking. Greedy injection works since each component has at most q-1 vertices and all lists have that many choices. Paths crossing v have maximum M; paths in a component have distinct labels. Since n=p+q<2q, a centroid has components of size <=floor(n/2)<=q-1. Thus this proves the conditional case where a global-largest label occurs at a centroid (or any vertex with that component bound). It does not permit moving the largest label from a leaf's list to the centroid. Distinct labels everywhere cannot work uniformly: identical q-element lists occur on p+q>q vertices.

Route B, leaf deletion induction: if a leaf has the global maximum, assigning it that label and deleting it is safe only if the remaining tree can be ranked from lists of size q-1 after removing the used label. If its parent has degree at least three, the remaining parameters are (p,q-1), so p<q survives only if q>=p+2. The boundary q=p+1 leaves p=q-1 and exits the induction hypothesis. If the parent has degree two, deleting the leaf makes that parent a leaf: the new parameters are (p-1,q), not (p-1,q-1); lists have only q-1 remaining choices, so the conjectured q-leaf upper bound cannot be invoked either. Retaining the leaf label in other lists may also fail ranking: another occurrence is an endpoint of a path with no guaranteed larger interior label. Thus neither deletion case pays the list-size invariant at the critical boundary.

Route C, adapting the actual special-vertex proof: source Theorem 6.3 reserves p leaves with increasing chosen labels below an internal maximum m_u and deletes p+1 labels before induction in a large component. It needs q_u-p >= q/2-p >= 2^((p-1)+2)-2(p-1)-4. Under only q=p+1, q/2-p is negative for p>1, so the component can have no usable leaf reserve at all. The no-special-vertex branch likewise uses residual size q/2-2p+1>p, which is false near q=p+1. This is a precise obstacle to compressing its threshold, not a proof the conjecture fails. Potential repair would be a variable-list induction that charges deletions only on paths whose separators already have larger labels, but no such invariant or complete exchange lemma was obtained. Stars/double stars are proved publicly in Propositions 6.1/6.2, so extending those special cases alone earns zero.

Unpaid lemma: for every tree with p<q and every q-uniform list assignment, choose separators and repeated labels so every equal-label pair is separated by a larger label, including the q=p+1 boundary, without requiring a centroid to contain a prescribed large label. No ordinary full solution. Formal feasibility: finite trees and finite ordered lists are manageable definitions, but the combinatorial lemma is missing; no Lean execution authorized.

## 346850: shortest paths rather than arbitrary rainbow paths

Read 2006.06551v1 introduction definitions, main bound and proof-outline, Lemmas 1-3 with proofs in Take 1, final Lemma 32 and Theorem 1 proof and conclusion Conjectures 1/2. Much of middle Take 3 and its material figures remains unread, so its full proof is not certified by this lane. The separately identified final 2022 Discrete Mathematics 345,112829 body at the author institution returned403 before body; no alternate delivery attempted. Search excerpts do not pay that body gate.

Route A, maximum induced forest F: give its edges different colors, then try to attach outside vertices using existing colors. Maximality proves every outside vertex has two neighbors in one forest component, for otherwise adding it creates no cycle. That is the source's actual Lemma2. It gives detours and the source's rainbow construction, but the conjecture requires shortest rainbow paths. A spanning tree path may be much longer than the graph geodesic, and contractions lose distances. Therefore even a successful f-1 rainbow coloring via this route would not prove the strong conjecture. The missing lemma must control a geodesic for every pair, not merely preserve connectivity through contracted trees. Paying the additive-three gap in rc separately still would not supply this lemma.

Route B, pendant-clique obstruction attempt: take a clique of k>=3 support vertices, attaching one private leaf to each. All k pendant edges must receive different colors: the unique shortest path between leaves at distinct supports uses both. Thus src>=k. Conversely use k distinct pendant colors and color the clique edge ij with any pendant color belonging to a third support. Every leaf-leaf geodesic uses its two distinct pendant colors and that third color; leaf-support geodesics use two different colors. Hence src=k. Every induced forest contains at most two clique vertices, while all k leaves can be retained with two supports, so f=k+2. Thus the attractive forced-distinct-edge obstruction has src=f-2, not a refutation. For multiple private leaves at each of >=3 supports, the same proof works with all pendant colors distinct and core-edge colors chosen outside its two support groups: again f=number_of_leaves+2 and src=number_of_leaves. This is an ordinary symbolic family, not a finite enumeration; it is not offered as new.

Route C, block/tree extension: a tree has f=n and every pair of distinct edges lies on some path, which is its endpoints' unique geodesic. Hence all tree edges must be distinct and src=n-1=f-1. Attaching triangles or clique blocks can permit color reuse, but the pendant-clique calculation shows that forcing independent colors simultaneously increases the induced forest. No gadget with a proved mismatch between the metric coloring obstruction and maximum induced forest size was obtained. A block induction would need a palette-combination lemma for geodesics through all cut vertices while accounting for exactly the forest vertices saved by each block; neither the all-block case nor arbitrary 2-connected blocks is paid.

Strongest significance case for a real witness: geodesic color conflicts can differ radically from ordinary rainbow detours, so a short metric witness would not automatically be routine. None was found. Disposition PARK with precise metric palette lemma and final-source403 gate, no full survivor.
