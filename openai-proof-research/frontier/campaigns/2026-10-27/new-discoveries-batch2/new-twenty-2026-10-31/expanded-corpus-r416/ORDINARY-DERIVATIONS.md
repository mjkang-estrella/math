## 336960 — full scope positive proof
Statement: every finite non-walk-regular simple graph G on n vertices has at most n-1 positive real beta with walk entropy log n. Entropy presupposes n>0; the empty graph is not a non-walk-regular counterexample. No connectedness or regularity assumption is imposed.

Let A be the real symmetric zero-diagonal adjacency matrix. Choose an orthonormal real eigenbasis, group equal eigenvalues, and denote the distinct eigenvalues by lambda_1,...,lambda_d, d<=n. Spectral projection E_j gives A^l=sum lambda_j^l E_j and exp(tA)=sum exp(t lambda_j) E_j. These identities follow by applying both sides to every vector in the eigenbasis; hence repeated eigenvalues and disconnected graphs cause no gap. Exp(tA) is positive definite because every exp(t lambda_j)>0. Its diagonal entries q_v(t) are positive, and their sum Z(t) is positive.

Put p_v=q_v/Z. The entropy equals log n exactly when p_v=1/n for all v. Indeed strict convexity of x log x on (0,infinity), whose second derivative is 1/x, gives sum p_v log(n p_v)>=0, with equality precisely when all n p_v are equal. Thus equality of entropy is equivalent to equality of all q_v.

Non-walk-regularity supplies vertices u,v and an integer l>=0 with (A^l)_uu != (A^l)_vv: matrix multiplication counts closed walks by appending the final edge, proved inductively in l. Consequently f(t)=q_u(t)-q_v(t)=sum_j c_j exp(lambda_j t) is not identically zero: its l-th derivative at zero is that nonzero difference. Omit zero coefficients and write m<=d for the number remaining. Notice f(0)=0 and f'(0)=0, since A^0 has diagonal 1 and A has diagonal 0.

Zero lemma including multiplicities: a nonzero sum of m real exponentials with distinct real exponents has at most m-1 real zeros counted with multiplicity. Proof by induction on m. For m=1 there are none. Order exponents and divide by the positive exponential of the least exponent. Zeros and multiplicities are unchanged. The derivative is a nonzero sum of m-1 exponentials with distinct exponents (each remaining coefficient is multiplied by a nonzero exponent difference). For any finite ordered list of zeros with total multiplicity N, the derivative has at least N-1 zeros counted with multiplicity: a zero of order a contributes a-1 at that point, and Rolle contributes one in each open interval between consecutive distinct zeros. Induction gives N-1<=m-2. If there were infinitely many zeros, a finite list violating this bound could be chosen. This also proves finiteness. A zero of infinite order would force every derivative to vanish and hence the analytic finite exponential sum to vanish identically; alternatively the same induction excludes arbitrarily high orders. All functions used are real analytic.

The zero at 0 has order at least two. Thus there are at most m-3 positive zeros of f. Necessarily m>=3. Every positive maximum-entropy temperature is one of these zeros. Therefore there are at most d-3<=n-3, and in particular at most n-1, such temperatures. This closes every graph/temperature quantifier of 336960. The claimed stronger number is a byproduct, not a separate credited family.

Grouping repeated eigenvalues before differentiating prevents duplicate-exponent cancellation errors. A nonzero difference is selected from a known unequal walk count; no assumption that every pair differs is made. Multiplicity at zero was kept separate from positive temperatures.

## 336875 — exact finite refutation of printed statement

In cyclic order, two consecutive remaining vertices are adjacent in G unless the intervening deleted run has length at least two. There cannot be two such long runs, because they would require at least four deleted vertices. Hence all consecutive survivor links except possibly one exist. If none fail the survivors form a cycle; if one fails the remaining links form a spanning path. Thus G-D is connected for every |D|<4.
