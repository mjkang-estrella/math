336831 Let $G=\langle S\rangle$ be a group with $S=\{a^{\pm},b^{\pm}\}$, satisfying $(ab)^2=1$, and suppose that the vertex-connectivity of $\Gamma(G,S)$ satisfies

$$
\kappa(\Gamma(G,S))\geq 2.
$$

A **Hamilton circle** is a homeomorphic image of $S^1$ in the Freudenthal compactification that contains every vertex. **Rankin-type conjecture.** Then $\Gamma(G,S)$ contains a Hamilton circle. This is proposed as an infinite extension of Rankin's finite Hamilton-cycle theorem; the source does not establish the conjecture.
336832 Let $\Gamma$ be a two-ended transitive graph. A **finite Hamilton cover** is a finite collection of Hamiltonian spanning subgraphs whose union covers the relevant graph structure, as in the source's preceding discussion. **Hamilton-cover conjecture.** Every two-ended transitive graph has a finite Hamilton cover. The conjecture is motivated by constructions producing Hamilton double rays, Hamilton circles, and infinite Hamilton covers in two-ended Cayley graphs; its status is not resolved in the source.
336834 Let $A_i$ denote an antichain of size $i$, and let $P$ be a poset whose Hasse diagram is contained in the Hasse diagram of $A_k\oplus A_2$. Let $M^{P}$ be the promotion matrix of $P$. **Linear-factorization conjecture.** The characteristic polynomial of $M^{P}$ factors into linear terms. This proposes a further family of posets with linear characteristic polynomials beyond the posets treated earlier in the paper; the source gives no proof or resolution.
336875 Let $C_n^{2k}$ denote the $2k$-th power of the cycle $C_n$. Let $G$ be a $2k$-connected graph of order $n$. **Dankelmann–Swart–Oellermann conjecture.**

$$
\operatorname{sdiam}_k(G)\leq \operatorname{sdiam}_k(C_n^{2k}).
$$

This conjectures that the $2k$-th power of the cycle maximizes the Steiner $k$-diameter among $2k$-connected graphs of order $n$, generalizing the stated extremal result for $2$-connected graphs. The supplied text gives no resolution.
336900 Let ${\mathbf u}$ be an aperiodic fixed point of a primitive morphism whose language is closed under reversal. The palindromic defect $D({\mathbf u})$ is either zero or infinite.

**Zero defect conjecture.**

$$
D({\mathbf u})=0\quad\text{or}\quad D({\mathbf u})=+\infty.
$$

This conjecture concerns the possible palindromic defects of aperiodic fixed points of primitive morphisms. It was disproved by counterexamples; the source also records partial positive results for marked morphisms and for fixed points over binary alphabets.
336957 Let $G$ be a simple graph, let $n_G$ be its number of vertices, and let $S^V(G,\beta)$ denote its walk entropy at temperature $\beta\geq0$. The graph $G$ is **walk-regular** when every vertex is contained in the same number of closed walks of every length. **Benzi's conjecture.** A graph $G$ is walk-regular if and only if there exists $\beta>0$ such that

$$
S^V(G,\beta)=\log n_G.
$$

Benzi's earlier theorem proves the implication from equality of the walk entropy on any set of real temperatures having an accumulation point; this conjecture asks whether one positive temperature suffices and remains open in the source.
336958 Let $G$ be a simple graph, let $n_G$ be its number of vertices, and let $S^V(G,\beta)$ denote its walk entropy at temperature $\beta\geq0$. The graph $G$ is **walk-regular** when every vertex is contained in the same number of closed walks of every length. **Estrada's conjecture.** A graph $G$ is walk-regular if and only if

$$
S^V(G,1)=\log n_G.
$$

The paper proves that some positive temperature has this characterization, but does not identify such a temperature explicitly; temperature $1$ is presented as an open conjectural choice.
336960 Let $G$ be a simple graph, let $n_G$ be its number of vertices, and let $S^V(G,\beta)$ denote its walk entropy at temperature $\beta>0$. **Temperature-count conjecture.** If $G$ is not walk-regular, then there are at most $n_G-1$ values $\beta>0$ such that

$$
S^V(G,\beta)=\log n_G.
$$

The paper proves only that this set of temperatures is finite. The conjecture proposes the sharper bound $n_G-1$ in terms of the number of vertices.
337017 Let $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{P}^4)$ denote the set of $f$-vectors of all $4$-polytopes, and let $\mathbin{{\scriptstyle\boxplus}”}$ be the modified addition realized by connected sum operations. **Affine semigroup conjecture.** The $f$-vector set of all $4$-polytopes is closed under the modified addition $\mathbin{{\scriptstyle\boxplus}”}$. That is, $\big(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{P}^4),\mathbin{{\scriptstyle\boxplus}”}\big)$ is an affine semigroup. The preceding proposition establishes the analogous statement for $3$-polytopes, while the claim for $4$-polytopes is left as a conjecture; the source gives no resolution.
337023 Let $D_{s,t}(n)$ denote the determinant associated with the corresponding family of cyclically symmetric rhombus-tiling regions, with the parity of $s-t$ distinguishing ordinary tiling counts from weighted counts. **Combinatorial reciprocity conjecture.** For integers $n\geqslant r\geqslant1$,

$$
D_{2r-1,0}(2n+1)=D_{0,0}(2n-2r+2)\big|_{\mu\to1-\mu-6n}.
$$

This conjecture proposes a reciprocity between determinants that count cyclically symmetric rhombus tilings and determinants that perform weighted counts. For concrete integer parameters, one determinant may fall outside the stated combinatorial interpretation, for example when the hole is larger than the hexagon.
337042 Let $\mathcal{G}_g$ be a grid graph with $D$ dimensions, whose dimension sizes are given by $\mathbf{d}[i]$ for $i\in\{1,\ldots,D\}$. The preceding large-grid hypothesis assumes

$$
\mathbf{d}[D]\geq 3\quad\text{and}\quad \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,D-1\}:\ \mathbf{d}[i]\geq 2+2\prod_{j=i+1}^{D}\mathbf{d}[j].
$$

**Large-grid conjecture.** The forthcoming results apply for grid graphs such that

$$
\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,D\}:\ \mathbf{d}[i]\geq 6.
$$

This is presented as a conjectural condition preceding results about Euclidean translations by standard basis vectors or their negatives being pseudo-minimal on grid graphs. The source does not provide evidence that this claim has been resolved.
337052 Let $p$ be a prime, let $r$ be a non-negative integer, and let $A\subset \mathbb{Z}_{p}$ satisfy

$$
|A+A| = 2|A| + r -1 \leq \tfrac{p}{2}+|A|-2, \qquad r\leq |A|-3.
$$

**Serra–Zémor's conjecture.** Then $A$ is included in an arithmetic progression of length $|A| + r$.

This is a conjectured analogue in $\mathbb{Z}_p$ of Freiman's $3k-4$ theorem for integers. Serra and Zémor proposed it and proved a result toward it; the source does not state a resolution.
337053 Let $p$ be a prime, let $r$ be a non-negative integer, and let $A,B$ be non-empty subsets of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ with $|A|\geq |B|$ satisfying

$$
|A+B|=|A|+|B|+r-1\leq \tfrac{1}{2}(p+|A|+|B|)-2, \qquad r\leq |B|-3.
$$

**The $r$-critical pair conjecture.** There exist intervals $I,J,K\subset \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}_p\setminus\{0\}$ such that

$$
n\cdot A\subset I,\qquad n\cdot B\subset J,\qquad n\cdot(A+B)\supset K,
$$

and $|I|\leq |A|+r$, $|J|\leq |B|+r$, and $|K|\geq |A|+|B|-1$.

This is the asymmetric two-set extension of the $3k-4$ phenomenon in $\mathbb{Z}_p$, and the source attributes this version to a conjecture of Grynkiewicz. The source notes that versions were proved for small sets but gives no resolution of the stated general conjecture.
337054 Let $p$ be a prime, let $r$ be a non-negative integer, and let $A_1,A_2,A_3$ be subsets of $\mathbb{Z}_p$ satisfying

$$
|A_1|,|A_2|,|A_3|>r+2,\qquad |A_1|+|A_2|+|A_3|>p-r,\qquad |A_1+A_2+A_3|<p.
$$

**Symmetric three-set $r$-critical pair conjecture.** There exist intervals $I_1,I_2,I_3\subset \mathbb{Z}_p$ and $n\in\mathbb{Z}_p\setminus\{0\}$ such that $n\cdot A_j\subset I_j$ and $|I_j|\leq |A_j|+r$ for $j=1,2,3$.

The source presents this as an equivalent symmetric version of the preceding $r$-critical pair conjecture. It is notable because it treats the three summand sets symmetrically; no resolution is given in the source.
337061 Let $C_n(1,a_2,\dots,a_m)$ be an edge-transitive circulant graph. The parameters $a_2,\dots,a_m$ are connection parameters modulo $n$. **General edge-transitive circulant conjecture.** There exist indices $i$ and $j$, possibly equal, such that

$$
a_i a_j\equiv\pm1\pmod n.
$$

This generalizes the preceding 3-circulant claim by allowing any number of connection parameters and by permitting $i=j$. The source provides no resolution, so the proposed necessary condition remains open.
337066 Let $F({\mathbf z})\in\mathbb{Z}(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ be a rational function, and let (A5) denote that the Jacobian matrix of the relevant system of equations (GenSys1)–(GenSys5) has full rank at its solutions, while (J2) denotes that the Jacobian matrix of (GenSys1)–(GenSys6) is non-singular at its solutions. **Genericity conjecture.** Assumptions (A5) and (J2) hold generically. The source proves genericity of assumption (A4), but leaves these two Jacobian conditions as the conjectural part needed for the effective minimal-critical-point analysis.
337097 Let $s>2$ and $n>2s$, and let an **$n$-multiset** be a multiset with $n$ elements. Two $n$-multisets are **$s$-equivalent** when they have the same multiset of $s$-sums. **Magical Triplet Conjecture.** For any three distinct $n$-multisets, some two of them are not $s$-equivalent to each other. This proposes an update to an earlier question about the number of mutually $s$-equivalent multisets; it remains open in the supplied text.
337143 Let $G$ be a tree, and let $i(G,x)$ denote its independence polynomial. A polynomial is **stable** here when all of its roots lie in the left half-plane.
**Tree stability conjecture.** The independence polynomial $i(G,x)$ is stable; equivalently, all of its roots lie in the left half-plane. The source reports that the authors found no independence root of a tree in the right half-plane, but supplies no proof or resolution.
337188 Let $(\Gamma,+)$ be an abelian group, let $S$ be a generating set, and let $G(\Gamma,S)$ be the Cayley multigraph with vertex set $\Gamma$. Its simplification is the simple graph obtained by removing parallel edges. **Alspach's conjecture.** If the simplification of $G(\Gamma,S)$ is $2k$-regular for some $k$, then it has a Hamilton decomposition. The paper studies infinite analogues of finite Hamilton-decomposition conjectures; its main theorem proves the corresponding one-ended case when all generators have infinite order, but the stated conjecture is not otherwise resolved in the supplied text.
337206 Let ${\cal F}$ be the exponential family or a family of unicritical polynomials. Two maps in ${\cal F}$ are **combinatorially equivalent** when they have the same combinatorial data, such as orbit portraits. **Combinatorial rigidity conjecture, quasiconformal version.** Any two combinatorially equivalent functions $f_c,f_{c'}\in{\cal F}$ are quasiconformally conjugate. This is presented as another version of the combinatorial rigidity conjecture, originally stated in the cited literature. The survey does not give a resolution for the exponential and unicritical families in this formulation.
