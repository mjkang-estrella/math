## 371149: a full classical route, then exact public subsumption

Put V = Z/nZ and join distinct vertices at cyclic distance at most p. A face of the literal total 2-cut complex D is a subset sigma whose complement contains a nonedge. Thus D = K*, where K is the clique complex of C_n^p and * is Alexander duality on the fixed n-element ground set. This is an equality of face sets, not an analogy to the independence complex of C_n^p.

Adamaszek, arXiv:1104.0433, Proposition 2.2, proves by explicit acyclic matching that Cl(G^p) collapses to G whenever girth(G) >= 3p+1. Its proof groups new edges into their unique maximal clique and matches faces by a central vertex. For G=C_n this applies on the entire literal range and gives K homotopy equivalent to S^1. Corollary 6.7 independently gives the same endpoint.

Here is the missing step beyond the initially insufficient homology argument. Every clique has at most p+1 vertices: fix a vertex 0, represent other clique vertices in [-p,p]. If a<0<b occur, then b-a<=2p<n-p, so adjacency forces b-a<=p. All representatives therefore lie in an interval of length at most p. Since n>=3p+1 and p>=3, n-3>p+1. The complement of any three vertices cannot be a clique. Consequently D contains the full 2-skeleton of the simplex and is simply connected. Combinatorial Alexander duality over the integers gives reduced H_i(D)=reduced H^(n-i-3)(K), hence Z only in degree n-4 and zero elsewhere. A simply connected CW complex with this integral homology is homotopy equivalent to S^(n-4): Hurewicz inductively gives vanishing lower homotopy groups and a map from S^(n-4) representing a homology generator; the homological Whitehead theorem makes it an equivalence. This is a complete classical reduction of the exact literal claim. It supplies no new mathematical credit under the brief.

The subsequent primary-source search is decisive: Chauhan–Shukla–Vinayak, arXiv:2512.04486, Conjecture 1.2 explicitly identifies Shen et al. Conjecture 5.1 and Theorems 1.3/3.10 prove it. The proof uses sequential element matchings; its single critical face has complement {0,n-2p,n-p}, hence dimension n-4. Read the actual conclusion and matching proof endpoint, not just the abstract. Carnero Bravo, arXiv:2602.21427, Theorem 48(d), independently gives exactly the literal range; Lemma 46, Proposition 47, Theorems 5 and 8 give the connectivity/duality route above. The broader k question 371150 in r97 is distinct, was held without a full proof, and supplies no credit for this k=2 specialization. No Pi plan.

## 379934: literal domain defect and failed substantive completion route

Read Woerdeman's partial-matrix definitions, Section 3, Conjecture 3.1, Theorem 3.2 and its proof. The literal density requirement says every k by k submatrix, with no restriction k>=r. At k=1, r>=3 makes (2-r)r negative, which is impossible for a nonempty matrix. For r=2 the bound is zero, forcing every entry unspecified. If dimensions are at least 2 this admits a rank-2 completion; if dimensions are unrestricted, the 1 by 1 wholly unspecified pattern has no rank-2 completion. This is solely a dimension/wording correction, explicitly excluded from credit.

For r=1, a cycle in the bipartite pattern on k row and k column vertices has at least 2k specified entries in that k-square, exceeding 2k-1. Thus the pattern is a forest. Every specified scalar is nonzero by the full-rank 1 by 1 hypothesis. On each tree choose a nonzero root factor, and propagate row factors u_i and column factors v_j uniquely using u_i v_j=a_ij along edges. There is no consistency obstruction because there are no cycles. Isolated vertices may receive factor 1; independent components can be scaled separately. The outer product u v^T completes all specified entries and has rank 1 when both dimensions are positive. This is the known source r=1 argument, no new credit.

Under the intended substantive reading k>=r and r<=min(m,n), a natural weakly-simplicial-vertex elimination route writes entries as inner products of r-vectors. Each eliminated row requires solving a system against its specified neighboring columns. Local full rank does not by itself guarantee that an already chosen global realization leaves those columns independent. The density count controls numbers of constraints, not these accidental dependencies. ## 323428: sustained parity route, still missing a global involution

Read both arXiv versions and Section 4 of v2, including the actual page-14 triangular-domain and five n=17 drawings. D8-orbit counting gives t_n congruent to s_n modulo 2, where s_n counts fully symmetric combinatorial rectangulations. Source Lemmas 8–10 and Proposition 11 prove symmetric representatives exist, tile orbits have sizes 1,4,8, s_n=0 outside n=0,1 modulo 4, and s_(4k+1)=s_(4k+4) by central subdivision. The literal conjecture therefore reduces exactly to s_(4k+1) congruent to 1 for k even and 0 for k odd.

First attempted completion: pair the four-element tile orbits locally. It fails because a symmetric tiling can have arbitrarily many four-orbits along the diagonal, not just its central orbit. Orbit-size divisibility gives modulo 4 support, not modulo 8 parity. Second attempt: toggle a first eligible subdivision in the fundamental triangular domain. The source picture has diagonal sawteeth and an arbitrary interior rectangulation. A toggle can change the number of tiles by 4 or 8, so it does not automatically act on a fixed-n set; choosing the first apparent corner also changes eligibility after the toggle. Third attempt: use only the source Morse matching. Its critical cells enumerate a different signed sum over tile numbers and singular vertices; its wedge-of-spheres theorem does not give fixed-n total-count parity.

Precise next lemma: construct a canonical encoding of symmetric combinatorial rectangulations by sawtooth boundary data plus interior wall incidences, then a tile-count-preserving involution with an explicitly classified fixed set whose cardinality has the alternating parity above. No such involution was obtained. The five n=17 configurations are printed source data, not a new counterexample. v1 Conjecture 12 incorrectly prints 8k+3 while its neighboring table/prose use 8k+4; v2 Conjecture 3 corrects it.
