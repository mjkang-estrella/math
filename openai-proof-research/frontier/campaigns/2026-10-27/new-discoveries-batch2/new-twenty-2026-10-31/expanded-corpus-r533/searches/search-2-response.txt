The search turned up the conjecture and partial progress, but not a full proof of it for all m.

- **The conjecture:** Ghosh, Győri, Martin, and coauthors conjectured that the maximum number of P_{2m+1} copies in an n-vertex planar graph is about 4m(n/m)^{m+1}. Cox's paper states this with an O(n^m) error term and says it is established for m = 2 (N_P(n,P_{2m+1})=4m(n/m)^{m+1}+O(n^m) for all m≥2).
- **Cox–Martin:** They gave an analytic approach, reducing the upper bound to a convex optimization problem with value ρ(m). They showed that N_P(n,P_{2m+1}) ≤ (ρ(m)/2 + o(1))n^{m+1}, and they verified their conjecture for m=3 by showing that ρ(3)=8/27. The general-m value of ρ(m) is not given in these results.
- **Cohen Antonir–Shapira (2022/2023):** They prove the order of magnitude for all m. Their main result is N_P(n,P_{2m+1}) = O(m^{-m}n^{m+1}), which matches the conjectured m-dependence up to a constant. It does not give the exact constant 4m, so it does not settle the conjecture.

Two points I could not confirm from these results: whether the exact asymptotic is proven for every m, and what "cycle proved" refers to. For odd cycles, the related Heath–Martin work reports the conjecture verified for small m, and one snippet says it was extended to all m, but the snippet is ambiguous. The sources are the arXiv papers 2208.02097 and 2101.05911 and the Heath–Martin paper arXiv 2307.00116. Checking the latest versions of those papers would be the next step for the status of the exact asymptotic.
