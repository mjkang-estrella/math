## Exact semantics
For integers n>=4 and m>=1, C_n is the chordless cycle and K_m the complete graph. Shannon product means the strong graph product: distinct pairs (i,a),(j,b) are adjacent when i=j or i~j AND a=b or a~b. Thus this product is the clique blowup of C_n by m vertices per fiber. Use its entire Whitney/clique complex, with each oriented simplex an orthonormal basis vector. The intended endpoint is A=n^2/m, with A=product over k of pdet(L_k)^{k(-1)^{k+1}}; empty pseudodeterminant is 1. The n>=4 condition matters: a graph-theoretic C_3 has filled clique complex and A(K_3)=3 rather than 9. ## Lattice determinant identity (proof, no numerical experiment)
For a finite free integral chain complex with orthonormal integral chain bases, suppose all integral homology groups are free. Let Z_j be the kernel lattice, B_j the boundary lattice, z_j and b_j their Euclidean covolumes in their real spans, and h_j the covolume of the integral homology lattice projected into harmonic representatives. Give rank-zero lattices covolume 1.

Since the image C_j(Z)/Z_j is free, choose an integral basis of Z_j and extend it to a basis of C_j(Z). Orthogonal projection of the complementary basis into Z_j^perp has covolume 1/z_j: the concatenated integral basis has determinant of absolute value 1, and the block volume factors. Applying boundary to those complementary vectors gives an integral basis of B_{j-1}. The volume multiplication factor of boundary restricted to Z_j^perp is the product of its nonzero singular values, s_j. Hence b_{j-1}=s_j/z_j, so s_j=b_{j-1}z_j. Also B_j is primitive in Z_j because homology is free; extend an integral basis of B_j by homology lifts. Orthogonal projection gives z_j=b_j h_j. Consequently s_j^2=b_{j-1}^2 b_j^2 h_j^2. At j=0, z_0=1 and b_0 h_0=1.

The orthogonal exact/coexact decomposition gives pdet(L_k)=s_k^2 s_{k+1}^2, with s_0=1. In the weighted alternating product every s_j^2 has exponent (-1)^{j-1}. Substituting the last identity cancels every b_j for j>=1 and leaves b_0^2 times product_{j>=1} h_j^{2(-1)^{j-1}}. Using b_0=1/h_0, A=product_{j>=0} h_j^{2(-1)^{j+1}}. These finite products require no convergence assumption. This argument proves the metric identity used here directly; no unproved Reidemeister comparison is hidden in it.

## Integral topology, all parameters
Within a fiber any two vertices have identical closed neighborhoods. If v,w are such a pair, the clique complex collapses to the induced complex deleting v: pair each simplex sigma containing v but not w with sigma union {w}, processing sigma in decreasing size. Every coface of sigma not already removed is sigma union {w}; adjacency makes that a simplex, so these are elementary free-face collapses. Repeat until one vertex remains per fiber. The resulting complex is the n-cycle, since n>=4 gives no triangles. Thus integral H_0=Z, H_1=Z, all other homology zero. A loop selecting one representative in each fiber is a primitive H_1 generator; the fiber projection and inclusion fix that cycle. This pays all high-dimensional simplices, including the (2m-1)-simplices across adjacent fibers.

## Harmonic generator and determinant evaluation
Orient the cycle forward. Define a 1-cochain alpha to equal 1/n on every edge crossing from fiber i to i+1, minus 1/n on the reversed edge, and 0 on every edge within a fiber. Every triangle lies in one or two adjacent fibers, so its oriented edge sum is zero. At every vertex there are m forward and m backward crossing edges, so the divergence is zero. Thus alpha is harmonic. Its period on the primitive loop is 1. There are n m^2 crossing edges, so ||alpha||^2=m^2/n. If h is the harmonic projection of the primitive integral cycle, its pairing with alpha is 1. Harmonic dimension is one, giving h=alpha/||alpha||^2 and ||h||^2=n/m^2. The primitive H_0 generator projects to the constant vector 1/(nm), with squared norm 1/(nm). All higher h_j=1. The metric identity therefore gives A=(nm)(n/m^2)=n^2/m. This includes m=1; then it reduces to the ordinary cycle value n^2.
