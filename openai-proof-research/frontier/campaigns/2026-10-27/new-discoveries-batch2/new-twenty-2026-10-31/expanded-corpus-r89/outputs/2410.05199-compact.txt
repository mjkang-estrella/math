Abstract Motivated by colouring minimal Cayley graphs, in 1978, Babai conjectured that no-lonely-colour graphs have bounded chromatic number. We disprove this in a strong sense by constructing graphs of arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number that have a proper edge-colouring in which each cycle contains no colour exactly once. 1 Introduction A graph G is a no-lonely-colour graph if there is an edge-colouring of G such that • every vertex is incident with at most two edges of any colour, and • each cycle contains no colour exactly once. In 1978, Babai [ Bab78 ] conjectured that no-lonely-colour graphs have bounded chromatic number. Very recently, Garcia-Marco and Knauer [ GK24 ] made the weaker conjecture that graphs admitting a proper edge-colouring in which each cycle contains no colour exactly once have bounded chromatic number. We disprove both of these conjectures in a strong sense; our graphs can also have arbitrarily large girth. Theorem 1 . For every pair of positive integers g , k , there is a graph G g , k with girth at least g and chromatic number at least k that has a proper edge-colouring in which each cycle contains no colour exactly once. We remark that in 1995, Babai [ Bab95 ] also conjectured the opposite: the family of no-lonely-colour graphs has unbounded chromatic number. Thus, theorem 1 also proves this conjecture. theorem 1 also strengthens a recent theorem of Garcia-Marco and Knauer [ GK24 ] that there are graphs with large chromatic number that have a proper edge-colouring in which no cycle contains each colour at most once. Babai’s [ Bab78 ] conjecture was motivated by colouring minimal Cayley graphs. Given a group Γ and S ⊆ Γ , the (undirected) Cayley graph is defined to be a graph on the vertex set Γ and a ​ b is an edge whenever a ​ b − 1 or b ​ a − 1 is in S . We say S is a minimal generating set for Γ if S generates Γ and no proper subset of it does, in this case we call Cay ​ ( Γ , S ) a minimal Cayley graph. Aspects of minimal Cayley graphs and properties distinguishing them from general Cayley graphs has been extensively studied (see for instance [ Bab78 , Bab95 , FM16 , GK24 , God81 , LZ01 , MŠ20 , SS09 , Spe83 ] ). One can observe that every minimal Cayley graph is a no-lonely-colour graph, and thus Babai’s conjecture would have implied the following conjecture which remains open as the graphs we construct are not Cayley graphs. Conjecture 2 (Babai [ Bab78 ] ) . There is a constant c such that every minimal Cayley graphs has chromatic number at most c . Although there are various constructions of Cayley graphs with large chromatic number [ BMK18 , Dav23 , DMP23 , Dav24 , Fur77 , KM78 , LPS88 , Nic24 , Sár78 , Tom15 ] (and bounded clique number), it remains open whether or not there is even a minimal Cayley graph with chromatic number at least 5. Garcia-Marco and Knauer [ GK24 ] showed that the minimal Cayley graph Cay ​ ( ℤ 3 ⋊ ℤ 7 , { ( 0 , 1 ) , ( 1 , 0 ) } ) has chromatic number 4. They also proved that any minimal Cayley graph of a (finitely generated) generalised dihedral or nilpotent group has chromatic number at most 3 . We establish theorem 1 by an intricate explicit construction of graphs of arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number (that are furthermore no-lonely-colour graphs). Erdős [ Erd59 ] was the first who proved that there exist such graphs using the probabilistic method and Lovász [ Lov68 ] gave the first explicit construction. At this point many such constructions are known [ AKR+16 , BD23 , DKK+21 , Dav21 , KN99 , Kří89 , LPS88 , NR79 ] , many of which have various other desirable properties. Our construction is a delicate modification of Tutte’s [ Des47 , Des54 ] construction of triangle-free graphs with large chromatic number. Other modifications of Tutte’s construction have been used before, for example, by Nešetřil and Rödl [ NR79 ] to give a simple explicit construction of graphs with arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number, and more recently by the first author [ Dav21 ] who constructed intersection graphs of axis-aligned boxes and of lines in ℝ 3 with arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number. The key difference is a choice and construction of auxiliary hypergraphs of large girth and chromatic number and with further specific properties (we call these tranquil hypergraphs). To obtain a class of tranquil hypergraphs, we observe that certain hypergraphs with large girth and chromatic number arising from Gallai’s theorem (or equivalently, the multi-dimensional van der Waerden’s theorem) have desirable combinatorial properties that may make them a useful gadget of independent interest (see theorem 6 ). Another variant of these hypergraphs was recently used by Davies, Keller, Kleist, Smorodinsky, and Walczak [ DKK+21 ] to resolve Ringel’s [ Rin59 ] circle problem. The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In section 2 , we introduce our notation. In section 3 , we examine certain auxiliary hypergraphs that are needed for our construction. In section 4 , we prove theorem 1 . Finally, in section 5 , we discuss some related conjectures. 2 Notation An edge e of a multigraph G is a bridge if G − e has more connected components than G . A closed walk of length ℓ in a hypergraph H is a sequence of alternating vertices and hyperedges v 0 ​ F 0 ​ v 1 ​ F 1 ​ … ​ F ℓ − 1 ​ v 0 such that v i , v i + 1 ∈ F i for all 0 ≤ i < ℓ , and F i ≠ F i + 1 for each 0 ≤ i < ℓ where the sum i + 1 is taken modulo ℓ here and henceforth wherever appropriate, and v 1 , … , v ℓ − 1 are all pairwise distinct. Note that F 0 , … , F ℓ − 1 need not all be distinct. Similarly, we say that a collection of distinct hyperedges F 1 , … , F ℓ forms a cycle of length ℓ if there exist distinct elements x 1 , … , x ℓ such that for all i ∈ { 1 , … , ℓ − 1 } we have x i ∈ F i ∩ F i + 1 . Note that except the vertices x i ’s, the edges F i do not have to be disjoint. This is more commonly known in the literature as Berge cycles. The girth of a hypergraph is equal to the length of its shortest Berge cycle. The chromatic number of a hypergraph is the least amount of colours needed to colour the vertices of the hypergraph such that no edge is monochromatic. 3 Tranquil hypergraphs We call an r -uniform hypergraph H tranquil if there is a family of labellings λ = { λ F } F ∈ E ⁡ ( H ) such that for each hyperedge F ∈ E ⁡ ( H ) , λ F : F → { 1 , … , r } is such that for every closed walk W = v 0 ​ F 0 ​ v 1 ​ F 1 ​ … ​ F ℓ − 1 ​ v 0 of H , the multigraph with vertices { 1 , … , r } and edges { λ F i ​ ( v i ) ​ λ F i ​ ( v i + 1 ) : 0 ≤ i < ℓ } is bridgeless. We now show that G g , k has a proper edge-colouring in which each cycle contains no colour exactly once. In particular, this shows that G g , k is a no-lonely-colour graph. Lemma 7 . For every pair of positive integers g , k , the graph G g , k has a proper edge-colouring in which each cycle contains no colour exactly once. Proof. Clearly G g , 1 has such an edge-colouring since G g , 1 is edgeless. So, we argue inductively assuming that G g , k − 1 has a proper edge-colouring κ k − 1 : V ⁡ ( G ) → ℕ such that each cycle contains no colour exactly once. Let H = H ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ , k , r be the hypergraph from theorem 6 used in the last step of the construction of G g , k . We colour the edges inside of all copies of G g , k − 1 according to κ k − 1 . Then, for each F ∈ E ⁡ ( H ) we colour the matching between μ ⁡ ( F ) and T with a new colour, thus adding | E ⁡ ( H ) | colours, to obtain the edge-colouring κ k of G g , k . The edge-colouring κ k is proper as κ k − 1 is a proper edge-colouring and each new colour induces a matching between T and some μ ⁡ ( F ) . It remains to show that each cycle C of G g , k contains no colour exactly once. By induction, if C completely lies in some copy of G g , k − 1 in G g , k , then C contains no colour exactly once. So, we may assume otherwise. Let C be such a cycle, and W H ​ ( C ) be the walk it induces in H . Let λ = { λ F : F → { 1 , … , r } : F ∈ E ( H ) } be the collection of labellings witnessing that H is tranquil, i.e. that fulfills (TR) that is used in the construction of G g , k . As before let G 1 , … , G ℓ be the copies of G g , k − 1 that the cycle C passes through in that order and let P i be the maximal subpath of C in G i . Now suppose towards a contradiction that C contains an edge e such that κ k ​ ( e ) ≠ κ k ​ ( e ′ ) for all edges e ′ in C with e ≠ e ′ . As C contains an even number of edges from every added matching, the edge e has to lie in some C i . Consider the corresponding hyperedge F i and vertices x , y ∈ V ⁡ ( H ) such that λ F i ​ ( x ) = 𝗌𝗍𝖺𝗋𝗍 ⁡ ( C i ) and λ F i ​ ( y ) = 𝖾𝗇𝖽 ⁡ ( C i ) . As P | W H ​ ( C ) λ is bridgeless, the edge x ​ y is not a bridge and there is an x - y -path Q in P | W H ​ ( C ) λ − x ​ y , this corresponds to a x - y -walk in H , say using the edges F i 1 , … , F i h . We consider the Eulerian multigraph G ~ on the vertex set of G g , k − 1 obtained by adding disjoint copies of the paths P i 1 , … , P i h , and P i , that is, an edge lying in multiple of these paths is added multiple times. The underlying simple graph of G ~ is a subgraph of G g , k − 1 . If e has a parallel multi-edge in G ~ , then this edge corresponds to an edge in C having the same colour as e , a contradiction. So e lies on a cycle of length at least three in G ~ . This cycle corresponds to a cycle in G g , k − 1 and as such, by induction hypothesis, contains another edge e ′ with κ k ​ ( e ′ ) = κ k − 1 ​ ( e ′ ) = κ k − 1 ​ ( e ) = κ k ​ ( e ) , a contradiction. Thus no cycle in G g , k contains a colour of κ k exactly once. ∎ Next, we routinely show that G g , k has large girth and chromatic number. Lemma 8 . For every pair of positive integers g , k , the graph G g , k has girth at least g . Proof. Clearly, the lemma holds for k = 1 since G g , 1 is the single vertex graph. So, we proceed inductively on k assuming that G g , k − 1 has girth at least g . Let H = H ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ , k , r be the hypergraph from theorem 6 used in the last step of the construction of G g , k . Note in particular that H has girth at least ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ . Now let C be a shortest cycle in G g , k . If C completely lies in a copy of G g , k − 1 , then it has length at least g by induction, so we may assume otherwise. Then C induces the walk W H ​ ( C ) in H , which has length at least ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ , because H has girth at least ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ . As the copies of G g , k − 1 are connected to T by matchings, each edge in W H ​ ( C ) corresponds to a subpath of C of length at least three (two matching edges and at least one edge in a copy of G g , k ). Thus C is of length at least 3 ⋅ ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ ≥ g . ∎ Lemma 9 . For every pair of positive integers g , k , we have χ ⁡ ( G g , k ) ≥ k . Proof. Clearly, the lemma holds for k = 1 . So, we proceed inductively on k assuming that χ ⁡ ( G g , k − 1 ) ≥ k − 1 . Suppose towards a contradiction that there was a proper colouring of G g , k with k − 1 colours. Let again H = H ⌈ g / 3 ⌉ , k , r be the hypergraph from theorem 6 used in the last step of the construction of G g , k . As H has chromatic number at least k , there is a subset I ⊆ T such that F = ν ⁡ ( I ) is an edge in H , and F is monochromatic. As every vertex in μ ⁡ ( F ) has a neighbour in F this colour cannot be used in μ ⁡ ( F ) and thus μ ⁡ ( F ) is coloured with k − 2 colours, a contradiction. ∎ Together lemma 7 , lemma 8 , and lemma 9 imply our main result theorem 1 . 5 Concluding Remarks We conclude with two open problems. First, we believe the following natural extension of theorem 1 should be true as well. Conjecture 10 . For every pair of positive integers m , k , there exists a graph with chromatic number at least k that has a proper edge-colouring such that each cycle contains no colour between one and m times. Secondly, we would like to restate Babai’s original conjecture on minimal Cayley graphs and, in particular, ask whether our construction for theorem 1 can be modified to obtain a minimal Cayley graph disproving it.
