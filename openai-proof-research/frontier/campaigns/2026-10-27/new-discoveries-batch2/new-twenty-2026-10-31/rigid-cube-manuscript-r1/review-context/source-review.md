## Exact statement and source/version fidelity

The historical raw record calls Q_n the signed Hamming cube, defines maximal diameter-r by exact diameter r and exclusion of every outside point, defines rigidity by a diameter partner for every point, and asserts b'(n;r)=2^r whenever n≥r. It is a universal claim, not a request to determine b'(8;5) exactly.

Briggs–Feng–Wells, [arXiv:2408.01288v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01288v1) and [v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.01288v2), §2 explicitly use those diameter/maximality/rigidity definitions. Section 3 restates rigidity at scale r. Section 6 defines b' using maximal rigid diameter-r sets and gives Conjecture 1 with n≥r. These are not Euclidean-distance rigidity, affine rigidity, all-pairs equidistance, or a requirement that the entire VR complex have a particular homotopy type. The source identity d(x,y)=(n−〈x,y〉)/2 agrees with coordinate mismatch counting, not twice that distance.

Both inspected versions retain this target and its hypotheses. The live [landing page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01288), fetched 19:10 UTC, lists v1 submitted 2 August 2024 and v2 revised 5 August 2024; no journal reference or erratum is identified there. No material published comparator was located in these bounded checks, so I impose no invented unavailable-version hold. This does not prove absence of a journal version or correction. The source's displayed ordinary b(n;r) formula starts its sums at 1; the standard formula includes the zero term. That unrelated display issue does not affect the definitions of b', the 2^r conjecture, or this refutation.

The Lean universal includes natural n,r and r≤n; even if the prose convention requires positive parameters, the contradiction uses n=8,r=5, so no zero-dimensional endpoint convention contributes to it.

## Prior art and explicit subsumption checks

**Source constructions.** I read Theorem 4 and its full proof: a Hadamard simplex in Q_n has n+1 points, diameter (n+1)/2, is rigid, and is maximal by the inner-product sum/sum-of-squares argument. Proposition 5 and its full proof multiply rigid facets, add diameters and multiply sizes; outside maximality uses one strict offending distance and a partner at least the diameter in the other factor. Corollary 6 embeds rigid facets by fixed coordinates. Theorem 12 explicitly combines these into many different facet sizes.

A Hadamard factor of diameter s has 2s points, and 2s≤2^s for every positive integer s; a free s-cube has 2^s, a fixed-coordinate factor has one. Thus any product of these particular factors with total diameter r has cardinality ≤2^r. Equality/strictness is immaterial: multiplication preserves the bound. These mechanisms do not yield the proposed violation. Proposition 5 cannot be invoked with a nonrigid large anticode: its proof requires rigidity, exactly where a factor coordinate lies inside its set. Extending an arbitrary anticode greedily preserves size but need not make every point acquire a diameter partner.

**Classical diametric anticodes.** Diameter-5 anticodes in Q8 can be much larger: the canonical Q1×B_2(Q7) has 2(1+7+21)=58 points. Its centre in the Q7 factor has eccentricity only 1+2=3, so it is not rigid. Keeping only the weight-2 shell gives 42 points and diameter partners, but the missing centre can be added without exceeding 5, so it is not maximal. These are explicit reasons that simply citing a large anticode or deleting its interior does not settle b'. They do not rule out every possible modification of classical families.

**Bezrukov 1987, now body checked.** The Helly paper bibliography led to *Specification of all maximal subsets of the unit cube with respect to given diameter*. The lawful [Math-Net record](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/ppi768) identifies the published title *Description of Maximal Subsets of a Given Diameter in a Hamming space*, Probl. Peredachi Inf. 23:1 (1987), 106–109. I downloaded the linked four-page Russian primary PDF, extracted all pages and visually checked p.106. Its §1 explicitly defines d-maximal by **largest cardinality**, not inclusion-maximality. Theorem 1 gives balls/double balls; Theorem 2 classifies those maximum-cardinality objects when d<n−1 and gives antipodal transversals at d=n−1. The §2 proof uses complement/neighbourhood and isoperimetric extremality; §3 treats minimum diameter at prescribed size and intersection families. At (8,5), d<n−1; the applicable extremizers are exactly the nonrigid double-ball kind above. This body is not a classification of all source-rigid inclusion-maximal sets, and supplies no 34-point rigid construction. This resolves the specific title-level ambiguity rather than treating an unread title as clearance.

**Recent enumeration and Helly results.** Horlemann–Ravi, [arXiv:2610.31618v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31618v1), 22 October 2026, Definitions 2.1/2.4 distinguish diameter-bounded, maximum and inclusion-maximal anticodes. Lemma 3.1 and its proof show an inclusion-maximal anticode S equals the intersection of its radius-d balls and is determined by ≤2^(d+1) centres from S. Lemma 3.2 counts such descriptions. Theorem 3.11 estimates the total anticode count asymptotically, not rigid facet sizes. I read the relevant preliminaries, maximal-count proof and main count statements; not every asymptotic estimate was audited. Alon–Jin–Sudakov, [arXiv:2405.10275v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10275v2), Theorems 1.1/1.2, provide the actual Helly/intersection statement. At d=5 the bound is 64 determining centres; it neither forces every member to be such a centre nor constructs a rigid anticode larger than 32. No assertion about the exact b' follows.

**Linear/projective anticodes.** The counting paper's Lemma 2.6 proves dim C≤d for a linear d-anticode, by row-echelon pivots. Thus any binary linear or affine translate with diameter ≤5 has at most 32 points. This is a complete exclusion of a *direct* linear-code counterexample, not of arbitrary nonlinear modifications or extensions. In [arXiv:2406.07112v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07112v1), I read the projective-linear definitions, Theorem 2.1 antiGriesmer bound and proof, and Theorem 3.1/Corollary 3.1 complementary generator-column construction. They constrain maximum weights/construct linear codes; the same dimension bound prevents their direct use as a >32-point diameter-5 binary set. They do not assert ambient inclusion-maximality of nonlinear rigid sets. I do not claim to have checked every construction table or old Farrell/MacWilliams–Sloane body.

**VR predecessors/successors.** Targeted inspection of Feng 2305.07084 and Shukla 2202.02756 finds scale-3 facet/link/homotopy and homology constructions, not a scale-5 34-point rigid set. Their scale-3 classification statements do not themselves give a factor exceeding 2^3.
