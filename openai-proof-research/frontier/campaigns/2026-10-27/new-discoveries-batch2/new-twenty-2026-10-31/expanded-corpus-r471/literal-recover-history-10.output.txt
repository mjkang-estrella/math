320173 Let $A_i=\{p_i,q_i\}$, $i=1,2,3,4$, be four sets of two points each in $\mathbf{R}^3$. For a set of points, call it **convexly independent** when every point is a vertex of its convex hull. **Small-diameter obstruction conjecture.** There exists $\varepsilon>0$ such that, whenever

$$
\lVert p_i-q_i\rVert_2<\varepsilon
$$

for every $i$, the set of midpoints between points in different $A_i$,

$$
\bigcup_{\substack{i,j=1,2,3,4,\\ i\neq j}}\frac12(A_i+A_j),
$$

is not convexly independent. This is presented as a sufficient condition for the preceding non-realizability conjecture, and concerns the local obstruction created by four pairs whose members are sufficiently close together.
320252 For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, define the Engel words recursively by

$$
e_1(x,y)=[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1},\qquad e_n(x,y)=[e_{n-1}(x,y),y]\quad(n>1).
$$

For a group $G$, let $e_n:G\times G\to G$ be the corresponding Engel word map.

**Engel-word surjectivity conjecture.** For every $n\in\mathbb{N}$, the map $e_n$ is surjective for every finite simple non-abelian group $G$.

The conjecture extends the question of whether nontrivial word maps are surjective on finite simple groups. The source records the commutator case as the Ore conjecture, which is solved, but does not establish the assertion for all Engel words and all finite simple groups.
320457 A topological group $G$ is said to have **property (OA)** if, whenever $G=A*_CB$ is a non-trivial decomposition as a free product with amalgamation, the subgroups $A$, $B$, and $C$ are open in $G$. Here, non-trivial means that $A$ and $B$ are proper subgroups of $G$.

**Open amalgams conjecture.** Every completely metrisable topological group has property (OA).

Locally compact groups are known to have property (OA), and the paper proves the same for completely metrisable groups satisfying an additional somewhere-density condition. The conjecture proposes that this holds for all completely metrisable topological groups.
320492 Let $c>0$. Let ${\mathcal F}_c$ be a finite family of graphs, let $G$ be an almost simple group, and let $\Gamma$ be a connected tetravalent $G$-arc-transitive graph with vertex $v$. **Vertex-stabiliser growth conjecture.** If $\Gamma$ is not contained in ${\mathcal F}_c$, then

$$
|G_v|<|\mathrm V\Gamma|^c.
$$

The graphs $\Delta_m$ provide examples in which the vertex-stabiliser grows faster than any logarithmic function of the number of vertices, but slower than every positive power. The conjecture asserts that, apart from finitely many exceptional graphs for each $c$, this subpolynomial bound holds for all connected tetravalent arc-transitive graphs with almost simple automorphism group.
