## 358557: full Hermitian congruence and its classical subsumption

Exact source: Aguglia–Csajbók–Giuzzi, arXiv:2305.17103, Conjecture 1 after (30). The affine Hermitian equation is y^q+y=x^(q+1). In the conjecture q=p^(2h), h≥2; the proposed curves are y=a x^p+m x+d. The argument below works for every q=p^e, e≥1, and all a,m,d in K=F_(q²), so in particular any nonzero-a restriction is satisfied. Each x determines exactly one affine point; points at infinity are not counted.

Put k=F_q. Choose an F_p basis of K (dimension 2e) and an F_p basis of k (dimension e). The map N:K→k, x↦x x^q, is homogeneous quadratic in the 2e prime-field coordinates: x↦x^q is F_p linear and multiplication is bilinear. The map L(x)=Tr_(K/k)(a x^p+m x) is F_p linear, since pth powering, multiplication by a or m, and trace are F_p linear. c=Tr_(K/k)(d) is constant. Intersection points correspond bijectively to solutions of N(x)-L(x)-c=0 in k, hence to e coordinate equations Q_i(X)-l_i(X)-c_i=0 over F_p in 2e variables. Each Q_i is homogeneous quadratic.

Each nonzero coordinate of the norm is nonzero as a polynomial: the norm is onto k (for nonzero elements use the cyclic group K× and exponent q+1, with image of size q−1), so every nonzero k-coordinate functional is nonzero on a norm value. Thus there are e genuine quadratic polynomials, whose degree sum is 2e, exactly the number of variables. The simultaneous homogeneous zero locus is {0}, since x x^q=0 implies x=0.

Boundary Chevalley–Warning (Clark–Genao–Saia, Lemma 2.3, attributed to Heath-Brown 2011; Theorem 3.2(b) strengthens its modulus) says that deleting terms of degree below each polynomial's degree preserves the common-zero count modulo p when the degree sum is at most the number of variables. It gives N_intersection ≡ #Z(Q_1,…,Q_e)=1 (mod p), proving the full literal congruence.

Independent proof of the needed classical bridge: for f_i=Q_i-l_i-c_i set χ_f=∏(1-f_i^(p−1)) over F_p. Its sum over F_p^(2e) equals the integer zero count mapped into F_p. χ_f−χ_Q has degree strictly less than 2e(p−1): the sole possible top-degree product uses all e homogeneous quadratic terms, and those cancel. Every monomial of smaller degree has some variable exponent below p−1. Its sum vanishes, including exponent zero, whose sum is p=0; positive exponents below p−1 have zero sum over the cyclic multiplicative group. Thus the two counts agree modulo p. This also proves the p=2 case, without completing squares or dividing by 2.

Failed first shortcut: classical Chevalley–Warning with strict inequality does not apply directly because ∑deg=number of variables. Nor may the x^p term be treated as degree p after deciding to work in prime-field coordinates: as a polynomial function it is linear in those coordinates. Homogenization supplies another proof: the e quadratic homogenizations have 2e+1 variables, so their total zero count is divisible by p; splitting homogenizing coordinate zero/nonzero gives 0≡1+(p−1)N_intersection, hence N_intersection≡1.

Disposition: full ordinary endpoint, but a complete direct application of a public classical boundary theorem, with the semantic norm/trace coordinate bridge explicit above. This is excluded as a routine classical bridge, not because it is short. Zero credit and no execution dispatch. The source's sqrt(q)-intersection spectrum, code weights and figures do not need extension to prove this weaker modulo-p endpoint. v1/v2 were fetched; their complete bodies were not read. Latest v3 definitions and conjecture were read, plus the boundary theorem and its actual proof. The journal DOI landing was fetched but did not expose the journal proof body; no claim of journal/correction clearance.

## 359130: reversed Dickson periodicity and collision mechanisms

Exact source: Fang–Fernando–Wu, arXiv:2307.06325v1, Section 4, Conjecture 4.31; the same conjecture is present in the 2025 Involve journal article, p.325. E_0(1,x)=1, E_1(1,x)=1 and E_n=E_(n−1)−x E_(n−2). Equivalently E_n(1,x)=∑_(i=0)^floor(n/2) binom(n−i,i)(−x)^i. For x=y(1−y), y≠1/2 in F_(p²), the functional expression is (y^(n+1)−(1−y)^(n+1))/(2y−1). At x=1/4 it is (n+1)/2^n. Every x∈F_p has such a root y in F_(p²), since it solves a quadratic over F_p. At x=0 the value is always 1.

Route 1, full sufficiency: let T=p(p²−1). For x≠0,1/4 the two nonzero roots have multiplicative order dividing p²−1, so E_(n+T)(x)=E_n(x). At 1/4, n+T+1≡n+1 mod p and 2^T=1. At 0 both are 1. Thus T is a function period. E_2=1−x and E_3=1−2x are bijective for p>7, proving the conjectured two residue classes are sufficient. This is already source material, not discovery.

Route 2, checking whether shifts of those classes yield a counterexample: put n=r+j(p²−1), r∈{2,3}. Away from 1/4 the function is E_r. At 1/4 it is (r+1−j)/2^r. Since E_r is affine bijective, all other inputs already exhaust F_p except E_r(1/4); changing just the exceptional value remains bijective only if j≡0 mod p. Hence no counterexample comes from lifting the two obvious residues with an incorrect p-coordinate. This is a complete necessity argument only inside those two residues modulo p²−1.

Route 3, general collision test: E_n(0)=1, so any nonzero x with E_n(x)=1 kills permutation. For x≠0,1/4 this is the exact equation y^(n+1)−(1−y)^(n+1)=2y−1 on the split locus y∈F_p or nonsplit locus y^p=1−y. The involution y↔1−y labels the same x, so a paired solution is not a second distinct input. Testing only the split torus cannot rule out nonsplit collisions. Frobenius relates y and 1−y on the nonsplit locus, but does not simplify arbitrary n to 2 or 3. No universal solvability argument or in-scope finite witness was found. No table or mathematical search was executed.

Precise gap: for every p>7 and every index outside the two classes modulo T, prove a collision E_n(x)=E_n(z) with x≠z, or supply one outside-class permutation with its exact complete field semantics. Necessity of the two period classes remains unpaid. A proof of a period, of derivative conditions over Z_(p^t), or of first-kind Dickson behavior would not fill it. Earlier completed r107 work treated different first-kind and p=5 records; its bounds and exclusions are retained, not rerun. The journal still states the target conjecture. No new endpoint, no Pi plan.

## 359585: complete rooted bicircular construction, source-construction bridge

Exact source: Chan–Pak, arXiv:2308.10214v2, Conjecture 6.1. Concise means that every positive integer N occurs as b(M) for an explicitly presented bicircular matroid of size at most C(1+log N)^c for uniform constants C,c. This is an existence size bound, not a polynomial-time algorithm in N or log N. In the standard bicircular class, representing graphs may have loops and multiple edges. Giménez–de Mier–Noy, Introduction and proof of Theorem 2.2, explicitly treats trees with arbitrary attached loops. A graph loop is a one-edge cycle, not a matroid loop here.

For a finite connected rooted multigraph (G,r) with a cycle define U(G) as the number of spanning edge subsets whose every component is unicyclic. Define T(G,r) as the number of spanning edge subsets with the component containing r a tree and every other component unicyclic. Isolated vertices count as trees. U(G)=b(B(G)): independent edge subsets have at most one cycle per component; all-unicyclic subsets have |V| edges and are maximal. Conversely if an independent subset has a tree component, connectedness of G either supplies an unused edge to another component (merging preserves at most one cycle), or it is the whole graph and an unused cycle edge can be added. Thus it was not maximal. This proves the basis bridge rather than assuming an abstract count is the desired one.

Operation X: add one new loop at the root. In U states omitting it there are U old states; states using it are in bijection with old T states (delete the new loop). Adding it to an old root component already unicyclic would make two cycles and is prohibited. In T states the new loop cannot be used. Therefore (U,T)↦(U+T,T).

Operation Y: add a fresh vertex s and one edge rs, then make s the root. In U states the edge must be used since s cannot be an isolated tree; deletion of s and rs gives old U states. In T states, using rs corresponds to old T states, while omitting rs isolates the new root and leaves old U states. Thus (U,T)↦(U,U+T). These are disjoint exhaustive bijections. Both operations increase |V|+|E| by at most two. Start with one vertex and one loop, giving (U,T)=(1,1).

Every positive coprime ordered pair (N,a) can be reached: repeatedly subtract the smaller coordinate from the larger until (1,1), then reverse the steps with X or Y. The number of single subtractions is the sum of the continued-fraction quotients of a/N, minus one; it is enough to use the upper bound by that sum. (There is no algorithm being run here.) By a public small-sum continued-fraction theorem, for every N≥3 one may choose a coprime a<N whose quotient sum is O(log N log log N), hence polynomial in 1+log N. The primary Aistleitner–Borda–Hauke paper arXiv:2210.14095, Corollary 2, states the stronger explicit bound 12/pi² log N log log N+O(log N). Consequently our graph has polynomial logarithmic size and U=N. N=1 is the seed; N=2 follows by one X step, giving two loops. Edge-indexed encoding uses O(s log(s+1)) bits for s=|V|+|E|, still polynomial logarithmic. This proves the literal bicircular conciseness endpoint for the standard unrestricted class.

Failed shortcuts: a single vertex with N loops has b=N but size N, so is not concise. Direct sums multiply values but do not realize primes efficiently by themselves. Appending a looped leaf while keeping the old root does not merely double U: the additional root-tree states contribute. Counting only spanning trees is wrong because a bicircular basis may have several unicyclic components. The T state above fixes that defect. Simply subdividing a loop into a triangle does not preserve counts: it contributes three old U choices plus one T choice, so this proof does not assert a simple-graph variant.

Campaign eligibility disposition: zero-credit complete source-construction bridge. Chan–Pak Proposition 5.20 and its proof already supply the same Euclidean/Calkin–Wilf realization mechanism and logarithmic-size arithmetic for tree matching counts. The two exhaustive rooted-state bijections above transfer exactly that mechanism to the standard looped bicircular representation; they add no new arithmetic or nonroutine existence ingredient. This exclusion is based on the complete reduction, not on size, ease or the word “matroid.” Preserve the argument for root's eligibility review; it is not a claimed new-to-literature result. If a separate simple-graph or connectedness convention were intended, this packet must not silently change it. Connectedness holds for our construction; simplicity does not.

Read limitations: both source versions were fetched but only selected v2 statements and Proposition 5.20 proof were read. The author PDF also contains that proof and Conjecture 6.1. Bicircular definition and relevant counting proof passages in the 2005 paper were read; approximate counting paper definition/algorithm context was inspected, not its complete proof. The small-sum theorem statement was read; its long analytic proof was not. No journal/citing-proof-body novelty clearance is claimed. Thus, independently of the routine-bridge exclusion, this is not a source-qualified novel candidate eligible for dispatch.
