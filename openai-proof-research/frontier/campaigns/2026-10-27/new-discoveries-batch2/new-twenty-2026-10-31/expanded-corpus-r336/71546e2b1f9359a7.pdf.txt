     We investigate fixed points and cycle types of permutation polynomials and
     complete permutation polynomials arising from reversed Dickson polynomials of
     the first kind and second kind over F p . We also study the permutation behaviour
     of reversed Dickson polynomials of the first kind and second kind over Zm .


                                     1. Introduction

Let p be a prime, F p be the finite field with p elements, and F×p = F p \ {0}. A
polynomial f ∈ F p [x] is called a permutation polynomial of F p if the associated
mapping x 7 → f (x) from F p to F p is a permutation of F p . Every function from F p
to F p can be represented by a unique polynomial in F p [x]. That is, if φ : F p → F p is
an arbitrary function, then there exists a unique polynomial g ∈ F p [x] of degree at
most q−1 such that g(c) = φ(c) for all c ∈ Fq . The polynomial g can be found by
the Lagrange interpolation for the function φ. If φ is already given as a polynomial
function — say φ : c 7→ f (c), where f ∈ F p [x] — then g can be obtained from f
by reduction modulo x p − x. Finding new classes of permutation polynomials is
an important topic in the area of finite fields as they play a central role in both
arithmetic and combinatorial aspects of finite fields. Permutation polynomials have
many applications in the areas of coding theory, cryptography, combinatorics, finite
geometry, and computer science, among other fields. Here we give two examples
of permutation polynomials of F p .
Example 1.1. Every linear polynomial ax + b, where a ∈ F×p and b ∈ F p , is a
permutation polynomial of F p .
Example 1.2. The monomial x n is a permutation polynomial of F p if and only if
gcd(n, p − 1) = 1.

   Charles Hermite studied the permutation behaviour of polynomials over finite
prime fields. It was Leonard Eugene Dickson who studied polynomials for their
permutation behaviour over finite arbitrary fields. The n-th Dickson polynomial is
given by
                                 ⌊n/2⌋
                                  X n  n −i 
                     Dn (x, a) =                    (−a)i x n−2i ,
                                       n −i    i
                                    i=0

where a ∈ F p is a parameter. The recurrence relation of Dickson polynomials
Dn (x, a) is given by

D0 (x, a) = 2,    D1 (x, a) = x,    Dn (x, a) = x Dn−1 (x, a)−a Dn−2 (x, a) for n ≥ 2.

   Dickson studied these polynomials for their permutation property over finite
fields. The permutation property of Dickson polynomials is completely known.
When a = 0, we have Dn (x, 0) = x n , which is a permutation polynomial of F p if
and only if gcd(n, p − 1) = 1. Nöbauer [1968] proved that Dn (x, a), where a ̸= 0,
is a permutation polynomial of F p if and only if gcd(n, p 2 − 1) = 1.
   Issai Schur [1923] named the polynomials Dn (x, a) in Dickson’s honour. He
also introduced a variant of Dickson polynomials in [Schur 1973] which are called
Dickson polynomials of the second kind. For this reason, the polynomials given by
Dn (x, a) are now called Dickson polynomials of the first kind.
   The n-th Dickson polynomial of the second kind is given by
                                        ⌊n/2⌋
                                         X     n −i 
                         E n (x, a) =                 (−a)i x n−2i ,

where a ∈ F p is a parameter. The recurrence relation of Dickson polynomials of
the second kind E n (x, a) is given by

E 0 (x, a) = 1,   E 1 (x, a) = x,   E n (x, a) = x E n−1 (x, a)−a E n−2 (x, a) for n ≥ 2.

   The permutation property of Dickson polynomials of the second kind is not yet
completely known. We refer the reader to the monograph [Lidl et al. 1993] for more
details on Dickson polynomials, and to [Cipu and Cohen 2008] for the permutation
behaviour of Dickson polynomials of the second kind.
   Reversed Dickson polynomials of the first kind were introduced by Xiang-dong
Hou, Gary L. Mullen, Joseph L. Yucas and James Sellers [Hou et al. 2009] by

first kind. The n-th reversed Dickson polynomial of the first kind is given by the

D0 (a, x) = 2,     D1 (a, x) = a,    Dn (a, x) = a Dn−1 (a, x)−x Dn−2 (a, x) for n ≥ 2.

   In [Hou et al. 2009], the authors studied these polynomials for their permutation

polynomials, like its counterpart Dn (x, a), is also a rich source of permutation
polynomials. They showed that reversed Dickson polynomials are closely related
to almost perfect nonlinear (APN) functions which have many applications in
cryptography.
   Since 2009, many have studied reversed Dickson polynomials of the first kind
for their permutation property over finite fields. Xiang-dong Hou and Tue Ly [2010]
found necessary conditions for reversed Dickson polynomials of the first kind to be
permutational.

In [Fernando 2017a; 2017b; 2018], the second author of this paper studied the

   The set of permutation polynomials of F p of degree less than or equal to p−1
form a group under composition and reduction modulo x p − x, which is isomorphic
to S p . Ayça Çeşmelioğlu, Wilfried Meidl, and Alev Topuzoğlu [Çeşmelioğlu et al.
2008] studied the cycle structure of permutation polynomials over finite fields, Shair
Ahmad [1969] explained the cycle structure of Dickson permutation polynomials
Dn (x, a) for a = 0, and Rudolf Lidl and Gary L. Mullen [1991] determined the cycle
structure of Dickson permutation polynomials Dn (x, a) for a ∈ {−1, 1}. Moreover,
Adama Diene and Mohamed D. Salim [2013] determined the number of fixed points
of Dickson polynomials of the second kind E n (x, a). Motivated by their results,
we determine the number of fixed points and the cycle type of reversed Dickson
permutation polynomials of the first kind and second kind.
   Most studies on permutation polynomials have been over finite fields. However,
there have been a few studies on permutation polynomials over finite rings Zm , where
m > 1 is an integer, due to their applications in coding theory and cryptography.
We refer the reader to [Görcsös et al. 2018; Rivest 2001; Sun and Takeshita 2005;
Takeshita 2006] for further details on permutation polynomials over Zm . Longjiang
Qu and Cunsheng Ding [2014] investigated the permutation property of the Dickson
polynomials of the second kind E n (x, a) over Zm . Motivated by these results, we
investigate the permutation behaviour of reversed Dickson polynomials of the first
kind and second kind over finite rings.
   A permutation polynomial of F p is called a complete permutation polynomial
(complete mapping) of F p if f (x) + x is also a permutation polynomial of F p .
Complete permutation polynomials have many applications in orthogonal Latin
squares, combinatorics, and cryptography. Harald Niederreiter and Karl H. Robinson
[1982] conducted a detailed study of complete permutation polynomials over finite

fields. Gary L. Mullen and Harald Niederreiter [1987] determined the complete per-
mutation polynomials arising from Dickson polynomials. Motivated by their work,
we study complete permutation polynomials from reversed Dickson polynomials
of the first kind and second kind. We refer the reader to [Bassalygo and Zinoviev
2015; Muratović-Ribić and Pasalic 2014; Niederreiter and Robinson 1982] for more
about complete permutation polynomials and their applications in cryptography.
    The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we give some preliminary details.
In Section 3, we first present several known results on reversed Dickson polynomials
of the first kind including a conjecture on their permutation behaviour over F p .
We then explain the number of fixed points of each reversed Dickson permutation
polynomial. We also explain the cycle type of the reversed Dickson permutation
polynomials. Moreover, in Section 3, we discuss the permutation behaviour of
reversed Dickson polynomials of the first kind over Z2t , Z3 , and Z3t , and also explain

arising from reversed Dickson polynomials of the first kind.

polynomials of the second kind. We begin the section with some preliminary
details, and then explain necessary and sufficient conditions for reversed Dickson

p = 3. This leads us to explore their permutation behaviour over finite rings Z2t
and Z3t . Finding zeros of the regular derivative of polynomials in F p is an important
