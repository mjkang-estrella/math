Abstract
    

We prove the Forking Path Conjecture for all but one element in the symmetric group  S_{4} S4S_{4}. Two specific paths in the rex graph of that element give a counterexample for the conjecture. We propose a refined conjecture for the longest element of any  S_{n} SnS_{n}.

Introduction




In the 2011 paper [3], N. Libedinsky studied morphisms induced by paths in the reduced expression graph (see Definition 2.3) of extra-large Coxeter systems. He showed the surprising fact that morphisms induced by complete paths are idempotents on the corresponding Bott-Samelson bimodules.
In 2016, B. Elias provided in [1] an extension of his work with M. Khovanov [2], where they gave a diagrammatic presentation of the category of Bott-Samelson bimodules  \mathbb{BS} 𝔹​𝕊\mathbb{BS}Bim. Here, morphisms can be translated into linear combinations of planar graphs, and stacking planar graphs can be interpreted as composing morphisms. Elias used their diagrammatic calculus to construct an idempotent using the reduced expression graph of the longest element  w_{0} w0w_{0}, in the symmetric group  S_{n} SnS_{n}.
These results, plus thousands of cases checked by computer, motivated Libedinsky in 2017 to announce the Forking Path Conjecture [4, Section 6.3].



Conjecture (Forking Path Conjecture).



Let  x\in S_{n} x∈Snx\in S_{n}, let  p,q p,qp,q be two complete paths with the same starting points and the same ending points in the reduced expression graph of  x xx. The morphisms induced by these paths are equal.





In this document we prove the conjecture for all but one element in  S_{4} S4S_{4}. The outstanding element is the one that sends 1 to 4, 2 to 2, 3 to 3, and 4 to 1. It might seem surprising to find a counterexample in a group of such a low rank, but we need to recall that there is an infinity of paths for each graph.





Section 2 contains background material, notations and conventions. In Section 3 we prove the FPC for all but one element in  S_{4} S4S_{4}. In Section 4 we present a counterexample for the FPC with a diagrammatic verification. Finally, in Section 5 we explain (without giving a proof) how to generate a family of counterexamples. We also propose a refined conjecture for the longest element of any symmetric group.
