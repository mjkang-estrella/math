Abstract
    

The graphs with permutation-representation number (prn) at most two are known. While a characterization for the class of graphs with the prn at most three is an open problem, we summarize the graphs of this class that are known so far. Although it is known that the prn of trees is at most three, in this work, we devise a polynomial-time algorithm for obtaining a word representing a given tree permutationally. Consequently, we determine the words representing even cycles. Contributing to the class of graphs with the prn at most three, we determine the prn as well as the representation number of book graphs.
  



Keywords: Word-representable graphs, trees, cycles, book graphs, representation number, graph algorithm.




1 Introduction and Preliminaries




Let  X XX be a finite set. A word over  X XX is a finite sequence of elements of  X XX written by juxtaposing them. A word  u uu is called a subword of a word  w ww, denoted by  u\leq w u≤wu\leq w, if  u uu is a subsequence of  w ww. Suppose  w ww is a word over  X XX, and  Y YY is a subset of  X XX. The subword (possibly the empty sequence) of  w ww obtained by deleting all letters belonging to  X\setminus Y X∖YX\setminus Y from  w ww is denoted by  w_{Y} wYw_{Y}. Suppose  w ww is a word over  X XX and  a,b\in X a,b∈Xa,b\in X. We say  a aa and  b bb alternate in  w ww if  w_{\{a,b\}} w{a,b}w_{\{a,b\}} is of the form  abababa\cdots abababa⋯abababa\cdots or  bababa\cdots bababa⋯bababa\cdots of even or odd length. A word in which every letter appears exactly  k kk number of times is called a  k kk-uniform word.




A simple graph  G=(V,E) G=(V,E)G=(V,E) is called a word-representable graph if there exists a word  w ww over its vertex set  V VV such that, for all  a,b\in V a,b∈Va,b\in V,  \overline{ab}\in E a​b¯∈E\overline{ab}\in E if and only if  a aa and  b bb alternate in  w ww. In [14], it was proved that if a graph is word-representable then there are infinitely many words representing it. Further, every word-representable graph is represented by a  k kk-uniform word, for some positive integer  k kk. For a detailed introduction to the theory of word-representable graphs, one may refer to the monograph by Kitaev and Lozin [13].




A word-representable graph is said to be  k kk-word-representable if it is represented by a  k kk-uniform word. The smallest  k kk such that a graph  G GG is  k kk-word-representable is called the representation number of the graph, and it is denoted by  \mathcal{R}(G) ℛ⁡(G)\mathcal{R}(G). The class of word-representable graphs with representation number  k kk is denoted by  \mathcal{R}_{k} ℛk\mathcal{R}_{k} and the class with representation number at most  k kk is denoted by  \mathcal{R}_{\leq k} ℛ≤k\mathcal{R}_{\leq k}. While  \mathcal{R}_{1} ℛ1\mathcal{R}_{1} is the class of complete graphs, it was shown in [10] that  \mathcal{R}_{\leq 2} ℛ≤2\mathcal{R}_{\leq 2} is the class of circle graphs. A circle graph is the intersection graph of a set of chords of a circle. The class of circle graphs was characterized by three minimal forbidden vertex minors [1]. In [10], it was proved that deciding whether a word-representable graph has representation number  k kk, for any fixed  k kk,  3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil 3≤k≤⌈n2⌉3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil, is NP-complete. Nevertheless, the representation number for some specific classes of graphs was obtained, in addition to some isolated examples. For example, while the representation number was established for prisms [12] and crown graphs [7], upper bounds for the representation number were obtained for 3-colorable graphs [9], bipartite graphs [17] and  k kk-cubes [3].




A permutationally representable graph is a word-representable graph that can be represented by a word of the form  p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k} p1p2⋯pkp_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k} where each  p_{i} pip_{i} is a permutation of its vertices. Considering the number of permutations  k kk, such a graph is called a permutationally  k kk-representable graph. It was shown in [15] that the class of permutationally representable graphs is precisely the class of comparability graphs - the graphs which admit transitive orientation. An orientation of a graph is an assignment of direction to each edge so that the graph obtained is a directed graph. If the adjacency relation on the vertices of the resulting directed graph is transitive then the orientation is said to be transitive. The permutation-representation number (in short, prn) of a comparability graph  G GG, denoted by  \mathcal{R}^{p}(G) ℛp​(G)\mathcal{R}^{p}(G), is the smallest number  k kk such that the graph is permutationally  k kk-representable. The class of graphs with prn  k kk is denoted by  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{k} ℛkp\mathcal{R}^{p}_{k}, and the class with the prn at most  k kk is denoted by  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq k} ℛ≤kp\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq k}. Note that  \mathcal{R}(G)\leq\mathcal{R}^{p}(G) ℛ​(G)≤ℛp​(G)\mathcal{R}(G)\leq\mathcal{R}^{p}(G) and  \mathcal{R}_{\leq k}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq k} ℛ≤k⊆ℛ≤kp\mathcal{R}_{\leq k}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq k}. In [10], it was shown that determining the prn of a comparability graph is same as determining the dimension of the partially ordered set (poset) induced by the comparability graph. In [20], Yannakakis showed that deciding whether a poset with  n nn elements has dimension at most  k kk, for fixed  k kk, where  3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil 3≤k≤⌈n2⌉3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil, is NP-hard. Accordingly, it is NP-hard to decide whether a comparability graph with  n nn vetices has the prn at most  k kk, for fixed  k kk, where  3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil 3≤k≤⌈n2⌉3\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil. In [11], it was shown that it is NP-hard to approximate the dimension of a poset to within almost a square root factor.




It is clear that  \mathcal{R}_{1}=\mathcal{R}^{p}_{1} ℛ1=ℛ1p\mathcal{R}_{1}=\mathcal{R}^{p}_{1} is the class of complete graphs. In this paper, we reconcile the class  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} ℛ≤2p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} as the class of permutation graphs. For  k\geq 3 k≥3k\geq 3, characterizations for  \mathcal{R}_{k} ℛk\mathcal{R}_{k} and for  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{k} ℛkp\mathcal{R}^{p}_{k} are open problems. Focusing on identifying the subclasses of graphs with prn three, first we recall the classes of comparability graphs with prn at most three. Although it was known that the trees are in the class  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3} ℛ≤3p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3}, explicit construction of words representing trees permutationally is not available. In this work, we give a polynomial-time algorithm to generate 3-uniform words that represents a tree permutationally. This algorithm enables us to generate words for paths and even cycles11

2 The Class  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} ℛ≤2p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2}




In this section, we reconcile the characterization of  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} ℛ≤2p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} in terms of permutation graphs. In [5, 19], the concept of a permutation graph was introduced and characterized as a comparability graph whose complement is also a comparability graph.




A graph  G=(V,E) G=(V,E)G=(V,E) is called a permutation graph if there exist two permutations  p_{1} p1p_{1} and  p_{2} p2p_{2} on  V VV such that two vertices  a,b\in V a,b∈Va,b\in V are adjacent if and only if  ab\leq p_{1} a​b≤p1ab\leq p_{1} and  ba\leq p_{2} b​a≤p2ba\leq p_{2}. It can be easily observed that permutation graphs are permutationally 2-representable graphs. For instance, the word  p_{1}r(p_{2}) p1​r​(p2)p_{1}r(p_{2}), where  r(p_{2}) r⁡(p2)r(p_{2}) is the reversal of  p_{2} p2p_{2}, represents permutationally a permutation graph. Conversely, if a graph is permutationally 2-representable, it is a permutation graph. Note that a complete graph is a permutation graph. Accordingly, we record the characterization of the class  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} ℛ≤2p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 2} in the following result.



Theorem 2.1



The class of permutation graphs is precisely the class of graphs with prn at most two.





While [6] provides a list of forbidden induced subgraphs for permutation graphs, a succinct characterization of permutation graphs was given in terms of forbidden Seidel minors in [16]. In [8], an  O(n^{3}) O⁡(n3)O(n^{3})-time procedure was given for constructing words representing any permutation graph permutationally.





3 The Class  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3} ℛ≤3p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3}




This section, summarizes the comparability graphs whose prn was known to be at most three. Clearly, the class of permutation graphs belongs to this class. A treelike comparability graph is a graph that admits a transitive orientation in which the cover graph of the associated poset is a tree. In [21], it was proved that the treelike comparability graphs have the prn at most three. Consequently, the trees have the prn at most three. An outerplanar graph is a planar graph in which all its vertices lie on the unbounded face of a planar drawing. The bipartite graphs that are outerplanar are 2-word-representable and have the prn at most three [14, 22]. It was shown in [23] that the split comparability graphs are in  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3} ℛ≤3p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3}, where a split graph is a graph whose vertices can be partitioned into a clique and an independent set. Recently, it has been proved that the extended crown graphs defined by posets of width two are also in  \mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3} ℛ≤3p\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3} [18].





4 Trees




In this section, through a polynomial-time procedure, we construct three permutations on the vertices of a tree whose concatenation represents the tree permutationally.

A vertex  a aa is called a child of a vertex  b bb if  b bb is adjacent to  a aa on the path from the root  r rr to  a aa. In this case,  b bb is called the parent of  a aa. Any other vertex  c cc (other than the parent  b bb) on the path from  r rr to  a aa is called an ancestor of  a aa. If a vertex has no children, then it is called a leaf.
For a positive integer  k kk, a  k kk-ary tree is a tree in which every vertex has at most  k kk children.

7 Conclusion




Modular decomposition of a graph  G GG is a partition of the vertex set of  G GG into modules, where a module  M MM is an induced subgraph of  G GG such that every vertex in  M MM has the same neighborhood outside  M MM [6]. In [12], it was proved that  \mathcal{R}(G^{\prime})=\max\{\mathcal{R}(G),\mathcal{R}(M)\} ℛ⁡(G′)=max⁡{ℛ⁡(G),ℛ⁡(M)}\mathcal{R}(G^{\prime})=\max\{\mathcal{R}(G),\mathcal{R}(M)\}, where  G^{\prime} G′G^{\prime} is a modular extension of a word-representable graph  G GG by a module  M MM that is a comparability graph. Using the aforesaid result and the prn of a tree, we may identify a large class of graphs with the prn of at most three. For instance, if we replace every vertex of a tree with a module that is a permutation graph, then the resultant graph has the representation number two and the prn at most three. Further, based on our observations, we state the following conjecture on the comparability graphs with prn at most three.



Conjecture 1



Let  G GG be a comparability graph. If  G\in\mathcal{R}_{2} G∈ℛ2G\in\mathcal{R}_{2} then  G\in\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3} G∈ℛ≤3pG\in\mathcal{R}^{p}_{\leq 3}.
