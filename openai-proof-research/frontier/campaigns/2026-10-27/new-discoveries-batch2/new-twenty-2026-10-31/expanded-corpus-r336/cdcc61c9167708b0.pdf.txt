          Application of adaptive c-optimal design
              for benchmark dose estimation
                           Marleen Barron and Steven Kim
                              (Communicated by Sat N. Gupta)

     In dose-response studies, researchers use regression to describe a dose-response
     relationship and estimate a benchmark dose. Depending on an experimental
     design with living animals, we can gain more information about the benchmark
     dose with a fixed number of animals or the same amount of information with
     fewer animals. It is not only a mathematical and statistical problem but also an
     ethical problem. We demonstrate the potential benefit of an adaptive design which
     starts with a portion of animals to update information then uses remaining animals
     to maximize information about the benchmark dose. Simulation studies show
     that even a 4-phase locally optimal design is not robust, but a 2-phase Bayesian
     optimal design (BOD) is more robust than a 1-phase BOD, and a 4-phase BOD is
     not necessarily better than a 2-phase BOD. Since more phases require a longer
     experimental time, a practical recommendation is to use a 2-phase BOD.


                                      1. Introduction

In microbial and chemical risk assessment, the response variable of interest is
often binary, such as development of a tumor or not. Mathematical and statistical
modeling has been used to describe the dose-response relationship and determine a
benchmark dose to protect public health [Crump 1984; Piegorsch 2010]. In particu-
lar, researchers have considered various regression methods to model the probability
of an outcome as a function of dose [Kodell et al. 2002; Jensen et al. 2019].
   In the risk assessment, experimental units are living animals. Therefore, it
is not only a mathematical and statistical problem but also an ethical problem.
That is, researchers can obtain more information based on a fixed sample size or
the same amount of information based on a smaller sample size [Slob 2014]. In
addition to the sample size, a careful experimental design (i.e., how to distribute
experimental units on available doses) is an important consideration from the ethical

                                                                                                 6. Final remarks

We applied the locally optimal design (LOD) and Bayesian optimal design (BOD) in
an adaptive manner for the estimation of benchmark dose under the two-parameter
logistic regression. In practice, a 4-phase design would take twice as long as a
2-phase design, but the BOD-4 did not guarantee a substantially lower RMSE than
the BOD-2. A 2-phase design would take twice as long as a 1-phase design, and
the BOD-2 resulted in a substantially lower RMSE in Scenarios 1, 4, and 7 than the
LOD-1, BOD-1, and UD. To this end, a practical recommendation would be the
BOD-2 to balance between the time of an experiment and the quality of benchmark
dose estimation.
   The LOD-1 is often discussed in literature, and it was evaluated by the relative
efficiency [Zhai and Fang 2018]. We evaluated the LOD-k and BOD-k by the
RMSE based on the simulations for the following reasons. First, the interpretation
of relative efficiency relies on the large sample theory (which becomes more accurate
as N → ∞), but N → ∞ is not practical and ethical. For N = 200, which is a
fairly large number of animals, we have observed unstable variances as shown in
Table 2 indicated by “L.” Second, it is difficult to derive the relative efficiency
analytically for an adaptive design (because there are too many possibilities after
360                           MARLEEN BARRON AND STEVEN KIM


Phase 1). We instead used a large number of simulations to approximate the quality
of each experimental design.
   In the simulation study, we numerically investigated the impact of mis-specified
initial values of β and incorrectly specified prior distributions. The true parameter
values of β1 in Scenarios 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, and 9 are at a boundary of U (1.5, 3.5).
Another practical concern is model mis-specification [Dette et al. 2011; Moon et al.
2013; Piegorsch et al. 2013; Kim et al. 2014], and there is no consensus on how to
choose a reasonable and manageable number of models [Jensen et al. 2019]. We
used logistic regression and assumed that it is true under the simulation scenarios.
It is a limitation of our study, and a multimodel approach is one of our future
considerations.
   In the scope of the simulation scenarios presented in this paper, we conclude
that a 2-phase Bayesian c-optimal design is a reasonable approach for designing
an experiment for the benchmark dose estimation from both statistical and ethical
perspectives. From the practical perspective, it would take more time, but the
overarching goal is for the population safety, so we may consider the trade-off.
