## Literal endpoint and independent count

For every natural n with Odd n, and every real x≠0, D(F_{4,n},x)≠0. Source1501.05856v1 p2 defines n cycles of order q meeting at one common vertex; Conjecture1 p6 specifies q=4 and odd n. Use vertices h,a_i,b_i,c_i for 1≤i≤n, with exactly edges ha_i,a_ib_i,b_ic_i,c_ih. There are 3n+1 vertices. In particular odd n gives an EVEN graph order; the MathDB title's “odd-order” means odd parameter, not odd vertex count. The actual statement, not that loose title, is matched. Odd naturals are positive under either usual natural-number convention; n=0 is outside the claim.

The literal polynomial is the finite sum over ALL dominating subsets S of x^{|S|}. Grouping these subsets by cardinality equals the primary definition in source1501 pp1–2. This bridge does not define D by a convenient surrogate.

Partition dominating sets by membership of h. If h is selected, a_i and c_i are dominated already. The remaining b_i must meet S in its closed neighborhood {a_i,b_i,c_i}. Thus any nonempty subset of that triple is permitted and necessary. Its size weights are 3x+3x²+x³; choices are independent across petals. The contribution is x A^n, A=x³+3x²+3x.

If h is absent, domination inside each triple is that of the path a_i-b_i-c_i. Precisely its allowed subsets are {b_i}, {a_i,b_i}, {a_i,c_i}, {b_i,c_i}, and {a_i,b_i,c_i}. Necessity: if b_i is absent, both endpoints must be selected; if b_i is selected it dominates both endpoints. Sufficiency is direct. Local weight is B=x+3x²+x³. Hub h is undominated precisely when NO endpoint a_i or c_i is selected. Of the listed subsets only {b_i} has that property. Hence exactly the global all-opposite-vertices choice must be removed, with weight x^n. No exclusion of any other set is allowed. Contributions and sizes combine bijectively, so as a polynomial identity
D_n=x A^n+B^n-x^n=x^n[x p^n+q^n-1],
where p=x²+3x+3=(x+3/2)²+3/4>0 and q=x²+3x+1=p-2.

For n=1 this expands by hand to x⁴+4x³+6x²=x²[(x+2)²+2], the literal C4 polynomial and source base case. At x=0 the empty subset is not dominating and D_n(0)=0. The zero root is deliberately excluded. No even-n conclusion is inferred. The n=0 construction needs a hub convention and the displayed subtraction formula should not be extended to it; it is irrelevant to Odd n.

## Exhaustive real sign proof

Let n≥1 be odd and H=x p^n+q^n-1.

* x>0: q=1+x(x+3)>1; hence q^n>1 and x p^n>0, giving H>0.
* x<0 and q≤0: oddness yields q^n≤0, whereas x p^n<0; hence H<0.
* x≤-1 and q>0: 0<q<p implies q^n<p^n≤(-x)p^n; therefore x p^n+q^n<0 and H<0.
* -1<x<0 and q>0: 0<q=1+x(x+3)<1, hence q^n<1 and x p^n<0, giving H<0.

These cases are disjoint after the q≤0/q>0 split and cover every nonzero real x. Boundaries x=-1 and q=0 are included; x=-1 has q=-1 and H=-3; q=0 lies in the second case with H<0. No division by q, x+1, or a potentially zero term is used. Odd n ensures x^n has x's sign, so D_n>0 for every x≠0. The stronger strict positivity is proved, not assumed from positive coefficients on negative x. This is ordinary symbolic reasoning; no enumeration program, numerical evaluation, solver, CAS or Lean was run.

## Complete source-to-endpoint reduction

Read source1501.05856v1 Theorems1,3,4, the entire proof of Theorem4 (pp4–6), and Conjecture1, plus the complete bibliography. Theorem4 is genuinely about square flowers, n≥2, not triangles. Put C=(1+x)^3+x=A+1+x. Its recurrence is

The proof uses vertex elimination at v=b_n, edge elimination after contraction, a two-leaf graph, and a one-leaf graph, through its equations (1)–(4). The graph interpretation is consistent: deleting the closed neighborhood of b_n leaves F_{4,n-1}; avoiding its two neighbors in F-b_n forces h selected, giving p_v=x A^{n-1}. Contraction joins a_n to c_n. Relative to deleting b_n, it permits exactly the two extra singleton endpoint choices when h is absent, giving the difference 2x B^{n-1} in (2). With h selected the local endpoint choices agree. Deleting one of the two leaves produces the one-leaf graph; multiplying its polynomial by (1+x) overcounts exactly x B^{n-1}, giving (3). Formula (4) incorporates the condition that h itself was dominated in the old flower; the all-opposite choice contributes x^{n-1}. Combining the equations yields C as printed. The edge-elimination display has a denominator x-1, but the simplified polynomial relation is valid away from x=1 and extends as a polynomial identity; the independent subset argument above removes any need to assume its validity at that exceptional evaluation.

Here is a full recurrence solution, without an unproved “solve the recurrence” step. Suppose D_{n-1}=x A^{n-1}+B^{n-1}-x^{n-1}. Substitute it into the displayed recurrence. The coefficient of A^{n-1} becomes Cx-x²-x=xA. The coefficient of B^{n-1} becomes C-1-3x=A-2x=B. The coefficient of x^{n-1} becomes (1+x)^3-C=-x. Thus D_n=x A^n+B^n-x^n.
