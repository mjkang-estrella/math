 Abstract.

 
 We say that a convex polytope has the clique-face property if every clique in its 1-skeleton is the vertex set of a face.
We establish this property as a geometric necessary condition for quadratic generation of toric ideals. More precisely, we prove that every lattice polytope with primitive edges and a quadratic toric ideal has the clique-face property; in particular, this holds for every
 ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope with a quadratic toric ideal.
For ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes satisfying condition (E), we characterize the clique-face property in terms of divisibility by monomials occurring in quadratic binomials,
and show that, under the clique-face property, such toric ideals have no indispensable monomials of degree at least three.
For edge polytopes and cut polytopes, we prove that the clique-face property is equivalent to quadratic generation.
This yields new geometric characterizations of quadratic generation for these classes.
We also prove that all simple polytopes, matroid independence polytopes, and matroid base polytopes have the clique-face property, and discuss the case of stable set polytopes in connection with conjectures on quadratic toric ideals.

 


 
 Key words and phrases: 
lattice polytopes, simplex faces, graphs of polytopes, toric ideals, quadratic generation

 1. Introduction


 
 Let P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} be a convex polytope
and let V ⁡ ( P ) V(P) be the set of all vertices of P P .
The 1 1 -skeleton sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) of P P is the finite simple graph on the vertex set V ⁡ ( P ) V(P) whose edge set is

 

 
 
 { { v , w } : v , w ∈ V ( P ) , conv ( { v , w } )  is an edge of  P } . \{\{v,w\}:v,w\in V(P),{\rm conv}(\{v,w\})\mbox{ is an edge of }P\}. 
 
 
 
 A clique of a graph G G is the vertex set of a complete subgraph of G G .
We say that a convex polytope P P has the clique-face property if



 
 every clique of sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) is the vertex set of a face of P P .



 
 We denote this property by ( ∗ ) (*) .
Here, we regard P P itself as a face of P P ,
and hence simplices satisfy the clique-face property ( ∗ * ).
We also note that if P P has the clique-face property, then so does every face of P P . Consequently, every clique of sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) is the vertex set of a simplex face of P P . Indeed, if C C is the vertex set of a face F F , then every subset of C C is again a clique in sk ⁡ ( F ) {\rm sk}(F) , and hence is the vertex set of a face of F F . Therefore, F F is a simplex.



 
 For example, a simplicial polytope P P with ( ∗ * ) is called flag .
Any polytope P P of dimension ≤ 2 \leq 2 satisfies ( ∗ * ).
If dim P = 3 \dim P=3 , then by Whitney’s theorem [ 29 ] , the boundary cycles of the
2-faces of P P are precisely the induced non-separating cycles of
 sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) .
Thus a 3-polytope P P satisfies ( ∗ * )
if and only if
there is no triangle in sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) that separates sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) .
It is known [ 14 ] that order polytopes and chain polytopes of posets satisfy ( ∗ * ).



 
 The purpose of this paper is to introduce the clique-face property and establish it as a geometric necessary condition for quadratic generation of toric ideals of lattice polytopes. We also identify important classes of ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes for which this geometric condition characterizes quadratic generation.
Here, a lattice polytope is a convex polytope
all of whose vertices are integer vectors.
Let P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} be a lattice polytope with P ∩ ℤ d = { 𝐚 1 , … , 𝐚 n } P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\} .
Let K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] be a polynomial ring in n n variables over a field K K 
and let K ⁡ [ 𝐭 ± 1 , s ] := K ⁡ [ t 1 , t 1 − 1 , … , t d , t d − 1 , s ] K[{\mathbf{t}}^{\pm 1},s]:=K[t_{1},t_{1}^{-1},\dots,t_{d},t_{d}^{-1},s] be a Laurent polynomial ring
in d + 1 d+1 variables over K K .
Given an integer vector 𝐚 = ( a 1 , … , a d ) ∈ ℤ d {\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d} ,
we set 𝐭 𝐚 = t 1 a 1 ​ … ​ t d a d ∈ K ⁡ [ 𝐭 ± 1 , s ] {\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{a}}=t_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots t_{d}^{a_{d}}\in K[{\mathbf{t}}^{\pm 1},s] .
Then the toric ideal I P I_{P} of P P is the kernel of
a ring homomorphism π : K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] → K ⁡ [ 𝐭 ± 1 , s ] \pi:K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\rightarrow K[{\mathbf{t}}^{\pm 1},s] defined by π ⁡ ( x i ) = 𝐭 𝐚 i ​ s \pi(x_{i})={\bf t}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}s for each i = 1 , 2 , … , n i=1,2,\dots,n .
It is known that I P I_{P} is generated by homogeneous binomials.
In addition, we have

 

 
 (1.1) 
 
 x 1 α 1 ⋯ x n α n − x 1 β 1 ⋯ x n β n ∈ I P ⇔ α 1 𝐚 1 + ⋯ + α n 𝐚 n = β 1 𝐚 1 + ⋯ + β n 𝐚 n . x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}-x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\in I_{P}\iff\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}{\mathbf{a}}_{n}=\beta_{1}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{n}{\mathbf{a}}_{n}. 
 
 
 
 See, e.g., [ 10 ] for details on toric ideals.
The toric ideal I P I_{P} is said to be quadratic 
if I P I_{P} is generated by quadratic binomials.
A lattice segment is called primitive if its only lattice points are its endpoints.
Our first main result shows that the clique-face property is a geometric necessary condition for quadratic generation, provided that all edges of the polytope are primitive.



 
 Theorem 1.1 . 

 
 Let P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} be a lattice polytope
such that every edge of P P is primitive.
If I P I_{P} is quadratic,
then any clique of sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) is the vertex set of a face of P P . 





 
 If P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} is a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope, then
 P ∩ ℤ d P\cap{\mathbb{Z}}^{d} is the vertex set of P P .
In particular, every edge of P P is primitive.
Hence we have the following from Theorem 1.1 .



 
 Corollary 1.2 . 

 
 Let P ⊂ ℝ d P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} be a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope.
If I P I_{P} is quadratic,
then any clique of sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) is the vertex set of a face of P P . 





 
 For example, it is known that the toric ideals of ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes belonging to any of the following classes are quadratic:

 
 • 
 
 hypersimplices;




 • 
 
 order polytopes of posets;




 • 
 
 chain polytopes of posets;




 • 
 
 stable set polytopes of weakly chordal graphs, Meyniel graphs,
perfectly orderable graphs, and almost bipartite graphs;




 • 
 
 edge polytopes of chordal bipartite graphs and complete multipartite graphs;




 • 
 
 cut polytopes of graphs without K 4 K_{4} -minors.




 
 From Corollary 1.2 , P P has the clique-face property ( ∗ * ) if P P belongs to one of the above classes.



 
 On the other hand, the converse of Theorem 1.1 does not hold in general (e.g., Example 3.1 ).
However, under condition (E), the clique-face property ( ∗ * ) guarantees a necessary condition for quadratic toric ideals.
In [ 1 ] , a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope P P is said to satisfy (E) if



 
 
 (E) 
 
 two distinct vertices 𝐚 i {\mathbf{a}}_{i} and 𝐚 j {\mathbf{a}}_{j} of P P form an edge of P P if and only if
there exists a unique way to write 𝐚 i + 𝐚 j {\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{a}}_{j} as the sum of two vertices of P P .




 


 
 Note that the “only if” part holds in general.
It was shown in [ 1 ] that any matroid polytope
and any stable set polytope of a graph satisfy (E).
A binomial f f in I P I_{P} is said to be indispensable 
if either f f or − f -f appears in every system of binomial generators of I P I_{P} .
A monomial m m is said to be indispensable if every system of binomial generators of I P I_{P} 
contains a binomial f f such that m m is a monomial in f f .
In particular, two monomials in an indispensable binomial are indispensable.



 
 Theorem 1.3 . 

 
 Let P P be a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope satisfying condition (E).
Then P P has the clique-face property ( ∗ ) (*) if and only if
any monomial appearing in a binomial in I P I_{P} is divisible by
a quadratic monomial appearing in a quadratic binomial in I P I_{P} .
In particular, if P P has the clique-face property ( ∗ ) (*) , then
 I P I_{P} has no indispensable monomials of degree ≥ 3 \geq 3 . 





 
 From Corollary 1.2 and Theorem 1.3 , for a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope P P with (E), we have

 

 
 (1.2) 
 
 I P ​  is quadratic  ⇒ ( ∗ ) ⇒ I P ​  has no indispensable monomials of degree  ≥ 3 . I_{P}\mbox{ is quadratic }\Rightarrow(*)\Rightarrow I_{P}\mbox{ has no indispensable monomials of degree }\geq 3. 
 
 
 
 In the present paper,
for the following classes of ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes,
we will show that
these polytopes satisfy (E), and that
three conditions in ( 1.2 ) are equivalent:

 
 • 
 
 Edge polytopes of graphs;




 • 
 
 Cut polytopes of graphs.




 
 Next, we study classes of lattice polytopes whose toric ideals are conjectured to be quadratic.
Two important classes of lattice polytopes whose toric ideals are conjectured to be quadratic are smooth polytopes and matroid base polytopes.
Note that any smooth polytope P P is simple, i.e.,
 sk ⁡ ( P ) {\rm sk}(P) is a d d -regular graph with d = dim P d=\dim P .
We will show that

 
 • 
 
 Any simple polytope satisfies ( ∗ * );




 • 
 
 Any matroid independence polytope satisfies ( ∗ * );




 • 
 
 Any matroid base polytope satisfies ( ∗ * ).




 


 
 The present paper is organized as follows:
In Section 2, we give a sufficient condition for ( ∗ * ) in terms of toric ideals (Theorem 2.3 ).
From this, it follows that quadratic generation guarantees the clique-face property when every edge of the polytope is primitive (Theorem 1.1 ).
In particular, for ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes, quadratic generation guarantees ( ∗ * ) (Corollary 1.2 ).
In Section 3, we study ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytopes with (E).
A ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope P P with (E) satisfies ( ∗ * ) if and only if any monomial appearing in a binomial in I P I_{P} is divisible by a quadratic monomial appearing in a quadratic binomial in I P I_{P} (Theorem 1.3 ).
In particular,
if a ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) -polytope P P satisfies (E) and ( ∗ * ), then I P I_{P} has no indispensable monomials of degree ≥ 3 \geq 3 .
In addition, we will show that (i) edge polytopes of graphs and (ii) cut polytopes of graphs satisfy (E) and that
three conditions in ( 1.2 ) are equivalent for these polytopes.
In Section 4, we study classes of lattice polytopes whose toric ideals are conjectured to be quadratic.
We will show that any simple polytope, any matroid independence polytope, and any matroid base polytope satisfy ( ∗ * ).
Finally, we discuss stable set polytopes of graphs in connection with a conjecture.
