 Abstract

 
 Guo, Li, Shangguan, Tamo, and Wootters proposed a hypergraph analogue of the Nash–Williams–Tutte theorem, asserting that every k k -weakly-partition-connected hypergraph admits a k k -distinguishable tree assignment. We identify a sharp edge-count obstruction to the literal statement. A full tree assignment has ρ ⁡ ( H ) = ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( H ) ( | e | − 1 ) \rho(H)=\sum_{e\in E(H)}(|e|-1) labelled graph edges, whereas an ordered decomposition into k k spanning trees has exactly k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) k(t-1) edges. Since weak partition connectivity implies only ρ ⁡ ( H ) ≥ k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)\geq k(t-1) , every strict inequality rules out a full decomposition. In particular, for all t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , and q ≥ 1 q\geq 1 , the hypergraph consisting of k + q k+q labelled copies of the full hyperedge is k k -weakly-partition-connected but admits no k k -distinguishable full tree assignment. We therefore isolate the critical regime ρ ⁡ ( H ) = k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)=k(t-1) and prove that every tree assignment of a critical k k -weakly-partition-connected hypergraph admits a full ordered decomposition into k k spanning trees. Consequently, the critical conjecture reduces to the existence of some tree assignment having a decomposition whose signature fiber is a singleton. We establish this property for layer-contained certificates, without a star hypothesis or a rank restriction in interior layers. These certificates also imply weak partition connectivity by a quotient-rank argument and are stable under one-vertex sums. Finally, we derive a decomposition-indexed generalized-Laplace expansion for the relevant intersection-matrix minor, together with an exact formula for its collected monomial coefficients; we also give the required row-and-column perfect-shuffle normalization, identify criticality with the square full-assignment row count, and separate the critical conjecture from the additional overfull pruning problem.

 1 Introduction, notation, and main results


 
 The starting point of this note is the conjecture introduced by Guo, Li, Shangguan, Tamo, and Wootters in their work on Reed–Solomon list decoding  [ 1 ] . The conjecture was proposed as a hypergraph counterpart of the Nash–Williams–Tutte tree-packing theorem  [ 2 , 3 ] ; it is closely related to hypergraph decomposition results such as  [ 4 ] and was intended to provide a route to the intersection-matrix conjecture originating in  [ 5 ] . We retain the labelled-multihypergraph formulation required for that application and keep the relevant edge-count parameter explicit.



 
 Let H = ( V , E ) H=(V,E) be a finite labelled multihypergraph. Hyperedges are occurrences rather than merely subsets: occurrences with the same support remain distinct when their labels are distinct. We assume that every hyperedge occurrence is nonempty. A singleton occurrence has a unique assigned tree, namely the edgeless graph on its sole vertex. It contributes zero to ρ ⁡ ( H ) \rho(H) and to w H ​ ( 𝒫 ) w_{H}(\mathcal{P}) for every partition 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , and its signature coordinate is identically zero. Thus deleting all singleton occurrences preserves k k -WPC, criticality, the existence of full tree decompositions, and distinguishability after the corresponding zero coordinates are omitted. Conversely, the assigned graph and every ordered decomposition for the reduced hypergraph extend canonically when the edgeless singleton label classes are reinstated; in a layer-contained certificate, the reinstated singleton occurrences may be assigned arbitrary layer indices. We henceforth assume that every hyperedge occurrence has cardinality at least two. Write t = | V | t=|V| . The case t = 1 t=1 is immediate under the convention that the edgeless graph on one vertex is a tree, so throughout the substantive arguments we assume t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 .

 A tree assignment of H H chooses, for every hyperedge occurrence e e , a simple tree F e F_{e} on vertex set e e and labels every edge of F e F_{e} by e e .  Conjecture 1.1 (Hypergraph Nash–Williams–Tutte conjecture, literal form). 

 
 For positive integers t t and k k , every k k -WPC hypergraph on t t vertices has a k k -distinguishable tree assignment.
 





 
 The first result identifies the exact numerical defect in the literal formulation.



 
 Theorem 1.2 (Sharp excess obstruction). 

 
 Let H H be a labelled multihypergraph on t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 vertices. If H H has a k k -distinguishable tree assignment, then ρ ⁡ ( H ) = k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)=k(t-1) . Consequently, every k k -WPC hypergraph satisfying ρ ⁡ ( H ) > k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)>k(t-1) is a counterexample to Conjecture   1.1 .
 





 
 Theorem 1.3 (Infinite overfull counterexample family). 

 
 Fix t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , and q ≥ 1 q\geq 1 . Let H t , k , q H_{t,k,q} have vertex set V V and k + q k+q labelled hyperedge occurrences, each equal to V V . Then H t , k , q H_{t,k,q} is k k -WPC and has no k k -distinguishable tree assignment. The componentwise-minimal parameter triple is ( t , k , q ) = ( 2 , 1 , 1 ) (t,k,q)=(2,1,1) ; it yields the two-vertex hypergraph with two parallel labelled 2 2 -edges.
 





 
 Consequently, any valid universal theorem about full tree assignments in the k k -WPC class must restrict to, or otherwise force, the critical equality. The most direct corrected formulation imposes precisely this necessary equality.



 
 Definition 1.4 (Criticality). 

 
 A hypergraph H H on V V is k k -critical if ρ ⁡ ( H ) = k ⁡ ( | V | − 1 ) \rho(H)=k(|V|-1) .
 





 
 Conjecture 1.5 (Critical form). 

 
 Every k k -critical, k k -WPC hypergraph has a k k -distinguishable tree assignment.
 





 
 Criticality is not merely an edge count verified after a decomposition has been found. Weak partition connectivity supplies k k edge-disjoint spanning trees in every assigned graph, and at criticality those trees exhaust all assigned edges. Thus every tree assignment of a critical k k -WPC hypergraph has an ordered k k -tree decomposition. The unresolved assertion in Conjecture   1.5 is the existential statement that some tree assignment admits at least one decomposition whose signature fiber is a singleton.



 
 Our positive result is based on the following certificate. It contains the extremal-layer star constructions as special cases and permits arbitrary assigned trees, including labels of arbitrary rank in interior layers.



 
 Definition 1.6 (Layer-contained certificate). 

 
 A layer-contained certificate for a hypergraph H H consists of a tree assignment G = ⨄ e ∈ E ⁡ ( H ) F e G=\biguplus_{e\in E(H)}F_{e} , an ordered k k -tree decomposition 𝒯 = ( T 0 , … , T k − 1 ) \mathcal{T}=(T_{0},\ldots,T_{k-1}) of G G , and a map ℓ : E ⁡ ( H ) → { 0 , … , k − 1 } \ell:E(H)\to\{0,\ldots,k-1\} such that E ⁡ ( F e ) ⊆ E ⁡ ( T ℓ ⁡ ( e ) ) E(F_{e})\subseteq E(T_{\ell(e)}) for every hyperedge occurrence e e .
 





 
 No star hypothesis is imposed, and labels assigned to interior layers may have arbitrary rank.



 
 Theorem 1.7 (Layer-contained positive instances). 

 
 If a hypergraph H H has a layer-contained certificate, then H H is k k -critical and k k -WPC, and the certified decomposition is the unique ordered k k -tree decomposition having its signature. Hence the certified tree assignment is k k -distinguishable.
 





 
 The certificate is stable under the natural gluing operation.



 
 Theorem 1.8 (One-vertex sum closure). 

 
 Let H ( 1 ) H^{(1)} and H ( 2 ) H^{(2)} have layer-contained certificates for the same integer k k . Suppose that their vertex sets meet in exactly one vertex r r and that their hyperedge-occurrence sets are disjoint. Then the one-vertex sum H ( 1 ) ∨ r H ( 2 ) H^{(1)}\vee_{r}H^{(2)} has a layer-contained certificate. In particular, it is k k -critical, k k -WPC, and has a k k -distinguishable tree assignment.

 2 The excess obstruction and the critical regime


 
 This section establishes the excess obstruction and the exact packing statement available in the critical regime.

 Remark 2.3 (Full assignments versus distinguishable subgraphs). 

 
 The obstruction concerns the literal full-assignment statement. A result that asks only for a k k -distinguishable labelled subgraph of a tree assignment may delete assigned graph edges and is not contradicted by overfullness. The distinction is essential: a full tree assignment always has ρ ⁡ ( H ) \rho(H) edges, whereas a retained subgraph may have exactly k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) k(t-1) edges.
 





 
 Example 2.4 (The minimal literal counterexample). 

 
 Let V = { a , b } V=\{a,b\} , let k = 1 k=1 , and let H H consist of two labelled copies of { a , b } \{a,b\} . The only nontrivial partition has weak excess 2 ≥ 1 2\geq 1 , so H H is 1 1 -WPC. Every full tree assignment is the two-edge labelled multigraph on a , b a,b , while a one-tree decomposition on two vertices has exactly one edge. Thus the conjecture fails before signature uniqueness is considered.
 





 
 The following crossing bound separates the existence of a tree packing from the requirement that the packing exhaust all assigned edges.

 3 Critical normalization and the intersection-matrix route


 
 This section makes precise the role of criticality in the intersection-matrix argument of  [ 1 ] . The equality ρ ⁡ ( H ) = k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)=k(t-1) is not needed merely to deduce weak partition connectivity from the weight inequalities. It is the condition under which a full tree assignment supplies exactly the k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) k(t-1) labelled bottom rows required to complement the cycle-space block to a square minor.

 6 Corrected formulations, pruning, and conclusions


 
 The edge-count obstruction leads to three distinct universal statements. Their quantifiers and logical relation must be kept separate.



 
 
 (A) 
 
 Literal full-assignment statement. Every k k -WPC hypergraph has a k k -distinguishable full tree assignment. This is Conjecture   1.1 and is false by Theorem   1.3 .




 (B) 
 
 Critical full-assignment statement. Every k k -critical, k k -WPC hypergraph has a k k -distinguishable full tree assignment. This is Conjecture   1.5 . It is compatible with edge counting and is sufficient for the intersection-matrix implication in Theorem   3.6 . Under the canonical support representation, its input conditions coincide with the intersection weight conditions by Proposition   3.2 ; its distinguishability conclusion is a stronger structural certificate than matrix full column rank alone.




 (C) 
 
 Pruned-assignment statement. Every k k -WPC hypergraph H H has a tree assignment G G and a labelled spanning subgraph G ′ ⊆ G G^{\prime}\subseteq G with | E ⁡ ( G ′ ) | = k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) |E(G^{\prime})|=k(t-1) such that G ′ G^{\prime} has a signature-unique ordered k k -tree decomposition, where, for each label e e , the signature coordinate is computed using the retained class E ⁡ ( F e ) ∩ E ⁡ ( G ′ ) E(F_{e})\cap E(G^{\prime}) .




 


 
 Let (C > ) denote statement (C) restricted to overfull hypergraphs, namely those satisfying ρ ⁡ ( H ) > k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)>k(t-1) .



 
 Proposition 6.1 (Exact logical decomposition of pruning). 

 
 As universal statements over k k -WPC hypergraphs, (C) holds if and only if both (B) and (C > ) hold. In particular, the unrestricted pruning statement implies the critical full-assignment conjecture; it is not logically independent of it.
 





 
 Proof.

 
 Assume (C) and let H H be critical. Every full tree assignment G G of H H has | E ⁡ ( G ) | = ρ ⁡ ( H ) = k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) |E(G)|=\rho(H)=k(t-1) by Lemma   2.2 . The subgraph G ′ G^{\prime} supplied by (C) has the same number of edge occurrences as G G and is obtained by deletion, so G ′ = G G^{\prime}=G . Hence (B) holds. The restriction (C > ) follows immediately from (C). 



 
 Conversely, assume (B) and (C > ), and let H H be k k -WPC. By Lemma   2.1 , ρ ⁡ ( H ) ≥ k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)\geq k(t-1) . If equality holds, apply (B) and take G ′ = G G^{\prime}=G . If the inequality is strict, apply (C > ). These are the only two cases, so (C) holds.
 





 
 The overfull component cannot be solved by a universal rule that merely deletes whole hyperedge occurrences until the target excess is reached; whole-hyperedge contributions can be arithmetically too coarse.



 
 Example 6.2 (Whole-hyperedge pruning can miss the target). 

 
 Let V = { 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 } V=\{1,2,3,4\} , let k = 1 k=1 , and let H H have two hyperedges e 1 = { 1 , 2 , 3 } e_{1}=\{1,2,3\} and e 2 = { 1 , 2 , 4 } e_{2}=\{1,2,4\} . Then ρ ⁡ ( H ) = 4 > 3 = k ⁡ ( | V | − 1 ) \rho(H)=4>3=k(|V|-1) , but no subhypergraph has excess 3 3 : the possible excesses are 0 0 , 2 2 , and 4 4 . 



 
 The hypergraph is nevertheless 1 1 -WPC. Let 𝒫 \mathcal{P} have s s blocks. Since e 1 ∪ e 2 = V e_{1}\cup e_{2}=V , every block is met by at least one of the two hyperedges. The block containing vertex 1 1 is met by both. Counting block–hyperedge incidences gives p 𝒫 ​ ( e 1 ) + p 𝒫 ​ ( e 2 ) ≥ s + 1 p_{\mathcal{P}}(e_{1})+p_{\mathcal{P}}(e_{2})\geq s+1 , and hence w H ​ ( 𝒫 ) = p 𝒫 ​ ( e 1 ) + p 𝒫 ​ ( e 2 ) − 2 ≥ s − 1 w_{H}(\mathcal{P})=p_{\mathcal{P}}(e_{1})+p_{\mathcal{P}}(e_{2})-2\geq s-1 . 



 
 Thus deletion of whole hyperedge occurrences alone cannot be a valid universal exact-pruning rule. Any successful exact theorem must use a finer operation, such as deletion of assigned graph edges or determinant rows, or some other mechanism that is not constrained to change the excess by an entire hyperedge contribution.
 





 
 The logarithmic relaxation recorded in  [ 1 , Theorem 6.4] , based on results of Cheriyan–Salavatipour and Calinescu–Chekuri–Vondrák  [ 6 , 7 ] , yields a distinguishable subgraph under stronger weak partition connectivity. It motivates (C > ) but does not settle the exact k k -WPC formulation.



 
 The preceding results give the following corrected picture. The literal conjecture fails because weak partition connectivity supplies a lower bound on excess, while a full decomposition imposes equality. The family H t , k , q H_{t,k,q} gives counterexamples for every t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 , k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , and q ≥ 1 q\geq 1 . In the critical regime, every tree assignment already has a full k k -tree decomposition by Theorem   2.6 ; the remaining assertion is whether some assignment has a decomposition with singleton signature fiber. Layer-contained certificates establish this assertion for an explicit infinite class, with no star requirement and no restriction on the ranks of interior labels. Their weak partition inequalities follow from quotient rank, their signatures are isolated by a layer-by-layer capacity induction, and the construction is stable under iterated one-vertex sums.



 
 For the Reed–Solomon intersection-matrix route, the critical equality is wt ⁡ ( I [ t ] ) = k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \operatorname{wt}(I_{[t]})=k(t-1) . By Proposition   3.2 , the corresponding proper-subset weight inequalities are exactly the canonical form of critical weak partition connectivity. By Proposition   3.7 , the total equality is precisely the condition under which a full tree assignment supplies the k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) k(t-1) selected bottom rows required for the square minor. The decomposition-indexed generalized-Laplace expansion in Lemma   3.4 then identifies the signature of an ordered decomposition with the exponent vector of its determinant monomial, while its coefficient formula records the possible cancellation among decompositions in the same signature fiber.



 
 The remaining overfull problem is the separate component (C > ): if H H is k k -WPC and ρ ⁡ ( H ) > k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) \rho(H)>k(t-1) , must some tree assignment of H H contain a labelled spanning subgraph with exactly k ⁡ ( t − 1 ) k(t-1) edges and a signature-unique ordered k k -tree decomposition? By Proposition   6.1 , a universal pruned-assignment theorem consists exactly of the critical conjecture together with this overfull component. No claim of logical independence between them is justified.
