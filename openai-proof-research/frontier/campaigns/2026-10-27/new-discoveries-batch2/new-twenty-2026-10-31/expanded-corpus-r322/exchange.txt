 Abstract

 
 We reduce the embracing exchange distance of bases of oriented matroids to the metric of oriented matroid polyhedra. This allows us to disprove a recent conjecture of Caoduro, Khodamoradi, Paat, and Shepherd and its oriented-matroid extension introduced in  [ 2 ] . On the other hand, we show that any two embracing bases of an oriented matroid of rank r r can be transformed into each other in at most 2 ​ r log 2 ⁡ ( r ) + 3 2r^{\log_{2}(r)+3} steps and in at most r r steps in a graphic oriented matroid or a Lawrence oriented matroid, thus confirming the conjecture in these cases.

 It might be that the lower bound in Theorem   1.3 indeed always is an equality - we believe that this is an interesting question. In any case, choosing the counterexamples to the polyhedral Hirsch conjecture of Klee and Walkup  [ 10 ] and Todd  [ 19 ] as P ℳ ∗ , e P_{\mathcal{M}^{*},e} we can disprove Conjectures  1.1 and  1.2 .

 For every r ⩾ 1 r\geqslant 1 there is a realizable oriented matroid

 Note that a purely geometric disproof of Conjecture   1.1 has also been provided by the last author in his Bachelor’s thesis  [ 15 ] .



 
 Combining some oriented matroid restrictions with the fact that G ⁡ ( P ℳ ∗ , e ) G(P_{\mathcal{M}^{*},e}) obeys the proof of the upper bound on polyhedron diameter due to Kalai and Kleitman  [ 7 ] we also get a positive result:



 
 Corollary 1.5 . 

 
 Let ℳ = ( E , 𝒞 ) \mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{C}) be an oriented matroid of rank r ⩾ 1 r\geqslant 1 , e ∈ E e\in E , and let B , B ′ B,B^{\prime} be e e -embracing bases of ℳ {\mathcal{M}} . Then d e ​ ( B , B ′ ) ⩽ 2 ​ r log 2 ⁡ ( r ) + 3 d_{e}(B,B^{\prime})\leqslant 2r^{\log_{2}(r)+3} . 





 
 As a first concrete positive example, we study graphic oriented matroids. Here a tree T T of a digraph D D is e e -embracing if the fundamental cycle C ⁡ ( T , e ) C(T,e) is positively directed. In the graphic case, Conjecture   1.2 holds, and the exchange sequence can moreover be chosen monotone with respect to the target basis:



 
 Theorem 1.6 . 

 
 Let ℳ = ( E , 𝒞 ) \mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{C}) be a graphic oriented matroid of rank r r , e ∈ E e\in E , and let B , B ′ B,B^{\prime} be e e -embracing bases of ℳ \mathcal{M} . Then d e ​ ( B , B ′ ) ⩽ r d_{e}(B,B^{\prime})\leqslant r . 





 
 It would be natural to try and extend Theorem   1.6 to cographic or regular matroids and would be interested in seeing any progress into that direction..



 
 Finally, we study Lawrence oriented matroids  [ 13 , 17 ] , i.e., uniform oriented matroids obtained as the union of uniform oriented matroids of rank 1 1 . These oriented matroids were used in [ 16 ] to provide upper bounds for McMullen’s problem  [ 12 ] , in a generalization to k k -neighborly polytopes [ 5 ] and in relation to Roudneff’s conjecture [ 18 ] in [ 6 , 14 ] .
Here we prove Conjecture   1.2 for these oriented matroids:



 
 Theorem 1.7 . 

 
 Let ℳ = ( E , 𝒞 ) \mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{C}) be a Lawrence oriented matroid of rank r r , e ∈ E e\in E , and let B , B ′ B,B^{\prime} be e e -embracing bases of ℳ \mathcal{M} . Then d e ​ ( B , B ′ ) ⩽ r d_{e}(B,B^{\prime})\leqslant r . 





 
 Relation to previous versions.


 
 The present paper combines material from two earlier preprints. Bérczi and Nádor  [ 2 ] introduced the oriented-matroid formulation of the embracing exchange problem and proved the graphic case. Knauer and Montejano  [ 11 ] developed the connection with oriented matroid polyhedra, obtained the counterexamples and the general upper bound, and proved the result for Lawrence oriented matroids. The present four-author version unifies these results in a common framework and includes several revisions and strengthenings.







 
 2 General bounds


 
 For basic notions on oriented matroids we refer to  [ 3 , 1 , 20 ] .
Following  [ 3 , Definition 10.1.1] , given an oriented matroid ℳ = ( E , ℒ ) \mathcal{M}=(E,\mathcal{L}) with covectors ℒ \mathcal{L} and e ∈ E e\in E the oriented matroid polyhedron is P ℳ , e = { X ∈ ℒ ∣ ∀ f ∈ E : X ( f ) ⩾ 0  and  X e = + } P_{\mathcal{M},e}=\{X\in\mathcal{L}\mid\forall_{f\in E}:X(f)\geqslant 0\text{ and }X_{e}=+\} . The vertices of P ℳ , e P_{\mathcal{M},e} correspond to non-negative cocircuits 𝒞 e ∗ ⁣ + \mathcal{C}^{*+}_{e} of ℳ \mathcal{M} that have positive e e -coordinate. The graph G ⁡ ( P ℳ , e ) G(P_{\mathcal{M},e}) is the 1 1 -skeleton of P ℳ , e P_{\mathcal{M},e} . In particular, it has the same vertices and edges and can be viewed within the big face lattice of ℳ \mathcal{M} . The latter suffices to show that G ⁡ ( P ℳ , e ) G(P_{\mathcal{M},e}) is connected, see [ 4 , Lemma 3.2] .



 
 For the sake of this paper we restate the adjacency in oriented matroid polyhedra in the dual setting.
