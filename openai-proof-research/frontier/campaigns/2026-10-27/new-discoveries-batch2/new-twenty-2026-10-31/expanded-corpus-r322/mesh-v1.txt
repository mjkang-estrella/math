 On mesh patterns of short length: Equidistribution and enumeration

 Abstract. The classification and enumeration of short mesh patterns have emerged as two central directions in the area. We make substantial progress on both fronts. We construct an involution and a bijection that establish distributional equivalences for two classes of length- 2 2 mesh patterns, thereby resolving a conjecture from 2019 and a recent conjecture. As a consequence, the best known upper bounds for the numbers of distribution-equivalence and Wilf-equivalence classes drop to 106 106 and 47 47 , respectively. Combined with the known lower bounds of 105 and 46, conjectured to be exact, these results leave both classifications hinging on a single distribution-equivalence question conjectured in 2019, whose resolution would at once settle the remaining Wilf-equivalence case. We further conjecture that this unresolved equidistribution also holds for involutions, a subclass of all permutations.



 
 We also determine the distributions of three additional classes of length- 2 2 mesh patterns through a detailed structural analysis. Our work combines bijective techniques with generating-function methods, yielding new insights into the structure and enumeration of short mesh patterns.



 
 Keywords: mesh pattern, distribution, equidistribution, bijection, generating function. 
 



 
 AMS Subject Classifications: 05A05, 05A15, 05A19.



 
 1 Introduction


 
 In this paper, [ a , b ] [a,b] denotes the set of integers from a a to b b , that is, [ a , b ] := { a , a + 1 , … , b } [a,b]:=\{a,a+1,\ldots,b\} . In particular we write [ n ] := [ 1 , n ] [n]:=[1,n] . We denote the set of permutations of [ n ] [n] by 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} , express each permutation σ ∈ 𝔖 n \sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} using its one-line notation σ 1 ​ … ​ σ n \sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n} with σ i = σ ⁡ ( i ) \sigma_{i}=\sigma(i) for 1 ≤ i ≤ n 1\leq i\leq n , and refer to σ \sigma as an n n -permutation. A (classical) permutation pattern is a shorter permutation. Given two permutations σ ∈ 𝔖 n \sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} and π ∈ 𝔖 m \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{m} , we say that σ \sigma contains π \pi as a pattern if some subsequence of the entries of σ \sigma (again, in its one-line expression) has the same relative order as all of the entries of π \pi . Otherwise σ \sigma is said to avoid π \pi as a pattern. For instance, the permutation σ = 216435 \sigma=216435 contains both 132 132 and 321 321 as patterns, while it avoids the pattern 231 231 . Besides its well‑known origin in the stack‑sorting problem of algorithm analysis  [ 9 ] , the permutation pattern has proven strikingly versatile, characterizing structures across many areas of mathematics. A quite recent illustration is the following remarkable theorem in Schubert calculus, due to Chen, Fan, and Ye:



 
 Theorem 1.1 ( [ 2 , Theorem 1.1] ) . 

 
 The Grothendieck polynomial 𝔊 w ​ ( x ) \mathfrak{G}_{w}(x) is zero-one, i.e., has coefficients 0 0 or ± 1 \pm 1 , if and only if w w avoids the patterns 1432 1432 , 1342 1342 , 13254 13254 , 31524 31524 , 12534 12534 , and 21534 21534 . 





 
 As research on patterns progressed, it quickly became apparent that extending the original notion of permutation patterns was both necessary and beneficial. The notion of mesh pattern was the outcome of one such endeavour by Brändén and Claesson  [ 1 ] ; see Definition  2.1 . It subsumes several classes of patterns as special cases, such as consecutive, vincular, and bivincular patterns. For undefined terms and further information, see the monograph  [ 6 ] by the third author.



 
 From our perspective, research on mesh patterns primarily focuses on two major classes of problems, and the present paper makes contributions to both. The first class of problems is concerned with the classification. For all n , k ≥ 0 n,k\geq 0 , let 𝔖 n ​ ( p ) \mathfrak{S}_{n}(p) denote the set of n n -permutations that avoid a given pattern p p , and let s n , k ​ ( p ) s_{n,k}(p) be the number of n n -permutations with k k occurrences of p p . In particular, we see that s n , 0 ​ ( p ) = | 𝔖 n ​ ( p ) | s_{n,0}(p)=|\mathfrak{S}_{n}(p)| . Given two patterns p 1 p_{1} and p 2 p_{2} , we denote by 𝔖 n ​ ( p 1 , p 2 ) \mathfrak{S}_{n}(p_{1},p_{2}) the set of n n -permutations that avoid simultaneously both patterns p 1 p_{1} and p 2 p_{2} . We say that p 1 p_{1} and p 2 p_{2} are Wilf-equivalent if | 𝔖 n ​ ( p 1 ) | = | 𝔖 n ​ ( p 2 ) | |\mathfrak{S}_{n}(p_{1})|=|\mathfrak{S}_{n}(p_{2})| for all n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 . They are said to be equidistributed , if the condition s n , k ​ ( p 1 ) = s n , k ​ ( p 2 ) s_{n,k}(p_{1})=s_{n,k}(p_{2}) holds for all n , k ≥ 0 n,k\geq 0 , in which case we write p 1 ∼ p 2 p_{1}\sim p_{2} . If we use p ⁡ ( σ ) p(\sigma) to denote the number of occurrences of the pattern p p in a permutation σ \sigma , then the relation p 1 ∼ p 2 p_{1}\sim p_{2} can be rephrased in terms of generating functions as

 

 
 
 ∑ n ≥ 0 t n ​ ∑ σ ∈ 𝔖 n q p 1 ​ ( σ ) \displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}q^{p_{1}(\sigma)} 
 = ∑ n ≥ 0 t n ​ ∑ σ ∈ 𝔖 n q p 2 ​ ( σ ) . \displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{n}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}q^{p_{2}(\sigma)}. 
 
 
 


 
 A classification of mesh patterns of length 2 2 was initiated by Hilmarsson et al.  [ 5 ] , who showed that the number of Wilf-equivalence classes for length- 2 2 mesh patterns, denoted here by 𝗐𝗂𝗅𝖿 \mathsf{wilf} , is at most 56 56 , and conjectured that 𝗐𝗂𝗅𝖿 = 46 \mathsf{wilf}=46 . This upper bound was improved to 𝗐𝗂𝗅𝖿 ≤ 49 \mathsf{wilf}\leq 49 in a recent work by Su, Kitaev, and Zhang  [ 15 ] . The same work  [ 15 ] also provided a nearly complete classification of equidistribution equivalence classes for mesh patterns of length 2 2 . The authors showed that the number of such classes, denoted here by 𝖽𝗂𝗌𝗍 \mathsf{dist} , is at most 108 108 , and conjectured that 𝖽𝗂𝗌𝗍 = 105 \mathsf{dist}=105 . Interestingly, the patterns involved in the conjectured equivalences are the same as those appearing in the conjectured Wilf-equivalences, so that proving any of the equidistribution conjectures automatically implies the corresponding Wilf-equivalence conjectures.



 
 In the present work, we further reduce these upper bounds to 𝗐𝗂𝗅𝖿 ≤ 47 \mathsf{wilf}\leq 47 and 𝖽𝗂𝗌𝗍 ≤ 106 \mathsf{dist}\leq 106 , respectively, by resolving a 2019 conjecture from  [ 7 ] and a recent conjecture from  [ 15 ] . Unless otherwise noted, the class numbers used throughout the paper are those catalogued in  [ 15 ] .



 
 Theorem 1.2 . 

 
 For mesh patterns of length 2 2 , the number 𝖽𝗂𝗌𝗍 \mathsf{dist} of equidistribution equivalence classes satisfies 105 ≤ 𝖽𝗂𝗌𝗍 ≤ 106 105\leq\mathsf{dist}\leq 106 .

 We begin with a formal definition of a mesh pattern.



 
 Definition 2.1 .

 At present, it is not clear whether the original conjectured equidistribution or its restriction to involutions is the more structurally meaningful statement. It is conceivable that the involution-restricted version admits a more direct or simpler proof, or alternatively, that the full equidistribution is the more natural and tractable object. Clarifying this relationship remains an interesting direction for future research.
