 Abstract.

 
 Ballantine, Beck, and Merca defined the elementary symmetric partition map pre j \pre_{j} that sends a partition λ \lambda to a larger partition whose parts are the summands appearing in the evaluation of the j j -th elementary symmetric polynomial on λ \lambda . They conjectured that pre j \pre_{j} is injective on the set of partitions of n n with length ℓ ≥ j \ell\geq j . The ℓ = j \ell=j case was disproved by Devnani and Eyyunni; they instead conjectured the statement to be true for ℓ > j \ell>j . In this article, we answer this refined conjecture in the negative by proving that pre j \pre_{j} is not injective on partitions of n n with length 2 ​ j 2j for j ≥ 3 j\geq 3 . We also prove that the analogous map prh j \prh_{j} defined via the complete homogenous symmetric polynomial is injective on the set of all partitions.

 


 
 1. Introduction


 
 Throughout, let n n be a finite positive integer. A partition λ = ( λ 1 , … , λ ℓ ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}) is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers. Each λ i \lambda_{i} is called a part of λ \lambda , the number of parts ℓ = ℓ ⁡ ( λ ) \ell=\ell(\lambda) is called the length of λ \lambda , and the sum of parts λ 1 + ⋯ + λ ℓ = | λ | \lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=|\lambda| is called the size of λ \lambda . We identify a partition λ \lambda with its multiset of parts { { λ i } } 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ \{\!\!\{\lambda_{i}\}\!\!\}_{1\leq i\leq\ell} .



 
 The j j -th elementary symmetric polynomial is defined as,

 

 
 
 e j ( x 1 , … , x ℓ ) = ∑ 1 ≤ i 1 < ⋯ < i j ≤ ℓ x i 1 ⋯ x i j , \displaystyle e_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq\ell}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}, 
 
 
 
 for 1 ≤ j ≤ ℓ 1\leq j\leq\ell . In [ 2 ] , Ballantine, Beck, and Merca defined a function pre j \pre_{j} which sends a partition λ = ( λ 1 , … , λ ℓ ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}) to the partition whose multiset of parts is,

 

 
 
 { { λ i 1 ⋯ λ i j : 1 ≤ i 1 < i 2 < ⋯ < i j ≤ ℓ } } , \{\!\!\{\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{j}}:1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq\ell\}\!\!\}, 
 
 
 
 for 1 ≤ j ≤ ℓ 1\leq j\leq\ell . They called the resulting partition pre j ⁡ ( λ ) \pre_{j}(\lambda) an elementary symmetric partition since its parts are also the summands appearing in the evaluation e j ​ ( λ 1 , … , λ ℓ ) e_{j}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell}) .



 
 For example, take the partition λ = ( 4 , 3 , 3 , 1 ) \lambda=(4,3,3,1) and j = 3 j=3 . We evaluate e 3 ​ ( λ ) e_{3}(\lambda) as

 

 
 
 e 3 ​ ( 4 , 3 , 3 , 1 ) = 4 ⋅ 3 ⋅ 3 + 4 ⋅ 3 ⋅ 1 + 4 ⋅ 3 ⋅ 1 + 3 ⋅ 3 ⋅ 1 , \displaystyle e_{3}(4,3,3,1)=4\cdot 3\cdot 3+4\cdot 3\cdot 1+4\cdot 3\cdot 1+3\cdot 3\cdot 1, 
 
 
 
 and so,

 

 
 
 pre 3 ⁡ ( 4 , 3 , 3 , 1 ) = ( 36 , 12 , 12 , 9 ) . \displaystyle\pre_{3}(4,3,3,1)=(36,12,12,9). 
 
 
 


 
 The authors of [ 2 ] made the following conjecture regarding the function pre j \pre_{j} .



 
 Conjecture 1.1 (Conjecture 13 in [ 2 ] , Conjecture 1 in [ 1 ] ) . 

 
 For j ≥ 2 j\geq 2 , the function pre j \pre_{j} is injective on the set of partitions of size n n and length ℓ ≥ j \ell\geq j . 





 
 Li [ 8 ] proved the j = 2 j=2 case of Conjecture   1.1 . In fact, they proved a stronger statement where one replaces partitions with weakly decreasing sequences of positive real numbers; real partitions . More recently, Devnani and Eyyunni [ 5 ] disproved the ℓ = j \ell=j case of Conjecture   1.1 , instead conjecturing that the statement is true for ℓ > j \ell>j [ 5 , Conjecture 1.4] . In related work [ 3 ] , the authors considered a class of partitions that is neither a subset nor a superset of partitions of size n n and proved that pre j \pre_{j} is injective on this class. While not answering Conjecture   1.1 , their result provided further evidence towards it.



 
 In this article, we answer the ℓ = 2 ​ j \ell=2j case of Conjecture   1.1 in the negative, also disproving Devnani and Eyyunni’s refined conjecture [ 5 , Conjecture 1.4] . Our main result is the following theorem.



 
 Theorem 1.2 . 

 
 For each j ≥ 3 j\geq 3 , there are infinitely many pairs of distinct partitions λ \lambda and μ \mu of length 2 ​ j 2j such that | λ | = | μ | |\lambda|=|\mu| and pre j ⁡ ( λ ) = pre j ⁡ ( μ ) \pre_{j}(\lambda)=\pre_{j}(\mu) . 





 
 As with the results of Li [ 8 ] , our results may also be extended to real partitions (see Remark   4.10 ); a phenomenon we expect to hold for many results on pre j \pre_{j} . In [ 3 , Section 4] , the authors related elementary symmetric partitions to plethysm, a fundamental operation in the theory of symmetric polynomials. In particular, they pointed out that a consequence of the injectivity of pre 2 \pre_{2} [ 8 ] is that the family of plethysms { e k ​ [ e 2 ] } k ≥ 1 \{e_{k}[e_{2}]\}_{k\geq 1} determine the original elementary symmetric polynomials { e k } k ≥ 1 \{e_{k}\}_{k\geq 1} (in finitely many variables x 1 , … , x ℓ x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell} ). As a corollary of Theorem   1.2 , we now know that this is not true of the family of plethysms { e k ​ [ e j ] } k ≥ 1 \{e_{k}[e_{j}]\}_{k\geq 1} for j ≥ 3 j\geq 3 . We refer the reader to [ 9 ] for more on plethysm.



 
 Our proof of Theorem   1.2 involves defining a map that we call flip j \flip_{j} (see Definition   2.1 ) that preserves elementary symmetric partitions when λ \lambda has length 2 ​ j 2j (see Lemma   2.2 ). We propose the following conjecture regarding the map flip j \flip_{j} .



 
 Conjecture 1.3 . 

 
 Let λ \lambda and μ \mu be distinct partitions of length ℓ \ell . Then for any ℓ > j ≥ 2 \ell>j\geq 2 , pre j ⁡ ( λ ) = pre j ⁡ ( μ ) \pre_{j}(\lambda)=\pre_{j}(\mu) if and only if ℓ = 2 ​ j \ell=2j and flip j ⁡ ( λ ) = μ \flip_{j}(\lambda)=\mu . 





 
 Importantly, Conjecture   1.3 would imply that pre j \pre_{j} is injective on the set of partitions of length j ≤ ℓ j\leq\ell not equal to j j or 2 ​ j 2j . This would be strictly stronger than Conjecture   1.1 on partitions not of length j j or 2 ​ j 2j since we do not impose any size requirement.



 
 Remark 1.4 . 

 
 After completing this article, we learned of similar work by Thomas and Tung [ 10 ] that was done independently. They proved that pre 3 \pre_{3} is not injective on partitions of n n for infinitely many n n [ 10 , Theorem 1.3] . This is equivalent to the j = 3 j=3 case of Theorem   1.2 though our proof methods are distinct. They also proved that prh j \prh_{j} is injective on the set of all partitions [ 10 , Theorem 1.5] which we also proved in Theorem   5.1 . Though there are overlaps in our works, we highlight that their results were derived independently from ours.

 Remark 4.10 .

 5. Further generalizations


 
 We conclude with a discussion of generalizations of this injectivity question. In [ 2 ] , it was suggested that one could evaluate other symmetric polynomials, such as the monomial, complete homogeneous, and Schur polynomials, on any partition λ \lambda and define a partition with the summands as its parts.



 
 For example, if we used the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials h j ​ ( x 1 , … , x ℓ ) h_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}) , we could define prh j ⁡ ( λ ) \prh_{j}(\lambda) as the partition whose multiset of parts is

 

 
 
 { { λ i 1 ⋯ λ i j : 1 ≤ i 1 ≤ ⋯ ≤ i j ≤ ℓ } } \displaystyle\{\!\!\{\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{j}}:1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{j}\leq\ell\}\!\!\} 
 
 
 
 It turns out that the injectivity question is much easier for prh j \prh_{j} than for pre j \pre_{j} .



 
 Theorem 5.1 . 

 
 For j ≥ 1 j\geq 1 , the function prh j \prh_{j} is injective on the set of all partitions. 





 
 Proof.

 
 Let λ \lambda and μ \mu be partitions such that prh j ⁡ ( λ ) = prh j ⁡ ( μ ) \prh_{j}(\lambda)=\prh_{j}(\mu) . By definition ℓ ⁡ ( λ ) = ℓ ⁡ ( μ ) \ell(\lambda)=\ell(\mu) , call this length ℓ \ell . It suffices to show that λ i = μ i \lambda_{i}=\mu_{i} for all 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ 1\leq i\leq\ell . We proceed by induction. For the basecase, observe that, 

 

 
 
 λ 1 = max ⁡ ( prh j ⁡ ( λ ) ) j = max ⁡ ( prh j ⁡ ( μ ) ) j = μ 1 . \displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\sqrt[j]{\max(\prh_{j}(\lambda))}=\sqrt[j]{\max(\prh_{j}(\mu))}=\mu_{1}. 
 
 
 
 Assume that λ i = μ i \lambda_{i}=\mu_{i} for all 1 ≤ i ≤ k < ℓ 1\leq i\leq k<\ell . This implies that prh j ⁡ ( λ − ) = prh j ⁡ ( μ − ) \prh_{j}(\lambda^{-})=\prh_{j}(\mu^{-}) where λ − = ( λ 1 , … , λ k ) \lambda^{-}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}) and μ − = ( μ 1 , … , μ k ) \mu^{-}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}) . Next, notice that 

 

 
 
 λ 1 j − 1 ​ λ k + 1 = max ⁡ ( prh j ⁡ ( λ ) ∖ prh j ⁡ ( λ − ) ) = max ⁡ ( prh j ⁡ ( μ ) ∖ prh j ⁡ ( μ − ) ) = μ j − 1 ​ μ k + 1 . \displaystyle\lambda_{1}^{j-1}\lambda_{k+1}=\max(\prh_{j}(\lambda)\setminus\prh_{j}(\lambda^{-}))=\max(\prh_{j}(\mu)\setminus\prh_{j}(\mu^{-}))=\mu^{j-1}\mu_{k+1}. 
 
 
 
 Since λ 1 = μ 1 \lambda_{1}=\mu_{1} , it follows that λ k + 1 = μ k + 1 \lambda_{k+1}=\mu_{k+1} , completing the induction.
∎ 





 
 It is well known that the elementary and complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials have simple expansions in the monomial symmetric polynomial basis (we refer the reader to [ 9 ] for details):

 

 
 
 e j = m 1 j and h j = ∑ λ : | λ | = j m λ . \displaystyle e_{j}=m_{1^{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad h_{j}=\sum_{\lambda:|\lambda|=j}m_{\lambda}. 
 
 
 
 In this way, both are examples of homogeneous symmetric polynomials in which the coefficient of every monomial is zero or one; we call such symmetric polynomials simple . Since pre j \pre_{j} and prh j \prh_{j} have distinct behavior with respect to injectivity, it may be interesting to determine which simple symmetric polynomials give us injective maps analogous to pre j \pre_{j} and prh j \prh_{j} .



 
 Question 5.2 . 

 
 Let f f be a simple symmetric polynomial. Define prf ⁡ ( λ ) \prf(\lambda) as the partition whose multiset of parts is { { λ α : α ∈ supp ⁡ ( f ) } } \{\!\!\{\lambda^{\alpha}:\alpha\in\supp(f)\}\!\!\} . For which f f is prf \prf injective on the set of partitions? What about on the set of partitions of size n n ?





 
 Lastly, we consider the Schur polynomials s μ ​ ( x 1 , … , x ℓ ) s_{\mu}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}) . They are the generating functions for semistandard young tableaux; fillings of the diagram of μ \mu with numbers { 1 , … , ℓ } \{1,\ldots,\ell\} such that the rows weakly increase and the columns strictly increase. We denote the set of these as SSYT ​ ( μ , ℓ ) \text{SSYT}(\mu,\ell) and refer the reader to [ 9 ] for a more formal definition. Then, we could define prs μ ⁡ ( λ ) \prs_{\mu}(\lambda) as the partition whose multiset of parts is,

 

 
 
 { { λ 1 # ​  of 1’s in  ​ T ⋯ λ ℓ # ​  of  ℓ ’s in  ​ T : T ∈ SSYT ( μ , ℓ ) } } . \displaystyle\{\!\!\{\lambda_{1}^{\#\text{ of 1's in }T}\cdots\lambda_{\ell}^{\#\text{ of $\ell$'s in }T}:T\in\text{SSYT}(\mu,\ell)\}\!\!\}. 
 
 
 
 Another well known fact is that the elementary symmetric polynomials and the complete homogeneous polynomials are special cases of Schur polynomials,

 

 
 
 s ( 1 j ) = e j and s ( j ) = h j . s_{(1^{j})}=e_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{(j)}=h_{j}. 
 
 
 
 See [ 9 ] . Thus, prs 1 j \prs_{1^{j}} is not injective by Theorem   1.2 while prs j \prs_{j} is by Theorem   5.1 .



 
 Question 5.3 . 

 
 For which partitions μ \mu is prs μ \prs_{\mu} injective on the set of partitions? What about on the set of partitions of size n n ?





 
 Partial progress has already been made on Question   5.3 ; Thomas and Tung have proven prs ( 2 , 1 ) \prs_{(2,1)} is injective on the set of partitions of n n [ 10 , Theorem 1.7] using techniques similar to Li in [ 8 ] .
