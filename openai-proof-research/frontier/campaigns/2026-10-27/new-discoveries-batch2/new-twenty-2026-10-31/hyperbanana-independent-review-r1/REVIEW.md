## 2. Complete ordinary proof audit

Let T=C(d+1,2), k=C(b+1,2). All vector spaces and polynomials here are over R, b>=2 and d=2b.

### Bunches and simultaneous nondegeneracy

For C=A or B and every j=1,...,b put D_Cj=det[p_c1-p_uj,...,p_cd-p_uj]. Nonzero D_C1 says C together with u1 is a d-simplex. Choose u1 as origin; the edge equations at u1 and between the other vertices force the interpolating linear map L to satisfy <z,Lz>=0 on a basis and on its pairwise differences. Polarization gives <x,Ly>+<y,Lx>=0 on all vectors, so L is skew. Restoring the origin gives the unique motion t+Omega p. After subtracting that motion, every remaining u_j has velocity w orthogonal to the d columns defining D_Cj; hence w=0. These conditions also imply the bunch spans R^d, which makes the rigid-motion parameters injective.

Fix U once as 0,f1,...,f_(b-1) in a coordinate E-plane. Every D_Cj is a nonzero polynomial in the SAME 2d^2 freely varying A,B coordinates. For an explicit justification, for any chosen j set p_ci=p_uj+g_i, where the g_i form the standard basis of R^d: that factor has value 1. Individual factors need not be witnessed at the same configuration. Their product is nonzero in the polynomial ring, so there is a common real nonvanishing point. Equivalently one can place the d C-points affinely independently in a hyperplane avoiding all the finitely many U-points. This fills the author's compressed phrase about generic simplex/attachment positions. No positivity assumption beyond real nonzero determinants is needed.

### Direct sum and the relative-motion space

For any placement satisfying these determinants and affine independence of U, restriction to A union U determines a unique ambient rigid motion. Subtract it. The velocity on B union U is then t+Omega(p-u1), zero at u1, so t=0; it is zero on every U exactly when Omega E=0. Skewness gives <Omega w,e>=-<w,Omega e>=0, hence W=E-perp is invariant. Restriction and extension by zero give inverse linear maps between these Omega and skew(W), with dim W=d-b+1=b+1. Off-diagonal skew matrix entries give dim skew(W)=k.

The relative parameter-to-velocity map is injective: a relative motion zero on B union U is zero on a spanning bunch and therefore has zero parameters. The ambient motion map is likewise injective on A union U; consequently its intersection with the relative summand is zero. Every unaugmented motion decomposes as their sum by the subtraction construction. Thus its kernel really has dimension T+k, with no omitted stabilizer, quotient ambiguity or duplicate shared-vertex data.

### Exact rank of the b matching equations

Write x_i=proj_W(p_ai-u1), y_i=proj_W(p_bi-u1). For the residual velocity only the B endpoint moves, and the cross-edge row, up to sign, is x_i^T Omega y_i, since y_i^T Omega y_i=0. Ambient rigid motions satisfy all added rows automatically. On the fixed-U slice W has a constant orthonormal basis e0,...,eb. Taking x_i=e0 and y_i=ei for i=1,...,b gives the identity up to sign on the b skew coordinates Omega_0i. Therefore that b by b minor Q is a nonzero polynomial in the same free A,B variables as all D_Cj.

Distinct endpoints matter: each x_i and y_i is freely assignable. Repeated projections x_i do not identify vertices or restrict the other coordinate variables. This specialization can be highly degenerate as a bunch, and is used ONLY to show Q is not the zero polynomial. The nonzero polynomial Q times the product of all D_Cj has a real nonzero evaluation. One proves this fact by induction on variables and the univariate root bound, using that a polynomial ring over a field is a domain. Thus there is one compatible rigid-bunch placement where all b constraints are independent. Nullity there is T+k-b=T+C(b,2).

### The two parameter scopes and maximum-rank transfer

The preceding fixed-U slice establishes existence only. It is NOT open in R^(dn), and cannot by itself bound rank at arbitrary placements.

For the separate upper bound allow EVERY vertex coordinate to vary. Let O be the set where all D_Cj are nonzero and the Gram determinant of (u2-u1,...,ub-u1) is positive. This is open in full R^(dn); it is nonempty by the fixed-U product construction. On every point of O the direct-sum argument yields unaugmented nullity T+k, and adding b rows removes at most b dimensions. Hence rank on O is at most r=dn-T-k+b. Entries of the actual rigidity matrix are linear polynomials in full placement coordinates. Every (r+1)-minor vanishes on O. A real polynomial vanishing on a nonempty open box is identically zero, by the same variable induction. Thus rank is at most r at EVERY placement, even exceptional ones outside O. The compatible fixed-U placement attains r. Every maximum-rank placement therefore has rank r and kernel dimension T+C(b,2), without any further geometric assumptions on that placement. Rank-nullity and k-b=C(b,2) finish the all-b proof. No probabilistic genericity assertion or generic-independence axiom replaces this argument.

There is no substantive mathematical gap in r299's proof after making its implicit determinant choices explicit as above. These are explanatory expansions, not new mathematical hypotheses or a repair of its two-scope argument.

## 3. Complete classical bridge and credit assessment

I do not invoke Tay directly in dimension d on the original overlapping bodies: that would ignore the shared points. Nor does the usual hinge theorem apply directly: an affine (b-1)-flat here has codimension b+1, whereas a hinge in R^d has dimension d-2. The general body-and-joint rigidity problem is not settled merely by naming a counting theorem.

There is, however, a complete reduction to the classical TWO-body bar representation in dimension b. Here it is, including the attachment issue.

1. Replace each bunch by its rigid-motion parameters using the verified simplex and attachment argument. Subtract the first body's motion and impose equality at the common U-points. The preceding bijection leaves exactly skew(W), dim W=b+1. This treats the actual common affine intersection; it does not pretend that the original bodies are disjoint.
2. Under the coordinate pairing skew(W)^* = wedge^2(W), every added row is the decomposable bivector x_i wedge y_i. The matching makes these b pairs independently variable. Projection is surjective for each endpoint, so there is no hidden restriction to a proper family of possible pairs.
3. These are precisely classical projective body-bar row vectors in projective b-space. In an affine chart write x=alpha(1,a), y=beta(1,c), with alpha,beta nonzero. Write Omega in blocks [[0,-t^T],[t,S]], S skew on R^b. Direct multiplication gives x^T Omega y=alpha*beta*((a-c) dot t+a^T S c). The expression in parentheses is (a-c) dot (t+S a), the ordinary infinitesimal bar equation with one body fixed. Row scaling by alpha*beta and the linear bijection Omega <-> (t,S) preserve rank. This proves the representation identification, rather than assuming a projective invariance theorem with unchecked hypotheses.
4. Let D=C(b+1,2). The multigraph with two vertices and b parallel bars is independent in the union of D graphic matroids: put each bar in a separate forest, since b<=D. Tanigawa Theorem 3.1 proves that this is EXACTLY the generic body-bar matroid, including the restriction that rows be decomposable. Thus its generic bar rank is b. Ordinary finite endpoints suffice: the affine-chart conditions are additional nonzero polynomial factors. The locus of homogeneous endpoint pairs is freely parametrized; a nonzero wedge minor can be multiplied by those chart factors. Extra points may be supplied to span artificial reduced bodies if desired; they do not restrict attachment coordinates. No assertion that the projected original simplex is itself the required reduced body is needed.
5. Pull that nonzero rank-b minor back through the independently free endpoint coordinates, and multiply it by the bunch determinants as in Section 2. This enforces the original attachment hypotheses simultaneously. Use the separate full-ambient open-set argument to transfer the rank to every source-generic placement. The result is the exact T+C(b,2) nullity for ALL b>=2.

For this two-vertex case even the classical decomposability proof reduces to the coordinate wedge witness e0 wedge ei. It is exactly the restricted-row spanning mechanism in Tanigawa's proof, rather than a new generic gluing theorem. All remaining steps are the rigid-body restriction calculation and standard determinant specialization. The complete bridge covers endpoint freedom, common-point stabilizers, compatible bunch attachments and maximum-rank semantics; no missing generic-position hypothesis remains.
