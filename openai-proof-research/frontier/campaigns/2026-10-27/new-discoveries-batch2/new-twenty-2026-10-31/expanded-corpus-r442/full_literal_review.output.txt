
ID 337404 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06282
For a graph $G$, let $r,k\geq 1$ be integers, and let $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ be a function.

**Treewidth packing conjecture.** There is a function $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ such that every graph $G$ with

$$
\operatorname{tw}(G)\geq f(r)\,k\log(k+1)
$$

has $k$ vertex-disjoint subgraphs $G_1,\dots,G_k$, each satisfying

$$
\operatorname{tw}(G_i)\geq r.
$$

This would improve the logarithmic exponent in the polynomial Grid Theorem and is suggested as a possible route toward the tight Erdős–Pósa bound for planar-minor models. The source presents it as a natural side conjecture and gives no resolution.

ID 334910 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07189
Let $G$ be a graph, and let $TK_5$ denote a subdivision of $K_5$. A graph is **4-colorable** if its vertices can be colored with four colors so that adjacent vertices receive different colors.

**Hajós' conjecture.** Every graph containing no $TK_5$ is 4-colorable.

The conjecture would generalize the Four Color Theorem. The supplied source does not give a resolution for the $k=4$ case, so the conjecture remains open.

ID 330282 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.03602
Let a **digraph** be a directed graph, and call a set of vertices **undominated** if no vertex has an arc to every vertex in the set. 

**Myers's conjecture.** There is an integer $k$ such that every digraph either has a cycle of length at most $k$ or an undominated set of two vertices.

This conjecture was motivated by the Caccetta–Häggkvist conjecture in extremal graph theory and was proposed as a graph-theoretic route to small-support well-supported Nash equilibria. Myers conjectured that the assertion might hold with $k=3$, but Charbit proved that special case false; the supplied text does not establish whether the full conjecture is resolved.

ID 330490 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04065
An $(r,v;n,m)_d$-array is an array over a $d$-symbol alphabet in which every $n\times m$ window appears exactly once; when the array is a torus, windows wrap around the boundaries. The parameters $r$ and $v$ denote the array dimensions.

**Hurlbert–Mykkeltveit–Parker conjecture.** There exists an $(r,v;n,m)_d$-array whenever

$$
rv=d^{nm}
$$

if and only if the array is a torus, $r>n$ or $r=n=1$, and $v>m$ or $v=m=1$.

These conditions are conjectured to be sufficient in addition to the necessary conditions for de Bruijn arrays discussed in the paper. The source provides no resolution status for this conjecture.

ID 329286 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5681
Let $f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ be a function, let $k\in\mathbb{N}$, and let a digraph have directed tree width at least $f(k)$. A **cylindrical grid of order $k$** is the directed-grid object specified by the directed grid theorem, and a **butterfly minor** is the corresponding directed minor relation. **Reed–Johnson–Robertson–Seymour–Thomas conjecture.** There is a function

$$
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
$$

such that every digraph of directed tree width at least $f(k)$ contains a cylindrical grid of order $k$ as a butterfly minor. This is the proposed directed analogue of the undirected grid theorem and would provide a structural foundation for applying width-based methods to digraphs; the supplied source does not state whether the conjecture has been resolved.

ID 372461 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05221
Let $H$ be a graph with $k$ vertices, and let $R^{\mathrm{ind}}(H)$ denote the minimum number of vertices of a graph $G$ such that every red-blue edge-colouring of $G$ contains an induced monochromatic copy of $H$. **Erdős's conjecture.** There exists a constant $C>0$ such that

$$
R^{\mathrm{ind}}(H)\leqslant 2^{Ck}
$$

for every graph $H$ with $k$ vertices. This conjecture predicts an exponential upper bound for induced Ramsey numbers; it was recently proved by Aragão, Campos, Dahia, Filipe and Marciano.

ID 324823 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2474
Let $G$ be a graph and $t$ a positive integer. A **$t$-biclique covering** is a collection of complete bipartite graphs covering every edge of $G$ at least once and at most $t$ times; let $\mathbf{bp}_t(G)$ be the minimum size of such a covering. Let $\chi(G)$ be the chromatic number. **Generalized Alon–Saks–Seymour conjecture of order $t$.** There exists a polynomial $P_t$ such that for every graph $G$,

$$
\chi(G)\leq P_t(\mathbf{bp}_t(G)).
$$

The parameter generalizes ordinary bipartite packing, since $\mathbf{bp}_1(G)=\mathbf{bp}(G)$. The source poses this as a natural question and gives no resolution in the supplied text.

ID 331993 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02499
Let $G$ be a graph, and let $n$ denote the relevant maximum tangle order. A **canonical sequence of tree-decompositions** is a sequence $(T_i,\mathcal{V}_i)$ for $1\leq i\leq n$, where each $(T_i,\mathcal{V}_i)$ is a tree-decomposition of $G$, and where refinement and torsos are understood in the usual sense. A part is **inessential** at stage $i$ if it does not contain an $(i+1)$-tangle.

**Canonical sequence conjecture.** For every graph $G$ there exists a canonical sequence of tree-decompositions $(T_i,\mathcal{V}_i)$ for $1 \leq i \leq n$ of $G$ such that:

- $(T_i,\mathcal{V}_i)$ distinguishes every $i$-tangle in $G$ for each $i$;
- $(T_n,\mathcal{V}_n)$ distinguishes the set of maximal tangles in $G$;
- $(T_{i+1},\mathcal{V}_{i+1})$ refines $(T_i,\mathcal{V}_i)$ for each $i$;
- the torso of every inessential part in $(T_i,\mathcal{V}_i)$ has branch-width $<i$.

This is presented as the strongest form of a positive answer to the preceding question about bounding the branch-width of inessential parts. The preceding theorem establishes the existence of a canonical nested set distinguishing all tangles, but the claimed simultaneous sequence, refinement property, and branch-width bound remain conjectural.

ID 373385 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01020
Let $G$ be a graph. An **orientation** of $G$ is a digraph with underlying graph $G$ and at most one arc between each pair of vertices. For a digraph $D$, its dichromatic number $vec\chi(D)$ is the minimum number of colours in a vertex colouring whose colour classes induce acyclic subdigraphs; define

$$
\vec\chi(G)=\max_D\vec\chi(D),
$$

where the maximum is over all orientations $D$ of $G$. Let $\chi(G)$ denote the chromatic number of $G$.

**Erdős–Neumann-Lara conjecture.** For every integer $k$ there is an integer $f(k)$ such that, for every graph $G$, $\chi(G)\geq f(k)$ implies $\vec\chi(G)\geq k$.

This asks whether arbitrarily large chromatic number forces an orientation with arbitrarily large dichromatic number. The problem is open: $f(1)=1$ and $f(2)=3$ are possible, but it is unclear whether $f(3)$ exists.

ID 333482 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08827
For a graph $H$, let $H^c$ denote its complement. A graph $G$ is **$\{H,H^c\}$-free** if it has no induced subgraph isomorphic to either $H$ or $H^c$. A clique is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices, and a stable set is a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices.

**Self-complementary Erdős–Hajnal conjecture.** For every graph $H$, there exists a constant $\epsilon(H)>0$ such that every $\{H,H^c\}$-free graph $G$ has either a clique or a stable set of size at least $|V(G)|^{\epsilon(H)}$.

The source presents this as a potentially easier special-case weakening of the full Erdős–Hajnal conjecture, particularly for difficult graphs such as the five-cycle. It remains open in general.

ID 356624 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14762
Let $S(n,k)$ denote the simple graph obtained from the de Bruijn graph by removing orientations and loops and replacing multiple edges between a pair of vertices by a single edge. A graph is **word-representable** if there is a word over its vertex set in which two letters alternate if and only if they are adjacent.

**Non-word-representability conjecture.** All simplified de Bruijn graphs $S(n,k)$ are non-word-representable for $n\geq 4$ and $k\geq 3$.

The paper proves that binary simplified de Bruijn graphs $S(n,2)$ are word-representable for every $n\geq 1$, while $S(2,k)$ and $S(3,k)$ are non-word-representable for $k\geq 3$. The conjecture extends the latter non-word-representability results to all alphabet sizes $n\geq 4$ when $k\geq 3$.

ID 356879 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08105
Let $d$ and $n$ be integers with $d\ge 5$ and $n\ge d+3$, and let $f(n,d):=(d-1)^2[(d-2)!]^{\frac{n}{d+1}}$. A hamiltonian $d$-regular graph of order $n$ is a graph containing a hamiltonian cycle and having every vertex of degree $d$. **Haythorpe's conjecture.** Every hamiltonian $d$-regular graph of order $n$ has at least

$$
f(n,d)
$$

hamiltonian cycles. The paper states that this conjecture is disproved for every $d\ge 5$, extending earlier counterexamples for $d\in\{5,6,7\}$.

ID 367687 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20167
Let $K_{2n}$ be the complete graph on $2n$ vertices. A perfect matching is a spanning 1-regular subgraph, and a Hamiltonian cycle is a cycle containing every vertex. **Perfect 1-factorization conjecture.** For every integer $n\geq 2$, $K_{2n}$ can be decomposed into $2n-1$ perfect matchings such that the union of any two matchings forms a Hamiltonian cycle of $K_{2n}$. Perfect 1-factorizations connect edge decompositions with Hamiltonian cycle structure; the source gives no resolution status.

ID 349676 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04203
Let $G$ be a graph and let $T\subseteq V(G)$ such that there is no $X\subseteq V(G)\setminus T$ for which $d_G(X)$ is an odd natural number. A system $\mathcal{P}$ of edge-disjoint $T$-paths has the required cut property when, for every $t\in T$, one can choose exactly one edge from each path having $t$ as an end-vertex so that the resulting edge set $C$ is a cut separating $t$ from $T-t$.

**Lovász–Cherkassky conjecture.** Under these hypotheses, there exists a system $\mathcal{P}$ of edge-disjoint $T$-paths having the required cut property for every $t\in T$.

The paper proves the corresponding statement when $T$ is countable and conjectures that countability can be omitted. The unrestricted case is presented as open.

ID 346040 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00035
Let $t,k\in\mathbb{N}$. A bipartite graph has **average degree** at least $f(t,k)$ if its average degree is at least the value of a function $f:\mathbb{N}^2\to\mathbb{N}$.

**Thomassen's conjecture.** There is a function $f:\mathbb{N}^2\rightarrow\mathbb{N}$ such that, for all $t,k\in\mathbb{N}$, every bipartite graph of average degree at least $f(t,k)$ has a subgraph of average degree at least $t$ and girth at least $k$.

This is a conjecture about finding arbitrarily dense subgraphs of arbitrarily large prescribed girth in bipartite graphs. The paper notes that the case of girth $6$ is currently the best known.

ID 342128 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05877
Let $M$ be a matroid. A collection of independent sets **covers** $M$ if every element of $M$ belongs to at least one set in the collection. **Finiteness conjecture.** For every positive integer $k$, there are finitely many minor-minimal simple matroids that cannot be covered by $k$ independent sets. The case $k=2$ is known: the ten minor-minimal simple matroids are explicitly determined, but the assertion for all positive integers remains open.

ID 344275 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12109
Let $X$ be a proper hereditary class of graphs, meaning that $X$ is not the class of all graphs. For natural numbers $p$ and $q$, let $R_X(p,q)$ be the least integer $n$ such that every graph in $X$ with at least $n$ vertices has either a clique of size $p$ or an independent set of size $q$.

**Erdős–Hajnal conjecture.** There are constants $A$ and $k$ such that

$$
R_X(p,q)\leq A(p+q)^k
$$

for every $p,q\in\mathbb{N}$; equivalently, Ramsey numbers grow at most polynomially in $X$.

This is the paper’s formulation of the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture. The supplied text gives no resolution status.

ID 381465 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07648
Let $G$ be a finite group. For a sequence over $G$, let ${\mathsf d}(G)$ denote the maximal length of a zero-sum free sequence, and let ${\mathsf E}_m(G)$ denote the least integer $n$ such that every sequence over $G$ of length at least $n$ contains $m$ disjoint subsequences, each of length $|G|$ and each having sum zero in $G$.

**Generalized Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv conjecture.** For every finite group $G$,

$$
{\mathsf E}_m(G)={\mathsf d}(G)+m|G|.
$$

The equality is known for finite Abelian groups, nilpotent groups, groups of the form $C_m\ltimes_\varphi C_{mn}$, dihedral and dicyclic groups, and all non-Abelian groups of order $pq$ with $p$ and $q$ prime. The conjecture proposes that it holds for every finite group.

ID 335233 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.01607
Let $k$ be a positive integer. Let $T$ be a tournament and $G$ a graph on the same vertex set, and write $N_T^+(v)$ for the out-neighborhood of a vertex $v$ in $T$. The chromatic number of $G$ is denoted by $\chi(G)$.

**Scott–Seymour conjecture.** For every $k$, there exists $K$ such that, whenever $G$ has chromatic number at least $K$, there is a vertex $v$ such that the subgraph of $G$ induced by $N_T^+(v)$ has chromatic number at least $k$.

This conjecture formalizes the proposed principle that a sufficiently high-chromatic graph must retain substantial chromatic complexity in some tournament out-neighborhood. The supplied text attributes a very similar conjecture to Alex Scott and Paul Seymour and does not state a resolution.

ID 335906 SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.00896
Let $k$ be a positive integer. A **$k$-edge-coloured finite tournament** is a finite tournament whose edges are assigned one of $k$ colours, and a set $S\subseteq V(T)$ is reachable from a vertex by a monochromatic path if some monochromatic directed path from that vertex ends in a vertex of $S$. **Erdős's conjecture.** For every positive integer $k$ there is a least positive integer $f(k)$ such that every $k$-edge-coloured finite tournament admits a subset $S\subseteq V(T)$ of size at most $f(k)$ such that $S$ is reachable from every vertex by a monochromatic path. It is known that $f(1)=f(2)=1$ and that $f(3)\geq 3$, but no constant upper bound for $f(3)$ is known; Erdős conjectured that $f(3)=3$.
