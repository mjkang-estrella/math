## Exact bridge and its provenance

Put A = (e_i,e_j), the vertex configuration of Δ_(p−1) × Δ_(q−1), and let h_ij = a_ij. Use lower subdivisions. The polyhedron

Q_h = {(y,z) modulo (1_p,−1_q): y_i + z_j ≤ h_ij}

is precisely Develin–Sturmfels' polyhedron P_V, with V = h. Their *Tropical convexity*, [arXiv:math/0308254](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0308254), Theorem 1 and Section 4, especially Lemma 22 and the paragraph following its proof, gives the face/subdivision duality. A vertex of Q_h is dual to a maximal, full-dimensional subdivision cell. Its labelled vertex set is {(i,j): y_i+z_j=h_ij}. The bounded-face complex is the tropical complex; its vertices are pseudovertices, not necessarily the original tropical generators. Herrmann–Joswig–Speyer, [arXiv:1112.1278](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1278), Section 3, states the pseudovertex/maximal-cell correspondence and defines tropical rigidity as a secondary-fan ray, equivalently a coarsest nontrivial regular subdivision. Their Theorem 7 and Corollary 14 supply the Stiefel lift to a hypersimplex Dressian. Those are distinct bridges: cell counts in the hypersimplex cannot replace cell counts in the vertex figure.

Ardila–Develin, [arXiv:0706.2920](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.2920), Section 5, explicitly gives the realizable tropical-oriented-matroid/regular-subdivision correspondence. Its extension to arbitrary subdivisions is formulated there as a conjecture and partially proved in Section 6. No unproved nonrealizable correspondence is needed here. Santos, [arXiv:math/0312069](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0312069), explains the Cayley trick and mixed subdivisions; this also does not identify marginal-cone facets by itself.

Here is the additional **elementary marginal-cone deduction**, reproduced rather than attributed to a nonexistent theorem in those papers. The dual marginal cone, modulo the kernel of evaluation on its columns, is

K_r = {(h,b,c): h_ij+b_ik+c_jk ≥ 0 for all i,j,k} / L,

where L = {all evaluations zero}, of dimension p+q+r−1. A facet of P corresponds to an extreme ray of K_r. For any height h, take one layer for each of the m maximal subdivision cells: choose its supporting potentials (y^k,z^k), and set b_ik = −y_i^k, c_jk = −z_j^k. Then the zero set of layer k is exactly that labelled maximal cell.

To verify the facet bridge, impose the tight equations on all m layers. Each maximal cell has a connected spanning tight graph in K_(p,q), so its potentials are unique up to one scalar gauge. Projection of the solution space to h gives the linear span of the secondary cone: equalities enforce that each cell is affine, and the strict inequalities at h give relative interior. Thus the equality-space dimension is (p+q−1)+m+d, where d is the dimension of that secondary cone modulo affine heights. Quotienting L leaves dimension d. Therefore a nontrivial **coarsest regular subdivision**, with all its maximal cells represented, produces a facet of P^{p,q,m}. This is a sufficient construction; it is not a claim that arbitrary subdivisions produce facets or that every marginal-cone facet is in a simple bijection with an unlabelled subdivision. Coordinate-margin facets and repeated layers require separate bookkeeping.

In particular the literature's published duality plus standard secondary-cone extremality makes conversion of an explicit old coarsest subdivision into a marginal-cone facet short and routine. I did not locate a published statement explicitly naming this statistical conjecture in the tropical papers inspected. Absence of that name would not rescue campaign credit if older explicit data already supplies the example.

For collapsing the third variable, layers being identified must have **equal labelled zero sets**, not merely isomorphic cells. At p=q=4, r=5, any map to four states identifies two layers. Different zero sets then obstruct the pullback of any target set. Global row/column permutations act simultaneously on all cells; independent relabelling of each cell is inadmissible. Conversely duplicate supporting cells can be represented by duplicate potentials and merged. Thus the relevant obstruction is more than q distinct maximal cells in a coarsest subdivision, not more than q tropical generators, top-dimensional tropical regions, or hypersimplex cells. Rinaldo's thesis already states the distinct 0/* slice-pattern criterion in the discussion after Conjecture 4.3.5.
