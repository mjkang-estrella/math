To achieve this, we construct a bijection between finitary colored upho posets and atomic, left-cancellative, invertible-free monoids, which restricts to a correspondence between $\mathbb{N}$ -graded colored upho posets and left-cancellative homogeneous monoids. Furthermore, we introduce semi-upho posets and develop a convolution operation on colored upho posets with colored semi-upho posets within this monoid-theoretic framework. 1 Introduction A poset is called upper homogeneous, abbreviated as upho, if each principal order filter is isomorphic to the poset itself. Upho posets were introduced by Richard Stanley during his research on the enumeration properties of Stern’s triangle and its poset [14, 15]. The study of Stern’s poset, particularly regarding enumeration problems, has been a focal point of numerous research efforts [11, 17]. Upho posets, as a generalization of Stern’s poset, preserve the attribute of self-similarity, and hence exhibit many intriguing structural and enumeration properties. For example, in [4], Gao et al. give a concise characterization of $\mathbb{N}$ -graded planar upho posets using their rank-generating functions; in [6], Hopkins proves that the characteristic generating functions of upho posets are the inverse of their rank-generating functions. Moreover, the applications of upho posets span several domains, including lattice theory [6, 7, 5], commutative algebra [4, Conjecture 1.1], Coxeter groups [7, Corollaries 4.10 and 4.15], and finite geometry [7, Theorem 1.3]. For simplicity, we say a formal power series is an upho function if it is the rank-generating function of some upho poset. A fundamental problem is: Question 1.1. Is there a criterion to determine whether a formal power series is an upho function? In [4], Gao et al. prove that there are uncountably many different upho functions, and a straightforward corollary is that almost all upho functions are not rational functions. Hence, the complete characterization of upho functions is anticipated to be challenging. In this paper, we characterize the rank-generating functions of all Schur-positive upho posets, which are upho posets with a Schur-positive Ehrenborg quasi-symmetric function, thereby resolving the main conjecture proposed by Gao et al. in [4]. Theorem 1.2 ([4, Conjecture 3.3]). A formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is the rank-generating function of a Schur-positive upho poset if and only if $f(x)$ is totally positive. In Section 3, we introduce the notion of colored upho posets and establish a fundamental correspondence, detailed later, which serves as the primary tool for proving all results in this paper. Theorem 1.3. There exists a canonical bijection between finitary colored upho posets and atomic, left-cancellative, invertible-free monoids. Moreover, this bijection maps $\mathbb{N}$ -graded colored upho posets to left-cancellative homogeneous monoids and maps finite-type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded colored upho posets to finitely generated left-cancellative homogeneous monoids. This correspondence is crucial for the study of both upho posets and left-cancellative monoids. On one hand, it enables concrete calculations on upho posets using monoids from an algebraic perspective. On the other hand, it provides combinatorial insight into problems such as enumerating elements in left-cancellative monoids. We use regular upho posets to denote upho posets that admit a compatible coloring (in fact, we conjecture that all finitary upho posets admit such a coloring). Furthermore, we refer to their rank-generating functions as regular upho functions. In Section 4, we explore the properties of semi-upho posets, a generalization of upho posets that exhibit partial self-similarity. We build a framework to characterize all regular semi-upho functions, and prove that all log-concave formal power series are regular semi-upho functions. Theorem 1.4. Any log-concave formal power series $g(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is a regular semi-upho function. In Section 5, we define the $\mathbf{x}$ -convolution of a colored upho poset with a semi-upho spanning tree of a colored semi-upho poset, and shows that the $\mathbf{x}$ -convolution remains a colored upho poset using calculations based on monoids. A direct corollary is that multiplying a regular upho function by a regular semi-upho function yields another regular upho function. Theorem 1.5. Let $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ be a regular upho function, and $g(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ be a regular semi-upho function, then $f(x)g(x)$ is a regular upho function. This result serves as the main tool in the proof of Theorem 1.2. In Section 6, we explore the relationship between upho functions and totally positive power series. Definition 1.6 ([9]). A formal power series $\sumop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}$ is totally positive if all finite minors of the infinite Toeplitz matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&0&0&0&\cdots\\ a_{1}&a_{0}&0&0&\cdots\\ a_{2}&a_{1}&a_{0}&0&\cdots\\ \vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&\vbox{\kern 6.0pt\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}\hbox{$.$}}&\mathinner{\mkern 1.0mu\raise 7.0pt\vbox{\kern 7.0pt\hbox{$.$}}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 4.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 2.0mu\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$.$}\mkern 1.0mu}\end{pmatrix}$ are nonnegative. Our working definition of totally positive formal power series follows from Theorem 1.7. Theorem 1.7 ([1, 10]). A formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is totally positive if and only if $f(x)$ can be expressed in the form $\frac{g(x)}{h(x)}$ , where $g(x),h(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}[x]$ are polynomials with all complex roots of $g(x)$ real and negative, and all complex roots of $h(x)$ real and positive. By employing Theorem 1.3, Theorem 1.4, Theorem 1.5, and resolving an exceptional case by a miracle in linear algebra, we prove the following theorem. Theorem 1.8. Any totally positive formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is a regular upho function. And finally, we show that Theorem 1.2 is a corollary of Theorem 1.8. The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 provides necessary background on upho posets and introduces the notion of semi-upho posets. Section 3 through Section 6 are dedicated to the exposition and proofs of the aforementioned results. In Section 7, we propose further conjectures on upho posets and related structures and build a framework for characterizing all upho functions. 2 Preliminaries In this paper, we employ the standard terminology used in order theory. For a more detailed exposition, readers are referred to [13, Chapter 3]. 2.1 Upho Posets In this subsection, we introduce several definitions related to upho posets, accompanied by illustrative examples. Definition 2.1. A poset $P$ is upper homogeneous, abbreviated as upho, if for any $s\in P$ we have $V_{P,s}\cong P$ , where $V_{P,s}\coloneqq\{p\in P\mid s\leq_{P}p\}$ is the principal order filter generated by $s$ . Note that each principal order filter has a unique minimal element, so every upho poset $P$ has a unique minimal element, denoted $\hat{0}_{P}$ . We abbreviate $V_{P,s}$ to $V_{s}$ and $\hat{0}_{P}$ to $\hat{0}$ when the poset $P$ referred to is clear. A poset $P$ is said to be $\mathbb{N}$ -graded if $P$ can be written as a disjoint union $P=P_{0}\sqcup P_{1}\sqcup P_{2}\sqcup\dots$ such that every saturated chain has the form $p_{0}\lessdot_{P}p_{1}\lessdot_{P}p_{2}\lessdot_{P}\cdots$ , where $p_{i}\in P_{i}$ for all $i\in\mathbb{N}$ . The rank function $\rho:P\rightarrow\mathbb{N}$ of $P$ is defined by $\rho(p)=i$ for all $p\in P_{i}$ . We refer to $P_{i}$ as the $i$ -th layer of $P$ . An $\mathbb{N}$ -graded poset $P$ is said to be of finite type if $\left|P_{i}\right|$ is finite for all $i\in\mathbb{N}$ . The rank-generating function of a finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded poset $P$ is defined to be $F_{P}(x)\coloneqq\sumop\displaylimits_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|P_{k}\right|x^{k}$ . Definition 2.2. A formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}[[x]]$ is an upho function if it is the rank-generating function of a finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho poset. Definition 2.3 ([3] ). The Ehrenborg quasi-symmetric function of a finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded poset $P$ is defined to be $E_{P}\coloneqq\sumop\displaylimits_{n\geq 0}E_{P,n}$ , where $E_{P,0}=1$ , and $E_{P,n}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots\right)\coloneqq\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{0}=t_{0}\leq_{P}t_{1}\leq_{P}\cdots\leq_{P}t_{k-1}<_{P}t_{k}\\ \rho\left(t_{k}\right)=n\end{subarray}}x_{1}^{\rho(t_{1})-\rho(t_{0})}x_{2}^{\rho(t_{2})-\rho(t_{1})}\cdots x_{k}^{\rho(t_{k})-\rho(t_{k-1})},\ n\geq 1.$ Definition 2.4 ([4]). An finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho poset is a Schur-positive upho poset if its Ehrenborg quasi-symmetric function is a Schur-positive symmetric function. We state some fundamental properties of upho functions. Lemma 2.5 ([4, Lemma 2.2]). For a finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho poset $P$ , $E_{P}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots)=F_{P}(x_{1})F_{P}(x_{2})\cdots$ , hence $E_{p}$ is a symmetric function. Recall that the product of two posets $P$ and $Q$ , denoted $P\times Q$ , is a poset whose elements are given by the Cartesian product of $P$ and $Q$ , with the order defined by $(a,b)\leq(c,d)$ iff $a\leq_{P}c$ and $b\leq_{Q}d$ . Lemma 2.6 ([4, Lemma 2.3]). Let $P$ and $Q$ be finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho posets. Then $P\times Q$ is a finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho poset. Furthermore, $F_{P\times Q}=F_{P}F_{Q}$ , and $E_{P\times Q}=E_{P}E_{Q}$ . We define the following two finiteness conditions. It is worth noting that the terminology is not standard, and “finitary” here does not align with its use in “finitary distributive lattice.” Definition 2.7. A poset $P$ is Noetherian if its principal order filters satisfy ascending chain condition, that is, for any element $s\in P$ , there is no infinite strictly ascending chain $V_{s}\subseteq V_{s_{1}}\subseteq V_{s_{2}}\subseteq\cdots$ (or equivalently, $s>_{P}s_{1}>_{P}s_{2}>_{P}\cdots$ ). Definition 2.8. The height of an element $s$ in a poset $P$ is the minimal length of saturated chains in $P$ with $s$ as its maximum. A poset $P$ is finitary if every element in $P$ has finite height. All $\mathbb{N}$ -graded posets are finitary, and all finitary posets are Noetherian. Moreover, given a Noetherian upho poset $P$ , a finitary upho poset $P^{\prime}\subseteq P$ can be obtained by selecting all elements of finite height in $P$ , with $P^{\prime}$ inheriting the order of $P$ . Notation 2.9. In a poset $P$ , we denote the set of edges of $P$ by $\mathcal{E}_{P}\coloneqq\{(r,s)\mid r,s\in P,r\lessdot s\}$ , which corresponds to the edges in the Hasse diagram of $P$ if $P$ is finitary. If a poset $P$ has a unique minimum $\hat{0}$ , we denote the set of atoms of $P$ by $\mathcal{A}_{P}\coloneqq\{s\in P\mid\hat{0}\lessdot s\}$ . It can be easily verified that in a Noetherian poset, each saturated chain includes exactly one atom. However, both $\mathcal{A}_{P}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{P}$ can be empty if $P$ is non-Noetherian. See Example 2.10. We list below some examples of upho posets and upho functions. Example 2.10. Nonnegative real numbers $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ with usual order is a non-Noetherian upho poset, and there are no atoms in $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ . Let $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{N}$ be the poset product of $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ and $\mathbb{N}$ , both with usual order. Then $\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{N}$ is also a non-Noetherian upho poset, and $(0,1)$ is its only atom. Example 2.11. The poset $\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$ with lexicographic order forms a Noetherian upho poset which is not finitary. For any $(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$ , the isomorphism $\tau:V_{(m,n)}\cong\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}$ is given by $\tau(m,n+s)=(0,s),\tau(m+t,s)=(t,s)$ for all $s\in\mathbb{N},t\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}$ . Example 2.12. The upho poset $P_{M}$ is defined as follows. The elements in $P_{M}$ are those in the monoid $M=\langle x_{1},x_{2}\mid x_{1}^{3}=x_{2}x_{1}\rangle$ . The partial order of $P_{M}$ is defined by left-divisibility in $M$ , that is, $a\leq_{P_{M}}b$ if and only if there exists $c\in M$ such that $ac=b$ in $M$ . It can be shown that $P_{M}$ is a finitary upho poset but not an $\mathbb{N}$ -graded one. Example 2.13. The upho posets $P_{M_{1}}$ and $P_{M_{2}}$ are defined as follows. The upho poset $P_{M_{1}}$ consists of elements in the free monoid $M_{1}$ generated by $[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ , with a partial order defined by left-divisibility. Similarly, $P_{M_{2}}$ consists of elements in the free commutative monoid $M_{2}$ generated by $[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ , with its partial order also defined by left-divisibility. Both $P_{M_{1}}$ and $P_{M_{2}}$ are $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho posets that are not of finite type. The $d$ -th layer of $P_{M_{1}}$ and $P_{M_{2}}$ can be viewed as a $d$ -dimensional cube and a $d$ -dimensional simplex, respectively. Example 2.14. Figure 1 shows the Hasse diagrams of several $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho posets of finite type: $\mathbb{N}$ (with usual order), the full binary tree, Stern’s poset, and the bowtie poset, from left to right. On the other hand, it illustrates potential connections between upper homogeneity and linear recurrence, inspiring our proof of Proposition 6.3. 2.2 Semi-Upho Posets In this subsection, we introduce semi-upho posets, a generalization of upho posets exhibiting partial self-similarity. The primary motivation for defining such posets is to identify suitable formal power series $g(x)$ such that $f(x)g(x)$ is an upho function for any upho function $f(x)$ . As shown in Lemma 2.6, upho functions serve as appropriate choices for $g(x)$ . Furthermore, in Theorem 1.5, we extend this result to the rank-generating functions of semi-upho posets, with a mild restriction termed “regular.” Definition 2.16. Given posets $P^{\prime}$ and $P$ with unique minima $\hat{0}_{P^{\prime}}$ and $\hat{0}_{P}$ respectively, an injective mapping $\eta:P^{\prime}\hookrightarrow P$ is an induced saturated order embedding, abbreviated as isoembedding, if $\eta(\hat{0}_{P^{\prime}})=\hat{0}_{P}$ , and furthermore, for any chain $C$ in $P^{\prime}$ with given maximum $a$ and minimum $b$ , $C$ is a saturated chain with given maximum $a$ and minimum $b$ if and only if $\eta(C)$ is a saturated chain with maximum $\eta(a)$ and minimum $\eta(b)$ . For finitary posets, an isoembedding is an embedding that preserve the covering relation $\lessdot$ . Definition 2.17. A poset $S$ is a semi-upho poset if, for any $s\in S$ , there exists an isoembedding $V_{s}\hookrightarrow S$ . A formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}[[x]]$ is a semi-upho function if it is the rank-generating function of a finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded semi-upho poset. Upho posets form a subclass of semi-upho posets as isomorphisms are isoembeddings. Moreover, intuitively, a semi-upho poset can be thought of as an upho poset with some parts cut off. Definition 2.18. A finitary semi-upho poset $S$ is tree-like if for any two elements $s,t\in S$ , there does not exist an element $u\in S$ such that $s<_{S}u$ and $t<_{S}u$ . The Hasse diagram of a tree-like semi-upho poset is a tree. Example 2.19. Figure 3 is an example of a tree-like semi-upho posets. The red part is a principal order filter that can be isoembedded into the poset itself. Figure 3: A tree-like semi-upho poset. 2.3 Monoids In this paper, our main focus is on the upho posets derived from left-cancellative homogeneous monoids. Accordingly, we introduce some definitions and properties related to these monoids. For a more detailed exposition, readers are referred to [8]. It is worth noting that certain terms in this paper, such as “homogeneous” and “invertible-free,” are not yet standard terminology. A monoid $M$ is a set equipped with an associative binary operation, called multiplication, and an identity element $e$ such that $ex=x=xe$ for any $x\in M$ . Definition 2.20. A free monoid $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X})$ generated by a set $\mathbf{X}$ is defined as follows: • Elements in $\mathbf{X}$ are called letters. A finite sequence of letters $W=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}$ is called a word, with length denoted by $\ell(W)\coloneqq n$ . There exists a unique word of length $0$ , denoted by $e$ . • $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X})$ is the set of all words formed from letters in $\mathbf{X}$ , with multiplication of two words $W_{1}$ and $W_{2}$ defined by their concatenation. Let $\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{X})$ denote the set of all words of length $k$ in $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X})$ . To facilitate working with elements in monoids, we define the $k$ -lexicographic order $<_{k}$ on $\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{X})$ as follows. Definition 2.21. Given an ordered set $(\mathbf{X},<)$ , the $k$ -lexicographic order $<_{k}$ on $\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{X})$ is the total order defined by: for two distinct words $W_{1}=a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{k}$ and $W_{2}=b_{1}b_{2}\ldots b_{k}$ in $\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{X})$ , we have $W_{1}<_{k}W_{2}$ if and only if $a_{i}<b_{i}$ at the first position $i$ (counting from the left) where $W_{1}$ and $W_{2}$ differ. A relation on a set $S$ is a subset $R\subseteq S\times S$ . It is an equivalence if it is reflexive $(x,x)\in R$ , symmetric $(x,y)\in R\Rightarrow(y,x)\in R$ , and transitive $(x,y),(y,z)\in R\Rightarrow(x,z)\in R$ . A congruence on a monoid $M$ is an equivalence $R$ defined on $M$ such that $(xay,xby)\in R$ for any $(a,b)\in R$ and $x,y\in M$ . Given a relation $R$ on a monoid $M$ , we denote by $R^{c}$ the smallest congruence containing $R$ . Given a monoid $M$ , if $M\cong\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X}_{M})/{R_{M}^{c}}$ for a relation $R_{M}$ on a set $\mathbf{X}_{M}$ , then $M=\langle\mathbf{X}_{M}\mid R_{M}\rangle$ is called a presentation of $M$ . We call $\mathbf{X}_{M}$ the generating set and elements of $R_{M}$ the defining relations of $M$ . If $\mathbf{X}_{M}=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}$ and $R_{M}=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{m},b_{m})\}$ are finite, then $M$ is called finitely presented and written as $M=\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\mid a_{1}=b_{1},\dots,a_{m}=b_{m}\rangle$ . For any element $W\in M=\langle\mathbf{X}_{M}\mid R_{M}\rangle$ , if $W=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}$ with each $a_{i}\in\mathbf{X}_{M}$ , then $a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}$ is called a word representation of $W$ . Moreover, if two word representations $W_{1}$ and $W_{2}$ correspond to the same element $W$ in $M$ , we write $W_{1}=W_{2}=W$ . If, in addition, these word representations are identical in $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X}_{M})$ , meaning they use the same letters in the same order, we write $W_{1}\equiv W_{2}$ . A zero element of $M$ , denoted $0\in M$ , satisfies $0x=x0=0$ for any $x\in M$ . An element $x\in M$ is left-invertible if there exists $y\in M$ such that $yx=e$ , and right-invertible if there exists $y\in M$ such that $xy=e$ . An element $x\in M$ is invertible if it is both left- and right-invertible. Definition 2.22. A monoid $M$ is invertible-free if it has no left-invertible or right-invertible elements other than $e$ ; equivalently, $xy=e$ implies $x=y=e$ for any $x,y\in M$ . An element of $M$ is irreducible if it is not invertible and is not the product of two non-invertible elements. The set of all irreducible elements in $M$ is denoted $\mathcal{I}_{M}$ . A monoid is atomic if all its elements can be written as a finite product of irreducible elements. Definition 2.23. An atomic monoid $M$ is homogeneous if, for any $W\in M$ with $W=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{m}$ , where $a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathcal{I}_{M}$ , we have $n=m$ . We define the length of $W$ , denoted $\ell(W)$ , to be $n$ and let $\mathbf{W}^{M}_{n}$ be the set of all distinct elements of length $n$ . Definition 3.1. A colored upho poset $\tilde{P}$ consists of the data $(P,\operatorname{col}_{P})$ : the poset $P$ is an upho poset, and the color mapping $\operatorname{col}_{P}:\mathcal{E}_{P}\to\mathcal{A}_{P}$ satisfies the following conditions: • For any $t\in\mathcal{A}_{P}$ , $\operatorname{col}_{P}(\hat{0},t)=t$ ; • For any $s\in P$ , there exists an isomorphism $\phi_{s}:V_{s}\xrightarrow{\sim}P$ such that $\operatorname{col}_{P}(u,v)=\operatorname{col}_{P}(\phi_{s}(u),\phi_{s}(v))$ for all $(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{V_{s}}$ . It can be shown that such $\phi_{s}$ is unique for a given $s\in P$ . Finitary colored upho posets can be conceptualized as structures in which each edge extending upward from the same vertex in their Hasse diagrams is assigned a distinct color. Moreover, there exists a unique isomorphism between each principal order filter and the poset itself, which maps edges to identically colored ones. Similarly, we introduce colored semi-upho posets. Definition 3.2. A colored semi-upho poset $\tilde{S}$ consists of the data $(S,\operatorname{col}_{S})$ : the poset $S$ is a semi-upho poset, and the color mapping $\operatorname{col}_{S}:\mathcal{E}_{S}\to\mathcal{A}_{S}$ satisfies the following conditions: • For any $t\in\mathcal{A}_{S}$ , $\operatorname{col}_{S}(\hat{0},t)=t$ ; • For any $s\in S$ , there exists an isoembedding $\psi_{s}:V_{s}\hookrightarrow S$ such that $\operatorname{col}_{S}(u,v)=\operatorname{col}_{S}(\psi_{s}(u),\psi_{s}(v))$ for all $(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{V_{s}}$ . It can be shown that such $\psi_{s}$ is also unique for a given $s\in S$ . 3.2 Correspondence with Monoids In this subsection, we build the bijection between finitary colored upho posets and atomic LCIF monoids explicitly. We define a mapping $\mathcal{M}$ which maps finitary colored upho posets to LCIF monoids by the following rule. For a given finitary colored upho poset $\tilde{P}=(P,\operatorname{col}_{P})$ , the elements of $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ are the elements of $P$ . For any $s,t\in P$ , we define the multiplication $st$ by $st\coloneqq\phi_{s}^{-1}(t)$ . Lemma 3.3. The monoid $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ is a well-defined atomic LCIF monoid, and $\mathcal{A_{P}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})}$ . Proof. In the colored upho poset $P$ , since both $\phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{-1}$ and $\phi_{s_{1}}^{-1}\circ\phi_{s_{2}}^{-1}$ induces an isomorphism $P\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}$ , the uniqueness of such isomorphism in colored upho poset implies that $\phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{-1}=\phi_{s_{1}}^{-1}\circ\phi_{s_{2}}^{-1}$ . Therefore, the monoid $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ is well-defined since the identity element $e\in\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ corresponds to $\hat{0}\in P$ and $(s_{1}s_{2})t=\phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{-1}(t)=\phi_{s_{1}}^{-1}(\phi_{s_{2}}^{-1}(t))=s_{1}(s_{2}t)$ for any $s_{1},s_{2},t\in\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ . Suppose $st_{1}=st_{2}$ in $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ , then $\phi_{s}^{-1}(t_{1})=\phi_{s}^{-1}(t_{2})$ in $P$ . Since $\phi_{s}^{-1}$ is an isomorphism, it follows that $t_{1}=t_{2}$ , and hence $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ is left-cancellative. Suppose $st=e$ in $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ , then $\phi_{s}^{-1}(t)=e$ , which implies $s=e$ . Therefore, $st=e$ implies $s=t=e$ , and hence $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ is invertible-free. The irreducible elements of $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ are exactly $\mathcal{A_{P}}$ . Note that any nontrivial element $t\in\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ lie in the order filter generated by some atom $s$ . If $t\notin\mathcal{A_{P}}$ , then $t=\phi_{s}^{-1}(t_{1})$ for some $t_{1}\neq e$ , so $t=st_{1}\notin\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})}$ . This implies $\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})}\subseteq\mathcal{A_{P}}$ , and the converse inclusion is clear by definition. Finally, since $P$ is finitary, any element in $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ can be written as a finite product of elements in $\mathcal{A_{P}}=\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})}$ , and therefore, $\mathcal{M}(\tilde{P})$ is atomic. ∎ Conversely, we define a mapping $\mathcal{\tilde{P}}=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{C})$ which maps atomic LCIF monoids to finitary colored upho posets by the following rule. Given a formal power series $g(x)=1+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , the greedy $0$ -monoid series $\{M_{k}^{0,g}\}_{k\geq 1}$ of $g(x)$ is a sequence of monoids defined recursively as follows: • Define $M_{1}^{0,g}\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{X}\cup\{0\}\mid 0x_{i}=x_{i}0=0\text{ for any }x_{i}\rangle$ , where the ordered set $\mathbf{X}\coloneqq\{x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{b_{1}}\}$ . • For $M_{k}^{0,g}=\langle\mathbf{X}\cup\{0\}\mid R_{k}\rangle$ , if the inequality $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{0,g}}|\geq b_{k+1}$ holds, then define $M_{k+1}^{0,g}\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{X}\cup\{0\}\mid R_{k+1}\rangle$ , where $R_{k+1}$ is obtained by extending $R_{k}$ with additional $0$ -defining relations $W_{i}=0$ for the largest $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{0,g}}|-b_{k+1}$ elements $W_{i}$ in $\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{0,g}}$ under the $(k+1)$ -lexicographic order on $\mathbf{F}_{k+1}(\mathbf{X})$ . We call $M_{k}^{0,g}$ the $k$ -th greedy $0$ -monoid of $g(x)$ . We omit the superscript $g$ when the power series is clear from context. Notably, not all $g(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ admit an infinite greedy $0$ -monoid series, and for any $k\geq 1$ , $M_{k+1}^{0}$ is defined if and only if the $k$ -th greedy $0$ -monoid $M_{k}^{0}$ is defined and the inequality $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{0}}|\geq b_{k+1}$ holds. It can be easily verified that, given $g(x)=1+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , the rank-generating function of the semi-upho poset $\mathcal{\tilde{S}}(M_{k}^{0})$ corresponding to $M_{k}^{0}$ (see Corollary 3.10) has the initial $k+1$ coefficients $1,b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k}$ . Thus, if $g(x)$ admits an infinite greedy $0$ -monoid series, we define $M^{0}\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{X}\cup\{0\}\mid\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i\geq 1}R_{i}\rangle$ , where $R_{i}$ is the set of defining relations of $M_{i}^{0}$ . Consequently, the rank-generating function of $\mathcal{\tilde{S}}(M^{0})$ is exactly $g(x)$ , and hence by Corollary 3.10, $g(x)$ is the rank-generating function of a tree-like semi-upho poset, which is also a regular semi-upho function by Proposition 4.1. This result can be formulated as the following proposition. Proposition 4.7. Given a formal power series $g(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , if $g(x)$ admits an infinite greedy $0$ -monoid series $\{M_{k}^{0}\}_{k\geq 1}$ , then $g(x)$ is a regular semi-upho function. An equivalent yet more practical version of Proposition 4.7 is as follows. Proposition 4.8. Given a formal power series $g(x)=1+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , if for any integer $k\geq 1$ , the $k$ -th greedy $0$ -monoid $M_{k}^{0}$ is defined implies the inequality $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{0}}|\geq b_{k+1}$ holds, then $g(x)$ is a regular semi-upho function. Moreover, we conjecture that Proposition 4.7 and Proposition 4.8 characterize all regular semi-upho functions. Conjecture 4.9. A formal power series $g(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is a regular semi-upho function if and only if $g(x)$ admits an infinite greedy $0$ -monoid series $\{M_{k}^{0}\}_{k\geq 1}$ . Conjecture 4.10. A formal power series $g(x)=1+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is a regular semi-upho function if and only if for any integer $k\geq 2$ , the $k$ -th greedy $0$ -monoid $M_{k}^{0}$ is defined implies the inequality $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{0}}|\geq b_{k+1}$ holds. 4.3 Counting Elements in Greedy $0$ -Monoids As shown in Proposition 4.8, to prove that a formal power series is a regular semi-upho function, it suffices to count nonzero elements in greedy $0$ -monoids and compare them with the coefficients of the formal power series. In this subsection, we develop methods for counting nonzero elements in greedy $0$ -monoids. Throughout this subsection, in each lemma and proposition, we consider a formal power series $g(x)=1+b_{1}x+b_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ and its $k$ -th greedy $0$ -monoid $M_{k}^{0}=\langle\mathbf{X}\mid R_{k}\rangle$ , and denote $\mathbf{W}_{n}^{M_{k}^{0}}$ simply by $\mathbf{W}_{n}$ . Lemma 4.11. Let $L\equiv x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{n}}$ be the maximal word under the $n$ -lexicographic order of $\mathbf{F}_{n}(\mathbf{X})$ such that $L$ is nonzero in $M_{k}^{0}$ . Then for any word $U\equiv x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{s-1}}y$ where $s\leq n$ and $y<_{X}x_{a_{s}}$ , we have $W=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ if and only if $UW=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ for any word $W\in\mathbf{F}_{n-s}(\mathbf{X})$ . Proof. If $W=0$ , then clearly $UW=0$ . Conversely, suppose $UW=0$ . By Proposition 2.27, there exists a $0$ -defining relation $V=0$ such that $V$ is a consecutive subword of $UW$ . By the definition of greedy $0$ -monoids, the fact that $V=0$ is a $0$ -defining relation implies that any word $V^{\prime}\geq_{\ell(V)}V$ under the $\ell(V)$ -lexicographic order of $\mathbf{F}_{\ell(V)}(\mathbf{X})$ is also $0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ . If the leftmost letter of $V$ is $y$ , then $x_{a_{s}}x_{a_{s+1}}\dots x_{a_{s+\ell(V)-1}}>_{\ell(V)}V$ , which implies $0=x_{a_{s}}x_{a_{s+1}}\dots x_{a_{s+\ell(V)-1}}=L$ , leading to a contradiction. If the leftmost letter of $V$ is $x_{a_{i}}$ , then $x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{i+\ell(V)-1}}\geq_{\ell(V)}V$ , resulting in $0=x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{i+\ell(V)-1}}=L$ , which is also a contradiction. Hence, the leftmost letter of $V$ is in $W$ , which implies $W=0$ , completing the proof. ∎ Proposition 4.12. Let $L\equiv x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{n}}$ be the maximal word under the $n$ -lexicographic order of $\mathbf{F}_{n}(\mathbf{X})$ such that $L$ is nonzero in $M_{k}^{0}$ . Then for any integer $1\leq s\leq n$ , we have $|\mathbf{W}_{n}|\leq(a_{1}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n-1}|+(a_{2}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n-2}|+\dots+(a_{s-1}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n+1-s}|+a_{s}|\mathbf{W}_{n-s}|,$ and the equality holds when $s=n$ . Specifically, when $n=k$ , we have $b_{k}\leq(a_{1}-1)b_{k-1}+(a_{2}-1)b_{k-2}+\dots+(a_{s-1}-1)b_{k+1-s}+a_{s}b_{k-s}$ for any $1\leq s\leq k$ , and the equality holds when $s=k$ . Proof. Any nonzero word of length $n$ starts with a unique prefix $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{t-1}}y$ where $t\leq s$ and $y<_{X}x_{a_{t}}$ , or starts with $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{s}}$ . By Lemma 4.11, there are exactly $|\mathbf{W}_{n-t}|$ elements in $\mathbf{W}_{n}$ that start with each prefix $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{t-1}}y$ . Moreover, the number of elements in $\mathbf{W}_{n}$ starting with $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{s}}$ is no more than the number of all elements in $\mathbf{W}_{n-s}$ . As $b_{k-s}=|\mathbf{W}_{k-s}|$ , we have $|\mathbf{W}_{n}|=(a_{1}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n-1}|+(a_{2}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n-2}|+\dots+(a_{s-1}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n+1-s}|+(a_{s}-1)|\mathbf{W}_{n-s}|+|\mathbf{W}_{n-s}|,$ as desired. When $s=n$ , the number of elements in $\mathbf{W}_{n}$ starting with $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{s}}$ is exactly the number of all elements in $\mathbf{W}_{n-s}$ , so the equality holds. ∎ Proposition 4.12 provides a recursive formula for computing $|\mathbf{W}_{n}|$ using the maximal nonzero element of length $n$ . In practice, as shown in Proposition 4.8, the main interest is in calculating $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}|$ . While Proposition 4.12 gives an expression for $b_{k}$ based on the maximal nonzero element of length $k$ , it only allows us to compute $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}|$ using the maximal nonzero element of length $k+1$ , which can differ significantly from that of length $k$ . To establish a link between $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}|$ and $b_{k}$ , we provide the following technical yet practical proposition, offering an expression for $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}|$ based on the maximal nonzero element of length $k$ , rather than $k+1$ . Proposition 4.13. Let $L\equiv x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ be the maximal word in $\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{X})$ under the $k$ -lexicographic order such that $L$ is nonzero in $M_{k}^{0}$ . Then we have $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}|=(a_{1}-1)b_{k}+(a_{2}-1)b_{k-1}+\dots+(a_{s-1}-1)b_{k+2-s}+a_{s}b_{k+1-s}$ for some integer $s$ with $1\leq s\leq k$ . To prove Proposition 4.13, we first prove the following two technical lemmas. Lemma 4.14. Let $L\equiv x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ be the maximal word in $\mathbf{F}_{k}(\mathbf{X})$ under the $k$ -lexicographic order such that $L$ is nonzero in $M_{k}^{0}$ . Then there exists a positive integer $s$ with $1\leq s\leq k$ such that $U\equiv x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{s}}$ satisfies that $W=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ if and only if $UW=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ for any word $W\in\mathbf{F}_{k+1-s}(\mathbf{X})$ . Proof. If $W=0$ , then clearly $UW=0$ . Suppose the converse direction is false. We prove by contradiction. Let $V_{i}$ denote the maximal word in $\mathbf{F}_{k+2-i}(\mathbf{X})$ under the $(k+2-i)$ -lexicographic order such that $V_{i}\neq 0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ for $2\leq i\leq k$ . Claim 1: For any $2\leq i\leq k$ , we have $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V_{i}=0$ . By our assumption that the converse direction of the lemma is false, for any $2\leq i\leq k$ , there exists a word $W\in\mathbf{F}_{k+2-i}(\mathbf{X})$ such that $W\neq 0$ but $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}W=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ . Since $W\neq 0$ , there must be a $0$ -defining relation $x_{a_{j}}x_{a_{j+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}W^{\prime}=0$ for some prefix $W^{\prime}$ of $W$ . By the construction of greedy $0$ -monoids, this implies that all words greater than or equal to $x_{a_{j}}x_{a_{j+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}W^{\prime}$ under the $(i-j+\ell(W^{\prime}))$ -lexicographic order are also zero, and thus $x_{a_{j}}x_{a_{j+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V^{\prime}_{i}=0$ for the prefix $V^{\prime}_{i}$ of $V_{i}$ such that $\ell(V^{\prime}_{i})=\ell(W^{\prime})$ . Hence, we have $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V_{i}=0$ . This completes the proof of Claim 1. Claim 2: For any $2\leq i\leq k$ , we have $V_{i}^{-}>_{k+1-i}x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ , where $V_{i}\equiv V_{i}^{-}v_{i}$ with $v_{i}$ being the last letter of $V_{i}$ . We prove Claim 2 by induction on $i$ . For $i=2$ , by Claim 1, we know that $x_{a_{1}}V_{2}=0$ . Since $V_{2}\neq 0$ , there exists a $0$ -defining relation $x_{a_{1}}V^{\prime}_{2}=0$ such that $V^{\prime}_{2}$ is a prefix of $V_{2}$ . As no $0$ -defining relation $W=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ satisfies $\ell(W)\geq k+1$ , we have $\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})\leq\ell(V_{2})-1=\ell(V_{2}^{-})$ . Hence $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})+1}}$ is a prefix of $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ , resulting in $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})+1}}\neq 0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ . Moreover, since $x_{a_{1}}V^{\prime}_{2}=0$ is a $0$ -defining relation, by the construction of greedy $0$ -monoids, we have $x_{a_{1}}V^{\prime}_{2}>_{\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})+1}x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})+1}}$ , and hence $V^{\prime}_{2}>_{\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{\ell(V^{\prime}_{2})+1}}$ . Therefore, we have $V_{2}^{-}>_{k-1}x_{a_{2}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ and complete the proof of the base case. Suppose we have proved Claim 2 for all $i^{\prime}$ such that $2\leq i^{\prime}<i$ . By Claim 1, $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V_{i}=0$ . Case 1: If there exists a $0$ -defining relation $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V^{\prime}_{i}=0$ ( $1\leq r\leq i-1$ ), where $V^{\prime}_{i}$ is a prefix of $V^{\prime}_{i}$ such that $\ell(V^{\prime}_{i})\leq\ell(V_{i})-1=\ell(V_{i}^{-})$ , then $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1+\ell(V^{\prime}_{i})}}$ is a consecutive subword of $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ . Since $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}\neq 0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ , we have $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1+\ell(V^{\prime}_{i})}}\neq 0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ , and as $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V^{\prime}_{i}=0$ is a $0$ -defining relation, by the construction of greedy $0$ -monoids, we have $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V^{\prime}_{i}>_{r+\ell(V^{\prime}_{i})}x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1+\ell(V^{\prime}_{i})}}$ , and hence $V_{i}^{-}>_{k+1-i}x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ . Case 2: Otherwise, since $V_{i}\neq 0$ and no $0$ -defining relation $W=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ satisfies $\ell(W)\geq k+1$ , there exists a $0$ -defining relation $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V_{i}=0$ ( $1\leq r\leq i-2$ ). Since $V_{i-r}\neq 0$ , by the construction of greedy $0$ -monoids, we have $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V_{i}>_{k+2-i+r}V_{i-r}$ . Moreover, by induction hypothesis, $V_{i-r}\equiv V_{i-r}^{-}v_{i-r}>_{k+2-i+r}x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}v_{i-r}$ . Hence $V_{i}>_{k+2-i}x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}v_{i-r}$ , and thus $V_{i}^{-}\geq_{k+2-i}x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ . The equality is obtained only if $x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{i-1}}V_{i}^{-}\equiv V_{i-r}^{-}\equiv x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ , contradicting with the induction hypothesis $V_{i-r}^{-}>_{k+1-i+r}x_{a_{i-r}}x_{a_{i-r+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ . Hence, we have $V_{i}^{-}>_{k+2-i}x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}$ . Therefore, the proof of Claim 2 is complete. Claim 3: There does not exist a $0$ -defining relation $x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}y=0$ for any $1\leq i\leq k$ and $y\in X$ . The case $i=1$ is trivial, as no $0$ -defining relation $W=0$ in $M_{k}^{0}$ satisfies $\ell(W)\geq k+1$ . For $i\geq 2$ , by Claim 2, $x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}y<_{k+2-i}V_{i}$ . Thus, if $x_{a_{i}}x_{a_{i+1}}\dots x_{a_{k}}y=0$ , then $V_{i}=0$ , contradicting the nonzero nature of $V_{i}$ . This completes Claim 3. Hence, by Claim 3 and the fact that $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}\neq 0$ , we have $x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}y=0$ if and only if $y=0$ , contradicting our assumption. A fundamental question is: does any finitary upho poset have a monoid representation? We believe that Conjecture 3.12 and Conjecture 3.13 are central conjectures in the study of upho posets and semi-upho posets. As far as we know, even the finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded cases of these conjectures remain open, and proving them would represent significant progress in the study of upho posets. Conjecture 7.1. All finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded upho posets are regular. Conjecture 7.2. All finite type $\mathbb{N}$ -graded semi-upho posets are regular. Readers are referred to [5, Section 3] for further discussions on this problem. 7.2 Upho Posets beyond Finitary As shown in the proofs of Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.4, we have the following result. Lemma 7.3. A poset defined by the left-divisibility of a monoid is an upho poset if and only if the monoid is an LCIF monoid. As noted in Remark 2.26, LCIF monoids are not necessarily atomic, so the corresponding upho poset may not be finitary. Moreover, the upho poset corresponding to the LCIF monoid in Remark 2.26 is not even Noetherian. This raises the following question: Question 7.4. Characterize all upho posets defined by the left-divisibility of LCIF monoids. 7.3 Recursive Characterization of Semi-Upho Functions In Section 4.1 and Section 4.2, we establish a framework for characterizing all regular semi-upho functions, reducing the problem to proving Conjecture 4.9 and Conjecture 4.10. Furthermore, inspired by Lemma 4.3, we suggest that it may be possible to characterize all semi-upho functions without a complete proof of Conjecture 3.13. Conjecture 7.5. Given a finite type finitary semi-upho poset $S$ , there exists a tree-like semi-upho poset $T$ such that the Hasse diagram of $T$ is a spanning tree of the Hasse diagram of $S$ . Moreover, if $S$ is $\mathbb{N}$ -graded, then the rank-generating functions satisfy $F_{S}=F_{T}$ . If Conjecture 7.5 holds, then proofs of Conjecture 4.9 and Conjecture 4.10 would yield a complete characterization of all semi-upho functions. 7.4 Recursive Characterization of Regular Upho Functions Similar to Section 4.2, we introduce the greedy LCH monoid series to establish a recursive framework for characterizing all regular upho functions. Definition 7.6. Given a formal power series $f(x)=1+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , the greedy LCH monoid series $\{M_{k}^{f}\}_{k\geq 1}$ of $f(x)$ is a sequence of monoids defined recursively as follows: • Define $M_{1}^{f}$ as the free monoid generated by the ordered set $\mathbf{X}\coloneqq\{x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{c_{1}}\}$ . • For $M_{k}^{f}=\langle\mathbf{X}\mid R_{k}\rangle$ , if $c_{k}\leq c_{k+1}\leq|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{f}}|$ , then define $M_{k+1}^{f}\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{X}\mid R_{k+1}\rangle$ , where $R_{k+1}$ extends $R_{k}$ with additional head-changing relations $x_{i}W=x_{i-1}W$ for the largest $|\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{f}}|-c_{k+1}$ elements $x_{i}W$ in $\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{f}}$ under the $(k+1)$ -lexicographic order on $\mathbf{W}_{k+1}^{M_{k}^{f}}$ (see Definition 2.24). The condition $c_{k}\leq c_{k+1}$ ensures that each selected element $x_{i}W$ satisfies $i\geq 2$ , making the relation $x_{i}W=x_{i-1}W$ well-defined. We refer to $M_{k}^{f}$ as the $k$ -th greedy LCH monoid of $f(x)$ . We omit the superscript $f$ when the power series is clear from context. Analogous to Proposition 4.7 and Proposition 4.8, the following properties hold for regular upho functions. Proposition 7.7. Given a formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , if $f(x)$ admits an infinite greedy LCH monoid series $\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}$ , then $f(x)$ is a regular upho function. Proposition 7.8. Given a formal power series $f(x)=1+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ , if for any integer $k\geq 1$ , the $k$ -th greedy LCH monoid $M_{k}$ is defined implies the inequality $c_{k}\leq c_{k+1}\leq|\mathbf{W}_{k}^{M_{k+1}}|$ holds, then $f(x)$ is a regular upho function. Moreover, we conjecture that Proposition 7.7 and Proposition 7.8 characterize all regular upho functions. Conjecture 7.9. A formal power series $f(x)\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is a regular upho function if and only if $f(x)$ admits an infinite greedy LCH monoid series $\{M_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}$ . Conjecture 7.10. A formal power series $f(x)=1+c_{1}x+c_{2}x^{2}+\cdots\in 1+x\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}[[x]]$ is a regular upho function if and only if for any integer $k\geq 2$ , the $k$ -th greedy LCH monoid $M_{k}^{0}$ is defined implies the inequality $c_{k}\leq c_{k+1}\leq|\mathbf{W}_{k}^{M_{k}}|$ holds. If Conjecture 3.12 holds, then proving Conjecture 7.9 and Conjecture 7.10 would furthermore yield a complete characterization of all upho functions. 7.5 Characterizing Rational Upho Functions Proving Conjecture 7.9 and Conjecture 7.10 would yield a complete characterization of (regular) upho functions. However, due to the intrinsic difficulty of counting elements in LCH monoids, a more accessible characterization may be essential for practical applications. While an elegant and comprehensive characterization of all upho functions is likely unattainable [4, Theorem 1.4], many interesting upho posets in current research have rational rank-generating functions. For example, [6, Corollary 6] shows that the rank-generating function of any upho meet semilattice, and thus any upho lattice, is a reciprocal rational function, which is the multiplicative inverse of some polynomial. Additionally, [4, Theorem 1.3] provides a complete characterization of the rank-generating functions of planar upho posets, which are also reciprocal rational functions. Thus, the classification of rational upho functions is a significant problem in the study of upho functions. As our Theorem 1.8 shows that totally positive formal power series are upho functions, this is not an exhaustive list of rational upho functions. Example 2.15 illustrates that for a rational upho function $\frac{g(x)}{h(x)}$ , $g(x)$ may have real positive roots, and $h(x)$ may have real negative roots. Furthermore, by Corollary 5.17, $\frac{g(x)}{h(x)}=\frac{1-x+x^{2}}{(1-x)(1-4x+4x^{2})}=\frac{1}{1-x}\cdot\frac{1-x+x^{2}}{1-4x+4x^{2}}$ is a regular upho function, as $\frac{1}{1-x}$ is regular upho and $\frac{1-x+x^{2}}{1-4x+4x^{2}}$ is log-concave; but $g(x)$ have imaginary roots. Likewise, $h(x)$ can also have imaginary roots, as $\frac{1}{1-2x+x^{3}}$ is an upho function by [4, Theorem 1.3]. Thus, classifying rational upho functions remains a challenging problem. Question 7.11. Classify rational (regular) upho functions. A slightly simpler problem is to characterize all reciprocal rational upho functions. Question 7.12. Classify reciprocal rational (regular) upho functions. This question remains subtle, as [4, Theorem 1.3] implies that both $\frac{1}{1-2x+x^{3}}$ and $\frac{1}{1-3x+x^{2}+x^{3}}$ are upho functions, where the denominator may have imaginary or real negative roots.
