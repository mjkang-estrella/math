                                                                            1. Introduction
                                            Let N = {0, 1, 2, . . .} and Z+ = {1, 2, 3, . . .}. In 1961 P. Erdős, A.
                                          Ginzburg and A. Ziv [3] proved the following classical theorem.
                                          EGZ
                                          P      Theorem. Let a1 , . . . , a2n−1 ∈ Z with n ∈ Z+ . Then we have
                                            i∈I ai ≡ 0 (mod n) for some I ⊆ {1, . . . , 2n − 1} with |I| = n.
                                             For a finite additive abelian group G, its EGZ constant s(G) is defined as
                                          the least positive integer k such that any sequence over G of length k has
                                          a zero-sum subsequence of length exp(G), where exp(G) is the exponent of
                                          G. The EGZ theorem is equivalent to s(Zn ) = 2n − 1, where Zn denotes the
                                          cyclic group Z/nZ.
                                             Let n ∈ Z+ , and consider n − 1 copies of the four ordered pairs
                                                                       (0, 0), (0, 1), (1, 0), (1, 1).
                                          Clearly, no n of them sum to an ordered pair congruent to (0, 0) modulo
                                          n. So, s(Z2n ) > 4n − 4. On the other hand, a conjecture of A. Kemnitz [8]
                                          confirmed by C. Reiher [11] states that any sequence over Z2n of length at
                                          least 4n − 3 contains a zero-sum subsequence of length n. So s(Z2n ) = 4n − 3.
                                             For any prime p, the p-adic valuation of an integer m is defined as
                                                                     νp (m) = sup{n ∈ N : pn | m},
                                          and we also write pn ∥m when νp (m) = n ∈ N. At the end of [4], W. Gao et
                                          al. introduced the following definition involving p-adic valuations.
                                          Definition 1.1 (Gao-Hui-Jiang-Li-Wang). Let p be a prime and let α ∈ Z+ .
                                          Define the constant s(p, α) as the smallest positive integer k such that for
                                          any integers a1 , . . . , ak ̸≡ 0 (mod p) we can select p of them for which the
                                          p-adic valuation of their sum is exactly α.
