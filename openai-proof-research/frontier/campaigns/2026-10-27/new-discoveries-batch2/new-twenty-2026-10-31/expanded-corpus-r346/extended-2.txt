Abstract
A generator matrix of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is also a matrix of the same rank  $k$  over any
extension field  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  and generates a linear code of the same length, same dimension and
same minimum distance over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ , denoted by  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  and called a lifted code of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . Although  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and
their lifted codes  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  have the same parameters, they have different weight distributions
and different applications. Few results about lifted linear codes are known in the literature.
This paper proves some fundamental theory for lifted linear codes, settles the weight distributions of the
lifted Hamming codes and lifted Simplex codes as well as the lifted Reed-Muller codes of certain orders,
and investigates the  $2$ -designs and  $3$ -designs supported by these
lifted codes. Infinite families of  $2$ -designs and  $3$ -designs are obtained. In addition, an infinite family of two-weight projective
codes and two infinite families of three-weight projective codes are presented.
Keywords: 
Hamming code , lifted code , linear code , Reed-Muller codde , Simplex code ,  $t$ -design.

1 Introduction
1.1 The support designs of linear codes
Let  $v$  and  $k$  be positive integers with  $1\leq k\leq v$ . Let  $t$  be a positive integer with  $t\leq k$ .
Let  ${\mathcal{P}}$  be a set of  $v$  elements and let  ${\mathcal{B}}$  be a set of  $k$ -subsets of  ${\mathcal{P}}$ . The pair  ${\mathbb{D}}:=({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{B}})$  is an
incidence structure, where the incidence relation is the set membership. The pair
 ${\mathbb{D}}=({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{B}})$  is called a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design, or simply  $t$ -design, if every  $t$ -subset of  ${\mathcal{P}}$  is contained in exactly  $\lambda$  elements of
 ${\mathcal{B}}$ . The elements of the set  ${\mathcal{P}}$  are referred to as points, and those of the set  ${\mathcal{B}}$  are called blocks.
The set  ${\mathcal{B}}$  is called the block set.
The number of blocks in  ${\mathcal{B}}$  is usually denoted by  $b$ .
Let  $\binom{{\mathcal{P}}}{k}$  denote the set of all  $k$ -subsets of  ${\mathcal{P}}$ . Then the incidence structure  $\left({\mathcal{P}},\binom{{\mathcal{P}}}{k}\right)$  is a  $k$ - $(v,k,1)$  design,
which is called a complete design.
A  $t$ -design is said to be simple if  ${\mathcal{B}}$  does not contain
any repeated blocks. This paper
considers only simple  $t$ -designs.
A  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design is called a
Steiner system if  $t\geq 2$  and  $\lambda=1$ ,
and is denoted by  $S(t,k,v)$ .
There are several approaches to constructing  $t$ -designs with linear codes.
One of the coding-theoretic constructions of  $t$ -designs is the following.
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a  $[v,\kappa,d]$  linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ , which
is a  $\kappa$ -dimensional subspace of the vector space  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{v}$  with minimum Hamming distance  $d$  [17, 20]
and is also called a  $[v,\kappa,d]_{q}$  code. We sometimes use  $d({\mathcal{C}})$  to denote the minimum Hamming
distance of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
Let  $A_{i}$  or  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  denote the number of codewords with Hamming weight  $i$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ 
for  $0\leq i\leq v$ . The sequence  $(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{v})$  is called the weight distribution of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
For each  $k$  with  $A_{k}({\mathcal{C}})\neq 0$ , let  ${\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}})$  denote
the set of the supports of all codewords with Hamming weight  $k$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ ,
where  ${\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}})$  is not allowed to have repeated blocks.
Suppose that the coordinates of the codewords in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ 
are indexed by  $(p_{1},\ldots,p_{v})$ . Let  ${\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{v}\}$ . The incidence structure  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}))$ 
may be a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  simple design for some positive integer  $\lambda$ , which is called a
support design of the code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , and is denoted by  ${\mathbb{D}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}})$ . In this case, we say that the codewords of weight  $k$ 
in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  support or hold a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design, and for simplicity, we say that  ${\mathcal{C}}$  supports or holds a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design.
The following theorem, which was established by Assmus and Mattson and called the Assmus-Mattson Theorem,
says that the incidence structure  ${\mathbb{D}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}):=({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}))$  defined by
a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is a simple  $t$ -design under certain conditions [2].
Theorem 1.
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a  $[v,k,d]_{q}$  code. Let  $d^{\perp}$  denote the minimum distance of the dual code
 ${\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}$  of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
Let  $w$  be the largest integer satisfying  $w\leq v$  and
 $w-\left\lfloor\frac{w+q-2}{q-1}\right\rfloor<d.$ 
Define  $w^{\perp}$  analogously using  $d^{\perp}$ . Let  $(A_{i})_{i=0}^{v}$  and  $(A_{i}^{\perp})_{i=0}^{v}$  denote
the weight distribution of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and  ${\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}$ , respectively. Fix a positive integer  $t$  with  $t<d$ , and
let  $s$  be the number of  $i$  with  $A_{i}^{\perp}\neq 0$  for  $1\leq i\leq v-t$ . Suppose  $s\leq d-t$ . Then
1. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a  $t$ -design provided  $A_{i}\neq 0$  and  $d\leq i\leq w$ , and
2. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})$  is a  $t$ -design provided  $A_{i}^{\perp}\neq 0$  and
 $d^{\perp}\leq i\leq\min\{v-t,w^{\perp}\}$ .
The preceding Assmus-Mattson Theorem is a very useful tool for constructing  $t$ -designs from linear codes
(see, e.g., [10], [13], [15], [26], [27], [28]), but does not
characterize all linear codes supporting  $t$ -designs. The reader is referred to [24] for a generalized
Assmus-Mattson theorem.
The second sufficient condition for the incidence structure  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}))$  to be a  $t$ -design is via the
automorphism group of the linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . Before introducing this sufficient condition, we have to recall
several different automorphism groups of a linear code.
The set of coordinate permutations that map a code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to itself forms a group, where the binary operation
for this group is the function composition. This group
is referred to as the permutation automorphism group of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ 
and denoted by  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ . If the length of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is  $n$  and the coordinates of the codewords in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  are indexed
with the elements in the set  $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ , then  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a subgroup of the
symmetric group  ${\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}$ .
A monomial matrix over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is a square matrix that has exactly one
nonzero element of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  in each row and column. It is easily seen that a monomial matrix  $M$  can be written either in
the form  $DP$  or the form  $PD_{1}$ , where  $D$  and  $D_{1}$  are both diagonal matrices and  $P$  is a permutation
matrix. Clearly, the set of monomial matrices that map  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to itself forms a group denoted by  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ , which is called the
monomial automorphism group of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . By definition,
we have
 ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}}).$ 
By definition, every element in  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is of the form  $DP$ , where  $D$  is a diagonal matrix and
 $P$  is a permutation matrix.
An element of this group acts on a codeword  ${\mathbf{c}}$  as
 $(DP)({\mathbf{c}})={\mathbf{c}}DP^{-1}.$ 
For two elements  $(D_{i},P_{i}):=D_{i}P_{i}$  in  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  for  $i\in\{1,2\}$ ,
the corresponding binary operation of the group  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is defined by
 $(D_{2},P_{2})\circ(D_{2},P_{1})=(D_{1}P_{1}^{-1}D_{2}P_{1},P_{2}P_{1}).$ 
The automorphism group of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , denoted by  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ , is the set
of maps of the form  $M\gamma$ ,
where  $M$  is a monomial matrix and  $\gamma$  is a field automorphism, that map  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to itself.
For binary codes  ${\mathcal{C}}$ ,  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ ,  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  and  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  are the same. If  $q$  is a prime,  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  and
 ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  are identical. According to their definitions, we have the following relations:
 ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}}).$ 
By definition, every element in  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is of the form  $DP\gamma$ , where  $D$  is a diagonal matrix,
 $P$  is a permutation matrix, and  $\gamma$  is an automorphism of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ .
An element of this group acts on a codeword  ${\mathbf{c}}$  as
 $(DP\gamma)({\mathbf{c}})=\gamma(({\mathbf{c}}D)P^{-1}).$ 
For any two elements  $(D_{i},P_{i},\gamma_{i}):=D_{i}P_{i}\gamma_{i}$  in  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  for  $i\in\{1,2\}$ ,
the corresponding binary operation of the group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is defined by
 $(D_{2},P_{2},\gamma_{2})\circ(D_{1},P_{1},\gamma_{1})=(D_{1}P_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{-1}(D_{2})P_{1},P_{2}P_{1},\gamma_{2}\circ\gamma_{1}).$ 
The automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is said to be  $t$ -transitive if for every pair of  $t$ -element ordered
sets of coordinates, there is an element  $DP\gamma$  of the automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  such that its
permutation part  $P$  sends the first set to the second set. The automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is said to be  $t$ -homogeneous if for every pair of  $t$ -element
sets of coordinates, there is an element  $DP\gamma$  of the automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  such that its
permutation part  $P$  sends the first set to the second set.
With the help of the automorphism group of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , the following theorem gives another sufficient condition
for the code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to hold  $t$ -designs.
Theorem 2.
[17, p. 308]
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a linear code of length  $n$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  such that  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is  $t$ -transitive
or  $t$ -homogeneous. Then the codewords of any weight  $i\geq t$  of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  hold a  $t$ -design,
i.e.,  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a  $t$ -design for each  $i\geq t$ .
It is in general very hard to determine the full automorphism group of a linear code.
As long as a  $t$ -transitive or  $t$ -homogeneous subgroup of  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is found,
one could make use of Theorem 2 to prove the  $t$ -design
property of  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$ .
The third way for proving the  $t$ -design property of an incidence structure  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is the direct approach [11, 25, 29],
where the  $t$ -design property of an incidence structure  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}))$  is proved by verifying the conditions in the definition of  $t$ -designs directly. This direct approach may work only when the block size is very small.
1.2 Lifted linear codes
Let  $q$  be a power of a prime and let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be an  $[n,k,d]_{q}$  linear code with generator matrix  $G$ .
For a positive integer  $\ell$ ,  $G$  is also a matrix of rank  $k$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ . Let  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  denote the linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ 
generated by  $G$ , which is called the lifted code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . It is known that  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  and
 ${\mathcal{C}}$  have the same length, dimension and minimum distance [12, Theorem 7], but they have different weight distributions for  $\ell>1$  (see the examples and some general results in later sections).
Even if the weight
distribution of  ${\mathcal{C}}(q)$  is known, it may be very hard to determine the weight distribution of a lifted code
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  for  $\ell>1$ . By definition,  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is the subfield subcode over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  of the lifted code
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  [17, 20].
1.3 The idea of constructing new designs with lifted codes of a linear code
Suppose that  ${\mathcal{C}}$  supports some nontrivial  $t$ -designs.
One or both of the following cases may happen:
1. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{m}))$  is a  $t^{\prime}$ - $(n,i,\lambda^{\prime}_{i})$  design with  $(t^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}_{i})\neq(t,\lambda_{i})$ ,
while  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a  $t$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i})$  design for some  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})\neq 0$ .
2. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{m}))$  is a  $t^{\prime}$ - $(n,i,\lambda^{\prime}_{i})$  design with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{m}))\neq 0$ ,
while  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})=0$ .
If any of the two cases above happens, a new design could be obtained. This idea of obtaining new support
designs from lifted linear codes was considered in [8], where some  $5$ -designs were found.
The following theoretical result will be needed in subsequent sections and is a theoretical foundation of this paper.
Theorem 3.
[8]
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a linear code of length  $n$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ .
Let  $E$  be a subgroup of the monomial automorphism group  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ . Assume that  $E$  is  $t$ -transitive
or  $t$ -homogeneous. Then the following hold:
1. 
For each  $e\in E$ ,  $e({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))={\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$ .
2. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))$  is a  $t$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i})$  design with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))\neq 0$  for any  $i\geq t$ , where  $\lambda_{i}$ 
is an integer.
1.4 Motivations and objectives of this paper
Many infinite families of  $2$ -designs and  $3$ -designs supported by linear
codes are available in the literature [10, 26, 27].
Recently, several infinite
families of  $4$ -designs from linear codes were reported in [25, 29].
No infinite family of linear codes supporting an infinite family of nontrivial simple  $5$ -designs
is known.
It is not easy to construct  $5$ -designs from linear codes.
A very small number of  $5$ -designs from some linear codes were reported in [2],
[21], [10, Appendix A], and [8].
Until now no linear code supporting a nontrivial  $6$ -design is known in the literature.
Of course, there are algebraic, geometric and combinatorial approaches to constructing  $t$ -designs ([1], [4]).
Combinatorists are in general interested only in the following types of  $t$ -designs:
1. 
 $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  designs with large strength  $t$ .
2. 
 $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  designs with small value  $\lambda$ , in particular,  $\lambda=1$ .
3. 
Symmetric and quasi-symmetric  $t$ -designs.
However, coding theorists are also interested in  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  designs with small strength  $t$  and large  $\lambda$ , as some  $2$ -designs could be used to construct linear codes with very good parameters [12]. This is the main motivation of obtaining new  $2$ -designs by studying the lifted Hamming and Simplex codes in this paper.
The second motivation of this paper is that two-weight and three-weight linear codes have interesting
applications in association schemes, cryptography and graph theory.
As a follow-up of [8], we will do the following in this paper:
1. 
Add some new fundamental results for lifted linear codes.
2. 
Determine the weight distributions of lifted Hamming codes and lifted Simplex codes.
3. 
Study the  $2$ -designs supported by lifted Hamming codes and lifted Simplex codes.
4. 
Settle the weight distributions of the lifted codes of the Reed-Muller codes of some orders.
5. 
Present many infinite families of  $3$ -designs supported by lifted Reed-Muller codes.
The contributions of this paper are summarised in Section 6.
2 Fundamental results of lifted linear codes
In this section, we prove some fundamental results for lifted linear codes. The following theorem extends earlier results about lifted linear codes [12].
Theorem 4.
Let  $n$  and  $\ell$  be positive integers and let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be an  $[n,k,d]_{q}$  linear code with  $k\geq 1$ . Then the following hold.
1. 
If  $\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\}$  is a basis of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ , then the lifted code
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})=\{\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{c}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}{\mathbf{c}}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell}{\mathbf{c}}_{\ell}:\penalty\ {\mathbf{c}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}},\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq\ell\}.$ 
2. 
The lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  has parameters  $[n,k,d]_{q^{\ell}}$ . Furthermore,
 $A_{d}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))=\frac{q^{\ell}-1}{q-1}A_{d}({\mathcal{C}}),$ 
and every minimum weight codeword  ${\mathbf{c}}(q|q^{\ell})$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  is of the form  ${\mathbf{c}}(q|q^{\ell})=u{\mathbf{c}}$ , where  ${\mathbf{c}}$  is a minimum weight codeword in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and  $u\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{*}$ .
3. 
If  $G=[{\mathbf{g}}_{1}^{T}\ldots{\mathbf{g}}_{n}^{T}]$  is a generator matrix of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is projective, then
 $A_{w}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))=\left|\left\{B\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times k}:\left|V_{B}\cap\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}\right|=n-w\right\}\right|,$ 
where  $V_{B}=\{{\mathbf{g}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{k}:B{\mathbf{g}}^{T}={\mathbf{0}}^{T}\}$ .
Proof.
Let  $G$  be a generator matrix of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . Then  ${\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  if and only if  ${\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{b}}G$ , where  ${\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{k}$ . For any  ${\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{k}$ , there are  ${\mathbf{b}}_{1},{\mathbf{b}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{b}}_{\ell}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{k}$  such that
 ${\mathbf{b}}=\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{b}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}{\mathbf{b}}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell}{\mathbf{b}}_{\ell}.$ 
It follows that  ${\mathbf{c}}=\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{c}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}{\mathbf{c}}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell}{\mathbf{c}}_{\ell}$ , where  ${\mathbf{c}}_{i}={\mathbf{b}}_{i}G\in{\mathcal{C}}$ . It is straightforward to see that
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})=\{\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{c}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}{\mathbf{c}}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell}{\mathbf{c}}_{\ell}:\penalty\ {\mathbf{c}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}},\penalty\ 1\leq i\leq\ell\}.$ 
The desired Result 1 follows.
For any  ${\mathbf{c}}=\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{c}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}{\mathbf{c}}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell}{\mathbf{c}}_{\ell}$ , where  ${\mathbf{c}}_{i}=(c_{i1},c_{i2},\ldots,c_{in})\in{\mathcal{C}}$ , it is easily verified that
 $\displaystyle{\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})$ 
 $\displaystyle=|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{1})\cup{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{2})\cup\cdots\cup{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{\ell})|$ 
(1)
 $\displaystyle\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq\ell}\{{\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}}_{i})\}\geq d,$ 
where  ${\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i}):=\{j:\penalty\ {\mathbf{c}}_{ij}\neq 0,\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq n\}$ . Below we prove that  ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})=d$  if and only if there exist  ${\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathcal{C}}$  with  ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{b}})=d$  and  $u\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{*}$  such that  ${\mathbf{c}}=u{\mathbf{b}}$ .
Notice that  $|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i})|\geq d$  for any  ${\mathbf{0}}\neq{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}$ . It follows from (1) that  ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})=d$  if and only if there are  $i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\in\{1,2,\ldots,\ell\}$  such that
 ${\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{1}})={\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{2}})=\cdots={\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{t}})$ 
(2)
with  $|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{1}})|=d$  and  ${\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{j})=\emptyset$  for  $j\notin\{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{t}\}$ , where  $1\leq t\leq\ell$ .
1. 
If  $t=1$ ,  ${\mathbf{c}}=\alpha_{i_{1}}{\mathbf{c}}_{i_{1}}$ . The desired result follows.
2. 
If  $t\geq 2$ , it follows from (2) and  $|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{1}})|=|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{2}})|=\cdots=|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{i_{t}})|=d$  that there are  $\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{t}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}$  such that  ${\mathbf{c}}_{i_{j}}=\lambda_{j}{\mathbf{c}}_{i_{1}}$  for  $2\leq j\leq t$ . Consequently,
 ${\mathbf{c}}=(\alpha_{i_{1}}+\lambda_{2}\alpha_{i_{2}}+\cdots+\lambda_{t}\alpha_{i_{t}}){\mathbf{c}}_{i_{1}}.$ 
In summary,  ${\mathbf{c}}\in{\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  with  ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})=d$  if and only if there exist  ${\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathcal{C}}$  with  ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{b}})=d$  and  $u\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{*}$  such that  ${\mathbf{c}}=u{\mathbf{b}}$ . The desired Result 2 follows.
For any  ${\mathbf{c}}=\alpha_{1}{\mathbf{c}}_{1}+\alpha_{2}{\mathbf{c}}_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{\ell}{\mathbf{c}}_{\ell}$ , where  ${\mathbf{c}}_{i}=(c_{i1},c_{i2},\ldots,c_{in})\in{\mathcal{C}}$ , it is also easily verified that
 $\displaystyle{\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})$ 
 $\displaystyle=n-|{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{1})\cap{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{2})\cap\cdots\cap{\rm Supp}({\mathbf{c}}_{\ell})|$ 
 $\displaystyle=n-|\{j:\penalty\ c_{1j}=c_{2j}=\cdots=c_{\ell j}=0,\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq n\}|.$ 
(3)
Notice that  ${\mathbf{c}}_{i}={\mathbf{a}}_{i}G$  for some  ${\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{k}$  and  $c_{1j}=c_{2j}=\cdots=c_{\ell j}=0$  if and only if
 ${\mathbf{a}}_{1}{\mathbf{g}}_{j}^{T}={\mathbf{a}}_{2}{\mathbf{g}}_{j}^{T}=\cdots={\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}{\mathbf{g}}_{j}^{T}=0,$ 
i.e.,  ${\mathbf{g}}_{j}\in V_{B}$ , where
 $B:=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}\\
{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\\
\vdots\\
{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}\end{bmatrix}.$ 
Since  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is projective,  ${\mathbf{g}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}$  are pairwise distinct.
By (3), we get
 ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})=n-\left|\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},{\mathbf{g}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}\cap V_{B}\right|.$ 
(4)
Therefore,  ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}})=w$  if and only if  $\left|\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},{\mathbf{g}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}\cap V_{B}\right|=n-w.$ 
It is easily verified that  ${\mathbf{c}}$  runs over each codeword in  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  once when  $B$  runs over each  $\ell\times k$  matrix in  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times k}$  once. By (4), we obtain
 $A_{w}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))=\left|\left\{B\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times k}:\left|\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}\cap V_{B}\right|=n-w\right\}\right|.$ 
(5)
The desired Result 3 follows.
∎
The part of Theorem 4 about the dimension and minimum distance of  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  was implied in [12, Theorem 7], but the rest parts of Theorem 4 are new and will be used to check if some designs are new or not. Equations (4) and (5) are useful to settle the weight distribution of certain lifted codes.
Theorem 5.
Let  $n$  and  $\ell$  be positive integers and let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be an  $[n,k]_{q}$  linear code.
Then
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})^{\perp}={\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}(q|q^{\ell}).$ 
Proof.
By Theorem 4,  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})^{\perp}$  has dimension  $n-k$ . On the other hand,
 ${\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}$  has also dimension  $n-k$ . Consequently, any parity check matrix of
 ${\mathcal{C}}$  is also a generator matrix of  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})^{\perp}$ . The desired conclusion then follows.

3 Projective Reed-Muller codes and their designs
In this section, we introduce the projective Reed-Muller codes and their designs. These results are not new.
But we shall need them later when we study Hamming codes and lifted Hamming codes.
Let  $m\geq 2$  be an integer.
A point of the projective geometry  ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  is given in homogeneous coordinates by  $(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m})$  where all  $x_{i}$  are in  $\mathrm{GF}(q)$  and are not all zero. Every point of  ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  has  $q-1$  coordinate representations, as  $(ax_{1},ax_{2},\dots,ax_{m})$  and  $(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m})$  generate the same  $1$ -dimensional subspace of  $\mathrm{GF}(q)^{m}$  for any nonzero  $a\in\mathrm{GF}(q)$ .
Let  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}]$  denote the set of polynomials in  $m$  indeterminates over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ , which is a linear space over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ . Let  $A(q,m,h)$  denote the subspace of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}]$  generated by all the homogeneous polynomials of degree  $h$ . Let  $n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$  and let  $\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\mathbf{x}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}$  be a set of projective points in  $\mathrm{PG}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ . Then the  $h$ -th order projective Reed-Muller code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  of length  $n$  is defined by
 $\displaystyle\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)=\left\{\left(f(\mathbf{x}^{1}),f(\mathbf{x}^{2}),\dots,f(\mathbf{x}^{n})\right):f\in A(q,m,h)\right\}.$ 
The code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  depends on the choice of the set  $\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\mathbf{x}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}$  of coordinate representatives of the point set in  $\mathrm{PG}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ , but is unique up to the monomial equivalence. The parameters of  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  and  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)^{\bot}$  are known and documented in the following theorems [3, 18, 22].
Theorem 6.
Let  $m\geq 2$  and  $1\leq h\leq(m-1)(q-1)$ . Then the linear code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  has length  $n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$  and minimum Hamming distance  $(q-v)q^{m-2-u}$ , where  $h-1=u(q-1)+v$  and  $0\leq v<q-1$ . Furthermore, the dimension of the code is given as
 $\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h))=\sum_{t\equiv h\pmod{q-1}\atop 0<t\leq h}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{m}{j}\binom{t-jq+m-1}{t-jq}\right).$ 
Theorem 7.
Let  $m\geq 2$  and  $1\leq h\leq(m-1)(q-1)$ . If  $h\not\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}$ , then
 $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)^{\bot}=\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-h).$ 
By Theorem 6 and definition,  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,1)$ 
is monomially-equavalent to the Simplex code.
It then follows from Theorem 7 that
 $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-1)$ 
is monomially-equavalent to the Hamming code.
Thus, the Hamming codes and Simplex codes are special projective Reed-Muller codes.
This explains why we would introduce the projective Reed-Muller codes and their monomial automorphism groups in this section.
The weight distribution of  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,2)$  was settled in [19].
It was pointed out in [6, 22] that the code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  is not cyclic in general, but is equivalent to
a cyclic code if  $\gcd(m,q-1)=1$  or  $h\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}$ .
For a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , define
 $\pi{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{P:DP\gamma\in{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})\}$ 
and
 $\pi{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{P:DP\in{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\}.$ 
By definition
 $\displaystyle\pi{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq\pi{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}}).$ 
(6)
The following result was proved in [5] and will be very useful for studying the designs supported by the projective Reed-Muller codes, the Hamming and Simplex codes and the lifted codes of these codes.
Lemma 8.
Choose the point set  $\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\mathbf{x}^{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}$  of  $\mathrm{PG}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ 
to index the coordinates of the codewords in  ${\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h)$ . Then
 ${\mathrm{PGL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(q))\subseteq\pi{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h))\subseteq\pi{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h)),$ 
where  ${\mathrm{PGL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  denotes the projective general linear group [10, Chapter 1].
Theorem 9.
For each positive integer  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h))>0$ , the incidence structure  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h))$  is a  $2$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i})$  design for some  $\lambda_{i}$ .
Proof.
It is known that  ${\mathrm{PGL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  is doubly transitive [10, Chapter 1]. The desired conclusion then follows from
Lemma 8 and Theorem 2.

4 Lifted Simplex and Hamming codes
Throughout this section, let  $n=(q^{m-1}-1)/(q-1)$  with  $m\geq 2$  being an integer.
4.1 Hamming and Simplex codes over finite fields
There are several different constructions of the Hamming and Simplex codes. The first one is a trace construction
of the Hamming and Simplex codes. Let  $\alpha$  be a generator of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})^{*}$ .
Define
 $\Delta_{i}:=\alpha^{i}{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}=\{\alpha^{i}a:a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\}$ 
for all  $i$  with  $0\leq i\leq n-1$ . Clearly, the set  $\{\Delta_{i}:0\leq i\leq n-1\}$  forms a partition of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})^{*}$ .
Let  $i$  and  $j$  be a pair of distinct elements in the set  $\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}$ . Then any
 $a\in\Delta_{i}$  and  $b\in\Delta_{j}$  must be linearly independent over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ .
Let  $b_{i}\in\Delta_{i}$  for each  $i$  with  $0\leq i\leq n-1$ . Define
 $\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})=\left\{\left({\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{m}/q}(ab_{i})\right)_{i=0}^{n-1}:a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})\right\}.$ 
(7)
Then the set  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  defined above is a Simplex code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  with parameters
 $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),m,q^{m-1}]$  and weight enumerator  $1+(q^{m}-1)z^{q^{m-1}}$ . By definition, different choices of
the vector  $(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  in the set  $\Delta_{0}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{n-1}$ 
result in monomially-equivalent Simplex codes. It is well known that monomially-equivalent codes have the same parameters and same weight enumerator. Therefore, up to monomial equivalence, Simplex codes are unique, and are
denoted by  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}$  in this paper.
The dual code of any Simplex code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  is referred to as a Hamming code, denoted by
 ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$ . Thus, all the Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  are monomially-equivalent and unique up to monomial equivalence. Hence, we
denote them by  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$ . It is well known that  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  has
parameters  $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)-m,3]$ .
The second construction of the Hamming and Simplex codes is by matrix.
A parity check matrix  $H_{(q,m)}$  of the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ 
is defined by choosing its columns as the nonzero vectors from
all the one-dimensional subspaces of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{m}$ . In terms of finite geometry, the columns of  $H_{(q,m)}$  are the
points of the projective geometry  ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  [10, Section 1.8]. Hence,  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  has length  $n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$  and dimension
 $n-m$ . By definition, no two columns of  $H_{(q,m)}$  are linearly dependent over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ . The minimum weight of  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$ 
is at least 3. Adding two nonzero vectors from two different one-dimensional subspaces gives a nonzero vector
from a third one-dimensional space. Therefore,  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  has minimum weight 3.
It is also well known that any  $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)-m,3]$  code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is monomially equivalent to the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  [17, Theorem 1.8.2].
The third one is a contacyclic code construction of the Hamming and Simplex codes [23]. The fourth one is the following.
Theorem 10.
Each Simplex code is a projective Reed-Muller code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,1)$  and each Hamming code
is a projective Reed-Muller code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-1)$ .
Proof.
It is well known that any  $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)-m,3]$  linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is monomially equivalent to the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  [17, Theorem 1.8.2].
By Theorem 6,  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-1)$  has the same parameters as
a Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  and thus they are monomially-equivalent. Note all projective RM codes
are monomially-equivalent and all Hamming codes are monomially-equivalent. The desired second conclusion then
follows.
The desired first conclusion
then follows from Theorem 7.
∎
The weight distribution of  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  is given in the following lemma
[9].
Lemma 11.
[9, p. 2418]
The weight distribution of  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  is given by
 $\displaystyle q^{m}A_{k}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})=$ 
 $\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}\\
0\leq j\leq q^{m-1}\\
i+j=k\end{subarray}}\left[\binom{\frac{q^{m-1}-1}{q-1}}{i}\binom{q^{m-1}}{j}\Big((q-1)^{k}+(-1)^{j}(q-1)^{i}(q^{m}-1)\Big)\right]$ 
for  $0\leq k\leq(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$ .
As a corollary of Theorems 9 and 10, we have the following.
Theorem 12.
For each positive integer  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})>0$ , the incidence structure
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  is a  $2$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i})$  design for some  $\lambda_{i}$ .
Furthermore,  ${\mathbb{D}}_{q^{m-1}}({\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)})$  is a  $2$ - $(n,q^{m-1},(q-1)q^{m-2})$  design.
Theorem 12 documents all the support designs of the Hamming codes, which was
obtained by investigating the automorphism group of the Hamming codes.
With the help of the Assmus-Mattson theorem it was proved in [10, Theorem 10.23] that  ${\mathbb{D}}_{q^{m-1}}({\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)})$  is a  $2$ - $(n,q^{m-1},(q-1)q^{m-2})$  design and the following hold:
1. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{3}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  is a  $2$ - $(n,3,q-1)$  design for each prime power  $q\geq 2$ .
2. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{4}({\mathcal{H}}_{(3,m)})$  is a  $2$ - $((3^{m}-1)/2,4,\lambda_{m})$  design for some  $\lambda_{m}$ .
These are the known  $2$ -designs supported by the Hamming and Simplex codes obtained via the Assmus-Mattson theorem. Hence, the automorphism group is much more powerful than the Assmus-Matsson theorem approach when they are used for studying the designs supported by the Hamming codes.
4.2 Lifted Simplex and Hamming codes
Let  $\ell$  be a positive integer with  $\ell\leq m$  and let  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  denote the lifted code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  of
the Simplex code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}$ .
Theorem 13.
Let  $m\geq 2$ . Then the following hold.
1. 
The lifted code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  has parameters  $[n,m,q^{m-1}]_{q^{\ell}}$ .
2. 
The dual code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})^{\perp}$  has parameters  $[n,n-m,3]_{q^{\ell}}$ .
Proof.
It follows from Theorem 4 that  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  has the same parameters as the Simplex code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}$ .
It follows from Theorem 5 that  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})^{\perp}$  has the same parameters as the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$ .
The desired conclusions then follow from the parameters of the Simplex code and Hamming code.

Theorem 14.
Let  $m\geq 2$  and  $1\leq\ell\leq m$ . Then the lifted code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  has weight enumerator
 $\displaystyle 1+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(q^{\ell}-q^{j-1})(q^{m}-q^{j-1})}{q^{j-1}(q^{j}-1)}\right)z^{q^{m-r}(q^{r}-1)/(q-1)}.$ 
(8)
Proof.
We follow the notation of the proof of Theorem 5.
Let  $G=[{\mathbf{g}}_{1}^{T}\ldots{\mathbf{g}}_{n}^{T}]$  be a generator matrix of the Simplex code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}$ . By definition the set
 $\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}$  is a point set of
 ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ . For any  $B\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times m}$  with rank  $1\leq r\leq\ell$ , it is easily verified that
 $\left|\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},{\mathbf{g}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}\cap V_{B}\right|=\frac{q^{m-r}-1}{q-1}.$ 
By (4), the nonzero weights of  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  are  $\{w_{r}:=q^{m-r}(q^{r}-1)/(q-1):\penalty\ 1\leq r\leq\ell\}$ . Note that  $1\leq r\leq\ell$  and the number of  $\ell\times m$  matrices over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  with rank  $r$  is equal to
 $\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(q^{\ell}-q^{j-1})(q^{m}-q^{j-1})}{q^{j-1}(q^{j}-1)}.$ 
The total number of codewords with weight  $w_{r}$  in the lifted code
 ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  directly follows from (5). This completes the proof.

Theorem 22.
2. 
When  $A_{i}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})>0$ , the designs  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  and  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell}))$  may have different values  $\lambda_{i}$  for  $i>3$ . 5 Lifted Reed-Muller codes and their designs
The reader is referred to [10, Chappter 5] or other books on coding theory for a
description of the binary Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$  of order  $r$ . The parameters of
these Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$  are known and the weight distribution of  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$ 
is known for certain  $r$ . It is known that the codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$  support  $3$ -designs. However,
it is known that the generalised Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{q}(r,m)$  for  $q>3$  support only  $2$ -designs but do not support  $3$ -designs [10, Chapters 5 and 6]. The objectives of this section are to show that
many infinite families of  $3$ -designs are supported by the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  and settle the weight distributions of the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  and  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(m-2,m)(2|2^{\ell})$ .
The following theorem follows from Theorems 4 and 5 and the basic results about the Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$ .
Theorem 25.
Let  $m\geq 3$  and  $0\leq r<m$ . Then the lifted code  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  has parameters
 $\left[2^{m},\ \sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{m}{i},\ 2^{m-r}\right]_{2^{\ell}}.$ 
Furthermore,  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})^{\perp}$  has parameters
 $\left[2^{m},\ 2^{m}-\sum_{i=0}^{r}\binom{m}{i},\ 2^{r+1}\right]_{2^{\ell}}$ 
and
 ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})^{\perp}={\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(m-1-r,m)(2|2^{\ell}).$ 
The next theorem shows that many infinite families of  $3$ -designs are supported by the lifted
codes of the binary Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$  of order  $r$  for  $1\leq r<m$ .
Theorem 26.
Let  $m\geq 3$  and  $\ell\geq 1$ . For each  $i\geq 3$  with  $A_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))>0$ , the incidence structure
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))$  is a  $3$ - $(2^{m},i,\lambda_{i})$  design for some positive integer  $\lambda_{i}$ .
Proof.
Notice that
 ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m))={\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m))={\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m))={\mathrm{GA}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(2)),$ 
which is the general affine group and triply transitive acting on  ${\mathrm{GF}}(2)^{m}$  [10]. It then follows from Theorem
3 that  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))$  is a  $3$ -design for each  $i\geq 3$  with  $A_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))>0$ .
∎

It is well known that  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)$  has weight enumerator  $1+(2^{m+1}-2)z^{2^{m-1}}+z^{2^{m}}$ .
The following theorem gives the weight distribution of the lifted code  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)(2|2^{\ell})$ .
Theorem 28.
Let  $m\geq 3$  and  $1\leq\ell\leq m$ . Then the weight distribution of  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  is the following:
 $\displaystyle A_{0}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle 1,$ 
 $\displaystyle A_{2^{m}-2^{m-h}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle 2^{h}\prod_{j=1}^{h}\frac{(2^{\ell}-2^{j-1})(2^{m}-2^{j-1})}{2^{j-1}(2^{j}-1)}\mbox{ for each }1\leq h\leq\ell,$ 
 $\displaystyle A_{2^{m}}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle 2^{\ell(m+1)}-1-\sum_{h=1}^{\ell}A_{2^{m}-2^{m-h}},$ 
 $\displaystyle A_{i}$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle 0\ \mbox{ for other }\ i.$ 
Proof.
We follow the notation of the proof of Theorem 14.
Let  $n=2^{m}$ . Let  $\{{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\}={\mathrm{GF}}(2)^{m\times 1}$ . By definition,
 $\displaystyle G(1,m)=\left[\begin{array}[]{lll}{\mathbf{g}}_{1}&\cdots&{\mathbf{g}}_{n}\\
1&\cdots&1\end{array}\right]=\left[{\mathbf{a}}_{1}^{T}\cdots{\mathbf{a}}_{n}^{T}\right]$ 
is a generator matrix of  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)$ .
For any  $B=[B_{1}B_{2}]\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times(m+1)}$ , where  $B_{1}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times m}$  and  $B_{2}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{\ell\times 1}$ . It is easily seen that
 $\displaystyle B\left[\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m}\\
1\end{array}\right]={\mathbf{0}}^{T}\mbox{ if and only if }B_{1}\left[\begin{array}[]{l}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{m}\end{array}\right]=B_{2}.$ 
It follows that
 $\left|\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}\cap V_{B}\right|=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }{\mathrm{rank}}(B_{1})\neq{\mathrm{rank}}(B),\\
2^{m-h}&\mbox{ if }{\mathrm{rank}}(B_{1})={\mathrm{rank}}(B)=h,\end{cases}$ 
where  $1\leq h\leq\ell$ . By (4), the nonzero weights of  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  are
 $\{w_{h}:=2^{m}-2^{m-h}:\penalty\ 1\leq h\leq\ell\}\cup\{2^{m}\}.$ 
We now determine  $A_{2^{m}-2^{m-h}}$  for  $1\leq h\leq\ell$ . Let  ${\mathrm{rank}}(B_{1})=h$ . It is known that
 $\displaystyle|\{B_{1}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(2)^{\ell\times m}:{\mathrm{rank}}(B_{1})=h\}|=\prod_{j=1}^{h}\frac{(2^{\ell}-2^{j-1})(2^{m}-2^{j-1})}{2^{j-1}(2^{j}-1)}.$ 
(11)
Note that
 $\displaystyle|\{B_{1}{\mathbf{x}}:{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(2)^{m\times 1}\}|=2^{h}.$ 
(12)
Combining (5), (11) and (12) yields the desired formula for  $A_{2^{m}-2^{m-h}}$ . This completes the proof.
∎

6 Summary and concluding remarks
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.
1. 
Certain fundamental results for lifted linear codes were proved in Theorems 4 and 5.
2. 
The weight distributions of the lifted Simplex codes  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were settled in Theorem 14.
3. 
The support  $2$ -designs of the lifted Simplex codes  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were characterised in Theorem 17.
4. 
The weight distributions of the lifted Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were settled in Theorem 21.
5. 
The support  $2$ -designs of the lifted Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were characterised
in Theorem 22.
6. 
An infinite family of two-weight projective codes was obtained in Corollary 15.
7. 
Two infinite families of three-weight projective codes over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(4)$  were obtained in Corollaries 16 and 31.
8. 
The weight distributions of the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  were settled in Theorem 28.
9. 
The weight distributions of the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(m-2,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  were settled in Theorem
35.
10. 
It was proved in Theorem 26 and Corollary 29 that that many infinite families of  $3$ -designs are supported by
the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})$ .
The determination of the weight enumerator of a linear code is quite difficult in general. The settlement of
the weight enumerator of a lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  could be very difficult, even if the weight enumerator of the given code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is known. The reader is cordially invited to determine the weight enumerator of the lifted projective Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,2)(q|q^{\ell})$  for  $2\leq\ell\leq m$ ,
while the weight enumerator
of  ${\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,2)$  is known [19].
It is known that the permutation automorphism group  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,m)})$  is the general linear group
 ${\mathrm{GL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(2))$  [16]. It is impossible to characterise the permutation automorphism group  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  for  $q>2$ , as it varies from a Hamming code to another Hamming code of the same
parameters, although all Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  are monomially-equivalent. In a special case, the
permutation automorphism group  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  for  $q>2$  was settled [14].
