## 360636: full exact decision endpoint; complete routine self-reduction, zero credit

For a finite poset on n explicitly represented elements, prescriptions are equations f(z_i)=c_i for a bijective order-preserving f to {1,...,n}. Inconsistent repeated prescriptions, repeated positions for distinct elements, and out-of-range positions give zero. Sort the remaining prescriptions by position. Add relations z_i<z_j whenever c_i<c_j, take transitive closure, and reject cycles. This does not change the set of admissible extensions: every admissible f already satisfies every added relation. This chain-augmentation step is essential; testing the source's chain criterion on the unaugmented poset is invalid.

The source supplies polynomial feasibility and witness construction: Chan–Pak survey, Theorem 10.9; its companion Effective poset inequalities, Theorem 1.11 and Section 7 proof, explicitly explains the chain augmentation. Here is also an independent elementary feasibility bridge, so we do not leave the oracle as an unpaid lemma. Remove the prescribed elements and reserve their prescribed positions. For each remaining element x, allowable positions are the unused integers strictly above every prescribed predecessor's position and strictly below every prescribed successor's position. Build the bipartite graph of remaining elements and allowable positions. An admissible extension gives a perfect matching. Conversely, start from a perfect matching. If x<y but f(x)>f(y), swap their positions. The interval lower and upper bounds are monotone along x<y, so both new positions remain allowable: lower(x)<=lower(y)<=old f(y) and old f(x)<=upper(x)<=upper(y), with the other two inequalities immediate. Prescribed elements remain fixed. Fix any topological rank rho of the poset. Each such swap strictly increases sum rho(x)f(x), by (rho(y)-rho(x))(old f(x)-old f(y))>0. This integer is bounded by n^3, hence after polynomially many swaps there is no inversion. The resulting assignment is an extension. Bipartite perfect matching via augmenting paths is polynomial (at most n augmentations, polynomial graph searches). Thus feasibility is polynomial even with arbitrary many prescriptions.

Fix a total order of labels. Consider the rooted prefix tree of admissible extensions, branching by the next unused element in that label order. At every prefix, combine its position equations with the original equations and test feasibility as above; discard empty branches. A surviving leaf is exactly one admissible extension and each extension has one leaf. Traverse surviving branches depth first, stopping at the (m+1)st leaf. Before that stop, every visited surviving node belongs to one of at most m+1 root-to-leaf paths, so at most (m+1)(n+1) surviving nodes occur. At most n child feasibility tests occur per node; exhausted empty instances have only the root test. Thus O((m+1)n^2) polynomial feasibility calls suffice. If traversal exhausts at t<=m leaves, answer t=m; if it reaches m+1, answer false. Empty posets, m=0, and inconsistent prescriptions are covered by the same convention. For each fixed m this is polynomial in input length and resolves exactly {N_z,c(P)=m}. This is a proof about an algorithm, not execution of that algorithm.

Failed shortcut repaired: counting unconstrained remaining extensions ignores fixed positions. Blindly treating arbitrary prescribed elements as an existing chain can misapply Theorem 10.9. Both are repaired above. The full reduction is classical feasibility-to-bounded-counting self-reduction, with the source's polynomial feasibility theorem as sufficient input. No claim of a previously published explicit resolution of Conjecture 10.11 is made. All versions v1/v2 retain its formulation; author PDF dated February 25, 2025 retains it. Companion proof and original cited Daykin paper are distinguished: the companion proof was read; Daykin's original 1985 proof was not acquired. No novelty clearance follows.

## 364029: full exact formula; published journal theorem and independent classical bridge, zero credit

Let v=2^m and identify the code's coordinates with F_2^m. The lifted first-order binary Reed–Muller code over F_4 consists exactly of affine F_2-linear functions F_2^m -> F_4: the generator rows are the constant row and the m coordinate functions, and extending coefficients to F_4 gives exactly these functions. Write such a function as a(x)+omega b(x) with affine binary a,b and {1,omega} an F_2-basis of F_4. A word has weight 3v/4 precisely when its zero set has v/4 elements. A nonempty fiber of an affine map has size 2^(m-rank), so this happens precisely when the combined linear map (a_lin,b_lin) has rank 2. Its zero set is then an affine codimension-two flat. Conversely every such flat is a fiber of a rank-two linear map, hence occurs. Distinct supports correspond bijectively to distinct flats by complementation.

For each linear codimension-two subspace, ordered independent pairs of linear forms with that kernel number |GL(2,2)|=(4-1)(4-2)=6. There are (v-1)(v-2) ordered independent pairs in the dual, so the number of such kernels is (v-1)(v-2)/6. Each has four affine cosets. Thus the number of distinct supports is b=2(v-1)(v-2)/3. Equivalently every zero flat is obtained from six codewords; dividing by the scalar group of F_4 of order three would be wrong, because non-scalar binary changes of basis also preserve the zero flat. This repairs the first failed shortcut.

The affine group AGL(m,2) acts transitively on ordered triples of distinct points. Indeed translate the first to zero; the two remaining nonzero distinct differences are linearly independent over F_2; extend them to a basis and map that basis to the corresponding target basis. This group preserves the flat family and thus its complementary supports. Therefore every triple occurs in the same number lambda of supports. Count pairs (triple,block): lambda*binom(v,3)=b*binom(3v/4,3). Substituting b gives exactly the literal formula. This proof works for every m>=3, involves distinct supports rather than repeated codewords, and checks the source's m=4 example symbolically: b=140 and lambda=55; the source lists 840 weight-12 words, exactly six per block. No finite search was run.

Version review: arXiv v1/v2/v3 were acquired; actual target passages were visible in v2 and v3, whereas v1 target passage was not located and is NOT claimed reviewed. v2 labels it Conjecture 32, v3 Conjecture 35. Crucially the author-hosted final journal PDF, Designs, Codes and Cryptography 93 (2025), 1575–1595, DOI 10.1007/s10623-024-01549-w, contains Theorem 38 with proof via Corollary 34 and Lemma 37. Setting h=ell=2 gives b=4*(2^m-1)*(2^(m-1)-1)/3=2*(v-1)*(v-2)/3 and exactly this lambda. Example 40 is the same m=4 example. Thus it is already published, not a new candidate; even without the journal, the affine-flat reduction is a complete classical/source bridge. No priority claim. No CANDIDATE or Pi dispatch.

## 362193: sustained density obstruction; semantic boundary, zero credit

The literal record explicitly defines density as |V|/|L_n(q)|. Source Conjecture 6.2 uses the word density without resolving, in the reviewed passage, the distinction from Lubell density. For nonconstant progressions of dimensions, a single rank contains none of length two. Such a rank need not have density tending to zero in a finite-field subspace lattice; this defeats the naive transfer from Boolean-lattice concentration.

Here is a uniform proof, not a numeric experiment. Set q=2 and let G(n,r) be the number of r-dimensional subspaces. Its classical Gaussian quotient is G(n,r-1)/G(n,r)=(2^r-1)/(2^(n-r+1)-1). For r<=(n+1)/2 this is at most 2^(2r-n-1): after factoring the powers of 2, the remaining numerator is no larger than the denominator. By symmetry G(n,r)=G(n,n-r). If n=2a, the rank a-j has size at most G(n,a)*2^(-j^2); hence total size is at most G(n,a)*(1+2*sum_{j>=1}2^(-j^2))<=3G(n,a), using j^2>=j. If n=2a+1, the two middle ranks have equal maximum size, and each outer rank at offset j has size at most that maximum times 2^(-j(j+1)). The total is at most 2G(n,a)*(1+sum_{j>=1}2^(-j(j+1)))<= (8/3)G(n,a)<4G(n,a), using j(j+1)>=2j. Thus for every n a middle rank has density at least 1/4 (n=0 also has its single subspace). Taking k=2 and delta=1/4 refutes the record under the nonconstant-dimension-progression convention for every possible chosen n. It is not enough merely to exhibit arbitrarily large bad n because the literal quantifier is 'there exists n'; the uniform argument repairs that failed shortcut.

If repeated dimensions with common difference zero are allowed, several different subspaces of one dimension may qualify, and the preceding family is not a refutation. The literal wording does not explicitly require positive common difference or explain how repeated dimension values count. With a normalized Lubell density instead, the middle rank's density is 1/(n+1), also defeating the proposed obstruction. Consequently there is NO unconditionally source-qualified exact endpoint here; this is a definition-sensitive classical obstruction/correction, zero credit. Gaussian quotient counting can be proved by counting subspace flags; that combinatorial identity was used as classical mathematics, not computed. The journal was located but its full body was not read: the institutional page returned 403 and was not retried; no alternate denied route was attempted. Novelty and source gates stay unresolved.
