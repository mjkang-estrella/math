Let us remark that almost all mentioned algorithmic results work for the more general List- $k$ -Coloring problem, where each vertex $v$ of the input graph $G$ is equipped with a list $L(v)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}$ , and we ask for a proper coloring assigning to each vertex a color from its list.
The only exception is the case of $k=4$ and $t=6$ . Indeed, List- $4$ -Coloring is NP-hard in $P_{6}$ -free graphs [23].
There are also some results concerning (List-) $k$ -Coloring of $\mathcal{F}$ -free graphs, when $\mathcal{F}$ contains more graphs [5, 13, 10, 31, 21, 17, 6]; see also survey papers by Schiermeyer [37] and Golovach, Johnson, Paulusma, and Song [22].

2 Preliminaries
For an integer $n$ , by $[n]$ we denote the set $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ .
All logarithms in the paper are of base 2.
We say that sets $A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}$ form a partition of $X$ , if they are pairwise disjoint and their union is equal to  $X$ . Note that we do not insist that sets $A_{i}$ are non-empty.
Graph theory.
Let $G$ be a graph and let $X,Y$ be two disjoint subsets of $V(G)$ .
We say that $X$ is complete to $Y$ , or that $X$ and $Y$ are complete to each other, if $xy\in E(G)$ for any $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$ .
Similarly, $X$ and $Y$ are anticomplete if there are not edges between $X$ and $Y$ .
If one of the sets $X,Y$ is a singleton, say $X=\{x\}$ , we shortly say that $x$ is (anti)complete to $Y$ .
Let $v$ be a vertex of $G$ , and $X$ be a subset of $V(G)$ .
By $N(v)$ we denote the set of all neighbors of $v$ , and we define $N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}$ . We also write $N_{X}(v)$ as a shorthand for $N(v)\cap X$ .
