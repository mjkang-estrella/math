Lifted Hamming and Simplex Codes and Their Designs
Cunsheng Ding
Email: cding@ust.hk
Address: Department of Computer Science and Engineering, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Clear Water Bay, Kowloon, Hong Kong, China
Abstract
A generator matrix of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is also a matrix of the same rank  $k$  over any
extension field  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  and generates a linear code of the same length, same dimension and
same minimum distance over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ , denoted by  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  and called a lifted code of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . Although  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and
their lifted codes  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  have the same parameters, they have different weight distributions
and different applications. Few results about lifted linear codes are known in the literature.
This paper proves some fundamental theory for lifted linear codes, settles the weight distributions of
lifted Hamming codes and lifted Simplex codes, and investigates the  $2$ -designs supported by the
lifted Hamming and Simplex codes. Infinite families of  $2$ -designs are obtained. In addition, an infinite family of two-weight
codes and an infinite family of three-weight codes are presented.
Keywords: 
Lifted code , linear code ,  $t$ -design.
MSC94B05 , 51E05
1 Introduction
1.1 The support designs of linear codes
Let  $v$  and  $k$  be positive integers with  $1\leq k\leq v$ . Let  $t$  be a positive integer with  $t\leq k$ .
Let  ${\mathcal{P}}$  be a set of  $v$  elements and let  ${\mathcal{B}}$  be a set of  $k$ -subsets of  ${\mathcal{P}}$ . The pair  ${\mathbb{D}}:=({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{B}})$  is an
incidence structure, where the incidence relation is the set membership. The pair
 ${\mathbb{D}}=({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{B}})$  is called a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design, or simply  $t$ -design, if every  $t$ -subset of  ${\mathcal{P}}$  is contained in exactly  $\lambda$  elements of
 ${\mathcal{B}}$ . The elements of the set  ${\mathcal{P}}$  are called points, and those of the set  ${\mathcal{B}}$  are referred to as blocks.
The set  ${\mathcal{B}}$  is called the block set.
The number of blocks in  ${\mathcal{B}}$  is usually denoted by  $b$ .
Let  $\binom{{\mathcal{P}}}{k}$  denote the set of all  $k$ -subsets of  ${\mathcal{P}}$ . Then  $\left({\mathcal{P}},\binom{{\mathcal{P}}}{k}\right)$  is a  $k$ - $(v,k,1)$  design,
which is referred to as a complete design.
A  $t$ -design is said to be simple if  ${\mathcal{B}}$  does not contain
any repeated blocks. This paper
considers only simple  $t$ -designs.
A  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design is referred to as a
Steiner system if  $t\geq 2$  and  $\lambda=1$ ,
and is denoted by  $S(t,k,v)$ . By definition, it is easy to see that the parameters of a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design satisfy the following relation
 $\displaystyle\binom{v}{t}\lambda=\binom{k}{t}b.$ 
There are several approaches to constructing  $t$ -designs.
A coding-theoretic construction of  $t$ -designs is the following.
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a  $[v,\kappa,d]$  linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ , which
is a  $\kappa$ -dimensional subspace of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{v}$  with minimum Hamming distance  $d$  [17, 20]
and is also called a  $[v,\kappa,d]_{q}$  code.
Let  $A_{i}$  or  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  denote the number of codewords with Hamming weight  $i$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ 
for  $0\leq i\leq v$ . The sequence  $(A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{v})$  is called the weight distribution of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
For each  $k$  with  $A_{k}({\mathcal{C}})\neq 0$ , let  ${\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}})$  denote
the set of the supports of all codewords with Hamming weight  $k$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ ,
where  ${\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}})$  is not allowed to have repeated blocks.
Suppose that the coordinates of the codewords in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ 
are indexed by  $(p_{1},\ldots,p_{v})$ . Let  ${\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{v}\}$ . The incidence structure  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}))$ 
may be a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  simple design for some positive integer  $\lambda$ , which is called a
support design of the code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , and is denoted by  ${\mathbb{D}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}})$ . In this case, we say that the codewords of weight  $k$ 
in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  support or hold a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design, and for simplicity, we say that  ${\mathcal{C}}$  supports or holds a  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  design.
The following theorem, which was established by Assmus and Mattson and called the Assmus-Mattson Theorem,
says that the pair  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}))$  defined by
a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is a simple  $t$ -design under certain conditions [2].
Theorem 1.
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a  $[v,k,d]$  code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ . Let  $d^{\perp}$  denote the minimum distance of the dual code
 ${\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}$  of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
Let  $w$  be the largest integer satisfying  $w\leq v$  and
 $w-\left\lfloor\frac{w+q-2}{q-1}\right\rfloor<d.$ 
Define  $w^{\perp}$  analogously using  $d^{\perp}$ . Let  $(A_{i})_{i=0}^{v}$  and  $(A_{i}^{\perp})_{i=0}^{v}$  denote
the weight distribution of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and  ${\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}$ , respectively. Fix a positive integer  $t$  with  $t<d$ , and
let  $s$  be the number of  $i$  with  $A_{i}^{\perp}\neq 0$  for  $1\leq i\leq v-t$ . Suppose  $s\leq d-t$ . Then
1. 
all the codewords of weight  $i$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  support a  $t$ -design provided  $A_{i}\neq 0$  and  $d\leq i\leq w$ , and
2. 
all the codewords of weight  $i$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}^{\perp}$  support a  $t$ -design provided  $A_{i}^{\perp}\neq 0$  and
 $d^{\perp}\leq i\leq\min\{v-t,w^{\perp}\}$ .
The preceding Assmus-Mattson Theorem is a very useful tool for constructing  $t$ -designs from linear codes
(see, e.g., [10], [13], [15], [26], [27], [28]), but does not
characterize all linear codes supporting  $t$ -designs. The reader is referred to [24] for a generalized
Assmus-Mattson theorem.
The second sufficient condition for the incidence structure  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}))$  to be a  $t$ -design is via the
automorphism group of the linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . Before introducing this sufficient condition, we have to recall
several different automorphism groups of a linear code.
The set of coordinate permutations that map a code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to itself forms a group, where the binary operation
for this group is the function composition. This group
is referred to as the permutation automorphism group of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ 
and denoted by  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ . If the length of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is  $n$  and the coordinates of the codewords in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  are indexed
with the elements in the set  $\{1,2,\ldots,n\}$ , then  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a subgroup of the
symmetric group  ${\mathrm{Sym}}_{n}$ .
A monomial matrix over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is a square matrix that has exactly one
nonzero element of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  in each row and column. It is easily seen that a monomial matrix  $M$  can be written either in
the form  $DP$  or the form  $PD_{1}$ , where  $D$  and  $D_{1}$  are both diagonal matrices and  $P$  is a permutation
matrix. Clearly, the set of monomial matrices that map  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to itself forms a group denoted by  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ , which is called the
monomial automorphism group of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . By definition,
we have
 ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}}).$ 
By definition, every element in  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is of the form  $DP$ , where  $D$  is a diagonal matrix and
 $P$  is a permutation matrix.
An element of this group acts on a codeword  ${\mathbf{c}}$  as
 $(DP)({\mathbf{c}})={\mathbf{c}}DP^{-1}.$ 
For two elements  $(D_{i},P_{i}):=D_{i}P_{i}$  in  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  for  $i\in\{1,2\}$ ,
the corresponding binary operation of the group  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is defined by
 $(D_{2},P_{2})\circ(D_{2},P_{1})=(D_{1}P_{1}^{-1}D_{2}P_{1},P_{2}P_{1}).$ 
The automorphism group of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , denoted by  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ , is the set
of maps of the form  $M\gamma$ ,
where  $M$  is a monomial matrix and  $\gamma$  is a field automorphism, that map  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to itself.
For binary codes  ${\mathcal{C}}$ ,  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ ,  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  and  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  are the same. If  $q$  is a prime,  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  and
 ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  are identical. According to their definitions, we have the following relations:
 ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}}).$ 
By definition, every element in  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is of the form  $DP\gamma$ , where  $D$  is a diagonal matrix,
 $P$  is a permutation matrix, and  $\gamma$  is an automorphism of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ .
An element of this group acts on a codeword  ${\mathbf{c}}$  as
 $(DP\gamma)({\mathbf{c}})=\gamma(({\mathbf{c}}D)P^{-1}).$ 
For any two elements  $(D_{i},P_{i},\gamma_{i}):=D_{i}P_{i}\gamma_{i}$  in  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  for  $i\in\{1,2\}$ ,
the corresponding binary operation of the group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is defined by
 $(D_{2},P_{2},\gamma_{2})\circ(D_{1},P_{1},\gamma_{1})=(D_{1}P_{1}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{-1}(D_{2})P_{1},P_{2}P_{1},\gamma_{2}\circ\gamma_{1}).$ 
The automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is said to be  $t$ -transitive if for every pair of  $t$ -element ordered
sets of coordinates, there is an element  $DP\gamma$  of the automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  such that its
permutation part  $P$  sends the first set to the second set. The automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is said to be  $t$ -homogeneous if for every pair of  $t$ -element
sets of coordinates, there is an element  $DP\gamma$  of the automorphism group  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  such that its
permutation part  $P$  sends the first set to the second set.
With the help of the automorphism group of a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , the following theorem gives another sufficient condition
for the code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  to hold  $t$ -designs.
Theorem 2.
[17, p. 308]
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a linear code of length  $n$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  such that  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is  $t$ -transitive
or  $t$ -homogeneous. Then the codewords of any weight  $i\geq t$  of  ${\mathcal{C}}$  hold a  $t$ -design,
i.e.,  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a  $t$ -design for each  $i\geq t$ .
It is in general very hard to determine the full automorphism group of a linear code.
As long as a  $t$ -transitive or  $t$ -homogeneous subgroup of  ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})$  is found,
one could make use of Theorem 2 to prove the  $t$ -design
property of  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$ .
The third way for proving the  $t$ -design property of an incidence structure  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is the direct approach [11, 25, 29],
where the  $t$ -design property of an incidence structure  $({\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{C}}),{\mathcal{B}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}))$  is proved by verifying the conditions in the definition of  $t$ -designs directly. This direct approach may work only when the block size is very small.
1.2 Lifted linear codes
Let  $q$  be a power of a prime and let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  denote an  $[n,k,d]_{q}$  linear code with generator matrix  $G$ .
For any positive integer  $\ell$ ,  $G$  is also a matrix of rank  $k$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ . Let  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  denote the linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ 
generated by  $G$ , which is called the lifted code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ . It is known that  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  and
 ${\mathcal{C}}$  have the same length, dimension and minimum distance [12, Theorem 7], but they have different weight distributions for  $\ell>1$  (see the examples and some general results in later sections).
Even if the weight
distribution of  ${\mathcal{C}}(q)$  is known, it may be very hard to determine the weight distribution of a lifted code
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  for  $\ell>1$ . By definition,  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is the subfield subcode over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  of the lifted code
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  [17, 20].
1.3 The idea of constructing new designs with lifted codes of a linear code
Suppose that  ${\mathcal{C}}$  supports some nontrivial  $t$ -designs. Let  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  denote the number of codewords
with Hamming weight  $i$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
One or both of the following cases may happen:
1. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{m}))$  is a  $t^{\prime}$ - $(n,i,\lambda^{\prime}_{i})$  design with  $(t^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}_{i})\neq(t,\lambda_{i})$ ,
while  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}})$  is a  $t$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i})$  design for some  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})\neq 0$ .
2. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{m}))$  is a  $t^{\prime}$ - $(n,i,\lambda^{\prime}_{i})$  design with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{m}))\neq 0$ ,
while  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}})=0$ .
If any of the two cases above happens, a new design could be obtained. This idea of obtaining new support
designs from lifted linear codes was considered in [8], where some  $5$ -designs were found.
The following theoretical result will be needed in subsequent sections and is a theoretical foundation of this paper.
Theorem 3.
[8]
Let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be a linear code of length  $n$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ .
Let  $E$  be a subgroup of the monomial automorphism group  ${\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})$ . Assume that  $E$  is  $t$ -transitive
or  $t$ -homogeneous. Then the following hold:
1. 
For each  $e\in E$ ,  $e({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))={\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$ .
2. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))$  is a  $t$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i})$  design with  $A_{i}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))\neq 0$  for any  $i\geq t$ , where  $\lambda_{i}$ 
is an integer.
1.4 Motivations and objectives of this paper
Many infinite families of  $2$ -designs and  $3$ -designs supported by linear
codes are available in the literature [10, 26, 27].
Recently, several infinite
families of  $4$ -designs from linear codes were reported in [25, 29].
No infinite family of linear codes supporting an infinite family of nontrivial simple  $5$ -designs
is known.
It is not easy to construct  $5$ -designs from codes.
A very small number of  $5$ -designs from some linear codes were constructed in [2].
Several  $5$ -designs from some ternary codes were presented in [21].
A very small number of  $5$ -designs from linear codes were reported in
[10, Appendix A]. Several  $5$ -designs from some lifted linear codes
were reported in [8].
Only a small number of specific linear codes supporting  $5$ -designs are
available in the literature. Until now no linear code supporting a nontrivial  $6$ -design is known in the literature.
Of course, there are algebraic, geometric and combinatorial approaches to constructing  $t$ -designs ([1], [4]).
Combinatorists are in general interested only in the following types of  $t$ -designs:
1. 
 $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  designs with large strength  $t$ .
2. 
 $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  designs with small value  $\lambda$ , in particular,  $\lambda=1$ .
3. 
Symmetric  $t$ -designs.
But coding theorists are interested in  $t$ - $(v,k,\lambda)$  designs with small strength  $t$  and large  $\lambda$ , as some  $2$ -designs could be used to construct linear codes with very good parameters [12]. This is the main motivation of obtaining new  $2$ -designs by studying the lifted Hamming and Simplex codes in this paper.
The second motivation of this paper is that two-weight and three-weight linear codes have interesting
applications in association schemes, cryptography and graph theory.
As a follow-up of [8], this paper plans to do the following:
1. 
Add some new fundamental results for lifted linear codes.
2. 
Determine the weight distributions of lifted Hamming codes and lifted Simplex codes.
3. 
Study the  $2$ -designs supported by lifted Hamming codes and lifted Simplex codes.
The contributions of this paper are summarised in Section 5.
2 Fundamental results of lifted linear codes
In this section, we prove some fundamental results for lifted linear codes. The following theorem extends earlier results about lifted linear codes [12].
Theorem 4.
Let  $n$  and  $\ell$  be positive integers and let  ${\mathcal{C}}$  be an  $[n,k,d]_{q}$  linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  with  $k\geq 1$ .
Then the lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  has parameters  $[n,k,d]_{q^{\ell}}$ .
Furthermore, if  $k\geq 2$ ,  $d({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})\geq 3$  and
 $\displaystyle n-\frac{q^{k-2}-1}{q-1}>d,$ 
(1)
then
 $A_{d}({\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell}))=\frac{q^{\ell}-1}{q-1}A_{d}({\mathcal{C}})$ 
and every minimum weight codeword  ${\mathbf{c}}(q|q^{\ell})$  in  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  is of the form
 ${\mathbf{c}}(q|q^{\ell})=u{\mathbf{c}}$ , where  ${\mathbf{c}}$  is a minimum weight codeword in  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and  $u\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{*}$ .
Proof.
Let  $G=[{\mathbf{g}}_{1}\ldots{\mathbf{g}}_{n}]$  be a generator matrix of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , where each  ${\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{T}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{k}$  denotes the transpose of the column vector  ${\mathbf{g}}_{i}$ . It is easily seen that the rank of  $G$  is still equal to  $k$  when it is viewed as a matrix over any extension field  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$ . Consequently, the dimension of the lifted code
 ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  is also  $k$ . Below we prove other desired conclusions.
For each  ${\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{h})$ , where  $h$  is a positive integer, define
 ${\mathrm{HP}}_{({\mathbf{a}},q^{h})}=\{{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{h})^{k}:{\mathbf{b}}{\mathbf{a}}^{T}=0\},$ 
which is called the hyperplane defined by  ${\mathbf{a}}$  when  ${\mathbf{a}}$  is nonzero. Clearly,  ${\mathrm{HP}}_{({\mathbf{0}},q^{h})}={\mathrm{GF}}(q^{h})^{k}$ .
Let  $\gamma$  be a generator of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{*}$ . Each vector  ${\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{k}$  can be expressed as
 ${\mathbf{a}}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{i}\gamma^{i},$ 
where each  ${\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{i}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{k}$ .
By definition, each codeword in the lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  is of the form  ${\mathbf{c}}_{\mathbf{a}}={\mathbf{a}}G$  for some  ${\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{k}$ . Since  $G$  has rank  $k$ ,  ${\mathbf{c}}_{\mathbf{a}}$  runs over each codeword in  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  once when  ${\mathbf{a}}$  runs over each vector in  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})^{k}$  once. It is straightforward to see that
 $\displaystyle{\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}}_{\mathbf{a}})$ 
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|{\mathrm{HP}}_{({\mathbf{a}},q^{\ell})}\cap\{{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{T}\}|$ 
(2)
 $\displaystyle=$ 
 $\displaystyle n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}|{\mathrm{HP}}_{({\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{0},q)}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathrm{HP}}_{({\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{\ell-1},q)}\cap\{{\mathbf{g}}_{i}^{T}\}|.$ 
When  ${\mathbf{a}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}$ , at least one of the vectors in the set  $\{{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\}$ , say  ${\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{j}$ , is not the zero vector. It follows from (2) that
 ${\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}}_{\mathbf{a}})\geq{\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{j}})$ , where  ${\mathbf{c}}_{{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{j}}={\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{j}G$  is a nonzero codeword of  ${\mathcal{C}}$ .
By definition,  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is a subcode of the lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$ . Consequently,  ${\mathcal{C}}$  and the lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  have the same minimum distance.
If  $k\geq 2$ ,  $d({\mathcal{C}}^{\perp})\geq 3$ , and  ${\mathbf{a}}\neq u{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}$  for any  $u\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{\ell})$  and any  ${\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{k}$ , by (2) we have
 $\displaystyle{\mathtt{wt}}({\mathbf{c}}_{\mathbf{a}})$ 
 $\displaystyle=$

3 Projective Reed-Muller codes and their designs
In this section, we introduce the projective Reed-Muller codes and their designs. These results are not new.
But we shall need them later when we study Hamming codes and lifted Hamming codes.
Let  $m\geq 2$  be an integer.
A point of the projective geometry  ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  is given in homogeneous coordinates by  $(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m})$  where all  $x_{i}$  are in  $\mathrm{GF}(q)$  and are not all zero. Each point of  ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  has  $q-1$  coordinate representations, as  $(ax_{1},ax_{2},\dots,ax_{m})$  and  $(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m})$  generate the same  $1$ -dimensional subspace of  $\mathrm{GF}(q)^{m}$  for any nonzero  $a\in\mathrm{GF}(q)$ .
Let  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}]$  be the set of polynomials in  $m$  indeterminates over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ , which is a linear space over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ . Let  $A(q,m,h)$  be the subspace of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}]$  generated by all the homogeneous polynomials of degree  $h$ . Let  $n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$  and let  $\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\mathbf{x}^{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}$  be a set of projective points in  $\mathrm{PG}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ . Then the  $h$ -th order projective Reed-Muller code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  of length  $n$  is defined by
 $\displaystyle\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)=\left\{\left(f(\mathbf{x}^{1}),f(\mathbf{x}^{2}),\dots,f(\mathbf{x}^{n})\right):f\in A(q,m,h)\right\}.$ 
The code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  depends on the choice of the set  $\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\mathbf{x}^{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}$  of coordinate representatives of the point set in  $\mathrm{PG}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ , but is unique up to the monomial equivalence. The parameters of  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  and  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)^{\bot}$  are known and documented in the following theorems [3, 18, 22].
Theorem 6.
Let  $m\geq 2$  and  $1\leq h\leq(m-1)(q-1)$ . Then the linear code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  has length  $n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$  and minimum distance  $(q-v)q^{m-2-u}$ , where  $h-1=u(q-1)+v$  and  $0\leq v<q-1$ . Furthermore,
 $\displaystyle\dim(\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h))=\sum_{t\equiv h\pmod{q-1}\atop 0<t\leq h}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}\binom{m}{j}\binom{t-jq+m-1}{t-jq}\right).$ 
Theorem 7.
Let  $m\geq 2$  and  $1\leq h\leq(m-1)(q-1)$ . If  $h\not\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}$ , then
 $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)^{\bot}=\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-h).$ 
By Theorem 6 and definition,  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,1)$ 
is monomially-equavalent to the Simplex code. The weight distribution of  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,2)$  was settled in [19].
It was pointed out in [6, 22] that the code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,h)$  is not cyclic in general, but is equivalent to
a cyclic code if  $\gcd(m,q-1)=1$  or  $h\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}$ .
For a linear code  ${\mathcal{C}}$ , define
 $\pi{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{P:DP\gamma\in{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}})\}$ 
and
 $\pi{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})=\{P:DP\in{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\}.$ 
By definition
 $\displaystyle\pi{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathcal{C}})\subseteq\pi{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathcal{C}}).$ 
(4)
The following result was proved in [5].
Lemma 8.
Choose the point set  $\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\mathbf{x}^{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}$  of  $\mathrm{PG}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$ 
to index the coordinates of the codewords in  ${\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h)$ . Then
 ${\mathrm{PGL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(q))\subseteq\pi{\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h))\subseteq\pi{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h)),$ 
where  ${\mathrm{PGL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  denotes the projective general linear group [10, Chapter 1].
Theorem 9.
For each positive integer  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h))>0$ , the incidence structure  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,h))$  is a  $2$ - $(n,i,\lambda_{i}$  design for some  $\lambda_{i}$ .
Proof.
It is well known that  ${\mathrm{PGL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  is doubly transitive [10, Chapter 1]. The desired conclusion then follows from
Lemma 8 and Theorem 2.

4 Lifted Simplex and Hamming codes
Throughout this section,  $n=(q^{m-1}-1)/(q-1)$  with  $m\geq 2$  being an integer.
4.1 Hamming and Simplex codes over finite fields
There are several different constructions of the Hamming and Simplex codes. The first one is a trace construction
of the Hamming and Simplex codes. Let  $\alpha$  be a generator of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})^{*}$ .
Define
 $\Delta_{i}:=\alpha^{i}{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}=\{\alpha^{i}a:a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{*}\}$ 
for all  $i$  with  $0\leq i\leq n-1$ . Clearly, the set  $\{\Delta_{i}:0\leq i\leq n-1\}$  forms a partition of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})^{*}$ .
Let  $i$  and  $j$  be a pair of distinct elements in the set  $\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}$ . Then any
 $a\in\Delta_{i}$  and  $b\in\Delta_{j}$  must be linearly independent over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ .
Let  $b_{i}\in\Delta_{i}$  for each  $i$  with  $0\leq i\leq n-1$ . Define
 $\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})=\left\{\left({\mathrm{Tr}}_{q^{m}/q}(ab_{i})\right)_{i=0}^{n-1}:a\in{\mathrm{GF}}(q^{m})\right\}.$ 
(5)
Then the set  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  defined above is a Simplex code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  with parameters
 $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),m,q^{m-1}]$  and weight enumerator  $1+(q^{m}-1)z^{q^{m-1}}$ . By definition, different choices of
the vector  $(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  in the set  $\Delta_{0}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{n-1}$ 
result in monomially-equivalent Simplex codes. It is well known that monomially-equivalent codes have the same parameters and same weight enumerator. Therefore, up to monomial equivalence, Simplex codes are unique, and are
denoted by  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}$  in this paper.
The dual code of any Simplex code  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  is referred to as a Hamming code, denoted by
 ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$ . Thus, all the Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n-1})$  are monomially-equivalent and unique up to monomial equivalence. Hence, we
denote them by  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$ . It is well known that  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  has
parameters  $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)-m,3]$ .
The second construction of the Hamming and Simplex codes is by matrix.
A parity check matrix  $H_{(q,m)}$  of the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ 
is defined by choosing its columns as the nonzero vectors from
all the one-dimensional subspaces of  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)^{m}$ . In terms of finite geometry, the columns of  $H_{(q,m)}$  are the
points of the projective geometry  ${\mathrm{PG}}(m-1,{\mathrm{GF}}(q))$  [10, Section 1.8]. Hence  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  has length  $n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)$  and dimension
 $n-m$ . By definition, no two columns of  $H_{(q,m)}$  are linearly dependent over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$ . The minimum weight of  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$ 
is at least 3. Adding two nonzero vectors from two different one-dimensional subspaces gives a nonzero vector
from a third one-dimensional space. Therefore,  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  has minimum weight 3.
It is also well known that any  $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)-m,3]$  code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is monomially equivalent to the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  [17, Theorem 1.8.2].
The third one is a contacyclic code construction of the Hamming and Simplex codes [23]. The fourth one is the following.
Theorem 10.
Each Simplex code is a projective Reed-Muller code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,1)$  and each Hamming code
is a projective Reed-Muller code  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-1)$ .
Proof.
It is well known that any  $[(q^{m}-1)/(q-1),(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)-m,3]$  linear code over  ${\mathrm{GF}}(q)$  is monomially equivalent to the Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  [17, Theorem 1.8.2].
By Theorem 6,  $\mathrm{PRM}(q,m,(m-1)(q-1)-1)$  has the same parameters as
a Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  and thus they are monomially-equivalent. Note all projective RM codes
are monomially-equivalent and all Hamming codes are monomially-equivalent. The desired second conclusion then
follows.
The desired first conclusion
then follows from Theorem 7.

 ${\mathbb{D}}_{7}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)}(2|2^{2}))$  is a  $2$ - $(15,7,1095)$  design (different from  ${\mathbb{D}}_{7}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)})$ ).
6. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{8}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)}(2|2^{2}))$  is a  $2$ - $(15,8,1684)$  design (different from  ${\mathbb{D}}_{8}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)})$ ).
7. 
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)}(2|2^{2}))$  is a  $2$ - $(15,i,\binom{15}{i})$  design (a complete design) for each  $i$  with  $9\leq i\leq 15$ .
This example shows some differences and similarities of the designs supported by the Hamming code
 ${\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)}$  and
the lifted Hamming code  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(2,4)}(2|2^{2})$ .
Open Problem 24.
Determine the parameters of the  $2$ -designs
in Theorem 22.
5 Summary and concluding remarks
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.
1. 
Certain fundamental results for lifted linear codes were proved in Theorems 4 and 5.
2. 
The weight distributions of the lifted Simplex codes  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were settled in Theorem 14.
3. 
The support  $2$ -designs of the lifted Simplex codes  ${\mathcal{S}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were characterised in Theorem 17.
4. 
The weight distributions of the lifted Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were settled in Theorem 21.
5. 
The support  $2$ -designs of the lifted Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}(q|q^{\ell})$  were characterised
in Theorem 22.
6. 
An infinite family of two-weight projective codes was obtained in Corollary 15.
7. 
An infinite family of three-weight projective codes was obtained in Corollary 16.
The determination of the weight enumerator of a linear code is quite difficult in general. The settlement of
the weight enumerator of a lifted code  ${\mathcal{C}}(q|q^{\ell})$  could be very difficult, even if the weight enumerator of the given code  ${\mathcal{C}}$  is known. The reader is cordially invited to determine the weight enumerator of the lifted projective Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,2)(q|q^{\ell})$  for  $2\leq\ell\leq m$ ,
while the weight enumerator
of  ${\mathrm{PRM}}(q,m,2)$  is known [19].
It is known that the permutation automorphism group  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{H}}_{(2,m)})$  is the general linear group
 ${\mathrm{GL}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(2))$  [16]. It is impossible to characterise the permutation automorphism group  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  for  $q>2$ , as it varies from a Hamming code to another Hamming code of the same
parameters, although all Hamming codes  ${\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)}$  are monomially-equivalent. In a special case, the
permutation automorphism group  ${\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathcal{H}}_{(q,m)})$  for  $q>2$  was settled [14].
Finally, we mention that many infinite families of  $3$ -designs can be obtained by studying the lifted
codes of the binary Reed-Muller codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)$  of order  $r$  for each  $1\leq r<m-1$ . Notice that
 ${\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m))={\mathrm{MAut}}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m))={\mathrm{PAut}}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m))={\mathrm{GA}}_{m}({\mathrm{GF}}(2)),$ 
which is the general affine group and triply transitive acting on  ${\mathrm{GF}}(2)^{m}$  [10]. It then follows from Theorem
3 that  ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))$  is a  $3$ -design for each  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))>0$ . Thus, we have proved the following theorem.
Theorem 25.
Let  $m\geq 3$  and  $\ell\geq 1$ . For each  $i$  with  $A_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))>0$ ., the incidence structure
 ${\mathbb{D}}_{i}({\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell}))$  is a  $3$ - $(2^{m},i,\lambda_{i})$  design for some positive integer  $\lambda_{i}$ .
Open Problem 26.
Determine the values  $\lambda_{i}$  for the  $3$ - $(2^{m},i,\lambda_{i})$  designs in Theorem 25.
Open Problem 27.
Determine the weight enumerators of the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  for  $1\leq r\leq m-2$  and  $2\leq\ell\leq m$ .
It is feasible to determine the weight enumerator of the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(1,m)(2|2^{\ell})$ ,
but would be very hard to do this for the lifted codes  ${\mathrm{RM}}_{2}(r,m)(2|2^{\ell})$  for  $2\leq r\leq m-2$  and  $2\leq\ell\leq m$ .
