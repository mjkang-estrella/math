                                                      CYCLIC CYCLE DECOMPOSITIONS




arXiv:1709.05812v1 [math.CO] 18 Sep 2017
                                                        S. COSTA, F. MORINI, A. PASOTTI, AND M.A. PELLEGRINI


                                                   Abstract. Two k-cycle decompositions C and C ′ of a graph Γ are said to be
                                                   orthogonal if any cycle C ∈ C intersects any cycle C ′ ∈ C ′ in at most one
                                                   edge. In this paper we introduce a particular class of Heffter arrays, called
                                                   globally simple Heffter arrays, whose existence gives at once orthogonal cyclic
                                                   cycle decompositions of the complete graph and of the cocktail party graph. In
                                                   particular we provide explicit constructions of such decompositions for cycles
                                                   of length k ≤ 10.

   In [1] many applications of (simple) Heffter arrays are shown. In this paper
we are interested in the connection with orthogonal cyclic cycle decompositions
of the complete graph and of the cocktail party graph. Very few is known about
orthogonal decompositions; as far as we know, only asymptotic results have been
obtained, see [8, 9]. In Section 2 we will explain such relationship between globally
simple Heffter arrays and orthogonal cyclic cycle decompositions. Then in Section
3 we will present direct constructions of SH(n; k) for 6 ≤ k ≤ 10 and for any
admissible n. Combining the results of Sections 2 and 3 we obtain the following
theorem.
Theorem 1.8. Let 3 ≤ k ≤ 10. Then there exists a pair of orthogonal cyclic
k-cycle decompositions of the complete graph of order 2nk + 1 and of the cocktail
party graph of order 2nk + 2 for any integer n ≥ k such that nk ≡ 0, 3 (mod 4).

                  2. Orthogonal cyclic cycle decompositions
   Firstly, we recall some basic definitions about graph decompositions. Let Γ be
a graph with v vertices. A k-cycle decomposition of Γ is a set C of k-cycles with
vertices in Γ such that each edge of Γ belongs to a unique cycle of C. A k-cycle
decomposition of Γ is said to be cyclic if it admits Zv as automorphism group acting
sharply transitively on the vertices. We recall the following result.
Proposition 2.1. Let Γ be a graph with v vertices. A k-cycle decomposition C
of Γ is sharply vertex-transitive under Zv if and only if, up to isomorphisms, the
following conditions hold:
      • the set of vertices of Γ is Zv ;
      • for all C = (c1 , c2 , . . . , ck ) ∈ C, also C + 1 := (c1 + 1, c2 + 1, . . . , ck + 1) ∈ C.
   Clearly, to describe a cyclic k-cycle decomposition it is sufficient to exhibit a
complete system B of representatives for the orbits of C under the action of Zv .
The elements of B are called base cycles of C.
   Here we are interested in the cases in which Γ is either the complete graph Kv
whose vertex-set is Zv or the cocktail party graph K2t − I, namely the complete
graph K2t minus the 1-factor I whose edges are [0, t], [1, t + 1], [2, t + 2], . . . , [t −
1, 2t − 1]. The problem of finding necessary and sufficient conditions for cyclic
k-cycle decompositions of Kv and K2t − I has attracted much attention see, for
instance, [6, 21, 22] and [5, 7, 18, 19], respectively. One of most efficient tools
applied for solving this problem is the difference method.
Definition 2.2. Let C = (c1 , c2 , . . . , ck ) be a k-cycle with vertices in Zv . The
multiset
                        ∆C = {±(ch+1 − ch ) | 1 ≤ h ≤ k},
where the subscripts are taken modulo k, is called the list of differences from C.

   More generally, given a set B of k-cycles with vertices in Zv , by ∆B one means
the union (counting multiplicities) of all multisets ∆C, where C ∈ B.
Theorem 2.3. Let B be a set of k-cycles with vertices in Zv .
   (1) If ∆B = Zv \ {0} then B is a set of base cycles of a cyclic k-cycle decom-
       position of Kv .
   (2) If v = 2t and ∆B = Z2t \ {0, t} then B is a set of base cycles of a cyclic
       k-cycle decomposition of K2t − I.
   Here, we are interested in constructing pairs of orthogonal k-cycle decompositions
according to the following definition.
Definition 2.4. Two k-cycle decompositions C and C ′ of a graph Γ are said to be
orthogonal if for any cycle C ∈ C and any cycle C ′ ∈ C ′ , C intersects C ′ in at most
one edge.
Clearly, the same definition can be given for two arbitrary graph decompositions.
   Starting from a simple H(n; k) it is possible to construct two orthogonal cyclic
k-cycle decompositions of K2nk+1 , see [1, Proposition 2.1]. Firstly, we have to find
a simple ordering modulo 2nk + 1 for each row and each column. Then starting
from the simple orderings of the rows we can construct a set B of base cycles of a
cyclic k-cycle decomposition C of K2nk+1 . The vertices of the i-th cycle of B are
the partial sums modulo 2nk + 1 of the i-th row of H(n; k). Analogously, we can
obtain a set of base cycles B ′ of another cyclic k-cycle decomposition C ′ of K2nk+1
starting from the simple orderings of the columns. The decompositions C and C ′
result to be orthogonal.
