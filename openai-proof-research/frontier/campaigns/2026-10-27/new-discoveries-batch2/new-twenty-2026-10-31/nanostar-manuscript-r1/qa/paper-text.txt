 Abstract. We give an explicit positive-integer labeling of the four-stage nanostar tree as an iterated
 Euler-totient graph. Its orbit graph has 88 vertices and centre 1024. This refutes the final nonexistence
 conjecture of Ghanbari and Alikhani at stage 4, under the contracted-hexagon interpretation of the
 nanostar recursion. That interpretation is supported by the published figure and the cited
 Deutsch-Klavzar construction; it is not an explicit contraction definition in the source. A complete
 arithmetic table, independent finite verification, and a byte-identical previously checked Lean packet
 accompany this draft. The contribution is a modest exact counterexample found by routine
 inverse-totient backtracking.

 1. Definitions and the source conjecture
 For a positive integer n let phi(n) be Euler's totient, with phi(1) = 1. Define phi^0(n) = n and
 phi^(j+1)(n) = phi(phi^j(n)). For a set A of positive integers put A_phi = {phi^j(a) : a in A, j >= 0}. In
 particular, iteration zero is included, and 1 belongs to every nonempty orbit set. Let G_phi(A) have
 vertex set A_phi and unordered edges {n, phi(n)} with n in A_phi and n != phi(n). We omit the loop at 1
 and forget direction, in accordance with the source's simple-graph convention and explicit undirected
 remark [1, p. 265].

 A graph H is a G_phi-graph when it equals G_phi(A) after labeling its vertices, equivalently when it is
 isomorphic to G_phi(A). This explains the source's notation H = G_phi(A). Although the source allows
 arbitrary sets A, a finite realization has A finite because A is contained in A_phi. Thus finite positive
 witnesses suffice here.

 The published final conjecture [1, p. 271], quoted verbatim with mathematical notation rendered as
 G_phi, is: “There is no nanostar molecule which has grown at least four stages, as a G_phi-graph.”
 The preprint uses the title A graph related to Euler phi function; the published title inserts “the.” The
 MathDB record 349177 paraphrases “dendrimer nanostar”; the exact frozen raw line and its hash are in
 the dossier.

 Immediately before the conjecture, Theorem 11 states: “The graph D_2 is a G_phi-graph.” Its proof
 gives A = {3, 256, 376, 384, 564}. We reproduce and check this control, rather than infer impossibility
 at stage 3 from the conjecture's stage threshold. The preprint numbers the corresponding theorem and
 conjecture 3.4 and 3.5.

 2. Which nanostar tree is meant?
 Deutsch and Klavzar [2, Section 4.3] define a rooted graph F using two hexagons and endpoint edges,
 G_1 using a hexagon and a pendant root edge, G_k as a bouquet of G_(k-1), G_(k-1), and F at their
 roots, and D_k as a bouquet of three G_k. Here bouquet means identification of the specified roots; G_k
 is rooted at its remaining unique leaf. We interpret the tree structure by contracting each hexagon to one
 vertex, retaining junction vertices and intervening edges. Published Figure 10 [1, p. 271] and Figure 12
 [2, p. 639] have the same contracted skeleton. In the latter the circles represent hexagons. Frozen page
 images permit direct comparison.

 This contraction is our stated interpretation, not a sentence attributed to [1]. A cyclic molecular reading
 cannot give the intended G_phi-graph: nonempty positive-integer orbit graphs are trees. Indeed phi(n) <
 n for n > 1, and every vertex reaches 1. In a purported cycle its largest label would have two smaller
 neighbors, but only its single totient parent can be smaller. In particular, keeping molecular hexagons
 would already contradict Theorem 11. The distinct D_3[n] family in [3] is not silently substituted for the
 D_k recursion.

 After contraction, F is a rooted path of length 3 and G_1 is a rooted edge. Removing the common central
 root gives the following recursive arms: arm(0) is one vertex; arm(j+1) starts with two unary path
 vertices, followed by a branching vertex carrying two disjoint copies of arm(j). For k >= 1, D_k consists
 of a new centre joined to the roots of three copies of arm(k-1). This is precisely the family used below
 and in the formal definitions.

 If a_j is the order of arm(j), then a_0 = 1 and a_(j+1) = 3 + 2a_j, so a_j = 4*2^j - 3. Hence |D_k| = 1 +
 3a_(k-1) = 6*2^k - 8. In D_4 branching occurs at distances 3, 6, and 9 from the centre; leaves occur at
 distance 10. The distance-layer profile is 1, 3, 3, 3, 6, 6, 6, 12, 12, 12, 24.

 3. Explicit refutation and verification
 Theorem. Under this contracted-hexagon interpretation, D_4 is a G_phi-graph. Consequently the
 assertion that no D_k with k >= 4 is realizable is false.

 Take the following 23 generators A (all positive):
 {3, 167, 334, 19577, 31657, 39154, 63314, 702085, 983055, 1048576, 1123336, 1975461, 2370559, 2633948, 3352289,
 3561823, 4182461, 4741118, 5019445, 5866457, 8031112, 8364922, 11732914}.
 Proof. Tables 1a and 1b give a set V of distinct labels and every totient value, computed by phi(n) = product of p^(e-1)(p-1)
 over the prime powers p^e in n. Each factorization is prime-certified by trial division. Every printed parent is in V; phi(1) = 1 is
 the only omitted loop. Following these parents from the listed generators visits exactly V. Thus V = A_phi, not merely a closed
 superset, and the table determines every edge of G_phi(A).

 4. Formal statement, bridge, and audit scope
 The reused source packet formalizes R162.Tree with leaf, unary, binary, and ternary constructors.
 R162.size and R162.edges give disjoint preorder-indexed vertices and edges; treeGraph is the associated
 loopless SimpleGraph. R162.arm follows the recurrence in Section 2; nanostar k is ternary (arm (k-1))
 (arm (k-1)) (arm (k-1)). Only k >= 4 is quantified in the conjecture, so natural-number subtraction at
 zero is immaterial.

 R162.orbit A = {m : there exist a in A and j in N with (Nat.totient^[j]) a = m}. The adjacency of
 phiGraph A is u != v and (phi(u) = v or phi(v) = u). R162.Realizable t asserts existence of a Finset of
 positive natural numbers A and a graph isomorphism treeGraph t to phiGraph A. The exact proposition
 is:

 not Realizable (nanostar k)

 The bridge realizes_of_label takes an injective positive labeling, closure under totient, and an adjacency
 equivalence for every vertex pair. It uses all labels as the formal generator set. Induction on the iteration
 index proves that the orbit is exactly the label image; injectivity and surjectivity yield the graph
 isomorphism. This differs from the smaller 23-generator set only as a presentation of the same orbit

 5. Controls, significance, and literature limits
 The fresh checker verifies the source's D_2 witness {3, 256, 376, 384, 564}: 16 vertices, 15 edges,
 centre 16, maximum label 564, layers 1, 3, 3, 3, 6. The earlier discovery independent-control file has
 maximum 334 for its own different stage-2 witness. These are compatible, not conflicting computations
 of the same input.

 As supporting data, the stage-3 generators are {3, 167, 334, 13107, 16384, 16449, 19577, 21932, 31657,
 39154, 63314}. Their orbit gives 40 vertices, 39 edges, centre 128, maximum label 63314, and layers 1,
 3, 3, 3, 6, 6, 6, 12. This independently verified control is not needed for the refutation and is not a
 separate contribution. The source neither proves nor excludes stage 3.

 Not every tree is realizable. In K_(1,5), the vertex 1 would be a leaf and its neighbor would be 2: among
 positive integers other than 1 only 2 has totient 1. But phi(x) = 2 has exactly the solutions 3, 4, 6. To see
 exhaustion, every prime divisor p of x has p-1 dividing 2, so x = 2^a 3^b; the totient product then gives
 just those cases. Thus 2 has at most four neighbors, including 1, which excludes K_(1,5), as in [1,
 Theorem 5]. Large fibers at unspecified targets do not prescribe a compatible iterated tree rooted at 1.

 The significance is modest: routine inverse-totient backtracking found an explicit counterexample to a
 published nonexistence conjecture. No general realization method, minimality, uniqueness, higher-stage
 theorem, or lower bound on search difficulty is claimed. Theorem 11 gives a single D_2 labeling, not an
 algorithm for arbitrary stages. The source's caterpillar and alkane constructions do not directly label this
 branching pattern.

 The retained bounded literature review inspected the primary source, its published successor, the
 accessible chemical references, and the primary statements of cached totient-asymptotic and
 weighted-dilation/fiber manuscripts. The latter concern totient counts or large fibers, not the prescribed
 finite orbit geometry. Registry and title screens found no matching refutation in their stated scope; they
