Each attachment adds a fresh vertex by exactly one edge, preserving connectedness and absence of cycles. Thus T0 is a tree. Its only internal vertices u,v have degrees 3,4. Form T1 by attachments su,uz,uv,va,vw,wb,wc in that order, each fresh endpoint other than its parent. This is likewise a tree; u,v,w have degrees 3,3,3. All terminal vertices have degree 1, and these are exactly the leaves. Orientation away from s gives u children z,v in both trees; v children a,b,c in T0, and a,w in T1; w children b,c in T1. Hence no suppressed degree-two vertices, and every internal vertex has at least two children. All lengths are positive finite; both minima are exactly 1, witnessed by su. ## RNJ lower guarantee reconstructed from actual v1 proofs

Put epsilon=max terminal error < Delta/4 with 0<Delta<=minimum true edge. For distinct i,j,k, q(i,j)=(d(s,i)+d(s,j)-d(i,j))/2 equals true root-to-LCA depth. In qhat(i,j)-qhat(i,k), the s-i error cancels. The remaining four errors each have coefficient magnitude 1/2, so the difference error has absolute value at most 2epsilon<Delta/2. If LCA(i,j) is a proper descendant of LCA(i,k), their depth gap is at least Delta; therefore the estimated difference is >Delta/2. If the LCAs agree, the absolute estimated difference is <Delta/2. These prove both clauses of Lemma 2 condition (36), including the non-strict second clause.

Here is the induction omitted by the source outline. Current active leaves stand for disjoint contracted rooted subtrees. A maximizer pair must be siblings: otherwise one endpoint has its parent strictly below their LCA, and another leaf below a different child of that parent has a deeper LCA with that endpoint, contradicting the strict first clause. Given siblings i,j with parent f, any other active leaf k either has parent f, giving equal LCAs and difference at most Delta/2, or lies outside f, giving the proper-descendant difference >Delta/2. Thus step 2.3 selects exactly all remaining leaf children of f. Contract all these children to f. The surviving graph still has no internal unary nodes: f is now a leaf, while its parent has the same number of children. Step 2.4 makes qhat(k,f)=(qhat(k,i)+qhat(k,j))/2; unselected sibling children have been removed and never need contribute to that average.

A full comparison invariant is easiest expressed without assuming independently bounded errors on synthetic distance entries. Each active node A carries a probability weight on its original terminal descendants; initial weights are point masses, and the new f weight is half the i weight plus half the j weight (other removed children receive zero). For two different active nodes, their score is the weighted average of the original qhat scores. For any triple A,B,C, the comparison is consequently a convex combination, with common A representative, of original differences qhat(a,b)-qhat(a,c). Their true LCAs have the same relation as those of A,B,C: disjoint active rooted subtrees contain neither other active nodes nor their external LCAs. Thus equal comparisons remain bounded by 2epsilon<Delta/2 and proper-descendant comparisons remain >Delta/2; no accumulating synthetic error has been assumed. This proves preservation of (36) and its stronger strict equal case. Each iteration reduces the finite active set; when one node remains it is attached to s, recovering the final source edge. The initial two-destination case and multifurcation with all children removed satisfy the same argument. Internal parent f may be absent temporarily from the displayed V until its later attachment; the final graph has every such parent as a vertex. Output lengths are immaterial to the promised correct topology under noisy input. In exact input they follow the displayed depth-difference formulas.

Consequently the actual Algorithm 3, with supplied Delta not exceeding the true minimum, returns the correct topology for epsilon<Delta/4, for every tie choice.
