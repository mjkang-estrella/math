## Exact endpoint and preserved certificate

Let V be the RIGHT quaternionic space H², with matrices acting on the left. Put D(q)=diag(q,q) for q in Q8; c=−1/2; s=√3/2; R=((c,−s),(s,c)); H0=D(Q8); and G={D(q)R^a : q∈Q8, a∈{0,1,2}}. The independently reviewed endpoint is a finite unitary actual matrix subgroup G of order 24, quaternionically irreducible on all nonzero proper right subspaces, with H0 of order 8 maximal among ALL reducible subgroups, and infinitely many distinct H0-invariant quaternionic lines.

The full independent proof is frozen in ../quaternionic-lines-source-review-r1/REVIEW.md. Its essential bridges are: real R commutes with D(Q8), R³=I, its nontrivial powers have nonzero off-diagonal entries, so the 24 matrices are distinct; every nonzero proper right subspace of H² is a line; H0-invariant lines are precisely (1,t)H for real t and the vertical line, by the simultaneous i,j centralizer calculation. R preserves none of those lines since invariance forces s(1+t²)=0; it also moves the vertical line. Every subgroup strictly between H0 and G would contain R or R² and hence all of G. Finally n↦(1,n)H is injective into actual invariant subspaces. These are semantic subspace/group assertions, not a formal-pair proxy.

## Actual classical theorem comparison

**Read body:** Alex Bartel, *Further Representation Theory*, notes by Florian Bouyer, copyright 2014, publicly hosted at Warwick: https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/maths/people/staff/fbouyer/representation_theory.pdf . PDF SHA256 233e6d5ad54575077f883c3e59d17b2a105a40d1bd7762a65c62a004ee7afba6. Read selected module/Schur discussion pp.3–5 and Theorem 1.4 with its proof p.12. This is an independent source, not an alternate route to the denied MIT notes.

Schur's lemma says a homomorphism between simple A-modules is zero or invertible; consequently End_A(S) is a division ring. The notes also identify End_A(S⊕…⊕S) with M_n(End_A(S)). Applied to the right-H-linear H0-module S=H on which Q8 acts by left multiplication, a right-linear endomorphism has the form x↦a x. Commuting with left i and j forces a∈R. Thus End_H0(S)=R and End_H0(S⊕S)=M₂(R). Graphs of real scalar intertwiners yield the infinitely many invariant right lines. This explains the classical multiplicity mechanism, but Schur's lemma does not select a finite irreducible overgroup or establish the subgroup interval/maximality.

Theorem 1.4 assumes a finite group G, normal subgroup N and a simple F[G]-module for a FIELD F. It concludes that restriction is a direct sum of conjugate simple F[N]-modules with equal multiplicities. Its proof uses Frobenius reciprocity and semisimplicity of submodules. It is not an irreducibility converse and does not say that any prescribed repeated N-module admits a finite irreducible extension. H is not a field, so the theorem cannot be applied verbatim with F=H. Taking a real or complex restriction requires separate semantic work: quaternionic irreducibility is not simplicity of that restricted representation. In particular R has two complex eigenspaces after scalar restriction, so complex irreducibility cannot silently replace the quaternionic assertion. Even a suitable division-ring extension of Clifford restriction would still leave construction and maximality to establish. No complete exclusion bridge follows from this read theorem alone.

**Read frozen companion:** Waldron, *The geometry of the six quaternionic equiangular lines in H²* (author PDF November 25, 2024), Section 6 pp.15ff. The actual construction requires a one-dimensional irreducible H-submodule of multiplicity ONE. Here H0 has two equivalent copies of S, so that sufficient criterion does not apply. Its failure neither proves the counterexample nor supplies the finite overgroup. This corrects the invalid shortcut “not multiplicity one, therefore already subsumed.” Source Example 5.3 likewise has a continuous family inside a NONMAXIMAL reducible subgroup and does not supply the missing maximality step.
