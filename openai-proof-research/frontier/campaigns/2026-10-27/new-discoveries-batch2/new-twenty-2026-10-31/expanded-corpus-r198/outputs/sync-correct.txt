                                                                                           Abstract
                                                    Generalising the work of Dey, we define the notion of ultra-synchronicity of sequences
                                                of real numbers. Let Bn,k , Cn,k , Pn,k , Qn,k be the number of even permutations with k
                                                descents, odd permutations with k descents, even permutations with k excedances and
                                                odd permutations with k excedances respectively. We show that the four sequences are
                                                ultra-synchronised for all n ≥ 5. This proves a strengthening of two conjectures of Dey.

                                          Keyword: alternating group, real-rootedness, descent, excedance, eulerian numbers, log-concavity


                                          1 Introduction
                                          A sequence (ak )nk=1 of reals is said to be log-concave if for each 2 ≤ i ≤ n − 1, we have
                                          a2i ≥ ai+1 ai−1 . We refer the reader to the surveys of Stanley [9] and Branden [1] that showcase
                                          the ubiquity of these sequences in combinatorics and geometry and the variety of methods
                                          developed to prove log-concavity. For a positive integer n, denote by [n] the set {1, 2, . . . , n}
                                          and let Sn be the set of permutations on [n]. For π = π1 π2 . . . πn , define its descent set as
                                          DES(π) = {i ∈ [n − 1] : πi > πi+1 } and the descent number as des(π) = | DES(π)|.
                                          Define the set of excedances as EXC(π) = {i ∈ [n] : πi > i} and the number of excedances
                                          as exc(π) = | EXC(π)|. We denote the number of permutations in Sn with k descents by
                                          A (n, k) and the number of permutations in Sn with k excedances by E(n, k). It is well known
                                          result of MacMahon [6] that the descents and excedances are equidistributed over Sn , i.e.,
                                          A (n, k) = E(n, k). Define the Eulerian polynomial, An (t), by

Qn,k be number of even and odd permutations in Sn with k excedances respectively.
Conjecture 21. The polynomials ni=0 Pn,k tk and ni=0 Qn,k tk are all real-rooted for n ≥ 5.
