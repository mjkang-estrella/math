                                                                          Normal Form for GLT Sequences
                                                                                           Giovanni Barbarino
                                                                                           December 14, 2024

                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                     The algebra structure of the Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) sequences and symbols allows us to
                                                 quickly compute asymptotic distributions of eigenvalues and singular values, starting from the study of
                                                 simpler sequences. For matrices with finite dimensions, such results are in general not possible to obtain,




arXiv:1805.08708v2 [math.NA] 26 Jun 2018
                                                 since all the informations are asymptotic on the size, except for normal matrices. We want to justify the
                                                 behaviour of GLT sequences, introducing a normal form through the approximating classes of sequences
                                                 identification. Moreover, we formulate a conjecture on the uniqueness of this kind of structure, enticing that
                                                 all algebras of sequences and symbols embed into the GLT space.

                                           1    Introduction
                                           The theory of Generalized Locally Toeplitz (GLT) sequences is a powerful tool to obtain spectral information
                                           on linear systems derived from linear PDEs. In fact, it has been observed that many discretization methods
                                           applied to linear Partial Differential Equations lead to linear combinations of products of diagonal and Toeplitz
                                           sequences that possess known symbols. The GLT theory provides
                                               • A canonical symbol for diagonal and Toeplitz sequences, called GLT symbol;
                                               • Perturbation results that let us assert whenever the GLT symbol is also a spectral symbol;
                                               • Composition rules that assign GLT symbols to sequences in the algebra generated by diagonal and Toeplitz
                                                 sequences.
                                           We refer to [10],[11] for a complete exposition of the theory, and to [1],[2],[4] for extra informations. To explain
                                           the aim of this dissertation, let us first recall some notations and essential infos.
                                              Let E be the set of complex matrix sequences with increasing size

                                                                                         E := {{An }n |An ∈ Cn×n }.

                                           This set is in particular an algebra, when equipped with the usual component-wise sum and product of matrices

                                                                    {An }n · {Bn }n := {An Bn }n       {An }n + {Bn }n := {An + Bn }n .

                                           The sequence of identity matrices {In }n and the sequence of zero matrices {0n }n are the identity and zero
                                           elements of E , that is thus a non-commutative algebra with identity. The only sequences we care about are
                                           the ones that possess a singular value symbol, that is a measurable function f : D → C where D ⊆ Rn is a
                                           measurable set with non-zero Lebesgue measure, and describes the asymptotic behaviour of singular values in
                                           the sequences. In this case, we write {An }n ∼σ f . In the case the function describes the behaviour of the
                                           eigenvalues we call it spectral symbol and denote it by {An }n ∼λ f . Usually, a sequence {An }n may admit
                                           several different symbols, so we are interested to perform a meaningful choice depending on the structure of the
                                           matrices An .
                                              The algebra of GLT sequences is a special subalgebra G ⊆ E where every sequence in G possesses a singular
                                           value symbol over the domain D = [0, 1] × [−π, π]. Moreover, we couple every GLT sequence {An }n with one


                                                                                                       1
of its singular value symbols k over D, called GLT symbol of the sequence, and we denote it by {An }n ∼GLT k.
The algebra structure of GLT sequences induces an algebra structure on the GLT symbols, in particular

                                         {An }n ∼GLT f,       {Bn }n ∼GLT g

                               =⇒ {An + Bn }n ∼GLT f + g,         {An Bn }n ∼GLT f g,
meaning that sum and product of GLT sequences have the sum and product of GLT symbols as GLT symbols,
and in particular, as singular value symbols. This behaviour is quite surprising, and has an analogous in finite
size only when dealing with diagonal matrices, or equivalently, normal matrices that have the same unitary
diagonalizing base change.
   We want to show that the axioms of algebras on the GLT symbols are a natural consequence of the results
on normal matrices in the finite size setting. In fact for any GLT sequence we can find an equivalent sequence
in the algebra with the same symbol, composed only by normal matrices. Moreover, all the normal sequences
found can be diagonalized simultaneously through the same sequence of unitary base-change. All of this is
expressed in the following theorem.
Theorem 1. For every {An }n ∼GLT k there exists a sequence {QH
                                                             n DA,n Qn }n ∼GLT k where

    • DA,n are complex diagonal matrices with {DA,n }n ∼λ k,
    • Qn are unitary matrices that do not depend on {An }n .
   Actually, we will show that the matrices DA,n are built in order to contain an ordered approximation of the
sampling of the symbol k over a fixed domain D. Notice that an unitary transformation does not change the
eigenvalues and the spectral symbol, so {QH n DA,n Qn }n ∼λ k. We call this kind of sequence a Normal Form for
a GLT sequence.
   In a precedent paper [1], we showed that the GLT algebra cannot be expanded anymore. In fact, if {An }n ∼σ k
and it is compatible with all GLT sequences, meaning that

                          {Bn }n ∼GLT h =⇒ {An + Bn }n ∼σ h + k               ∀{Bn }n ∈ G ,

then {An }n was already in G . Theorem (1) let us formalize a new conjecture regarding the maximality of G
with respect to any other algebra that is possible to extract from E . Let MD be the set of complex-valued
measurable functions over D (up to almost everywhere equivalence).
Conjecture 1. Consider any subalgebra C ⊆ E and any map s : C → MD such that
    • {An }n ∈ C =⇒ {An }n ∼σ s({An }n ),
    • {An }n , {Bn }n ∈ C ,   s({An }n ) = f,   s({Bn }n ) = g

                                   =⇒ s({An + Bn }n ) = f + g,       s({An Bn }n ) = f g.

There exists a sequence of unitary matrices {Un }n such that

                                  {UnH An Un }n ∼GLT s({An }n )      ∀{An }n ∈ C .

   This last conjecture, together with Theorem 1, implies that any other algebra with the same properties of
the GLT algebra admits a normal form, meaning that the only kind of sequences that are allowed to induce an
algebra structure to the space of symbols are the normal sequences, as the finite dimensional setting seems to
suggest.

2.3    GLT Algebra
Let us denote by CD the set of couples ({An }n , k) ∈ E ×MD such that {An }n ∼σ k and where D = [0, 1]×[−π, π].
First of all we can see that it is well defined, because from the definition, if k, k ′ are two measurable functions
that coincide almost everywhere, then

                                         {An }n ∼σ k ⇐⇒ {An }n ∼σ k ′ ,

   The set of couples of GLT sequences and symbols G is a subset of CD , so when we say that {An }n is a GLT
sequence with symbol k and we write {An }n ∼GLT k, it means that ({An }n , k) ∈ G and in particular, it means
that {An }n ∼σ k. The set of GLT sequences is denoted with G .
   The GLT set is built so that for every {An }n ∈ E there exists at most one function k such that {An }n ∼GLT k,
but not every sequence in E is a GLT sequence. To understand what is the GLT space, we have to start
introducing its fundamental bricks, that are the already mentioned Toeplitz, diagonal and zero-distributed
sequences
   • Given a function f in L1 ([−π, π]), the GLT symbol of its associated Toeplitz sequence is f (θ) itself

                                                  {Tn (f )}n ∼GLT f (θ).

   • Given any a.e. continuous function a : [0, 1] → C, the GLT symbol of its associated diagonal sampling
     sequence is a(x)
                                              {Dn (a)}n ∼GLT a(x).

   • Any zero-distributed sequence {Zn }n has 0 as GLT symbol.

                                                       {Zn }n ∼GLT 0.

Notice that the GLT symbols are measurable functions k(x, θ) on the domain D, where x ∈ [0, 1], θ ∈ [−π, π].
Using these ingredients, we can build the GLT space through the algebra composition rules, and the acs
convergence. The uniqueness of the GLT symbol let us define a map

that associates to each sequence its GLT symbol

3     Normal GLT sequences
We want to show that every GLT sequence is acs equivalent to a sequence of normal matrices. In particular,
we will show that given a GLT sequence with symbol k, it is acs equivalent to a normal sequence that has k as
spectral symbol, called Normal Form.
   The proof will be constructive, in the sense that we will show it first for simple sequences, their composition,
and then we will generate all GLT sequences with Lemma 3.
   Let us start from the fundamental blocks of GLT theory: Toeplitz, diagonal and zero-distributed sequences.
Diagonal matrices are already normal matrices, and by Lemma 4, the sequence of normal matrices {0n }n is acs
equivalent to any zero-distributed sequence.
   In the next subsections, we show that also Toeplitz sequences can be rewritten in a normal form, and how
we can find an equivalent normal form also for linear composition and product of sequences.

3.4    Equivalence with Diagonal Algebra
We already know that G is the closure, with respect to the acs distance over E , of the algebra generated by
{Dn (a)}n and {Tn (f )}n where a ∈ C ∞ [0, 1] and f is a trigonometrical polynomial (actually, the functions
fn = exp(inθ) are sufficient). The choice of these particular generators is due to our knowledge about their
symbols.
   In the previous proof we showed that the sequences {LCn (a, 1)}n and {LCn (1, f )}n are also sufficient to
generate the same space, since they are equivalent to the previous generators. Here we report some of the
already discussed benefits to using such a set of generators:
   • The LC sequences are explicitly built using normal matrices whose eigenvalues, when diagonalized with
     a known unitary base change, are sorted according to the sampling of the symbols over a regular grid.
     Moreover, the base change is the same for every sequence.
   • Every sequence in the closure of the generated algebra can be expressed only by normal matrices, with
     the same diagonalizing base change, that sorts the eigenvalues according to an approximated sampling of
     the associated symbol.
   • Having the same diagonalizing base change, it is straightforward to compute the symbols of products and
     sums of matrices, since we can focus directly only on the eigenvalues.
The downsides are described in [16], where it is discussed why we use GLT and not GLC (Generalized Locally
Circulant) sequences. In fact, it is easy to describe a Toeplitz sequence {Tn (f )}n associated to f (θ) ∈ L1 [−π, π],
whereas a circulant sequence {Cn (f )}n associated to f can only be generated through acs convergence of simpler
circulant sequences, or through the Frobenius optimal circulant approximation [8], that requires a more involved
analysis.
   The analysis done in the previous sections is useful, though, because hints the existence for every algebra of
sequences and symbols of a "Normal Form", that is an equivalent normal sequence that can be diagonalized
through the same unitary base change. The diagonalization let us draw a connection between GLT sequences
and more simple diagonal sequences with one dimensional symbols.
   We have seen that, up to acs equivalence, every GLT sequence can be diagonalized with an unique unitary
base change. Elementary operations (sum, product, conjugation) work with GLT sequences and symbols because
they can be checked on their diagonalized sequences. We can thus try to embed the GLT space into a space of
simpler sequences, generated only by diagonal sequences associated to continuous functions a : [0, 1] → C.

4.2    Maximality
As we have seen, the strange behaviour of GLT sequences is explained through the deployment of a ’Normal
Form’, and it has been stressed that all the normal sequences can be diagonalized through the same set of
unitary matrices. It means that, up to an unitary base change, we are actually dealing with diagonal matrices,
that obviously have tons of good properties.
   In [1] we studied the maximality of the GLT algebra among other similar structures. Here we rephrase part
of the argument and results using our notations. Remember that we have already denoted the GLT algebra in
two different but equivalent ways:
   • The GLT algebra G is an algebra contained in the space CD made of the couples sequences-functions
     ({An }n , k) such that {An }n ∼σ k. In particular it is a subalgebra of E × MD .
   • The algebra of GLT sequences G is a subalgebra of E equipped with a function S : G → MD that
     associates to each sequence an unique measurable function, and that is a surjective homomorphism of
     algebras and isometry of pseudometric spaces.
Given any B ⊆ E and a map s : B → MD , we call s a Symbol Map if

                                     {An }n ∼σ s({An }n )     ∀{An }n ∈ B.

In the next sections we will talk about groups and algebras contained in CD or induced by partial homomorphisms
and symbol maps s : E → MD .

a normal form through the same unitary base change.
  This is actually a weaker conjecture that the precedent ones.
Lemma 18. Whenever Conjecture 4 or 3 is true then Conjecture 5 is also true.

Proof. Given any algebra A ⊆ CD , suppose that there exists a sequence of unitary matrices {Un }n such that

                                  {Un An UnH }n ∼GLT k      ∀({An }n , k) ∈ A.

We conclude that if Vn = UnH Qn , then A ⊆ SV .
   This conjecture is enough to prove that all cyclic algebras embed into the GLT space. When we consider only
normal (or Hermitian) sequences, the proof that cyclic algebras embed into the GLT algebra is straightforward,
so it could be a good starting point.
   The opposite implication of Lemma 18 may hold, but it needs further hypothesis. We can now formulate the
generalization of Theorem 3. In this case, D is any measurable set of positive measure in Rn .
Theorem 4. Suppose A ⊆ CD is an algebra induced by A ⊆ E and an algebra homomorphism s : A → MD
satisfying
   • there exists an unitary sequence {Vn }n such that A ⊆ SV ,
   • s is surjective,
   • A is closed.

the map

is an isomorphism of *-algebras over C and an isometry.
Proof. The proof is the same as Theorem 3, substituting Qn with Vn and G with A.
  Any closed algebra with normal form and all measurable functions as symbols is thus isomorphic to D, and
by extension to G. When D = [0, 1] × [−π, π], the isomorphism between A and G is actually an embedding, so
we conclude the following result.
Corollary 5. If A ⊆ CD is an algebra induced by A ⊆ E and an algebra homomorphism s : A → MD satisfying
   • there exists an unitary sequence {Vn }n such that A ⊆ SV ,
   • s is surjective,
   • A is closed,
then A ≤ G ≤ A.
   Using the latest result, we infer that if Conjecture 5 holds, and any algebra A ⊆ CD can be extended to an
algebra presenting all MD as symbols, then Conjecture 3 holds.

We set D = [0, 1] × [−π, π] at the start. But what happens when we swap D with any measurable set in Rn
with finite non-zero measure?
   One noticeable invariance is that the sequences contained in CD do not change. One could, at this point,
generalize the definition of embedding by letting also the domain of the symbol change as done in Theorem
4. This allows to unify the theory of GLT to the multilevel GLT theory, since multivariable symbols can be
transformed into equally distributed classical 1 variable functions.
   Several results on NU ({An }n , k), SU and S (k) are already available, but their structure is yet to be studied.
for example, we know that

are all closed set into E , and that k is a rearranged version of h if and only if one of the following holds:
    • S (k) ∩ S (h) 6= ∅ (and in this case S (k) = S (h)),
    • there exists {Un }n unitary such that SU (k) ∩ SU (h) 6= ∅ (and in this case SU (k) = SU (h)),
    • there exist {Un }n unitary and {An }n such that NU ({An }n , h) ∩ NU ({An }n , k) 6= ∅ (and in this case,
      NU ({An }n , h) = NU ({An }n , k)).
Remember that every hermitian GLT sequence {An }n ∼GLT k has k also as spectral symbol. Using the results
in this paper, it is possible to prove that it hold also for normal sequences.
Lemma 19. If {An }n is a normal sequence, then {An }n ∼GLT k =⇒ {An }n ∼λ k.
It is actually a corollary of the existence of a normal form for GLT and the following perturbation result.
Lemma 20. If {Xn }n ∼λ k is a normal sequence, and {Yn }n is a zero distributed sequence with Xn + Yn
normal for every n, then {Xn + Yn }n ∼λ k.
One last note goes to the definition of Normal Form. We may notice that if {An }n ∈ SU (k), then it is equivalent
to a normal sequence {Un Dn UnH }n with {Dn }n ∼λ k, but
    • the spectral symbol does not change after a permutation {Pn }n of the eigenvalues,
    • a rearrangement h of the symbol is also a spectral symbol for the same sequence.
In this sense, we now that SU (k) = SUP (k) = SU (h) = SUP (h). A way to control this phenomenon is to
change the definition of Normal Form, including further conditions on the eigenvalues of Dn . In fact, the GLT
space suggests that the diagonal matrices may contain an approximated and sorted sampling of the associated
symbol (a concept that in [3] has been traduced into the Piecewise Convergence). This would entice that for every
unitary sequence {Un }n there exists only one function k such that {An }n ∈ SU (k), creating a correspondence
between (a subset of) E and MD that links sequences to an unique symbol.
