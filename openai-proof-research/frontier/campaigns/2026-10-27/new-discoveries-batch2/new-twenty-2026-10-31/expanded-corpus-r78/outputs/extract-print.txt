316194 Alon–Saks–Seymour conjecture 
 Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph. For disjoint nonempty sets $L,R\subseteq
V$ such that $\{u,v\}\in E$ for all $u\in L$ and $v\in
R$, write $B(L,R)=\{\{u,v\}: u\in L,\ v\in
R\}$ for the edge set of the corresponding complete bipartite subgraph of $G$.
Call $bp(G)$ the least $k$ for which there exist such pairs
$(L_1,R_1),\dots,(L_k,R_k)$ with

$$
E=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{k} B(L_i,R_i),
$$

that is, the sets
$B(L_1,R_1),\dots,B(L_k,R_k)$ are pairwise disjoint and their union is $E$. Let
$\chi(G)$ denote the chromatic number of $G$.

Then for every finite simple graph $G$,

$$
\chi(G)\le bp(G)+1,
$$

equivalently, if $\chi(G)=k+1$ then every partition of $E$ into edge sets of
complete bipartite subgraphs of $G$ uses at least $k$ parts.
