Target: arXiv:2511.18747, Conjecture 7.3. Put m=2t+1, t>=1, and let W=C_m join K_1, with hub *. Then

χ_f(W □ W) = (6t²+7t+3)/(2t²+t+1).

The following uses the standard definition of a fractional coloring as nonnegative weights on independent sets covering each vertex to weight at least one. No linear-program duality theorem is required.

## Rectangular-grid lemma

For positive integers u,v, α(P_u □ P_v)=ceil(uv/2). The larger checkerboard class gives the lower bound. For the upper bound there is a matching covering every vertex except possibly one: if u is even, pair successive rows; if v is even, pair successive columns; if both are odd, pair the first u-1 rows, then pair successive columns of the last row. Each independent set uses at most one endpoint of every matched edge, plus the unmatched vertex.

If a nonempty independent subset A of C_m has a vertices, its deletion leaves exactly a nonempty path components. Their orders sum to m-a. Write o_A for the number of odd component orders. Then o_A<=a and o_A≡m-a (mod 2). Since m is odd, o_A cannot equal a, so o_A<=a-1. This bound is attained: choose a-1 gaps of length one and one even gap of length m-2a+1 between the deleted vertices; a<=t makes this last length at least two.

Consequently, for nonempty independent A,B of sizes a,b, the product of the two deletion graphs is a disjoint union of rectangles, and its independence number is

((m-a)(m-b)+o_A o_B)/2 <= ((m-a)(m-b)+(a-1)(b-1))/2.

This upper bound is attained by the gap choices just described. These are statements about actual induced graphs, not merely profile inequalities.

## Universal weighted independent-set bound

Give (*,*) weight 2, each vertex (i,*) or (*,j) weight t+1, and every rim-rim vertex weight 2. Every independent set I has weight at most

C=2(2t²+t+1).

If (*,*) belongs to I, both mixed layers are empty. Each of the m rim rows has at most t selected vertices, giving weight <=2+2mt=C.

Otherwise let A={i:(i,*)∈I}, B={j:(*,j)∈I}, with a=|A|, b=|B|. Both are independent in C_m. All rim-rim vertices of I lie in (C_m-A) □ (C_m-B).

If a=b=0, its size is at most mt by the row bound. If exactly one is positive, say b, the residual graph is C_m □ (C_m-B), with at most t vertices per surviving cycle fiber, hence at most t(m-b). Its total weight is at most 2tm+(1-t)b<C.

If a,b>=1, the rectangular-grid lemma bounds the weight by

(t+1)(a+b)+(m-a)(m-b)+(a-1)(b-1).

Subtracting C yields F(a,b)=2t-(t+1)(a+b)+2ab. This bilinear function attains its maximum on the corners of [1,t]²: fix one coordinate and maximize the affine function in the other, then repeat. Its values are 0 at (1,1) and (t,t), and -(t-1)² at the other two corners. Thus F<=0.

The total weight of all graph vertices is S=2(6t²+7t+3). For any fractional coloring (z_I), summing the vertex coverage inequalities multiplied by the vertex weights gives S <= sum_I z_I weight(I) <= C sum_I z_I. Therefore χ_f>=S/C.

## Matching fractional coloring

There are actual independent sets with the following three orbit profiles (hub-hub, mixed, rim-rim):

P0=(1,0,tm), P1=(0,2,2t²), P2=(0,2t,t²+1).

For P0 choose (*,*) and rim vertices (i,j) for which i-j modulo m is in {0,2,...,2t-2}. That residue set and its shifts by ±1 are disjoint, so the set is independent. For P1 choose one vertex from each mixed hub layer. The surviving rim graph is P_(2t) □ P_(2t); choose one checkerboard class. For P2 choose maximum independent A,B of size t in the mixed layers. Each deletion cycle consists of t-1 singleton components and one two-vertex path. Choose a maximum checkerboard class separately in each product component. Its size is ((t+1)²+(t-1)²)/2=t²+1.

Let Γ be the finite automorphism group generated by independent dihedral actions on the two rim cycles and interchange of coordinates. It is transitive on each of the three vertex classes, whose sizes are 1,2m,m². Averaging each independent set Pj over Γ therefore gives a fractional independent-set family whose coverage at a vertex in class i is Pj_i divided by that class size. This follows by double counting pairs (g,v) with v∈g(Pj).

Put D=2t²+t+1. Give the three averaged families total coefficients

λ0=1, λ1=m/D, λ2=m²/D.

The hub class is covered exactly once. The mixed class coverage is (2λ1+2tλ2)/(2m)=1 because 1+tm=D. The rim class coverage is (tm+2t²λ1+(t²+1)λ2)/m²=1: after multiplying by D/m, the identity reduces to tD+2t²+(t²+1)m=mD. All coefficients are nonnegative. Their sum is

1+m/D+m²/D=(6t²+7t+3)/D.

This proves the upper bound and hence the formula for every t>=1 (and in particular the source’s studied range t>=2).
