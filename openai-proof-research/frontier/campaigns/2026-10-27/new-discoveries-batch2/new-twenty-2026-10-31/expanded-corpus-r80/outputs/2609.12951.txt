Abstract.
We prove that all Morita classes  \mu_{k}\in H_{4k}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2});{\mathds{Q}})  are nonzero using a novel description of the Hopf algebra structure on Steinberg homology. We prove analogous results with twisted coefficients. Our results disprove a case of a finiteness conjecture of Kontsevich.

1. Introduction
Borinsky–Vogtmann proved that the Euler characteristic of  {\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n})  grows super-exponentially [BV23, Theorem 1.1], so  {\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})  and  {\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n})  have an enormous amount of rational homology. Despite the ubiquity of homology, no explicit infinite family of nonzero rational homology classes in positive degrees had been found. A similar situation for mapping class groups was called the dark matter problem by Farb (see e.g. Margalit [Mar19, Problem 9.2]).
In [Mor99, p. 391], Morita constructed classes in  H_{4k}({\mathrm{Out}}(F_{2k+2});{\mathds{Q}})  for  k\geq 1 , which lift to classes  \mu_{k}\in H_{4k}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2});{\mathds{Q}})  that we refer to as Morita classes; see Definition 5.1 for a definition suited to our arguments. It is known that the first three Morita classes  \mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3}  are nonzero (Morita [Mor99, Proposition 6.13], Conant–Vogtmann [CV04, Proposition 5.4], and Gray [Gra11, Theorem 3.3.4]). It is a well-known conjecture that all of the Morita classes are nonzero (see e.g. Morita [Mor06, Conjecture 9] or Church–Farb–Putman [CFP14, Question 15]). Our main result confirms this conjecture.
Theorem A.
For  k\geq 2 , the images of the Morita classes  \mu_{k}\in H_{4k}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2});{\mathds{Q}})  under the map induced by the abelianisation map  {\mathrm{ab}}\colon{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{2k+2}(\mathds{Z})  are nonzero. In particular, for  k\geq 1 , the Morita classes and their images in  H_{4k}({\mathrm{Out}}(F_{2k+2});{\mathds{Q}})  are nonzero.
Remark.
This answers a question of Bridson and Vogtmann [BV06, Question 32] on the image of  \mu_{2}  in  H_{8}({\mathrm{GL}}_{6}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q})  and the analogous problem of Morita for general  \mu_{k}  [Mor06, Problem 37]. We note the image of  \mu_{1}  vanishes in  H_{4}({\mathrm{GL}}_{4}(\mathds{Z});{\mathds{Q}})  and in fact  H_{4}({\mathrm{GL}}_{4}(\mathds{Z});{\mathds{Q}})=0  by work of Lee and Szczarba [LS78, Theorem 2].
To prove Theorem A, we interpret the image of the Morita classes as obtained by a push-pull construction from a span
 {\mathrm{GL}}_{2k}({\mathds{Z}})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}({\mathds{Z}})\longleftarrow P_{2k,2}({\mathds{Z}})\longrightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2k+2}({\mathds{Z}}) 
with  P_{2k,2}({\mathds{Z}})  a certain parabolic subgroup, and pair it against a cohomology class obtained under Bieri–Eckmann duality by a product in homology with Steinberg coefficients. This reduces the result to a computation in the Hopf algebra structure of Ash and the last two authors [AMP24].
Using the same techniques we also prove a nonvanishing result with twisted coefficients. Let  \mathds{Q}^{\det}  denote the determinant representation of  {\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathds{Z}})  and  {\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n}) .
Theorem B.
For  k\geq 2 , there exist classes  \alpha_{k}\in H_{2k-1}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k});\mathds{Q}^{\det})  that map nontrivially to  H_{2k-1}({\mathrm{GL}}_{2k}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det}) .
A consequence of Theorem A is the resolution of a case of a conjecture by Kontsevich. In [Kon93], he defined the notions of even and odd graph homology associated to the commutative, associative, and Lie cyclic operads. In his main theorem [Kon93, Theorem 1.1], he relates the latter two even graph homology groups to the cohomology groups of the mapping class groups of surfaces and to the cohomology groups of  {\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n}) , respectively. In all six cases, he made finite-dimensionality conjectures about these graph homology groups [Kon93, Section 7C].
In the even Lie case, Kontsevich’s finite-dimensionality conjecture can be reformulated as the statement
 H_{2n-3-i}({\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n});\mathds{Q})=0\text{ for }n\gg i; 
see e.g. Brun–Willwacher [BW24, Theorem 5].
This can be interpreted as the eventual vanishing of the codimension- i  homology using that  {\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n})  has virtual cohomological dimension is  2n-3  by work of Culler and Vogtmann [CV86]. Our Theorem A show this conjecture is false for  i=1 .
Corollary C.
For  i=1 ,  H_{2n-3-i}({\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n});\mathds{Q})\neq 0  for all even  n\geq 2 .
The odd Lie case of Kontsevich’s conjecture is equivalent to the statement  H_{2n-3-i}({\mathrm{Out}}(F_{n});\mathds{Q}^{\det})=0  for  n\gg i . This is not contradicted by Theorem B, and still open to the knowledge of the authors.
Remark.
The even and odd commutative cases of Kontsevich’s conjecture true; see Campos–Idrissi–Lambrechts–Willwacher
[CILW24, Lemma A.10] and Willwacher [Wil15, Theorem 1.1]. The even associative case is false by work of Chan, Galatius, and Payne [CGP21, Theorem 1.1], who proved the codimension-1 homology of mapping class group does not eventually vanish.
Notation.
Throughout the remainder of this paper we will work rationally, e.g. take (co)homology with  {\mathds{Q}} -coefficients and rationalise the Steinberg modules, and suppress this from the notation.

2. Bieri–Eckmann duality
In this section, we give a short recollection of Bieri–Eckmann duality [BE73] and provide some results that we will need in the subsequent sections.
Definition 2.1.
A (discrete) group  G  is a rational (Bieri–Eckmann) duality group of dimension  n  if there is a  \mathds{Q}[G] -module  D  and an element  x\in H_{n}(G;D)  such that the cap product
 x\cap-\colon H^{i}(G;A)\longrightarrow H_{n-i}(G;A\otimes D) 
is an isomorphism for all  \mathds{Q}[G] -modules  A  and  i\in\mathds{Z} . In this case,  D  is called the (rational) dualising module of  G .
It is immediate that  n  is the rational cohomological dimension of  G  and that  D  is uniquely determined by the isomorphism  D\cong H^{n}(G;\mathds{Q}[G]) . For short exact sequences of groups, Bieri–Eckmann prove the following:
Theorem 2.2 ([BE73, Theorem 3.5]).
Consider a short exact sequence of groups
 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 1. 
Assume that  K  and  Q  are rational duality groups of dimensions  d  and  d^{\prime}  with dualising modules  D  and  D^{\prime} , respectively. Then  G  is a rational duality group of dimension  d+d^{\prime}  with dualising module  D\otimes D^{\prime} , where  G  acts on  D\cong H^{d}(K;\mathds{Q}[K])  via conjugation and on  D^{\prime}  through  G\to Q .
We may define a fibre integration map from the cohomology of  G  to the cohomology of  Q  by using Bieri–Eckmann duality twice:
Definition 2.3.
Consider a short exact sequence of groups
 1\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q\longrightarrow 1. 
Assume that  K  and  Q  are rational duality groups of dimensions  d  and  d^{\prime}  with dualising modules  D  and  D^{\prime} , respectively. Let  A  be a  \mathds{Q}[Q] -module. Define the fibre integration map
 {\textstyle\int_{q}}\colon H^{i}(G;A)\longrightarrow H^{i-d}(Q;D_{K}\otimes A) 
as the dashed left vertical map in
 {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}(G;A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{d+d^{\prime}-i}(G;D\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i-d}(Q;D_{K}\otimes A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{d+d^{\prime}-i}(Q;D_{K}\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A).}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\int_{q}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{BE}}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{*}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{BE}}} 
Its restriction from  H^{d}(G)  to  H^{d}(K)  can be described via the fibre integration map as described in the following lemma.
Lemma 2.4.
Consider a short exact sequence of groups
 1\longrightarrow K\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathrm{inc}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q\longrightarrow 1. 
Assume that  K  and  Q  are rational duality groups of dimensions  d  and  d^{\prime}  with dualising modules  D  and  D^{\prime} , respectively. Let  A  be a  \mathds{Q}[Q] -module. Then
 {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d}(G;A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d}(K)\otimes A}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(Q;D_{K}\otimes A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(Q;H^{d}(K)\otimes A)}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\int_{q}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{inc}}^{*}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{BE}}} 
commutes up to a sign.
Proof.
Consider the two Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences
 \displaystyle E^{pq}_{2}=H^{p}(Q;H^{q}(K)\otimes A) 
 \displaystyle\implies H^{p+q}(G;A) 
 \displaystyle{\overline{E}}^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}(Q;H_{q}(K;D)\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A) 
 \displaystyle\implies H_{p+q}(G;D\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A). 
We first observe that  {\mathrm{inc}}^{*}  agrees with the composition
 H^{d}(G;A)\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{edge}}}H^{0}(Q;H^{d}(K)\otimes A)\xhookrightarrow{\phantom{edge}}H^{d}(K)\otimes A 
and  q_{*}  is given by
 H_{d^{\prime}}(G;D\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A)\xrightarrow{{\overline{{\mathrm{edge}}}}}H_{d^{\prime}}(Q;H_{0}(K;D)\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A), 
where  {\mathrm{edge}}  and  {\overline{{\mathrm{edge}}}}  are the edge maps of the above two spectral sequences, respectively. (This does not require the groups to be duality groups.) It thus suffices to show the following square commutes
 {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d}(G;A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{0}(Q;H^{d}(K)\otimes A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{d^{\prime}}(G;D\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A)}  {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{d^{\prime}}(Q;H_{0}(K;D)\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A).}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{BE}}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{edge}}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{BE}}}  \scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{{\mathrm{edge}}}}} 
As the Bieri–Eckmann isomorphisms are defined by the cap products, [Bie72, Lemma 1.3.2] (applied with  N=K ,  G/N=Q ,  A=A ,  C=D\otimes D^{\prime} , restricting the top-left corner to  {\mathds{Q}}\{x\}\otimes E^{p,q}_{2}  with  x  the generator of  \smash{{\overline{E}}{}^{2}_{d^{\prime},d}} ) states that
 E^{pq}_{2}=H^{p}(Q;H^{q}(K)\otimes A)\overset{{\mathrm{BE}}}{\longrightarrow}H_{d^{\prime}-p}(Q;H_{d-q}(K;D)\otimes D^{\prime}\otimes A)={\overline{E}}^{2}_{d^{\prime}-p,d-q} 
gives rise to an isomorphism of spectral sequences when multiplied with the correct sign. Moreover, by [Bie72, Lemma 1.3.1] (applied to same data as above), this isomorphism on the spectral sequences converges to the Bieri–Eckmann isomorphism on the abutment. Thus the Bieri–Eckmann isomorphisms commute with the edge morphisms.

3. A geometric description of the coproduct in Steinberg homology
In this section, we give a geometric model of a coproduct introduced in [AMP24, Definition 2.7]. Brown, Galatius, Chan, and Payne [BCGP24] independently constructed a coproduct using different methods.
3.1. The coproduct in Steinberg homology
We start with some recollections. For  V  an  n -dimensional  \mathds{Q} -vector space, recall that the Tits building  \mathcal{T}(V)  is the order complex of the poset of proper nonzero subspaces of  V  ordered by inclusion. The Solomon–Tits Theorem says that  \mathcal{T}(V)  is  (n-2) -spherical and we define the Steinberg module of  {\mathrm{GL}}(V)  as  {\mathrm{St}}(V)\coloneq\widetilde{H}_{n-2}(\mathcal{T}(V))  for  n>0  and define it to be  \mathds{Q}  for  n=0 . The Steinberg module is generated by apartment classes: let  v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}  be a basis of  V , then there is an associated apartment class in  {\mathrm{St}}(V)  defined by the formula (viewed as a top-degree reduced cellular chain  \widetilde{C}_{n-2}(\mathcal{T}(V)) )
 [\![v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\!]\coloneq\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S_{n}}}}(-1)^{\sigma}{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\sigma(1)})<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\sigma(1)},v_{\sigma(2)})<\ldots<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n-1)}). 
Definition 3.1.
Let  V  be an  n -dimensional  \mathds{Q} -vector space and  \chi\colon\mathds{Z}^{\times}\to\mathds{Q}^{\times}  a group homomorphism, i.e. either the inclusion or the trivial map. We denote by  \mathds{Q}^{\chi}  the trivial  {\mathrm{GL}}(V) -module if  \chi  is the trivial map and  \Lambda^{n}V  if  \chi  is the inclusion. Let  {\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(V)  be  {\mathrm{St}}(V)\otimes\mathds{Q}^{\chi} . If  \chi  is the inclusion, we will also write  {\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(V)  for  {\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(V)  and  \mathds{Q}^{\det}  for  \mathds{Q}^{\chi} , and if  \chi  is the trivial map, we simply write  {\mathrm{St}}(V)  for  {\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(V)  and  \mathds{Q}  for  \mathds{Q}^{\chi} .
Notation 3.2.
By abuse of notation, we also use the notation  [\![v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\!]  for the element of  {\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(V)  given by tensoring the same-named apartment class with  v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n} .
We now describe the coproduct map. Let  {\mathrm{Sh}}_{a,b}\subseteq{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}  denote the subset of the symmetric group given by  a,b -shuffles. For  a+b=n , we define the map (well-defined by [AMP24, Lemma 2.10])
 \displaystyle\Delta_{a,b}\colon{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{n}) 
 \displaystyle\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{V\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{n},\,\dim(V)=a}{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(V)\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{n}/V) 
 \displaystyle[\![v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\!] 
 \displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathrm{Sh}}_{a,b}}(-1)^{\sigma}[\![v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(a)}]\!]\otimes[\![v_{\sigma(a+1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}]\!]. 
We next introduce the following parabolic subgroup of  {\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathds{Z}}) 
 P_{a,b}({\mathds{Z}})\coloneq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
0&C\end{bmatrix}\,\,\middle|\,\,\text{with $A\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}({\mathds{Z}})$, $B\in{\mathrm{Mat}}_{a,b}({\mathds{Z}})$, $C\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}({\mathds{Z}})$}\right\}. 
There is an action of  P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z})  on
 {\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{b})  through the projection map
 {\mathrm{pr}}\colon P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z})\longrightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z}), 
which appears when applying Shapiro’s lemma to obtain the isomorphism
 H_{\ast}\Big({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});\bigoplus_{V\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{n},\,\dim(V)=a}{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(V)\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{n}/V)\Big)\cong H_{*}\Big(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{b})\Big). 
Since the action factors over the projection, we also have an induced map
 H_{*}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{b}))\longrightarrow H_{*}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{b})). 
The composition of  \Delta_{a,b}  with these maps and the Künneth map endows the bigraded  {\mathds{Q}} -vector space
 \bigoplus_{n,i\geq 0}H_{i}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{\chi}(\mathds{Q}^{n})) 
with a coproduct, as is verified in [AMP24, Lemma 2.10]. In that paper, an explicit resolution of  {\mathrm{St}}(\mathds{Q}^{n})  due to Lee and Szczarba [LS76] is used to compute group homology but the maps on homology do not depend on the choice of resolution. Note that  H_{*}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{n}))  is trivial for  n  odd by the “centre kills” trick.
3.2. A Bieri–Eckmann duality description of the coproduct
We now give an alternative description of this coproduct through Bieri–Eckmann duality [BE73], see Theorem 3.5. Recall that the coproduct involves two steps:
(1) 
the coproduct map on dualising modules followed by Shapiro’s lemma,
(2) 
the map induced by the projection  {\mathrm{pr}} .
We start with a Bieri–Eckmann dual description of (1). Let  V  be an  n -dimension  \mathds{Q} -vector space. Let  {\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(V)  denote  {\mathrm{St}}(V)  if  n  is odd and  {\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(V)  if  n  is even. Work of Borel–Serre [BS73] implies that  {\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z})  and  P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z})  are rational duality groups of dimension  \smash{\nu_{n}\coloneq{n\choose 2}} . The rational dualising module of  {\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z})  is  {\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{n}) ; see Putman–Studenmund [PS22, Theorem C]. The rational dualising module of  P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z})  is  {\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{b})  if  a  and  b  have the same parity, which may be seen applying Theorem 2.2 to the short exact sequence
 1\longrightarrow{\mathds{Z}}^{ab}\longrightarrow P_{a,b}({\mathds{Z}})\overset{{\mathrm{pr}}}{\longrightarrow}{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}({\mathds{Z}})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}({\mathds{Z}})\longrightarrow 1, 
in which the left term  {\mathds{Z}}^{ab}  is a Poincaré duality group and hence rational duality group with rational dualising module  {\mathds{Q}}  and  {\mathrm{GL}}_{a}({\mathds{Z}})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}({\mathds{Z}})  is also a rational duality group with dualising module  {\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{b}) . As  (A,B)\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z})  acts on the dualising module of  \mathds{Z}^{ab}  by  \det(A)^{b}\cdot\det(B)^{-a} , the middle term has dualising module
 {\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{b})={\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{b}) 
if  a  and  b  are both even, and
 (\mathds{Q}^{\det}\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{a}))\otimes(\mathds{Q}^{\det}\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{b}))={\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{b}) 
if  a  and  b  are both odd.
Take  n=a+b , let  \widetilde{C}_{i}  denote reduced cellular chains, and let  *  denote the simplicial join. In [Ree91], Reeder defined the following map:
 \displaystyle s^{a,b}\colon\widetilde{C}_{p}(\mathcal{T}(\mathds{Q}^{n})) 
 \displaystyle\longrightarrow\widetilde{C}_{p-1}(\mathcal{T}(\mathds{Q}^{a})*\mathcal{T}(\mathds{Q}^{b})) 
 \displaystyle(V_{0}<\ldots<V_{p}) 
 \displaystyle\longmapsto\begin{cases}(V_{0}<\ldots<V_{j-1})*((V_{j+1}\cap\mathds{Q}^{b})<\ldots<(V_{p}\cap\mathds{Q}^{b})),&\text{if }V_{j}=\mathds{Q}^{a},\\
0,&\text{if }V_{j}\neq\mathds{Q}^{a}\text{ for all }j.\end{cases} 
Here, we view  \mathds{Q}^{n}  as  \mathds{Q}^{a}\oplus\mathds{Q}^{b} , where  \mathds{Q}^{a}\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{n}  is the subspace of vectors whose last  b  coordinates are zero and  \mathds{Q}^{b}\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{n}  is the subspace of vectors whose first  a  coordinates are zero. Theorem 3.3 (Reeder).

We now compare Reeder’s map  s^{a,b}  and the map  \Delta_{a,b} .
Lemma 3.4.
After identifying  \mathds{Q}^{b}  with  \mathds{Q}^{n}/\mathds{Q}^{a} , the projection of
 \Delta_{a,b}\colon{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{n})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{V\subseteq\mathds{Q}^{n},\,\dim(V)=a}{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(V)\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{n}/V) 
to the summand associated to  V=\mathds{Q}^{a}  agrees with  s^{a,b}\colon{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{n})\to{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{a})\otimes{\mathrm{St}}^{\det}(\mathds{Q}^{b}) .
Proof.
It suffices to check this claim on apartments, viewing  \mathds{Q}^{n}  as  \mathds{Q}^{a}\oplus\mathds{Q}^{b} .
Observe that  s^{a,b}([\![v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\!])=0  if  v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}  does not contain a basis of  \mathds{Q}^{a} . Thus we assume  v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}  does contain a basis of  \mathds{Q}^{a} , and then there is a unique  (a,b) -shuffle  \sigma  with  v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(a)}  a basis of  \mathds{Q}^{a} . If  \tau\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}  does not equal  \sigma  modulo  {\mathfrak{S}}_{a}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{b} , then the flag
 {\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)})<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)},v_{\tau(2)})<\ldots<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)},\ldots,v_{\tau(n-1)}) 
is mapped to  0  by  s^{a,b} .
If  \tau\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}  is equal to  \sigma  modulo  {\mathfrak{S}}_{a}\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{b} , then the flag
 {\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)})<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)},v_{\tau(2)})<\ldots<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)},\ldots,v_{\tau(n-1)}) 
is mapped by  s^{a,b}  to
 \Big({\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)})<\ldots<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(1)},\ldots,v_{\tau(a-1)})\Big)*\Big({\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(a+1)})<\ldots<{\mathrm{span}}(v_{\tau(a+1)},\ldots,v_{\tau(n-1)})\Big). 
It follows from this and the definition of apartment classes that we have an equality
 s^{a,b}([\![v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\!])=(-1)^{\sigma}[\![v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(a)}]\!]\otimes[\![v_{\sigma(a+1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)}]\!] 
in this case. This is exactly the projection of  \Delta_{a,b}([\![v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\!]) .
∎

4. Unipotent abelian cycles in  {\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n}) 
In this section, we describe a procedure for constructing nonzero homology classes for  {\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n}) .
Definition 4.1.
Let  n  be even and  a+b=n . We call a map  \alpha\colon\mathds{Z}^{ab}\to{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})  a unipotent abelian cycle if the composition
 \mathds{Z}^{ab}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ab}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z}) 
is equal to the composition  \mathds{Z}^{ab}\to P\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z}) , where  P  is a subgroup of  {\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z})  that stabilizes a rank- a  summand  M  of  \mathds{Z}^{n}  and  \mathds{Z}^{ab}\to P  is an injection whose image is the subgroup of invertible matrices that fix pointwise the elements of  M  and  \mathds{Z}^{n}/M .
A unipotent abelian cycle often gives a nonzero homology class of  {\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n}) :
Theorem 4.2.
Let  n  be even and  a+b=n , and assume that  a\equiv 0,3\pmod{4}  if  a=b . Let  \alpha\colon\mathds{Z}^{ab}\to{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n})  be a unipotent abelian cycle.
(i) 
If  a  and  b  are both even, then the following composition of induced maps is injective:
 H_{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{ab}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ab}}_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{ab}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z})). 
(ii) 
If  a  and  b  are both odd, then the following composition of induced maps is injective:
 H_{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{ab}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{n});\mathds{Q}^{\det})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ab}}_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{ab}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det}). 
Using a change of basis, we may assume that  P=P_{a,b}({\mathds{Z}}) . By definition of unipotent abelian cycles, Theorem 4.2 follows immediately from the following statements:
Proposition 4.3.
Let  n  be even and  a+b=n , and assume that  a\equiv 0,3\pmod{4}  if  a=b :
(i) 
If  a  and  b  are both even, then the following composition of induced maps is injective:
 H_{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\longrightarrow H_{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z}))\longrightarrow H_{ab}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z})). 
(ii) 
If  a  and  b  are both odd, then the following composition of induced maps is injective:
 H_{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\longrightarrow H_{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det})\longrightarrow H_{ab}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det}). 
Before proving this proposition, let us prove a useful lemma.
Lemma 4.4.
Let  n  be even and  a+b=n .
(i) 
If  a  and  b  are both even, the image of the fundamental class through
 H_{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\longrightarrow H_{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z})) 
is given by applying  ({\textstyle\int}_{\mathrm{pr}})^{\vee}  to the generator  1\otimes 1\in H_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}({\mathds{Z}}))\otimes H_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{b}({\mathds{Z}}))  up to a sign.
(ii) 
If  a  and  b  are both odd, the image of the fundamental class through
 H_{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\longrightarrow H_{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det}) 
is given by applying  ({\textstyle\int}_{\mathrm{pr}})^{\vee}  to the generator  1\otimes 1\in H_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}({\mathds{Z}}))\otimes H_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{b}({\mathds{Z}}))  up to a sign.
Proof.
We will apply Lemma 2.4 to the short exact sequence
 1\longrightarrow\mathds{Z}^{ab}\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{inc}}}P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z})\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{pr}}}{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z})\longrightarrow 1. 
Note that  H^{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})  is one-dimensional, and  {\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z})  acts on it trivially in case (1) and via the determinant in case (2).
Thus, in case (i), we see that
 H^{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z}))\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{inc}}^{*}}H^{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\cong\mathds{Q}\quad\text{and}\quad H^{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z}))\xrightarrow{\int_{{\mathrm{pr}}}}H^{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z}))\cong\mathds{Q} 
agree up to a sign. In case (ii), we similarly see that
 \displaystyle H^{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det})\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{inc}}^{*}}H^{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\otimes\mathds{Q}^{\det}\cong\mathds{Q}\quad\text{and} 
 \displaystyle H^{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det})\xrightarrow{\int_{{\mathrm{pr}}}}H^{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z})\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z});H^{ab}(\mathds{Z}^{ab})\otimes\mathds{Q}^{\det})\cong\mathds{Q} 
agree up to a sign. Dualising both observations yields the statement.
∎
Proof of Proposition 4.3.
By Lemma 4.4, the image of the fundamental class—in  H_{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z}))  if  a  and  b  are even and in  H_{ab}(P_{a,b}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det})  if  a  and  b  are odd—can (up to sign) be described as  ({\textstyle\int}_{\mathrm{pr}})^{\vee}(1\otimes 1) , where  1\otimes 1\in H_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}({\mathds{Z}}))\otimes H_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{b}({\mathds{Z}}))  is the canonical generator. Thus by Theorem 3.5,
 \langle x,{\mathrm{inc}}_{*}({\textstyle\int}_{\mathrm{pr}})^{\vee}(1\otimes 1)\rangle=\langle{\textstyle\int}_{\mathrm{pr}}{\mathrm{inc}}^{*}(x),1\otimes 1\rangle=\langle({\mathrm{BE}}\otimes{\mathrm{BE}})^{-1}\Delta_{a,b}({\mathrm{BE}}(x)),1\otimes 1\rangle 
for any  x\in H^{ab}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z}))  if  a  and  b  are even, and any  x\in H^{ab}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det})  if  a  and  b  are odd.
Writing  1^{\vee}\in H^{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(\mathds{Z}))  for the generator, we denote  {\mathrm{BE}}(1^{\vee})\in H_{\nu_{k}}({\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{k}))  as  t_{k} . For degree reasons, the classes  t_{k}  are primitive [AMP24, Proposition 4.2]. We now take  x={\mathrm{BE}}^{-1}(t_{a}t_{b})  where  t_{a}t_{b}  is formed with respect to the product
 H_{\nu_{a}}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{a}))\otimes H_{\nu_{b}}({\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{b}))\longrightarrow H_{\nu_{a}+\nu_{b}}({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\mathds{Z});{\mathrm{St}}^{{\mathrm{or}}}(\mathds{Q}^{n})) 
of [AMP24]. Note that here we use that  a  and  b  have the same parity.
Then, the above evaluation is nonzero if and only if  \Delta_{a,b}(t_{a}t_{b})  has a nonzero  t_{a}\otimes t_{b} -component, because this is the only element taken by  ({\mathrm{BE}}\otimes{\mathrm{BE}})^{-1}  to  1^{\vee}\otimes 1^{\vee}\in H^{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(\mathds{Z}))\otimes H^{0}({\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(\mathds{Z})) . As the product and coproduct assemble to a Hopf algebra structure [AMP24, Theorem A] and the classes  t_{a}  and  t_{b}  are primitive, we compute
 \Delta(t_{a}t_{b})=(1\otimes t_{a}+t_{a}\otimes 1)(1\otimes t_{b}+t_{b}\otimes 1)=1\otimes t_{a}t_{b}+t_{a}\otimes t_{b}+(-1)^{(\nu_{a}+a)(\nu_{b}+b)}t_{b}\otimes t_{a}+t_{a}t_{b}\otimes 1. 
This has a nonzero  t_{a}\otimes t_{b} -component if  a\neq b  (e.g. because for  a\neq b ,  t_{a}\otimes t_{b}  lies in a different summand of the tensor product than  t_{b}\otimes t_{a} ). Moreover, if  a=b , then  (\nu_{a}+a)=(\nu_{b}+b)  is even if and only if  a\equiv 0,3\pmod{4} . This completes the proof of Proposition 4.3.
∎
5. The Morita classes
In this section, we apply Theorem 4.2 to show that Morita classes are nonzero by verifying that they are represented by unipotent abelian cycles (Definition 4.1).
5.1. Morita classes and their images
We start by recalling the description of the Morita classes from Conant–Hatcher–Kassabov–Vogtmann [CHKV16, Proposition 4.4]. Denote the generators of the free group  F_{2k+2}  by  x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k+2}  and consider the following  4k  automorphisms: each of them is the identity on all generators except for
 \displaystyle\lambda_{i}\colon x_{i} 
 \displaystyle\longmapsto x_{2k+1}x_{i}\qquad\text{for $1\leq i\leq 2k$}, 
 \displaystyle\rho_{i}\colon x_{i} 
 \displaystyle\longmapsto x_{i}x_{2k+2}\qquad\text{for $1\leq i\leq 2k$}. 
These visibly commute so they induce a homomorphism  \phi\colon{\mathds{Z}}^{4k}\to{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2}) .
Definition 5.1.
The Morita class  \mu_{k}\in H_{4k}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2}))  is the image of the fundamental class  [{\mathds{Z}}^{4k}]\in H_{4k}({\mathds{Z}}^{4k})  under  \phi_{*} .
We will prove that this class is nontrivial for  k\geq 2  (Theorem A) by observing that  \phi  is a unipotent abelian cycle and applying Theorem 4.2. Note that indeed,  {\mathrm{ab}}\circ\phi  factors through
 \mathds{Z}^{4k}\longrightarrow P_{2k,2}({\mathds{Z}})\longrightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2k+2}(\mathds{Z}) 
and thus is a unipotent abelian cycle. Because  2k\neq 2  for  k\geq 2 , Theorem 4.2 implies that
 H_{4k}(\mathds{Z}^{4k})\xrightarrow{\phi_{*}}H_{4k}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k+2}))\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{ab}}}H_{4k}({\mathrm{GL}}_{2k+2}(\mathds{Z})) 
is injective. This implies Theorem A.
5.2. Twisted Morita classes
A second family of easy-to-define unipotent abelian cycles
 \psi\colon\mathds{Z}^{2k-1}\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k}) 
is given by the commuting automorphisms that are the identity on all generators except for
 \lambda_{i}\colon x_{i}\longmapsto x_{2k}x_{i}\qquad\text{for $1\leq i\leq 2k-1$.} 
As  2k-1\neq 1  for  k\geq 2  and both are odd, Theorem 4.2 implies that
 H_{2k-1}(\mathds{Z}^{2k-1})\xrightarrow{\psi_{*}}H_{2k-1}({\mathrm{Aut}}(F_{2k});\mathds{Q}^{\det})\xrightarrow{{\mathrm{ab}}}H_{2k-1}({\mathrm{GL}}_{2k}(\mathds{Z});\mathds{Q}^{\det}) 
is injective. Let  \alpha_{k}  be the image of the fundamental class  \psi_{*}([\mathds{Z}^{2k-1}]) . This establishes Theorem B.
Remark 5.2.
In [BW24, Conjecture 14], Brun–Willwacher ask whether different cycles that they construct in  H_{2k-1}({\mathrm{Out}}(F_{2k});\mathds{Q}^{\det})  are nonzero. We believe these are also unipotent abelian cycles and thus nonzero, but will not prove this here. In any case, our methods do not allow us to distinguish Brun–Willwacher’s classes from the classes  \alpha_{k} .
