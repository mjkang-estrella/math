Asymptotics for  t -Core Partitions and Stanton’s Conjecture
Matt Tyler
Abstract
A partition is a  t -core partition if  t  is not one of its hook lengths. Let  c_{t}(N)  be the number of  t -core partitions of  N . In 1999, Stanton conjectured  c_{t}(N)\leq c_{t+1}(N)  if  4\leq t\neq N-1 . This was proved for  t  fixed and  N  sufficiently large by Anderson, and for small values of  t  by Kim and Rouse. In this paper, we prove Stanton’s conjecture in general.
Our approach is to find a saddle point asymptotic formula for  c_{t}(N) , valid in all ranges of  t  and  N . This includes the known asymptotic formulas for  c_{t}(N)  as special cases, and shows that the behavior of  c_{t}(N)  depends on how  t^{2}  compares in size to  N . For example, our formula implies that if  t^{2}=\kappa N+o(t) , then  c_{t}(N)=\frac{\exp\left(2\pi\sqrt{AN}\right)}{BN}\left(1+o(1)\right)  for suitable constants  A  and  B  defined in terms of  \kappa .
1 Introduction and statement of results
Let  \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})  with  \lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{k}>0  be a partition of  |\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{k} . The Ferrers–Young diagram of  \lambda  consists of  k  rows of left-justified boxes, with  \lambda_{i}  boxes in row  i . Let  \lambda_{j}^{\prime}  be the number of boxes in column  j  (so  \lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\lambda_{\ell}^{\prime})  is the conjugate partition to  \lambda ). The hook length for the box at row  i  and column  j  is  (\lambda_{i}-j)+(\lambda_{j}^{\prime}-i)+1 . In other words, the hook length is the number of boxes directly to the right, plus the number of boxes directly below, plus  1  for the box itself. We say  \lambda  is a  t -core partition if  t  is not one of its hook lengths. See Figure 1.1 for an example.

We write  c_{t}(N)  for the number of  t -core partitions of  N , and  p(N)  for the number of partitions of  N .
Much of the motivation for studying  t -core partitions comes from the representation theory of the symmetric group  S_{N} . The irreducible complex representations of  S_{N}  are parameterized by the partitions  \lambda  of  N . The representation associated to  \lambda  has dimension  \frac{N!}{\prod_{h\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda)}h}  where  \mathcal{H}(\lambda)  is the multiset of hook lengths of  \lambda .
When  p  is prime, a  p -core partition of  N  is therefore a partition for which the power of  p  dividing  |S_{N}|  is the same as the power of  p  dividing the dimension of the associated representation. These are the projective irreducible representations in characteristic  p , i.e. the  p -blocks of defect  0 . More generally, by Nakayama’s conjecture (proved by Brauer and Robinson),  p -core partitions parameterize the  p -blocks of representations of  S_{n} . See [JK84, sections 2.7 and 6.1] for details.
Another application of  t -core partitions is due to Garvan, Kim, and Stanton [GKS90], who used  t -core partitions for  t=5,7,11  to find combinatorial proofs of Ramanujan’s congruences
 \displaystyle p(5N+4) 
 \displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 5), 
(1.1)
 \displaystyle p(7N+5) 
 \displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 7),\quad\text{and} 
 \displaystyle p(11N+6) 
 \displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 11). 
The  t -core partition conjecture states that  c_{t}(N)>0  for all  t\geq 4  and  N\geq 1 . This was proved for  t=5,7  by Erdmann and Michler [EM96], when  t  has a prime factor  \leq 11  by Ono [Ono94, Ono95], and in general by Granville and Ono [GO00]. In 1999, Stanton [Sta99] made the “possibly rash” conjecture that in fact much more is true.
Conjecture 1.1 (Stanton).
For all  4\leq t\neq N-1 ,  c_{t}(N)\leq c_{t+1}(N) .
We always have  c_{t}(N)\leq p(N) , with equality if  t>N , so the conjecture is obvious if  t\geq N . The restriction  t\neq N-1  is necessary because  c_{N-1}(N)=p(N)-N+1=c_{N}(N)+1 .
In the case  t>\frac{N}{2} , Stanton’s conjecture was quickly proved by Craven [Cra06] using combinatorial arguments. In 1999, Lulov and Pittel [LP99] used the circle method to show that if  t\geq 6 , then
 c_{t}(N)=A_{t}(N)\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}\left(N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}\right)^{\frac{t-3}{2}}+O_{t}\left(N^{\frac{t-1}{4}}\right) 
(1.2)
where  A_{t}(N)  is an arithmetic quantity satisfying  \left|A_{t}(N)-1\right|\leq\left|\zeta(\frac{t-3}{2})-1\right| . This was rediscovered in 2008 by Anderson [And08], who used it to prove the conjecture when  t  is fixed and  N  is sufficiently large. This work was made explicit by Kim and Rouse [KR14] in 2014, who proved the conjecture for certain values of  t .22
            2
          There may be a technical error in their proof, but it works without modification for  t\ll\log N , and may be extendable up to  t\ll N^{\frac{1}{4}} . For completeness, we will independently treat the case  t\geq 8  here, and rely on their work for  4\leq t<8 , which is unaffected by the error.
In this paper, we will prove Stanton’s conjecture.
Theorem 1.2.
Stanton’s conjecture 1.1 is true.
Remark 1.3.
Our proof shows that the inequality is strict for  4\leq t<N-1 , except for  c_{5}(10)=c_{6}(10)=12 .
Previously,  c_{t}(N)  has only been understood when  t  is of constant size, as in (1.2), or when  t>(1+\epsilon)\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2\pi}\sqrt{N}\log N , in which case  c_{t}(N)\sim p(N)  [Mor19]. Our contribution is a saddle point asymptotic formula for  c_{t}(N)  that is valid in all ranges of  t  and  N  and includes these formulas as special cases. Before stating the theorem, we will need a few definitions.
Let  e(z)=e^{2\pi iz} , and for  \operatorname{Im}z>0 , let  \eta(z)  denote the Dedekind eta function
 \eta(z)=e\left(\frac{z}{24}\right)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-e(nz)). 
(1.3)
Let
 f_{t}(z)=\frac{\eta(tz)^{t}}{\eta(z)}, 
(1.4)
which is a modular form of weight  \frac{t-1}{2}  and level  t , and which will be of central importance in this paper. The relationship between  f_{t}(z)  and  t -core partitions comes from the remarkable classical identity for the generating function of  c_{t}(N)  [JK84, (2.7.17)]
 \sum_{N=0}^{\infty}c_{t}(N)e(Nz)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-e(ntz))^{t}}{1-e(nz)}=e\left(\tfrac{1-t^{2}}{24}z\right)f_{t}(z). 
(1.5)
We also define
 \mu_{k}(z)=-\frac{z^{k+1}}{2\pi i}\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{k}\log\eta(z). 
(1.6)
When  k\geq 1 ,  \mu_{k}(z)=-\frac{z^{k+1}}{24}E_{2}^{(k-1)}(z)  where  E_{2}(z)=1-24\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)e(nz)  is the Eisenstein series of weight  2  and  \sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d . In particular, note that  \mu_{1}(iy)=\frac{y^{2}}{24}\left(1-24\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma(n)e^{-2\pi ny}\right)  is real-valued.
Our main theorem is the following asymptotic formula for  c_{t}(N) . Unless otherwise indicated with a subscript, all implied constants are universal.
Theorem 1.4.
Suppose  N\geq 0  and  t\geq 6 .
1. 
There is a unique value of  y>0  satisfying
 \frac{\mu_{1}(ity)-\mu_{1}(iy)}{y^{2}}=N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}. 
Moreover,  y  lies in the range
 \frac{t-1}{4\pi\left(N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}\right)}<y<\frac{1}{\frac{3}{\pi}+\sqrt{24N-1+\frac{9}{\pi^{2}}}}. 
2. 
For this value of  y ,
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{y^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{2\pi y\left(N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}\right)}f_{t}(iy)}{\sqrt{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\min(t,\sqrt{N})}\right)\right). 
3. 
For this value of  y , if  ty\geq\frac{1}{2} , then
 c_{t+1}(N+t)-c_{t}(N+t)=(2\pi ty-1)c_{t}(N)\left(1+O\left(y+tye^{-2\pi ty}\right)\right). 
Theorem 1.4 shows that  c_{t}(N)  exhibits a change in behavior depending on how  t^{2}  compares in size to  N . In order to interpret this result, let us examine in turn the ranges where  t^{2}  is much smaller than  N , similar in size to  N , and much bigger than  N .
In the range  t^{2}\log t<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{1+\epsilon}N  where  \epsilon>0 , we have  y\approx\frac{t-1}{4\pi\left(N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}\right)} , and part 2 of Theorem 1.4 implies
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}\left(N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}\right)^{\frac{t-3}{2}}\left(1+O\left(t^{-\min\left(\epsilon,1\right)}\right)\right). 
(1.7)
This expands the range of validity of Lulov and Pittel’s formula (1.2) from fixed  t  to  t^{2}\log t<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{1+\epsilon}N . When  t^{2}\log t<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{2+\epsilon}N , a slightly different approach allows us to refine the error term in (1.7) to be exponentially small.
Theorem 1.5.
For all  \epsilon>0 , if  t\geq 6  and  t^{2}\log t<\frac{8\pi^{2}}{2+\epsilon}N , then
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}\left(N+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}\right)^{\frac{t-3}{2}}\left(1+O\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}t}+e^{-\frac{1}{6}t}+te^{-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{t(t-1)}N}\right)\right). 
In the range  t>(1+\epsilon)\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2\pi}\sqrt{N}\log N , we have  y\approx\frac{1}{\frac{3}{\pi}+\sqrt{24N-1+\frac{9}{\pi^{2}}}} , and part 2 of Theorem 1.4 tells us only that
 c_{t}(N)=p(N)\left(1+O\left(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\log N\right)\right). 
(1.8)
In this range, almost all partitions are  t -cores, and we may once again refine (1.8), this time to evaluate the remainder.
Theorem 1.6.
For all  \epsilon>0 , if  t>(1+\epsilon)\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2\pi}\sqrt{N}\log N , then
 p(N)-c_{t}(N)=tp(N-t)+O_{\epsilon}\left(t^{2}p(N-2t)\right). 
(1.9)
In the intermediary range  t\asymp\sqrt{N} ,  t -core partitions have not previously been understood. It turns out that  c_{t}(N)  has an asymptotic formula of the same shape as the Hardy–Ramanujan asymptotic formula for partitions.
Corollary 1.7.
Suppose  t^{2}=\kappa N+o(t) . Then,
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{e^{2\pi\sqrt{A(\kappa)N}}}{B(\kappa)N}\left(1+o(1)\right) 
where  A(\kappa)  and  B(\kappa)  are constants defined by choosing  v  so that
 \frac{1}{24v^{2}}-\frac{1}{24}+\frac{1}{v^{2}}\mu_{1}(iv)=\frac{1}{\kappa}, 
and letting
 A(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{v^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{12}-\mu_{0}(iv)+\mu_{1}(iv)\right)^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad B(\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{v^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{12}-\mu_{2}(iv)}. 
We will show in §5.3 that the function  \frac{1}{24v^{2}}-\frac{1}{24}+\frac{1}{v^{2}}\mu_{1}(iv)  is a monotonically decreasing bijection  (0,\infty)\to(0,\infty) , so  v  always exists and is unique.

The constants  A(\kappa)  and  B(\kappa)  of Corollary 1.7 satisfy
 \lim_{\kappa\to\infty}A(\kappa)=\frac{1}{6}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}B(\kappa)=4\sqrt{3}, 
(1.10)
which makes sense in light of (1.8) because  p(N)=\frac{e^{2\pi\sqrt{N/6}}}{4\sqrt{3}N}\left(1+o(1)\right) .
When  \frac{t^{2}}{N}  is bounded from above, the ratio of main terms for  c_{t+1}(N)  and  c_{t}(N)  in part 2 of Theorem 1.4 is uniformly bounded from below by a constant larger than  1 . This will prove Stanton’s conjecture in this range for sufficiently large  t . When  \frac{t^{2}}{N}  grows larger, (1.8) presents an obstruction — both  c_{t+1}(N)  and  c_{t}(N)  are approximately the same size as  p(N) . In this case, it is better to consider their difference  c_{t+1}(N)-c_{t}(N)  rather than their ratio, and we will use part 3 of Theorem 1.4 to complete the proof of Stanton’s conjecture for  t  sufficiently large.
Since our goal is to prove Stanton’s conjecture for all  t , not just  t  sufficiently large, we will actually keep track of the constants involved and prove an explicit version of Theorem 1.4 in Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 and an explicit version of Theorem 1.5 in Remark 5.4. This will allow us to precisely determine which values of  t  and  N  are large enough for the asymptotic formulas to settle the conjecture. The remaining cases will be few enough that we can check them manually with SageMath [Sage].
The organization of this paper is as follows. In §2, we will sketch the argument used to prove Theorem 1.4 and motivate why the main terms take the form they do. In §3, we will bound the contribution away from the saddle point. This is the heart of the paper, and is the main input that allows us to evaluate  c_{t}(N)  in new ranges. In §4, we will prove some technical lemmas that will be useful in §5, mostly involving bounding the functions  \mu_{k}  for  k\leq 4 . In §5, we will use the saddle point method to prove explicit versions of Theorems 1.4 and 1.5 and also prove Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 1.7. In §6, we will prove Stanton’s conjecture.
Notation
Because our goal is to prove an exact inequality, we will require the use of an explicit version of big  O  notation. To that end, we use the symbol  \vartheta  to denote a complex number of magnitude  \leq 1  which is allowed to depend on all parameters, and which may be different at each occurrence. For example,  a=b+\vartheta c  means  |a-b|\leq|c| . As usual with big  O  notation, equations involving  \vartheta  should be read from left to right, so for example  1=2\vartheta , but it would not be correct to write  2\vartheta=1 .

2 Sketch of the proof of Theorem 1.4
Let us begin by sketching the method used to prove Theorem 1.4. Over the course of §3, §4, and §5, we will make this argument rigorous and explicit.
In contrast to previous papers on this subject [GO00, And08, KR14], which decompose  f_{t}(z)  into a sum of Eisenstein series and cusp forms, our approach is to use the saddle point method to evaluate the integral
 c_{t}(N)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-Mz\right)f_{t}(z)dx=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-Mz+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log f_{t}(z)\right)dx. 
(2.1)
The functions  \mu_{k}(z)  defined in (1.6) arise from the term  \frac{1}{2\pi i}\log f_{t}(z)  in the exponential above, whose Taylor expansion is
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\log f_{t}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\frac{\mu_{k}(iy)-\mu_{k}(ity)}{(iy)^{k+1}}. 
(2.2)
In §3, we will use the functional equation for  \eta(z)  to show that this integral is dominated by the contribution when  x  is close to  0 , and in §4, we will show that the higher order terms in the expansion (2.2) are small compared to the second order term.
Together, these estimates imply that we may approximate  c_{t}(N)  as
 c_{t}(N)\approx\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e\left(-M(x+iy)+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\frac{\mu_{k}(iy)-\mu_{k}(ity)}{(iy)^{k+1}}\right)dx. 
(2.3)
So far, this approximation holds for any  y>0 , and we are free to choose whichever value of  y  makes (2.3) easiest to evaluate. To that end, note that the integrand in (2.3) is an exponential of a quadratic polynomial in  x , and if we choose  y  satisfying  \frac{\mu_{t}(ity)-\mu_{1}(iy)}{y^{2}}=M , as in part 1 of Theorem 1.4, then the linear term vanishes. With this value of  y , our integral reduces to
 \displaystyle c_{t}(N)\approx e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2}\frac{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}{(iy)^{3}}\right)dx. 
(2.4)
If we expand the range of integration now to  (-\infty,\infty) , then what remains is simply a Gaussian integral, which we can evaluate to find
 c_{t}(N)\approx e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\sqrt{\frac{y^{3}}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}}. 
(2.5)
This is the main term in part 2 of Theorem 1.4.
For part 3 of Theorem 1.4 we must analyze the difference
 c_{t+1}(N+t)-c_{t}(N+t)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-(M+t)z\right)f_{t}(z)\left(e\left(-\frac{2t+1}{24}z\right)\frac{f_{t+1}(z)}{f_{t}(z)}-1\right)dx. 
(2.6)
Let us consider the last term  e\left(-\frac{2t+1}{24}z\right)\frac{f_{t+1}(z)}{f_{t}(z)}-1  in the integrand. So long as  z  is small enough (once again afforded by §3) and  ty  is large enough, we can use the approximation
 \displaystyle e\left(-\frac{2t+1}{24}z\right)\frac{f_{t+1}(z)}{f_{t}(z)} 
 \displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(1-e(n(t+1)z)\right)^{t+1}}{\left(1-e(ntz)\right)^{t}} 
(2.7)
 \displaystyle\approx 1+te(tz)-(t+1)e((t+1)z) 
 \displaystyle\approx 1-e(tz)\left(2\pi itz+1\right). 
Therefore,
 \displaystyle c_{t+1}(N+t)-c_{t}(N+t) 
 \displaystyle\approx\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-(M+t)z\right)f_{t}(z)(-2\pi itz-1)e(tz)dx 
(2.8)
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-Mz\right)f_{t}(z)(2\pi ty-1-2\pi itx)dx 
 \displaystyle=(2\pi ty-1)c_{t}(N)-2\pi it\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}xe\left(-Mz\right)f_{t}(z)dx. 
Since our chosen value of  y  makes  e(-Mz)f_{t}(z)  approximately an even function of  x , this last integral is small, and we obtain
 c_{t+1}(N+t)-c_{t}(N+t)\approx(2\pi ty-1)c_{t}(N). 
(2.9)
This is the main term in part 3 of Theorem 1.4.
3 Bounding the contribution away from the saddle point
Our starting point is the Fourier inversion integral
 c_{t}(N)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-Mz\right)f_{t}(z)dx. 
(3.1)
The Fourier coefficients  c_{t}(N)  of  f_{t}(z)  are all non-negative, so  \left|f_{t}(z)\right|\leq\left|f_{t}(iy)\right|=f_{t}(iy) , and hence the magnitude of the integrand in (3.1) is maximized when  x=0 . Our goal in this section is to prove the following proposition, which shows that this estimate can be substantially improved so long as  x  is not too close to  0 .
Proposition 3.1.
If  \min(t,\frac{1}{y})\geq 1000 , then
 \int_{\frac{y}{3}\leq\left|x\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx<ye^{-\frac{1}{70}\min(t,\frac{1}{y})}. 
Recall that the group  \SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})  of  2\times 2  integer matrices with determinant  1  acts on the upper half plane  \mathbb{H}=\{z\mid y>0\}  by linear fractional transformations
 \gamma z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\text{ for }\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}). 
(3.2)
The Dedekind eta function (1.3) satisfies the transformation formula
 \eta\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=e\left(\frac{a+d}{24c}+\tfrac{1}{2}s(-d,c)\right)\sqrt{-i(cz+d)}\eta(z)\text{ for all }\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) 
(3.3)
where  s(h,k)  is the Dedekind sum  \sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\frac{r}{k}\left(\left\{\frac{hr}{k}\right\}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) . This enables us to approximate  \eta(z)  anywhere in the upper half plane, and in particular implies
 \displaystyle\eta(-1/z) 
 \displaystyle=\sqrt{-iz}\eta(z)\quad\text{and} 
(3.4)
 \displaystyle\left|\eta(\gamma z)\right| 
 \displaystyle=\left(\frac{y}{\operatorname{Im}\gamma z}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\eta(z)\right|\text{ for all }\gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) 
by applying the matrix  \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}  and taking magnitudes, respectively.
We will need the following two lemmas for the proof of Proposition 3.1.
Lemma 3.2.
For all  x\in\mathbb{R}  and  y\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} ,
 \left|\eta(z)\right|=e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}y+\vartheta 1.01e^{-2\pi y}}. 
Proof.
Suppose  y\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} . Since  \log\eta(z)=\frac{\pi i}{12}z-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma(n)}{n}e(nz) , we may use the elementary bound  \sigma(n)\leq n^{2}  to find
 \left|\log\eta(z)-\frac{\pi i}{12}z\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma(n)}{n}e^{-2\pi yn}<\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}ne^{-2\pi yn}\leq\frac{e^{-2\pi y}}{(1-e^{-\sqrt{3}\pi})^{2}}<1.01e^{-2\pi y}. 
(3.5)
∎
Lemma 3.3.
For all  x\in\mathbb{R}  and  y>0 ,
 \left|\eta(z)\right|<\frac{7}{9}y^{-\frac{1}{4}}. 
In fact,  y^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\eta(z)\right|  is maximized when  z=\frac{1+i\sqrt{3}}{2} , where it equals  \left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi}{3\Gamma(2/3)^{3}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=0.7723\dots  [Rou08].
Proof.
By the second line in (3.4), the function  (\operatorname{Im}z)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\eta(z)\right|  is  \SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) -invariant, so we may assume  z=x+iy  is in the standard fundamental domain  \left\{z\in\mathbb{H}\mid\left|z\right|\geq 1,\left|x\right|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\right\}  for  \SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) , and in particular  y\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} . By Lemma 3.2,
 y^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\eta(x+iy)\right|\leq y^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}y+1.01e^{-\sqrt{3}\pi}}\leq\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\frac{3}{\pi}+1.01e^{-\sqrt{3}\pi}}<\frac{7}{9}. 
(3.6)
∎
The idea behind the proof of Proposition 3.1 is that Lemma 3.2 provides us with a good bound for  \eta(z)  so long as  y  is large, so we choose  \gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})  so that  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z  is large, and use (3.4) to relate  \left|\eta(\gamma z)\right|  with  \left|\eta(z)\right| . Sometimes, we are lucky and  \operatorname{Im}\gamma tz  is also large. If not, we will need to find another element of  \SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})  whose action on  tz  sends it to a value with large imaginary part.
Proof of Proposition 3.1.
Choose  \gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})  such that  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} , and assume without loss of generality that  c>0  and  0\leq d<c . By (3.4) and Lemma 3.2,
 \displaystyle\eta(iy) 
 \displaystyle=y^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta(i/y)\leq y^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\frac{1}{y}+1.01e^{-2000\pi}}\quad\text{and} 
(3.7)
 \displaystyle\left|\eta(z)\right| 
 \displaystyle=\left(\frac{y}{\operatorname{Im}\gamma z}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left|\eta(\gamma z)\right|\geq\left(\frac{y}{\operatorname{Im}\gamma z}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\operatorname{Im}\gamma z-1.01e^{-\sqrt{3}\pi}}, 
so we obtain
 \left|\frac{\eta(iy)}{\eta(z)}\right|<\left(y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\left(\frac{1}{y}-\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\right)+\frac{1}{228}}. 
(3.8)
It remains to bound  \left|\frac{\eta(tz)^{t}}{\eta(ity)^{t}}\right| , for which we split up the proof into two cases depending on whether  ty\geq 1  or  ty\leq 1 .
First, suppose  ty\geq 1 , so that by Lemma 3.2,
 \left|\frac{\eta(tz)^{t}}{\eta(ity)^{t}}\right|=e^{\vartheta 2.02te^{-2\pi ty}}\leq e^{2.02e^{-2\pi}\frac{1}{y}}<e^{\frac{1}{265}\frac{1}{y}}, 
(3.9)
and hence
 \left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|=\left|\frac{\eta(tz)^{t}}{\eta(ity)^{t}}\right|\left|\frac{\eta(iy)}{\eta(z)}\right|<\left(y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\left(\frac{1}{y}-\operatorname{Im}\gamma z-\frac{12}{\pi}\frac{1}{265}\frac{1}{y}\right)+\frac{1}{228}} 
(3.10)
Note that  y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z=\frac{y^{2}}{(cx+d)^{2}+c^{2}y^{2}} . If  c>1 , then  y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\leq\frac{1}{c^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4} . Otherwise,  c=1  and  d=0 , and  y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z=\frac{y^{2}}{x^{2}+y^{2}}\leq\frac{9}{10} . The function  v\mapsto v^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{12}\frac{1}{y}v}  is bounded in the range  \left[\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}y,\frac{9}{10}\right]  by its value at  \frac{9}{10} , so
 \left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|<\left(\frac{9}{10}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\frac{1}{y}\left(1-\frac{9}{10}-\frac{12}{\pi}\frac{1}{265}\right)+\frac{1}{228}}<e^{-\frac{1}{70}\frac{1}{y}} 
(3.11)
as claimed.
Now, suppose  ty\leq 1 , so that by Lemma 3.2,
 \eta(ity)^{t}\geq(ty)^{-\frac{t}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\frac{1}{y}-1.01e^{-2\pi}t}, 
(3.12)
and hence
 \left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|\leq\left|\eta(tz)\right|^{t}(ty)^{\frac{t}{2}}(y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{12}\operatorname{Im}\gamma z+\frac{1}{528}t}. 
(3.13)
We will split up the remaining argument into two further cases, depending on whether  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t  or  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t .
In the first case, when  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t , we may use Lemma 3.3 to bound  \eta(tz)  by  \frac{7}{9}(ty)^{-\frac{1}{4}}  and obtain
 \left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|\leq\left(\frac{7}{9}\right)^{t}(ty)^{\frac{t}{4}}(y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\frac{\pi}{12}\operatorname{Im}\gamma z+\frac{1}{528}t}, 
(3.14)
which is maximized in the range  \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\leq\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t  when  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t , where it is equal to
 \left(\frac{7}{9}\right)^{t}(ty)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{24}\pi+\frac{1}{528}\right)t}<1.04(ty)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{1}{45}t}. 
(3.15)
Let us consider now the second case, where  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t . Let  \gamma^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}*&*\\
c/(t,c)&dt/(t,c)\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) , which exists since  \frac{c}{(t,c)}  and  \frac{dt}{(t,c)}  are coprime. Then,
 \left|\eta(tz)\right|=\left(\frac{ty}{\operatorname{Im}\gamma^{\prime}tz}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left|\eta(\gamma^{\prime}tz)\right|, 
(3.16)
and
 \operatorname{Im}\gamma^{\prime}tz=\frac{ty}{\left|\frac{c}{(t,c)}tz+\frac{dt}{(t,c)}\right|^{2}}=\frac{(t,c)^{2}}{t}\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}. 
(3.17)
Therefore,
 \left|\eta(tz)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{(t,c)}{t}}\left(\frac{y}{\operatorname{Im}\gamma z}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}\frac{(t,c)^{2}}{t}\operatorname{Im}\gamma z+1.01e^{-\sqrt{3}\pi}}, 
(3.18)
so
 \left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|\leq(t,c)^{\frac{t}{2}}(y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}((t,c)^{2}-1)\operatorname{Im}\gamma z+\frac{1}{159}t}. 
(3.19)
Suppose first that  (t,c)>1 . The function  v\mapsto v^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{12}((t,c)^{2}-1)v}  is maximized at  v=\frac{3}{\pi}\frac{t-1}{(t,c)^{2}-1}<\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t  and decreasing for larger  v , so the right-hand side of (3.19) is bounded in the range  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t  by its value when  \operatorname{Im}\gamma z=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t . Hence,
 \displaystyle\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right| 
 \displaystyle\leq(t,c)^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}ty\right)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{24}\pi((t,c)^{2}-1)t+\frac{1}{159}t} 
(3.20)
 \displaystyle<2^{\frac{t}{2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}ty\right)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{8}\pi t+\frac{1}{159}t} 
 \displaystyle<\left(ty\right)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{1}{3}t}. 
What remains is the case  (t,c)=1 , where the right-hand side of (3.19) collapses to become  (y\operatorname{Im}\gamma z)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{\frac{1}{159}t} . Letting
 \mathfrak{m}(c,d)=\left\{x\mid\operatorname{Im}\gamma z\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\right\}=\left\{x\mid\left|\frac{x+\frac{d}{c}}{y}\right|<\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{1}{c^{2}ty}-1}\right\} 
(3.21)
denote the relevant portion of the interval, we have
 \int_{\frac{y}{3}\leq\left|x\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx\leq\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}(1,0)\\
\left|x\right|>\frac{y}{3}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx+\sum_{c=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=0\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{c-1}\int_{\mathfrak{m}(c,d)}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx+1.04(ty)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{1}{45}t}. 
(3.22)
Letting  u=\frac{x+\frac{d}{c}}{y} , we find
 \displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{m}(c,d)}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx 
 \displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{1}{159}t}\int_{\mathfrak{m}(c,d)}\left(\frac{y^{2}}{\left|cz+d\right|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}dx 
(3.23)
 \displaystyle=yc^{\frac{1-t}{2}}e^{\frac{1}{159}t}\int_{\left|u\right|<\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{1}{c^{2}ty}-1}}(u^{2}+1)^{\frac{1-t}{4}}du 
 \displaystyle<0.2yc^{\frac{1-t}{2}}e^{\frac{1}{159}t}, 
so
 \displaystyle\sum_{c=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=0\\
(c,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{c-1}\int_{\mathfrak{m}(c,d)}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx<0.2y\left(\zeta\left(\tfrac{t-3}{2}\right)-1\right)e^{\frac{1}{159}t}<ye^{-\frac{1}{3}t}. 
(3.24)
Letting  v=1+(\frac{x}{y})^{2} , we also have
 \displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}(1,0)\\
\left|x\right|>\frac{y}{3}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx 
 \displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{1}{159}t}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}(1,0)\\
\left|x\right|>\frac{y}{3}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{y^{2}}{\left|z\right|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}dx 
(3.25)
 \displaystyle=ye^{\frac{1}{159}t}\int_{\frac{10}{9}}^{\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{1}{ty}}v^{\frac{1-t}{4}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{v-1}}dv 
 \displaystyle<3ye^{\frac{1}{159}t}\int_{\frac{10}{9}}^{\infty}v^{\frac{1-t}{4}}dv 
 \displaystyle<0.02ye^{-\frac{1}{50}t}. 
Altogether, we have
 \int_{\frac{y}{3}\leq\left|x\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx<0.02ye^{-\frac{1}{50}t}+ye^{-\frac{1}{3}t}+1.04(ty)^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{1}{45}t}<ye^{-\frac{1}{70}t}, 
(3.26)
as claimed.
∎
4 Technical lemmas
4.1 Explicit Gaussian integrals
In §5, we will use the following explicit bound for the error term in a Gaussian integral. The proof is elementary, but somewhat long.
Lemma 4.1.
Suppose  \alpha>38 ,  \beta\in\left(-\frac{2}{25},\frac{2}{25}\right) , and  \varepsilon\in(-1,1) . Then,
 \displaystyle\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}dx 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(1+\vartheta\frac{3.45}{\alpha}\right) 
 \displaystyle\left|\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}xe^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}dx\right| 
 \displaystyle\leq 2\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}}. 
Proof.
Let
 I=\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}dx, 
(4.1)
which we decompose as  I=I_{1}-\varepsilon I_{2}+\vartheta I_{3}  where
 \displaystyle I_{1} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx, 
(4.2)
 \displaystyle I_{2} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}2\pi i\alpha x^{3}e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx,\quad\text{and} 
 \displaystyle I_{3} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}\left|(1-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3})e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}-e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}\right|dx. 
The main term  I_{1}  is simply
 \displaystyle I_{1} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx+\vartheta 2\int_{1/3}^{\infty}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx 
(4.3)
 \displaystyle=e^{-\pi\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}+\vartheta 2\int_{1/3}^{\infty}\frac{2\pi\alpha x}{2\pi\alpha\frac{1}{3}}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx 
 \displaystyle=e^{-\pi\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}+\vartheta\frac{3}{\pi\alpha}e^{-\frac{\pi}{9}\alpha}. 
Similarly, the term  I_{2}  is
 \displaystyle I_{2} 
 \displaystyle=2\pi\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ix^{3}e(\beta x)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx+\vartheta 4\pi\alpha\int_{1/3}^{\infty}x^{3}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx 
(4.4)
 \displaystyle=e^{-\pi\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(-3\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+2\pi\frac{\beta^{3}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)+\vartheta\left(\frac{2}{9}+\frac{2}{\pi\alpha}\right)e^{-\frac{\pi}{9}\alpha} 
As for  I_{3} , using Taylor’s theorem in the form  e^{z}=1+ze^{sz}=1+z+\frac{1}{2}z^{2}e^{s^{\prime}z}  for some  s,s^{\prime}\in(0,1) , we find that the integrand in  I_{3}  is equal to
 \displaystyle e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}\left|(1-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3})-e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}\right| 
(4.5)
 \displaystyle\leq e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}\left(\left|(1-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3})-e^{-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3}}\right|+\left|e^{-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3}}-e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}\right|\right) 
 \displaystyle\leq e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}\left(2\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}x^{6}+3\pi\alpha x^{4}e^{3\pi\alpha x^{4}}\right). 
Therefore,
 \displaystyle I_{3} 
 \displaystyle\leq\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}\left(2\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}x^{6}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}+3\pi\alpha x^{4}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}(1-3x^{2})}\right)dx 
(4.6)
 \displaystyle\leq 2\pi^{2}\alpha^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{6}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx+3\pi\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{4}e^{-\frac{2}{3}\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx 
 \displaystyle=\left(\frac{15}{4\pi}+\frac{(3/2)^{\frac{9}{2}}}{\pi}\right)\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}}. 
Altogether, we have
 \displaystyle I 
 \displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\pi\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(1+3\varepsilon\frac{\beta}{\alpha}-2\pi\varepsilon\frac{\beta^{3}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)+\vartheta e^{-\frac{\pi}{9}\alpha}\left(\frac{5}{\pi\alpha}+\frac{2}{9}\right)+\vartheta\left(\frac{15}{4\pi}+\frac{(3/2)^{\frac{9}{2}}}{\pi}\right)\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}} 
(4.7)
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(1+\vartheta\frac{3.45}{\alpha}\right), 
as claimed.
We will now treat the second integral in the lemma. Let
 J=\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}xe^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}dx. 
(4.8)
Since  xe^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}  is odd, we can write
 J=\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}x\left(e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}-e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}\right)dx. 
(4.9)
The integrand has magnitude bounded by
 \displaystyle\left|x\right|e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}\left|e^{-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3}+\vartheta 3\pi\alpha x^{4}}-1\right| 
(4.10)
 \displaystyle\leq\left|x\right|e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}\left|-2\pi i\varepsilon\alpha x^{3}+\vartheta 3\pi\alpha x^{4}\right|e^{3\pi\alpha x^{4}} 
 \displaystyle\leq\left(2\pi\alpha x^{4}+3\pi\alpha\left|x\right|^{5}\right)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}(1-3x^{2})}, 
so we find
 \displaystyle\left|J\right| 
 \displaystyle\leq\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}\left(2\pi\alpha x^{4}+3\pi\alpha\left|x\right|^{5}\right)e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}(1-3x^{2})}dx 
(4.11)
 \displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(2\pi\alpha x^{4}+3\pi\alpha\left|x\right|^{5}\right)e^{-\frac{2}{3}\pi\alpha x^{2}}dx 
 \displaystyle=\frac{(3/2)^{\frac{7}{2}}}{\pi}\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{81}{4\pi^{2}}\alpha^{-2} 
 \displaystyle<2\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}}. 
∎
4.2 The functions  \mu_{k} 
Recall the functions
 \mu_{k}(z)=-\frac{z^{k+1}}{2\pi i}\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)^{k}\log\eta(z) 
(4.12)
from the introduction (1.6), which appear in the Taylor expansion
 \frac{1}{2\pi i}\log f_{t}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\frac{\mu_{k}(iy)-\mu_{k}(ity)}{(iy)^{k+1}}. 
(4.13)
Our main goal in this section is to bound the third and fourth terms in this Taylor expansion in terms of the second.
Using the functional equation  \eta(z)=(-iz)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta(-1/z) , we find two different expansions for  \mu_{k}(z) , useful depending on whether  \operatorname{Im}z  or  \operatorname{Im}\frac{-1}{z}  is large. We have
 \displaystyle\mu_{k}(z) 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{k+1}(2\pi in)^{k-1}\sigma(n)e(nz)-\begin{cases}z^{2}/24&\text{if $k=0,1$}\\
0&\text{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases} 
(4.14)
 \displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{k}\left(\frac{2\pi in}{z}\right)\sigma(n)e(\tfrac{-n}{z})+\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{24}+\frac{z}{4\pi i}\begin{cases}\log(-iz)&\text{if $k=0$}\\
(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!&\text{if $k\geq 1$}\end{cases} 
where  P_{k}(r)  is the degree  k-1  polynomial (Laurent in the case  k=0 ) defined by the recurrence  P_{0}(r)=r^{-1}  and
 P_{k}(r)=(r-k)P_{k-1}(r)-rP_{k-1}^{\prime}(r). 
(4.15)
We will also need expansions for  \mu_{k}^{\prime}(z) . We have
 \displaystyle\mu_{k}^{\prime}(z) 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left((2\pi inz)^{k+1}+(k+1)(2\pi inz)^{k}\right)\frac{\sigma(n)}{2\pi in}e(nz)-\begin{cases}z/12&\text{if $k=0,1$}\\
0&\text{if $k\geq 2$}\end{cases} 
(4.16)
 \displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}Q_{k}\left(\frac{2\pi in}{z}\right)\frac{\sigma(n)}{2\pi in}e(\tfrac{-n}{z})+\frac{1}{4\pi i}\begin{cases}1+\log(-iz)&\text{if $k=0$}\\
(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!&\text{if $k\geq 1$}\end{cases} 
where  Q_{k}(r)  is the degree  k+1  polynomial defined by the recurrence  Q_{0}(r)=r+1 , and
 \displaystyle Q_{k}(r) 
 \displaystyle=(r-k+1)Q_{k-1}(r)-rQ_{k-1}^{\prime}(r) 
(4.17)
 \displaystyle=r^{2}(P_{k}(r)-P_{k}^{\prime}(r)). 
The first few values of  P_{k}(r)  and  Q_{k}(r)  are given in the table (4.18) below.
 \begin{array}[]{c|c|c}k&P_{k}(r)&Q_{k}(r)\\
\hline\cr 0&r^{-1}&r+1\\
1&1&r^{2}\\
2&r-2&r^{3}-3r^{2}\\
3&r^{2}-6r+6&r^{4}-8r^{3}+12r^{2}\\
4&r^{3}-12r^{2}+36r-24&r^{5}-15r^{4}+60r^{3}-60r^{2}\end{array} 
(4.18)
As a first observation, we can see directly from (4.16) and (4.18) that
 \mu_{2}^{\prime}(z)+\mu_{2}^{\prime}(\tfrac{-1}{z})=-\frac{1}{4\pi i}. 
(4.19)
Lemma 4.2.
1. 
The function  y\mapsto i\mu_{2}^{\prime}(iy)  is a monotonically increasing bijection  (0,\infty)\to(-\frac{1}{4\pi},0) .
2. 
The function  y\mapsto\mu_{2}(iy)  is a monotonically decreasing bijection  (0,\infty)\to(0,\frac{1}{12}) .
Suppose  0<y\leq\frac{1}{10}  and  t>1 .
3. 
If  ty\leq 1 , then
 \frac{1}{8\pi}<\frac{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}{ty-y}<\frac{1}{4\pi}.

Our next goal is to replace  e\left(-\tfrac{2t+1}{24}z\right)\frac{f_{t+1}(z)}{f_{t}(z)}-1  in the integrand with  -(2\pi itz+1)e(tz)  in the range  \left|x\right|<\frac{y}{3} . To that end, let  \Upsilon(z)=\frac{f_{t+1}(z)}{f_{t}(z)}e\left(-\tfrac{2t+1}{24}z\right) , and apply Lemma 5.3 to find
 \displaystyle{\displaystyle\left|\Upsilon(z)-1+e(tz)(2\pi itz+1)\right|} 
(5.41)
 \displaystyle\leq{\left|\log\Upsilon(z)+e(tz)(2\pi itz+1)\right|}+{\left|\Upsilon(z)-1-\log\Upsilon(z)\right|} 
 \displaystyle\leq(40\left|z\right|+22e^{-2\pi ty})\left|tz\right|e^{-2\pi ty}+\frac{1}{2}\left|7.5tze^{-2\pi ty}\right|^{2}e^{7.5\left|tz\right|e^{-2\pi ty}} 
 \displaystyle\leq\left(45y+85tye^{-2\pi ty}\right)tye^{-2\pi ty}. 
Therefore, if we let
 \alpha=\frac{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}{y}\in\left(\frac{\min(t,\frac{1}{y})}{26},\frac{\min(t,\frac{1}{y})}{12}\right) 
(5.42)
as in §5.2, then applying (5.18), we have
 \displaystyle 
 \displaystyle\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e(-(M+t)z)f_{t}(z)\left(e\left(-\tfrac{2t+1}{24}z\right)\frac{f_{t+1}(z)}{f_{t}(z)}-1\right)dx 
(5.43)
 \displaystyle=\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e(-(M+t)z)f_{t}(z)e(tz)(-2\pi itz-1)dx 
 \displaystyle+\vartheta\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\alpha}}ye^{2\pi(M+t)y}f_{t}(iy)\left(45y+85tye^{-2\pi ty}\right)tye^{-2\pi ty} 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e(-Mz)f_{t}(z)(-2\pi itz-1)dx 
 \displaystyle+\vartheta\left(56y+105tye^{-2\pi ty}\right)\frac{ty^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy). 
By Lemma 4.1 and (5.15), we have
 \displaystyle\left|\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e(-Mz)f_{t}(z)(-2\pi itx)dx\right| 
 \displaystyle=2\pi ty^{2}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\left|\int_{-1/3}^{1/3}xe^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}\left(1+2i\varepsilon x+\vartheta 3x^{2}\right)}dx\right| 
(5.44)
 \displaystyle\leq 2\pi ty^{2}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\left(2\alpha^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right) 
 \displaystyle\leq 327\frac{ty^{3}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy). 
What remains is
 \displaystyle 
 \displaystyle\int_{-y/3}^{y/3}e(-Mz)f_{t}(z)(2\pi ty-1)dx 
(5.45)
 \displaystyle=(2\pi ty-1)\left(\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e(-Mz)f_{t}(z)+\vartheta 3ye^{-\frac{1}{140}\frac{1}{y}}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\right) 
 \displaystyle=(2\pi ty-1)\left(c_{t}(N)+\vartheta ye^{-\frac{1}{70}\min(t,\frac{1}{y})}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\right) 
Altogether, combining (5.35, 5.43, 5.44, 5.45), we have
 \displaystyle c_{t+1}(N+t)-c_{t}(N+t) 
 \displaystyle=(2\pi ty-1)c_{t}(N)+\vartheta tye^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy) 
(5.46)
 \displaystyle\cdot\left(11e^{-\frac{1}{70}\min(t,\frac{1}{y})}+383\frac{y^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}+105\frac{ty^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}e^{-2\pi ty}\right). 
Finally, note that by Theorem 5.1,
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\alpha}}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)\left(1+\vartheta\frac{3.51}{\alpha}\right)>0.9\frac{y}{\sqrt{\alpha}}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy), 
(5.47)
so we may write (5.46) as
 \displaystyle c_{t+1}(N+t)-c_{t}(N+t) 
(5.48)
 \displaystyle=c_{t}(N)\left(2\pi ty-1+\vartheta\frac{ty}{0.9}\left(11\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{y}e^{-\frac{1}{70}\min(t,\frac{1}{y})}+383y+105tye^{-2\pi ty}\right)\right) 
 \displaystyle=c_{t}(N)\left(2\pi ty-1+\vartheta ty\left(705y+120tye^{-2\pi ty}\right)\right), 
as claimed.
∎
5.5 Proof of Theorem 1.5
As in §5.2, we may assume  N  and  t  are sufficiently large. Let  \hat{\epsilon}=\min(\epsilon,3)  and choose  y=\frac{t-1}{4\pi M} , which we may assume is sufficiently small. The hypotheses in Theorem 1.5 imply
 ty<\frac{2\pi}{(2+\hat{\epsilon})\log t}. 
(5.49)
We may repeat the proof of Proposition 3.1 to find
 \int_{y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}\leq\left|x\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\left|\frac{f_{t}(z)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right|dx\ll\frac{y}{\sqrt{t-1}}e^{-\frac{1}{6}t} 
(5.50)
The hypothesis (5.49) also implies that if  \left|x\right|\leq y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}} , then
 \operatorname{Im}\frac{-1}{tz}\geq\frac{1}{(2+\hat{\epsilon})ty}>\frac{\log t}{2\pi}, 
(5.51)
which means
 \displaystyle f_{t}(z) 
 \displaystyle=t^{-\frac{t}{2}}(-iz)^{\frac{1-t}{2}}\frac{\eta\left(\frac{-1}{tz}\right)^{t}}{\eta\left(\frac{-1}{z}\right)} 
(5.52)
 \displaystyle=t^{-\frac{t}{2}}(-iz)^{\frac{1-t}{2}}e^{\vartheta 2.02te^{-2\pi\operatorname{Im}\frac{-1}{tz}}} 
 \displaystyle=t^{-\frac{t}{2}}(-iz)^{\frac{1-t}{2}}\left(1+O\left(te^{-2\pi\operatorname{Im}\frac{-1}{tz}}\right)\right), 
and in particular  f_{t}(iy)\leq 2t^{-\frac{t}{2}}y^{\frac{1-t}{2}} .
Our goal is to evaluate
 \displaystyle c_{t}(N) 
 \displaystyle=t^{-\frac{t}{2}}\int_{-y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}}^{y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}}e(-Mz)(-iz)^{\frac{1-t}{2}}dx 
(5.53)
 \displaystyle+O\left(t^{\frac{2-t}{2}}\int_{-y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}}^{y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}}\left|e(-Mz)(-iz)^{\frac{1-t}{2}}e^{-2\pi\operatorname{Im}\frac{-1}{tz}}\right|dx\right) 
 \displaystyle+O\left(e^{2\pi My}t^{-\frac{t}{2}}y^{\frac{1-t}{2}}\frac{y}{\sqrt{t-1}}e^{-\frac{1}{6}t}\right). 
Let  \mathcal{L}  be the contour that traverses in straight lines from  -y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}-i\infty  to  -y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}+iy  to  y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}+iy  to  y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}-i\infty . Hankel’s formula states
 \int_{\mathcal{L}}e(-Mz)(-iz)^{\frac{1-t}{2}}dz=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}}, 
(5.54)
which means that the first line in the right-hand side of (5.53) is
 \frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}}+O\left(e^{2\pi My}t^{-\frac{t}{2}}y^{\frac{1-t}{2}}\frac{(1+\hat{\epsilon})^{\frac{1-t}{4}}}{M}\right). 
(5.55)
By widening the domain of integration from  \left|x\right|\leq y\sqrt{1+\hat{\epsilon}}  to  \left|x\right|\leq 2y  and making the change of variables  u=\frac{x}{y} , we find that the second line in the right-hand side of (5.53) is
 \ll e^{2\pi My}t^{\frac{2-t}{2}}y^{\frac{3-t}{2}}\int_{-2}^{2}(1+u^{2})^{\frac{1-t}{4}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}\frac{1}{1+u^{2}}}du. 
(5.56)
We will now show
 \int_{-2}^{2}(1+u^{2})^{\frac{1-t}{4}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}\frac{1}{1+u^{2}}}du\ll\max\left(e^{-\frac{1}{5}t},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-1}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}}\right). 
(5.57)
Indeed, the function  v\mapsto v^{\frac{t-1}{4}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}v}  is maximized at  v=\frac{t(t-1)y}{8\pi} . Therefore, if  \frac{t(t-1)y}{8\pi}\leq 1 , then the integrand in (5.57) is bounded by  e^{-\frac{1}{4}t} , while if  \frac{t(t-1)y}{8\pi}\geq 1 , then the integrand in (5.57) is bounded by  e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}}  and decreasing in  u . This is sufficient to prove (5.57) when  \frac{t(t-1)y}{8\pi}\leq\frac{5}{4} . When  \frac{t(t-1)y}{8\pi}\geq\frac{5}{4} , note that  \frac{1}{1+u^{2}}\geq 1-u^{2}  and  1+u^{2}\geq e^{\frac{5}{6}u^{2}}  if  \left|u\right|\leq\frac{1}{2} . Therefore,
 \displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}(1+u^{2})^{\frac{1-t}{4}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}\frac{1}{1+u^{2}}}du 
 \displaystyle\ll\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e^{-\frac{5}{24}(t-1)u^{2}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}(1-u^{2})}du 
(5.58)
 \displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{120}(t-1)u^{2}}du 
 \displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-1}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}}, 
proving (5.57).
Applying (5.55, 5.56, 5.57) to (5.53), we find
 \displaystyle c_{t}(N) 
 \displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}} 
(5.59)
 \displaystyle+O\left(e^{2\pi My}t^{-\frac{t}{2}}y^{\frac{1-t}{2}}\left(\frac{(1+\hat{\epsilon})^{\frac{1-t}{4}}}{M}+ty\max\left(e^{-\frac{1}{5}t},\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-1}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}}\right)+\frac{y}{\sqrt{t-1}}e^{-\frac{1}{6}t}\right)\right). 
Recalling the choice  y=\frac{t-1}{4\pi M}  (which minimizes  e^{2\pi My}y^{\frac{1-t}{2}} ) and applying Stirling’s formula, we find
 e^{2\pi My}t^{-\frac{t}{2}}y^{\frac{1-t}{2}}=t^{-\frac{t}{2}}\left(\frac{4e\pi M}{t-1}\right)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}\ll\frac{\sqrt{t-1}}{y}\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}}, 
(5.60)
and hence
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}}\left(1+O\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\epsilon)^{-\frac{1}{4}t}+e^{-\frac{1}{6}t}+te^{-\frac{2\pi}{ty}}\right)\right), 
(5.61)
as claimed.
Remark 5.4.
In §6, we will require an explicit version of Theorem 1.5 to prove Stanton’s conjecture for  8\leq t\leq 1000 . By keeping careful track of constants and repeating the proof above with  \epsilon=3 , we can show that if  t\geq 8 ,  M\geq 100\,000 , and  \frac{t(t-1)}{4\pi M}<\frac{1}{5}\min\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}},\frac{2\pi}{\log t}\right) , then
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}}\left(1+\vartheta\left(2.5e^{-\frac{1}{8}t}+20t^{-4}\right)\right). 
(5.62)
5.6 Proof of Theorem 1.6
We may assume  N\geq 3t , since otherwise  c_{t}(N)=p(N)-tp(N-t)+\frac{1}{2}(t^{2}-3t)p(N-2t) .
Let
 y=\frac{1}{\frac{3}{\pi}+\sqrt{24(N-2t)-1+\frac{9}{\pi^{2}}}}, 
(5.63)
so that our assumption  t>(1+\epsilon)\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2\pi}\sqrt{N}\log N  implies  \left|te(tz)\right|=te^{-2\pi ty}\ll_{\epsilon}N^{-\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\log N .
Consider the integrals
 \displaystyle p(N) 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-\left(N-\tfrac{1}{24}\right)z\right)\frac{1}{\eta(z)}dx\quad\text{and} 
(5.64)
 \displaystyle c_{t}(N) 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-\left(N-\tfrac{1}{24}\right)z\right)\frac{1}{\eta(z)}e\left(-\tfrac{t^{2}}{24}z\right)\eta(tz)^{t}dx. 
Note that
 \log\left(e\left(-\tfrac{t^{2}}{24}z\right)\eta(tz)^{t}\right)=-\frac{2\pi i}{z}\left(\mu_{0}(tz)+\frac{(tz)^{2}}{24}\right)=-te(tz)+O_{\epsilon}\left(te(2tz)\right), 
(5.65)
so
 \displaystyle c_{t}(N) 
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-\left(N-\tfrac{1}{24}\right)z\right)\frac{1}{\eta(z)}e^{-te(tz)+O_{\epsilon}\left(te(2tz)\right)}dx 
(5.66)
 \displaystyle=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}e\left(-\left(N-\tfrac{1}{24}\right)z\right)\frac{1}{\eta(z)}\left(1-te(tz)+O_{\epsilon}\left(t^{2}e(2tz)\right)\right)dx 
 \displaystyle=p(N)-tp(N-t)+O_{\epsilon}\left(t^{2}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\left|e\left(-\left(N-2t-\tfrac{1}{24}\right)z\right)\frac{1}{\eta(z)}\right|dx\right) 
 \displaystyle=p(N)-tp(N-t)+O_{\epsilon}\left(t^{2}p(N-2t)\right), 
with this last equality implied by e.g. (5.18) applied to  c_{N}(N-2t)=p(N-2t) .
6 Proof of Stanton’s conjecture
In this section, we will prove Stanton’s conjecture 1.1.
Recall from the introduction that we may assume  4\leq t<N-1 . To begin, we check using SageMath [Sage] that Stanton’s conjecture holds for all  t<N-1  and  N<100\,000 , and also that it holds for all  t<1000  and  N<500\,000 .
Kim-Rouse [KR14] verified the conjecture for  4\leq t<8 . In §6.1, we will use Theorem 1.5 (or rather, the explicit version mentioned in Remark 5.4) to verify the conjecture for  8\leq t<1000  and  N\geq 500\,000 .
If  t\geq 1000  and  N\geq 100\,000 , then as in §5.1, choose  y  in the range
 \frac{t-1}{4\pi M}<y<\frac{1}{\frac{3}{\pi}+\sqrt{24N-1+\frac{9}{\pi^{2}}}}<\frac{1}{1000} 
(6.1)
such that
 \frac{\mu_{1}(ity)-\mu_{1}(iy)}{y^{2}}=M. 
(6.2)
We will complete the proof of Stanton’s conjecture by treating the case  ty\leq\frac{1}{2}  in §6.2 using Theorem 5.1, and the case  ty\geq\frac{1}{2}  in §6.3 using Theorem 5.2.
Remark 6.1.
For any  \epsilon>0  and  t  and  N  sufficiently large depending on  \epsilon , we could prove Stanton’s conjecture using Theorem 1.5 if  ty<(1-\epsilon)\frac{\pi}{\log t} , using Theorem 5.1 if  ty<(1-\epsilon)\frac{\log t}{2\pi} , using Theorem 5.2 if  ty\geq\frac{1}{2} , and using Theorem 1.6 if  ty>(1+\epsilon)\frac{\log t}{\pi} , giving us some leeway in deciding which theorem to use for which range. Our decision was based on an attempt to minimize the number of cases that must be checked by hand.
6.1 Stanton’s conjecture for  8\leq t<1000  and  N\geq 500\,000 
With these assumptions on  t  and  N , we have  \frac{t(t-1)}{4\pi M}<\frac{1}{5}\min\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}},\frac{2\pi}{\log t}\right) , so by Remark 5.4,
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{t-1}{2}}}{t^{\frac{t}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}M^{\frac{t-3}{2}}\left(1+\vartheta E(t)\right) 
(6.3)
where  E(t)=2.5e^{-\frac{1}{8}t}+20t^{-4} .
Applying the inequalities
 \frac{t^{\frac{t}{2}}}{(t+1)^{\frac{t+1}{2}}}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{e(t+1)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)}>\sqrt{\frac{2}{t}} 
(6.4)
yields
 \frac{c_{t+1}(N)}{c_{t}(N)}>\sqrt{\frac{4\pi M}{et(t+1)}}\frac{1-E(t+1)}{1+E(t)}. 
(6.5)
For all  8\leq t<1000 , we have
 \sqrt{\frac{4\pi\cdot 500\,000}{et(t+1)}}\frac{1-E(t+1)}{1+E(t)}>1.5, 
(6.6)
which proves Stanton’s conjecture in this range.
6.2 Stanton’s conjecture for  t\geq 1000 ,  N\geq 100\,000 , and  ty\leq\frac{1}{2} 
Under these assumptions, we may use Theorem 5.1, which tells us
 c_{t}(N)=\frac{y^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{2\pi My}f_{t}(iy)}{\sqrt{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}}\left(1+\vartheta\frac{3.5y}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}\right). 
(6.7)
This same value of  y  also satisfies the hypotheses in Theorem 5.1 for  c_{t+1}(N) . Indeed, by the mean value theorem,
 \displaystyle\left|y\left(\frac{\mu_{1}(i(t+1)y)-\mu_{1}(iy)}{y^{2}}-\left(N+\frac{(t+1)^{2}-1}{24}\right)\right)\right| 
 \displaystyle=\left|\frac{\mu_{1}(i(t+1)y)-\mu_{1}(ity)}{y}-\frac{2t+1}{24}y\right| 
(6.8)
 \displaystyle=\left|i\mu_{1}^{\prime}(isy)-\frac{sy}{12}\right| 
for some  s\in(t,t+1) , and by (4.16),
 \displaystyle\left|i\mu_{1}^{\prime}(isy)-\frac{sy}{12}\right|=\left|\frac{1}{4\pi}-\frac{sy}{12}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi n}{sy}\right)^{2}\frac{\sigma(n)}{2\pi n}e^{-2\pi n/(sy)}\right|<\frac{1}{4\pi}. 
(6.9)
Therefore, Theorem 5.1 also tells us
 c_{t+1}(N)=\frac{y^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{2\pi\left(M+\frac{2t+1}{24}\right)y}f_{t+1}(iy)}{\sqrt{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}}\left(1+\vartheta\frac{3.5y}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}\right). 
(6.10)
By part 1 of Lemma 4.2, we have
 \frac{3.5y}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}\leq\frac{3.5}{-i(t-1)\mu_{2}^{\prime}(\frac{i}{2})}\leq\frac{45}{t} 
(6.11)
and
 \frac{3.5y}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}\leq\frac{3.5}{-it\mu_{2}^{\prime}(\frac{501i}{1000})}\leq\frac{45}{t}, 
(6.12)
so
 \frac{c_{t+1}(N)}{c_{t}(N)}=e^{2\pi y\frac{2t+1}{24}}\frac{f_{t+1}(iy)}{f_{t}(iy)}\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}}\frac{1+\vartheta\frac{45}{t}}{1+\vartheta\frac{45}{t}}. 
(6.13)
As in (5.28), there is some  s\in(t,t+1)  such that
 \displaystyle\log\left(e^{2\pi\left(\frac{2t+1}{24}\right)y}\frac{f_{t+1}(iy)}{f_{t}(iy)}\right) 
 \displaystyle=-2\pi i\left(\mu_{0}^{\prime}(isy)+\frac{isy}{12}\right) 
(6.14)
 \displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\log(esy)+\frac{\pi}{6}sy-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2\pi n}{sy}+1\right)\frac{\sigma(n)}{n}e^{-2\pi n/(sy)} 
 \displaystyle>0.1. 
We also have
 \frac{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(ity)}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}=1-\frac{\mu_{2}(ity)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}{\mu_{2}(iy)-\mu_{2}(i(t+1)y)}=1+\vartheta\frac{1.1}{t}, 
(6.15)
so altogether (6.13, 6.14, 6.15) imply
 \frac{c_{t+1}(N)}{c_{t}(N)}>e^{0.1}\sqrt{1-\frac{1.1}{1000}}\frac{1-\frac{45}{1000}}{1+\frac{45}{1000}}>1. 
(6.16)
6.3 Stanton’s conjecture for  t\geq 1000 ,  N\geq 100\,000 , and  ty\geq\frac{1}{2} 
With these assumptions, let us choose  y^{\prime}  such that
 \frac{\mu_{1}(ity^{\prime})-\mu_{1}(iy^{\prime})}{y^{\prime 2}}=N-t+\frac{t^{2}-1}{24}. 
(6.17)
Since  \frac{\mu_{1}(ity)-\mu_{1}(iy)}{y^{2}}  is monotonically decreasing in  y  by (5.3), we have  y^{\prime}>y\geq\frac{1}{2t} .
If  t>\frac{N}{2}  (so  c_{t}(N)=p(N)-tp(N-t) ), then Stanton’s conjecture was proved by Craven [Cra06]. Otherwise,  N-t\geq\frac{N}{2}\geq 50\,000 , so
 \frac{1}{y^{\prime}}>\frac{3}{\pi}+\sqrt{24\cdot 50\,000-1+\frac{9}{\pi^{2}}}>1000, 
(6.18)
and Theorem 5.2 applies, which means
 \displaystyle c_{t+1}(N)-c_{t}(N) 
 \displaystyle=c_{t}(N-t)\left(2\pi ty^{\prime}-1+\vartheta ty^{\prime}\left(705y^{\prime}+120ty^{\prime}e^{-2\pi ty^{\prime}}\right)\right), 
(6.19)
 \displaystyle\geq c_{t}(N-t)\left(2\pi ty^{\prime}-1-ty^{\prime}\left(\frac{705}{1000}+60e^{-\pi}\right)\right) 
 \displaystyle>0.
