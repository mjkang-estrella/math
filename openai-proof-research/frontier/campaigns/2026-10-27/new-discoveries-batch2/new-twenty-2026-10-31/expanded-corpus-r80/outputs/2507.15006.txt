Abstract.
In this paper, we introduce a new depicting of the so-called numerical semigroup tree  \mathcal{T} . By exploring computationally this
improved picture, relying on the type notion of a semigroup, we found that the number of semigroups of genus  g  and type  t  is constant when  t  is close to  g  while  g  grows. We also study the unimodality of various sequences as well as the behavior of the leaves in  \mathcal{T} . We put forward several conjectures that are supported by various computational experiments.
Key words and phrases: Numerical Semigroup, Tree
2010 Mathematics Subject Classification05A15, 05E40, 11D07
1. Introduction
A numerical semigroup  \Lambda  is a subset of the non-negative integers  \mathbb{N}_{0} , containing  0 , closed under addition and with finite complement in  \mathbb{N}_{0} .
It is known that each numerical semigroup has a unique minimal system of generators. The cardinality of the minimal system of generators is called the embedding dimension and denoted  e(\Lambda) .
The elements of  \mathbb{N}_{0}  that are not in  \Lambda  are called the gaps. We denote by  Gap(\Lambda)  the set of gaps of  \Lambda . The number of gaps  g(\Lambda)  is called the genus of  \Lambda .
A relevant invariant of  \Lambda  is the so-called Frobenius number  F(\Lambda)  defined as the largest gap in  \Lambda  (we refer the reader to the book [15] for a thorough discussion on the Frobenius number).
The numerical semigroup tree, first introduced in Rosales’ thesis [17] (and discussed in [18, Introduction]), is the infinite tree  \mathcal{T}  whose root is the semigroup  \mathbb{N}_{0}  and in
which the parent of a numerical semigroup  \Lambda  is the numerical semigroup  \Lambda^{\prime}  obtained by adjoining to  \Lambda  its
Frobenius number. In turn, the descendants of a numerical semigroup  \Lambda  are the numerical semigroups obtained by taking away one by one the minimal generators that are larger than  F(\Lambda) . For instance, the descendants of  \Lambda=\langle 3,7,8\rangle  are  \Lambda^{\prime}=\langle 3,7,11\rangle  and  \Lambda^{\prime\prime}=\langle 3,8,10\rangle  (or equivalently, the parent of  \Lambda^{\prime}  and  \Lambda^{\prime\prime}  is  \Lambda ). Indeed, since  7,8>F(\Lambda)=5  are minimal generators of  \Lambda  then  \Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda\setminus\{8\}  and  \Lambda^{\prime\prime}=\Lambda\setminus\{7\}  (or equivalently,
 \Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime}\cup{\{}F(\Lambda^{\prime}){\}}=\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cup{\{}F(\Lambda^{\prime\prime}){\}}  with  F(\Lambda^{\prime})=8  and  F(\Lambda^{\prime\prime})=7 ).
We notice that all numerical semigroups are in  \mathcal{T}  and the parent of a numerical semigroup of genus  g  has genus  g-1 , see Figure 1.
Figure 1. First levels of tree  \mathcal{T}  where yellow, blue and red hexagons denote elements, gaps and generators greater than the Frobenius number of the corresponding semigroup respectively.
The tree  \mathcal{T}  has been the object of many investigations in different contexts: Frobenius pseudo-varieties [16], number of numerical semigroups of given genus
[1, 7, 8], Wilf’s conjecture [4, 12, 13], etc.
In this paper, we introduce a new approach to study  \mathcal{T} . We put forward a new way to represent  \mathcal{T}  by using the notion of type of a numerical semigroup. This representation reveals unexpected behaviors of numerical semigroups according with both their type and genus. We hope (and expect) that this investigation leads not only to a better comprehension of  \mathcal{T}  but also to open new methods to attack well-known open problems.
The type of a numerical semigroup  \Lambda  is defined in [9] as the cardinality of the set of its pseudo-Frobenius numbers. The former also equals the Cohen-Macaulay type of the semigroup ring  K[\Lambda] , where  K  is any field. In general, it is difficult to estimate the type in terms of the minimal generators of the semigroup, see [21].
In the next section, we introduce the type-representation depicting  \mathcal{T}  and present some preliminary results on the parent-descendants types relationship.
Let  n(g,t)  be the number of numerical semigroups of genus  g  and type  t . In Section 3, we investigate a mysterious stabilizer behavior of the value  n(g,t)  when  t  is close to  g  while  g  grows, as indicated by the data in Table 4.
We show that the value  n(g,t)  only depends on  g-t  when  g  is large enough and  t\geqslant(2g-1)/3  (Theorem 3.7). We also study the shape of the generators of these stabilizers semigroups (Theorem 3.5, Propositions 3.8 and 3.9) allowing us to obtain the lower bound  n(g,g-\ell)\geqslant\ell^{2}-3\ell+10  for  \ell\geqslant 6  and  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  (Theorem 3.10).
One tool is to represent the gaps relevant for deciding the type of the semigroup in terms of what we call gap vectors. These are  (0/1) -vectors of length  2(g-t)  with equal number of  0 ’s and  1 ’s. When  t\geqslant(2g-1)/3  we show that the semigroup is essentially determined by its genus and the gap vector (Proposition 3.3).
In Section 4, we focus our attention to the study of the unimodality property of various sequences, for instance, the one obtained by the number of semigroups with fixed genus and increasing type that are leaves in  \mathcal{T} . We put forward a number of conjectures/problems supported by various experimental computations using the numericalsgps package [5] in GAP [11].

2. Type-representation
2.1. Some preliminaries
The type  t(\Lambda)  of  \Lambda  is the cardinality of the set
 PF(\Lambda)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\Lambda\ |\ x+\ell\in\Lambda\text{ for all }\ell\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\}. 
The elements of  PF(\Lambda)  are called pseudo-Frobenius numbers. This concept was introduced in [9]. Notice that  F(\Lambda)=\max\{p\ |\ p\in PF(\Lambda)\} .
The partial order  \leqslant_{\Lambda}  induced by  \Lambda  on the integers is defined as follows:  x\leqslant_{\Lambda}y  if  y-x\in\Lambda . It is well-known [9] that
1)  PF(\Lambda)=\text{Maximals}_{\leqslant_{\Lambda}}(\mathbb{Z}\setminus\Lambda) 
2)  x\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\Lambda  if and only if  f-x\in\Lambda  for some  f\in PF(\Lambda) .
The multiplicity of  \Lambda , denoted by  m(\Lambda) , is the least positive element in  \Lambda .
If  \Lambda  is a numerical semigroup with positive genus, that is,  \Lambda\subsetneq\mathbb{N} , then  \Lambda\cup\{F(\Lambda)\}  is also a numerical semigroup whose embedding dimension is at most one more that of  \Lambda . Also,
 m(\Lambda\cup\{F(\Lambda)\})=\begin{cases}m(\Lambda)-1&\text{ if }t(\Lambda)=g(\Lambda),\\
m(\Lambda)&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases} 
We have, by construction, that  g(\Lambda)>g(\Lambda\cup F(\Lambda))=g(\Lambda)-1 .
2.2. New depicting of  \mathcal{T} 
Let  \Lambda  be a numerical semigroup in which  x  and  y  are two minimal generators with  y,x>F(\Lambda) .
Often, when depicting the tree  \mathcal{T} , if  \Lambda^{\prime}=\Lambda\setminus\{x\},\Lambda^{\prime\prime}=\Lambda\setminus\{y\}  are descendants of  \Lambda 
then  \Lambda^{\prime}  comes before  \Lambda^{\prime\prime}  if  x<y .
We propose a different depicting of  \mathcal{T}  according with the types.
The type-representation of  \mathcal{T}  is given by ordering each level of  \mathcal{T}  by increasing type order; see Figure 2.
Figure 2. First levels of the type-representation of  \mathcal{T} . In each level, semigroups of the same type  t  are grouped together in a box.
2.3. Parent-descendants types
We present a few results relating  t(\Lambda)  and  t(\Lambda^{\prime})  where  \Lambda^{\prime}  is a descendant of  \Lambda . We have the following
Proposition 2.1.
Let  \Lambda^{\prime}  be a numerical semigroup of genus  g>0 . Let  \Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime}\cup\{F(\Lambda^{\prime})\} . Then,
(i) 
 PF(\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus\{F(\Lambda^{\prime})\}\subseteq PF(\Lambda) , with equality if and only if  t(\Lambda^{\prime})=g(\Lambda^{\prime}) .
(ii) 
 t(\Lambda)\geqslant t(\Lambda^{\prime})-1 , with equality if and only if  t(\Lambda^{\prime})=g(\Lambda^{\prime}) .
(iii) 
If  m(\Lambda^{\prime})<F(\Lambda^{\prime})  then  t(\Lambda)\geqslant t(\Lambda^{\prime}) .
We first notice that, by definition,  PF(\Lambda)\subseteq Gaps(\Lambda)  and thus obtaining the obvious bound  t(\Lambda)\leqslant g(\Lambda) . The equality can be characterized as follows.
Proposition 2.2.
Let  \Lambda  be a numerical semigroup. Then, the following statements are equivalent.
(i) 
 t(\Lambda)=g(\Lambda) .
(ii) 
 m(\Lambda)=F(\Lambda)+1 .
(iii) 
 F(\Lambda)<m(\Lambda) .
(iv) 
 \Lambda=\langle c,c+1,\dots,2c-1\rangle  for some integer  c\geqslant 1 .
If these hold then  g(\Lambda)=t(\Lambda)=F(\Lambda)=c-1 .
Proof.
The equality  t(\Lambda)=g(\Lambda)  means that all gaps in  \Lambda  are pseudo-Frobenius numbers, that is, these are not comparable with respect to the  \leqslant_{\Lambda}  partial order. In particular, for any gap  x<m(\Lambda)  we get that  x+m(\Lambda)  is not a gap. Thus  (i)  is equivalent to the fact that  1,\dots,m(\Lambda)-1  are all the gaps in  \Lambda , which implies  (ii) . Clearly,  (ii)\implies(iii)\implies(iv)\implies(i) , and from the latter we get that  c-1=t(\Lambda)=g(\Lambda)=F(\Lambda) .
∎
Proof of Proposition 2.1.
For  (i)  we distinguish two cases. First, assume  t(\Lambda^{\prime})=g(\Lambda^{\prime}) . By Proposition 2.2 we get  \Lambda^{\prime}=\langle c,c+1,\dots,2c-1\rangle  with  c=g(\Lambda^{\prime})+1=F(\Lambda^{\prime})+1 , and  \Lambda=\langle c-1,c,\dots,2c-3\rangle . Hence,  PF(\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus\{F(\Lambda^{\prime})\}=\{1,\dots,c-2\}=PF(\Lambda) .
Now, assume  t(\Lambda^{\prime})<g(\Lambda^{\prime}) , that is,  m(\Lambda^{\prime})<F(\Lambda^{\prime})  by Proposition 2.2. We verify that  F(\Lambda^{\prime})-m(\Lambda^{\prime})\in PF(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda^{\prime}) . Indeed,  F(\Lambda^{\prime})-m(\Lambda^{\prime})<_{\Lambda^{\prime}}F(\Lambda^{\prime})\in PF(\Lambda^{\prime}) , hence  F(\Lambda^{\prime})-m(\Lambda^{\prime})\notin PF(\Lambda^{\prime}) . Also, for any  0\neq h\in\Lambda  we have  F(\Lambda^{\prime})-m(\Lambda^{\prime})+h\geqslant F(\Lambda^{\prime}) , thus  F(\Lambda^{\prime})-m(\Lambda^{\prime})\in PF(\Lambda) .
Let  a\in PF(\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus\{F(\Lambda^{\prime})\} . Therefore,  a\notin\Lambda^{\prime}\cup\{F(\Lambda^{\prime})\}  and  a+(\Lambda^{\prime}\setminus\{0\})\subset\Lambda^{\prime} . As  a+F(\Lambda^{\prime})\geqslant F(\Lambda^{\prime})+1 , we obtain  a+F(\Lambda^{\prime})\in\Lambda . This implies that  a+(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})\subset\Lambda , that is,  a\in PF(\Lambda) .
Consequently,  PF(\Lambda^{\prime})\setminus\{F(\Lambda^{\prime})\}\subsetneq PF(\Lambda) . This finishes the proof of part  (i) .
Part  (ii)  follows at once from  (i) , and also part  (iii)  by using Proposition 2.2.
  \square

Before establishing the latter formally in Theorem 3.7 below, we discuss some connections between the type and genus of a numerical semigroup.
We first show the following inequality.
This is known [14, Proposition 2.2]; also see [6]. Nevertheless, we include a proof for completeness.
Proposition 3.1.
For any numerical semigroup  \Lambda  we have
 F(\Lambda)+t(\Lambda)\leqslant 2g(\Lambda). 
Proof.
Set  g=g(\Lambda)  and  t=t(\Lambda) . We denote  A=\Lambda\cap[1,\dots,F(\Lambda)]  and  B=Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda) . Clearly,  |B|=g-t , and since  PF(\Lambda)\cup A\cup B  is a partition of  [1,\dots,F(\Lambda)]  it follows that  |A|=F(\Lambda)-g .
The map
 \varphi:(PF(\Lambda)\setminus\{F(\Lambda)\})\cup A\to Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus\{F(\Lambda)\} 
given by  \varphi(x)=F(\Lambda)-x  is well defined and injective, hence
 |(PF(\Lambda)\setminus\{F(\Lambda)\})\cup A|\leqslant|Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus\{F(\Lambda)\}|, 
that is  (t-1)+(F(\Lambda)-g)\leqslant g-1 , leading to the desired inequality.
∎
Let  \Lambda  be a numerical semigroup with genus  g(\Lambda)>0  and type  t(\Lambda)=g(\Lambda)-\ell , for  0\leqslant\ell\leqslant g-1 .
The case  \ell=0  is fully understood by Proposition 2.2.
Assume now  \ell>0 .
Since  m(\Lambda)\geqslant t(\Lambda)+1  (cf. [19, Corollary 2.23]) and, by Proposition 3.1, we have
 F(\Lambda)\leqslant 2g(\Lambda)-t(\Lambda)=g(\Lambda)+\ell, 
we obtain
 \{1,\dots,g-\ell\}\subseteq Gaps(S)\subseteq\{1,\dots,g+\ell\}. 
Motivated by these inclusions, we may attach to  \Lambda  the  (0/1) -vector  v(\Lambda)=(v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell}) , where
 v_{i}=\begin{cases}1\text{ if }g-\ell+i\in Gaps(\Lambda),\\
0\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases} 
It is clear that  \ell  of the entries in  v(\Lambda)  are equal  1 , and the rest of  \ell  entries are  0 .
We call  v(\Lambda)  the gap vector of  \Lambda  (a similar binary encoding was used in [3]).
For any  (0/1) -vector  v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell})  and any integer  g>\ell  we denote
 \displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{g,v} 
 \displaystyle= 
 \displaystyle\{1,\dots,g-\ell\}\cup\{g-\ell+i:1\leqslant i\leqslant 2\ell\text{ and }v_{i}=1\}, 
 \displaystyle\Lambda_{g,v} 
 \displaystyle= 
 \displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{g,v}. 
The semigroup  \Lambda  and its gaps
can be recovered from  v(\Lambda)  and  g(\Lambda)  since
 Gaps(\Lambda)=\mathcal{G}_{g(\Lambda),v(\Lambda)}\text{ and }\Lambda=\Lambda_{g(\Lambda),v(\Lambda)}. 
Remark 3.2.
(a) It is not always true that the set  \mathcal{G}_{g,v}  is the set of gaps of a numerical semigroup. Indeed, for  v=(0,1,\dots,0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2\ell}  and  g=3\ell-2  the set
 \mathcal{G}_{3\ell-2,v}=\{1,\dots,g-\ell=2\ell-2\}\cup\{2\ell,2\ell+2,2\ell+4,\dots,g+\ell=4\ell-2\} 
is not the set of gaps of a numerical semigroup since  g-\ell+1=2\ell-1\notin\mathcal{G}_{3\ell-2,v} , yet  2(2\ell-1)=4\ell-2\in\mathcal{G}_{3\ell-2,v} .
b) Even if  \mathcal{G}_{g,v}  were the set of gaps of a numerical semigroup, its type could be different from  \ell . Indeed, for the vector  v=(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)  with  \ell   1 ’s and  \ell   0 ’s, and any  g>\ell>1  one has
 \mathcal{G}_{g,v}=\{1,2,\dots,g\}, 
and its complement  \Lambda_{g,v}=\{0,g+1,g+2,\dots\}  is a numerical semigroup of type equal to  g , by Proposition 2.1.
Proposition 3.3.
Let  \ell\geqslant 1  and  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  be positive integers and let  v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell})  be any vector where half of the entries are  0  and the other half are  1 . Then,
(i) 
 \Lambda_{g,v}  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g .
(ii) 
 Gaps(\Lambda_{g,v})\setminus PF(\Lambda_{g,v})=\{j-i:1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2\ell,v_{i}=0,v_{j}=1\} .
(iii) 
 t(\Lambda_{g,v})=g-|\{j-i:1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2\ell,v_{i}=0,v_{j}=1\}| .
Proof.
We write  \Lambda=\Lambda_{g,v} , for short.
 (i)  We clearly have that  2\min(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})\geqslant 2(g-\ell+1)  and  \max\mathcal{G}_{g,v}\leqslant g+\ell .
The hypothesis  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  gives  2(g-\ell+1)\geqslant g+\ell+1 , and therefore  \Lambda  is a numerical semigroup with  Gaps(\Lambda)=\mathcal{G}_{g,v}  and genus  g(\Lambda)=g .
 (ii)  Let  a\in Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda) . Then there exists  b\in Gaps(\Lambda)  and  0\neq\lambda\in\Lambda  with  a+\lambda=b . Since  g+\ell\geqslant b>\lambda\geqslant m(\Lambda)\geqslant g-\ell+1 , we may write  b=g-\ell+j ,  \lambda=g-\ell+i  with  1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2\ell ,  v_{j}=1  and  v_{i}=0 . Therefore,  a=b-\lambda=j-i .
Conversely, if  a=j-i  with  1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2\ell  such that  v_{j}=1  and  v_{i}=0 , we may write  a=(g+\ell+j)-(g+\ell+i)  with  g+\ell+j\in Gaps(\Lambda)  and  g+\ell+i\in\Lambda .
We clearly have that  (iii)  is a straight consequence of  (ii) .
∎
The previous result motivates the following definition. For any  (0/1) -vector  v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell}) , its cotype is the integer
 cotype(v)=|\{j-i:1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2\ell\text{ with }v_{i}=0\text{ and }v_{j}=1\}|. 
Under the assumptions of Proposition 3.3, we have
(1)
 type(\Lambda_{g,v})=g-cotype(v). 
Example 3.4.
The following vectors have  \ell\geqslant 1  entries equal to  1  and  \ell  entries equal to  0 .
(i) 
 cotype(1,\dots,1,0,\dots,0)=|\emptyset|=0 .
(ii) 
 cotype(0,\dots,0,1,\dots,1)=|\{1,\dots,\ell,\ell+1,\dots,2\ell-1\}|=2\ell-1 .
(iii) 
 cotype(0,1,0,1,\dots,0,1)=|\{1,3,\dots,2\ell-1\}|=\ell .
Theorem 3.5.
Let  g\geqslant t>0  be integers with  t\geqslant(2g-1)/3  (or equivalently  g\leqslant(3t+1)/2 ).
(a) 
If  \Lambda  is any numerical semigroup of genus  g  and type  t , then
 \Gamma=\{0\}\cup\{x+1:0\neq x\in\Lambda\} 
is a numerical semigroup of genus  g+1  and type  t+1 .
(b) 
If  \Gamma  is any numerical semigroup of genus  g+1  and type  t+1 , then
 \Lambda=\{0\}\cup\{x-1:0\neq x\in\Gamma\} 
is a numerical semigroup of genus  g  and type  t .
We give two proofs of Theorem 3.5. The first one is quite straightforward. However, it does not give much information about the shape of the generators of these stabilizer semigroups. The second one allows us to better understand such generators.
First proof of Theorem 3.5.
First, suppose that  \Lambda  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g  and type  t . Let  \gamma_{1}  and  \gamma_{2}  be two positive elements of  \Gamma . Since  \gamma_{1}-1  and  \gamma_{2}-1  are in  \Lambda  and since  m(\Lambda)\geqslant t+1  (cf. [19, Corollary 2.23]), we have that
 \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}-1=(\gamma_{1}-1)+(\gamma_{2}-1)+1\geqslant 2m(\Lambda)+1\geqslant 2t+3. 
Moreover, since  3t+1\geqslant 2g  by hypothesis and since  F(\Lambda)+t\leqslant 2g  by Proposition 3.1, we obtain that
 \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}-1\geqslant 2t+3\geqslant 2g-t+2\geqslant F(\Lambda)+2. 
It follows that  \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}-1\in\Lambda  and thus  \gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma . Therefore  \Gamma  is closed under addition. Since  |\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus\Gamma|=g+1  by definition, we have that  \Gamma  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g+1 .
Conversely, suppose that  \Gamma  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g+1  and type  t+1 . Let  \lambda_{1}  and  \lambda_{2}  be two positive elements of  \Lambda . Since  \lambda_{1}+1  and  \lambda_{2}+1  are in  \Gamma  and since  m(\Gamma)\geqslant t+2  (cf. [19, Corollary 2.23]), we have that
 \lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+1=(\lambda_{1}+1)+(\lambda_{2}+1)-1\geqslant 2m(\Gamma)-1\geqslant 2t+3. 
Moreover, since  3t+1\geqslant 2g  by hypothesis and since  F(\Gamma)+t+1\leqslant 2g+2  by Proposition 3.1, we obtain that
 \lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+1\geqslant 2t+3\geqslant 2g-t+2\geqslant F(\Gamma)+1. 
It follows that  \lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+1\in\Gamma  and thus  \lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda . Therefore  \Lambda  is closed under addition. Since  |\mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus\Lambda|=g  by definition, we have that  \Lambda  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g .
For the types, we can show that
(2)
 Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda)=Gaps(\Gamma)\setminus PF(\Gamma). 
Let  a\in Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda) . Since  a\notin PF(\Lambda) , there exists  b\in\Lambda^{*}  such that  a+b\in Gaps(\Lambda) . Since  a+b+1\in Gaps(\Gamma)  with  b+1\in\Gamma^{*} , it follows that  a\in Gaps(\Gamma)\setminus PF(\Gamma) .
Conversely, let  a\in Gaps(\Gamma)\setminus PF(\Gamma) . Since  a\notin PF(\Gamma) , there exists  b\in\Gamma^{*}  such that  a+b\in Gaps(\Gamma) . If  b=1 , then  \Gamma=\mathbb{N}_{0}  of genus  0 , in contradiction with the hypothesis that  \Gamma  is of genus  g+1 . Since  a+b-1\in Gaps(\Lambda)  with  b-1\in\Lambda^{*} , it follows that  a\in Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda) .
From (2), we obtain that
 g-t(\Lambda)=|Gaps(\Lambda)\setminus PF(\Lambda)|=|Gaps(\Gamma)\setminus PF(\Gamma)|=g+1-t(\Gamma). 
Therefore, we have  t(\Lambda)=t(\Gamma)-1 . This completes the proof.
  \square 
Second proof of Theorem 3.5.
Set  \ell=g-t . The condition  t\geqslant(2g-1)/3  is equivalent to  g\geqslant 3\ell-1 .
(a): If  \ell=0 , then by Proposition 2.2 we have  \Lambda=\{0,g+1,g+2,\dots\} . Hence  \Gamma=\{0,g+2,g+3,\dots\}  has genus and type equal to  g+1 , again by Proposition 2.2.
Assume  \ell>0 . Let  v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell})  the gap vector of  \Gamma . So,  \Lambda=\Lambda_{g,v}  and  Gaps(\Lambda)=\mathcal{G}_{g,v} . By construction, the set  \mathbb{N}_{0}\setminus\Gamma=\{1\}\cup\{x+1:x\in Gaps(\Lambda)\}  has cardinality  g+1  and the former equals  \mathcal{G}_{g+1,v} . Therefore,  \Gamma=\Lambda_{g+1,v} .
As  g+1>g\geqslant 3\ell-1 , by Proposition 3.3 we obtain that  \Gamma  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g+1  and using (1) twice we get
 t(\Gamma)=g+1-cotype(v)=(g-cotype(v))+1=t(\Lambda_{g,v})+1=t(\Lambda)+1=t+1. 
(b): Let  w  be the gap vector of  \Gamma . Then  \Gamma=\Lambda_{g+1,w}  and  \Lambda=\Lambda_{g,w} .
As  \ell=g-t=(g+1)-(t+1) , and  g+1>g>3\ell-1 , from Proposition 3.3 applied twice we have that  \Lambda  is a numerical semigroup of genus  g  and  t(\Lambda)=g-cotype(w)=g-(g+1-t(\Gamma))=t(\Gamma)-1=t .
  \square 
For integers  g,t\geqslant 1 , we denote by  \mathcal{L}(g,t)  the set of numerical semigroups of genus  g  and type  t .
We notice that the second proof of Theorem 3.5 relies on the fact that the numerical semigroups  \Gamma  and  \Lambda  have the same gap vector. We have the following
Corollary 3.6.
Let  \ell  and  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  be integers. Then, the set
 \left\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2\ell}:v=v(\Lambda)\text{ for some }\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(g,g-\ell)\right\} 
does not depend on  g .
The vector  v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2\ell}  is called a stable gap vector if there exists  \Lambda  a numerical semigroup of genus  g  and type  g-\ell  with  v=v(\Lambda)  for some (and hence for all)  g\geqslant 3\ell-1 . We denote  \mathcal{V}(\ell)  the set of stable gap vectors in  \mathbb{Z}^{2\ell} .
Theorem 3.7.
Let  \ell  and  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  be integers. Then,
 n(g,g-\ell)=|\mathcal{V}(\ell)| 
where  \mathcal{V}(\ell)  denotes the set of stable gap vectors in  \mathbb{Z}^{2\ell} .
Proof.
Consider the map  \varphi:\mathcal{L}(g,g-\ell)\to\mathcal{V}(\ell)  where  \varphi(\Lambda)=v(\Lambda) . By Corollary 3.6,
the image of  \varphi  does not depend on  g , thus  \varphi  is surjective.
We note that if  \varphi(\Lambda)=v  then  \Lambda=\Lambda_{g,v} . This proves that  \varphi  is injective.
∎
It would be interesting to determine in terms of  \ell  the stabilized value of  n(g,g-\ell)  and the set  \mathcal{L}(g,g-\ell)  for  g\gg 0 .
According to Theorem 3.7, this process goes hand in hand with finding the set  \mathcal{V(\ell)}  of stable gap vectors for any  \ell .
The following two propositions are on this direction.

Proof.
Set  \ell=g-t . The case (a) when  \ell=0  is covered by Proposition 2.2. For the rest,
by Proposition 3.3, it suffices to find the vectors in  v\in\{0,1\}^{2\ell}  having  \ell  entries equal to  0  and  cotype(v)=\ell , for  \ell=1,2,3 .
When  \ell=1 , the only possible gap vectors are  (0,1)  and  (1,0) . Since  cotype(0,1)=1  and  cotype(1,0)=0  then  (1,0)  is the only stable gap vector.
When  \ell=2 , there are  6  possible gap vectors:

In the next proposition we describe for each integer  \ell>0  several families of stable gap vectors in  \{0,1\}^{2\ell} .
Proposition 3.9.
Let  \ell\geqslant 1  be an integer and let  v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{2\ell})  be a vector with  \ell  entries equal to  0  and  \ell  entries equal to  1 .
(i) 
Assume  v_{i}=0  for a unique  1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell . Then,  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if
(a) 
 i=1 , or
(b) 
 2\leqslant i\leqslant\ell  and  v_{2\ell}=1 .
(ii) 
Assume  v_{1}=\dots=v_{\ell-2}=1  and that  v_{\ell+m}=v_{\ell+n}=1  for  0\leqslant m<n\leqslant\ell . Then,  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if
(a) 
 n=\ell-1  and  \ell/2-1\leqslant m\leqslant\ell-2 , or
(b) 
 n=\ell  and  0\leqslant m\leqslant\ell/2-1. 
(iii) 
Assume  v_{2}=v_{4}=\dots=v_{2\ell}=1 . Then,  v  is a stable gap vector.
(iv) 
Assume  \ell\geqslant 5,v_{2}=\dots=v_{\ell-2}=v_{\ell}=1 , and that  v_{\ell+m}=v_{\ell+n}=1  for some  1\leqslant m<n\leqslant\ell . Then,  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if
(a) 
 1\leqslant m<n\leqslant\ell-2  with  m\neq\ell-3  and  n\neq\ell-3 , or
(b) 
 m=1  and  n=\ell-1 , or
(c) 
 \ell\geqslant 6 ,  m=2  and  n=\ell .
(v) 
Assume  v_{2}=\dots=v_{\ell-1}=1 , and that  v_{\ell+m}=v_{\ell+n}=1  for some  1\leqslant m<n\leqslant\ell . Then,  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if
(a) 
 2\leqslant m<n\leqslant\ell-2 , or
(b) 
 m=1  and  n\neq\ell-1 .
Proof.
For each of the formats analyzed, we have to chararacterize when  cotype(v)=\ell .
We denote  \mathcal{D}=\{j-i:1\leqslant i<j\leqslant 2\ell\text{ with }v_{i}=0\text{ and }v_{j}=1\} , so  cotype(v)=|\mathcal{D}| .
 (i) ] It follows that  v_{k}=1  for all  1\leqslant k\leqslant\ell  with  k\neq i . Since there are  \ell  entries equal to  1  in  v , we have  v_{\ell+m}=1  for a unique  1\leqslant m\leqslant\ell , and  v_{\ell+n}=0  for all  1\leqslant n\leqslant\ell  where  n\neq m . So  \mathcal{D}  contains  \ell+m-i , the numbers  1,\dots,\ell-i  if  i<\ell , and  1,\dots,m-1  if  m\geqslant 2 . Hence,  |\mathcal{D}|\leqslant 1+\max\{\ell-i,m-1\}\leqslant\ell . Therefore, if  v  is a stable gap vector then  \ell-i=\ell-1 , or  m-1=\ell-1 .
If  i=1  then  \mathcal{D}=\{1,\dots,\ell-1,\ell-1+m\} , and  v  is a stable gap vector for all  1\leqslant m\leqslant\ell .
If  i>1  and  m=\ell , then  \mathcal{D}=\{1,\dots,\ell-1,2\ell-i\} , and  v  is a stable gap vector.
 (ii) ] From the hypothesis  v_{\ell-1}=0 . So,  \mathcal{D}=\{1,\dots,m+1\}\cup(\{1,\dots,n+1\}\setminus\{n-m\}) .
If  n-m\leqslant m+1 , that is,  n\leqslant 2m+1 , then  |\mathcal{D}|=n+1 . Hence  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if  n=\ell-1  and  \ell/2-1\leqslant m<n=\ell-1 .
If  n-m>m+1 , that is,  n\geqslant 2m+2 , then  |\mathcal{D}|=n . Hence  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if  n=\ell  and  0\leqslant m\leqslant(n-2)/2 .
 (iii) ] This follows from Example 3.4  (iii) .
 (iv) ] Clearly,  v_{1}=v_{\ell-1}=0 . Considering the differences  j-i  in  \mathcal{D}  where  i<j\leqslant\ell  we obtain that  \{1,\dots,\ell-3,\ell-1\}\subseteq\mathcal{D} . When  j\in\{\ell+m,\ell+n\}  and  i\in\{1,\ell-1\}  we get that also  m+1,n+1,\ell+m-1,\ell+n-1\in\mathcal{D} .
We already notice  \ell  distinct values in  \mathcal{D} . So,  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if
(3)
 \mathcal{D}=\{1,\dots,\ell-3,\ell-1,\ell+m-1,\ell+n-1\}. 
If  n\leqslant\ell-2  then for any difference  j-i  in  \mathcal{D}  with  \ell<i<j\leqslant 2\ell  and  v_{j}=1,v_{i}=0  we have  j-i\leqslant\ell-3 . So, in this case we have that  cotype(v)=\ell  if and only if  m\neq\ell-3  and  n\neq\ell-3 .
Assume  n=\ell-1 . If  cotype(v)=\ell , then  v_{\ell+1}=1  and  m=1 , otherwise  \ell-2=(2\ell-1)-(\ell+1)\in\mathcal{D} , a contradiction with (3).
Conversely, if  m=1  it is immediate to check that (3) holds.
Assume  n=\ell . If  cotype(v)=\ell  then  v_{\ell+2}=1  and  m=2 , arguing as before. Note that if  \ell=5  then  m+1=3=\ell-2 , so (3) does not hold. However, it is straightforward to check that once  \ell>5  and  m=2  then (3) is verified.
 (v) ] Clearly,  v_{1}=v_{\ell}=0 . As in the proof of  (v) , considering the differences  j-i  where  i\leqslant\ell  and  v_{i}=0 ,  v_{j}=1 , we obtain that  \{1,2,\dots,\ell-2,m,n,\ell+m-1,\ell+n-1\}\subseteq\mathcal{D} .
So,  v  is a stable gap vector if and only if
(4)
 \mathcal{D}=\{1,2,\dots,\ell-2,\ell+m-1,\ell+n-1\}. 
Assume  m>1 . Then  \ell<\ell+m-1<\ell+n-1 . So, if (4) holds then  m<n\leqslant\ell-2 . Conversely, it is easy to verify that (4) holds when  n\leqslant\ell-2 .
If  m=1  and (4) holds, then  n\neq\ell-1 . Conversely, it is easy to check that whenever  2\leqslant n\leqslant\ell  and  n\neq\ell-1 , equation 4 holds.
∎
Theorem 3.10.
Let  \ell\geqslant 6  and  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  be integers. Then,  n(g,g-\ell)\geqslant\ell^{2}-3\ell+10 .
Proof.
We count the stable gap vectors presented in Proposition 3.9.
There are  \ell  vectors for  (i)(a) ,  \ell-1  vectors for  (i)(b)  and  1  vector for  (iii) .
For  (ii) , we note that for each  0\leqslant m\leqslant\ell-2  and  m\neq\ell/2  we may find a unique  n  in  \{\ell-1,\ell\}  such that  v  is a stable gap vector. If  \ell  is even, then for  m=\ell/2 ,  n  can be any of  \ell  or  \ell-1  in a stable gap vector. Therefore, for  (ii)  we identified  \ell-1 , or  \ell  vectors depending on  \ell  being odd, or even, respectively.
For  (iv) ,  {\ell-3\choose 2}+2  stable gap vectors are described, while for  (v)   {\ell-3\choose 2}+\ell-2  more vectors are given.
The only overlap between the cases in Proposition 3.9 is the vector which occurs at  (i)(b)  for  i=\ell-1  and at the same time at  (ii)(b)  for  m=0,n=\ell .
Hence, if  \ell\geqslant 6  and  g\geqslant 3\ell-1  then
 \begin{array}[]{ll}n(g,g-\ell)&\geqslant 2\ell-1+(\ell-1)+1+\left({\ell-3\choose 2}+2\right)+\left({\ell-3\choose 2}+\ell-2\right)-1\\
&=\ell^{2}-3\ell+10.\end{array}

4. Unimodality and leaves
A sequence  a_{0},\dots,a_{n}  of real numbers is said to be unimodal if for some  0\leqslant j\leqslant n  we have  a_{0}\leqslant a_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant a_{j}\geqslant a_{j+1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant a_{n} .
Theorem 4.1.
The sequence  n(g,1),n(g,2),\dots,n(g,g)  is unimodal for each  1\leqslant g\leqslant 33 .

Conjecture 4.2.
Let  1\leqslant t\leqslant g  be integers. Then, the sequence  n(g,1),n(g,2),\dots,n(g,g)  is unimodal.
Let  n(g)  be the number of numerical semigroups of genus  g . There is a great deal of literature to understand the asymptotic behavior of the sequence  n(g) . Bras-Amorós [1] conjectured that
for each  g\geqslant 1,\ n(g-2)+n(g-1)\leqslant n(g) .
In fact, the following far weaker version of the conjecture is still open
for each  g\geqslant 1,\ n(g)<n(g+1) .
On this direction, we put forward the following type-version
Conjecture 4.3.
For each  2\leqslant t\leqslant g ,  n(g,t)<n(g+1,t) .
Table 4 supports this conjecture for each  2\leqslant t\leqslant 33 . Notice that the sequence  n(g,1)  (corresponding to symmetric semigroups) is not increasing, for instance,  n(22,1)>n(23,1) .
Let  v_{i}(\Lambda)  be the number of descendants of  \Lambda  of type  i . One may wonder whether the sequence  v_{i}(\Lambda)  is increasing (or unimodal). The latter is not true in general. We thus have that  v_{1}(\Lambda)=0,v_{2}(\Lambda)=1,v_{3}(\Lambda)=0  and  v_{4}(\Lambda)=2 .
