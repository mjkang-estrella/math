## Independent proof, including the added step

Use R=Z[t,(x_a),(y_(u,j))] and its fraction field K; all arc markers are independent. Delete base loops, which cannot occur in any forest; retain distinct parallel arcs as distinct x_a. Define the row Laplacian L on base functions by

  (Lf)(u)=sum_(a:u->v) x_a(f(u)-f(v)).

On V=K^U tensor K^p the product Laplacian M acts by

  (Mf)(u,i)=sum_(a:u->v) x_a(f(u,i)-f(v,i))
              +Y_u f(u,i)-sum_j y_(u,j)f(u,j),
  Y_u=sum_j y_(u,j).

The term j=i cancels, so no vertical loops have been introduced. Let C consist of functions constant in i for each u. C is invariant and M|C=L. More explicitly M=L tensor I + diag(Y_u) tensor I - B, where Bf(u,i)=sum_j y_(u,j)f(u,j). The image of B is contained in C, although its coefficients vary with u. Both remaining summands preserve C, and on

  V/C = K^U tensor (K^p/K*1)

the induced map is (L+diag(Y_u)) tensor I_(p-1). This is an induced quotient map, not a claim that a chosen complement is invariant. A basis of C extended to V makes M block triangular. Its quotient block is a direct sum of p-1 copies of L+diag(Y_u). No diagonalizability, common eigenvectors for different u, irreducibility or connectivity is required. Equation (12), in the source's outward-row/inward-root convention, therefore gives

  P(t,x,y)=det(tI+L) det(tI+L+diag(Y_u))^(p-1).             (*)

Here P is the forest enumerator with weight t per root. Equation (12) has arbitrary indeterminate weights; aggregation of parallel arcs is justified by linearity in each possible outgoing choice: expanding an aggregate weight selects the actual parallel arc. Loops are omitted, not retained in the diagonal of L. This is an ordinary extension to finite labelled multidigraphs; the printed matrix statement itself is for simple digraphs. There is no new infinite-graph claim.

Both sides of (*) lie in R. Equality in K is equality in R by injectivity, so the argument does not require any probability weights to be positive, or specializations to avoid poles. In fact one can choose an integral basis using 1,e_2,...,e_p in each fibre, so denominators are avoidable. For p=1 the quotient has dimension zero, no vertical arcs occur, and the second determinant contributes 1. Empty U gives P=1 and an empty independent family. Isolated vertices, disconnected bases, k_u=0 and an edgeless F_0 cause no exception. Source scope has p positive; p=0 is unnecessary.

Define Q(t,x,Z)=det(tI+L)det(tI+L+diag(Z_u))^(p-1) in Z[t,x,Z]. Its image under Z_u->Y_u is P. Extract x^h and homogeneous degree k_u separately in every y-group. Since substitution preserves EACH group degree, precisely the monomial Z^k survives; there is no mixing of local degrees. With t=1 the restricted enumerator is

  P_(h,k)(y)=c_(h,k) product_u Y_u^k_u,
  c_(h,k)=[x^h Z^k]Q(1,x,Z) in Z.

The coefficient c_(h,k) is nonnegative: it is also the coefficient with y_(u,1)^k_u in the nonnegative forest enumerator. Nonempty fibre gives N=P_(h,k)(1)=c_(h,k)p^(sum k_u)>0, hence c_(h,k)>0. This also shows every count-vector pattern with the specified totals has the multiplicity asserted below. One cannot simply assume positivity from the factored determinant, whose expanded coefficients must first be interpreted. Root count is fixed by projection, so t=1 removes no relevant conditioning; alternatively retain the forced t^r.

For m_(u,j)>=0 with sum_j m_(u,j)=k_u, the coefficient of product y_(u,j)^m_(u,j), divided by N, is

  product_u [ k_u! / (product_j m_(u,j)!) * p^(-k_u) ].

For incorrect totals it is zero. Summing over all other groups uses the multinomial theorem and gives each marginal as the corresponding factor, proving mutual independence for ALL base vertices. For k_u=0 the factor is the point mass at the empty multispin. This does not assert independence of the individual vertical arcs, which lack fixed identities under the conditioning, or of their subforests. The latter stronger statement is explicitly false in the source.

Uniform conditional sampling is valid regardless of arbitrary formal, signed or zero background weights, because those weights do not define this uniform law. A weighted probability version needs nonnegative weights and strictly positive total mass. If the base product weight vanishes, conditioning that weighted measure is undefined even though the uniform fibre remains nonempty. Arbitrary layer-dependent horizontal weights or arbitrary spin-dependent vertical weights are outside the stated product model. Adding destination-dependent nonnegative vertical weights b_(u,j) to the proved identity yields independent multinomial marginals with probabilities b_(u,j)/sum_j b_(u,j) whenever the conditioning has positive mass. This optional extension is not needed to resolve the record.
