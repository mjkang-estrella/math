## 349582: full refutation, excluded as a complete source-lemma adaptation

Literal target: for every permitted even n and odd prime p <= ceil(log_2 n) with 2 primitive modulo p, gamma(KG_n) <= ceil(n/p). Source: Racicot–Rosso 2102.00505v4, Definition 1.1, Theorem 2.4, Proposition 3.5, Corollary 3.6, Conjecture 4.1. Current abstract history identifies v4, 2 May 2022; PDF is journal formatted, accepted 30 March 2022. No independent journal-byte identity is asserted.

First route: transplant the divisibility construction. For n=2pq the paper takes the q vertices 2pl and the q vertices 2pl-1, and uses primitive-root residues to find a neighbor for each remaining vertex. If n=2pq+r with 0<r<2p even, wrapping a chosen representative by n changes its residue modulo p by r. Thus the argument that both possible representatives remain in the same periodic dominating set fails. Translating the whole set by r does not remove that discrepancy: every crossing still changes residue by r. An additional boundary repair would have to fit in ceil(r/p) vertices, not r vertices. No such all-parameter repair was obtained. For p=3 the existing n/4 upper bound is already stronger than the requested n/3 bound, so that subfamily gives no discovery credit.

Second route: use bipartition capacity instead of trying to repair the set. This gives a complete counterexample, n=24, p=5.

1. n is even and >=6. Since 16<24<32, floor(log_2 24)=4 and ceil(log_2 24)=5. The integer 5 is odd prime, and the successive residues of 2 modulo 5 are 2,4,3,1; hence its order is 4=phi(5), so it is primitive. All source hypotheses of Conjecture 4.1 are paid.
2. The graph has vertices 0,...,23 and an edge between distinct x,y exactly when x+y is congruent to one of 1,3,7,15 modulo 24. These four odd residues are distinct. For every vertex x they give four distinct neighbors; none equals x because 2x is even modulo 24. Every edge joins an even to an odd vertex. Thus this is a simple 4-regular bipartite graph, with parts E,O of size 12 each. This is the source's graph, not a generalized fixed-degree variant.
3. Suppose a dominating set D has size s<=5. Let a be its smaller bipartition intersection and b the other, so a<=2 and a+b=s<=5. Consider the part of size 12 opposite the smaller intersection. Its vertices covered by D consist of at most 4a neighbors plus b vertices already in D. Therefore at most 4a+b=3a+s<=6+5=11 of its 12 vertices are dominated, a contradiction. Hence gamma(KG_24)>=6.
4. Since 4<24/5<5, ceil(24/5)=5. Consequently gamma(KG_24)>ceil(24/5), refuting the exact universal literal target, not merely a stronger nearby claim. The source's Theorem 2.3 also supplies gamma<=24/4=6, so gamma=6, though equality is unnecessary for refutation.

Complete earlier-result bridge for exclusion: Corollary 3.6 applies with its k=4, j=2, r=4. Indeed 24=2*2*(4+1)+4 and 4<=r<k+1=5. Its conclusion is gamma(KG_24)>ceil(24/(4+1))=5. Together with the independently checked p=5 hypotheses above this is precisely the counterexample required by Conjecture 4.1 and MathDB 349582. No new combinatorial ingredient is needed. No global priority claim is made. Independent review/root adjudication remains separate; credit zero and no Pi dispatch.

## 350144 / 350145: sustained full-scope routes, no endpoint

Source 2103.10713v1 (19 March 2021; current metadata lists only v1) defines finite simple undirected graphs without isolated vertices, ordinary domatic partitions using closed neighborhoods and total domatic partitions using open neighborhoods. Its Conjectures 6.1 and 6.2 match the two literal bounds. Section 3's actual proof and Section 6's statements were read; output truncation in the initial large extraction is not a whole-paper-read claim.

First route, minimum degree. In any total k-domatic coloring, every open neighborhood contains all k colors, so k<=delta(X). In an ordinary k-domatic coloring its closed neighborhood does, so k<=delta(X)+1. In a Cartesian product each vertex has exactly deg_G(g)+deg_H(h) neighbors; choosing minimum-degree vertices shows delta(G square H)=delta(G)+delta(H).
If both factors are total domatically full, writing M=max(dt(G),dt(H)), this yields dt(G square H)<=delta(G)+delta(H)=dt(G)+dt(H)<=2M. If both factors are domatically full, writing M=max(d(G),d(H)), it yields d(G square H)<=delta(G)+delta(H)+1=d(G)+d(H)-1<=2M-1. Thus those families satisfy the requested bounds. This does not prove either universal target: d<=delta+1 and dt<=delta have the wrong direction to replace delta by a multiple of a factor's domatic number. No equality hypothesis may be silently added.

Second route, color projection. Fix a putative k-coloring c of the product. For a cell (g,h), define A(g,h) as the set of colors seen on its horizontal G-neighbors, and B(g,h) the corresponding set on its vertical H-neighbors. For total domination A(g,h) union B(g,h) must be all k colors; for ordinary domination add c(g,h). If a fixed global partition of colors into two groups could ensure one group were always supplied horizontally or vertically, factor colorings might follow. But the supply sets depend on BOTH g and h. A color can be supplied horizontally at one cell and vertically at another, so a cellwise pigeonhole argument does not yield a single valid coloring of G or H. The unpaid lemma is a global consistency/selection theorem converting every product coupon coloring with k>2max(dt(G),dt(H))+1 (respectively ordinary d) into an impossible factor coloring. No proof was obtained.

Third route, counterexample mechanism. A graph with minimum degree much larger than its total domatic number avoids the first route's tight-factor obstruction. The paper cites such examples, but an existence citation alone gives neither an explicit product coloring nor the ordinary domatic number. Incidence graphs of non-2-colorable uniform hypergraphs are an exploratory class: total 2-coloring would induce a nonmonochromatic point-coloring on every hyperedge, which is impossible if the hypergraph is not 2-colorable. This condition only bounds the factor; it does NOT construct a 4-total-domatic coloring of its square. No particular incidence graph is asserted to be a witness. The unpaid endpoint requires an explicit graph, exact factor bounds, a product coloring, and verification of every open/closed neighborhood, and for the ordinary target independent d-bounds. No finite sampling or computed evidence is claimed. Park both full targets.
