## 339058: full finite refutation, already in the source family

The literal conjecture classifies all diamond-saturated graphs in [floor(n/2)ceil(n/2)-n+7, floor(n/2)ceil(n/2)]. The initial route was a shortest-odd-cycle proof: deleting a shortest odd cycle of length 2t+1 and bounding its external incidences gives the source's upper bound floor((n-1)/2)ceil((n-1)/2)+2 for non-bipartite graphs. This does NOT cover the literal interval's lower portion. Thus invoking Theorem 2(a) alone would be an invalid full proof.

A second route uses the source's Construction A, rather than an unverified search. Take n=30, a=13, b=12, C empty. Vertices: x; u1,u2; v1,v2; A of size 13; B of size 12. Edges: every pair between {u1,u2} union A and B; every pair between A and {v1,v2}; xu1,xu2,xv1,xv2; u1v1,u2v2. No other edges. There are 30 vertices and 15*12+13*2+4+2=212 edges, lying in [202,225]. The only triangles are xu1v1 and xu2v2. They share no edge, so no diamond occurs (every diamond has two triangles with a common edge).

Every missing edge creates a diamond, by the following exhaustive symbolic categories, not executed enumeration. For an edge within {u1,u2} union A use any two distinct vertices of B. Within B use u1,u2. For v1v2 use x,u1. For vi joined to b in B use x,ui. For x joined to a in A use v1,v2. For x joined to b in B use u1,u2. For ui joined to v_(3-i) use x,vi. These exhaust nonedges since all remaining cross pairs were already specified. Each listed four-vertex set contains exactly five edges after the addition.

It is not bipartite, since it has triangles. The original Fuller–Gould description (Emory author manuscript, lines 617–629) makes the other proposed type a complete bipartite graph on n-1 vertices plus one degree-two vertex joined to an adjacent pair, hence exactly one triangle. Our graph has exactly two triangles, so is not that type. If one uses only the MathDB wording, “3-partite” without the source's edge pattern is ambiguous: many graphs are 3-colorable, and “nearly equal” is not a literal numerical predicate. The source-restored classification, not an invented threshold for nearly equal, is the endpoint being refuted.

## 343827: inertia, a complete known direction and two failed converse routes

Source definitions matter: semisimple graphs permit loops; full-rank typical means full rank is the MINIMUM attainable completion rank on a nonempty open set. “Minimally completable” does not mean deleting a specified entry, contrary to the record's prose. The primary definition is at arXiv lines 106–120. The universal-completion inertia assertion uses the source convention. A result exploiting the deletion wording alone would be an endpoint correction.

Let a proper bicoloring of G^c have classes R,S of sizes m,n. Then G contains all loops and all within-class edges. Set those within-class specified matrices to I_m and -I_n, and any specified cross entries to zero. Every completion has form [[I,X],[X^T,-I]]. If it annihilates (u,v), then u=-Xv and X^T u-v=0, giving (I+X^T X)v=0, so v=u=0. More precisely, block congruence gives diag(I_m,-I_n-X^T X); the second block is negative definite, so inertia is exactly (m,n,0). Small perturbations of the specified positive/negative definite blocks preserve this argument. Empty classes cause no difficulty. This proves sufficiency and an open full-rank region. It is precisely the argument preceding source Conjecture 4.6 and Proposition 2.5, also present in the 2021 journal PDF at lines 848–862: zero-credit known direction.

Converse route A: vary a single missing entry by a large parameter. Its symmetric off-diagonal contribution has one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Constancy of inertia across all completions forces at least one positive and one negative eigenvalue when any missing edge exists. For a matching of r missing pairs, send all r entries to large magnitude: its principal 2r by 2r block has r positive and r negative eigenvalues, so interlacing forces min(m,n)>=r. This proves a necessary matching-number bound. It does not enforce the individual component bipartition sizes. For example, the complement star K_(1,3) has matching number one but only bicolor class sizes (1,3) and (3,1), leaving (2,2) unexcluded by this argument. The missing lemma is a component-sensitive inertia restriction, not another finite test.

Converse route B: write each connected bipartite component of G^c with its two sides, and try to force every nonsingular affine completion family to choose opposite signs on those sides. The sufficiency construction assumes definite principal blocks. Arbitrary prescribed within-side blocks need not be definite; congruences diagonalizing them mix coordinates and destroy the missing-entry pattern. Sylvester's law alone cannot restore a vertex coloring. Required lemma: from nonsingularity of M(x) for every real missing-entry vector, derive that its inertia equals the sum of one of the two bipartition-size orientations for each connected complement component (with isolated vertices independently signed). No proof of this lemma is supplied. We have not converted a necessary matching bound into that stronger claim.

The actual conjecture and adjacent proofs were read in v1, v2, and journal PDF; full journal/correction/citing/companion review remains incomplete. Related Bernstein–Blekherman–Sinn 1802.09513 is a specifically material unread companion. No full ordinary proof, no finite counterexample, no precise qualified Pi endpoint. HOLD, zero credit.

## 338443: source-qualified known counterexample family

The tempting shortcut AP symmetric implies instability fails: symmetry gives AP=P^T A, a compatibility relation, not automatically a pair of distinct two-fold automorphisms of A. One cannot infer instability from column permutation alone.

The paper itself resolves the exact question. Choose coprime odd integers l=3,m=5, and t=11 modulo 15; t=-1 mod 3 and t=1 mod 5. Let Gamma=Cay(Z/15,{1,14,11,4}). It is connected since 1 is a generator. It is non-bipartite: the steps of size 1 alone form a 15-cycle. If two neighborhoods agree, S+h=S for a nonzero h. Then S is a union of cosets of the odd-order nontrivial subgroup generated by h, whose order would divide |S|=4, impossible. Thus it is vertex-determining.

For sigma(x)=11x, sigma is nonidentity, sigma squared is identity, and (sigma(x)-x)=10x lies in the subgroup of multiples of 5, disjoint from S. Also sigma preserves S. Consequently sigma(y)-x belongs to S iff y-sigma(x) belongs to S iff sigma(x)-y belongs to S. This is exactly source Lemma 5.1, proving compatibility with the permutation matrix for sigma. Multiplication by 11 and negation act transitively on S, so the graph is arc-transitive. Source Theorem 1.6 says a connected, non-bipartite, vertex-determining arc-transitive circulant is stable. Thus Gamma refutes the iff. This is not a self-contained new proof of the stability theorem: it is explicitly a deduction from the cited theorem.

## 343898: category obstruction, not a new accepted solution

The source's incidence algebra has intervals as a basis and concatenation as multiplication, in v1, v2 and the 2020 author version. At a level with a circle, the nesting poset has a bounded and an unbounded region in containment order, giving a two-element chain. Its algebra has diagonal idempotents e_p,e_q and an interval element a with one of e_p*a and a*e_p equal to a and the other zero. Hence it is noncommutative. A direct sum of copies of k is commutative. Any decomposition as a direct sum of k-algebra interval objects evaluates at that level to a commutative algebra, contradiction. This is a categorical/wording obstruction. If decomposition means underlying VECTOR SPACE zigzag, interval decomposition is the classical type-A representation theorem; it neither establishes multiplicative compatibility nor proves the further recovery of the poset-equivalence class.
