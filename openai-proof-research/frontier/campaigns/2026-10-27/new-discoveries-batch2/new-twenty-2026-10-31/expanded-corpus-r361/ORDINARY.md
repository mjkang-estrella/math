## 355016: categorical genericity of the derived matroid

Exact endpoint: for every finite representable matroid M, every field and every representation R of M, each dependent set of delta M is dependent among the R-circuit vectors. The ground set is the set C of circuits of M, not E(M). Weak order has the source's explicitly stated orientation: D(delta M) is a subset of D(delta_OW(R)). This is the current arXiv Conjecture 8.4, formerly 8.5. The journal retains the same conjecture as Conjecture 43, with the restricted result in Lemma 40. Journal pp. 22–23 and definitions on pp. 2–4 were actually surfaced through public PDF text. The journal is not a resolution.

The source defines A0 = {A subset C : |A| > nullity(union A)}. Its iteration adds supersets and sets (A1 union A2) minus {C0}, provided A1,A2 are already marked dependent but their intersection is not yet marked. Actual arXiv Definitions 4.1–4.3, Remark 4.4, Lemma 8.1 proof, Corollary 5.4 proof and Example 8.5 were read in excerpts. Earlier version weak-order passages were compared; v3 explicitly corrects errors in an earlier Section 6. The fetched earlier HTML bodies have similar content, so their request URLs alone do not certify version fidelity.

First route: prove the universal statement by induction through this iteration. Let Q be the row space of a representation, and let q_C be a nonzero kernel vector with support C. For A in A0 and S=union A, all q_C lie in the space of kernel vectors supported on S. This space has dimension |S|-rank(M restricted to S). Since there are more vectors than that dimension, they are dependent. This proves the initial step. Superset closure preserves dependence.

The elimination step does not follow. From A1 intersection A2 not in the current Ai one cannot conclude that these circuit vectors are independent in R. Thus the represented matroid's dependent-set elimination axiom is not applicable as written. This is a real logical issue, not a missing tactic.

Explicit diagnostic over Q: take a=e1, b=e2, c=e1+e2, d=e3, e=e4 in Q^4. The two sets {a,b,c,d} and {a,b,c,e} are dependent. Their intersection {a,b,c} is dependent too; deleting a from their union leaves {b,c,d,e}, which is independent, since the first two columns have independent first-two-coordinate vectors and d,e occupy the last two coordinates. Hence dependence of the two parents alone cannot justify the desired elimination. This is NOT a counterexample to the MathDB conjecture: these abstract columns were not realized as the circuit-vector configuration of a common underlying matroid with the required Ai history.

Second route: use minimal members of Ai in place of arbitrary dependent parents. If they were circuits of delta_OW(R), ordinary circuit elimination would give a circuit in the child. But Ai-minimality is not minimality among all R-dependent sets; an unmarked proper subset can already be R-dependent. This is the same obstacle in a more precise form. The K4 source example demonstrates why representation-specific extra dependences are expected, so treating them as impossible is particularly unsafe. The characteristic-two Fano specialization does not refute the desired order: it adds dependences on the represented side, which is the allowed direction.

Third route: the Jackson–Tanigawa maximal-matroid construction. The introductory rank bound and Lemma 1.2 were actually read. It offers a unique-maximality theorem conditional on submodularity of a specific rank-bound function and suitability of the prescribed circuit family. Neither the submodularity nor the identification of its maximal matroid with this delta M has been proved here. Moreover, A0-minimal families need not stay circuits under a special representation; mere dependence is weaker. This companion therefore cannot be imported without a new full bridge.

Remaining precise obligation: prove iteration preservation of R-dependence with a property special to circuit-vector configurations (stronger than arbitrary matroid elimination), or exhibit a specific representable M, explicit R and Ai derivation with an R-independent marked child. No such object or theorem is supplied. The restricted case delta M=(C,A0) is already Lemma 8.1, with uniform high-rank cases supplied by source Corollary 5.4. Those complete bridges earn zero credit. Novelty, full ordinary proof and eventual formalization gates remain separately closed.

## 355426: common factors of triangular-grid numerators

Exact recorded endpoint: gcd(num(L_s^(1)), num(L_(s+1)^(2)))>1 for every integer s>=1, with numerator taken after full rational reduction. This depends on the source's graph, row reduction, row/diagonal/edge indices, and initial grid size; arbitrary rational sequences with a matching first value are not enough.

Actual source passages read: Definitions 2.2–2.4, local edge transformation formulas and Lemmas 4.2–4.4, Corollary 5.1 with its proof, Definition 6.2, Theorem 6.5 and induction proof, Lemma 7.1 and proof, Corollary 7.2 and Conjecture 8.1. The older v1 Section 7 and Conjecture 8.1 were compared. The public journal search exposes the same conjecture and local reduction setup; the complete journal body was not read. The 2024 companion arXiv:2109.01959v4 introduction explains its ratio-constructed graphs, rather than proving that all initially unit grids attain those limiting ratios. Its introduction is not a substitute for its later proof bodies.

Use Delta(x,y,z)=xy/(x+y+z) for the adjacent star leg and Y(x,y,z)=(xy+yz+zx)/x for the edge opposite x. Every denominator is positive for positive initial resistances. A boundary edge is the sum of two appropriate Delta legs; an interior edge is Y applied to three such legs. Thus one can in principle propagate exact rational labels using only local formulas.

First route: reduce to the uniform center. For two identical boundary triangles with labels (L_s,B_s,B_s), the boundary formula gives

L_(s+1)=2 L_s B_s/(L_s+2 B_s).

Consequently 1/L_(s+1)-1/L_s=1/(2B_s), and positivity proves L_(s+1)<L_s. This is the source's existing monotonicity argument, not a new proof of its arithmetic conjecture. In particular one equation with the new auxiliary quantity B_s is not a closed recurrence for L_s. The older printed expression involving B_(s+1) is not silently used as a valid recurrence; it must be reconciled with this algebra and indexing.

There is a further scope obstacle: Definition 6.2 locates the uniform center at r>=s+d (and n>=4s). On diagonal 1 that begins at r=s+1. Yet the displayed definition in Section 7 and Conjecture 8.1 uses row r=s, and diagonal 2 has its own center threshold r=s+2. An equality that is true in the uniform center therefore cannot simply be assigned to the literal row-s variables. This could be an index error, but correcting it would earn no credit and would not prove the arithmetic assertion.

Second route: use the full local formulas instead of imposing uniformity. The left-edge formula involves three parent triangles, including the preceding diagonal. For L_(s+1)^(2) this introduces neighboring base/right labels and top-of-center data. The uniform-center theorem proves constancy of labels in its stated interval; it does not supply closed rational formulas for the required boundary-of-interval terms. A finite state recurrence in two diagonals has not been derived with the correct row-s endpoint. Without it, an alleged numerator invariant is unsupported.

Third route: arithmetic propagation, assuming such a recurrence were established. An unreduced numerator factor is not sufficient. If a prime ell divides a candidate unreduced numerator a but also the denominator b, reduction may remove it completely. The necessary statement is v_ell(a)>v_ell(b), for the relevant reduced fraction, together with divisibility in the other reduced numerator. Positivity and monotonicity of real resistances give no such valuation control. Even rational expressions sharing an unreduced factor can reduce to coprime numerators. No invariant ensuring a surviving common prime was found.

These are two substantive missing bridges: exact semantic identification and full recurrence/valuation preservation. No mathematical search or sample generation was run. Before renewed investment, resolve the row and n quantifiers against the journal's actual formulas and figures, then derive the coupled recurrence symbolically by hand. No bounded dormant Pi certificate plan is justified by the current incomplete route.

## 355433: number characterization in bidding games

The intended exact endpoint is the equivalence of hereditary option separation and hereditary strict interpolation, for finite loop-free game forms at each fixed total budget, using the source's discrete Richman bids, marker transfers and terminal auction. Order is universal contextual order: G>=H means every test game X and budget/marker partition has outcome(G+X)>=outcome(H+X). This is much stronger than agreement of standalone outcomes.

Read passages: latest Conjecture 68, game forms and auction rules, definition of order and numbers, Theorems 18–28 with their proof passages, earlier v1 definitions and future-work passages; companion arXiv:2207.08073 Theorem 1 and Outcome Monotonicity proof excerpts. The institutional journal record identifies the December 2024 IJGT article and its DOI; its complete proof body is unread and remains a material gate. Earlier/latest arXiv abstract pages list v1 (2022) and v2 (2023).

The reverse implication is complete and elementary: if every follower lies strictly between each of its left and right options, transitivity yields H^L<H^R at every follower. A proof of one direction is a helper and earns zero credit.

First forward route: structural induction on birthday. Every proper follower inherits option separation, so the induction hypothesis can identify all proper options as numbers, with conjugate inverses by source Theorem 26. What remains is a new interpolation lemma: when all options are numbers and every left option is below every right option in bidding contextual order, H itself is above each left option and below each right option. Invoking the definition of number at this point assumes exactly the conclusion.

Second route: subtract an invertible left option A. By order preservation and cancellation, H>A would follow from H+(-A)>0. The source supplies a sufficient 0-bid comparison method, but one must construct its strategy for H+(-A), for all contexts/partitions, without already assuming H is a number. Moves in -A can create sums involving H with smaller options of A; those have a usable smaller combined birthday, but a move in H can select a different left option incomparable with A. A blanket copycat strategy is therefore not justified by pairwise left-versus-right separation alone. The needed invariant must allow the comparison strategy to select the relevant left option and control every opposing move and budget change. No such invariant was proved.

Third route: distinguish failure of contextual order by a test game. A table of standalone outcomes at fixed budget would not refute H>A, since the order quantifies all X. Conversely a genuine explicit X and partition could refute one required inequality, but the hereditary premise still needs a proof of every required option comparison, not just favorable standalone outcomes. No game-tree enumeration was performed. The source companion's monotonicity in money and marker worth only compare partitions for a fixed game; they do not provide contextual option interpolation.

Quantifier fidelity also requires care for one-sided options: joint quantification over both option sets is vacuous if either is empty, while the customary number definition imposes each applicable one-sided inequality separately. No creditable counterexample is manufactured from that wording ambiguity. TB=0 is ordinary alternating play by the companion's explicit rule argument, hence the classical theorem is already known in that special case. A source-qualified proof for positive total budget remains absent.

## Additional finite endpoint 330886: correction only

For a simple loop-deleted Br(m,1), the graph is K_m: any two distinct one-letter words are adjacent. Map letter i to the ith unit vector in {0,1}^m and append a common extra coordinate 1. Thus this is a domain correction, not an eligible new resolution.
