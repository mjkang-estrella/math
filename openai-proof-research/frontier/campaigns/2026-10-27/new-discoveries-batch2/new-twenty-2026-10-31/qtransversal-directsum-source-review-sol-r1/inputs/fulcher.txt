                                           1    Introduction
                                           Matroid theory has a rich and well-developed history, dating back to Whitney’s foundational work in 1935
                                           [16]. The theory provides an axiomatic framework that captures a wide range of combinatorial structures
                                           [14]. One central example is the association of matroids to error-correcting codes. Matroids arising from
                                           linear codes are called representable matroids and have been the subject of extensive study. The theory of
                                           representable matroids has seen significant development, as well as applications in coding theory [5, 15].
                                               A prevailing theme in combinatorics is that of q-analogues, where combinatorics over a set is extended to
                                           that over a vector space. The theory of q-matroids first appeared in Crapo’s PhD thesis, but was formalised
                                           only much later in [11], where several concepts from matroid theory were extended to the vector space setting.
                                           Since then, many further notions from matroid theory have been shown to extend, often in non-trivial ways,
                                           to the q-analogue [6]. However, other concepts remain elusive or have been shown not to extend at all.
                                               One particularly challenging notion is that of representability. Compared to the classical setting, the
                                           theory of representable q-matroids poses additional difficulties and remains comparatively undeveloped. At
                                           the same time, there is strong motivation for a deeper understanding of representable q-matroids, since this
                                           class corresponds precisely to those q-matroids arising from vector rank-metric codes.
                                               A foundational result in matroid theory is that the class of representable matroids is closed under the
                                           binary operations of direct sum and free product [8, 12]. Analogous operations have been developed for
                                           q-matroids [2, 7]. However, the behaviour of representability under these operations is markedly different in
                                           the q-analogue. In particular, it has been shown that the direct sum of representable q-matroids need not
                                           be representable [10]. Moreover, it remains open whether the free product of representable q-matroids is
                                           representable.
                                               Understanding when representability of q-matroids is preserved under binary operations is therefore an
                                           important problem. Such a characterisation would provide structural tools for constructing new vector rank-
                                           metric codes. Some progress in this direction has recently been made in [3], which also highlights connections
                                           between q-matroid theory and finite geometry.
                                               One of the rare instances in which a concept from matroid theory extends to q-matroid theory in a
                                           particularly direct way is that of cyclic flats. This has been explored in [1, 2, 9], where the theory of cyclic
                                           flats for q-matroids was developed and their behaviour under binary operations was studied.



                                                                                                      1
    In this paper we formalise a correspondence between matroids and a subclass of q-matroids that we call
coordinate q-matroids, via their weighted lattices of cyclic flats. Through this correspondence we prove a
cyclic flat embedding theorem showing that transversal matroids embed into transversal q-matroids. This
embedding is the central contribution of the paper and provides a mechanism for transferring structural
results from transversal matroid theory to the q-matroid setting. As an immediate consequence, we obtain
the representability of a large class of q-matroids not previously known to be representable, including the
fundamental class of nested q-matroids.
    This cyclic flat embedding theorem suggests a canonical method of embedding matroids into q-matroids
in such a way that certain structural properties are preserved. In particular, it raises the question of whether
cyclic flat embedding provides a canonical means of embedding representable matroids into representable
q-matroids. This is only one of several natural questions that arise from this perspective.
    We also investigate the behaviour of transversal q-matroids under binary operations. We provide a
constructive proof that the class of transversal q-matroids is closed under the free product. Moreover, using
the cyclic flat embedding theorem, we obtain the analogous result for the class of coordinate transversal
q-matroids. In doing so, we propose a presentation for the direct sum of transversal q-matroids motivated
by the corresponding presentation in the matroid setting.
    More broadly, the cyclic flat embedding perspective suggests a guiding principle for extending construc-
tions from matroid theory to the q-analogue. If a matroid operation or property admits a meaningful
q-analogue, then one may expect that the cyclic flat embedding of a matroid possessing this property should
yield a q-matroid exhibiting the corresponding q-analogue. In this sense, cyclic flat embedding provides a
natural testing ground for proposed extensions of matroid-theoretic constructions to the q-matroid setting.
    The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 establishes notation and definitions, and recalls
several results that will be required later in the paper. Section 3 contains the main result of this paper. In
this section, we formalise a correspondence between matroids and coordinate q-matroids and show that the
transversality (and thus representability) of a coordinate q-matroid can be decided by the transversality of its
corresponding matroid. As a corollary, we show that all nested q-matroids are transversal and representable.
Section 4 establishes the closure of the class of transversal q-matroids under free products. We also show
that closure of the class of coordinate transversal q-matroids under direct sums follows from the cyclic flat
embedding theorem of the previous section. This yields a further class of representable q-matroids.


2    Preliminaries
We assume that the reader has some familiarity with matroid theory [14] and q-matroid theory [11], for
which we have provided references. q-matroids can be defined in several cryptomorphic ways. In this paper,
we focus on definitions via the rank function, independent spaces, and cyclic flats. Our primary viewpoint
will be through the rank function.
Notation 2.1. We denote by M = (E, ρ) a q-matroid on the ambient space E with rank function ρ. We
denote the set of independent spaces (those X ≤ E such that ρ(X) = dim(X)) of M by I(M ).

   The theory of cyclic flats for q-matroids was formalised in [1]. We recall the definition here and establish
notation.
Definition 2.2. For a q-matroid M = (E, ρ), we say that X ≤ E is a flat if ρ(X) < ρ(X ⊕ ⟨v⟩) for all
v ∈ E \ X. We say that X is cyclic if ρ(H) = ρ(X) for all H ≤ X such that dim(H) = dim(X) − 1.
Notation 2.3. For a q-matroid M , we denote its set of flats by F(M ) and its set of cyclic spaces by C(M ).
The set of cyclic flats is F(M )∩C(M ), which we denote by Z(M ). We may use the same established notation
I, F , C, and Z analogously for matroids.
    It was established in [1, 11] that a q-matroid is uniquely determined not only by its rank function, but
alternatively by its independent spaces, flats, cyclic spaces, or cyclic flats together with the rank function

restricted to the cyclic flats. Therefore, a q-matroid may equivalently be defined using any one of these
notions.
    The following standard definitions from q-matroid theory will be convenient for our purposes.
Definition 2.4. For a q-matroid M = (E, ρ) and X ≤ E, an inclusion-maximal element of I(M ) that is
contained in X is called a basis of X in M . An inclusion-minimal element of C(M ) is called a circuit of
M.
    It is well established that for any X ≤ E, all bases of X in M are equidimensional. It was shown in [11]
that any q-matroid is uniquely determined by its set of independent spaces. It was shown in [1] that any
q-matroid is uniquely determined by its set of cyclic flats together with the respective ranks of those cyclic
flats.

    We recall the following result from [9], which draws an important parallel with matroid theory.
Theorem 2.9. [9, Theorem 6.2] For q-matroids M1 and M2 ,

                         Z(M1 ⊕ M2 ) = {Z1 ⊕ Z2 : Z1 ∈ Z(M1 ) and Z2 ∈ Z(M2 )}.

    The important parallel that the above theorem draws with matroid theory is that if N1 and N2 are
matroids, then Z(N1 ⊕ N2 ) = {Z1 ∪ Z2 : Z1 ∈ Z(N1 ) and Z2 ∈ Z(N2 )}. This analogy with respect to the
direct sum does not occur for other q-matroidal notions such as independent spaces, flats, and so on.
    In the recent paper [13], the theory of q-transversals was developed. Moreover, a class of q-matroids
arising from this theory was introduced. These objects are central to this paper, and we recall the relevant
definitions here.
Definition 2.10. Let S be a finite set and let A be a collection of subsets of S. We say that X ⊆ S has an
avoidance transversal of A if there is an ordering X = {x1 , . . . , xℓ } and an ordering A = (A1 , . . . , Ak )
such that xi ∈
             / Ai for each i ∈ [ℓ].
    From the above definition, it is clear that a necessary condition for X to have an avoidance transversal
of A is that ℓ ≤ k. It is a classical result that the set of subsets of S that have avoidance transversals of A
coincides with the set of independent sets of a matroid on the ambient set S. We recall the corresponding
definition, which is analogous to the classical definition of a transversal matroid.
Definition 2.11. Let A = (A1 , . . . , Ak ) be a collection of subsets of a finite set S. An avoidance transver-
sal matroid M = (S, r) with presentation A is the matroid whose independent sets are precisely those
subsets X ⊆ S that have an avoidance transversal of A.
   By taking complements, it is clear that the class of transversal matroids coincides with the class of
avoidance transversal matroids. For the q-analogue, however, it is necessary to use the notion of avoidance
transversals.
Definition 2.12. [13] Let X = (X1 , . . . , Xk ) be a collection of subspaces of a vector space E. We say that
X ≤ E is a partial q-transversal of X if all linear bases of X has an avoidance transversal of X .
   From the above definition, it was shown in [13] that the set of partial q-transversals of X coincides with
the set of independent spaces of a q-matroid. We therefore recall the following definition.
Definition 2.13. [13] Let X = (X1 , . . . , Xk ) be a collection of subspaces of a finite vector space E. A
transversal q-matroid M = (E, ρ) with presentation X is the q-matroid whose independent spaces are
precisely the subspaces X ≤ E that are partial q-transversals of X .
    In this paper, we leverage the following representability result from [13] to show the representability of a
class of q-matroids not previously known to be representable.
Theorem 2.14. [13, Theorem 24] Let X be a collection of subspaces of E. If there exists a fixed linear
basis β of E such that each member of X is the span of a subset of β, then the transversal q-matroid with
presentation X is representable.


3     The cyclic flat embedding theorem
This section contains the main result of this paper. Our goal is to formalise a correspondence between
matroids and a class of q-matroids whose cyclic flats are coordinate. Through this correspondence, structural
questions about such q-matroids can be reduced to the corresponding questions for matroids.
    In particular, we show that if the cyclic flats of a q-matroid are coordinate, then the questions of whether
it is transversal and representable can be decided by examining its corresponding matroid. The resulting
cyclic flat embedding theorem provides a mechanism for transferring structural results from transversal
matroid theory to the q-matroid setting.

                                         ρ(H ∩ Z) < ρ(Z) = ρ(H ∩ Z),
since Z is cyclic. Hence λ = 0, which implies that i ∈
                                                     / σ(Z), contradicting the original assumption.
    We therefore conclude that if ei ∈
                                     / Z, then i ∈
                                                 / σ(Z). The result follows.
   Combining the above lemmas, we have shown that ϕ(Z(M )) = Z(ϕ(M )). We now show that ρ(ϕ(Z)) =
r(Z) for each Z ∈ Z(M ). We will then be ready to prove the main theorem of this paper.
Lemma 3.14. For all Z ∈ Z(M ), we have ρ(ϕ(Z)) = r(Z).
Proof. Let Z ∈ Z(M ) and let B be a basis of Z in M . By Lemma 3.7, ϕ(B) ∈ I(ϕ(M )). We claim that
ϕ(B) is a basis of ϕ(Z) in ϕ(M ).
   Suppose that ϕ(B) ⊕ ⟨v⟩ ∈ I(ϕ(M )) for some v ∈ ϕ(Z) \ ϕ(B). Let β be a linear basis of ϕ(B) ⊕ ⟨v⟩
containing v. Since v ∈
                      / ϕ(B), there exists ℓ ∈ σ(v) \ B. As v ∈ ϕ(Z), we have ℓ ∈ Z.
   Since ϕ(B) ⊕ ⟨v⟩ is independent, the basis β has an avoidance transversal in X . By Lemma 3.6, this
implies that the set {ei : i ∈ B} ∪ {eℓ } has an avoidance transversal in X . It follows that B ∪ {ℓ} has an
avoidance transversal in S, and hence B ∪ {ℓ} ∈ I(M ). This contradicts the fact that B is a basis of Z.
   Therefore ϕ(B) is a basis of ϕ(Z) in ϕ(M ), and we conclude that ρ(ϕ(Z)) = dim(ϕ(B)) = |B| = r(Z).
    The following theorem is the main result of this paper. For its proof, we no longer assume the previously
fixed notation M = ([n], r) and ϕ(M ) = (Fnq , ρ).
Theorem 3.15 (The cyclic flat embedding theorem for transversal matroids). If the corresponding matroid
of a coordinate q-matroid is transversal, then the q-matroid is transversal and representable.
Proof. Let Mq be a coordinate q-matroid whose corresponding matroid is M = ([n], r). Suppose that M is
avoidance transversal with presentation S = (S1 , . . . , Sk ). Let ϕ(M ) = (Fnq , ρ) be the transversal q-matroid
with presentation X = (X1 , . . . , Xk ), where Xi = ϕ(Si ) for each i ∈ [k].
   By Corollary 3.12, Lemma 3.13, and Lemma 3.14, we obtain that Z(ϕ(M )) = ϕ(Z(M )) and that r(Z) =
ρ(ϕ(Z)) for all Z ∈ Z(M ).
   By the definition of a corresponding matroid, we have Z(Mq ) = ϕ(Z(M )), and for each Z ∈ Z(Mq ) its
rank in Mq is r(ϕ−1 (Z)). Observe that
                                      r(ϕ−1 (Z)) = ρ(ϕ(ϕ−1 (Z))) = ρ(Z).
   It follows that Z(Mq ) = Z(ϕ(M )) and that the ranks agree on these cyclic flats. By [1, Theorem 4.11],
the q-matroids Mq and ϕ(M ) are equal. By [13, Theorem 24], ϕ(M ) is a representable transversal q-matroid.
The result follows.
   Theorem 3.15 allows us to apply tools from transversal matroid theory to representable q-matroid theory
from the perspective of cyclic flats. We highlight one instance of this in the following corollary, in which we
identify a class of representable q-matroids not previously known to be representable.
Corollary 3.16. All nested q-matroids are transversal and representable.
Proof. Let Mq = (Fnq , ρ) be a nested q-matroid. This means that its lattice of cyclic flats is a chain. Therefore
there exists a linear basis β of Fnq such that each cyclic flat is the span of a subset of β. Hence, up to a
change of basis (and thus q-matroid equivalence), we may assume that β is the set of standard basis vectors
of Fnq . It follows that Mq is coordinate and that its corresponding matroid is a nested matroid, which is
known to be transversal. By Theorem 3.15, it follows that Mq is transversal and representable.
   A benefit of working with cyclic flats is that they form a structural feature shared by both matroids and
q-matroids. This naturally raises the question of whether similar embedding results hold for other classes
of matroids. That is, if a matroid M has a property P , does it follow that its corresponding q-matroid also
has property P ? The following conjecture provides a plausible example.
Conjecture 3.17. If the corresponding matroid of a coordinate q-matroid is representable, then the q-
matroid is representable.

4     Closure of q-transversal matroids under binary operations
In this section we investigate the behaviour of transversal q-matroids under two fundamental binary oper-
ations: the direct sum and the free product. Our aim is to show that the class of transversal q-matroids is
closed under these operations.
    As shown in [2] and [9], the structures of the cyclic flats of the free product and direct sum of q-matroids
behave in a manner analogous to the matroid setting. In particular, for two matroids M1 and M2 we have

             Z(ϕ(M1 ⊕ M2 )) = Z(ϕ(M1 ) ⊕ ϕ(M2 ))             and     Z(ϕ(M1 □ M2 )) = Z(ϕ(M1 ) □ ϕ(M2 )).

    Consequently, if every transversal q-matroid were coordinate up to equivalence, then by Theorem 3.15 the
closure of transversal q-matroids under these binary operations would follow directly from the corresponding
closure of transversal matroids. We therefore begin by verifying that this is not the case.
    The following example shows that there exist transversal q-matroids that are not coordinate up to equiv-
alence. It is the smallest such example that we were able to find.
Example 4.1. Let M = (F6q , ρ) be the transversal q-matroid with presentation

                    X = (X1 , X2 , X3 ) = (⟨e1 , e2 , e3 ⟩, ⟨e4 , e5 , e6 ⟩, ⟨e1 + e4 , e2 + e5 , e3 + e6 ⟩).

   We show that X1 is cyclic in M . Since dim(X1 ) = 3 and X1 intersects both X2 and X3 trivially, each
hyperplane of X1 is a partial q-transversal. Moreover, X1 itself is not a transversal of X . Therefore X1 is
cyclic.
   By the same argument, X2 and X3 are also cyclic. By [13, Theorem 21], it follows that {X1 , X2 , X3 } ⊆
Z(M ), and hence M is not equivalent to a coordinate q-matroid.
    We begin with the following fundamental lemma.

Lemma 4.2. Let M = (E, ρ) be a q-matroid. Any linear basis of E contains a linear basis of a basis of M .
Proof. Let β = {b1 , . . . , bn } be a linear basis of E, and suppose that B = ⟨e1 , . . . , ek ⟩ is a maximally
independent space in M spanned by elements of β. Then for each i ∈ [n] \ [k] we have

                                                    ρ(B) = ρ(B ⊕ ⟨bi ⟩).

By submodularity it follows that ρ(B) = ρ(E). Hence B is a basis of M , and {b1 , . . . , bk } is a linear basis of
B contained in β.
    For the remainder of this section we fix the following notation.
Notation 4.3. Let M1 = (E1 , ρ1 ) and M2 = (E2 , ρ2 ) be transversal q-matroids with respective presentations

                                   X1 = (A1 , . . . , Ak )   and     X2 = (B1 , . . . , Bℓ ).

    Define
                              X1 □ X2 = (A1 ⊕ 0, . . . , Ak ⊕ 0, E1 ⊕ B1 , . . . , E1 ⊕ Bℓ )
and
                           X1 ⊕ X2 = (A1 ⊕ E2 , . . . , Ak ⊕ E2 , E1 ⊕ B1 , . . . , E1 ⊕ Bℓ ).
    We now establish that the class of transversal q-matroids is closed under free products.

Theorem 4.4. The q-matroid M1 □ M2 is a transversal q-matroid with presentation X1 □ X2 .

Corollary 4.5. Let M1 and M2 be coordinate transversal q-matroids. Then M1 □ M2 is a coordinate
transversal q-matroid.
   We now turn to the case of the direct sum. This situation is more delicate than the free product, so
we restrict ourselves here to the direct sum of coordinate transversal q-matroids. Nevertheless, the result
suggests a natural presentation for the direct sum in the general case.
Lemma 4.6. Let N1 = (S, r1 ) and N2 = (T, r2 ) be avoidance transversal matroids with respective presen-
tations (S1 , . . . , Sk ) and (T1 , . . . , Tℓ ), where S ∩ T = ∅. Then N1 ⊕ N2 is an avoidance transversal matroid
with presentation
                                          S = (S1 ∪ T, . . . , Sk ∪ T, S ∪ T1 , . . . , S ∪ Tℓ ).




                                                         10
Proof. Let N denote the avoidance transversal matroid with presentation S. We show that N = N1 ⊕ N2 .
    Observe that no element of S avoids any member of (S ∪ T1 , . . . , S ∪ Tℓ ), and no element of T avoids any
member of (S1 ∪ T, . . . , Sk ∪ T ). Hence a set I ⊆ S ∪ T is an avoidance transversal of S if and only if I ∩ S
is an avoidance transversal of (S1 , . . . , Sk ) and I ∩ T is an avoidance transversal of (T1 , . . . , Tℓ ).
    Therefore I is independent in N if and only if I ∩ S is independent in N1 and I ∩ T is independent in
N2 . This is exactly the definition of independence in N1 ⊕ N2 , and hence N = N1 ⊕ N2 .
Theorem 4.7. If M1 and M2 are coordinate transversal q-matroids, then M1 ⊕M2 is a coordinate transversal
q-matroid with presentation X1 ⊕ X2 .
Proof. Since M1 and M2 are coordinate, we may fix linear bases β1 = {a1 , . . . , am } and β2 = {b1 , . . . , bn } of
E1 and E2 such that every member of Z(M1 ) and Z(M2 ) is the span of a subset of β1 and β2 . Let ϕ−1 (M1 )
and ϕ−1 (M2 ) denote the corresponding matroids.
   By [13, Theorems 21 and 23], we have Ai ∈ Z(M1 ) and Bj ∈ Z(M2 ) for each i ∈ [k] and j ∈ [ℓ], so every
member of the presentations X1 and X2 is coordinate. Consequently, by Theorem 3.15, the families

                           S1 = (σ(A1 ), . . . , σ(Ak ))   and    S2 = (σ(B1 ), . . . , σ(Bℓ ))

are presentations of ϕ−1 (M1 ) and ϕ−1 (M2 ).
   We regard ϕ−1 (M1 ) and ϕ−1 (M2 ) as matroids on disjoint ground sets S and T . By Lemma 4.6, the direct
sum ϕ−1 (M1 ) ⊕ ϕ−1 (M2 ) is a transversal matroid with presentation

                             (σ(A1 ) ∪ T, . . . , σ(Ak ) ∪ T, S ∪ σ(B1 ), . . . , S ∪ σ(Bℓ )).

   By [9, Theorem 6.2], the q-matroid associated to ϕ−1 (M1 ) ⊕ ϕ−1 (M2 ) is precisely M1 ⊕ M2 . Applying
Theorem 3.15, it follows that M1 ⊕ M2 has a presentation obtained by applying ϕ to the members of the
above family. This yields
                              (A1 ⊕ E2 , . . . , Ak ⊕ E2 , E1 ⊕ B1 , . . . , E1 ⊕ Bℓ ),
which is exactly X1 ⊕ X2 .
    Theorem 4.7 suggests that if the class of transversal q-matroids is closed under direct sums, then the
presentation of any such direct sum can be formed analogously to the matroid case. We therefore record the
following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.8. Let M1 and M2 be transversal q-matroids with presentations X1 and X2 . Then M1 ⊕ M2
is a transversal q-matroid with presentation X1 ⊕ X2 .
   The following consequence illustrates how the preceding results recover and extend known representability
results for uniform q-matroids. Its proof follows immediately from Corollary 4.5 and Theorem 4.7.

Corollary 4.9. Let M1 , . . . , Mt be uniform q-matroids. Then any q-matroid obtained from M1 , . . . , Mt
by iteratively applying direct sums and free products is a coordinate transversal q-matroid and therefore
representable.
    In particular, this recovers the representability of arbitrary direct sums of uniform q-matroids established
in [3], which may suggest a useful connection between q-transversals and finite geometry.
