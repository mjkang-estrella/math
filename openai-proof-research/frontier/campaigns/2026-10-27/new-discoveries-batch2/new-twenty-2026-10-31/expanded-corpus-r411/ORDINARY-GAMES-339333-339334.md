Let m=maxS and F(x,y) be current-player optimal score difference from zero score. Terminal F=0; otherwise
 F(x,y)=max({s-F(x-s,y):s in S,s<=x} union {s-F(x,y-s):s in S,s<=y}).
This is a mathematical definition and personal induction, not an executed mathematical program.

Paid helper: bounded integer outcomes for any finite number of piles.
Induct on total pebbles. If terminal use0. Otherwise let M be the greatest currently legal action among all piles. In every child, all legal actions were legal before, so its largest legal action is at most M; by induction every child value lies between0 and M. Each candidate s-F(child)<=s<=M. Choose an actual legal action s=M: its candidate M-F(child)>=0. Thus0<=F<=M<=m. Integer-valuedness follows from the finite max and induction. This closes the bounded-state assumption without using the source's single-pile result on a sum.

Route A, 339333: fixed strip finite-state proof plus sharp-period gap.
Fix Y>=0 and compute the mathematical columns C_x=(F(x,0),...,F(x,Y)). For x>=m, each entry depends on the previous m columns through x-s and on already determined entries in the same column through y-s. Increasing y therefore determines C_x uniquely from the previous m columns. Each entry is an integer in{0,...,m}; the length-m window has at most Q=(m+1)^(m(Y+1)) states. A deterministic map of a finite set eventually repeats a state; from the first repeat every subsequent state repeats with the same positive period p<=Q. This proves eventual horizontal periodicity at every fixed y, and by symmetry vertical periodicity. It is the known unrestricted-period component, not the requested period<=2m; zero credit.
Failed shortcut: finiteness of Q does not bound the cycle length by2m. A driven finite-state recurrence can have a subharmonic period; the source's one-pile periodicity cannot simply be inserted as an assertion for a nonzero second pile.
Continuation: one sufficient strong lemma would be: for each Y there is N(Y,S) such that for all x>=N and0<=y<=Y, removing m from the first heap is optimal, i.e. F(x,y)=m-F(x-m,y). Applying it twice yields F(x+2m,y)=F(x,y). This lemma is UNPAID and may be stronger than necessary.
Attempted extension of Theorem3: against greedy m-removal, each smaller action loses at least one point before the large-heap endgame. At entry, however, the other heap may contain Y pebbles and the opponent may choose it on intervening turns. The one-pile proof's endgame budget m-1 and claim that every relevant response removes m are no longer valid. Replacing the budget by m+Y could bound sacrifices but still does not justify reordering a strategy on the large heap across turns on the other heap: strategy-dependent continuation and tie-breaking remain. Exact unpaid lemma is a value-preserving interchange/coupling across the disjunctive sum, or a different proof that all eventual cycles of this specific strip recurrence have period<=2m. No such proof is asserted. No numerical search was used to assume it.

Route B, 339334: paired diagonal and boundary propagation.
Paid helper: F(x,x)=0 for all x. The second player can copy each first-player move in the other, equal pile. It remains legal because that other pile had the same size before the first move. Each paired round returns to equal piles and cancels score contributions; finite descent ends after a complete pair with score0. Thus optimal first-player value<=0, and the preceding nonnegativity proof gives equality. This handles offset0 only, a helper/instance with zero credit.
For k>=0, try the same mirror strategy from (x,x+k). Paired legal rounds preserve offset k. The first failed mirror is a move in the larger pile with size s exceeding the smaller pile size u; then u<m and larger pile u+k<m+k, hence the residual total is less than2m+k. This is a genuine bounded endgame observation for this specific mirror policy. It gives neither optimality of that policy nor the arrival residue/state under all opponent choices.
Failed shortcut: fixed offset does not give a closed fixed-width recurrence. A move changes offset to k+s or k-s; iterating recurrence accesses arbitrarily distant offsets. There is no finite-state diagonal window merely from0<=F<=m.
Continuation: a full approach would show that for each k all optimal continuations can be replaced by a value-preserving policy whose offset stays inside a fixed band, with eventually periodic boundary data; then finite-state propagation might close. Exact unpaid claims are confinement/value-preserving replacement and periodic boundary compatibility. Alternatively a proven greedy convergence theorem in every offset direction could suffice. Neither follows from the bounded mirror endgame, and no claim of diagonal periodicity for nonzero k is made.
