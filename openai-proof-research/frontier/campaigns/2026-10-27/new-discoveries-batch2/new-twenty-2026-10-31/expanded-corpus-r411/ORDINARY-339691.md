Each row and column has distinct colors, so all edges are properly colored. Neighborhood-color sets, derived directly from the row/column adjacency, are:
(1,1) a:{b,c,d}; (3,3) a:{c,d,e};
(1,2) b:{a,c,e}; (2,1) b:{a,c,d};
(1,3) c:{a,b,d}; (2,2) c:{b,d,e};
(2,3) d:{a,b,c}; (3,1) d:{a,b,e};
(3,2) e:{a,b,c,d}.
The two sets in each repeated color differ, and e occurs once. This checks every same-color pair, with no computation or enumeration program. Hence chiNL(K3 square K3)<=5.
In K3[K3], any distinct vertices either have different first coordinates, hence adjacent, or have equal first coordinates and different second coordinates, hence adjacent. Thus K3[K3] is K9. Every proper coloring of K9 is injective and needs9 colors; assigning9 singleton colors is neighbor-locating vacuously. So chiNL(K3[K3])=9>5>=chiNL(K3 square K3). This single admissible pair negates the full universal claim. No exact value of the Cartesian invariant is necessary.
