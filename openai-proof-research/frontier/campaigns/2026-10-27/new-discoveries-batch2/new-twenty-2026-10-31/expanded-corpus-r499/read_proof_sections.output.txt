                                   5. Concluding remarks
  In this paper we studied the threshold n0 (r) in the Erdős–Trotter problem (Problem 1.1).
For r = 2 and r = 3 one has n0 (2) = 3 and n0 (3) = 8. For all integers r ≥ 4, Theorem 1.4 and
Theorem 1.5 yield the two-sided estimate
                         2r + 2 ≤ n0 (r) ≤ 2r + 2 log2 r + O(log2 log2 r).
In particular, this determines the leading linear term of n0 (r) as 2r.
   A natural next question is whether the error term beyond the linear main term is bounded.
Problem 5.1. Does there exist an absolute constant C > 0 such that n0 (r) ≤ 2r + C for all
r ≥ 4?
   For several small values of r, namely r = 4, 5, . . . , 10, we found explicit r-multiplicity an-
tichains on [n] with n = 2r + 5 and
                                    S(F) = {2, 3, . . . , n − 2}.
Equivalently, g(2r + 5, r) = (2r + 5) − 3 for 4 ≤ r ≤ 10. The corresponding construc-
tions are recorded in [1, 2r_plus_5_r_4_to_10.txt]. These examples show that the extremal
value g(n, r) = n − 3 is attained already at n = 2r + 5 for 4 ≤ r ≤ 10, which provides
some numerical evidence toward Problem 5.1. Using our current search implementation [1,
antichain_solver_one_based.py], we did not obtain a construction for r = 11 within 24
hours of runtime.

      4. The chromatic interpretation: a critically 3-chromatic
                           3-graph with δ = 7
   We now turn to the chromatic-number interpretation from Section 2.
In the language of Property B, we seek a 3-uniform hypergraph that is
minimally non-2-colourable under both edge and vertex deletion, while
simultaneously forcing all degrees to be at least 7. Colour-critical hypergraphs
have been studied since the work of Toft and others,[8, 9, 11] but the particular
combination “3-uniform + weak colouring + minimum degree constraint +
edge- and vertex-criticality” does not seem to be covered by general structural
results. We therefore give an explicit compact example.
