## Units gate: PASS

Fix arbitrary N>=2 and let L be its binary bitlength: 2^(L-1)<=N<2^L. In particular L>=2, so every rational division below is by a positive number. Let p_1<...<p_k be the distinct prime divisors. Their product divides N: repeated use of Euclid's lemma for distinct primes proves this even when N has repeated prime powers. For each subset of these primes, its product d divides N and exactly N/d members of [0,N) are multiples of d, including 0. A residue is coprime to N exactly when none of these primes divides it. Finite inclusion-exclusion, or expansion of the product of indicator complements, therefore proves phi(N)=N product(1-1/p_i). Each factor is positive. No asymptotic theorem is used.

Since p_1>=2 and the increasing integer sequence has p_i>=i+1, p_i/(p_i-1)<= (i+1)/i. Multiplication gives N/phi(N)<=k+1. Also 2^k<=product p_i<=N<2^L; monotonicity of powers of 2 implies k<L, hence k+1<=L by integrality. Consequently phi(N)>=N/L. These statements apply equally to prime powers, primes and general composites. For N>=2, gcd(0,N)=N is not 1; the counted units are exactly in [1,N-1]. The proof does not accidentally count 0 as a usable numerator. No distribution of primes is assumed.

## Congruence fibers and bad-set gate: PASS

For fixed 1<=q<N let g=gcd(q,N)>0, q=gq', N=gn'. For either sign epsilon, aq congruent to epsilon*r modulo N implies g divides r. Conversely if r=gt, cancellation in integer divisibility shows the congruence is exactly n' divides aq'-epsilon*t. Coprimality gcd(q',n')=1 follows since a common divisor d>1 would make gd a common divisor of q,N larger than g. Bezout gives xq'+yn'=1. Thus b equal to the least nonnegative residue of epsilon*x*t modulo n' is a solution, and multiplication of the congruence by x proves every solution is congruent to b. This proves existence AND uniqueness of the reduced residue class for composite N as well as prime N. Its representatives in [0,gn') are precisely b+j*n', 0<=j<g: division of a-b by n' proves exhaustiveness, and the bounds prove exactly that range of j. The degenerate modulus n'=1 also has the unique class b=0 and g representatives; it is in fact unnecessary for q<N, but not a flaw in the argument.

For fixed q, sign and integer K>=1, all allowed positive remainders divisible by g are exactly r=g*t with 1<=t<=floor(N/(K*q*g)). There are that many, and each has g solutions for a. Hence the number of PAIRS (a,r) is exactly g*floor(N/(K*q*g))<=N/(K*q). There is just one solution-fiber factor g. The allowed-remainder count already divides by g; there is no second multiplicity or additive error. Excluding r=0 is essential and is done consistently.

All witnesses are finite: K,q>=1 and K*q*r<=N imply 1<=r<=N. The image on the a coordinate of the witness set has cardinal at most the witness set. Summing the preceding pair bound over q=1,...,N-1 and two signs gives |B_K|<=(2N/K)*H_(N-1). Duplicate witnesses, and signs agreeing modulo N, only overcount. This count bounds all residues, so it can safely bound the subset of units in B_K without any unjustified equidistribution assumption.

The dyadic blocks [2^j,2^(j+1)) for j=0,...,L-1 cover every q in [1,N-1], since q<N<2^L. A full block has 2^j integers, each reciprocal <=2^(-j); truncating at N cannot increase its sum. Thus H_(N-1)<=L. Set K=4L^2, a positive integer. Then |B_K|<=N/(2L)<N/L<=phi(N). If every unit were bad this would contradict cardinal monotonicity. Therefore choose a unit a outside B_K, with 1<=a<N. This is an unconditional fixed-denominator argument for every N; no new oracle premise has appeared.

## Euclidean invariants and quotient gate: PASS

Start r_0=N, r_1=a. Divide r_(i-1)=Q_i*r_i+r_(i+1) until the first zero, r_(s+1)=0. Strict descent proves finite termination, and preservation of common divisors proves r_s=gcd(N,a)=1. At every division 1<=r_i<r_(i-1), so Q_i>=1 and r_(i-1)>=2. The last quotient is Q_s=r_(s-1)>=2, including s=1.

Define q_0=0, q_1=1, q_(i+1)=Q_i*q_i+q_(i-1). All q_i for 1<=i<=s+1 are positive. The invariant N=q_i*r_(i-1)+q_(i-1)*r_i starts at i=1, and substituting both recurrences shows its right side at i+1 equals its right side at i. It holds also at i=s+1, although only i<=s is needed for witnesses. In particular 2*q_i<=N for 1<=i<=s, and 1<=q_i<N.

The signed congruence r_i congruent to (-1)^(i-1)*a*q_i modulo N starts at i=1. The separate i=2 base is r_2=N-Q_1*a congruent to -a*q_2. For i>=2 the recurrence subtracts Q_i times the i-th congruence from the (i-1)-st. The previous sign is the negative of the current sign, so the result is the next sign times a*(Q_i*q_i+q_(i-1)), as required. This covers i=s+1 too if desired. There is no negative exponent at i=0: its separate congruence is merely r_0 congruent to 0=a*q_0. The author does not use a formula with an undefined sign there.

If any Q_i>=K for 1<=i<=s, then N>=q_i*r_(i-1)>=Q_i*q_i*r_i>=K*q_i*r_i. With q=q_i and r=r_i these are valid positive witness bounds. Multiplying the signed congruence by the same sign gives a*q congruent to epsilon*r, exactly the convention defining B_K. This contradicts the choice of a. The final division uses r_s=1, never the zero r_(s+1), so it is controlled too. Thus EVERY positive Euclidean quotient is strictly less than 4L^2.

## Actual graph and encoding gate: PASS

The arithmetic word is interpreted in actual rooted, connected, finite edge-indexed looped graphs. U counts all-unicyclic spanning edge subsets and T counts subsets with a tree at the root and unicyclic other components. For a connected ambient graph containing a cycle, these U subsets are exactly bicircular bases: U subsets have |V| edges and cannot be extended as pseudoforests. Conversely a tree component of an independent subset can be augmented by an unused ambient edge to another component, or, if spanning, by an unused edge creating a cycle. The first augmentation merges a tree with a tree or unicyclic component; neither creates a second cycle. Thus such a subset was not maximal. This validates the ordinary B(G) basis interpretation, including isolated subset vertices and loops.

At the seed (one vertex, one labeled loop), U=T=1. Adding a new root loop splits U states by omission/inclusion into old U/old T, while T states must omit it. Adding a new rooted leaf splits new T states by absence/presence of its edge into old U/old T; every new U state must include that edge and corresponds to old U by leaf deletion. These restrictions and additions are inverse, disjoint and exhaustive. They establish exactly X and Y above. Distinct loops remain distinct elements; there is no automorphism quotient. Connectedness and a cycle persist, so the basis bridge continues to apply. Thus U=N is a graph cardinality, not a recurrence renamed as a matroid.

Writing s_G=v+m, X adds one unit of size and Y adds two. With S-1 letters, s_G<=2+2(S-1)=2S<16L^3, hence the claimed weak bound holds. For N=1 the seed has L=1 and s_G=2<=16; this case does not invoke the arithmetic lemma.

A precise decoder for the stated encoding reads ones until the first zero to get v, then ones until the next zero to get m; it then reads exactly 2m blocks of v bits and rejects an incomplete string, extra trailing data, v=0, or an endpoint >=v. The graph has vertices 0,...,v-1 and edges 0,...,m-1 with the two decoded endpoints at each edge position, viewed undirected. Every constructed graph gives a valid string because v>=1 and each index <v<=2^v, so leading-zero-padded v-bit blocks exist uniquely for fixed indices. Equal endpoints give loops; equal endpoint pairs at different positions give different edges. Root information is unnecessary in the final graph presentation. The decoder recovers the labeled graph chosen by the encoder; canonicalization under vertex relabeling or endpoint reversal is unnecessary.

The actual length is v+m+2+2mv. Since v,m<=s_G and s_G>=2, it is <=s_G+2+2s_G^2<=4s_G^2: the latter follows from s_G+2<=2s_G^2. Finally 4*(16L^3)^2=1024L^6. The seed's same encoding is covered at L=1. These are uniform constants for every positive N. No simple-graph or efficient-unit-selection strengthening is inferred.
