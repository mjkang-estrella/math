## Decomposition and exact colon audit

I inspected Kumar–Pomeroy–Tran §3, including Theorem 3.3's proof, and Herzog–Hibi–Hreinsdottir–Kahle–Rauh, arXiv:0909.4717v2, Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.2, Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.8 and Corollary 3.9 and their relevant proofs. These supply radicality over arbitrary fields, the completed-component prime decomposition, and the cut-point criterion. The generic determinantal blocks are geometrically prime: their coordinate rings embed by x_v = s t_v, y_v = t t_v in polynomial rings; for disjoint blocks use independent parameters. Thus the sums here remain prime over K, without the false general claim that an arbitrary tensor product of domains is a domain.

I also checked the crown classification directly. With at least two survivors on each side and at least five total, the graph is connected: a 3 by 2 subgraph is connected because each column misses only its matching partner, and all other vertices attach. If one side has zero survivors and the cut is mixed, an added vertex from the other side is isolated, hence is not a cut vertex. If one side has one survivor, an added deleted vertex from the opposite side has degree at most one and cannot be a cut vertex. With two survivors on both sides, unequal index pairs give at least three edges and a connected graph. Equal pairs leave exactly the two cross edges; adding any deleted vertex connects these components. The one-side cut cases are X, X minus a_i, and their symmetric versions. The empty cut is present because the full crown is connected. Each listed nonempty cut satisfies the strict component-count criterion for n >= 4. This fills the zero-survivor case implicit in the printed mixed-case argument.

Consequently the actual distinct minimal primes are P0, PX, PY, Q_i, R_i and M_ij (i < j). Q_i's star completion includes a_i together with all Y minus b_i; b_i is isolated. These ideals are incomparable, and their intersection is precisely J, not merely its radical or an ideal with the same K-rational zero set.

For a finite incomparable prime decomposition, (J:f) is the intersection of the primes not containing f. If this equals a proper prime P, the intersectand list is nonempty and P is contained in each intersectand. If none equals P, choose one element outside P in each and multiply; the product is in the intersection and outside P, a contradiction. The intersectand equal to P excludes every other intersectand by incomparability. This proves both directions of the separator equivalence with the required hypotheses. Distributing ideal colon also gives (J:P) = intersection of the other primes, since (P:P)=S and (Q:P)=Q for Q != P. A class there nonzero modulo J must lie outside P. All uses of exact colon in the author proof are valid after deduplication.

## Arbitrary polynomial lower bound

Every actual generator is homogeneous for deg x_v = deg y_v = e_v, so membership in each ideal, including intersections, holds componentwise. A component with a target cut vertex is already in that target. For the remaining components, support size is at most total degree, including constants and repeated vertex degrees. Zeroing coordinates outside the support is a ring retraction. For each testing prime used in the proof the support is disjoint from that prime's cut set; its image is exactly the ideal of the displayed surviving minors. Hence image and contraction agree. No surviving a_i is zeroed, and membership is never tested term by term in a sum of monomials.

For PX a surviving component h is in K[x_b,y_b : b in Y]. Each Q_i imposes D(Y minus b_i). Support of size zero or one gives zero immediately; size two gives zero by deleting one supported vertex. For support three and degree at most three, the degree is exactly three and each supported vertex has degree one. Two different deletions force division by [b_s,b_t] and [b_r,b_t]. In the ambient polynomial UFD these are irreducible, nonassociate quadrics. For example in the coefficient ring omitting x_bs, the coefficients y_bt and -x_bt y_bs are coprime, so Gauss's lemma applies to the primitive linear polynomial. Nonassociation follows from their different vertex degrees. Irreducibles are prime in this UFD, so their product divides any common multiple; the degree of a nonzero product is at least four. This is valid in characteristic two and does not use factoriality of a determinantal quotient. PX and PY have no separator of degree <= 3.

For Q_i, support outside the target cut lies in {a_i} union Y. Membership in PX forces a_i to occur in every nonzero component. If b_i is absent, P0 contracts to D(U), contained in Q_i. If b_i occurs, a cubic has at most one further supported vertex b_j. The actual M_{ {i,j} } contracts to ([a_i,b_j]); its other cross minor has the unsupported endpoint a_j. If there is no b_j, choose any j != i and the contraction is zero. The generators obtained are in Q_i. This includes degree-two support with powers, arbitrary coefficients, cancellations and characteristic two. Swapping the two sides proves R_i.

For P0, PX and PY force both sides of the support to occur. At degree <= 3 one side is a singleton. Q_i for the singleton a_i (or R_i for singleton b_i) contracts to minors already in P0. For M_ij only its four surviving vertices need be considered. Again both sides occur and one side is a singleton; Q_i or Q_j, or the symmetric R prime, contracts to at most the corresponding cross minor of M_ij. The cut of this testing prime is disjoint from the support. These are distinct primes even at n = 4. This proves every family, not just empty and mixed cases already claimed by the source.

Thus, for every target tau and homogeneous degree d <= 3, membership in all the other distinct primes implies membership in the target. Equivalently their degree-d intersection equals J_d. Arbitrary homogeneous polynomials follow by summing the multigraded components. The zero polynomial cannot be an exact-colon witness for a proper prime.

## All quartic witnesses

The full-side witnesses use four distinct vertices and are available at n = 4. All remaining choices need only three distinct indices. I checked every family rather than relying on the source's copied Q_i conclusion.

* P0: x_a1 x_a3 x_b1 x_b2 meets every other cut. A mixed survivor has only two index pairs whereas these vertices involve three indices. Under y = 0 the polynomial survives and D(V) vanishes.
* PX: [b1,b2][b3,b4] is outside L(X). For every Q_i one of the disjoint pairs avoids i and is a determinantal generator. PY and every R_i kill an endpoint in each Y pair; each mixed prime kills at least one of the four Y vertices. P0 contains both minors. PY is symmetric.
* Q_i: x_ai x_bk [b_i,b_j] with i,j,k distinct lies in PX and Q_l for l != i via x_ai, and in P0 via its minor. PY and R_l kill x_bk unless l = k; then they kill both endpoints of the minor. A mixed prime kills x_ai or x_bk unless its survivor pair is {i,k}; in that case it kills b_j. For nonmembership retain a_i, set all block y coordinates to zero, set x_bi = 0, y_bi = s, and keep block x coordinates independent. The image is -x_ai x_bk x_bj s != 0 in every field. R_i is symmetric.
* M_ij: [a_i,a_j][b_i,b_j] is in P0, both full sides, all Q_l and all R_l. Any different unordered mixed pair kills one of a_i,a_j. For the target assign columns a_i,b_j to (1,0) and a_j,b_i to (0,1), others zero. Its defining cross minors vanish while the witness is -1, also nonzero in characteristic two.

Each is nonzero and homogeneous of total degree four. Exact separator equivalence now gives (J:f) = P_tau, and the lower bound gives the exact minimum 4 for every cut prime. The source's Q paragraph conclusion using PX is incorrect notation, not an additional mathematical result. The direct verification above supplies the correct Q_i conclusion.
