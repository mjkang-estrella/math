                                           Abstract. If H = (V, E) is a hypergraph, its edge intersection hypergraph EI(H) = (V, E EI ) has
                                           the edge set E EI = {e1 ∩ e2 | e1 , e2 ∈ E ∧ e1 6= e2 ∧ |e1 ∩ e2 | ≥ 2}. In the present paper, we
                                           consider 4- and 5-uniform hypergraphs H, respectively, with EI(H) = Cn . Our results fill the

                                           Keywords. Edge intersection hypergraph, cycle

                                           1. Introduction and basic definitions

                                           All hypergraphs H = (V (H), E(H)) and (undirected) graphs G = (V (G), E(G)) considered in
                                           the following may have isolated vertices but no multiple edges or loops.
                                               A hypergraph H = (V, E) is k-uniform if all hyperedges e ∈ E have the cardinality k. Trivially,
                                           any 2-uniform hypergraph H is a graph. The degree d(v) (or dH (v)) of a vertex v ∈ V is the
                                           number of hyperedges e ∈ E being incident to the vertex v. H is r-regular if all vertices v ∈ V
                                           have the same degree r = d(v). In standard terminology we follow Berge [1].
                                               If H = (V, E) is a hypergraph, its edge intersection hypergraph EI(H) = (V, E EI ) has the edge
                                           set E EI = {e1 ∩ e2 | e1 , e2 ∈ E ∧ e1 6= e2 ∧ |e1 ∩ e2 | ≥ 2}.
                                               Let e = {v1 , v2 , . . . , vl } ∈ E EI be a hyperedge in EI(H). By definition, in H there exist (at
                                           least) two hyperedges e1 , e2 ∈ E(H) both containing all the vertices v1 , v2 , . . . , vl , more precisely
                                           {v1 , v2 , . . . , vl } = e1 ∩ e2 – we say, e1 and e2 generate the hyperedge e ∈ E EI . In this sense,
                                           the hyperedges of EI(H) describe sets {v1 , v2 , . . . , vl } of vertices having a certain, "strong"
                                           neighborhood relation in the original hypergraph H.
                                               For an application as well as to distinguish the edge intersection hypergraph from the notion
                                           intersection graph (known from the literature) see [4] and [5], respectively.
                                               Obviously, for certain hypergraphs H the edge intersection hypergraph EI(H) can be 2-
                                           uniform; in this case EI(H) is a simple, undirected graph G with V (G) = V (H). Note that we
                                           consistently use our notion "edge intersection hypergraph" also when the hypergraph EI(H) is
                                           2-uniform.
                                               The question, which hypergraphs are edge intersection hypergraphs EI(H), seems to be a
                                           difficult one. To simplify the problem, as a first step we can restrict ourselves to the case that the
                                           edge intersection hypergraph EI(H) is 2-uniform. Moreover, the situation that the underlying
                                           hypergraph H is k-uniform (k ≥ 3) may also be easier to handle than the general case.

   Problem 1. Find classes of graphs being edge intersection hypergraphs of k-uniform (k ≥ 3)
or non-uniform hypergraphs.
   In [4] the trees and a large class of cacti, respectively, being edge intersection hypergraphs of
3-uniform hypergraphs, have been characterized.
    For simplification, we often identify the vertices v1 , v2 , . . . , vn of a hypergraph H = (V, E)
with their indices, then we have V = {1, 2, . . . , n}. In general, the vertices in V will be always
taken modulo n.
    Let Cn = (V, E) be the cycle having n vertices, such that V = {1, 2, . . . , n} and E = {{i, i +
1} | i ∈ V }. For n ≥ 5, it is trivial to find a 3-uniform hypergraph H with EI(H) = Cn (see
Theorem 2 in Section 2). The analogous problem becomes much more difficult if we consider k-
uniform hypergraphs H for k > 3. In [5] the 6-uniform case has been investigated in combination
with the aim to minimize the number of hyperedges in the hypergraph H. In a certain sense,
the minimization of |E(H)| corresponds to an as simple as possible structure of the underlying
hypergraph H.
    This leads to a general question.
   Problem 2. Let G be a class of graphs, k ≥ 3, n0 ∈ IN+ , n ≥ n0 and Gn ∈ G a graph
with n vertices. What is the minimum cardinality |E| of the edge set of a k-uniform hypergraph
Hn = (V, E) with EI(Hn ) = Gn ?

section hypergraph EI(H) = Cn . That is in reference to Problem 2, G is the class of cycles Cn .

2. Some preliminary results

At first, we give a – simple, but very useful – sufficient condition for |E(H)| to be minimum.
Theorem 1. ([3]) Let H = (V, E) be k-uniform with EI(H) = Cn . If H is 3-regular, then H has
a minimum number |E| of hyperedges for all k-uniform hypergraphs H0 with EI(H0 ) = Cn , i.e.
|E| = µkn .
Proof. Since H is 3-regular, every vertex v is contained       P       in exactly three hyperedges of H. In
connection with the k-uniformity we obtain 3n = v∈V dH (v) = |E| · k.
Assume, there is a k-uniform hypergraph H0 = (V, E 0 ) with EI(H0 ) = Cn and |E 0 | < |E|. Then
|E 0 | · k < |E| · k = 3n implies the existence of a vertex u in H0 having the degree dH0 (u) < 3.
This contradicts EI(H0 ) = Cn : In Cn every vertex u is incident to two distinct edges e, e0 ∈
E(Cn ) = E(EI(H0 )); therefore in H0 there have to be hyperedges e1 , e2 , e01 , e02 ∈ E(H0 ) such that
e1 ∩ e2 = e and e01 ∩ e02 = e0 , where |{e1 , e2 , e01 , e02 }| ≥ 3. Clearly, u has to be contained in each of
the hyperedges e1 , e2 , e01 and e02 , i.e. dH0 (u) ≥ 3.
   Looking at the end of the proof, for the situation that two hyperedges e1 , e2 ∈ E generate the
edge e = e1 ∩ e2 ∈ E(Cn ), we also say that e1 and e2 contribute to e – they serve as half-edges
in EI(H).

hyperedges in the k-uniform case.

In the 5-uniform case, (3, 2)-hyperedges are "good" ones in the following sense. They can con-
tribute to generate three edges in EI(H) = Cn (they provide three half-edges) and they are
"easier to handle" than hyperedges containing a 4-section (i, i + 1, i + 2, i + 3) or a 5-section
(i, i + 1, i + 2, i + 3, i + 4). To see this, let us have a look at the "middle vertices" i + 1, i + 2
of a 4-section or a 5-section. In order to generate the edge {i + 1, i + 2} in EI(H) = Cn , it is
compelling to have a hyperedge with the 2-section {i + 1, i + 2} in H – that can be an annoying
restriction. So, in a first step, we deal with (3, 2)-hyperedges.
