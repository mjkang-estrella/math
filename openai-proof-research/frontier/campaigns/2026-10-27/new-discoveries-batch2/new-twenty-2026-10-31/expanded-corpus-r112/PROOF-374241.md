# MathDB 374241: cyclic Ramsey numbers of stars versus monotone paths

Status: candidate proposed by the r112 investigator. Zero credit until independent review and root acceptance.

## Literal statement

MathDB 374241 (raw line in `raw/374241.jsonl`, provenance in `extracted.json`) states:

> Let S_a be the start-central star with parameter a, and let P_b^mon be a monotone path of order b. Star–monotone-path conjecture: for any a ≥ 3 and b ≥ 4, R_cyc(S_a, P_b^mon) = 1 + (a − 1)(b − 2).

Source: N. Bašić, I. Damnjanović, D. Stevanović and I. Stošić, *Some results on small ordered and cyclic Ramsey numbers*, arXiv:2604.16188v1, Conjecture 4.16, with Table 11 as supporting data. The local HTML is `sources/2604.16188.html` (its sha256 is in the receipt). The definitions used are the paper's own.

* Vertex sets are {0, …, k−1}. S_a^sc is the star of order a with centre 0. P_b^mon has edges {i, i+1}.
* R_cyc(H₁, H₂) is the least n such that every 2-edge-colouring of K_n contains H_j in colour j for some j, via an embedding φ that is increasing up to a cyclic permutation. That means some t has (φ(t), …, φ(k−1), φ(0), …, φ(t−1)) increasing.
* For stars, the centre does not matter in the cyclic setting (the paper's discussion before Proposition 2.2), and S_a^sc is used.

## Theorem

For all integers a ≥ 3 and b ≥ 4, R_cyc(S_a, P_b^mon) = 1 + (a−1)(b−2).

### Lower bound

This is the source's own Theorem 4.5 with H = S_a. For m ≤ (a−1)(b−2), colour {u,v} with colour 1 iff ⌊u/(a−1)⌋ = ⌊v/(a−1)⌋.

* There is no colour-1 S_a: all a vertices would lie in one block of size a−1.
* There is no colour-2 cyclic P_b^mon. Along the rotated increasing order, the block index is non-decreasing. It strictly increases across each of the at least b−2 rotated consecutive pairs that are path edges; only the wrap pair (b−1, 0) of the path is not an edge. So it takes at least b−1 values, but only b−2 block indices exist.

### Upper bound (new): Theorem A

Let t ≥ 1, b ≥ 4 and N = (t+1)(b−2)+1. Let G be a graph on ℤ_N, read clockwise, in which every vertex has at most t non-neighbours. Then G contains a clockwise path on b vertices: distinct x_1, …, x_b with x_i ~ x_{i+1}, and clockwise offsets from x_1 strictly increasing and < N. (This is exactly a cyclic embedding of P_b^mon.)

*Proof.* For a vertex v, let J(v) be the least j ≥ 1 with v ~ v+j, and K(v) the least k ≥ 1 with v ~ v−k.

1. **Jump bounds.** Since v+1, …, v+t+1 are t+1 distinct vertices and at most t are non-neighbours, J(v) ≤ t+1. Likewise K(v) ≤ t+1.
2. **J + K ≤ t + 2.** The runs v+1, …, v+J−1 and v−1, …, v−K+1 are non-neighbours of v. They are disjoint because J+K ≤ 2t+2 ≤ N+1. So (J−1)+(K−1) ≤ t.
3. **Forced maximal jumps.** Suppose there is no clockwise b-path, and fix any s. Let g(v) = v + J(v). The sequence s−K(s), s, g(s), g²(s), …, g^{b−2}(s) is a clockwise path on b vertices whenever its span K(s) + J(s) + Σ_{i=1}^{b−3} J(gⁱ(s)) is ≤ N−1. So the span is ≥ N. By steps 1 and 2 the span is ≤ (t+2) + (b−3)(t+1) = N. Equality is forced, in particular J(g(s)) = t+1 (b−3 ≥ 1 because b ≥ 4). Since s was arbitrary, J(x + J(x)) = t+1 for every x.
4. **Contradiction.** Let u = g(0). Then J(u) = t+1, so K(u) = 1 by step 2, i.e. u ~ u−1. Applying step 3 repeatedly, J(u + k(t+1)) = t+1 for every k ≥ 0. At k = b−2 this gives u + (b−2)(t+1) = u + N − 1 ≡ u − 1 and J(u−1) = t+1 ≥ 2. So u−1 is not adjacent to (u−1)+1 = u, contradicting u ~ u−1. ∎

*Application.* Let N = 1+(a−1)(b−2). Take a 2-colouring of K_N.

* If some vertex has at least a−1 colour-1 neighbours, those a vertices, listed clockwise from the centre, give a cyclic colour-1 S_a.
* Otherwise every vertex has at most t = a−2 ≥ 1 colour-1 neighbours, i.e. at most t non-neighbours in the colour-2 graph. Theorem A gives a cyclic colour-2 P_b^mon.

Together with the lower bound and monotonicity in n, this gives R_cyc = N.

### Remarks

* Theorem A is sharp. Taking b−2 blocks of sizes as equal as possible shows that the maximum minimum degree of a graph on N cyclic points with no clockwise b-path is N − ⌈N/(b−2)⌉. The SAT data in `logs/mindeg-b{4,5,6}.log` confirm this exact value for b = 4, 5, 6 and N ≤ 13. That table is a diagnostic only.
* t ≥ 1 is needed: when t = 0, K_{b−1} has no b-path. This matches a ≥ 3. b ≥ 4 is needed in step 3: when b = 3, perfect matchings on K_4 (a = 4) are counterexamples, matching Table 11.
* The source's lower-bound theorem is used only for the lower bound. The upper-bound argument (jump functions, forced equality, orbit wrap) is not in the source. The source has only the ordered upper bound 1+(a−1)(b−1) via Theorem 4.1. The source's Table 11 lists exact values only up to a modest range, with "≥" entries elsewhere.
