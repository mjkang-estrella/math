# 321318: restored statement and obstruction

Source: Borg, Cross-intersecting sub-families of hereditary families, arXiv:1103.3858; author's final manuscript, Conjecture 3.1, definitions in Section 1, Sections 3–6 inspected. The hypothesis is finite H subset 2^[n], downward closed, H != {emptyset}, and k>=n+1. The intended nontrivial case has H nonempty (empty H makes the inequality vacuous). Cross-intersection means every A in A_i meets every B in A_j for i != j. The A_i may coincide or be empty. A largest star is S=H<x> of maximum cardinality. The exact conclusion is sum_i |A_i|<=k|S|, attained by all A_i=S. It is not a uniqueness assertion.

For every intersecting A subset H, take k=n+1 and A_i=A for all i. These are cross-intersecting, and the claimed conclusion gives (n+1)|A|<=(n+1)|S|, hence |A|<=|S|. This is exactly the unrestricted Chvatal star property. The paper explicitly gives this implication in Section 3. A compression proof must therefore overcome the full Chvatal obstacle; compressing H changes the problem unless the requisite preservation statements are proved. The author's main Theorem 3.11 assumes H is compressed with respect to an element, and cannot be applied to every hereditary H.

The historical classification as merely Chvatal-hard is now incomplete: live primary bodies include Chang–Liu–Liu, arXiv:2609.19123v1 (2026-09-16), Theorems 1.1–1.4 and Section 5's weighted result; Keevash, arXiv:2609.23595v1 (2026-09-20), Theorem 1.1 on Kahn's flow conjecture and its correlation consequences; Ellis–Filmus–Friedgut, arXiv:2609.28404v1 (2026-09-23), Theorems 1–2, including the projection-packing strengthening. These prove substantially stronger same-object hereditary statements than the old special cases. Palomar revision651 has a registered Lean formalization of the first paper, including a weighted theorem, at PALOMAR-2026-09-17-000004. Its preserved commit is c46f715af9d400523eb0d4c9c0d2335abbdc9fdd; current repository head retrieved here is c19ed3aaac9e42d446f963a862d39d8a09eddbf9. The pinned Main.lean theorem body was inspected. No claim of new Chvatal progress is available.

These statements do not, just by their wording, establish Borg's exact cross-sum bound. To see the remaining quantitative issue, put U=union A_i, U*=sets in U meeting every member of U, and U'=U\U*. Each member of U' belongs to at most one A_i; U* is intersecting. Therefore sum |A_i|<=k|U*|+|U'|. Chvatal alone gives |U*|<=|S|, but the needed strengthened bound is

    |U*| + |U'|/(n+1) <= |S|.

That would establish the entire requested range. No derivation of this inequality from the new weighted/flow/projection theorems has been completed here. One cannot silently replace U* and U' by a hereditary family; generally neither is downward closed. A crude argument using the hereditary tag product H times {emptyset,{1},...,{k}} and Chvatal only yields the weaker upper bound max((k+1)|S|,|H|), because original-coordinate stars acquire k+1 copies; it misses the exact k bound. This route was assessed structurally, not accepted as a proof.

Later Borg work on unions of levels (2018 preprints, including arXiv:1806.01093; search-level comparison only) imposes size/rank hypotheses and does not supply an inspected unrestricted full-scope theorem here. No counterexample, unrestricted bound, Lean endpoint, or exhaustive novelty clearance is claimed. Disposition: park this target for this task; zero credit.
