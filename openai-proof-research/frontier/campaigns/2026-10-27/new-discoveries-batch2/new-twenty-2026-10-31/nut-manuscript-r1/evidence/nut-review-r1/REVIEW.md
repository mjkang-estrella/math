`Cubic` is degree exactly three at every vertex. `adjacency` is the ordinary real 0–1 simple-graph adjacency operator. `Nut` requires at least two vertices, real nullity exactly one, and a nonzero kernel vector with every coordinate nonzero. On a one-dimensional kernel this is equivalent to every nonzero kernel vector being full. `Circulant 8` requires one graph automorphism with exactly eight integer-power orbits, all of a common size. CyclicBridge proves `cyclicGroupCirculant_iff` using a literal cyclic subgroup of vertex permutations preserving adjacency, not the full automorphism group. A finite cyclic subgroup has a generator, and its orbits coincide with that generator's integer-power orbits. Equal cycle lengths imply its common period and a semiregular action. No transitivity of the whole graph or stronger symmetry is assumed.

`Target` quantifies over all finite types in Type 0, with decidable equality and adjacency. This includes the ordinary finite graph universe in the source: every finite graph transports to Fin n, so the universe restriction is representational, not a missing family. Decidability is likewise available for finite graphs and imposes no mathematical restriction. Empty or singleton graphs cannot satisfy Nut. Positive orbit size follows from nonemptiness. The identity generator and common size m = 1 are included: this is the arbitrary eight-vertex graph case, not silently excluded. For m = 1 there are no internal shifts; for m = 2 the only possible nonzero internal shift is the unique involution. Both fit the extraction without an extra lower bound on m.

## B. Mathematics and architecture: PASS

I independently re-derived the reduction as follows.

Choose one representative from each of the eight cycles. The map (i,a) to σ^a of its representative is bijective because each cycle has exact length m. This gives Fin 8 × ZMod m coordinates and simultaneous translation invariance of adjacency. GlobalLabel proves the bijection and wraparound explicitly.

An automorphism pullback commutes with adjacency, so it acts by a scalar ε on the one-dimensional real kernel. Its finite positive period implies ε^m = 1, hence ε is +1 or -1, with m even in the latter case. Crucially this is one scalar for the entire kernel, not a separately chosen sign for each orbit. Thus u(i,a) = ε^a v_i, with every v_i nonzero.

Set C_ij to the sum of ε^b over neighbors (j,b) of (i,0). Edge reversal replaces b by -b; periodicity and ε in {±1} imply equal character values, so C is symmetric. For any vector w, the lifted vector ε^a w_i satisfies A(lift w)(i,a) = ε^a (Cw)_i. Evaluation at a = 0 is a left inverse, so this lift is injective on kernels. The original full kernel vector gives a nonzero full vector in ker C; injection into the graph kernel gives nullity at most one. Consequently C has nullity exactly one. KernelLift and QuotientExtraction prove these actual graph constructions without assuming the needed lift or stability as an unproved bridge.

A disconnected nut graph would allow restriction of its full kernel vector to one component, giving an independent or nonfull nonzero kernel vector. NutConnected formalizes this through arbitrary cuts. Coherence of character contributions is also forced: two opposite contributions among three nonzero neighbor values would cancel, forcing the third value to zero. Therefore |C_ij| equals the number of actual neighbors in orbit j; absolute row sums are three, and signed support cannot erase a crossing edge. This establishes connected support. A magnitude-three entry saturates both endpoint rows and isolates at most two vertices, contradicting connectedness on eight vertices; a diagonal three similarly isolates one.

Internal shifts are stable under b ↦ -b and exclude zero by looplessness. If there is just one, b = -b and b.val = m/2; its contribution is the SAME ε^(m/2) in every orbit having a semi-edge. If there are two, coherence gives diagonal +2 or -2. AdmissibleDiagonal derives precisely these alternatives, including small m, and EndpointOfFinite assembles the grammar for the SAME signed quotient used by the kernel proof. It does not replace that matrix with a convenient surrogate.

For A4, suppose a principal minor of the symmetric corank-one matrix were singular. Extend a nonzero minor kernel vector by inserting a zero at the removed coordinate. Its matrix product can be supported only at that coordinate. Pairing with the full kernel vector and using symmetry forces that remaining product coordinate to vanish too. The extended vector is then a kernel vector with a zero coordinate, contradicting the one-dimensional full kernel. Thus every principal minor is nonsingular. FullCofactor proves this directly and MatrixExtraction transfers determinant/cofactor identities from real to integer matrices.
