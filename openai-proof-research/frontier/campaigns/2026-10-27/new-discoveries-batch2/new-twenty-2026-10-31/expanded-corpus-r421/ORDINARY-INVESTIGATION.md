## 365146: the literal counterexample and the full source-scope symmetry route

Primary body: arXiv:2410.04611v3, dated 4 November 2024, acquired through the guard. Read introduction (range of X), Definition 2.1 and equation (1), section 2.2 and Algorithm 1, Definition 3.1, section 3.1.2 and Conjecture 3.2, and transformation/orthotope discussion around Conjectures 3.4 and 3.6–3.9. This is not a claim of reading every proof or figure in the 53-page manuscript. Its computational tables are observations, not premises in my argument.

The source defines X_K^D={0,...,2^(DK)-1}. Definition 3.1 uses the special word “subset” for all labels having the same radius after projection. This is substantially stronger than arbitrary set inclusion in the MathDB literal.

For the unrestricted literal take D=2, K=2, n=1, T={0,1,2,3}. These are four distinct members of {0,...,15}. Their sum is 6, whereas Psi=2^(2(2+1)-1)-2^(2-1)=32-2=30. This is a hand calculation, not a finite search. It disproves the unrestricted statement. It does not disprove the intended radius-class statement. Treating it as discovery would be a domain/wording correction and earns zero.

The stronger source-scope route actually closes an ordinary identity. Assume D,K are positive integers. Write the DK bits of x as b_(i,j), with dimension i and position j. Let C(x)=2^(DK)-1-x. This flips every bit, without carries, because x+(bitwise complement x) has every bit equal to 1. For a suffix of length L>=1, the BitDistance in dimension i is

  B_i = sum_(j=1)^L (2 b_(i,j)-1) 2^(-j).

Multiplying B_i by 2^L gives an odd integer: all terms except the last are even and the last is either 1 or -1. Consequently B_i is nonzero. In particular each normalization denominator H=(sum_i B_i^2)^(1/2) is positive; this pays the division-by-zero issue in Algorithm 1 for every nonempty suffix. The correction S_K=sum_(j=1)^K 2^(-j) is positive too.

Complementing all bits negates every B_i at every suffix. Each H is unchanged. Each normalized suffix vector therefore changes sign. Algorithm 1 takes a weighted sum of these vectors, with weights depending only on K and suffix position, not on x. Hence P(C(x))=-P(x) exactly, and ||P(C(x))||=||P(x)||. This argument works over ordinary Euclidean real coordinates in every positive dimension; it does not use approximate equality of computed radii, or assume the output lies on the unit sphere.

For a full radius class T={x in X: ||P(x)||=rho}, C is thus a bijection T to itself. Changing variables in a finite sum gives

  sum_(x in T) x = sum_(x in T) (2^(DK)-1-x),
  2 sum_(x in T) x = |T|(2^(DK)-1).

If |T|=2^D n, this yields

  sum_(x in T) x = 2^(D-1) n (2^(DK)-1)
                 = n(2^(D(K+1)-1)-2^(D-1)),

exactly the requested expression in the intended scope. An empty radius class has n=0 and also satisfies the formula. K=0 is outside the algorithm because its correction denominator vanishes; it has not been silently included. D=0 similarly lies outside the geometric definition.

A second symmetry route is even stronger structurally: flip only the bits belonging to one fixed dimension. This negates just that coordinate of every suffix vector, preserves every suffix norm, and reflects P in that coordinate hyperplane. The (Z/2Z)^D action preserves each radius class and acts freely on labels, since flipping a nonempty choice of dimension bit strings changes at least one bit when K>=1. Each orbit therefore has 2^D labels. At each of the DK bit positions exactly half those labels have a 1, so an orbit sum is 2^(D-1)(2^(DK)-1)=Psi. A full radius class is a disjoint union of these orbits. This proves its size divisibility as well as the sum, without the source's unproved orthotope decomposition, and checks the first route independently. It does not prove that the geometric orthotopes or dictionary mechanisms in later conjectures agree with these label orbits.

Adversarial limitation: a proper subset of a radius class need not be invariant under these flips. For instance T={0,1,2,3} above is not complement invariant. Thus replacing full classes by arbitrary inclusion destroys the proof. Conversely, interpreting “subset” by Definition 3.1 restores the proof but changes the apparent MathDB quantifier. This lane does not resolve that fidelity choice in its own favor. Strongest significance case: the uniform orbit proof establishes both a numerical conjecture and divisibility for every D,K, beyond the reported computations. Contrary gate: it is a source-definition-bound identity, and the literal-versus-source domain mismatch is precisely an excluded form of correction. No complete applicable literature-to-target bridge establishing routine adaptation has been found; I do not exclude it merely because symmetry is elementary. No full exact-literal qualified survivor is asserted.

## 329700: edge-poset quotient, raising operators, and chain routes

Primary: 1501.04540v2, 10 December 2017; read Definition 1.1, Conjecture 1.3, Definition 1.4, background definitions 2.4–2.8, Proposition 3.7 and its proof, duality 3.10–3.14, Lemma 3.15 and proof, Lemma 3.17 and proof, Theorem 3.18 and proof, and the construction of H through Proposition 3.26 and Corollary 3.27. The companion 2014 REU report was independently acquired, not recovered from forbidden history. Read section 9.3's explicit failed raising-operator diagram and section 9.4. These are actual primary bodies.

Let Q=B_n/G. An edge is an adjacent-rank comparable pair (O,O'); the edge order is the transitive closure of simultaneous covers in both coordinates. Coordinatewise comparability alone is not its definition. A group orbit of a Boolean edge determines (O,O'), but several edge orbits can have the same pair of endpoint orbits. The canonical map q:E(B_n)/G -> E(Q) is surjective, not generally bijective. Therefore the familiar theorem that quotients of unitary Peck posets are Peck does not finish the problem.

The exact source bridge is complete for CCT actions: H(B_n) is a disjoint union of n Boolean algebras B_(n-1); its unitary Peck property gives Peckness of H(B_n)/G. This maps bijectively and rank/order preservingly to E(B_n)/G. For CCT, q is also a bijective morphism. Peckness transfers under a bijective rank/order preserving morphism because conjugating an order-raising operator preserves both support on covers and its complementary-rank isomorphisms. Thus the source already proves all CCT instances. There is no new credit for those instances, for complement self-duality, or for rephrasing this chain of maps.

Route 1 tried to remove CCT by descending a raising operator U. Let V_i be the vector space on edge orbits and W_i the space on quotient edges, and extend q linearly. A descent Ubar satisfying q_(i+1) U_i=Ubar_i q_i exists exactly when U_i(ker q_i) is contained in ker q_(i+1). Here ker q_i is spanned by differences of edge orbits with the same endpoint orbit pair. A choice of representative edge cannot supply this invariance. The number of upward extensions into a given endpoint-pair class depends on stabilizers of representatives, not merely on the two endpoint orbits. Averaging over G only makes coefficients invariant on individual edge orbits, not between the different edge orbits merged by q. I have neither proved kernel invariance nor exhibited a hand-authenticated action where every possible raising operator fails. Invariance for one natural U would still need the complementary-rank maps on the quotient to be invertible; invertibility on V alone need not survive an arbitrary family of subspaces ker q_i.

Route 2 used separately averaged tensor coordinates. The natural embedding of quotient-edge space in V(Q_i) tensor V(Q_(i+1)) suggests U_i tensor U_(i+1), followed by projection back to comparable endpoint pairs. Both raising factors may be injective while their composition with that projection has a kernel. The companion report section 9.3 explicitly studies this mechanism and records failure of the required kernel containment. Thus this is a concrete prior failed mechanism, not an unread abstract hint. I do not repeat it as an original proof proposal. A new operator would need a positivity or representation argument controlling exactly the removed incomparable-pair terms. No such lemma is proved here.

Route 3 considered symmetric chains in Q. Complement descends to an order-reversing map on Q, and (O,O')->(complement O',complement O) reverses E(Q), proving rank symmetry. But rank symmetry plus Peckness of Q does not imply Peckness of E(Q). The source's Example 3.12 has a self-dual unitary Peck P whose edge rank sizes have a middle dip. It is not known here to be a Boolean quotient, so it is not a counterexample to the assigned conjecture. Lifting a symmetric chain decomposition of Q to its edges requires compatible choices between many overlapping chains; edges between different chains are not covered by the induced edge chains. I found no uniform matching covering these leftover edges. The unpaid lemma is a full collection of complementary-rank disjoint-chain matchings, or equivalently a suitable Lefschetz system, for arbitrary permutation groups. No finite orbit enumeration was performed.

Strongest significance case: an operator or chain construction valid without CCT would settle the exact universal conjecture and is not excluded by the public special-case theorem. Current outcome PARK, ordinary proof incomplete. Search located the final Order DOI 10.1007/s11083-016-9408-x; its actual version-of-record body was not fetched/read. That is a named final-source HOLD if a future survivor appears, not a global novelty veto or an access denial. The companion already establishes that the tensor route is not an unexamined shortcut.

## 329846: primitive elements of flat additive-type group schemes

Primary 1502.02150: read standing flatness and characteristic-p assumptions, Definitions 2.2 and 2.6, Proposition 3.6 with proof, Remark 3.7, Proposition 3.9 with proof, and section 8 through Theorem 8.12 and Remark 8.13. The source's Gr_A^+ is a category of affine commutative flat A-group schemes locally embeddable in a product of additive groups. The base is a ring of characteristic p. Dropping flatness of B_G is not allowed. Its finite, finitely presented subcategory already has locally free primitives by Proposition 3.6.

For an augmented commutative Hopf algebra B, write I=ker epsilon. The unit splits B=A direct-sum I as an A-module. A primitive element has epsilon zero (apply epsilon tensor epsilon to the primitive equation), so primitives are the kernel on I of the reduced coproduct

  d:I -> I tensor_A I,  d(b)=Delta(b)-b tensor 1-1 tensor b.

B flat makes I flat as a direct summand and I tensor I flat. Route 1 tried to conclude that ker d is flat. Kernels of maps between flat modules are not automatically flat over an arbitrary ring: the cokernel must satisfy appropriate Tor/purity conditions. In particular the Hopf structure, rather than the bare presentation as an equalizer, must do the work. Neither the unit splitting nor the counit supplies a projection from I onto ker d. Over a PID, I is torsion-free, its submodule ker d is torsion-free, and torsion-free modules are flat; over fields this is immediate as well. These cover restricted bases only. They do not settle arbitrary characteristic-p rings with zero divisors.

Route 2 sought an explicit nonflat primitive module inside a flat Hopf algebra. Start with B=A[x]/(a x^p), x primitive and a a nonzero nonunit. The relation is indeed a Hopf ideal since (x+y)^p=x^p+y^p. But as an A-module B has a direct graded component (A/aA)x^p. If A/aA is not flat, B is not flat. It therefore fails the standing source hypothesis and cannot be a counterexample. Replacing it by a monic additive equation B=A[x]/(x^p-a x) restores finite freeness, but then Proposition 3.6 applies and primitives are already locally free; this is not an unresolved case. Over characteristic 2, B=A[x,y]/(x^2-a y) is free on the monomials x^i y^j with i=0,1, since the equation is monic in x. The mixed coproduct of xy has x tensor y+y tensor x; these are distinct basis tensors, so multiplying by an annihilator of a does not kill them. This rules out the tempting elementary mixed-term primitive. It does not classify all primitives or certify a counterexample. No solver/CAS computation was used.

Route 3 tried a finite-stage approximation. An element of a finitely generated polynomial presentation uses finitely many generators and relations; one might hope that primitives are a filtered colimit of locally free primitive modules of finite flat additive-type groups. If such a compatible representation existed, flatness would follow because filtered colimits of flat modules are flat, and filtered colimits of modules commute with finite kernels. However a finite set of Hopf algebra generators gives a finite-type object, not necessarily a finite locally free algebra. Taking a finite sub-Hopf algebra over a general ring also does not automatically preserve flatness. For example G_a itself has polynomial algebra of infinite rank; its obvious finite-degree pieces are not subalgebras. Finite quotient constructions alpha_(p^m) have a different direction from subalgebra approximations and do not automatically present every B. The source's field-only inverse-limit argument in Remark 8.13 does not pay this general-base gap. Section 8 also explicitly leaves primitive-lifting Conjecture 8.5 open; invoking its conclusion is not a proof of 8.2.

The precise remaining alternatives are a Hopf-specific purity theorem for ker d in arbitrary flat additive-type algebras, a compatible flat finite-stage presentation, or a flat additive-type Hopf algebra with a rigorously nonflat primitive module. None is established. Strongest significance case: either would settle the literal full source-scope conjecture beyond its finite classification. No routine-adaptation exclusion is justified. PARK for a substantial unpaid algebra lemma; current literature and final-source coverage are incomplete, separately from the mathematical failure.

## Sequencing: scope audit and why the surviving route is not a solution

For 329762 and 329763, 1501.06872v1 explicitly defines s_1,...,s_k, not s_0,...,s_k, and excludes 0 from A. Thus the zero total s_k=0 is allowed without creating a duplicate at s_0. The source's Proposition 1.1 deletes a nonzero last element a: the remaining set has sum -a, so Alspach's nonzero-total conjecture would give a sequencing with all earlier sums nonzero. Appending a adds only the final sum zero. This is a complete public conditional bridge, not an unconditional resolution.

The MathDB phrase “no contiguous run has sum zero” in 329763 cannot include the entire nonempty zero-total ordering. For a concrete hand witness satisfying the no-opposites hypothesis, A={1,2,4} in Z_7 has total zero, has no zero or element of order 2, and no two opposite elements. Every ordering has the full run summing to zero; nevertheless the ordering (1,2,4) has partial sums 1,3,0, all distinct. Therefore the purported literal equivalence is false, while the meaningful sequencing assertion survives. This is a wording correction, not a refutation of the source problem.

A greedy extension can avoid at most the already visited partial sums: after r entries it forbids at most r residues. It guarantees progress only while enough unused choices remain and cannot force the last half of an arbitrary set. No-opposites rules out zero runs of length two, not longer runs; e.g. the full run in the Z_7 example. Adjacent swaps can move a collision to another interval, and no decreasing potential for all proper zero intervals was proved. Finite small-k results in the source are public and do not resolve arbitrary k. Both IDs remain PARK with source-fidelity warnings and zero credit.
