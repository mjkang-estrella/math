                                                                                             Abstract
                                                         Let a, b and c be positive integers. Let (G, +) be a finite abelian group of order
                                                     abc. A G-magic rectangle set MRSG (a, b; c) is a collection of c arrays of size a × b whose
                                                     entries are elements of a group G, each appearing exactly once, such that the sum of each
                                                     row in every array equals a constant γ ∈ G and the sum of each column in every array
                                                     equals a constant δ ∈ G. This paper establishes the necessary and sufficient conditions
                                                     for the existence of an MRSG (a, b; c) for any finite abelian group G, thereby confirming a
                                                     conjecture presented by Cichacz and Hinc.

                                           Keywords: magic square; magic rectangle set; abelian group


                                           1         Introduction
                                           Let m be a positive integer. A magic square of order m is an m × m array with entries from
                                           the set {1, 2, . . . , m2 }, each appearing exactly once, such that the sum of the m numbers in
                                           each row, each column, and each of the two main diagonals is the same. A magic square of
                                           order m exists for any positive integer m 6= 2. We refer the reader to [12] for a brief history
                                           and constructions for magic squares.
                                               Magic rectangles are a natural generalization of magic squares. Let a and b be positive
                                           integers. A magic rectangle MR(a, b) is an a × b array with entries from the set {1, 2, . . . , ab},
                                           each appearing exactly once, such that the sum of the entries in each row is a constant α, and
                                           the sum of the entries in each column is another constant β. The total sum of all entries in an
                                           MR(a, b) is aα = bβ = 1+2+· · ·+ab = ab(ab+1)/2. Thus α = b(ab+1)/2 and β = a(ab+1)/2.
                                           Since α and β are both integers, a and b must have the same parity. Harmuth [10, 11] settled
                                           the existence problem of magic rectangles as early as 1881.

                                           Theorem 1.1. [10, 11] For a, b > 1, there exists an MR(a, b) if and only if a ≡ b (mod 2) and
                                           (a, b) 6= (2, 2).

                                               Following the work by Bier and Rogers [2], Bier and Kleinschmidt [1] provided a simple
                                           proof for Theorem 1.1 by utilizing centrally symmetric rectangles. Subsequently, Hagedorn [9]
                                           further simplified Bier and Kleinschmidt’s method, resulting in an entirely theoretical proof
                                           that does not depend on any concrete example of magic rectangles given in [1, Section 2.7].

    Motivated by the need to schedule a special kind of incomplete round robin tournaments,
called handicap tournaments, Froncek [6] proposed a generalization of magic rectangles, which
he termed magic rectangle sets. Let a, b and c be positive integers. A magic rectangle set
MRS(a, b; c) is a collection of c arrays of size a × b, whose entries are elements of {1, 2, . . . , abc},
each appearing exactly once, such that the sum of each row in every a × b array equals a
constant b(abc + 1)/2 and the sum of each column in every a × b array equals another constant
a(abc + 1)/2. Froncek [7] settled the existence problem for magic rectangle sets.

Theorem 1.2. [7, Theorem 3.2] For a, b > 1, there exists an MRS(a, b; c) if and only if either
abc ≡ 1 (mod 2) or a ≡ b ≡ 0 (mod 2), and (a, b) 6= (2, 2).

    Let a, b and c be positive integers. Let (G, +) be a finite abelian group of order abc. A
G-magic rectangle set MRSG (a, b; c) is a collection of c arrays of size a × b with entries from
the group G, each appearing exactly once, such that all row sums in every a × b array equal a
constant γ ∈ G and all column sums in every a × b array equal another constant δ ∈ G. When
c = 1, an MRSG (a, b; 1) is called a G-magic rectangle, and is simply written as an MRG (a, b).
    Denote by Zv the additive group of integers modulo v. Evans [5, Theorem 1] investigated
the existence of an MRZv (a, b), which he termed a modular magic rectangle. Cichacz and Hinc
[3] generalized Evans’s result to any finite abelian group.

Theorem 1.3. [3, Theorem 3.11] For any abelian group G of order ab with a, b > 1, an
MRG (a, b) exists if and only if either a ≡ b (mod 2), or the Sylow 2-subgroup of G is noncyclic.

    Furthermore, Cichacz and Hinc [3] examined the existence of a G-magic rectangle set for any
finite abelian group G. They established the following necessary condition for the existence of
an MRSG (a, b; c) (see [3, Observations 1.6, 1.7 and 3.8]). We outline the proof for completeness.

Lemma 1.1. [3] Let G be an abelian group of order abc with a, b > 1. If there exists an
MRSG (a, b; c), then either a ≡ b ≡ 0 (mod 2), or the Sylow 2-subgroup of G is trivial or
noncyclic, and when 2 ∈ {a, b}, ab ≡ 0 (mod 4).

Proof. If the Sylow 2-subgroup of G is cyclic and nontrivial, then G has the unique element
of order 2, denoted by θ. Let M be an MRSG (a, b; c). Let γ and δ be the row sum and the

y ∈ H such that δ2 = 2y. It follows that δ = (δ1 , δ2 ) = 2(x, y). Without loss of generality, we
can assume that the element in the first row and the first column in the first 2 × (2l + 1) array
is (x, y), but then the element in the second row and the first column in this array has to be
(x, y) as well, a contradiction. Therefore, if there exists an MRSG (a, b; c), and one of a and b is
2, then the other cannot be odd.

    Cichacz and Hinc [3, Conjecture 5.1] conjectured that the necessary conditions for the
existence of an MRSG (a, b; c) given in Lemma 1.1 are also sufficient. They confirmed this
conjecture when there do not exist positive integers l and n such that either a = 2l + 1 and
b = 2n , or a = 2n and b = 2l + 1.

Lemma 1.2. [3, Theorem 3.9] For any abelian group G of order abc, when a, b > 1 and
{a, b} 6= {2l + 1, 2n } for some positive integers l and n, an MRSG (a, b; c) exists if and only if
either a ≡ b ≡ 0 (mod 2) or the Sylow 2-subgroup of G is trivial or noncyclic.

  This paper is devoted to examining all of the remaining cases not covered by Lemma 1.2.
We prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4. Let G be an abelian group of order abc with a, b > 1. An MRSG (a, b; c) exists
if and only if either a ≡ b ≡ 0 (mod 2), or the Sylow 2-subgroup of G is trivial or noncyclic,
and when 2 ∈ {a, b}, ab ≡ 0 (mod 4).

    In Section 2, we reduce the existence problem of an MRSG (a, b; c) over a finite abelian
group G to that of an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4, |S2|/4), where p is an odd prime and S2 is an abelian
and noncyclic 2-group. The exponent of a finite group G, denoted by exp(G), is the least
common multiple of the orders of all elements of G. In Section 3, we establish the existence of
an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4, |S2 |/4) for the case when the exponent of S2 is no more than 4. In Section
4, we establish the existence of an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4, |S2|/4) for the case when the exponent of S2
is larger than 4. Section 5 concludes this paper.


2     Problem reduction
By Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2, in order to prove Theorem 1.4, it remains to examine the existence of
an MRSG (2l + 1, 2n ; c), where l ⩾ 1 and n ⩾ 2. In this section, we reduce the existence problem
of an MRSG (2l + 1, 2n ; c) to that of an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4, |S2|/4), where p is a prime factor of 2l + 1
and S2 is the Sylow 2-subgroup of G.
    The following two constructions play an important role in our reduction of the problem.

Construction 2.1. [3, Lemma 3.3] Let G1 be an abelian group of order abc1 . Let G2 be an
abelian group of order c2 , whose Sylow 2-subgroup is either trivial or noncyclic. If there exists
an MRSG1 (a, b; c1 ), then there exists an MRSG1 ⊕G2 (a, b; c1 c2 ).

Construction 2.2. [3, Lemma 3.4] Let H be a finite abelian group. Let k and t be positive
integers such that t is a divisor of 2k + 1. Let hti be the subgroup of order (2k + 1)/t in Z2k+1 .
If there exists an MRShti⊕H (a, b; c), then there exists an MRSZ2k+1 ⊕H (a, b; ct).

Lemma 2.1. Let a ⩾ 3 be an odd integer and n ⩾ 2 be an integer. Let G be an abelian group
of order 2n ac, whose Sylow 2-subgroup S2 is noncyclic. Let p be a prime factor of a. If there
exists an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4; |S2|/4), then there exists an MRSG (a, 2n ; c).

Proof. For n ⩾ 2, if there exists an MRSG (a, 4; 2n−2 c), which consists of 2n−2 c arrays, each
of size a × 4, then we can select any 2n−2 of these arrays to form an array of size a × 2n .
Consequently, by considering c distinct ways to choose such a subset of arrays, we obtain an
MRSG (a, 2n ; c). Therefore, in order to establish the existence of an MRSG (a, 2n ; c), it suffices
to examine the existence of an MRSG (a, 4; 2n−2c).

     Furthermore, since p is a prime factor of a, if there exists an MRSG (p, 4; 2n−2ac/p), which
consists of 2n−2 ac/p arrays, each of size p × 4, then we can select any a/p of these arrays to
form an array of size a × 4. Consequently, by considering 2n−2 c distinct ways to choose such a
subset of arrays, we obtain an MRSG (a, 4; 2n−2c). Therefore, establishing the existence of an
MRSG (p, 4; 2n−2ac/p) is sufficient to derive the existence of an MRSG (a, 4; 2n−2c).
     Note that G ∼= Zpα1 1 ⊕ · · · ⊕ Zpαr r ⊕ S2 , where r ⩾ 1, p1 , . . . , pr are odd primes and α1 , . . . , αr
are positive integers. Without loss of generality, assume that p1 = p and α1 = α. Applying
Construction 2.2 with an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4; |S2|/4), which exists by assumption, we obtain an
MRSZpα ⊕S2 (p, 4; pα−1 |S2 |/4). Then apply Construction 2.1 to obtain an MRSG (p, 4; |G|/4p),
i.e., an MRSG (p, 4; 2n−2ac/p), as desired.

   By Lemmas 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1, to prove Theorem 1.4, it suffices to establish the existence of
an MRSZp ⊕S2 (p, 4; |S2|/4) for any odd prime p and any abelian and noncyclic 2-group S2 .
   In what follows we always assume that S2 is an abelian and noncyclic 2-group.
