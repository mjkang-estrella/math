## 351661: balanced V posets, full k and arbitrary positive m_i
Actual 2108.08033v1 definitions: V_m,n has a common minimum and two chains with m and n further points, with all cross-branch pairs incomparable. Ordinary copies are induced; weak copies only preserve order. Theorem1.3 proves the two-color balanced case; Theorem1.2 proves all-color m_i=1. Conjecture5.1 is precisely the assigned all-color extension, not the asymmetric result.

Put M=sum_i m_i. A rigorous lower-bound mechanism for BOTH notions is to color ranks0,...,M-1 in consecutive blocks of lengths m_i, and put the unique top of B_M into any color. Without the top, color i has only m_i ranks, so cannot contain a chain with m_i+1 points. If a weak V_mi,mi uses the top, each branch chain, including its common root, needs m_i+1 distinct ranks; both branches must end at the unique top. Injection prohibits their sharing that point. Thus the coloring avoids even weak copies and R_w>=M+1. This is the source's rank-block mechanism extended with explicit weak-copy justification; no independent credited theorem.

Route A: dimension-removal induction. In B_(M+1), suppose the bottom has color i. If the proper nonempty sets of color i have height at most m_i-1, then adding bottom gives height at most m_i, excluding the top. Decompose into m_i antichains by assigning to each point its longest-chain length. Repeatedly apply actual Lemma2.2 (antichain deletion leaves an induced Boolean lattice of dimension one less). Obtain B_(M+1-mi) avoiding color i except possibly its top; that top may be recolored, because the top of an induced Boolean lattice cannot belong to an induced V. Induct on the remaining colors. This fully pays the bounded-height branch of the proposed induction.

The other branch is the obstacle. A proper nonempty i-colored m_i-chain C lies in B^{u,v} with u in its least set and v outside its greatest. The opposite B^{v,u} has dimension M-1 and is incomparable to C. If it has an i-colored m_i-chain, C plus that chain and the bottom solves the problem. Otherwise removing its i-colored sets costs at most m_i-1 antichains and leaves only B_(M-mi). The required induction dimension is M-mi+1: exactly one dimension is lost. Merely quoting Mirsky or Theorem1.3 does not recover it.

Route B: coatom extension, following actual proof of Theorem1.3 on p8. When all coatoms avoid the bottom's color, TWO colors force them all into the other color. Apply induction with that other branch length reduced by one and append two suitable coatoms. For k>=3 the coatoms can have several colors. A repeated coatom color by pigeonhole does not ensure that the two branch endpoints of the smaller monochromatic V lie below two distinct coatoms of that same color. Nor can one merge other colors: a monochromatic copy for the merged color may mix original colors. Unpaid lemma: in every countercoloring of B_(M+1), produce a color j and a smaller j-colored V_(mj-1,mj-1) with two distinct compatible j-colored coatoms, or resolve the i-chain branch. This is a concrete extension lemma, not proved here.

Route C: Lubell/height approach. A chain can be V-free but occupy many ranks. Thus one cannot replace the missing induced branching information by a height<=m_i assertion on each color. A bound of m_i+1 per color would in any event lose k rather than the needed single unit in total dimension. No sharp multicolor flag inequality was established. No all-k upper bound, exact countercoloring, or literal Lean endpoint is complete. A finite-poset theorem could be feasibly formalized if the extension lemma existed; no execution authorized.
