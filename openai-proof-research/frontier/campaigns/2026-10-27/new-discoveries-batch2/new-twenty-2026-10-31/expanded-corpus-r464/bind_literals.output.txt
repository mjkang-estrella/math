ID 330842 
 Let $G$ be an infinite graph with vertex set $E$. For distinct vertices $x,y\in E$, write $G|E\setminus\{x\}$ for the subgraph induced by deleting $x$, and write $\sim$ for graph isomorphism. **Ulam reconstruction conjecture.** If

$$
\forall (x,y)\in E^2,\quad x\neq y;\quad G|E\setminus\{x\}\sim G|E\setminus\{y\},
$$

then $G$ is Ulam-reconstructible. The source presents this as a conjectural sufficient condition for infinite-graph reconstruction, with no supplied resolution.

Let $F_{\widehat{(G,\mathbf{D})}}$ be the associated space and let $\operatorname{Gr}^{>0}_{m,n}$ denote the totally positive Grassmannian. **Global-branch positivity conjecture.** One can choose a brushing and signs for which the higher-intersection positivity conjecture holds, and

$$
F_{\widehat{(G,\mathbf{D})}}\cap\left(\operatorname{Gr}^{>0}_{m,n}\times\operatorname{Gr}^{>0}_{m,D^{(1)}}\times\cdots\times\operatorname{Gr}^{>0}_{m,D^{(\ell)}}\right)

is the disjoint union of a fixed number of graphs of functions from $\operatorname{Gr}^{>0}_{m,n}$ to $\prod_{D\in\mathbf{D}}\operatorname{Gr}^{>0}_{m,D}$. This remains open beyond the illustrated cases.

ID 371195 
 Let $R=(R_1,\ldots,R_k)$ be a sequence of rectangles. Suppose the distinct rectangles $R_j$ occur with multiplicities $m_j$, and set

$$
d_R=\gcd(m_j).
$$

For $i\in[d_R]$, let $\ell_{d_R}^{(i)}$ denote the corresponding cyclic character, and let $s_{R_j}$ be the Schur function associated with $R_j$. The charge-$i$ connected component of the generalized dual equivalence graph $\mathcal{T}(R)$ is assigned its character. **Cyclic-character formula conjecture.** That character is

$$
\ell_{d_R}^{(i)}\left[s_{R_1}^{m_1/d_R}\cdots s_{R_k}^{m_k/d_R}\right].
$$

This refines the conjectured coincidence of the three stratifications by giving the character of each charge connected component. The paper proves that the total character is the product of the rectangular Schur functions and that the latter two stratifications coincide, but the displayed componentwise plethystic formula remains conjectural.

ID 372283 
 Let ${\mathcal C}$ be an $[n,k,d]_q$ generalized Reed–Solomon (GRS) code, and let $\operatorname{GRS\text{-}cover}$ denote the covering algorithm. The **average number of punctures** is the expected number of coordinates punctured before the algorithm succeeds in returning a codeword within the covering radius.

**Puncture-count conjecture.** For an $[n,k,d]_q$ GRS code, the average number of punctures needed for $\operatorname{GRS\text{-}cover}$ to succeed in returning a codeword within the covering radius is less than

$$
\begin{cases}
c_1(d-1) & \text{when } \operatorname{GRS\text{-}decode \text{ is a unique decoder},\\
c_2 & \text{when } \operatorname{GRS\text{-}decode}=\operatorname{GS} \text{ is the list decoder},
\end{cases}
$$

for some positive constants $c_1<1$ and $c_2$.

The source later states that the first part does not hold in general; the second part remains unresolved there. Thus this combined conjecture is refuted, with its list-decoder component still open.

 Let $(\Gamma,+)$ be a finite abelian group, and let $\mathcal{G}$ be the set of finite abelian groups that either have odd order or contain more than one involution. For integers $m,n>1$ and $c\geq 1$, an $\mathrm{MRS}_\Gamma(m,n;c)$ is a set of $c$ $m\times n$ arrays whose entries are the elements of $\Gamma$, each appearing once, with constant row sums and constant column sums in every array. **Cichacz–Hinc conjecture.** An $\mathrm{MRS}_\Gamma(m,n;c)$ exists if and only if $m$ and $n$ are both even, or $\Gamma\in\mathcal{G}$ and $\{m,n\}\neq\{2\ell+1,2\}$. ID 352903 
 Let $T(K_n;a,b)$ denote the Tutte polynomial of the complete graph $K_n$, and let $p$ be an odd prime and $k$ a positive integer. Let $
u$ denote congruence modulo the indicated power of $p$. **A.P. Mani and R.J. Stones' conjecture.** If $n\geq p^k$, $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$, and $b\not\equiv 1\pmod p$, then

$$
T(K_n;a,b)\equiv\begin{cases}
b^{\frac{\phi(p)}{2}-1} & \text{if }p\geq 3,\ n=p,\text{ and }a\equiv 1\pmod p,\\
b^{\frac{\phi(p^k)}{2}}T(K_{n-\phi(p^k)};a,b) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}\pmod p.
$$

If $n\geq p^k$, $a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$, and $b\equiv 1\pmod p$, then

$$
T(K_n;a,b)\equiv\begin{cases}
(n+a-1)^{p^k}T(K_{n-p^k};a,b) & n>p^k,\\
(a-1)^{p^k-1} & n=p^k
\end{cases}\pmod {p^k}.
$$

This conjecture refines the known congruence for $T_n(1,b)$ modulo prime powers by proposing recurrences for the complete-graph Tutte polynomial with general integer parameters $a$ and $b$. The supplied source does not establish a resolution, so the conjecture is recorded as open.

ID 347057 
 Let $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$ be finite spatial graphs corresponding to the same combinatorial graph $\mathcal{G}$. A **forest–tangle decomposition** is a decomposition of a spatial graph as a connected sum

$$
\Gamma=\overline{T}\mathbin{\sharp}\overline{\beta},
$$

where $\overline{T}$ is a forest and $\overline{\beta}$ is a tangle.

**Decomposition criterion.** The graphs $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma'$ are equivalent if and only if they admit decompositions

$$
\Gamma=\overline{T}\mathbin{\sharp}\overline{\beta},\qquad \Gamma'=\overline{T'}\mathbin{\sharp}\overline{\beta'},
$$

where $\overline{T}$ is equivalent to $\overline{T'}$ as spatial graphs and $\overline{\beta}$ is equivalent to $\overline{\beta'}$ as tangles.

This criterion would separate equivalence of spatial graphs into the equivalence of their forest and tangle components, addressing the nonuniqueness of the decomposition arising from the choice of a maximal forest. The supplied text does not establish the claim or indicate whether it has been proved or disproved.

ID 342139 
 Let $V$ be a finite vertex set, let $D$ be the presheaf of hypergraphs, and let $C\subset D$ be a subfunctor. For each vertex $x\in V$, write $\jmath_x:V-x\hookrightarrow V$ for the inclusion and let $C(\jmath_x):\mathbf{C}_V\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{V-x}$ be the corresponding deletion functor. The diagonal functor is

$$
\Delta C_V:\mathbf{C}_V\longrightarrow\prod_{x\in V}\mathbf{C}_{V-x}.
$$

An isomorphism in the image of $\Delta C_V$ is called a **hypomorphism**. **The reconstruction conjecture.** Two hypergraphs in $\mathbf{C}_V$ are isomorphic if and only if they are hypomorphic. Equivalently, $\Delta C_V$ is essentially injective. This is the sheaf-theoretic formulation of the usual reconstruction conjecture for the class of graphs defined by $C$; its status is not resolved in the source.

 A hypergraph reconstruction context consists of a category $\mathfrak{C}$ of applicable prestacks, a deleting process $\mathcal{D}:\mathfrak{C}\rightarrow[\mathbb{X}^{op};\mathbf{Cat}]$, and a natural transformation $\gamma:\imath_{\mathfrak{C}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{D}$. For each applicable prestack $C\in\mathfrak{C}$ and each $V$, let $\mathcal{D}C_V$ be the category of pieces, and call two hypergraphs **$\mathcal{D}$-hypomorphic** when their images in $\mathcal{D}C_V$ are isomorphic. **The deleting-process reconstruction conjecture.** For each $V$, two hypergraphs in $C_V$ are isomorphic if and only if they are $\mathcal{D}$-hypomorphic; equivalently, each functor

$$
\gamma_V:C_V\rightarrow\mathcal{D}C_V
$$

is essentially injective. This axiomatizes the ordinary and disjoint reconstruction conjectures in a common reconstruction context; the source provides no resolution.

ID 343484 
 Fix $0<q<1$. Let $G(n,q)$ be an Erdős–Rényi random graph, let $p$ be a prime, and let $\Gamma$ be a finite abelian $p$-group equipped with a **perfect pairing** $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$. Let $K(G(n,q))_{(p)}$ denote the Sylow $p$-subgroup of the critical group, equipped with its associated monodromy pairing. For a paired group, write $\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ for the automorphisms preserving the pairing.

**Clancy–Leake–Payne conjecture.** As $n$ tends to infinity, the probability that $K(G(n,q))_{(p)}$ with its monodromy pairing is isomorphic to $(\Gamma,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ is

$$
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{1-2i})}{|\Gamma|\,|\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)|}.
$$

Here $\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)$ is the set of automorphisms of $\Gamma$ that preserve the pairing. This is the paired analogue of the distribution theorem for Sylow subgroups of critical groups of Erdős–Rényi random graphs; the source gives no resolution evidence.

ID 344274 
 Let $X$ be a finitely defined class of graphs, and let its minimal forbidden induced subgraphs be given. A graph is **$P_3$-free** if it has no induced path on three vertices; a **forest** is a graph without cycles; and the complement of a graph is obtained by replacing edges with nonedges and nonedges with edges.

**Forbidden-subgraph conjecture for linear Ramsey numbers.** The Ramsey numbers of $X$ are linear if and only if the set of minimal forbidden induced subgraphs for $X$ contains a $P_3$-free graph, the complement of a $P_3$-free graph, a forest, and the complement of a forest.

This conjecture is presented as the expected consequence of the conjecture relating linear Ramsey numbers to bounded co-chromatic number, together with the cited characterization of bounded co-chromatic number. Its status is not resolved in the supplied text.

ID 338254 
 Let $G$ be a graph with $V\geq 9$ vertices. A $(G,k)$-cockade is obtained recursively by starting with a graph isomorphic to $G$ and repeatedly identifying complete subgraphs of size $k$ of two such cockades. For graphs $K_{a_1,\ldots,a_r}$, the notation denotes the complete multipartite graph with parts of sizes $a_1,\ldots,a_r$.

**K10-minor extremal graph conjecture.** A graph on $V\geq 9$ vertices with no $K_{10}$-minor has at most $8V-36$ edges, unless it is isomorphic to one of the following graphs: a $(K_{1,1,2,2,2,2,2},7)$-cockade; $K_{1,2,2,2,3,3}$; $K_{2,2,2,2,2,3}$; $K_{2,2,2,2,2,3}$ with an edge deleted; $K_{2,3,3,3,3}$; $K_{2,3,3,3,3}$ with an edge deleted; $K_{2,2,3,3,4}$; or the graph obtained from the disjoint union of $K_{2,2,2,2}$ and $C_5$ by adding all edges joining them.

This conjecture generalizes the known extremal results for graphs with no $K_8$- or $K_9$-minor, identifying the possible exceptional graphs in the $K_{10}$-minor case. Its resolution is not supplied in the source.

ID 335262 
 An **ultrafilter satisfying a partition relation** is an ultrafilter with some partition property, and a forcing is **$c3$-closed** when every descending sequence of conditions of length less than $c3$ has a lower bound. A **topological Ramsey space** is a space whose finite-approximation structure satisfies the Ramsey-space axioms.

**Topological Ramsey space forcing conjecture.** Every ultrafilter which satisfies some partition relation and is forced by some $c3$-closed forcing is actually forced by some topological Ramsey space.

The conjecture proposes a broad connection between ultrafilters with weak partition properties and topological Ramsey spaces, extending the examples in which dense subsets of the relevant forcings form topological Ramsey spaces. Its status is not resolved in the supplied source.

 Let ${\mathcal{M}}$ be an oriented matroid, and let ${\mathcal{M}}_1\leadsto {\mathcal{M}}_2$ denote a rank-preserving weak map. The graphical oriented matroids associated with the directed graphs $K_4$ and $K_{2,3}$ are denoted by ${\mathcal{M}}_{\vec K_4}$ and ${\mathcal{M}}_{\vec K_{2,3}}$.

 Let $\mathbb F$ be a finite field, let $m$ be a positive integer, and let $\mathcal M$ be a minor-closed class of $\mathbb F$-represented matroids. A frame template specifies a class $\mathcal{M}(\Phi)$ of represented matroids conforming to it, and $\widetilde M$ denotes the underlying matroid of a represented matroid $M$; when $\mathbb F$ has characteristic $p\ne0$, $\mathbb F_p$ is its prime subfield.

**Frame-template conjecture.** There exist $k\in\mathbb Z_+$ and frame templates $\Phi_1,\ldots,\Phi_s,\Psi_1,\ldots,\Psi_t$ such that $\mathcal{M}$ contains each class $\mathcal{M}(\Phi_i)$, contains the duals of the represented matroids in each class $\mathcal{M}(\Psi_j)$, and, whenever $M$ is a simple vertically $k$-connected member of $\mathcal M$ whose underlying matroid $\widetilde M$ has no $PG(m-1,\mathbb F_p)$-minor, either $M$ belongs to some $\mathcal{M}(\Phi_i)$ or $M^*$ belongs to some $\mathcal{M}(\Psi_j)$.

ID 332619 
 A graph is **$n$-apex** if deleting $n$ or fewer vertices results in a planar graph. A graph is a **minor** of another graph if it can be obtained by contracting edges in a subgraph. Let $\mathcal{O}_n$ be the set of graphs that are not $n$-apex although every proper minor is. The **$K_{n+5}$ and $K_{3^2,1^n}$ families conjecture.** For each $n>0$, the $K_{n+5}$ and $K_{3^2,1^n}$ families are in $\mathcal{O}_n$. Here $K_{n+5}$ is the complete graph on $n+5$ vertices, $K_{3^2,1^n}$ is the complete multipartite graph with two parts of three vertices and a further $n$ singleton parts, and each family consists of graphs obtained through zero or more triangle-Y or Y-triangle moves. The conjecture would identify these families as minor-minimal obstructions to the $n$-apex property and provides evidence toward determining the finite obstruction sets $\mathcal{O}_n$; the paper reports computational evidence but does not establish the claim in general.
