The original paper is Li, Li, Miller, Mizgerd, Sun, Xia, and Zhou, "Deterministic Zeckendorf Games," Fibonacci Quart. 58 (2020), no. 5, 152–160.

- **The four variants:** The authors studied four deterministic variants of the game, where there was a fixed order on which available move one takes: Combine Largest, Split Largest, Combine Smallest and Split Smallest.
- **Split Smallest:** The paper's abstract describes it as the variant with the most moves. It has the largest number of moves over all possible games, and is close to the new upper bound. That upper bound is 3n − 3Z(n) − IZ(n) + 1, where Z(n) counts the Zeckendorf terms and IZ(n) sums their indices.
- **Contrast with Combine Largest and Split Largest:** These two realize the lower bound. Both have MS(n) = 0 and MC(n) = n − Z(n).
- **Conjectures:** The paper conjectures that for Split Smallest, the number of moves grows linearly with n; numerically the constant appears to be the golden mean squared. For Combine Smallest, the constant appears to be approximately 1.206.
- **Later confirmation:** A 2022 Fibonacci Quarterly follow-up says the Split Smallest game described in [6] is a deterministic example of this algorithm, and therefore, realizes the longest game.
