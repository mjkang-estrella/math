## 364771: complete planar-class refutation and decisive routine-adaptation exclusion

Source: Morelle–Sau–Thilikos, arXiv:2409.08883v1, section 2.2 and Conjecture 1 in section 6. An identification partition has order equal to the number of vertices in its support; it is not the number of pair-identification operations. Identifying a set replaces it by its neighbour-union heir. Edge contractions have their usual unit-edge cost. These definitions were read. The corpus phrase “edge identifications” is imprecise; the following refutes the intended source parameter and also the pair-identification-operation interpretation (fixed cost 1). If one interprets that phrase as allowing only contraction of existing edges, the conjecture becomes tautological; this wording ambiguity is a separate scope gate, not a claimed discovery.

Let P be the minor-closed class of finite planar simple graphs. For each integer L>=3 let S_L be the graph obtained from K5 by replacing every edge with an internally vertex-disjoint path of exactly L edges. Its five original vertices are called branches.

Claim 1: id_P(S_L)=2 under the source definition. Identify any two branches u,v. To draw the quotient, start with a planar drawing of K4 on the merged branch w and the remaining three branches. Each of the three edges from w has two parallel internally disjoint length-L paths; put the second path beside the first in a disjoint narrow ribbon. The three edges between the other branches have one length-L path each. Finally the old u-v path becomes a length-L cycle attached only at w; draw it in a small free sector at w. All internal vertices remain distinct, and these ribbons and the attached cycle meet only at intended endpoints. This is a planar drawing of exactly the identification quotient. Thus the two-vertex partition suffices. A partition with support 0 or 1 cannot change the graph, and S_L is nonplanar: contracting along each subdivided path gives K5; planar graphs remain planar under contractions; K5 is nonplanar by Euler's edge bound 10>3*5-6=9. Hence cost exactly 2.

Claim 2: ec_P(S_L)>=L. Before a contraction class contains two distinct branches, every class either lies within one original path or contains exactly one branch with initial segments of its incident paths. This follows because the only junctions of original paths are the branches. Consequently the quotient still consists of five distinct branch images joined by ten internally disjoint paths with at least one edge per path. There is only one original path per branch pair, so simplification removes no pair of distinct connecting paths. It is still a subdivision of K5 and is nonplanar. To merge two branches, the contracted original-edge set must contain a connected subgraph joining them, hence a path of length at least L (all branch-to-branch distances are at least L). A sequence of t contractions can be lifted to a forest of t original edges giving its contraction classes: at each step choose one original edge joining the two prior classes. Thus t>=L at the first merger. Any planar quotient needs such a merger, proving the claim. Conversely contracting a whole original path costs L and gives the same ribbon-planar K4 picture with the attached cycle removed, so ec_P(S_L)=L, although only the lower bound is needed.

For any proposed f_P:N->N choose L=max(3,f_P(2)+1). Then ec_P(S_L)>=L>f_P(2)=f_P(id_P(S_L)). This is the exact universal functional-bound negation for the source interpretation. No monotonicity assumption on f is used.

Earlier-result bridge: Golovach–van 't Hof–Paulusma, Obtaining Planarity by Contracting Few Edges, MFCS 2012 author PDF, introduction p.3, already constructs K5 plus long paths, deletes the ten core edges, and explicitly proves that the resulting uniformly subdivided K5 needs at least the full length of one path in contractions. Its graph G*_p, with p=L-1, is planar after contracting an original core edge uv. Delete the other core edges after that contraction. The resulting graph is precisely S_L with its nonadjacent branches u and v identified. Planarity is preserved by deletions. This gives id<=2 directly from the published construction; the lower bound is its stated example. The only remaining observation is that singleton identifications cannot alter S_L. This completes the earlier-result reduction. Our K3,3 route is the same subdivision mechanism and does not evade the family exclusion. Exclusion is the complete explicit adaptation, not simplicity or shortness. No claim is made that a published paper explicitly names this as a refutation of the later conjecture.

Initial failed shortcut: contracting a chosen short connection between identified vertices would simulate identification, but arbitrary graphs do not bound that distance by identification support size. The long-subdivision family is exactly the missing-distance obstruction. The forest lemma avoids it by bounding long-cycle minors via identification distance; planar graphs admit arbitrarily long cycles and therefore its premise cannot be transferred.

## 362228: complete endpoint refutation, quantitative route remains unpaid

The survey's definition makes tau(G) the largest balanced biclique subgraph size, not necessarily induced. Let F be all forests. It is minor-closed and proper, since it excludes a triangle. In the host edge ab, take connected vertex sets A={a} and B={a,b}. Their intersection graph is K2. Thus K2 belongs to int(F), tau(K2)=1, and both vertex degrees equal 1. For every finite real constant c and every ordinary logarithm base greater than 1, c*1*log(1)=0. There is no vertex of degree at most zero. This negates the literal display even with nonempty graphs and nonempty connected representing sets.

This is only an endpoint correction. Adding 1 inside the logarithm or restricting to tau>=2 evades this object. The survey explicitly identifies a generalization of a Fox–Pach separator lemma as the missing piece for the intended quantitative claim. Degree-boundedness alone supplies no O(t log t) rate, and success for intersection graphs of subtrees does not transfer to all proper minor-closed hosts. No full proof of that repaired substantive assertion was obtained. No credit or Pi work.
