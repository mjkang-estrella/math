## 365839: Helly–Radon equality — three structural routes and exact gap

Use the source convention: h and r are largest H-independent and R-independent cardinalities. Do not replace r by the first cardinality forcing dependence, which differs by one in other literature. Delta closure repeatedly adds a vertex completing a triangle with an adjacent pair already present. H-dependence means intersection over s in S of cl(S\{s}) is nonempty. R-dependence means some partition S=A disjoint-union B has intersecting hulls. The published2025 chapter retains Conjecture1 and directs readers to arXiv for proofs omitted for page limits. Its actual HTML was fetched and the definitions/conjecture compared. Printed examples' text labels were seen; their full graphical incidence has not been independently reconstructed.

Route1: seek a direct implication H-dependent=>R-dependent. For a common hull vertex w, select inclusion-minimal seed supports U subset S with w in cl(U). H-dependence says their total intersection is empty. Radon dependence follows if two such supports are disjoint, by placing them on opposite sides of a partition. But empty total intersection does NOT imply a disjoint pair: {a,b},{b,c},{a,c} is the elementary obstruction. Thus ordinary Helly/Levi inequalities give only h<=r, not equality. A missing graph-specific support-uncrossing theorem would have to rule out all such intersecting support configurations in an R-independent set, or replace the set without reducing its size.

Route2: investigate whether the triple support obstruction is realizable by triangle closure. In an R-independent set S, no member s belongs to cl(S\{s}), because singleton versus complement would already be a Radon partition. For |S|=3, if it were H-dependent its common hull point would lie outside S. Each two-point hull must therefore be nontrivial. A nonadjacent pair has no first triangle-completion step and is closed; consequently all three pairs in S must be edges. But then S itself is a triangle, and any vertex lies in the closure of the other two, a contradiction. Hence every R-independent triple is H-independent. Singletons and pairs are also H-independent under this closure. This establishes a small structural obstruction, NOT the full conjecture and NOT new credit. At size4 and above a missing vertex leaves three or more seeds and can start a multi-stage propagation; the two-point argument stops.

Route3: try decomposing the graph into triangle blocks, with edges connected through triangles sharing an edge. Tree-like triangle assemblies permit support leaf elimination; this is closely aligned with the source's chordal results and does not yield a new target. In general, closure can use several initially separate growing sets together. A vertex-level decomposition cannot simply assume that the hull of a union is the union of hulls. Closing a newly available edge can create further triangles. To finish positively one needs either (i) a rigorous exchange/uncrossing lemma for maximum R-independent sets that produces an equal-size H-independent set, including such cyclic interactions, or (ii) a different global upper bound r<=h. To refute equality, an explicit R-independent S and proof of a STRICTLY SMALLER global h are both required. Merely exhibiting one H-dependent R-independent S would not suffice: another H-independent set of the same size might exist. No such graph and global bound were obtained. No enumeration was run.

Disposition: parked, precise full-scope gap. No dormant Pi execution plan is justified by the small-set helper.

## 363648: supereulerian digraphs — bottleneck and maximal-trail routes

The source is loopless and has no parallel arcs. Dominated means sharing an in-neighbor; dominating means sharing an out-neighbor. Degree is total in+out degree, permitting antiparallel arcs. Supereulerian means containing a spanning Eulerian subdigraph, equivalently a spanning closed arc-distinct trail; it is not Hamiltonicity and cannot be replaced by an arbitrary closed walk with repeated arcs.

Route1: construct a bottleneck that forces re-use of an arc. Take vertices u,v and two nonempty disjoint bidirected complete modules A,B of orders a,b. Put u->x and x->v for each module vertex x, and the unique return arc v->u; no other intermodule arcs. The digraph is strong. A spanning Eulerian subdigraph would need positive flow through both modules, so would use v->u at least twice, impossible for an arc subset. This is a genuine nonsupereulerian template. However x in A and y in B are both dominated by u and dominate v, with degrees2a and2b. Their sum is2(a+b)=2n−4, one BELOW the required2n−3. Saturating each module cannot repair the deficit. Adding u->v changes no such degrees. Adding a directed intermodule bridge permits one passage u through the first module, then the second, then v->u, destroying the intended bottleneck. Thus neither the sparse template nor its dense-module repair is a counterexample.

Route2: attempt to prove the result from a maximal closed trail S and a shortest bypass T=y0 x1 ... xt y(p+1), outside S internally. Maximality and minimal bypass length force x_i nonadjacent to internal replaced vertices y_j. The endpoint pairs {x1,y1} and {xt,yp} share a predecessor/successor, so the actual conjectural degree condition applies to them. Insertion lemmas bound how external vertices meet S. This is a concrete full-scope combinatorial route, not a Hamiltonian surrogate.
