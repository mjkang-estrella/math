## 329705: extension of a resolving set

Primary source: Estrada-Moreno–Ramirez-Cruz–Rodriguez-Velazquez, arXiv:1501.04647, Section 4.1, Theorem 38, Proposition 41 and Conjecture 42. The actual proofs of Theorem 38 and Proposition 41 were read. This is genuinely a conjecture, despite the neutral corpus wording. The source reports exhaustive checks only for order at most 11, minimum degree two, and k in {3,4}. Its Figure 6 is a nine-vertex tightness example, not a counterexample. No proposed witness is being extracted from that figure.

Let A be a k-adjacency basis of H. Put B_v = A minus N_H(v), a_v = |B_v|. Here N is the open neighborhood, so v belongs to B_v if v belongs to A. If u is the added universal vertex, the distinguishing set for (u,v) is {u} union (V(H) minus N_H(v)). Distinguishing sets for old pairs are unchanged by the join. Consequently a sufficient general extension lemma is:

There is T contained in V(H) minus A, with |T| at most k−1, such that |T minus N_H(v)| is at least max(0,k−1−a_v) for every v.

Then A union T union {u} resolves the join and has size at most |A|+k. This lemma is stronger than the conjecture: failure for a chosen optimal A need not refute the conjecture, because a join basis might replace elements of A or omit u.

Pairwise separation imposes a useful necessary condition a_v+a_w >= k for distinct v,w. Indeed the old distinguishing set is (N(v) symmetric-difference N(w)) union {v,w}. Restricted to A, its symmetric-difference contribution has size a_v+a_w−2|B_v intersection B_w|. If v,w are adjacent their endpoints are already in that symmetric difference; if nonadjacent, every endpoint in A belongs to the intersection and its additional count is absorbed by the subtracted twice-intersection term. Thus its cardinality is at most a_v+a_w.

This closes k=3 by ordinary reasoning. If some a_v=0, Proposition 41 applies verbatim: choose two vertices outside N(v) and outside A and add them together with u. Availability follows from k <= C(K1+H), since 1+|V(H) minus N(v)| >= k. If all a_v >=1, at most one vertex has a_v=1, by the pairwise bound. If none does, A union {u} suffices. If exactly one v does, choose t outside A and N(v), possible since |V(H) minus N(v)|>=2 while a_v=1. Then A union {u,t} suffices. This is only a routine parameter case, not the literal general endpoint and not eligible credit. It does not justify a Lean build for this campaign.

The general attempted route fails at simultaneously satisfying the deficits. Pairwise separation of B_v is not by itself a common hitting-set theorem. Independent greedy repairs can cost a sum of deficits much larger than k−1. Choosing a vertex with minimum a_v handles the a_v=0 case, but for a_v>0 the source's argument no longer forces every other deficit to vanish. A second attempted refutation uses many almost-universal vertices with pairwise disjoint missing subsets of A; making their remaining neighborhoods nearly complete can force expensive repairs, but creates close twins or violates C(K1+H)>=k. This invalidates that naive construction before any computation.

Next Pi task, if root prioritizes it: search only a deliberately designed incidence-code family, initially k=4 or 5 and order 12–16, beyond the source's reported range. A certificate must contain a labeled simple graph, integer k, all distinguishing masks, an explicit H basis A, and an exhaustive or RUP certificate that the join has no resolving set of size |A|+k. Proving A optimal is unnecessary for a refutation: adim(H)<=|A| and adim(join)>|A|+k already suffice. Prove the mask semantics, the join identity, threshold admissibility and the finite unsatisfiability checker in Lean. No search or formalization was run here. A failed extension of A is not a certificate. Full source example inventory and later-literature clearance must precede nomination.

## 361905: odd hull size

Primary 2402.11044v1 was read in full, including definitions, Theorem 1 and its proof, Lemma 4 and its proof, staircase/comet constructions, Observations 2/6, Conjectures 2/3, all six figure captions and the enumeration/code reference. Its constructions and catalogued 6- and 8-point order types are printed prior art, not available discovery credit.

The literal odd conjecture fails trivially at n=5. Choose five points on a strictly convex pentagon. Partition edges by their smaller endpoint under a labeling 1,...,5; the four nonempty parts are stars centered at 1,...,4. Every part is plane because all edges share its center. There are four = ceil(5/2)+1 parts and hull size five > four. This is precisely the standard n−1 star decomposition recalled by the source. It is an excluded source-own construction/small-order correction, not a substantive discovery. No finite witness or Lean implementation is proposed for credit.

For n=2k−1>=7, the attempted structural route starts from k+1 forests with total edge count (2k−1)(k−1). On all n vertices a forest with c components has n−c edges, so total component count is (k+1)(2k−1)−(2k−1)(k−1)=4k−2. This falls far short of forcing the even-case perfect matching plus broken double stars. Isolated vertices must count as components here. Deleting a vertex from an even staircase only gives already printed constructions; adding a new vertex to an odd decomposition has no established way to extend every forest while retaining planarity. Thus the even Observation 6 does not settle the remaining odd range. A hypothetical proof must either classify these odd decompositions or establish a direct hull charging inequality allowing the extra component slack. No such lemma was obtained. No larger witness was proposed, so source examples/figures are not being repackaged.

Next Pi computation, only after root rules the remaining-range question eligible: a single rational 7- or 9-point configuration with hull greater than ceil(n/2)+1, together with an edge partition into that many plane star forests. Certificate validation is elementary: exact orientation determinants for general position and every pair of disjoint edges, all edges covered exactly once, each component a star. A SAT search must encode shared-center intersections correctly. A certificate for n=5 is not useful. No computation occurred here, and there is no claim that the author's linked full code/data has been exhaustively compared.

## 329726: bow-tie extendability

Primary definition: vertices (i,j), 1<=i<=m, 1<=j<=n; Cartesian C_m times P_n edges, plus seam (i,1)--(m+2−i,n), with first coordinate modulo m. This is not an arbitrary bow-tie graph or the Cartesian torus. Exact target m even >=6, n odd >=5, every matching of three edges extends to a perfect matching. The source proves m=6 only. The journal's generalized sentence drops the n bounds; the reserved literal bounds remain controlling.

Attempt 1: use the Cartesian subgraph. Its bipartition by i+j has equal parts, but a seam has endpoints with the same parity (m and n−1 are even). Thus a prescribed matching using seams cannot simply be extended by a bipartite-product theorem. The parity imbalance from its removed endpoints must be repaired by additional seam edges. There are at most three prescribed seams, but their row positions constrain the repair and prevent a free parity argument.

Attempt 2: remove unused paired row strips and induct on m. Reflection i->2−i makes arbitrary deletion of an adjacent pair incompatible with the twist: a reflection-invariant adjacent pair would require 2i=1 modulo even m. Deleting two reflected pairs can respect the seam but changes the cylindrical adjacency; reconnecting the gap introduces edges absent from the original graph. The needed bridge is a matching-transfer lemma that expands every used reconnected edge into a compatible path through the deleted strip, disjoint from the three prescribed edges. This has not been proved. The source's m=6 separator casework does not supply it automatically.

Next Pi task: inspect all source/journal examples first, then exact m=8,n=5 as a diagnostic, not proof of the family. For a negative certificate output the three disjoint edges M and a Tutte set S of G−V(M) whose number of odd components is greater than |S|. This is a compact ordinary nonextension proof, and Lean can verify the explicit finite components and Tutte obstruction without accepting a matching solver. A positive enumeration at one parameter is not an endpoint. A general proof needs the reflected-strip transfer lemma above or new uniform separator casework. No matching search or Lean build was performed.
