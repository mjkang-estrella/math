## Exact mathematics and consequence: PASS

Here DOM means the maximum, over **every orientation**, of the minimum cardinality of a vertex subset T with |N⁺[T]| at least half the vertices. Neighborhoods are closed and united, not counted with multiplicity. This differs from signed majority functions and from full domination. Let positive r≤s, retain DOM≠1, and put h=ceil((r+s)/2).

For any orientation, let a and b be the largest outdegrees in the r- and s-parts. Edge counting gives rs≤ra+sb≤s(a+b), hence a+b≥r. If r<s and any arc goes from the smaller to the larger part, a>0 and the second inequality is strict, giving a+b≥r+1. For opposite-part maximizers u,v, the union of their closed out-neighborhoods has exactly a+b+1 vertices: their open out-neighborhoods lie in disjoint parts and the cross arc accounts for exactly one overlap with the two selected vertices.

If s≤r+2 this pair covers at least r+1≥h. Thus DOM≤2, and positivity and DOM≠1 give DOM=2. If s>r+2, put k=ceil((s-r)/2)≥2, so h=r+k. With a smaller-to-larger arc the pair covers at least r+2 vertices; adding at most k−2 uncovered vertices reaches h because each added vertex covers itself. There are enough uncovered vertices whenever the threshold has not been reached. If every arc goes from the larger to the smaller part, k vertices of the larger part cover r+k vertices (k≤s). Conversely in that orientation every T covers at most r+|T| vertices; reaching h forces |T|≥k. This proves both branches at all sizes with no source theorem assumed.

The Lean endpoint Majority.mathdb_326640 spells the ceiling as (s-r+1)/2, on Fin r ⊕ Fin s. The file includes orientation representation, arbitrary finite-subset coverage, exact minimum/maximum semantics, and the integer majority criterion; no additional unproved source hypothesis was found. This report relies on the separate strict replay rather than claiming a new one. Frozen Lean SHA256 751ac866bed61eb765969f5ea08e53c582f79431400786e418cfd1278e07d850; zip SHA256 5249402f7bf10a6e3176f66602a8438b2791fbfe5d080e6c3f992d73978a7ae6.

The consequence is a sharp two-parameter infinite-family minimax value, including arbitrary mixed orientations and arbitrary selected subsets. In particular the near-balanced nonsingleton regime is uniformly 2 regardless of order, whereas the unbalanced value grows with the part-size gap. The source's DOM=1 exception is essential; for example K₂,₄ is excluded. This is neither a singleton classification nor an extension of the star formula merely by changing a parameter name.

## Concrete prior-theorem comparisons

**The source itself.** Proposition 4.6 already uses an out-neighborhood followed by uncovered vertices for stars. Proposition 4.7 uses a two-vertex coverage seed followed by augmentation for double stars. Those proof patterns are old and deserve no new credit. Proposition 4.9's proof establishes the singleton exceptions by a special bounded-outdegree orientation; outside the near-balanced regime it uses the same one-way orientation as the candidate. It does not prove the upper bound for arbitrary orientations. Its near-balanced construction is an existential lower bound, not the opposite-pair degree lemma. The genuinely remaining step in the candidate is the uniformly large opposite-part pair, including the strict extra unit in the unequal-part case. I did not find that step stated or proved in the source.

Source Proposition 3.8 combined with the ordinary orientable-domination theorem DOMordinary(Kr,s)=s yields only DOMmajority(Kr,s)≤ceil(s/2). This is a concrete bridge, but not the target formula: r=10,s=14 gives 7 instead of 2. Source Theorem 3.10 gives an upper bound h−Δ⁺ when no singleton suffices. Simple edge averaging gives only Δ⁺≥ceil(rs/(r+s)); at (10,14) this yields 12−6=6, again not 2. These observations rule out these specific direct reductions, not every conceivable sharpening.

**Accessible survey chapter, actual body inspected.** [Haynes–Hedetniemi–Henning, Domination in Digraphs](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-58892-2_13), pp.387–428, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-58892-2_13, was lawfully returned by the publisher as a complete PDF (chapter-pdf.body). I read Section 3.1's relevant degree bounds and Section 5.1, pp.408–413, including every theorem there and the supplied proofs, and screened the chapter's text and references for majority, partial and bipartite coverage. This is not a claim to have read every proof of the whole book. Although some index metadata calls it 2020, the publisher PDF/book records ©2021 and May 2021 publication; the primary date takes precedence.

Theorem 33's upper bound is γ(D)≤n−Δ⁺, proved by taking V minus the open out-neighborhood of a maximum-degree vertex. Theorem 61(a) gives DOMordinary(G)≤n−ν(G); its proof partitions into matching edges and isolated leftover vertices and unions dominating sets, using Lemma 60. Theorem 63 then proves DOMordinary(G)=α(G) for bipartite G by matching duality. These are full-domination statements. Their proofs do not furnish the extremal pair lemma. Selecting tails of disjoint matching edges for partial coverage gives only approximately h/2 vertices while h≤2r, and h−r beyond that range; at (10,14) the former gives 6 instead of 2. This matching argument therefore does not subsume the result either.

The chapter also quotes 1996 results as Theorems 51–52 (lower orientable domination equals ordinary domination, and intermediate values), and records their special-family determinations. No majority-set or general partial-coverage theorem resolving the target was found in the inspected chapter. Theorems 54–58 cite Caro–Henning's greedy partition method, but the original 2011/2012 papers and their full proofs were not obtained here; stronger intermediate lemmas there remain an explicit limit.

**Later multipartite work, actual body inspected.** [Anderson–Brešar–Klavžar–Kuenzel–Rall, Orientable domination in product-like graphs](https://users.fmf.uni-lj.si/klavzar/preprints/2211.02395.pdf), author-hosted manuscript with arXiv v1 header (November 2022), Theorem 2.3 and Section 5.1, Proposition 5.3–Theorem 5.5 with proofs, were read. In the first branch a dominating part may be large; this argument supplies no two-vertex partial-coverage seed. For two parts it supplies only DOMordinary(Kr,s)=s and the weak bridge above.
