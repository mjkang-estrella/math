# Eight-vertex refutation of MathDB 328367 (zero campaign credit)

Vertices 0,1,2,3 induce K4. Vertices 4,5 are adjacent precisely to 0,1; vertices 6,7 precisely to 2,3. Let H be the induced graph on {0,1,4,5}, and let K be the induced complement on {2,3,6,7}. Both are diamonds, are of minimum degree 2, and G is connected.

A diamond's only vertex cover of cardinality at most 2 is its two central vertices. Attacking an edge to a peripheral vertex forces that vertex to be occupied in the response. There is then no vertex cover of cardinality at most 2. Three guards suffice (all three-element covers form a closed defending family). Thus each diamond has eternal covering number 3.

Five guards defend G. Keep guards on all four central vertices and one peripheral vertex s. If both attacked endpoints have guards, swap them. Otherwise a central vertex u moves to the attacked unoccupied peripheral vertex v. If s and v belong to the same diamond, move s to u, keeping the other guards fixed. If they belong to opposite diamonds, choose a central neighbor w of s; move s to w, w to u, and u to v. In both cases all four central vertices remain occupied and v is the new spare peripheral vertex. Every move is along one edge; simultaneous arrivals cause no collisions. This is an invariant under arbitrarily long, adaptively chosen attacks. Therefore τ(G)≤5<6=τ(H)+τ(K).
