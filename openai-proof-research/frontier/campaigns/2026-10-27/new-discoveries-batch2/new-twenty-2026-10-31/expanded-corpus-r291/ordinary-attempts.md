# Ordinary attempts; zero credit

These are incomplete attempts at the three full literal endpoints, not proposed helper projects. No computation, enumeration, solver, numerical experiment or Lean execution was performed.

## 360815: all cyclic groups and all d dividing N

Definitions 1–2 and Conjecture 4 in the January 23, 2026 EJC article are the assigned scope: repeated generators are allowed, all nonempty subset sums must lie in A, and the sum is taken modulo N. The source establishes special cases; these are not additional discoveries. The earlier v1 and v2 statements have the same scope. The printed residue-class extremizer was inspected. The progression auxiliary is Conjecture 10 in the journal and Conjecture 3 in the preprints.

Attempt one extends the source repeated-generator mechanism. If x,2x,...,(d-1)x all lie in A, and |A|>(d-1)N/d, the d translates A-jx, 0≤j≤d-1, have nonempty intersection: the union of their complements has size at most d(N-|A|)<N. Any y in that intersection gives the d generators consisting of d-1 copies of x and one y. Sums without y are the listed multiples and sums with y are the translated membership tests. This is a complete conditional deduction, already in the source. The missing alternative was to force such a progression at the claimed density.

That alternative is publicly false. The primary public gist supplies B={0,4,12,20,24,28,36} in Z_40, A=Z_40 minus B, and d=5. Here is an independent hand check of the progression obstruction. Write B=4C with C={0,1,3,5,6,7,9} mod 10. If x is odd, 4x is in B; if x=2u with u odd, 2x is in B. If x=4u and u mod 10 is not in C, its residue is 2,4 or 8: respectively 3u,4u or 2u is 6 modulo 10. Thus one of x,2x,3x,4x is in B in every case. A has 33 elements, exceeding 32. This is a known auxiliary refutation, not a refutation of 360815. In fact (1,1,1,5,8) generates a 5-cube in A: its subset sums lie in [0,3] union [5,8] union [8,11] union [13,16], excluding zero for a nonempty subset. The resulting positive sums avoid B. This explains exactly why repurposing the public witness fails.

Attempt two builds a general cube inductively instead of requiring a progression. Given k generators whose nonempty sums are in A, put T={0} union their sums. A new generator y must lie in the intersection of A-t over t in T. The direct covering estimate only ensures this when |T|(N-|A|)<N. Since |T| can be 2^k, this produces the weaker density threshold 1-2^{-(d-1)}, not 1-1/d. It is not legitimate to replace 2^k by k+1 unless the generators already have exceptional additive structure. This is the precise failed shortcut.

Attempt three uses the journal prime-power layer proof and CRT. On a product of cyclic prime-power factors, a projected cube need not lift to a cube in A: every subset-sum coordinate must use the same lifted generators. High average fibre density does not imply that the needed fibres contain all compatible lifts. A complete route needs either a cube-compatible fibre selection lemma at complement density <1/d, or an alternative small-subset-sum structural dichotomy handling the public progression obstruction. Neither lemma is proved here. The journal specific-case proof bodies were read, but no general mixed-prime argument survives. HOLD. No full dormant Pi plan is justified.

## 359776: 2-edge-connected pairwise graph intersections

The entire five-page preprint was read, including its connected-intersection proof, projection proofs, Conjecture 5 and its other open questions. The published Discrete Mathematics article was identified, but its body returned 403 and was not retried. Thus source qualification remains incomplete independently of the mathematical gap.

Attempt one adapts the source vertex-deletion proof. For Hamiltonian, or 2-vertex-connected, intersections, every deletion remains connected. Applying the connected-intersection bound on each (n-1)-vertex projection and the uniform cover inequality yields exactly the exponent binom(n,2)-n. For bridgeless connected intersections that step is false. Two triangles meeting at one common vertex give a connected bridgeless graph; deleting that common vertex disconnects it into two edges. Taking the family consisting of this graph already disproves the needed projection premise. Therefore Theorem 4 is not a routine resolution of Conjecture 5.

Attempt two deletes edges instead. Put M=binom(n,2), n≥3. Every single-edge deletion of a bridgeless connected intersection is connected. On each M-1-coordinate projection, the connected-intersection bound is at most 2^(M-n), by the same cut-space argument on the connected host K_n-e. Every edge coordinate occurs in M-1 of these projections. Uniform covering then gives log_2 |F|≤M(M-n)/(M-1)=M-M(n-1)/(M-1). To reach M-n one needs M(n-1)/(M-1)≥n, equivalent to n≥M. This fails for n≥4 except the boundary n=3. Thus edge deletion spends almost all of the extra bridgeless information without delivering the missing factor two. A sharper joint entropy deficit, not independent projection bounds, is needed.

Attempt three extends the cut-space packing proof. Cuts span dimension n-1; every nonzero cut forces an empty crossing in the intersection of two graphs differing by that cut. A dimension n packing would give the desired factor. Adding a single edge as a new generator fails immediately, since its complement K_n-e is itself bridgeless connected for n≥4. Adding the all-edge vector fails for balanced cuts: the complement of the all-edge vector plus a cut is a complete bipartite graph with both parts of size at least two, hence bridgeless and connected. The required extra generator must make every vector in its cut coset have a complement that is disconnected or has a bridge. No such construction or replacement nonlinear packing is proved. This is a specific full-bound obstruction, not an instruction to search for a solver witness. Degenerate n=1 wording is not a significant refutation. HOLD.

## 375521: arbitrary induced subsets of Z^d of cardinality n^d

Both arXiv versions were inspected at the literal conjecture and product scope. In v1 the full statement is Conjecture 9.1, d≥2. In v2 it is Conjecture 6.1, d≥3; the planar case is attributed to a later public proof with a Palomar formalization. The product Theorem 3.2, its proof and perfect-power Corollary 3.3 in v2 were read. None asserts arbitrary-shape control. The author planar page was denied locally; managed search exposed its theorem and outline, not a locally preserved full body. No retry was made. This is not full literature qualification.

Attempt one uses boxes as possible finite counterexamples. Every d-dimensional product of paths with volume n^d is already covered by the source corollary: the logarithmic side lengths majorize their constant mean log n, and the source heat-trace comparison gives the equal-sided product as the unique optimum. Thus unbalanced product boxes cannot be new counterexamples. Nor does the paper torus counterexample help: quotient adjacency creates wraparound edges absent from nearest-neighbor induced subsets of Z^d. No semantic embedding preserving those edges has been supplied.

Attempt two bounds arbitrary shapes by their edge count. If V=|S| and P_i is the cardinality of the coordinate projection deleting coordinate i, the number of edges in direction i is at most V-P_i: each nonempty line contributes at most its size minus one. Loomis–Whitney and arithmetic–geometric mean give sum P_i≥d V^((d-1)/d), so E≤dV-dV^((d-1)/d), with the cube attaining the bound at V=n^d. But the matrix-tree eigenvalue arithmetic–geometric mean bound is only tau≤(1/V)(2E/(V-1))^(V-1). It is strictly slack on the cube, whose positive Laplacian eigenvalues are not all equal. Hence maximum edges does not compare the actual two spanning-tree counts. An isoperimetric proof alone stops at precisely this gap.

Attempt three tries a determinant-monotone coordinate compression or a hull-removal inequality. Translating fibres to align intervals preserves cardinality and may add some adjacency, but it can remove other edges; graph inclusion monotonicity is unavailable. One needs a proved comparison of Laplacian cofactors under the actual fibre move, for every surrounding graph, including equality. Alternatively, a d-dimensional normalized hull penalty must match an exact rectangular area penalty. The planar outline uses duality and vacancy control; no higher-dimensional replacement is given. Neither compression determinant inequality nor matching hull penalty has been proved. The equality characterization is also outstanding. No non-box finite witness was found by ordinary reasoning. HOLD, no Pi execution.
