Let V={a,b,c,A,B,C} and E={ab,bc,ca,aA,bB,cC}. This is the triangle with one pendant leaf on each vertex. It is a finite connected simple graph with six vertices and nonempty edge set. Its only cycle is abc: a degree-one vertex cannot be in a cycle, and the subgraph on a,b,c is exactly a triangle. Thus there are no two vertex-disjoint odd cycles.

Normality bridge (fully paid, no analogy between rings): Dupont–Rentería-Márquez–Villarreal, arXiv:0803.1208v2 (29 April 2010), Theorem 3.2 gives the integer rounding property for the connected graph incidence system x>=0,xA<=1 iff every pair of vertex-disjoint odd cycles has connected induced union. Here the universal pair condition is vacuous. Theorem 3.3(d) says precisely that K[t,x_1t,...,x_nt,x^{v_1}t,...,x^{v_q}t] is normal. Its generators agree term by term with the S above: the six singleton generators, six listed squarefree edge generators, and t. This is normality of the required ring over the field of the sources, not normality of an unrelated edge/cover ideal.

Unmixedness bridge: In every maximal independent set F, exactly one of a,A is present. At most one is possible because aA is an edge. If neither were present, A could be added since its only neighbor is a, contradicting maximality. The same argument applies to b,B and c,C. Consequently every maximal independent set has exactly three vertices; at least one exists, e.g. {A,B,C}. Complements give a bijection between maximal independent sets and inclusion-minimal vertex covers: a complement is a cover exactly when its complementary set is independent, and minimality is exactly maximality. Therefore every minimal vertex cover has size three and tau(G)=3. This proves unmixedness in the source's exact sense.

Reduction bridge: the induced subgraphs on {a,A},{b,B},{c,C} are edges; their vertex sets partition V, and tau(G)=3=1+1+1 is the sum of their cover numbers. They are a tau-reduction. Definition 3.16 calls it a ceil(n/2)-tau-reduction when all but the last part are edges and the last is an edge or C3,C5,C7. Here all are edges and ceil(6/2)=3. Its definition of strong is a disjunction: it holds automatically if the last part is an edge. There is no unfulfilled odd-cycle-neighborhood condition in this branch. Even if property (P) is requested for the matching edges, it holds: for aA the only neighbor of A is a, so for any edges az and Az' one has z'=a and zz'=za is the already given edge az. The same proof applies to the other two pendant edges.

Failure of Gorensteinness bridge: Theorem 3.14 of the 2021 source assumes normality and even n and concludes S is Gorenstein iff G is unmixed bipartite. We proved normality and n=6 is even. G contains abc and cannot be bipartite: assigning opposite colors along ab and bc gives a,c the same color, contradicting ca. Thus S is not Gorenstein. All hypotheses of the literal right side hold and the left side fails. This refutes its biconditional.

## 352004: rank-two symmetry; two failed mechanisms and exact unpaid endpoint

Source: Jiayuan Wang, arXiv:2109.09773v3, 19 October 2021, section 4.2 and Conjecture 4.8. Current guarded arXiv metadata confirms v1,v2,v3 with v3 the latest displayed version. The presentation of a[b]a is <x,y | x^a=y^a=1, alternating(x,y,b)=alternating(y,x,b)>. Length counts positive generator letters; powers are repeated letters. Full commutativity alone is insufficient in the b=2 comparison: the record explicitly restricts to elements with a unique reduced expression. Swapping parameters is not simply relabeling the two generators: it changes both the orders and braid length. Finite exceptional tables do not prove every positive a,b.

First route: count words avoiding x^a,y^a and both alternating length-b subwords, then transpose run lengths. Avoidance is necessary for a unique reduced word: powers shorten, and a braid gives a different same-length word. It is not sufficient in this non-Coxeter presentation. For a=b=3, inverse braid gives x^2 y^2 x^2 = y^2 x^2 y^2. Both words avoid length-three constant runs and alternating triples. If the common element has length six these are two distinct reduced words; if it has shorter length both are nonreduced. Either way these locally admissible words cannot be accepted as unique reduced words. This is an ordinary relation derivation, not enumeration. Thus the local-language shortcut cannot support an all-parameter bijection.

Second route: use run-length paths or a dual complex polytope to exchange a and b. An actual endpoint must preserve geodesic length AND uniqueness under every relation consequence, not just map a presentation diagram. No such map has been proved here. The exact unpaid lemma is a characterization of the full language U(a,b) of positive words which are the sole minimum-length representatives, together with a length-preserving bijection U(a,b)->U(b,a). A local forbidden-subword list without completeness is inadequate. A group isomorphism alone would also be inadequate unless it preserved the chosen word metric and unique geodesics.

Boundary checks only: a=b is tautological. If b=2 and a=m>=2 the group is C_m x C_m; exactly pure powers x^j,y^j (1<=j<m), plus the identity, have unique reduced expressions. Mixed powers commute and have multiple reduced expressions. In 2[m]2 the usual dihedral presentation has two unique alternating words at lengths 1 through m-1 and none at/above m. These boundary families are already discussed immediately after source Conjecture 4.8, and do not solve the general question. The source also reports exceptional pairs (3,4)/(4,3) and (5,3)/(3,5). No finite table was recomputed and no completeness claim made. General symmetry is HOLD, with final 2023 journal counterpart not body-compared; no candidate or certificate dispatch.
