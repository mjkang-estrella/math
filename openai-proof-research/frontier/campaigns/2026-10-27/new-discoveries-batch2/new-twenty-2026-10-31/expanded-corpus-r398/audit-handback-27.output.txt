   To establish ϕ1 , we construct a sequence π1 , . . . , πn = ϕ1 (σ) of forests such that πj is deter-
mined by the subword σ[j] of σ. For each πj , the labelled leaves and the internal nodes with
two empty leaves are called terminal nodes, and the internal nodes with exactly one empty leaf
are called intermediate nodes. For example, let π be the forest shown on the right of Figure 6.
The terminal (intermediate, respectively) nodes of π are these nodes with labels {4, 5} ({3, 6},
respectively). The construction of π1 , . . . , πn is described below.
Algorithm A
   The initial forest π1 is the tree consisting of the root, labelled by integer 1, and one empty
leaf, where the root is white (black, respectively) if σ[1] = 1 (−1, respectively). Suppose πj−1
has been determined (j ≥ 2), we locate the entry x of the subword σ[j] with |x| = j, and then
find out how σ[j] is obtained from σ[j−1] :
      (i) x is inserted at the right of σ[j−1] . Then πj is obtained from πj−1 by adding a new
          component consisting of the root, labelled with j, and an empty leaf, where the root
          is white (black, respectively) if x = j (−j, respectively).

         (ii) x is inserted at the immediate left of a double-ascent element, say y, of σ[j−1]. Find
              the intermediate node v in πj−1 with ℓ(v) = |y|. Then πj is obtained from πj−1 by
              labelling the empty leaf adjacent to v by integer j. Moreover, the node labelled j is
              assigned two empty leaves if x > 0.
        (iii) x is inserted at the immediate right of a peak, say y, of σ[j−1] . Find the terminal node
              v in πj−1 with ℓ(v) = |y|. If y > 0 then v has two empty leaves, and πj is obtained from
              πj−1 by labelling the right child of v by integer j. If y < 0 then v is a labelled leaf,
              and πj is obtained from πj−1 by adding a node labelled j (empty leaf, respectively) as
              the left (right, respectively) child of v. Moreover, the node labelled j is assigned two
              empty leaves if x > 0.
   Notice that the peaks (double-ascent elements, respectively) of σ[j] are in one-to-one correspon-
   dence with the terminal (intermediate, respectively) nodes of πj .
