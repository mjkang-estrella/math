## Independent mathematical challenge

Use common option sets: Z={}, S={Z}, T={S}, U={Z,S}, B={U}, V={S,T}, C={V}. Under the full four-case source adjoint: Z-adjoint=S, S-adjoint=T, U-adjoint=V and B-adjoint=C. Nonterminal impartial games are P exactly when every option is N; terminal Z is N.

Here is a direct backward proof, independent of Lean simplification. S is P. T and U are N because they have S as an option. B is P. V is N because it can move to S, so C is P. S+S is N because it moves to S. S+T has options T and S+S, both N, hence is P. T+U is N since U can move to S, leaving T+S (P). S+B is N since S can move to Z, leaving B (P). T+B has only the option types S+B and T+U, both N, hence is P. Therefore B+S-adjoint=B+T is P, Z+B-adjoint=C is P, and Z+S-adjoint=T is N. These prove the two arrows and the failed composite.

It obtains Left-second and Right-second wins for the first two sums and losses for the third; it reproduces every outcome in the author's table.
