                                                             THE SPLITTING SUBSPACE CONJECTURE

                                                                           ERIC CHEN AND DENNIS TSENG

                                                 Abstract. We answer a question by Niederreiter concerning the enumeration of a class of
                                                 subspaces of finite dimensional vector spaces over finite fields by proving a conjecture by
                                                 Ghorpade and Ram.

                                                                                   1. Introduction
                                            We positively resolve the Splitting Subspace Conjecture, stemming from a question posed
                                          by Niederreiter (1995) [4, p. 11] and stated by Ghorpade and Ram [3]. The conjecture was
                                          inspired by earlier work from Zeng, Han and He [5] and Ghorpade, Hasan and Kumari [2] .
                                          We first define the notion of a σ-splitting subspace.
                                          Definition. In the vector space Fqmn over the finite field Fq , given a σ ∈ Fqmn such that
                                          Fqmn = Fq (σ), a (m-dimensional) subspace W of Fqmn is a σ-splitting subspace if
                                                                           W ⊕ σW ⊕ · · · ⊕ σ n−1 W = Fqmn .
                                                          
                                             For example, 1, σ m , σ 2m , . . . , σ (n−1)m spans a σ-splitting subspace. If n = 1, then Fqmn
                                          is the only σ-splitting subspace; if m = 1, then each 1-dimensional subspace of Fqmn is
                                          σ-splitting.
                                          Conjecture 1 (Ghorpade-Ram). The number of σ-splitting subspaces is
                                                                                  q mn − 1 m(m−1)(n−1)
                                                                                          q            .
                                                                                  qm − 1
                                          This follows as Corollary 3.4 from our main result, Theorem 3.3. The next two sections are
                                          devoted to proving this theorem. We first construct a recursion that gives the cardinality of
                                          more general classes of subspaces, including the σ-splitting subspaces, and then solve this
                                          recurrence to obtain the result. Finally, we discuss some special cases of our more general
                                          result.

Proposition 4.1. There is a bijection between sets of the form (k1 , k1 − 1) and (k2 , k2 − 1)
when k1 , k2 ≤ N − 1.
Proof. It suffices to show that there exists a bijection between (k, k − 1) and (k − 1, k − 2)
for 2 ≤ k ≤ N − 1. Define
                                               φ : (k − 1, k − 2) → (k, k − 1)
                                                              W 7→ W + σW.

     The map φ is well defined:
                                                       dim(W + σW ) = k,
                              dim((W + σW ) ∩ (σ −1 W + W )) = k − 1.
The second equality follows from the fact that (W + σW ) ∩ (σ −1 W + W ) contains W and
has dimension strictly less than k by Proposition 2.1.
  Next, φ is injective: if W1 , W2 ∈ (k−1, k−2) and W1 +σW1 = W2 +σW2 = W 0 ∈ (k, k−1),
then W 0 ∩ σ −1 W 0 = W1 = W2 .
  Finally, φ is surjective: if W 0 ∈ (k, k − 1), then W 0 ∩ σ −1 W 0 ∈ (k − 1, k − 2) since
(W 0 ∩ σ −1 W 0 ) + σ(W 0 ∩ σ −1 W 0 ) ⊆ W 0 ; in fact (W 0 ∩ σ −1 W 0 ) + σ(W 0 ∩ σ −1 W 0 ) = W 0 . 
