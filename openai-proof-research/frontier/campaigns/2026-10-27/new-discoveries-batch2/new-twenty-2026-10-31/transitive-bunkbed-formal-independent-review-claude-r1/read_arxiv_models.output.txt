We will first consider hypergraphs, and now define the models we will consider. Model 4.1 ($E_{4}^{T}$; conditioned hypergraphs) . For a hypergraph $\mathcal{H}$ and subset $T\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})$, let $E_{4}^{T}(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})$ be the set of those $F\subseteq E(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})$ containing precisely one of $e^{(0)}$ and $e^{(1)}$ for each $e\in E(\mathcal{H})$, and precisely those vertical edges $v^{(0)}v^{(1)}$ for which $v\in T$. It was conjectured by Linusson [ 9 ] that a version of the bunkbed conjecture should also hold on hypergraphs. In our notation, the conjecture is as follows. Conjecture 4.2 ( [ 9 ] Conjecture 2.7) . For any hypergraph $\mathcal{H}$ and set $T\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})$ of posts, $\text{BBC}(E_{4}^{T},\mathcal{H})$ holds. That is, for any vertices $u,v\in V(\mathcal{H})$, we have $\Prob(u^{(0)}\xleftrightarrow{E_{4}^{T}(\mathcal{H})}v^{(0)})\geq\Prob(u^{(0)}\xleftrightarrow{E_{4}^{T}(\mathcal{H})}v^{(1)}).$ Note that Theorem 1.6 disproves this conjecture. Similarly to in the site percolation case, we will use our results concerning Model 4.1 to prove a result about an unconditioned model. Model 4.3 ($E_{5}$; unconditioned hypergraphs) . For a hypergraph $\mathcal{H}$, let $F\subseteq E(\tilde{\mathcal{H}})$ be chosen uniformly at random amongst all such subsets. Keep precisely these edges, and discard the others. Finally, we consider digraphs, and, following the same pattern as before, have two models. Model 4.4 ($E_{6}^{T,F}$; conditioned digraphs) . For a digraph $D$, subset $T\subseteq V(D)$ of vertices, and subset $F\subseteq E(D)$ of (directed) edges, let $E_{6}^{T,F}(\tilde{D})$ be the family of those subsets of $E(\tilde{D})$ containing both $e^{(0)}$ and $e^{(1)}$ for all $e\in F$, exactly one of $e^{(0)}$ and $e^{(1)}$ for all $e\in E(D)\setminus F$, and precisely those vertical edges $v^{(0)}v^{(1)}$ for which $v\in T$. It was conjectured by Leander [ 8 ] that the bunkbed conjecture holds for acyclic directed graphs (in the same sense as discussed here), and it was furthermore stated that this should broaden to hold for any directed graph. The latter is disproved by Theorem 1.6 , but the example we construct has many cycles, and so it remains open whether the conjecture holds for acyclic directed graphs. We can now remove the conditioning from this model, but at the cost of working with multigraphs. Model 4.5 ($E_{7}$; unconditioned digraphs) . For a directed multigraph $D$, let $F\subseteq E(\tilde{D})$ be chosen uniformly at random amongst all such subsets. Keep precisely these edges, and discard the others. As stated in Theorem 1.6 , all of $\text{BBC}(E_{4}^{T},\mathcal{H})$, $\text{BBC}(E_{5},\mathcal{H})$, $\text{BBC}(E_{6}^{T,F},D)$, and $\text{BBC}(E_{7},D)$ are false for suitable hypergraphs $\mathcal{H}$, digraphs $D$, subsets of vertices $T$ and subsets of edges $F$. Conjecture 6.4 . $\text{BBC}(E_{7},T_{n})$ holds for $T_{n}$. the transitive tournament on $n$ vertices. We found some positive results in Section 3.3 concerning the case of site percolation for some very simple classes of graphs. It would be interesting to consider more complex graphs, with a view towards ascertaining whether or not the bunkbed conjecture failing for site percolation is in general the exception or the rule. Indeed, one may ask the following (perhaps optimistic) question. Question 6.5 . What is the probability that the assertion in the bunkbed conjecture for site percolation $\text{BBC}(E_{1},G)$ holds for $G\sim G(n,p)$ the Erdős-Rényi random graph for $n$ large? To what extent does this probability depend on the behaviour of $p$ as a function of $n$? There are of course many other classes of graph one could consider; graphs of diameter $2$ (or simply of radius 1), and highly symmetric graphs (e.g. those with transitive automorphism group) would seem to be natural candidates for investigation.
