Definition 1.1 (Bunkbed Graph). For Q a graph, digraph, or hypergraph, we can define the bunkbed
structure Q̃ as follows. Take two copies, Q0 and Q1 , of Q , labelling the copy of vertex v and edge e
in Qi as v (i) and e(i) respectively. We will call such edges e(i) horizontal edges. E(Q̃ ) then consists of
E(Q0 ) ∪ E(Q1 ) along with an edge between v (0) and v (1) for each v ∈ V (Q ), which will be called a
vertical edge. In the case of directed graphs, this vertical edge will be bidirectional. Q0 will be called
the lower bunk and Q1 the upper bunk of Q̃ .

   We will be concerned in this paper with percolation on the bunkbed graph under various uniform
models of percolation. We will consider two types of model: site percolation and bond percolation.
In both cases, we will ultimately take a family E (Q̃ ) of subsets of E(Q̃ ), which we will sample from
uniformly at random to produce some set X ⊆ E(Q̃ ) of edges which will be retained. We now define
these concepts formally.

Definition 1.2 (Percolation Model). If Q is a graph, digraph, or hypergraph, then a percolation model
is a family E (Q̃ ) is a family of subsets of E(Q̃ ). We perform percolation by selecting an element
X ∈ E (Q̃ ) uniformly at random, and then retain precisely those edges in X . For two vertices
x, y ∈ V (Q̃ ), we write
            E (Q̃ )                    E (Q̃ )
       P(x −−→ y)         or     P(x ←−→ y)
for the probability that there is a directed path of retained edges from x to y in the former, or a
bidirectional path in the latter case, in Q̃ under model E (Q̃ ). When Q is a hypergraph, a path in Q
is a sequence of vertices in which consecutive vertices are both members of some hyperedge in Q .

  We can then use this definition to define site percolation as a special case of bond percolation.
We will only consider site percolation on graphs, so for simplicity we only define this case.

Definition 1.3 (Site Percolation Model). If G is a graph, then a percolation model E (G̃) is said to be a
site percolation model if, for every X ∈ E (G̃) there is some set U ⊆ V (G̃), which we shall refer to as
the set of open vertices, such that X consists of precisely those edges with both ends in U.

   We consider site percolation as a special case of our general (bond) percolation model for
convenience; it will be useful for us to still have all vertices present, even if they are in no edges.
   We remark that in much other work on the topic, percolation is phrased in terms of sampling
edges independently at random with some probability p. Our sampling as above is equivalent to
this in the case that E (Q̃ ) is the powerset P (E(Q̃ )), and p = 1/2. This restriction to p = 1/2 turns
out to not be important, as will be discussed later.
   We now provide a very general statement of the bunkbed conjecture in terms of a general model,
before we proceed to consider several special cases.

Definition 1.4 (Bunkbed Conjecture for a General Model E ). Let Q be a graph, hypergraph, or digraph,
E (Q̃ ) be a model of percolation on Q̃ as defined above. The bunkbed conjecture for model E states
that for any Q , and any vertices u, v ∈ V (Q ), we have
                E (Q̃ )            E (Q̃ )
       P(u(0) −−→ v (0) ) ≥ P(u(0) −−→ v (1) ).
Let BBC(E (Q )) denote the above statement.

they are used somewhat interchangeably in the literature; Rudzinski and Smyth [3] detail a few
of these statements. One such form of the conjecture, which fits the notation we have described
above, concerns the following model of percolation.

   It was conjectured by Leander [16] that the bunkbed conjecture holds for acyclic directed graphs
(in the same sense as discussed here), and it was furthermore stated that this should broaden to
hold for any directed graph. The latter is disproved by Theorem 1.6, but the example we construct
has many cycles, and so it remains open whether the conjecture holds for acyclic directed graphs.
   We can now remove the conditioning from this model, but at the cost of working with
multigraphs.

Model 4.5 (D7 (D); Unconditioned Digraphs). For a directed multigraph D, let F ⊆ E(D̃) be chosen
uniformly at random amongst all such subsets. Keep precisely these edges, and discard the others.

    As stated in Theorem 1.6, all of BBC(H4 (H , T )), BBC(H5 (H)), BBC(D6 (D, T , F )), and BBC(D7 (D)) are
false for suitable hypergraphs H, digraphs D, subsets of vertices T and subsets of edges F . In the next
section, we give constructions of such graphs.
