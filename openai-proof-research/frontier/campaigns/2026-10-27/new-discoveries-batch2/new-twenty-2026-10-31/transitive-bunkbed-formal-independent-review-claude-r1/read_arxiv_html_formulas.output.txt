Definition 1.1 (Bunkbed graph) . For $Q$ a graph, digraph, or hypergraph, we can define the bunkbed structure $\tilde{Q}$ as follows. Take two copies, $Q_{0}$ and $Q_{1}$, of $Q$, labelling the copy of vertex $v$ and edge $e$ in $Q_{i}$ as $v^{(i)}$ and $e^{(i)}$ respectively. We will call such edges $e^{(i)}$ horizontal edges . $E(\tilde{Q})$ then consists of $E(Q_{0})\mathbin{\cup}E(Q_{1})$ along with an edge between $v^{(0)}$ and $v^{(1)}$ for each $v\in V(Q)$, which will be called a vertical edge . In the case of directed graphs, this vertical edge will be bidirectional. $Q_{0}$ will be called the lower bunk and $Q_{1}$ the upper bunk of $\tilde{Q}$. We will be concerned in this paper with percolation on the bunkbed graph under various uniform models of percolation. We will consider two types of model: site percolation and bond percolation. In both cases, we will ultimately take a family $\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})$ of subsets of $E(\tilde{Q})$, which we will sample from uniformly at random to produce some set $X\subseteq E(\tilde{Q})$ of edges which will be retained. We now define these concepts formally. Definition 1.2 (Percolation model) . If $Q$ is a graph, digraph, or hypergraph, then a percolation model is a family $\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})$ is a family of subsets of $E(\tilde{Q})$. We perform percolation by selecting an element $X\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})$ uniformly at random, and then retain precisely those edges in $X$. For two vertices $x,y\in V(\tilde{Q})$, we write $\displaystyle\Prob(x\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})}y)\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\Prob(x\xleftrightarrow{\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})}y)$ for the probability that there is a directed path of retained edges from $x$ to $y$ in the former, or a bidirectional path in the latter case, in $\tilde{Q}$ under model $\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})$. When $Q$ is a hypergraph, a path in $Q$ is a sequence of vertices in which consecutive vertices are both members of some hyperedge in $Q$. We can then use this definition to define site percolation as a special case of bond percolation. We will only consider site percolation on graphs, so for simplicity we only define this case. Definition 1.3 (Site percolation model) . If $G$ is a graph, then a percolation model $\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G})$ is said to be a site percolation model if, for every $X\in\mathcal{E}(\tilde{G})$ there is some set $U\subseteq V(\tilde{G})$, which we shall refer to as the set of open vertices , such that $X$ consists of precisely those edges with both ends in $U$. We consider site percolation as a special case of our general (bond) percolation model for convenience; it will be useful for us to still have all vertices present, even if they are in no edges. We remark that in much other work on the topic, percolation is phrased in terms of sampling edges independently at random with some probability $p$. Our sampling as above is equivalent to this in the case that $\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})$ is the powerset $\mathcal{P}(E(\tilde{Q}))$, and $p=1/2$. This restriction to $p=1/2$ turns out to not be important, as will be discussed later. We now provide a very general statement of the bunkbed conjecture in terms of a general model, before we proceed to consider several special cases. Definition 1.4 (Bunkbed conjecture for a general model $\mathcal{E}$) . Let $Q$ be a graph, hypergraph, or digraph, $\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})$ be a model of percolation on $\tilde{Q}$ as defined above. The bunkbed conjecture for model $\mathcal{E}$ states that for any $Q$, and any vertices $u,v\in V(Q)$, we have $\Prob(u^{(0)}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})}v^{(0)})\geq\Prob(u^{(0)}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{E}(\tilde{Q})}v^{(1)}).$ Let $\text{BBC}(\mathcal{E},Q)$ denote the above statement.
