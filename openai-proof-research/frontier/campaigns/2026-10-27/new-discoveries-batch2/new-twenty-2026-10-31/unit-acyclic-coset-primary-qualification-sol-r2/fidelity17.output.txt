                                                                            1. Introduction
                                             Let B be a finite subset of the abelian group G which does not contain the
                                           neutral element. For any subset A in G with the same cardinality as B, a
                                           matching from A to B is defined to be a bijection f : A → B such that for
                                           any a ∈ A we have a + f (a) 6∈ A. For any matching f as above, the associated
                                           multiplicity function mf : G → Z≥0 is defined via the rule:
                                                            ∀x ∈ G,      mf (x) = #{a ∈ A : a + f (a) = x}.                     (1)
                                           A matching f : A → B is called acyclic if for any matching g : A → B, mf = mg
                                           implies f = g. The notion of matchings in abelian groups was introduced by
                                           Fan and Losonczy in [5] in order to generalize a geometric property of lattices in
                                           Euclidean space. The motivation to study acyclic matchings is their relations
                                           with an old problem of Wakeford concerning canonical forms for symmetric
                                           tensors [7]. This notion has been investigated in literature in different aspects
                                           (e.g., the existence of acyclic matchings in subsets of abelian torsion-free groups
                                           and acyclic groups of prime order.) In this paper, we will investigate acyclic
                                           matchings in certain subsets of abelian groups. We will introduce the concept
                                           of strongly acylically matched subsets of abelian groups and will provide a
                                           family of such subsets. Finally, we introduce the notion of weakly matchable
                                           subsets and prove that it implies the existence of matchings in usual sense.

that a torsion-free abelian group G possesses the matching property: For any two
subsets A and B as in Definition 1.1, there exists a matching f : A → B. In [26],
Losonczy proves that abelian groups with the matching property are precisely
those that are either torsion-free or cyclic of prime order; namely, groups that do
not possess any non-trivial proper finite subgroup. Indeed, torsion-free abelian
groups admit the stronger acyclic matching property in the sense that for any
A and B of the same cardinality with 0 ∈      / B, there exists an acyclic matching
f : A → B [4, 26]. The situation for groups Z/pZ of prime orders is more subtle.
Paper [1] shows that for primes p with p ≡ −1 (mod 8) the group Z/pZ does not
have the acyclic matching property via exhibiting an explicit subset of Z/pZ that
does not admit any acyclic matching onto itself. Based on experimental evidence,
it is conjectured in [3] that Z/pZ does not admit the acyclic matching property
for any prime p > 5. We shall prove the following theorems on the existence of
matchings between certain subsets of a cyclic group of prime order.

number. Suppose A satisfies A ∩ 2A = ∅ and is of size k where k.2k−1 < p.1 Then

exists an acyclic matching f : A → B.
The theorems will be established in §2; the proof of the first one is based on
a linear algebra argument while the second one utilizes a result from additive

   The condition A ∩ 2A = ∅ in Theorem 1.2 is necessary for id : A → A to
be a matching. In general, all bijections f : A → B are matchings provided
that A ∩ (A + B) = ∅.2 The profusion of matchings then can possibly imply the
existence of an acyclic matching from A to B.
Question 1.4. Let A, B be subsets of the cyclic group Z/pZ where p is a prime
number. Suppose A and B are of the same size k. Does the condition A∩(A+B) =
∅ guarantee the existence of an acyclic matching f : A → B?
A partial answer will be provided in Proposition 2.6.

subsets A and B of G that have the same cardinality and satisfy A ∩ (A + B) = ∅.

345545 Let $S_n$ be the symmetric group, let $s:S_n\to S_n$ be the stack-sorting map, and let $\deg(s:S_n\to S_n)$ denote its maximum fiber size. The paper considers the exponential growth rate

$$
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\deg(s:S_n\to S_n)^{1/n}.
$$

**Stack-sorting growth conjecture.** The value of this limit lies in the interval $(1.68,1.73)$.

The interval is suggested by computations on random permutations for $n=100$ and $n=300$. The problem of obtaining improved asymptotic estimates, or exact formulas, remains open.
346167 Let $G$ and $\overline{G}$ be nontrivial connected graphs of order $n\geq 8$. Write $rvd(G)$ for the rainbow vertex-disconnection number of $G$. **Rainbow vertex-disconnection Nordhaus–Gaddum conjecture.**

$$
rvd(G)+rvd(\overline{G})\geq n.
$$

This conjecture proposes an improvement of the established lower bound for the sum of the rainbow vertex-disconnection numbers of a graph and its complement. The paper notes that the bound holds when $rvd(G)=1$, but does not establish it for all nontrivial connected graphs of order at least $8$.
347230 Let ${\mathbb Q}\langle a,b\rangle_n$ denote the vector space of homogeneous noncommutative $ab$-polynomials of degree $n$, and let $I_2:{\mathbb Q}\langle a,b\rangle_n\rightarrow {\mathbb Q}\langle a,b\rangle_{n+1}$ be the interval transform of the second kind. Let $A_{\mathbb Q}\langle a,b\rangle_n$ be the specified subspace of ${\mathbb Q}\langle a,b\rangle_n$. 

**Kernel conjecture.** For each $n\geq 1$, 

$$
\ker I_2=A_{\mathbb Q}\langle a,b\rangle_n.
$$

The conjecture identifies precisely the kernel of the second-kind interval transform, whose nontrivial kernel distinguishes it from the corresponding Tchebyshev operator framework. Its status is not resolved in the supplied source.
347712 Let $\mathbb{S}_n$ be the symmetric group on $n$ elements. A **Latin strategy** is obtained by choosing an $n\times n$ Latin square $S$; Alice's message identifies a row of $S$ that agrees with the input permutation at the maximum possible number of positions. An optimal strategy is one attaining the maximum success probability.

**Latin-strategy characterization conjecture.** Optimal strategies are exactly Latin strategies.

This conjecture proposes a complete characterization of optimal strategies, extending the explicitly described shift strategies. The paper states it as an open question and does not prove the characterization.
349177 A **dendrimer nanostar** is the nanostar structure obtained by growing a dendrimer through a specified number of stages; a graph is a **$G_\phi$-graph** if it is realized as $G_\phi(A)$ for an appropriate set $A$, using the Euler $\phi$ function as in the paper. **Nonexistence conjecture for dendrimer nanostars.** There is no dendrimer nanostar which has grown at least four stages, as a $G_\phi$-graph. The paper proves that the two-stage nanostar $D_2$ is a $G_\phi$-graph, while reporting that attempts to prove nonexistence for nanostars grown through at least four stages have failed; the conjecture remains open.
349441 Let $G$ be a connected graph. Write $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=1}}}(G)$ for the Kirchhoff index based on the hubs-repelling resistance distance, $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}}_{\alpha=0}(G)$ for the standard Kirchhoff index, and $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=-1}}}(G)$ for the Kirchhoff index based on the hubs-attracting resistance distance.

**Kirchhoff-index ordering conjecture.** For $\alpha\in\{-1,1\}$,

$$
\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=1}}}(G)\geq\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}}_{\alpha=0}(G)\geq\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=-1}}}(G),
$$

with equality if and only if $G$ is regular.

The conjecture was formulated after computational experiments on more than 12,000 connected graphs with $5\leq n\leq 8$, and predicts that hubs-repelling navigation gives the largest Kirchhoff index while hubs-attracting navigation gives the smallest. Its general validity is left open in the source.
350452 Let $P_n$ denote the path on $n$ vertices and $C_n$ the cycle on $n$ vertices. An **IR-graph** is a graph arising as an irredundance graph.

**Non-IR conjecture for paths and cycles.** The path $P_n$ is not an IR-graph for each $n\geq 3$, and the cycle $C_n$ is not an IR-graph for each $n\geq 5$.

These are open problems concerning which graphs can occur as irredundance graphs. They are stated together in the paper's list of conjectures and open problems and are attributed there to Mynhardt (2019).

For $\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_n$, let $\operatorname{type}(\pi)$ be its cycle type, and let $\operatorname{RLMv}(\pi)$ and $\operatorname{EXCv}(\pi)$ denote its right-to-left-minimum-value and excedance-value multisets, respectively. 350979 Let $n$ be an integer, and let a **circulant graph** mean a graph whose vertices are arranged cyclically with adjacency determined by a fixed set of cyclic differences. The monophonic position number is denoted by $a$, and the diameter by $D$. **Circulant graph conjecture.** For any $n\geq 11$, there is a circulant graph with order $n$, monophonic position number $a=2$, and diameter $D=2$. The preceding theorem establishes existence of some graph with these parameters if and only if $n\in\{3,4,5,8\}$ or $n\geq 11$; the conjecture strengthens the large-order existence assertion by requiring the graph to be circulant, based on computational evidence.
352253 Let $G$ be a doubled 3-regular graph. Perform the YY-$\Delta$ transformation on all wyes in all possible ways to obtain a graph $G'$ of smallest size. A **chain of 3-cycles** is a chain of one or more 3-cycles, each connected by a vertex that is also doubly adjacent to one more vertex, with the 3-cycles at either end having one vertex with exactly 4 neighbours and no connections of size greater than 2.

**Minimality criterion conjecture.** The graph $G'$ is a minimal graph if and only if it does not contain such a chain of 3-cycles.

The conjecture proposes that, for doubled 3-regular graphs, checking the more restricted subgraphs illustrated in the first column of the paper's figure suffices to determine minimality. The source gives examples and discusses related reducible subgraphs, but provides no proof or resolution.
352496 Let $M_n$ be the monoid of rook diagrams and let $\mathbf{0}$ denote its zero element. Let $\psi(n)$ be the number of nonzero products arising from $M_n\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$.

**Polynexus conjecture.** For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $\psi(n)$ equals the $(n+1)$th Polynexus number of order $7$, namely

$$
\psi(n)=\frac{(n+1)^7-n^7-(n+1)^3+n^3}{120}.
$$

The formula was suggested by numerical evaluation of the sextuple sum defining $\psi(n)$ and comparison with the Polynexus numbers. An analytic evaluation of that sum was not available in the source, so the conjecture remains open here.
352827 Let $k$ and $r$ be integers with $2\leq r\leq k-2$, and let $D(G)$ denote the diameter of a graph $G$. For $n=2k+r$, write $\operatorname{SG}(n,k)$ for the Schrijver graph. **Unit-gap conjecture.** If $2\leq r\leq k-2$, then

$$
D\left(\operatorname{SG}(2k+r,k)\right)-D\left(\operatorname{SG}(2k+r+1,k)\right)\in\\{0,1\\}.
$$

The conjecture concerns the possibility of larger drops in the diameter as $r$ increases. It is based on computations and is open; the paper notes that the analogous differences are in $\\{0,1\\}$ for $k\leq 5$, while a larger gap occurs at the transition from $r=1$ to $r=2$ when $k\geq 6$.
