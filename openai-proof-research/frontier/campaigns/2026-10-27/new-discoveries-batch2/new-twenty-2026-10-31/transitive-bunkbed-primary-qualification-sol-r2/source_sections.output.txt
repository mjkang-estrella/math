Model 1.5 (E0 (G, T ), the Standard Bunkbed Model). For an undirected graph G and a fixed set
T ⊆ V (G), a set X ⊆ E(G̃) of horizontal edges is chosen uniformly at random, and retained along

    In this setting, BBC(E0 (G, T )) is equivalent to many statements referred to as ‘the bunkbed
conjecture’. When stated, the conjecture is often given in a slightly more general form, retaining
each horizontal edge of G̃ with probability p for some fixed p ∈ (0, 1), as in for example the work
of Häggström [4,5]. However, as proved by Rudzinski and Smyth [3, Corollary 1.7], the case for any
specific probability p implies the general case, and so the truth of BBC(E0 (G, T )) would suffice to
prove the more general conjecture.
    The conjecture BBC(E (G)) is false for general percolation models E ; this is unsurprising as our
definition has no requirement of symmetry, and so permits many pathological models. However,
we will show that BBC(E (G)) is also false for several highly symmetric models E , some of which
have been conjectured to be true by various authors. This is particularly surprising as much of the
same intuition which tells us that BBC(E0 (G, T )) should be true also applies to these other – false –
cases.
    The conjecture has attracted significant attention over the last few years, being resolved in
progressively more general special cases. One initial sequence of results by de Buyer and then
van Hintum and Lammers [6–8] resolved the case of the complete graph. This was then further
generalised by Richthammer [9] to a broader class of highly symmetric graphs. Linusson [10,11] took
a different route, and both considered generalisations of the conjecture and related the conjecture
to a problem concerning randomly directed graphs. This work was then built upon by Hutchcroft,
Nizić-Nikolac, and Kent [12], who resolved the conjecture in the case of general G and probability
p ↑ 1. A different angle on the p ↑ 1 limit was later considered by the author [13], proving the
conjecture holds in this case by working with graph cuts.

the bunkbed conjecture to try to generalise the statement of the problem, in the hope that this
may be conducive to an inductive proof. Indeed, upon attacking the conjecture BBC(E0 (G, T )), a
generalisation to hypergraphs soon arises as a tempting possibility. Likewise, directed graphs allow
for the possibility of attaching ‘gadgets’ to a graph to easily generalise the conjecture further, and on
generalising to site percolation, one no longer needs to consider paths which return to an already-
visited pair of vertices in the bunkbed graph. However, our main result is the following, which says
that all of these generalisations are in fact false.

Theorem 1.6.         The generalised bunkbed conjecture is false for site percolation, hypergraphs, and

Definition 1.6.2. A graph G is an ({k}, d)-vertex-expander if every S ⊆ V (G) with |S| = k has

Theorem 1.6.3. Fix d > 1 and ε ≥ ε0 (d) > 0 such that ε ≤ 1/8, let k ∈ N satisfy k ≤ ε−2 d, and let
the graph G be an ({k}, d)-vertex-expander. Then, if p = (1 + ε)/d, then Gp contains a cycle of length
at least Ωε (kd) with probability at least 1 − exp(−Ωε (k/d)).

7.1.1   Proof outline

As is the case with much of the work in this area, the proof of Theorem 1.6.3 runs by careful analysis of
a modified depth-first search (DFS) algorithm. The precise details of the algorithm will be discussed in
Section 7.2.1, but the idea of the proof is as follows.
    We will use sprinkling, writing p = 1 − (1 − p1 )(1 − p2 ) with p1 ≈ p large enough so that dp1 > 1,
but also far enough from p that p2 is not too much smaller than p. We expose edges with probability p1
for running the DFS, and then expose edges in a second round with probability p2 . This second round
will reveal a few more edges, with which we are likely to find a long cycle. Indeed, we seek an edge xy
of G such that there is a long path between x and y in Gp1 ; in fact, we find many of these edges, so that
it is likely that one of them is present in Gp2 .

 Abstract. 
 
 We construct a simple acyclic directed graph for which the Bunkbed Conjecture is false, thereby resolving conjectures posed by Leander and by Hollom. 
 
 
 
 1. Introduction 

 
 The Bunkbed Conjecture, introduced by Kasteleyn in 1985 [ 9 , Remark 5] , postulates a certain monotonicity of connectivity probabilities in Bernoulli bond percolation on an undirected graph. Being an intuitive, but surprisingly hard to prove, statement, a lot of effort has been put in proving its correctness. Despite that, the original conjecture turned out to be false, as shown recently by Gladkov, Pak and Zimin [ 2 ] who built on the work of Hollom disproving the hypergraph variant [ 4 ] . 
 
 
 Since the main conjecture does not hold, the problem of finding broad families of graphs, or other general models, where it does hold is of great interest.
One of the remaining promising general models considered is the setup of acyclic directed graphs put forward by Leander [ 5 ] , and highlighted by Hollom [ 4 ] . Nevertheless, in this work we prove that the Bunkbed Conjecture does not hold in this case either. 
 
 
 Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a (directed or undirected) graph, and let ( V − , E − ) (V^{-},E^{-}) and ( V + , E + ) (V^{+},E^{+}) be two copies of G G . For v ∈ V v\in V let v − v^{-} , v + v^{+} denote the copies of v v in V − V^{-} and V + V^{+} , respectively. The bunkbed graph G ¯ = ( V ¯ , E ¯ ) \overline{G}=(\overline{V},\overline{E}) of G G is the graph defined by V ¯ = V − ⊔ V + \overline{V}=V^{-}\sqcup V^{+} and E ¯ = E − ⊔ E + ⊔ E ~ \overline{E}=E^{-}\sqcup E^{+}\sqcup\widetilde{E} , where E ~ \widetilde{E} consists of bidirected edges ( v − , v + ) (v^{-},v^{+}) for all v ∈ V v\in V . We will call the edges in E ~ \widetilde{E} vertical , and the edges in E − ⊔ E + E^{-}\sqcup E^{+} horizontal . 
 
 
 The bunkbed percolation model is a probabilistic measure on the subgraphs of the bunkbed graph. The most commonly chosen measure is the p p -Bernoulli bond percolation ℙ p \mathbb{P}_{p} of the edges of the bunkbed graph, i.e., a random subgraph of the edges of the bunkbed graph, in which every edge is retained with probability p p , or deleted with probability 1 − p 1-p , independently of other edges. 
 
 
 The original Bunkbed Conjecture states the following: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1 . 
 
 For every undirected graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) and every u u , v ∈ V v\in V 
 

 
 
 ℙ p ​ ( u − ↔ v − ) ≥ ℙ p ​ ( u − ↔ v + ) , \mathbb{P}_{p}(u^{-}\leftrightarrow v^{-})\geq\mathbb{P}_{p}(u^{-}\leftrightarrow v^{+}), 
 
 
 where x ↔ y x\leftrightarrow y is the event that in the percolated bunkbed graph there is a path connecting vertices x x and y y . 
 
 
 
 This conjecture was disproved by Gladkov, Pak and Zimin [ 2 ] . Their counterexample is ingeniously based on a graph found by Hollom [ 4 ] , which in turn served as a counterexample to the Hypergraph Bunkbed Conjecture. The apparent intuition behind the conjecture is that vertices u − u^{-} and v − v^{-} are closer to each other than u − u^{-} and v + v^{+} for any conceivable notion of closeness , and so the chance of an open path between u − u^{-} and v − v^{-} should be at least as large as the chance of open path between u − u^{-} and v + v^{+} . There are certain instances where the conjecture does hold, notably on complete graphs [ 1 , 10 ] , for adjacent vertices in edge-transitive graphs [ 8 ] , for vertices u u , v v with the same neighbourhood [ 8 ] ; and also for the random cluster model with parameter q = 2 q=2 [ 3 ] ; for more thorough discussion see [ 2 ] . 
 
 
 Leander considered the Bunkbed Conjecture for directed graphs in [ 5 ] , in which case one compares the probability of existence of directed paths in a Bernoulli percolated bunkbed graph. This directed case was disproved by Hollom, who found a directed multigraph counterexample. Hollom conjectured that one can avoid the use of multiple edges. Further, Leander considered the conjecture for acyclic directed graphs. Interestingly, Hollom’s counterexample contains a lot of directed cycles and as such does not disprove Leander’s acyclic variant. The acyclic graphs seem promising to satisfy the conjecture as they might be amenable to some inductive argument. Despite that, we find a simple acyclic directed graph not satisfying the conjecture, thereby resolving the conjectures by Leander and by Hollom.

is outerplanar.

cycle of G of maximum length. Suppose first that G has a node x that does
not lie on C. Since G is 2-connected there exist two edge disjoint paths from
x to distinct nodes y and z on C so that these paths have only the node x in
common. If the edge yz exists and is an edge of C, then C can be extended
to a longer cycle using the two paths which contradicts the maximality of
C. If y and z are not joined by an edge of C then C and the two paths
are easily reduced to a K2,3 minor of G, which gives another contradiction.
Hence all nodes of G must be nodes of C.
