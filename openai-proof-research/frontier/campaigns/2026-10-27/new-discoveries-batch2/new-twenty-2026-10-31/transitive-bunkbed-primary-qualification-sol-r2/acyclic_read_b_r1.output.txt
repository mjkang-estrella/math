## Exact source scope

The literal record is “The bunkbed conjecture for transitive tournaments”, MathDB 382297, UUID 6d5510a4-f51a-47f3-b2ab-520750f25ac5. Its E7 name is defined by Hollom, arXiv:2406.01790, Definitions 1.1–1.4, Model 4.5 and Conjecture 6.4. I independently extracted the MathML `alttext` from the original saved HTML: the supplied clean-text transformation replaces formulas by [FORMULA], so that transformation alone cannot authenticate this endpoint.

For n ≥ 0 take vertex indices 0,...,n−1 and arcs i→j exactly when i<j. In the bunkbed, each horizontal arc has its own copy in each layer. Each index also has ONE bidirectional vertical-edge token. Uniformly select a subset of these n(n−1)+n=n² tokens. Equivalently every token is an independent fair bit. A retained vertical token enables both directions. For every fixed u,v prove

P(Reach((u,0),(v,0))) ≥ P(Reach((u,0),(v,1))).

Reach is reflexive transitive closure. The source speaks of a directed path and does not explicitly spell out “length zero”; the conventional zero-length path interpretation gives the stated diagonal case. It is fixed as part of the reviewed semantic endpoint, not hidden as an implementation choice. Distinct endpoints have no such convention issue.

The published journal body, European Journal of Combinatorics 128 (2025), 104188, has the same definitions, with the name D7 in Model 4.5 and Conjecture 6.4. Definition 1.1 (printed p.2) makes the vertical edge bidirectional. Printed p.14 explicitly says the opposite vertical arcs are both retained or both removed, then separately defines D8 with independently retained vertical arcs. Thus E7 and D7 agree token for token. Independent horizontal bits follow from uniform sampling of the full powerset, not a pairing assumption. E6/D6 conditions exactly one horizontal copy outside F, and is a different measure. Neither that conditioned model nor D8 is substituted here.

Hollom's thesis, *Extremal, Probabilistic, and Infinitary Problems in Combinatorics*, title dated October 2025, declaration signed February 5, 2026, Model 6.4.5 and Conjecture 6.6.4, preserves this precise D7 tournament conjecture. It explicitly acknowledges Przybyłowski's negative acyclic result immediately before posing the tournament conjecture. Deposit/HTTP modification dates are not proof of when every sentence was authored.

## Independent full ordinary audit and proof

Let Ω be all bit assignments to the n² distinct tokens, with uniform measure. All arguments below are finite counting arguments; no continuous independence assumption is needed. Fix u. Every arc weakly increases the index, and every horizontal arc strictly increases it. Thus a path to index ≤k cannot visit index >k. A vertex below u is unreachable. Deleting all indices below u does not alter any path from u to a later index. Bits on those discarded tokens and on future tokens contribute common independent fiber factors; they are not conditioned to be absent.

For k≥u reveal exactly the tokens whose endpoints have index ≤k. This is the full prefix assignment H_k, not a revelation of future reachability. Define R_k^ell as indices i≤k with a prefix path from (u,0) to (i,ell). Since no index below u is reachable these sets lie in [u,k]. The no-return property proves equality of prefix reachability and full-graph reachability for such endpoints. Set A_k=|R_k^0| and B_k=|R_k^1|. Vertical cycles do not invalidate this: they preserve the index and at a new index can only connect its two copies.

At k=u the only reachable copy initially is (u,0). It reaches (u,1) iff the u vertical bit is one. The distribution is μ_u(1,0)=1/2 and μ_u(1,1)=1/2. Revealing tokens at indices below u does not alter this law.

Fix any full prefix assignment h of positive probability, with R_k^0=S, R_k^1=T, a=|S|, b=|T|. The next index j=k+1 has a fresh horizontal token (i,j,0) for every i∈S and a different fresh token (i,j,1) for every i∈T. These token sets are disjoint even when S and T overlap. Under the fiber H_k=h every assignment to the unrevealed tokens has equal multiplicity. Hence their restrictions are independent uniform assignments, independent also of the next vertical bit Z. Defining X and Y as the ORs of the indicated layer-0 and layer-1 token sets gives

P(X=0|h)=2^(−a), P(Y=0|h)=2^(−b), P(Z=0|h)=1/2,

with joint product law, including a=0 or b=0. Edges from unreachable parents into j do not affect reachability, and summing over their bits is a common multiplicity. Conditioning on counts alone is a mixture of these full-prefix fibers; because every fiber with the same counts gives this same triple law, the kernel really depends only on (a,b).

A path first entering j enters horizontally from a reachable old copy. Once at j it can only use its vertical token or go to a greater index; it cannot go back into the prefix. Therefore the indicators that the two new copies are reachable are exactly

U = X ∨ (Z ∧ Y), V = Y ∨ (Z ∧ X).

This proves the Reach-to-count bridge, rather than defining a proxy process. The next count state is (a+U,b+V). Write q=2^(−a), r=2^(−b). Its transition kernel K is supported on the four increments (0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1), with probabilities respectively

qr, (1−q)r/2, q(1−r)/2, 1−(q+r)/2.

These formulas sum to one and are nonnegative. They authenticate every event fiber weight. With reflection ρ(a,b)=(b,a), swapping q,r swaps the two single-layer increments and leaves the others fixed. Thus K(ρs,ρt)=K(s,t).

Let “above” mean a>b. A count difference changes by U−V ∈ {−1,0,1}. Since the old difference is an integer ≥1, a one-step transition from above to strictly below is impossible; the reverse is also impossible. Above can hit the diagonal and diagonal can enter either side. Those diagonal cases must be retained, not excluded by a false pathwise domination claim.

For any finite distribution μ suppose δμ(s)=μ(s)−μ(ρs)≥0 above. Extend the finite support to a reflection-stable finite square containing it and its successors. For t above, using equivariance and pairing all reflected predecessors,

δ(μK)(t)
= Σ_s μ(s)[K(s,t)−K(s,ρt)]
= Σ_(s above) [μ(s)−μ(ρs)][K(s,t)−K(s,ρt)]
= Σ_(s above) δμ(s) K(s,t) ≥0.

For a diagonal predecessor s=ρs, equivariance gives K(s,t)=K(s,ρt), so its contribution cancels. For an above predecessor, ρt is strictly below and the no-crossing property sets K(s,ρt)=0. All paired sums are finite, so no convergence or lost boundary term is involved. This proves reflection dominance by induction from μ_u. It does not assert A_k≥B_k pathwise, or infer distributional dominance from E(A_k−B_k).

Finally U−V=(1−Z)(X−Y), so the authenticated fiber law gives

g(a,b)=E(U−V|H_k=h)=(r−q)/2.

This is antisymmetric, vanishes on the diagonal and is nonnegative above. Pairing the finite count distribution yields

E(U−V)=Σ_(a>b) [μ_k(a,b)−μ_k(b,a)] g(a,b) ≥0.

For u<v use k=v−1. The exact recursion says U and V are precisely the indicators of the two Reach events at v. The prefix restriction says they equal the full-graph indicators. Their expectations are their event probabilities under Ω. Hence this is the literal conjectured inequality. If v<u both probabilities are zero. If v=u the probabilities are one and one-half. For n=0 there are no endpoints. Relabeling any transitive tournament by its unique linear order transports tokens, outcomes and paths bijectively and preserves the uniform measure, so the result is independent of index naming.

No mathematical gap was found in this argument. The explicit four-entry kernel and fiber argument above expand the author's terse proof; they preserve its target and measure.

## Adversarial questions and answers

1. Does a reachable parent set depend on the incoming bits being sampled? No: it is determined by H_k; paths returning from j would be necessary, and index monotonicity forbids them.
2. Can an index reachable in both layers reuse a bit? No: layer is part of a horizontal token. Only the vertical token is bidirectional and shared.
3. Does counts-only conditioning create correlations? Every full-prefix fiber has the same triple law once counts are fixed; mixing identical laws leaves it unchanged.
4. Can old reachability change after exposing the next index? No; a path cannot decrease index. This is exactly where general acyclic graphs alone do not give total-count sufficiency for each target.
5. Can reflection paths jump across the diagonal? A change by at most one cannot cross an integer gap of at least one into the strict opposite half-plane. Diagonal departures cancel by equivariance.
6. Does a nonnegative mean count difference suffice? No. The terminal function is nonlinear; the full signed mass invariant is essential.
7. Is the conclusion only for a random source or an averaged endpoint? No: u is fixed before any bits are sampled, and v is fixed before choosing the terminal prefix.
8. Does averaging over random vertical bits disguise a conditioned-post theorem? No: all n vertical bits remain independent fair bits in Ω throughout.
9. What would be invalid formal evidence? A theorem about K without the finite-bit fiber and reflexive-Reach bridges, or a terminal expectation theorem without equality to the two literal event cardinalities.

## Identified prior results, subsumption and chronology

Leander (2009), Section 5, Model 3 (Model D), Conjecture 6 and Theorems 10–12, including their proofs, was read directly. Model D colors each base edge red or blue, allows color changes at specified T, and assumes a source/sink arrangement. Theorem 10 requires T to contain a separating cutset or an endpoint; Theorem 11 requires completion to a cutset with the extra vertices on no u-to-v path; Theorem 12 assumes T is an antichain. Its mirroring proof flips edges beyond the appropriate prescribed posts. These are not all E7 outcomes: two horizontal copies can both be absent or both present; T is random; and multiple interior posts on the ordered chain are comparable. Even after conditioning on paired-edge states one has arbitrary missing and deterministic double edges, with no guarantee of these cutset/antichain hypotheses. The direct u→v arc also bypasses every strictly interior post set in the full tournament. I found no complete bridge from these theorems to E7; I do not exclude the candidate on that basis. The document is a 2009 thesis/report; it should not be called a PhD thesis solely from Hollom's bibliographic shorthand.

Van Hintum–Lammers, *The bunkbed conjecture on the complete graph*, EJC 76 (2019), 175–177; arXiv:1803.07647v4 dated 27 March 2021: the actual theorem and entire short proof were read. Its hypotheses are undirected K_n, arbitrary fixed vertical set H always open, independent horizontal Bernoulli p, fixed endpoints. Its proof first uses full vertex-permutation symmetry to replace the source by a uniform random vertex. It removes the terminal vertex, partitions the remaining undirected graph into components c_i and pairs a layer swap to obtain a product proportional to (c_i^-−c_i^+)(q^(c_i^+)−q^(c_i^-))≥0. Although the same elementary monotone exponential difference appears here, directed reachability is not an undirected component partition, and ordered vertices are not exchangeable. Transitive tournaments have no symmetry sending arbitrary fixed u to a uniform index. Replacing components by reachable sets and source weights by count-law masses is a new deduction requiring the entire kernel/reflection induction above. The actual older proof contains neither that sufficient-statistic kernel nor its reflection-dominance conclusion. Thus no complete routine adaptation exclusion has been established. This is a bounded assessment, not a claim that reflection methods themselves are new.

Przybyłowski, arXiv:2506.22284v1 dated 27 June 2025 and v2 dated 11 November 2025: I read v2's definitions, Theorem 1.2, Proposition 3.2 proof and Section 4 proof in full text, then the full extracted-text v1/v2 diff. The theorem is existence of a simple acyclic directed graph with the reverse inequality. It uses the particular sparse nine-vertex graph G1, conditioned posts {2,5,8}, event-mirroring cancellations and a negative residual, then substitutes k+2-vertex gadgets to approximate forced posts and obtain an unconditioned counterexample. Its Section 4 does not prove a tournament theorem. A simple acyclic graph embeds in a transitive tournament as a subgraph, but the inequality difference is not known monotone under adding arcs: monotonicity of each individual connection probability does not compare their difference.
