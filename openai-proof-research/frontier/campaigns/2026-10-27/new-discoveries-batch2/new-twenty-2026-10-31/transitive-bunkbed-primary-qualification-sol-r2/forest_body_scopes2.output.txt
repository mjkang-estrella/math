FOREST FULL                                                AN ELEMENTARY PROOF OF THE BUNKBED CONJECTURE FOR FORESTS

                                                                SERTE DONDERWINKEL1 , JOOST JORRITSMA2 , GUILLEM PERARNAU3,4


                                                    Abstract. Although false for general graphs, this note gives an elementary proof of the bunkbed conjecture
                                                    for any acyclic graph. The argument is short and self-contained, and may be of educational interest.

                                              Intuitively, it should be more likely to maintain an open path between vertices on the same level, than
                                           to cross layers. However, a counterexample was given recently by Gladkov, Pak and Zimin [2], based on a
                                           counterexample of Hollom for the hypergraph version of the conjecture [5]. Since the conjecture fails in full
                                           generality, it becomes even more interesting to understand for which cases it remains valid. The conjecture
                                           has proven to be true for certain classes of graphs such as complete graphs [3], wheels [7] or highly symmetric
                                           graphs [11] and when p tends to 0 or to 1 [4, 6]. In particular, Linusson proved the conjecture for outerplanar
                                           graphs [8], which includes our setting here. We present an elementary proof of
                                           Theorem. The bunkbed conjecture holds for any forest (acyclic graph).
                                           Our theorem holds for any set H, and thus the theorem holds also for cases in which H is random, independent
                                           of the percolation configuration. Another proof of our result is implied by [9] which studies graphs ‘glued’
                                           along a vertex. We note that the bunkbed conjecture is false for some directed acyclic graphs [10].

19 347712 Latin-strategy characterization conjecture for the symmetric group SCOPE: Let $\mathbb{S}_n$ be the symmetric group on $n$ elements. A **Latin strategy** is obtained by choosing an $n\times n$ Latin square $S$; Alice's message identifies a row of $S$ that agrees with the input permutation at the maximum possible number of positions. An optimal strategy is one attaining the maximum success probability.

**Latin-strategy characterization conjecture.** Optimal strategies are exactly Latin strategies.

This conjecture proposes a complete characterization of optimal strategies, extending the explicitly described shift strategies. The paper states it as an open question and does not prove the characterization.
20 349177 Nonexistence conjecture for dendrimer nanostars of at least four stages SCOPE: A **dendrimer nanostar** is the nanostar structure obtained by growing a dendrimer through a specified number of stages; a graph is a **$G_\phi$-graph** if it is realized as $G_\phi(A)$ for an appropriate set $A$, using the Euler $\phi$ function as in the paper. **Nonexistence conjecture for dendrimer nanostars.** There is no dendrimer nanostar which has grown at least four stages, as a $G_\phi$-graph. The paper proves that the two-stage nanostar $D_2$ is a $G_\phi$-graph, while reporting that attempts to prove nonexistence for nanostars grown through at least four stages have failed; the conjecture remains open.
21 349441 Estrada–Mugnolo's Kirchhoff-index ordering conjecture for hubs-biased resistance distances SCOPE: Let $G$ be a connected graph. Write $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=1}}}(G)$ for the Kirchhoff index based on the hubs-repelling resistance distance, $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}}_{\alpha=0}(G)$ for the standard Kirchhoff index, and $\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=-1}}}(G)$ for the Kirchhoff index based on the hubs-attracting resistance distance.

**Kirchhoff-index ordering conjecture.** For $\alpha\in\{-1,1\}$,

$$
\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=1}}}(G)\geq\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}}_{\alpha=0}(G)\geq\mathcal{\mathcal{\mathscr{R}}_{\mathnormal{\alpha=-1}}}(G),
$$

with equality if and only if $G$ is regular.

The conjecture was formulated after computational experiments on more than 12,000 connected graphs with $5\leq n\leq 8$, and predicts that hubs-repelling navigation gives the largest Kirchhoff index while hubs-attracting navigation gives the smallest. Its general validity is left open in the source.
22 350452 Non-IR conjecture for paths and cycles SCOPE: Let $P_n$ denote the path on $n$ vertices and $C_n$ the cycle on $n$ vertices. An **IR-graph** is a graph arising as an irredundance graph.

**Non-IR conjecture for paths and cycles.** The path $P_n$ is not an IR-graph for each $n\geq 3$, and the cycle $C_n$ is not an IR-graph for each $n\geq 5$.

These are open problems concerning which graphs can occur as irredundance graphs. They are stated together in the paper's list of conjectures and open problems and are attributed there to Mynhardt (2019).
23 350721 Signed cycle-type restriction polynomiality conjecture SCOPE: Let $n$ be a positive integer, let $k$ be a fixed positive integer with $1\leq k\leq n$, and let $\mathfrak{S}_n$ denote the symmetric group on $n$ elements. For $\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_n$, let $\operatorname{type}(\pi)$ be its cycle type, and let $\operatorname{RLMv}(\pi)$ and $\operatorname{EXCv}(\pi)$ denote its right-to-left-minimum-value and excedance-value multisets, respectively. Then **the polynomiality conjecture.**

$$
\sum_{\substack{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_n\\min(\operatorname{type}(\pi))\geq k}}(-1)^{\operatorname{inv}(\pi)}\mathbf{x}_{\operatorname{RLMv}(\pi)}\mathbf{y}_{\operatorname{EXCv}(\pi)}
$$

is an element of $\mathbb{N}[x_1,\dotsc,x_n,y_1,\dotsc,y_n]$. This generalizes the derangement case by imposing a lower bound on the lengths of all cycles; the source provides no resolution or further evidence for the claim, so its status remains open.
24 350979 Circulant graph conjecture for monophonic position number two SCOPE: Let $n$ be an integer, and let a **circulant graph** mean a graph whose vertices are arranged cyclically with adjacency determined by a fixed set of cyclic differences. The monophonic position number is denoted by $a$, and the diameter by $D$. **Circulant graph conjecture.** For any $n\geq 11$, there is a circulant graph with order $n$, monophonic position number $a=2$, and diameter $D=2$. The preceding theorem establishes existence of some graph with these parameters if and only if $n\in\{3,4,5,8\}$ or $n\geq 11$; the conjecture strengthens the large-order existence assertion by requiring the graph to be circulant, based on computational evidence.
25 352253 Subgraph criterion for minimal doubled 3-regular graphs SCOPE: Let $G$ be a doubled 3-regular graph. Perform the YY-$\Delta$ transformation on all wyes in all possible ways to obtain a graph $G'$ of smallest size. A **chain of 3-cycles** is a chain of one or more 3-cycles, each connected by a vertex that is also doubly adjacent to one more vertex, with the 3-cycles at either end having one vertex with exactly 4 neighbours and no connections of size greater than 2.

**Minimality criterion conjecture.** The graph $G'$ is a minimal graph if and only if it does not contain such a chain of 3-cycles.

The conjecture proposes that, for doubled 3-regular graphs, checking the more restricted subgraphs illustrated in the first column of the paper's figure suffices to determine minimality. The source gives examples and discusses related reducible subgraphs, but provides no proof or resolution.
26 352496 The Polynexus formula for nonzero products in the rook monoid SCOPE: Let $M_n$ be the monoid of rook diagrams and let $\mathbf{0}$ denote its zero element. Let $\psi(n)$ be the number of nonzero products arising from $M_n\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}$.

**Polynexus conjecture.** For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, $\psi(n)$ equals the $(n+1)$th Polynexus number of order $7$, namely

$$
\psi(n)=\frac{(n+1)^7-n^7-(n+1)^3+n^3}{120}.
$$

The formula was suggested by numerical evaluation of the sextuple sum defining $\psi(n)$ and comparison with the Polynexus numbers. An analytic evaluation of that sum was not available in the source, so the conjecture remains open here.
27 352827 Unit-gap conjecture for diameters of Schrijver graphs SCOPE: Let $k$ and $r$ be integers with $2\leq r\leq k-2$, and let $D(G)$ denote the diameter of a graph $G$. For $n=2k+r$, write $\operatorname{SG}(n,k)$ for the Schrijver graph. **Unit-gap conjecture.** If $2\leq r\leq k-2$, then

$$
D\left(\operatorname{SG}(2k+r,k)\right)-D\left(\operatorname{SG}(2k+r+1,k)\right)\in\\{0,1\\}.
$$

The conjecture concerns the possibility of larger drops in the diameter as $r$ increases. It is based on computations and is open; the paper notes that the analogous differences are in $\\{0,1\\}$ for $k\leq 5$, while a larger gap occurs at the transition from $r=1$ to $r=2$ when $k\geq 6$.
28 352972 Balanced-graph conjecture for flat graphs SCOPE: Let $G$ be a flat graph, and let $c_i(G)$ denote the number of $i$-cliques in $G$. Define

$$
D_{clique}(G)=\left|\sum_{i:\ i\ \mathrm{is\ odd}}c_i(G)-\sum_{i:\ i\ \mathrm{is\ even}}c_i(G)\right|.
$$

Call $G$ **balanced** when $D_{clique}(G)\leq 1$.

**Balanced-graph conjecture.** Any flat graph is a balanced graph.

The source notes that related subclasses of flat graphs satisfy $D_{clique}(G)=1$. The supplied text gives no resolution of the conjecture, so it remains open.
29 353203 Uniqueness conjecture for power domination reconfiguration graphs of complete bipartite graphs SCOPE: Let $K_{s,t}$ be the complete bipartite graph with part sizes $s$ and $t$, and let $\mathscr{PD}^{TAR}(K_{s,t})$ denote its power domination reconfiguration graph under token addition/removal reconfiguration. **Uniqueness conjecture.** $\mathscr{PD}^{TAR}(K_{s,t})$ is unique if and only if $s\ne 2$ and $t\ne 2$. This is supported by the uniqueness results for $K_{1,t}$ with $t\ge 3$ and $K_{3,3}$, together with Sage computations for $s\in\{3,4\}$ and $t\in\{3,4,5\}$; the conjecture proposes the complete characterization, including the cases not covered by those results.
30 354228 Even-arity complete full tree local antimagic chromatic number conjecture SCOPE: Let $T$ be a complete full $t$-ary tree, meaning a rooted tree in which every nonleaf has exactly $t$ children and all leaves have the same depth. Let $l$ be the number of leaves and let $\chi_{la}(T)$ be the local antimagic chromatic number. **Even-arity complete full tree conjecture.** For every even integer $t\geq2$,

$$
\chi_{la}(T)=l+1.
$$

The paper explicitly states this conjecture as open; it would sharpen the established upper bound $\chi_{la}(T)\leq l+2$, while the equality is proved in the paper for odd $t$.
31 355140 Improved upper bounds for Staller–Maker–Breaker domination numbers SCOPE: Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices, with minimum degree $\delta(G)$. The S-game and D-game parameters are denoted by $\gamma_{\rm SMB}'(G)$ and $\gamma_{\rm SMB}(G)$, respectively.

**Improved SMBD-number bounds.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(i) If }\gamma_{\rm SMB}'(G)<\infty,\text{ then }\gamma_{\rm SMB}'(G)\le \left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil-\delta(G)+1;\\
&\text{(ii) If }\gamma_{\rm SMB}(G)<\infty,\text{ then }\gamma_{\rm SMB}(G)\le \left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor-\delta(G)+1.
\end{aligned}
$$

These bounds would improve the known sharp general upper bounds when the minimum degree is at least two; the authors note that sharp examples under this degree condition are not known.
32 355217 Strict middle-graph increase conjecture for outer-connected domination SCOPE: Let $G$ be a graph of order $n\ge 2$. The **strict middle-graph increase conjecture** asserts that

$$
\gamma{\tilde{}} _{c}(G) < \gamma{\tilde{}} _{c}(M(G)).
$$

The paper notes that this inequality holds for trees and for several standard graph families, including complete graphs, wheels, friendship graphs, complete bipartite graphs, and cycles. Whether it holds for every graph of order at least two remains open.
33 355956 Yip's maximal subfield clique conjecture for generalized Paley graphs SCOPE: Let $d>1$ be an integer. Let $q \equiv 1 \pmod {2d}$ be a power of a prime $p$, and let $r$ be the largest integer such that $d \mid \frac{q-1}{p^r-1}$. The subfield $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$ is a clique in the generalized Paley graph $GP(q,d)=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb{F}_q^+;(\mathbb{F}_q^*)^d)$. **Yip's maximal subfield clique conjecture.** The subfield $\mathbb{F}_{p^r}$ forms a maximal clique in $GP(q,d)$. This conjecture asserts that the largest subfield producing a subfield clique cannot be extended to a larger clique. The paper resolves it in a stronger form under a sufficient-size hypothesis, while the unrestricted statement is the conjecture being addressed.
34 356266 Hurlbert's ordering conjecture for universal cycles of permutations SCOPE: Let $S_n$ be the set of permutations of $[n]$, and let $\mathcal{O}(n)$ be the clustered graph of overlapping $n$-permutations: its edges are the permutations in $S_n$, and consecutive permutations overlap in their last and first $n-1$ entries up to reduction. **Hurlbert's ordering conjecture.** Given any Euler tour of the clustered graph of overlapping permutations (that is, an ordering of $S_n$ such that each consecutive pair of permutations overlaps), there is a universal cycle for permutations that covers $S_n$ in the same order. Universal cycles correspond to Euler tours in this graph, but it remains open whether every such ordering can be realized by a universal cycle.
35 356756 The prime-modulus conjecture for progression-free digit sets SCOPE: Let $p\geq 13$ be a prime with $p\equiv 1\bmod 4$, and let $n$ be an integer divisible by $(p+3)/2$. For a digit set $D\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_p$, write $S(D,n)$ for the set of vectors in $\mathbb{Z}_p^n$ in which every digit of $D$ occurs the same number of times. Define

$$
D=\left\{0,1,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p+3}{2}\right\}.
$$

**Prime-modulus progression-free-set conjecture.** The set $S(D,n)$ does not contain any arithmetic progression of length $4$.

This conjecture extends the progression-free constructions for odd progression lengths to a case not covered by the stated theorem, motivated by computations with small primes. Its status is unresolved in the source.
36 359279 Linear-bound multiset reconstruction conjecture for tableau minors SCOPE: Let $k\geq 1$ and let $T\in\operatorname{YT}(n)$, where $n\geq k+4$. Here $\operatorname{mM}_k(T)$ denotes the multiset of $k$-minors of the standard Young tableau $T$. **Multiset reconstruction conjecture.** The multiset $\operatorname{mM}_k(T)$ determines $T$. This proposed linear bound is supported by computer verification for $k\leq 5$, and concerns reconstructibility from multisets of $k$-minors.
37 360653 The GM-rule Nash equilibrium conjecture for exact slow NIM SCOPE: Consider the exact slow game NIM$(n,n-1)$ for $\ell$ players: a position is a non-negative $n$-vector, players move cyclically, and on each move a player keeps one entry unchanged and decreases each of the other $n-1$ positive entries by $1$. A position with at least two non-positive entries is terminal; the player whose turn it is loses, and the other $\ell-1$ players share the payoff $\frac{C-L(P)}{\ell-1}$, where $L(P)$ is the play length and $C$ exceeds the length of every play from the initial position. The GM-rule specifies a strategy for each player, including the move that keeps the largest entry when no entry is a multiple of $\ell$. **GM-rule Nash equilibrium conjecture.** The set of $\ell$ GM-strategies forms a uniform Nash equilibrium. The claim concerns the strategic behavior induced by the GM-rule in the multiplayer exact slow NIM game; the supplied text does not state whether it has been proved or disproved.
