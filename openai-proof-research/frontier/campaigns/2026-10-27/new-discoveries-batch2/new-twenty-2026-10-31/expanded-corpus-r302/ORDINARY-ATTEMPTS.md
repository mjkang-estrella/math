## 367661: square obstruction and why it is excluded

Take Delta=[0,1]^2 in Z^2. Its lattice points are exactly (0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1): each coordinate is an integer between 0 and 1. Every edge has lattice length 1, whose unique positive decreasing partition is (1). The displayed literal sum over all four edges is therefore 4, equal to the lattice-point count. Any source-normalized ZMLP on this polygon has boundary coefficients equal to binomial(1,0)=binomial(1,1)=1 by Corollary 1.9. There are no interior coefficients. Thus it must equal 1+x+y+xy=(1+x)(1+y). Both factors are nonunits in C[x^{+-1},y^{+-1}] (Laurent units are nonzero monomials); hence no irreducible such polynomial exists. A bijection from irreducible ZMLPs to the nonempty tuple set is impossible in the literal all-edge interpretation.

This closes that literal interpretation, but is not a qualified discovery. Definition 1.11 says explicitly to count only one of two parallel edges in required divisibility. The square has only two edge directions, giving weight 2 rather than 4 under that convention. Treating the literal formula as a new substantive source conjecture would exploit the lost convention. Zero credit for scope correction. A second route, imposing all divisibility conditions as a square linear system, fails because the count of equations does not imply independence or irreducibility. Definition 1.11 defines required divisibility by logical dependence; arbitrary formal tuples need not encode independent equations. Proposition 1.19 does not establish this sufficiency. Printed Tom/Jerry and Figure 6 show why actual versus required divisibility must remain distinct. Full source-normalized sufficiency is parked; no semantic Lean execution plan is justified.

## 362694: complete classical abelian bridge, nonabelian obstruction

Write c=2-1/(n+1). In an abelian group let H be the period of A-A. It is finite since A-A is finite and nonempty. Put B=image(A) in G/H and m=|B|. Kneser's inequality gives |A-A|>=2|A+H|-|H|=(2m-1)|H|. Also |A-A|<c|A|<=cm|H|<2m|H|. Because A-A is H-periodic its size is an integer multiple of |H|, so equality |A-A|=(2m-1)|H| follows. Then (2m-1)<cm implies m<n+1, hence m<=n. Choose one representative from A per occupied H-coset, forming A0. Then A subset A0+H, |A0+H|=m|H|, and A-A=A0-A0+H. The normalizer is all G. Finally |A|>c^-1|A-A|=c^-1(2m-1)|H|. These are every clause of the target for abelian groups; this is a complete classical bridge for that subcase, zero credit.

The general group target is not covered: a left/right period of Q=A^-1 A need not force A0 into one coset of N(H), and a nonabelian Kneser inequality of the required sharp form cannot be imported without hypotheses. Second continuation follows source induction: define Q+= {g:r(g)>|Q|-|A|}, r(g)=|{(a,b):a^-1 b=g}|. The source's n=2 proof produces a stabilizer from r(g)+r(g0)>|A| and a covering by at most two H-cosets. At the larger threshold c approaching 2, those inequalities do not supply the needed normalizer control or the sharp (2m-1)|H| formula. Required lemma: from |Q|<c|A| produce H and a cover by m<=n disjoint H-cosets whose representatives lie in one N(H)-coset and whose quotient set has precisely 2m-1 H-cosets. That is substantive unresolved mathematics, not a certificate task. Progressions of length n+1 have |A^-1 A|=2n+1=c(n+1), equality at the excluded threshold, so are not counterexamples. Dense full-group sets are handled by H=G; they also do not refute it. Journal landing did not supply the full journal proof; no clearance claimed.

## 367969: failed spectral mechanism, retained correction

For s=t=3,n=7, every allowed 2-regular triangle-free graph on 5 vertices is C5: finite simple 2-regular components are cycles of length >=3, triangle-freeness forces each length >=4, leaving a single length-5 component. Thus L consists of K2 join C5 and Y of I2 join C5. Their equitable signless-Laplacian quotient matrices are [[7,5],[2,6]] and [[5,5],[2,6]], respectively. Positive quotient eigenvectors lift to positive vectors, so by Perron their radii are (13+sqrt41)/2 and (11+sqrt41)/2, both below 10 since 41<49. This part is exact ordinary algebra, not a numerical eigensolver result.

But proposed G=K6 disjoint union K1 is NOT K3,3-plus-free: on its six clique vertices choose two triples, retain every cross edge and one edge in the first triple. Noninduced subgraph containment is all that is required. Hence its radius 10 is irrelevant. Correcting clique size to K5 plus two isolated vertices gives radius 8, below the L radius (sqrt41>3 gives it >8), so this continuation also fails. Joining the isolates to a clique vertex creates no established improvement and requires a new subgraph-freeness proof; no witness claimed. The published body was denied 403 and not retried.
