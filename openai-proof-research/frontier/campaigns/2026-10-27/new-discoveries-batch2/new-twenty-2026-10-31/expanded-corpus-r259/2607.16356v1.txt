                                               A PROOF OF THE CYCLE DOUBLE COVER CONJECTURE BY OPENAI:
                                                                    AN EXPOSITION

                                                                                          SANG-IL OUM




                                                  Abstract. The cycle double cover conjecture states that every bridgeless graph has a list of
                                                  cycles such that every edge is in exactly two of them. In July 2026, OpenAI announced a proof.
                                                  This exposition presents the proof with slight modifications intended to make it more accessible.


                                                                                       1. Introduction
                                              A graph is Eulerian if every vertex has even degree. A cycle of a graph is a 2-regular con-
                                           nected subgraph.
                                              A cycle double cover (CDC) is a list of cycles such that every edge is in exactly two of them.




arXiv:2607.16356v1 [math.CO] 17 Jul 2026
                                           Since every Eulerian subgraph can be decomposed into pairwise edge-disjoint cycles, for the
                                           existence of a cycle double cover, it is equivalent to define a cycle double cover as a list of
                                           Eulerian subgraphs such that every edge is in exactly two of them.
                                              A bridge of a graph is an edge whose deletion increases the number of components. Ob-
                                           serve that a bridge is not in any cycle. Thus to have a cycle double cover, it is necessary to be
                                           bridgeless.
                                              This note is to discuss a proof of the cycle double cover conjecture, announced by Ope-
                                           nAI [15].
                                           Theorem 1. Every bridgeless graph has a cycle double cover.
                                              According to Zhang [22, 23], this conjecture was independently stated by Seymour [17] for
                                           all bridgeless graphs and Szekeres [19] for all bridgeless cubic graphs. An equivalent version
                                           was stated by Itai and Rodeh [8].
                                              One of the motivations to study the cycle double cover conjecture was the following stronger
                                           conjecture on embeddings of graphs. An embedding of a graph on a surface is a drawing of G
                                           on the surface such that no two edges cross. An embedding is called a 2-cell embedding if every
                                           face is homeomorphic to an open disk. A strong embedding of a graph G on a surface Σ is a
                                           2-cell embedding such that every face boundary is a cycle of G.
                                           Conjecture 2 (Strong Embedding Conjecture). Every 2-connected graph has a strong embed-
                                           ding on a surface.
                                              Conjecture 2 implies Theorem 1, just by first reducing to 2-connected graphs and taking all
                                           face boundaries. Conjecture 2 remains open. For more about the cycle double cover conjecture,
                                           survey papers and books were written by Jaeger [10], Zhang [22, 23, 21], and Chan [4].
                                              The proof presented in this note is due to GPT 5.6 announced by OpenAI [15] in July 2026.
                                           The proof was modified mainly in two parts.
                                                 • The choice of two values of F32 at each edge is now symmetric. We do not choose a
                                                   particular edge at each vertex.

                                        2. Splitting lemma
   For an integer k, a graph G with at least two vertices is k-edge-connected if for every X ⊆
V (G) with 0 < |X| < |V (G)|, G has at least k edges having one end in X and the other end in
V (G) \ X. We omit the proof of the following lemma.
Lemma 3 (Fleischner’s splitting lemma [6, Lemma III.26]). Let G be a 2-edge-connected graph,
let v be a vertex of degree at least four, and let e1 , e2 , e3 be distinct edges incident with v. For
each i, let vi be the other end of ei . If G − {e1 , e2 , e3 } is connected, then at least one of
                       (G − {e1 , e2 }) + v1 v2   and   (G − {e2 , e3 }) + v2 v3
is 2-edge-connected.
   If G is 3-edge-connected and d(v) ≥ 4, then the three edges in Lemma 3 can always be
chosen. If G − v is connected, a spanning tree of G − v together with one edge incident with v
is a spanning tree of G that omits at least three edges incident with v. If G − v is disconnected,
every component of G − v sends at least three edges to v. Taking a spanning tree in each
component and one edge from that component to v again gives a spanning tree of G that omits
at least three edges incident with v. Any three such omitted edges have the required property.

          3. Minimum counterexample to the cycle double cover conjecture
  We say a graph is cubic if every vertex has degree 3. The following reduction is well known,
see Zhang [22].
Proposition 4. The cycle double cover conjecture is true if and only if it is true for cubic 3-
edge-connected graphs.
Proof. Suppose the cycle double cover conjecture is false, and let G be a counterexample with
|E(G)| minimum. Deleting a loop and covering it twice shows that G has no loops, and con-
sidering components shows that G is connected.
   We first show that G is 3-edge-connected. Otherwise it has a 2-edge-cut {e1 , e2 }. Contract e1 ,
and let w be the resulting vertex. Contraction cannot create a bridge, so G/e1 is a smaller
bridgeless graph and hence has a cycle double cover C by minimality.
   Consider the two shores of G − {e1 , e2 }. If e2 is not a loop in G/e1 , a cycle of C containing e2
passes through w from one shore to the other, and a cycle not containing e2 cannot do so. Split w
back into the ends of e1 and insert e1 into the two cycle occurrences containing e2 ; all other cycles
lift unchanged. If e2 is a loop in G/e1 , replace its two occurrences instead by two copies of the
2-cycle formed by e1 and e2 . In either case we obtain a cycle double cover of G, a contradiction.
Thus G is 3-edge-connected.
   Suppose that d(v) ≥ 4 for some vertex v. Choose e1 , e2 , e3 as above and apply Lemma 3. Let
H be the resulting 2-edge-connected graph with |E(H)| < |E(G)|, so by minimality it has a
          A PROOF OF THE CYCLE DOUBLE COVER CONJECTURE BY OPENAI: AN EXPOSITION                    3

cycle double cover. Replacing each occurrence of its new edge by the corresponding two-edge
path through v, and decomposing closed trails into cycles if necessary, gives a cycle double
cover of G, a contradiction. Hence G is cubic.                                            □

                                   4. Disjoint spanning trees
Theorem 5 (Tutte [20] and Nash-Williams [14]). A graph G has t edge-disjoint spanning trees
if and only if for every partition V1 , . . . , Vm of V (G) with nonempty parts, there are at least
t(m − 1) edges joining distinct parts.
  One of the easiest ways to prove Theorem 5 is to use the matroid union theorem. There are
purely graph-theoretic proofs known [13]. We omit its proof.
Corollary 6. If G is 2k-edge-connected, then G has k edge-disjoint spanning trees.
Proof. Let V1 , . . . , Vm be a partition of V (G) with nonempty parts. Each set Vi has at least 2k
edges in its edge boundary. When summing these for all i, we count each crossing edge twice.
Therefore, the number of edges joining distinct parts of V1 , . . . , Vm is at least km, which is at
least k(m − 1).                                                                                   □

                                     9. More conjectures
9.1. 5-cycle double cover conjecture. A k-cycle double cover (k-CDC) is a collection of at
most k Eulerian subgraphs such that every edge is in exactly two of them.
  The proof of Theorem 1 also yields the following.
Theorem 16. Every bridgeless graph has an 8-cycle double cover.
  Since the Peterson graph is not 3-edge-colorable, not all graphs have a 4-cycle double cover
by the following theorem.
Theorem 17 (Jaeger [10]). The following are equivalent for loopless cubic graphs G.
    (i) G is 3-edge-colorable.
   (ii) G has a 3-cycle double cover.
  (iii) G has a 4-cycle double cover.
Proof. (iii)→(ii): If G has a 4-cycle double cover C1 , C2 , C3 , C4 , then C1 △C2 , C1 △C3 , C1 △C4
is a 3-cycle double cover.
   (ii)→(i): Let C1 , C2 , C3 be a 3-cycle double cover. Color each edge by the unique index
i ∈ {1, 2, 3} for which e ∈ / Ci . At every vertex, each Ci has degree two: its degree is even and
the sum of these three degrees is six. Thus each Ci omits exactly one of the three incident edges.
Since every edge is omitted by exactly one Ci , the three incident edges receive distinct colors.
Hence this is a proper 3-edge-coloring.
   (i)→(iii): Let M1 , M2 , M3 be the three color classes of a proper 3-edge-coloring. They are
perfect matchings forming a partition of E(G). The Eulerian subgraphs
                             M1 △M2 ,        M2 △M3 ,        M1 △M3
form a 3-cycle double cover: an edge in Mi belongs to precisely the two symmetric differences
containing Mi . This is also a 4-cycle double cover.                                       □
  There is a conjecture that one can reduce 8 to 5.
Conjecture 18 (Celmins [3], Preissmann [16]). Every bridgeless graph has a 5-cycle double
cover.
9.2. Orientable cycle double cover conjecture. An orientable cycle double cover of a graph G
is a list of Eulerian subgraphs, each equipped with a directed Eulerian orientation, such that
every edge of G is used exactly once in each direction. An orientable k-cycle double cover of
a graph G is an orientable cycle double cover with at most k Eulerian subgraphs.
Conjecture 19 (Archdeacon [1] and Jaeger [11]). Every bridgeless graph admits an orientable
5-cycle double cover.

9.3. Berge-Fulkerson conjecture. An m-cycle k-cover is a list of m Eulerian subgraphs such
that every edge belongs to exactly k of them.
Theorem 21 (Bermond, Jackson, and Jaeger [2]). Every bridgeless graph has a 7-cycle 4-cover.
Proof. It suffices to prove the claim for each connected component of G. Thus, after covering
any loops twice, we may assume that G is 2-edge-connected. By Theorem 9, let C1 , C2 , C3 be
the supports of the three coordinates of a nowhere-zero F32 -flow. Every edge belongs to at least
one of these Eulerian subgraphs. The seven nonzero linear combinations
                        C1 , C2 , C3 , C1 △C2 , C2 △C3 , C1 △C3 , C1 △C2 △C3
form a 7-cycle 4-cover, because every nonzero vector of F32 has dot product 1 with exactly four
of the seven nonzero vectors of F32 .                                                        □
    By using the 6-flow theorem of Seymour [18], Fan [5] showed the following.

Conjecture 23 (Berge and Fulkerson [7]). Every bridgeless cubic graph admits 6 perfect match-

   If we take the complement of each perfect matching, we deduce the following equivalent form
