                                                   Abstract. Graphical functions are single-valued complex functions which
                                                   arise from Feynman amplitudes. We study their properties and use their con-
                                                   nection to multiple polylogarithms to calculate Feynman periods. For the zig-
                                                   zag and two more families of φ4 periods we give exact results modulo products.
                                                   These periods are proved to be expressible as integer linear combinations of
                                                   single-valued multiple polylogarithms evaluated at one. For the larger family
                                                   of ’constructible’ graphs we give an algorithm that allows one to calculate their
                                                   periods by computer algebra. The theory of graphical functions is used in [18]
                                                   to prove the zig-zag conjecture.

1.7. Constructible graphs. We have seen that permuting the labeled vertices
and appending an edge to z maps B into B. A third operation that is trivially of

contributes non-trivially to the graphical function only when it connects 0 or 1 to
z. In this case the graphical function picks up a factor of 1/zz or 1/(z − 1)(z − 1),
respectively.

1.10. Two dimensions. A special case are graphical functions in two dimensions.
Whereas in higher dimensions propagators correspond to bosonic particles, in two
dimension we consider holomorphic and antiholomorphic propagators which are
more closely related to fermionic particles. A definition of two-dimensional graphi-
cal functions is given in §5. In 5.12 it is conjectured that the maximum weight piece
of the periods of graphical functions in two dimensions reduce modulo products to
the sum of two cell zeta values [13].

Remark 3.35. Primitive constructible n loop periods are in Hsv (Z)2n−3 . The cru-

the integral (reflecting the renormalization scheme dependence of the period). It is
possible to prove that primitive constructible graphs fulfill (3.38):
   In the process of constructing the graph—up to swapping the labels 0 and 1—one
of the five completed primitive graphs in figure 14 occur after we have appended the
edge connected to z. In the following situations (and only in these) an integrand is
generated that is not compatible with (3.38).

z and ∞ and simultaneously swap 0 and 1. We obtain a factor of |z − 1|−4 if the
constructible graph has an edge that connects the vertices z and 1 (after the swap).
Because the completed graph is 4-regular we have four edges that cut the graph: the
two edges leading into 0 and ∞ (after the swap) and the two edges leading out of z
and 1. A non-trivial four edge cut is equivalent to the graph having a subdivergence.
An analogous situation arises if 0 and 1 are interchanged leading to a subdivergent
factor of |z|−4 .
   In the third graph we may have to swap z and 1 and simultaneously ∞ and 0
to append an edge to 1. If thereafter the graph has an edge connecting z and ∞
this edge will be invisible in the graphical function and we obtain a trivial denom-
inator 1 (which is not compatible with (3.38)). This again leads to a graph with a
subdivergence. The same situation arises if 0 and 1 are interchanged.
   Appending an edge to any of the vertices of graph one, or (without restriction
by choice of order) to the rightmost vertex in graphs four and five always lead to
(3.38).

Summary. Completed primitive φ4 graphs with vertex connectivity three reduce
to products of lower loop order graphs. For irreducible φ4 periods we have:
   The zig-zag periods exhaust the irreducible primitive φ4 periods up to 5 loops.
Their periods are proved to all orders in [18] as (1.3). The type A and B periods
start to differ from the zig-zags at 6 loops where A2,0 = B2,0 = P6,2 in [31]. The
type A and B periods can be calculated and reduced to a standard MZV basis up
to 12 loops with polylog procedures. The first constructible periods which are
neither zig-zags nor type A or B arise at nine loops. Constructible periods can
be calculated and reduced to a standard basis up to ten loops with the period
command in polylog procedures. They are in Hsv (Z).
   MZV periods (possibly not in Hsv (Z)) which are not constructible arise first at
six loops as P6,3 and P6,4 in [31]. The period P6,3 was calculated by E. Panzer in
2012 by implementing the theory of F. Brown on integration in parametric space
[10]. The period P6,4 was calculated in [29]. At seven loops exact numerical methods
showed that (at least) eight of the nine periods are MZVs. Their results are in [7],
[31] and [6]. The missing seven loop period is a likely candidate for a cube root of
unity extension of MZVs [17]. At eight loops exact numerical methods showed that
at least 16 out of at most 31 periods are MZVs.
   Conjectured non-MZV type periods start at eight loops where four periods with
higher dimensional geometries were found. They are P8,37 , P8,38 , P8,39 , P8,41 in
[31]. All geometries are modular [17]. The case P8,37 was studied in detail in [15].
Its period is by standard transcendentality conjectures not of MZV type [14].
   Periods with geometries which are not modular (of small level) first appear at
nine loops. Their periods are conjectured to be not of MZV-type. At very high
loop order most periods are of this type.
