329015: A natural strategy is for Gentle to use a fresh colour on each turn. Because Aut(G) has odd order (Cauchy's theorem under the no-involution hypothesis), any stabilizer of a final colour class of size two fixes its two vertices individually. This suggests maintaining singleton/doubleton colours and shrinking stabilizers. The flaw is that Rascal can later add a third vertex to a previous doubleton colour class; an odd-order permutation can then rotate that triple. The final colouring stabilizer need not be a subgroup of the stabilizer of an earlier partial colouring. Thus a greedy point-stabilizer argument does not establish monotonic progress. The source proves only the prime-order group case in Proposition 2.3. A full route needs a strategy invariant surviving all later class enlargements, or a finite odd-order permutation action whose equality-pattern game is losing. No such invariant or witness was obtained. Do not run a graph sweep: reduce first to a faithful odd-order permutation group action and justify the finite-colour/equality-pattern reduction for both starting players. A game certificate would list legal states, all Rascal replies, Gentle's response and terminal stabilizers, and require an explicit graph realizing the action. No certificate exists here.

329533 / 329534: Let M_N(Q) count symmetric binary matrices with row sums at most one by the number of ones on/above the diagonal. Splitting at the last row/column gives

    M_N = (1+Q)M_(N−1) + (N−1)Q M_(N−2), M_0=1, M_1=1+Q.

The three cases are unused last vertex, a diagonal one, or a matched off-diagonal pair. For fixed weight i, choosing j off-diagonal pairs and i−j loops gives

    [Q^i]M_N = sum_j N! / ((N−i−j)! (i−j)! j! 2^j),

where 0≤j≤min(i,N−i). This is a finite exact combinatorial identity, not a proof about the Type A sum. The source's Definition 4.4 defines each component as a product of q-integers and inverse factors over a noncrossing diagram, with Q exponent number of up arrows plus arcs. At q=1 the missing semantic bridge is a bijection or sum identity equating the weighted component sum to M_N. It cannot be assumed from the matching analogy. Complementing loops at unmatched vertices gives M_N palindromic; it does not prove the Type A/BIII equality. A possible next symbolic task is to derive the displayed recurrence directly from the complete diagram sum, keeping all normalization factors, and then separately prove that BIII at q=1 is M_N(Q+Q^−1). Neither bridge is proved here. The source's printed Conjectures 6.6–6.7 and small polynomials were checked; reproducing them is zero credit. No Pi implementation is recommended before these bridges and later Shigechi literature are qualified.

329135: A finite counterexample, if one exists, can be certified without an infinite search. A finite directive word defines iterated palindromic closures P_0=empty, P_(j+1)=(P_j a_j)^(+). Extend the directive with a recurrent tail using all letters; each P_j persists as a prefix of a standard episturmian word. A binary projection of a finite factor with fewer than length+1 distinct palindromic factors disproves richness of that infinite projected word. The certificate would be the directive, every closure step, the projection, factor indices and the complete palindrome set. However no bad factor is known here, and this is not an executable candidate backed by a witness. The ternary result in the source does not extend by grouping several letters: the projected word need not remain episturmian, and palindromic closure does not commute with identifying letters. Current Omega episturmian matches concern Diophantine exponent / bounded powers rather than binary projection richness; only the exact criterion endpoint and selected dependency portions were read, not an exhaustive dependency audit. No search is requested.
