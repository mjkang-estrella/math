#### 360008 | The weak Weyl-sum conjecture for prime-denominator incomplete sums | open | conjecture | year 2023 | src https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11590 | sols 0
tags ['exponential-sums', 'finite-fields', 'warings-problem']
STATEMENT(644):
Let $d,N\in\mathbb N$, let $q$ be a prime number with

$$
q\in[N^{d/2},N^d],
$$

and let $t,x\in\mathbb R$. Suppose $1\leq a\leq q-1$ and

$$
\left|t-\frac aq\right|\leq\frac1{q^2}.
$$

**Weak Weyl-sum conjecture.** There is a constant $C_{d,\varepsilon}>0$ such that, for every $\varepsilon>0$,

$$
\left|\sum_{n=1}^N e^{2\pi i t n^d+2\pi i xn}\right|\leq C_{d,\varepsilon}N^\varepsilon q^{1/d}.
$$

This is presented as a weak version of the sharp Weyl-sum conjecture. Because $q$ is much larger than $N$, the source notes its equivalence to the corresponding sum with $t$ replaced by $a/q$; the incomplete finite-field sum remains difficult.

STATEMENT(693):
Let $\boldsymbol u=\langle u_n\rangle_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}$ be a simple rational LRBS taking values in $\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{b}]$ for some non-zero integer $b$. A sequence has **no zero** when $u_n\neq 0$ for every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. **Skolem's exponential local-global principle.** The sequence $\boldsymbol u$ has no zero if and only if there is an integer $m\geq 2$ with $\gcd(b,m)=1$ such that $u_n\not\equiv 0\pmod m$ for every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. This principle says that the absence of an integer zero in a simple rational LRBS should be witnessed by a single modulus; it is introduced as a conjecture motivating the Bi-Skolem Problem, and its status is not resolved in the supplied source.

STATEMENT(738):
Let $\xi$ be a random variable supported on finitely many points. For $k\geq 1$, let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{k+1}$ be independent samples of $\xi$, and let $M_n(\xi)$ be the associated random matrix. **General corank conjecture.** For every $\epsilon>0$, there is $n_0=n_0(\xi,k,\epsilon)$ such that, for $n\geq n_0$,

$$
\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{corank}M_n(\xi)\geq k]\leq(\mathbb{P}[\xi=0]+\epsilon)^{kn}+(\mathbb{P}[\xi_1=\cdots=\xi_{k+1}]+\epsilon)^n.
$$

The conjecture gives a general upper bound for the probability that a finite-support random matrix has corank at least $k$, extending the proved Bernoulli estimate and accounting for both zero rows and repeated-value configurations. Its status is not resolved in the supplied source.

STATEMENT(504):
Let $p>2$ be prime, and let $A'$ and $B'$ be coprime integers. A Ramanujan-type congruence for Euler's totient function $cvarphi$ would have the form

$$
\varphi(A'n+B')\equiv0\,\,({\rm mod}\,\,{p})

#### 344707 | Guo–Schlosser's supercongruence for primes congruent to 2 modulo 3 | open | conjecture | year 2019 | src https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00663 | sols 0
tags ['hypergeometric-series', 'number-theory', 'supercongruences']
STATEMENT(453):
Let $(x)_0=1$ and $(x)_k=x(x+1)\cdots(x+k-1)$ denote the Pochhammer symbol. **Guo–Schlosser's supercongruence.** For every prime $p\equiv 2\pmod{3}$, one has

$$
\sum_{k=0}^{(p+1)/3}(6k-1)\frac{\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)_k^4(1)_{2k}}{(1)_k^4\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)_{2k}}\equiv p\pmod{p^3}.
$$

This is a supercongruence for a truncated hypergeometric series. It was proposed by Guo and Schlosser and later confirmed by Jana and Kalita, so it is solved.

#### 338173 | Cluckers' quasi-homogeneous generalization of Igusa's conjecture | open | conjecture | year 2018 | src https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.02910 | sols 0
tags ['exponential-sums', 'number-theory', 'quasi-homogeneous-polynomials']
STATEMENT(788):
Let $f\in\mathbf{Z}[x]$ be a non-constant quasi-homogeneous polynomial in $n$ variables, let $\sigma_0(f)$ denote its log canonical threshold at the origin, and define

$$
S_f(p,m):=\frac{1}{p^{mn}}\sum_{x\in\{0,\ldots,p^m-1\}^n}\exp\left(2\pi\mathbf{i}\frac{f(x)}{p^m}\right).
$$

**Cluckers' generalization of Igusa's conjecture.** There exists a constant $c\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}$ such that

$$
\lvert S_f(p,m)\rvert\leq c p^{-\sigma_0(f)m}m^{n-1}
$$

for all primes $p$ and all integers $m\geq1$.

This strengthens Igusa's conjecture from homogeneous to quasi-homogeneous polynomials. The surrounding text reports that Cluckers proved this bound for non-constant quasi-homogeneous non-degenerate polynomials, so the stated strengthening remains open without the non-degeneracy hypothesis.

STATEMENT(705):
Let $\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. A real number is **$\frac{1}{q^2\cdot\log^A q}$-badly approximable** if there is a positive constant, depending on the number, such that its rational approximations satisfy the corresponding lower bound with denominator $q$. **Lang's conjecture.** For every $\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, there exists a constant $A=A(\alpha)>0$ such that $\alpha$ is $\frac{1}{q^2\cdot\log^{A}q}$-badly approximable. STATEMENT(576):
For an integer $N\ge 1$, let $f(n+1,N)$ be the least positive value of

$$
\left|1+\zeta_1+\cdots+\zeta_n\right|
$$

where $\zeta_1^N=\cdots=\zeta_n^N=1$.

**Polynomial lower-bound conjecture.** For given $n\ge 1$ there exist constants $c(n)>0$ and $\lambda(n)>0$ such that

$$
f(n+1,N)\ge c(n)N^{-\lambda(n)}
$$

for all $N\ge 1$.

Polynomial lower bounds are known in some small cases, while the general case, in particular for $n\ge 4$, is presented as unknown. The conjecture is described as folklore and would follow from a positive answer to a question posed by Myerson.

#### 325848 | The zero-digit conjecture for products in base q | open | conjecture | year 2013 | src https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1188 | sols 0
tags ['digit-functions', 'number-theory']
STATEMENT(542):
Fix an integer base $q>1$. A product of $k$ digits greater than $1$ for base $q$ means a product of $k$ elements of $\{2,\ldots,q-1\}$. **Zero-digit conjecture for products in base $q$.** There exists a positive integer $k_0(q)$ such that, for every $k\geq k_0(q)$, the base-$q$ expansion of every product of $k$ digits greater than $1$ contains at least one digit equal to zero.
