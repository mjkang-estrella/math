                                                                                      1       Introduction

                                                 A graph G = (V, E) has a vertex set V = V (G) and an edge set E = E(G). The
                                           elements of V and E are called vertices and edges, respectively. If G has n vertices and m
                                           edges, then G has order n and size m.
                                                 The Fibonacci sequence, introduced in the 13th century by Leonardo of Pisa (Fibonacci)
                                           in his study of idealized rabbit populations, has since become one of the most celebrated
                                           sequences in mathematics due to its elegant recursive structure and deep arithmetic pro-
                                           perties. In this paper, we define the (a, b)-Fibonacci sequence that satisfies the following
                                           recursive relation:
                                                                     F0 = a,      F1 = b,     Fn = Fn−2 + Fn−1 for n ≥ 2,
                                           where F0 = a and F1 = b are called initial values. Throughout this paper, we assume
                                           that (a, b) ̸= (0, 0). Hence, (0, 1)-Fibonacci sequence is the classical Fibonacci sequence and
                                           (2, 1)-Fibonacci sequence is the classical Lucas sequence.
                                                  The Legendre symbol, introduced by Adrien-Marie Legendre in the late 18th century,
                                           serves as a compact notation for determining whether an integer is a quadratic residue of an
                                           odd prime p, taking the value
                                                                           
                                                                           
                                                                             1    if a is a quadratic residue of p,
                                                                           
                                                                   (a/p) = −1 if a is a quadratic nonresidue of p,
                                                                           
                                                                           
                                                                              0    if a ≡ 0(mod p).
                                                                           
One important property of the Legendre symbol is that if a and b are integers and a ≡
b(mod p), then (a/p) = (b/p).
       These two classical mathematical concepts play important roles across number theory:
Fibonacci numbers appear in combinatorics, algebra, and modular recurrences, while the
Legendre symbol underlies quadratic reciprocity, primality tests, and the structure of con-
gruences. Together, their interaction gives rise to intriguing problems involving periodicity
and arithmetic behavior modulo primes.

Conjecture 4.1. For any integers a and b with (a, b) ̸= (0, 0), there exists a real number c
with 0 < c < 1 such that ζ(a,b) (k) > ck 2 for all k ∈ χ(a, b).
