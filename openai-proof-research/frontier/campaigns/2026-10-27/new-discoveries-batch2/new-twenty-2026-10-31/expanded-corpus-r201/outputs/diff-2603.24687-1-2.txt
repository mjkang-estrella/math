+ Appendix A. A strengthening of the Boone–Higman–Thompson theorem
+   Recall from Remark 5.6 the Boone–Higman–Thompson theorem, that a finitely gener-
+ated group has solvable word problem if and only if it embeds in a finitely generated sim-
+ple group that embeds in a finitely presented group [BH74, Tho80]. In this appendix we
+slightly strengthen this theorem, and also discuss connections to twisted Brin–Thompson
+groups and the Boone–Higman conjecture. The statement can be phrased in a number of
+equivalent ways: the following is left to the reader.
+Lemma A.1. Let Γ ≤ P be groups. Then the following are equivalent.
+   • Every non-identity element of Γ normally generates P .
+   • Every proper normal subgroup of P intersects Γ trivially.
+   • Every non-trivial quotient of P is injective on Γ.                                            □
+  We will need the following lemma.
+Lemma A.2. Let Γ be a finitely presented group and 1 ̸= g ∈ Γ. Then there exists a
+finitely presented group P containing Γ, such that g normally generates P .
+Proof. First embed Γ into a finitely generated simple group G, for instance the twisted
+Brin–Thompson group ΓVΓ . We claim that there exists a finitely presented cover P → G
+satisfying the hypotheses. Indeed, since Γ is finitely presented, only finitely many relations
+from G suffice to ensure that Γ embeds. Moreover, g normally generates G since G is
+simple, that is, every generator of G is a product of conjugates of powers of g, and this
+can again be expressed by only finitely many relations.                                     □
+Remark A.3. Recently, Chatterji–Kassabov proved that P can moreover be chosen to
+have property (T) [CK], hence property (T) can be assumed of the container group in
+Proposition A.4. In fact, via an easy construction using small cancellation theory over
+acylindrically hyperbolic groups [Hul16], the container group P may be chosen to be a
+quotient of any given finitely presented acylindrically hyperbolic group. Since these ad-
+ditional properties are not relevant to us, we only need Lemma A.2, for which we could
+present a self-contained elementary proof.
+  The main result of this appendix is the following.
+Proposition A.4. Let Γ be a finitely generated group. Then Γ has solvable word problem
+if and only if there exists a finitely presented group P such that Γ embeds as a subgroup of
+P trivially intersecting every proper normal subgroup of P .
++42                    F. FOURNIER-FACIO, X. WU, AND M. C. B. ZAREMSKY
+
+Proof. First suppose Γ has solvable word problem, so by the Boone–Higman–Thompson
+theorem there exist a finitely generated simple group G and a finitely presented group P
+such that Γ ≤ G ≤ P . By Lemma A.2, there exists a finitely presented group Q containing
+P such that an element 1 ̸= g ∈ G normally generates Q. Since G is simple, it follows
+that every non-identity element in G, hence in particular in Γ, normally generates Q. We
+conclude by Lemma A.1.
+   Now suppose Γ is a subgroup of a finitely presented group P such that Γ trivially
+intersects every non-trivial normal subgroup of P . We must show that Γ has solvable word
+problem.
+   We follow the classical Kuznetsov algorithm [Kuz58], which treated the case when P
+is simple. Fix a finite presentation ⟨S | R⟩ for P , and fix a word w in the alphabet S
+representing an element of Γ. We run a “day and night” algorithm, which returns “yes” if
+w represents the identity, and “no” otherwise.
+   During the day, we enumerate all relations in the presentation ⟨S | R⟩, and check whether
+w appears as one of them. If it does, then the algorithm stops and returns “yes”.
+   During the night, we enumerate all relations in the presentation ⟨S | R∪{w}⟩, and check
+whether each generator in S appears as a relation. If they all do, then the algorithm stops
+and returns “no”.
+   We claim that this algorithm terminates. Indeed, if w represents the identity, then it
+must eventually appear in an enumeration of all the relations. Otherwise, w represents an
+element in Γ \ {1}, hence a normal generator of P , by Lemma A.1. Therefore ⟨S | R ∪ {w}⟩
+is a presentation for the trivial group. Thus, all the generators in S must eventually appear
+in an enumeration of all the relations of this new presentation, and we are done.          □
+  This immediately gives us the following strengthening of the Boone–Higman–Thompson
+theorem:
+Corollary A.5. A finitely generated group Γ has solvable word problem if and only if it
+embeds in a finitely generated simple group G that embeds in a finitely presented group P ,
+such that G normally generates P .                                                      □
+   The relative Boone–Higman conjecture amounts to saying that the group P in Proposi-
+tion A.4 can always be taken to be relatively simple, so conjecturally the statement about
+trivially intersecting every proper normal subgroup boils down to just trivially intersecting
+one proper normal subgroup (the largest). Note that if Γ is infinite, then since it embeds
+in every non-trivial quotient of P by Lemma A.1, it follows that P has no non-trivial finite
+quotients. Thus a Zorn’s lemma argument going back to Higman [Hig51] shows that P
+admits a (finitely generated) simple quotient. If one could show that, in this situation,
+P admits a finitely presented simple quotient, then this would prove the Boone–Higman
+conjecture.
