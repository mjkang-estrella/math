                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                              The study of integer partitions was initiated by Euler who showed the generating function
                                           of the numbers of partitions as an infinite product formula. Later, mathematicians inten-
                                           sively studied various aspects of integer partitions, including their relations to combinatorics,
                                           representation theory, and mathematical physics. For example, the irreducible representa-
                                           tions of symmetric groups, as well as the polynomial representations of general linear groups,
                                           can be indexed by partitions. The dimension of representations can be computed by using
                                           the hook-lengths of corresponding partitions. In 2006, Nekrasov and Okounkov [17] pro-
                                           vided a formula when they studied the Seiberg–Witten theory and random partitions. This
                                           formula relates a sum over products of hook-lengths of partitions to the powers of Euler’s
                                           infinite product formula. Various new proofs and generalizations of this Nekrasov–Okounkov
                                           formula have been explored, as discussed in [9, 10, 11, 21, 25, 27] and references therein. In-
                                           spired by Nekrasov–Okounkov’s hook-length formula, and Stanley’s analogous hook-content
                                           formula [21], Amdeberhan proposed several conjectures about infinite product formulas for
                                           certain generating functions of hook-lengths and symplectic/orthogonal contents [1]. These
                                           conjectures may have interests in the representation theory of symplectic/orthogonal groups
                                           (see [6, 14, 24]) and mathematical physics, especially in integrable systems (see, for exam-
                                           ples, [12, 19]). Recently, some special cases of Amdeberhan’s conjectures were proved by
                                           Amdeberhan, Andrews and Ballantine [2]. In this paper, we will prove the general cases of
                                           Amdeberhan’s conjectures. The key point is the use of vertex operators.
                                              A partition of a nonnegative integer n isP a sequence of weakly decreasing positive integers
                                           λ = (λ1 , ..., λl ) ⊢ n satisfying n = |λ| = li=1 λi , where l is called its length. In general,
                                           one can also write λi = 0 for i > l. The conjugation of λ is a new partition λt defined
                                           by λti = #{j|λj ≥ i} for i = 1, ..., λ1 . For each pair of positive integers (i, j) ∈ λ, i.e.,
                                           1 ≤ j ≤ λi , one can define the following combination numbers related to the given partition
                                           λ. The content is c(i, j) := j − i and the hook-length is h(i, j) := λi + λtj − i − j + 1. We
                                           recommend the books [3, 15] to readers for more interesting properties of partitions. The

2.2. Inner product and the vacuum expectation value. There is a standard inner
product on the ring C[p1 , p2 , ...] by letting (sλ (p))λ∈P be a standard normalized basis (see
[15] for more details).
   Below, we introduce the notation of vacuum expectation value, which is more convenient
for our purposes (see, for example, [5]). To avoid the misuse of symbols, we employ the
(q) = (qk ) coordinates and vectors that appear in vacuum expectation value are viewed as
elements in C[q]. First, denote by |λi the Schur function sλ (q) when regarding it as a vector
in the vector space C[q], and hλ| is its dual. The vacuum |0i denotes the vector corresponding
to the Schur function labeled by the empty partition, which is the constant function 1, and
h0| is its dual. Then the vacuum expectation value is of the following form
                          hλ|A|µi := (|λi, A · |µi) = (A∗ · |λi, |µi),
where A is an operator acting on the ring C[q], and (·, ·) is the standard inner product.
