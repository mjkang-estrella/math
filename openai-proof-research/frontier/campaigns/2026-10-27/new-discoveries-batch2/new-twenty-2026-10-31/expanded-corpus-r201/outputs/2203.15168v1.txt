   In light to (1.1) and (1.2), a Rogers–Ramanujan type identity asserts the equality
of a q-summation and an infinite product. Also, on the summation side, we often
require that the summand contains a factor in which the power of q is quadratic in
the summation variable(s).
   Further, we may distinguish Rogers–Ramanujan type identities according to the
shape of the summation and product sides:
   For the summation side, we mainly focus on whether it is a single or multiple
series.
   For the product side, we say that it is periodic if there exists a positive integer
M such that the infinite product has the form

Further, for a periodic Rogers–Ramanujan type identity, we say that it is symmetric
if the powers δm satisfy δm = δM−m for all 1 ≤ m ≤ M − 1, and asymmetric
otherwise.
   Most known Rogers–Ramanujan type identities are symmetric. In particular, all
130 identities in Slater’s list are single-symmetric, and the infinite products in them
may be deduced through Jacobi’s triple product identity or the quintuple product
identity. There are also many multiple-symmetric identities of Rogers–Ramanujan
type, among which the most important one is the generalization of (1.1) and (1.2)
due to G. E. Andrews [1] and B. Gordon [11]:

4. Conclusion
The entire surprise comes from the single-asymmetric Rogers–Ramanujan type
identity (1.5), which is completely weird, as pointed out by George Andrews in
a personal communication. Although its a-generalization (1.6) presents a similar
shape to the Lebesgue identity (1.3) or the Corteel–Savage–Sills identity (1.4), the
unexpectedness rises as two extra q-shifted factorials are attached to the summand
in (1.6) in comparison to the original (1.5). It is also natural to ask if there are other
instances that behave as “unreasonable” as (1.5). A thorough computer search will
be carried out in a forthcoming project.
