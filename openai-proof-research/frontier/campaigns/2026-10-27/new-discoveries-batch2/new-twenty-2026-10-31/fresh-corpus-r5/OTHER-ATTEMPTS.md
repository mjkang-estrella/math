# Selected unresolved targets

## 326519: unbounded orbital/chromatic root separation

Read arXiv:1310.3792v1, definitions before Equation (1), Theorem 1.2 and its full proof, Proposition 2.3 and Example 2.4, Reduction Lemma 3.1 and proof, and Conjecture 4.2. This target is NOT the already disproved Cameron upper-bound question. It asks arbitrarily large **positive separation**, not merely orbital roots beyond ordinary roots. Current primary searches did not locate a full later answer; registry/title checks have the limits in PROOF.md and receipts.

Approach A: amplify the source's C6 involution example. For the source's rooted H_{n,s} attachment, the chromatic factor per vertex is f(x)=(x−1)…(x−n+1)(x−n)^s. Suppressing identity terms at x0 uses n=floor(x0). This introduces an ordinary chromatic root n. The sign certificate at x0 guarantees a separation x0−n<1, irrespective of how large x0 is. For the C6 construction n=1, all orbital summands are nonnegative at x≥2, so even its true separation is bounded by 1. Increasing attachment multiplicity does not prove the target.

Approach B: graph products. Disjoint union with a product action multiplies the orbital polynomials; the largest real roots of both products are maxima of the component largest roots, which cannot amplify a uniform gap. Joining with a fixed clique shifts both root sets equally (and adds small integer roots), again preserving the gap. These are exact algebraic obstructions, not failed numerical sweeps.

Approach C: clique blow-up C6[K_m] with the antipodal involution. Its quotient is K_{3m}, promising a quotient chromatic root 3m−1 while the base may have a smaller largest real root. But a large quotient root does not supply a negative value of the **orbit average**: the degree-6m identity summand can dominate the degree-3m quotient term. Reusing Theorem 1.2 to suppress it restores the floor(x0) root and loses the large gap. No sign inequality or alternative low-root attachment survives this obstruction. A decisive missing lemma is a family of rooted attachments with chromatic factor at a high x0 small enough to suppress identity contributions while all real attachment roots stay arbitrarily far below x0, or a direct orbit-average family with the required sign. No Lean endpoint is claimed.

## 325921: GT projection-fibre polynomiality

Read both arXiv:1307.3999v1 (15 July 2013) and the current v3 (27 June 2014). v1 Section 3 defines φ as lexicographic floor to stretched integral patterns, ψ_k=τ_k^{-1}φ, and Conjecture 16 is the literal fibre question. The phrase 'inverse image under ψ_k^{-1}' is source notation for the fibre, not an invertible map assumption. Read v1 Lemma 13/Corollary 14 and proof, Proposition 15 proof; current Lemma 6, Corollary 19/Proposition 20, and final Conjectures 21–22. The current version instead asks polynomiality of cumulative p_G(k)=#{H≤lex kG}. Therefore the record's description as a 'later formulation' is misleading, but that alone earns zero credit.

Approach A: Ehrhart interpretation. Lexicographic truncation partitions the GT polytope into finitely many relatively open rational slices. General rational Ehrhart theory yields quasi-polynomial behavior, not period one. Integrality of ordinary GT polytopes without the fixed row-sum/type constraints does not prove integrality after imposing those constraints. No proof of cancellation of the periods was found. Known polynomiality of the **total** stretched Kostka number cannot be distributed across fibres without an additional argument.

Approach B: prove equivalence with the revised conjecture. Order the base patterns G_1<…<G_m and let F_i(k) be their lex-floor fibre sizes. Corollary 14 ensures the stretched maximum is kG_m and lies in the final fibre alone. Hence F_m=1 and F_i=p_{i+1}−p_i for i<m. Conversely p_i=1+Σ_{j<i}F_j. Thus the old and revised all-pattern polynomiality assertions are equivalent. This resolves the version bridge, but does not prove either conjecture.

Approach C: exact bounded diagnostics in `diagnostics.py` enumerate two fixed shapes for k=1,…,6, with exact interlacing and row sums. They verify the fibre/cumulative identities, but provide neither a counterexample nor general evidence with discovery credit. No broad sweep or polynomial interpolation is presented as proof. The decisive lemma is a period-one theorem for these lexicographic truncations (or an explicit shape with proved nonconstant residue dependence). No ordinary full proof survived, so no partial Lean file is passed off as an endpoint.
