## 338640: literal minimum comparison is mathematically sound

Source: Bovdi–Leung, https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03457, PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03457, header **1803.03457v1, 9 March 2018**, SHA256 `16f59e138804ca930faa14fe2421274937ea4ca0377fef539eca29e60503e69e`. I read Section 3 pp.5–8: the definition, Fano lines, Lemma 3, ten classes, Theorem 1 and its proof, cardinal formula, and Conjecture 2. The primary claim asks minimum among **all** m-bicommutative systems in P_n(m), for n=4k+7, m=2k+3, k>=2. A counterexample at k=2 suffices. It does not require a minimum witness or refutation at every k.

An s-bicommutative system is exactly an intersecting family of s-subsets such that every outside s-subset is disjoint from some member. In particular the paper explicitly distinguishes this from an algebraic system: adding even disjoint sets is not a closure requirement here. No Grassmann-algebra closure, field characteristic, complement-pair rule, symmetry, invariance, Fano structure, or restricted maximality is imposed on competitor families. Oddness is satisfied by m=7. Complementation is merely a way to generate neighbors, not an extra axiom on the witness.

Here is the full source identification at k=2 after subtracting one from source point labels. Put D={0,...,7}, C={8,...,14}. The seven ordered Fano lines are

    {8,9,12}, {8,10,13}, {8,11,14}, {9,10,14},
    {10,11,12}, {12,13,14}, {9,11,13}.

For i=1,...,7, B_i={L_i union T: T subset D, |T|=4}. B_8={R union T: R subset C, |R|=2, T subset D, |T|=5}. B_9 uses |R|=1, |T|=6. B_10 consists of the seven-subsets of D. The common restriction B_i subset P_15(7) supplies the cardinalities implicit in the source's first seven and tenth definitions. These ten classes are disjoint; their sizes are 70 seven times, 1176, 196, and 8. Their union is exactly the seven-subsets whose core intersection has size <=2 or is one of the seven lines. Hence

    |A_7| = 7*C(8,4) + C(7,2)*C(8,5) + 7*C(8,6) + C(8,7) = 1870.

Theorem 1's proof uses Fano intersections for the first seven classes and pigeonhole intersection in D for the other pairs; outside core sizes 7,6,5,4,3 are blocked respectively using B_10, B_10, B_9, B_8, or a disjoint Fano line together with the D-complement. This establishes the source construction's role without modifying its quantifiers. My check additionally verifies its intersection and maximality directly.

Interpreting each submitted integer mask literally as a subset of Fin 15, I independently checked 1505 distinct seven-subsets, all 1,131,760 unordered distinct pairs, and all C(15,7)=6435 seven-subsets. Every pair intersects; self-intersection follows from size seven. For every one of 4930 outside sets I checked the submitted disjointness witness and independently found a member disjoint from it. The saved map is complete. Thus the finite verification supplies an ordinary exact certificate of an m-bicommutative F with |F|=1505<1870. There is no missing maximality bridge at the ordinary level. The integer-mask representation is injective on 15-bit masks and bitwise AND is intersection. The conclusion is Spec(2,2)<=1505, not equality or asymptotics.

## 339278: positive refutation verified, source interpretation unresolved

Exact sources are arXiv1805.07019v1 (18 May 2018), original Conjecture 7.2 and Proposition 7.1, and the 2025 *Games of No Chance 6* chapter, pp.405–431, Question 10.2 and Proposition 10.1. PDFs: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.07019 and https://library.slmath.org/books/Book71/files/GONC6-17.pdf; hashes respectively `6c20762f24c5eff9384ac8475e1c355f72e8f0678ae2f10228859e658f93a4ab` and `2c96e1097c701165075f61061bc015d7df32312171a6327c3a7b682172155aa4`. I read the question contexts, definitions of value/strategy, winning priority, nonnegative-pile notation and the full proposition proof. The r19 note's original-definition numbering is inaccurate: original Definitions 2.5–2.7 also contain strategy/semiratio/pile notation; this review uses the actual bodies.

At every P-position Luca moves freely, necessarily creating nonzero xor. Windsor must preserve his winning status by choosing a xor-zero successor. Such moves replace pile a_i with a_i xor x<a_i, where x is current xor: necessity and sufficiency follow by cancelling xor of all other piles. From nonzero xor at least one such pile exists (highest set bit of x). Each move strictly decreases total candies, and a nonempty P-position cannot move directly to empty. Thus alternating these roles through zero/nonzero xor implements the source winning priority exactly; it does not let Windsor sacrifice the win for candy. Since total candies are fixed, Luca minimizes Windsor's final take while Windsor maximizes it. The independent DP implements these opposite objectives, retaining labeled zero piles, and verifies every table entry by induction in total candies.

The two rectangles have 6825 and 23625 states. Every source-table signed value equals total minus twice the independently recomputed Windsor total: Windsor takes 9 from [12,20,24] and 10 from [4,8,20,24], so V=38 and 36. The logs count every legal recurrence edge; full arrays are retained. This is an exact finite verification with a transparent induction proof, not a Lean endpoint. Nonzero-xor continuation entries are Windsor-to-move values, not an assertion that arbitrary initial source games all have the same P-game convention.

The binary sum-minus-xor identity sums 2^i*(c_i-(c_i mod 2)) over bit multiplicities c_i. All terms are nonnegative, so equality forces disjoint set-bit supports. With 12=4+8, all positive parts occupy disjoint nonempty subsets of these two bits. For j>1 the unique split is {4,8}. Independent enumeration of every positive integer partition of 12 confirms this without assuming the classification. Pile permutation preserves the legal game tree, and zero deletion does also. Thus this example refutes the positive-parts MathDB statement; it is not the excluded power-of-two/no-split shortcut.

The primary source does **not literally impose positive split parts**. Definition 2.7 allows a_i>=0 in both versions, and neither question explicitly overrides it. With zeros allowed, [0,a,b,c] has exactly G's game tree after deletion of the inert heap, making the existential question true with equality for every G. The candidate cannot refute that permissive reading.

Nevertheless, rejecting solely for the absent word “positive” would overlook substantial context: Proposition 10.1 proves a nontrivial 3-to-4-pile comparison; the following general-play paragraph expressly seeks its extension; j>1 is meaningful for increasing the number of nonempty piles; and the authors' counterexample in Remark 10.3 uses five positive binary parts. The published version retains this context. These support the inference that genuine splitting was intended; they do not logically enforce positivity under the paper's explicit notation. The proposition proof itself uses zero piles as intermediate positions, so zeros cannot simply be declared globally disallowed.
