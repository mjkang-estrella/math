55: 1.2. Open problems and conjectures. The question of whether or not µk+n ≤ λk holds for non-
56: convex domains remains open.1 More precisely, given a Dirichlet eigenvalue λk , one can ask how
57: many Neumann eigenvalues, µ, exist with µ ≤ λk , and how this number depends on the geometry
58: of Ω. We investigate this when k = 1, where little is known beyond the general results described
59: above.
60: The case k = 1 is particularly important when studying nodal domains of eigenfunctions. This is
61: because any eigenvalue λ is necessarily the first Dirichlet eigenvalue on any nodal domain of its
62: associated eigenfunction. Several applications of this idea are described in Section 2.

626:                                                 5. Conclusion
627: 
628: In this paper, we numerically approximated the quantity N (Ω) for many planar domains, with
629: varying geometry and topology. In all cases, we observed that N (Ω) is controlled by the isoperi-
630: metric ratio, I(Ω). Based on these observations, we hypothesized that this relationship always
631: holds (Conjecture 1). We also suggested that this holds in higher dimensions (Conjecture 2). We
632: also discussed some implications these conjectures would have if they are indeed true. In particular,
633: our conjectures, combined with results from [13], yield a direction connection between the spectral
634: position of an eigenfunction and the isoperimetric ratio of its nodal sets.
635: We suggest that these conjecture be approached using the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map formalism in
636: [13, 16]. This approach seems promising in light of results giving isoperimetric control on Steklov
637: eigenvalues; see, for instance, [11].
638: In terms of the finite-element experiments presented in this paper, a natural generalization would
639: be the numerical study of domains in higher dimensions, where the geometry and topology can be
640: much more complicated than in the planar case studied here.
