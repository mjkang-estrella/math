31:                                                                                             Abstract
32: 
33:                                                             Bit threads provide an alternative description of holographic entanglement,
34:                                                          replacing the Ryu-Takayanagi minimal surface with bulk curves connecting pairs
35:                                                          of boundary points. We use bit threads to prove the monogamy of mutual in-
36:                                                          formation (MMI) property of holographic entanglement entropies. This is ac-
37:                                                          complished using the concept of a so-called multicommodity flow, adapted from
38:                                                          the network setting, and tools from the theory of convex optimization. Based
39:                                                          on the bit thread picture, we conjecture a general ansatz for a holographic state,
40:                                                          involving only bipartite and perfect-tensor type entanglement, for any decompo-
41:                                                          sition of the boundary into four regions. We also give new proofs of analogous
42:                                                          theorems on networks.

84: a region in the boundary conformal field theory (CFT) is dual to a geometric extrem-

385: allows for one bit of (quantum) information to be communicated between different
386: regions on the spatial boundary. The amount of information that can be communicated
387: between two spatially separated boundary regions is then determined by the number of
388: channels that the bulk geometry allows between the two regions. Importantly, whereas
389: the maximizing bit thread configuration may change depending on the boundary region
390: we choose, the set of all allowable configurations is completely determined by the
391: geometry. The “channel” should be viewed as a metaphor, however, similar to how a
392: Bell pair can be be viewed as enabling a channel in the context of teleportation. While
393: it is known that Bell pairs can always be distilled at an optimal rate S(A), we conjecture
394: a more direct connection between bit threads and the entanglement structure of the
395: the underlying holographic states, elaborated in Section 4.

1368:    We remind the reader that, throughout this paper, we have been working in the
1369: classical, or large-N , limit of the holographic system, and we emphasize that the state
1370: decomposition conjectures stated above should be understood in this sense. Thus we
1371: are not claiming that the state takes the form (4.2) or (4.4) exactly, but rather only up
1372: to corrections that are subleading in 1/N . If we consider, for example, a case where
1373: I(A : B) = 0 at leading order, such as where A and B are well-separated regions,
1374: the three-party decomposition (4.2) would suggest that ρAB = ρA ⊗ ρB . However,
1375: even in this case I(A : B) could still be of order O(1), so we should not expect this
1376: decomposition to hold approximately in any norm, but rather in a weaker sense.
1377:    Support for these conjectures comes from tensor-network toy models of hologra-
1378: phy [19,31,34]. Specifically, it was shown in [31] that random stabilizer tensor network
1379: states at large N indeed have the form (4.2), (4.4) at leading order in 1/N . More
1380: precisely, these decompositions hold provided one traces out O(1) many degrees of
1381: freedom in each subsystem. In other words, there are other types of entanglement
1382: present (such as GHZ-type entanglement), but these make a subleading contribution
1383: to the entropies. We believe that it would be interesting to prove or disprove the state

1385: possible form of the 1/N corrections.
