## 319471: uniform random Fibonacci cut product

Use exactly Nilsson Definition 1: A_1={0}, A_2={1}, A_n=A_{n-1}A_{n-2} union A_{n-2}A_{n-1}, all words of length f_n. Write a_n=|A_n| and p_n(t)=|A_n[1,t]|; reversal invariance identifies the suffix count with p_n(f_n-t). The literal endpoint is existence of one finite real C bounding p_n(t)p_n(f_n-t)/a_n for every n and every nontrivial t. The stray empty fraction in the record is not used as a counterexample. A finite list of successful or failed bounds cannot establish unboundedness.

Route 1, sharpen the source induction. Proposition 7 gives prefix stabilization and Proposition 8 encloses A_n in a product with two unconstrained boundary bits. For t <= f_n/2, one has t < f_{n-1}, so the short prefix stabilizes to A_{n-1}. Applying Proposition 8, then completing the truncated suffix, gives
p_n(t)p_n(f_n-t) <= 4 p_{n-1}(t)p_{n-1}(f_{n-1}-t) a_{n-2}.
The source's exact recurrence is a_n=((n-1)/(n-2))a_{n-1}a_{n-2}. Thus c_n <= 4((n-2)/(n-1))c_{n-1} wherever the cut is nontrivial at the previous level (boundary cases handled separately). Iteration still loses exponentially; the telescoping rational factor contributes only order 1/n. Replacing a_n by its exact product does not cancel 4. Missing lemma: a uniformly bounded total boundary ambiguity, or a summably small multiplicative loss, rather than two independent free bits at every depth. This is a genuine missing estimate, not a proof that no C exists.

Route 2, splice-and-repair. For a prefix u and suffix v from separate words, concatenate uv. An injection from these pairs into A_n times a fixed finite repair-label set would prove the conjecture. Fixed cut positions make pair -> uv injective, but uv need not lie in A_n: already A_3 has prefixes/suffixes {0,1}, producing 00 and 11 absent from A_3. A boundary change at one substitution scale can change the desubstitution alignment at the next scale. Merely recording its two-bit correction at each scale again gives a label set of size 4^n. The needed stronger statement is a repair algorithm with O(1) labels independent of depth and a proved inverse, including all overlapping A_{n-1}A_{n-2} / A_{n-2}A_{n-1} decompositions. No such inverse was established.

Route 3, entropy and bounded mutual dependence. Nilsson Theorem 3 equates normalized logarithmic growth, but log(p_n(t)p_n(f_n-t)/a_n)=o(f_n) would still allow ratios tending to infinity. Even an O(n) logarithmic loss is consistent with the desired claim failing. The source already proves the sufficient entropy estimate with a 4^(n-2) loss. Therefore entropy equality supplies no bounded-distortion theorem. A quasi-Bernoulli inequality on the uniform finite-word measures, uniform over n and cuts, would suffice but is exactly the unproved content, not a standard consequence of entropy equality.

## 348006: polychromatic 2-factor monotonicity

For each used color c put H_c=(V,E(K_n) minus edges of color c). The hypothesis says that H_c has no 2-regular subgraph of order exactly j for every c. A violation is a 2-factor on some subset of size ell>j in one H_c. For two colors both H and its complement have no j-vertex 2-factor; thus this is a simultaneous forbidden-factor problem, not arbitrary pancyclicity of H. Merging all other colors into a single color preserves the hypothesis if both colors were used. A two-color theorem would therefore imply the multicolor theorem. This reduction is exact and routine; it does not solve the two-color statement.

Route 1, delete a vertex on a violating factor. If a component C_r with r>=4 has no c-colored edges, deleting v preserves a factor if its two neighbors are adjacent in H_c. If not, the shortcut edge has color c. Triangle components cannot be shrunk by one. Hence a factor does not automatically have all smaller orders. Under a minimal excess ell-j, every order-reducing shortcut that would hit j is forced to color c. In particular when ell=j+1 and all components have size>=4, all distance-two cycle edges are c; for larger excess one must control several shortening operations and triangles. Missing lemma: these forced edges and the complete-graph complement force a j-factor missing some color. The source's cycle theorem requires at least three colors and its connected-cycle twist does not justify disconnected-factor monotonicity for two colors.

Route 2, complete bipartite obstruction for odd j. Color K_{a,b} blue and the two internal cliques red. Blue has no odd-order 2-factor. To violate monotonicity it needs a 2-factor of even order at least j+1, forcing a,b >= (j+1)/2. But for odd j>=7 these sizes imply a red j-factor: choose one clique with size at least ceil(j/2) and the other at least floor(j/2), and take disjoint cycles on those numbers of vertices; both sizes are at least 3 and sum to j. This violates the original hypothesis. For j=5 the only analogous low-order obstruction has n<9, explicitly excluded; larger part sizes allow a red triangle plus enough other vertices or a red 5-cycle. Thus the familiar odd-cycle exception does not furnish an allowed counterexample here. This is an exact exclusion of this construction, not a universal exclusion of all constructions.

Route 3, use disjoint short cycles to defeat subset sums. Taking H as a union of triangles avoids j-factors if 3 does not divide j, while allowing larger multiples of 3. However for at least two triangles its complement contains a 6-cycle, and with at least three triangles complete multipartite structure supplies many smaller cycle lengths. In particular three triangles have a complementary 4-cycle (two vertices from each of two parts), a 5-cycle (parts 2,2,1), and a 7-cycle (parts 3,2,2), so the natural j=4,5,7 attempts fail at the hypothesis. General mixtures require a joint analysis of both complement factor spectra. No exact finite in-scope witness was produced, and no finite search is proposed as a proof of the positive statement.

The journal record is Discrete Mathematics 345(8) (2022), 112896, DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2022.112896. Its HTTP200 page contains metadata/abstract only, NOT the journal proof body. One listed arXiv version, journal/corrections/citations not fully cleared. Park.

## 368214: odd (k+2)-coloring of k-trees

Use source proper odd coloring, not an arbitrary nonproper map. A color at a vertex must have odd neighbor multiplicity. At a root k-clique the colors are distinct. A new simplicial vertex has exactly these k neighbors and initially meets the odd condition automatically. Properness leaves two choices out of k+2, but existing neighbor parity vectors change by toggling the chosen coordinate.

Route 1, greedy extension. For an already colored neighbor v let S_v be its set of colors with odd neighbor count. Adding a leaf of color a destroys the condition exactly when S_v={a}. Thus each clique neighbor with a singleton witness can forbid one of the two remaining colors. Different neighbors may forbid different colors. Existing oddness only asserts S_v nonempty and supplies no common spare color. Needed lemma: a recoloring/extension invariant preventing simultaneous singleton obstructions for the two available colors at each attachment clique. This was not proved. The algebraic obstruction is a failure of the induction invariant, not a constructed k-tree counterexample.

Route 2, preserve a permanent witness. Requiring one neighbor color to remain unique works locally but descendants on different k-cliques can repeat that color. Odd witness preservation only needs parity, but a new vertex toggles the same coordinate at each member of its attachment clique, so independent witness assignments are coupled. Source Theorem 3 repairs a root clique by repeated halving using r=floor(log_2 k)+1 choices and protected extra colors, ending at k+2r+1. Replacing r by one is unjustified: a single bipartition of witness states can leave floor(k/2) unresolved root vertices. Missing lemma: a structural collapse of these root states special to k-trees that allows two spare colors for all k.

Route 3, clique-separator parity states. A branch can be represented by possible proper root colorings and parity vectors of contributions at its k boundary vertices. Composition is XOR for parity contributions. Acceptable internal vertices must all have nonzero odd-color vector. These are finite states for fixed k, but proving an accepting state for arbitrary depth and arbitrary k requires an inductive closure theorem for realizable boundary states. Source's intricate 2-tree and 3-tree branch arguments do not establish it for k>=4. No transition enumeration was run. The required endpoint remains every positive k and every finite k-tree; proving k=4 alone is insufficient.
