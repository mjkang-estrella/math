                                                                                        Abstract
                                                         Inspired by Coxeter’s notion of Petrie polygon for d-polytopes (see [Cox73]),
                                                     we consider a generalization of the notion of zigzag circuits on complexes and
                                                     compute the zigzag structure for several interesting families of d-polytopes,
                                                     including semiregular, regular-faced, Wythoff Archimedean ones, Conway’s 4-
                                                     polytopes, half-cubes, folded cubes.
                                                         Also considered are regular maps and Lins triality relations on maps.

                                                     Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 52B05, 52B10; Secondary 05C30,
                                                     05C10.
                                                     Keywords: graphs, zigzags, complexes, regularity.

      The medial of a polytope P , denoted by Med(P ), is the polytope formed by the
convex hull of the midpoints of all edges of P . It can also be defined combinatorially
on maps on surfaces by taking as vertices the edge of the original map, by taking as
edges the pair of edges sharing an incident vertex and an incident face and by taking
as faces the vertices and faces of the original map. This notion of medial can also
be defined combinatorially on d-dimensional complexes, including maps, i.e. the case
d = 2.


2     Zigzags for d-dimensional complexes
We extend here the definitions of zigzags to any complex. A chain of length k in a
partially ordered set is a sequence (x0 , . . . xk ), such that xi < xi+1 . A chain C is a
subchain of another chain C ′ if it is obtained by removing some elements in C ′ .
       A chain is maximal if it is not a subchain of another chain. The rank rank(x)
of an element x is the maximal length of chains, beginning at the lowest elements
0 and terminating at x. A partially ordered set is called ranked if there is a lowest
element 0 and a greatest element 1 and if, given two elements x < y with no elements
z satisfying to x < z < y, one has rank(y) = 1 + rank(x).
       A partially ordered set is called a lattice if for any two elements x and y, there
are an unique smallest element s and an unique greatest element t, such that x ≤ s,
y ≤ s and x ≥ t, y ≥ t.
       The dimension of an element is defined as rank(x) − 1.
       A d-dimensional complex K is a finite partially ordered set, such that it holds:
       (i) K has a smallest 0 and highest element 1,
       (ii) K is ranked and all maximal chains have length d + 2,
       (iii) given two elements x and y with x ≤ y and dim(y) = 2 + dim(x), there are
exactly two elements u, u′, such that x ≤ u ≤ y and x ≤ u′ ≤ y.
       A d-dimensional complex is called simplicial if for every element x of dimension
d, there is exactly d + 1 elements of dimension 0 contained in it.
       In a d-dimensional complex, a maximal chain is called a flag; it necessarily
begins at 0 and terminates at 1.
       Using (iii), one can define the following permutation operator on flags. For
1 ≤ i ≤ d + 1, denote by σi the operator transforming (0, x1 , . . . , xi , . . . , xd+1 , 1)
into the flag (0, x1 , . . . , x′i , . . . , xd+1 , 1) with x′i being the unique element satisfying to
x′i 6= xi and xi−1 ≤ x′i ≤ xi+1 . One has σi2 = 1 and σi σj = σj σi if i < j − 1.

Definition 2.1 Let K be a d-dimensional complex, then:
     (i) denote by F (K) the set of flags of K,
     (ii) denote by G(K) the graph having, as vertex-set, F (K), with two flags being
adjacent if they are obtained one from the other by a permutation σi ,
     (iii) the complex K is said to be orientable if G(K) is bipartite; an orientation
of K consists in selecting one of the two connected components.

Theorem 2.6 Every zigzag Z in K corresponds to an unique zigzag Z ∗ in K∗ with
the same length.

Proof. Given a flag f = (0, x1 , . . . , xd+1 , 1) of K, one can associate to it a flag
f ′ = (1, xd+1 , . . . , x1 , 0) of K∗ . Denote by σi′ the operator on K∗ , which acts by
changing the i-th element. It is easy to see that its action on f ′ corresponds to the
action of σd+2−i on f . So, one has T ′ (f ′ ) = (T −1 f )′ and every zigzag (f1 , . . . , fl ) of
K corresponds to a zigzag (fl′ , . . . , f1′ ) of K∗ .                                          ✷
      In the case of maps (i.e. for d = 2), every intersection in K corresponds to an
intersection in K∗ with type I or II interchanged. This is not, a priori, the case of
complexes of dimension d > 2.
      A d-dimensional complex K is said to be z-transitive if its symmetry group
Sym(K) is transitive on zigzags. It is said to be z-knotted if it has only one zigzag,
Note that the stabilizer of a flag is necessarily the trivial group, i.e., every orbit of
flags has the size |Sym(K)|.
      Denote by Z(K) the graph formed by the set of zigzags of a complex K with
two zigzags being adjacent if the signature of their intersection is different from (0, 0).
In the case of a 2-dimensional complexes, we prove (see Section 6) that Z(K) is
connected. In the case of complexes of dimension d > 2, there is no reason to think
that connectivity will still hold.

Proposition 2.7 If a d-dimensional complex K is regular, then:
     (i) K is z-transitive,
     (ii) if Z(K) is connected, then either zigzags have no self-intersections, or K is
z-knotted.

Proof. The transitivity on zigzags is obvious. If a zigzag has a self-intersection,
then, by transitivity, all flags correspond to a self-intersection of zigzags. Since Z(K)
is connected, it means that there is only one zigzag.                                   ✷

Conjecture 2.8 The signature of any zigzag in any odd-dimensional complex is

The above conjecture is strange and we do not see why it would be true. Nevertheless,

        self.ids = set(map(str, json.loads((self.lane / "reservation.json").read_text())["ids"]))
        self.denied = json.loads((self.lane / "inherited-denials.json").read_text())
        self.opener = opener  # Tests inject a local fake; CLI always uses urllib.
        self.ledger = self.lane / "guard-ledger.jsonl"

    @contextlib.contextmanager
    def locked(self):
        with (self.lane / "guard.lock").open("a") as lock:
            fcntl.flock(lock, fcntl.LOCK_EX)
            records, previous = [], "0" * 64
            if self.ledger.exists():
                for raw in self.ledger.read_bytes().splitlines(keepends=True):
                    record = json.loads(raw)
                    if record.get("seq") != len(records) or record.get("previous") != previous:
                        raise PolicyError("Broken guard ledger chain; no request permitted")
                    records.append(record)
                    previous = hashlib.sha256(raw).hexdigest()
            yield records, previous

    def append(self, records, previous, event):
        record = dict(event, seq=len(records), previous=previous,
                      utc=datetime.datetime.now(datetime.timezone.utc).isoformat())
        raw = (json.dumps(record, sort_keys=True) + "\n").encode()
        with self.ledger.open("ab") as f:
            f.write(raw)
            f.flush()
        return record

    def note(self, event):
        with self.locked() as (records, previous):
            return self.append(records, previous, event)

    def reserve(self, url, routes, plan_sha):
        k = key(url)
        if url in ROOTS or k in {key(x) for x in ROOTS}:
            routes = sorted(self.ids)  # Shared roots can never be undercharged.
        if not routes or not set(routes) <= self.ids or len(routes) != len(set(routes)):
            raise PolicyError("Routes must be exact unique reserved IDs")
        with self.locked() as (records, previous):
            attempts = [r for r in records if r["event"] == "attempt"]
            failures = [r for r in records if r["event"] == "unavailable"]
            denied_keys = set(self.denied["blocked_keys"]) | {r["url_key"] for r in failures}
            denied_artifacts = set(self.denied["blocked_artifacts"])
            for r in failures:
                denied_artifacts.update(r.get("artifacts", []))
            if k in denied_keys or artifacts(url) & denied_artifacts:
                raise PolicyError("Inherited/current unavailable route or artifact: no retry")
            if any(r["url_key"] == k for r in attempts):
                raise PolicyError("Already attempted URL; use saved bytes/outcome, no refetch/query variant")
            if len(attempts) >= GLOBAL_CAP:
                raise PolicyError("Global GET-hop cap48 exhausted")
            for route in routes:
                if sum(route in r["routes"] for r in attempts) >= PER_ID_CAP:
                    raise PolicyError("Per-ID GET-hop cap12 exhausted: " + route)
            return self.append(records, previous, {
                "event": "attempt", "url": url, "url_key": k,
                "routes": routes, "plan_sha256": plan_sha,
                "ordinal": len(attempts) + 1,
            })

    def fetch(self, plan):
        if not isinstance(plan, dict) or set(plan) != {"url", "routes", "destination", "source_identity", "purpose"}:
            raise PolicyError("Exactly one explicit plan object required; no URL arrays/seed traversal")
        if not isinstance(plan["url"], str) or not isinstance(plan["routes"], list):
            raise PolicyError("Invalid URL/routes types")
        if not all(isinstance(x, str) for x in plan["routes"]):
            raise PolicyError("Route IDs must be strings")
        if not all(isinstance(plan[x], str) and len(plan[x].strip()) >= 12 for x in ["purpose", "source_identity"]):
            raise PolicyError("Concrete source identity and assigned-target purpose required")
        dest = (self.lane / plan["destination"]).resolve()
        if not dest.is_relative_to(self.lane / "sources") or dest == self.lane / "sources" or dest.exists():
            raise PolicyError("Destination must be a NEW file under own sources/")
        plan_sha = hashlib.sha256(json.dumps(plan, sort_keys=True).encode()).hexdigest()
        current = plan["url"]
        attempt = self.reserve(current, plan["routes"], plan_sha)
        guard = self

        class Redirect(urllib.request.HTTPRedirectHandler):
            max_redirections = 5

            def redirect_request(self, req, fp, code, msg, headers, newurl):
                nonlocal current, attempt
                guard.note({"event": "redirect", "url": current, "status": code, "location": newurl})
                current = newurl
                attempt = guard.reserve(current, plan["routes"], plan_sha)
                return super().redirect_request(req, fp, code, msg, headers, newurl)

        opener = self.opener or urllib.request.build_opener(Redirect())
        http_status = None
        observed_body = None
        response_content_type = None
        try:
            req = urllib.request.Request(current, headers={"User-Agent": "Research-source-reader/1.0", "Accept-Encoding": "identity"}, method="GET")
            with opener.open(req, timeout=20) as response:
                status = response.status
                http_status = status
                response_content_type = response.headers.get("Content-Type")
                if status != 200:
                    raise RuntimeError("Non-200 status " + str(status))
                body = response.read(MAX_BYTES + 1)
                observed_body = body
                if not body or len(body) > MAX_BYTES:
                    raise RuntimeError("Empty or oversized body; unavailable, no retry")
                if "html" in response.headers.get("Content-Type", "").lower() or body.lstrip().lower().startswith((b"<!doctype html", b"<html")):
                    start = body[:12000].lower()
                    if any(x in start for x in [b"verify you are human", b"cf-chl-", b"challenge-platform", b"robot check", b"access denied", b"incapsula", b"request unsuccessful"]):
                        raise RuntimeError("Challenge/denial body; unavailable, no retry")
                dest.parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
                with dest.open("xb") as f:
                    f.write(body)
                return self.note({"event": "success", "url": current, "status": status,
                                  "destination": str(dest.relative_to(self.lane)), "bytes": len(body),
                                  "sha256": hashlib.sha256(body).hexdigest(), "ordinal": attempt["ordinal"]})
        except Exception as error:
            self.note({"event": "unavailable", "url": current, "url_key": key(current),
                       "artifacts": sorted(artifacts(current)), "error": repr(error),
                       "status": getattr(error, "code", http_status), "ordinal": attempt["ordinal"],
                       "response_content_type": response_content_type,
                       "observed_bytes_read": len(observed_body) if observed_body is not None else None,
                       "observed_body_sha256": hashlib.sha256(observed_body).hexdigest() if observed_body is not None else None,
                       "body_may_be_truncated": len(observed_body) > MAX_BYTES if observed_body is not None else None})
            raise


def main():
    if len(sys.argv) != 2:
        raise PolicyError("Usage: source_get_guard.py OWN_EXPLICIT_PLAN.json")
    lane = Path(__file__).resolve().parent
    plan_path = Path(sys.argv[1]).resolve()
    if not plan_path.is_relative_to(lane):
        raise PolicyError("Plan must be an own-lane file")
    print(json.dumps(Guard(lane).fetch(json.loads(plan_path.read_text())), sort_keys=True))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
