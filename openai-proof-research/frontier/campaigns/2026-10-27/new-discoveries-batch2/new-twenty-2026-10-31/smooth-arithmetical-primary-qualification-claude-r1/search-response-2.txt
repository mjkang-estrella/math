- **Glass and Wagner (arXiv 1903.01398, published in Integers 2020):** They count smooth arithmetical structures on paths with one doubled edge for small cases, and they find the count grows like a polynomial of degree m−1 in n. They conjecture that the number of smooth arithmetical structures on the graph grows at the same rate as a binomial coefficient, C(m+n−1, n). A later source says the conjecture concerns how the count depends on path length and where the doubled edge sits. Glass and Wagner formulated a conjecture for how the count grows in this case, depending on the path length and the location of the doubled edge.
- **Bounding paper (arXiv 2007.15100):** It gives general upper bounds for arithmetical structures on graphs, including multigraphs. It doesn't address the doubled-path conjecture directly.
- **Braun et al. (arXiv 1701.06377):** This is the foundational result for plain paths and cycles. Counts on paths follow Catalan numbers, and the paper links them to ballot-number refinements.
