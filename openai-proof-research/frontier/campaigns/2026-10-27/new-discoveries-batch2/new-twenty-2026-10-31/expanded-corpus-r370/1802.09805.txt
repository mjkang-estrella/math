                                                                                           Abstract
                                                     There is a natural analogue of weak Bruhat order on the involutions in any Coxeter group.
                                                 The saturated chains of intervals in this order correspond to reduced words for a certain set of
                                                 group elements called atoms. Brion gives a general formula for the cohomology class of a K-orbit
                                                 closure in an arbitrary flag variety, where K is a symmetric subgroup of a complex algebraic
                                                 group. In type A, the terms in this formula are indexed by atoms for permutations. We study
                                                 the combinatorics of atoms for involutions in the group of signed permutations. In particular,
                                                 we give a compact description of the atom set for any signed involution and endow it with the
                                                 structure of a graded poset. Our main result, as an application, is to identify explicitly the
                                                 terms in Brion’s cohomology formula in types B and C. These descriptions apply to all K-orbits
                                                 in these types and are the first of their kind outside of type A.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           This article is about the properties of certain elements, which we call atoms, associated to involu-
                                           tions in Coxeter groups. Here is a quick definition. Let (W, S) be a Coxeter system with length
                                           function ℓ : W → N = {0, 1, 2, . . . }. There is a unique associative product ◦ : W × W → W such
                                           that s ◦ s = s for s ∈ S and u ◦ v = uv for u, v ∈ W with ℓ(u) + ℓ(v) = ℓ(uv) [21, §7.1]. This is
                                           sometimes called the Demazure product, while (W, ◦) is the 0-Hecke monoid of (W, S).
                                               Write I(W ) = {w ∈ W : w−1 = w} for the set of involutions     in W . The operation w 7→ w−1 ◦ w
                                           is a surjective map W → I(W ), and we let AHecke (z) = w ∈ W : w−1 ◦ w = z for each z ∈ I(W ).
                                                                                                     

                                           This set is always nonempty and we define A(z) to be its subset of minimal length elements.
                                           Following [12], we refer to the elements of AHecke (z) as the Hecke atoms of z ∈ I(W ), while the
                                           elements of A(z) are the atoms of z. When W is finite, the atoms of z ∈ I(W ) are precisely the
                                           minimal length elements w ∈ W with wz ≤ w in (strong) Bruhat order [12, Theorem 4.12].

                                           1.1   Motivation
                                           One reason why atoms are interesting objects is that their reduced expressions correspond to
                                           saturated chains in the involution weak (Bruhat) order introduced by Richardson and Springer
                                           [27]. This is the partial order on I(W ) with x ≤I y if there exists w ∈ W such that w−1 ◦ x ◦ w = y.
                                               The involution weak order is relevant to the study of spherical varieties. Let G be a complex
                                           algebraic group with Borel subgroup B, and let θ be a self-inverse automorphism of G that preserves
                                           B. The symmetric subgroup K = {g ∈ G : θ(g) = g} acts with finitely many orbits on the flag

closures are the Schubert varieties Xw indexed by the elements of the Weyl group W .
    Fix a K-orbit O in G/B. Brion gives a general formula describing the cohomology class of the
closure of O in terms of Schubert varieties in [2]. We can express this formula as

precise definitions of ABrion (O) and dO (w) involve a partial order on the K-orbits in G/B that lifts
the involution weak order. We review this material in Section 8.
    For example, if G = GL(n, C) and θ(g) = (g −1 )T , then we have K = O(n, C) and W = Sn . In
this case, the K-orbit closures Oz are indexed by z ∈ I(Sn ), the integer dOz (w) is the number of
2-cycles in z for all w, and the set ABrion (Oz ) is precisely the set of atoms A(z). One goal of this
paper is to clarify the extent to which this parallel between ABrion (O) and A(z) holds outside type
A. We focus on the classical types B and C, when G = Sp(2n, C) or SO(n, C).
    Other motivations for studying atoms come from their enumerative properties. Several authors
have noted remarkable features of atoms for involutions in the symmetric group. For example, the
inverse Hecke atoms AHecke (z)−1 = {w−1 : w ∈ AHecke (z)} are precisely the equivalence classes in Sn
under the so-called Chinese relation from [8, 9]. There are also explicit combinatorial descriptions
of A(z) for z ∈ I(Sn ) [4, 5], which are used in [12] to show that A(z) is naturally a bounded,
graded poset (and conjecturally, a lattice). Another goal of the present work is to generalize these
descriptions to atoms for signed permutations. Some of our theorems in this direction can be viewed
as strengthening results of Hu and Zhang, who prove an analogue of the Matsumoto-Tits theorem
for the reduced expressions of atoms for signed permutations in [17, 18].

A word is a finite sequence of integers. A (one-line) descent of a word w = w1 w2 · · · wn is a
pair (wi , wi+1 ) with wi > wi+1 . Let Des(w) be the set of descents of w. A subword of w is
any (not necessarily consecutive) subsequence. Define sortL (w) (respectively, sortR (w)) to be
the subword of w formed by omitting wi+1 (respectively, wi ) whenever (wi , wi+1 ) ∈ Des(w).
Adapt these definitions to elements of Sn or WnBC by identifying permutations with their one-
line representations. For example, if w = 2134765 ∈ S7 then Des(w) = {(2, 1), (7, 6), (6, 5)},

5     Noncrossing shapes
The connected posets (A(z)−1 , <B ) and (A(z)−1 , ≪B ) for z ∈ I(WnBC ) are no longer intervals,
though examples suggest they may be meet semilattices. In this section, we characterize these
posets’ extremal elements.
     Consider a subset X ⊂ [±n] with X = −X. A matching in (the complete graph on) X is a set M
of pairwise disjoint 2-element subsets of X. A matching M is symmetric if {−i, −j} ∈ M whenever

8     Cohomology formulas for orbit closures
In this section we explain how the sets A(z) are related to cohomology classes of orbit closures in
flag varieties of types B and C. Our goal is to prove a precise form of Theorem 1.4.

8.1   Brion’s cohomology formula
Let G be a connected, complex, simple algebraic group with a holomorphic automorphism θ that
is an involution. Fix a Borel subgroup B containing a torus T . Assume both groups are preserved
by θ. The subgroup of fixed points K = Gθ := {g ∈ G : g = θ(g)} is called a symmetric subgroup
[27]. Every symmetric subgroup acts on the flag variety G/B with finitely many orbits.
    There is a useful weak order on the finite set K\G/B, introduced in [27, 28]. Fix a simple
generator s ∈ S for the Weyl group W = NG (T )/T and write α for the corresponding simple root.
Let Pα be the minimal parabolic subgroup of G of type α containing B and let πα : G/B → G/Pα
be the canonical projection. If O is a K-orbit in G/B, then πα−1 (πα (O)) contains a unique dense

                                                  25
K-orbit, denoted s · O. The weak order on the finite set of K-orbits in G/B is the transitive closure
of the relation that has O < O′ whenever O ̸= O′ = s · O for some s ∈ S (see [27] or [32, §1.4]).
    An algebraic subgroup H ⊆ G is spherical if H acts on G/B with finitely many orbits. This
includes H = K as a special case. If H = B then the orbits are in bijection with W and the
closures BwB/B of these orbits are the well-known Schubert varieties. In [2], Brion derives a
general formula for the cohomology classes of H-orbit closures in G/B as linear combinations of
classes for Schubert varieties. When H = K is a symmetric subgroup, this goes as follows.

s ∈ S corresponds to a simple root α for which the projection πα : G/B → G/Pα restricted to the

8.2     Clans
Choose integers p, q ∈ N with n = p + q. A (p, q)-clan is a sequence of n symbols (c1 , c2 , . . . , cn ),
in which each ci is either an integer or one of the signs + or −. Each integer which appears
must appear exactly twice, and the difference between the number of +’s and the number of −’s
appearing in the string must be precisely p − q. Two such sequences are considered to represent
the same clan if the sets of pairs {i, j} giving the positions of matching integers ci = cj are the
same in each. For example, (1, −, 2, 1, +, 2) and (7, −, 0, 7, +, 0) are the same (3, 3)-clan. For any
clan γ = (c1 , c2 , . . . , cm ), let signs(γ) be the subsequence of signs ci ∈ {±}.
    A clan (c1 , c2 , . . . , cn ) is symmetric if it is equal to (cn , . . . , c2 , c1 ), and skew-symmetric if
it is equal to (−cn , . . . , −c2 , −c1 ). For example, (1, +, 1, 2, +, 2) is a symmetric (4, 2)-clan and
(1, −, +, 1, 3, −, +, 3) is a skew-symmetric (4, 4)-clan. Bingham, Can, and Uğurlu derive gener-
ating functions to count such clans in [1, 6]. Given a symmetric or skew-symmetric clan γ =
(c−n , . . . , c−1 , c0 , c1 , . . . , cn ) or γ = (c−n , . . . , c−1, , c1 , . . . , cn ), define ιBC (γ) ∈ I(WnBC ) to be the
unique involution that has (i, j) as a 2-cycle for distinct i, j ∈ [±n] if and only if ci = cj ∈ Z.

10     Atomic elements
An involution z in a Coxeter group is atomic if |A(z)| = 1. An element of a Coxeter group is fully
commutative if each of its reduced words can be transformed to any other by a sequence commuta-
tions. A fully commutative involution is always atomic [12, Proposition 7.12]. The converse holds
in type A [24, Corollary 6.17], but does not extend to WnBC . Theorem 5.8 implies:

if and only if neg(z) ≤ 1 and 0B (z) = 0B (z, Mmin ) = 1B (z, Mmin ).

Proposition 10.2. An involution in WnBC is atomic if and only if it has at most one negated point
and it does not have two cycles {a, d}, {b, c} in [±n] with −d ̸= a < b ≤ c < d.
