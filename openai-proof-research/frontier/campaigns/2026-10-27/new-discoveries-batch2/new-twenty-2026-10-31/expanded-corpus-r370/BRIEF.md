## Sustained ordinary derivation 1: 332639, complete but zero-credit correction

Definitions 1–2 and the reconstruction rule of Dukes–White identify the two-peg n-edge world with permutations pi of n positions. A surjective edge colouring with ell colours reconstructs by retaining the order inside each colour on each peg and stacking colour subdiagrams in colour order. Matrix entry M[D,D](x) counts those colourings reconstructing D by number of colours. The trace sums over all n! diagrams.

For ell=n-1 there is exactly one colour class of size two; all others have size one. Thus the reconstructed permutation is a direct sum of n-2 singleton permutations and the two-element permutation of the shared-colour edges. It is either the identity or a permutation with exactly one adjacent transposition. This establishes necessity directly, without assuming that every assertion in the general reconstruction theorem is correct.

For identity input, choose the unordered pair of edges sharing a colour in binom(n,2) ways, then biject its n-1 colour classes to the ordered palette in (n-1)! ways. Every resulting reconstruction is identity because both peg orders agree in every class. Its contribution is binom(n,2)(n-1)!.

For a fixed adjacent-transposition input, the two crossing edges must be the shared pair: otherwise their output blocks are singleton blocks, and no crossing survives. There is only this pair. If the transposition is at positions j,j+1, its colour must have rank j amongst the n-1 classes. Assign the remaining n-2 singleton edges to the remaining colours in (n-2)! ways. Their permutation relative to each other changes no diagram, since singleton components coincide. There are n-1 possible adjacent transpositions, hence contribution (n-1)(n-2)!=(n-1)!. No other input can contribute, by the direct-sum necessity above.

Therefore, for every n>=2,
  [x^(n-1)] tr M = (n-1)!(binom(n,2)+1)
                 = (n-1)!(n^2-n+2)/2.

## Sustained ordinary route 2: 332463, exact probability obstruction

Let A=Aut(G), |V|=N, and every list have k elements. For a subgroup H<=A, let E_H be the event that every H-orbit is monochromatic. Independence gives the exact formula
  P(E_H)= k^(-N) product over H-orbits O of |intersection(v in O) L_v|.
Indeed, on each orbit choose one colour from the intersection; the orbits are disjoint, so multiply counts. Each factor is at most k. For identical lists this upper bound k^(number of H-orbits-N) is attained. Thus every fixed-automorphism event, and every intersection of such events (use the subgroup generated by those automorphisms), is maximized by identical lists.

This proves the desired statement completely when A has prime order p: all nonidentity elements generate A, so the event of any nontrivial colour-preserving automorphism is just E_A, and the formula applies. It also gives the source's expectation comparison by summing individual probabilities, for an arbitrary finite graph.

The full endpoint is a union, not that expectation. Inclusion-exclusion reads P(union_g E_<g>) as alternating sums of P(E_<g1,...,gt>). Termwise maximization of all intersections does not preserve the alternating sum. Already for two events the expression is a+b-c: increasing c may reduce the union. A proof therefore needs more than the subgroup formula. A candidate colour-compression operation replacing a list colour b by a would have to control every inclusion-exclusion term together, or give an injection from nondistinguishing colourings before compression to such colourings after compression. No such injection was obtained: the automorphism that witnesses an input may change with the input, and orbitwise colour relabellings need not agree across witnesses.

A second attempt used disjoint invariant components. If their automorphism actions were independent, probability of a nontrivial stabilizer would be 1-product(1-p_i), so maximizing each p_i would suffice. But diagonal actions of one group on several orbits are not independent: the same group element must stabilize the relevant colour classes simultaneously. Componentwise expectation arguments cannot be substituted. This is a precise missing simultaneous coupling, not a claim that counterexamples exist.

Actual primary comparison: arxiv1602.03741 Conjecture9 states all graphs. The independently hosted author PDF Conjecture13 states connected vertex-transitive graphs and discusses a joined pair of Kneser graphs, followed by referee acknowledgments. That discussion gives an expectation observation and does not provide an explicit strict inequality between probabilities. It therefore neither refutes the literal universal probability claim nor proves its restricted variant. ## Sustained ordinary route 3: 333610, hyperfield weak closure

Anderson1607.04868v4 (23July2018) defines hyperfield sums as nonempty subsets, vector hyperaddition coordinatewise, and V*(M)=C(M)^perp for strong matroids (Definition2.17). A covector Z must be orthogonal to EVERY circuit. Thus the exact task for X,Y in V* is to select one Z(e) in X(e) boxplus Y(e) for each e satisfying ALL circuit equations at once.

First route: choose a candidate sum for each circuit separately. Even if for every circuit C there is some Z_C in X boxplus Y orthogonal to C, it does not follow that one common Z works; the intersection over C may be empty. Distributivity gives inclusion relations of hyper-sums, not equality of all simultaneous choices. This is the unpaid global compatibility lemma. The empty-sum obstruction in section7 applies to general tracts, where x boxplus y can be empty. It cannot refute this conjecture, which restricts to hyperfields, where every binary sum is nonempty.

Second route, a full valid subcase: for the phase hyperfield and a single circuit constraint, write the orthogonality condition as existence of positive real coefficients on the nonzero phase terms summing to zero. For X and Y separately choose complex coordinate lifts x_e,y_e with the prescribed phases and satisfying the one linear constraint (zero circuit coefficients impose no restriction). Then z=x+y satisfies the same linear constraint. Its phase is a permitted coordinatewise hyper-sum of the two input phases, and is orthogonal to that circuit. Coordinates absent in one summand and coordinates summing to zero obey the same conclusion by the phase-hyperaddition definition. Consequently this one-constraint case cannot furnish a counterexample.

For a matroid pushed forward from a complex subspace U, the same argument works if BOTH supplied covectors lift to vectors of that SAME U. Proposition4.6 gives inclusion of phase images in covectors, not equality in general. Section5.4.4 explicitly warns that V* can fail to equal V-perp: the source takes U=Row[[1,0,1+i,1-i],[0,1,1-i,1+i]], with a vector X=(1,1,exp(i(pi+.01)),exp(i(pi+.01))) in V(M) and (1,1,1,1) in V*(M) that are not orthogonal. The phase semicircle criterion is stated in section5.4.3. This defeats a double-orthogonal-space shortcut but supplies no pair X,Y with all sums inadmissible. We did not silently turn the duality failure into a weak-closure refutation.

A counterexample would require a certified hyperfield, a strong matroid circuit set satisfying modular elimination, TWO actual covectors, and a proof that the product of their coordinate-sum sets has empty intersection with the orthogonal set of all circuits. An elimination-property failure (Eppolito, mentioned without details in section7) only forbids a sum with a prescribed zero coordinate and need not forbid every sum.
