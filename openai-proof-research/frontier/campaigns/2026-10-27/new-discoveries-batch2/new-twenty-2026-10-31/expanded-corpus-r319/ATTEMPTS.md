## 359841: perfect fractional matchings in arbitrary looms

The actual Definition 1.5 requires orthogonality, uniformity r and s, integral covering numbers s and r, and the exact two-way minimum-cover closures A=C_r(B), B=C_s(A). Retaining just cross-intersection would lose the problem. Lemma 1.12 has a useful exact saturation argument: for any fractional matching w on A and any b in B, orthogonality gives sum_a w_a = sum_{v in b} sum_{a containing v} w_a <= s. Equality forces every vertex of b to be saturated. Since the B edges cover V, an A matching of mass s is perfect. Conversely, a perfect matching has mass s by the same equation. LP duality makes this the exact tau*(A)=s endpoint, with a symmetric argument for B.

First route: average uniform edge weights. This would work if the incidence degrees of every vertex were equal, but Definition 1.5 does not supply regular incidence degrees. Uniformity refers to edge size. Normalizing each edge by its size also does not normalize the loads of vertices.

Second route: write the vertex-by-edge incidence matrix U_A. A perfect fractional matching is exactly U_A w=1, w>=0. Farkas separation reduces existence to excluding every real vector y with U_A^T y>=0 and sum_v y_v<0. Orthogonality supplies U_A^T 1_b=1 for each b, so differences 1_b-1_b' are annihilated by U_A^T. This only describes a subspace of the annihilator; it does not place 1 in the nonnegative cone of columns of U_A. Integral minimum-cover closure restricts 0/1 cover vectors; no valid rounding from an arbitrary signed separator to a smaller integral cover was found. Assuming such rounding would silently impose an integrality property not proved for these hypergraphs.

Third route: use decomposition into known looms. The regular-graph component proof in Theorem 6.5 controls odd cuts, invokes the fractional edge-coloring formula, and forces every positive-weight graph matching to be perfect by the total-degree equality. It applies to the graph-derived family, not every loom. Section 7 classifies (r,2)-looms; Section 8 covers (3,3). Neither is an exhaustive decomposition for arbitrary r,s. The remaining exact lemma is either the separator exclusion above, or a genuinely general structural classification. Known grid, graph-derived and low-uniformity cases are zero-credit source results. Historical r54 covers the neighboring union fractional-cover question and is not recycled.

## 361235: linear quotients for every power

Theorem 3.1's full proof splits I=x_1 I_1+I_2 by divisibility of its minimal generators, proves I_2 subset I_1, and proves the two branches componentwise polymatroidal. Its colon gluing is precise: for a generator v of I_2, (x_1 I_1:v)=(x_1), since v belongs to I_1 and is not divisible by x_1. Concatenating branch orders gives linear quotients for I.

First route: apply the same theorem to I^k. This requires I^k to be componentwise polymatroidal. Products of componentwise polymatroidal ideals need not be in the class, as the source explicitly warns immediately before Conjecture 3.3. This warning alone is not a counterexample for powers or for linear quotients; no such stronger-to-weaker inference is made.

Second route: exchange on factorizations u=u_1...u_k and v=v_1...v_k. If the factors all have a common degree, the polymatroid exchange mechanism is available. In the componentwise setting, total degree comparisons do not give a pairing of factors with comparable degrees and the required exponent deficit in the same pair. An exchanged factor may leave the chosen product branch, or produce a redundant generator rather than the needed earlier minimal generator. No factor-pairing lemma was established.

Third route: expand I^k=sum_{t=0}^k x_1^t I_1^t I_2^{k-t}. The nesting I_2 subset I_1 suggests ordering these layers. But mixed products I_1^t I_2^{k-t} have not been shown to have linear quotients, and removing redundant generators affects the colon order. The source's induction on variable and generator count does not supply induction on all these mixed products. The exact unproved bridge is an order on minimal generators for which every colon ideal is generated by variables, uniformly in k. No finite search was run, and no small example of failure is asserted.

## 372714: a multiplicative basis adapted to the full center

The source graph construction is based on a multiplicative basis: the bracket of any two basis vectors is zero or proportional to a single basis vector. A basis vertex has no outgoing edge precisely when its adjoint action is zero, so these vertices are exactly the central vectors among the chosen basis. Thus the exact conjecture asks for a multiplicative basis containing a basis of Z(g), not merely any linear basis adapted to Z(g).

The source's v1 Conjecture 52 persists as v2 Conjecture 49. Lemma 46 only adjoins a center basis to an existing spanning set; this retains bracket closure but generally creates a redundant graph. It does not close the minimal endpoint.

A first replacement route puts central linear combinations into a basis by Gaussian elimination. Brackets between retained original vectors still land in original output directions. If one of those directions was removed, its new expression can have several nonzero basis coefficients. Multiplicativity is then lost. Arbitrary basis extension or quotient splitting alone is not a proof.

A second route preserves the derived algebra D=[g,g]. The following sufficient case has a complete ordinary argument but is only an uncredited helper: if Z(g) intersects D trivially, the conjecture holds. Let B be an existing multiplicative basis and let O be the subset of B consisting of all nonzero bracket output directions. Then D=span(O). Since Z intersects D trivially, the images of O in g/Z are linearly independent. Extend them to a basis of g/Z by images of a subset S of B containing O. Let C be any basis of Z. Then S union C is a basis of g: S maps bijectively onto a quotient basis and intersects Z trivially. Brackets involving C vanish; brackets of vectors in S are zero or proportional to an element of O subset S. This is a multiplicative basis adapted to Z. The no-outgoing vertices span exactly Z by the graph definition.

The difficult remaining case Z intersects D nontrivially. Here the images of output directions in g/Z can be dependent, and deleting those directions may destroy multiplicativity. A central relation in the derived algebra need not be a coordinate subspace in the initial basis. No elimination procedure retaining every bracket output as one basis direction, and no obstruction proving that all such procedures fail, was established. The source Figure 8 example aff(R) direct sum R shows that changing basis can work; a bad initial graph is not a counterexample to the existential conjecture. No helper is promoted to CANDIDATE.md or a Pi task.

## Public-source endpoints and scope traps

372429 is already Corollary 3.3 in both versions of arXiv:2601.03809. Its precise polynomial order is coefficientwise nonnegative difference, not arbitrary real variable evaluation. The read Lemma 3.1 kills intersecting or incomplete pairs with the derivative/specialization operators, leaving independent bipartitions. Theorem 3.2 differentiates the resulting Lorentzian binary polynomial to a quadratic. Its equal diagonal Hessian entries and the Lorentzian reverse Cauchy inequality give (k!)^2 pi_kk >= (k-1)!(k+1)! pi_(k-1,k+1), hence the factor 1+1/k. Lemma 2.2 transfers this to the independent-set polynomial inequality. This exact public theorem excludes discovery credit without assuming a conjecture label is reliable.

372749 is explicitly claimed and proved in the primary source by Theorem 1.5 and its decomposition proof. Only selected Section 12 proof was read, not every upstream graphic/cographic lemma. The averaging bridge is complete: a SRC4 tuple totals at most 2r+4, so one circuit has integer size at most floor((r+2)/2). For the source-intended r+1 nonempty colors without singleton classes, retain two elements of each color. Order those color pairs and consider the rank r_j of the restriction to the first j pairs. Initially j-r_j<=0 because the matroid is simple; at the final pair j-r_j>=1. At the first j where j-r_j>=1, it equals 1: rank is nondecreasing and each step increases j-r_j by at most one. Thus that restriction has exactly rank+1 pairs and is simple regular. Apply Theorem 1.5 there; its rank is at most the original r, and its rainbow circuit remains a circuit in the original matroid. This is a public-source reduction, not a new solution. If “r+1-coloring” allowed unused labels, that is a scope issue and not an eligible discovery.
