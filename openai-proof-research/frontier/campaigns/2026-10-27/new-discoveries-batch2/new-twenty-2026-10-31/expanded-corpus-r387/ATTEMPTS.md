## 339993 — consumption order, sustained composite-degree construction

Primary: Ji, arXiv:1808.01074, current PDF v2; Definitions 5.1–5.3, Theorem 5.4 proof, Example 5.6, Section 8.3 Conjecture 8.8. Version history: v1 3 August 2018; v2 7 April 2019. Older search excerpts number this 7.8; current PDF says 8.8. A group H<=S_n consumes lambda when SOME partition of the points with those labeled block sizes has trivial common setwise stabilizer in H. The ordering lambda>=c mu quantifies over EVERY subgroup H and allows a DIFFERENT labeling for mu. Dominance compares all initial sums. To disprove equality of the two orders at a composite n one needs lambda>=mu and one H consuming lambda but consuming NO labeling of mu. One successful labeling of lambda and one unsuccessful labeling of mu do not suffice.

Source Example 5.6: regular V4 on four points consumes (3,1), since fixing the singleton fixes a regular-action point and hence is identity. Every two-subset is a coset of a subgroup of order two of V4, so its nonidentity translation preserves both it and its complementary two-subset. Thus V4 does not consume (2,2). Dominance (3,1)>=(2,2) is immediate (3>=2; sums4). This is a known source example, no new credit.

First attempted generalization: pad the V4 action with n-4 fixed points. It fails, not just for lack of a proof: for n>=5, a two-subset consisting of one moved point and one fixed point has trivial setwise stabilizer in V4. Any element preserving it must fix the moved point, since it cannot interchange a fixed point with a moved point. Thus the padded H consumes (n-2,2). Meanwhile it consumes (n-1,1) using a moved singleton. The hoped-for nonconsumption obstruction vanishes. The n=1 nonprime wording is not offered as a campaign solution.

Second route, full composite factorization: write n=pq with p prime, q>=2. Use q blocks of p points, coordinates (i,x) in Z/q x F_p. Let H=F_p^q semidirect C_q act by independent translations of the blocks and cyclic block rotation. A distinguishing partition into a large class and q singletons can be made by placing one distinctly colored singleton in each block: each block is fixed and then every translation is zero. For mu=(n-q,q), every q-subset either has an empty block (a nonzero translation in that empty block preserves it) or meets every block exactly once. In the latter case, rotating the blocks and choosing the independent translations to align the selected points preserves the subset and its complement. Thus H does not consume mu. HOWEVER lambda=(n-q,1^q) is dominated BY mu, not the other way round: their first sums agree and mu has second part q rather than1. This construction proves no separation of the required orders; refinement already predicts this direction.

Third repair: constrain the translations to sum zero, using H0={v:sum_i v_i=0} semidirect C_q, which is indeed a subgroup because rotations preserve the sum. Now lambda=(n-q+1,1^(q-1)) is consumed: put distinctly colored singletons in q-1 separate blocks. A nonidentity cyclic block rotation cannot fix even one labeled block, so the rotation is identity; the q-1 corresponding translations vanish and the sum condition kills the last. This lambda dominates mu=(n-q,q): first sum is larger by1; thereafter the mu prefix is already n whereas lambda reaches n only at its last part, so WAIT: at two parts lambda sums n-q+2, less than n for q>2. Hence these partitions are incomparable for q>2, another exact failure of the desired bridge! For q=2 lambda=(n-1,1) does dominate mu=(n-2,2).

There is a further independent obstruction to the repair even if different lambda were chosen: for p odd and q>=3, select a q-subset with one empty block, a two-subset in a second block, and one point in every other block. The unique empty block forces any preserving cyclic rotation to be identity. A nonzero translation of F_p has no proper nonempty invariant subset, so translations of all nonempty blocks are zero (the two-subset is proper since p>=3). The sum-zero condition then kills the empty-block translation. This q-subset distinguishes H0. Consequently the asserted universal nonconsumption of mu also fails. For p=2 the two-subset is full, so that argument does not apply; this is not clearance of that remaining branch. For q=2 and p odd the same empty-plus-two selection leaves the other block full only when p=2; for p odd it is proper and the same argument kills the stabilizer, so no all-composite witness results.

Unpaid lemma: for every composite n>=4 construct partitions with the correct dominance direction and one explicit subgroup with a proof for ALL labelings of the smaller partition. Neither padding, unrestricted wreath action, nor the sum-zero repair supplies it. The prime-degree r368 result remains unresolved; it is neither identical to this composite target nor evidence of novelty. No candidate survives for formal dispatch.

## 341232 — Ferrers rook equivalence graph

Primary: Barrese arXiv:1812.00533v2, Sections 3–5 and 8; journal EJC28(4) P4.13 (2021), Conjecture24. Journal header: submitted 9 January2019, accepted27 September2021, published22 October2021. Journal still prints the target; this is bounded version evidence, not current worldwide status. Author abstract search hit announces possible discussion in a 2019 talk; no proof obtained from that abstract and no exclusion based on it.

A Ferrers board has nonnegative weakly increasing column heights b_i; leading zero columns identify the same board. Its root word z_i=i-1-b_i satisfies z_1<=0 and z_(i+1)<=z_i+1. With enough leading zeros all roots can be nonnegative. Fixed-length padding must be consistent throughout: it cannot be counted as extra graph vertices. Goldman–Joichi–White factorization and Corollary2 say boards of equal padded length are rook equivalent exactly when their root multisets agree. Edges change two columns by moving a positive number of squares from one to the other, preserving rook equivalence; Theorem6 plus the immediate converse identifies edges with swaps of unequal entries yielding valid root words. Each swap changes heights by opposite amounts; both valid words give Ferrers boards, paying the geometric edge condition.

Route1: show a connected graph on at least3 boards must contain a triangle, excluding complete bipartite graphs. The local triangle mechanism is real: if two positions contain value a and a third contains b!=a and all three placements of b among those positions are valid, each pair differs by a single swap, giving K3. It is not universal. This is exactly why the source discusses odd cycles as well as triangles.

A hand-derived diagnostic: use root multiset {0,0,1,1,2}. The six valid words are A=00112, B=01012, C=01210, D=01120, E=00121, F=01201. The source's product formula yields binom(3,2)*binom(2,1)=6, so this list exhausts the equivalence class without a computational enumeration. Every listed word begins0 and rises by at most1. Their column-height vectors (one-based z_i=i-1-b_i) are respectively (0,1,1,2,2), (0,0,2,2,2), (0,0,0,2,4), (0,0,1,1,4), (0,1,1,1,3), (0,0,0,3,3); all are nonnegative weakly increasing, distinct even after identifying leading zeros. Direct comparison gives precisely AB, BC, CD, DE, EA, CF. Thus the graph is a five-cycle with one pendant edge. In particular the triangle-only lemma is false, while a genuine odd-cycle route remains plausible. This is already the source's small-case territory, not a new resolution.

Route2: exploit connectedness and the multiplicity product. In the nonnegative normalization the source connectedness theorem forces any repeated value below the next-to-largest level to propagate repetition to the next level. For a hypothesized K_(a,b), connectedness applies when a,b>=1. The number of vertices is product_i binom(v_i+v_(i+1)-1,v_(i+1)). A vertex count a+b alone cannot recover the adjacency graph; many multiplicity profiles have the same product. The source's K2,2 proof uses exactly this plus exceptional profiles, and cannot be promoted by replacing4 with a+b. Single-value mobility gives complete graphs, with K2 the permissible example. The six-word calculation above shows the next obstruction is an odd5-cycle rather than a universal triangle.

Route3: an ambient transposition argument. If a word has two valid swaps on disjoint pairs, doing both gives a potential square, but validity of the double swap must still be proved from the neighboring inequalities, especially where the swapped pairs touch. A complete bipartite conclusion would require controlling ALL common neighbors and excluding additional words; ambient permutation parity is useless for multiset words, since stabilizers contain odd transpositions of equal entries. The local inequalities cannot be dropped. No proof that every connected triangle-free root-word graph is either K1,K2 or contains an odd5-cycle was established.

Exact unpaid lemma: classify connected root-multiset swap graphs sufficiently to exclude every K_(a,b) with a+b>=3, including unbalanced stars; alternatively exhibit a full equivalence class that really is such a graph. The proposed odd-cycle embedding must preserve valid roots, padding identifications and the entirety of the equivalence class. K2,2 or K3,3 exclusions and single-profile diagnostics are not the all-parameter theorem. No formal candidate.

## 336207 — total dominator chromatic stability

Primary: Ghanbari–Alikhani arXiv:1705.10231v1; Introduction, Definition1.1, Theorems4.1–4.6/proof passages, Conjecture4.7. Arxiv submission29 May2017; PDF itself displays12 October2018. These different dates are recorded, not silently conflated into an authenticated new version. A TD-coloring is proper and each vertex is adjacent to ALL vertices of some nonempty other color class. Initially the definition requires no isolated vertices. Stability is the minimum number of deleted vertices changing chi_td. The source later uses deletions leaving isolated points (e.g. path proof) and gives an edgeless residual TD number1; no single reconciled convention was authenticated here. The literal MathDB record omits domain qualifiers. That is a semantic hold, not a new counterexample.

Route1, leaf: let v have only neighbor u, in a graph with a valid optimal k-color TD-coloring. Necessarily {u} is a singleton color class: the nonempty class dominated by v is a subset of {u}. Restrict to G-v. Properness remains. For x!=u,v, any previously dominated class could not contain v, since x is not adjacent to v. Such classes survive unchanged. Only u may lose its witness when the class was exactly {v}. If that class contains another vertex it survives and remains dominated by u. If {v} was singleton and u has a different neighbor w, recoloring w as a fresh singleton repairs u, and splitting a class does not remove other witnesses except an emptied singleton, in which case the replacement singleton has the same vertex. Hence chi_td(G-v)<=k+1 under the no-isolate hypothesis after deletion. This is an UPPER bound; it proves neither inequality nor equality with k, hence not stability1.

Route2, force a change by deleting u instead. This leaves v isolated, so it does not even stay inside the initial TD domain. Simply declaring its color irrelevant imports an unproved convention. Even if isolated vertices were ignored, vertices formerly dominated by {u} may have no intact witness after restriction. The forced singleton argument cannot establish chi_td(G-u)<=k-1. A local deletion affecting a color class is not a proof that the optimum changes.

Route3, degree2 vertex v with neighbors u,w. In any TD-coloring it dominates a class contained in {u,w}. Either one of the neighbors is singleton, or {u,w} is a two-element color class, which requires u,w nonadjacent. These cases exhaust nonempty subsets. Deleting {u,w} isolates v, repeating the domain problem. Deleting v can invalidate witnesses only for u,w, so at most two fresh singleton neighbor classes could repair a restriction if suitable surviving neighbors exist, but chi(G-v)<=k+2 still does not prove St<=2. The remaining need is an optimum-sensitive dichotomy: if no single-vertex deletion changes the optimum, some two-vertex deletion does, with all residual-domain conditions specified. This was not proved; special path/cycle values do not supply it for arbitrary graphs containing a low-degree vertex.

No endpoint correction is claimed as a solution. Unpaid: authenticate the deletion convention in the intended source/journal setting, and prove that optimum-sensitive dichotomy for all allowed graphs (or a complete counterexample under those same semantics). No dispatch.
