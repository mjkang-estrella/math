## 373908: degree-four median eigenvalues

Read the primary Conjecture 6.2, the spectral definitions, and the Section 2 quartic trace argument. The literal exception is important: projective-plane incidence components cannot themselves be submitted as counterexamples.

**Route A: perturb the extremal incidence graph.** Normalize the nonzero vectors of F_3^3 by their first nonzero coordinate to obtain 13 points and 13 line normals. Let B_xy=1 exactly when x dot y=0. Counting common incident lines gives B B^T=3I+J: each point lies on four lines and distinct points on one common line. Therefore its adjacency spectrum is +4,-4 and +sqrt(3),-sqrt(3), each latter with multiplicity 12. This is the explicitly excluded 26-vertex graph.

Deleting an edge or performing a degree-preserving two-switch breaks its incidence design while preserving maximum degree four. The script checks all 52 single-edge deletions and 400 seeded switch sequences of lengths 1 through 12. No diagnostic violation: the deletion medians have absolute value about 1.111264158, and the largest sampled switch median about 0.885159667. All graphs/edges are retained. This is not exhaustive isomorphism-class enumeration; the script does not certify nonisomorphism to the excluded design. A finite upper-bound scan alone cannot establish the conjecture.

**Route B: disconnected padding and inertia.** Eigenvalue multisets add under disjoint union. For a graph on n vertices, both medians lie in [-s,s] iff the numbers of eigenvalues strictly greater than s and strictly less than -s are each at most floor((n-1)/2). This follows directly by taking their positions in decreasing order. Consequently appending isolated vertices to one excluded design does not preserve its median obstruction: one isolated vertex gives central eigenvalue zero, and two isolated vertices give two central zeroes. A disjoint union of excluded designs remains excluded. Padding needs a component with a suitably unbalanced inertia count; arbitrary padding is not a counterexample mechanism. No such component was found or proved impossible.

**Route C: improve the magic polynomial.** The source proof uses tr(A)=0, tr(A^2)=sum degrees, tr(A^3)>=0, and tr(A^4)>=sum_v(2d_v^2-d_v), with eigenvalues in [-4,4]. The design saturates the moment identities at the sqrt(3) distribution. Thus these constraints cannot alone imply sqrt(2); a structural deficit excluding designs is necessary. Rank-two edge-deletion interlacing bounds the changes but does not provide the global central-eigenvalue gap. The unresolved lemma is a uniform inertia/structural-gap theorem for arbitrary degree-four components, rather than a proof for one perturbed design. Lean would require a genuine ordered-spectrum/characteristic-polynomial bridge, not a custom list called a spectrum.

## 374231: odd edge coloring

Read Conjecture 6, the four-connected theorem, the explicit degree-three obstruction (Theorem 10), and Section 5's obstruction to the proof strategy.

**Route A: transfer the cubic parity obstruction.** At a vertex of degree three, valid color-degree triples are permutations of (3,0,0) or (1,1,1). At degree four, they are permutations of (3,1,0). Theorem 10's bipartite double count relies precisely on the former restriction at all but one vertex. Raising minimum degree to four destroys the divisibility equation used in that proof; it is not a persistent obstruction. Globally each color's set of positive-degree vertices has even cardinality, by the handshake lemma. On odd-order graphs each color therefore misses an odd number of vertices. These necessary conditions do not contradict minimum degree four.

**Route B: test the source's failed-proof graph.** Take four copies of the octahedral graph K_6 minus a perfect matching, remove the edge (0,2) in each, add w adjacent to all 24 old vertices, add x1 adjacent to each copy's 0 vertex, x2 adjacent to each copy's 2 vertex, and edge x1x2. The source proves that a particular odd-support T-join required by its four-connected argument is absent. This is a failure of that sufficient method, not absence of a three-coloring. The diagnostic explicitly finds a three-coloring of this 27-vertex graph and validates each vertex/color degree by integer counting. Full edges and colors are retained in outputs/odd-diagnostic.json. Therefore this graph cannot be promoted as a conjecture counterexample.

**Route C: general parity-constrained edge assignment.** Encode every edge as exactly one color, and its incidence count at v,c as 2z_vc+y_vc, with y binary and count<=degree(v)*y. This enforces count zero or odd. A MILP solution is merely a proposed assignment; the script independently recomputes all incidence counts. All 13 tested odd circulants plus the source graph pass this integer verification. Neither optimization infeasibility nor timeouts would certify noncolorability. To finish positively one needs a construction/recoloring invariant across arbitrary three-vertex cuts that replaces the unavailable odd-support T-join. No such lemma was proved. Lean feasibility would be good for an explicit counterexample with a sound finite checker, but there is none here.

## 374390: Bose and minimum BCH distance

Read the primary code definitions, Conjecture 1.1, Theorem 4.4 and Remark 4.2. The domain requires t<m and an admissible designed distance; division by zero at t=m is not a campaign solution.

**Route A: exact binary cyclic generators.** In F_(2^m), take the closure of exponents 1,...,2^t under multiplication by two modulo 2^m-1. Multiply the corresponding linear factors. Frobenius stability puts the resulting generator in F_2[X]; the script asserts this directly and checks that its chosen field element has full multiplicative order. The shifts of this monic generator form the ordinary cyclic-code basis: multiples with degree below the length have unique quotient of degree below the dimension. Gray-code enumeration computes all nonzero weights when dimension<=20. There are 27 pairs (m,t), 3<=m<=8, 1<=t<m; 11 were fully enumerated, the other 16 were explicitly skipped. Every enumerated minimum equals the proposed Bose-distance formula. This establishes no result for skipped dimensions or arbitrary q.

**Route B: locator polynomials, not just a sweep.** The source obtains words of weight q^t+1 when m is a multiple of p*t using a polynomial (X-1)L(X) that splits appropriately in the extension field. The classical delta-divides-length cases are already known. Extending the same linearized-polynomial construction to arbitrary t requires a splitting/root-count argument without the subfield F_(q^t) sitting in F_(q^m). Such a subfield exists iff t divides m; pretending it is always present invalidates the construction. A BCH lower bound supplies d>=d_B but cannot produce the missing low-weight word.

**Route C: use the Bose jump.** The target is d_B, which need not be the nominal designed distance: for m=5,t=3 the tested binary generator has dimension 11 and minimum 11, while the nominal designed distance is 9. Searching only weight 9 would falsely suggest a counterexample. Even the source's known q=2,t=m-2 family must be separated from other pairs. A new full-scope proof needs either uniformly splitting locators at exactly d_B or another uniform low-weight construction; a counterexample needs an exact minimum-distance lower bound strictly exceeding d_B. Neither was obtained. No isolated numerical parameter instance would earn credit.
