                    3. Cauchy-Davenport type theorems
  The Cauchy-Davenport theorem, see [1] and [3], is one of the first significant
achievements in the field of additive theory:
Theorem 1 (Cauchy-Davenport theorem). Let A be a group of prime order p
and X, Y non-empty subsets of A. Then |X + Y | ≥ min(p, |X| + |Y | − 1).
   The theorem applies especially to the additive group of the integers modulo a
prime. Many extensions to composite moduli are known, and a couple of them, due
to I. Chowla and S. S. Pillai, have been recently sharpened in [12] as a byproduct
of Theorem 5 below. In the same paper, there also appears the following definition
(though in a different notation), which lies at the heart of the present work:
Definition 2. For a set X ⊆ A, we let
                          γA (X) := sup          inf      ord(x − x0 )
                                       x0 ∈X × x0 6=x∈X

Then, for X1 , . . . , Xn ⊆ A we define
                   γA (X1 , . . . , Xn ) := max(γA (X1 ), . . . , γA (Xn )), ,
and refer to γA (X1 , . . . , Xn ) as the Cauchy-Davenport constant of the n-tuple
(X1 , . . . , Xn ) relative to A (we omit the subscript ‘A’ if clear from the context).
   Any tuple of subsets of A has a well-defined Cauchy-Davenport constant (rela-
tive to A), and it is interesting to compare it with other “structural parameters”:
Lemma 3. Let X, Y be subsets of A and assume that A is cancellative and X × +
Y × is non-empty. Then γ(X, Y ) ≥ min(γ(X), γ(Y )) ≥ γ(X + Y ) ≥ p(A).
  Lemma 3 is proved by the end of Section 4 and applies, on the level of groups,
to any pair of non-empty subsets. The following example suggests that the in-
equalities in the lemma are, in general, rather pessimistic:
Example 4. Fix an integer m ≥ 2 and prime numbers p, q with m < p < q. Then
set n := mpq, X := {mk mod n : k = 0, . . . , p − 1} and Y := {mk mod n : k =
1, . . . , p}. We have |X + Y | = 2p, γ(X) = γ(Y ) = pq and γ(X + Y ) = q, while
p(Z/nZ) is the smallest prime divisor of m, where Z/nZ is the additive group

of the integers modulo n. Thus, γ(X, Y ) > γ(X + Y ) > p(Z/nZ), and indeed
γ(X + Y ) ≫ p(Z/nZ) for q ≫ m, and γ(X, Y ) ≫ γ(X + Y ) for p ≫ 2, where the
symbol ≫ reads “(comparatively) much larger than”.
  Note that, for X ⊆ A, it holds γ(X) = 0 if X × is empty, and then γ(·) provides
no significant information on A. However, this is not the case, e.g., when X 6= ∅
and A is a group (with the result that X × = X), which is the “moral basis” for
the non-trivial bounds below. To start with, we have:
Theorem 5. Suppose A is cancellative and let X, Y be non-empty finite subsets
of A such that hY i is commutative. Then |X + Y | ≥ min(γ(Y ), |X| + |Y | − 1).
    The result appears as Theorem 8 in [12] and leads us to the following:
Conjecture 6. Let X1 , . . . , Xn be subsets of A with |Xi | ≥ 2 for each i. If A is
cancellative, then |X1 + · · · + Xn | ≥ min(γ(X1 , . . . , Xn ), |X1 | + · · · + |Xn | + 1 − n).

   Next, we show with the following example that cancellativity is a somewhat
necessary assumption for anything in the lines of Conjecture 6 to be true:

Example 16. Let X and Y be non-empty disjoint sets with |X| < ∞, and denote
by (FX , ·X ) and (FY , ·Y ), respectively, the free abelian semigroups on X and Y .
For a fixed e ∈/ FX ∪ FY , we define a binary operation · on F := FX ∪ FY ∪ {e}
by u · v := u ·X v if u, v ∈ FX , u · v := u ·Y v if u, v ∈ FY , and u · v := e otherwise.
  It is seen that · is associative; we write F for a unitization of (F, ·) and 1 for the
identity of F. Then taking Z := Y ∪ {1} gives γF (Z) = ∞ and X · Z := {x · z :
x ∈ X, z ∈ Z} = X ∪ {e}, with the result that |X · Z| < |X| + |Z| − 1 ≤ γF (X, Z),
viz |X · Z| < min(γF (X, Z), |X| + |Z| − 1), where the right-hand side can be made
arbitrarily larger than the left-hand side.

   Lastly, we record here that if A is cancellative and X is a non-empty finite
subsemigroup of A with |X| ≥ 2, then |X + X| = |X| < 2|X| − 1, no matter
whether or not X contains units.
   On the one hand, this means that the trivial lower bound provided by Proposi-

is, in some sense, a trivial observation). On the other hand, the problem arises
of characterizing the pairs (X, Y ) of subsets of A such that |X + Y | < γ(X + Y ),
which we plan to investigate in future work.
