## 329274: complete fixed-alphabet reduction, not a new result

Exact source definition: Haviv–Xie arXiv:1411.4692v1, Section 1.2, Conjecture 1.4. Three distinct vectors form a sunflower when each coordinate is all equal or all distinct. The intended nondegenerate alphabet is D>=3. The record does not explicitly bind D; this is a fidelity qualification, not authorization to replace the problem by a parameter case.

Read Ellenberg–Gijswijt arXiv:1605.09223v1, Proposition 2, Theorem 4 and its proof, and Corollary 5. Theorem 4 gives, for A subset F_3^n with only diagonal solutions to a+b+c=0,
  |A| <= 3 m_(2n/3),
where m counts monomials with individual exponents in {0,1,2} and total exponent <=2n/3. This is a published theorem from May 2016, later than the November 2014 question and far earlier than this campaign. We use its exact statement, not a catalogue inference.

Explicit exponential bound without numerical diagnostics: for t=1/2 and every exponent vector e with sum(e)<=2n/3, t^sum(e)>=t^(2n/3). Therefore
 m_(2n/3) <= t^(-2n/3)(1+t+t^2)^n = c^n,
 c=(7/4)2^(2/3)<3,
since c^3=343/16<27. Put rho=c/3 in (0,1).

Fix any D>=3 and any sunflower-free F subset [D]^n. Independently choose a uniformly random three-element subset S_i of [D] for each coordinate. For each v, P(v in product S_i)=(3/D)^n. Thus E|F intersect product S_i|=|F|(3/D)^n. Relabel each S_i bijectively by F_3. This preserves all-equal/all-distinct coordinate patterns. In F_3 a+b+c=0 holds precisely for triples that are all equal or all distinct: if two entries agree then the third must agree, while the three distinct entries sum to zero. A vector solution which is not diagonal has three distinct vectors (two equal vectors in a solution force the third equal). Hence each relabelled intersection is cap-set-like in exactly Theorem 4's sense. Its size is at most 3c^n. Averaging yields
  |F| <= 3 (D rho)^n.

Set epsilon= -log(rho)/(2 log(D))>0. Choose n0 so 3 rho^(n/2)<1 for every n>=n0, which exists because sqrt(rho)<1. Then
  |F| < (D sqrt(rho))^n = D^((1-epsilon)n)
for n>=n0. Contraposition proves the complete intended fixed-D assertion for every fixed D>=3, with epsilon and n0 allowed to depend on D. This is a complete elementary bridge from an actual prior theorem, so it earns zero as a routine source/classical reduction. It is not rejected because it is short or familiar.

Two failed extensions are important. The epsilon above tends to zero as D grows, so it does NOT directly prove 329273, which requires one epsilon for all large D. Also D=2 is not an intended nondegenerate alphabet: at every coordinate, all-distinct is impossible, so no three distinct vectors can be a sunflower, and the entire cube is a counterexample. D=1 has the same no-three-distinct issue. These endpoint corrections earn zero and do not resolve the significant uniform statement by changing its domain.

## 327713: literal source-definition refutation; correction only

Section 2 defines simple digraphs without loops or repeated arcs and defines pancyclic to mean directed cycles of lengths 2 through n. Section 4 Conjecture 2 uses the stated 3n+1 inequalities when x,y are a nonadjacent pair. In the standard source convention such a pair consists of distinct vertices. The record's phrase 'every length allowed by the conjecture' must be resolved from this definition, not silently changed to 3 through n.

Let V={0,1,2} and A={(0,1),(1,2),(2,0)}. There are no loops or repeated arcs. For each ordered pair of distinct vertices, following the displayed triangle gives a directed path of one or two arcs, so the graph is strongly connected. Every unordered pair of distinct vertices carries one of the three arcs; hence there are no nonadjacent pairs. For every triple with x nonadjacent to y, the premise is false, so both universal degree implications hold, independently of the numerical threshold. There is no directed 2-cycle: each of the three arcs has its reverse absent. Therefore the digraph is not pancyclic under Section 2's explicit 2..n convention. This is a complete ordinary counterexample under the source's conventional distinct-pair semantics. The MathDB wording does not explicitly specify distinctness or a length interval, so literal machine-statement fidelity itself stays HOLD until these semantics are fixed.

## 331918: sustained spectral and fibre-compression routes, no endpoint

Read arXiv:1511.08694v2 Section 5, Conjecture 19, Theorems 20–22, and proof of Theorem 4 through the boundary comparison (42). Read the final Cambridge journal conclusion saying Conjecture 19 remains open. The record's reference to a conjecture 'first proved' belongs instead to Conjecture 23 (Cameron–Ku) in Section 6. It cannot clear the all-size Ben Efraim statement.

Definitions: T_n has S_n as vertices, with two permutations adjacent if their quotient is a transposition (equivalently swap two positions). It is regular of degree Delta=binom(n,2). For A, |boundary A|=Delta|A|-2e(A), since summing degrees counts internal edges twice. Lex order compares the first position at which image values differ.

Route 1, exact classical spectral bridge: write f=1_A and mu=|A|/n!. The Laplacian has gap n (source formula (39) and dominance comparison (40)); the constant component has energy zero. Consequently
 |boundary A|=n!<f,Lf> >= n n! ||f-mu||^2
 = n|A|(1-|A|/n!) = |A|(n!-|A|)/(n-1)!.
For |A|=k(n-1)!, the lex family is {sigma: sigma(1)<=k}. Each vertex has exactly n-k transpositions moving its first image outside [k], so boundary=k(n-k)(n-1)!, exactly the spectral lower bound. Thus this entire cardinality subfamily is already covered by a full prior-result bridge and is not a new endpoint. For arbitrary sizes the spectral inequality need not equal the lex boundary. The source's Theorem 4 only covers (n-t)! for n sufficiently large depending on t; its proof uses stability and positive error terms for sufficiently large n. Neither quantifier can be dropped.

Route 2, fibre induction: partition S_n into fibres sigma(1)=i. Each fibre induces T_(n-1). Between distinct fibres i,j, every vertex has exactly one neighbour in the other fibre: swap position 1 with the unique position carrying the other value. The cross-fibre graph is a perfect matching. If A_i are the slices and a_i=|A_i|, then
 e(A) <= sum_i E_(n-1)(a_i) + sum_(i<j) min(a_i,a_j),
where E_(n-1)(a) is the maximum induced-edge count at size a. This inequality is proved by bounding each cross matching separately. Unfortunately it loses their simultaneous compatibility.

The obstruction can be made explicit without enumeration. T_3 is K_(3,3): permutations split by parity, and each even permutation is adjacent to all three odd permutations (the three transpositions are all odd permutations in S_3). Hence E_3(3)=2. For n=4 and slice sizes (3,3,3,0), this relaxation permits 3*2+3*3=15 internal edges. The lex set of size 9 has a full first fibre of size6 and an initial second-fibre segment of size3. The full T_3 has9 edges; the second segment has2; cross matching contributes3; total14. Thus the generic fibre bound permits a value above the lex objective and cannot prove the desired inequality merely by independently maximizing slices and cross matchings. This is NOT an actual counterexample: simultaneous attainment of those three cross matchings was not shown. A new coherence inequality linking the cross-fibre matchings, or a compression preserving their combined edge count, is the precise remaining lemma.

Route 3, source stability refinement: the actual proof decomposes E=A\C and M=C\A near a t-coset, uses e(E,C)<=|E| and expansion within C, and concludes a strictly positive boundary increase only when the n-dependent error is small. For all-size lex sets, C is generally a union of unequal nested fibres, not a single t-coset; the one-neighbour bound into C no longer supplies the same error estimate. An all-size stability/compression theorem has not been obtained. Park, no enumeration, no solver, no Pi plan.
