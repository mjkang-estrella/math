## 347655: exact threshold, complete public-counterpart bridge

Primary: Kaul–Mudrock–Pelsmajer, arXiv:2008.08926v2, Conjecture 9; final Australasian J. Combin. 80(3) (2021) 419–441, Conjecture 1.9, printed p424. Equitable means each color used at most ceil(n/k), for every assignment of exactly k colors per vertex; color classes induce forests. It does NOT require a proper coloring or equally sized classes for an arbitrary color palette. The final introduction and extension lemma/proof were read, not merely the abstract. Theorem 1.6 only gives k>=p; Proposition 1.8 improves to p-1. Neither settles all p. My initial suspicion that the source already proves the exact threshold was overturned by the actual body.

Route A, single-vertex greedy: since at most floor(p/2) colors occur twice in the previous p vertices, k>=ceil((p+1)/2) gives a color with at most one previous neighbor of that color. This preserves a forest (a newly added vertex has at most one earlier monochromatic neighbor). It does not bound total color use by ceil(n/k). Saturated colors can consume the available choices. This proves only a nonequitable helper and is not a full solution.

Route B, remove k initial vertices and give them distinct colors: the established extension lemma needs |N(v_i) outside S|<=2i-1. For S the first k vertices this quantity can be p-k+i, which at i=1 is p-k+1, generally greater than1 at the conjectural k. Failure of this sufficient lemma is not a counterexample. Increasing to 2k vertices and retaining two uses of each color avoids this bottleneck.

For all positive n,p,k with p<=2k-1, prove sufficiency by strong induction on n, including n<p+1 in induction. If n<=k greedily assign distinct colors: each list has k colors and fewer than k colors have been used. If k<n<=2k greedily assign each color at most twice: before a step fewer than2k vertices are colored, so fewer than k colors have already been used twice; at least one choice remains in a k-element list. Thus classes have size <=ceil(n/k) and at most2 vertices, hence are forests.

For n>2k, delete S={v_1,...,v_(2k)} and apply induction to the remaining path power, for the restricted arbitrary k-lists. Each old color class has size <=ceil((n-2k)/k)=ceil(n/k)-2. For color c in L(v_i), let t_i(c) be the number of c-colored outside neighbors. Start with slots (c,0),(c,1). Keep both when t=0, only (c,0) when t=1, and neither when t>=2. Outside neighbor count r_i is max(0,min(n, i+p)-2k)<=i-1. Deleting slots costs min(t_i(c),2)<=t_i(c); summing over c in L(v_i) costs at most r_i. Therefore the slot list M_i has size >=2k-i+1.

One can choose distinct slots greedily in order v_(2k),v_(2k-1),...,v_1: when reaching v_i at most2k-i slots have been used and |M_i|>=2k-i+1. This is the public Hall condition written as an explicit constructive choice; no unproved matching selection remains in the ordinary argument. Project the selected slots to colors. Each color appears at most twice in S. Any new use with an outside neighbor requires (c,0), so there is at most one such use and it has exactly one outside neighbor. The new monochromatic graph has <=2 vertices and is a forest; the old one is a forest; at most one joining edge exists, so their union cannot contain a cycle. This last fact follows because a cycle crossing the two parts must cross the cut at least twice. Counts are <=ceil(n/k)-2+2. This proves sufficiency for EVERY admissible k, not just the minimum one; no monotonicity in list size was assumed.

For necessity with n>=p+1, the first p+1 vertices form a clique. Assign the same k colors everywhere. If 2k<p+1, some color appears at least3 times in this clique, giving a triangle; a forest cannot contain it. Integer k>=ceil((p+1)/2) is equivalent to2k>=p+1.
