Let a_i=(n_{1,i},n_{2,i},n_{3,i})^t, N=(n_1,n_2,n_3)^t, B=J_3-I_3, E=n_1n_2+n_1n_3+n_2n_3, c_i=n_{1,i}n_{2,i}+n_{1,i}n_{3,i}+n_{2,i}n_{3,i}, and d_i=a_i^t B N. Each component is nonempty and every original vertex has positive degree, so d_i>0. Summation gives sum_i d_i=N^t B N=2E>0. Set S=sum_i c_i/d_i.

CONTRACTION BRIDGE. Contract every tree component of F. Between distinct contracted vertices i,j the number of parallel original edges is w_ij=a_i^t B a_j. Loops are discarded. A tree extending F contracts to a tree of this multigraph with each chosen parallel edge retaining its original identity. Conversely a contracted tree together with F is connected and has (n-k)+(k-1)=n-1 edges, hence is a tree. These maps are mutually inverse. Thus its weighted simple-graph tree sum tau, with weights w_ij, equals tau_F. The contracted multigraph is connected: the image of any original path gives a walk. This includes k=1, for which tau=1.

Let U have columns a_i, D=diag(d_i), A=U^t B U. These are auxiliary matrices: A has diagonal 2c_i, retained solely for normalization; these diagonal numbers are not loops being counted in tau. Its row sums are d_i. Hence L=D-A is exactly the loopless weighted Laplacian: off-diagonal entries -w_ij and diagonal sum_{j!=i}w_ij. Define H=D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2} and K=I-H=D^{-1/2} L D^{-1/2}. The vector v=(sqrt(d_i)) satisfies Hv=v. The identity x^t L x=sum_{i<j}w_ij(x_i-x_j)^2 shows positive semidefiniteness and, by connectedness, ker L=span(1). Thus ker K=span(v).

INERTIA BRIDGE WITHOUT AN UPSTREAM CLASSIFICATION. For y in R^3, y^t B y=(sum_s y_s)^2-sum_s y_s^2. On the two-dimensional plane sum_s y_s=0 this is nonpositive. Consequently the quadratic form of H is nonpositive on the codimension-at-most-one subspace of vectors x with sum_s(UD^{-1/2}x)_s=0. If H had two positive eigenvalues, their orthogonal eigenvectors would span a two-dimensional positive definite subspace meeting that hyperplane nontrivially, a contradiction. As 1 is already a positive eigenvalue, all other eigenvalues are <=0. Write them -u_1,...,-u_{k-1} with u_j>=0, allowing zeros. Symmetry gives a real orthonormal eigenbasis. In particular no Perron hypothesis about diagonal loops is needed.

TRACE BRIDGE. tr H=sum_i A_ii/d_i=2S. On the eigenbasis it is 1-sum_j u_j. Hence sum_j u_j=1-2S. The nonzero eigenvalues of K are 1+u_j. Their product P satisfies P>=1+sum_j u_j=2(1-S): expand the finite product, whose omitted terms are all nonnegative. This expansion is an ordinary algebraic argument, not finite enumeration or an experiment.

NORMALIZED MATRIX-TREE BRIDGE. Every principal cofactor of L is tau. For completeness this follows by orienting the weighted edges and writing a deleted-row incidence matrix C: the deleted Laplacian is C diag(w) C^t. Cauchy–Binet expands its determinant as a sum over (k-1)-edge sets of det(C_T)^2 product(w_e). If those edges contain a cycle their columns are dependent (the oriented cycle sum is zero). If disconnected the rows have a dependence supported on a component missing the deleted vertex. For a tree the determinant is +/-1, proved by removing a leaf different from the deleted root and expanding its single incident entry; induction ends with the empty determinant1. Thus exactly trees contribute, each once with its edge-weight product. This also handles parallel edges by keeping incidence columns distinct, or by grouping their weights.

For k>=2 the corresponding principal minor of K is tau/(product_{j!=i}d_j). The coefficient of t in det(K+tI) is the sum of its principal (k-1)-minors, therefore tau*(sum_i d_i)/(product_i d_i). By diagonalization the same coefficient is P (the zero eigenvalue is simple). Consequently
 tau = (product_i d_i)/(2E) * P >= (product_i d_i)/E * (1-S),
which is exactly the immutable displayed lower bound. For k=1, d_1=2E, c_1=E, so the right side is1=tau. Thus every forest component count is paid. Integer tree counts are cast to reals before division. There is no assumption about sizes of individual components beyond nonemptiness and no missing asymptotic range.
