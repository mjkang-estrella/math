                                          1    Introduction
                                          We denote by [q, k] the integer interval {q, q + 1, . . . , k}, and write [k] for [1, k]. Given a collection
                                          of graphs G1 , . . . , Gk , the Ramsey number R(G1 , . . . , Gk ) is defined as the smallest positive integer
                                          n such that every edge-coloring τ : E(Kn ) → [k] contains a monochromatic copy of Gi in color i for
                                          some i ∈ [k]. When G1 = · · · = Gk = G, it is customary to abbreviate R(G1 , . . . , Gk ) as Rk (G).
                                              A linear forest is a forest whose connected components are nontrivial paths. If all its components
                                          have the same order, then the linear forest is called a path-matching. Such a graph is usually denoted
                                          by nPm , namely, the vertex-disjoint union of n copies of the path Pm on m vertices.
                                              In this paper, we are mainly concerned with the case m = 3. A copy of K1,2 ∼         = P3 is often called

    Ramsey theory admits numerous variations, one of which restricts the allowable colorings and
thereby often simplifies the multicolor setting. An edge-coloring τ : E(G) → [k] is called a Gallai
coloring if it contains no rainbow triangle, that is, no triangle whose three edges receive pairwise
distinct colors. This notion originates from work of Gallai [8, 11] and is supported by a fundamental
structural theorem.

Theorem 1.7 (Gallai, 1967). Let (G, τ ) be a Gallai k-colored complete graph on at least two vertices.
Then the vertex set of G can be partitioned into nonempty parts V1 , . . . , Vp with p ≥ 2 such that at
most two colors appear on the edges between different parts, and for each pair (Vi , Vj ), all edges
between Vi and Vj receive the same color.

    Motivated by this restriction, the Gallai–Ramsey number GR(G1 , . . . , Gk ) is defined as the small-
est integer N such that every Gallai coloring τ : E(KN ) → [k] contains a monochromatic copy of Gi
in color i for some i ∈ [k]. When G1 = · · · = Gk = G, we simply write GRk (G).
    We now consider Gallai–Ramsey numbers for disjoint unions of cherries. Owing to the structural
restrictions imposed by Gallai colorings, these numbers admit a simple uniform formula, in contrast
to the situation for general multicolor colorings.
    Wu, Magnant, Nowbandegani, and Xia [15] studied the Gallai–Ramsey number of cherries nP3 .
They provided upper and lower bounds for GR(n1 P3 , . . . , nk P3 ) and conjectured that the given lower
bound is the exact value.

GAUSS DEFINITION   Following [24, Deﬁnition 1], a subset G of A is called a gaussoid on [n] if it satisﬁes the
following four conditions for all pairwise distinct i, j, k ∈ [n] and all L ⊆ [n]\{i, j, k}:
(G1) {aij|L , aik|jL } ⊂ G implies {aik|L , aij|kL} ⊂ G,

(G2) {aij|kL , aik|jL} ⊂ G implies {aij|L , aik|L } ⊂ G,

(G3) {aij|L , aik|L } ⊂ G implies {aij|kL , aik|jL} ⊂ G,
                                                         
(G4) {aij|L , aij|kL } ⊂ G implies aik|L ∈ G or ajk|L ∈ G .
Axiom (G1) is the deﬁnition of a semigraphoid, and (G2) is known as the intersection axiom.
Axiom (G3) is a converse to intersection, and axiom (G4) is called weak transitivity.
    Being a gaussoid is a necessary condition for a subset G ⊆ A to comprise the vanishing
almost-principal minors of a positive deﬁnite symmetric n × n-matrix Σ. The gaussoid G is
called realizable if such a matrix Σ exists. All gaussoids are realizable for n = 3. This is no
longer true for n ≥ 4, as shown in [10, 24]. For an explicit example see Remark 4.3 below.

    Gaussoids are analogous to matroids. In matroid theory, one asks which sets of maximal
minors of a rectangular matrix can be simultaneously nonzero. Being a matroid is necessary
but not suﬃcient for this to hold. Vámos [42] suggested that there is no ﬁnite axiom system
for realizability of matroids. Mayhew, Newman and Whittle [27, 28] ﬁnally proved this fact,
and Sullivant [39] established the same result for gaussoids.
    One of the many axiom systems for matroids is the combinatorial compatibility with the
quadratic Plücker relations that deﬁne the Grassmannian [8, § 4]. Our aim is to derive the
analogous result for gaussoids. The role of the Grassmannian Gr(n, 2n) is now played by a
projection of the Lagrangian Grassmannian LGr(n, 2n), namely the variety V (Jn ).

all f in Jn if and only if it is realizable by a symmetric n × n-matrix Σ with complex entries.
