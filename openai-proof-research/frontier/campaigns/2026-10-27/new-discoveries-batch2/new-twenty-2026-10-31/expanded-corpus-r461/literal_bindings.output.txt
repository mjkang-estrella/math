330519 Let a graph $G$ be a **$(\Delta+1)$-graph** if it admits an incidence coloring with $\Delta(G)+1$ colors, and let $\mathbin{\square}$ denote the Cartesian product of graphs. Let $H$ be a graph whose incidence chromatic number satisfies $\chi_i(H)\leq\Delta(H)+2$. **Incidence coloring conjecture for Cartesian products.** If $G$ is a $(\Delta+1)$-graph and $H$ satisfies $\chi_i(H)\leq\Delta(H)+2$, then 
$$
\chi_i(G\mathbin{\square}H)\leq\Delta(G\mathbin{\square}H)+2.
$$
 This would extend the classes of Cartesian products known to satisfy the Brualdi–Massey bound; the source proves the result under additional degree conditions, but leaves this general assertion as a belief.
330841 Let $G$) be an infinite graph with vertex set $E$. For $x\in E$, write $G|E\setminus\{x\}$ for the subgraph induced by deleting $x$, and write $\sim$ for graph isomorphism. **Ulam reconstruction conjecture.** If

$$

332481 Let $\mathcal{G}$ be an oligomorphic permutation group on a set $G$, and let $\mathbb{G}$ be a countable smooth digraph of algebraic length $1$ on $G$ that is invariant under $\mathcal{G}$. A **pseudoloop** is an edge obtained from a loop by applying elements of $\mathcal{G}$ to its endpoints. **Pseudoloop lemma conjecture.** Either $\mathbb{G}$ contains a pseudoloop, or $\mathbb{G}$ together with the orbits of $\mathcal{G}$ on finite tuples pp-interprets $\mathbb{K}_3$ with parameters, where $\mathbb{K}_3=(\{1,2,3\};\neq)$. This would generalize the finite loop lemma to the stated infinite, invariant-digraph setting; the conjecture is presented as open in the source.
332894 Let $s,k,n\in\mathbb{N}$ satisfy

$$
n\geq(s+1)k.
$$

Let $d\in\mathbb{N}$, and let $\mathcal{F}\subseteq[n]^{(k)}$ have matching number $m(\mathcal{F})=s$. If

$$
|\mathcal{F}|\geq\max\left\{\binom{k(s+1)-1}{k}+1,\binom{n}{k}-\binom{n-s}{k}-\binom{n-s-d}{k-1}+\binom{n-s-d}{k-d}\right\},
$$

**Sharp stability conjecture.** There exists $B\in[n]^{(s)}$ such that

$$
|\mathcal{F}\setminus\operatorname{OR}_B|\leq\binom{n-s-d}{k-d}.
$$

This conjecture strengthens the paper's stability theorem for the Erdős matching problem. The surrounding text identifies resolving the Erdős matching conjecture itself as the main open question and presents this as a further sharp stability problem.
333017 Let $H$ be a hypergraph on vertex set $[n]$, let $M$ be a positive integer, and let $f$ be an objective function used to assign each edge its weight. Write $Z(H,M,f)$ for the set of isolating weights, and let $Y(M,n)$ denote the universal lower bound on $|Z(H,M,f)|$. The singleton hypergraph $S_n$ has vertex set $[n]$ and edges $\\{1\\},\dots,\\{n\\}$.

**Singleton-hypergraph extremal conjecture.**

$$
Y(M,n) = n\sum_{i=0}^{M-1} i^{n-1}
$$

The conjecture asserts that the singleton hypergraph, and equivalently its complementary hypergraph in the stated extremal count, gives the tight universal bound for the number of isolating weight functions.
333089 Let $q$ be odd and $2\le k\le q-2$. Represent a word of $\mathbb{F}_q^{q+1}$ as $(u_f,v)$, where $u_f=(f(\alpha_1),\ldots,f(\alpha_q))$ is defined by a polynomial $f$ of degree at most $q-1$ and $v\in\mathbb{F}_q$.

**Projective deep-hole conjecture.** The set

$$
\{(u_f,v)\mid \deg(f)=k,\ v\in\mathbb{F}_q\}
$$

is precisely the set of all deep holes of $PRS(q+1,k)$.

The conjecture is stronger than the covering-radius conjecture and aims to classify every deep hole of these projective Reed–Solomon codes. The paper proves the assertion under additional hypotheses and gives partial exclusion results for words with $\deg(f)\ge k+1$.
333091 Let $q$ be odd, and let $C$ be a linear MDS code with parameters $[q+1,k]$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Its covering radius is denoted by $\rho(C)$.

**General covering-radius conjecture.** For $2\le k\le q-2$,

$$
\rho(C)=q-k.
$$

This extends the covering-radius conjecture from projective Reed–Solomon codes to every MDS code of the longest conjecturally possible length $q+1$. It is known in several parameter ranges; the source notes that non-PRS MDS codes exist, so the claim does not follow formally from the projective Reed–Solomon case.
333538 Let $bbda=(bbda_1,dots,bbda_m)$ be a partition, and let $C_{bbda}(x,q)$ be its orbit $q$-Eulerian polynomial. A factor is **unitary** when it is a factor of the form $1+xq^j$. **Rectangularity conjecture.** The polynomial $C_{bbda}(x,q)$ has a unitary factor if and only if $bbda=(r^m)$ is a rectangle with $r$ odd and $m$ even. In that case,

$$
C_{r,m}(x,q)=(1+xq^{\frac{rm}{2}})C'_{r,m}(x,q),
$$

where $C'_{r,m}(x,q)$ has no unitary factor. This generalizes the established one-column factorization and the cited equal-part odd case, but the full assertion remains open.
333728 Let $T$ be a tree with $n+1$ vertices, and identify the vertices of $K_{2n+1}$ with the integers modulo $2n+1$. A cyclic decomposition into copies of $T$ consists of the cyclic shifts of one copy of $T$ forming an edge decomposition. **Ringel--Kotzig conjecture.** For any $(n+1)$-vertex tree $T$, the complete graph $K_{2n+1}$ can be cyclically decomposed into copies of $T$. This strengthens Ringel's conjecture by requiring the decomposition to arise from cyclic shifts, and remains open in the source's discussion.

334807 Let $G$ be a graph. Write ${\rm STAB}(G)$ for its stable set polytope and ${\rm ASTAB}^*(G)$ for the polyhedral relaxation associated with joined a-perfect graphs. A graph is **${\rm LS}_+$-perfect** when ${\rm LS}_+(G)={\rm STAB}(G)$, where ${\rm LS}_+(G)$ is the Lovász–Schrijver positive semidefinite relaxation applied to ${\rm ESTAB}(G)$.

**${\rm LS}_+$-perfect graph conjecture.** A graph $G$ is ${\rm LS}_+$-perfect if and only if

$$
{\rm STAB}(G)={\rm ASTAB}^*(G).
$$

Equivalently, the conjecture asserts that ${\rm LS}_+$-perfect graphs coincide with joined a-perfect graphs, with ${\rm ASTAB}^*(G)$ serving as the corresponding polyhedral relaxation of ${\rm STAB}(G)$.
336031 Let $T$ be a locally finite tree, and write $S\equiv^{\sharp}T$ when $S$ and $T$ are mutually topological minors, meaning $S\leq^{\sharp}T$ and $T\leq^{\sharp}S$. Count the isomorphism classes of such trees $S$.

**Topological Tree Alternative Conjecture.** For a given locally finite tree $T$, the number of isomorphism classes of trees that are mutually topological minors with $T$ is either $1$ or $\mathfrak c$.

This is the topological-minor analogue of the Tree Alternative Conjecture. It gives a sharp alternative for the size of each mutual-topological-minor equivalence class, and the supplied text does not state that it has been resolved.

A graph class is **$k$-cluster critical** if it is obtained as a wedge-product of $v$ copies of $\mathcal{I}$ and $p$ copies of $\mathcal{P}$, in some order, with $v+2p=k+1$. **Cluster-critical conjecture.**

\chi_{\star}(\mathcal{M})\geqslant k+1

 if and only if $\mathcal{G}\not\subseteq\mathcal{M}$ for some $k$-cluster critical class $\mathcal{G}$. 337425 Let $J_n$ be the ideal whose quadrics include the trinomials defining compatibility of gaussoids, and let a **gaussoid** be a collection of almost-principal minors satisfying the gaussoid axioms. A gaussoid is **compatible** with a polynomial when the corresponding vanishing and nonvanishing pattern satisfies that polynomial.

**All-quadrics compatibility conjecture.** Every gaussoid is compatible with all quadrics in $J_n$, not just trinomials.

This conjecture proposes that compatibility with the quadratic relations of $J_n$ is already determined by the trinomial relations. The paper presents it as a direction for developing gaussoid theory in parallel with matroid theory; its resolution is not given here.
337512 Let $P_3$ denote the path on three vertices, and let $gr_k(K_3:n_1P_3, ldots,n_kP_3)$ be the corresponding multicolor Gallai-Ramsey number. For positive integers $k,n_1,n_2,\ldots,n_k$ with $n_1=\max\{n_i\}$, **the $P_3$ Gallai-Ramsey conjecture.**

337592 Let a **TDAG** be a transitive directed acyclic graph, let d-minor operations be the directed-minor operations for TDAGs, and let a class be **d-minor-closed** when it is closed under those operations. For a class $\mathcal{G}$ of TDAGs, let $G_1,\ldots,G_r$ be TDAGs.

**A Wagner conjecture for TDAGs.** Every d-minor-closed class $\mathcal{G}$ of TDAGs has a finite set of excluded TDAGs $R=\{G_1,\ldots,G_r\}$ such that

$$
G\in\mathcal{G}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\text{no }G_i\in R\text{ is a d-minor of }G.
$$

This is the directed analogue of Wagner's conjecture for undirected graph minors. The supplied text presents it as a major question and gives no resolution, so its status remains open.
340514 Let $G$ be a connected cubic graph, and let $V(G)$ denote its vertex set. A **dominating set** is a subset of vertices such that every vertex outside the subset has a neighbor in it. **Reed's conjecture.** Every connected cubic graph $G$ contains a dominating set of at most

$$
\left\lceil \frac{|V(G)|}{3} \right\rceil
$$

vertices. This conjecture gives a universal upper bound on the domination number of connected cubic graphs; the source presents it as a statement that has been suggested and widely discussed, without supplying evidence of a resolution.
340826 Let $G$ be a $k$-connected graph, and let $S$ be a set of $k$ independent edges of $G$. Here, independent edges have no common endpoint.

**Lovász–Woodall conjecture.** If $k$ is even or $G-S$ is connected, then $G$ contains a cycle containing every edge in $S$.

This conjecture is part of the classical problem of finding cycles through prescribed edges. The supplied source does not state whether it has been resolved.
370193 Let $T$ be a tree with $n$ edges, and let $K_{n,n}$ be the balanced complete bipartite graph with $n$ vertices in each part.

**Graham–Häggkvist conjecture.** The edge set of $K_{n,n}$ can be decomposed into copies of every $n$-edge tree $T$.

370999 Let $G$ be a connected cubic graph of order $n$, and let $i(G)$ denote its independent domination number. Reed's bound concerns domination in cubic graphs, and the exceptional graphs here are $K_{3,3}$ and the $5$-prism $C_5 \mathbin{\Box} K_2$.

**Goddard–Henning conjecture.** If

$$
G \notin \{K_{3,3}, C_5 \mathbin{\Box} K_2\},

then

$$
i(G) \leq \frac{3}{8}n.

The conjecture asks whether Reed's $\frac{3}{8}$ upper bound for the domination number extends to independent domination, apart from the two stated exceptions. The supplied source presents it as a conjecture; its resolution is not specified here.
