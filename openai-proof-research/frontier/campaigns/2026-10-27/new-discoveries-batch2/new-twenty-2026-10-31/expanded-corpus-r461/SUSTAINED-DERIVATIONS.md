## 337425: all quadrics of the gaussoid ideal

Source actually read: Geometry of gaussoids, current unversioned PDF delivering 1710.07175, definitions pp2–6, Theorem2.4 proof, Conjecture2.5, Corollary4.4 proof, Proposition6.4 and Theorem6.5 statement and substantial proof pp20–24. This is not a final-journal equivalence assertion. Compatibility means that a polynomial has either zero or at least two surviving monomials after setting precisely the almost-principal variables in G to zero. Principal variables are never in G. It does NOT mean setting all other variables to one solves the relation. G1–G4 are the four three-index axioms printed on p4.

Route A, extend the three-face argument. Every edge trinomial lives on a three-dimensional face; restricting a gaussoid to each such face proves compatibility with all edge trinomials (source Theorem2.4). Square trinomials have two principal-only monomials, so are automatically compatible. One might try deleting an index from an arbitrary quadric, applying induction, then reassembling. The difficulty is real: a quadric can involve a partition of an arbitrarily large index set. Distinct surviving monomials can become identified under reassembly or cancel between weight components. No bounded-three-face support decomposition of an arbitrary circuit was obtained. The exact missing lemma is: for every n and every circuit q of (J_n)_2, the G1–G4 support conditions prohibit exactly one monomial of q from surviving. Checking just the local edge circuits is precisely the original hypothesis, not this lemma.

Route B, representation-theoretic spanning. Theorem6.5 supplies four highest-weight families and their lowering operations; the families include alternating partition sums with unbounded m. Accepting its spanning result does not complete the target. The predicate 'number of surviving monomials is not one' is not closed under linear combinations: abstractly, x²+y² and x²+2y² each have two survivors, while their difference has only one. This example is a logical obstruction to the proposed inference, not a gaussoid counterexample or an assertion that these particular forms lie in J_n. Thus even checking every displayed highest-weight generator and its lowerings would leave cancellation in arbitrary linear combinations unpaid. An exact linear-algebra reformulation is useful: let pi_G kill degree-two monomials divisible by a variable in G. The target is that pi_G((J_n)_2) contains no nonzero scalar multiple of a surviving monomial. A proof must establish that statement for the entire image subspace, rather than a selected spanning list. Equivalently, for the coefficient pairing on surviving monomials, for each surviving monomial m there must be a vector in the annihilator of pi_G((J_n)_2) with nonzero m-coordinate. No construction of these separating vectors from G1–G4 was obtained. This is an exact all-n gap, not a computational proposal.

Route C, realization. A complex symmetric realization with all principal minors nonzero and exactly G vanishing almost-principal minors would imply compatibility with EVERY relation: a unique surviving nonzero monomial cannot evaluate to zero. Source Corollary4.4 does this for n=4 by non-Gaussian real realizations in symmetry orbits; those public small cases earn zero. Positive Gaussian realizability is stronger, and its failure is not a counterexample to quadratic compatibility. Proposition6.4, which gives scheme-theoretic quadratic defining equations, also does not turn a combinatorial support pattern into a point of that scheme. No arbitrary-gaussoid complex realization or weaker degree-two separating construction was found. Search identified Boege's 2022 dissertation and a talk repeating this question; those bodies were NOT acquired and supply no proof clearance. They are not identified affirmative proofs. A new proof here would have substantial all-n content; no routine-adaptation exclusion is justified. PARK at the circuit/separating-vector lemma.

## 330519: incidence coloring under Cartesian products

Source actually read: Gregor–Luzar–Sotak 1505.04908v3, definitions, Observation1, Theorem4 and its full proof, Corollary5, and Conclusion Conjecture3. Figures2 and3 on printed pages4 and5 were rendered and inspected. Figure2 distinguishes arbitrary (Delta+1)-graphs from the stronger one-incoming-color property; Figure3 displays all three mixed-axis conflicts. The remaining Section4 special-class construction was only partially read; no claim of full-paper reading.

Write g=Delta(G), h=Delta(H). Disjoint palettes need (g+1)+(h+2)=g+h+3 colors, one more than the target. The empty-factor/edgeless-factor cases need no new mechanism: if G is edgeless, the product is a disjoint union of copies of H; if H is edgeless, it is a disjoint union of copies of G. Assume both have edges.

Route A, overlap and missing-color replacement. Take a palette A of size g+1 for G and a palette B for H, inside q=g+h+2 colors. For each v of H, replace shared G colors by an injective map into B minus the entire H-spectrum at v, and use the same replacement in that G-fiber. Same-axis conflicts remain absent because the replacement is injective. Every mixed-axis conflict compares a G color outside the H-spectrum at v with an H color in that spectrum: this covers outgoing-outgoing at the same vertex, incoming-G/outgoing-H, and outgoing-G/incoming-H. This proves the source construction exactly when the injections exist.

For H contained in a d-regular (d+1)-graph H', use B of size d+1 and overlap of size d-h. Its inherited H coloring has spectrum size deg_H(v)+1, so at least d-deg_H(v) >= d-h missing colors. The condition d-h <= g+1 permits the palettes. This recovers Theorem4 completely and, taking H'=K_|H|, Corollary5. These are public proper-scope results, not a full solution. The literal assumption chi_i(H)<=h+2 gives only h+2 palette colors, and at a degree-h vertex the incoming incidences can use TWO colors, filling the entire palette. Then there is no missing color to absorb even a one-color overlap. Palette count alone therefore does not extend this mechanism. No regular supergraph H' satisfying the required degree surplus was supplied for every H in the literal hypothesis.

Route B, merge incoming colors. At a product vertex of maximum degree g+h, its g+h outgoing incidence colors are distinct. All incoming incidence colors avoid all outgoing colors. With q=g+h+2 total colors, the union of incoming colors can therefore have at most two colors. A regular (g+1)-colorable G has one incoming color at each vertex; a regular H with an (h+2)-coloring has at most two. Naively juxtaposing them gives up to three incoming colors. A more flexible full proof could align the G incoming singleton with one of H's incoming colors using permutations depending on fibers. Let C_v(u,u') be the G-arc color and D_u(v,v') the H-arc color. Exact mixed-axis requirements are O_G(u,v) disjoint S_H(u,v) and O_H(u,v) disjoint S_G(u,v), with O outgoing and S full spectra. Same-axis constraints must also hold within each fiber. Local alignment of incoming colors alone does not ensure these stronger disjointness constraints. The unpaid lemma is existence of fiber colorings satisfying these two set-disjointness conditions simultaneously for all u,v, from just the two literal factor bounds. No global compatible choice of permutations or recolorings was proved.

Route C, square coloring. A (q,1)-incidence coloring corresponds to a square-graph vertex coloring. If both factors had suitable locally injective vertex labels one could seek a product label construction. But the literal G assumption does not imply (g+1,1): Figure2's five-cycle with a pendant edge has incidence number4 and square chromatic number5. Its square contains the five cycle vertices as a clique, so the lower bound5 is direct. Consequently replacing the hypothesis by square colorability discards valid factors. The regular case's single incoming color cannot be assumed for arbitrary G. No reduction restoring the irregular factors was found. PARK at the global alignment/extension lemma, not at a vague novelty objection. A full product-closure theorem would be significant; standard coloring tools and the one-color deficit do not make it routine.

## 333017: isolation bound, complete PUBLIC bridge

Original source1604.07035 definitions pp2–6 require an inclusion-free hypergraph (or discard supersets), f:[M]->R_{>0} strictly increasing, and count the empty hypergraph as having every weight isolating. Y is the minimum over H,f. These standing conventions defeat constant-objective and empty-hypergraph objections. For n>=1 the singleton count is n sum_{j=0}^{M-1}j^(n-1): choose the unique minimum vertex and its value t, then assign every other coordinate a value greater than t. For n=1 use 0^0=1, giving M weights.

Initial route: Ta-Shma sends w in {2,...,M}^n to w-chi_e for a minimum edge e. For every competing h, the coordinates decremented in e minus h strictly improve e, while coordinates in h minus e do not change. Inclusion-freeness ensures e minus h nonempty. The resulting unique minimum edge recovers e and then recovers w, proving injection and (M-1)^n. This does NOT yield the conjectured sharp bound. Layer-one inputs with a unique coordinate1 cause trouble when all minimum edges contain that coordinate: decrementing all of e exits the box. Omitting that coordinate works if one chooses a private distinguishing subset, but an injective choice of subset for every input was not obtained.

The actual July7,2026 primary2607.06171v1 (all8 pages read, no figures) supplies exactly the missing fractional charging argument, Theorem5, with a generalized-objective extension on p8. For an antichain A, a private pair (a,S) has S subset a but S not subset any other member. Fix one witness x_b in a minus b for each b!=a; T_a={x_b} has size<=|A|-1. Supersets S of T_a in a are private. The identity sum_{T subset S subset a}1/binom(|a|,|S|)=(|a|+1)/(|T|+1) shows total private-pair charge sum 1/((|a|+1)binom(|a|,|S|))>=1.

For a weight w with unique coordinate i at1, if some minimum edge e avoids i, send charge1 to w-chi_e. Otherwise all minimum edges contain i: apply the private-pair bound to {e minus{i}:e minimum}; send 1/(|e| binom(|e|-1,|S|)) to w-chi_S for each private pair. Decrementing S isolates e. For general strictly increasing f, the comparison of e against a nonminimum h cannot worsen, since S subset e. For another minimum h, private means some decremented coordinate lies in e minus h, yielding strict improvement; no equal-step assumption on f is needed. Each input emits at least1. For a target u with unique minimum edge e, if I(u), its set of1-coordinates, is not contained in e, only the first rule applies and the only preimage is u+chi_e. Otherwise every preimage comes from the second rule, has unique1-coordinate i in I(u), and its decremented S obeys I(u) minus{i} subset S subset e minus{i}. Ignoring other restrictions increases incoming charge. The same binomial identity bounds the charge for each i by1/|I(u)|, hence total<=1. This establishes at least n(M-1)^(n-1) isolating weights in layer1.

For layer j replace f by f_j(t)=f(t+j-1) on [M-j+1]; this remains positive strictly increasing. Subtract j-1 from all coordinate indices to biject the layer with layer1 and preserve every edge objective. Sum the layer bounds to obtain n sum_{a=0}^{M-1}a^(n-1). The singleton count attains it. Empty H has all M^n weights; the singleton count is at most M^n since it counts a subset of that same box, so the empty convention is harmless. Positive f lets one discard strict supersets for arbitrary nonempty H. This is a complete applicable primary-to-literal bridge, including generalized f, not an analogy from the offset-only Theorem1. The public paper's extension explicitly covers strictly increasing coordinate functions; the argument above verifies the relevant two properties personally. It earns zero NEW credit. Its public significance is substantial; public provenance, rather than shortness or a routine label, excludes it.
