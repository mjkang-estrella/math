## Complete independent ordinary proof

Let X=H_k and let Gamma=S_k act on X by transport of edges. For a real stochastic rule R define

A_R(F,H) = (1/k!) sum_{sigma in S_k} R(sigma^{-1} F, sigma^{-1} H).

1. **Fibers and orbit sums.** For x=(F,H), let B=Gamma x and T={tau:tau x=x}; T is the common stabilizer of the pair, equivalently Stab(F) intersect Stab(H). For any y in B choose gamma with gamma x=y. The permutations sigma with sigma^{-1}x=y are exactly those with sigma gamma in T, that is T gamma^{-1}. Thus each fiber has |T| elements. The fibers partition Gamma, so k!=|B||T|. This proof does not assume a free action, distinct F,H, or independent stabilizers. Consequently

A_R(F,H) = (|T|/k!) sum_{(F',H') in B} R(F',H')
             = (1/|B|) sum_{(F',H') in B} R(F',H').

Both |B| and k! are positive. Hence equality of all orbit sums is equivalent to equality of every A entry, in both directions. Relabelling F,H together merely reindexes the permutation sum, so A_R is simultaneously invariant. It is itself stochastic: entries are nonnegative, and for fixed F the sum over H is the average, over sigma, of the sum of the row R(sigma^{-1}F,-), since H -> sigma^{-1}H is bijective. That sum is 1. All this is finite real algebra; no rational approximation is needed.

2. **Necessity and the labelled n=k test.** Fix arbitrary F,H in X, use n=k, and start at the deterministic labelled graph F. Each sampled tuple is uniquely (sigma(1),...,sigma(k)) for a permutation sigma, each with probability 1/k!. Its drawn graph is sigma^{-1}F. To obtain exactly the labelled target H after replacing all internal pairs, the unique replacement graph is sigma^{-1}H. Thus

P_R^{(k)}(F,H)=A_R(F,H).

If K2 holds, equality of the time-1 probabilities of {G_1=H}, for each initial F, proves equality of all A entries. Step 1 gives K1. This uses only a case explicitly quantified in K2. Projecting the target to its isomorphism class would lose this entrywise identification, which is why the labelled source semantics were checked first.

3. **Every larger n: uniform conditional ordering.** Assume K1 and fix n>=k, labelled global graphs G,G' on [n]. For each k-subset U of [n] choose any bijection f:[k]->U, and put F=f^*(G[U]), H=f^*(G'[U]). Write C_U(G,G') for agreement of G,G' on every unordered vertex pair not wholly contained in U. Each unordered U is sampled with probability k!/(n)_k=1/binom(n,k): exactly k! injective tuples have image U. Conditioned on U, those tuples are uniform, and each is uniquely f composed with sigma for sigma in S_k.

If C_U fails, replacement on U cannot yield G', so the conditional transition probability is zero. If C_U holds, for tuple f composed with sigma the drawn graph is sigma^{-1}F and the unique replacement yielding G' is sigma^{-1}H. Therefore the conditional transition probability is A_R(F,H). Changing f to f composed with tau replaces (F,H) by (tau^{-1}F,tau^{-1}H); simultaneous invariance from Step 1 proves independence from the chosen identification.

The exact full transition formula is

P_R^{(n)}(G,G') = (1/binom(n,k)) sum_{U subset [n], |U|=k}
    1_{C_U(G,G')} A_R(f_U^*G[U], f_U^*G'[U]).

Each summand agrees for R and R' by Step 1; hence every transition matrix agrees. There is no uniqueness-of-support assertion. If the changed edges fit in several U, every such U contributes with its own weight. Overlap between distinct U does not invalidate the formula: the conditioning events are distinct values of the sampled subset, hence disjoint events in the sampling space. For G'=G every U is compatible and the diagonal A_R(F,F) contributions are included. Edges crossing from U to its complement must also agree; the condition is on all pairs outside binom(U,2), not merely edges entirely in the complement. Zero entries and self-transitions require no exceptions.

4. **All time and path laws.** For a deterministic initial g_0 and any specified finite path (g_0,...,g_t), the probability is product_{i=1}^t P_R^{(n)}(g_{i-1},g_i); an inconsistent initial vertex has probability zero. The Markov property follows from the source's step rule. Equality of kernels makes these products equal for every t, including the empty product at t=0. Summing these products over the finitely many unspecified intermediate states gives every finite-dimensional marginal, with arbitrary gaps between sampled times. Mixing over any common random initial distribution also preserves equality, although K2 only needs deterministic initial graphs. Finally the graph state space on [n] is finite, and coordinate cylinders form a generating pi-system for the product sigma-algebra on its countable sequence space. Probability-measure uniqueness (the pi-lambda argument) gives equality of the actual entire process laws. Conversely a whole-process law gives each time-1 marginal used in Step 2. This proves K1 iff K2 without weakening or strengthening either source condition.

No fatal ordinary mathematical gap remains. This proves the same-order literal target, not a new theorem about other sampling mechanisms, unlabelled output laws, non-Markov rules, graphon equivalence, or different-order rules. The different-order lifting comment is contextual, not needed as an additional hypothesis or claimed new result here.
