## 328314: a finite certificate can establish the infinite seed hypothesis

Fox's sole arXiv version, Section 6 Conjecture 2, is the literal recurrence question. Definitions 11–14, representation-word Theorem 3 and Section 4.2 define the operational objects. T(a) is {a_i−1 : i≥2} union {1,…,a_1−1}. This is not arbitrary subtraction Nim. A representation word replaces the last greedy digit by a_1 precisely at 2-volatile positions. The printed “let n≥2 be an integer” where m is introduced is a source typo, not a creditable refutation. Work with m≥2, strictly increasing positive a beginning at 1, and the displayed last-step equality.

For a finite seed a=(1,a_1,…,a_n) with q=a_n and k=a_1, the representation word is q-periodic. For 0≤r<q, its value is k if r=a_j−1 for some 2≤j≤n, and otherwise is determined by repeatedly subtracting the largest a_j≤r until r<k. For positions ≥q, reduction modulo q follows from the greedy representation: adding q changes only the leading digit and does not change the last two digits. At q−1 the representation of the successor is 10…0, so the exceptional symbol is k. Thus the periodic block can be calculated exactly from the seed.

Here is an ordinary finite-check lemma, independent of any search. Let S be a finite positive subtraction set, M=max S, and let W be an explicitly q-periodic candidate word. If the mex recurrence holds for every 0≤r<q+M, then W is the Nim sequence on all natural positions. Proof: for r≥q+M, every s∈S is legal at both r and r−q, and W(r−s)=W(r−q−s), while W(r)=W(r−q). Induct on r. Earlier positions are exactly the finite hypothesis. This closes the infinite seed premise; checking one short prefix without this bridge does not.

A refutation certificate should contain a seed, m, all seed mex checks through q+M−1, the recurrence-generated b terms through the first one exceeding B+1, exact representation digits through B+1, and the mex table for T(b) through B with a least mismatch. For m≥2 growth is automatic: if x>y>0 then ((m+1)x−(m−1)y)−x = x+(m−1)(x−y)>0. Hence once b_j>B+1, all later subtractions b_j−1 exceed B and cannot affect the mex table; later representation places cannot affect positions through B+1. This proves the finite truncation bridge needed for an infinite counterexample.

The source's failed (1,2,5)→(1,2,5,16) example is NOT a counterexample to Conjecture 2: its last seed step fails the required m=3 equality. The printed (1,2,5,13,42), m=3 seed is source-owned, so testing its first few terms gives no discovery. A positive proof for it alone would also not prove the full conjecture.

A conditional next Pi task: first establish the finite-word convention against the source and later citing literature; then, only after root clearance, inspect k in {2,3}, m in {2,3,4}, 2≤n≤5, strictly increasing seeds with q≤128 and a_n=(m+1)a_{n−1}−(m−1)a_{n−2}; test the FULL seed premise using the lemma above; test extension positions only through B=4096. Fixed maximum 50,000 seeds, stop at first mismatch or resource cap. This is a proposed task, not an experiment performed here. Candidate certificate is O(q·|S|+B·log B) integers, usually well below a megabyte. The Lean task would require source-faithful greedy digits, volatility, mex uniqueness, periodic-seed lemma, recurrence growth and the finite-tail cutoff. A successful bounded search is a refutation; a failed search supplies no theorem.

Positive route attempted: Theorem 3 gives a block decomposition, but mex compatibility across the new block boundary involves differences between volatile and nonvolatile zends of different scales. The existing Fibonacci/Beatty lemmas do not cover arbitrary seeds or m>2. Missing lemma: a recurrence extension preserves both move exclusion for equal symbols and access to every lower mex symbol at every position. Recurrence length identities by themselves give neither. Park a general proof until such a lemma is supplied.

## 328558: reduce noncontractibility to one finite UNSAT certificate

The original v2 Definition 2.13 and the journal body use INDUCED simple cycles: no additional adjacency is allowed among loop vertices. The intended setting is connected images (explicit discussion after Theorem 3.7). Two isolated vertices would only exploit the dropped connectedness in the MathDB summary; it is ineligible. Theorem 4.7 handles chordal graphs. Conjecture 4.8 asks for all connected graphs without induced cycles of length ≥5, a strictly larger class. C4 itself is digitally contractible, so ordinary topological cycle/homology arguments are unsound here.

For a proposed finite connected graph G, define R(G) to be the maps f:V→V satisfying (i) f(v)=v or f(v) adjacent v, and (ii) f(v)=f(w) or f(v) adjacent f(w) for each edge vw. These are exactly the one-step homotopies from the identity under Definition 2.5. If every f in R(G) is surjective, G cannot contract to a point when |V|>1. Proof: in any homotopy starting at id, suppose h_t is bijective. It is a graph automorphism, by source Lemma 2.3. The continuous map h_{t+1}∘h_t^{-1} lies in R(G), so is surjective. Induction makes every stage surjective, contradicting a constant final stage. Equivalence to a point would yield precisely such a contraction via Definition 2.6. This is the source's irreducibility mechanism, not a new credited theorem.

Exact finite certificate format: adjacency bitmasks; a spanning-tree connectivity certificate; for each vertex subset of size ≥5, evidence that its induced subgraph is not a connected 2-regular graph; and UNSAT of the following CNF. Variables x_vu select exactly one f(v)=u, restricted to u∈N[v]. For each edge vw and each pair u,z with u≠z and uz a nonedge, include ¬x_vu∨¬x_wz. Selector y_r chooses an omitted target; require at least one y_r and clauses ¬y_r∨¬x_vr for every v,r. The formula is satisfiable exactly when a nonsurjective f in R(G) exists. A proof-logged UNSAT result plus a proved encoding bridge is sufficient for noncontractibility; SAT is merely a reducibility witness.

A proper positive shortcut exists for graphs with a dominating clique C: choose f(v)∈C∩N[v], giving a continuous one-step map into a clique, and then contract the clique. This defeats several attractive dense candidate families, including complements of cycles with a dominating independent triple in the original cycle. It does not prove that all connected 4-chordal graphs have such a clique: paths already disprove that intermediate claim. Chordal elimination also fails directly because a C4 has no simplicial vertex. The remaining positive lemma would need a reducible collection of vertices or a separator-compatible homotopy for every 4-chordal graph; neither has been established here.

## 328770: exact fibre certificates avoid trusting a Groebner run

Davis's journal Conjecture 21 (arXiv Conjecture 4.4) concerns the VERTICES of S_n=2Σ_n, not all integral symmetric row-sum-two matrices. The journal has n≥2; the MathDB literal removes n=2. Treat “degree 3(n−2)” as a degree bound for source fidelity, not an opportunity to refute the paper using known lower-degree relations. Work at n≥4. Only an exponent ≥n in a MINIMAL generator, or a genuine degree exceeding 3(n−2), would be substantive.

Let A have the vertex matrices as columns, flattened with consistent off-diagonal coordinates. All columns have the same positive total entry sum, so a fibre Az=b has a fixed total monomial degree. Under a specified graded reverse lexicographic order, u is nonstandard exactly when some v in its fibre is smaller. A nonstandard u is a minimal initial-ideal generator exactly when every immediate divisor u−e_i, i in supp(u), is standard. Proof: the toric kernel is spanned by differences of monomials in each A-fibre; a nonzero polynomial whose leading term is u must have a smaller term in that fibre. Conversely u−v lies in the kernel. Divisor closure of the monomial initial ideal then reduces minimality to immediate divisors.

Thus a finite certificate is a vertex list with exact vertex legitimacy proofs; a variable permutation defining the order; u,v with Au=Av, u leading; and, for each i∈supp(u), a complete enumeration of the fibre A(u−e_i) with its least monomial equal to u−e_i. Exponent or degree violation then refutes the conjecture directly. A single binomial or a nonminimal leading monomial does not suffice. Enumeration must be complete: branch on exponents 0..degree using exact nonnegative residual matrix tests, and certify all pruned branches by a negative coordinate or nonzero residual at the leaf.

A small explanatory relation exposes an important trap. For a triangle on 1,2,3 and loops on remaining vertices, let T have weight-one triangle edges and diagonal 2 on remaining vertices; let I=2 times the identity; let E12,E13,E23 be the doubled transposition vertices. Entrywise, 2T+I=E12+E13+E23. This is a cubic toric relation, with exponent two on T. It violates neither the intended degree bound nor n-freeness for n≥3 and is not a candidate. Combining copies does not establish minimality and cannot by itself make a counterexample.

Conditional next Pi task: after later Davis/citing-work and full source-example clearance, construct the exact vertices at n=4 and check their vertex proofs; inspect at most 100 fixed variable orders and total degree ≤8, capped at 100,000 fibre nodes. Stop on a certified violation or cap. Prefer a fibre-minimality certificate to an unaudited algebra-system claim. Do not invest in a generic Lean toric/Groebner library before an actual violation and independent significance decision. No initial ideal, witness or computation has been produced here.
