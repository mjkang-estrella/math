Let $K$ and $K'$ be knots, and let $rm(K)$ denote the reversed mirror of $K$. A finite quandle is a set with a self-distributive binary operation, and ${\rm Col}_{X}(K)$ denotes the number of colorings of a knot diagram of $K$ by a finite quandle $X$. **Quandle-coloring distinguishability conjecture.** If $K'\ne K$ and $K'\ne rm(K)$, then there is a finite quandle $X$ such that

$$
{\rm Col}_{X}(K)\ne {\rm Col}_{X}(K').
$$

Quandle colorings cannot distinguish a knot from its reversed mirror, so this conjecture asserts that this is the only obstruction to distinguishing knots by finite-quandle coloring numbers. The source recalls this as a conjecture of Carter, Elhamdadi, Saito and Yoshiura; no resolution is given here.
