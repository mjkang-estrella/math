                                                         Directed cycle double covers and cut-obstacles
                                                                         Andrea Jiménez∗                 Martin Loebl†




arXiv:1405.6929v1 [math.CO] 27 May 2014
                                                                                             Abstract
                                                     A directed cycle double cover of a graph G is a family of cycles of G, each provided
                                                 with an orientation, such that every edge of G is covered by exactly two oppositely directed
                                                 cycles. Explicit obstacles to the existence of a directed cycle double cover in a graph are
                                                 bridges. Jaeger [2] conjectured that bridges are actually the only obstacles. In consequence, the
                                                 difficulty of Jaeger’s directed cycle double cover conjecture lies in discovering and overcoming
                                                 implicit obstacles, which have an unknown and, so far, in any attempt of defining them,
                                                 complex structure. In this work, we suggest a way to circumvent this difficulty. We formulate
                                                 a conjecture on graph connections (see [1] for recent monograph), whose validity follows by
                                                 the successful avoidance of an explicit cut-type obstacle that we call cut-obstacle. The main
                                                 result of this work claims that a weaker version of our graph connections conjecture already
                                                 implies Jaeger’s directed cycle double cover conjecture.



                                          1      Introduction, outline of methods and main results.
                                          Jaeger’s directed cycle double cover conjecture [2] asserts that for every 2-connected graph G there
                                          exists a family of cycles C of G such that it is possible to prescribe an orientation to each cycle
                                          of C in such a way that each edge e of G belongs to exactly two cycles of C and these cycles
                                          induce opposite orientations on e. For a thorough introduction to this conjecture as well as to our
                                          previous related work we refer the reader to [3]. In [3], we have introduced key notions of reductions
                                          and cut-obstacles, and showed the validity of Jaeger’s directed cycle double cover conjecture in a
                                          subclass of cubic bridgeless graphs that we called lean fork graphs; the crucial characteristic of
                                          lean fork graphs that we used to prove the aforementioned result is that lean fork graphs admit ear
                                          decompositions that are, in a sense, well-connected — in more technical words, ear decompositions
                                          that do not create cut-obstacles.
                                              In this work, we develop further the approach presented in [3]. We establish a direct link
                                          between the directed cycle double cover conjecture and the extensively studied theory of graph
                                          connections; see [1] for a recent monograph on this topic.

1.1    Robust trigraphs
We introduce the novel class of trigraphs. In this note, an ear is a path on at least 3 vertices or a star
on 4 vertices; in particular, an edge is not an ear. A trigraph is a cubic graph that can be obtained
from a cycle by sequentially adding short ears, that is, ears on at most 5 vertices. Whenever H
is a trigraph, the expression (H0 , Hi , Li )n stands for the ear decomposition of H, where H0 is
the initial cycle of the ear decomposition, Li is the i-th short ear, Hi is the intermediate graph
obtained from H0 by adding the first i short ears and Hn = H.
    For each ear L, we denote by I(L) the set of its vertices of degree at least 2 and if L is a path
we say that L is a k-ear if the cardinality of I(L) is k. Let (H0 , Hi , Li )n be an ear decomposition
of H. The descendant of I(Li ), where Li is a 3-ear, is the maximal subgraph D of H − V (Hi ) such
that H[I(Li ) ∪ V (D)] is connected; the notation H[X] represents the induced subgraph of H on
vertex set X ⊂ V (H). In other words, D is the descendant of I(Li ) if and only if D is the maximal
subgraph of H − V (Hi ) such that for each component C of D there exists an edge connecting C
and I(Li ).

Definition 1 (Weakly robust). Let (H0 , Hi , Li )n be an ear decomposition of a trigraph H. We say
that (H0 , Hi , Li )n is weakly robust if for each 3-ear, say Li , the descendant of I(Li ) is composed
of at most 2 connected components. Moreover, if the descendant is composed of two connected
components, then one of them is an isolated vertex adjacent to two vertices in V (H0 ).

   In the beginning of Section 2 we formalize and generalize the concepts of ear decomposition
and descendant for all cubic bridgeless graphs.


1.2    Correct reductions
In our previous work [3], we introduced the notions of mixed graphs, reductions and cut-obstacles.
In order to make this paper as self-contained as possible, in the next paragraphs we present some
definitions that can be also found in [3].
    A mixed graph is a 4-tuple (V, E, A, R), where V is the vertex set, E is the edge set, A is the
set of directed edges (arcs), and R is a subset of A×A, that is, a set of pairs of arcs. It is require
that in the graph (V, E), that is, the graph on vertex set V and edge set E, each vertex has degree
at least two and at most three, and that, in (V, E, A, R), each vertex of degree two in (V, E) is the
tail of exactly one arc and the head of exactly one arc.
    Throughout this note, {u, v} denotes the non-directed edge with end vertices u, v and (u, v)
denotes the arc directed from u to v; if no end vertices are specified ~e denotes an arc.
    Let (V, E, A, R) be a mixed graph and U ⊆ V . A correct reduction of U on (V, E, A, R) is a
procedure that outputs a new mixed graph (V 0 , E 0 , A0 , R0 ) and a list S of directed paths and cycles
such that the following items hold:

   i) V 0 = V − U .

  ii) E 0 = {{u, v} ∈ E : {u, v} ∩ U = ∅}.

  iii) Let Ã denote the set of arcs obtained by replacing each edge in E incident to a vertex of
       U by two arcs oppositely directed and A(U ) be the subset of A that contains all directed
       edges incident to a vertex of U . The list S is the result of partitioning A(U )∪Ã into correct
       directed paths and correct directed cycles. A cycle or a path is correct if no pair of its arcs
       is an element of R, and if it is not a 2-cycle composed of only arcs from Ã. In addition, a
       correct path must have both end vertices in V −U .

  iv) A0 = {(x, y) ∈ A : {x, y} ∩ U = ∅} ∪ A00 .
      The set A00 is the set of arcs obtained by replacing each correct directed path P ∈ S by a new
      directed edge, say ~eP , with both end vertices in V −U and such that ~eP has the orientation
      of P .

  v) R0 = {{(x, y), (x0 , y 0 )} ∈ R : {x, y, x0 , y 0 } ∩ U = ∅} ∪ R00 ∪ R̃.

   The proof of the following proposition is discussed in Section 2 of [3].

Proposition 1. Let G be a cubic graph and V0 , V1 , . . . , Vk be a 2-connected partition of V (G). Each
consecutive correct reduction of Vk , Vk−1 , . . . , V0 constructs a directed cycle double cover of G.

Cut-obstacles
Let (V, E, A, R) be a mixed graph and U ⊆ V . We denote by CU the subset of E ∪ A that contains
all edges and arcs with exactly one end vertex in U . We say that U is a cut-obstacle in (V, E, A, R)
—alternatively, that there exists a cut-obstacle at U — if CU consists of exactly one edge (that
is, CS is a bridge), or if the number of edges in CU is strictly less than twice the number of arcs
in CU . For an illustration see Figure 6(a).
    A cut-obstacle is a potential obstacle to performing correct reductions in the following sense:
if we assume that all pairs of arcs in CU belong to R, then there is not correct reduction of U
on (V, E, A, R).


1.3    Main Contribution
The main contribution of this paper is the formulation of two conjectures on graph connections
for trigraphs, namely the strong and the weak cut-avoidance conjectures. We prove that both
conjectures imply the general directed cycle double cover conjecture.

Definition 2 (Superb). Let H be a trigraph. We say that an ear decomposition (H0 , Hi , Li )n of
H is superb if there exists a consecutive correct reduction of I(Ln ), I(Ln−1 ), . . . , I(Lj ) for some
j ∈ [n] such that the following properties hold:

  (i) j = 1 or I(Lj−1 ) cannot be correctly reduced, and

 (ii) a cut-obstacle at the internal vertices of a 3-ear in {Lj−1 , Lj , . . . , Ln } is never created.

Conjecture 1. Let H be a trigraph. Every weakly robust ear decomposition (H0 , Hi , Li )n of H is
superb.

   The second conjecture is a bit more technical. We first introduce two definitions.

Definition 3. Let H be a trigraph and let H0 be an induced cycle of H. We say that a set S of
subgraphs of H is H0 -valid if S consists of:

   – a subset S1 of stars, each with at least two leaves in V (H0 ),

   – a set S2 of paths on 5 vertices, each with both end vertices in V (H0 ),

   – a set S3 of paths on 5 vertices, each with exactly one end vertex in V (H0 ) and one end vertex
     in the set of internal vertices of some element of S2 ,

   – a set S4 of stars, each with one leaf equal to an internal vertex of an element of S2 , and
     another leaf equal to an internal vertex of an element of S3 .



                                                     4
Definition 4. Let H be a trigraph, H0 be an induced cycle of H and S be a H0 -valid set. We say
that a weakly robust ear decomposition (H0 , Hi , Li )n of H admits H0 , S if for each element s ∈ S
there is i ≤ n such that s = Li .

     In the weak cut-avoidance conjecture we essentially assert that for every trigraph H that admits
a weakly robust ear decomposition (H0 , Hi , Li )n , there exists a superb ear decomposition starting
at H0 that contains a permutation of a the elements of a special subsequence of the building ears
L1 , . . . , Ln (see Definition 4).

Conjecture 2 (Weak cut-avoidance conjecture). Let H be a trigraph, H0 be an induced cycle of
H and S be a H0 -valid set such that there is a weakly robust ear decomposition of H that admits
H0 , S. Then there is a superb weakly robust ear decomposition of H that admits H0 , S.

    We observe that both conjectures hold in the class of planar trigraphs. In the last section of
this work, we discuss further supporting evidence for both conjectures.

Observation 1. Conjecture 1 is valid for planar trigraphs.

Proof. Let H be a planar trigraph and (H0 , Hi , Li )n be a weakly robust ear decomposition of H.
Let us consider an embedding of H in the plane and let C be the set of facial cycles defined by this
embedding. The set C is a directed cycle double cover of H: we can prescribe clockwise orientation
to the facial cycles of interior faces and anticlockwise orientation to the facial cycle of the external
face. Moreover, C encodes a consecutive correct reduction of I(Ln ), I(Ln−1 ), . . . , I(L1 ), V (H0 ).
We claim that such consecutive correct reduction does not create cut-obstacles at any 3-ear. The
rest of the proof is devoted to prove this.
      Let Li be a 3-ear, i ∈ [n] and V (Li ) = αv 1 v 2 v 3 β. We assume that I(Li+1 ), . . . , I(Ln ) has been
already correctly reduced according to C and let H 0 denote the obtained mixed graph.
      Suppose first that there are adjacent internal vertices of Li , without loss of generality v 1 and
v 2 , such that if x (resp. y) is the neighbour of v 1 (resp. of v 2 ) that is not in V (Li ), then x and y
are in the same component, say D, of the descendant of I(Li ). By planarity and since H is cubic,
it holds that the path xv 1 v 2 y is a subgraph of a facial cycle C of C. Again by planarity and using
the fact that V (D) is connected, we have that all vertices in V (C) \ {v 1 , v 2 } belong to V (D). If
so, all vertices in V (C) \ {v 1 , v 2 } are inner vertices of ears that were already reduced and thus, in
H 0 , there is an arc with end vertices v 1 , v 2 .
      We now suppose that there are no such internal vertices. Since (H0 , Hi , Li )n is weakly robust,
it implies that x, z, where z is the neighbour of v 3 that is not in V (Li ) are in the same component,
say D0 , of the descendant of I(Li ). Moreover, the component of the descendant of I(Li ) to which
y belongs contains only y (thus, y in an internal vertex of a star in Li+1 , . . . , Ln ) and in the planar
embedding, y is not in the same side (with respect to Li ) where x and z are since (H0 , Hi , Li )n
is weakly robust and thus H is 2-edge-connected. As before, it follows that the path xv 1 v 2 v 3 z
belongs to a path that is a subgraph of a facial cycle C 0 of C and all vertices in V (C 0 ) \ {v 1 , v 2 , v 3 }
are in V (D). We conclude that in H 0 there is an arc with end vertices v 1 , v 3 .

   We now formalize the promised implication of Conjecture 2 on Jaeger’s directed cycle double
cover conjecture.

Theorem 2. If Conjecture 2 holds, then the directed cycle double conjecture holds in general
graphs.

    The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we define the class of robust
graphs. Informally, robust graphs are cubic bridgeless graphs that admit ear decompositions with
connected descendants. It turns out that all cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs are robust
(see Theorem 3). Then, in Section 3 we describe the essential step in the proof of Theorem 2:
roughly speaking, for each robust graph G we construct a trigraph H(G) so that each consecutive
correct reduction of H(G), without cut-obstacles at 3-ears, corresponds to a directed cycle double
cover of G. In Section 3, we complete the proof of Theorem 2. Finally, we provide examples of
graphs on which our conjectures are valid.


2     Robust ear decompositions
As anticipated, we first formalize the notion of ear decomposition of cubic bridgeless graphs used in
this work. Let G be a cubic bridgeless graph. Let G0 , G1 , . . . , Gl and P1 , . . . , Pl be two sequences
of subgraphs of G such that G0 is a cycle of G, Gl = G, for each i ∈ [l], the subgraph Pi is
an ear, V (Pi ) ∩ V (Gi−1 ) is the set of leaves of Pi , E(Pi ) ∩ E(Gi−1 ) = ∅, and Gi is the union of
Gi−1 and Pi , that is, V (Gi ) = V (Gi−1 ) ∪ V (Pi ) and E(Gi ) = E(Gi−1 ) ∪ E(Pi ). We say that
(G0 , G1 , . . . , Gl , P1 , . . . , Pl ), in short (G0 , Gi , Pi )l , is an ear decomposition of G. Moreover, in the
case that t ≤ l, we say that (G0 , Gi , Pi )t is a partial ear decomposition of G and if {P1 , . . . , Pl }
also contains edges, then we refer to (G0 , Gi , Pi )l as an edge+ear decomposition of G.
    We now need to generalize the concept of descendant. Let i ∈ [l] such that |I(Pi )| ≥ 3 and
V (Pi ) = {αi , vi1 , . . . , vik , βi }, where V (Pi ) ∩ V (Gi−1 ) = {αi , βi }. We say that S ⊂ {vi1 , . . . , vik } is
a segment of Pi if |S| ≥ 3 and S induces a connected subgraph of Pi . Let G0i denote the graph
obtained from G by deleting V (Gi ). For each segment S of Pi , the maximal subgraph GSi of G0i
such that G[V (GSi ) ∪ S] is connected is called the descendant of S.

Definition 5 (Robust ear decomposition). An edge+ear decomposition, say (G0 , Gi , Pi )l , of G is
robust if the following conditions hold:

 (a) G − V (G0 ) is a connected graph, and

  (b) for each i ∈ [l] such that |I(Pi )| ≥ 3 and for each segment S of Pi , the descendant of S is
      connected.

    In order to show the main result of this work we are seeking a robust ear decomposition of G.
If G admits a robust ear decomposition, we refer to G as a robust graph.
    We recall that a cubic graph is cyclically 4-edge-connected if it is 3-edge connected and each
3-edge cut is trivial.

Observation 2. The directed cycle double cover conjecture holds if and only if it holds in the class
of cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs.

Theorem 3. Every cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph G is robust.

2.1     Proof of Theorem 3
This section is devoted to proving that all cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graphs have a robust
ear decomposition. Recall that we denote by I(P ) the set of all internal vertices of a path P . For
the sake of simplicity, we refer to the descendant of I(P ) as the descendant of P . The following
observation is straightforward.

Observation 3. Let (G0 , Gi , Pi )l be an edge+ear decomposition of a cubic bridgeless graph G.
For each i ∈ [l] such that |I(Pi )| ≥ 3, if the descendant of Pi is connected, then the descendant of
any segment of Pi is connected.

   We first prove that every cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph has a robust edge+ear de-
composition. By Observation 3 it suffices to show the following statement.

Lemma 4 (Robustness). Let G be a cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic graph. There exists an
edge+ear decomposition (G0 , Gi , Pi )l of G such that G0 is an induced cycle, G − V (G0 ) is con-
nected, {P1 , . . . , Pl } does not contain stars, and for every i ∈ {1, . . . , l} such that |I(Pi )| ≥ 3, the
descendant of Pi is connected.

    Before to give the proof of Lemma 4, we need to introduce new terminology. Let (G0 , Gi , Pi )l be
an edge+ear decomposition of a cubic bridgeless graph G. For i ∈ {1, . . . , l} such that |I(Pi )| ≥ 3,
we say that a connected component C of the descendant of Pi is connecting if there exists an edge
in E(G) with one end vertex in V (C) and the other end vertex in V (Gi−1 ). Otherwise, we say
that C is non-connecting. Let C(Pi ) denote the set of all connecting components of the descendant
of Pi .

Proof of Lemma 4

3.1     Complex Gadget
As we explained in the previous section, the task of the basic gadget is to model vertices of G.
Informally, a complex gadget models long ears of a robust ear decomposition of G and it is obtained
by a sequential concatenation of basic gadgets.
    Let l ≥ 3, j ∈ {1, . . . , l}, G ∗ (uj ) be a basic* gadget and {G(ui )}i∈{1,...,l}−{j} be a family of
basic gadgets. A j-complex gadget, Gj (u1 , . . . , ul ), is a sequence H1 , H2 , . . . , Hl of basic and basic*
gadgets obtained by the concatenation of the sequences in {G(ui )}i∈{1,...,l}−{j} ∪{G ∗ (uj )}, following
the next rules:

   • For every p ∈ {1, . . . , j − 1}, it holds Hp = G(up ) such that for each i ∈ {1, . . . , j − 2} the
     joint of G(ui ) is identified with the grounded vertex of G(ui+1 ).

3.3    A directed cycle double cover for G
In this section we complete the proof of Theorem 2; we want to prove that Conjecture 2 implies
the directed cycle double cover in general graphs.
    Let H(G), Γ and G0 be as in Section 3.2.

Lemma 9. Each valid ear decomposition of H(G) is weakly robust.

Proof. It follows from the fact that the ear decomposition of G used in the construction of H(G)
is robust.

   We now need to extend Definition 8.

Definition 10 (Reduction process). Let (H0 , Hi , Li )n be an ear decomposition of H(G). A reduc-
tion process of L1 , . . . , Ln is a consecutive correct reduction of a subsequence I(Ln ), . . . , I(Lj ) of
I(Ln ), . . . , I(L1 ), so that

  (i) either j = 1, or

 (ii) j > 1 and I(Lj−1 ) does not have correct reduction or I(Lj−1 ) is a cut-obstacle.

If (i) holds, we say that the reduction process is complete. Otherwise, we refer to it as j-incomplete.

3.4     An example
Let G be a graph embedded on the torus obtained from the toroidal square grid T by deleting
a perfect matching composed of horizontal edges. We consider a robust ear decomposition of G
starting from a cycle C that contains only vertical edges of T . Let G0 be the cubic graph obtained
from G as described in Section 3.2. Let us fix a valid ear decomposition of H(G0 ) and discuss the
question: Does H(G0 ) satisfy Conjecture 1?
    There is a natural directed cycle double cover of G, which consists of all the facial cycles defined
by the embedding of G on the torus. This induces a reduction process on H(G0 ). We specify neither
the details of the chosen robust ear decomposition of G, nor the valid ear decomposition of H(G0 ).
However, a critical part of the reduction process is the case that the complex gadget G(C) of H(G0 )
corresponding to cycle C is reduced. In order to study this case, let us assume that all complex
gadgets of H(G0 ) but G(C) are correctly reduced without creating cut-obstacle (according to the
reduction process that the embedding of G induces on H(G0 )) and let H 0 denote the resulting
mixed graph. Observe that H 0 may be obtained following the next rules:

    • Let M be the graph obtained from C by (1) subdividing one edge, say e = uv, by one vertex,
      say w, and (2) adding new vertex x connected by v,w. Let C0 the path with set of edges
      E(C)−{e}∪{u, w}. Recall that G0 is obtained from G in an analogous way — see Section 3.2.

    • We consider the ear decomposition of M that starts from the triangle with vertex set {x, v, w}
      and has the building ear C0 . Let H(M ) be the corresponding trigraph constructed according


                                                       23
     to the rules of construction described in Section 3.2.

   • The mixed graph H 0 has underlying graph H(M ) and one can easily note that the set of arcs
     of H 0 must induce two disjoint directed cycles.

   Any valid ear decomposition of H(M ) induces an ear decomposition of H 0 . We observe that
each such ear decomposition of H 0 is superb.
