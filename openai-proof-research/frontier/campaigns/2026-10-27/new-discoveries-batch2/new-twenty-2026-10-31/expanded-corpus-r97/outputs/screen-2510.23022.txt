1974: With  k=3  and  h\geq 4 , Nathanson [Nat25c, Theorem 9] found the exact form of  \mathcal{R}(h,k) , showing there are many more gaps in  \mathcal{R}(h,k) . With  k=4  and  h\geq 4 , experimental data suggests  5h\not\in\mathcal{R}(h,4) , which is an example of a conjectured gap. The only (conjectured) gaps in  \mathcal{R}(h,k)  outside  \Delta_{h,k}  we have found with  h\leq k+1  occur when  (h,k)\in\{(4,3),(4,4),(5,4)\} . When  h  is significantly larger than  k , there are many more gaps in  \mathcal{R}(h,k)  than can be explained by Theorem 2.2. One could conjecture where these other gaps occur, and try to prove that any of them exist.
