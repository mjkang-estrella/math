# Sustained ordinary routes and exact gaps

All work here is ordinary reasoning. No finite sweep, matching enumeration, solver, CAS or Lean execution was used.

## 368449: deletion of a rightmost minimum-letter pair

Latest primary v2 Definitions 4.1 and 4.3 say: positions i<j admit an arc precisely when a_j=a_i+2; an arc configuration is a perfect matching of positions by such arcs. Crossings are allowed: confusing configurations with Catalan or irreducible configurations changes the question. Definition 6.16 takes the equivalence relation generated by pairs occurring in at least one complete configuration. Thus form the graph E*(w) consisting of matching-extendible allowed edges. The word is conf-connected exactly when E*(w) is connected. The words are even-integer sequences; shifting to minimum 0 preserves the entire configuration family.

First mechanism: choose an arbitrary configuration and force the desired pair (i,j), where i is the rightmost minimum a and j the rightmost a+2. The minimum cannot be the upper endpoint of any arc; since a configuration exists, i must match a later a+2, so i<j. If a configuration pairs i to j' rather than j, and j to p, then p is either an earlier minimum a or a later a+4. In the former case, switching (i,j'),(p,j) to (i,j),(p,j') preserves colours and order because p<=i<j'<=j. In the latter case, j is a lower endpoint for a+4 at p; switching to (i,j) would require pairing the remaining a+2 at j' with p, which is also permitted since j'<=j<p. This establishes an elementary switching route to a configuration containing (i,j), and hence existence of at least one residual configuration. It does NOT prove that all residual positions remain conf-connected. A single perfect matching has separate two-point components, and existence is much weaker than the target.

Second mechanism: use alternating cycles relative to a fixed configuration containing (i,j). The graph of matching-extendible edges is bipartite by the parity of a_s/2; differences by 2 cross its two classes. Differences of two perfect matchings are alternating cycles. One would need to replace every connection between residual components that uses i or j by an alternating cycle avoiding i,j. General elementary bipartite graphs do not have this deletion property: a cycle on six vertices is matching-connected, but deleting the endpoints of an edge leaves a four-vertex path whose unique perfect matching has two components. This is a conceptual obstruction to the GENERAL graph lemma, not an in-scope word counterexample. A proof must exploit the nested-by-position neighbourhoods among adjacent letter levels to rule out this obstruction. I have not proved that step, or shown that a word can realize the obstruction. No numerical verification of the source's length-10 claim was repeated.

Source Lemma 6.21 proves deletion implies Conjecture 6.14(2) by shifting the chosen pair by a generic complex parameter, creating one more conf-connected component. Lemma 6.15 then obtains undirected connectivity from (2). Those actual proof bodies were inspected. They transfer an eventual solution; they do not close the residual alternating-cycle lemma. This keeps both 368449 and 368448 at HOLD/PARKED.

## 368575: explicit cover, then endpoint obstruction

Primary source Sections 2 and 5 give combined-voltage semantics and identify F_2(K_(1,n)) with the one-subdivision of K_n. Let n=2m+1 be odd; for positive n, n divides n(n+1)/2 exactly when n is odd. Label the leaves by Z/nZ and the centre by *. The token vertices are A_i={*,i} and the unordered pairs B_(i,d)={i,i+d}, where 1<=d<=m. Every unordered pair of distinct leaves has a unique representative (i,d): the two oriented differences are nonzero opposites, and exactly one lies in {1,...,m}. Adjacency is precisely A_i—B_(i,d)—A_(i+d); there are no same-type edges.

Construct a base with hub h and m vertices q_d, two parallel directed edges h to q_d with voltages 0 and -d respectively, and trivial vertex subgroups. In the source's convention an edge of voltage a joins hub coordinate i to q_d coordinate i+a. Map (h,i) to A_i and (q_d,j) to {j,j+d}. Voltage 0 gives A_j; voltage -d gives hub i=j+d. Hence exactly both token incidences and no others occur. All vertex maps are bijections, so this proves the corrected (n+1)/2-base-vertex cover assertion. The cyclic action translating all leaves is free: a translation fixing a two-set must either fix both leaves or interchange them; interchange would require 2c=0, hence c=0 for odd n. This is the complete standard free-action/voltage-lift bridge, rather than an assertion that familiar graphs are automatically excluded.

But the EXACT record says n-k vertices without defining k, and source Conjecture 5.1 does the same. The preceding Section 4 uses k=(n+1)/2 for a different token graph. If that k is inherited, n-k=m, whereas a combined lift on m vertices over C_n has at most mn vertices because each vertex fibre is a subgroup index <=n. The target has n(n+1)/2=n(m+1) vertices. So that interpretation is impossible even with nontrivial subgroups. Substituting k=(n-1)/2 silently repairs the question. The two alternatives are a public classical construction for a corrected endpoint, or a base-count wording correction; neither is an eligible new exact solution. Figures 1–3 captions and surrounding proof text were read, but the actual diagrams were not visually reviewed, so no diagram clearance is claimed.

## 368999: stabilizer route beyond a failed prime-order shortcut

A prime-order cyclic group has only the trivial and full subgroup, which immediately explains why prime examples cannot decide this all-n assertion. The appropriate composite-order mechanism is to build a difference set that is a union of cosets of a nontrivial proper subgroup. S'={0,1,m} in C_(2m) does exactly that at length 2. The complete proof, exact n=8 witness, admissibility, full word-set equality, exact stabilizer and latest-version check are in ORDINARY-PROOF-368999.md.
