## Literal and model
For every integer m>=1, P_m is the set of laws of the strict ordering of m independent real Borel probability random variables, with P(X_i=X_j)=0 for i!=j (source Section 5.1, p21). We prove its real semialgebraic dimension is C(m)=sum_{k=2}^m binom(m,k)(k-1)!. This equals the literal sum m!/[k(m-k)!]. The source does not include m=0; if extended to the empty product its unique ordering law is a point and the empty sum is zero. For m=1 the same conclusion is immediate. Dimension means maximum smooth stratum dimension as described after Definition E.3, not affine span or rank by definition.

## Algebra and independent directions
Let A have real basis the empty word 1 and every distinct-letter word on [m]. The product of two basis words is concatenation when their supports are disjoint and zero otherwise. For three words, either all three supports are pairwise disjoint, when both parenthesizations concatenate, or some letter repeats, when both are zero. Bilinearity proves associativity; 1 is the unit. For a one-letter basis element a_i, a_i^2=0, hence (1+t a_i)^-1=1-t a_i.
Let G be the subgroup of A's units generated by these elements for all real t. Every element and its inverse is a finite elementary product. Let V be the span of all individual conjugates g a_i g^-1. It is invariant under conjugation by G, since conjugating a generator of the span produces another such generator. For v in V,
Ad_(1+a_i)(v)-Ad_(1-a_i)(v)=2(a_i v-v a_i).
Indeed the two expansions have the same -a_i v a_i term. Thus [a_i,v] belongs to V. Every right-nested bracket in distinct letters belongs to V.
For each nonempty subset S choose one fixed anchor j in S (for example its minimum). For every ordering i_1,...,i_{k-1} of S\{j}, put b=[a_i1,[a_i2,...,[a_i(k-1),a_j]...]]. Its expansion has exactly one word ending in j, namely i_1...i_(k-1)j, of coefficient +1. Inductively, in [a_i,B]=a_i B-B a_i, only the first summand can end in j and the assertion propagates. These ending-j coordinates give an identity matrix for the (k-1)! brackets on S. Different S lie in disjoint support-graded subspaces. Including the m singletons, V therefore has dimension at least D=sum_{nonempty S}(|S|-1)!=m+C(m). No claim about a free Lie basis or about equality dim V=D is needed.
Select D linearly independent INDIVIDUAL conjugates q_l=g_l a_(i_l) g_l^-1 from the spanning set: iteratively select a conjugate outside the span of the ones already chosen, which is possible until D are chosen because dim V>=D. There is no assumption that this selection contains prescribed singleton conjugates.

## One word and positivity
Define Q(s)=product_{l=1}^D g_l(1+s_l a_(i_l))g_l^-1. Q(0)=1, so its lth partial derivative at 0 is exactly q_l: all other conjugate blocks specialize to 1. Expand every fixed g_l and inverse into finite elementary factors, retaining separate positions even for equal adjacent letters or zero weights. This produces one finite word W and an affine parameter placement beta(s) with each s_l in its own middle factor; the other weights are fixed real numbers. Write F_W(alpha)=product_h(1+alpha_h a_(W_h)). Then Q=F_W o beta and dF_W at beta(0) has rank at least D.
To ensure every letter occurs, append one factor (1+z_i a_i) per i, set all these new z_i=0 at the witness, and retain the previous derivatives. This explicitly solves the all-symbol occurrence issue regardless of the spanning selection. All weights of the enlarged W are subsequently independent variables.
A D by D minor of the polynomial Jacobian dF_W is nonzero at that signed witness, hence a nonzero real polynomial. A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on the open positive orthant: induction on the number of variables, using the univariate root bound on intervals, proves the assertion. Thus there is a strictly positive alpha* where rank dF_W>=D. This is existence, with no numerical search or negative probability interpretation. Each individual symbol mass r_i=sum_{h:W_h=i}alpha_h is positive throughout this orthant.

## ALL subset coordinates and exact model binding
For positive alpha let independent X_i choose the integer position h of symbol i with probability alpha_h/r_i. Supports for different i are disjoint, so noncollision holds. Their ordering law p(alpha) is precisely Definition 5.2's word map, hence belongs to Section 5.1's P_m. Its coordinates are rational smooth functions on the orthant, with denominators product_i r_i.
For a distinct-letter word u with support S, expansion of F_W gives
[u]F_W = sum_{h_1<...<h_|S|, W_h1...W_h|S|=u} product_t alpha_ht.
Dividing by product_{i in S}r_i is the probability that the chosen positions for the variables in S occur in order u. The independent choices for the missing letters each sum to probability one; every complete choice has one full permutation sigma, and the restriction of that sigma to S is u exactly when the S positions have this order. Consequently
[u]F_W=(product_{i in S}r_i) sum_{sigma: sigma|S=u}p_sigma.
This includes the empty word: the empty product is 1 and sum_sigma p_sigma=1. It includes singletons: the marginal sum is 1 and the coordinate is r_i. It includes full words and every intermediate subset, so no untracked Jacobian directions remain.
Define H(r,p) on all r,p by these polynomial coordinate formulas. On the positive orthant F_W=H(r(alpha),p(alpha)). Therefore
D <= rank dF_W <= rank d(r,p) <= m+rank dp,
and rank dp(alpha*)>=C(m). Precisely m INDIVIDUAL masses are subtracted, not one common scaling coordinate.
Choose a nonzero C(m)-minor of dp at alpha*. Fix all the other input coordinates and project onto its output coordinates. The inverse function theorem gives an open C(m)-dimensional set in that projection of the image of p, on a sufficiently small positive neighborhood. Equivalently the corresponding graph in the image is a smooth embedded C(m)-manifold. Thus dim P_m>=C(m) in the real semialgebraic sense. This step uses the semialgebraic dimension/projection theorem (or smooth stratification and dimension monotonicity), not the definition 'dimension equals Jacobian rank'.

## Finite support, upper bound, and source dependencies
No short-support constraint belongs to the literal target. Our W has finite, unrestricted length and gives actual product measures, so its entire image lies in P_m even if its length exceeds the source bound. Theorem 5.4 states N=m(m!+1) suffices for every noncolliding product law; Lemma 5.5 in fact shortens to <=m! positions per symbol. Its proof conditions on one variable, applies Caratheodory in the probability hyperplane, and repeats for each symbol. Theorem 5.4's proof approximates inverse CDFs by atomic choices, bounds intersecting quantile boxes by 2j-1 for each pair and error by m^2/j, then shortens and takes a subsequential limit in the compact finite union of normalized word parameter simplices. On each fixed combinatorial word use integer positions, so no collision is introduced by a limit of positions. Zero weights may be deleted; each symbol still has normalized total one. This proves exact representation, not only density or a closure definition.
Finite many words of bounded length, normalized per-letter simplex parameters and polynomial ordering probabilities imply P_m is semialgebraic by Tarski-Seidenberg. A single sufficiently long cyclic word can also cover every bounded word by subsequence padding with zero weights. Consecutive equal letters may first be merged. This polynomial parametrization has an irreducible Zariski closure, since its simplex domain has relative interior in an affine space and polynomial identities pull back to polynomial identities there.
Use the actual source Theorem 5.13 upper bound, with Lemmas 5.11/5.12 and Appendix E, as a sourced ordinary dependency. Here is its full endpoint bridge. For each permutation pi having >=2 nontrivial disjoint cycles, construct the +/- events from the signed nested bracket expansion on each cycle, with other letters free. Events depend on disjoint groups of independent variables. Their full sign refinements are independent; thus the even/odd product equality holds. At the uniform ordering law each sign event on a cycle of length r has probability 2^(r-2)/r!>0, and each conjunction has the common positive product b. The gradient of the even-minus-odd constraint is +/- b^(2^(k-1)-1) times the shuffle of the cycle brackets, with singleton cycles inserted by shuffling. Hence it is NONZERO. By source Lemma 5.11 these gradients and the all-ones normalization vector (identity permutation) are independent. Their number is m!-C(m). The uniform law is in P_m, obtained from independent continuous identically distributed uniforms. A polynomial minor of these gradients is nonzero there.
Pull this minor back along a universal normalized polynomial word map. It is not the zero polynomial, so its zero locus has lower dimension in the irreducible image closure. At generic smooth points of P_m the m!-C(m) gradients remain independent. Since all constraints vanish on P_m, they annihilate stratum tangents. Thus maximum stratum dimension is <=C(m). This is the correct generic interpretation of source E.4; the printed assertion 'all points' is unnecessarily strong and is not used here. Combining gives equality for every m>=1.

## Additional adversarial source qualification
Appendix E.1 as worded assumes independence of the union events E_i, which alone does NOT imply independence of their +/- refinements. The application here has the stronger property actually needed: every refinement on cycle i is measurable in its own disjoint independent coordinate block. Factoring the conjunction probabilities is therefore justified for our application. No general assertion for arbitrarily split independent union events is imported. This is another zero-credit source wording repair, not an extra theorem claim.
