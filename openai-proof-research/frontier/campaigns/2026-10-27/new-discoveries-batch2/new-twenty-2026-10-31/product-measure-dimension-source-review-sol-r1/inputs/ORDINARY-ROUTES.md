# Sustained routes without full endpoints

361174 (actual Conjecture2.18 read; Proposition2.17 entire algorithm/proof read). In d=m-1 dimensions with d fixed coordinate hyperplanes, a fully generic n-new-plane arrangement reaches f_d(n)=binom(n+d-1,d). Moving A planes through a simple d-fold point produces deficiency f_d(A), and several suitably generic separated such choices add deficiencies subject to the sum of numbers of new planes <=n-1. This is the source construction, not a new result. An integer-representation approach asks to write every desired deficit f_d(n)-k as a sum of f_d(A_j) with total A_j<=n-1. Source's asymptotic argument controls only an upper interval and then duplicates planes; its constants leave the small n/all-k uniform claim unpaid. In particular the obvious greedy largest-binomial choice has remainder of order A^{d-1}; bounding its cost recursively asymptotically does not prove the sharp finite budget.
A second deformation route fails: for fixed normals in general position all generic offsets yield the SAME maximal bounded count. A simple (d+1)-fold concurrence gives a one-region loss only ON the discriminant, rather than monotonically connecting all counts. Passing through a sequence of discriminants does not accumulate simultaneous deficits. A third induction adds a plane, whose bounded-region increment is the bounded count of a restricted (d-1)-arrangement, but its induced intersections must simultaneously agree with the old arrangement chosen to realize the residual count. Arbitrarily selecting both counts independently is invalid. Exact unpaid lemma: all desired residual/incidence restrictions realizable with only n added planes for every d>=3,n>=d+1. No finite enumeration performed; no candidate.

356297 (all eight pages of extracted primary text including definitions, proofs and continued-fraction section read). Source P counts the EMPTY subsequence, as gen(1,1) has P=1; literal counts nonempty. Thus source's coprime-state model for a literal target n has a+b=n+2, not n+1. This harmless shift is explicitly paid and not promoted as a discovery. For nonempty distinct subsequence counts define states a=1+#nonempty starting A and b=1+#nonempty starting B. Prepending A sends (a,b) to (a+b,b), prepending B to (a,a+b), with initial(1,1); coprimality persists, every coprime positive pair reduces uniquely by subtracting its smaller coordinate until(1,1). Length is exactly the number of these subtractions. The desired all-n bound is therefore the existence for each N=n+2 of a coprime 1<=a<N with subtractive Euclidean cost O(log N) for (a,N-a).
Route1 balanced splitting a near N/phi: balanced initial coordinates do NOT imply uniformly bounded later quotients, and the gcd condition can obstruct the chosen closest numerator. A single late long Euclidean run can dominate total length. Route2 concatenation: the exact source bilinear concatenation formula can assemble some totals efficiently, but a product/factorization construction does not cover arbitrary prime totals; additive one-step correction acts on an entire state pair, not just the count, and may cost a long run. Route3 source Theorem4 couples a bounded-partial-quotient irrational to a reduced a/N: its error term N*sqrt(delta*C^3) is logarithmic only under an approximation far stronger than nearest-integer delta=O(1/N). Uniformly obtaining suitable coprime numerators/irrationals is precisely an unpaid number-theoretic input, not an elementary rounding fact. Source's Rukavishnikova application yields O(log N log log N), and density-one bounded quotients do not imply every N. Unpaid: uniform O(log N) minimum partial-quotient SUM (bounded maximum is stronger and sufficient). No numerical tests or algorithm execution.
