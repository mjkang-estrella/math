# MathDB 379886: independent ordinary presentation audit

This audit derives the presentation from Definition 2.3, rather than accepting r274's presentation as a theorem. It is not a second-agent acceptance review and does not prove Conjecture 8.12. All statements below have m an integer at least 2. No matrix was generated or reduced.

## Actual cohomology

Use the unreduced chromatic cochain complex, M=A=A_m=Z[x]/(x^m), with deg(x)=1. A state is a subset s of the TEN edges, its cohomological degree is |s|, and its summand is one tensor factor A per connected component of the spanning subgraph. The differential adds an edge, multiplies the two labels when components merge, and has cube sign (-1)^{#{f in s:f<e}}. There is no label-dependent Koszul sign. In the source's ordinary version a same-component edge acts by identity. The variant in preprint Remark 2.4 instead makes that map zero. These coincide through the maps relevant here: a two-edge state in the simple graph K5 is always a forest. There is no use of Hochschild homology for K5.

At j=3m-2, every two-edge state has exactly three components, whose total degree is at most 3(m-1)<j. Thus C^{2,j}=0. Consequently H^{1,j}=C^{1,j}/im(d^0), canonically, not merely after tensoring with Q. Higher-edge states are irrelevant to this equality. The degree of d is (1,0).

Let F_m be the free abelian group on symbols g_{uv}(c; (d_w)_{w not in {u,v}}), for unordered pairs {u,v} in {1,...,5}, with c,d_w nonnegative and c+sum d_w=m-2. The c coordinate belongs to the merged component, and the other three coordinates remain VERTEX-LABELLED. Each coordinate automatically is at most m-2. There are 10 binomial(m+1,3) symbols.

For every b=(b_1,...,b_5) with 0<=b_i<=m-1 and sum b_i=2m-3 impose exactly the relation

R_b = sum_{u<v, b_u+b_v>=m-1} g_{uv}(b_u+b_v-(m-1); (b_w)_{w not in {u,v}})=0.

The empty sum is allowed and adds no relation. Denote the quotient by P_m.

The map F_m -> C^{1,j} sends g to the state with edge uv, merged exponent m-1-c, and isolated exponents m-1-d_w. Its explicit inverse subtracts each component exponent from m-1. These maps are inverse on bases and extend Z-linearly. Similarly, the empty-state basis with exponents a_i corresponds bijectively to b_i=m-1-a_i, because sum a_i=3m-2 iff sum b_i=2m-3. Its differential has ALL plus signs. A term survives iff a_u+a_v<m, equivalently b_u+b_v>=m-1, and then has exactly the displayed merged deficit. Therefore the two basis maps identify im(d^0) with the subgroup generated by ALL R_b. They induce inverse homomorphisms P_m <-> H^{1,3m-2}. This proves both directions of the presentation bridge. It does not identify P_m with the conjectured elementary divisors.

The number of source generators is the coefficient of t^{2m-3} in (1+...+t^{m-1})^5, namely binomial(2m+1,4)-5 binomial(m+1,4), using binomial(n,4)=0 for n<4. This is a symbolic stars-and-bars count, not an enumeration.

## Further uniform local reduction, with proof

For fixed b let Q_b have edge uv exactly when b_u+b_v>=m-1. It has no two disjoint edges: two such edges would require total deficit at least 2m-2, whereas the available total is 2m-3. Every nonempty pairwise-intersecting family of two-element sets is a star or a triangle. Indeed, choose two distinct edges uv,uw; if every edge contains u it is a star. Otherwise the edge meeting both and avoiding u must be vw, and any edge meeting all three must belong to that triangle. Hence every relation has support a star of size at most FOUR, or all three edges of a triangle. A singleton is a star; zero relations were handled separately. No symmetric-group averaging or division was used.

There is also an exact criterion for generators killed by a singleton relation. Write g=g_{uv}(c;d_1,d_2,d_3), s=max d_i. Every empty-state lift of g has

b_u=t, b_v=m-1+c-t, b_w=d_w, with c<=t<=m-1.

Pairs wholly outside uv never survive, because d_i+d_j<=m-2. There are no further edges incident with u or v precisely when t+s<=m-2 and m-1+c-t+s<=m-2. Such an integer t exists iff

c+2s<=m-3.

For sufficiency take t=c+s+1; for necessity combine t>=c+s+1 and t<=m-2-s. These inequalities imply the stated admissible lift bounds. Thus g is the ONLY term of some R_b iff this criterion holds; in particular such g vanishes in P_m. This does NOT assert that every generator vanishing in the full quotient satisfies the criterion.

## Sustained route and exact failure

First discard these explicit zero generators, substituting zero in every remaining star/triangle relation. Relations with two remaining terms transport one generator to minus another; relations with three or four terms prevent this from being just a signed graph incidence problem. To finish an integral reduction one must specify pivots and surviving generators uniformly in m, prove every original star/triangle relation reduces to the proposed ones, and exhibit maps back whose composites differ from the identity by integral combinations of R_b. No terminating, confluent transport scheme or complete residual relation system was obtained. Sorting the deficit tuple alone loses which vertex is merged and is not a valid quotient operation.

Triangle supports suggest where sign transport could produce 2-primary relations, but they do not prove the exact numbers of Z_2 factors. Likewise, a putative length-m transport does not prove an independent Z_m summand unless its order is exactly m and all extension relations are controlled. The overlap of stars and triangles is the precise unresolved integral step. No asserted normal form or parity proof is hidden in this description.

## Known boundary cases and explicit exclusions

For m=2, the C1 symbols are ten edges with all deficits zero. The five empty-state relations are the vertex stars. This is the transpose unsigned vertex-edge incidence presentation of K5. Eliminate the four edges 1i using the relations for vertices i=2,...,5. The remaining relation is twice the sum of the six edges among {2,3,4,5}. That sum is primitive, so its quotient is Z^5 plus Z/2. This is a complete elementary proof, and is already source Theorem 3.1/Corollary 3.2: p1=10-5+1=6 and K5 is nonbipartite. It receives no new credit.

For m=3, source Corollary 4.9 applies with n=5 and j=2n-3=7=3m-2. It gives Z/2 plus (Z/3)^4 plus Z^{5*4*(10-7)/6}=Z/2 plus (Z/3)^4 plus Z^10. Its proof uses the source's cell complex and UCT; this is an explicit printed subsumer of that case, not a new all-m theorem.

The m=4 case is exceptional even among even m; it cannot be inferred from the stable even clause. The m=5 case is both in the printed finite checks and the separate A5 complete-graph conjecture, not a proved all-m bridge. All printed m<=9 checks are prior computations.

Rational rank, finite-field rank, chromatic polynomial, or an S5-isotypic decomposition after division by 120 would fail to control this integral quotient. Z/4 and (Z/2)^2 illustrate why rank and even order alone are insufficient. No known source result read here resolves the entire P_m family. There is no predicted discrepant m>=10 or credible fixed certificate to justify a blind Smith run.
