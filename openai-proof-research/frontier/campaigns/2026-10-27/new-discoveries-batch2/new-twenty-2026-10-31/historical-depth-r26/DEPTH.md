Mechanism A: diagonalize B, expand the exponential by ordered-simplex Dyson integrals, and sum closed paths. For each path gamma, its contribution is the pushforward of a positive simplex measure, weighted by the cyclic product of A entries. The zero-jump terms give exp(a_ii) delta_bi. Theorem2 already gives exactly this formula for general Hermitian A and distinct b_i. Thus merely writing this expansion is not new.

Sustained obstruction: in real symmetric dimension three, two-edge loops are nonnegative but triangular loops carry a12*a23*a31. With A=[[0,1,1],[1,0,-2],[1,-2,0]], B=diag(0,2,1), each of the six oriented length-three loops has weight -2. This is a diagnostic against termwise positivity, NOT a counterexample to BMV (which is true). Diagonal phases cannot remove it: the product of their three factors telescopes to one. Since B has simple spectrum, diagonalizing it leaves only diagonal phases/permutations; no general simultaneous entrywise-nonnegative reduction follows. Shifting A by a scalar only rescales the measure and leaves off-diagonal cyclic products intact.

Mechanism B: regroup by three-state visit counts and resum the signed triangle contributions hypergeometrically. The old proof does this, rather than ignoring the negative loops. In Lemma9 the sign argument requires omega>=0 and lambda<=0; its last paragraph explicitly translates these back to Theorem1's size/diagonal restrictions. Our example fails |a12|/sqrt(b2-b1)>=|a13|/sqrt(b3-b1), since 1/sqrt(2)<1; hence that proof cannot be declared universal. A permutation might repair a particular example, but no all-input covering argument has been proved here. General complex Hermitian triangular phases add another bridge; a real-symmetric theorem is insufficient.

Mechanism C: Stahl's density sum over contour integrals of exp(lambda_j(zeta)+t*zeta) is a correct semi-explicit general answer. Section4.2 verifies its Laplace transform by Fubini, telescoping, and residues, but the paper explicitly separates positivity into Section5. Cubic radicals in lambda_j would leave contour/branch choices and positivity, and do not automatically yield the requested simple proof. No new elementary all-input positivity identity found.

## Rank 2: 384601 — four sharp Hermite–Hadamard constants

Record says only 'Find the optimal constants in the inequalities.' The source is Steinerberger's workshop question; the AIM page and workshop PDF could not be fetched. Read Steinerberger arXiv1808.07794v2 introduction, Theorems1–4 and integration-by-parts setup pp1–3. Read Larson arXiv2005.01853v2 Theorems1.1/1.2, proof of upper bound in Section2 pp6–7 (equations14–21). Read the ENTIRE mathematical body of Ferudun's four-page October15,2026 preprint, Theorem1, Proposition2 and proofs, smoothing, quantitative error and scope discussion. This is an unrefereed public prior proof, not a newly discovered campaign endpoint. Original workshop identity has corroboration from its explicit Problem6.1 reference; full original context remains unavailable.

The natural scope is two normalizations, for each of convex and subharmonic nonnegative functions on bounded convex domains. Write I=int_Omega f, J=int_boundary f, V=volume, S=surface. Boundary ratio R=SI/(VJ); volume-power ratio Q=I/(V^(1/n)J). Their relation is Q=V^((n-1)/n)R/S. Larson gives R<n for each fixed domain, with sharp supremum n in the subharmonic class. His proof combines inradius r<=nV/S, Sperb ||grad u||^2<=2||u|| and heat-kernel comparison ||u||<r²/2. These yield ||grad u||<r and the stated upper bound. External heat-kernel/Sperb proofs not audited.

First exact constructive attempt: a fixed affine function on a collapsing simplex. It cannot saturate n; Ferudun explicitly obtains only 2n/(n+1) when its localization parameter d=1. Thus fixed-affine thin-domain tests are insufficient.

Second sustained route: localized convex hinge f_d=(d-s)_+ on K_h={x_i>=0,y>=0,s+y/h<=1}, s=sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_i. Let k=n-1, A=1+sqrt(1+kh²). Direct slice integration gives V=h/n!, S=(A+kh)/(n-1)!, I=h*d^n/n!*(1-k*d/(n+1)), J=d^(n-1)/n!*[d*A+kh*(n-(n-2)d)]. All coordinate faces, bottom and sloped face are included; the support cut s=d is not a boundary face. Consequently R=n*(A+kh)*(1-k*d/(n+1))/(A+(kh/d)*(n-(n-2)d)). With h=d², d->0, R->n. This is precisely the public October15 construction, not ours. Smooth strictly positive strictly convex approximation uses (d-s+sqrt((d-s)^2+epsilon²))/2+epsilon*|z|², with epsilon chosen after fixing the simplex. All these known mechanisms are uncredited.

Third route: transfer to Q using the exact normalization identity. The same h=d² family has S->2/(n-1)!, V~d²/n!, R->n; therefore Q->0 for n>=2. It cannot prove sharpness for either volume-power problem. At fixed h>0 and d->0 even R->0, so exchanging limits does not salvage it. Isoperimetry supplies only an upper bound, and equality requirements conflict with the degenerating simplex. On a ball with f=1, Q=1/(n*omega_n^(1/n)); symmetry gives the same ratio for positive affine tests. Neither this lower bound nor the classical subharmonic/convex inclusion gives the optimal all-domain constant. Required residual: an exact universal Q bound with a matching admissible extremizing family, separately for convex and subharmonic f. No source-scope widening and no claim the full four-constant problem is known solved. Outcome: public boundary-half overlap plus unresolved full-scope/source gap; zero Lean investment on known half.

## Rank 3: 384725 — linear crossing families already publicly proved

Concrete proof mechanism: obtain separated m-point sets A,B with m proportional to n and avoidance defect t<=m²/4, using cited Pach–Rubin–Tardos Lemma3.3. Dualize points (r,s) to y=rx-s, red slopes negative and blue positive. Perturb preserving all orientation signs to separate event times. Partition red-blue intersections into 2m-1 rank levels. A defective same-color event marks the red line exiting the lower-k set, or the blue line entering it. For every line, number d_k of red-blue occurrences satisfies d_k<=1+u_k, because between repeat occurrences it must transition while sandwiched between two opposite-color lines. This produces a mark. Marked lines lose at most sum d_k<=2 sum u_k=2T_k incidences. Clean intersections have no repeated lines; Lemma5.1's four strict inequalities prove opposite-side tests for both segments, hence proper crossings. Summing levels loses at most 2t and averaging leaves >=(m²-2t)/(2m-1)>=m/4. With m=floor(n/H), H>=256K, c=1/(8H), the large-n case follows; n<2H uses one segment.
