A graph G G is said to be generically universally rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} if every d d -dimensional generic framework ( G , p ) (G,p) is universally rigid. In this paper we focus on the case d = 1 d=1 . We give counterexamples to a conjectured characterization of generically universally rigid graphs from [ 7 ] . We also introduce two new operations that preserve the universal rigidity of generic frameworks, and the property of being not universally rigid, respectively. One of these operations is used in the analysis of one of our examples, while the other operation is applied to obtain a lower bound on the size of generically universally rigid graphs. This bound gives a partial answer to a question from [ 11 ] . Keywords: rigid graph, universally rigid graph, generic framework 1 Introduction A d d -dimensional (bar-and-joint) framework is a pair ( G , p ) (G,p) , where G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) is a graph and p p is a configuration of the vertices, that is, a map from V V to ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We consider the framework to be a straight line realization of G G in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Two frameworks ( G , p ) (G,p) and ( G , q ) (G,q) are equivalent if ‖ p ⁡ ( u ) − p ⁡ ( v ) ‖ = ‖ q ⁡ ( u ) − q ⁡ ( v ) ‖ \|p(u)-p(v)\|=\|q(u)-q(v)\| holds for all pairs u , v u,v with u ​ v ∈ E uv\in E , where ∥ . ∥ \|.\| denotes the Euclidean norm in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Frameworks ( G , p ) (G,p) , ( G , q ) (G,q) are congruent if ‖ p ⁡ ( u ) − p ⁡ ( v ) ‖ = ‖ q ⁡ ( u ) − q ⁡ ( v ) ‖ \|p(u)-p(v)\|=\|q(u)-q(v)\| holds for all pairs u , v u,v with u , v ∈ V u,v\in V . This is the same as saying that ( G , q ) (G,q) can be obtained from ( G , p ) (G,p) by an isometry of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Let ( G , p ) (G,p) be a d d -dimensional framework for some d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . We say that ( G , p ) (G,p) is rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} if there is a neighborhood U p U_{p} in the space of d d -dimensional configurations such that if a d d -dimensional framework ( G , q ) (G,q) is equivalent to ( G , p ) (G,p) and q ∈ U p q\in U_{p} , then q q is congruent to p p . The framework ( G , p ) (G,p) is called globally rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} if every d d -dimensional framework ( G , q ) (G,q) which is equivalent to ( G , p ) (G,p) is congruent to ( G , p ) (G,p) . We obtain an even stronger property by extending this condition to equivalent realizations in any dimension: we say that ( G , p ) (G,p) is universally rigid if it is a unique realization of G G , up to congruence, with the given edge lengths, in all dimensions ℝ d ′ \mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}} , d ′ ≥ 1 d^{\prime}\geq 1 . Deciding whether a given framework is rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , for d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 (resp. globally rigid in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , for d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 ) is NP-hard [ 1 , 14 ] . The complexity of the corresponding decision problem for universal rigidity seems to be open, even for d = 1 d=1 . These problems become more tractable, however, if we assume that there are no algebraic dependencies between the coordinates of the points of the framework. A framework ( G , p ) (G,p) is said to be generic if the set containing the coordinates of all its points is algebraically independent over the rationals. It is well-known that the rigidity (resp. global rigidity) of frameworks in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} is a generic property for all d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 , that is, the (global) rigidity of ( G , p ) (G,p) depends only on the graph G G and not the particular realization p p , if ( G , p ) (G,p) is generic [ 2 , 6 , 10 ] . This property does not hold for universal rigidity, even if d = 1 d=1 , which follows by considering different generic realizations of a four-cycle on the line. See Figure 1 . A graph G G is called generically rigid (resp. generically globally rigid , generically universally rigid ) in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} if every d d -dimensional generic framework ( G , p ) (G,p) is rigid (resp. globally rigid, universally rigid). Generically rigid and globally rigid graphs are well-characterized for d ≤ 2 d\leq 2 . It remains an open problem to extend these results to d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 . The characterization of generically universally rigid graphs is an open problem for all d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . We refer the reader to [ 12 , 15 ] for more details on the theory of rigid graphs and frameworks. In this paper we focus on universally rigid frameworks and generically universally rigid graphs in ℝ 1 \mathbb{R}^{1} . We give counterexamples to a conjectured characterization of generically universally rigid graphs from [ 7 ] . We introduce a new operation that preserves the universal rigidity of generic frameworks and use it to construct infinite families of counterexamples. We also show that the so-called degree-2 extension operation preserves the property of being not universally rigid, for d = 1 d=1 . This operation is applied in the proof of a new lower bound on the size of generically universally rigid graphs. It was conjectured, by participants of a workshop in 2011, that for d = 1 d=1 every generically universally rigid graph can be obtained from a set of triangles by this operation, and edge addition. [ 7 ] A graph G G on at least three vertices is generically universally rigid in ℝ 1 \mathbb{R}^{1} if and only if G G can be obtained from a set of triangles by edge reduced attachment and edge addition operations.
