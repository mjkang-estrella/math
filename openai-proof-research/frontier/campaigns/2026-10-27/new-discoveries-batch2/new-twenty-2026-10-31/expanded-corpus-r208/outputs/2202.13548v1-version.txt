Email: M.S.Keane@tudelft.nl Date: February 28, 2022 Abstract The Oldenburger Kolakoski sequence is fixed point of a two-block substitution. We consider in general two-block substitutions and their fixed points. We prove that some of them have a simple structure: their fixed points are morphic sequences. We further introduce the Thue-Morse two-block substitution whose fixed points behave like the Kolakoski sequence. Keywords: two-block substitutions, Kolakoski sequence, morphic words 1 Introduction Let A = { 0 , 1 } A=\{0,1\} , A ∗ A^{*} the monoid of all words over A A , and let T ∗ T^{*} be the submonoid of 0-1-words of even length. A two-block substitution κ \kappa is a map κ : { 00 , 01 , 10 , 11 } → A ∗ . \kappa:\>\{00,01,10,11\}\rightarrow A^{*}. A two-block substitution κ \kappa acts on T ∗ T^{*} by defining for w 1 ​ w 2 ​ … ​ w 2 ​ m − 1 ​ w 2 ​ m ∈ T ∗ w_{1}w_{2}\dots w_{2m-1}w_{2m}\in T^{*} κ ⁡ ( w 1 ​ w 2 ​ … ​ w 2 ​ m − 1 ​ w 2 ​ m ) = κ ⁡ ( w 1 ​ w 2 ) ​ … ​ κ ​ ( w 2 ​ m − 1 ​ w 2 ​ m ) . \kappa(w_{1}w_{2}\dots w_{2m-1}w_{2m})=\kappa(w_{1}w_{2})\dots\kappa(w_{2m-1}w_{2m}). In the case that κ ⁡ ( T ∗ ) ⊆ T ∗ \kappa(T^{*})\subseteq T^{*} , we call κ \kappa 2-block stable . This property entails that the iterates κ n \kappa^{n} are all well-defined for n = 1 , 2 , … n=1,2,\dots . The most interesting example of a two-block map that is not 2-block stable is the Oldenburger-Kolakoski two-block map κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} given by κ K ​ ( 00 ) = 10 , κ K ​ ( 01 ) = 100 , κ K ​ ( 10 ) = 110 , κ K ​ ( 11 ) = 1100 . \kappa_{\rm\sc K}(00)=10,\hskip 9.24994pt\kappa_{\rm\sc K}(01)=100,\hskip 9.24994pt\kappa_{\rm\sc K}(10)=110,\hskip 9.24994pt\kappa_{\rm\sc K}(11)=1100. The fact that κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} is not 2-block stable, and so its iterates κ K n \kappa_{\rm\sc K}^{n} are not defined, makes it very hard to establish properties of the fixed point x K = 110010 ​ … x_{\rm\sc K}=110010\dots (usually written as 221121…) of κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} , see, e.g., [ 2 ] . In Section 2 we show that even if a two-block substitution κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} is not 2-block stable, then still it can be well-behaved in the sense that its fixed points are pure morphic words. In Section 3 we give a way to find morphisms that are conjugated to two-block maps.
