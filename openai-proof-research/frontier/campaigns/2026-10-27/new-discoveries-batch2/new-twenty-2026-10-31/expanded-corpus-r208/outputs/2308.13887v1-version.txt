In this context, Farhi and Gutmann [ 20 ] first employed continuous-time quantum walks to examine various quantum transportation phenomena. The graph G G is said to exhibit perfect state transfer (PST) between two vertices u u and v v if there exists τ ∈ ℝ \tau\in{\mathbbm{R}} such that U ⁡ ( τ ) ​ 𝐞 u = γ ​ 𝐞 v , U\left(\tau\right){\bf e}_{u}=\gamma{\bf e}_{v}, for some complex number γ \gamma called phase factor . A graph is called periodic if it is periodic at all vertices at the same time. Perfect state transfer represents accurate transmission of quantum states within a quantum spin network, which is critical for short distance communication in a physical quantum computing scheme [ 27 ] . The study of perfect state transfer on graphs was initiated by Bose [ 9 ] and Christandl et al. [ 13 ] . In [ 12 ] , we find that P 2 P_{2} and P 3 P_{3} are the only paths that exhibit PST. Following this negative result, several classes of graphs admitting PST have been studied, such as the cubelike graphs [ 8 , 11 ] , distance regular graphs [ 14 ] , integral circulant graphs [ 3 , 7 , 41 ] , Hadamard diagonalizable graphs [ 27 , 29 ] , and quotient graphs [ 6 , 21 ] . PST has also been investigated on various graph products. For example, it is known that the Cartesian product of graphs with PST at the same time also exhibits PST, and so one can build bigger graphs with PST from smaller ones. Christandl et al. used this fact to build bigger graphs with PST over long distances [ 12 ] . PST was also studied in the context of circulant join of graphs [ 4 , 3 ] , corona products [ 1 ] , weak and (generalized) lexicographic products [ 21 ] , non-complete extended p-sums (NEPS) [ 34 , 35 ] , tensor product of graphs [ 15 ] and the merge operation [ 27 ] . Despite the tremendous amount of research on graphs admitting perfect state transfer, it was shown that PST in simple unweighted graphs is a rare phenomenon [ 25 ] . This prompted researchers to relax the notion of PST to what is known as pretty good state transfer (PGST) [ 24 , 44 ] . A graph G G exhibits PGST between two vertices u u and v v if there is a sequence τ k ∈ ℝ \tau_{k}\in{\mathbbm{R}} such that lim k → ∞ U ⁡ ( τ k ) ​ 𝐞 u = γ ​ 𝐞 v , for some ​ γ ∈ ℂ . \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}U\left(\tau_{k}\right){\bf e}_{u}=\gamma{\bf e}_{v},~\text{for some }\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}. (1) Equivalently, PGST occurs between u u and v v if the probability | U ​ ( t ) u , v | 2 |U(t)_{u,v}|^{2} can be made arbitrarily close to one through appropriate choices of t t . Several classes of trees have been investigated for PGST, such as the paths [ 17 , 22 , 42 ] , double stars [ 19 ] , and 1 1 -sum of stars [ 26 ] . Some subclasses of circulant graphs are also known to exhibit PGST [ 38 , 39 , 36 ] . Our goal in this paper is to systematically study quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs. There are several variants of the blow-up operation on graphs (see [ 32 ] ), but here, we consider the more familiar type of blow-up where every vertex of a graph is replaced by an independent set. This variant of the blow-up operation is a special case of the lexicographic product. Recall that the lexicographic product G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] of two graphs G G and H H is the graph G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] obtained by replacing every vertex of G G by a copy of H H , and adding all possible edges between the vertices in the copies of H H corresponding to adjacent vertices in G G . In [ 21 ] , Ge at al. provided sufficient conditions for PST to occur in lexicographic products. Their results about PST in G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] require that H H exhibits PST. But in our case, we have that H H is an empty graph which cannot exhibit PST, and so their results are not applicable. Monterde, on the other hand, investigated sedentariness, a type of low probability quantum transport, on several variants of the blow-up operation [ 32 ] . Apart from these two papers, we are unaware of results in the literature that relate quantum state transfer and the blow-up operation. This is our first and main motivation for studying quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs. For brevity, if H H is an empty graph on n n vertices, then we write G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] as ⊎ 𝑛 ​ G \overset{n}{\uplus}~G . Our second motivation is to explore a graph operation that induces strong cospectrality between many pairs of vertices. Strong cospectrality is a desirable property because it is a requirement for PST and PGST to occur. It turns out that if 0 0 is not in the eigenvalue support of vertex u u in G G , then the two copies of u u are strongly cospectral in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G (see Theorem 2 (2)). Hence, every vertex in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G pair up to exhibit strong cospectrality whenever G G has an invertible adjacency matrix (see Corollary 1 ). Therefore, while the blow-up of a graph is less sparse than the original, it has the advantage of producing pairs of vertices that are promising for high probability quantum transmission. As far as we know, no other graph operation induces this property in the resulting graph, which makes blow-up graphs desirable for quantum state transfer. The last motivation for considering the blow-up operation is that the two copies of every vertex in G G are twins in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G . This allows us to utilize some of the recent results about quantum state transfer between twins [ 28 , 30 , 31 , 40 ] . In particular, we give characterizations of strong cospectrality, PST, PGST and periodicity in blow-up graphs (see Theorems 2 , 3 , 4 , and 6 respectively). In particular, we have characterized PST and PGST in blow-ups of complete graphs, cycles, paths, stars, double stars, subdivided stars and cones. Apart from the work of Ge et al. [ 21 ] and Monterde [ 32 ] , we are unaware of any other work about quantum state transfer on blow-ups of graphs. Hence, we have some questions. One line of investigation would be to determine the quantum state transfer properties of blow-ups of other classes of graphs. In particular, we ask, which Cayley graphs exhibit PST and PGST in their blow-ups? The same question can be asked for trees in general. We would also like to know whether Conjecture 1 is true. If it turns out to be false, then we further ask: for which integers t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 and odd primes r r does ⊎ 2 ​ P 2 t ​ r − 1 \overset{2}{\uplus}~P_{2^{t}r-1} exhibit PGST between ( 0 , u ) (0,u) and ( 1 , u ) (1,u) , whenever u u is a multiple of 2 t − 1 2^{t-1} ? Lastly, we ask, if G G is a cone on a non-regular graph, then when does a blow-up of G G exhibit periodicity, PST and PGST? Note that the blow-up of G G in this case is a disconnected double cone on a non-regular graph. Thus, to answer this question, one needs identify the eigenvalues in the support of the apex of G G .
