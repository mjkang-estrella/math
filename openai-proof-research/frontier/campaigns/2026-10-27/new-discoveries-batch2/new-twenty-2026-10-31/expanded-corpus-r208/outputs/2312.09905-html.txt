About the existence of oriented paths with three blocks Batoul Tarhini Abstract A path P ⁡ ( k 1 , k 2 , k 3 ) P(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}) is an oriented path consisting of k 1 k_{1} forward arcs, followed by k 2 k_{2} backward arcs, and then by k 3 k_{3} forward arcs. We prove the existence of any oriented path of length n − 1 n-1 with three blocks having the middle block of length one in any ( 2 ​ n − 3 ) (2n-3) -chromatic digraph, which is an improvement of the latest bound reached in this case. Concerning the general case of paths with three blocks, we prove, after partitioning the problem into three cases according to the values of k 1 k_{1} , k 2 k_{2} and k 3 k_{3} , that the chromatic number of digraphs containing no P ⁡ ( k 1 , k 2 , k 3 ) P(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}) of length n − 1 n-1 is bounded above by 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) + k 3 2(n-1)+k_{3} , 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) + k 2 + k 3 − k 1 2(n-1)+k_{2}+k_{3}-k_{1} and 2 ​ ( n + k 2 − 1 ) − k 1 2(n+k_{2}-1)-k_{1} in the three cases respectively. Digraphs contained in every n n -chromatic digraph are called n n -universal. In 2015, El Sahili [ 7 ] proposed the following conjecture: Conjecture 1 For n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 , every oriented path of order n n is n n -universal. In 1980, Burr [ 4 ] conjectured the following: Conjecture 2 Every oriented tree of order n n is ( 2 ​ n − 2 ) (2n-2) -universal. This is a generalization of Sumner’s conjecture which states that every oriented tree of order n n is contained in every tournament of order 2 ​ n − 2 2n-2 . Our interests are directed towards studying the universality of oriented paths which are, clearly, special types of oriented trees. Before showing our result, we will state main previous results in this field. Regarding oriented paths in general, Burr [ 4 ] showed that any oriented path of length n − 1 n-1 is ( n − 1 ) 2 (n-1)^{2} -universal. In the case of tournaments, Havet and Thomasse [ 9 ] showed that except for three particular cases, any tournament of order n n contains any oriented path of length n − 1 n-1 . Gallai [ 8 ] and Roy [ 11 ] proved that any directed path of length n − 1 n-1 is n n -universal. El-Sahili [ 5 ] conjectured that every path of order n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 with two blocks is n n -universal. L. Addario-Berry et al. [ 1 ] used strongly connected digraphs and a theorem of Bondy [ 3 ] to prove the correctness of El Sahili’s conjecture. The case of three blocks is then our concern. M. Mourtada et al. proved in [ 10 ] that the path P ⁡ ( n − 3 , 1 , 1 ) P(n-3,1,1) is ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -universal. Tarhini et al. proved the existence of any P ⁡ ( k , 1 , l ) P(k,1,l) of length n − 1 n-1 in any ( 2 ​ n + 2 ) (2n+2) -chromatic digraph and that if D D is an n n -chromatic digraph containing a Hamiltonian directed path, then it contains any P ⁡ ( k , 1 , l ) P(k,1,l) of length n − 1 n-1 . In this paper, we improve the bound reached in [ 12 ] , so that we prove the following: Let D D be a ( 2 ​ n − 3 ) (2n-3) -chromatic digraph; then D D contains any path P ⁡ ( k , 1 , l ) P(k,1,l) of length n − 1 n-1 , with n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 . This confirms that the path P ⁡ ( k , 1 , l ) P(k,1,l) satisfies Conjecture 2 . Concerning the general case of three blocks paths P ⁡ ( k , l , r ) P(k,l,r) , we partition the problem into three cases according to the values of k , k, l l and r r . We establish a linear bound for the chromatic number which is 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) + r 2(n-1)+r , 2 ​ ( n − 1 ) + l + r − k 2(n-1)+l+r-k and 2 ​ ( n + l − 1 ) − k 2(n+l-1)-k in the three cases of the problem respectively. The reached bound is at most 3 ​ n − 6 3n-6 for two cases of the problem and 4 ​ n − 13 4n-13 for the remaining case in its worst scenario, and so it is the first linear bound that hits 3 ​ n 3n and it is an improvement of the bound reached by El Joubbeh [ 6 ] . In the cases under study, the followed technique has served us in improving the latest previous bounds of the chromatic number. We also believe that it may be useful in dealing with different types of oriented paths. This technique is followed in the two theorems that we prove, but with some differences that have advantages when the second block is of length 1 1 (Theorem 2.1 ). Let P P and N N be the union of the paths P i P_{i} and their neighbors N i N_{i} respectively. The next step is partitioning the set of neighbors N N into subsets according to the localization of their neighbors on the the paths P i P_{i} . We suppose that the digraph is P ⁡ ( k , l , r ) P(k,l,r) -free and start assigning the colors to its vertices. In the coloring process, we have benefit that there are no edges between one path and another, and that we know the chromatic number bound of the remaining subdigraph since it contains no directed path of the specified length. When dealing with the subsets forming the set of neighbors, we use the concept of final outforest and inforest, and proceed the coloring of each subset according to its definition based on the localization of its neighbors.
