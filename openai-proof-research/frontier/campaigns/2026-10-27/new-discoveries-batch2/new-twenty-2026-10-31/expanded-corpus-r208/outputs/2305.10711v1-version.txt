Math., FU Berlin, Arnimallee 2, 14195 Berlin, Germany Email address: nikolasdvk@gmail.com Abstract. A decade ago two groups of authors, Karasev, Hubard & Aronov and Blagojević & Ziegler, have shown that the regular convex partitions of a Euclidean space into n n parts yield a solution to the generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao conjecture when n n is a prime power. This was obtained by parametrising the space of regular equipartitions of a given convex body with the classical configuration space. Now, we repeat the process of regular convex equipartitions many times, first partitioning the Euclidean space into n 1 n_{1} parts, then each part into n 2 n_{2} parts, and so on. In this way we obtain iterated convex equipartions of a given convex body into n = n 1 ⋯ n k n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k} parts. Such iterated partitions are parametrised by the (wreath) product of classical configuration spaces. We develop a new configuration space – test map scheme for solving the generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao conjecture using the Hausdorff metric on the space of iterated convex equipartions. The new scheme yields a solution to the conjecture if and only if all the n i n_{i} ’s are powers of the same prime. In particular, for the failure of the scheme outside prime power case we give three different proofs. 1. Introduction and statement of the main result The following intriguing question was asked by Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao in a Nandakumar’s blog post [ 20 ] from 2006. Conjecture 1.1 (Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao) . Every convex polygon P P in the plane can be partitioned into any prescribed number n n of convex pieces that have equal area and equal perimeter. Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao [ 21 ] gave an answer in case n = 2 n=2 using the intermediate value theorem and proposed a proof for the case n = 2 k ≥ 4 n=2^{k}\geq 4 . Bárány, Blagojević & Szűcs [ 2 , Thm. 1.1] gave the positive answer for the case n = 3 n=3 by setting an appropriate configuration space – test map (CS–TM) scheme which allowed efficient used of Fadell–Husseini ideal valued index theory. Soberón [ 25 , Thm. 1] solved a similar question, namely the Bárány’s conjecture of equally partitioning d d measures in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} into k k pieces, using Dold’s theorem [ 10 ] . Naturally, a higher dimensional extension of Conjecture 1.1 was soon after formulated in [ 17 , Thm. 1.3] , [ 7 , Thm. 1.3] . Conjecture 1.2 (Generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao) . Let K K be a d d -dimensional convex body in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , let μ \mu be an absolutely continuous probability measure on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , let n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 be any natural number, and let φ 1 , … , φ d − 1 \varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d-1} be any d − 1 d-1 continuous functions on the metric space of d d -dimensional convex bodies in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Then there exists a partition of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} into n n convex pieces P 1 , … , P n P_{1},\dots,P_{n} such that equalities μ ⁡ ( P 1 ∩ K ) = ⋯ = μ ⁡ ( P n ∩ K ) \mu(P_{1}\cap K)=\dots=\mu(P_{n}\cap K) and φ i ​ ( P 1 ∩ K ) = ⋯ = φ i ​ ( P n ∩ K ) \varphi_{i}(P_{1}\cap K)=\dots=\varphi_{i}(P_{n}\cap K) hold for every 1 ≤ i ≤ d − 1 1\leq i\leq d-1 . In the previous statement, absolute continuity of probability measure in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} is meant with respect to the Lebesgue measure on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , and the set of d d -dimensional convex bodies in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} is endowed with the Hausdorff metric. Karasev, Hubard & Aronov [ 17 ] proposed a solution for the generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao problem using a CS–TM scheme based on the generalised Voronoi diagrams. The scheme was used to show that Conjecture 1.2 follows from the non-existence of an 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -equivariant map F ⁡ ( ℝ d , n ) ⟶ S ⁡ ( W n ⊕ d − 1 ) , F(\mathbb{R}^{d},n)\longrightarrow S(W_{n}^{\oplus d-1}), (1) where the symmetric group 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} acts on F ⁡ ( ℝ d , n ) F(\mathbb{R}^{d},n) and W n = { x ∈ ℝ n : x 1 + ⋯ + x n = 0 } W_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1}+\dots+x_{n}=0\} by permuting the points and coordinates, respectively. Using a homological analogue of obstruction theory, they showed [ 17 , Thm. 1.10] non-existence of the map ( 1 ) when n n is a prime power, and so gave the positive answer to the conjecture in this case. Blagojević & Ziegler [ 7 , Thm. 1.2] showed the non-existence of a map ( 1 ) if and only if n n is a prime power using equivariant obstruction theory. In order to apply the method, they developed a Salvetti-type CW-model ℱ ⁡ ( d , n ) \mathscr{F}(d,n) of the configuration space F ⁡ ( ℝ d , n ) F(\mathbb{R}^{d},n) , which is moreover its equivariant deformation retract. Resistance of Conjecture 1.2 to the existing topological methods is manifested in the fact that the map ( 1 ) exists whenever n n is not a prime power. Blagojević, Lück & Ziegler [ 5 , Thm. 8.3] showed the non-existence of a map ( 1 ) for n n a prime using an analogue of Cohen’s vanishing theorem [ 9 , Thm. 8.2] . In this paper we develop a cohomological method for identifying new classes of solutions to the generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao conjecture. The method stems from a geometric idea of iterated generalised Voronoi diagrams. Namely, let n = n 1 ⋯ n k n=n_{1}\cdots n_{k} be a multiplicative decomposition of the total number of parts in which we want to partition the convex body. In the first iteration the convex body is partitioned into n 1 n_{1} convex pieces of equal area, and inductively, for each 2 ≤ i ≤ k 2\leq i\leq k , in the i i th iteration one further divides each of the n 1 ⋯ n i − 1 n_{1}\cdots n_{i-1} pieces into n i n_{i} convex pieces of equal area. We call such partitions iterated of level k k and type ( n 1 , … , n k ) (n_{1},\dots,n_{k}) . Our method is developed in three steps. We begin by fixing a d d -dimensional convex body K K in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} In the first step, presented in Section 2 , we define a space C k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) C_{k}({d};{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) , called the wreath product of configuration spaces , which is used to parametrise iterated partitions of level k k and type ( n 1 , … , n k ) (n_{1},\dots,n_{k}) of K K . Namely, we produce a map C k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) ⟶ EMP μ ⁡ ( K , n ) , C_{k}({d};{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}})~\longrightarrow~\EMP_{\mu}(K,n), (2) where the codomain is the (metric) space of equal mass partitions of K K into n n pieces. Moreover, our setting has an appropriate symmetry of the iterated semi-direct product 𝒮 k ​ ( n 1 , … , n k ) \mathscr{S}_{k}({n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) of the symmetric groups, and the map ( 2 ) is equivariant with respect to it. Now, by setting an appropriate CS–TM scheme and using the parametrisation map ( 2 ), we show that the existence of an iterated solution of level k k and type ( n 1 , … , n k ) (n_{1},\dots,n_{k}) to the general Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao problem follows from non-existence of an 𝒮 k ​ ( n 1 , … , n k ) \mathscr{S}_{k}({n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) -equivariant map of the form C k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) ⟶ S ⁡ ( W k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) ) . Here W k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) W_{k}({d};{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) denotes the relevant 𝒮 k ​ ( n 1 , … , n k ) \mathscr{S}_{k}({n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) -representation, and S ⁡ ( W k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) ) S(W_{k}({d};{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}})) is the unit sphere in it. In the second step, which is “outsourced” to Section 6 , we prove the result on continuity of partitions. Namely, the CS–TM scheme from [ 17 ] and [ 7 ] used the fact that for a d d -dimensional convex body K ⊆ ℝ d K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} there exists a continuous 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} -equivariant map F ⁡ ( ℝ d , n ) ⟶ EMP ⁡ ( K , n ) , x ⟼ ( K 1 ​ ( x ) , … , K n ​ ( x ) ) , F(\mathbb{R}^{d},n)~\longrightarrow~\EMP(K,n),\qquad x~\longmapsto~(K_{1}(x),\dots,K_{n}(x)), where ( K 1 ​ ( x ) , … , K n ​ ( x ) ) (K_{1}(x),\dots,K_{n}(x)) denotes the convex partition of K K into n n parts of equal measure obtained from the generalised Voronoi diagram with sites encoded by x ∈ F ⁡ ( ℝ d , n ) x\in F(\mathbb{R}^{d},n) . The existence of such a map follows from the theory of optimal transport. We generalise this fact and prove that the map 𝒦 d × F ⁡ ( ℝ d , n ) ⟶ ( 𝒦 d ) × n , ( K , x ) ⟼ ( K 1 ​ ( x ) , … , K n ​ ( x ) ) ∈ EMP ⁡ ( K , n ) \mathscr{K}^{d}\times F(\mathbb{R}^{d},n)~\longrightarrow~(\mathscr{K}^{d})^{\times n},\qquad(K,x)~\longmapsto~(K_{1}(x),\dots,K_{n}(x))\in\EMP(K,n) is continuous, where 𝒦 d \mathscr{K}^{d} denotes the space of all d d -dimensional convex bodies in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} endowed with the Hausdorff metric. Continuity of this map, stated in Theorem 6.6 , is a crucial result which justifies the continuity of the parametrisation map ( 2 ), and therefore verifies the validity of the CS–TM scheme from Section 2.3 . In the final and third step, contained in Section 3 , we prove the main topological result of the paper using equivariant obstruction theory. Theorem 1.3 . Let k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , and n 1 , … , n k ≥ 2 n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2 be integers. Then, an 𝒮 k ​ ( n 1 , … , n k ) \mathscr{S}_{k}({n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) -equivariant map of the form C k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) ⟶ S ⁡ ( W k ​ ( d , n 1 , … , n k ) ) C_{k}({d};{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}})\longrightarrow S(W_{k}({d};{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}})) (3) does not exist if and only if n 1 , … , n k n_{1},\dots,n_{k} are powers of the same prime number. Therefore, this result gives a complete answer to the question of effectiveness of the introduced CS–TM scheme in the following sense: – If all the numbers n 1 , … , n k n_{1},\dots,n_{k} are powers of the same prime number, then an 𝒮 k ​ ( n 1 , … , n k ) \mathscr{S}_{k}({n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) -equivariant map of the form ( 3 ) does not exist. Consequently, the CS–TM scheme guaranties existence of an iterated solution of Conjecture 1.2 . – If n 1 , … , n k n_{1},\dots,n_{k} are not all powers of the same prime number, then there exists an 𝒮 k ​ ( n 1 , … , n k ) \mathscr{S}_{k}({n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}) -equivariant map of the form ( 3 ), hence the CS–TM scheme is not able to give any insights into the existence of a solution to Conjecture 1.2 . In addition, using alternative arguments, we give two more proofs of the existence of the equivariant map in Theorem 1.3 . More precisely, in Section 4 we prove the existence of an equivariant map ( 3 ) using the structural map of the little cubes operad, and in Section 5 by appropriately adapting the trick of Özaydin in [ 22 ] . A direct consequence of Theorem 1.3 is a new positive solution of the generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao conjecture. Corollary 1.4 (Iterated solution to generalised Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao) . Let K K be a d d -dimensional convex body in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , let μ \mu be an absolutely continuous probability measure on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , let n 1 , … , n k ≥ 2 n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2 be integers, and let φ 1 , … , φ d − 1 \varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d-1} be any d − 1 d-1 continuous functions on the metric space of d d -dimensional convex bodies in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . If n 1 , … , n k n_{1},\dots,n_{k} are all powers of the same prime number, then there exists an iterated partition of K K of level k k and type ( n 1 , … , n k ) (n_{1},\dots,n_{k}) into n := n 1 ⋯ n k n:=n_{1}\cdots n_{k} convex pieces K 1 , … , K n K_{1},\dots,K_{n} such that equalities μ ⁡ ( K 1 ) = ⋯ = μ ⁡ ( K n ) \mu(K_{1})=\dots=\mu(K_{n}) and φ i ​ ( K 1 ) = ⋯ = φ i ​ ( K n ) \varphi_{i}(K_{1})=\dots=\varphi_{i}(K_{n}) hold for every 1 ≤ i ≤ d − 1 1\leq i\leq d-1 . Moreover, the proof method, as noted by Karasev [ 16 , Thm. 1.6] , implies also the following result, Corollary 1.5 . Let K K be a d d -dimensional convex body in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , let μ \mu be an absolutely continuous probability measure on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , let n 1 , … , n k ≥ 2 n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 2 be integers, and let φ 1 , … , φ d − 1 \varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d-1} be any d − 1 d-1 additive, continuous functions on the metric space of d d -dimensional convex bodies in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Then there exists an iterated partition of K K of level k k and type ( n 1 , … , n k ) (n_{1},\dots,n_{k}) into n := n 1 ⋯ n k n:=n_{1}\cdots n_{k} convex pieces K 1 , … , K n K_{1},\dots,K_{n} such that equalities μ ⁡ ( K 1 ) = ⋯ = μ ⁡ ( K n ) \mu(K_{1})=\dots=\mu(K_{n}) and φ i ​ ( K 1 ) = ⋯ = φ i ​ ( K n ) \varphi_{i}(K_{1})=\dots=\varphi_{i}(K_{n}) hold for every 1 ≤ i ≤ d − 1 1\leq i\leq d-1 . The idea of iterated partitions seems to have appeared first in context of the Gromov’s waist of the sphere theorem [ 13 ] , [ 19 ] . There, Gromov [ 13 , Thm. 4.4.A] considered partitions of the sphere into 2 i 2^{i} pieces, which were parametrised by the wreath products of spheres. In order to obtain a slightly different waist of the sphere result, Palić [ 23 , Thm. 5.2.5] considered partitions of the sphere into p i p^{i} pieces indexed by the i i th wreath product of the configuration spaces on p p points. Iterated partitions of Euclidean spaces appeared in the work of Blagojević & Soberón [ 6 , Sec. 2] , where they were parametrised by the i i th join of the configuration space. For the purposes of this paper we use a more general version of the wreath product of configuration spaces, in the sense that the number of points of the configuration space in different levels do not need to coincide. 2. Known topological methods are able to give solutions to the Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao conjecture in the form of regular partitions. Since the space EMP μ ⁡ ( K , n ) \EMP_{\mu}(K,n) of convex equipartitions of K K is larger than the set of regular partitions of K K , a natural question arises: Are there solutions to the Nandakumar & Ramana-Rao conjecture which are not regular?
