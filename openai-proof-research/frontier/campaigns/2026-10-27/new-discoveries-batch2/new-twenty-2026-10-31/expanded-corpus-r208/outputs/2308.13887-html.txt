Our goal is to investigate quantum state transfer on a blow-up graph ⊎ 𝑛 ​ G \overset{n}{\uplus}~G relative to the adjacency matrix. We characterize strong cospectrality, periodicity, perfect state transfer (PST) and pretty good state transfer (PGST) in blow-up graphs. It turns out, if ⊎ 𝑛 ​ G \overset{n}{\uplus}~G admits PST or PGST, then one must have n = 2 . n=2. In particular, if G G has an invertible adjacency matrix, then each vertex in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G pairs up with a unique vertex to exhibit strong cospectrality. We then apply our results to determine infinite families of graphs whose blow-ups admit PST and PGST. Keywords: perfect state transfer, pretty good state transfer, graph spectra, blow-up, twin vertices, adjacency matrix. MSC: 05C50, 05C76, 15A16, 15A18, 81P45. 1 Introduction A quantum spin network is modelled by an undirected graph G G , where the vertices and edges of G G represent the qubits and their interactions in the network. A continuous-time quantum walk on G G is described by the unitary matrix U ⁡ ( t ) = exp ⁡ ( i ​ t ​ A ) U(t)=\exp{\left(itA\right)} where t ∈ ℝ t\in{\mathbbm{R}} , i = − 1 i=\sqrt{-1} , and A A is the adjacency matrix of G G . Here, | U ​ ( t ) u , v | 2 |U(t)_{u,v}|^{2} represents the probability that the quantum state assigned at vertex u u is found in vertex v v at time t t . We say perfect state transfer (PST) between u u and v v in G G at time τ > 0 \tau>0 if | U ​ ( τ ) u , v | = 1 |U\left(\tau\right)_{u,v}|=1 . In this case, the quantum state initially at vertex u u is transmitted to vertex v v at time τ \tau with probability one. If | U ​ ( τ ) u , u | = 1 |U\left(\tau\right)_{u,u}|=1 , then u u is said to be periodic in G G at time τ \tau . A graph is periodic if it is periodic at all vertices at the same time. PST is critical for short distance communication in a physical quantum computing scheme [ 1 ] . The study of PST was initiated by Bose [ 2 ] and Christandl et al. [ 3 ] . In [ 4 ] , we find that P 2 P_{2} and P 3 P_{3} are the only paths that admit PST. Following this negative result, several classes of graphs admitting PST have been studied, like cubelike graphs [ 5 , 6 ] , distance-regular graphs [ 7 ] , integral circulant graphs [ 8 , 9 ] , Hadamard diagonalizable graphs [ 1 , 10 ] , and quotient graphs [ 11 , 12 ] . In [ 4 ] , Christandl et al. used Cartesian products to construct graphs with PST over long distances. PST was also studied under joins [ 13 , 14 ] , non-complete extended p-sums [ 15 , 16 ] and other graph products [ 17 , 12 , 18 ] . Since PST in simple unweighted graphs is rare [ 19 ] , the notion of pretty good state transfer (PGST) was introduced [ 20 , 21 ] . A graph G G exhibits PGST between u u and v v if there is a sequence τ k ∈ ℝ \tau_{k}\in{\mathbbm{R}} such that lim k → ∞ | U ​ ( τ k ) u , v | = 1 \lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|U\left(\tau_{k}\right)_{u,v}|=1 , i.e., | U ​ ( t ) u , v | 2 |U(t)_{u,v}|^{2} can be made arbitrarily close to one through appropriate choices of t t . If lim k → ∞ | U ​ ( τ k ) u , u | = 1 \lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|U\left(\tau_{k}\right)_{u,u}|=1 , then G G is said to be almost periodic at u u with respect to the sequence τ k \tau_{k} , and G G is said to be almost periodic if there is a sequence τ k ∈ ℝ \tau_{k}\in{\mathbbm{R}} such that lim k → ∞ U ⁡ ( τ k ) = γ ​ I , for some ​ γ ∈ ℂ , \lim\limits_{k\to\infty}U\left(\tau_{k}\right)=\gamma I,~\text{for some }\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}}, where I I is the identity matrix of appropriate order. Infinite families of paths [ 22 , 23 , 24 ] , double stars [ 25 ] and circulant graphs are known to exhibit PGST [ 26 , 27 , 28 ] . Our goal in this paper is to systematically study quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs. Here, we consider the more familiar type of blow-up where every vertex of a graph is replaced by an independent set. This variant of the blow-up operation is a special case of the lexicographic product. The lexicographic product G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] of G G and H H is the graph G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] obtained by replacing every vertex of G G by a copy of H H , and adding all possible edges between the vertices in the copies of H H corresponding to adjacent vertices in G G . In [ 12 ] , Ge at al. provided sufficient conditions for PST to occur in lexicographic products. Their results about PST in G ⁡ [ H ] G[H] require that H H exhibits PST. But in our case, H H is an empty graph which cannot exhibit PST, and so their results are not applicable. This is our main motivation for studying quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs. Another motivation is to explore a graph operation that induces strong cospectrality between many pairs of vertices. Strong cospectrality is an important property because it is a requirement PGST. It turns out that if 0 0 is not in the eigenvalue support of vertex u u in G G , then the two copies of u u are strongly cospectral in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G (see Theorem 2 (2)). Hence, each vertex in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G pair up to exhibit strong cospectrality whenever G G has an invertible adjacency matrix (see Corollary 1 ). Thus, blow-ups have the advantage of producing pairs of vertices that are promising for high probability state transfer. Lastly, the two copies of a vertex in G G are twins in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G . This allows us to utilize some of the recent results on quantum state transfer between twins [ 29 , 30 , 31 , 32 ] . In particular, we give characterizations of strong cospectrality, PST, PGST and periodicity in blow-up graphs (see Theorems 2 - 5 ). We then characterize PST and PGST in blow-ups of special classes of graphs (see Sections 7 - 10 ).
