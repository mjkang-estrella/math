Akademika Koptyuga 4 4 , Novosibirsk, Russia 630090 630090 Abstract We define a triangle design as a partition of the set of 2 2 -dimensional subspaces of an n n -dimensional vector space into triangles, where a triangle consists of three subspaces with the trivial, 0 0 -dimensional, intersection and 1 1 -dimensional mutual intersections. A triangle design is balanced if all nonzero vectors are involved in the same number of triangles. Over the binary field GF ( 2 ) (2) , we construct balanced triangle designs for all admissible n n (congruent to 1 1 modulo 6 6 ) and an infinite class of balanced block-divisible triangle designs. We also prove that the existence of a triangle design over GF ( 2 ) (2) invariant under the action of the Singer cycle group is equivalent to the existence of a partition of Z 2 n − 1 \ { 0 } Z_{2^{n}-1}\backslash\{0\} into special 18 18 -subsets and find such designs for n = 7 n=7 , 13 13 , 19 19 . Keywords: Subspace design, graph decomposition, triangle design. 1 Introduction Subspace, or q q -ary, designs are subspace analogs of classical combinatorial designs, where the role of sets and subsets of cardinality i i is played by spaces and i i -dimensional subspaces. For example, balanced incomplete block design and its subspace analog with parameters t t - ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda) are defined as a set S S of cardinality v v (subspace of dimension v v over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} ) and a collection of k k -subsets of S S (respectively, k k -dimensional subspaces), called blocks, such that every t t -subset (respectively, t t -dimensional subspace) is included in exactly λ \lambda blocks. Of special interest are designs with λ = 1 \lambda=1 , called Steiner systems [ 8 ] . The only known nontrivial parameters of subspace analogs of Steiner systems with t > 1 t>1 are 2 2 - ( 13 , 3 , 1 ) (13,3,1) , q = 2 q=2 [ 1 ] . Steiner systems with t = 2 t=2 can be considered as decompositions of the complete graph K v K_{v} on v v vertices into complete subgraphs on k k vertices such that every edge of K v K_{v} belongs to exactly one subgraph from the decomposition. Generalizing Steiner systems, decompositions of K v K_{v} into isomorphic copies of some other subgraph were considered, see the survey [ 4 ] . A 2 2 - ( v , K , λ ) (v,K,\lambda) design over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} called graph decomposition is a collection of graphs (blocks) isomorphic to K K with the following properties: the vertex set of every block is a subspace of PG ( v , q ) (v,q) ; every two distinct points of PG ( v , q ) (v,q) are adjacent in exactly λ \lambda blocks, where PG ( v , q ) (v,q) is the ( v − 1 ) (v-1) -dimensional projective space over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} and K K is a simple graph of order q k − 1 q − 1 \frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1} for some k k , see [ 6 ] . The definition of graph decomposition over finite field covers, in particular, the well-known concept of a 2 2 - ( v , k , λ ) (v,k,\lambda) design over finite field corresponding to the case that K K is complete. In this paper, we consider similar configurations in the subspace case, decomposing the set of all 2 2 -dimensional subspaces of the given space into one of the simplest substructures from three two-dimensional subspaces, called triangles. We consider the case q = 2 q=2 , and for this value of q q , our triangle designs are also a special case of triangle decompositions of Steiner triple systems (STS). Such decompositions were considered in [ 10 ] , [ 13 ] , [ 14 ] ; the results include the existence of triangle-decomposable STS ( v ) (v) s for each admissible v v [ 14 ] , the existence of an exponential number of triangle-decomposable STS ( v ) (v) s for an infinite number of values of v v [ 10 ] , and some recursive constructions that guarantee, in particular, the existence of triangle decomposition of 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} for all n ≡ 0 , 1 mod 6 n\equiv 0,1\bmod 6 [ 13 , Corollary 7.4.1] (and even for all other n n if we allow one or two lines to be uncovered by triangles). We aim to construct balanced triangle decompositions, where balanced means that all nonzero vectors occur in the same number of triangles from the decomposition. Our main results are the following. We construct balanced triangle designs over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} of all admissible parameters n ≡ 1 mod 6 n\equiv 1\bmod 6 (Theorem 7 ). We construct, for every n ≡ 0 mod 6 n\equiv 0\bmod 6 , a balanced block-divisible triangle design in 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} with 6 6 -subspaces in the role of blocks (balanced designs and block-divisible designs are defined in a standard design-theory manner, see the next section for formal definitions). We find symmetric balanced triangle designs in 𝔽 2 7 \mathbb{F}_{2}^{7} , 𝔽 2 13 \mathbb{F}_{2}^{13} , and 𝔽 2 19 \mathbb{F}_{2}^{19} , and develop some theoretical tools, relating the existence of balanced triangle designs 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} invariant under the action of the Singer cycle group with the existence of partitions of ℤ 2 n − 1 \ { 0 } \mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}-1}\backslash\{0\} into 18 18 -subsets of form Γ ⁡ ( a ) ∪ Γ ⁡ ( b ) ∪ Γ ⁡ ( a + b ) \Gamma(a)\cup\Gamma(b)\cup\Gamma(a+b) , where Γ ⁡ ( a ) \Gamma(a) is the minimum set containing a a and closed under the operations x → − x x\to-x and x → z ⁡ ( x ) x\to\mathrm{z}(x) , Zech’s logarithm. While the concrete existence results of this section (Theorems 1 , 3 , 5 ) are computational, Sections 3.2 and 3.4 include also important theoretical conclusions that reduce the existence of triangle designs invariant under certain automorphism groups to some numerical configurations, which are conjectured to exist for all parameters satisfying the straightforward divisibility conditions. Finding a triangle design is obviously a special case of the exact covering problem: we need to find a partition of the given set (in our case, the set of all 2 2 -subspaces of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} ) into subsets from the given family (in our case, the family of all triangles). For small parameters, this problem can be solved with existing software, e.g., [ 11 ] . However, the size of the exact covering problem grows rapidly with increasing the parameters of the original task, and a standard approach is to reduce the task with prescribing some symmetry to a covering to find. If we imply that the covering is invariant under the action of some prescribed automorphism group, then the original task can be reduced to the problem of covering the orbits of elements by the orbits of subsets. This approach is well described in the famous paper of Kramer and Mesner [ 12 ] , and there is no need to explain it here. We only note that the automorphisms we prescribe, the Singer cycle and the Frobenius automorphism, are the most popular in searching different kinds of designs over subspaces of a finite vector space, see, e.g., [ 1 ] , [ 2 ] , [ 3 ] .
