Abstract We consider in general two-block substitutions and their fixed points. We prove that some of them have a simple structure: their fixed points are morphic sequences. Others are intrinsically more complex, such as the Kolakoski sequence. We prove this for the Thue-Morse sequence in base 3/2. 1 Introduction Let A = { 0 , 1 } A=\{0,1\} , A ∗ A^{*} the monoid of all words over A A , and let T ∗ T^{*} be the submonoid of 0-1-words of even length. A two-block substitution κ \kappa is a map κ : { 00 , 01 , 10 , 11 } → A ∗ . \kappa:\>\{00,01,10,11\}\rightarrow A^{*}. A two-block substitution κ \kappa acts on T ∗ T^{*} by defining for w 1 ​ w 2 ​ … ​ w 2 ​ m − 1 ​ w 2 ​ m ∈ T ∗ w_{1}w_{2}\dots w_{2m-1}w_{2m}\in T^{*} κ ⁡ ( w 1 ​ w 2 ​ … ​ w 2 ​ m − 1 ​ w 2 ​ m ) = κ ⁡ ( w 1 ​ w 2 ) ​ … ​ κ ​ ( w 2 ​ m − 1 ​ w 2 ​ m ) . \kappa(w_{1}w_{2}\dots w_{2m-1}w_{2m})=\kappa(w_{1}w_{2})\dots\kappa(w_{2m-1}w_{2m}). In the case that κ ⁡ ( T ∗ ) ⊆ T ∗ \kappa(T^{*})\subseteq T^{*} , we call κ \kappa 2 2 -block stable . This property entails that the iterates κ n \kappa^{n} are all well-defined for n = 1 , 2 , … n=1,2,\dots . The most interesting example of a two-block substitution that is not 2-block stable is the Oldenburger-Kolakoski two-block substitution κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} given by κ K ​ ( 00 ) = 10 , κ K ​ ( 01 ) = 100 , κ K ​ ( 10 ) = 110 , κ K ​ ( 11 ) = 1100 . \kappa_{\rm\sc K}(00)=10,\quad\kappa_{\rm\sc K}(01)=100,\quad\kappa_{\rm\sc K}(10)=110,\quad\kappa_{\rm\sc K}(11)=1100. The fact that κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} is not 2-block stable, and so its iterates κ K n \kappa_{\rm\sc K}^{n} are not defined, makes it very hard to establish properties of the fixed point x K = 110010 ​ … x_{\rm\sc K}=110010\dots (usually written as 221121…) of κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} , see, e.g., [ 4 ] . In Section 2 we show that even if a two-block substitution κ K \kappa_{\rm\sc K} is not 2-block stable, then still it can be well-behaved in the sense that its fixed points are pure morphic words. In Section 3 we prove that the Thue-Morse word in base 3/2 is not well-behaved: it can not be generated as a coding of a fixed point of a morphism. 2 Two-block substitutions with conjugated morphisms Let κ \kappa be a two-block substitution on T ∗ T^{*} , and let σ \sigma be a morphism on A ∗ A^{*} with σ ⁡ ( T ∗ ) ⊆ T ∗ \sigma(T^{*})\subseteq T^{*} . We call κ TM \kappa_{\rm\sc TM} the Thue-Morse two-block substitution . We now discuss the Kolakoski word x K x_{\rm\sc K} . This word was introduced by Kolakoski (years after Oldenburger [ 11 ] ) as a problem in [ 7 ] . The problem was to prove that x K x_{\rm\sc K} is not eventually periodic. Its solution in [ 8 ] is however incorrect (The claim that words w w with minimal period N N in w ​ w ​ w ​ … www\dots map to words with period N 1 N_{1} satisfying N < N 1 < 2 ​ N N<N_{1}<2N by replacing run lengths by the runs themselves is false. For example, if the period word is w = 21221 w=21221 , then w ​ w ww maps to the period word 2212211211211221, or its binary complement image.) A stronger result was proved by both Carpi [ 3 ] and Lepistö [ 9 ] : x K x_{\rm\sc K} does not contain any cubes. The fixed point x 3 / 2 x_{3/2} of κ TM \kappa_{\rm\sc TM} has more repetitiveness. It contains for example the fourth power 01010101. The Thue-Morse word is a purely morphic word, i.e., fixed point of a morphism. It is known that the Kolakoski word is not purely morphic ( [ 4 ] ). However it is still open whether the Kolakoski word is morphic, i.e., image under a coding (letter to letter map) of a fixed point of a morphism. The tool here is the subword complexity function ( p ⁡ ( N ) ) (p(N)) , which gives the number of words of length N N occurring in an infinite word. A well known result tells us that when the subword complexity function increases too fast, faster than N 2 N^{2} , then a word can not be morphic. There is one example of a two-block substitution which yields a word that is not morphic given by Lepistö in the paper [ 10 ] .
