## A short generic reduction of the exact universal claim

This is a review diagnostic of routine subsumption, not a repaired candidate proof or proposed additional counted result. It uses the standard 2×2 characteristic polynomial and basic irreducibility over a rational-function field.

1. Choose eleven algebraically independent positive reals x_1,…,x_11, let B be their set, and K=Q(x_1,…,x_11). Such positive choices exist: successively avoid the countable algebraic closure of the field generated by the previously chosen finite tuple. B has size 11 and is fully allowed by the source's positive-real domain.

2. From every four-element subset of B choose two entries {a,d} as the diagonal pair and use the remaining {b,c} as the off-diagonal pair. Use a fixed ordering inside both pairs to get one actual positive matrix [[a,b],[c,d]]. There are exactly

   6 binomial(11,4) = 1,980

   such choices. All these matrices also belong to (B∪{0})^{2×2}.

3. Their characteristic polynomials are f(T)=T²−(a+d)T+ad−bc. Each is irreducible over K. Indeed its discriminant is (a−d)²+4bc. Regard b as an indeterminate over the field generated by the other ten variables. Because a,d,b,c are four distinct independent variables and c≠0, that discriminant is a degree-one polynomial in b. Its simple zero has odd valuation, so it cannot be a square in the rational-function field K. In characteristic zero this proves irreducibility of the quadratic. Algebraic independence identifies the actual field K with this rational-function field, so specialization to the chosen real tuple introduces no lost relation.

4. Distinct choices give distinct monic polynomials: equality of traces a+d forces the same unordered diagonal pair by algebraic independence; equality of constants then forces the same unordered off-diagonal pair bc. If two of their positive spectral radii coincided, that common real algebraic element over K would have both distinct irreducible monic quadratics as minimal polynomial, impossible. Perron positivity, or the elementary quadratic formula, ensures that each selected matrix has a spectral radius which is a root of its f. Therefore |Ω_2(B∪{0})|≥1,980. This argument does not pretend that different formal radicals are automatically different.

5. Take A={1,2,4,…,2^11}, a positive alphabet of size twelve. For any 2×2 matrix over A, the spectral radius is determined by the unordered diagonal pair and the product of the two off-diagonal entries. There are binomial(13,2)=78 diagonal pairs with repetitions, and only 23 possible products 2^j, 0≤j≤22. Consequently

   r_12 ≤ |Ω_2(A)| ≤ 78·23 = 1,794 < 1,980 ≤ |Ω_2(B∪{0})|.

This contradicts both < and ≤ versions of the universal claim. No assumption about a geometric progression attaining the minimum is used; A supplies just an upper bound. No full generic-cardinality formula is required. All that matters is a quartic number of generically distinct irreducible quadratics versus a cubic upper bound for a geometric alphabet. The calculation does **not** prove the submitted numerical k=5 witness; it subsumes the exact source resolution, namely existence of a counterexample to the universal assertion. The campaign does not award a new credit for a smaller parameter witness without an independent significant endpoint such as a sharp minimality theorem, which this packet does not claim.
