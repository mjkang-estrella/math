A UNIFORM DISCREPANCY BOUND FOR ALTERNATING SHOE PAIRS
MathDB 324571 - implementation report, 8 November 2026 UTC

1. Statement and exact source experiment
For every discrete probability law p, the total variation distance between the conditional matching-pair color X and the first chronological left-right matching color Y satisfies D(p,p) <= 255/256. Consequently sup_p D(p,p) <= 255/256 < 1. This addresses the strict bound, not the optimal-constant conjecture.

The copied full MathDB literal asks sup_p D(p,p)<1 without a support-size restriction. Arratia and DeSalvo, On the Random Sampling of Pairs, with Pedestrian Examples, arXiv:1211.6486v2 (2013), Sections 2, 4, 9.1-9.2, Conjecture 4 equation (30), specifies the model. The final version, Advances in Applied Probability 47 (2015), 292-305, DOI 10.1239/aap/1427814592, Sections 9.1-9.2, Conjecture 4 equation (19), retains it. Their Theorem 3 gives a limiting-family formula, not a universal bound. Original and final copied bodies supply the actual definitions.

Colors are numbered by Nat in the source, with finite alphabets padded by zero. Independent colors arrive as L1,R1,L2,R2,... . A match requires one shoe of each side. If the first match occurs on a new arrival, its color is the new shoe's color; multiple matching partners cannot create a color tie. Simultaneous-pair and independently randomized-side rules are different experiments and are not used.

Put s=sum_i p_i^2. Then 0<s<=1, p_i<=sqrt(s), and x_i=P(X=i)=p_i^2/s. With iid infinite coordinates, no match through 2N arrivals implies all N aligned left-right pairs are unequal. Thus P(T>2N)<=(1-s)^N. This tends to zero, proving almost-sure termination. Arbitrary fallback colors on the null nontermination event do not change the law.

The formal model uses PMF, Measure.infinitePi, a prefix HasMatch predicate, Nat.find for the least matching prefix, and measurable winner pushforwards. ConditionalColorMeasure is the normalized restriction of the genuine iid space to equality of its first two coordinates, then projection to the first color. No desired law relation or bound is an assumption.

2. Ordinary proof of the uniform constant
Write y_i=P(Y=i). We prove y_i>=x_i/256 pointwise. If s>=1/64, L1=R1=i forces Y=i, so y_i>=p_i^2=s*x_i>=x_i/256. This includes point masses and the threshold equality.

If 0<s<1/64, set K=floor(1/(4sqrt(s))). Since the quantity floored exceeds 2, K>=1/(8sqrt(s)), K<=1/(4sqrt(s)), K^2>=1/(64s), and K^2*s<=1/16. A zero coordinate is immediate. For a positive coordinate, p_i<=sqrt(s)<1/8, so 1-p_i>7/8 and K*p_i<=1/4.

For a,b among the first K positions on each side, let E_ab specify that exactly L_a and R_b have color i within this block. Its probability is p_i^2*(1-p_i)^(2K-2). These K^2 events are disjoint. Let B_ab be the event of another cross-side match, avoiding the two designated positions. Conditional on E_ab, the remaining coordinates are independent with masses p_j/(1-p_i) for j != i. A competing pair has equality probability at most s/(1-p_i)^2. There are (K-1)^2 such pairs. The union bound therefore gives P(B_ab|E_ab)<=4/49<1/2.

The implementation bounds E_ab intersect each competing collision by the unrestricted four-coordinate event, of probability at most p_i^2*s after summation over colors. Thus P(F_ab)>=p_i^2/2-K^2*p_i^2*s>=7*p_i^2/16>=p_i^2/4. This is the actual ShoeCollision and ShoeMinorization route; the preceding conditional argument is a separate valid ordinary proof. No independence of competing-pair events is assumed.

On F_ab=E_ab minus B_ab, i is the only common color on the two sides. The later designated arrival is the chronological first match, in either arrival order. Thus F_ab implies Y=i. Bernoulli's inequality gives (1-p_i)^(2K-2)>=1-2K*p_i>=1/2. Summing disjoint F_ab gives y_i>=(K^2/4)*p_i^2>=p_i^2/(256s)=x_i/256.

The countable series sum_i |x_i-y_i| converges, being bounded termwise by x_i+y_i. Hence dTV(X,Y)=1-sum_i min(x_i,y_i)<=255/256. The subset-supremum convention agrees with half the l1 sum: the positive-difference subset attains it, and every subset is bounded by that value. The same constant for every p bounds the supremum strictly below 1.

3. Arbitrary countable colors and finite zero extension
Let A be any countable discrete measurable alphabet and p a PMF on A. Countability supplies an injective code e:A->Nat; no ordering of colors or positive-support assumption is imposed. Push p forward as p.map e. Coordinatewise relabeling pushes the actual infinite iid product measure to the Nat product measure. This follows from the product-measure mapping theorem and the PMF-to-measure mapping identity.

Injectivity preserves HasMatch at each prefix, hence FirstMatch, termination, and the least prefix length. On terminating paths the winner is mapped by e. With the fallback mapped as well, the explicitly defined colorWinner commutes pathwise with coding even on nonterminating paths. Its measurability is proved by countable winner-fiber decomposition. The resulting winner pushforward measure is exactly the Nat sourceWinner measure after coding.

For the conditional experiment, equality of the first two coordinates is preserved by injectivity. Its probability becomes the Nat collisionMass. Mapping the normalized restriction and first-coordinate projection gives exactly conditionalColorMeasure of p.map e. This proves transport of the actual conditional law rather than postulating a relation between arbitrary PMFs. Separate normalization certificates show the conditional and winner measures are probability measures and that their toPMF pushforwards are exactly sourceX and sourceY.

For any measures mu,nu on the discrete countable alphabet, the range of absolute subset-probability differences agrees with that of their injective pushforwards. A source subset is represented by its image; a Nat subset pulls back. This proves equality of the TV suprema. The source-defined colorDiscrepancy therefore equals sourceDiscrepancy(p.map e,e(c)). The existing Nat bound now proves countable_color_discrepancy_bound and countable_color_strict_universal_supremum; no duplicate elementary inequality proof is introduced.

Finite alphabets use Fin.val. The iid law, chronological winner, conditional law and discrepancy are preserved. Every Nat index outside the finite range has PMF mass exactly zero. Zero coordinates, finite support, singleton laws and countably infinite support are permitted. The empty alphabet admits no PMF or fallback; no probability law is excluded by requiring a fallback element. Generic almost-sure termination follows by pulling back the Nat null event, and fallback independence follows on this full-measure event.

Formal map: ShoeRelabel handles chronology; ShoeColorMeasure handles iid coding; ShoeColorWinner handles measurable winner transport; ShoeColorConditional handles normalized conditioning; ShoeColorEndpoint handles TV and the universal supremum; ShoeColorNormalization and ShoeColorPMF provide probability and PMF certificates. The original Nat core is unchanged.
