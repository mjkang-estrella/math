Three sustained routes and their exact stopping points
====================================================

375666: distinct products in finite abelian groups

Source Conjecture 1.1 of 2606.30506 fixes k strictly below the least prime dividing the group order, distinct a entries, and arbitrary b entries. The repeated-b allowance is essential: Arsovski's Snevily theorem for two sets is not this statement. The primary paper records the abelian p-group case and the stronger criterion that every other prime exceed k!, and extends a p-group theorem to nonabelian groups. None resolves all mixed-prime abelian groups.

First sweep: every cyclic C_n, 2<=n<=15, all k in 1..min(p-1,3), all a subsets and b multisets. Sorting b loses nothing because all its permutations are tried. The script tests 207553 instances.

Second sweep: full k=4 cases C35 and C55, after justified symmetry reductions. Translation of A shifts every output by the same group element. Translation of B does likewise. Multiplying both A and B by a unit is a bijection on outputs. Consequently every A is equivalent to one of the affine orbit representatives among four-subsets containing zero; after choosing it, B can be translated to contain zero. Taking every such B multiset is overinclusive, so no further simultaneous stabilizer reduction is needed. Counts: C35 has 76 affine A orbits and 7770 B multisets per orbit, 590520 tests; C55 has 174 orbits and 29260 B multisets per orbit, 5091240 tests. No failure. These are exactly relevant k<p mixed-prime instances; the other prime 7 or 11 is not >4!=24.

Proof mechanism attempted: a local swap of two assigned b entries repairs a collision unless it creates another collision. With k<p, a cyclic chain of collisions cannot close by a translation of order smaller than p. But the chain can branch and the forbidden differences depend on both a entries: no proof was obtained that every failed greedy assignment yields a short torsion relation. This is the actual missing lemma, not something supplied by the finite search. Passing to Sylow factors independently is also insufficient because the required permutations must agree across factors. A successful counterexample would admit a finite permutation certificate suitable for Lean, but no witness exists in these searches. No Lean theorem is claimed.

376257: exact tree-free clique count

Source 2607.23960 Conjecture 1.1 asks for the exact remainder term a*choose(k-1,r)+choose(b,r), with n=a(k-1)+b. Trees are subgraphs, not induced subgraphs. First sweep covers all unlabelled host graphs through n6, all trees 3<=k<=min(n+1,6), r3..k. Second sweep constructs every eight-vertex graph up to isomorphism, with duplicates: delete a vertex, identify the remaining graph with one of the 1044 atlas graphs of order7, and enumerate all 128 neighborhoods of the new vertex. This gives 133632 hosts. All 6, 11 and 23 tree shapes of orders6,7,8 and every r3..k-1 are considered. Hosts below the proposed bound need no embedding test. Every host above a bound is tested for a non-induced injective embedding of every relevant tree, using a rooted tree ordering and distinct host vertices. 200993 actual tree embedding tests; no counterexample.

Primary later paper 2608.25746 was read in its introduction. It proves large-r and leaf-rich ranges and expressly distinguishes the exact formula from the linear clique estimate. Even accepting the full Erdős–Sós edge theorem, the linear bound n/(k-1)*choose(k-1,r) leaves a rounding gap at every nonzero remainder b. For example k6,r3,n8 gives linear16 versus exact11. Thus replacing the target with a known average-degree consequence would be invalid.

Structural attempt: adding any edge from a K_(k-1) component to a new vertex creates every k-vertex tree. To see this, choose a leaf of the target tree, map that leaf to the outside endpoint, its neighbor to the clique endpoint, and the remaining vertices injectively to the clique. Hence a perturbation of the extremal disjoint-clique construction must also delete edges. This eliminates naive bridge additions as a counterexample route, but does not control simultaneous additions/deletions or dense non-clique components. Missing theorem: an exact, remainder-sensitive clique deficit for each non-clique T-free component, strong enough to sum over components. No such theorem was proved, and no surrogate linear inequality was formalized.

376305: weighted Erdős–Kleitman extremizers

Source 2607.25611 Conjecture 1.1 assumes n>=s>=2 and sum_i w_i<s, beyond the abbreviated MathDB description. These are part of the intended scope.

Reduction proved for diagnostics: the upward closure of a family with no s pairwise disjoint members still has no such matching. If disjoint supersets B_1,...,B_s had original subsets A_i in the family, the A_i would be pairwise disjoint too. First remove the empty-set issue, as the source explicitly does in its introduction: if the empty set is in the family, some singleton must be missing, since otherwise n>=s gives a forbidden matching of singletons. Replace the empty set by that missing singleton. Every matching using the new singleton can be mapped to a matching in the old family by replacing it with the empty set; other members are unchanged and distinct. Thus this replacement preserves cardinality and cannot increase matching number. All members are now nonempty. An upward-closed family is determined by an antichain of nonempty subsets. The script enumerates all such antichains for n<=5; n5 has 7580 (all 7581 monotone Boolean functions except the full family including the empty set).

Exact matching numbers are computed by subset packing. Rational weight vectors with denominator12, sorted entries in [0,1], are searched with strict total weight<s. Since the claim is existence of at least one weighted extremizer, finding one reaching the independently computed maximum suffices for each bounded case; it does not require every optimum to be a threshold family. For n5,s3 the exact optimum26 is witnessed by weights(5,7,7,7,7)/12 with total33/12<3. Other tested maxima are recorded in outputs/weighted-loop.json.

Proof attempt: coordinate compression can put an extremizer in an order ideal/upset representation but does not imply threshold representability. A separating hyperplane exists only if weighted inequalities for included and excluded subsets are jointly feasible; equivalently one must exclude balanced trade obstructions. No reason was proved that an extremal matching-free upset has no such obstruction, nor that replacing it by a threshold family preserves matching-freeness and cardinality. That threshold replacement statement is the missing full-scope lemma. The later paper's theorem for fixed m and large s is a parameter range, not a solution of the meta-conjecture.

All three routes have zero discovery credit. These notes document attempts, not a claim of full resolution or literature novelty.
