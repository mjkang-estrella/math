## 342739: arbitrary Eulerian covers

Read the covering definition, Theorems 1.1–1.11, the orientation/half-graph bijections and proofs in Section 2, and the complete slot-graph reduction and double-cover argument in Section 3 of arXiv:1905.06678v5. All five arXiv versions retain the target (v1 Conjecture 5.1 with an apparent e/epsilon typo; later Conjecture 6.1). The published EJC 27(3) P3.25 body retains it as Conjecture 16. No proof of the arbitrary-k result is in those target passages.

First route: iterate the proved two-cover bound. This proves the desired inequality for a tower of double covers. An arbitrary k-cover need not admit such a factorization, even for powers of two; the edge permutations need a compatible intermediate block system. No construction supplies that block system. A degree-three cover already falls outside this argument. This is a genuine scope gap, not a minor induction detail.

Second route: encode orientations as matchings. Put an edge-node for each e and d(v)/2 slots at v, each adjacent to incident edge-nodes. For a fixed Eulerian orientation, its incoming edge-nodes can be bijected to the slots in product_v (d(v)/2)! ways. Thus pm(G*) = epsilon(G) product_v (d(v)/2)!. A cover H induces a k-cover H* of G*; the factorial factor is raised to k. A general bound pm(H*) <= pm(G*)^k would therefore settle the exact target. The source already uses this construction for k=2 (Lemma 3.1, Lemma 3.3, Theorem 1.7). Merely extending its notation to k does not prove the missing matching inequality. The projected matching for k=2 is cycles/double edges, enabling its two-lifts-per-cycle argument. For k>2 it is a k-regular bipartite multigraph, and counting its decompositions into matchings versus compatible lifts is the unpaid step. No new inequality or injection is supplied here.

Third route: treat the local balanced-orientation indicator as an attractive graphical-model potential. This fails the direct log-supermodularity test: two distinct balanced subsets can have an intersection and union that are unbalanced. For degree two, the two singleton indicators are 1, but their meet and join indicators are 0. Thus the cited attractive-model cover inequalities cannot simply be applied. A nontrivial transformed potential would need a proof preserving the exact partition function. Park; no finite witness or uniform proof.

## 325667: necessity of (A4)

Read Theorem 1.2 (q even, q>2, q^2 affine C-points), the Bruck–Bose definitions, Theorem 3.2 statement and initial coordinate proof, the places where (A4) is used in Section 4, and Section 5 including Lemma 5.1 and its proof. The supplied v1 does not contain the literal sentence “Is (A4) necessary?”; the interpretation as redundancy under (A1)–(A3) must remain explicit. Theorem 3.2 already proves necessity for pointsets arising from the stated translation ovals. Repeating that direction would be a source theorem, not the redundancy claim.

First route: incidence counts. Any C-point lies on (q^2-1)/(q-1)=q+1 C-planes by (A1)–(A2); there are q(q+1) C-planes. Each has q^2-q affine non-C points, so their total external incidence count is q(q+1)(q^2-q)=q^4-q^2. This equals the number of external affine points. These counts are compatible with (A3), which forbids external overlaps. Distinct C-planes intersect affinely either in a C-point or not at all: two C-points would violate (A2), an external common point violates (A3). Also C has no three collinear points: the unique C-plane containing the first two would contain the third, contradicting its q-arc property. These observations recover strong incidence restrictions but do not determine intersections with planes outside the designated family.

Second route: closure of a triple. For three C-points outside one C-plane, (A4) demands that their ambient plane contain a fourth C-point (and no fifth). The affine incidence structure does not by itself identify its abstract parallelogram completion with a point in that ambient plane. The missing lemma is precisely that geometric compatibility, uniformly in even q; the source uses (A4) to produce a fourth point. Using that source lemma without reproving it independently of (A4) would be circular.

Third route: q=4. Even here an external plane containing exactly three C-points would violate (A4); the q-arc bound for designated planes says nothing directly about that plane. No explicit C and designated plane family meeting all three axioms is in hand. Any counterexample must certify all affine external points, every pair of C-points and all designated q-arcs, not just a triple. A generic finite-geometry solver encoding is not a qualified certificate plan. The source's regular-spread/regulus-reversal constructions preserve the original C and do not refute (A4). No new construction or all-recipe comparison exists. Park.

## 375789: cycle-extremal PED count

Read the PED definition (only outside edges have exactly one dominator), the complete valid-colouring correspondence in Section 2.2, path recurrence and star count proofs, statements and portions of tree transformations, the conclusion, Conjecture 5.1 and cycle recurrence proof (Theorem 5.2). Historical r279 treated the same source's different, unrestricted extremal classification (MathDB 375790); its partial routes cannot be counted again as a solution here.

First route: add edges to a tree and hope PED count increases. There is no containment map: a newly added unselected edge can have zero or two selected neighbours, invalidating a previous PED set. Conversely selecting it can invalidate other edges. Source tree extremality and cycle recurrence only settle trees, not all connected graphs.

Second route: branch on white vertices. If W is the set incident to no selected edge, then the selected edges must be exactly E(G-W): otherwise an unselected edge with two non-white ends would have at least two selected neighbours. For a graph without isolated vertices, validity is equivalent to W independent, G-W having no isolated vertices, and every neighbour of W having degree exactly one in G-W. This is the source colouring correspondence rewritten. It gives a precise combinatorial family but no upper bound by the circular word count. Counting all independent W is far too weak. Branching at a vertex needs boundary states remembering whether each neighbour has already acquired its sole non-white neighbour; the number of states and their dependence increase with degree. No domination of that state recurrence by c_n=c_(n-1)+c_(n-3) has been proved.

Third route: attach small cyclic blocks. A windmill of t>=2 triangles sharing one centre has exactly two PED sets: if the centre is black, all other vertices are non-white and the result is the whole edge set; if white, each outer pair is yellow and its edge is selected; if yellow, each triangle would require a non-white neighbour, contradicting its unique such neighbour. This does not beat the cycle. Triangle separation from the unrestricted source problem is not valid automatically within the connected class: the transformation may disconnect. Joining extremal disconnected blocks by bridges introduces boundary compatibility conditions, so their product count is not preserved. No bridge construction beating c_n is proved. Park without a witness; do not send Pi a generic graph search.
