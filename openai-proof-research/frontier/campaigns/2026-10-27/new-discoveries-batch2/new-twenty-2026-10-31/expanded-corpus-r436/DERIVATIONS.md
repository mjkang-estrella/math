## 342217 — arbitrary-color odd-distance circulants

Actual source: sources/color.pdf, arXiv:1903.09444v2, dated 28 May 2020, printed journal attribution SEMR17 (2020)590–603. Read definitions, Propositions1–3, Conjecture1, finite-cover discussion, Theorem1, Lemmas1–4 and their proof bodies. Material Figures4–6 on printed pages10–12 were rendered and visually inspected. This is not a final-publisher equivalence audit. Other figures not visually inspected; no claimed witness relies on them.

Let c:Z→{1,...,k} be perfect, with row M_a specifying the multiset of neighbor colors. Put E_a for the a-th unit row. Neighbor sets of i and i+2 differ by removal of i−2n+1 and addition of i+2n+1. Therefore
M_{c(i+2)}−M_{c(i)} = E_{c(i+2n+1)}−E_{c(i−2n+1)}. (A)
This is a vector identity for every i, every n≥1 and every color coordinate. The common neighbor count is 2n−1 (not a dependence on the source's typographical count). If c(i)=c(i+2), (A) forces equality of the two boundary colors. If the two parameter rows differ, that difference is exactly a difference of two unit vectors. No arithmetic experiments are needed.

First route: propagate this identity to get the classification. Knowing a sufficiently long consecutive block and M determines the next endpoint from its neighbor-count equation; it also determines the preceding endpoint. The finite alphabet and reversible block transition imply periodicity: on the finite set of blocks actually appearing, successor and predecessor are inverse, so every orbit is a cycle. This recovers the source's Proposition2; it does not bound the period by the three conjectured cover sizes or imply that c is path-perfect. Periodicity alone is therefore insufficient, even though every periodic coloring descends to some finite circulant.

Second route: try to collapse to the source's two-color theorem. The binary proof uses that every unequal color has the unique complementary color. Figures4–6 and Lemmas2–4 then propagate alternating chains. For k≥3 an unequal boundary color is not fixed by knowing just that it differs from another one. A map merging colors gives a perfect binary coloring only when each row's sum into each merged class is constant on every merged class. This extra quotient condition is not in the literal. Thus arbitrary binary merges are invalid, and a binary-period conclusion would not automatically lift to the original colors.

Third route: use all root-vector row differences in (A). Form the graph of colors that occur at distance2. On an edge the corresponding M rows coincide or differ by one transfer. This provides local constraints, but proving that they force either path-neighbor determinacy or a period dividing 4n−2,4n,4n+2 is unpaid. A common period equal to an arbitrary least common multiple would not suffice: the literal requires an actual coloring induced from one of the three covers or a path-perfect coloring. No finite witness was constructed. Exact unpaid lemma: every perfect k-coloring satisfying (A) is path-perfect or has one of those three global periods. This is the full structural bottleneck, not a completed reduction to a binary theorem.

## 343211 — profile-dependent skew cross-intersection

Actual source: sources/cross.pdf, arXiv:1907.06019v2 (header dated2020), Conjecture6.3 exact. Read Theorem4.5, Corollaries4.6,4.9,4.10 with proofs and the entire Section4.2 construction/inequality argument, relevant definitions and Section6 conjecture. Dependencies on generic-projection lemmas were identified, not independently reproved or completely audited. Search lead DOI10.1112/jlms.12484 is a specific final version not acquired; no final-equivalence or novelty clearance.

Route1 (reordering): distinct profiles have full cross-intersection, so all pairs can be reordered by profile while preserving the given order inside each profile block. If the set of profiles is totally ordered by (a,b)≼(a',b') iff a≤a' and b≥b', this reordering meets Corollary4.6 and gives the desired original weighted sum. All nonemptiness and diagonal disjointness assumptions remain intact. This is a restricted source corollary, not full scope and not new credit. Profiles (1,1) and (2,2) demonstrate why the order is not total: neither orientation meets both inequalities. They are genuinely feasible under the literal: ({1},{2}) and ({2,3},{1,4}) are diagonally disjoint and cross-intersect in both directions. Thus one cannot infer anti-monotone profiles from intersection assumptions.

Route2 (padding): order profiles by a and enlarge every B to b=max b_i using private new elements disjoint from all original A's. Intersections are preserved, diagonal disjointness preserved, and each B now has size b. Corollary4.6 yields sum_i 1/binom(a_i+b,a_i)≤1. However b≥b_i makes these weights LESS THAN OR EQUAL to the requested weights; the implication back is invalid. Taking separate profile blocks gives a separate ≤1 per block and no total ≤1. The exterior proof's normalized-density transport cannot simply be used for a step in which both a and b increase: the proof's four cases depend on b not increasing.

Route3 (permutation-event replacement): for each i let E_i be the event that every A_i element precedes every B_i element in a uniformly random ordering of the finite union. Its probability is exactly 1/binom(a_i+b_i,a_i), since the relative order of A_i∪B_i is uniform. Events from distinct profiles are disjoint: choose x∈A_i∩B_j and y∈A_j∩B_i, then E_i forces x before y and E_j forces y before x. Within one profile the skew hypothesis alone does NOT make events disjoint. Explicit allowed two-pair example: A_1={1,2},B_1={3,4},A_2={2,5},B_2={1,6}; A_1∩B_2={1}, A_2∩B_1=∅, and both E_1,E_2 hold in the order 2,5,1,3,4,6. Every size is2, every diagonal disjointness holds. This is a counterexample to the proposed event-disjointness argument, NOT to the weighted conjecture (the sum is1/3). Need replacement measurable events/weighted dimension blocks with the same per-pair weights whose different-profile supports are disjoint and whose internal multiplicities obey a single global bound. No such construction has been supplied.

Exact unpaid lemma: combine skew block capacity with the symmetric inter-profile constraints without losing original binomial weights for profiles increasing in both coordinates. No complete proof/counterexample or significance classification is asserted.

## 343592 — real-valued linear extension

Actual source: sources/poset.pdf arXiv:1908.08349v1, Dovgoshey, Conjecture4.24; read Theorem4.21 statement and proof, Corollary4.22, Problem4.23, Conjecture4.24. The ultrametric theorem itself assumes the real-embeddable extension and cannot supply it. Its other earlier dependencies were not audited. No claim about a later final/correction is cleared.

An extension as in (i) is equivalent to an injective isotone map f:Q→R+: pull back the real order for the converse. Necessity of the cardinal and chain conditions follows by restriction and injectivity. Sufficiency is unpaid.

A precise alternative criterion: such a map exists iff Q has a COUNTABLE family (D_j) of downward closed subsets separating every pair of distinct elements. Forward: for f injective isotone, use rational strict cuts D_q={x:f(x)<q}; a rational between f(x) and f(y) separates x,y. Reverse: define g(x)=sum_{j≥1}3^(−j)1_{x∉D_j}. The convergent nonnegative series lies in[0,1/2]. If x≤y then each digit for x is≤the corresponding digit for y, so g is isotone. For x≠y take the first differing digit; its magnitude3^(−j) exceeds the total subsequent possible difference (1/2)3^(−j), so g is injective. This proves the equivalence, with no computation. It does NOT prove that the chainwise hypothesis gives such a separating family.

Countable Q has the family of principal ideals {↓x:x∈Q}; these separate each distinct pair (if y is not≤x, ↓x separates, otherwise ↓y separates). Antichains of size≤continuum also have countable separating subsets via an injection into binary sequences, all subsets being downward closed. These subclasses do not resolve arbitrary uncountable Q. Merely taking an arbitrary linear extension does not control its real embeddability.

Attempted counterexample R² with coordinatewise order fails. Choose countably many downward rational coordinate cuts x<q or y<q. They separate all distinct pairs; the series construction gives an injective isotone map. Thus product-order dimension alone is no obstruction. A doubled real chain would fail the hypothesis itself and cannot refute the implication. A putative tree obstruction would need an actual poset of cardinal≤continuum, all chains real-embeddable, and a proof that every countable family of downsets misses a pair. No such tree or unconditional construction was established; no independence result is claimed.

Exact unpaid lemma: derive countable downset separation from the cardinal bound plus real-embeddability of every chain, or exhibit a rigorously verified failure of that implication. No full-scope ordinary result, no formal dispatch.
