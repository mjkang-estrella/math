## 359752: triangular-grid area with holes

Exact endpoint: for every connected triangular-grid polygon P with h holes, C(P) <= A(P)/6 + h + 1; A counts unit triangular cells, not Euclidean area without normalization. Zhu 2309.00100v3 Conjecture 4.7 is the matching source. Definition 2.2 describes *simply connected* generalized polygons and therefore does not already encompass holes. Its component count cannot be substituted for h+1.

Read source mechanisms: Lemma 2.3 and its proof use a horizontal-strip tree, relying on a path separating a disk. Proof 4.5 of Theorem 4.4 removes a billiards cycle and charges at least six removed triangles to each remaining component in the primitive case. Defant–Jiradilok 2202.06943v2 Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.2 give the gluing law, with an explicit interface-size error. These are actual proof passages, rather than an inference from the abstract. Zhu's Figure 21 caption reports five triangular cycles in a one-hole example. The figure itself is not visually audited here; no coordinates or witness counts are claimed from the drawing.

Route A: cut h slits to obtain a simply connected surface without adding triangles. If C(P) <= C(P_cut)+h held, the hole-free theorem would give the entire desired bound immediately. But cutting a path containing L panes changes L local trajectory connections. A single transposition changes a permutation's cycle count by one; the naive surgery bound is L rather than one. Nothing read proves that the L effects cancel to at most one for each slit. Replacing arbitrary slits by horizontal strips does not remove this issue because the strip graph has cycles around holes. This is a precise missing uniform slit lemma, not a proof by topology alone.

Route B: extend cycle removal directly. Suppose removal of a cycle c leaves k pieces Q_i, preserves all other cycles, removes a triangular cells, and leaves h_i holes in each piece. Induction gives
C(P) = 1 + sum C(Q_i) <= 1 + (A(P)-a)/6 + sum h_i + k.
Thus the desired induction closes provided
a >= 6*(k + sum h_i - h).
This exact inequality is the needed charge statement. If its right side is nonpositive it is automatic. The interesting case is separating many pieces while leaving holes intact. The disk proof only handles the h=0 setting with a strip tree. Need to define the surgery on an annular/multiply connected simplicial complex, prove cycle preservation, and charge topology-changing events without counting a removed triangle twice. These are not proved here.

Route C: fill the holes. The disk theorem gives an area term A(P)+A(holes), which is too large. To cancel it one would need a lower bound for the number of cycles gained on filling, tied to hole area. Interface gluing Lemma 3.1 has an eta term depending on the number of panes, not merely h; it does not supply this cancellation. The read theorem is therefore not a complete routine bridge for the exact hole claim.

Version coverage: v1, v2 and current v3 bodies fetched; selected definitions/conjectural endpoint/proof passages reviewed, not every proof in every version. Defant's author mentoring page identifies Adv. Appl. Math. 168 (2025); final journal body not located/read in this pass. A citing 2502.06013 body was fetched but not technically reviewed beyond its different toric-refraction context. Journal, correction and figure qualification remains HOLD. No Pi plan is justified.

## 360150: weighted oriented edge ideals

Exact endpoint is equivalence of componentwise linearity, linear quotients, and vertex splittability for every weighted oriented graph over the source's base field. Edge generators are x*y^(w(y)) for oriented edges x -> y. The source's recursive vertex splitting requires I=x I1+I2, I2 subset I1, disjoint minimal-generator union, and I1,I2 vertex splittable in the ring without x (Definition 2.9). The standard implications vertex splittable => linear quotients => componentwise linear are background implications; they do not give the converse.

Route A: force a splitting variable by co-chordality. The original source Theorem 3.8 gives a co-chordal underlying graph. Taking a vertex simplicial in the complement makes its ordinary neighbourhood a vertex cover. For a weight-one vertex v, group the generators as
I(D)=v*J + I(D-v),
J=(u^(w(u)):v->u) + (u:u->v).
If J is vertex splittable and every generator of I(D-v) is divisible by a generator of J, this is a valid splitting; the generator union is disjoint because one part contains v and the other does not. At most one non-linear pure-power generator in J suffices for J to be vertex splittable: successively split the linear generators, leaving a principal ideal.

The unpaid step is existence of such v in every componentwise linear D. Ordinary vertex-cover containment only gives divisibility by u, whereas J may contain u^w(u) instead. An edge u->z can then contribute u*z^w(z), which is not divisible by u^w(u) if w(u)>1. The complement-simplicial choice by itself is insufficient. The read chordal proof invokes split-graph structure and further orientation restrictions to repair exactly this sort of containment. Extending that proof requires a new weighted elimination lemma. Inherited componentwise linearity under deletion would allow induction once the splitting conditions are met; it does not establish them.

Route B: use polarization and Alexander duality. Componentwise linearity corresponds to sequential Cohen–Macaulayness of the dual, while vertex splitting corresponds to vertex decomposition. General sequential Cohen–Macaulay complexes need not be vertex decomposable, so a generic equivalence is invalid. One must prove the restricted polarized oriented-edge family admits a shedding variable, or construct a member of that family whose dual defeats it. An arbitrary non-shellable complex is not a valid witness: no representation by generators x*y^w(y) has been supplied. No CAS or finite enumeration was used.

Route C: compare later theorems rather than declare novelty from the 2023 record. 2509.14637v1 treats house-free/complete multipartite classes; selected introductory and theorem proof passages were read. 2609.39165v1 classifies linear support-two ideals, including an equigenerated star theorem (Theorem 4.7) and a general co-chordality implication. But each degree component I_<d> of a mixed-degree weighted edge ideal contains multiples with larger supports; it is not the same minimal support-two family. Applying that classification degree by degree is therefore not a complete bridge to Conjecture 4.10.

Important source discrepancy: the 2025 journal page 10.1007/s10801-025-01401-x says 'certain chordal graphs' in its abstract, while the v1 arXiv text says chordal graphs. The live journal HTML returned 200 (with a cookies_not_supported query in the redirect); accessible abstract/introduction/reference text was read, but no full journal proof audit or theorem-by-theorem comparison was completed. This difference prevents treating the v1 chordal theorem as unqualified current final scope. The cited companion also has inconsistent bibliography metadata for the journal paper; do not inherit that metadata as verified. The final journal proof and relevant printed orientation diagrams remain material unpaid comparators. HOLD, zero credit, no dormant formal execution plan.

## 360432: monotonicity of accurate caching-game triplets

Read 2310.13777v2 definitions, Conjectures 1.5/1.7, Lemma 2.2, Theorems 2.3/2.5/2.7 context and proofs, and explicit d=2 strategy in Theorem 3.5; v1 corresponding endpoint was checked. Read companion 1702.00635 Theorem 2, counting Lemma 3 proof and nearby large-n claims. Accuracy means both discrete and continuous values equal U(n,d,k)=k^d/binomial(n+d-1,d), on legitimate positive integer game parameters. Arbitrary negative 'integer triplets' are not a meaningful refutation.

Route A: ordinary value monotonicity. Gifting/removing a treasure gives v_M(n,d+1,k)<=v_M(n,d,k). If the original triplet is accurate, this only gives v_M(n,d-1,k)>=U(n,d,k). The desired value is larger by the factor (n+d-1)/(d*k). Thus gifting alone loses the exact normalization. Similarly adding boxes decreases U; a value-decreasing comparison gives the wrong direction to prove equality. More boxes, smaller k and fewer treasures must be treated by quantitative transfers, not qualitative monotonicity.

Route B: embed a smaller n game into larger n' by uniformly choosing n active boxes. For a hiding distribution occupying r boxes, probability all occupied boxes lie in the active set is binomial(n'-r,n-r)/binomial(n',n). This depends on r. It is not the uniform distribution over weak compositions that defines the counting upper bound, and multiplying by U(n,d,k) does not give one constant U(n',d,k) across all hiding configurations. An occupancy-dependent mixture/weighting theorem is missing.

Route C: thin each k-query to k' boxes. A preselected distinguished successful response survives uniform thinning with probability k'/k, suggesting the factor (k'/k)^d needed for the bound. But the physical hider chooses a revealed treasure *after seeing the pruned query*. In the simulation, the next full-strategy branch can depend on the pruning randomness. One cannot multiply a marginal survival factor by the original worst-case winning probability without a coupling valid for every adaptive revealer. The source explicitly shows revealer choices matter; substituting a random or cooperative revealer would solve a different game. Need a non-anticipating thinning/strategy-transfer theorem or a genuine in-scope counterexample. None supplied.

Route D: dual saturation. A uniform weak-composition hider bounds a deterministic search tree by at most k^d successful allocations. Equality forces saturation in the relevant support. This suggests an exact fractional-cover transfer on full adaptive trees. However tree-leaf counts alone bound an average and do not ensure a lower bound on every allocation against every revealer. Establishing such transfer is the full missing combinatorial content, not a certificate-generation instruction.

The d=2 and d=3 accuracy families in the source are already public and do not settle all d. No finite exceptional values were computed. Current World Scientific journal URL returned 403; it was not retried or replaced by another publisher route. Final journal proof/corrections remain unread. HOLD, zero credit, no Pi dispatch.

359985: the source calls EP a cardinality-minimal non-four-colourable maximal planar graph, not an arbitrary 'edge-to-edge plane graph'. By the classical Four Colour Theorem every finite simple planar graph is four-colourable, hence no such EP exists. Formally, for every G, (finite simple planar G and not four-colourable G and minimality) implies False; ex falso gives nonadjacency. This is a complete classical bridge on the intended domain, zero credit. If one instead reads the corpus as all ordinary plane graphs, the icosahedral graph has adjacent degree-five vertices, but that is a wording/domain correction, not a new solution to the intended question. No FCT proof audit or new formalization attempted.
