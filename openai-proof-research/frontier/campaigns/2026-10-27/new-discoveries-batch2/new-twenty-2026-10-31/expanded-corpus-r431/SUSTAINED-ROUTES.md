For a fixed finite positive subtraction set S, let g be its mex nim-sequence. Adding s preserves g exactly when g(n+s) differs from g(n) for all n>=0: necessity follows since a move cannot reach its own mex value; sufficiency is induction because adding an option unequal to an existing mex preserves that mex (all smaller nonnegative values remain represented). This full bridge is the content of the source Remark1; the induction explains it independently.

Route A, eventual parity: if g eventually alternates0,1, an admissible s must be odd, since every sufficiently large n collides for even s. Every original move is likewise odd. This controls parity, not the finite prefix. It does not prove nonexpandability. At the first heap s for a missing move s, the added option is g(0)=0: this forces a change only if g(s)=0. Missing odd s may have positive g(s), so the first-heap argument does not close.

Moving from M to an arbitrary smaller move in the proof is unjustified: the terminal witness g(n0-1-M)=1 is known for M specifically, and n+s may fall before n0. Neither an induction on M nor removal of a move preserves the ultimately bipartite hypothesis. The unpaid lemma is a prefix collision for every missing odd s<M.
