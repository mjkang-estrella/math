## 338686: two constructive routes and the exact cut deficit

The actual acquired Segal-Halevi v3 uses an interval cake, nonatomic measures and normalized nonnegative entitlements summing to one. Atoms are not admissible counterexamples. Its Theorems 1–4 and final conjecture were read. The actual Crew–Narayanan–Spirkl author manuscript gives 3n-4, not 2n-2; its full induction and concluding topological conjecture were read. No final-version equivalence is asserted.

First route: safely remove an agent with two cuts, then recurse. On the circle, it would suffice to find an agent i and an arc I satisfying mu_i(I)>=t_i and mu_j(I)<=t_i for every j!=i. Assign I to i. For j!=i the complement has value at least 1-t_i, so a proportional division there with normalized demands t_j/(1-t_i) gives j its original demand. Parametrize the complementary arc as an interval. The original seam can split a physical interval, but does not add interior cuts; two boundary cuts plus 2(n-1)-2 recursive cuts give 2n-2. Zero demands may first be deleted, and t_i=1 is trivial. This establishes the implication, NOT the safe-arc lemma.

Stromquist–Woodall gives simultaneous exact fractions using up to n-1 arcs, which costs up to 2n-2 before recursion and therefore does not supply that lemma. For each fixed i, the moving arc of mu_i-value t_i can be parametrized after a positive-density approximation. Its other n-1 values are continuous functions, but separate average bounds do not force a common point where they are all <=t_i. Their bad sets can cover the circle. Selecting a different i changes both the target fraction and these functions; no common covering contradiction has been proved. This remains unpaid even for three unequal demands. It would be incorrect to invoke the two-measure argument independently for each competitor.

Second route: recursively split agents into two nonempty groups P,Q and divide the circle into complementary arcs X,Xc, with mu_i(X)>=sum(P demands) for i in P and mu_i(Xc)>=sum(Q demands) for i in Q. Two cuts and induction give 2+2|P|-2+2|Q|-2=2n-2. The actual Crew et al. Conjecture 3.1 is the stronger equality version of precisely this separator. Their proved median construction allows one pivot agent to participate on BOTH sides. For N original agents the recursive populations a,b then satisfy a+b=N+1; counting the one dividing cut gives 1+(3a-4)+(3b-4)=3N-4. Replacing 3s-4 by hoped-for 2s-2 would give 2N-1, one cut too many. A proof must either remove the shared pivot or guarantee that one recursive cut coincides with the outer cut. Neither follows from equality of the pivot's total demand; its two allocated pieces may be separated by pieces for other agents. Arbitrary rational cloning does not control this deficit: it makes D-1 cuts depending on a denominator D rather than n.

One genuine reduction: a uniform 2n-2 proof for rational demands would extend to real demands for these nonatomic measures. Approximate the vector by rational simplex vectors. Write each allocation using exactly M=2n-2 ordered endpoints (allow repeated endpoints) and a word of M+1 owners. There are finitely many words; take a subsequence with constant word, then a convergent subsequence of endpoint vectors in the compact ordered cube. Each measure of an interval converges since nonatomic finite measures have continuous distribution functions. Thus each owner's finite union value converges, and the limiting inequalities imply the real demands. This does NOT justify limits of arbitrary many-piece clone divisions. Rational vectors with unbounded denominator remain the full unpaid case.

No full solution or refutation. Formal feasibility would require finite measures/nonatomic interval continuity, circle-arc reconstruction and compactness, plus the currently unproved separator; no dormant full-candidate plan is represented as complete.

## 363909: spectral and unbounded-family routes

Actual Lin–Zhao v4 defines the leaf boundary, harmonic extension and Rayleigh quotient, and Conjecture 3.6 with sufficiently large ell. Definition 3.5's step 3 prints D children while describing D-1 shares; the literal uses the consistent intended D-1 version. This typographical inconsistency receives zero credit. Observation 2.11 is a small-order phenomenon, not a refutation of an eventual conjecture.

Route A is a minimax proof by suppressing degree-two vertices and then balancing branch leaf counts. Suppression is valid: if a path of L edges has endpoint values a,b, its minimum energy is (a-b)^2/L, attained by linear interpolation. Contraction reduces L and raises this energy for every boundary vector; taking minimum over interior values preserves the order, and the variational characterization raises every Steklov eigenvalue. Leaf number and the degrees of surviving vertices are unchanged. Thus it is enough to consider trees with all internal degrees at least three. This does NOT order two different branching trees. Moving a hanging branch changes the harmonic extension as well as the boundary zero-sum eigenvector. The difference of the two minimized quadratic forms need not be inferred from just branch cardinalities. The missing lemma is a boundary-form comparison (or direct lambda2 comparison) for every admissible balancing move, with equality/degeneracy controlled. A diameter upper bound alone does not attain the candidate's eigenvalue.

Actual companion 2412.12787v2 Theorem 1.1 fixes leaf count AND total vertex count, with no specified maximum-degree constraint. Its optimizers can have high-degree centers. There is no conversion from that admissible class to the fixed-degree class preserving the bound or equality. Accordingly its statement was compared but its full proof was not imported as an exclusion.

Route B tries to propagate a small strict spectral comparison to arbitrarily many leaves. Here is an exact symbolic mechanism, not a numerical test. In a degree-three tree, attach to every old leaf a full binary tree of height h, with m=2^h new leaves. The resistance from its root to its equipotential leaves is r=sum_{j=1}^h 2^{-j}=1-1/m (parallel reduction level by level). Let S be the old Dirichlet-to-Neumann matrix, a the old leaf values, and x the new common values in each cluster. Harmonic balance gives Sa=(x-a)/r, so x=(I+rS)a and the total flux into each cluster is Sa. The flux at each of its m symmetric leaves is Sa/m. Therefore each old eigenvalue lambda contributes the eigenvalue lambda/[m+(m-1)lambda] on the cluster-constant subspace. The formula is strictly increasing in lambda>=0. It preserves strict comparisons between two old trees in this subspace. The attached pieces' transverse modes must additionally be bounded below before claiming this is the new lambda2; no transfer conclusion is being taken without that check.

Crucial failed bridge: expanding the balanced eight-leaf, degree-three tree gives root cluster sizes 3m,3m,2m. The literal balanced tree on 8m leaves instead has the three root sizes floor(8m/3),ceil(8m/3),ceil/floor(8m/3). For m>1 these generally differ. Thus even a fully paid spectral substitution cannot compare the proposed competitor to the REQUIRED new balanced tree. Relabeling the expansion as balanced would be false. One needs an unbounded family with the correct recursive residues and strict comparison to that exact candidate. This is the precise missing lemma, not merely a request for a larger finite computation.

Actual 2603.26251v1 gives a public small (ell,D)=(8,3) comparison, displayed split (4,2,2) versus (3,3,2), and explicitly retains the extremal problem. Its Figure4 was NOT visually inspected; its numerical experiments were neither executed nor treated as proof. Its general upper bound D/ell does not identify the fixed-D maximizer. Source relevant statements were read; its entire proof was not audited. No full-scope ordinary endpoint results.
