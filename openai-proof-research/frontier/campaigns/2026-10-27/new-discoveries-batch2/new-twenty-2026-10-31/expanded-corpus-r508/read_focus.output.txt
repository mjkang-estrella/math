                            5. Further remarks and open problems
5.1. Intersecting families. Theorem 1.7 states that any uniform, (ℓ1 , ℓ2 )-intersecting family of dis-
tinct subsets of [n] contains at most n sets. This bound is tight, as can be observed from the family
F = {{i} | i ∈ [n]}.
   Noga Alon brought to our attention an interesting variant of Theorem 1.7. The variant states that
any (ℓ1 , ℓ2 )-intersecting (not necessarily uniform) family of distinct subsets of [n] contains at most
n+2 sets. The idea for the proof of this variant is that the Gram matrix of the characteristic vectors
of the sets has some values on the main diagonal, ℓ1 on the two next diagonals and ℓ2 everywhere else.
If ℓ1 = ℓ2 then the statement follows from Bose’s theorem (Theorem 1.6). Otherwise, subtracting
ℓ2 J (where J is the all-ones matrix that has rank 1), we obtain a similar matrix with ℓ2 replaced
by 0 and ℓ1 replaced by ℓ1 − ℓ2 . This matrix contains an (m − 1) × (m − 1) invertible triangular
submatrix that has rank m − 1, implying that the rank of the Gram matrix is at least m − 2. Its
rank is clearly at most n, implying that m ≤ n + 2. It is not known whether this bound is tight as
well.
   After determining the extremal value for a problem in extremal combinatorics, a natural next
step is to characterize the objects that achieve this extremum. In the case of Bose’s theorem
(described in Theorem 1.6), extremal constructions, known as symmetric block designs, have been
extensively studied in design theory. Various families of symmetric block designs, such as projective
spaces and Hadamard designs, are already known, and several necessary conditions for symmetric
block designs have been established (see, e.g., [5, Part II, Section 6] for further details). However,
a complete characterization of symmetric block designs remains an open problem.
   Given that the bound in Theorem 1.7 generalizes Bose’s theorem and is tight, it is natural to seek
a characterization of its extremal constructions as well. It is worth noting that symmetric block
designs naturally arise as extremal k-uniform, (ℓ1 , ℓ2 )-intersecting families, satisfying the property
ℓ1 = ℓ2 . Conversely, any extremal family with ℓ1 = ℓ2 is a symmetric block design. However,
other extremal families with ℓ1 ̸= ℓ2 may also exist. To the best of our knowledge, there has been
no analysis of such families to date. The only such family we have found (modulo permuting the
elements of the sets and complementing the sets) is:
