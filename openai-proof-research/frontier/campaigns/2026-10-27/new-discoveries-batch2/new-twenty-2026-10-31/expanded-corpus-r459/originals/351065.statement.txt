Let $C$ be a cake configuration, let $\operatorname{Ex}(C)$ denote its extremal cherries, and let $\operatorname{R}(C)$ denote its red cherries. A move is **optimal** if it is optimal for the player selecting the next cherry. **Strong greedy-move conjecture.** If

$$
\operatorname{Ex}(C) \cap \operatorname{R}(C) \neq \emptyset,
$$

then every move that takes a red cherry is optimal. This is presented as a stronger form of the Greedy-move conjecture; the paper states that the two conjectures are not known to hold and later proves that the former implies the latter.
