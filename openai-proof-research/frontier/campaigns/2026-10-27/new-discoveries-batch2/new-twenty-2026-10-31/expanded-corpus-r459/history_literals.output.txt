ID 351065 
 Let $C$ be a cake configuration, let $\operatorname{Ex}(C)$ denote its extremal cherries, and let $\operatorname{R}(C)$ denote its red cherries. A move is **optimal** if it is optimal for the player selecting the next cherry. **Strong greedy-move conjecture.** If

$$
\operatorname{Ex}(C) \cap \operatorname{R}(C) \neq \emptyset,
$$

then every move that takes a red cherry is optimal. This is presented as a stronger form of the Greedy-move conjecture; the paper states that the two conjectures are not known to hold and later proves that the former implies the latter.

ID 351904 
 Let $I_N=\{1,\dots,N\}$, let $\epsilon>0$, and let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be a strictly convex function. For a set $I$, write $f(I)=\{f(i)\mid i\in I\}$. **Erdős's conjecture.**

$$
|f(I_N)+f(I_N)|\gg_{\epsilon}N^{2-\epsilon}.
$$

The conjecture formalises the expectation that convexity disrupts additive structure and therefore makes the sumset of the image of an interval nearly quadratic in size. The paper discusses estimates for convex sets that approach known lower bounds, but the conjectured bound is not stated as resolved here.

ID 352320 
 Let $\mathcal{G}(n,p)$ denote the Erdős–Rényi random graph, let $\mathcal{G}(n,p,d)$ denote the random geometric graph in dimension $d$, and let $p_0$ be the associated comparison parameter. Suppose that

$$
p\sim c_0/n \quad\text{for some } c_0>0,
$$

and

$$
d\sim c_1n\log^2 n \quad\text{for some } c_1>0.
$$

**Inclusion-divergence conjecture.** Under these assumptions,

$$
\operatorname{I-Div}\big(\mathcal{G}(n,p,d)\,\|\,\mathcal{G}(n,p_0)\big)\to 0.
$$

This conjecture concerns the boundary at which the random geometric model becomes equivalent to its Erdős–Rényi comparison in inclusion divergence. The supplied text does not state whether the conjecture has been resolved.

ID 345439 
 Let $R_n$ be the squared Reed–Muller code matrix, let $U^n=R_nX^n$, and let $A_i$ denote the degree layer associated with row $i$. For indices $i,j
gin[n]$, write $H(U_i\mid U^{i-1},Y^n)$ for the corresponding conditional entropy.

**Twin-Reed–Muller conjecture.** The twin-Reed–Muller code is the Reed–Muller code; equivalently, for $i,j\in[n]$,

$$
|A_i|>|A_j|\implies H(U_i\mid U^{i-1},Y^n)\ge H(U_j\mid U^{j-1},Y^n).
$$

In words, conditional entropy should be non-decreasing as one moves to lower-degree layers. This conjecture would identify the capacity-achieving twin-Reed–Muller construction with the ordinary Reed–Muller code, thereby implying capacity achievement for Reed–Muller codes on every BMS channel. It remains open.

ID 345440 
 Let $R_n$ be the squared Reed–Muller code matrix and $U^n=R_nX^n$. Consider an index $i$ that is the last row in a degree layer of $R_n$, excluding the last layer; then $i+1$ is the first row in the next layer.

**Layer-crossing conjecture.**

$$
H(U_i\mid U^{i-1},Y^n)\ge H(U_{i+1}\mid U^i,Y^n).
$$

Thus, conditional entropy should be non-decreasing when crossing from one degree layer to the next. By the symmetry of Reed–Muller codes, the source states that this conjecture implies the stronger twin-Reed–Muller ordering conjecture. It is presented as an open problem.

 Let $G$ be a graph, let $t$ be an integer, and for $v\in V(G)$ let $N_G[v]$ denote the closed neighborhood of $v$. **Local Hadwiger conjecture.** If $G$ is a graph without a $K_{t+1}$-minor, then there exists $v \in V(G)$ such that $\chi(N_G[v]) \leqslant t$. The source says this follows from the tree-chromatic weakening if that conjecture holds, and notes that it is still open even with the proposed constant-factor relaxation.

ID 346095 
 Let $G=G_1\cup G_2$ be an **Earth-Moon graph**, meaning that $G_1$ and $G_2$ are planar subgraphs. The **Alon–Tarsi conjecture.** Every Earth-Moon graph $G$ satisfies

$$
\operatorname{AT}(G)\leqslant 9.

This would follow if the product of suitable non-vanishing monomials from the graph polynomials of $G_1$ and $G_2$ were itself non-vanishing. The statement concerns Ringel's Earth-Moon graph challenge and remains open in the source.

This conjecture asks for finite generation of the graded pieces of graph braid groups under deletion and contraction of graphs. The supplied source does not state whether the conjecture is open or has been resolved.

 Let $alt(n)$ denote the alternating sequence of $0$s and $1$s of length $n$ that ends with $0$. Let $\d835\d835\mathcal{L}_n$ be the de Bruijn sequence obtained from the lexicographic-composition construction, and let $\d835\d835\mathcal{LC}_n$ be the de Bruijn sequence returned by applying the de Bruijn successor $LC$ to $0alt(n-1)$. **Equivalence of the lexicographic-composition constructions.** The de Bruijn sequences $\d835\d835\mathcal{LC}_n$ and $\d835\d835\mathcal{L}_n$ are the same. This identifies the sequence produced by the successor-based construction with the sequence from the lexicographic-composition construction. ID 341720 
 Let $G=(V,E)$ be a multigraph. Define its density by

$$
\Gamma(G)=\max\left\{\frac{2|E(U)|}{|U|-1}:U\subseteq V,\ |U|\geq 3\text{ and odd}\right\},
$$

and call $G$ **elementary** when $\chi'(G)=\lceil\Gamma(G)\rceil$.

**Elementary-multigraph conjecture.** Every multigraph $G$ with

$$
\chi'(G)\geq\Delta(G)+2
$$

is elementary.

The source presents this as a reformulation of the Goldberg–Seymour conjecture, which was proved in the paper; hence this formulation is solved as well.

ID 342050 
 An **oriented** digraph is a digraph whose underlying undirected graph is simple, and a digraph is $k$-colourable if its vertices admit a colouring with $k$ colours such that every colour class is acyclic.

**Two-Colour-Conjecture.** Every oriented planar digraph $D$ is $2$-colourable.

This is the directed analogue of the Four-Colour Theorem. The conjecture was posed by Erdős and Neumann-Lara, and independently by Skrekovski, and remains open.

 Let $D$ be the presheaf of hypergraphs and let $C\subset D$ be a **proper** presheaf, meaning that disjoint union preserves membership in $C$. For each finite vertex set $V$, let $\mathbf{d}C_V$ be the functor obtained from the disjoint deletion process, and call the isomorphisms in its image **weak hypomorphisms**. **Disjoint reconstruction conjecture.** Two hypergraphs in a proper presheaf $C\subset D$ are isomorphic if and only if they are weakly hypomorphic. Equivalently, $\mathbf{d}C_V$ is essentially injective. This conjecture is introduced as an analogue of the ordinary reconstruction conjecture after replacing the diagonal deletion data by disjoint-union deletion data; the source gives no resolution.

ID 343125 
 Let $D=(V,A)$ be a **$(k+1)$-arc-strong** semicomplete digraph, meaning that $D$ remains strongly connected after deletion of any set of at most $k$ arcs. Let $A'\subset A$ be any set of $k$ arcs.

**Arc-avoidance conjecture.** The digraph $D\setminus A'$ is supereulerian.

This is the arc-connectivity analogue of the Fraisse–Thomassen theorem for hamiltonian cycles in tournaments. The source does not state whether this conjecture has been resolved.

ID 343165 
 Let $D$ be a digraph on $n$ vertices, let $G(D)$ be its associated bipartite graph, and let $M_D$ be the perfect matching associated with $D$. If $J$ is the defining ideal of the Rees algebra $R[I(G(D))t]$, **Rees-algebra formulation of the Caccetta-Häggkvist conjecture.** then, for some $q\leq\ell$, $J$ has a binomial generator of degree $q$ that is square-free and has relatively prime terms, one of which is a product of elements of $M_D$. The paper obtains this reformulation from the Jacobian-dual formulation using its theorem on the defining ideal of the Rees algebra; it is therefore equivalent to the original directed-cycle conjecture.
