## 351065: strong greedy convex grabbing

Source: acquired 2106.11247v3, pp.1–4 definitions and order-type transport, pp.11–12 Conjectures22–24 and Proposition25–26. The legal moves are precisely extreme points of a finite set in general position in R²; weights are 0 or 1. This excludes taking a hidden red point merely because the corpus says “every move that takes a red cherry.” The source's Proposition26 concerns NO-REVEAL, not strong greediness. Its figure was not visually inspected, and no counterexample is borrowed from it. The corpus reverses the verbal order of the implication in one sentence; actual Proposition25 states weak greediness implies strong greediness. Correcting that sentence earns zero.

Route A: exact minimax reduction and red-red exchange.
Let R(C) count reds and V(C) be the maximum number of reds the player about to move can force. Empty value is 0. Since total score is R(C), backward induction gives
 V(C)=max_{x in Ex(C)} [w(x)+R(C\x)-V(C\x)]
     =R(C)-min_{x in Ex(C)} V(C\x).
Thus a move x is optimal iff V(C\x) attains that minimum. In particular the full target is the inequality V(C\r)<=V(C\x) for EVERY legal red r and EVERY legal x, on EVERY cake.
If r,s are distinct legal reds, each remains extreme after deleting the other: a separating affine functional for an extreme point on C still separates on a subset. Under the induction hypothesis that all legal reds are optimal on smaller cakes,
 V(C\r)=R(C)-1-V(C\{r,s})=V(C\s).
So all red moves have equal values, conditional on smaller strong greediness. This does NOT compare them to a green move. If g is green, r remains legal in C\g, and the smaller-cake hypothesis gives
 V(C\g)=R(C)-V(C\{g,r}).
The availability of g in C\r gives only
 V(C\r)>=R(C)-1-V(C\{r,g})=V(C\g)-1.
This is a LOWER bound on the red-deleted value. The desired upper bound does not follow. The exact unpaid induction lemma is V(C\r)<=R(C)-V(C\{r,g}) for every legal red r and green g. Assuming it is circular, since it is exactly the missing red-versus-green optimality comparison.

Route B: strategy stealing / delayed red.
One might simulate a green-first optimal strategy while taking a red r immediately, postponing the green g until the simulation asks for r. Extremality is hereditary under deletion, so any simulated legal point still present is legal after extra deletions. This supplies the geometric half of the simulation. It does not supply turn synchronization: after skipping r in the simulation the actual and simulated players have opposite parity until a second skipped move. Taking a compensating green may be legal but consumes an actual move and can expose further reds to the opponent. A valid full strategy transfer must maintain both the player-to-move and a red-score debt of at most zero at terminal time. I have no invariant paying that debt. The source's geometric sun invariants (semi-exposed beams) are specific to carefully generated sun states and cannot be assumed for arbitrary cakes.

Route C: exact easy class and its failure to extend.
If every point is extreme, every subset is in convex position and V(C)=ceil(R(C)/2). Induct on total cardinality. A red move leaves R-1 reds and yields R-ceil((R-1)/2)=ceil(R/2); a green move, when present, yields R-ceil(R/2)=floor(R/2). Hence all red moves are optimal in this class. The same argument covers cakes of size at most three. It fails at the first hidden red: the child game is no longer characterized by R alone. No finite test or enumeration is substituted. A counterexample route therefore needs exact realizable coordinates, all orientation signs/general position, legal-move sets on each relevant subposition, and an explicit minimax tree establishing a strictly better green move. No such witness was found personally.

Route A: integral commutator algebra.
For an ordinary group P, write gamma1=P and gamma_{j+1}=[gamma_j,P]. The commutator identities [ab,c]=[a,c]^b[b,c] and [a,bc]=[a,c][a,b]^c, with a consistent conjugation convention, imply bilinearity after quotient by the next lower-central term. The weight identity [gamma_a,gamma_b] subset gamma_{a+b} follows by induction from Hall-Witt / the three-subgroup identity; its role is that changing one input modulo gamma2 changes an i-fold commutator only modulo gamma_{i+1}. Iterating the definition of gamma_i shows that left-nested length-i commutators generate gamma_i/gamma_{i+1}: commutators of products split modulo gamma_{i+1}, inverses give negatives, and conjugation of a weight-i element differs by a weight-(i+1) element. Consequently the map
 (P/[P,P]) tensor_Z ... tensor_Z (P/[P,P]) -> gamma_i(P)/gamma_{i+1}(P)
 sending a pure tensor to a nested commutator is a natural surjection. It is integral; a rational Lie-algebra statement would not suffice for the target's integral modules. Inner automorphisms act trivially on each LCS quotient, so basepoint changes by inner conjugation cause no ambiguity at this algebraic stage (component choices of disconnected configuration spaces still require the exact category setup).

This pays objectwise generation and naturality for homomorphisms. It does NOT pay finite generation of the tensor power as a representation. If F=H1(UConf_n(-)), a categorical finite generating set for F yields a surjection from a finite sum of principal modules to F. Its i-fold tensor power surjects onto F^tensor i. The missing lemma is that the relevant diagonal tensor products of these principal modules, or at least of this particular F, admit bounded graph supports and finitely many generating classes. Local Noetherianity only says submodules of already finitely generated objects are finitely generated. It cannot supply finite generation of an arbitrary tensor product for free.

Route B: common-support graph factorization.
A pure tensor at a graph Gamma uses finitely many H1 classes, each transported from a bounded source graph Gamma_j along potentially different minor morphisms. To make it the image of a SINGLE tensor on a bounded graph Theta, all morphisms must factor through one common Gamma -> Theta. The union of bounded target graphs is not automatically a common quotient: the contracted branch sets in Gamma can overlap in arbitrarily complicated ways. In a category where a minor morphism specifies contraction forests and deleted edges, consider the schematic doubled path: choose opposite copies in its two spanning trees. The two contractions to a point share no contracted edge and have no commonly deleted edge. A simultaneous intermediate reduction cannot contract or delete any edge. This illustrates precisely why objectwise target size need not bound a joint factorization. Route A: pointwise successor equality.
Let f(w)=w1 xor w2 xor wn and F(w)=w2...wn f(w), the pure run-length register PRR. The source chooses one LC representative per non-root PRR cycle and toggles f at each representative and its first-bit conjugate. The root is the alternating cycle. Conjugating an LC representative increases the number of runs by one, so the parent cycle has strictly larger run count and the cycle-joining graph is a rooted tree. This proves the LC rule is a de Bruijn successor (Theorem18 via16), which is weaker than equality with the 1977 word.
For any fixed n, equality of the two linear words follows if (1) their initial seeds/output conventions match, and (2) for every n-bit window w in the lexicographic-composition word, its next bit is exactly the LC successor bit. Proof: both start in the same state; induct on output length, using deterministic successor equality to preserve the current n-window and next bit. Conversely, equal cyclic words have the same successor on every n-word since each occurs once. Thus a precise full-scope reduction is to the local splice criterion: the 1977 construction differs from PRR exactly on the set LC(n) union conjugate(LC(n)). That criterion is unpaid without the old algorithm's actual definition and its cyclic boundary behavior.

Route B: ordered cycle tree isomorphism.
A different route matches the 1977 composition blocks with the source's PRR cycles. A cycle tree records (a) parent, (b) child attachment port, (c) cyclic order of the ports within a parent, and (d) the root seed. Matching only the set of cycles or the number of runs does not determine the Euler word: different attachment ports change the output. The source's Observation15 says a child excursion starts at the parent's conjugate port and completes at its representative, with descendants nested. Therefore, a full induction could identify the ordered subtrees and prove equality of their emitted strings. The unpaid bridge is a bijection from the old lexicographic composition records to PRR cycles preserving all four data, including periodic composition/complement tie cases. A mere numerical equality of total length, or the fact that both outputs are de Bruijn sequences, cannot replace this bridge.

Route C: induction on run-length encodings and the tempting false shortcut.
The PRR preserves run count: if w1=w2, then f(w)=wn, so neither deleting w1 nor appending f changes the number of runs; if w1!=w2, deletion loses one run and append adds one.
