===== 359433 The polynomial-exponent conjecture for ordered Ramsey numbers of path powers
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04357 | Lior Gishboliner, Zhihan Jin and Benny Sudakov, “Ramsey problems for monotone paths in graphs and hypergraphs”, arXiv:2308.04357 (2023).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) 04d66212d8bade64
ST: Let $P_n^t$ be the $t$th power of the ordered path on $n$ vertices, and let $K_n$ be the ordered complete graph on $n$ vertices. Write $R_{<}(G,H)$ for the ordered Ramsey number. **Polynomial-exponent conjecture.** The bound

$$
R_{<}(P_n^t,K_n)\leq n^{O(t)}
$$

should hold. The paper proves the stronger explicit bound $R_{<}(P_n^t,K_n)\leq 2^{2t-1}n^{t(2t-1)}$, so this open problem is resolved affirmatively in the source.
REF: Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04357

Status: the source reports no resolution (as of August 2023); treated as **open**. (An automated reading suggested “solved”, which the source does not establish — worth checking against the literature.)
===== 355862 Conjecture on the bipartite Ramsey number for $C_4$-free color classes
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12563 | Felix Joos and Dhruv Mubayi, “Ramsey theory constructions from hypergraph matchings”, arXiv:2208.12563 (2022).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) efa6163af4723998
ST: Let $r(K_{n,n},C_4,3)$ denote the corresponding Ramsey number for the complete bipartite graph $K_{n,n}$.

**Bipartite Ramsey conjecture.**

$$
r(K_{n,n}, C_4, 3) = \frac{2n}{3} + O(1)
$$

and

$$
r(K_{n,n}, C_4, 3) = \frac{2n}{3}
$$

for infinitely many $n$.

This is posed as a further conjecture following the nonbipartite case. The supplied context gives no additional proof or resolution, so both the bounded-error estimate and the infinitely-often equality remain open here.
REF: Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12563

Status: no resolution is reported in the source (as of August 2022).
===== 340527 Conjecture on decompositions of complete uniform hypergraphs into regular Hamiltonian cycles
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09302 | Amin Bahmanian and Sadegheh Haghshenas, “Partitioning The Edge Set of a Hypergraph Into Almost Regular Cycles”, arXiv:1809.09302 (2018).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) be1d9c25fbe942a1
ST: Let $K_n^h$ denote the complete $h$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices, and let $bb K_n^h$ be the hypergraph with $bb$ copies of every edge. A **regular Hamiltonian cycle** is a Hamiltonian cycle that is regular as a hypergraph. **Hamiltonian-cycle decomposition conjecture.** For sufficiently large $n$, $\lambda K_n^h$ can be decomposed into regular Hamiltonian cycles if and only if

$$
n\mid \lambda \binom{n}{h}.
$$

This conjecture proposes that the divisibility condition obtained by counting edges is also sufficient for decomposing a sufficiently large complete uniform hypergraph into regular Hamiltonian cycles.
REF: Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09302

Status: no resolution is reported in the source (as of September 2018).
===== 321035 Linear overhead conjecture for greedy random walk on complete graphs
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.5711 | Tal Orenshtein and Igor Shinkar, “Greedy Random Walk”, arXiv:1101.5711 (2012).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) ffffc98b1e58366a
ST: Let $K_n$ be the complete graph on $n$ vertices, and let $C_E(K_n)$ denote the edge cover time of greedy random walk. The expected edge cover time is conjectured to satisfy

**Linear overhead conjecture.**

$$
\mathbb{E}[C_E(K_n)]=|E|+\Theta(n).
$$

Thus the overhead beyond traversing all edges is conjectured to be linear in the number of vertices. The paper explains that the available argument gives an $O(n\log n)$ bound, whereas linear growth of the relevant bad sets would yield an $O(n)$ overhead; the conjecture is presented as a stronger expected bound for the complete graph.

===== 360013 Jackson's Hamiltonicity conjecture for regular oriented graphs
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11677 | Allan Lo, Viresh Patel and Mehmet Akif Yıldız, “Cycle Partitions in Dense Regular Digraphs and Oriented Graphs”, arXiv:2309.11677 (2025).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) 22dfb1f9f1674d52
ST: Let $G$ be an oriented graph, meaning a directed graph with at most one directed edge between each pair of vertices. It is **$d$-regular** when every vertex has $d$ in-neighbours and $d$ out-neighbours.

**Jackson's conjecture.** For each $d>2$, every $d$-regular oriented graph on $n\leq 4d+1$ vertices has a Hamilton cycle.

The conjecture asserts that regularity substantially lowers the degree threshold for Hamiltonicity in oriented graphs. The paper proves it for sufficiently large $n$ as a consequence of a stronger dense cycle-cover theorem, but the stated conjecture is not presented as completely resolved here.

===== 369268 Li and Rousseau's asymptotic Ramsey conjecture for complete fans
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09832 | Ting Huang, Yanbo Zhang and Yaojun Chen, “Fan-goodness of sparse graphs”, arXiv:2507.09832 (2025).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) 44d978ad8e128f61
ST: Let $F_k=K_1+kK_2$ be the graph consisting of $k$ triangles sharing one vertex, and let $r(G,H)$ denote the Ramsey number of graphs $G$ and $H$. Here $k$ is a fixed positive integer and $n$ tends to infinity.

**Li and Rousseau's conjecture.**

$$
r(K_n,F_k)=(1+o(1))\frac{n^2}{\log n}.
$$

This conjecture extends the asymptotic prediction for $r(K_n,F_1)=r(K_n,K_3)$ and remains open. Known upper bounds and the classical lower-bound framework establish the scale of the quantity, but not the asserted asymptotic constant.
REF: Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09832

===== 347656 Conjecture on the threshold for large monochromatic k-connected subgraphs
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09001 | Chunlok Lo, Hehui Wu and Qiqin Xie, “A Ramsey Type problem for highly connected subgraphs”, arXiv:2008.09001 (2023).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) 6fd4a753d1cb574f
ST: Let $k,n$ be positive integers, and let $K_n$ be the complete graph on $n$ vertices. A **2-edge-coloring** assigns one of two colors to every edge; a subgraph is **$k$-connected** if it has more than $k$ vertices and has no vertex cut of size at most $k-1$.

**Threshold conjecture.** If

$$
n>5k-\lfloor2\sqrt{2k-1}\rfloor-3,
$$

then every 2-edge-colored $K_n$ contains a $k$-connected monochromatic subgraph with at least $n-2k+2$ vertices.

This conjecture proposes an improved threshold after the earlier Bollobás–Gyárfás conjecture was disproved. The paper establishes the result for a larger threshold and reports a remaining gap of order $\Theta(\sqrt{k})$, leaving this sharper bound open.
REF: Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09001

Status: no resolution is reported in the source (as of August 2020).

For a threshold graph $G$ of order $n$, let $\mathcal{K}(G)$ denote its Kemeny constant, and let $r$ be one of the integers specified in the preceding proposition: $r\in\{\lfloor\sqrt{2n}\rfloor-1,\lfloor\sqrt{2n}\rfloor\}$ for $3\leq n\leq 20$, and $r\in\{\lfloor\sqrt{2n}\rfloor,\lfloor\sqrt{2n}\rfloor+1\}$ for $n\geq 21$. **Asymptotic maximum conjecture.** The maximum value of $\mathcal{K}(G)$ over all threshold graphs $G$ of order $n$ is

===== 318758 Critical-threshold conjecture for fixed-neighbour bootstrap percolation on the hypercube

ST: Let $[2]^d$ be the $d$-dimensional hypercube, and let $p_c([2]^d,r)$ denote the critical probability for $r$-neighbour bootstrap percolation on this graph. Here $r$ is fixed as $d$ tends to infinity.

**Fixed-neighbour hypercube threshold conjecture.**
For $r$ fixed,

$$
p_c([2]^d,r) = \exp\Big(-\Theta\left(d^{1 / 2^{r-1}}\right)\Big).
$$

The authors describe this as a fairly weak conjecture that they are unable to prove even for $r=3$, extending their results for the two-neighbour process to general fixed $r$.

===== 325013 Conjecture on the threshold function for nonnegative k-sums
SRC https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3636 | Stephen G. Hartke and Derrick Stolee, “A Branch-and-Cut Strategy for the Manickam-Miklos-Singhi Conjecture”, arXiv:1302.3636 (2013).
NEWLINE True SHA(with nl) 94d44773e799b0e2
ST: For each $k$, let $f(k)$ be the smallest integer such that every multiset of $n$ real numbers with nonnegative total sum has at least $\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ nonnegative $k$-sums for all $n\geq f(k)$. Let $N_k$ be the smallest integer satisfying

$$
\binom{N_k-3}{k} \geq \binom{N_k-1}{k-1}.
$$

**Threshold conjecture.** One has $f(k)=N_k$, and hence

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(k)}{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{N_k}{k}\approx 3.147899.
$$

The conjecture refines the Manickam–Miklós–Singhi problem by predicting the exact threshold. The source proves the values $f(4)=14$, $f(5)=17$, $f(6)=20$, and $f(7)=23$, while the asserted formula and asymptotic limit are not established in general.
REF: Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3636

Status: no resolution is reported in the source (as of February 2013).
