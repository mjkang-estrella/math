## 346253: complete prior-to-target bridge, KNOWN

Read Chirvasitu arXiv:2004.10880v1, pages 1–5, Definitions 1.1–1.2, 2.1, Theorem 2.2, Lemma 2.3, Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.5. Here is an independent reconstruction that also pays the matrix/path bridge rather than repeating the source's imprecise sentence identifying paths with entries. The positive support of a nonnegative nilpotent matrix is acyclic: a positive directed cycle gives a positive diagonal term in arbitrarily high powers, contradicting nilpotence. Conversely a finite acyclic support has no sufficiently long walks, so the matrix is nilpotent. Expansion of matrix multiplication says that the SUM of weights of all length-k walks is |A^k|. In a DAG every such walk is a path, but several paths can contribute to ONE entry.

Fix a weighted DAG and objective F=sum of products along k-paths. If distinct positive edges e,f occur in no common k-path, F=C+a*lambda(e)+b*lambda(f), where a,b are nonnegative sums of exclusive path products independent of both labels. Move the smaller-coefficient edge's entire weight into the larger-coefficient edge. F does not decrease, total weight is preserved, support shrinks, and acyclicity remains. After finitely many eliminations, every two remaining edges belong to one k-path. For k=1 there is at most one edge. For k>1, with positive objective, the remaining edges are totally ordered by directed precedence, since every pair occurs on a directed path. Consecutive edges in this order must share the head/tail vertex: a positive connecting path otherwise inserts another support edge between them. Thus the support is one directed path. Its first and last edges lying in a k-path force its length at most k, and positive objective forces length at least k. It therefore has exactly k edges. AM–GM bounds their product by (N/k)^k. Zero objective is already bounded. For N=0 all entries vanish. A (k+1)-vertex path whose k labels are N/k realizes equality and is nilpotent, including N=0. The independent k-real product maximum follows from the same AM–GM and balanced tuple.

Contrary assessment: a fixed matrix dimension below k+1 would prevent positive equality, but the record maximizes over square matrices without fixing dimension. Nonnegative entries and nilpotence are essential. Nothing new is claimed: the complete applicable prior bridge is Corollary 2.5, public April 2020, long before this campaign. Source minor wording slips do not invalidate the repaired path-sum bridge. This is a known exact solution, not a novel correction to the theorem.

## 346597: completed applicable prior bridge, KNOWN

Jackson–Nixon–Tanigawa 2005.11051v2 (2 July 2020), section 1 explicitly states the assigned conjecture as Conjecture 1.1 and says it is completely verified. The full proof of Theorem 3.2 has now been read, together with pinned independence Lemma 2.2 and circuit Lemma 3.1; no source theorem is inferred from abstract alone. Theorem 3.3's printed word "independent" conflicts with its rigidity proof: we rely on the precise independence criterion in 3.2 and the following direct bridge instead.

Let d>=2t, t>=1, and put r=d-t. Then d>=2 and r>=ceil(d/2)>=floor(d/2). If G has a spanning t-tight F, add the prescribed r loops at every vertex. The resulting F^[r] is d-tight: every vertex subset gets r|X| additional loops. It has at least floor(d/2) loops at every vertex. Its underlying edge graph cannot contain K_(d+2), because such a vertex set would have (d+2)(d+1)/2>t(d+2) edges, contradicting t-sparsity. Theorem 3.2 therefore makes F^[r] independent. Its d|V| rows give full column rank and hence infinitesimal rigidity; adding remaining rows from G^[r] preserves that rank. Generic placements exist over the reals because the required independent-row minors are nonzero polynomials.

Conversely, if G^[r] has an infinitesimally rigid realization, a nonzero maximal minor ensures full generic rank as well. The set of just the added r loops per vertex is independent at a generic realization: their vectors at each vertex are independent since r<=d and different vertices have disjoint column blocks. Extend this set of rows to a basis of the full d|V|-column rigidity matrix. The selected spanning looped subgraph H contains every added loop, has d|V| rows, and any induced vertex subset has at most d|X| selected rows, by linear independence in d|X| columns. Delete exactly those r added loops per vertex from H. The remaining spanning subgraph F lies in G, has t|V| rows and at most t|X| induced edges and loops for every X. Thus F is t-tight. Loops are distinct constraints even at the same vertex, as in the source's looped-simple convention. This pays necessity and sufficiency for the precise record; no d=2t-1 exception is imported. The result was public in 2020. It receives zero credit; no correction to Theorem 3.3 is proposed as a new scientific result.
