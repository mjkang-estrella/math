### Independent mathematical check

Take the finite product of independent rate-one exponential variables T_v and define Z_e=min_{v∈e}T_v for each nonempty edge. This probability space exists for every finite V. All Z_e are nonnegative measurable functions.

1. For any fixed labelled tuple (e_1,…,e_d), the pointwise layer-cake formula for a product and Tonelli give
   E[∏_i Z_{e_i}]=∫_{[0,∞)^d} P(T_v>t_i for every i and v∈e_i) dt.
   Independence makes the integrand exp(-Σ_v max({t_i:v∈e_i}∪{0})). Strict versus weak inequalities has no effect for exponential variables. Repeated edges give repeated constraints, not independent copies of Z_e.
2. Remove the finitely many coordinate tie hyperplanes, a Lebesgue-null set. The remaining domain is the disjoint union of d! chambers t_{π(1)}>…>t_{π(d)}>0 (coordinate-zero boundaries also null). These are chambers of integration coordinates even if some edges coincide. There is no division by a stabilizer of the edge tuple.
3. In such a chamber put u_j=t_{π(j)}-t_{π(j+1)}, with the last t zero. The inverse is t_{π(i)}=Σ_{j≥i}u_j and the Jacobian has absolute determinant one. For each vertex let k be its first occurrence among e_{π(1)},…,e_{π(d)}. Its maximum threshold is Σ_{j≥k}u_j. Summing over vertices therefore gives Σ_j |∪_{i≤j}e_{π(i)}|u_j. This verifies the orientation of the *prefix* unions, a potential source of error.
4. Each chamber integral factors into ∏_j ∫_0^∞ exp(-a_j u)du=∏_j a_j^{-1}, with a_j=|∪_{i≤j}e_{π(i)}|>0. Tonelli applies to this nonnegative integrand. Thus the mixed moment is the sum of these products over π.
5. Expand the dth power of the finite linear form and sum over all ordered tuples. For each fixed π, permuting the tuple coordinates is a bijection of E^d, and the product of weights is unchanged. Each permutation contributes exactly the source polynomial. Consequently
   **E[(Σ_e x_e Z_e)^d]=d! f_d(x).**
   In particular, the all-equal tuple contributes E[Z_e^d]=d!/L^d, consistent with the source's repeated-edge coefficient 1/L^d. The factor is d!, not its reciprocal on the right of this displayed identity.
6. Finiteness is genuine: write S=Σ_v T_v. Then Z_e≤S, so |Σ_e x_e Z_e|≤(Σ_e |x_e|)S. For d≥1, S^d≤n^{d-1}Σ_v T_v^d and E[T_v^d]=d!<∞. This also covers all mixed products and permits expectation of the finite expansion. When E is empty use the direct zero identity instead.
7. For x,y≥0 and θ∈[0,1], the two linear forms are nonnegative. Scalar convexity of s↦s^d on [0,∞), applied pointwise and integrated, proves the Jensen inequality for f_d after division by the positive d!. Restricting to Δ_m gives the literal conjecture for every source n,L,d. No assertion that positive monomial coefficients imply convexity is used.

There is no remaining ordinary proof gap in these steps. r40's rational identity diagnostics/results were inspected as diagnostics, not used as proof. No new numerical identity test was needed.

Any disjoint pair containing a size-six matching therefore has total at most nine. Otherwise each member has size at most five and total at most ten. The graph's degree-three vertices are exactly 0,1,4, so the two omitted edges must cover these three vertices. To cover three vertices with two edges at least one omitted edge must join two of them, and the only such edge is 01 (label 0). The other must meet 4, hence is label 3,4 or 11.
