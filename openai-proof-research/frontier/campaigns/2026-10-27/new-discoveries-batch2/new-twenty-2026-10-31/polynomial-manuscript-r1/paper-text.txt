 Convexity of ordered prefix-union polynomials
 A moment representation for the full hypergraph convexity conjecture

 Draft manuscript for MathDB 347913 | 1 November 2026 | No author attribution assigned

 Abstract
 For every complete L-uniform hypergraph on n vertices and every integer degree d, we identify
 the polynomial with ordered prefix-union denominators as the dth moment of a random
 nonnegative linear form, divided by d!. The random coefficients are minima of independent
 rate-one exponential clocks. A labelled chamber decomposition proves the coefficient identity,
 including repeated edges. Scalar power convexity then proves convexity on the nonnegative
 orthant and hence on the sum-one simplex. This proves the full mathematical statement of
 Conjecture 1 of Brosch, Laurent and Steenkamp, without a degree ceiling or an L <= n
 restriction. We give all integrability and change-of-variable details and a correspondence with
 the accompanying unchanged Lean certificate.

 1. Literal statement and conventions
 Let n, L, d be arbitrary integers at least two. Write [n] = {1,...,n}, let E be the set of all
 L-element subsets of [n], and put m = |E|. A weight vector x is a real-valued function on E. Every
 sum over E to the dth power below is over ordered tuples with repetition. For a tuple e =
 (e_1,...,e_d), define its inclusive prefix unions U_j(e).




 Each nonempty tuple has |U_j(e)| >= L > 0. There is no factorial or multinomial coefficient in
 this definition. If L > n, E is empty, the polynomial is zero for d >= 2, and the sum-one simplex is
 empty. No barycenter is assigned when m = 0.

 2. Exponential clocks and genuine integrability
 Use Omega = [0,infinity)^n with its Borel sigma-algebra and the product probability measure
 whose density is exp(-sum_v t_v) with respect to n-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Let T_v be
 the coordinate functions. They are independent rate-one exponentials. For every e in E define
 Z_e = min over v in e of T_v. Finite minima of coordinate functions are continuous and
 measurable, and Z_e >= 0 everywhere on this space. Repeated appearances of an edge always
 use this same random variable.




 For every integer r >= 0, direct integration gives E[T_v^r] = integral from zero to infinity of t^r
 exp(-t) dt = r!. At r = 0 the integral is one. For r >= 1, integration by parts gives r times the
 preceding integral; t^r exp(-t) tends to zero at infinity, for example by bounding the exponential
 below by a larger-degree term of its power series. This also proves convergence.




 The first inequality follows from scalar convexity of t to the rth power on nonnegative reals,
 applied to the average of the n clocks (equality for r = 1). Hence S has every finite integer
 moment. For any tuple e and k >= 1, (product_i Z_{e_i})^k <= S^(dk), since every lifetime lies
 between zero and S. The functions involved are measurable finite products and sums, so these
 dominations prove absolute integrability of all mixed products, their integer powers, and A_x^r
 for every signed real vector x. The zero powers are the constant one. If E is empty, A_x = 0 and
 the conclusions are immediate. Thus no formal convention for a divergent integral is used.

 Joint survival and the layer-cake formula
 Lemma 2 (joint survival)
 Fix any ordered tuple e in E^d, allowing repeated edges. For t in the nonnegative orthant of
 R^d and a vertex v, let h_v(e,t) be the largest threshold among coordinates whose edges contain
 v, together with the baseline zero.




 Proof. Each event Z_{e_i} > t_i says T_v > t_i for every v in e_i. Their intersection imposes the
 single maximal threshold at each vertex which appears in some tuple edge. At absent vertices
 the baseline zero imposes no probability cost, since T_v > 0 almost surely. Independence
 multiplies the tails exp(-h_v), giving the formula. Weak inequalities give the same probability:
 the finite union of events T_v = t_i has probability zero, since the clocks have densities. The h_v
 are finite maxima of continuous coordinate functions and zero, so the survival integrand is
 measurable. No independence between the Z_e is asserted. QED.

 Lemma 3 (product layer cake)




 Proof. For any nonnegative finite numbers z_1,...,z_d, the product of their lengths is the
 Lebesgue volume of the box product_i (0,z_i]. Thus product_i z_i equals the integral over
 (0,infinity)^d of product_i 1_{t_i <= z_i}. Apply this equality to z_i = Z_{e_i}. The subset
 {(omega,t): 0 < t_i <= Z_{e_i}(omega) for every i} is measurable, because coordinate
 evaluations and the lifetimes are measurable. Its indicator is nonnegative. Tonelli on the product
 of the finite clock probability measure and sigma-finite Lebesgue measure permits interchange
 of integrals without a preliminary integrability assumption. Lemma 2 supplies the inner
 probability. The domination in Section 2 then shows that the resulting integral is finite. QED.

 The variables t_i here are independent integration coordinates, not samples of the lifetimes.
 Even if e_i = e_j, the coordinates t_i and t_j remain distinct labels. Their equality hyperplane is
 null for d-dimensional volume; equality of the associated lifetimes need not be a probability-null
 event. This distinction is essential for the next step.

 Normalization control
 If all tuple edges equal one edge e, each prefix has cardinality L, so c(e,...,e) = L^(-d). The
 symmetrized coefficient is d!/L^d; it is not L^(-d) after quotienting by the stabilizer.
 Independently P(Z_e > t) = exp(-Lt), and integration by parts (or the one-dimensional moment
 integral with s = Lt) gives E[Z_e^d] = d!/L^d. This verifies both the factorial direction and the
 repeated-edge convention. It is a symbolic control, not a numerical argument.

 Proof of Theorem 1
 Take x,y >= 0 and theta in [0,1]. For every clock outcome A_x,A_y >= 0, and A_{theta
 x+(1-theta)y} = theta A_x+(1-theta)A_y. On the nonnegative half-line the function s -> s^d is
 convex: for d >= 2 its second derivative is d(d-1)s^(d-2) >= 0 on the positive half-line, and
 continuity extends the convexity inequality to zero. Apply it to these two linear forms and take
 expectations. All three powers are integrable by Section 2. Proposition 5 and division by d! > 0
 give the claimed inequality for f_d. The nonnegative orthant is convex; the additional linear
 condition sum x_e = 1 defines a convex subset. Restriction proves the simplex claim. If L > n
 that subset is empty, so the quantified convexity assertion is vacuous. QED.

 The signed-weight identity is not used to assert odd-power convexity on all real space.
 Nonnegativity is an explicit hypothesis in the pointwise convexity argument.

 Relation to the source and the literature
 3. Scope of the resolution
 Brosch, Laurent and Steenkamp [1] study two different classes. Their polynomial p_d uses only
 the final union cardinality as denominator. Its full convexity theorem is not a theorem about the
 successive prefix-union products defining f_d. For d = 2 one has f_2 = p_2/L; this relation does
 not remove the intermediate denominators at higher degrees. Their published Section 4 retains
 general f_d convexity as Conjecture 1, while proving degree three for L = 2. Our coefficient
 identification addresses those intermediate denominators directly.

 Polak [2], Theorem 1.1 in the inspected arXiv v2 body, proves L = 2 and d <= 9, for all n. The
 reduction writes the Hessian as a sum of monomials times matrices indexed by multigraphs with
 d-2 edges; for each fixed d it reduces positive semidefiniteness for all n to finitely many constant
 matrices. The stated verification reaches d <= 9. This is not a general-degree or general-L
 theorem. The CWI PDF retrieved under the older review's label 'polak-published' is
 byte-identical to the arXiv preprint; it does not provide an independently inspected typeset
 journal body. The published metadata is SIAM J. Discrete Math. (2022), DOI
 10.1137/21M1413298. We rely on the actual v2 theorem/proof context, not on the label of that
 cached file.

 Brosch's 2022 thesis [3], Chapter 5, Conjecture 5.17, again states general convexity and
 develops the Hessian coefficient-matrix reduction. Cardinaels, Borst and van Leeuwaarden [4],
 in the inspected arXiv v2 body, relate light-traffic queue comparisons to coefficients alpha_q via
 Theorem 1 and Lemma 3. The discussion preceding Corollary 1 invokes Polak for q <= 9 and
 describes larger q as numerical support. That body does not supply the general moment identity
 proved here. These observations locate the result relative to specific inspected bodies; they do
 not claim exhaustive later-literature coverage.

 4. Classical antecedent and explicit priority limit
 The probability model is a special case of the classical Marshall-Olkin common-shock
 construction [5]. To see the identification directly, assign vertex v the set S_v = {e in E : v in e}.
 The independent clock T_v is a shock that kills exactly these edge lifetimes. Each edge survives
 until the first shock involving it, which is precisely min_{v in e} T_v. Equal S_v can be grouped
 by adding their rates. The resulting survival exponent is the sum, over shocks, of the largest
 threshold of their affected lifetimes. This is antecedent methodology; no new distribution is
 claimed.
