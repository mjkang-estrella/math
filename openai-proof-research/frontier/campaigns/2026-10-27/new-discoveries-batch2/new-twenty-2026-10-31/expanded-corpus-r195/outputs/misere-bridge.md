The single-theater game is ordinary bounded subtraction under the source's disjunctive-sum convention. Let g(r)=r mod (m+1). A move changes g to a different residue, and every smaller residue is available by a legal subtraction. For a sum, classify it losing exactly when (a) all residues are 0 or 1 and the number of 1 residues is odd, or (b) some residue is at least 2 and their XOR is zero.

This is verified by induction on total heap size. In the all-small case, changing a small residue to a large residue cannot yield a losing position with exactly one large residue; changing between small residues flips parity. In the XOR-zero large case, at least two large residues occur, so after any move a large residue remains and the XOR becomes nonzero. From a large position with nonzero XOR, use the usual Nim reduction to a smaller residue if at least two large residues occur (the resulting zero XOR cannot have exactly one large residue). If exactly one large residue occurs, reduce it to 0 or 1 to leave odd parity of ones. From an all-small position with even parity, change a residue 1 to 0 if present; otherwise a nonzero heap with residue 0 can move to residue 1 by subtracting m. The genuinely terminal empty sum is winning, as required under misere play.

Thus the pair (XOR of residues, whether any residue >=2 exists) is a finite monoid invariant under disjoint union (XOR, OR), with finitely many possible values for fixed m. Equal invariants are interchangeable in every sum. The indistinguishability quotient is therefore finite. This is a routine elementary bridge from misere Nim and the source's modular Grundy formula, not a campaign discovery. Allen's 2007 Theorem 6 gives older, broader tame-subtraction periodicity context; its entire general theory is not newly formalized here.
