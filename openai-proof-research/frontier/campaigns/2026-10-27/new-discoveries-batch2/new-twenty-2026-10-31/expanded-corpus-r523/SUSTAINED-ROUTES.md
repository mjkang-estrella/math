## 348662: star-target p-monotonicity

Exact source: Eagles et al. 2011.06063v2 Definition 1.1 and Conjecture 9.6; acquired EJC29(1)(2022) P1.28 final Conjecture79 and Proposition80 agree on all n>=1 and every G. X is the canonical monomial extension from r=n+1 variables, not an arbitrary lift. The final has the same connected/large-n sufficient proposition. Lin–Pierson 2511.08665 Section3.1 Proposition3.1.1/Lemma3.1.2 and proof pp18–20 establish all disconnected bipartite G only when n is at least the sum of the larger bipartition sizes. Their proof explicitly explains the small-n truncation gap. No credit for this known regime or changing its one scalar binomial estimate. The full target genuinely includes the missing finite-variable regime.

Route A: a direct orientation formula. For nontrivial connected bipartite components with side sizes a_i,b_i, a homomorphism to a star chooses a side in each component to collapse onto its center. For a choice giving total center load A and remaining load B, the r-variable expression divided by n! is sum_j x_j^A(p_1-x_j)^B. Thus its stable expression is sum_{h=0}^B (-1)^h binom(B,h) p_{A+h}p_1^{B-h}. The finite expression must be extended by its MONOMIAL expansion, and any monomials using more than r distinct variables are discarded. If B<=n, no discard occurs. After omega and summing component choices, the coefficient factors into binomial differences, exactly as in the public follow-up. When B>n, using the stable expression unchanged is invalid: truncation does not commute with its power-sum expression. Isolated components require their separate unconstrained color choices; the displayed orientation formula is restricted to components containing edges. No full proof from this shortcut.

Route B: exact finite integer coefficient model, avoiding a field-valued formal endpoint. Let N=|V(G)| and r=|V(H)|. Let pi run over set partitions of V(G) into b independent blocks, b<=r. Form the simple quotient Q_pi whose distinct blocks are adjacent if some original edge joins them. Set c(pi)=(r-b)! times the number of injective homomorphisms Q_pi -> H. For fixed pi, sum over injective assignments of variable indices to its blocks, giving each index exponent equal to its block size. Multiplying this sum by c(pi) and summing pi gives X_G^H exactly: for any fixed distinct labels on the blocks, an injective embedding in H chooses their preimages and the remaining r-b target vertices can be labelled in (r-b)! ways. Every original coloring has exactly one fiber partition pi.

For a partition rho of the b blocks of pi, inclusion–exclusion on equality of their variable indices gives its power-sum coefficient mu(rho)=product over C in rho of (-1)^(|C|-1)(|C|-1)!. Its power-sum index is lambda(pi,rho), the sorted sums of original block sizes in each C. Therefore the literal omega coefficient at lambda is the following INTEGER:
 S_lambda = sum_{pi,rho:lambda(pi,rho)=lambda} c(pi) (-1)^(N+b) product_C (|C|-1)!.
Indeed mu has sign (-1)^(b-|rho|), while omega contributes (-1)^(N-|rho|), so the product sign is (-1)^(N+b). This supplies an exact coefficient bridge, including all finite n, disconnected G, isolates and nonbipartite zero cases. It does NOT prove that the integers S_lambda have a common sign. Contributions from fiber partitions with opposite parities b may cancel differently for different lambda. The missing full lemma is: for H=K_1,n, the collection (S_lambda)_lambda is either all >=0 or all <=0. A sign-reversing involution would have to preserve lambda, the target embedding multiplicity and factorial weights simultaneously. Pairing arbitrary refinements of pi does not preserve these constraints.

Route C: finite truncation by hand. The r=2 case has only fibers of size one or two blocks. For N>1 and a two-block partition of sizes a,b, the injective variable sum equals p_a p_b-p_N; after omega both terms have sign (-1)^N, since the length-two term acquires (-1)^(N-2) and the minus length-one term acquires -(-1)^(N-1). One-block contributions, possible for edgeless G, have that opposite sign before aggregation; they cannot simply be ignored. For illustration, the truncated edgeless N=5,r=3 polynomial is
 m_5+5m_41+10m_32+20m_311+30m_221
 =36p_5-30p_41-30p_32+10p_311+15p_221.
Applying omega makes all displayed coefficients positive. These are symbolic hand expansions, no computation or enumeration. Similarly for the nontrivial test G=P3 disjoint-union K2, r=3, the orientation expression is proportional to p_2111-2p_311+p_41. Removing its forbidden length-four monomials gives p_311+3p_221-5p_41-5p_32+6p_5, again omega-positive. These examples expose real cancellation, and do not establish a uniform sign theorem or furnish a counterexample. No unchanged attempt was rerun. PARK at the exact integer signed-sum lemma.

## 364538: sharp degree balance in all regular graphs

Exact source Lužar–Przybyło–Soták 2408.16121v2 March2025, concluding Conjecture3.2. Source explicitly restricts to simple graphs. Corollary3.1 proves d=3 except K4,3K4,K3,3; Observation2 pays d=2. These instances are known and earn zero. Source d>=4 restriction is structural: the induction is a cubic decomposition with specific small exceptional components, not a final scalar comparison. Its authors explicitly say they do not know how to adapt it to d>=4. No classical criterion or previously solved neighboring model was authenticated as a full subsumer. Final EJC2025 DOI10.1016/j.ejc.2025.104169 was identified in search but not acquired; no final equivalence asserted.

Route A: parity and integer rounding. Put n=(d+1)q+r, 0<=r<=d. In the nonexceptional branch for even d, the prescribed error d/(d+1) forces every histogram entry m_k to be q or q+1. Exactly r entries must be q+1, since their sum is n. For odd d, n is even and hence r is even. The sharper (d-1)/(d+1) bound still permits q,q+1 for 2<=r<=d-1, and forces exact q when r=0. At r=0, sum k m_k =q*d(d+1)/2 is odd precisely when q and ceil(d/2) are odd; then Handshaking forbids exact balance and explains the separate error-one branch. Thus the stated parity exception is real. There is no additional rounding contradiction: odd-d residues 1 and d cannot occur because n is even. A proposed residue counterexample dies at this parity check and is not a new domain correction.

Route B: alternating-trail exchanges. Adding an absent edge with current endpoint degrees a,b changes the histogram by e_{a+1}-e_a+e_{b+1}-e_b. Removing a present edge gives the analogous downward move. The weighted sum of entries changes by +/-2, preserving Handshaking. Along a trail alternating between present and absent edges, toggling every edge preserves each internal vertex degree and changes only endpoint degrees by one (with signs determined by the end edges). This suggests sending surplus histogram mass to deficient degree levels through endpoint moves. But endpoint vertices are not arbitrary: an alternating trail of the required parity and end type may not exist. Minimizing a quadratic histogram discrepancy over all spanning H supplies only inequalities for available moves. It does not create the missing alternating trails. An exact sufficient lemma would show that any histogram outside the claimed rounding set admits a degree-preserving internal alternating-trail move that strictly improves an explicit potential, apart from finitely many bounded-order d-regular graphs. Neither a bound on the obstructing components nor the required improvement lemma was proved. Merely applying a bounded-degree discrepancy theorem gives error depending on d, far above this target.

Route C: disconnected obstructions and complement. If h(H)=(m_0,...,m_d) is feasible then h(G minus E(H)) reverses it. For a disjoint union, feasible histograms add. This makes repeated small components a natural attempt at an infinite refutation: find a component C whose feasible histogram semigroup never contains the prescribed near-uniform vectors at infinitely many multiplicities. The parity functional is automatically shared, and complementary choices alone only symmetrize m_k+m_{d-k}; they do not make all d+1 coordinates equal. Conversely a convex combination of histograms equal to the uniform vector would not pay exact integer multiplicities for arbitrary disjoint components or arbitrary orders. The unpaid alternative is an explicit persistent lattice obstruction beyond Handshaking, or a saturated-integer-exchange theorem for all regular components. No enumeration performed and no unproved semigroup saturation claimed. PARK at this all-d exact realization gap.

Lean feasibility: YES, the literal itself admits a light formulation: finite simple graphs, spanning edge subsets, integer histograms, natural d,n and finite exceptional families. All fractions and error conditions can be cleared by d+1, with the parity branch encoded arithmetically. Unlike the analytic targets this requires no real analysis, linear algebra or field theory. No endpoint proved or executed.

## 358527: a cycle in some tournament out-neighborhood

Exact primary Conjecture11 in 2305.15585v2. Read the closing statement, Proposition5 hypercube proof, Theorem2/Lemma8/Schrijver construction and Theorem6 proof. Its headline refutation only makes the out-neighborhood bipartite, not acyclic; Proposition5 gives arbitrary regularity with forest neighborhoods but the underlying hypercube has chromatic number two. Neither refutes the literal. The corpus reference to Lovász concerns the source's internal Kneser theorem, not Conjecture11. No correction credit claimed.

Route A: cycle domination bound, proved by hand. Suppose every G[N_T^+(v)] is a forest, and let C be any G-cycle of length l. No vertex x outside C can dominate every vertex of C in T, because then C would lie in G[N_T^+(x)]. Thus some c in C dominates x. Within C, at most one vertex has no incoming arc from C, since two such vertices cannot both dominate their mutual tournament edge. Consequently the l open out-neighborhoods of vertices of C cover V(G) except at most one vertex of C. Each of their G-induced graphs is a forest and hence 2-colorable. Assign vertices to one covering set, use two fresh colors per set, and give the one uncovered vertex a fresh color. This proves chi(G)<=2l+1. In particular any putative counterexample with huge chi must have huge girth. This is NOT a contradiction: high-girth high-chromatic graphs exist. The missing lemma would force a cycle of bounded length in this tournament-local-forest class, or give another bounded coloring that avoids dependence on l.

Route B: critical graphs and double counting. A q-critical induced subgraph has minimum degree at least q-1, and the local-forest property survives induced restriction of both G and T. Summing e(G[N_T^+(v)])<=max(0,|N_T^+(v)|-1) counts each G-edge xy as many times as x,y have common predecessors in T. There is no uniform positive lower bound on this multiplicity: even a pair in a tournament can have zero common predecessors. Thus high minimum degree alone cannot turn the upper bound into a constant q bound. Need a structural dichotomy forcing enough common predecessors on edges, or a bounded coloring on the sparse-predecessor alternative. Proposition5's arbitrarily regular cubes show why degeneracy alone cannot supply it.

Route C: try extending the source counterconstruction. Source Schrijver neighborhoods use two independent classes, sets containing r or r+1. Those classes may contain alternating even cycles between them. Bipartite does not imply forest. Deleting edges to destroy all such cycles may erase the Schrijver chromatic lower bound; no robust high-chromatic subgraph satisfying all the neighborhood acyclicity constraints was built. The hypercube doubling construction controls both closed neighborhood types because exactly one matching edge links the two forest pieces, but doubling preserves a two-colorable underlying graph. Replacing matching links by chromatic-raising links loses the single-edge forest gluing property. The precise unpaid construction lemma is a chromatic-raising operation preserving all local forest constraints, or conversely a proof none exists strong enough to yield a universal chi bound. PARK, no full negation.

Lean feasibility: YES, the exact statement is existence of a natural chromatic threshold for all finite simple graphs and tournaments, with an actual finite cycle witness. Natural colorings, finite words/walks and finite adjacency suffice. The auxiliary fractional-coloring/source topological machinery is not proposed as a formal dependency. No formal execution.
