                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                                                                         ∞
                                              For a, b, c ∈ R and abc 6= 0, we let {an }n=0 be a sequence of real numbers satisfying the three-term
                                           recurrence
                                           (1.1)                                can+2 + ban+1 + aan = 0, n ≥ 0,
                                           with the initial values (a0 , a1 ) 6= (0, 0). This sequence includes many classic sequences in math-
                                           ematics such as the Fibonacci, Lucas, and Pell sequences (see [1, 2]). It is known that we can
                                           obtain a closed formula for an from the zeros, denoted by α and β, of the characteristic polynomial
                                           c + bt + at2 . In this paper, these zeros play a central role in determining the location of the zeros of
                                                                                                                                               ∞
                                           Taylor polynomials associated with this sequence. The sequence of Taylor polynomials {Pm (z)}m=0
                                                                                 ∞

                                                                                                   m

                                           The study of locations of zeros of Taylor polynomials from general sequences (not necessarily from
                                           those satisfying (1.1)) is of interest to many mathematicians. A classic example is the case an =
                                           1/n!, where the zeros of the associate Taylor polynomials approach the famous Szego curve (see
                                                                                       ∞
                                           [3]). It is not difficult to show when {an }n=0 satisfies (1.1) and |α| > |β|, most of the zeros of Pm (z)
                                           approach the circle radius |c|/|aα| = |β| (c.f. Remark 4) as m → ∞. In this paper we provide
                                           conditions under which the zeros Pm (z),m  1, lie on one side of this circle. There are many
                                           studies of polynomials whose zeros lie on one side of a circle or a half plane. We refer the readers
                                           to a development by Borcea and Branden ([2]) on linear operator preserving zeros of polynomials
                                           on a circular domain. We state the three main theorems of this paper.
                                           Theorem 1. Let α be the largest (in modulus) zero of at2 + bt + c. If ac < 0, a0 b(a1 c + ba0 ) ≥ 0,
                                           then with the possible one exceptional zero, all the zeros of Pm (z), m  1, lie outside the open ball
                                           centered at 0 with radius |c|/|aα|. Moreover, the exceptional zero occurs if and only if
                                                                                       |a1 c + ba0 | > |aαa0 |.
