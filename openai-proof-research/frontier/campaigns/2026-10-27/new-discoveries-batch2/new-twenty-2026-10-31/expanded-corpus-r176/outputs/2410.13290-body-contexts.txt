Introduction A family \mathcal{H} of graphs packs into a graph G if G contains pairwise edge-disjoint copies of the members of \mathcal{H}, i.e. a packing of \mathcal{H}. A packing is perfect if it uses each edge of G. A graph H decomposes a graph G if there is a perfect packing of copies of H into G. Packing and decomposition problems have a long history, naturally relating to Euler’s 1782 question on the existence of orthogonal latin squares, and to the existence of designs. We are interested in packing trees into complete bipartite graphs. 1.1.
