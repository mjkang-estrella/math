Rather than claiming a natural isomorphism between model constructions, we establish the universal property of the Henkin (or Skolem) construction: the term model arising from a Henkin extension is an initial object in an appropriate category of models. We show that for any consistent theory T and its Henkin extension T^{\dagger}, the term model F(T) admits a unique homomorphism to any model of T^{\dagger}, and this construction is natural with respect to theory morphisms that preserve the additional witness constants. This result provides a rigorous foundation for understanding the completeness theorem from a categorical viewpoint, avoiding the pitfalls of claiming unwarranted isomorphisms while preserving the essential structural insights. We provide counterexamples. Counterexample 1 (Different Cardinalities) . Consider the theory \mathrm{ACF}_{0} of algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. This is a complete theory. • The Henkin construction produces a countable term model F(\mathrm{ACF}_{0}) . • By the compactness theorem, \mathrm{ACF}_{0} has models of every infinite cardinality, including uncountable models like \mathbb{C} . • Clearly, F(\mathrm{ACF}_{0}) and \mathbb{C} are not isomorphic as they have different cardinalities. This shows that models constructed by the Henkin method and compactness-based methods are not generally isomorphic. Counterexample 2 (Incomplete theory) . Proof. • Reflexivity: T^{\dagger}\vdash t=t by the reflexivity axiom of equality. • Symmetry: If T^{\dagger}\vdash t=s, then T^{\dagger}\vdash s=t by the symmetry axiom. • Transitivity: If T^{\dagger}\vdash t=s and T^{\dagger}\vdash s=u, then T^{\dagger}\vdash t=u by the transitivity axiom and modus ponens. ∎ A.2 Verification of Functoriality Lemma A.2 (Functoriality up to reduct) .
