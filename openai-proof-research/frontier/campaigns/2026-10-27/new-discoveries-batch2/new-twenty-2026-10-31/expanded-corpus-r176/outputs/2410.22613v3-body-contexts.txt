Following [ 37 ] , these families are: affine, almost simple, diagonal type, product type, and twisted wreath products. Questions related to base sizes have been considered for all five of these families; for example, see [ 8 , 12 , 21 , 22 , 28 , 32 , 33 ] . In particular, a significant quantity of research has been conducted on classifying primitive groups of small base size. A project initiated by Saxl in the 1990s to classify primitive base-two groups (i.e. groups of base size 2) is one of the most intense research areas in this vein, however the problem remains open in general. For base-two groups G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega), Burness and Giudici [ 10 ] defined the Saxl graph \Sigma(G), whose vertices are the points of \Omega, and where two vertices are adjacent if and only if they form a base for G. This graph satisfies a number of interesting properties. For example, if G is transitive, then \Sigma(G) is G-vertex-transitive, and if G is primitive, then \Sigma(G) is connected. Burness and Giudici also made a rather striking conjecture that if G is primitive, then any two vertices in \Sigma(G) have a common neighbour (see [ 10 , Conjecture 4.5] ).
