## 1. Precise single-block theorem

Let A be a commutative ring with identity, m>=0, S=A[x_1,y_1,...,x_m,y_m], and I the ideal generated by x_i y_j-x_j y_i for 1<=i<j<=m. Let F=A[s,t,z_1,...,z_m], with all displayed indeterminates distinct. There is an A-algebra homomorphism phi:S->F specified by x_i |-> s z_i and y_i |-> t z_i. Then ker(phi)=I. In particular this is an arbitrary-field theorem. There is no surjectivity assertion onto F.

By a polynomial ring here we mean finite formal sums of monomials, with coefficientwise equality and multiplication by finite convolution. Thus monomials are an A-module basis by definition, even if A has torsion or zero divisors. Substitution on monomials followed by finite summation constructs phi and verifies addition, multiplication and constants. No assertion about functions on A-points is involved.

For M=product_i x_i^{a_i}y_i^{b_i}, put d_i=a_i+b_i, r=sum_i a_i, D=sum_i d_i. If i<j, b_i>0 and a_j>0, write M=H y_i x_j, with H the monomial obtained by subtracting those two exponents, and replace M by M'=H x_i y_j. Then

M-M'=-H(x_i y_j-x_j y_i) in I.

All exponents remain nonnegative. The invariant (d,r) is unchanged. The nonnegative integer weight w(M)=sum_i i a_i strictly decreases by j-i. There can be no infinite reduction sequence; any choice of available pair terminates in at most w(M) swaps. One can make the procedure deterministic by choosing the lexicographically first available pair, but this is unnecessary for uniqueness.

For any d in N^m and 0<=r<=D, define

g_i=min(d_i,max(0,r-sum_{h<i}d_h)),
N(d,r)=product_i x_i^{g_i}y_i^{d_i-g_i}.

Induction on i proves sum_{h<=i}g_h=min(r,sum_{h<=i}d_h): when the prior prefix is already >=r the next summand is zero; otherwise it is the smaller of d_i and the remaining r minus the prior prefix. Consequently 0<=g_i<=d_i and sum_i g_i=r. This is the greedy allocation of the r x-factors to earliest vertices.

A terminal monomial is uniquely N(d,r). Indeed, if all b_i=0 then r=D and the formula gives g_i=d_i=a_i. Otherwise let k be the first index with b_k>0. All earlier a_i=d_i, and all later a_j=0, since otherwise (k,j) is reducible. Therefore a_k=r-sum_{i<k}d_i, and the formula gives a_i=g_i at every index. Conversely N(d,r) is terminal: if its i-th column has a y-factor, its x-prefix has used all r x-factors by index i, so no later column has an x-factor. This also covers r=0, D=0 and zero-degree columns.

Telescoping any terminating reduction gives an actual finite certificate

M-N(d,r)=-sum_l H_l(x_{i_l}y_{j_l}-x_{j_l}y_{i_l}).                 (1)

This is not a definition of normal form using the desired kernel. It is an explicit formula from exponents, independently verified by terminating swaps. Confluence is established by the terminal characterization, not assumed.

Write an arbitrary polynomial uniquely as f=sum_{a,b} c_{a,b} M(a,b), summing only its finite support. For each (d,r) set

C_{d,r}=sum_{a+b=d, sum a_i=r} c_{a,b},

again restricting to that finite support. Define NF(f)=sum_{d,r} C_{d,r} N(d,r). Multiplying (1) by each arbitrary coefficient c_{a,b} and summing proves f-NF(f) in I. In particular the multipliers in this ideal certificate are arbitrary finite polynomials over A, with their original signs and coefficients, not just positive or squarefree combinations.

Direct substitution gives phi(M)=s^r t^{D-r} product_i z_i^{d_i}. Two distinct (d,r) give distinct target monomials: their z-exponents recover every d_i, and their s-exponents recover r. Thus the coefficient of that target monomial in phi(f) is exactly C_{d,r}. If phi(f)=0 all these coefficients vanish, so NF(f)=0 and the finite certificate implies f in I. Conversely every generator maps to s t z_i z_j-s t z_j z_i=0; every finite polynomial combination of generators maps to zero. This proves both kernel inclusions for every A.

It also proves uniqueness of the normal representative of each class: if two linear combinations of distinct N(d,r) differ by an element of I, their images have identical coefficients at distinct target monomials, so their coefficients agree. The quotient has the N(d,r) as an A-module basis. This statement is freeness; if A itself has additive torsion, the quotient can have that same torsion. Over a field it is in particular a vector-space basis. With A=B[w_1,...,w_h], the argument is still valid for exact polynomial coefficients in the w variables: all operations and coefficient comparisons are over A, and no nonzero coefficient needs to be invertible or cancellable.

For m=0 the sole source monomial is 1, d is empty and r=0; I=0 and phi is the injective constant inclusion A->A[s,t]. For m=1 there are no minors and N(d,r)=x_1^r y_1^{d-r}; the proof gives injectivity, without excluding these cases. Ordered pairs in either direction generate the same I because the reversed minor is its negative and the self-minor is zero. This observation works in characteristic two as well.

## 2. Disjoint blocks and killed coordinates: simultaneous constructive proof

Let V be finite, T any subset, U=V minus T, and let (B_c)_{c in C} be a finite partition of U into nonempty disjoint blocks. Choose an order on each block. Let

S=A[x_v,y_v:v in V],
I_T=(x_v,y_v:v in T)+(x_u y_v-x_v y_u:u,v in B_c for some c).

The coefficient ring A can again be any commutative ring with identity. Define the free polynomial ring

F_T=A[s_c,t_c:c in C; z_v:v in U],

where the three types of parameter symbols are disjoint, even if their underlying indices have the same names. Define Phi(x_v)=Phi(y_v)=0 for v in T and, for v in B_c, Phi(x_v)=s_c z_v, Phi(y_v)=t_c z_v. This is one simultaneous map into one polynomial ring. Then ker(Phi)=I_T.

First, delete from the monomial expansion of f every monomial using a coordinate of T, obtaining f_surv in the surviving variables, embedded back in S. Each deleted monomial has a coordinate factor x_v or y_v for some v in T; factor one such variable with positive exponent. Multiplication by its coefficient and summation supplies a finite expression for f-f_surv in the coordinate ideal. The zero-coordinate substitution has exactly that ideal as kernel: its surviving monomials retain distinct exponent vectors, so their arbitrary coefficients must be zero when its image is zero. Also Phi(f)=Phi(f_surv).

For a surviving monomial set d_v=a_v+b_v and r_c=sum_{v in B_c} a_v. Independently normalize the factors of each block as in section 1; other block factors are just monomial multipliers of its certificates. Alternatively every swap strictly decreases the single weight sum_c sum_{i=1}^{|B_c|} i a_{c,i}. Thus the entire process terminates. Its unique terminal monomial is

N(d,(r_c))=product_c N_c((d_v)_{v in B_c},r_c),

where the local N_c is the explicit greedy formula. Empty C gives N=1. By local uniqueness each block is determined, and disjointness makes their product a monomial with uniquely determined exponents at every surviving variable. Tensor products, extension of prime ideals, and any theorem that a tensor product of domains is a domain are unnecessary.

Group the finite coefficients of f_surv by the tuples (d,(r_c)); call their sums C_{d,(r_c)}. Multiplying and adding the local certificates, including the deleted monomial certificates, proves

f - sum_{d,(r_c)} C_{d,(r_c)} N(d,(r_c)) in I_T.                (2)

The image of a surviving monomial is exactly

(product_c s_c^{r_c} t_c^{D_c-r_c})(product_{v in U} z_v^{d_v}),
where D_c=sum_{v in B_c}d_v.

Distinct tuples give distinct target monomials: the z-coordinates recover d and each separate s_c-coordinate recovers its own r_c. Consequently the coefficient of this image monomial is exactly C_{d,(r_c)}. If Phi(f)=0, every such coefficient is zero and (2) puts f in I_T. Conversely all killed coordinates map to zero and each within-block minor maps to s_c t_c(z_u z_v-z_v z_u)=0, proving I_T subset ker(Phi). No cross-block minor was introduced. These inclusions concern the ORIGINAL generator ideal, rather than an ideal defined to be a kernel.

The same comparison proves that the surviving normal monomials are an A-module basis of S/I_T: spanning follows from (2), and an ideal relation among them maps to a relation among distinct target monomials, whose coefficients all vanish. Thus all simultaneous independence and coefficient extension steps are paid explicitly. If T=V, then C=U=empty, F_T=A, Phi is evaluation at zero in every variable, and the normal representative is the constant coefficient. Singleton blocks work exactly as in section 1, with their own independent parameters; they impose no equations on their two original coordinates. Empty blocks may be omitted; if included artificially their extra s,t variables are unused and change no kernel.

## 3. Properness and primality over a field, without determinantal primality

Take A=K a field (more generally an integral domain). A polynomial ring in finitely many variables over K is a domain by this direct argument. Order the finite list of variables and lexicographically order exponent vectors. A nonzero polynomial has a greatest exponent in its finite support. For nonzero f,g with greatest exponents alpha,beta, the product's coefficient at alpha+beta is exactly f_alpha*g_beta !=0. Indeed every other pair has alpha'<=alpha and beta'<=beta with one inequality strict; the lex order is translation-invariant and addition preserves these inequalities, so alpha'+beta'<alpha+beta. Thus no other pair contributes at alpha+beta. The same proof for zero variables is the field/domain axiom.

Accordingly F_T has 1!=0 and no zero divisors. Since Phi fixes coefficients, Phi(1)=1, so I_T=ker(Phi) is proper. If fg in I_T, Phi(f)Phi(g)=0, hence one image is zero and f or g belongs to I_T. This proves genuine primality. Equivalently the quotient embeds by [f] |-> Phi(f); the kernel equality proves both well-definedness and injectivity. No UFD property of the quotient, algebraic closure, division by a parameter, localization or density statement is used.

## 4. Identification with the ACTUAL Pc(T)

Read specification: CrownVAlg defines Pc(T) as the ideal generated by both coordinates of each v in T and by minor(u,w) for u,w outside T satisfying SameC(univ minus T,u,w). The latter denotes path-connectivity inside the surviving induced crown graph. It does not mean only original edges, and it does not connect different surviving components.

For any finite graph and any T, partition U by this connectivity relation restricted to U. Reflexivity is the length-zero path at a surviving vertex; symmetry reverses a path; transitivity concatenates paths. Thus each u lies in exactly one equivalence class B_c, all such blocks are nonempty, and there are finitely many because U is finite. A list can be obtained by repeatedly taking any unused vertex and removing its equivalence class; each iteration removes at least one vertex, so this construction terminates after at most |U| iterations. This is an ordinary finite construction, not executed enumeration. It requires no graph classification theorem.

For surviving u,w, SameC holds if and only if u,w belong to the same block, by the definition of an equivalence class and transitivity. Consequently each completed-component generator in the actual Pc(T) is a within-block generator of I_T; conversely every within-block minor is one of its completed-component generators. Coordinate generators agree literally. Both generated-ideal inclusions follow by finite sums of polynomial multiples; self-minors are zero, and reversing the chosen block order only negates a generator. Therefore actual Pc(T)=I_T=ker(Phi), for every T, not just valid cuts. Sections 2 and 3 prove its properness and primality over every field. This does not claim that all T are cut sets or that all such primes are minimal over J.

## 5. Faithful noncoordinate crown specializations, n>=4

Let V=X disjoint union Y, X={a_i}, Y={b_i}, with edges a_i b_j for i!=j. The supplied completed ordinary proof, corrected paper section 2, and completed cut-family specifications agree on the following cuts. Classification exhaustiveness is taken from those authorized completed inputs; the component descriptions needed here can also be checked directly as follows.

Empty cut: the graph is connected. Distinct a_i,a_j have a common b_k with k different from i,j; the same holds on Y. For opposite vertices a_i,b_j with i!=j there is an edge. For the missing matched edge a_i,b_i choose k!=i and l outside {i,k}; a_i,b_k,a_l,b_i is a path. All vertices survive and these paths prove the one block V. Thus P_0=D(V) is the kernel of x_v->s z_v, y_v->t z_v with one row pair for all V.

One-side deletion T=X minus {a_i}: the surviving graph consists of the star block B={a_i} union (Y minus {b_i}), and the isolated vertex b_i. Every leaf in B is adjacent to a_i, so B is connected; b_i has no surviving neighbor. Hence the actual ideal is

Q_i=L(X minus {a_i})+D({a_i} union (Y minus {b_i})).

Use distinct row pairs (s_B,t_B) and (s_iso,t_iso), and separate z_v for every survivor. Map killed coordinates to zero, vertices of B to (s_B z_v,t_B z_v), and b_i to (s_iso z_bi,t_iso z_bi). The kernel theorem proves precisely this Q_i prime; the isolated coordinates have no relation. In particular a_i must be in the large block. For T=Y minus {b_i}, the same direct description with X,Y exchanged gives

R_i=L(Y minus {b_i})+D({b_i} union (X minus {a_i})),

with isolated a_i and separate row pair. The same kernel proof applies; no extra symmetry assertion about formal code is needed.

Mixed cuts: let i!=j and T=V minus {a_i,a_j,b_i,b_j}. The only surviving edges are a_i b_j and a_j b_i. Thus the two actual components are B_1={a_i,b_j} and B_2={a_j,b_i}, and

M_ij=L(T)+([a_i,b_j],[a_j,b_i]).

Use row pairs (s_1,t_1),(s_2,t_2) and four distinct z variables, with the two endpoints of each B_h using its own pair. The kernel is exactly the displayed original ideal. It is the same cut for i,j or j,i; valid mixed families require distinct indices. The cut-family syntax can admit equal indices but that is not a valid mixed cut claim here (and the general all-T theorem still applies with its actual components).

Independent row pairs are essential: for u in B_1 and v in B_2 the cross-block minor maps to z_u z_v(s_1 t_2-s_2 t_1). Its two monomials are distinct and have coefficients 1 and -1, nonzero over every field, including characteristic two. Thus it is not in the kernel. Sharing one row pair would incorrectly kill this polynomial. The same observation separates the star and isolated vertex in Q_i and R_i.

The already treated full-side cuts X and Y have singleton survivor components and coordinate-only ideals. Their ordinary coordinate primality need not be reproved here; the general theorem is consistent with their supplied completed ideal equalities. In every other family above there is at least one block with two vertices. Its minor is a nonzero polynomial entirely in surviving variables, so cannot belong to L(T): the zero-coordinate substitution fixes it. Thus the general Pc(T) must not be replaced by a coordinate ideal alone.
