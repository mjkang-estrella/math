## 355751: counting formulas, then injection, then exact source-to-target bridge

P is a finite poset, labels are bijections to [n], and sorting time is the least nonnegative number of extended promotions giving a linear extension. Inflation phi:P->Q is surjective; each fiber has a unique minimum; different fibers compare exactly as their Q images. Q is a rooted tree with maximal root; leaf fibers are singletons. We may merge consecutive internal unary nodes into fibers, preserving the unique-minimum property, the cross-fiber order condition and singleton leaves. Thus the subposet of internal nodes of Q may be taken reduced as in Remark4.2.

For leaf ell, let u_ell,0=ell,...,u_ell,omega be the path to the root. b_ell,j is the total fiber size at nodes <=u_ell,j-1; c_ell,j is the total fiber size at nodes <u_ell,j. Write
A_ell=product_{j=2..omega}(b_ell,j-1)/(c_ell,j-1),
D_ell=product_{j=2..omega}(b_ell,j-2)/(c_ell,j-2),
E_ell=product_{j=2..omega}((b_ell,j-1)(b_ell,j-2))/((c_ell,j-1)(c_ell,j-2)).
Empty products equal 1. For every occurring j>=2 the predecessor u_ell,j-1 is internal. Reduction of the internal-node tree implies u_ell,j has another internal child; hence b>=2 and c>=b+2>=4. This pays positivity of every denominator and nonnegativity of the numerators. (The T configurations have an even longer initial chain, but b>=2 suffices.) Thus
0 <= (b-2)/(c-2) <= (b-1)/(c-1) <=1,
since the difference of the middle two fractions is (c-b)/((c-1)(c-2)). Multiplying gives 0<=D_ell<=A_ell and E_ell>=0, including empty products.

R comprises minimal elements whose unique parent has multiple-parent-branch context as defined on source p9; T comprises minimal elements whose parent is its own parent's only child; S comprises minimal elements whose parent has precisely two children. These are source sets, not arbitrary subsets. For n>=3 the actual proof's Equation(2), avoiding the ambiguous j=1 zero-over-zero notation of Theorem4.5, gives
Tangled/(n-1)! = sum_{ell in T} A_ell + sum_{ell in R} A_ell.
Theorem4.6 gives
Quasi/(n-1)! = 2 sum_T A_ell - (1/(n-1)) sum_T D_ell + 2 sum_R A_ell + sum_S E_ell.
Consequently
(Quasi-Tangled)/(n-1)! = sum_T (A_ell-D_ell/(n-1)) + sum_R A_ell + sum_S E_ell >=0.
Indeed each T summand is >=(1-1/(n-1)) A_ell>=0. This is an all-parameter bridge wherever the source counting theorem applies, with no n-specific experiment.

For n=1 the sole labeling has time0=n-1, whereas n-2=-1 is not a sorting time. Thus the unqualified one-point reading of the literal fails; that is an endpoint/domain correction, zero credit, not a significant refutation of the intended counting problem.

The location of n-1 can change the branch where promotion pulls down a future label; if n-1 sits at another minimum, the swapped labeling can still have n at a minimum, so the elementary necessary test does not certify sorting time n-2. ## 350310: planar construction, connectivity and root conventions

Read actual original Definition4.1/4.2, cycle-realizer discussion around Fig2, Section7.3 forbidden-cycle passage and exact Conjecture7.10. Chords are perfect matchings of [2n], rooted at the chord incident to1, with cyclic endpoints read in order. Connectivity is intersection-graph connectivity, not merely indecomposability. The odd top/bottom cycles are the two realizers of induced cycles; the source identifies avoiding them with bipartite intersection graph. Figures themselves were not visually audited, so this equivalence was used as an attributed source assertion, not as a new proven classification.

Route A: choose a proper 2-coloring of the connected bipartite intersection graph. Place one chord color inside the Hamiltonian circle and the other outside. Chords of each color do not interlace and can be drawn disjointly. Add consecutive circle edges; every endpoint now has degree3 and the circle is Hamiltonian. Conversely, a planar cubic map with a distinguished Hamiltonian cycle leaves a perfect matching when cycle edges are removed, and inside/outside of its cycle gives two noninterlacing chord classes. This pays the planar/cubic structural idea without computation.

Route A's exact connectivity argument was pursued further: in a graph containing a spanning cycle, every nonempty proper vertex cut has at least two cycle edges. A 2-edge cut must therefore consist of two cycle edges, so the vertices on one side form a cyclic interval closed under the chord matching. No chord then interlaces a chord wholly in the complementary interval, making the intersection graph disconnected (assuming both sides contain a chord). Thus a connected diagram produces no cut of size<=2 in the nondegenerate case. The reverse implication needs the lemma that a disconnected intersection graph has a nonempty proper cyclic interval closed under matching. One can try to take an innermost intersection component: other components lie in complementary gaps of its endpoints, then descend to an innermost such gap. I did not complete a fully stated proof covering nested components and small multigraph conventions. This remains an exact unpaid bridge, not an authenticated all-n theorem.

Route B: cut a rooted cubic planar map open along its distinguished Hamiltonian cycle and recover the two noncrossing matchings. This exposes a separate root/orientation issue. The connected bipartite intersection graph has two global colorings; flipping them swaps inside/outside. A chord diagram's root point is fixed, whereas a conventional rooted planar map may root at any dart, including a chord dart, and may retain an orientation of the surface.
