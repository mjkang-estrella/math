## 334316 and 334317: full source bridge, public resolution

Work in K = F_(q²), q = 3^k, k positive. Let U = {t : t^(q+1)=1}. Then t^q=t^(-1) on U. For a polynomial f(x)=x^r h(x^(q-1)), the power map x↦x^(q-1) has image U and fibers cosets of F_q^*. On each fiber f(xa)=a^r f(x) for a in F_q^*. If h has no zero on U, f is bijective iff gcd(r,q-1)=1 and t↦t^r h(t)^(q-1) is bijective on U: the latter decides distinct image fibers and the former decides bijectivity within each fiber. Also f(0)=0 when all displayed exponents are positive. This proves the criterion rather than merely citing its title. Admissible polynomial exponents are needed; the corpus does not explicitly quantify l. Negative-exponent exploits or term-coincidence wording issues would be correction-only, not substantive new solutions.

For the three trinomials take r=l(q+1)+5, l(q+1)+1, l(q+1)+1 respectively, with h(t)=1+t^5-t^(-1), 1-t^4+t^(-2), 1+t²-t^(-2). These Laurent expressions are evaluated only at nonzero t; one can shift r by multiples of q-1 to obtain ordinary-polynomial presentations on K^*. The induced maps, by t^q=t^(-1), are exactly

1. t^5(1+t^(-5)-t)/(1+t^5-t^(-1)) = (-t^7+t^6+t)/(t^6+t-1).
2. t(1-t^(-4)+t²)/(1-t^4+t^(-2)) = (t^6+t^4-1)/(-t^7+t³+t).
3. t(1+t^(-2)-t²)/(1+t²-t^(-2)) = (-t^5+t³+t)/(t^4+t²-1).

Further r ≡ 2l+5 or 2l+1 modulo q-1, giving the literal gcd conditions. Thus 334316 and 334317 are the same substantive family, not two credits.

## 329987: full exact endpoint, already public

Source definitions actually read: Chen–Reidys arXiv:1502.07674 current v4, Definition 1 and diagonal identity D=sπ^(-1), §6 signed sets and skew symmetry, Conjectures 1/2, concluding acknowledgment of their resolution. A skew-symmetric long cycle is s=(0,a1,…,an,−an,…,−a1) on {−n,…,n}; exact means some ai<0. Set p=(0,−1,…,−n,n,…,1), the same cycle as the source pr up to rotation. D=p^(-1) implies π=p s. This fixes composition order; no inverse convention is silently substituted.

I pursued a full proof rather than a special example. Let R(x)=−x, fixing 0, and write π=(pR)(Rs). Both factors are involutions. The first is (−n,n−1)(−n+1,n−2)…(−1,0)(n), with unique fixed point n. The second is (0,−a1)(a1,−a2)…(a_(n−1),−an)(an), with unique fixed point an. All letters occur once in each decomposition because the signed cycle contains every letter exactly once.

Here is a complete ordinary proof of the needed involution lemma. For two involutions A,B with unique fixed points a,b, draw a colored edge for every nontrivial transposition of each involution. If a=b the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise a and b have degree one and every other vertex degree two, counting the two colors separately. The finite connected component of a is a path: a walk beginning at its unique incident edge cannot branch; it cannot close without making the entry vertex have three incident edge occurrences, and finiteness forces a second endpoint. The only other degree-one vertex is b. Edge colors alternate, the first edge at a has the color of B and the last at b that of A. Consequently the path has even length 2m, and (AB)^m sends a to b (follow first B then A). Thus a,b lie in one cycle of AB. This handles parallel double-colored edges too: those form isolated length-two cycles, never the component of a. Applying the lemma with A=pR,B=Rs proves n and an are in one cycle of π.

This proves the stronger endpoint without the exactness or displayed π(s_(i−1)) condition. Every reserved instance satisfies the weaker assumptions, so its conclusion follows. But Bura–Chen–Reidys arXiv:1602.00778, Lemmas 4.2–4.5 and Corollary 4.1 (lines 344–507), already contains exactly this involution factorization and endpoint. The current original source also explicitly acknowledges the proof. The alternative path argument is a classical reformulation, not a new result. No credit; no dormant Pi candidate is warranted.

## 334915 (and 334914): spectral route sustained, exact gap remains

Actually read: Bogosel–Bonnaillie-Noël arXiv:1612.07296v2, definitions (1.1)–(1.3), Theorem 1.1, Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.2, definition (3.3), conclusions Conjectures 6.1/6.2; the journal text is separately acquired. Partitions have k connected open disjoint cells and the strong covering condition; energies are NORMALIZED power means of first Dirichlet eigenvalues. The literal 'domain' cannot be used to manufacture a disconnected-domain counterexample ignoring this source scope.

Let M_p(x)=(k^(-1)Σx_i^p)^(1/p), x_i>0, and F(p)=min_D M_p(λ(D)), using source existence. For 1≤p<q<∞, Jensen applied to u↦u^(q/p), strictly convex, gives M_p≤M_q, with equality iff all x_i coincide. Suppose F(p)=F(q). Choose a q-minimizer D_q. Then F(p)≤M_p(D_q)≤M_q(D_q)=F(q)=F(p). Equality in Jensen forces D_q to be an equipartition. Its common eigenvalue c equals F(q); hence F(∞)≤c, while F(q)≤F(∞). It follows that F(∞)=c, and monotonicity gives F(r)=c for every r≥p. This is a complete helper theorem, including minimizer existence and equality conditions, and it also shows that any failure of strict increase forces a constant tail.

However, this does NOT show p∞∈{1,∞}. It leaves open a finite transition p0>1 from strict increase to a constant tail. Monotonicity alone is insufficient. Explicit abstract obstruction to that inference: minimize power means over the two positive vectors (1,3) and (5/2,5/2). The first has M1=2<5/2 and limit M∞=3>5/2. Its power mean is continuous and strictly increasing, so there is a finite p0>1 at which it crosses 5/2. Their minimum increases below p0 and is constant above it. This is an illustrative analytic construction, NOT an in-scope spectral counterexample. No mathematical enumeration was used.

A second route is to show that every finite-p optimal equipartition is already 1-optimal, perhaps by shape variation across an interface. First variations at an equipartition have proportional weights for every finite p, so stationarity transfers to p=1, but stationarity does not imply global minimality. No valid global comparison or signed second-variation argument has been obtained. The precise missing lemma is: a regular finite-p minimal equipartition of a connected planar domain minimizes the average first eigenvalue. Proving only the source Proposition 3.2 (larger q) does not give this backward implication.
