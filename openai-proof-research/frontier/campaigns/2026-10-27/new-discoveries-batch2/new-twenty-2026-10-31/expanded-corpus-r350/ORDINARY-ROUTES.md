## 375782: minor exclusion and CFI witnesses

Exact endpoint: for every proper minor-closed disjoint-union-closed class F of finite simple graphs, exhibit nonisomorphic X,Y with hom(A,X)=hom(A,Y) for every A in F.

First route: choose a missing connected graph H and hope every weak oddomorphism onto H forces an H minor. The July paper gives counterexamples to that implication. Its split-off-minor replacement is insufficient: closure under ordinary minors need not imply closure under splitting off length-two paths. Even on a planar star, splitting repeatedly and contractions can create structural relations not available by the same branch sets. The published October strong-conjecture refutation likewise does not itself refute the weak endpoint; homomorphism-distinguishing closure is stronger than failing to distinguish all graphs.

Second route: it suffices to exclude oddomorphisms onto a sufficiently large clique depending on the missing minor, rather than onto each individual H. This is a different, weaker structural target, and survives the first obstruction. The current author page points to the October 14 public Zenodo working paper 23370177. Its actual PDF states Theorem 1.3: for each t>=1, some N>=2 admits no weak oddomorphism from any K_t-minor-free graph. Theorem 1.2 is the CFI count criterion. The paper's proof uses bounded-width vortex parity groups, surface structure, minor models, apices and clique sums. I read the endpoint statements, oddomorphism definitions, initial CFI deduction, outline and final Theorem 10.6/1.3 assembly; I did not independently verify every intermediate topological/group proof.

Complete published-theorem-to-literal bridge: choose H not in F, and put t=max(1,|V(H)|). If K_t belonged to F, its subgraph H (including isolated vertices, with the empty case treated separately) would belong to F. Thus no member A of F can contain K_t as a minor, since F is minor closed. Apply public Theorem 1.3 to t, obtaining N. Let X and Y be the even and odd CFI graphs over connected K_N. For every A in F, the criterion says unequal counts occur exactly when A has a weak oddomorphism to K_N. Hence all counts are equal. The identity on K_N is an oddomorphism, so the criterion gives hom(K_N,X)!=hom(K_N,Y); isomorphic graphs would have all incoming hom counts equal, so X and Y are nonisomorphic. If F is empty, any two nonisomorphic graphs work directly. The disjoint-union condition is permitted but unnecessary for this stronger public theorem.

This is already public before this investigation; no first-merge chronology or independent priority claim. EXCLUDE, zero. No dormant Pi plan is warranted for duplicating it.

## 368354/368355: separators, quasi-isometry, and induced minors

A (k,r)-centred set S is covered by k radius-r balls in the ambient graph. For every nonnegative vertex weighting, balancing requires every component of G-S to have at most half of the total weight. Exact 368354 requires k depending only on each planar H. Exact 368355 requires k,q depending only on H, for every H-induced-minor-free G.

First attempt: obtain bounded ordinary treewidth from induced-minor exclusion and use its balanced bag. This fails in scope: dense graphs may have huge ordinary treewidth but bounded diameter/coarse treewidth. K_n has arbitrary treewidth and diameter one. It is quasi-isometric to a point with bounded additive distortion; its whole vertex set is one closed neighbourhood and is a balanced separator. Thus large clique/treewidth is not a coarse obstruction or a witness against either claim.

Second attempt: use Conjecture 5 (368354) as input to the source Theorem 4, then Theorem 1 to obtain 368355. Heredity is valid: any induced subgraph of an H-induced-minor-free G remains H-induced-minor-free; otherwise concatenate the vertex deletions/contractions. Thus a proved uniform k(H) would apply to every induced subgraph. However Theorem 4 also assumes G is K_t,t-induced-subgraph-free. General H-induced-minor-free graphs need not satisfy any bounded t exclusion: take H=P4 and G=K_t,t for arbitrary t. Every induced minor of K_t,t is a join of a clique (the branch sets meeting both original sides) with a complete bipartite graph (the remaining singleton branch sets); vertex deletion preserves this form. Such graphs have no induced P4: any clique vertex is universal, and a complete bipartite graph has no induced P4. Therefore none has P4 as an induced minor. This valid example only disproves automatic biclique exclusion; it does not disprove the conjecture, since these graphs have diameter at most two and the P4-free case is known. Even without this additional restriction, using separator existence to prove separator existence is circular. A complete implication in the biclique-free subclass is source routine, zero credit.

Third attempt: compress large-diameter dense pieces before applying the biclique-free theorem. Bounded diameter pieces can collapse with bounded distortion, but no bounded-diameter partition with an H-dependent distortion and a biclique-free quotient was constructed. Arbitrary contractions can create extra adjacency and lose the radius-one separator conclusion. Merely passing to a distance graph requires a controlled transfer of all weightings and induced-minor obstructions. The companion arXiv:2502.20182 proves radius reductions and logarithmic-in-order approximations; its displayed radius r(log k+log log n+log 12) is not independent of n. Theorem 1 of 2501.10840 translates already centred decompositions to quasi-isometry; it supplies no decomposition from the forbidden H alone.

The June 2026 companion arXiv:2606.14974 Theorem 1.5 improves the radius and polynomial parameter dependence for K_t,t-induced-minor-free graphs with bounded clique number AND assumed (k,r)-separability. Reading the actual hypotheses prevents using it as the missing separator-existence theorem. No current full subsumption or new proof is claimed.

Precise gaps: prove separator existence from arbitrary planar induced-minor exclusion, or independently produce centred decompositions with uniformly bounded parameters. For the compression alternative, supply a quotient construction with explicit uniform metric inequalities, a valid obstruction-transfer lemma, and a lift of every quotient separator to boundedly many radius-one balls. No generic solver plan replaces these gaps. HOLD both exact endpoints.

## 371332: signed paths beyond the source shortcut

Signed induced containment includes vertex switching. Balanced sets have positive sign on every cycle. For a fixed linear forest F the endpoint is a uniform bound on the number of balanced classes in every signed graph avoiding switching copies of all-negative K4 and induced F. Forest signs are switch equivalent, so underlying induced paths are the relevant objects.

First route: imitate the negative-triangle path-growing argument. With no switching-negative triangle, each closed neighbourhood is balanced; repeated removal of neighbourhoods loses a controlled amount of balanced chromatic number and leaves a long induced path. K4 exclusion does not give balanced neighbourhoods. Source item 23 proves the conditional bound chi_b <= b*2^(k-3) for P_k-free graphs with all closed neighbourhoods b-balanced-colorable. Invoking it with b=1 would assume the missing property.

Second route: normalize all edges incident with u positive. A negative triangle inside N(u) together with u is switching-equivalent to all-negative K4: switch u in the all-negative K4. A K4 with precisely a negative matching is also switching-equivalent to all-negative K4: switch two vertices, preserving negative edges within the two pairs. Thus N(u) excludes these two FIXED signatures. It does not exclude every switching of a negative triangle. The induced negative-edge graph is triangle-free, but triangle-freeness alone does not bound its chromatic number. Nor does underlying P_k-freeness simply imply P_k-freeness of the negative-edge graph: positive chords disappear when taking negative edges. Conversely a negative-edge proper coloring only gives an upper bound on chi_b; failure to color that negative graph would not prove large chi_b. This avenue does not close the endpoint.

The reverse universal-vertex construction, source item 26, shows that proving a uniform balanced bound for the fixed-sign forbidden class would close the neighbourhood gap. An all-positive universal vertex cannot lie in an induced P_k for k>=4, since its degree within such a path would exceed two; small k need their own conventions. This establishes why the fixed-sign reformulation is essentially the remaining hard lemma rather than a resolution.

Third route: use full-join decomposition. If A join B contains a negative edge xy in A, partition vertices of B into negative neighbours of x, remaining negative neighbours of y, and common positive neighbours. The first two parts have no internal negative edge, else a fixed all-negative triangle; the last has no internal negative edge, else xy plus that edge is K4 with a negative matching. Hence B's negative-edge graph is 3-colorable. If both sides contain negative edges, disjoint palettes give six colors in the whole negative-edge graph and hence six balanced colors. This is the complete explicit source Lemma 27/28 bridge. P4-free graphs admit recursive union/join decompositions, so this helps the source short-path cases. Arbitrary P_k-free graphs for k>4 need not have such a decomposition, and no replacement decomposition with bounded losses was derived.

The unpaid statement is a b_k bound for chi_b of every fixed-sign {negative K3, negative-matching K4, P_k}-free signed graph, followed by the source path and forest-assembly lemmas. Source item 29 also requires common-neighbour bounds to combine forest components. These requirements cannot be omitted. Source abstract versus conclusion uses inconsistent path length/order wording; that discrepancy is a correction, not a new proof. HOLD; no qualified ordinary endpoint.
