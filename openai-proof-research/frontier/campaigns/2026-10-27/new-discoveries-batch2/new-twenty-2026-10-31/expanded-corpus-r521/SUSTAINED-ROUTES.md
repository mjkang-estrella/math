## 336091: arbitrary randomized matching to asymptotic deterministic matching
Actual primary: 1705.06631v1, header June 25 2018, arXiv stamp May 18 2017, Section 4 pp15–21, Conjecture 14, Lemmas16–21 and the component concentration proof. These dates are preserved rather than silently reconciled. The weights are nonnegative; the game denominator is opt_k=max weight of a matching of cardinality at most k. A matching's profile is the sum of its k largest weights. The quantifier needed is K(epsilon) independent of graph, weights and support size. Fixing a graph first is insufficient.

Route A, empirical sparsification. Let X_M(k)=w(M_k)/opt_k in [0,1] (zero denominator constraints may be omitted). For L independent samples from a distribution, Hoeffding and the union bound give simultaneous additive epsilon approximation at the finitely many relevant cardinalities with L at least log(2|E|/delta)/(2epsilon^2). This is an ordinary symbolic inequality, not an experiment. It does not yield the required constant support. A geometric grid in k improves the number of constraints: if k<=j<=(1+eta)k, concavity of each prefix-sum profile and of the optimal matching value gives controlled interpolation. Nevertheless the number of grid points grows with log |E|. The resulting support still depends on the graph. A constant-support theorem needs an additional uniform entropy or one-sided approximation lemma for these normalized profiles. Monotonicity of w(M_k) alone does not give monotonicity of its ratio to opt_k.

Route B, repeated source merging. Source Lemma17 merges two profiles with a loss factor 1-epsilon and a cutoff depending only on epsilon. A balanced binary merge of L support atoms has depth ceil(log_2 L). Setting the per-level loss epsilon/depth bounds the final relative loss, but now K depends on L. A sequential merge is worse. Finite support for each fixed graph does not commute with the outer infimum over all graphs. Thus Lemma17 alone does not prove Conjecture14; this is exactly where source Lemma16 uses the special logarithmic rounding distribution. Replacing Lemma16 with ordinary sampling does not remove that dependence.

Route C, simultaneous component rounding. For TWO matchings the symmetric difference has degree at most two and consists of paths and cycles. The source erases cheap edges, splits long components, then removes components heavy relative to top-K weight by whole-component exchange. The remaining bounded components permit concentration simultaneously in all large cardinalities. For a union of L matchings the degree is up to L, alternating paths are no longer independent components and choosing independently among them can create shared endpoints. Fractional matching marginals do not determine expected top-k values: top-k is a nonlinear truncation of the selected edge multiset. A rounding theorem preserving all truncated-weight profiles with error tending to zero in K, independently of support size, remains unpaid. This is a genuine missing theorem, not a failure of a numerical search.

Disposition PARK. No uniform sparsification, no support-free rounding and no full equality proof. The source proves only its special-distribution lower guarantee, not the conjecture. No claim that the conjecture is false or already publicly solved. Formal feasibility would require finite weighted matching profiles, optimization over distributions and a uniform analytic limiting statement; no small finite certificate is available.

## 370014: neighborly boxes limit
Actual primary: 2508.20648v1 pp1–9 read, including Conjectures1–2, normalized boxes and ternary strings, all construction lemmas and both asymptotic theorems. The proved limit is for k=d-s with fixed s; our assigned limit holds k fixed. Substituting s=d-k into the fixed-s limit is invalid. The text itself notes a version difference in the Cheng-Wang-Xu-Yip upper bound; that attributed input was not independently fetched or audited.

A code is a family in {0,1,*}^d with 1<=dist(x,y)<=k for distinct members, where only opposite binary symbols count. All subsequent arguments refer to this exact distance, not ordinary Hamming distance.

Route A, Fekete. Concatenation gives n(k1+k2,d1+d2)>=n(k1,d1)n(k2,d2): a pair distinct in one factor has positive distance and distances add. This changes k, so Fekete on dimension does not apply to fixed k. Repeating a k-code t times in a product yields tk-neighborliness, not k-neighborliness. Appending joker coordinates gives mere monotonicity in d. Monotonicity plus Theta(d^k) does not establish convergence after division by d^k.

Route B, coordinate sampling. Projecting a code to q coordinates never increases distance. A pair can collapse to distance zero if every coordinate at which it has an opposite binary symbol is deleted. If that pair has just one conflict, its survival probability under a uniformly random q-subset of coordinates is q/d. Therefore a universal argument assuming survival probability (q/d)^k for all pair relations has the wrong premise. The issue is extracting a large subfamily without zero-distance pairs, not bounding the diameter. A quantitative extraction retaining approximately (q/d)^k times the code cardinality would give useful normalized comparisons, but no such extraction is proved here.

Route C, coordinate inflation. Duplicating each coordinate multiplies the number of conflicts for each pair and breaks the k budget. Replacing each bit by a 1-neighborly code could in principle add internal choices, but a source word may have many specified coordinates. Independent choices there create additional conflicts even for words sharing a base symbol; k already may be saturated by pairs with k opposite coordinates. What is needed is an assignment of at most k effective choice positions per word, compatible for ALL word pairs, together with an inflation from d to roughly td and cardinality roughly t^k. Neither a universal choice-position certificate nor a substitute inflation is available. The source near-diagonal construction does not supply it.

Disposition PARK at a scaling/extraction theorem. This would be a substantive full-scope result if proved, unlike reproducing k=1 or the known near-diagonal asymptotics. No finite enumeration or mathematical code was used.

## 357454: sharp lower half versus full conjunction
Actual primary: 2302.08557v1 pp1–6 definitions/Conjecture8 and Section4 pp11–12 proof of Theorem7 read. Other oriented/multicolour passages were read as documented in runner outputs; material figures were NOT visually inspected. The literal contains BOTH a sharp finite trigonometric lower bound and an asymptotic upper bound when maximum degree tends to infinity. A proof of the first is not a full survivor.

Route A, finite zonogon refinement (author ordinary lower-half argument). Let T have l>=2 leaves and at least three vertices. Its l leaf edges are distinct. Remove them to leave a connected core (possibly one vertex). For any unit complex edge colouring, write c for the sum on the core and u_1,...,u_l for the leaf-edge colours. Every c+sum_{i in A}u_i is the sum on a connected subtree, for A any leaf subset; the singleton core has sum zero when needed. Their convex hull is the translated zonogon Z=c+sum_i[0,u_i]. If every subtree sum has norm at most D, convexity puts Z inside the disk of radius D.

If the generators are not all parallel, Z has m<=2l vertices and perimeter exactly 2 sum_i|u_i|=2l: sort the generator directions modulo pi; each segment direction appears once on each boundary chain, with parallel generators combined. A convex m-gon in a radius-D disk has perimeter at most 2mD sin(pi/m). One elementary justification is to maximize perimeter over m points in the closed disk. Perimeter of their convex hull is a convex function of each point (by the planar width integral), so a maximum can be moved to the boundary one point at a time. For boundary points in cyclic order the chord lengths are 2D sin(theta_i/2), sum theta_i=2pi, and Jensen gives the bound. Fewer vertices only decrease the maximum since x sin(pi/x) increases for x>=2: its derivative is sin t-t cos t>=0 for 0<=t<=pi/2. Thus 2l<=4lD sin(pi/(2l)), and D>=1/(2 sin(pi/(2l))). If all generators are parallel, the zonogon is a segment of length l, whose containment gives D>=l/2; concavity of sine on [0,pi/2] gives l sin(pi/(2l))>=1, so this is stronger. The two-vertex tree has D=1 and satisfies the l=2 lower bound separately. Taking the minimum over colourings proves the sharp LOWER HALF for every nontrivial tree.

This is a conventional convex-geometric strengthening of the source leaf-subset argument. The source uses the same core and leaf deletion, averaging projected lengths and the triangle inequality. The zonogon replaces the circular perimeter bound by the finite polygon bound. There is no new tree decomposition or colouring construction. Strongest positive case: it proves the exact deliberately conjectured finite constant rather than only its leading term. Strongest objection: one estimate in the source geometry changes; AND the upper half is unpaid. No credit or full-candidate promotion is proposed.

Route B, propagate a regular-polygon colouring. On a star, evenly spread unit directions control all subset sums. On a general tree one may alternate colours along each degree-two path, keeping partial sums small. At a branching vertex, different incident paths need a common phase choice to control all connected unions; controlling each branch separately by its own optimal phase does not control their Minkowski sum. Many branching vertices remain even if ONE vertex has unbounded degree. No all-subtree phase-compatibility invariant with total error o(l) was found.

Route C, grafting obstruction. A possible refutation of the upper half would start with a bounded-degree family whose discrepancy is at least (1/pi+eta)l, and attach an o(l)-leaf star of degree tending to infinity. Discrepancy is monotone under taking the original tree as a subgraph; total leaves change by o(l), so such a family would refute the degree-qualified upper half. But no bounded-degree family with this stronger constant has been proved. A fixed small gadget alone is insufficient: discrepancy of connected sums is not known to be additive, since edge colours may rotate independently and cancel. Thus this is an explicit unpaid obstruction, not a counterexample.

Disposition PARK full target, lower half only. Formal cost includes complex vectors, convex hulls and polygon geometry; it is inferior to a finite natural-number witness for current kernel constraints.
