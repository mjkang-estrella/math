                                         Abstract

    A base b junction number u has the property that there are at least two
ways to write it as u = v + s(v), where s(v) is the sum of the digits in the
expansion of the number v in base b. For the base 10 case, Kaprekar in the
1950’s and 1960’s studied the problem of finding K(n), the smallest u such
that the equation u = v + s(v) has exactly n solutions. He gave the values
K(2) = 101, K(3) = 1013 + 1, and conjectured that K(4) = 1024 + 102. In
1966 Narasinga Rao gave the uppe
