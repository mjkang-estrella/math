## 361986 / 361987: exact image of the Rademacher-difference map
Primary: Xiao-Jie Zhu, Explicit formulae for linear characters of Gamma_0(N), arXiv:2402.14796v2 (3 June 2025). Read actual Psi definition (1), Proposition 2.1, Corollary 2.3, Section 3 including homomorphism proof, and Section 6.1/6.2 conjectures with table. Source Section 5 not fully read; no whole-body audit. Actual source still presents Conjectures 6.5 and 6.6. Native search yielded no authenticated matching later proof; this is not a current status certification.

Psi is the modified Rademacher function, not any unqualified standard Psi. For gamma=(a b;c d), c>0,
 Psi(gamma)=(a+d)/c+12s(-d,c)-3,
and conjugation by B_l=diag(1,l) sends gamma to (a lb;c/l d). Hence sigma_{N,l}=Psi(gamma)-Psi(B_l^{-1}gamma B_l). The source proves it is a homomorphism and sigma(T)=1-l. Beta(N,l)>0 is the positive generator of its image.

Route A, explicit generators and cusps. T is in every Gamma_0(N), so beta(N,l) divides l-1. Since Gamma_0(N) is a subgroup of Gamma_0(l), beta(l,l) divides beta(N,l). For U_c=(1 0;c 1), with c>0 divisible by N, s(1,c)=(c-1)(c-2)/(12c). This follows directly by summing r/c*(r/c-1/2), using the integer sums of r and r^2. Consequently Psi(U_c)=-c and sigma(U_c)=-(c/l)(l-1). Thus these obvious two cusp generators yield only multiples of l-1 and cannot prove the proposed smaller beta values. Conjugating parabolics requires nontrivial Dedekind-sum periods; simply adding cusp widths does not manufacture a unit period. Exact gap: a controlled family of determinant-one matrices at EVERY extra level whose sigma values have the predicted gcd, not a finite check of levels.

Route B, transfer. Let G=Gamma_0(l), H=Gamma_0(N), d=[G:H]. Given the integer homomorphism sigma on G, the group transfer V:G_ab->H_ab satisfies sigma|H(V(g))=d*sigma(g). One proves this by choosing right coset representatives, writing the transfer factors t*g*t'^{-1}, applying sigma, and cancelling the sum of sigma(t) with the permuted sum of sigma(t'). Therefore beta(N,l) divides d*beta(l,l). With the preceding bounds,
 beta(l,l) | beta(N,l) | gcd(l-1,d*beta(l,l)).
This proves dependence in the special case gcd(d,(l-1)/beta(l,l))=1. It does NOT prove the full statement: the index can share those primes. It also does not supply the diagonal beta. These are helpers only, no credit. Unpaid lemma: rule out a proper cyclic congruence quotient of the period homomorphism after imposing the extra lower-left divisibility.

Route C, eta multipliers and maximal roots. By source equation (3), the eta-ratio multiplier of eta(l*tau)/eta(tau) is exp(-2pi*i*sigma/24), with the same factor c*tau+d in the two transformations. Thus standard eta multipliers should control sigma mod24. The printed table has a compact proposed form:
 B(l)=gcd(l-1,24) when l is a square, and gcd(l-1,12) otherwise.
Checking the table symbolically: all residues other than 1,9 have gcd(l-1,24)=gcd(l-1,12); squares mod24 can only have residues 0,1,4,9,12,16, and the exceptional changes in residues1,9 match the printed factors2. This is a restatement of the actual table, not a proof that beta=B.
Even a complete multiplier formula mod24 only bounds gcd(beta,24); T bounds beta by l-1, which may have primes>3 and higher valuations. To conclude beta=B requires ruling out extra divisibility of all integral periods, equivalently maximal roots of this modular unit on X_0(N) with the appropriate character. Source Remark6.4 cites Newman1959; actual Newman theorem/proof was not acquired/read, so no classical prior exclusion is claimed. Precise gap: prove the maximal period/root assertion for arbitrary N,l, including powers of2/3 and all extra primes. A shortcut based only on the usual eta-quotient sufficient criterion would be invalid and scientifically routine even if applicable. No full claim survives.

Lean endpoint: define Gamma_0(N) as the subgroup of SL(2,Z) with N|c, define the FINITE rational Dedekind sum and piecewise integer Psi exactly, then prove image equality sigma(Gamma_0(N))=B(l)Z. Mathlib has matrices, determinant, SL(2,Z)/modular-group infrastructure, finite sums and subgroup/range machinery; exact availability/names of this modified Psi and Dedekind-sum results were not checked. They cannot be assumed. A literal theorem must prove integrality, homomorphism, all sign/c=0 branches and the image-generator bridge. A conditional theorem about an arbitrary integer homomorphism is not the endpoint. Analytic eta/modular-unit route would be substantially more expensive than finite period certificates. No reason to dispatch without the unpaid arithmetic lemma.

## 359519: coprimality in the large-index construction
Primary: Esser and Totaro, Log canonical pairs with conjecturally minimal volume, arXiv:2308.08034v4, 22 May 2026. Actual Section3 definitions, Proposition4.1/Lemma4.2 and their descending induction proof, Section7 definitions (7.1),(7.2), Proposition7.3 proof portion and Conjecture7.4 were read. Source proofs of unrelated geometric claims and whole article not audited. It is the LARGE-INDEX m'_n,E_n conjecture, not the small-mld last-weight theorem, which the very same source already proves. No conflation or credit for the latter.

For a list i1,...,ik define B=b_i1*...*b_ik-b_i1*...*b_i(k-1)+...+(-1)^k. Initial b_i=s_i (Sylvester numbers) for 0<=i<=r; the remaining b follow the explicit zigzag recurrence in Section3. All use integer products, not geometric abstractions.

Route A, naive Euclidean algorithm. For n=2r+1 set P=b1*...*b2r, C=B_{1,2r,...,r,r+1}, D=B_{r+1,r,...,2r,1}. Source (7.1) gives E=(P+1)/2, and the definition of b' gives b'_{2r+1}=E+D, because s1-1=2. Equation(7.2) gives m'=2(E+D)C-1. Thus
                        gcd(E,m')=gcd(E,2DC-1).
The tempting assertion that m' is 1 or -1 mod E is false as a symbolic deduction: it leaves the factor DC. No prime can divide E and P, but DC is not P. This is the exact failure of the one-step coprimality shortcut.

Route B, exploit the small-mld product theorem. In the SAME dimension pair, C=m_{2r}, D=u_{2r}; Proposition4.1 at k=1 gives
                        2CD-1=P*v_{2r}.
Since P=2E-1 is coprime to E, the preceding reduction strengthens to the exact identity
                    gcd(E_{2r+1},m'_{2r+1})=gcd(E_{2r+1},v_{2r}).
This is proved by the two congruences and Euclid's lemma, without computational testing. It is not a coprimality proof: Proposition4.1 only forces C,D to be invertible modulo a hypothetical common prime, and allows v_{2r}=0 there. Thus the known small-mld theorem does not automatically settle the large-index conjecture. Unpaid lemma: v_{2r} coprime to (P+1)/2, uniformly in r. A proof only for odd n would still leave full literal unpaid.

Route C, descending zigzag invariants and possible resultant obstruction. The source proof gives b_k*w_k=(s_k-1)^2*g_k+w_{k+1}, and g_k-s_k*g_{k+1}=(s_k-1)*P_inner*w_{k+1}. If p divides E and w1=v_{2r}, these force w2=-4g1 mod p and 2g1*t1=1 mod p, while P=-1 mod p. Thus p does not divide g1,t1 (and p!=2). This propagates relations, not zeros: one cannot infer p divides w2, since the nonzero g1 term remains. The Sylvester prime-factor disjointness controls factors of products b_i, whereas E is product-plus-one. It does not rule out these residue solutions. A successful descent needs an invariant eliminating the inhomogeneous g terms, or a resultant proving any simultaneous divisor divides a fixed unit. No such invariant was found. For even dimensions E=(8*b2*...*b_{2r-1}+1)/3 and m'=3*b'_{2r}*B_{2,2r-1,...,r,r+1}-2; division by3 requires its own p=3 case. An odd-dimension reduction cannot silently pay it. No finite or numerical program was run.

Significance test: the source proves closely related product identities, so an extension merely changing the last exponent in the same induction would face the one-estimate/parameter-adaptation objection. Our actual reduction exposes a distinct unpaid product-plus-one coprimality statement. It is not a full result and not evidence of novelty. Search did not authenticate any exact public theorem for it; open source label is not status clearance.

Lean endpoint: for every n>=2, Nat.Coprime (mPrime n) (E n), after defining Sylvester numbers, the finite zigzag index lists, B, and all odd/even recurrences with division integrality. Natural/integer gcd, coprime, recursive sequences, finite products/lists exist in mathlib; these specific sequences are project definitions and all identification bridges are unpaid. No geometry is necessary for the EXACT literal coprimality statement; deriving quotient index would additionally require the source geometric theorem. Literal kernel endpoint is plausible arithmetic if the missing induction exists. No conditional coprimality theorem or finitely many dimensions substitutes for all n.

## Arithmetic wording erratum
The sentence above asserting gcd(l-1,24)=gcd(l-1,12) in every residue other than1,9 is false at residue17: those gcds are8 and4. Residue17 never contains a square. The compact B(l) formula is still a faithful restatement of Table2, since nonsquare residue17 takes gcd(l-1,12)=4.
