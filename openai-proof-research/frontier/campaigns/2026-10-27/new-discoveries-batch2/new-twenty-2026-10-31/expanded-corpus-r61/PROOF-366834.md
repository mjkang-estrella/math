# MathDB 366834 — refutation of the caterpillar maximum-centre conjecture

Status: candidate refutation with a complete Lean endpoint. Zero credit until independent review and root acceptance.

## Source and exact statement

- MathDB 366834, "The caterpillar maximum-center neighbor conjecture"; raw line and hashes in `extracted.json` and `raw/366834.jsonl`.
- Source: G. Hurlbert, *A Survey of the Holroyd–Talbot Conjecture*, arXiv:2501.16144v4 (22 Aug 2026), Section 4, Conjecture 39, attributed to Zhang, Ran and Huang, *On the star of the family of independent sets in a graph*, Discrete Appl. Math. 344 (2024) 170–178 (reference [73]). Fetched copy: `sources/2501.16144.html` (receipt alongside).
- Literal text of Conjecture 39: "If T is a caterpillar, r ≥ 1, and z is a maximum r-center, then the neighbor of z has maximum degree."
- Definitions (survey, Section 1): 𝓘^r(G) is the family of independent r-sets of G; 𝓘^r_v(G) is the star of sets containing v; v is a maximum r-center when |𝓘^r_v(G)| is maximum over all vertices.
- A caterpillar is a tree in which every vertex is on, or adjacent to, a central path (equivalently, deleting all leaves leaves a path).

## The witness

Spine v0 – v1 – v2 – v3 – v4, with pendant leaves 5 at v0; 6 and 7 at v1; 8 at v2; 9 and 10 at v4. There are 11 vertices and 10 edges.

- Degrees: v0 = 2, v1 = 4, v2 = 3, v3 = 2, v4 = 3, leaves = 1. So Δ = 4, attained only at v1.
- Take r = 6. The independence number is 7 (the six leaves plus v3), so r < α.
- Star sizes |𝓘^6_v| for v = 0, …, 10: 7, 1, 2, 12, 0, 8, 13, 13, 12, 14, 14.
- The maximum 6-centres are exactly the leaves 9 and 10. Each has the single neighbour v4, of degree 3 < 4 = Δ.

## Ordinary proof

1. W is a tree: it is connected (every vertex is joined to v0 along the spine plus at most one pendant edge) and has 11 vertices and 10 edges. It is a caterpillar with spine v0 … v4, since every leaf is adjacent to a spine vertex.
2. Star sizes. For a leaf z with neighbour w, |𝓘^6_z| = i_5(W − z − w), where i_5 counts independent 5-sets. A direct count gives the eleven values above. Example: for z = 9, W − {9, v4} is the forest made of the path v0 v1 v2 v3 with leaves 5, 6, 7, 8 and the isolated vertex 10, and its number of independent 5-sets is 14. The Lean file checks all eleven values exhaustively by kernel computation over the 462 six-subsets.
3. So max_v |𝓘^6_v| = 14, attained exactly at 9 and 10. Their only neighbour v4 has degree 3, while deg v1 = 4.

Hence Conjecture 39 fails for T = W and r = 6.

## Robustness

- Universal reading ("every maximum r-centre z and every neighbour of z: the neighbour has degree Δ"): refuted.
- Existential reading ("for each r ≥ 1 some maximum r-centre has a neighbour of degree Δ"): refuted too, because no maximum 6-centre is adjacent to v1.
- This is not an endpoint artefact. The literal statement already fails trivially at r = 1 (all vertices tie) and at r = 2 (|𝓘^2_v| = n − 1 − deg v, so every leaf is a maximum 2-centre). Those failures would only be wording/endpoint corrections and are **not** the claimed refutation. The witness has 3 ≤ r = 6 < α = 7, its maximum centres are unique up to the swap 9 ↔ 10, and it defeats the charitable existential reading.
- Diagnostic scans (`scripts/caterpillar_dp.py`, logs `logs/cat-dp-*.log`, outputs in `outputs/`): among all caterpillars with n ≤ 11 this is the unique strong failure up to reversal, so it is the minimum order. For n ≤ 19 there are 5200 strong failures. They include the apparent infinite pattern with leaf counts (m − 1, m, m − 1, 0, m), which fails at fixed r = 5 while α = 4m − 1 grows (m = 3, 4 checked). This is a diagnostic only; no general proof of the family is claimed.
