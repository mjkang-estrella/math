223:                                  2. Log-concavity of counts of flats
224:   For a matroid M, let Wi denote the number of flats of M of rank i. These are known as the Whitney
225: numbers of the second kind.
226: Conjecture 2.1. Let M be a matroid of rank r. Then for any 1 ≤ i ≤ r − 1, we have Wi2 ≥ Wi+1 Wi−1 .
227:    This was conjectured by Mason in [Mas72, Conjecture 1B], where he also conjectured two stronger versions
228: predicting the ultra log-concavity of the Wi . The weaker statement that the Wi are unimodal was conjectured
229: by Rota in [Rot71]. Conjecture 2.1 is Problem 25(c) in Stanley’s list of positivity problems and conjectures
230: in algebraic combinatorics [Sta00]. There, he also mentions the log-concavity conjectures for the coefficients
231: of the characteristic polynomial of a matroid and for the number of independent sets of a given size in
232: a matroid. He speculates that all three of these conjectures are false. However, the log-concavity of the
233: coefficients of the characteristic polynomial and the number of independent sets were proven in [AHK18].
234:    One case that has attracted particular attention is log-concavity at W2 : that W22 ≥ W1 W3 . This is known
235: as the points-lines-planes conjecture, although this is sometimes used to refer to the stronger conjectures
236:                                         1 −1
237: that W22 ≥ 32 W1 W3 or that W22 ≥ 32 W W1 −2 W1 W3 . This strongest form was proven by Seymour for matroids
238: where each flat of rank 2 has size at most four [Sey95]. In particular, this applies to all binary matroids.
239: This case was previously proven for graphic matroids in [Sto75], and it was subsequently studied by Dukes
240: [Duk08].
241:    In contrast to Conjecture 1.1, there are very few families of matroids for which Conjecture 2.1 is known.
242: It holds trivially for paving matroids, and it is easy to check for matroids associated to the complete graph
243: or projective geometries. See [Aig87] for a survey and a few additional cases.
