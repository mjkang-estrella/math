## 360951: C-vine ideals — full counting mechanism, unqualified source mismatch

The literal record and arXiv v2 Conjecture 7.11 use dimension n and sum from i=0 to n of ((i+1)^(n-i)-i^(n-i)). Definitions 3.4, 3.6–3.7, 3.12–3.15 and 7.1 include singleton nodes and ordinary downward-closed subsets, including the empty ideal. Already the single-node C-vine has two ideals, whereas the literal formula gives one. This negative endpoint is an indexing/domain correction and earns zero. The journal Conjecture 7.11 instead says rank ell and the sum to ell+1 with exponent ell+1-i. Its own rank convention still puts minimal elements at rank one and R-vine rank equal to dimension. Thus the journal differs materially from the literal assignment. Do not silently substitute the journal statement. The 2025 corrigendum concerns author affiliations, not a mathematical proof or a resolution of these indexing conventions.

Here is a sustained all-dimension argument for the actual node-poset count, retained for root's assessment rather than discarded after the small case.

Normal form: for a C-vine on n ordered centers, write its nodes x_(r,j), 0<=r<j<=n. The rank is r+1. Minimal nodes are x_(0,j). For r>=1 the two lower covers of x_(r,j) are x_(r-1,r) and x_(r-1,j). To justify this for every C-vine, choose the first associated star's center as label 1. The next associated tree has vertices the edges (1,j); choose its star center (1,2). Its edges have children (1,2),(1,j). Repeat on the successive associated stars, choosing label r for the center's remaining index. Induction gives exactly those two covers at every rank. Choices of star centers in the two-vertex and one-vertex terminal trees only relabel an isomorphic poset. This establishes a normal form for all C-vines, not merely the displayed n=4 object. The source's Figure 4 text labels are consistent with these covers; its actual drawing has not been visually inspected here, so printed-object QA remains a gate.

For an ideal I, column j is a chain; define h_j as the number of its included nodes. Then 0<=h_j<=j and the included nodes are precisely x_(r,j) with 0<=r<h_j. A top node x_(j-1,j) forces the full C-vine on labels 1,...,j: its covers force column j-1 to be full and the rest follows inductively. Therefore any full column forces all earlier columns to be full.

There is one ideal with every column full. Otherwise let m be the length of the initial consecutive run of full columns. Thus 0<=m<n. Column m+1 has height in {0,...,m}, giving m+1 choices. Every later column j has height in {0,...,m+1}; a height >=m+2 would include x_(m+1,j) and force column m+1 to be full, a contradiction. Conversely these height choices always give an ideal: every selected node's same-column predecessor is selected; each diagonal predecessor x_(r-1,r) belongs to a full column r<=m. There are no other covering relations. Hence the choices are independent and give (m+1)(m+2)^(n-m-1) ideals.

Consequently the number is

  F(n) = 1 + sum_(m=0)^(n-1) (m+1)(m+2)^(n-m-1).

A change of index in the two sums of

  sum_(i=0)^(n+1) [(i+1)^(n+1-i)-i^(n+1-i)]

cancels all but the constant 1 and terms (j-1)j^(n+1-j), 2<=j<=n+1. This is exactly F(n). Use the usual combinatorial convention a^0=1, including 0^0; the final difference at exponent zero is zero. This proves the formula with n+1, not the assigned n. No executed counting experiment supports this proof; the bijection above is the argument.

The normal form and height-vector argument supply a credible whole finite combinatorial proof for the corrected journal expression, but not a significant new exact literal answer under the present assignment. ## 360289: arbitrary grids — sustained constructive routes, no complete partition

Scope is every positive dimension and every positive side length, for every prescribed target. Actual move: move all C(u)>0 cups from occupied u to occupied v only when graph distance(u,v)=C(u). Distance is in the original graph, not a chosen traversal. The source's Lemma 3 proof, move definitions, two-dimensional Theorem 7 proof and cube-layer mechanisms were read. Both arXiv versions were obtained; the current author-hosted final Discrete Mathematics 347 (2024), 114097 PDF was obtained and its Conjectures 28–29 inspected. The final retains arbitrary grids as conjectural. Full final-PDF proof/figures and later citing bodies were not fully reviewed; no novelty clearance.

Route A, product induction: stack each fiber H x {t} to a selected vertex. This proves fiber feasibility internally, but yields piles of |H| cups. A pile may next move only distance |H|. In thin products such as a large hypercube, |H| grows exponentially while the diameter grows linearly, so this invariant freezes the construction. It is a failure of this strategy, not a counterexample: another schedule may work. Missing lemma: an induction preserving a decomposition into pile sizes equal to actual remaining target distances, rather than just a single full fiber pile.

Route B, Hamiltonian snake: a rectangular grid has a spanning snake path. Path stackability does not transfer from that subgraph: grid shortcuts shorten distances, invalidating path moves of the required length. The correct sufficient certificate is a partition of all non-target cups into isometric paths or internally verified stackable subconfigurations, each of size exactly the distance of its final occupied vertex to the target. The source's stacking-partition lemma then proves the whole target. No such partition is constructed for arbitrary side lengths and dimension. Asking for a generic solver to find it would be an unproved helper plan and is not a result.

Route C, orthants and symmetric chains: split about the target to reduce boundary bookkeeping to orthant grids. In a corner hypercube, a block of size q can jump only from weight q. Symmetric chain decomposition controls occupied ranks but central chains can be too short to match their weight; the source's cube proof repairs layers with nontrivial Johnson-graph groupings and ad hoc small cubes. Arbitrary dimensions require a uniform regrouping preserving actual distances and the exact block-size endpoint. The proven up-to-20 result and two-dimensional grid theorem do not provide this lemma. Arbitrary side lengths require a product-of-chains version as well. Parked with these three precise obstructions, not excluded merely because the family is familiar.
