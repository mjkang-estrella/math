## B. Ordinary proof: pass after expanding compressed justifications

All polynomial calculations below are over F_2. I found no fatal gap. The following details are needed in a complete write-up.

1. **Order modulo 3^j.** Lagrange bounds ord(2) by 2*3^(j-1), and reduction modulo 3 forces it even. For s>=1 and 3 not dividing u, the expansion (1+3^s u)^3-1 has valuation s+1: after division by 3^(s+1), the remaining factor is u modulo 3. Induction gives v_3(4^(3^r)-1)=r+1. Therefore no smaller even divisor of 2*3^(j-1) is an order. The half-order power is -1, since an odd prime power has exactly the two square roots +/-1 of 1. The elementary valuation proof is sufficient; a generic LTE theorem is unnecessary.

2. **Frobenius orbit.** For nonzero z, equality u+u^-1=z+z^-1 puts u among the two roots z,z^-1 of T^2+(z+z^-1)T+1. For primitive z of order 3^j, the Frobenius period of z+z^-1 is thus the least h with 2^h congruent to +/-1 modulo 3^j. It is 3^(j-1). The orbit product is its irreducible minimal polynomial P_j, monic of that odd degree. Since 2 generates all units modulo 3^j, every primitive root gives the same P_j. Distinct j give distinct degrees. P_1=X+1.

3. **Trace.** For j>=2 the orbit/sign pairs exhaust the primitive exponents. Their sum is the sum of primitive 3^j-th roots, zero because the coefficient below the leading term in T^(2*3^(j-1))+T^(3^(j-1))+1 is zero. Thus the candidate's trace coefficient is correct. Translation flips the coefficient of X^(d-1) in any monic odd-degree polynomial of degree d. **The original coefficient need not be zero. The trace calculation can be deleted in full.**

4. **Root multiplicities.** For m odd, the m-th roots of unity are simple. All nonidentity roots z occur in inverse pairs; none satisfies z+z^-1=0. The evaluation identity gives (m-1)/2 distinct roots of f_m. By induction, deg f_m=m-1, it is monic, and its powers have even exponents for m odd. Hence it is a square. The root count exhausts its degree, so each distinct root has multiplicity exactly two. For m=3^k the inverse pairs split into the primitive 3^j layers, giving f_(3^k)=product P_j^2. This also checks the factorization's degree: sum 2*3^(j-1)=3^k-1. One must not infer multiplicities solely from evaluating at roots.

5. **Duplication.** Evaluation at z+z^-1 yields f_(2m)=X f_m^2; the algebraic closure is infinite and the evaluation map has infinite image (fibers have at most two elements), so it is an identity of polynomials. A recurrence/addition-identity proof also works. Therefore f_(2*3^k)=X product P_j^4; its degree is 2*3^k-1.

6. **All cross factors.** Translation is a ring automorphism and preserves monicity, irreducibility and degree. If i != j, P_i and P_j(X+1) have different degrees. If i=j, the odd-degree coefficient flip makes them different. Thus every such pair is coprime, including i=j=1. For j>=2, degree at least three precludes equality with X or X+1. In f_(2*3^k), X has valuation 1 and X+1 has valuation 4. In its translate these valuations are 4 and 1. Every other cross pair is coprime. The monic gcd is exactly X(X+1), with both multiplicities one, not its square or fourth power.

7. **Grid recurrence.** Write v_i as column vectors, with zero boundary rows v_0=v_(n+1)=0. The kernel equation is v_(i+1)=(I+A_path)v_i+v_(i-1). Induction gives v_i=f_i(I+A_path)v_1. The last boundary equation is precisely f_(n+1)(I+A_path)v_1=0. First-row restriction and recursive reconstruction are mutually inverse linear maps. There is no wrap-around condition. Column notation removes the minor left/right convention ambiguity in the candidate's “row vectors.”

8. **Cyclic module.** A_path^r e_1 has zero coordinates beyond r+1 and value 1 at r+1 for 0<=r<n: there is exactly one length-r path reaching that far. The Krylov matrix is triangular with diagonal 1, hence invertible. The tridiagonal characteristic polynomial obeys c_0=1,c_1=X,c_n=X c_(n-1)+c_(n-2), so c_n=f_(n+1). Equivalently use the cyclic basis and the boundary recurrence to construct the quotient directly, avoiding determinant theory. The map p -> p(A_path)e_1 factors through F_2[X]/(f_(n+1)) and is a linear isomorphism. Under it, f_(n+1)(I+A_path) is multiplication by f_(n+1)(X+1).

9. **Kernel dimension.** Let f=d g, h=d q with d the monic gcd. Then gcd(g,q)=1 even when g and d are not coprime. The cancellation f|hp iff g|p is valid by Bezout. Multiplication by g identifies F_2[X]/(d) with this kernel: well-defined because gd=f, injective by cancellation, and surjective by the divisibility criterion. Hence dimension equals deg d. This proves the candidate's basis claim and supplies the needed scalar-linear isomorphism.
