
Furthermore, a coefficient-wise monotonicity result for the h^{*} -polynomial of any generalized snake is given (Theorem 4.19 ), bounding the h^{*} -vectors of any generalized snake posets with the h^{*} -vectors of the ladder and the regular snake (Corollary 4.20 ). 
 
 • 
 
 
 
 We conclude with conjectures for further research in Section 5 regarding the real rooted-ness of h^{*} -polynomials and Ehrhart positivity. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 2. Background & Preliminaries 

 
 
 
 Our main focus of study is the lattice-point enumeration of polytopes that arise from a special partially ordered set (poset), namely a generalized snake poset, which we now present. 
 
 
 
 
 2.1. Generalized snake posets 

 
 
 
 The family of generalized snake posets P(\mathbf{w}) are distributive lattices with width two and were studied geometrically in [ 3 ] . 
 
 
 
 Definition 2.1 (Definition 3.1, [ 3 ] ) . 
 
 
 
 For n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} , a generalized snake word is a word of the form \mathbf{w}=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{n} where w_{0}=\varepsilon is the empty letter and w_{i} is in the alphabet \{L,R\} for i=1,\ldots,n .
The length of the word is n , which is the number of letters in \{L,R\} . 
 
 
 
 
 Definition 2.2 (Definition 3.2, [ 3 ] ) . 
 
 
 
 Given a generalized snake word \mathbf{w}=w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{n} , the generalized snake poset P(\mathbf{w}) is recursively defined as follows: 
 
 • 
 
 
 
 P(w_{0})=P(\varepsilon) is the poset on elements \{0,1,2,3\} with cover relations 1\prec 0 , 2\prec 0 , 3\prec 1 and 3\prec 2 . 
 
 • 
 
 
 
 P(w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{n}) is the poset P(w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{n-1})\cup\{2n+2,2n+3\} with the added cover relations 2n+3\prec 2n+1 , 2n+3\prec 2n+2 , and 
 

 
 
 \begin{cases}2n+2\prec 2n-1,&\text{ if }n=1\text{ and }w_{n}=L,\text{ or }n\geq 2\text{ and }w_{n-1}w_{n}\in\{RL,LR\},\\
2n+2\prec 2n,&\text{ if }n=1\text{ and }w_{n}=R,\text{ or }n\geq 2\text{ and }w_{n-1}w_{n}\in\{LL,RR\}.\end{cases}

uld not fall under the purview of Stanley’s monotonicity theorem since the order polytopes arising from each of the generalized snake words would be of the same dimension and would not be completely contained in one another. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 5. Future directions 

 
 
 
 To conclude, we present some conjectures for further investigation based on our computations. 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 5.1 . 
 
 
 
 Let \mathbf{w} be a generalized snake word of length n+1 , 
 
 (1) 
 
 
 
 All roots of h^{*}(\mathbf{w};z) are real and negative. 
 
 (2) 
 
 
 
 All roots of L(\mathbf{w};t) are contained in the disk |z-\frac{n+4}{2}|\leq\frac{n+2}{2},z\in\mathbb{C} with axis of symmetry x=\frac{-n-4}{2} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 We have verified 5.1 for snake words of length up to 9 . 
 
 
 
 
 We also observed Ehrhart positivity empirically. 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 5.2 . 
 
 
 
 The order polytope \mathcal{O}(P(\mathbf{w})) is Ehrhart positive, i.e., L(\mathbf{w};t) has nonnegative coefficients. 
 
 
 
 
 
 We have also observed a similar coefficient-wise inequality for the Ehrhart polynomial under the swap operator, parallel to Theorem 4.19 . 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 5.3 . 
 
 
 
 For k>0 , the following coefficient-wise inequality holds: 
 

 
 
 [t^{k}]L(\varphi_{i}(\mathbf{w});t)\leq[t^{k}]L(\mathbf{w},t). 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 One plausible way of proving Conjecture 5.2 , is to prove the Ehrhart positivity of the regular snake case using Theorem 4.8 , then prove Conjecture 5.3 . 
 
 
 
 
 We also propose to generalize the operator \varphi_{i} to other graded posets, and to analyze the behavior of the h^{*} -vectors or the Ehrhart polynomials of other order polytopes under the generalized \varphi_{i} ; the aim is to ascertain whether analogous monotonicity properties persist in those cases.
