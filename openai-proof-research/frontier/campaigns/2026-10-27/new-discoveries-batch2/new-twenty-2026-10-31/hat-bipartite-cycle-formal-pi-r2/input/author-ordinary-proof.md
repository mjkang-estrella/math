## 325100: complete all-s proof (author assessment, independent review unpaid)
Source: Peter Heinig, arXiv:1303.0026, Definition 3, Conjecture 5; extracted text lines 144–146 and 214–224. Let A have s vertices, B have s−1, let a,b be distinct members of A, and add z adjacent precisely to a,b to K=K(A,B). This is exactly the source graph, with a=1,b=2s−1,z=0. Work in the F2 edge space. A cycle vector means an even-degree edge subset; this agrees with the span of simple circuits by repeatedly removing a circuit from a nonempty even-degree subgraph. Every Hamilton circuit has even degree, giving the easy inclusion.

Fix s>=4 and let U=A\{a,b}, so |U|>=2. Let W be the span of Hamilton circuit vectors of G. I claim every rectangle R(x,w;p,q), with distinct x,w in A and distinct p,q in B, belongs to W.

First suppose x=a and w=u in U. Choose y in U\{u}. Order A with first three entries a,y,u and last entry b, filling intervening entries with the remaining A vertices in any order. This is possible even for s=4. Choose a permutation of B whose first two entries are p,q. Alternating these two lists gives a Hamilton a-to-b path P in K. Swapping just p and q gives another Hamilton path P'. Adding za and zb to each gives Hamilton circuits H,H'. Their symmetric difference is exactly {ap,aq,up,uq}: the edges yp and yq occur in both and cancel, as do all other edges. Hence R(a,u;p,q) belongs to W. To obtain R(b,u;p,q), reverse the construction, ending the A order with u,y,b and placing p,q in the final two B slots. Both paths still have endpoints a,b.

For fixed p,q, rectangles satisfy R(x,w;p,q)+R(w,t;p,q)=R(x,t;p,q), since edges incident with w cancel. The graph on A whose edges are au and bu for u in U is connected. Summing rectangles along any path in this graph gives every A-pair rectangle. Thus the claim holds for all pairs.

Rectangles span Z1(K): fix a0 in A and p0 in B. For any even-degree edge subset C of K, for each edge xp in C with x!=a0 and p!=p0 add R(x,a0;p,p0). This clears every edge off the double star consisting of edges incident with a0 or p0. The residue still has even degrees and is supported on that tree. A nonempty even-degree subgraph of a finite tree is impossible (take a leaf of the nonempty edge support). Thus the residue is zero. This proves the spanning claim without dimension computations or enumeration.

Choose any Hamilton circuit H0 of G; alternating arbitrary orders of A starting a and ending b and of B constructs it. If C is any cycle vector of G, the even-degree condition at z says that za and zb occur either both or neither. If both occur, add H0. In either case the resulting vector has no z edges, hence lies in Z1(K), which is already contained in W. Therefore C belongs to W. Consequently Z1(G;F2)=span H(G) for EVERY s>=4, exactly Conjecture 5. The s=3 failure reported by the source does not contradict the construction: U then has only one member, so the required y does not exist.

Validation: manual symbolic cancellation and arbitrary-order extension, no mathematical program or enumeration. The source's s=4 rank computation is not used as a proof or recomputed. The graph including z is balanced bipartite on 2s vertices with minimum degree 2; general dense-graph minimum-degree theorems do not directly cover arbitrarily large s. No claim of significance/novelty is inferred from this observation. Actual final-source/correction and relevant exact prior comparisons remain open pending body evidence. ## 325572: exact source theorem, zero
Chen–Li–Lian, arXiv:1305.3414, Theorem 2.2 literally states the MathDB biconditional. Lemma 2.1 identifies the spectral condition with every even cycle being uniformly oriented. Their proof removes an edge, applies induction and transports the switches back. If the last edge disagrees with the elementary X-to-Y orientation, a cycle through it would have k−1 instead of k correctly directed arcs in a 2k-cycle, violating uniform orientation; it is therefore a bridge, and switching one component fixes it. Conversely elementary orientation satisfies the spectral condition and switching is diagonal sign conjugation, preserving spectrum. Isolated vertices and edgeless components cause no restriction and can be treated componentwise. These are the same orientations, spectrum and switching operations as the record, not a weakened graph class. Exact published proof is enough for exclusion, with no novelty clearance required.
