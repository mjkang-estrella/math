115: (v0 , v1 , v2 , . . . , v2 , v1 ) (in other words, vi = vj whenever i ≡ −j mod n).
116: Conjecture 1.3. Suppose that n ∈ N is an odd number that is not divisible by
117: 7. Let v ∈ Fn2 , and suppose that v and e work together. Then v is symmetric.
118:    In Lemma 3.6 we prove that if e, v and w work together and are linearly
119: independent, neither v nor w can be symmetric. Thus, Conjecture 1.3 would
120: imply Theorem 1.2. However, we unfortunately were unable to prove this
121: conjecture.
122:    In Section 4, we make partial progress towards the conjecture. To each
123: vector v ∈ Fp2 , we associate the Cayley digraph Gv on vertex set Z/pZ with
124: the set A of indices i with vi = 1 as generating set (that is, (i, j) is an arc
125: if and only if vj−i = 1). This graph is simple1 if and only if v is symmetric.
126: We show in Proposition 4.1 that e and v work together if and only if Gv has
127: no induced subgraph on an odd number of vertices where each vertex has odd
128: outdegree. This shows Conjecture 1.3 is equivalent to the following conjecture.
129: Conjecture 1.4. Suppose that n ∈ N is an odd number that is not divisible by
130: 7. Let G be a Cayley digraph on Z/nZ. Then G is a simple graph if and only
131: if G has no odd-sized induced subgraph where each vertex has odd outdegree.
132:   Note that one of the directions follows from the Handshaking Lemma. For
133: non-symmetric v, the goal is now to find a ‘bad’ subgraph in the graph Gv in
134: order to show e and v do not work together. We define the girth of v to be
135: the length of the shortest directed cycle in Gv . In Proposition 4.7, we reduce
136: to the case in which the girth of v is 4 or 6. For this, we use several additive
137: results. The main observation is that the generating set A ⊆ Z/pZ has nice
138: additive properties if Gv has no small cycles. This then allows us to conclude
139: that A is contained in a small arithmetic progression, which gives us enough
140: control on the edges of Gv to find the ‘bad’ subgraph.
141:   Even though we cannot prove Conjecture 1.3, we make enough progress in
142: Section 4 to push through the proof of Theorem 1.2 in Section 5 via a case
143: analysis on the girth of the vectors v, w and v + w.
144:   In Section 6, we consider the problem of bounding hq (n). This was studied
145: by Cameron, Ellis and Raynaud [2] and, amongst many other results, they
146: show that the lower bound h2 (mn) ≥ max{h2 (m), h2 (n)} mentioned earlier
147: holds even when 2 is replaced by any prime power q. We show how our lower
148: bound also holds in Fnq and extend the upper bound given in Theorem 2.3 to
149: this setting as well. Finally, we show that hq (p) = 0 when q is a prime and
150: p > q is a prime with q as a primitive root.
151:   Our conjecture, and many other interesting questions outlined in Section 7,
152: are left open.
