## 375591 — binary avalanche complexes: sustained route

Definition 2.2 uses parallel firing. Section 3.1 calls P_n a directed path with n non-sink vertices and one sink, not literally n total vertices as the database says. The sink is excluded from firing sets (Remark 2.5). Preserve this discrepancy; it cannot earn credit. Conjecture 3.17 in v2 also says n>1. The nonbinary example in the database is not a binary counterexample.

For binary input c=(c_1,...,c_n), put S={j:c_j=1}. On the directed path each non-sink vertex has outdegree one. Inductively every c_t is binary and c_t(j)=c_0(j-t) for j>t, and zero otherwise: an occupied site loses its one grain and receives exactly the preceding site's grain. Thus the full avalanche complex is
K(S,n)={A subset [n]: exists t>=0, A subset ((S+t) intersect [n])}.
There are no firings after n steps. This is an exact reduction, not a check of finitely many inputs. The literal all-binary endpoint would follow from H_i(K(S,n))=0 for i>floor(n/2)-1, with correct non-sink indexing.

First mechanism: dimension. Every facet has at most |S| vertices, so vanishing is immediate in the regime |S|<=floor(n/2). This does not settle dense inputs. The source's single-zero configurations have |S|=n-1 and nonzero homology at floor(n/2)-1 (Proposition 3.15). There is therefore no useful global bound on facet size, and replacing K by its clique complex changes the object: for S={1,2,4}, a firing set is a triangle, and compatibility of pairs need not give a common time for larger faces.

Second mechanism: a Morse matching by the least active vertex. Strip leading zeros using Lemma 3.2. With min S=1, vertex 1 belongs only to F_0=S. If |S|>=2, the simplex F_0 can collapse onto its face F_0 minus {1}, relative to the other generating simplices: the faces containing 1 occur in F_0 only. This removes vertex 1 without changing homotopy type. It does NOT preserve the binary translate form, because the later facets remain translates of the original S while F_0 has lost a point. Iterating a theorem only for translate complexes at this stage would be invalid.

A stronger attempted state includes all truncated translates and deleted early vertices, with a matching sigma <-> sigma union {v} whenever adding the current pivot is legal. The desired critical-face argument would assign a distinct excluded companion to each selected critical vertex, yielding 2(i+1)<=n for a critical i-face. The missing lemma is a globally acyclic pivot matching on these states, together with the disjoint companion assignment. Local collapses alone prove neither assertion. Do not turn this into a Pi implementation task. The nerve route (Section 2.2) replaces facets by firing times but requires simultaneous intersection of every time in a face; pairwise intersections cannot justify a flag-complex bound.

The source proves special configurations by explicit collapses (Lemmas 3.4,3.6,3.8,3.10; Proposition 3.13 and 3.15), and reports verification for n<20. None of these proves the arbitrary S lemma. Both arXiv versions were downloaded; relevant v2 definitions/proof bodies and examples were read. No visual figure inspection, exhaustive version comparison, or full citing-literature clearance was completed. Parked with the exact matching lemma unpaid.

## 376099 — cograph zombie damage: sustained route

Read Definition 1, Proposition 9 with proof, Theorem 10 with proof, and Conjecture 20. Games have perfect information; the zombie chooses its initial vertex, the survivor responds, and the zombie moves first along a shortest path. A vertex is damaged by a surviving noncapturing pursuer turn followed by a survivor move/pass. The score counts distinct damaged vertices, not visits.

For co-component H, q(H)=|H|-1-Delta(H). Choose x_1,x_2 realizing the two smallest q values. Alternating x_1,x_2 is legal whenever the survivor avoids immediate capture: a survivor nonadjacent to x_1 lies in H_1 and x_2 is a common neighbor; similarly at the next turn. Damaged vertices lie in the nonneighborhoods of x_1 and x_2. This proves the full upper bound from source Proposition 9, already public, zero credit. The outstanding direction is the lower bound against every adaptive zombie strategy.

First mechanism: imitate complete multipartite lower bound. If the zombie visits a part infinitely often, greedily select an undamaged vertex in that part different from the zombie. With independent co-components the choices remain safe and force the source Theorem 10 lower bound. For general cographs a co-component has internal edges; even after a safe response, the zombie may make a shortest internal move instead of leaving the co-component. A proof that assumes alternation is not full scope.

Second mechanism: use disconnectedness of a nontrivial cograph co-component H. A safe survivor placed in a different connected component of H from the zombie prevents an internal geodesic first step. However the available reservoir is |H|-size(C_z), which can be smaller than q(H); q counts nonneighbors inside C_z as well. Exact diagnostic object: H=C_4 disjoint union K_1 has |H|=5, Delta=2, q=2, but if the zombie is in C_4 this different-component reservoir has size one. Joining H to an independent pair gives a connected cograph with no universal vertex and predicted value 3. This object invalidates the reservoir proof, not the conjecture. An isolated-vertex survivor can accumulate that vertex and one vertex of the pair, then enter an antipodal C_4 vertex to gain a third surviving damage before an internal chase. This is not a 2-damage refutation, and no exact global minimax value is claimed.

Third mechanism: induct on the cotree with a budget for already damaged vertices in each module. A sufficient lemma must permit a survivor either to force departure from a module or to accumulate its q-budget during the internal pursuit, with the sum of two smallest top-level budgets eventually reached, allowing the zombie to change both module and blocked vertex. Cardinality |nonneighbors(z) in H|>=q(H) alone is inadequate: different safe sets can lie in a previously damaged reservoir and internal response vertices need not be adjacent to the survivor's current position. The missing lemma is this strategic module induction, including legal responses and progress under arbitrary internal geodesics. No finite minimax search/certificate was produced. Parked; source/citing coverage not clear enough for novelty either.

## 375429 — two-row Schur partition reconstruction: sustained route

Definition 2.17 is the multiset of positive monomial evaluations of the ordinary Schur polynomial s_alpha, counted with their coefficients. This is not just its numerical sum and not an arbitrary skew shape alpha/beta. Database title says skew, but the literal subscript is an ordinary two-row partition. Conjecture 6.3 is the surviving question. The Hadelyn et al. refutation mentioned in the database concerns pre_j and an earlier question; it does not refute Conjecture 6.3.

Let alpha=(a,b). Knowing the largest original part L would allow a plausible greedy subtraction strategy: subtract all tableau monomials using already reconstructed variables, then inspect the largest remaining product. For shape (a,b), the top tableau products involve at least two distinct variable labels when b>0, so the very first largest product is L^a times the second part to power b, rather than a pure power of L. Its value alone does not separate these two parts. For two input parts, the tableau monomials have exponents (a-j,b+j), 0<=j<=a-b. If a=b, the output on two-part inputs is just (xy)^b. Equal sum n and product xy determine the unordered pair (roots of X^2-nX+xy); hence this potential simple collision fails. This resolves only the length-two input subcase, not all partitions.

Second mechanism: output power sums determine s_alpha(x_1^q,...,x_l^q) for every positive q. For alpha=(2,1), source Lemma 5.3 and Theorem 1.7 recover the maximum via p_{3q}=p_q^3-3s_(2,1)(x^q); start with p_1=n, iterate q=3^j, then recover L and greedily all parts. This is a complete existing source result and earns zero credit. It cannot be extrapolated blindly: in the Frobenius expansion of a Schur polynomial of degree d=a+b, the coefficient of p_d is chi^alpha((d))/d. For b>=2 the shape is not a hook and that character is zero; the one-step pure p_d recovery is unavailable. For general hooks with b=1 other mixed power sums also enter. No argument to recover all of these from the single supplied Schur multiset has been supplied.

Third mechanism: reciprocal/complement transformation of tableau products. Such transformations can preserve multisets for elementary symmetric maps and are the origin of public pre_j collisions. They change highest weights for ordinary Schur shapes and do not automatically preserve the same two-row alpha or the original sum n. The source's explicit pre_3 collision is not a counterexample here. Missing lemma: recover the largest original part from the full two-row monomial multiset plus total n for arbitrary length, or exhibit an exact equal-sum pair and prove equality with tableau multiplicities. No collision search was executed. Parked, no full endpoint.

## 374615 — sustained matroid route terminated by a public complete proof

Original Definition 2.1, Proposition 2.2, Theorem 3.2 and its proof, Proposition 3.5 and proof, truncation Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.4, Conjectures 5.4 and 5.6 were read. The natural induction on rank through the recurrence for Y needs cancellation among low-rank flats. Writing y_k(M)=[s^k]Y_M, it asks for
sum_(F<E) rk(F)y_k(F)+|E| sum_(F<E)y_(k-1)(F)=0
for k<rk(M). Vanishing of Y_F to order rk(F) alone does not prove this sum. In degrees one and two parallel-class decompositions explain cancellation; promoting those to all degrees without a general word formula would leave an unpaid lemma.

Ferroni–Vecchi arXiv:2606.05869 supplies exactly that formula. Read the Master Theorem, its entire double-induction proof and Möbius-transform proof in Section 2, and Section 3.2 Theorem 1.4 with proof. For loopless M the coefficient of (-s)^m in Z_M is the sum over words e_1,...,e_m of the product over j of 1/rk({e_1,...,e_j}). In truncation to rank r-1, every prefix of a word of length m<r has the same rank as in M; hence every summand and coefficient agrees. Multiplication by m! gives equality of derivatives. Equivalently, the Möbius sum selects only spanning words, absent in lengths <r; at length r each ordered basis contributes 1/r!, giving (-1)^r times the number of bases. No novel deduction remains.

Keep endpoint conventions visible: the source setup is loopless, whereas a positive-size rank-one matroid truncates to a looped matroid and original Definition 2.1 sets its zeta function to zero. Literal r>0 including rank one therefore needs a convention/scope correction, not discovery credit. For r>=2 the loopless truncation word argument is complete. A single loopless rank-one element has Z=1/(1+s), while its ordinary rank-zero truncation has loops and Z=0; even the k=0 identity fails with that definition. This is a source endpoint correction and receives zero credit. No CANDIDATE or dormant execution request is appropriate for this known/scope-correction outcome.
