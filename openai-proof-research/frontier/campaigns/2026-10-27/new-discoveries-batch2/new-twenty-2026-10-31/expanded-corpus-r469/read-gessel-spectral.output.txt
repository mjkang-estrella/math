                                            A NOTE ON 2-DISTANT NONCROSSING PARTITIONS AND
                                                        WEIGHTED MOTZKIN PATHS




arXiv:1003.5301v2 [math.CO] 2 Nov 2010
                                                                        IRA M. GESSEL AND JANG SOO KIM


                                                  Abstract. We prove a conjecture of Drake and Kim: the number of 2-distant
                                                  noncrossing partitions of {1, 2, . . . , n} is equal to the sum of weights of Motzkin
                                                  paths of length n, where the weight of a Motzkin path is a product of certain
                                                  fractions involving Fibonacci numbers. We provide two proofs of their conjec-
                                                  ture: one uses continued fractions and the other is combinatorial.




                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                            A Motzkin path of length n is a lattice path from (0, 0) to (n, 0) consisting of up
                                         steps U = (1, 1), down steps D = (1, −1) and horizontal steps H = (1, 0) that never
                                         goes below the x-axis. The height of a step in a Motzkin path is the y coordinate
                                         of the ending point.
                                            Given two sequences b = (b0 , b1 , . . .) and λ = (λ0 , λ1 , . . .), the weight of a Motzkin
                                         path with respect to (b, λ) is the product of bi and λi for each horizontal step and
                                         down step of height i respectively, see Figure 1. Let Motn (b, λ) denote the sum of
                                         weights of Motzkin paths of length n with respect to (b, λ). This sum is closely
                                         related to orthogonal polynomials; see [5, 6].
                                            Drake and Kim [1] defined the set NCk (n) of k-distant noncrossing partitions of
                                         [n] = {1, 2, . . . , n}. For k ≥ 0, a k-distant noncrossing partition is a set partition of
                                         [n] without two arcs (a, c) and (b, d) satisfying a < b ≤ c < d and c − b ≥ k, where
                                         an arc is a pair (i, j) of integers contained in the same block which does not contain
                                         any integer between them. For example, π = {{1, 5, 7}, {2, 3, 6}, {4}} is a 3-distant
                                         noncrossing partition but not a 2-distant noncrossing partition because π has two
                                         arcs (1, 5) and (3, 6) with 5 − 3 ≥ 2. Note that the 1-distant noncrossing partitions
                                         are the ordinary noncrossing partitions, which implies that # NC1 (n) is equal to

                            3. A combinatorial proof
   Let b, λ and d be the sequences defined in (2) and (3).
   Recall that in the previous section we have shown that Dyckn (d) = Motn (b, λ)
by changing a Dyck path of length 2n to a Motzkin path of length n. We can do
the same thing after deleting the first and the last steps of a Dyck path. More
precisely, for a Dyck path of length 2n, we delete the first and the last steps, take
two steps at a time in the remaining 2n − 2 steps, and change U U , U D, DU and
DD, respectively, to U , H, H and D. Then we obtain a Motzkin path of length
n − 1. This argument shows that
(5)                Dyckn (d) = d0 · Motn−1 (α, β) = Motn−1 (α, β),
where αn = d2n + d2n+1 and βn = d2n+1 d2n+2 . By (3) and Lemma 2.2, we have
α = (2, 3, 3, . . .) and β = (1, 1, . . .). Note that we can also prove Theorem 2.4 using
(5).
   To find a connection between Motn−1 (α, β) and NC2 (n) we need the following
definition.
   A Schröder path of length 2n is a lattice path from (0, 0) to (2n, 0) consisting
of up steps U = (1, 1), down steps D = (1, −1) and double horizontal steps H 2 =
HH = (2, 0) that never goes below the x-axis. Let SCHeven (n) denote the set of
Schröder paths of length 2n such that all horizontal steps have even height.
Proposition 3.1. Let α = (2, 3, 3, . . .) and β = (1, 1, . . .). Then, for n ≥ 1, we
have
                       Motn (α, β) = # SCHeven (n).
Proof. From a Motzkin path of length n we obtain a Schröder path of length 2n as
follows. Change U and D to U U and DD respectively. For a horizontal step H, if
its height is 0, we change it to either U D or HH, and if its height is greater than
0, we change it to either U D, DU or HH. Then we get an element of SCHeven (n).
Since the weight of a horizontal step H in the Motzkin path is equal to the number
of choices, the theorem follows.                                                   
Remark 1. The definition of SCHeven (n) in [2] is the set of Schröder paths of length
2n which have no peaks at even height. From such a path, by changing all the
horizontal steps at odd height to peaks, we get a Schröder path whose horizontal
steps are all at even height, and this transformation is easily seen to be a bijection.

   Kim [2] found a bijection between NC2 (n) and SCHeven (n − 1). Using Kim’s
bijection in [2], Proposition 3.1, (5) and Corollary 2.3 we finally get the following
sequence of identities which implies Theorem 1.1:
     # NC2 (n) = # SCHeven (n − 1) = Motn−1 (α, β) = Dyckn (d) = Motn (b, λ).

                             1. Introduction
                                 Spectral localization for nonsymmetric Laplacian matrices is closely related to the classical Karpelevič problem
                             of determining the possible eigenvalues of stochastic matrices [9, 8, 10].
                                 To see this connection, recall that a standardized Laplacian matrix 𝐿̃ of order 𝑛 is a real matrix with zero row sums
                                                          [ 1 ]
                             and off-diagonal entries in − 𝑛 , 0 (see Section 2). Let Λ𝑛 denote the set of all eigenvalues of such matrices. Then
                             𝑃 =𝐼 −𝐿  ̃ is stochastic, and every stochastic 𝑛 × 𝑛 matrix whose off-diagonal entries do not exceed 1∕𝑛 arises in this
                             way. Since 𝜎(𝑃 ) = 1 − 𝜎( ̃
                                                       𝐿), the eigenvalue region of this stochastic subclass is 1 − Λ𝑛 , the reflection of Λ𝑛 about 1∕2.
                                 However, by the complementary-digraph symmetry [1, Theorem 1], Λ𝑛 is invariant under reflection about 1∕2:
                             1 − Λ𝑛 = Λ𝑛 . Thus the two eigenvalue regions coincide, and Λ𝑛 ⊆ Θ𝑛 , where Θ𝑛 is the Karpelevič region of all
                             possible eigenvalues of stochastic 𝑛 × 𝑛 matrices. A polygon 𝑆𝑛 ⊆ Λ𝑛 was constructed in [1], where it was also
                             conjectured that this inclusion is in fact an equality:

                                                 𝑆𝑛 = Λ𝑛 .

                                 To the best of our knowledge, this conjecture has remained unresolved since its formulation in 2005.
