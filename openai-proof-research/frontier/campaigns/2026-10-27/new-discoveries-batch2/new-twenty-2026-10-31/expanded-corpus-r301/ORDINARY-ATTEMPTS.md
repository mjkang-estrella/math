## 316383: cycle presentations and a complete classical bridge

The historical v1 Definition 2.1 requires each arc to have no self crossing and each pair to have consistent over/under crossings; it does not require the ordering of the arcs in the over/under relation to agree with their cyclic order along the knot. Conjecture 3.4 quantifies over every presentation, not only minimal ones. The February 2026 v2 changes terminology to segment number and omits the old conjecture as a numbered claim; its Definition 2.1 retains these conditions. This version change cannot be ignored.

First attempt: a finite DAG has a topological ordering, so perhaps traversing its segments gives a descending knot diagram. Failure: topological order is a permutation of segments, while a traversal must follow their fixed cyclic order. Relabelling cannot change that cyclic order. The source's three-segment argument does not extend this way.

Second attempt: peel a source segment by an overpass isotopy and merge its two neighbours. Removing an arc alone opens the knot. The replacement joins two neighbours whose over/under relations with a third segment can conflict. There is no general cycle-presentation-preserving merge. This is a structural failure, not an unproved induction silently assumed.

Third attempt gives the complete exclusion bridge. Adams et al., arXiv:1208.5742, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.3 (proof body read), prove that every knot has a projection with a single multicrossing; the strands at that crossing have distinct linearly ordered heights. Choose any nontrivial knot, for example the trefoil, to which their construction applies. Take a sufficiently small planar disk about the multicrossing so that it contains one straight strand per level and no other crossings. Outside the disk the projection has no crossings. Mark one ordinary point on each connector joining one central strand to the next. These marked points partition the knot into arcs, each comprising one central strand and half of each of its incident outside connectors.

Perturb the central strands generically in projection, keeping the boundary attachments fixed in small crossing-free collars, and keep their heights separated. One can take parallel translations of the central straight pieces and a sufficiently small perturbation avoiding triple intersections; all pairwise intersections remain inside the disk. This is an isotopy of the spatial knot: different central strands occupy disjoint height slabs, and the perturbation has support away from the connector collars. Outside connectors remain unchanged and mutually disjoint in projection. Each partition arc has no self crossing. Every crossing between two partition arcs is between their central strands and is directed from greater height to lesser height. Thus every directed edge strictly decreases the integer height, so there is no directed cycle. The resulting regular finite diagram represents the same nontrivial knot and meets both historical and latest literal presentation definitions. Minimality is unnecessary and is not asserted.

This is a full ordinary refutation of the old universal claim by an immediate explicit adaptation of the public single-multicrossing theorem (2012 preprint, later publication). It receives ZERO under the complete classical-bridge rule. No new theorem or Pi task is proposed. The latest source's omission is reported, not interpreted as a published refutation. A direct finite diagram, a knot invariant certificate and a formal PL isotopy development are not needed to manufacture a credit claim for an excluded classical consequence. Printed figures were not visually audited; the argument relies on the actual theorem and its analytic construction, not a guessed picture.

## 316624: representation compression, sustained but incomplete

Read source Conjectures 5.1 and 5.2, Theorems 2.4 and 2.5 and the nearby proofs, plus the 2009 printed journal statement. For a permutation representation rho of G, d is the sum of the squared degrees of its distinct nontrivial complex irreducible constituents. Multiplicities do not contribute to d. Stable equivalence means the same nontrivial constituents, and supplies affine equivalence; it is not automatically lattice equivalence.

Attempt 1: remove fixed points and duplicate orbits. This is safe for constituent support, but irredundant orbits can still share many constituents. The sum of the separate orbit dimensions double-counts this overlap, so it gives no global 2d bound.

Attempt 2: keep at most one orbit for each newly covered irreducible constituent. This bounds the number of chosen orbits by the number of constituents. It does not bound their degrees. The regular orbit has |G| points; selecting it introduces every missing constituent, including ones not present in rho. It cannot replace an arbitrary orbit cover while preserving the polytope.

Attempt 3: use a generic d-dimensional projection of the matrix affine span, retain d independent matrix-entry coordinates, and realize them by a permutation action on at most 2d points. The projection can be an affine injection, but selected entries are not a G-invariant coordinate set. Orbit closure can add arbitrarily many coordinates. Furthermore rational affine coordinates do not supply an integral unimodular map of the ambient affine lattices. The 0/1-polytope finiteness Proposition 1.3 supplies some bound, not 2d and not a permutation realization.

Attempt 4: the transitive multiplicity bound m_sigma <= deg(sigma) gives n-1 <= sum deg(sigma)^2 = d for a single transitive orbit. For a direct sum of transitive orbits, this controls each orbit but again fails to pay for overlapping constituents globally. The exact next lemma for the weaker affine route is: every occurring support of nontrivial irreducibles in a permutation character has a covering by transitive permutation characters with the SAME support and sum of their degrees at most twice the sum of squared degrees in that support. This is stronger than dimension counting and remains unproved. Even it leaves the stronger lattice clause unpaid. No full proof or counterexample; park. Journal/source-family/citing clearance incomplete, no novelty claim.

## 380352: pair contraction and partition compression, sustained but incomplete

Read Meagher thesis Section 7.5 definitions and Conjecture 7.5.1. S(n-1,k) is the Stirling number, counting partitions into k nonempty classes, not an unexplained free extremal parameter. Represent a partition P by E(P), the unordered pairs contained in a common block. Then partial 2-intersection is exactly E(P) intersect E(Q) nonempty.

Route 1: if all partitions contain one fixed pair {a,b}, contraction of that pair is a bijection to partitions of n-1 elements into k nonempty blocks. Expand the contracted point into a,b to invert it. This proves both the proposed extremal family's size and the conjecture for families with a common pair. Pairwise intersection does NOT imply a common pair. An intersecting family of pair-sets can have empty total intersection, so this is not the general endpoint.

Route 2: choose a pair separately for each P and contract it, hoping for an injection into the same Stirling set. Different contractions can yield the same target after relabelling. A missing Hall statement is that every subfamily has at least its own size many distinct allowed contracted outputs with a common encoding. Pairwise intersection of E(P) supplies no such neighbourhood bound. No Hall proof established; no generic matching program proposed.

Route 3: apply ordinary shifting to E(P) and then use intersecting-set theory. Edge sets E(P) are unions of disjoint complete graphs. Shifting individual edges can break transitivity of the underlying equivalence relation, so the image need not be any partition. Taking transitive closure changes the number of blocks. A valid shift must preserve k nonempty blocks, cardinality of the family and pairwise partial intersection. None proved.

Route 4: split families by whether two distinguished elements share a block. The common-pair part contracts to n-1,k. The other part is not forced empty, and must be charged against missing common-pair partitions. An exact deficiency injection is needed, not merely separate upper bounds summing to more than S(n-1,k). At the boundary n=k+1 the only possible nonsingleton block is a pair, hence distinct partitions cannot partially 2-intersect and the bound 1 is exact. At n=k+2 the possibilities already include one triple or two pairs; the general argument must handle their interactions. These structural boundary observations are ordinary reasoning, not an executed enumeration and not extra endpoints. Park pending a valid block-preserving compression or deficiency injection. Later uniform-partition results do not settle nonuniform n<=2k-1.

## 316761: complete routine existence bridge, no geometric-bijection claim

Source Proposition 3.2 proves equality of the bivariate perimeter generating functions and its footnote specifies separate horizontal and vertical perimeter preservation. Normalize polygons up to translation in the source's counting convention. At fixed horizontal and vertical perimeters there are finitely many boundary step words. Give each normalized polygon a canonical least boundary word, including the source's marked staircase/box data when that data is part of the object; order those finite codes lexicographically. Equality of the two generating functions gives equal cardinality of each bidegree fibre. Map the j-th member of the first fibre to the j-th member of the second fibre. The inverse uses the same rank. Union over all bidegrees is a full perimeter-preserving bijection.

This explicitly settles bare existence by a complete classical coefficient-to-finite-rank bridge, with zero credit. It does not supply the natural local geometric bijection motivating the source. No computational enumeration was run to define or test this abstract map. If the intended endpoint demands a natural geometric operation, that remains unproved. Source figures/marking conventions not visually audited, so no new source-qualified geometric endpoint is asserted.
