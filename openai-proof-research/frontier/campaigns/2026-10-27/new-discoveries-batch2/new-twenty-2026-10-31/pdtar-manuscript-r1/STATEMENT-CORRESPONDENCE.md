The star proof applies to any complete partition into a leaf part of size at least 3 and a singleton centre. Specializing the pair-fort proof needs only a nonempty opposite part. The centre is PD, while every leaf singleton is non-PD, witnessed by a fort on two other leaves. A supported fort containing the centre either is a proper component (contradicting PD at the centre), or is a pair fort. Swapping the two vertices of a pair fort preserves adjacency; the proved relabelling transport then equates their singleton PD status, again a contradiction. Thus the supported fort is the leaf pair. Its closed neighbourhood forces both centre edges, and a third leaf excludes every internal leaf edge. This proves labelled equality, a stronger conclusion than the required isomorphism. The two orientations are separate specializations of this generic argument.

For K(3,3), `small_k33_pair` proves every distinct pair is PD directly using at most one missing vertex after domination; `small_k33_singleton` supplies a fort obstruction to every singleton. These are statements about one fixed complete bipartite graph, not an enumeration of arbitrary graphs. For a comparison H, `small_k33_three_neighbors` uses a fort avoiding a neighbour's singleton to give three distinct neighbours at every vertex. If a degree were at least 4, its closed singleton neighbourhood would leave at most one unobserved vertex, and `small_step_of_one_missing` would make it PD. Consequently every degree is 3.

The final bipartition proof replaces the source's odd-cycle classification by a fort argument. Fix v and a fort F avoiding its closed singleton neighbourhood. Every b in B is adjacent to v; its fort condition forces adjacency to both members of F. Every vertex already has its three cross neighbours, so degree 3 excludes all internal edges.
