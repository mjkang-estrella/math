## 319972: rainbow perfect matching in a cycle decomposition

Source Conjecture 8 (1005.5469v1, lines 204–212) is about a 2d-regular graph, each colour class a single C_(2d). It is not a proper edge colouring: each vertex on a colour cycle has two incident edges of that colour. The source says d=2 has a proof by Király, credited as personal communication. That case cannot be a new outcome here.

First route: use regularity to force an ordinary perfect matching, then exchange its repeated colours. Double counting gives |E|=d|V| and |E|=2d c, so |V|=2c. In fact every connected component has even order, since each colour cycle is entirely inside one component. Thus odd order or an odd connected component cannot furnish a counterexample. This eliminates the first natural obstruction, but even regular graphs with even order need not have perfect matchings without further hypotheses. No applicable matching theorem covering this particular decomposition was established.

Second route: impose both vertex and colour equations. Assign x_e=1/(2d) to every edge. At each vertex the incident sum is 1; for each colour cycle the sum is 1. This is a feasible fractional exact cover. Its integral points are exactly the desired rainbow perfect matchings. The associated columns contain two vertex incidences and one colour incidence; it is not the bipartite graph incidence matrix to which total unimodularity would automatically apply. Neither the fractional point nor the usual matching-polytope theorem settles the added colour equations. An odd-cut check for ordinary perfect matchings would still omit those equations.

Third route: alternating exchanges. Start from a maximum rainbow matching M missing colour c. A c-cycle whose every edge meets V(M) has at least d covered vertices, but these can come from many matched edges. Replacing one M edge by a c edge can free a different colour while blocking the old partner. The cycle parity gives no demonstrated monotone augmenting path invariant. The missing lemma is a rainbow augmenting structure that strictly increases |M| while preserving distinct colours, or an explicit decomposition exhibiting a Hall-type obstruction to *all* such choices. No such structure or witness is available. A generic SAT encoding would not fill this gap. No Pi assignment recommended.

Printed-object comparison: source's periodic vector-pairing obstruction (directions (1,0),(0,1),(1,1)) concerns a more restricted construction; it is not a counterexample to Conjecture 8. No claim that the printed construction is subsumed or modified to obtain a new witness.

## 320583: normal-matrix Hausdorff distance

Read Conjecture 8 and its preceding definitions in both 1010.5167 versions. For n≥2, set S=union of spectra of principal (n−1)-compressions. Every eigenvalue of a compression is a Rayleigh quotient of A, so S⊆W(A). Hence the symmetric Hausdorff expression reduces to sup_(z in W(A)) dist(z,S). This is a sound simplification, not a solution.

Normalize a smallest enclosing spectral disk to centre 0, radius R=1. Since A is normal, W(A) is the convex hull of its eigenvalues. This only proves S and W(A) lie in the unit disk; a point of one set can be distance 2 from a point of the other. It does not give distance 1. A second proposed reduction, checking only vertices of W(A), is invalid: the distance to a union of points is generally not convex. Covering each extreme point by a possibly different disk around S does not cover the hull. Thus neither elementary normalization nor an extreme-point test closes the statement.

The principal-compression characteristic polynomials sum to the derivative of det(zI−A). But roots of a sum cannot be replaced by the union of roots, so this identity alone gives no covering theorem. More decisively, for A unitarily conjugate to a diagonal eigenvalue matrix by a flat Fourier unitary, all standard basis vectors are trace vectors; all principal characteristic polynomials equal (1/n) times the derivative. The target then contains Schmeisser's assertion for every point of the convex hull of arbitrary polynomial roots. The source explicitly states this bridge immediately after Conjecture 8. This is a demonstrated hard subproblem, not a proof or a routine-bridge exclusion. Missing lemma: a disk covering theorem for these compression spectra, including the identical-compression case. No finite rational normal matrix with a certified violating point was obtained. No n=1/empty-spectrum correction is proposed for credit.

## 320280: maximal degeneracy, source and prior obstruction

Read the transition rules (10→01,11→00; equal left creation/annihilation rates at λ=1 and right exit β), column-generator convention, M1–M3, Hadamard triangularization, Theorem 8, and the concluding admission that maximum degeneracy is unexplained. 'Exactly 2L eigenvectors' means total dimension of eigenspaces, not cardinality of the set of nonzero eigenvectors. Algebraic multiplicities or the characteristic polynomial alone do not determine this number.

A potentially finite route is to choose positive rational α,β away from collisions, exhibit one repeated eigenvalue with two independent kernel vectors, and add at least one eigenvector for every other distinct eigenvalue. That would contradict total geometric multiplicity 2L. To be substantive it must use an allowed nonsingular-rate instance, not α=0 or an eigenvalue collision excluded by the generic source convention. The triangularization does not automatically imply one Jordan block per eigenvalue: paths through different diagonal levels may create effective same-level couplings, so just counting visible nonzero off-diagonal entries is unsound. A Schur elimination at a repeated eigenvalue could give an exact rank certificate, but no such certificate was executed or invented here.

Before investing in it, live search returned the public trureturing page 'Ayyer–Mallick maximal degeneracy of the annihilation-process generator', claiming the same geometric-multiplicity refutation from L=5, sampled totals 8,12,16,26,40 for L=4…8, and an explicit kernel-vector family. Direct public fetch returned 403; stopped, with no alternate access attempt. The search excerpt is a routing/prior warning, not independently verified proof. There is no new candidate here, and no claim that this public proof is correct. Root would need lawful access to its exact witness/proof and chronology before any revived mechanism could be novel. It would be misleading to present the same L=5 rank-search idea as clear of prior art.
