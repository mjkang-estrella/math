                                                            On the Treewidth of Token and Johnson Graphs∗
                                                 Ruy Fabila-Monroy†              Sergio Gerardo Gómez-Galicia†‡                César Hernández-Cruz§
                                                                                         Ana Trujillo-Negrete¶
                                                                                              April 21, 2025




arXiv:2402.17962v3 [math.CO] 17 Apr 2025
                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                      Let G be a graph on n vertices and 1 ≤ k ≤ n a fixed integer. The k-token graph of G is the graph
                                                  Fk (G) whose vertex set consists of all k-subsets of the vertex set of G, where two vertices A and B are
                                                  adjacent in Fk (G) whenever their symmetric difference A△B is an edge of G. In this paper we study the
                                                  treewidth of Fk (G) when G is a star, path, or a complete graph. We show that in the first two cases,
                                                  the treewidth is of order Θ(nk−1 ), and of order Θ(nk ) in the third case. We conjecture that our upper
                                                  bound for the treewidth of Fk (Kn ) is tight. This is particularly relevant since Fk (Kn ) is isomorphic to
                                                  the well known Johnson graph J(n, k).

                                               Keywords: Token graph, Johnson graph, Bramble number, Treewidth
                                               MSC (2020): 05C05, 05C75, 05C76


                                           1     Introduction
                                           Let G be a graph on n vertices, and let 1 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 be an integer. The k-token graph of G, denoted
                                           by Fk (G), is the graph whose vertex set consists of all k-subsets of vertices of G, where two vertices A and
                                           B are adjacent in Fk (G) whenever their symmetric difference A△B is an edge of G. See Figure 1 for an
                                           example. The name “token graphs” comes from the following interpretation. Consider k indistinguishable
                                           tokens placed at the vertices of G, at most one token per vertex. Create a new graph whose vertices are
                                           all possible token configurations, and make two adjacent if one token configuration can be reached from
                                           the other by sliding a token along an edge to an unoccupied vertex. The resulting graph is isomorphic to
                                           Fk (G). This interpretation was given by Fabila-Monroy, Flores-Peñaloza, Huemer, Hurtado, Urrutia, and
                                           Wood [11]. However, token graphs have been defined independently several times and under different names
                                           (see [1, 3, 11, 15, 17, 19]).

                                           family of sets Vt ⊆ V (G), called bags, satisfying the following conditions:

The width of a tree decomposition (T, V) is max{|Vt | − 1 : t ∈ V (T )}, and the treewidth of G, denoted
tw(G), is the minimum width over all tree decompositions of G (see Figure 2 for an example). If the tree is
restricted to be a path, the decomposition is called a path decomposition and the corresponding parameter
is the pathwidth of G, denoted by pw(G). It is clear from the definitions that pw(G) ≥ tw(G). Some basic
examples include the facts that the treewidth of a tree is one, while the treewidth of the complete graph on
n vertices is n − 1. Moreover, if H is a subgraph of G, then tw(H) ≤ tw(G). In this paper, we establish
both lower and upper bounds for the treewidth of Fk (G) when G is a star, path, or a complete graph.
    For the k-token graphs of stars and paths, we establish the following results.
Theorem 1. Let Sn be the star graph on n + 1 vertices, and let 1 ≤ k ≤ n be a fixed integer. Then

                                          tw(Fk (Sn )) = Θ(nk−1 ).


Theorem 2. Let Pn be the path graph on n vertices, and let 1 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 be a fixed integer. Then

                                          tw(Fk (Pn )) = Θ(nk−1 ).

    We say that two subsets of V (G) touch if either they have a vertex in common, or there is an edge
between them in G. A bramble, B, is a family of pairwise touching subsets of vertices of G, each of which
induces a connected subgraph. A subset S of vertices of G is said to cover B, if every set of B contains a
vertex in S; we call S a hitting set for B. The order of B, denoted ||B||, is the least number of vertices in
a hitting set of B. The bramble number of G, denoted by bn(G), is the maximum order of a bramble of G.
Seymour and Thomas found the following duality theorem, which relates brambles and treewidth.
Theorem 6 ([18]). For any graph G,
                                                tw(G) = bn(G) − 1.
