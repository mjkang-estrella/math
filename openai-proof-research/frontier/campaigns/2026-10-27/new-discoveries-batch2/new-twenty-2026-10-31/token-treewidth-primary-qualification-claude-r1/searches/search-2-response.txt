I ran one search. It turned up no clean general formula for a product of d factors, but several relevant results for two factors.

- **Highly connected factors (Wood, 2011):** For k-connected graphs G and H, each with at least n vertices, the treewidth of their Cartesian product is at least k(n−2k+2)−1. For n ≫ k this lower bound is asymptotically tight for particular graphs G and H. (arXiv:1105.1586)

- **Connected factors with many vertices (Structural Properties of Graph Products):** This paper gives a bound for Cartesian products. If one factor has no small ε-separations and the other factor is connected with many vertices, the product has large treewidth. (arXiv:2110.00721)

- **Trees and grid minors (secondary review, The Moonlight):** This review reports that the Cartesian product of two connected n-vertex graphs contains an Ω(√n)×Ω(√n) grid minor. The bound is tight for a star and a tree, and the paper proves a sharper bound for that case. Because this is a third-party review, check it against the original paper before relying on it. (themoonlight.io)

- **Two-factor lower bound (Kaul, arXiv:2607.16778):** This preprint, dated July 2026, has a Cartesian product lower bound in its Section 2, but the search did not show its statement. Its strong-product pathwidth bound is pw(G⊠H) ≥ (pw(G)+1)(pw(H)+1)−1.

- **Related paper:** Kozawa, Otachi, and Yamazaki, "Lower bounds for treewidth of product graphs" (Discrete Applied Mathematics, 2014), appeared only as a bibliographic entry, so I could not check its results.

For d factors, I found no direct result. The most promising route is probably to apply the two-factor bounds inductively, using the fact that G□H is a factor of the d-fold product. I did not verify that route.
