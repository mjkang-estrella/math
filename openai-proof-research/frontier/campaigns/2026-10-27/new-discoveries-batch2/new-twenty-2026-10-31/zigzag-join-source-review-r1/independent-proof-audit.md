# Independent chain and period audit

All arguments here are symbolic, with no finite-state execution. Claims concern the corrected codimension-two family only.

Let disjoint sets A,B have cardinalities 4,7. Define K={X⊆A∪B: A⊄X and B⊄X}. Every face extends to a maximal face by adding all but one vertex from each block. Conversely such a face cannot be enlarged. Hence the facets are exactly (A\{a})∪(B\{b}), of cardinality nine, and there are 28. K is pure of dimension eight, rank nine, with eleven vertices.

A ridge of cardinality eight omits either (2,1) or (1,2) vertices from the two blocks; completing it to a facet gives exactly two choices. All lower rank-two intervals are Boolean intervals on two vertices. Adding bottom and top therefore gives the actual thin ranked face poset of the source. Facets are connected by replacing the missing A coordinate and then the missing B coordinate: each such replacement is ridge adjacency. Every face residue has the same join-of-proper-subsets form on the remaining vertices, so the corresponding adjacency connectivity holds there too. Adjacent swaps connect all orderings inside each facet; crossing a ridge connects flags on adjacent facets. This supplies flag connectivity, not only skeleton connectivity. Geometrically K=∂Δ³*∂Δ⁶, a sphere S²*S⁵=S⁸; polytopality also follows from the classified dual product, but topology is not needed for the period proof.

A maximal face chain is uniquely encoded by an ordered facet (v0,…,v8): its entries are recovered as the unique newly added vertices of prefix sets. Adjoin the empty face and the greatest element. For 1≤j≤8, changing the rank-j face with all other faces fixed exchanges v(j−1),vj. This follows from the two-element difference between the fixed neighboring prefix sets. Their sequential application moves v0 to the last position. Sigma9 then changes the facet above the fixed ridge {v1,…,v8}. Its unique alternative last vertex is the omitted vertex in the same block as v0. Thus actual T=sigma9⋯sigma1 sends

    (v0,v1,…,v8) ↦ (v1,…,v8, m_block(v0)).

The new missing vertex of that block is v0. This also verifies preservation of legal flags. It does not assume that a queue recurrence is the definition of a zigzag.

Let w be the binary block word and let Q_A be the A subsequence of the facet followed by its omitted A vertex; define Q_B likewise. This is an injective encoding: the word interleaves the first three A and six B queue entries and the last entries are omitted. One T step rotates w left; it rotates just the departing block's queue left once. Distinct labels imply that a queue of length q returns precisely at multiples of q. Induction gives the exact return criterion: w must return, 4 must divide the number of departed A letters, and 7 must divide the number of departed B letters. Sufficiency uses injectivity, so no missing phase condition is hidden.

Choose F=(a0,b0,b1,a1,b2,b3,a2,b4,b5), missing a3,b6. Its word ABBABBABB has least rotation period three: it repeats a three-letter block and is not constant. A return time is 3j, with j A and 2j B departures. The divisibilities are 4|j and 7|2j; equivalently 28|j. Therefore the least positive flag return is 84, not just a return bound.

Choose G=(a0,a1,a2,b0,b1,b2,b3,b4,b5), missing a3,b6. AAABBBBBB has rotation period nine: least rotation period divides nine; periods one and three are impossible. A return time is 9j, with 3j A and 6j B departures. Since gcd(3,4)=gcd(6,7)=1, the necessary and sufficient condition is 28|j. The least positive return is 252.

For reversal, the source triangular face array gives the reverse vertex ordering (v8,…,v0) in this simplicial case, by repeatedly taking the common subfaces. Reversal R is an involution and RTR=T^−1, as is also proved by the successor's Lemma 1. Thus R maps a T-orbit of least period L to an orbit of the SAME least period: an earlier return after reversal would imply an earlier original return. Identification with the reverse pairs whole circuits; it does not halve L. Consequently an 84-orbit cannot be the reverse, a cyclic re-rooting, or an automorphic image of a 252-orbit. Even self-reversal is absent here: T preserves the oriented circular order of the four distinct A labels, whereas R reverses it. A four-element distinct circular order differs from its reverse. No duplicate-unoriented-orbit artifact explains the two lengths.

For any seven-vertex face the omitted block counts are (3,1),(2,2),(1,3). The links are respectively the triangle boundary, the join of two two-point sets (a square), and the triangle boundary. Their numbers of facets are 3,4,3. On any nine-vertex facet, deleting two same-block vertices produces a length-three link; deleting different-block vertices produces a length-four link. The equivalence classes used in the primary characteristic-partition construction are therefore its three A vertices and six B vertices. More explicitly Definition 1.2's primed choices identify one added vertex per block; rejecting multiple occurrences gives exactly all facets omitting one of each extended block, so the abstract K(P) correspondence is exact.

The characteristic partition is (3,6), neither two equal halves nor all even, and K is not a simplex (11 rather than 10 vertices at rank nine). Intended Conjecture 4.9(i) predicts z-uniformity, contradicted by 84≠252. The pair satisfies gcd(84,252)=84; this does not refute the gcd addendum. Nor does a two-orbit demonstration prove that addendum for every other orbit length in K; no such universal claim is made. No assertion about all zigzags, orbit multiplicities, minimal dimension, or clauses (ii)–(iv) is needed or credited.
