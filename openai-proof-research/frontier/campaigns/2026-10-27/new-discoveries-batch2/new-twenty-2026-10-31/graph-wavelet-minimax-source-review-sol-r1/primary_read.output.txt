## 327053: two layers do not control subsequent shells

In R^10 with its standard inner product put h=(1/2,...,1/2,0), with nine half coordinates, and L=Z^10+Zh. Since 2h is integral, L is the disjoint union Z^10 and h+Z^10. The ten vectors h,e2,...,e10 are a real basis: the first coordinate of h is nonzero and the remaining vectors form the other coordinate axes. Their integer span equals L, since e1=2h-e2-...-e9. Every vector in the nonintegral coset has its first nine coordinates half-integral, so its squared norm is at least9/4. Equality holds precisely when those coordinates are each ±1/2 and the tenth is0. An integral vector has integral squared norm. The first two nonzero squared lengths of L are therefore1 and2; no nonintegral vector can interfere. Their shells are exactly X1={±ei} and X2={±ei±ej:i<j}. ## 332073: balanced cardinality need not minimize minimax support

Source actually read: Chen–Varma–Singh–Kovacevic arXiv1512.05406v1, p14 Definition9 (oriented incidence difference operator), p15 Definition10 and connected local sets, p17 Algorithm1 and basis construction, p18 Theorem8 orthonormality proof, Theorem9 sparsity proof, Corollary2, Conjecture1 and following community-size caveat. Take the simple undirected unweighted connected graph on vertices1,...,8 whose induced subgraphs on A={1,2,3} and B={4,5,6,7,8} are complete, with exactly one cross-edge {3,4}. Put K=1, permitted by Definition9. If a signal has different values at two vertices u,v in either clique, choose a third vertex w of that clique. Besides the inconsistent edge uv, at least one of uw,vw is inconsistent, since otherwise xu=xw=xv. Thus any signal with at most one inconsistent edge is constant on each clique. Conversely every x=a1_A+b1_B has no inconsistent clique edge and at most the bridge inconsistent. Therefore PCG(1)=span{1_A,1_B}, exactly, for all real a,b (including zero and a=b).

Choose an orthonormal basis F beginning f1=1_A/sqrt3 and f2=1_B/sqrt5. Complete on A with (e1-e2)/sqrt2 and (e1+e2-2e3)/sqrt6; complete on B with the four Helmert contrasts (sum_{i=1}^j e_{i+3}-j e_{j+4})/sqrt(j(j+1)), j=1,2,3,4. Each contrast has zero sum, norm1, and distinct contrasts have zero inner product (the later contrast is constant on the support of the earlier zero-sum one). Disjoint clique supports are orthogonal. There are eight vectors, so F is a full orthonormal basis of R^8. Every x in PCG(1) has at most two nonzero F-coordinates. The maximum is2, attained when a,b are both nonzero. In fact every basis has worst case at least2: a two-dimensional real subspace cannot be contained in finitely many coordinate lines; choose a vector outside the finitely many one-dimensional intersections. Hence the global minimax optimum equals2; this lower bound is not necessary for the strict counterexample but confirms the entire optimization target.

Here is a permitted perfectly even connected hierarchy for W. Split V into {1,2,3,4}|{5,6,7,8}; split the left child into {1,2}|{3,4} and the right into {5,6}|{7,8}; split each pair into singletons. Every child set induces a connected subgraph: {3,4} is the bridge, {1,2} is a clique edge, the right sets lie in K5; the left four-set is connected through3–4, and the right four-set is K4. Every split has exactly equal cardinalities. Algorithm1 therefore permits this hierarchy. Its normalized columns are the global mean 1_V/sqrt8; the root contrast (1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-1)/sqrt8; left contrast (1,1,-1,-1,0,0,0,0)/2; right contrast (0,0,0,0,1,1,-1,-1)/2; and four pair contrasts. Directly, norms1 and zero inner products follow from disjoint or nested supports and zero sums, so all eight columns form the required W.

For x=1_A, Delta x is nonzero only on {3,4}, so x is admissible. Its W-coordinates along the mean, root, left contrast and pair{3,4} are respectively 3/sqrt8,3/sqrt8,1/2,1/sqrt2; all are nonzero. All other columns have zero inner product with x. Thus its support is4 and max_PC(1)||W^T x||0>=4>2=max_PC(1)||F^T x||0. W is not a minimizer. This compares complete orthonormal bases for exactly the stated admissible class, not an overcomplete dictionary or merely an upper bound.

Robustness beyond this hierarchy: in any perfectly even hierarchy the global mean has nonzero coefficient on1_A, and the root contrast does too, since a split of the three A-vertices between two four-sets gives unequal integer counts. At least one four-set contains both A and B vertices, hence its restricted signal is nonconstant. Some descendant contrast in that set must then have nonzero coefficient, as these contrasts span its mean-zero space. Thus every perfectly even local-set basis has at least three coefficients on1_A and cannot attain2. The explicit legal hierarchy above additionally avoids any ambiguity over whether all connected graphs permit even partitions.

Failed/insufficient mechanisms: the complete graph with K=1 admits only constant signals, so it cannot provide a gap; the balanced3+3 bridge graph allows its balanced root to align with the only cut, so it also fails. Comparing the paper's upper bound K*log N to an alternative upper bound alone would not prove a counterexample. The unequal3+5 cliques isolate the full admissible subspace, and the explicit nonzero inner products establish a strict actual gap. The omission of a constant coefficient in the source's general sparsity bound is a separate issue: the present gap does not depend on correcting that bound and persists even if the mean is omitted (3 versus at most2).
