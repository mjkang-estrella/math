\begin{abstract}
We give an explicit binary saturated context tree, with exactly one infinite
branch and no infinite shift-stable subtree, equipped with fair probabilities
at every finite and infinite context. Its cascade series for the fixed
alpha-LIS $1$ converges with sum at most $2$, but admits no eventual bound
$Cr^n$ with $C>0$ and $0<r<1$. The index is the length grading of equation (5)
of C\'enac, Chauvin, No\^us, Paccaut and Pouyanne [1]. This contradicts the
explicit geometric-rate clause of their Conjecture 4.2, under this literal
rate interpretation. It does not refute the universal bare-convergence
conjecture. Classical prefix-code counting and Kraft's inequality receive no
novelty credit. A closed Lean certificate accompanies the construction;
formal checking supplies no literature-priority conclusion.
\end{abstract}
\section{Source conventions and exact claim}
A word is a finite string over $\{0,1\}$; $\epsilon$ is empty. Prefixes and
suffixes retain the written left-to-right order. A rooted context tree $T$
is prefix closed and saturated: every internal node has both children.
Finite contexts are leaves; infinite contexts are branches all of whose
finite prefixes are nodes. We require at most countably many infinite
contexts and a nontrivial tree. A probabilisation assigns to each finite
\emph{and infinite} context $c$ a positive law $q_c(0)+q_c(1)=1$.

For a nonempty noninternal word $w$, its LIS $s$ is its longest internal
\emph{strict} suffix. Write $w=\beta_1\cdots\beta_p\alpha s$.
The alpha-LIS is $\alpha s$. For any noninternal word $v$, let
$\operatorname{cont}(v)$ be its unique context prefix. With $\sigma$ deleting
the first letter, the source cascade is
\[
 \operatorname{casc}(w)=\prod_{i=1}^p
 q_{\operatorname{cont}(\sigma^i w)}(\beta_i).
\]
The minimality implicit in LIS makes each context in this product well defined.
For a context alpha-LIS $t$, the source series has terms
\[
 \kap_t(n)=\sum_{\substack{c\text{ finite context}:\ \alpha\text{-LIS}(c)=t\\
 |c|=|t|+n-1}}\operatorname{casc}(c),\qquad n\geq1.
\]
The certificate also sets the $n=0$ term, which is zero for $t=1$ here.

\section{Statement and tree construction}
A stable tree has $\sigma(T)\subseteq T$. By an infinite shift-stable subtree
we mean an infinite prefix-closed node set contained in $T$ and closed under
$\sigma$; our exclusion below even allows subtrees without saturation.
Define geometric decay to mean
\[
 \exists C>0\ \exists r\in(0,1)\ \exists n_0\ \forall n\geq n_0,
 \quad \kap_t(n)\leq Cr^n.
\]
The source's phrase ``geometrical rates'' is not a separate formal definition.
We use this standard length-based meaning, tied to its equation (5).
An exponential bound on nonnegative series tails implies this term bound,
so that stronger interpretation is excluded too.

\begin{theorem}
There is a non-null probabilised binary context tree with countably many
contexts and no infinite shift-stable subtree, for which $1$ is a context
alpha-LIS and
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}\kap_1(n)\leq2,\qquad
 \neg\exists C>0,\ r\in(0,1),\ n_0:\ \forall n\geq n_0,
 \kap_1(n)\leq Cr^n.
\]
\end{theorem}
Let $b=(01)^\infty$ and let $p_m$ be its prefix of length $m$. Put
\[
 N_0=0,\quad N_k=k^2(N_{k-1}+k+10),\qquad a_k=(01)^k00\quad(k\geq1).
\]
Set
\[
 I=\{p_m:m\geq0\}\ \cup\!
 \bigcup_{k\geq1}\{a_kv:|a_kv|<N_k\},\quad
 T=I\cup\{wa:w\in I,\ a\in\{0,1\}\}.
\]
The $a_k$ are prefix incomparable: for $j<k$, their letters at position
$2j+2$ (numbering from one) disagree. Each $a_k$ is the off-spine child
of $p_{2k+1}$. The recurrence gives strictly increasing depths and
$N_k>2k+2$; in particular $N_1=11$ and $N_2=92$.
The set $I$ is prefix closed. Completing it by both children gives precisely
$I$ as internal set: an internal descendant of a node outside $I$ would
contradict prefix closure of $I$. Thus $T$ is saturated. Each bush has all
its bottom leaves at depth $N_k$.

A branch leaving $b$ either reaches an ordinary leaf immediately or enters
one finite bush. Consequently $b$ is the unique infinite context. Finite
words are countable, so all contexts are countable. Define
$q_c(0)=q_c(1)=1/2$ on every context, including $b$. This is normalized and
uniformly positive. It represents a fair iid process; no new process-mixing
property is claimed.

\section{Excluding stable subtrees and fixing one alpha-LIS}
Every word of $T$ of length at least three starts with $01$.
Indeed spine nodes and all bushes do, and the only shorter off-spine
possibilities terminate by depth two. In particular $10,11\notin T$.
If a prefix-closed, shift-closed $S\subseteq T$ contained a word of length
at least three, its three-letter prefix $01a$ would belong to $S$.
Shifting gives $1a\in S\subseteq T$, a contradiction. Such $S$ therefore
has only words of length at most two, hence is finite.

Fix $k\geq3$ and put
\[
 H=N_{k-1}+3,\quad R=N_k-(2k+2)-H=kB+r,\quad0\leq r<k.
\]
Here $R\geq0$: using $N_k=k^2(N_{k-1}+k+10)$, subtracting
$N_{k-1}+2k+5$ leaves a positive integer for $k\geq3$.
For each independent choice of words $u_i$ of length $k-2$, take
\[
 w=a_k(11u_1)(11u_2)\cdots(11u_B)1^{r+H}.
\]
A block $11u_i$ has exactly $k$ letters; there is no trailing extra letter.
These words have length $N_k$ and are leaves in bush $k$. Fixed block
boundaries recover every free bit, so their number is exactly
$2^{(k-2)B}$.

\begin{lemma}
Every nonempty proper suffix of every selected $w$ is noninternal.
Consequently its LIS is $\epsilon$ and its alpha-LIS is $1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The terminal run has at least $H\geq3$ ones. Suffixes of length one and two
are $1,11$, neither internal. A longer suffix cannot be a spine prefix,
since an alternating spine prefix cannot end in $111$.

An internal suffix in bush $j<k$ would have length less than
$N_j\leq N_{k-1}$. It would lie wholly in the terminal ones, and could
not begin with $a_j$, which starts with zero.

For $j\geq k$, an anchor occurrence requires an initial alternating segment
$(01)^k$ of length $2k$. No such segment starts in the tail after $a_k$.
To see this explicitly, the forced $11$ pairs begin $k$ positions apart.
A start inside a block before the next block boundary encounters the next
complete pair within the next $k+1$ letters. If it is in the last block
and no next block exists, it encounters two terminal ones within the next
$k+1$ letters. A start at a boundary already encounters $11$, and a start
in the terminal run does as well. A $2k$ window, whenever it fits, thus
contains equal adjacent letters. This also covers $B=0$, when the whole
tail is ones.
\newpage
\noindent\emph{Completion of the suffix proof.}
A proper start within $a_k$ at a letter $1$ cannot start an anchor.
A start at even offset $2h$, $1\leq h<k$, leaves only $2(k-h)$ alternating
letters before the $00$ defect. A start at its penultimate zero meets
that defect at once. A start at its last zero meets a tail beginning $11$
and alternates for at most two letters. None admits the required $2k$
letters. At offset zero, $j>k$ is blocked by the same $00$ defect;
$j=k$ gives $w$ itself rather than a proper suffix. These possibilities
exhaust both kinds of internal nodes. The empty suffix is internal,
so the LIS is empty and the preceding letter is the final $1$.
\end{proof}

Also $1$ itself is a leaf with alpha-LIS $1$, so the chosen index belongs
to the source's set of context alpha-LIS.

\section{Counting and failure of geometric decay}
Fair probabilities give one factor $1/2$ for each letter preceding the
alpha-LIS. Thus a selected leaf has cascade $2^{-(N_k-1)}$. Since
$|1|=1$, the equation (5) index is exactly its length, not an arbitrary
enumeration of contexts. Define
\[
 F_k=(k-2)B,\qquad D_k=N_k-1-F_k.
\]
The selected leaves give
\[
 \kap_1(N_k)\geq2^{F_k-(N_k-1)}=2^{-D_k}.
\]
We have $F_k\leq N_k-1$, so $D_k\geq0$. Euclidean division gives
$B\geq R/k-1$ and hence
\begin{align*}
 0\leq\frac{D_k}{N_k}
 &\leq \frac2k+\frac{N_{k-1}}{N_k}+\frac{3k+5}{N_k}\\
 &\leq\frac2k+\frac1{k^2}+\frac{3k+5}{k^2(k+10)}\longrightarrow0.
\end{align*}
For the first inequality use
$N_k-1-(k-2)B\leq2N_k/k+(2k+2)+H+k$.
The recurrence gives the other bounds, as well as $N_k\to\infty$.

Suppose an eventual $Cr^n$ bound existed. The subsequence lower bound
would imply
\[
 -\frac{D_k}{N_k}\log2\leq\frac{\log C}{N_k}+\log r
\]
for all sufficiently large $k$. The left side tends to zero and the right
side to $\log r<0$, a contradiction. This proves the rate obstruction
with all constants and cutoffs quantified. We do not need an optimal rate
or an exact root-limit statement.

\section{Convergence and literal formal scope}
Finite contexts form a prefix-free family. For any finite subfamily,
the fair binary cylinder probabilities $2^{-|c|}$ sum to at most one,
because the cylinders are disjoint. Taking the supremum over finite
subfamilies gives the classical countable Kraft bound. In the subfamily
with alpha-LIS $1$, the cascade is $2^{1-|c|}$. Grouping nonnegative terms
by their lengths therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}\kap_1(n)
 =\sum_{\alpha\text{-LIS}(c)=1}2^{1-|c|}\leq2.
\]
This completes the theorem. Kraft and its application here are classical
helpers, not additional proposed outcomes.

The nine immutable mathematical Lean modules are DepthDefs, SourceDefs,

and the exact finite length sum. Its default definitions outside valid
