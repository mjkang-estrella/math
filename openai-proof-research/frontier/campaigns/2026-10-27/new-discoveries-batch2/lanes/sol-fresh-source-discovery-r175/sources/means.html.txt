Abstract
For 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
, let 
\( L=L(a,b),I=I(a,b),A=A(a,b),G=G(a,b),A_{p}=A_{p}(a,b)\)
 denote the logarithmic mean, identric mean, arithmetic mean, geometric mean and 
p
-order power mean, respectively. In 2003, Alzer and Qiu asserted “there does not exist a real number p such that 
\(\sqrt{LI}<A_{p}(A,G)<(L+I)/2\)
 is valid for all 
\(a,b>0\)
” , which inspired us to prove the assertion and to find new sharp bounds for the product and sum of logarithmic and identric means. Precisely, we proved that the inequalities 
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}< &   \sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}<A_{8/9}\left( A,G\right)<A_{1/2}, \\ A_{q_{0}}< &   A_{\ln 2}\left( A,G\right)<\frac{L+I}{2}, \\ \frac{L+I}{2}< &   \frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}<A_{1/2} \end{aligned}$$
hold for 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
, where 
\(q_{0}=\left( \ln 2\right) /\left( 1+\ln 2\right) \)
. These yield several nice chains of inequalities for means. Finally, a conjecture is proposed.

Introduction
Inequalities involving classical means—such as the arithmetic, geometric, harmonic, logarithmic, and identric means—form an essential foundation in mathematical analysis and have far-reaching implications in optimization and applied mathematics. These inequalities provide sharp bounds for integrals, series, and operator norms, enabling precise error estimates in numerical analysis and approximation theory. The study of mean inequalities not only deepens the theoretical understanding of functional relationships but also facilitates the design of robust computational methods across scientific and engineering disciplines. The logarithmic mean play a vital role in various fields of applied science. In thermodynamics and heat transfer, the logarithmic mean temperature difference (LMTD) is a fundamental parameter for accurately modeling heat-exchange processes. The identric mean, although less commonly used, has gained attention in information theory, statistical mechanics, and geometric function theory due to its intrinsic connection with entropy-like measures and exponential growth processes.

Throughout the paper, we assume that 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
. The logarithmic mean, identric mean and power mean of order 
p
 of two positive numbers 
a
 and 
b
 are defined by
$$\begin{aligned} L\equiv &   L(a,b)=\frac{a-b}{\ln a-\ln b}\text {, \ \ }I\equiv I(a,b)=e^{-1}\left( \frac{a^{a}}{b^{b}}\right) ^{1/(a-b)}, \\ A_{p}\equiv &   A_{p}(a,b)=\left( \frac{a^{p}+b^{p}}{2}\right) ^{1/p}\text { if }p\ne 0\text { and }A_{0}(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}, \end{aligned}$$
respectively. As special cases, 
\(A\equiv A(a,b)=A_{1}(a,b)\)
 and 
\(G\equiv G(a,b)=A_{0}(a,b)\)
 are the arithmetic mean and geometric mean, respectively.

$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{AG}<\sqrt{LI}<\frac{L+I}{2}<\frac{A+G}{2} \end{aligned}$$
hold, and conjectured that the best lower power mean bound for 
\(\left( L+I\right) /2\)
 is 
\(A_{q_{0}}\)
, where 
\(q_{0}=\left( \ln 2\right) /\left( 1+\ln 2\right) \)

$$\begin{aligned} A_{q_{0}}<\frac{L+I}{2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.1)

\(q_{0}=\left( \ln 2\right) /\left( 1+\ln 2\right) \)

$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}<A_{p}\left( A,G\right) <\frac{L+I}{2} \end{aligned}$$

\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)

) to hold is 
\(p\ge 8/9\)
 and 
\(p\le \ln 2\)
. Therefore, they asserted that “there does not exist a real number 
p
 such that (
1.2
) is valid for all 
\(a,b>0\)
”. Then a problem naturally arises.

                
Problem 1

                
Do the two inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}<A_{8/9}\left( A,G\right) \text { \ and \ }A_{\ln 2}\left( A,G\right) <\frac{L+I}{2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.3)
                
really hold?

              
On the other hand, it was established that the best power mean bounds for 
L
 and for 
I
:
$$\begin{aligned} G< &   L<A_{1/3}, \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.4)
                
$$\begin{aligned} A_{2/3}< &   I<A_{\ln 2}, \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.5)
                
where (
1.4
) was due to Lin [
7
], while (
1.5
) was proved in [
5
] by Stolarsky and [
6
] by Pittenger. These remind us to consider another problem.

                
Problem 2

                
Are the following comparison inequalities true?
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}<\left(>\right) \sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}\text { \ and \ }\frac{L+I}{2} <\left( >\right) \frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}\text {.} \end{aligned}$$

              
The aim of this paper is to give positive answers to the above two problems. Our main results are stated as follows.

                
Theorem 1

                
Let 
\(q\in \left[ 0,1/2\right] \)
 and 
\(p\in \mathbb {R}\)
. The following statements are valid.

                
(i) The inequality
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}<\sqrt{A_{q}A_{1-q}} \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.6)
                
holds if and only if 
\(q\in \left[ 1/3,1/2\right] \)
. Moreover, the upper bound is increasing in 
q
 on 
\(\left[ 0,1/2\right] \)
.

                
(ii) The inequality
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}<A_{p}\left( A,G\right) \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.7)
                
holds if and only if 
\(p\ge 8/9\)
.

              
Taking 
\(q=1/3\)
 and 
\(p=8/9\)
 in Theorem 
1
 gives the following corollary.

                
Corollary 1

                
The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}<\sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}<A_{8/9}\left( A,G\right) \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.8)
                
hold.

              
                
Theorem 2

                
Let 
\(q\in \left( 0,1/2\right) \)
. The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} A_{q}<A_{p}\left( A,G\right) <\frac{L+I}{2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.9)
                
hold if and only if 
\(p\le 0\)
 or 
\(q/\left( 1-q\right) \le p\le \ln 2\)
.

              
Taking 
\(q=q_{0}=\left( \ln 2\right) /\left( 1+\ln 2\right) \)
 and 
\(p=\ln 2\)
 in Theorem 
2
 gives the following corollary.

                
Corollary 2

                
The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} A_{q_{0}}<A_{\ln 2}\left( A,G\right) <\frac{L+I}{2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.10)
                
hold.

              
                
Theorem 3

                
The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{L+I}{2}<\frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}<A_{p}\left( A,G\right) \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.11)
                
hold if and only if 
\(p\ge 1\)
.

              
                
Remark 1

                
Clearly, Theorems 
1
–
3
 give positive answers to Problems 
1
 and 
2
.

              
                
Remark 2

                
Inequalities (
1.8
) show that the mean 
\(\sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}\)
 separates the means 
\(\sqrt{LI}\)
 and 
\(A_{8/9}\left( A,G\right) \)
; Inequalities (
1.10
) strengthen Alzer and Qiu’s (
1.1
); Inequalities (
1.11
) for 
\(p=1\)
 indicate that it can also be inserted 
\(A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}\)
 between 
\(L+I\)
 and 
\(A+G\)
.

              
                
Remark 3

                
From the first inequalities of (
1.8
) and (
1.11
), it will be asked, are the inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}<\sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}<\frac{L+I}{2}<\frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2} \end{aligned}$$
valid? Unfortunately, the answer is negative because that 
\(\sqrt{ A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}\)
 and 
\(\left( L+I\right) /2\)
 are not comparable for all 
\( a,b>0 \)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
.

              
Let 
M
(

,

) be a homogeneous mean of positive arguments 
a
 and

. Then
$$\begin{aligned} M(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}M(e^{t},e^{-t}), \end{aligned}$$
where 
\(t=\ln \sqrt{a/b}\)
. If 
\(M\left( a,b\right) \)
 is symmetric with respect to 
a
 and

\(a>b>0\)
, and

,

 can be represented by
$$\begin{aligned} \begin{array}{ll} L(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}\dfrac{\sinh t}{t}, &  I\left( a,b\right) =\sqrt{ab}\exp \left( \dfrac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}-1\right) \\ A_{p}(a,b)=\sqrt{ab}\cosh ^{1/p}(pt), &  A_{p}(A,G)=\sqrt{ab}\left( \dfrac{ \cosh ^{p}t+1}{2}\right) ^{1/p}. \end{array} \end{aligned}$$

                    (1.12)

–
4

–
3

). In the last section, we present several remarks and a conjecture.
2 
Proof of Theorem 
1

                     
It is known that the function 
\(p\mapsto A_{p}(a,b)\)
 is continuous and strictly increasing on 
\(\mathbb {R}\)
. The following lemma shows that it also has log-convexity, which will be used in the proof of Theorem 
1
.

                
Lemma 1

                
The function 
\(p\mapsto A_{p}\left( a,b\right) \)
 is log-convex on 
\(\left( -\infty ,0\right) \)
 and log-concave on 
\(\left( 0,\infty \right) \)
 
.

              
To prove Theorem 
1
, we also need the following inequality.

                
Lemma 2

                
The inequality
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\sinh 3t}{3t}>\frac{\cosh 2t}{\cosh t}, \end{aligned}$$

                    (2.1)
                
holds for 
\(t>0\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
The inequality (
2.1
) is equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} \sinh 3t\cosh t-3t\cosh 2t>0. \end{aligned}$$
Expanding in power series yields
$$\begin{aligned}  &   \sinh 3t\cosh t-3t\cosh 2t=\frac{1}{2}\sinh 2t+\frac{1}{2}\sinh 4t-3t\cosh 2t\\  &   \qquad =\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }\frac{2^{2n}}{\left( 2n+1\right) !}t^{2n+1}+\frac{1}{2 }\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }\frac{4^{2n+1}}{\left( 2n+1\right) !} t^{2n+1}-3\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }\frac{2^{2n}}{\left( 2n\right) !}t^{2n+1} \\  &   \qquad = \sum _{n=2}^{\infty }\frac{2^{2n}-\left( 3n+1\right) }{\left( 2n+1\right) ! }\left( 2t\right) ^{2n+1}>0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(t>0\)
. 
\(\square \)

              
Now, we can prove Theorem 
1
.

                
Proof of Theorem 1

                
(i) The inequality (
1.6
) is equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} F_{q}\left( t\right) =\ln \frac{\sinh t}{t}+\left( \frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t} -1\right) -\frac{1}{q}\ln \cosh \left( qt\right) -\frac{1}{1-q}\ln \cosh \left( t-qt\right) <0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(t>0\)
. Expanding in power series gives
$$\begin{aligned} F_{q}\left( t\right) =\frac{1}{36}t^{4}\left( 3q-1\right) \left( 3q-2\right) +O\left( t^{6}\right) . \end{aligned}$$
If the inequality (
1.6
) holds for all 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
, then
$$\begin{aligned} \lim _{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{F_{q}\left( t\right) }{t^{4}}=\frac{1}{4}\left( q- \frac{1}{3}\right) \left( q-\frac{2}{3}\right) \le 0, \end{aligned}$$
which reveals that the necessary condition for the inequality (
1.6
) to hold for all 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
 is: 
\(q\in \left[ 1/3,1/2 \right] \)
.

                
Let 
\(\xi \left( q\right) =\ln A_{q}\)
. By Lemma 
1
, we have
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial }{\partial q}\ln \left( A_{q}A_{1-q}\right) =\xi ^{\prime }\left( q\right) -\xi ^{\prime }\left( 1-q\right) =\left( 2q-1\right) \xi ^{\prime \prime }\left( \theta \right) >0, \end{aligned}$$
where 
\(\theta \in \left( q,1-q\right) \)
. This conclusion indicates that the function 
\(q\mapsto \sqrt{A_{q}A_{1-q}}\)
 is increasing on 
\(\left[ 0,1/2\right] \)
. Thus, to prove the inequality (
1.6
) holds, it suffices to prove that 
\(F_{1/3}\left( t\right) <0\)
 for all 
\(t>0\)
. Differentiation yields
$$\begin{aligned} F_{1/3}^{\prime }\left( t\right)= &   \coth t-\frac{1}{t}+\coth t-\frac{t}{ \sinh ^{2}t}-\tanh \frac{t}{3}-\tanh \frac{2t}{3} \\= &   2\frac{\cosh t}{\sinh t}-\frac{t}{\sinh ^{2}t}-\frac{1}{t}-\frac{2\sinh t }{\cosh \left( t/3\right) +\cosh t} \\= &   -\frac{f\left( t\right) }{t\left( \cosh \left( t/3\right) +\cosh t\right) \sinh ^{2}t}, \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} f\left( t\right)= &   \left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}+\cosh t\right) \sinh ^{2}t-2\left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\cosh t+1\right) t\sinh t\\  &   +\left( \cosh \frac{t }{3}+\cosh t\right) t^{2}. \end{aligned}$$
Factoring gives
$$\begin{aligned} f\left( t\right) =t^{2}\left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}+\cosh t\right) \left[ \frac{ \sinh t}{t}-f_{1}\left( t\right) \right] \left[ \frac{\sinh t}{t}-\frac{1}{ f_{1}\left( t\right) }\right] , \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} f_{1}\left( t\right) =\frac{\cosh \left( t/3\right) \cosh t+1+\sinh \left( t/3\right) \sinh t}{\cosh \left( t/3\right) +\cosh t}. \end{aligned}$$
Simplifying leads to
$$\begin{aligned} f_{1}\left( t\right) =\frac{\cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }{\cosh \left( t/3\right) }. \end{aligned}$$
By Lemma 
2
 and 
\(f_{1}\left( t\right) >1/f_{1}\left( t\right) \)
, we arrive at 
\(f\left( t\right) >0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
, and hence, 
\(F_{1/3}^{\prime }\left( t\right) <0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
, with 
\(\lim _{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}F_{1/3}\left( t\right) =0\)
. Then the sufficiency follows.

                
(ii) The inequality (
1.7
) is equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {F}_{p}\left( t\right) =3\ln \cosh \frac{t}{3}+\frac{3}{2}\ln \cosh \frac{2t}{3}-\frac{2}{p}\ln \left( \frac{\cosh ^{p}t+1}{2}\right) <0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(t>0\)
. Expanding in power series gives
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {F}_{p}\left( t\right) =-\frac{1}{144}\left( 9p-8\right) t^{4}+O\left( t^{6}\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Then the necessary condition for the inequality (
1.7
) to hold follows from
$$\begin{aligned} \lim _{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{\mathcal {F}_{p}\left( t\right) }{t^{4}}=-\frac{1}{ 144}\left( 9p-8\right) \le 0. \end{aligned}$$
Since 
\(p\mapsto A_{p}\left( A,G\right) \)
 is increasing on 
\(\mathbb {R}\)
, to prove the sufficient condition for the inequality (
1.7
) to hold, it suffices to prove that
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {F}_{8/9}\left( t\right) =\frac{3}{2}\ln \cosh \frac{t}{3}+\frac{3}{4 }\ln \cosh \frac{2t}{3}-\frac{9}{8}\ln \frac{\cosh ^{8/9}t+1}{2}<0. \end{aligned}$$
Differentiating and simplifying yield
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {F}_{8/9}^{\prime }\left( t\right)= &   \frac{\sinh \left( t/3\right) }{2\cosh \left( t/3\right) }+\frac{\sinh \left( 2t/3\right) }{2\cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }-\frac{1}{\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}}\frac{\sinh t}{\left( \cosh t\right) ^{8/9}+1} \\= &   \frac{1}{2}\frac{\sinh t}{\cosh \left( t/3\right) \cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }-\frac{\sinh t}{\cosh t+\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}} \\= &   -\frac{1}{2}\left( \sinh t\right) \frac{-\cosh t-\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}+2\cosh \left( t/3\right) \cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }{\left( \cosh t+\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}\right) \cosh \left( t/3\right) \cosh \left( 2t/3\right) } \\= &   -\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sinh t}{\cosh \left( t/3\right) \cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }\frac{\cosh \left( t/3\right) -\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}}{ \cosh t+\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}}. \end{aligned}$$
It is easy to check that
$$\begin{aligned} \left( \cosh x\right) ^{9}-\cosh \left( 3x\right)= &   \cosh ^{9}x-4\cosh ^{3}x+3\cosh x \\= &   \left( \cosh ^{4}x+2\cosh ^{2}x+3\right) \sinh ^{2}x\cosh x>0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(x>0\)
, which, by a change of variable 
\(x=t/3\)
, yields 
\(\cosh \left( t/3\right) -\left( \cosh t\right) ^{1/9}>0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
. Hence, 
\(\mathcal {F} _{8/9}^{\prime }\left( t\right) <0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
, and then, 
\(\mathcal {F} _{8/9}\left( t\right) <0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
, which proves the sufficiency.

                
This completes the proof. 
\(\square \)

              
3 
Proof of Theorem 
2

                     
In this section, we will prove Theorem 
2
. For ease of reading, we divide Theorem 
2
 into two parts: Propositions 
1
 and 
2
.

                
Proposition 1

                
The inequality
$$\begin{aligned} \left( \frac{A^{p}+G^{p}}{2}\right) ^{1/p}<\frac{L+I}{2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (3.1)
                
holds if and only if 
\(p\le \ln 2\)
.

              
                
Proposition 2

                
Let 
\(q\in \left( 0,1/2\right) \)
. The the inequality
$$\begin{aligned} A_{q}<\left( \frac{A^{p}+G^{p}}{2}\right) ^{1/p} \end{aligned}$$

                    (3.2)
                
holds if and only if 
\(p\le 0\)
 or 
\(p\ge q/\left( 1-q\right) \)
.

              
3.1 
Proof of Proposition 
1

                        
The proof of Proposition 
1
 depends on the following lemmas.

                  
Lemma 3

                  
The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \exp \left( \frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}-1\right)> &   \frac{2}{e}\cosh t, \end{aligned}$$

                    (3.3)
                
$$\begin{aligned} \exp \left( \frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}-1\right)> &   \frac{2\cosh t+1}{3} \end{aligned}$$

                    (3.4)
                
hold for 
\(t>0\)
.

                
                  
Proof

                  
(i) It was proved in [
28
] that 
\(I>2A/e\)
, which, by the relations (
1.12
), yields (
3.3
).

                  
(ii) It was proved in [
11
] that 
\(I>\left( 2A+G\right) /3\)
 , which, by the relations (
1.12
), yields (
3.4
). 
\(\square \)

                
                  
Lemma 4

                  
Let 
\(p=\ln 2\)
 and 
\(t\ge \ln \left( 4+\sqrt{15}\right) \approx 2.063\)
. The inequality
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\frac{\sinh t}{t}>\frac{1}{ep}\cosh ^{1-p}t+\frac{1-p}{2ep^{2}}\cosh ^{1-2p}t \end{aligned}$$

                    (3.5)
                
holds.

                
                  
Proof

                  
Let
$$\begin{aligned} G_{1}\left( y\right) =\frac{1}{ep}y^{1-p}+\frac{1-p}{2ep^{2}}y^{1-2p}. \end{aligned}$$
Then the inequality (
3.5
) is equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} g\left( t\right) :=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sinh t}{G_{1}\left( \cosh t\right) }-t>0 \text { \ for }t\ge \ln \left( 4+\sqrt{15}\right) \text {.} \end{aligned}$$
Differentiation yields
$$\begin{aligned} G_{1}^{\prime }\left( y\right)= &   \frac{1-p}{ep}y^{-p}+\frac{\left( 1-p\right) \left( 1-2p\right) }{2ep^{2}}y^{-2p}, \\ g^{\prime }\left( t\right)= &   \frac{1}{2}\frac{\cosh t}{G_{1}\left( \cosh t\right) }-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sinh ^{2}t}{G_{1}^{2}\left( \cosh t\right) } G_{1}^{\prime }\left( \cosh t\right) -1:=G_{2}\left( \cosh t\right) , \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} G_{2}\left( y\right) =\frac{1}{2}\frac{y}{G_{1}\left( y\right) }-\frac{1}{2} \frac{y^{2}-1}{G_{1}^{2}\left( y\right) }G_{1}^{\prime }\left( y\right) -1. \end{aligned}$$
Substituting the expressions of 
\(G_{1}\left( y\right) \)
 and

Proof

\(t\in \left( t_{1},\infty \right) \)
. It then follows that
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( t\right)> &   \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( 0\right) =0\text { \ for }t\in \left( 0,t_{1}\right) , \\ \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( t\right)> &   \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( \infty \right) = \frac{1}{q}-\frac{p+1}{p}\text { for }t\in \left( t_{1},\infty \right) . \end{aligned}$$
Subcase 3.1:
 
\(p<2q\)
 and 
\(1/q-\left( p+1\right) /p\ge 0\)
. Then 
\( \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( t\right) >0\)
 for all 
\(t>0\)
, which implies (
3.12
).

                  
Subcase 3.2:
 
\(p<2q\)
 and 
\(1/q-\left( p+1\right) /p<0\)
. We have shown that 
\(\mathcal {H}_{p,q}^{\prime }\left( t\right) >0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( 0,t_{1}\right) \)
 and 
\(\mathcal {H}_{p,q}^{\prime }\left( t\right) <0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( t_{1},\infty \right) \)
. This together with 
\(\mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( 0\right) =0\)
 and 
\(\mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( \infty \right) =1/q-\left( p+1\right) /p<0\)
 reveals that there is a 
\(t_{0}>0\)
 such that 
\( \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( t\right) >0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( 0,t_{0}\right) \)
 and 
\( \mathcal {H}_{p,q}\left( t\right) <0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( t_{0},\infty \right) \)
.

                  
Taking into account Cases 1, 2 and Subcase 3.1, the inequality (
3.12
) holds for 
\(t>0\)
 if
$$\begin{aligned} \left( p,q\right) \in \left\{ p\ge 2q\right\} \cup \left\{ p<2q,\frac{1}{q}- \frac{p+1}{p}\ge 0\right\} =\left\{ p\le 0\right\} \cup \left\{ p\ge \frac{q}{1-q}\right\} , \end{aligned}$$
which proves the sufficiency, thereby completing the proof of Proposition 
2
. 
\(\square \)

                
4 
Proof of Theorem 
3

                     
In this section, we will prove Theorem 
3
. For ease of reading, we divide Theorem 
3
 into two parts: Propositions 
3
 and 
4
.

                
Proposition 3

                
The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{L+I}{2}<\frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (4.1)
                
hold if and only if 
\(p\ge 1\)
.

              
                
Proposition 4

                
The inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}<A_{p}\left( A,G\right) \end{aligned}$$

                    (4.2)
                
hold if and only if 
\(p\ge 1\)
.

              
The following lemma is needed to prove Proposition 
3
.

                
Lemma 7

                
Let
$$\begin{aligned} J_{1}\left( 3s\right)= &   27s^{3}\left( 3\cosh s+\cosh \left( 3s\right) \right) \\  &   -9s^{2}\left[ 2\cosh \left( 2s\right) +\cosh \left( 4s\right) +5\right] \sinh \left( 3s\right) +4\sinh ^{3}\left( 3s\right) , \\ J_{2}\left( 3s\right)= &   36s^{2}\sinh \left( 3s\right) \cosh s-108s^{3}\cosh \left( 2s\right) , \end{aligned}$$
Then
$$\begin{aligned} J_{3}\left( s\right) =\frac{J_{1}\left( 3s\right) -\sqrt{\cosh \left( 2s\right) }J_{2}\left( 3s\right) }{27s^{3}}>0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(s>0\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
Note that 
\(J_{3}\left( s\right) \)
 can be represented as
$$\begin{aligned} J_{3}\left( s\right)= &   4\frac{\sinh ^{3}\left( 3s\right) }{\left( 3s\right) ^{3}}-\left( 5+4\sqrt{\cosh \left( 2s\right) }\cosh s+2\cosh \left( 2s\right) +\cosh \left( 4s\right) \right) \frac{\sinh \left( 3s\right) }{3s} \\  &   +4\sqrt{\cosh \left( 2s\right) }\cosh \left( 2s\right) +3\cosh s+\cosh \left( 3s\right) , \end{aligned}$$
which can also be regarded as a cube polynomial
$$\begin{aligned} J_{3}\left( s\right) =\mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y\right) =4y^{3}-c_{1}y+c_{0}, \end{aligned}$$
where 
\(y=\sinh \left( 3s\right) /\left( 3s\right) \)
 and
$$\begin{aligned} c_{1}= &   5+4\sqrt{\cosh \left( 2s\right) }\cosh s+2\cosh \left( 2s\right) +\cosh \left( 4s\right) , \\ c_{0}= &   4\sqrt{\cosh \left( 2s\right) }\cosh \left( 2s\right) +3\cosh s+\cosh \left( 3s\right) . \end{aligned}$$
By the inequality (
3.7
), we have
$$\begin{aligned} y= &   \frac{\sinh \left( 3s\right) }{3s}=\frac{\sinh s}{s}\frac{4\cosh ^{2}s-1 }{3} \\> &   5\frac{\left( 2\cosh ^{2}s+10\cosh s+9\right) \left( 4\cosh ^{2}s-1\right) }{2\cosh ^{2}s+101\cosh s+212}=:y_{0}. \end{aligned}$$
Since 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}^{\prime \prime }\left( y\right) =24y>0\)
, we have
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y\right)>\mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y_{0}\right) +\left( y-y_{0}\right) \mathcal {J}_{3}^{\prime }\left( y_{0}\right) \text { \ for } y>y_{0}. \end{aligned}$$
If we prove that 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}^{\prime }\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
 and 
\( \mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
 then 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y\right) >0\)
, and the proof is done. To verify 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}^{\prime }\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
 and 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
, we let 
\(\cosh t=x>1\)
 and note that
$$\begin{aligned} \cosh \left( 2s\right) =2x^{2}-1\text {, \ }\cosh \left( 3s\right) =4x^{3}-3x \text {, \ }\cosh \left( 4s\right) =8x^{4}-8x^{2}+1. \end{aligned}$$
Then
$$\begin{aligned} y_{0}= &   5\frac{\left( 2x^{2}+10x+9\right) \left( 4x^{2}-1\right) }{ 2x^{2}+101x+212}, \\ c_{1}= &   4x\sqrt{2x^{2}-1}+8x^{4}-4x^{2}+4, \\ c_{0}= &   4\left( 2x^{2}-1\right) \sqrt{2x^{2}-1}+4x^{3}. \end{aligned}$$
Thus,
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {J}_{3}^{\prime }\left( y_{0}\right)= &   12y_{0}^{2}-c_{1}=12\left[ 5 \frac{\left( 2x^{2}+10x+9\right) \left( 4x^{2}-1\right) }{2x^{2}+101x+212} \right] ^{2} \\  &   -\left( 4x\sqrt{2x^{2}-1}+8x^{4}-4x^{2}+4\right) \\= &   4\frac{p_{8}\left( x\right) }{\left( 2x^{2}+101x+212\right) ^{2}}-4x\sqrt{ 2x^{2}-1}, \end{aligned}$$
where 
\(p_{8}\left( x\right) >0\)
 for 
\(x>1\)
 due to Example 
1
. It follows from Example 
2
 that
$$\begin{aligned} \left[ \frac{p_{8}\left( x\right) }{\left( 2x^{2}+101x+212\right) ^{2}} \right] ^{2}-\left( x\sqrt{2x^{2}-1}\right) ^{2}=\frac{\left( x-1\right) p_{15}\left( x\right) }{\left( 2x^{2}+101x+212\right) ^{4}}>0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(x>1\)
. We then deduce that 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}^{\prime }\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
.

                
Finally, we check that 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
. We have
$$\begin{aligned} \mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y_{0}\right)= &   4y_{0}^{3}-c_{1}y_{0}+c_{0}=4\left[ 5 \frac{\left( 2x^{2}+10x+9\right) \left( 4x^{2}-1\right) }{2x^{2}+101x+212} \right] ^{3} \\  &   -\left( 4x\sqrt{2x^{2}-1}+8x^{4}-4x^{2}+4\right) 5\frac{\left( 2x^{2}+10x+9\right) \left( 4x^{2}-1\right) }{2x^{2}+101x+212} \\  &   +4\left( 2x^{2}-1\right) \sqrt{2x^{2}-1}+4x^{3} \\= &   \frac{4\left( x-1\right) ^{2}}{\left( 2x^{2}+101x+212\right) ^{3}}\left[ p_{10}\left( x\right) -\sqrt{2x^{2}-1}p_{7}\left( x\right) \right] , \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} p_{7}\left( x\right)= &   4\left( 2x^{2}+101x+212\right) ^{2}\left( 10x^{3}+69x^{2}+120x+53\right) , \\ p_{10}\left( x\right)= &   63\,680x^{10}+1053\,440x^{9}+6612\,480x^{8}+20\,126\,008x^{7} \\  &   +30\,975\,008x^{6}+21\,605\,538x^{5}+2993\,233x^{4} \\  &   -281\,002x^{3}+7205\,820x^{2}+7733\,240x+1931\,355. \end{aligned}$$
Due to 
\(p_{7}\left( x\right) ,p_{10}\left( x\right) >0\)
 for 
\(x>1\)
, it follow from Example 
3
 that
$$\begin{aligned} p_{10}\left( x\right) ^{2}-\left( 2x^{2}-1\right) p_{7}\left( x\right) ^{2}=\left( x-1\right) p_{19}\left( x\right) >0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(x>1\)
. We thus conclude that 
\(\mathcal {J}_{3}\left( y_{0}\right) >0\)
.

                
This completes the proof. 
\(\square \)

              
Now, we prove the first part of Theorem 
3
, that is, Proposition 
3
.

                
Proof of Proposition 3

                
The inequalities (
4.1
) are equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{\sinh t}{t}+\exp \left( \dfrac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}-1\right) <\left( \cosh \dfrac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}+\left( \cosh \dfrac{2t}{3}\right) ^{3/2} \end{aligned}$$

                    (4.3)
                
for 
\(t>0\)
. To prove the inequality (
4.3
), it suffices to prove
$$\begin{aligned} J\left( t\right)= &   1+\frac{\sinh t}{t}\exp \left( -\frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t} +1\right) \\  &   -\left[ \left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}+\left( \cosh \frac{2t}{3} \right) ^{3/2}\right] \exp \left( -\frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}+1\right) <0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(t>0\)
. Differentiation yields
$$\begin{aligned} \exp \left( \frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}-1\right) J^{\prime }\left( t\right)= &   \frac{t^{2}-\sinh ^{2}t}{t^{2}\sinh t}+\frac{\cosh t\sinh t-t}{\sinh ^{2}t} \\  &   \times \left[ \left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}+\left( \cosh \frac{2t}{3 }\right) ^{3/2}\right] \\  &   -\left[ \sinh \frac{t}{3}\cosh ^{2}\frac{t}{3}+\sqrt{\cosh \frac{2t}{3}} \sinh \frac{2t}{3}\right] . \end{aligned}$$
Note that
$$\begin{aligned} \left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}= &   \frac{3}{4}\cosh \frac{t}{3}+\frac{1 }{4}\cosh t, \\ \sinh \frac{t}{3}\cosh ^{2}\frac{t}{3}= &   \frac{1}{4}\sinh \frac{t}{3}+\frac{ 1}{4}\sinh t. \end{aligned}$$
Then
$$\begin{aligned} -4t^{2}\sinh ^{2}t\exp \left( \frac{t\cosh t}{\sinh t}-1\right) J^{\prime }\left( t\right) =J_{1}\left( t\right) -\sqrt{\cosh \frac{2t}{3}}J_{2}\left( t\right) , \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} J_{1}\left( t\right)= &   t^{3}\left( 3\cosh \frac{t}{3}+\cosh t\right) +4\sinh ^{3}t \\  &   -t^{2}\left( 3\cosh \frac{t}{3}\cosh t-\sinh \frac{t}{3}\sinh t+5\right) \sinh t, \\ J_{2}\left( t\right)= &   4t^{2}\left( \cosh \frac{2t}{3}\cosh t-\sinh \frac{2t }{3}\sinh t\right) \sinh t-4t^{3}\cosh \frac{2t}{3}. \end{aligned}$$
Let 
\(t=3s>0\)
. Then
$$\begin{aligned} J_{1}\left( 3s\right)= &   27s^{3}\left( 3\cosh s+\cosh \left( 3s\right) \right) \\  &   -9s^{2}\left[ 2\cosh \left( 2s\right) +\cosh \left( 4s\right) +5\right] \sinh \left( 3s\right) +4\sinh ^{3}\left( 3s\right) , \\ J_{2}\left( 3s\right)= &   36s^{2}\sinh \left( 3s\right) \cosh s-108s^{3}\cosh \left( 2s\right) , \end{aligned}$$
and
$$\begin{aligned} J_{3}\left( s\right) :=\frac{J_{1}\left( 3s\right) -\sqrt{\cosh \left( 2s\right) }J_{2}\left( 3s\right) }{27s^{3}}. \end{aligned}$$
By Lemma 
7
, we get 
\(J_{3}\left( s\right) >0\)
. Then 
\(J^{\prime }\left( t\right) <0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
, and hence, 
\(J\left( t\right) <\lim _{t\rightarrow 0}J\left( t\right) =0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
.

                
This completes the proof of proposition 
3
. 
\(\square \)

              
Next, we prove the second part of Theorem 
3
, that is, Proposition 
4
.

                
Proof of Proposition 4

                
The inequalities (
4.2
) are equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} \left( \cosh \dfrac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}+\left( \cosh \dfrac{2t}{3}\right) ^{3/2}<2\left( \dfrac{\cosh ^{p}t+1}{2}\right) ^{1/p} \end{aligned}$$

                    (4.4)
                
for 
\(t>0\)
.

                
The necessary condition for inequality (
4.4
) to hold for 
\(t>0\)
 follows from
$$\begin{aligned} \lim _{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{4}}\left[ \left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}+\left( \cosh \frac{2t}{3}\right) ^{3/2}-2\left( \frac{\cosh ^{p}t+1}{2} \right) ^{1/p}\right] =-\frac{1}{16}\left( p-1\right) \le 0. \end{aligned}$$
Since 
\(p\mapsto A_{p}\left( A,G\right) \)
 is increasing on 
\(\mathbb {R}\)
, to prove the sufficient condition for inequality (
4.4
) to hold for 
\(t>0\)
, it suffices to prove that
$$\begin{aligned} \left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}+\left( \cosh \frac{2t}{3}\right) ^{3/2}<\cosh t+1, \end{aligned}$$
which, due to
$$\begin{aligned} \cosh t+1-\left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}\right) ^{3}=\frac{1}{4}\left( 3\cosh t-3\cosh \frac{t}{3}+4\right) , \end{aligned}$$
is equivalent to
$$\begin{aligned} K\left( t\right) =\frac{3}{2}\ln \cosh \frac{2t}{3}-\ln \left( 3\cosh t-3\cosh \frac{t}{3}+4\right) +\ln 4<0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(t>0\)
. Differentiation yields
$$\begin{aligned} K^{\prime }\left( t\right)= &   \frac{\sinh \left( 2t/3\right) }{\cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }+\frac{\sinh \left( t/3\right) -3\sinh t}{3\cosh t-3\cosh \left( t/3\right) +4} \\= &   \frac{K_{1}\left( t\right) }{\left( 3\cosh t-3\cosh \left( t/3\right) +4\right) \cosh \left( 2t/3\right) }, \end{aligned}$$
where
$$\begin{aligned} K_{1}\left( t\right)= &   4\sinh \frac{2t}{3}-3\cosh \frac{2t}{3}\sinh t+3\sinh \frac{2t}{3}\cosh t-3\cosh \frac{t}{3}\sinh \frac{2t}{3}\\  &   +\cosh \frac{2t}{3} \sinh \frac{t}{3}. \end{aligned}$$
Simplifying leads to
$$\begin{aligned} K_{1}\left( t\right) =4\sinh \frac{2t}{3}-5\sinh \frac{t}{3}-\sinh t=-4\left( \cosh \frac{t}{3}-1\right) ^{2}\sinh \frac{t}{3}<0 \end{aligned}$$
for 
\(t>0\)
. Hence, 
\(K\left( t\right) <K\left( 0\right) =0\)
 for 
\(t>0\)
.

                
This completes the proof. 
\(\square \)

              
5 
Concluding Remarks
In this paper, we found new sharp bounds for the product and sum of logarithmic and identric means, which give positive answers to Problems 
1
 and 
2
, strengthen several known results. Precisely, we proved that the inequalities
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{LI}< &   \sqrt{A_{1/3}A_{2/3}}<A_{8/9}\left( A,G\right)<A_{1}\left( A,G\right) =A_{1/2}, \\ A_{q_{0}}< &   A_{\ln 2}\left( A,G\right)<\frac{L+I}{2}<\frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3} }{2}<A_{1/2} \end{aligned}$$
hold for 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
, where 
\(q_{0}=\left( \ln 2\right) /\left( 1+\ln 2\right) \)
.
Finally, we present several remarks.

                
Remark 4

                
Using the second inequality of (
1.10
), (
1.5
) and (
1.11
) for 
\(p=1\)
, we have
$$\begin{aligned} A_{1/2}>\frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}>\frac{L+I}{2}>A_{\ln 2}(A,G)>I(A,G)>A_{2/3}\left( A,G\right) ; \end{aligned}$$
putting 
\(q=2/5\)
 and 
\(p=2/3\)
 of the first inequality of (
1.9
 ) reveals that
$$\begin{aligned} A_{2/5}<A_{2/3}\left( A,G\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Combining these and (
1.4
), we obtain a new chain of inequalities for means:
$$\begin{aligned} A_{1/2}> &   \frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}>\frac{L+I}{2}>A_{\ln 2}(A,G) \\> &   I(A,G)>A_{2/3}\left( A,G\right)>A_{2/5}>A_{1/3}>L>G. \end{aligned}$$

              
                
Remark 5

                
Taking into account the second inequality of (
1.4
) and (
1.10
), we can derive another new chain of inequalities for means:
$$\begin{aligned} A_{1/2}>\frac{A_{1/3}+A_{2/3}}{2}>\frac{L+I}{2}>A_{\ln 2}\left( A,G\right)>A_{q_{0}}>A_{1/3}>L>G. \end{aligned}$$

              
                
Remark 6

                
By the first inequalities of (
1.8
) and (
1.11
), we deduce that
$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{L}+\sqrt{I}<\sqrt{A_{1/3}}+\sqrt{A_{2/3}}. \end{aligned}$$
Similarly, we have
$$\begin{aligned} \root 4 \of {L}+\root 4 \of {I}<\root 4 \of {A_{1/3}}+\root 4 \of {A_{2/3}}. \end{aligned}$$
Repeating such step, we have, for 
\(n\in \mathbb {N}\)
$$\begin{aligned} L^{1/2^{n}}+I^{1/2^{n}}<A_{1/3}^{1/2^{n}}+A_{2/3}^{1/2^{n}}. \end{aligned}$$
From these we propose the following conjecture.

              
                
Conjecture 1

                
For 
\(\lambda \in (0,1]\)
, the inequality
$$\begin{aligned} L^{\lambda }+I^{\lambda }<A_{1/3}^{\lambda }+A_{2/3}^{\lambda } \end{aligned}$$
holds for all 
\(a,b>0\)
 with 
\(a\ne b\)
.

              
                
Remark 7

                
Although the results obtained in this paper are quite elegant, we have not yet found a mean that lies strictly between the geometric and arithmetic means of 
L
 and 
I
. Hence, identifying such a mean remains an open problem.

Appendix
Appendix
Recall that a formal power series 
\(P\left( t\right) \)
 given by
$$\begin{aligned} P\left( t\right) =-\sum _{k=0}^{m}a_{k}t^{k}+\sum _{k=m+1}^{\infty }a_{k}t^{k} \end{aligned}$$
is called an “Negative-Positive type power series”, or “NP type power series” for short, if its coefficients 
\(a_{k}\)
 satisfies (i) 
\(a_{k}\ge 0\)
 for all 
\(k\ge 0\)
; (ii) there exist at least two integers 
\( 0\le k_{1}\le m\)
 and 
\(k_{2}\ge m+1\)
 such that 
\(a_{k_{1}},a_{k_{2}}\ne 0\)
 . Similarly, 
\(-P\left( t\right) \)
 is called a “Positive-Negative type power series”, or “PN type power series” for short (see [
29
]). In particular, if 
\(a_{k}=0\)
 for 
\(k\ge k_{2}+1\)
, then 
\(P\left( t\right) \)
 is called a “Negative-Positive type polynomial of degree 
\(k_{2}\)
”, or “NP type polynomial” for short. Likewise, 
\(-P\left( t\right) \)
 is called a “Positive-Negative type polynomial of degree 
\(k_{2}\)
”, or “PN type polynomial” for short. The definitions of NP and PN type polynomials appeared first in [
30
].
For the NP or PN type power series and polynomials, Yang and his coauthors offered a simple but efficient sign rule in [
31
,
32
,
33
].

                  
Lemma 8

                  
Let 
\(P\left( t\right) \)
 be an NP (PN) type power series converging on the interval 
\(\left( 0,r\right) \)
 (
\(r>0\)
). (i) If 
\(P\left( r^{-}\right) \le \left( \ge \right) 0\)
, then 
\(P\left( t\right) <\left( >\right) 0\)
 for all 
\(t\in \left( 0,r\right) \)
. (ii) If 
\(P\left( r^{-}\right) >\left( <\right) 0\)
, then there is a unique 
\(t_{0}\in \left( 0,r\right) \)
 such that 
\(P\left( t\right) <\left( >\right) 0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( 0,t_{0}\right) \)
 and 
\(P\left( t\right) >\left( <\right) 0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( t_{0},r\right) \)
.

                
By Lemma 
8
, we immediately deduce the following sign rule for an NP type polynomial.

                  
Corollary 3

                  
Let 
\(P\left( t\right) \)
 be an NP (PN) type polynomial. Then there is a unique 
\(t_{0}\in \left( 0,\infty \right) \)
 such that 
\(P\left( t\right) <\left( >\right) 0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( 0,t_{0}\right) \)
 and 
\(P\left( t\right) >\left( <\right) 0\)
 for 
\(t\in \left( t_{0},\infty \right) \)
. Consequently, if 
\(P\left( t_{1}\right) <\left( >\right) 0\)
 for 
\(t_{1}>0\)
 then 
\(P\left( t\right) <\left( >\right) 0\)
 for all 
\(t\in \left( 0,t_{1}\right) \)
; if 
\(P\left( t_{2}\right) >\left( <\right) 0\)
 for 
\(t_{2}>0\)
 then 
\(P\left( t\right) >\left( <\right) 0\)
 for all 
\(t\in \left( t_{2},\infty \right) \)
.

                
As applications of Corollary 
3
, we list several examples, which will be used in the proof of Lemma 
7
.

                  
Example 1

                  
Let
$$\begin{aligned} p_{8}\left( x\right)= &   4792x^{8}+47\,192x^{7}+138\,706x^{6}+106\,756x^{5} \nonumber \\  &   -62\,943x^{4}-62\,580x^{3}-4505x^{2}-29\,324x-38\,869. \end{aligned}$$

                    (6.1)
                
Clearly, 
\(p_{8}\left( x\right) \)
 is an NP type polynomial with 
\(p_{8}\left( 1\right) =99\,225>0\)
, which, by Corollary 
3
, leads to 
\(p_{8}\left( x\right) >0\)
 for 
\(x>1\)
.

                
                  
Example 2

                  
Let
$$\begin{aligned} p_{15}\left( x\right)= &   22\,963\,264x^{15}+475\,251\,392x^{14}+4031\,694\,560x^{13}+18\,146\,471 \,168x^{12} \\  &   +46\,858\,638\,164x^{11}+69\,933\,448\,340x^{10}+57\,918\,918\,520x^{9}\\  &   +26\,394\,611\,472x^{8} +12\,291\,041\,907x^{7}+5447\,666\,219x^{6}\\  &   -12\,609\,807\,098x^{5}-23\,369\,566\,082x^{4} -15\,998\,875\,067x^{3}\\  &   -7020\,458\,075x^{2}-3790\,388\,273x-1510\,799\,161. \end{aligned}$$
Clearly, 
\(p_{15}\left( x\right) \)
 is an NP type polynomial with 
\( p_{15}\left( 1\right) =177\,220\,811\,250>0\)
, and by Corollary 
3
, 
\( p_{15}\left( x\right) >0\)
 for 
\(x>1\)
.

                
                  
Example 3

                  
Let
$$\begin{aligned} p_{19}\left( x\right)= &   4055\,142\,400x^{19}+138\,221\,260\,800x^{18}+2090\,122\,547\,200x^{17} \\  &   +18\,585\,072\,788\,480x^{16}+108\,658\,025\,241\,600x^{15}\\  &   +442\,835\,971\,127\,040x^{14} +1303\,435\,953\,487\,552x^{13}\\  &   +2842\,203\,921\,546\,176x^{12}+4710\,261\,666\,782\,240x^{11} \\  &   +6169\,332\,170\,083\,984x^{10}+6828\,743\,289\,081\,364x^{9}\\  &   +6885\,814\,076\,035\,736x^{8} +6221\,519\,599\,639\,009x^{7}\\  &   +4003\,891\,339\,924\,557x^{6}+234\,764\,775\,683\,145x^{5} \\  &   -3035\,827\,288\,115\,659x^{4}-3695\,331\,474\,882\,293x^{3}\\  &   -2226\,827\,762\,821\,265x^{2} -708\,495\,318\,911\,657x\\  &   -94\,515\,355\,320\,409. \end{aligned}$$
Since 
\(p_{19}\left( x\right) \)
 is an NP type polynomial with 
\(p_{19}\left( 1\right) =30\,011\,281\,060\,320\,000>0\)
, it follows from Corollary 
3
 that 
\(p_{19}\left( x\right) >0\)
 for 
\(x>1\)
.
