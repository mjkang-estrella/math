Though much is known about 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -lecture hall polytopes, there are still many unanswered questions. In this paper, we show that 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -lecture hall polytopes satisfy the integer decomposition property (IDP) in the case of monotonic 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -sequences. Given restrictions on a monotonic 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -sequence, we discuss necessary and sufficient conditions for the Fano, reflexive and Gorenstein properties. Additionally, we give a construction for producing Gorenstein/IDP lecture hall polytopes. Moreover, these polynomials are real-rooted and hence unimodal. The theory of lecture hall polytopes and lecture hall partitions is extensive [ 12 ] and many questions have been answered. Some particular motivating work includes the thorough study of Gorenstein properties for 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -lecture hall cones [ 1 ] . These results imply Ehrhart theoretic properties of the rational 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -lecture hall polytopes 𝐑 ( 𝕤 ) d {\bf R}_{({\mathbb{s}})}^{d} , but do not imply the same properties for ℙ ( 𝕤 ) d {\mathbb{P}}_{({\mathbb{s}})}^{d} . Additionally, the existence of a unimodular triangulation for the 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} -lecture hall cone of 𝕤 = ( 1 , 2 , ⋯ , d ) {\mathbb{s}}=(1,2,\cdots,d) was recently shown [ 2 ] . However, showing the existence or nonexistence of a unimodular triangulation of ℙ ( 𝕤 ) d {\mathbb{P}}_{({\mathbb{s}})}^{d} for most 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} is still an open question. This motivates the following unanswered questions: • For what 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} is ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} Fano, reflexive, or Gorenstein? • For what 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} does ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} satisfies the integer decomposition property? • If ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} satisfies the integer decomposition property, for what conditions will it admit a unimodular triangulation? In this paper, we answer these questions for particular large classes of 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} as progress towards a complete characterization. First we consider ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} when 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} is a monotonic sequence. In the two latter cases, we can also provided necessary and sufficient conditions for when ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} is Gorenstein. We continue to show that ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} satisfies the integer decomposition property for all monotonic 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} and show that in some special cases, we can prove that ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} admits a unimodular triangulation, which is a stronger condition. Concluding Remarks While we have been able to ascertain many previously unknown properties of lecture hall polytopes, full characterizations of all of these properties remain elusive. We conclude with two conjectures. Conjecture 5.1 . For any 𝕤 = ( s 1 , ⋯ , s d ) {\mathbb{s}}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{d}) , ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} is IDP. For many randomly generated 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} , we have found ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} to be IDP and we have been unable to find an example of a non IDP lecture hall polytope. Additionally, the convenient description of dilates of lecture hall polytopes, namely c ​ ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) = ℙ d ( c ​ s 1 , c ​ s 2 , ⋯ , c ​ s d ) c{\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})}={\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{(cs_{1},cs_{2},\cdots,cs_{d})} , suggests that one may be able to generalize our arguments for monotone sequences to arbitrary 𝕤 {\mathbb{s}} . Conjecture 5.2 . For any 𝕤 = ( s 1 , ⋯ , s d ) {\mathbb{s}}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{d}) , ℙ d ( 𝕤 ) {\mathbb{P}}_{d}^{({\mathbb{s}})} admits a unimodular triangulation. We have come across no examples of lecture hall polytopes which do not admit a unimodular triangulation. However, using Gröbner bases has not proved fruitful given that though a variable ordering and monomial ordering which yield a quadratic squarefree Gröbner basis seem to always exist, it is not always the same ordering. A positive answer to this conjecture would resolve Conjecture 5.1 as well. Moreover, a counterexample, or a positive partial result such as the monotone case would be of great interest.
