                                                   Abstract. We propose the Factorial Separation Conjecture (FSC), a




arXiv:2610.25661v1 [math.NT] 19 Oct 2026
                                                   strict strengthening of earlier factorial gap conjectures:
                                                                                 |n! − mk | > n
                                                   for all integers n ≥ 8 and m, k ≥ 2. The inequality excludes every
                                                   displacement up to and including the factorial index. We prove the
                                                   conjecture unconditionally for exponents divisible by three, for even
                                                   exponents at least four, and for every exponent at least two when the
                                                   largest prime divisor of the base is at most n + 2. For exponents divisible
                                                   by five, we prove the finite interval 8 ≤ n ≤ 1100 and all sufficiently large
                                                   indices, with an effective but uncomputed threshold. The conclusion
                                                   for every n ≥ 8 and every exponent divisible by five is proved under
                                                   the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) for a specified family of
                                                   Dedekind zeta functions. Here GRH asserts that their nontrivial zeros
                                                   have real part 1/2. The arguments combine valuations, cyclotomic
                                                   factorizations, cubic reciprocity, character estimates and explicit formulas.
                                                   All finite comparisons are justified in the text. The general conjecture
                                                   remains open.

                               7. Remaining cases
  The preceding results do not establish Conjecture 1.1 in full. They give
the following exact reduction.
Corollary 7.1. Conjecture 1.1 is equivalent to excluding, for n ≥ 8, the two
families
(7.1)             n! = a2 (b2 − 1),                  1 ≤ a2 ≤ n,
(7.2)            mℓ = n! + εp,                           n/2 < p ≤ n,
where a, b ≥ 1, m ≥ 2, ℓ ≥ 5 is an odd prime, p is prime and ε ∈ {−1, 1}. In
(7.1), one necessarily has gcd(a, b) = 1, b odd, gcd(b, n!) = 1 and P + (b) >
n+2. In (7.2), one may additionally require P + (m) > n+2. Unconditionally,
the subfamily ℓ = 5 may be restricted to 1100 < n < N5 , where N5 is the
effective uncomputed threshold of Theorem 6.10. Under the GRH hypothesis
of Section 6.5, the second family may instead be restricted to ℓ ≥ 7.
Proof. Choose a prime divisor ℓ of the exponent of a counterexample and
rewrite the same number as an ℓ-th power. For ℓ = 2, Theorem 2.6 gives (7.1).
Since a2 ≤ n, every prime divisor of a is at most n; applying Theorem 2.8
to the base ab therefore gives P + (b) > n + 2. Theorem 4.3 excludes ℓ = 3.
For an odd prime ℓ ≥ 5, Theorem 3.4 gives (7.2). Theorem 2.8 further
gives P + (m) > n + 2 in this family. Conversely, each displayed equation
violates the conjectured inequality. Corollary 6.7 and Theorem 6.10 give the
finite index restriction when ℓ = 5. The conditional restriction follows from
Theorem 6.12.                                                               □
   Thus the unresolved square case includes the Brocard–Ramanujan con-
jecture. At odd exponents, the remaining equations impose simultaneous
residue conditions at small primes. The unconditional analytic argument ex-
cludes all sufficiently large fifth-power displacement primes, but its threshold
has not been computed. Completing the fifth-power family without GRH
therefore requires an effective overlap with the finite interval proved here, or
another argument covering the remaining indices. In the global conditional
argument, the sole conditional input remains the detecting prime supplied
by Lemma 6.11.
