Conjecture 1. For every $a > 0$ and $b\in (0,1/2)$ , one has $$\begin{equation*} \mathbf{B}'_{a,b} + \mathbf{B}'_{a,b} \stackrel{d}{=} \mathbf{B}'_{2a, b} \times \left( 1 + \sqrt {\frac{\mathbf{B}_{a,1/2}}{\mathbf{B}_{b,1/2-b}}}\right). \end{equation*}$$ As can be seen from Proposition 5 , this conjecture is equivalent to the following non-conventional integral representation of a certain ${}_3F_2(1)$ , to be compared with the aforementioned Pochhammer formula: $$\begin{align*} & {}_{3}F_{2}\biggl [\begin{matrix} a+s/2\mskip 8mu\mskip 8mua+(s+1)/2\mskip 8mu\mskip 8mu1/2\\[1.0pt] a+1/2\mskip 8mu\mskip 8mua+b+1/2\end{matrix}\,;1\biggr ] \\ & \qquad = \frac{\Gamma (b-s)\,\Gamma (a+1/2)\,\Gamma (a+b+1/2)}{\Gamma (a)\, \Gamma (1/2-b)\,\Gamma (a+b)\, \Gamma (b-s/2)\,\Gamma (b+(1-s)/2)}\qquad \qquad \end{align*}$$ $\times { \int _0^1\int _0^1 u^{a-1}\,(1-u)^{-1/2}\, v^{b-1}\,(1-v)^{-1/2-b} \left( 1+\sqrt {\frac{u}{v}}\right)^s du \mskip 3mu dv,}$ or alternatively $$\begin{align*} & {}_{3}F_{2}\biggl [\begin{matrix} a+s/2\mskip 8mu\mskip 8mua+(s+1)/2\mskip 8mu\mskip 8mu1/2\\[1.0pt] a+1/2\mskip 8mu\mskip 8mua+b+1/2\end{matrix}\,;1\biggr ] \\ & \qquad = \frac{4\,\Gamma (b-s)\,\Gamma (a+1/2)\,\Gamma (a+b+1/2)}{\Gamma (a)\, \Gamma (1/2-b)\,\Gamma (a+b)\, \Gamma (b-s/2)\,\Gamma (b+(1-s)/2)}\qquad \qquad \end{align*}$$ $\times { \int _0^1\int _0^1 u^{2a-1}\,(1-u^2)^{-1/2}\, v^{2b-s-1}\,(1-v^2)^{-1/2-b}\, (u + v)^s du \mskip 3mu dv}$ for every $a > 0, b \in (0,1/2)$ and $s\in (-2a,b)$ . The non-conventional character of this representation comes from the square root inside the integral. In the cases $a = 1/2$ and $a = 1-b$ , we were able to show the representation essentially with the help of Thomae’s relations on ${}_3F_2(1)$ and the reduction of Appell series of the first kind, our main tools in the above proof. Unfortunately, we do not know as yet how to extend such arguments to the general case. There is a huge list of classic transformations on ${}_3F_2(z)$ displayed in Section 7.4. of Reference 9 , and in the case $a=1/2$ we have made a crucial use of Formula 7.4.1.11. therein. Our feeling is that these classic transformations might not be enough to tackle Conjecture 1 in general. In this respect, let us refer to Reference 19 for a recent and non-classical transformation on some ${}_3F_2(1)$ , which however does not seem to be very helpful for our problem. See also Remark 7 for another integral representation of some ${}_2F_1(z)$ which would give an answer to Conjecture 1 in the case $a+b =1/2$ .
