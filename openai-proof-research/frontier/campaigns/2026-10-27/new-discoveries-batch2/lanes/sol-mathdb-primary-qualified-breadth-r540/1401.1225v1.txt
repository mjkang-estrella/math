 Abstract 
 
 
 We study a min-max relation conjectured by Saks and West:
For any two posets  P P  and  Q Q  the size of a maximum semiantichain and the
size of a minimum unichain covering in the product  P × Q P\times Q  are equal.
For positive we state conditions on  P P  and  Q Q  that imply
the min-max relation. Based on these conditions we identify some new
families of posets where the conjecture holds and get
easy proofs for several instances where the conjecture had been verified before.
However, we also have examples showing that in
general the min-max relation is false, i.e., we disprove the
Saks-West conjecture. 
 
 
 Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010)
06A07, 05B40, 90C46. 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 This paper is about min-max relations with respect to chains and
antichains in posets. In a poset,  chains  and  antichains 
are sets of pairwise comparable and pairwise incomparable elements,
respectively. By the  height   h ⁡ ( P ) h(P)  and the  width   w ⁡ ( P ) w(P) 
of poset  P P  we mean the size of a largest chain and a largest
antichain in  P P , respectively. The product  P × Q P\times Q  of two posets  P P  and
 Q Q  is an order defined on the product of their underlying sets by
 ( u , x ) ⩽ P × Q ( v , y ) (u,x)\leqslant_{P\times Q}(v,y)  if and only if  u ⩽ P v u\leqslant_{P}v  and  x ⩽ Q y x\leqslant_{Q}y . 
 
 
 Dilworth  [ 2 ]  proved that any poset  P P  can be covered with a
collection of  w ⁡ ( P ) w(P)  chains. Greene and Kleitman  [ 5 ] 
generalized Dilworth’s Theorem. A  k k -antichain  in  P P  is a
subset of  P P  which may be decomposed into  k k  antichains. We denote
the size of a maximal  k k -antichain of  P P  by  d k ​ ( P ) d_{k}(P)  or simply  d k d_{k} 
if the poset is unambiguous from the context. The theorem of Greene
and Kleitman says that for every  k k  there is a chain-partition
 𝒞 \mathcal{C}  of  P P  such that
 d k ​ ( P ) = ∑ C ∈ 𝒞 min ⁡ ( k , | C | ) d_{k}(P)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\min(k,|C|) . In  [ 12 ]  Saks
proves the theorem of Greene and Kleitman by showing the following
equivalent statement: 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1 . 
 
 In a product  C × Q C\times Q  where  C C  is a chain, the size of a maximum
antichain  A A  equals the size of a minimum chain covering with
chains of the form  { c } × C ′ \{c\}\times C^{\prime}  and  C × { q } C\times\{q\} . In
particular this number is  d | C | ​ ( Q ) d_{|C|}(Q) . 
 
 
 
 The  Saks-West Conjecture  is about a generalization of
Theorem  1.1 . A chain in a product  P × Q P\times Q  is a
 unichain  if it is of the form  { p } × C ′ \{p\}\times C^{\prime}  or
 C × { q } C\times\{q\} . A  semiantichain  is a set  S ⊆ P × Q S\subseteq P\times Q 
such that no two distinct elements of  S S  are contained in an
unichain. With this notation we are ready to state the Saks-West
conjecture: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1 . 
 
 In every product  P × Q P\times Q  of two posets the size of a largest semiantichain
equals the size of a smallest unichain covering. 
 
 
 
 The conjecture had already been around for a while when it appeared in
print  [ 22 ] . Theorem  1.1  deals with the special case
of conjecture where one of  P P  and  Q Q  is a
chain. Several partial results mostly regarding special classes of
posets that satisfy the conjecture have been verified. 
 
 •  
 
 Tovey and West  [ 13 ]  relate the problem to dual pairs of
integer programs of packing and covering type and explain the
interpretation as independence number and clique covering in the
product of perfect graphs. Furthermore, they take first steps
towards the investigation of posets with special chain- and
antichain-decomposability properties. Using these they verify the
conjecture for products of posets admitting a symmetric chain
decomposition or a skew chain partition. This extends investigations
of the largest Whitney numbers, i.e., width, of such products
in  [ 8 ]  and  [ 21 ] . 
 
 •  
 
 Tovey and West  [ 14 ]  deepen the study of the conjecture
as a dual integrality statement in linear programs using a network
flow approach. 
 
 •  
 
 West  [ 20 ]  constructs special unichain coverings for
posets with the nested saturation property. These are used to prove
the conjecture for products  P m × P m P_{m}\times P_{m}  where  P k P_{k}  is a member
from a special family of polyunsaturated posets introduced in  [ 19 ] . 
 
 •  
 
 According to the abstract of  [ 23 ] , Wu provides another sufficient
condition for posets to satisfy the conjecture. 
 
 •  
 
 Liu and West  [ 11 ]  verify three special cases of the
Saks-West Conjecture: 
 
 –  
 
 both posets have width at most  2 2 . 
 
 –  
 
 both posets have height at most  2 2 . 
 
 –  
 
 P P  is a  weak order  (a.k.a. ranking) and  Q Q  is a poset
of height at most  2 2  whose comparability graph has no cycles. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 This paper is organized as follows. In the last subsection of this
introduction we connect semiantichains and unichains to independent
sets and clique covers in products of comparability graphs. This is
used to reprove that products of posets of height 2 satisfy the
conjecture. In Section  2  we study  d d - and
 c c -decomposable posets. These are used to state conditions on  P P 
and  Q Q  that make  P × Q P\times Q  satisfy the conjecture. The main result
is Theorem  2.4 , it allows us to reproduce known results as
well as contribute new classes satisfying the conjecture. In
Theorem  2.6  we show that a new class of  rectangular  posets
has the property that whenever  P P  is rectangular, the
conjecture holds for  P × Q P\times Q  with arbitrary  Q Q . For negative, in
Section  3  we provide a counterexample to the Saks-West
Conjecture. In particular we can produce an arbitrary large gap
between the size of a largest semiantichain and the size of a smallest
unichain covering. In Section  4  we comment on some
natural dual versions of the Saks-West Conjecture raised by Trotter
and West  [ 15 ]  and conclude with open problems. 
 
 
 1.1  Products of comparability graphs 

 
 For a graph  G G , let  α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G)  and  θ ⁡ ( G ) \theta(G)  denote
the size of the largest independent set in  G G  and the minimum size of
clique covering of  G G , respectively. The comparability graph of a poset  ( P , ≤ ) (P,\leq) 
is denoted  G P G_{P} . The semiantichain conjecture has a nice
reformulation in terms of products of two comparability graphs, where the product  G ​ □ ​ G ′ G\Box G^{\prime}  of two graphs has an edge  ( v , v ′ ) ∼ ( u , u ′ ) (v,v^{\prime})\sim(u,u^{\prime})  iff  v = u v=u  and  v ′ ∼ u ′ v^{\prime}\sim u^{\prime}  or  v ∼ u v\sim u  and  v ′ = u ′ v^{\prime}=u^{\prime} . Note
that in general  G P × Q ≠ G P ​ □ ​ G Q G_{P\times Q}\neq G_{P}\Box G_{Q}  indeed if  u < P v u<_{P}v 
and  x < Q y x<_{Q}y , then  ( u , x ) < P × Q ( v , y ) (u,x)<_{P\times Q}(v,y)  by transitivity, but in
 G P ​ □ ​ G Q G_{P}\Box G_{Q}  there is no edge between  ( u , x ) (u,x)  and  ( v , y ) (v,y) .
In fact unichains in  P × Q P\times Q  and cliques in  G P ​ □ ​ G Q G_{P}\Box G_{Q}  are in bijection.
Hence, the following holds: 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   α ⁡ ( G P ​ □ ​ G Q ) \alpha(G_{P}\Box G_{Q})  equals the size of the largest
semiantichain in  P × Q P\times Q . 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   θ ⁡ ( G P ​ □ ​ G Q ) \theta(G_{P}\Box G_{Q})  equals the size of the minimum
unichain decomposition of  P × Q P\times Q . 
 
 
 Thus we can reformulate Conjecture  1  as: 
 
 
 Conjecture 2 . 
 
 For any two posets  P P  and  Q Q  it holds  α ⁡ ( G P ​ □ ​ G Q ) = θ ⁡ ( G P ​ □ ​ G Q ) \alpha(G_{P}\Box G_{Q})=\theta(G_{P}\Box G_{Q}) . 
 
 
 
 Using this version of the conjecture we now show: 
 
 
 Proposition 1.2 . 
 
 For posets  P P  and  Q Q  of height at most 2 the conjecture is true. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The comparability graph of a poset of height 2 is bipartite.
Next we observe: 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   If  G G  and  G ′ G^{\prime}  are bipartite graphs, then  G ​ □ ​ G ′ G\Box G^{\prime}  is again bipartite. 
 
 
 Bipartite graphs are perfect, hence in particular
 α ⁡ ( G ) = θ ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G)=\theta(G)  for every bipartite graph.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Remark:  The identity  α ⁡ ( G ) = θ ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G)=\theta(G)  used in the
proof can also be obtained directly from Dilworth’s theorem, we only
have to observe that if  G G  is bipartite, then  G = G P G=G_{P}  for some poset
 P P  and that  α ⁡ ( G ) = w ⁡ ( P ) \alpha(G)=w(P)  while  θ ⁡ ( G ) \theta(G)  equals the minimum
size of a chain decomposition of  P P . 
 
 
 
 
 2  Constructions 

 
 In this section we obtain positive results for posets admitting special chain and antichain partitions. Dual to the concept of
 k k -antichain we call a subset of  P P  a  k k -chain if it is the union
of  k k  disjoint chains. Similarly to  d k ​ ( P ) d_{k}(P)  we denote the size of a
maximal  k k -chain of  P P  by  c k ​ ( P ) c_{k}(P)  or simply  c k c_{k} .
The main tool for our proof is Theorem  2.1 , which has been
obtained by Greene  [ 4 ] . The theorem is a common generalization of the Theorem
of Greene-Kleitman and its dual which has also be obtained by Greene  [ 4 ] .
Both theorems have been generalized in several directions and
have been reproved using different methods. Surveys have been given by
Greene and Kleitman  [ 6 ]  and West  [ 18 ] . A more recent
survey on a generalization to directed graphs is  [ 1 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 2.1 . 
 
 For any poset  P P  there exists a partition
 λ P = { λ 1 P ≥ … ≥ λ w P } \lambda^{P}=\{\lambda^{P}_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda^{P}_{w}\}  of  | P | |P|  such
that  c k ​ ( P ) = λ 1 P + … + λ k P c_{k}(P)=\lambda^{P}_{1}+\ldots+\lambda^{P}_{k}  and
 d k ​ ( P ) = μ 1 P + … + μ k P d_{k}(P)=\mu^{P}_{1}+\ldots+\mu^{P}_{k}  for each  k k , where  μ P \mu^{P}  denotes
the conjugate to  λ P \lambda^{P} . 
 
 
 
 Following Viennot  [ 16 ]  we call the Ferrers diagram of
 λ P \lambda^{P}  the  Greene diagram  of  P P , it is denoted  G ⁡ ( P ) G(P) .
A poset  P P  is  d d -decomposable  if it has an antichain
partition  A 1 , A 2 , … , A h A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{h}  with  | ⋃ i = 1 k A i | = d k |\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}|=d_{k}  for
each  k k . This is,  | A k | = μ k P |A_{k}|=\mu_{k}^{P}  for all  k k .
Dual to the notion of  d d -decomposability we
call  P P   c c -decomposable  if it has a chain partition
 C 1 , C 2 , … , C w C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{w}  with  | ⋃ i = 1 k C i | = c k |\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}|=c_{k} , i.e.,
 | C k | = λ k P |C_{k}|=\lambda_{k}^{P}  for all  k k . Chain partitions with this
property have been referred to as  completely saturated ,
see  [ 13 ,  10 ] .

 For posets  P P  and  Q Q  with families of disjoint antichains
 { A 1 , … , A k } \{A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\}  and  { B 1 , … , B ℓ } \{B_{1},\ldots,B_{\ell}\} , respectively,

that can be obtained this way is called  decomposable
semiantichain , see  [ 13 ] . By our definitions we have the
following: 
 
 
 Observation 2.2 . 
 
 If  P P  and  Q Q  are  d d -decomposable with height  h P h_{P}  and  h Q h_{Q} ,
then  P × Q P\times Q  has a decomposable semiantichain of size 
 

 
 
 ∑ i = 1 min ⁡ ( h P , h Q ) μ i P ​ μ i Q . \sum_{i=1}^{\min(h_{P},h_{Q})}\mu_{i}^{P}\mu_{i}^{Q}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 In order to construct unichain coverings for  P × Q P\times Q  one can apply
Theorem  1.1  repeatedly. The resulting coverings are called
 quasi-decomposable  in  [ 13 ] . More precisely: 
 
 
 Proposition 2.3 . 
 
 In a product  P × Q P\times Q  where
 𝒞 \mathcal{C}  is a chain covering of  P P  there is a unichain covering
of size 
 

 
 
 ∑ C ∈ 𝒞 d | C | ​ ( Q ) . \sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}d_{|C|}(Q). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Use Theorem  1.1  on every  C × Q C\times Q  for
 C ∈ 𝒞 C\in\mathcal{C}  to get a unichain covering of size  d | C | ​ ( Q ) d_{|C|}(Q) .
The union of the resulting unichain coverings is a
unichain covering of  P × Q P\times Q .
∎ 
 
 
 
 The following theorem has already been noted implicitly by Tovey and
West in  [ 13 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 2.4 . 
 
 If  P P  is  d d -decomposable and
 c c -decomposable and  Q Q  is  d d -decomposable, then the size of a
maximum semiantichain and the size of a minimum unichain covering in
the product  P × Q P\times Q  are equal. The size of these is obtained by
the two above constructions, i.e., 
 

 
 
 ∑ i = 1 min ⁡ ( h P , h Q ) μ i P ​ μ i Q = ∑ j = 1 w ⁡ ( P ) d λ j P ​ ( Q ) . \sum_{i=1}^{\min(h_{P},h_{Q})}\mu_{i}^{P}\mu_{i}^{Q}=\sum_{j=1}^{w(P)}d_{\lambda^{P}_{j}}(Q). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Since  P P  and  Q Q  are  d d -decomposable, there is a
semiantichain of size  ∑ i = 1 min ⁡ ( h P , h Q ) μ i P ​ μ i Q \sum_{i=1}^{\min(h_{P},h_{Q})}\mu_{i}^{P}\mu_{i}^{Q}  by
Observation  2.2 . On the other hand if we take a chain
covering  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  of  P P  witnessing that  P P  is
 c c -decomposable we obtain a unichain covering of size
 ∑ j = 1 w ⁡ ( P ) d λ j P ​ ( Q ) \sum_{j=1}^{w(P)}d_{\lambda^{P}_{j}}(Q)  with

coincide. To this end consider the Greene diagrams  G ⁡ ( P ) G(P)  and
 G ⁡ ( Q ) G(Q) . Their  merge   G ⁡ ( P , Q ) G(P,Q)  (see Figure  1 ) is
the set of unit-boxes at coordinates  ( i , j , k ) (i,j,k)  with  j ≤ w ⁡ ( P ) = μ 1 P j\leq w(P)=\mu^{P}_{1} ,  i ≤ min ⁡ ( λ j P , h Q ) i\leq\min(\lambda^{P}_{j},h_{Q}) , and  k ≤ μ i Q k\leq\mu_{i}^{Q} .

 μ 1 Q + … + μ min ⁡ ( λ j P , h Q ) Q = d λ j P ​ ( Q ) \mu^{Q}_{1}+\ldots+\mu^{Q}_{\min(\lambda^{P}_{j},h_{Q})}=d_{\lambda^{P}_{j}}(Q)

∎

 Theorem  2.4  includes some interesting cases
for the min-max relation that have been known but also adds a few new
cases. These instances follow from proofs that certain classes of
posets are  d d -decomposable, respectively  c c -decomposable. 
 
 
 A graded poset  P P  whose ranks yield an antichain partition witnessing
that  P P  is  d d -decomposable is called  strongly Sperner ,
see  [ 9 ] . For emphasis we repeat 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   Strongly Sperner posets are  d d -decomposable. 
 
 
 For a chain  C C  in  P P  denote by  r ⁡ ( C ) r(C)  the set of ranks used by  C C .
A chain-partition  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  of  P P  is called  nested  if for
each  C , C ′ ∈ 𝒞 C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}  we have  r ⁡ ( C ) ⊆ r ⁡ ( C ′ ) r(C)\subseteq r(C^{\prime})  if
 | C | ≤ | C ′ | |C|\leq|C^{\prime}| . The most examples of nested chain partitions are
symmetric chain partitions. In  [ 9 ]  Griggs observes that
nested chain-partitions are completely saturated and that posets
admitting a nested chain partition are strongly Sperner. Hence we
have the following 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   Posets that have a nested chain partition are  d d -decomposable
and  c c -decomposable. 
 
 
 The fact that products of posets with nested chain partitions satisfy
the Saks-West Conjecture was shown by West  [ 20 ] . A
special class of strongly Sperner posets are  LYM posets . A
conjecture of Griggs  [ 7 ]  that remains
open  [ 3 ,  17 ]  and seems interesting in our context is that
LYM posets are  c c -decomposable.

 ∙ \bullet   Series-parallel orders are  d d -decomposable and
 c c -decomposable. 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   Weak orders are  d d -decomposable and
 c c -decomposable. 
 
 
 Since weak orders are a subclass of series-parallel orders
the second item follows from the first which is
implied by the following lemma. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.5 . 
 
 If  P P  and  P ′ P^{\prime}  are  d d -decomposable (resp.  c c -decomposable),
then the same holds for their series composition  P ∗ Q P*Q 
and their parallel composition  P + Q P+Q . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 For  d d -decomposability let  𝒜 = { A 1 , … , A h ⁡ ( P ) } {\cal A}=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{h(P)}\}  and  𝒜 ′ = { A 1 ′ , … , A h ⁡ ( P ′ ) ′ } {\cal A^{\prime}}=\{A^{\prime}_{1},\dots,A^{\prime}_{h(P^{\prime})}\}  be
witnesses for  d d -decomposability of  P P  and  P ′ P^{\prime} ,

 With the next result we provide a rather general extension of Theorem  1.1 , i.e., we exhibit a class of posets such that
every product with one of the factors from the class satisfies the
conjecture. 
 
 
 A poset  P P  is  rectangular  if  P P  contains a poset

 4  Further comments 

 
 4.1  A question of Trotter and West 

 
 In  [ 15 ]  it is shown that the size of a minimum semichain
covering equals the size of a largest uniantichain. They continue to
define a  uniantichain  as an antichain in  P × Q P\times Q  in which
one of the coordinates is fixed, and a  semichain  is a set
 T ⊆ P × Q T\subseteq P\times Q  such that no two distinct elements of  T T  are
contained in an uniantichain. They state the open problem whether the
size of a minimum uniantichain covering always equals the size of a
largest semichain. Recall the version of the Saks-West conjecture
stated as Conjecture  2 . A uniantichain is a
clique in  G P ¯ ​ □ ​ G Q ¯ \overline{G_{P}}\Box\overline{G_{Q}}  where  G s ¯ \overline{G_{s}}  denotes the
complement of the comparability graph of poset  S S . The question about
minimum uniantichain coverings and maximal semichains in products
therefore translates to the question whether 
 

 
 
 α ⁡ ( G P ¯ ​ □ ​ G Q ¯ ) = θ ⁡ ( G P ¯ ​ □ ​ G Q ¯ ) \alpha(\overline{G_{P}}\Box\overline{G_{Q}})=\theta(\overline{G_{P}}\Box\overline{G_{Q}}) 
 
 
 holds for all posets  P P  and  Q Q . We have: 
 
 
 ∙ \bullet   This duality does not hold in general. 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let  P ∗ P^{*}  and  V ∗ V^{*}  be the two-dimensional conjugates of  P P  and  V V 
from the previous section. The definition of conjugates implies
 G P ∗ ¯ = G P ¯ \overline{G_{P^{*}}}=\overline{G_{P}}  and  G V ∗ ¯ = G V ¯ \overline{G_{V^{*}}}=\overline{G_{V}} , hence,
 P ∗ × V ∗ P^{*}\times V^{*}  is an example of a product where the cover
requires more uniantichains than the size of a maximum semichain.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Our positive results may also be “dualized” to provide conditions
for equality and classes of posets where the size of a minimum

 4.2  Open problems 

 
 In the present paper the concept of  d d -decomposability is of some
importance. This notion is also quite natural in the context of
Greene-Kleitman Theory. We wonder if there is any “nice”
characterization of  d d -decomposable posets. Let  P P  be the
six-element poset on  { x 1 , x 2 , x 3 , y 1 , y 2 , y 3 } \{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}  with  x i ⩽ y 2 x_{i}\leqslant y_{2} 
and  x 2 ⩽ y i x_{2}\leqslant y_{i}  for all  i = 1 , 2 , 3 i=1,2,3 . Is it true that any poset
excluding  P P  as an induced subposet is  d d -decomposable? 
 
 
 Another set of questions arises when considering complexity
issues. How hard are the optimization problems of determining the size
of a largest semiantichain or a smallest unichain covering for a given
 P × Q P\times Q ? What is the complexity of deciding whether  P × Q P\times Q 
satisfies the Saks-West Conjecture?
In particular, what can be said about the above questions in the case that  Q Q  is the poset  V V  from Figure  3 .
