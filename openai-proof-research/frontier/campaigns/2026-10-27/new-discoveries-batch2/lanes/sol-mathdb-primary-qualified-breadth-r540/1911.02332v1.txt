 Abstract 
    
 Let  G G  be a graph and  a ⁡ ( G ) a(G) ,  LIF ​ ( G ) \textup{LIF}(G)  denote the maximum orders of an induced forest and an induced linear forest of  G G , respectively. It is well-known that if  G G  is an  r r -regular graph of order  n n , then  a ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 r + 1 ​ n a(G)\geq\frac{2}{r+1}n . In this paper, we generalize this result by showing that  LIF ​ ( G ) ≥ 2 r + 1 ​ n \textup{LIF}(G)\geq\frac{2}{r+1}n . It was proved that for every graph  G G ,  a ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ∑ i = 1 n 2 d i + 1 a(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2}{d_{i}+1} , where  d 1 , … , d n d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}  is the degree sequence of  G G . Here, we conjecture that for every graph  G G  with  δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 \delta(G)\geq 2 ,  LIF ​ ( G ) ≥ ∑ i = 1 n 2 d i + 1 \textup{LIF}(G)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2}{d_{i}+1} . 
  
 
 
 Keywords:  Induced forest, Induced path, Regular graph. 
 
 
 2010 AMS Subject Classification Number:  05C38, 05C85 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Let  G G  be a graph with vertex set  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  and edge set  E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) . The number of vertices and edges of  G G  are called  order  and  size  of  G G , respectively. In this paper, we just consider simple graphs. The complement of  G G  is denoted by  G ¯ \overline{G} . The degree of a vertex  v v  is denoted by  d G ​ ( v ) d_{G}(v) . Let  δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G)  be the minimum degree of  G G . For two positive integers  m m  and  n n  let  K n K_{n}  and  K m , n K_{m,n}  denote the complete graph of order  n n  and the complete bipartite graph with part sizes  m m  and  n n . Let  d 1 , … , d n d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}  be the degree sequence of  G G  and  t ⁡ ( G ) = ∑ i = 1 n 1 d i + 1 t(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{d_{i}+1} . For every  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G)  and any subgraph  H H  of  G G ,  d H ​ ( v ) d_{H}(v)  indicates the number of neighbors of  v v  in  V ⁡ ( H ) V(H) . Let  a ⁡ ( G ) a(G)  denote the maximum order of an induced forest in  G G . Let  LIP ​ ( G ) \textup{LIP}(G)  denote the order of a longest induced path in  G G . If every connected component of a forest  F F  is a path, then  F F  is said to be a  linear forest . Let  LIF ​ ( G ) \textup{LIF}(G)  denote the order of the largest induced linear forest in  G G .
The well-known Caro-Wei bound states that  G G  contains an independent set of size at least  t ⁡ ( G ) t(G) , see  [ 1 ] ,  [ 5 ] , and  [ 10 ] . In  [ 9 ] , it is shown that for a connected graph  G G  of order  n n  and size  m m ,  a ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 8 ​ n − 2 ​ m − 2 9 a(G)\geq\frac{8n-2m-2}{9} . The right hand side of this inequality is negative for  r r -regular graphs with  r ≥ 8 r\geq 8 , which is trivial. In this paper, we provide a non-trivial lower bound for  a ⁡ ( G ) a(G) , where  G G  is a regular graph. Moreover, there are some lower bounds on the order of the largest induced linear forest in a triangle-free planar graph and an outerplanar graph  [ 2 ] ,  [ 7 ] . If  δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 1 \delta(G)\geq 1 , then it is shown that  a ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ t ​ ( G ) a(G)\geq 2t(G) , see  [ 8 ] . Also, the author shows that for an  r r -regular graph  G G  of order  n n ,  a ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 r + 1 ​ n a(G)\geq\frac{2}{r+1}n . Here, we generalize this result to linear induced forest and prove that  LIF ​ ( G ) ≥ 2 r + 1 ​ n \textup{LIF}(G)\geq\frac{2}{r+1}n  for a general  r r -regular graph. Furthermore, we show that every  r r -regular graph  G G  with  LIP ​ ( G ) = k \textup{LIP}(G)=k  has order at least  2 ​ k + ⌈ − 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) r ⌉ 2k+\lceil-\frac{2(k-1)}{r}\rceil . In addition, there is a graph for which this bound is sharp. Next, we present a Nordhaus-Gaddum type inequality for  a ⁡ ( G ) a(G)  and provide a family of examples where equality holds for them, see  [ 6 ] . Finally, we present some computational results on order of the largest induced forest in a graph and conjecture that for every graph with  δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 \delta(G)\geq 2 ,  LIF ​ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ t ​ ( G ) \text{LIF}(G)\geq 2t(G) .

 3  Sharp Upper Bound for the Order of the Longest
 Induced Path in Regular Graphs

 Theorem 2 
 
 .  
For every  r r -regular graph  G G  of order  n n  with  LIP ​ ( G ) = k \textup{LIP}(G)=k , the following holds:

Each non-leaf vertex in  P P  has exactly  r − 2 r-2  neighbors outside of the path and each of the end vertices of  P P  has  r − 1 r-1  neighbors outside of  P P . On the other hand, each vertex of  G ∖ V ⁡ ( P ) G\setminus V(P)  is adjacent to at most  r r  vertices of  P P . Hence, there are at least  r ​ k − 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) r = k − 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) r {\frac{rk-2(k-1)}{r}}=k-{\frac{2(k-1)}{r}}  vertices in  G ∖ V ⁡ ( P ) G\setminus V(P) . Therefore,  G G  has order at least  2 ​ k + ⌈ − 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) r ⌉ 2k+\lceil\frac{-2(k-1)}{r}\rceil . Since the sum of all degrees in  G G  is even, if  r r  and  ⌈ − 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) r ⌉ \lceil\frac{-2(k-1)}{r}\rceil  are both odd, then the order of  G G  is at least  2 ​ k + ⌈ − 2 ​ ( k − 1 ) r ⌉ + 1 2k+\lceil\frac{-2(k-1)}{r}\rceil+1 . 
 
 
 We show that there are some graphs which meet the upper bound. Now, for every positive integer  r r , we construct an  r r -regular graph  G G  of order  n n  with  LIP ​ ( G ) = r \textup{LIP}(G)=r , where the equality holds. Consider a path of order  r r , say  P P , and a set of vertices  S S  outside of  P P . If  r r  is even, let  S S  consist of  r − 1 r-1  vertices. Otherwise, let  S S  contain  r r  vertices. Afterward, remove the edges of an arbitrary matching of size  r − 2 2 \frac{r-2}{2}  between the non-end vertices of  P P  and  S ∖ { v } S\setminus\{v\} . Now, join  v v  to all vertices of the removed matching. 
 
 
 If  r r  is odd, then pick  u u  and  w w  from  S S  and join all other  r − 2 r-2  vertices of  S S  to all vertices of  P P  to obtain  K r , r − 2 K_{r,r-2} . now remove a matching of size  r − 2 r-2  of this  K r , r − 2 K_{r,r-2} . Finally, join  u u  to one of the end vertices of  P P  and join  w w  to the other one. One can check that these graphs are  r r -regular and have a longest induced path of order  r r . Therefore, the equality holds for this family of graphs.

 Corollary 1 
 
 In any  r r -regular graph of order  n n  with  LIP ​ ( G ) = k \textup{LIP}(G)=k ,  k ≤ r ​ n − 2 2 ​ r − 2 k\leq\frac{rn-2}{2r-2} . 
 
 
 
 Note on Complexity.  In  [ 3 ] , it is shown that determining whether the graph  G G  has an induced path of length at least  k k , with arbitrary  k k , is NP-complete. Consequently, finding the LIP of the graph is NP-complete, too. However, while this theorem holds for general graphs, finding the longest induced path, to the best of our knowledge, is neither proved to be NP-complete, nor any polynomial time algorithm has been presented for, up to now. 
 
 
 
 4  Nordhaus-Gaddum Inequality for the Order of 
 Largest Induced Forest 

 
 In this section, we present a Nordhaus-Gaddum type inequality for the order of maximum induced forest and present a family of examples which the equality case holds for them. 
 
 
 Theorem 3 
 
 .  

Let
 G G 
be a graph of order  n n . Then,  a ⁡ ( G ) + a ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≤ n + 4 a(G)+a(\overline{G})\leq n+4  and this bound is sharp. 
 
 
 
 Proof.  
 
 Let  F 1 F_{1}  and  F 2 F_{2}  be the largest induced forests of  G G , and  G ¯ \overline{G} , respectively. By contradiction, assume that  a ⁡ ( G ) + a ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≥ n + 5 a(G)+a(\overline{G})\geq n+5 . Since  | V ⁡ ( F 1 ) ∪ V ⁡ ( F 2 ) | ≤ n |V(F_{1})\cup V(F_{2})|\leq n , we have  | V ⁡ ( F 1 ) ∩ V ⁡ ( F 2 ) | ≥ 5 |V(F_{1})\cap V(F_{2})|\geq 5 . Let  X = V ⁡ ( F 1 ) ∩ V ⁡ ( F 2 ) X=V(F_{1})\cap V(F_{2})  and  | X | = t |X|=t . With no loss of generality and using pigeonhole principle, the induced subgraph on  X X  in  F 1 F_{1}  has at least  ( t 2 ) 2 = t ⁡ ( t − 1 ) 4 \frac{\binom{t}{2}}{2}=\frac{t(t-1)}{4}  edges. Since  t ≥ 5 t\geq 5 ,  t ⁡ ( t − 1 ) 4 ≥ t \frac{t(t-1)}{4}\geq t  and the induced subgraph on  X X  in  F 1 F_{1}  contains a cycle we obtain a contradiction. Hence,  a ⁡ ( G ) + a ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≤ n + 4 a(G)+a(\overline{G})\leq n+4 , for any graph of order  n n . To prove the sharpness of the bound, we present a family of graphs for which the equality holds. Consider  P n P_{n}  for  n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . The path  P n P_{n}  is an induced forest and therefore,  a ⁡ ( G ) = n a(G)=n . Clearly,  P n ¯ \overline{P_{n}}  has an induced subgraph  P 4 P_{4} . Thus, we have  a ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≥ 4 a(\overline{G})\geq 4  and the proof is complete.
   □ \Box 
 
 
 
 Theorem  3  has an immediate corollary. 
 
 
 Corollary 2 
 
 Let  G G  be a graph of order  n n . Then,  LIF ​ ( G ) + LIF ​ ( G ¯ ) ≤ n + 4 \textup{LIF}(G)+\textup{LIF}(\overline{G})\leq n+4 . Moreover, this bound is sharp.
