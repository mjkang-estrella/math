# Complete uncredited helper: exact q-covering certificate

Let q be a nonempty word of length m. An infinite word V is q-covered precisely when (i) its prefix of length m is q, (ii) the ends e_0=m<e_1<... of successive occurrences of q are unbounded and every gap e_(i+1)-e_i is at most m. Necessity: position1 can only be covered by an occurrence starting1; any end gap larger than m leaves position e_i+1 uncovered. Sufficiency: intervals [e_i-m+1,e_i] start at1 and subsequent intervals touch or overlap, and their unbounded ends cover every position. This includes overlapping occurrences.

Maintain the suffix of length at most m of the processed output, age since the latest completed q (initially processed length), and whether a q has completed. When the new suffix equals q, reset age to0. Otherwise increment age, rejecting when it reaches m. Before the first completion this enforces prefix q; afterwards it enforces the gap bound. Only finitely many states exist. Thus an infinite run avoiding rejection is equivalent to q-coverage, not merely arbitrarily many successful finite checks.

For any fixed nonerasing f, process all output characters of each f(letter) as one input transition. Infinite input gives infinite output, so the inverse-image automaton accepts exactly {W: f(W) is q-covered}. Its nonempty infinite-path problem reduces to a reachable cycle in a finite directed graph. In particular every nonempty such language has an ultimately periodic input, but that input may be quasiperiodic and this observation alone does not prove weak quasiperiodicity.

For a specified input AB^omega, checks.py first processes A. It then processes complete periods B, storing the state at each period boundary. Rejection disproves coverage; repetition of a state certifies that every future period repeats a finite safe cycle. Termination follows from finiteness. For W=ab^omega, every nonempty prefix contains a, and a occurs only at position1, so any such prefix has at most one occurrence and cannot cover the infinite word. Hence each positive computed record (A,B,q) really certifies weak quasiperiodicity of the corresponding binary f. The bound12 concerns which q are attempted, not the correctness of positive certificates.

This is a full ordinary argument for the helper and individual witnesses. It is routine finite-state theory, not a source-conjecture solution, not a new fullscope result, and receives no credit.
