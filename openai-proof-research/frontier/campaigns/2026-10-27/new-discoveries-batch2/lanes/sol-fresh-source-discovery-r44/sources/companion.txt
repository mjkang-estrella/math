                                                  Abstract. Koutschan, Krattenthaler and Schlosser recently considered a fam-
                                                  ily of binomial determinants. In this work, we give combinatorial interpretations
                                                  of two subclasses of these determinants in terms of domino tilings and nonin-
                                                  tersecting lattice paths, thereby partially answering a question of theirs. Fur-
                                                  thermore, the determinant evaluations established by Koutschan, Krattenthaler
                                                  and Schlosser produce many product formulas for our weighted enumerations
                                                  of domino tilings and nonintersecting lattice paths. However, there are still two
                                                  enumerations left corresponding to conjectural formulas made by the three. We
                                                  hereby prove the two conjectures using the principle of holonomic Ansatz plus
                                                  the approach of modular reduction for creative telescoping, and hence fill the
                                                  gap.

          2. Domino tilings and nonintersecting lattice paths
   The first act features a duet performed by the two combinatorial objects of our
interest — domino tilings and nonintersecting lattice paths.
   To start with, we recall that a (generalized) partition of length n is a sequence
λ := (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λn ) of n weakly decreasing nonnegative integers λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ · · · ≥
λn ≥ 0. Throughout, all partitions are such generalized ones, with zero allowed
as a part.

2.1. Nonintersecting lattice paths. We begin with the simpler objects, non-
intersecting lattice paths. For (i1 , j1 ), (i2 , j2 ) ∈ Z2 , we first define a Delannoy path
from (i1 , j1 ) to (i2 , j2 ) as a lattice path using only east (→), north (↑) or northeast
(↗) steps. For example, in Fig. 2, we show all five Delannoy paths from (0, 0)
to (1, 2). Furthermore, we define an H-Delannoy path from (i1 , j1 ) to (i2 , j2 ) as a
Delannoy path that does not end with an east step. Therefore, in Fig. 2, only the
first path is not H-Delannoy because its last step is an east one.




                   Figure 2. Delannoy paths from (0, 0) to (1, 2)

  Now let us fix a partition λ = (λ1 , . . . , λn ) of length n.
  A system of nonintersecting Delannoy paths marked by λ is an n-tuple p =
(p1 , . . . , pn ) of Delannoy paths such that pj moves from (−j, j) to (λj − j, n) for
1 ≤ j ≤ n and that these Delannoy paths are pairwise nonintersecting in the
sense that every two different paths do not have any lattice point in common. We
denote by Lλ (n) the set of such nonintersecting Delannoy paths.
  Similarly, a system of nonintersecting H-Delannoy paths marked by λ is an n-
tuple p̂ = (p̂1 , . . . , p̂n ) of H-Delannoy paths such that p̂j moves from (−j, j) to
(λj − j, n + 1) for 1 ≤ j ≤ n and that these H-Delannoy paths are pairwise
nonintersecting. The set of nonintersecting H-Delannoy paths marked by λ is
written as Lbλ (n).
