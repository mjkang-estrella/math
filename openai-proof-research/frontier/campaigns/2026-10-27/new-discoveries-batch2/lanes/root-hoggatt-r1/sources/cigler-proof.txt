Proof of Cigler’s conjecture on q-Hoggatt numbers



Shane Chern

S. ChernFakultät für Mathematik, Universität Wien, Oskar-Morgenstern-Platz 1, Wien 1090, Austria

Email address: chenxiaohang92@gmail.com, xiaohangc92@univie.ac.at

W. ShiSchool of Mathematical Sciences, Dalian University of Technology, Dalian 116024, P.R. China

Abstract.
    

We prove the nonnegativity and palindromicity of a family of polynomials arising from q-Hoggatt numbers. The nonnegativity is derived from Stanley’s (P,\omega)-partition theory through a standard Young tableau formula, while the palindromicity is proved by an involution on rectangular standard Young tableaux. Our result confirms a conjecture of Cigler.
  




Key words and phrases: q-Hoggatt number, nonnegativity, palindromicity, standard Young tableau.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification05A19, 05A15, 05E05, 11B65.




1. Introduction




A polynomial p(x) is palindromic if the sequence of its coefficients reads the same forwards from the lowest power to the highest as it does backwards. In other words, p(x) satisfies the condition





\displaystyle p(x)=x^{N}p(x^{-1}),



where N is the sum of the lowest and highest exponents. A classic example of palindromic polynomials is the q-binomial coefficient





\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}},&\text{if $0\leq k\leq n$},\\[10.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}



where the q-Pochhammer symbol is defined by





\displaystyle(a;q)_{m}
\displaystyle:=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-aq^{j}).



As a polynomial in q, the q-binomial coefficient {\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q} has lowest exponent 0 and highest exponent k(n-k), while it is a standard result [4, p. 353, eq. (I.47)] that





\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q^{-1}}=q^{k(k-n)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q}.






In [2], Cigler introduced a q-analog of Hoggatt numbers originating from [3], and conjectured a palindromic property for a relevant series. Let





\displaystyle\langle n\rangle_{q}^{(d)}:={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+d-1}{d}}_{q},\qquad\qquad\langle n\rangle_{q}^{(d)}!:=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle j\rangle_{q}^{(d)}.



Cigler’s q-Hoggatt numbers are defined by




(1.1)

\displaystyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}:=\frac{\langle n\rangle_{q}^{(d)}!}{\langle k\rangle_{q}^{(d)}!\langle n-k\rangle_{q}^{(d)}!}=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+j}{k}}_{q}}{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+j}{k}}_{q}}.



For d=1, 2, and 3, the q-Hoggatt numbers {\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q}^{(d)} reduce to the q-binomial coefficients, the q-Narayana numbers, and the q-Baxter numbers, respectively.




Cigler [2, Conjecture 10] proposed the following conjecture on q-Hoggatt numbers.




Cigler’s Conjecture.



Let d and k be any positive integers. The series




(1.2)

\displaystyle C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q):=(x;q)_{kd+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}x^{n}



is a bivariate polynomial in x and q with nonnegative coefficients, with the highest exponent of x being (k-1)(d-1). Writing





\displaystyle C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q)=:\sum_{j=0}^{(k-1)(d-1)}c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q)x^{j},



it is further true that c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q) is palindromic in q for every k.





The main purpose of this note is to prove Cigler’s conjecture by means of Stanley’s (P,\omega)-partition theory [5, 6, 7].




Recall that an integer partition \lambda is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers known as parts; the size of this partition, denoted by |\lambda|, is the sum of all its parts. We may visually represent a partition \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{l}) by its Young diagram, which is depicted by boxes placed in rows such that there are \lambda_{i} boxes in the i-th row.




Let \lambda be a fixed partition. A standard Young tableau of shape \lambda is a filling T of the
boxes in the Young diagram of \lambda with 1,2,\ldots,|\lambda|, each used exactly once, such that the
entries are strictly increasing along rows and down columns. Let \operatorname{SYT}(\lambda) denote the set of such tableaux.




For T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda) and 1\leq i\leq|\lambda|, let





\displaystyle\rho_{i}=(r_{i},s_{i}):=T^{-1}(i)



denote the box occupied by i in T. Define the descent set





\displaystyle\operatorname{Des}(T):=\{i:1\leq i\leq|\lambda|-1,\ r_{i+1}>r_{i}\}.



We require the descent statistic





\displaystyle\operatorname{des}(T):=|\operatorname{Des}(T)|,



and the comajor index statistic





\displaystyle\operatorname{comaj}(T):=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Des}(T)}(|\lambda|-i).






Our main result is to express Cigler’s series C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q) as the generating function for certain standard Young tableaux of rectangular shapes, thereby indicating Cigler’s conjecture. To start with, we let





\displaystyle(k^{d}):=(\underbrace{k,k,\ldots,k}_{\text{$d$ parts}}),



the partition into d parts of size k. Its Young diagram has a rectangular shape with d rows and k columns.




Theorem 1.1.



For every positive integers d and k,




(1.3)

\displaystyle(x;q)_{kd+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}x^{n}=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d})}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)-k\binom{d}{2}}x^{\operatorname{des}(T)-(d-1)}.



Moreover, Cigler’s conjecture is true. In particular, the coefficients at the lowest and highest powers of x are respectively





\displaystyle c_{k,0}^{(d)}(q)
\displaystyle=1,\qquad\qquad c_{k,(k-1)(d-1)}^{(d)}(q)=q^{2\binom{k}{2}\binom{d}{2}}.







This note is organized as follows. In Section 2, we interpret Cigler’s series C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q) combinatorially by proving (1.3); we also determine the coefficients of the two endpoint powers of x. Next, in Section 3, we establish the palindromicity for all coefficients c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q); our argument is built upon a natural involution on rectangular standard Young tableaux. Finally, we close this paper with further discussions on Fibonacci–Hoggatt numbers and resolve another conjecture of Cigler in Section 4.





2. Main Theorem, Part I: Combinatorics




In this part, we prove (1.3). Our starting point is the following observation.




Lemma 2.1.



For every nonnegative integer n and every positive integers d and k,




(2.1)

\displaystyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d})}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)-k\binom{d}{2}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{(n+d-1)-\des(T)+kd}{kd}}_{q}.







Proof.



We begin with a generic partition \lambda and a generic nonnegative integer m. Note that






\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-\des(T)+|\lambda|}{|\lambda|}}_{q}





\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}q^{\operatorname{des}(T)\cdot|\lambda|-\operatorname{maj}(T)}q^{(m-\operatorname{des}(T))\cdot|\lambda|}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-\des(T)+|\lambda|}{|\lambda|}}_{q^{-1}},



where the major index of T is given by





\displaystyle\operatorname{maj}(T):=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Des}(T)}i.



Thus,






\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-\des(T)+|\lambda|}{|\lambda|}}_{q}





\displaystyle\qquad=q^{m|\lambda|}\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}q^{-\operatorname{maj}(T)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-\des(T)+|\lambda|}{|\lambda|}}_{q^{-1}}.



In light of [8, p. 364, Proposition 7.19.12], the sum on the right-hand side of the above can be expressed in terms of a Schur function:




(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(\lambda)}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m-\des(T)+|\lambda|}{|\lambda|}}_{q}=q^{m|\lambda|}s_{\lambda}(1,q^{-1},\ldots,q^{-m}).






According to the above discussions, we have





\displaystyle\operatorname{RHS}\eqref{eq:gf-middle}=q^{kd(n+d-1)-k\binom{d}{2}}s_{(k^{d})}(1,q^{-1},\ldots,q^{-(n+d-1)}).



Now we recall the principal evaluation of Schur functions [8, p. 374, Theorem 7.21.2] so that





\displaystyle s_{(k^{d})}(1,q^{-1},\ldots,q^{-(n+d-1)})
\displaystyle=q^{-k\binom{d}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-q^{-(n+d+j-i)}}{1-q^{-(k+d-i-j+1)}}





\displaystyle=q^{-k\binom{d}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-q^{-(n+i+j-1)}}{1-q^{-(i+j-1)}}.



Therefore,





\displaystyle\operatorname{RHS}\eqref{eq:gf-middle}=q^{kd(n+d-1)-2k\binom{d}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-q^{-(n+i+j-1)}}{1-q^{-(i+j-1)}}=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1-q^{n+i+j-1}}{1-q^{i+j-1}}.






Finally, we know from the definition (1.1) that





\displaystyle\operatorname{LHS}\eqref{eq:gf-middle}={\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+k+j}{k}}_{q}}{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+j}{k}}_{q}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1-q^{n+i+j-1}}{1-q^{i+j-1}},



thereby giving the claimed relation (2.1) by interchanging the indices i and j.
∎





In view of (2.1), we have





\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}x^{n}=\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d})}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)-k\binom{d}{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{(n+d-1)-\des(T)+kd}{kd}}_{q}x^{n}.



To simplify the inner sum on the right-hand side, we need to determine the range of \operatorname{des}(T).




Lemma 2.2.



For standard Young tableaux T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}), we have




(2.3)

\displaystyle d-1\leq\operatorname{des}(T)\leq k(d-1).



Moreover, both inequalities are attainable, each by a unique standard Young tableau.





Proof.



For the lower bound, we assume that in the standard Young tableau T, the entries in the first column are u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{d}. For each u_{i+1}-1 with 1\leq i\leq d-1, it is clear that its row index must be smaller than that of u_{i+1}, thereby implying that u_{i+1}-1 is a descent. Hence,





\displaystyle\operatorname{des}(T)\geq\big|\{u_{2}-1,u_{3}-1,\ldots,u_{d}-1\}\big|=d-1.



On the other hand, suppose the entries in the last column are u^{\prime}_{1}<u^{\prime}_{2}<\cdots<u^{\prime}_{d}. It is also true that all u^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,u^{\prime}_{d-1} are descents. To ensure \operatorname{des}(T)=d-1, we must have u^{\prime}_{i}=u_{i+1}-1 for 1\leq i\leq d-1. In this situation, the only possibility is the standard Young tableau T^{\dagger} of shape (k^{d}) in which the entry in the i-th row and j-th column is j+(i-1)k.




For the upper bound, we assume that the first row of the standard Young tableau T is filled with v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{k}. For each v_{j+1}-1 with 1\leq j\leq k-1, its row index is at least 1, which is never smaller than that of v_{j+1}. Thus,





\displaystyle\operatorname{des}(T)\leq\big|\{1,2,\ldots,kd-1\}\backslash\{v_{2}-1,v_{3}-1,\ldots,v_{k}-1\}\big|=k(d-1).



Suppose further that the entries in the last row are v^{\prime}_{1}<v^{\prime}_{2}<\cdots<v^{\prime}_{k}. It is clear that v^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{k-1} are non-descents. Thus, to ensure \operatorname{des}(T)=k(d-1), we must have v^{\prime}_{j}=v_{j+1}-1. Then the only possible standard Young tableau T^{\ddagger} is such that the (i,j)-th box is filled with i+(j-1)d.
∎





According to (2.3), for every T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}),





\displaystyle(d-1)-\operatorname{des}(T)\leq 0.



Thus,





\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{(n+d-1)-\des(T)+kd}{kd}}_{q}x^{n}=x^{\operatorname{des}(T)-(d-1)}\sum_{i\geq 0}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{i+kd}{kd}}_{q}x^{i}.



Now we can apply the following evaluation of q-binomial series [1, p. 36, eq. (3.3.7)]:





\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{i+j}{j}}_{q}x^{i}=\frac{1}{(x;q)_{j+1}},



from which we get





\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{(n+d-1)-\des(T)+kd}{kd}}_{q}x^{n}=\frac{x^{\operatorname{des}(T)-(d-1)}}{(x;q)_{kd+1}}.






Finally, we conclude that





\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}x^{n}=\frac{1}{(x;q)_{kd+1}}\sum_{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d})}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)-k\binom{d}{2}}x^{\operatorname{des}(T)-(d-1)},



thereby producing the desired relation (1.3). In addition, we have the coefficient of the lowest power of x:





\displaystyle c_{k,0}^{(d)}(q)=q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T^{\dagger})-k\binom{d}{2}}=1,



while the coefficient of the highest power of x is





\displaystyle c_{k,(k-1)(d-1)}^{(d)}(q)=q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T^{\ddagger})-k\binom{d}{2}}=q^{2\binom{k}{2}\binom{d}{2}};



here the standard Young tableaux T^{\dagger} and T^{\ddagger} are as in the proof of Lemma 2.2.





3. Main Theorem, Part II: Palindromicity




A direct indication of the relation (1.3) and the bounds (2.3) is that Cigler’s series C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q) is in \mathbb{N}[q,q^{-1}][x], while the highest exponent of x is (k-1)(d-1). Now we shall show that the coefficients c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q) of x^{j} in C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q) are indeed polynomials in \mathbb{N}[q] instead of Laurent polynomials. For this purpose, we have to bound \operatorname{comaj}(T) for standard Young tableaux T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}).




Lemma 3.1.



For standard Young tableaux T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}), we have




(3.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{comaj}(T)\geq k\binom{d}{2}.







Proof.



We rewrite \operatorname{comaj}(T) as





\displaystyle\operatorname{comaj}(T)=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Des}(T)}(kd-i)=\sum_{m=1}^{kd}\big|\{l\in\operatorname{Des}(T):l<m\}\big|.



Now we prove that, for each m, there are at least r_{m}-1 descents smaller than m, where m is placed in the (r_{m},s_{m})-th box. In the s_{m}-th cloumn, we assume that the first r_{m} entries are u_{1}<u_{2}<\cdots<u_{r_{m}}=:m. It is clear that in each interval \{u_{i},u_{i}+1,\ldots,u_{i+1}-1\} with 1\leq i\leq r_{m}-1, there exists at least one descent l_{i}. Moreover, l_{i}\leq u_{i+1}-1\leq u_{r_{m}}-1<m. Since there are r_{m}-1 such nonintersecting intervals, we have





\displaystyle\big|\{l\in\operatorname{Des}(T):l<m\}\big|\geq r_{m}-1.



Finally,





\displaystyle\operatorname{comaj}(T)\geq\sum_{m=1}^{kd}(r_{m}-1)=k\sum_{i=1}^{d}(i-1)=k\binom{d}{2},



as claimed.
∎





Now we have seen that





\displaystyle C_{k}^{(d)}(x,q)\in\mathbb{N}[q][x].



Moreover, the coefficients c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q) are given by





\displaystyle c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d})\\
\operatorname{des}(T)=j+d-1\end{subarray}}q^{\operatorname{comaj}(T)-k\binom{d}{2}}.



To show these coefficients are palindromic, we need the following observation.




Lemma 3.2.



Define a map \theta:\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d})\to\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}) by





\displaystyle\theta(T)(r,s):=kd+1-T(d+1-r,k+1-s).



Then \theta is an involution. In addition,




(3.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{des}(\theta(T))=\operatorname{des}(T),



and




(3.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{comaj}(\theta(T))=kd\operatorname{des}(T)-\operatorname{comaj}(T).







Proof.



It is a direct verification that \theta is an involution on \operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}). Now we check the two relations on statistics. First,





\displaystyle\operatorname{Des}(\theta(T))=\{kd-i:i\in\operatorname{Des}(T)\},



so that (3.2) holds. Next,





\displaystyle\operatorname{comaj}(\theta(T))=\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Des}(\theta(T))}(kd-i^{\prime})=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Des}(T)}i,



thereby leading us to (3.3).
∎





Note that the map \theta, if restricted to the subset of standard Young tableaux





\displaystyle\{T\in\operatorname{SYT}(k^{d}):\operatorname{des}(T)=j+d-1\},



is still an involution. Moreover,





\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{comaj}(T)-k\binom{d}{2}\right)+\left(\operatorname{comaj}(\theta(T))-k\binom{d}{2}\right)=kd(j+d-1)-2k\binom{d}{2}=kdj,



which is independent of T. These facts tell us that in c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q), the sum of the lowest and highest exponents of q is kdj. Also, by pairing T and \theta(T), we have





\displaystyle c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q)=q^{kdj}c_{k,j}^{(d)}(q^{-1}),



and hence conclude the palindromicity.





4. Closing remarks




In [2], Cigler also considered a family of Fibonacci–Hoggatt numbers. For nonzero numbers a and b, let (F_{a,b}(n))_{n\geq 0} be a sequence defined by the recurrence





\displaystyle F_{a,b}(n)=aF_{a,b}(n-1)+bF_{a,b}(n-2),



together with the initial values F_{a,b}(0)=0 and F_{a,b}(1)=1. When a=b=1, the sequence reduces to the conventional Fibonacci numbers.




In analogy to (1.1), define the Fibonacci–Hoggatt numbers by





\displaystyle{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}:=\frac{\langle n\rangle_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}!}{\langle k\rangle_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}!\langle n-k\rangle_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}!}=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\frac{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+j}{k}}_{F_{a,b}}}{{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{k+j}{k}}_{F_{a,b}}},



where





\displaystyle\langle n\rangle_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}:={\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+d-1}{d}}_{F_{a,b}},\qquad\qquad\langle n\rangle_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}!:=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle j\rangle_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)},



with





\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{F_{a,b}}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{F_{a,b}(n-j)}{F_{a,b}(k-j)},&\text{if $0\leq k\leq n$},\\[10.0pt]
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}






Note that the numbers F_{a,b}(n) can be expressed by Binet’s formula:





\displaystyle F_{a,b}(n)=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},



where





\displaystyle\alpha:=\frac{a+\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}{2},\qquad\qquad\beta:=\frac{a-\sqrt{a^{2}+4b}}{2}.



Putting





\displaystyle q:=\frac{\beta}{\alpha},



and noting that \alpha\beta=-b, we have





\displaystyle F_{a,b}(n)=\left(-\frac{b}{q}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q},



so that





\displaystyle{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{F_{a,b}}=\left(-\frac{q}{b}\right)^{\frac{k(k-n)}{2}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}}_{q}.






In [2], Cigler considered the following series




(4.1)

\displaystyle G_{k,F_{a,b}}^{(d)}(x):=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{kd+1}(-1)^{\binom{m+1}{2}}b^{\binom{m}{2}}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{kd+1}{m}}_{F_{a,b}}x^{m}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{F_{a,b}}^{(d)}x^{n}\right).



If we put





\displaystyle y:=\left(-\frac{b}{q}\right)^{\frac{kd}{2}}x,



it follows from a direct computation that





\displaystyle G_{k,F_{a,b}}^{(d)}(x)=(y;q)_{kd+1}\sum_{n\geq 0}{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+k}{k}}_{q}^{(d)}y^{n},



where we have applied the following evaluation [1, p. 36, eq. (3.3.6)] to the first sum in (4.1):





\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{j}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{j}{i}}_{q}(-1)^{i}z^{i}q^{\binom{i}{2}}=(z;q)_{j}.






Note that y and x only differ by a scalar. By virtue of Theorem 1.1, we may immediately confirm the following conjecture of Cigler [2, Conjecture 18], which also reduces to [2, Conjecture 14] at the a=b=1 specialization.




Theorem 4.1.



G_{k,F_{a,b}}^{(d)}(x) is a polynomial in x of degree (k-1)(d-1) with constant term being 1 and the coefficient of the highest power of x being b^{2\binom{k}{2}\binom{d}{2}}.
