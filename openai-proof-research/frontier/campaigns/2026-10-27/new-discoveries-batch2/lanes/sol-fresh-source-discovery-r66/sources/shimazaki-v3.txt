                                                              Macdonald principal specialization ratios
                                                                 and their generating polynomials
                                                                                    Tatsushi Shimazaki1



                                                                                            Abstract

                                                     We establish independence of q as a necessary condition for coefficientwise nonnegativity
                                                 of normalized principal specialization ratios of monic Macdonald polynomials. For arbitrary
                                                 pairs of partitions, we characterize independence of q. For denominator partitions with
                                                 largest and smallest parts differing by at least two, every ratio independent of q is a power
                                                 of t. We classify the nonnegative ratios under addition of maximal rows and columns of




arXiv:2609.21431v3 [math.CO] 19 Oct 2026
                                                 fixed height. For rows of length at least two, the classification is equivalent to rectangular
                                                 complementation. For arbitrary initial partitions, the generating polynomials of successive
                                                 row ratios at the smallest number of variables admissible for all stages have simple negative
                                                 real zeros with lower bounds for the ratios of consecutive absolute zeros. We derive a
                                                 reciprocity criterion, bounds for gamma coefficients, and the corresponding Jack limit.
                                                 For generalized staircases, we determine shortest horizontal and conjugate vertical strip
                                                 sequences and obtain terminating basic hypergeometric representations.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           Macdonald’s principal specialization formula expresses evaluations of monic Macdonald polyno-
                                           mials as products in q and t [12, 14]. Cancellations among the factors in their ratios do not
                                           decide coefficientwise nonnegativity. We prove independence of q for every normalized ratio with
                                           nonnegative coefficients, using the limit t → 1− for fixed q. We characterize q independence
                                           by counting differences between parts at fixed row distances. For denominator partitions with
                                           largest and smallest parts differing by at least two, every ratio independent of q is a power
                                           of t. For smaller differences, we reduce the remaining nonnegativity question to quotients of
                                           Gaussian polynomials [7].
                                              We classify the nonnegative ratios obtained by adjoining maximal rows or columns of
                                           fixed height. For rows of length at least two, the classification is equivalent to rectangular
                                           complementation. For columns, nonnegativity requires the minimum number of variables, except
                                           in the elementary symmetric case. We determine the unique shortest sequence of horizontal
                                           strips joining consecutive generalized staircases and its conjugate vertical sequence. At the
                                           minimum number of variables, the horizontal ratios yield a terminating basic hypergeometric
                                           polynomial.
                                              For an arbitrary initial partition, the generating polynomial of successive row ratios at the
                                           smallest number of variables admissible for all stages has negative real zeros [13]. We give a
                                           direct proof of their simplicity and establish lower bounds for the ratios of consecutive absolute
                                           zeros using Schur–Szegő composition and finite multiplier sequences. We characterize reciprocity
                                           of the centered polynomial through rectangular complementation. For generalized staircases,
                                           reciprocal pairs of zeros yield bounds on the coefficients of its symmetric expansion. The Jack
                                           limit leads to Jacobi polynomials.
                                              The organization of this paper is as follows. Section 2 gives preliminaries. Section 3 establishes
                                           the specialization ratios, and Section 4 treats their generating polynomials.
