(section "2. Block identities"):

    "The above results of course involve the classification of partitions which are cores
    for two primes simultaneously, due to the Nakayama conjecture. Actually for the
    classification p and q need not be prime numbers, only relatively prime positive
    integers. There is a unique partition κpq of the maximum number (p^2 − 1)(q^2 − 1)/24,
    which is a p-core and a q-core. It is still an open question whether the Young
    diagram of any other partition which is a p-core and a q-core is contained in that
    of κpq . See [5] for details."

    "[5] J.B. Olsson, D. Stanton, Block inclusions and cores of partitions, Preprint 2005. To appear."

Note (same page, spin/bar-core analogue — contrast): "In this case it can be shown that there is
a maximal partition κ̂pq , whose Young diagram contains those of all the others." — for partitions
into distinct parts that are bar cores, containment IS asserted as shown.

Quantifier translation: p, q relatively prime positive integers; "any other partition which is a
p-core and a q-core" = every simultaneous (p,q)-core (the question as stated includes λ = κpq
trivially; MathDB 393907 phrases it as every λ). "Contained in that of κpq" = Young diagram
containment, i.e. rowwise λ_i ≤ (κpq)_i. MathDB record matches this literally. This is a
QUESTION in 2006; [5] is the Olsson-Stanton paper (published: Aequationes Math. 74 (2007) 90-110).
