## Sustained routes and result

First route: enumerate connected finite structures with a marked boundary,
relaxing the hub constraint. For a rooted H compare the minimum cost when its
root is identified with a hub, and when it is attached to a hub by one extra
bridge. Positive excess of3*cost over edge count in the latter model exposes a
repeatable local block. All connected labeled graphs of orders2..6 were covered
in four shards. This produced the diamond with a two-edge pendant path as a
rooted H; adding the bridge gives eight edges and minimum local cost3.
The naive root-identification variant was not substituted for this actual
bridge construction. Exact counts and positive witnesses are retained.

Second route: variational local exchanges in complete graphs, checking all
locally irregular subgraphs of K3..K6. Adding one edge is too restrictive
(the empty graph is stuck). At K6 even allowing up to two added edges can
leave a local optimum with8 retained edges, whereas the true maximum is11.
Radius3 with two-add/one-remove passes this tiny case. These finite facts do
not prove a general exchange lemma. This route was superseded by the explicit
infinite-family obstruction, not claimed as a source theorem.

The final ordinary proof is in PROOF.md. For each t>=2, use t private diamonds
{ab,ac,ad,bc,bd}, attach each at c to a common h by c-x-y-h. Every valid deletion
set removes at least two diamond edges (three symmetry cases, both possible
boundary degrees) and at least one path edge (otherwise x-y joins two degree2
vertices). These disjoint charges give3t. Deleting ab,ad,xy in each block
attains3t; retained degree pairs are(1,3),(2,3),(2,1),(3,1),(1,t).
Thus I_e=3t, m=8t, and I_e-m/3=t/3 is unbounded. This negates the actual
source's existential absolute constant, for connected graphs in its domain.
