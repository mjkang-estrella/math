# Continuous C*-Smale counterexample — proposed, uncounted

Target: Krishna arXiv:2206.08154v1 Conjecture2.1 (weak norm constant1), and hence its smaller constant and order-strong forms. Exact publisher body and MathDB id not yet retrieved. Primary pp4-5 text read by parent. No novelty or formal claim yet.

Let X=[-1,1], A=C(X,C), y(t)=t, q(t)=sqrt(t²+1/16)>0. Set P(w)=w³−3q²w, literally the fully split product w(w−sqrt(3)q)(w+sqrt(3)q). Its source ordered-deletion derivative is Pprime(w)=3(w²−q²), fully split 3(w−q)(w+q).

At z=y, Pprime(z)=−3/16·1, hence ||Pprime(z)||=3/16 and it is pointwise invertible, stronger than merely nonzero. Both critical elements bplus=q and bminus=−q exist. Any continuous critical element satisfies b(t)=±q(t) pointwise; q is nowhere zero, and connectedness forces globally one branch. This is a continuous-selection argument, not a global integral-domain inference in C(X).

At t=−1, P(z)=35/16 and P(q)=−2q³, with q=sqrt(17)/4>1. Therefore ||P(z)−P(q)||>35/16+2>4. At t=+1 the opposite branch gives the same lower bound. On all X, ||y||≤1 and ||q||≤5/4, so ||z−b||≤9/4 for both critical elements. The source norm bound would imply ||P(z)−P(b)||≤||Pprime(z)||·||z−b||≤27/64, contradiction. Both denominators are strictly positive. Every critical element fails.

This refutes the norm constant1 already at degree3 in a connected commutative unital C*-algebra with polynomial and derivative both fully split. Scalar Smale is untouched: the pointwise best branch switches.
