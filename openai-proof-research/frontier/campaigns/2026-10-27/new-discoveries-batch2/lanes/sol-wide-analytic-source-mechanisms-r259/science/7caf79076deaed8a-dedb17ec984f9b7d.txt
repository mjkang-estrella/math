    The logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture was proved in dimension n = 2 by Böröczky, Lut-
wak, Yang, and Zhang [1]. In dimension n = 3, the inequality was proved for two special classes
of convex bodies [22]. In higher dimensions, it is known under additional symmetry assumptions,
including for unconditional convex bodies [20], complex convex bodies [18], and convex bodies in-
variant under n independent linear reflections [2]. Local results are known near the Euclidean ball
[5] and when the reference body is a zonoid [21]. Recent preprints treat the case of n − 2 reflection
symmetries [14] and the local problem for bodies of revolution [10].
    Combining Saroglou’s result [19] with the second-variation computation of Cordero-Erausquin

    Second variation arguments lead to quadratic-form formulations of local Lp -Brunn–Minkowski
inequalities and, equivalently, to spectral formulations in the smooth setting. Colesanti [4] derived
Poincaré-type inequalities on the boundary of a convex body from the Brunn–Minkowski inequality.
Colesanti, Livshyts, and Marsiglietti [5] formulated the local log-Brunn–Minkowski inequality as a
Poincaré-type inequality for the cone-volume measure. Kolesnikov and Milman [11] related local Lp -
Brunn–Minkowski inequalities to spectral-gap estimates for the Hilbert–Brunn–Minkowski operator.
Further developments of the local theory can be found in [3, 16, 15, 13]. Related Brunn–Minkowski
inequalities for Gaussian measure were established in [12, 9]. In the present setting, the corresponding
Poincaré-type inequality (1) is formulated for the Gaussian measure restricted to the convex body,
whereas the local inequalities above are formulated in terms of measures on the sphere.

We will show that in this case (1) holds for every origin-symmetric convex set K. This follows from
the symmetry-improved Gaussian Poincaré inequality of Cordero-Erausquin, Fradelizi, and Maurey
[6], after a linear change of variables reducing the covariance matrix Σ to the identity. More precisely,

Theorem 1. Let Σ ∈ S≻0 and A ∈ D. Then the following are equivalent:
    (i) A and Σ commute.

 (ii) Inequality (1) holds for every origin-symmetric orthogonal box K ⊆ Rn .
    The proof of Theorem 1 gives a simple way to construct counterexamples to (1) in the non-
commuting case. Namely, given A and Σ which do not commute, one can always find an origin-
symmetric orthogonal box K for which (1) fails. The boxes obtained by this construction are not
coordinate boxes; a counterexample with a coordinate box would give a negative answer to the log-
Brunn–Minkowski conjecture. Nevertheless, the construction yields orthogonal boxes arbitrarily close
to coordinate boxes. Cordero-Erausquin and Rotem [7, Theorem 6] used a thin-box argument and
a second-variation computation to show that the strong (B)-property fails for Gaussian measures
with covariance matrices arbitrarily close to the identity. In the present setting, this idea leads from
(1) to a directional inequality, and requiring this inequality in every direction is equivalent to the
commutativity of A and Σ.

    Theorem 1 shows that commutativity is the natural condition guaranteeing (1) over all origin-
symmetric orthogonal boxes. In the non-commuting case, the geometry of K becomes important.
Although the inequality may fail for some orthogonal boxes, additional symmetries of the body with
respect to an eigenbasis of Σ can still guarantee its validity. The next theorem shows that uncondi-
tionality is sufficient when Σ is diagonal.
    Our result in the non-commuting setting is the following.
Theorem 2. Inequality (1) holds for any unconditional convex body K, any Σ ∈ D≻0 , and any
A ∈ S.
    As a consequence of Theorem 2, (1) holds for K = B2n and arbitrary Σ ∈ S≻0 , since the Euclidean
ball is unconditional with respect to every orthonormal basis.
