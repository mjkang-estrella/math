                                                  A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO NIRENBERG’S CONJECTURE
                                                         ON CLOSED ASYMPTOTIC CURVES

                                              HARRISON BANKS, MOHAMMAD GHOMI, HONGDA QIU, AND MATTEO RAFFAELLI




arXiv:2610.13848v2 [math.DG] 21 Oct 2026
                                                   Abstract. We construct a smooth embedded annulus in Euclidean 3-space with
                                                   negative Gauss curvature in its interior whose boundary consists of strictly convex
                                                   curves in planes tangent to the annulus, and which contains closed asymptotic curves.
                                                   This gives a counterexample to a 1962 conjecture of Nirenberg. The construction is
                                                   based on Lelieuvre’s formula, which yields a closed curve that is then realized as an
                                                   asymptotic curve on a ruled annulus. The second asymptotic curve is obtained from
                                                   a periodic solution of a Riccati equation.




                                                                                  1. Introduction
                                             In 1938, Alexandrov [1] showed that real analytic tight surfaces in Euclidean space
                                           R3 are isometrically rigid. In his celebrated program [7–9, 16] to extend Alexandrov’s
                                           theorem to smooth surfaces, Nirenberg [13] conjectured that a negatively curved annulus
                                           bounded by convex curves in tangent planes cannot contain a closed asymptotic curve.
                                           Here we construct a counterexample.
                                           Theorem 1.1. There exists a smooth embedded annulus M ⊂ R3 whose interior has
                                           negative Gauss curvature and contains two distinct closed asymptotic curves. The bound-
                                           ary components of M are strictly convex curves in parallel planes, each tangent to M
                                           along the corresponding boundary component.
                                              The construction uses Lelieuvre’s classical formula [11] to produce a closed curve and
                                           then realizes it as an asymptotic curve on a ruled annulus. Modifying the height near
                                           its ends produces the required boundary tangency while preserving negative curvature.
                                           The asymptotic curves transverse to the rulings satisfy a Riccati equation. Using the
                                           first curve as a known solution, we reduce this equation to a linear equation and obtain a
                                           second closed curve. The construction is explicit, and the required estimates are proved
                                           analytically. The accompanying Mathematica notebook [3] checks the computations and
                                           generates the figures below.
                                              The significance of asymptotic curves in Nirenberg’s program stems from their role as
                                           characteristics of the hyperbolic PDE governing isometric immersions in the negatively
                                           curved region. Nirenberg established rigidity under additional assumptions, including

                          2. Construction of the Annulus
2.1. Lelieuvre’s formula. By smooth we mean C ∞ . A smooth curve Γ : (a, b) → R3
is regular if its derivative Γ′ ̸= 0. A regular curve Γ on a smooth surface S ⊂ R3 is
asymptotic if its normal curvature II(Γ′ , Γ′ ) = 0, or equivalently
                                       ⟨Γ′′ , N ⟩ = 0,
where N is a unit normal vector field of S. A parametrization X(u, v) of S is asymptotic
if ⟨Xuu , N ⟩ = ⟨Xvv , N ⟩ = 0. Lelieuvre’s formula [4, Sec. 1.3; 11, p. 126; 14] gives
a convenient description for such parametrizations when the Gauss curvature K < 0:
after interchanging u and v if necessary, the normal field µ := (−K)−1/4 N satisfies
Xu = µ × µu , Xv = µv × µ. In particular, if v = v0 is fixed and Γ(u) := X(u, v0 ),
µ(u) := µ(u, v0 ), we may write
(1)                                     Γ′ = µ × µ′ .
We will use this formula in the reverse direction: start with a smooth closed curve
µ : R/(2πZ) → R3 satisfying µ × µ′ ̸= 0, define Γ by (1), and construct a smooth
surface through Γ which is orthogonal to µ. On such a surface, Γ is asymptotic, since
⟨Γ′′ , µ⟩ = ⟨µ × µ′′ , µ⟩ = 0. More specifically, we choose a transverse vector field ξ along
Γ orthogonal to µ. Then
(2)                                     Γ(t) + rξ(t)
is a ruled surface near r = 0. Along Γ its tangent vectors are Γ′ and ξ, both perpendicular
to µ. Hence µ is normal to this surface along Γ, and Γ is asymptotic on it. We need to
choose µ and ξ so that Γ closes.
