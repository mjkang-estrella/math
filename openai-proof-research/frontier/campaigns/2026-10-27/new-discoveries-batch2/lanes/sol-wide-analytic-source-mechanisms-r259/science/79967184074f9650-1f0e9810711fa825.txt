                                                  An isoperimetric problem for Fourier zeros of centrally
                                                                symmetric convex bodies

arXiv:2609.10517v1 [math.MG] 9 Sep 2026
                                                                                                 Abstract
                                                      Given a centrally symmetric convex body, consider the zero set of the Fourier transform of its
                                                  characteristic function. We study how large the distance from this set to the origin can be when
                                                  the volume of the body is fixed. A 2009 conjecture of Benguria, Levitin, and Parnovski asserts
                                                  that the maximum is attained by a Euclidean ball. We disprove it in all dimensions greater than
                                                  one. In the plane, every regular centrally symmetric polygon with at least twelve sides outperforms
                                                  the disk, albeit by a tiny margin, and the regular dodecagon is best among them. In dimensions
                                                  three and higher, no maximiser exists.

                                 §1.5. A note on computer-assisted proofs
In this paper, several results, including Theorems 1.5 and 4.6, have some computer-assisted component.
In recent years, these techniques have been more widely successful in mathematics. In the context of
spectral problems, similar computational techniques were very recently applied in [FLPS23, FLPS26,
GHLZ26, DGSPA26] where rigorous asymptotic analysis of spectral properties was performed. We
refer to the survey [GS19] for a more specific treatment of computer-assisted proofs in PDEs.
     The main idea is to substitute floating point computations by rigorous upper and lower bounds,
which are then propagated through every computer instruction and augmented if necessary by taking
into account any error made throughout the process. In our concrete case, we will use computer-assisted
verification specifically in the proof of Theorems 4.4 and 4.5, and Theorems A.1 and A.2. All of these
verified intervals refer to a finite number of evaluations of expressions involving special functions. In all
cases, the implementation was either direct or via a branch and bound method. The implementation is
described in §4.5.

In §2 we give a proof of (1.4), in particular constructing counterexamples to [BLP09, Conjecture 2.2]
in dimension 𝑑 ≥ 3. In §3 we prove the crucial property that the zeros of 𝜒d       𝑃2𝑛 nearest to the origin

then use this property to prove another of our main results: in §4, we prove a statement about the

in dimension 𝑑 = 2, see Theorem 1.5. Appendices A and B contain the proof of (3.18), the certified
constants and elementary bounds used in §4, and a toolbox of Bessel function estimates.

All computer-assisted steps of this section, the zero enclosures of Theorems 4.4 and 4.5, and the
constants of Theorems A.1 and A.2, are verified by interval arithmetic using the C++ version of
Arb/Flint [Jo17], see [Tu11] for background on validated numerics). Every quantity is enclosed in
an interval mathematically guaranteed to contain it, every claimed inequality is checked between the
corresponding interval endpoints, and the routine terminates with an error if any single check fails;
ordinary floating point is used only to select candidate brackets, never in a verification. The only
transcendental evaluations required are 𝐽0 , . . . , 𝐽3 on [0, 4.2], enclosed by the truncated series (B.1)
with a rigorous tail bound, and the elementary functions sin, cos, sec and cot, likewise enclosed by
their Maclaurin series with a rigorous tail bound; the profile 𝑞𝑛 is evaluated through the cancellation-
free formula (4.2), in the rescaled form (4.3). The routine, its documentation, and a floating-point
cross-check of all constants are provided in the accompanying repository at

                                https://github.com/javigomez2001/BLP.
