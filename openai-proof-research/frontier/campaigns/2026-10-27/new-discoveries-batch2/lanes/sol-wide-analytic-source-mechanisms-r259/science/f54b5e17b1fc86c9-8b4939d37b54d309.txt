                                                  EXPONENTIAL PROFILES FOR THE INTRINSIC VOLUMES OF ORLICZ BALLS

                                                                                                  MYKYTA SADOK


                                                    A BSTRACT. An Orlicz ball is the set of points where the sum of a fixed even convex function of the coordinates
                                                    does not exceed a given radius, here proportional to the dimension. For a broad class of such functions, includ-
                                                    ing non-homogeneous ones, we find the exponential rate of the intrinsic volumes whose order is proportional to
                                                    the dimension and express it by a two-parameter variational formula involving only one-dimensional integrals.




arXiv:2610.16904v1 [math.MG] 12 Oct 2026
                                                    As a function of the order-to-dimension ratio, this rate is concave and continuous on the closed unit interval,
                                                    and the convergence is uniform in the order. This proves the exponential-profile conjecture of Kabluchko,
                                                    Marynych, and Prochno for these bodies and recovers their result for ℓ p balls with p > 1. The proof relies
                                                    on the theory of large deviations. An exact formula for the Laplace transform of the intrinsic volumes in the
                                                    radius, extending theirs for ℓ p balls, plays the role of a moment generating function, and a Laplace method
                                                    gives its exponential rate. An exponential change of measure, analogous to that used in Cramér’s theorem, then
                                                    recovers the intrinsic volumes. The key step is a lower bound on the tilted density at its mean, obtained from
                                                    log-concavity rather than a law of large numbers.

Remark 2.4 (On the role of (M2)). Assumption (M2) enters in two ways. First, through the curvature
formulas of Section 3. The C2 regularity of M off the origin makes ∂ Kn (c) a C2 hypersurface off the
coordinate hyperplanes, which is the hypothesis of Lemma 3.3, and it makes the curvature integrand of
Section 3.3 continuous, hence measurable, on the complement of the coordinate hyperplanes. Second, it
gives the local absolute continuity of M ′ established at the beginning of the proof of Lemma 4.1. All
remaining arguments, in particular the one-dimensional estimates of Section 4 and the inversion of Section 5,
use only that M ′ is odd, nondecreasing, and locally absolutely continuous with M ′′ ≥ 0 almost everywhere.
We do not pursue the resulting relaxation of (M2) here.

                                          7. C ONCLUDING REMARKS

   Generalized Orlicz balls. For coordinate-dependent functions M1 , . . . , Mn as in [7], the curvature, coarea,
and Gamma steps of Section 3 go through, and the radius Laplace transform of V j is again an integral in θ
of a sum over coordinate subsets of products of one-dimensional integrals. The sum no longer collapses to
a binomial coefficient, and identifying its exponential rate is open.
   Curvature measures. Remark 4.6 identifies the mixture α µZ,θ ,λ + (1 − α)µB,θ ,λ at the optimal param-
eters as the natural candidate for the limit law of one coordinate under the normalized curvature measure
C jn (Kn (nρ), ·)/V jn (Kn (nρ)), as in [8, Theorem 3.1] for ℓ p balls, and Proposition 3.1 is the natural starting
point for such a Maxwell–Poincaré–Borel law.
   Regularity and symmetry of M. Remark 2.4 isolates where (M2) is used, and Section 2.2 explains how
evenness can be replaced by the requirement that 0 minimizes M. Extending the curvature-measure step to
functions with locally absolutely continuous derivative would remove the C2 requirement.
   Rates of convergence. Proposition 5.3 compares n−1 logV jn (Kn (nρ)) with the minimum of the finite-n
transform objective up to O(log n/n). A rate of convergence to the limiting profile would in addition require
a quantitative version of Proposition 4.3, that is, a Laplace-method expansion of the θ -integral in (3.1) with
errors uniform in λ on compact sets, which we have not attempted.
