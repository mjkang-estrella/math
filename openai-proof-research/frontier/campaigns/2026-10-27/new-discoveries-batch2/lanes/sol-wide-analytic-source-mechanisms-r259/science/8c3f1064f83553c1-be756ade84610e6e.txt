                                                   A polynomial bound in Dvoretzky’s theorem
                                                                       Boaz Klartag and Shahar Moshe




arXiv:2610.03204v1 [math.FA] 2 Oct 2026
                                                                                          Abstract
                                                    We present a simple proof of the ε-Dvoretzky conjecture, which asserts that the depen-
                                                dence on the approximation parameter ε in Dvoretzky’s theorem is polynomial in 1/ε. In
                                                particular, if n ≥ (C/ε)ℓ/2+1 , then any n-dimensional convex body has, through any given
                                                interior point, an ℓ-dimensional section that is ε-close to a Euclidean ball. Here, C > 0 is a
                                                universal constant. We in fact obtain a sharper dependence on ε. The proof is probabilistic,
                                                but uses a different probabilistic model from those employed previously. We also prove a
                                                simultaneous version of our theorem for a finite family of convex bodies with the origin in
                                                their interior, yielding a common linear subspace on which all of the bodies have nearly-
                                                Euclidean sections. Finally, we compare the radius of the approximating Euclidean ball with
                                                familiar geometric parameters, such as the mean widths of the body and its dual.

    Dvoretzky’s theorem was originally conjectured in the 1950s by Grothendieck [14] and by
others (see [7]), and it was proven around 1960 in [8, 9]. In addition to Milman’s proof, there are
alternative arguments in Szankowski [30] and in Figiel [10]. All of these proofs are probabilistic
in nature and yield an exponential (or worse) dependence on 1/ε already when ℓ = 3, similarly
to the estimate (2). Polynomial dependence on 1/ε was known to hold in the particular case
where K is assumed to have the symmetries of the cube, see Bourgain and Lindenstrauss [2],
Tikhomirov [31] and Fresen [11].
   The case ℓ = 2 was different. In this case, there is a topological proof yielding polynomial
dependence on 1/ε, which is attributed to Gromov by Milman [21]. Burago, Ivanov and Tabach-
nikov [6] analyzed the shortcomings of certain topological approaches to Dvoretzky’s theorem

   Traditionally, the probabilistic approaches to Dvoretzky’s theorem followed a strategy that
can be summarized as follows:

  Apply an appropriate invertible affine map to K (i.e., put K in the right “position”). Then,
 let E be a random ℓ-dimensional subspace, distributed uniformly in the Grassmannian Gn,ℓ .

In our context, this approach is already problematic in the case of the cube K = [−1, 1]n , where
it yields a suboptimal, exponential dependence on 1/ε. The case of the cube is analyzed in
Gordon, Litvak, Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann [13], where an interesting approach is proposed
for remedying the situation. They showed that in the case of the cube, polynomial dependence
on 1/ε may be obtained if the subspace E takes the form E = T (Rℓ ) where

   For a general convex body K ⊆ Rn containing the origin in its interior, our probabilistic
approach for the proof of Theorem 1.1 may be summarized as follows:

   Consider the convex body B consisting of all linear maps T : Rℓ → Rn with T (B ℓ ) ⊆ K.
           Let T be a random, uniformly distributed element of B. Set E = T (Rℓ ).

This probabilistic model yields a somewhat more “invariant” way to sample a random subspace
in a finite-dimensional normed space; it circumvents the need to choose a position for K. The
drawback of this probability distribution is that it is very much tailored to K, rather than being a
Haar measure on a homogeneous space as in the traditional approach to Dvoretzky’s theorem.
