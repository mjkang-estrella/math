# Outcome and unresolved pivot

No eligible discovery. The requested biquadratic problem is retired by Man 2024 Example 4.7; see PRIOR-RESOLUTION.md. It would be incorrect to search for a novel middle-radicand witness now. The graph existence endpoint in PROOF.md is complete but routine and receives no nomination.

The surviving research pivot is Mareike Dressler, *Real zeros of SONC polynomials*, arXiv:1909.06707v2, §5, pp.21–25: given a finite Γ⊂R^n, determine dim C_(n,2d)(Γ), and identify when it equals dim P_(n,2d)(Γ). This is an open-ended dimension problem, not a universal equality conjecture. The paper then specializes to unit-coordinate zeros, with 2^n or 2^(n−1) points. Its univariate answer and special upper bounds are already proved and are not targets. Current specialist literature for this pivot has not been cleared; it is a research question, not an eligible open-status certification.

Route 1: circuit span. Since each nonnegative circuit is nonnegative at every Γ-point, a sum vanishing on Γ must consist of circuits vanishing there individually. For a nonzero positive point v, equality in weighted AM–GM fixes each surviving circuit up to positive scalar: its coefficients are proportional to λ_j/v^(α_j), with inner coefficient −1/v^β. At other torus points, signs and absolute-value ratios filter admissible circuits. This gives a finite rank computation at fixed n,d for torus Γ. Source Proposition 5.1 already explicitly identifies the rank-of-agiform-matrix mechanism for its cube cases; presenting that rank itself as a new dimension formula would be routine and circular.

Route 2: first-order vanishing. At a positive zero, every nonnegative polynomial has zero value and gradient, so the SONC span lies in the kernel of the value-and-gradient evaluation map. Its rank gives a bound only; it does not prove attainability by circuits or nonnegative polynomials. At Γ={±1}^n, all surviving circuit exponents are even. Writing y_i=x_i² reduces the span to affine dependencies among A={a∈N^n:|a|≤d}. For d≥1, the extreme simplex vertices are 0,de_1,…,de_n. For each other a, its barycentric circuit in its minimal containing face supplies an independent affine dependence with coefficient −1 at a. Thus under the usual SONC convention allowing lower-dimensional circuits, these circuits span the full affine-dependency kernel of dimension |A|−n−1. This is a routine simplex linear-algebra control, not a resolution for arbitrary Γ. For d=0 the face is zero. If a convention restricts generators to full-dimensional proper circuits, the face-boundary step must be checked before using this calculation.

Exact missing work: for arbitrary Γ, including coordinate-zero points and multiple distinct absolute-value patterns, determine the span of all circuit families meeting every zero constraint without replacing it by the ambient jet kernel. Even for nonnegative P, the jet kernel need not be attained as the span of its face without additional independence/positivity hypotheses. A complete equality classification needs both span-attainability statements, not merely matrix dimensions. No such lemma is proved here, and no PROOF.md for this full dimension question exists.
