## Sustained exact square route and missing universal lemma

Source Theorem2.6 proves that any square within distance n of n!, n≥4, must be ABOVE it and have the form

    n!=a²(b²−1), 1≤a²≤n, m=ab, gcd(a,b)=1, b odd,
    gcd(b,n!)=1, and b≥n+1.

Its proof uses valuation dichotomy to reduce displacements to squares or primes in(n/2,n]. Below squares are excluded by a prime3-mod4 obstruction; above prime displacements p≡1mod8 admit q<p with Legendre(p/q)=−1 by quadratic reciprocity. The remaining square displacement gives m=ab and the displayed equation. These are public reductions, not new results.

For the remaining equation put u=(b−1)/2 and v=(b+1)/2. Then v−u=1, gcd(u,v)=1 and uv=N:=n!/(4a²). Conversely any positive pair u,v with uv=N,v−u=1 gives b=2u+1 and n!=a²(b²−1); with a²≤n it contradicts the square subcase. If 4a² does not divide n!, no odd-b solution exists. For integral N write its unique prime-power factorization N=product(q_i). Coprimality forces every entire q_i into exactly one of u,v; conversely every such partition is coprime. Thus existence is EXACTLY equivalent to a subset J satisfying

    product(i notin J,q_i) − product(i in J,q_i)=1.

This is an exhaustive finite algorithm for EACH n,a, but not a uniform exclusion. Source inequalities about prime support do not bound the separation of these two partition products. The missing square lemma is: for EVERY n≥8 and EVERY a≥1 with a²≤n and 4a²|n!, no such subset exists. Its a=1 case already contains Brocard. No proof of this lemma is supplied.

There is an exact universal limitation of fixed-modulus exclusion, beyond numerical tests: given ANY finite list of positive moduli M_i, choose n≥max M_i. Every M_i divides n!, so n!+1≡1² modulo every M_i simultaneously. Hence testing quadratic residuosity modulo any fixed finite list cannot rule out the a=1 equation for all sufficiently large n. This does not rule out growing moduli, estimates or global factorization. It is a method obstruction, not a source counterexample.

Even proving the square lemma would not finish FSC. Source Corollary7.1 additionally leaves m^ℓ=n!+εp for all odd primes ℓ≥5, ε=±1, and primes n/2<p≤n, with P+(m)>n+2. Fifth powers reduce unconditionally to the uncomputed finite gap1100<n<N5; GRH eliminates that family conditionally but does not settle the square case or ℓ≥7. No universal detecting-prime theorem for these remaining exponents was proved. The full missing bridge has both components.
