# Eight additional family screens

These are eight distinct families beyond r230's eleven, not eight certified open problems. None is a discovery or novelty-cleared survivor. Actual question bodies were read in frozen primary authored problem collections, with primary PDF/text bindings regenerated locally. Old partial-result references below are attributed as the question author describes them; unless explicitly stated otherwise their original full proofs were NOT retrieved, and they cannot clear novelty or support acceptance. This limitation is a reason to defer, not a theorem-announcement substitute.

P = Anton Petrunin, *A gallery of open problems in geometry that keep me puzzled and amused*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11908v2, dated 7 September 2026, first version 13 October 2025. T = Yana Teplitskaya, *Open problems on Steiner trees and maximal distance minimizers*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18217v1, 22 November 2025. G = Szymon Głąb and Franciszek Prus-Wiśniowski, *Achievement sets – current results and open problems*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17285v1, 19 December 2025 (body dated 22 December). The recent collection dates do not make all their older questions recent; specific antecedents span 1995, 2003, 2005, 2020, and 2025. No familiar hard problem is ranked just for having an open label.

1. **Hilbert quotient versus biquotient.** P§3 pp3–4 asks whether every compact simply connected Riemannian manifold that is an isometric quotient of Hilbert space by isometries (or the target of a Riemannian submersion) is a compact Lie-group biquotient with bi-invariant metric. Actual nearby proof read: P sketches the EASY converse by rescaled products G^n and their Hilbert ultralimit, attributed to Lytchak; it also cites Terng–Thorbergsson 1995 §4 and Lebedeva–Petrunin–Zolotov 2019. Mechanism: recover a finite-dimensional Lie presentation from horizontal distributions. Decisive obstruction: the ultralimit construction only maps biquotients into the proposed larger class; it does not reconstruct group structure in reverse. **Reject for depth now:** no rigidity invariant, original 1995/2019 full proofs unread. This differs from r230's finite isotropy bound.

2. **Complete nested convex realization.** P§4 p4 asks which metrics on S^n are realizable as complete smooth convex hypersurfaces nested in complete smooth convex hypersurfaces in Euclidean space; it specifically raises whether nonnegative cosectional curvature suffices. The source explains necessity by a curvature-tensor calculation, and cites Petrunin 2003 for a local strictly positive version without completeness. Mechanism: globalize local convex realizations by controlled extension. Decisive check: an extension must preserve completeness at every nesting stage, not just embed each compact patch. **Defer:** exact 2003 proof unread, no global extension estimate. The Berger-sphere diagnostic suggested by the author is a tractable test but only a parameter instance, not the full classification. Distinct from nonsmooth two-convex planar sections.

3. **Length of an involution-fixed geodesic.** P§7 p6: if a closed geodesic γ is the fixed-point set of an isometric involution on (S³,g), sec(g)≥1, must length(γ)≤2π? Includes the analogous Alexandrov spherical-polyhedral question. The author gives a genuine nearby argument for S¹-actions: the quotient is a convex positively curved disc and the fixed geodesic becomes its boundary. Mechanism: replace the orbit-disc argument by a comparison invariant for the Z/2 quotient. Decisive check: involution quotients need not be discs, so boundary-length comparison cannot simply be imported. **Defer:** Fujioka 2025's nonsharp bound is cited but its proof unread; no involution replacement argument. No numerical length experiment is a universal proof.

4. **Faceting the Fubini–Study metric.** P§8 pp6–7: does CP² admit nonnegatively curved Euclidean-simplex polyhedral metrics converging to its Fubini–Study metric? The full question requires convergence to that metric and nonnegative Alexandrov curvature throughout. Mechanism: construct compatible polyhedral Kähler triangulations with mesh tending to zero and bound cone angles. The actual authored source explains Kähler rigidity and algebraic singular sets, citing Panov 2009 and de Borbon–Panov 2026. Decisive check: general PL approximation does not control codimension-two angle sums≤2π. **Defer:** cited proof bodies unread; no angle-controlled approximation, and no use of their recent theorem titles as evidence of a new solution.

5. **Zero-curvature geodesic avoidance.** P§15 pp9–10: on a genus-two surface with nonpositive curvature, does a random geodesic meet the negative-curvature region with probability one? Attribution is to Burago and a discussion with Burns, Brin and Pesin; a 2003 Rodriguez Hertz source and Wu 2015 are cited. Mechanism: show the invariant set trapped in the zero-curvature locus has Liouville measure zero, using recurrence plus a transverse expansion estimate. The source states Wu handles finitely many negative-curvature components. Decisive check: passing from finitely many to arbitrarily many components needs a uniform estimate; exhaustion alone does not preserve it. **Defer:** original proofs unread and no uniform estimate. This is an ergodic-geometric problem, not a random-point finite computation or a recycled stable/median family.

6. **Guth's small-volume sponge embedding.** P§18 p11: for every n is there ε_n>0 such that every bounded open subset of R^n of volume≤ε_n admits a length-increasing embedding into the unit ball? Guth's 2005 thesis is the attributed source; a 2016 Glynn-Adey thesis supplies a planar strip-target weakening. Mechanism: fold a thin cellular decomposition into a bounded target while preserving injectivity and a lower differential singular-value bound. Decisive check: volume alone does not bound diameter or topology; a strip construction supplies neither a ball target nor a uniform fold-separation estimate. **Reject for present depth:** no new mechanism beyond that missing estimate and original thesis bodies unread. Not ranked as a familiar hard filler.

7. **Maximal-distance minimizer reconstruction and uniqueness.** T§1.2 p2 Questions1.5–1.6 form ONE family: if Σ minimizes length among connected compact sets covering M within r, is it the unique r-minimizer for its own closed r-neighborhood B_r(Σ)? Does the energetic-point set G_Σ determine a minimizer for fixed M,r? Energetic means there is y∈M at distance r with the open ball B_r(y) disjoint from Σ. The source states Σ remains a minimizer for its neighborhood and distinguishes that elementary observation from uniqueness. Mechanism: prove equality rigidity in the covering constraint, or construct two inequivalent networks with exactly equal offset neighborhoods. Decisive check: equal lengths and common terminal points do not imply equal offset neighborhoods; a Steiner-tree tie alone is insufficient. **Provisional follow-up only:** actual original underlying [2] proof body unread, and no equal-neighborhood counterexample. Regularity Question1.4, horseshoe classification, random Steiner constants and every Steiner topology are NOT padded into more family counts.

8. **Compact realization of prescribed centers of distances.** G§7 p12 Problem34: given compact C⊂[0,∞) with 0∈C, is there compact K⊂R such that C(K)=C, where C(K)={a≥0:∀x∈K ∃y∈K, |x−y|=a}? Problem33's Borel variant belongs to the same inverse-realization family. The author dates the compact question to a 2020 workshop and cites Bartoszewicz et al., *On the operator of center of distances between the spaces of closed subsets of the real line*, DOI https://doi.org/10.12775/TMNA.2023.023 (2024), Theorem4.10 for a partial answer. Actual question body and nearby results read: arbitrary set realization by transfinite induction gives no descriptive/compactness control. Mechanism: intersect nested finite symmetric realizations while retaining a designated partner for every x and every a∈C. Decisive check: Hausdorff limits can add universal distances, so one also needs exclusion witnesses for every a outside C. **Provisional follow-up only:** cited original partial proof unread; no complete construction or safe exclusion invariant. Jones problems, Cantorval unions and spectrum questions are not counted as further new families.
