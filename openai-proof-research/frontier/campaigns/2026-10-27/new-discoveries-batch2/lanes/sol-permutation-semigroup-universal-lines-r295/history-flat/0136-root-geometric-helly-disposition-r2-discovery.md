Printed p1: "a set of points is in c.s.c. position … if it is contained in the boundary of a centrally symmetric convex body. **Question.** Does there exist a number k such that for any planar set X the following holds: If any k points from X are in c.s.c position, then the whole set X is in c.s.c. position." Attributed to K. Swanepoel, private communication 2010. The paper proves k ≥ 9 (Thm 1.1, nine-point example, pp3–4) and the closed-curve case with 6 (Thm 1.2, pp4–7). Neither is a general theorem.

Literal-scope findings (rigorous, **wording/domain corrections only — not creditable**):

1. *Unbounded sets.* Every finite subset of the parabola Π = {y = x²} is c.s.c. (Lemma T4 below with v = (0,1)), but Π is not contained in the boundary of any compact convex body (unbounded), nor of any unbounded closed centrally symmetric convex set with interior (in the plane these are strips and R², whose boundaries are ≤ 2 parallel lines). So the literal "any planar set" statement is false for every k. The authors' intent (finite, or at least bounded, sets) is clear from their §2 and Theorem 1.2.
2. *Small-set vacuity.* If |X| < k the hypothesis is vacuous; the nine-point non-c.s.c. set then refutes every k ≥ 10. The nonvacuous reading is |X| ≥ k, equivalently "every subset of at most k points" (c.s.c. is hereditary).
3. *Bounded infinite sets reduce to finite sets* (Theorem T below). So the meaningful question is exactly the finite one: **is there k such that every finite planar X with |X| ≥ k, all of whose k-subsets are c.s.c., is c.s.c.?** Equivalently: are minimal non-c.s.c. finite sets of uniformly bounded size?

## 1. Exact center criterion (Lemma A) — ordinary, proved here, used by all checks

Let X be finite, noncollinear, in convex position, P = conv X, h = h_P, and D the set of outer edge normals of P together with their negatives. Then O is an admissible center (X ∪ (2O − X) in convex position) iff

  for every x ∈ X there is n ∈ D with n·x = h(n) and 2 n·O ≤ h(n) − h(−n).

*Proof.* x is on the boundary of conv(X ∪ X_O) iff some unit n supports it: n·x ≥ n·z for z ∈ X (i.e. n in the normal cone N_x, an arc of length < π) and n·x ≥ 2n·O − n·y for all y ∈ X. Put g(n) = n·x + min_y n·y − 2 n·O. On the cone generated by N_x, g is positively homogeneous and linear on each subcone between consecutive rays of D ∩ N_x (the minimizer y changes only where −n is an edge normal; the endpoints of N_x are edge normals). A linear function on a planar cone spanned by two rays is ≥ 0 somewhere iff it is ≥ 0 on a bounding ray. ∎

Consequences: (i) M_X is the closed set ∩_x ∪_{n∈D∩N_x} {n·O ≤ μ(n)}, μ(n) = (h(n) − h(−n))/2, a union of closed faces of the arrangement of the midparallel lines {n·O = μ(n)}; every face is pointed (≥ 2 nonparallel lines), so **M_X ≠ ∅ iff some arrangement vertex lies in M_X**. This gives an exact finite Fraction decision procedure (`csc.py`, `csc2.py`), cross-validated against brute-force hull computation (0 mismatches in 41,656 sample/center pairs; `t_basic.py`, `t_fast.py`) and reproducing the source's nine-point minimal example. (ii) Geometric form (Lemma B): M_X = R² \ ∪_x int((x + P)/2 + T_x), with T_x = {v : v·n ≥ 0 ∀ n ∈ N_x}; for a triangle this is exactly the source's Lemma 2.2 (checked exactly on random triangles, 0 mismatches, `t_topo.py`).

## 2. Theorem T (transfer; rigorous; reduction only, no credit)

*If k has the finite property (every finite F, |F| ≥ k, whose k-subsets are c.s.c., is c.s.c.), then every **bounded** planar X with |X| ≥ k whose k-subsets are c.s.c. is c.s.c.* The converse is trivial. Proof outline (all steps written out in progress notes; collinear X trivial):

* T1. For bounded noncollinear X, O is admissible (X ⊂ ∂ cl conv(X ∪ X_O)) iff O ∈ M_F for every finite F ⊆ X. (⇐ uses int cl C = int C for convex C and a finite simplex witness of interiority.)
* T2. Hypothesis + finite property + heredity ⇒ every finite F ⊆ X has M_F ≠ ∅ (closed, finite union of polyhedra by Lemma A).
* Case A: some ball B_R meets every M_F. Then {M_F ∩ B_R} are compact with the finite intersection property (M_{F∪F'} ⊆ M_F ∩ M_{F'}), giving a common admissible center.
* Case B: otherwise every M_G is unbounded; the sets V_G of unit recession directions of M_G are nonempty, closed, and have the FIP, so some v ∈ ∩_G V_G.
* T3. If M_G contains a ray O + tv (t ≥ 0) then G ⊂ ∂(conv G + R₊v): otherwise a ball B(x,ε) ⊂ conv(G ∪ (G + Sv)); each g + Sv is within O(1/t) of conv(G ∪ {2O − g₀ + 2tv}), so B(x, ε/2) ⊂ conv(G ∪ G_{O+tv}) for large t, contradicting O + tv ∈ M_G. By a finite witness again, X ⊂ ∂(cl conv X + R₊v).
* T4. (Coordinates v = (0,1), x-range [a,b] of X, c = (a+b)/2, Y = sup|y|.) If X ⊂ ∂(cl conv X + R₊v) then O = (c,T), T ≥ Y, is admissible: for a supporting normal n of the region at x with n_y < 0, h_{X_O}(n) − n·x ≤ −2|n_y|(T − Y) ≤ 0, uniformly; for n = (±1,0) the x-ranges of X and X_O coincide. ∎

This replaces the parallelogram/closed-curve approximations, which do not preserve the hereditary hypothesis.

## 3. Mechanism (a): the near-centrally-symmetric regime is a Hadwiger transversal problem

Let Q = {±q_1,…,±q_m} be a centrally symmetric convex 2m-gon (ccw q_1,…,q_m,−q_1,…), d_j = q_{j+1} − q_j, and X = {q_i + εu_i}. First-order analysis of Lemma A (the two near-parallel midparallel lines of each edge pair pass through the pair midpoints w_j = (u_j + u_{j+m})/2; each line through w_j serves x_j on one side and x_{j+m} on the other) gives the **model**:

  X is c.s.c. ⇔ ∃ O with O − w_j ∈ W_j for all j, W_j the closed double cone spanned by ±d_{j−1}, ±d_j,

and the arcs A_j = [dir d_{j−1}, dir d_j] (mod π) tile RP¹. Deleting one point of pair j drops constraint j and leaves the others unchanged (the unmatched partner is served by the far chord line). The model agreed with the exact test on all 392 random instances (m = 3, 4; ε = 10⁻⁴; `validate_model.py`). This first-order correspondence is diagnostic, not a proof for all ε.

Under projective duality (point (a,b) ↔ line y = ax − b) "line O w_j has direction in A_j" becomes "the dual line O* meets the segment σ_j of w_j* over the x-interval A_j". The A_j have disjoint interiors, so the σ_j lie over disjoint x-intervals and any nonvertical transversal meets them in x-order. **Hadwiger's transversal theorem** (ordered transversals of disjoint convex sets: Helly number 3) therefore gives: an infeasible model system has an infeasible subsystem of 3 constraints (touching segments at shared arc endpoints need a limiting argument not supplied here). Exact search found no 4-element minimal infeasible system in 140,586 random infeasible instances (`search4.py`) and only size-3 minimal subsystems in 36,271 reductions (`search_model.py`).

Consequence: in this regime, a non-c.s.c. X of ≥ 4 pairs still fails after deleting a point of any pair outside an infeasible triple, so **minimal obstructions near a centrally symmetric polygon have at most 3 pairs (≤ 6 points) to first order**. This is the strongest classical objection to route (a): perturbative symmetric families cannot produce unbounded minimal obstructions. It also explains the source's closed-curve number 6.

## 4. Mechanism (b) and the triangle family: what failed

* **Three-triangle Helly is false.** A natural route to k = 9 is "if M_X = ∅ then three triangles T₁,T₂,T₃ ⊆ X have ∩ M_{T_i} = ∅" (the source's 9-point argument has this flavour). Exact refutation: the six-point minimal obstruction recorded in `logs/t_six.txt` (seed 11, `t_six.py`) has ∩ of all 20 M_T empty, but **no** three of the 20 M_T have empty intersection. The float-prefiltered search (`t_three_fast.py`) found no three-triangle witness for any of 47 minimal obstructions (sizes 6–9).
* **Topological Helly is unavailable directly.** M_T is contractible, but pairwise intersections M_T ∩ M_{T'} from convex configurations already have up to 4 components, and 5-fold intersections up to 8 (`t_topo.py`, `t_topo2.py`, exact nerve computation). Every computed component was acyclic (Euler characteristic = number of components), so a Kalai–Meshulam (disjoint union of ≤ r acyclic sets ⇒ Helly ≤ 3r) argument would need a uniform r for **all** subfamily intersections, which the data contradict for arbitrary subfamilies. Bounding components only of the sets M_Y (all triangles of Y) is not a Helly family and was not pursued to a theorem.
* No uniform tallest-triangle branch certificate was found.

## 5. Mechanism (a) far from symmetry: adversarial search

Exact searches for minimal obstructions (non-c.s.c. X with every one-point deletion c.s.c., deletion centers recorded exactly):

* perturbed regular odd n-gons, radial perturbations of order δ_n = (1 − cos(π/n))/2: n = 9 gives many minimal 9-point obstructions (889 of 2,343 obstructions in 50 s); n = 11 gives **none** among 2,721 obstructions (`search_odd.py`);
* greedy reduction of random obstructions: minimal sizes {4, 6, 7, 8, 9} only, in 1,752 (odd regime) + 6,528 (near-circle regime) reductions (`reduce_search.py`); size 4 = non-convex position;
* hill climbing toward 10- and 11-point minimal obstructions (`climb.py`, `climb_insert.py`): see progress.md for the final tallies; the best states reached 9 of 10 and 9 of 11 c.s.c. deletions, never all.

These are finite diagnostics. They support the conjecture that **k = 9 is the exact answer** for finite (hence bounded) sets, but no proof was obtained.

## 6. Exact missing statement and next step

Missing: a uniform bound on minimal non-c.s.c. finite convex configurations (conjecturally 9). It cannot come from (i) Helly for the triangle sets M_T (refuted), (ii) perturbations of symmetric polygons (Hadwiger bound 6), or (iii) adding parallelograms or curve approximation (hypothesis not preserved). A plausible proof object: a Hadwiger-type ordered-transversal reformulation for general X, in which the role of the pair constraints D_j is played by the per-vertex runs of critical directions (the sign-pattern description in progress.md, §"odd function"), with bounded-size certificates per constraint. Decisive next calculation: decide whether the sign-vector constraints admit an ordering compatible with a Hadwiger/Wenger theorem; failing that, attempt a 10-point minimal obstruction by a two-scale construction (near-regular 9-gon with a point inserted at scale δ²).
