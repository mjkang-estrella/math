# Eight additional attributed families

All are reused r263 leads, re-read from flat hash-bound primary bodies. This is eight distinct families additional to the biquadratic target, not eight new questions or certified open problems. The Taylor source was frozen in the initial batch but deliberately not pursued, and the visible-lattice, grid and Schur targets were not duplicated. No r268/r269 changing output was read. Broad specialist follow-up is incomplete except for the decisive biquadratic retirement; no absence claim establishes clearance.

1. **Pourhadi–Faghani, arXiv:1909.02634v1, Problems 2.21–2.22, printed pp.11–12.** Does an (m,n)-biregular bipartite graph with m≠n fail the quasi-λ property? Does a quasi-λ-DB graph without edge transitivity exist? These are one related family, counted once. The literal property uses a single rational λ>1 across all adjacent pairs, permitting reciprocal orientation. Actual Proposition 2.18 proves a construction via explicit distance layers. The diameter-three incidence mechanism gives the full 2.22 endpoint in PROOF.md, but is routine. The guarded current arXiv query and source-version page did not supply a later vertex-property proof body; the edge-property 2406.11876 is a different object and was not substituted. Reject nomination. Next useful question would require the exact Conjecture 2.9 scope (two distinct degrees and transmissions), not treating this existence witness as a counterexample to it: this graph has exactly two of each.

2. **Siddhi Pathak, arXiv:1909.03972v1, Conjecture 1 and Question 1.4, pp.1,4.** Conjecture 1 concerns all periodic ±1 values away from multiples of q, zero on multiples, and nonvanishing of the convergent sum f(n)/n. The source's Question 1.4 instead says q>2 and integer k>1, L(k,f)≠0; Remark 1.5 immediately proves it using |f(1)|≤ζ(k)−1<1. This actual proof makes 1.4 an already answered question, not a survivor. Theorem 1.3's density-zero exceptional set for odd moduli does not prove the full k=1 conjecture. Reject k>1; park the famous k=1 question without a new mechanism. Next calculation, if justified by new structure, would be exact rational relations among digamma values for a fixed modulus; such an instance would not solve the universal question.

3. **Bell–Hamidizadeh–Huang–Venegas, arXiv:1909.04023v1, Question 4.1, printed p.16.** For every positive-characteristic field k and affine domain A of GK dimension one with k inseparably closed in A, is A cancellative? This does not mean k is algebraically closed. Actual preceding proof constructs noncancellation by incompatible commutator ratios; the following paragraph forces any surviving negative example to be Azumaya with center k'[x], nontrivial Brauer group and discrepant iterative/Hasse–Schmidt invariants, using Propositions 3.4 and 2.7. No counterexample satisfying the inseparable-closure hypothesis is supplied. Hold; next step is a descent theorem or a concrete twisted Azumaya example meeting all these constraints. No characteristic-zero slice argument establishes it.

4. **Kerstetter–O’Neill, arXiv:1909.04281v3, Conjecture 1.1 and Remark 1.2, printed pp.1–2.** For eventually increasing integer polynomials f_i, Betti elements of P_n=<f_1(n),…,f_k(n)> are eventually quasipolynomial, with Frobenius number/genus/type claimed as consequences. The source itself states that the eventual quasipolynomial claims were subsequently proved by formal logic/Presburger arithmetic, while the informal implication remains open. Actual Example 7.1 lists nonlinear minimal presentations, and the paper proves the linear case. Retire the already proved numerical laws; do not turn an explicitly informal “consequence” into a precise independent theorem. Next calculation would first need a well-defined functorial bridge from the parametrized Betti data to the other invariants, rather than more interpolation.

5. **Vladimir Petrov Kostov, arXiv:1909.05532v1, Problems 1–2, printed pp.1–3.** Problem 1 asks, given coefficient sign changes/preservations of a hyperbolic polynomial with distinct root moduli, which positions are occupied by positive roots. The paper resolves degree six with two sign changes, not all degrees. Problem 2 allows nonhyperbolic polynomials, with nonzero integer positive/negative root counts subject to Descartes parity and upper bounds; it is not the same question. Actual Remark 2 transports realizations under reversion, reducing cases, while §3 classifies the degree-six patterns. Hold arbitrary-degree classification; next mechanism is an inductive realizability obstruction stable under root insertion and reversion. A degree-six recheck receives no credit; later arbitrary-degree bodies have not been cleared.

6. **Amenta–Conde-Alonso–Cruz-Uribe–Ocáriz, arXiv:1909.05987v1, §4 and original cited Problems A.2/A.3.** Characterize the dual for variable Lebesgue/sequence exponents with unbounded essential supremum, and construct practical dense subsets. The source explicitly answers the cited dense-subset question: Proposition 4.4 partitions by p in [k,k+1) and glues simple approximations with errors ε2^-k. Thus density alone is retired. Its paragraph immediately before §4 says the residual sequence dual characterization remains open; that is an open-ended representation task, not a crisp norm identity. Next mechanism would characterize the annihilator of the explicitly constructed dense class without silently setting the germ dual equal to bounded finitely additive measures. No complete representation lemma found here.

7. **Mareike Dressler, arXiv:1909.06707v2, §5, pp.21–25.** For finite Γ⊂R^n, determine dim C_(n,2d)(Γ), and when it equals dim P_(n,2d)(Γ). Actual Proposition 5.1 uses the rank of a finite agiform coefficient matrix; Lemma 5.3/Proposition 5.4 restrict support parity for certain half-cube sets. The univariate case is complete. This is the remaining research pivot, with two routes and the missing span-attainability lemma in discovery.md. Next step is symbolic two-torus-point compatibility plus a specialist follow-up screen. General Γ, coordinate-zero cases, and the P-face comparison remain unresolved here. No claim of priority.

8. **Maurice Margenstern, arXiv:1909.01893v1, conclusion and Theorems 7,9,11–15.** Introductory questions ask how preferred-son properties change with black/white root, assignment, and metallic versus nonzero-metallic digit systems. The conclusion says Theorem 7 already establishes the white metallic preferred-son property for every assignment; Theorem 9 singles out the nonzero-code white assignment, and the black-tree statements differ. These are cases actually treated, not a fresh universal conjecture licensed by the closing phrase “other problems are probably still open.” Reject as an unquantified survivor. Next calculation only after locating an exact untreated assignment/code statement in the cited predecessor; an automaton check of a treated case would duplicate the body.

Ranking: biquadratic is decisively retired; graph 2.22 has a full but routine endpoint; SONC is a technical research pivot with an explicit full-scope gap. Cancellation has precise hypotheses but a substantial structural obstruction. None passes the campaign's novelty and endpoint gates.
