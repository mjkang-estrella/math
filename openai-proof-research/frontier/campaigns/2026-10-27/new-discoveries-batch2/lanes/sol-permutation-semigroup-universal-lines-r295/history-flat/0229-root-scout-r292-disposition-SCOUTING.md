## Screen

1. **Minimal AP-irreducible sparsity / algebraic positivity. RETIRED CAMPAIGN DUPLICATE; full ordinary repeat proof preserved.** Sunil Das, [2112.00442v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.00442v3), 22 March 2022; §3 p10 definition, Lemma3.10 p12, §5 Conjectures5.1–5.2 p22. Literal C5.1: for every order n and every minimally AP-irreducible zero-diagonal sign pattern A, nz(A)≤2n−2. “Minimal” means that deleting any nonzero entry destroys the specified property, not minimal positivity of a particular numerical realization. C5.2 asks whether allowing algebraic positivity is equivalent to AP-irreducibility of A or −A; that endpoint is not resolved here. Early exact-family history hit: r245 and sol-das-ap-irreducible-source-formal-r1, initially missed in truncated output and later read. The former already has a stronger4n−10 family; this lane offers no fresh eligibility. Closest predecessor body [Abagat–Pelejo 1806.09641v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09641v2), Theorem4 and block-eigenvector argument in §2, gives B_A irreducibility as necessary for allowing positivity. Das's actual Lemma3.10 contraction proof assumes no positive arcs between positive SCCs. Actual successor [Das 2412.06379v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.06379v4), §2 Lemma2.5 and eigenvector obstruction proofs, then §3 cycle-pattern classifications, treats **requiring** algebraic positivity with a cycle-support restriction, not general minimal-AP sparsity. Mechanism: exploit opposite essential cuts in A and B_A. Decisive exact calculation: n=5, nz=9; every deletion has an explicit cut or positive row/column obstruction. Strongest routine objection: is this just Lemma3.10 with its contraction extended? No: positive chain arcs cross positive SCCs, and a single contraction no longer preserves both minimality conditions. PROOF.md gives a structural family for every n≥5, all with 2n−1 entries. Eligibility fails freshness: this is already a campaign family. Published-source/current-prior-art holds from the older work are not resolved by this repeat.

2. **Unique sepr-sequence characterization. OPEN-IN-SOURCE lead, no full result.** Hogben–Lin–Olesky–van den Driessche, [1807.04874v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04874v1), Conjecture3.8 pp7–8. For every n×n sign pattern P, a singleton set of sepr-sequences for all real realizations Q(P) is conjectured equivalent to: at each order k, either every principal k-subpattern has fixed determinant sign, or the common term is S* and three principal subpatterns have fixed signs +,−,0. S* means all three kinds occur. Actual Corollary3.5 proves sufficiency; Proposition3.11 uses generic perturbations to force an identically zero minor; Lemma3.12 simultaneously zeros ambiguous 2×2 determinants; Proposition3.13 proves necessity through n≤4. These actual proofs and Example3.1's direct-sum phenomenon were inspected. The live exact-phrase successor query returns this source only; that is weak coverage. Early compact-history exact hit: none. Mechanism: engineer correlated ambiguous ≥3 minors that always include positive and negative values, without individually sign-fixed minors of both signs. Decisive task is an all-real qualitative-class sign certificate, not a sample of integer realizations. Routine objection: block direct sums already have public masking examples, and the source tests them; a new example must fail the exact fixed-minor condition. Rankable only provisionally, with current broader body-search gap. No expensive search run.

3. **Completeness of permutation numerical semigroup families. OPEN-IN-SOURCE; defer.** S. Ugolini, [1609.01133v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.01133v2), §5.1 Conjecture5.1 p43. In the 3-permutation setting, every such semigroup with multiplicity ≥12 is proposed to belong to one of H1,k,…,H16,k defined in §4. A 3-permutation semigroup has its increasing positive enumeration partitioned into consecutive triples, each a permutation modulo3, and is generated by its first three positives. The printed conjecture says “3-numerical”; its surrounding definition and family discussion are essential, and no exploit of that wording is claimed. Source §3 classifies the 2-permutation case by initial residue-order contradictions; §4's construction/proof framework uses interval blocks, Apéry sets and Frobenius bounds. Only the initial construction framework and §3 proof were closely read, not all sixteen long family proofs. The exact successor query located no relevant successor, only unrelated semigroups. Early compact-history exact hit: none. Mechanism: finite Apéry certificate for a triple outside all sixteen formulas, or classify residue-order chambers. Routine objection: an arbitrary numerical semigroup is outside the intended 3-permutation class; finite initial triples alone do not establish eventual enumeration behavior. Decisive next task: prove a conductor-complete checking criterion and extract all sixteen formulas before search. Not ranked because full family-body comparison is incomplete.

4. **Double blocking sets with two long secants. OPEN-IN-SOURCE construction lead; defer.** Csajbók–Héger, [1805.01267v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.01267v2), 19 February2019, Conjecture1.3 p2: for every prime power q≥13 with q not congruent2 mod3, there exists a double blocking set in PG(2,q) of size3q−1 with two (q−1)-secants. Each projective line must meet the set at least twice. Actual §2 coordinate normalization and Proposition2.6 symmetry/cross-ratio argument inspected; theorem1.1 and §3 resolve the distinct triangle six-deletion/five-addition question, and §4 supplies particular finite constructions. Neither these nor examples q≤43 prove all q. Exact-family compact-history hit: none. Mechanism: exploit a field-polynomial orbit construction meeting every slope twice, or give one finite-field nonexistence certificate for an allowed q. Routine objection: field order ≡2 is explicitly excluded, and verifying a published small set is uncredited. Missing task: current exact successor-body screen and an all-q construction, or an exhaustive exact certificate for one allowed order. No claim that the Hill resolutions themselves are new. Not ranked because current successor search was not completed.

5. **Simplified de Bruijn graph non-word-representability. UNRESOLVED-IN-READ-BODIES; defer.** Anthony Petyuk, [2210.14762v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.14762v2), 12 July2023, §4 Conjecture1 p5: all S(n,k), n≥4 and k≥3, are non-word-representable. All vertices are length-n words, with directed de Bruijn overlaps simplified to undirected edges and loops removed. Actual proofs show binary graphs 3-colorable and establish nonrepresentability for n=2,3 by induced obstructions. The available actual successors [2312.10377v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.10377v1), embedding theorems4–5, and [2605.02268v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02268v2), §3 Theorem3.1 orientation proof, establish word-representability for structured induced shift subgraphs, not all S(n,k). Increasing coordinate chains prohibit shortcuts in that proof; repeated letters in full de Bruijn graphs invalidate that argument. Early historical de Bruijn hit is a different factor-count family. Mechanism: find one full S(n,3) semi-transitive orientation or a uniform induced nonrepresentable obstruction for all n. Routine objection: orienting a shift subgraph or exhibiting a graph homomorphism to a representable graph does not settle full S(n,k). No full certificate; n grows exponentially. Not selected in the initial deep slot.

6. **Extremal word representation number. FAMILY OVERLAP; defer, no reopen.** Akgün–Gent–Kitaev–Zantema, [1808.01215v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.01215v1), §2 Conjectures1–2: crown H_(n,n) maximizes representation number among bipartite graphs on2n vertices; apex-crown G_n maximizes it among word-representable graphs on2n+1 vertices. The latter has the known value n; crown values for n≥5 are ceil(n/2). Source's SAT encoding and computational discussion are actual bodies, not a proof of either universal conjecture. The exact r249 history instead asks about the largest induced crown; an order-dimension bridge there is for permutational representation, not arbitrary representation. Live search surfaced [2506.01057v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01057v1) claiming substantial bipartite partial progress, but its actual body was not fetched, so its metadata cannot retire the remaining even balanced case. Mechanism would need a word-representation lower certificate for a specific competing graph. Strong objection: high poset dimension alone is insufficient. Conservative same-family overlap prevents deep investment.

7. **Generalized Lyndon-factor counting identities/root locations. REJECT as low-value literal defect; merged endpoints.** Hossein Teimoori Faal, [2205.13752v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13752v1), §2.2 PDFpIV, Conjectures1–2. S(N;n,k) counts length-N words with exactly k distinct Lyndon factors in their nonincreasing factorization, with the printed convention S(N,n,0)=1. For every N≥n>1, C1 asserts the alternating sum is0. C2, for every N≥n≥1, asserts the resulting polynomial is real-rooted and has −1 as a root. At N=n=2, aa,ab,bb have one distinct factor and ba has two. Thus the literal polynomial is1+3x+x² and its value at−1 is−1. This rejects the asserted identity and −1 endpoint, not real-rootedness (its roots are real). The paper's nearby graph lemmas are stated without proofs and do not supply a counting mechanism. Exact compact-history hit: none. Strong eligibility objection: elementary definition/convention mismatch; not a substantive accepted discovery, and no PROOF endpoint or count for this family. No expensive search.

8. **Lyndon components as whole-factor selections. SOURCE-AMBIGUITY/BODY GAP; defer.** Same [2205.13752v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.13752v1), §2.2 Question1 pIV. For every represented word w=l1…lh in nonincreasing Lyndon factorization and every connected component G_con of G(w), does a representing word exist as a concatenation of some of the original factors (indices in source are nondecreasing)? This is a graph-component/factor-compatibility family, distinct from scalar counting above. Source Definition1's scattered-subword graph and Lemmas3–4/Proposition3 are relevant, but their proofs are omitted or delegated by analogy. No exact compact-history hit. Mechanism: a finite word whose component size/edges cannot be produced from any allowed whole-factor selection; decisive enumeration must settle whether repeated indices are allowed by the literal displayed convention. Objection: changing to arbitrary induced subwords would trivialize the target while losing its factor-selection clause. Not viable without a source convention clarification and the original structural proof body; no claim of refutation.

10. **Minimal Hamming distance inside Parikh M-classes. This new2024 question is outside the preferred original2000–2023 window and the original2022 body remains unread. Mechanism would be an explicit unbounded-distance sequence of complete M-classes, not just pairs of equivalent words. Routine objection: two distant members do not bound the minimum over the whole class. No nomination.

11. **M-ambiguity under repeated letter duplication. PUBLICLY SOLVED endpoint; retire.** Poovanandran–Teh [1901.03937v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.03937v1), Question4.1 p8: for every ordered alphabet, fixed x,y and letter a, is the ambiguity sequence of xa^k y eventually periodic? Nearest historical Parikh hit is not this endpoint. Mechanism: Presburger/polyhedral projection already supplies the answer; a finite oscillating prefix is irrelevant to eventual periodicity. No new credit.

12. **Proper-orientation bounds by maximum average degree. CAMPAIGN COVERED / publicly solved basic question; no reopen.** Chen–Mohar–Wu [2110.07005v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.07005v2), 8 December2022. Problem1.7 asks whether one function of mad(G) bounds the proper orientation number for every finite graph. Actual Theorem1.8 and its fractional-orientation/Hall method give the affirmative nonlinear bound, and Theorems1.2/1.4 settle bipartite and planar boundedness. §7 Question7.2 p20 asks the stronger existence of universal constants C,D with proper orientation number≤C·mad(G)+D for every finite graph; this is not established by those theorems. r262 already covered the broad mad family. Routine objection: a small exact proper-orientation value only tests a parameter instance. Not ranked.
