
### 346458 The Markoff graph connectivity conjecture modulo primes 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05315 
Let $\mathcal M$ be the set of positive integer solutions to

$$
x^2+y^2+z^2=3xyz,
$$

and let $\mathcal M_p$ be the set of non-zero solutions modulo a prime $p$. The functional graph is generated by the involutions

$$
\mathcal R_1(x,y,z)=(3yz-x,y,z),
$$

and the analogous maps $\mathcal R_2,\mathcal R_3$, together with permutations of the coordinates. Let $\mathcal C_p\subseteq\mathcal M_p$ denote the largest connected component of the resulting graph. **Baragar–Bourgain–Gamburd–Sarnak conjecture.** For every prime $p$, one has

$$
\mathcal C_p=\mathcal M_p.
$$
 This asserts that the Markoff triples generate all non-zero solutions modulo $p$ and that the associated functional graph is connected; the supplied text gives no evidence resolving the conjecture.

### 346488 The Fermat Deligne-period quotient conjecture 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06722

$$
\frac{c^+(\mathbf{H}^n_{a,j})}{c^-(\mathbf{H}^n_{a,j})}=\sigma i,

### 346505 Martin and Thatte's maximum agreement subtree conjecture for balanced trees 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07357 
Let ${\mathcal S}$ and ${\mathcal T}$ be balanced rooted binary phylogenetic $X$-trees, meaning that each has $2^m$ leaves for some non-negative integer $m$ and height $m$, and let $n=|X|$. Write ${\rm mast}({\mathcal S},{\mathcal T})$ for the maximum size of a common restriction that is an agreement subtree of ${\mathcal S}$ and ${\mathcal T}$. **Martin and Thatte's conjecture.**

$$
{\rm mast}({\mathcal S},{\mathcal T})\geq n^{\frac12}.
$$

The conjecture proposed a square-root lower bound for the size of a maximum agreement subtree of two balanced rooted binary phylogenetic trees. It is refuted by the paper's main theorem, which gives, for every $c>0$, balanced rooted binary trees with maximum agreement subtree of size less than $c n^{\frac12}$; hence the conjecture is false.

### 346824 Esperet–Kang–Thomassé conjecture on dense induced bipartite subgraphs 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05101 
Let $d$ be a positive real number, and let $G$ be a triangle-free graph with minimum degree at least $d$. $$

### 346837 Grzesik–Janzer–Nagy conjecture on Turán numbers of graph blow-ups 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05897

**Grzesik–Janzer–Nagy conjecture.** If

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=O(n^{2-\alpha})
$$

for some constant $0\leq\alpha\leq1$, then for every positive integer $r$,

$$
\mathrm{ex}(n,F[r])=O\left(n^{2-\frac{\alpha}{r}}\right).
$$

The conjecture is known when $F$ is a tree and when $F=K_{s,t}$ with $\alpha=1/s$, including the case $C_4=K_{2,2}$, but it remains open already for even cycles $F=C_{2k}$ with $k\geq3$.

### 347227 Even-path inverse Turán conjecture

**Degenerate-graph density conjecture.** For every fixed $k$-degenerate graph $H$,

$$
C(H,\mathcal{D}_k,n) \in \Theta\left(n^{f_k(H)}\right).
$$

This is proposed as a generalisation of the paper's bounds for degenerate graph classes. The source does not report a proof or disproof, so the conjecture remains open.

### 348155 The improved ball-number bound for links 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00580 
Let $L$ be a link. Its **ball number** $\mathsf{ball}(L)$ is the minimum number of non-overlapping solid balls needed in a necklace representation of $L$, and its **crossing number** $\mathsf{cr}(L)$ is the minimum crossing number among diagrams of $L$.

**Improved ball-number conjecture.** For every link $L$,

$$
\mathsf{ball}(L)\leq 4\mathsf{cr}(L).
$$

Moreover, equality holds if $L$ is alternating.

The paper proves the weaker upper bound $\mathsf{ball}(L)\leq 5\mathsf{cr}(L)$ and presents an explicit construction of necklace representations. The proposed improvement to $4\mathsf{cr}(L)$, together with the alternating-link equality statement, is not resolved in the supplied text.

### 348183 The aging conjecture for the running maximum of the Mott random walk 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01779 
Let $\rho<1$, let $\beta,\lambda\geq0$, and let $Z^{\beta,\lambda}$ be the limiting process. For $h>1$, define

$$
\theta(h):=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\leq1}Z^{\beta,\lambda}_s=\sup_{s\leq h}Z^{\beta,\lambda}_s\right).
$$

**Aging conjecture.**

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}^{\beta,\lambda/n}\left(\sup_{s\leq n^{1+1/\rho}}X_s=\sup_{s\leq n^{1+1/\rho}h}X_s\right)=\theta(h)\qquad\text{for every }h>1,
$$

with

$$
\lim_{h\to1}\theta(h)=1,\qquad \lim_{h\to\infty}\theta(h)=0.
$$

This predicts aging of the running maximum: on the anomalous time scale, the maximum over a time interval remains unchanged when the observation interval is enlarged by a factor $h$ with probability tending to $\theta(h)$. The source presents this as an expected property and does not establish it.

### 348240 NP-hardness conjecture for promise CSPs between $\mathbf{LO}_k$ and $\mathbf{LO}_l$ 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04623 
For integers $2\leq k<l$, let $\mathbf{LO}_k$ and $\mathbf{LO}_l$ denote the corresponding symmetric relational templates, and let $\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{LO}_k,\mathbf{LO}_l)$ be the promise problem whose instances homomorphically map to $\mathbf{LO}_k$ and whose outputs must homomorphically map to $\mathbf{LO}_l$. **$\mathbf{LO}$-template hardness conjecture.** For every $2\leq k<l$, $\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{LO}_k,\mathbf{LO}_l)$ is NP-hard. The paper has classified the relevant three-element cases except for $\mathbf{LO}_3$; the smallest case $\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{LO}_2,\mathbf{LO}_3)$ is therefore among the unresolved instances motivating the conjecture.

### 348249 The square-root error conjecture for optimal partial-feedback card guessing 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05059 
Let $\mathcal{P}_{m,n}^+$ denote the maximum expected number of correctly guessed cards in the partial-feedback card-guessing model with $m$ card types and $n$ copies of each type. **Square-root error conjecture.** For every $\varepsilon>0$ and $n$ sufficiently large,

$$
\mathcal{P}_{m,n}^+=m+O(m^{1/2+\varepsilon}).
$$

The conjecture would improve the paper's proved upper bound $m+O(m^{3/4}\log m)$ and would nearly match the known lower bound $m+\Omega(m^{1/2})$. The authors identify two parts of the proof where the current error term is oversized, so the conjecture remains open.

### 348343 Post-first-step monotonicity conjecture for majority dynamics 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08172 
Let $G_{n,p}$ be a random graph sampled independently of an arbitrary initial red-blue coloring of its vertices, and let $\varepsilon>0$. Assume

$$
p\geq \frac{(1+\varepsilon)\log n}{n}.
$$

After the first step of majority dynamics, compare the numbers of vertices of the two colors. **Post-first-step monotonicity conjecture.** With probability tending to $1$, whichever color is more numerous after the first step will have increasingly more vertices after every subsequent step, until all vertices have that color. This stronger conjecture would, together with the paper's central limit theorem, determine the value of $\varepsilon$ in the one-extra-vertex conjecture.

### 348419 Eventual uniqueness of maximum independent sets in monomial graphs 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11112 
For positive integers $n$ and nonnegative integers $d$, let $G_n(d)$ be the graph whose vertices are the monomials of degree $d$ in $n$ variables, with two monomials adjacent exactly when their least common multiple has degree $d+1$. Let $\mathbf{a}_n(d)$ denote the number of maximum independent sets of $G_n(d)$. **Eventual uniqueness conjecture.** For any $n \geq 1$, there exists some $N \geq 0$ such that $\mathbf{a}_n(d)=1$ whenever $d\equiv 0\pmod n$ and $d\geq N$.

The conjecture extends the paper's established uniqueness results in selected cases, particularly for $n=3$ with $d$ divisible by $3$ and for $n=4$ with $d$ even. It predicts eventual uniqueness along the multiples of the number of variables for every $n$.

### 348541 The entropy-density conjecture for multigraph product extremal numbers 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01626 
Let $a,r,s,d$ be integers with $a,r\geq 1$, $d\in[0,a-1]$, and $s\geq (r-1)(d+1)+2$. For sufficiently large $n$, write $\mathrm{ex}_{\Pi}(n,s,q)$ for the maximum edge-product among $(n,s,q)$-graphs, let $\Sigma_{r,d}(a,s)$ be the relevant maximum number of edges in an $s$-vertex set, and let $\Pi_{r,d}(a,n)$ denote the product supplied by the construction $\mathcal{T}_{r,d}(a,n)$. **Entropy-density conjecture.** For all such parameters and all sufficiently large $n$, 

$$
\mathrm{ex}_{\Pi}(n,s,\Sigma_{r,d}(a,s))=\Pi_{r,d}(a,n).
$$

Roughly, this says that the multigraph from $\mathcal{T}_{r,d}(a,n)$ maximises the edge-product subject to the prescribed local edge bound. The paper proves the $d=0$ case, while the general assertion remains open.

### 348569 Four-line generation conjecture for line-arrangement ideals 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02450 
Let $L=(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L},\mathcal{I})$ be a line arrangement on $[n]$, and let $I_L$ and $G_L$ be the line-arrangement ideal and the collection of polynomials defined in the paper. If $L$ has at most four lines, then

**Four-line generation conjecture.**

$$
I_L=\langle G_L\rangle.
$$

The claim is supported by the line arrangements that the authors were able to compute, but the supplied text does not report a proof or a resolution for all line arrangements with at most four lines.

### 348675 Arithmetic pattern conjecture 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07056 
Let $k$ and $N$ be positive integers. A $k$-term arithmetic progression has a basepoint $x$ and a common difference. Let $G_k(N)$ be the size of the smallest set of integers containing such a progression with basepoint $x$ for every $x\in[N]$, and let $G'_k(N)$ be the analogous minimum when the basepoints are $N$ distinct integer values. **Arithmetic pattern conjecture.**

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log G_k(N)}{\log N}=1
$$

and

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log G'_k(N)}{\log N}=1.
$$

This is presented as the type-(a) dual of the arithmetic Kakeya problem and is stated to be equivalent to the arithmetic Kakeya conjecture. The source gives no resolution of either formulation.

### 348676 The $(U_k)$-pattern conjecture 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07056 
Let $(U_k)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}$ be an increasing chain of subsets of $\mathbb{Q}^+$ with $|U_k|=k$. A $U_k$-pattern is a set obtained from $U_k$ by a common scaling and translation; $G'_{U_k}(N)$ denotes the size of the smallest set of integers containing a $U_k$-pattern with basepoint $x$ for $N$ distinct integer values of $x$. **The $(U_k)$-pattern conjecture.** For every such increasing chain,

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log G'_{U_k}(N)}{\log N}=1.
$$

This is technically a family of conjectures, one for each chain $(U_k)$. The source notes that integer and rational basepoints give the same quantity after clearing denominators.

### 348677 The restricted $(U_k)$-pattern conjecture 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07056 
Let $(U_k)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}$ be an increasing chain of subsets of $\mathbb{N}^+$ with $|U_k|=k$. Let $G_{U_k}(N)$ be the size of the smallest set of integers containing a $U_k$-pattern with basepoint $x$ for every $x\in[N]$. **The restricted $(U_k)$-pattern conjecture.** For every such chain,

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log G_{U_k}(N)}{\log N}=1.
$$

The source states that, for chains contained in $\mathbb{N}^+$, this restricted conjecture is equivalent to the preceding pattern conjecture and hence to the relevant arithmetic Kakeya formulation. No resolution is given.

### 348680 The $n$-dimensional finite-field $(U_k)$-pattern conjecture 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.07056 
Fix a prime $p$ and let $\pi_p:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{F}_p$ be the canonical projection. For $U\subset\mathbb{Z}$, a $U$-pattern in $\mathbb{F}_p^n$ is a set of the form $x+\pi_p(U)\cdot r$ with $x,r\in\mathbb{F}_p^n$ and $r\ne0$. Let $(U_k)$ be a sequence with $|U_k|=k$, and let $g_{n,U_k}(p)$ be the size of the smallest set containing a $U_k$-pattern with basepoint $x$ for every $x\in\mathbb{F}_p^n$. **The $n$-dimensional finite-field $(U_k)$-pattern conjecture.**

$$
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{\log g_{n,U_k}(p)}{\log p}=n.
$$

This is technically a family indexed by $n$ and by the sequence $(U_k)$. The supplied text does not state any resolution or further consequences.

### 348747 Herscovici–Hester–Hurlbert Target Conjecture for graph pebbling 
SOURCE https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10623 
Let $G$ be a graph and let $D$ be a target distribution on $G$. Write $|D|$ for the total demand, $\pi(G,D)$ for the smallest integer $m$ such that every configuration of $m$ pebbles is $D$-solvable, and $\pi_t(G)$ for the maximum $t$-fold pebbling number over all target vertices, where $t=|D|$. **Target Conjecture.** Every graph $G$ satisfies

$$
\pi(G,D)\leq \pi_{|D|}(G)
$$

for every target distribution $D$. The conjecture is known for trees, cycles, complete graphs, and cubes, and the general case remains open; it is intended as a tool for studying pebbling numbers more broadly, including chordal graphs and Cartesian products.
