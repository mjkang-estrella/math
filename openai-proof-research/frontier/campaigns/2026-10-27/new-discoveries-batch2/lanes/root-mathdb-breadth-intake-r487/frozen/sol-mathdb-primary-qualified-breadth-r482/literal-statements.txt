ID 342260 Jacobsthal's conjecture on the maximum and primorial Jacobsthal functions
For $n\in\mathbb{N}$, the Jacobsthal function is

$$
j(n)=\min\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid\forall a\in\mathbb{Z}\ \exists x\in\{1,\dots,m\}:a+x\text{ is coprime to }n\}.
$$

For $k\in\mathbb{N}$, let $h(k)=j(p_k\#)$ be its value at the product of the first $k$ primes, and let $H(k)$ be the maximum of $j(n)$ over products of $k$ distinct primes:

$$
H(k)=\max_{d_{\mathbb{P}}(n)=k}j(n)=\max_{\substack{\pi_i\in\mathbb{P}\\ \pi_1<\dots<\pi_k}}j\left(\prod_{i=1}^k\pi_i\right).
$$

**Jacobsthal's conjecture.** For all $k\in\mathbb{N}$,

$$
H(k)=h(k).
$$

Jacobsthal proposed this equality based on the agreement of the two functions for all then-known small values. The conjecture has subsequently been disproved, so the statement is refuted.

ID 342262 Conjecture on the doubling bound for the maximum Jacobsthal function

$$
H(k)<2\cdot H(k-1).

The source notes that this is equivalent to $\Omega(k)\le 2\cdot\Omega(k-1)+1$, would simplify the calculation of $H(k)$, and is supported by all data known there. Its validity would also imply $H(k)<2^k$ for $k\ge3$, although the weaker bound $H(k)\le2^k$ was already proved by Kanold.
