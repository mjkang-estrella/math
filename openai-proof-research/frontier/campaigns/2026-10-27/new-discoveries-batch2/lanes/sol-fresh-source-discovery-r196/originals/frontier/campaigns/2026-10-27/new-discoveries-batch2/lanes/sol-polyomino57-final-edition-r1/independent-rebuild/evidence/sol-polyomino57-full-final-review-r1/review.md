## Ordinary mathematical gate — PASS

I rederived the argument rather than inheriting the previous mathematical PASS. The signed map u=x+y,v=x−y identifies integer cells with equal-parity pairs; adjacency changes each signed coordinate by ±1. Connectivity supplies paths of at most ten edges between extreme cells, and existence of an edge makes both spans positive. Thus each span lies in [1,10]. Minima normalization shifts signed coordinates by (−U,e−V), e≡V−U mod2; this has an integer Cartesian inverse. Swapping signed spans and removing e=1 whenever either span is odd gives 55 span pairs plus 15 even/even parity alternatives, exactly 70 cases.

The odd-a correction has signed form (a−u,v−1), Cartesian linear part (−y,−x), translation ((a−1)/2,(a+1)/2). The odd-b correction (u,b+1−v) has linear part (y,x), translation ((b+1)/2,−(b+1)/2). All are affine D4 maps. C is invariant under all eight signed coordinate permutations. Applying the inverse symmetry to a normalized placement restores a translation of the original P. This is not a rotation privilege in the conclusion. Symmetry.lean's recover_original_translation proves exactly that implication; CanonicalCoverage composes it through all branches, including negative minima.

For a normalized exact-extrema piece, centers are exactly a−5≤s≤5, e+b−5≤t≤e+5 with s≡t mod2. These necessary and sufficient inequalities omit no feasible translation. Collision offsets in signed coordinates are (±4,0),(0,±4), exactly the four Cartesian deletions. Failure to fit is equivalent to hitting every collision group plus four extremal-line groups. Duplicates can be discarded. Superset pruning is equivalent only when every discarded group contains a retained one; for exclusion, any selected original subfamily suffices.
