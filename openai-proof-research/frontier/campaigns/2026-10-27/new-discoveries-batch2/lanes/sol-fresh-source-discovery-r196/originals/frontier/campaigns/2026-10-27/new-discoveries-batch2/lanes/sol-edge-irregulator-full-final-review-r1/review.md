## Ordinary mathematics, independently checked

For t≥2 take a hub and six distinct private vertices per block; edges are ab,ac,ad,bc,bd,cx,xy,yh. There are 6t+1 vertices, 8t distinct unordered edges, no loops, and a path from every vertex to the hub. This proves finite simplicity, counts, connectivity and nonvacuity.

Fix any valid deletion set S and use final retained degrees. Let ε∈{0,1} record whether cx survives. With no diamond deletion retained ab joins degree 3 to degree 3. With just ab deleted, ad joins 2 to 2. With just ad deleted, a has degree 2, b has degree 3, c has degree 2+ε: ac conflicts when ε=0 and bc conflicts when ε=1. With just ac deleted, ad joins 2 to 2. Swapping a,b covers bd,bc respectively and fixes the attachment at c. Thus every possible zero/one diamond deletion fails in both boundary states. No untracked exterior edge touches a,b,c,d. At least two diamond edges must be deleted.

If cx,xy,yh all survive, x and y both have final degree 2 and xy conflicts. Thus at least one of these three path edges is deleted, regardless of the final hub degree. Diamond and path charges are disjoint; eight-edge block sets partition the graph edges and are pairwise disjoint even though they share h. Hence |S|≥3t for every valid S, including choices disconnecting the retained graph. This is a global arbitrary-S argument, not an assumption that local optimal choices can be glued.
