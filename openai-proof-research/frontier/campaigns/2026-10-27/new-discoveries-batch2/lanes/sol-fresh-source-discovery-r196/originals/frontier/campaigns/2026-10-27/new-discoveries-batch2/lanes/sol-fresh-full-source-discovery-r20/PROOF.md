# Proposed ordinary proof of the full strong-PV discreteness conjecture

Status: complete ordinary argument proposed for independent adversarial review. No novelty, substantive-eligibility, acceptance or formal-verification certification.

## Exact source and theorem

Fenner–Green–Homer, *Fixed-Parameter Extrapolation and Aperiodic Order*, Discrete Comput. Geom. 76 (2026), 1–74, DOI 10.1007/s00454-025-00816-4, published 11 June 2026, Conjecture 11.1. For every complex number λ, let Qλ be the least subset containing 0,1 and closed under (u,v)↦(1−λ)u+λv. Then Qλ is discrete if and only if λ is an algebraic integer all of whose conjugates other than λ and its complex conjugate lie in the open real interval (0,1). Such integers are called strong PV (sPV).

The novelty-bearing direction below is necessity for algebraic integers. We explicitly use the already established integrality theorem: if Qλ is discrete, λ is an algebraic integer. Its precise statement is Theorem 8.2 of the authors' extended manuscript arXiv:1212.2889v6, and it is explicitly described as an established result in the published article's introduction. This existing, substantial theorem is an external dependency, not reproved or claimed new here. The remaining analytic, algebraic and source-polynomial bridges are supplied below. The sufficiency direction is also known; we give its norm proof.

## 1. Integer approximation with fixed endpoint values

If h:[0,1]→R is continuous and h(0),h(1) are integers, it is a uniform limit of integer-coefficient polynomials with exactly those endpoint values.

Proof. In the ordinary Bernstein approximant B_n h, round each unnormalized coefficient binom(n,k)h(k/n) to an integer, leaving k=0,n exact. The resulting polynomial H_n=Σ b_k x^k(1−x)^(n−k) has integer coefficients and the required endpoints. Its distance from B_n h is bounded by (1/2)Σ_{k=1}^{n−1}x^k(1−x)^(n−k). This sum tends uniformly to zero. Indeed for δ≤x≤1−δ it is at most (n−1)(1−δ)^n; for x≤δ<1/2 it is at most δ/(1−2δ), by summing a geometric series, and symmetrically near 1. First choose δ small, then n large. Ordinary Bernstein approximation gives B_n h→h uniformly (for example split its binomial average according to |k/n−x|<η, bounding the complementary probability by 1/(4nη²)). This proves the assertion.

## 2. A positivity lifting lemma modulo a minimal polynomial

Let m∈Z[x] have distinct roots and satisfy m(0)m(1)≠0. Let a∈Z[x] with a(0)=a(1)=0. Suppose 0<a(μ)<1 at every real root μ of m in (0,1). Then there is P∈Z[x] with

* P−a divisible by m in Z[x];
* P(0)=P(1)=0;
* 0<P(x)<1 for every 0<x<1.

Proof. Choose integers N0,N1 so that a'(0)+m(0)N0>0 and a'(1)−m(1)N1<0. Near 0 prescribe g(x)=a(x)+m(x)x(1−x)N0, and near 1 prescribe g(x)=a(x)+m(x)x(1−x)N1. Shrink the endpoint neighborhoods until 0<g<1 there. Around every root μ∈(0,1) prescribe g=a, again in sufficiently small disjoint neighborhoods where 0<a<1. Join the finitely many prescribed pieces by continuous curves in (0,1), for example linear interpolation between their endpoint values. Thus g is continuous, equals the prescribed polynomials near each exceptional point, and is strictly between 0 and 1 in the interior.

The function h=(g−a)/(m x(1−x)) extends continuously across each root μ (it is zero in its neighborhood), and across the endpoints with h(0)=N0,h(1)=N1. Moreover there exists c>0 such that g(x)≥c x(1−x) and 1−g(x)≥c x(1−x) throughout [0,1]. The first ratio extends at the endpoints with positive limits g'(0),−g'(1), and the second ratio tends to positive infinity there; compactness supplies c.

Let M=max_[0,1]|m|>0. By §1 choose H∈Z[x] with ||H−h||∞<c/(2M). Set P=a+m x(1−x)H. Then |P−g|<(c/2)x(1−x) in the interior. All three conclusions follow. Notice the endpoint derivative adjustment: uniform approximation of g alone would not suffice.

## 3. Such a P belongs to the literal source polynomial hull

Let Q[x] be generated by 0,1 under (f,g)↦(1−x)f+xg. Every integer polynomial strictly between 0 and 1 on (0,1) is in Q[x]. This is the published Theorem 3.6. Here is an independent standard Bernstein justification sufficient for its use.

For fixed n≥deg P write P=Σ b_k x^k(1−x)^(n−k). These b_k are integers: the monomial x^j has b_k=binom(n−j,k−j) for k≥j. An integer polynomial F positive on (0,1) has all these b_k nonnegative for sufficiently large n. To see this, factor all endpoint zeros as F=x^r(1−x)^s G with G strictly positive on [0,1]. For G=Σ c_j x^j the normalized Bernstein coefficients are Σ c_j (k)_j/(n)_j, uniformly within O_G(1/n) of G(k/n); hence they are positive eventually. Multiplication by x^r(1−x)^s preserves nonnegativity in the unnormalized basis, shifting the degree and indices. Apply this separately to P and 1−P. For one sufficiently large common n, 0≤b_k≤binom(n,k).

A full binary tree of depth n contributes one monomial x^k(1−x)^(n−k) per leaf of Hamming weight k. Label exactly b_k of the binom(n,k) such leaves by 1, and all other leaves by 0. Evaluating the tree with the source binary operation gives exactly P. Thus P∈Q[x]. Substitution gives P(λ)∈Qλ.

## 4. Density of a proper embedding projection

Let K be a number field, E=R^r×C^s its full Minkowski space (one representative of each complex pair). Let J⊂K be any full-rank Z-lattice. Projection of J to any proper nonempty subset of the embedding coordinates is dense in that coordinate space.

Proof. If its additive closure were proper, the elementary classification of closed subgroups of finite-dimensional real space supplies a nonzero real-linear functional ℓ on the selected coordinates with ℓ(π(J))⊂Z. To recall this separation fact: a closed subgroup is a vector space plus a lattice in a complementary subspace; if the vector-space span is proper use a functional vanishing on its span, and otherwise use a nonzero dual-lattice functional annihilating its vector part.

The functional L=ℓ∘π on E is integer-valued on J. Restricting to K gives a Q-linear functional K→Q, since J spans K over Q. By nondegeneracy of the trace pairing it equals z↦Tr_K/Q(bz) for some b∈K. Its real extension to E is Σ_real σ(b)zσ + 2 Re Σ_complex σ(b)zσ. Because at least one embedding coordinate was omitted, the corresponding coefficient must be zero. A field embedding is injective, so b=0. This forces L=0 and then ℓ=0, contradiction. (Trace nondegeneracy follows, for instance, from separability in characteristic zero.)

This is a classical Kronecker/Minkowski projection argument, not a new standalone theorem.

## 5. Necessity for every algebraic integer

Let λ be an algebraic integer which is not sPV. If λ∈(0,1), the distinct powers λ^n lie in Qλ and accumulate at 0, so Qλ is not discrete. The integers 0 and 1 are sPV, so exclude them. Let K=Q(λ) and m the monic minimal polynomial. It is separable and m(0)m(1)≠0.

Assume now λ∉(0,1). In the Minkowski coordinates select the physical coordinate λ (one real coordinate if λ is real, otherwise its complex pair), and every real conjugate μ∈(0,1). Since λ is not sPV, at least one other real or complex coordinate is omitted. The selected set is therefore proper.

Use the full-rank lattice J=λ(1−λ)Z[λ]. By §4 choose z∈J whose physical coordinate has 0<|z|<1/2 and whose selected internal coordinates all belong to (1/3,2/3). These are nonempty open conditions. Write z=a(λ) with a=x(1−x)q for q∈Z[x]. Then a(0)=a(1)=0, and all internal roots μ satisfy 0<a(μ)<1. The lifting lemma gives P congruent to a modulo m, with 0<P<1 on (0,1). By §3, z=P(λ)∈Qλ.

Qλ is closed under multiplication: for fixed u∈Qλ, evaluate the finite binary expression for v∈Qλ using leaves 0,u instead of 0,1; the result is uv and belongs to Qλ. Thus all z^n belong to Qλ. Their moduli strictly decrease to 0, so they are distinct and accumulate at 0∈Qλ. This disproves discreteness.

Combining this with the established integrality theorem proves the full necessity direction for all complex λ.

## 6. Sufficiency and conclusion

For sPV λ and distinct u=P(λ),v=R(λ) in Qλ, d=u−v is a nonzero algebraic integer. At every other conjugate μ∈(0,1), source binary closure gives P(μ),R(μ)∈[0,1], hence |σ(d)|≤1. Consequently 1≤|Norm(d)|≤|d|^e, where e=1 for real physical λ and e=2 for non-real λ. Thus |u−v|≥1. This includes the degree-one and imaginary-quadratic trivial sPV cases, and gives uniform discreteness. Therefore the complete published iff statement follows.

## Adversarial review priorities

The decisive bridge is §2, not a claim that every cut-and-project point belongs to Qλ. We restrict to the full-rank ideal λ(1−λ)Z[λ]; unrestricted endpoint congruences can obstruct lifting. In particular this proof does not assert equality with the source's entire closed-window model set. Section 4 must select whole complex pairs and omit at least one whole Minkowski coordinate. The resulting contraction is nonzero by its selected physical open annulus.

The strongest potential mathematical falsifier is a counterexample to §2 with a(0)=a(1)=0 and all real internal-root values strictly in (0,1), or an obstruction to §4 for a proper embedding subset. The strongest eligibility objection is that this exact combination of classical integer approximation and Minkowski projection may already be known, or may be judged routine subsumption despite resolving the full printed conjecture. No decision on that objection is assigned here.
