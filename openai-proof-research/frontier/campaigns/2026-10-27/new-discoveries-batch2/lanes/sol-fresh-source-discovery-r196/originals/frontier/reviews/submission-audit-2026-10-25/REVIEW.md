# Review of all 31 submitted papers against updates through October 25, 2026

This is an **update-impact and dependency review**, not an independent line-by-line reproof of 31 research papers. I retrieved every public Zenodo PDF and supporting ZIP, verified all 62 files against the public MD5 checksums, extracted the PDF text, and inspected the claims, source assumptions, relevant proof interfaces, references and formal-coverage disclosures. The inventory is the 31 public records returned for Myeongjin Kang, including all eleven most recent submissions. It does not assume that a Zenodo or MathDB upload is peer-review acceptance.

The earlier project instruction to accept the pinned OpenAI results as true was set aside for this review in response to the user's new request. Earlier internal acceptance means the deduction was reviewed under that assumption; it is not independent verification of the assumption.

## Main findings

1. **No direct dependency on the three withdrawn manuscripts was found.** OpenAI's October 7 history records withdrawals of its split-abelian-eightfold Weil-class paper, Kuga–Satake algebraicity paper, and rational Hodge paper for products of K3 surfaces after a sign error. The relevant notices leave those claims unproved. None appears in the deposited manuscript citations or the 43 source directories' README/bibliography files checked here. This is a direct/first-hop citation check, not an exhaustive transitive dependency proof. [OpenAI revision history](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/history.md).

2. **The mathematical source revision has not changed since the papers' pin.** Comparing `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb` with current HEAD `1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a` found only the added pull-request locking workflow. The October 7 repairs/withdrawals are already present at the October 8 pin. That establishes version alignment, not correctness. [Exact comparison](https://github.com/openai/math/compare/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb...1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a).

3. **Formal coverage is the main unresolved dependency issue.** The exact statement used matters more than the family label. There are concrete mismatches for four-state Potts, mapping-class irreducibility and no-bigeodesics; several other operative families have no linked formalization. Even a matching headline theorem does not verify internal Faber, pruning, nonsingularity, module-map or routing lemmas. OpenAI's own scope notes support these distinctions. [Potts scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/229.md), [character-variety scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/027.md), [first-passage scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/docs/212.md).

4. **Six papers present proofs that do not leave an OpenAI theorem as an unproved premise; two split into an independent main contribution and a conditional consequence; 23 retain operative source assumptions.** These are dependency categories, not correctness verdicts. In particular, a self-contained argument can still contain an error. The mixed cases are continuum-Coulomb membership versus hardness/completeness, and finite-size SK comparison versus the asymptotic limiting-law corollary.

5. **Three concrete publication updates are warranted.** R040 needs a comparison with a new primary paper; R033 and R041 contain stale review-status wording. Details below. No evidence found in this bounded review justifies blanket withdrawal of all 31 papers. It also does not justify advertising all 31 as independently established theorems.

## What the reported “rejection” may refer to

The three Hodge withdrawals are actual withdrawals. Separately, Bastounis, Circelli and Hansen identify mismatches between natural-language Navier–Stokes arguments and their Lean translations; they expressly do not claim to decide the correctness of the natural-language proof. None of these 31 manuscripts cites that Navier–Stokes proof as an operative premise. [Their paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08144v1).

Asaf Karagila argued that the Partition Principle manuscript deserved desk rejection for exposition and declined to review it. That is an expert's criticism, not evidence that a journal rejected your papers or the whole collection. None of these submissions uses that result. [Karagila, October 8](https://karagila.org/2026/openai-pp/).

MathVet's October 25 inventory distinguishes full headline coverage, partial coverage, weaker statements and supporting-only statements. It is a pre-referee review, not a list of false theorems. I used it to locate issues, then checked relevant OpenAI scope documents directly. Its sensitivity entry and OpenAI's documentation disagree about selected coverage; the actual Comparator challenge only states the quantitative ratio. That distinction must not be promoted into a claim that the fixed-exponent theorem is false. [MathVet status](https://github.com/mathvet/mathvet/blob/899d421729f1e4c5f08d302773762b141c52b845/reviews/openai-math/STATUS.md), [selected sensitivity statement](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/ComparatorChallenges/SensitivitySeparation.lean). Comparator challenge files deliberately contain placeholders; their presence is not an unproved-step finding about the separate solutions.

Carlos Toledo's independent audit likewise distinguishes unsupported scope from false mathematics; it reports no false claim among the finite objects it decided and no rejected proof among completed kernel runs. I did not replay that audit here and do not use it to certify your extensions. [Audit](https://carlostoledo.co/reports/openai-math.html).

OpenAI's October 15 update announces support for mathematical examination through a Simons Foundation initiative; it is not a new theorem repair or independent validation. [Release update](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/).

## Concrete revision items

**R040 — new primary literature, not a refutation.** David Jekel's *On counterexamples to the unification of free entropy*, submitted October 19, 2026, gives a detailed related construction and discusses earlier Stein-kernel and information-geometric work. Its Theorem 1.4 uses an `(m+1)`-tuple with sufficiently large `m`; it does not state an exactly-two-variable counterexample. R040's published bibliography cites Jekel–Pi (2024), but not this new work. Add a precise comparison and reassess the attribution of inherited Fisher-information/block-structure ideas. The claimed two-variable compression must be evaluated separately. I read the new abstract, main setup/theorems, proof-technique discussion and acknowledgments; I did not independently verify Jekel's full proof. [Jekel v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27243v1).

**R033 — deposited PDF p. 8.** Section 6 still says that a separate independent manuscript review is required, whereas the release records report completed internal review. Update the sentence against the actual receipt, distinguishing internal AI review from human peer review. The formal-support paragraph's phrase “human arguments” should become “prose arguments” or “arguments outside the formalization”; the available provenance does not establish human proof authorship. This is an accuracy/provenance issue, not evidence of a false theorem.

**R041 — deposited PDF p. 1.** The front-page review-status block still presents the proof for independent manuscript review and says the implementation confers no release. The frozen release record reports completion of its internal gates. Replace the stale lifecycle wording with a dated, accurate internal-review statement, preserving the limited formal coverage and absence of external peer review.

**Prioritized source audit queue:** R009 four-state posterior inequality; R039 original no-bigeodesics proof; R036 the determinant-one irreducibility input; R023 fitted-class Liouville; R025 kinetic/history estimates; R034 quantum-correspondence S2; R010 arithmetic extraction; R033's Gaussian asymptotic input. These are unresolved validation tasks, not eight detected counterexamples. R012 equality rigidity also deserves an exact statement check rather than reliance on an attainment theorem.

For any future publication revision, retain this principle: state the exact imported claim as an assumption until the proof or an appropriate independent theorem has been checked. Do not label a conditional result unconditional because a broad source family has a Lean link. Conversely, do not withdraw a self-contained calculation merely because an unrelated source paper was withdrawn.

## Evidence, reproducibility and limits

- `published-inventory.json` records the 31 public records and all 62 deposited-file URLs, SHA256 hashes and verified public MD5 checksums. `published/` preserves the exact PDFs/ZIPs and extracted text.
- `paper-assessments.json` and `paper-assessments.csv` contain the 31 curated dispositions. The automated `dependency-crosswalk.json` is a discovery aid: citations are not automatically logical dependencies, and source archives contain historical review materials. The curated assessment resolves those distinctions.
- `upstream-compare.json`, `upstream-history.md`, and `upstream-bibliography-receipts.json` preserve the revision evidence. The latter records 89 retrieved files: 86 README/bibliography files for 43 cited/related source directories, plus the three withdrawal notices. No matching withdrawal/Navier–Stokes/Partition-Principle citation was located in the checked operative-source bibliography material.
- `primary-formal-scope/` and `formal-scope-receipts.json` preserve direct, pinned scope documents and the selected sensitivity challenge. Missing scope files for families 011, 029, 063, 101, 217, 364 and 377 are recorded as HTTP 404; this supports “no linked scope located,” not a proof that no related formal work exists anywhere.
- `mathvet-fidelity.json` and `mathvet-status.md` are third-party snapshots used for triage. They are not treated as peer review or automatically inherited as verdicts.

This review did not rebuild all Lean developments, re-prove the analytic arguments, certify novelty across all literature, inspect a journal's private decision letters, or exhaustively close every transitive citation chain. It inspected the actual deposited Zenodo versions; it did not claim a fresh independent comparison against every MathDB version. No public record was modified or withdrawn, and no frozen manuscript package was changed.
