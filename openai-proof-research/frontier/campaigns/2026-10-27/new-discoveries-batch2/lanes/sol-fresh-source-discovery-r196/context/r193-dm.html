[1] arXiv:2610.35994 [pdf, other]
Title: Re-Ordering the Generalized Hofstadter Functions G, H, and Beyond
Pierre Letouzey (IRIF (UMR\_8243), PICUBE)
Subjects: Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Formal Languages and Automata Theory (cs.FL); Number Theory (math.NT)

Hofstadter's G function is recursively defined via G(0) = 0 and then G(n) = n-G(G(n -1)). Following Hofstadter, we vary the number k of nested recursive calls in this equation and obtain a family of functions (F\_k). In a previous article, we proved that this family is ordered pointwise: for all k and n, we have $F_k(n) $le$ F_{k+1}(n)$. Here we propose a much shorter proof of this theorem, without infinite morphic words. The key ingredient for this direct proof is a new formula expressing iterates of the preimages of $F_k$ . Thanks to this formula, we also prove several conjectures mentioned in our previous article. In particular we obtain that $F_k(n) < F_{k+1}(n)$ when n > (k + 1)(k + 6)/2. We provide a certified formalization of all these results in the Rocq proof assistant.

[2] arXiv:2610.36630 (cross-list from cs.DS) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Uniformity Testing for the Monomer-Dimer Model
Joy Qiping Yang, Yuichi Yoshida
Comments: To appear in SODA 2027
Subjects: Data Structures and Algorithms (cs.DS); Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM)

We study uniformity testing for the monomer-dimer model on a known graph $G=(V,E)$. Given samples from an unknown distribution $D_w(M)\propto \prod_{e\in M} w_e$ over matchings $M$ of $G$, we ask whether $D_w$ is the uniform distribution over all matchings of $G$ or is $\varepsilon$-far from it in total variation distance.
Our main result is that, for every maximum-degree bound $\Delta$, uniformity can be tested with $\widetilde O_\Delta(\sqrt m/\varepsilon^2)$ samples, where $m=|E|$. We complement this with an information-theoretic lower bound for general graphs: for every pair of parameters $n\le m\le n^2$, there are graphs with $\Theta(n)$ vertices and $\Theta(m)$ edges on which every tester requires $\Omega(\sqrt m/\varepsilon^2)$ samples. Thus, for bounded-degree graph families with $m=\Theta(n)$, testing uniformity over the space of matchings has $\widetilde \Theta(\sqrt n/\varepsilon^2)$ sample complexity, in contrast with the $\Theta(n/\varepsilon^2)$ samples needed to estimate $D_w$ in total variation distance.

[3] arXiv:2507.16648 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: An Unconditional Lower Bound for the Active-Set Method in Almost-Linear Programming
Eleon Bach, Yann Disser, Sophie Huiberts, Nils Mosis
Subjects: Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Computational Complexity (cs.CC); Data Structures and Algorithms (cs.DS); Combinatorics (math.CO)

We prove that, for every fixed $\epsilon\in(0,1]$, the active-set method requires exponentially many iterations in the worst case to maximize a convex objective of the form $(x_1)^{1+\epsilon}+\vec c^{\top}\vec x$ subject to linear constraints, regardless of the pivot rule used. Our lower bound therefore holds for objectives arbitrarily close to the linear case $\epsilon=0$, where the active-set method coincides with the simplex method and a lower bound for all pivot rules would constitute a major breakthrough. The special case $\epsilon=1$ yields a lower bound in convex quadratic maximization. This improves on the best previously known lower bound [IPCO 2025] requiring polynomial objectives of degree $\omega(\log d)$ in dimension $d$ and firmly resolves the open question of whether a constant degree suffices.
Our result is based on a novel extended formulation, recursively constructed using deformed products. Its key feature is that it projects onto a polygonal approximation of a parabola while preserving all of its exponentially many vertices. We define a convex objective with a single non-linear variable that forces the active-set method to follow the parabolic boundary of this projection, without allowing any shortcuts along chords corresponding to edges of its full-dimensional preimage.

[4] arXiv:2609.30302 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Hull Games of Induced Path Convexities in Graphs
Eurinardo Costa, Leon Almeida, Rudini Sampaio
Subjects: Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Combinatorics (math.CO)

In 1984, Frank Harary introduced the first convexity games in graphs, all of them based on the geodesic convexity, which is the graph convexity related to shortest paths. In 2024, Araújo et al. obtained the first PSPACE-hardness proofs on some of these geodesic games and generalized them to any graph convexity. In this paper, we investigate convexity games on several known induced path convexities: the monophonic $\mathrm{m}$-convexity and the $\ell_k$-convexities, based on induced paths and on induced paths of size at most $k$. We prove that the hull games $\mathrm{CHG}_{\mathrm{m}}$, $\mathrm{CHG}_{\ell_k}$ and their misère variants are PSPACE-complete for every $k\ge2$ even in graphs with diameter at most 3. We also use the Sprague-Grundy Theory to obtain a polynomial time algorithm to decide the winner of the games $\mathrm{CHG}_{\mathrm{m}}$ and $\mathrm{CHG}_{\ell_k}$ for any $k\ge2$ in disjoint unions of paths and cycles. For $k\ge3$ odd, we prove that Alice (1st player) wins $\mathrm{CHG}_{\ell_k}$ in the path $P_n$ if and only if $n$ is odd and she wins in the cycle $C_n$ if and only if $n=3$ or $n=\alpha\cdot (k+1)-1$ with $\alpha\ge2$. For $k\ge2$ even, the only periodic nimber sequences of $\mathrm{CHG}_{\ell_k}$ obtained through extensive computational testing occurred for $k=2^h-4$ with $h\ge3$, e.g, $k\in\{4,12,28,60,\ldots\}$. In this case ($k=2^h-4$ with $h\ge3$), we prove that the nimber sequences of $\mathrm{CHG}_{\ell_k}$ in $P_n$ and in $C_n$ are periodic and Alice loses (resp. wins) in $P_n$ (resp. $C_n$) with $n>k$ only when $n=3k+4$ (resp. $n\in\{2k+1,5k+3\}$). Finally, we show that, for $k=2$, the game $\mathrm{CHG}_{\ell_2}$ in paths $P_n$ is closely related to the classical game \emph{Couples-are-Forever} of J. H. Conway: it is still an open problem if the nimber sequence is periodic or not and Alice loses only for 12 values of $n$ up to $150$ million.

[5] arXiv:2610.34378 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Logarithmic asymptotics for polynomial threshold functions
Martin Anthony
Comments: 39 pages. Revised exposition and organisation, with expanded explanations. Main results and theorem statements unchanged
Subjects: Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Information Theory (cs.IT); Combinatorics (math.CO)

We count Boolean functions represented as the sign of a real polynomial of bounded degree on the $n$-dimensional cube. We prove that the logarithm of their number is asymptotic to that of the classical hyperplane-arrangement upper bound, uniformly over all degrees, with relative error $O((\log n)^2/n)$. We also obtain sharper additive and relative error estimates in particular degree ranges, including an additive error $O(n^2)$ between the logarithms throughout a small linear degree range and improved bounds just below the central degree. The proof combines probabilistic estimates for Reed--Muller codes with a deletion argument for sets of evaluation vectors, an entropy count, and a sharp-threshold estimate for expected rank deficiency.

[6] arXiv:1909.12353 (replaced) [pdf, other]
Title: Interactive Particle Systems on Hypergraphs, Drift Analysis and the WalkSAT algorithm
Gabriel Istrate, Cosmin Bonchis, Mircea Marin
Comments: Journal of Complexity (to appear)
Subjects: Data Structures and Algorithms (cs.DS); Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Combinatorics (math.CO); Probability (math.PR)

We analyze the expected running time of WalkSAT, a well-known local search procedure for satisfiability solving, on satisfiable instances of the k-XOR SAT problem. We obtain estimates of this expected running time by reducing the problem to a setting amenable to classical techniques from drift analysis.
A crucial ingredient of this reduction is the definition of (new, explosive) hypergraph versions of interacting particle systems, notably of coalescing and annihilating random walks as well as the voter model. The use of these tools allows to show that the expected running time of WalkSAT depends on structural parameter (we call odd Cheeger drift) of the dual of the formula hypergraph.

[7] arXiv:2210.11132 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: A Search-Based Algorithm for Multistage Robust Discrete Linear Optimization and its Implementation
Michael Hartisch, Ulf Lorenz
Subjects: Optimization and Control (math.OC); Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Data Structures and Algorithms (cs.DS)

Robust optimization models that allow decisions to adapt to newly revealed information remain computationally challenging and are often limited to two decision stages or are addressed heuristically or via approximation schemes. This challenge is further amplified when uncertainty sets depend on earlier decisions. Conventional approaches to such problems often require problem-specific reformulations and tailored algorithms, highlighting the need for an exact model-and-run approach. We address this gap by presenting an algorithmic framework for solving multistage robust discrete linear optimization problems. This exact approach adapts and integrates ideas from game-tree search, quantified conflict-driven clause learning, and integer linear programming to explore the minimax tree and is implemented in the open-source solver Yasol. The solver supports discrete decision variables across multiple stages, discrete uncertainty sets with polyhedral or decision-dependent structure, and mixed-integer recourse in the final stage. It can be used as a standalone executable or through a C++ library interface, allowing it to serve as a reference solver for exact methods in multistage robust discrete linear optimization. In an extensive computational study, we demonstrate the capabilities of our solver. The results show competitive performance against a reference approach from the literature for a protection-interdiction problem, as well as strong performance on bilevel benchmark instances. For a multistage cutting-stock problem, we demonstrate the substantial loss in solution quality that can arise from neglecting the multistage structure and show that our solver significantly outperforms decision-rule approximations in terms of computational efficiency while certifying the true multistage optimum.

[8] arXiv:2411.07030 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Polynomial and Hyperplane Avoidance over the Integers with Applications to Integer-Point Counting
Grigorii Dakhno, Dmitry Gribanov, Nikita Kasianov, Anastasiia Kats, Andrey Kupavskii, Nikita Kuz'min, Stanislav Moiseev
Comments: This version is an improved version of the previous variant of the work, in which the case of a system consisting of polynomials is also considered. The overview part of the work has also been substantially expanded
Subjects: Computational Complexity (cs.CC); Computational Geometry (cs.CG); Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM); Data Structures and Algorithms (cs.DS); Combinatorics (math.CO)

We study the \emph{Polynomial Avoidance Problem}: given a finite collection $F \subseteq Z[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ of nonzero polynomials, find a vector $x \in Z^n$ of minimum $\ell_p$-norm such that $f(x)\neq0$ for every $f \in F$. Each polynomial is given sparsely as a list of its nonzero terms. The affine special case is the \emph{Hyperplane Avoidance Problem}. This special case arises in Barvinok-type algorithms for counting integer points in polyhedra, when certain rational generating functions must be evaluated at a removable singularity. Our main results are as follows:
1) Let $D:=\sum_{f\in F} \text{deg} f$. Every instance admits a feasible vector $x$ satisfying
\[
\|x\|_1 \leq
\min\left\{D,\frac{D+n}{2}\right\}.
\]
This bound is tight. Such a vector can be found by a deterministic algorithm whose bit complexity is polynomial in $D$ and the sparse encoding length of the input. Our proof uses a new algorithmic variant of the \emph{Combinatorial Nullstellensatz};
2) In the affine case, with $m$ input polynomials, such a vector can be constructed by a fast deterministic polynomial-time algorithm using $O(nm)$ arithmetic operations. For the homogeneous case, Barvinok's original approach provides only the weaker guarantee $\|x\|_1 = O((nm)^{n-1})$;
3) The problem is NP-hard for every $p\in R_{\geq 1}\cup\{\infty\}$, even for homogeneous linear forms;
4) As an application, we improve the complexity bound for counting integer points in general rational polytopes. We also improve the bound for polyhedra of bounded codimension; this includes the counting version of the \emph{Unbounded Subset-Sum} problem as a special case.

[9] arXiv:2506.12774 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: On the Vertices of Delta-modular Polyhedra
Bludov Mikhail, Gribanov Dmitry, Klimenko Maxim, Kupavskii Andrey, Lángi Zsolt, Rogozin Alexander, Voronov Vsevolod
Comments: This version of the work has been substantially extended in the following way: it has been proved that the bound on the diameter is tight with respect to the parameter. A section has also been added that provides an in-depth comparison of existing parameters for polyhedra
Subjects: Combinatorics (math.CO); Computational Geometry (cs.CG); Discrete Mathematics (cs.DM)

Let $P$ be a polytope defined by the system $A x \leq b$, where $A \in R^{m \times n}$, $b \in R^m$, and $\text{rank}(A) = n$. We give a short geometric proof of the following tight upper bound on the number of vertices of $P$: $$
n! \cdot \frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{\text{average}}} \cdot \text{vol}(B_2) \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi n}} \cdot \left(\frac{2 \pi}{e}\right)^{n/2} \cdot n^{n/2} \cdot \frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{\text{average}}}, $$ where $\Delta$ is the maximum absolute value of $n \times n$ subdeterminants of $A$, and $\Delta_{\text{average}}$ is the average absolute value of subdeterminants of $A$ corresponding to a triangulation of $P$'s normal fan. Assuming that $A$ is integer, such polyhedra are called $\Delta$-modular polyhedra. Note that in the integer case, the bound can be simplified via the inequality $\Delta_{\text{average}} \geq \Delta_{\min} \geq 1$, where $\Delta_{\min}$ is the minimum absolute value of subdeterminants of $A$ corresponding to feasible bases of $A x \leq b$. For this, we prove and use a symmetric variant of Macbeath's theorem.
Additionally, we give a direct argument based on prior results in the field, showing that the graph diameter of $P$ is bounded by $O\bigl(n^3 \cdot \frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{\min}} \cdot \ln (n \frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{\min}}) \bigr)$. Thus, both characteristic of $P$ are linear in $\Delta/\Delta_{\min}$.
From an algorithmic perspective, we demonstrate that:
Given $A \in Q^{m \times n}$, $b \in Q^m$, and an initial feasible solution to $A x \leq b$, the convex hull of $P$ can be constructed in $O(n)^{n/2} \cdot m^2 \cdot \frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{\text{average}}}$ operations. For simple polyhedra, the dependence on $m$ reduces to linear;
Given $A \in Z^{m \times n}$ and $b \in Q^m$, the number $|P \cap Z^n|$ can be computed in $O(n)^n \cdot \frac{\Delta^4}{\Delta_{\text{average}}}$ arithmetic operations.
