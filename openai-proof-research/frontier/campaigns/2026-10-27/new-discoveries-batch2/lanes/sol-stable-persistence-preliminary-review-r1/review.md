## MATH: independent analytic stress test

1. **Time change and constants.** Conditional on Y_0=x, Y_s=e^(-s/alpha)(x+L_(e^s-1)) has transition innovation scale (1-e^-h)^(1/alpha), exactly source Lemma5.1. Signs are preserved by the deterministic factor. At block time T, t=e^T-1 and c=e^(-T/alpha). The integral for Q_Tw uses 1+c^p y^p, not 1+y^p. This scaling is essential.

Choose beta=alpha/2<p<alpha. The elementary inequality |u+v|^p<=C_p(|u|^p+|v|^p) holds also for p>1. On x<=t^(1/alpha), c^p x^p<=1 and c^p t^(p/alpha)<=1, so H gives Q_Tw<=C x^beta t^-1/2. Since sup x^beta/(1+x^p) is finite, division by w gives Ce^-T/2 for T>=1. On x>=t^(1/alpha), use the unrestricted kernel: 1/(1+x^p)<=t^-p/alpha and t^(p/alpha)/(1+x^p)<=1. Every term is bounded by Ce^-pT/alpha<=Ce^-T/2. All comparison constants here depend on alpha,p, not T>=1. This independently confirms the indispensable uniform-in-block-length estimate.

2. **Continuous killing versus dense grids.** A strictly negative value at a time before T has a right interval of negative values by right continuity. Every such interval contains a sufficiently fine dyadic sample. For exit by hitting zero before T, use strong Markov at exit and regularity of zero for the open negative half-line. This is regularity for entering negatives immediately, not merely the possibility of hitting the point zero. It holds for symmetric stable processes throughout 0<alpha<2: for example the standard Levy regularity criterion has integral int_0^1 P(L_s<0) ds/s=infinity because P(L_s<0)=1/2. It does not require points to be nonpolar. Thus alpha<=1 causes no exception. Strictly negative endpoint values are detected by the endpoint sample, and the remaining zero endpoint event has probability zero by its density. At x=0 the same negative-half-line regularity forces grid-intersection probability zero. Usual Hunt-process exit/strong-Markov facts cover the first exit; a continuous-path assumption is neither available nor needed. All assertions are for each fixed initial x; no common probability-one set over uncountably many x is needed.

3. **Unbounded payoff and Dini.** For fixed m and x in [0,R], sampled coordinates have continuous stable marginals and hence no atom at zero. Coupling by the same L makes the indicators converge almost surely when x varies to a fixed x; the payoff is dominated by C_R(1+|L_t|^p), integrable since p<alpha. This proves K_mw continuity including x=0. For Q_Tw in the interior, on a bounded y interval use joint kernel continuity and the free density's boundedness; on the unbounded tail use k_t<=p_t and (1+y^p)y^(-1-alpha), uniformly for x in a compact set. At zero H instead gives Q_Tw(x)<=C_T x^beta. Thus Q_Tw extends continuously to zero, while K_mw(0) need not vanish at finite m. Nested events and the nonnegative payoff give d_m down to zero. These are precisely the continuity and monotonicity hypotheses required by Dini on [0,R]. The nonzero finite-grid boundary value is harmless and was not suppressed.

4. **Uniform tail and operator space.** Failure of continuous positivity implies M_t>=x, including a touch. On the excess event the positive endpoint payoff is at most C_T(1+x^p+M_t^p). Dividing its expectation by 1+x^p yields the stated tail estimate, with a larger harmless C_T. Levy's maximal inequality on finite grids, then cadlag density of grids, gives E M_t^p<=C E|L_t|^p<infinity for every p<alpha, including p>1. Therefore the normalized tail vanishes uniformly in m. Constants may depend on fixed T at this stage.

Together compact Dini and the tail estimate imply sup_x d_m(x)/w(x)->0. On the Banach space B_w of Borel functions with finite weighted supremum, the kernels are bounded, positive, and measurable; the difference kernel is positive because continuous survival is contained in sampled survival. For any |f|<=w, |(K_m-Q_T)f|<=(K_m-Q_T)w, and equality of norms is attained by f=w. Thus no unproved total-variation or spectral-radius identification is hidden in the norm claim. Measurability follows from the finite-grid kernels and standard killed transition kernel, extended to integrable weighted functions.

5. **Long time and limit order.** Each b_m is finite and strictly positive, since a finite positive-grid event has positive probability for x>0. K_m^k is exactly k blocks at the SAME mesh. For fixed m,T,x, its bound by w(x)b_m^k gives the rate inequality along n=k2^m. Source existence of the full n-rate identifies that subsequence rate; log w(x)/(kT) vanishes. No uniformity in x or mesh is required for that limit. Then norm convergence gives limsup b_m<=Ce^-T/2. Applying -log via an epsilon upper bound yields liminf lambda(h_m)/h_m>=1/2-log(C)/T; no equality of spectral radii, interchange of k and m, or positivity of the right side for every T is needed. Next use the source full h-limit, then T->infinity. Finally multiply by alpha. Scale and starting-point independence are used separately at each mesh. This establishes the proposed literal symmetric endpoint as an ordinary analytic deduction.

These are independent inequalities and event/continuity checks, not a finite numerical validation.

## SUBSTANTIVE and NOVELTY: strongest objection

The strongest objection is that this is a classical weighted approximation argument once w=1+x^p is chosen. A complete prior norm-convergence theorem for densely monitored killed kernels under this polynomial moment and boundary regularity, coupled with the old all-time half-space bound, might make the entire resolution a routine application. This objection is material even if nobody previously printed Conjecture6.1's exact formula.

I inspected the actual closest cited general bodies rather than dismissing the objection by their titles:

- AMZ1703.06447v4 Definition1.1, Theorem1.2, Lemma1.6 and its proof formulate spectral persistence via bounded measurable/continuous functions, compactness, and an operator-norm continuity hypothesis. Conjugating our B_w by f->f/w puts the problem in a bounded-function space, but it does NOT prove that hypothesis. The weighted Dini-plus-tail estimate remains to be supplied. Their AR Theorem2.6 additionally requires exponential innovation-density decay, which stable innovations lack, and treats interior contractive coefficients rather than identifying the normalized boundary limit. Thus this body supplies classical architecture, not a complete bridge under the present hypotheses. Our proof does not need Lemma1.6's general spectral-radius claim at all, only finite powers and their norm bound; no assessment of that lemma's maximum generality is required here.
- FFM2112.04820v3 Theorem5(II), Remark6 and Section7.2/Proposition7.1 prove dense-sampling continuity for Gaussian stationary processes with spectral conditions. Its proof smooths spectral measures and compares Gaussian processes. Stable non-Gaussian OU paths for alpha<2 do not meet that starting hypothesis; covariance/spectral-density conditions cannot simply be imported. This is a complete Gaussian bridge, not the required full-alpha theorem.
- HKW1801.04485v2 provides the initial-condition rate equivalence used above and discusses adapted Banach spaces/quasicompactness. Its Section6 specifically separates regularly varying innovations from its stronger precise-asymptotic machinery. The inspected theorem bodies do not prove the dense-killing weighted norm limit.

The distinctive part remaining after these comparisons is the finite-time WEIGHTED UNIFORM kernel difference, including starting points arbitrarily close to zero and arbitrarily large. Pointwise finite-time convergence alone is insufficient: without the weight, sup_x Q_T1(x)=1 for every T, and neither that norm nor the finite-time limit yields a positive persistence exponent by this method.
