                                                                                 1. Introduction
                                              An integer partition λ of n is a nonincreasing sequence λ = (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λk ) of
                                           positive integers that add up to n. We write λ ⊢ n to mean that λ is a partition
                                           of n. The numbers λi in the sequence are called the parts of λ. We identify a
                                           partition with its multiset of parts. We denote by P (n) the number of partitions
                                           of n and set P (0) := 1. We occasionally use the frequency notation of partitions.
                                           Exponents of parts represent their multiplicity. For example, (32 , 24 , 12 ) is the
                                           partition (3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1). For more on the theory of partitions, we refer the
                                           reader to [1].
                                              Given a partition λ = (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λk ), we define the polynomial

                                 6. Open Questions
   Analogously to srB (n, x) for the binary partitions of n, we can define srA (n, x)
for any subset A(n) of partitions of n and study the questions posed in this article.
We offer the following conjectures.
   Let O(n) be the set of odd partitions of n, that is, partitions whose parts are all
odd.
Conjecture 8. For n ≥ 1, numO (n, −1) = o(n!).
  Let T (n) be the set of ternary partitions of n, that is, partitions whose parts are
powers of 3.
Conjecture 9. Let s(n) = numT (n, −1). Then s(1) = s(2) = 1 and s(3n) =
s(3n + 1) = s(3n + 2) = 3val3 ((3n)!) .
Conjecture 10. Let t(n) = numT (n, 1). Then t(1) = t(2) = 1 and t(3n) =
t(3n + 1) = t(3n + 2). Also, t(n) = ∆(t(3n − 2)/22n ).
  Finally, we note that one can replace xi in sp(λ, x) and sr(n, x) by xi or xi ,
where i and i, are the rising and falling factorials, respectively. Conjecturally, these
polynomials have interesting properties, and we hope the interested reader will
explore them.
