## Independent mathematical audit and complete specialized bridge

This is also the full bridge required to assess the strongest routine-adaptation objection. It does not assume a transport theorem or a large-deviation principle as a black box.

1. Put a=2/3, θ=2π/3. The proposed odds r(u)=[sin(θu)/sin(θ(1-u))]^(1/a) increase from 0 to infinity: the derivative of the sine ratio is θ sinθ/sin²(θ(1-u))>0. Inverting the CDF below gives exactly these odds. The probability p=1/(1+r) extends continuously to p(0)=1,p(1)=0. For midpoint u_j=(j−1/2)/(2n), all 2n parameters are strictly between 0 and 1. Pairing u and 1−u proves p(1−u)=1−p(u), so E Y_n=n exactly. The product of 2n linear Bernoulli PGFs has real negative roots and full support {0,...,2n}.

2. The positive measure density is k(r)=(sinθ/π)r^(a−1)/(1+2r^a cosθ+r^(2a)). Substituting v=r^a integrates it to [atan((r^a+cosθ)/sinθ)−(π/2−θ)]/θ; its limits are 0 and 1, including the principal-arctan choice when θ>π/2. Its inverse simplifies to the sine ratio in step1. For the Stieltjes identity use h(w)=w^(a−1)/(1+w^a) on the principal slit plane. There are no poles there, because |arg(w^a)|<aπ<π. On the two banks of the negative axis Im h(−r+i0)=−πk(r); the lower value is its conjugate. Cauchy's formula for h(w)/(w−z), z>0, on the slit annulus gives h(z)=∫k(r)/(r+z)dr. Outer and inner circular errors are O(R^−1) and O(ε^a), respectively; for sufficiently large/small radius the denominator is uniformly bounded away from zero in the requisite scale. The density is O(r^(a−1)) at zero and O(r^(−a−1)) at infinity, so passage to the banks is integrable. Multiplication by z yields ∫z/(z+r)dμ=z^a/(1+z^a). Sign, normalization and fractional exponent all check.

3. Let F_n(t)=n^−1 log E exp(tY_n). For fixed t, the midpoint summand g_t(u)=log(1−p(u)+p(u)e^t) is continuous on the closed unit interval, even though the odds themselves diverge. Thus F_n(t)→2∫g_t du. Its derivative is the tilted success probability, bounded between zero and one; its second derivative is q_t(1−q_t)≤1/4. These are continuous in u and uniformly bounded for t in any compact interval. Midpoint convergence holds for these derivatives as well; no n-dependent infinite tilt is invoked. The Stieltjes identity gives F'(t)=2e^(at)/(1+e^(at)). Since F(0)=0, F(t)=3log((1+e^(2t/3))/2), precisely the normalized log MGF of (2/3)Bin(3n,1/2). In particular F''(t)>0 and F'(R)=(0,2).

4. Set I(x)=3[q log(2q)+(1−q)log(2(1−q))], q=x/2, on (0,2). The optimizer is t(x)=(3/2)log(x/(2−x)); I decreases strictly below 1 and increases strictly above 1. Chernoff gives limsup n^−1 log P(Z_n≤x)≤−I(x) for x<1 and limsup n^−1 log P(Z_n>x)≤−I(x) for x>1, with Z_n=Y_n/n or (2/3)X_n/n. For the matching lower bound choose y<x below the mean and δ<x−y. Tilt by t=t(y), so dP_t/dP=exp(tnZ_n−nF_n(t)). The tilted mean tends to y and the variance of Z_n is at most 1/(2n) for Y and 1/(3n) for the scaled binomial. The interval |Z_n−y|<δ has tilted probability tending to one. Undoing the tilt bounds its original probability below by exp(n[F_n(t)−ty−|t|δ]) times that tilted probability. Let δ↓0 then y↑x. For the strict upper tail take y>x and δ<y−x, then y↓x. This proves the exact logarithmic rates for the non-strict left and strict right tails. Lattice rounding causes no difficulty because the interior interval has positive tilted probability eventually. This is a triangular-array argument, not an unjustified iid Cramér application.

5. Write A_n=(2/3)X_n/n, B_n=Y_n/n. Suppose quantiles have a persistent gap, and take a subsequence with Q_A(u_n)→x<y←Q_B(u_n). Pick x<c<d<y on one side of 1. If c<d<1, the left-tail rates and I(c)>I(d) give P(A_n≤c)<P(B_n≤d) eventually. If 1<c<d, the strict upper-tail rates and I(c)<I(d) give P(A_n>c)>P(B_n>d), hence the same CDF ordering. Every positive gap, including one crossing the mean, contains such a pair. For large n, Q_A(u_n)<c and Q_B(u_n)>d, so the left-quantile definition gives u_n≤P(A_n≤c) and P(B_n≤d)<u_n, a contradiction. Reverse the variables for the opposite gap. Compact support [0,2] supplies the initial subsequences and therefore uniform convergence of quantiles. A common uniform random variable couples them with distance at most the quantile supremum. Rescale by n to obtain W∞=o(n).

Step5 makes no lower bound assumption on u_n or 1−u_n. Superexponential probabilities, the endpoint atoms, CDF jumps, and the gap across the mean are explicitly covered. Both supports have the same endpoints. Interior fixed cut points, not tail approximations at moving endpoints, drive the contradiction. Thus the divergent endpoint odds create no hidden obstruction to this o(n) assertion. Near zero r(u) is a positive constant times u^(3/2), and near one its reciprocal behaves similarly. Endpoint masses may differ at subexponential order; the proof neither requires their exact equality nor implies O(1). In general unequal support endpoints could break this compactness argument; they are equal here.
