Ranjan and Singh proved that for every composite $n$ which is not twice an odd prime, $\varphi(n)$ is at least one plus the sum of $\varphi$ over the prime divisors of $n$, with equality exactly for $n=4,12,30$. \new{We study the weighted equation $\varphi(n)=c+α\sum_{p\mid n}\varphi(p)$. We determine the parameters $(c,α)$ admitting infinite families of solutions, prove that all remaining solutions are finite in number and arise from an explicit linear mechanism, and compute them exactly in a range of parameters; the finiteness of $\{4,12,30\}$ becomes a special case. We also settle the analogous equations for the divisor functions $τ$ and $σ$ and for the Jordan
totients
, and, from known results on the sum of the prime divisors, derive the full asymptotic expansion of the average
deficiency
.} \K{In the range $|c|\le10$, $1\leα\le6$ the composite solution set is determined completely\V{, and the number and the size of the sporadic solutions are shown to be bounded by polynomials in $|c|+α$}.} \B{Finally, replacing the prime values $\varphi(p)$ by their iterates $\varphi^{(k)}(p)$, we determine all solutions of $\varphi(n)=1+\sum_{p\mid n}\varphi^{(k)}(p)$: the solution sets are $\{1,4,12,30\}$, $\{1,3,4,10\}$ and, for every $k\ge3$, $\{1,3,4\}$; for the weighted iterated equation we exhibit an infinite family governed by the safe primes.

We begin by introducing an interesting class of functions, known as the Schemmel
totient
functions, that generalizes the Euler
totient
function. For each Schemmel
totient
function $L_m$, we define two new functions, denoted $R_m$ and $H_m$, that arise from iterating $L_m$. Roughly speaking, $R_m$ counts the number of iterations of $L_m$ needed to reach either $0$ or $1$, and $H_m$ takes the value (either $0$ or $1$) that the iteration trajectory eventually reaches. Our first major result is a proof that, for any positive integer $m$, the function $H_m$ is completely multiplicative. We then introduce an iterate summatory function, denoted $D_m$, and define the terms $D_m$-
deficient
, $D_m$-perfect, and $D_m$-abundant. We proceed to prove several results related to these definitions, culminating in a proof that, for all positive even integers $m$, there are infinitely many $D_m$-abundant numbers. Many open problems arise from the introduction of these functions and terms, and we mention a few of them, as well as some numerical results.
