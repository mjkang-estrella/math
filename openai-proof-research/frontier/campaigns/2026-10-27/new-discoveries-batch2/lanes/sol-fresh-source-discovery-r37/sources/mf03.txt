                                                 Abstract
                                                                                            √
            Let rm be the diagonal [m/m] Padé approximant at the origin to cosh z. We
        prove that rm exists, has no poles in |z| ≤ 3, and satisfies |rm (z) − 1| ≤ 2 there, for every
        m ≥ 0. The bound is strict for m ≥ 2 and is attained for m = 1 at z = 3. The large-order
        argument follows from a Schur-function formula for the denominator: its jth coeﬀicient
        in absolute value is bounded by the jth power of the tail sum of the reciprocals of the
        zeros of the entire function. Only the orders 1 ≤ m ≤ 15 remain; they are covered by a
        previously published lemma and, independently, by supplied exact-rational certificates.
        This settles Conjecture 4.6 of Nadukandi and Higham for the stated scalar disk.

Corollary 4. For any complex square matrix A with spectral radius ρ(A) ≤ 3, the denomi-
nator qm (A) is invertible and
                                 ρ(I − rm (A)) ≤ 2.
The inequality is strict when m ≥ 2.
Proof. Apply Theorem 1 to every eigenvalue of A and use the spectral mapping theorem for
a rational function without poles on the spectrum.
Remark 5. The universal constant 2 cannot be reduced, because m = 1, A = 3I attains it.
Accordingly, the non-strict inequality in Corollary 4 is necessary when order one is included.
The strict matrix inequality printed as (4.9) in [1] needs this boundary qualification. No
operator-norm bound for arbitrary nonnormal matrices is asserted here.

pm , qm for 1 ≤ m ≤ 15. The standard-library Python verifier wave_kernel_certificate.py
checks the normalization and all coeﬀicients of (2) through degree 2m. It then forms, in exact
