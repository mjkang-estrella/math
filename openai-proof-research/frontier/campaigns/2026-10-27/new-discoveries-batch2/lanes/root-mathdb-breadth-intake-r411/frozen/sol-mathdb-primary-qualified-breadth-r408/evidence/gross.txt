    What is the true exceptional set in Gross’
                   Theorem?
                              A. Eremenko
                              April 4, 2015


   Let f be a meromorphic function in the complex plane. Suppose that z is
not a critical point, w = f (z), and consider the germ φz of the inverse, such
that φz (w) = z. Gross’ Theorem [1] says that φz has an analytic continuation
along almost every ray {w + reiθ : 0 ≤ r < ∞} (with respect to the Lebesgue
measure on the unit circle).
   Can the exceptional set be improved in this theorem?
   The only known example, where the exceptional set has the power of
continuum, is given by Theorem 17 on p. 71 of [2]. In this example the
exceptional set has zero capacity, and it is in fact much smaller than that.

[1] R. Nevanlinna, Analytic Functions.
[2] L.I. Volkovyskii, Research on the type problem of a simply connected Rie-
mann surface, Proc. Steklov Inst. Math., XXXIV, Moscow 1950. (Russian).
