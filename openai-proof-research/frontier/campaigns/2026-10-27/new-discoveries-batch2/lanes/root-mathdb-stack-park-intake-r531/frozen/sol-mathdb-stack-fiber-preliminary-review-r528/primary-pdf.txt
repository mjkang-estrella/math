                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                             Let Sn be the symmetric group. Consider West [16]’s stack-sorting map s : Sn → Sn . A
                                           stack, which is required to be increasing, processes the input permutation τ ∈ Sn in a right-
                                           greedy manner. In other words, the map s uses a stack that avoids the pattern 21. In 2019,
                                           Cerbai et al. [5] generalized West’s stack-sorting map. The map sσ : Sn → Sn processes
                                           the input permutation through a stack in a similar manner as s, but with a stack that must
                                           avoid some pattern σ, as in Figure 1. Since then, the map sσ and variants have been studied
                                           extensively [3, 5, 6, 11]. Most relevant to our work is Defant and Zheng [11] that introduced
                                           the consecutive-pattern-avoiding stack-sorting map SCσ : Sn → Sn , a modification of sσ
                                           where the stack must consecutively avoid σ, as in Figure 2.
                                             Babson and Steingrı́msson [1] first introduced vincular pattern avoidance. When consider-
                                           ing whether a permutation π contains a vincular pattern σ, some elements may be required
                                           to be adjacent in π, as indicated by underlined terms in σ. For instance, the pattern 1423
                                           contains 123 and 123, but avoids 123 and 123. Note that classical and consecutive pattern
                                           avoidance are both special cases of vincular pattern avoidance.
                                             Therefore, a natural extension of Cerbai et al. [5] and Defant and Zheng [11]’s maps is the
                                           generalized map SCσ : Sn → Sn that sorts with a stack which avoids a vincular pattern σ.
                                           Consider the permutation τ = 514362. Figures 1 to 4 demonstrate the process of sending
                                           τ through the classically-123-avoiding stack s123 = SC123 , the consecutively-123-avoiding

                                           SC123 (τ ) = 463215, SC123 (τ ) = 263415, SC123 (τ ) = 426315, and SC123 (τ ) = 632415. In
                                           particular, the four maps produce different images after acting on τ .

                                        2. Preliminaries
2.1. Definitions and Notation. Consider π = π1 π2 · · · πn . We define the reverse of π as
rev(π) = πn πn−1 · · · π1 and the complement of π as comp(π) = (n+1−π1)(n+1−π2) · · · (n+
1 − πn ). For instance, rev(1342) = 2431 and comp(1342) = 4213. These definitions extend
easily to patterns σ. An element τi is a left-to-right minimum of τ if, for all j < i, we have
τi < τj . We often split a permutation τ into the concatenation of two or more subsequences.
For example, we may let τ = τ a τ b , where τ a = τ1 · · · τi and τ b = τi+1 · · · τn . We consider a
and b as permutations in their own right, and they can be processed by stack-sorting maps.
  Two sequences τ and π of size n are order-isomorphic if, for each pair of indices i and j,
we have τi < τj if and only if πi < πj . We write τ ≃ π if τ and π are order-isomorphic.
For a permutation π and pattern σ, we say π avoids σ if there exists no subsequence of π
order-isomorphic to σ. Else, we say π contains σ. As a generalization, we allow σ to be a
vincular pattern, where terms in the subsequence of π corresponding to underlined terms in

3.1. The Sorting Classes of SC123 , SC321 , and SC321 . In this section, we study sorting
classes that can be described by pattern avoidance. The characterizations and sizes of

Lemma 3.2. Consider τ ∈ Sn that is being processed by SC123 , and suppose τi is an element
currently in the stack. Subsequently, after each time an element enters the stack, the stack
will contain some element of value at most τi .
Proof. Let τp be the next element to be added to the stack. If no elements are popped
immediately before τp is added, then τi remains in the stack. Else, suppose τq is the element
popped immediately before τp is added to the stack so that τp < τq . Then, τq ≤ τi , for
otherwise the stack would already contain 123. Thus, τp < τi , and the conclusion follows. 

  With Lemma 3.2, we are ready to directly describe Sortn (SC123 ).

Theorem 3.3. The sorting class of SC123 is characterized by
                               Sortn (SC123 ) = Avn (132, 3214, 4213).
Proof. Let π = SC123 (τ ). We first show that if τ does not avoid 132, 3214, and 4213, then π
contains 231. If τ contains τi τj τk ≃ 132, Lemma 3.2 implies that when τj enters the stack,
there is some term τi′ ≤ τi below it. We split into three possibilities:
     • If τj has not popped when τk enters (and neither has τi′ ), then the stack, and thus π
        will contain τk τj τi′ ≃ 231.
     • Suppose τj , but not τi′ , was popped before τk enters. Suppose further that τj but not
        τr was popped when τp was being added to the stack, because τp τq τr ≃ 123. If τr 6= τi′
        and τi′ < τq , then τq τr τi′ ≃ 231 within the stack and thus π. As such, it suffices to
        consider only the case τq < τl . Meanwhile, if τr = τi′ , then τq < τr = τi′ < τk . We
        proceed with the assumption τq < τk . By Lemma 3.2, when τk enters the stack, there
        is some term τp′ ≤ τp below it. Therefore, π contains τq τk τp′ ≃ 231.
     • Lastly, suppose that τi′ was popped before τk enters the stack. As above, consider
        τp , τq , and τr . If τq 6= τi′ , we must have τp < τi′ , as otherwise the stack contains
        τi′ τq τr ≃ 123. Otherwise, if τq = τi′ , then τp < τq = τi′ . In either case, we have τp < τi′ .
        Again by Lemma 3.2, when τk is at the top of the stack, there is some term τp′ ≤ τp
        below it. Therefore, π contains τi′ τk τp′ ≃ 231.
    As per the above cases, τ will not be in the sorting class of SC123 if it contains 132.
Henceforth, assume that τ ∈ Avn (132). If τ contains τi τj τk τl ≃ 3214 and neither of τi and τj
have popped when τk enters the stack, then the stack will contain τk τj τi ≃ 123. In order for
the stack to avoid the pattern 123, there must be some element τs < τk between τi and τj
in the stack. But then τ contains τs τj τk ≃ 132, a contradiction. Thus, at least one of τi or
τj will be popped before τk enters. By Lemma 3.2, there is some τk′ ≤ τk in the stack when
τl is added. Then, π will contain either τi τl τk′ ≃ 231 or τj τl τk′ ≃ 231. Lastly, if τ contains

τl < τ1 , for otherwise τ1 τi τl ≃ 132. So, we set i = 1 without changing the relative ordering
of τi τj τk τl . But τ1 can never be popped, so we may simply proceed as before. In conclusion,
if τ is not in Avn (132, 3214, 4213), it is not in Sortn (SC123 ).

4.2. Periodic points of the maps SCσ . Consider π ∈ Sn . In what follows, let π ∗ ∈ Sn−1
obtained by removing the element 1 from π and shifting the remaining elements of π down

Theorem 4.20. For σ ∈ {132, 231, 132, 231}, the periodic points of SCσ in Sn are precisely
Avn (132, 231). For n ≥ 2, these points have period 2.
Proof. Let σ = 231, where a similar argument applies to the other patterns. We proceed
by induction on n, where the n = 1 case is trivial. Consider τ ∈ Sn and note that the
elements τi = 1 and τj = 2 cannot be premature. Thus, if τj occurs before τi in τ , their
order will be flipped in SC231 (τ ). Next, we suppose that τi occurs before τj in τ . When τj
enters the stack, all of the elements above τi in the stack will be popped. Therefore, τi and
τj will be adjacent in the stack, and thus in π = SC231 (τ ). But for permutations π such
that the element 1 and 2 are adjacent, we can easily check that SC231 (π)∗ = SC231 (π ∗ ) and
that π ∗ ∈ Avn−1 (132, 231) if and only if π ∈ Avn (132, 231). Since π ∗ is eventually sorted
to Avn−1 (132, 231) by repeated applications of SC231 , π will also eventually be sorted to
Avn (132, 231) by applications of SC231 . We conclude by induction.
   Lastly, consider τ ∈ Avn (132, 231). Note that when τ is sorted by SC231 , the stack never
triggers. Thus, SC231 (τ ) = rev(τ ), and indeed SC2231 (τ ) = τ .                         

  Furthermore, the proof of Theorem 4.20 bounds the number of applications of SCσ neces-
sary for τ ∈ Sn to reach a periodic point.
