                                                  ON COHEN-MACAULAY NON-PRIME COLLECTIONS OF CELLS

                                                           CARMELO CISTO, RIZWAN JAHANGIR, AND FRANCESCO NAVARRA

                                                 Abstract. In this paper we investigate Cohen-Macaulayness, Gorensteinness and the Hilbert-
                                                 Poincaré series for some classes of non-prime collections of cells. In particular, we show that all
                                                 closed path polyominoes are Cohen-Macaulay and we characterize those that are Gorenstein.

                                                                                         Introduction
                                             Combinatorial Commutative Algebra has been an intensive area of research since the pioneering
                                          work of R. Stanley ([33]). In 2012 a new topic emerged in this field due to the work of A.
                                          A. Qureshi ([28]). She established a connection between collections of cells and Commutative
                                          Algebra, assigning to every collection P of cells the ideal of the inner 2-minors of P in a suitable
                                          polynomial ring SP . This ideal IP is called the inner 2-minor ideal of P and K[P] = SP /IP is
                                          said to be the coordinate ring of P. In particular, if P is a polyomino, that is a collection of
                                          cells where the cells are joined edge by edge, then IP is called the polyomino ideal of P. The
                                          aim of the research is to study the main algebraic properties of K[P] depending on the geometry
                                          of P. The most investigated include primality, Cohen-Macaulayness and Gorensteinness. As
                                          usual, we say that a collection of cells is prime (respectively, Cohen-Macaulay or Gorenstein) if
                                          the related binomial ideal is prime (respectively, Cohen-Macaulay or Gorenstein). Regarding the
                                          classification of the primality for polyominoes, it is proven in [18, 19, 30] that if P is simple,
                                          which means that P has no hole, then K[P] is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain. Nowadays, the
                                          study is devoted to non-simple polyominoes but a complete characterization of primality, Cohen-
                                          Macaulay and Gorenstein properties is still unknown, despite the efforts of many mathematicians
                                          (see [1, 3, 5, 7, 18, 19, 21, 25, 26, 29, 30, 31, 32]). Other very interesting combinatorial problems
                                          on polyomino ideals have been investigated as shown in [8, 10, 13, 15, 23].
                                             Cohen-Macaulayness and Goresteiness have so far been discussed only for prime collections of
                                          cells. In all the papers where Cohen-Macaulayness is studied ([6, 21, 24, 26, 30]), the demonstrative
                                          strategy is based on two well-known theorems of Sturmfels ([35, Chapter 13]) and Hochster ([2,
                                          Theorem 6.3.5]), which state that a toric ring whose defining ideal admits a squarefree initial ideal
                                          is a normal Cohen-Macaulay ring. For Goresteiness, a well-known result of Stanley ([34]) allows
                                          to characterize the prime polyominoes which are Gorenstein, by checking if the h-polynomial is
                                          palindromic. In such a context, the so-called rook polynomial plays a crucial role. The rook
                                          polynomial is a polynomial whose k-th coefficient is the number of ways of placing k non-attacking
                                          rooks in a polyomino. The maximum number of non-attacking rooks is called the rook number of P.
                                          The connection of the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of K[P] with the rook number was shown
                                          in [14] for the first time. So far, the study of the h-polynomial in terms of the rook polynomial
                                          has produced a relevant number of articles, including [7, 11, 22, 24, 29, 31], and it could help to
                                          characterize the prime polyominoes that are Gorenstein. Indeed, if the h-polyonomial of a prime
                                          polyomino P is the rook polynomial, then we can check the palindromicity counting the number
                                          of suitable configurations of non-attacking rooks in P. On the other hand, discussing non-prime
                                          polyominoes which are Gorenstein seems very hard and such classes have not appeared so far.

   In this context a natural question arises: what can we say about the Cohen-Macaulay and the
Gorenstein properties for non-prime collections of cells? Motivated by this question, we begin to
investigate these properties for non-prime collections of cells. From [25, Corollary 3.6] we know
that if a collection of cells contains the so-called zig-zag walks (see [25, Definition 3.2]), then the
related binomial ideal is not prime. This suggests to consider as a first step a class of non-prime
collections of cells made up of zig-zag walks, which we called zig-zag collections. As conjectured
in [25, Conjecture 4.6], every non-prime collection of cells should contain a zig-zag collection, so
the latter could be useful for gaining information on non-prime collections of cells, by applying a
decomposition of them into suitable zig-zag collections. This method is used to study the before
mentioned properties for the closed paths, a kind of non-simple polyominoes introduced for the
first time in [3], in the non-prime case. In particular, for this class of polyominoes, we state the
following result at the end of this paper:
    Theorem 4.18 Let P be a closed path polyomino. Then:
     (1) K[P] is Cohen-Macaulay with Krull dimension |V (P)| − |P|; moreover, if P does not
         contain any zig-zag walk, then K[P] is also a normal domain.
     (2) The h-polyonomial of K[P] is the rook polynomial of P and reg(K[P]) = r(P).
     (3) K[P] is Gorenstein if and only if P consists of maximal blocks of rank three.
   The article is structured as follows. Section 1 is devoted to provide some basic definitions and
properties of collections of cells and the associated coordinate rings. In Section 2 we prove that
the h-polynomial of a simple collection of cells is equal to the product of the h-polynomial of each
connected component (Theorem 2.2), exploiting the monomial order in [27] and [5, Theorem 3.3].
As a direct consequence, we can generalize [25, Theorem 3.13], obtaining in Corollary 2.5 that the
h-polynomial of a simple thin collection of cells coincides with the rook-polynomial. In Section
3 we introduce a new class of non-prime collections of cells, named zig-zag collections. Taking
advantage of some recent results shown in [9], we show that their coordinate rings are Cohen-
Macaulay (Theorem 3.7), we compute the Hilbert-Poincaré series in terms of the switching rook
polynomial (Corollary 3.8) and we eventually discuss the Gorenstein property (Corollary 3.9). The
results achieved in the previous sections are collected in Section 4 to prove Theorem 4.18, which
is our main result.

1.3. The coordinate ring attached to a collection of cells. Following [28], we define a
binomial ideal attached to a collection of cells. Let P be a collection of cells. Set SP = K[xv |v ∈
V (P)], where K is a field. If [a, b] is an inner interval of P, with a,b and c,d respectively diagonal
and anti-diagonal corners, then the binomial xa xb − xc xd is called an inner 2-minor of P. We
defined the binomial ideal IP in SP as the ideal generated by all the inner 2-minors of P and we
call it the inner 2-minors ideal of P. If P is a polyomino, then IP is simply said the polyomino
ideal of P. We set also K[P] = SP /IP , which is the coordinate ring of P. We denote by G(P)
the set of the generators of IP . For a generalization of collections of cells and the related algebras
look at [8].
Remark 1.2. Let P be a collection of cells and assume that it is written as a disjoint union of
the collection of cells PN1 , . . . , Pr such that V (P) is the disjoint union of vertex sets V (Pj ), with
j ∈ [r]. Then K[P] ∼          r
                      = i=1 K[Pj ]. It follows that K[P] is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain if
and only if K[Pj ] is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain for all j ∈ [r]. Hence it is not restrictive to
consider just weakly connected collections of cells. For this reason, whenever the term “collection
of cells” is introduced in the paper, we mean a weakly connected collection of cells.
For utility, we state the following result about the Cohen-Macaulay property of the simple collection
of cells, that in the literature we found stated in this form only in the case P is polyomino (see
[18, Theorem 2.1] and [19, Corollary 3.3]). For the sake of completeness, we also provide its proof.
Proposition 1.3. Let P be a simple collection of cells. Then K[P] is a normal Cohen-Macaulay
domain of dimension |V (P)| − |P|.
Proof. By [5, Theorem 3.3] we know that IP = JP where JP is the toric ring of a weakly chordal
bipartite graph. In particular IP is prime and, by [27], IP has a squarefree quadratic Gröbner basis.
A toric ring whose toric ideal admits a squarefree initial ideal is normal, see [16, Corollary 4.26] and
by a theorem of Hochster (see [2, Theorem 6.3.5]), a normal toric ring is Cohen–Macaulay. Finally,
by [28, Theorem 3.5] and [28, Corollary 3.6] we know that IP = IΛ for a saturated lattice Λ such that
rankZ (Λ) = |P|. Hence height(IP ) = |P| (see [16, Problem 3.12]) and so dim K[P] = |V (P)| − |P|
(see also [36, Corollary 3.1.7]).                                                                      

                                        3. Zig-zag collections
In this section we discuss the algebraic properties of the coordinate ring of a new family of collec-
tions of cells called zig-zag collections. Roughly speaking, a zig-zag collection is a collection of cells
with just one hole that comes from the intervals related to a zig zag-walk. We start by pointing
out that definition.
Definition 3.1. Let I1 , . . . , Iℓ be a sequence of distinct intervals in Z2 . For all i ∈ {1, . . . , ℓ}
denote by Pi the collection of cells related to Ii and P = ℓi=1 Pi . P is a zig-zag collection if
                                                                  S
satisfies:
    (1) I1 ∩ Iℓ = {v1 = vℓ+1 } and Ii ∩ Ii+1 = {vi+1 }, for all i = 1, . . . , ℓ − 1;

    (3) Ii ∩ Ij = ∅ for all {i, j} ⊆ [ℓ] with i < j, such that j 6= i + 1 and (i, j) 6= (1, ℓ).

Theorem 4.13. Let P be a closed path with a zig-zag walk. Then K[P] is Cohen-Macaulay of
dimension |V (P)| − |P|.
Proof. By Lemma 4.5 we have depth(K[P]) ≥ min{depth(K[Qr ]), |V (P)| − |P|} and by
Lemma 4.12 depth(K[Qr ]) ≥ |V (Qr )| − |Qr |. Moreover, by the proof of Lemma 4.5 we also
have |V (Qr )| − |Qr | = |V (P)| − |P|. Hence, depth(K[P]) ≥ |V (P)| − |P|. By [10, Theorem
3.4] we also know that dim(K[P]) = |V (P)| − |P|, so K[P] is Cohen-Macaulay with the given
dimension.                                                                                

Proposition 4.17. Let P be a closed path with a zig-zag walk. Then K[P] is not Gorenstein.
Proof. Denote by h(t) = sk=1 hk tk the h-polynomial of K[P]. From Theorem 4.15, h(t) is the
                            P
rook polynomial of P and s = r(P). Suppose by contradiction that K[P] is Gorenstein, so it
follows from [36, Corollary 5.3.10] that hk = hs−k for all k ∈ {0, . . . , s}. In particular we have that
hs−1 = h1 = |P| and also hs = h0 = 1. We examine the following three cases.
    (1) Let us start showing that |I| ≤ 3 for every maximal cell interval I of P. In fact, suppose
        there exists a maximal interval I = [A, B] with |I| ≥ 4. Since |I| ≥ 4, then we can consider
        two distinct cells C, D ∈ I \ {A, B}. Note that every s-rook configuration of P has a rook
        in a cell of I \ {A, B}. Let T be an s-rook configuration of P with a rook placed in C.
        Therefore, we obtain a new s-rook configuration of P, moving the rook in C into D, so
        hs ≥ 2 > h0 = 1, that is a contradiction. Hence, |I| ≤ 3 for every maximal interval I of P,
        as claimed.
    (2) Now, suppose that P contains a sub-polyomino as in Figure 10b. Arguing as done before,
        there exists an s-rook configuration U in P having a rook placed in W and another one in
        Z. Moreover, we can get from U another s-rook configuration in P, moving the rook in U
        from W to X. Therefore the same previous contradiction arises.
    (3) From the previous cases it follows that every maximal interval of P has a rank less than
        or equal to 3 and P does not contain any sub-polyomino as in Figure 10b. Hence the only
        possibility is that P is as in Figure 3c. In such a case, Macaulay2 ([4, 12]) computations
        show that hK[P](t) = 1 + 16 t + 100 t2 + 308 t3 + 486 t4 + 376 t5 + 134 t6 + 20 t7 + t8 , so
        h7 = 20 6= 16 = h1 , which is a contradiction.
In all three cases we get a contradiction, so we conclude that K[P] cannot be Gorenstein.              

is a closed path polyomino.
Theorem 4.18. Let P be a closed path polyomino. Then:
  (1) K[P] is Cohen-Macaulay with Krull dimension |V (P)| − |P|; moreover, if P does not
      contain any zig-zag walk, then K[P] is also a normal domain.
                       ON COHEN-MACAULAY NON-PRIME COLLECTIONS OF CELLS                                         25

   (2) The h-polyonomial of K[P] is the rook polynomial of P and reg(K[P]) = r(P).
   (3) K[P] is Gorenstein if and only if P consists of maximal blocks of rank three.
Proof. We need to discuss just two cases. In the first one assume that P has a zig-zag walk.
Then (1) and (2) follow from Theorems 4.13 and 4.15. Concerning (3), we know that K[P] is
not Gorenstein from Proposition 4.17. Moreover, by [3, Proposition 6.1] P does not contain any
L-configuration, so any two maximal inner intervals of P intersect themselves in the cells displayed
in Figure 10a or 10b (up to reflections or rotations). Hence, we argue that if P has a zig-zag walk
then it contains maximal blocks of length two. This means that (3) holds if P has zig-zag walks.
Now, in the second case suppose that P does not contain a zig-zag walk. The claims (1) and (2)
are already proved in [7, Theorem 5.5] and (3) in [7, Theorem 5.7]. In conclusion, the theorem is
completely proved.                                                                                 
   We proved that the zig-zag collections and the closed paths with zig-zag walks are Cohen-
Macaulay, as well as the collections of cells Qi and Ri,k introduced respectively in Set-up 4.2 and
Set-up 4.8. Nowadays, an example of a collection of cells having a not Cohen-Macaulay coordinate
ring is still unknown. Therefore, from [18, 19, 30] and from the results of this work, the following
general question naturally arises.
