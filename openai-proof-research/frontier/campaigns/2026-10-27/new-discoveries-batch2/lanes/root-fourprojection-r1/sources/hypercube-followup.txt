                                               HYPERCUBE C*-ALGEBRAS AND AN APPLICATION TO
                                                             MAGIC ISOMETRIES


                                                                                 BJÖRN SCHÄFER


                                                   Abstract. We study C*-algebras generated by two partitions of unity with
                                                   orthogonality relations governed by hypercubes Qn for n ∈ N \ {0}. These




arXiv:2510.15586v1 [math.OA] 17 Oct 2025
                                                   “hypercube C*-algebras” are special cases of bipartite graph C*-algebras which
                                                   have been investigated by the author in a previous work. We prove that the
                                                   hypercube C*-algebras C ∗ (Qn ) are subhomogeneous and obtain an explicit
                                                   description as algebra of continuous functions from a standard simplex into a
                                                   finite-dimensional matrix algebra with suitable boundary conditions. Thus, we
                                                   generalize Pedersen’s description of the universal unital C*-algebra C ∗ (p, q) of
                                                   two projections. We use our results to prove that any 2 × 4 “magic isometry”
                                                   matrix can be filled up to a 4 × 4 “magic unitary” matrix. This answers a
                                                   question from Banica, Skalski and Soltan.

   It is an easy observation that a bipartite graph C*-algebra C ∗ (G) is not nuclear
if K2,3 ⊂ G, where K2,3 is the complete bipartite graph with two vertices on one
side and three vertices on the other side, see [16, Corollary 2.9]. However, the
inverse implication is not known, and the following remains an open problem.
Problem 1.2. Is C ∗ (G) nuclear whenever K2,3 ̸⊂ G holds?

Q1 , Q2 , Q3 are sketched in Figure 1. To study C ∗ (Qn ) we use a method inspired by
Roch and Silbermann [15] whose approach was outlined above. In fact, we exploit

is obtained. Recall that a C*-algebra is called k-subhomogeneous if the dimension

    As an application, we use our findings to analyze 2 × 4 magic isometries. Let us
first recall their definition from e.g. [6, Definition 5.1].
Definition 1.3. Let m ≤ n and let A be a unital C ∗ -algebra. An m × n-matrix
P = (Pij )i≤m,j≤n ∈ Mm,n (A) with entries from A is called a magic isometry if
the entries Pij are projections which form a partition of unity along rows and are
pairwise orthogonal along columns, i.e.
  (Pij )j=1,...,n is a partition of unity for each fixed i ≤ m,
 (Pij )i=1,...,m is a family of pairwise orthogonal projections for each fixed j ≤ n.
Here, with a partition of unity we mean a family of projections in a unital C ∗ -
algebra that sum up to the unit.
   If m = n and the projections Pij form a partition of unity along both rows and
columns, then we call P a magic unitary.
   The following problem was first raised by Banica, Skalski and Soltan in [6, Section
5], see also Weber’s introductory article [20, Problem 3.4].

matrix, i.e. given a magic isometry

                                 2. Preliminaries
   In this section we collect some basic definitions about bipartite graph C*-algebras
and hypercubes. Definitions and first observations regarding bipartite graph C*-
algebras are copied verbatim from [16].

2.1. Bipartite graph C*-algebras. Bipartite graph C*-algebras have been in-
troduced by the author in a previous paper. We recall their definition shortly and
refer to [16] for more details.

Definition 2.1. A bipartite graph G consists of two vertex sets U and V together
with an edge set E ⊂ {{u, v} | u ∈ U, v ∈ V }. We write G = (U, V, E).

   For two vertices in a bipartite graph G = (U, V, E) let us write u ∼ v if {u, v} ∈ E
and let N (u) := {v ∈ V | v ∼ u} (N (v) := {u ∈ U | v ∼ u}) be the neighbors
of u (v). For vertices x ∈ U ∪ V or edges e ∈ E we also write x ∈ G or e ∈ G,
respectively, and say that x or e are contained in G.
   A path µ in a bipartite graph G is a finite sequence of vertices x1 . . . xn such
that xi ∼ xi+1 holds for all i < n. For every i < n, we say that xi is contained in
µ. We set s(µ) = x1 and r(µ) = xn . For two paths µ = x1 . . . xm and ν = y1 . . . yn
with r(µ) = s(ν) we write µν for the combined path x1 . . . xm y1 . . . yn .
   Subgraphs and induced subgraphs of a bipartite graph are defined in the natural
way, for more details see [16, Definition 2.3]. Examples of bipartite graphs include
the complete bipartite graphs Km,n for m, n ∈ N.
   Let us next recall the definition of bipartite graph C*-algebras.

Definition 2.2 ([16, Definition 2.4]). Given a bipartite graph G = (U, V, E) let
C ∗ (G) be the universal C ∗ -algebra generated by a family of projections (px )x∈U ∪V

Proposition 2.3 ([16, Proposition 2.6]). Let G be a bipartite graph and for every

Then the elements pµ span a dense subset of C ∗ (G).

             4. Rank-one representations and edge weightings

                       7. Application to magic isometries
   In this final section we apply our findings about the C ∗ -algebra of the cube Q3
in order to solve a problem about magic unitaries. We discussed this problem and
its background from [6, Section 5] in the introduction. Recall the notions of an
n × m-magic isometry and an n × n-magic unitary from Definition 1.3.
   The general question is whether a given n × m-magic isometry can be completed
to an n × n-magic unitary by adding further projections. In this section we investi-
gate the particular case of a 2×4-magic isometry and its completion to a 4×4-magic
unitary. Thus, the problem we solve is the following.
