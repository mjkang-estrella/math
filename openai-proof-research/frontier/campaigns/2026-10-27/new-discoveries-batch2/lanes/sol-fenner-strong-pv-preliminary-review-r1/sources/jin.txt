    Discreteness of sets generated by fixed-parameter extrapolation
                                                 Kaiwen Jin

                                            October 14, 2026


                                                  Abstract
          Starting from 0 and 1, repeatedly apply the operation (a, b) 7→ (1 − λ)a + λb, with a fixed
      complex parameter λ. We prove that the resulting set is discrete exactly when λ is an algebraic
      integer whose other conjugates, apart from λ and λ, are real and lie in (0, 1). This gives the
      converse to the known sufficient condition for separated extrapolation sets. The proof adjusts
      integer polynomials within a residue class while preserving their values at λ. If there is an
      additional conjugate outside (0, 1), density of the corresponding field projections then produces
      nonzero generated points arbitrarily close to 0.


1    The discreteness question
For λ ∈ C, write
                                         a ⋆λ b = (1 − λ)a + λb,
and let Qλ be the smallest subset of C containing 0, 1 and closed under ⋆λ . Each point of Qλ is
obtained by finitely many operations; no topological closure is taken. The operation has been used
to generate point sets in aperiodic order, where separation distinguishes discrete patterns from
arbitrarily fine ones [1].
    Following Fenner, Green and Homer [1, Definition 4.1], an algebraic integer λ is strong PV if
every conjugate other than λ and λ is real and belongs to (0, 1). Integers and nonreal quadratic
algebraic integers satisfy this condition vacuously. Strong PV parameters give uniformly discrete
sets [1, Theorem 4.12]; discreteness also forces algebraic integrality [2, Theorem 8.2]. Campbell
recently proved the necessary conjugate restriction for totally real algebraic integers [3, Theorem 2].
The full complex-parameter converse is Conjecture 11.1 of [1].

Theorem 1. For every λ ∈ C, the set Qλ is discrete if and only if λ is strong PV. In that case,

                                   |x − y| ≥ 1      (x, y ∈ Qλ , x ̸= y).

    The main issue is the converse. A generated point can be represented by an integer polynomial
that stays between 0 and 1 on the real unit interval. We show that the interval condition can
be imposed after fixing the polynomial’s residue modulo the minimal polynomial of λ. An extra
conjugate outside (0, 1) leaves an unbounded field direction; projecting away that direction gives
enough small residues to contradict discreteness. The argument treats real parameters with nonreal
conjugates and nonreal parameters of arbitrary degree together.

3    Density after omitting a conjugate
We use a character test for additive subgroups of a real Euclidean space. Its short proof is included
to make the density argument explicit.
Lemma 4. If an additive subgroup G ⊆ RN is not dense, there is a nonzero real linear functional
L with L(G) ⊆ Z.
Proof. Let A = G and choose a vector subspace V ⊆ A of maximal dimension. Orthogonal projection
gives A = V + D, where D = A ∩ V ⊥ . The subgroup D is discrete. Otherwise some nonzero
dj ∈ D tend to 0, and a subsequence of dj /|dj | tends to a unit vector v ∈ V ⊥ . For every u ∈ R
choose integers kj with kj |dj | → u. Closedness gives kj dj → uv ∈ D, so V + Rv ⊆ A, contrary to
maximality.
