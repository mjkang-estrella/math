                                                                           DIETRICH BURDE AND KAREL DEKIMPE

                                                   Abstract. We study post-Lie algebra structures on pairs of Lie algebras (g, n), which describe
                                                   simply transitive nil-affine actions of Lie groups. We prove existence results for such structures

                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                              Post-Lie algebras and post-Lie algebra structures are an important generalization of left-
                                           symmetric algebras (also called pre-Lie algebras) and left-symmetric algebra structures on Lie
                                           algebras, which arise in many areas of algebra and geometry [7], such as left-invariant affine
                                           structures on Lie groups, affine crystallographic groups, simply transitive affine actions on Lie
                                           groups, convex homogeneous cones, faithful linear representations of Lie algebras, operad the-
                                           ory and several other areas. A particular interesting problem with a long history concerns
                                           crystallographic groups and crystallographic structures on groups. Here a Euclidean crystal-
                                           lographic structure on a group Γ is a representation ρ : Γ → Isom(Rn ) letting Γ act properly
                                           discontinuously and cocompactly on Rn . By the Bieberbach theorems, the groups admitting a
                                           Euclidean crystallographic structure are precisely the finitely generated virtually abelian groups.
                                           The torsion-free crystallographic groups, called Bieberbach groups, are exactly the fundamen-
                                           tal groups of compact flat Riemannian manifolds. John Milnor addressed the question how
                                           this result can be generalized to affine crystallographic structures for Γ, i.e., for representa-
                                           tions ρ : Γ → Aff(Rn ) letting Γ act properly discontinuously and cocompactly on Rn . More
                                           precisely he asked whether or not every virtually polycyclic group admits an affine crystallo-
                                           graphic structure. Although there was a lot of evidence for Milnor’s question having a positive
                                           answer, counterexamples were found [3, 4], in particular in terms of left-symmetric structures
                                           on Lie algebras. For the history and references see [7]. Another natural question then was
                                           whether one could find a reasonable class of generalized affine structures on Γ such that Mil-
                                           nor’s question would have a positive answer. This was indeed possible and lead to the notion
                                           of a so-called nil-affine crystallographic structure for Γ, i.e., a representation ρ : Γ → Aff(N),
                                           letting Γ act properly discontinuously and cocompactly on a simply connected, connected nilpo-
                                           tent Lie group N. Here Aff(N) = N ⋊ Aut(N) denotes the affine group of N. It was shown in

The natural generalization of left-symmetric structures, which correspond to the case N = Rn ,
are exactly the post-Lie algebra structures on pairs of Lie algebras. This is the reason that
these structures are so fundamental for nil-affine crystallographic structures. In the nilpotent
case, these structures can also be formulated by nil-affine actions of a nilpotent Lie group G
on another nilpotent Lie group N, and such Lie group actions can be translated to the level of
Lie algebras leading directly to post-Lie algebra structures [11].
As in the case of left-symmetric structures, the existence question of post-Lie algebra structures
is very important (and very hard in general). We already studied an intermediate case, the
so-called LR-structures on Lie algebras in [9, 10], and have obtained first results for the general
case in [11, 13]. In this paper we are able to prove more general results. One result concerns
perfect Lie algebras g, i.e., satisfying [g, g] = g. It has been proved in [23] that a perfect Lie
algebra over a field of characteristic zero does not admit a left-symmetric structure. We extend
this result and show that if g is perfect and n is solvable and non-nilpotent, then there is no
post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n). We will also prove that if (g, n) is a pair of Lie algebras,
where g is simple, and n is not isomorphic to g, then there is no post-Lie algebra structure on
(g, n). This shows that the assumption that g is simple is very strong. Already the case that n
is semisimple has strong implications. For example, there are no post-Lie algebra structure on
(g, n) with n semisimple and g solvable.
In the last part of the paper we study commutative post-Lie algebra structures. A post-Lie
algebra structure on a pair (g, n) is called commutative, if the Lie algebras g and n coincide,
so that the product is commutative. In this case we just write g instead of the pair (g, g). In
this special case one could perhaps hope for a good classification result. We first show that
any commutative post-Lie algebra structure on a semisimple Lie algebra is trivial, i.e., is given
by the zero product. Then we consider commutative post-Lie algebra structures on perfect Lie
algebras. This involves the study of the outer derivation algebra of a perfect Lie algebra, and
more generally its Lie algebra cohomology. It is known that cohomological questions for perfect
Lie algebras can be difficult, e.g., see [2], [21]. We prove that commutative post-Lie algebra
structures on perfect Lie algebras are trivial in several cases, and conjecture that this is always
the case. We also classify commutative post-Lie algebra structures in low dimension.
Finally we mention that post-Lie algebra structures have become more popular also in other
areas. As an example, post-Lie algebras have been studied by Vallette and Loday in connection
with homology of partition posets and the study of Koszul operads [24], [18]. Also, post-
Lie algebras have been studied in connection with isospectral flows, Yang-Baxter equations,
Lie-Butcher Series and Moving Frames [15].




                                       2. Preliminaries
   All Lie algebras are assumed to be finite-dimensional over a field k. For the structure results
we usually assume that k is the field of complex numbers. Post-Lie algebra structures on pairs
of Lie algebras (g, n) are defined as follows, see [11]:
                                 POST-LIE ALGEBRA STRUCTURES                                         3
Definition 2.1. Let g = (V, [ , ]) and n = (V, { , }) be two Lie brackets on a vector space V . A
post-Lie algebra structure on the pair (g, n) is a k-bilinear product x · y satisfying the identities:
(1)                             x · y − y · x = [x, y] − {x, y}
(2)                                 [x, y] · z = x · (y · z) − y · (x · z)
(3)                               x · {y, z} = {x · y, z} + {y, x · z}
for all x, y, z ∈ V .
   We denote by L(x) the left multiplication operators of the algebra A = (V, ·), and by R(x)
the right multiplication operators. So we have L(x)(y) = x · y and R(x)(y) = y · x for all
x, y ∈ V . By (3), all L(x) are derivations of the Lie algebra (V, {, }). Moreover by (2), the left
multiplication
                              L : g → Der(n) ⊆ End(V ), x 7→ L(x)
is a linear representation of g. Suppose that x · y is a post-Lie algebra structure on the pair
of Lie algebras (g, n) such that n is centerless and satisfies Der(n) = ad(n). Then there is a
unique ϕ ∈ End(V ) such that
                                          x · y = {ϕ(x), y}
for all x, y ∈ V , see Lemma 2.9 in [11]. This means L(x) = ad(ϕ(x)) for the linear operators
L(x). In this case the axioms of a post-Lie algebra structure can be formulated as follows, see
[11]:
Proposition 2.2. Let n be a semisimple Lie algebra and ϕ ∈ End(V ). Then the product
x · y = {ϕ(x), y} is a post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n) if and only if
                              {ϕ(x), y} + {x, ϕ(y)} = [x, y] − {x, y},
                                             ϕ([x, y]) = {ϕ(x), ϕ(y)}
for all x, y ∈ V .
  The second condition says that ϕ : g → n is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

                         3. Existence results with respect to g
  We study the existence question of post-Lie algebra structures on pairs (g, n), where the
algebraic structure of g is given.
Case 1: Assume, that g is a simple Lie algebra. It turns out that this assumption is very strong.
We will show that a post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n) with g simple exists if and only if n is
isomorphic to g. This generalizes Proposition 4.9 in [11]. Furthermore, if n ∼= g then either the
product x · y is trivial and [x, y] = {x, y}, or we have [x, y] = −{x, y} = x · y, see Proposition
4.6 in [11].
Theorem 3.1. Let (g, n) be a pair of Lie algebras, where g is simple and n is arbitrary. Suppose
that (g, n) admits a post-Lie algebra structure. Then n is isomorphic to g.
Proof. Denote by rad(n) the solvable radical of n. Consider the Levi decomposition n = s ⋉
rad(n) with a semisimple subalgebra s ∼= n/ rad(n). We have s 6= 0 by Theorem 4.2 of [13],
which is a non-trivial result. For any derivation D ∈ Der(n) we have D(rad(n)) ⊆ rad(n).

                                                                        = s. Consider the
composition
                                   p ◦ L : g → Der(s) ∼

where L : g → Der(n) is the representation given by the left multiplication maps L(x). Either

is injective. Then dim(g) ≤ dim(s) ≤ dim(n), hence dim(s) = dim(n). It follows that n = s,

  In other words, if (g, n) is a pair of Lie algebras, such that g is simple, and n is a Lie algebra
which is not isomorphic to g, then there is no post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n).
Case 2: Assume, that g is a semisimple Lie algebra. Then we have the following result, see
Theorem 4.2 of [13]:
Theorem 3.2. Let (g, n) be a pair of Lie algebras, where g is semisimple and n is solvable.
Then there is no post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n).
Case 3: Assume, that g is a perfect Lie algebra, i.e., [g, g] = g.
Proposition 3.3. Let (g, n) be a pair of Lie algebras over a field of characteristic zero, where
g is perfect and n is abelian or 2-step nilpotent. Then there is no post-Lie algebra structure on
(g, n).
Proof. Assume that there exists a post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n) with abelian n. By
definition this is a compatible left-symmetric structure on the perfect Lie algebra g. However,
it has been shown in [23], Theorem 1 and Proposition 1, that any Lie algebra admitting such
a structure must be solvable. This is a contradiction. For the field of complex numbers, the
solvability of g has been already shown in [16], Proposition 20.
Now assume that n is 2-step nilpotent. By Proposition 4.2 of [11] it follows that g admits a
left-symmetric structure. This is impossible, as we have just seen.                          

                         4. Existence results with respect to n
  If we assume that n is simple, no such strong result holds as in the case where g is simple. In
fact, given a semisimple Lie algebra n, there exists always a solvable, non-nilpotent Lie algebra
g such that there is a post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n), see Proposition 3.1 in [13]. Hence n
and g need not be isomorphic in general. On the other hand, if n is simple and g is nilpotent,
then there is no post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n). This follows from Theorem 3.3 of [13],
which we recall here for convenience:
Theorem 4.1. Let n be a semisimple Lie algebra, and g be a solvable, unimodular Lie algebra.
Then there is no post-Lie algebra structure on (g, n).
  One might ask, given a simple Lie algebra n, for which Lie algebras g there exists a post-Lie
algebra structure on (g, n). For example, for n = sl2 (C) there exists a post-Lie algebra structure
on (g, n) exactly for g = sl2 (C), and for all 3-dimensional solvable Lie algebras which are not
unimodular, see Proposition 4.7 in [11]. For n = sl3 (C) we may even take Lie algebras g which
are neither solvable nor semisimple:

                        5. Commutative post-Lie algebra structures
  A post-Lie algebra structure on a pair of Lie algebras (g, n) is commutative, if the algebra
product is commutative, i.e., x · y = y · x for all x, y. This means [x, y] = {x, y}, so that the
two Lie algebras are identical. We only write g instead of the pair (g, g) in this case.
Definition 5.1. A commutative post-Lie algebra structure on a Lie algebra g is a k-bilinear
product x · y satisfying the identities:
(4)                                      x·y =y·x
(5)                                 [x, y] · z = x · (y · z) − y · (x · z)
(6)                                 x · [y, z] = [x · y, z] + [y, x · z]
for all x, y, z ∈ V .
   There is always the trivial commutative post-Lie algebra structure on g, given by x · y = 0
for all x, y ∈ g.
Example 5.2. Let g be an abelian Lie algebra. Then a commutative post-Lie algebra structure
on g corresponds to a commutative, associative algebra.
  Using (4), (5) and [x, y] = 0 we have
                            x · (z · y) = x · (y · z) = y · (x · z) = (x · z) · y
for all x, y, z ∈ g.
Definition 5.3. Two commutative post-Lie algebra structures (A, ·) and (B, ◦) on g are called
isomorphic, if there is a bijective linear map ϕ : g → g satisfying
                                           ϕ(x · y) = ϕ(x) ◦ ϕ(y)
                                          ϕ([x, y]) = [ϕ(x), ϕ(y)]

for all x, y ∈ g.
  Note that this is in general more than just an isomorphism of the commutative algebras (A, ·)
and (B, ◦). We require in addition that the algebra isomorphism is also an automorphism of
the Lie algebra g.
We start with an important result on commutative post-Lie algebra structures.
Proposition 5.4. Any commutative post-Lie algebra structure on a semisimple Lie algebra g
is trivial.
Proof. Since g is semisimple, we have L(x) = ad(ϕ(x)) for some ϕ ∈ End(g), such that x · y =
[ϕ(x), y] and
                                         [ϕ(x), y] = [ϕ(y), x]
for all x, y ∈ g, see Proposition 2.2. It follows ϕ ∈ D(0, 1, 1) = 0 by Proposition 5.10 in [13], or
Lemma 6.1 in [17]. Hence x · y = 0 for all x, y ∈ g.                                              
Corollary 5.5. Let x · y be a commutative post-Lie algebra structure on a Lie algebra g. Then
g · g ⊆ rad(g).
Proof. Since the left multiplication operators L(x) are derivations of g for all x ∈ g, we have
L(x)(r) ⊆ r, where r = rad(g) denotes the solvable radical of g. It follows that g · r ⊆ r, and of
course also r · g ⊆ r. Therefore, there is an induced product ◦ on the semisimple Lie algebra
s = g/r which is given by
                    ◦ : s × s → s : (x + r, y + r) 7→ (x + r) ◦ (y + r) = x · y + r.
We have [x, y]g + r = [x + r, y + r]s , and it is obvious that ◦ is a commutative post-Lie algebra
structure on (s, s). By Proposition 5.4, ◦ is the trivial zero product. But this exactly means
that g · g ⊆ r.                                                                                 
Corollary 5.6. Let g = s ⊕ r be a direct Lie algebra sum of a semisimple Lie algebra s and
a solvable Lie algebra r. Then for any commutative post Lie structure on g we have that
s · g = g · s = 0.
Proof. Let x ∈ g and y, z ∈ s, then
                         x · [y, z] = [x · y, z] + [y, x · z] ∈ [r, s] + [s, r] = 0.
As [s, s] = s, the result follows.                                                                

Lemma 5.14. Let n be a nilpotent Lie algebra and assume that h is a subalgebra of n for which
h + [n, n] = n. Then h = n.
Proof. For any integer i ≥ 0, we define hi = h + ni , where the ni denote the terms of the lower
central series of n, with n0 = n. By assumption we have h1 = h + [n, n] = n and hi = h for all i
bigger or equal than the nilpotency class of n.
It is easy to see that hi+1 is an ideal of hi for any i, and that the quotient hi /hi+1 is abelian. Now
assume that h 6= n. Then there exists a positive integer i with hi = n and hi+1 6= n. For this i
we have that hi /hi+1 = n/hi+1 is abelian and hence [n, n] ⊆ hi+1 . But then n = h + [n, n] ⊆ hi+1
which is a contradiction.                                                                             
Lemma 5.15. Suppose that I is an ideal of a perfect Lie algebra g = s ⋉ n with s ⊆ I. Then
it follows that I = g.
Proof. We first consider the case where n is abelian. Since I contains s, we have that
                                          I = s + (I ∩ n).
Now, I ∩ n is also an ideal of g and so I ∩ n is an s–submodule of n. As s is semisimple, there
exist a complementary s-submodule a of n so that n = (I ∩ n) ⊕ a. It follows that a is also
an ideal of g and that g = I ⊕ a. But then [I, a] = 0, which gives [g, a] = 0. This implies
g = [g, g] ⊆ [I, I] ⊆ I, so that I = g.
Now we consider the general case. When g = n ⋊ s is perfect, then also g/[n, n] = (n/[n, n]) ⋊ s
is perfect. From the previous case, we then find that
                                   (I + [n, n])/([n, n]) = g/[n, n]
and hence I + [n, n] = g. It follows that
                                  g = s + n = s + (I ∩ n) + [n, n].
Hence we obtain (I ∩ n) + [n, n] = n. By the previous lemma we have (I ∩ n) = n. From this,
it follows that I = g.                                                                   
   Assume that x·y defines a commutative post-Lie structure on the perfect Lie algebra g = s⋉n.
Note that [n, n] is both a Lie ideal of g and an ideal for the post-Lie product, because all left and
right multiplications are derivations of g. It follows that there is an induced post-Lie product
on g/[n, n]. We have the following reduction result:
Proposition 5.16. Let x·y be a commutative post-Lie product on a perfect Lie algebra g = s⋉n.
If the induced product on g/[n, n] is the zero product, then also the product on g is zero.
Proof. Recall that when D is a derivation of a nilpotent Lie algebra n for which D(n) ⊆ [n, n],
then D(ni ) ⊆ ni+1 . For the left multiplication operator L(x) of the commutative post-Lie
algebra product x · y on g we have by assumption that L(x)(g) ⊆ [n, n]. Now, L(x) acts as a
derivation on [n, n]. Hence we find that Li (x)(g) ⊆ ni+1 for all i ≥ 1. It follows that L(x) is
a nilpotent map. Hence L : g → Der(g) is a morphism of Lie algebras, where each element of
L(g) is a nilpotent map. By Engel’s theorem it follows that L(g) is a nilpotent Lie algebra. But
then we must have s ⊆ ker(L). But as the kernel of L is an ideal of g containing s, it follows
that ker(L) = g and so the product x · y is the zero product.                                 

     Now we can apply the previous lemmas to commutative post-Lie algebra structures.
Theorem 5.19. Let g = a ⋊ s be a perfect Lie algebra with an abelian radical a. Assume that
a, as an s–module, decomposes into pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible s–modules. Then any
commutative post-Lie algebra structure on g is trivial.
Proof. Let x · y be a commutative post-Lie algebra structure on g with left multiplication
maps L(x). As g · g ⊆ a by Corollary 5.5, we have that L(x) ∈ Der(g, a). Moreover,
Ders (a) = Homs (a, a) is an abelian Lie algebra, because a decomposes into pairwise noniso-

algebra. But then L : g → Der(g, a) : x 7→ L(x) is a Lie algebra morphism with solvable image.
Hence L(s) = 0, and so s ⊆ ker(L). By Lemma 5.15 we can deduce that ker(L) = g and so the
post-Lie product is trivial.                                                                
Theorem 5.20. Let g = a ⋊ s be a perfect Lie algebra with an abelian radical a and without

    7. Commutative post-Lie algebra structures on nilpotent Lie algebras
   As we have seen in Proposition 6.3, the left multiplication operators L(x) are nilpotent for
any commutative post-Lie algebra structure on the Heisenberg Lie algebra h1 . We can extend
this observation to all non-abelian 2-generated nilpotent Lie algebras. This includes filiform
nilpotent Lie algebras.
Proposition 7.1. Let n be a non-abelian nilpotent Lie algebra which is generated by two ele-
ments. Then for any commutative post-Lie algebra structure on n the left multiplication oper-
ators L(x) are nilpotent.
Proof. The product on n induces a commutative post-Lie structure on n/n2 . As n/n2 is the
3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra, we know by the previous discussion that the induced left
multiplication maps are nilpotent. This means that for any x ∈ n, the map L(x) ∈ Der(n) is a
derivation which induces a nilpotent map on n/n2 , and hence also on n/n1 , since we have
                                   (n/n1 )/(n/n2 ) ∼
                                                   = n1 /n2 .
Since any derivation of n which induces a nilpotent derivation on n/n1 is itself nilpotent, we
can conclude that indeed all maps L(x) are nilpotent.                                       
Corollary 7.2. Let x · y be a commutative post-Lie algebra structure on a complex nilpotent
Lie algebra of dimension n ≤ 4 without abelian factor. Then all left multiplication maps L(x)
are nilpotent.
  We cannot extend the result to nilpotent Lie algebras with an abelian factor:

Example 7.3. Let n = h1 ⊕C be the direct sum of the Heisenberg Lie algebra h1 and the abelian
Lie algebra C. Let (e1 , . . . , e4 ) be a basis of n with [e1 , e2 ] = e3 . Then
                                  e1 · e1 = e1 · e4 = e4 · e1 = e4 · e4 = e4
defines a commutative post-Lie algebra structure on n, where not all maps L(x) are nilpotent.
  It is also clear that the result holds for all characteristically nilpotent Lie algebras, because
the derivation algebra of such Lie algebras is nilpotent. The above results and some more
computational evidence leads to the following question in general:
Question 7.4. Is it true that all left multiplication maps are nilpotent for every commutative
post-Lie algebra structure on n, where n is a nilpotent Lie algebra without abelian factor ?
