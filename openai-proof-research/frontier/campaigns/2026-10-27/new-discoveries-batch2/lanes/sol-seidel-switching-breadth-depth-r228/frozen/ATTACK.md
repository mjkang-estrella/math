## Route A: universal block construction and its obstruction

Let C be a symmetric conference matrix of order r, C²=(r−1)I, and let t>1 be odd. Put S=J_t⊗(C−I_r)+I_(tr). This is Seidel. Its eigenvalues are 1 (multiplicity (t−1)r) and 1+t(±sqrt(r−1)−1), each with multiplicity r/2. Thus the familiar non-square conference examples cannot answer Question A: two eigenvalues are irrational. This is an exact obstruction for every t, not a few computed examples.

More strongly, S has a ±1 eigenvector iff C has a ±1 eigenvector. For an eigenvector of S with eigenvalue other than 1, all t blocks must coincide, so it is (y,...,y), Cy=±sqrt(r−1)y. For eigenvalue 1, block sum z satisfies (C−I)z=0. Since r>2, C−I is invertible; hence z=0. No coordinate sum of an odd number of ±1 values is zero. This proves the equivalence. A regular switching is exactly a ±1 eigenvector: DSD has constant row sum iff S(D1)=θ(D1).

When r−1 is square, therefore, the construction pushes the ENTIRE obstruction into the original conference class; it does not produce a counterexample on its own. The Petersen conference C=J−I−2A has r=10, C²=9I and C1=3·1. Its odd blowups all have a regular switching (indeed the displayed representative is regular). checks.json verifies this exact example. Greaves Remark 3.6 already owns the non-square conference mechanism. No novelty is claimed for this reduction. Next decisive calculation: obtain a rational-spectrum conference class with no sign eigenvector, or prove such a conference class cannot exist. Neither has been established here.

## Route B: integer spectral projectors and local walk congruences

For integer eigenvalues λ,μ,ν, Pν=((S−λI)(S−μI))/((ν−λ)(ν−μ)) is the ν-projector. Regular switching means there exists s∈{±1}^n with sᵀPνs=n for at least one ν. Equivalently the exact integer conditions sᵀSs=νn and sᵀS²s=ν²n hold together: the squared norm of Ss−νs is zero. Thus an exact certificate excluding all three sign intersections would refute the FULL source question. Rational eigenvalues of an integer symmetric matrix are integers, so no rational-denominator escape exists.

The first known-existing spectrum in Greaves Table 2 with a question mark is n=28, {−5^14,3^7,7^7}. For the three complementary pairs, |n−1+λμ| is 12,8,48; the strict <8 theorem misses all three, including the boundary 8. This is a concrete candidate object class, not a claimed counterexample matrix. Its cubic identity is S³=5S²+29S−105I, so every diagonal entry of S³ is 30. If a switching were regular, its degree would be 16,12 or10. Applying source Lemma 3.3 gives closed three-walk counts 66,24,12 respectively, i.e. 33,12,6 triangles through every vertex. All are legal integers: this invariant falsifies the proposed shortcut of excluding regular switching by triangle parity. It does not exclude sign eigenvectors.

The two routes differ: A controls whole block families by eigenspace structure; B searches for an exact integral obstruction using projectors and walk constraints. Neither is a finite-check extrapolation.
