                                             On the Gaussian product inequality conjecture for
                                           disjoint principal minors of Wishart random matrices

                                                   Christian Genest*                   Frédéric Ouimet†                               Donald Richards‡


                                                                                                 Abstract
                                                This paper extends various results related to the Gaussian product inequality (GPI)
                                                conjecture to the setting of disjoint principal minors of Wishart random matrices.
                                                This includes product-type inequalities for matrix-variate analogs of completely mono-
                                                tone functions and Bernstein functions of Wishart disjoint principal minors, respec-
                                                tively. In particular, the product-type inequalities apply to inverse determinant pow-
                                                ers. Quantitative versions of the inequalities are also obtained when there is a mix of
                                                positive and negative exponents. Furthermore, an extended form of the GPI is shown
                                                to hold for the eigenvalues of Wishart random matrices by virtue of their law being
                                                multivariate totally positive of order 2 (MTP2 ). A new, unexplored avenue of research
                                                is presented to study the GPI from the point of view of elliptical distributions.

                                                Keywords: Bernstein function; complete monotonicity; Gaussian product inequality; Laplace
                                                transform order; multivariate Gamma distribution; principal minors; Wishart distribution.
                                                MSC2020 subject classifications: Primary 60E15, Secondary 26A48; 44A10; 62E15; 62H10.

orthogonal transformation such that the transformed polynomials are dependent on dis-

This inequality is homogeneous in the sense that each random variable Zi can be mul-

one assumes, without loss of generality, that the covariance matrix of Z , denoted by
Σ = (σij )1≤i,j≤d , is a correlation matrix, i.e., σii = 1 for every integer i ∈ {1, . . . , d}.
    Here is a point-by-point list, ordered chronologically, of what is known about (1.2):

  (a) For all α ∈ [0, ∞)2 , GPI2 (α) holds as a consequence of (|Z1 |, |Z2 |) being MTP2 ; see
      Karlin and Rinott [25].

 (b) For all d ∈ N, GPId (1, . . . , 1) holds, as proved by Frenkel [14], who exploited Wick’s
     formula in conjunction with properties of permanents and Hafnians.

  (c) For all m, n ∈ N0 , GPI3 (m, m, n) holds, as proved by Lan et al. [30], based on a
      careful analysis of Gaussian hypergeometric functions.

 (d) For all d ∈ N and n ∈ Nd0 , GPId (n) holds whenever the correlation matrix Σ
     is completely positive, as proved in [15], based on a combinatorial methodology
     closely tied to the log-convexity of the gamma function.

  (e) For all d ∈ N and n ∈ Nd0 , GPId (n) holds whenever all the entries of the correlation
      matrix Σ are nonnegative, as proved by Russell and Sun [43], using the Isserlis–
      Wick type formula of Song and Lee [52]; see Song [51] for a more detailed proof.
      This result was generalized by Edelmann et al. [11] to the case in which the
      squared Gaussian random variables Z12 , . . . , Zd2 are replaced by the components of
      a multivariate Gamma random vector in the sense of Krishnamoorthy and Parthasarathy
      [29]. In the version given in [11], the correlation matrix Σ is allowed to have
      nonnegative entries up to a conjugation transformation S 7→ SΣS by a signature
      matrix S . As highlighted in [16], this last result can be generalized even further to
      random vectors of traces of disjoint diagonal blocks of Wishart random matrices.
      The multivariate Gamma distribution corresponds to the special case in which the
      diagonal blocks have size 1 × 1.

  (f) For all (n1 , n2 , n3 ) ∈ {1}×{2, 3}×N0, GPI3 (n1 , n2 , n3 ) holds, as validated by Russell
      and Sun [43], who employed a brute-force combinatorial approach.

 (g) For all (n1 , n2 , n3 ) ∈ N0 ×{3}×{2}, GPI3 (n1 , n2 , n3 ) holds, as stated in Theorem 4.1
     of Russell and Sun [46], taking advantage of a sums-of-squares methodology com-
     bined with in-depth symbolic calculations using Macaulay2 and Mathematica.

 (h) For all (n1 , n2 , n3 , n4 ) ∈ N0 × {1} × {1} × {1}, GPI4 (n1 , n2 , n3 , n4 ) holds, as stated in
     Theorem 4.2 of Russell and Sun [46], using the same methodology as for (g). The
     case (α1 , α2 , α3 , α4 , α5 ) ∈ [1/20, ∞) × {1} × {1} × {1} × {1} is treated in Theorem 5.1
     of the same paper.

     A complementary direction of research that has emerged in the literature comprises
the so-called ‘opposite GPIs’, an expression coined by Russell and Sun [44]. In this
setting, the exponents in (1.2) are assumed to be a mix of nonpositive and nonnega-
tive numbers and the problem is to determine whether the inequality holds in a specific
direction and, if so, under what conditions. Russell and Sun [44] established, by employ-

d = 2 and every (α1 , α2 ) ∈ (−1/2, 0] × [0, ∞), thereby completing the exploration of the
GPI conjecture in the bivariate case. Tighter bounds were subsequently investigated by
Hu et al. [22] in this context.

   The second objective of the present paper is to generalize both (1.4) and (1.5) to the
setting in which the squared components Z12 , . . . , Zd2 are replaced by disjoint principal

   As mentioned in item (o) above, the study of inequality (1.3) can be extended to the
case in which negative power functions are replaced by completely monotone functions.
Another similar idea, which was tackled in [16], is to investigate what happens if one
replaces completely monotone functions with Bernstein functions (also referred to as
Laplace exponents by some probabilists). It was shown in [16] that if (X1 , X2 ) is a
random pair consisting of the two disjoint traces inside a Wishart random matrix that
has been partitioned into a 2 × 2 block matrix, then, for any given Bernstein functions f
and g , one has
                                 E{f (X1 )g(X2 )} ≥ E{f (X1 )}E{g(X2 )}.
    The third and final objective of the present paper is to generalize this result to the
setting in which the traces of disjoint diagonal blocks of a Wishart random matrix are
replaced by the corresponding disjoint principal minors; see Theorem 3.12.
    The paper’s outline is as follows. Some definitions and preliminary results are given
in Section 2. The main results are stated in Section 3. All proofs are deferred to
Section 4. In Section 5, a new, unexplored avenue of research is presented to study the
GPI from the point of view of elliptical distributions. Some technical lemmas used in the
proofs are relegated to Appendix A. In Appendix B, necessary and sufficient conditions
are derived under which the expectations in Corollary 3.4 and Proposition 3.11 are
finite. In Appendix C, the integrals appearing in the upper bound in Corollary 3.4 are
investigated.
    We conclude this introduction with a new conjecture which seems natural, given the
results presented here pertaining to the GPI for disjoint principal minors of Wishart
random matrices.
