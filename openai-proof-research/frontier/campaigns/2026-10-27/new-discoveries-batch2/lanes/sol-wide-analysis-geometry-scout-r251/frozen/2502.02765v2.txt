                                                  averages and some associated maximal functions like the bilinear spherical maximal function and its lacunary
                                                  counterpart. We describe necessary conditions satisfied by triples in the Lp improving region of a bilinear
                                                  spherical averaging operator and the localized bilinear spherical maximal function, as well as describe the

                                                  best-known boundedness regions to date. We state some open questions along the way to motivate future
                                                  research on this topic, and we exploit some possible generalizations.

   Another class of estimates of interest concerns the Lebesgue boundedness of the bilinear spherical averages
At . Given a bi(sub)linear operator B, we say that B is bounded on Lp (Rd ) × Lq (Rd ) → Lr (Rd ) if there exists
C > 0 such that
                             ∥B(f, g)∥Lr ≤ C∥f ∥Lp ∥g∥Lq , for all f, g ∈ C0∞ (Rd )
and we define the Lebesgue boundedness region of B as
        R(B) = {(1/p, 1/q, 1/r) : 1 ≤ p, q ≤ ∞, r > 0 and B : Lp (Rd ) × Lq (Rd ) → Lr (Rd ) is bounded}.
For all the bilinear operators that we will be interested in, the triples in R(B) must satisfy that 1/p + 1/q ≥
1/r, so in particular R(B) will be contained in the box [0, 1] × [0, 1] × [0, 2].
   Another simple but useful fact is that if two bi(sub)linear operators B1 and B2 satisfy a pointwise domina-
tion of the form |B1 (f, g)(x) ≤ |B2 (f, g)(x)| for all x ∈ Rd and for all f, g ∈ C0 (Rd ), then one has containment
of the boundedness regions R(B2 ) ⊂ R(B1 ).
   In this survey, we will focus on the Lebesgue boundedness properties of bilinear spherical averages. We are
interested in R(A1 ), or, in other words, in the description of the parameters p, q ∈ [1, ∞] and r > 0 for which

Question 6.3. In d = 1, can we prove continuity estimates for M̃?
   Other works have proved continuity estimates for M̃ with different approaches. Palsson and Sovine
[46] proved continuity estimates for A1 for any d ≥ 2 and for M̃ in any d ≥ 4 by using L2 × L2 → L1

   Several multilinear analogues of the bilinear results discussed earlier have been established. For instance,
Dosidis [19] obtained a multilinear extension of the Jeong–Lee bounds for M in dimensions d ≥ 2, described
in Theorem 2.2. In the one-dimensional case, Dosidis and Ramos [20] proved multilinear generalizations of
their results for M stated in Theorem 2.3. A multilinear counterpart of the lacunary bounds for Mlac in
d ≥ 2 from [7] was later established by Gao [29].
   The results of Cho, Lee, and Shuin [14] on MSlack (f, g) and AS1 k are also formulated in full multilinear
generality. In dimension d = 1, Shrivastava and Shuin [55] not only obtained Lp –improving bounds for the
bilinear operator A1 , but also extended these estimates to its multilinear variants. Finally, the L2 (Rd ) ×
L2 (Rd ) → L1 (Rd ) bound for MSlack when k ≥ d + 1 established in [33, Theorem 1.4] may be viewed as a
special case of the multilinear estimate

valid whenever the hypersurface S ⊂ Rmd has more than (m − 1)d nonvanishing principal curvatures.


                                                    8. Recent developments
   In the recent preprint [15], there have been notable advances addressing a couple of the questions raised in
this survey. For instance, for d ≥ 4, the authors establish that the lacunary maximal function Mlac satisfies
Hölder-type bounds
                                                                             pq
                                          Mlac : Lp (Rd ) × Lq (Rd ) → L p+q (Rd )
at the boundary p = 1 or q = 1, excluding the endpoint (p, q) = (1, 1). Moreover, they improve the known
region of Hölder boundedness for the bilinear spherical maximal function with a fractal dilation set ME
where E ⊂ [1, 2] has upper Minkowski dimension β. In particular, for d ≥ 2 and certain ranges of β, they
obtain an enlarged region of boundedness compared to the previously known results (recall that, except for
the case E = {1}, the only known region coincided with the one for M described earlier in Theorem 2.2).
