                                                          A BOMBIERI-TYPE INEQUALITY AND
                                                            EQUIDISTRIBUTION OF POINTS

                                              UJUÉ ETAYO, HAAKAN HEDENMALM, AND JOAQUIM ORTEGA-CERDÀ


                                                    Abstract. In recent work, Etayo introduces a new Bombieri-type in-
                                                    equality for monic polynomials. Here we reinterpret this new inequality




arXiv:2507.20303v1 [math.CA] 27 Jul 2025
                                                    as a more general integral inequality involving the Green function for the
                                                    sphere. This rather geometric interpretation allows for generalizations of
                                                    the basic inequality, involving fractional zeros while also opening up the
                                                    possibility to extend the setting to general compact Riemann surfaces.
                                                    We derive a sharp form of these generalized Bombieri-type inequalities
                                                    for the case of the sphere and the torus. These inequalities involve a
                                                    quantity we call the packing number, which in turn is inspired by the
                                                    geometric zero packing problems considered by Hedenmalm in the con-
                                                    text of the asymptotic variance of the Bergman projection of a bounded
                                                    function. As for the torus, we introduce analogs of polynomials (pseu-
                                                    dopolynomials) based on the classical Weierstrass σ function, and we
                                                    explain how such pseudopolynomials fit in with the extended geometric
                                                    Bombieri-type inequality. The sharpness of the packing number bound
                                                    on the torus involves the construction of a lattice configuration on the
                                                    torus for any given integer number of points. The corresponding bound
                                                    for the sphere instead relies on the existence of well-conditioned poly-
                                                    nomials in the sense of Shub and Smale.

iterated inequality (1.4.4) to be sharp as soon as it involves three or more

2.2. Generalization to compact Riemann surfaces. The function in-
troduced in the preceding subsection US (z, w) can be understood as a ver-
sion of the Green function for the sphere S. Of course, it is well-known
that on a compact Riemann surface S there is no function which is har-
monic except for a single logarithmic “pole”. However, there exists instead
a bipolar Green function LS (z, w, w′ ), with two logarithmic poles w, w′ of
opposite sign, for w ̸= w′ . If in the corresponding charts the function
has the local expansions LS (z, w, w′ ) = log |z − w| + O(1) for z ∼ w and
L(z, w, w′ ) = − log |z − w′ | + O(1) for z ∼ w′ , it is then uniquely determined
up to an additive constant. To fix the constant as well, we will require that
                         Z
                               LS (z, w, w′ ) dAS (z) = 0,
                          S
where dAS is the canonical area measure on S , having constant Gaussian
curvature. There is also a unipolar Green function US (z, w), which we obtain
from LS (z, w, w′ ) by simply averaging over w′ with respect to the canonical
area measure. In other words, if w′ ∈ S is thought of as random with density
proportional to the surface area measure dAS , then the expected value of
LS (z, w, w′ ) equals the function US (z, w). It is worth noticing that the
bipolar Green function can be recovered from the unipolar Green function
via the simple formula
                    LS (z, w, w′ ) = US (z, w) − US (z, w′ ).
It is easy to see that the function US (z, w) when S = S then coincides with
the expression US (z, w) given by eq. (2.1.1).

5.2. Linear structure: fibers of toroidal pseudopolynomials. We con-
sider the manifold of toroidal pseudopolynomials of degree N , denoted by
ψpolN (C/Λ), which consists of all functions λΠα where λ ∈ C and α =
(α1 , . . . , αN ) ∈ CN . Clearly, it has C-dimension N + 1. As defined, all we
can do is to move zero sets α and the “leading coefficient” parameter λ. How-
ever, sometimes the linear combination of two toroidal pseudopolynomials
is again a toroidal pseudopolynomial. To describe when this happens, we
introduce fibers in ψpolN (C/Λ). Given a point γ ∈ C, we consider the fiber
               ψpolγN (C/Λ) := λΠα : α1 + · · · + αN − γ ∈ Λ, λ ∈ C .
                              

In view of the defining condition, we can add an element of the lattice Λ to
the parameter γ without altering the fiber. This means that we may think
of γ as a point on the torus: γ ∈ C/Λ. Since in the definition of the fiber,
we reduce the manifold by one (complex) condition, the C-dimension of the
fiber drops down to N .

fiber in terms of two simple conditions: (1) a differential equation, and (2)
periodicity-type conditions involving the two generators of the lattice Λ. In
particular, the fiber is a C-linear manifold of dimension N .
