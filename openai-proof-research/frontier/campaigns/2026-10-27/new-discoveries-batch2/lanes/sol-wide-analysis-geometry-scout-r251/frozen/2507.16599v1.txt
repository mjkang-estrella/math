                                                  Abstract. We investigate trace and observability inequalities for Laplace eigenfunctions on the
                                                  d-dimensional torus Td , with respect to arbitrary Borel measures µ. Specifically, we characterize
                                                  the measures µ for which the inequalities
                                                           Z            Z                         Z           Z
                                                              |u|2 dµ ≲ |u|2 dx (trace),             |u|2 dµ ≳ |u|2 dx (observability)

                                                  hold uniformly for all eigenfunctions u of the Laplacian. Sufficient conditions are derived based

                                                  the dimension of the support of the measure.
                                                     These results generalize classical theorems of Zygmund and Bourgain–Rudnick to higher
                                                  dimensions. Applications include results in the spirit of Cantor–Lebesgue theorems, constraints
                                                  on quantum limits, and control theory for the Schrödinger equation.

                                                  decoupling estimates; and the construction of eigenfunctions exhibiting strong concentration or
                                                  vanishing behavior, tailored respectively to the trace and observability inequalities.

rational hyperplane, or on a cylinder of the type {(x1 , . . . xd ) ∈ Td : (x1 , . . . xk ) ∈ Z}, where
k ≤ d − 1 and Z the zero set of an eigenfunction in Tk . Indeed, there are eigenfunctions with
arbitrarily large eigenvalues vanishing on such sets. The semiclassical observability inequality
does not hold either for measures supported on general hyperplanes, as a consequence of a
Diophantine approximation argument.
   Finally, the very recent manuscript [BZ25] by two of the authors of the present paper addresses
the question of observability for the time-dependent Schrödinger equation (with potential) on
the torus, which is closely related to the observability of eigenfunctions. This paper shows that,
under rather weak conditions, trace inequality implies observability inequality. In particular, it
shows that (weak forms of) uniform Lp bounds (p > 2) for eigenfunctions imply observability
from general Borel sets with positive measures.
1.3. Broader context. The trace inequality studied in the present manuscript is uniform in
λ. Some toral eigenfunction bounds, however, do grow with λ, and a complementary line of
research aims at finding optimal estimates in this regime [Bou93; BD15; HZ21]. One can also
consider quasimodes, i.e. functions with narrow spectral localization [GM22; Ger23].
   This trace inequality has a natural counterpart in Euclidean spaces. Choosing µ as a probability
measure on Rd , the problem is to find conditions on µ such that the Fourier extension operator
g 7→ F(g dσSd−1 ) (where F is the Fourier transform on Rd ) is a bounded map from L2 (Sd−1 ) to
L2 (dµ) (note that the parameter λ disappears by scale invariance). A sufficient condition on µ
was conjectured by Mizohata and Takeuchi and was very recently disproved [Cai25], see also
[CIW24] and references therein for positive results.
   Eigenfunction bounds have also been investigated for general compact Riemannian manifolds.
Universal estimates are known [BGT07; Sog17] and are usually saturated on spheres.
   Similarly, one can ask about observability inequalities for general compact Riemannian
manifolds. The answer depends strongly on the global geometry of the manifold. For instance,
observability inequalities from nonempty open sets hold true on surfaces with negative curvature
[Jin17; DJN22], but not on spheres, where highest weight spherical harmonics may concentrate
on great circles.
   Finally, a last line of research consists in adding a potential on the torus and asking for
observability inequalities; for this we refer to [BZ25].

Remark 4.3. The above theorem can be extended rather straightforwardly to the following cases
     • Superficial measures supported on non-smooth curves, dµ = dσΓ . One may assume
       either Γ is twice continuously differentiable with non-vanishing curvature, or Γ is convex
       and rely on [Cha06] to obtain decay for the Fourier transform.
     • Functions in Sobolev spaces W ϵ,p with ϵ > 0 and p ≥ 1 (clearly it gives new results only
       when p ∈ [1, 2)). To show this, it suffices to use Jarnik’s lemma as above, and notice
       that the Sobolev regularity gives polynomial gain in λ while the number of lattice points
       on the circle is subpolynomial.
Motivated these results, it is natural to investigate whether the trace and observability inequalities
extend to broader classes of measures and sets. In particular, the following cases are of interest
and open to the best of our knowledge:
     • Trace inequality: Densities belonging to Lp (T2 ) for 1 < p < 2.
     • Trace inequality: Densities in Lp (Σ), where Σ ⊂ T2 is a smooth curve.
     • Observability inequality: Subsets of a smooth curve Σ ⊂ T2 with non-vanishing
       curvature, provided these subsets have positive measure.
     • Trace and observability inequalities: Measures supported on sets with Hausdorff
       dimension strictly greater than 1.
