    The symmetric-square structure has a parallel interpretation in characteristic p. For an
elliptic curve over a finite field, Frobenius acts on its first cohomology with two eigenvalues
αp , βp . On the symmetric square the eigenvalues become
                                         αp2 ,      αp βp ,      βp2 .
Thus the same rank-two-to-rank-three passage that appears analytically in (1.6) also governs
the p-adic arithmetic of the Domb period. A second ingredient is complex multiplication. If
τ0 ∈ H is imaginary quadratic, then C/(Z + τ0 Z) has complex multiplication by an order in
an imaginary quadratic field. At such parameters, modular functions take algebraic values
and the Frobenius behavior is controlled by the splitting of p in that field. At a prime
inert in the imaginary quadratic field underlying the complex multiplication, good reduction
is supersingular. The Hasse invariant therefore vanishes. In the level-6 family considered
here, the truncated C-period Ap is the Hasse scalar, and the finite form of (1.15) forces the
associated truncated Domb period to vanish modulo p2 . At a split prime, the reduction is
ordinary and one Frobenius eigenvalue is a p-adic unit. Beukers’ excellent-Frobenius formalism
identifies the truncated Domb period, at the relevant point of complex multiplication, with
the square of this unit root modulo p3 . The determinant-1 and determinant-3 matrices
