                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                              The q-integers 1 + q + · · · + q n−1 are the most basic q-analogues of integers. Morier-Genoud
                                           and Ovsienko extended them to all rational numbers by replacing the Pascal triangle in the
                                           recursion of the Gaussian binomial coefficients with the Farey graph, and they related the
                                           resulting polynomials to quiver representations and to the Jones polynomials of rational knots
                                           [MO20]. Before that, Lee and Schiffler had expressed the Jones polynomials of rational links
                                           through cluster algebras and continued fractions [LS19], see also [ÇS18], and Kogiso and Wakui
                                           had related them to Conway–Coxeter friezes [KW19]. The q-deformed rational numbers are
                                           compatible with the action of the modular group [LM21], and they appear as boundary points
                                           of a compactified space of stability conditions on the 2-Calabi–Yau category of type A2 [BBL23].
                                           They also appear in the study of Markov numbers [LLS23], of the Burau representation of the
                                           braid group on three strands [MOV24] and of character varieties [JPT26a].
                                              The denominators of q-deformed rational numbers are polynomials in q with integer coef-
                                           ficients, and it is natural to ask how they factor over Q. Evans, Veselov and Winn studied
                                           the q-deformed rational numbers whose denominators are products of cyclotomic polynomials
                                           [EVW26]. In this paper we determine which cyclotomic polynomials divide the denominators,
                                           by identifying the specialization of the q-deformed modular group at a root of unity with a
                                           triangle group, and we show that for several infinite families the denominators are irreducible
                                           up to these cyclotomic factors.
                                              For an irreducible fraction r/s with s > 0 the q-deformed rational number is a rational
                                           function [r/s]q = Rr/s (q)/Sr/s (q), where Rr/s (q) and Sr/s (q) are coprime Laurent polynomials
                                           with integer coefficients, normalized by Sr/s (q) ∈ Z[q] and Sr/s (0) = 1 [MO20], [BRY26, Section
                                           2]. We call them the numerator and the denominator of [r/s]q . They satisfy Rr/s (1) = r and
                                           Sr/s (1) = s, and for r/s > 1 they are polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients. The
                                           definition is recalled in Section 2. The q-deformation is compatible with the action of the
                                           modular group Γ = PSL(2, Z) on P1 (Q) = Q ∪ {∞} through a q-deformation of Γ [MO20],
                                           [LM21], which coincides, after projectivization, with the Burau representation of the braid
                                           group on three strands up to the sign of the parameter [MOV24]. Divisibility of Laurent
                                           polynomials is understood in Z[q ±1 ]. For n ≥ 1 let [n]q = 1 + q + · · · + q n−1 be the q-integer.

3.1. Specialization at roots of unity. Let d ≥ 2. The quotient ∆d = Γ/Nd has the presen-
tation ⟨s, t | s2 , (st)3 , td ⟩, where s and t are the images of S and R. This is the triangle group of

Fuchsian group [Mag74], [Bea83].

4.3. Further questions.
Question 4.9. Let r/s be an irreducible fraction with s > 0, and let f ∈ Z[q] be an irreducible
factor of Sr/s (q) with positive leading coefficient. If f is not a cyclotomic polynomial, does
f (1) ≥ 2 hold?
   For a prime s = p Question 4.9 is equivalent to Conjecture 1.2. Indeed, let a be prime to p
with a ̸≡ ±1 (mod p). Then Sa/p has no cyclotomic factor by Corollary 3.9 and Sa/p (1) = p.
Since Sa/p has nonnegative coefficients and Sa/p (0) = 1, its irreducible factors with positive
leading coefficients are positive on (0, ∞). Their values at q = 1 are positive integers whose
product is p, and therefore all of them but one take the value 1. Thus Sa/p has the property
in question if and only if it is irreducible. For a ≡ ±1 (mod p) we have Sa/p = [p]q = Φp (q),
which is irreducible and has no non-cyclotomic factor, and both the property in question and
the conclusion of Conjecture 1.2 hold for a/p. Corollary 4.7 answers the question positively if
s > 5b2 and r ≡ ±b or br ≡ ±1 (mod s) for some b ∈ {2, 3, 4, 6}.
