                                                                                   1. Introduction
                                             A generalized Frobenius partition of n is a two-rowed array

                                           ordering condition. When both rows are strictly decreasing, these arrays are the classical
                                           Frobenius symbols and are in bijection with ordinary partitions of n. In his 1984 Memoir

                                           Frobenius partitions. We write cφk (n) for their number and

   The arithmetic of cφk (n) has since been studied intensively, so we mention only some
highlights. Kolitsch [13, 14] introduced the companion function cφk (n), proved cφk (n) ≡ 0
(mod k 2 ), and related cφk to ordinary partitions for k = 5, 7, 11; Sellers [16, 17] sharpened
Kolitsch’s congruence on the progressions kn for k = 2, 3, 5, 7, 11. The Andrews–Sellers
conjecture on cφ2 modulo powers of 5 [1, 18] was proved by Paule and Radu [15] using
modular functions. Baruah and Sarmah [3, 4] studied cφ4 and cφ6 and posed conjectures
on cφ6 that were settled by Xia [19]. Hirschhorn [11] subsequently gave further proofs of
congruences for cφ6 , and Cui and Gu [6] established congruences modulo powers of 2 for
cφ6 .
   Garvan and Sellers [10] observed that the congruence (1+X)p ≡ 1+X p (mod p), applied
factor by factor inside Andrews’ constant-term representation, transfers any congruence
cφk (pn + r) ≡ 0 (mod p) to cφpN +k (pn + r) ≡ 0 (mod p) for all N ≥ 0. A unifying
framework was provided by Chan, Wang, and Yang [5], who showed that CΦk (q) is (up to
a power of q and an eta-quotient) a modular form and expressed it explicitly as a linear
combination of eta-quotients for many k. Using Kolitsch’s congruence they also proved [5,
Theorem 5.3] that, for p prime, α ≥ 1 and p ∤ N ,
(1)                           cφpα N (n) ≡ cφpα−1 N (n/p)            (mod p2α ),
where cφk (x) = 0 for x ∈ / Z. Recently Cui, Gu, and Tang [7, 8] developed the “method
of constant terms” and integer matrix exact covering systems to obtain expressions for
CΦk (q) with integral coefficients, together with several infinite families of congruences.
   For k even, (1) with p = 2 reads cφk (n) ≡ cφk/2 (n/2) (mod 22v2 (k) ). When k ≡ 2
(mod 4) this gives information modulo 4 only, namely cφk (2n + 1) ≡ 0 and cφk (2n) ≡
cφk/2 (n) (mod 4); see [5, (6.7), (6.22), (6.33)] for k = 6, 10, 14. The first purpose of this
paper is to show that an elementary refinement of the Garvan–Sellers idea goes beyond
modulus 4 for such k.

   Our proofs are elementary. Theorem 1.1 rests on the congruence (1+X)2 ≡ (1+X 2 )2
(mod 2m ) applied factor by factor inside Andrews’ constant-term representation, together
with an exact evaluation of the even part in z of the square of the Jacobi theta function.
For k = 18, the exact formula of Chan, Wang, and Yang for CΦ9 collapses modulo 4
to a two-term expression involving the Borwein cubic theta function a(q) (Lemma 4.1);
combined with the observation f13 ≡ ψ(q) (mod 4) this yields Theorem 1.3, and the
pentagonal theta series then appears through the classical 3-dissection of ψ(q). For k = 10
the same constant-term argument gives CΦ5 (q) modulo 2.
   The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 collects the q-series identities
we need, Section 3 proves Theorem 1.1, Section 4 proves Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 and their
corollaries, Section 5 proves Theorem 1.7, and Section 6 ends the paper with some ideas
for future study.
