                                           EQUALITY CASES FOR THE Lp -ROGERS–SHEPHARD INEQUALITY
                                          IN THE PLANE AND FOR LOCALLY ANTI-BLOCKING BODIES IN Rn

                                           MATTHIEU FRADELIZI, AUTTAWICH MANUI, MARK MEYER, AND CHEIKH SALIOU NDIAYE


                                                 Abstract. The classical Rogers–Shephard inequalities were extended to the Firey Lp -
                                                 summation by Bianchini and Colesanti in the plane and by Zvavitch and the second and
                                                 fourth authors for locally anti-blocking convex bodies in Rn , leaving open the equality
                                                 cases. We characterize the equality cases of these inequalities: in both cases, for p > 1,
                                                 equality holds if and only if the convex body is a simplex with one vertex at the origin.

   Bianchini and Colesanti [6, Theorem 1.1] were able to prove inequality (4) in the planar
case n = 2 by making a key observation about parallel chord movements of convex bodies.
Zvavitch, together with the second and fourth named authors of this paper, managed to show
that inequality (4) is true in dimension n ≥ 2 if one restricts to locally anti-blocking convex
bodies [23, Lemma 23], which are the convex bodies for which all orthogonal projections onto
and intersections with the coordinate hyperplanes are identical. Moreover, they established
that equality holds among anti-blocking convex bodies if and only if the body is a simplex.
Recall that an anti-blocking convex body is a locally anti-blocking convex body included in
the positive orthant.
   In the forthcoming paper [14], the authors of the present paper show that conjecture (4) is
true for L2 -zonoids, and even more, that in this setting it is possible to replace the constant
κn,p with the improvement 2n .
   The purpose of this paper is to fill in two gaps in the literature by characterizing the
conditions of equality for the planar inequality of Bianchini and Colesanti, and the locally
anti-blocking inequality of Zvavitch, with the second and fourth named authors of this paper.
In the case of locally anti-blocking convex bodies, we will show:
Theorem 1. Let p ∈ (1, +∞], and let K ⊂ Rn be a locally anti-blocking convex body. Then
                                   |K ⊕p (−K)| = κn,p |K|
holds if and only if K is a simplex of the form K = conv(0, α1 e1 , . . . , αn en ) for non zero
real numbers αi .
    In the planar case, we will make use of a technique of Mathieu Meyer [24] to prove:
Theorem 2. Let p ∈ (1, +∞], and let K ⊂ R2 be a convex body such that 0 ∈ K. Then
                                   |K ⊕p (−K)| = κ2,p |K|
holds if and only if K is a triangle with one vertex at the origin.
   We dedicate the remainder of the paper to the proof of the above two theorems. We will
give a review of relevant preliminary facts in Section 2. The following sections, Sections 3
and 4, cover the proofs of Theorems 1 and 2.

                                  3. Proof of Theorem 1
Lemma 4. Let A, B ⊂ Rn+ be anti-blocking convex compact sets such that A ⊕p −B is
full-dimensional. Then
(7)                                 |A ⊕p B| ≤ |A ⊕p −B|.
Moreover, if there is equality, then for all i ∈ [n]
                              Si (A) ⊕p Si (−B) = Si (A ⊕p −B).
Proof. To study the equality case, we first need to recall the proof of the inequality from
[23]. In [23, Lemma 18], it is established that for any convex sets K, L
                                Si (K) ⊕p Si (L) ⊂ Si (K ⊕p L).
To prove the bound (7), we recall the chain of inequalities established in [23, Lemma 19]:
(8)         |A ⊕p −B| = |Sn (A ⊕p −B)| ≥ |Sn (A) ⊕p Sn (−B)| = |A ⊕p Sn (−B)|,
where the first equality in (8) is due to the fact that the operator Sn preserves volume and
the last equality follows from Sn (A) = A. We repeat the process by applying the other
operators Sj for j < n to obtain
         |A ⊕p −B| ≥ |A ⊕p Sn (−B)| ≥ · · · ≥ |A ⊕p S1 · · · Sn−1 Sn (−B)| = |A ⊕p B|,
where the last equality is true because S1 . . . Sn−1 Sn (−B) = B. Now, if |A⊕p B| = |A⊕p −B|,
then there is equality in all the inequalities of the line above. In particular, |Sn (A ⊕p −B)| =
|Sn (A) ⊕p Sn (−B)|. But, since one set is included in the other and both are convex bodies,
this implies that Sn (A ⊕p −B) = Sn (A) ⊕p Sn (−B). Notice that the shakings in different
coordinate directions commute for A and −B, because they are just symmetries with
respect to coordinate hyperplanes. So we can use the same argument to conclude that
Si (A) ⊕p Si (−B) = Si (A ⊕p −B) for all i.                                                     □
