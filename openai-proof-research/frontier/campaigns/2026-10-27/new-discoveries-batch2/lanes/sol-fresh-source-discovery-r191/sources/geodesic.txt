                                                                                               Abstract

                                                      For a given connected graph G(V, E), the geodesic transversal problem finds a set S of
                                                   vertices of G with minimum cardinality such that every maximal geodesic of G contains at least
                                                   one vertex of S. The cardinality of such a smallest set S is called the gt-number of G and is
                                                   denoted as gt(G). In this paper, we introduce the Edge geodesic transversal problem as the edge-
                                                   based extension of the geodesic transversal problem. The problem of determining a set S ⊆ E(G)
                                                   with minimum number of elements such that every maximal geodesic P in G traverses at least
                                                   one edge of S is called the edge geodesic transversal problem, and the cardinality of such a set is
                                                   called the edge geodesic transversal number or the gte -number of G, denoted as gte (G). Further,
                                                   we investigate the effect of an edge cut of a graph on the edge geodesic transversal problem and
                                                   prove a novel result called the edge cut transversal theorem which is used in the computation of
                                                   edge geodesic transversal number of hypercube, stacked prism and ladder graph.

                                                Keywords: maximal geodesic, geodesic transversal, cartesian product of graphs, hypercube.
                                           AMS Subject Classification: 05C12


                                           1      Introduction

                                           Graphs are widely used to represent real-world systems in which objects(nodes or vertices) interact
                                           through connections(edges). Examples include communication networks, transportation systems,
                                           social networks, chemical structures, and biological structures. A fundamental objective in the
                                           study of graphs is to analyze how information, resources, or entities move through such networks.
                                           For this reason, shortest paths play a crucial role in graph theory.
                                                 Given a connected undirected graph G(V, E), the shortest path between two vertices u, v ∈ V (G)
                                           is called a geodesic. Geodesics provide a natural measure of efficiency in a network because they
                                           represent routes or paths with minimum distance. As a result, geodesic-based investigations have


                                                                                                   1
become an important area of research in both theoretical and applied graph theory. Geodesic-based
concepts play a significant role in optimization problems that aim to identify a vertex or a set of
vertices with a distinguished role in relation to shortest paths. Such investigations help identify
critical locations within a network and have applications in routing, network monitoring, facility
location, communication networks, and network control.
    The geodesic transversal problem is one such optimization problem which was introduced by
Brešar, Klavžar and Manuel [1] in 2022. A set S ⊆ V (G) is a geodesic transversal of G, if the
vertices of S collectively hits all maximal geodesics in G. A geodesic transversal with minimum
elements is called a geodesic transversal set or gt-set and its cardinality is the geodesic transversal
number or the gt-number of G.
    While the geodesic transversal problem focuses on selecting vertices that intersect every max-
imal geodesic, a natural extension of this concept considers the role of edges. In many network
applications, communication and transportation occur through links rather than through individ-
ual nodes\vertices. This observation motivates the introduction of the edge geodesic-transversal
problem.
    An edge geodesic-transversal is a set S ⊆ E(G) such that every maximal geodesic of G passes
through at least one edge of S. An edge geodesic-transversal with minimum cardinality is called
the edge geodesic transversal set or the gte -set and the number of edges in S is called the edge
geodesic transversal number or the gte -number of G, denoted as gte (G). The problem of finding
the gte -number of any given connected graph is known as the edge geodesic transversal problem.
    In this paper, we have solved the edge geodesic transversal problem for r-dimensional hyper-
cube. The r-dimensional hypercube is a fundamental interconnection network that has been widely
investigated in graph theory and parallel computing. Its regular structure, high degree of symmetry,
strong connectivity, and logarithmic diameter enable efficient communication and routing among
networks. For basic properties of the hypercube, the reader is referred to [3], [6] and [7].

3    Preliminaries

Consider a path Pn on n vertices. Clearly, the path is the only maximal geodesic and every single
edge is an edge geodesic transversal set and hence gte (Pn ) = 1. In case of a complete graph
Kn , n ≥ 1, every edge is a maximal geodesic and hence gte (Kn ) = n(n − 1)/2. We begin with the
following lemma which establishes a relationship between gte (G) and gt(G).

Lemma 1. If G is a connected graph, then gte (G) ≥ gt(G).

Proof. Let T be a gte -set of G with cardinality k. Suppose that T = {ei : ui vi |i = 1, 2, ..., k}. Then,
the collection of one of the terminal vertices of these k edges, say S1 = {u1 , u2 , . . . , uk } clearly forms
a geodesic transversal of G. Thus, gt(G) ≤ k = gte (G).

Lemma 2. Let G(V, E) be a connected graph and S = {u1 , u2 , . . . uk } be a gt-set of G. Then,
gte (G) ≤ ki=1 d(ui ), ui ∈ S.

4    Edge cut Transversal Theorem

Let G = (V, E) be a connected graph and X ⊆ V (G). An edge cut is a subset of edges of the form
E[X, V \X], consisting of all edges with one end vertex in X and the other end in the complement

                                                      3
V \X. The removal of an edge cut from G disconnects G. In this section, we derive a relationship
between the cardinality of an edge cut and the edge geodesic transversal number of a graph G,
subject to certain conditions.

Lemma 3. Let G be a connected graph and S be an edge cut of G. If for every maximal geodesic
P in G, |E(P ) ∩ S| = 1, then gte (G) ≤ |S|.

Proof. Assume the contrary that gte (G) > |S| and let S ′ be a gte -set of G such that |S ′ | > |S|.
Clearly, S ′ contains at least one edge e ∈ S ′ \S. If every maximal geodesic through e intersects S,
then S ′ \{e} remains a gte -set which contradicts the minimality of S ′ . Hence, there exists a maximal
geodesic Pe in G containing e such that |E(Pe ) ∩ S| = ∅. But then, |E(P ) ∩ S| = ∅ contradicts the
hypothesis of the lemma and hence gte (G) ≤ |S|.

Lemma 4. If in a connected graph G, there exist k mutually edge disjoint maximal geodesics, then
gte (G) ≥ k.

Proof. Every edge geodesic transversal of G intersects each of k mutually edge disjoint maximal
geodesics of G, hence gte (G) ≥ k.

   From Lemma 3 and 4, we derive the following edge cut transversal theorem which states that if
an edge cut S separates G into two connected components such that every maximal geodesic P of G
contains exactly one edge of S and G admits at least |S| mutually edge disjoint maximal geodesics,
then the edge geodesic transversal number of G is equal to the cardinality of G.

Theorem 5. Edge-cut transversal Theorem
Let S = {e1 , e2 , ..., ek } be an edge cut of G satisfying the following conditions.

  1. The removal of S disconnects G into two connected components G1 and G2 .

  2. |E(P ) ∩ S| = 1, for every maximal geodesic P in G.

  3. G has at least k mutually edge disjoint maximal geodesics.

Then gte (G) = |S|.

differ by exactly one bit. The length of a diametral path of Qr is r [8]. A stacked prism is defined as

Corollary 1. If Qr is a hypercube of r- dimension, then gt(Qr ) = 2r−1 .

Proof. Qr is obtained from two copies of Qr−1 , namely Q0r−1 and Q1r−1 . If x ∈ V (Qr−1 ), then
the corresponding vertices in the two copies of Qr are denoted by 0x and 1x respectively. Hence,
V (Qn ) = {0x : x ∈ V (Qr−1 )} ∪ {1x : x ∈ V (Qr−1 )}. We observe that the set S = {(0x, 1x) : 0x ∈
V (Q0r−1 ), 1x ∈ V (Q1r−1 )} of edges that joins the two copies is an edge cut of Qr . We now claim that
every maximal geodesic of Qr contains exactly one edge of S. Suppose on the contrary, there exist
a maximal geodesic P (x, y) in Qr containing two edges e1 , e2 ∈ S, then both end vertices of P lie
either in Q0r−1 or Q1r−1 . Without loss of generality assume x, y ∈ Q0r−1 . As every maximal geodesic
is a diametral path, l(P ) = r. But then there exists a geodesic P 0 (x, y) that lies entirely in Q0r−1
such that l(P 0 ) ≤ r − 1 since diam(Q0r−1 ) = r − 1. This contradicts the assumption that P is a
maximal geodesic. Thus, for every maximal geodesic P in Qr , |E(P ) ∩ S| = 1.

5    Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce the edge geodesic transversal problem, the edge version of the geodesic
transversal problem and compute an upper and lower bound for the gte -number of G. We also prove
a general result, the edge cut transversal theorem, which has a lot of significance in the computation
of edge geodesic transversal number of several graphs. These results not only broaden the theoretical
understanding of geodesic covering parameters but also provide further scope for investigating the
edge geodesic transversal problem in other graph products, such as the lexicographic and corona
products of graphs.
