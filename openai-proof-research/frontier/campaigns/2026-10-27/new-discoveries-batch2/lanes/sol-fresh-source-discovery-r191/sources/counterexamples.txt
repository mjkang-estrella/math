                                                   AN INFINITE FAMILY OF COUNTEREXAMPLES TO THE
                                                             POLYCIRCULANT CONJECTURE

                                                                     SAUL D. FREEDMAN AND MELISSA LEE




arXiv:2607.23423v2 [math.GR] 19 Oct 2026
                                                    A BSTRACT. We disprove the Polycirculant Conjecture, which states that every
                                                    transitive 2-closed permutation group is non-elusive, i.e. contains a derangement
                                                    of prime order. In fact, we strengthen our result by answering a long-standing
                                                    question of Marušič and Jordan: there exists a vertex-transitive graph admit-
                                                    ting no semiregular automorphism. To do so, we generalise recently developed
                                                    methods of Chen et al. for constructing elusive groups via non-split extensions,
                                                    allowing us to construct an elusive group 76 ¨PSU3 p3q of degree 16,464. We
                                                    show that this group is the full automorphism group of seven of its orbital graphs
                                                    and hence is 2-closed. Our example extends to infinitely many counterexamples
                                                    of the Polycirculant Conjecture, and infinitely many vertex-transitive graphs ad-
                                                    mitting no semiregular automorphism.




                                                                                1. I NTRODUCTION

                                              A derangement is a finite permutation that has no fixed points. A classical theo-
                                           rem of Jordan from the nineteenth century asserts that every finite transitive group
                                           contains a derangement. Using the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, Fein,
                                           Kantor and Schacher [16] showed that Jordan’s theorem may be strengthened to
                                           require the existence of a derangement of prime power order. However, perhaps
                                           surprisingly, it is false that every finite transitive permutation group must contain
                                           a derangement of prime order. For instance, the Mathieu group M11 acting on 12
                                           points has derangements only of orders 4 and 8. Due to the seeming rarity of finite
                                           transitive permutation groups containing no derangements of prime order, such a
                                           group is termed elusive.
                                              Elusive groups have attracted significant interest (see [3, 10, 11, 12, 13, 19, 24,
                                           25] for example), in part due to their connection to the well-known Polycirculant
                                           Conjecture, posed by Klin [14, Problem BCC15.12] in 1997. Recall that the 2-
                                           closure of a permutation group G ď SympΩq is the largest subgroup of SympΩq
                                           with the same orbits on Ω ˆ Ω as G, and that G is 2-closed if it is its own 2-closure.

Conjecture 1.1 (The Polycirculant Conjecture). No elusive group is 2-closed.

    This conjecture extends the earlier Semiregularity Problem, formulated by Marušič
[31] and Jordan [29] in the 1980s. A non-trivial permutation is called semiregular
if all of its cycles are the same length (so in particular, it has no trivial cycles).
Problem 1.2 (The Semiregularity Problem). Does there exist a vertex-transitive
directed graph admitting no semiregular automorphism?

   This problem includes the case where the arc set of the directed graph is sym-
metric, so that the graph can be considered undirected. Observe that each derange-
ment of prime order is semiregular, and each semiregular permutation has a power
equal to a derangement of prime order. Hence the Semiregularity Problem asks if
there is a vertex-transitive directed graph whose full automorphism group is elu-
sive. Note that, by definition, the automorphism group of any finite directed graph
is 2-closed. Hence a positive answer to the Semiregularity Problem would imply
that the Polycirculant Conjecture is false. On the other hand, there exist 2-closed
groups that are not the full automorphism groups of any directed graph, such as the
subgroup C2 ˆ C2 of S4 ; additional examples are given in [5, 7, 20]. Therefore,
a counterexample to the Polycirculant Conjecture might not provide an answer to
the Semiregularity Problem.
   The Polycirculant Conjecture and Semiregularity Problem have received con-
siderable attention across dozens of papers; see [2] for a survey of results. Perhaps
most notably, Giudici [18] determined all elusive permutation groups with at least
one transitive minimal normal subgroup (hence all elusive quasiprimitive groups)
and showed that each satisfies the Polycirculant Conjecture. A few years later,
Giudici and Xu [22] proved that the conjecture is satisfied for every group that is
biquasiprimitive, i.e. transitive with each minimal normal subgroup having at most
two orbits. Hence in any counterexample to the conjecture, each minimal normal
subgroup must be intransitive, and at least one must have at least three orbits.
   Recently, Chen et al. [15] described a novel method of constructing elusive
groups via non-split extensions, and used the method to construct the first known
examples of elusive groups with odd and twice-odd degree. Their examples again
satisfy the Polycirculant Conjecture.
   In this paper, we further the methods of Chen et al. to construct the first known
groups that are counterexamples to the Polycirculant Conjecture, and the first known
graphs that are positive answers to the Semiregularity Problem. In fact, we con-
struct infinitely many such groups and graphs.
   To state our main results, we require the following notation. We will write N ¨G
to denote a non-split extension of a group N by a group G, with N : G denoting
a split extension of N by G (not to be confused with the index |G : H| in G of
a subgroup H, nor the set rG : Hs of right cosets of H in G). Additionally, for
a prime p and a positive integer a, we write pa to denote the elementary abelian
group of order pa . Recall also that an orbital O of a permutation group G on a set

Remark 1.4. A well-known open problem of Lovász [30] asks whether every
vertex-transitive graph has a Hamiltonian path. This problem is often stated as a
conjecture in the affirmative, and it has been similarly conjectured (see [8, pp. 162–
163] and [23, p. 46]) that, apart from five known counterexamples, every vertex-
transitive graph has a Hamiltonian cycle. On the other hand, Babai [6] conjectured
that infinitely many counterexamples exist. As certain methods of constructing
Hamiltonian paths and cycles rely on the existence of semiregular automorphisms
(see [1]), it is natural to test the graphs ΓO from Theorem 1.3 against these con-
jectures, especially since the valency of each ΓO is small compared to its order.
However, straightforward computations in SageMath [33] show that each of these
graphs has a Hamiltonian cycle.

   Theorem 1.3, together with the elementary result Proposition 2.4 on direct prod-
ucts and wreath products of 2-closed groups and elusive groups, immediately yields
the following corollary.
Corollary 1.5. The elusive permutation group X is 2-closed. Moreover, for each
integer k ě 1, any group constructed from k copies of X via direct products and/or
wreath products is isomorphic to a 2-closed elusive group of degree 16,464k . These
groups are therefore all counterexamples to the Polycirculant Conjecture.

   As discussed in Section 3, this corollary gives a positive answer to [15, Problem
5.2] on counterexamples to the Polycirculant Conjecture constructed via non-split
extensions with quasiprimitive quotients.

   We also need some theory concerning graph products to provide the machinery
for our proof of Corollary 1.7. Let Γ1 “ pV1 , E1 q and Γ2 “ pV2 , E2 q be two
simple graphs, i.e. without loops or multiple edges. We recall two well-known
graph products. The Cartesian product Γ1 ˝ Γ2 and strong product Γ1 b Γ2 each
have vertex set
         V pΓ1 ˝ Γ2 q “ V pΓ1 b Γ2 q “ tpv, wq | v P V pΓ1 q, w P V pΓ2 qu.
Two distinct vertices pv, wq and pv 1 , w1 q are adjacent in the Cartesian product if
and only if either v “ v 1 and w is adjacent to w1 in Γ2 , or w “ w1 and v is adjacent
to v 1 in Γ1 . They are adjacent in the strong product if and only if v and v 1 are either
equal or adjacent in Γ1 and w and w1 are either equal or adjacent in Γ2 . Hence
Γ1 ˝ Γ2 is a subgraph of Γ1 b Γ2 .
   A graph is called prime with respect to one of these graph products if it is non-
trivial and cannot be written as the product of two non-trivial graphs. If a graph
is non-trivial and not prime, we say that it is decomposable with respect to that
product. We further say that two graphs are relatively prime with respect to a
graph product if there is no non-trivial graph that (up to isomorphism) is a factor of
both of them. We now prove some properties of graphs constructed from Cartesian
and strong products.

4.2. Proof of Corollary 1.7. Let T be the set of seven pairwise non-isomorphic
orbital graphs corresponding to the set U of orbitals from Computational Propo-
sition 4.2. Additionally, let pΓi | i P Nq be any infinite sequence of graphs
with Γi P T for each i and Γ1 ‰ Γ2 . We define an associated infinite family
G :“ pGi | i P Nq of graphs by G1 :“ Γ1 and, for each i P N,
               G2i :“ G2i´1 ˝ Γ2i ,         and        G2i`1 :“ G2i b Γ2i`1 .
We claim that each member of G is a connected, vertex-transitive graph admitting
no semiregular automorphism. Our computations reveal that, in each graph in T ,
no two vertices have the same closed neigbourhoods. Moreover, [26, Theorem
6.13] shows that if any graph Γ P T is not prime with respect to the Cartesian
product, then its automorphism group is a direct product of wreath products. By
[26, Lemma 7.2, Theorem 7.18] and the above closed neighbourhood property, the
same is true for the strong product. However, Computational Proposition 4.2 shows
that X is the full automorphism group of Γ. The unique proper non-trivial normal
subgroup of X is N – 76 , which has no complement in X. Hence X cannot be
written as a direct product, nor as a single wreath product. Therefore, Γ must be
prime with respect to the Cartesian and strong products.
   We now proceed via induction. Suppose that, for some i P N, the graph Gi is
connected and vertex-transitive, that no two of its vertices have equal closed neigh-
bourhoods, and that it does not admit any semiregular automorphism. We imme-
diately deduce from Proposition 2.5 that Gi`1 is connected and vertex-transitive,
and that no two of its vertices have equal closed neighbourhoods. By applying the
previous paragraph when i “ 1, or Proposition 2.6 and the definition of Gi when
i ą 1, we see that Gi is prime with respect to the product used to construct Gi`1 ,
and also relatively prime to Γi`1 . As each graph in T admits no semiregular au-
tomorphism, it follows from Proposition 2.5(2)–(3) and our inductive hypothesis
that Gi`1 also admits no semiregular automorphism. Using Proposition 2.6 and
the fact that swapping Γ1 and Γ2 does not change the graphs in G, it is easy to
see that, for each integer n ě 2, there is a two-to-one correspondence between the
choices of Γ1 , . . . , Γn and the resulting graphs G1 , . . . , Gn (up to isomorphism).
Since |V pGi q| “ 16,464i for each i ě 1, we obtain Corollary 1.7.
Remark 4.5. Let Λ1 and Λ2 be two vertex-transitive (or, more generally, regu-
lar) graphs. By the definitions of their Cartesian and strong products, the valency
valpΛ1 ˝ Λ2 q of Λ1 ˝ Λ2 is equal to valpΛ1 q ` valpΛ2 q, while valpΛ1 b Λ2 q ` 1 “
