                                           1    Introduction
                                           All graphs are finite and, unless stated otherwise, simple and undirected. We write V (G) and
                                           E(G) for the vertex and edge sets of a graph G. Let T be a uniformly chosen spanning tree of a
                                           connected graph G. For distinct edges e, f ∈ E(G),

                                                                       P(e, f ∈ E(T )) ≤ P(e ∈ E(T ))P(f ∈ E(T )).

                                           Thus, the edge indicators are pairwise negatively correlated; see [6, Section 1]. A natural question
                                           is whether the same inequality holds when the spanning tree is replaced by a uniformly chosen
                                           forest or connected spanning subgraph.
                                               For a graph G, define

                                                              F(G) := {(V (G), X) : X ⊆ E(G), (V (G), X) is acyclic},
                                                              C(G) := {(V (G), X) : X ⊆ E(G), (V (G), X) is connected}.

                                           A forest in F(G) need not be connected or maximal. In particular, (V (G), ∅) ∈ F(G). Choosing
                                           uniformly from F(G) gives the uniform forest measure. For connected G, the uniform measure on
                                           C(G) is the uniform connected-subgraph measure. For X ⊆ E(G), let FX (G) and CX (G) denote
                                           the respective subfamilies consisting of subgraphs that contain every edge of X. Following Oxley
                                           [10, Section 15.8, p. 603], define

                                                                        iX (G) := |FX (G)|,       jX (G) := |CX (G)|.

                                           We omit empty subscripts and subscript braces. Thus, i(G) = i∅ (G), ie (G) = i{e} (G), and
                                           ief (G) = i{e,f } (G). The forest and connected-subgraph conjectures take the following form.

                                           Conjecture ([6], Conjecture 1.1). For every finite graph G and distinct edges e, f ∈ E(G),

                                                           ie (G)if (G) − ief (G)i(G) ≥ 0,        je (G)jf (G) − jef (G)j(G) ≥ 0.

                                               These inequalities express pairwise negative correlation under the uniform forest measure
                                           and, for connected G, under the uniform connected-subgraph measure. This property is called
                                           edge-negative-association in [6]. For the forest conjecture, Pemantle [11, Section 3.1] records a

question raised by Peter Winkler. Oxley [10, p. 603] attributes this conjecture to Kahn [9] and
the connected-subgraph conjecture to Grimmett and S. N. Winkler.
   We study both conjectures on complete bipartite graphs, allowing their two part sizes to
vary independently. Throughout, let L and R be disjoint sets with |L| = m and |R| = n. The
complete bipartite graph Km,n has vertex set L ∪ R and edge set
                                        
                            E(Km,n ) = {u, v} : u ∈ L, v ∈ R .

Unless stated otherwise, the part sizes satisfy m, n ≥ 2. Our first theorem proves the forest
conjecture for complete bipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.1. For integers m, n ≥ 2 and distinct edges e, f of Km,n ,

                           ie (Km,n )if (Km,n ) − ief (Km,n )i(Km,n ) > 0.

   Our second theorem gives the corresponding result for connected spanning subgraphs.

Theorem 1.2. For integers m, n ≥ 2 and distinct edges e, f of Km,n ,

                          je (Km,n )jf (Km,n ) − jef (Km,n )j(Km,n ) > 0.

    If either part has size one, every spanning subgraph is a forest, so the edge indicators are
independent under the uniform forest measure. The only connected spanning subgraph is Km,n
itself. Hence, both correlation differences are zero.
    Negative correlation for uniform matroid bases was studied by Seymour and Welsh [13], and
Feder and Mihail [5] introduced balanced matroids. For uniform forests, Grimmett and Winkler
[6, Theorem 1.3] verified the conjecture for graphs with at most eight vertices and for nine-vertex
graphs with at most eighteen edges. For arbitrary graphs, Brändén and Huh [2, Propositions 4.21
and 4.25] proved ief (G)i(G) ≤ 2ie (G)if (G). However, the conjecture requires the factor 1.
    For complete graphs, Stark [14] proved the forest conjecture for sufficiently large orders, and
Gupta [7, Theorem 1.2] established it for every Kn with n ≥ 2. For complete bipartite graphs,
Stark [16, Theorem 1] obtained an asymptotic formula for i(Km,n ) as m → ∞, with m ≤ n and
n = o(m6/5 ). In an earlier preprint, Stark [15, Theorem 3] proved strict negative correlation
for adjacent edges as m → ∞, with m ≤ n and n = o(m8/7 ), leaving the disjoint-edge case
unresolved. For enumeration, Stones [17] gives an algorithm for counting forests of Km,n with
a prescribed number of edges. Other forest results concern adjacent edges at sufficiently large
common edge weights on each fixed finite connected graph [8, Theorem 1] and strict negative
correlation for uniform forests on wheel graphs [1, Theorem 1.1].
    For connected spanning subgraphs, Tang and Zhang [18, Part I, Theorem 1.4] established
the uniform inequality for sufficiently large complete graphs. Çiçeksiz and Ravichandran [3,
Corollary 1.10] proved the uniform inequality for complete graphs of every order. The random-
cluster result of Tang and Zhang for high-degree regular graphs [19, Theorem 1.2, Corollary 6.2
and Section 6.3] includes the uniform connected-subgraph measure on Kn,n for sufficiently large
n. This regular-graph result does not cover Km,n with m ̸= n.
    Weighted versions are known for several graph classes. Semple and Welsh [12, Proposition 3.7
and Theorem 4.4] proved the forest and connected-subgraph inequalities for graphs obtained by
series and parallel extensions from graphs whose maximal 2-connected subgraphs are K4 or its
minors; this class includes K2,n . Wagner [20, Theorem 5.8(b),(c) and Example 5.2] established
preservation under two-sums and reported a computational verification of the weighted forest
inequality for K3,4 . Erickson [4, Theorem 1.4] expressed the unnormalized forest correlation
difference for series-parallel graphs as a sum of monomials times squares.
    For K3,3 , Erickson’s calculation for the cographic matroid [4, Section 5], together with [12,
Proposition 3.3], implies negative correlation for the connected-subgraph measure under arbitrary
positive edge weights. At unit weights, Erickson’s basis lower bound equals the unnormalized

                                                 2
spanning-tree correlation difference for K3,3 , which is positive for every edge pair. Thus, the
uniform connected-subgraph inequality is strict. These results already cover negative correlation
for connected spanning subgraphs of K2,n and K3,3 .
    The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 proves that two-edge inclusion counts are largest
for disjoint edge pairs, even with the total number of edges fixed. Edge transitivity then reduces
both main theorems to these pairs. In Section 3, we enumerate forests subject to component
restrictions by a signed count of directed graphs. Edge-deletion and path-deletion bijections,
together with recurrence relations, give formulas for the edge-inclusion counts. These formulas
reduce the proof of Theorem 1.1 to showing that an explicit expression is positive. In Section 4,
we group connected spanning subgraphs by their restriction outside the endpoints of two disjoint
edges. Möbius inversion expresses a weighted sum of extension counts in terms of products over
the components of the restriction. Product inequalities for its components yield an inequality
for the extension counts with a parameter independent of the fixed subgraph. Summing these
inequalities proves Theorem 1.2 for m, n ≥ 3. The cases m = 2 or n = 2 follow from direct
counting. Together with the comparisons in Section 2, these estimates give strict negative
correlation between any two distinct edges of a fixed copy of K2,2 after conditioning on the
subgraph induced by the remaining vertices; see Corollary 4.6.


2    Reduction to disjoint edge pairs
We reduce both main theorems to disjoint edge pairs by comparing joint edge-inclusion counts
for the two uniform measures.
    Write uv for the edge {u, v}. For U ⊆ V (G), the graph G − U is obtained by deleting U and
all incident edges. The induced subgraph G[U ] has vertex set U and all edges of G with both
endpoints in U ; the same convention applies to digraphs, with arcs in place of edges. For an
edge set X whose endpoints lie in V (G), define

                 G − X := (V (G), E(G) \ X),               G + X := (V (G), E(G) ∪ X);

we abbreviate G ± {e} to G ± e. Let Comp(G) denote the set of connected components of G,
including isolated vertices, and put ω(G) := | Comp(G)|. Two edges are disjoint if they have no
common endpoint. In subscripts, commas separate distinct edges written by their endpoints; for
example, jab,cd (G) = j{ab,cd} (G).

       ie (Km,n )if (Km,n ) − ief (Km,n )i(Km,n ) ≥ ie′ (Km,n )if ′ (Km,n ) − ie′ f ′ (Km,n )i(Km,n ),

Proof. Automorphisms of Km,n obtained by independently permuting L and R act transitively
on edges and on disjoint edge pairs, and preserve forests and connected subgraphs. Thus, the
one-edge inclusion counts are equal for all edges, and all disjoint edge pairs have the same two-edge
inclusion counts. By Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2 at t = 1, the two-edge inclusion counts
are largest for disjoint pairs. Since i(Km,n ), j(Km,n ) ≥ 0, the assertion follows.

3     The uniform forest measure
We prove Theorem 1.1 by computing i(Km,n ), ie (Km,n ), and ief (Km,n ) for disjoint edges e, f .
Deleting an edge from a forest separates its endpoints. This suggests counting forests in which
each component contains at most one vertex of a prescribed set. The component-partition
identities in Proposition 2.1 then express the disjoint-edge count in terms of these forests. We
enumerate these forests by a signed count of directed graphs.
