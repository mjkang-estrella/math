## Independently checked universal proof

Set v(0)=+∞; for nonzero x set v(x)=−ω(x) in the first kind, and v(x)=ω(x) in the second. These give an integer-valued multiplicative min valuation: v(xy)=v(x)+v(y) and v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)), with equality when the summands have distinct values. In particular v(1)=v(−1)=0, since additivity gives v(1)=2v(1) and 2v(−1)=v(1). No characteristic restriction enters. Let a=v(c_0)=−m or m, respectively. Both source inequalities become n v(c_j)≥(n−j)a; c_0,c_n are nonzero, and v(c_n)=0.

Use integer-scaled weights to avoid any need to extend the value group: for a nonzero polynomial P=Σ p_iX^i define B(P)=min_{p_i≠0}(n v(p_i)+ia), and let L(P),U(P) be the least and greatest attaining indices. I verified the following product lemma in full, including possible cancellation:

    B(PQ)=B(P)+B(Q),
    L(PQ)=L(P)+L(Q), U(PQ)=U(P)+U(Q).

Every product term has weight at least B(P)+B(Q). At degree L(P)+L(Q), a term has that weight only if both its indices minimize their own weights. Since both indices are at least their respective least minimizers, precisely one pair has this degree: (L(P),L(Q)). Its product is nonzero in a domain. All other nonzero terms in that coefficient have strictly larger valuations (the degree term ia is fixed). Their finite sum has valuation strictly larger than the unique minimal term, so adding it cannot cancel that term. Every coefficient below this degree has all terms of weight strictly larger than the bound, hence its weight is larger too, or its coefficient is zero. This proves B and L. Applying the identical argument at the greatest minimizing indices, with coefficients above their sum, proves U. Empty coefficient sums have value +∞. The reasoning allows equal valuations and arbitrarily extensive cancellation away from these two extremes.

For f both endpoints have weight na and all other coefficients have at least that weight, so L(f)=0 and U(f)=n. In fact no interior coefficient ties: n v(c_j)=(n−j)a would imply n divides j, using gcd(n,a)=1. This strictness is not needed for the endpoint argument.

Suppose f=gh with degrees s,t>0. Degree additivity in a domain gives s+t=n. The least-index identity forces L(g)=L(h)=0. The greatest-index identity and U(g)≤s,U(h)≤t force U(g)=s,U(h)=t. Thus the constant and leading coefficients of g are nonzero and have equal weights:

    n v(g_0) = n v(g_s)+s a.

Hence n divides s a. Since gcd(n,a)=1, n divides s, contrary to 0<s<n. This proves all n>1 and both kinds for every integral domain admitting the stated valuation. It uses no factorization property of R, normality, Noetherianity, separability, characteristic bound, completion, or numerical inference. Candidate PROOF.md's rational-weight formulation is equivalent and complete.

## Complete bridge to the published earlier theorem

This is the substantive reason for exclusion, not the length of the preceding proof. I read El Fadil, *A generalization of Eisenstein–Schönemann’s irreducibility criterion*, Tatra Mt. Math. Publ. 83 (2023), 51–60, definitions pp52–54, actual Theorem 3.1 p55, and its proof p56. The latter applies the Newton-polygon product theorem to monic factors, bounds the number of irreducible factors by gcd(height,length), and bounds each factor degree by length/gcd times deg(φ). It is not merely an abstract promising a related result. Fresh pdftotext extraction from the copied PDF of physical pages 5–6 is retained as elfadil-pages55-56.txt. Overbars are lost in text extraction: the statement's “f is a power of φ” concerns reductions modulo the maximal ideal, as the surrounding notation explains.

Here are all substitutions and hypotheses needed for this target:

1. Put K=Frac(R). Extend the signed valuation by v(b/c)=v(b)−v(c), for c≠0, and v(0)=+∞. If b/c=b'/c', equality bc'=b'c proves equality of the assigned values. Products are immediate. A common denominator and the min inequality on R prove the min inequality on K, including zero numerators. Thus it is a field valuation, even though first-kind R generally is not contained in its valuation ring.
2. The finite image on K× is a subgroup of Z. It is nonzero because v(c_0)=±m≠0, hence equals dZ for a positive integer d. Define ν=v/d, with image exactly Z. This is a normalized rank-one discrete valuation. Necessarily d divides m. Put M=m/d>0. Any common divisor of n and M divides n and m, so gcd(n,M)=1. This is the height required by the published theorem; silently assuming d=1 would be a gap, but neither proof does so.
3. For second kind take P=f/c_n ∈ K[X]. It is monic of degree n; ν(P_0)=M and for each j<n, nν(P_j)≥(n−j)M. Consequently every nonleading nonzero coefficient has strictly positive valuation and lies in the maximal ideal of the valuation ring A={x∈K:v(x)≥0}; zero coefficients also lie there. The leading coefficient is 1, so P∈A[X] and its reduction is X^n.
4. For first kind take P=X^n f(1/X)/c_0. Division by c_0, rather than c_n, makes P monic. At degree k its coefficient is c_{n−k}/c_0. From n v(c_{n−k})≥−km obtain n v(P_k)≥(n−k)m. Its constant has value m, and its leading coefficient is 1. After division by d it has exactly the same positive normalized height M, valuation-ring membership, and reduction X^n as in step 3. In particular applying the theorem directly to f with negative height would not suffice; reversal and this normalization are necessary here.
5. In El Fadil's notation use φ=X, whose reduction is monic irreducible of degree 1 over any residue field. Its φ-expansion is the ordinary power expansion of P. Theorem 3.1 requires ν(P_i)≥ν(P_0)−iλ with λ=ν(P_0)/n=M/n; these are precisely the inequalities just proved. Reduction is φ^n, and gcd(ν(P_0),n)=1. Therefore the theorem's factor degree lower bound is n and its factor number bound is 1 over K^h. In particular P is irreducible over K. No extra assumption that the original K is henselian, perfect, or a number field occurs in Theorem 3.1. Its valuation-ring/monic Gauss step is over the integrally closed valuation ring after extension, not over the arbitrary R.
6. For second kind scalar multiplication transfers a positive-degree factorization of f over K to one of P. For first kind f(0)=c_0≠0, so both factors in any such factorization have nonzero constants. If f=gh, with degrees s,t and s+t=n, then X^s g(1/X) and X^t h(1/X) have degrees exactly s,t: their leading coefficients are g(0),h(0), both nonzero. Their product is X^n f(1/X), so dividing one by c_0 gives a positive-degree factorization of P. Finally R embeds into K and this embedding preserves nonzero leading coefficients and degrees. A positive-degree factorization in R[X] would thus give one in K[X], impossible. No converse Gauss lemma on R is used.

All-degree, both-sign, non-UFD and nonnormal scope therefore survives the comparison. Theorem 3.1 is an actual earlier stronger field statement after elementary transformations, and the missing transformations are now completely supplied. This suffices for routine substantive exclusion under the batch rule.

## Strongest objection and boundary controls

The introduction p3 explicitly notes that reducibility over Q(A) need not descend to A for arbitrary integral domains, then claims the general approach cannot simply follow field methods. The first warning is valid. It does not obstruct the present argument: field **irreducibility implies absence of positive-degree domain factors** under the injective coefficient map. That direction is always valid. The countercase would matter if this review tried to infer reducibility over R from a K-factorization; it does not. I interpret the source's justification as conflating the directions insofar as it is offered as an obstruction to proving this specific conjecture. This is a mathematical scope assessment, not a claim about the author's intentions or every other result in the paper.

The integer-valued premise is indispensable to the numerical gcd test. In R=F[t], ω=degree_t/2 has Γ=(1/2)N₀; f=X²−t² has ω(c_0)=1 coprime to 2 and zero intermediate coefficient, yet factors. Thus Remark 1.3/2.10's possible Γ extensions do not enlarge the proven target. Similarly height 2 and degree 2 with the usual degree valuation violate gcd and factor as (X−t)(X+t); in characteristic 2 this becomes a repeated factor, not an exception to the proof. Constant nonunit factors, zero intermediate coefficients, and positive characteristic are all accounted for above.

Garg–Singh arXiv:2511.21427v2 is later than Širola (v2 dated 12 June 2026). Its Theorem 1 at j=n,k=0 gives another field route after the same value-group checks and the strict interior inequalities. However its printed general Lemma 5 asserts ig∉Γ even though g∈Γ, an immediate impossibility; its proof instead uses γ=g/N. I do not import that lemma or rely on a silently repaired version. El Fadil 2023 and the independent ordinary proof suffice. No claim is made that El Fadil or Garg–Singh explicitly published a resolution naming Širola's conjecture.
