## What is old and what is added

Family098 already supplies the fixed doubling Hilbert obstruction, compactness extraction of finite witnesses, finite-dimensional Dvoretzky placement into arbitrary infinite-dimensional Banach spaces, and separated shrinking-cluster assembly with a universal doubling bound. I read its complete main statement, source-set doubling construction, and compact Banach corollary proof. These already settle the disconnected compact version at the full Banach scope. None is counted as CDF098's contribution. Summable tours of finite segment graphs and the addition of a spine are elementary once those graphs have the required geometry.

The additional operation is the finite lemma: **every finite D-doubling Hilbert set has an ambient connected finite polygonal thickening in H⊕R, containing the original set isometrically, with doubling constant bounded only by D**. The candidate gives 256D^10. It does not require a bound on cardinality, minimum separation, span dimension, or the total length of the finite thickening. Those absences are necessary because the source's finite witnesses have no uniform cardinality or aspect-ratio bound.

The operation couples a metric net hierarchy to a new *geometric* coordinate: a scale-s vertex is placed at height s and its parent edge stays between heights s and 2s. This supplies direct control of ambient balls even at edge interiors. A ball high above the base meets only boundedly many comparable scales. Near the base, all sufficiently fine edges are close to their original X endpoints and can be grouped using X's doubling cover; the remaining scales are few and their separated endpoints admit packing bounds. Bottom vertical segments have a separate product-cover argument. Thus the added coordinate is doing more than displaying an existing abstract net tree.

This is a substantive additional construction for the campaign: it resolves the missing ambient connected-completion operation uniformly on arbitrary finite witnesses. The proof is short and uses elementary packing, but it changes the admissible geometry without changing the witness distances. It is not a numerical constant improvement, parameter choice, target-space transfer already in the source, or a routine inference from finite length. The ensuing curve can retain the full nonembedding obstruction precisely because no distortion of its bottom witness is incurred by thickening.

## Actual primary operation comparisons

### Gupta–Talwar: doubling geodesic completion

I read the relevant statements, full structure theorem proof, and full graph/spanner proof in the **latest retrieved v2**, not just v1: [arXiv:0712.3331v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3331v2), Sections 1, 3 and 4. Their net hierarchies, scale separation and packing are antecedents and should be credited. Theorem13 characterizes doubling of the **shortest-path convex closure** by controlling long edges incident near a vertex. The graph construction modifies a bounded-degree spanner through edge donation to obtain that control while preserving original vertex distances up to 1+epsilon.

This is not an ambient Hilbert completion theorem. In its metric, getting near an edge interior requires traveling along an edge from an endpoint. In an ambient norm ball, segments can pass close to the center while both endpoints are far away. The intrinsic long-edge proof therefore cannot be invoked after drawing the graph in Hilbert space; a Lipschitz straight-line realization does not by itself preserve doubling. Nor does the theorem furnish a Hilbert embedding of its whole convex closure with a controlled distortion. CDF098 supplies a different invariant, the physical height of every edge, that directly bounds which large scales can enter an ambient ball. I found no same-operation deduction from the inspected proof that would make this invariant unnecessary.

### Semmes: the previously unread comparator is resolved

I rendered and visually read the **entire Section6**, printed pages391–396 (PDF pages55–60), of [Semmes1996, DOI10.4171/RMI/201](https://doi.org/10.4171/RMI/201). Theorem1.15 and the introductory definitions were also inspected. This removes the author's concrete unread-primary caveat; it is not an exclusion inferred from the abstract.

Theorem6.3 starts with a snowflake E in R^n of order s>1 and defines w(x)=dist(x,E)^{n(s−1)}. It extends the prescribed metric on E, up to constants, to the weighted path metric D_w. Its proof partitions paths into maximal dyadic intervals away from E; the metric on E controls their chain costs. Porosity and weight estimates establish the strong A_infinity property. Combined with Assouad's snowflake embedding, this proves Theorem1.15 for arbitrary doubling spaces.

That operation creates a connected abstract doubling ambient metric by changing lengths in Euclidean space. It does not preserve an existing Hilbert realization of E or realize D_w as the restricted norm of a Hilbert space. Applying it to the source's finite witnesses gives an abstract completion but still leaves exactly the Hilbert-realization interface needed for Dvoretzky on the whole graph. The source's Dvoretzky step only applies to finite-dimensional normed spans, not arbitrary weighted path geometries. Section6 does not contain the lifted-segment ambient-ball lemma. Its general extension-principle remark is not a theorem supplying this missing realization.

### Schul and the later net-based curve constructions

I read Schul's statements and complete sufficient-direction construction, Section4, in [math/0602675v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0602675v2). It uses local farthest insertion, flat/nonflat cases, virtual segments and Jones beta-number length accounting to produce a finite-length connected superset. It supplies no bound for the output's ambient doubling constant in terms of the input's doubling constant. Finite length is insufficient for such a bound: a union of arbitrarily many short orthogonal spokes has finite length but unbounded covering multiplicity at the common endpoint. This observation does not claim that every Schul output fails doubling; it identifies the additional conclusion not provided by the read operation.

For a later operational comparison I read Naples's Theorem C and the beginning/main geometric construction in Section6.1, [2002.07570v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07570v1). It connects successive nets using edges, frozen bridges and flat/nonflat cases; its output is compact, connected and length-controlled. No uniform output-doubling conclusion is present in the inspected statement or construction. I did not read its entire length proof. David–Schul's [1902.04030v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04030v2), Theorem A, **assumes** the curve is doubling. Badger–Naples–Vellis [1806.01197v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01197v3), inspected introduction and proof overview, develops parametrizations and Hölder control, not the needed ambient doubling output. The latter two were scoped statement/overview screens, not full proof audits.

These comparisons positively locate the extra operation. The verdict does not rest on failure to find the exact title or on calling the final target stronger. The graph-completion and curve-construction antecedents each provide part of the desired package; the candidate's finite lemma supplies the missing simultaneous ambient realization and covering control.
