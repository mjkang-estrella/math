## Target and dependency boundary

For every real C>0 the target requires a finite nonconstant total Boolean function f, zero at the zero input, invariant under a subgroup transitive on **all raw input coordinates**, with bs(f,0)>C s(f)^2. Here s is ordinary sensitivity maximized over every Boolean input. In addition, one fixed alpha>2 must work for a sequence of such functions F_m with bs(F_m,0)>=s(F_m)^alpha and diverging zero-input block sensitivity.

## Independent deductions

**Orientation, degree, and forbidden sets.** For prime p=3 mod4, −1 is nonsquare, so every nonzero field element lies in exactly one of H or −H, where H is the multiplicative group of nonzero squares. This gives exactly one orientation between two distinct vectors, using their first differing coordinate. For each first difference j there are r choices for the oriented difference and p^(h−j) choices for the suffix, hence the outdegree is r sum_j p^(h−j)=(p^h−1)/2, independent of the row.

For a head-constant set I, partition by first coordinate. If distinct fibers c and c' both point to fiber e, any fixed v in the latter has incoming labels e−c and e−c'; these cannot both equal m_v. Thus the quotient tournament has indegree at most one, so its number b of vertices satisfies b(b−1)/2<=b and b<=3, including empty and singleton cases. Within each fiber the unchanged labels and head assignments satisfy the lower-dimensional condition. Induction from h=0 gives |I|<=3^h. Labels of vertices with no incoming edge impose no extra requirement and do not invalidate the induction. At h=4, every head-constant set has size at most81, which suffices to exclude82 rows.

**Simultaneous symmetry and raw coordinates.** For lambda in H and b in F_p^4, the first differing coordinate is unchanged by v↦lambda v+b, its difference multiplies by lambda, and square membership is preserved. Thus orientation and the exact gate label transform together. The action on (v,c) sends it to (lambda v+b,lambda c). Given (v,c) and (w,e), lambda=e/c is in H and b=w−lambda v gives the required move. Composition and inversion remain within this affine group.

At level zero, equal-block permutations and independent within-block permutations preserve the set of Hamming-ball centers. They preserve the minimum-distance function D and hence every threshold predicate simultaneously; they are transitive on all LM bits. At a higher level, independent child automorphisms preserve every child index, while the affine group permutes whole children with their identical internal indexing. Covariance sends each complete clause to another complete clause, at every parent index. Moving the child first and its internal bit second proves transitivity on all N_l raw bits. The same group preserves the whole nested family, not just one predicate, which handles the two different child indices in each clause. No averaging of Boolean functions is used.

**Definitions and base bounds.** The largest radius is d, while distinct centers are at distance2(d+2). A rejecting input can be one successful bit flip from at most one center: otherwise their distance would be at most2d+2. There are at most R+1<=L such flips. The one-side bound LM is the entire input dimension. The function D is integer-valued and 1-Lipschitz across cube edges. A flip therefore cannot change two predicates of distinct integer radii in the same direction; both initial joint profiles vanish. These assertions cover all raw inputs, including points outside every relevant ball. The threshold indices remain valid because 1<=Q−q<Q. Increasing parent q decreases the child gate index and strengthens its required zero condition. Each clause uses distinct children for its r targets and A gates.

**Four arbitrary-input recurrence inequalities.** Write S_b,J_b for child profiles. At an accepting parent input fix a satisfied clause; every output-changing flip must break that same clause, so S_new,1<=A S_0+r S_1. For a joint accepting transition at q<q', fix a clause initially true at stronger index q'. The gate indices satisfy g'=Q−q'<g=Q−q<Q. If a target is not broken, breaking even the weaker parent clause forces its gate g from zero to one; nesting forces g' to change also. The gate cost is therefore A J_0, giving J_new,1<=A J_0+r S_1. This reasoning fixes the witness before counting flips.

At a rejecting input fix the initial lists T of clauses with exactly one failed target and no failed gate, and G with exactly one failed gate and no failed target. A raw bit affects at most one condition of each fixed clause. Every newly true clause must therefore belong to these lists, whether or not the same bit also affects other clauses.

For u,v in T and edge u→v, u's passing gate says the child at a(u,v) is zero at a weaker index, hence also zero at strongest index Q. Since row v has exactly one failed target, this label equals its unique failed target position. Thus T is head-constant and |T|<=81<82. Its repair contribution is at most t S_0.

For G, all target children of each member row are true. Each internal outgoing edge therefore fails a gate of its tail. Since every G clause has exactly one failed gate, all induced outdegrees are at most one and |G|<=3. There is at most one induced-outdegree-zero member. Every other member's unique failed gate points into a G row with strongest target Q initially one. Repairing its weaker gate makes Q zero also, so it costs J_1 for two distinct indices. The possible exceptional member costs S_1. Hence S_new,0<=t S_0+S_1+3J_1. Counting three joint contributions in addition to an exceptional one is a safe overcount.

For joint rejecting transitions at q<q', use the initial T,G lists for weaker q. A clause finally true at stronger q' is also finally true at q, so must be on these same lists. For a G witness the initially failed gate g is one and its smaller index g' is also one; final truth at q' requires g'=0 and hence g=0. This supplies two changed distinct child predicates even for the exceptional G member. Thus J_new,0<=t S_0+3J_1. Additional unrepaired failures at q' exclude a witness rather than create uncounted flips. Union bounds are legitimate even when clauses share children. If no parent index pair exists, the joint maxima are zero. This rederives all four recurrences for the changed parameters at every input.

**Normalization and integer parameters.** M>=p²/2 follows from A=(p^4−1)/2>=p^4/4 for p>=2; A<=M² by definition. Thus r/M<=1/p and t/M<=164/p²<=1/p when p>=167. Primes3 mod4 are unbounded: a prime factor3 mod4 of4P−1 is outside any given finite product P of such primes. For a chosen depth d, take p>=max(167,3^(d+1)). This yields epsilon=max(r,t)/M<=2/3^(d+1).

Dividing by M^(l+b) gives ordinary bounds (1+r/M)u and (1+t/M)u+3v, and joint bounds v+(r/M)u and 3v+(t/M)u. They imply exactly u_l<=(1+epsilon)u_(l−1)+3v_(l−1), v_l<=epsilon u_(l−1)+3v_(l−1). For equality comparison sequences U_0=L,V_0=0, induction using earlier U<=2L gives V_l<=epsilon L(3^l−1). Summing U increments using only already established earlier bounds gives U_l<=L+epsilon L[(3/2)(3^l−1)−l]<2L for l<=d. There is no circular induction. Therefore S_l,b<=2L M^(l+b).

For the lower ratio, M<=sqrt A+1<=3p²/4+1<=4p²/5 for p²>=20. Hence k/M²>=25/16>3/2. Rounding M upward has therefore not eliminated the exponential gain. No assumption that the old exact relation k=2M²+1 still holds is made.

**Zero witnesses, balancing, and quantifiers.** The base blocks E_i send zero to a center, making every predicate one. Copy one earlier common block into every child of a single row. Its union makes all strongest targets in that row true; every gate lies in a distinct row left at zero. This gives M k^l disjoint nonempty common blocks, each of size L r^l. Different rows are disjoint; within a row the earlier disjoint families stay disjoint in each child. All predicates themselves remain zero at zero because every target there fails.

For the OR of M identical disjoint copies of P_d,1, zero-side sensitivity is at most M S_d,0. At a one input with two or more accepting copies it is zero, and with exactly one accepting copy it is at most S_d,1. Thus s<=2L M^(d+1), while the disjoint union of zero witnesses gives bs(f,0)>=M² k^d. The inherited independent groups and permutations of the copies act transitively on all n=L M²(kr)^d bits. Nonconstancy follows from a sensitive block, and s>0 follows from a cube path between inputs with different values. Consequently bs(f,0)/s(f)^2>=(3/2)^d/[4(d+2)^2], which tends to infinity. Choose d first to exceed the requested C, then choose p; both parameters are finite. This proves the strict every-C inequality.

**Fixed exponent and iterated transitivity.** At d=20, L=22, take A_*=44M^21 and B_*=M²k^20. The exact inequality 3^20=3486784401>2030043136=2^20·1936 proves B_*>A_*². Thus A_*>1 and alpha=log B_*/log A_*>2 is one fixed real number after choosing a single seed. For a disjoint composition, sensitivity counts are bounded by the number of sensitive outer coordinates times the maximum inner sensitivity. At zero, the Cartesian products of a disjoint outer block family and a disjoint inner block family are pairwise disjoint, nonempty and sensitive, provided the inner function is zero at zero. These give s(F_m)<=A_*^m and bs(F_m,0)>=B_*^m. Outer coordinate automorphisms permute identical children; independent inner automorphisms preserve each child and move its bits. Their group is transitive on all product coordinates. Induction preserves totality and F_m(0)=0. Finally s(F_m)^alpha<=B_*^m<=bs(F_m,0), and B_*>1 ensures divergence and nonconstancy for every m>=1.
