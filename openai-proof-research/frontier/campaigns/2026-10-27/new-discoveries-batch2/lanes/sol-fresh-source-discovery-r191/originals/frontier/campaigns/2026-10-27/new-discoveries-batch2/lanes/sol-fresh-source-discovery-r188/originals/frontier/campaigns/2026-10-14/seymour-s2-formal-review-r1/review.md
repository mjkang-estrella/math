## Exact declaration coverage

All names below are in namespace `SeymourSupport`.

1. `boundary_weak`, `boundary_strict`, `boundary_indicators` prove integer consequences of the explicit identity `dD=dH+q+b-a` and `dD<=-1`. Weakness requires the stated indicator bounds; strictness requires `a<=q`. The Boolean theorem splits all eight combinations of hits/avoidance/return indicators. It proves no graph identity or protected subset inequality.
2. `sum_with_specials`, `aggregate_bound`, `aggregate_negative` quantify over an arbitrary finite index set I, distinguished subset S, integer-valued f, and integers n,m. The bounds on and off S yield `sum f <= |I|n-|S|m`. With `|I|=nm`, `|S|>=m-1`, and `m=n^2+2`, the result is `sum f<=-m<0`. The distinguished subset need not exhaust all improved fibers. Casts and subtraction are genuinely integer-valued. These declarations assume the supplied family bounds and counts; they do not construct anchors.
3. `fiber_cardinality_strict`, `fiber_sigma_strict` convert a negative sum of actual finite-fiber cardinality defects into a strict inequality, including cardinalities of the actual tagged sigma families. No identification of these families with graph coordinate images is proved.
4. `complement_gain` uses actual finite subsets of an ambient U, disjoint first images, and two strict image-cardinality inequalities to obtain the complement gain. P and Q need not be subsets of U for this elementary implication. `pruning_objective_gain` uses actual returned subsets and their deletion budget to improve the objective. `complement_pruning_contradiction` combines these with an explicitly assumed upper bound supplied by maximality. It does not prove a maximum exists, construct pruning output, establish compatibility, or infer the deletion budget. Its conclusion False is the intended arithmetic contradiction from these explicit hypotheses, not an axiom hiding the graph theorem.

The source and expanded compiler type output expose no additional hidden premise. The finite-family hypotheses have a concrete sharp realized example, and the complement hypotheses have a concrete finite-set realization; they are not vacuous universal claims. The contradiction theorem intentionally assumes a jointly impossible maximality/pruning configuration, and the missing proof of how a graph counterexample would supply that configuration is expressly outside formal coverage.

Unformalized bridges remain: minimum-counterexample and unique-good reduction; protected deficits; exact graph boundary identity; strong connectivity after deletion; transitive-blowup image bound; compatibility and anchor-path exclusion; index counts and coordinate-image/fiber identification; pruning existence, charges, diagonal retention and admissibility; objective-maximality application; and the full graph conclusion. The two stipulated upstream theorems are not installed as Lean axioms. `coverage-audit.json` records this boundary in machine-readable form.
