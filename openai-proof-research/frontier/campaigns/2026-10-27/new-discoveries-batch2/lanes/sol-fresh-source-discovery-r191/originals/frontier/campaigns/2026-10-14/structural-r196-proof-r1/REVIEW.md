## 2. Incidence, ports and every law — resolved

There are q(q−1) choices of offset s and color c, each with five blocks, giving m=5q(q−1). A color has 5(q−1) block types and two ports each, hence M=10(q−1). An ordered pair of distinct ports for the two branches has M(M−1) choices in each color. The signs and independent choices among ten conjugators for each of the 2q slots therefore give exactly

`k_q = [M(M−1)]^q · 10^(2q) · 2^(2q)`.

No choice is divided by a symmetry: these are labeled marked bodies. All quantities are finite for each q; the statement requires no bound uniform in q.

At a vertex v of the degree-d cover, a port of type (s,c,k) identifies either the edge beginning at v or the edge ending at v, according to its end. For 0<s<q≤d these are distinct edges. If 2s=d they connect the same two vertices, but they are distinct lifted edges, with different starting vertices. Different type labels remain distinct parallel edges. Thus distinct base ports yield precisely distinct incident edges, and all choices of two edges of each color occur among the lifted laws. This includes d=q and even q.

For an avoiding endpoint assignment let D(v) be the colors for which v receives at most one edge. If D(v) were empty, choosing two received edges in each color would produce a law all assigned to v. Thus D(v) is nonempty. Whenever u,v are joined by an offset edge, they have at least five parallel edges of each color between them. A shared deficient color would permit at most two of these edges to be received at their two endpoints, contradicting assignment of all five. Hence D(u)∩D(v)=∅.

The q vertices v,v+1,…,v+q−1 are distinct for d≥q, and any two are joined by an edge of one of the allowed offsets. Their q nonempty pairwise disjoint subsets of a q-element color set must be singletons. Every such consecutive block uses every color once. Comparing successive blocks gives c(v+q)=c(v). The resulting bi-infinite color sequence has periods q and d, so it has period gcd(q,d); since its first q entries are distinct this gcd must be q. Thus q|d. This proves the divisibility claim without assuming primality or parity.

Conversely, when q|d use c(v)=1+(v mod q). Endpoints of every allowed edge have different chosen colors. Assign the edge to an endpoint whose chosen color differs from the edge's color; at least one such endpoint always exists. No vertex then receives an edge in its selected color.
