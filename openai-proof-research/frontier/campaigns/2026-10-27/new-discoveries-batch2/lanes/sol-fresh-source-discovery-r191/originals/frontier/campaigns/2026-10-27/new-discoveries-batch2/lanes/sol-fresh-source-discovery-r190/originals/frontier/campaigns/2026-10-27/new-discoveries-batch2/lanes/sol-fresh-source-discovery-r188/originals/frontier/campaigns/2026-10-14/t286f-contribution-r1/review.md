Reason in one paragraph. Every operation in the proof is either a stipulated source operation or a standard finite-index or finite-group template. The proof does not overcome any obstruction that these templates leave open. The full class 𝒦, arbitrary μ and all bifinite bimodules come entirely from the stipulated exhaustion theorem and the source's full-germ calculus. After that, the argument is the familiar finite-Hecke reduction. A finite sub-collection of a Hecke-type category has a finite-dimensional identity sector. That sector defines one finite-index normal subgroup K. Every commensurator germ in the collection preserves K, so the collection becomes a finite (twisted) Hecke datum over the finite quotient. Its dual with respect to the regular algebra Fun(Γ/K) is then pointed. The uniform kernel is extracted from the identity sector `Rep(Γ/K)` by the sandwich `X⊗H(id,ρ)⊗X̄`. That is a tidy device. It is still the categorical form of the usual "shrink to a normal subgroup that every relevant commensurator normalizes" step (Deprez–Vaes, the source's own endpoint grading). The basic-construction corner is the standard finite-index Q-system Morita equivalence. The source performs the same identity-degree / minimal-corner compression on a finite-index extension. The subfactor corollary without irreducibility is the classical even-part Morita equivalence.

**Answer to the review question.** Is the common identity-sector kernel extraction a standard finite-Hecke reduction template? Yes, in substance. The only part not found verbatim in the read bodies is its *packaging*: one uniform K obtained from `D0` together with the sandwich `X⊗H(id,ρ)⊗X̄`, instead of per-object normal cores. That packaging is the categorical restatement of a step that every Hecke-category argument performs. A finite set of commensurator elements has a finite-index normal subgroup that all of them normalize, and the Hecke data then descend to a finite quotient. For finite (twisted or groupoid) Hecke categories, group-theoreticality is known (EGNO Ex. 9.7.4 model; Gödicke Cor. 2.6 for finite groupoids, untwisted). For C*-tensor Hecke categories of infinite pairs, Deprez–Vaes Thm 4.2 and Arano–Vaes (Hecke category `C(K<G)`, `Rep K` as a full subcategory, Morita independence of K) are the standard frame. An expert who knows the correspondence classification would perform this reduction directly. It removes no obstruction beyond routine bookkeeping. The twist is carried by standard cocycle algebra: multipliers cancel in `σ⊗σ̄`, and the phases on `N` are those of a twisted crossed product. Lattice irreducibility and subfactor irreducibility never enter, so dropping them adds no difficulty.

**Why not SUBSTANTIAL.** The new statement is broad, and it has consequences (for example, no non-group-theoretical finite-depth standard invariant, such as Haagerup's, embeds as bimodules of these factors). However, all of that strength is imported from the stipulated exhaustion theorem `thm:arithmetic-exhaustion` and `thm:correspondences`. The delta beyond them is the uniform kernel, the restriction to invertibles and the Q-system corner, each a known operation. Under the campaign rules, a wide scope inherited from a stipulated classification is not a substantial bridge. The author makes the same caveat (`checkpoint.md` line 13; `source-and-credit286f.md` final paragraph).

**Why not DUPLICATE.** No read body states the theorem for twisted lattice factors, for 𝒦, or for an abstract infinite twisted Hecke correspondence category. Vaes 2008 and Deprez–Vaes compute bimodule categories for specific group-measure-space factors without deriving group-theoreticality of finite subcategories. Arano–Vaes lines 20–349 contain no finite-subcategory group-theoretical theorem. Gödicke Cor. 2.6 is finite-groupoid and untwisted only. Falguières–Raum (every finite C*-tensor category arises as a bimodule category of some II₁ factor) is consistent with, and does not contradict, the theorem for these specific factors. Bounded search is not a worldwide novelty clearance.

## 4. Objections

Required before any parent use of this packet as a technical, uncounted result:

1. **Credit the normal-core template.** `source-and-credit286f.md` cites Vaes 2008, Donvil–Vaes, EGNO, Gödicke, BKLR and Izumi, but not Deprez–Vaes (JNCG 5 (2011) 523–545). Deprez–Vaes contains both the explicit "make Γ₁ normal in G" reduction and the identification of the finite-index bimodule bicategory with a twisted Hecke bicategory (§4, Thm 4.2). Arano–Vaes, *C\*-tensor categories and subfactors for totally disconnected groups* (arXiv:1602.03047), should be cited as the C\*-tensor Hecke-category frame. §3 of the candidate should say that the uniform-kernel step is this standard reduction in categorical form, not a "proposed mechanism".
2. **Credit the source's own corner step.** §§5–6 should point to `lem:endpoints-bounded-grades`, `lem:endpoints-minimal-corner` and `prop:endpoints-corner-constraints` of the stipulated companion. These already compress a finite-dimensional identity degree of a finite-index extension to a corner whose degrees are unitary lines.
3. **No novelty wording.** Any record must say "new to this project", not new to the literature. The search is bounded (see `reading-limits.md`, `search-log.json`).

Optional:

4. Note that the conclusion is a corollary-type consequence of the exhaustion theorem. Any group or twisted-Hecke correspondence category with the same full-germ calculus would admit the same argument. The author already says this for Vaes 2008 (`source-and-credit286f.md` line 15).
5. The Gödicke dependence on Cor. 11.4 / FGJS22 Prop. 4.9 is unread on both sides. If those bodies contain a general infinite-to-finite Hecke reduction, the classification could only move toward DUPLICATE.
