# Four algebraic properties of signed-set fusion

For every set A, identify a signed subset of A with an ordered pair (P,N) of disjoint subsets of A. P records the positive elements and N the negative elements. Write 0=(∅,∅), and put −(P,N)=(N,P).

For X=(P,N) and Y=(Q,M), define

\[
 X\oplus Y=((P\setminus M)\cup(Q\setminus N),
 (N\setminus Q)\cup(M\setminus P)).
\]

These are exactly the ordinary set operations obtained by removing each element whose opposite occurs in the other operand, then taking the union. They implement Definitions 1.4, 1.8 and 1.12 of [Gatica–Bustamante, arXiv:2501.00047v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00047v1).

## The generated space and its formal representation

The source's integer space is generated by fusing an arbitrary positive subset P of A and an arbitrary negative subset Q of A. In coordinates this gives (P\Q,Q\P), always disjoint. Conversely any disjoint (P,N) is obtained by taking these same two subsets, since P\N=P and N\P=N. Thus the generated space is exactly the type of disjoint signed pairs; this is not a restriction to finite A or a proper subspace.

In Lean, `Space α` is the subtype of pairs of `Set α` satisfying `Disjoint`. Choose α to be the subtype of members of A to obtain the original A⊆N formulation. `purePositive`, `pureNegative`, `generated_is_fusion`, and `generated_space_exact` certify the two directions of this representation. No cardinality assumption is used.

## Theorem

The operation is closed, commutative, has a unique two-sided identity, and each element has a unique two-sided annihilating inverse.

**Closure.** Suppose an atom belongs to both output coordinates. A choice from the first positive component and first negative component would put it in both P and N, contradicting their disjointness. A choice from the second and second would put it in both Q and M. Either mixed choice contradicts one of the defining set differences. These are all four possibilities.

**Commutativity and identity.** Interchanging the inputs interchanges the two terms of each union. The empty pair leaves both coordinates unchanged. If e is any two-sided identity, applying its identity property to 0 and using 0⊕e=e gives e=0.

**Existence of inverse.** In X⊕(−X), each coordinate is a union of differences of a set from itself, and is therefore empty.

**Uniqueness of inverse.** If X⊕Y=0, its positive coordinate gives P\M=∅ and Q\N=∅, so P⊆M and Q⊆N. Its negative coordinate gives N\Q=∅ and M\P=∅, so N⊆Q and M⊆P. Hence Q=N and M=P: Y=−X. The same inverse works on both sides by commutativity.

`conjecture_3_2` bundles precisely these four claims for every ground type. These prove the full four-part statement in source Conjecture 3.2.

## Why this is not a loop theorem in the usual sense

One also has X⊕X=X because the coordinates of X are disjoint. For X≠0, translation by X sends both X and 0 to X, so it is not injective. Thus the quasigroup division property required of an ordinary loop fails. The source conjecture lists only closure, identity, annihilating inverses, and commutativity; our theorem covers that exact list.

Moreover, for nonempty X,
\[
 ((-X)\oplus X)\oplus X=X,\qquad
 (-X)\oplus(X\oplus X)=0,
\]
so associativity fails. Both observations are formalized. They are clarification of the algebra, not extra counted outcomes.
