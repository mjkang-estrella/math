## Independent complete mathematics

For total-two occupancy vectors x,y, let M=sum_i min(x_i,y_i). At least 2−M tokens must leave excess positions. Each move can reduce the excess by at most one, giving a lower bound. In K_n each excess can move directly to a deficit, attaining the bound. Hence d(x,y)=2−M, equivalently half the L1 distance.

Choose the eight landmarks 12,23,34,45,67,78,89,{9,10}. These are legal distinct vertices. For a landmark {i,j}, i≠j, its overlap with x equals 1_{i∈T}+1_{j∈T}, where T is the support of x. Thus its distance is two minus that incidence count, even when x is doubled. The support has cardinality at most two.

On a five-vertex path, if supports S,T of cardinality at most two have equal four incidence counts, z=1_S−1_T obeys z_i+z_{i+1}=0. Therefore z=(a,−a,a,−a,a). The integer a belongs to {−1,0,1}. If a=1, S contains the three odd positions; if a=−1, T does. Either contradicts the cardinality bound. Thus a=0 and S=T. The argument includes the empty support. Apply it separately to the two blocks. The eight counts recover all of T. A one-point support means a double token there; a two-point support means one token at each point. This recovers x uniquely on all 55 vertices, so dim(F_2(K_10))≤8<9.

The independently written `checker.py` generates all combinations with replacement, constructs adjacency by every possible one-token move, and runs BFS from each vertex. It checks all 3,025 ordered distances against the derived formula, eight legal landmarks, 55 distinct signatures, all 16 local supports including empty, and the P3 obstruction. It passes. It uses no candidate/root checker or signature table. The proof above, rather than the experiment, establishes the result.

Mathematical falsifier: an illegal landmark, two distinct total-two vectors with equal eight distances, or a legal path shorter than the excess-token lower bound. The source model and preceding proof exclude each. No lower bound proving dimension exactly eight has been supplied or needed.

## Strongest objection: complete classical adaptation

I read Bailey–Cameron, *Base size, metric dimension and other invariants of groups and graphs*, author manuscript pp24–25, Theorem 3.32 and Corollary 3.33, with their proof bodies. For n≥6 they give the Johnson/Kneser dimension 2(n−i)/3+i, n≡i mod 3, i∈{0,1,2}. Their underlying graph is the simple two-subset Johnson graph, and their principal construction is a spanning union of P3s. Their five signature cases distinguish selected edges, the edge closing a local triangle, and edges between blocks. The coordinates 0,1,2 are exactly two minus the same landmark-intersection counts used here. The classical theorem does not literally include doubled configurations. Indeed the P3 landmarks 12,23 give the same code (1,1) to 22 and 13; external disjoint blocks add identical coordinates.

That literal failure does not clear the substantive gate. Here is the full adaptation, without an unproved bridge: retain the classical spanning-path-edge landmarks and incidence coordinates, but use paths on m≥5 vertices. Equal local codes again have indicator difference (a,−a,a,−a,...). If a is nonzero, at least one support contains an entire parity class of cardinality at least three. Thus all local supports of size≤2, including singleton and empty supports, are distinguished. Disjoint blocks concatenate these recoveries. Recover the total-two occupancy from its support as above. Therefore every partition of n into t parts of size≥5 yields a resolving set with n−t landmarks. For every n≥10 take t=floor(n/5) parts: t−1 parts of size 5 and a final part of size 5+(n mod 5). This proves

    dim(F_2(K_n)) ≤ n − floor(n/5) < n−1,   n≥10.

The candidate is exactly its n=10 instance. P4 would not suffice: its two parity supports both have size two. P5 is the first path for which the standard alternating-kernel obstruction exceeds the permitted support size. There is no new metric formula, signature mechanism, decoding theory, optimization theorem, or difficult cross-block compatibility in this bridge. The only repair is enlarging the familiar block until its sole alternating ambiguity cannot fit. This is why I recommend routine-adaptation exclusion; I am not inferring exclusion from the number of proof lines, the finiteness of the witness, or failure to prove an exact optimum.
