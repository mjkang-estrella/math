## Ranked provisional next investments

1. **Sun4.4, all odd powers and all p-adic inputs.** Closest body-grounded result is S Theorem1.3, motivating its conjecture, not a later proof. Let N=p-2k (odd) and F(X)=sum_(r<p)(-1)^r binom(X,r)^h with h=2n+1. Since r! is a p-unit for r<p, F belongs to Z_p[X]. Pairing r and N-r gives F(N)=0 exactly; terms r>N vanish. Therefore F(N+pu)≡pu F'(N) modp^2. The ENTIRE p-adic-x quantifier reduces to F'(N)=0 modp for the permitted k,h. Terms r>N contribute zero to the derivative since h>=5. **Decisive calculation:** evaluate

       h sum_(r=0)^N (-1)^r binom(N,r)^h (H_N-H_(N-r)) mod p

   uniformly under hk<=p+1, including h divisible by p (then the displayed prefactor already helps). That harmonic sum, or an exact terminating hypergeometric identity, is the remaining mathematical issue. This reduction is not a proof of vanishing. **Prior objection:** 2014 conjecture may have later proof or q-microscoping subsumer; targeted arXiv searches here do not clear that. Straub1803.07146v1's actual Theorem1.2/Cor1.1 concern different q-Apéry polynomials and scaling congruences, not this derivative sum.

2. **Sun4.3, full valuation/denominator package.** Define s(n)=n^(-2)sum_(k<n)(2k+1)(-1)^k A_k(1/4), t(n)=n^(-2)sum_(k<n)(2k+1)(-1)^k D_k(-1/4)^3. Exact reduced denominators are claimed to be2^(2v2(n!)) and2^[3(n-1+v2(n!))-v2(n)]. Numerators of s(n) are1 mod12 for odd n and7 mod12 for even n. For every odd prime p and a>=1,s(p^a)≡t(p^a)≡1 modp, with s(3^a)≡4 mod9,t(3^a)≡-8 mod243. Finally for every prime p,n>=1,x∈Z_p, valuations of n^(-1)sum_(k<n)(2k+1)(-1)^k A_k(x), respectively the analogous D_k(x)^3 sum, are at least min(vp(n),vp(4x-1)), respectively min(vp(n),vp(4x+1)). **Decisive calculation:** obtain an integral binomial expansion of each normalized sum around x=1/4 and x=-1/4, separating the constant term and n-adic content; test symbolic coefficients for uniform factorial valuation bounds. **Closest body:** Guo–Zeng1101.0983v2's actual Pfaff–Saalschütz expansion and normalized-sum identities. **Objection:** Delaygue1310.4131v2 proves digit-valuation results for individual Apéry-like sequences, not visibly these weighted sums, but a complete p-adic subsumption screen remains outstanding. Exact denominator AND attainment clauses must not be replaced with only an upper bound. No separate subclause nomination.

3. **Monomial digraph fixed finite count family, all prime powers.** Closest actual theorem M Theorem1 proves prime-field classification via group structure; Theorem2 exposes extension-field exceptions. **Decisive calculation:** translate counts of a fixed directed cycle/bipartite pattern into ranks and character sums of exponent maps, and determine whether a uniformly bounded pattern size recovers the simultaneous-unit/semilinear orbit. To disprove, one needs for every finite H a nonisomorphic large-q pair matching its counts, not one pair matching a few patterns. **Objection:** finite-field model theory/coherent configurations could already subsume the assertion; neither a full invariant nor counterfamily is present. A general Lovász theorem allowing patterns growing with the graph order does not settle the fixed-family quantifier.

4. **Sun4.5, all admissible m.** Source Theorem1.1 proves neighboring Apéry/W transformations modulo p^2 but not the literal weighted three-sum comparison. **Decisive calculation:** use its exact binomial identity to convert the first two sums to a common terminated hypergeometric expression, retain first-order p-adic correction terms, and see whether the p≡3 mod4 involution cancels precisely those terms. Rational factors4m+1 and4m are p-units by hypothesis. **Objection:** modular hypergeometric transformations and later supercongruence literature have not been exhaustively checked. Evaluating only m=1 or sample primes would be insufficient.
