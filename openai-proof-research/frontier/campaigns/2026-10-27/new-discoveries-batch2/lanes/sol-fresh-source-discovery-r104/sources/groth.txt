Abstract.
We study the Grothendieck ring of finite-dimensional non-degenerate evolution algebras over a field 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}, with addition induced by direct sum and multiplication induced by tensor product. Although its underlying abelian group is freely generated by indecomposable isomorphism classes, the ring structure is much smaller: tensor products create systematic non-cancellation phenomena and many zero-divisors. The key invariant is the balance of the directed graph associated with a natural basis. We prove that, for non-degenerate evolution algebras, this balance is independent of the chosen natural basis. We then show that balance controls cancellation after tensoring: indecomposable factors of balance 11 cancel, whereas factors of larger balance produce zero-divisor relations under mild hypotheses. Finally, we analyze the subring generated by cyclic evolution algebras and prove the predicted zero-divisor criterion for all finite direct sums of cyclic algebras.
1. Introduction
Evolution algebras form a class of commutative, generally non-associative algebras designed to encode non-Mendelian inheritance. Their defining feature is the existence of a natural basis B={e1,…,en}B=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\} in which mixed products vanish, ei​ej=0e_{i}e_{j}=0 for i≠ji\neq j. Consequently, the whole multiplication is encoded by the squares of the basis elements, or equivalently by a single structure matrix. This places evolution algebras at the intersection of non-associative algebra, weighted directed graphs, and Markov-chain dynamics [12, 11, 8, 9].
A central problem in the theory is classification, usually understood as the determination of the isomorphism types of indecomposable evolution algebras. Although low-dimensional classifications are known [4], the case-by-case approach quickly becomes unfeasible. In fact, realization results show that every finite group occurs as the full automorphism group of a non-degenerate, finite-dimensional indecomposable evolution algebra [6, 7]. Thus, the classification problem for evolution algebras is at least as complicated as the classification of finite groups, and cannot reasonably be expected to admit a simple complete invariant. This obstruction motivates the categorical viewpoint adopted in this paper: we study how evolution algebras combine, rather than trying to enumerate them one by one.
The category Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} of finite-dimensional non-degenerate
evolution algebras over a field 𝕂{\mathbb{K}} is closed under two operations:
the direct sum ⊕\oplus, which is indeed the categorical product in Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}, and the tensor
product ⊗\otimes. Both are symmetric monoidal, and ⊗\otimes is
distributive with respect to ⊕\oplus, see Section 4. This is exactly the input needed
to form a Grothendieck ring 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}), in which isomorphism
classes are added by ⊕\oplus and multiplied by ⊗\otimes, see Section 5.
The pay-off is a change of perspective on what “classification” should
mean. As an abelian group, (𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd),+)(\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}),+) is free on the isomorphism
classes of non-degenerate indecomposable algebras, so describing its additive generators
is just the original, intractable problem in disguise. As a ring,
however, 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) is “smaller”; there exist indecomposable non-degenerate evolution algebras which are tensorially decomposable, see Section 12, and moreover 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) has many zero-divisors, see Theorem A below. Therefore, we propose to classify ring generators of 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}): these are some of the additive generators but also exploit the multiplication, and
there are far fewer of them.
Understanding 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) as a ring, rather than merely as an abelian
group, reframes the classification of evolution algebras. A description of
its ring generators and of its ideal of zero-divisors would, in
particular, open the door to a probabilistic classification (asking
for the typical behaviour of a random evolution algebra rather than a
complete list), and to the study of growth, that is, the asymptotics of the
number of indecomposables of a given dimension.
We conclude this section by proving that the ring 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) contains zero-divisors. This introductory argument also serves as a preview of the methods developed in the sequel, where the relevant constructions and proofs are given in full detail. The central invariant in the arguments is the balance of the directed graph associated with a natural basis. While the graph itself may depend on the chosen basis, its balance does not: for non-degenerate evolution algebras it is an intrinsic invariant (Theorem 6.1).

While we do not have a complete description of all zero-divisors in 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}), the following conjecture appears to be well motivated.
Conjecture 1.1.
An evolution 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebra is a zero-divisor in 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) if and only if
each of its indecomposable direct summands has balance greater than 11.
Remark 1.2.
Notice that Lemma 5.3 allows one to combine the zero-divisor relations associated with the indecomposable summands into a single relation for their direct sum. This establishes precisely the sufficiency direction of Conjecture 1.1, which Theorem A already proves for arbitrary direct sums.
By contrast, Lemma 5.3 does not reduce the necessity direction to the indecomposable case. There is no converse “de-combination” principle: a direct sum containing both a balance-11 summand and summands of balance greater than 11 could, a priori, be a zero-divisor for reasons that are not detected at the level of the individual summands. Therefore, Conjecture 1.1 cannot, on the basis of Lemma 5.3, be reduced to the indecomposable case.
2. Graphs and the balance invariant
In this section we briefly recall the basics in Graph Theory we use in the following sections. All graphs in this paper are directed, and we follow the conventions of [8, 9, 10].
Definition 2.1.
A graph is a pair
Γ=(V,E)\Gamma=(V,E) consisting of a finite set of vertices VV and a set of
edges (or arrows) E⊆V×VE\subseteq V\times V.
Definition 2.2.
A path in Γ\Gamma is a sequence
γ=(v0,e1,v1,…,vn−1,en,vn),n≥0,\gamma=(v_{0},e_{1},v_{1},\dots,v_{n-1},e_{n},v_{n}),\qquad n\geq 0,
with v0,…,vn∈Vv_{0},\dots,v_{n}\in V and e1,…,en∈Ee_{1},\dots,e_{n}\in E such that, for each i∈{1,…,n}i\in\{1,\dots,n\}, either ei=(vi−1,vi)e_{i}=(v_{i-1},v_{i}) (the edge is traversed forwards) or ei=(vi,vi−1)e_{i}=(v_{i},v_{i-1}) (traversed backwards). A (non-simple) cycle is a path with v0=vnv_{0}=v_{n}. The balance of γ\gamma is the integer
b⁡(γ)=#⁡{i:ei=(vi−1,vi)}−#⁡{i:ei=(vi,vi−1)},\operatorname{b}(\gamma)=\#\{\,i:e_{i}=(v_{i-1},v_{i})\,\}-\#\{\,i:e_{i}=(v_{i},v_{i-1})\,\},
that is, +1+1 for each edge traversed in the “right” direction (from
v0v_{0} towards vnv_{n}) and −1-1 for each edge traversed in the “wrong”
direction, summed over ii.
Remark 2.3.
Observe that balance behaves naturally with respect to the two elementary operations on paths: it is additive under concatenation and changes sign under reversal. Thus, if α\alpha and β\beta are paths in Γ\Gamma with the same final vertex, then α⋆β−1\alpha\star\beta^{-1} is a well-defined path, where ⋆\star denotes concatenation and β−1\beta^{-1} is the path β\beta traversed in the opposite direction. Hence
b⁡(α⋆β−1)=b⁡(α)+b⁡(β−1)=b⁡(α)−b⁡(β).\operatorname{b}(\alpha\star\beta^{-1})=\operatorname{b}(\alpha)+\operatorname{b}(\beta^{-1})=\operatorname{b}(\alpha)-\operatorname{b}(\beta).
Definition 2.4.
The balance of Γ\Gamma is the greatest common divisor of the
absolute values of the balances of its cycles,
b⁡(Γ)=gcd⁡{|b⁡(γ)|:γ​ a cycle in ​Γ}.\operatorname{b}(\Gamma)=\gcd\{\,|\operatorname{b}(\gamma)|:\gamma\text{ a cycle in }\Gamma\,\}.
Remark 2.5.
The notions of balance of a path and of a graph introduced here agree with those in [8, 9] and constitute the terminology commonly used in the context of evolution algebras. They also coincide with the notions of weight introduced by Chen and Chen in [5].
Definition 2.6.
The indegree and outdegree of a vertex v∈Vv\in V are
deg−⁡(v)=#⁡{w∈V:(w,v)∈E},deg+⁡(v)=#⁡{w∈V:(v,w)∈E}.\deg^{-}(v)=\#\{\,w\in V:(w,v)\in E\,\},\qquad\deg^{+}(v)=\#\{\,w\in V:(v,w)\in E\,\}.
The vertex vv is a source if deg−⁡(v)=0\deg^{-}(v)=0 and a sink if
deg+⁡(v)=0\deg^{+}(v)=0. The graph Γ\Gamma is connected if its underlying
undirected graph is connected, that is, if for every v,w∈Vv,w\in V there is a
path from vv to ww.
The following results characterize the balance of connected graphs.

3. Basic definitions on evolution algebras
In this section, we establish the notation and recall the basic definitions concerning evolution algebras that are used throughout the paper. Unless stated otherwise, 𝕂{\mathbb{K}} denotes a field, and all 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebras are assumed to be finite-dimensional.
Definition 3.1.
A 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebra XX is an evolution algebra if it admits a basis
B={e1,…,en}B=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}, called a natural basis, such that
ei​ej=0e_{i}\,e_{j}=0 whenever i≠ji\neq j.
Relative to such a basis the products of the basis elements with
themselves are recorded by scalars wi​k∈𝕂w_{ik}\in{\mathbb{K}} via
ei 2=∑k=1nwi​k​ek,e_{i}^{\,2}\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{ik}\,e_{k},
and the matrix MB​(X)=(wi​k)M_{B}(X)=(w_{ik}) is the structure matrix of XX
relative to BB.
Evolution algebras are commutative because the product of distinct basis vectors is zero. However, they are generally neither associative nor power-associative. Once a natural basis BB is fixed, the algebra is completely determined by its structure matrix MB​(X)=(wi​k)M_{B}(X)=(w_{ik}). Geometrically and biologically, this structure carries two primary interpretations [11]: in genetics, each generator eie_{i} represents a type and ei2e_{i}^{2} its offspring distribution, while the Markov-chain interpretation takes the wi​kw_{ik} to be transition weights from state ii to state kk.
Remark 3.2.
The natural basis is not unique, and neither is the structure matrix: a given evolution algebra can carry several genuinely different natural bases. This non-uniqueness is the source of most of the subtlety in the theory and is precisely what the rigidified category of Section 7 is designed to control.
Definition 3.3.
An evolution 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebra XX is non-degenerate if it admits a natural basis B={e1,…,en}B=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\} such that ei2≠0e_{i}^{2}\neq 0 for i=1,…,n.i=1,\ldots,n.
Remark 3.4.
The property of being non-degenerate is independent of the chosen basis. Indeed, according to [3, Corollary 2.19], an evolution algebra XX is non-degenerate if and only if
Ann⁡(X)={x∈X:x​X=0}=0.\operatorname{Ann}(X)=\{x\in X:xX=0\}=0.
Definition 3.5.
Let XX be an evolution algebra with natural basis B={e1,…,en}B=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\} and structure matrix (wi​k)(w_{ik}). The associated graph Γ⁡(X,B)\Gamma(X,B) is defined as the graph on the vertex set {1,…,n}\{1,\dots,n\} that has an edge (i,k)(i,k) whenever wi​k≠0w_{ik}\neq 0 [8, 9].
Many algebraic features of XX (nilpotency, simplicity, ideals) are visible in Γ⁡(X,B)\Gamma(X,B). However, the correspondence between evolution algebras and their associated graphs is neither bijective nor functorial: it depends on the chosen natural basis (Remark 3.2), and a homomorphism of evolution algebras need not rescale the prescribed natural bases, hence need not induce a morphism of graphs. Nevertheless, we prove in Section 6 that the balance of the graph Γ⁡(X,B)\Gamma(X,B) is an intrinsic invariant of XX that does not depend on the chosen natural basis BB if XX is non-degenerate.
4. Monoidal structure on Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}
Recall the category Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} is defined as the full subcategory of the category of 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebras which is spanned by finite-dimensional non-degenerate evolution algebras. The category Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} is closed under two operations:
•
The direct sum X⊕YX\oplus Y: It is the categorical
product in Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}. Indeed, given XX, and YY non-degenerate evolution algebras with natural basis
BB and B′B^{\prime} respectively, then B⊕{0}⊔{0}⊕B′B\oplus\{0\}\sqcup\{0\}\oplus B^{\prime} is a natural basis of X⊕YX\oplus Y, whose
structure matrix is the block diagonal matrix of those of XX and YY. Moreover, since multiplication in X⊕YX\oplus Y is component wise, the square of every element in B⊕{0}⊔{0}⊕B′B\oplus\{0\}\sqcup\{0\}\oplus B^{\prime} is non trivial, that is X⊕YX\oplus Y is non-degenerate. Moreover, X⊕YX\oplus Y is the categorical product: the projections
πX:X⊕Y→X\pi_{X}\colon X\oplus Y\to X and πY:X⊕Y→Y\pi_{Y}\colon X\oplus Y\to Y are
homomorphisms of evolution algebras, and for any non-degenerate evolution
𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebra ZZ with homomorphisms f:Z→Xf\colon Z\to X and g:Z→Yg\colon Z\to Y,
the map z↦(f⁡(z),g⁡(z))z\mapsto(f(z),g(z)) is the unique homomorphism Z→X⊕YZ\to X\oplus Y
with πX∘(f,g)=f\pi_{X}\circ(f,g)=f and πY∘(f,g)=g\pi_{Y}\circ(f,g)=g; it is multiplicative
because products across the two blocks vanish.
Every non-degenerate evolution algebra admits a unique optimal direct-sum decomposition into indecomposable evolution ideals [3, Theorem 5.11]. In other words, every non-degenerate evolution algebra decomposes in a unique way as a direct sum of indecomposable evolution algebras. Finally, the zero algebra 00 is non-degenerate evolution algebra since it admits an empty natural basis, and it is a unit for this operation.
•
The tensor product X⊗YX\otimes Y:
Let XX and YY be evolution algebras over a field 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}, each endowed with natural bases B={ei}i∈IB=\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I} and B′={fj}j∈JB^{\prime}=\{f_{j}\}_{j\in J} respectively. It is well known, e.g. [2, Theorem 3.2], that the tensor product algebra X⊗YX\otimes Y admits a natural basis given by B⊗B′={ei⊗fj}(i,j)∈I×JB\otimes B^{\prime}=\{e_{i}\otimes f_{j}\}_{(i,j)\in I\times J}, with multiplication determined by
(ei⊗fj)2=ei2⊗fj2,(ei⊗fj)​(ei′⊗fj′)=0​ for ​(i,j)≠(i′,j′).(e_{i}\otimes f_{j})^{2}=e_{i}^{2}\otimes f_{j}^{2},\quad(e_{i}\otimes f_{j})(e_{i^{\prime}}\otimes f_{j^{\prime}})=0\text{ for }(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}).
Hence X⊗YX\otimes Y is itself an evolution algebra, its structure matrix is the Kronecker product of the structure matrices of XX and YY [2, Remark 3.3]. Moreover, if both XX and YY are non-degenerate, then for every basis element ei⊗fje_{i}\otimes f_{j} one has
(ei⊗fj)2=ei2⊗fj2≠0,(e_{i}\otimes f_{j})^{2}=e_{i}^{2}\otimes f_{j}^{2}\neq 0,
since both tensor factors are nonzero, and X⊗YX\otimes Y is again non-degenerate
[2, Lemma 3.8]. Therefore, the class of non-degenerate evolution algebras is closed under tensor products. The one-dimensional algebra 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}, acts as a unit for this operation.
Since the usual direct sum and tensor product of 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebras are associative and symmetric up to canonical
isomorphism, it follows that the category Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} inherits a symmetric monoidal structure from the ambient
category of 𝕂{\mathbb{K}}-algebras when considering these two operations. This is made explicit in the following statement.
Proposition 4.1.

10. Local monomorphisms, transfer of isomorphism, and balance
In this section we show that the isomorphism type of tensor products in NEvol𝕂nd\nevol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} is indeed controlled by local data of the common factor, and that non-cancellation phenomena appears when this common factor has higher balance.
We first define how we do compare the local data of two evolution algebras.
Definition 10.1.
Let A=⨁i∈I𝕂​aiA=\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\mathbb{K}}a_{i} and B=⨁p∈P𝕂​bpB=\bigoplus_{p\in P}{\mathbb{K}}b_{p} be objects in NEvol𝕂nd\nevol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} with associated structure matrices (mi​j)(m_{ij}) and (ℳp​q)(\mathcal{M}_{pq}) respectively, and let

12. Indecomposability of Tensor Products
As noted in Section 5, understanding the ring generators of 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) requires identifying when the tensor product of two indecomposable algebras remains indecomposable. We first show that the balance of the factors provides lower bounds for the number of summands in the optimal direct-sum decomposition into indecomposable evolution ideals [3, Theorem 5.11].

Notice that Theorem 12.1 identifies a necessary condition for the tensor product to be indecomposable.
Corollary 12.3.
Let X1,X2∈Evol𝕂ndX_{1},X_{2}\in\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} be indecomposable, di=b⁡(Xi)d_{i}=\operatorname{b}(X_{i}), and d=gcd⁡(d1,d2)d=\gcd(d_{1},d_{2}). If X1⊗X2X_{1}\otimes X_{2} is indecomposable, then d=1d=1.
The converse of Corollary 12.3 holds, and we can now give a complete characterization of indecomposable tensor products. The following is a reinterpretation of [5, Theorem 15] in evolution-algebra form.
Theorem 12.4.
Let X1,X2∈Evol𝕂ndX_{1},X_{2}\in\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} be indecomposable, and di=b⁡(Xi)d_{i}=\operatorname{b}(X_{i}).
Then X1⊗X2X_{1}\otimes X_{2} is indecomposable if and only if
gcd⁡(d1,d2)=1\gcd(d_{1},d_{2})=1, and in that case
b⁡(X1⊗X2)=d1​d2\operatorname{b}(X_{1}\otimes X_{2})=d_{1}d_{2}.
Proof.
Recall that the algebra XiX_{i} is non-degenerate. Hence, the graph Γ⁡(Xi,Bi)\Gamma(X_{i},B_{i}) has no sinks and has non-trivial balance for any natural basis BiB_{i} of XiX_{i}. Moreover, the notion of balance used here agrees with the notion of weight in [5] (see Remark 2.5). Since, for non-degenerate evolution algebras, indecomposability is equivalent to weak connectedness of the associated graph [3], the statement follows directly from [5, Theorem 15].
∎
There is also a multiplicative description of indecomposability for

[5, Corollary 16], which we record here.
Proposition 12.5.
Let X1,…,Xk∈Evol𝕂ndX_{1},\dots,X_{k}\in\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} be indecomposable. Then X1⊗⋯⊗XkX_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{k} is indecomposable if
and only if the balances b⁡(X1),…,b⁡(Xk)b(X_{1}),\dots,b(X_{k}) are pairwise coprime, and
in that case
b(X1⊗⋯⊗Xk)=∏i=1kb(Xi).b\bigl(X_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes X_{k}\bigr)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}b(X_{i}).
13. Zero-divisors among direct sums of cyclic algebras
Conjecture 1.1predicts that an algebra of Evol𝕂nd\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}} is a zero-divisor
in 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) precisely when every one of its indecomposable direct
summands has balance strictly greater than 11. The sufficiency
direction holds in full generality: Theorem A produces a zero-divisor
relation for any direct sum ⨁jXj\bigoplus_{j}X_{j} all of whose summands have
balance >1>1, by combining the relations of the summands through
Lemma 5.3. The necessity direction is known only for indecomposable algebras (Remark 11.2, via the cancellation Theorem 11.1), and its extension to reducible
algebras is a natural open problem. In this section we settle it for
arbitrary direct sums of cyclic algebras.
We now introduce the main object of study of this section.
Definition 13.1.
Let ℛ⊆𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}) be the subring generated by the classes of the
cyclic algebras {[Cn]:n≥1}\{[C_{n}]:n\geq 1\}, where C1=𝕂C_{1}={\mathbb{K}} is the unit.
By Example 12.2 the product of two generators is a single generator up
to an integer factor,
(21)
[Cn]​[Cm]=gcd⁡(n,m)​[Clcm⁡(n,m)],[C1]=1,[C_{n}]\,[C_{m}]=\gcd(n,m)\,[C_{\lcm(n,m)}],\qquad[C_{1}]=1,
so products of generators never leave the ℤ\mathbb{Z}-span of
{[Cn]}\{[C_{n}]\}. As the CnC_{n} are pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposables,
their classes are part of the free ℤ\mathbb{Z}-basis of 𝒢⁡(Evol𝕂nd)\mathcal{G}(\evol^{\mathrm{nd}}_{\mathbb{K}}); hence
ℛ=⨁n≥1ℤ⁡[Cn]\mathcal{R}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{Z}\,[C_{n}]
is free abelian on {[Cn]}\{[C_{n}]\}, with multiplication given by Equation (21).
For a positive integer FF, write τ⁡(F)\tau(F) for its number of divisors, and define
ℛF=⨁d|Fℤ⁡[Cd]⊆ℛ.\mathcal{R}_{F}=\bigoplus_{d\mid F}\mathbb{Z}\,[C_{d}]\subseteq\mathcal{R}.
Since d|Fd\mid F and e|Fe\mid F imply that lcm⁡(d,e)|F\lcm(d,e)\mid F, each ℛF\mathcal{R}_{F} is a

For each integer m≥1m\geq 1 there is a unique ring homomorphism
