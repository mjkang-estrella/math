Idempotent-free non-solvable evolution algebras over ℂ\mathbb{C}

Xing-Yu Hu School of Mathematics and Statistics, Hanjiang Normal University, Shiyan 442000, Hubei, China huxingyu@hjnu.edu.cn and Ran Wen* School of Mathematics and Physics, Xinjiang Hetian College, Hetian 848000, Xinjiang, China 1837496033@qq.com
Abstract.

A recent conjecture states that a finite-dimensional complex evolution algebra is solvable if and only if it has no non-zero idempotents. We exhibit a three-dimensional counterexample over ℂ\mathbb{C} whose isomorphism class already appears in the classification of three-dimensional complex evolution algebras. Since the conjecture is known in dimensions one and two, this counterexample has the smallest possible dimension. The algebra is defined over every field of characteristic different from 22 and is the exceptional member of a one-parameter family of pairwise non-isomorphic non-solvable evolution algebras whose idempotents are determined explicitly. For the exceptional parameter, the derived series stabilises at a non-zero two-dimensional subalgebra whose only idempotent is zero. Direct sums with zero algebras give complex counterexamples in every dimension at least three. We also prove the conjectured equivalence whenever the stable term of the derived series is an evolution algebra.

Key words and phrases:
Evolution algebra, solvable algebra, idempotent, derived series

1. Introduction

An evolution algebra over a field 𝕂\mathbb{K} is a commutative 𝕂\mathbb{K}-algebra EE, not necessarily associative, that admits a basis B={e1,…,en}B=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\} such that

ei​ej=0(i≠j).e_{i}e_{j}=0\qquad(i\neq j).

Such a basis is called a natural basis [16, p. 112]. If

ei2=∑j=1nai​j​ej,e_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij}e_{j},

then MB​(E)=(ai​j)M_{B}(E)=(a_{ij}) denotes the structure matrix relative to BB [9, p. 600]. Evolution algebras were introduced in connection with non-Mendelian genetics and have since been studied as a class of non-associative algebras [16, 15]. Subsequent work has developed their nilpotency and solvability theory [2, 4, 8, 11], graph-theoretic methods [7, 6], and the structure of evolution algebras satisfying E2=EE^{2}=E [14, 17, 9].

For subspaces U,VU,V of an algebra, write U​VUV for the span of the products u​vuv with u∈Uu\in U and v∈Vv\in V. Define the derived series by

A(1)=A,A(m+1)=A(m)​A(m)(m≥1).A^{(1)}=A,\qquad A^{(m+1)}=A^{(m)}A^{(m)}\quad(m\geq 1).

The algebra AA is solvable if A(m)=0A^{(m)}=0 for some mm [9, p. 602]. An evolution algebra EE is regular (or perfect) if E2=EE^{2}=E [9, p. 600]. An idempotent is an element x∈Ax\in A satisfying x2=xx^{2}=x [9, p. 601]. We call AA idempotent-free if 0 is its only idempotent.

García-Martínez and Pérez-Rodríguez proved that every finite-dimensional complex regular evolution algebra admits a non-zero idempotent [9, Theorem 3.5]. They then conjectured that, for a finite-dimensional complex evolution algebra EE,

(1) E​ is solvable⟺E​ has no non-zero idempotents.E\text{ is solvable}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E\text{ has no non-zero idempotents}.

They verified (1) in dimensions one and two [9, p. 602, following Conjecture 3.6].

A related pair of claims appears in [5, Proposition 3 and Corollary 2], where every SS-graphicable algebra is said to have only the zero idempotent and to be non-solvable. The first claim fails in general. For the SS-graphicable algebra over ℂ\mathbb{C} associated with K2K_{2},

e12=e2,e22=e1,e_{1}^{2}=e_{2},\qquad e_{2}^{2}=e_{1},

the element e1+e2e_{1}+e_{2} is a non-zero idempotent. Thus these claims do not establish a counterexample to (1).

We construct a one-parameter family of non-solvable evolution algebras and determine the idempotents of every member. Proposition 2.4 shows that the squares of distinct members are non-isomorphic, and hence so are the members themselves. Its exceptional member has integral structure constants, is idempotent-free, and gives a three-dimensional counterexample over every field of characteristic different from 22. Over ℂ\mathbb{C}, no counterexample exists in smaller dimension.

The classification in [1, Theorem 3.5] covers all three-dimensional complex evolution algebras. Over ℂ\mathbb{C}, the isomorphism class of E2E_{2} is already listed there. By [6, Proposition 9], only four connected three-vertex pseudodigraph configurations remain potentially solvable. For each complex member of the family in Theorem 2.1, the pseudodigraph associated with the displayed natural basis lies outside those four configurations. The algebra in [16, Example 1.2] is isomorphic to E1E_{1}. Remark 2.5 records the explicit isomorphism, the position of E2E_{2} in the classification, and the difference between the structure-matrix conventions.

Theorem 2.1 determines the idempotents of every member of the family. At λ=2\lambda=2, the algebra E2E_{2} has only the zero idempotent and therefore disproves (1). Its stable term of the derived series is not an evolution algebra, and Proposition 2.8 shows that every finite-dimensional complex counterexample must have this property.

2. Main results

2.1. A non-solvable family and its idempotents

Theorem 2.1.

Let 𝕂\mathbb{K} be a field, let λ∈𝕂×\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}, and let EλE_{\lambda} be the evolution algebra with natural basis B={e1,e2,e3}B=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\} and multiplication

(2) e12=−e1−e3,e22=−2​e1+λ​e2+(λ−2)​e3,e32=e1+e3.e_{1}^{2}=-e_{1}-e_{3},\qquad e_{2}^{2}=-2e_{1}+\lambda e_{2}+(\lambda-2)e_{3},\qquad e_{3}^{2}=e_{1}+e_{3}.

Then EλE_{\lambda} is not solvable. If λ≠2\lambda\neq 2, it has exactly one non-zero idempotent, namely

(3) 1λ​(2−λ)​(e1+e3)+1λ​(e2+e3).\frac{1}{\lambda(2-\lambda)}(e_{1}+e_{3})+\frac{1}{\lambda}(e_{2}+e_{3}).

If λ=2\lambda=2, then 0 is its only idempotent.

Proof.

Put

u=e1+e3,v=e2+e3,u=e_{1}+e_{3},\qquad v=e_{2}+e_{3},

and let S=span⁡{u,v}S=\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}. From (2),

e12=−u,e22=−2​u+λ​v,e32=u.e_{1}^{2}=-u,\qquad e_{2}^{2}=-2u+\lambda v,\qquad e_{3}^{2}=u.

Since λ≠0\lambda\neq 0, we have Eλ2=SE_{\lambda}^{2}=S. The products in SS are

(4) u2=0,u​v=u,v2=−u+λ​v.u^{2}=0,\qquad uv=u,\qquad v^{2}=-u+\lambda v.

Thus S2=SS^{2}=S, because u=u​vu=uv and v=(u+v2)/λv=(u+v^{2})/\lambda. It follows that

Eλ(m)=S(m≥2),E_{\lambda}^{(m)}=S\qquad(m\geq 2),

so EλE_{\lambda} is not solvable.

Let x∈Eλx\in E_{\lambda} be an idempotent. Since x=x2x=x^{2}, we have x∈Eλ2=Sx\in E_{\lambda}^{2}=S. Write x=a​u+b​vx=au+bv. By (4), the equation x2=xx^{2}=x is equivalent to

2​a​b−b2=a,λ​b2=b.2ab-b^{2}=a,\qquad\lambda b^{2}=b.

The second equation implies b=0b=0 or b=1/λb=1/\lambda. If b=0b=0, then the first equation gives a=0a=0. If b=1/λb=1/\lambda and λ≠2\lambda\neq 2, then

a=1λ​(2−λ),a=\frac{1}{\lambda(2-\lambda)},

so xx is the idempotent displayed in (3). If λ=2\lambda=2, then λ∈𝕂×\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{\times} forces char⁡𝕂≠2\operatorname{char}\mathbb{K}\neq 2, and the first equation becomes

a−14=a,a-\frac{1}{4}=a,

which is impossible. The theorem follows. ∎

Specialising to λ=2\lambda=2 gives the counterexample.

Corollary 2.2.

Let 𝕂\mathbb{K} be a field of characteristic different from 22, and let E=E2E=E_{2}. Then EE is a three-dimensional non-solvable evolution algebra whose only idempotent is 0.

Proof.

This is Theorem 2.1 with λ=2\lambda=2. ∎

Lemma 2.3.

Let 𝕂\mathbb{K} be a field, let λ∈𝕂×\lambda\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}, and let EλE_{\lambda} be as in Theorem 2.1. Set Sλ=Eλ2=span⁡{e1+e3,e2+e3}S_{\lambda}=E_{\lambda}^{2}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1}+e_{3},e_{2}+e_{3}\}. Then SλS_{\lambda} is not an evolution algebra.

Proof.

Write u=e1+e3u=e_{1}+e_{3} and v=e2+e3v=e_{2}+e_{3}. Suppose that SλS_{\lambda} has a natural basis

f=a​u+b​v,g=c​u+d​v.f=au+bv,\qquad g=cu+dv.

Then a​d−b​c≠0ad-bc\neq 0 and f​g=0fg=0. Since

u2=0,u​v=u,v2=−u+λ​v,u^{2}=0,\qquad uv=u,\qquad v^{2}=-u+\lambda v,

we have

f​g=(a​d+b​c−b​d)​u+λ​b​d​v.fg=(ad+bc-bd)u+\lambda bdv.

Because λ≠0\lambda\neq 0, the equality f​g=0fg=0 implies b​d=0bd=0. If b=0b=0, then a​d≠0ad\neq 0, but the coefficient of uu in f​gfg is a​dad, a contradiction. If d=0d=0, then b​c≠0bc\neq 0, but the coefficient of uu is b​cbc, again a contradiction. ∎

Proposition 2.4.

Let 𝕂\mathbb{K} be a field and let λ,μ∈𝕂×\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{K}^{\times}. Then Eλ2E_{\lambda}^{2} and Eμ2E_{\mu}^{2} are isomorphic as 𝕂\mathbb{K}-algebras if and only if λ=μ\lambda=\mu. Consequently, EλE_{\lambda} and EμE_{\mu} are isomorphic as 𝕂\mathbb{K}-algebras if and only if λ=μ\lambda=\mu.

Proof.

Only the forward implication in the first assertion requires proof. For ν∈{λ,μ}\nu\in\{\lambda,\mu\}, write Sν=Eν2S_{\nu}=E_{\nu}^{2} and choose uν,vνu_{\nu},v_{\nu} as in the proof of Theorem 2.1. Thus

Sν=span⁡{uν,vν},uν2=0,uν​vν=uν,vν2=−uν+ν​vν.S_{\nu}=\operatorname{span}\{u_{\nu},v_{\nu}\},\qquad u_{\nu}^{2}=0,\qquad u_{\nu}v_{\nu}=u_{\nu},\qquad v_{\nu}^{2}=-u_{\nu}+\nu v_{\nu}.

If x=a​uν+b​vν∈Sνx=au_{\nu}+bv_{\nu}\in S_{\nu} and x2=0x^{2}=0, then the coefficient of vνv_{\nu} in x2x^{2} is ν​b2\nu b^{2}. Since ν≠0\nu\neq 0, this gives b=0b=0. Hence the square-zero elements of SνS_{\nu} form the line 𝕂​uν\mathbb{K}u_{\nu}.

Suppose that φ:Sλ→Sμ\varphi\colon S_{\lambda}\to S_{\mu} is an isomorphism. There are α,β,γ∈𝕂\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{K} with α​γ≠0\alpha\gamma\neq 0 such that

φ​(uλ)=α​uμ,φ​(vλ)=β​uμ+γ​vμ.\varphi(u_{\lambda})=\alpha u_{\mu},\qquad\varphi(v_{\lambda})=\beta u_{\mu}+\gamma v_{\mu}.

Applying φ\varphi to uλ​vλ=uλu_{\lambda}v_{\lambda}=u_{\lambda} gives γ=1\gamma=1. Comparing the coefficients of vμv_{\mu} in

φ​(vλ)2=(2​β−1)​uμ+μ​vμandφ​(vλ2)=(−α+λ​β)​uμ+λ​vμ\varphi(v_{\lambda})^{2}=(2\beta-1)u_{\mu}+\mu v_{\mu}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi(v_{\lambda}^{2})=(-\alpha+\lambda\beta)u_{\mu}+\lambda v_{\mu}

then gives λ=μ\lambda=\mu. The converse is immediate. Finally, any isomorphism Eλ→EμE_{\lambda}\to E_{\mu} maps Eλ2E_{\lambda}^{2} onto Eμ2E_{\mu}^{2}, so the last assertion follows. ∎

Remark 2.5.

For E=E2E=E_{2}, the structure matrix with respect to the displayed natural basis is

MB​(E)=(−10−1−220101).M_{B}(E)=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0&-1\\ -2&2&0\\ 1&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

This matrix has rank two. More generally, the structure matrix of every finite-dimensional complex counterexample to (1) must be singular, since every regular complex evolution algebra has a non-zero idempotent by [9, Theorem 3.5].

Over ℂ\mathbb{C}, put

f1=12​e2,f2=e3,f3=i​e1.f_{1}=\frac{1}{2}e_{2},\qquad f_{2}=e_{3},\qquad f_{3}=\mathrm{i}e_{1}.

This is again a natural basis, and a direct calculation shows that

f12=f1+i2​f3,f22=f2−i​f3,f32=f2−i​f3.f_{1}^{2}=f_{1}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}f_{3},\qquad f_{2}^{2}=f_{2}-\mathrm{i}f_{3},\qquad f_{3}^{2}=f_{2}-\mathrm{i}f_{3}.

Consequently,

M{f1,f2,f3}​(E)=(10i/201−i01−i).M_{\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}}(E)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\mathrm{i}/2\\ 0&1&-\mathrm{i}\\ 0&1&-\mathrm{i}\end{pmatrix}.

In [1, p. 73], structure matrices are transposed relative to our convention, so the tables in that paper must be compared with the transpose of the displayed matrix. The case dimE2=2\dim E^{2}=2 in [1, Theorem 3.5(iii)] already contains the isomorphism class of the counterexample. For the representative E2E_{2}, Theorem 2.1 shows that E2(m)=E22≠0E_{2}^{(m)}=E_{2}^{2}\neq 0 for every m≥2m\geq 2 and that 0 is its only idempotent. Hence E2E_{2} disproves (1).

Over ℂ\mathbb{C}, the algebra AA in [16, Example 1.2] is isomorphic to E1E_{1} via a1↦e1+2​e3a_{1}\mapsto e_{1}+2e_{3}, a2↦2​e1+e3a_{2}\mapsto 2e_{1}+e_{3}, and a3↦e2a_{3}\mapsto e_{2}. In the notation of that example, A2=span⁡{a1+a2,a1+a3}A^{2}=\operatorname{span}\{a_{1}+a_{2},a_{1}+a_{3}\}, and the isomorphism maps A2A^{2} onto E12E_{1}^{2}. Thus E12E_{1}^{2} already fails to be an evolution algebra, whereas the idempotent-free counterexample occurs at λ=2\lambda=2.

2.2. Minimal dimension and counterexamples in all dimensions

Corollary 2.6.

Over ℂ\mathbb{C}, the equivalence between solvability and the absence of non-zero idempotents holds in dimensions at most two and fails in every dimension at least three.

Proof.

In any algebra, solvability implies the absence of non-zero idempotents, since every non-zero idempotent belongs to every term of the derived series. Conversely, in dimension one write e2=λ​ee^{2}=\lambda e. If λ=0\lambda=0, the algebra is solvable. If λ≠0\lambda\neq 0, then λ−1​e\lambda^{-1}e is a non-zero idempotent.

Now let EE be two-dimensional and assume that it has no non-zero idempotents. If its structure matrix had full rank, then E2=EE^{2}=E, making EE regular. It would then have a non-zero idempotent by [9, Theorem 3.5], a contradiction. Thus dimE2≤1\dim E^{2}\leq 1. If E2=0E^{2}=0, then EE is solvable. Otherwise, write E2=ℂ​uE^{2}=\mathbb{C}u. Since u2∈E2u^{2}\in E^{2}, we have u2=λ​uu^{2}=\lambda u for some λ∈ℂ\lambda\in\mathbb{C}. If λ≠0\lambda\neq 0, then λ−1​u\lambda^{-1}u is a non-zero idempotent. Hence u2=0u^{2}=0, and therefore E(3)=0E^{(3)}=0. The equivalence follows in dimensions at most two, while Corollary 2.2 supplies a counterexample in dimension three.

For n>3n>3, let F=E2F=E_{2} be the three-dimensional algebra in Corollary 2.2, let Zn−3Z_{n-3} denote the (n−3)(n-3)-dimensional zero algebra, and put

En=F⊕Zn−3.E_{n}=F\oplus Z_{n-3}.

Combining a natural basis of FF with one of Zn−3Z_{n-3} gives a natural basis of EnE_{n}, so EnE_{n} is an evolution algebra. The proof of Theorem 2.1 gives F(m)=F2≠0F^{(m)}=F^{2}\neq 0 for every m≥2m\geq 2. Hence En(m)=F2⊕0≠0E_{n}^{(m)}=F^{2}\oplus 0\neq 0 for every m≥2m\geq 2, so EnE_{n} is not solvable. If (x,z)∈En(x,z)\in E_{n} is idempotent, then x2=xx^{2}=x and z2=zz^{2}=z. The multiplication on Zn−3Z_{n-3} is zero, so z=0z=0, and Corollary 2.2 then gives x=0x=0. ∎

Remark 2.7.

The minimal-dimension statement in Corollary 2.6 is specific to ℂ\mathbb{C}. Over 𝔽3\mathbb{F}_{3}, the two-dimensional simple evolution algebra EE given by

e12=e1+2​e2,e22=e1+e2e_{1}^{2}=e_{1}+2e_{2},\qquad e_{2}^{2}=e_{1}+e_{2}

has no non-zero idempotents [10, Example 44]. Since E2E^{2} is a non-zero ideal and EE is simple, we have E2=EE^{2}=E, so the algebra is not solvable.

2.3. Stable derived terms and solvability

A sufficient condition for the conjectured equivalence is that the stable term of the derived series be an evolution algebra.

Proposition 2.8.

Let EE be a finite-dimensional complex evolution algebra. The derived series of EE stabilises. Let DD denote its stable term. If DD is an evolution algebra under the induced multiplication, then the following conditions are equivalent.

  1. (i)

    EE is solvable.

  2. (ii)

    EE has no non-zero idempotents.

Proof.

By induction, every derived term is a subalgebra and the derived series is descending. Indeed, E(1)=EE^{(1)}=E is a subalgebra. If E(r)E^{(r)} is a subalgebra, then

E(r+1)=E(r)​E(r)⊆E(r).E^{(r+1)}=E^{(r)}E^{(r)}\subseteq E^{(r)}.

Since E(r+1)⊆E(r)E^{(r+1)}\subseteq E^{(r)}, products of elements of E(r+1)E^{(r+1)} lie in E(r)​E(r)=E(r+1)E^{(r)}E^{(r)}=E^{(r+1)}. Hence E(r+1)E^{(r+1)} is also a subalgebra. Finite-dimensionality therefore gives an index mm such that

D=E(m)=E(m+1).D=E^{(m)}=E^{(m+1)}.

Every idempotent belongs to every derived term by induction from x=x2x=x^{2}, so solvability excludes non-zero idempotents. Conversely, if EE is not solvable, then D≠0D\neq 0 and D2=DD^{2}=D. By hypothesis, DD is an evolution algebra and hence a regular complex evolution algebra. By [9, Theorem 3.5], it contains a non-zero idempotent, which is also an idempotent of EE. ∎

Corollary 2.9.

Let EE be a finite-dimensional complex evolution algebra with natural basis BB, and define the evolution operator LB:E→EL_{B}\colon E\to E by

LB​(ei)=ei2L_{B}(e_{i})=e_{i}^{2}

for every ei∈Be_{i}\in B [15, Definition 5, p. 29]. If LBL_{B} is an algebra endomorphism, then EE is solvable if and only if it has no non-zero idempotents.

Proof.

Under this hypothesis, every term of the derived series is an evolution subalgebra by [8, Proposition 3.1]. In particular, the stable term DD in Proposition 2.8 is an evolution algebra, and the conclusion follows. ∎

Remark 2.10.

For the algebra in Corollary 2.2, the stable term D=E2D=E^{2} satisfies D2=DD^{2}=D and has no non-zero idempotents, but Lemma 2.3 shows that DD is not an evolution algebra. Hence Proposition 2.8 does not apply. The same lemma shows that Eλ2E_{\lambda}^{2} is not an evolution algebra for any member of the family, although Theorem 2.1 gives a non-zero idempotent whenever λ≠2\lambda\neq 2. The evolution operator LBL_{B} for the displayed natural basis B={e1,e2,e3}B=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\} is not an algebra endomorphism either, since LB​(e2​e3)=0L_{B}(e_{2}e_{3})=0 whereas LB​(e2)​LB​(e3)=2​(e1+e3)≠0L_{B}(e_{2})L_{B}(e_{3})=2(e_{1}+e_{3})\neq 0.

Related work treats subalgebras with natural bases extendible to the ambient algebra [3], evolution subalgebras generated by idempotents [13], and closure under subalgebras in the regular case [12]. The counterexample and Proposition 2.8 lead to the following more specific question.

Question 2.11.

For which finite-dimensional evolution algebras is the stable term of the derived series an evolution algebra?
