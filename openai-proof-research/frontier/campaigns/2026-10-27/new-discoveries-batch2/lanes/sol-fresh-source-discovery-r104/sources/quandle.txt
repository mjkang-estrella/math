Abstract.
It was conjectured that the nonzero idempotents of the integral quandle ring of a finite latin quandle are trivial. We prove this holds for latin dihedral quandles. Furthermore, we discuss counterexamples against this conjecture.
Key words and phrases: Quandles, Quandle rings, Affine quandles
2020 Mathematics Subject Classification17D99,20N02
1. Introduction
A quandle is an algebraic structure (Q,⊳)(Q,\triangleright) where the binary operation satisfies the following properties.
(1)
The map Rx:Q→Q,Rx​(y)=y⊳xR_{x}:Q\rightarrow Q,~R_{x}(y)=y\triangleright x is an automorphism for all x∈Qx\in Q.
(2)
Rx​(x)=xR_{x}(x)=x for all x∈Qx\in Q.
An affine quandle Aff⁡(A,f){\rm Aff}(A,f) is an additive abelian group AA with f∈Aut⁡(A)f\in{\rm Aut}(A) with quandle operation x⊳y=f⁡(x)+(1−f)​(y)x\triangleright y=f(x)+(1-f)(y). In particular, if A=ℤnA=\mathbb{Z}_{n} and f⁡(x)=−xf(x)=-x, then the affine quandle is known as a dihedral quandle. We denote it by ℛn\mathcal{R}_{n}.
A quandle ring is defined analogously to a group ring. It is introduced in [1]. Let KK be an associative ring. Consider the following set of finite linear combinations.
K⁡[Q]={∑iax​x;ax∈K,x∈Q}K[Q]=\left\{\sum\limits_{i}a_{x}x\;;a_{x}\in K,x\in Q\right\}
Then K⁡[Q]K[Q] forms an abelian group with the usual addition. The following multiplication equips it with a ring structure.
(∑xax​x)⋅(∑yby​y)=∑x,yax​by​(x⊳y)\left(\sum\limits_{x}a_{x}x\right)\cdot\left(\sum\limits_{y}b_{y}y\right)=\sum\limits_{x,y}a_{x}b_{y}(x\triangleright y)
The mapping ε:K⁡[Q]→K,∑xax​x↦∑xax\varepsilon:K[Q]\rightarrow K,\sum\limits_{x}a_{x}x\mapsto\sum\limits_{x}a_{x} is a ring homomorphism. This is known as augmentation mapping.
The authors give various results about quandles QQ by studying properties of K⁡[Q]K[Q]. Furthermore, the authors in [3] and [2] study the idempotent elements of K⁡[Q]K[Q] and give applications of idempotents in knot theory for the construction of invariants of knots and links. In particular, they state the following conjectures.
Conjecture 1.1.
[2, Conjecture 3.10]
The integral quandle ring of a semi-latin quandle has only trivial idempotents. In particular, the integral quandle ring of a finite latin quandle has only trivial idempotents.
Conjecture 1.2.
[3, Conjecture 7.6]
The Peirce spectrum of the complex quandle algebra of the dihedral quandle of odd order is {0,1,−1}\{0,1,-1\}.
In the section 2, we prove that the integral quandle ring of latin dihedral quandles has only trivial idempotents. Furthermore, in section 3, we compute the Peirce spectrum of ℂ⁡[ℛn]\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{R}_{n}] for nn odd. In section 4, we show that for an integral ring of affine connected quandles of prime order, we can construct infinitely many nontrivial idempotents given a nontrivial idempotent.
