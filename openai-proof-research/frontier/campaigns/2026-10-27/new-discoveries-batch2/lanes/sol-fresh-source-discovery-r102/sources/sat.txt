 Huaqin Li, Lihua You*, Xiaoxue Zhang
 
 
 
 School of Mathematical Sciences
 South China Normal University, Guangzhou, 510631, P. R. China
 2024022055@m.scnu.edu.cn ,  ylhua@scnu.edu.cn ,  zhang_xx1209@163.com 
 
 
 
 Abstract:  A graph  G G  is said to be  H H -saturated if  G G  contains no copy of  H H , but  G + e G+e  contains a copy of  H H  for any edge  e e  of  G ¯ \overline{G} . The saturation number of  H H , denoted by  sat ⁡ ( n , H ) \mathrm{sat}(n,H) , is the minimum number of edges among all  H H -saturated graphs of order  n n . In this paper, we investigate the saturation number of  K 3 ∨ P k K_{3}\vee P_{k}  for  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3  and show that  sat ⁡ ( n , K 3 ∨ P k ) = 3 ​ n − 6 + sat ⁡ ( n − 3 , P k ) \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{3}\vee P_{k})=3n-6+\mathrm{sat}(n-3,P_{k})  holds for all  n ≥ a k + 3 n\geq a_{k}+3  and  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , which verifies the conjecture [Discrete Appl. Math, 394(2026) 62-80] on  sat ⁡ ( n , K s ∨ P k ) \mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s}\vee P_{k})  is true for the case  s = 3 s=3 , where  a k = k a_{k}=k  for  3 ≤ k ≤ 5 3\leq k\leq 5 ,  a k = 3 ⋅ 2 t − 1 − 2 a_{k}=3\cdot 2^{t-1}-2  for  k = 2 ​ t ≥ 6 k=2t\geq 6 , and  a k = 2 t + 1 − 2 a_{k}=2^{t+1}-2  for  k = 2 ​ t + 1 ≥ 7 k=2t+1\geq 7 . 
 
 
 
 Keywords:  Saturation number; Saturation graph; Minimal 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Throughout this paper, we only consider simple, finite, and undirected graphs. 
 
 
 Let  G = ( V ⁡ ( G ) , E ⁡ ( G ) ) G=(V(G),E(G))  be a graph with vertex set  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  and edge set  E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) . The order of  G G  is  | V ⁡ ( G ) | |V(G)|  and the size of  G G  is  | E ⁡ ( G ) | |E(G)| . A path, complete graph, or cycle of order  n n  is denoted by  P n P_{n} ,  K n K_{n}  or  C n C_{n} , respectively. For a vertex  u ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u\in V(G) , the set of neighbors of  u u  in  G G  is denoted by  N G ​ ( u ) N_{G}(u) ,  d G ​ ( u ) = | N G ​ ( u ) | d_{G}(u)=|N_{G}(u)|  is the degree of  u u  in  G G , and  N G ​ [ u ] = N G ​ ( u ) ∪ { u } N_{G}[u]=N_{G}(u)\cup\{u\}  is the closed neighborhood of  u u  in  G G . For  u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u,v\in V(G) , the distance between  u u  and  v v  is the length of the shortest path connecting  u u  and  v v  in  G G , and is denoted by  d G ​ ( u , v ) d_{G}(u,v) . The diameter of  G G  is the greatest distance between two vertices of  G G , denoted by  diam ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{diam}(G) . Let  δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G)  and  Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G)  be the minimum degree and maximum degree of vertices in  G G , respectively. When no ambiguity arises, we use  N ⁡ ( u ) N(u) ,  d ⁡ ( u ) d(u) ,  N ⁡ [ u ] N[u] ,  d ⁡ ( u , v ) d(u,v) ,  δ \delta  and  Δ \Delta  instead of  N G ​ ( u ) N_{G}(u) ,  d G ​ ( u ) d_{G}(u) ,  N G ​ [ u ] N_{G}[u] ,  d G ​ ( u , v ) d_{G}(u,v) ,  δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G)  and  Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) , respectively. A vertex of degree zero is called an isolated vertex. An independent set in a graph is a set of vertices no two of which are adjacent. For  X ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq V(G) , we use  G ⁡ [ X ] G[X]  to denote the subgraph of  G G  induced by  X X . For  X ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq V(G)  and  Y ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ X Y\subseteq V(G)\setminus X ,  E G ​ [ X , Y ] E_{G}[X,Y]  is the set of edges of  G G  between  X X  and  Y Y , denoted simply by  E ⁡ [ X , Y ] E[X,Y] . The union of two graphs  G 1 G_{1}  and  G 2 G_{2}  is the graph  G 1 ∪ G 2 G_{1}\cup G_{2}  with vertex set  V ⁡ ( G 1 ) ∪ V ⁡ ( G 2 ) V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})  and edge set  E ⁡ ( G 1 ) ∪ E ⁡ ( G 2 ) E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2}) . The join of graphs  G 1 G_{1}  and  G 2 G_{2}  is obtained from  G 1 ∪ G 2 G_{1}\cup G_{2}  by joining each vertex of  G 1 G_{1}  to each vertex of  G 2 G_{2} , denoted by  G 1 ∨ G 2 G_{1}\vee G_{2} . 
 
 
 A graph  H H  is called a subgraph of  G G  if  V ⁡ ( H ) ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) V(H)\subseteq V(G)  and  E ⁡ ( H ) ⊆ E ⁡ ( G ) E(H)\subseteq E(G) . A copy of  H H  in  G G  is a subgraph of  G G  which is isomorphic to  H H . A graph  G G  is called  H H -saturated if  G G  contains no copy of  H H , but  G + e G+e  contains a copy of  H H  for any edge  e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ¯ ) e\in E(\overline{G}) . The saturation number of  H H , denoted by  sat ⁡ ( n , H ) \operatorname{sat}(n,H) , is the minimum number of edges among all  H H -saturated graphs of order  n n . If  G G  is an  H H -saturated graph with  sat ⁡ ( n , H ) \operatorname{sat}(n,H)  edges, we say  G G  is a minimal  H H -saturated graph. 
 
 
 The concept of the saturation number was introduced by Erdős, Hajnal, and Moon   [ 11 ]  in 1964. They established the exact value for complete graphs as  sat ⁡ ( n , K p ) = ( p − 2 ) ​ ( n − p + 2 ) + ( p − 2 2 ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{p})=(p-2)(n-p+2)+\binom{p-2}{2}  and characterized  K p − 2 ∨ K ¯ n − p + 2 K_{p-2}\vee\overline{K}_{n-p+2}  as the unique minimal saturated graph. Since then, many exact saturation numbers are now known. These include stars, paths, and matchings  [ 22 ] ; cycles  [ 7 ,  8 ,  15 ,  17 ,  23 ,  24 ,  29 ] ; complete bipartite and multipartite graphs  [ 1 ,  9 ,  18 ,  21 ] ; nearly complete graphs  [ 13 ] ; linear forests  [ 3 ,  5 ,  12 ] ; vertex-disjoint cliques  [ 4 ,  14 ,  30 ] ; regular saturated graphs  [ 16 ,  28 ] ; hypergraphs  [ 25 ] ; books and generalized books  [ 6 ]  and so on. More results can be found in  [ 10 ] . 
 
 
 The join of graphs is an important binary operation in graph theory, and the saturation numbers concerning the joins of certain specific graphs have attracted considerable attention. In 2024, Hu, Luo, and Peng  [ 20 ]  showed that if  D D  is a graph without isolated vertices, then  sat ⁡ ( n , K s ∨ D ) = ( s 2 ) + s ⁡ ( n − s ) + sat ⁡ ( n − s , D ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{s}\vee D)=\binom{s}{2}+s(n-s)+\operatorname{sat}(n-s,D) 
holds when  s ≥ 1 s\geq 1  and  n ≥ 3 ​ s 2 − s + 2 ​ sat ⁡ ( n − s , D ) + 1 n\geq 3s^{2}-s+2\operatorname{sat}(n-s,D)+1 . However, Qiu, He, Lu, and Xu  [ 26 ]  pointed out that under the condition  n ≥ 3 ​ s 2 − s + 2 ​ sat ⁡ ( n − s , D ) + 1 n\geq 3s^{2}-s+2\operatorname{sat}(n-s,D)+1 , the graph  D D  must contain a  K 2 K_{2} . Therefore, it is interested to investigate the saturation numbers when  D D  does not contain a  K 2 K_{2} . Qiu, He, Lu, and Xu  [ 26 ]  proved that  sat ⁡ ( n , K 1 ∨ C k ) = n − 1 + sat ⁡ ( n − 1 , C k ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee C_{k})=n-1+\operatorname{sat}(n-1,C_{k})  for  k ≥ 8 k\geq 8  and  n ≥ 56 ​ k 3 n\geq 56k^{3} . When  k = 4 k=4 , Song, Hu, Ji, and Cui  [ 27 ]  proved that  sat ⁡ ( n , K 1 ∨ C 4 ) = n − 1 + sat ⁡ ( n − 1 , C 4 ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee C_{4})=n-1+\operatorname{sat}(n-1,C_{4})  for  n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 . In 2025, Hu, Ji, and Cui  [ 19 ]  proved that  sat ⁡ ( n , K 1 ∨ P k ) = n − 1 + sat ⁡ ( n − 1 , P k ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{1}\vee P_{k})=n-1+\operatorname{sat}(n-1,P_{k})  for  k ≥ 5 k\geq 5  and sufficiently large  n n . Furthermore, in 2026, Zhang, You, and Zhao  [ 31 ]  proved that  sat ⁡ ( n , K 2 ∨ P k ) = 2 ​ n − 3 + sat ⁡ ( n − 2 , P k ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{2}\vee P_{k})=2n-3+\operatorname{sat}(n-2,P_{k})  for  n ≥ a k + 2 n\geq a_{k}+2  and  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , and proposed a conjecture about  sat ⁡ ( n , K s ∨ P k ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{s}\vee P_{k})  as follows.

 Then  sat ⁡ ( n , K s ∨ P k ) = ( s 2 ) + s ⁡ ( n − s ) + sat ⁡ ( n − s , P k ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{s}\vee P_{k})=\binom{s}{2}+s(n-s)+\operatorname{sat}(n-s,P_{k}) .

 4  Conclusion 

 
 In this paper, we investigate the saturation number of  K 3 ∨ P k K_{3}\vee P_{k}  for  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3  and  n ≥ a k + 3 n\geq a_{k}+3  and show Theorem  1.2  holds by showing ( 3.1 ) holds.
In the proof, we adapt the vertex-partition method of  [ 31 ] ,
introduce the sets  V K 3 V_{K_{3}} ,  V N ​ K 3 V_{NK_{3}} ,  U ≥ p U_{\geq p}  to obtain a further refinement,
define the notation  G K 3 G_{K_{3}} ,  G N ​ K 3 G_{NK_{3}} ,  r = | C ⁡ ( Q ) ∩ U | r=|C(Q)\cap U|  and so on, and obtain the lower bound on  | E ⁡ ( G ) | |E(G)|  given by a simpler expression. 
 
 
 Furthermore, natural problems are to characterize all minimal  ( K 3 ∨ P k ) (K_{3}\vee P_{k}) - saturated graphs and to investigate whether analogous local reductions can determine  sat ⁡ ( n , K s ∨ P k ) \operatorname{sat}(n,K_{s}\vee P_{k})  for larger fixed values of  s s [ 31 ] .
