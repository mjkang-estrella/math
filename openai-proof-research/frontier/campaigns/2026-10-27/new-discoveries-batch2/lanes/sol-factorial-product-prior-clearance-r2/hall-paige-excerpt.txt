                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                           A bijection θ : G Ñ G of a finite group G is a complete mapping of G if the mapping
                                           x Ñ xθpxq is a bijection of G. Complete mappings were introduced by Mann in 1942
                                           in connection with the construction of orthogonal Latin squares [Man42], and were
                                           subsequently studied systematically by Paige [Pai51]. They arise naturally from the
                                           Cayley table of a group: a finite group admits a complete mapping if and only if its
                                           Cayley table has a transversal, or equivalently an orthogonal mate.
                                           Hall and Paige proved in 1955 that a necessary condition for a finite group G
                                           to admit a complete mapping is that the Sylow 2-subgroup of G is either trivial
                                           or noncyclic, and conjectured that this condition is also sufficient [HP55]. They
                                           proved the conjecture for several important classes of groups, including all solvable
                                           groups. The conjecture remained open for more than fifty years and was eventually
                                           completed through the work of Wilcox, Evans and Bray [Wil09, Eva09]. Thus a
                                           finite group admits a complete mapping if and only if its Sylow 2-subgroup is trivial
                                           or noncyclic. Recently, complete mappings and the Hall–Paige theorem have been
                                           revisited from different points of view; the work of Eberhard, Manners and Mrazovi’c
                                           obtained asymptotic formulae for the number of complete mappings of large groups
                                           in [EMM22], while Müyesser and Pokrovskiy use techniques from probabilistic
                                           combinatorics to prove a "random version" of the Hall–Paige theorem [MP25].
                                           A related notion is that of an orthomorphism. A bijection θ : G Ñ G of a finite
                                           group G is called an orthomorphism if the mapping x Ñ x´1 θpxq is also a bijection,
                                           and θ is called a strong complete mapping if both x ÞÑ xθpxq and x ÞÑ x´1 θpxq are
                                           bijections. Thus a strong complete mapping is simultaneously a complete mapping
                                           and an orthomorphism. Evans raised the problem of characterising the groups which
                                           contain strong complete mappings (see [Eva12]). In particular, Evans proved that
                                           a finite abelian group admits a strong complete mapping if and only if neither its

The purpose of the present paper is to consider a simultaneous version of complete
mappings. Instead of a single bijection being both a complete mapping and an
orthomorphism (as in the case of strong complete maps) we require one bijection
to form a complete-mapping-type pair with each automorphism in an arbitrary
collection of automorphisms. It is worth mentioning that the definition of property Pk
arose from our attempt to generalize the findings in [LS26] to over three subgroups,
specifically, by seeking common transversals for four or more subgroups of the same
index within an abelian group (see Section 4).(A transversal of a subgroup H in a
group G is a set with one representative from each coset of H in G.)
We define the property Pk for any integer k ě 1 as follows.

Definition 1. We say that a group G satisfies Pk , for some k ě 1, if for every β1 , . . . , βk P
AutpGq, there exists a bijection f of G such that every map g ÞÑ βi pgq f pgq is a bijection
of G.

The case k “ 1 is exactly the classical complete-mapping problem. Indeed, if a
complete map f exists in G then for any β1 P AutpGq, the map f ˝ β1 is a bijection of
G while g Ñ β1 pgq f pβ1 pgqq is also a bijection. Hence the Hall–Paige theorem gives a
complete characterization of the groups satisfying P1 .
Things get more interesting when k “ 2. While every abelian group satisfying
property P2 is straightforwardly shown to possess a strong complete map, these
two properties are not equivalent even for abelian groups. For instance, consider
the abelian group G “ Z2 ˆ Z2 . According to Evans’ criterion for abelian groups,
G admits a strong complete mapping. However, property P2 fails for the pair of
automorphisms β1 , id, where β1 interchanges the two copies of Z2 , i.e., β1 p1, 0q “ p0, 1q
and β1 p0, 1q “ p1, 0q. (This can be easily verified as follows; first translate f so that
f pp0, 0qq “ p0, 0q. If both f and id ` f are permutations on the 3 non-zero elements
of G, then f acts on these as a fixed-point-free permutation, hence as one of the
two 3-cycles. But then one of those cycles gives pβ1 ` f qpp1, 0qq “ p0, 0q, and the other
gives pβ1 ` f qpp0, 1qq “ p0, 0q, while pβ1 ` f qpp0, 0qq “ p0, 0q.)
Our main existence result reads:

Theorem A. Assume G is a finite group. Let p be the smallest prime divisor of the
order |G| of G. Then G satisfies Pk for all 1 ď k ă p ´ 1.

As an application we prove the following (see Section 4 for all the relevant definitions):


Corollary 1. Let G be a finite group whose smallest prime divisor is p. If k ă p ´ 1,
then for any automorphisms β1 , . . . , βk P AutpGq the Latin squares Li px, yq “ βi pxqy
have a common transversal and a common orthogonal mate.

Theorem A offers insights beyond the Hall-Paige theorem only for odd-order groups
(and thus solvable), where k ě 2. For its proof we first proved it for finite abelian

The threshold p ´ 1 cannot be improved in general; we prove that cyclic p-groups do
not satisfy Pp´1 for any prime p (see Theorem 5 ). On the other hand, we show that the
obstruction at p ´ 1 disappears for non-cyclic homocyclic p-groups: every such group
satisfies Pp´1 , with the simultaneous bijection chosen (again) to be an automorphism
(see Proposition 2). These results suggest the following natural analogue, for abelian
p-groups, of the Hall–Paige characterization: does every noncyclic finite abelian
p-group satisfy Pp´1 ? We have not managed to answer this question.
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we prove an extension result that
groups with property Pk satisfy and prove Theorem A along with special results for
abelian groups and homocyclic p-groups. In Section 3 we prove that the upper bound
k ă p ´ 1 is sharp and finally in Section 4 we apply the theory to Latin squares and
transversals of subgroups.
