Abstract
Let GG be a graph of order nn. For a positive integer pp, GG is said to be
a 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph if n≥pn\geq p and every pp pairwise disjoint
independent sets of GG are contained within pp pairwise disjoint maximum
independent sets. In this paper, we establish that every connected
𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph GG is pp-quasi-regularizable if and only if
n≥(p+1)⋅αn\geq(p+1)\cdot\alpha, where α\alpha is the independence number of GG and
p≠2p\neq 2. This finding ensures that the independence polynomial of a connected
𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph GG is log-concave whenever (p+1)⋅α≤n≤p⋅α+2​p⋅α+p(p+1)\cdot\alpha\leq n\leq p\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{p\cdot\alpha+p} and α24​(α+1)≤p\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\left(\alpha+1\right)}\leq p, or p⋅α+2​p⋅α+p<n≤(α2+1)⋅p+(α−1)2α−1p\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{p\cdot\alpha+p}<n\leq\frac{\allowbreak\left(\alpha^{2}+1\right)\cdot p+\left(\alpha-1\right)^{2}}{\alpha-1} and α⁡(α−1)α+1≤p\frac{\alpha\left(\alpha-1\right)}{\alpha+1}\leq p.
Moreover, the clique corona graph G∘KpG\circ K_{p} serves as an example of the
𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph class. We further demonstrate that the independence
polynomial of G∘KpG\circ K_{p} is always log-concave for sufficiently large pp.
Keywords: very well-covered graph; quasi-regularizable
graph; corona graph; 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph; independence polynomial;
log-concavity.

1 Introduction
Throughout this paper GG is a finite, undirected, loopless graph without
multiple edges, with vertex set V⁡(G)V(G) of cardinality |V⁡(G)|=n⁡(G)\left|V\left(G\right)\right|=n\left(G\right), and edge set E⁡(G)E(G) of size
|E⁡(G)|=m⁡(G)\left|E\left(G\right)\right|=m\left(G\right). An edge
e∈E⁡(G)e\in E(G) connecting vertices xx and yy will also be denoted as x​yxy or
y​xyx. In this case, xx and yy are said to be adjacent. An
independent set in GG is a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices. A
maximal independent set of GG is the one that cannot be extended
further. A largest independent set in a graph is called a maximum
independent set, and its cardinality is denoted by α⁡(G)\alpha(G), known as the
independence number of GG. It is well known that α⁡(G)=1\alpha(G)=1 if
and only if GG is a complete graph.
Let AA be a subset of V⁡(G)V(G). We denote G⁡[A]G[A] the induced subgraph of GG on
AA, while by G−AG-A we mean G⁡[V⁡(G)−A]G[V(G)-A]. The neighborhood of AA in GG is the
set
NG​(A)={v:v∈V⁡(G)−A​ and ​u​v∈E⁡(G)​ for some ​u∈A},N_{G}(A)=\{v:v\in V(G)-A\text{ and }uv\in E(G)\text{ for some }u\in A\},
the closed neighborhood of SS is NG​[A]=A∪NG​(A)N_{G}[A]=A\cup N_{G}(A), and the
localization of GG with respect to AA is GA=G−NG​[A]G_{A}=G-N_{G}[A]. If
A={v}A=\{v\}, we write NG​(v)N_{G}(v) (resp. NG​[v]N_{G}[v], GvG_{v}, G−vG-v) instead of
NG​({v})N_{G}(\{v\}) (resp. NG​[{v}]N_{G}[\{v\}], G{v}G_{\{v\}}, G−{v}G-\{v\}). The number
degG⁡(v)=|NG​(v)|\deg_{G}(v)=\left|N_{G}(v)\right| is called the degree of
vv in GG. A vertex of degree zero is an isolated vertex. In
addition, δ⁡(G)\delta(G) is the minimum degree of vertices of GG.
A graph is called well-covered if all its maximal independent sets
have the same size [32, 33]. For instance, all complete graph
on nn vertices, denoted by KnK_{n}, is well-covered and the only cycles that
are well-covered are C3,C4,C5,C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}, and C7C_{7}. Additionally, the path
on nn vertices, denoted by PnP_{n}, is well-covered if and only if n=1,2,4n=1,2,4.
In this paper, we extend the exploration of well-covered graphs. In 1975,
Staples introduced the hereditary 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} as a generalization of
well-covered graphs [35, 36]. For a positive integer pp,
a graph GG belongs to class 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} if n⁡(G)≥pn(G)\geq p and for every
pp pairwise disjoint independent sets A1,…,ApA_{1},\ldots,A_{p} there exist pp
pairwise disjoint maximum independent sets S1,…,SpS_{1},\ldots,S_{p} in GG such
that Ai⊆SiA_{i}\subseteq S_{i} for 1≤i≤p1\leq i\leq p. The graph G∈𝐖pG\in\mathbf{W}_{p} is also called the 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph. Observe, by
definition, that a graph is 𝐖1\mathbf{W}_{1} if and only if it is
well-covered, and
𝐖1⊇𝐖2⊇𝐖3⊇⋯⊇𝐖p⊇⋯.\mathbf{W}_{1}\supseteq\mathbf{W}_{2}\supseteq\mathbf{W}_{3}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathbf{W}_{p}\supseteq\cdots.
Various methods for constructing 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs are presented in
details in [31, 36].
A well-covered graph (with at least two vertices) is 11-well-covered
if the deletion of any vertex of the graph leaves it well-covered
[36]. For instance, K2K_{2} is 11-well-covered, while a path on
four vertices P4P_{4} is well-covered, but not 11-well-covered. Furthermore,
the close relationship between 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs and 11-well-covered
graphs is demonstrated as follows.
Theorem 1.1
[35, 36] Let GG be a graph without isolated
vertices. Then GG is 11-well-covered if and only if G\ G is a
𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph.
The 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs exhibit numerous intriguing properties and
characteristics, both in combinatorics and commutative algebra, as detailed in
[15, 27, 29, 30, 33]. To illustrate, consider the polynomial
ring R=K⁡[x1,…,xn]R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] in nn variables over a field KK, and let GG
be a graph with vertex set V={1,…,n}V=\{1,\ldots,n\}. We associate to the graph GG a
quadratic square-free monomial ideal
I⁡(G)=(xi​xj∣i​j∈E⁡(G))⊆R,I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid ij\in E(G))\subseteq R,
which is called the edge ideal of GG. We say that GG is
Cohen–Macaulay (resp. Gorenstein) if I⁡(G)I(G) is a
Cohen–Macaulay (resp. Gorenstein) ideal. Notably, every Gorenstein graph is
Cohen–Macaulay, while the converse is not generally true.
It has been established that GG is well-covered whenever it is
Cohen–Macaulay [38, Proposition 6.1.21], and GG belongs to
𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} whenever it is Gorenstein [15, Lemma 2.5]. For
triangle-free graphs, 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs are also Gorenstein
[15, Theorem 4.4]. However, in general, not all 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs
are Gorenstein. An example of a 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph that is always
Cohen–Macaulay is the clique corona graph. Let ℋ={Hv:v∈V⁡(G)}\mathcal{H}=\{H_{v}:v\in V(G)\} be a family of non-empty graphs indexed by the vertex set of a graph
GG. The corona G∘ℋG\circ\mathcal{H} of GG and ℋ\mathcal{H} is
defined as the disjoint union of GG and HvH_{v} for each v∈V⁡(G)v\in V(G), with
additional edges connecting each vertex v∈V⁡(G)v\in V(G) to all the vertices of
HvH_{v} [10]. When all graphs HvH_{v} in ℋ\mathcal{H} are
complete graphs, G∘ℋG\circ\mathcal{H} is referred to as a clique
corona graph. A clique corona graph is not only a well-covered graph
[37, Theorem 1], but also a Cohen–Macaulay graph [14, Theorem
2.6]. If Hv=KpH_{v}=K_{p} for every v∈V⁡(G)v\in V(G), we use G∘KpG\circ K_{p} to
denote G∘ℋG\circ\mathcal{H}.
Definition 1.2
[26] For λ>0\lambda>0, a graph GG is λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable
if
λ⋅|S|≤|NG​(S)|,\lambda\cdot\left|S\right|\leq|N_{G}(S)|,
for every independent set SS of GG.
If λ=1\lambda=1, then GG is said to be a quasi-regularizable graph
[3].
Theorem 1.3
[3] Every well-covered graph without isolated vertices
is quasi-regularizable.
It is noteworthy pointing out that the structure of λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable graph implies the correlation between the number of
vertices with the independence number. In particular, by the theorem above,
one obtains that n⁡(G)≥2​α​(G)n\left(G\right)\geq 2\alpha\left(G\right) holds for
every well-covered graph GG. Furthermore, if GG is a 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph
with p≥2p\geq 2, then it is (p−1)(p-1)-quasi-regularizable [13, Theorem 2.6
(iii)]. We have also conjectured that GG is pp-quasi-regularizable
[13, Conjecture 2.7]. However, it turned out that this assertion does
not hold in general. For example, C5∈𝐖2C_{5}\in\mathbf{W}_{2}, but it is not
22-quasi-regularizable. Nonetheless, in Section 2, we confirm the following.
Theorem 2.8 Let GG be a connected WpW_{p} graph, where p≠2p\neq 2. Then GG is pp-quasi-regularizable if and only if n⁡(G)≥(p+1)⋅α⁡(G)n(G)\geq(p+1)\cdot\alpha(G).
Beyond exploring graph structures, graph theory presents numerous intriguing
problems related to log-concavity, a concept that has deep implications in
combinatorics and algebra [34]. For instance, it is well-known that the
matching polynomial of a graph has only real zeros, making it log-concave
[12]. This property is significant for understanding graph matchings
and their applications in network theory. Recently, a major breakthrough was
achieved with the resolution of the log-concavity of the chromatic polynomial
of a graph [17], which has important implications for graph coloring
and phase transitions in statistical physics. Another key polynomial
associated with a graph is the independence polynomial of a graph
GG, denoted I⁡(G,x)I(G;x), which is defined in [11] as follows:
I⁡(G,x)=∑k=0α⁡(G)sk​xk=s0+s1​x+⋯+sα⁡(G)​xα⁡(G),I(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{k}x^{k}=s_{0}+s_{1}x+\cdots+s_{\alpha(G)}x^{\alpha(G)},
where sks_{k} represents the number of independent sets of cardinality kk in
the graph GG. The independence polynomial I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is said to be:
•
log-concave if sk2≥sk−1⋅sk+1s_{k}^{2}\geq s_{k-1}\cdot s_{k+1} for all
1≤k≤α⁡(G)−11\leq k\leq\alpha(G)-1; or
•
unimodal if there exists an index 0≤k≤α⁡(G)0\leq k\leq\alpha(G) such
that
s0≤⋯≤sk−1≤sk≥sk+1≥⋯≥sα⁡(G).s_{0}\leq\cdots\leq s_{k-1}\leq s_{k}\geq s_{k+1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{\alpha(G)}.
A well-known result by Chudnovsky and Seymour in [7] states that all the
roots of I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) are real whenever GG is a claw-free graph, which also
implies the log-concavity of I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) for all claw-free graphs GG. The
study of the independence polynomial is a rich area with extensive literature,
including works addressing the log-concavity problems (see [1, 7, 16, 22, 24, 26, 39, 40] and their references).
Lemma 1.4
[19]  If P⁡(x)P(x) is log-concave and Q⁡(x)Q(x) is unimodal,
then P⁡(x)⋅Q⁡(x)P(x)\cdot Q(x) is unimodal, while the product of two log-concave
polynomials is log-concave.
In [1], Alavi, Malde, Schwenk, and Erdös proved that for any
permutation π\pi of {1,2,…,α⁡(G)}\{1,2,\dots,\alpha(G)\}, there is a graph GG such
that
sπ⁡(1)<sπ⁡(2)<⋯<sπ⁡(α⁡(G)).s_{\pi(1)}<s_{\pi(2)}<\cdots<s_{\pi(\alpha(G))}.
This result highlights the varied behaviors that graph polynomials can
display. Additionally, they conjectured that the independence polynomial
I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is unimodal for any tree or forest GG. This conjecture remains
unresolved and continues to inspire ongoing research. Recently, it was
demonstrated that there are infinite families of trees whose independence
polynomials are not log-concave [18]. This finding challenges earlier
assumptions and suggests new directions for exploring the conditions under
which log-concavity and unimodality hold. It is also worth noting that the
independence polynomials of some well-covered graphs are not log-concave
[26], [28].
Revisiting the open conjecture of the unimodality of independence polynomials
of trees stated in [1], it is known that I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave
whenever GG is a well-covered spider [25]. Additionally, Radcliffe
verified that the independence polynomials of trees with up to 2525 vertices
are log-concave [2]. Zhu and Chen, in [40], applied
factorization methods to show the log-concavity of independence polynomials in
some special cases of trees. In contrast, in [18], the authors
demonstrated that there exist exactly two trees of order 2626 whose
independence polynomials are not log-concave.
It is known that for every well-covered graph GG whose girth⁡(G)≥6\mathrm{girth}(G)\geq 6 and G≠{C7,K1}G\neq\{C_{7},K_{1}\} then GG is well-covered if and only if G=H∘K1G=H\circ K_{1} for some graph HH [9]. The conjecture about the
unimodality of I⁡(G∘K1,x)I(G\circ K_{1};x) for all graphs GG was stated in
[23, Conjecture 3.3], validated in the case α⁡(G)≤4\alpha\left(G\right)\leq 4 in [23, Conjecture 3.3] and extended to 5≤α⁡(G)≤85\leq\alpha\left(G\right)\leq 8 in [6]. In addition, the unimodality of the
independence polynomial of clique corona graphs G∘KpG\circ K_{p} was
investigated for various classes of graphs such as: GG is a claw-free graph
[24, Corollary 3.12]; GG is a quasi-regularizable graph with
α⁡(G)≤4\alpha(G)\leq 4; GG is an arbitrary graph and pp satisfying (p+1)​(p+2)≥n⁡(G)+1(p+1)(p+2)\geq n(G)+1; and GG is quasi-regularizable graph with α⁡(G)≤p+1\alpha(G)\leq p+1
[13]. As an application, it was proven that I⁡(Sn∘Kp,x)I(S_{n}\circ K_{p};x) is
unimodal for all p≥n+1−2p\geq\sqrt{n+1}-2, where SnS_{n} is the complete bipartite
graph K1,nK_{1,n} [13]. Furthermore, for any graph HH, taking into
account that H∘Kp∈𝐖pH\circ K_{p}\in\mathbf{W}_{p} [13, Corollary 2.3], the
log-concavity of independent polynomials of 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs can be
further employed in this specific ones. Consequently, it yields that I⁡(G∘Kp,x)I(G\circ K_{p};x) is log-concave whenever pp is large enough in correspondence to
n⁡(G).n(G).
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we study some
structural properties of 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs. Section 3 presents
findings related to log-concave properties of I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) for 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p}
graphs GG. As an application, the remainder of Section 3 is
dedicated to examining the log-concavity of I⁡(H∘Kp,x)I(H\circ K_{p};x) for
sufficiently large pp. Finally, in Conclusion, we suggest potential
directions for future research.

Let GG be a well-covered graph. Then

[5, 33] GvG_{v} is well-covered and α⁡(Gv)=α⁡(G)−1\alpha(G_{v})=\alpha(G)-1

[9, Lemma 1] If SS is an independent set of GG, then GSG_{S}

Let GG be a 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph with α⁡(G)>1\alpha(G)>1. Then

[29, Theorem 2] GvG_{v} is also a 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph for

[36]  Let p≥2p\geq 2 and let GG be a connected

Then the following assertions are true.
(i)

(ii)

Clearly, Lemma 2.4 holds true for well-covered graphs as well.
Lemma 2.5
If GG is a 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph, then every connected
component of GG contains at least pp vertices, and, consequently, n⁡(G)≥p⋅c⁡(G)n(G)\geq p\cdot c\left(G\right), where c⁡(G)c\left(G\right) is the number of

Proof. By the definition of 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs, n⁡(G)≥pn(G)\geq p. Let v1,v2,…,vp∈V⁡(G)v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{p}\in V\left(G\right). Hence, there exists pp pairwise

Let HH be a connected component of GG. Due to the maximum of the independent
set SiS_{i}, we have |Si∩V⁡(H)|>0\left|S_{i}\cap V(H)\right|>0 for all 1≤i≤p1\leq i\leq p. Therefore, S1∩V⁡(H),S2∩V⁡(H),…,Sp∩V⁡(H)S_{1}\cap V(H),S_{2}\cap V(H),\ldots,S_{p}\cap V(H) are pp pairwise disjoint non-empty maximum independent sets in
HH. This implies that n⁡(H)≥pn(H)\geq p, and consequently, n⁡(G)≥p⋅c⁡(G)n(G)\geq p\cdot c(G).

A graph is 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} if and only if each of its
every connected component is also 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p}.

4 Conclusion
This paper primarily addresses problems related to log-concavity
of 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs. Our results indicate that the independence
polynomial I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave whenever pp is sufficiently large
relative to the independence number α⁡(G)\alpha(G) of GG. Furthermore, Zhu, in
[39, Corollary 3.3], demonstrated the log-concave preservation of the
independence polynomial of G∘KpG\circ K_{p} whenever the independence polynomial
of GG is log-concave. Theorem 3.5 leads us to the following.
Problem 4.1
What conditions on the 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph GG guarantee that the
independence polynomial I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave or at least unimodal?
In addition, Theorem 2.8 motivates the following.
Conjecture 4.2
Let GG be a connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph. Then GG is 22-quasi-regularizable if and only if n⁡(G)≥3⋅α⁡(G)n(G)\geq 3\cdot\alpha(G).
