 Abstract 
    
 Let  G G  be a graph of order  n n . For a positive integer  p p ,  G G  is said to be
a  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph if  n ≥ p n\geq p  and every  p p  pairwise disjoint
independent sets of  G G  are contained within  p p  pairwise disjoint maximum
independent sets. In this paper, we establish that every connected
 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph  G G  is  p p -quasi-regularizable if and only if
 n ≥ ( p + 1 ) ⋅ α n\geq(p+1)\cdot\alpha , where  α \alpha  is the independence number of  G G  and
 p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 . This finding ensures that the independence polynomial of a connected
 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph  G G  is log-concave whenever  ( p + 1 ) ⋅ α ≤ n ≤ p ⋅ α + 2 ​ p ⋅ α + p (p+1)\cdot\alpha\leq n\leq p\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{p\cdot\alpha+p}  and  α 2 4 ​ ( α + 1 ) ≤ p \frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\left(\alpha+1\right)}\leq p , or  p ⋅ α + 2 ​ p ⋅ α + p < n ≤ ( α 2 + 1 ) ⋅ p + ( α − 1 ) 2 α − 1 p\cdot\alpha+2\sqrt{p\cdot\alpha+p}<n\leq\frac{\allowbreak\left(\alpha^{2}+1\right)\cdot p+\left(\alpha-1\right)^{2}}{\alpha-1}  and  α ⁡ ( α − 1 ) α + 1 ≤ p \frac{\alpha\left(\alpha-1\right)}{\alpha+1}\leq p .
Moreover, the clique corona graph  G ∘ K p G\circ K_{p}  serves as an example of the
 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph class. We further demonstrate that the independence
polynomial of  G ∘ K p G\circ K_{p}  is always log-concave for sufficiently large  p p . 
    
 Keywords : very well-covered graph; quasi-regularizable
graph; corona graph;  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph; independence polynomial;
log-concavity.

 1  Introduction 

 
 Throughout this paper  G G  is a finite, undirected, loopless graph without
multiple edges, with vertex set  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  of cardinality  | V ⁡ ( G ) | = n ⁡ ( G ) \left|V\left(G\right)\right|=n\left(G\right) , and edge set  E ⁡ ( G ) E(G)  of size
 | E ⁡ ( G ) | = m ⁡ ( G ) \left|E\left(G\right)\right|=m\left(G\right) . An edge
 e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e\in E(G)  connecting vertices  x x  and  y y  will also be denoted as  x ​ y xy  or
 y ​ x yx . In this case,  x x  and  y y  are said to be  adjacent . An
 independent set  in  G G  is a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices. A
 maximal independent set  of  G G  is the one that cannot be extended
further. A largest independent set in a graph is called a  maximum
independent set , and its cardinality is denoted by  α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G) , known as the
 independence number  of  G G . It is well known that  α ⁡ ( G ) = 1 \alpha(G)=1  if
and only if  G G  is a complete graph. 
 
 
 Let  A A  be a subset of  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . We denote  G ⁡ [ A ] G[A]  the induced subgraph of  G G  on
 A A , while by  G − A G-A  we mean  G ⁡ [ V ⁡ ( G ) − A ] G[V(G)-A] . The neighborhood of  A A  in  G G  is the
set 
 

 
 
 N G ​ ( A ) = { v : v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) − A ​  and  ​ u ​ v ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) ​  for some  ​ u ∈ A } , N_{G}(A)=\{v:v\in V(G)-A\text{ and }uv\in E(G)\text{ for some }u\in A\}, 
 
 
 the closed neighborhood of  S S  is  N G ​ [ A ] = A ∪ N G ​ ( A ) N_{G}[A]=A\cup N_{G}(A) , and the
 localization  of  G G  with respect to  A A  is  G A = G − N G ​ [ A ] G_{A}=G-N_{G}[A] . If
 A = { v } A=\{v\} , we write  N G ​ ( v ) N_{G}(v)  (resp.  N G ​ [ v ] N_{G}[v] ,  G v G_{v} ,  G − v G-v ) instead of
 N G ​ ( { v } ) N_{G}(\{v\})  (resp.  N G ​ [ { v } ] N_{G}[\{v\}] ,  G { v } G_{\{v\}} ,  G − { v } G-\{v\} ). The number
 deg G ⁡ ( v ) = | N G ​ ( v ) | \deg_{G}(v)=\left|N_{G}(v)\right|  is called the  degree  of
 v v  in  G G . A vertex of degree zero is an  isolated vertex . In
addition,  δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G)  is the minimum degree of vertices of  G G . 
 
 
 A graph is called  well-covered  if all its maximal independent sets
have the same size  [ 32 ,  33 ] . For instance, all complete graph
on  n n  vertices, denoted by  K n K_{n} , is well-covered and the only cycles that
are well-covered are  C 3 , C 4 , C 5 , C_{3},C_{4},C_{5},  and  C 7 C_{7} . Additionally, the path
on  n n  vertices, denoted by  P n P_{n} , is well-covered if and only if  n = 1 , 2 , 4 n=1,2,4 . 
 
 
 In this paper, we extend the exploration of well-covered graphs. In 1975,
Staples introduced the hereditary  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  as a generalization of
well-covered graphs  [ 35 ,  36 ] . For a positive integer  p p ,
a graph  G G  belongs to class  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  if  n ⁡ ( G ) ≥ p n(G)\geq p  and for every
 p p  pairwise disjoint independent sets  A 1 , … , A p A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}  there exist  p p 
pairwise disjoint maximum independent sets  S 1 , … , S p S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}  in  G G  such
that  A i ⊆ S i A_{i}\subseteq S_{i}  for  1 ≤ i ≤ p 1\leq i\leq p . The graph  G ∈ 𝐖 p G\in\mathbf{W}_{p}  is also called the  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph . Observe, by
definition, that a graph is  𝐖 1 \mathbf{W}_{1}  if and only if it is
well-covered, and 
 

 
 
 𝐖 1 ⊇ 𝐖 2 ⊇ 𝐖 3 ⊇ ⋯ ⊇ 𝐖 p ⊇ ⋯ . \mathbf{W}_{1}\supseteq\mathbf{W}_{2}\supseteq\mathbf{W}_{3}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathbf{W}_{p}\supseteq\cdots. 
 
 
 Various methods for constructing  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graphs are presented in
details in  [ 31 ,  36 ] . 
 
 
 A well-covered graph (with at least two vertices) is  1 1 - well-covered 
if the deletion of any vertex of the graph leaves it well-covered
 [ 36 ] . For instance,  K 2 K_{2}  is  1 1 -well-covered, while a path on
four vertices  P 4 P_{4}  is well-covered, but not  1 1 -well-covered. Furthermore,
the close relationship between  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graphs and  1 1 -well-covered
graphs is demonstrated as follows. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1 
 
 [ 35 ,  36 ]  Let  G G  be a graph without isolated
vertices. Then  G G  is  1 1 -well-covered if and only if  G \ G  is a
 𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graph. 
 
 
 
 The  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graphs exhibit numerous intriguing properties and
characteristics, both in combinatorics and commutative algebra, as detailed in
 [ 15 ,  27 ,  29 ,  30 ,  33 ] . To illustrate, consider the polynomial
ring  R = K ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]  in  n n  variables over a field  K K , and let  G G 
be a graph with vertex set  V = { 1 , … , n } V=\{1,\ldots,n\} . We associate to the graph  G G  a
quadratic square-free monomial ideal 
 

 
 
 I ⁡ ( G ) = ( x i ​ x j ∣ i ​ j ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) ) ⊆ R , I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\mid ij\in E(G))\subseteq R, 
 
 
 which is called the  edge ideal  of  G G . We say that  G G  is
 Cohen–Macaulay  (resp.  Gorenstein ) if  I ⁡ ( G ) I(G)  is a
Cohen–Macaulay (resp. Gorenstein) ideal. Notably, every Gorenstein graph is
Cohen–Macaulay, while the converse is not generally true. 
 
 
 It has been established that  G G  is well-covered whenever it is
Cohen–Macaulay  [ 38 , Proposition 6.1.21] , and  G G  belongs to
 𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  whenever it is Gorenstein  [ 15 , Lemma 2.5] . For
triangle-free graphs,  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graphs are also Gorenstein
 [ 15 , Theorem 4.4] . However, in general, not all  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graphs
are Gorenstein. An example of a  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph that is always
Cohen–Macaulay is the clique corona graph. Let  ℋ = { H v : v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) } \mathcal{H}=\{H_{v}:v\in V(G)\}  be a family of non-empty graphs indexed by the vertex set of a graph
 G G . The  corona   G ∘ ℋ G\circ\mathcal{H}  of  G G  and  ℋ \mathcal{H}  is
defined as the disjoint union of  G G  and  H v H_{v}  for each  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , with
additional edges connecting each vertex  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G)  to all the vertices of
 H v H_{v}   [ 10 ] . When all graphs  H v H_{v}  in  ℋ \mathcal{H}  are
complete graphs,  G ∘ ℋ G\circ\mathcal{H}  is referred to as a  clique
corona graph . A clique corona graph is not only a well-covered graph
 [ 37 , Theorem 1] , but also a Cohen–Macaulay graph  [ 14 , Theorem
2.6] . If  H v = K p H_{v}=K_{p}  for every  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , we use  G ∘ K p G\circ K_{p}  to
denote  G ∘ ℋ G\circ\mathcal{H} . 
 
 
 Definition 1.2 
 
 [ 26 ]  For  λ > 0 \lambda>0 , a graph  G G  is  λ \lambda -quasi-regularizable
if 
 

 
 
 λ ⋅ | S | ≤ | N G ​ ( S ) | , \lambda\cdot\left|S\right|\leq|N_{G}(S)|, 
 
 
 for every independent set  S S  of  G G . 
 
 
 
 If  λ = 1 \lambda=1 , then  G G  is said to be a  quasi-regularizable  graph
 [ 3 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3 
 
 [ 3 ]  Every well-covered graph without isolated vertices
is quasi-regularizable. 
 
 
 
 It is noteworthy pointing out that the structure of  λ \lambda -quasi-regularizable graph implies the correlation between the number of
vertices with the independence number. In particular, by the theorem above,
one obtains that  n ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ α ​ ( G ) n\left(G\right)\geq 2\alpha\left(G\right)  holds for
every well-covered graph  G G . Furthermore, if  G G  is a  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph
with  p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , then it is  ( p − 1 ) (p-1) -quasi-regularizable  [ 13 , Theorem 2.6
(iii)] . We have also conjectured that  G G  is  p p -quasi-regularizable
 [ 13 , Conjecture 2.7] . However, it turned out that this assertion does
not hold in general. For example,  C 5 ∈ 𝐖 2 C_{5}\in\mathbf{W}_{2} , but it is not
 2 2 -quasi-regularizable. Nonetheless, in Section  2 , we confirm the following. 
 
 
 Theorem 2.8   Let  G G  be a connected  W p W_{p}  graph, where  p ≠ 2 p\neq 2 . Then  G G  is  p p -quasi-regularizable if and only if  n ⁡ ( G ) ≥ ( p + 1 ) ⋅ α ⁡ ( G ) n(G)\geq(p+1)\cdot\alpha(G) . 
 
 
 Beyond exploring graph structures, graph theory presents numerous intriguing
problems related to log-concavity, a concept that has deep implications in
combinatorics and algebra  [ 34 ] . For instance, it is well-known that the
matching polynomial of a graph has only real zeros, making it log-concave
 [ 12 ] . This property is significant for understanding graph matchings
and their applications in network theory. Recently, a major breakthrough was
achieved with the resolution of the log-concavity of the chromatic polynomial
of a graph  [ 17 ] , which has important implications for graph coloring
and phase transitions in statistical physics. Another key polynomial
associated with a graph is the  independence polynomial  of a graph
 G G , denoted  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x) , which is defined in  [ 11 ]  as follows: 
 

 
 
 I ⁡ ( G , x ) = ∑ k = 0 α ⁡ ( G ) s k ​ x k = s 0 + s 1 ​ x + ⋯ + s α ⁡ ( G ) ​ x α ⁡ ( G ) , I(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{k}x^{k}=s_{0}+s_{1}x+\cdots+s_{\alpha(G)}x^{\alpha(G)}, 
 
 
 where  s k s_{k}  represents the number of independent sets of cardinality  k k  in
the graph  G G . The independence polynomial  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  is said to be: 
 
 
 
 •  
 
 log-concave  if  s k 2 ≥ s k − 1 ⋅ s k + 1 s_{k}^{2}\geq s_{k-1}\cdot s_{k+1}  for all
 1 ≤ k ≤ α ⁡ ( G ) − 1 1\leq k\leq\alpha(G)-1 ; or 
 
 •  
 
 unimodal  if there exists an index  0 ≤ k ≤ α ⁡ ( G ) 0\leq k\leq\alpha(G)  such
that 
 

 
 
 s 0 ≤ ⋯ ≤ s k − 1 ≤ s k ≥ s k + 1 ≥ ⋯ ≥ s α ⁡ ( G ) . s_{0}\leq\cdots\leq s_{k-1}\leq s_{k}\geq s_{k+1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{\alpha(G)}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 A well-known result by Chudnovsky and Seymour in  [ 7 ]  states that all the
roots of  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  are real whenever  G G  is a claw-free graph, which also
implies the log-concavity of  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  for all claw-free graphs  G G . The
study of the independence polynomial is a rich area with extensive literature,
including works addressing the log-concavity problems (see  [ 1 ,  7 ,  16 ,  22 ,  24 ,  26 ,  39 ,  40 ]  and their references). 
 
 
 Lemma 1.4 
 
 [ 19 ]   If  P ⁡ ( x ) P(x)  is log-concave and  Q ⁡ ( x ) Q(x)  is unimodal,
then  P ⁡ ( x ) ⋅ Q ⁡ ( x ) P(x)\cdot Q(x)  is unimodal, while the product of two log-concave
polynomials is log-concave. 
 
 
 
 In  [ 1 ] , Alavi, Malde, Schwenk, and Erdös proved that for any
permutation  π \pi  of  { 1 , 2 , … , α ⁡ ( G ) } \{1,2,\dots,\alpha(G)\} , there is a graph  G G  such
that 
 

 
 
 s π ⁡ ( 1 ) < s π ⁡ ( 2 ) < ⋯ < s π ⁡ ( α ⁡ ( G ) ) . s_{\pi(1)}<s_{\pi(2)}<\cdots<s_{\pi(\alpha(G))}. 
 
 
 
 
 This result highlights the varied behaviors that graph polynomials can
display. Additionally, they conjectured that the independence polynomial
 I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  is unimodal for any tree or forest  G G . This conjecture remains
unresolved and continues to inspire ongoing research. Recently, it was
demonstrated that there are infinite families of trees whose independence
polynomials are not log-concave  [ 18 ] . This finding challenges earlier
assumptions and suggests new directions for exploring the conditions under
which log-concavity and unimodality hold. It is also worth noting that the
independence polynomials of some well-covered graphs are not log-concave
 [ 26 ] ,  [ 28 ] . 
 
 
 Revisiting the open conjecture of the unimodality of independence polynomials
of trees stated in  [ 1 ] , it is known that  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  is log-concave
whenever  G G  is a well-covered spider  [ 25 ] . Additionally, Radcliffe
verified that the independence polynomials of trees with up to  25 25  vertices
are log-concave  [ 2 ] . Zhu and Chen, in  [ 40 ] , applied
factorization methods to show the log-concavity of independence polynomials in
some special cases of trees. In contrast, in  [ 18 ] , the authors
demonstrated that there exist exactly two trees of order  26 26  whose
independence polynomials are not log-concave. 
 
 
 It is known that for every well-covered graph  G G  whose  girth ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 6 \mathrm{girth}(G)\geq 6  and  G ≠ { C 7 , K 1 } G\neq\{C_{7},K_{1}\}  then  G G  is well-covered if and only if  G = H ∘ K 1 G=H\circ K_{1}  for some graph  H H   [ 9 ] . The conjecture about the
unimodality of  I ⁡ ( G ∘ K 1 , x ) I(G\circ K_{1};x)  for all graphs  G G  was stated in
 [ 23 , Conjecture 3.3] , validated in the case  α ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 4 \alpha\left(G\right)\leq 4  in  [ 23 , Conjecture 3.3]  and extended to  5 ≤ α ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 8 5\leq\alpha\left(G\right)\leq 8  in  [ 6 ] . In addition, the unimodality of the
independence polynomial of clique corona graphs  G ∘ K p G\circ K_{p}  was
investigated for various classes of graphs such as:  G G  is a claw-free graph
 [ 24 , Corollary 3.12] ;  G G  is a quasi-regularizable graph with
 α ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 4 \alpha(G)\leq 4 ;  G G  is an arbitrary graph and  p p  satisfying  ( p + 1 ) ​ ( p + 2 ) ≥ n ⁡ ( G ) + 1 (p+1)(p+2)\geq n(G)+1 ; and  G G  is quasi-regularizable graph with  α ⁡ ( G ) ≤ p + 1 \alpha(G)\leq p+1 
 [ 13 ] . As an application, it was proven that  I ⁡ ( S n ∘ K p , x ) I(S_{n}\circ K_{p};x)  is
unimodal for all  p ≥ n + 1 − 2 p\geq\sqrt{n+1}-2 , where  S n S_{n}  is the complete bipartite
graph  K 1 , n K_{1,n}   [ 13 ] . Furthermore, for any graph  H H , taking into
account that  H ∘ K p ∈ 𝐖 p H\circ K_{p}\in\mathbf{W}_{p}   [ 13 , Corollary 2.3] , the
log-concavity of independent polynomials of  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graphs can be
further employed in this specific ones. Consequently, it yields that  I ⁡ ( G ∘ K p , x ) I(G\circ K_{p};x)  is log-concave whenever  p p  is large enough in correspondence to
 n ⁡ ( G ) . n(G). 
 
 
 The paper is organized as follows. In Section  2 , we study some
structural properties of  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graphs. Section  3  presents
findings related to log-concave properties of  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  for  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p} 
graphs  G G . As an application, the remainder of Section  3  is
dedicated to examining the log-concavity of  I ⁡ ( H ∘ K p , x ) I(H\circ K_{p};x)  for

 2  Quasi-regularizability of  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graphs 

 
 The following characterization of the localization of a
 𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph serves as a valuable tool in establishing the proof of
our main theorem in this section. Recall that  𝐖 1 \mathbf{W}_{1}  denotes the
family of all well-covered graphs. Several important results are known, as
summarized below: 
 
 
 Lemma 2.1 
 
 Let  G G  be a well-covered graph. Then 
 
 
 
 1.  
 
 [ 5 ,  33 ]   G v G_{v}  is well-covered and  α ⁡ ( G v ) = α ⁡ ( G ) − 1 \alpha(G_{v})=\alpha(G)-1 
for all  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) . 
 
 2.  
 
 [ 9 , Lemma 1]  If  S S  is an independent set of  G G , then  G S G_{S} 
is well-covered and  α ⁡ ( G ) = α ⁡ ( G S ) + | S | \alpha(G)=\alpha(G_{S})+|S| . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Lemma 2.2 
 
 Let  G G  be a  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graph with  α ⁡ ( G ) > 1 \alpha(G)>1 . Then 
 
 
 
 1.  
 
 [ 29 , Theorem 2]   G v G_{v}  is also a  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graph for
all  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) . 
 
 2.  
 
 [ 15 , Lemma 3.3]  If  S S  is an independent set of  G G  and  | S | < α ⁡ ( G ) \left|S\right|<\alpha(G) , then  G S G_{S}  is also a  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graph. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Furthermore, Staples offered essential evaluations for the general case of the

 It is established that taking the corona of any graph  G G  with  K 1 K_{1}  yields
the very well-covered graph  G ∘ K 1 G\circ K_{1}  (see  [ 37 , Corollary 3] ). By
applying this method, we can generate an infinite family of very well-covered
trees based on any given tree. This brings us to the following. 
 
 
 Corollary 3.14 
 
 For any tree  T T  with  α ⁡ ( T ) ≤ 5 \alpha(T)\leq 5 , the independence polynomials of the
following graphs 
 

 
 
 T ∘ K 1 , ( T ∘ K 1 ) ∘ K 1 , ( ( T ∘ K 1 ) ∘ K 1 ) ∘ K 1 , ( ( ( T ∘ K 1 ) ∘ K 1 ) ∘ K 1 ) ∘ K 1 , … T\circ K_{1},\left(T\circ K_{1}\right)\circ K_{1},\left(\left(T\circ K_{1}\right)\circ K_{1}\right)\circ K_{1},\left(\left(\left(T\circ K_{1}\right)\circ K_{1}\right)\circ K_{1}\right)\circ K_{1},\ldots 
 
 
 are log-concave. 
 
 
 
 
 4  Conclusion 

 
 This paper primarily addresses problems related to log-concavity
of  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graphs. Our results indicate that the independence
polynomial  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  is log-concave whenever  p p  is sufficiently large
relative to the independence number  α ⁡ ( G ) \alpha(G)  of  G G . Furthermore, Zhu, in
 [ 39 , Corollary 3.3] , demonstrated the log-concave preservation of the
independence polynomial of  G ∘ K p G\circ K_{p}  whenever the independence polynomial
of  G G  is log-concave. Theorem  3.5  leads us to the following. 
 
 
 Problem 4.1 
 
 What conditions on the  𝐖 p \mathbf{W}_{p}  graph  G G  guarantee that the
independence polynomial  I ⁡ ( G , x ) I(G;x)  is log-concave or at least unimodal? 
 
 
 
 In addition, Theorem  2.8  motivates the following. 
 
 
 Conjecture 4.2 
 
 Let  G G  be a connected  𝐖 2 \mathbf{W}_{2}  graph. Then  G G  is  2 2 -quasi-regularizable if and only if  n ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 ⋅ α ⁡ ( G ) n(G)\geq 3\cdot\alpha(G) .
