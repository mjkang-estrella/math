The 22-Quasi-Regularizability Conjecture and Independence Polynomials of 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} Graphs
Kevin Pereyra
Email: kdpereyra@unsl.edu.ar
Address: Departamento de Matemática, Universidad Nacional de San Luis, San Luis, Argentina
Abstract
Hoang, Levit, Mandrescu and Pham asked for structural conditions ensuring that the independence polynomial of a 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph is log-concave, or at least unimodal, and conjectured that a connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph is 22-quasi-regularizable if and only if n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G)n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G) (2026). We prove the conjecture. The key point is a local expansion theorem: if GG is connected and belongs to 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2}, then every non-maximum independent set AA satisfies
|NG​(A)|≥2​|A|.|N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A|.
Thus the only possible obstruction to 22-quasi-regularizability in a connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph comes from maximum independent sets, where the condition is exactly n⁡(G)−α⁡(G)≥2​α​(G)n(G)-\alpha(G)\geq 2\alpha(G).
We also give coefficient criteria for log-concavity and unimodality of independence polynomials of 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs. These criteria combine the standard two-sided coefficient inequalities, as collected by Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham, with λ\lambda-quasi-regularizability. The new p=2p=2 threshold proved here inserts the missing case into the same framework and yields explicit log-concavity and unimodality regions for connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs.
Keywords:
well-covered graph; 11-well-covered graph; 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph; quasi-regularizable graph; independence polynomial; log-concavity; unimodality.

1 Introduction
Let GG be a finite simple graph. Its independence polynomial, introduced by Gutman and Harary [10], is
I⁡(G,x)=∑k=0α⁡(G)sk​xk,I(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{k}x^{k},
where sks_{k} denotes the number of independent sets of cardinality kk in GG. The sequence (s0,s1,…,sα⁡(G))(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{\alpha(G)}) is a natural enumerative invariant of GG, but it is far from being constrained in general: Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and Erdos showed that prescribed order patterns can occur among its coefficients [1]. Nevertheless, important graph classes do force regular behavior. For instance, Heilmann and Lieb’s real-rootedness theorem for matching polynomials [11], and Chudnovsky and Seymour’s real-rootedness theorem for independence polynomials of claw-free graphs [5], both imply log-concavity in their respective settings. Root locations and coefficient questions for independence polynomials have been studied from several viewpoints; see, for example, Brown–Hickman–Nowakowski [3], the survey of Levit–Mandrescu [20], and the later work on trees and clique-cover products [36, 37, 17]. The broader role of log-concavity in combinatorics is surveyed by Stanley [31], and the modern theory is also influenced by Huh’s work on chromatic and matroidal log-concavity [15, 16].
This paper concerns the class 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} introduced by Staples [32, 33]. Well-covered graphs originate in the work of Plummer [29, 30]; very well-covered graphs were introduced by Favaron [7], and structural results for special well-covered families include [8, 34]. A graph is in 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} if every pp pairwise disjoint independent sets can be enlarged to pp pairwise disjoint maximum independent sets. The class 𝐖1\mathbf{W}_{1} is precisely the class of well-covered graphs. The class 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} is especially important because it coincides, for graphs without isolated vertices, with the class of 11-well-covered graphs [33, 25]; related 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} subclasses were studied by Pinter [28]. These graph classes also occur naturally in edge-ideal theory and Gorenstein graph theory [35, 14]. On the enumerative side, counterexamples, roller-coaster phenomena and positive results for very well-covered graphs appear in [27, 21, 24, 23, 4]. Corona constructions, introduced by Frucht and Harary [9], provide a large source of well-covered and 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} examples. In a recent paper, Hoang, Levit, Mandrescu and Pham studied quasi-regularizability and log-concavity for 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs and proved the pp-quasi-regularizability threshold for connected 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs when p≠2p\neq 2 [12]. They left the following two questions.
Problem 4.1 (Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham).
What conditions on the 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph GG guarantee that the independence polynomial I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave or at least unimodal?
Conjecture 4.2 (Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham).
Let GG be a connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph. Then GG is 22-quasi-regularizable if and only if
n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G).n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G).
The exclusion of p=2p=2 in the known threshold is not cosmetic. The cycle C5C_{5} belongs to 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} but is not 22-quasi-regularizable: if SS is a maximum independent set, then |S|=2|S|=2 and |N⁡(S)|=3<4|N(S)|=3<4. This is exactly the failure of the inequality n⁡(C5)≥3​α​(C5)n(C_{5})\geq 3\alpha(C_{5}); see Figure 1. The main result below proves that this is the only kind of failure.
Main structural theorem. Let GG be a connected graph in 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2}. If AA is an independent set which is not maximum, then
|NG​(A)|≥2​|A|.|N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A|.
Consequently, a connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph is 22-quasi-regularizable if and only if n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G)n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G).
The proof is local. If a smallest counterexample AA existed, then |N⁡(A)|=2​|A|−1|N(A)|=2|A|-1. Each vertex of AA would have a unique private neighbor; all other neighbors would see at least two vertices of AA; private neighbors would be forced away from the localization GAG_{A}; every non-private vertex touching GAG_{A} would be universal on AA; and finally a second localization would build an augmenting set that contradicts the 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} avoidance property. Figures 4–7 show the successive reductions.
The local expansion theorem and its application to Conjecture 4.2 are the new structural part of the paper. The coefficient criteria in Section 3 are included to make the consequence for Problem 4.1 self-contained: they combine known two-sided coefficient inequalities with the quasi-regularizability input, and in the case λ=p\lambda=p recover the same discriminant range used in [12, Theorem 3.3].
For Problem 4.1 we give a flexible coefficient answer. If GG is connected, G∈𝐖pG\in\mathbf{W}_{p}, n=n⁡(G)n=n(G), α=α⁡(G)\alpha=\alpha(G), and GG is λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable, then the inequalities
(k+1)​sk+1≤(n−(λ+1)​k)​sk,p⁡(α−k)​sk≤(k+1)​sk+1(k+1)s_{k+1}\leq\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)s_{k},\qquad p(\alpha-k)s_{k}\leq(k+1)s_{k+1}
lead to explicit log-concavity and unimodality criteria. The new p=2p=2 threshold supplies the missing quasi-regularizability input exactly when n≥3​αn\geq 3\alpha.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes the notation and records the standard tools on independence polynomials, quasi-regularizability and 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs. Section 3 proves the coefficient criteria used to answer Problem 4.1. Section 4 proves the local expansion theorem for connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs, and Section 5 applies it to Conjecture 4.2. Section 6 derives the corresponding log-concavity and unimodality consequences, and the last section closes with brief remarks.
2 Preliminaries
All graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected, and simple. For undefined graph-theoretic terminology we refer the reader to Diestel [6]; for matching terminology we refer to Lovász and Plummer [26].
We write V⁡(G)V(G) and E⁡(G)E(G) for the vertex set and the edge set, n⁡(G)=|V⁡(G)|n(G)=|V(G)|, and α⁡(G)\alpha(G) for the independence number. The family of independent sets is denoted by Ind⁡(G)\operatorname{Ind}(G), and the family of maximum independent sets by Ω⁡(G)\Omega(G). For A⊆V⁡(G)A\subseteq V(G), the open neighborhood is
NG​(A)={v∈V⁡(G)−A:u​v∈E⁡(G)​ for some ​u∈A},N_{G}(A)=\{v\in V(G)-A:\ uv\in E(G)\text{ for some }u\in A\},
with NG​[A]=A∪NG​(A)N_{G}[A]=A\cup N_{G}(A). When AA is independent, the localization of GG at AA is
GA=G−NG​[A].G_{A}=G-N_{G}[A].
For A={v}A=\{v\} we write GvG_{v}. Figure 2 illustrates the localization operation.

Definition 2.1 (𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs).
Let p≥1p\geq 1. A graph GG belongs to 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} if n⁡(G)≥pn(G)\geq p and, for every pp pairwise disjoint independent sets A1,…,ApA_{1},\ldots,A_{p}, there exist pp pairwise disjoint maximum independent sets S1,…,Sp∈Ω⁡(G)S_{1},\ldots,S_{p}\in\Omega(G) such that Ai⊆SiA_{i}\subseteq S_{i} for every ii.
Definition 2.2 (Quasi-regularizability).
Let λ>0\lambda>0. A graph GG is λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable if
λ​|A|≤|NG​(A)|\lambda|A|\leq|N_{G}(A)|
for every independent set AA of GG. The case λ=1\lambda=1 is the usual notion of quasi-regularizability due to Berge [2].
Definition 2.3 (Independence polynomial, log-concavity, unimodality).
Let
I⁡(G,x)=∑k=0α⁡(G)sk​xk=s0+s1​x+⋯+sα⁡(G)​xα⁡(G)I(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha(G)}s_{k}x^{k}=s_{0}+s_{1}x+\cdots+s_{\alpha(G)}x^{\alpha(G)}
be the independence polynomial of GG, where sks_{k} is the number of independent sets of cardinality kk. Following [12], we say that I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is
•
log-concave if
sk2≥sk−1​sk+1(1≤k≤α⁡(G)−1),s_{k}^{2}\geq s_{k-1}s_{k+1}\qquad(1\leq k\leq\alpha(G)-1),
•
unimodal if there exists an index 0≤t≤α⁡(G)0\leq t\leq\alpha(G) such that
s0≤⋯≤st−1≤st≥st+1≥⋯≥sα⁡(G).s_{0}\leq\cdots\leq s_{t-1}\leq s_{t}\geq s_{t+1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{\alpha(G)}.
Lemma 2.4 (Keilson–Gerber product lemma).
Let P⁡(x)P(x) and Q⁡(x)Q(x) have nonnegative coefficients and no internal zero coefficients. If P⁡(x)P(x) is log-concave and Q⁡(x)Q(x) is unimodal, then P⁡(x)​Q​(x)P(x)Q(x) is unimodal. If both P⁡(x)P(x) and Q⁡(x)Q(x) are log-concave, then P⁡(x)​Q​(x)P(x)Q(x) is log-concave.
The lemma is the standard polynomial form of a convolution theorem for nonnegative sequences. Keilson and Gerber established the classical strong-unimodality principle behind the first assertion [18]; in the present nonnegative setting one must include the no-internal-zero condition, as in the corrected formulation of Levit and Mandrescu [19, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3]. The compact independence-polynomial wording also appears explicitly in [22, Theorem 1] and is the form quoted by Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham [12, Lemma 1.4]. Since independence polynomials have strictly positive coefficients from degree 00 through degree α⁡(G)\alpha(G), the hypothesis is automatic in all applications below.
Lemma 2.5 (Standard facts on 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs).
Let G∈𝐖2G\in\mathbf{W}_{2}.
(i)
GG has no isolated vertices.
(ii)
If A∈Ind⁡(G)A\in\operatorname{Ind}(G) and |A|<α⁡(G)|A|<\alpha(G), then GA∈𝐖2G_{A}\in\mathbf{W}_{2} and α⁡(GA)=α⁡(G)−|A|\alpha(G_{A})=\alpha(G)-|A|.
(iii)
A graph is in 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} if and only if each of its connected components is in 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2}.
(iv)
If A∈Ind⁡(G)A\in\operatorname{Ind}(G) is not maximum and v∉Av\notin A, then there is S∈Ω⁡(G)S\in\Omega(G) such that A⊆SA\subseteq S and v∉Sv\notin S.
(v)
If CC is connected, C∈𝐖2C\in\mathbf{W}_{2}, and C≠K2C\neq K_{2}, then CC has no leaf. In particular, if CC is connected, C∈𝐖2C\in\mathbf{W}_{2}, and α⁡(C)>1\alpha(C)>1, then δ⁡(C)≥2\delta(C)\geq 2.
Proof.
For (i), if zz were isolated, then every maximum independent set would contain zz, contradicting the existence of two disjoint maximum independent sets in the definition of 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} applied to the two empty independent sets. The localization statement (ii) is the standard localization theorem for 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graphs; see [25, Corollary 2.3(i)] and also [12, Lemma 2.7(iii)]. The identity for the independence number follows because a maximum independent set of GAG_{A}, together with AA, is independent in GG, and conversely any maximum independent set of GG containing AA leaves a maximum independent set in GAG_{A}.
For (iii), one may either check the defining extension property component by component, or quote [12, Theorem 2.6]. Maximum independent sets in a disjoint union are exactly unions of maximum independent sets of the components, and restricting the defining property to one component gives the converse.
Assertion (iv) is the avoidance characterization [25, Theorem 2.2(vii)]; it also follows directly from the definition of 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2}: apply it to the two disjoint independent sets AA and {v}\{v\}. We obtain disjoint maximum independent sets S,TS,T with A⊆SA\subseteq S and v∈Tv\in T, hence v∉Sv\notin S.
Finally, (v) is [25, Corollary 2.3(ii)]. For completeness we indicate the short argument. Suppose that a connected C∈𝐖2C\in\mathbf{W}_{2}, C≠K2C\neq K_{2}, has a leaf xx with unique neighbor yy. Since C≠K2C\neq K_{2}, the vertex yy has another neighbor zz. By (iv), applied to the non-maximum independent set {z}\{z\} and the vertex xx, there is a maximum independent set SS with z∈Sz\in S and x∉Sx\notin S. Then y∉Sy\notin S, and hence S∪{x}S\cup\{x\} is independent, contradicting the maximality of SS.
∎
Lemma 2.6 (Known coefficient inequalities).
Let GG have order nn, independence number α\alpha, and independence polynomial I⁡(G,x)=∑k=0αsk​xkI(G;x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}s_{k}x^{k}.
(i)
If GG is λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable, then
(k+1)​sk+1≤(n−(λ+1)​k)​sk,0≤k≤α−1.(k+1)s_{k+1}\leq\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)s_{k},\qquad 0\leq k\leq\alpha-1.
This is due to Levit and Mandrescu [24, Theorem 2.1].
(ii)
If GG is connected and G∈𝐖pG\in\mathbf{W}_{p}, then
p⁡(α−k)​sk≤(k+1)​sk+1,1≤k≤α−1.p(\alpha-k)s_{k}\leq(k+1)s_{k+1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq\alpha-1.
This is due to Hoang, Levit, Mandrescu and Pham [13, Theorem 2.6].
These two inequalities are also recorded together as [12, Lemma 3.2]; we quote them here because they are the coefficient input for the criteria below.
Corollary 2.7.
Let GG be a graph with connected components H1,…,HqH_{1},\ldots,H_{q}. If each
I⁡(Hi,x)I(H_{i};x) is log-concave, then I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave. If each
I⁡(Hi,x)I(H_{i};x) is unimodal and all but possibly one of them are log-concave,
then I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is unimodal.
Proof.
The independence polynomial is multiplicative over disjoint unions:
I⁡(G,x)=∏i=1qI⁡(Hi,x).I(G;x)=\prod_{i=1}^{q}I(H_{i};x).
The assertions follow from Lemma 2.4.
∎
3 Coefficient criteria for Problem 4.1
The following criterion is the coefficient engine behind all log-concavity applications in this paper. It separates the enumerative step from the structural question of when a 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graph is λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable.
Theorem 3.1 (General log-concavity criterion).
Let GG be a connected graph in 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p}, with n=n⁡(G)n=n(G) and α=α⁡(G)\alpha=\alpha(G). Suppose that GG is λ\lambda-quasi-regularizable. If
Φp,λ,n,α​(k)=(k+1)​p​(α−k+1)−k⁡(n−(λ+1)​k)≥0\Phi_{p,\lambda,n,\alpha}(k)=(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)-k\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)\geq 0
for every 1≤k≤α−11\leq k\leq\alpha-1, then I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave.
Proof.
Apply Lemma 2.6(i) with index kk and Lemma 2.6(ii) with index k−1k-1. For 1≤k≤α−11\leq k\leq\alpha-1,
sk+1≤n−(λ+1)​kk+1​sk,sk−1≤kp⁡(α−k+1)​sk.s_{k+1}\leq\frac{n-(\lambda+1)k}{k+1}s_{k},\qquad s_{k-1}\leq\frac{k}{p(\alpha-k+1)}s_{k}.
Multiplying gives
sk−1​sk+1≤k⁡(n−(λ+1)​k)(k+1)​p​(α−k+1)​sk2.s_{k-1}s_{k+1}\leq\frac{k\bigl(n-(\lambda+1)k\bigr)}{(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)}s_{k}^{2}.
The hypothesis is exactly the assertion that the rational factor is at most 11.
∎
Corollary 3.2 (The pp-quasi-regularizable specialization).
Let GG be connected, G∈𝐖pG\in\mathbf{W}_{p}, with n=n⁡(G)n=n(G) and α=α⁡(G)\alpha=\alpha(G). Suppose that GG is pp-quasi-regularizable. If
fp,n,α​(k)=k2−(n−p​α)​k+p​α+p≥0f_{p,n,\alpha}(k)=k^{2}-(n-p\alpha)k+p\alpha+p\geq 0
for every 1≤k≤α−11\leq k\leq\alpha-1, then I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave.
Proof.
Put λ=p\lambda=p in Theorem 3.1. The inequality
(k+1)​p​(α−k+1)−k⁡(n−(p+1)​k)≥0(k+1)p(\alpha-k+1)-k(n-(p+1)k)\geq 0
is equivalent to the displayed quadratic inequality.
∎
Theorem 3.3 (Explicit interval form).
Let GG be connected, G∈𝐖pG\in\mathbf{W}_{p}, with n=n⁡(G)n=n(G) and α=α⁡(G)≥2\alpha=\alpha(G)\geq 2. Suppose that GG is pp-quasi-regularizable. Then I⁡(G,x)I(G;x) is log-concave whenever either
(p+1)​α≤n≤p​α+2​p​α+p,α24​(α+1)≤p,(p+1)\alpha\leq n\leq p\alpha+2\sqrt{p\alpha+p},\qquad\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4(\alpha+1)}\leq p,
or
p​α+2​p​α+p<n≤(α2+1)​p+(α−1)2α−1,α⁡(α−1)α+1≤p.p\alpha+2\sqrt{p\alpha+p}<n\leq\frac{(\alpha^{2}+1)p+(\alpha-1)^{2}}{\alpha-1},\qquad\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\alpha+1}\leq p.
Proof.
By Corollary 3.2, it is enough to guarantee
f⁡(k)=k2−(n−p​α)​k+p​α+p≥0(1≤k≤α−1).f(k)=k^{2}-(n-p\alpha)k+p\alpha+p\geq 0\qquad(1\leq k\leq\alpha-1).
Write r=n−p​αr=n-p\alpha and c=p​α+pc=p\alpha+p. The discriminant is
Δ=r2−4​c.\Delta=r^{2}-4c.
If Δ≤0\Delta\leq 0, then f⁡(k)≥0f(k)\geq 0 for all real kk. This is equivalent to
n≤p​α+2​p​α+p.n\leq p\alpha+2\sqrt{p\alpha+p}.
Because pp-quasi-regularizability already implies n≥(p+1)​αn\geq(p+1)\alpha, the first interval is relevant exactly when
(p+1)​α≤p​α+2​p​α+p,(p+1)\alpha\leq p\alpha+2\sqrt{p\alpha+p},
which is equivalent to α2/(4​(α+1))≤p\alpha^{2}/(4(\alpha+1))\leq p.
Assume now that Δ>0\Delta>0. Let
ρ1=r−Δ2,ρ2=r+Δ2\rho_{1}=\frac{r-\sqrt{\Delta}}{2},\qquad\rho_{2}=\frac{r+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}
be the two real roots. Since ff opens upward, f⁡(k)≥0f(k)\geq 0 for every integer 1≤k≤α−11\leq k\leq\alpha-1 whenever α−1≤ρ1\alpha-1\leq\rho_{1}. This condition is equivalent to
Δ≤r−2​α+2,\sqrt{\Delta}\leq r-2\alpha+2,
where the right-hand side must be nonnegative. Squaring (1) gives
r2−4​(p​α+p)≤(r−2​α+2)2,r^{2}-4(p\alpha+p)\leq(r-2\alpha+2)^{2},

5 Solution of Conjecture 4.2
Theorem 5.1 (Conjecture 4.2).
Let GG be a connected graph in 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2}. Then GG is 22-quasi-regularizable if and only if
n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G).n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G).
Proof.
If GG is 22-quasi-regularizable and S∈Ω⁡(G)S\in\Omega(G), then
n⁡(G)−α⁡(G)=|NG​(S)|≥2|S|=2​α​(G),n(G)-\alpha(G)=|N_{G}(S)|\geq 2|S|=2\alpha(G),
so n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G)n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G).
Conversely, assume n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G)n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G) and let A∈Ind⁡(G)A\in\operatorname{Ind}(G). If AA is not maximum, then Theorem 4.1 gives
|NG​(A)|≥2​|A|.|N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A|.
If AA is maximum, then
|NG​(A)|=n⁡(G)−α⁡(G)≥2​α​(G)=2​|A|.|N_{G}(A)|=n(G)-\alpha(G)\geq 2\alpha(G)=2|A|.
Thus |NG​(A)|≥2​|A||N_{G}(A)|\geq 2|A| for every independent set AA, which is precisely 22-quasi-regularizability.
∎
Remark 5.2.
Theorem 4.1 is stronger than Conjecture 4.2. The global inequality n⁡(G)≥3​α​(G)n(G)\geq 3\alpha(G) is needed only for maximum independent sets. Every non-maximum independent set in a connected 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} graph satisfies the 22-quasi-regularizability inequality automatically.

7 Final remarks
The local theorem explains why the case p=2p=2 was delicate. In a smallest hypothetical counterexample, localizing at A−{x}A-\{x\} creates a K2K_{2} component {x,mx}\{x,m_{x}\}. Such a component is allowed in 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2}, whereas higher pp arguments usually benefit from stronger local degree constraints. The proof above eliminates precisely this obstruction by showing that the remaining connection to GAG_{A} must pass through a unique universal vertex, and then using the 𝐖2\mathbf{W}_{2} extension property one final time.
For independence polynomials, the conclusion is that the conditions in Problem 4.1 can now be applied uniformly at the quasi-regularizability threshold: for p≠2p\neq 2 by the theorem of Hoang–Levit–Mandrescu–Pham, and for p=2p=2 by Theorem 5.1. Componentwise versions follow from Lemma 2.4, which is why log-concavity results for connected components extend cleanly to disconnected 𝐖p\mathbf{W}_{p} graphs.
