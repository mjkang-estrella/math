1. Introduction
    Ratios and logarithmic derivatives of Iν and Kν , the standard modiﬁed
Bessel functions of the ﬁrst and second kinds of order ν, appear through-
out analysis and probability. They control condition numbers in the back-
ward recurrences of Amos [1], sharp ratio bounds and Turán-type inequali-
ties [28, 19, 27, 11, 17, 3, 16, 25, 9, 24, 10], normal distribution functions on
spheres and related probabilistic models [8, 23], and complete-monotonicity
and inﬁnite-divisibility results of Ismail and Grosswald [13, 14, 15, 6]. Bern-
stein functions provide the probabilistic bridge: they are Laplace exponents
of possibly killed subordinators [22], so a Bernstein certiﬁcate for a Bessel
ratio is a reusable positivity statement for subordinated kernels and for
Bessel-ratio inequalities that enter such kernels. A well-known survey of
open functional inequalities for these functions is the question list of Baricz,
Ponnusamy, and Vuorinen [5], hereafter BPV; a companion source of open
problems is Baricz’s study of bounds via coeﬃcient ratios [2]. Yang and Tian
[32, Conjecture 3] formulate a conjecture for the Bernstein-function behavior
of Wν (x1/θ ) in the range θ ≥ 2; below we state and prove the corresponding
Bernstein-property formulation with τ = 1/θ. This sits in a recent line of
monotonicity and convexity results for modiﬁed Bessel ratios and products
[34, 33, 26, 31, 18, 32].
    The present paper settles a group of these problems with a single orga-
nizing device. The modiﬁed Bessel equation converts second-order informa-
tion about Iν or Kν into a quadratic polynomial in the logarithmic deriva-
tive; every monotonicity or concavity statement treated here is the assertion
that this quadratic has a ﬁxed sign on its domain. For the overview, write
Wν (z) = zIν (z)/Iν+1 (z), rν (u) = Iν′ (u)/Iν (u), qν (u) = Iν+1 (u)/Iν (u), and
yν (u) = uKν′ (u)/Kν (u); these ratios are deﬁned formally in Section 2.

      After one substitution through the modiﬁed Bessel equation, each
      claim below is a sign statement for a quadratic polynomial in a
      logarithmic derivative. When the claim is true, the quadratic is
      controlled globally by a pole–zero representation; when it is false,
      it is refuted locally by an exact rational enclosure at a single point.

tiﬁcate, endpoint classiﬁcation, and transfer obstruction.

2. Preliminaries
    We recall the standard vocabulary; see [22] for Bernstein-function theory,
[12] for the Riccati equation in the standard logarithmic-derivative sense, and
[29, 21] for Bessel facts.
Deﬁnition 2.1 (Completely monotone and Bernstein classes). Let all func-
tions below be deﬁned on (0, ∞) and take values in [0, ∞).
 (i) A function f is completely monotone, written f ∈ CM, if f ∈ C ∞ and
     (−1)n f (n) (x) ≥ 0 for every n ≥ 0 and x > 0.

 (ii) A function g is a Bernstein function, written g ∈ BF, if g ∈ C ∞ and
      g ′ ∈ CM.
A sequence (cn )n≥0 is completely monotone if ∆k cn ≥ 0 for all n, k ≥ 0, where
∆cn = cn − cn+1 , and strictly so if all these inequalities are strict.
For sequences this is the Hausdorﬀ ﬁnite-diﬀerence convention for moment
sequences on [0, 1] [7, 30].
By the Bernstein–Widder theorem, f ∈ CM exactly when f is the Laplace
transform of a positive measure on [0, ∞) [30, 22]; in this paper only the
elementary sign conditions of Deﬁnition 2.1 are used.
Two standard facts are used repeatedly; both are in [22].
Proposition 2.2 (Closure properties).      (i) If f, g ∈ BF, then f ◦ g ∈ BF.
 (ii) x 7→ xα belongs to BF if and only if 0 ≤ α ≤ 1.

5. The Bessel-K quotient: classiﬁcation and the endpoint question
    BPV prove that u 7→ Kν′ (u)/Kν (u)2 is strictly decreasing on (0, ∞) when
|ν| ≥ 1 [5, Theorem 2(a)], and BPV Question 2 asks what happens on the
remaining range |ν| < 1. For recent adjacent monotonicity and complete-
monotonicity results for functions built from modiﬁed Bessel K-ratios and
products, see [34, 31, 26, 18]. The Riccati normal form of Lemma 2.5 reduces
the question to the endpoint signs of Fν , which are decided by the classical
expansions.

7. Concluding remarks
    The tally is one Yang–Tian Bernstein-property formulation proved with
a sharp exponent range (Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 3.3), one BPV mono-
tonicity classiﬁcation completed (Theorem 5.3, with the explicit half-order


                                       15
maximum of Proposition 5.2), and three source problems answered nega-
tively: BPV Question 1, BPV Question 7, and Baricz Problem 4.3(c) (Coun-
terexamples 4.3–4.5, 5.5, and 6.2). All items are statements about logarith-
mic derivatives of modiﬁed Bessel functions, and all proofs pass through the
same Riccati normal forms (Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5).
    The division of labor is the one announced in the introduction. On the
true side, the partial-fraction expansion over squared Bessel zeros (Proposi-
tion 3.1) carries the entire sign pattern, and composition closure converts it
into the stated Bernstein property. On the false side, the quadratic normal
form concentrates each claim into the value of a ratio at one point, and Ap-
pendix Appendix A supplies the ﬁnite rational certiﬁcate: truncated positive
series with geometric tails, compared as integers. The certiﬁed positive mar-
gin at (ν, u) = (0, 10) is small. This explains why these problems sit close
to the boundary of truth: the refuted inequalities hold on large parts of the
parameter space and fail by less than 3 × 10−4 at the certiﬁed obstruction.
   √Three threads seem worth pursuing. First, the exact log-concavity region
of u Iν (u) in the (ν, u)-plane is a natural next object: the certiﬁcate above
excludes a neighborhood of (0, 10), and the boundary is the zero set of the
Riccati quadratic in Lemma 2.4. Second, the endpoint-sign method of Sec-
tion 5 should classify the remaining monotonicity windows for Kν -quotients
in the BPV list, with the half-order line as the canonical probe. Third, Coun-
terexample 6.2 invites a characterization question: which additional structure
on the coeﬃcient ratio or denominator coeﬃcients restores the transfer? Any
positive answer would interpolate between the coeﬃcient-level and zero-level
mechanisms that this paper keeps deliberately separate.
