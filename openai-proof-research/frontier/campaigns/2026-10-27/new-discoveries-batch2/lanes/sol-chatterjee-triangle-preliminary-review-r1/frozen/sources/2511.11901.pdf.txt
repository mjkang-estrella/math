                                                                   A SOLUTION TO BEZDEK’S CONJECTURE


                                                                          KOSTIANTYN DRACH AND KATERYNA TATARKO


                                                    Abstract. For a given λ > 0, a convex body in Rn is λ-convex if it is the intersection of (finitely
                                                    or infinitely many) balls of radius 1/λ. In this note, we show that among all λ-convex bodies in




arXiv:2511.11901v1 [math.MG] 14 Nov 2025
                                                    Rn , n ⩾ 2, with a given inradius, the λ-convex lens (i.e., the intersection of two balls of radius
                                                    1/λ) has the largest mean width. This gives an affirmative answer to the conjecture of K. Bezdek.
                                                    Under an additional symmetry assumption on λ-convex bodies, we resolve the analogous inradius
                                                    conjecture of Bezdek for arbitrary intrinsic volumes.
                                                        We also establish an answer to the corresponding conjecture of K. Bezdek about the circum-
                                                    radius. In particular, we prove that the λ-convex spindle (i.e., the intersection of all balls of
                                                    radius 1/λ containing a given pair of points) is the unique minimizer of the mean width among
                                                    all λ-convex bodies with a fixed circumradius.

2.2. λ-convexity. In this subsection, we collect some definitions and properties related to λ-convex
bodies.
   Let λ > 0. A λ-convex polytope is a λ-convex body given as the intersection of finitely many
balls of radius 1/λ. An (n − 1)-dimensional facet of a λ-convex polytope P is the intersection of
the corresponding ball of radius 1/λ with the boundary of P . Two facets are either disjoint or
intersect. In the latter case, their intersection is called a (lower-dimensional) face. The faces of
dimension 0 and 1 are called vertices and edges of the polytope P , respectively. The λ-convex lens
is an example of a λ-convex polytope.
   We also recall that a convex body K ⊂ Rn is called λ-convex if for every point p ∈ ∂K there
exists a neighborhood Up and a ball Bλ,p of radius 1/λ such that p ∈ ∂Bλ,p and Up ∩ K ⊂ Bλ,p
(see Figure 1). When ∂K is at least C 2 -smooth, this condition is equivalent to requiring that the
principal curvatures ki (p), i ∈ {1, . . . , n − 1}, with respect to the inward-pointing normal at every
p ∈ ∂K, satisfy ki (p) ⩾ 1.

   For intrinsic volumes different from the volume, the surface area, and the mean width, our method
