Stack-sorting with stacks avoiding vincular patterns

Email address: williamzsrv@gmail.com
Abstract.
We introduce the stack-sorting map \text{SC}_{\sigma} that sorts, in a right-greedy manner, an input permutation through a stack that avoids some vincular pattern \sigma. The stack-sorting maps of Cerbai et al. in which the stack avoids a pattern classically and Defant and Zheng in which the stack avoids a pattern consecutively follow as special cases. We first characterize and enumerate the sorting class \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}), the set of permutations sorted by s\circ\text{SC}_{\sigma}, for seven length 3 patterns \sigma. We also decide when \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) is a permutation class. Next, we compute \max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\sigma}^{-1}(\pi)| and characterize the periodic points of \text{SC}_{\sigma} for several length 3 patterns \sigma. We end with several conjectures and open problems.
Keywords: Stack sort; Pattern avoidance; Permutation
1. Introduction
Let \mathfrak{S}_{n} be the symmetric group. Consider West [16]’s stack-sorting map s:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n}. A stack, which is required to be increasing, processes the input permutation \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} in a right-greedy manner. In other words, the map s uses a stack that avoids the pattern 21. In 2019, Cerbai et al. [5] generalized West’s stack-sorting map. The map s_{\sigma}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n} processes the input permutation through a stack in a similar manner as s, but with a stack that must avoid some pattern \sigma, as in Figure 1. Since then, the map s_{\sigma} and variants have been studied extensively [3, 5, 6, 11]. Most relevant to our work is Defant and Zheng [11] that introduced the consecutive-pattern-avoiding stack-sorting map \text{SC}_{\sigma}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n}, a modification of s_{\sigma} where the stack must consecutively avoid \sigma, as in Figure 2.
Babson and Steingrímsson [1] first introduced vincular pattern avoidance. When considering whether a permutation \pi contains a vincular pattern \sigma, some elements may be required to be adjacent in \pi, as indicated by underlined terms in \sigma. For instance, the pattern 1423 contains 1\underline{23} and 123, but avoids \underline{12}3 and \underline{123}. Note that classical and consecutive pattern avoidance are both special cases of vincular pattern avoidance.
Therefore, a natural extension of Cerbai et al. [5] and Defant and Zheng [11]’s maps is the generalized map \text{SC}_{\sigma}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n} that sorts with a stack which avoids a vincular pattern \sigma. Consider the permutation \tau=514362. Figures 1, 2, 3 and 4 demonstrate the process of sending \tau through the classically-123-avoiding stack s_{123}=\text{SC}_{123}, the consecutively-123-avoiding stack \text{SC}_{\underline{123}}, and the vincular-avoiding-stacks \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3} and \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}, respectively. We find that \text{SC}_{123}(\tau)=463215, \text{SC}_{\underline{123}}(\tau)=263415, \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(\tau)=426315, and \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}(\tau)=632415. In particular, the four maps produce different images after acting on \tau.

It is well-known [13] that the permutations sorted onto the identity by s are those that avoid the pattern 231. Since West [16]’s introduction of the deterministic stack-sorting map, researchers studied the permutations sorted onto the identity by several applications of s [4, 8, 10, 13, 16, 18]. The map \text{SC}_{\sigma} has also been studied extensively from a sorting point of view. For classical and consecutive patterns \sigma, the articles [2, 5, 6, 11] focused on the permutations \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) sorted to the identity by s\circ\text{SC}_{\sigma}. Cerbai et al. [5], which first introduced and studied the map \text{SC}_{\sigma} for classical patterns \sigma, was able to describe and enumerate the sorting class \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) for \sigma=123 and \sigma=321. Since then, variations and generalizations of Cerbai et al. [5]’s work have been investigated [2, 6, 11]. The sorting class of \text{SC}_{132} was found by [6], and the sorting classes of \text{SC}_{\underline{132}}, \text{SC}_{\underline{123}}, and \text{SC}_{\underline{321}} were found by Defant and Zheng [11].
Another recent topic of study is the dynamical properties of \text{SC}_{\sigma}, first examined by Berlow [3]. Various authors worked on the maximum number of preimages under s and \text{SC}_{\sigma} [3, 8, 9, 11]. Most relevantly to our work, Berlow [3] showed that, for patterns \sigma of length 3, the maximum number of preimages a permutation can have under \text{SC}_{\sigma} is given by C_{n-1}. Furthermore, the periodic points of the map \text{SC}_{\sigma} have been studied [3, 7, 11], especially for classical and consecutive patterns \sigma of length 3. In particular, Defant and Zheng [11] showed that the periodic points of \text{SC}_{132} are the permutations that avoid the patterns 132 and 231, and the periodic points of \text{SC}_{312} are the permutations that avoid the patterns 312 and 213.

Additionally, we decide whether \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) is a permutation class. Our results are summarized in Table 1. 2. Preliminaries
2.1. Definitions and Notation
Consider \pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}. We define the reverse of \pi as \text{rev}(\pi)=\pi_{n}\pi_{n-1}\cdots\pi_{1} and the complement of \pi as \text{comp}(\pi)=(n+1-\pi_{1})(n+1-\pi_{2})\cdots(n+1-\pi_{n}). For instance, \text{rev}(1342)=2431 and \text{comp}(1342)=4213. These definitions extend easily to patterns \sigma. An element \tau_{i} is a left-to-right minimum of \tau if, for all j<i, we have \tau_{i}<\tau_{j}. We often split a permutation \tau into the concatenation of two or more subsequences. For example, we may let \tau=\tau^{a}\tau^{b}, where \tau^{a}=\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{i} and \tau^{b}=\tau_{i+1}\cdots\tau_{n}. We consider a and b as permutations in their own right, and they can be processed by stack-sorting maps.
Two sequences \tau and \pi of size n are order-isomorphic if, for each pair of indices i and j, we have \tau_{i}<\tau_{j} if and only if \pi_{i}<\pi_{j}. We write \tau\simeq\pi if \tau and \pi are order-isomorphic. For a permutation \pi and pattern \sigma, we say \pi avoids \sigma if there exists no subsequence of \pi order-isomorphic to \sigma. Else, we say \pi contains \sigma. As a generalization, we allow \sigma to be a vincular pattern, where terms in the subsequence of \pi corresponding to underlined terms in \sigma must be adjacent. Henceforth, we work only with vincular pattern avoidance. Let \mathcal{S}_{n} be the set of the vincular patterns of length n, and let \mathcal{S}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{n}.
If \Sigma is a list of patterns, let \text{Av}_{n}(\Sigma) be the permutations in \mathfrak{S}_{n} that avoid all the patterns in \Sigma. Let \text{Av}(\Sigma)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\text{Av}_{n}(\Sigma). It is well-known [17] that |\text{Av}_{n}(\sigma)|=C_{n} for each \sigma\in S_{3}.
Throughout the remainder of the article, let \text{SC}_{\sigma} denote the stack-sorting map which uses a stack that avoids \sigma\in\mathcal{S}. Note that s=\text{SC}_{21}. Naturally, the stack-sorting process of \text{SC}_{\sigma} keeps track of three sequences: the input sequence, the stack, and the output sequence. For brevity, we describe an element as being popped from or added to the stack of \text{SC}_{\sigma} without explicitly mentioning the stack. A premature element \tau_{i} is one that is popped before the input sequence is empty. For example, the elements 4, 6, and 3 are premature in Figure 1. Note that \tau_{1} is not premature when \tau is sorted by \text{SC}_{\sigma} for \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}.
2.2. Preliminary Lemmas
Lemma 2.1 follows from the definition of the map \text{SC}_{\sigma}.
Lemma 2.1 (Defant and Zheng [11, Lemma 1.2]).
For a pattern \sigma\in\mathcal{S} of length at least 2, we have
\text{SC}_{\text{comp}(\sigma)}=\text{comp}\circ\text{SC}_{\sigma}\circ\text{comp}.
Next, we show that avoiding some vincular patterns is equivalent to avoiding others.
Lemma 2.2.
For pairs (\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})\in\{(\underline{13}2,132),(\underline{31}2,312),(2\underline{13},213),(2\underline{31},231)\}, we have \text{Av}(\sigma_{1})=\text{Av}(\sigma_{2}).
Proof.
We show that containing \underline{13}2 is equivalent to containing 132, where the other cases follow similarly. If \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} contains \underline{13}2, then it clearly also contains 132. For the other direction, suppose \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\simeq 132. But there must exist some index i\leq l<j satisfying \tau_{l}<\tau_{k}<\tau_{l+1}, so that \tau contains \tau_{l}\tau_{l+1}\tau_{k}\simeq\underline{13}2.
∎
Lemma 2.2 implies the following.
Corollary 2.3.
The maps \text{SC}_{\underline{13}2}, \text{SC}_{\underline{31}2}, \text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}, and \text{SC}_{2\underline{13}} are equivalent to \text{SC}_{132}, \text{SC}_{312}, \text{SC}_{231}, and \text{SC}_{213}, respectively.
3. The sorting classes \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma})
In this section, we study the sorting class \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) of \text{SC}_{\sigma}, the set of permutations in \mathfrak{S}_{n} sorted onto the identity by s\circ\text{SC}_{\sigma}. Let \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma})=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}). Additionally, a permutation class is a (possibly infinite) set \Pi of permutations such that every \tau contained in \pi\in\Pi is also in \Pi. We begin with the following well-known lemma.
Lemma 3.1 (Knuth [13]).
The set of permutations sorted by s to the identity is \text{Av}(231).
It thus suffices to find the permutations sent by \text{SC}_{\sigma} to \text{Av}(231). We now characterize and enumerate \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) for several \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}. Furthermore, we decide whether \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}) is a permutation class.
3.1. The Sorting Classes of \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}, \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}, and \text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}
In this section, we study sorting classes that can be described by pattern avoidance. The characterizations and sizes of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}), \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}), and \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}) are listed in Table 1. We begin by describing one aspect of \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}} in Lemma 3.2.
Lemma 3.2.
Consider \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} that is being processed by \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}, and suppose \tau_{i} is an element currently in the stack. Subsequently, after each time an element enters the stack, the stack will contain some element of value at most \tau_{i}.
Proof.
Let \tau_{p} be the next element to be added to the stack. If no elements are popped immediately before \tau_{p} is added, then \tau_{i} remains in the stack. Else, suppose \tau_{q} is the element popped immediately before \tau_{p} is added to the stack so that \tau_{p}<\tau_{q}. Then, \tau_{q}\leq\tau_{i}, for otherwise the stack would already contain 1\underline{23}. Thus, \tau_{p}<\tau_{i}, and the conclusion follows.
∎
With Lemma 3.2, we are ready to directly describe \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}).
Theorem 3.3.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}} is characterized by
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}})=\text{Av}_{n}(132,3214,4213).
Proof.
Let \pi=\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}(\tau). We first show that if \tau does not avoid 132, 3214, and 4213, then \pi contains 231. If \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\simeq 132, Lemma 3.2 implies that when \tau_{j} enters the stack, there is some term \tau_{i}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{i} below it. We split into three possibilities:
•
If \tau_{j} has not popped when \tau_{k} enters (and neither has \tau_{i}^{\prime}), then the stack, and thus \pi will contain \tau_{k}\tau_{j}\tau_{i}^{\prime}\simeq 231.
•
Suppose \tau_{j}, but not \tau_{i}^{\prime}, was popped before \tau_{k} enters. Suppose further that \tau_{j} but not \tau_{r} was popped when \tau_{p} was being added to the stack, because \tau_{p}\tau_{q}\tau_{r}\simeq 1\underline{23}. If \tau_{r}\neq\tau_{i}^{\prime} and \tau_{i}^{\prime}<\tau_{q}, then \tau_{q}\tau_{r}\tau_{i}^{\prime}\simeq 231 within the stack and thus \pi. As such, it suffices to consider only the case \tau_{q}<\tau_{l}. Meanwhile, if \tau_{r}=\tau_{i}^{\prime}, then \tau_{q}<\tau_{r}=\tau_{i}^{\prime}<\tau_{k}. We proceed with the assumption \tau_{q}<\tau_{k}. By Lemma 3.2, when \tau_{k} enters the stack, there is some term \tau_{p}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{p} below it. Therefore, \pi contains \tau_{q}\tau_{k}\tau_{p}^{\prime}\simeq 231.
•
Lastly, suppose that \tau_{i}^{\prime} was popped before \tau_{k} enters the stack. As above, consider \tau_{p}, \tau_{q}, and \tau_{r}. If \tau_{q}\neq\tau_{i}^{\prime}, we must have \tau_{p}<\tau_{i}^{\prime}, as otherwise the stack contains \tau_{i}^{\prime}\tau_{q}\tau_{r}\simeq 1\underline{23}. Otherwise, if \tau_{q}=\tau_{i}^{\prime}, then \tau_{p}<\tau_{q}=\tau_{i}^{\prime}. In either case, we have \tau_{p}<\tau_{i}^{\prime}. Again by Lemma 3.2, when \tau_{k} is at the top of the stack, there is some term \tau_{p}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{p} below it. Therefore, \pi contains \tau_{i}^{\prime}\tau_{k}\tau_{p}^{\prime}\simeq 231.
As per the above cases, \tau will not be in the sorting class of \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}} if it contains 132. Henceforth, assume that \tau\in\text{Av}_{n}(132). If \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\simeq 3214 and neither of \tau_{i} and \tau_{j} have popped when \tau_{k} enters the stack, then the stack will contain \tau_{k}\tau_{j}\tau_{i}\simeq 123. In order for the stack to avoid the pattern 1\underline{23}, there must be some element \tau_{s}<\tau_{k} between \tau_{i} and \tau_{j} in the stack. But then \tau contains \tau_{s}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\simeq 132, a contradiction. Thus, at least one of \tau_{i} or \tau_{j} will be popped before \tau_{k} enters. By Lemma 3.2, there is some \tau_{k}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{k} in the stack when \tau_{l} is added. Then, \pi will contain either \tau_{i}\tau_{l}\tau_{k}^{\prime}\simeq 231 or \tau_{j}\tau_{l}\tau_{k}^{\prime}\simeq 231. Lastly, if \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\simeq 4213, we proceed similarly as before in most cases. The only new scenario we must consider is if \tau_{i}, but not \tau_{j}, is popped when \tau_{k} enters, as \tau_{i}\tau_{l}\tau_{k}^{\prime}\not\simeq 231. However, note that \tau_{l}<\tau_{1}, for otherwise \tau_{1}\tau_{i}\tau_{l}\simeq 132. So, we set i=1 without changing the relative ordering of \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}. But \tau_{1} can never be popped, so we may simply proceed as before. In conclusion, if \tau is not in \text{Av}_{n}(132,3214,4213), it is not in \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}).
Next, we show that if \pi contains \pi_{i}\pi_{j}\pi_{k}\simeq 231, then \tau contains at least one of 132, 3214, or 4213. We split into the following cases based on the order in which \pi_{i}, \pi_{j}, and \pi_{k} appear in \tau:
•
Suppose \pi_{i} occurs before \pi_{j} in \tau, so that \pi_{i} must pop before \pi_{j} enters the stack. Say \pi_{i} was popped when \tau_{p} was being added, as \tau_{p}\tau_{q}\tau_{r}\simeq 1\underline{23}. Note that \tau_{q}\leq\pi_{i}, for otherwise the stack would contain \pi_{i}\tau_{q}\tau_{r}\simeq 1\underline{23}. Then, \tau contains \tau_{r}\tau_{q}\tau_{p}\pi_{j}, which forms either the 3214 or 4213 pattern.
•
If \tau contains \pi_{k}\pi_{j}\pi_{i}\simeq 132, we are done.
•
Next, suppose \tau contains \pi_{j}\pi_{i}\pi_{k}, so that \pi_{i} pops before \pi_{k} enters the stack. As before, say \pi_{i} was popped when \tau_{p} was being added to the stack, as \tau_{p}\tau_{q}\tau_{r}\simeq 1\underline{23}. If \tau_{q}<\pi_{i}, then \tau contains \tau_{q}\pi_{j}\pi_{i}\simeq 132. Else, \pi_{i}<\tau_{q}, so that \pi_{i}\tau_{q}\tau_{r}\simeq 1\underline{23}, but then \pi_{j} would have popped before \pi_{i} entered, a contradiction.
•
Lastly, if \tau contains \pi_{j}\pi_{k}\pi_{i}, the element \pi_{j} must pop before \pi_{k} enters but after \pi_{i} enters, which is impossible.
∎
Proposition 3.4.
It holds that \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}) is a permutation class as every permutation in \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}) necessarily avoids every permutation outside of \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}).
The following two lemmas allow us to enumerate \text{Av}_{n}(132,3214,4213) in Theorem 3.7.
Lemma 3.5 (Simion and Schmidt [15, Proposition 11]).
It holds that |\text{Av}_{n}(132,321)|=\binom{n}{2}+1.
Lemma 3.6 (Simion and Schmidt [15, Proposition 8]).
It holds that |\text{Av}_{n}(132,213)|=2^{n-1} for n\geq 1. Additionally, |\text{Av}_{0}(132,213)|=1.
Theorem 3.7.
It holds that |\text{Av}_{n}(132,3214,4213)|=2^{n}-n.
Proof.
Let \tau^{a} and \tau^{b} be subsequences of \tau so that \tau=\tau^{a}n\tau^{b}. We claim that the following three conditions are equivalent to \tau\in\text{Av}_{n}(132,3214,4213).
(1)
each element of \tau^{a} is larger than each element of \tau^{b},
(2)
\tau^{a}\in\text{Av}(132,321), and
(3)
\tau^{b}\in\text{Av}(132,213).
Condition (1) follows by letting n correspond to the 3 in the pattern 132. Conditions (2) and (3) follow by letting n correspond to the 4 in the patterns 3214 and 4213. To check that the conditions are sufficient, suppose they all hold. Let i be the index such that \tau_{i}=n.
•
We show that each subsequence \tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l} of \tau avoids 132. Clearly, we have j\neq i and l\neq i. Since \tau^{a} and \tau^{b} individually avoid 132, we only need to consider when j<i<l. But it suffices to let k=i, which is taken care of by condition (1).
•
We show that each subsequence \tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\tau_{m} of \tau avoids 3214. Clearly, we must have j,k,l\neq i. If m\leq i, it suffices to only consider m=i, which is satisfied as \tau^{a} avoids 321. Else, if i<m, the inequality \tau_{j}<\tau_{m} along with the condition (1) implies that i<j. But \tau^{b} avoids 213, and thus also 3214.
•
We show that each subsequence \tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\tau_{m} of \tau avoids 4213. Clearly, we must have k,l,m\neq i. If i\leq j or if j\leq i<k, only need to consider j=i. Since \tau^{b} avoids 213, the case j=i is covered. Note that the cases l<i<k and i<k<l are impossible by condition (1) and the inequality \tau_{l}<\tau_{k}<\tau_{m}. Lastly, say m<i. But \tau^{a} avoids 321, and thus also 4213.
Due to the condition (1), the index i determines the values of the elements of \tau^{a} and \tau^{b}. Those elements are then independently ordered by the conditions (2) and (3). We thus sum over i, using Lemmas 3.5 and 3.6:
\displaystyle|\text{Av}_{n}(132,3214,4213)|
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\text{Av}_{i-1}(132,321)|\cdot|\text{Av}_{n-i}(132,213)|
\displaystyle=\left(\binom{n-1}{2}+1\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\left(\binom{k}{2}+1\right)2^{n-k-2}
\displaystyle=\left(\binom{n-1}{2}+1\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\binom{k}{2}2^{n-2-k}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}2^{n-k-2}
\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(n-2)+1\right)+\left(2^{n-1}-1-\frac{1}{2}n(n-1)\right)+(2^{n-1}-1)
\displaystyle=2^{n}-n,
as desired.
∎
Corollary 3.8 then follows from Theorem 3.3 and Theorem 3.7.
Corollary 3.8.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}} is enumerated by
|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}})|=2^{n}-n.
Corollary 3.8 completes our analysis of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}). Next, we find \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}) and \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}). Recall the following results, which we generalize in Theorem 3.11:
Theorem 3.9 (Cerbai et al. [5, Corollary 3.3]).
For k\geq 3, we have
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{k(k-1)\cdots 1})=\text{Av}_{n}(12\cdots k,132).
Theorem 3.10 (Defant and Zheng [11, Theorem 6.1]).
For k\geq 3, we have
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{k(k-1)\cdots 1}})=\text{Av}_{n}(\underline{12\cdots k},132).
The following is a natural extension of Theorems 3.9 and 3.10.
Theorem 3.11.
For k\geq 3, let \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k} be a pattern with the values k(k-1)\cdots 1. Then,
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\sigma})=\text{Av}_{n}(\text{rev}(\sigma),132).
Proof.
Let \pi=\text{SC}_{\sigma}(\tau). Suppose that \tau contains \text{rev}(\sigma), and let the leftmost occurrence of \text{rev}(\sigma) in \tau be \tau_{l_{1}}\tau_{l_{2}}\cdots\tau_{l_{k}}. Evidently, \pi contains \tau_{l_{k-1}}\tau_{l_{k}}\tau_{l_{k-2}}\simeq 231. If \tau avoids \text{rev}(\sigma), then \text{SC}_{\sigma}(\tau)=\text{rev}(\tau). As such, \tau must also avoid \text{rev}(231)=132.
To check the converse, suppose \pi contains 231. Then, either \pi\neq\text{rev}(\tau) or \pi=\text{rev}(\tau). In the first scenario, \tau must contain \text{rev}(\sigma). In the second, \tau must contain 132.
∎
It follows from Theorem 3.11 the characterizations of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}) and \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}). Lemma 3.12 then simplifies our description of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}).
Lemma 3.12.
It holds that \text{Av}_{n}(1\underline{23},132)=\text{Av}_{n}(\underline{123},132).
Proof.
Clearly, each permutation in \text{Av}_{n}(1\underline{23},132) is also in \text{Av}_{n}(\underline{123},132). Now, suppose for contradiction that \tau avoids \underline{123} and 132 but not 1\underline{23}. Say \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j-1}\tau_{j}\simeq 1\underline{23}, with i<j-2. Since \tau avoids \underline{123}, \tau_{j-2} is required to be greater than \tau_{j-1}. But then \tau_{i}\tau_{j-2}\tau_{j-1} is an instance of 132. The conclusion follows.
∎
Theorem 3.11 and Lemma 3.12 give the following characterization of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}).
Corollary 3.13.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1} is characterized by
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1})=\text{Av}_{n}(\underline{123},132).
Proposition 3.14.
It does not hold that \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}) is a permutation class since 2314 contains 123 but \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}(2314)=4132 avoids 231 while \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}(123)=231 does not avoid 231.
Note that from Theorem 3.10, we have \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{321}})=\text{Av}_{n}(\underline{123},132)=\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}). Additionally, for \tau\in\text{Av}_{n}(\underline{123},132), we note that \text{SC}_{\underline{321}}(\tau)=\text{rev}(\tau)=\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}(\tau). However, the two maps are distinct. For example, \text{SC}_{\underline{321}}(1324)=4231\neq 2341=\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}(1324). Next, Lemma 3.15 allows us to enumerate \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}).
Lemma 3.15 (Hein and Huang [12, Proposition 3.2]).
It holds that |\text{Av}_{n}(\underline{12\cdots k},132)|=M_{k-1,n}, where M_{m,n} denotes the generalized Motzkin number, for k\geq 3.
Recalling that M_{2,n}=M_{n} [12], Corollary 3.16 follows from Corollary 3.13 and Lemma 3.15.
Corollary 3.16.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1} is enumerated by
|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1})|=M_{n}.
Having completed our analysis of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}), we move on to characterize \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}). Lemma 3.17 simplifies our description of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}).
Lemma 3.17.
It holds that \text{Av}_{n}(\underline{12}3,132)=\text{Av}_{n}(123,132).
Proof.
Clearly, each permutation in \text{Av}_{n}(123,132) is also in \text{Av}_{n}(\underline{12}3,132). Next, suppose for contradiction that \tau avoids \underline{12}3 and 132 but not 123. Say \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\simeq 123, where i<j-1. If \tau_{j-1}<\tau_{j}, then \tau_{j-1}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\simeq\underline{12}3. Else, if \tau_{j}<\tau_{j-1}, then \tau_{i}\tau_{j-1}\tau_{j}\simeq 132. The conclusion follows.

Theorem 3.11 and Lemma 3.17 then imply Corollary 3.18.
Corollary 3.18.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{3\underline{21}} is characterized by

Corollary 3.21 follows from Corollary 3.18 and Lemma 3.20, completing our analysis of \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}).
Corollary 3.21.

In this section, we introduce mesh patterns in order to describe \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{13}2}) and \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}). A permutation \pi contains \mu=(\sigma,A) if \pi has an occurrence of \sigma such that no elements of \pi lie within the forbidden squares in A. Henceforth, let \mu_{132}=(132,\{(0,2),(2,0),(2,1)\}) and let \mu_{2413}=(2413,\{(1,0),(2,1),(2,2)\}). The plots of \mu_{132} and \mu_{2413} are shown in Figure 5.

As immediate corollaries of Theorems 3.22 and 3.23, we describe \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\underline{13}2}).

The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{13}2} is characterized by

The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{13}2} is enumerated by

Next, we show that \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}) can also be described with mesh pattern avoidance. We first show Lemma 3.27 about one property of \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}.
Lemma 3.27.
Consider \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} that is being processed by \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}, and suppose \tau_{i} is an element currently in the stack. Subsequently, after each time an element enters the stack, the stack will contain some element of value at most \tau_{i}.
Proof.
When \tau_{i} is popped from the stack, the next element to be added to the stack must be less than \tau_{i}.
∎
With Lemma 3.27, we describe \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}) in Theorem 3.28.
Theorem 3.28.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1} is characterized by
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1})=\text{Av}_{n}(1324,\mu_{2413}).
Proof.
Let \pi=\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\tau). We first show that if \tau does not avoid 1324 and \mu_{2413}, then \pi contains 231. Suppose \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\simeq 1324. By Lemma 3.27, there is some term \tau_{i}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{i} in the stack when \tau_{j} enters. If \tau_{j} is not popped when \tau_{k} enters, the stack, and thus \pi, would contain \tau_{k}\tau_{j}\tau_{i}^{\prime}\simeq 231. Thus, \tau_{j} is popped before \tau_{k} enters. Lemma 3.27 implies that there is some \tau_{k}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{k} in the stack when \tau_{l} enters. Then, \pi contains \tau_{j}\tau_{l}\tau_{k}^{\prime}\simeq 231. Now assuming that \tau avoids 1324, say \tau contains \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}\simeq\mu_{2413}. In what follows, we consider the elements of \tau relative to the plot of \tau_{i}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}. Note that the (0,0) square is forbidden, as any element in that square would form the 1324 pattern in \tau along with \tau_{i}\tau_{k}\tau_{l}. By Lemma 3.27, there is some element \tau_{i}^{\prime}\leq\tau_{i} in the stack when \tau_{j} enters. Since the (1,0) square is banned, we have that \tau_{k}\leq\tau_{i}^{\prime}. We now claim that there will be some element \tau_{j}^{\prime}\geq\tau_{l} in the stack when \tau_{l} enters. Suppose \tau_{j} was popped before \tau_{k} enters the stack while \tau_{p} was being added because \tau_{p}\tau_{j}\tau_{r}\simeq\underline{23}1. Then, \tau_{k}<\tau_{r}, which implies that \tau_{k}<\tau_{p}. As (2,1) and (2,2) are forbidden, we have that \tau_{l}<\tau_{r}, which implies that there will be some \tau_{j}^{\prime}\geq\tau_{l} in the stack when \tau_{k} enters. But after \tau_{k} enters, \tau_{j}^{\prime} cannot be popped, so that the stack, and thus \pi, contains \tau_{l}\tau_{j}^{\prime}\tau_{i}^{\prime}\simeq 231.
Next, we show that if \pi contains \pi_{i}\pi_{j}\pi_{k}\simeq 231, its preimage \tau contains either 1324 or \mu_{2413}. Consider the following four cases for the ordering of \pi_{i}, \pi_{j}, and \pi_{k} in \tau:
•
Suppose \pi_{i} occurs before \pi_{j} in \tau, so that \pi_{i} must be popped from the stack before \pi_{j} enters. Say \pi_{i} was popped when \tau_{p} was being added to the stack because \tau_{p}\pi_{i}\tau_{r}\simeq\underline{23}1. Then \tau contains \tau_{r}\pi_{i}\tau_{p}\pi_{j}\simeq 1324.
•
Next, suppose \tau contains \pi_{k}\pi_{j}\pi_{i}, so that \pi_{j} must not pop before \pi_{i} enters. Then, there must be some element less than \pi_{k} between \pi_{i} and \pi_{j} in the stack. Choose l to be the least index of such an element \tau_{l}. Henceforth, we consider the elements of \tau with respect to the plot of \pi_{k}\pi_{j}\tau_{l}\pi_{i}\simeq 2413. By our choice of \tau_{l}, the squares (2,0) and (2,1) are empty. If there existed some element \tau_{s} in the (1,0) square, then before \tau_{l} is added to the stack, all the elements between \pi_{j} and \tau_{l} as well as \pi_{j} itself will be popped, a contradiction. Next, if the (2,2) square is not empty, choose the least index t corresponding to some \tau_{t} in the (2,2) square. Then before \tau_{t} is added to the stack, all the elements between \pi_{j} and \tau_{l}, as well as \pi_{j} itself, will be popped, again a contradiction. Thus, \tau contains \pi_{k}\pi_{j}\tau_{l}\pi_{i}\simeq\mu_{2413}.
•
Say \tau contains \pi_{j}\pi_{i}\pi_{k}. Similarly to the previous case, \pi_{j} must not pop before \pi_{i} enters. However, both elements must pop before \pi_{k} enters. Therefore, there exists an element \pi_{k}^{\prime}<\pi_{i} below \pi_{j} as well as an element \tau_{l}<\pi_{k}^{\prime} between \pi_{i} and \pi_{j} in the stack. Repeat the previous argument, we have that \tau contains \pi_{k}^{\prime}\pi_{j}\tau_{l}\pi_{i}\simeq\mu_{2413}.
•
Lastly, if \tau contains \pi_{j}\pi_{k}\pi_{i}, the element \pi_{j} must pop before \pi_{k} enters but after \pi_{i} enters, which is impossible.
∎
Proposition 3.29.
It does not hold that \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}) is a permutation class since 25314 contains 2413 but \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(25314)=54132 avoids 231 while \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(2413)=3142 does not avoid 231.
The first ten terms of (|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1})|)_{n\geq 0} are
1,1,2,6,22,90,394,1806,8558,41586.
These terms match the OEIS sequence A006318, the Schröeder numbers. We leave this enumeration as a conjecture.
Conjecture 3.30.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1} is enumerated by

3.3. The Sorting Classes of \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}} and \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}
The classes \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}) and \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}) cannot be described succinctly with (mesh) pattern avoidance. We begin by showing the following property of \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}.
Lemma 3.31.
For \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}, its image \pi under \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}} is a decreasing sequence if and only if \tau is increasing. In other words, \pi=n(n-1)\cdots 1 if and only if \tau=12\cdots n.
Proof.
Since 12\cdots n avoids \text{rev}(1\underline{32})=\underline{23}1, its image under \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}} indeed is n(n-1)\cdots 1. To show that \tau=12\cdots n is unique, suppose that \pi is decreasing. As \tau_{1} cannot be popped, we have \tau_{1}=\pi_{n}=1. Then, \tau_{2} also cannot be popped, as it sits on top of \tau_{1}=1 in the stack. Thus, \tau_{2}=\pi_{n-1}=2. Repeating this argument shows the desired.
∎
Split \tau into subsequences by \tau=m_{1}a_{1}m_{2}a_{2}\cdots m_{k}a_{k}, where m_{i} is the i-th left-to-right minimum of \tau. Note that m_{k}=1. With Lemma 3.31, we characterize \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}) in Theorem 3.32.
Theorem 3.32.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}} is composed of the permutations \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} such that \text{rev}(a_{1})\text{rev}(a_{2})\cdots\text{rev}(a_{k}) is decreasing.
Proof.
We first show that none of the left-to-right minimums of \tau are premature. Indeed, if m_{i} is premature, then the term \tau_{j} immediately below m_{i} in the stack must be less than m_{i}. But \tau_{j} comes before m_{i} in \tau, contradicting the left-to-right minimality of m_{i}.
Let b_{i}=\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}(a_{i}). Since each term of a_{i} is greater than m_{i}, the subsequence a_{i} is sorted independently of the elements below m_{i} in the stack. When m_{i+1} is added, the remaining elements of a_{i} still in the stack are all popped, as m_{i+1} and m_{i} form the 1\underline{32} pattern with the element immediately above m_{i} in the stack. It follows that
\pi=\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}(\tau)=b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{k}m_{k}m_{k-1}\cdots m_{1}.
For \pi to avoid 231, the subsequence b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{k} must be decreasing, by letting m_{k}=1 correspond to the 1 in the pattern 231. As a consequence of Lemma 3.31, we have that each a_{i} is increasing. In particular, b_{i}=\text{rev}(a_{i}), so that \text{rev}(a_{1})\text{rev}(a_{2})\cdots\text{rev}(a_{k}) must be decreasing, as claimed.
The condition is sufficient, as \pi then consists of a decreasing sequence followed by an increasing sequence, which clearly avoids 231.
∎
Proposition 3.33.
It does not hold that \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}) is a permutation class since 2413 contains 132 but \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}(2413)=4312 avoids 231 while \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}(132)=231 does not avoid 231.
Defant and Zheng [11] studied the sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{132}}. Their conclusion is listed in Theorem 3.34.
Theorem 3.34 (Defant and Zheng [11, Proposition 4.1]).
A permutation \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} is in \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{132}}) if and only if \text{rev}(a_{1})\text{rev}(a_{2})\cdots\text{rev}(a_{k}) is decreasing.
It follows from Theorem 3.32 and Theorem 3.34 that \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}})=\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{132}}). For \tau\in\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}), we additionally note that \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}(\tau)=\text{SC}_{\underline{132}}(\tau), from the proof of Theorem 3.34 in Defant and Zheng [11]. However, the two maps are distinct. For example, \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}(2431)=3412\neq 1342=\text{SC}_{\underline{132}}(2431).
Theorem 3.35 (Defant and Zheng [11, Theorem 4.4]).
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{132}} is enumerated by
|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{132}})|=C_{n}.
Corollary 3.36 follows from Theorem 3.35, completing our investigation on \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}}).
Corollary 3.36.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{1\underline{32}} is enumerated by
|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{1\underline{32}})|=C_{n}.
Next, we describe \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}), beginning with Lemma 3.37 and Lemma 3.38.
Lemma 3.37.
Consider \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} that is being processed by \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}, and suppose \tau_{i} is an element currently in the stack. Subsequently, after each time an element enters the stack, the stack will contain some element of value at most \tau_{i}.
Proof.
When \tau_{i} is popped from the stack, the next element \tau_{p} to be added to the stack must be less than \tau_{i}.
∎
Lemma 3.38 (Defant and Zheng [11, p.6]).
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{12} is characterized by
\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{12})=\text{Av}_{n}(213).
Now, let \tau^{a} and \tau^{b} be subsequences of \tau so that \tau=\tau^{a}n\tau^{b}. We use Lemma 3.37 and Lemma 3.38 to characterize \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}).
Theorem 3.39.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3} consists of the permutations \tau such that
(1)
each element in \tau^{a} is greater than each element in \tau^{b},
(2)
\tau^{a}\in\text{Av}(3\underline{21},132), and
(3)
\tau^{b}\in\text{Av}(213).
Proof.
Let \pi=\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(\tau). Since n cannot be popped from the stack, once it is added, the rest of the input sequence is essentially sorted by a 12-avoiding stack. Thus, define \pi^{b}=\text{SC}_{12}(\tau^{b}). Let \pi^{a} consist of the premature elements of \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(\tau^{a}), and let \pi^{c} be the remaining elements, so that \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(\tau^{a})=\pi^{a}\pi^{c}. By following the definition of the stack-sorting map, we see that \pi=\pi^{a}\pi^{b}n\pi^{c}.
We first show that the listed conditions are necessary for \pi to avoid 231. Letting n correspond to the 3 in the pattern 231, we see that each element of \pi before n must be less than each element of \pi after n. In particular, each element of \pi^{a} must be less than each element of \pi^{c}. Lemma 3.37 implies that the smallest element of \tau^{a} cannot be premature, and thus will be in \pi^{c}. As a result, \pi^{a} must be empty, from which condition (1) follows. Furthermore, \tau^{a} must avoid \text{Av}(3\underline{21}), so that \pi^{c}=\text{rev}(\tau^{a}). Since \pi^{c} avoids 231, we have that \tau^{a} avoids 132. Lastly, Lemma 3.38 implies condition (3), since \pi^{b} has to avoid 231.
To check that the conditions are sufficient, suppose they all hold. Let i be the index such that \pi_{i}=n. We show that each subsequence \pi_{j}\pi_{k}\pi_{l} of \pi avoids 231. Clearly, we have that j\neq i and l\neq i. If j and l are both greater than i or both less than i, then conditions (2) and (3) ensure that \pi_{c} and \pi_{b} individually avoid 231. Lastly, if j<i<l, it suffices to consider only the case k=i. Condition (1) finishes.
∎
Proposition 3.40.
It does not hold that \text{Sort}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}) is a permutation class since 4132 contains 132 but \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(4132)=3214 avoids 231 while \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(132)=231 does not avoid 231.
Lemma 3.41 then allows us to enumerate \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}) in Theorem 3.42.
Lemma 3.41.
We have |\text{Av}_{n}(3\underline{21},132)|=2^{n-1} for n\geq 1. Additionally, |\text{Av}_{0}(3\underline{21},132)|=1.
Proof.
Consider \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} and let \tau=\tau^{a}n\tau^{b}, as usual. We claim that \tau\in\text{Av}_{n}(3\underline{21},132) is equivalent to the following two conditions:
(1)
\tau^{b}=12\cdots(n-i), and
(2)
\tau^{a}\in\text{Av}(3\underline{21},132).
Letting n correspond to the 3 in the pattern 3\underline{21}, we see that \tau_{b} must be increasing. Furthermore, letting n correspond to the 3 in the pattern 132, we have that each element of \tau^{b} must be less than each term of \tau^{a}. Condition (1) follows. Condition (2) is clearly necessary. The conditions are sufficient, as \tau^{a} and \tau^{b} both individually avoid 3\underline{21} and 132.
Clearly, |\text{Av}_{0}(3\underline{21},132)|=|\text{Av}_{1}(3\underline{21},132)|=1. Now, let \tau_{i}=n. Due to condition (1), the index i determines the values of the elements of \tau^{a} and \tau^{b}. Those elements are then independently ordered by the two conditions. By summing over i, we have that
|\text{Av}_{n}(3\underline{21},132)|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\text{Av}_{n-i}(3\underline{21},132)|.
From this recurrence follows the lemma.
∎
Finally, we enumerate \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}) with Theorem 3.39 and Lemma 3.41.
Theorem 3.42.
The sorting class of \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3} is enumerated by
|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3})|=C_{n-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}2^{n-2-i}C_{i}.
Proof.
Let \tau=\tau^{a}n\tau^{b}, as usual. From Theorem 3.39, the index i of n determines \tau^{a} and \tau^{b}. Summing over i, with Lemma 3.41, we have that
\displaystyle|\text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3})|
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\text{Av}_{i-1}(3\underline{21},132)|\cdot|\text{Av}_{n-i}(213)|
\displaystyle=C_{n-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}2^{i-2}C_{n-i}=C_{n-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}2^{n-2-i}C_{i},
as desired.
∎

Proof.

4. Dynamics of the maps \text{SC}_{\sigma}
In this section, we study some dynamical properties of \text{SC}_{\sigma}. Section 4.1 is dedicated to finding the maximum number of preimages a permutation in \mathfrak{S}_{n} can have under \text{SC}_{\sigma} for several \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}. We additionally describe the unique permutation \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} fulfilling that maximum. Section 4.2 deals with the periodic points of \text{SC}_{\sigma} for several \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}.
4.1. Maximum preimages of the maps \text{SC}_{\sigma}
Define the movement sequence of a permutation \tau under \text{SC}_{\sigma} as a word composed of n copies each of the letters N and X that represents the sequence of steps the stack of \text{SC}_{\sigma} takes as it processes \tau. We say such a movement sequence has size n. We use N to denote a push operation (entry) and X to denote a pop operation (exit), as in Berlow [3]. For example, the movement sequence of \tau=514362 under \text{SC}_{123} is NNNXNNXXNXXX (see Figure 1). The following result from Berlow [3] bounds the number of preimages under \text{SC}_{\sigma}.
Theorem 4.1 (Berlow [3, Theorem 3.2]).
Every permutation \pi of length n\geq 3 has at most C_{n-1} preimages under the map \text{SC}_{\sigma}, for all patterns \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}. That is,
|\text{SC}_{\sigma}^{-1}(\pi)|\leq C_{n-1}.
Fix \pi and consider permutations \tau satisfying \text{SC}_{\sigma}(\tau)=\pi. Since \tau_{1} cannot be premature, at any point (excepting the beginning and end) of the sorting process, there must have been at least one more push than pop to the stack of \text{SC}_{\sigma}. Thus, each proper prefix of a movement sequence must have at least one more N than X. Berlow [3] showed Theorem 4.1 using this criteria on the possible movement sequences. Furthermore, should |\text{SC}_{\sigma}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1} for some \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} and \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}, every valid movement sequence of size n must correspond to an image \tau of \pi.
Recall that by Lemma 2.1 we have |\text{SC}_{\sigma}^{-1}(\pi)|=|\text{SC}_{\text{comp}(\sigma)}^{-1}(\text{comp}(\pi))|. Therefore, it holds that \max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\sigma}^{-1}(\pi)|=\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\text{comp}(\sigma)}^{-1}(\pi)|.
4.1.1. The maps \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3} and \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}
As noted, it suffices to only consider the map \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}. We show that the C_{n-1} bound proposed in Theorem 4.1 can be met.
Lemma 4.2.
Let \pi=(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 1n. Then, \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(\tau)=\pi if and only if \tau_{1}=n and \tau^{\prime}\in\text{Av}_{n-1}(213), where \tau^{\prime}=\tau_{2}\tau_{3}\cdots\tau_{n}. In particular,
|\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}^{-1}(\pi)|=|\text{Av}_{n-1}(213)|=C_{n-1}.
Proof.
As \tau_{1} always stays in the stack, \tau_{1}=n is necessary. Henceforth, \tau^{\prime} is essentially sorted by a stack avoiding 12. The conclusion follows from Lemma 3.38.
∎
From Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.2 it holds the following.
Corollary 4.3.
We have
\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}^{-1}(\pi)|=\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1}.
We additionally show that the permutation in Lemma 4.2 is unique.
Theorem 4.4.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 1n.
Proof.
Let \pi satisfy |\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1}. By the proof of Theorem 4.1, each valid movement sequence corresponds to some \tau such that \text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}^{-1}(\tau)=\pi. Consider the movement sequence N(NX)^{n-1}X. Following the definition of the movement sequence, we have that \tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}\tau_{1}\simeq 123 for each i>1. Thus \tau_{n}<\tau_{n-1}<\cdots<\tau_{1}, implying that \tau=n(n-1)\cdots 21. Finally, \pi=\text{SC}_{\underline{12}3}(\tau)=(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 21n, as desired.
∎
With Lemma 2.1, we extend the above to \text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}.
Corollary 4.5.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{\underline{32}1}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=23\cdots n1.
4.1.2. The maps \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1} and \text{SC}_{\underline{21}3}
We primarily consider \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}. We show that the C_{n-1} bound proposed in Theorem 4.1 can be met.
Lemma 4.6.
Let \pi=n(n-1)\cdots 1. Then, \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\tau)=\pi if and only if \tau_{1}=1 and \tau^{\prime}\in\text{Av}_{n-1}(213), where \tau^{\prime}=\tau_{2}\tau_{3}\cdots\tau_{n}. In particular,
|\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{-1}(\pi)|=|\text{Av}_{n-1}(213)|=C_{n-1}.
Proof.
As \tau_{1} always stays in the stack, \tau_{1}=1 is necessary. Henceforth, \tau^{\prime} is essentially sorted by a stack avoiding 12. The conclusion follows from Lemma 3.38.
∎
From Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.6 it holds the following.
Corollary 4.7.
We have
\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{-1}(\pi)|=\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{\underline{21}3}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1}.
We additionally show that the permutation in Lemma 4.6 is unique.
Theorem 4.8.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=n(n-1)\cdots 1.
Proof.
Let \pi satisfy |\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1}. By the proof of Theorem 4.1, each valid movement sequence corresponds to some \tau such that \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{-1}(\tau)=\pi. Consider the movement sequence N(NX)^{n-1}X. Following the definition of the movement sequence, we have that \tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}\tau_{1}\simeq 231 for each i>1. Thus \tau_{1}<\tau_{n}<\tau_{n-1}<\cdots<\tau_{2}, implying that \tau=1n(n-1)\cdots 2. Finally, \pi=\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\tau)=n(n-1)\cdots 1, as desired.
∎
With Lemma 2.1, we extend the above to \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}.
Corollary 4.9.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=12\cdots n.
4.1.3. The maps \text{SC}_{2\underline{31}} and \text{SC}_{2\underline{13}}
We primarily consider \text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}, recalling that \text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}=\text{SC}_{231} by Corollary 2.3. Consider the following special case of Theorem 3.7 from Berlow [3].
Theorem 4.10 (Berlow [3, Theorem 3.7]).
Let \sigma\in S_{3} be a classical pattern. There exists a permutation \pi satisfying |\text{SC}_{\sigma}(\pi)|=C_{n-1} if and only if \sigma_{1} and \sigma_{2} are consecutive numbers.
Since 2 and 3 are consecutive, we have the following from Theorem 4.10.
Corollary 4.11.
We have
\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}^{-1}(\pi)|=\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{2\underline{13}}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1}.
We additionally show the following.
Theorem 4.12.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=n(n-1)\cdots 1.
Proof.
Let \pi satisfy |\text{SC}_{231}^{-1}(\pi)|=C_{n-1}. By the proof of Theorem 4.1, each valid movement sequence corresponds to some \tau such that \text{SC}_{231}^{-1}(\tau)=\pi. Consider the movement sequence N(NX)^{n-1}X. Following the definition of the movement sequence, we have that \tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}\tau_{1}\simeq 231 for each i>1. Thus \tau_{1}<\tau_{n}<\tau_{n-1}<\cdots<\tau_{2}, implying that \tau=1n(n-1)\cdots 2. Finally, \pi=\text{SC}_{231}(\tau)=n(n-1)\cdots 1, as desired.
∎
With Lemma 2.1, we extend the above to \text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}.
Corollary 4.13.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{2\underline{31}}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=12\cdots n.
4.1.4. The maps \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}} and \text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}
We primarily consider \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}.
Conjecture 4.14.
For n\geq 2, it holds that
\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi)|=\max_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}|\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}^{-1}(\pi)|=2^{n-2}.
We first verify that a permutation achieving the aforementioned maximum exists.
Lemma 4.15.
Let \pi=(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 1n. Then, \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}(\tau)=\pi if and only if \tau_{1}=n and \tau^{\prime}\in\text{Av}_{n-1}(132,213), where \tau^{\prime}=\tau_{2}\tau_{3}\cdots\tau_{n}. In particular,
|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi)|=|\text{Av}_{n-1}(132,213)|=2^{n-2}.
Proof.
As the first element of \tau always stays in the stack, \tau_{1}=n is necessary. Henceforth, the entire stack (except \tau_{1}) will pop if there is ever added an element less than the second bottom-most element in the stack. Thus, \tau^{\prime} is the concatenation of increasing intervals of consecutive numbers, such that each interval begins with a left-to-right minimum of \tau^{\prime}. In the notation of Defant and Zheng [11], \tau^{\prime} is reverse-layered. It is known [14] that the reverse-layered permutations precisely form the set \text{Av}(132,213). We thus are able to enumerate |\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi)| with Lemma 3.6.
∎
We partially resolve Conjecture 4.14 by induction on n, with the aim of showing that a permutation \pi achieving the maximum value of |\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}| is \pi=(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 1n. The bound claimed by Conjecture 4.14 would then follow by Lemma 4.15. The inductive base case n=2 is obvious. In what follows, for a permutation \pi, let \pi^{\prime} be obtained by swapping the elements \pi_{n} and \pi_{n}+1 in \pi. The following two lemmas ultimately work to show Corollary 4.18.
Lemma 4.16.
Consider a permutation \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} satisfying \pi_{n}<n and \pi_{n-1}\neq\pi_{n}+1. Then,
|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi)|\leq|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi^{\prime})|.
Proof.
Let \tau be a preimage of \pi. Evidently, \tau_{1}=\pi_{n}. We claim that the elements \pi_{n} and \pi_{n}+1 are never consecutive entries in the stack. Indeed, if they were, then \pi_{n}+1 would sit immediately above of \pi_{n} in the stack, so that \pi_{n}+1 will never be popped from the stack. Therefore, \pi_{n}+1 and \pi_{n} will be adjacent in \pi, contradicting our assumptions. Now, let \tau^{\prime} be obtained by swapping \pi_{n} and \pi_{n}+1 in \tau. Note that \pi_{n} and \pi_{n}+1 have the same size relative to the other elements of \tau, and that \pi_{n} and \pi_{n}+1 cannot both be a part of the 1\underline{23} pattern the stack checks for. It follows that the sequences of push and pop operations that sort \tau and \tau^{\prime} are identical. In particular, \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}(\tau^{\prime})=\pi^{\prime} for all preimages \tau of \pi. As the map \tau\mapsto\tau^{\prime} is injective, the lemma follows.
∎
Lemma 4.17.
Consider \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} satisfying \pi_{n-1}=\pi_{n}+1. Then,
|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi)|\leq 2^{n-3}.
In particular, Lemma 4.15 implies that \pi does not achieve the maximum value of |\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}| over \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}.
Proof.
Let \tau be a preimage of \pi. Evidently, \tau_{1}=\pi_{n}. We claim that \tau_{2}=\pi_{n}+1 if \pi_{n-1}=\pi_{n}+1. Note that \pi_{n}+1 must be immediately above \pi_{n} in the stack at some point. Thus, when \pi_{n}+1 is the first element in the input sequence, all the terms in the stack (except \pi_{n}) must be popped. If \tau_{i} is the element immediately above \pi_{n} in the stack at that moment, we require (\pi_{n}+1)\tau_{i}\pi_{n}\simeq 1\underline{23}, which is impossible. Thus, \tau_{2}=\pi_{n}+1.
In what follows, let \tau^{*}\in S_{n-1} be obtained by removing \pi_{n}+1 from \tau and shifting all the other elements of \tau greater than \pi_{n} down by 1. Define \pi^{*}\in S_{n-1} similarly. Note that there is a bijection between \tau and \tau^{*}, as well as between \pi and \pi^{*}. As \pi_{n}+1 will never be popped from the stack, we see that \text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}(\tau^{*})=\pi^{*}. Therefore,
|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi)|=|\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}(\pi^{*})|\leq 2^{n-3},
by inductively using the statement of Conjecture 4.14.
∎
We are now ready to partially describe permutations that maximize |\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}| by smoothing.
Corollary 4.18.
There exists \pi achieving the maximum value of |\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}| over \mathfrak{S}_{n} such that the last element of \pi is \pi_{n}=n.
Proof.
Begin with some optimal \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}. If \pi_{n}<n and \pi_{n-1}=\pi_{n}+1, then \pi is not optimal, by Lemma 4.17. Thus \pi_{n}<n and \pi_{n-1}=\pi_{n}+1, so that \pi^{\prime} has at least as many preimages as \pi, by Lemma 4.16. Henceforth, we consider \pi^{\prime}, and repeat these steps until we arrive at an optimal permutation such that its last element is n.
∎
We end by conjecturing that the permutation in Lemma 4.15 is unique.
Conjecture 4.19.
The maximum of |\text{SC}_{1\underline{23}}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=(n-1)(n-2)\cdots 1n. Furthermore, the maximum of |\text{SC}_{3\underline{21}}^{-1}| is uniquely achieved by \pi=23\cdots n1.
4.2. Periodic points of the maps \text{SC}_{\sigma}
Consider \pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}. In what follows, let \pi^{*}\in S_{n-1} obtained by removing the element 1 from \pi and shifting the remaining elements of \pi down by 1.
Theorem 4.20.
For \sigma\in\{\underline{13}2,\underline{23}1,1\underline{32},2\underline{31}\}, the periodic points of \text{SC}_{\sigma} in \mathfrak{S}_{n} are precisely \text{Av}_{n}(132,231). For n\geq 2, these points have period 2.
Proof.
Let \sigma=\underline{23}1, where a similar argument applies to the other patterns. We proceed by induction on n, where the n=1 case is trivial. Consider \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} and note that the elements \tau_{i}=1 and \tau_{j}=2 cannot be premature. Thus, if \tau_{j} occurs before \tau_{i} in \tau, their order will be flipped in \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\tau). Next, we suppose that \tau_{i} occurs before \tau_{j} in \tau. When \tau_{j} enters the stack, all of the elements above \tau_{i} in the stack will be popped. Therefore, \tau_{i} and \tau_{j} will be adjacent in the stack, and thus in \pi=\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\tau). But for permutations \pi such that the element 1 and 2 are adjacent, we can easily check that \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\pi)^{*}=\text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\pi^{*}) and that \pi^{*}\in\text{Av}_{n-1}(132,231) if and only if \pi\in\text{Av}_{n}(132,231). Since \pi^{*} is eventually sorted to \text{Av}_{n-1}(132,231) by repeated applications of \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}, \pi will also eventually be sorted to \text{Av}_{n}(132,231) by applications of \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}. We conclude by induction.
Lastly, consider \tau\in\text{Av}_{n}(132,231). Note that when \tau is sorted by \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}, the stack never triggers. Thus, \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}(\tau)=\text{rev}(\tau), and indeed \text{SC}_{\underline{23}1}^{2}(\tau)=\tau.
∎
Furthermore, the proof of Theorem 4.20 bounds the number of applications of \text{SC}_{\sigma} necessary for \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} to reach a periodic point.
Corollary 4.21.
For n\geq 2, it holds that \text{SC}_{\sigma}^{2n-4}(\tau)\in\text{Av}_{n}(132,231) where \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n} and \sigma\in\{\underline{13}2,\underline{23}1,1\underline{32},2\underline{31}\}.
Proof.
We induct on n, with the n=2 case being apparent. From the proof of Theorem 4.20, the elements 1 and 2 are adjacent in \text{SC}_{\sigma}^{2}(\tau). It follows from \text{SC}_{\sigma}^{2n-6}(\tau^{*})\in\text{Av}_{n-1}(132,231) that \text{SC}_{\sigma}^{2n-4}(\tau)\in\text{Av}_{n}(132,231).
∎
With Lemma 2.1, we extend Theorem 4.20 and Corollary 4.21 to the following.
Corollary 4.22.
For \sigma\in\{\underline{31}2,\underline{21}3,3\underline{12},2\underline{13}\}, the periodic points of \text{SC}_{\sigma} of length n are \text{Av}_{n}(312,213). When n\geq 2, these points have period 2. Furthermore, \text{SC}_{\sigma}^{2n-4}(\tau)\in\text{Av}_{n}(312,213) for each \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}, for n\geq 2.
5. Future Directions
In this work, we introduced the vincular-pattern-avoiding stack-sorting maps \text{SC}_{\sigma} as a generalization of Cerbai et al. [5]’s and Defant and Zheng [11]’s stack-sorting maps. A stack, that avoids \sigma, processes the input permutation \tau in a right-greedy manner. For vincular patterns \sigma of length 3, we studied the sorting classes \text{Sort}_{n}(\text{SC}_{\sigma}), the preimages \text{SC}_{\sigma}^{-1}, and the periodic points of \text{SC}_{\sigma}.

In Sections 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3, we fully characterized the sorting class of \text{SC}_{\sigma} for seven \sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}. In Section 3.4, we discussed whether the five remaining sorting classes formed permutation classes. Lastly, we discussed the periodic points of the maps \text{SC}_{\sigma} in Section 4.2. Another area of future study is to evaluate the tightness of the bounds in Corollary 4.21 and Corollary 4.22. Defant and Zheng [11] showed, for each permutation \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}, that \text{SC}_{132}^{n-1}(\tau)\in\text{Av}_{n}(132,231) and \text{SC}_{312}^{n-1}(\tau)\in\text{Av}_{n}(312,213). Analogous results for the remaining maps \text{SC}_{\sigma} are left as future directions.
