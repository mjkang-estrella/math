57:     Few values of cq (n, r) are known. Here, our concern is the values of c(q) := cq (3, 1).
58: Some work [13, 16, 15] proved bounds for c(q). In Corollary 1.4, we establish c(q) for
59: odd values of q and improve both existing bounds for the even values. In order to do
60: this, we introduce a relation between short coverings, projective spaces and toroidal
61: (chess)boards. The number of rooks needed to cover an n × n toroidal board is well
62: known, clearly n. Some studies on covering and packing of queens in toroidal boards
63: were made by [2] and [1]. We introduce a piece with range between a rook and a
64: queen, as described next.
65:     We will use 1, . . . , n as standard representatives for the classes of Zn . The toroidal
66: n × n board will be modeled by Z2n , with the ﬁrst coordinate indexing the column
67: and the second the row, in such a way that (1, 1) corresponds to the southwestern
68: square and (n, n) to the northeastern square (the orientation is similar to a cartesian
69: plane). The diagonal of (a, b) ∈ Z2n is the set D(a, b) := {(a + t, b + t) : t ∈ Zn }. The
70: vertical and horizontal lines of (a, b) ∈ Z2n are respectively deﬁned by V (a, b) :=
71: {(a, t) : t ∈ Zn } and H(a, b) := {(t, b) : t ∈ Zn }. The semiqueen of (a, b) ∈ Z2n is
72: the set SQ(a, b) := D(a, b) ∪ V (a, b) ∪ H(a, b).
73:     We denote by Dn the toroidal n × n board without the southwest-northeast di-
74: agonal: Dn := Z2n − D(1, 1). We also denote by ξ(n) and ξD (n) the respective sizes
75: of minimum coverings of Z2n and Dn by semiqueens of Z2n . Next we state our main
76: results. The next theorem establishes a relation between the values of c(q) and
77: ξD (q − 1).
