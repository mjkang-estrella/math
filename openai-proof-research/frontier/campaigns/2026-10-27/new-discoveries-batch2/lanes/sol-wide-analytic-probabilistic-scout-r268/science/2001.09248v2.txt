                                                                        1. Basic notions and main result
                                             Linear recurrence relations with various types of coefficients have been studied for more
                                           than a century and appear in different contexts throughout the whole body of mathematics.
                                           A natural univariate set-up in this area is as follows.
                                              Let {Pn (z)} be a polynomial sequence satisfying a finite linear recurrence relation of the
                                           form
                                           (1)          Pn (z) + Q1 (z)Pn−1 (z) + Q2 (z)Pn−2 (z) + · · · + Qk (z)Pn−k (z) = 0,
                                           with polynomials coefficients Q1 (z), . . . , Qk (z) and initial polynomial conditions of the form
                                           P0 (z) = p0 (z), P−1 (z) = p−1 (z), . . . , and P−k+1 (z) = p−k+1 (z).
                                           Problem 1.1. In the above notation, describe the (asymptotic) behavior of the roots for
                                           polynomials in {Pn (z)}.
                                             A major result related to Problem 1.1 has been proven in [1], [2]. It states that in-
                                           dependently of the initial conditions, the sequence {µn } of the root-counting measures
                                           of {Pn (z)} converges in the weak sense to the measure µQ supported on ΓQ , where

(3)                              ΓQ := {z ∈ C | t1 (z) = t2 (z)}.

expression below.
   In the majority of the situations, the roots of Pn (z) only tend to the Beraha-Kahane-
Weiss curve ΓQ when n → ∞ and it is difficult to say something about their location for
finite n. The only general exception from this rule is probably the case when all polynomials
in {Pn (z)} are real-rooted which is often discussed in the literature and important for
applications.
   However in [6] Khang Tran was able to find a non-trivial situation in which the roots
of Pn (z) are not necessarily real and (almost all of them) still lie on the limiting curve
ΓQ for all n. Observe that this property is destroyed by small generic deformations of
coefficients/initial polynomials.

                                      3. Final remarks
1. The most important question related to this note is to find the analog of the inequality
in (5) describing on which part of the real algebraic curve given by (7) the roots of {Pn (z)}
are located and become dense when n → ∞. Some special cases are considered in [4].
2. Choosing an initial k-tuple IN = {P0 (z), . . . , P−k+1 (z)}, find other examples/classes of
pairs (Q, IN ) where (almost all) zeros of {Pn (z)} lie on a fixed curve in the complex plane
which is different from an affine line. One can try to find such examples for relations of
order 4.
3. Theorem A apparently has a multivariate generalization when one considers a multi-
variate polynomial generating function for multinomial coefficients whose indices belong
to a hyperplane given by an equation x1 `1 + x2 `2 + · · · + xu `u = n. It is very tempting
to find this generalization and check whether it leads to further implications related to
Problem 1.1 and its version à la Tran.
