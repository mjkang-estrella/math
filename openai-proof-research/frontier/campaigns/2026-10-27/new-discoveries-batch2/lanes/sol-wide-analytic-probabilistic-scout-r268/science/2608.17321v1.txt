p = ∞, they are the weighted Chebyshev polynomials. The quantities Wp,n are
called the Widom factors. The capacity normalization removes the common expo-

measures the size of the density relative to equilibrium measure, which is the natural
reference measure for the capacity normalization. On one interval, after an affine

   For the unweighted Chebyshev problem on a finite-gap set K ⊂ R, the factors
W∞,n (K, 1) are asymptotic to an almost periodic sequence [11, Theorem 1.8 and
Remark 3 following Theorem 1.6], and need not converge even when K consists
of two intervals [36, p. 128]. The unweighted Chebyshev polynomials on every
finite-gap set also have Szegő–Widom asymptotics [11, Theorem 1.9]. Beyond the
finite-gap setting, strong Szegő–Widom asymptotics for the unweighted Chebyshev
polynomials on regular Parreau–Widom sets satisfying the direct Cauchy theorem
condition were proved in [9, Theorem 1.3].
   For measures of the form above whose regular part K0 is finite-gap, any two
among (S), (B), and the two-sided L2 Widom condition (U2 ) + (L2 ) imply the third
[10, Theorem 4.1]. When K0 is a regular Parreau–Widom set, Christiansen proved
that, under (B), condition (S) is equivalent to (W2 ), and either condition yields
(U2 ) and (L2 ) [8, Theorem 2 and the following corollary]. His result already allows
a finite or denumerable exterior point spectrum. Strong asymptotics for orthogonal
polynomials on homogeneous sets with exterior masses, under an additional condi-
tion on the poles and zeros of the associated Stieltjes function, were proved in [25,
Theorem in Section 6, pp. 144–145].
   The quantitative estimates closest to ours come from several parts of the literature.
For 0 < p < ∞ and without isolated points, [4, Theorem 2.1] gives the fixed-degree
bound Wp,n (K, ν) ≥ S(K, ρ)1/p for dν = ρ dµK + dνs , dνs ⊥ dµK . Its sharpness was
proved in [4, Theorem 2.2], and sharpness on a fixed real compact set was proved in
[5, Theorem 2.2]. On regular Parreau–Widom sets, the weighted Chebyshev upper
bound and the corresponding Szegő theorem were proved in [13, Theorems 3.2 and
3.3]. For the unweighted problem on the semi-regular supports considered here,
Dukes and Zinchenko proved W∞,n (K, 1) ≤ 2ePW(K)+BC(K) for every n ≥ 1 [15,
Theorem 3.3, (3.7), p. 13] and lim inf n→∞ W∞,n (K, 1) ≥ 2eBC(K) [15, Corollary 4.5,
(4.6), p. 17]. Under (P ), uniform boundedness of {W∞,n (K, 1)} is equivalent to
(B) [15, Theorem 5.1, p. 17]. When K0 is an interval, W∞,n (K, 1) → 2eBC(K) [15,
Theorem 5.4, (5.3), p. 18].

  This settles several questions in the literature and strengthens earlier partial con-
verse results. Christiansen, Simon, and Zinchenko asked whether the middle-thirds
Cantor set, which is not Parreau–Widom, could nevertheless have bounded Cheby-
shev Widom factors [11, Remark 3 following Theorem 1.4]. Alpan and Goncharov
asked more generally whether a regular non-Parreau–Widom compact subset of R
can have bounded Chebyshev Widom factors, whether zero Lebesgue measure forces
those factors to be unbounded, and the corresponding questions for the L2 Widom
factors of the equilibrium measure [3, Problem 8]. The Chebyshev question was
posed again in [12, Open Problem 2.2]. The characterization above answers these
questions within the class of regular compact subsets of R.
  There were also partial converse results under additional hypotheses. Under the
assumption that K has a canonical generator, [9, Theorem 1.4] proved that bounded
Chebyshev Widom factors imply the Parreau–Widom condition. The analogous
implication for the L2 Widom factors and the equivalence of the two boundedness

conditions, under the corresponding character-density hypothesis, were obtained
in [1, Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.5]. The characterization above removes these
additional hypotheses for regular compact subsets of R.
   Section 4 examines the sharpness of the preceding implications. Several of its
constructions are valid over broad ranges of p and show that natural converses and
stronger conclusions fail without additional assumptions. For p = 2, the analysis is
complete: every sign pattern not excluded by Theorems 3.1 and 3.5 is realized.
   The elementary and discrete constructions account for eleven of the seventeen sign
patterns not excluded for p = 2. The remaining six are realized by Examples 4.5,
4.6, 4.7(a)–(b), and 4.9(a)–(b). Consequently, for p = 2, no additional implication
among (P ), (B), (S), (U2 ), and (L2 ) can hold in general.

3.3. The four-way theorem.
Corollary 3.6 (Four-way Szegő theorem). Let 2 ≤ p ≤ ∞, and assume the hypothe-
ses of Theorem 3.1. When p = ∞, assume also that w is upper semicontinuous on
K. Then any three of the following four assertions imply the remaining one:
   (P ) K0 is a Parreau–Widom set.
   (B) X = {xj } satisfies the Blaschke condition, BC(K) < ∞.
   (S) The weight w satisfies the Szegő condition, S(K, w) > 0.
  (Wp ) 0 < lim supn→∞ Wp,n (K, µ) < ∞.
Equivalently, if any two of these four assertions hold, then the remaining two asser-
tions are equivalent.
Proof. If (P ), (B), and (S) hold, Corollary 3.2(a) gives (Up ) and (Lp ), and hence
(Wp ). If (P ), (B), and (Wp ) hold but (S) fails, Corollary 3.2(b) gives Wp,n (K, µ) →

Theorem 3.5 gives (P ).                                                               □
