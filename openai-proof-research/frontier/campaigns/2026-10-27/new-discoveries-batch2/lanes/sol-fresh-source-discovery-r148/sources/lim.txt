Evans’s modularity conjectures. These are the explicit descriptions in the next four cases
in k for m+k (p) and mk (p). Based on extensive computations, Ron Evans observes that the
sequences of (the negative of the pure parts of) the moments {m+      k (p)}p for k = 5, 6, 8 and
{mk (p)}p for k = 3, 4, 6 coincide, up to constant powers of p, with the Fourier coefficients of
certain cuspidal Hecke eigenforms of weights k − 2 and k, respectively, at least for p > k.
In addition, the moments {m+       7 (p)}p>7 and {m5 (p)}p>5 are obtained from the symmetric
squares of Hecke eigenforms of weight 3, twisted by constant powers of p and Dirichlet
characters. See [Eva10, §4] where our moments m+       k (p) and mk (p) are denoted by Tk (1, 0)
and Tk (ϕ, 0) = Tk (ϕ, 0, 1), respectively. See Theorem 1.2.2 and also Remarks 3.2.1 and 3.2.6
below for the precise statements in the case mk (p). In the appearance of his paper, this
modularity for m+       +
                   5 , m6 , m3 , m4 has been obtained by various authors as mentioned therein
(see also the note in [Eva10, p.530] for m4 ).
Galois representations. Using ideas from the geometric Langlands program and tools includ-
ing affine Grassmannians and homogeneous Fourier transforms, Yun [Yun15] constructs a
continuous ℓ-adic representation of Gal(Q/Q), for each prime ℓ, which is a subquotient of
the étale cohomology group of a certain projective smooth variety over Q, whose trace of a
geometric Frobenius element at an unramified prime p coincides with (the pure part of) the
negative moment −m+      k (p) for each fixed k. Applying Serre’s modularity conjecture [Ser87],
proved by Khare and Wintenberger [KW09a, KW09b], to such Galois representations, Yun
then proceeds to reprove the modularity for the moments m+                    +
                                                                 5 (p) and m6 (p), confirm Evans’s
modularity conjecture for m+                                 +
                                 7 (p), and reduce that for m8 (p) to a finite calculation, which is
done by Vincent in an appendix of the same paper.
   On the other hand, aiming to understand the shape of the completed L-function attached
to the pure part of the moments m+        k (p) for each k and its functional equation, observed
by Broadhurst and Roberts [Bro17] via intensive numerics, Fresán, Sabbah and Yu [FSY22]
develop a different approach for the arithmetic structures of m+     k (p) originated from a view-
point of (irregular) Hodge theory. The authors obtain an explicit hypersurface K in a torus
whose étale cohomology in the middle degree contains the Galois representation constructed
by Yun, up to semisimplification, as a direct summand. The geometric model is simple
enough to allow the authors to extract detailed information on ramification behaviors at
bad primes for m+   k (p), at least for p > 2. Moreover, by an investigation of the characteris-
tic zero analogues of the Kloosterman sheaf and its symmetric powers, the Hodge numbers
underlying (the motive of) the Galois representation are determined. With the current ad-
vances of automorphic theory, this is sufficient to conclude that the Galois representations
are potentially automorphic.

   The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In §2, we recall the construction of the
Kloosterman sheaf Kl2 on Gm in positive characteristic. We investigate in detail the local
structures of the sheaf Klf2 , which is the pullback of Kl2 by a double cover of Gm , and use
them to obtain the cohomological properties for mk (p) for fixed positive k and odd p. The
approach and the main results in this section are based on the work [FW10] in an essential
way. In §3, we construct the associated Galois representation for mk (p) for fixed k, mainly by
extracting the necessary information from [FSY22], and draw some arithmetic and analytic
consequences from the potential automorphy of the representation. We then prove Theorem
1.2.2 relating mk (p) for k = 3, 4, 5, 6 to modular forms using Serre’s modularity conjecture.

Still, this approach relies on the calculations in [FW10]. In §A.2, we record the relationship
between the twisted Kloosterman sums and hypergeometric type character sums and their
modularity. We record some facts about the associated L-functions in §A.3.
