                                               DIFFERENTIAL POSETS AND SMITH NORMAL FORMS




arXiv:0811.1983v1 [math.CO] 12 Nov 2008

                                                   Abstract. We conjecture a strong property for the up and down maps U
                                                   and D in an r-differential poset: DU + tI and U D + tI have Smith normal
                                                   forms over Z[t]. In particular, this would determine the integral structure of
                                                   the maps U, D, U D, DU , including their ranks in any characteristic.
                                                      As evidence, we prove the conjecture for the Young-Fibonacci lattice YF
                                                   studied by Okada and its r-differential generalizations Z(r), as well as verifying
                                                   many of its consequences for Young’s lattice Y and the r-differential Cartesian
                                                   products Y r .




                                                             1. Introduction and the main conjecture
                                             The focus of this paper is Conjecture 1.1 below. We provide here some back-
                                          ground and motivation before stating it.
                                             For a positive integer r, an r-differential poset is a graded poset P with a min-
                                          imum element, having all intervals and all rank cardinalities finite, satisfying two
                                          crucial extra conditions:
                                            (D1) If an element of P covers m others, then it will be covered by m + r others.
                                            (D2) If two elements of P have exactly m elements that they both cover, then
                                                   there will be exactly m elements that cover them both.
                                          Favorite examples include the 1-differential poset Young’s lattice Y of all number
                                          partitions ordered by inclusion of their Ferrers diagrams, the r-differential Cartesian
                                          product order Y r , and the Fibonacci posets Z(r) discussed in below. See Stanley
                                          [19, 20] for more on differential posets, and Fomin [4, 5] for the more general notion
                                          of dual graded graphs.
                                             There is an important rephrasing of the properties (D1, D2) above. Let Pn
                                          denote the nth rank of P , say with rank size pn := |Pn |. For any ring R, consider
                                          the free R-module Rpn having basis indexed by the elements of Pn , and define the
                                          up and down maps
                                                                                t
                                                                       Un (= Dn+1   ) : Rpn → Rpn+1
                                                                                 t
                                                                          Dn (= Un−1 ) : Rpn → Rpn−1
                                          in which a basis element is sent by Un (resp. Dn ) to the sum, with coefficient 1, of
                                          all elements that cover (resp. are covered by) it. Using the abbreviated notations
                                                                                 U Dn := Un−1 Dn
                                                                                 DUn := Dn+1 Un

                                          graphs.

the conditions (D1, D2) can be rephrased as saying that, for each n ≥ 1,
                                  DUn − U Dn = rI
or equivalently, that

   This turns out to imply that, working over rings R of characteristic zero, Un , Dn
are of maximal rank, via a simple calculation of the eigenvalues and multiplicities
for the nonnegative definite operators DUn , U Dn ; see Proposition 2.1 below. The
main conjecture of this paper, Conjecture 1.1 below, gives further information about
the maps DUn , U Dn (as well as Un , Dn themselves) which is independent of the
ring R.
   For a ring R and an m × n matrix A in Rm×n say that A
      • is in Smith form if it is diagonal in the sense that aij = 0 for i 6= j, and its
         diagonal entries si := aii have si dividing si+1 in R for each i.
      • has a Smith form over R if it can be brought into Smith form by change-
         of-bases in the free modules Rm , Rn , that is, if there exist matrices P in
         GLm (R) and Q in GLn (R) for which P AQ is in Smith form.
      • has a parametrized Smith form for A + tI if A is square and the matrix
         A + tI in R[t]n×n has a Smith form over R[t].
   We will mainly be interested in the case where R = Z. In this situation, since
Z is a principal ideal domain (PID), having a Smith form for a matrix A over R is
guaranteed, but most square matrices A over R will not have a parametrized Smith
form for A + tI, as Z[t] is not a PID. Having such a parametrized Smith form leads
to very strong consequences, including a prediction for the Smith forms over Z for
all specializations A + kI; see the Appendix (Section 8) below.
Conjecture 1.1. In an r-differential poset, for every n ≥ 0, DUn in Zpn ×pn has
a parametrized Smith form for DUn + tI over Z[t].
   Note that due to equation (1.1), it would be equivalent to conjecture that U Dn
when n ≥ 1 has a parametrized Smith form for U Dn +tI, as the ring automorphism
of Z[t] that maps t 7→ t + r would convert one Smith form to the other.
   The paper is structured as follows.
   Section 2 discusses two conjectures (Conjectures 2.3 and 2.4) on r-differential
posets that are closely related to Conjecture 1.1.
   Section 3 discusses some computer experiments checking consequences of Con-
jecture 1.1.
   Section 4 discusses two standard constructions for producing r-differential posets,
and their behavior with respect to the conjectures.
   Section 5 proves all of the conjectures for the Young-Fibonacci poset YF and
its r-differential generalizations Z(r).
   Section 6 discusses Young’s lattice Y and the r-fold Cartesian products Yr .
Although we have not been able to prove Conjecture 1.1 in these cases, some of its
predictions are verified here.
   Section 7 discusses some remarks and questions regarding attempts to generalize
parts of the conjecture to the dual graded graphs of Fomin [4].
   The appendix Section 8 discusses general facts about Smith forms over rings
which are not necessarily PID’s, and the consequences of having a parametrized
Smith form for A + tI.

2.2. On the up and down maps themselves.
Conjecture 2.4. For every n, the matrix Un in Zpn+1 ×pn has only ones and zeroes
in its Smith form over Z, that is, coker(Un : Zpn → Zpn+1 ) is free abelian.
Note that it would be equivalent to conjecture that Dn+1 has free cokernel, or only
ones and zeroes in its Smith form of Z, since Dn+1 = Unt .
Proposition 2.5. Whenever Conjecture 2.3 holds, then Conjecture 1.1 implies
Conjecture 2.4.
Proof. One can be more precise about the relationship: whenever Conjecture 2.3
holds, one has equivalence between the assertion of Conjecture 2.4 and the cor-
rectness of the Smith form over Z for U Dn which is predicted by setting t = 0
in the Smith form over Z[t] for U Dn + tI that follows from Conjecture 1.1 and
Proposition 8.4.
   To see this, recall that Proposition 2.1 allows one to rephrase Conjecture 2.3
as saying that the the zero eigenvalue for U Dn achieves the largest multiplicity
among all of its non-unit eigenvalues. Thus whenever this holds, Proposition 8.4
says that the Smith form over Z for U Dn predicted by Conjecture 1.1 will have
only zeroes and ones as the diagonal entries. This is equivalent to coker(U Dn ) =
Zpn −pn−1 , which by Proposition 8.3 is equivalent to coker(U ) = Zpn −pn−1 , that is,
to Conjecture 2.4.                                                                  

Remark 2.6. Note that Conjecture 2.4 is equivalent (via Proposition 8.1) to saying
that the gcd of all of the maximal minor subdeterminants in Un or Dn+1 is 1.
In fact, many examples exhibit a strictly stronger property: Un has at least one
maximal minor subdeterminant equal to ±1. We suspect that this stronger property
does not hold for all r-differential posets, although we have not yet encountered any
counterexamples

                                   3. Computer tests
   We report here on a few numerical experiments.
   The first author and P. Byrnes used a computer to find all possible partial 1-
differential posets (in the sense described in Section 4.1 below)
      • of rank 8, coming in 643 poset isomorphism classes, and
      • of rank 9, coming in 44605 poset isomorphism classes.
Any of these partial 1-differential posets can be extended to a full 1-differential
poset by Wagner’s construction from the next section, and therefore represent all
the possibilities for the first 8 or 9 ranks.
   The data up through rank 9 exhibits three isomorphism classes where the first
differences of the rank numbers are not weakly increasing; see the remark at the
end of Section 2.1 for the significance. All three of these isomorphism classes classes
have rank sizes
                  (p0 , p1 , p2 , . . . , p9 ) = (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 15, 26, 36)
whose first differences are
                   (∆p1 , ∆p2 , . . . , ∆p9 ) = (0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4, 11, 10)
so that ∆8 = 11 > 10 = ∆9 .
   Regarding Conjecture 1.1, if it were correct, then the Smith form over Z[t] would
have its diagonal Smith entries si (t) given explicitly by (2.1), via Proposition 2.1
and Proposition 8.4. As mentioned in Remark 8.7 of the Appendix, this then
predicts the Smith form over Z (which always exists) for DUn + kI when k is any
chosen integer. For each of the 643 non-isomorphic partial 1-differential posets of
rank 8, considering DUn + kI with n = 0, 1, 2, . . . , 7, this predicted Smith form over
Z was checked to be correct for all integers k in the range [−10, +10]. In addition,
in Young’s lattice Y, the Smith form for DUn (= DUn + 0 · I) was checked to obey
the prediction up through n = 20.

                4. Constructions for r-differential posets
  We recall here two known ways to construct new differential posets from old
ones, and discuss how these constructions respect Conjectures 1.1, 2.3, and 2.4.
4.1. Wagner’s reflection-extension construction. Wagner’s construction pro-
duces an r-differential posets from a partial r-differential poset of some finite rank
(see [19, §6]). Let P be a finite graded poset of rank n with a minimum element,
and assume that for i = 0, 1, . . . , n − 1 one has
                                      DUi − U Di = rI.
One calls P a partial r-differential poset of rank n. Let P + be the poset of rank
n + 1 obtained from P in the following way: for each v ∈ Pn−1 , add a vertex v ∗ of
rank n + 1 to P that covers exactly those x ∈ Pn covering v. Finally, above each
6                     ALEXANDER MILLER AND VICTOR REINER


x ∈ Pn we adjoin r new elements covering only x in P + . It is not hard to see that
iterating this construction produces an r-differential poset.
   An important example of this construction are the posets Z(r) described ex-
plicitly in Section 5 below, but which can also be obtained by applying Wagner’s
construction to the partial poset of rank zero having only one element. As an
example, see Figure 1 for Z(2).
