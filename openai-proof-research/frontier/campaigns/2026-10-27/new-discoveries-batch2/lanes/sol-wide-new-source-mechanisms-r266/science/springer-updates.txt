A set \(A\subseteq V\) satisfying \(\sum \limits _{a\in A}deg (a)\le \left| E\right|\) is an annihilation set ; if, in addition, \(deg \left( v\right) +\sum \limits _{a\in A}deg (a)>\left| E\right|\) , for every vertex \(v\in V(G)-A\) , then A is a maximal annihilation set in G . In Larson and Pepper (Graphs with equal independence and annihilation numbers. Electron J Comb 18:180, 2011) it was conjectured that the following assertions are equivalent: (i) \(\alpha \left( G\right) =a\left( G\right)\) ; (ii) G is a König–Egerváry graph and every maximum independent set is a maximal annihilating set. Recently, it turned out that the implication “ (i) \(\Longrightarrow\) (ii) ” was not true. A series of corresponding counterexamples can be found in Hiller (Counterexamples to the characterisation of graphs with equal independence and annihilation number. arXiv:2202.07529v1 [math.CO], 2022). In Levit and Mandrescu (On an annihilation number conjecture. Ars Math. Contemp. 18, 359–369, 2020), we presented an infinite family of non-bipartite König–Egerváry graphs that invalidate the “ (ii) \(\Longrightarrow\) (i) ” part of this conjecture. In this paper, we provide two more infinite families of counterexamples, one consisting of trees and the other one comprising non-tree bipartite graphs. We also show that the above conjecture is true for trees with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) , disconnected non-bipartite König–Egerváry graphs with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) , and disconnected bipartite graphs with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) excluding the three following counterexamples: \(C_{4}\cup 2K_{2},Domino\cup K_{2}\) and \(K_{3,3}-e\) . Combinatorics Combinatorial Geometry Criticality Graph Theory Reasoning Graph Theory in Probability Combinatorial Structures and Intersection Theorems 1 Introduction Throughout this paper \(G=(V,E)\) is a finite, undirected, loopless graph without multiple edges, with vertex set \(V=V(G)\) of cardinality \(\left| V\left( G\right) \right| =n\left( G\right)\) , and edge set \(E=E(G)\) of size \(\left| E\left( G\right) \right| =m\left( G\right)\) . If \(X\subset V(G)\) , then G [ X ] is the subgraph of G induced by X . By \(G-v\) we mean the subgraph \(G[V(G)-\left\{ v\right\} ]\) , for \(v\in V(G)\) . By \(G-e\) we mean the graph obtained by deleting the edge \(e\in E(G)\) , while \(G+e\) is the graph obtained by adding an extra edge e to E ( G ). \(K_{n},K_{m,n},P_{n},C_{n}\) denote respectively, the complete graph on \(n\ge 1\) vertices, the complete bipartite graph on \(m,n\ge 1\) vertices, the path on \(n\ge 1\) vertices, and the cycle on \(n\ge 3\) vertices, respectively. The disjoint union of graphs \(G_{1},G_{2}\) is the graph \(G_{1}\cup G_{2}\) having the disjoint union of \(V(G_{1}),V(G_{2})\) as a vertex set, and the disjoint union of \(E(G_{1}),E(G_{2})\) as an edge set. In particular, nG denotes the disjoint union of \(n>1\) copies of the graph G . A set \(S\subseteq V(G)\) is independent if no two vertices from S are adjacent, and by \(\mathrm {Ind}(G)\) we mean the family of all independent sets of G . An independent set of maximum size is a maximum independent set of G , and \(\alpha (G)=\max \{\left| S\right| :S\in \mathrm {Ind}(G)\}\) . Let \(\Omega (G)\) denote the family of all maximum independent sets. A matching in a graph G is a set of edges \(M\subseteq E(G)\) such that no two edges of M share a common vertex. A matching of maximum cardinality \(\mu (G)\) is a maximum matching , and a perfect matching is one saturating all vertices of G . It is known that \(\lfloor n\left( G\right) /2\rfloor +1\le \alpha (G)+\mu (G)\le n\left( G\right)\) holds for every graph G . If \(\alpha (G)+\mu (G)=n\left( G\right)\) , then G is called a König–Egerváry graph [ 10 , 36 ]. For instance, each bipartite graph is a König–Egerváry graph [ 12 , 20 ]. The origin of König–Egerváry graphs can be found in the papers of König [ 20 ] and Egerváry [ 12 ], and substantial advancements in describing these graphs were first done by Deming [ 10 ], Sterboul [ 36 ], Korach [ 21 ], Lovász [ 30 ], Lovász and Plummer [ 31 ], Bourjolly and Pulleyblank [ 4 ]. Some more properties of König–Egerváry graphs can be found in [ 3 , 22 , 23 , 25 , 26 , 27 , 29 , 35 ] . It is well known that computation of the independence number of a graph is NP -hard, while computation of its matching number is polynomial. It turns out that for König–Egerváry graphs, the computation of their independence numbers can be done in polynomial time. The problem of recognizing König–Egerváry graphs is polynomial as proved by Deming [ 10 ], of complexity \(O(n\left( G\right) \cdot m\left( G\right) )\) . Gavril [ 13 ], has described a recognition algorithm for König–Egerváry graphs of complexity \(O(n\left( G\right) +m\left( G\right) )\) . For \(X\subseteq V(G)\) , the number \(\left| X\right| -\left| N(X)\right|\) is the difference of X , denoted d ( X ). The critical difference d ( G ) is \(\max \{d(X):X\subseteq V(G)\}\) . The number \(\max \{d(I):I\in \mathrm {Ind}(G)\}\) is the critical independence difference of G , denoted id ( G ). Clearly, \(d(G)\ge id(G)\) . It was shown in [ 39 ] that \(d(G)=id(G)\) holds for every graph G . If A is an independent set in G with \(d\left( X\right) =d(G)\) , then A is a critical independent set . A maximum critical independent set is a critical independent set of maximum cardinality (denoted \(\alpha ^{\prime }\left( G\right)\) ). Theorem 1.1 [ 23 , 25 ] For a graph G the following assertions are equivalent (i) G is a König–Egerváry graph; (ii) some maximum independent set is critical, i.e., \(\alpha ^{\prime }\left( G\right) =\alpha \left( G\right)\) ; (iii) every maximum independent sets is critical. Let \(d_{1}\le d_{2}\le \cdots \le d_{n}\) be the degree sequence of a graph G . Pepper [ 33 , 34 ] defined the annihilation number of G , denoted \(a\left( G\right)\) , to be precisely the largest integer k such that \(\sum \nolimits _{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\le m\left( G\right)\) . This definition is closely related to the Havel-Hakimi algorithm solving the graph realization problem [ 15 , 16 ]. For \(A\subseteq V(G)\) , let \(deg (A)=\sum \nolimits _{v\in A}deg (v)\) . Every \(A\subseteq V\left( G\right)\) satisfying \(deg (A)\le m\left( G\right)\) is an annihilating set . Clearly, every independent set is annihilating. An annihilating set A is maximal if \(deg \left( A\cup \{v\}\right) >m\left( G\right)\) , for every vertex \(v\in V(G)-A\) , and it is maximum if \(\left| A\right| =a\left( G\right)\) [ 33 ]. For example, if \(G=K_{p,q}=(A,B,E)\) and \(p>q\) , then A is a maximum annihilating set, while B is a maximal annihilating set. One can easily infer that every maximum annihilating set is also a maximal annihilating set, since if the sum of the \(a+1\) smallest entries from the degree sequence \(D=(d_{1}\le d_{2}\le \cdots \le d_{n})\) is greater than m ( G ), then the same is true for every \(a+1\) entries of D . There is a number of relations between the annihilation number and various domination parameters of a graph: the domination number [ 6 , 14 ], the total domination number [ 5 , 11 , 37 ], the double domination number [ 1 ], the 2-domination number [ 19 , 38 ], the Roman domination number [ 2 ], the rainbow domination number [ 7 ], the paired-domination number [ 8 ], the locating-total domination number [ 32 ]. For instance, in [ 9 , 11 ], it was conjectured that \(\gamma _{t}(G)\le a(G)+1\) holds for every connected graph G , where \(\gamma _{t}(G)\) denotes the total domination number of G , i.e., the size of a smallest subset \(D\subseteq V(G)\) such that \(\left| D\cap N\left[ v\right] \right| \ge 1\) for every \(v\in V\left( G\right)\) . In [ 5 ] the conjecture was validated for cactus graphs and block graphs. Further, the inequality was proved for more general graph families, namely, for so-called tree-like graphs [ 37 ]. Theorem 1.1 implies the following. Corollary 1.2 Let G be a König–Egerváry graph. Then \(\alpha (G)=a(G)\) if and only if \(\alpha ^{\prime }\left( G\right) =a(G)\) , i.e., each maximum (critical) independent set is a maximum annihilating set. Recall that a vertex-cover of a graph G is a subset of vertices \(W\subseteq V\left( G\right)\) such that \(W\cap \left\{ u,v\right\} \ne \emptyset\) holds for every \(uv\in E\left( G\right)\) . Proof Let A be an independent vertex cover for a graph G . Since A is independent, we get that \(deg \left( A\right) =m\left( G\right)\) . For instance, \(a\left( C_{5}\right) =\alpha \left( C_{5}\right) =\left\lfloor \frac{n\left( C_{5}\right) }{2}\right\rfloor\) , \(a\left( \overline{P_{5}}\right) =3>\alpha \left( \overline{P_{5}}\right) =\left\lfloor \frac{n\left( \overline{P_{5}}\right) }{2}\right\rfloor\) , \(a\left( K_{1,3}\right) =\alpha \left( K_{1,3}\right) >\left\lfloor \frac{n\left( K_{1,3}\right) }{2}\right\rfloor\) , while \(a\left( \overline{C_{6}}\right) =\left\lfloor \frac{n\left( \overline{C_{6}}\right) }{2}\right\rfloor >\alpha \left( \overline{C_{6}}\right)\) . Theorem 1.5 [ 24 ] Let G be a graph with \(a\left( G\right) \ge \frac{n\left( G\right) }{2}\) . If G is a König–Egerváry graph and every \(S\in \Omega (G)\) is a maximum annihilating set, then \(\alpha \left( G\right) =a\left( G\right)\) . Recently, there was found a series of counterexamples showing that the converse of Theorem 1.5 is not true [ 17 ]. However, for some specific families of graphs the converse still holds [ 17 ]. For instance, by Corollary 1.2 , it is true for König–Egerváry graphs. Conjecture 1.6 [ 24 ] Let G be a graph with \(a\left( G\right) \ge \frac{n\left( G\right) }{2}\) . Then the following assertions are equivalent: (i) \(\alpha \left( G\right) =a\left( G\right)\) ; (ii) G is a König–Egerváry graph and every \(S\in \Omega (G)\) is a maximal annihilating set. In [ 17 ], the “ (i) \(\Longrightarrow\) (ii) ” part of Conjecture 1.6 was invalidated, by providing non-König–Egerváry counterexamples. On the other hand, Conjecture 1.6 is true for König–Egerváry graphs with \(\alpha \left( G\right) \le 3\) [ 28 ]. In [ 28 ] we invalidated the “ (ii) \(\Longrightarrow\) (i) ” part of Conjecture 1.6 , by presenting an infinite family of non-bipartite König–Egerváry counterexamples for every \(\alpha \left( G\right) \ge 4\) . In this paper we provide some generic counterexamples for the “ (ii) \(\Longrightarrow\) (i) ” part of Conjecture 1.6 , including: trees for each \(\alpha \left( G\right) \ge 5\) , bipartite graphs that are not trees for every \(\alpha \left( G\right) \ge 4\) , disconnected graphs for each \(\alpha \left( G\right) \ge 5\) . In addition, we validate Conjecture 1.6 for trees with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) and disconnected non-bipartite König–Egerváry graphs with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) . Case 2 . \(G=H_{1}\cup H_{2}\) , where \(H_{1},H_{2}\) are connected König–Egerváry graphs with \(\alpha (H_{1})=\alpha (H_{2})=2\) . Only \(G\in\) \(\left\{ 2P_{4},P_{4}\cup \left( K_{3}+e\right) ,P_{4} \cup \left( K_{4}-e\right) ,2\left( K_{3}+e\right) ,\left( K_{3}+e\right) \cup \left( K_{4}-e\right) \right\}\) satisfies \(a(G)=5\) (see Fig. 10 ). It is easy to see that: 1. \(2P_{4}\) has a maximum independent set S containing all the leaves, and S is not maximal annihilating; 2. \(P_{4}\cup \left( K_{3}+e\right)\) has \(\left\{ u_{2},u_{4} ,y_{2},y_{4}\right\}\) as a maximum independent set that is not maximal annihilating; 3. \(P_{4}\cup \left( K_{4}-e\right)\) has \(\left\{ u_{2},u_{4} ,z_{2},z_{4}\right\}\) as a maximum independent set that is not maximal annihilating; 4. \(2\left( K_{3}+e\right)\) has \(deg(S)=6\) for every maximum independent set S , which means that none of them is maximal annihilating; 5. \(\left( K_{3}+e\right) \cup \left( K_{4}-e\right)\) has \(\left\{ y_{2},y_{4},z_{2},z_{4}\right\}\) as a maximum independent set that is not maximal annihilating. In summary, there are no counterexamples in Case 2. Case 3 . \(G=H\cup2 K_{2}\) , where H is a connected König–Egerváry graph \(\alpha \left( H\right) =2\) . Only \(G\in\) \(\left\{ P_{4}\cup 2K_{2},C_{4}\cup 2K_{2},\left( K_{3}+e\right) \cup 2K_{2},\left( K_{4}-e\right) \cup 2K_{2}\right\}\) satisfies \(a(G)=5\) (consider Fig. 10 ). It is easy to see that: 1. \(P_{4}\cup 2K_{2}\) has a maximum independent set S including all the leaves, and S is not maximal annihilating; 2. \(C_{4}\cup 2K_{2}\) is a counterexample. The graph \(C_{4}\cup 2K_{2}\) has \(m(C_{4}\cup 2K_{2})=6\) and the degree sequence \(\left( 1,1,1,1,2,2,2,2\right)\) . Hence, \(a(C_{4}\cup 2K_{2})>\alpha (C_{4}\cup 2K_{2} )\) . In addition, every maximum independent set S has \(deg(S)=6\) , which means that S is maximal annihilating as well. Thus, Conjecture 1.6 fails for \(C_{4}\cup 2K_{2}\) , which is bipartite. 3. \(\left( K_{3}+e\right) \cup 2K_{2}\) has \(\left\{ v_{2},v_{4} ,y_{2},y_{4}\right\}\) as a maximum independent set that is not maximal annihilating; 4. \(\left( K_{4}-e\right) \cup 2K_{2}\) has \(\left\{ v_{2},v_{4} ,z_{2},z_{4}\right\}\) as a maximum independent set that is not maximal annihilating. In summary, there is only one counterexample, in Case 3, namely, \(C_{4} \cup 2K_{2}\) . Case 4 . \(G=4K_{2}\) . It is not a counterexample, because \(a(G)=4\) . \(\square\) Finally, taking into account Corollary 4.2 and Proposition 4.5 , one can conclude that Conjecture 1.6 is true for trees with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) , disconnected non-bipartite König–Egerváry graphs with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) , and disconnected bipartite graphs with \(\alpha \left( G\right) =4\) but \(C_{4} \cup 2K_{2}\) , \(Domino\cup K_{2}\) , \(\left( K_{3,3}-e\right) \cup K_{2}\) . 5 Conclusions Problem 5.1 Characterize (bipartite) graphs whose all maximum independent sets are maximal annihilating. If \(V\left( G\right) -S\) is an independent set for some \(S\in \Omega (G)\) , then G is bipartite. Problem 5.2 Characterize bipartite graphs such that \(V\left( G\right) -S\) is independent for every \(S\in \Omega (G)\) . In other words, every vertex cover is independent. An immediate example of a graph with only independent vertex covers is a strong unique maximum independence graph (see Theorem 2.1 due to [ 18 ]). By Lemma 1.3 , every solution of Problem 5.2 is a solution of Problem 5.1 as well. If G is either a path graph or a regular graph, then there exists a maximum independent set comprising vertices with smallest \(\alpha (G)\) degrees in the degree sequence of G . Problem 5.3 Characterize graphs G whose first \(\alpha (G)\) degrees \(d_{1}\le d_{2} \le \cdots \le d_{\alpha (G)}\) correspond to a maximum independent set. One of the ideas that we used in order to validate or invalidate Conjecture 1.6 was to find a maximum independent set having the minimum sum of degrees possible. Clearly, it is NP -hard for general graphs. This motivates the following. Problem 5.4 Given a König–Egerváry graph G , compute \(\min \{deg (S):S\in \Omega (G)\}\) .
