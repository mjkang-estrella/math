Abstract
    

Let VV be a finite-dimensional real vector space. A collection 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} of pairs of subspaces of VV is called a skew Bollobás system if dim(Ai∩Bi)=0\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{i})=0 for each i∈[m]i\in[m] and
dim(Ai∩Bj)>0\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{j})>0 for all 1≤i<j≤m1\leq i<j\leq m.
Assume that V=V(1)⊕⋯⊕V(r)V=V^{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{(r)} and 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} is a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of VV satisfying Ai=⨁k=1r(Ai∩V(k))A_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(A_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) and Bi=⨁k=1r(Bi∩V(k))B_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(B_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) for each i∈[m]i\in[m]. Denote ai,k=dim(Ai∩V(k))a_{i,k}=\dim(A_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) and bi,k=dim(Bi∩V(k))b_{i,k}=\dim(B_{i}\cap V^{(k)}). Suppose that a1,k≤⋯≤am,ka_{1,k}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m,k} and b1,k≥⋯≥bm,kb_{1,k}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m,k} for each k∈[r]k\in[r]. Using the exterior algebraic method developed by Lovász and Scott–Wilmer, we prove that
    




∑i=1m1∏k=1r(ai,k+bi,kai,k)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}}\leq 1.


    

This generalizes the results of Alon (JCTA, 1985) and Scott–Wilmer (JLMS, 2021) to multipart weighted setting.
Secondly, we solve a conjecture of Hegedüs (AJC, 2015) concerning projective subspaces, showing that any skew Bollobás system of projective subspaces in an nn-dimensional projective space contains at most 2n+1−22^{n+1}-2 pairs.
Thirdly, we prove that
if 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} is a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of VV with ai=dim(Ai)a_{i}=\dim(A_{i}) and bi=dim(Bi)b_{i}=\dim(B_{i}), then
    




∑i=1m1(ai+bi+1)​(ai+biai)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{(a_{i}+b_{i}+1)\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.


    

This gives an extension to the subspace setting of the results of Hegedüs–Frankl (EUJC, 2024) and Yue (DM, 2026).
Finally, we extend the above inequality to systems of dd-tuples of subspaces, giving a unified bound that implies the corresponding results for dd-tuples of subsets.

1 Introduction




For two integers a≤na\leq n, we denote [a,n]={a,a+1,…,n}[a,n]=\{a,a+1,\ldots,n\} and [n]=[1,n][n]=[1,n]. For r∈[0,n]r\in[0,n], let ([n]r)={A⊆[n]:|A|=r}\binom{[n]}{r}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|=r\}.
A collection 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} of pairs of subsets of [n][n] is called a Bollobás system if it satisfies
Ai∩Bi=∅​ for every ​i∈[m]A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset\text{ for every }i\in[m],
and
Ai∩Bj≠∅​ for all ​1≤i≠j≤m.A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset\text{ for all }1\leq i\neq j\leq m.
In 1965, Bollobás [4] proved the following celebrated result, known as the Two Families Theorem.



Theorem 1.1 (Bollobás [4]).



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n]. Suppose that |Ai|=a|A_{i}|=a and |Bi|=b|B_{i}|=b for every i∈[m]i\in[m]. Then m≤(a+ba)m\leq{a+b\choose a}.





The first standard approach to proving Theorem 1.1 utilizes the combinatorial technique: the inductive argument [4], the counting argument [14] and the probabilistic method [2, page 11]. The original proof [4] reveals a weighted version for pairs of non-uniform families; see [5, page 66].



Theorem 1.2 (Weighted version).



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n]. Suppose that |Ai|=ai|A_{i}|=a_{i} and |Bi|=bi|B_{i}|=b_{i} for every i∈[m]i\in[m]. Then




∑i=1m1(ai+biai)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.







The second approach to proving Theorem 1.1 was introduced by Lovász [16], who used the anti-symmetric tensor product from exterior algebra method. This elegant algebraic method is also valid under the weaker assumption, saying that Ai∩Bj≠∅A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset for all i<ji<j, instead of for all i≠ji\neq j; see [3, Theorems 5.6 and 6.12] for detailed arguments.
In the set-theoretic context, a system 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} satisfying this weak assumption together with Ai∩Bi=∅A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset for all i∈[m]i\in[m] is termed a skew Bollobás system.
Moreover, this algebraic method also gives a natural extension to the system of subspaces of a finite dimensional vector space.
This observation was also illustrated by Frankl [7] using the symmetric tensor product. We refer to [8, page 167].




Let VV be a real vector space with dimension nn.
A collection 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} of pairs of subspaces of VV is called a skew Bollobás system if
dim(Ai∩Bi)=0​ for every ​i∈[m]\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{i})=0\text{ for every }i\in[m];
and
dim(Ai∩Bj)>0​ for all ​1≤i<j≤m.\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{j})>0\text{ for all }1\leq i<j\leq m. In fact,
the algebraic approaches of Lovász [16] and Frankl [7] directly imply the following subspace extension of Theorem 1.1.



Theorem 1.3 (Skew version, Lovász [16]).



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of V≅ℝnV\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}. Suppose that dim(Ai)=a\dim(A_{i})=a and dim(Bi)=b\dim(B_{i})=b for every i∈[m]i\in[m]. Then




m≤(a+ba).m\leq\binom{a+b}{a}.







Using a standard construction, mapping a set to a subspace, we see that the above space system version implies the set system version in Theorem 1.1.
Indeed, suppose that 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} is a Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n] with |Ai|=a|A_{i}|=a and |Bi|=b|B_{i}|=b for every i∈[m]i\in[m]. Let {e1,…,en}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\} be the standard basis of ℝn\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Mapping each set AiA_{i} to the subspace Ai′=span​{ep:p∈Ai}⊆ℝnA_{i}^{\prime}=\text{span}\{e_{p}:p\in A_{i}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n} and each BiB_{i} to the subspace Bi′=span​{eq:q∈Bi}⊆ℝnB_{i}^{\prime}=\text{span}\{e_{q}:q\in B_{i}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}, we see that
𝒫′={(Ai′,Bi′)}i=1m\mathcal{P}^{\prime}=\{(A_{i}^{\prime},B_{i}^{\prime})\}_{i=1}^{m} is a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of ℝn\mathbb{R}^{n} with
dim(Ai′)=a\dim(A_{i}^{\prime})=a and dim(Bi′)=b\dim(B_{i}^{\prime})=b. Thus, the inequality of Theorem 1.3 holds for 𝒫′\mathcal{P}^{\prime}, and it coincides with the bound of Theorem 1.1 for 𝒫\mathcal{P}.




Motivated by Theorem 1.3, it is natural to extend the weighted version in Theorem 1.2 to the skew version for pairs of vector subspaces.
Unfortunately, Babai and Frankl [3, Exercise 5.1.1] showed that the skew version of Theorem 1.2 does not hold in general.
By developing the exterior algebraic method, Scott and Wilmer [18] achieved the first breakthrough by establishing a skew version of Theorem 1.2 in a more general vector space setting under a suitable condition.



Theorem 1.4 (Weighted skew version, Scott and Wilmer [18]).



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of V≅ℝnV\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}. Suppose that dim(Ai)=ai\dim(A_{i})=a_{i} and dim(Bi)=bi\dim(B_{i})=b_{i} for every i∈[m]i\in[m], and that the sequences satisfy a1≤a2≤⋯≤ama_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m} and b1≥b2≥⋯≥bmb_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m}. Then




∑i=1m1(ai+biai)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.







For more related variations, we refer to [1, 9, 10, 19, 12, 20, 13, 15] and references therein. At the last paragraph in [18, Section 6], Scott and Wilmer remarked that “Can any of these variations be further addressed with exterior algebra methods?” In this paper, we provide positive evidence for supporting Scott–Wilmer’s question by establishing several new weighted skew Bollobás-type inequalities for systems of vector spaces.
Using the exterior algebra method, we first show a unified extension of the results of Alon [1] and Scott–Wilmer [18]; see the forthcoming Section 1.1. Moreover, we shall prove some other related results for weighted skew version using different methods.




1.1 Weighted inequalities for multipart structures




The classical Two Families Theorem and its variants typically treat the ground set or vector space as a single entity. A significant step toward a more structured setting was taken by Alon [1], who considered the case where the ground set is partitioned into several disjoint blocks.



Theorem 1.5 (Alon [1]).



Let [n][n] be the disjoint union of some sets X1,…,XrX_{1},\ldots,X_{r}.
Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n]. Suppose that |Ai∩Xk|=ak|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|=a_{k} and |Bi∩Xk|=bk|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|=b_{k} for every i∈[m]i\in[m] and k∈[r]k\in[r]. Then




m≤∏k=1r(ak+bkak).m\leq\prod_{k=1}^{r}{a_{k}+b_{k}\choose a_{k}}.







Alon’s result requires the intersection sizes ak,bka_{k},b_{k} to be constant across all pairs. A natural question is whether this uniformity condition can be relaxed to allow varying sizes while still obtaining a meaningful bound.
We answer this question by proving a weighted extension in the more general setting of vector spaces. The first main result of this paper generalizes Theorems 1.4 and 1.5.



Theorem 1.6.



Let V=V(1)⊕V(2)⊕⋯⊕V(r)V=V^{(1)}\oplus V^{(2)}\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{(r)} be a finite-dimensional real vector space, and let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of VV satisfying Ai=⨁k=1r(Ai∩V(k))A_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(A_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) and Bi=⨁k=1r(Bi∩V(k))B_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(B_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) for each i∈[m]i\in[m]. Denote
ai,k=dim(Ai∩V(k))a_{i,k}=\dim(A_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) and bi,k=dim(Bi∩V(k))b_{i,k}=\dim(B_{i}\cap V^{(k)}).
Assume that a1,k≤a2,k≤⋯≤am,ka_{1,k}\leq a_{2,k}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m,k} and b1,k≥b2,k≥⋯≥bm,kb_{1,k}\geq b_{2,k}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m,k} for each k∈[r]k\in[r].
Then




∑i=1m1∏k=1r(ai,k+bi,kai,k)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}}\leq 1.







Theorem 1.6 recovers Theorem 1.4 by setting r=1r=1. Moreover, Theorem 1.6 implies the following uniform case, which generalizes both Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.5.



Corollary 1.7.



Let V=V(1)⊕V(2)⊕⋯⊕V(r)V=V^{(1)}\oplus V^{(2)}\oplus\cdots\oplus V^{(r)} be a finite-dimensional real vector space, and let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of VV satisfying Ai=⨁k=1r(Ai∩V(k))A_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(A_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) and Bi=⨁k=1r(Bi∩V(k))B_{i}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r}(B_{i}\cap V^{(k)}) for each i∈[m]i\in[m]. Suppose that
dim(Ai∩V(k))=ak\dim(A_{i}\cap V^{(k)})=a_{k} and dim(Bi∩V(k))=bk\dim(B_{i}\cap V^{(k)})=b_{k} for every i∈[m]i\in[m] and k∈[r]k\in[r].
Then




m≤∏k=1r(ak+bkak).m\leq\prod_{k=1}^{r}{a_{k}+b_{k}\choose a_{k}}.







Theorem 1.6 yields the following result for set systems via a standard construction.



Corollary 1.8.



Let [n][n] be the disjoint union of some sets X1,…,XrX_{1},\ldots,X_{r}.
Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n]. Suppose that |Ai∩Xk|=ai,k|A_{i}\cap X_{k}|=a_{i,k} and |Bi∩Xk|=bi,k|B_{i}\cap X_{k}|=b_{i,k} for every i∈[m]i\in[m] and k∈[r]k\in[r], and that for each k∈[r]k\in[r],
a1,k≤a2,k≤⋯≤am,ka_{1,k}\leq a_{2,k}\leq\cdots\leq a_{m,k} and b1,k≥b2,k≥⋯≥bm,k.b_{1,k}\geq b_{2,k}\geq\cdots\geq b_{m,k}.
Then




∑i=1m1∏k=1r(ai,k+bi,kai,k)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{r}\binom{a_{i,k}+b_{i,k}}{a_{i,k}}}\leq 1.








1.2 Projective subspaces and Hegedüs’ conjecture




Let 𝔽\mathbb{F} be an arbitrary field and let VV be an (n+1)(n+1)‑dimensional vector space over 𝔽\mathbb{F} with n≥0n\geq 0.
The projective space of dimension nn is defined as
ℙ(V)=(V∖{0})/∼\mathbb{P}(V)=\bigl(V\setminus\{0\}\bigr)/\sim, where the equivalence relation
u∼vu\sim v if and only if ∃λ∈𝔽∖{0}​such that​u=λ​v.\exists\ \lambda\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}\ \text{such that}\ u=\lambda v.
An element [v]∈ℙ⁡(V)[v]\in\mathbb{P}(V) is called a projective point, which corresponds to the one‑dimensional subspace span​{v}\mathrm{span}\{v\}.
A subset A⊆ℙ⁡(V)A\subseteq\mathbb{P}(V) is called a projective subspace if there exists a subspace U⊆VU\subseteq V, U≠{0}U\neq\{0\}, such that
A=ℙ⁡(U)={[u]:u∈U∖{0}}.A=\mathbb{P}(U)=\{[u]:u\in U\setminus\{0\}\}.
In 2015, Hegedüs [11] proposed the following conjecture for projective subspace.



Conjecture 1.9 (Hegedüs [11]).



Let WW be an nn-dimensional projective space over an arbitrary field 𝔽\mathbb{F} and let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of projective subspaces of WW, i.e., Ai∩Bi=∅​ for every ​i∈[m]A_{i}\cap B_{i}=\emptyset\text{ for every }i\in[m];
and
Ai∩Bj≠∅​ for all ​1≤i<j≤mA_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset\text{ for all }1\leq i<j\leq m. Then




m≤2n+1−2.m\leq 2^{\,n+1}-2.







The term skew Bollobás system is the same as the classical set-pair theorem. Here the objects are projective subspaces and the intersection conditions are imposed in the projective sense.
The second main result of this paper is an affirmative solution of Hegedüs’s conjecture.



Theorem 1.10.



Let WW be an nn-dimensional projective space over an arbitrary field 𝔽\mathbb{F} and let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of projective subspaces of WW. Then




m≤2n+1−2.m\leq 2^{\,n+1}-2.








1.3 Weighted inequalities for subspace pairs




The weighted version in Theorem 1.2 does not hold under the weaker skew intersection condition, since Babai and Frankl [3, Exercise 5.1.1] provided an example by considering all pairs (A,[n]∖A)(A,[n]\setminus A), where AA runs over all subsets of [n][n], arranged in decreasing order of their size. Then 𝒫={(Ai,[n]∖Ai)}i=12n\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},[n]\setminus A_{i})\}_{i=1}^{2^{n}} forms a skew Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n], and ∑i=12n1/(ai+biai)=n+1\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}1/{a_{i}+b_{i}\choose a_{i}}=n+1.




Recently, Hegedüs and Frankl [12] provided an interesting variant of Theorem 1.2 by showing that if 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} is a skew Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n], then




∑i=1m1(ai+biai)≤n+1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq n+1.

(1)


Using a probabilistic method, Yue [20] removed the explicit dependence on nn.



Theorem 1.11 (Yue [20]).



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subsets of [n][n]. Suppose that |Ai|=ai|A_{i}|=a_{i} and |Bi|=bi|B_{i}|=b_{i} for every i∈[m]i\in[m]. Then




∑i=1m1(ai+bi+1)​(ai+biai)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\bigl(a_{i}+b_{i}+1\bigr)\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.







As the third result of this paper, we extend Theorem 1.11 to the following setting for subspaces, which substantially removes the monotonicity condition of Theorem 1.4.



Theorem 1.12.



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás system of subspaces of V≅ℝnV\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}. Suppose that dim(Ai)=ai\dim(A_{i})=a_{i} and dim(Bi)=bi\dim(B_{i})=b_{i} for every i∈[m]i\in[m]. Then




∑i=1m1(ai+bi+1)​(ai+biai)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\bigl(a_{i}+b_{i}+1\bigr)\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}}{a_{i}}}\leq 1.







By the standard construction of mapping sets to subspaces, Theorem 1.12 implies Theorem 1.11.
Our proof in the subspace version is motivated by the set version of Hegedüs and Frankl [12], where we reduce the present problem to the previous Lovász theorem by a clever argument on maximality assumption.
This is different from the probabilistic proof of Theorem 1.11.




In 1984, Füredi [9] employed so-called general position arguments to generalize Theorem 1.3 to the tt-intersecting setting.
Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a collection of pairs of subspaces of VV. For a non-negative integer tt, we say that
𝒫\mathcal{P} is a skew Bollobás tt-system if
dim(Ai∩Bi)≤t​ for any ​i∈[m]\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{i})\leq t\text{ for any }i\in[m] and
dim(Ai∩Bj)>t​ for any ​1≤i<j≤m\dim(A_{i}\cap B_{j})>t\text{ for any }1\leq i<j\leq m.
The case t=0t=0 reduces to the ordinary skew Bollobás system. We refer to [18, 19] for related results.
Using Füredi’s general position arguments [9], we extend Theorem 1.12 to this tt-intersecting setting.



Corollary 1.13.



Let 𝒫={(Ai,Bi)}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i},B_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a skew Bollobás tt-system of subspaces of V≅ℝnV\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}. Suppose that dim(Ai)=ai\dim(A_{i})=a_{i} and dim(Bi)=bi\dim(B_{i})=b_{i} for every i∈[m]i\in[m], and that am,b1≥ta_{m},b_{1}\geq t. Then




∑i=1m1(ai+bi−2​t+1)​(ai+bi−2​tai−t)≤1.\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\bigl(a_{i}+b_{i}-2t+1\bigr)\binom{a_{i}+b_{i}-2t}{a_{i}-t}}\leq 1.







Setting t=0t=0 in Corollary 1.13 reduces to Theorem 1.12.
Moreover, the condition am,b1≥ta_{m},b_{1}\geq t is natural in this setting, as it ensures that the binomial coefficients are well-defined.





1.4 Weighted inequalities for dd-tuples of subspaces




The Bollobás two families theorem admits a natural extension. Let d≥2d\geq 2 and 𝒫={(Ai(1),…,Ai(d))}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\dots,A_{i}^{(d)})\}_{i=1}^{m} be a collection of dd-tuples of subsets of [n][n]. We say that 𝒫\mathcal{P} is a Bollobás system if


(i) 



Ai(p)∩Ai(q)=∅​ for any ​i∈[m]​ and ​1≤p<q≤dA_{i}^{(p)}\cap A_{i}^{(q)}=\emptyset\text{ for any }i\in[m]\text{ and }1\leq p<q\leq d; and


(ii) 



for any 1≤i≠j≤m1\leq i\neq j\leq m, there exist 1≤p<q≤d1\leq p<q\leq d such that
Ai(p)∩Aj(q)≠∅A_{i}^{(p)}\cap A_{j}^{(q)}\neq\emptyset.



Moreover, 𝒫\mathcal{P} is called a skew Bollobás system
if the above condition i≠ji\neq j is replaced by i<ji<j.
For d=2d=2, these definitions reduce to the classical set-pair systems. For non-negative integers a1,…,ada_{1},\ldots,a_{d} and n≥a1+⋯+adn\geq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}, the multinomial coefficient is denoted by
(na1,…,ad):=n!a1!⋯ad!(n−a1−⋯−ad)!\binom{n}{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}}:=\frac{n!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{d}!(n-a_{1}-\cdots-a_{d})!}.
Extending (1),
Hegedüs and Frankl [12] established the tight bound




∑i=1m1(ai(1)+⋯+ai(d)ai(1),…,ai(d))≤(n+d−1d−1).\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\displaystyle\binom{a_{i}^{(1)}+\cdots+a_{i}^{(d)}}{a_{i}^{(1)},\dots,a_{i}^{(d)}}}\leq\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}.



The following theorem extends Theorem 1.11 to a skew Bollobás system of dd-tuples of subsets.



Theorem 1.14 (Yue [21]).

Our approaches.




For Theorems 1.6 and 1.10,
we employ the exterior algebra approach of Lovász [16], Scott and Wilmer [18]. The multipart structure in Theorem 1.6 demands a refined analysis, as projection dimensions in different components must be controlled simultaneously. We overcome this by a component‑wise induction that carefully interleaves projections and wedge products, tracking dimensions separately in each block and combining them via a product formula.
Theorem 1.10 is proved by associating each projective subspace with a corresponding non-trivial vector subspace and then applying a linear independence argument in the exterior algebra over an arbitrary field.




The proofs of Theorems 1.12 and 1.15 develop the approach introduced by Hegedüs and Frankl [12], modifing it in two essential aspects. Firstly, we transfer the problem to the vector space setting, overcoming the difficulty that the collection of all subspaces is infinite by working with a suitably constructed finite family.
Secondly, we work with a more general weighted sum and design an extension operation that does not increase this sum. We introduce a carefully chosen bounded potential function that strictly increases with each operation. Since the potential is bounded, the process must terminate after finitely many steps. The final configuration then satisfies a known bound, yielding the required inequality.
Our method is distinct from the probabilistic technique used by Yue [20, 21] for set systems, and applies directly to vector space configuration.




The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects the necessary preliminary results from exterior algebra. Section 3 presents the proofs of Theorems 1.6 and 1.10. Section 4 contains the proofs of Theorems 1.12 and 1.15, together with Corollary 1.13.

The definition of the exterior algebra given in

Step 3. Iterating the extension until all tuples are full.
Define the deficiency of the ii-th tuple (Ai(1),…,Ai(d))(A_{i}^{(1)},\dots,A_{i}^{(d)}) by di=n−(ai(1)+⋯+ai(d))≥0d_{i}=n-(a_{i}^{(1)}+\cdots+a_{i}^{(d)})\geq 0. The tuple is called full if di=0d_{i}=0 (i.e., ⨁k=1dAi(k)=V\bigoplus_{k=1}^{d}A_{i}^{(k)}=V).
Define the potential function of 𝒫={(Ai(1),…,Ai(d))}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\dots,A_{i}^{(d)})\}_{i=1}^{m} as




Φ⁡(𝒫)=∑i=1m2n−di.\Phi(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}2^{\,n-d_{i}}.



Assume that in the chosen maximal‑weight system 𝒫\mathcal{P} some tuple (Ai(1),…,Ai(d))(A_{i}^{(1)},\dots,A_{i}^{(d)}) is not full, say with deficiency di≥1d_{i}\geq 1.

Thus,




Δ​Φ=d⋅2n−di+1−2n−di=2n−di​(2​d−1)>0.\Delta\Phi=d\cdot 2^{n-d_{i}+1}-2^{n-d_{i}}=2^{n-d_{i}}(2d-1)>0.



Since m≤dnm\leq d^{\,n}, we have Φ⁡(𝒫)≤m⋅2n≤dn⋅2n\Phi(\mathcal{P})\leq m\cdot 2^{n}\leq d^{n}\cdot 2^{n}.
Because Φ\Phi is a positive integer that strictly increases with each extension operation, the process must terminate after finitely many steps.
Termination occurs exactly when every tuple is full.
All extension operations preserve the weight and the skew Bollobás property. Therefore, the final system, which we still denote by 𝒫={(Ai(1),…,Ai(d))}i=1m\mathcal{P}=\{(A_{i}^{(1)},\dots,A_{i}^{(d)})\}_{i=1}^{m}, satisfies

At this point, ai(1)+⋯+ai(d)=na_{i}^{(1)}+\cdots+a_{i}^{(d)}=n for all i∈[m]i\in[m]. Hence, the weight simplifies to




ω⁡(𝒫)=∑i=1m1(nai(1),…,ai(d))​(n+d−1d−1).\omega(\mathcal{P})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{a_{i}^{(1)},\dots,a_{i}^{(d)}}\,\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}}.



Given a non-negative integer vector (a1,…,ad−1)(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1}), define

For every i∈I⁡(a1,…,ad−1)i\in I(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1}), we have dim(Ai(k))=ak\dim(A_{i}^{(k)})=a_{k} for k∈[d−1]k\in[d-1] and dimAi(d)=ad:=n−(a1+⋯+ad−1)\dim A_{i}^{(d)}=a_{d}:=n-(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{d-1}).

By Theorem 4.3, we obtain




|I⁡(a1,…,ad−1)|≤(na1,…,ad).|I(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1})|\leq\binom{n}{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}.



Partitioning the sum according to the first d−1d-1 dimensions gives




∑i=1m1(nai(1),…,ai(d))=∑(a1,…,ad−1)∑i∈I⁡(a1,…,ad−1)1(na1,…,ad).\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{a_{i}^{(1)},\dots,a_{i}^{(d)}}}=\sum_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1})}\;\sum_{i\in I(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1})}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}}.

∑i∈I⁡(a1,…,ad−1)1(na1,…,ad)≤|I⁡(a1,…,ad−1)|(na1,…,ad)≤1.\sum_{i\in I(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1})}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}}\leq\frac{|I(a_{1},\dots,a_{d-1})|}{\binom{n}{a_{1},\dots,a_{d}}}\leq 1.



The number of classes equals the number of non‑negative integer solutions of a1+⋯+ad−1≤na_{1}+\dots+a_{d-1}\leq n, which is (n+d−1d−1)\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}.

Because the extension procedure does not change the weight, the original maximal‑weight system 𝒫\mathcal{P} satisfies ω⁡(𝒫)≤1\omega(\mathcal{P})\leq 1.
Hence, every skew Bollobás dd-tuple system obeys the same inequality.
