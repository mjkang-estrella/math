Abstract.
    

We discuss several congruences satisfied by the coefficients of meromorphic modular forms, or equivalently, pp-adic behaviors of meromorphic modular forms under the UpU_{p} operator, that are summarized from numerical experiments, connecting meromorphic modular forms to symmetric powers of elliptic curves. We also provide heuristic explanations for these congruences as well as prove some of them using hypergeometric functions and the Borcherds–Shimura lift.
  



Key words and phrases: meromorphic modular forms, symmetric powers of elliptic curves, magnetic modular forms, supercongruences

1. Introduction




Coefficients of modular forms have always been one of the central topics in the field of modular forms. The vast majority of work in this field is restricted to the study of holomorphic or weakly holomorphic modular forms. In this paper, we instead turn to meromorphic modular forms. The main objects of interest are meromorphic modular forms of level 11 with precisely one pole at a non-cuspidal point. Specifically, we will focus on modular forms of the form



(1)

A1⋅gj−c+A2⋅g(j−c)2+⋯+Ar⋅g(j−c)r,\displaystyle A_{1}\cdot\frac{g}{j-c}+A_{2}\cdot\frac{g}{(j-c)^{2}}+\cdots+A_{r}\cdot\frac{g}{(j-c)^{r}},



where c∈ℚc\in\mathbb{Q}, each Ai∈ℚA_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}, and g∈ℤ⁡[[q]]g\in\mathbb{Z}[[q]] is a holomorphic modular form of level 11 such that gj−c\frac{g}{j-c} has a pole. We will discuss the (conjectural) pp-adic properties possessed by these meromorphic modular forms as well as the heuristic reasons for those properties. In particular, we hope to convince readers that these meromorphic modular forms of weight kk behave similarly to symmetric (k−2)(k-2)-nd powers of elliptic curves. Indeed, the paper by Brown–Fonseca [BF25] already evidences such a connection.




In this introduction, we will motivate the subject through several phenomena satisfied by the modular form E4j−c\frac{E_{4}}{j-c} for some c∈ℚc\in\mathbb{Q}, some provable and some conjectural. We start with the cases when c∈{0,1728}c\in\{0,1728\}, i.e., E4j\frac{E_{4}}{j} and E4j−1728\frac{E_{4}}{j-1728}. Here we make a definition that a (general) modular form ∑n=1∞an​qn∈ℤ⁡[[q]]\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[[q]] is called magnetic if n|ann{\hskip 1.0pt}|{\hskip 1.0pt}a_{n}, and specifically rr-magnetic for some r∈ℤ≥0r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} if nr|ann^{r}{\hskip 1.0pt}|{\hskip 1.0pt}a_{n} for all n∈ℤ+n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}. We have the following result by Li–Neururer [LN19, Theorem 1.5] and Paşol–Zudilin [PZ22, Theorem 1].



Theorem 1.1.



Both E4j\frac{E_{4}}{j} and E4j−1728\frac{E_{4}}{j-1728} are 11-magnetic.





The first numerically observed example of a magnetic modular form appeared in Broadhurst–Zudilin [BZ19, Conjecture 4] with motivation coming from the Hall effect in electromagnetism. The magnetic property of that example, together with that of 64​E4j\frac{64E_{4}}{j}, was proved in Li–Neururer [LN19]. Their proof uses the Borcherds–Shimura lift [Bor98]: one identifies the half-integral weight preimage, proves certain divisibility properties of the coefficients of the preimage, and deduces the magnetic property of the image. This method was later generalized by Paşol–Zudilin [PZ22] to show the magnetic property of other meromorphic modular forms, including E4j\frac{E_{4}}{j} and E4j−1728\frac{E_{4}}{j-1728}, all of which have poles at CM points. For a detailed discussion of magnetic modular forms, we also refer to the paper by Bönisch–Duhr–Maggio [BDM24] and the references therein.

The reason that all the phenomena have rather neat formulations in weight 44 is that there is no cusp form. In general, the coefficients of linear combinations of {g(j−j⁡(C))r}r\big\{\frac{g}{(j-j(C))^{r}}\big\}_{r} are an interaction of the elliptic curve C/ℚC/\mathbb{Q} and the cusp forms of weight kk, whose formulation is more complicated. For ease of exposition, we will mostly focus on the cases when k∈{4,6,8,10,14}k\in\{4,6,8,10,14\} so that there is no influence from cusp forms but that there is still a (canonical) choice for gg, i.e., the Eisenstein series EkE_{k} of weight kk. In Section 7.2, we will describe the way of generalizing the whole setting to other weights.




It should also be noted that while this paper heavily adopts the word “conjectures”, they should be more accurately named as numerical observations, as they are summarized from numerical experiments with these meromorphic modular forms.

3. Poles of higher order




As suggested constantly, meromorphic modular forms of weight kk with precisely one pole at a non-cuspidal point should behave similarly to the symmetric (k−2)(k-2)-nd power of the elliptic curve corresponding to the non-cuspidal pole. 7.2. Generalization to other weights




We will now discuss the generalization of the whole setting to other weights. In general, the coefficients of meromorphic modular forms having precisely one pole at a non-cuspidal point are an interplay between cusp forms and the elliptic curve corresponding to the pole.




Let g∈ℤ⁡[[q]]g\in\mathbb{Z}[[q]] be a holomorphic modular form of weight kk and level 11 and let C/ℚC/\mathbb{Q} be an elliptic curve (with j⁡(C)∉{0,1728}j(C)\notin\{0,1728\}) such that gj−j⁡(C)\frac{g}{j-j(C)} has a pole. For r∈ℤ+r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}, write




Fg,C(r):=g(j−j⁡(C))r=∑n=1∞an​(Fg,C(r))​qn.\displaystyle F_{g,C}^{(r)}\;:=\;\frac{g}{(j-j(C))^{r}}\;=\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(F_{g,C}^{(r)})q^{n}.

This is possible via the help of relations. An integer sequence λ=(λm)m∈ℤ+\lambda=(\lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}} is called a relation for Sk​(1)S_{k}(1) if


(1) 



λm=0\lambda_{m}=0 for all but finitely many mm;


(2) 



∑m=1∞λm​am= 0\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{m}a_{m}\;=\;0 for any cusp form ∑m=1∞am​qm∈Sk​(1)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{m}q^{m}\in S_{k}(1).

f(j−j⁡(α))r=h(j−j⁡(α))r−1+λ⋅g(j−j⁡(α))r.\displaystyle\frac{f}{(j-j(\alpha))^{r}}\;=\;\frac{h}{(j-j(\alpha))^{r-1}}+\lambda\cdot\frac{g}{(j-j(\alpha))^{r}}.

∎
