Abstract.
    

In this paper, we prove a conjecture by Daniele Mundici on the sum of squared distances between consecutive elements in the QQ-th Farey sequence for Q∈ℤQ\in\mathbb{Z} and Q≥2Q\geq 2.
  



Key words and phrases: Farey sequence, asymptotic formula, explicit error bounds


1991 Mathematics Subject ClassificationPrimary:11B57. Secondary:11N37, 11N56.




1. Introduction




Let Q≥2Q\geq 2 be an integer. The QQ-th Farey sequence FQF_{Q} is defined as follows:




FQ:={aq:1≤a≤q≤Q,(a,q)=1}.\displaystyle F_{Q}:=\left\{\frac{a}{q}:1\leq a\leq q\leq Q,\ (a,q)=1\right\}.






Assume |FQ|=NQ|F_{Q}|=N_{Q} such that




FQ:={γ1,γ2,…,γNQ}\displaystyle F_{Q}:=\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{N_{Q}}\}



with γ1<γ2<⋯<γNQ\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}<\dots<\gamma_{N_{Q}}. For simplicity, we will write N=NQN=N_{Q}, but keeping the dependence of QQ in mind.




Farey sequences have been studied for a long time, with one of the more extensively studied topics being the distribution of spacings between consecutive Farey fractions in a subinterval II of (0,1](0,1]. One direction is to explore the distribution of powers of the consecutive spacing. For example, the case I=(0,1]I=(0,1] has been considered in [3] for any power m≥2m\geq 2. Boca, Cobeli, and Zaharescu later extended the results to subintervals of (0,1](0,1] in [2]. Other results along this direction can be found in [5, 6, 9]. Another possible direction is to study the hh-th level consecutive spacing for any h≥2h\geq 2, for example, see [1, 2]. One can also find results in other directions in [4, 7].




In this paper, we go along the first direction and study the distribution when the power of consecutive spacing is 22. Let



(1.1)

S2​(Q):=∑j=1N−1(γj+1−γj)2,\displaystyle S_{2}(Q):=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(\gamma_{j+1}-\gamma_{j})^{2},



which is the sum of squared distances between consecutive elements in the QQ-th Farey sequence. In [9], it was mentioned that this quantity is an important special case in the study of sums of powers of consecutive distances between Farey points because it can be interpreted as the average length of the interval [γj,γj+1)[\gamma_{j},\gamma_{j+1}) which a random uniformly distributed point in (0,1](0,1] falls in. In [3], Hall proved that



(1.2)

S2​(Q)=12π2​Q2​(log⁡Q+γ+1/2−ζ′​(2)ζ⁡(2))+O⁡(log2⁡QQ3).\displaystyle S_{2}(Q)=\frac{12}{\pi^{2}Q^{2}}\left(\log Q+\gamma+1/2-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\zeta(2)}\right)+O\left(\frac{\log^{2}Q}{Q^{3}}\right).



Later, in [5, 6], Kanemitsu et al. refined Hall’s result by showing that the error term can be reduced to O⁡(log⁡Q/Q3)O(\log Q/Q^{3}). The established asymptotic formula is as follows:



(1.3)

S2​(Q)=12π2​Q2​(log⁡Q+γ+1/2−ζ′​(2)ζ⁡(2))+4​U​(Q)​log⁡QQ3+O⁡(log⁡QQ3),\displaystyle S_{2}(Q)=\frac{12}{\pi^{2}Q^{2}}\left(\log Q+\gamma+1/2-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\zeta(2)}\right)+\frac{4U(Q)\log Q}{Q^{3}}+O\left(\frac{\log Q}{Q^{3}}\right),



where




U⁡(x)=∑Q≤xμ⁡(Q)Q​(xQ−[xQ]−12).U(x)=\sum_{Q\leq x}\frac{\mu(Q)}{Q}\left(\frac{x}{Q}-\left[\frac{x}{Q}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\right).






Define



(1.4)

C⁡(Q):=S2​(Q)⋅Q2log⁡Q.\displaystyle C(Q):=\frac{S_{2}(Q)\cdot Q^{2}}{\log Q}.



Upon checking the first 10001000 values of QQ, Daniele Mundici came up with the following conjecture:



Conjecture 1.1.






C⁡(Q)<3​ for all ​Q>1,C(Q)<3\text{ for all }Q>1,



where C⁡(Q)C(Q) is defined in (1.4).





In follow-up private conversations, Mundici suggested that Conjecture 1.1 might be provable assuming the Riemann Hypothesis. In this paper, we show that the assumption of the Riemann Hypothesis is not needed, and prove Mundici’s original conjecture unconditionally. Our main result is the following.



Theorem 1.2.






C⁡(Q)<3​ for all ​Q>1,C(Q)<3\text{ for all }Q>1,



where C⁡(Q)C(Q) is defined in (1.4).





Along the result, we give an asymptotic formula of S2​(Q)S_{2}(Q) with an explicit error bound.



Theorem 1.3.






S2​(Q)\displaystyle S_{2}(Q)
=12​log⁡Qπ2​Q2−2Q2​ζ′​(2)ζ​(2)2+(2​γ+1)​6Q2​π2+R14,\displaystyle=\frac{12\log Q}{\pi^{2}Q^{2}}-\frac{2}{Q^{2}}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(2)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}+(2\gamma+1)\frac{6}{Q^{2}\pi^{2}}+R_{14},



where

The proof we give here is different from Hall’s proof in [3].
