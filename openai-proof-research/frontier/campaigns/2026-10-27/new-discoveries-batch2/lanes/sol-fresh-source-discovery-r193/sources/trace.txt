                                                                                               Abstract
                                                     A classical problem in spectral graph theory is to determine, among all simple graphs with a fixed
                                                 number of vertices and edges, those that maximize the number of spanning trees. Such graphs are
                                                 called t-optimal. Petingi and Rodrı́guez [Discr. Math. 244 (2002), 351–373] reduced the construction




arXiv:2610.36127v1 [math.CO] 26 Oct 2026
                                                 of infinitely many t-optimal graphs to the determination of almost-regular graphs with a minimum
                                                 number of induced 3-paths, known as almost-regular ν-min graphs. They conjectured that the class
                                                 of d-regular ν-min graphs is invariant under the addition or deletion of copies of the complete graph
                                                 Kd+1 , which would provide a method to construct large families of t-optimal graphs. This conjecture
                                                 was recently verified for d ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} by Gong et al. [Discr. Math. 349(4), 2026].
                                                     In this work, we establish an asymptotic version of this conjecture. We prove that the class of
                                                 d-regular ν-min graphs on at least n vertices is invariant under the addition or deletion of copies of
                                                 Kd+1 whenever d ≥ 3 and n ≥ d3 . In addition, we introduce a new method to construct t-optimal
                                                 graphs based on the concept of trace-minimal graphs, originally introduced by Ábrego et al. [Linear
                                                 Algebra Appl. 412 (2006), 161–221]. As a consequence, we obtain infinitely many graphs that are
                                                 both trace-minimal and t-optimal.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           In the first half of the nineteenth century, Kirchhoff, in his study of linear resistive circuits, proved that
                                           the number of spanning trees of a graph equals any cofactor of its Laplacian matrix [3]. As a consequence,
                                           the number of spanning trees of a graph, sometimes called the tree-number, can be computed efficiently.
                                           This raises a natural extremal question: given integers n and m, how can one construct simple graphs
                                           on n vertices and m edges that maximize the tree-number? Graphs attaining this maximum are called
                                           t-optimal graphs, and they play a role in network reliability analysis [4, 14, 13].
                                               Several classes of t-optimal graphs are known. Kelmans and Chelnokov [10] proved that the complete
                                           graph Kn minus a matching is t-optimal, and Cheng [5] showed that every regular complete multipartite
                                           graph is t-optimal. Boesch [4] conjectured that every t-optimal graph is almost regular, a statement later
                                           confirmed asymptotically by Petingi and Rodrı́guez [12]. They generalized Cheng’s result by proving that
                                           every almost-regular complete multipartite graph is t-optimal. Additionally, they provided a method to
                                           construct t-optimal graphs by means of the determination of almost-regular graphs that minimize the
                                           number of induced 3-paths, known as almost-regular ν-min graphs. Petingi et al. [11] and later Petingi
                                           and Rodrı́guez [12]  exhibited infinitely many t-optimal
                                                                                                    graphs on n vertices and m edges for the ranges

                                            2 − n ≤ m ≤ 2 − n/2 and 2 − 3n/2 ≤ m ≤ 2 − n, respectively.
                                               A central open question arising from [12] concerns the structure of d-regular ν-min graphs. Petingi
                                           and Rodrı́guez conjectured that the class of all d-regular ν-min graphs is invariant under the addition
                                           or deletion of copies of the complete graph Kd+1 . If true, this would yield a recursive mechanism to
                                           construct infinitely many regular ν-min graphs, and consequently, large families of t-optimal graphs.
                                           This conjecture has recently been verified for d ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} by Gong et al. [6], who also obtained new
                                           infinite families of t-optimal graphs whose complements are either 4-regular or 5-regular. Additional
                                           constructions have been obtained for graph classes of small corank. In this direction, Kahl [8] introduced
                                           the concept of Tutte-maximum graphs, which are also t-optimal, and Kahl and Luttrell [9] determined
                                           all such graphs with corank at most 3.

                                                                                                    1
    A key ingredient for our approach comes from a different line of research. Ábrego et al. [1], in the
context of D-optimal weighing designs, introduced the notion of trace-minimal graphs. These graphs are
defined through a lexicographic minimization of trace sequences of powers of the adjacency matrix, and
were used to construct D-optimal weighing designs (i.e., how to estimate the weight of some objects with
minimum uncertainty). As we show in this work, this concept is also well suited to the study of extremal
problems for the number of spanning trees.

    The purpose of this article is twofold. First, we establish an asymptotic version of the conjecture
of Petingi and Rodrı́guez. Specifically, we prove that the class of d-regular ν-min graphs on at least n
vertices is invariant under the addition or deletion of copies of Kd+1 whenever d ≥ 3 and n ≥ d3 (see
Proposition 26).
    Second, we introduce a new method to construct t-optimal graphs based on trace-minimal graphs. By
relating trace-minimality to a Laplacian-based lexicographic minimization, we obtain a framework that
refines the method of Petingi and Rodrı́guez [12]. As a consequence, we prove the existence of infinitely
many graphs that are simultaneously trace-minimal and t-optimal (see Theorem 36), yielding infinitely
many extremal graphs.

    The article is organized as follows. Section 2 presents basic concepts in graph theory. Section 3 reviews
results from spectral graph theory and summarizes key ideas from [12, 1], together with auxiliary results
on trace-minimal graphs. The main results are given in Section 4, where we prove the asymptotic version
of the conjecture (Proposition 26) and develop the new construction method based on trace-minimality
(Theorem 36). Finally, in Section 5, we construct infinitely many 6-regular and 7-regular graphs whose
complements are both trace-minimal and t-optimal.


2    Concepts
Let Sn,m be the class of simple graphs on n vertices and m edges, and let G ∈ Sn,m . We denote its
vertex and edge sets by V (G) and E(G), respectively, and write V (G) = {v1 , v2 , . . . , vn }. Two vertices
vi and vj are said to be adjacent if vi vj ∈ E(G); in this case, the edge vi vj is incident to both vi and vj .
The degree of a vertex vi , denoted by di , is the number of edges incident to vi . The degree sequence of
G is d(G) = (d1 , d2 , . . . , dn ).
    We say that G is d-regular if di = d for all i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , n}, and we denote by Rd (n) the class of
d-regular graphs on n vertices. We say that G is almost-regular if the degrees of any two of its vertices
differ by at most 1, and we denote by An,m the class of almost-regular graphs in Sn,m .
    We denote by In , 0n , and Jn the identity matrix, the all-zero matrix, and the all-one matrix of
size n × n, respectively. The trace of a matrix M , denoted tr(M ), is the sum of its diagonal entries.
The adjacency matrix of G is A(G) = (ai,j )1≤i,j≤n , where ai,j = 1 if vi vj ∈ E(G) and ai,j = 0
otherwise. Let D(G) be the diagonal matrix with diagonal entries d1 , . . . , dn . The Laplacian matrix of
G is L(G) = D(G) − A(G), and its characteristic polynomial is PG (x) = det(xIn − L(G)). The Laplacian
spectrum of G is the multiset consisting of all roots of PG (x).
    For each S ⊆ V (G), the subgraph of G induced by S, denoted G[S], is the graph with vertex set S and
all edges of G with both endpoints in S. Let t(G), τ (G), and ν(G) denote the number of spanning trees,
induced triangles, and induced 3-paths of G, respectively. We say that G is t-optimal if t(G) ≥ t(H) for
all H ∈ Sn,m .
    If G ∈ An,m , we say that G is ν-min if ν(G) ≤ ν(H) for all H ∈ An,m , and τ -max if τ (G) ≥ τ (H)
for all H ∈ An,m . The complement of G is denoted by G. The union of two disjoint graphs G and H is
denoted by G ∪ H. The join of G and H, denoted G ∧ H, is obtained from G ∪ H by adding all edges
between V (G) and V (H). We denote by G(n) the join of n disjoint copies of G, and by n(G) the number
of vertices of G.
    We denote by S(G), A(G), and R(G) the classes of simple, almost-regular, and regular graphs with
the same number of vertices and edges as G, respectively. We denote by Pn , Cn , Kn , and En the path,
cycle, complete graph, and edgeless graph on n vertices, respectively. The complete bipartite graph Kr,s
is defined as Er ∧ Es .

4    Main results
The main results of this section are Proposition 26 which shows that Conjecture 10 proposed by Petingi
and Rodrı́guez is true in an asymptotic sense, and Theorem 36 which gives a methodology to construct
infinitely many trace-minimal graphs that are t-optimal.

    We now study Conjecture 10. The key is Lemma 25. Its proof is inspired by the proof of Lemma 17
that appeared in [1]. Recall that if d and n are positive integers such that d ≤ n − 1 and dn is even then
Harary graph Hd (n) is a d-regular graph on n vertices thus Rd (n) is nonempty; a construction of Hd (n)
is given in [7]. A consequence is Remark 24.
Remark 24. If Rd (n) is nonempty and we let q and r be the only integers such that n = q(d + 1) + r

it suffices to prove that for each positive integer p the graph G0 (p) is τ -max in R(G0 (p)). We will

gives that Kd+1 is a component of G. But then, G = H ∪ Kd+1 . As G is τ -max and the number

                    τ (G) = τ (H) + τ (Kd+1 ) = τ (G0 (p)) + τ (Kd+1 ) = τ (G0 (p + 1)),

sequences ai (G) and ai (H) are also identical. Finally, if condition (ii) holds then there exists a positive
integer j such that for each k ∈ {1, 2, . . . , j − 1} it holds that ℓk (G) = ℓk (H) but ℓj (G) < ℓj (H). In this
case Equation (11) gives that ai (G) = ai (H) when i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , j − 1} but aj (G) < aj (H). In either
case, (ai (G)) ⪯ (ai (H)) and G is trace-minimal in Rn−1−d (n). The lemma follows.
    Lemma 35 establishes a correspondence between L-trace-minimal graphs in Rd (n) and trace-minimal
graphs in Rn−1−d (n). As a consequence, the problem of identifying L-trace-minimal graphs can be
reduced to the study of trace-minimal graphs, for which several structural results and constructions are
already available in the literature.
    Our findings are summarized in Theorem 36.
Theorem 36. For each pair of integers d and n such that d ≥ 3 and n ≥ d3 and each graph G0 in
Rd (n) whose complement is trace-minimal, there exists infinitely many nonnegative integers p such that
G0 (p) is both a trace-minimal and a t-optimal graph.


                                                              12
Proof. Let d and n be integers such that d ≥ 3 and n ≥ d3 , and let G0 be any graph in Rd (n) whose
complement is trace-minimal. Let p be any nonnegative integer. By Lemma 33, it suffices to prove
that G0 (p) is L-trace minimal. Let Hp be any graph with as many vertices and edges as G0 (p) whose
complement is trace-minimal. As Hp has at least n vertices and n ≥ d3 , a repeated application of
                               (p)
Lemma 18 gives that Hp = Ed+1 ∧ H0 , or equivalently, Hp = H0 ∪ pKd+1 , where H0 ∈ Rd (n) and its
complement is trace-minimal. As G0 and H0 are trace-minimal, both graphs have identical adjacency
spectrum. Consequently, G0 (p) and Hp also have identical adjacency spectrum and G0 (p) is also trace-
minimal. As G0 (p) is trace-minimal, Lemma 35 gives that G0 (p) is L-trace-minimal. By Lemma 33,
there exists p0 such that G0 (p) is t-optimal whenever p ≥ p0 . The theorem is proved.
    We are now in a position to establish a two-step method to find new t-optimal graphs. First, pick
integers d and n such that d ≥ 3 and n ≥ d3 . Then, find some graph G in Rd (n) whose complement
is trace-minimal. Theorem 36 asserts that there exists a nonnegative integer p0 such that G ∪ pKd+1 is
t-optimal (and trace-minimal) whenever p ≥ p0 .
    Our method avoids spectral comparisons as well as the determination of infinitely many almost-regular
ν-min graphs that appear in [12]. Moreover, it allows us to rely on existing tools for the construction of
trace-minimal graphs available in the literature.


5      Construction of regular t-optimal graphs
A generous list of trace-minimal graphs can be found in [1]. Among them, we can find all trace-minimal
graphs in each nonempty class Rn−1−d (n) such that d ∈ {1, . . . , 5}. By Theorem 36, such classes
of trace-minimal graphs are also t-optimal when the number of vertices is large enough. Petingi and
Rodrı́guez [12] found the only t-optimal graph in Rn−1−d (n) when d ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3} and n is large enough.
We found the only t-optimal graph in Rn−1−d (n) when d = 4 and n is large enough [15]. Independently,
Gong et al. [6] found the only t-optimal graph in Rn−1−d (n) when d ∈ {4, 5} and n is large enough.

determined by Ábrego et al. [1]. In Lemma 37 and Lemma 38 we find the only trace-minimal graph in
