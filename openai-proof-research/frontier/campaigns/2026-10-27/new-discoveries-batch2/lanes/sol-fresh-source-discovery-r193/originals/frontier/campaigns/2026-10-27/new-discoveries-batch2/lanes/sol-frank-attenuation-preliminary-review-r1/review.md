## Ordinary MATH: pass

An independent derivation starts with a=e^(-8u), b=1-e^(-8v), q=e^(-8), d=1-q. Differentiating C(u,v)=-(1/8)log(1-(1-a)b/d) in u gives H_u(v)=ab/(d-b+ab). Its denominator is at least qd>0 on the square, since b<=d and a>=q. Its density and conditional moments are consequently smooth there. Integration of 1-H_0 gives m(0)=1/8-q/d. Differentiation of 1-H gives

    m'(0)=8/d² ∫_0^1 (1-e^(-8v))(e^(-8v)-q)dv
          =(1+q)/(2d)-8q/d².

For clarity, expanding the integrand gives (1+q)e^(-8v)-e^(-16v)-q; its integral is (1-q²)/16-q. This independently checks both the sign and the factor 8. Radial symmetry gives m(1)=1-m(0); the endpoint chord is 3/4+2q/d>3/4. The rational Taylor lower sum through degree seven exceeds 1000, so q<1/1000 and m'(0)<1001/1998<3/4. Hence m cannot be affine. There is no numerical nonaffineness premise.

## Strongest objection at full strength: a complete classical bridge

There is an even simpler general argument than the candidate's symmetric Rayleigh quotient. Let (U,V) have uniform margins; let m(u)=E[V|U=u] have a C1 version. Assume m is nonaffine, and set l=u-1/2, r=Corr(U,V), h=m-1/2-rl and A=∫h²>0. Then ∫h=∫hl=0. Perturb only the first margin: F_e(u)=u+e h(u). Conditional expectation and orthogonality give exactly

    Corr(F_e(U),V) = (rs+eA)/sqrt(s(s+e²A)),  s=1/12.

Its derivative at zero is A/s>0. For sufficiently small positive e it is strictly increasing and raises correlation. For a negative baseline correlation, choose sufficiently small negative e instead to increase absolute correlation. With r=0, either nonzero perturbation gives a strict absolute gain. Normalize its range and quantize F_e and the identity separately into consecutive integer scores. Uniform convergence and positive limiting variances preserve the strict gain at finite category counts, with all category probabilities positive. The same number of categories can be used in both margins. Exchangeability, positive association, an eigenfunction expansion, attainment of maximal correlation and an explicit optimizer are unnecessary.

Equivalently, universal attenuation over all finite conventional ordinal margins forces affine conditional regression whenever that regression is C1. This is the standard first-variation/equality obstruction for correlation, followed by elementary approximation. It closes the potentially nontrivial admissibility issue: the identity sits inside the cone of increasing C1 transformations because its derivative is strictly positive. A bounded derivative perturbation remains in that cone; conventional integer scores are dense after range rescaling. An unrestricted HGR bound alone would not close this issue. The argument here does close it, explicitly.

Applying that general classical bridge to Frank requires only the conditional-CDF differentiation and two elementary endpoint evaluations above. There is no remaining Frank-specific structural lemma, new optimization theory, or delicate threshold obstruction. This is the basis for EXCLUDE under the campaign's routine-adaptation rule. Proof shortness alone, or the fact that the source conjecture is false, is not the basis. I am not claiming that Beare already published this exact ordinal theorem. A new explicit diagnostic correction to a recent paper can be useful without qualifying as a substantive campaign discovery. The 20-category certificate does not cure this eligibility issue; it is a parameter witness for the same family.
