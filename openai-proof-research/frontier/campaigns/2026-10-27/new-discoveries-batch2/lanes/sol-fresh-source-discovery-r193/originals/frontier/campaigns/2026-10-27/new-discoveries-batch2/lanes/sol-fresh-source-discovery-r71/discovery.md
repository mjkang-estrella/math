## Rigorous route 1: factorization box and short cycles

Write PF={h+j alpha:1<=j<=n-1}. First alpha is a gap: otherwise adding alpha to the least PF number produces another gap that must belong to H. Every gap g is dominated by a pseudo-Frobenius number in the H-order (successively add positive generators while staying a gap; finiteness ensures termination). Apply this to alpha. If h+(j-1)alpha is in H, j cannot exceed1, since then it is a listed PF number. Hence h belongs to H, in particular h>=0. Thus the full source's AP-only statement legitimately reaches the nonnegative-h setting; no endpoint repair is being counted.

For c in H, the same domination argument proves

    c+alpha not in H  iff  h-c in H.                         (1)

Indeed any dominating PF has difference h+(j-1)alpha-c in H. Adding c excludes j>=2. The reverse implication follows because h+alpha is a gap. These are public Kien boundary lemmas.

Let Z(h) be all factorizations, M_i=max{z_i:z in Z(h)}, h'=sum M_i*a_i. Then

    M_i*a_i+alpha notin H,
    (M_i+1)*a_i+alpha in H,
    h'-h in H.                                             (2)

The first two follow from (1); the third follows by subtracting any actual factorization coordinatewise. h is uniquely factored iff h'=h. This is public Theorem3.6, not a new result.

Attempted extension of the cycle argument: put b_i=(M_i+1)a_i+alpha and choose an arrow i->j from a factorization of b_i containing a_j. A directed r-cycle C yields

    sum_{i in C} M_i*a_i+r*alpha in H.

For a genuine SINGLE factorization q of h, this would allow adding its complementary coordinates to obtain h+r*alpha in H, a contradiction whenever r<n. But the coordinate maxima M_i need not occur simultaneously. In the unrestricted case the summation yields h'+r*alpha in H after adding complementary maxima, NOT h+r*alpha in H. Subtracting h'-h is invalid in a semigroup. This is the exact missing step, not an asserted proof. Taking an arbitrary q instead loses the positive threshold membership in (2).

A complete way forward would prove h'=h from the full AP/type hypothesis, or replace this cycle argument with a genuinely new mechanism. Merely restating that uniqueness follows from determinantality would be circular.

## Rigorous route 2: boundary generating series

Let D_h={c in H:h-c in H}, and B_alpha={c in H:c-alpha notin H}. Both are finite; (1) identifies the negative boundary with D_h. Writing H(z)=sum_{c in H}z^c gives the EXACT formal identity

    (1-z^alpha) H(z) = B_alpha(z)-z^alpha D_h(z).           (3)

This follows coefficientwise from the two indicator differences; no convergence assumption. In particular |B_alpha|-|D_h|=alpha, by multiplying H(z) by 1-z and evaluating at1 using the finite gap polynomial. Also D_h(z)=z^h D_h(1/z), by c->h-c. If h has unique factorization q, every divisor has a unique subfactorization and

    D_h(z)= product_i (1+z^{a_i}+...+z^{q_i*a_i}).          (4)

An attempted proof from reciprocity, (3), and principal boundary alone fails: those hypotheses need not force the rectangular divisor structure (4).

Exact obstruction H=<5,8,11,14>: Ap(H,5)={0,11,22,8,14} indexed by residues, PF={17}, alpha=1, h=16. D_h={0,5,8,11,16} equals the entire negative shift boundary, and is reciprocal. Yet 16=2*8=5+11, M=(1,2,1,0), h'=32. The maximal-coordinate factorization graph has multiple successors. Thus neither principal boundary nor reciprocity suffices. This H has type1, not n-1=3: it is NOT a counterexample to the source. The missing ingredient in both routes is the full AP length relative to the number of minimal generators.
