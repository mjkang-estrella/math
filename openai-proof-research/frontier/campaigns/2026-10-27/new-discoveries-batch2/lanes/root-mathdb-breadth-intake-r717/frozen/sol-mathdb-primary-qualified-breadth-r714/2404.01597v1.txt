                                          Abstract: In 2019, Bóna and Smith introduced the notion of strong pattern avoidance, that is,
                                          a permutation and its square both avoid a given pattern. In this paper, we enumerate the set of
                                          permutations π which not only strongly avoid the pattern 312 or 231 but also avoid the pattern
                                          τ , for τ ∈ S3 and some τ ∈ S4 . One of them is to give a positive answer to a conjecture of Archer
                                          and Geary.

                                          Keywords: Permutation; strong pattern avoidance; pattern 312; pattern 231.

                                          Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 05A05, 05A15


                                          1       Introduction
                                          Let Sn denote the symmetry group on [n] := {1, 2, . . . , n}. It is well-known that every permutation
                                          π in Sn can be written in its one-line notation as π = π1 π2 · · · πn , where πi = π(i) for all i ∈ [n].
                                          From the perspective, the patterns can be considered in the permutation. Let π = π1 π2 · · · πn ∈ Sn
                                          and τ = τ1 τ2 · · · τk ∈ Sk with k ≤ n. If there exists a subset of indices 1 ≤ i1 < i2 < · · · < ik ≤ n
                                          such that πis > πit if and only if τs > τt for all 1 ≤ s < t ≤ k, then we say that τ is contained
                                          in π and the subsequence πi1 πi2 · · · πik is called an occurrence of τ in π and denoted by τ ≤ π.
                                          For example, 132 ≤ 24153, because 2, 5, 3 appear in the same order of size as the letters in 132.
                                          The permutation that does not contain an occurrence of τ is called a τ -avoiding permutation.
                                          The theory of pattern avoidance in permutations was introduced by Knuth in [8], which has been
                                          widely studied for half a century, refer to [3, 11]. Recently, the research of the pattern avoidance
                                          concerning the symmetric group concept has attracted the attention of some scholars. However,
                                          it becomes more complicated as the conditions strengthen and the results about this topic can be
                                          seen in [1, 2, 4–6, 8–10].
                                               In 2019, Bóna and Smith proposed the definition of strong pattern avoidance in [5], that is,
                                          a permutation π strongly avoids τ if both π and π 2 avoid τ . Shortly afterward, Burcroff and
                                          Defant [6] introduced the definition of powerful avoidance, namely, a permutation π powerfully
                                          avoids τ if every power of π avoids τ . More recently, Archer and Geary [2] generalized the ideas
                                          of strongly avoidance and powerful avoidance to what they called chain avoidance, that is, a
                                          permutation π avoids a chain of patterns (τ1 : τ2 : · · · : τk ) if the i-th power of π avoids the pattern
                                          τi . In addition, this definition can be extended to the set of patterns as well, which is separated
                                          by a comma. For example, if π avoids the chain (σ1 , σ2 : τ ), then π avoids both σ1 and σ2 , and π 2
                                          avoids τ . Let Cn (σ1 , σ2 : τ ) denote the set of permutations in Sn that avoid the chain (σ1 , σ2 : τ ),
                                          and cn (σ1 , σ2 : τ ) denote the number of such permutations. It is worth mentioning that Archer
                                          and Geary [2] not only gave a formula of computing the number of unimodal permutations whose
                                          square avoids every τ ∈ S3 but also proposed an interesting conjecture as shown below:

2     Preliminaries
Let π = π1 π2 · · · πn ∈ Sn . Recall that the reverse of π is the permutation rev(π) = πn πn−1 · · · π1 ,
the complement of π is the permutation comp(π) = (n + 1 − π1 )(n + 1 − π2 ) · · · (n + 1 − πn ),
and the reverse complement of π is the permutation comp(rev(π)). In addition, let S be a set of
permutations. We use comp(rev(S)) to denote the set {comp(rev(π))|π ∈ S}. Inspired by Bóna
and Smith, Burcroff and Defant observed that comp(rev(π k )) = comp(rev(π))k for any positive
integer k. Therefore, it is easy to deduce the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1 Let (S1 : S2 : · · · : Sk ) and (S1′ : S2′ : · · · : Sk′ ) be two chains of patterns such that
Si′ = comp(rev(Si )) for i = 1, 2, . . . , k. Then cn (τ1 : τ2 : · · · : τk ) = cn (τ1′ : τ2′ : · · · : τk′ ).

Actually, applying Lemma 2.1, we see that all results of Archer and Geary [2] can be attributed to
their inverse complements, such as cn (132, 231 : 123) = ⌊ n2 ⌋ for n ≥ 8, cn (132, 231 : 213) = n + 1
for n ≥ 3, and so on. Note that 312 = comp(rev(231)) and 3214 = comp(rev(1432)). Therefore,
cn (231, 1432 : 231) = cn (312, 3214 : 312). Additionally, Bóna and Smith [5] obtained a result about
strongly avoidance of the pattern 312 that will be repeatedly applied, as follows:

Theorem 2.2 ([5, Theorem 3.1]) For any permutation π ending in 1, the following two statements
are equivalent.
    (A) The permutation π is strongly 312-avoiding.
    (B) The permutation π has form π = (k + 1)(k + 2) · · · nk(k − 1)(k − 2) · · · 1 where k ≥ n2 .
    That is, π is unimodal beginning with its n − k ≤ n2 largest entries in increasing order followed
    by the remaining k smallest entries in decreasing order.

Thereby, we enumerate the set of permutations π which not only strongly avoid the pattern 312
but also avoid the pattern τ , for τ ∈ S3 and some τ ∈ S4 . In particular, we shall give a positive
answer to the Conjecture 1.1 by computing cn (312, 3214 : 312).
