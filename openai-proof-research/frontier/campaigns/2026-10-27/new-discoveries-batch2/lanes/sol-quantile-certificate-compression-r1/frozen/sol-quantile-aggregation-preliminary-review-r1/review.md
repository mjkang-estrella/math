## Strongest substantive objections

Source Example 2 (p8) uses equiprobable {0,1} and {0,3}; its quantile average is equiprobable {0,2}. A source law can charge 1 or 3, while every target sum is supported on {0,2,4}. That is an actual prior atomic separation. Ordinary atom smoothing preserves some strict bounded-payoff separations, but it does NOT automatically put marginals into the decreasing-density class.

There is a concrete obstruction to the proposed limiting argument. For any nonincreasing nonnegative density f on a support interval containing adjacent intervals [a,b] and [b,c], the average-density inequality gives integral_b^c f <= ((c-b)/(b-a)) integral_a^b f. If such densities converged weakly to (delta_0+delta_1)/2, take [a,b]=[1/4,3/4] and [b,c]=[3/4,5/4]. Both intervals must be within the support interval for sufficiently close approximations, since positive mass must occur near 0 and 1. Extend f by zero to the right if necessary; this preserves the inequality. The middle mass would converge to 0, whereas the right mass would converge to 1/2, contradicting the inequality. The same argument after scaling applies to {0,3}. Thus these atomic marginals cannot even be obtained as such weak limits. Convolution creates two humps or requires filling an intervening region with nonvanishing mass. A substantial change of marginals might still preserve separation, but requires a new explicit coupling, dual bound and positive error margin. None is supplied by Example 2. This establishes failure of the automatic smoothing bridge, not impossibility of all elementary adaptations. No shortness criterion was used to dismiss credit.

Kantorovich weak duality supplies the inequality mechanism; it does not supply convex marginal quantiles, the feasible dual and permutation, or the strict gap after continuous errors. The exact certificate fills these particular obligations. A numerical witness alone would not do so.

Source Theorem 1 (pp4-5), including its actual Birkhoff/random-permutation proof, concerns mixtures of CDFs. Replacing that mixture by an average of inverse CDFs is unjustified. Proposition 1 (p8) identifies the convex-order upper hulls, through the common comonotone sum; it neither characterizes the actual aggregation sets nor tests this nonconvex payoff. Theorem 3 (pp11-13) compares worst VaR level by level; separate optimizers at each level need not realize an entire fixed law. Example 3 (pp25-26) concerns point masses via the mean-length criterion, not preservation of this nonconstant H. Source Proposition 2 is for uniform marginals and n>=3.

The actual 2019 Cambridge primary PDF was obtained independently: Mao-Wang-Wang, **Sums of standard uniform random variables**, DOI 10.1017/jpr.2019.52, pp918-936 (the source abbreviates its title). Theorem 5 (p931) states D_n^U=C_n^U only for n>=3. Its proof uses bi-atomic reduction and mixture/weak-closure arguments. For n=2 Section 3 instead provides restricted characterizations; Proposition 1 and Theorem 1 (p921) include monotone/unimodal sum densities, not arbitrary sums of decreasing-density marginals. Even the known strict D2^U subset C2^U does not produce a counterexample to the requested inclusion: one must realize its excluded law with the other two decreasing marginals whose quantile average is that same uniform. In fact if the average of two convex quantiles is affine, both are affine, so the original marginals must also be uniform. No full bridge follows from a convex-order exclusion alone.

The actual convolution primary Theorem 1 (pp8-9) bounds supremal RVaR, with sharpness conditions on tail densities; its objective is not all-law inclusion. The actual 2204.11438v4 Theorem 1 (p5) assumes a joint mix plus conditional independence/monotonicity to prove negative association. Theorem 3 and Proposition 4 (pp8-9) decompose constant-sum vectors or exchangeable joint mixes. They supply no assertion preserving every nonconstant source sum law. These statements do not subsume this counterexample. The 2016 Joint Mixability original remains unread: its publisher returned 403, stopped. Its mean-length result is attributed only through the source's actual quotation, not claimed independently cleared.

## Minimal literal formal endpoint

Define the source density class by existence of a Lebesgue density nonincreasing on its interval support.
