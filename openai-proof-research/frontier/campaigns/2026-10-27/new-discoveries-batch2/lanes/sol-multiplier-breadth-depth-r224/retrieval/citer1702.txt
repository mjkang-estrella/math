                                                ZEROS OF SOME SPECIAL ENTIRE FUNCTIONS

                                                                ÁRPÁD BARICZ AND SANJEEV SINGH




arXiv:1702.00626v1 [math.CA] 2 Feb 2017
                                                  Abstract. The distribution of real and complex zeros of some spe-
                                                  cial entire functions such as Wright, hyper-Bessel, and a special case of
                                                  generalized hypergeometric functions is studied by using some classical
                                                  results of Laguerre, Pólya, Obreschkhoff and Runckel. The obtained
                                                  results extend the well-known theorem of Hurwitz on complex zeros of
                                                  Bessel functions of the first kind. Moreover, results on zeros of Bessel
                                                  function derivatives and cross-product of Bessel functions are also given,
                                                  which are related to some recent open problems.




                                                              1. Introduction and Main Results
                                             Because of the numerous applications in applied mathematics, the zeros
                                          of special functions and orthogonal polynomials have been investigated fre-
                                          quently by many scientists. Although it is a classical subject, nowadays still
                                          there are many investigations in this topic. The zeros of Bessel functions of
                                          the first kind Jν of order ν have been studied frequently: Lommel proved
                                          the reality of the zeros of Bessel functions for ν > −1, while Hurwitz [Hu89]
                                          completed the picture of the behavior of the zeros of Bessel functions of the
                                          first kind by finding the number of complex zeros for ν < −1. For Hurwitz’s
                                          theorem many other interesting proofs were found out, see the papers of Hilb
                                          [Hi22], Obreschkoff [Ob29], Pólya [Po29], Hille and Szegő [HS43], Pey-
                                          erimhoff [Pe66], Runckel [Ru69], Ki and Kim [KK00]. Hurwitz’s original
                                          proof (based on Lommel polynomials) is quite long and difficult to read in
                                          some places, Watson [Wa21] even corrected some logical gap in the proof of
                                          Hurwitz. In the (unsuccessful) process of trying to extend Hurwitz’s theo-
                                          rem on zeros of Bessel functions to zeros of regular Coulomb wave functions
                                          we found two papers (written in German) by Nikola Obreschkoff [Ob29] and
                                          George Pólya [Po29]. Although the main results of the above mentioned
                                          papers of Obreschkoff and Pólya were not useful to obtain the extension
                                          what we wanted to do, we realized that the results of Obreschkoff and Pólya
                                          are quite useful to study the distribution of real and complex zeros of those
                                          special entire functions of which coefficients are expressed in terms of the
                                          reciprocal gamma function. We found out later that some similar results

   Recently, Kalmykov and Karp [KK16] conjectured that if p < q, b >
0, and ak > bk for k ∈ {1, . . . , p}, then all zeros of p Fq (a; b; z) are real
and negative. Applying directly Laguerre’s Lemma 1, we conclude that if
b > 0, then all zeros of 0 Fq (−; b; z) are real and negative. This particular
result suggest the validity of the above conjecture. Moreover, by a simple
application of Lemma 1 it is possible to obtain the following result: if p ≤ q,
b > 0 and a can be reindexed so that ak = bk + nk for nk ∈ N and k ∈
{1, . . . , p}, then the function p Fq (a; b; z) has only negative real zeros. This
result was stated by Richards [Ri90], who used Laguerre’s Lemma 1. The
main idea is that the function
                               Γ(a1 + z) · · · Γ(ap + z)
                                Γ(b1 + z) · · · Γ(bq + z)
is a meromorphic function as a quotient of two entire functions, however,
after the reindexation the poles of the numerator are absorbed by those of
the denominator, and hence it becomes an entire function of growth order
1 for which Lemma 1 can be applied. We note that the reality of the ze-
ros of p Fq (a; b; z) follows also immediately from Obreschkoff’s Lemma 2.
Moreover, by using Pólya’s Lemma 3 we obtain the following result, which
complements [KK16, Theorem 4].
Theorem 3. Suppose that b > 0 and a can be reindexed so that ak = bk +nk
for nk ∈ N and k ∈ {1, . . . , p}.

1.3. Concluding remarks. In this paper we have seen that in very special
cases (when the coefficients of entire functions contain almost only recipro-
cal gamma function terms) we can find the maximal or the exact number of
complex zeros of the entire functions in the question. However, for example
in the above mentioned conjecture of Kalmykov and Karp [KK16, Conjec-
ture 3] the coefficients are meromorphic and so far we are not aware on any
result in the literature which would help to verify this conjecture. Craven
and Csordas [CC06, Problem 1.2] posed the following problem:
