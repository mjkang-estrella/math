                                               SUPERHARMONICITY OF THE FRACTIONAL GROUND STATE

                                             NICOLA DE NITTI, XAVIER FERNÁNDEZ-REAL, FLORIAN GRUBE, AND HARSH PRASAD


                                                   Abstract. We prove that the positive ground state u of the fractional Laplacian (−∆)s ,
                                                   s ∈ (0, 1), on an arbitrary open set Ω satisfies −∆u > λs (Ω)1/s u in Ω. In one dimension,
                                                   this yields strong concavity and settles a conjecture of Bañuelos, Kulczycki, and Méndez-
                                                   Hernández. We establish a hierarchy of pointwise inequalities comparing different powers
                                                   of the Laplacian. In balls, these inequalities give a quantitative Hessian estimate for log u,
                                                   and hence log-concavity in every dimension. By contrast, we show that log-concavity fails

                                                   in general convex domains by constructing counterexamples in sufficiently thin ellipsoids.
                                                   We also extend the superharmonicity principle to ground states of ψ(−∆) for complete

The fractional Brascamp–Lieb problem. The conjecture (1.2) belongs to a broader
question raised in [7]. The classical Brascamp–Lieb theorem states that the ground state
of −∆ on every bounded convex domain is log-concave [9, Theorem 6.1]. Besides its intrin-
sic geometric content, this property has applications in analysis, geometry, mathematical
physics, and probability. In particular, log-concavity leads to estimates of the spectral gap
µ2 − µ1 between the first and second Dirichlet eigenvalues, see, e.g., [30, 26]. This gap
describes the rate to equilibrium of Brownian motion conditioned to remain in the domain;
see [35] and [7, p. 205].
   Motivated by the classical Brascamp–Lieb theorem, Bañuelos, Kulczycki, and Méndez-
Hernández raised the following question in [7]:
   Given a bounded convex domain Ω ⊂ Rn and s ∈ (0, 1), is the ground state of (1.1)
log-concave?
   One probabilistic proof of the classical Brascamp–Lieb theorem proceeds through the
log-concavity of the finite-dimensional distributions of Brownian motion. For stable pro-
cesses this argument breaks down: their transition densities are not log-concave, and sub-
ordination does not preserve log-concavity in the required form [7].
   Corollary 1.2 gives a positive answer in intervals. For balls in every dimension, the
higher-order inequalities in Theorem 1.3 lead to a quantitative Hessian estimate. Radial
symmetry reduces the problem to two one-dimensional inequalities, and (1.8) gives the
same conclusion for ground states of ψ(−∆).

the whole-space equation is
(1.11)                               ((−∆)s − λ)u = −µ.

once more. Since µ is non-negative and supported in Ωc , its half Laplacian has the opposite
sign at interior points in Ω. Thus, formally,
                          −∆u − λ2 u = −(−∆)1/2 µ > 0          in Ω.
This is the basic positivity mechanism in the half-Laplacian argument.
  For a general s, however, no finite number of applications of (−∆)s reaches the ordinary
Laplacian unless 1/s is an integer; see Remark 3.3. For example, when s > 1/2, applying
the operator once more still gives the formally useful inequality
                         (−∆)2s u − λ2 u = −(−∆)s µ > 0         in Ω.
One might then try to use
                                (−∆)2s u = (−∆)2s−1 (−∆u)
and apply a weak maximum principle for (−∆)2s−1 to −∆u.
   The issue lies at the boundary ∂Ω, even though u is smooth in Ω. In a smooth do-
main, the natural boundary profile dist(x, ∂Ω)s , has a second normal derivative of size
dist(x, ∂Ω)s−2 , which is not locally integrable in the normal direction. The distributional
second normal derivative of its zero extension is therefore not a Radon measure. Thus
interior smoothness does not justify regarding −∆u as an admissible function with zero
exterior values, as required by this weak maximum-principle argument.
   The factorization (1.10) avoids this difficulty by
                                 −∆u − λ1/s u = −G(−∆)µ.
The key difference is that G(−∆) acts directly on the Radon measure µ, rather than on
the singular global distribution −∆u. Its complete Bernstein representation, given by
Proposition 3.1, expresses the right-hand side as a positive integral of Bessel potentials in
Ω.
