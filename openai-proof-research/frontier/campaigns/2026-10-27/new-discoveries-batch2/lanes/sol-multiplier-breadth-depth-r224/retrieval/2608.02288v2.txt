                                                                                                   Abstract
                                                      We study a reconstruction problem of planar domains from non-local integral-geometric invariants. We
                                                  show that a generic polygonal domain, not necessarily convex, is uniquely determined, up to Euclidean
                                                  isometry, by the interpoint distance distribution (IDD), which, for convex domains, is equivalent to the chord
                                                  length distribution. Using a boundary representation of the Riesz energy function, we replace the IDD of
                                                  the domain by an equivalent boundary IDD weighted by the scalar product of the outer unit normals, which
                                                  we call ν-weighted IDD of the boundary. It enables us to reduce the problem to a one-dimensional problem.
                                                  By analyzing jumps and blow-up terms of the second and third derivatives of the ν-weighted IDD of the
                                                  boundary, we recover the side lengths, their cyclic incidence, and the exterior angles of the polygon. This
                                                  can be considered as extending Waksman’s classical generic reconstruction theorem for convex polygons to
                                                  non-convex setting, as well as extending generic polygonal reconstruction from directional covariogram data
                                                  to a one-dimensional invariant in which directional information has been integrated out.

                                               Keywords: Integral geometry, polygon, interpoint distance distribution, covariogram, Riesz energy, chord length,
                                           reconstruction.

                                           1      Introduction
                                           We consider a distance-based reconstruction problem: to what extent can a domain Ω be recovered from non-
                                           local invariants deﬁned by pairwise distances? The spaces that we study here are planar polygons, and the
                                           invariants are the interpoint distance distribution (IDD) ΨΩ (r), which maps each r > 0 to the product measure
                                           of the set of pairs (x, y) ∈ Ω × Ω whose distance |x − y| is at most r, and, for convex bodies, the chord length
                                           distribution ΦΩ (r), which assigns to r > 0 the measure of the set of lines meeting Ω in a chord of length at most
                                           r, where we use a measure on the space of aﬃne lines in R2 that is isometry-invariant. Here, a body in Euclidean
                                           space refers to a compact subset equal to the closure of its interior. Using the Riesz energy function, which
                                           will be deﬁned in Section 2, we provide a weighted boundary expression of the interpoint distance distribution,
                                           which allows us to reduce problems on two-dimensional domains to those on one-dimensional closed polygonal
                                           lines.
                                               Our main result is
                                           Theorem (see Theorem 4.3). A generic planar polygonal domain is determined up to Euclidean isometry by its
                                           interpoint distance distribution.
                                           Here, the generic condition ensures that information about distances and angles does not interfere with each
                                           other. First, we deﬁne a critical length to be either an inter-vertex distance or an interior height, which is the
                                           length of the perpendicular dropped from a vertex to the interior of a side. Then the genericity assumptions
                                           require that the critical lengths are dissociated, that no adjacent exterior height accidentally coincides with a

Matheron’s conjecture asks whether a convex body Ω is uniquely determined up to translation and reﬂection by
its covariogram ([14]). This conjecture was answered aﬃrmatively for planar convex polygons by Nagel [15] and
for general planar convex bodies by Averkov and Bianchi [1]. Schmitt [22] gave a constructive reconstruction
procedure for a generic class of planar polygons, not necessarily convex, using directional discontinuities of
second derivatives of the covariogram. However, uniqueness fails for general non-convex polygons, as shown by
homometric examples ([2, 8]). For a survey of covariogram see [3].
    Schmitt’s genericity assumptions are formulated in terms of the edge conﬁguration and translation vectors
between vertices. In contrast, the interpoint distance distribution considered here is obtained by integrating
the covariogram over centered disks and hence loses the directional variable:

In general, however, ΨΩ does not determine the full covariogram. Thus our reconstruction problem asks whether
a generic polygon can still be recovered after this directional information has been integrated out.
    Another related line of research concerns the chord Minkowski problem introduced by Lutwak, Xi, Yang and
Zhang ([10]). They deﬁned chord measures as ﬁrst variations of the moment integrals Iq (Ω) given by (1.1), and
studied the associated Minkowski problems. We remark that, in the case of bodies in Rn , the chord Minkowski
problem prescribes a directional measure on S n−1 , whereas the reconstruction problem considered here uses
only the direction-averaged one-dimensional chord length distribution.
    The key steps of the proof are as follows. First, we recall the deﬁnition of the Riesz energy function, and
show the equivalence with the interpoint distance distribution. Next, application of Stokes’ theorem yields
a boundary integral representation of the Riesz energy for a compact body, expressed as a boundary Riesz
energy weighted by hνx , νy i, where ν denotes the unit outer normal vector [19]. Then, this is equivalent to the
ν-weighted boundary interpoint distance distribution Ψν;Γ , where Γ is the boundary of Ω. Thus the problem is
reduced to one-dimensional problem.


                                                         2
    This ν-weighted distribution is smooth near r = 0, but not on the half line {r ≥ 0}. The geometric data
of Γ such as lengths and angles can be obtained from the locations where the smoothness of Ψν;Γ breaks down
and the nature of these breakdowns.
    Under the genericity conditions, the regularity breaks down at the critical lengths. The ﬁrst derivative of Ψν;Γ
at r = 0 uniquely determines the perimeter; together with the recovered critical lengths and the dissociation
condition, this identiﬁes the side lengths. By computing the jumps (the diﬀerences between the left and right
limits) in the second and third derivatives at these critical lengths, we recover local geometric information, such
as the angles between the corresponding sides. The genericity condition is introduced precisely to prevent these
jump contributions from interfering with one another, ensuring that the geometric parameters can be recovered
unambiguously. The polygon is then reconstructed by matching the extracted side lengths and angles, using
the genericity conditions.
    Finally, we remark that this study is a continuation of the author’s recent research on spatial discrimination.
On the one hand, maximally symmetric spaces such as balls can be distinguished by geometric invariants like
Riesz energy functionals, thanks to the isoperimetric inequality ([16]); on the other hand, suﬃciently generic
(asymmetric) ﬁnite metric spaces are also distinguishable by invariants such as the magnitude ([17]) and the q-
spectrum ([18]). However, among spaces exhibiting intermediate symmetry, counterexamples exist that cannot
be distinguished by such invariants.

4    Reconstruction of generic polygonal domains
We give a theorem that includes an analogue of Waksman’s theorem. Recall C is a multiset of the critical
lengths, i.e. side lengths, diagonal lengths, and interior heights (Deﬁnition 3.6). Let A = [θ1 , . . . , θn ] be the
multiset of the signed exterior angles. We call Pj Hji (j = i − 2, i + 1) an adjacent exterior height if Hji 6∈ Ei .


                                                           18
Deﬁnition 4.1. A polygonal domain Ω is generic if
                  X
 (1) The equation     εi ci = 0 (εi = 0, ±1) does not have a non-trivial solution.
                       ci ∈C

      (This implies that the multiset C has no repeated elements, namely, it is in fact a set. )
 (2) No adjacent exterior height coincides with a critical length in C.
 (3) | tan θi | (θi ∈ A) are all distinct. (We agree that | tan(±π/2)| = ∞.)
   A multiset that satisﬁes the condition (1) is said to be a dissociated set.
Proposition 4.2. Fix n ≥ 3, and let Pn denote the set of ordered n-tuples
                                                                  n
                                          (P1 , . . . , Pn ) ∈ R2

which form a simple closed polygonal curve and for which no two adjacent sides are collinear. Then the set of
non-generic polygons is contained in a ﬁnite union of proper real-analytic subsets of Pn .
Sketch of proof. For each of conditions (1)–(3) in Deﬁnition 4.1, failure of the condition is described by ﬁnitely
many nontrivial real-analytic equations in the vertex coordinates. Hence the set of non-generic polygons is
contained in a ﬁnite union of proper real-analytic subsets of Pn .
Theorem 4.3. If a polygonal domain Ω is generic then it is determined up to isometry by any of the Riesz
energy function BΩ (z) and the interpoint distance distribution ΨΩ (r) of the domain, and the ν-weighted Riesz
energy function Bν; Γ (z) and the ν-weighted interpoint distance distribution Ψν; Γ (r) of the boundary Γ = ∂Ω.
Proof. Recall that BΩ (z), ΨΩ (r), Bν; Γ (z), Ψν; Γ (r) are equivalent by Theorem 2.1.
    First we determine the side lengths. By Proposition 3.21 and the condition (1) of genericity we obtain the
set of critical lengths C. By Corollary 3.5 we obtain the perimeter of Ω. Then by condition (1) of genericity the
set of side lengths is determined. Let it be S = {ℓλ }1≤λ≤n , which is the same as {ai }1≤i≤n as a set. At this
moment, we do not know the cyclic order of side lengths ℓλ in the conﬁguration of the polygon.
    Next we determine the set {tan θ1 , . . . , tan θn }. The condition (3) of genericity implies that ±π/2 can appear
in A at most once. Whether ±π/2 appears in A can be determined by whether Ψ′′ν;Γ diverges at some elements
of S. By relabeling the indices if necessary, we may assume these elements to be, for example, ℓ1 and ℓn . In
this case, by applying a reﬂection if necessary, we may assume that the angle is π/2.
    Deﬁne i(λ) ∈ {1, . . . , n} so that Ei(λ) is the side with length ℓλ (1 ≤ λ ≤ n). If Ψ′′ν;Γ does not blow up at
r = ℓλ , i.e. none of the exterior angles θi(λ)−1 , θi(λ) at the endpoints of the side Ei(λ) is equal to ±π/2, the pair
{tan θi(λ)−1 , tan θi(λ) } is determined up to sign by Proposition 3.22 (2). Fix the signs of tan θj ’s inductively as
follows.
    Let us start with λ = 1. If θi(n) = π/2 then the sign of tan θi(1) is ﬁxed by Proposition 3.22 (1), otherwise
choose either choice of sign of {tan θi(1)−1 , tan θi(1) }. Note that changing the sign of tan θi(1) can be realized
by reﬂection. Next, take the unique element λ′ ∈ {2, . . . , n} ({2, . . . , n − 1} if θi(n) = π/2) such that one of
| tan θi(λ′ )−1 | and | tan θi(λ′ ) | is equal to | tan θi(1) |. The uniqueness of λ′ is a consequence of the condition (3)
of genericity. Fix the sign of {tan θi(λ′ )−1 , tan θi(λ′ ) } so that it is consistent with the sign of tan θi(1) . Thus we
can inductively ﬁx all the signs of tan θj ’s and the cyclic order of ℓλ ’s.
    Finally, we resolve the π-ambiguity of θj as follows. If θi 6= π/2 then π/2 < |θi | < π if and only if
min{ai , ai+1 }| sin θi | appears in C. Indeed, if π/2 < |θi | < π and if min{ai , ai+1 } = ai , say, then the foot of
the perpendicular from Pi−1 to ℓi+1 lies in IntEi+1 , whereas if 0 < |θi | < π/2 then min{ai , ai+1 }| sin θi | is
an adjacent exterior height and hence is not an element of C. We remark that the genericity condition (2) is
imposed precisely to rule out the possibility that an angle with |θi | < π/2 is misidentiﬁed as π/2 < |θi | < π due
to an accidental coincidence between min{ai , ai+1 }| sin θi | and an unrelated critical length in C.
    Now the cyclic order of the side lengths and the signed exterior angles are determined, the polygonal domain
Ω can be reconstructed up to isometry.
