                                           1      Introduction
                                           A two-terminal graph is a graph G equipped with 2 vertices in V (G) called terminals. Let Tn,m be the
                                           set consisting of all two-terminal graphs on n vertices and m edges. Let G be in Tn,m and let p be in
                                           [0, 1]. The two-terminal reliability of G at p, denoted RG (p), is the probability that G has a path joining
                                           its terminals after each of its edges is independently removed with probability 1 − p. We say G is a
                                           uniformly most reliable two-terminal graph if RG (p) ≥ RH (p) for each H in Tn,m and for every p in [0, 1].
                                           We say G is a locally most reliable two-terminal graph (LMRTTG) if for each H in Tn,m there exists a
                                           positive real number δ such RG (p) ≥ RH (p) for each H in Tn,m and for every p in (0, δ).
                                                Independent works of Bertrand et al. [2] and Xie et al. [8] conclude that there is no uniformly most
                                                               graph on n vertices and m edges when n ≥ 11 and either 20 ≤ m ≤ 3n − 9 or
                                           reliable two-terminal
                                           3n − 5 ≤ m ≤ n2 − 2. Considering that for most cases uniformly most reliable two-terminal graphs
                                           do not exist, effort was carried out to characterize LMRTTGs. Bertrand et al. [2] determined the only
                                           LMRTTG in each Tn.m when n ≥ 4 and 5 ≤ m ≤ 3n − 6. More recently,            Gong and Lin [5] succeeded
                                           to determine the only LMRTTG in Tn,m when n ≥ 6, 2n − 3 ≤ m ≤ n2 and further, the first Zagreb
                                                                   1                   1                                   1         1
                                           index of the graphs Cn−2,m−2n+3      and Sn−2,m−2n+3    are different, where Cn,m    and Sn,m  stand for the
                                           quasi-complete and quasi-star graphs, respectively.
                                                The gap unresolved in earlier works is filled here. This article is organized as follows. Section 2
                                           includes graph theoretic concepts that are used throughout the document. Section 3 presents the concept
                                           of LMRTTGs, a well known methodology to construct such two-terminal graphs, and the progress in the
                                           determination of LMRTTGs. The main theorem of this article is presented in Section          4. This theorem
                                           asserts that there exists a unique LMRTTG in Tn,m when n ≥ 4 and 5 ≤ m ≤ n2 , that is called Gn,m .

2     Basic concepts
All graphs in this work are finite and undirected, with no loops nor multiple edges. Let G be a graph.
The complement of G is denoted G. The vertex set and edge set of G are denoted V (G) and E(G),
respectively. A subgraph H of G is a graph such that V (H) ⊆ V (G) and E(H) ⊆ E(G). A spanning
graph of G is a subgraph H of G such that V (H) = V (G). Two vertices vi and vj in G are adjacent
when vi vj ∈ E(G). A vertex v in G is universal (isolated) when v is adjacent to all (resp. none) of
the vertices in V (G) − v. A threshold graph is a graph that can be constructed from K1 by repeatedly
adding an isolated vertex or a universal vertex. Let Pn , Cn , and Kn be the path, the cycle, and the
complete graph on n vertices, respectively. The graph C3 is sometimes called the triangle. The number
of triangles, 3-paths, and 4-paths in G are denoted k3 (G), p3 (G), and p4 (G), respectively. For each v in
V (G) we denote dG (v) its degree in G; we will denote d(v) insteadP   of dG (v) when the graph G is clear
from context. The first Zagreb index of G, denoted M1 (G), equals v∈V (G) dG (v)2 . The second Zagreb
                                     P
index of G, denoted M2 (G), equals vi vj ∈E(G) dG (vi )dG (vj ). The union between two graphs G and H is
a graph whose vertex set is V (G) ∪ V (H) and whose edge set is E(G) ∪ E(H). For each positive integer
s we denote sG the graph consisting of a union of s disjoint copies of the graph G. The join G ∨ H
between the graphs G and H arises from G ∪ H by adding precisely one edge joining every vertex of G
to every vertex of H.
