Abstract.
    

We study numerical semigroups whose defining ideals admit determinantal presentations by the 2\times 2 minors of a 2\times n matrix. A conjecture of Cuong, Kien, Matsuoka, and Truong relates such presentations to arithmetic progressions of pseudo-Frobenius numbers. We prove a numerical criterion for this determinantal presentation, reducing the conjecture to a problem on factorizations of
maximal elements of an Apéry set in the numerical semigroup. As an application, we verify the conjecture in embedding dimension four under a unique factorization assumption on an Apéry set.
  



1. Introduction



In this paper, we study numerical semigroups whose defining ideals are generated by the 2\times 2 minors of a 2\times n matrix.
We begin by recalling some basic notation and background; we refer to [RGS] for general background on numerical semigroups. For positive integers a_{1},\dots,a_{n} with \gcd(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=1, let





\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle=\left\{\lambda_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}a_{n}\;\middle|\;\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right\}



be the numerical semigroup generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}.
Fix a field \mathbb{K}. If
H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
is minimally generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}, then the defining ideal I_{H} of the numerical semigroup ring \mathbb{K}[H] is the kernel of the homomorphism





\pi:\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}[H]=\mathbb{K}[t^{a_{1}},\dots,t^{a_{n}}],\qquad\pi(x_{i})=t^{a_{i}}.



Determining generators of the defining ideal of a numerical semigroup ring is a fundamental problem in the study of numerical semigroups; see, for example, [MS] for an overview of problems and recent developments.
A classical result of Herzog [He] describes the defining ideals of numerical semigroups of embedding dimension three. In particular, if H is generated by three elements and is not symmetric, then I_{H} is generated by the 2\times 2 minors of a 2\times 3 matrix.
Recently, Cuong, Kien, Matsuoka, and Truong proposed the following conjectural characterization of higher-dimensional numerical semigroups with such a determinantal defining ideal; see [KMO, Conjecture 1.1]. Recall that an integer
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\setminus H
is called a pseudo-Frobenius number of H if
w+a_{i}\in H
for every i. We denote by \mathrm{PF}(H) the set of pseudo-Frobenius numbers of H.
For a matrix M, we write I_{2}(M) for the ideal generated by its
2-minors.



Conjecture 1.1 (Cuong–Kien–Matsuoka–Truong).


Let n\geq 3, and let
H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup minimally generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}. Then the following conditions are equivalent.


(A) 


I_{H} is generated by the 2\times 2 minors of a
2\times n whose entries are homogeneous elements of positive degree.


(B) 


There exist positive integers c_{1},\dots,c_{n},d_{1},\dots,d_{n} such that




(1.1)

\displaystyle I_{H}=I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{c_{1}}&\cdots&x_{n-1}^{c_{n-1}}&x_{n}^{c_{n}}\\
x_{2}^{d_{2}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{d_{n}}&x_{1}^{d_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.




(C) 


\mathrm{PF}(H) forms an arithmetic progression of length n-1.






The implication (A)\Rightarrow(C) was proved in [KM]; hence the open part of the conjecture is basically the implication (C)\Rightarrow(B). The conjecture is known in several special cases, including almost symmetric numerical semigroups [GKMT], numerical semigroups whose embedding dimension is large relative to their multiplicity [KM, Ta], and stretched numerical semigroups [KMO].
Algebraic properties of numerical semigroup rings with determinantal defining ideals as in (1.1) have also studied in [KMN].



A difficulty in proving (C)\Rightarrow(B) is that, even after suitable candidates for the exponents
c_{1},\dots,c_{n},d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
have been identified, one still has to prove that the corresponding determinantal ideal is indeed the defining ideal I_{H}. Our first main result gives a numerical criterion that removes this difficulty.



To explain the result, let us recall a consequence of condition (B). It was proved in [KM, Theorem 3] that, if (1.1) holds, then





\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{h+\alpha,h+2\alpha,\dots,h+(n-1)\alpha\},



where





h=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(c_{i}-1)a_{i}\text{ and }\alpha=d_{i+1}a_{i+1}-c_{i}a_{i}\text{ for all
$i$},



where we consider d_{n+1}=d_{1} and a_{n+1}=a_{1}. Moreover, one obtains explicit numerical expressions of pseudo-Frobenius numbers in terms of the exponents c_{i} and d_{i}:




(1.2)

\displaystyle h+k\alpha=-(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})+\sum_{\ell=2}^{k+1}d_{\ell}a_{\ell}+\sum_{\ell=k+1}^{n}c_{\ell}a_{\ell}



for k=0,1,2,\dots,n.
Thus condition (B) imposes very explicit numerical identities on the pseudo-Frobenius numbers. A natural question is whether, conversely, these numerical identities are sufficient to recover the determinantal presentation of I_{H}. Our first main theorem shows that this is indeed the case.



Theorem 1.2.


Let
H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup minimally generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}, and assume that





\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{h+\alpha,h+2\alpha,\dots,h+(n-1)\alpha\}



for some h,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}, where \alpha\neq 0. Let
c_{1},\dots,c_{n},d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
be positive integers. Then the following conditions are equivalent.


(1) 


I_{H}=I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{c_{1}}&\cdots&x_{n-1}^{c_{n-1}}&x_{n}^{c_{n}}\\
x_{2}^{d_{2}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{d_{n}}&x_{1}^{d_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.


(2) 


The identities (1.2) hold for all k=0,1,2,\dots,n.






The significance of Theorem 1.2 is that it separates the numerical-semigroup-theoretic part of Conjecture 1.1 from the commutative-algebraic verification of the defining ideal.
In the proofs of the previously known cases of the conjecture [GKMT, KM, KMO, Ta], one first determines a candidate determinantal presentation and then proves separately that the resulting determinantal ideal coincides with I_{H}.
Theorem 1.2 shows that the latter step follows automatically once the appropriate numerical identities for the pseudo-Frobenius numbers have been established.



In the second part of the paper,
as an application of Theorem 1.2, we obtain a new partial affirmative result for Conjecture 1.1 in embedding dimension four.
For w\in H, we say that w has a unique factorization in H if there is a unique tuple
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
such that
w=k_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}a_{n}.
For a nonzero element a\in H, let





\mathrm{Ap}(H,a)=\{w\in H\mid w-a\notin H\}



be the Apéry set of H with respect to a.
We say that \mathrm{Ap}(H,a) has unique factorizations in H if every element of \mathrm{Ap}(H,a) has a unique factorization in H.
Our second main result is the following.



Theorem 1.3.


Conjecture 1.1 holds when n=4 and \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{i}) has unique factorizations in H for some i.




At first sight, the assumption that \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{i}) has unique factorizations may appear restrictive. However, among semigroups satisfying condition (B), this condition is fairly mild in embedding dimension four. Indeed, as shown in Lemma 2.9, if





I_{H}=I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{c_{1}}&x_{2}^{c_{2}}&x_{3}^{c_{3}}&x_{4}^{c_{4}}\\
x_{2}^{d_{2}}&x_{3}^{d_{3}}&x_{4}^{d_{4}}&x_{1}^{d_{1}}\end{pmatrix},



then \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) has unique factorizations if and only if





d_{2}\leq c_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{4}\leq d_{4}.



Thus the failure of the unique-factorization condition is governed by simple inequalities among the exponents. In this sense, assuming Conjecture 1.1, Theorem 1.3 applies to a substantial part of the embedding-dimension-four case.
We also note that, in the previously known cases of the conjecture [GKMT, KM, KMO, Ta], apart from Herzog’s classical three-generated case [He], at least half of the exponents c_{1},\dots,c_{n},d_{1},\dots,d_{n} are equal to 1. Our result does not require such a restriction.



This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we recall some basic facts on numerical semigroups,
Apéry sets, and Gröbner bases.
In Section 3, we establish Theorem 1.2, our numerical criterion
for determinantal presentations.
In Section 4, we study the embedding-dimension-four case and prove
Theorem 1.3.
We conclude with some remarks and examples.




2. Preliminary



In this section, we recall some basic facts about Apéry sets,
pseudo-Frobenius numbers, numerical semigroup rings, and Gröbner bases.



Throughout the paper for integer vectors v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{t}),u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{t})
we write v\geq u if v_{i}\geq u_{i} for all i.
Let \leq_{H} be the partial order on \mathbb{Z} defined by
a\leq_{H}b if b-a\in H.
We recall the following standard properties of Apéry sets;
see [RGS, Lemma 2.4 and Proposition 2.20].



Lemma 2.1.


Let H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle be the numerical semigroup
minimally generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}, and let 0\neq a\in H.
Then the following statements hold.


(1) 


a+\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{a+p\mid p\in\mathrm{PF}(H)\} is exactly the set of maximal
elements of \mathrm{Ap}(H,a) with respect to the partial order \leq_{H}.


(2) 


For each i=0,1,\dots,a-1, there is a unique element
w\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a) such that w\equiv i\pmod{a}.


(3) 


If w\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a) and u\leq_{H}w, then u\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a).






We next recall the algebraic interpretations of Apéry sets and
pseudo-Frobenius numbers.
Let H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle be a numerical semigroup
minimally generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}, and let
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] be the polynomial ring with grading
\deg x_{i}=a_{i} for all i.
The numerical semigroup ring of H over \mathbb{K} is





\mathbb{K}[H]=\mathbb{K}[t^{a_{1}},\dots,t^{a_{n}}]\subseteq\mathbb{K}[t].



Its defining ideal I_{H}\subseteq S is the kernel of the graded
homomorphism





\pi:S\longrightarrow\mathbb{K}[H]



defined by \pi(x_{i})=t^{a_{i}} for all i.
The following statement follows directly from the definition of an
Apéry set.



Lemma 2.2.


With the notation above,
x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}} is nonzero in S/(I_{H}+(x_{i}))
if and only f
w=k_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}a_{n}\in H belongs to \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{i}).




We also need the following fact.



Lemma 2.3 (see [KMO, Proposition 2.10]).


Let H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle be a numerical semigroup
generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}.
Let J be a graded ideal of S such that J\subseteq I_{H}.
If J+(x_{1})=I_{H}+(x_{1}), then J=I_{H}.




We now recall the relation between pseudo-Frobenius numbers and socles.
For a graded \mathbb{K}-algebra A with its graded maximal ideal \mathfrak{m}_{A},
its socle is





\mathrm{socle}(A)=\{f\in A\mid\mathfrak{m}_{A}f=0\}.



We will also use the following elementary observation.



Lemma 2.4.


Let I be a graded ideal of S such that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(S/I)<\infty.
For every nonzero element f of S/I, there is an element
m\in S such that
0\neq mf\in\mathrm{socle}(S/I).




Socles are closely related to pseudo-Frobenius numbers.
The following result may be viewed as an algebraic analogue of
Lemma 2.1(1).



Lemma 2.5 (see [HMR, §5.1.1]).


With the notation above, the set





\{t^{w}\mid w\in a_{i}+\mathrm{PF}(H)\}



forms a \mathbb{K}-basis of
\mathrm{socle}(\mathbb{K}[H]/(t^{a_{i}})).




We next recall some basic facts about Gröbner bases.
We refer the reader to [Ei, §15] and [HH, Chapter 2] for the basic theory of
Gröbner bases.
Let >_{\mathrm{rev}} be the weighted graded reverse lexicographic
order on S with x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}.
Thus, for monomials
u=x_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}} and
v=x_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\ell_{n}}, one has
u>_{\mathrm{rev}}v if and only if


(i) 


\deg u>\deg v, or


(ii) 


\deg u=\deg v and the leftmost nonzero entry of
(\ell_{1}-k_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}-k_{n}) is positive.



Here the grading on S is given by \deg x_{i}=a_{i}.
For a polynomial 0\neq f\in S and an ideal I\subset S, we write
\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f) and \mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I) for the initial monomial of f and the
initial ideal of I with respect to >_{\mathrm{rev}}, respectively.
A subset \mathcal{G}\subset I is called a Gröbner basis of
I if





\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I)=(\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{G}).





Lemma 2.6 (see [Ei, Proposition 15.12]).


For any graded ideal I of S, one has





\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I+(x_{1}))=\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I)+(x_{1}).






For a monomial ideal I of S, we write \mathcal{M}(I) for the set
of monomials in S that are not contained in I.



Lemma 2.7 (see [HH, Proposition 2.2.5]).


For any ideal I of S, the set \mathcal{M}(\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I)) forms an
\mathbb{K}-basis of S/I.




We will also use the following inequality comparing the socles of
S/I and S/\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I).



Lemma 2.8 (see [HH, Theorem 3.3.1]).


For any graded ideal I of S, one has





\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\big(\mathrm{socle}(S/I)\big)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\big(\mathrm{socle}(S/\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(I))\big).






We finally discuss when \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) has unique factorizations in H
in the case where I_{H} has the determinantal presentation
(1.1).
Assume that
H=\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\rangle
is the numerical semigroup minimally generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{4} and





I_{H}=I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{c_{1}}&x_{2}^{c_{2}}&x_{3}^{c_{3}}&x_{4}^{c_{4}}\\
x_{2}^{d_{2}}&x_{3}^{d_{3}}&x_{4}^{d_{4}}&x_{1}^{d_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.



As follows from (1.2), we have





\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{p_{1}=h+\alpha,p_{2}=h+2\alpha,p_{3}=h+3\alpha\},



where





\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{1}&=h+\alpha=-a_{1}+(c_{2}+d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(c_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}-1)a_{4},\\
p_{2}&=h+2\alpha=-a_{1}+(d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(c_{3}+d_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}-1)a_{4},\\
p_{3}&=h+3\alpha=-a_{1}+(d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(d_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}+d_{4}-1)a_{4}.\end{array}





Lemma 2.9.


With the same notation as above, one has


(1) 


a_{1}+p_{1} has a unique factorization in H if and only if
c_{4}\leq d_{4}.


(2) 


a_{1}+p_{2} has a unique factorization in H if and only if
c_{4}\leq d_{4} or d_{2}\leq c_{2}.


(3) 


a_{1}+p_{3} has a unique factorization in H if and only if
d_{2}\leq c_{2}.



In particular, \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) has unique factorizations in H if and
only if c_{4}\leq d_{4} and d_{2}\leq c_{2}.




Proof.


Recall that an element w\in H belongs to \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) if and only if
it has no factorization of the form
w=k_{1}a_{1}+k_{2}a_{2}+k_{3}a_{3}+k_{4}a_{4}
with k_{1},\dots,k_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} and k_{1}>0.
Modulo (x_{1}), the only generator of I_{H}+(x_{1}) that can identify
two nonzero monomials is





x_{3}^{c_{3}+d_{3}}-x_{2}^{c_{2}}x_{4}^{d_{4}}.



Indeed, all the other 2\times 2 minors reduce modulo (x_{1}) to
monomials, which vanish in S/(I_{H}+(x_{1})).
Hence, if





w=k_{2}a_{2}+k_{3}a_{3}+k_{4}a_{4}\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}),



then the monomial x_{2}^{k_{2}}x_{3}^{k_{3}}x_{4}^{k_{4}} admits another
monomial representative of the same H-degree if and only if it is
divisible by either x_{3}^{c_{3}+d_{3}} or x_{2}^{c_{2}}x_{4}^{d_{4}}.
Equivalently, w has more than one factorization in H if and only if
k_{3}\geq c_{3}+d_{3} or (k_{2},k_{4})\geq(c_{2},d_{4}).
Applying this criterion to (2), we obtain
statements (1)–(3).



Finally, \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) has unique factorizations in H if and only if
its maximal elements a_{1}+p_{1},a_{1}+p_{2},a_{1}+p_{3} have unique
factorizations in H.
Indeed, if w\leq_{H}v and w has two distinct factorizations, then
any factorization of v-w extends them to two distinct factorizations
of v.
∎





3. Proof of Theorem 1.2



In this section, after establishing a few preliminary lemmas, we prove
our first main theorem.
Throughout this section, let
H=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle be a numerical semigroup minimally
generated by a_{1},\dots,a_{n}, and let
S=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}] with \deg x_{i}=a_{i}.
We also fix positive integers
c_{1},\dots,c_{n},d_{1},\dots,d_{n} and write





J=I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{c_{1}}&\cdots&x_{n-1}^{c_{n-1}}&x_{n}^{c_{n}}\\
x_{2}^{d_{2}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{d_{n}}&x_{1}^{d_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.



To simplify notation, we set
x_{n+1}=x_{1}, c_{n+1}=c_{1}, and d_{n+1}=d_{1}.
We note that J is homogeneous with respect to the grading
\deg x_{i}=a_{i} if and only if
c_{i}a_{i}-d_{i+1}a_{i+1} is independent of i.



Lemma 3.1.


If J is a graded ideal of S, then the set





\mathcal{G}=\{x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j}^{c_{j}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}



is a Gröbner basis of J with respect to >_{\mathrm{rev}}.




Proof.


We use Buchberger’s criterion (see [HH, Theorem 2.3.2]).
We write
f\xrightarrow{g}h
if h is obtained from f by a single polynomial reduction using g,
and
f\xrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}h
if f reduces to h by a finite sequence of polynomial reductions
using elements of \mathcal{G}.
For 1\leq i<j\leq n, let





f_{i,j}=x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j}^{c_{j}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}.



Thus \mathcal{G}=\{f_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\}.
It suffices to prove that





S(f_{i,j},f_{k,\ell})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0



for all 1\leq i<j\leq n and 1\leq k<\ell\leq n.
To prove this we may assume i\leq k.



Since each f_{i,j} is homogeneous by our assumption,
we have





\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f_{i,j})=x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j}^{c_{j}}.



If \mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f_{i,j}) and \mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f_{k,\ell}) are relatively prime then S(f_{i,j},f_{k,\ell}) reduces to 0 (see [HH, Lemma 2.3.1]).
Under the assumption i\leq k, if
\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f_{i,j}) and \mathop{\mathrm{in}}(f_{k,\ell}) are not relatively prime,
then either j=\ell, i=k, or k=j-1.
Hence it suffices to consider the following cases.



Case (a). Suppose that j=\ell or i=k.
If j=\ell,
then we have





\displaystyle S(f_{i,j},f_{k,j})=x_{k+1}^{d_{k+1}}f_{i,j}-x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}f_{k,j}=x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}\left(x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{k}^{c_{k}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{k+1}^{d_{k+1}}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{i,k}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0



as desired.
The case i=k is similar.
In this case we may assume j<\ell by exchanging the ordering of f_{i,j} and f_{i,\ell} if necessary,
and we have





S(f_{i,j},f_{i,\ell})=-x_{i}^{c_{i}}(x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}x_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}}-x_{j}^{c_{j}}x_{\ell+1}^{d_{\ell+1}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{j,\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.





Case (b). Suppose k=j-1.
By Case (a) we may assume i<j-1 and j<\ell.
Assume c_{j}\geq d_{j}.
In this case we have





S(f_{i,j},f_{k,\ell})=x_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}}f_{i,j}-x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j}^{c_{j}-d_{j}}f_{j-1,\ell}=x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j-1}^{c_{j-1}}x_{j}^{c_{j}-d_{j}}x_{\ell+1}^{d_{\ell+1}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}x_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}}.



Observe that f_{j,\ell}=x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}x_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}}-x_{j}^{c_{j}}x_{\ell+1}^{d_{\ell+1}}
and
f_{i,j-1}=x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j-1}^{c_{j-1}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{j}^{d_{j}}.
One can see





\displaystyle S(f_{i,j},f_{k,\ell})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{i,j-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{j}^{d_{j}}x_{j}^{c_{j}-d_{j}}x_{\ell+1}^{d_{\ell+1}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}}x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{j,\ell}}}{{\longrightarrow}}0



as desired.
The case c_{j}<d_{j} is treated similarly. Indeed, in this case, we have





S(f_{i,j},f_{k,\ell})=x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}x_{j-1}^{c_{j-1}}x_{\ell+1}^{d_{\ell+1}}-x_{i}^{c_{i}}x_{j}^{d_{j}-c_{j}}x_{j+1}^{d_{j+1}}x_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}},



and it is easy to see that this again reduces to zero, first by f_{i,j-1} and then by f_{j,\ell}.
∎




We next use Lemma 3.1 to describe a \mathbb{K}-basis of
S/(J+(x_{1})).
Let \mathrm{Mon}(S) be the set of all monomials of S.
For monomials m_{1},\dots,m_{t}\in S,
we write





\langle m_{1},\dots,m_{t}\rangle=\{m\in\mathrm{Mon}(S)\mid m\mbox{ divides }m_{k}\mbox{ for some }k\}.



For k=2,3,\dots,n, we define





u_{k}=\left(\prod_{2\leq i\leq k}x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)x_{k}\left(\prod_{k\leq j\leq n}x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\right).





Lemma 3.2.


With the same notation as in Lemma 3.1, one has





\mathcal{M}\big(\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))\big)=\langle u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\rangle.






Proof.


By Lemmas 2.6 and 3.1,





\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))=(x_{1})+(x_{j}^{c_{j}}x_{i+1}^{d_{i+1}}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n).



It is immediate from the definition that none of
u_{2},\dots,u_{n} belongs to \mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1})).
Hence





\langle u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))).





For the reverse inclusion, let
m=x_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots x_{n}^{k_{n}}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))).
If k_{i}<d_{i} for every i=2,\dots,n, then m divides u_{n}.
Otherwise, let \ell be the smallest integer such that k_{\ell}\geq d_{\ell}.
Then





k_{i}\leq d_{i}-1\qquad(2\leq i<\ell).



Since x_{\ell}^{d_{\ell}}x_{j}^{c_{j}}\in\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J)
for j=\ell,\dots,n,
we also have





k_{\ell}\leq d_{\ell}+c_{\ell}-1\quad\text{and}\quad k_{j}\leq c_{j}-1\qquad(\ell<j\leq n).



Therefore m divides u_{\ell}.
∎




Lemma 3.3.


With the same notation as in Lemma 3.1, the set





\mathcal{B}=\{u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\}



is a \mathbb{K}-basis of \mathrm{socle}\big(S/(J+(x_{1}))\big).




Proof.


It follows from Lemma 3.2 that
\mathcal{B} is the set of maximal monomials in
\mathcal{M}(\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))), and hence forms a \mathbb{K}-basis of
\mathrm{socle}(S/\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))).
By Lemmas 2.7 and 3.2,
the set \mathcal{B} is linearly independent in S/(J+(x_{1})).
Moreover, Lemma 2.8 gives





\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{socle}\big(S/(J+(x_{1}))\big)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\mathrm{socle}\big(S/\mathop{\mathrm{in}}(J+(x_{1}))\big)=|\mathcal{B}|.



Thus, it remains only to prove that every u_{k} belongs to \mathrm{socle}\big(S/(J+(x_{1}))\big).



Fix 2\leq k,\ell\leq n.
We claim x_{\ell}u_{k}=0 in S/(J+(x_{1})).
For this purpose, we show that




(3.1)

\displaystyle x_{s}\left(\prod_{2\leq i\leq s}x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)x_{t}\left(\prod_{t\leq j\leq n}x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\right)\equiv 0\ \ \mbox{ mod }J+(x_{1})



for all s,t with 2\leq s\leq t\leq n.
The desired equality x_{\ell}u_{k}=0 follows by taking
(s,t)=(\ell,k) if \ell\leq k, and
(s,t)=(k,\ell) if \ell\geq k.



We prove (3.1) by induction on s.
If s=2 then the assertion follows from





x_{s}\!\left(\prod_{2\leq i\leq s}\!x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\!\right)\!x_{t}\!\left(\prod_{t\leq j\leq n}\!x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\!\right)\!\equiv\!x_{1}^{c_{1}}x_{t+1}^{d_{t+1}}\!\left(\!\prod_{t<j\leq n}\!x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\!\right)\!\equiv\!0\pmod{J+(x_{1})}.



The case t=n is similar.
Suppose s>2 and t<n.
Then





x_{s}\left(\prod_{2\leq i\leq s}x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)x_{t}\left(\prod_{t\leq j\leq n}x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\right)\equiv x_{s-1}^{c_{s-1}}\left(\prod_{2\leq i<s}x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)x_{t+1}^{d_{t+1}}\left(\prod_{t<j\leq n}x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\right)



mod J since x_{s}^{d_{s}}x_{t}^{c_{t}}-x_{s-1}^{c_{s-1}}x_{t+1}^{d_{t+1}}\in J.
The monomial on the right-hand side is equal to





x_{s-1}^{c_{s-1}-1}x_{t+1}^{d_{t+1}-1}\left[x_{s-1}\left(\prod_{2\leq i\leq s-1}x_{i}^{d_{i}-1}\right)x_{t+1}\left(\prod_{t+1\leq j\leq n}x_{j}^{c_{j}-1}\right)\right].



The expression in brackets belongs to J+(x_{1}) by the induction
hypothesis, and hence so does the right-hand side.
∎




We now prove our main theorem.



Proof of Theorem 1.2.


As explained in the introduction, the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2) follows from [KM, Theorem 3].
We prove (2)\Rightarrow(1).
Let





J=I_{2}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{c_{1}}&x_{2}^{c_{2}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{c_{n}}\\
x_{2}^{d_{2}}&x_{3}^{d_{3}}&\cdots&x_{1}^{d_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.



We will prove that J=I_{H}.



By (1.2),
d_{i+1}a_{i+1}-c_{i}a_{i}=\alpha
for all i.
Hence J is a graded ideal of S and J\subseteq I_{H}.
By Lemma 2.3, it is therefore enough to show that
J+(x_{1})=I_{H}+(x_{1}).
Consider the natural surjection





\psi:S/(J+(x_{1}))\longrightarrow S/(I_{H}+(x_{1})).



By assumption, the identities (1.2) hold.
A direct computation from the definition of u_{k} gives





\deg u_{k}=a_{1}+h+(k-1)\alpha\qquad(k=2,\dots,n).



Therefore, under the natural isomorphism
S/I_{H}\cong\mathbb{K}[H],
the set
\mathcal{B}=\{u_{2},\dots,u_{n}\}
maps onto
\{t^{a}\mid a\in a_{1}+\mathrm{PF}(H)\}.
By Lemma 2.5, \mathcal{B} is a \mathbb{K}-basis of
\mathrm{socle}(S/(I_{H}+(x_{1}))).
By Lemma 3.3, \mathcal{B} is also a \mathbb{K}-basis of
\mathrm{socle}\big(S/(J+(x_{1}))\big).
Hence \psi induces an isomorphism
from
\mathrm{socle}\big(S/(J+(x_{1}))\big)
to
\mathrm{socle}\big(S/(I_{H}+(x_{1}))\big).



We claim that \psi is injective.
Suppose, to the contrary, that
0\neq f\in\ker\psi.
By Lemma 3.2, the quotient S/(J+(x_{1})) is
finite-dimensional over \mathbb{K}.
Then, by Lemma 2.4, there exists m\in S such that
0\neq mf\in\mathrm{socle}\big(S/(J+(x_{1}))\big).
However,
\psi(mf)=m\psi(f)=0,
contradicting the injectivity of \psi on the socle.
Thus \psi is injective, and hence
J+(x_{1})=I_{H}+(x_{1}).
Lemma 2.3 now gives J=I_{H}.
∎





4. Proof of the second main result



In this section, we prove our second main theorem.
Throughout this section, let





H=\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}\rangle



be the numerical semigroup minimally generated by
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}, and assume that





\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{p_{1}=h+\alpha,p_{2}=h+2\alpha,p_{3}=h+3\alpha\}



for some h,\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}.
We note that h\in H; see [KM, Lemma 2].
We also note that the roles of p_{1} and p_{3} can be exchanged by
replacing \alpha with -\alpha.
Indeed, setting h^{\prime}=h+4\alpha, we have
p_{3}=h^{\prime}+(-\alpha),
p_{2}=h^{\prime}+2(-\alpha),
and p_{1}=h^{\prime}+3(-\alpha).
For every w\in H, we define





h_{i}(w)=\max\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid w-ka_{i}\in H\}.



If w\in H has a unique factorization in H, then





w=h_{1}(w)a_{1}+h_{2}(w)a_{2}+h_{3}(w)a_{3}+h_{4}(w)a_{4}.



For simplicity, throughout this section we write





\mathrm{Ap}=\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}).



Also, for w\in\mathbb{Z}, let \overline{w} denote the unique element of
\mathrm{Ap} satisfying





\overline{w}\equiv w\pmod{a_{1}};



see Lemma 2.1(2).
Let





p^{+}_{1}=a_{1}+p_{1},\ p^{+}_{2}=a_{1}+p_{2},\text{ and }p^{+}_{3}=a_{1}+p_{3}.



By Lemma 2.1(1),
p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+},p_{3}^{+} are the maximal elements of \mathrm{Ap}
with respect to \leq_{H}.
In particular, for every w\in\mathrm{Ap}, there exists
i\in\{1,2,3\} such that w\leq_{H}p_{i}^{+}.



Lemma 4.1.


Let w\in\mathrm{Ap} and i\in\{2,3,4\} such that h_{i}(w)=0, and set
u=\overline{w-a_{i}}.
Then the following statements hold.


(1) 


If w-\alpha\in\mathrm{Ap} and h_{i}(w-\alpha)>0, then





u\not\leq_{H}p_{2}^{+},\ u\not\leq_{H}p_{3}^{+},\ u\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}-a_{i},\text{ and }u\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}.




(2) 


If w+\alpha\in\mathrm{Ap} and h_{i}(w+\alpha)>0, then





u\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+},\ u\not\leq_{H}p_{2}^{+},\ u\not\leq_{H}p_{3}^{+}-a_{i},\text{ and }u\leq_{H}p_{3}^{+}.








Proof.


Statement (2) follows from statement (1) by the symmetry obtained by
replacing \alpha with -\alpha and exchanging the roles of
p_{1} and p_{3}.
Thus it suffices to prove (1).
Since w-a_{i}\notin H and u is the smallest element of H
congruent to w-a_{i} modulo a_{1}, we have
u>w-a_{i}.
Since u\equiv w-a_{i}\pmod{a_{1}}, we have


(i) 


(w-\alpha-a_{i})+(p_{2}^{+}-u)\equiv p_{1}^{+}\pmod{a_{1}} and (w-\alpha-a_{i})+(p_{2}^{+}-u)<p_{1}^{+}.
Since p_{1}^{+}\in\mathrm{Ap}, Lemma 2.1(2) implies that no
smaller integer congruent to p_{1}^{+} modulo a_{1} belongs to H.
Hence (w-\alpha-a_{i})+(p_{2}^{+}-u)\not\in H.
Since w-\alpha-a_{i}\in H, it follows that p_{2}^{+}-u\not\in H.


(ii) 


(w-\alpha-a_{i})+(p_{3}^{+}-u)\equiv p_{2}^{+}\pmod{a_{1}} and (w-\alpha-a_{i})+(p_{3}^{+}-u)<p_{2}^{+}. Arguing as in (i), we have (w-\alpha-a_{i})+(p_{3}^{+}-u)\not\in H and hence (p_{3}^{+}-u)\not\in H.


(iii) 


w+(p_{1}^{+}-a_{i}-u)\equiv p_{1}^{+}\pmod{a_{1}} and w+(p_{1}^{+}-a_{i}-u)<p_{1}^{+}.
Hence w+(p_{1}^{+}-a_{i}-u)\not\in H.
Since w\in\mathrm{Ap}\subset H, it follows that (p_{1}^{+}-a_{i}-u)\not\in H.



Statements (i)–(iii) show that u\not\leq_{H}p_{2}^{+}, u\not\leq_{H}p_{3}^{+} and u\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}-a_{i}.
Since u\leq_{H}p_{j}^{+} for some j,
we must have u\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}.
∎




Corollary 4.2.


Let w\in\mathrm{Ap} and i\in\{2,3,4\}.
If w-\alpha,w+\alpha\in\mathrm{Ap}, then





h_{i}(w)\geq\min\{h_{i}(w-\alpha),h_{i}(w+\alpha)\}.






Proof.


Let h=h_{i}(w).
Suppose, to the contrary, that





h<\min\{h_{i}(w-\alpha),h_{i}(w+\alpha)\}.



By the definition of h and Lemma 2.1(3),
w-\alpha-ha_{i},w-ha_{i},w+\alpha-ha_{i} are all elements of \mathrm{Ap}.
Also,
h_{i}(w-\alpha-ha_{i})>0, h_{i}(w+\alpha-ha_{i})>0 and h_{i}(w-ha_{i})=0.
Set u=\overline{(w-ha_{i}-a_{i})}.
Applying Lemma 4.1 to w-ha_{i}, we obtain u\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}, u\not\leq_{H}p_{2}^{+} and u\not\leq_{H}p_{3}^{+}.
This contradicts the maximality statement in
Lemma 2.1(1).
∎




Proof of Theorem 1.3



We now prove Theorem 1.3.
After relabeling the generators if necessary, we may assume that
\mathrm{Ap}=\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) has unique factorizations in H.
We write





\displaystyle p_{1}^{+}
\displaystyle=z_{12}a_{2}+z_{13}a_{3}+z_{14}a_{4}




\displaystyle p_{2}^{+}
\displaystyle=z_{22}a_{2}+z_{23}a_{3}+z_{24}a_{4}




\displaystyle p_{3}^{+}
\displaystyle=z_{32}a_{2}+z_{33}a_{3}+z_{34}a_{4}



with z_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.
We note that z_{ij}=h_{j}(p_{i}^{+}).
We need the following technical lemma.



Lemma 4.3.



(1) 


If z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32}, z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}, and z_{13}<z_{23},
then
\alpha\equiv(z_{22}+1)a_{2}\pmod{a_{1}} and z_{22}=z_{32}.


(2) 


If z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32}, z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}, and z_{33}<z_{23},
then
-\alpha\equiv(z_{24}+1)a_{4}\pmod{a_{1}} and z_{24}=z_{14}.






Proof.


The statement (2) is equivalent to the statement (1) if we replace \alpha with -\alpha and exchange a_{2} and a_{4}.
Hence it suffices to prove (1).



Consider the set





X=\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{\geq 0}\mid s\leq z_{13},t\leq z_{14}\}.



For each (s,t)\in X, the elements





p^{+}_{1}-(z_{22}a_{2}+sa_{3}+ta_{4})\text{ and }p^{+}_{2}-(z_{22}a_{2}+sa_{3}+ta_{4})



belong to \mathrm{Ap} by Lemma 2.1(3).
Since p^{+}_{1}=p^{+}_{2}-\alpha and h_{2}\big(p^{+}_{2}-(z_{22}a_{2}+sa_{3}+ta_{4})\big)=0,
by Lemma 4.1(1) if we set




(4.1)

\displaystyle u_{s,t}=\overline{p_{2}^{+}-((z_{22}+1)a_{2}+sa_{3}+ta_{4})},



then
u_{s,t}\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+} and u_{s,t}\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}-a_{2}.
Since u_{s,t}\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+} and both u_{s,t} and p_{1}^{+}
have unique factorizations, the coefficients in the factorization of
u_{s,t} are bounded above by those of p_{1}^{+}.
Moreover, u_{s,t}\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}-a_{2} forces the coefficient
of a_{2} to be z_{12}.
Hence





u_{s,t}=z_{12}a_{2}+\theta_{s,t}a_{3}+\eta_{s,t}a_{4}



for some (\theta_{s,t},\eta_{s,t})\in X.
We claim
u_{z_{13},z_{14}}=z_{12}a_{2}.



We first prove





\theta_{z_{13},t}=0\qquad\text{for all }t=0,1,\dots,z_{14}.



Let s<z_{13}. By the definition of u_{s,t},





u_{s+1,t}+a_{3}\equiv u_{s,t}\pmod{a_{1}},



and hence





(\theta_{s+1,t}+1)a_{3}+\eta_{s+1,t}a_{4}\equiv\theta_{s,t}a_{3}+\eta_{s,t}a_{4}\pmod{a_{1}}.



The right-hand side belongs to \mathrm{Ap}, since it is less than or equal
to p_{1}^{+} with respect to \leq_{H}. The left-hand side also belongs
to \mathrm{Ap}, since
\theta_{s+1,t}+1\leq z_{13}+1\leq z_{23}
and
\eta_{s+1,t}\leq z_{14}\leq z_{24}.
Thus the two sides are equal. Since \mathrm{Ap} has unique factorizations,
we obtain
\theta_{s,t}=\theta_{s+1,t}+1 and
\eta_{s,t}=\eta_{s+1,t}.
Consequently, we have





\theta_{z_{13},t}=\theta_{z_{13}-1,t}-1=\cdots=\theta_{0,t}-z_{13}.



Since 0\leq\theta_{0,t}\leq z_{13},
we get
\theta_{z_{13},t}=0 as desired.



We next determine \eta_{z_{13},z_{14}}.
Since \theta_{z_{13},t}=0 for every t, we have
u_{z_{13},t}=z_{12}a_{2}+\eta_{z_{13},t}a_{4}.
Let t<z_{14}. By the definition of u_{s,t},
we have u_{z_{13},t+1}+a_{4}\equiv u_{z_{13},t}\pmod{a_{1}}.
Hence





(\eta_{z_{13},t+1}+1)a_{4}\equiv\eta_{z_{13},t}a_{4}\pmod{a_{1}}.



Since
\eta_{z_{13},t+1}+1\leq z_{14}+1\leq z_{34},
both
(\eta_{z_{13},t+1}+1)a_{4}
and \eta_{z_{13},t}a_{4}
are less than or equal to p^{+}_{3} with respect to \leq_{H}.
Therefore they must be equal and we obtain





\eta_{z_{13},t}=\eta_{z_{13},t+1}+1\text{ for }t<z_{14}.



But since 0\leq\eta_{z_{13},t}\leq z_{14} the above equality guarantees
\eta_{z_{13},z_{14}}=0,
and hence
u_{z_{13},z_{14}}=z_{12}a_{2}
as desired.



We now complete the proof.
Recall u_{s,t}=\overline{(p_{2}^{+}-(z_{22}+1)a_{2}-sa_{3}-ta_{4})}. Then





p^{+}_{1}=u_{z_{13},z_{14}}+z_{13}a_{3}+z_{14}a_{4}\equiv p_{2}^{+}-(z_{22}+1)a_{2}\pmod{a_{1}}.



Since p_{2}^{+}=p_{1}^{+}+\alpha this proves the first statement
\alpha\equiv(z_{22}+1)a_{2}\pmod{a_{1}}.
Next, we prove z_{22}=z_{32}.
If z_{22}>z_{32}, then





p^{+}_{2}-z_{32}a_{2}=p^{+}_{3}-z_{32}a_{2}-\alpha\text{ and }p^{+}_{3}-z_{32}a_{2}



are elements of \mathrm{Ap} and h_{2}(p^{+}_{3}-z_{32}a_{2})=0.
Hence by Lemma 4.1(1) we have
\overline{p^{+}_{3}-(z_{32}+1)a_{2}}\leq_{H}p^{+}_{1} and
\overline{p^{+}_{3}-(z_{32}+1)a_{2}}\not\leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}-a_{2}.
Such an element must be of the form





z_{12}a_{2}+sa_{3}+ta_{4}



for some (s,t)\in X.
This implies





\displaystyle p_{2}^{+}
\displaystyle\equiv p_{3}^{+}-\alpha





\displaystyle\equiv p_{3}^{+}-(z_{22}+1)a_{2}





\displaystyle\equiv\bigl(z_{12}-(z_{22}-z_{32})\bigr)a_{2}+sa_{3}+ta_{4}\pmod{a_{1}}.



The last expression must belong to \mathrm{Ap}
since it is \leq_{H}p_{1}^{+}.
But this means that this element equals p^{+}_{2} since \mathrm{Ap} contains exactly one element in its congruence class modulo a_{1},
which contradicts the maximality of p_{2}^{+}.
Hence we have z_{22}=z_{32}.
∎




We now use Theorem 1.2 to prove that I_{H} is generated
by 2\times 2 minors.
Since p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+},p_{3}^{+} are pairwise incomparable
with respect to \leq_{H},
we have z_{1j}>z_{2j} for some j.
After relabeling a_{2},a_{3},a_{4} if necessary, we may assume that
z_{12}>z_{22}.
Then by Corollary 4.2 we have z_{22}\geq z_{32}.
Since p^{+}_{2} and p^{+}_{3} are incomparable with respect to \leq_{H},
we have z_{33}>z_{23} or z_{34}>z_{24}.
By exchanging a_{3} and a_{4} if necessary,
we may assume z_{34}>z_{24}.
In particular, in this setting we have z_{14}\leq z_{24} by Corollary 4.2.
To summarize, we can assume




(4.2)

\displaystyle z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32}\text{ and }z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}.





[Step 1]
We prove that the case
z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33} cannot occur.
Suppose z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33}.
Since p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}, and p_{3}^{+} are incomparable with respect to \leq_{H},
we must have





z_{12}>z_{22}>z_{32},\ \ z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33},\text{ and }z_{14}<z_{24}<z_{34}.



Let \alpha_{3}=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid ka_{3}\in\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{4}\rangle\} and assume that \alpha_{3}a_{3}=\tau_{1}a_{1}+\tau_{2}a_{2}+\tau_{4}a_{4} for some \tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.
Since p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+},p_{3}^{+} have unique factorizations in H,
we must have z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33}<\alpha_{3}.
We actually have





z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33}=\alpha_{3}-1.



Indeed, since (\alpha_{3}-1)a_{3}\not\in\langle a_{1},a_{2},a_{4}\rangle, we must have (\alpha_{3}-1)a_{3}\in\mathrm{Ap}=\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}).
Hence (\alpha_{3}-1)a_{3}\leq_{H}p^{+}_{i} for some i,
which proves z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33}\geq\alpha_{3}-1.



Since p_{1},p_{3}\in\mathrm{PF}(H),
p_{1}+a_{4}\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{4}) and p_{3}+a_{2}\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{2}). Thus





\displaystyle p_{1}^{+}-a_{1}+a_{4}
\displaystyle=p_{1}+a_{4}=k_{1}a_{1}+k_{2}a_{2}+k_{3}a_{3},




\displaystyle p_{3}^{+}-a_{1}+a_{2}
\displaystyle=p_{3}+a_{2}=k^{\prime}_{1}a_{1}+k^{\prime}_{3}a_{3}+k^{\prime}_{4}a_{4}



for some k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{1}^{\prime},k_{3}^{\prime},k_{4}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.
Using the relation \alpha_{3}a_{3}=\tau_{1}a_{1}+\tau_{2}a_{2}+\tau_{4}a_{4},
we can take such integers with k_{3}<\alpha_{3} and k_{3}^{\prime}<\alpha_{3}.
Substituting p_{1}^{+}=z_{12}a_{2}+z_{13}a_{3}+z_{14}a_{4} and p_{3}^{+}=z_{32}a_{2}+z_{33}a_{3}+z_{34}a_{4} into the above equations, we have




(4.3)

\displaystyle(z_{12}-k_{2})a_{2}+(z_{13}-k_{3})a_{3}+(z_{14}+1)a_{4}=(k_{1}+1)a_{1}



(4.4)

\displaystyle(z_{32}+1)a_{2}+(z_{33}-k^{\prime}_{3})a_{3}+(z_{34}-k_{4}^{\prime})a_{4}=(k^{\prime}_{1}+1)a_{1}.



If (z_{12}-k_{2},z_{13}-k_{3},z_{14}+1)\leq(z_{22},z_{23},z_{24}) then we can rewrite p_{2}^{+}=z_{22}a_{2}+z_{23}a_{3}+z_{24}a_{4} in the form





p_{2}^{+}=(k_{1}+1)a_{1}+\ell_{2}a_{2}+\ell_{3}a_{3}+\ell_{4}a_{4}



with \ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}, which contradicts p_{2}^{+}\in\mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}).
Since z_{13}-k_{3}\leq z_{23} and
z_{14}+1\leq z_{24}, it follows that z_{12}-k_{2}>z_{22}.
Similarly, by comparing (z_{32}+1,z_{33}-k_{3}^{\prime},z_{34}-k_{4}^{\prime}) and (z_{22},z_{23}=z_{33},z_{24}) we have z_{34}-k_{4}^{\prime}>z_{24}.
Hence




(4.5)

\displaystyle z_{12}-k_{2}>z_{22}\geq z_{32}+1\text{ and }z_{34}-k_{4}^{\prime}>z_{24}\geq z_{14}+1.



By taking the difference between (4.3) and (4.4) we have




(4.6)

\displaystyle\big((z_{12}\!-\!k_{2})\!-\!(z_{32}\!+\!1)\big)a_{2}\!+\!(k_{3}^{\prime}\!-\!k_{3})a_{3}\!=\!(k_{1}\!-\!k_{1}^{\prime})a_{1}\!+\!\big((z_{34}\!-\!k_{4}^{\prime})\!-\!(z_{14}\!+\!1)\big)a_{4}.



We conclude a contradiction using (4.5) and (4.6).
Observe that |k_{3}^{\prime}-k_{3}|<\alpha_{3}.
Since ((z_{12}-k_{2})-(z_{32}+1),k_{3}^{\prime}-k_{3})\leq(z_{12},z_{13}=\alpha_{3}-1),
if k_{1}-k_{1}^{\prime}\geq 0, then (4.5) and (4.6)
give two distinct factorizations of p_{1}^{+}, a contradiction (note that k_{3}^{\prime}-k_{3} could be negative).
On the other hand,
since (k_{3}-k^{\prime}_{3},(z_{34}-k^{\prime}_{4})-(z_{14}+1))\leq(z_{33}=\alpha_{3}-1,z_{34}),
if k_{1}^{\prime}-k_{1}\geq 0, then (4.5) and (4.6)
give two distinct factorizations of p_{3}^{+}, again a contradiction.
This proves that z_{13}=z_{23}=z_{33} cannot occur.



[Step 2]
We next reduce the proof to the case where




(4.7)

\displaystyle z_{12}>z_{22}=z_{32},\ z_{13}<z_{23}>z_{33},\text{ and }z_{14}=z_{24}<z_{34}.



We proved in Step 1 that z_{13},z_{23},z_{33} are not all equal.
Since replacing \alpha with -\alpha and simultaneously
exchanging a_{2} and a_{4} preserves (4.2) while exchanging
the roles of z_{13} and z_{33}, we may assume
z_{13}\neq z_{23}.
We prove that we can assume z_{13}<z_{23}.



Suppose that z_{13}>z_{23}. Then by Corollary 4.2
we have z_{13}>z_{23}\geq z_{33}
and therefore





z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32},\ z_{13}>z_{23}\geq z_{33},\text{ and }z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}.



However, since p^{+}_{2} and p^{+}_{3} are incomparable with respect to \leq_{H},
we have either z_{22}\neq z_{32} or z_{23}\neq z_{33}.
By exchanging a_{2} and a_{3} if necessary,
we may assume z_{23}\neq z_{33}.
In this setting, we have





z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32},\ z_{13}>z_{23}>z_{33},\text{ and }z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}.



But if we exchange \alpha and -\alpha as well as a_{2} and a_{4},
the situation becomes





z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32},\ z_{13}<z_{23}<z_{33},\text{ and }z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}.



(We actually soon see that this situation cannot occur.)
Hence we may assume both (4.2) and
z_{13}<z_{23}.



We now show the desired condition (4.7).
By (4.2) and
z_{13}<z_{23}, we have





z_{12}>z_{22}\geq z_{32},\ z_{13}<z_{23},\text{ and }z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}.



Then by Lemma 4.3(1)
we have \alpha\equiv(z_{22}+1)a_{2}\pmod{a_{1}}
and





z_{12}>z_{22}=z_{32},\ z_{13}<z_{23},\text{ and }z_{14}\leq z_{24}<z_{34}.



Since p_{2}^{+} and p_{3}^{+} are incomparable with respect to \leq_{H},
the above conditions say z_{23}>z_{33}.
Then by Lemma 4.3(2),
we have -\alpha\equiv(z_{24}+1)a_{4}\pmod{a_{1}}
and we get the desired condition (4.7).



We finally prove that condition (2) in Theorem 1.2 is satisfied (and therefore I_{H} is generated by 2-minors).
Recall that p_{1}=h+\alpha, p_{2}=h+2\alpha, and p_{3}=h+3\alpha.
By (4.7),
there exist positive integers
c_{2},c_{3},c_{4},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4},\beta such that





\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}h+\alpha&=p_{1}=-a_{1}+(c_{2}+d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(c_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}-1)a_{4},\\
h+2\alpha&=p_{2}=-a_{1}+(d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(\beta-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}-1)a_{4},\\
h+3\alpha&=p_{3}=-a_{1}+(d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(d_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}+d_{4}-1)a_{4}.\end{array}



Since \alpha\equiv d_{2}a_{2}\pmod{a_{1}} by Lemma 4.3(1),
we also have





h+a_{1}=p_{1}^{+}-\alpha\equiv(c_{2}-1)a_{2}+(c_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}-1)a_{4}\pmod{a_{1}}.



Since h\in H we have p_{1}^{+}-\alpha=h+a_{1}\in H.
Then since the right-hand side of the above equation belongs to \mathrm{Ap}
and since \alpha<d_{2}a_{2} (as \alpha\equiv d_{2}a_{2}\pmod{a_{1}}, \alpha\not\in H and d_{2}a_{2}\in\mathrm{Ap}) we must have




(4.11)

\displaystyle h+a_{1}=p_{1}^{+}-\alpha=c_{1}a_{1}+(c_{2}-1)a_{2}+(c_{3}-1)a_{3}+(c_{4}-1)a_{4}



for some positive integer c_{1}.
Similarly,
since -\alpha\equiv c_{4}a_{4}\pmod{a_{1}}, we have




(4.12)

\displaystyle h+4\alpha+a_{1}=p^{+}_{3}+\alpha=(d_{2}-1)a_{2}+(d_{3}-1)a_{3}+(d_{4}-1)a_{4}+d_{1}a_{1}



for some positive integer d_{1}.
Then, by Theorem 1.2 and equations (4), (4.11) and (4.12),
to complete the proof of the theorem
what we must prove is \beta=c_{3}+d_{3}.



We first note that by equations (4), (4.11) and (4.12),
we have




(4.13)

\displaystyle\alpha
\displaystyle=p_{1}-(p_{1}-\alpha)=d_{2}a_{2}-c_{1}a_{1},



(4.14)

\displaystyle\alpha
\displaystyle=p_{2}-p_{1}=(\beta-c_{3})a_{3}-c_{2}a_{2},



(4.15)

\displaystyle\alpha
\displaystyle=p_{3}-p_{2}=d_{4}a_{4}-(\beta-d_{3})a_{3},



(4.16)

\displaystyle\alpha
\displaystyle=(p_{3}+\alpha)-p_{3}=d_{1}a_{1}-c_{4}a_{4}.



By (4.14) and (4.15),
we have





(2\beta-c_{3}-d_{3})a_{3}=c_{2}a_{2}+d_{4}a_{4}.



If (2\beta-c_{3}-d_{3})<\beta, then the factorization of p^{+}_{2} in H is not unique, so we must have \beta\geq c_{3}+d_{3}.
Set





q=p_{1}^{+}-(c_{3}-1)a_{3}-(c_{4}-1)a_{4}=(c_{2}+d_{2}-1)a_{2}\text{ and }u=\overline{q-a_{3}}.



Since q,q+\alpha\in\mathrm{Ap}, h_{3}(q)=0, and h_{3}(q+\alpha)>0,
by Lemma 4.1(2) we have u\leq_{H}p_{3}^{+}, u\not\leq_{H}p^{+}_{1},u\not\leq_{H}p^{+}_{2} and u\not\leq_{H}p^{+}_{3}-a_{3}.
Hence u can be written in the form





u=sa_{2}+(d_{3}-1)a_{3}+ta_{4}\ \ \text{with }s\leq d_{2}-1\text{ and }c_{4}\leq t\leq c_{4}+d_{4}-1



On the other hand, since





u\equiv q-a_{3}=(c_{2}+d_{2}-1)a_{2}-a_{3}\pmod{a_{1}}



and u>q-a_{3},
there is a positive integer \sigma such that





sa_{2}+(d_{3}-1)a_{3}+ta_{4}=u=(c_{2}+d_{2}-1)a_{2}-a_{3}+\sigma a_{1}



and we get





(c_{2}+d_{2}-1-s)a_{2}+\sigma a_{1}=d_{3}a_{3}+ta_{4}.



Consider the following equation which follows from (4.14) and (4.16)





c_{2}a_{2}+d_{1}a_{1}=(\beta-c_{3})a_{3}+c_{4}a_{4}.



Taking the difference between the above two equations, we obtain




(4.17)

\displaystyle(d_{2}-1-s)a_{2}+(\beta-c_{3}-d_{3})a_{3}=(t-c_{4})a_{4}+(d_{1}-\sigma)a_{1}.



Observe that the coefficients d_{2}-1-s,\beta-c_{3}-d_{3},t-c_{4} are non-negative. Moreover





(d_{2}-1-s)a_{2}+(\beta-c_{3}-d_{3})a_{3}\leq_{H}p^{+}_{2}\text{ and }(t-c_{4})a_{4}\leq_{H}p^{+}_{3},



so both elements on the left belong to \mathrm{Ap}.
It follows that d_{1}-\sigma=0.
Indeed, if d_{1}-\sigma>0, then (4.17) implies that the
left-hand side does not belong to \mathrm{Ap}; if d_{1}-\sigma<0, then
the right-hand side does not belong to \mathrm{Ap}.
Hence





(d_{2}-1-s)a_{2}+(\beta-c_{3}-d_{3})a_{3}=(t-c_{4})a_{4}



but
since every element of \mathrm{Ap} has a unique factorizations in H,
the last equality forces
d_{2}-1-s=0,\beta-c_{3}-d_{3}=0 and t-c_{4}=0.
This proves \beta=c_{3}+d_{3},
completing the proof of Theorem 1.3.



Remark 4.4.


Numerical semigroups having an Apéry set of unique expression were studied by Rosales [Ros]. In particular, it was shown in [Ros] that, under this assumption, a minimal presentation of the numerical semigroup can be described in terms of the minimal elements lying outside the Apéry set. Our proof of Theorem 1.3 first obtains explicit presentations of the maximal elements of \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{1}) and then uses these presentations to control the defining ideal. Rosales’ result appears to be closely related to the second step of our proof, although we have not investigated whether it can be applied directly in our setting.






Final remark and examples



Although \mathrm{Ap}(H,a_{i}) does not always have a unique factorization,
we still think that the approach of this paper is useful to study Conjecture 1.1 at least for the case when n=4.
For example, Lemma 2.9 suggests that if the conjecture holds then by an appropriate choice of i one should have (i) a_{i}+p_{1} and a_{i}+p_{2} have the unique factorizations in H,
or (ii) a_{i}+p_{2} and a_{i}+p_{3} have the unique factorizations in H. If one can prove such a uniqueness statement only assuming that \mathrm{PF}(H) forms an arithmetic progression of length 3,
some of the argument in this paper will be applicable to attach the n=4 case of the conjecture.



We conclude the paper with two examples of four-generated numerical semigroups whose pseudo-Frobenius numbers form an arithmetic progression, one for which the Apéry set has unique factorizations and one for which it does not.



Example 4.5.


Let H=\langle a_{1}=17,a_{2}=40,a_{3}=42,a_{4}=45\rangle.
Then
\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{145,151,157\}
with h=139 and \alpha=6.
Thus, we have





I_{H}=\mathrm{I}_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{2}&X_{2}^{3}&X_{3}^{2}&X_{4}\\
X_{2}&X_{3}^{3}&X_{4}^{2}&X_{1}^{3}\end{matrix}\right).



Since 1=d_{2}\leq c_{2}=3 and 2=d_{4}\geq c_{4}=1, by Lemma 2.9, this Apéry set has unique factorizations in H.




Example 4.6.


Let H=\langle a_{1}=7,a_{2}=15,a_{3}=16,a_{4}=17\rangle.
Then
\mathrm{PF}(H)=\{25,26,27\}
with h=24 and \alpha=1.
We have





I_{H}=I_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{2}&X_{2}&X_{3}&X_{4}^{2}\\
X_{2}&X_{3}&X_{4}&X_{1}^{5}\end{matrix}\right).



The element 32 of \mathrm{Ap} has the following two factorizations,





32=a_{2}+a_{4}=2a_{3}.



Thus, this Apéry set does not have unique factorizations in H.
