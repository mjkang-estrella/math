                                                  Abstract. For a representation of a connected compact Lie group G in a
                                                  finite dimensional real vector space U and a subspace V of U, invariant under
                                                  a maximal torus of G, we obtain a sufficient condition for V to meet all G-
                                                  orbits in U, which is also necessary in certain cases. The proof makes use of the
                                                  cohomology of flag manifolds and the invariant theory of Weyl groups. Then
                                                  we apply our condition to the conjugation representations of U (n), Sp(n),
                                                  and SO(n) in the space of n × n matrices over C, H, and R, respectively. In
                                                  particular, we obtain an interesting generalization of Schur’s triangularization
                                                  theorem.




                                                                               1. Introduction
                                             Let ρ be a representation of a connected compact Lie group G in a finite di-
                                         mensional real vector space U. We say that a linear subspace V ⊂ U is universal
                                         if every G-orbit meets V. Examples of universal subspaces occur in many places.
                                         For instance, when ρ is irreducible and nontrivial, it is easy to show (see [12]) that
                                         every hyperplane of U is universal. For the adjoint representation of G in its Lie
                                         algebra g, it is well known that every Cartan subalgebra is universal. If we consider
                                         the complexified adjoint representation of G in the complexification gC of g, then
                                         every Borel subalgebra of gC is universal, and if moreover G is semisimple, then the
                                         sum of all root spaces in gC is also universal ([8]). Questions of similar nature have
                                         been recently studied in the context of representation theory of algebraic groups,
                                         see e.g. [12, 20].
                                             In this paper we consider the case where the stabilizer of V in G contains a
                                         maximal torus T of G. Our main result (Theorem 4.2) gives a sufficient condition
                                         for the universality of V, which is easy to check in concrete examples. With an
                                         additional restriction on V this condition becomes also necessary (see Theorem
                                         4.4).
                                             Our method goes as follows. Consider the trivial vector bundle EU = G/T × U
                                         over the flag manifold G/T . Since V is T -invariant, there is a well-defined subbundle
                                         EV of EU whose fiber at gT is ρ(g)(V). Every vector u ∈ U, viewed as a constant
                                         section of EU , induces a section su of the quotient bundle EU /EV . It is easy to see
                                         that the G-orbit of u meets V if and only if su has a zero. So V is universal if and
                                         only if su has a zero for every u ∈ U. A sufficient condition for this is that EU /EV
                                         is orientable and has a nonzero Euler class. If we choose a T -invariant subspace W
                                         of U complementary to V and construct similarly the subbundle EW of EU , then
                                         EW ∼  = EU /EV . It is easy to see that EW is always orientable and is equivalent to

   An easy dimension argument shows that if V is universal, then dim V ≥ dim U −
dim G/T . The case of equality is of particular interest (indeed, for many representa-
tions, a universal subspace V whose universality can be detected using our method
can be shrunk to a smaller universal subspace for which the equality holds). In this
case, the rank of EW is equal to dim G/T , and fV has degree m = 21 dim G/T . By
invariant theory of Weyl groups, a homogeneous polynomial of degree m is in I if
and only if it is orthogonal to the fundamental harmonic polynomial f0 (see (2.5))
with respect to a W -invariant inner product on S. So if we define the characteristic
number CV of V as the inner product of fV and f0 (up to a constant factor, which
will be chosen in a way suitable for the computation of intersection numbers), then
fV ∈/ I if and only if CV 6= 0, which gives a simpler equivalent sufficient condition
for the universality of V. Moreover, if we assume that the intersection indexes of
the above section su and the zero section s0 of EW are all equal whenever su is
transversal to s0 , then V is universal if and only if CV 6= 0.
   In concrete examples that we shall consider, the characteristic polynomials and
numbers are easy to compute. We shall do this for the conjugation representations
of U (n), Sp(n), and SO(n) in the spaces M(n, F) of n × n matrices over F = C, H,
and R, respectively, for subspaces of M(n, F) determined by zero patterns. A zero
pattern is a subset I of {(i, j)|i, j ∈ {1, . . . , n}, i 6= j} of cardinality n(n − 1)/2. For
F = C or H and a zero pattern I, we denote by MI (n, F) the subspace of M(n, F)
consisting of matrices whose (i, j)-th entry is 0 whenever (i, j) ∈ I. We shall apply
the above result to MI (n, F) and obtain a sufficient condition for the universality
of MI (n, F) expressed as a simple property of I. In particular, we show that if
I contains exactly one of (i, j) and (j, i) for i 6= j (we call such I a simple zero
pattern), then MI (n, F) is universal. Similar results are also obtained for F = R.
Note that if I0 = {(i, j)|1 ≤ i < j ≤ n}, MI0 (n, C) is the subspace of all complex
lower triangular matrices, its universality is known as Schur’s triangularization
theorem [25]. So our result gives a genuine generalization of Schur’s theorem. If
F = C and I is a bitriangular zero pattern (defined in Section 5), the above sufficient
                                UNIVERSAL SUBSPACES                                     3


condition for the universality of MI (n, C) is also necessary, and the number of flags
in U (n)/U (1)n sending a generic matrix into MI (n, C) can be computed directly
from I.
    The problem of universality of subspaces of M(n, C) determined by zero patterns
has been raised by researchers in linear algebra. For instance, the universality
of MI (4, C) when I = {(1, 3), (1, 4), (2, 4), (3, 1), (4, 1), (4, 2)} has been studied by
several authors. It was shown by Pati [23] that it is universal. A simpler proof was
given later in [5] where it is also shown that the number of flags sending a generic
matrix A ∈ M(4, C) into MI (n, C) is equal to 12. This zero pattern is bitriangular
and so both results follow immediately from Theorem 5.2. Another simple example
is the problem of universality of MI (3, C) where I = {(1, 3), (2, 1), (3, 2)}. This was
raised a few years ago [7] but remained unsettled until now. In our terminology I is
a simple zero pattern, so MI (3, C) is universal (see Theorem 5.6 and the paragraph
preceding it). For further information along this direction see e.g. [6, 15].
    The arrangement of this paper is as follows. In Section 2 we shall recall some
preliminaries on Euler classes, intersection indexes, cohomology of flag manifolds,
and invariant theory of Weyl groups. The definitions of the characteristic poly-
nomial and the characteristic number of a subspace and their properties will be
given in Section 3. The main results will be proved in Section 4. In Section 5 we
shall study the universality of subspaces determined by zero patterns, generalizing
Schur’s triangularization theorem. Then in Section 6 we shall briefly discuss the
possibility of generalizing our method and propose a question.

                                  2. Preliminaries
  In this section we recall some basic facts about Euler classes of oriented vector
bundles, cohomology of the flag manifold G/T , and invariant theory of Weyl groups.
Throughout this paper, we denote by G a connected compact Lie group and by T
a maximal torus of G.
2.1. Euler classes and intersection indexes. Let M be a connected compact
smooth n-dimensional manifold, and let E be a smooth real oriented vector bundle
over M of rank r. It is well known that if the Euler class e(E) ∈ H r (M, Z) of
E is nonzero, then every smooth section of E has a zero. If E = E1 ⊕ E2 as
oriented vector bundles, then e(E) = e(E1 )e(E2 ). If E admits a complex structure
compatible with the orientation, then the top Chern class ctop (E) of E is equal to
e(E) (see [3, 22]).
   Now we assume that M is oriented and r = n. Let s0 be the zero section of
E. Then for any smooth section s of E, we have e(E)([M ]) = I(s, s0 ), where
[M ] ∈ Hn (M, Z) is the fundamental class of M and I(s, s0 ) is the intersection
number of s and s0 . Let Z(s) = {x ∈ M |s(x) = 0} be the zero locus of s. We
write s ⋔x s0 if s is transversal to s0 at the point x ∈ Z(s), and P  s ⋔ s0 if s ⋔x s0
for all x ∈ Z(s). If s ⋔ s0 , then Z(s) is finite and I(s, s0 ) =       x∈Z(s) indx (s),
where indx (s) = ±1 is the intersection index of s and s0 at x. Assume that E
is a subbundle of a trivial bundle M × U, where U is a real vector space, and let
us view s as a map M → U. For x ∈ Z(s), we can compute indx (s) as follows.
Since s ⋔x s0 , the tangent map (ds)x : Tx M → T0 U ∼  = U is injective and has image
Ex , the fiber of E at x. If we view (ds)x as an invertible map Tx M → Ex , then
indx (s) = sgn((ds)x ). (Here the sign sgn(A) of an invertible linear map A : V1 → V2

                              6. Concluding remark
   In the previous sections, we assumed that T ⊂ GV , and then constructed the
vector bundles EW ∼  = EU /EV over G/T , and reduced the question of universality
of V to the existence of zeros of certain sections of EW . A sufficient condition for
this is that the Euler class e(EW ) is nonzero.
   This method remains valid if we replace T by any closed subgroup H of GV .
The condition that T ⊂ GV can be also dropped. More precisely, for any such H,
we let W be a GV -invariant subspace of U complementary to V, and construct the
subbundles EH,V and EH,W of the trivial bundle EH,U = G/H × U whose fibers
at gH are ρ(g)(V) and ρ(g)(W), respectively. Similarly to Theorem 4.2, if certain
characteristic class o(EH,W ) (e.g. Euler, top Stiefel-Whitney, or top Chern class)
of EH,W is nonzero, which ensures that every smooth section of EH,W has a zero,
then V is universal. For example, if H preserves an orientation on W, then EH,W
is orientable and we can use the Euler class.
   If H1 ⊂ H2 are two closed subgroups of GV , then there is a natural quotient map
q : G/H1 → G/H2 , and it is easy to see that EH1 ,W ∼   = q ∗ (EH2 ,W ). Hence we have
               ∗                 ∗
o(EH1 ,W ) = q (o(EH2 ,W )). If q is not injective, it may happen that o(EH1 ,W ) = 0
but o(EH2 ,W ) 6= 0. So by using a larger subgroup H of GV , we may get stronger
sufficient condition for the universality of V.
   An interesting case occurs when dim V = dim U − dim G/GV and T ⊂ GV . We
further assume that T is the identity component of GV . Then GV /T is a subgroup
of the Weyl group W . To avoid the trouble with non-orientability, we assume that
U admits a complex structure such that the G-action is complex linear, and that
V and W are complex subspaces. Then we can use the top Chern class (which
is equal to the Euler class of the underlying real bundle). We choose H1 = T ,
H2 = GV . Then dim G/T = dim G/GV = 2m, and ctop (ET,W ) ∈ H 2m (G/T, Z),
ctop (EGV ,W ) ∈ H 2m (G/GV , Z). It is easy to prove that G/GV is non-orientable if
and only if GV /T contains an odd element of W . From algebraic topology, if G/GV
                                    UNIVERSAL SUBSPACES                                          19


is orientable, then H 2m (G/GV , Z) ∼   = Z and q ∗ : H 2m (G/GV , Z) → H 2m (G/T, Z)
is injective. So we obtain the same information from the top Chern classes by
using T and GV . But if G/GV is non-orientable, then H 2m (G/GV , Z) ∼     = Z/2Z and
q ∗ : H 2m (G/GV , Z) → H 2m (G/T, Z) is equal to zero. In this case we always have
ctop (ET,W ) = q ∗ (ctop (EGV ,W )) = 0, but it may happen that ctop (EGV ,W ) 6= 0, and
if this is the case, we get more information than by using T and we can prove the
universality of V. We demonstrate this in the following simple example.

Question. Suppose that dim V = dim U − dim G/GV and T ⊂ GV . Does the

of non-vanishing sections, is nonzero?
   We remark that if the condition dim V = dim U − dim G/GV or T ⊂ GV is

counter-examples.
   For the case of T 6⊂ GV , consider the representation eiθ 7→ diag(eiθ , e2iθ ) of U (1)

the sum of all root spaces in gC , V is universal by [8], Theorem 3.4.
