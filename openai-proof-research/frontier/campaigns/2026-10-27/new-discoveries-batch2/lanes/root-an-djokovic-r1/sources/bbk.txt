 Koustav Banerjee
 
 ,  
 Kathrin Bringmann
 
  and  
 William Keith

 Email address:  kbanerj1@uni-koeln.de 
 
 Email address:  kbringma@uni-koeln.de

 Abstract. 
    
 Recently, Andrews and Bachraoui considered a generating function  F_{k,m}(q)  associated with certain two-color partitions, and conjectured that this function has non-negative coefficients for  m=1 . They showed this property for  1\leq k\leq 4 . In this note, we prove that  F_{k,1}(q)  has non-negative coefficients for  5\leq k\leq 10 . Moreover, we show that, as  k\to\infty ,  F_{k,1}(q)  is related to Ramanujan’s third order mock theta function  {\omega}(q)  and to quotients of certain  q -binomial coefficients. 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  mock theta functions, partitions,  q -series.
 
 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification 05A17, 11P81.
 
 
 1.  Introduction and Statement of Results 

 
 Throughout let  (a)_{n}=(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j})  for  n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\} . Moreover let  (a_{1},...,a_{\ell})_{n}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell};q)_{n}:=(a_{1})_{n}\cdots(a_{\ell})_{n}  for  \ell\in\mathbb{N} .
Andrews and Bachraoui  [ 3 , Conjecture 2.8 and Conjecture 2.9]  conjectured that for  k,m\in\mathbb{N}  and for  k=2,m=4 , the coefficients of the  q -series 
 

 
 
 \sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}q^{m(2n+1)}=:\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{k,m}(n)q^{n}=:F_{k,m}(q) 
 
 
 are positive. Note that for  m=1 , we have 
 

 
 (1.1) 
 
 F_{k,1}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2n+1};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}q^{2n+1}. 
 
 
 
 
 Andrews and Bachraoui  [ 3 , (2.2), (2.3), (2.5), Theorem 2.6]  proved that  c_{k,1}(n)>0  for  n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}  and  1\leq k\leq 4 . Moreover they proposed the following conjecture (see  [ 3 , Conjecture 2.8] ), which we partially prove in this paper. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1 . 
 
 For  k\in\mathbb{N}  and  n\in\mathbb{N}_{0} ,  c_{k,1}(n)>0 . 
 
 
 
 To state our theorems, for  A(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a(n)q^{n}  we mean, by  A(q)\succeq 0  (resp.  A(q)\succ 0 ) that  a(n)\geq 0  (resp.  a(n)>0 ) for  n\in\mathbb{N}_{0} . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.2 . 
 
 For  k\in\{5,6,7\} , we have  F_{k,1}(q)\succeq 0 . 
 
 
 
 Using a computational approach we extend this to  k\leq 10 . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3 . 
 
 For  k\in\{8,9,10\} , we have  F_{k,1}(q)\succeq 0 . 
 
 
 
 Our next result relates  F_{k,1}  to the mock theta function  \omega  of Ramanujan  [ 5 ] 
 

 
 (1.2) 
 
 \omega(q):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{n+1}}. 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem 1.4 . 
 
 We have 
 

 
 
 \lim_{k\to\infty}F_{k,1}(q)=q\omega(q). 
 
 
 
 
 
 In the following theorem, we obtain more information about the function that prevents  F_{k,1}(q)  from agreeing with  q{\omega}(q) . To state our result, for  A(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a(n)q^{n}  and  m\in\mathbb{N}_{0} , define 
 

 
 
 \operatorname{coeff}_{\left[q^{m}\right]}A(q):=a(m). 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem 1.5 . 
 
 For  k\in\mathbb{N} , we have 
 

 
 
 q\omega(q)-F_{k,1}(q)=q^{2k+1}E_{k}(q)\ \text{with}\ E_{k}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[[q]]\ \text{and}\ \operatorname{coeff}_{\left[q^{0}\right]}E_{k}(q)=1. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem  1.5  immediately implies the following. 
 
 
 Corollary 1.6 . 
 
 For  k\in\mathbb{N}  and  1\leq m\leq k , we have 
 

 
 
 \operatorname{coeff}_{\left[q^{m}\right]}\left(q\omega(q)-F_{k,1}(q)\right)=0\ \ \text{for}\ \ 1\leq m\leq 2k. 
 
 
 
 
 
 The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section  2  we recall some standard  q -series transformations and define notation. In Section  3  we transform  F_{k,1}(q)  into certain forms which assist in proving Theorems  1.2 ,  1.4 , and  1.5 . In Section  4 , we prove Theorem  1.2  and Theorem  1.3 , showing the positivity of the coefficients of  F_{k,1}(q)  for  5\leq k\leq 10 . Next, we prove  Theorem   1.4 , and  Theorem   1.5 , exhibiting the relations between  F_{k,1}  and the third order mock theta function  \omega . Finally, in  Section   5 , we presents a few plausible approaches to address  Conjecture   1.1 .

 2.  Preliminaries 

 
 Recall Heine’s transformation  [ 4 , Corollary 1.2.4] . 
 
 
 Lemma 2.1 . 
 
 For  |q|,|t|<1 ,  0<|c|<|b|<1 , we have 
 

 
 
 \sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(a,b)_{n}t^{n}}{(q,c)_{n}}=\frac{\left(\frac{c}{b},bt\right)_{\infty}}{(c,t)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(\frac{abt}{c},b\right)_{n}\left(\frac{c}{b}\right)^{n}}{(q,bt)_{n}}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 By  [ 1 , Theorem 1] , we have the following transformation. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.2 . 
 
 We have 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(B,-Abq\right)_{n}q^{n}}{\left(-aq,-bq\right)_{n}} 
 \displaystyle=-\frac{a^{-1}\left(B,-Abq\right)_{\infty}}{\left(-aq,-bq\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(A^{-1}\right)_{n}\left(\frac{Abq}{a}\right)^{n}}{\left(-\frac{B}{a}\right)_{n+1}} 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt+\left(1+b\right)\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-a^{-1}\right)_{n+1}\left(-\frac{ABq}{a}\right)_{n}\left(-b\right)^{n}}{\left(-\frac{B}{a},\frac{Abq}{a}\right)_{n+1}}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 We next state the Rogers–Fine identity  [ 1 , equation (2.7)] . 
 
 
 Lemma 2.3 . 
 
 We have 
 

 
 
 \sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(\alpha\right)_{n}w^{n}}{\left(\beta\right)_{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(\alpha,\frac{\alpha wq}{\beta}\right)_{n}\beta^{n}w^{n}q^{n^{2}-n}\left(1-\alpha wq^{2n}\right)}{\left(\beta\right)_{n}\left(w\right)_{n+1}}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 The  Gaussian polynomial  is defined by (see  [ 2 , Definition 3.1] ) 
 

 
 
 \begin{bmatrix}n\\
m\end{bmatrix}_{q}:=\begin{cases}\frac{(q)_{n}}{(q)_{m}(q)_{n-m}}&\quad\text{if}\ 0\leq m\leq n,\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} 
 
 
 From  [ 2 , equation (3.3.6)] , we have, for  n\in\mathbb{N} , 
 

 
 (2.1) 
 
 \left(a;q\right)_{N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\begin{bmatrix}N\\
j\end{bmatrix}_{q}(-1)^{j}a^{j}q^{\frac{j^{2}-j}{2}}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 3.  Some  q -series transformations 

 
 The following lemma is a key step for proving Theorem  1.2 . 
 
 
 Lemma 3.1 . 
 
 For  k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3} , we have 
 

 
 
 F_{k,1}(q)=\frac{q}{\left(1-q\right)\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\left(1+\left(1-q\right)\sum_{n=1}^{k-2}\frac{\left(q^{4-2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}}\frac{q^{(2k-1)n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}\right). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Using ( 1.1 ), we obtain 
 

 
 (3.1) 
 
 \displaystyle F_{k,1}(q) 
 \displaystyle=\frac{q\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{n}q^{2n}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}\left(q^{2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}. 
 
 
 Applying Lemma  2.1  with  q\mapsto q^{2} ,  a=b=q ,  c=q^{2k} , and  t=q^{2} , we have 
 

 
 
 \sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{n}q^{2n}}{\left(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}=\frac{\left(q^{2k-1},q^{3};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2k},q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{4-2k},q;q^{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{2},q^{3};q^{2}\right)_{n}}q^{(2k-1)n}. 
 
 
 Plugging this into ( 3.1 ), it follows that 
 

 
 (3.2) 
 
 \displaystyle F_{k,1}(q) 
 \displaystyle\!=\frac{q}{\left(1-q\right)\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\left(1+\left(1-q\right)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left(q^{4-2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}}\frac{q^{(2k-1)n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}\right). 
 
 
 Note that for  n\geq k-1 ,  (q^{4-2k};q^{2})_{n}=0 . Therefore, by ( 3.2 ), we have for  k\geq 3 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle F_{k,1}(q)=\frac{q}{\left(1-q\right)\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\left(1+\left(1-q\right)\sum_{n=1}^{k-2}\frac{\left(q^{4-2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}}\frac{q^{(2k-1)n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}\right). 
 
 
 This concludes the proof.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Next, we present two lemmas which are used in the proof of Theorem  1.5 . 
 
 
 Lemma 3.2 . 
 
 For  k\in\mathbb{N} , we have 
 

 
 
 F_{k,1}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2k-1};q^{2}\right)_{n}q^{n+1}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 By ( 3.1 ), we have 
 

 
 (3.3) 
 
 F_{k,1}(q)=\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})^{2}_{\infty}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{n}q^{2n+1}}{\left(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}. 
 
 
 Taking  q\mapsto q^{2} ,  a=-1,b=-q^{2k-2},A=q^{1-2k} , and  B=q  in Lemma  2.2 , using the fact that for  n\in\mathbb{N}_{0} ,  (1;q^{2})_{n+1}=0 , we obtain 
 

 
 
 \sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{n}q^{2n+1}}{\left(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2}\right)_{n}}=\frac{(q;q^{2})^{2}_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2k-1};q^{2}\right)_{n}q^{n+1}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}}. 
 
 
 Plugging this into ( 3.3 ), we get 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle F_{k,1}(q) 
 \displaystyle=\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)^{2}_{\infty}\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\frac{(q;q^{2})^{2}_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2k-1};q^{2}\right)_{n}q^{n+1}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}} 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2k-1};q^{2}\right)_{n}q^{n+1}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}}. 
 
 
 This gives the lemma.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 4.  Proof of Theorems  1.2 ,  1.3 ,  1.4 , and  1.5 

 
 We are now ready to prove Theorem  1.2 . 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  1.2 . We show Theorem  1.2  in three parts. First we prove the claim for  k=5 . By Lemma  3.1 , we have 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle F_{5,1}(q) 
 \displaystyle=\frac{q}{\left(1-q\right)\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{4}}\left(1+\left(1-q\right)\sum_{n=1}^{3}\frac{\left(q^{-6};q^{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}}\frac{q^{9n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}\right) 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle=\frac{q}{(1-q)\left(1-q^{3}\right)\left(1-q^{5}\right)\left(1-q^{7}\right)}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}+\frac{1-q^{-6}}{1-q^{2}}\frac{q^{9}}{1-q^{3}}\right. 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left.+\frac{\left(1-q^{-6}\right)\left(1-q^{-4}\right)}{\left(1-q^{2}\right)\left(1-q^{4}\right)}\frac{q^{18}}{1-q^{5}}+\frac{\left(1-q^{-6}\right)\left(1-q^{-4}\right)\left(1-q^{-2}\right)}{\left(1-q^{2}\right)\left(1-q^{4}\right)\left(1-q^{6}\right)}\frac{q^{27}}{1-q^{7}}\right). 
 
 
 Simplifying the factors in the summands on the right-hand side, we obtain 
 

 
 
 F_{5,1}(q)=\frac{q}{(1-q)\left(1-q^{3}\right)\left(1-q^{5}\right)\left(1-q^{7}\right)}\\
\times\left(\frac{1}{1-q}-\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)\frac{q^{3}}{1-q^{3}}+\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)\frac{q^{8}}{1-q^{5}}-\frac{q^{15}}{1-q^{7}}\right). 
 
 
 
 
 The claim follows once we prove that 
 

 
 
 \frac{1}{1-q}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}-\frac{\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)q^{3}}{1-q^{3}}+\frac{\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)q^{8}}{1-q^{5}}-\frac{q^{15}}{1-q^{7}}\right)\succeq 0. 
 
 
 We now show that we can treat the first and the second together as well as the third and fourth. First we write 
 

 
 
 \frac{\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)q^{3}}{1-q^{3}}=-1-q-q^{2}-q^{4}+\frac{1+q+q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}. 
 
 
 Then 
 

 
 
 \frac{1}{1-q}+1+q+q^{2}+q^{4}-\frac{1+q+q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}\succeq 0. 
 
 
 Next we write 
 

 
 
 \frac{\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)q^{8}}{1-q^{5}}-\frac{q^{15}}{1-q^{7}}=q^{8}\bigg(\frac{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}}+1-\frac{1}{1-q^{7}}\bigg). 
 
 
 Now 
 

 
 
 \frac{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}}-\frac{1}{1-q^{7}}\succeq\frac{1}{1-q^{5}}-\frac{1}{1-q^{7}}=\frac{q^{5}-q^{7}}{\left(1-q^{5}\right)\left(1-q^{7}\right)}=\frac{q^{5}\left(1-q^{2}\right)}{\left(1-q^{5}\right)\left(1-q^{7}\right)}. 
 
 
 Dividing by  \frac{1}{1-q}  gives 
 

 
 
 \frac{q^{5}(1+q)}{(1-q^{5})(1-q^{7})}\succeq 0. 
 
 
 This finishes the proof of the case  k=5 . 
 
 
 Next, we show the case  k=6 . By Lemma  3.1 , we have 
 

 
 
 F_{6,1}(q)=\frac{q}{(1-q)(q;q^{2})_{5}}\left(1+(1-q)\sum_{n=1}^{4}\frac{\left(q^{-8};q^{2}\right)_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}\frac{q^{11n}}{1-q^{2n+1}}\right). 
 
 
 Simplifying the factors  \frac{(q^{-8};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{n}}  (for  1\leq n\leq 4 ) in the sum on the right-hand side of the above identity, we have 
 

 
 
 F_{6,1}(q)=\frac{q}{(q;q^{2})_{5}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}-\frac{q^{3}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}+q^{6}\right)}{1-q^{3}}+\frac{q^{8}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)\left(1+q^{4}\right)}{1-q^{5}}\right.\\
\left.-\frac{q^{15}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}+q^{6}\right)}{1-q^{7}}+\frac{q^{24}}{1-q^{9}}\right). 
 
 
 Using long division, we write 
 

 
 
 
 \displaystyle-\frac{q^{3}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}+q^{6}\right)}{1-q^{3}}=2+q+q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}+q^{6}-\frac{2+q+q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}, 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\frac{q^{8}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)\left(1+q^{4}\right)}{1-q^{5}}=-1-q-2q^{2}-q^{3}-q^{4}-q^{5}-q^{6}-2q^{7}-q^{9}-q^{11} 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 270.30118pt+\frac{1+q+2q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}}, 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle-\frac{q^{15}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}+q^{6}\right)}{1-q^{7}}=1+q+q^{3}+q^{5}+q^{7}+q^{8}+q^{10}+q^{12}+q^{14} 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 301.59938pt-\frac{1+q+q^{3}+q^{5}}{1-q^{7}}. 
 
 
 Thus, we need 
 

 
 
 
 \displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}+2+q+q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}+q^{6}-\frac{2+q+q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}-1-q-2q^{2}-q^{3}-q^{4}\right. 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\left.-q^{5}-q^{6}-2q^{7}-q^{9}-q^{11}+\frac{1+q+2q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}}+1+q+q^{3}+q^{5}+q^{7}+q^{8}\right. 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 210.55022pt\left.+q^{10}+q^{12}+q^{14}-\frac{1+q+q^{3}+q^{5}}{1-q^{7}}\right) 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-q}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}+2+q-q^{2}+q^{3}-q^{7}+q^{8}-q^{9}+q^{10}-q^{11}+q^{12}+q^{14}\right. 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 81.09052pt\left.-\frac{2+q+q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}+\frac{1+q+2q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}}-\frac{1+q+q^{3}+q^{5}}{1-q^{7}}\right)\succeq 0. 
 
 
 Now we write 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\frac{1+q+2q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{4}}{1-q^{5}} 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-q}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{5}},\quad-\frac{2+q+q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}=-\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}-\frac{1}{1-q}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle-\frac{1+q+q^{3}+q^{5}}{1-q^{7}} 
 \displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1-q}+\frac{q^{2}+q^{4}+q^{6}}{1+q^{7}}. 
 
 
 So we want 
 

 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\frac{1}{1-q}\Biggl(2+q-q^{2}+q^{3}-q^{7}+q^{8}-q^{9}+q^{10}-q^{11}+q^{12}+q^{14}-\frac{1}{1-q^{3}} 
 
 
 (4.1) 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 182.09746pt+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{5}}+\frac{q^{2}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)}{1-q^{7}}\Biggr)\succeq 0. 
 
 
 To show this, first note that 
 

 
 
 
 \displaystyle\frac{1}{1-q}\!\left(-\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}\!+\!\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{5}}\!+\!\frac{q^{2}\left(1+q^{2}+q^{4}\right)}{1-q^{7}}\right) 
 
 
 (4.2) 
 
 
 \displaystyle\hskip 147.95424pt\!\succeq\!\frac{1}{1-q}\!\left(\!-\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}\!+\!\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{5}}\!+\!\frac{q^{2}\left(1+q^{2}\right)}{1-q^{7}}\right). 
 
 
 Now we prove that 
 

 
 (4.3) 
 
 \frac{1}{1-q}\!\left(-\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{5}}+\frac{q^{2}\left(1+q^{2}\right)}{1-q^{7}}\right)\succeq-\frac{1+q}{1-q}. 
 
 
 This is equivalent to showing that 
 

 
 (4.4)

 This concludes the proof.∎ 
 
 
 
 5.  Potential extensions of current approaches 

 
 Each of the approaches employed in this paper runs into difficulties, which we now discuss, followed by some suggestions on extensions or alternatives. 
 
 
 In the proof of Theorem  1.2 , for  k=5 , we first simplify  [\begin{smallmatrix}k-2\\
n\end{smallmatrix}]_{q^{2}} , then do long division and combine. However we are unable to find a uniform process of simplification, as shown in the proof of Theorem  1.2  for  k\in\{6,7\} . If a persistent pattern could be found then the entire conjecture might be provable in this way. 
 
 
 In the proof of Theorem  1.3 , the calculation is algorithmic but the lcm grows exponentially in  k . For  k=11  we are unable to continue. A more careful choice of where to cancel might allow further calculation; sufficient systematic insight, possibly including some combinatorial injections, might even be able to fully establish the conjecture. We make the following connection with  q -binomial quotients, which may hold independent interest for some readers. Recall  Lemma   3.2 
 

 
 (5.1) 
 
 F_{k,1}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(q^{2k-1};q^{2}\right)_{n}q^{n+1}}{\left(q;q^{2}\right)_{n+1}}=:\sum_{n\geq 0}G_{k,n}(q)q^{n+1}. 
 
 
 We make the following refined conjecture, which would in turn imply  Conjecture   1.1 . Next, we rewrite  G_{k,n}(q)  as a quotient of  q -binomials. 
 

 
 (5.2) 
 
 G_{k,n}(q)=\frac{1}{1-q^{2n+1}}\frac{\begin{bmatrix}2k+2n-2\\
2n\end{bmatrix}_{q}}{\begin{bmatrix}k+n-1\\
n\end{bmatrix}_{q^{2}}}. 
 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 5.1 . 
 
 We have  G_{k,n}(q)\succeq 0 . 
 
 
 
 The form given in ( 5.2 ), the symmetry of  q -binomial coefficients about their central peaks, and computational data suggest the following further conjecture: 
 
 
 Conjecture 5.2 . 
 
 For  n\in\mathbb{N} ,  k\geq n+1 , we have 
 

 
 
 G_{k,n}-G_{k+1,n-1}\succeq 0. 
 
 
 
 
 
 We can establish the following. 
 
 
 Lemma 5.3 . 
 
 For  n\in\mathbb{N} ,  1\leq j\leq n ,  G_{n+2-j,n}=G_{n+2,n-j} . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Using ( 5.1 ) with  k=n-j+2  and then using ( 5.1 ) with  k=n+2  and  n\mapsto n-j , we conclude the proof. ∎ 
 
 
 
 Since  G_{k,0}(q)=\frac{1}{1-q}  has nonnegative coefficients for all  k , it follows from Lemma  5.3  that Conjecture  5.2  would imply the nonnegativity of all  G_{k,n}  and hence  Conjecture   1.1 . Lemma  5.3  also has the consequence that if we can show that  G_{k,n}\succeq 0  for each  k  and  0\leq n\leq k-2 , then all  G_{k,n}\succeq 0  and Conjecture  1.1  would be proved. Finally, the forms exhibited in the proof of Theorem  1.2 , along with computational evidence, further suggest the following conjecture, which would also be sufficient to establish the positivity of  F_{k,1}(q) : 
 
 
 Conjecture 5.4 . 
 
 We have  G_{k,n}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{z_{k,j}(q)}{1-q^{2j+1}}  where all  z_{k,j}(q)  have nonnegative coefficients.
