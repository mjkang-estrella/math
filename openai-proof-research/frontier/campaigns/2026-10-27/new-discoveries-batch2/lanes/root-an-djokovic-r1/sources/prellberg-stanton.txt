 A positivity conjecture of Prellberg and Stanton 
 
 
 Thomas Prellberg
 
 Address:  School of Mathematical Sciences, Queen Mary University of London, London E1 4NS, United Kingdom
 
 Email address:  t.prellberg@qmul.ac.uk 
 
 Date: October 8, 2026 

 Abstract. 
    
 We prove the conjecture of Prellberg and Stanton (2003) that
 \frac{1-q}{(q^{n};q)_{m}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q}  has nonnegative power-series coefficients for  0<m<n ,
except when  n  is even and  m\in\{2,n-2\} .
We obtain a polynomial version by bounding a Möbius correction by
first differences of Gaussian coefficients. The proof uses partitions
and quadratic and cubic semi-invariants of binary forms.
An extension of this bound proves the Reiner–Stanton–White conjecture
on positivity of orbit polynomials for flags of subspaces. 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  Gaussian polynomial, integer partition, semi-invariant, Singer cycle
 
 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification Primary 05A17; Secondary 05A30, 13A50
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 The purpose of this paper is to prove a conjecture of Prellberg and
Stanton  [ 20 , Conjecture 1]  on the monotonicity of partition counts.
The same method proves the conjecture of Reiner, Stanton and
White  [ 21 , Conjecture 10.3]  on orbit polynomials for flags of subspaces. 
 
 
 We use the notation  [r]_{q}=(1-q^{r})/(1-q) ,
 (x;q)_{r}=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-xq^{j}) , and
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{r}_{q}=(q;q)_{n}/((q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{n-r}) .
Throughout,  \mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}  and  P\mathrel{\geq_{q}}Q  means that  P-Q  has
nonnegative coefficients. 
 
 
 Prellberg and Stanton  [ 20 ]  proved that, for odd  n\geq 3 , the number
of partitions of  N  with parts in  \{n,n+1,\ldots,2n-1\}  is
nondecreasing for  N\geq 1 . This continued the study of partition
monotonicity in Friedman, Joichi and Stanton  [ 9 ] .
Prellberg and Stanton also conjectured that 
 

 
 
 F_{n,m}(q)=\frac{1-q}{(q^{n};q)_{m}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q} 
 
 
 has nonnegative coefficients for  0<m<n , except when  n  is even and
 m\in\{2,n-2\} . 
 
 
 Here is the partition interpretation. Let  a_{n,m}(N)  count partitions
of  N  with parts in  \{1,\ldots,n-m\}\cup\{n,\ldots,n+m-1\} , using
at most  m  parts from the first interval. The small parts fit in an
 m\times(n-m)  rectangle; the large parts have unrestricted multiplicities.
Thus 
 

 
 
 \sum_{N\geq 0}a_{n,m}(N)q^{N}=\frac{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q}}{(q^{n};q)_{m}},\qquad[q^{N}]F_{n,m}=a_{n,m}(N)-a_{n,m}(N-1), 
 
 
 where  a_{n,m}(-1)=0 . The conjecture says that these partition counts
are nondecreasing. We prove the following polynomial version. 
 
 
 Theorem 1 . 
 
 For  0<m<n , except when  n  is even and  m\in\{2,n-2\} , the polynomial 
 

 
 
 R_{n,m}(q)=\frac{[m]_{q^{n+1}}}{[n]_{q}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{m-1}_{q}\frac{[m]_{q^{n+1}}}{[m]_{q}} 
 
 
 has nonnegative integer coefficients. Consequently  F_{n,m}\in\mathbb{N}[[q]]  for all these pairs. 
 
 
 
 For  m=1 , we have  F_{n,1}=1 . For  m\geq 2 , the consequence follows from 
 

 
 (1) 
 
 F_{n,m}=\frac{R_{n,m}}{(1-q^{m(n+1)})(q^{n+2};q)_{m-2}}\qquad(m\geq 2). 
 
 
 At  q=1 , 
 

 
 
 R_{n,m}(1)=\frac{m}{n}\binom{n}{m}=\binom{n-1}{m-1}, 
 
 
 the number of compositions of  n  into  m  positive parts. We do not yet
have a natural statistic on these compositions, or a direct partition
model, whose generating function is  R_{n,m}  in general.
Section  5  gives a Dyck-path construction in the coprime
case and asks for a construction in the remaining cases. Such a model,
together with ( 1 ), would also count the first differences of
 a_{n,m}(N)  directly. 
 
 
 The coprime case of Theorem  1  follows from the positivity of
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q}/[n]_{q} , the rational  q -Catalan polynomial associated with
the coprime pair  (m,n-m) ; see
Haiman  [ 14 , Propositions 2.5.3 and 2.5.4] 
and Andrews  [ 1 ] .
More generally, if  g=\gcd(n,m) , then
 [g]_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q}/[n]_{q}\in\mathbb{N}[q]  by Brunetti and Del Lungo  [ 4 ] ;
see also Chen and Hou  [ 5 , Theorem 8]  and
Guo and Krattenthaler  [ 13 , Lemma 5.2] .
To obtain  R_{n,m}  from this quotient, one multiplies by
 [m]_{q^{n+1}}/[g]_{q} . For  g>1  this is a polynomial with coefficient
 -1  at  q , so the known positivity does not suffice. 
 
 
 We will subtract a Möbius correction from a Gaussian polynomial and
bound that correction by the Gaussian first differences.
Sylvester’s theorem identifies these differences with dimensions of
spaces of semi-invariants. We use quadratic and cubic semi-invariants
with the bidegrees of the independent family constructed by Jafari and
Najafi Amin  [ 15 ] .
For related comparisons of Gaussian polynomials, see
Chen and Jia  [ 6 ,  7 ] ; strict unimodality and
quantitative lower bounds were studied by Pak and
Panova  [ 18 ,  19 ] . 
 
 
 Put  a=n-m ,  g=\gcd(a,m) ,  C=n+1 , and 
 

 
 (2) 
 
 Q_{d}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n/d-1}{m/d-1}_{q^{d}}\ (d\mid g),\quad G=Q_{1},\quad A=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid g\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)Q_{d},\quad H=\frac{G-[C]_{q}A}{[m]_{q}}, 
 
 
 where  \mu  is the Möbius function. Empty sums are zero. We suppress
 n,m  in  A,H  and  Q_{d}  when there is no ambiguity. 
 
 
 Theorem 2 . 
 
 For every  0<m<n , one has  A\in\mathbb{N}[q] ,  H\in\mathbb{Z}[q] , and
 H_{n,m}=H_{n,n-m} . Moreover, 
 

 
 
 H\in\mathbb{N}[q]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad n\text{ is odd or }m\notin\{2,n-2\}. 
 
 
 For even  n\geq 4 , the exceptional values are
 H_{n,2}=H_{n,n-2}=-q^{n-1} . 
 
 
 
 Theorem  1  follows from Theorem  2  and the identity 
 

 
 (3) 
 
 R_{n,m}=[m]_{q^{C}}H+[C]_{q^{m}}A. 
 
 
 Section  2  bounds  A  by first differences of  G .
Section  3  uses this bound to prove
Theorem  2 .
Corollary  1  gives the subspace case of the orbit-polynomial
conjecture, and Section  4  proves the result for flags.
Armstrong  [ 2 , Section 11.2]  discusses the subspace case and
proposes a further coefficientwise monotonicity conjecture. 
 
 
 
 2.  Two coefficient comparisons 

 
 The proof of Theorem  2  requires a lower bound for the first
differences of a Gaussian polynomial and an upper bound for the correction
 A . We obtain both bounds from the same family of partitions. 
 
 
 Lemma 1 . 
 
 For positive integers  a,b , let  \mathcal{B}_{a,b}(q)  count partitions with parts  2,\ldots,b , assigning cost two to each even part and cost three to each odd part, with total cost at most  a . Then 
 

 
 (4) 
 
 [q^{s}](1-q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a+b}{b}_{q}\ \geq\ [q^{s}]\mathcal{B}_{a,b}(q)\qquad(0\leq s\leq ab/2). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 We use Sylvester’s theorem to count semi-invariants. Give  x_{i}  degree one
and weight  i  in  \mathbb{Q}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{b}] , and let  V_{a,s}  be the component
of degree  a  and weight  s . For
 D=\sum_{i=1}^{b}x_{i-1}\partial_{x_{i}} , the theorem
(see  [ 7 , Theorem 3.1] , after rescaling its coordinates by  x_{i}=a_{i}/i! ) gives 
 

 
 
 \dim(\ker D\cap V_{a,s})=[q^{s}](1-q)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a+b}{b}_{q}\qquad(0\leq s\leq ab/2). 
 
 
 It is enough to construct independent semi-invariants indexed by the
partitions counted by  \mathcal{B}_{a,b} . The required degrees are two and three,
as in  [ 15 ] . Put 
 

 
 
 \Phi(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{b}x_{i}y^{i},\qquad\Phi_{2}(y)=\Phi(y)\Phi(-y),\qquad\Phi_{3}(y)=\Phi(y)^{2}\Phi(-2y). 
 
 
 Since  D\Phi(y)\equiv y\Phi(y)\pmod{y^{b+1}} , the product rule gives
 D\Phi_{2}(y)\equiv D\Phi_{3}(y)\equiv 0\pmod{y^{b+1}} :
the argument multipliers sum to zero in each product. Define 
 

 
 
 f_{k}=\begin{cases}[y^{k}]\Phi_{2}(y),&k\text{ even},\\
[y^{k}]\Phi_{3}(y),&k\text{ odd},\end{cases}\qquad 2\leq k\leq b. 
 
 
 Thus  f_{k}  is a semi-invariant of weight  k  and of degree two or three
according as  k  is even or odd. These are the assigned costs.
The highest-index term of  f_{k}  is  2x_{0}x_{k}  for even  k  and
 (2-2^{k})x_{0}^{2}x_{k}  for odd  k , with all remaining terms involving only
 x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1} .
Successively solving for  x_{k}  after inverting  x_{0}  gives 
 

 
 
 \mathbb{Q}[x_{0}^{\pm 1},x_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{b}]=\mathbb{Q}[x_{0}^{\pm 1},x_{1},\ldots,x_{b}]. 
 
 
 This ring has transcendence degree  b+1 , so the  b+1  elements
 x_{0},x_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{b}  are algebraically independent.
A partition of weight  s , with multiplicities  r_{k}  and cost  c\leq a ,
now gives the monomial  x_{0}^{a-c}\prod_{k}f_{k}^{r_{k}}  in  \ker D\cap V_{a,s} .
These monomials are linearly independent, proving ( 4 ).
∎ 
 
 
 
 Lemma 2 . 
 
 For every  0<m<n , the correction in ( 2 ) satisfies 
 

 
 (5) 
 
 0\leq_{q}A\leq_{q}\sum_{p\mid g}Q_{p},\qquad A-Q_{p}\mathrel{\geq_{q}}0\quad(p\mid g), 
 
 
 where  p  ranges over prime divisors of  g .
If  a=n-m\geq m\geq 3 , then 
 

 
 (6) 
 
 0\leq_{q}A\leq_{q}\mathcal{B}_{a,m-1}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 First we realize  A  as the generating function of a union. Let
 B_{0}=\{1,\ldots,m-1\}  and give an  (m-1) -subset  S  of
 \{1,\ldots,n-1\}  weight  \operatorname{wt}(S)=\sum_{t\in S}t-\binom{m}{2} .
Put  X_{d}=\{S:S\mathbin{\triangle}B_{0}\subseteq d\mathbb{Z}\} . A subset in  X_{d} 
agrees with  B_{0}  at the nonmultiples of  d  and chooses  m/d-1  multiples. On writing
 U=\{t/d:t\in S,\ d\mid t\} , its weight is
 d\bigl(\sum_{u\in U}u-\binom{m/d}{2}\bigr) , so  X_{d}  has generating function  Q_{d} .
Since  X_{d}\cap X_{d^{\prime}}=X_{\lcm(d,d^{\prime})} , inclusion–exclusion shows that  A  counts
 \bigcup_{p\mid g}X_{p} . This proves ( 5 ).
To prove ( 6 ), assume  a\geq m\geq 3 . The case  g=1  is immediate,
since  A=0 . For  g>1  we compare coefficients at weight  s  as follows;
here  p,p^{\prime}  are primes: 
 

 
 
 \begin{array}[]{c|l}s\leq a&\text{inclusion--exclusion without a length constraint},\\
s>a,\ g=p^{r}&\text{the direct inclusion }Q_{p}\leq_{q}\mathcal{B}_{a,m-1},\\
s>a,\ p,p^{\prime}\mid g\ (p\neq p^{\prime})&\text{disjoint classes with specified odd parts}.\end{array} 
 
 
 
 
 Low weights and prime powers. 
For  d>1 , the conjugate rectangle interpretation of  Q_{d}  counts partitions
all of whose parts lie in  \{d,2d,\ldots,m-d\} , with at most  a/d  parts.
At weight  s\leq a , the length constraint is vacuous. Also
 \mathcal{B}_{a,m-1}  counts every partition into  2,\ldots,m-1 , since cost
never exceeds weight. The divisibility classes intersect by least common
multiples, so their union proves ( 6 ) in this range.
If  g  is a prime power,  A=Q_{p} :
its partitions have at most  a/p  parts, each of cost at most  p .
Thus every such partition is counted by  \mathcal{B}_{a,m-1} , at every weight. 
 
 
 Disjoint classes at higher weights. 
Suppose now that  g  has at least two distinct prime factors and  s>a .
We assign to each prime in ( 5 ) a disjoint class of partitions
counted by  \mathcal{B}_{a,m-1} . Each class will dominate its corresponding  Q_{p} .
If  2\mid g , all-even partitions count  Q_{2}  exactly.
For each odd  p\mid g , put  h=m/p-1 . If  h\leq 1 , then  \deg Q_{p}=ha\leq a ,
so there is nothing to prove at weight  s .
For  h\geq 2 , use partitions with exactly one odd part  p  when  s  is odd,
and exactly two odd parts  3,p  when  s  is even. For  p=3  the latter means
two copies of  3 . The remaining parts are even. Thus different primes use
disjoint classes, also disjoint from the all-even class when it is present. 
 
 
 Let  \varepsilon=1  or  2  according as  s  is odd or even, and put 
 

 
 
 E=\left\lfloor\frac{m-1}{2}\right\rfloor,\quad\ell_{\varepsilon}=\left\lfloor\frac{a-3\varepsilon}{2}\right\rfloor,\quad w_{1}=p,\quad w_{2}=p+3. 
 
 
 After removing these odd parts, we may use at most  \ell_{\varepsilon}  even
parts, each at most  2E . Halving the even parts gives the target count
 [z^{v}]\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{E+\ell_\varepsilon}{E}_{z} , where  v=(s-w_{\varepsilon})/2 .
A nonzero source coefficient has  p\mid s  and  s\leq ha . By Gaussian
symmetry it equals  [z^{u}]\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{h+a/p}{h}_{z} , where
 u=\min(s,ha-s)/p .
The comparison has a simple diagram interpretation. Divide the original
parts by  p  and conjugate to obtain a partition in the  h\times(a/p) 
rectangle. If  s>ha/2 , take its rectangular
complement, so its size is  u . After transposing this rectangle if needed,
view the same Ferrers diagram inside the target rectangle. This injects the
source partitions into the target partitions of size  u .
Symmetry and unimodality compare the target coefficients at  u  and  v ,
provided 
 

 
 (7) 
 
 h\times(a/p)\text{ fits in }E\times\ell_{\varepsilon},\qquad 0\leq u\leq v\leq E\ell_{\varepsilon}-u, 
 
 
 where transposition of the source rectangle is allowed. Gaussian unimodality
follows from the same Sylvester theorem used in Lemma  1 . 
 
 
 Verification of the two bounds. 
Since  h\geq 2 , we have  a\geq m\geq 3p , and hence  s>3p .
For  u\leq v , it suffices that  (p-2)s\geq pw_{\varepsilon} .
When  s  is odd, use  s>3p  and  3(p-2)\geq p .
When  s  is even and  p\geq 5 , use  s>3p  and  3(p-2)\geq p+3 .
For  p=3  and even  s , the hypotheses give  m\geq 12  and  6\mid s ;
therefore  s>a\geq m  implies  s\geq 18 , as required.
Also  u+v\leq(ha-w_{\varepsilon})/2 . Thus the upper bound in ( 7 )
follows if 
 

 
 (8) 
 
 \eta_{\varepsilon}:=2E\ell_{\varepsilon}-ha+w_{\varepsilon}\geq 0. 
 
 
 We check the rectangle inclusion and ( 8 ) together. It suffices to
treat  \varepsilon=2 :  \ell_{1}\geq\ell_{2}+1  and
 \eta_{1}\geq\eta_{2}+2E-3 , with  2E\geq 3 . Indeed  m\geq 12  for  p=3 
(the value  m=9  would make  g  a prime power), and  m\geq 15  for  p\geq 5 . 
 
 
 If  p\geq 5 , then  a\geq m\geq 15 ,  h\leq E ,  \ell_{2}\geq(a-7)/2\geq a/p , and 
 

 
 
 2E\ell_{2}-ha\geq\frac{(m-2)(a-7)}{2}-(m/5-1)a=\frac{m(3a-35)}{10}+7>0. 
 
 
 For  p=3 , write  m=3r ,  a=3t , so  t\geq r\geq 4 . Both  E  and  \ell_{2}  are at least  r-1 . If  t\geq 6 , then  \ell_{2}\geq t ; otherwise  E\geq 5\geq t . Thus the rectangles fit. As  a  increases by three,  \ell_{2}  increases by at least one and  2E>3h , so  \eta_{2}  increases. At  a=m  its value is 
 

 
 
 \eta_{2}=\begin{cases}(m-6)(m-12)/6,&m\text{ even},\\
((m-9)^{2}-24)/6,&m\text{ odd}.\end{cases} 
 
 
 These are nonnegative, since  m\geq 12  and odd  m\geq 15 .
At the smallest case  m=a=12 , the source rectangle is  3\times 4  and
the target is  5\times 3 , illustrating why transposition is allowed in ( 7 ).
Thus ( 7 ) holds in every case. The disjoint classes dominate the
prime sum in ( 5 ) for  s>a , and the low weights were already proved.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 3.  Positivity of the corrected quotient 

 
 The following division lemma turns symmetric unimodality into positivity
of a quotient. 
 
 
 Lemma 3 . 
 
 Let  r,\tau  be positive integers and let  P\in\mathbb{N}[q]  be symmetric and
unimodal. If  P/[r]_{q^{\tau}}  is a polynomial, it belongs to  \mathbb{N}[q] . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 This is  [ 13 , Lemma 5.1] , with exponents  \tau  and  r\tau .
We include the short argument. Write  P=\sum_{j=0}^{D}p_{j}q^{j}  with  p_{j}=p_{D-j} ,
allowing zero coefficients at both ends, and set  p_{j}=0  outside  [0,D] .
Below the midpoint of the quotient its coefficient at  k  is 
 

 
 
 \sum_{t\geq 0}(p_{k-tr\tau}-p_{k-\tau-tr\tau})\geq 0. 
 
 
 All indices lie at or below  (D-(r-1)\tau)/2\leq D/2 , where the
coefficients of  P  are nondecreasing. Symmetry gives nonnegativity for the
remaining coefficients. Division by the monic polynomial  [r]_{q^{\tau}} 
gives integrality. This also covers a specified centre with zero
coefficients at both ends, and the case  P=0 .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  2 . 
 
 Lemma  2  gives  A\in\mathbb{N}[q]  for all  n>m .
For  H , we first prove polynomiality and remove the small widths.
We then write  H  as a sum of two nonnegative polynomials. 
 
 
 1. Polynomiality. 
Let  \zeta  have order  e\mid m , with  e>1 . For  d\mid g , the order of
 \zeta^{d}  is  e^{\prime}=e/\gcd(e,d) , and  e^{\prime}\mid m/d .
If  e\nmid n , then  e^{\prime}\nmid n/d . In the Gaussian  Q_{d}(\zeta) , the
bottom residue is  e^{\prime}-1  and the top residue is smaller.
The  q -Lucas evaluation  [ 22 , Theorem 2.2, pp. 131–132] 
(see also the earlier proofs  [ 17 ,  8 ] ) therefore gives
 Q_{d}(\zeta)=0 , including  G(\zeta)=0  when  d=1 .
If  e\mid n , the same evaluation gives 
 

 
 
 G(\zeta)=f(e),\qquad Q_{d}(\zeta)=f(\lcm(d,e)),\qquad f(t)=\binom{n/t-1}{m/t-1}. 
 
 
 Since  e\mid g , choose a prime  p\mid e . Pair squarefree divisors of  g 
by inserting or deleting  p . The pairs have the same  \lcm(d,e)  and
opposite Möbius signs. Hence  \sum_{d\mid g}\mu(d)Q_{d}(\zeta)=0 , so
 A(\zeta)=G(\zeta) . Also  [C]_{\zeta}=1 .
In both cases the numerator of  H  vanishes at  \zeta .
Since  [m]_{q}  is monic with simple roots,  H\in\mathbb{Z}[q] . 
 
 
 2. Symmetry and small widths. 
For each  d\mid g , 
 

 
 (9) 
 
 \frac{Q_{d}}{[m]_{q}}=\frac{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n/d}{m/d}_{q^{d}}}{[n]_{q}}. 
 
 
 Gaussian symmetry therefore gives  H_{n,m}=H_{n,n-m} .
We may therefore assume  a=n-m\geq m  when proving positivity.
If  m=1 , then  H=1 ; if  m=2  and  n  is odd, then
 H=[(n-1)/2]_{q^{2}} . For even  n\geq 4 , one has  G=[n-1]_{q}  and  A=1 , so 
 

 
 
 H_{n,2}=\frac{[n-1]_{q}-[n+1]_{q}}{[2]_{q}}=-q^{n-1}. 
 
 
 Symmetry gives the same value at  m=n-2 , so these exceptions are
necessary. It remains to prove positivity for  a\geq m\geq 3 . 
 
 
 Put  T=(m-1)a ,  L=T+1-C=(m-2)a-m , and
 \delta_{s}=[q^{s}](1-q)G . Lemmas  1  and  2  give 
 

 
 
 \delta_{s}\geq A_{s}:=[q^{s}]A\qquad(0\leq s\leq T/2). 
 
 
 We will subtract intervals from the usual unimodal expansion of  G ,
using these inequalities to keep the coefficients nonnegative. The
subtraction leaves a reflected correction, which we treat first. 
 
 
 3. The reflected correction. 
We claim that 
 

 
 (10) 
 
 W=\frac{A(q)-q^{L}A(q^{-1})}{1-q^{m}}\in\mathbb{N}[q]. 
 
 
 For  d\mid g ,  d>1 , let  D_{d}=(m/d-1)a=\deg Q_{d}  and
 b_{d}=L-D_{d}=(m-1-m/d)a-m .
Then  b_{d}\geq a-m\geq 0  and  g\mid b_{d} .
A root of  [m/g]_{q^{g}}  has order dividing  m  but not  g , and therefore
not  n . Step 1 gives  [m/g]_{q^{g}}\mid Q_{d} .
Both polynomials are in  t=q^{d} , with divisor  [m/g]_{t^{g/d}} .
Since  Q_{d}  is symmetric and unimodal in  t , Lemma  3  in
that variable gives 
 

 
 
 K_{d}=\frac{(1-q^{b_{d}})Q_{d}}{1-q^{m}}=[b_{d}/g]_{q^{g}}\frac{Q_{d}}{[m/g]_{q^{g}}}\in\mathbb{N}[q], 
 
 
 where  [0]_{q^{g}}=0 .
Reciprocity of  Q_{d}  now gives
 W=-\sum_{d\mid g,\,d>1}\mu(d)K_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[q] . 
 
 
 If  g=1 , then  W=0 . Otherwise let  p  be its least prime divisor and
 V=A-Q_{p}\mathrel{\geq_{q}}0 . If  g  is a prime power, then  V=0  and  W=K_{p} .
If the next prime divisor is  p^{\prime}\geq 3 , the union interpretation gives 
 

 
 
 \deg V\leq(m/p^{\prime}-1)a\leq L/2, 
 
 
 since 
 

 
 
 \frac{L}{2}-(m/p^{\prime}-1)a=\frac{m}{2}\bigl((1-2/p^{\prime})a-1\bigr)\geq 0. 
 
 
 In either case, 
 

 
 
 W-K_{p}=\frac{1}{[m]_{q}}\sum_{0\leq j\leq L/2}[q^{j}]V\,q^{j}[L-2j]_{q}. 
 
 
 The nonzero intervals in the numerator all have centre  (L-1)/2 .
Their sum is nonnegative, symmetric and unimodal. Since  W-K_{p}  is a
polynomial, Lemma  3  gives  W-K_{p}\mathrel{\geq_{q}}0  and proves ( 10 ). 
 
 
 4. Symmetric unimodal completion. 
We now use  \delta_{s}\geq A_{s}  to form 
 

 
 (11) 
 
 \begin{split}P&=\sum_{0\leq s\leq T/2}(\delta_{s}-A_{s})q^{s}[T+1-2s]_{q}\\
&=G-\frac{A(q)-q^{T+1}A(q^{-1})}{1-q}.\end{split} 
 
 
 The second equality follows from the first-difference expansion of  G 
and the bound
 \deg A\leq(m/2-1)a<T/2  from ( 5 ); the equality also holds if  A=0 .
The first expression is a nonnegative sum of intervals with centre  T/2 .
Thus  P  is nonnegative, symmetric and unimodal.
Since  T+1=C+L , direct cancellation yields 
 

 
 (12) 
 
 H=\frac{P}{[m]_{q}}+q^{C}W. 
 
 
 Since  H,W\in\mathbb{Z}[q] , the quotient  P/[m]_{q}  is a polynomial.
It is nonnegative by Lemma  3 , and  W  is nonnegative by
step 3. Hence  H\in\mathbb{N}[q] .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  1  and the exceptional cases. 
 
 Theorem  2  and ( 3 ) prove  R_{n,m}\in\mathbb{N}[q]  for
the asserted pairs, and ( 1 ) gives  F_{n,m}\in\mathbb{N}[[q]]  when  m\geq 2 .
For  m=1  both expressions equal  1 . 
 
 
 For even  n\geq 4 , the excluded width two has 
 

 
 
 R_{n,2}=[n]_{q^{2}}-q^{n-1},\qquad F_{n,2}=\frac{1-q^{n-1}}{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{n+1})}. 
 
 
 In each expression the coefficient of  q^{n-1}  is  -1 .
Gaussian symmetry gives 
 

 
 
 F_{n,n-2}=\frac{F_{n,2}}{(q^{n+2};q)_{n-4}},\qquad R_{n,n-2}=R_{n,2}[(n-2)/2]_{q^{2(n+1)}}. 
 
 
 The additional factors have constant term  1  and no other term of degree
at most  n-1 , so the same negative coefficient persists. Thus all the
exceptions in Theorem  1  are necessary.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Dennis Stanton observed that Theorem  2  implies the following
corollary, and suggested the flag extension in Section  4 .
The corollary proves the free-orbit assertion for subspaces in Reiner,
Stanton and White  [ 21 , Conjecture 10.3] : the case  a=1  and
 \mathbf{k}=(m,n-m) . For prime powers  q , the quotient below counts orbits
of size  [n]_{q}  of the Singer cycle
 \mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}  on the  m -dimensional
 \mathbb{F}_{q} -subspaces of  \mathbb{F}_{q^{n}} ; its polynomiality was proved
in  [ 21 , Corollary 10.2] . 
 
 
 Corollary 1 . 
 
 For  0<m<n , 
 

 
 
 \frac{1}{[n]_{q}}\sum_{d\mid\gcd(n,m)}\mu(d)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n/d}{m/d}_{q^{d}}\in\mathbb{N}[q]. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Put 
 

 
 
 B=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid g\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n/d}{m/d}_{q^{d}}. 
 
 
 We again use the subset construction of Lemma  2 , now with
 m -subsets  S  of  \{1,\ldots,n\}  of weight
 \sum_{t\in S}t-\binom{m+1}{2} . The sets 
 

 
 
 Y_{d}=\{S:S\mathbin{\triangle}\{1,\ldots,m\}\subseteq d\mathbb{Z}\}\qquad(d\mid g) 
 
 
 have generating polynomials  \genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n/d}{m/d}_{q^{d}}  and satisfy
 Y_{d}\cap Y_{e}=Y_{\lcm(d,e)} . Inclusion–exclusion therefore expresses
 B  as the generating polynomial of  \bigcup_{p\mid g}Y_{p} , where  p 
runs over the prime divisors of  g . In particular,  B\in\mathbb{N}[q] . 
 
 
 Identity ( 9 ) gives  B=[n]_{q}A/[m]_{q} , and hence 
 

 
 
 \frac{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{m}_{q}-B}{[n]_{q}}=H+qB, 
 
 
 since  [n+1]_{q}=1+q[n]_{q} .
Theorem  2  proves the assertion except when  n  is even
and  m\in\{2,n-2\} . In these cases  B=[n/2]_{q^{2}}  and  H=-q^{n-1} ,
so the quotient is  q[n/2-1]_{q^{2}}\in\mathbb{N}[q] .
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 4.  Orbit polynomials of flags 

 
 For a composition  \mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})  of  N , write 
 

 
 
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{k_1,\ldots,k_r}_{q}=\frac{(q;q)_{N}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(q;q)_{k_{i}}}. 
 
 
 We now prove the flag case of Conjecture 10.3 of Reiner, Stanton and
White  [ 21 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 3 . 
 
 Let  \mathbf{k}  be a composition of  N  and let  n  be a positive divisor
of  N . Then 
 

 
 
 \mathcal{O}_{n,\mathbf{k}}(q)=\frac{1}{[n]_{q}}\sum_{d\mid\gcd(n,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})}\mu(d)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N/d}{k_1/d,\ldots,k_r/d}_{q^{d}}\in\mathbb{N}[q]. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Here  N=an  in the notation of  [ 21 ] , and the quotient is the
free-orbit polynomial. For prime powers  q , it counts the orbits of
size  [n]_{q}  of
 \mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}  on flags in
 \mathbb{F}_{q^{N}}  with successive dimension increments  k_{1},\ldots,k_{r} .
For  d\mid n , the number of orbits with stabilizer
 \mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}  is
 \mathcal{O}_{n/d,\mathbf{k}/d}(q^{d})  if  d\mid\gcd(n,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}) ,
and zero otherwise  [ 21 , Proposition 9.2] .
Their sum counts all orbits. Thus Theorem  3  covers
every orbit size and every  a\geq 1 . 
 
 
 The proof needs the following coefficient comparison for the full
Gaussian polynomial. 
 
 
 Lemma 4 . 
 
 Let  a\geq b>0 ,  g=\gcd(a,b) , and put 
 

 
 
 T_{d}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{(a+b)/d}{b/d}_{q^{d}}\quad(d\mid g),\qquad B=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid g\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)T_{d}. 
 
 
 Then  B\in\mathbb{N}[q]  and 
 

 
 
 [q^{s}](1-q)T_{1}\geq[q^{s}]B\qquad(0\leq s\leq ab/2). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The subset union in Corollary  1  gives
 0\leq_{q}B\leq_{q}\sum_{p\mid g}T_{p} , with  p  prime.
By Lemma  1 , the first differences are bounded below by
 \mathcal{B}_{a,b} . Since  T_{d}  counts partitions with parts  d,2d,\ldots,b 
and at most  a/d  parts, the low-weight and prime-power arguments of
Lemma  2  give  B\leq_{q}\mathcal{B}_{a,b}  for  s\leq a , and for
all  s  if  g  is a prime power. The case  g=1  is immediate. 
 
 
 Suppose  g  has at least two distinct prime factors and  s>a .
We bound the prime sum by constructing independent semi-invariants.
Put  c_{j}=[y^{j}]\Phi_{3}(y) , as in Lemma  1 . For
 2\leq j\leq b ,  c_{j}  is a cubic semi-invariant of weight  j , and
 c_{j}=f_{j}  when  j  is odd. If  2\mid g , the even  f_{j}  give monomials counted
by  T_{2} , after padding with  x_{0}  to degree  a . For each odd prime
 p\mid g , multiply monomials in the even  f_{j}  by  c_{p}  for odd  s 
and by  c_{p+3}  for even  s , then pad to degree  a . These markers are
available since  b\geq 2p\geq p+3 . 
 
 
 To prove independence, use 
 

 
 
 \Phi_{3}(y)\Phi_{3}(-y)=\Phi_{2}(y)^{2}\Phi_{2}(2y). 
 
 
 Since  c_{0}=x_{0}^{3}  and  c_{1}=0 , coefficient comparison expresses each
even  c_{k}  recursively in the even  f_{j}  and odd  c_{j}  with  j\leq k-3 ,
over  \mathbb{Q}(x_{0}) . In particular, 
 

 
 
 c_{6}=\frac{c_{3}^{2}}{2x_{0}^{3}}+R_{3},\qquad c_{p+3}=\frac{c_{3}c_{p}}{x_{0}^{3}}+R_{p}\quad(p>3), 
 
 
 where  R_{3}  involves only even generators, and  R_{p}  involves even
generators and odd  c_{j}  with  j<p .
By Lemma  1 , the even  f_{j}  and odd  c_{j}  are algebraically
independent. At even weight, compare the largest prime first, then
 c_{3}^{2}  if  3\mid g , and finally the all-even family. At odd weight,
compare the distinct  c_{p} . The even monomials within each family are
also independent. By Sylvester’s formula, it remains to count them. 
 
 
 Each odd-prime marker has degree three, so its family contributes 
 

 
 
 [z^{v}]\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{E+\ell}{E}_{z},\qquad E=\lfloor b/2\rfloor,\quad\ell=\lfloor(a-3)/2\rfloor,\quad v=(s-w)/2, 
 
 
 where  w=p  or  p+3  according as  s  is odd or even.
For a nonzero coefficient of  T_{p} , put  h=b/p  and
 u=\min(s,ha-s)/p . By the comparison in Lemma  2 ,
it suffices to show that 
 

 
 
 h\times(a/p)\text{ fits in }E\times\ell,\qquad 0\leq u\leq v\leq E\ell-u. 
 
 
 
 
 Except when  (a,b)=(6,6) , we have  (p-2)a\geq 4p : this follows from
 a\geq b\geq 2p  for  p\geq 5 , while  p=3  forces  a\geq 12 .
Thus  h\leq E  and  \ell\geq(a-4)/2\geq a/p , so the rectangles fit.
Since  s>a  and  p\mid s , 
 

 
 
 (p-2)\frac{s}{p}\geq(p-2)\left(\frac{a}{p}+1\right)\geq p+2. 
 
 
 When  s  is even the left side is even, hence at least  p+3 ;
in either parity it is at least  w . Consequently  u\leq s/p\leq v .
Also  u+v\leq(ha-w)/2 , so the remaining bound follows from
 \eta_{w}:=2E\ell-ab/p+w\geq 0 . If  b  is even, then 
 

 
 
 \eta_{w}=b(\ell-a/p)+w\geq w>0. 
 
 
 If  b  is odd, the two distinct common primes force  a\geq b\geq 15 , and 
 

 
 
 \eta_{w}\geq\frac{(b-1)(a-4)}{2}-\frac{ab}{3}+3=\frac{(a-12)(b-3)}{6}-1\geq 5. 
 
 
 
 
 For  (a,b)=(6,6) , only the count needs a separate check. Above weight
 6 ,  T_{3}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{4}{2}_{q^{3}}  contributes once at weights  9  and  12 .
The same construction gives  x_{0}c_{3}f_{6}  and  x_{0}c_{6}f_{6} , respectively,
both of degree six and independent of the all-even family. Thus the
marked families dominate the prime sum for every  s>a .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  3 . 
 
 By  [ 21 , Corollary 10.2] , the quotient lies in  \mathbb{Z}[q] .
If  r=1 , the quotient is  1  for  n=1  and  0  otherwise.
Assume  r\geq 2 , and put 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle M 
 \displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N}{k_1,\ldots,k_r}_{q},\qquad D=\sum_{i<j}k_{i}k_{j}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle g 
 \displaystyle=\gcd(n,k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}),\qquad B=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid g\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N/d}{k_1/d,\ldots,k_r/d}_{q^{d}}. 
 
 
 Then  \mathcal{O}_{n,\mathbf{k}}=(M-B)/[n]_{q} . 
 
 
 First factor  M=\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}G_{i} , where
 N_{i}=k_{i}+\cdots+k_{r}  and  G_{i}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N_i}{k_i}_{q} .
Let  B_{i}  be its correction from Lemma  4 , using all
primes dividing  \gcd(N_{i},k_{i}) . By Gaussian symmetry we may take the
smaller rectangle side as the width.
Apply the weighted  k_{i} -subset construction of Corollary  1 
to each factor, with sets  Y_{i,d} .
For  d\mid g , the product  Y_{d}=\prod_{i}Y_{i,d}  has generating
polynomial  \genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N/d}{k_1/d,\ldots,k_r/d}_{q^{d}}  and satisfies
 Y_{d}\cap Y_{e}=Y_{\lcm(d,e)} .
Thus  B  counts  \bigcup_{p\mid g}\prod_{i}Y_{i,p} , which is contained in
 \prod_{i}\bigcup_{p\mid\gcd(N_{i},k_{i})}Y_{i,p} , whence 
 

 
 
 0\leq_{q}B\leq_{q}\prod_{i}B_{i}. 
 
 
 Also  \deg B\leq D/2  when  B\neq 0 , since each term with  d>1  has
degree  D/d . 
 
 
 For a nonnegative symmetric unimodal polynomial  F , with
 F(q)=q^{L}F(q^{-1}) , put 
 

 
 
 \Delta F=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\bigl([q^{j}](1-q)F\bigr)q^{j}. 
 
 
 The symmetric interval expansion and 
 

 
 
 [u]_{q}[v]_{q}=\sum_{t=0}^{\min(u,v)-1}q^{t}[u+v-1-2t]_{q}\qquad(u,v\geq 1) 
 
 
 give  \Delta(FG)\mathrel{\geq_{q}}(\Delta F)(\Delta G)  for two such polynomials
 F,G , by retaining the interval with  t=0  in each product.
Since  M  has symmetry degree  D , Lemma  4  gives 
 

 
 
 \Delta M\mathrel{\geq_{q}}\prod_{i}\Delta G_{i}\mathrel{\geq_{q}}\prod_{i}B_{i}\mathrel{\geq_{q}}B. 
 
 
 
 
 To apply Lemma  3 , write  \delta_{j}=[q^{j}](1-q)M  and
 b_{j}=[q^{j}]B , and put 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle P 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}(\delta_{j}-b_{j})q^{j}[D+1-2j]_{q}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle V 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b_{j}q^{j}[D-2j]_{q}. 
 
 
 Both are nonnegative, symmetric and unimodal;  V=0  is allowed.
The interval expansions give 
 

 
 
 V=\frac{B(q)-q^{D}B(q^{-1})}{1-q},\qquad M-B=P+qV. 
 
 
 Since  (M-B)/[n]_{q}  is a polynomial,  B(\zeta)=M(\zeta)  at every
nontrivial  n th root of unity  \zeta . By reciprocity, 
 

 
 
 B(\zeta)=M(\zeta)=\zeta^{D}M(\zeta^{-1})=\zeta^{D}B(\zeta^{-1}), 
 
 
 so  V(\zeta)=0 . Hence  V/[n]_{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[q] , and
 P/[n]_{q}=\mathcal{O}_{n,\mathbf{k}}-qV/[n]_{q}  is also an integral polynomial.
Lemma  3  makes both quotients nonnegative, and
 \mathcal{O}_{n,\mathbf{k}}=P/[n]_{q}+qV/[n]_{q}  proves the theorem.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 5.  Remarks and questions 

 
 Theorem  1  leaves several combinatorial questions. We first compare
its product formula with other positivity results.
Gatzweiler and Krattenthaler  [ 11 ]  study quotients
of Gaussian polynomials with a common top argument (Conjecture 1), and
products  \prod_{i}(1-q^{r+i})^{a_{i}}/(1-q^{i})^{a_{i}}  with a symmetric
exponent sequence (Conjecture 3). The latter extend Stanton’s fake
Gaussian sequences  [ 23 ] .
In  R_{n,m}  the factor  [m]_{q^{n+1}}  dilates the exponents of  [m]_{q} ;
in those products a common shift translates the degrees. We do not
identify  R_{n,m}  as a specialization of either family.
For cyclotomic generating functions more generally, see
Billey and Swanson  [ 3 ]  and Gatzweiler,
Levicán-Santibáñez and Yoshida  [ 12 ] . 
 
 
 There is a simple obstruction to applying the regular-sequence criterion
of  [ 12 , Proposition 3.3]  to the product 
 

 
 
 R_{n,m}=\frac{(1-q^{mC})\prod_{j=n-m+1}^{n-1}(1-q^{j})}{(1-q^{C})\prod_{j=2}^{m}(1-q^{j})},\qquad C=n+1 
 
 
 for  m\geq 2 . The criterion requires every selection of denominator
degrees to generate an additive monoid containing at least as many
numerator degrees, counted with multiplicity.
Suppose  g=\gcd(n,m)>1 . Select the  m/g+1  denominator degrees
 C,g,2g,\ldots,m . They generate  \langle g,C\rangle .
Below  C , this monoid contains only multiples of  g , so it contains
exactly  m/g-1  degrees from the numerator interval and also the degree
 mC . There are only  m/g  such numerator degrees.
Thus the criterion fails whenever  g>1 , even in positive cases such as
 (n,m)=(6,3) . This concerns the displayed product; another Hilbert-series
realization may still be possible. 
 
 
 At  q=1  there is an elementary necklace interpretation.
Let  \mathcal{N}_{n,m}^{\rm prim}  be the binary necklaces of length  n 
with  m  ones and least period  n . Möbius inversion on repetitions,
followed by division by the orbit size  n , gives 
 

 
 
 \#\mathcal{N}_{n,m}^{\rm prim}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid g}\mu(d)\binom{n/d}{m/d},\qquad G(1)-A(1)=m\,\#\mathcal{N}_{n,m}^{\rm prim}. 
 
 
 The second equality uses
 \binom{n/d-1}{m/d-1}=(m/n)\binom{n/d}{m/d} .
Lemma  2  interprets  G-A  at general  q  as the weight
enumerator of subsets outside  \bigcup_{p\mid g}X_{p} .
It would be interesting to find a cyclic action and a compatible statistic
giving a cyclic-sieving interpretation of these polynomials. 
 
 
 A direct proof of the original conjecture would give an injection between
successive partition sets. Let  \mathcal{P}_{n,m}(N)  be the set counted by
 a_{n,m}(N) . 
 
 
 Problem 1 . 
 
 For each nonexceptional pair  0<m<n , give a uniform combinatorial rule
for an injection from  \mathcal{P}_{n,m}(N)  into
 \mathcal{P}_{n,m}(N+1)  for every  N\geq 0 . 
 
 
 
 The polynomial version suggests a second question. 
 
 
 Problem 2 . 
 
 Find a natural nonnegative integer statistic on compositions of  n  into
 m  positive parts such that, for every nonexceptional pair  0<m<n , 
 

 
 
 R_{n,m}(q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}=n\\
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\geq 1\end{subarray}}q^{\operatorname{stat}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Since  R_{n,m}(1)=\binom{n-1}{m-1} , Theorem  1  permits an
arbitrary assignment of exponents to compositions. We ask for a direct
combinatorial rule. When  m\geq 2 , one could then use ( 1 ) to count
 a_{n,m}(N)-a_{n,m}(N-1)  by adjoining a partition with parts in
 \{m(n+1)\}\cup\{n+2,\ldots,n+m-1\} , the latter set being empty for  m=2 . 
 
 
 There is such a statistic when  n,m  are coprime. The rational shuffle
theorem  [ 16 ]  and the specialization in
Garsia–Leven–Wallach–Xin  [ 10 , identity (I.1)]  give 
 

 
 
 \frac{1}{[m]_{q}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n-1}{m-1}_{q}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{m,n-m}}q^{\operatorname{coarea}(D)+\operatorname{dinv}(D)}. 
 
 
 Here  \mathcal{D}_{m,n-m}  consists of Dyck paths above the diagonal in the
 m\times(n-m)  rectangle, with the statistics of  [ 10 ] .
Given a composition  (a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}) , the cycle lemma gives a unique
cyclic rotation of  (a_{1}-1,\ldots,a_{m}-1)  encoding the numbers of north
steps before successive east steps of a path  D . Let
 j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}  count the left cyclic rotations taking this
encoding back to the original tuple. Then 
 

 
 
 \operatorname{stat}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=(n+1)j+\operatorname{coarea}(D)+\operatorname{dinv}(D) 
 
 
 has generating polynomial  R_{n,m} . The remaining question is to find
a rule for the noncoprime pairs.
