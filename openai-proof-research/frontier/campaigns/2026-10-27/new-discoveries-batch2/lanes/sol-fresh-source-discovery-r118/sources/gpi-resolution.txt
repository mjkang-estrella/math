A proof of the strong Gaussian product inequality conjecture



Frédéric Ouimet


Dylan Greaves

Université du Québec à Trois-Rivières, Trois-Rivières, Canada

San Francisco, USA





Abstract

Let 𝑿=(X1,…,Xn)\boldsymbol{X}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}) be a centered Gaussian vector, not necessarily nondegenerate. It is proved that, for every α1,…,αn>0\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}>0,





𝖤​[∏i=1n|Xi|αi]≥∏i=1n𝖤​[|Xi|αi].\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}\right]\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{E}\left[|X_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}\right].




When all marginal variances are positive, equality holds if and only if the coordinates are independent. This settles Frenkel’s 18-year-old Gaussian product inequality (GPI) conjecture and, in fact, its later strengthening to arbitrary positive exponents. Through Frenkel’s hafnian formulation, this result also provides a short proof of the 28-year-old real linear polarization constant conjecture, which was settled this year as a consequence of the strong polarization inequality. The main result leads to the exact real linear polarization constant, a sharp weighted product inequality for real linear functionals, spherical moment bounds, hafnian inequalities for positive-semidefinite matrices together with complete characterizations of their equality cases, logarithmic variance and covariance inequalities, a Rényi total-correlation certificate, and unconditional versions of mixed-sign Gaussian moment bounds previously conditional on the positive-exponent GPI.



keywords:  Gaussian product inequality , Gaussian random vector , hafnian , divergence , mixed-sign moments , real linear polarization constant , strong polarization inequality




2020 MSC: Primary: 60E15, 60E05; Secondary: 15A15, 44A10, 46C05, 46N30, 60G15





1 Introduction




The Gaussian product inequality (GPI) conjecture grew out of the real linear polarization constant problem in functional analysis. A norm-one linear functional on a Hilbert space attains its norm on the unit ball, but several norm-one functionals need not be simultaneously large at the same point. The real linear polarization constant measures the worst possible loss in their product when one common point must be used. For continuous real linear functionals f1,…,fnf_{1},\ldots,f_{n} on a real Hilbert space ℋ\mathcal{H}, Benítez et al. [2] sought the optimal universal lower bound for the norm of the homogeneous polynomial ∏i=1nfi\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}. When dim(ℋ)≥n\dim(\mathcal{H})\geq n, their conjecture states that





‖∏i=1nfi‖≥n−n/2​∏i=1n‖fi‖.\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\right\|\geq n^{-n/2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|f_{i}\|.

(1)



Orthogonal functionals show that the factor n−n/2n^{-n/2} in (1) cannot be improved.






The corresponding problem over complex Hilbert spaces was solved by Arias-de-Reyna [1], whereas the real problem remained open for nearly three decades. As detailed in Section 4, Martínez and Ortega-Moreno [22] recently proved the strong polarization conjecture, a reciprocal-square strengthening of (1), and characterized all extremal configurations for the real linear polarization problem; Galicer et al. [5] subsequently proved a weighted extension.




In 2008, Frenkel [4] formulated the GPI conjecture, which states that for every centered Gaussian vector with covariance matrix Σ=(σi​j)\Sigma=(\sigma_{ij}) and every m∈ℕ≡{1,2,…}m\in\mathbb{N}\equiv\{1,2,\ldots\},





𝖤​[∏i=1nXi2​m]≥∏i=1n𝖤​[Xi2​m].\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2m}\right]\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{E}\left[X_{i}^{2m}\right].

(2)



By Wick’s formula, this takes the equivalent hafnian form haf⁡(Σ​[2​m])≥∏i=1n(2​m−1)!!​σi​im\operatorname{haf}\left(\Sigma[2m]\right)\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2m-1)!!\,\sigma_{ii}^{m}, where Σ​[2​m]\Sigma[2m] is the matrix obtained by repeating each row index and its corresponding column index exactly 2​m2m times. Frenkel observed that its validity for all positive integers mm would imply the sharp real linear polarization estimate in (1). The implication follows by representing the functionals as Gaussian linear forms, comparing their 2​m2mth moments with the radial moment of a standard Gaussian vector, and letting m→∞m\to\infty. Li and Wei [20] subsequently proposed a stronger conjecture allowing arbitrary positive exponents:





𝖤​[∏i=1n|Xi|αi]≥∏i=1n𝖤​[|Xi|αi],α1,…,αn>0.\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}\right]\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{E}\left[|X_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}\right],\qquad\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}>0.

(3)



The strong GPI conjecture (3) is proved in Theorem 1; this establishes both the original GPI conjecture (2) and Frenkel’s equivalent hafnian formulation.




Before the present work, partial positive-exponent GPI results (for given dimensions n≤5n\leq 5, restricted classes of covariance matrices, specific exponents, or a combination thereof) were obtained by [15, 4, 18, 8, 28, 3, 30, 29, 12, 17, 16, 13]. Negative-exponent and mixed-sign extensions were obtained by [33, 27, 14, 34, 25, 24, 19].
Closely related Wishart extensions appear in [9, 6, 7]. A chronological description of these contributions (except for [25, 24, 19, 13]) is given by Genest et al. [6]. The proof of the strong GPI herein uses methods entirely different from those developed in the GPI works cited above, so their approaches are not reviewed here. Although the proof does not use the strong polarization inequality proved by Martínez and Ortega-Moreno [22] or its weighted extension proved by Galicer et al. [5], one specialization of its central matrix identity recovers the cancellation underlying their proofs; see Section 3.




The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 proves the strong GPI along with the characterization of equality in terms of independence. Section 3 details the connection with the works of [22, 5]. Corollaries of the strong GPI are collected in Section 4.

4 Consequences of Theorem 1





The consequences collected in this section include the exact real linear polarization constant when dim(ℋ)≥n\dim(\mathcal{H})\geq n (Corollary 1), a sharp weighted product inequality for real linear functionals (Corollary 2), spherical product-moment bounds (Corollary 3), a family of unequal-multiplicity hafnian inequalities for positive-semidefinite matrices together with a complete characterization of their equality cases (Corollary 4), logarithmic variance and covariance inequalities (Corollary 5), and a Rényi total-correlation certificate (Corollary 6). The theorem also removes the positive-side GPI hypothesis from the mixed-sign lower bounds of Lan et al. [19] (Corollary 7).




As observed by Frenkel [4] and later emphasized by Malicet et al. [21], the even-exponent GPI in (2) implies the conjectured value of the real linear polarization constant. The conclusion is stated in the functional-analytic form introduced by Benítez et al. [2] and used in (1), together with its equivalent geometric formulation. Once Theorem 1 is available, the lower bound follows from the Riesz representation theorem, Gaussian radial decomposition, the standard Gaussian and chi moment formulas, and Stirling’s formula, while optimality follows from the AM–GM inequality and Bessel’s inequality.

Remark 4.2.




The weighted inequality in Corollary 2 was proved recently, in an equivalent normalized form, by Galicer et al. [5, Theorem 2], who established the optimality of its constant in Section 2.3. Gaussian moments and spherical averages are linked by radial decomposition, a connection already exploited by Frenkel [4] for products of squared linear forms. Combining this decomposition with Theorem 1 gives a benchmark for unequal powers that is sharp when d≥nd\geq n: orthogonal directions then minimize the spherical product moment. This turns the probabilistic inequality into a geometric integral inequality on every Euclidean sphere.




Corollary 3 (Spherical product moments).




Let EE be a dd-dimensional real Hilbert space, let 𝚯\boldsymbol{\Theta} be uniformly distributed on its unit sphere, let 𝐮1,…,𝐮n∈E\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{n}\in E be unit vectors, and let α1,…,αn>0\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}>0. Let A=∑i=1nαiA=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}. Then





𝖤​[∏i=1n|⟨𝚯,𝒖i⟩|αi]≥Γ​(d/2)Γ​((d+A)/2)​∏i=1nΓ​((αi+1)/2)π.\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\langle\boldsymbol{\Theta},\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\rangle|^{\alpha_{i}}\right]\geq\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma((d+A)/2)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma((\alpha_{i}+1)/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}}.




Equality holds if and only if 𝐮1,…,𝐮n\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{n} are pairwise orthogonal. In particular, if d≥nd\geq n, the constant is optimal.




Proof.



Let 𝑮\boldsymbol{G} be a standard Gaussian vector in EE and write 𝑮=S​𝚯\boldsymbol{G}=S\boldsymbol{\Theta}, where S=‖𝑮‖S=\|\boldsymbol{G}\| is independent of 𝚯\boldsymbol{\Theta}. Theorem 1 and the standard Gaussian and chi moment formulas give





𝖤​[SA]​𝖤​[∏i=1n|⟨𝚯,𝒖i⟩|αi]=𝖤​[∏i=1n|⟨𝑮,𝒖i⟩|αi]≥∏i=1n2αi/2​Γ​((αi+1)/2)π,\mathsf{E}[S^{A}]\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\langle\boldsymbol{\Theta},\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\rangle|^{\alpha_{i}}\right]=\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\langle\boldsymbol{G},\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\rangle|^{\alpha_{i}}\right]\geq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2^{\alpha_{i}/2}\Gamma((\alpha_{i}+1)/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}},




where 𝖤​[SA]=2A/2​Γ​((d+A)/2)/Γ​(d/2)\mathsf{E}[S^{A}]=2^{A/2}\Gamma((d+A)/2)/\Gamma(d/2). This proves the inequality. By the equality statement in Theorem 1, equality holds if and only if the Gaussian linear forms are independent, which is equivalent to the orthogonality of 𝒖1,…,𝒖n\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_{n}. Such a configuration exists when d≥nd\geq n.
∎

For α1=⋯=αn=2\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{n}=2, Corollary 3 recovers the spherical moment inequality of Frenkel [4, Theorem 2.2].





Wick’s formula identifies even Gaussian product moments with hafnians of repeated covariance matrices; see Frenkel [4, Eq. (4)]. Frenkel’s hafnian conjecture is an algebraic reformulation of the equal-even-exponent GPI. Theorem 1 proves Frenkel’s hafnian conjecture and yields the following extension to unequal multiplicities.

(14)

Proof.



Let 𝑿∼𝒩​(𝟎,Σ)\boldsymbol{X}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{0},\Sigma). Wick’s formula and the one-dimensional Gaussian moment formula give





𝖤​[∏i=1nXi2​mi]=haf⁡(Σ​[2​𝒎]),𝖤​[Xi2​mi]=(2​mi−1)!!​σi​imi.\mathsf{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2m_{i}}\right]=\operatorname{haf}\left(\Sigma[2\boldsymbol{m}]\right),\qquad\mathsf{E}\left[X_{i}^{2m_{i}}\right]=(2m_{i}-1)!!\,\sigma_{ii}^{m_{i}}.

Remark 4.4.




View the 2​mi2m_{i} copies of index ii as vertices, assign weight σi​j\sigma_{ij} to an edge joining a copy of ii to a copy of jj, and define the weight of a matching as the product of its edge weights. Let PP be the joint law of a random vector and let QQ be the product of its marginals. The total correlation introduced by Watanabe [32] is the Kullback–Leibler divergence DKL​(P∥Q)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q) and measures departure from mutual independence. A natural order-γ\gamma Rényi analogue of total correlation is obtained by replacing DKL​(P∥Q)D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\|Q) with Dγ​(P∥Q)D_{\gamma}(P\|Q) while keeping PP and QQ fixed; for background on Rényi divergence, see van Erven and Harremoës [31]. At order γ=2\gamma=2, when P≪QP\ll Q, this divergence is given by





D2​(P∥Q)=log​∫(d​P/d​Q)2​dQ,D_{2}(P\|Q)=\log\int(\mathrm{d}P/\mathrm{d}Q)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}Q,




and it is defined to be +∞+\infty otherwise. It is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when P=QP=Q, equivalently, when the coordinates are mutually independent. The next result converts the excess in Theorem 1 into an explicit lower bound on D2​(P∥Q)D_{2}(P\|Q). The bound uses a product moment as a test function and therefore requires no direct evaluation of the joint density ratio.

Mixed-sign moments combine inverse powers, which are singular near the origin, with positive powers, which emphasize the tails. Consequently, such moments are not controlled directly by a one-sided positive-moment inequality. Lan et al. [19] recently obtained a conditional-variance-adjusted lower bound contingent upon the corresponding lower-dimensional positive-side GPI. Theorem 1 removes that hypothesis in every dimension and makes the following estimate unconditional.
