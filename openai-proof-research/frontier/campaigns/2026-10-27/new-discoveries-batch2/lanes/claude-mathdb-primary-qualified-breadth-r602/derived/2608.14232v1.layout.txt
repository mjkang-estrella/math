                                                                  OLLIVIER–RICCI CURVATURE ON
                                                                 GROUPS OF POLYNOMIAL GROWTH

                                                                          CAMILLO BRENA AND ELIA BRUÈ




                                               Abstract. We study Ollivier–Ricci curvature on Cayley graphs of groups of polynomial
                                               growth. Our main result shows that non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature forces the group
                                               to be virtually abelian. As an application, we prove that connected vertex-transitive graphs
                                               of polynomial growth and non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature are quasi-isometric to Zk , for
                                               some k ∈ N.

   Let Γ be a finitely generated group and let S ⊆ Γ be a fixed finite symmetric set of generators.
We consider the Cayley graph of Γ with respect to S, i.e., the undirected graph whose vertex set
is Γ and such that g ∼ h if and only if g −1 h ∈ S. In other words, the edges are (g, gs)g∈Γ,s∈S .
Notice that to the Cayley graph is naturally associated a shortest-path distance d, which
corresponds to the algebraic word-length function defined in (2.1) below. Of course, d depends
on S, but we will not make this dependence explicit in this introduction to keep the notation
as light as possible. In this introduction, we will always assume e ∈    / S, which, in particular,
implies g ≁ g. Without this convention, (1.1), (1.3) and (1.9) below have to be corrected. This
assumption will be dropped when we set up the precise framework.

Remark 1.3. Another notion of Ricci curvature for graphs has been defined by Lin–Lu–Yau

  Given the flexibility in the choice of the optimal plan, one may wonder whether every Cayley
graph has non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. Of course, this is not the case, as the following
three examples show.
Example 1.4. Consider the (finite) dihedral group corresponding to the symmetries of the
hexagon, Γ := ⟨s, t | t6 = e, s2 = e, sts = t−1 ⟩ and let S := {s, st, st3 }, which is a finite symmet-
ric set of generators. A tedious computation shows that κ(e, st) < 0. Hence, the Cayley graph
of Γ with respect to S does not have non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature.
Example 1.5. If Γ := ⟨x1 , x2 | y := [x1 , x2 ] is central⟩ = H3 (Z) is the Heisenberg group, for every
finite symmetric set of generators S, the Cayley graph of Γ with respect to S does not have
non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. This is an immediate consequence of our Theorem 1.9
below.
Example 1.6. Let Γ := ⟨a, b⟩, the free group on two generators, where S := {a, a−1 , b, b−1 } is a
finite symmetric set of generators. It is easy to realize that the Cayley graph of Γ with respect
to S does not have non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. Actually, from the main result of
the forthcoming [4], for every finite symmetric set of generators S, the Cayley graph of Γ with
respect to S does not have non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature.
  On the other hand, we also have plenty of Cayley graphs of non-negative Ricci curvature.
Example 1.7. Let Γ be a finitely generated group and let S be a finite symmetric set of gener-
ators. Assume one of the following.
     • Γ is abelian.
     • S is conjugation invariant, meaning that tst−1 ∈ S for every t, s ∈ S.
     • Γ is finite and S = Γ \ {e}, meaning that the Cayley graph of Γ with respect to S is a
       complete graph.
Then, it is easy to verify that the Cayley graph of Γ with respect to S has non-negative Ricci
curvature.
  By this example, we have an abundance of Cayley graphs with non-negative Ollivier–Ricci
curvature. One may wonder whether the cases above are exhaustive. The answer is negative,
as the next example shows.
Example 1.8. Consider the infinite dihedral group Γ := ⟨s, t | s2 = e, sts = t−1 ⟩, with S :=
{s, t, t−1 }, which is a finite symmetric set of generators. Notice that any element of Γ can be
written either as stj or tj , for j ∈ Z.
  A direct computation shows that the Cayley graph of Γ with respect to S has non-negative
Ricci curvature. Moreover, Γ is infinite, virtually abelian (ΓN := ⟨t⟩ is cyclic and of finite index),
but not abelian. Finally, Γ does not possess any finite conjugation invariant set of generators.
Indeed, any set of generators must contain stj for some j, which satisfies tk stj t−k = stj−2k , and
hence has infinite conjugacy class.

  Thus, no classification of groups admitting Cayley graphs with non-negative Ollivier–Ricci
curvature can be extracted from Example 1.7. In our first main result, stated below, we give
the correct characterization of such groups for finitely generated virtually nilpotent groups.
Theorem 1.9. Let Γ be a finitely generated virtually nilpotent group. Then, the following are
equivalent
   (1) Γ is virtually abelian,
   (2) There exists a finite symmetric set of generators S such that the Cayley graph of Γ with
        respect to S has non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature.
   We recall that Γ is virtually nilpotent (resp. virtually abelian) if there exists a nilpotent
(resp. abelian) normal subgroup ΓN ⊴ Γ of finite index, i.e., Γ/ΓN < ∞. The class of virtually
nilpotent groups is relevant and well motivated in this setting. Indeed, the celebrated Gromov’s
Theorem [16] states that a finitely generated group Γ is virtually nilpotent if and only if it has
polynomial growth. Polynomial growth means that some (hence all) Cayley graph of the group
has polynomial growth as a metric space.
   Moreover, in the forthcoming [4], it is proved that groups admitting a Cayley graph of
non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature have polynomial growth. This not only makes the set-
ting natural, but implies that the virtually nilpotent assumption in Theorem 1.9 can in fact
be dropped, thus yielding a complete characterization of finitely generated groups admitting
Cayley graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature.

1.2. Ollivier–Ricci curvature at large scales. A somewhat unpleasant feature of the Ollivier–
Ricci curvature is that it depends on the set of generators. In particular, a group may have,
at the same time, Cayley graphs with and without non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. This
happens for instance for the finite dihedral group of Example 1.4 (which is of course virtu-
ally abelian), recall also Example 1.7. On the other hand, we have seen in Theorem 1.9 that
those finitely generated virtually nilpotent groups admitting a Cayley graph with non-negative
Ollivier–Ricci curvature are precisely the virtually abelian groups. It is then natural to ask
whether virtually abelian groups can be actually recognized by looking at some notion of cur-
vature, in a way that does not depend on the chosen set of generators. In other words, the
question is whether there exists some quantity (related to Ollivier–Ricci curvature) which is
suitable to tell apart the purely virtually nilpotent case and the virtually abelian case, inde-
pendently of the set of generators. As we will argue below, it turns out that the correct notion
is that of Ollivier–Ricci curvature at large scales, the intuition being that, asymptotically, the
effect played by the choice of the generators should play a smaller role.
   Another indication that this notion is more robust than plain Ollivier–Ricci curvature is the
role that it has in the proof of Theorem 1.14, where we are going to need the characterization
of finitely generated groups of polynomial growth with non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature
at large scales (but not necessarily with non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature). For details, see
that discussion below Theorem 1.14 and its proof.

1.3. Transitive graphs. Let G = (V, E) be an undirected graph. Here, V is the set of vertices
and E is the set of edges. For x, y ∈ V , we write x ∼ y if x and y are connected by an edge of
E. We assume throughout that graphs are connected and have bounded degree.
  A bijection φ : V → V is called a graph automorphism, and we write φ ∈ Aut(G), if
                     φ(x) ∼ φ(y) if and only if x ∼ y          for every x, y ∈ V .              (1.18)
Notice that to G = (V, E) is naturally associated a shortest-path distance d and that graph
automorphisms are precisely the isometries. We say that a graph is vertex-transitive, or simply
transitive, if the automorphism group acts transitively on the vertex set. Of course, Cayley
graphs are transitive graphs (multiplications by elements of the groups are automorphisms),
but the converse need not hold. However, see the discussion below Theorem 1.14 for a partial
converse.
  We have a natural generalization of the notion of Ollivier–Ricci curvature to this setting. We
assume in this introduction that the graph has no self-loops, i.e. x ≁ x. This assumption serves
only to simplify the notation and will later be removed. We consider, as in (1.1), the 1/2-lazy
symmetric random walk originating from x, namely

1.5. Nilpotent structures and curvature. The relation between nilpotent structures and
spaces with uniform curvature bounds has a long history. In 1978, Gromov proved his celebrated
almost flat manifold theorem, later generalized and refined in [10, 33]. It states that a compact
Riemannian manifold (M n , g) with sufficiently small sectional curvature | Secg | ≤ ε(n), and
bounded diameter diamg (M ) ≤ 1, is finitely covered by a nilmanifold, that is, by a quotient of
a nilpotent Lie group by a cocompact lattice. A basic example is the Heisenberg nilmanifold
Nil3 := H3 (R)/H3 (Z), see (5.9). Later, Fukaya developed a local version of this picture [14],
describing manifolds with bounded sectional curvature and sufficiently collapsed geometry in
terms of fibration structures whose fibers carry nilpotent features.
   It was then understood that the two-sided sectional curvature bound can be weakened to a
lower curvature bound, while still retaining nilpotent structure at the level of the local funda-
mental group. In [15], Fukaya–Yamaguchi proved a generalized Margulis-type result showing
that, under a lower sectional curvature bound Secg ≥ −1, the image of the local fundamental
group π1 (Bε(n) (p)) → π1 (B1 (p)) is virtually nilpotent. Later, Kapovitch–Petrunin–Tuschmann
[20] refined this analysis by proving quantitative bounds on the index and the nilpotency
length. Finally, Kapovitch–Wilking [21] established analogous results under lower Ricci curva-
ture bounds.
   In the setting of complete manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, the first results for
fundamental groups go back to Milnor’s 1968 work [26], where he proved polynomial growth
for finitely generated subgroups of the fundamental group. Combined with Gromov’s theorem
on groups of polynomial growth [16], this yields virtual nilpotency of every finitely generated
subgroup of the fundamental group. In the same 1968 paper, Milnor formulated his famous
conjecture that the fundamental group of a complete manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature
should always be finitely generated. This conjecture was disproved recently [8, 9].
   While lower curvature bounds, such as Secg ≥ −1 or Ricg ≥ −(n − 1), make nilpotent
structures appear, non-negative curvature often forces these structures to further rigidify to
abelian ones. This class of phenomena is closer in spirit to the main result of the present paper,
see Theorem 1.12. One of the first instances of this phenomenon appears in Milnor’s 1968 paper.
He observed that the fundamental group of the Heisenberg nilmanifold Nil3 := H3 (R)/H3 (Z)
has quartic polynomial growth, due to its nilpotent structure. This growth is faster than the
general bound he proved for finitely generated subgroups of fundamental groups of complete
n-dimensional manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, which is polynomial of degree at
most n. In particular, the Heisenberg nilmanifold cannot admit any Riemannian metric with
non-negative Ricci curvature, even though it admits metrics with sectional curvature arbitrarily
close to 0.
   More generally, Cheeger and Gromoll used their splitting theorem [11] to show that the
fundamental group of a compact manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature is virtually abelian.
The compactness assumption is fundamental, as shown by Wei [39]; see also [40]. Fukaya and
Yamaguchi conjectured [15] that, under non-negative sectional curvature, the index of the
abelian subgroup should be bounded by a constant depending only on the dimension. This
conjecture remains open. Recently, Bruè–Naber–Semola disproved the analogous statement
under non-negative Ricci curvature [7].
   Finally, we mention Carnot–Carathéodory structures, which naturally appear as blow-downs
of nilpotent structures; see Section 5. They are known not to satisfy lower Ricci curvature
bounds in the Lott–Sturm–Villani synthetic sense, see for instance [19, 24, 32, 13, 2, 18, 25], even
in basic examples such as the Heisenberg group. This is consistent with the heuristic that non-
abelian nilpotent structures carry negative curvature at some scale: after rescaling and passing
to the blow-down, this negative contribution degenerates in the limiting sub-Riemannian ge-
ometry.

   We conclude this section by presenting one further analogy between the discrete framework
of this paper and the Riemannian framework discussed above. Let G be an abstract group. By
[40, Theorem 2.1] ((2) ⇒ (1) is proved in [12, 11]), the following are equivalent:
   (1) G is finitely generated and virtually abelian;
   (2) there exists a complete Riemannian manifold (M    f, ge) with non-negative Ricci curvature
       such that G is a discrete closed subgroup of Iso(M  f, ge) acting freely and cocompactly;
   (3) there exists a complete Riemannian manifold (M   f, ge) with non-negative sectional curva-
       ture such that G is a discrete closed subgroup of Iso(Mf, ge) acting freely and cocompactly.
On the discrete side, the following are equivalent:
   (1’) G is finitely generated and virtually abelian;
   (2’) there exist a group Γ and a finite symmetric set of generators S such that the Cayley
        graph of Γ with respect to S has non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature, and G acts
        freely and cocompactly on it by graph isomorphism;
   (3’) there exist a group Γ and a finite symmetric set of generators S such that the Cayley
        graph of Γ with respect to S satisfies (3.7), and G acts freely and cocompactly on it by
        graph isomorphism.
Indeed, for (1′ ) ⇒ (3′ ), we take Γ = G and S given by Theorem 3.5, which is a strengthened
version of Theorem 1.9. Of course, G acts on itself by left-multiplication. (3′ ) ⇒ (2′ ) is trivial.
Now, take Γ and S as in (2′ ). By the main result of the forthcoming [4] and Gromov’s Theorem
[16], Γ is virtually nilpotent. By Theorem 1.9, Γ is virtually abelian, hence quasi-isometric to
Zd , for some d. As the Cayley graph of Γ is locally finite and the action is free, the action is
also proper. Hence, by the Švarc–Milnor Lemma (e.g., [6, Proposition 8.19]), we see that G is
quasi-isometric to Γ, hence quasi-isometric to Zd , for some d. Therefore, (1′ ) follows from [35,
Theorem 1.1].
   We finally remark that, in the above equivalence, Cayley graphs can be replaced by transitive
graphs. In particular, finitely generated virtually abelian groups are precisely those admitting
a free and cocompact action by graph automorphisms on a connected transitive graph of non-
negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. This is proved exactly as above.

   This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem 3.2. As anticipated in the introduction, it is
based on a duality argument: we construct a suitable Kantorovich potential by exploiting the
nilpotent, non-abelian structure. To this end, it is convenient, though not strictly necessary,
to first reduce to the case of step-two groups. This reduction is carried out in Section 6.1.
We then introduce Malcev coordinates and an auxiliary distance. The latter is introduced in
Subsection 6.3. Its meaning and role have been discussed in Section 5 in terms of the asymptotic
structure of virtually nilpotent groups.
6.1. Reduction to step two. In the notation of Section 2.1, we have the isolated lower central
series (2.9), consisting of normal subgroups of Γ. We apply Lemma 2.3 with K := ΓN,3 ⊴ Γ.
   It turns out that ΓN /ΓN,3 is nilpotent, torsion-free, non-abelian, and of step two. Indeed, it is
nilpotent because it is a quotient of the nilpotent group ΓN . It is torsion-free by the definition of
the isolated lower central series: if g k ∈ ΓN,3 for some k ≥ 1, then g ∈ ΓN,3 . Moreover, relation
(2.8) gives [ΓN , ΓN,2 ] ⊆ ΓN,3 , and therefore the commutator subgroup of ΓN /ΓN,3 is central.
Thus ΓN /ΓN,3 has nilpotency step at most two. Finally, since Γ is not virtually abelian, the
finite-index subgroup ΓN is not abelian. Hence ΓN,2 ̸= {e}. Moreover, ΓN,2 ̸= ΓN,3 , since the
isolated lower central series is strictly decreasing until it terminates; see the proof of Lemma
4.2 for a clarification of this point.
