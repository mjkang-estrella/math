Abstract
    
A graph is  K_{r+1} Kr+1K_{r+1}-saturated if it is  K_{r+1} Kr+1K_{r+1}-free and adding
any missing edge creates a copy of  K_{r+1} Kr+1K_{r+1}. Kim, Kim, Kostochka,
and O conjectured that  K_{r-1}\vee(n-r+1)K_{1} Kr−1∨(n−r+1)​K1K_{r-1}\vee(n-r+1)K_{1} minimizes the
spectral radius among all  n nn-vertex  K_{r+1} Kr+1K_{r+1}-saturated graphs.
They proved the case  r=2 r=2r=2, and the cases  r=3 r=3r=3 and  r\in\{4,5\} r∈{4,5}r\in\{4,5\}
were subsequently established by Kim, Kostochka, O, Shi, and Wang,
and by Wang and Hou, respectively. We settle the conjecture for all
 r\geq 3 r≥3r\geq 3: if  n\geq r+1 n≥r+1n\geq r+1 and  G GG is an  n nn-vertex
 K_{r+1} Kr+1K_{r+1}-saturated graph, then

    




 \rho(G)\geq\frac{r-2+\sqrt{(r-2)^{2}+4(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}, ρ⁡(G)≥r−2+(r−2)2+4​(r−1)​(n−r+1)2,\rho(G)\geq\frac{r-2+\sqrt{(r-2)^{2}+4(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2},


    
with equality if and only if  G\cong K_{r-1}\vee(n-r+1)K_{1} G≅Kr−1∨(n−r+1)​K1G\cong K_{r-1}\vee(n-r+1)K_{1}.
We also prove O’s local two-walk conjecture for every  r\geq 2 r≥2r\geq 2:

    




 \sum_{w\in N_{G}(v)}d_{G}(w)\geq(r-2)d_{G}(v)+(r-1)(n-r+1)\qquad(v\in V(G)). ∑w∈NG​(v)dG​(w)≥(r−2)​dG​(v)+(r−1)​(n−r+1)(v∈V⁡(G)).\sum_{w\in N_{G}(v)}d_{G}(w)\geq(r-2)d_{G}(v)+(r-1)(n-r+1)\qquad(v\in V(G)).


    
If  G GG has no universal vertex, the inequality holds with the additional
term  (r-1)(r-2) (r−1)​(r−2)(r-1)(r-2) on the right-hand side. This constant is best possible
uniformly in  n nn for every fixed  r rr, and gives a strict improvement
when  r\geq 3 r≥3r\geq 3. A corresponding spectral bound follows.

1 Introduction



For a fixed graph  F FF, a graph  G GG is called  F FF-saturated if  G GG is  F FF-free and  G+e G+eG+e contains a copy of  F FF for every  e\in E(\overline{G}) e∈E⁡(G¯)e\in E(\overline{G}). The saturation number  \operatorname{sat}(n,F) sat⁡(n,F)\operatorname{sat}(n,F) is the minimum number of edges in an  n nn-vertex  F FF-saturated graph.




A spectral analogue is obtained by minimizing the adjacency spectral radius. Writing  \rho(G) ρ⁡(G)\rho(G) for the adjacency spectral radius of  G GG, we define the spectral saturation number by





 \operatorname{sat}_{\rho}(n,F)=\min\bigl\{\rho(G):|V(G)|=n,\ G\text{ is }F\text{-saturated}\bigr\}. satρ(n,F)=min{ρ(G):|V(G)|=n,G is F-saturated}.\operatorname{sat}_{\rho}(n,F)=\min\bigl\{\rho(G):|V(G)|=n,\ G\text{ is }F\text{-saturated}\bigr\}.


The associated extremal problems are to determine these two parameters and characterize the graphs attaining the respective minima. In this paper, we focus on the spectral saturation problem for complete graphs.




For integers  r\geq 2 r≥2r\geq 2 and  n\geq r+1 n≥r+1n\geq r+1, let
 S_{n,r}=K_{r-1}\vee(n-r+1)K_{1} Sn,r=Kr−1∨(n−r+1)​K1S_{n,r}=K_{r-1}\vee(n-r+1)K_{1}.
The theorem of Erdős, Hajnal, and Moon [2] states
that every  n nn-vertex  K_{r+1} Kr+1K_{r+1}-saturated graph  G GG satisfies





 e(G)\geq(r-1)n-\binom{r}{2}, e⁡(G)≥(r−1)​n−(r2),e(G)\geq(r-1)n-\binom{r}{2},


with equality if and only if  G\cong S_{n,r} G≅Sn,rG\cong S_{n,r}.
Thus the minimum edge count and its unique extremal structure
are completely determined.




A natural spectral analogue asks whether  S_{n,r} Sn,rS_{n,r} also minimizes
the adjacency spectral radius among graphs in the same class.
This replaces the number of edges by a quantity that also reflects
how those edges are distributed among the vertices.
Its spectral radius is





 \rho(S_{n,r})=\frac{r-2+\sqrt{(r-2)^{2}+4(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}. ρ⁡(Sn,r)=r−2+(r−2)2+4​(r−1)​(n−r+1)2.\rho(S_{n,r})=\frac{r-2+\sqrt{(r-2)^{2}+4(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}.


The classical edge bound alone does not yield this spectral bound:
combining it with  \rho(G)\geq 2e(G)/n ρ⁡(G)≥2​e​(G)/n\rho(G)\geq 2e(G)/n gives only





 \rho(G)\geq 2(r-1)-\frac{r(r-1)}{n}, ρ⁡(G)≥2​(r−1)−r⁡(r−1)n,\rho(G)\geq 2(r-1)-\frac{r(r-1)}{n},


which remains bounded for fixed  r rr, whereas
 \rho(S_{n,r})\sim\sqrt{(r-1)n} ρ⁡(Sn,r)∼(r−1)​n\rho(S_{n,r})\sim\sqrt{(r-1)n} as  n\to\infty n→∞n\to\infty.
Obtaining the sharp spectral bound therefore requires additional
information about the local structure imposed by saturation.
Kim, Kim, Kostochka, and O [4] proposed the following
conjecture.




Conjecture 1.1.

Let  r\geq 3 r≥3r\geq 3 and  n\geq r+1 n≥r+1n\geq r+1. If  G GG is an  n nn-vertex

For every fixed  r rr, this bound is asymptotically sharp as
 n\to\infty n→∞n\to\infty. They also settled the case  r=2 r=2r=2: every  n nn-vertex
 K_{3} K3K_{3}-saturated graph satisfies  \rho(G)\geq\sqrt{n-1} ρ⁡(G)≥n−1\rho(G)\geq\sqrt{n-1}, with
equality if and only if  G GG is the star  K_{1,n-1} K1,n−1K_{1,n-1} or a Moore

Further progress established the conjectured exact bound
for several small values of  r rr.
Kim, Kostochka, O, Shi, and Wang [5]

Our main new ingredient is a uniform
bound on independent set multiplicities. For comparison, the proof for
 K_{5} K5K_{5}-saturated graphs in [1, Section 4, Case 2] estimates
the local two-walk count through a case analysis involving triangles
in a vertex neighborhood. In Proposition 3.1, we
work in the complement and control the overlaps of independent sets
using two inequalities. Hajnal’s intersection–union lemma bounds
the sum of the sizes of the union and intersection of a family of
maximum independent sets. Surányi’s inequality bounds the
neighborhood of that intersection inside an anti-neighborhood.
Together, they yield the multiplicity estimate
(3.1) for every  r\geq 2 r≥2r\geq 2.




Both ingredients apply without a connectedness hypothesis on the
complement. The final reduction therefore consists of deleting the
universal vertices, applying Proposition 3.1 to the
remaining graph, and tracking the local surplus when the deleted
vertices are restored.
