Abstract
    
We study domino tilings of certain regions  R_{\lambda} RλR_{\lambda}, indexed by partitions  \lambda λ\lambda, weighted according to generalized area and dinv statistics. These statistics arise from the  q,t q,tq,t-Catalan combinatorics and Macdonald polynomials. We present a formula for the generating polynomial of these domino tilings in terms of the Bergeron–Garsia nabla operator. When  \lambda=(n^{n}) λ=(nn)\lambda=(n^{n}) is a square shape, domino tilings of  R_{\lambda} RλR_{\lambda} are equivalent to those of the Aztec diamond of order  n nn. In this case, we give a new product formula for the resulting polynomials by domino shuffling and its connection with alternating sign matrices. In particular, we obtain a combinatorial proof of the joint symmetry of the generalized area and dinv statistics.

  



Keywords. Alternating sign matrices; domino tilings; domino shuffling; nabla operator;  q,t q,tq,t-Catalan combinatorics; symmetric functions.

1 Introduction



The study of Macdonald polynomials has produced many interesting combinatorial objects, often expressed as weighted sums over sets of lattice paths. Perhaps the most famous and well-studied such objects are the  q,t q,tq,t-Catalan numbers introduced by Garsia and Haiman [9], which can be defined combinatorially as the sum over Dyck paths weighted by the area and dinv statistics (see the book [11] and the references therein). Among the many generalizations of the  q,t q,tq,t-Catalan numbers are the extension to Schröder paths defined by Egge, Haglund, Killpatrick, and Kremer [5], and to nested families of Dyck paths due to Loehr and Warrington [13]. All of these objects have natural interpretations in terms of Macdonald polynomials often expressed via the nabla operator  \nabla ∇\nabla on symmetric functions introduced by Bergeron and Garsia [1]. In this paper, we study the common generalization to nested families of Schröder paths and their connection to domino tilings.

2.3 Bijection between Domino Tilings and Schröder-like Paths



In general, there is a bijection [14] between domino tilings of a region  R RR on the square lattice and families of non-intersecting paths contained in  R RR. Given a domino tiling of  R_{\lambda} RλR_{\lambda}, the corresponding family of paths is given by decorating each of the four types of dominoes that can appear relative to the checkerboard coloring with a (possibly empty) path step as shown in Figure 5. 2.5 Domino shuffling



Domino shuffling is first presented in [6, 7] and is used to inductively enumerate domino tilings of  \AD_{n} ADn\AD_{n}. It is a process that involves moving dominoes in a specific way, we follow closely their idea and summarize some facts about the domino shuffling below.




Recall that we equip  \AD_{n} ADn\AD_{n} the checkerboard coloring so that the unit squares along the top right side are black. Given a domino tiling  T TT of  \AD_{n} ADn\AD_{n}, if we can find a  2\times 2 2×22\times 2 block in  T TT (consisting of two horizontal or two vertical dominoes), then we say this  2\times 2 2×22\times 2 block is even (resp., odd) if the top right unit square in this block is black (resp., white).
