                                             Congruences for 9-regular partitions modulo 3




arXiv:1306.0136v2 [math.CO] 6 Jun 2013
                                                                          William J. Keith
                                                                             May 19, 2018


                                                                                Abstract
                                                   It is proved that the number of 9-regular partitions of n is divisible by
                                               3 when n is congruent to 3 mod 4, and by 6 when n is congruent to 13
                                               mod 16. An infinite family of congruences mod 3 holds in other progres-
                                               sions modulo powers of 4 and 5. A collection of conjectures includes two
                                               congruences modulo higher powers of 2 and a large family of ”congruences
                                               with exceptions” for these and other regular partitions mod 3.

                                            In recent years there has been a spate of work ([1], [8], [5], [7], [6] and more)
                                         on the divisibility properties of bℓ (n) in arithmetic progression for various ℓ,
                                         especially on their parity. Many Bℓ (q) possess numerous progressions of values
                                         divisible by 2 and 3, in stark contrast to the usual partition function, where such
                                         sequences are unknown and generally held to be almost certainly impossible. In
                                         another contrast, theorems of the form bℓ (An + B) ≡ 0 mod M often have A
                                         and M coprime.
                                            Furcy and Penniston in [5] were able to prove infinite families of congruences
                                         modulo 3 for many ℓ ≡ 1 mod 3. Cui and Gu in [8] find parity results for ℓ = 2j
                                         up to 16. Hirschhorn and Sellers in [7] produce results for ℓ = 5 as well as 13.
                                         The modulus ℓ = 9 was studied for its parity by Xia and Yao ([12]); here we
                                         study it modulo 3.

4    Higher congruences
The self-similarity phenomenon observed above appears for many ℓ and numer-
ous small moduli. It is easy to conjecture candidates for such self-similarity by
numerical experimentation; so many exist that it seems an important problem
in the area would be to develop a unified approach to the existence, frequency,
formulas and symmetries among similarities of the form
                     ∞
                     X
                           bℓ (An + B)q n ≡ q j Bℓ (q k ) mod m.
                     n=0

    Cui and Gu, and Andrews, Hirschhorn and Sellers, prove such infinite fami-
lies divisible by 2 (and a few by 3), using dissections; Furcy and Penniston prove
infinite families modulo 3, using the properties of modular forms. However, for
ℓ and progression moduli of greater size, dissections begin to be unwieldy, and
the modular form machinery almost exclusively gives progressions whose moduli
are powers of the modulus of the congruence, or multiples thereof, which are
only a small fraction of the congruences apparently holding.
    An alternative approach might be found in an interesting proof of a result
by Penniston and Lovejoy (Theorem 3, [5]) from which it can be extracted that

Corollary 1 b3 (5n + 2) ≡ b3 (7k + 4) ≡ 0 mod 9 unless n or k are divisible by
5 or 7 respectively.

    Their proof is of an unusual sort for the literature, referencing the arithmetic
geometry of a particular surface, and their remarks at the end of the paper
pessimistic regarding future applications – but a relation to the self-similarity
problem suggests that there is some hope of use to gained by adapting that
paper’s arguments. It seems very P  likely that this corollary can be considered a
consequence of the self-similarity n=0 b3 (5n + 2)q n ≡ 2B3 (q 5 ) mod 9.
    A congruence holding mod 9 brings us to a remaining area of related interest,
to date apparently completely unexplored: the behavior of l-regular partitions
modulo 2j and 3j for higher powers of j. For instance, the b3 (7n+4) progression
mentioned above may hold mod 27. There is also evidence for progressions in
b9 which are divisible by 27 and 54. However, work of Gordon and Ono ([4])
informs us that b9 (n) is almost always divisible by 3j for any integer j, and so
spurious progressions of this sort are possible, and caution advised.
    Any congruence p(An + B) ≡ 0 mod M for the ordinary partition function
implies one mod M for bAk (An + B) for any multiple of A, and work of Ono and
Mahlburg (with many others) gives us such congruences for powers of any prime
at least 5. However, we have no such congruences for the ordinary partition
function with A or M = 2j or 3j , and yet several such progressions exist for
several bℓ . Therefore, it seems that an important open question is:

Problem: Do there exist progressions bℓ (An + B) ≡ 0 mod 2j or 3j for indefi-
nitely high j?
