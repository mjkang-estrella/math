 I  Introduction 

 
 Scalar maximum-distance-separable (MDS) codes are a basic model for
storage-optimal erasure protection. An  ( n , k ) (n,k)  scalar MDS code over a
field  K K  stores one  K K -symbol in each of  n n  nodes and allows the
original  k k  information symbols to be recovered from any  k k  nodes.
Equivalently, it tolerates any  n − k n-k  node erasures with the minimum
possible redundancy. This global recovery property, however, does not
ensure communication-efficient repair of an individual failed node.
A direct repair procedure downloads one complete  K K -symbol from each
of  k k  surviving nodes and reconstructs the entire codeword, even
though only one symbol is missing. 
 
 
 This inefficiency motivates the array-code viewpoint. In an MDS array
code over a base field  F F , each node stores a vector of  ℓ \ell 
subsymbols, and a helper may transmit functions of its stored vector
rather than its entire contents. The regenerating-code framework of
Dimakis  et al.   [ 1 ]  formalizes the resulting
tradeoff between storage and repair communication. If a failed node
is repaired from  d d  helpers and  γ \gamma  denotes the total number of
downloaded  F F -symbols, then every MDS array code satisfies the cut-set
bound 
 

 
 
 γ ≥ d ​ ℓ d + 1 − k . \gamma\geq\frac{d\ell}{d+1-k}. 
 
 (1) 
 
 An MDS array code attaining equality in
( 1 ) is a minimum-storage regenerating (MSR) code. Much of the literature on
exact-repair MSR codes has focused on MDS array codes; representative
constructions include the product-matrix construction
 [ 2 ]  and high-rate constructions with optimal
repair bandwidth  [ 3 ] . The node size  ℓ \ell , called the
 subpacketization , determines the granularity available to a
repair scheme and is a principal measure of its algebraic and
implementation complexity. Fundamental lower bounds on the
subpacketization of MSR array codes were developed in
 [ 4 ,  5 ,  6 ] . On the construction side, Liu, Li, and Tang
constructed high-rate optimal-access MSR codes over small finite fields
 [ 7 ] , while Li, Tang, and Tian developed a generic
transformation that, when applied iteratively, enables optimal repair
of all nodes with controlled subpacketization  [ 8 ] . Liu, Li, and Tang later extended
this line to MDS array codes supporting multiple repair degrees, with
smaller subpacketization than earlier multi-degree constructions
 [ 9 ] . More recently, Wang, Li, Hu, and Ye constructed
MSR codes with  d = k + 1 d=k+1  and subpacketization  2 ⌈ n / 3 ⌉ 2^{\lceil n/3\rceil}  while maintaining a
field size linear in  n n   [ 10 ] . With  s = d + 1 − k s=d+1-k , Li, Wang, Hu, and Ye
subsequently gave, for every admissible repair degree, optimal-access
MSR codes over fields of size  O s ​ ( n ) O_{s}(n)  with subpacketization
 ℓ = s ⌈ n / s ⌉ \ell=s^{\lceil n/s\rceil} 
and also non-optimal-access MSR codes with the smaller subpacketization
 s ⌈ n / ( s + 1 ) ⌉ s^{\lceil n/(s+1)\rceil}   [ 11 ] .
These developments highlight the central role of subpacketization in
general MSR array-code design and motivate the corresponding minimum
subpacketization problem for the much more rigid class of scalar
Reed-Solomon codes. 
 
 
 Reed-Solomon (RS) codes  [ 12 ]  are one of the most important families of scalar MDS codes and are widely used in communication and storage systems. Let  F = 𝔽 q F=\mathbb{F}_{q}  and  K = 𝔽 q ℓ K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\ell}} . Although an RS code is a scalar code over  K K , after fixing an  F F -basis of  K K , each symbol in  K K  can be represented as a vector in  F ℓ F^{\ell} . Thus, the same code can be viewed as an MDS array code over  F F  with subpacketization  ℓ \ell . This scalar-to-array viewpoint has also been used more generally in the repair of scalar MDS codes  [ 13 ] . 
 
 
 This viewpoint allows the repair of RS codes to be studied within the regenerating-code framework. However, unlike unrestricted MDS array codes, RS codes are subject to additional algebraic constraints arising from their scalar extension field and polynomial-evaluation structure. Consequently, the repair bandwidth and subpacketization of RS codes have attracted substantial attention. In this paper, we require exact  F F -linear repair of every coordinate from every set of  d d  helper nodes with bandwidth meeting the cut-set bound in ( 1 ), and seek the minimum possible extension degree  ℓ = [ K : F ] \ell=[K:F] . 
 
 
 I-A   Linear Repair of Reed-Solomon Codes 

 
 The systematic study of bandwidth-efficient linear repair of scalar RS
codes was initiated by Guruswami and Wootters  [ 14 ] . They
established a dual-code characterization of exact  F F -linear repair,
reducing the design of a repair scheme to the construction of suitable
collections of dual codewords, and used trace maps to obtain
nontrivial repair schemes for full-length RS codes. Building on this
framework, Dau and Milenkovic  [ 15 ] , and later Dau
 et al.   [ 16 ] , developed optimal repair schemes for
additional families of full-length RS codes, including constructions
based on subspace polynomials. Here optimality refers to their
full-length families and specified redundancy ranges under their
linear-repair lower bounds; it need not mean equality in
( 1 ). Li, Wang, and Jafarkhani
 [ 17 ]  further investigated explicit tradeoffs
between repair bandwidth and subpacketization. 
 
 
 A major breakthrough was achieved by Tamo, Ye, and Barg
 [ 18 ,  19 ] , who constructed RS codes attaining the cut-set
bound for the repair of any single failed node from any prescribed set
of  d d  helper nodes. With  s = d + 1 − k s=d+1-k , the subpacketization of their construction is 
 

 
 
 ℓ = s ​ ∏ i = 1 n p ~ i , \ell=s\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{p}_{i}, 
 
 
 where  p ~ 1 , … , p ~ n \tilde{p}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{p}_{n}  are distinct primes satisfying
 p ~ i ≡ 1 ( mod s ) \tilde{p}_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{s} . Their framework also extends to multiple
erasures. Subsequent work developed optimal-access and
error-resilient repair schemes  [ 20 ] , while
near-optimal repair bandwidth with substantially smaller
subpacketization was studied in  [ 21 ] . 
 
 
 In our prior work  [ 22 ] , we developed a
basis-transformation method for constructing the required repair
subspaces for every integer relative extension degree  p ≥ s p\geq s ,
without the congruence restriction  p ≡ 1 ( mod s ) p\equiv 1\pmod{s} .
Applying this method to the Tamo-Ye-Barg framework, we obtained
RS codes attaining the cut-set bound using arbitrary distinct prime
degrees  p i ≥ s p_{i}\geq s . 
 
 
 These constructions establish the existence of cut-set-optimal RS
repair schemes, but do not determine the least possible subpacketization:
assigning distinct prime degrees is only one way to meet the repair
requirements. The present paper studies the minimum over all RS
evaluation sets, allowing more general degree assignments and deriving
lower bounds that apply to every such code.

 VI  Conclusion 

 
 In this paper, we studied the minimum subpacketization of Reed-Solomon
codes admitting cut-set-optimal exact  F F -linear repair of every
coordinate from any  d d  helper nodes. By translating the repair
requirements into degree constraints on cross-ratio fields and
combining the resulting lower bounds with a basis-transformation
construction, we established 
 

 
 
 ℓ min ​ ( n , k , d , q ) = s ​ Δ s ​ ( n , d ) = s ​ G s ​ ( n ) \ell_{\min}(n,k,d;q)=s\Delta_{s}(n,d)=sG_{s}(n) 
 
 
 whenever  d ⁡ ( d + 2 ) ≥ 4 ​ ( n + 1 ) d(d+2)\geq 4(n+1) . In particular, this determines the
minimum subpacketization in the full-helper case  d = n − 1 d=n-1  with
 3 ≤ k < n − 1 3\leq k<n-1 , resolving the corresponding exact linear-repair case of
Open Problem 20 of Ramkumar  et al. 
 [ 23 , Open Problem 20] . We also obtained explicit
formulas for  G s ​ ( n ) G_{s}(n)  and determined the asymptotic order of
 log ⁡ ℓ min ​ ( n , k , d , q ) \log\ell_{\min}(n,k,d;q)  for fixed  k k  and  d d . 
 
 
 The expression  s ​ G s ​ ( n ) sG_{s}(n) , however, is not valid for all parameters.
The affine-plane construction in Appendix  B 
provides admissible parameters for which 
 

 
 
 ℓ min ​ ( n , k , d , q ) ≤ s ​ Δ s ​ ( n , d ) < s ​ G s ​ ( n ) . \ell_{\min}(n,k,d;q)\leq s\Delta_{s}(n,d)<sG_{s}(n). 
 
 
 Thus, although  s ​ G s ​ ( n ) sG_{s}(n)  is exact in the range above,
 s ​ Δ s ​ ( n , d ) s\Delta_{s}(n,d)  is the natural candidate for a general formula.
This motivates the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1 . 
 
 For every prime power  q q  and all integers  3 ≤ k < d ≤ n − 1 3\leq k<d\leq n-1 ,
with  s = d + 1 − k s=d+1-k , 
 

 
 
 ℓ min ​ ( n , k , d , q ) = s ​ Δ s ​ ( n , d ) . \ell_{\min}(n,k,d;q)=s\Delta_{s}(n,d). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Our main result proves this conjecture whenever
 d ⁡ ( d + 2 ) ≥ 4 ​ ( n + 1 ) d(d+2)\geq 4(n+1) . Since the matching upper bound holds for all
admissible parameters, it remains to establish
 ℓ min ​ ( n , k , d , q ) ≥ s ​ Δ s ​ ( n , d ) \ell_{\min}(n,k,d;q)\geq s\Delta_{s}(n,d)  outside this range.
