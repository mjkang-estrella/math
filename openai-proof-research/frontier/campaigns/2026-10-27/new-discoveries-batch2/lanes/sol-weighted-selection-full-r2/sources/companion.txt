Abstract
    

We resolve the threshold part of Question 4 of the COLT 2025 open problem
“Data Selection for Regression Tasks” of Hanneke, Moran, Shlimovich and
Yehudayoff. We study vector-valued linear regression with square loss
ℓ(x,y)​(W)=∥W​x−y∥22\ell_{(x,y)}(W)=\lVert Wx-y\rVert_{2}^{2}, where x∈ℝdx\in\mathbb{R}^{d} and y∈ℝmy\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.
The learner returns the minimum-Frobenius-norm empirical risk minimizer.
We prove that the minimal budget of weighted examples for recovering the full-data loss
on every finite dataset is exactly n⋆​(d,m)=(m+1)​dn^{\star}(d,m)=(m+1)d. We
determine the weighted selection profile Fweighted​(d,m,n)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,n) at the near-threshold
budget: Fweighted​(d,m,(m+1)​d−1)=1+1/(d​m2)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,(m+1)d-1)=1+1/(dm^{2}). We recover the known spanning-budget
value Fweighted​(d,m,d)=d+1F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,d)=d+1 for every mm, and Fweighted​(d,m,n)=∞F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,n)=\infty
for n<dn<d. For the smallest open intermediate cell (d,m)=(2,2)(d,m)=(2,2) we prove
Fweighted​(2,2,3)∈[13/8, 15/8]F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(2,2,3)\in[13/8,\,15/8] and Fweighted​(2,2,4)∈[5/4, 3/2]F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(2,2,4)\in[5/4,\,3/2]. We reduce
the conjectured exact values 13/813/8 and 5/45/4 to a finite moment problem on
the circle with at most seven atoms and assemble structural evidence for it.
The upper bounds use a fixed-basis conic compression lemma, a
determinant–facet rigidity theorem for maximal certificates, and sharp
sparsification lemmas for zero-mean weighted point systems. These tools may
be of independent interest. We also exhibit an explicit six-point integer
dataset with d=m=2d=m=2 on which no weighted selection of 2​d2d points
recovers the optimal loss. Thus the scalar sufficient budget 2​d2d does not
extend to vector-valued outputs. Our new regression-profile results for m≥2m\geq 2 extend the scalar theory
for m=1m=1.

1 Introduction



Data curation and reusable representations (Gadre et al., 2023; Oquab et al., 2023)
call for precise theoretical benchmarks for data selection.



How well can a fixed, natural learning rule learn from
only nn examples selected from a larger dataset? This question
underlies coreset construction, data pruning and subsampling for regression.
A typical multi-output instance is a linear head trained on a fixed
representation, where all outputs must share one selected set of examples
and one weight vector. Hanneke et al. (2025b) posed a worst-case version for basic regression
in a COLT 2025 open-problem note, with a companion paper
(Hanneke et al., 2025a). For scalar linear regression they determined the endpoint
regimes under weighted selection and the minimum-norm empirical risk
minimizer. The worst-case ratio between the selected model’s loss and the
optimal full-data loss is infinite for n<dn<d, equals d+1d+1 at n=dn=d, and
equals 11 for n≥2​dn\geq 2d (Hanneke et al., 2025b, Theorem 1). The intermediate range
d<n<2​dd<n<2d was posed as their Question 2. Their note concludes with a unified
vector-valued formulation using predictors W:ℝd→ℝmW\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m},
square loss and the minimum Frobenius-norm ERM. It asks, as Question 4:


Given d,m,nd,m,n, what is the value of Fweighted​(d,m,n)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,n)? In particular, what is the
minimal n=n⁡(d,m)n=n(d,m) such that this ratio equals 11?


This paper resolves the threshold question completely and determines several
further values of the profile Fweighted(d,m,⋅)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,\cdot). Throughout, a dataset
D=(zi)i=1ND=(z_{i})_{i=1}^{N} is a finite sequence of points
zi=(xi,yi)∈ℝd×ℝmz_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}. We write ℓz​(W)=∥W​x−y∥22\ell_{z}(W)=\lVert Wx-y\rVert_{2}^{2},
LD​(W)=1N​∑iℓzi​(W)L_{D}(W)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\ell_{z_{i}}(W) and LD⋆=minW⁡LD​(W)L^{\star}_{D}=\min_{W}L_{D}(W). The learner AA returns
the empirical risk minimizer of minimal Frobenius norm. Weighted selection of
budget nn chooses nn points of DD (repetitions allowed) and a convex
combination FF of their losses. Its loss on the full dataset is LD​(A​(F))L_{D}(A(F)).
The profile Fweighted​(d,m,n)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,n) is the supremum over datasets of
infFLD​(A⁡(F))/LD⋆\inf_{F}L_{D}(A(F))/L^{\star}_{D}, with degenerate-ratio conventions fixed in

n⋆​(d,m)=min⁡{n:Fweighted​(d,m,n)=1}n^{\star}(d,m)=\min\{n:F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,n)=1\}.

Techniques.



The threshold upper bound uses a
fixed-basis conic compression lemma (Lemma B.1).
After we pin a feature basis of rr points, the negated sum of their
residual dyads lies in the cone of the remaining dyads. Conic
Carathéodory represents it with at most m​rmr of them, giving an exact
certificate on (m+1)​r(m+1)r points. The argument is shorter than the
Steinitz-based scalar route of Hanneke et al. (2025a) and is data-dependent. The
near-threshold upper bound uses a determinant–facet rigidity
theorem (Theorem 4.1). Consider a certificate of maximal size
(m+1)​d(m+1)d. Its weighted feature Gram determinant is a polynomial
of degree at most dd in the weights, although the dyads span an
m​dmd-dimensional space. It vanishes on every facet of the normalized
certificate polytope. This forces a simplex and decomposes the
certificate into dd disjoint (m+1)(m+1)-point circuits on dd independent
feature lines. A sharp mm-point mean lemma for zero-mean weighted
point systems then finishes the assembly. For the (2,2)(2,2) cell, a complex-variable
dictionary turns whitened instances into moment systems on the unit circle
and the selection cost into a convex combination of two-point
interpolation coefficients (Lemma 6.2).





1.1 Related and concurrent work



Prior work. The problem and the scalar endpoint results are due to
Hanneke et al. (2025b); Hanneke et al. (2025a). The scalar value at n=2​d−1n=2d-1 is proved in
Dewasurendra (2026a) and Zhang (2026), and further scalar intermediate
values in Zhang (2026). The budget-dd upper
bound uses volume sampling and the exact expectation formulas of
Dereziński and Warmuth (2017) (Theorem 5, used as an inequality; Section 6.1).
The general kk-point mean lemma
(Theorem G.1) is proved in Appendix G by an
elementary argument. For k=2k=2 and M≥3M\geq 3, its constant coincides with, and
follows from, Theorem 2 of Dewasurendra (2026b). That theorem covers neither M=2M=2 nor
general kk. Tinarrage (2026) proves a radius-normalized, equal-weight
analogue. Carathéodory-type
compressions also underlie exact weighted coresets for least squares. For
example, the summaries of Maalouf et al. (2019) preserve the covariance sums of the input.
An exact coreset preserves the objective for every parameter. Here the
summary must reproduce the model returned by a fixed learner retrained
on the selected original points. We obtain matching lower bounds on the
support and exact worst-case ratios below it.



Concurrent unrefereed preprints. Recent unrefereed preprints treat
other questions of Hanneke et al. (2025b): Dewasurendra (2026b) determines the
budget-22 column of Question 1, and Dewasurendra (2026a) proves the scalar value
at budget 2​d−12d-1 (Question 2). Neither manuscript treats the weighted
vector-valued Question 4. The relation of our
sparsification results to Theorem 2 of Dewasurendra (2026b) is stated above. We
make no priority claim on them.

The scalar case m=1m=1, Question 2 of
Hanneke et al. (2025b), is the subject of our companion paper (Zhang, 2026),

4 Rigidity of maximal certificates



Theorem 3.1 gives τ⁡(D)≤max⁡{1,(m+1)​r}\tau(D)\leq\max\{1,(m+1)r\}. The upper bound
at budget
(m+1)​d−1(m+1)d-1 requires understanding the datasets with τ⁡(D)=(m+1)​d\tau(D)=(m+1)d, which
we call certificate-maximal. The key is a rigidity theorem for
maximal certificates.



Theorem 4.1 (Determinant–facet rigidity).


Let the features span ℝd\mathbb{R}^{d} and suppose τ⁡(D)=k:=(m+1)​d\tau(D)=k:=(m+1)d. Let cc be a
spanning zero certificate with |supp⁡c|=k|\operatorname{supp}c|=k and support SS. Then SS splits
uniquely (up to permutation) into dd pairwise disjoint blocks
S=C1⊔⋯⊔CdS=C_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup C_{d} with |Cj|=m+1|C_{j}|=m+1, where each CjC_{j} supports a
positive circuit of the dyads whose features span a one-dimensional line
UjU_{j}, and U1,…,UdU_{1},\dots,U_{d} are linearly independent.




Proof sketch.


Vectorize the dyads of SS into a matrix 𝖠\mathsf{A} with m​dmd rows and
kk columns. The normalized nonnegative kernel
P={θ≥0:𝖠θ=0, 1⊤θ=1}P=\{\theta\geq 0:\mathsf{A}\theta=0,\ \mathbf{1}^{\top}\theta=1\} is a polytope of
dimension h−1≥d−1h-1\geq d-1 whose relative interior consists of the strictly
positive points. The polynomial f⁡(θ)=det(∑iθi​xi​xi⊤)f(\theta)=\det(\sum_{i}\theta_{i}x_{i}x_{i}^{\top}),
of degree at most dd, is positive on the relative interior and must vanish
at every boundary point: a boundary point with f>0f>0 would be a spanning
zero certificate of smaller support. A nonzero polynomial of degree ≤d\leq d
vanishing on every facet is divisible by the facets’ affine forms, so PP
has at most dd facets. A bounded (h−1)(h-1)-polytope has at least hh facets,
so h=dh=d and PP is a simplex (a point if d=1d=1). The dd extreme rays give
the blocks CjC_{j}. Dropping a ray gives a boundary point where the features
cannot span. This forces the feature spans UjU_{j} of the blocks to be a
minimal spanning family, hence lines. The bound |Cj|≤m+1|C_{j}|\leq m+1 then forces
|Cj|=m+1|C_{j}|=m+1 and disjointness. The full proof is in Appendix D.
∎

7 Discussion and open problems



The threshold splits conceptually as (m+1)​d=d+m​d(m+1)d=d+md: dd points to pin a
feature basis and m​dmd points to cancel the m×dm\times d residual gradient.
The lower-bound instances realize this as dd independent copies of weighted
mean estimation in ℝm\mathbb{R}^{m}, one per feature direction. Each requires its
full simplex of m+1m+1 points. At m=1m=1 the two mechanisms coincide.
Table 2 in Appendix E separates the values of
explicit instances from the global profile values at (2,2)(2,2). Our theorems
concern a fixed learner and a fixed finite dataset. Certificates use
the full-data minimizer W∘W^{\circ}, with arbitrary nonnegative
real weights. Some intermediate bounds are attained only as limits. The theorems
describe what a small weighted subsample can represent and its exact
budget limits. They provide neither a faster selection algorithm nor
guarantees for unseen data.




Open problems.



(1) The intermediate curve. Determine Fweighted​(d,m,n)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,n) for d,m≥2d,m\geq 2 and
d<n<(m+1)​d−1d<n<(m+1)d-1. Even the scalar case is largely open
(Hanneke et al. (2025b), Question 2; see Zhang (2026) for the cells
resolved so far). Axial instances reduce to an allocation problem over
per-line mean-estimation deficits (Theorem G.1 supplies the
sharp per-line constants).
(2) The (2,2)(2,2) cell (Conjecture 6.5): a question about at
most seven atoms on the circle. A proof must use selections with μ≠0\mu\neq 0
and cannot rest on comparing aggregate second moments of original-weight
subsets.
(3) Unweighted vector selection. Relating the unweighted profile
F⁡(d,m,n)F(d,m,n) of Question 3 of Hanneke et al. (2025b) to the weighted threshold
(m+1)​d(m+1)d.
(4) Other learning rules. How does the threshold depend on the ERM
tie-breaking rule? Our upper bounds use it only through
Lemma 2.2, while the lower bounds use it in their
rank-deficient branches.





7.1 Conclusion



We establish the exact threshold n⋆​(d,m)=(m+1)​dn^{\star}(d,m)=(m+1)d for weighted
data selection with the minimum-Frobenius-norm ERM to recover the full-data
loss of every dataset. This answers the threshold part of Question 4 posed at COLT 2025 by Hanneke et al. (2025b). We also determine the
profile at two further budgets, Fweighted​(d,m,(m+1)​d−1)=1+1/(d​m2)F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,(m+1)d-1)=1+1/(dm^{2}) and
Fweighted​(d,m,d)=d+1F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(d,m,d)=d+1. The proofs rest on three reusable tools: fixed-basis conic
compression, determinant–facet rigidity of maximal certificates, and sharp
sparsification of zero-mean weighted point systems. The proofs also use a
complex-variable dictionary that reduces the smallest open cell to a
seven-atom moment problem. The intermediate curve
d<n<(m+1)​d−1d<n<(m+1)d-1 for d,m≥2d,m\geq 2 remains open. For d=1d=1 the complete curve is
given by Corollary G.3. Even on the open curve the inner
infimum in FweightedF_{\mathrm{weighted}} need not be attained (Remark E.2: an
integer instance with d=m=2d=m=2, n=3n=3). The first target is the (2,2)(2,2)
conjecture Fweighted​(2,2,3)=13/8F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(2,2,3)=13/8, Fweighted​(2,2,4)=5/4F_{\mathrm{weighted}}(2,2,4)=5/4.
