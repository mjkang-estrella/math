 Abstract 
 
 
 This paper presents the first systematic study of [F] -irregular graphs, a concept that parallels classical F -irregularity. For a fixed graph F , a graph G is [F] -irregular if the
numbers of its induced subgraphs isomorphic to F containing a given vertex are pairwise distinct for all vertices of G . We prove that there exist infinitely many [P_{n}] -irregular graphs for any path P_{n} of order n\geq 3 .
We establish that a non-trivial [P_{3}] -irregular graph of order k exists if and only if k\geq 7 .
Finally, we propose the Strong Conjecture on [F] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 Keywords : induced subgraphs, induced paths, graph irregularity, [F] -degree of a vertex, [F] -irregular graph, Strong Conjecture on [F] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 1 Introduction 

 
 
 While regular graphs are central to algebraic and structural graph theory, vertex-distinguishing problems have motivated multiple paradigms of irregularity [ 1 ] . In what follows, we exclusively consider simple, finite, and undirected graphs. Since no non-trivial graph can have pairwise distinct vertex degrees, a core challenge is to define generalized degree concepts that distinguish all vertices of the graph. 
 
 
 
 One such direction originates from the seminal work of Chartrand, Holbert, Oellermann, and Swart [ 2 ] , who associated each vertex v of a graph G with the number of its subgraphs isomorphic to a fixed graph F that contain v . This quantity, termed the F -degree of v , naturally captures structural asymmetry: a graph G is called F -irregular if its vertices have mutually distinct F -degrees. In [ 2 ] , the authors put forward a fundamental conjecture on the existence of such graphs, motivated by their study of the cases where F is a complete graph or a star. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1 (Chartrand et al. [ 2 ] , 1987) . 
 
 
 For every connected graph F of order |F|\geq 3 , there exists a non-trivial F -irregular graph. 
 
 
 
 
 Further progress for broader classes of graphs was made only in 2024, when Dovzhenok, Filuta, and Chuhai [ 3 ] demonstrated that infinite families of F -irregular graphs exist for all 2 -connected graphs F with minimum degree 2 . In the same work, the authors proposed a significantly stronger version of Conjecture 1 . 
 
 
 Conjecture 2 (Strong Conjecture about F -irregular graphs, Dovzhenok et al. [ 3 ] , 2024) . 
 
 
 For every connected graph F of order |F|\geq 3 , there are infinitely many F -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 Subsequently, Dovzhenok verified Conjecture 1 for paths [ 4 ] and confirmed Conjecture 2 for all graphs F of diameter 2 [ 5 ] . 
 
 
 
 In the present paper, in contrast to the classical setting based on ordinary subgraphs, we focus on irregularity through the lens of induced subgraphs. This approach was originally suggested by Ali, Chartrand, and Zhang in [ 1 ] . 
 
 
 Definition 1 . 
 
 
 Let F and G be graphs. The induced F -degree (or simply [F] -degree ) of a vertex v in G is the number of induced subgraphs of G that are isomorphic to F and contain v . A graph G is [F] -irregular if the [F] -degrees of its vertices are pairwise distinct. 
 
 
 
 
 Unlike ordinary subgraphs, which are preserved under edge addition, their induced counterparts are highly sensitive to any such modification. This structural fragility complicates the construction of [F] -irregular graphs compared to the classical case. 
 
 
 
 As far as we are aware, this work initiates the systematic investigation of [F] -irregularity, focusing on the canonical class of paths. Specifically, we completely solve the existence problem for [P_{n}] -irregular graphs posed in [ 4 ] , and fully determine the possible orders of non-trivial [P_{3}] -irregular graphs. Our main contributions are summarized in the following theorems. 
 
 
 Theorem 1 . 
 
 
 For any path P_{n} of order n\geq 3 , there exist infinitely many [P_{n}] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 Theorem 2 . 
 
 
 For an integer k , a non-trivial [P_{3}] -irregular graph of order k exists if and only if k\geq 7 . 
 
 
 
 
 Our approach is constructive: for each n\geq 3 , we provide explicit families of graphs, determine the exact values of all [P_{n}] -degrees, and show that they are pairwise distinct. 
 
 
 
 The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the necessary preliminaries and notation. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem 2 , characterizing [P_{3}] -irregular graphs. Sections 4 and 5 focus on [P_{4}] - and [P_{5}] -irregularity, respectively. In Section 6, we settle the general case for all n\geq 6 , thereby completing the proof of Theorem 1 . Finally, Section 7 outlines the Strong Conjecture on [F] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 2 Preliminaries 

 
 
 All numerical parameters in this paper are integers. Any set of indexed elements of the form \bigl\{\alpha_{x}\bigm|i\leq x\leq j\bigr\} is also written as \{\alpha_{i},\dots,\alpha_{j}\} . For a graph G , let V(G) and E(G) be its vertex and edge sets, respectively. The order of G is |G|=|V(G)| , and G is non-trivial if |G|\geq 2 . An edge incident to vertices u and v is denoted by uv . The degree of a vertex v\in V(G) is the number of edges incident to v , and G is regular if all its vertices have the same degree. We use the notation K_{m} for the complete graph of order m .
A path P_{n} of order n is a graph with V(P_{n})=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\} and E(P_{n})=\bigl\{u_{i}u_{i+1}\bigm|1\leq i\leq n-1\bigr\} . We write such a path as the vertex sequence u_{1}u_{2}\dots u_{n} , where u_{1} and u_{n} are called the end-vertices .
If a graph contains a unique path with the end-vertices u and v , this path is denoted by u\dots v ; in particular, u\dots u represents the single vertex u . A graph is connected if every pair of its vertices is joined by a path. 
 
 
 
 A subgraph H of G is induced if E(H)=\bigl\{uv\in E(G)\bigm|u,v\in V(H)\bigr\} . For a vertex v\in V(G) , we denote by \mathcal{P}_{n}(G,v) the family of all induced subgraphs of G isomorphic to P_{n} and containing v . Consequently, the [P_{n}] -degree of a vertex v in G is exactly |\mathcal{P}_{n}(G,v)| . 
 
 
 
 
 3 On the order of [P_{3}] -irregular graphs 

 
 
 3.1 Construction of [P_{3}] -irregular graphs of odd order k\geq 9 

 
 
 We begin by introducing a parameterized family of graphs \bigl\{G(m,3)\bigr\}_{m\geq 4} , where each graph has a corresponding odd order k=2m+1 . 
 
 
 Definition 2 . 
 
 
 Let m\geq 4 be an integer. The graph G(m,3) , schematically shown in Figure 1, is specified by its vertex and edge sets as follows: 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle V\bigl(G(m,3)\bigr) 
 \displaystyle=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\}\cup\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}\cup\{x\}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle E\bigl(G(m,3)\bigr) 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{xa_{i}\bigm|1\leq i\leq m-1\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{a_{i}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\leq m\bigr\}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Figure 1: Graph G(m,3) . 
 
 
 
 For the graph G(m,3) , let X , A_{i} , and B_{j} denote the [P_{3}] -degrees of the vertices x , a_{i} , and b_{j} , respectively, where 1\leq i,j\leq m . 
 
 
 Lemma 1 . 
 
 
 The vertex [P_{3}] -degrees in G(m,3) are given by 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle X 
 \displaystyle=(m-1)^{2},\qquad A_{m}=m(m-1), 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle A_{i} 
 \displaystyle=m(i+1)-2\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m-1, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle B_{j} 
 \displaystyle=m(m-j+1)-1\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 Clearly, the [P_{3}] -degree of a vertex v in G(m,3) is obtained by summing the cardinalities of disjoint subfamilies into which \mathcal{P}_{3}(G(m,3),v) decomposes. 
 
 
 
 Computation of X . For the vertex x , this decomposition takes the form \mathcal{X}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{X}_{2} where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{xa_{i}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\leq m-1\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{X}_{1}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m-1); 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{i}xa_{k}\bigm|1\leq i<k\leq m-1\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{X}_{2}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(m-1)(m-2). 
 
 
 
 Hence, X=|\mathcal{X}_{1}|+|\mathcal{X}_{2}|=(m-1)^{2} . 
 
 
 
 Computation of A_{i} . For i=m , the family \mathcal{P}_{3}(G(m,3),a_{m}) splits into \mathcal{A}_{m,1}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{m,2} where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{i}b_{j}a_{m}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\leq m-1\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{A}_{m,1}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m-1); 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m,2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{b_{j}a_{m}b_{k}\bigm|1\leq j<k\leq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{A}_{m,2}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m-1). 
 
 
 
 Consequently, A_{m}=|\mathcal{A}_{m,1}|+|\mathcal{A}_{m,2}|=m(m-1) . 
 
 
 
 Now fix 1\leq i\leq m-1 . The family \mathcal{P}_{3}(G(m,3),a_{i}) partitions into \bigsqcup_{t=1}^{4}\mathcal{A}_{i,t} where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{i}xa_{k}\bigm|1\leq k\leq m-1,\,k\neq i\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{A}_{i,1}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=m-2; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{i}b_{j}a_{k}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i,\,j\leq k\leq m,\,k\neq i\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{A}_{i,2}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(m-j)=\frac{1}{2}i(2m-i-1); 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,3} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{xa_{i}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{A}_{i,3}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=i; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,4} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{b_{j}a_{i}b_{k}\bigm|1\leq j<k\leq i\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle\bigl|\mathcal{A}_{i,4}\bigr| 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}i(i-1). 
 
 
 
 Straightforward calculation shows that A_{i}=\sum_{t=1}^{4}|\mathcal{A}_{i,t}|=m(i+1)-2 . 
 
 
 
 Computation of B_{j} . For any 1\leq j\leq m , we have \mathcal{P}_{3}(G(m,3),b_{j})=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{3}\mathcal{B}_{j,t} where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{xa_{i}b_{j}\bigm|j\leq i\leq m-1\bigr\},\quad\mathcal{B}_{j,2}=\bigl\{a_{i}b_{j}a_{k}\bigm|j\leq i<k\leq m\bigr\}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j,3} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{b_{j}a_{i}b_{k}\bigm|j\leq i\leq m,\,1\leq k\leq i,\,k\neq j\bigr\}, 
 
 
 
 with the respective cardinalities 
 

 
 
 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{j,1}|=m-j,\qquad|\mathcal{B}_{j,2}|=\frac{1}{2}(m-j+1)(m-j), 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{j,3}|=\sum_{i=j}^{m}(i-1)=\frac{1}{2}(m-j+1)(m+j-2). 
 
 
 
 Summing these values yields B_{j}=\sum_{t=1}^{3}|\mathcal{B}_{j,t}|=m(m-j+1)-1 . ∎ 
 
 
 
 Theorem 3 . 
 
 
 For every integer m\geq 4 , the graph G(m,3) is [P_{3}] -irregular. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 Fix an integer m\geq 4 . Modulo m , the vertex [P_{3}] -degrees in G(m,3) obtained in Lemma 1 split into four disjoint congruence classes 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle X 
 \displaystyle\equiv 1\pmod{m}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle A_{m} 
 \displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{m}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle A_{i} 
 \displaystyle\equiv m-2\pmod{m}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m-1, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle B_{j} 
 \displaystyle\equiv m-1\pmod{m}\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq m. 
 
 
 
 This partition and the strict monotonicity of the sequences \bigl\{A_{i}\bigr\}_{i=1}^{m-1} and \bigl\{B_{j}\bigr\}_{j=1}^{m} imply that all these [P_{3}] -degrees are pairwise distinct. Thus, G(m,3) is [P_{3}] -irregular.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Corollary 1 . 
 
 
 There exist infinitely many [P_{3}] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 
 3.2 Proof of Theorem 2 

 
 
 Recall that Theorem 2 states that a non-trivial [P_{3}] -irregular graph of order k exists if and only if k\geq 7 . 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 Necessity. To begin with, every [P_{3}] -irregular graph is inherently asymmetric, i.e.,
its automorphism group is trivial. According to [ 6 ] , there are no
asymmetric graphs of orders 2\leq k\leq 5 , and there are exactly eight such graphs of order 6 , as shown in Figure 2 . A direct check reveals that none of them is [P_{3}] -irregular; there, in each graph, two vertices with the same [P_{3}] -degree are marked with their equal values. Consequently, no [P_{3}] -irregular graph exists
for any order 2\leq k\leq 6 . The necessity is proved. 
 
 
 Figure 2: The eight asymmetric graphs of order 6 , in each of which two vertices are numerically labeled with their equal [P_{3}] -degree values. 
 
 
 
 Sufficiency. By Theorem 3 , for each integer m\geq 4 , the graph G(m,3) of odd order k=2m+1\geq 9 is [P_{3}] -irregular, and the exact formulae in Lemma 1 ensure that all its vertex [P_{3}] -degrees are strictly positive. To complete the odd orders, Figure 3 depicts the [P_{3}] -irregular graph G(3,3) of order 7 . It contains exactly 16 induced subgraphs isomorphic to P_{3} :
 v_{1}v_{2}v_{3} , v_{1}v_{2}v_{6} , v_{1}v_{2}v_{7} , v_{1}v_{5}v_{3} , v_{1}v_{5}v_{6} ;
 v_{2}v_{1}v_{5} , v_{2}v_{3}v_{4} , v_{2}v_{3}v_{5} , v_{2}v_{6}v_{5} ;
 v_{3}v_{2}v_{6} , v_{3}v_{5}v_{6} , v_{3}v_{7}v_{6} ;
 v_{4}v_{3}v_{5} , v_{4}v_{3}v_{7} ; v_{5}v_{3}v_{7} , and v_{5}v_{6}v_{7} .
Consequently, the [P_{3}] -degrees of the vertices v_{1},\dots,v_{7} in G(3,3) are 6,8,10,3,9,7 , and 5 , respectively, which are also strictly positive. 
 
 
 Figure 3: [P_{3}] -irregular graph G(3,3) of order 7 . 
 
 
 
 A [P_{3}] -irregular graph of every even order k\geq 8 can be constructed by adjoining an isolated vertex to G\left(\frac{1}{2}(k-2),3\right) , thereby completing the proof of sufficiency.
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 4 [P_{4}] -irregular graphs 

 
 
 In this section, we construct [P_{4}] -irregular graphs of every order k\geq 17 , focusing first on the case of odd k . 
 
 
 Definition 3 . 
 
 
 Let m\geq 7 be an integer. The graph G(m,4) of odd order 2m+3 , depicted in Figure 4 , is characterized by the vertex and edge sets 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle V\bigl(G(m,4)\bigr) 
 \displaystyle=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\}\cup\{b_{1},\dots,b_{m}\}\cup\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{1}\}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle E\bigl(G(m,4)\bigr) 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{i}a_{j}\bigm|1\leq i<j\leq m\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{a_{i}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\leq m\bigr\} 
 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\quad\cup\bigl\{x_{2}a_{i}\bigm|1\leq i\leq m\bigr\}\cup\{x_{1}x_{2},\,b_{1}y_{1},\,b_{m}y_{1}\}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Figure 4: Graph G(m,4) . 
 
 
 
 For the graph G(m,4) , let X_{1} , X_{2} , A_{i} , B_{j} , and Y_{1} denote the [P_{4}] -degrees of the vertices x_{1} , x_{2} , a_{i} , b_{j} , and y_{1} , respectively, where 1\leq i,j\leq m . 
 
 
 Lemma 2 . 
 
 
 The vertex [P_{4}] -degrees in G(m,4) are given by 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle X_{1} 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1), 
 \displaystyle X_{2} 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+m+1, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle A_{i} 
 \displaystyle=2i+2\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m-1, 
 \displaystyle A_{m} 
 \displaystyle=4m-3, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle B_{j} 
 \displaystyle=2m-2j+3\quad\text{for }2\leq j\leq m-1, 
 \displaystyle B_{1} 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+2m-2, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle B_{m} 
 \displaystyle=3m-2, 
 \displaystyle Y_{1} 
 \displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+3m-4. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 The [P_{4}] -degrees in G(m,4) are determined analogously to the proof of Lemma 1 using U=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1}\}\cup\{b_{2},\dots,b_{m-1}\}\cup\{x_{2}\} . 
 
 
 
 Computation of X_{1},X_{2} . For x_{1} , we have \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),x_{1})=\bigl\{x_{1}x_{2}a_{i}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\leq m\bigr\} , which yields X_{1}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m(m+1) . 
 
 
 
 For x_{2} , \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),x_{2}) partitions into \bigsqcup_{t=1}^{3}\mathcal{X}_{2,t} , where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2,1} 
 \displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),x_{1}), 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{X}_{2,1}| 
 \displaystyle=X_{1}=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1); 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2,2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{x_{2}a_{i}b_{1}y_{1}\bigm|1\leq i\leq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{X}_{2,2}| 
 \displaystyle=m; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{2,3} 
 \displaystyle=\{x_{2}a_{m}b_{m}y_{1}\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{X}_{2,3}| 
 \displaystyle=1. 
 
 
 
 Consequently, X_{2}=\sum_{t=1}^{3}|\mathcal{X}_{2,t}|=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+m+1 . 
 
 
 
 Computation of A_{i} . Fix 1\leq i\leq m-1 . Then \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),a_{i})=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}_{i,t} , where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{x_{1}x_{2}a_{i}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq i\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{i,1}| 
 \displaystyle=i; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{b_{j}a_{i}b_{1}y_{1}\bigm|2\leq j\leq i\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{i,2}| 
 \displaystyle=i-1; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i,3} 
 \displaystyle=\{x_{2}a_{i}b_{1}y_{1},\,a_{i}b_{1}y_{1}b_{m},\,a_{i}a_{m}b_{m}y_{1}\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{i,3}| 
 \displaystyle=3. 
 
 
 
 It follows that A_{i}=\sum_{t=1}^{3}|\mathcal{A}_{i,t}|=2i+2 . 
 
 
 
 For i=m , the family \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),a_{m}) decomposes into \bigsqcup_{t=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}_{m,t} , where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{x_{1}x_{2}a_{m}b_{j}\bigm|1\leq j\leq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{m,1}| 
 \displaystyle=m; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m,2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{va_{m}b_{1}y_{1}\bigm|v\in\{x_{2}\}\cup\{b_{2},\dots,b_{m-1}\}\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{m,2}| 
 \displaystyle=m-1; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m,3} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{ua_{m}b_{m}y_{1}\bigm|u\in U\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{m,3}| 
 \displaystyle=2m-2. 
 
 
 
 This leads to A_{m}=\sum_{t=1}^{3}|\mathcal{A}_{m,t}|=4m-3 . 
 
 
 
 Computation of B_{j} . For j=1 , we obtain \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),b_{1})=\bigsqcup_{t=1}^{4}\mathcal{B}_{1,t} , where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{x_{1}x_{2}a_{i}b_{1}\bigm|1\leq i\leq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{1,1}| 
 \displaystyle=m; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1,2} 
 \displaystyle=\mathcal{X}_{2,2}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{1,2}| 
 \displaystyle=m; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1,3} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{a_{i}b_{1}y_{1}b_{m}\bigm|1\leq i\leq m-1\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{1,3}| 
 \displaystyle=m-1; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1,4} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{b_{j}a_{i}b_{1}y_{1}\bigm|2\leq j\leq i\leq m,\,j\neq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{1,4}| 
 \displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)-1. 
 
 
 
 Thus, B_{1}=\sum_{t=1}^{4}|\mathcal{B}_{1,t}|=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+2m-2 . 
 
 
 
 Fix 2\leq j\leq m-1 . We express \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),b_{j}) as the disjoint union \bigsqcup_{t=1}^{3}\mathcal{B}_{j,t} , where 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j,1} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{x_{1}x_{2}a_{i}b_{j}\bigm|j\leq i\leq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{j,1}| 
 \displaystyle=m-j+1; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j,2} 
 \displaystyle=\bigl\{b_{j}a_{i}b_{1}y_{1}\bigm|j\leq i\leq m\bigr\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{j,2}| 
 \displaystyle=m-j+1; 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j,3} 
 \displaystyle=\{b_{j}a_{m}b_{m}y_{1}\}, 
 \displaystyle|\mathcal{B}_{j,3}| 
 \displaystyle=1. 
 
 
 
 Hence, B_{j}=\sum_{t=1}^{3}|\mathcal{B}_{j,t}|=2m-2j+3 . 
 
 
 
 Finally, in the case j=m , the relation \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),b_{m})=\{x_{1}x_{2}a_{m}b_{m}\}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{m,3}\sqcup\mathcal{B}_{1,3} implies B_{m}=1+(2m-2)+(m-1)=3m-2 . 
 
 
 
 Computation of Y_{1} . Since \mathcal{P}_{4}(G(m,4),y_{1})=\mathcal{X}_{2,2}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{m,3}\sqcup\mathcal{B}_{1,3}\sqcup\mathcal{B}_{1,4} , we conclude that 
 

 
 
 Y_{1}=m+(2m-2)+(m-1)+\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)-1=\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+3m-4.\qed 
 
 
 
 
 
 Theorem 4 . 
 
 
 For every integer m\geq 7 , the graph G(m,4) is [P_{4}] -irregular. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 Fix an integer m\geq 7 . By Lemma 2 , the sequence \bigl\{A_{i}\bigr\}_{i=1}^{m-1} is strictly increasing with a maximum of A_{m-1}=2m and contains only even values, whereas \bigl\{B_{j}\bigr\}_{j=2}^{m-1} is strictly decreasing with a maximum of B_{2}=2m-1 and comprises only odd values, ensuring that their terms are pairwise distinct. Moreover, we have the following chain of inequalities 
 

 
 
 2m<B_{m}<A_{m}<X_{1}<X_{2}<B_{1}<Y_{1}. 
 
 
 
 These arguments collectively demonstrate that all vertex [P_{4}] -degrees in G(m,4) are pairwise distinct, meaning that G(m,4) is [P_{4}] -irregular.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Corollary 2 . 
 
 
 For every integer k\geq 17 , there exists a [P_{4}] -irregular graph of order k . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 For odd orders, the existence follows directly from Theorem 4 . For any even order k\geq 18 , the desired graph is obtained by adding an isolated vertex to the [P_{4}] -irregular graph G\bigl(\frac{1}{2}(k-4),4\bigr) of odd order k-1 having strictly positive vertex [P_{4}] -degrees by Lemma 2 .
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 
 5 [P_{5}] -irregular graphs 

 
 
 This section presents a construction of [P_{5}] -irregular graphs of every order k\geq 19 , starting with odd values of k . 
 
 
 Definition 4 . 
 
 
 Let m\geq 7 be an integer. The graph G(m,5) of odd order 2m+5 , illustrated in Figure 5 , is defined by 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle V\bigl(G(m,5)\bigr)

 Theorem 5 . 
 
 
 For every integer m\geq 7 , the graph G(m,5) is [P_{5}] -irregular. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 Fix an integer m\geq 7 . Immediate inspection of Lemma 3 yields the strict ordering 
 

 
 
 A_{m}<X_{1}<B_{m}<X_{2}<Y_{1}<X_{3}<B_{1}<Y_{2}. 
 
 
 
 Next, we observe that the sequence \bigl\{A_{i}\bigr\}_{i=1}^{m-1} is strictly increasing with a maximum of A_{m-1}=3m-1 , and its terms leave a remainder of 2 upon division by 3 . In turn, the sequence \bigl\{B_{j}\bigr\}_{j=2}^{m-1} is strictly decreasing with a maximum of B_{2}=3m-3 , and its terms leave a remainder of 0 upon division by 3 . Thus, the entries of these two sequences are pairwise distinct. Combined with the inequality B_{2}<A_{m-1}<A_{m}=4m-1 , this implies that all the vertex [P_{5}] -degrees in G(m,5) are pairwise distinct, confirming that G(m,5) is [P_{5}] -irregular .

 For each admissible pair (i,j) , we show that any two [P_{n}] -degrees from distinct sets \{X_{1},Y_{1}\} and \{A_{i},B_{j}\} have incongruent residues modulo n-1 . 
 
 
 
 Indeed, by Lemmas 4 – 7 , we have 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle X_{1}-A_{i} 
 \displaystyle=m-2-i(n-1)\equiv m-2\pmod{n-1}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle X_{1}-B_{j} 
 \displaystyle=2m-mn-6+j(n-1)\equiv m-6\pmod{n-1}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle Y_{1}-A_{i} 
 \displaystyle=5m-9-i(n-1)\equiv 5m-9\pmod{n-1}, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle Y_{1}-B_{j} 
 \displaystyle=6m-mn-13+j(n-1)\equiv 5m-13\pmod{n-1}. 
 
 
 
 If n=7 and m\equiv 1\pmod{6} , these differences yield the non-zero residues 5,1,2,4\pmod{6} , respectively.
If n\geq 6 and n\neq 7 , the condition m\equiv 3\pmod{n-1} implies that the respective residues are 1,n-4,6,2\pmod{n-1} , none of which is congruent to 0 .
Hence, the assertion follows.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Lemma 12 . 
 
 
 If n\geq 7 , then the [P_{n}] -degrees of vertices in the graph G(m,n) satisfy 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle L>X_{n-2}>Y_{n-3}>X_{n-3}>B_{m} 
 \displaystyle>X_{n-4}, 
 
 (1) 
 
 
 \displaystyle X_{i}>Y_{i} 
 \displaystyle>X_{i-1}\qquad\text{for }n\geq 8\text{ and }4\leq i\leq n-4, 
 
 (2) 
 
 
 \displaystyle X_{3}>Y_{3}>B_{1}>Y_{2}>X_{2}>Y_{1}>X_{1} 
 \displaystyle>A_{m},

 whereas for n=6 the linear order takes the form 
 

 
 
 L>X_{4}>Y_{3}>X_{3}>B_{m}>B_{1}>Y_{2}>X_{2}>Y_{1}>X_{1}>A_{m}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 By virtue of Lemmas 4 – 8 , the required inequalities follow from the constraint m\geq 2n\geq 12 and the evaluations below. 
 
 
 
 Chain ( 1 ) is established by 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle L-X_{n-2} 
 \displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{6}\bigl(m^{3}-3m^{2}+14m-6n(m+1)+6\bigr)\geq\dfrac{1}{6}(m^{3}-6m^{2}+11m+6)>0, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle X_{n-2}-Y_{n-3} 
 \displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}(m^{2}-3m+14)>0, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle Y_{n-3}-X_{n-3} 
 \displaystyle=m-5>0,\quad X_{n-3}-B_{m}=m(n-3)>0, 
 
 
 
 \displaystyle B_{m}-X_{n-4} 
 \displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}m^{2}-mn+\dfrac{5}{2}m+2\geq\dfrac{5}{2}m+2>0. 
 
 
 
 Inequalities ( 2 ) follow from 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle X_{i}-Y_{i} 
 \displaystyle=4>0,\qquad Y_{i}-X_{i-1}=\dfrac{1}{2}(m^{2}-m-4)>0. 
 
 
 
 Chain ( 3 ) is obtained by 
 

 
 
 \displaystyle X_{3}-Y_{3} 
 \displaystyle=4>0,\quad Y_{3}-B_{1}=\frac{1}{6}m(m^{2}+23-6n)+1\geq\frac{1}{6}m(m^{2}+23-3m)+1>0,

 \displaystyle=m(n-4)+1>0,\quad Y_{2}-X_{2}=2m-6>0,

 \displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}(m^{2}-5m+16)>0,\quad Y_{1}-X_{1}=4m-7>0,

 If n=6 , the relations L>X_{4}>Y_{3}>X_{3}>B_{m} and B_{1}>Y_{2}>X_{2}>Y_{1}>X_{1}>A_{m} hold identically to ( 1 ) and ( 3 ) for n\geq 7 , respectively. The remaining link between B_{m} and B_{1} follows from

 Theorem 6 . 
 
 
 For all integers n\geq 6 and m\geq 2n satisfying either n=7 with m\equiv 1\pmod{6} , or n\neq 7 with m\equiv 3\pmod{n-1} , the graph G(m,n) is [P_{n}] -irregular. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 By Lemmas 9 – 12 , all vertex [P_{n}] -degrees in G(m,n) are pairwise distinct. This immediately implies that G(m,n) is [P_{n}] -irregular.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Corollary 4 . 
 
 
 For every integer n\geq 6 , there exist infinitely many [P_{n}] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 By Corollaries 1 – 4 , the assertion of Theorem 1 holds. 
 
 
 
 
 7 Conclusion 

 
 
 This paper provides the first structural results on [F] -irregularity. For any path P_{n} of order n\geq 3 , we explicitly constructed infinite families of graphs with pairwise distinct vertex [P_{n}] -degrees, and established that the order k of a non-trivial [P_{3}] -irregular graph can be any integer k\geq 7 and no other.
These results prompt us to formulate the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 3 (Strong Conjecture about [F] -irregular graphs) . 
 
 
 For every connected graph F of order |F|\geq 3 , there exist infinitely many [F] -irregular graphs. 
 
 
 
 
 A natural next step is to extend the concept of [F] -irregularity to oriented graphs, as was recently done for classical F -irregularity [ 7 ] . First and foremost, we pose the existence problem for [F] -irregular oriented graphs where F is a directed path or a directed cycle, both of order n\geq 3 .
