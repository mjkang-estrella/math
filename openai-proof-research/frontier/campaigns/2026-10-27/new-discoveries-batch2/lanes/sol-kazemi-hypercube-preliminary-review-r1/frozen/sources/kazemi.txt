                                                                                   Abstract
        A proper coloring of the vertices of a graph G is called a total dominator coloring if every vertex of G is adjacent to all vertices
        of at least one color class. The total dominator chromatic number of G, denoted by χtd (G), is the minimum number of color
        classes required in such a coloring. In this paper, the total dominator chromatic number of the hypercube Q2n is determined
        for n ≥ 3. Also, algorithms are developed to obtain the maximum number 2n of disjoint total dominating sets in Q2n for
        n = 3 and n = 4. A notable feature of this study is the introduction and utilization of novel combinatorial structures termed
        semi-Latin squares, pairs of mate semi-Latin squares, and chess Latin squares, which play a crucial role in constructing
        the corresponding colorings and domination partitions.
        Keywords: total dominator coloring; total domination number; total domatic number; k-dominating colorable graph;
        hypercube; (pair of mate) semi-Latin square; chess Latin square.
        2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C15, 05C69, 05B15.

2.   Preliminaries

In a graph G = (V (G), E(G)), denote by degG (v), NG (v), and NG [v], respectively, the degree, open neighborhood, and closed
neighborhood of a vertex v ∈ V (G). The minimum degree, maximum degree, and independence number of G are denoted
by δ = δ(G), ∆ = ∆(G), and α = α(G), respectively. Also, a complete graph of order n is denoted by Kn , and a subgraph of
G induced by a subset S ⊆ V is denoted by G[S]. For any positive integers k and ℓ, we use [ℓ] and k + [ℓ] to denote the sets
{1, 2, . . . , ℓ} and {k + 1, k + 2, . . . , k + ℓ}, respectively. Also, for any two sets A and B, AB denotes the set {ab | a ∈ A, b ∈ B},
and aB = AB where A = {a}.
    For any two graphs G and H, the Cartesian product G□H is a graph with vertex set V (G) × V (H) and edge set

                             E(G□H) = {(g, h1 )(g, h2 ) | h1 h2 ∈ E(H)} ∪ {(g1 , h)(g2 , h) | g1 g2 ∈ E(G)}.

Thus, G□H is obtained from G by substituting a copy a H of H for every vertex a of G, and then joining the the vertex (a, c)
of a H to the vertex (b, c) of b H whenever ab ∈ E(G). By assumption, Q1 = K2 , and by induction, we define Qn = Qn−1 □K2
as the n-dimensional hypercube. In fact, Qn is a simple bipartite graph whose vertex set is

                                                   {a1 a2 . . . an | ai ∈ {0, 1} for all i},

and whose edges are the pairs of n-tuples that differ in exactly one position.
    A dominating set S of a graph G is a subset of V (G) such that every vertex in V (G) − S is adjacent to at least one vertex
in S. A total dominating set (briefly, TD-set) S of G, where G has no isolated vertex, is a subset of V (G) such that every
vertex in V (G) is adjacent to at least one vertex in S. The domination number γ(G) and the total domination number γt (G)
of G are the minimum cardinalities of a dominating set and a TD-set of G, respectively. The total domatic number tdom(G)
of G is the maximum order of a vertex partition of G into total dominating sets.
    For the set [k] of k colors, a function f : V (G) → [k] is a proper k-coloring of G if f is a homomorphism from G to the
complete graph Kk with vertex set [k]; that is, f (x) ̸= f (y) for every edge xy ∈ E(G). A graph that has a proper k-coloring
is called k-colorable. The chromatic number χ(G) of a graph G is the minimum number k of colors needed in a proper
coloring of G.
    In a proper coloring of a graph, a color class is a set consisting of all vertices assigned the same color. If f is a proper
coloring of G with color classes V1 , V2 , . . . , Vℓ such that every vertex in Vi has color i, we simply write f = (V1 , V2 , . . . , Vℓ ).
For a proper coloring f = (V1 , V2 , . . . , Vℓ ) of a graph G, a vertex v is called a common neighbor of Vi , or equivalently, we say
that v is totally dominated by Vi , or that Vi totally dominates v, and we write v ≻t Vi if Vi ⊆ NG (v). Otherwise, we write
v ̸≻t Vi .
    A vertex v is called a private neighbor of Vi with respect to f if v ≻t Vi and v ̸≻t Vj for all j ̸= i. The set of all common
neighbors of Vi , denoted by CNG (Vi ) or simply by CN (Vi ), is called the common neighborhood of Vi in G with respect to f .
    A new type of vertex coloring of a graph was introduced in [9] as follows.

Definition 2.1. A total dominator coloring, briefly TD-coloring, of a graph G without isolated vertices is a proper coloring
of G such that every vertex of G is adjacent to all vertices of some color class. The minimum number of color classes in a
TD-coloring of G is called the total dominator chromatic number of G, denoted by χtd (G). A TD-coloring of G using exactly
χtd (G) colors is called a minimum total dominator coloring (or simply, a min-TD-coloring.

To prove that the total domatic number of the hypercube Q16 is 16, we introduce and utilize novel combinatorial structures
termed semi-Latin squares, pairs of mate semi-Latin squares, and chess Latin squares, which play a crucial role in constructing
the corresponding colorings and domination partitions.

6.      A Question and a Conjecture

We recall that a graph is called well-totally-dominated if all of its minimal total dominating sets have the same size.
Consequently, its upper total domination number and total domination number are equal. This naturally leads to the
following question.

Question 6.1. Is there any well-totally-dominated hypercube?

  Since the total domination number of a graph is a lower bound for its total dominator chromatic number, by (1) and
