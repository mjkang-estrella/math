                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                              Nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials Eµ (x1 , . . . , xn ; q, t) = Eµ are an important family
                                           of polynomials indexed by compositions, whose coefficients are rational functions in param-
                                           eters q and t. They were introduced by Opdam [13], Macdonald [11], and Cherednik [4] to
                                           settle conjectures related to symmetric Macdonald polynomials. Since then, a lot of research
                                           has been done on nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials, connecting them to physics [5],
                                           DAHA [11], various combinatorial models [2, 7, 9, 14], and other families of polynomials
                                           via specializations of q and t: Key polynomials (q = t = 0) and Type A Demazure atoms
                                           (q, t → ∞) [12]. Additionally, various generalizations of Eµ include permuted basement non-
                                           symmetric Macdonald polynomials [1], Koornwinder polynomials [11], and nonsymmetric
                                           Macdonald superpolynomials [8]. In this paper, we are interested in studying two models:
                                           the nonattacking filling model of Haglund, Hamian, and Loehr [9], and the multiline queue
                                           model of Corteel, Mandelshtam, and Williams in [7]. We write MLQ for the latter model
                                           and HHL for the former.
                                              The first combinatorial model for nonsymmetric Macdonald polynomials was due to Haglund,
                                           Haiman, and Loehr [9]. Their model depends on certain fillings, called nonattacking fillings,
                                           of augmented diagrams and only works for type GLn , see Section 2.1 for more details. It
                                           was built on work they did for the modified Macdonald polynomials. Soon after, Ram and
                                           Yip [14] defined the alcove walk model which works for all Lie types. In 2018, based on the
                                           work of Cantini, de Gier, and Wheeler [3], Corteel, Mandelshtam, and Williams [7] modified
                                           the multiline queue model and showed that it is equivalent to nonsymmetric Macdonald
                                           polynomials indexed by partitions. In that same paper, their computations showed that
                                           there are fewer multiline queues than nonattacking fillings, and they noticed that a sum of a
                                           subset of permuted basement nonattacking fillings corresponded to a single multiline queue.
                                                                                               1
Furthermore, they left open the question of whether there is an extension of the MLQ model
to all compositions. The primary goal of this paper is to provide a first step in that direction.
   To do so, we analyze the HHL and MLQ models to understand the key differences between
them. As a result, we introduce a variant of the HHL model that includes the notion of a
trivial pair from the MLQ model to nonattacking fillings. The combinatorial objects in this
family form a subset of nonattacking fillings that we call restricted nonattacking fillings;
see Section 3.2 for more details. We can view them as an extension of the MLQ model to
compositions because, in the partition case, the number of restricted nonattacking fillings is
equal to the number of multiline queues; see Section 3.2 for a proof of this. Also, we define a
modified q, t-weight based on the q, t-weight of the HHL model that takes into account trivial
pairs for general augmented diagrams. We conjecture that the restricted nonattacking fillings
form a combinatorial model for Eµ .

   While working on this paper, Yu Jin and Lin sketched a proof of a similar result in their
paper [10]. The primary differences between our results and theirs are the set of fillings and
the proof method. The set of restricted nonattacking fillings that we describe in this paper
is more restrictive, resulting in our model having fewer terms for general compositions. For
the partition case, we get the same number of terms and the same weight. As for the proof
methods, we do not use superization, which is a method by which the set of allowable entries
is extended, and the q, t-weight may be negative depending on some details that we do not
go into. Due to the similarity between our results and theirs, we believe it is possible to
prove Conjecture 1.1 using their techniques.

and the multiline queue model. In Section 3, we introduce mixed nonattacking fillings and

nonattacking fillings, and we study their effect on the major index and coinversions statistics.
Section 5 contains the proof of the compression formula for the nontrivial subset of compo-
sitions mentioned above. Finally, in Section 6, we explain why it is challenging to extend

                                     2. Background
   In this section, we introduce the two models studied in this paper. We begin by reviewing
the nonattacking fillings model introduced by Haglund, Haiman, and Loehr in 2008. See [9]
for more details and proofs. Then, we move on to the multiline queue model that was
introduced by Corteel, Mandelshtam, and Williams in 2018. See [7] for more details and
proofs.
2.1. Nonattacking Fillings. A (weak) composition is a sequence µ = (µ1 , . . . , µn ) of non-
negative integers. The µi ’s are referred to as the parts of µ, and we define the length of µ,
denoted ℓ(µ), to be the number of parts. If the parts form a weakly decreasing sequence,
then the composition is called a partition, which we denote by λ = (λ1 , . . . , λn ) where
λ1 ≥ . . . λn ≥ 0.
   Given a composition µ, we define the diagram of µ, dg′ (µ), to be the set of coordinate
pairs (i, j), with 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ(µ) and 1 ≤ j ≤ µi . A diagram is represented by µi boxes in
column i. The row numbers increase from bottom to top, and the column numbers increase
left to right like Cartesian coordinates. The coordinates (i, j) of a box indicate the column
and row of a box. The prime on dg is there to differentiate it from the convention where the
parts of µ form rows instead of columns.
   The augmented diagram of µ, c   dg(µ), is obtained from the diagram dg′ (µ) by adding the
boxes (i, 0) for 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ(µ). This augmented row is called the basement. A pair of boxes
is said to attack each other if they are in the same row, or they are in consecutive rows
where the box in the lower row is to the right of the box in the upper row. If u is a box
in the diagram of µ, then we denote the box directly beneath u by d(u). Note that d(u) is
in the augmented diagram. In Figure 1, we show the diagram and augmented diagram of
µ = (0, 2, 2, 2, 0, 4, 2, 4).

2.2. Multiline Queue Model. Let L and n be positive integers. An L × n ball system B
is an array where each position is either empty or occupied by a ball, the number of balls
weakly decreases from bottom to top, and there is at least one ball in row L. Thus, L is the
number of rows and n is the number of columns of the array. The rows are labeled 1 through
L from bottom to top. See Figure 5 for an example of a 4 × 7 ball system with 12 balls.

                            Row 4

                            Row 3

       Figure 5. A 4 × 7 ball system with 4 balls in row 1, 3 balls in row 2, 3 balls
       in row 3, and 2 balls in row 4.

   A multiline queue of a ball system B is a diagram where each ball is matched with a ball
in the row below and assigned a label in [L] according to rules that we describe below. We
visualize a pairing in the ball diagram via the shortest possible strand that either travels
straight down or left to right, wrapping around if necessary.
   We define an algorithm that takes in a ball system B and outputs a multiline queue Q.
Suppose B is an L × n ball system. First, each ball in row L is paired with a ball in row
L − 1 in the following manner: if a ball in row L has a ball directly below it, then we must
pair those balls; such a pairing is called a trivial pair ; otherwise, a ball in row L is paired
with a ball in row L − 1 by the shortest possible strand that travels left to right, wrapping
around if necessary. Each ball paired at this step is labeled by an L. Then, we proceed to
repeat the previous step, maintaining the same requirements for trivial pairs, for the balls
labeled by an L in row L − 1, and labeling the balls paired at this step with an L. We repeat
this for each ball labeled by an L, decreasing by one row at each step, ending once each ball
in row L is connected to a ball in row 1. Next, we repeat the same procedure that we used
with the balls in row L, for each unpaired ball in row L − 1, but instead we label each ball
paired at this step with an L − 1. We continue in this way for each set of unpaired balls in
rows L, L − 1, . . . , 2. Finally, any remaining unpaired ball in row 1 is labeled by a 1. This
completes the algorithm. The result is a series of strands paired with the last ball in row 1.
Remark 2.2. A ball b in a pairing step pairs with a ball b′ directly below b if and only if b′
is free.

and the major index statistics. In some cases, we can use this action to show that the
mixed polynomial of one set of mixed fillings is equal to the polynomial of another set of

to use the operators to describe the exact set of nonattacking fillings that sum to a single

                                                 6. Conclusion
   In this section we compare the restricted fillings model with the HHL and MLQ models. In
particular, we note that the number of restricted fillings for a partition λ equals the number
of multiline queues of type λ. Also, we explain the difficulty of extending our proof methods
to more than two columns. We do have some ideas to get around this difficulty and are
currently working on them.
6.1. Number of Nonattacking Fillings and Restricted Nonattacking Fillings. For
compositions in general, the number of restricted nonattacking fillings can be significantly
smaller compared to the classical nonattacking fillings. We show this data in Table 3.
  In comparison with the MLQ model, we notice that for partitions the number of restricted
nonattacking fillings is equal to the number of multiline queues. We currently have a bijection
between these two sets that is not weight-preserving, see 3.2. If Conjecture 1.1 is true, then
the pair of statistics for q and t in the restricted nonattacking filling case is equidistributed
                                                           26
with the corresponding statistics for q and t in the MLQ model. It would be interesting to
find a bijection between restricted nonattacking fillings and multiline queues for partitions
that is weight-preserving.
