                                                                                  Abstract
                                                    This paper investigates the behaviour of one of the most famous Smaran-
                                                 dache’s sequence given by A061078 on oeis [1] . We’ll solve some open
                                                 problems such as the determination of an upper bound for the sequence
                                                 (which holds for all n ∈ N) and the determination of a closed formula for
                                                 a(n). Every result will be accompanied by Wolfram Mathematica scripts
                                                 examples in order to support our thesis.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           Definition 1. Let n ∈ N. We define a(n) as the nth term of the Smarandache’s
                                           sequence [2], or the sum of the products of the digits of the first n even numbers.
                                               The first terms of the sequence are:

                                                                  0, 2, 6, 12, 20, 20, 22, 26, 32, 40, 40, . . .

                                           Definition 2. Let p ∈ N be an even number. We define the contribution of p
                                           in a(n) as the product of its digits. We’ll indicate it using the notation C(p).
                                               For example:
                                                                      C(3688) = 1152 = 3 · 6 · 8 · 8
                                           Definition 3. Let b, c ∈ N even numbers and a(n) be the nth term of the
                                           Smarandache’s sequence; we define C(b → c) as the sum of the ”contributes” of
                                           all the even numbers from b to c. So:
                                                                                      c   b   
                                                                     C(b → c) = a         −a   −1
                                                                                        2    2
                                               For example:

                                                    C(14 → 22) = 1 · 4 + 1 · 6 + 1 · 8 + 2 · 0 + 2 · 2 = a(11) − a(6)
