## MATH: exhaustive attack and independent reconstruction

The imported radicality has exactly the needed hypotheses: Theorem 2.3(4), n=4,c=2, distinct positive exponents. The proof makes the complete intersection generically regular and then reduced; it is not merely generic-fiber radicality. Theorem 3.1 specializes at a=2 to normal-domain precisely when b≢2 mod4 and b≢2 mod3. For ν3(b)=0, its second alternative succeeds exactly when ν3(b−2)=0; if 3 divides b the first alternative succeeds. These statements exclude all other residues from the requested nonnormal-domain classification, once the exceptional nonprime cases are handled.

Here is the complete applicable classical bridge; the conclusion below does not rest on proof length.

1. Put Q=V(p2)⊂P3. Its quadratic form has rank four, so Q is smooth. Over C choose coordinates giving X0X3−X1X2=0; the Segre map identifies Q with P1×P1. Let A and B be the classes of fibers of its two projections. These generate Pic(Q): a divisor restricts to a principal divisor on A2 because C[u,v] is factorial; subtract that principal divisor and the remaining divisor is supported on the two boundary fibers. Independence follows by degrees on the two fibers. Their intersections are A²=B²=0 and A·B=1 (move fibers, or compute transverse local coordinates). A hyperplane is A+B. Thus C_b=V_Q(pb) has class (b,b). An effective nonzero curve has class (c,d) with c,d≥0, because intersection with general fibers gives nonnegative degrees. Its hyperplane degree is c+d.

2. Use source reducedness. If C_b is reducible, partition ALL of its irreducible components into any two nonempty sets D,E. They share no component, regardless of how many components either contains. Classes are (c,d) and (b−c,b−d), with 0≤c,d≤b, 1≤t=c+d≤2b−1. Intersection is I=b(c+d)−2cd. On a smooth surface, the intersection of two effective divisors without common components is the sum of lengths O_Q,P/(f_D,f_E). This follows by restricting the defining section of E to D and counting its zeros with length; the degree is the class intersection just computed. At a node the completed equation has two distinct smooth tangent branches. If branches are split between D and E, the local ideal is a pair of transverse parameters, length one. If they are in the same part, the other part misses the point. A smooth point cannot lie in both parts. Therefore I≤N for a curve with N ordinary nodes and no other singularities. This deals with arbitrary partitions, not just two irreducible components.

3. Since cd≤t²/4, I≥bt−t²/2. This concave quadratic has minimum b−1/2 on [1,2b−1]; integrality yields I≥b. If there is no line component, D and E each have degree at least two: an effective degree-one divisor is a single ruling line. Now t∈[2,2b−2], giving I≥2b−2. Consequently N<b, or no lines together with N<2b−2, implies irreducibility. Neither hypothesis says simply that a singular curve must be irreducible.

4. Specialize the source's Theorem 3.1 proof, cases (a)–(d), at a=2. Let h=b−2. At every nonzero singular point the Jacobian rows are proportional, so every supported coordinate has the same nonzero h-th power. Normalize the first supported coordinate to one. All others are h-th roots of unity, hence of modulus one, and pb=p2 at that point. For b≡5 mod6, h is an odd multiple of three. For b≡8 mod12, h≡6 mod12. Neither allows ±i as an h-th root. Support one is impossible on Q; support two would require ratio ±i. For support four, four unit squares summing to zero split into opposite pairs, also requiring ±i. For completeness, if z1+z2=w≠0, the unordered unit pair with sum w is uniquely the intersection of the unit circle with Re(z conjugate(w))=|w|²/2. Apply this to −z3,−z4; if w=0 the pairing is immediate. This proof includes repeated entries.

5. Thus support has size three. Its squares, after normalization, are 1,ω,ω² in either order. For odd h, exactly one square root of each of ω and ω² has h-th power one: the cube roots themselves. There are two points per choice of missing coordinate, hence eight. For h≡6 mod12 both signs of both square roots work: two orders times four signs times four choices gives thirty-two. Normalizing the first supported coordinate removes projective repetitions. These are exhaustive statements for all b in the two families, not extrapolations from the checker.

6. At a normalized singular x, take F=pb−(b/2)p2. It has zero gradient. Its Hessian is diagonal with b(b−2) on the three supported directions and −b on the absent-coordinate direction. The tangent hyperplane to Q is x⊥. The supported rank-three dot product restricted to the isotropic plane x⊥ has radical exactly Cx, since the ambient form is nondegenerate and (x⊥)⊥=Cx. Modulo this radial direction the remaining supported quadratic form is nondegenerate of dimension one. The absent-coordinate direction is orthogonal and has nonzero coefficient −b. Thus the two-dimensional projective tangent quadratic form is nondegenerate. In a local chart on Q the restricted function has multiplicity exactly two and distinct tangent factors, so the singularity is an ordinary node with two transverse smooth branches. The analytic/complete-local-coordinate Morse lemma, or solving the distinct tangent factors formally, gives this standard node assertion. It yields length one when branches are partitioned, not length two. At x=(1,ω,ω²,0), the quotient representatives (−ω,1,0,0),(0,0,0,1) give determinant b²(b−2)ω≠0. The radical argument handles every sign and permutation, not just this representative.

7. Rule out all lines in the required even family (in fact the argument works for both). Restrict coordinates to linear forms l_i on a hypothetical line L. Ignore zero forms and group proportional nonzero forms. For m≤4 distinct directions choose binary variables so representatives are u+t_j v with distinct finite t_j. The coefficients in degrees v^0,...,v^(m−1) of their b-th powers form diag(binomial(b,k)) times the Vandermonde matrix (t_j^k). All binomials are nonzero for b≥3, so the powers are independent. The identity Σl_i^b=0 forces cancellation separately in each group. No group can have one member. The coordinate map has rank two on a line, requiring at least two groups. Four coordinates therefore give exactly two groups of size two and no zero coordinate. Write the directions L,M. Their squares are independent; p2=0 forces the scalar ratio in each pair to be ±i. Cancellation in pb then requires (±i)^b=−1, exactly b≡2 mod4. It cannot occur for b≡0 mod4 or odd b. This includes every ruling and does not assume a special paired-coordinate form of L at the outset.

8. For b=6k+5,k≥1, N=8<b (minimum b=11). For b=12k+8,k≥1, no lines and N=32<2b−2 (minimum b=20). Steps 1–7 imply C_b irreducible. Its affine zero locus is the cone over C_b, including its vertex; the punctured cone is covered by line-bundle charts and is irreducible, and the vertex lies in its closure. The homogeneous source ideal is radical, so Nullstellensatz identifies it with the prime vanishing ideal of this irreducible cone. This is where radicality prevents hidden embedded-origin or thickening concerns. Normality fails by the source classification.

9. Close every excluded case. If b≡2 mod4, (p2,pb) is strictly inside the height-two prime (x1−ix2,x3−ix4), and has height two by the source complete-intersection theorem. Strictness follows because the former has no linear form. A strict containment of primes in a polynomial ring increases height, so the former cannot be prime. At b=5, Newton identities give p5∈(p1,p2); this latter height-two ideal is prime because eliminating p1 leaves a rank-three quadratic in three variables, irreducible over C. Strictness again follows from degree one. At b=8 set E=x2²x3²+x2²x4²+x3²x4²−x1⁴ and Z=x1x2x3x4. Modulo p2, Newton identities in y_i=x_i² give p8=2(E²−2Z²). The two degree-four factors E±√2 Z are nonzero modulo (p2,p8): any degree-four ideal membership would imply divisibility by p2, whereas at (1,i,0,0) both evaluate to −1. They are nonzero zero divisors. The remaining nonnormal residues b≡2 mod3 outside b≡2 mod4 are exactly odd b≡5 mod6 and even b≡8 mod12. Removing b=5,8 gives exactly the two claimed families. The source has a=2<b, so no claim for b≤2 is needed.

Lemma 3.2 already contains the unit-triangle and opposite-pair reasoning. These are necessary additions; the source's mere existence-of-singularities theorem does not itself imply primality.

The strongest argument for investment is the choice to pass from affine normality to global reducibility on Q: it is a useful geometric synthesis, and the source explicitly leaves the endpoint conjectural.
