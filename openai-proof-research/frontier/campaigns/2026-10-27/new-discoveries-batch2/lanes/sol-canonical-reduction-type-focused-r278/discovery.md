# Canonical reduction: focused research hold

No complete resolution, no proposed discovery credit, no PROOF.md. Counts remain root-owned: 9 additional, 39 total, next 40.

## Literal endpoint and source scope

Moscariello–Strazzanti, arXiv:2003.05391v1, printed p9, Question 4.4: for EVERY numerical semigroup S minimally generated by four positive integers m=n1<n2<n3<n4, does the condition m+F-f in S for EVERY f in PF(S) imply |PF(S)|<=4? Here F is the largest gap, and PF consists of gaps f such that f+ni is in S for every minimal generator. The source first phrases this as the semigroup ring having a canonical reduction, then explicitly gives this arithmetic equivalence. There is no nearly-Gorenstein hypothesis. Moscariello–Sammartano arXiv:2406.00790v3, printed p9, Problem 21 repeats exactly this scope.

Rahimi arXiv:1712.00755v3, Theorem 3.12 pp12, gives the canonical-reduction equivalence for ALL gaps. The source cites its published counterpart as Theorem 3.13: the version numbering differs. Its actual proof uses the valuation of a canonical reduction to obtain the coefficient of t^m and conversely constructs t^(F+m)T. Reduction from all gaps to PF is elementary: extend any gap g upward in the S-order to a maximal gap f, so f-g in S; then m+F-g=(m+F-f)+(f-g). These bodies do not prove a type bound.

## Re-derived starting reductions

Put A=Ap(S,m), W=max A=F+m. Under x<=S y iff y-x in S, maximal A elements are m+PF(S). Indeed w maximal means w-m is a gap with (w-m)+ni in S for every ni; the converse follows by subtracting a nonzero S-summand from any purported larger Apéry element. Distinct PF elements are incomparable: if g-f is a nonzero element of S, the defining PF property puts g in S.

For maximal w=m+f distinct from W, W-w=F-f is a gap by incomparability. Hence the source condition is exactly W+m-w in A for all such w; w=W contributes m in S automatically. Every factorization of x_f=m+F-f=W-f in S has zero m coefficient, or it would put F-f in S. Thus x_f=a2*n2+a3*n3+a4*n4. In every RF+ row f=-ni+sum(j!=i)b_ij*nj, ai>0 forces b_i1=0: adding to F-f=-m+sum aj*nj otherwise expresses F in S. This applies to every factorization and every row, rather than a convenient selected matrix.

## New route in this slot: divisor boxes of the TOP Apéry element

This is uncredited structural scaffolding, with a complete restricted-case argument below. Let T=<n2,n3,n4>, let Z(W) be ALL nonnegative three-coordinate factorizations of W, and define

    D(W) = {u in N^3 : W-u.n in S}.

Since W is Apéry, any factorization of W or of its S-divisors contains no m. Consequently

    D(W) = union over v in Z(W) of the boxes {u: 0<=u<=v coordinatewise}.       (B)

For each non-F pseudo-Frobenius f, EVERY factorization a of x_f is a minimal excluded vector of D(W). In fact W-a.n=f is a gap, whereas for each i with ai>0,

    W-(a-e_i).n = f+ni in S.

All proper coordinate predecessors are then in D(W) by downward closure. Moreover every such a represents an actual Apéry element x_f, not just a formal corner. If f and g differ, no factorization a of x_f can be coordinatewise <= any factorization b of x_g: otherwise x_g-x_f=f-g would be in S, contradicting PF incomparability. This retains multiple factorizations and all binomial identifications of equal weighted degree.

Restricted conclusion, valid for all multiplicities and generator sizes: IF W has a unique factorization v in T, THEN the desired type bound holds. By (B) the minimal excluded vectors are precisely (v_i+1)e_i for i=2,3,4. Each non-F PF value supplies one of these three vectors; distinct values have distinct weights x_f. Hence there are at most three non-F PF values, and t<=4. More generally, if each x_f admits any pure-power factorization, there is at most one f per generator ray, since two different multiples have difference in S. This argument is a direct monomial-divisor observation and is NOT nominated as a new-to-literature theorem or a source resolution.

For TWO distinct factorizations v,w of W, one can completely list the minimal excluded vectors of their union of boxes. There are the three pure vectors (max(v_i,w_i)+1)e_i; in addition, for i with v_i>w_i and j with v_j<w_j, there is (w_i+1)e_i+(v_j+1)e_j. These are all: a vector outside both boxes either exceeds their common coordinate maximum or exceeds v in one coordinate and w in a different coordinate. Minimality forces exactly the listed thresholds. As both sign sets are nonempty and disjoint in three coordinates, there are at most two mixed vectors, hence at most five candidates. This only proves t<=6 in the two-factorization case, before exact weight/Apéry/PF filtering. It does not establish t<=4. More factorizations give a union with more corners, not a single box.

The explicit missing universal lemma is: for every actual minimal four-generator semigroup satisfying the source reflection, among ALL minimal excluded vectors of D(W), the weights x in Ap(S,m) whose complementary gaps W-x are non-F PF numbers number at most THREE. Repeated weights are counted once. A proof must use the residue-minimum property of Apéry, positivity/minimality of the three generators, and the PF tests for coordinates absent from each excluded vector. Counting corners of arbitrary three-variable monomial downsets cannot replace these hypotheses.

## Exact obstruction to collapsing the boxes

The source example S=<16,17,19,39> has W=95 with exactly the factorizations (0,5,0) and (1,0,2) in coordinates (17,19,39). Its non-F reflections are 58=(0,1,1), 36=(1,1,0), and 34=(2,0,0). For 58, its predecessors 19 and 39 lie in DIFFERENT top boxes; no single factorization of W contains both. For 36, predecessors 17 and 19 likewise require different boxes. Selecting one top factorization and treating D(W) as its box therefore loses admissible mixed reflections even in the original sharp example. The five two-box corners have weights 34,114,117,36,58; only the three weights 34,36,58 survive the exact Apéry/PF filters. This is the concrete reason the restricted argument does not close the source question.

The known example has F=79, PF={37,59,61,79}. The canonical values are 58,36,34,16. It is not nearly Gorenstein: at generator17, candidate NG entries 37,59,61 fail against q=79 with negative values -25,-3,-1; entry79 fails against q=37 because 59 is a gap. Theorem 2.4's all-row proof cannot supply the missing lemma. Its first key step uses a second NG value fi=F-ni+nl to force ai=0, then two negative rows to force an RF+ row onto a single ray. Neither second row follows from canonical reduction.

Two hypothesis-deletion countermodels remain relevant: <6,7,8,9,10,11> has canonical reduction and type5 but six generators; <9,10,12,13> has four generators, PF={11,14,15,16,17}, and fails reflection. The latter even has uniquely factorized W=26=2*13, so unique factorization alone without canonical reduction does not suffice.

## Checks, limits, and classical objection

checks.py uses integer Dijkstra Apéry data and independent complete finite dynamic-programming membership/PF verification, followed by complete factorizations of reflected elements, all RF rows, and all top factorizations. It checks the divisor-box minimal-exclusion and cross-factorization antichain statements. Four fixed deformations of the source, m in [8,180], yield 519 minimally four-generated semigroups, 218 canonical; no full-union-support/repeated-support/multiple-reflection-factorization or type>=5 witness in that limited collection. This is a structural falsifier diagnostic, not an enlarged general census, not evidence for a universal support classification, and not a parameter-family discovery. The source example already refutes the pure-reflection shortcut. All ranges and exact retained certificates are in exact-checks.json.

Strongest COMPLETE classical-adaptation objection to the partial work: socle/Apéry maxima, RF zero products, monomial divisibility, and a union of boxes are standard algebraic encodings. The unique-top-factorization argument is a short consequence of these encodings. Murai–Takahashi's current successor already uses unique Apéry factorizations and socle monomials, albeit with a different exact PF hypothesis. Nothing here establishes novelty of the partial case. A full result needs the universal multi-box admissibility bound above or one exactly certified type>=5 canonical semigroup. Neither was obtained.
