                                                    Abstract. Let X1 , . . . , Xn be independent random points in the unit ball of Rd such that Xi follows
                                                    a beta distribution with the density proportional to (1 − ∥x∥2 )βi 1{∥x∥<1} . Here, β1 , . . . , βn > −1
                                                    are parameters. We study random polytopes of the form [X1 , . . . , Xn ], called beta polytopes. We




arXiv:2503.22488v1 [math.PR] 28 Mar 2025
                                                    determine explicitly expected values of several functionals of these polytopes including the number of
                                                    k-dimensional faces, the volume, the intrinsic volumes, the total k-volume of the k-skeleton, various
                                                    angle sums, and the S-functional which generalizes and unifies many of the above examples. We identify
                                                    and study the central object needed to analyze beta polytopes: beta cones. For these, we determine
                                                    explicitly expected values of several functionals including the solid angle, conic intrinsic volumes and
                                                    the number of k-dimensional faces. We identify expected conic intrinsic volumes of beta cones as a
                                                    crucial quantity needed to express all the functionals mentioned above. We obtain a formula for these

1.1. Summary of main results. In the present paper we study problems of geometric probability
related to the d-dimensional beta distribution. This rotationally invariant probability distribution on
the d-dimensional unit ball Bd := {x ∈ Rd : ∥x∥ ≤ 1} is defined via its Lebesgue density

Here, β > −1 is a parameter and ∥x∥ is the Euclidean norm of x ∈ Rd . If a random point X in Bd
follows this distribution, we write X ∼ fd,β . For instance, the beta distribution with β = 0 is the
uniform distribution on the ball Bd . The weak limit of the beta distribution as β ↓ −1 is the uniform
distribution on the unit sphere Sd−1 := {x ∈ Rd : ∥x∥ = 1}. For this reason, we say that a random
point X follows a beta distribution with parameter β = −1 if X is uniformly distributed on Sd−1 and
write X ∼ fd,−1 even though fd,−1 does not exist as a probability density. The beta distribution has
been introduced into geometric probability by Miles [31] and Ruben and Miles [36]. Their results are
reviewed in [29]; see also [5, 6, 7, 20, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28] for further papers on stochastic geometry
where the beta distributions play a major role.
1.1.1. Beta polytopes. The main object of investigation in the present paper are random beta polytopes
defined in the following way. Let X1 , . . . , Xn be independent random points in Bd , where each Xi
follows a beta-distribution fd,βi with its own parameter βi ≥ −1. The convex hull of these points,
denoted by
          β1 ,...,βn
    P := Pn,d        := [X1 , . . . , Xn ] := {λ1 X1 + . . . + λn Xn : λ1 ≥ 0, . . . , λn ≥ 0, λ1 + . . . + λn = 1},
is called a beta polytope. We shall also express the fact that P is a beta polytope in the following way,
                                          P ∼ BetaPoly(Rd ; β1 , . . . , βn ).
In the course of this work, we shall give explicit formulas for expected values of the following functionals
of beta polytopes:
     • fk (P), the number of k-dimensional faces of P, for all k ∈ {0, . . . , d − 1};
     • Vold (P), the volume of P;
     • Vk (P), the k-th intrinsic volume of P;
     • the total k-volume of the k-dimensional skeleton of P and its Lp -generalization;
     • the sum of internal and the sum of external angles at all k-dimensional faces of P;
     • the sum of conic intrinsic volumes of tangent cones of P at its k-dimensional faces;
     • the beta content, that is the probability that an additional independent beta-distributed point
        X ∼ fd,β falls into P;
     • the S-functional, to be introduced in Section 6.4, which unifies and generalizes many of the
        above mentioned examples.

Remark 1.3 (On beta type distributions). It is possible to develop a theory parallel to the one
presented here by replacing the beta distribution with the beta prime distribution whose probability
density is proportional to (1 + ∥x∥2 )−β , with parameter β > d/2, on the whole of Rd . Further,
                                                                             2    2
the isotropic normal distribution with density proportional to e−∥x∥ /(2σ ) is a limit of appropriately
scaled beta and beta prime distributions as β → ∞. For this reason, all results of the present paper
have analogues for Gaussian polytopes generated by random Gaussian points X1 , . . . , Xn in Rd with
possibly different variances σ12 , . . . , σn2 . We refrain from presenting these extensions here.

1.3. Motivation to introduce different parameters and beta cones. To conclude the introduc-
tion, we would like to motivate the study of beta cones and beta polytopes with different paramaters
β1 , . . . , βn . Up until recently, the work on random beta polytopes has been mostly in the setting where
all parameters are equal, that is, β1 = · · · = βn ≥ −1, such as [5, 6, 7, 19, 20, 22, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28].
Notable exceptions are the papers of Ruben and Miles [31, 35, 36], where the authors introduced and
studied random beta simplices, that is, convex hulls of at most d + 1 independent beta-distributed
points in Rd , allowing for possibly different parameters. An extensive study of beta simplices was
conducted in [29]. Recently, Moseeva [32] derived formulas for the expected volume of beta polytopes
with different parameters adapting the method of [27].
   Let us consider two natural problems that crucially rely on allowing for different parameters. Both
problems lead in a natural way to beta cones, which motivates their study.

λ1 , . . . , λm is called a linear combination of v1 , . . . , vm . A linear combination λ1 v1 + . . . + λm vm is
called
         • a positive combination if λ1 ≥ 0, . . . , λm ≥ 0;
         • a convex combination if λ1 ≥ 0, . . . , λm ≥ 0 and λ1 + . . . + λm = 1;
         • an affine combination if λ1 + . . . + λm = 1.
    The linear hull lin A (respectively, the positive hull pos A, the convex hull conv A and the affine
hull aff A) of a set A ⊆ Rd is the set of all linear (respectively, positive, convex, affine) combinations
of elements from A. The convex hull of finitely many points v1 , . . . , vm is also denoted by [v1 , . . . , vm ],
and their positive hull is denoted by pos(v1 , . . . , vm ).
    A set A ⊆ Rd is called a linear subspace (respectively, a convex cone, a convex set or an affine
subspace) if it coincides with its own linear (respectively, positive, convex, affine) hull. We denote
by G(d, k), respectively A(d, k), the set of k-dimensional linear, respectively affine, subspaces of Rd ,
where k ∈ {0, . . . , d}. For an affine subspace A ∈ A(d, k) let ΠA : Rd → A be the orthogonal projection
onto A. Also, let A⊥ := {x ∈ Rd : ⟨x, y⟩ = 0 for all y ∈ A − a0 } (where a0 ∈ A is arbitrary) denote
the orthogonal complement of A. Note that A⊥ is always a linear subspace. If v ∈ Rd is a non-zero
vector, then v ⊥ denotes its orthogonal complement.
    For a set A ⊆ Rd , its relative interior, denoted by relint A, is the interior of A taken with respect
to aff A as the ambient space.
    A polyhedron is an intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces. A polytope is a convex hull of
finitely many points. Equivalently, by Minkowski’s theorem, a polytope can be defined as a bounded
polyhedron. The dimension dim P of a polyhedron P is the dimension of its affine hull aff P . A
polyhedral cone C is the positive hull of finitely many vectors in Rd . Equivalently, a polyhedral cone
is an intersection H1 ∩ . . . ∩ Hp of finitely many half-spaces of the form Hi = {x ∈ Rd : ⟨x, ui ⟩ ≤ 0},
where u1 , . . . , up ∈ Rd \{0}. The polar (or dual ) of a polyhedral cone C is defined by
                                  C ◦ := {v ∈ Rd : ⟨v, z⟩ ≤ 0 for all z ∈ C}.
The double dual theorem states that C ◦◦ = C.
   Unless stated otherwise, beta cones are denoted with C, beta polytopes with P, arbitrary determ-
inistic polyhedral cones with C and deterministic polytopes with P .

6.4. Expected S-functional. In this section we introduce a general class of functionals of polytopes
that allows to treat in a unified way many natural examples.
6.4.1. Definition of the S-functional. We start with some preliminaries.
Space of simplices. Let Simpl(Rk ) be the set of k-dimensional simplices [x1 , . . . , xk+1 ] in Rk . Two sim-
plices differing only by the order of vertices are considered equal. A functional φ : Simpl(Rk ) → R is
called rotationally invariant if φ([Ox1 , . . . , Oxd+1 ]) = φ([x1 , . . . , xd+1 ]) for every orthogonal trans-
formation O : Rk → Rk and every simplex [x1 , . . . , xk+1 ] ∈ Simpl(Rk ). Further, φ is said to
be δ-homogeneous with δ ∈ R if φ([cx1 , . . . , cxk+1 ]) = cδ · φ([x1 , . . . , xk+1 ]), for all c > 0 and
                                      BETA POLYTOPES AND BETA CONES                                             53


all [x1 , . . . , xk+1 ] ∈ Simpl(Rk ). For example, the k-dimensional volume is rotationally invariant
and k-homogeneous. A functional φ : Simpl(Rk ) → R is called measurable if the function φ̄ :
(x1 , . . . , xk+1 ) 7→ φ([x1 , . . . , xk+1 ]) is Borel-measurable. The function φ̄ is defined on the (open)
set of tuples (x1 , . . . , xk+1 ) ∈ Rk(k+1) with the property that x1 , . . . , xk+1 are affinely independent.
Space of cones. Let PolyCones(Rd ) be the set of all polyhedral cones in Rd . Let PolyConesk (Rd ) be
the set of those polyhedral cones whose lineality space has dimension k ∈ {0, . . . , d}. A functional
ψ : PolyConesk (Rd ) → R is called rotationally invariant if ψ(OC) = ψ(C) for every cone C ∈
PolyConesk (Rd ) and every orthogonal transformation O : Rd → Rd . We endow PolyConesk (Rd ) with
the Borel-σ-algebra induced by the Hausdorff distance; see Remark 3.5.
Definition 6.17 (S-functional). Let P ⊆ Rd be a simplicial polytope, and let k ∈ {0, . . . , dim P −
1}. Consider two measurable, rotationally invariant functionals φ : Simpl(Rk ) → [0, ∞] and ψ :
PolyConesk (Rd ) → [0, ∞]. The S-functional of P is then defined as

                                              F ∈Fk (P )

We recall that Iaff F is an isometry between aff F and Rk . To simplify the notation, we shall write
φ(F ) := φ(Iaff F (F )).

special cases of the S-functional. Examples
