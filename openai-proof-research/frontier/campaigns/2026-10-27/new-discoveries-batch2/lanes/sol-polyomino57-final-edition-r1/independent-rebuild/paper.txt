# A 57-cell container for every fixed 11-omino
A computer-assisted counterexample to the formula in OEIS A395495
Research candidate | 29 October 2026 | proof replay and final review completed

## Abstract
We give a connected set of 57 lattice cells containing an integer translate of every edge-connected 11-cell set. Orientations are fixed and holes are permitted. This yields a(11) ≤ 57 for the translation-only universal-container problem. The formula a(n) = (n² - n + 6)/2 conjectured in OEIS A395495 predicts 58 at n = 11 and is therefore false. The proof reduces the assertion to 70 signed-diagonal domains: 35 empty-group cases, four forced-cell cases, and 31 finite potential certificates. We explain the certificate conditions and prove their soundness. The accompanying Lean sources close the literal endpoint without premises. No optimality of 57, first failing index, asymptotic statement, or algorithmic performance result is asserted.

## 1. Statement and witness
A cell is a point of Z², with adjacency at Manhattan distance one. A polyomino is a nonempty finite edge-connected set of cells. Let a(n) be the least size of a connected finite container C such that every n-cell polyomino P satisfies P + t ⊆ C for some t ∈ Z². Each orientation is an input in its own right; placement permits translations only.

THEOREM. Put D₅ = {(x,y) ∈ Z² : |x| + |y| ≤ 5} and
    C = D₅ \ {(2,2), (2,-2), (-2,2), (-2,-2)}.
Then |C| = 57, C is edge-connected, and every 11-cell polyomino has an integer translate contained in C. In particular a(11) ≤ 57 < 58.

## 2. Signed-diagonal normalization
Write u = x + y and v = x - y. Integer cells correspond exactly to pairs u ≡ v (mod 2); the inverse is x = (u + v)/2, y = (u - v)/2. An edge changes both signed coordinates by one in absolute value, and |x| + |y| = max(|u|, |v|).

For a connected 11-cell set P, let a and b be its u- and v-spans. Each lies between 1 and 10. Positivity follows from any edge. For the upper bound, choose cells realizing opposite extrema and take a simple path in P between them: it has at most ten edges, each changing either signed coordinate by one.

Reflection y ↦ -y swaps u and v, so we may arrange a ≥ b. If U = min u and V = min v, choose e ∈ {0,1} congruent to V - U modulo 2. Translation by (-U, e - V) in signed coordinates is integral in Cartesian coordinates because its two components have equal parity. The resulting set Q has extrema 0,a in u and e,e+b in v. Thus
    Q ⊆ V(a,b,e) = {(u,v) : 0 ≤ u ≤ a,
                            e ≤ v ≤ e+b, u ≡ v (mod 2)}.
It meets each of the four extremal boundary lines.

Initially there are 2·(1 + 2 + ... + 10) = 110 triples with 1 ≤ b ≤ a ≤ 10. When e = 1 and a is odd, use
    (u,v) ↦ (a-u, v-1).
Its Cartesian linear part is (x,y) ↦ (-y,-x), with integral translation ((a-1)/2,(a+1)/2). If instead b is odd, use
    (u,v) ↦ (u, b+1-v).
Its linear part is (x,y) ↦ (y,x), with integral translation ((b+1)/2,-(b+1)/2). These maps preserve adjacency and normalize e to zero. Keep both parities only when a and b are even. The 55 span pairs plus 15 even/even pairs give 70 canonical domains. Coverage, rather than uniqueness of representatives, is sufficient.

RECOVERING THE ORIGINAL ORIENTATION. Every transformation above has the form Q = gP + d with g a square-lattice rotation or reflection and d an integer vector. The actual witness C is invariant under every such g: both the diamond and the four deleted points are invariant. If a normalized placement gives Q - c ⊆ C, apply g^(-1) to obtain
    P + g^(-1)(d-c) ⊆ g^(-1)C = C.
The operation on the original piece is therefore a translation. This use of symmetry in the analysis does not authorize rotating a piece during placement.

The formal coverage layer uses literal Cartesian cells with signed-coordinate predicates. Its normalization theorems account for negative extrema and parity; a finite sample of normalized shapes is not a premise.

## 3. Every possible placement becomes a hitting constraint
For a normalized set with the four exact extrema of Section 2, a radius-five diamond center c = (s,t) in signed coordinates must, and may, satisfy
    a-5 ≤ s ≤ 5,   e+b-5 ≤ t ≤ e+5,   s ≡ t (mod 2).
Let S(a,b,e) be this finite set. These inequalities follow by applying the radius bound at both extrema of each coordinate. Conversely they place the whole domain inside the centered diamond. Thus no boundary center is omitted.

The deleted offsets become (4,0), (-4,0), (0,4), (0,-4). For each c ∈ S form its collision group
    F_c = V(a,b,e) ∩ {c+(4,0), c-(4,0),
                      c+(0,4), c-(0,4)}.
Failure of every placement in C is equivalent to meeting every F_c. Add the four extremal-line groups. Any counterexample is now a connected 11-vertex set in the finite domain meeting all these groups.

Discarding duplicate groups, or a group containing a smaller retained group, preserves the hitting condition when every discarded group contains a retained one. More generally, to rule out a counterexample it suffices to use any selected subfamily of the original groups: hitting all original groups implies hitting this subfamily. The formal table instances prove membership in the literal original family individually. They do not assume that an external pruning algorithm is complete.

An empty collision group makes a counterexample impossible. This closes 35 domains. An empty CENTER SET is different: it leaves the four extremal groups. The domain (10,10,1) has no centers and is handled by a potential certificate, not discarded as a vacuous success.

## 4. The four forced-cell cases
Singleton groups force their vertices into any hitting set. In domain (7,3,0), sixteen distinct vertices are forced, contradicting size eleven immediately. In (7,4,0) and (8,3,0), ten vertices are forced. In (8,4,0), eight are forced. Exact finite exhaustion closes these three remaining cases: every completion to eleven vertices either is disconnected or misses an original group.

For orientation, the earlier independent mathematical checker exhausted supersets of size AT MOST eleven: respectively 0, 11, 9, and 576 supersets in the four domains, with 0, 0, 0, and 3 connected supersets. The three connected supersets in (8,4,0) miss a required group. It would be false to describe every superset there as disconnected. The formal proof instead exhausts exact eleven-cell completions using powerset-cardinality identities, certified cuts and missing-group checks; these are different exhaustion interfaces to the same obstruction.

The remaining 31 domains use Section 5. Thus 35 + 4 + 31 = 70 covers every normalized case. This arithmetic is a guide to the proof, not a substitute for the proved case disjunction.

## 5. Why the potential certificates prove impossibility
Work in a finite undirected graph on a domain D, with a nonempty finite set M of group labels and membership predicate H(i,z). For every nonempty A ⊆ M and vertex z ∈ D, a table supplies a natural number L(A,z). The sufficient certificate conditions are:

BASE. If H(i,z) holds for every i ∈ A, then L(A,z) ≤ 1.
EDGE. For every adjacent z,w ∈ D, L(A,z) ≤ L(A,w) + 1.
MERGE. For disjoint nonempty A,B ⊆ M,
    L(A ∪ B,z) + 1 ≤ L(A,z) + L(B,z).
FULL. For every z ∈ D, L(M,z) ≥ 12.

LEMMA. The first three conditions imply L(A,z) ≤ |T| whenever T ⊆ D is nonempty and connected, z ∈ T, and T meets all groups in nonempty A ⊆ M. Consequently FULL excludes any connected hitting set of at most eleven vertices.

PROOF. Choose a spanning tree on T rooted at z. Assign each label i ∈ A to one chosen witness vertex of T in its group. A vertex may receive many labels, but each label is assigned exactly once. For a vertex v let A_v be the labels assigned in its descendant subtree. Whenever A_v is nonempty, let K_v consist of v and all paths from v to its assigned witnesses. We prove L(A_v,v) ≤ |K_v| upward through the tree.

If v carries labels B ≠ ∅, BASE supplies the initial bound L(B,v) ≤ 1 on {v}. For every relevant child w, induction gives L(A_w,w) ≤ |K_w|. EDGE gives L(A_w,v) ≤ |K_w| + 1, a bound on K_w ∪ {v}. If v has no assigned labels, initialize with the first relevant child; such a child exists because A_v is nonempty. Merge the remaining child contributions, and any initial labels at v, using MERGE. Their label masks are disjoint. Their vertex sets intersect exactly at v, so each subtraction of the common root counts the union correctly. This proves the inductive bound. A labeled leaf uses BASE alone; an unlabeled vertex with one relevant child uses EDGE alone. Unlabeled subtrees are ignored. At z we have A_z = A and K_z ⊆ T, proving the lemma. Combining L(M,z) ≥ 12 with |T| ≤ 11 is impossible.

No rule for the empty mask, monotonicity in labels, disjointness of group memberships, or optimality of a computed recurrence is required. Disjoint ASSIGNMENTS of labels justify MERGE even when the groups overlap.

For the 31 finite instances, literal tables establish BASE, EDGE, MERGE and FULL. The generator is a data supplier; its search procedure is outside this inference. The earlier independent mathematical review checked 31 original tables totaling 1,874,144 byte entries, including all relevant base, edge and merge inequalities. The formal packet can select smaller original group subfamilies and recertifies their tables. The old entry count is not asserted as the size of the final formal table payload.
