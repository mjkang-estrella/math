Abstract
Let A=\left(a_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n} be a Hermitian matrix of size
n\geq 2, and set
\displaystyle\rho\left(A\right)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij},
\displaystyle disc\left(A\right)
\displaystyle=\max_{X,Y\subset\left[n\right],X\neq\varnothing,Y\neq\varnothing}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{i\in Y}\left(a_{ij}-\rho\left(A\right)\right)\right|.
We show that the second singular value \sigma_{2}\left(A\right) of A
satisfies
\sigma_{2}\left(A\right)\leq C_{1}disc\left(A\right)\log n,
for some absolute constant C_{1}, and this is best possible up to a
multiplicative constant. Moreover, we construct infinitely many dense regular
graphs G such that
\sigma_{2}\left(A\left(G\right)\right)\geq C_{2}disc\left(A\left(G\right)\right)\log\left|G\right|
where C_{2}>0 is an absolute constant and A\left(G\right) is the
adjacency matrix of G. In particular, these graphs disprove two conjectures
of Fan Chung.
Keywords: discrepancy, graph eigenvalues, second singular
value, pseudo-random graphs, quasi-random graphs
1 Introduction
Given a Hermitian matrix A of size n, let \mu_{1}\left(A\right)\geq...\geq\mu_{n}\left(A\right) be its eigenvalues, and \sigma_{1}\left(A\right)\geq...\geq\sigma_{n}\left(A\right) be its singular
values. As A is Hermitian, the values \sigma_{i}\left(A\right) are the
moduli of \mu_{i}\left(A\right) taken in descending order, so
\sigma_{1}\left(A\right) is the l_{2} operator norm and the spectral
radius of A.
Our graph-theoretic notation is standard (e.g., see [1]). For
simplicity, graphs are assumed to be defined on the vertex set \left[n\right]=\left\{1,...,n\right\}. Occasionally, to remind the reader of
this, we write G\left(n\right) for a graph of order n, G\left(n,m\right) for a graph of order n and size m. We write e\left(X\right) for e\left(G\left[X\right]\right) if it is understood
which graph G is to be taken. Given a graph G, we let \mu_{i}\left(G\right)=\mu_{i}\left(A\left(G\right)\right), and \sigma_{i}\left(G\right)=\sigma_{i}\left(A\left(G\right)\right), where
A\left(G\right) is the adjacency matrix of G. Given a graph G and
X,Y\subset V\left(G\right), we denote by e\left(X,Y\right) the
number of the ordered pairs \left(u,v\right) such that u\in X, v\in X, and u is adjacent to v.
For every graph G=G\left(n\right), set \rho\left(G\right)=e\left(G\right)\binom{n}{2}^{-1} and let
\displaystyle disc_{1}\left(G\right)
\displaystyle=\max_{X\subset V\left(G\right),X\neq\varnothing}\left\{\frac{1}{\left|X\right|}\left|e\left(X\right)-\rho\left(G\right)\binom{\left|X\right|}{2}\right|\right\},
\displaystyle disc_{2}\left(G\right)
\displaystyle=\max_{X,Y\subset V\left(G\right),X\neq\varnothing,Y\neq\varnothing}\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-\rho\left(G\right)\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\right|\right\}.
The function disc_{1}\left(G\right) is, in fact, Thomason’s coefficient
\alpha in his definition of \left(p,\alpha\right)-jumbled graphs (see,
e.g. [11], [12]), on which he based his study of pseudo-random
graphs. In principle, disc_{2}\left(G\right) has the same role as
disc_{1}\left(G\right), although the two invariants may differ
significantly for certain graphs, e.g., the star K_{1,n}. Chung, Graham, and
Wilson [6] (see also [10]) used coarser functions to describe
the edge distribution of a graph, thus introducing the quasi-random graph
properties. Surprisingly, these properties can be expressed in terms of the
two largest moduli of the eigenvalues (or equivalently, the two largest
singular values) of the adjacency matrix of a graph; we refer the interested
reader to [10] for more details. A natural question is, whether similar
relations exists between singular values and the functions disc_{1}\left(G\right) and disc_{2}\left(G\right).
Chung ([7], p. 35) made the following interesting conjecture concerning
\sigma_{2}\left(G\right) and disc_{2}\left(G\right).
Conjecture 1
There is an absolute constant C such that for every regular
graph G,
\sigma_{2}\left(G\right)<Cdisc_{2}\left(G\right).
(1)
The main goal of this paper is to study similar questions for graphs and
Hermitian matrices. In particular, in section 2 we define a function
disc\left(A\right) for a Hermitian matrix A that naturally extends the
function disc_{2}\left(G\right), and show that there is some constant
C^{\prime} such that for every Hermitian matrix A of size n\geq 2, we
have
\sigma_{2}\left(A\right)<C^{\prime}disc\left(A\right)\log n.
(2)
We explicitly construct a nonnegative symmetric matrix showing that
(2) is best possible up to a multiplicative constant. Moreover, in
section 3 we construct infinitely many dense regular graphs G such
that
\mu_{2}\left(G\right)>C^{\prime\prime}disc_{2}\left(G\right)\log\left|G\right|
for some absolute constant C^{\prime\prime}>0, thus disproving Conjecture
1. In fact, as we show in section 3.4, these graphs
disprove also another conjecture of Chung stating a similar problem for
Laplacian eigenvalues ([8], p. 77).
In particular, in section 3.1 we show that the bound on disc_{1}\left(G\right) due to Thomason ([13], Theorem 1) can be easily extended to
disc_{2}\left(G\right).
Recently Chung and Graham discussed in [5] quasi-random graph
properties of sparse graphs. In particular, they asked whether for sparse
graphs small discrepancy implies small second singular value, as in the case
of dense graphs. Krivelevich and Sudakov gave an explicit example in
[10] that answers this question in the negative. In section 4
we describe a general construction showing that such examples are not exceptional.
2 Second singular value and discrepancy of Hermitian
matrices
Given a matrix A=\left(a_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n} and nonempty sets
I,J\subset\left[n\right], we denote by A\left[I,J\right] the
submatrix of the entries a_{ij} with i\in I, j\in J. We write E_{n}
for the n\times n matrix of all ones, and denote by \left\langle\mathbf{x,y}\right\rangle the standard inner product of two vectors
\mathbf{x,y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}. For a Hermitian matrix A=\left(a_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n} set
\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\left(A\right)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{ij},
\displaystyle disc\left(A\right)
\displaystyle=\max_{X,Y\subset\left[n\right],X\neq\varnothing,Y\neq\varnothing}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{j\in Y}\left(a_{ij}-\rho^{\prime}\left(A\right)\right)\right|.
(3)
In this section we investigate the relationship between \sigma_{2}\left(A\right) and disc\left(A\right). Our main goal is to prove Theorem
2 and to show that the assertion is best possible up to a
multiplicative constant.
Observe that for a graph G the value \rho\left(G\right) is, generally
speaking, different from \rho^{\prime}\left(A\left(G\right)\right)
of the adjacency matrix A\left(G\right) of G. However, setting
A=A\left(G\right), we easily see that for every two nonempty sets
X,Y\subset V\left(G\right),
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|\left|\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{j\in Y}\left(a_{ij}-\rho^{\prime}\left(A\right)\right)\right|-\left|\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{j\in Y}\left(a_{ij}-\rho\left(G\right)\right)\right|\right|
\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|\rho^{\prime}\left(A\right)-\rho\left(G\right)\right|\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|
\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}\left|\frac{1}{n^{2}}-\frac{1}{n\left(n-1\right)}\right|2e\left(G\right)\leq\frac{2e\left(G\right)}{n\left(n-1\right)}.
Therefore,
\left|disc_{2}\left(G\right)-disc\left(A\right)\right|\leq\frac{2e\left(G\right)}{n\left(n-1\right)}\leq 1,
(4)
i.e., the function disc\left(A\right) closely approximates
disc_{2}\left(G\right).
2.1 An upper bound on \sigma_{2}
Observe that for every Hermitian matrix A of size n, and every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}, the Rayleigh principle states that
\mu_{n}\left(A\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2}\leq\left\langle A\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle\leq\mu_{1}\left(A\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2};
thus, we see that for every \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},
\left|\left\langle A\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle\right|\leq\left\|A\right\|\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|=\sigma_{1}\left(A\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2}.
(5)
Theorem 2
There is some constant C such that for every Hermitian matrix
A of size n\geq 2,
\sigma_{2}\left(A\right)\leq Cdisc\left(A\right)\log n,
Before proceeding to the proof of this theorem let us prove some technical
results. We shall prove first a curious lemma that is somewhat stronger than needed.
Lemma 3
Let p\geq 1, n\geq 1 and 0<\varepsilon<1. Then for every
\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{1}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n} with \|\mathbf{x}\|_{p}=1, there is a vector \mathbf{y}=(y_{i})_{1}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n} such
that y_{i} take no more than
\left\lceil\frac{8\pi}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{4n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil
values and \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon.
Proof We shall prove first that if x_{i} are nonnegative reals, then there is a
\mathbf{y}=(y_{i})_{1}^{n} such that y_{i} are nonnegative reals,
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon, and y_{i} take no more
than
k=\left\lceil\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{2n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil
different values. We may and shall assume x_{1}\geq...\geq x_{n}\geq 0.
Let us define a sequence n_{1}<n_{2}<\ldots<n_{l}\leq n as follows. Set
n_{1}=1; having defined n_{i}, let s be the maximal index such that
x_{s}\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)x_{n_{i}};
if i=k+1 or s=n, stop the sequence; otherwise, let n_{i+1}=s+1. Finally,
for 1\leq j\leq n, set y_{j}=x_{n_{i+1}-1} if n_{i}\leq j<n_{i+1} and
y_{j}=0 if n_{k}<j\leq n. For the sake of convenience, set n_{l+1}=n+1.
Then, if l\leq k,
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left|x_{j}-y_{j}\right|^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{h=1}^{l}\sum\limits_{j=n_{h}}^{n_{h+1}-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}x_{n_{h+1}-1}\right)^{p}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{p}=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}<\varepsilon^{p}.
Let now l\geq k+1; observe, that the choice of k implies for every
j=n_{k+1},...,n,
x_{j}\leq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{k}x_{1}\leq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2n}.
Hence, for l\geq k+1, we obtain,
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left|x_{j}-y_{j}\right|^{p}
\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n_{k+1}-1}\left|x_{j}-y_{j}\right|^{p}+\sum\limits_{j=n_{k+1}}^{n}x_{j}^{p}
\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{h=1}^{k}\sum\limits_{j=n_{h}}^{n_{h+1}-1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}x_{n_{h+1}-1}\right)^{p}+n\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2n}\right)^{p}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}\leq\varepsilon^{p}.
Consequently, \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon, as required.
Let now \mathbf{x}=(x_{j})_{1}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n} be an arbitrary vector
and for every j\in\left[n\right], let
x_{j}=\left|x_{j}\right|\exp\left(\theta_{j}2\pi i\right),
where 0\leq\theta_{j}<1. Set
\mathbf{x}^{\ast}\mathbf{=}\left(\left|x_{1}\right|,\ldots,\left|x_{n}\right|\right).
According to the above, there exists \mathbf{z}=(z_{j})_{1}^{n}, such that
\|\mathbf{x}^{\ast}-\mathbf{z}\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon/2, z_{j}\geq 0
and z_{j} take at most
k=\left\lceil\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{4n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil
different values. Let
m=\left\lceil\frac{8\pi}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil.
We shall show that the vector \mathbf{y=}(y_{i})_{1}^{n} defined by
\left|y_{j}\right|=z_{j},\text{ }\arg\left(y_{j}\right)=\frac{\left\lfloor m\theta_{j}\right\rfloor}{m}2\pi
is as required. Let us first check that \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{p}\leq\varepsilon. Indeed, define \mathbf{z}^{\ast}=(z_{j}^{\ast})_{1}^{n} by
z_{j}^{\ast}=z_{i}\exp\left(\theta_{j}2\pi i\right).
We have, by the triangle inequality,
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}^{\ast}\|_{p}+\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{z}^{\ast}\|_{p}.
(6)
On the one hand,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|x_{j}-z_{j}^{\ast}\right|^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\left|x_{j}\right|-z_{j}\right|^{p}\exp\left(\theta_{j}2\pi i\right)^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\left|x_{j}\right|-z_{j}\right|^{p}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}.
(7)
On the other hand,
\left|\exp\left(\frac{\left\lfloor m\theta_{j}\right\rfloor}{m}2\pi i\right)-\exp\left(\theta_{j}2\pi i\right)\right|\leq 2\sin\left(\frac{1}{2m}2\pi\right)<\frac{2\pi}{m}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4},
and hence,
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|y_{j}-z_{j}^{\ast}\right|^{p}
\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|z_{j}\exp\left(\theta_{j}2\pi i\right)-z_{j}\arg\left(\frac{\left\lfloor m\theta_{j}\right\rfloor}{m}2\pi i\right)\right|^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)^{p}\left|z_{j}\right|^{p}
\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{p}\left(\left\|\mathbf{z}\right\|_{p}\right)^{p}\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)^{p}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|_{p}\right)^{p}<\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}.
Hence, in view of (6) and (7), we obtain
\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{p}\leq\left(\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\varepsilon.
To complete the proof, observe that y_{i} take at most
km\leq\left\lceil\frac{8\pi}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\log\frac{4n}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil
different values.
\Box
We say that a partition X=\cup_{i=1}^{m}P_{i} is proper if the sets
P_{i} are nonempty.
Lemma 4
Let B=\left(b_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n} be a Hermitian matrix
and \left[n\right]=\cup_{i=1}^{m}P_{i} be a proper partition. Let
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}, and \mathbf{x=}\left(x_{i}\right)_{i}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n} be such that x_{i}=y_{j} for every i\in P_{j}. Then
the Hermitian matrix C=\left(c_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{m} defined by
c_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|P_{i}\right|\left|P_{j}\right|}}\sum_{r\in P_{i}}\sum_{s\in P_{j}}b_{rs}
satisfies
\left|\left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sigma_{1}\left(C\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2}.
Proof For every k\in\left[m\right], set t_{k}=\sqrt{\left|P_{k}\right|}y_{k}, and let \mathbf{t=}\left(t_{1},...,t_{m}\right), so that
\left\|\mathbf{t}\right\|=\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|. Also, we see
that
\displaystyle\left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}x_{i}\overline{x}_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{t_{i}\overline{t}_{j}}{\sqrt{\left|P_{i}\right|\left|P_{j}\right|}}\sum_{r\in P_{i}}\sum_{s\in Pj}b_{rs}
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{ij}t_{i}\overline{t}_{j}=\left\langle C\mathbf{t,t}\right\rangle.
Hence, from (5), we obtain
\left|\left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sigma_{1}\left(C\right)\left\|\mathbf{t}\right\|^{2}=\sigma_{1}\left(C\right)\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|^{2},
completing the proof.
\Box
Proof of Theorem 2 Set \rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}\left(A\right) and let
B=A-\rho^{\prime}E_{n}.
Our first goal is to show that
\sigma_{2}\left(A\right)\leq\sigma_{1}\left(B\right).
(8)
Indeed, we have
\displaystyle\mu_{1}\left(A-B\right)
\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\left(\rho^{\prime}E_{n}\right)=\rho^{\prime}n,
\displaystyle\mu_{k}\left(A-B\right)
\displaystyle=\mu_{k}\left(\rho^{\prime}E_{n}\right)=0,\text{ for }k=2,...,n.
Weyl’s inequalities (e.g., see [9], p. 181) imply, that if C and D
are two Hermitian matrices of order n then
\mu_{2}\left(C+D\right)\leq\mu_{2}\left(C\right)+\mu_{1}\left(D\right),
and
\mu_{n}\left(C+D\right)\leq\mu_{n}\left(C\right)+\mu_{1}\left(D\right).
Hence, we see that
\mu_{2}\left(A\right)\leq\mu_{1}\left(B\right)+\mu_{2}\left(A-B\right)=\mu_{1}\left(B\right)+\mu_{2}\left(\rho E_{n}\right)=\mu_{1}\left(B\right),
and thus,
\mu_{2}\left(A\right)\leq\mu_{1}\left(B\right)\leq\sigma_{1}\left(B\right).
(9)
Similarly,
\mu_{1}\left(-B\right)+\mu_{n}\left(A\right)\geq\mu_{n}\left(A-B\right)=\mu_{n}\left(\rho E_{n}\right)=0.
and thus,
\sigma_{1}\left(B\right)\geq\mu_{1}\left(-B\right)\geq-\mu_{n}\left(A\right).
This, together with (9), implies (8).
Let now \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{n} be a unit vector such that \left|\left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle\right|=\sigma_{1}\left(B\right). Applying Lemma 3 with \varepsilon=1/3, we can find a
vector \mathbf{y}=\left(y_{i}\right)_{1}^{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
satisfying
\left\|\mathbf{x-y}\right\|\leq 1/3,
such that y_{i} take m distinct values \alpha_{1}<...<\alpha_{m}, where
m\leq\left\lceil\frac{8\pi}{1/3}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{4}{1/3}\log\frac{4n}{1/3}\right\rceil.
For every i\in\left[m\right], let
P_{i}=\left\{j:y_{j}=\alpha_{i}\right\};
clearly, \left[n\right]=P_{1}\cup...\cup P_{m} is a proper partition.
We shall prove that
\sigma_{1}\left(B\right)\leq\frac{9}{2}\left|\left\langle B\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle\right|.
(10)
Indeed, we have
\displaystyle\left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle-\left\langle B\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle
\displaystyle=\left\langle B\left(\mathbf{y-x}\right),\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\overline{\left\langle\mathbf{y,B\left(x\mathbf{-y}\right)}\right\rangle}
\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|+\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|\right)\left\|B\left(\mathbf{y-x}\right)\right\|
\displaystyle\leq\left(2+\frac{1}{3}\right)\sigma_{1}\left(B\right)\left\|\mathbf{x-y}\right\|\leq\frac{7}{9}\sigma_{1}\left(B\right).
Hence if \left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle=\sigma_{1}\left(B\right) then (10) holds. Also, we have
\displaystyle\left\langle B\left(\mathbf{y-x}\right),\mathbf{x}\right\rangle+\overline{\left\langle\mathbf{y,B\left(x\mathbf{-y}\right)}\right\rangle}
\displaystyle\geq-\left(\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|+\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|\right)\left\|B\left(\mathbf{y-x}\right)\right\|\geq
\displaystyle-\left(2+\frac{1}{3}\right)\sigma_{1}\left(B\right)\left\|\mathbf{x-y}\right\|
\displaystyle\geq-\frac{7}{9}\sigma_{1}\left(B\right),
and thus, (10) holds also if \left\langle B\mathbf{x,x}\right\rangle=-\sigma_{1}\left(B\right).
Define the Hermitian matrix C=\left(c_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{m} by
c_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|P_{i}\right|\left|P_{j}\right|}}\sum_{r\in P_{i}}\sum_{s\in P_{j}}b_{rs}.
Applying Lemma 4 to the partition \left[n\right]=P_{1}\cup...\cup P_{m} and the vector \mathbf{y} we find that
\left|\left\langle B\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle\right|\leq\sigma_{1}\left(C\right).
Hence, in view of (8) and (10), we see that
\sigma_{2}\left(A\right)\leq\sigma_{1}\left(B\right)\leq\frac{9}{2}\sigma_{1}\left(C\right).
Observe that,
\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\left(C\right)
\displaystyle=\max\left(\left|\mu_{1}\left(C\right)\right|,\left|\mu_{n}\left(C\right)\right|\right)\leq m\max_{i,j\in\left[m\right]}\left|c_{ij}\right|
\displaystyle\leq\left\lceil\frac{8\pi}{1/3}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{4}{1/3}\log\frac{4n}{1/3}\right\rceil\max_{i,j\in\left[m\right]}\left|c_{ij}\right|.
Since,
\max_{i,j\in\left[m\right]}\left|c_{ij}\right|\leq disc\left(A\right),
we obtain
\sigma_{2}\left(A\right)\leq\frac{9}{2}\left\lceil\frac{8\pi}{1/3}\right\rceil\left\lceil\frac{4}{1/3}\log\frac{4n}{1/3}\right\rceil disc\left(A\right),
(11)
and the proof is completed.
\Box
In the arguments above we made no attempt to optimize the constant in Theorem
2. As the right-hand side of (11) is bounded above by
\left(4104\log n+10260\right)disc\left(A\right),
we can take C to be 18906.
2.2 Tightness of the upper bound on \sigma_{2}
For n=2k\geq 2, let A^{\prime}=\left(a_{ij}^{\prime}\right)_{i,j=1}^{k}
be defined by
a_{ij}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}},
and let A=\left(a_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n} be the block matrix
A=\left(\begin{array}[c]{ll}E_{k}+A^{\prime}&E_{k}-A^{\prime}\\
E_{k}-A^{\prime}&E_{k}+A^{\prime}\end{array}\right).
Clearly, A is nonnegative and symmetric. As we shall see the matrix A
shows that Theorem 2 is best possible up to a multiplicative constant.
Theorem 5
For the matrix A defined above we have
\mu_{2}\left(A\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}disc\left(A\right)\log n.
(12)
Proof In fact, we shall show that \mu_{2}\left(A\right) and disc\left(A\right) satisfy
\mu_{2}\left(A\right)\geq 2\log n
and
disc\left(A\right)<4.
(13)
Indeed, the sum of every row of A is exactly n, and, since A is
nonnegative, it follows that \mu_{1}\left(A\right)=n. Note that the
vector \mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} of all ones is an eigenvector of A to
\mu_{1}\left(A\right). By the Rayleigh principle
\mu_{2}\left(A\right)=\max_{\mathbf{y\bot j,y\neq 0}}\frac{\left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}},
so our goal is to find a nonzero \mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} such that
\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{j,} and the ratio \left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle/\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2} is sufficiently large.
Define the vector \mathbf{y}=\left(y_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n} by
y_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}1/\sqrt{i}&\text{if}&i\leq k\\
-1/\sqrt{i-k}&\text{if}&i>k.\end{array}\right..
From
\sum_{i=1}^{2k}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i-k}}=0
we see that \mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{j.} Setting
\xi_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i},
we deduce
\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i-k}}=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i}=2\xi_{k}.
Next, we shall compute \left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle. Recall
that
a_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cccc}1+1/\sqrt{ij}&\text{if}&i\leq k,&j\leq k\\
1-1/\sqrt{ij}&\text{if}&i\leq k,&j>k\\
1-1/\sqrt{ij}&\text{if}&i>k,&j\leq k\\
1+1/\sqrt{ij}&\text{if}&i>k&j>k\end{array}\right..
Thus we have
\displaystyle\left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=1}^{2k}a_{ij}y_{i}y_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a_{ij}}{\sqrt{ij}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k}\frac{a_{ij}}{\sqrt{\left(i-k\right)\left(j-k\right)}}
\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k}\frac{a_{ij}}{\sqrt{i\left(j-k\right)}}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a_{ij}}{\sqrt{\left(i-k\right)j}}
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\right)
\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\right)
\displaystyle=4\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}=4\left(\xi_{k}\right)^{2}.
Hence,
\mu_{2}\left(A\right)\geq\frac{\left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}}\geq 2\xi_{k}>2\log n.
Let us now turn to our proof of (13). Since the sum of every row of A
is exactly n, we have \rho^{\prime}\left(A\right)=1.
Assume X_{0},Y_{0}\subset\left[n\right] are nonempty sets, maximizing
the right-hand side of (3), i.e. satisfying
disc\left(A\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X_{0}}\sum_{j\in Y_{0}}\left(a_{ij}-1\right)\right|.
(14)
Set
\displaystyle X_{1}
\displaystyle=X_{0}\cap\left[k\right],\text{ }X_{2}=X_{0}\cap\left[k+1,n\right],
\displaystyle Y_{1}
\displaystyle=Y_{0}\cap\left[k\right],\text{ }Y_{2}=Y_{0}\cap\left[k+1,n\right].
Then the right-hand side of (14) is equal to
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X_{1}}\sum_{j\in Y_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}+\sum_{i\in X_{2}}\sum_{j\in Y_{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}-\sum_{i\in X_{1}}\sum_{j\in Y_{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}-\sum_{i\in X_{2}}\sum_{j\in Y_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\right|
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\left(\sum_{i\in X_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}-\sum_{i\in X_{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\left(\sum_{i\in Y_{1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}-\sum_{i\in Y_{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\right|
Since disc\left(A\right) is maximal, one of X_{1},X_{2} is empty, and
one of Y_{1},Y_{2} is empty. By symmetry we can assume that X_{2}=\varnothing, Y_{2}=\varnothing. Then the matrix A\left[X_{0},Y_{0}\right]=A\left[X_{1},Y_{1}\right] is in the upper-left-hand
corner of A and
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X_{0}}\sum_{j\in Y_{0}}\left(a_{ij}-1\right)\right|
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\left|X_{0}\right|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\left|Y_{0}\right|}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i}}\right)
\displaystyle<\frac{4\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}\sqrt{\left|Y_{0}\right|}}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}=4.
\Box
It is not impossible that the constant 4 appearing in (13) is fairly
close to be the best possible.
3 A class of dense regular graphs
Our goal in this section is to construct infinitely many regular graphs G
such that
\mu_{2}\left(G\right)\geq Cdisc_{2}\left(G\right)\log\left(\left|G\right|\right)
for some absolute constant C>0. In fact, for every sufficiently large prime
p and k=\left\lceil p^{1/5}\right\rceil we shall construct a matrix
\mathcal{A} such that:
(a) \mathcal{A} is a square, symmetric, \left(0,1\right)-matrix
of size 2kp with zero main diagonal;
(b) all row sums of \mathcal{A} are equal to kp;
(c) \mu_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right) satisfies
\mu_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}p\log k;
(d) disc\left(\mathcal{A}\right) satisfies
disc\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\leq 12p.
The matrix \mathcal{A} will be constructed as a block matrix of 4k^{2}
blocks, each block being a square matrix of size p.
We shall select a symmetric matrix of integers that is roughly proportional to
the matrix A of section 2.2, and then we shall replace each entry
of that matrix by a p\times p, symmetric, \left(0,1\right)-matrix of
low discrepancy and density equal to the value of the corresponding entry.
Before describing the blocks of \mathcal{A}, we shall consider a corollary
of a theorem of Thomason.
3.1 A theorem of Thomason
Thomason ([13], Theorem 2) proved a widely-applicable result about
bipartite graphs with vertex classes of equal size; for convenience, we shall
restate his theorem in matrix form.
Theorem 6
Let 0<p<1, \mu\geq 0, and A be a square \left(0,1\right)-matrix of
size n. If each row of A has at least pn ones, and the inner product of
every two distinct rows is at most p^{2}n+\mu, then for every X,Y\subset\left[n\right],
\left|\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{j\in Y}\left(a_{ij}-p\right)\right|\leq\varepsilon\left|Y\right|+\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\left(pn+\mu\left|X\right|\right)},
where \varepsilon=1 if p\left|X\right|<1 and \varepsilon=0 otherwise.
Applying this theorem to the adjacency matrix of a graph G, we obtain
immediately the following generalization of Theorem 1 in [13].
Theorem 7
Let 0<p<1, \mu\geq 0, and G be a graph of order n. If
d\left(u\right)\geq pn for every u\in V\left(G\right), and
\left|\Gamma\left(u\right)\cap\Gamma\left(v\right)\right|\leq p^{2}n+\mu
for every two distinct u,v\in V\left(G\right), then for every
X,Y\subset V\left(G\right),
\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-p\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\right|\leq\varepsilon\left|Y\right|+\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\left(pn+\mu\left|X\right|\right)},
where \varepsilon=1 if p\left|X\right|<1 and \varepsilon=0 otherwise.
Next we shall describe a family of symmetric \left(0,1\right)-matrices
of size p that we shall use as blocks of \mathcal{A}.
3.2 The blocks of \mathcal{A}
Let p be a sufficiently large prime, \mathbb{Z}_{p} be the field of order
p, and t\in\left[p\right]. Let Q\left(p,t\right) be the graph
whose vertex set is \left[p\right], and two distinct u,v\in\left[p\right] are joined if
\left\{\frac{\left(u-v\right)^{2}}{p}\right\}\leq\frac{t}{p},
where \left\{x\right\} is the fractional part of x. The graphs
Q\left(p,t\right) were introduced by Bollobás and Erdős in
[3], as examples of pseudo-random graphs. The following lemma summarizes
the properties of Q\left(p,t\right) that we shall be interested in.
Lemma 8
The graph Q\left(p,t\right) is a regular graph of order p
such that
(i) the degree d of Q\left(p,t\right) satisfies
\left|d-t\right|\leq\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2};
(ii) the adjacency matrix A of Q\left(p,t\right) satisfies
disc\left(A\right)<2p^{3/4}\log p.
Proof Since Q\left(p,t\right) is invariant under the cyclic shift
z\rightarrow z+1 \operatorname{mod} p, it is clear that Q\left(p,t\right) is regular. In fact, (i) follows from a much stronger result of
Burgess [4].
To prove (ii) we shall first recall that Theorem 3.16 in [2] states
that, for any two vertices u,v of Q\left(p,t\right), we have
\left|\left|\Gamma\left(u\right)\cap\Gamma\left(v\right)\right|-\frac{t^{2}}{p}\right|<\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2},
(15)
Setting \beta=d/p, from (i) and (15), for every two vertices u,v
of Q\left(p,t\right), we obtain
\displaystyle\left|\Gamma\left(u\right)\cap\Gamma\left(v\right)\right|
\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{p}+\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\left(\beta p+\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}\right)^{2}}{p}+\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}
\displaystyle=\beta^{2}p+2\beta\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}+\left(\log p\right)^{4}+\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}
\displaystyle<\beta^{2}p+3\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}+\left(\log p\right)^{4}.
Suppose that X,Y\subset\left[p\right] are nonempty sets. Assuming
\left|X\right|\leq\left|Y\right|, by Theorem 7,
we obtain
\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-\beta\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\right|\leq\left|X\right|+\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}\sqrt{\beta p+\left(3\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}+\left(\log p\right)^{4}\right)\left|Y\right|}.
Hence, noting that \left|Y\right|\leq p and \beta<1, we find
that
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-\beta\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\right|
\displaystyle\leq 1+\sqrt{\beta p+\left(3\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}+\left(\log p\right)^{4}\right)p}
\displaystyle<2p^{3/4}\log p.
Let A=\left(a_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{p}. Since, for every X,Y\subset\left[p\right], we have
\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{j\in Y}a_{ij}=e\left(X,Y\right),
and \rho^{\prime}\left(A\right)=\beta, we deduce
disc\left(A\right)<2p^{3/4}\log p,
as claimed.
\Box
Let \mathcal{V}_{p} be the set of the degrees of the graphs Q\left(p,t\right) for t\in\left[p\right]. From Lemma 8, (i), we see
that for every s\in\left[p\right] there is a d\in\mathcal{V}_{p}, such
that there exists a d-regular graph H\left(p,d\right) with
\left|d-s\right|\leq\sqrt{p}\log^{2}p,
and
disc_{2}\left(H\left(p,d\right)\right)<2p^{3/4}\log p.
Now, for every d\in\mathcal{V}_{p}, let A\left(p,d\right) be the
adjacency matrix of H\left(p,d\right). The properties of the matrices
\left\{A\left(p,d\right):d\in\mathcal{V}_{p}\right\} are summarized
in the following lemma.
Lemma 9
For every integer s\in\left[p\right], there exist
d\in\mathcal{V}_{p} and a matrix A\left(p,d\right), such that
(i) \left|d-s\right|<\sqrt{p}\log^{2}p;
(ii) A\left(p,d\right) is a symmetric \left(0,1\right)-matrix of size p with zero main diagonal;
(iii) all row sums of A\left(p,d\right) are equal to d;
(iv) the function disc\left(A\left(p,d\right)\right)
satisfies
disc\left(A\left(p,d\right)\right)<2p^{3/4}\log p.
If A is a square \left(0,1\right)-matrix of size n, we call the
matrix
\overline{A}=E_{n}-A
the complement of A. Observe that if A is a square \left(0,1\right)-matrix then
\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\left(\overline{A}\right)
\displaystyle=1-\rho^{\prime}\left(A\right),
\displaystyle disc\left(\overline{A}\right)
\displaystyle=disc\left(A\right).
Hence, the complement of any matrix A\left(p,d\right) satisfies
disc\left(\overline{A\left(p,d\right)}\right)<2p^{3/4}\log p.
The matrices \left\{A\left(p,d\right):d\in\mathcal{V}_{p}\right\}
together with their complements will be used as blocks of the matrix
\mathcal{A}.
3.3 The construction of \mathcal{A}
For every s\in\left[2k\right], set
I_{s}=\left\{i:\left(s-1\right)p\leq i<sp\right\}.
Define the matrix D=\left(d_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{k} by
d_{ij}=q,\text{ }q\in\mathcal{V}_{p}\text{, }\left|q-\left(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{p}{2\sqrt{ij}}\right)\right|=\min_{x\in\mathcal{V}_{p}}\left|x-\left(\frac{p}{2}+\frac{p}{2\sqrt{ij}}\right)\right|.
From Lemma 8, (i), we see that
\left|2d_{ij}-\left(p+\frac{p}{\sqrt{ij}}\right)\right|\leq 2\sqrt{p}\log^{2}p.
(16)
The matrix D will be the cornerstone of our construction. Note that D is
symmetric and the values of its entries belong to the set \mathcal{V}_{p}.
Now, let us define \mathcal{A}^{\prime} as a block matrix by
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccc}A\left(p,d_{11}\right)&A\left(p,d_{12}\right)&.&A\left(p,d_{1k}\right)\\
A\left(p,d_{12}\right)&A\left(p,d_{22}\right)&.&.\\
.&.&.&.\\
A\left(p,d_{1k}\right)&.&.&A\left(p,d_{kk}\right)\end{array}\right),
(17)
and set
\mathcal{A}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}&E_{kp}-\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\\
E_{kp}-\mathcal{A}^{\prime}&\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\end{array}\right).
(18)
By our construction \mathcal{A} is a symmetric \left(0,1\right)-matrix
of size 2pk, and its main diagonal is zero, so \mathcal{A} satisfies (a).
Also, we see that every row sum of \mathcal{A} is exactly kp, so
\mathcal{A} satisfies (b) as well. In the following two theorems we shall
prove that \mathcal{A} satisfies also (c) and (d).
For the sake of convenience, set \mathcal{A}=\left(a_{ij}\right)_{i,i=1}^{2pk} and \mathcal{A}_{ij}=\mathcal{A}\left[I_{i},I_{j}\right]
for i,j\in\left[2k\right]. Observe that the row sums of any matrix
\mathcal{A}_{ij} are equal, and from Lemma 9 and what follows, we
have
disc\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)\leq 2p^{3/4}\log p.
(19)
Theorem 10
The second eigenvalue \mu_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right) of
the matrix \mathcal{A} defined by (18) satisfies
\mu_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}p\log k.
Proof Indeed, from (18) we see that the sum of every row of \mathcal{A} is
exactly kp. Since \mathcal{A} is nonnegative, it follows that \mu_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=pk and the vector \mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{2pk} of all ones is an eigenvector of \mathcal{A} to \mu_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}\right). By the Rayleigh principle
\mu_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=\max_{\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{j,y\neq 0}}\frac{\left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}},
so our goal is to find a nonzero \mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2pk} such that
\mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{j} and the ratio \left\langle A\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle/\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2} is sufficiently large.
Define the vector \mathbf{y}=\left(y_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n} by
y_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{lll}1/\sqrt{s}&\text{if}&i\in I_{s},\text{ }s\leq k\\
-1/\sqrt{s-k}&\text{if}&i\in I_{s},\text{ }s>k.\end{array}\right..
From
\sum_{i=1}^{2pk}y_{i}=\sum_{s=1}^{2k}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}-\sum_{s=k+1}^{2k}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s-k}}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\frac{p}{\sqrt{s}}-\sum_{s=1}^{k}\frac{p}{\sqrt{s}}=0
we see that \mathbf{y}\bot\mathbf{j.} Also, for \left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2} we have
\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\frac{1}{s}+\sum_{s=k+1}^{2k}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\frac{1}{s-k}=2\sum_{s=1}^{k}\sum_{i\in I_{s}}\frac{1}{s}=2p\sum_{s=1}^{k}\frac{1}{s}=2p\xi_{k}.
On the other hand, for \left\langle\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle we
see that
\left\langle\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{2pk}\sum_{j=1}^{2pk}a_{ij}y_{i}y_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=1}^{2k}\sum_{s\in I_{i}}\sum_{t\in I_{j}}a_{st}y_{s}y_{t}.
By (17) and (18), we have
\sum_{s\in I_{i}}\sum_{t\in I_{j}}a_{st}=\left\{\begin{array}[c]{llll}pd_{ij}&\text{if}&i\leq k&j\leq k\\
p\left(p-d_{i\left(j-k\right)}\right)&\text{if}&i\leq k&j>k\\
p\left(p-d_{\left(i-k\right)j}\right)&\text{if}&i>k&j\leq k\\
pd_{\left(i-k\right)\left(j-k\right)}&\text{if}&i>k&j>k\end{array}\right..
Hence, by the choice of \mathbf{y},
\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle
\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{pd_{ij}}{\sqrt{ij}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k}\frac{pd_{\left(i-k\right)\left(j-k\right)}}{\sqrt{\left(i-k\right)\left(j-k\right)}}
\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{2k}\frac{p\left(p-d_{i\left(j-k\right)}\right)}{\sqrt{i\left(j-k\right)}}-\sum_{i=k+1}^{2k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{p\left(p-d_{\left(i-k\right)j}\right)}{\sqrt{\left(i-k\right)j}}
\displaystyle=2p\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{d_{ij}}{\sqrt{ij}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{p-d_{ij}}{\sqrt{ij}}\right)=2p\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{2d_{ij}-p}{\sqrt{ij}}\right).
From (16), we have
\frac{2d_{ij}-p}{\sqrt{ij}}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}\left(\frac{p}{\sqrt{ij}}-2\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}\right)=\frac{p}{ij}-\frac{2\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{ij}},
and so,
\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle
\displaystyle>2p^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{ij}\right)-4p\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{ij}}
\displaystyle>2p^{2}\left(\xi_{k}\right)^{2}-16kp\sqrt{p}\left(\log p\right)^{2}.
Hence, as k\leq p^{1/5} and p is large, \left\langle\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle>p^{2}\left(\xi_{k}\right)^{2}, and thus,
\mu_{2}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\geq\frac{\left\langle\mathcal{A}\mathbf{y,y}\right\rangle}{\left\|\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}p\xi_{k}>\frac{1}{2}p\log k
as claimed.
\Box
Theorem 11
If p is large, disc\left(\mathcal{A}\right) of the
matrix \mathcal{A} defined by (18) satisfies
disc\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\leq 12p.
Proof Since all row sums of \mathcal{A} are exactly pk, we deduce \rho^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=1/2.
As before, assume X_{0},Y_{0}\subset\left[2kp\right] are nonempty sets,
maximizing the right-hand side of (3), i.e. satisfying
disc\left(\mathcal{A}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X_{0}}\sum_{j\in Y_{0}}\left(a_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|.
(20)
Set
J_{1}=\left[kp\right],\text{ }J_{2}=\left[kp+1,2kp\right]
and let
X_{i}=X_{0}\cap J_{i},\text{ }Y_{i}=Y_{0}\cap J_{i},\text{ }i=1,2.
For i,j=1,2 consider the value
\Delta_{ij}=\max_{X\subset J_{i},Y\subset J_{j},X\neq\varnothing,Y\neq\varnothing}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\left|\sum_{i\in X}\sum_{j\in Y}\left(a_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|
By (18), we have
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left[J_{1},J_{1}\right]
\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}\left[J_{2},J_{2}\right],
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\left[J_{1},J_{2}\right]
\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}\left[J_{2},J_{1}\right]=E_{kn}-\mathcal{A}\left[J_{1},J_{1}\right],
and hence,
\Delta_{11}=\Delta_{12}=\Delta_{21}=\Delta_{22}.
Consequently
\displaystyle disc\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{s\in X_{i}}\sum_{t\in Y_{j}}\left(a_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|
\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Delta_{ij}\sqrt{\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|}
\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{11}}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left(\sqrt{\left|X_{1}\right|}+\sqrt{\left|X_{2}\right|}\right)\left(\sqrt{\left|Y_{1}\right|}+\sqrt{\left|Y_{2}\right|}\right)
\displaystyle\leq 2\Delta_{11}\frac{\sqrt{\left(\left|X_{1}\right|+\left|X_{2}\right|\right)\left(\left|Y_{1}\right|+\left|Y_{2}\right|\right)}}{\sqrt{\left(\left|X_{1}\right|+\left|X_{2}\right|\right)\left(\left|Y_{1}\right|+\left|Y_{2}\right|\right)}}=2\Delta_{11}.
To complete our proof we shall show that
\Delta_{11}<6p.
Fix some nonempty sets X_{0},Y_{0}\subset\left[kp\right] such that
\Delta_{11}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{s\in X_{i}}\sum_{t\in Y_{j}}\left(a_{st}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|,
(21)
and for every i\in\left[k\right], set
X_{i}=X_{0}\cap I_{i},\text{ }Y_{i}=Y_{0}\cap I_{i}.
Observe that for i,j\in\left[k\right] we have
\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{d_{ij}}{p}-\frac{1}{2},
hence, by (16),
\left|\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\right|<\frac{1}{2\sqrt{ij}}+\frac{2\left(\log p\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{p}},
and so,
\displaystyle\left|\sum_{s\in X_{i}}\sum_{t\in Y_{j}}\left(a_{st}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|
\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{s\in X_{i}}\sum_{t\in Y_{j}}\left(a_{st}-\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)\right)\right|+\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|\left|\rho^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\right|
\displaystyle\leq disc\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)\sqrt{\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|}+\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{ij}}+\frac{2\left(\log p\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{p}}\right).
Recalling (21), we see that
\displaystyle\Delta_{11}
\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|\sum_{s\in X_{i}}\sum_{t\in Y_{j}}\left(a_{st}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|
\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}disc\left(\mathcal{A}_{ij}\right)\sqrt{\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|}
(22)
\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|}{\sqrt{ij}}
(23)
\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2\left(\log p\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{p}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|
(24)
\displaystyle=A+B+C.
We shall estimate the terms (22), (23) and (24) separately.
From (19) we obtain
A\leq\frac{2p^{3/4}\log p}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|}.
Hence, by
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{\left|X_{i}\right|\left|Y_{j}\right|}
\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sqrt{\left|X_{i}\right|}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sqrt{\left|Y_{j}\right|}\right)
\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{k}\sqrt{k}=k,
we have
A\leq 2kp^{3/4}\log p\leq p.
(25)
Next we turn to (23). Obviously,
B=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left|X_{i}\right|}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left|Y_{i}\right|}{\sqrt{i}}\right).
(26)
We shall show that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left|X_{i}\right|}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\leq 2\sqrt{2p}.
(27)
Indeed, set
s=\left\lfloor\frac{\left|X_{0}\right|}{p}\right\rfloor,
and observe that the left-hand side of (27) attains its maximum when
\displaystyle\left|X_{i}\right|
\displaystyle=p,\text{ \ \ \ }1\leq i\leq s,
\displaystyle\left|X_{s+1}\right|
\displaystyle=\left|X_{0}\right|-ps,
\displaystyle\left|X_{i}\right|
\displaystyle=0,\text{ \ \ \ }s+1<i\leq 2k.
Obviously (27) holds if s=0, so we shall assume s\geq 1. Then we
have,
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left|X_{i}\right|}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}\frac{p}{\sqrt{i}}\leq\frac{2p\sqrt{s+1}}{\sqrt{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\leq\sqrt{2p\frac{sp}{\left|X_{0}\right|}}\leq 2\sqrt{2p}
and (27) follows.
Similarly, we see that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|Y_{0}\right|}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left|Y_{i}\right|}{\sqrt{i}}\right)\leq 2\sqrt{2p}
and hence, in view of (26), we find
B\leq 4p.
(28)
Finally,
C=\left(\frac{2\left(\log p\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{p}}\right)\frac{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}{\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}}\leq\left(\frac{2\left(\log p\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{p}}\right)\sqrt{kp}<p.
(29)
Now, replacing (22), (23), (24) by (25), (28),
(29), we obtain
\Delta_{11}<6p,
and the proof is completed.
\Box
3.4 A conjecture of Chung
In [8] Chung studies a version of the Laplacian matrix a graph G that
she denotes by \mathcal{L}\left(G\right). If G is d-regular of order
n the matrix \mathcal{L}\left(G\right) is given by
\mathcal{L}\left(G\right)=I_{n}-\frac{1}{d}A,
(30)
where A is the adjacency matrix of G\left(n\right). Following Chung’s
notation, the eigenvalues of \mathcal{L}\left(G\right) are \lambda_{0}\leq...\leq\lambda_{n-1}, with \lambda_{0}=0.
Set \overline{\lambda}=\max_{i\neq 0}\left|1-\lambda_{i}\right|, and for
every X\subset V\left(G\right), let vol X=\sum_{v\in X}d\left(v\right). Chung asked the following question.
Let G be a nonempty graph and \alpha>0 is such that if X,Y\subset V=V\left(G\right) then
\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-\frac{vol\text{ }X\text{ }vol\text{ }Y}{vol\text{ }V}\right|\leq\alpha\frac{\sqrt{vol\text{ }X\text{
}vol\text{ }Y\text{ }vol\text{ }\left(V\backslash X\right)\text{
}vol\text{ }\left(V\backslash Y\right)}}{vol\text{ }V}.
(31)
Is there an absolute constant C such that \overline{\lambda}\leq C\alpha?
We shall check that the graph G_{p}, whose adjacency matrix \mathcal{A}_{p}=\mathcal{A} we constructed in the previous section, answers this
question in the negative. Indeed, recall that G_{p} is kp-regular graph of
order n=2kp. Theorem 11 implies that
\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-\frac{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|}{2}\right|\leq\frac{C}{k}\sqrt{\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\left(n-\left|X\right|\right)\left(n-\left|Y\right|\right)}
for some absolute constant C>0, so (31) holds with \alpha=C/k. By
(30) and Theorem 10, we see that
\overline{\lambda}\geq\left|1-\lambda_{1}\right|\geq 1-\left(1-\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)=\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\geq\frac{p\log k}{2kp}=\frac{\log k}{2k}
and \overline{\lambda} is greater than any fixed multiple of \alpha.
4 Sparse graphs with low discrepancy and high second
eigenvalue
In [5] Chung and Graham extend quasi-random properties to sparse
graphs, i.e., graphs G\left(n,m\right) with m=o\left(n^{2}\right).
Their approach is based on the following. Fix a function p=p\left(n\right) with 0<p<1 and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}pn=\infty.
Let \mathcal{G}_{p} be an infinite family of graphs \left\{G\left(n\right):n\rightarrow\infty\right\} such that, for every G\left(n\right)\in\mathcal{G}_{p},
e\left(G\left(n\right)\right)=\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)p\binom{n}{2}.
(32)
Chung and Graham investigated a number of properties that a family
\mathcal{G}_{p} can have; we shall be concerned with the following two here
([5], p. 220):
DISC(1): For every G\left(n\right)\in\mathcal{G}_{p}, and for
all X,Y\subset V\left(G\right),
\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-p\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\right|=o\left(pn^{2}\right).
EIG: For every G\left(n\right)\in\mathcal{G}_{p},
\mu_{1}\left(G\right)=\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)pn,\text{ and
}\sigma_{2}\left(G\right)=o\left(pn\right).
Chung and Graham proved that EIG implies DISC(1) (Theorem 1
in [5]), and asked the following natural question ([5], p. 230).
Question Does DISC(1) imply EIG?
Recently Krivelevich and Sudakov ([10], p. 9,) constructed an example
that answers this question in the negative. To conclude the paper we give a
general construction that we believe sheds more light on the relationship
between DISC(1) and EIG.
Proposition 12
For p=p\left(n\right)=o\left(1\right) let \mathcal{G}_{p} be a
family of graphs having the property EIG. Let \mathcal{G}_{p}^{\ast} be the family of the graphs that can be represented as disjoint unions
G\left(n\right)\cup K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor},
where G\left(n\right)\in\mathcal{G}_{p}. Then \mathcal{G}_{p}^{\ast}
has DISC(1) but does not have EIG..
Proof Note that
e\left(G\left(n\right)\cup K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}\right)=\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)p\binom{n}{2}+\binom{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}{2}=\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)p\binom{n+\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}{2},
so \mathcal{G}_{p}^{\ast} is defined according to (32). Also, given
G^{\prime}=G\left(n\right)\cup K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor},
Z=V\left(K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}\right) and X,Y\subset V\left(G^{\prime}\right), we have
\displaystyle\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-p\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|\right|
\displaystyle\leq\left|e\left(X\backslash Z,Y\backslash Z\right)-p\left|X\backslash Z\right|\left|Y\backslash Z\right|\right|
\displaystyle+\left|e\left(X,Y\right)-e\left(X\backslash Z,Y\backslash Z\right)\right|+\left|p\left|X\right|\left|Y\right|-p\left|X\backslash Z\right|\left|Y\backslash Z\right|\right|
\displaystyle\leq o\left(pn^{2}\right)+2e\left(Z\right)+p\left|Z\right|\left(\left|X\right|+\left|Y\right|\right)
\displaystyle\leq o\left(pn^{2}\right)+p^{2}n^{2}+2p^{2}n^{2}=o\left(pn^{2}\right).
Thus, \mathcal{G}_{p}^{\ast} has DISC(1). However, since
G^{\prime} is a union of the disjoint graphs G\left(n\right) and
K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}, we find that
\displaystyle\min\left\{\mu_{1}\left(G\left(n\right)\right),\mu_{1}\left(K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}\right)\right\}
\displaystyle\leq\mu_{2}\left(G^{\prime}\right)\leq\mu_{1}\left(G^{\prime}\left(n\right)\right)
\displaystyle=\max\left\{\mu_{1}\left(G\left(n\right)\right),\mu_{1}\left(K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}\right)\right\}.
Hence, from \mu_{1}\left(G\left(n\right)\right)=\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)pn and \mu_{1}\left(K_{\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor}\right)=\left\lfloor pn\right\rfloor-1, we see that
\mu_{2}\left(G^{\prime}\right)=\left(1+o\left(1\right)\right)pn,
and so, \mathcal{G}_{p}^{\ast} does not have EIG.
\Box
