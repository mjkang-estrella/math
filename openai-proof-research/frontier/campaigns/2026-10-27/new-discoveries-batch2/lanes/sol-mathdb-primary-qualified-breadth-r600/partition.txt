Abstract
We consider the two block stochastic block model on nn nodes with asymptotically equal cluster sizes. The connection probabilities within and between cluster are denoted by pn:=annp_{n}:=\frac{a_{n}}{n} and qn:=bnnq_{n}:=\frac{b_{n}}{n} respectively.
Mossel et al.[25] considered the case when an=aa_{n}=a and bn=bb_{n}=b are fixed. They proved the probability models of the stochastic block model and that of Erdös-Rényi graph with same average degree are mutually contiguous whenever (a−b)2<2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}<2(a+b) and are asymptotically singular whenever (a−b)2>2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}>2(a+b). Mossel et al. [25] also proved that when (a−b)2<2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}<2(a+b) no algorithm is able to find an estimate of the labeling of the nodes which is positively correlated with the true labeling.
It is natural to ask what happens when ana_{n} and bnb_{n} both grow to infinity. We prove that their results extend to the case when an=o⁡(n)a_{n}=o(n) and bn=o⁡(n)b_{n}=o(n). We also consider the case when ann→p∈(0,1)\frac{a_{n}}{n}\to p\in(0,1) and (an−bn)2=Θ⁡(an+bn)(a_{n}-b_{n})^{2}=\Theta(a_{n}+b_{n}). Observe that in this case bnn→p\frac{b_{n}}{n}\to p also. We show that here the models are mutually contiguous if (an−bn)2<2​(1−p)​(an+bn)(a_{n}-b_{n})^{2}<2(1-p)(a_{n}+b_{n}) and they are asymptotically singular if (an−bn)2>2​(1−p)​(an+bn)(a_{n}-b_{n})^{2}>2(1-p)(a_{n}+b_{n}). Further we also prove it is impossible find an estimate of the labeling of the nodes which is positively correlated with the true labeling whenever (an−bn)2<2​(1−p)​(an+bn)(a_{n}-b_{n})^{2}<2(1-p)(a_{n}+b_{n}). The results of this paper justify the negative part of a conjecture made in Decelle et al.(2011) [15] for dense graphs.
1 Introduction
In the last few years the stochastic block model has been one of the most active domains of modern research in statistics, computer science and many other related fields. In general a stochastic block model is a network with a hidden community structure where the nodes within the communities are expected to be connected in a different manner than the nodes between the communities. This model arises naturally in many problems of statistics, machine learning and data mining, but its applications further extends to
from population genetics [28] , where genetically similar
sub-populations are used as the clusters, to image processing [30], [31] , where the group of similar images acts as cluster, to the study of social networks ,
where groups of like-minded people act as clusters [27].
Recently a huge amount of effort has been dedicated to find out the clusters. Numerous different clustering algorithms have been proposed in literature. One might look at [20],[16], [11], [17], [8], [7], [14], [29], [23] for some references.
One of the easiest examples of the stochastic block model is the planted partition model where one have only two clusters of more or less equal size. Formally,
Definition 1.1.
For n∈ℕn\in\mathbb{N}, and p,q∈[0,1]p,q\in[0,1] let 𝒢⁡(n,p,q)\mathcal{G}(n,p,q) denote the model of random,±\pm labelled graphs in which each
vertex uu is assigned (independently and uniformly at random) a label σu∈{±1}\sigma_{u}\in\{\pm 1\} and each edge between uu and vv are included independently with probability pp if they have the same label and with probability qq if they have different labels.
The case when pp and qq are sufficiently close to each other has got significant amount of interest in literature. Decelle et al. [15] made a fascinating conjecture in this regard.
Conjecture 1.1.
Let p=anp=\frac{a}{n} and q=bnq=\frac{b}{n} where aa and bb are fixed real numbers. Then
i) If (a−b)2>2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}>2(a+b) then one can find almost surely a bisection of the vertices which is positively correlated with the original clusters.
ii) If (a−b)2<2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}<2(a+b) then the problem is not solveable.
iii) Further, there are no consistent estimators of aa and bb if (a−b)2<2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}<2(a+b) and there are consistent estimators of aa and bb whenever (a−b)2>2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}>2(a+b).
Coja-Oghlan [13] solved part OPENi)i) of the problem when (a−b)2>C⁡(a+b)(a-b)^{2}>C(a+b) for some large CC and finally
part OPENi​i)ii) and OPENi​i​i)iii) of Conjecture 1.1 was proved by Mossel et al. [25] and part OPENi)i) was solved by Mossel et al. [24] and Massoulié [22] independently.
Typically the problem is much more delicate when more than two communities are present in the sparse case. To keep things simple let us consider the general stochastic block model with kk asymptotically equal sized blocks with connection probabilities within and between blocks are given by an\frac{a}{n} and bn\frac{b}{n} respectively. It was conjectured in Mossel et al [25] that for kk sufficiently large, there is a constant c⁡(k)c(k) such that whenever
c⁡(k)<(a−b)2a+(k−1)​b<kc(k)<\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{a+(k-1)b}<k
the reconstruction problem is solvable in exponential time, it is not solvable if (a−b)2a+(k−1)​b<c⁡(k)\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{a+(k-1)b}<c(k) and solvable in polynomial time if k<(a−b)2a+(k−1)​bk<\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{a+(k-1)b}. The upper bound is known as Kesten-Stigum threshold. Bordenave et al. [9] solved the reconstruction problem above a deterministic threshold by spectral analysis of non-backtraking matrix. One might look at Banks et al. [6] for the non solvability part. They prove that the probability models of stochastic block model and that of Erdös-Rényi graph with same average degree are contiguous and the reconstruction problem is unsolvable if
d<2​log⁡(k−1)k−1​1λ2.d<\frac{2\log(k-1)}{k-1}\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}.
Here d=a+(k−1)​bkd=\frac{a+(k-1)b}{k} and λ=a−bk​d\lambda=\frac{a-b}{kd}.
Abbe et al. [1] provides an efficient algorithm for reconstruction above the Kesten-Stigum threshold. Abbe et al. [1] and Banks et al. [6] also provide cases strictly below the Kesten-Stigum threshold where the problem is solvable in exponential time.
On the other hand, a different type of reconstruction problem was considered in Mossel et al. [26] for denser graphs. They considered two different notions of recovery. The first one is weak consistency where one is interested in finding a bisection σ^\hat{\sigma} such that σ\sigma and σ^\hat{\sigma} have correlation going to 11 with high probability. The second one is called strong consistency. Here one is interested in finding a bisection σ^\hat{\sigma} such that σ^\hat{\sigma} is either σ\sigma or −σ-\sigma with probability tending to 11. Mossel et al. [26] prove that weak recovery is possible if and only if n​(pn−qn)2pn+qn→∞\frac{n(p_{n}-q_{n})^{2}}{p_{n}+q_{n}}\to\infty and strong recovery is possible if and only if
(an+bn−2​an​bn−1)​log⁡n+12​log⁡log⁡n→∞.\left(a_{n}+b_{n}-2\sqrt{a_{n}b_{n}}-1\right)\log n+\frac{1}{2}\log\log n\to\infty.
Here an=n​pnlog⁡na_{n}=\frac{np_{n}}{\log n} and bn=n​qnlog⁡nb_{n}=\frac{nq_{n}}{\log n} respectively. Abbe et al. [2] studied the same problem independently in the logarithmic sparsity
regime. They prove that for a=n​pnlog⁡na=\frac{np_{n}}{\log n} and b=n​qnlog⁡nb=\frac{nq_{n}}{\log n} fixed, (a+b)−2​a​b>1(a+b)-2\sqrt{ab}>1 is sufficient for strong consistency and that (a+b)−2​a​b≥1(a+b)-2\sqrt{ab}\geq 1
is necessary. We note that their results are implied by Mossel et al.[26].
However, according to the best of our knowledge questions similar to part OPENi​i)ii) and OPENi​i​i)iii) of Conjecture 1.1 have not yet been addressed in dense case (i.e. when aa and bb increase to infinity) which is the main focus of this paper.
Before stating our results we mention that the results in Mossel et al. [25] is more general than part OPENi​i​i)iii) of Conjecture 1.1. Let ℙn\mathbb{P}_{n} and ℙn′\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\prime} be the sequence of probability measures induced by 𝒢⁡(n,p,q)\mathcal{G}(n,p,q) and 𝒢⁡(n,p+q2,p+q2)\mathcal{G}(n,\frac{p+q}{2},\frac{p+q}{2}) respectively. Then [25] prove that whenever aa and bb are fixed numbers and (a−b)2<2​(a+b)(a-b)^{2}<2(a+b), the measures ℙn\mathbb{P}_{n} and ℙn′\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\prime} are mutually contiguous i.e. for a sequence of events AnA_{n}, ℙn​(An)→0\mathbb{P}_{n}(A_{n})\to 0 if and only if ℙn′​(An)→0\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\prime}(A_{n})\to 0. Now part OPENi​i​i)iii) of Conjecture 1.1 directly follows from the contiguity. The proof in Mossel et al. [25] is based on calculating the limiting distribution of the short cycles and using a result of contiguity (Theorem 1 in Janson [19] and Theorem 4.1 in Wormald [33]). However, one should note that the result from [25] doesn’t directly generalize to the denser case. Since, one requires the limiting distributions of short cycles to be independent Poisson in order to use Janson’s result. In our proof instead of considering the short cycles we consider the “signed cycles”(to be defined later) which have asymptotic normal distributions. We also find a result analogous to Janson for the normal random variables in order to complete the proof.
On the other hand the original proof of non-reconstruction from Mossel et al. [25] relies on the coupling of ℙn\mathbb{P}_{n} and ℙn′\mathbb{P}_{n}^{\prime} with probability measure induced by Galton Watson trees of suitable parameters. However, it is well known that when the graph is sufficiently dense i.e. an>>no⁡(1)a_{n}>>n^{o(1)} the coupling argument doesn’t work. So our proof is based on fine analysis of some conditional probabilities. Technically, this proof is closely related to the non-reconstruction proof in section 6.2 of Banks et al. [6] rather than the original proof given in Mossel et al. [25].
The paper is organized in the following manner. In Section 2 we build some preliminary notations and state our results. Section 3 is dedicated for building a result analogous to Theorem 1 in Janson [19]. In Section 4 we define signed cycles and find their asymptotic distributions. Section 5 is dedicated to complete the proofs of our contiguity results. In Section 6 we prove the non-reconstruction result. Finally, the paper concludes with an Appendix containing a proof of a result from random matrix theory used in this paper.

Definition 7.1.
(𝒮\mathcal{S} words)
Given a set 𝒮\mathcal{S}, an 𝒮\mathcal{S} letter ss is simply an element of
