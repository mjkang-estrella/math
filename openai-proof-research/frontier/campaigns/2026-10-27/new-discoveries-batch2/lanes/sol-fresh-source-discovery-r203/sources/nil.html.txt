For 𝕜 = ℤ p , its adjoint group can also be generated by ten elements. Keywords : nil ring, Jacobson radical, finitely presented algebra. 1 Introduction We consider three questions concerning associative nil rings and the Jacobson radical. The first one concerns the nilpotency of a nil ring with a finitely generated adjoint group. The second one asks whether a nil algebra is nilpotent provided its Lie algebra is finitely generated. The third question asks whether the Jacobson radical of a finitely presented algebra is necessarily a nil ideal. We construct counterexamples to all three statements under the indicated conditions on the field. All rings and algebras are associative and are not necessarily unital. For a nil ring R , the operation a ∘ b = a + b + a ​ b defines the adjoint group R ∘ . We denote by A ( − ) the Lie algebra on the space A with the bracket [ a , b ] = a ​ b − b ​ a . For a prime p , put ℤ p = ℤ / p ​ ℤ . The structures R ∘ and R ( − ) of a radical ring R are closely related. In 1955, S. A. Jennings proved [ 11 ] that R ∘ is nilpotent if and only if R ( − ) is nilpotent. Question 1.1 (B. Amberg, L. Kazarin, 2007) . Let R be a nil ring such that the group R ∘ is finitely generated. Is R necessarily nilpotent? Question 1.2 (B. Greenfeld, A. Leroy, A. Smoktunowicz, M. Ziembowski, 2015) . Let A be a nil algebra over a field 𝕜 such that the Lie algebra A ( − ) is finitely generated. Is A necessarily nilpotent? The first question was posed for nil algebras by B. Amberg and L. Kazarin [ 3 , Question 9] and stated for nil rings in [ 14 , Question 3] . The nilpotency of radical rings with a two-generated adjoint group was proved in [ 15 ] . B. Greenfeld obtained a similar result for boundedly generated adjoint groups [ 9 ] . Recall that a group G is boundedly generated if G = H 1 ​ … ​ H k for some cyclic subgroups H 1 , … , H k ⊆ G . The second question was posed without restrictions on the characteristic in [ 10 , Question 37] (2015). It was repeated for fields of characteristic different from 2 in [ 14 , Question 4] (2018). A. Alahmadi and H. Alsulami proved for fields of characteristic different from 2 that all positive terms of the derived series of A ( − ) are finitely generated if the associative algebra A is generated by finitely many nilpotent elements [ 1 ] . However, this does not imply that A ( − ) itself is finitely generated. J. P. Bell and B. W. Madill constructed an infinite-dimensional graded-nil algebra of quadratic growth which is finitely generated as a Lie algebra but is not a nil algebra [ 7 , Theorem 6.1] . Theorem 1.3 . For every field 𝕜 , there exists a non-nilpotent nil algebra A over 𝕜 such that the Lie algebra A ( − ) is generated by ten elements. If 𝕜 = ℤ p , the algebra A can be chosen so that its adjoint group A ∘ is also generated by ten elements. Thus, the answers to Questions 1.1 and 1.2 are negative. For the second question, no restriction on the characteristic is required. Over an infinite field, the adjoint group of a nonzero nil algebra is not finitely generated [ 9 ] . A. Smoktunowicz stated the following question in her 2001 survey [ 13 , Question 28] , attributing it to S. A. Amitsur and referring to the 1999 survey of E. R. Puczylowski [ 12 ] . Question 1.4 (S. A. Amitsur, survey of 2001) . Let A be a finitely presented associative algebra over a field 𝕜 . Is its Jacobson radical J ⁡ ( A ) necessarily a nil ideal? Over an uncountable field, the answer is positive. Indeed, if dim A < | 𝕜 | , then J ⁡ ( A ) is a nil ideal [ 4 ] . Theorem 1.5 . Let 𝕜 be a field algebraic over its prime subfield. Then there exists a finitely presented associative algebra B over 𝕜 whose Jacobson radical J ⁡ ( B ) is not a nil ideal. Theorem 1.5 gives a negative answer to Question 1.4 over the indicated fields. The proof of Theorem 1.3 uses the Golod–Shafarevich inequality [ 8 ] , an algebra with a basis consisting of factors of an infinite word [ 7 , 6 ] , and approximation by elements of the adjoint group [ 9 ] . For Theorem 1.5 , we construct a finitely presented algebra A and an idempotent E ∈ A such that E ​ A ​ E ≅ 𝕜 ​ [ t ] ( t ) . Under this isomorphism, the image of t belongs to J ⁡ ( E ​ A ​ E ) ⊆ J ⁡ ( A ) but is not nilpotent.
