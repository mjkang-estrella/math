# Retired biquadratic target

Source: Krásenský–Tinková–Zemková, arXiv:1909.05422v2, final Open questions, printed p.30, frozen text lines 2359–2363. The literal question asks whether an element from Q(sqrt(m)) or Q(sqrt(s)) can decompose in the biquadratic extension, or whether this happens only for Q(sqrt(t)). The word “indecomposable” is supplied by the immediately preceding sentence and the paper's definition in §2.4; without this context every positive integer 2 already decomposes. Convention 2.2 orders the three squarefree radicands 1<m<s<t. There is no restriction to ceiling-root elements. This is an existential question whose negative preservation interpretation quantifies over every such field and every totally positive quadratic indecomposable.

The live arXiv abstract page still identifies v2, revised 26 May 2020, after initial submission 12 September 2019. The source's Theorem 1.3 and Propositions 4.9–4.11 concern M, S, and conditionally 2M−1, not all indecomposables. The earlier Čech–Lachman–Svoboda–Tinková–Zemková paper arXiv:1802.07811 supplies the continued-fraction sufficient conditions restated as Theorem 2.4. Neither that statement nor those selected elements give global preservation.

Decisive later primary body: Siu Hang Man, *Minimal rank of universal lattices and number of indecomposable elements in real multiquadratic fields*, arXiv:2307.04908v2, 6 May 2024; v1 submitted 10 July 2023. Section 4, printed pp.12–17, Theorem 4.3 and Propositions 4.4–4.6; Example 4.7 on p.17. The introduction explicitly says Theorem 1.5 answers the KTZ conjecture. More decisively than that description, Example 4.7 uses p=14,q=91,r=26, so the ordered source radicands are m=14,s=26,t=91. Its element belongs to the **middle**, not largest, subfield:

    26+5√26 = (13+(5/2)√14+√91+(5/2)√26)
              +(13−(5/2)√14−√91+(5/2)√26).

Thus the source existential question has a published affirmative answer for the middle subfield. No construction campaign or all-integral-basis search is warranted. Theorem 4.3 is not falsely translated as preservation of the two numerically smallest subfields: in its types (1),(2) it preserves Kp,Kq with p<r, even when q>r; types (3),(4) take p<q<r and preserve Kp,Kq.

Exact independent verification of the published instance: gcd(14,91)=7, 14·91/7²=26. This is basis B1: 14≡2,91≡3 mod4. The first summand is 13+√91+5(√14+√26)/2, hence integral in that displayed integral basis; the other is integral by subtraction from 26+5√26. `checks.py` isolates each square root between floor(10^8√n)/10^8 and that number plus 10^-8, using isqrt. The four strict lower bounds for the first summand are positive, the smallest exceeding 0.067. The second summand is its Galois conjugate, so is totally positive too.

In Z[√26], any decomposition of α=26+5√26 into positive β=a+b√26 and α−β requires 26>(|b|+|5−b|)√26; since 7√26>26, this forces 0≤b≤5. For each integer j in 0..5, 5j≤j√26<5j+1 (strict on the left for j>0). Strict positivity forces a≥5b+1 and 26−a≥5(5−b)+1. Their sum is ≥27, contradiction. Also α>0 in both embeddings since 26²>25·26. This independently certifies quadratic indecomposability; it agrees with the semiconvergent 1+5(5+√26) for √26=[5;10,10,…]. These controls validate attribution, not a new result.

Proof mechanism already in Man's body: relative conjugate sums produce a positive quadratic E1; its one-sided approximation property bounds the denominator of a competing approximation formed from the off-subfield coefficients. For a true convergent this contradicts optimality; for a semiconvergent Corollary 4.2 forces a multiple of the adjacent opposite convergent. The determinant relation s_(i−1)t_i−s_i t_(i−1)=(−1)^i then contradicts the integral-basis parity alternatives. Propositions 4.4 and 4.5 handle half- and quarter-integral coefficients; Proposition 4.6 imports the old distinguished-q case and swaps the all-1-mod4 radicands. Totally positive quadratic-unit rescaling preserves both indecomposability and decomposability, so does not evade this argument.

This is a same-object public counterexample predating this campaign by over two years. Strongest adaptation objection is decisive: reproducing the witness, the parity proof, a unit orbit, or a smaller numerical control would be known mathematics. No novelty or discovery credit requested. Ternary nonuniversality is excluded throughout.
