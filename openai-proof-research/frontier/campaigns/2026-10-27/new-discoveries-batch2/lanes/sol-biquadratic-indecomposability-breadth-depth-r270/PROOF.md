# Routine graph endpoint; not a discovery nomination

This file concerns the pivot source family, not the retired biquadratic question. Pourhadi–Faghani, arXiv:1909.02634v1, Problem 2.22, asks for a quasi-λ-distance-balanced graph without edge transitivity. The following complete elementary construction answers that literal existence question. Its routine nature disqualifies it under the campaign rules; no claim of first public resolution is made.

Let A=Z/5Z. Form a multiset of triples consisting of all ten 3-subsets of A, plus the five triples T_i={i,i+1,i+2}, with arithmetic modulo 5. Make a bipartite incidence graph with A as one part and fifteen **distinct block vertices** as the other. A repeated triple gives different vertices with equal neighborhoods, never parallel edges. This is a finite simple graph.

Every point occurs in six of the ten triples and three cyclic triples, hence has degree nine. Every block vertex has degree three. Every two points share a block; every two blocks intersect since they are 3-subsets of a 5-set. Consequently all distinct same-part vertices have distance two. An incident point-block pair has distance one; a nonincident pair has distance three, by passing through a point of the block and a block containing the two points. The graph is connected with diameter three.

Fix an edge uv with u a point and v a block. Among point vertices, u and the two points outside v are closer to u: three vertices. Among block vertices, the eight other blocks incident with u are closer to u; v and the six blocks not incident with u are closer to v. Thus |W_uv|=11 and |W_vu|=9 for every edge, so the graph is quasi-(11/9)-distance-balanced, exactly with the paper's global rational λ>1 and allowing reciprocal edge orientation.

The degree classes prevent any automorphism from interchanging parts. A cyclic triple has two block vertices with its neighborhood, whereas a noncyclic triple has one. Equality of neighborhoods and the size of that equivalence class are automorphism invariants. There are edges incident with each type of block vertex, so these edges cannot lie in the same automorphism orbit. Hence the graph is not edge-transitive. This proves the literal endpoint.

Strongest complete classical-adaptation objection: for any connected bipartite graph of diameter three with part sizes a,b and constant degrees r,s, the same count gives |W_uv|=a−s+r and |W_vu|=b−r+s. An incidence structure with constant replication and block size, pairwise intersecting blocks, and all point pairs covered therefore supplies such examples immediately. The construction merely chooses a nonuniform twin multiplicity to destroy transitivity. The source already uses layer counts in the proof of Proposition 2.18 and gives biregular constructions. This is a short standard incidence-graph adaptation, regardless of whether this exact 20-vertex graph previously appeared. It is explicitly **not eligible**, does not establish a new-to-literature theorem, and must not initiate Lean or manuscript work. `checks.py` verifies all forty-five edges by integer BFS; the proof above is independent of that control.
