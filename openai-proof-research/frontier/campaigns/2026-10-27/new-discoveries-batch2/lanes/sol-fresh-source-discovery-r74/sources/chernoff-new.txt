                                           The density f of Z is sometimes known as Chernoff’s density, which first arose in Chernoff’s study
                                           of mode estimation [2] and has since become a canonical non-Gaussian limit law in asymptotic
                                           theory. In particular, it governs the pointwise limiting behavior of estimators in isotonic regression,
                                           monotone density and hazard estimation, interval censoring, and several other shape-constrained
                                           problems; see, for example, the applications discussed in [5]. Balabdaoui and Wellner [1] proved
                                           that f is log-concave and conjectured that f is strongly log-concave, i.e.,
                                                                                        inf −(log f )′′ (x) > 0.
                                                                                        x∈R

                                           Strong log-concavity places a distribution in a substantially more rigid class than ordinary log-
                                           concavity, as a uniform lower bound on the curvature of − log f yields Gaussian-type concentration
                                           and associated functional inequalities. See Saumard and Wellner [9] for a comprehensive review on
                                           the properties of log-concave and strongly log-concave distributions.

    The proof of Theorem 1 has two key ingredients, built on the Airy random series representation
used by Balabdaoui and Wellner [1] and recorded in Section 2. First, in Section 3, we prove a
general exponential peeling lemma and apply it to obtain the strict pointwise bound v(x) > 0.
Second, in Section 4, we use an Airy convolution identity of Menon and Srinivasan [6] to obtain the
linear bound v(x) ≥ 4x. Since f (x) ∝ g(x)g(−x), these complementary bounds yield κ(x) > 4|x|;
continuity and a compactness argument then imply that inf x∈R κ(x) > 0.
    We note that Balabdaoui and Wellner [1] already provided numerical evidence for the strong
log-concavity of f , though they did not obtain a proof. The purpose of this note is to give a fully
analytic proof, establishing in particular that κ is strictly positive everywhere and that κ is bounded
away from zero in the tails.

5    Proof of Theorem 1
Corollary 5 proves (4); in particular, g is strictly log-concave. Lemma 7 then proves (5). If x ≥ 0,
equations (3), (4), and (5) yield

                                     κ(x) = v(x) + v(−x) > 4x.

The function κ is even, so
                                       κ(x) > 4|x|,       x ∈ R,
including x = 0 because κ(0) = 2v(0) > 0. This proves (6) and shows that κ(x) → ∞ as |x| → ∞.
    Finally, g is smooth and strictly positive, so κ is continuous. On the compact interval [−1, 1],
the positive function κ attains a strictly positive minimum m0 . Outside that interval, (6) gives
κ(x) > 4. Therefore
                                     inf κ(x) ≥ min{m0 , 4} > 0.
                                     x∈R

This proves (7) and completes the proof of Theorem 1.
