                                              MILNOR NUMBERS OF PROJECTIVE HYPERSURFACES
                                                AND THE CHROMATIC POLYNOMIAL OF GRAPHS




arXiv:1008.4749v1 [math.AG] 27 Aug 2010
                                                                                  JUNE HUH


                                                  Abstract. The chromatic polynomial χG (q) of a graph G counts the number
                                                  of proper colorings of G. We give an affirmative answer to the conjecture of
                                                  Read [22] and Rota-Heron-Welsh [24] that the absolute values of the coeffi-
                                                  cients of the chromatic polynomial form a log-concave sequence. We define a
                                                  sequence of numerical invariants of projective hypersurfaces analogous to the
                                                  Milnor number of local analytic hypersurfaces. Then we show log-concavity
                                                  of the sequence which includes the conjecture on χG (q) as a special case. As
                                                  a byproduct of our approach, we obtain an analogue of Kouchnirenko’s theo-
                                                  rem relating the Milnor number with the Newton polytope; we also answer a
                                                  question posed by Trung-Verma in [33].




                                                                             1. Introduction
                                          In a paper in 1912, Birkhoff introduced a function χG (q), defined for all positive
                                          integers q and a finite graph G, which counts the number of proper colorings of G
                                          with q colors. As it turns out, χG (q) is a polynomial in q with integer coefficients,
                                          called the chromatic polynomial of G. Although Birkhoff’s hope of using the chro-
                                          matic polynomial χG (q) to prove the four-color theorem did not bear fruit, χG (q)
                                          remains as an important invariant of G.
                                             Recall that a sequence a0 , a1 , . . . , an of real numbers is said to be unimodal if
                                          for some 0 ≤ i ≤ n,
                                                              a0 ≤ a1 ≤ · · · ≤ ai−1 ≤ ai ≥ ai+1 ≥ · · · ≥ an ,
                                          and is said to be log-concave if for all 0 < i < n,
                                                                                ai−1 ai+1 ≤ a2i .
                                          Clearly a log-concave sequence of positive terms is unimodal. Like many other
                                          combinatorial invariants, the chromatic polynomial shows a surprising log-concavity
                                          property. See [2, 26, 27] for a survey of known results and open problems on log-
                                          concave and unimodal sequences arising in algebra, combinatorics, and geometry.
                                          The goal of this paper is to answer the following question concerning the coefficients
                                          of chromatic polynomials.
                                          Conjecture 1. Let χG (q) = an q n − an−1 q n−1 + · · · + (−1)n a0 be the chromatic
                                          polynomial of a finite graph G. Then the sequence a0 , a1 , . . . , an is log-concave.

multiplicities. We give an elementary proof using Okounkov bodies [11, 15, 18].

2.3. Mixed multiplicities of ideals and mixed volumes of polytopes. It fol-
lows from Ehrhart’s theorem [3, Section 6.3] that the multiplicity of a toric algebra
is the normalized volume of the associated lattice polytope. Trung and Verma use
this relation to show in [33, Corollary 2.5] that the normalized mixed volumes of
lattice convex polytopes in Rn are mixed multiplicities of certain monomial ideals.
We follow the notation and convention of [4] and write M Vn for the mixed volume
of convex polytopes in Rn .
Theorem 4 (Trung-Verma). Let ∆1 , . . . , ∆n be lattice convex polytopes in Rn . Let
Ji be an ideal of k[z0 , z1 , . . . , zn ] generated by a set of monomials of the same degree
such that ∆i is the convex hull of exponents of their dehomogenized monomials in
k[z1 , . . . , zn ]. Then
                     M Vn (∆1 , . . . , ∆n ) = e(0,1,...,1) (m|J1 , . . . , Jn ).
Therefore, by the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality on mixed volumes of convex bod-
ies, if J is an ideal generated by monomials of the same degree in k[z0 , z1 , . . . , zn ],
then
                   ei−1 (m|J)ei+1 (m|J) ≤ ei (m|J)2  for 0 < i < n.
So mixed multiplicities of m and J form a log-concave sequence in this case. In this
simple setting, the question of Trung and Verma can be stated as follows.
Question. Under what conditions do ei (m|J) form a log-concave sequence?
In [33, Question 2.7], Trung and Verma suggest the condition ht(J) = n. We show
in Theorem 14 that when J is generated by elements of the same degree, ei (m|J)
form a log-concave sequence. Example 15 shows that the answer to the question in
its original formulation is no in general.
Remark 5. The question is interesting in view of a theorem of Teissier [30] and
Rees-Sharp [23] on mixed multiplicities. The theorem of Teissier and Rees-Sharp
says that if J is an m-primary ideal in a local ring, then
                  ei (m|J)2 ≤ ei−1 (m|J)ei+1 (m|J)                for 0 < i < n.
In other words, ei (m|J) form a log-convex sequence.

3.2. Log-concavity of mixed multiplicities. We give a sufficient condition for
mixed multiplicities to form a log-concave sequence. Theorem 14 is a small variation
of a theorem of Teissier [31] and Khovanskii [12] formulated in terms of mixed
multiplicities. We give an elementary proof using the theory of Okounkov bodies
as developed in [15].
Theorem 14. Let S be a standard graded domain of dimension n + 1 over an
algebraically closed field, m the irrelevant ideal of S. If J is generated by elements
of the same degree, then
                                ei−1 (m|J)ei+1 (m|J) ≤ ei (m|J)2
for 0 < i < n.
Since jacobian ideal of a homogeneous polynomial is generated by elements of the
same degree, it follows that µi (h) form a log-concave sequence. Also, because a
sequence of mixed multiplicities has no internal zeroes [33, Corollary 1.8], µi (h)
form a unimodal sequence.
