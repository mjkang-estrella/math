        BASIC WRONSKIANS IN THE RIEMANN HYPOTHESIS


                                       JOHN NUTTALL


         Abstract. We have previously proposed that the sign-regularity properties of
         a function Φ(t) related to the transform of the ζ-function could be the key to
         an analytic approach to the Riemann hypothesis. These properties are related
         to a corresponding Wronskian. In this report we find an explicit form for the
         Wronskian of a function based on the first term in the expansion of Φ(t) (case
         β). For a simplification (case α) of this case, the properties, in particular the
         location of the zeros, was soved by Szegö in 1924 in his study of partial sums
         of the exponential function.




                                   1. INTRODUCTION
1.1. Pólya [6] in 1926 suggested that, when searching for an approach to the Rie-
mann Hypothesis (RH), the properties of the function Φ(t) might be of consider-
able importance. With this in mind, following the lead of Csordas, Norfolk and
Varga [1], [2], we have argued [4] that the sign-regularity behavior of Φ(t) and its
cumulants could be useful in an analytic (rather than arithmetic) method. Re-
cently we have suggested [5] 1 that, it might be helpful to study the properties of
a simplified version of the function Φ(t), consisting of the first term in the infinite
series that represents Φ(t) .

          4. LOCATION OF THE ZEROS OF THE WRONSKIANS
4.1. In the application of the Wronskian Wrβ (y) to the RH, the location of the zeros
of w(r, t), or equivalently Wrβ (y), is of interest when considering sign-regularity. It
turns out that the case of Wrα (y) is simpler in ths regard, since this function is
the partial sum of the power series for e−y . Beginning with Szegö in 1924, the
location of the zeros of functions such as Wrα (y) has been extensively studied. A
recent survey of the subject may be found in Vargas [7], and useful results appear
in Zemyan [8].
   Relevant information includes the following.
        • If r is even then Wrα (y) has no real zeros.
        • If r is odd then Wrα (y) has one real zero, say z(r), and z(r) > 0.
        • If r2 > r1 then z(r2 ) > z(r1 ).
        • The quantity z(2r − 1)/(2r − 1) → ẑ, r → ∞, where ẑ exp(1 + ẑ) = 1, so
          that ẑ = 0.278464 . . .

4.2. Our numerical investigations [5] suggest that the structure of the zeros of
Wrβ (y) is similar to that of Wrα (y). We have started to study this question - but
perhaps there has been previous work on the problem.


                                    5. DISCUSSION
   It seems from calculations [5] that corresponding properties also apply to the
cumulants of g(t). An interesting project is to explore whether the method of this
report may be extended to that case.
