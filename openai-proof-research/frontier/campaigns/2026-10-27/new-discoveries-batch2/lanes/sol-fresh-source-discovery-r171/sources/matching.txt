 Abstract. 
    
 For a finite simple graph  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , we consider the quotient algebra
 ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G)  of the polynomial ring in the variables  u e u_{e}  (for  e ∈ E e\in E )
by the ideal generated by all products  u e ​ u f u_{e}u_{f}  for non-disjoint edges
 e e  and  f f  (this includes all squares  u e 2 u_{e}^{2} ).
This quotient is called the matching algebra of  G G , since it has a basis
indexed by the matchings of  G G .
In this quotient, we define a subalgebra  𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G)  generated by
the signed incidence sums  θ v = ∑ e = ( v , w ) u e − ∑ e = ( w , v ) u e \theta_{v}=\sum_{e=(v,w)}u_{e}-\sum_{e=(w,v)}u_{e} 
for all  v ∈ V v\in V  (where all edges of  G G  are oriented arbitrarily);
we call this the Dunkl matching algebra. 
    
 We show that, as a graded vector space,  𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G)  is dual to the span of all
polynomials  p M = ∏ ( i , j ) ∈ M ( x i − x j ) p_{M}=\prod_{(i,j)\in M}(x_{i}-x_{j}) ,
where  M M  ranges over all matchings of  G G .
For the complete graph  K n K_{n} , the latter span is a direct sum of
two-row Specht modules (one in each degree);
thus its Hilbert series is that of the Catalan triangle, and, in
characteristic zero, the Dunkl matching algebra can be presented by
linear and quadratic relations.
For arbitrary graphs, we formulate the saturation
problem of deciding when the selected matching Specht generators  p M p_{M} 
in a given degree  k k  span the full two-row Specht module  S ( n − k , k ) S^{(n-k,k)} .
We show that saturation in degree  k k  forces
 k k -connectivity, that saturation in degree  2 2  is equivalent to
 2 2 -connectivity, and that  k k -linked graphs are saturated in degree  k k .
We also prove saturation in every possible degree whenever the complement of
 G G  is a matching. We show that the Dunkl matching algebra equals
the full matching algebra exactly for forests, and give an explicit Hilbert
series formula for unicyclic graphs. Finally, we compare with a related
Postnikov–Shapiro quotient: depending on the ambient algebra, its Hilbert
series is either the central zonotopal specialization of the Tutte polynomial
or a regraded all-terminal reliability polynomial.

Specht module, Lefschetz property, zonotopal algebra, Tutte polynomial

 1.  Introduction 

 
 Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  be a finite simple graph. The matching generating polynomial
of  G G  records the numbers of sets of pairwise vertex-disjoint edges. In this
note we lift this elementary invariant to a graded algebra 
 

 
 
 ℳ ( G ) = 𝐤 [ u e : e ∈ E ] / ( u e u f : e ∩ f ≠ ∅ ) . \mathcal{M}(G)=\mathbf{k}[u_{e}:e\in E]/(u_{e}u_{f}:e\cap f\neq\varnothing). 
 
 
 In this algebra, we define the signed incidence sums 
 

 
 
 θ v = ∑ e = ( v , w ) u e − ∑ e = ( w , v ) u e  for all  ​ v ∈ V , \theta_{v}=\sum_{e=(v,w)}u_{e}-\sum_{e=(w,v)}u_{e}\qquad\text{ for all }v\in V, 
 
 
 where an orientation of  G G  has been chosen. We denote by  𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G) 
the subalgebra generated by the  θ v \theta_{v}  and call it the  Dunkl
matching algebra .
The goal of the present note is to understand this algebra
 𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G) , in particular its Hilbert series. 
 
 
 The homogeneous parts  𝒟 ​ ( G ) k \mathcal{D}(G)_{k}  of  𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G) 
stand in a natural duality to certain subspaces of the polynomial ring
 𝐤 ⁡ [ x v ∣ v ∈ V ] \mathbf{k}[x_{v}\mid v\in V] .
Namely, for each matching  M M , define the polynomial 
 

 
 
 p M = p M ​ ( x ) = ∏ ( i , j ) ∈ M ( x i − x j ) ∈ 𝐤 ⁡ [ x v ∣ v ∈ V ] . p_{M}=p_{M}(x)=\prod_{(i,j)\in M}(x_{i}-x_{j})\in\mathbf{k}[x_{v}\mid v\in V]. 
 
 
 For any  k ≥ 0 k\geq 0 , we define the vector subspace

 Motivation. 
This work is inspired by the
Fomin–Kirillov approach to Schubert calculus, see  [ 10 ,  2 ,  18 ] . However in our set-up the adjective “Dunkl” is motivational only. Unlike the
Fomin–Kirillov algebra  [ 10 ] , our matching algebra is commutative and
kills every product of two incident edge generators. 
 
 
 Recall that the
Fomin–Kirillov quadratic algebra  ℰ n \mathcal{E}_{n}  is generated by
antisymmetric symbols  [ i ​ j ] = − [ j ​ i ] [ij]=-[ji]  subject, in particular, to
 [ i ​ j ] 2 = 0 [ij]^{2}=0 , commutation of generators with disjoint supports, and
certain three-term relations on triples of indices. Its distinguished
elements 
 

 
 
 ϑ i = ∑ j ≠ i [ i ​ j ] \vartheta_{i}=\sum_{j\neq i}[ij] 
 
 
 are known as the Dunkl elements. A fundamental feature of the construction is
that these elements commute, and the commutative subalgebra which they
generate provides an algebraic model for the cohomology ring of the
complete flag variety; see  [ 10 ] . This point of view has subsequently
led to variants for other Coxeter groups, quantum cohomology,  K K -theory,
and Nichols–Woronowicz algebras. 
 
 
 Blasiak, Liu, and Mészáros  [ 7 , Definition 2.3]  have
introduced restrictions of this algebra to a graph. For a graph  G G 
on  [ n ] [n] , they define  ℰ G \mathcal{E}_{G}  to be the subalgebra of
 ℰ n \mathcal{E}_{n}  generated by those  [ i ​ j ] [ij]  for which  { i , j } \{i,j\}  is an edge
of  G G . Their graph restriction is thus a subalgebra construction,
rather than the quotient obtained by setting nonedge generators equal
to zero. 
 
 
 Our construction may be viewed as a
quotient of this picture. Give the generator
corresponding to an unoriented edge  { i , j } \{i,j\}  the sign determined by an
orientation and write it as  u i ​ j = − u j ​ i u_{ij}=-u_{ji} . If, in addition to the
Fomin–Kirillov relations, one kills every product 
 

 
 
 u i ​ j ​ u k ​ ℓ with { i , j } ∩ { k , ℓ } ≠ ∅ , u_{ij}u_{k\ell}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\{i,j\}\cap\{k,\ell\}\neq\varnothing, 
 
 
 and kills the generators corresponding to nonedges of  G G , then the
resulting commutative quotient is precisely the matching algebra
 ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) . Under this quotient the Dunkl element
 ϑ i \vartheta_{i}  descends to the signed incidence sum  θ i \theta_{i} .
Thus  𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G)  can be interpreted as the part of the
Dunkl-element construction which survives after only products supported
on matchings are retained.
Equivalently, the quotient map
 ℰ n ↠ ℳ ⁡ ( K n ) \mathcal{E}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}(K_{n})  that kills products of
incident edge generators restricts to a surjection
 ℰ G ↠ ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{E}_{G}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{M}(G) , where  ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G)  is
identified with the subalgebra of  ℳ ⁡ ( K n ) \mathcal{M}(K_{n})  generated by the
edges of  G G . 
 
 
 This truncation changes the nature of the problem considerably.
The flag-variety and Bruhat-order connections of the classical setting
are replaced by the incidence structure of a graph, while the linear
forms  x i − x j x_{i}-x_{j} , i.e., the roots of type  A A , reappear on the dual side.
One may therefore ask how much of the structure carried by Dunkl
elements survives under this severe matching truncation. The results
below show that the answer retains a surprising amount of structure:
for the complete graph the homogeneous components are governed by
two-row Specht modules, whereas for arbitrary graphs the failure or
success of spanning the corresponding Specht module detects
connectivity properties of the graph. In this sense, the matching
quotient provides a bridge between the Dunkl-element philosophy,
matching theory, and the representation theory of the symmetric group. 
 
 
 Related work. 
Subalgebras generated by vertices in the commutative algebra
generated by edges have been considered in e.g.,  [ 24 ,  25 ] .
Moreover, in the case of complete graphs they also have a geometric
interpretation, see  [ 26 ] .
Products of arbitrary subfamilies of a vector configuration have a rich
theory related to Tutte polynomials; see Berget  [ 5 ]  and the
zonotopal literature  [ 1 ,  15 ] . Our restriction to vertex-disjoint
subfamilies is not matroidal: it remembers the incidence of edges at vertices.
It also differs from graph Specht modules and their relationship with matching
polytopes studied by Liu  [ 21 ] . The construction lies naturally
between these two subjects. 
 
 
 Several nearby constructions attach graded objects to matchings but use a
different algebraic mechanism. Numata  [ 23 ]  studies an Artinian
Gorenstein algebra obtained by apolarity from a weighted matching generating
function and proves a strong Lefschetz property. Li  [ 19 ]  proves strong
equivariant log-concavity for the graded permutation representation on the
matchings of an arbitrary graph. For perfect matchings of complete graphs,
Filmus and Lindzey  [ 11 ]  develop harmonic polynomial models involving
Specht polynomials and matching-inclusion matrices. Bedratyuk’s graph
algebra  [ 4 ] , whose basis is indexed by all simple graphs on a
fixed vertex set, also carries Boolean up/down operators and two-row Specht
modules. Bergeron, Chan, Soltani, and Zabrocki  [ 3 ]  study a
construction deceptively parallel to our  P k ​ ( K n ) P_{k}(K_{n})  in an exterior algebra: products of differences
 ξ j − ξ i \xi_{j}-\xi_{i}  indexed by noncrossing pairings give bases of exterior
quasisymmetric harmonic spaces whose degree- k k  dimensions agree with  dim P k ​ ( K n ) \dim P_{k}(K_{n}) . We compare the two constructions more closely in
 Remark   4.3 . None of these constructions is the
incidence-generated subalgebra  𝒟 ⁡ ( G ) ⊆ ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{D}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(G)  studied
here. 
 
 
 There is one direct overlap at the level of the abstract complete-graph
presentation. Jonsson Kling, Lundqvist, Mohammadi, Orth, and
Sáenz-de-Cabezón  [ 17 ]  study the more general almost complete
intersections 
 

 
 
 𝐤 ⁡ [ z 1 , … , z n ] / ( z 1 2 , … , z n 2 , ( z 1 + ⋯ + z n ) q ) . \mathbf{k}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]/(z_{1}^{2},\ldots,z_{n}^{2},(z_{1}+\cdots+z_{n})^{q}). 
 
 
 The quotient occurring in our characteristic-zero presentation of
 𝒟 ⁡ ( K n ) \mathcal{D}(K_{n})  is their case  q = 1 q=1 .
The contribution here is therefore not the abstract quotient itself, but its
realization inside a matching algebra, its matching-product duality, and the
resulting saturation problem for arbitrary graphs.

 2.  The matching algebra 

 
 Let  G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E)  be a finite simple graph.
Its edges are  2 2 -element sets of vertices;
thus, two edges  e , f e,f  satisfy  e ∩ f ≠ ∅ e\cap f\neq\varnothing 
if and only if they have a common endpoint (this allows  e = f e=f ).
Two edges  e e  and  f f  are said to be  incident  if
 e ∩ f ≠ ∅ e\cap f\neq\varnothing . 
 
 
 Definition 2.1 . 
 
 The  matching algebra  of  G G  is the commutative  𝐤 \mathbf{k} -algebra 
 

 
 
 ℳ ( G ) := 𝐤 [ u e : e ∈ E ] / ( u e u f : e ∩ f ≠ ∅ ) . \mathcal{M}(G):=\mathbf{k}[u_{e}:e\in E]/\bigl(u_{e}u_{f}:e\cap f\neq\varnothing\bigr). 
 
 
 That is,  ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G)  is the quotient of the polynomial ring
 𝐤 [ u e : e ∈ E ] \mathbf{k}[u_{e}:e\in E]  by the monomial ideal generated by all products
 u e ​ u f u_{e}u_{f}  with  e e  and  f f  being two incident edges
(including the case  e = f e=f ).
It is graded by degree, where every generator  u e u_{e}  has degree  1 1 . 
 
 
 
 For a matching  M ⊆ E M\subseteq E  of  G G , define the element 
 

 
 
 u M := ∏ e ∈ M u e ∈ ℳ ⁡ ( G ) ,  in particular  ​ u ∅ := 1 . u_{M}:=\prod_{e\in M}u_{e}\in\mathcal{M}(G),\qquad\text{ in particular }u_{\varnothing}:=1. 
 
 
 
 
 Proposition 2.2 . 
 
 The elements  u M u_{M} , indexed by the matchings  M M  of  G G , form a homogeneous
 𝐤 \mathbf{k} -basis of  ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) . Consequently, the Hilbert series of
 ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G)  is 
 

 
 
 Hilb ⁡ ( ℳ ⁡ ( G ) , t ) = ∑ k ≥ 0 m k ​ ( G ) ​ t k , \operatorname{Hilb}(\mathcal{M}(G),t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}m_{k}(G)t^{k}, 
 
 
 where  m k ​ ( G ) m_{k}(G)  is the number of  k k -matchings of  G G . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The defining ideal  ( u e u f : e ∩ f ≠ ∅ ) \bigl(u_{e}u_{f}:e\cap f\neq\varnothing\bigr)  is monomial.
Its standard monomials are the square-free
products  u e 1 u e 2 ⋯ u e k u_{e_{1}}u_{e_{2}}\cdots u_{e_{k}}  of generators
such that no two of the edges  e 1 , e 2 , … , e k e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{k}  have
a common endpoint; these are exactly the  u M u_{M} 
corresponding to the matchings  M M  of  G G .
Thus, these  u M u_{M}  (or, rather, their residue classes) form
a basis of the quotient algebra  ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G) .
The formula for the Hilbert series follows directly from this.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Thus, for the complete graph  K n K_{n} , we have 
 

 
 
 Hilb ⁡ ( ℳ ⁡ ( K n ) , t ) = ∑ 0 ≤ k ≤ n / 2 n ! 2 k ​ k ! ​ ( n − 2 ​ k ) ! ​ t k , \operatorname{Hilb}(\mathcal{M}(K_{n}),t)=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n/2}\frac{n!}{2^{k}k!(n-2k)!}t^{k}, 
 
 
 and the complete bipartite graph  K a , b K_{a,b}  satisfies 
 

 
 
 Hilb ⁡ ( ℳ ⁡ ( K a , b ) , t ) = ∑ k ≥ 0 k ! ​ ( a k ) ​ ( b k ) ​ t k . \operatorname{Hilb}(\mathcal{M}(K_{a,b}),t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}k!\binom{a}{k}\binom{b}{k}t^{k}. 
 
 
 In particular,  dim ℳ ⁡ ( K n ) \dim\mathcal{M}(K_{n})  is the number of involutions in  S n S_{n} . 
 
 
 Equivalently, if  Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G)  denotes the matching complex of  G G  (the
independence complex of the line graph  L ⁡ ( G ) L(G) ), then  ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G)  is the
Artinian monomial quotient obtained from its Stanley–Reisner ring
 𝐤 ⁡ [ Δ ⁡ ( G ) ] \mathbf{k}[\Delta(G)]  by adjoining the squares  u e 2 u_{e}^{2}  to the Stanley–Reisner
ideal. Thus  ℳ ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{M}(G)  itself is not a Stanley–Reisner ring in the
usual sense. Matching complexes and their face rings are classical; see, for
example, Björner–Lovász–Vrećica–Živaljević  [ 6 ] 
and Jonsson  [ 16 , Chapter 5] . Ferrarello and Fröberg  [ 9 ] 
explicitly relate the Hilbert series of the ordinary Stanley–Reisner ring of
a matching complex to graph-subgraph polynomials.

 There is also a simple sufficient condition in every degree. 
 
 
 Definition 5.10 . 
 
 A graph  G G  is  k k -linked  if, whenever

 Proof.

 x a i − x b i = ∑ u ​ v ∈ E ⁡ ( Q i ) ε u ​ v ​ ( x u − x v ) , ε u ​ v ∈ { ± 1 } . x_{a_{i}}-x_{b_{i}}=\sum_{uv\in E(Q_{i})}\varepsilon_{uv}(x_{u}-x_{v}),\qquad\varepsilon_{uv}\in\{\pm 1\}.

 p M ∈ P k ​ ( G ) p_{M}\in P_{k}(G) . Thus  P k ​ ( K n ) ⊆ P k ​ ( G ) P_{k}(K_{n})\subseteq P_{k}(G) , and the result follows.

 Thus, for  1 ≤ k ≤ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ 1\leq k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor , we have the useful chain
