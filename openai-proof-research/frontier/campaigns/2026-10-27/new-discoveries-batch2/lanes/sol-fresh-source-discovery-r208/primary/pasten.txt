                                                   that obey the Leibniz rule and are additive on this one equation. An arithmetic Wronskian then
                                                   gives an abc-type inequality governed by the norm ∥ψ∥ of the derivation, and Pasten showed

                                                   that sufficiently small derivations exist under the abc conjecture and give an abc inequality with
                                                   a fixed exponent.
                                                       We determine exactly which values this Wronskian takes, and we show that its estimate with
                                                   exact prime weights has the best possible coefficient. The quality X = log c/ log rad(abc) factors
                                                   into an approximation gain and a power gain for each chosen presentation of the triple, and
                                                   the resulting inequality bounds X and both gains. For coprime polynomials the same argument

                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                              The proof of the Mason–Stothers theorem for polynomials rests on the derivative [Mas84,
                                           Sto81, Sny00]. Its arithmetic counterpart is the abc conjecture of Masser and Oesterlé [Oes88],
                                           which asserts that for every ε > 0 only finitely many coprime triples a + b = c satisfy c >
                                           rad(abc)1+ε . Over Z there is no derivative of the same kind, since every additive map that obeys
                                           the Leibniz rule vanishes. Following an observation of Pheidas that additivity is needed only
                                           finitely many times, Pasten constructed, for a given coprime triple a + b = c, derivatives that
                                           obey the Leibniz rule and are additive on this one equation [Pas22]. Their size is measured by a
                                           norm ∥ψ∥, the largest absolute value taken on the primes. Pasten proved an abc-type inequality
                                           in which ∥ψ∥ appears, showed by geometry of numbers that such derivatives exist with controlled
                                           norm, and proved that sufficiently small derivatives exist under the abc conjecture and give an
                                           abc inequality with a fixed exponent.
                                              The present note places Pasten’s inequality in the quality plane of Laniewski–Müller [LM26].
                                           In this plane the quality X and the weak quality Y of a triple serve as coordinates, and a chosen
                                           presentation P of the triple factors the quality into an approximation gain and a power gain,
                                           following Müller and Taktikos [MT26] and de Weger [dW26]. Section 2 recalls these notions.
                                           Section 3 treats polynomials, where the Wronskian proof of the Mason–Stothers theorem gives
                                           Ga < 1 and Gp < 3 for every presentation. Section 4 recalls Pasten’s construction, determines
                                           the exact image and the optimal norm coefficient of the Wronskian, and states the inequality
                                           as a bound on X, from which the bounds on the two gains follow for every presentation. Under

                                      3. The polynomial model
    Let A, B, C be coprime polynomials in C[t] with A + B = C, not all constant. Put

Lemma 3.1. The Wronskian W is non-zero, and ABC/ rad(ABC) divides W .
Proof. If W = 0, then (A/B)′ = 0, so that A is a constant multiple of B. Coprimality then

The same holds for the roots of B, and for the roots of C through the form AC ′ − CA′ of (3.1).
Since A, B and C are coprime, the claim follows.                                               □

Proof. Choose in (3.1) the form of W that omits a polynomial of degree N . In this form each

Definition 3.3. A presentation of the polynomial triple is a monic polynomial P dividing
ABC with rad(P ) = rad(ABC). Its approximation gain and power gain are Ga = N/ deg P and
Gp = deg P/D.
  Writing H = deg P , one has D ≤ H ≤ T , and the gains satisfy Ga Gp = X = N/D, with
weak quality Y = T /D.
Corollary 3.4. Every presentation of a polynomial triple satisfies Ga < 1 and Gp < 3.
Proof. By Theorem 3.2, Ga = N/H ≤ N/D < 1. Since each of A, B and C has degree at most
N , one has T ≤ 3N , and therefore Gp = H/D ≤ T /D ≤ 3N/D < 3.                      □
   In the polynomial case the quality and the weak quality thus satisfy X < 1 and Y < 3, and
these bounds pass to every presentation. Both come from the drop in degree under differentia-
tion, which the inequality deg W ≤ T − N − 1 expresses.

                    6. Fermat triples and the presentation by bases
   Pasten deduced from Conjecture 4.5 an asymptotic form of Fermat’s last theorem by trans-
lating a Wronskian proof of the polynomial case [Pas22, Propositions 3.10 and 3.11]. The
presentation framework gives the same exponent bound through the approximation gain of a
natural presentation.
Proposition 6.1. Let a, b, c be pairwise coprime positive integers with an + bn = cn for some
n ≥ 2, numbered so that a < b, which is possible since a = b would force cn = 2. Consider the
triple (an , bn , cn ) with the presentation P = abc by its bases. Then Ga > n/3. In particular, if
the quality of this triple is at most κ, then n < 3κ.
