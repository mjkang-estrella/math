 Abstract. 
    
 We investigate the log-concavity on the half-line of the Wright function  ϕ ⁡ ( − α , β , − x ) , \phi(-\alpha,\beta,-x),  in the probabilistic setting  α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1)  and  β ≥ 0 . \beta\geq 0.  Applications are given to the construction of generalized entropies associated to the corresponding Mittag-Leffler function. A natural conjecture for the equivalence between the log-concavity of the Wright function and the existence of such generalized entropies is formulated. The problem is solved for  β ≥ α \beta\geq\alpha  and in the classical case  β = 1 − α \beta=1-\alpha  of the Mittag-Leffler distribution, which exhibits a certain critical parameter  α ∗ = 0.771667 ​ … \alpha_{*}=0.771667...  defined implicitly on the Gamma function and characterizing the log-concavity. We also prove that the probabilistic Wright functions are always unimodal, and that they are multiplicatively strongly unimodal if and only if  β ≥ α \beta\geq\alpha  or  α ≤ 1 / 2 \alpha\leq 1/2  and  β = 0 . \beta=0.

One purpose of this paper is to show some basic, albeit non-trivial, properties of these densities, which transfer immediately to the Wright functions of the second kind. Our first finding is the following. 
 
 
 Theorem A​​  . 
 
 The random variable  𝐌 α , β {\bf M}_{\alpha,\beta}  is unimodal. 
 
 
 
 This result is easy to prove for  β ≥ α \beta\geq\alpha  and the density  φ α , β \varphi_{\alpha,\beta}  is then non-increasing. For  β < α , \beta<\alpha,  the density is increasing-then-decreasing and the proof, which relies on strong unimodality and a detailed analysis of the distributions with binomial moments introduced in  [ 30 ] , is more involved. In the case  β = 1 − α , \beta=1-\alpha,  the random variable  𝐌 α , 1 − α = 𝐌 α {\bf M}_{\alpha,1-\alpha}={\bf M}_{\alpha}  has a so-called Mittag-Leffler distribution with moment generating function 
 

 
 
 𝔼 ⁡ [ e z ​ 𝐌 α ] = E α ​ ( z ) , {\mathbb{E}}[e^{z{\bf M}_{\alpha}}]\;=\;E_{\alpha}(z),

If  1 − α ≤ α , 1-\alpha\leq\alpha,  the unimodality of  𝐌 α {\bf M}_{\alpha}  follows then from the well-known fact - see Section 8 in  [ 6 ]  and the references therein - that it is distributed as the positive part of a real spectrally negative stable random variable with parameter  1 / α ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] , 1/\alpha\in[1,2],  which is unimodal by Yamazato’s theorem - see Theorem 53.1 in  [ 33 ] . 
 
 
 The random variable  𝐌 α {\bf M}_{\alpha}  plays a central role in various probabilistic contexts. If  { Z t ( α ) , t ≥ 0 } \{Z^{(\alpha)}_{t},\;t\geq 0\}  denotes the  α − \alpha- stable subordinator with Laplace transform  𝔼 ⁡ [ e − λ ​ Z t ( α ) ] = e − t ​ λ α {\mathbb{E}}[e^{-\lambda Z^{(\alpha)}_{t}}]=e^{-t\lambda^{\alpha}}  for  t , λ ≥ 0 t,\lambda\geq 0  and if we set  𝐙 α = Z 1 ( α ) , {\bf Z}_{\alpha}=Z^{(\alpha)}_{1},  then it is classical and easy to show by moment identification and self-similarity that

 The Mittag-Leffler random variable also appears in limit theorems for occupation times of Markov processes  [ 5 ] , Pólya urn schemes  [ 21 ] , coalescents  [ 31 ] , and elephant random walks  [ 3 ] . This list is non-exhaustive and we refer to the survey paper  [ 19 ]  for an account on  𝐌 α {\bf M}_{\alpha}  and other related random variables. It is natural to ask for basic distributional properties of  𝐌 α , {\bf M}_{\alpha},  and we focus in this paper on the log-concavity of the density. This property is equivalent to the strong additive unimodality of  𝐌 α {\bf M}_{\alpha}  in the non-degenerate case  α ≠ 1 \alpha\neq 1  by Ibragimov’s theorem  [ 20 ] , and can hence be viewed as a refinement of unimodality. However, the sufficient condition given in Part (b) (ii) is more difficult to prove in the relevant case  α > 4 / 5 . \alpha>4/5. More detail on this open problem is given in the last section of the paper.  6.1.  Multiplicative strong unimodality 

 
 The proof of Theorem A depends on the notion of multiplicative strong unimodality (MSU), introduced in  [ 9 ] . For a positive random variable  X X  with density  f f , it is easy to check that the MSU property is implied by the non-increasing character of  f f , in other words by the unimodality at zero.
