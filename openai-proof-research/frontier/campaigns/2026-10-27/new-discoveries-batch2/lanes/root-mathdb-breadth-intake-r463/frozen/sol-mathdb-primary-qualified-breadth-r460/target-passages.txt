20:                                          modulo n. Recently, Banks et al [1] made the following conjecture:

22:                                              The analogous question for the Euler function φ(n) is known as Carmichael’s conjecture
23:                                          and remains unsolved. If there are counterexamples to Conjecture 1, the authors of [1] proved
24:                                          that all such counterexamples n are multiples of the smallest counterexample n0 . Further,
25:                                          they showed that if n0 exists, then n0 is divisible by every prime less than 30000. In this
26:                                          note, we prove that Conjecture 1 follows from the Extended Riemann Hypothesis (ERH) for
27:                                          Dirichlet L-functions, and also we come very close to proving the conjecture unconditionally.

29:                                          [a1 , . . . , ak ] denotes the least common multiple of a1 , . . . , ak , λ(pe ) = pe−1 (p − 1) when p
30:                                          is odd or e ≤ 2, and λ(2e ) = 2e−2 when e ≥ 3. The following is proved in §7 of [1].
