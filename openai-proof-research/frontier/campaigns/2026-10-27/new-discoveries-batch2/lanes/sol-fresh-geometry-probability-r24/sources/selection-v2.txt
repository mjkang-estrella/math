Hanneke, Moran, Shlimovich and Yehudayoff (COLT 2025) posed this question for linear regression with the minimum-norm empirical risk minimizer. A selector sees a finite dataset D ⊆ ℝ d × ℝ D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R} , keeps at most n n examples with nonnegative weights, and F w ​ ( d , n ) F_{\mathrm{w}}(d,n) is the worst-case ratio between the full-data loss of the trained predictor and the optimal loss. The value is ∞ \infty for n < d n<d , d + 1 d+1 at n = d n=d and 1 1 for n ≥ 2 ​ d n\geq 2d , and the regime d < n < 2 ​ d d<n<2d was left open. We settle several cases. For every d d we prove F w ​ ( d , 2 ​ d − 1 ) = 1 + 1 / d F_{\mathrm{w}}(d,2d-1)=1+1/d , which confirms a claim stated without proof in the original note. We also prove F w ​ ( 3 , 4 ) = 5 / 3 F_{\mathrm{w}}(3,4)=5/3 , F w ​ ( 4 , 5 ) = 2 F_{\mathrm{w}}(4,5)=2 and F w ​ ( 4 , 6 ) = 3 / 2 F_{\mathrm{w}}(4,6)=3/2 , the three smallest cells not covered by that formula. For every intermediate budget n = d + k n=d+k we prove the lower bound F w ​ ( d , d + k ) ≥ 1 + Γ d , k F_{\mathrm{w}}(d,d+k)\geq 1+\Gamma_{d,k} , where Γ d , k \Gamma_{d,k} is an explicit harmonic quantity over balanced partitions of d d . This bound is the exact minimax value on the class of datasets whose whitened systems split into orthogonal circuit blocks. All proved values equal 1 + Γ d , k 1+\Gamma_{d,k} , and we conjecture that this holds throughout the open regime. Our upper bounds combine a rigidity theorem for positive spanning configurations of loss gradients with normal forms of the small positive bases in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} and ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} . These forms are special cases of the classification of Cornaz, Kerleau and Royer; we also control all gradients outside the basis. Coresets summarize large datasets for regression and clustering ( Feldman and Langberg, 2011 ; Phillips, 2017 ; Boutsidis et al., 2013 ) , data pruning discards examples before training deep networks ( Paul et al., 2021 ; Sorscher et al., 2022 ) , and active regression pays for only a few labels ( Chen and Price, 2019 ; Musco et al., 2022 ) . Most guarantees in this literature have the form 1 + ε 1+\varepsilon with budgets that grow like d / ε d/\varepsilon up to logarithmic factors. Hanneke et al. (2025a) asked a sharper worst-case question. For a fixed learning rule and a fixed budget n n , what is the best multiplicative loss achievable on the full dataset, uniformly over all datasets? The selector sees the whole dataset, labels included, so the object is the exact compression limit of a fixed learner at a fixed budget rather than a label-efficient algorithm. Hanneke et al. (2025b) distilled the open cases into concrete problems. We address their Question 2, weighted data selection for linear regression. The aim is to identify the precise worst-case loss that even an optimal selector cannot avoid. This benchmark separates budget limits from the shortcomings of a selection rule. The setting is simple. A dataset D D is a finite multiset in ℝ d × ℝ \mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R} with squared losses ℓ ( x , y ) ​ ( w ) = ( ⟨ w , x ⟩ − y ) 2 \ell_{(x,y)}(w)=(\langle w,x\rangle-y)^{2} . A selector sees all of D D , picks at most n n examples and a convex combination F F of their losses, and hands F F to the min-norm ERM A ⋆ A^{\star} . The learner returns the minimizer of F F of smallest Euclidean norm. We study the worst-case ratio F w ​ ( d , n ) F_{\mathrm{w}}(d,n) of the returned predictor’s full-data loss to the optimal full-data loss (Section 2 ). Hanneke et al. (2025a) proved a sharp trichotomy: F w ​ ( d , n ) F_{\mathrm{w}}(d,n) equals ∞ \infty below budget d d , equals d + 1 d+1 at budget d d , and equals 1 1 from budget 2 ​ d 2d onward, where selection loses nothing at all. The endpoint arguments use different geometries: volume sampling at n = d n=d and Steinitz’s interior-point theorem at n = 2 ​ d n=2d . The whole regime d < n < 2 ​ d d<n<2d between them was left open. Its value was known only to lie in ( 1 , d + 1 ] (1,d+1] and to be O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) . This gap leaves the transition from severe compression to lossless selection unresolved. The note adds that for n = 2 ​ d − 1 n=2d-1 the authors believe the value is 1 + 1 d 1+\frac{1}{d} , “though the argument is somewhat ad hoc and does not clearly extend to other values of n n ” ( Hanneke et al., 2025b , p. 3) . Proof architecture. After whitening, the full-data optimum sits at q = 0 q=0 , the risk ratio is 1 + ∥ q ∥ 2 1+\lVert q\rVert^{2} , and the loss gradients at the optimum sum to zero. If some 2 ​ d − 1 2d-1 gradients positively span ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , a strictly positive zero-sum weighting turns them into a strictly convex objective whose exact minimizer is q = 0 q=0 : selection is free. The whole difficulty is the opposite case. At budget 2 ​ d − 1 2d-1 a rigidity lemma shows that this case is rigid: all gradients lie on d d lines. The covariance identity forces the lines orthogonal with unit energy each. The problem reduces to d d one-dimensional regressions, where leaving out one point on the lightest line costs exactly the harmonic factor 1 d \frac{1}{d} . At smaller budgets the failure of positive spanning no longer forces an axis configuration. We instead use the classification of small positive bases. Minimal positively spanning sets of gradients decompose into few circuits. Minimal cone representations control extra gradients through low-rank circuits. Each resulting geometry yields an exact certificate, splits into orthogonal blocks, or admits completion through quotient volume sampling. The hardest configuration in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} consists of two coupled triangles with two independent cross ears. We call it the rectangle. It resists the partition arguments available to us, but an extremal-basis lemma converts its sign-cone geometry into a five-point selection. Reading guide. (2025a) , which treats mean estimation, classification and stochastic convex optimization in the same worst-case sense. The weighted relaxation is the one studied in the coreset literature. For least-squares regression, coresets and subsampling schemes based on leverage scores ( Drineas et al., 2006 ) , deterministic sparsification ( Batson et al., 2012 ; Boutsidis et al., 2013 ) and volume sampling ( Dereziński and Warmuth, 2017 ; Dereziński et al., 2018 ; Dereziński and Mahoney, 2021 ) give ( 1 + ε ) (1+\varepsilon) -approximate solutions from O ⁡ ( d / ε ) O(d/\varepsilon) rows up to logarithmic factors, and active regression ( Chen and Price, 2019 ; Musco et al., 2022 ) obtains constant-factor approximations from O ⁡ ( d ) O(d) labels. These results use budgets that grow with d d and 1 / ε 1/\varepsilon ; we seek the exact worst-case constant at a fixed budget between d d and 2 ​ d 2d , for a fixed learner whose tie-breaking rule matters. The budget- d d endpoint rests on volume sampling ( Dereziński and Warmuth, 2017 ) , and the budget- 2 ​ d 2d endpoint on Steinitz’s theorem ( Steinitz, 1916 ) . Our intermediate results connect these two geometries, a task that the original authors anticipated would require new ideas. The theory of positive spanning sets and positive bases goes back to Davis (1954) ; McKinney (1962) ; see Regis (2016) for a survey, Audet (2011) for a short proof of the maximal-size theorem, and Schoch (2020) for decomposition theorems. (2021) study structured intermediate positive bases. The normal forms we use in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} and ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} are the cases ℓ = 3 , 4 \ell=3,4 of these characterizations. Our contribution there is the analysis of the gradients outside the basis and the resulting risk bounds. Further questions. Three directions seem natural. First, the unweighted and vector-valued variants of the problem (Questions 3 and 4 of Hanneke et al., 2025b ) interact with the present geometry through the same gradient structure, and it would be interesting to determine which parts transfer. Second, our selections are constructive but not optimized. The complexity of computing an optimal weighted selection for a given dataset is open. Third, the interplay between the normal forms of small positive bases ( Cornaz et al., 2024 ) and the control of the vectors outside the basis developed here may be of use in derivative-free optimization, where positive spanning sets are a basic primitive.
