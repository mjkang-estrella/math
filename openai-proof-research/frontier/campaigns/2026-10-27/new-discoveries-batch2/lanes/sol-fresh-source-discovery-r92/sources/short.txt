1.2. Outline.
• Section 2 regards a bijection from a poset to itself known as echelonmotion. This map depends
  on a choice of a linear extension of the poset. It is defined using the Bruhat decomposition of the
  general linear group, and it generalizes the classical rowmotion map. We prove a conjecture of
  Defant, Jiang, Marczinzik, Segovia, Speyer, Thomas, and Williams [13] concerning echelonmotion
  on modular lattices (Theorem 2.1), which in turn yields bijective proofs of a classical result of
  Dilworth [16].
• Section 3 regards certain statistics on parking functions. Some of the relevant generating functions
  are specializations of generating functions studied by Stanley and Yin [35]. We first prove a
  refined version of a conjecture of Hopkins about the equidistribution of some of these statistics
  (Theorem 3.1). We then prove two additional enumerative conjectures of Hopkins that relate to
  simsun permutations and alternating permutations (Theorems 3.6 and 3.7).
• Section 4 regards the plactic monoid. Suppose u is a word in [m]∗ whose RSK insertion tableau
  has ℓ rows. We prove that if w is in the plactic centralizer of u, then all entries in the first ℓ rows
  of the insertion tableau of w are at most m (Theorem 4.3); this settles a conjecture of Sagan and
  Wilson [32]. We then prove a different conjecture of Sagan and Wilson that relates the plactic
  centralizer of the m-reverse complement of u to the m-evacuation of the plactic centralizer of u
  (Theorem 4.7).

                           2. Echelonmotion on Modular Lattices
   Rowmotion is a well-studied bijective operator on the set of order ideals of a finite poset. It ap-
pears in numerous settings, including dynamical algebraic combinatorics [6, 18, 27, 29, 36], matroid
theory [15], lattice theory [7, 14, 37], and representation theory [20, 26]. Recently, Klász, Marcz-
inzik, and Thomas discovered a way to obtain rowmotion via the Bruhat decomposition of the
general linear group [23]. This provides a natural extension of the definition of rowmotion to an
arbitrary finite poset, provided one fixes a linear extension of the poset in advance. In a subsequent
article, Defant, Jiang, Marczinzik, Segovia, Speyer, Thomas, and Williams named this extension
echelonmotion [13], and they proved that special instances of echelonmotion recover other notable
lattice-theoretic extensions of rowmotion.
   Let R be a poset with n elements. For x, y ∈ R, we say y covers x, denoted x ⋖ y, if x < y and
there does not exist z ∈ R such that x < z < y. A linear extension of R is a bijection σ : R → [n]
such that σ(x) ≤ σ(y) for all x, y ∈ R with x ≤ y. Define the Cartan matrix of R with respect to
a linear extension σ to be the n × n matrix W R,σ whose (i, j)-entry is

This matrix is lower-triangular with 1’s on the diagonal, so it is invertible. The classical Bruhat
decomposition of the general linear group GLn (C) states that

                           4. Centralizers in the Plactic Monoid
   Given a word w, we are interested in the Robinson–Schensted–Knuth (RSK) insertion tableau
P (w), which is a semistandard Young tableau. Two words v, w are Knuth equivalent, written
v ≡ w, if one can be obtained from the other by a sequence of Knuth relations, which state that
for u, v ∈ N∗ and a, b, c ∈ N, we have
                                    uacbv ≡ ucabv       if a ≤ b < c
and
                                 ubacv ≡ ubcav if a < b ≤ c.
Knuth proved that v ≡ w if and only if P (v) = P (w) [21]. The plactic monoid is the quotient of the
free monoid N∗ by Knuth equivalence; it was introduced in this form by Lascoux and Schützenberger
12                                           COLIN DEFANT


[25]. We use standard facts about RSK and jeu-de-taquin; convenient references are the works of
Sagan [30, 31] and Stanley [34].
   For a word u, Sagan and Wilson [32] defined its plactic centralizer to be the set
                     C(u) = {w ∈ N∗ : uw ≡ wu} = {w ∈ N∗ : P (uw) = P (wu)}.
They used this object to study when certain cut-and-paste operations on reading words preserve a
Knuth class, and they established several necessary conditions and examples before posing conjec-
tures. Later work of Sagan and Zhao [33] considers a related stability problem for plactic centralizers
of powers of a fixed word.
   Rows of tableaux are numbered from top to bottom. If T is a semistandard Young tableau,
rw(T ) is its row word, read from left to right along each row, starting with the bottom row and
moving upward. Then P (rw(T )) = T . For a positive integer m, let T≤m be the subtableau of T
consisting of entries at most m, and let T>m be the complementary skew tableau.
   We will use the following result due to Sagan and Wilson.
Lemma 4.1 ([32, Corollary 3.2]). Let u ∈ N∗ , and let w ∈ C(u). If a letter b does not appear in
u, then no b can be bumped while forming P (wu) from P (w) by row insertion.
     We will also need the following classical theorem due to Greene.
Theorem 4.2 ([17]). Let w be a word, and let λ = (λ1 , . . . , λℓ ) be the shape of P (w). For every
k ≥ 1, the maximum size of a subword of w that can be decomposed into k weakly increasing
subwords is λ1 + · · · + λk . Let λ′ = (λ′1 , . . . , λ′ℓ′ ) be the conjugate of λ. For every k ≥ 1, the
maximum size of a subword of w that can be decomposed into k strictly decreasing subwords is
λ′1 + · · · + λ′k .
4.1. Bounding Entries. The following theorem was conjectured by Sagan and Wilson [32].
Theorem 4.3. Let u be a nonempty word, and let m be the maximum letter in u. Let ℓ be the
number of rows of P (u). If w ∈ C(u), then all entries in the first ℓ rows of P (w) are at most m.
Proof. Fix w ∈ C(u), and let P (w) = T . Suppose by way of contradiction that row r of T contains
an entry larger than m, where 1 ≤ r ≤ ℓ. Let h be the number of entries in row r of T that are at
most m.
   Since w ∈ C(u), we have w ∈ C(uN ) for every N ≥ 1. Every entry larger than m is absent from
u , so Lemma 4.1 implies that no entry larger than m can be bumped while forming P (wuN ) by
  N

inserting uN into T .
   All letters inserted from uN are at most m. Since row r contains an entry larger than m, any
letter from [m] inserted into row r cannot be appended to the end of the row, so it must bump
some entry. We have established that this bumped entry cannot be larger than m. This shows
that the number of entries at most m in row r remains fixed during the insertion of uN . Hence,
the number of entries in row r of P (wuN ) that are at most m is exactly h for every N ≥ 1.
   By the standard restriction property of RSK,
                                      P (wuN )≤m = P (w≤m uN ),
where w≤m denotes the subword of w consisting of the entries that are at most m. Therefore the
r-th row of P (w≤m uN ) has length h for every N .
   Because P (u) has ℓ rows, we know by Theorem 4.2 that there is a strictly decreasing subword of
u of length ℓ. Therefore, w≤m uN contains N disjoint strictly decreasing subwords of length ℓ. Let
λ(N ) be the shape of P (w≤m uN ). According to Theorem 4.2, we have

                                    (λ(N ) )′1 + · · · + (λ(N ) )′N ≥ ℓN.

                      ℓN ≤ (λ(N ) )′1 + · · · + (λ(N ) )′N ≤ hm + (N − h)(r − 1).
