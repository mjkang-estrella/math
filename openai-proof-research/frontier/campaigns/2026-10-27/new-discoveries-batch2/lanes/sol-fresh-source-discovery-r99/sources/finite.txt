 Abstract 
    
 The author, together with Nagy, studied the following problem on unavoidable intersections of given size in binary affine spaces. Given an  m m -element set  S ⊆ 𝔽 2 n S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , is there guaranteed to be a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat, that is, a  k k -dimensional affine subspace of  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  containing exactly  t t  points of  S S ? Such problems can be viewed as generalizations of the cap set problem over the binary field. They conjectured that for every fixed pair  ( k , t ) (k,t)  with  k ≥ 1 k\geq 1  and  0 ≤ t ≤ 2 k 0\leq t\leq 2^{k} , the density of values  m ∈ { 0 , … , 2 n } m\in\{0,...,2^{n}\}  for which a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat is guaranteed tends to  1 1 . 
    
 In this paper, motivated by the study of the smallest open case  ( k , t ) = ( 3 , 1 ) (k,t)=(3,1) , we present explicit constructions of sets in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  avoiding  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -flats for exponentially many sizes. These sets rely on carefully constructed binary linear codes, whose weight enumerators determine the size of the construction. 
    
 Keywords : affine space, linear code, unavoidable, weight enumerator 
  
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Consider the points of the  n n -dimensional affine space  AG ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AG}(n,2)  over the binary field  𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} , which also correspond to the elements of the vector space  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} . 
 
 
 Let  S ⊆ AG ⁡ ( n , 2 ) S\subseteq\mathrm{AG}(n,2)  be a point set of a fixed cardinality  m m . We investigate the intersections of  S S  with the  k k -dimensional affine subspaces of  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , referred to as  k k -flats . We say that  S S   induces  a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat if there exists a  k k -flat  F k ⊆ 𝔽 2 n F_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  such that  | S ∩ F k | = t |S\cap F_{k}|=t . Otherwise we say that  S S  is a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -avoider . 
 
 
 Our question is the following: for what quadruples  ( n , m , k , t ) (n,m;k,t)  does it hold that every  S ⊆ 𝔽 2 n S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  of size  m m  induces a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat? If this is true, we write  [ n , m ] → [ k , t ] [n,m]\to[k,t] . The question was posed by the author and Nagy  [ 10 ]  as a  q q -analogue of a graph-theoretical problem by Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós  [ 4 ] . Note that a  [ k , 0 ] [k,0] -avoider is also called a  blocking set  with respect to  k k -dimensional subspaces, which is an extensively covered topic in the projective and affine settings (see  [ 1 ]  for a survey). Also note that the corresponding question in the ternary field  𝔽 3 \mathbb{F}_{3}  contains the well-known  cap set problem   [ 3 ] , asking for the largest size of a subset in  𝔽 3 n \mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}  without a full line. This would correspond to  k = 1 k=1  and  t = 3 t=3 . 
 
 
 We defined the  ( n , k , t ) (n;k,t) -spectrum   Sp ⁡ ( n , k , t ) \Sp(n,k,t) , and its  density ,  ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) \rho(n;k,t) , by 
 

 
 
 Sp ⁡ ( n , k , t ) : = { 0 ≤ m ≤ 2 n : [ n , m ] → [ k , t ] } , ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) : = | Sp ⁡ ( n , k , t ) | 2 n . \begin{split}\Sp(n;k,t)&:=\{0\leq m\leq 2^{n}:[n,m]\to[k,t]\},\\
\rho(n;k,t)&:=\frac{|\Sp(n;k,t)|}{2^{n}}.\end{split} 
 
 
 
 
 Here, and throughout this paper, we assume that  ( n , k , t ) (n,k,t)  is a triple of integers with  n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 ,  1 ≤ k ≤ n 1\leq k\leq n  and  0 ≤ t ≤ 2 k 0\leq t\leq 2^{k} . 
 
 
 We presented the following main conjecture: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1   (Kovács, Nagy  [ 10 ] ) . 
 
 For every  k ≥ 1 k\geq 1  and  0 ≤ t ≤ 2 k 0\leq t\leq 2^{k} , we have 
 

 
 
 lim n → ∞ ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) = 1 . \lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n;k,t)=1. 
 
 
 
 
 
 The conjecture was shown true for  t ∈ { 0 , 2 k − 1 , 2 k } t\in\{0,2^{k-1},2^{k}\}  when  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , and for all  t t  when  k ∈ { 1 , 2 } k\in\{1,2\} . The main result used for this (apart from the basic cases) was a result similar to Szemerédi’s Cube Lemma (see  [ 15 , Corollary 2.1] ), which also follows from a work of Bonin and Qin  [ 2 ] , stating that for  m ≥ 5 2 ⋅ 2 n ⁡ ( 1 − 1 2 k − 1 ) m\geq\frac{5}{2}\cdot 2^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\right)} , we have  [ n , m ] → [ k , 2 k ] [n,m]\to[k,2^{k}] . The proof proceeded by picking the value  𝐝 ∈ 𝔽 2 n \mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  that appears the most commonly as a difference of two elements in  S S , and applying the statement inductively for the quotient space  𝔽 2 n / ⟨ 𝐝 ⟩ \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}/\left\langle\mathbf{d}\right\rangle . 
 
 
 We also showed that  lim inf ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) > 0 \liminf\rho(n;k,t)>0  when  t t  is of the form  2 a 2^{a}  or  3 ⋅ 2 a 3\cdot 2^{a}  for some integer  1 ≤ a ≤ k − 2 1\leq a\leq k-2 . The proof of the latter result was more involved, using the notion of  additive energy  defined by Tao and Vu  [ 17 ] , and bounding sizes of hypercube cuts based on a theorem of Hart  [ 7 ] . However,  1.1  stays open in these cases too. 
 
 
 Improving on an argument by Guruswami  [ 5 ] , we also used the probabilistic method to obtain so-called  [ k , c ] [k,c] -evasive  sets, defined as sets in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  intersecting every  k k -flat in at most  c c  points. Our result was the following: 
 
 
 Theorem 1.2 . 
 
 Let  k , c ∈ ℤ > 0 k,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}  with  c ≥ k + 1 c\geq k+1 . Then for some  K > 0 K>0 , there exists a  [ k , c ] [k,c] -evasive set in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  of size at least  K ⋅ 2 n ⁡ ( 1 − k c ) K\cdot 2^{n\left(1-\frac{k}{c}\right)}  when  n n  is sufficiently large. 
 
 
 
 As  [ k , c ] [k,c] -evasive sets are  [ k , t ] [k,t] -avoiders for every  t > c t>c , this theorem can easily be used to show that the number of sizes  m m  outside  Sp ⁡ ( n , k , t ) \Sp(n;k,t)  is exponentially large for each pair  ( k , t ) (k,t)  with  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3  and  ( k , t ) ≠ ( 3 , 4 ) (k,t)\neq(3,4) . For example, since the complement of any  [ 3 , 6 ] [3,6] -evasive set is a  [ 3 , 1 ] [3,1] -avoider, there exist  [ 3 , 1 ] [3,1] -avoiders of all sizes  2 n − K ⋅ 2 n / 2 ≤ m ≤ 2 n 2^{n}-K\cdot 2^{n/2}\leq m\leq 2^{n}  by  Theorem 1.2 . However, as these  [ k , t ] [k,t] -avoiders are obtained with a random method, there is no systematic way to describe them. 
 
 
 The research presented in this paper mainly concerns the smallest open case of  1.1 , which is  ( k , t ) = ( 3 , 1 ) (k,t)=(3,1) . Our main result is a construction of an explicit family of  [ 3 , 1 ] [3,1] -avoiders that still exhibit exponentially many different sizes. We call this the  code-based construction , which takes an arbitrary binary linear code as an input, and derives a  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoider (for fixed  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 ) whose size only depends on the weight enumerator of the code evaluated at certain inputs. 
 
 
 Let us make note of the following definitions. 
 
 
 Definition 1.3   (Weight of a codeword) . 
 
 In a binary linear code  C C , the  weight  of a codeword  𝐱 ∈ C \mathbf{x}\in C  is the number of coordinates of  𝐱 \mathbf{x}  equal to  1 1 . 
 
 
 
 Definition 1.4   (Weight enumerator) . 
 
 The  weight enumerator  of a binary code  C ⊆ 𝔽 2 ℓ C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}  is a bivariate polynomial  W C W_{C}  defined by 
 

 
 
 W C ​ ( x , y ) = ∑ w = 0 ℓ A w ​ x w ​ y ℓ − w , W_{C}(x,y)=\sum_{w=0}^{\ell}A_{w}x^{w}y^{\ell-w}, 
 
 
 
 
 where  ℓ \ell  is the length of  C C , and  A w A_{w}  is the number of codewords in  C C  having weight  w w  for each  0 ≤ w ≤ ℓ 0\leq w\leq\ell . 
 
 
 
 For further details on the coding-theoretical terminology used in the paper, see Subsection  2.1 . We now state our main results. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.5 . 
 
 Fix integers  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3  and  ℓ ≥ 1 \ell\geq 1 . Take a binary linear code  C ≤ 𝔽 2 ℓ C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell} . Then for  n = ℓ ⁡ ( k − 1 ) n=\ell(k-1) , there exists a  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoider  S S  in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  for which  | S | = 2 n − W C ​ ( 1 , 2 k − 1 − 1 ) |S|=2^{n}-W_{C}(1,2^{k-1}-1) . 
 
 
 
 Here the set  S S  is obtained using the code-based construction, which is detailed in Construction  4.1 . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.6 . 
 
 Suppose that  n ≥ 4 n\geq 4  is an even integer. Then the number of different sizes of  [ 3 , 1 ] [3,1] -avoiders in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  given by the code-based construction is at least  ( ⌊ ( n − 4 ) / 4 ⌋ ⌊ ( n − 4 ) / 8 ⌋ ) 1 / 3 ≥ c ⋅ 2 n / 12 n \binom{\left\lfloor(n-4)/4\right\rfloor}{\left\lfloor(n-4)/8\right\rfloor}^{1/3}\geq c\cdot\frac{2^{n/12}}{\sqrt{n}}  for some absolute constant  c > 0 c>0 . 
 
 
 
 Note that here we got at least  ≈ c n ⋅ 1.059 n \approx\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\cdot 1.059^{n}  different sizes, but as we will remark later, by the MacWilliams identity, the number of different sizes obtainable by this method is bounded above by  c ⋅ n ⋅ ( 3 ) n ≈ c ⋅ n ⋅ 1.732 n c\cdot n\cdot(\sqrt{3})^{n}\approx c\cdot n\cdot 1.732^{n} . 
 
 
 The outline of the paper is the following. In Section  2 , we set the basic notation and definitions. In Section  3 , we give examples of  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoiders that are built from affine subspaces using union and symmetric difference operations. We then describe a construction that uses the independent sets of a simple graph or a hypergraph. Then in Section  4 , we present our code-based construction, which provides a proof of Theorem  1.5 . We then present a coding-theoretical limitation that puts an upper bound on the number of possible sizes of  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoiders resulting from this construction. Finally, Section  5  is devoted to the proof of Theorem  1.6 . Here we introduce two transformations of linear codes that each increase the length of a code by two, and we apply a sequence of such transformations to obtain a family of exponentially many codes. Using a group-theoretical tool, we show that these codes give rise to  [ 3 , 1 ] [3,1] -avoiders of exponentially many sizes. 
 
 
 
 2  Notation and preliminaries 

 
 By convention, elements of  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  will be considered as  1 × n 1\times n  row vectors over  𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} . 
 
 
 Similarly to  [ 10 ] , we define the  k k -profile   pf k ⁡ ( S ) \pf_{k}(S)  of a set  S ⊆ 𝔽 2 n S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  as the set of all possible intersection sizes of  S S  and a  k k -flat of  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} . So  t ∉ pf k ⁡ ( S ) t\not\in\pf_{k}(S)  implies that  | S | ∉ Sp ⁡ ( n , k , t ) |S|\not\in\Sp(n;k,t) . We say that the  k k -profile of  S S  is  even  if all of its elements are even. 
 
 
 In a vector space, the direct sum of the two subspaces  A A  and  B B  is denoted by  A ⊕ B A\oplus B . 
 
 
 The complement of a set  S S  will be denoted by  S ¯ \overline{S} . Recall that the  symmetric difference  of the  a a  sets  S 1 , S 2 , … , S a S_{1},S_{2},...,S_{a} , denoted by  △ i = 1 a S i \mathop{\bigtriangleup}_{i=1}^{a}S_{i} , consists of those elements in  ⋃ i = 1 a S i \bigcup_{i=1}^{a}S_{i}  that appear in an odd number of sets among  S 1 , S 2 , … , S a S_{1},S_{2},...,S_{a} . When  a = 2 a=2  (so for the symmetric difference of two sets), we can also use the notation  S 1 ​ △ ​ S 2 S_{1}\triangle S_{2} . 
 
 
 We let  [ n ] = { 1 , … , n } [n]=\{1,...,n\} . If  A ⊆ [ n ] A\subseteq[n]  is an arbitrary subset, one can define its characteristic vector  𝟙 A \mathbbm{1}_{A} , which is a  0 0 - 1 1  sequence of length  n n . If  𝐯 = 𝟙 A \mathbf{v}=\mathbbm{1}_{A} , then for every  i ∈ [ n ] i\in[n] , 
 

 
 
 𝐯 i = { 1 if  i ∈ A , 0 if  i ∉ A . \mathbf{v}_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i\in A$,}\\
0&\text{if $i\not\in A$.}\\
\end{cases} 
 
 
 
 
 Similarly one can define the characteristic vector  𝟙 S \mathbbm{1}_{S}  for a subset  S ⊆ 𝔽 2 n S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , which is a  2 n 2^{n} -long  0 0 - 1 1  sequence with entries indexed by the vectors  𝐱 ∈ 𝔽 2 n \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} . 
 
 
 In this paper, an  alphabet  is a finite set  Σ \Sigma , whose elements are called  letters . For example,  Σ = { 𝚊 , 𝚋 } \Sigma=\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\} . If  r ≥ 0 r\geq 0 , then a sequence of  σ 1 ​ σ 2 ​ … ​ σ r \sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}...\sigma_{r}  of  r r  letters is called a  word  of length  r r , and the set of such words is denoted  Σ r \Sigma^{r} . We write  Σ ∗ = ⋃ r ≥ 0 Σ r \Sigma^{*}=\bigcup_{r\geq 0}\Sigma^{r}  for the set of all words over  Σ \Sigma . 
 
 
 We let  ℤ ≥ 0 \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}  and  ℤ > 0 \mathbb{Z}_{>0}  denote the set of nonnegative and positive integers, respectively. 
 
 
 2.1  Notions from coding theory 

 
 Let us recall the following basic notions from coding theory. A  binary linear code   C C  of length  ℓ \ell  and dimension  d d  is a linear subspace of  𝔽 2 ℓ \mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}  of dimension  d d . The elements of  C C  are called  codewords , and the  size  of the code is the number of codewords, which is equal to  2 d 2^{d} . 
 
 
 The dual code of  C C  is defined as  C ⟂ = { 𝐲 ∈ 𝔽 2 ℓ : 𝐲𝐜 T = 0 ​ ∀ 𝐜 ∈ C } C^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}:\mathbf{y}\mathbf{c}^{T}=0\penalty\ \forall\mathbf{c}\in C\} , i.e., it is the orthogonal complement of the subspace  C ≤ 𝔽 2 ℓ C\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell}  with respect to the standard inner product. Its dimension is  ℓ − d \ell-d . 
 
 
 A  generator matrix   G G  for  C C  is a  d × ℓ d\times\ell  matrix whose rows form a basis for  C C , and a  parity check matrix   H H  for  C C  is a generator matrix for  C ⟂ C^{\perp} , so it is an  ( ℓ − d ) × ℓ (\ell-d)\times\ell  matrix. If  H H  is a parity check matrix for  C C , then for every  𝐲 ∈ 𝔽 2 ℓ \mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\ell} , we have  𝐲 ∈ C ⇔ H ​ 𝐲 T = 𝟎 \mathbf{y}\in C\Leftrightarrow H\mathbf{y}^{T}=\mathbf{0} . 
 
 
 We recall the MacWilliams identity, which gives a link between the weight enumerator of a code and its dual: 
 
 
 Lemma 2.1 . 
 
 [MacWilliams identity  [ 12 , p. 127] ] For a binary linear code  C C  of dimension  d d , 
 

 
 
 W C ⟂ ​ ( x , y ) = 1 2 d ​ W C ​ ( y − x , y + x ) . W_{C^{\perp}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2^{d}}W_{C}(y-x,y+x). 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 2.2  Notions from group theory 

 
 The identity element of a group  G G  will be denoted  1 G 1_{G} . The notation  H ≤ G H\leq G  means that  H H  is a subgroup of  G G . The subgroup of  G G  generated by the two subgroups  H 1 , H 2 ≤ G H_{1},H_{2}\leq G  is denoted by  ⟨ H 1 , H 2 ⟩ \langle H_{1},H_{2}\rangle . 
 
 
 The free group over the generator set  S S  will be denoted by  F ⁡ ( S ) F(S) , and the free product of two groups  G 1 G_{1} ,  G 2 G_{2}  will be denoted by  G 1 ∗ G 2 G_{1}\ast G_{2} . 
 
 
 
 
 3  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoiders 

 
 Our first fundamental example of  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoiders (for  k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 ) is obtained from the following basic observations: 
 
 
 Observations 3.1 . 
 
 
 (a)  
 
 If  H H  is an  ( n − k + 1 ) (n-k+1) -flat of  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , then it has an even  k k -profile, so in particular  H H  is a  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoider. 
 
 (b)  
 
 The union of an arbitrary collection of  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoiders in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  is also a  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoider. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 
 (a)  
 
 If the affine subspace  H ⊆ 𝔽 2 n H\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  has dimension  n − k + 1 n-k+1 , then for any  k k -flat  F k ⊆ 𝔽 2 n F_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , either  H ∩ F k = ∅ H\cap F_{k}=\emptyset , or by the dimension theorem, the intersection is a flat of dimension  dim H + dim F k − dim ⟨ H , F k ⟩ = n + 1 − dim ⟨ H , F k ⟩ \dim H+\dim F_{k}-\dim\left\langle H,F_{k}\right\rangle=n+1-\dim\left\langle H,F_{k}\right\rangle . Here  ⟨ H , F k ⟩ ⊆ 𝔽 2 n \left\langle H,F_{k}\right\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  has dimension at most  n n , therefore the dimension of  H ∩ F k H\cap F_{k}  is at least 1, and so  | H ∩ F k | ≥ 2 |H\cap F_{k}|\geq 2  is an even power of two. 
 
 (b)  
 
 If  { S i : i ∈ I } \{S_{i}:i\in I\}  is a collection of  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoiders in  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} , and  S = ∪ i ∈ I S i S=\cup_{i\in I}S_{i} , then let  F k F_{k}  be an arbitrary  k k -flat. We show that  | F k ∩ S | = 1 |F_{k}\cap S|=1  cannot hold. Assume to the contrary that it does. Then there exists  i ∈ I i\in I  such that  F k ∩ S i ≠ ∅ F_{k}\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset . Take such an  i i  and let  | F k ∩ S i | = c |F_{k}\cap S_{i}|=c . Since  S i S_{i}  was a  [ k , 1 ] [k,1] -avoider, we must have  c ≥ 2 c\geq 2 . However  F k ∩ S i ⊆ F k ∩ S F_{k}\cap S_{i}\subseteq F_{k}\cap S , therefore  | F k ∩ S | ≥ c ≥ 2 |F_{k}\cap S|\geq c\geq 2  as well, giving a contradiction.∎ 
 
 
 
 
 
 Putting together these two observations, we obtain the following: 
 
 
 Corollary 3.2 .

 3.2  Reed-Muller code perspective

 Problem 3.6 .

 For  r = 1 r=1  this is an easy question to investigate using the affine space itself: a symmetric difference of a finite number of  ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -flats (halfspaces) is either  ∅ \emptyset , the full space  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}  or an  ( n − 1 ) (n-1) -flat.
