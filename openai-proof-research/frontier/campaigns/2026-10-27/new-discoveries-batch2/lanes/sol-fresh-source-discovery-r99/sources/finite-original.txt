 Abstract 
    
 We study the set of intersection sizes of a  k k -dimensional affine subspace and a point set of size  m ∈ [ 0 , 2 n ] m\in[0,2^{n}]  of the  n n -dimensional binary affine space  AG ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AG}(n,2) . Following the theme of Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós, we partially determine which local densities in  k k -dimensional affine subspaces are unavoidable in all  m m -element point sets in the  n n -dimensional affine space.
 We also show constructions of point sets for which the intersection sizes with  k k -dimensional affine subspaces takes values from a set of a small size compared to  2 k 2^{k} . These are built up from affine subspaces and so-called subspace evasive sets. Meanwhile, we improve the best known upper bounds on subspace evasive sets and apply results concerning the canonical signed-digit (CSD) representation of numbers. 
 Keywords : unavoidable, affine subspaces, evasive sets, random methods, canonical signed-digit number system. 
  
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 In this paper, we address the following general problem. Let  S S  be a subset of size  m m  of the affine space  AG ⁡ ( n , q ) \mathrm{AG}(n,q) . Does there always exist a  k k -dimensional affine subspace which contains exactly  t t  points of  S S ? A  k k -dimensional affine subspace will be referred to as a  k k - flat , and a  k k -flat which contains exactly  t t  points of a given set  S S  will be called a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat (induced by  S S ) . If for every  m m -element set  S S  in the  n n -dimensional affine space, there is a  k k -flat containing exactly  t t  points of  S S , then we say that the pair  [ n , m ] [n,m]  forces the pair  [ k , t ] [k,t] , or that  t t -sets are unavoidable in  k k -flats. We use the notation  [ n , m ] q → [ k , t ] [n,m]_{q}\rightarrow[k,t]  for this concept. 
 
 
 Our main focus will be the case  q = 2 q=2 , and we will omit the index  q q  except when we wish to refer to arbitrary finite fields. 
 
 
 The graph theoretic analogue of this problem was initiated by Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós  [ 11 ] . 
 
 
 Let  G ⁡ ( n , m ) G(n,m)  denote a graph on  n n  vertices and  m m  edges. Fix a positive integer  k k  and a pair of integers  ( n , m ) (n,m)  such that  0 ≤ m ≤ ( n 2 ) 0\leq m\leq\binom{n}{2} . For which  t t  does it hold that
any  n n -vertex graph with  m m  edges contains an induced subgraph on  k k  vertices having exactly  t t  edges? Equivalently, we are seeking pairs  ( k , t ) (k,t)  such that  k k -vertex induced subgraphs with  t t  edges are unavoidable in graphs of the form  G ⁡ ( n , m ) G(n,m) . Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós introduced the notation  ( n , m ) → ( k , t ) (n,m)\rightarrow(k,t)  for the case when this is true. 
 
 
 Their main result showed that forced pairs  ( k , t ) (k,t)  are rare in the following sense. Consider the set  S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) Sp(n;k,t)  of all edge cardinalities  m m  such that  ( n , m ) → ( k , t ) (n,m)\rightarrow(k,t) .
Its density is the ratio  | S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) | ( n 2 ) \frac{|Sp(n;k,t)|}{\binom{n}{2}} . They proved that the limit superior of this density is bounded from above by  2 / 3 2/3  apart from a handful cases, and is  0 0  for the majority of the pairs  ( k , t ) (k,t)  for fixed and large enough  k k . Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós
conjectured that in fact it is bounded from above by  1 / 2 1/2  apart from finitely many pairs  ( k , t ) (k,t) . This was confirmed recently by He, Ma and Zhao  [ 17 ] . Several related problems have been studied in the last couple of years  [ 1 ,  2 ,  7 ] . 
 
 
 Our problem can be viewed as the  q q -analogue of this problem, in which we investigate whether all subspaces of given dimension  k k  can avoid to have an intersection of size  t t  with an  m m -element set of the space  AG ⁡ ( n , q ) \mathrm{AG}(n,q) , which corresponds to  𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} . 
 
 
 Note that problems of similar flavour have gained significant interest in the past and are trending as well in recent years. The celebrated Sylvester-Gallai theorem showed that if a set of  m m  points in the Euclidean  2 2 -dimensional space is not collinear, then there will be a line which intersects the point set in exactly  2 2  points. 
 Considering the case  q = 3 q=3 ,  k = 1 k=1  and  t = 3 t=3 , we in turn get the famous cap set problem, which asks for the maximum number of points in  AG ⁡ ( n , 3 ) \mathrm{AG}(n,3)  without creating a line, or in other words, without containing a  3 3 -term arithmetic progression. There has been a recent breakthrough due to Ellenberg and Gijswijt  [ 10 ] , building upon the ideas of Croot, Lev and Pach  [ 9 ] , which showed that to avoid complete lines,  | S | / 3 n |S|/3^{n}  has to be exponentially small. The arithmetic removal lemma implies a supersaturation result on the number of complete lines  [ 14 ] , from which bounds on the case  [ 1 , 3 ] [1,3]  translate to similar bounds for the case of complete  k k -flats, i.e., cases  [ k , 3 k ] [k,3^{k}] . This connection highlights the complexity of the problem. 
 
 
 There has been significant interest in the case when we want to forbid each intersection of size larger than or equal to  t t  instead of avoiding only  t t -sets in  k k -flats. If there exists a set  S ⊆ AG ⁡ ( n , q ) S\subseteq\mathrm{AG}(n,q)  for which all  k k -dimensional affine subspaces contain at most  c c  points of  S S , then  S S  is called  ( k , c ) (k,c) -subspace evasive . The importance of such sets is highlighted by its connections to explicit constructions of bipartite Ramsey graphs by Pudlák and Rödl  [ 23 ]  and to list-decodable codes by Guruswami  [ 15 ] . 
 
 
 By a standard application of the first moment method, Guruswami obtained random subsets of  𝔽 n \mathbb{F}^{n}  of large size which are  ( k , c ) (k,c) -subspace evasive. (Here and throughout the paper,  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  denotes a finite field.) 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1   (Guruswami,  [ 15 ] ) . 
 
 For any fixed pair  ( k , c ) (k,c) , there exists a  ( k , c ) (k,c) -evasive set in  𝔽 n \mathbb{F}^{n}  of size at least  C ⋅ | 𝔽 | n ⁡ ( 1 − 2 ​ k c ) C\cdot|\mathbb{F}|^{n(1-\frac{2k}{c})} , where  C > 0 C>0  is a constant independent of  n n . 
 
 
 
 Corollary 1.2 . 
 
 For any fixed pair  ( k , t ) (k,t)  with  t > 1 t>1 , there exists a constant  C > 0 C>0  for which the following holds: if  m ≤ C ⋅ 2 n ⁡ ( 1 − 2 ​ k t − 1 ) m\leq C\cdot 2^{n(1-\frac{2k}{t-1})} , then  t t -sets are avoidable in the  k k -flats of  AG ⁡ ( n , 2 ) \mathrm{AG}(n,2) , i.e., in this case  [ n , m ] ↛ [ k , t ] [n,m]\not\rightarrow[k,t] . 
 
 
 
 Surprisingly it turned out that the obtained bounds are sharp in a weak sense. Improving the results of Ben-Aroya and Shinkar  [ 3 ] , Sudakov and Tomon proved the following. 
 
 
 Theorem 1.3   (Sudakov and Tomon,  [ 25 ] ) . 
 
 Let  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  be a field,  k ∈ ℤ + k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}  and  ε ∈ ( 0 , 0.05 ) \varepsilon\in(0,0.05) . If  n n  is sufficiently large with respect to  k k , and  S ⊆ 𝔽 n S\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{n}  has size  m ≥ | 𝔽 | n ⁡ ( 1 − ε ) m\geq|\mathbb{F}|^{n(1-\varepsilon)} , then  S S  is not  ( k , k − log 2 ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) 8 ​ ε ) (k,\frac{k-\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)}{8\varepsilon}) -subspace evasive. 
 
 
 
 Note however that  S S  being non- ( k , c ) (k,c) -evasive does not necessarily imply that  S S  contains a  k k -flat with  c + 1 c+1  points, except when  c = | 𝔽 | n − 1 . c=|\mathbb{F}|^{n}-1.  The latter case on the other hand is not covered by the theorem, as  k − log 2 ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) 8 ​ ε < 2 k − 3 \frac{k-\log_{2}(1/\varepsilon)}{8\varepsilon}<2^{k-3}  holds for every  ε ∈ ( 0 , 0.05 ) \varepsilon\in(0,0.05) . 
 
 
 1.1  Our main results 

 
 Similarly to the investigation in  [ 11 ] , we define the set of forcing sizes  m m  with respect to  [ k , t ] [k,t] , which are the sizes for which a  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flat is unavoidable, as follows. 
 
 
 Definition 1.4 . 
 
 

 
 
 S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) := { m : [ n , m ] q → [ k , t ] } Sp(n;k,t):=\{m:[n,m]_{q}\rightarrow[k,t]\} 
 
 
 is called the  set of forcing sizes with respect to  n , k , t n,k,t , and we refer to it as the  ( n , k , t ) (n;k,t) -spectrum .
 
 

 
 
 ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) := | S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) | | 𝔽 | n \rho(n;k,t):=\frac{|Sp(n;k,t)|}{|\mathbb{F}|^{n}}

 (We will always assume that  k ≤ n k\leq n , even if it is not specifically mentioned.) 
 
 
 Our aim is to characterize the spectra or at least bound the density of the spectra for various values of  k k  and  t t . Note that from now on,  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  is considered to be the binary field. 
 
 
 In their paper  [ 11 ] , Erdős, Füredi, Rothschild and T. Sós construct some graph families which only induce  k k -vertex subgraphs of specific sizes. These families are the combination of graphs which are very dense (cliques, or graphs close to cliques in edit distance) and sparse graphs (forests, or graphs with high girth). 
 
 
 In that aspect, we follow their footsteps and provide point sets which are combinations of disjoint full flats and point sets which hit every  k k -flat in only a small number of points. 
 
 
 These constructions show that the binary representation of  m m  and  t t  will be key in our results concerning sizes  m m  not in the spectrum.

 If  0 < t < 2 k 0<t<2^{k}  then  S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) Sp(n;k,t)  will not contain those values  m < 2 n m<2^{n}  which have fewer  1 1 -digits than the number of  1 1 -digits of  t t  in the binary representation, denoted by  s 2 ​ ( t ) s_{2}(t) . Since  S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) Sp(n;k,t)  cannot contain  2 n 2^{n}  either, this means that at least 
 

 
 
 1 + ∑ i = 0 s 2 ​ ( t ) − 1 ( n i ) 1+\sum_{i=0}^{s_{2}(t)-1}\binom{n}{i} 
 
 
 values are missing from the  ( n , k , t ) (n;k,t) -spectrum. 
 
 
 
 In fact, another numeral system, the so-called canonical signed-digit binary representation of integers will also have a central role. We formulate the corresponding Proposition  3.10  in Section  3 , which is the analogue of Theorem  1.5 . 
 
 
 These results can be combined with the former results on  ( k , c ) (k,c) -subspace evasive sets. Indeed, if we take a point set  S 0 S_{0}  which avoids  k k -dimensional subspaces having intersection of size  r r  for all  r ∈ [ t 1 , t 2 ] r\in[t_{1},t_{2}] 
with  c ≤ t 2 − t 1 c\leq t_{2}-t_{1} , then the union of  S 0 S_{0}  and a  ( k , c ) (k,c) -subspace evasive set  S 1 S_{1}  will avoid  k k -dimensional subspaces with intersections of size  t ∈ [ t 1 + c , t 2 ] t\in[t_{1}+c,t_{2}] .

 Conjecture 1.11 . 
 
 

 
 
 lim n → ∞ ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) = 1 . \lim_{n\to\infty}\rho(n;k,t)=1.

 2  Preliminaries, small cases for  k k 

 
 2.1  Notations and tools 

 
 For a pair of integers  a ≤ b a\leq b ,  [ a , b ] [a,b]  will be used to refer to the set  { a , a + 1 , … , b } ⊆ ℤ \{a,a+1,...,b\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z} .
We use the standard additive combinatorial notation for the sumset  A + B A+B  of two subsets  A A  and  B B  of an abelian group  G G  as the set of all sums of an element from  A A  with an element from  B B , i.e.,  A + B = { a + b : a ∈ A , b ∈ B } A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\} .  6  Concluding remarks 

 
 For general pairs  ( k , t ) (k,t) , it remained open to decide whether the density of the spectrum  ρ ⁡ ( n , k , t ) = S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) 2 n \rho(n;k,t)=\frac{Sp(n;k,t)}{2^{n}}  tends to  1 1  as  n n  tends to infinity, or even to obtain a general constant lower bound on this density for a given pair  ( k , t ) (k,t)  with  0 ≤ t ≤ 2 k 0\leq t\leq 2^{k} . The proof of Theorem  1.10  suggests that such a result might be deduced from a supersaturation type result on  [ k , t ] [k,t] -flats, similar to the lower bound on  F 2 , 3 F_{2,3}  derived from Proposition  5.2 . 
 
 
 We did not elaborate on the case  q > 2 q>2 . However, note that the approach we used to exclude several values from the spectra  S ​ p ​ ( n , k , t ) Sp(n;k,t)  applies for  q > 2 q>2  as well. Indeed, we can construct point sets as a
combination of disjoint full flats and point sets which hit every  k k -flat in small number of points, i.e., evasive sets. However, proving density results on the spectrum appear to be much harder, as it was the case even for  q = 3 q=3  with lines (or full flats), as the general problem contains the determination of the order of magnitude of the maximum size of  q q -AP-free sets of  𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} . 
 
 
 While we focused on the sets in  AG ⁡ ( n , q ) \mathrm{AG}(n,q) ,  q = 2 q=2 , we mention that the analogous question was investigated by Ihringer and Verstraëte  [ 19 ]  for point sets  X X  in a projective space  PG ⁡ ( n , q ) \mathrm{PG}(n,q)  where all  k k -dimensional subspaces must have a bounded number of points. However their results concern the case when  q q  is large.
