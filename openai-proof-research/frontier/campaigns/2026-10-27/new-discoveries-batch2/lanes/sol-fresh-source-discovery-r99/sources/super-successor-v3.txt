                                                                                       Abstract
                                                     The dominating set problem (DSP) is one of the most famous problems in
                                                 combinatorial optimization. It is defined as follows. For a given simple graph
                                                 G = (V, E), a dominating set of G is a subset S ⊆ V such that every vertex in
                                                 V \ S is adjacent to at least one vertex in S. Furthermore, the DSP is the problem
                                                 of finding a minimum-size dominating set and the corresponding minimum size,
                                                 the domination number of G.
                                                     In this, work we investigate a variant of the DSP, the super dominating set
                                                 problem (SDSP), which has attracted much attention during the last years. A
                                                 dominating set S is called a super dominating set of G, if for every vertex u ∈ S =
                                                 V \ S, there exists a v ∈ S such that N (v) ∩ S = {u}. Analogously, the SDSP is to
                                                 find a minimum-size super dominating set, and the corresponding minimum size,
                                                 the super domination number of G. The decision variants of both the DSP and
                                                 the SDSP have shown to be N P-hard.
                                                     In this paper, we present tight bounds for the super domination number of
                                                 the neighbourhood corona product, r-gluing, and the Hajós sum of two graphs.
                                                 Additionally, we present infinite families of graphs attaining our bounds. Finally,
                                                 we give the exact number of minimum size super dominating sets for some graph
                                                 classes. In particular, the number of super dominating sets for cycles has quite
                                                                                                                           2
                                                 surprising properties as it varies between values of the set {4, n, 2n, 5n −10n
                                                                                                                             8   } based
                                                 on n mod 4.

1    Introduction
Notations and definitions. Let G = (V, E) be a graph with vertex set V and edge
set E. Throughout this paper, we consider finite undirected graphs without loops. For
each vertex v ∈ V , the set N (v) = NG (v) = {u ∈ V | uv ∈ E} refers to the open
neighbourhood of v in G and the set N [v] = NG [v] = NG (v) ∪ {v} refers to the closed
neighbourhood of v in G. If the graph G is clear from the context, we will omit the
corresponding index G. The degree of v is the cardinality of N (v). Throughout this
paper, for a set S ⊆ V (G), the expression S always stands for V (G) \ S.
    A set S ⊆ V is called a dominating set if every vertex in S = V \ S is adjacent to at
least one vertex in S. The domination number γ(G) is the cardinality of a minimum
size dominating set in G. For a detailed treatment of domination theory, we refer the
reader to [11].
    A dominating set S of G is called a super dominating set of G, if for every vertex
u ∈ S, there exists a v ∈ S such that N (v) ∩ S = {u}. In this case, we say that v
super dominates u. The super domination number G is the cardinality of a minimum
size super dominating set of G, denoted by γsp (G) [14]. We refer the reader to [1, 4, 5,
6, 12, 13, 17] for more details on super dominating sets of a graph. Some applications
can be found in [14].
    In this work, we often call vertices “black” (abbreviated by “B”) if they are con-
tained in a given super dominating set and “white” (abbreviated “W”) if they are not
contained in it.


Graph classes. We will consider the following well-known graph classes: path graph,
cycle graph, star graph, complete graph, complete bipartite graph. A friendship graph
Fn is a collection of n triangles, where all triangles have one vertex, the central vertex,
in common (see Figure 1).
    Given two simple graphs G1 and G2 , the corona product of G1 and G2 , denoted
by G1 ◦ G2 is the graph arising from the disjoint union of G1 with |V (G1 )| copies of
G2 , by adding edges between the i-th vertex of G1 and all vertices of the i-th copy
of G2 [10]. The neighbourhood corona product of G1 and G2 , denoted by G1 ⋆ G2 , is
the graph obtained by taking one copy of G1 and |V (G1 )| copies of G2 and joining the
neighbours of the i-th vertex of G1 to every vertex in the i-th copy of G2 [8]. Thus,
this graph has |V (G1 )| · (|V (G2 )| + 1) vertices. Figure 2 shows C4 ⋆ K3 , where Cn is
the cycle of order n and Kn is the complete graph of order n. For more results on the
neighbourhood corona product of two graphs, we refer the reader to [2, 7, 15].

    Besides being studied in exact graph classes, super domination has also been stud-
ied for different graph products. In particular, Dettlaff et al. [4] have studied the super
domination number of lexicographic products and joins and also shown that determin-
ing the super domination number of a graph is N P-hard. Klein et al. [13] have studied
Cartesian products and (usual) corona products.

Our results. In this paper, we continue the study of the super domination number of
a graph, started in [6, 7]. First in Section 3, we present a key lemma which will be used
throughout this paper. In Section 3, we find the exact value of the super domination
number of the neighbourhood corona product of two graphs. In Section 4, we present
tight lower and upper bounds on the r-gluing of two graphs and provide infinite families
of graphs attaining these bounds. We study the super domination number of the Hajós
sum of two graphs and find tight upper and lower bounds for it, together with infinite
families of examples attaining the bounds, in Section 5. Finally, in Section 6, we count
exactly the number of minimum size super dominating sets of some graph classes.


2    Key lemma
We introduce a technical key lemma for analysing super dominating sets. It will be
needed in most of the following results.
Lemma 2.1 Let S be a super dominating set in a graph G.
 (a) Then there is a super dominating set S ′ with same cardinality with S ⊆ S ′ and
     S ′ ⊆ S.
      Furthermore, there is a bijective function f : S ′ → S so that f (a) = b holds if and
      only if a super dominates b for the super dominating set S and b super dominates
      a for the super dominating set S ′ .

                                                  5
 (b) Let D = S ∩ S ′ . Then V (G) can be partitioned as V (G) = S ∪˙ S ′ ∪˙ D, where it
     holds that S = S ′ ˙∪ D, S ′ = S ∪˙ D.
 (c) Let S have cardinality |V (G)|/2. Then S is a super dominating set with the same
     cardinality and it holds that S = S ′ and S ′ = S.
       Furthermore, each vertex in S super dominates exactly one vertex in S and vice
       versa, i.e., the function f : S → S ′ from (a) is uniquely determined.

Proof.
 (a) Let S ⊆ V (G) be a super dominating set in G. Then for each b ∈ S there exists a
     vertex a ∈ S such that N (a) ∩ S = {b}. We construct the new super dominating
     set S ′ by replacing each a ∈ S by the corresponding b ∈ S. Denote A ⊆ S as
     the set of all vertices a removed from S during this process. Let f : A → S
     be the corresponding function with f (a) = b for each a ∈ A and b ∈ S super
     dominated by a. By construction, A = S ′ , |S| = |S ′ | and |S| = |S ′ | hold. As each
     vertex a ∈ S ′ can super dominate only one vertex b ∈ S, f is well defined. By
     construction, for each b we have only one a, i.e., f is also injective. Because of
     |S ′ | = |S|, it is also bijective.
       Claim: S ′ is a super dominating set in G.
       Proof (Claim): Let f (a) = b with a ∈ S ′ , b ∈ S. Assume that b does not
       super dominate a. Then b is also adjacent to another a′ ∈ S ′ . Thus, there is a
       b′ ∈ S with f (a′ ) = b′ . This would mean that a′ super dominates b′ , but it is also
       adjacent to b, which is a contradiction. Thus, b super dominates a, and the claim
       follows.
 (b) This follows easily from (a).
    (c) Here D = S ∩ S ′ = ∅ holds. From (b) it follows that S = S ′ and S ′ = S.
        Since each vertex can super dominate at most one other vertex, each vertex in S
        (and each vertex in S ′ ) super dominates exactly one vertex. Thus, f is uniquely
        determined.                                                                    


3      Super domination number of neighbourhood corona prod-
       uct of two graphs
In this section, we study the super domination number of the neighbourhood corona
product of two graphs. Let G and H be two graphs of orders n, m ∈ N, respectively.
It is clear that

                              γsp (G ⋆ H) ≤ γsp (G) + nm,                                (1)

since if we consider all vertices of all copies of H in our super dominating set, then we
only need to find a super dominating set for G and the claim follows by the definition
of a super dominating set.

                                              6
     Later (in Corollary 3.4) we will show that, under some conditions, it holds that
γsp (G⋆H) = n(γsp (H)+1). The following proposition shows that this is an improvement
over the trivial upper bound from (1) for m ≥ 2.

Proposition 3.1 Let G and H be two connected graphs of orders n and m 6= 1, re-
spectively. Then
                        n(γsp (H) + 1) < γsp (G) + nm.

Proof. By Theorem 1.1, we know that γsp (H) ≤ m − 1. So we have n(γsp (H) + 1) =
nγsp (H) + n ≤ nm. Hence, we have n(γsp (H) + 1) < nm + γsp (G) and therefore, we
have the result.                                                                
   Next we present a tight upper bound for γsp (G ⋆ H).

Theorem 3.2 Let G and H be two connected graphs of orders n, m ∈ N. Then

                             γsp (G ⋆ H) ≤ n(γsp (H) + 1).

Proof. Let SH be a super dominating set for H. We create a set S for G⋆H by placing
all vertices of G in S. Next, for each copy of H, we place all vertices corresponding to
SH in S. In the following, we show that S is a super dominating set for G ⋆ H.
    Let u ∈ S. Then u ∈ V (H ′ ) for some copy of H. Let v ∈ V (H ′ ) super dominate
u in H ′ . If v does not super dominate u in G ⋆ H, then v has another neighbour in
S ∩ (V (G ⋆ H) \ V (H ′ )). However, this is not possible, since V (G) ⊆ S and there are
no edges between different copies of H. Thus, S is a super dominating set for G ⋆ H
with cardinality nγsp (H) + n = n(γsp (H) + 1) and the assertion follows.             
     In the following theorem, we show that the upper bound in Theorem 3.2 is actually
the super domination number of G ⋆ H, when G and H 6= K1 are connected graphs and
γsp (H) < m − 1 or it holds that H = Km .

Theorem 3.3 Let G and H be two connected graphs of orders n and m 6= 1, respec-
tively, where it holds that γsp (H) < m − 1 or it holds that H = Km . Then

                             n(γsp (H) + 1) ≤ γsp (G ⋆ H).

set for G ⋆ H. Let Hw beSthe copy of H corresponding to w ∈ V (G). Then it holds
that V (G ⋆ H) = V (G) ∪ w∈V (G) V (Hw ).
Claim 1: In each copy of H, the set S has at least γsp (H) vertices.
Proof (Claim 1): For a given w ∈ V (G), consider the copy Hw of H. Assume
that r := |S ∩ V (Hw )| < γsp (H). As H is connected, it follows by Theorem 1.1 that
r < γsp (H) ≤ m − 1. Thus, this copy of H has at least two vertices which are contained
in S.
    As S ∩ V (Hw ) is not a super dominating set of Hw , there must exist a vertex
u ∈ S ∩ V (Hw ), such that there does not exist a vertex v ∈ S ∩ V (Hw ) for which

   As v ∈ V (G ⋆ H) \ V (Hw ) holds, we have two possibilities. Either v ∈ V (G) or

     In the following, for a given w ∈ V (G), we define a block of vertices Bw (G ⋆ H) =
{w} ∪ V (Hw ) (or shortly Bw ). The blocks Bw clearly partition the vertex set V (G ⋆ H).
     For the given super dominating set S, we define a block as over-satisfied, if it has
more than γsp (H) + 1 vertices (note that for m = 1, i.e., H = K1 , a block can never be
over-satisfied), as satisfied, if it has exactly γsp (H) + 1 vertices, and as under-satisfied,
if it has less than γsp (H) + 1 vertices. Note that by Claim 1, an under-satisfied block
Bw has always exactly γsp (H) vertices in S and that w lies in S.
Claim 2: Let w ∈ V (G) and let Bw be under-satisfied. Let x ∈ V (G) \ {w} be chosen
so that a vertex in Bx super dominates w ∈ V (G) (and {w, x} ∈ E(G)). Then Bx is
an over-satisfied block.
Proof (Claim 2): As we have mentioned above, in an under-satisfied block Bw there
are exactly γsp (H) vertices in S and w ∈ S. Thus, at least one vertex y ∈ V (Hw ) lies
in S.
    First, assume that x ∈ S. On the one hand, each vertex z ∈ V (Hw ) adjacent to y
is also adjacent to x, i.e., z cannot super dominate y. On the other hand, each vertex
z∈ / V (Hw ) adjacent to y is also adjacent to w, i.e., z cannot super dominate y either. In
summary, y cannot be super dominated by another vertex of S. Thus, by contradiction
x ∈ S follows.
    We continue by dividing the proof into two cases.

Case 1: γsp (H) < m − 1.
      Assume that there are b, c ∈ V (Hx ) ∩ S. Each vertex z ∈ V (Hx ), adjacent to b or
      c, is also adjacent to w, and if z 6∈ V (Hx ) is adjacent to b, then it is also adjacent
      to c and vice versa. So neither b nor c can be super dominated by another vertex.
      Thus, there are at least γsp (H) + 1 vertices in V (Hx ) ∩ S, and Bx is over-satisfied.

Case 2: H = Km .
      Assume that there is a b ∈ V (Hx ) ∩ S. By the choice of x ∈ V (G), one vertex of
      Bx super dominates w. This cannot be x, as x is also adjacent to y. On the other
      hand, as H = Km , each other vertex in Bx super dominating w is also adjacent
      to b, a contradiction. Thus, there does not exist such b, and Bx is over-satisfied.

4    Super domination number of r-gluing of two graphs
In this section, we give exact upper and lower bounds for the super domination number
of r-gluing of two graphs.
    Since for every two graphs G1 and G2 , G1 ∪K0 G2 is their disjoint union, by Propo-
sition 1.4, we have the following result:

            γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ) − 0 ≤ γsp (G1 ∪K0 G2 ) ≤ γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ).

Also G1 ∪K1 G2 is same as the chain of two graphs and by Theorem 1.5, we have the
following result:

            γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ) − 1 ≤ γsp (G1 ∪K1 G2 ) ≤ γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ).

In Theorem 4.1, we consider the r-gluing of two graphs, and we generalize this result
for each r-clique.

Theorem 4.1 Let G1 = (V1 , E1 ) and G2 = (V2 , E2 ) be two graphs with clique number
at least r ∈ N. Then,

            γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ) − r ≤ γsp (G1 ∪Kr G2 ) ≤ γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ).

Proof. Let S1 and S2 be two minimum size super dominating sets for G1 and G2 ,
respectively. Let the vertex sets V ′ = {vi′ | 1 ≤ i ≤ r} and V ′′ = {vi′′ | 1 ≤ i ≤ r}
form r-cliques in G1 and G2 , respectively. Furthermore, let us create a vertex wi by

                                              10
identifying the vertices vi′ and vi′′ for each i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , r} (see Figure 7 for the case
r = 2) and denote W = {wi | 1 ≤ i ≤ r}. Denote by G the graph which we obtain in
this way. When r = 1, the claim follows from Theorem 1.5. Hence, we assume from
now on that r ≥ 2. We divide the proof into two parts, namely the lower and the upper
bound.
Lower bound γsp (G1 ) + γsp (G2 ) − r ≤ γsp (G1 ∪Kr G2 ).
We use Lemma 2.1 to show that we can assume two cases for the minimum size super
dominating set S:

   • W ⊆ S,

   • |W ∩ S| = r − 1 and one vertex of W , say w1 , super dominates the missing vertex,
     say wr .

To show this, assume that |S ∩ W | ≤ r − 1 and, without loss of generality, wr ∈ / S. By
Lemma 2.1, there is another minimum size super dominating set S ′ , the corresponding
set D = S ∩ S ′ and a bijective function f : S ′ → S with the mentioned characteristics.
If no vertex of W ∩ S super dominates another vertex, then W ∩ S ⊆ D follows, and
we can replace S by S ′ , where W ⊆ S ′ holds. On the other hand, if W * S and one
vertex of W ∩ S super dominates another vertex, then |W ∩ S| = r − 1 and, without
loss of generality, w1 super dominates the missing vertex wr . This finishes the proof of
this assumption. We split our considerations into three subcases.
    Here we split our considerations into two subcases.

  (i) W ⊆ S.
      Let

7      Conclusions and future work
In this paper, we obtained tight results on the super domination number of graphs,
particularly on the neighbourhood corona product, r-gluing and Hajós sum of two
graphs. For each of these, we presented tight lower and upper bounds together with
constructions attaining these upper bounds. Moreover, we gave the exact number of
minimum size super dominating sets of some graph classes such as paths and cycles.
Finally, we provide the following suggestions for future research.

 (a) The formula γsp (G ⋆ H) = n(γsp (H) + 1) of Corollary 3.4 does not cover the case
     H = K1 . Thus, it would be interesting to compute γsp (G ⋆ K1 ). By Eq. (1),
     γsp (G ⋆ K1 ) ≤ γsp (G) + n is a (trivial) upper bound, but it is tight only for some
     graphs G.

 (b) We found a connection for the super domination number between the usual corona
     product of two graphs [13] and the neighbourhood corona product of two graphs.
     Does a generalization exist for the corona products that preserve the result of
     Corollary 3.4?

    (c) Give general upper and lower bounds for the number of minimum size super
        dominating sets with help of γsp (G) and possibly some other graph parameters.

 (d) Count the number of (minimum size) super dominating sets in trees.
