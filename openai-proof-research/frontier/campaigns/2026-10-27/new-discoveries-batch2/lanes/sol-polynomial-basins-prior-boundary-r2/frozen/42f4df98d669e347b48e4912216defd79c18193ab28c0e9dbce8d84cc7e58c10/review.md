## SOURCE

I read all six pages of the actual JIS 2013 body, both pages of arXiv:1301.0517v1, and all five pages of arXiv:1505.03237v1 through frozen extracted texts. I independently regenerated each extract from the frozen PDF; all three match the supplied extracts byte for byte. Exact bytes, URLs and coverage are in provenance.json. Journal Q10 is on printed p4; the precursor's unnumbered question is on p2. The question was public on 2013-01-03, before the journal publication on 2013-10-12. The follow-up v1 is dated 2015-05-13.

The journal asks for two different fixed points and a sufficiently high iterate modulo every prime assigning the endpoint according to whether the initial first coordinate is zero. The natural substantive reading prescribes two basins, one for each initial class. It quantifies over all initial points of affine space over F_p, not a selected subvariety. There is no fixed dimension, dominance assumption, or bound on iteration time across primes. For each fixed prime, point-dependent absorption is enough for this proof; a common time also follows by finiteness and fixedness. The two branches may be interchanged, even differently at different primes.

Important precision: the printed question does not separately say the two fixed points remain distinct modulo every prime. r237's assertion that the source explicitly requires that is too strong as a textual claim. This does **not** invalidate the negative answer for fixed distinct integral points: a nonzero integer coordinate difference has only finitely many prime divisors, so the reductions differ outside a finite set. The same argument works for rational points after also excluding denominator primes. Thus the finite-exception theorem resolves the literal original under its usual meaning of fixed points admitting reduction, even if finitely many reductions coincide. Arbitrarily varying distinct F_p targets is a stronger theorem. The source supplies no definition of reducing arbitrary nonrational complex fixed points; I do not claim to resolve an invented interpretation of that phrase.

Two different fixed points without the basin prescription are not ruled out (the identity is an immediate example). Nor does the proof rule out maps whose final branch depends on some other property of the initial point. Allowing both initial classes to choose the same target is not the intended two-basin problem. Distinct target points themselves, if present in the finite field and included among all initial points, cannot both be absorbed into just one of them.

The 2013 proofs were checked, not just the question. Theorem2's additive trap evolves the ratio y/x by addition of 1 while defined and kills the orbit at a coordinate zero. Its first output x²y has a zero with x nonzero. Theorem4's multiplicative trap advances the ratio by multiplication by n and needs the stated generator/divisibility conditions. Theorem6's power trap advances it by its nth power and the collapse condition is the prime-factor restriction on p−1. Theorem8 takes direct sums and intersects nilpotency loci. None preserves the requested partition into two basins.

The 2015 Theorem1 uses periodic geometric-point density over extensions; it is not a prime-field two-basin theorem. Definitions2/3 distinguish a single contracting iterate of a subvariety from pointwise contraction of geometric points. Theorems5/7/9 homogenize traps on selected subvarieties, with Pollard-style synchronization, additive translation, or Fibonacci recursion. They contract to one point, without the prescribed initial coordinate partition. Q12 on p5 asks about several fixed image points for geometric points of a geometrically irreducible Y; neither the all-prime-field hypothesis nor the prescribed hyperplane/complement basins is its hypothesis. r237 does not settle Q12. Extraction has ratio/fraction layout artifacts and Theorem9's prose says uk=0 where the multiplicative identity uk=1 is intended; I do not rely on that typo as a target or purported resolution.

## MATH: independent reconstruction

1. Fix a good prime. Write H={x1=0}, U=its complement. If the eventual fixed targets A and B are distinct, an orbit starting in H cannot move into U: its shifted orbit would have eventual target B and the original target A. The reverse crossing is likewise impossible. This uses the hypothesis for **every** initial point, so it applies to the image point, and uses fixedness to compare endpoints at a common time. Thus P(a,b)=0 iff a=0 for the first coordinate P of F. This is a valid one-step invariant deduction, not an unproved assumption about the map's algebraic form.

2. For every integer tuple b, the integer P(0,b) vanishes modulo infinitely many primes, hence is zero. Induction using the univariate root bound proves that a rational polynomial vanishing on the integer lattice is identically zero. So x divides P. P is nonzero, since evaluation at (1,0) is nonzero at every good prime. Factor the maximum power x^k, with k≥1, leaving H not divisible by x.

3. If H depends on a remaining variable yi, its leading coefficient in yi is a nonzero polynomial in the other variables, including x. The nonzero polynomial x times that leading coefficient has a nonzero value at an integer tuple. Hence specialize with x=a≠0 and nonzero leading coefficient; f(yi) is still nonconstant. Schur's prime-divisor argument produces infinitely many primes dividing **nonzero** values of f. Exclude the finite bad-prime set and the prime divisors of a. The resulting root of H modulo such a prime gives a zero of P with x≠0, contradiction. This treats each possible extra variable; dimension one simply skips this step.

4. Now H=h(x). Maximality of k says h(0)≠0. If h is nonconstant, choose a prime divisor of a nonzero h(m) outside the bad set and outside divisors of h(0). Then m mod p cannot be zero (otherwise p divides h(0)). This again contradicts the preservation condition. Thus P=c x^k, c≠0. Every prime factor of c must be exceptional, by evaluating at x=1. This is an identity over Z obtained using varying primes, not an invalid lift of a single finite-field zero set to a polynomial identity.

5. For completeness, Schur here needs no irreducibility or prime-values hypothesis. If S were the finite set of prime divisors of all nonzero f(m), choose A=f(t0)≠0 and M=product(S). Expansion gives g(t)=f(t0+AMt)/A in Z[t], with constant 1 and other coefficients divisible by M. Nonconstancy provides |g(t)|>1. A prime divisor r of g(t) lies outside S, yet divides the nonzero f(t0+AMt). This is a contradiction. In particular zeros among some polynomial values cause no gap.

6. Choose an odd prime q not dividing k, and N=q product(E), with E any finite exceptional set including prime divisors of c. A prime divisor p of S=1+N+...+N^(q−1)>1 divides neither N nor q, since S≡1 at every prime dividing N. Thus p∉E. N^q≡1 mod p, but N≢1 mod p, since the latter would imply p divides q. Since q is prime, z=N mod p has order q and q divides p−1. This directly supplies z; the extra appeal to cyclicity/Cauchy in r237 is valid but unnecessary. Existence of odd q outside divisors of k follows from infinitude of primes. No Dirichlet theorem or unproved distribution conjecture is needed.

7. Start at (1,0,...,0) and (z,0,...,0). Since c≠0 mod p, both first-coordinate sequences stay nonzero. Induction gives v_t/u_t=z^(k^t). Because q∤k, q∤k^t for every t≥0, so the ratio is never 1. Both points start in U, hence eventually reach B. At max(their two absorption times), fixedness makes the full points, hence their first coordinates, equal: contradiction.

Boundary audit: k=1 gives a constant nontrivial ratio and works; dimension one works; p=2 alone has a singleton nonzero first-coordinate set and provides no contradiction, but the chosen p satisfies q|(p−1) with q odd, so p≠2. All finite exceptional primes are avoided constructively. Negative c and c=±1 are harmless; c=0 was excluded. Fixed points may depend on p and the argument never compares them across primes. No equality of their first coordinates is assumed until the two U orbits have reached the same full point. No uniform absorption bound across primes is used.

The runnable checker.py separately tests 36 small (k,E) constructions with several c and finite time steps, a complete 729-polynomial coefficient box, and a single-prime nonmonomial diagnostic. All pass. These checks are diagnostics only; the argument above supplies the universal proof.

## SUBSTANTIVE and NOVELTY

I do not recommend routine exclusion on the evidence obtained. Elementary tools alone are not a complete exclusion rationale. Here the critical assembled bridge is: exact basin specification forces a one-step hyperplane equivalence; Schur plus a carefully nondegenerate specialization rigidifies a multivariate coordinate to a monomial; prime-order ratios obstruct synchronization even with unrestricted auxiliary coordinates. That bridge is fully proved above. It is a short, elementary solution to the natural full question, not a small parameter witness, a certificate of a known answer, or merely failure of the author's example.

Closest actual classical bodies inspected:

* Min Sha, arXiv:1101.3482v2, Introduction and §3 Proposition3.1 with its proof (pp1–4). For the pure power map on units modulo p^m, the periodic-point count is the part of p^(m−1)(p−1) coprime to k. At m=1 this gives the familiar obstruction to all unit orbits of x↦x^k collapsing to 1. This accounts for the finite multiplicative dynamics ingredient. It does not classify the multivariate first coordinate from the basin hypothesis, and does not by itself handle the factor c. Ratios of two orbits remove c; that additional bridge is explicitly supplied here.
* Guralnick–Rosenberg–Zieve, arXiv:0707.1837v2, pp1–4 through Corollary1.3: definition of exceptional polynomial, monodromy Theorem1.1, new-family Theorem1.2 and classification Corollary1.3. These concern polynomials giving bijections over infinitely many extensions of a fixed finite field and separable indecomposable exceptional polynomials. They do not assume or conclude preservation of the unique zero over almost all varying prime fields. The hypothesis here does not imply a permutation polynomial: x² preserves zero/nonzero at every prime but is not a permutation at any odd prime. Consequently their classification cannot simply be invoked as the missing monomial lemma. I have not read or cleared all proofs and cited predecessors of that classification.
* Borisov's own power-trap Theorem6 already displays the smooth-order obstruction. The additive/multiplicative traps cannot be promoted to the two-basin construction without violating the invariant partition. These observations explain the source connection but are not a printed full Q10 solution.

Thus there is a **logical reduction to classical ingredients** (as for many new elementary theorems), but no located **same-object prior printed theorem** supplying the whole result. I cannot honestly turn this into a published-priority claim or assert that nobody previously noticed the specialization argument. Conversely, no independently established classical theorem has been produced here from which the full arbitrary-dimensional prescribed-basin obstruction follows by just substituting parameters. If root adopts a stricter judgment that the assembled bridge itself is routine, it should identify that bridge as the reason, not mislabel the exceptional-polynomial theorem as a subsumer.

## FORMALFEASIBILITY

The exact ordinary endpoint is: for every n≥1, F=(F_i) in Z[X_0,...,X_(n−1)]^n and finite prime set E, it is false that for every prime p∉E there exist distinct A_p,B_p in F_p^n, both fixed by the reduced F, such that every a with a_0=0 has an iterate equal to A_p and every a with a_0≠0 has an iterate equal to B_p. The time may depend on a and p. This stronger endpoint avoids fixing integral targets and needs no dominance. The literal integral-fixed-target question follows by excluding denominator/difference primes where appropriate.

Local source pin is reported in the existing arcsinh pins.json as d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612; no git check was made, so that is a reported pin, not a new verified repository-head claim. MvPolynomial/Funext.lean provides evaluation extensionality on infinite fields. A direct use of z=N mod p avoids group cyclicity entirely.

Largest work items: integer-lattice specialization for an arbitrary finite number of variables; the monomial rigidity factorization and maps/evaluations to ZMod; the elementary Schur theorem with nonzero-value and finite-exception bookkeeping; geometric-sum prime construction; iteration/reduction compatibility and shifted-orbit fixed-target arguments. A bounded path probe did not find a NumberTheory/Polynomial/Schur file; this is not a theorem-library absence claim. These bridges must be proved, not supplied as assumptions, opaque axioms, or a replacement finite-field endpoint. No Lean invocation, build, declaration count, axiom audit or investment occurred. The theorem is finitistic and plausible in ordinary Mathlib, but implementation effort is material and feasibility is not formal correctness.
