It uses exact Fraction Gaussian elimination: fix one triangular outer face at (0,0),(1,0),(0,1), solve the 29 interior equilibrium equations with unit interior stresses, solve the outer stresses, and integrate face-plane differences. This is a deterministic construction, not a coordinate search. No Tutte/Maxwell theorem is trusted for the result: all output incidence is directly tested.

For EACH graph, enumeration of all C(32,3)=4960 triples finds exactly 60 supporting triples, all noncollinear and exactly the supplied triangular facets. Every off-facet vertex is strictly on the inner side. Translate by the average of all 32 vertices to get q_i with sum zero. For each triangle compute its outward normal n and positive support value c, and record p_f=n/c. Check q_i dot p_f <=1, with equality exactly at its three triangle vertices. Enumerate all nonsingular triples of the 32 equations q_i dot x=1: their feasible intersections are precisely the 60 distinct p_f. Incidence gives exactly 12 facet lists of length 5 and 20 of length 6. Two lists intersect in two primal vertices exactly when the corresponding original labels are an edge of G or H. Maximum coordinate numerator/denominator bit lengths are 107 for G, 102 for H. Guarded construction completed in about 3.3 seconds at sampled group RSS about 13 MB; caps were not raised.

### Complete classical finite bridge for the coordinates

Let Q=conv{q_i} and P={x in R^3 : q_i dot x<=1 for every i}. The strict supporting tests ensure Q has dimension 3. Its average-zero representation has strictly positive coefficients on every vertex, hence 0 is interior: a supporting functional at 0 would have nonpositive values on every q_i with zero sum, forcing all values zero, contradicting full dimension. Thus P is bounded (an epsilon ball inside Q bounds the norm of every x in P), and 0 is strictly feasible, so P has dimension 3.

At a vertex of P the active normals span R^3: otherwise a nonzero orthogonal direction permits small positive and negative perturbations satisfying every inequality, contradicting extremality. Conversely three independent active normals force a unique point, which is extreme. Therefore the enumerated feasible triple intersections are exactly all vertices of P.

For completeness P is the convex hull of these vertices without invoking a black-box polyhedral theorem: at a point whose active normals have rank r<3 choose a line direction annihilating them. Boundedness supplies two endpoints of its feasible interval, with the original point a convex combination. At each endpoint a new active normal has nonzero pairing with the direction and hence increases rank. Recurse at most three times to express the point as a convex combination of rank-three intersections. The same argument with a specified equality maintained shows each section F_i={x in P:q_i dot x=1} is the hull of its recorded incident vertices.

Each F_i has dimension 2 (noncollinear incident vertices), and is a facet. Every facet is one of these: take a relative interior point of the facet avoiding intersections with the finitely many constraint hyperplanes that do not contain it. Some inequality must be tight there, since otherwise it is interior in P. Thus one hyperplane contains the facet, and dimension/maximality gives F_i. For each p_f precisely three independent constraints are active. Locally their three halfspaces give three incident rays, so P is simple/cubic. Within each F_i the active-pair edges join its incident vertices in a cycle, a pentagon or hexagon. Two facets share an edge iff their recorded intersection contains its two endpoints. The exact graph equality check makes the facet-adjacency graph G or H. This proves existence of the required primal convex cubic 60-vertex polytope, rather than merely the skeleton of an unspecified realization. It also describes Q/P one-sided polar incidence explicitly; no absolute-polar API is substituted.

All steps in this paragraph are elementary classical arguments but remain new formal dependencies.
