Definition1.1: Curv(R^n) consists of complex measure-valued functions on nonempty compact convex bodies that are local, translation invariant and weakly continuous. Locality means equality of measures on Borel subsets of any open set on which the two bodies agree. The topology is induced by compact convergence on measures; it is not being silently replaced by total variation or a metrizable topology. Curv_k denotes degree-k homogeneity; ± denotes parity. Smooth means integration of a smooth translation-invariant differential (n−1)-form over the normal cycle, plus a volume term at degree n.

Conjecture1.2: for every dimension n and every k in {0,...,n−2}, Curv_k^sm is dense in Curv_k. Conjecture1.3: for these degrees and each parity, the closed invariant kernel Z_k^± of globalization Φ↦[K↦Φ(K,R^n)] is an irreducible GL(n,R) representation (no nontrivial proper closed invariant subspace). The two conjectures are distinct: source proves the second implies the first, not equivalence. Source Theorem1.4 already settles k=0,n−2; n−1,n are described separately. Those cases and dimensional instances are not nominated. Corollary1.5's length-at-most-two claim is conditional on1.3.

## Full mathematical attempts on the geometric source

Attempt1: let G=GL(n,R), choose a smooth compactly supported approximate identity ψε on G and form Φε=∫G ψε(g)(g·Φ)dg in the complete locally convex representation. Continuity/equicontinuity on compact group sets imply Φε→Φ; differentiating the translated test function gives G-smooth orbit vectors. Locality, homogeneity and parity are preserved by linear combinations/integration of group translates. This is the standard dense smooth-vector construction. It proves only density of Curv_k^∞. Source Lemma6.14 gives Curv_k^sm⊆Curv_k^∞, the WRONG direction to finish the argument. We have not represented Φε by a smooth normal-cycle form. Calling orbit smoothness geometric smoothness would assume the unanswered conclusion. No claimed counterexample to density.

Attempt2: use globalization and first moments to reconstruct a geometrically smooth representative. If Φ∈Curv_k^∞, its globalization is a smooth valuation. Source Theorem6.13 supplies a geometrically smooth Ψ with the same globalization (in degrees below n this uses translation-invariant normal-cycle forms). Thus replace Φ by Φ−Ψ∈Z_k^∞. The Bernig embedding B is continuous, injective and equivariant:

    BΦ(K)=(∫x_1 dΦ(K),...,∫x_n dΦ(K)) ∈ Val_(k+1)(R^n,C^n).

Each component of BΦ is GL-smooth and translation invariant; the scalar valuation representation theorem supplies forms ω_j representing them. The missing step is a simultaneous local lift, not scalar representability. For a translation-invariant (n−2)-form η, Stokes on the closed normal cycle yields

    B(I(dη))_j(K)=∫nc(K) x_j dη=−∫nc(K) dx_j∧η.

Here I(dη) has zero globalization. If one could find a single η of bidegree(k,n−2−k) for which all ω_j equal −dx_j∧η modulo the VALUATION integration kernel, then injectivity5.6 would give Φ=I(dη), closing the kernel problem. Separate component forms do not establish that simultaneous compatibility. The kernel for globalization of a form is also not the kernel for curvature measures; interchanging them would discard local information. Source Lemma5.9 gives a genuine necessary image restriction BΦ(K)∈dir(aff K), but we did not prove this condition sufficient for such a lift.

In representation language the required equality is B(Z_k^∞)=im(B∘I∘d). The source already proves that im(B∘I∘d) is nonzero and closed in the ambient smooth vector-valued valuation representation, by Casselman–Wallach. It obtains equality only by assuming the irreducibility at issue (Proposition6.15). Lemma6.9 gives finite length of the tensor-valued ambient module; finite length is not length two and does not make an arbitrary nonzero proper submodule irreducible. Extending Proposition6.10 without proving its length-two hypothesis is invalid. We establish neither that hypothesis in all intermediate degrees nor the needed image equality.

Attempt3: exploit the closest localization companion rather than assume scalar Alesker automatically handles locality. Hofstätter–Knoerr [2510.25698v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25698v2), actual latest history/PDF, November27,2025 (not an inferred date from search metadata), TheoremsA/B/C/D, actual Theorem4.3 induction and full Theorem4.4 proof/Remark4.5 read. Theorem4.4 DOES extend the scalar representation to GL-smooth polynomial valuations. For any polynomial p(x), K↦Φ(K,p) is a polynomial valuation: translating K replaces p(x) by p(x+v), a polynomial in v of degree≤deg p. The finite-dimensional polynomial action and smoothness of Φ give smoothness of these scalar valuations. Thus the theorem represents every fixed polynomial moment by SOME smooth form. It does not assert one common form ω with moment representatives pω, nor compatibility as degree increases. Its localization partitions valuations by vertical support and may break translation invariance; it is not a locality-preserving smoothing theorem for measure-valued Φ. Remark4.5 expressly warns that representation forms need not inherit valuation invariance. No complete bridge from its result to the simultaneous lift above was found.
