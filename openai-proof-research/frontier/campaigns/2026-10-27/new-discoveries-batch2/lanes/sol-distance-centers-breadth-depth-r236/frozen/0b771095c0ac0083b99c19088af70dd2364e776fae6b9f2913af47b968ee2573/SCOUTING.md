1. **Annular dyadic wavelet frames.** Bownik–Rzeszotnik, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.08230v1, 20 May 2019, Problem 2.7 p7, attributed to Bownik–Weber 2003. For each 1/8<b<=1/6, determine whether hat(psi)=1_{(-1,-b) union (b,1)} generates a dyadic integer-translate frame of L2(R), i.e. whether there exist positive finite frame bounds valid for all f. The actual report gives the surrounding-range classification table on p8; the original 2003 proof body is unread. **Rank1 provisional.** ATTACK.md derives the full exact coefficient energy, proves the Bessel upper bound and sustains coercivity and cancellation routes. Missing universal step is a measured Poincare bound or positive-measure low-energy witnesses. Finite rational graph samples are evidence only. A classical general frame theorem could subsume this calculation.

2. **Positive periodic singular ODE.** Armengol Gasull, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.02524v1, 4 December 2020, Problem9 pp10–11. Given every p>0, T>0 and continuous nonzero T-periodic f, find necessary and sufficient conditions for existence of a positive T-periodic C2 solution of x^p x''=f. Actual preceding/following body integrates f/x^p to zero and integrates by parts to prove integral f<0; hence f must change sign. It explicitly reports sufficiency under p>=2 and nondegenerate zeros and a p=5/3 smooth counterexample to those two conditions alone; the original cited proofs [126–127] were not retrieved. **Rank2 provisional.** Mechanism: periodic shooting in (x,x') or a constrained variational existence argument, with a priori positive lower bounds. Next decisive calculation: extract the cited p=5/3 obstruction and determine whether a usable exact functional criterion is available. Do not nominate the two necessary conditions or simply drop the nondegeneracy premise. Full classification may be too broad.

3. **Zero-level translational tiling density.** Mihail N. Kolountzakis–Nir Lev, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.09410v2, submitted2020, Discrete Analysis2021:12, Section6.1 p21. For every nonzero f in L1(R) and nonempty bounded-density discrete Lambda with sum_{lambda in Lambda}f(x-lambda)=0 almost everywhere, must Lambda have uniform density? Its Fourier equivalent asks whether a spectral gap of delta_Lambda on some open interval forces uniform density. Actual Lemma3.2 and Section6.1 distinguish a gap near zero from an arbitrary spectral gap; the source expressly supplies examples where the transform is not a scalar delta near zero. Mechanism: translate a gap to quantitative counting using test functions; obstruction is loss of positivity after modulation. Next decisive calculation is a uniform large-interval counting estimate. **Defer, no rank:** no new way to recover positivity; original older proof bodies/citation history incomplete. Compact-support periodicity Theorem1.5 has the wrong hypotheses.

4. **Polynomial-growth Radon injectivity.** A.G. Ramm, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0603631v1, 27 March2006, Problem1 pp1–2. For every continuous f:R2->R (or C) with |f(x)|<=c(1+|x|^m), some c>0,m>=0, with f absolutely integrable on EVERY affine line and line integral zero for EVERY line, must f vanish identically? The source states known injectivity under weighted global L1 and known nonzero entire counterexamples without the growth premise. No earlier cited proof body is supplied in this short note or retrieved here. Mechanism: distributional Fourier slice theorem with cutoffs. Missing step: polynomial growth plus linewise integrability does not directly justify interchanging the unbounded transverse integral. **Defer.** No Schwartz/Radon specialization silently substituted.

5. **Mixed-sign elliptic-system uniqueness.** Same Ramm source, Problem2 p2. For every bounded domain D subset R^n, n>1, with smooth connected boundary, selfadjoint strictly positive second-order real elliptic Dirichlet operators L,M, and strictly positive smooth a,b on closure(D), do Lu+av=0, -bu+Mv=0 and u=v=0 on boundary imply u=v=0? Actual note explicitly rejects adding hypotheses such as L=M or small |a-b|, and cites a one-dimensional positive answer. No proof of the full endpoint is in the note. Mechanism: fourth-order elimination or a weighted energy identity. Obstacle: weighted uv integrals have opposite signs with different weights; positivity cannot simply be multiplied through noncommuting operators. **Defer**, earlier one-dimensional body and current descendants unread. This differs from Radon and Fourier-rank questions in object and mechanism.

6. **Infinite rank of restricted Fourier kernels.** Same Ramm source, Problem3.1 pp2–3. Let nonzero f in L2(D), D a bounded box/ball in R3, F(z)=integral_D f(x)e^(iz.x)dx. For algebraic varieties L_j={z:P_j(z)=0}, deg P_j>=1, transversal in the source's tangent-span sense, and smooth rapidly decreasing strictly positive measures mu_j making the exponentials and stated kernel operator well-defined, is T:L2(L1,mu1)->L2(L2,mu2), Th(v)=integral h(u)F(u+v)dmu1(u), always infinite rank? The related Problem3.2 asks arbitrarily large independent translate families. Actual note explains rank-one delta sources are excluded by L2; it contains no full proof body. Mechanism: finite-rank translation kernels imply exponential-polynomial structure. Missing bridge: restricted algebraic translates do not yield the unrestricted translation theorem. Tangent-space wording is not silently resolved into stronger global assumptions. **Defer**, no routine delta-function witness.

7. **Gated/one-limited queue joint distribution.** Boon–Boxma–Winands, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.0142v1, posted1August2014, internal January2011, Open problem1 p4 with exact model Section2 p2: two cyclic queues with independent Poisson inputs of rates lambda_i, iid general service times B_i and switch-over times S_i, all primitive processes mutually independent, parameters admitting joint steady state; gated Q1, one-limited Q2. Determine the joint queue-length PGFs at polling instants. Actual Section3.1 derives Eq4; Section3.2 explains why substituting h1=beta1 and iterating introduces nested shifted arguments. Exhaustive/one-limited's constant reduction is explicitly proved nearby and does not extend. Mechanism: shifted Riemann–Hilbert boundary problem. Next decisive calculation: a unique analytic solution of Eq4 with normalization. **Defer**, general stationary Markov-chain representation is not a new explicit solution; current literature query was noisy and incomplete. Renewal switch-over asymptotics are merged here, not another slot.

8. **Topological size of Brownian-sheet zero slices.** Davar Khoshnevisan, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0510518v1, 24October2005, Open Problem1 p33. For d in {2,3} and standard d-dimensional two-parameter Brownian sheet B, is there a SINGLE null set outside which B^{-1}{0} intersect ({s} times (0,infinity)) is finite for EVERY s>0? Author conjectures no. Actual Theorem1.2 states all those slices have Hausdorff dimension zero on one common full-probability event; Section6 proves that metric result via covering/probability estimates. Dimension zero is not finiteness, and fixed-s Brownian polarity does not handle exceptional s. Mechanism: construct an exceptional slice with infinitely many hits by capacity/nested conditioning. **Defer**, no such construction obtained; current query does not clear twenty years of specialist work. Double-point/projection variants are not counted or reopened.

9. **Total variation from an empty multiserver queue.** Serguei Foss, https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.0462v1, 2September2009, Section1.1 p2. Under stationary ergodic input {(sigma_n,t_n)}, strict stability E sigma < m E t with the source finite-mean assumptions, start ordered Kiefer–Wolfowitz workload W1=0 and recurse W_(n+1)=R(W_n+e1 sigma_n-1 t_n)^+. Must its distribution converge in total variation to the minimal stationary workload distribution? Actual preceding body constructs increasing backwards workloads and weak convergence, and contrasts known TV under mixing/renovation. The note supplies no proof of the extra general TV step. Mechanism: eventual coupling to the stationary workload or a stationary-ergodic counterexample preventing exact coupling. **Defer**: weak convergence of point masses is not TV; no mixing assumption may be inserted. Rare-event/moment questions in the same note are not extra families.

10. **Cubic rigid limit-cycle bound.** Armengol Gasull, same2020 source, Problem1 p3. For all real a,b,c,d,e, does the planar system x'=-y+x(a+bx+cy+dx^2+exy), y'=x+y(a+bx+cy+dx^2+exy) have at most two limit cycles, with two attainable? Source normalizes d to0 or1 by linear changes and relates this to an Abel equation; the adjacent Theorem2.1 and its proof concern homogeneous components and do not give the rigid cubic upper bound. Mechanism: polar r'=ar+(b cos theta+c sin theta)r²+(d cos² theta+e cos theta sin theta)r³, theta'=1; seek a return-map zero bound. **Defer**, a sharp uniform Abel zero bound is missing and may hide a hard Hilbert16 subproblem. Singular forced ODE family2 is a different equation/object, not a parameter case of this family. No finite search supports a universal cycle count.
