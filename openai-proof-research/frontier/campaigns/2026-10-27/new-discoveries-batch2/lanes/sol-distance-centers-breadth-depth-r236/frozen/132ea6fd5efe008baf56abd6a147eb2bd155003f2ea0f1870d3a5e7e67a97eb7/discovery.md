## Strongest target and quantifiers

For EVERY finite simple undirected connected graph G with at least two vertices, write Delta for its maximum degree, i for the minimum size of an independent dominating vertex set, and s for the minimum number of edges in an inclusion-maximal matching. The conjecture is

    s(G) <= ceil(Delta(G) i(G) / 2).

Minimum and maximal are distinct. No assertion for one matching, a bounded degree, a special construction, or finitely many graphs settles this universal target. In particular the cube has i=2, Delta=3 and s=3 but also has a perfect maximal matching of size4. A universal assertion about every maximal matching is false.

## Mathematical route A: exposed independent set and the exact missing saving

Let D be a minimum independent dominating set and k=|D|. Assign every vertex outside D to an adjacent vertex of D. Each resulting cell has size at most Delta+1, so n <= (Delta+1)k. Every matching has at most floor(n/2) edges. This only gives s <= floor((Delta+1)k/2), not the conjecture.

For any matching M, let U be its exposed vertices. M is maximal iff U is independent: an uncovered edge extends M, and every extension would have two exposed endpoints. Conversely an independent U whose complement has a perfect matching produces a maximal matching of size (n-|U|)/2. Thus the exact still-missing construction is an independent U with a perfectly matchable complement and

    |U| >= n - 2 ceil(Delta k/2).

Writing slack=(Delta+1)k-n >=0, the sufficient stronger condition |U|>=k-slack would give s<=Delta k/2. A parity allowance of one is present in the actual ceiling. In the efficient-code cubic case n=4k, this asks to expose at least k vertices when k is even and at least k-1 when k is odd. Domination alone produces the independent D but does not make its complement perfectly matchable.

The tempting cell-pairing argument also lacks a step: connectivity of a quotient of domination cells does not give a matching pairing almost all cells (K1,4 has matching number1). This is a falsifier of that proposed implication, not a graph counterexample to Achour. No assertion that every abstract cell quotient is realizable with all desired capacities is needed for this logical obstruction.

Known bounds were read for scope, not claimed as exclusions. Cames van Batenburg arXiv2008.01863v3 Theorem2 gives s<=5n/12+1/2 for connected cubic graphs. Combining only n<=4i gives 5i/3+1/2, which does not imply ceil(3i/2). Baste–Fürst–Henning–Mohr–Rautenbach arXiv1905.12241v1 Theorem2 concerns regular graphs and a different order bound. Neither statement alone supplies the missing independent-domination saving; no shortness-based classical exclusion is asserted. The attempted legitimate 2023 Tavakoli–Došlić repository PDF URL returned404; its full body was not read or used as a subsumer. No worldwide classical clearance is inferred.

## Mathematical route B: efficient-code cubic graphs and odd-component repair

Take k independent centers and 3k other vertices, each joined to exactly one center, with three leaves per center. Add a 2-regular graph on the other vertices. All vertices have degree3. The centers dominate independently, and n<=4i proves i=k exactly. This family retains the difficult zero-slack regime without computing i heuristically.

If the leaf graph is a union of odd cycles, a matching leaving every center exposed cannot be maximal. Indeed every leaf has an exposed neighboring center, hence must be covered; this would be a perfect matching of the odd components. This proves a genuine obstruction to the construction U=D, not to the source inequality.

For triangle leaf components, an exact finite-state recurrence allows all possible repairs. Index the k triangles by triples t of their center neighbors, each center appearing three times. For each triangle choose A, the subset of its cross-spokes used in M, and either no internal edge or one internal edge among the remaining triangle vertices. At most one triangle vertex may remain exposed. Record (m,req,cost): the set m of centers already matched by a spoke, centers req adjacent to exposed leaves, and edge count. Reject repeated matched centers, union req over triangles, and minimize costs for each state. A terminal state is maximal exactly when req is a subset of m: unmatched centers have no uncovered incident spoke, exposed leaves are pairwise nonadjacent inside each triangle, and there are no intertriangle leaf edges. This enumerates all matchings and all maximality constraints for this family; it is not a universal proof.

For the Fano triples (013),(124),(235),(346),(450),(561),(602), n=28, Delta=3, i=7, the recurrence gives s=9<=11. Yet deleting D leaves seven triangles with no perfect matching. This simultaneously defeats the all-centers-exposed shortcut and shows why that shortcut's failure is not a counterexample. Thirty further seeded regular triple systems with k=3,...,8 also satisfy the conjectured bound. Connectivity is checked and reported per object; no untested connectedness assumption is used for a source refutation.
