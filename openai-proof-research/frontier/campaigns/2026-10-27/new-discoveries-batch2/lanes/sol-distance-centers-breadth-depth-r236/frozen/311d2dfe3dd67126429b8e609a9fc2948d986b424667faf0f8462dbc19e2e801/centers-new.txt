                                                                                     Abstract

                                                     We investigate the finite ultrametric spaces (X, d) that have a given cardi-
                                                 nality of the center of distances and a minimal cardinality of the set X. It is
                                                 shown that such spaces are isometric if and only if their centers of distances
                                                 are the same. The representing trees of these spaces are characterized up to
                                                 isomorphism.

Definition 1.1. Let (X, d) be a metric space and let D(X) be the distance set
of (X, d),
                       D(X) := {d(x, y) : x, y ∈ X}.
The center of distances C(X) of (X, d) is the set of all t ∈ D(X) such that for
each p ∈ X there exists x ∈ X with d(p, x) = t.

    In [6], the concept of the center of distances was used to generalize the
theorem of John von Neumann on permutations of two sequences having the
same set of cluster points. The center of distances has turned out to be an
effective tool for solving a number of interesting problems connected with metric
spaces (see, e.g., papers [1–4, 24, 25]).
    In the case of ultrametric spaces, the structure of the center of distances was
recently studied in [17] and [18].
    The following theorem was proved in [18].

Theorem 1.2. Let (X, d) be a finite ultrametric space and let n := |X|. Then
the inequality
                         |C(X)| ≤ 1 + ⌊log2 (n)⌋
holds, where log2 (n) is the binary logarithm of n and ⌊log2 (n)⌋ is the integer
part of log2 (n). Moreover, for every integer n ≥ 1 there exists an ultrametric
space (Y, ρ) such that |Y | = n and

                             |C(Y )| = 1 + ⌊log2 (n)⌋.

    Theorem 1.2 implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3. The inequality

                                 |X| ≥ 2|C(X)|−1

holds for each finite ultrametric space (X, d).

    The present paper is motivated by the following problem.

Problem 1.4. Let MCD (maximal center of distances) denote the class of all
finite ultrametric spaces (X, d) satisfying the equality

                                 |X| = 2|C(X)|−1 .

Describe the MCD-spaces up to isometry.

    It was shown by Vladimir Gurvich and Mikhail Vyalyi that any finite ul-
trametric space is isometrically describable by a rooted tree equipped with a
specific labeling of the vertex set [22]. The corresponding geometric interpreta-
tion of the Gurvich-Vyalyi representation was obtained in [26] and it provides a
framework for addressing various extremal problems related to finite ultrametric
spaces [14–16]. Analogues of the Gurvich-Vyalyi representation and its geomet-
ric interpretation have recently been obtained for totally bounded ultrametric
spaces [11].
    The present paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains some defini-
tions and facts from the theory of ultrametric spaces and graph theory. Section 3
collects several auxiliary lemmas.
    The main results of the paper are proved in Section 4. The structure of
representing trees of MCD-spaces is described in Theorem 4.1 up to isomor-
phisms of labeled rooted trees. The necessary and sufficient conditions under
which MCD-spaces are isometric are given in Theorem 4.2.
    In the final Section 5, we formulate some conjectures and open problems
related to MCD-spaces and finite homogeneous ultrametric spaces.

2     Preliminaries

In what follows we denote by R+ the set [0, ∞), R+ := [0, ∞).

Definition 2.1. Let X be a non-empty set. A metric on X is a function
d : X × X → R+ , such that for all x, y, z ∈ X:

    (i) d(x, y) = d(y, x),

 (ii) d(x, y) = 0 ⇐⇒ x = y,

(iii) d(x, y) ≤ d(x, z) + d(z, y).

     If the inequality
                              d(x, y) ≤ max{d(x, z), d(z, y)}                 (2.1)
holds instead of (iii), then d : X × X → R+ is called an ultrametric on X.
    Inequality (2.1) is often called the strong triangle inequality. See, for exam-
ple, book [23, p. 40].
    Let S be a non-empty subset of a metric space (X, d). The quantity

                             diam S := sup{ d(x, y) : x, y ∈ S }

is called the diameter of S.

    The next our goal is to remind the definition of representing trees of finite
ultrametric spaces. To give a definition of representing trees, we will use the

Definition 2.18. Let k ≥ 2 be an integer number. A non-empty graph G is
called complete k-partite if its vertices can be divided into disjoint non-empty
sets X1 , . . . , Xk so that there are no edges joining the vertices which belong to
the same Xi , and, moreover, any two vertices from different Xi and Xj are
adjacent. In this case we write G = G[X1 , . . . , Xk ].
   We shall say that G is a complete multipartite graph if there exists k such
that G is complete k-partite.
Definition 2.19. Let (X, d) be a finite metric space with |X| ≥ 2. The diamet-
rical graph GX of (X, d) is a graph such that V (G) = X and, for all u, v ∈ X,

                     {u, v} ∈ E(GX ) ⇐⇒ d(u, v) = diam X.

Theorem 2.20. Let (X, d) be a finite ultrametric space with |X| ≥ 2. Then
the diametrical graph GX is complete multipartite.

trees TX = TX (rX , lX ) of finite ultrametric spaces (X, d).
    Construction of representing trees. Let (X, d) be a finite ultrametric
space.
    The tree TX (rX , lX ) has the root rX := X with lX (rX ) := diam X.
    If X is a single-point set, then the root X is the unique vertex of TX , and
the construction is completed.

complete multipartite, GX = GX [X1 , . . . , Xk ] with k ≥ 2. In this case the sets
X1 , . . . , Xk are the nodes of the first level of TX (rX ) and lX (Xi ) := diam Xi for
i = 1, . . . , k. The nodes of the first level with the label 0 are leaves, and those

indicated by strictly positive labels are internal nodes of TX (rX ). If all nodes of
the first level are leaves, then the representing tree TX (rX , lX ) is constructed.

               |X| = |Y | = 2n   and |C(X)| = |C(Y )| = n + 1.

Hence, the equalities

                     |X| = 2 |C(X)|−1   and |Y | = 2 |C(Y )|−1

hold, and, consequently, (X, d) and (Y, ρ) are MCD-spaces. Thus (X, d) and
(Y, ρ) are isometric by Theorem 4.2.

5    Problems and expected results

Following [7, p. 38] we say that a metric space (X, d) is homogeneous if, for
any two points x, y ∈ X, there exists a self-isometry F : X → X satisfying the
equality F (x) = y.
   The authors believe the following conjecture is true.
Conjecture 5.1. Let (X, d) be a MCD-space and let Is(X) be the set of all
self-isometries of (X, d). Then the following statements hold.
Maximal Center of Distances of Finite Ultrametric Spaces                           27


 (i) (X, d) is homogeneous.

 (ii) The equality
                                    log2 | Is(X)| = |X| − 1                     (5.1)
     holds.

Conjecture 5.2 (Prove or disprove). Let (X, d) be a finite homogeneous ultra-
metric space. Then equality (5.1) holds if and only if (X, d) ∈ MCD.

   For each metric space (X, d), the set Is(X) of all self-isometries of (X, d) is a
group with respect to the operation of composition of self-isometries of (X, d).

Problem 5.3. Describe the structure of the groups Is(X) of MCD-spaces
(X, d) up to isomorphism.

   The following problem is closely related with Corollary 4.6.

Problem 5.4. Let b0 , . . . , bn be a finite sequence of positive integer numbers.
Find conditions under which there exist a finite ultrametric space (X, d) having
the center of distances

                     C(X) = {c0 , . . . , cn },   0 = c0 < · · · < cn ,

such that the equality

                             bi = |{x ∈ X : d(p, x) = ci }|                     (5.2)

holds for all p ∈ X and i ∈ {0, . . . , n}.

     for all p ∈ X and i ∈ {0, . . . , n}.

 (ii) The space (X, d) is a MCD-space with |X| = 2n .
