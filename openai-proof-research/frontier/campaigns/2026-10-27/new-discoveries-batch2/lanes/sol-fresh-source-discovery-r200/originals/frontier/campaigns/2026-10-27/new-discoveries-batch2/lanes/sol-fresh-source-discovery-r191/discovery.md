## Exact two-coset route

For a finite group X and subgroups Y,Z, form a simple bipartite incidence graph with disjoint vertex parts {Yx:x∈X} and {Zx:x∈X}, with an edge joining Yx and Zx for each x∈X (duplicate edges identified). Right multiplication by X is edge-transitive. A vertex Yx has degree [Y:Y∩Z], and a vertex Zx degree [Z:Y∩Z]: at the identity coset its incident edges correspond to elements of Y, with equal opposite cosets precisely when their representatives differ by an element of Y∩Z. Thus the graph is regular exactly when |Y|=|Z|, and then the two parts have equal sizes. Connectivity is equivalent to ⟨Y,Z⟩=X: paths starting at the identity edge reach precisely the cosets with representatives in that subgroup. The kernel of the diagonal action is core_X(Y)∩core_X(Z), so this intersection being trivial makes the action faithful.

An element g fixes a coset Yx exactly when xgx^(-1)∈Y; similarly for Z. Therefore the diagonal subgroup contains no prime-order derangement on the union of the two parts exactly when every prime-order element of X lies in the union of all X-conjugates of Y and Z. This union condition is enough; it is not necessary that both separate coset actions be elusive. It suggests using the source's non-split-extension group X and equal-order stabilizers, possibly from its distinct elusive actions, then checking generation and an orbital relation. This is a plausible finite structural counterexample route rather than an all-size numerical extrapolation.

The exact missing bridge is decisive: exhibit suitable Y,Z (and, if needed, an appropriate cross orbital), prove connectedness and equal valencies, and prove the FULL automorphism group has no prime-order derangement and does not interchange the two parts. Elusiveness of the diagonal X alone is insufficient. Even finding two actions does not imply there is a suitable full-group orbital. No pair/graph with all these properties is produced here. The source's two different elusive actions do not certify this step; its Remark1.6 actually warns that one action has a larger2-closure with prime derangements. No unsupported new theorem follows from that warning.

## Universal obstructions to tempting transfers

Double cover: for every finite simple graph Γ, its bipartite double cover has vertices(v,i), i∈{0,1}, with edges(u,0)(v,1) and(v,0)(u,1) for each uv∈E(Γ). The map(v,i)↦(v,1−i) is always an automorphism, has order2, and fixes no vertex. Thus this transfer can NEVER yield a counterexample, independent of any source group. If Γ is vertex-transitive, each base automorphism lifts to both parts and the deck swap connects their vertex orbits, so the cover is also vertex-transitive, even if disconnected. This independently fails semisymmetry.

Subdivision: subdividing every edge of a d-regular graph gives degrees d at old vertices and2 at new ones. For the actual source d=63 this is not regular. If d=2 and the base is finite simple connected, it is a cycle and its subdivision is a cycle, hence vertex-transitive. Therefore uniform subdivision of connected regular base graphs supplies no semisymmetric counterexample by this method (d=0 gives the trivial isolated case, d=1 is not regular after subdivision).

Cartesian and strong products in the source preserve vertex-transitivity, as proved in its Proposition2.5 and Corollary1.7; all factors from its family remain outside the selected class. Finally, subgroup elusiveness is not inherited by an overgroup: the order2 point-stabilizer in S3 fixes a vertex on3 points and has no semiregular nonidentity element, but full Aut(K3)=S3 contains a fixed-point-free3-cycle. This small example is a method countermodel, not a semisymmetric source counterexample.
