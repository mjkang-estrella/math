## Sustained full-scope C6 attack

Write idempotent equations as x^[2]=Lx, with L=(M^t)^(-1) invertible. Every such L defines a perfect evolution algebra. Leading terms x_i^2 give a length2^n finite complete intersection; zero is reduced since its Jacobian is −L. Thus the unique-nonzero case requires concentrating all remaining length2^n−1 at one point. This is a necessary multiplicity condition, **not** a construction.

Triangular systems do not settle the problem: projection to the first j−1 coordinates is surjective on complex solutions because the last monic quadratic always has a root. Along the all-zero prefix there are two roots 0 and1, so each successive coordinate adds at least one distinct point. Consequently this source-style triangular route always has at least n+1 points. Direct sums multiply counts, so repeatedly adjoining the known two-dimensional unique-nonzero example multiplies the total count; it does not preserve two points.

We next attack maximal degeneration at a full-support nonzero point p. Normalize p to the all-ones vector by coordinate scaling. If the Jacobian there has rank one, then J=2I−L has rank one and J1=1. Hence J=1*v^t for sum(v_i)=1, so

    L = 2I − 1*v^t,    det L = 2^(n−1).

These are all normalized full-support rank-one-Jacobian candidates, not just numerical samples. Translating x=1+y gives

    y_i^2 + sum_j v_j*y_j = 0  for every i.

All y_i have the same square. Therefore either y=0 or y=t*epsilon, epsilon_i in {−1,1}. Substitution gives t^2+t*(v·epsilon)=0. Thus the full complex solution set is exactly

    {1} union {1 − epsilon*(v·epsilon) : epsilon in {−1,1}^n}.

Complementary sign vectors give the same point. Distinct sign pairs with nonzero v·epsilon give distinct points: equality forces their sign vectors to agree up to sign. Let Z be the number of complementary pairs with v·epsilon=0. The exact distinct count is

    N = 1 + 2^(n−1) − Z.

Choose an index with v_i !=0. For each assignment of the other signs, at most one choice of epsilon_i annihilates v·epsilon. Thus at most2^(n−1) sign vectors vanish, or Z<=2^(n−2), and

    N >= 1 + 2^(n−2).

Equality is attained in this restricted family by v=(1/2,1/2,0,...,0). For n>=3 this gives at least three points. **The entire full-support rank-one-Jacobian route therefore fails to produce the two-point endpoint.** This is a route obstruction, not a source refutation: the conjecture allows a higher-rank Jacobian or a nonzero point with zero coordinates. The n2 example is consistent: its inverse structure matrix is [[3/2,−1/2],[−1/2,3/2]], exactly this family with v=(1/2,1/2).

## Strongest objection, falsifier and precise gap

The strongest objection is that neither length nor maximum Jacobian corank controls the distinct-count spectrum. Every restricted obstruction above may be evaded by a genuinely nontriangular L with a different local singularity. No universal local-multiplicity upper bound ruling out length2^n−1 was proved; no family attaining that length was constructed. Even a unique-nonzero construction would leave the intermediate counts m unresolved unless a uniform collision/splitting construction supplied **every** m without loss of invertibility or additional roots.

An actual C6 proof needs, for every n and every2<=m<=2^n, an explicit or rigorous existential invertible L and a full complex root count. A refutation needs some dimension and count proved impossible for **all** invertible L; failure of the sign family is insufficient. These are the precise remaining gaps.
