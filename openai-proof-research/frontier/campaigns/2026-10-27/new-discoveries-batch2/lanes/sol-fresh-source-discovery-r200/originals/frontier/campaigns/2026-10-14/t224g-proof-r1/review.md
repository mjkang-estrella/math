## Re-derivation of the new interfaces (what I checked, and how)

1. **Exact lifetime decomposition.** Persistent particles have intensity λe^{-aT}, initial deaths λ(1−e^{-aT}), births λ dz ds. These classes are independent, and the base mesh is e′=e e^{aT/2}. The two-replica marking has a common persistent intensity λe^{-2aT}, mixed initial types of order λa, double deaths of order λa², and independent births. These are standard marked-Poisson facts, and I verified them directly. The bounded-box Poisson perturbation identity holds for every bounded path functional at fixed e, by the exponential moments of the rare count in B.

2. **Simultaneous-subset shielding and arm necessity (absolute/TV bound).** H=Σ_S(−1)^{k−|S|}F(base+S) is nonzero only if each i has, at some setting of the others, F(base+S+i)≠F(base+S). A life probe of the V05 forest contains no selected location in its buffer. Its occupancy screens are measured on the base only, and adding nuclei cannot remove the screening base nuclei or let an outside cell cross a screened shell (V02 Lemma localization: "even a very long cell belonging to a remote prescribed insertion cannot cross a screened shell"). So the probe's tiling and colors are the same for every S and every time. V02 Lemma pivotal-arms applies to *arbitrary* local tiling changes held against a common annulus, so a presence change already forces four alternating arms (or the straight-side/one-arm tests) in the probe, on base colors. The black/white sandwich is not even needed. The witness quad and time vary, but the arm event depends only on the probe and the quad choice: |𝒬| choices per life, at most 2k lives. Hence |H|≤2^k·1{∪_choices∩_C probe events}, with the source outermost-failure sum. This is an absolute bound, and (5)–(6) follow verbatim from V05 lines 285–383. Location UI follows from the pointwise forest bound (V05 lines 421–433), as claimed.

3. **Local two-time algebra.** I re-derived it by hand and checked it exactly: 800 cases of random rational (A,B,C,D) over all sandwich triples. Birth=(C+D/2)E(v−u)−Dp/4, death=(B+D/2)E(v−u)−Dp/4, one refresh minus constant=−Dp/4, and constant insertion=(B+C+D)E(v−u). The sandwich v_w≤u≤v_b is genuinely used: control `sandwich_dropped` shows that the identities fail without it. The cancellation of the −Dp/4 term between D_i and C_i is exact and pointwise in the inside configuration, so only the marginal E_c v−u remains.

4. **Exterior measurability and the message contract.** I read A05 1–175 and 800–960. The message is a seam-conditioned zero-mass signed field, with ess-sup TV norm over seam variables including colors. Seams belong to the base process, and fixed outside data are allowed (Corollary tr:insertion). On {exactly four through walls in h..l}, which is E_i-measurable, the payoff is f(u,v) with coefficients measurable in E_i, and u,v are functions of (inside connection state, E_i-measurable annulus diagram). Hence |E[(C+D/2)(v−u)]|≤sup|C+D/2|·‖I_h‖≤C^k1_{J_{−i}}·C(e/h)^{2+ν}. No inside-pivotal conditioning occurs. The extension of the two-cap normal form to arbitrary boundary switch counts (cut walls at every contact; compose inner caps with the filling; noncrossing pairings of 4 through ends: exactly 2) is correct. It strictly extends A02 Lemma an:caps, which is stated for four-switch caps. Monotonicity of the "black arcs connected" bit gives the sandwich.

5. **Cancellation for every formal cap state; retention of the other arms.** If any other site j lacks its probe arms, pivotal-arms (which uses colors only in j's agreement annulus) cancels the j operator for every filling of i's disk. A05 (lines 56–65) formal pairings are realizable as planar abstract fillings, as the author argues, so the bound holds on the full formal state space. J_{−i} is measurable in disjoint R-balls. The normalization (λr_e)^k a_e^{k−1}(e/h)^{2+ν}≍e^{3/4+ν}h^{−2−ν}→0, using a_e≍e^{5/4} from A Theorem (r_e~c_V^{−1}e^{3/4}) and λr_ea_e≍1.

6. **Six walls.** I read V02 Lemma color-arms. Six alternating arms are BKR(5∘1) on a fixed geometry, and at fixed radii the bound is (h/l)^{2+β}.
