  Abstract. 
    
 Preorder polytopes, defined from preorders on
finite sets, are introduced and studied from a
lattice point enumeration point of view. They
naturally generalize arbor polytopes, recently
introduced and studied by the second named
author. Preorder
polytopes are shown to be lattice polytopes
which satisfy a certain duality relating their
Ehrhart polynomials with the zeta polynomials
of their posets of lattice points. A combinatorial
interpretation of the normalized volume of a
preorder polytope is proven, together with
formulas for the Ehrhart polynomial and
the     h  ∗   h^{\ast}   -polynomial, and a combinatorial
interpretation of the latter is conjectured.
Several conjectures and results on the lattice
point enumeration of arbor polytopes are
generalized to preorder polytopes, new
conjectures are proposed and new interesting
examples of preorder polytopes are studied. 
  
 
 
  1.  Introduction 

 
 Arbor polytopes are lattice polytopes
introduced and studied in  [ Cha25b ] . This
paper studies a natural generalization. Let us
first recall the definition and main properties
of arbor polytopes. Given a finite set    E  E   , an
 arbor  on    E  E    is a rooted tree with
vertices the blocks of a partition of    E  E   . The
 arbor polytope  associated to such an arbor
   σ  \sigma    is the polytope     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    in     ℝ  E   \mathbb{R}^{E}   
defined by the linear inequalities      x  e   ≥  0   x_{e}\geq 0   
for     e  ∈  E   e\in E    and 
 

  
  (1)  
  
       ∑   e  ∈   𝒟  ⁡   (  v  )       x  e    ≤   |   𝒟  ⁡   (  v  )    |    \sum_{e\in\mathscr{D}(v)}x_{e}\leq|\mathscr{D}(v)|    
    
 
 for every vertex    v  v    of    σ  \sigma   , where
    𝒟  ⁡   (  v  )    \mathscr{D}(v)    is the union of all vertices which
are descendants of    v  v    in    σ  \sigma    (including
   v  v    itself). The set of lattice points of
    𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}   , denoted by     P  σ   P_{\sigma}    and
partially ordered as a subposet of the product
order     ℕ  E   \mathbb{N}^{E}   , also plays a central role in
 [ Cha25b ] . It was shown there that
    𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    is a simple lattice polytope with
very interesting properties from a lattice
point enumeration point of view. 
 
 
 Let us present two conjectures and one result
about arbor polytopes which partly motivated
the present work (see Section   2  
for relevant background and any undefined
terminology). Let us denote by      h  i   ​   (  σ  )    h_{i}(\sigma)   
the number of lattice points of     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}   
having    i  i    nonzero coordinates for     0  ≤  i  ≤  n   0\leq i\leq n    and set      h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    =    ∑   i  =  0   n     h  i   ​   (  σ  )   ​   t  i      h(\sigma,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}h_{i}(\sigma)t^{i}   , where    n  n    is the cardinality of
the ground set    E  E   . Following  [ Cha25b ] ,
we call     h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    h(\sigma,t)    the     h  h   -polynomial 
of    σ  \sigma   . 
 
 
   Conjecture 1.1   .  
 
 ( [ Cha25b , Conjecture 1.1] ) 
The polynomial     h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    h(\sigma,t)    is equal to the
   h  h   -polynomial of an    n  n   -dimensional simplicial
polytope for every arbor    σ  \sigma    on an
   n  n   -element set    E  E   . In particular,     h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    h(\sigma,t)

   Conjecture 1.2   .  
 
 ( [ Cha25b , Conjecture 3.2] ) 

For every arbor    σ  \sigma   , all roots of the

    h  ∗   h^{\ast}   -polynomial of     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    has only real
roots.  
 
 
 
 An arbor    σ  \sigma    is said to be  linear  if
the associated rooted tree is a path. The reverse
arbor     rev  ⁡   (  σ  )    {\rm rev}(\sigma)    is then defined by reversing
the order of the vertices of this path. The
following statement was conjectured in
 [ Cha25b , Section 4] . 
 
 
   Theorem 1.3   .  
 
 ( [ Ath25 , Theorem 1.3] ) 
The Ehrhart polynomial of     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    and the
zeta polynomial of     P   rev  ⁡   (  σ  )     P_{{\rm rev}(\sigma)}    are related
by the identity  
 

  
  
      Ehr  ⁡   (   𝒬  σ   ,  t  )    =   𝒵  ⁡   (   P   rev  ⁡   (  σ  )     ,   t  +  1   )     {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma},t)={\mathcal{Z}}(P_{{\rm rev}(\sigma)},t+1)    
    
 
  for every linear arbor    σ  \sigma   .

 This paper generalizes arbor polytopes by
replacing arbors on finite sets by arbitrary
preorders. A  preorder  on a finite set
   E  E    can be viewed as a partial order on the
set of blocks of a partition of    E  E   , called
vertices (see Section   2.2  ).
The example shown in Figure   1   will
serve as a running example. Given a preorder
   τ  \tau    on a finite set    E  E   , the  preorder
polytope  associated to    τ  \tau    is the polytope
    𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}    in     ℝ  E   \mathbb{R}^{E}    defined by the linear
inequalities      x  e   ≥  0   x_{e}\geq 0    for     e  ∈  E   e\in E    and 
 

  
  (2)  
  
       ∑   e  ∈  ℐ     x  e    ≤   |  ℐ  |    \sum_{e\in{\mathcal{I}}}x_{e}\leq|{\mathcal{I}}|    
    
 
 for all order ideals    ℐ  {\mathcal{I}}    of    τ  \tau   . This
definition recovers and extends the definition
of arbor polytopes. Indeed, every arbor    σ  \sigma   
can be considered as a preorder    τ  \tau    having
the root of    σ  \sigma    as the maximum vertex.
Then, for every vertex    v  v    of    σ  \sigma    the set
    𝒟  ⁡   (  v  )    \mathscr{D}(v)    is a principal order ideal of    τ  \tau   
and inequalities of the form
(  2  ) associated to
nonprincipal order ideals of    τ  \tau    are
consequences (namely sums) of inequalities
associated to principal order ideals. 
 
 
 It is not obvious that preorder polytopes are
lattice polytopes and no longer true that they
are always simple. Our first main result shows
that preorder polytopes are indeed lattice
polytopes which form a subclass of the class of
generalized permutohedra  [ Pos09 ] . 
 
 
   Theorem 1.4   .  
 
  Let    τ  \tau    be a preorder on an    n  n   -element set.
Then,     𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}    is an    n  n   -dimensional lattice
polytope which is unimodularly equivalent to a
generalized permutohedron.  
 
 
 
 An advantage of considering preorder polytopes,
compared to the more restrictive class of arbor
polytopes, is that there is a natural notion of
duality for preorders. This notion allows for a
generalization of Theorem   1.3   which
we call  Ehrhart-Zeta duality . Another
obvious advantage is that the new setting allows
for more interesting examples. We denote by
    τ  ∗   \tau^{\ast}    the dual of the preorder    τ  \tau    and
define the poset     P  τ   P_{\tau}    of lattice points of
    𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}    as we did for arbors. The following
statement is the second main result of this
paper. 
 
 
   Theorem 1.5   .  
 
  Let    τ  \tau    be a preorder on an    n  n   -element set
   E  E   .  
 
  (a)  
 
  The Ehrhart polynomial of      𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}     is given by
the formula  
 

  
  
       Ehr  ⁡   (   𝒬  τ   ,  t  )    =    ∑    (   a  e   )   ∈   P   τ  ∗        ∏   e  ∈  E     (     t  +   a  e    −  1    a  e    )      ,   {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau},t)=\sum_{(a_{e})\in P_{\tau^{\ast}}}\prod_{e\in E}\binom{t+a_{e}-1}{a_{e}},    
    
 
  where the sum ranges over all lattice points
     (   a  e   )   (a_{e})     of      𝒬   τ  ∗    {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau^{\ast}}    . In particular, the
number of lattice points of      𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}     is equal to
that of      𝒬   τ  ∗    {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau^{\ast}}    .  
 
 
  The normalized volume of      𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}     is equal to the
number of words      w  ∈   E  n    w\in E^{n}     such that for every order
ideal     ℐ  {\mathcal{I}}     of     τ  \tau    , the elements of     ℐ  {\mathcal{I}}     appear a
total of at least      |  ℐ  |   |{\mathcal{I}}|     times in     w  w    .  
  
  (b)  
 
  The Ehrhart polynomial of      𝒬  τ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau}     and the zeta
polynomial of      P   τ  ∗    P_{\tau^{\ast}}     are related by the identity  
 

  
  
       Ehr  ⁡   (   𝒬  τ   ,  t  )    =   𝒵  ⁡   (   P   τ  ∗    ,   t  +  1   )     .   {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\tau},t)={\mathcal{Z}}(P_{\tau^{\ast}},t+1).    
    
 
  
 
 
 
 
 The proofs of both results rely on the theory
of generalized permutohedra  [ Pos09 ] . An
interesting feature of our study of preorder
polytopes is the variety of conjectures on their
lattice point enumeration that can be stated,
some of which extend
Conjecture   1.1   and (partly)
Conjecture   1.2   on arbor
polytopes. 
 
 
 The content and structure of this paper can be
summarized as follows. Section   2  
discusses notation and reviews basic
definitions and background on poset and preposet
combinatorics, Ehrhart theory of lattice
polytopes, generalized permutohedra and related
topics. Section   3   establishes
basic properties of preorder polytopes and
their posets of lattice points.
Theorems   1.4   and   1.5  
are proven in Sections   3  
and   4  , respectively, by
applying results from the theory of generalized
permutohedra  [ Pos09 ] .
Section   4   also conjectures
a stronger form of Ehrhart-Zeta duality, as well
as a combinatorial interpretation of the
    h  ∗   h^{\ast}   -polynomial of any preorder polytope
which refines that of the normalized volume
given by Theorem   1.5   (see
Conjectures   4.2  
and   4.4  ) and provides partial
results and a formula for the
    h  ∗   h^{\ast}   -polynomial. Ehrhart polynomials of
preorder polytopes may have nonreal complex
roots. However, a weaker property (called
magic positivity) which implies the
real-rootedness of the     h  ∗   h^{\ast}   -polynomial
is conjectured in Section   4  
(see Conjecture   4.8  ). 
 
 
 Section   5   discusses the
natural extension of
Conjecture   1.1   for preorder
polytopes and conjectures the
   γ  \gamma   -positivity and real-rootedness of
   h  h   -polynomials of preorders, as well as
their invariance under duality.
Section   6   studies reflexive
polytopes which can be obtained as
Minkowski sums of preorder polytopes and
standard simplices. Section   7  
provides a formula for the double Ehrhart
polynomial of a preorder polytope and the
standard simplex and conjectures an identity
(see Conjecture   7.2  ) which is
stronger than Conjecture   4.4  .
Section   8   briefly
discusses an important enumerative invariant
of the poset of lattice points of a preorder
polytope, namely the    M  M   -triangle.
Section   9   discusses some
interesting examples and confirms some of
the conjectures of this paper for them. 
 
 
 
  2.  Preliminaries 

 
 This section establishes notation to be followed
throughout the paper and reviews definitions and
background on the combinatorics of partial orders
and preorders, lattice polytopes and generalized
permutohedra. More information about these topics
can be found in  [ Hi92 ,  Sta96 ,  Sta11 ,  Pos09 ] . 
 
 
  2.1.  Notation 

 
 Let    E  E    be a nonempty finite set. We will denote
by      (   𝐞  e   )    e  ∈  E    {({\rm\mathbf{e}}_{e})}_{e\in E}    the standard
basis of the space     ℝ  E   \mathbb{R}^{E}   . We will use boldface
letters to denote vectors     𝐱  =    (   x  e   )    e  ∈  E     \mathbf{x}=(x_{e})_{e\in E}    of     ℝ  E   \mathbb{R}^{E}   , or collections of
real numbers. We will denote by     |  𝐱  |   |\mathbf{x}|   
the sum of the coordinates of a point     𝐱  ∈   ℝ  E    \mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}    and by     |  S  |   |S|    or     #  ​  S   \#S    the cardinality
of a finite set    S  S   . 
 
 
 
  2.2.  Preorders 

 
 Let    E  E    be a nonempty finite set. A  preorder 
on    E  E    is defined as a reflexive and transitive
relation    ≤  \leq    on    E  E   . Declaring two elements      a  ,  b   ∈  E   a,b\in E    to be equivalent if     a  ≤  b   a\leq b    and     b  ≤  a   b\leq a   ,
one gets an equivalence relation on    E  E   . The induced
order on the set of equivalence classes, which we
call  vertices , is a partial order denoted by
   ⪯  \preceq   . Thus, a preorder    ≤  \leq    on the ground set
   E  E    can also be defined as a partial order    ⪯  \preceq   
on the set of blocks of a partition of    E  E   . We will
depict    ≤  \leq    by the Hasse diagram
 [ Sta11 , Section 3.1]  of the associated partial
order    ⪯  \preceq    (where, as usual, the order increases
from bottom to top). In the language of
species  [ BLL98 ] , we are working with the
composition of the species of partial orders with
that of nonempty finite sets. Preorders on a finite
set    E  E    are in one-to-one correspondence with
topologies on    E  E     [ Sta11 , Exercise 3.3] . We
will usually denote preordered finite sets by small
greek letters. We will refer to the cardinality of
the ground set    E  E    as the  size  of a preorder
on    E  E    and to the cardinality of a vertex as the
 size  of this vertex. Figure   1   shows
the Hasse diagram of a preorder of size 5; there is
one vertex of size two and three single element
vertices. 
 
 
 Basic constructions for posets
 [ Sta11 , Sections 3.1-3.2] , such as
disjoint unions, ordinal sums, duality, order
ideals and filters and principal order ideals and
filters, extend naturally to preorders. For example,
an  order ideal  of a preorder on the ground
set    E  E    is defined as any subset     ℐ  ⊆  E   {\mathcal{I}}\subseteq E   
such that for every     b  ∈  ℐ   b\in{\mathcal{I}}    we have     a  ∈  ℐ   a\in{\mathcal{I}}   
for every     a  ∈  E   a\in E    with     a  ≤  b   a\leq b   . Thus, order
ideals are unions of vertices which are ideals of
the associated partial order    ⪯  \preceq   . Similarly,
an  order filter  (or  dual order ideal )
is defined as any subset     𝒥  ⊆  E   {\mathcal{J}}\subseteq E    such
that for every     a  ∈  𝒥   a\in{\mathcal{J}}    we have     b  ∈  𝒥   b\in{\mathcal{J}}    for
every     b  ∈  E   b\in E    with     a  ≤  b   a\leq b   . We will denote by
    𝒟  ⁡   (  e  )    \mathscr{D}(e)    (respectively,     𝒰  ⁡   (  e  )    \mathscr{U}(e)   ) the principal
order ideal (respectively, principal order filter)
generated by     e  ∈  E   e\in E   . We will sometimes abuse notation
and write     𝒟  ⁡   (  v  )    \mathscr{D}(v)    and     𝒰  ⁡   (  v  )    \mathscr{U}(v)    for a vertex    v  v   . 
 
 
 A preorder    ≤  \leq    on    E  E    is called
a  chain  if     a  ≤  b   a\leq b    or     b  ≤  a   b\leq a    for all
     a  ,  b   ∈  E   a,b\in E    (equivalently, the induced partial
order    ⪯  \preceq    is a total order). Chains are
preorders associated to linear arbors
 [ Cha25b , Section 4] ; the corresponding
arbor polytopes were studied in  [ Ath25 ] 
and called  composition polytopes . 
 
 
 
  2.3.  Poset combinatorics 

 
 Let     (  P  ,  ⪯  )   (P,\preceq)    be a finite poset and let
   d  d    be the length (one less than the cardinality)
of the longest chain in    P  P   . The  zeta
polynomial   [ Sta11 , Section 3.12]  of    P  P   
is the unique polynomial     𝒵  ⁡   (  P  ,  t  )    {\mathcal{Z}}(P,t)   
with the property that     𝒵  ⁡   (  P  ,   m  +  1   )    {\mathcal{Z}}(P,m+1)    is equal
to the number of    m  m   -element multichains in    P  P   ,
meaning sequences     (   p  1   ,   p  2   ,  …  ,   p  m   )   (p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{m})    of
elements of    P  P    such that      p  1   ⪯   p  2   ⪯  ⋯  ⪯   p  m    p_{1}\preceq p_{2}\preceq\cdots\preceq p_{m}   , for every     m  ∈  ℕ   m\in\mathbb{N}   . The degree of     𝒵  ⁡   (  P  ,  t  )    {\mathcal{Z}}(P,t)    is equal to    d  d   
and the leading coefficient is equal to the
number of (necessarily maximal) chains of    P  P   
of length    d  d   , divided by     d  !   d!   . We say that
   P  P    is  graded  if all maximal chains in
   P  P    have length    d  d   . 
 
 
 
  2.4.  Ehrhart theory 

 
 Let    𝒬  {\mathcal{Q}}    be a    d  d   -dimensional lattice polytope
in     ℝ  n   \mathbb{R}^{n}    (thus, every vertex of    𝒬  {\mathcal{Q}}    belongs
to     ℤ  n   \mathbb{Z}^{n}   ). The  Ehrhart polynomial  of
   𝒬  {\mathcal{Q}}    is the unique polynomial     Ehr  ⁡   (  𝒬  ,  t  )    {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}},t)   
with the property that      Ehr  ⁡   (  𝒬  ,  m  )    =   #  ⁡   (    m  ​  𝒬   ∩   ℕ  n    )     {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}},m)=\#(m{\mathcal{Q}}\cap\mathbb{N}^{n})    for every     m  ∈  ℕ   m\in\mathbb{N}   . The
Ehrhart polynomial     Ehr  ⁡   (  𝒬  ,  t  )    {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}},t)    has degree
   d  d    and thus 
 

  
  (3)

  2.5.  Generalized permutohedra 

 
 We briefly recall, with slightly adapted notation,
the definitions and some basic results from
Postnikov’s theory of generalized permutohedra, as
introduced in  [ Pos09 ] . This involves two
distinct notions, namely generalized permutohedra
and the subclass of    Y  Y   -type generalized
permutohedra. 
 
 
 Let    E  E    be a nonempty finite set. For a nonempty
subset    I  I    of    E  E   , the standard simplex     Δ  I   \Delta_{I}   
is the convex hull of the set of standard basis
vectors
    {   𝐞  e   :   e  ∈  I   }   \{{\rm\mathbf{e}}_{e}:e\in I\}   .    Y  Y   -type
generalized permutohedra are defined as Minkowski
sums of scaled standard simplices. More precisely,
given a collection     𝐲  =    {   y  I   }   I    \mathbf{y}={\{y_{I}\}}_{I}    of
nonnegative real numbers indexed by nonempty
subsets of    E  E   , 
 

  
  (4)  
  
       𝒫  y   ​   (  𝐲  )    =    ∑   ∅  ≠  I  ⊆  E      y  I   ​   Δ  I      {\mathcal{P}}^{y}(\mathbf{y})=\sum_{\varnothing\not=I\subseteq E}y_{I}\Delta_{I}

associated to    𝐲  \mathbf{y}   . 
 
 
 On the other hand, generalized permutohedra are
defined by linear equations and inequalities as
follows. Given a collection     𝐳  =    {   z  I   }   I    \mathbf{z}={\{z_{I}\}}_{I}    of nonnegative real numbers indexed

to    𝐳  \mathbf{z}   . By  [ Pos09 , Proposition 6.3] ,
every    Y  Y   -type generalized permutohedron is a

defining the collection    𝐳  \mathbf{z}    from the
collection    𝐲  \mathbf{y}    by setting 
 

  
  (6)  
  
      z  J   =    ∑   ∅  ≠  I  ⊆  J     y  I     z_{J}=\sum_{\varnothing\not=I\subseteq J}y_{I}

of polytopes:       𝒫  y   ​   (  𝐲  )    =    𝒫  z   ​   (  𝐳  )     {\mathcal{P}}^{y}(\mathbf{y})={\mathcal{P}}^{z}(\mathbf{z})   .

  8.  The    M  M   -triangle 

 
 This section discusses another enumerative
invariant of the poset of lattice points of a
preorder polytope, namely the    M  M   -triangle, and
extends in this setting a conjecture of
 [ Cha25b ]  on arbor polytopes. This
conjecture provided much of the motivation
behind the study of arbor polytopes; see

picture, which should extend verbatim to all
preorder polytopes.
