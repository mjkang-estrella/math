  Abstract. 
    
 An    n  n   -dimensional lattice polytope     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}   
can be associated to any composition    σ  \sigma    of
a positive integer    n  n   , as a special case of
constructions due to Pitman–Stanley and Chapoton.
The entries of the    h  h   -vector of    σ  \sigma   ,
introduced by Chapoton, enumerate the lattice
points in     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    by the number of their
nonzero coordinates. Chapoton conjectured that
this vector is equal to the    h  h   -vector of a
flag simplicial polytope. This paper proves this
conjecture. Moreover, it shows that the
gamma-vector associated to the    h  h   -vector of
   σ  \sigma    is nonnegative by means of an explicit
combinatorial interpretation and confirms certain
other conjectures of Chapoton on the lattice point
enumeration of composition polytopes. A
combinatorial interpretation of their
    h  ∗   h^{\ast}   -polynomials is deduced. 
  
 
 
  1.  Introduction 

 
 A composition of a positive integer    n  n    is
defined as a sequence     σ  =   (   r  1   ,   r  2   ,  …  ,   r  k   )    \sigma=(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k})   
of positive integers whose sum is equal to    n  n   .
Given such a composition    σ  \sigma   , we consider the
polytope     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    in     ℝ  n   {\mathbb{R}}^{n}    defined by the
inequalities      x  i   ≥  0   x_{i}\geq 0    for     1  ≤  i  ≤  n   1\leq i\leq n    and 
 

  
  (1)  
  
       x  1   +   x  2   +  ⋯  +   x   s  i     ≤   s  i    x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{s_{i}}\leq s_{i}    
    
 
 for     1  ≤  i  ≤  k   1\leq i\leq k   , where      s  i   =    r  1   +   r  2   +  ⋯  +   r  i     s_{i}=r_{1}+r_{2}+\cdots+r_{i}    and      x  1   ,   x  2   ,  …  ,   x  n    x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}    are the
standard coordinates of     ℝ  n   {\mathbb{R}}^{n}   . We refer to the
polytopes     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    in this paper as
 composition polytopes . 
 
 
 Composition polytopes are special cases of the
polytopes associated to arbors (defined as rooted
trees on the vertex set of blocks of a partition
of a finite set), introduced and studied by
Chapoton  [ 7 ] . They also arise as
Pitman–Stanley polytopes  [ 16 ]  when some of
the parameters defining these polytopes are
allowed to be zero. The composition polytope
    𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    reduces to the    n  n   th dilate of the
   n  n   -dimensional simplex in     ℝ  n   {\mathbb{R}}^{n}    with vertices
the origin and the unit coordinate vectors, when
   σ  \sigma    has one part, and to the Pitman–Stanley
polytope with all parameters equal to 1, when all
parts of    σ  \sigma    are equal to 1. 
 
 
 The face lattice of composition polytopes is well
understood. As observed in
 [ 16 , Theorem 20] ,     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}    is
combinatorially isomorphic to a product of
simplices, the dimensions of which are the parts
of    σ  \sigma   . The lattice point enumeration of
composition polytopes, which is more interesting,
has been studied for the more general classes of
arbor polytopes  [ 7 ]  and Pitman–Stanley
polytopes  [ 16 ] . Chapoton defined the
    h  h   -vector  of a composition    σ  \sigma    of    n  n   
(more generally, of an arbor) as the sequence
     h  ⁡   (  σ  )    =    (    h  i   ​   (  σ  )    )    0  ≤  i  ≤  n     h(\sigma)=(h_{i}(\sigma))_{0\leq i\leq n}   , where
     h  i   ​   (  σ  )    h_{i}(\sigma)    is the number of points in      𝒬  σ   ∩   ℕ  n    {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{n}    having exactly    i  i    nonzero coordinates
(this vector should not be confused with the
   h  h   -vector of the simple polytope     𝒬  σ   {\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma}   ).
We note that       h  0   ​   (  σ  )    =    h  n   ​   (  σ  )    =  1   h_{0}(\sigma)=h_{n}(\sigma)=1   .
The following statement is a special case of an
intriguing conjecture of Chapoton
 [ 7 , Conjecture 0.1] , stated for all
arbor polytopes; we refer to the discussion in
 [ 7 , Section 0.1]  for the motivation
behind it. 
 
 
   Conjecture 1.1     ( [ 7 ] ) .  
 
  The vector     h  ⁡   (  σ  )    h(\sigma)    is equal to the    h  h   -vector
of an    n  n   -dimensional simplicial polytope for every
composition    σ  \sigma    of    n  n   .  
 
 
 
 In particular,     h  ⁡   (  σ  )    h(\sigma)    should be palindromic
and unimodal, meaning that       h  i   ​   (  σ  )    =    h   n  −  i    ​   (  σ  )     h_{i}(\sigma)=h_{n-i}(\sigma)    for all     0  ≤  i  ≤  n   0\leq i\leq n    and       h  0   ​   (  σ  )    ≤    h  1   ​   (  σ  )    ≤  ⋯  ≤    h   ⌊   n  /  2   ⌋    ​   (  σ  )     h_{0}(\sigma)\leq h_{1}(\sigma)\leq\cdots\leq h_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(\sigma)   . Moreover, it is
suggested in  [ 7 , Section 0.1]  that
    h  ⁡   (  σ  )    h(\sigma)    should in fact be equal to the
   h  h   -vector of an    n  n   -dimensional flag simplicial
polytope (a simplicial polytope    𝒬  {\mathcal{Q}}    is said to
be  flag  if every clique in the one-skeleton
of    𝒬  {\mathcal{Q}}    is the vertex set of a face of    𝒬  {\mathcal{Q}}   ).
Thus, in view of Gal’s conjecture
 [ 9 , Conjecture 2.1.7]  (see also

is reasonable to expect that the generating
polynomial      h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    :=    ∑   i  =  0   n     h  i   ​   (  σ  )   ​   t  i      h(\sigma,t):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}h_{i}(\sigma)t^{i}    of     h  ⁡   (  σ  )    h(\sigma)    is
   γ  \gamma   -positive, meaning that

      h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    =    ∑   i  =  0    ⌊   n  /  2   ⌋      γ  i   ​   (  σ  )   ​   t  i   ​    (   1  +  t   )    n  −   2  ​  i        h(\sigma,t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\gamma_{i}(\sigma)t^{i}(1+t)^{n-2i}    
    
 
 for some nonnegative integers      γ  i   ​   (  σ  )    \gamma_{i}(\sigma)   .
The first main result of this paper states that
all these claims are true for composition polytopes. 
 
 
   Theorem 1.2   .  
 
  The vector     h  ⁡   (  σ  )    h(\sigma)    is equal to the    h  h   -vector
of an    n  n   -dimensional flag simplicial polytope
for every composition    σ  \sigma    of    n  n   . Moreover,
    h  ⁡   (  σ  ,  t  )    h(\sigma,t)    is    γ  \gamma   -positive and, in
particular, palindromic and unimodal.

conjecture of Chapoton  [ 7 ] , concerning
the Ehrhart polynomial of composition polytopes.
We recall (see, for instance,
 [ 14 , Section 4.6] ) that the  Ehrhart

   Theorem 1.3   .

     Ehr  ⁡   (   𝒬  σ   ,  t  )    =   𝒵  ⁡   (   P   rev  ⁡   (  σ  )     ,   t  +  1   )     {\rm Ehr}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\sigma},t)={\mathcal{Z}}(P_{{\rm rev}(\sigma)},t+1)

 Here is another intriguing conjecture
from  [ 7 ] , stated there for all arbor

   Conjecture 1.4     ( [ 7 ] ) .  
 
  For every composition    σ  \sigma   , all roots of the
