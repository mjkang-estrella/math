 Abstract. 
    
 The Links–Gould invariant  LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , t 1 ) \mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},t_{1})  of a link  L L  is a two-variable quantum generalization of the Alexander–Conway polynomial  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t)  and has been shown to share some of its most geometric features in several recent works. Here we suggest that  LG \mathrm{LG}  likely shares another of the Alexander polynomial’s most distinctive - and mysterious - properties: for alternating links, the coefficients of the Links-Gould polynomial alternate and appear to form a log-concave two-indexed sequence with no internal zeros. The former was observed by Ishii for knots with up to 10 crossings. We further conjecture that they satisfy a bidimensional property of unimodality, thereby replicating a long-standing conjecture of Fox (1962) regarding the Alexander polynomial, and a subsequent refinement by Stoimenow. We also point out that the Stoimenow conjecture reflects a more structural phenomenon: after a suitable normalization, the Alexander polynomial of an alternating link appears to be a Lorentzian polynomial. We give compelling experimental and computational evidence for these different properties.

 1.  Introduction 

 
 1.1.  The Alexander and Links-Gould polynomials of links 

 
 The Alexander polynomial  Δ L \Delta_{L}  is an isotopy invariant of links  L L  valued in  ℤ ⁡ [ t ± 1 ] \mathbbm{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]  and defined up to units. It was first defined in 1928 by James Waddell Alexander  [ Ale28 ]  and was the first polynomial invariant of knots. Remarkably, the Alexander polynomial remained the only known polynomial link invariant until Vaughan Jones discovered his eponymous polynomial half a century later  [ Jon85 ] . Designed to distinguish links up to isotopy, the Alexander polynomial also relates to some fundamental geometric properties of knots and links. For example, the degree of  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t)  is a lower bound for the 3-genus of  L L   [ Sei35 ] , and the Alexander polynomial of a fibered knot is monic  [ Neu65 ,  Rap60 ,  Sta61 ] . The Alexander invariant is also relevant to study the four-dimensional properties of a knot. For example, for a topologically slice knot  K K ,  Δ K ​ ( t ) \Delta_{K}(t)  takes a particular form known as the Fox-Milnor condition  [ FM66 ] . 
 
 
 It is also worth noting that  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t)  can be constructed as a quantum link invariant, that is, a Reshetikhin–Turaev-type invariant where it occurs in its palindromic normalization as the Alexander–Conway polynomial. It arises in this way by considering representations of either: the Hopf algebra  U q ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 ) U_{q}{\mathfrak{sl}(2)}  at fourth roots of unity, or the Hopf superalgebra  U q ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 1 | 1 ) U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(1|1) , see  [ Mur92 ,  Vir06 ] . In the latter perspective, the Alexander polynomial is the first in the family of Links–Gould invariants  LG m , n ​ ( L , t 0 , t 1 ) ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ t 0 ± 1 , t 1 ± 1 ] \mathrm{LG}^{m,n}(L;t_{0},t_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{\pm 1}]  obtained from the Reshetikhin–Turaev construction applied to representations of the Hopf superalgebras  U q ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m | n ) U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(m|n) , see  [ LG92 ,  DWLK99 ,  DW01 ] . The simplest case after the Alexander polynomial is derived from  U q ​ 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 2 | 1 ) U_{q}\mathfrak{gl}(2|1) . We shall denote the associated invariant  LG 2 , 1 \mathrm{LG}^{2,1}  by  LG \mathrm{LG}  and refer to it as the Links–Gould polynomial. 
 
 
 Although  LG \mathrm{LG}  is not yet known to have a classical geometric interpretation that would relate it even more closely to the Alexander polynomial, recent results show that it reproduces some of the Alexander polynomial’s most distinctive characteristics. Specifically, the degree of the Links–Gould polynomial gives a lower bound for the genus of a knot by work of two of the authors  [ KT ] . Also, the  LG \mathrm{LG}  invariant of a fibered link is  ℤ ⁡ [ q ± 1 ] \mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}] -monic following the recent work of López-Neumann and van der Veen  [ LNvdV ] . These two properties imply and improve upon similar statements for the Alexander polynomial. This is because  LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , t 1 ) \mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},t_{1})  specializes to  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t)  in at least two different ways  [ DWIL05 ,  Koh16 ,  KPM17 ] : 
 

 
 
 LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , − t 0 − 1 ) = Δ L ​ ( t 0 2 ) , LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , t 0 − 1 ) = Δ L ​ ( t 0 ) 2 . \mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},-t_{0}^{-1})=\Delta_{L}(t_{0}^{2})\,,\qquad\mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},t_{0}^{-1})=\Delta_{L}(t_{0})^{2}. 
 
 (1) 
 
 It is therefore reasonable to imagine that other properties of the Alexander polynomial should have their counterparts for the Links-Gould invariant. 
 
 
 
 1.2.  The Alexander polynomial of alternating links 

 
 A class where the Alexander polynomial has an interesting behavior is that of alternating links: the Alexander polynomial of an alternating link is alternating with no internal zeros by works of Crowell  [ Cro59 ]  and Murasugi  [ Mur58 ] . This means that if we consider the Conway normalization of  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t) , we can write: 
 

 
 
 Δ L ​ ( t ) = ∑ k = − n n a k ​ t k , with  ​ a l ⋅ a l + 1 < 0 ​  for all  ​ l ∈ [ [ − ( n − 1 ) , n − 1 ] ] . \Delta_{L}(t)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}a_{k}t^{k},\quad\text{with }a_{l}\cdot a_{l+1}<0\text{ for all }l\in[\![-(n-1),n-1]\!]\,. 
 
 
 Moreover, Fox conjectured that the absolute values of the coefficients of the Alexander polynomial of an alternating link form a unimodal sequence  [ Fox61 ] , i.e. 
 

 
 
 | a − n | ≤ | a − n + 1 | ≤ ⋯ ≤ | a k | = | a k + 1 | = ⋯ = | a m | ≥ | a m + 1 | ≥ ⋯ ≥ | a n | ​ ,  − n ≤ k ≤ m ≤ n . |a_{-n}|\leq|a_{-n+1}|\leq\cdots\leq|a_{k}|=|a_{k+1}|=\cdots=|a_{m}|\geq|a_{m+1}|\geq\cdots\geq|a_{n}|\text{, }-n\leq k\leq m\leq n\,. 
 
 
 Stoimenow refined the statement by conjecturing that the sequence should be log-concave with no internal zeros  [ Sto05 ] . This in turn would imply that the sequence is trapezoidal: 
 

 
 
 | a − n | < | a − n + 1 | < ⋯ < | a k | = | a k + 1 | = ⋯ = | a m | > | a m + 1 | > ⋯ > | a n | ​ ,  − n ≤ k ≤ m ≤ n . |a_{-n}|<|a_{-n+1}|<\cdots<|a_{k}|=|a_{k+1}|=\cdots=|a_{m}|>|a_{m+1}|>\cdots>|a_{n}|\text{, }-n\leq k\leq m\leq n\,. 
 
 
 Different forms of the Fox conjecture have been proved over the years for many infinite families of knots and links, see  [ Par78 ,  Har79 ,  Mur85 ,  OS03 ,  Jon10 ,  Sto16 ,  AC21 ,  HMV24 ,  AJK ,  KMP ] . A summary of these studies and their scope can be found in  [ AJK ] . It also includes several recent works  [ AC21 ,  HMV24 ,  AJK ,  KMP ] . The general conjecture, however, remains unsolved to this day. 
 
 
 
 1.3.  Similar properties for the Links-Gould polynomial of alternating links? 

 
 In his early foundational studies on the Links-Gould invariant, Ishii conjectured that  LG \mathrm{LG}  displays the same “alternating” behavior as the Alexander polynomial on alternating knots. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1   ( [ Ish06 ] ) . 
 
 The LG polynomial  LG ⁡ ( K , t 0 , t 1 ) = ∑ i , j a i ​ j ​ t 0 i ​ t 1 j \mathrm{LG}(K;t_{0},t_{1})=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}t_{0}^{i}t_{1}^{j}  of an alternating knot  K K  is “alternating”:
 a i ​ j ​ a i ′ ​ j ′ ≥ 0 if  ​ i + j − i ′ − j ′ ​  is even, and a i ​ j ​ a i ′ ​ j ′ ≤ 0 otherwise a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\geq 0\quad\text{if }i+j-i^{\prime}-j^{\prime}\text{ is even, and}\quad a_{ij}a_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\leq 0\quad\text{otherwise} . 
 
 
 
 Ishii verified Conjecture  1.1  for all prime knots with up to  10 10  crossings. Using the fact that  LG \mathrm{LG}  is equivalent by a change of variables to the Garoufalidis–Kashaev  V 1 V_{1} -polynomial of links  [ GK ,  GHK+c ,  GHK+a ] , the computations of Garoufalidis–Li for  V 1 V_{1}  show that the Ishii conjecture is true for all prime knots with up to  16 16  crossings, see  [ GL ] . 
 
 
 
 In this work we conjecture that once again the Links-Gould invariant  LG \mathrm{LG}  and the Alexander polynomial follow similar patterns. First, Conjecture  1.1  can be made more precise, echoing the behavior of the Alexander polynomial.

 A two-indexed sequence  ( a i ​ j ) i , j ∈ ℤ (a_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}  is said to have  no interior zeros  if 
 

 
 
 Conv ​ ( Supp ​ ( a i ​ j ) ) ∩ ℤ 2 = Supp ​ ( a i ​ j ) , \text{Conv}(\text{Supp}(a_{ij}))\cap\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\text{Supp}(a_{ij})\,, 
 
 
 where  Supp ​ ( a i ​ j ) = { ( i , j ) ∈ ℤ 2 : a i ​ j ≠ 0 } \text{Supp}(a_{ij})=\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,:\,a_{ij}\neq 0\}  and  Conv ​ ( X ) \text{Conv}(X)  is the convex hull of a subset  X X  of  ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.3   (strong version of the Ishii conjecture) . 
 
 The LG polynomial  LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , t 1 ) = ∑ i , j a i ​ j ​ t 0 i ​ t 1 j \mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},t_{1})=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}t_{0}^{i}t_{1}^{j}  of an  alternating link   L L  with  μ \mu  components is “alternating” with 
 

 
 
 a i ​ j = ( − 1 ) μ + i + j + 1 ​ | a i ​ j | a_{ij}=(-1)^{\mu+i+j+1}\,|a_{ij}| 
 
 
 for all  i , j i,j . Moreover,  ( a i ​ j ) (a_{ij})  has no interior zeros. 
 
 
 
 Remark 1.4 . 
 
 Conjecture  1.3  is  not  true for other two-variable generalizations of the Alexander polynomial, i.e. for the two variable polynomial invariant  Δ 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \Delta_{\mathfrak{sl}_{3}}  derived from representations of  U q ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 U_{q}\mathfrak{sl}_{3}  at a fourth root of unity, studied by one of the authors  [ Har , Figure 10] . 
 
 
 
 We further propose that  LG \mathrm{LG}  should satisfy bidimensional properties of log-concavity and unimodality, thereby generalizing, at least in spirit, the Fox and Stoimenow conjectures. 
 
 
 Definition 1.5 . 
 
 A sequence  ( a i ) i ∈ ℤ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}  is  log-concave  if, for every  i ∈ ℤ i\in\mathbb{Z} , we have:  a i − 1 ​ a i + 1 ≤ a i 2 a_{i-1}\,a_{i+1}\leq a_{i}^{2} . 
 
 
 
 Remark 1.6 . 
 
 Log-concavity, as well as its generalizations we will define hereafter, is invariant under multiplication by a monomial. 
 
 
 
 Definition 1.7 . 
 
 We will refer to a bidimensional sequence  ( a i ​ j ) i , j ∈ ℤ (a_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}  as  2d log-concave  if for every  i , j , k , l ∈ ℤ i,j,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}  : 
 

 
 
 a i + k , j + l ​ a i − k , j − l ≤ a i ​ j 2 . a_{i+k,j+l}\,a_{i-k,j-l}\leq a_{ij}^{2}\,. 
 
 
 
 
 
 We understand 2d log-concavity as being indicative of the sequence being log-concave “in all possible directions”. In particular if a sequence is 2d log-concave, its restriction to any row, column or diagonal in  ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2}  is a log-concave singly-indexed sequence. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.8   (2d log-concavity) . 
 
 For any alternating link  L L , the absolute values of the coefficients of  LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , t 1 ) \mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},t_{1})  form a 2d log-concave sequence (with no interior zeros). 
 
 
 
 Definition 1.9 . 
 
 We say that a two-indexed sequence  ( a i ​ j ) i , j ∈ ℤ (a_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}  is  unimodal  if, for any  K ≥ 0 K\geq 0 , the two-indexed sequence  ( a i ​ j ​  1 | a i ​ j | ≥ K ) i , j ∈ ℤ (a_{ij}\,\mathds{1}_{|a_{ij}|\geq K})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}  has no interior zeros. 
 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.10   (unimodality) . 
 
 For any alternating link  L L , the 2d sequence of absolute values of the coefficients of  LG ⁡ ( L , t 0 , t 1 ) \mathrm{LG}(L;t_{0},t_{1})  is unimodal. 
 
 
 
 We will give evidence in support of these assumptions. Specifically, we show that Conjectures  1.3 ,  1.8 , and  1.10  hold for all alternating prime knots with up to 12 crossings, as well as for several infinite families of alternating links. 
 
 
 Note that despite the obvious similarities between Conjectures  1.3 ,  1.8 , and  1.10  and comparable statements for the Alexander polynomial, these conjectures are not straightforward generalizations of the well-known properties for  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t) . None of the specializations from Equation ( 1 ) allow the properties of the Alexander polynomial on alternating links to be understood as consequences of the properties of the Links–Gould polynomial. This was pointed out in  [ Koh18 ]  about the Ishii conjecture, and remains true here. This suggests that unlike the genus bounds and fiberedness criteria, these properties for alternating links are more likely the trace of a deep structural behavior common to both link invariants. 
 
 
 Let us point out that in our journey to understand whether the  LG \mathrm{LG}  polynomial is more closely related to the Jones polynomial or to the Alexander polynomial, these new hypothetical properties of the Links-Gould polynomial reinforce its proximity with  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t) . Indeed, the Jones polynomial of an alternating link is alternating  [ Thi87 ] , however, it is not always unimodal. Consider, for example,

 1.4.  A strengthening of the Fox-Stoimenow conjecture for the Alexander polynomial 

 
 In recent work, Hafner, Mészáros, and Vidinas proved the Fox–Stoimenow conjecture for special alternating links  [ HMV24 ]  by showing that a well chosen normalization and homogenization of the Alexander polynomial is Lorentzian, a property introduced and studied by Brändén and Huh  [ BH20 ] . Observe that being Lorentzian is a very strong assumption and, as we will see, it is not satisfied by the  LG \mathrm{LG}  invariant for any obvious choice of variables, not even for all  special  alternating links. However, in the case of  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t) , we show that for  any  alternating link, the Stoimenow extension of the Fox conjecture is equivalent to the divided power normalization of the Alexander polynomial being Lorentzian. We refer to the Definitions  3.5  and  3.8  for precise definitions of the (divided power) normalization and Lorentzian property. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.11   (Lorentzian property for Alexander) . 
 
 The homogenized Alexander polynomial of an alternating link  Homog ​ ( Δ L ​ ( − t ) ) \mathrm{Homog}(\Delta_{L}(-t))  is denormalized Lorentzian if and only if the absolute values of the coefficients of the Alexander polynomial of the link form a log-concave sequence (with no internal zeros). 
 
 
 
 That the Alexander polynomial is denormalized Lorenztian follows from the structure of  Δ L ​ ( t ) \Delta_{L}(t)  for  L L  an alternating link and from Proposition  1.11 , which is an application of the following elementary algebraic result. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.12 . 
 
 For  P ⁡ ( t ) P(t)  a  one-variable  polynomial with nonnegative real coefficients, its sequence of coefficients is log-concave if and only if  Q ⁡ ( t , z ) := Homog ⁡ ( P ) Q(t,z):=\mathrm{Homog}(P)  is denormalized Lorentzian. 
 
 
 
 Thus, following Stoimenow  [ Sto05 ]  the Alexander polynomial satisfies the Lorentzian property for all alternating knots with up to 16 crossings, and this is conjecturally true for all alternating links. 
 
 
 
 We conclude this introduction with a question. 
 
 
 Question 1.13 . 
 
 Is the homogenized Links–Gould polynomial of alternating links denormalized Lorentzian after a proper change of variables? 
 
 
 
 
 1.5.  Organization of the paper 

 
 Section  2  contains the definition and elementary properties of the Links–Gould link polynomial. Some basic properties of log-concave sequences are presented in Section  3 , as well as the definition and characteristics of Lorentzian polynomials. In particular, log-concavity is related to unimodality and being a trapezoidal sequence. These properties will be useful to simplify the calculations carried out later in the paper. Propositions  1.11  and  1.12  are proved in Section  4  and the implications for the Fox–Stoimenow conjecture are discussed. Section  5  presents experimental and computational evidence in support of Conjectures  1.3 ,  1.8  and  1.10 . Indeed, using a  Python  program  [ HKST ]  that analyzes the data from the  LG \mathrm{LG} -explorer  [ DW ] , we confirm that for all alternating knots available in the  LG \mathrm{LG} -explorer database, these conjectures are true. This includes all alternating knots with up to 12 crossings, and a selection of 13 to 16 crossing alternating knots with small braid index. We also show that the Links–Gould polynomial of the trefoil is not denormalized Lorenztian for various choices of variables. Moreover, we prove that all three conjectures are true for two infinite families of links:  ( 2 , n ) (2,n) -torus links and twist knots. Their Links–Gould invariants are presented in the Figures of Section  6 .

 2.  The Links-Gould polynomial: definition and properties 

 
 In this section we closely follow the ideas exposed in  [ GHK+c ,  GHK+a ] . For a more representation theoretic approach to the Links–Gould invariant see  [ DWLK99 ,  GHK+b ] . 
 
 
 2.1.  The Reshetikhin–Turaev functor 

 
 We review briefly the well-known Reshetikhin–Turaev functor
  [ RT90 ,  Tur94 ]  from tangles to tensor products of endomorphisms of a vector
space and its dual. 
 
 
 A tangle diagram can be decomposed into a finite number of elementary
oriented tangle diagrams shown in Figure  1 . Moreover, up to
isotopy, we may assume that for any horizontal line drawn on the diagram there is
at most one critical point or a single crossing. 
 
 
 Figure 1.  Elementary oriented tangles. 
 
 
 Set  V V  a vector space over a field  𝕂 \mathbbm{K}  of characteristic zero and consider
 V ∗ = End ⁡ ( V , 𝕂 ) V^{\ast}=\mathrm{End}(V,\mathbbm{K})  its dual. Each elementary oriented tangle is associated
to a linear map under the Reshetikhin-Turaev functor
 F F , as shown in Figure  2 . By composing these elementary maps, one obtains a linear map  F T F_{T}

 3.  Log-concavity, unimodality, Lorentzian polynomials: properties, examples, counter-examples 

 
 3.1.  Log-concavity, unimodality 

 
 First we give some elementary properties of log-concave sequences that will be helpful in the next section. The following definitions, as mentioned in the introduction, are recalled below. 
 
 
 
 We say that a sequence  ( a i ) i ∈ ℤ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}  is  log-concave  if, for every  i ∈ ℤ i\in\mathbb{Z} , we have: 
 

 
 
 a i − 1 ​ a i + 1 ≤ a i 2 . a_{i-1}\,a_{i+1}\leq a_{i}^{2}\,. 
 
 
 Likewise, we refer to a bidimensional sequence  ( a i ​ j ) i , j ∈ ℤ (a_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}  as  2d log-concave  if for every  i , j , k , l ∈ ℤ i,j,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}  : 
 

 
 
 a i + k , j + l ​ a i − k , j − l ≤ a i ​ j 2 . a_{i+k,j+l}\,a_{i-k,j-l}\leq a_{ij}^{2}\,. 
 
 
 
 
 Proposition 3.1 . 
 
 For  ( a i ) i ∈ ℤ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}  a log-concave sequence of non-negative real numbers with no internal zeros, the sequence  ( a i / a i − 1 ) i ∈ ℤ (a_{i}/a_{i-1})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}  is non-increasing. 
 
 
 
 Corollary 3.2 . 
 
 If  ( a i ) i ∈ ℤ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}  is a log-concave sequence of non-negative real numbers with no internal zeros, then the sequence is trapezoidal and, in particular, it is unimodal. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 The sequence  ( r i ) = ( a i / a i − 1 ) (r_{i})=(a_{i}/a_{i-1})  is non-increasing on the interval where it is well-defined. So for some  − ∞ ≤ p ≤ q ≤ + ∞ -\infty\leq p\leq q\leq+\infty  one can write: 
 

 
 
 r i > 1 ⇔ i ≤ p ,  and  r i < 1 ⇔ i ≥ q . r_{i}>1\iff i\leq p\,,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad r_{i}<1\iff i\geq q\,. 
 
 
 This precisely means that  ( a i ) (a_{i})  is trapezoidal and therefore unimodal.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Remark 3.3 . 
 
 Corollary  3.2  is not true if one does not assume that the sequence does not have internal zeros. Indeed, a sequence can have multiple local maxima and still be log-concave, as long as the “bumps” are far apart enough. Consider for example: 
 

 
 
 ( a i ) = ( … , 0 , 1 , 0 , 0 , 1 , 0 , 0 , … ) . (a_{i})=(\ldots,0,1,0,0,1,0,0,\ldots)\,. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Corollary 3.4 . 
 
 [Generalized log-concavity inequality]
Suppose  ( a i ) i ∈ ℤ (a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}  is a log-concave sequence of non-negative real numbers with no internal zeros. For all  i , k ∈ ℤ i,k\in\mathbb{Z} , we have: 
 

 
 
 a i − k ​ a i + k ≤ a i 2 . a_{i-k}\,a_{i+k}\leq a_{i}^{2}\,. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 If any of the three terms is zero, then the inequality obviously holds, because the sequence has no internal zeros. So we can suppose that all three terms are nonzero. In that case, the inequality we wish to prove can be written as follows: 
 

 
 
 a i + k a i ≤ a i a i − k . \frac{a_{i+k}}{a_{i}}\,\leq\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i-k}}\,. 
 
 
 But we know that the sequence  ( a i / a i − 1 ) (a_{i}/a_{i-1})  is non-increasing and thus: 
 

 
 
 b i = a i a i − k = a i a i − 1 × a i − 1 a i − 2 × … × a i − k + 1 a i − k b_{i}=\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i-k}}=\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i-1}}\times\frac{a_{i-1}}{a_{i-2}}\times\ldots\times\frac{a_{i-k+1}}{a_{i-k}} 
 
 
 is the product of  k k  positive non-increasing sequences. So  ( b i ) (b_{i})  is a non-increasing sequence and we have: 
 

 
 
 b i + k ≤ b i ,  i.e.  a i + k a i ≤ a i a i − k . ∎ b_{i+k}\leq b_{i}\,,\quad\text{ i.e. }\quad\frac{a_{i+k}}{a_{i}}\,\leq\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i-k}}\,.\qed

 Proposition  1.11  shines a new light on the Fox-Stoimenow conjecture. Indeed, it suggests that a reasonable strategy to attempt to prove this conjecture in full generality is to further pursue the Hafner–Mészáros–Vidinas approach  [ HMV24 ] . From Proposition  3.11 , the Lorentzian property of the denormalized Alexander polynomial is independent of its presentation as an honest polynomial. 
 
 
 Moreover, assuming that the Fox–Stoimenow conjecture is true, Proposition  3.10  implies that the homogenization of the square of the Alexander polynomial  Homog ⁡ ( Δ L ​ ( − t ) 2 ) \mathrm{Homog}(\Delta_{L}(-t)^{2})  for an alternating link  L L  is also denormalized Lorentzian, and in turn its sequence of coefficients is log-concave, see Proposition  3.9 . Recall that the square of the Alexander polynomial is a specialization of the Links–Gould invariant of links, see Equation ( 1 ). This observation suggests that some form of the Fox–Stoimenow conjecture lifts to  LG \mathrm{LG} , which we formulate in Conjectures  1.3 ,  1.8 , and  1.10 . 
 
 
 Regarding the Lorentzian property for  LG \mathrm{LG} , note that it is sensitive to the choice of variables used to write the polynomial. Although the tests we carried out have failed for the obvious choices of variables, see Subsection  5.4 , it is our prediction that  LG \mathrm{LG}  is Lorentzian for some choice of variables yet to be determined. Indeed, the 2d log-concavity property that was tested successfully shows that  LG \mathrm{LG}  displays a very structured behavior on alternating links.
