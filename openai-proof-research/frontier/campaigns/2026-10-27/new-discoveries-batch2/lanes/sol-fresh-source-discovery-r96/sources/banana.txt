 Log-Concavity of the Grothendieck Classes of Banana Graphs and Clasped Necklaces 
 
 
 Stephanie Chen
 
 Address:  Department of Mathematics, Harvard University, Cambridge, Massachusetts, 02138,
U.S.A.
 
 Email address:  stephchen@math.harvard.edu 
 

 Abstract. 
    
 The Grothendieck classes of melonic graphs satisfy a recursive relation and may be written as polynomials in the class of the moduli space  ℳ 0 , 4 \mathcal{M}_{0,4}  with nonnegative integer coefficients, conjectured to be log-concave. In this article, we investigate log-concavity and ultra-log-concavity for the Grothendieck class of banana graphs and the three families of polynomials involved in the recursive relation. We prove that all four are log-concave, establishing the specific case of banana graphs for the log-concavity conjecture. We additionally introduce the infinite family of clasped necklaces, melonic graphs obtained by replacing an edge of a  2 2 -banana with a string of  m m -bananas. Using the recursive relation, we explicitly compute the classes of clasped necklaces and prove that they too are log-concave.

 1.  Introduction 

 
 In a series of papers beginning with  [ AM09 ] , Aluffi and Marcolli study the algebro-geometric Feynman rule defined by associating to a Feynman graph  Γ \Gamma  the class of its graph hypersurface complement  𝕌 ⁡ ( Γ ) \mathbb{U}(\Gamma)  in the Grothendieck ring of varieties. Aluffi, Marcolli, and Qaisar apply this process to melonic graphs in  [ AMQ23 ] , motivated by the appearance of melonic graphs as the dominating diagrams in the large  N N  limit for the  O ​ ( N ) 3 O(N)^{3}  tensor model of Klebanov and Tarnopolsky  [ KT17 ] . After obtaining a recursive formula for computing the Grothendieck classes of melonic graphs, they proved that the classes may be written as polynomials in the class  𝕊 := [ ℳ 0 , 4 ] \mathbb{S}:=[\mathcal{M}_{0,4}]  with positive integer coefficients and further conjectured that these polynomials are log-concave, i.e. the coefficients satisfy the inequality  a i − 1 ​ a i + 1 ≤ a i 2 a_{i-1}a_{i+1}\leq a_{i}^{2} . 
 
 
 In this note, we prove the conjecture for two infinite families of melonic graphs and further investigate log-concavity and related properties of the polynomials which appear in the recursive formula. 
 
 
 1.1.  Banana graphs 

 
 In scalar field theory, one studies the Feynman graphs of the theory; these are finite graphs with both internal edges (edges which have both a starting and ending vertex) and external ‘half edges’ (only one end attached to a vertex) that satisfy certain properties in relation to the Langrangian for the theory. To each Feynman graph of a given theory we associate a Feynman integral which captures the combinatorics of the graph. In sufficiently large spacetime dimension, the integral is defined on the complement of the vanishing locus of the Kirchhoff-Symanzik polynomial of the graph  Ψ Γ ​ ( t ) \Psi_{\Gamma}(t) , a homogeneous polynomial in the internal edges of the graph. Consequently, in  [ AM11a ] , Aluffi and Marcolli define the Grothendieck class of a graph with  n n  internal edges to be the class  [ 𝔸 n ∖ X ^ Γ ] [\mathbb{A}^{n}\setminus\hat{X}_{\Gamma}]  in the Grothendieck ring of varieties  K 0 ​ ( Var ℚ ) K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}}) , where  X ^ Γ \hat{X}_{\Gamma}  is the vanishing locus of  Ψ Γ ​ ( t ) \Psi_{\Gamma}(t)  in  𝔸 n \mathbb{A}^{n} . This process is described briefly in section  2.2  below and in far more detail in  [ Mar10a ]  and  [ Mar10b ] . 
 
 
 Banana graphs are graphs consisting of two vertices and multiple edges between them. An  n n -banana, denoted  Γ n \Gamma_{n} , is a banana graph with  n n  edges. The physical motivation behind studying banana graphs comes from scalar field theories with an interaction term of the form  ℒ int ​ ( ϕ ) = ϕ n \mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}(\phi)=\phi^{n} , for which  Γ n \Gamma_{n}  is a vacuum bubble Feynman graph, i.e. a Feynman graph without external edges.  1.2.  Log-concavity 

 
 A sequence  a 0 , … , a n a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}  of real numbers is  log-concave  if it satisfies the property  a k 2 ≥ a k − 1 ​ a k + 1 a_{k}^{2}\geq a_{k-1}a_{k+1}  for all  0 < k < n 0<k<n .
One classical example of a log-concave sequence is given by the binomial sequence  ( n 0 ) , ( n 1 ) , … , ( n n ) {n\choose 0},{n\choose 1},\ldots,{n\choose n} . A real polynomial  f ⁡ ( x ) = a n ​ x n + … + a 0 f(x)=a_{n}x^{n}+\ldots+a_{0}  is said to be log-concave if its coefficients  a 0 , … , a n a_{0},\ldots,a_{n}  form a log-concave sequence. By a result of Newton’s, any real-rooted polynomial is log-concave  [ Sta06 , Thm 2] ,  [ Brä14 , Lem 1.1] . 
 
 
 Log-concavity has been a central property of many conjectures, one of the most famous being the Heron-Rota-Welsh conjecture, which states the the characteristic polynomial of a matroid is log-concave. This conjecture was settled positively by Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz in  [ AHK18 ]  using a combinatorial analogue of the Hodge-Riemann relations by studying the Chow ring of a matroid. A brief discussion of the proof is given in  [ Bak18 ] . See  [ Sta06 ] ,  [ Bre94 ] ,  [ Brä14 ]  for more general surveys on log-concavity, related properties, and methods to prove sequences have these properties.

 1.3.  Summary of paper 

 
 The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we recall the recursive formula for the Grothendieck class of a melonic graph and some key results on log-concave polynomials that we use in our proofs. We present results on the log-concavity and ultra log-concavity of the polynomials ( 1.4 ) in section 3. For clarity, most of the computations used in the proofs are deferred until section 5. Finally, in section 4, we derive the Grothendieck classes of clasped necklaces and establish their log-concavity as polynomials in  𝕊 \mathbb{S} .

 2.  Grothendieck classes of melonic graphs 

 
 2.1.  Grothendieck ring of varieties 

 
 We first briefly recall the construction of the Grothendieck ring of varieties. Fix a field  k k  of characteristic 0. The Grothendieck group of  k k -varieties, denoted  K 0 ​ ( Var k ) K_{0}(\text{Var}_{k}) , is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of  k k -varieties modulo the cut-and-paste relation 
 

 
 
 [ X ] = [ Y ] + [ X ∖ Y ] [X]=[Y]+[X\setminus Y] 
 
 
 for any variety  X X  and closed subvariety  Y Y .
The group  K 0 ​ ( Var k ) K_{0}(\text{Var}_{k})  admits a ring structure by defining multiplication of classes of varieties by  [ X ] ⋅ [ Y ] = [ X × Y ] [X]\cdot[Y]=[X\times Y]  and extending by linearity. The resulting ring is called the Grothendieck ring of  k k -varieties. One may observe that any ring-valued invariant of algebraic  k k -varieties which is preserved by isomorphisms, satisfies the cut-and-paste relation, and is multiplicative over products must factor through  K 0 ​ ( Var k ) K_{0}(\text{Var}_{k}) , hence the assignment of a variety  X X  to its Grothendieck class  [ X ] ∈ K 0 ​ ( Var k ) [X]\in K_{0}(\text{Var}_{k})  may be viewed as a ‘universal Euler characteristic’. 
 
 
 We fix notation for a couple key classes. First, we denote by  𝕃 := [ 𝔸 1 ] \mathbb{L}:=[\mathbb{A}^{1}]  the class of the affine line.
We also define  𝕋 := [ 𝔾 m ] = 𝕃 − 1 \mathbb{T}:=[\mathbb{G}_{m}]=\mathbb{L}-1  and  𝕊 := [ ℙ 1 ∖ { 0 , 1 , ∞ } ] = 𝕋 − 1 \mathbb{S}:=[\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}]=\mathbb{T}-1 , the classes of the multiplicative group and the projective line with three points removed respectively. Note that  𝕊 \mathbb{S}  also arises as the class  [ ℳ 0 , 4 ] [\mathcal{M}_{0,4}]  of the moduli space of stable genus  0 0  curves with  4 4  marked points.

They defined a motivic Feynman rule to be one that factors through  K 0 ​ ( Var ℚ ) K_{0}(\text{Var}_{\mathbb{Q}})  via the Grothendieck class of a graph, to be defined below.

 2.3.  Melonic graphs 

 
 The authors of  [ AMQ23 ]  were interested in melonic graphs due to their role as the dominating diagram in the CTKT models of  [ BGRR11 ]  in the large  N N  limit. Melonic graphs are graphs that may be constructed by starting with a single edge (and two vertices) and repeatedly replacing edges with strings of banana graphs. More precisely, a melonic construction is defined as follows.
