                                                      On a positivity conjecture of Fasi and Higham for Padé
                                                             approximants to the matrix exponential
                                                                                         Francesco Scardino∗

                                                                                               Abstract
                                                       We study the positivity conjecture of Fasi and Higham [1, Conjecture 3.2], which concerns
                                                   the Taylor coefficients of the reciprocal of the denominator qk,m (z) of the Padé approximant
                                                   to the matrix exponential. The conjecture asserts that these coefficients are positive whenever
                                                   m is odd. We prove full positivity for k = 0 and eventual positivity whenever the unique
                                                   positive real zero of qk,m is the unique zero of minimal modulus. We also give an explicit
                                                   computable sufficient threshold beyond which all coefficients are positive. Using Hermite scaling
                                                   with leading-coefficient normalization, we prove that this spectral minimality holds for each fixed
                                                   odd m and all sufficiently large k.
                                                       The unrestricted conjecture is nevertheless false. We give exact rational
                                                                                                                             √       counterexamples
                                                   and prove a transient scaling theorem showing that on the scale n ≍ k + 1 the normalized
                                                   coefficients converge to an explicit oscillatory Hermite profile. For m = 5 this profile is negative
                                                   at one point, yielding infinitely many counterexamples. The counterexamples are not diagonal,
                                                   and the diagonal case k = m, which is the case relevant in practice, remains open. These results
                                                   do not imply a failure of the Fasi–Higham algorithm. Rather, they delimit the range in which
                                                   the auxiliary positivity conjecture can be used as a theorem.

    Fasi and Higham [1] developed an algorithm based on Padé approximation for computing the
matrix exponential eA , A ∈ Cd×d , in arbitrary precision. They also derived a new representation
for the truncation error of a general [k/m] Padé approximant and showed how it can be used to
compute practical error bounds for truncated Taylor series and diagonal Padé approximants. In
the first case, the bound is cheap to compute, requiring only O(d2 ) flops [1]. For diagonal Padé
approximants the new bound requires O(d3 ) low-precision flops, but if their positivity conjecture
were true then this cost would be reduced to O(d2 ) flops.
    We now restate that conjecture.
Conjecture 1. Let k ≥ 0 and m ≥ 1 be integers. If m is odd, then all the coefficients of the Taylor
              −1
expansion of qk,m (z) at 0 are positive.
    The present paper concerns the conjecture as a mathematical statement in its full stated range
of parameters. Our counterexamples do not show that the algorithm of Fasi and Higham fails. The
case m = 1, which occurs in one of the algorithmic settings, is positive for every k, and the explicit
finite counterexamples below have k substantially larger than m. Moreover, the O(d2 ) bound that
the conjecture would provide concerns the diagonal approximants k = m, whose denominators
are normalized reverse Bessel polynomials [3]. The diagonal case is precisely the case that our
counterexamples leave untouched, and it remains open. The practical availability of that bound is
therefore neither established nor excluded by the present results. Thus the point of the counterex-
amples is that the unrestricted positivity conjecture cannot be used without additional hypotheses.
The positive results below identify such hypotheses, while the transient scaling theorem explains
the mechanism by which the unrestricted statement fails.

    The paper is organized as follows. Section 3 proves the case k = 0. Section 4 introduces
the general odd-m notation and the representation formulas and proves that all zeros are simple.
Section 5 gives the Eneström–Kakeya bounds and the elementary zero-location and monotonicity
facts, including the unique positive real zero for odd m. Sections 6 and 7 prove the Hermite
zero asymptotics and Property (D1) for large k. Sections 8 and 9 use partial fractions to obtain
the positivity threshold, the exact counterexamples, and the transient scaling limit. Section 10
summarizes the results.

from math import comb, prod

def omega(k, m, j):
    den = prod(range(k + m - j + 1, k + m + 1))
    return Fraction(comb(m, j), den)

def coeff(k, m, n):
    c = [Fraction(0) for _ in range(n + 1)]

        )
    return c[n]

10     Conclusion
We have shown that the positivity conjecture of Fasi and Higham is true in the case k = 0 for
every odd m, and that for general odd m Property (D1) implies eventual positivity of the Taylor
                 −1
coefficients of qk,m . A Hermite asymptotic analysis based on leading-coefficient normalization proves
that Property (D1) holds for each fixed odd m and all sufficiently large k.
    The unrestricted conjecture is nevertheless false. Exact rational computations give concrete
finite counterexamples,
           √               and the transient scaling theorem gives a structural explanation: on the
scale n ≍ k + 1 the normalized coefficients converge to an explicit oscillatory profile determined
by the zeros of Hm . For m = 5 this profile is negative, yielding infinitely many counterexamples.
Thus the positivity phenomenon is real but conditional, and it cannot be asserted for all odd m
and all k without further restrictions.
    The diagonal case k = m, which is the case relevant to the error bounds of [1], remains an open
problem. It is not contradicted by the counterexamples above, and Theorem 7.1 does not cover it.
