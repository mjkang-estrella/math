                                                 THE DISCRETE MONOTONICITY PROPERTY IN RANK TWO

                                                                                            FEI XUE


                                                    Abstract. The Betke–Henk–Wills conjecture predicts a bound for lattice-point counts
                                                    in terms of successive minima. Malikiosis introduced the discrete monotonicity property
                                                    for normalized residue counts as a sufficient condition for this bound and its extension
                                                    to nonsymmetric convex bodies. We prove that every compact convex set satisfies this
                                                    property for lattice subgroups of rank at most two, in arbitrary ambient dimension.

   By the reductions and initial cases in Section 2, it suffices to compare moduli r and r + 1
for planar lattice-convex sets. We first choose a lattice-convex subset containing exactly one
point from each represented class at the larger modulus (Lemma 3.1). A suitable lattice basis
then places the points of each class at the smaller modulus among four corners of a lattice
parallelogram (Corollary 3.3). Unless all classes at the smaller modulus are represented,
we select lattice segments whose number equals the difference between the residue counts
at moduli r + 1 and r. Their residue images are distinct lines in at most three directions
(Section 4). By Proposition 5.1, these lines are contained in a cover of the finite torus with
2r + 1 = (r + 1)2 − r2 lines. Lemma 5.2 bounds the number of points covered by the selected
lines, proving the comparison from r to r + 1.

Lemma 4.3 (Intersections and concurrence). Two selected segments whose directions form
a basis of L meet in a lattice point. Moreover, if three selected segments have pairwise-
unimodular directions and their images modulo rL have a common point, then the three
segments meet at one lattice point.

modulo sL. Each of the two segments contains a representative of that class, and injectivity
modulo sL forces the two representatives to coincide.
