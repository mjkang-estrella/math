                                                                                     Abstract
                                                   For the similarity kernel e−|x−y| , we determine the probability measure of max-
                                                   imum diversity on every planar disk and three-dimensional ball, answering a
                                                   question of Leinster and Roff. In each dimension the measure is uniform on the
                                                   boundary up to a threshold. Above it, the support is a closed central ball and
                                                   the outer sphere, separated by a mass-free annulus. In dimensions two and three,
                                                   their conjecture of finitely many supporting spheres therefore holds only up to
                                                   the respective threshold. We obtain the measure explicitly up to one scalar root
                                                   in three dimensions and through Fredholm integral equations and a scalar con-
                                                   tact equation in the plane. Computations in dimensions four and five suggest
                                                   that interior supporting spheres appear one at a time.

                                                   Keywords: Maximum diversity, maximum entropy, magnitude, equilibrium measure,
                                                   obstacle problem, Bessel capacity

                                                   MSC Classification (2020): Primary 31B15; Secondary 45B05 , 45E10 , 51F99

     Rotational symmetry on a ball leaves the distribution of mass over the radii to

determine the maximizing measure for every radius in dimensions two and three using
a potential-theoretic approach.

    For a probability measure µ on E put Uµ = e−|·| ∗ µ and I(µ) = Uµ dµ. We call
                                                                        R

a minimizer of I over these probability measures an equilibrium measure. It is the
maximizing measure, whose uniqueness was proved by Meckes [1, Proposition 8.8]. Its
equilibrium level γ = I(µ) equals 1/Dmax (E), and the potential Uµ is their typicality
function [1, Definition 3.4]. A maximizing measure is balanced, with Uµ = γ on its
support [1, Proposition 6.3], and Uµ ≥ γ on E [1, Lemma 7.7(i)]. We call the latter
the equilibrium inequality, and {x ∈ E : Uµ (x) = γ} the contact set.
    The support proof is the same for both ball problems. For the equilibrium measure
µ of a ball put U = Uµ and J = (1 − ∆)U = (N − 1)(e−|·| /| · |) ∗ µ. At an interior
contact point U has a local minimum, so formally ∆U ≥ 0 and J = U − ∆U ≤ γ; the
latter inequality is proved in Lemma 3.6. In dimensions two and three J is strictly
subharmonic on every open set disjoint from the support, so the maximum principle,
applied to J and then to U − γ, excludes a mass-free annulus between interior contact
radii, and also a mass-free central ball bounded by an interior contact sphere. An
open annulus next to the outer sphere is always mass-free, and together these force
the supercritical support and contact set in Proposition 3.11 (Fig. 1).
    To recover the density we invert convolution with e−|·| . In R3 the identity (1 −

uniform measure on a ball is normalized Lebesgue measure [1, Theorem 9.9]. In three
dimensions the density is constant on the central ball, whose radius is R − O(1) and
which contains all but O(1/R) of the mass (Remark 4.6), so after rescaling to the unit
ball the maximizing measure approaches it in total variation at the rate O(1/R).

fields [10, 11]. The volume part in [11] reaches the sphere; the exponential equilibrium
on a ball has an intervening mass-free annulus between these components.

3.3 Interior contact estimates
Remark 3.5 The conjecture suggested examining what a finite collection of supporting spheres
would leave empty. Once a central atom is excluded such a configuration leaves a mass-free
ball around the origin. We sought a maximum principle argument to test whether this is
compatible with equilibrium. The auxiliary potential J = (1 − ∆)U gives a second-order
argument for the support without a formula for the measure. Pursuing that avenue requires
the interior contact bound J ≤ γ, which Lemma 3.6 proves through second differences, not
needing to assume the measure has a density.

6 Discussion
How many interior support components can occur in dimensions N ≥ 4? The outer
sphere still carries positive mass, with an open annulus separating it from any interior
support (Lemma 3.7). We had hoped to extend the same support argument to higher
dimensions. The interior contact bound J ≤ γ persists (Lemma 3.6), but ∆L in
(3) changes sign for N ≥ 4. The kernel calculation therefore no longer provides the
subharmonicity used above to exclude mass-free regions between interior contact radii.
See Appendix B for numerical experiments performed for N = 4, 5.

                        Dmax (Eh ) ≤ Dmax (E) ≤ e2h Dmax (Eh ).
A measurable nearest point map T : E → Eh satisfies |T x − T y| ≥ |x − y| − 2h, so
pushing a probability measure forward increases its energy by at most a factor e2h .
Minimizing and taking reciprocals proves the right inequality, and inclusion proves
the left. With rigorous bounds for the finite optimization the estimate bounds the
maximum diversity value. Support transitions still require control of the potential
between the sampled points.
    Leinster and Roff also ask about cubes. On a box with the ℓ1 metric the exponential
kernel factorizes, and the positive weight measure is a product of interval weight
measures [9]. For the Euclidean metric, the proof of Lemma 3.6 uses only that U ≥ γ
near the contact point, so J ≤ γ at interior contact on any compact set, and for
N = 2, 3 the function J is strictly subharmonic off of the support whatever the set.
The cube lacks the radial control of J up to the boundary of a mass-free region
(Lemma 3.8) and the radial monotonicity that puts the whole boundary in the support
(Lemma 3.7), and its support is yet to be determined.
    Does the interior support component remain separated from the outer boundary
under a small smooth perturbation of a three-dimensional ball with R > R3 ? A per-
turbed construction would require constant potential on an unknown interior region
and nonnegative surface masses on both boundaries, with the equilibrium inequal-
ity between them. The potential excess must be controlled up to the moving inner
boundary, where it vanishes.
    In the planar construction the contraction factor tends to one as the inner radius
grows. Direct Neumann iteration gets slow on large disks, and the inverse square root
edge law suggests putting the singular factor into the numerical basis. Quantitative
error bounds for the quadrature and scalar root are needed as the inner radius grows.
The planar large-radius asymptotics also remain to be determined.

without continuation, finds the same number of spheres between successive birth radii.
The intervals between successive birth radii shrink by the factors in the table, so the
births appear to accumulate near the listed accumulation radii. Up to those radii the
