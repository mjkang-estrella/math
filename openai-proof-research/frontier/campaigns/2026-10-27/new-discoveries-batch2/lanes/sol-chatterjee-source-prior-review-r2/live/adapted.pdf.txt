                                                   Abstract. Recently studied dependence measures, such as Chatterjee’s rank correlation, that charac-
                                                   terize both independence and perfect functional dependence, provide a powerful framework for detecting
                                                   nonlinear dependencies. However, these measures cannot be weakly continuous, which limits the appli-
                                                   cability of classical plug-in estimators based on empirical distributions. This obstruction is natural, as
                                                   such measures are defined via conditional distributions and not through their joint law alone.
                                                   In this paper, we introduce an optimal transport-based mode of convergence that captures weak conver-
                                                   gence of conditional distributions and restores continuity for a broad class of dependence measures. We
                                                   relate this mode of convergence to the adapted Wasserstein distance, the Knothe-Rosenblatt distance
                                                   and the d1 -metric on copulas. Building on this perspective, we propose a copula estimator based on the
                                                   adapted empirical measure and compare it with the classical rank-based checkerboard estimator. For
                                                   both estimators, we derive O(N −1/3 )-rates of convergence with respect to metrics that capture condi-
                                                   tional weak continuity. As a consequence, we obtain the same rates for plug-in estimators of several
                                                   classes of dependence measures, including rank-based and rearranged dependence measures.




                                                                                1. Introduction and main results
                                             Quantifying dependence between random variables is a fundamental problem in statistics, with im-
                                          plications ranging from theoretical probability to data-driven inference. In recent years, the theory of
                                          dependence measures has seen significant progress, with increasing attention given to measures that cap-
                                          ture complex, non-monotone relationships and remain meaningful beyond the classical linear or monotone
                                          setting; see [21, 19, 17] for an overview.
                                             Literature has focused on dependence measures κ on the space of probability measures P(X × Y) that
                                          satisfy the following axioms, assuming X and Y are Polish spaces:
                                               (I) κ(π) ∈ [0, 1] for all π ∈ P(X × Y),
                                              (II) Zero-independence: κ(π) = 0 if and only if π = π1 ⊗ π2 ,
                                             (III) Max-functionality: κ(π) = 1 if and only if π = (x, f (x))# π1 for some measurable function
                                                   f : X → Y.
                                          In the language of random variables (X, Y ) : (Ω, F, P) → (X × Y) with distribution π, the value κ(π) = 0
                                          characterizes independence of X and Y . Conversely, κ(π) = 1 corresponds to perfect dependence of Y
                                          on X, i.e. there exists a measurable function f such that Y = f (X) almost surely. Note that f is not
                                          assumed to be monotone. In the above, we implicitly assume that π2 = Y# P is non-degenerate, thereby
                                          ruling out the pathological case where Y is both independent of and perfectly dependent on X.
                                             As pointed out by Bücher and Dette [18, Corollary 1.1], a dependence measure κ that fulfills Axioms
                                          (II) and (III) cannot satisfy

The lack of weak continuity has several drawbacks. First, the empirical distribution function cannot
serve as a basis for a consistent plug-in estimator. Second, dependence measures satisfying (II) and (III)
lack robustness under slight model misspecifications with respect to the weak topology. Furthermore,
independence tests based on these measures may suffer from trivial power against sequences of alterna-
tives that converge weakly to the independent coupling. Thus, to nevertheless establish robustness and
convergence guarantees, a different notion of continuity is needed which accounts for weak convergence of
conditional distributions. In this paper, we introduce such a notion of continuity for dependence measures
and exploit this notion for stability results. Moreover, for the checkerboard estimator and an alternative
copula estimator that we construct via the adapted empirical measure in [12], we determine rates of
convergence that translate directly to dependence measures.

1.2. Rates of convergence for copula estimators. In the remainder of this section, we focus on the
univariate case X = Y = [0, 1]. There are different ways to extend the below results to the multivariate
setting; see e.g. [14] and the discussion in Remark 4.8. The aim of this section is to introduce the so-
called adapted copula estimator and determine its rates of convergence by leveraging recent results on the
adapted empirical measure established in [12]. Similar techniques allow us to resolve an open problem:
deriving rates of convergence for the well-known checkerboard estimator of copulas under metrics that

the checkerboard copula approaches.

1.4. Literature review. Optimal transport: We refer to [48, 62, 52, 4] for an overview of optimal
transport theory. Adapted optimal transport has been introduced in a series of papers [3, 35, 50, 34, 38,
1, 16]. We will particularly make use of results from [14] in the discussion below. Interestingly, [13] states
that all adapted topologies introduced in these papers coincide.
Dependence modeling: We refer to [27, 46] for a comprehensive overview of copula theory. Various
metrics and modes of convergence for copulas have been investigated in [9, 23, 39, 43, 44, 60]; see [27,
Section 4] for an overview. Weak convergence of the empirical copula process is studied in [22, 28, 30, 47,
49], strong consistency of the checkerboard copula estimator with respect to the d1 -metric is documented
in [31, 59].
Dependence measures: As mentioned above, in recent years there have been many proposals for
dependence measures satisfying Axioms (I)–(III); see e.g. [10, 11, 17, 20, 24, 32, 36, 58, 59, 63] and the
references therein.
The connections between dependence modeling and adapted optimal transport are relatively unexplored
except for [63] to the best of our knowledge. This paper aspires to fill this gap. We hope to provide a
bridge between the fields of OT, statistics and copula theory, clarifying their various interconnections.
1.5. Structure of the paper. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the
notation and the basic concepts of optimal transport and dependence modeling that we use in this paper.
Section 3 provides a comparison between different notions of convergence arising in optimal transport
and copula theory. In particular, we show that convergence in adapted Wasserstein distance and d1 -
convergence of copulas are equivalent; see Corollary 3.3. For stochastically increasing distributions, these
modes of convergence are also equivalent to metrics of weak convergence; see Corollary 3.7. In Section
4, we focus on copula estimators derived from the adapted empirical measure and the checkerboard
approach. While the checkerboard estimator—frequently used in the dependence modeling community—
relies on binning rank-transformed observations, the adapted empirical copula operates directly on the
original observations. Yet, as established in Corollary 4.4 and Theorem 4.7, both estimators achieve the
same rates of convergence—with respect to metrics of conditional weak convergence. In Section 5, we
provide strongly consistent plug-in estimators for rank-based dependence measures and determine their
speed of convergence. Estimating rearranged dependence measures is covered in Section 6. Some proofs
and auxiliary results are deferred to the appendix.

   As discussed in the Introduction, many dependence measures satisfying Axioms (I)–(III) are defined via

to derive a new copula estimator. Subsequently we compare it to the rank-based checkerboard estimator.
It will turn out that both estimators have the same rates of convergence under regularity assumptions.

                                    6. Rearranged dependence measures
    Rearranged dependence measures [59] are defined via increasing rearranged copulas applied to a func-
tional η that satisfies axioms (I)–(III) on the class of SI copulas. More precisely, we assume that η : C ↑ → R
fulfills
   (C1) η(C) ∈ [0, 1] for all C ∈ C ↑ ,
   (C2) η(C) = 0 if and only if C = C ⊥ ,
   (C3) η(C) = 1 if and only if C = C + .
Estimating rearranged dependence measures also requires continuity of η with respect to pointwise con-
vergence of copulas, i.e.
   (C4) For Cn , C ∈ C ↑ , Cn (u, v) → C(u, v) for all (u, v) ∈ [0, 1]2 implies η(Cn ) → η(C).
Popular examples of functionals η that satisfy the properties (C1)–(C4) are Spearman’s rho, Kendall’s
tau or Schweizer-Wolff     measures; see [59]. Further examples are standardized linear functionals of the
form π 7→ g dπ where g : [0, 1]2 → R is supermodular.3
