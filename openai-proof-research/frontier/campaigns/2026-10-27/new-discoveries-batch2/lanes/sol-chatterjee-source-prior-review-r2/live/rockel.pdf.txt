                                                                                        Abstract
                                                   This paper studies closed-form expressions for multiple association measures of copulas
                                                commonly used for approximation purposes, including Bernstein, shuffle–of–min, checkerboard
                                                and check–min copulas. In particular, closed-form expressions are provided for the recently
                                                popularized Chatterjee’s ξ, which quantifies the dependence between two random variables.
                                                Given an absolutely continuous bivariate copula C with TP2 density and approximating n×n-
                                                checkerboard copula Cn , we show that ξ(Cn ) ≤ ξ(C) with ξ(Cn ) → ξ(C) as n → ∞.
                                           Keywords Bernstein, checkerboard, Chatterjee’s xi, Kendall’s tau, Spearman’s rho, shuffle–of–
                                           min, tail dependence


                                           1    Introduction
                                           Measures of association—most prominently Spearman’s rho and Kendall’s tau—are fundamental
                                           tools for studying statistical dependence, and [7] and [6] popularized Chatterjee’s xi to capture
                                           arbitrary functional dependence. Closed–form expressions for these statistics, however, exist only
                                           for a handful of copula families (see [5, Table 6]); in general one must resort to numerical or sam-
                                           pling–based procedures. Near the boundaries of the unit square, such procedures often become
                                           unstable because they require evaluating (conditional) distribution functions where numerical pre-
                                           cision can quickly become poor. We therefore study measures of association for several popular
                                           approximation families, which we introduce in Section 2.
                                               Bernstein and checkerboard constructions, in particular, have a rich history and broad practical
                                           use, see, e.g., [8, 17, 18, 19, 27, 29]. Closed–form formulas for Spearman’s rho and Kendall’s tau
                                           are already known for these families, the most elegant arguably appearing in [12]. In Section 3, we
                                           extend this catalogue by deriving explicit formulas for Chatterjee’s xi not only for Bernstein and
                                           checkerboard copulas, but also for the check–min and check–w variants, whose grid cells exhibit
                                           perfect dependence. We additionally collect complete closed–form expressions for Spearman’s rho,
                                           Kendall’s tau, and the tail–dependence coefficients, thereby unifying and extending earlier results.
                                               In Section 4, we focus on bounding Chatterjee’s xi via checkerboard approximations. Combining

2     Preliminaries
In this section, we introduce the basic concepts and notation used that are required to formulate
the main results of this paper. First, we introduce the fundamental concept of a copula, before
focusing on the specific types of copulas that are of interest in this paper. Finally, we introduce
the studied measures of association.

2.1    Copulas
A bivariate copula is a function C : [0, 1]2 → [0, 1] that is grounded (i.e., C(u, 0) = C(0, v) = 0 for
all u, v ∈ [0, 1]), 2-increasing (meaning that for every 0 ≤ u1 ≤ v1 ≤ 1 and 0 ≤ u2 ≤ v2 ≤ 1 it
holds that C(v1 , v2 ) − C(u1 , v2 ) − C(v1 , u2 ) + C(u1 , u2 ) ≥ 0), and has uniform marginals (so that
C(u, 1) = u and C(1, v) = v for all u, v ∈ [0, 1]). Sklar’s theorem (see, e.g., [25, Theorem 2.3.3])
states that for any bivariate distribution function F with univariate marginals F1 and F2 , there
exists a copula C such that

                       F (x1 , x2 ) = C (F1 (x1 ), F2 (x2 ))   for all (x1 , x2 ) ∈ R2 ,             (2)

and C is uniquely determined on Ran(F1 ) × Ran(F2 ). Conversely, if C is any copula and F1 , F2
are univariate distribution functions, then the function defined by (2) is a bivariate distribution
function.
   Denote by C2 the collection of all bivariate copulas. Important examples include the indepen-
dence copula Π(u, v) = uv, the upper Fréchet bound M (u, v) = min{u, v} and the lower Fréchet
bound W (u, v) = max{u+v −1, 0}. Classically, if (X, Y ) ∼ C, the upper and lower Fréchet bounds
represent the extreme cases of dependence with perfect co- and countermonotonicity, respectively,
whilst the independence copula represents the case of no dependence at all between X and Y . Fur-
thermore, for any C ∈ C2 , it holds that W ≤ C ≤ M pointwise on [0, 1]2 , see standard references
such as [25] or [11].

2.5    Measures of association
Two classical measures of association are Spearman’s rho and Kendall’s tau, which provide alter-
natives to the Pearson correlation coefficient that do not depend on the marginal distributions of
the random variables. Both of them can be expressed as an integral over the unit square [0, 1]2 .
That is, for a bivariate copula C, one can write Spearman’s rho as

(and classical) interpretation of Kendall’s τ is in terms of concordant and discordant pairs of
observations: if (U1 , V1 ) and (U2 , V2 ) are two independent draws from the copula C, then

                 τ (C) = P [(U1 − U2 )(V1 − V2 ) > 0] − P [(U1 − U2 )(V1 − V2 ) < 0] ,            (18)

i.e. the probability of concordance minus the probability of discordance, see, e.g., [25, Section
5.1.1]. This probabilistic view is particularly handy for copulas supported on discrete sets such as
shuffle–of–min constructions (see Section 3.2).
Next to these two measures, which take values in [−1, 1], it is also interesting to measure the
strength of dependence between two random variables X and Y . Chatterjee’s xi is one way to

and Y , and 1 with perfect dependence, i.e. Y = f (X) for some measurable function f , see [1,
Theorem 2.2]. Like Spearman’s rho and Kendall’s tau, Chatterjee’s xi can be expressed as an
