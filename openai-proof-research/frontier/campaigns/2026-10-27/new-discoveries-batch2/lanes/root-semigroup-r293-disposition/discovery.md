## Routine finite checking criterion, explicitly uncredited

Let a<b<d be the first positives and gcd(a,b,d)=1. For a>=12 the intended property implies d<2a. The initial justification was wrong: a and 2a do not always repeat a residue. Here is the repaired argument, with no minimal-generator assumption.

If d>=2a, either (I) b<2a,d=2a or (II) b=2a,2a<d<=3a. In (I), a=0 mod3 fails immediately; otherwise the first triple forces b=0 mod3. If 2b<3a the next triple is (a+b,2b,3a); if 2b>3a it is (a+b,3a,2b). Both repeat residue0. If 2b=3a, gcd(a,b,2a)=a/2, forcing a=2, excluded. In (II), d=3a has gcd=a. Otherwise the first triple forces a nonzero mod3,d=0 mod3. The next triple is (3a,a+d,4a), repeating the residue of a. These are the actual next triples: the other generator sums are larger than their last entries. The alternatives exhaust d>=2a since 2a and 3a are already semigroup elements. Thus d<2a for the target domain. boundary_window.py independently tested 4908 gcd/residue-eligible boundary candidates over 12<=a<=180 and found no prefix survivor. The erroneous initial shortcut is preserved in failures.json.

For any eligible triple, independently of this window problem, let A_r be the exact Apéry minimum in residue r modulo a. Then n belongs to G iff n>=A_(n mod a): subtracting the minimum leaves a nonnegative multiple of a, and minimality proves the reverse implication. The conductor is c=max A−a+1. Shortest paths on residues, with generator-sized positive edges, compute A; gcd=1 ensures reachability. The independent checker recomputes every A_r by Boolean membership DP through max A+a+5, not merely through the conductor.

Every enumeration triple wholly in [c,infinity) is three consecutive integers. A triple containing a positive element below c can contain at most two tail elements, so its last entry is <=c+1. Enumeration through c+5 therefore contains all potentially bad triples and enough tail to settle every alignment; checking all complete aligned triples in that prefix is sufficient. This criterion does not certify that a,b,d are the first three: that is separately checked. It is a routine finite reduction, with no discovery credit.

## Mechanism 1: restricted exact generator sweep

checks.py enumerates 12<=a<=180 and a<b<d<2a, filters distinct residues and gcd1, rejects failures in a DP prefix through 12a, then computes exact Apéry sets and checks the conductor-complete prefix for every prefix survivor. 186645 gcd/residue-eligible triples were tested; 1283 passed the preliminary prefix and 505 passed the complete test. All 505 matched a listed family. Certificates and failed-prefix counts are preserved in checks-result.json. independent_checks.py independently recomputes the 505 Apéry sets and membership and confirms the aligned-triple checks. No outside-family witness was found. With the repaired boundary argument this is exhaustive for 12<=a<=180, not for unbounded multiplicity.

## Mechanism 2: exact rational order chambers within d<2a

Set x=b/a,y=d/a in 1<x<y<2. For an integer horizon H, every semigroup value <=Ha is represented by ua+vb+wd with u+v+w<=H; except Ha itself the degree is <H. chambers.py lists those forms, subdivides the rational triangle by all pair-equality lines, and tests aligned residue triples for each of the six generator residue permutations. In a strict chamber there are no duplicate sums; on edges/vertices equal forms are merged and their generator-residue evaluations must agree.

At H=4, 40 open chambers leave 19 prefix survivors; at H=6, 478 leave 2; at H=8, 2823 leave 0. The H=8 boundary test retains 46 segments and 32 vertices. Its ten line supports in (a,b,d) are:

3b−2d=0; a−2b+d=0; 2a+2b−3d=0; 3a−3b+d=0; 3a−b−d=0; 3a−2d=0; 3a+b−3d=0; 4a−3b=0; 4a−3d=0; 4a+b−3d=0.

This is a computer-supported necessary-prefix reduction restricted to d<2a, not a classification theorem. The complete rational polygons and boundary endpoints are saved. A separate checker confirms polygon area 1/2, no straddled hyperplane, and rejection of all 16938 open-chamber/residue cases. It does not independently compute a planar union; coverage relies on the clipping construction. The endpoint test uses gcd1 to choose the unique primitive integer triple at a rational ratio vertex: eight vertices are eligible at multiplicity >=12; seven pass and are known families, one fails. vertices-result.json records the exact data.

Decisive next calculations: retain and independently review the repaired generator-window argument above, then classify integer points on the 46 retained segments via two-generator lattice parameters and exact Apéry chambers. An interval's endpoint checks do not prove its interior. Even within a line, primitive gcd1 points can form an infinite two-parameter family; a finite parameter scan cannot close it.
