It is obvious that using a fixed stepsize h for the method is not useful, since T(p)
will generally have a large range of values, and with a fixed stepsize either the
method is too crude for small T(p) or takes too much time for large T(p). Also
our kind of problems often run into parameter ranges where interior or boundary
layers occur, so the stepsize should vary along orbits so as to take proper account
of these phenomena. We use the iteration process in our implicit method as a
means to control h: The iteration is stopped when the relative error is less then
lo-control , where control is a parameter to be entered into the program by the
user. Subsequently h is altered in a way as to ensure that the mean number
of iterations is always 2. To make the process smooth we define "number of
iterations" by the actual number of steps minus I plus the quotient of the error
and lO-control. This method seems to work well even in tough situations.
