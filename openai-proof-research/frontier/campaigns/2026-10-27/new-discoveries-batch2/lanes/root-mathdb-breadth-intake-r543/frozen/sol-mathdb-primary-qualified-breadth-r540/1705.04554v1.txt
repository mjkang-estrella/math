 Abstract   In this paper, we show that an order  m m  dimension 2 tensor is primitive if and only if its majorization matrix is primitive, and then we obtain the characterization of order  m m  dimension 2 strongly primitive tensors and the bound of the strongly primitive degree. Furthermore, we study the properties of strongly primitive tensors with  n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , and propose some problems for further research. 
 
 
 Keywords:  Tensors; Strongly primitive; Primitive; Characterization; Strongly primitive degree 
 
 
 AMS Classification:   15A69 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 A nonnegative square matrix  A = ( a i ​ j ) A=(a_{ij})  of order  n n  is nonnegative primitive (or simply, primitive) if  A k > 0 A^{k}>0  for
some positive integer  k k . The least such  k k  is called the primitive exponent (or simply, exponent) of  A A  and is
denoted by  exp ⁡ ( A ) \exp(A) . 
 
 
 Since the work of Qi  [ 7 ]  and Lim  [ 5 ] , the study of tensors which regarded as the generalization of matrices,
the spectra of tensors (and hypergraphs) and their various applications has attracted much attention and interest. 
 
 
 As is in  [ 7 ] , an order  m m  dimension  n n  tensor  𝔸 = ( a i 1 ​ i 2 ​ … ​ i m ) 1 ≤ i j ≤ n ⁡ ( j = 1 , … , m ) \mathbb{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}})_{1\leq i_{j}\leq n\hskip 5.69046pt(j=1,\ldots,m)} 
over the complex field  ℂ \mathbb{C}  is a multidimensional array with all entries 
 

 
 
 a i 1 ​ i 2 ​ … ​ i m ∈ ℂ ( i 1 , … , i m ∈ [ n ] = { 1 , … , n } ) . a_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}}\in\mathbb{C}\,(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}). 
 
 
 In  [ 1 ]  and  [ 2 ] , Chang et al investigated the properties of the spectra of nonnegative tensors,
defined the irreducibility of tensors and the primitivity of nonnegative tensors (as Definition  1.1 ),
and extended many important properties of primitive matrices to primitive tensors.

denoted by  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \gamma(\mathbb{A}) . 
 
 
 
 Recently, Shao  [ 8 ]  defined the general product of two n-dimensional tensors as follows.

 The tensor product is a generalization of the usual matrix product, and satisfies a very useful property:
the associative law ( [ 8 ] , Theorem 1.1).
With the general product, when  k = 1 k=1  and  𝔹 = x = ( x 1 , … , x n ) T ∈ ℂ n \mathbb{B}=x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}  is a vector of dimension  n n ,

 As an application of the general tensor product defined by Shao  [ 8 ] ,
Shao presented a simple characterization of the primitive tensors.
Now we give the definition of “essentially positive” which introduced by Pearson. 
 
 
 Definition 1.3 . 
 
 (See  [ 6 ] , Definition 3.1)  A nonnegative tensor  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is called essentially positive,
if for any nonnegative nonzero vector  x ∈ ℝ n , 𝔸 ​ x > 0 x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{A}x>0  holds. 
 
 
 
 Proposition 1.4 . 
 
 (See  [ 8 ] , Proposition 4.1)  
Let  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  be an order  m m  and dimension  n n  nonnegative tensor. Then the following three conditions are equivalent: 
 
 
 (1)   For any  i , j ∈ [ n ] , a i ​ j ​ … ​ j > 0 i,j\in[n],a_{ij\ldots j}>0  holds. 
 
 
 (2)   For any  j ∈ [ n ] , 𝔸 ​ e j > 0 j\in[n],\mathbb{A}e_{j}>0  holds  ( where  e j e_{j}  is the  j j -th column of the identity matrix  I n I_{n} ) . 
 
 
 (3)   For any nonnegative nonzero vector  x ∈ ℝ n , 𝔸 ​ x > 0 x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{A}x>0  holds. 
 
 
 
 By Proposition  1.4 , the following Definition  1.5  is equivalent to Definition  1.3 .
What’s more, in Proposition  1.6 , Shao showed a characterization of primitive tensors and defined the primitive degree by using
the properties of tensor product and the zero patterns which defined by Shao in  [ 8 ] . 
 
 
 Definition 1.5 . 
 
 (See  [ 8 ] , Definition 4.1)  
A nonnegative tensor  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is called essentially positive, if it satisfies one of the three conditions in Proposition  1.4 . 
 
 
 
 Proposition 1.6 . 
 
 (See  [ 8 ] , Theorem 4.1) 
A nonnegative tensor  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is primitive if and only if there exists some positive integer  r r  such that  𝔸 r \mathbb{A}^{r}  is essentially positive. Furthermore, the smallest such  r r  is the primitive degree of  𝔸 \mathbb{A} ,  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \gamma(\mathbb{A}) . 
 
 
 
 The concept of the majorization matrix of a tensor introduced by Pearson is very useful. 
 
 
 Definition 1.7 . 
 
 (See  [ 6 ] , Definition 2.1) 
The majorization matrix  M ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) M(\mathbb{A})  of the tensor  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is defined as  ( M ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) ) i ​ j = a i ​ j ​ … ​ j , i , j ∈ [ n ] (M(\mathbb{A}))_{ij}=a_{ij\ldots j},i,j\in[n] . 
 
 
 
 By Definition  1.5 , Proposition  1.6  and Definition  1.7 ,
the following characterization of the primitive tensors was easily obtained. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.8 . 
 
 (See  [ 10 ] , Remark 2.6) 
Let  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  be a nonnegative tensor with order  m m  and dimension  n n .
Then  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is primitive if and only if there exists some positive integer  r r  such that  M ⁡ ( 𝔸 r ) > 0 . M(\mathbb{A}^{r})>0. 
Furthermore, the smallest such  r r  is the primitive degree of  𝔸 \mathbb{A} ,  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \gamma(\mathbb{A}) . 
 
 
 
 On the primitive degree  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \gamma(\mathbb{A}) , Shao proposed the following conjecture for further research. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.9 . 
 
 (See  [ 8 ] , Conjecture 1) 
When  m m  is fixed, then there exists some polynomial  f ⁡ ( n ) f(n)  on  n n  such that  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) ≤ f ⁡ ( n ) \gamma(\mathbb{A})\leq f(n)  for
all nonnegative primitive tensors of order  m m  and dimension  n n . 
 
 
 
 In the case of  m = 2 m=2  ( 𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is a matrix), the well-known Wielandt’s upper bound tells us that we can take
 f ⁡ ( n ) = ( n − 1 ) 2 + 1 . f(n)=(n-1)^{2}+1. 
Recently, the authors  [ 10 ]  confirmed Conjecture  1.9  by proving Theorem  1.10 . 
 
 
 Theorem 1.10 . 
 
 (See  [ 10 ] , Theorem 1.2) 
Let  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  be a nonnegative primitive tensor with order  m m  and dimension  n n .
Then its primitive degree  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) ≤ ( n − 1 ) 2 + 1 \gamma(\mathbb{A})\leq(n-1)^{2}+1 , and the upper bound is tight. 
 
 
 
 They also showed that there are no gaps in the tensor case in  [ 11 ] ,
which implies that the result of the case  m ≥ 3 m\geq 3  is totally different from the case  m = 2 m=2 .
In  [ 8 ] , Shao also proposed the concept of strongly primitive for further research. 
 
 
 Definition 1.11 . 
 
 (See  [ 8 ] , Definition 4.3) 
Let  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  be a nonnegative tensor with order  m m  and dimension  n n . If there exists some positive
integer  k k  such that  𝔸 k > 0 \mathbb{A}^{k}>0  is a positive tensor, then  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is called strongly primitive,
and the smallest such  k k  is called the strongly primitive degree of  𝔸 \mathbb{A} . 
 
 
 
 Let  𝔸 = ( a i 1 ​ i 2 ​ … ​ i m ) \mathbb{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{m}})  be a nonnegative tensor with order  m m  and dimension  n n .
It is clear that if  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is strongly primitive, then  𝔸 \mathbb{A}  is primitive.
For convenience, let  η ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \eta(\mathbb{A})  be the strongly primitive degree of  𝔸 \mathbb{A} .
Clearly,  γ ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) ≤ η ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \gamma(\mathbb{A})\leq\eta(\mathbb{A}) .
In fact, it is obvious that in the matrix case  ( m = 2 ) (m=2) ,
a nonnegative matrix  A A  is primitive if and only if  A A  is strongly primitive, and  γ ⁡ ( A ) = η ⁡ ( A ) = exp ⁡ ( A ) \gamma(A)=\eta(A)=\exp(A) .
But in the case  m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 , Shao gave an example to show that these two concepts are not equivalent in the case  m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 .
In  [ 11 ] , the authors proposed the following question. 
 
 
 Question 1.12 . 
 
 ( [ 11 ] , Question 4.18) 
Can we define and study the strongly primitive degree, the strongly primitive degree set,
the  j j -strongly primitive of strongly primitive tensors and so on? 
 
 
 
 Based on Question  1.12 , we study primitive tensors and strongly primitive tensors in this paper,
show that an order  m m  dimension 2 tensor is primitive if and only if its majorization matrix is primitive,
and obtain the characterization of order  m m  dimension 2 strongly primitive tensors and the bound of the strongly primitive degree. Furthermore, we study the properties of strongly primitive tensors with  n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , and propose some problems for further research.

 In  [ 4 ,  10 ] , the authors gave some algebraic characterizations of a nonnegative primitive tensor,
and in  [ 3 ] , the authors showed that a nonnegative tensor is primitive if and only if the greatest common divisor of all the
cycles in the associated directed hypergraph is equal to 1. It is natural for us to consider the following. 
 
 
 Question 4.5 . 
 
 Study the algebraic or graphic characterization of a nonnegative strongly primitive tensor.
