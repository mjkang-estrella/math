 Abstract 
    
 A  k k -coloring  of a graph  G G  is a partition of the vertices of  G G  into  k k  independent sets, which are called  colors .
A  k k -coloring is  neighbor-locating  if any two vertices belonging to the same color can be distinguished from each other by the colors of their respective neighbors.
The  neighbor-locating chromatic number   χ N ​ L ( G ) \chi_{{}_{NL}}(G)  is the minimum cardinality of a neighbor-locating coloring of  G G . 
    
 In this paper, we determine the neighbor-locating chromatic number of paths, cycles, fans and wheels.
Moreover, a procedure to construct a neighbor-locating coloring of minimum cardinality for these families of graphs is given.
We also obtain tight upper bounds on the order of trees and unicyclic graphs in terms of the neighbor-locating chromatic number.
Further partial results for trees are also established.

 1  Introduction 

 
 This work is devoted to studying a special type of vertex partitions, the so-called  neighbor-locating colorings , whithin
the family of graphs known as  pseudotrees , that is to say, the set of all connected graphs containing at most one cycle. 
 
 
 There are mainly two types of location, metric location and neighbor location.
Metric-locating sets (also known as resolving sets) were introduced simultaneously in  [ 12 ,  15 ] , meanwhile
neighbor-locating sets were introduced in  [ 16 ] .
In  [ 8 ] , the notion of metric location was brought to the ambit of vertex partitions, and in  [ 6 ] , there were first studied the so-called  locating colorings , i.e., locating partitions (also known as resolving partitions) formed by independents sets.
Both resolving partitions and locating colorings have been extensively studied since then.
See e.g.  [ 5 ,  9 ,  10 ,  11 ,  13 ,  14 ]  and  [ 2 ,  3 ,  4 ,  7 ,  17 ] , respectively. 
 
 
 In  [ 1 ] , we started the study of neighbor-locating partitions formed by independent sets, which we named  neighbor-locating colorings .
More specifically, we considered vertex colorings such that any two vertices with the same color can be distinguished from each other by the colors of their respective neighbors.
This paper continues that line of research focusing on the neighbor-locating colorings of pseudotrees, i.e., of paths, cycles, trees and unicyclic graphs. 
 
 
 1.1  Basic terminology 

 
 All the graphs considered in this paper are connected, undirected, simple and finite.
The vertex set and the edge set of a graph  G G  are denoted by  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  and  E ⁡ ( G ) E(G) , respectively.
We let  n ⁡ ( G ) n(G)  be the order of  G G , i.e.  n ⁡ ( G ) = | V ⁡ ( G ) | n(G)=|V(G)| .
The  neighborhood  of a vertex  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G)  is the set  N ⁡ ( v ) = { w ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) : v ​ w ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) } \displaystyle N(v)=\{w\in V(G):vw\in E(G)\} .
The  degree  of  v v , defined as the cardinality of  N ⁡ ( v ) N(v) , is denoted by  deg ⁡ ( v ) \deg(v) .
When  deg ⁡ ( v ) = 1 \deg(v)=1 ,  v v  is called a  leaf .
The  maximum degree   Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G)  of  G G  is defined to be  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = max ⁡ { d ​ e ​ g ​ ( v ) : v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) } \Delta(G)=\max\{deg(v):v\in V(G)\} .
The  distance  between two vertices  v , w ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v,w\in V(G)  is denoted by  d ⁡ ( v , w ) d(v,w) .
The  diameter  of  G G  is  diam ( G ) = max { d ( v , w ) : v , w ∈ V ( G ) } {\rm diam}(G)=\max\{d(v,w):v,w\in V(G)\} . 
 
 
 Let  Π = { S 1 , … , S k } \Pi=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\}  be a  k k -partition of  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) , i.e., a partition of the set of vertices of  G G  into  k k  subsets.
If all the elements of  Π \Pi  are independent sets, then they are called  colors  and  Π \Pi  is said to be a  coloring  of  G G  (also a  k k - coloring  of  G G ).
We say that a vertex  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G)  has color  i i , or that is colored with  i i , whenever  v ∈ S i v\in S_{i} . 
 
 
 Given a  k k -coloring  Π = { S 1 , … , S k } \Pi=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\}  of a graph  G G  and a vertex  v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , the  color-degree  of  v v  is defined to be the number of different colors of  Π \Pi  containing some vertex of  N ⁡ ( v ) N(v) , i.e.,
 | { j : N ⁡ ( v ) ∩ S j ≠ ∅ } | |\{j:N(v)\cap S_{j}\not=\emptyset\}| . 
 
 
 
 1.2  Neighbor-locating colorings 

 
 A coloring  Π = { S 1 , … , S k } \Pi=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{k}\}  of a graph  G G  is called a   neighbor-locating coloring , an  NL-coloring  for short, if for every pair of different vertices  u , v u,v  belonging to the same color  S i S_{i} , the set of colors of the neighborhood of  u u  is different from the set of colors of the neighborhood of  v v , that is, 
 

 
 
 { j : N ⁡ ( u ) ∩ S j ≠ ∅ } ≠ { j : N ⁡ ( v ) ∩ S j ≠ ∅ } . \{j:N(u)\cap S_{j}\not=\emptyset\}\not=\{j:N(v)\cap S_{j}\not=\emptyset\}. 
 
 
 
 
 The  neighbor-locating chromatic number   χ N ​ L ( G ) \chi_{{}_{NL}}(G) , the  NLC-number  for short, is the minimum cardinality of an NL-coloring of  G G . 
 
 
 Both neighbor-locating colorings and the neighbor-locating chromatic number of a graph were introduced in  [ 1 ] .
As a straightforward consequence of these definitions the following remark is derived. 
 
 
 Remark 1 . 
 
 Let  Π = { S 1 , … , S k } \Pi=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\}  be a  k k -NL-coloring of a graph  G G  order  n n  and maximum degree  Δ \Delta .
For every  1 ≤ i ≤ k 1\leq i\leq k , there are at most  ( k − 1 j ) \binom{k-1}{j}  vertices in  S i S_{i}  of color-degree  j j , where  1 ≤ j ≤ k − 1 1\leq j\leq k-1  and, consequently,
 | S i | ≤ ∑ j = 1 Δ ( k − 1 j ) \displaystyle|S_{i}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\Delta}\binom{k-1}{j} .

 Let  G G  be a non-trivial connected graph of order  n ⁡ ( G ) = n n(G)=n  and maximum degree  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = Δ \Delta(G)=\Delta 
such that  χ N ​ L ( G ) = k \chi_{{}_{NL}}(G)=k . Then, 
 
 (1)  
 
 n ≤ k ⁡ ( 2 k − 1 − 1 ) \displaystyle{n\leq k\,(2^{k-1}-1)} . Moreover, this bound is tight. 
 
 (2)  
 
 If  Δ ≤ k − 1 \Delta\leq k-1 , then  n ≤ k ​ ∑ j = 1 Δ ( k − 1 j ) . \displaystyle{n\leq k\,\sum_{j=1}^{\Delta}\binom{k-1}{j}}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 To simplify the writing, given a  k k -NL-coloring of a graph  G G  with maximum degree  Δ \Delta , we denote by  a j ​ ( k ) a_{j}(k)  the maximum number of vertices of color-degree  j j  and by  ℓ ⁡ ( k ) \ell(k) , the maximum number of vertices of color-degree  1 1  or  2 2 , where  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3  and  1 ≤ j ≤ Δ 1\leq j\leq\Delta .
By Remark  1 , we have:

 The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section  2 , the neighbor-locating chromatic number of paths and of cycles is determined.
Section  4  deals with unicyclic graphs, providing a tight upper bound on the order of a unicyclic graph with a given fixed neighbor-locating chromatic number.
In Section  5 , the neighbor-locating colorings of trees is studied. Among other results,
a tight upper bound on the order of a tree with a given fixed NLC-number.
Finally, in Section  6 , we summarize our results and pose some open problems. 
 
 
 
 
 2  Paths and Cycles 

 
 This section is devoted to determine the NLC-number of all paths and cycles, i.e., all graphs with maximum degree  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 2 \Delta(G)=2 . 
 
 
 Proposition 3 . 
 
 If  G G  is a graph of order  n n  and maximum degree  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 2 \Delta(G)=2  such that  χ N ​ L ( G ) = k ≥ 3 \chi_{{}_{NL}}(G)=k\geq 3 , then
 n ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ℓ ⁡ ( k ) n(G)\leq\ell(k) . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Since all vertices have color-degree at most 2, we have  n ≤ a 1 ​ ( k ) + a 2 ​ ( k ) = ℓ ⁡ ( k ) n\leq a_{1}(k)+a_{2}(k)=\ell(k) .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Corollary 4 . 
 
 If  G G  is either a path or a cycle such that  n ⁡ ( G ) > ℓ ⁡ ( k − 1 ) n(G)>\ell(k-1) , then  χ N ​ L ( G ) ≥ k \chi_{{}_{NL}}(G)\geq k . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 This inequality directly follows from Proposition  3  taking into account that  ℓ \ell  is an increasing function. ∎ 
 
 
 
 Remark 5 . 
 
 Let  { S 1 , … , S k } \{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\}  be a  k k -NL-coloring of a graph  G G  of order  n = ℓ ⁡ ( k ) n=\ell(k)  and maximum degree  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 2 \Delta(G)=2 . If  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , then for every  i ∈ { 1 , … , k } i\in\{1,\ldots,k\} : 
 
 (1)  
 
 | S i | = ( k 2 ) \displaystyle|S_{i}|=\binom{k}{2} ; 
 
 (2)  
 
 there are exactly  ( k − 1 2 ) \binom{k-1}{2}  vertices in  S i S_{i}  of color-degree 2; 
 
 (3)  
 
 there are exactly  k − 1 k-1  vertices in  S i S_{i}  of color-degree 1. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Since  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 2 \Delta(G)=2 , by Remark  1  we have  | S i | ≤ ( k − 1 1 ) + ( k − 1 2 ) = ( k 2 ) |S_{i}|\leq\binom{k-1}{1}+\binom{k-1}{2}=\binom{k}{2} , for every  i ∈ { 1 , … , k } i\in\{1,\dots,k\} .
If  | S i | < ( k 2 ) |S_{i}|<\binom{k}{2}  for some  i ∈ { 1 , … , k } i\in\{1,\dots,k\} , then
 n ⁡ ( G ) = ∑ j = 1 k | S j | < k ​ ( k 2 ) = ℓ ⁡ ( k ) n(G)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|S_{j}|<k\binom{k}{2}=\ell(k) , a contradiction. Hence, (1) holds.
Moreover, if, for some  i ∈ { 1 , … , k } i\in\{1,\dots,k\} , the number of vertices of color-degree 2 is less than  ( k − 1 2 ) \binom{k-1}{2}  or
the number of vertices of color-degree 1 is less than  k − 1 k-1 , then  | S i | < ( k − 1 1 ) + ( k − 1 2 ) = ( k 2 ) |S_{i}|<\binom{k-1}{1}+\binom{k-1}{2}=\binom{k}{2} ,
which contradicts (1).
∎

 In what follows, several technical results are given.
They are needed to prove Theorem  17 , where the NLC-number of all paths and cycles of order greater than 9 is determined. 
 
 
 Lemma 8 . 
 
 In any NL-coloring of the cycle  C n C_{n} , every vertex of color-degree  1 1  has at least one neighbor of color-degree 2. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let  x x  be a vertex of color-degree 1.
In order to derive a contradiction, suppose that its two neighbors  y y  and  z z  have also color-degree 1.
Then,  y y  and  z z  have the same color, and each one of them has its two neighbors with the same color, indeed the color of  x x .
A contradiction in any NL-coloring.
∎

 Definition 10 .

 6  Conclusions and open problems 

 
 We have determined the NLC-number of paths, cycles (see Theorem  7  and Theorem  17 ),
and also of fans and wheels (see Theorem  19  and Theorem  20 ). 
 
 
 In  [ 1 ] , the order of a graph is bounded from above by a function of its NLC-number.
In the present paper, we have achieved better bounds both for unicyclic graphs (see Theorem  21 ) and for trees (see Theorem  25 ).
Moreover, we have shown that these new bounds are tight for NLC-number  k ≥ 5 k\geq 5  in the case of unicyclic graphs (see Proposition  23 ); and for NLC-number  k ≥ 6 k\geq 6  in the case of trees (see Proposition  26 ).
In this last case, we have proved that the bound is achieved by a caterpillar.
For trees with NLC-number  k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 , the maximum order according to our bound is  68 68 , but the maximum order that we have obtained is  66 66 .
The existence or not of trees with NLC-number  5 5  and order  67 67  or  68 68  remains open. 
 
 
 According to Theorem  2 (2), if  χ N ​ L ( G ) = k \chi_{{}_{NL}}(G)=k  and  Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ k − 1 \Delta(G)\leq k-1 , then  n ⁡ ( G ) ≤ ∑ j = 1 Δ ⁡ ( G ) a j ​ ( k ) . \displaystyle{n(G)\leq\,\sum_{j=1}^{\Delta(G)}a_{j}(k)}. 
In general, it is not known whether or not this bound is tight.
For  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = k − 1 \Delta(G)=k-1 , this bound match with the one given in Theorem  2 (1) which is known to be tight.
Since we have proved that if  G G  is a cycle, then  n = a 1 ​ ( k ) + a 2 ​ ( k ) n=a_{1}(k)+a_{2}(k) , this fact implies that the referred bound is tight for graphs with maximum degree  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 2 \Delta(G)=2 .
What does it happen for graphs with  Δ ⁡ ( G ) = 3 \Delta(G)=3 ?
We have shown that if  G G  is either a tree or an unicyclic graph, then  n ≤ 2 ​ a 1 ​ ( k ) + a 2 ​ ( k ) n\leq 2a_{1}(k)+a_{2}(k)  and, obviously,  2 ​ a 1 ​ ( k ) + a 2 ​ ( k ) < a 1 ​ ( k ) + a 2 ​ ( k ) + a 3 ​ ( k ) 2a_{1}(k)+a_{2}(k)<a_{1}(k)+a_{2}(k)+a_{3}(k) , so the bound is not achieved by these graphs.
Is it achieved by other kind of graphs?
This is an open problem, not only for graphs with maximum degree  Δ = 3 \Delta=3 , but also for graphs with  4 ≤ Δ ≤ k − 2 4\leq\Delta\leq k-2 . 
 
 
 We know that the bound given by the Proposition  28  is not tight.
Indeed, for a tree  T T  with NLC-number  3 3 , Proposition  28  states that  Δ ⁡ ( T ) ≤ 5 \Delta(T)\leq 5 .
However, by Theorem  2 (1), we have that  n ⁡ ( T ) ≤ 9 n(T)\leq 9 .
Then, it is easy to verify that  χ N ​ L ( T ) = 3 \chi_{{}_{NL}}(T)=3  and  n ⁡ ( T ) ≤ 9 n(T)\leq 9  implies  Δ ⁡ ( T ) ≤ 4 \Delta(T)\leq 4 . 
 
 
 In general, we postulate the following. 
 
 
 Conjecture 29 .

If  T T  is a tree with  χ N ​ L ( T ) = k \chi_{{}_{NL}}(T)=k , then  Δ ⁡ ( T ) ≤ ( k − 1 ) 2 \Delta(T)\leq(k-1)^{2} .
Moreover, this bound is tight for every integer  k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 .
