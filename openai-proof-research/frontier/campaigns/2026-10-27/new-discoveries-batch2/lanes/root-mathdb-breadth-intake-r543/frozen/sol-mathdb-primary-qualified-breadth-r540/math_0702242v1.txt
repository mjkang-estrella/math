 Abstract. 
    
 A  quasi-polynomial  is a function defined of the form
 q ⁡ ( k ) = c d ​ ( k ) ​ k d + c d − 1 ​ ( k ) ​ k d − 1 + ⋯ + c 0 ​ ( k ) q(k)=c_{d}(k)\,k^{d}+c_{d-1}(k)\,k^{d-1}+\dots+c_{0}(k) ,
where  c 0 , c 1 , … , c d c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}  are periodic functions in  k ∈ ℤ k\in\mathbb{Z} .
Prominent examples
of quasi-polynomials appear in Ehrhart’s theory as integer-point
counting functions for rational polytopes, and McMullen gives upper bounds for the
periods of the  c j ​ ( k ) c_{j}(k)  for Ehrhart quasi-polynomials.
For generic polytopes, McMullen’s bounds seem to be sharp,
but sometimes smaller periods exist. We prove that the
second leading coefficient of an Ehrhart quasi-polynomial always has
maximal expected period and present a general theorem that yields
maximal periods for the coefficients of certain quasi-polynomials.
We present a construction for (Ehrhart) quasi-polynomials
that exhibit maximal period behavior and use it to answer a question
of Zaslavsky on convolutions of quasi-polynomials. 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  Ehrhart quasi-polynomial, period, lattice points, rational
polytope, quasi-polynomial convolution

 1.  Introduction 

 
 A  quasi-polynomial  is a function defined on  ℤ \mathbb{Z}  of the form 
 

 
 (1) 
 
 q ⁡ ( k ) = c d ​ ( k ) ​ k d + c d − 1 ​ ( k ) ​ k d − 1 + ⋯ + c 0 ​ ( k ) , q(k)=c_{d}(k)\,k^{d}+c_{d-1}(k)\,k^{d-1}+\dots+c_{0}(k)\,, 
 
 
 where  c 0 , c 1 , … , c d c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}  are periodic functions in  k k , called the
 coefficient functions  of  q q . Assuming  c d c_{d}  is not identically
zero, we call  d d  the  degree  of  q q . Quasi-polynomials play a
prominent role in enumerative combinatorics  [ 8 , Chapter
4] . Arguably their best known appearance is in Ehrhart’s
fundamental work on integer-point enumeration in rational polytopes
 [ 1 ,  3 ] . For more applications, we refer to the
recent article  [ 4 ] . 
 
 
 A  rational polytope   𝒫 ⊂ ℝ n {\mathcal{P}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}  is the convex hull of
finitely many points in  ℚ n \mathbb{Q}^{n} . The  dimension  of a polytope  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is the dimension  d d  of the smallest affine space containing  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} , in which case we call  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  a  d d -polytope. A  face  of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is a subset of
the form  𝒫 ∩ H {\mathcal{P}}\cap H , where  H H  is a hyperplane such that  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is
entirely contained in one of the two closed half-spaces of  ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}  that
 H H  naturally defines. A  ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -face of a  d d -polytope is a  facet , and a  0 0 -face is a
 vertex . The smallest  k ∈ ℤ > 0 k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}  for which the
vertices of  k ​ 𝒫 k{\mathcal{P}}  are in  ℤ n \mathbb{Z}^{n}  is the  denominator  of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} .
Ehrhart’s theorem states that the integer-point counting function
 L 𝒫 ​ ( k ) := # ⁡ ( k ​ 𝒫 ∩ ℤ n ) L_{\mathcal{P}}(k):=\#\left(k{\mathcal{P}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)  is a quasi-polynomial of degree  d d 
in  k ∈ ℤ > 0 k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0} , and the denominator of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is a period of each
of the coefficient functions. 
 
 
 In general, many of the coefficient functions will have
smaller periods. Suppose  q q  is given by ( 1 ). The
 minimum period  of  c j c_{j}  is the smallest  p ∈ ℤ > 0 p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}  such that
 c j ​ ( k + p ) = c j ​ ( k ) c_{j}(k+p)=c_{j}(k)  for all  k ∈ ℤ k\in\mathbb{Z}  (any multiple of  p p  is, of
course, also a period of  c j c_{j} ). The  minimum period  of  q q  is
the least common multiple of the minimum periods of  c 0 , c 1 , … , c d c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d} . In this paper, we study the minimum periods of the  c j c_{j} . All
of our illustrating examples can be realized as Ehrhart
quasi-polynomials. Ehrhart’s theorem tells us that the minimum period
of each  c j c_{j}  divides the denominator of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} . 
 
 
 The following theorem due to McMullen  [ 7 , Theorem
6]  gives a more precise upper bound for these
periods. For  0 ≤ j ≤ d 0\leq j\leq d , define the  j j -index  of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  to be
the minimal positive integer  p j p_{j}  such that the  j j -dimensional faces
of  p j ​ 𝒫 p_{j}{\mathcal{P}}  all span affine subspaces that contain integer lattice points. 
 
 
 Theorem 1   (McMullen) . 
 
 Given a rational
 d d -polytope  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} , let  p j p_{j}  be the  j j -index of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} . If  L 𝒫 ​ ( k ) = c d ​ ( k ) ​ k d + c d − 1 ​ ( k ) ​ k d − 1 + ⋯ + c 0 ​ ( k ) L_{\mathcal{P}}(k)=c_{d}(k)\,k^{d}+c_{d-1}(k)\,k^{d-1}+\dots+c_{0}(k)  is the
Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} , then the minimum period of  c j c_{j} 
divides  p j p_{j} . 
 
 
 
 Note that  p d | p d − 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ p 0 p_{d}|p_{d-1}|\cdots|p_{0} . Since  p 0 p_{0}  is the
denominator of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} , this is a stronger version of Ehrhart’s theorem.
If we further assume that  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is full-dimensional, then  p d = 1 p_{d}=1 , and
so  c d ​ ( k ) c_{d}(k)  is a constant function. In this case, it is well known
that  c d ​ ( k ) c_{d}(k)  is the Euclidean volume of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} 
 [ 1 ,  3 ] . 
 
 
 These bounds on the periods seem tight for generic rational
polytopes, that is,  p j p_{j}  is the minimum period of  c j c_{j} , but this
statement is ill-formed (we make no claim what notion of the term
 generic  should be used here) and conjectural. One of the
contributions of this paper is a step in the right direction: for any
 p d | p d − 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ p 0 p_{d}|p_{d-1}|\cdots|p_{0} , there does indeed exist a polytope
such that  c j c_{j}  has minimum period  p j p_{j} . 
 
 
 Theorem 2 . 
 
 Given distinct positive
integers  p d | p d − 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ p 0 p_{d}|p_{d-1}|\cdots|p_{0} , the simplex

 Theorem  2  complements recent literature  [ 2 ,  6 ]  that contains several special classes of polytopes
that defy the expectation that  c j c_{j}  has minimum period  p j p_{j} . De

quasi-polynomials have arbitrary period collapse (though not for the
periods of the individual coefficient functions), as well as an
example of non-monotonic minimum periods of the coefficient functions.

 2.  The Second Leading Coefficient of an Ehrhart
Quasi-Polynomial 

 
 In this section we prove Theorem  3 , namely the
minimum period of the second leading coefficient of the Ehrhart
quasi-polynomial of a rational  d d -polytope  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  equals the
 ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -index of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} .
Most of the work towards Theorem  3 
is contained in the proof of the following result. 
 
 
 Proposition 8 . 
 
 If  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is a rational
 d d -polytope with Ehrhart quasi-polynomial  L 𝒫 ​ ( k ) = c d ​ ( k ) ​ k d + c d − 1 ​ ( k ) ​ k d − 1 + ⋯ + c 0 ​ ( k ) L_{\mathcal{P}}(k)=c_{d}(k)\,k^{d}+c_{d-1}(k)\,k^{d-1}+\dots+c_{0}(k) , then  c d − 1 c_{d-1}  is
constant if and only if the  ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -index of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is  1 1 . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 If the  ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -index of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is  1 1 , then  c d − 1 c_{d-1}  is constant by
McMullen’s Theorem  1 . 
 
 
 For the converse implication, we use the  Ehrhart-Macdonald
Reciprocity Theorem   [ 1 ,  5 ] . It says that for a
rational  d d -polytope  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} , the evaluation of  L 𝒫 L_{\mathcal{P}}  at negative
integers yields the lattice-point enumerator of the interior
 𝒫 ∘ {\mathcal{P}}^{\circ} , namely, 
 

 
 
 L 𝒫 ​ ( − k ) = ( − 1 ) d ​ L 𝒫 ∘ ​ ( k ) . L_{\mathcal{P}}(-k)=(-1)^{d}L_{{\mathcal{P}}^{\circ}}(k)\,. 
 
 
 This identity implies that the lattice-point enumerator for the
boundary of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is the quasi-polynomial  L ∂ 𝒫 ​ ( k ) = L 𝒫 ​ ( k ) − ( − 1 ) d ​ L 𝒫 ​ ( − k ) L_{\partial{\mathcal{P}}}(k)=L_{\mathcal{P}}(k)-(-1)^{d}L_{\mathcal{P}}(-k) . Since  L ∂ 𝒫 ​ ( k ) L_{\partial{\mathcal{P}}}(k)  counts integer points in a
 ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -dimensional object, it is a degree  d − 1 d-1  quasi-polynomial, and we see that its leading coefficient is  c d − 1 ​ ( k ) + c d − 1 ​ ( − k ) c_{d-1}(k)+c_{d-1}(-k) . 
 
 
 Now suppose the  ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -index of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  is  m > 1 m>1 . Then there is a facet
of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}}  whose affine span has no lattice points when dilated by  1 , m + 1 , 2 ​ m + 1 , … 1,m+1,2m+1,\dots  On the other hand, the affine span of  any 
facet contains lattice points when dilated by multiples of  m m . The
leading coefficient of the quasi-polynomial  L ∂ 𝒫 ​ ( m ​ k ) L_{\partial{\mathcal{P}}}(mk) 
is a constant measuring the volume of the facets relative to the
sublattices in their affine spans (for the same reason that the
leading coefficient of  L 𝒫 ​ ( k ) L_{{\mathcal{P}}}(k)  measures the volume of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} :
asymptotically, the volume is a good approximation for the number of
integer points). The same can be said for the leading
coefficient of the polynomial  L ∂ 𝒫 ​ ( 1 + m ​ k ) L_{\partial{\mathcal{P}}}(1+mk) ; however, now
some of these sublattices are empty, and thus the leading coefficient
of  L ∂ 𝒫 ​ ( 1 + m ​ k ) L_{\partial{\mathcal{P}}}(1+mk)  is smaller than the leading coefficient
of  L ∂ 𝒫 ​ ( m ​ k ) L_{\partial{\mathcal{P}}}(mk) , i.e.,  c d − 1 ​ ( 1 + m ​ k ) + c d − 1 ​ ( − 1 − m ​ k ) < c d − 1 ​ ( m ​ k ) + c d − 1 ​ ( − m ​ k ) c_{d-1}(1+mk)+c_{d-1}(-1-mk)<\,c_{d-1}(mk)+c_{d-1}(-mk) . Hence  c d − 1 c_{d-1}  is
not constant.
∎ 
 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  3 . 
 
 Let  p p  be the minimal period of  c d − 1 c_{d-1}  and  q q  be the
 ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -index of  𝒫 {\mathcal{P}} . By McMullen’s Theorem  1 ,
 p | q p|q . On the other hand, the second-leading coefficient of  L p ​ 𝒫 L_{p{\mathcal{P}}}  is constant, and by Proposition  8 , the
 ( d − 1 ) (d-1) -index of  p ​ 𝒫 p{\mathcal{P}}  is 1, which implies  q | p q|p .
∎ 
 
 
 
 
 3.  Some General Results on Quasi-Polynomial
Periods 

 
 A key ingredient to proving Theorem  4  is a basic
result (see, e.g.,  [ 1 , Chapter 3]  or  [ 8 , Chapter
4] ) about a quasi-polynomial  q ⁡ ( k ) q(k)  and its generating
function  r ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ k ≥ 0 q ⁡ ( k ) ​ x k r(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}q(k)x^{k} , which is easily seen to be a
rational function. 
 
 
 Lemma 9 . 
 
 Suppose  q q  is a quasi-polynomial
with rational generating function  r ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ k ≥ 0 q ⁡ ( k ) ​ x k r(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}q(k)\,x^{k} . Then  n n  is a period of  q q  and  q q  has degree  d d  if and only
if all poles of  r r  are  n th n^{\text{th}}  roots of unity of order  ≤ d + 1 \leq d+1  and
there is a pole of order  d + 1 d+1 . 
 
 
 
 The above result will be useful again in the proof of
Theorem  2 . Recall that the statement of
Theorem  4  is that given a periodic function  c ⁡ ( k ) c(k) 
with minimum period  n n  and a nonnegative integer  m m , the only poles
of the rational generating function  ∑ k ≥ 0 c ⁡ ( k ) ​ k m ​ x k \sum_{k\geq 0}c(k)k^{m}x^{k} 
are  n th n^{\text{th}}  roots of unity, and each pole has order  m + 1 m+1 . 
 
 
 Proof of Theorem  4 .

 6.  Open Problems 

 
 
 (1)  
 
 For an Ehrhart quasi-polynomial, period collapse cannot happen in
relation to the  j j -index for the first two coefficients. On the
other side, McAllister–Woods  [ 6 ]  showed that
period collapse can happen for any other coefficient, however, it is
still a mystery to what extent. For example, can one construct
polygons whose Ehrhart periods are  ( 1 , s , t ) (1,s,t)  (the minimum periods of  c 2 ​ ( x ) c_{2}(x) ,  c 1 ​ ( x ) c_{1}(x) , and  c 0 ​ ( x ) c_{0}(x) , respectively), for given  s s  and  t t ?
Even simple examples are not easy to come by, e.g., we could
construct polygons with period sequence  ( 1 , s , 1 ) (1,s,1)  for  1 ≤ s ≤ 5 1\leq s\leq 5 
but have not been successful for  s = 6 s=6 . 
 
 (2)  
 
 In constructing the simplex with maximal period behavior, we required
that the integers  p 0 , … , p d p_{0},\dots,p_{d}  be distinct, but perhaps this
restriction is not necessary. Does the statement still hold true if we
weaken the conditions, or do there exist counterexamples? 
 
 (3)  
 
 In the example of periods of quasi-polynomial convolution, Theorem
 7 , our methods require that we assume that
 α d | α d − 1 | ⋯ | α e | β e ​ | α e − 1 | ​ β e − 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ α 0 | β 0 \alpha_{d}|\alpha_{d-1}|\cdots|\alpha_{e}|\beta_{e}|\alpha_{e-1}|\beta_{e-1}|\cdots|\alpha_{0}|\beta_{0} , rather than
the more natural  α d | α d − 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ α 0 \alpha_{d}|\alpha_{d-1}|\cdots|\alpha_{0}  and
 β e | β e − 1 ​ | ⋯ | ​ β 0 \beta_{e}|\beta_{e-1}|\cdots|\beta_{0} . We conjecture that the
theorem is still true in this case. 
 
 (4)  
 
 More generally, this would follow from a conjecture about a special
class of generating functions: 
 
 
 Conjecture 11 . 
 
 Let  a 1 , a 2 , … , a n a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}  be given positive integers. Let  q ⁡ ( k ) = c d ​ ( k ) ​ k d + ⋯ + c 0 ​ ( k ) q(k)=c_{d}(k)\,k^{d}+\dots+c_{0}(k)  be the
quasi-polynomial whose generating function  r ⁡ ( x ) = ∑ k ≥ 0 q ⁡ ( k ) ​ x k r(x)=\sum_{k\geq 0}q(k)x^{k}  is given by 
 

 
 
 1 ( 1 − x a 1 ) ( 1 − x a 2 ) ⋯ ( 1 − x a n ) . \frac{1}{(1-x^{a_{1}})(1-x^{a_{2}})\cdots(1-x^{a_{n}})}\,. 
 
 
 For a positive integer  m m , define  b m = # { i : m ∣ a i } b_{m}=\#\{i:\ m\mid a_{i}\} . For
 0 ≤ j ≤ d 0\leq j\leq d , let  p j = lcm { m : b m > j } p_{j}=\lcm\{m:\ b_{m}>j\} . Then the minimum period
of  c j ​ ( k ) c_{j}(k)  is  p j p_{j} . 
 
 
 (5)  
 
 There are several multi-parameter versions of Ehrhart polynomials to
which a generalization of McMullen’s
Theorem  1  applies. Beyond McMullen’s theorem, not
much is know about periods and minimum periods (which are now lattices
in some  ℤ m \mathbb{Z}^{m} ) of these multivariate quasi-polynomials and
coefficient functions.
