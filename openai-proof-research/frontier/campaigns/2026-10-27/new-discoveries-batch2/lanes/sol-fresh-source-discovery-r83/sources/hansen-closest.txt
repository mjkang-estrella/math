Abstract
In this work, we compute the first and second Zagreb indices for the commuting conjugacy class graphs associated with finite groups. We identify multiple classes of finite groups whose commuting conjugacy class graphs are shown to satisfy the Hansen-Vukičević conjecture. Specifically, we prove that the conjecture holds for the commuting conjugacy class graphs of dihedral groups (\(D_{2m}\)), dicyclic groups, semidihedral groups, and various other two-generator groups. Moreover, we examine the case where the quotient G/Z(G) is isomorphic to \(D_{2m}\), \(\mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\), a Frobenius group of order pq or \(p^2q\), or any group of order \(p^3\), for primes p and q. In each of these cases, we demonstrate that the corresponding commuting conjugacy class graph satisfies the Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.

1 Introduction
Let \(\mathcal {G}\) denote the collection of all graphs. A topological index for a graph \(\Gamma \in \mathcal {G}\) is expressed as \(T(\Gamma )\), where T is a real-valued function from \(\mathcal {G}\) to \(\mathbb {R}\). Originally, such indices were introduced to characterize various chemical attributes of molecular structures. Since 1947, numerous topological indices have been formulated based on different graph parameters. The earliest of these is the Wiener index, a distance-based measure introduced by Wiener [33]. Within the category of degree-based indices, the Zagreb indices are historically the first and were proposed by Gutman and Trinajstić in 1972 [10]. These indices were initially employed to investigate how the total \(\pi \)-electron energy of a molecule correlates with its structural features. As mentioned in [23], the Zagreb indices have also been applied in the analysis of molecular complexity, chirality, ZE-isomerism, and heterosystems, among others. Over time, mathematicians have explored the general mathematical characteristics of a variety of topological indices. A comprehensive overview of the mathematical aspects of Zagreb indices is available in the survey article [11]. More recent developments on Zagreb indices can be found in e.g. [14, 24, 25].
Let \(\Gamma \) be a simple, undirected graph characterized by the vertex set \(v(\Gamma )\) and edge set \(e(\Gamma )\). Two well-known degree-based topological indices for such graphs are the first and second Zagreb indices, denoted respectively by \(M_{1}(\Gamma )\) and \(M_{2}(\Gamma )\). These are formally defined as follows:$$ M_{1}(\Gamma ) = \sum \limits _{v \in v(\Gamma )} \deg (v)^{2} \quad \text {and} \quad M_{2}(\Gamma ) = \sum \limits _{uv \in e(\Gamma )} \deg (u)\deg (v), $$
where \(\deg (v)\) denotes the degree of a vertex v, that is, the number of edges incident to it. In an effort to explore the relationship between these two indices, Hansen and Vukičević [12] proposed a conjecture in 2007, which compares the behavior of \(M_1(\Gamma )\) and \(M_2(\Gamma )\) for various classes of graphs.
                
Conjecture 1.1
                
(Hansen-Vukičević Conjecture) Assume that \(\Gamma \) is a simple finite graph. We have the following inequality.$$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{M_{2}(\Gamma )}{\vert e(\Gamma ) \vert } \ge \dfrac{M_{1}(\Gamma )}{\vert v(\Gamma ) \vert } . \end{aligned}$$
                    (1)
                
              
It was demonstrated in [12] that the conjecture does not hold in general, as evidenced by the counterexample \(\Gamma = K_{1, 5} \sqcup K_3\). Nevertheless, Hansen and Vukičević [12] verified that Conjecture 1.1 remains valid within the class of chemical graphs. The validity of the conjecture has also been established for trees in [31], with equality in Eq. (1) occurring specifically when \(\Gamma \) is a star graph. For connected unicyclic graphs, the conjecture was confirmed in [18], where equality is attained in the case of cycle graphs. A more detailed examination of equality conditions in (1) was conducted in [32], and a broader survey on the comparison of Zagreb indices is available in [19].
Despite these advances, the complete classification of graphs that either satisfy or violate Conjecture 1.1 remains an open area of investigation. Recently, Das et al. [8] identified various finite non-abelian groups whose commuting or non-commuting graphs fulfill the Hansen-Vukičević conjecture. Similarly, Das and Nath [9] provided several examples of finite groups whose super commuting graphs also conform to the conjecture.
In this paper, we focus on the commuting conjugacy class graphs of finite groups. Specifically, we compute their first and second Zagreb indices and identify several families of finite groups for which these graphs satisfy the Hansen-Vukičević conjecture. Section 2.1 is devoted to analyzing the commuting conjugacy class graphs of certain group families. These include the dihedral groups \(D_{2m}\) defined by the presentation \(\langle a, b: a^m = b^2 = 1, bab^{-1} = a^{-1} \rangle \) for \(m \ge 3\); the dicyclic groups \(Q_{4m}\) presented as \(\langle a, b: a^{2m} = 1, b^2 = a^m, bab^{-1} = a^{-1} \rangle \) for \(m \ge 2\); and the semidihedral groups \(SD_{8m}\) with presentation \(\langle a, b: a^{4m} = b^2 = 1, bab = a^{2m-1} \rangle \) for \(m \ge 2\).
In addition to these, we examine the group \(V_{8m}\) given by \(\langle a, b: a^{2m}=b^{4}=1, ba=b^{-1}a^{-1}, b^{-1}a=a^{-1}b \rangle \) for \(m \ge 1\); the group \(U_{(n,m)}\) defined by \(\langle a, b: a^{2n}=b^m=1, a^{-1}ba=b^{-1} \rangle \) for \(m \ge 3\), \(n \ge 2\); and the group G(p, m, n) with the presentation \(\langle a,b: a^{p^m}=b^{p^n}=[a,b]^p=1, [a,[a,b]]=[b,[a,b]]=1 \rangle \) for any prime p and integers \(m, n \ge 1\). Section 2.2 explores commuting conjugacy class graphs of groups G for which the quotient group G/Z(G) is isomorphic to one of several specific types. These include \(D_{2m}\), \(\mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\), Frobenius groups of order pq and \(p^2q\), and all groups of order \(p^3\), where p and q are prime numbers.
The commuting conjugacy class graph of a group G, abbreviated as the CCC-graph, is defined as a simple undirected graph in which each vertex corresponds to a conjugacy class of a non-central element of G. Two distinct vertices, denoted \(x^G\) and \(y^G\)–where \(x^G\) represents the conjugacy class containing an element \(x \in G\)–are joined by an edge if and only if there exist representatives \(x' \in x^G\) and \(y' \in y^G\) such that \(x'y' = y'x'\). This graph is denoted by \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) and was first introduced by Herzog et al. [13] in 2009. Subsequent research has explored various structural properties of \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\). In 2016, Mohammadian et al. [21] classified all finite groups G for which the CCC-graph \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) is triangle-free. Further developments were made in [29, 30], where Salahshour and Ashrafi studied the structures of \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) for several families of finite CA-groups. Additional work by Salahshour in [28] examined CCC-graphs in cases where the quotient group G/Z(G) is isomorphic to a dihedral group. More recently, Rezaei et al. [26] determined the structure of \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) when G/Z(G) corresponds to a Frobenius group of order pq or \(p^2q\), where p and q are distinct prime numbers. Spectral characteristics of CCC-graphs have also been explored in a series of papers, including [4, 6, 16, 17]. In [5], the genus of \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) was computed, and various families of groups were identified whose CCC-graphs are planar, toroidal, double-toroidal, or even triple-toroidal. A comprehensive overview of the CCC-graph and its applications to group theory is available in the survey article [7].

2 Zagreb indices of CCC-graphs
The following result, obtained in [8], is useful in our computations.
                
Theorems 2.1
                
Suppose that \(\Gamma \) is the disjoint union of the graphs \(\Gamma _{1}, \Gamma _{2}, \dots , \Gamma _{n}\). Let \(\Gamma _{i} = l_{i}K_{m_{i}}\) for \( i = 1, 2, \dots , k\), where \(K_{m_{i}}\)’s are complete graphs on \( m_{i} \) vertices and \(l_iK_{m_i}\) is the disjoint union of \(l_i\) copies of \(K_{m_i}\). We obtain$$ M_{1}(\Gamma ) = \sum _{i=1}^{k}l_{i}m_{i}(m_{i}-1)^{2} ~~~ \text {and} ~~~ M_{2}(\Gamma ) = \sum _{i=1}^{k}l_{i}\dfrac{m_{i}(m_{i}-1)^{3}}{2}. $$
              
2.1 Zagreb indices of CCC-graphs of certain well-known finite groups
In this section, we consider the CCC-graphs for the groups \(D_{2m}, Q_{4m}, V_{8m}, SD_{8m}, U_{(n,m)}\) and G(p, m, n) and compute their Zagreb indices to finally check if they satisfy Hansen-Vukičević conjecture. The structures of the CCC-graphs of the above mentioned groups are obtained in [30]. However, while obtaining the structure of the CCC-graph of \(U_{(n,m)}\) in [30], one case was missed, which has been pointed out by Jannat and Nath in [15].
                  
Theorems 2.2
                  
If G is the dihedral group of order 2m, where \(m \ge 3\), then$$ M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G)) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \frac{(m-1)(m-3)^2}{8}, &  ~~~~\text { when } m \text { is odd} \\ \frac{(m-2)(m-4)^2}{8}, &  ~~~~\text { when } m \text { is even and } \frac{m}{2} \text { is even} \\ \frac{(m-2)(m-4)^2}{8} +2, &  ~~~~\text { when } m \text { is even and } \frac{m}{2} \text { is odd} \end{array}\right. } $$
and$$ M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G)) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \frac{(m-1)(m-3)^3}{32}, &  ~~~~\text { when } m \text { is odd} \\ \frac{(m-2)(m-4)^3}{32}, &  ~~~~\text { when } m \text { is even and } \frac{m}{2} \text { is even} \\ \frac{(m-2)(m-4)^3}{32} +1, &  ~~~~\text { when } m \text { is even and } \frac{m}{2} \text { is odd.} \end{array}\right. } $$
Further, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \ge \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\) with equality when \(m=3, 4\) and 6.
                
                  
Proof
                  
Case 1. m is odd.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{\frac{m-1}{2}} \sqcup K_1\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{m-1}{2}+1=\frac{m+1}{2}\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{m-1}{2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{(m-1)(m-3)}{8}\). Therefore,$$ \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{(m-1)(m-3)^2}{4(m+1)} \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{(m-3)^2}{4}. $$
Hence,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{(m-3)^2}{4}\left( \frac{2}{m+1}\right) \ge 0 $$
for all \(m \ge 3\).
                  
Case 2. m is even and \(\frac{m}{2}\) is even.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{\frac{m}{2}-1} \sqcup 2K_1\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{m}{2}-1+2=\frac{m+2}{2}\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{m}{2}-1\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{(m-2)(m-4)}{8}\). Therefore,$$ \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{(m-2)(m-4)^2}{4(m+2)} \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{(m-4)^2}{4}. $$
Hence,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{(m-4)^2}{4}\left( \frac{4}{m+2}\right) \ge 0 $$
for all \(m \ge 4\).
                  
Case 3. m is even and \(\frac{m}{2}\) is odd.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{\frac{m}{2}-1} \sqcup K_2\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{m}{2}-1+2=\frac{m+2}{2}\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{m}{2}-1\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +1=\frac{(m-2)(m-4)}{8}+1\). Therefore,$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&= \frac{(m-2)(m-4)^3 + 32}{4((m-2)(m-4) + 8)} \text { and } \\ \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&= \frac{(m-2)(m-4)^2 + 16}{4(m+2)}. \end{aligned}$$
Hence,$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&- \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\\&=\frac{m^2(m^2-16m+68)+20m(m-10)+8(m+18)}{(m+2)(m-2)(m-4)+8(m+2)} :=\frac{f(m)}{g(m)}. \end{aligned}$$
Note that \(g(m)=16\times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). We have \(f(6)=0\). Also, since \(m(m-16)+68>0\) for all \(m \ge 10\), therefore \(\frac{f(m)}{g(m)} \ge 0\). Hence, the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Theorems 2.3
                  
If G is the dicyclic group of order 4m, where \(m \ge 2\), then$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G)) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} (m-1)(m-2)^2, &  \text { when } m \text { is even} \\ (m-1)(m-2)^2+2, &  \text { when } m \text { is odd} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G)) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \frac{(m-1)(m-2)^3}{2}, &  \text { when } m \text { is even} \\ \frac{(m-1)(m-2)^3}{2}+1, &  \text { when } m \text { is odd.} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$
Further, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \ge \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\) with equality when \(m=2\) and 3.
                
                  
Proof
                  
Case 1. m is even.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{m-1} \sqcup 2K_1\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=m-1+2=m+1\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}m-1\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}{2}\). Therefore,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = (m-2)^2 \text { and } \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{(m-1)(m-2)^2}{m+1}. $$
Hence,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} =\frac{2(m-2)^2}{m+1} \ge 0 $$
for all \(m \ge 2\).
                  
Case 2. m is odd.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{m-1} \sqcup K_2\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=m-1+2=m+1\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}m-1\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +1=\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}{2}+1\). Therefore,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{(m-1)(m-2)^3+2}{(m-1)(m-2)+2} \text { and } \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{(m-1)(m-2)^2+2}{m+1}. $$
Hence,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} =\frac{m^2(m^2-8\,m+14)+8\,m(m-3)+9}{(m+1)(m-1)(m-2)+2(m+1)}:=\frac{f(m)}{g(m)}. $$
Note that \(g(m)=2 \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). We have \(f(3)=0\) and \(f(5)=64 >0\). Since \(m(m-8)+14 > 0\) for all \(m \ge 7\), we have \(\frac{f(m)}{g(m)} \ge 0\). Hence the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Theorems 2.4
                  
If G is the semidihedral group of order 8m, where \(m \ge 2\), then$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G)) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} (2m-1)(2m-2)^2, &  \text { when } m \text { is even} \\ (2m-2)(2m-3)^2+36, &  \text { when } m \text { is odd} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G)) = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \frac{(2m-1)(2m-2)^3}{2}, &  \text { when } m \text { is even} \\ (m-1)(2m-3)^3+54, &  \text { when } m \text { is odd.} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$
Further, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \ge \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\) with equality when \(m=3\).
                
                  
Proof
                  
Case 1. m is even.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{2m-1} \sqcup 2K_1\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=2m-1+2=2m+1\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}2m-1\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +0=\frac{(2m-1)(2m-2)}{2}\). Therefore,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = (2\,m-2)^2 \text { and } \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{(2\,m-1)(2\,m-2)^2}{2\,m+1}. $$
Hence,$$ \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} =\frac{2(2\,m-2)^2}{2\,m+1} \ge 0 $$
for all \(m \ge 1\).
                  
Case 2. m is odd.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{2m-2}\sqcup K_4\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=2m-2+4=2m+2\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}2m-2\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}4\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{(2m-2)(2m-3)}{2}+6=(m-1)(2m-3)+6\). Therefore,$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&= \frac{(m-1)(2\,m-3)^3+54}{(m-1)(2\,m-3)+6} \text { and } \\ \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&= \frac{(m-1)(2\,m-3)^2+18}{m+1}. \end{aligned}$$
Hence,$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&=\frac{8m^3(m-7)+4m(30m-18)}{(m+1)(m-1)(2m-3)+6(m+1)} :=\frac{f(m)}{g(m)}. \end{aligned}$$
Note that \(g(m)=\frac{1}{2} \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). We have \(f(3)=0\) and \(f(5)=640>0\). Also, since \(30m-18>0\) for all \(m \ge 7\), therefore \(\frac{f(m)}{g(m)} \ge 0\). Hence the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Theorems 2.5

2.2 Zagreb indices of CCC-graphs of groups with a given central quotient
In this section, we consider the CCC-graphs of the groups G when G/Z(G) is isomorphic to \(D_{2m}\), \(\mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\), Frobenious group of order pq and \(p^2q\) or any group of order \(p^3\) for any two primes p and q. The structures of CCC-graphs of groups when its central quotient is isomorphic to a dihedral group have been obtained in [28, Theorem 1.2]. Using which we get the following result.
                  
Theorems 2.8
                  
Let G be a finite group such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong D_{2m}\), where \(m \ge 3.\) Then$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))= {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \frac{1}{8}\left( (mx-x)(mx-x-2)^2+2x(x-2)^2\right) , &  \text { when } m \text { is even} \\ \frac{1}{8}\left( (mx-x)(mx-x-2)^2+8x(x-1)^2\right) , &  \text { when } m \text { is odd} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$

Proof

for all \(m \ge 4\).
                  
Case 2. m is odd.
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{\frac{x(m-1)}{2}} \sqcup K_x\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{mx-x}{2}+x=\frac{1}{2}(mx+x)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{mx-x}{2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}x\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{1}{8}((mx-x)(mx-x-2)+4x(x-1))\). The structures of CCC-graphs of groups when its central quotient is isomorphic to \(\mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\) (for any prime p) have been obtained in [29, Theorem 3.1]. Therefore, we get the following result.
                  
Theorems 2.9
                  
Let G be a finite group and \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong \mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\), where p is a prime. where \(x=|Z(G)|\). Further, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\).
                
                  
Proof
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G) = (p+1)K_{\frac{x(p-1)}{p}}\). Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=(p+1) \times \frac{x(p-1)}{p}=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=(p+1)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x(p^2-1)}{2p^2}(px-x-p)\). We have$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}&=\frac{p^2x^2-2px^2+x^2-2p^2x+2px+p^2}{p^2} = \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}. \end{aligned}$$
Hence, the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Corollary 2.10
                  
If G is a group of order \(p^n\) and \(|Z(G)|=p^{n-2}\), where p is a prime and \(n \ge 3\), then$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=p^{3n-5}-2p^{3n-6}+2p^{3n-8}-p^{3n-9}-2p^{2n-3}+2p^{2n-4}+2p^{2n-5}\\&\quad -2p^{2n-6} +p^{n-1}-p^{n-3} \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=p^{4n-7}-3p^{4n-8}+2p^{4n-9}+2p^{4n-10}-3p^{4n-11}\\&\quad +p^{4n-12}-3p^{3n-5}+6p^{3n-6} \\&\quad \quad -6p^{3n-8}+3p^{3n-9}+3p^{2n-3}-3p^{2n-4}-3p^{2n-5}\\&\quad +3p^{2n-6}-p^{n-1}+p^{n-3}. \end{aligned}$$
Further, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\).
                
                  
Proof
                  
Clearly, \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\cong \mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\) and \(\frac{(p-1)|Z(G)|}{p}=(p-1)p^{n-3}\). Therefore, the result follows from Theorem 2.9. \(\square \)
                
Putting \(n =3\) in Corollary 2.10 we get the following result.
                  
Corollary 2.11
                  
If G is a non-abelian group of order \(p^3\) then$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=p^4 - 4p^{3} + 3p^2 + 4p - 4, \\ M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&= p^5 - 6p^4 + 2p^3 + 9p^3 - 2p^2 -12p +8 \end{aligned}$$
and \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} = \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\).

Theorems 2.12
                  
Let p and q be two primes. Let G be a finite group such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong \mathbb {Z}_q \rtimes _f \mathbb {Z}_p\), the Frobenious group of order pq with kernel \(\mathbb {Z}_q\) and complement \(\mathbb {Z}_p\). Then$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=\frac{x}{p^3}\left( q^3x^2-3q^2x^2+3qx^2+p^6x^2-3p^5x^2\right. \\&\quad \left. +3p^4x^2-p^3x^2-x^2-2pq^2x \right. \\&\quad \left. +4pqx-2p^5x+4p^4x-2p^3x-2px+p^2q+p^4-p^3-p^2 \right) \ \text {and} \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=\frac{x}{2p^4}\left( q^4x^3-4q^3x^3+6q^2x^3-4px^3-p^3q-4p^7x^3 \right. \\&\quad \left. +6p^6x^3-4p^5x^3+3px^2\right. \\&\quad \left. +3p^2q^2x-3pq^3x^2+9pq^2x^2-9pqx^2-3p^7x^2\right. \\&\quad \left. +9p^6x^2-9p^5x^2+3p^4x^2 \right. \\&\quad \left. +p^4x^3-6p^2qx+3p^6x-6p^5x+3p^4x+3p^2x\right. \\&\quad \left. +x^3+p^8x^3-p^5+p^4+p^3 \right) \end{aligned}$$
where \(x=|Z(G)|\). Further, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \ge \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\).
                
                  
Proof
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{\frac{x(q-1)}{p}} \sqcup K_{x(p-1)}\), where \(x=|Z(G)|\).Therefore, by Theorem 2.1, we get the expressions for \(M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\) and \(M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))\).
                  
Note that \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{x(q-1)}{p}+x(p-1)=\frac{x}{p}(p^2+q-p-1)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(q-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}x(p-1)\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x}{2p^2}(p^2x+q^2x+p^4x+x-2qx-pq-2p^3x-p^3+p^2+p)\). It can be seen that$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} =\frac{f(p,q,x)}{g(p,q,x)}, \end{aligned}$$
where$$\begin{aligned} f(p,q,x)&=p^2q^4x^3-pq^4x^3-p^4q^3x^3+2p^3q^3x^3-5p^2q^3x^3\\&\quad +4pq^3x^3-p^6q^2x^3+3p^5q^2x^3 \\&\quad -5p^3q^2x^3+9p^2q^2x^3-6pq^2x^3+p^8qx^3-4p^7qx^3\\&\quad +8p^6qx^3-10p^5qx^3-p^3x^3 \\&\quad +4p^4qx^3+4p^3qx^3-7p^2qx^3+4pqx^3-p^8x^3\\&\quad +4p^7x^3-7p^6x^3+7p^5x^3-px^3 \\&\quad +2p^2x^3-2p^3q^3x^2+2p^2q^3x^2+4p^5q^2x^2\\&\quad -8p^4q^2x^2-6p^2q^2x^2-2p^7qx^2 \\&\quad +6p^6qx^2-14p^5qx^2+18p^4qx^2-14p^3qx^2\\&\quad +6p^2qx^2+2p^7x^2-6p^6x^2 \\&\quad +10p^5x^2-3p^4x^3-10p^4x^2+6p^3x^2-2p^2x^2 \end{aligned}$$
and \(g(p,q,x) = p^2(p^2+q-p-1)(p^2x+q^2x+p^4x+x-2qx-pq-2p^3x-p^3+p^2+p)\). On factorization, we have \(f(p,q,x)=px^2(p-1)(q-1)(p+q-p^2-1)^2(p^2x+qx-x-2p-px)\). Suppose that \(p^2x+qx-x-2p-px=p(x(p-1)-2)+x(q-1):=S_1 + S_2\), where \(S_1=p(x(p-1)-2)\) and \(S_2=x(q-1) >0\) for all prime q and \(x \ge 1\). Clearly, \(S_1 \ge 0\) for all prime \(p \ge 3\) and \(x \ge 1\). Now, \(f(2,q,1)=2(q-1)(q-3)^2(q-3) \ge 0\) for all \(q \ge 3\). Also, \(g(p,q,x)=\frac{2p^5}{x^2} \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). Therefore, \(\frac{f(p,q,x)}{g(p,q,x)} \ge 0\) and hence the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Theorems 2.13
                  
Let G be a finite group such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is isomorphic to a Frobenious group of order \(p^2q\), where p and q are primes. Then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture if \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong A_4\) or \(p<q\) and \(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|\ne 12\) or \(p>q\).
                
                  
Proof
                  
We have the following three cases.
                  
Case 1. \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong A_4\).
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{2x} \sqcup K_x\) or \(K_{2x} \sqcup K_{\frac{x}{2}}\), where \(x=|Z(G)|\). If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{2x} \sqcup K_x\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=2x(2x-1)^2+x(x-1)^2=9x^3-10x^2+3x \text { \quad and} \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=2x\frac{(2x-1)^3}{2}+x\frac{(x-1)^3}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left( 17x^4-27x^3+15x^2-3x\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=2x+x=3x\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}2x\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}x\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{1}{2}(5x^2-3x)\). Therefore, for all \(x \ge 1\), we have$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{6x^5-4x^4}{3x(5x^2-3x)}>0. \end{aligned}$$
If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{2x} \sqcup K_{\frac{x}{2}}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=2x(2x-1)^2+\frac{x}{2}\left( \frac{x}{2}-1\right) ^2=\frac{1}{8}\left( 65x^3-68x^2+20x\right) \text { \quad and} \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=2x\frac{(2x-1)^3}{2}+\frac{x}{2}\frac{(\frac{x}{2}-1)^3}{2}=\frac{1}{32}\left( 257x^4-390x^3+204x^2-40x\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=2x+\frac{x}{2}=\frac{5x}{2}\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}2x\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x}{2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{1}{8}(17x^2-10x)\). Therefore, for all \(x \ge 1\), we have$$\begin{aligned} \frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{180x^5-144x^4}{20x(17x^2-10x)}>0. \end{aligned}$$
                  
Case 2. \(p<q\) and \(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|\ne 12\).
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{\frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}}\sqcup K_{x(p^2-1)}\), where \(x=|Z(G)|\). As such, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}+x(p^2-1)=\frac{1}{p^2}(p^4x+qx-p^2x-x)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}x(p^2-1)\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{1}{2p^4}( q^2x^2-2qx^2+p^8x^2-2p^6x^2+p^4x^2+x^2-p^2qx-p^6x+p^4x+p^2x)\). By Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}\left( \frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^2+x(p^2-1)\left( x(p^2-1)-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{1}{p^6}\left( q^3x^3-3q^2x^3+3q x^3+p^{12}x^3-3p^{10}x^3+3p^8x^3-p^6x^3-2p^2q^2x^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -x^3+4p^2qx^2+4p^8x^2-2p^{10}x^2-2p^6x^2+p^4qx \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -2p^2x^2+p^8x-p^4x-p^6x\right) \text { \quad and } \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned}&M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}\frac{\left( \frac{x(q-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^3}{2}+x(p^2-1)\frac{\left( x(p^2-1)-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{1}{2p^8}\left( q^4x^4-4q^3x^4+6q^2x^4-4qx^4+p^{16}x^4-4p^{14}x^4-4p^{10}x^4+6p^{12}x^4 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +x^4+p^8x^4-3p^2q^3x^3+9p^2q^2x^3-9p^2qx^3+9p^{12}x^3-3p^{14}x^3 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +3p^8x^3-9p^{10}x^3+3p^4q^2x^2+3p^2x^3-6p^4qx^2+3p^{12}x^2\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -6p^{10}x^2+3p^4x^2 +3p^8x^2-p^{10}x-p^6qx+p^6x+p^8x\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Therefore, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{f_1(p,q,x)}{g_1(p,q,x)}\), where \(f_1(p,q,x)=p^2x^4(p-1)(p+1)(p^4-p^2-q+1)^2(q-1)(p^2(p^2x-x-2)+x(q-1))\) after factorization and \(g_1(p,q,x)=2p^{10} \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| >0\). For all prime \(p \ge 2\) and \(x \ge 1\), we have \(x(p^2-1) > 2\). As such, \(f_1(p,q,x) >0\) and therefore, \(\frac{f_1(p,q,x)}{g_1(p,q,x)} >0\).
                  
Case 3. \(p > q\).
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{x(q-1)}\sqcup K_{\frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}}\) or \(K_{x(q-1)}\sqcup (p+1)K_{\frac{x(p-1)}{pq}}\). If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{x(q-1)}\sqcup K_{\frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=x(q-1)\left( x(q-1)-1\right) ^2+\frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{1}{q^3}\left( q^6x^3-3q^5x^3+3q^4x^3-q^3x^3+p^6x^3-3p^4x^3+3p^2x^3-2q^5x^2-x^3 \right. \\&\qquad \left. +4q^4x^2-2q^3x^2-2p^4qx^2+4p^2qx^2-2qx^2+q^4x -q^3x+p^2q^2x-q^2x\right) \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned}&M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=x(q-1)\frac{\left( x(q-1)-1\right) ^3}{2}+\frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{1}{2q^4}\left( q^8x^4-4q^7x^4+6q^6x^4-4q^5x^4+p^8x^4+q^4x^4-4p^6x^4+6p^4x^4 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -4p^2x^4+x^4-3q^7x^3+9q^6x^3-9q^5x^3+3q^4x^3-3p^6qx^3+9p^4qx^3 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -9p^2qx^3+3q^6x^2+3qx^3-6q^5x^2+3p^4q^2x^2+3q^4x^2-6p^2q^2x^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +3q^2x^2-q^5x+q^4x+q^3x-p^2q^3x\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=x(q-1)+\frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}=\frac{1}{q}(q^2x-qx+p^2x-x)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}x(q-1)\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p^2-1)}{q}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{1}{2q^2}(q^4x^2-2q^3x^2+q^2x^2+p^4x^2-2p^2x^2-q^3x+x^2-p^2qx+q^2x+qx)\). Therefore, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{f_2(p,q,x)}{g_2(p,q,x)}\), where \(f_2(p,q,x)=qx^4(p-1)(p+1)(q-1)(q^2-p^2-q+1)^2(x(p^2-q-1)+q(qx-2))\) after factorization and \(g_2(p,q,x)=2q^5 \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| >0\). Since \(p>q\), so \(p^2-q>1\) and \(qx>2\) for all prime \(q \ge 2\) and \(x \ge 1\). As such, \(f_2(p,q,x)>0\) and therefore, \(\frac{f_2(p,q,x)}{g_2(p,q,x)}>0\).
                  
If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{x(q-1)}\sqcup (p+1)K_{\frac{x(p-1)}{pq}}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=x(q-1)\left( x(q-1)-1\right) ^2+(p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{pq}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{pq}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{1}{p^3q^3}\left( p^3q^6x^3-3p^3q^5x^3+3p^3q^4x^3-p^3q^3x^3+p^4x^3-2p^3x^3+2px^3\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -2p^3q^5x^2-x^3+4p^3q^4x^2-2p^4qx^2-2p^3q^3x^2+2p^2qx^2\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +2p^3qx^2+p^3q^4x-2pqx^2+p^4q^2x-p^3q^3x-p^2q^2x\right) \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned}&M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=x(q-1)\frac{\left( x(q-1)-1\right) ^3}{2}+(p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{pq}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{pq}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{1}{2p^4q^4}\left( p^4q^8x^4-4p^4q^7x^4+6p^4q^6x^4-4p^4q^5x^4+p^4q^4x^4+p^5x^4-3p^4x^4 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +2p^2x^4+2p^3x^4-3px^4+x^4-3p^4q^7x^3+9p^4q^6x^3-9p^4q^5x^3\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +3p^4q^4x^3-3p^5qx^3+6p^4qx^3-6p^2qx^3+3p^4q^6x^2+3pqx^3 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. - 6p^4q^5x^2+3p^5q^2x^2+3p^4q^4x^2-3p^3q^2x^2-3p^4q^2x^2+3p^2q^2x^2\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -p^4q^5x+p^4q^4x+p^3q^3x-p^5q^3x\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=x(q-1)+(p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{pq}=\frac{1}{pq}(pq^2x-pqx+p^2x-x)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}x(q-1)\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +(p+1)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{pq}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{1}{2p^2q^2}(p^2q^4x^2-2p^2q^3x^2+p^2q^2x^2+p^3x^2-px^2-p^2x^2+x^2-p^2q^3x+p^2q^2x+pqx-p^3qx)\). Therefore, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{f_3(p,q,x)}{g_3(p,q,x)}\), where \(f_3(p,q,x)=pqx^4(p-1) (p+1)(q-1)(pq^2-pq-p+1)^2(pq(qx-x-2)+x(p-1))\) after factorization and \(g_3(p,q,x)=2p^5q^5 \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| >0\). Since \(x\ge p > q\), so \(x(q-1)>2\). As such, \(f_3(p,q,x)>0\) and therefore, \(\frac{f_3(p,q,x)}{g_3(p,q,x)}>0\).
                  
In all the cases, we have \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} > \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\) and hence the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Theorems 2.14
                  
Let G be a non-abelian group such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is abelian and \(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|=p^3\), where p is a prime. Then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
                
                  
Proof
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_m \sqcup p^2K_n\) or \((p^2+p+1)K_n\), where \(m=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}, n=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\) and \(x=|Z(G)|\). If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_m \sqcup p^2K_n\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))= \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2+p^2 \times \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{p^4}\left( p^6x^2-2p^4x^2+2p^2x^2-2p^5x+2p^4+p^5x^2-2p^3x^2-px^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -2p^4x+4p^2x+p^3+x^2\right) \quad \text { and } \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned}&M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))= \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2}+p^2 \times \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^6}\left( p^9x^3+p^8x^3-3p^8x^2-3p^7x^3-3p^7x^2-3p^6x^3+6p^6x^2\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +3p^7x +3p^6x+3p^5x^3+6p^5x^2+3p^4x^3-6p^4x^2-6p^4x \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +3p^3x^2-4p^2x^3-p^5-2p^6-x^3-3p^2x^2+3px^3\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}+p^2 \times \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}=x(p-1)(p+2)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +p^2\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^2}(p^3x+p^2x-2p^2-2x-p)\). Therefore,$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} -\frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{1}{g(p,x)}\\&\qquad \times \left( p (p^2-1)\left( p^4x^3(p^3-2p-4)+p^4x^2(p^2x-2x-5p)+x^2(2px-3p-3x-1)\right. \right. \\&\qquad \left. \left. +p^6x^2(x-3)+3p^5x+p^2x(5x^2-4x-6)+p^4(4x^2+3x-2)+p^3(x^3+12x^2-1)\right) \right) \\&\quad :=\frac{f(p,x)}{g(p,x)}, \end{aligned}$$
where \(g(p,x)=p^4(p+2)(p^3x+p^2x-2p^2-2x-p)=p^4(p+2)(x(p^3-2)-p+p^2(x-2)) > 0\) since \(p^3-2>p\) and \(x>2\). Now, \(f(2,x)=750x^3-1290x^2+720x-240 > 0\) for all \(x \ge p^2 \ge 4\). Also, for all \(p > 2\), we have \(p^3-2p-4> 0, p^2x-2x-5p>0, 2px-3p-3x-1>0\) and \(5x^2-4x-6>0\). Thus, \(\frac{f(p,x)}{g(p,x)}>0\).
                  
If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=(p^2+p+1)K_n\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))= (p^2+p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{x(p^3-1)}{p^6}\left( p^2x^2-2px^2+x^2-2p^3x+2p^2x+p^4\right) \text { \quad and } \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned}&M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))= (p^2+p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{x(p^3-1)}{2p^8}\left( p^3x^3-3p^2x^3+3px^3-x^3-3p^4x^2 \right. \\&\qquad \left. +6p^3x^2-3p^2x^2+3p^5x -3p^4x-p^6\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=(p^2+p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}=\frac{x(p^3-1)}{p^2}\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=(p^2+p+1)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) \) \(=\frac{x(p^3-1)}{2p^4}(px-x-p^2)\).
                  
It can be seen that$$ \frac{M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{p^2x^2-2px^2+x^2-2p^3x+2p^2x+p^4}{p^4} = \frac{M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}. $$
In both the cases, we have \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \ge \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\) and hence the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Corollary 2.15
                  
Let G be a non-abelian group of order \(p^n\) such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is abelian and \(|Z(G)|=p^{n-3}\), where p is a prime and \(n \ge 4\). Then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
                
                  
Proof
                  
Clearly \(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|=p^3\) and so the result follows from Theorem 2.14. \(\square \)
                
In the remaining part of this section we consider non-abelian groups G such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is a group of order \(p^3\) for any prime p. The CCC-graphs of these groups have been obtained in [29].
                  
Theorems 2.16
                  
Let G be a non-abelian group such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is non-abelian and \(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|=p^3\), where p is a prime. Then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
                
                  
Proof
                  
We have \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_m \sqcup kpK_{n_1} \sqcup (p-k)K_{n_2}, (kp+1)K_{n_1} \sqcup (p+1-k)K_{n_2}, K_m \sqcup pK_{n_2}, (p^2+p+1)K_{n_1}\) or \(K_{n_1} \sqcup (p+1)K_{n_2}\), where \(x=|Z(G)|, m=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}, n_1=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}, n_2=\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\) and \(1 \le k \le p\). If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_m \sqcup kpK_{n_1} \sqcup (p-k)K_{n_2}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2+kp\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad \quad +(p-k)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{p^6}\left( p^8x^2+p^7x^2-2p^7x-p^6x^2-4p^6x-4p^5x^2+2p^4x^2+p^3x^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +2p^4x+p^5+4p^5x+2p^6-2kp^2x^2+kpx^2-4kp^4x \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +2kp^3x-kp^5x^2+2kp^4x^2+2kp^5x\right) \text { \quad and} \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned}&M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2}+kp\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad \quad +(p-k)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^8}\left( p^{11}x^3-3p^9x^3-2p^5x^3-3p^{10}x^2+3p^8x^2-6p^6x^2+3p^9x \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -6p^7x-2p^8+p^{10}x^3-2p^8x^3+6p^6x^3-p^4x^3-3p^9x^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +12p^7x^2-3p^5x^2+6p^8x-3p^6x-p^7+2kp^4x^3-3kp^3x^3\right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +3kp^2x^3-kpx^3+6kp^4x^2-3kp^3x^2+6kp^6x-3kp^5x \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -kp^7x^3+3kp^6x^3-3kp^5x^3+3kp^7x^2-6kp^6x^2-3kp^7x \right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}+kp\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}+(p-k)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}(2p^2+p)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +kp\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +(p-k)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^4}(p^5x+2p^4x-2p^3x-2p^4-p^2x-p^3+2p^2kx-kpx-kp^3x)\). Therefore,$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} =\frac{f_1(p,k,x)}{g_1(p,k,x)}, \end{aligned}$$
where$$\begin{aligned} f_1(p,k,x)&=p^{13}x^3-4p^{11}x^3-2p^{12}x^2+2p^{10}x^2+2p^{10}x^3-2p^8x^3+2p^{11}x^2-2p^9x^2 \\&\quad -kp^6x^3-10kp^5x^3+4kp^4x^3+2kp^3x^3+8kp^6x^2+4kp^5x^2-4kp^9x^3-2kp^8x^3 \\&\quad +11kp^7x^3+4kp^9x^2+14kp^8x^2+3p^9x^3-kp^2x^3-2kp^4x^2-24kp^7x^2 \\&\quad -4kp^{10}x^2+5k^2p^4x^3-4k^2p^3x^3+12k^2p^6x^2-8k^2p^5x^2+4k^2p^7x^3 \\&\quad -5k^2p^6x^3-8k^2p^7x^2+k^2p^2x^3 +2k^2p^4x^2+kp^{11}x^3-k^2p^8x^3+2k^2p^8x^2 \end{aligned}$$
and \(g_1(p,k,x) = p^4(2p^2+p)(p^5x+2p^4x-2p^3x-2p^4-p^2x-p^3+2p^2kx-kpx-kp^3x)\). On factorization, we have$$\begin{aligned} f_1(p,k,x)&=p^2x^2(p-1)^2\left( p^6x(p^3-p-2)+p^7(px-2p-2) +p^4x(p^4-k^2) \right. \\&\quad \left. +2k^2p^2(p^2+1)+kx(k-1)+kp^4(x(p^3-p-6)-4p+10) \right. \\&\quad \left. +kp^2(5x-2)+2k^2p(p^2x-x-2p^2)+2kp^6(x-2)\right) . \end{aligned}$$
For all prime \(p \ge 2, 1 \le k \le p\) and \(x \ge p^2\), we have \(p^3-p-2> 0, px-2p-2>0, p^4-k^2>0, p^2x-x-2p^2>0\) and \(x(p^3-p-6) > 4p-10\). As such, \(f_1(p,k,x) > 0\). Also, \(g_1(p,k,x)=\frac{2p^{10}}{x^2(p-1)^2} \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). Therefore, \(\frac{f_1(p,k,x)}{g_1(p,k,x)}>0\).
                  
If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=(kp+1)K_{n_1} \sqcup (p+1-k)K_{n_2}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned} M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G)&=(kp+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad +(p+1-k)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2 \\&= \frac{x(p-1)}{p^6}\left( p^6x^2+p^6+p^5-2kp^2x^2+kpx^2-4kp^4x+2kp^3x+p^2x^2 \right. \\&\quad \left. -2px^2+x^2-2p^3x+2p^2x+p^4-p^5x^2-p^4x^2-2p^6x+p^3x^2 \right. \\&\quad \left. +2p^4x-kp^5x^2+2kp^4x^2+2kp^5x \right) \text { \quad and } \end{aligned}$$$$\begin{aligned} M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))&=(kp+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^3}{2}+(p+1-k)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&=\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^8}\left( 2kp^4x^3-3kp^3x^3+3kp^2x^3-kpx^3+6kp^4x^2-3kp^3x^2+p^3x^3 \right. \\&\quad \left. +6kp^6x -3kp^5x-3p^2x^3+3px^3-x^3-3p^4x^2+6p^3x^2-p^6 \right. \\&\quad \left. -3p^2x^2+3p^5x-3p^4x-2p^7x^3+2p^5x^3-3p^8x^2+3p^7x^2 \right. \\&\quad \left. +p^8x^3+3p^8x-3p^5x^2-p^8-p^4x^3+3p^6x^2-3p^6x-p^7 \right. \\&\quad \left. -kp^7x^3+3kp^6x^3-3kp^5x^3+3kp^7x^2-6kp^6x^2-3kp^7x \right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=(kp+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}+(p+1-k)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}(p^2+p+1)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=(kp+1)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +(p+1-k)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^4}(2kp^2x-kpx+px-x-p^2+p^4x+p^4-p^2x-p^3-kp^3x)\). Therefore,$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \\&\quad =\frac{f_2(p,k,x)}{g_2(p,k,x)}, \end{aligned}$$
where$$\begin{aligned} f_2(p,k,x)&=kp^9x^3-k^2p^8x^3-3kp^8x^3+p^8x^3+4k^2p^7x^3-3p^7x^3-5k^2p^6x^3+9kp^6x^3 \\&\quad +p^6x^3-10kp^5x^3+5p^5x^3+5k^2p^4x^3-kp^4x^3-5p^4x^3-4k^2p^3x^3+8kp^3x^3 \\&\quad -p^3x^3+k^2p^2x^3-5kp^2x^3+3p^2x^3+kpx^3-px^3-2kp^9x^2+2k^2p^8x^2 \\&\quad +6kp^8x^2-2p^8x^2-8k^2p^7x^2-2kp^7x^2+6p^7x^2+12k^2p^6x^2-12kp^6x^2\\&\quad -4p^6x^2-8k^2p^5x^2+18kp^5x^2 -4p^5x^2+2k^2p^4x^2-10kp^4x^2+6p^4x^2 \\&\quad +2kp^3x^2-2p^3x^2 \end{aligned}$$
and \(g_2(p,k,x) = p^4(p^2+p+1)(2kp^2x-kpx+px-x-p^2+p^4x+p^4-p^2x-p^3-kp^3x)\). On factorization, we have \(f_2(p,k,x)=px^2(p-1)^4(p+1-k)(kp+1)(p^2x-x-2p^2)\). For all \(p \ge 2, 1 \le k \le p\) and \(x \ge p^2\), we have \(p+1-k > 0\) and \(x(p^2-1) > 2p^2\). As such, \(f_2(p,k,x) > 0\). Also, \(g_2(p,k,x)=\frac{2p^{10}}{x^2(p-1)^2} \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). Therefore, \(\frac{f_2(p,k,x)}{g_2(p,k,x)}>0\).
                  
If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_m \sqcup pK_{n_2}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2+p \frac{x(p-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{p^3}\left( p^5x^2-p^3x^2+2px^2-2p^4x+2p^3+p^4x^2-4p^2x^2+x^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +2px-4p^3x+4p^2x+p^2 \right) \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned}&M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2}+p \frac{x(p-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^4}\left( p^7x^3-3p^5x^3-2px^3-3p^6x^2+3p^4x^2-6p^2x^2+3p^5x-6p^3x \right. \\&\qquad \left. -2p^4+p^6x^3-2p^4x^3+6p^2x^3-x^3-3p^5x^2+12p^3x^2-3px^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +6p^4x-3p^2x-p^3 +6p^4x-3p^2x-p^3\right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}+p\frac{x(p-1)}{p}=\frac{x(p-1)}{p}(2p+1)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p^2-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +p\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^2}(p^3x-2px+2p^2x-2p^2-x-p)\). Therefore, for all \(p \ge 2\) and \(x \ge p^2\), we have$$\begin{aligned}&\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} \\&\quad =\frac{p^6x^3(p^2-4)+2p^5x^2(x-p^2)+p^3x^3(3p-2)+2p^6x^2+2p^4x^2(p-1)}{p^2(2p+1)\{x(p^3-2p-1)+p(2px-2p-1)\}} >0. \end{aligned}$$
If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=(p^2+p+1)K_{n_1}\) then the result follows from Theorem 2.14. If \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)=K_{n_1} \sqcup (p+1)K_{n_2}\) then, by Theorem 2.1, we have$$\begin{aligned}&M_1(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^2+(p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^2 \\&\quad =\frac{x(p-1)}{p^6}\left( p^6x^2-p^5x^2-p^4x^2+p^3x^2-2p^6x+p^6+2p^4x+p^5+p^4 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. +p^2x^2-2px^2+x^2-2p^3x+2p^2x \right) \end{aligned}$$
and$$\begin{aligned}&M_2(\mathcal {CCC}(G))=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}-1\right) ^3}{2}+(p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\frac{\left( \frac{x(p-1)}{p}-1\right) ^3}{2} \\&\quad = \frac{x(p-1)}{2p^8}\left( p^8x^3-2p^7x^3+2p^5x^3-p^4x^3-3p^8x^2+3p^7x^2+3p^6x^2 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -3p^5x^2+3p^8x-3p^6x+3p^5x-3p^4x-p^8-p^7-p^6+p^3x^3 \right. \\&\quad \quad \left. -3p^2x^3+3px^3-x^3-3p^4x^2+6p^3x^2-3p^2x^2 \right) . \end{aligned}$$
Also, \(|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}+(p+1)\frac{x(p-1)}{p}=\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}(p^2+p+1)\) and \(|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|=\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p^2}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) +(p+1)\left( {\begin{array}{c}\frac{x(p-1)}{p}\\ 2\end{array}}\right) =\frac{x(p-1)}{2p^4}(p^4x+px-p^2x-p^4-p^3-p^2-x)\). Therefore, \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} - \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}=\frac{f_3(p,x)}{p^4(p^2+p+1)(p^4x+px-p^2x-p^4-p^3-p^2-x)}:=\frac{f_3(p,x)}{g_3(p,x)}\), where$$\begin{aligned}&f_3(p,x)=p^8x^3-3p^7x^3+p^6x^3+5p^5x^3-5p^4x^3-p^3x^3+3p^2x^3-px^3-2p^8x^2 \\&\quad +6p^7x^2-4p^6x^2-4p^5x^2+6p^4x^2-2p^3x^2. \end{aligned}$$
On factorization, we have \(f_3(p,x)=px^2(p+1)(p-1)^4(p^2x-x-2p^2)\). For all prime \(p \ge 2\) and \(x \ge p^2\), we have \(x(p^2-1)>2p^2\). As such, \(f_3(p,x)> 0\) and \(g_3(p,x)=\frac{2p^{10}}{x^2(p-1)^2} \times |e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))| \times |v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|>0\). Therefore, \(\frac{f_3(p,x)}{g_3(p,x)}>0\).
                  
In all the cases, we have \(\frac{M_{2}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|e(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|} > \frac{M_{1}(\mathcal {CCC}(G))}{|v(\mathcal {CCC}(G))|}\). Hence, the result follows. \(\square \)
                
                  
Corollary 2.17
                  
Let G be a non-abelian group of order \(p^n\) such that \(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is non-abelian and \(|Z(G)|=p^{n-3}\), where p is a prime and \(n \ge 4\). Then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
                
                  
Proof
                  
Clearly \(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|=p^3\) and so the result follows from Theorem 2.16. \(\square \)
                
                  
Corollary 2.18
                  
If G is a non-abelian group of order \(p^4\) then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
                
                  
Proof
                  
We have \(|G|=p^4\), p is a prime, then |Z(G)| must be p or \(p^2\). If \(|Z(G)|=p^2\) then the result follows from Corollary 2.10. Otherwise, if \(|Z(G)|=p\) then the result follows from Corollary 2.17. \(\square \)
                

3 Concluding remarks
In this paper we have shown that \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture if
                

                  
G is isomorphic to \(D_{2m}, Q_{4m}, SD_{8m}, V_{8m}, U(n,m)\) and G(p, m, n).
                
                

                  
\(\frac{G}{Z(G)}\) is isomorphic to \(D_{2m}, \mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\) and Frobenious group of order pq and \(p^2q\) for any two primes p and q.
                
                

                  
\(|\frac{G}{Z(G)}|=p^3\) for any prime p.
                
              
Moreover, it was also seen that if G is any non-abelian group of order \(p^3\) or \(p^4\), where p is any prime, then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture. Using Theorems 2.8, 2.9 and the characterizations of 4-centralizer (“G is a 4-centralizer finite group if and only if \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong \mathbb {Z}_2 \times \mathbb {Z}_2\)” [3, Theorem 2]), 5-centralizer (“G is a 5-centralizer finite group if and only if \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong \mathbb {Z}_3 \times \mathbb {Z}_3\) or \(D_{2\times 3}\)” [3, Theorem 4]) finite groups we have the following result.
                
Theorems 3.1
                
If G is a 4-centralizer or 5-centralizer finite group then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
              
Using Theorem 2.9 and the characterization of \((p+2)\)-centralizer finite p-group (“If G is a finite p-group then it is \((p+2)\)-centralizer if and only if \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong \mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\)” [2, Lemma 2.7]) we have the following result.
                
Theorems 3.2
                
If G is a \((p+2)\)-centralizer finite p-group then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
              
If \(\{g_1, g_2, \dots , g_m\}\) is a set of pairwise non-commuting elements of G having maximal size then G is a 4-centralizer group if \(m = 3\) and a 5-centralizer group if \(m = 4\) (see [1, Lemma 2.4]). Therefore, if \(m = 3\) and 4 then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) graphs of such groups satisfy Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
Let p be the smallest prime divisor of |G|. Then the fact that “commuting probability of G, \(\Pr (G) = \frac{p^2 + p - 1}{p^3}\) if and only if \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong \mathbb {Z}_p \times \mathbb {Z}_p\)” [20, Theorem 3] along with Theorem 2.9 give the following result.
                
Theorems 3.3
                
If p is the smallest prime divisor of |G| and \(\Pr (G) = \frac{p^2 + p - 1}{p^3}\) then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
              
In addition, if \(\Pr (G) = \frac{5}{14}, \frac{2}{5}, \frac{11}{27}, \frac{1}{2}\) or \(\frac{5}{8}\) then \(\frac{G}{Z(G)} \cong D_{2\times 7}, D_{2\times 5}, D_{2\times 4}, D_{2\times 3}\) or \(\mathbb {Z}_2 \times \mathbb {Z}_2\) (see [27, pp. 246] and [22, pp. 451]). Therefore, the following result follows in view of Theorems 2.8 and 2.9.
                
Theorems 3.4
                
If \(\Pr (G) = \frac{5}{14}, \frac{2}{5}, \frac{11}{27}, \frac{1}{2}\) or \(\frac{5}{8}\) then \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) satisfies Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.
              
It is worth noting that in this work we have not found any group G such that \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) does not satisfy Hansen-Vukičević conjecture. We conclude this paper with the following question.
                
Question 3.5
                
Is there any finite group G such that \(\mathcal {CCC}(G)\) does not satisfy Hansen-Vukičević conjecture?
