Abstract
    

Let mm be a positive integer. Brualdi and Hoffman proposed the problem to determine the (connected) graphs with maximum spectral radius in a given graph class and they posed a conjecture for the class of graphs with given size mm.
After partial results due to
Friedland and Stanley,
Rowlinson completely confirmed the conjecture.
The distance spectral radius of a connected graph is the largest eigenvalue of its distance matrix.
We investigate the problem to determine the connected graphs with minimum distance spectral radius in the class of graphs with size mm.
Given mm, there is exactly one positive integer nn such that (n−12)<m≤(n2){n-1\choose 2}<m\leq{n\choose 2}.
We establish some structural properties of the extremal graphs for all mm and solve the problem for
(n−12)+max⁡{n−62,1}≤m≤(n2){n-1\choose 2}+\max\{\frac{n-6}{2},1\}\leq m\leq{n\choose 2}. We give a conjecture for the remaining case.
To prove the main results, we also determine the the complements of forests of fixed order with large and small distance spectral radius.


MSC: 05C12, 05C50, 15A48

Key words: distance spectral radius, complement of a forest, graph size
  




1 Introduction



Throughout this paper, we consider simple graphs. Let GG be a connected graph with vertex
set V⁡(G)V(G) and edge set E⁡(G)E(G). Denote m⁡(G)m(G) the size of GG.
The distance between vertices u,v∈V⁡(G)u,v\in V(G),
denoted by dG​(u,v)d_{G}(u,v), is the length of a shortest path between them.
The distance matrix of GG, denoted by D⁡(G)D(G), is the matrix
D⁡(G)=(dG​(u,v))u,v∈V⁡(G)D(G)=(d_{G}(u,v))_{u,v\in V(G)}.
Since D⁡(G)D(G) is real and
symmetric, its eigenvalues are all real.
The distance spectral radius of GG, denoted by ρ⁡(G)\rho(G), is the largest
eigenvalue of D⁡(G)D(G). For |V⁡(G)|≥2|V(G)|\geq 2, since D⁡(G)D(G) is
irreducible, we have by the Perron-Frobenius theorem that ρ⁡(G)\rho(G)
is simple and positive, and there is a unique positive unit eigenvector x⁡(G)x(G) of D⁡(G)D(G)
corresponding to ρ⁡(G)\rho(G), which we call the distance Perron
vector of GG.



The study of eigenvalues of the distance matrix of a connected graph dates back to the
classical work of Graham and Pollak [10], Graham and Lovász [9] and Edelberg et al. [7].
The distance spectral radius was used as a
molecular descriptor [3].
Ruzieh and Powers [17] and Stevanović and Ilić [19] showed that among the connected
graphs of order nn,
the complete graph KnK_{n} is the unique graph with
minimum distance spectral radius, while the path PnP_{n} is the unique
graph with maximum distance spectral radius, where the uniqueness of the path was established in [19]. Related to many problems and properties of graphs such as Laplacian spectral properties [14] and graph parameters based on distance [2],
the distance eigenvalues and especially of the distance spectral radius
received much attention, see the surveys [1, 12] (and the references therein) and the very recent [5]. We mention that such research was also extended to Laplacian and signless Laplacian versions [16],
hypergraphs [21] and other distances such as Steiner distance [6].



Let mm be a positive integer. There is exactly one positive integer nn such that (n−12)<m≤(n2){n-1\choose 2}<m\leq{n\choose 2}.
It is easily seen that 1+8​m2≤n<3+8​m+12\frac{1+\sqrt{8m}}{2}\leq n<\frac{3+\sqrt{8m+1}}{2}.
Thus n=⌈1+8​m2⌉n=\left\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{8m}}{2}\right\rceil.
After showing that the maximum adjacency spectral radius of a graph with (n2){n\choose 2} edges, n≥2n\geq 2, is n−1n-1, with equality precisely when the only nontrivial component of the graph is KnK_{n},
Brualdi and Hoffman conjectured that among all graphs with mm edges, (n−12)<m<(n2){n-1\choose 2}<m<{n\choose 2}, the maximal index is attained precisely when the only nontrivial component is the graph obtained from Kn−1K_{n-1} by adding one new vertex of degree m−(n−12)m-{n-1\choose 2}. Friedland [8] and Stanley [18]
gave some partial results on this conjecture, which was confirmed finally by
Rowlinson [15]. Let 𝔾⁡(m)\mathbb{G}(m) be the set of connected graphs with mm edges.
Evidently, 𝔾⁡(m)={Pm+1}\mathbb{G}(m)=\{P_{m+1}\} when m=1,2m=1,2. Thus, we assume in the following that m≥3m\geq 3. Then m=(n−12)+sm={n-1\choose 2}+s with 1≤s≤n−11\leq s\leq n-1. So the graph GmG_{m} obtained from Kn−1K_{n-1} by adding a new vertex of degree ss is the unique graph achieving the maximum adjacency spectral radius.



In this paper, we study the following problem.



Problem 1.1.


Determine the graphs in 𝔾⁡(m)\mathbb{G}(m) with maximum and minimum distance spectral radius.




It turns out that the maximum part is easy. However, the minimum is much more difficult. By existing results and experiments, the graphs with maximum (minimum, resp.) adjacency spectral radius happen to be the graphs with minimum (maximum, resp.) distance spectral radius over many classes of graphs, including trees and graphs with given parameters such as order, connectivity, clique number, etc. Our results show that this is not the case in the class 𝔾⁡(m)\mathbb{G}(m) for s<n−2s<n-2. The main results are listed below.
