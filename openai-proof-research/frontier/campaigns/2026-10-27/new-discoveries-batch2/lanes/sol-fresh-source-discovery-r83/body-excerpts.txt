25:     Abstract The commuting graph of a finite non-abelian group G is a graph whose vertex set
26: is the non-central elements of G and two distinct vertices are adjacent if they commute. The
27: complement of commuting graph is called non-commuting graph. In this paper, we compute
28: first and second Zagreb indices of commuting and non-commuting graphs of finite groups and
29: determine several classes of finite groups such that their commuting and non-commuting graphs
30: satisfy Hansen-Vukičević conjecture.

34: 1 Introduction
35: Let G be the set of all graphs. A topological index is a function T : G → R such that T (Γ1 ) =
36: T (Γ2 ) whenever the graphs Γ1 and Γ2 are isomorphic. By using different parameters of graphs
37: many topological indices have been defined since 1947. Wiener index is the first topological
38: index, introduced by Wiener [37], and it is a distance based index. Among the degree based
39: topological indices, the first two (known as Zagreb indices) were introduced by Gutman and
40: Trinajstić [19] in 1972. Initially, topological indices were used to describe several chemical
41: properties of molecules. In particular, Zagreb indices were used in examining the dependence of
42: total π -electron energy on molecular structure. As noted in [29], Zagreb indices are also used in
43: studying molecular complexity, chirality, ZE-isomerism and heterosystems etc. Zagreb indices
44: for chains of identical hexagonal cycles were computed in [25]. Later on, general mathematical
45: properties of various topological indices are also studied by many mathematicians. A survey on
46: mathematical properties of Zagreb indices can be found in [18]. Certain chromatic versions of
47: Zagreb indices are also considered in [17] recently.
48:     Computing formulas for Zagreb indices of non-commuting graph N C (G) of a finite group G
49: wereP obtained in [24]. However,
50:                              P their formulas are not closed because of the presence of terms
51: like x∈G\Z (G) CG (x) and xy∈ e(N C (G)) |CG (x)||CG (y )|, where CG (x) = {g ∈ G : xg = gx}
52: (the centralizer of x ∈ G) and e(N C (G)) is the set of edges of N C (G). Zagreb indices of
53: commuting graphs of groups are yet not explored.

4246: [24] to show this. Let GG be the group of order 6464 which is
4247: SmallGroup(64,182) in the SmallGroups library [10] in
4248: GAP [38]. A Schur cover of GG is a group HH of maximal
4249: order subject to having a subgroup Z≤H′∩Z⁡(H)Z\leq H^{\prime}\cap Z(H) such that
4250: H/Z≅GH/Z\cong G. The Schur multiplier of GG has order 22, so any Schur
4251: cover of GG has order 128128. Moreover, the Bogomolov multiplier (see
4252: [24] for context and other references) of GG is equal to the
4253: Schur multiplier, which implies that any Schur cover HH is
4254: commutation-preserving: that is, two elements a,b∈Ha,b\in H
4255: commute if and only if their projections Z​a,Z​b∈GZa,Zb\in G commute. This
4256: implies that the commuting graph of a Schur cover is obtained from the
4257: commuting graph of GG by replacing each vertex with a clique of size
4258: 22, with all edges between cliques corresponding to adjacent
4259: vertices. This procedure also describes the commuting graph of
4260: G×C2G\times C_{2}. On the other hand, it is easy to verify computationally
