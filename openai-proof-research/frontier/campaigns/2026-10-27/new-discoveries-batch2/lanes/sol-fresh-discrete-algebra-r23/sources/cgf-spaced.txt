 Abstract. 
    
 We give a sufficient combinatorial condition for the non-negativity of the coefficients of polynomial quotients of products of  q q -integers, also known as  cyclotomic generating functions (CGFs) . This slightly extends work by Iano-Fletcher, Pizzato, Sano and Tasin, who studied this condition as a criterion for quasismoothness of complete intersections in weighted projective spaces. As a consequence, we solve a problem by Billey and Swanson, prove most cases of an unpublished conjecture by Stanton and most cases of two conjectures by Gatzweiler and Krattenthaler. We also study sufficient conditions given by structural properties of the division lattice.

 1.  Introduction 

 
 Let  f ⁡ ( q ) f(q)  be a non-zero polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients in the variable  q q . Then, the polynomial  f ⁡ ( q ) f(q)  is called a  cyclotomic generating function (CGF)  if there exist  α , β ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 \alpha,\beta\in\ZZ_{\geq 0}  and  a 1 , … , a n , b 1 , … , b n ∈ ℤ + a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}  such that 
 

 
 (1) 
 
 f ⁡ ( q ) = α ​ q β ​ ∏ i = 1 n [ a i ] q [ b i ] q = α ​ q β ​ ∏ i = 1 n 1 − q a i 1 − q b i , f(q)=\alpha q^{\beta}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{[a_{i}]_{q}}{[b_{i}]_{q}}=\alpha q^{\beta}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{a_{i}}}{1-q^{b_{i}}}, 
 
 
 where  [ n ] q = ( 1 − q n ) / ( 1 − q ) [n]_{q}=(1-q^{n})/(1-q)  is a  q q -integer.
CGFs are of great significance in combinatorics as they are the generating functions of many combinatorial objects with respect to statistics on these objects. A prototypical example of such generating functions is the  q q -binomial coefficient, being the generating function of integer partitions inside a  k × ( n − k ) k\times(n-k)  frame with respect to the area statistic: 
 

 
 (2) 
 
 ∑ λ ⊂ k × ( n − l ) q | λ | = ( n k ) q = ∏ i = 1 n ( 1 − q i ) ∏ i = 1 k ( 1 − q l ) ​ ∏ i = 1 n − k ( 1 − q i ) . \sum_{\lambda\subset k\times(n-l)}q^{|\lambda|}=\binom{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1-q^{l})\prod_{i=1}^{n-k}(1-q^{i})}. 
 
 
 For further reading, the paper  [ 1 ]  offers an excellent survey on CGFs from different perspectives and a long (but not exhaustive) list of sources of CGFs throughout combinatorics. 
 
 
 Considering the expression in ( 2 ), it is not  a priori  clear that the right-hand side is a polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients.
In general, given  a 1 , … , a n , b 1 , … , b n ∈ ℤ + a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}  and assuming that the quotient 
 

 
 (3) 
 
 ∏ i = 1 n 1 − q a i ∏ i = 1 n 1 − q b i . \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}{1-q^{a_{i}}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}{1-q^{b_{i}}}}. 
 
 
 simplifies into a polynomial in  q q , it is not easy to determine whether it has non-negative integer coefficients. Nevertheless, there are many known rational functions in the form of ( 3 ) that are polynomials with non-negative integer coefficients, as all CGFs are essentially of this form up to a constant and a power of  q q . 
 
 
 The polynomiality and non-negativity of fractions of the form ( 3 ) have been extensively studied in the literature. For instance, in the unpublished note  [ 12 ] , Stanton posed the following conjecture related to  fake Gaussian sequences . 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1 . 
 
 [ 12 , Conjecture 1]  
Let  𝐚 = ( a 1 , … , a n ) \mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})  be a sequence of non-negative integers.
If 
 

 
 (4) 
 
 ∏ i = 1 n ( 1 − q m + i ) a i ∏ i = 1 n ( 1 − q i ) a i . \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{m+i})^{a_{i}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i})^{a_{i}}}. 
 
 
 is a polynomial for all non-negative integers  m m  and  𝐚 \mathbf{a}  is symmetric, then ( 4 ) has non-negative integer coefficients for all  m m . 
 
 
 
 More recently, Gatzweiler and Krattenthaler  [ 3 ]  study the following conjecture. 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.2 . 
 
 [ 3 , Conjecture 1]  
Let  n , k , l ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 n,k,l\in\ZZ_{\geq 0} . If 
 

 
 (5) 
 
 ( n k ) q ( n l ) q = [ l ] q ! ​ [ n − l ] q !  is a polynomial, then it has non-negative integer coefficients. 
 
 
 
 Note that in Conjecture  1.2  it suffices to consider the case where  1 ≤ l < k ≤ n / 2 1\leq l<k\leq n/2 . In this case, ( 5 ) is equal to 
 

 
 (6) 
 
 ∏ i = n − k + 1 n − l ( 1 − q i ) ∏ i = l + 1 k ( 1 − q i ) . \frac{\prod_{i=n-k+1}^{n-l}(1-q^{i})}{\prod_{i=l+1}^{k}(1-q^{i})}. 
 
 
 In  [ 3 ] , the authors prove Conjecture  1.2  for the cases in which  l = k , k − 1 , k − 2 , k − 3 l=k,k-1,k-2,k-3  and  l = 0 , 1 , 2 l=0,1,2  by direct arguments involving cyclotomic polynomials. 
 
 
 Furthermore, Gatzweiler and Krattenthaler claim that, in Conjecture  1.1 , the conditions regarding the polynomiality of  m m  and the symmetry of  𝐚 \mathbf{a}  can be dropped. In other words: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.3 . 
 
 [ 3 , Conjecture 4]  
Let  m , n m,n  be positive integers with  m ≥ n m\geq n , and let  𝐚 \mathbf{a}  be a sequence of non-negative integers.
If ( 4 ) is a polynomial in  q q , then it has non-negative coefficients. 
 
 
 
 In this article, we study sufficient conditions for rational functions of the form ( 3 ) to have non-negative integer coefficients in the cases where these functions are polynomials. We classify our criteria into two categories: algebro-geometric and lattice-theoretic. The algebro-geometric point of view is particularly effective; we slightly extend ideas by  [ 4 ]  and  [ 6 ]  to give a necessary and sufficient combinatorial condition for the existence of  empty  complete intersections in weighted projective spaces. This condition involves a family of Frobenius coin problems. It can be stated as follows:

 In  [ 1 ] , the authors define the  HSOP monoid  (HSOP stands for  homogeneous system of parameters ) as the set of CGFs that are Hilbert series of the quotient of  ℂ ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] \mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]  by a regular sequence of homogeneous elements  f 1 , … , f n f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}  of maximal length. They note that this set is a monoid. They propose the following:

 From the lattice-theoretic point of view, we give sufficient criteria involving lower order ideals of the division lattice of positive integers (Proposition  4.1 ). This is because certain CGFs are non-negative for  analytic  reasons; we mention relations to Poincaré multipliers and normal distributions. 
 
 
 
 2.  Preliminaries 

 
 In this section we summarise preliminaries that will be used in the rest of the paper. We recall basic facts about cyclotomic polynomials, CGFs, the Frobenius coin problem and regular sequences. We also introduce notation for multisets. 
 
 
 The symbols  ℤ + \mathbb{Z}_{+}  and  ℤ ≥ 0 \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}  will always denote the positive and non-negative integers, respectively. 
 
 
 2.1.  Multisets 

 
 We use square brackets  [ [  and  ] ]  for multisets (e.g.,  [ 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 ] [1,1,2,3] ). 
 
 
 For a multiset  S S , we write  set ⁡ ( S ) \toset(S)  for its underlying set, so  set ⁡ ( [ 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 ] ) = { 1 , 2 , 3 } \toset([1,1,2,3])=\{1,2,3\} . We also write  # ​ S \#S  for its  multiset cardinality , so  # ⁡ [ 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 ] = 4 \#[1,1,2,3]=4 . 
 
 
 For an indexed multiset  S = [ s 1 , … , s n ] S=[s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}]  and a subset of indices  I ⊆ { 1 , … , n } I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\} , we define  S I ≔ [ s i : i ∈ I ] S_{I}\coloneq[s_{i}:i\in I] . 
 
 
 For two multisets  S 1 S_{1}  and  S 2 S_{2} , we write  S 1 ⊎ S 2 S_{1}\uplus S_{2}  or  ⨄ i = 1 2 S i \biguplus_{i=1}^{2}S_{i}  for  multiset  or  additive union  (so  [ 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 ] = [ 1 , 2 ] ⊎ [ 1 , 3 ] [1,1,2,3]=[1,2]\uplus[1,3] ) and  S 1 ∖ S 2 S_{1}\setminus S_{2}  for  multiset difference  (so  [ 1 , 3 ] = [ 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 ] ∖ [ 1 , 2 , 4 ] [1,3]=[1,1,2,3]\setminus[1,2,4] ). 
 
 
 Finally, for a multiset  S S  and a set  S ′ S^{\prime} , we define  S ∩ ¯ S ′ ≔ [ s ∈ S : s ∈ S ′ ] S~\overline{\cap}~S^{\prime}\coloneq[s\in S:s\in S^{\prime}] . For example,  [ 1 , 1 , 2 ] = [ 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 ] ​ ∩ ¯ ​ { 1 , 2 } [1,1,2]=[1,1,2,3]~\overline{\cap}~\{1,2\} .

 2.3.  Cyclotomic generating functions (CGFs)

 Theorem 2.2 .

Let  f ⁡ ( q ) f(q)  be a non-zero polynomial with non-negative integer coefficients in the variable  q q .
Then, the following are equivalent:

 f ⁡ ( q ) f(q)  is a CGF.

 (Rational form.)  f ⁡ ( q ) f(q)  is of the form ( 1 ). 
 
 3.

 2.4.  The Frobenius coin problem 

 
 Let  S = { s 1 , … , s n } ⊂ ℤ + S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{+}  with  s i < s i + 1 s_{i}<s_{i+1}  for all  i = 1 , … , n − 1 i=1,\ldots,n-1 . The  numerical semigroup  generated by  S S  is 
 

 
 
 ⟨ S ⟩ semigroup ≔ { c 1 ​ s 1 + c 2 ​ s 2 + ⋯ + c n ​ s n : c i ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 } . \langle S\rangle_{\text{semigroup}}\coloneq\{c_{1}s_{1}+c_{2}s_{2}+\cdots+c_{n}s_{n}:c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}. 
 
 
 
 
 Numerical semigroups are intimately related to the  Frobenius coin problem , which informally is about finding the monetary amounts that we can make with coins of given denominations. The classical formulation is actually to find the largest monetary amount that we  cannot  make with these coins, but we will use the following stronger formulation. 
 
 
 Problem 2.4   (Frobenius coin problem) . 
 
 Assume  gcd ⁡ ( S ) = 1 \gcd(S)=1 . Determine  ℤ ∩ ⟨ S ⟩ semigroup \mathbb{Z}\cap\langle S\rangle_{\text{semigroup}} . In other words, the problem is to find all integers that are representable as a linear combination of elements of  S S  with non-negative integer coefficients.

 3.  The algebro-geometric point of view 

 
 In this section we give a necessary and sufficient combinatorial condition for the existence of regular sequences on graded polynomial rings with given sequences of degrees. This condition is given in terms of a family of Frobenius coin problems. To do so, we rely heavily on ideas in  [ 4 , Theorem 8.7]  and  [ 6 , Proposition 3.1] , who first introduced this as a criterion for quasismoothness of  weighted complete intersections (WPIs) , which is the corresponding object in projective algebraic geometry. We use  [ 11 , Corollary 3.3]  to translate this into a sufficient condition for the non-negativity of cyclotomic generating functions (CGFs). As a consequence, we solve Problem  1.5  and prove most cases of Conjectures  1.1 ,  1.2  and  1.3 . 
 
 
 We work over  ℂ \mathbb{C} .  4.  The lattice-theoretic and analytic points of view 

 
 In this section we give more sufficient conditions for the non-negativity of cyclotomic generating functions (CGFs). This time, the conditions arise from structural properties of the  division lattice   ( ℤ ≥ 0 , ∣ ) (\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mid) . We will also see that such conditions cannot be necessary, since CGFs are often non-negative for  analytic  reasons. In particular, we will briefly discuss Poincaré multipliers and asymptotic normality. 
 
 
 By abuse of notation, we will sometimes denote  ( ℤ ≥ 0 , ∣ ) (\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\mid)  by  ℤ ≥ 0 \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} .
