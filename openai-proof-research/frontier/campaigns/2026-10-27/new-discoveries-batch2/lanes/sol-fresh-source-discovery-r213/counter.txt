                                               Abstract
                                         Counter machines are a particularly simple, Turing-complete model of computation. We study the




arXiv:2610.23495v2 [cs.FL] 19 Oct 2026
                                         expressive power of nondeterministic 2-counter machines which are symmetric, in that their set of
                                         update transitions is invariant under swapping of counters. Our model is an extension of symmetric
                                         2-vass, in that we allow modifying both counters within a single transition. As the main result
                                         we demonstrate a dramatic impact of symmetry: we show that symmetric 2-counter machines are
                                         not Turing-complete, by proving decidability of their reachability problem. We conjecture that the
                                         decidability extends to machines with 3 or more counters that are symmetric, meaning the set of
                                         update transitions is invariant under any permutation of counters. We leave this problem open
                                         for machines with 3 and more counters, while identifying a frontier of the impact of symmetry: we
                                         prove undecidability for 3-counter machines invariant under the cyclic permutation group of order 3.

     1     Introduction
    Counter machines (cms) are a particularly simple model of computation [23]. A counter
    machine has a number of registers, called counters, each register storing a natural number.
    Although the instruction set may vary, a common one consists of: counter increment, counter
    decrement, and counter zero-test. Many variants of the model have been introduced and
    studied, and both deterministic and nondeterministic machines are widely studied in the
    literature (often under diﬀerent names, for instance counter automata). Numerous syntactic
    restrictions of the model are also widely studied [12, 14, 13, 10] since, as shown already by
    Minsky in the early 1960s [22], in its full generality the model is Turing-complete: determ-
    inistic 2-counter machines can faithfully simulate Turing machines and have an undecidable
    halting problem.
        The purpose of this paper is to understand the impact of symmetry on the expressive
    power of cms. We investigate the nondeterministic model, where a machine may choose
    between diﬀerent instructions executable in the same state.

    Symmetric counter machines. Imposing a symmetry condition on the transitions of a
    counter machine is inherently sensitive to what can be expressed within a single instruction.
    As such, we speciﬁcally choose the set of machine instructions: counter updates that sim-
    ultaneously modify all counters by corresponding deltas, and counter zero-tests, which are
    executable only if a given counter has value zero. Every transition (q, i, q 0 ) of a machine
    changes its state from q to q 0 , and executes an attached instruction i. Executing a transition
    in a given conﬁguration constitutes a step of a machine. The model we investigate is thus
    essentially vass (vector addition systems with states) [13] extended with zero-tests, and is
    clearly equivalent to standard cms. In this paper we mostly focus on cms that are symmetric,
    by which we mean that the set of update transitions is invariant under all permutations σ of
    counters: for every update transition (q, i, q 0 ), the machine has also a transition (q, σ(i), q 0 ),
    where σ acts on an update instruction i by permuting the roles of the counters (see Figure 1
    for an example). To stress this point, we choose to impose the invariance only on update
    transitions, but not on zero-test transitions — imposing the invariance (symmetry) also on
    zero-tests is not needed for our main decidability proof.

    Contribution. The initial motivation of this study is the immense impact of symmetry on
    the complexity of the vass reachability problem: while the problem is Ackermann-complete
    in general [20, 5, 19], the complexity drops to only PSpace-complete for symmetric vass
    [15]. As our main result, we conﬁrm the immense drop in case of 2-cms (in this case,
    symmetry amounts to invariance under all swaps of counters). We prove decidability of the
    conﬁguration-to-conﬁguration reachability problem, where one asks if a given machine has
    a sequence of steps starting in a given source conﬁguration and ending in a given target one:
K. Jaros, Ł. Kamiński, M. Kądziołka, S. Lasota                                                    3


I Theorem 1. The reachability problem is decidable for symmetric 2-counter machines.

    Symmetric two-counter machines are thus not Turing-complete. We ﬁnd this result quite
surprising, as it applies to a model with restricted update instructions but unrestricted zero-
test instructions. Interestingly, the converse restriction, namely imposing symmetry on
zero-test transitions but not on update transitions, remains undecidable.
    It is natural to consider invariance under subgroups of permutations: for every subgroup
G ≤ Sd one can consider the class of G-counter machines (G-cms), namely d-counter ma-
chines invariant under all permutations from G. On one end, when G = Sd , the general
deﬁnition instantiates to symmetric d-cms. On the opposite end, when G is the trivial
group one gets classical counter machines. Between the two extremes there is a landscape
of models, whose expressive power is related according to the permutation subgroup rela-
tion: whenever G ≤ H, every H-cm is automatically a G-cm. For instance, every S3 -cm is
automatically a Z3 -cm, for the cyclic permutation group Z3 ≤ S3 . As an additional result,
we identify a bound on the impact of symmetry, already for 3-counter machines, namely we
prove undecidability for Z3 -counter machines (even under an additional assumption that
zero-tests are symmetric):

I Theorem 2. The reachability problem is undecidable for Zd -counter machines, for all
d ≥ 3.

Both the above results hold in the hardest case: decidability with unrestricted zero-tests,
and undecidability with symmetric ones. This is unsurprising, as in Section 8 we show that
assuming symmetry of zero-tests does not weaken the model.

Open questions. A fundamental question is whether Theorem 1 extends to larger numbers
of counters. In particular, is the reachability problem still decidable for symmetric 3-counter
machines, whose update transitions are invariant under all 6 permutations of the 3 counters?
Our decidability proof does not extend to 3 counters, as the argument relies on a number
of low-dimensional properties of reachable sets and reachability relations. We thus leave
this intriguing question for further research, conjecturing decidability for any number of
counters:

I Conjecture 3. The reachability problem is decidable for symmetric counter machines.

    We focus on decidability, thus leaving open the exact complexity of the problem for
symmetric 2-cms. While we obtain an elementary upper bound for our decision procedure,
the only lower bound is PSpace-hardness inherited from symmetric 2-vass [15]. It is possible
that the reachability problem is PSpace-complete in symmetric d-cms for every d ≥ 2.
    Symmetric cms where the only update instructions are increments or decrements of a
single counter, constitute a syntactic fragment of our setting. It remains to be established
the expressive power of the fragment, and the complexity of its reachability problem.

    Related research. Identifying decidable restrictions of cms is a very active line of research
    since the 1960s, for instance 1-counter, reversal-bounded, or restricted 2-counter machines
    [12, 14, 10]. A prominent example is cms without zero-tests, that is vector addition systems
    with states (vass), shown to have decidable reachability in the early 1980s [21, 17, 18]. The
    decidability extends to cms with only one zero-tested counter [3], or with nested zero-tests
    [27, 11]. On the other hand, most natural restrictions preserve Turing-completeness, as long
    as no constraint is imposed on zero-tests. For instance, considering deterministic cms, the
    reachability problem is still undecidable in the subclass of reversible cms, where the step
    relation is reverse deterministic, and hence every step may be uniquely undone [24].
        In the special case of 1 counter the symmetry requirement is void, and the reachability
    problem is NP-complete if transition updates are encoded in binary, and NL-complete in
    unary encoding [12]. The known complexity bounds separate symmetric 2-cms from 1-cms,
    the former being PSpace-hard while the latter NP-complete, thus proving that symmetric
    2-cms are not (easily) simulated by 1-cms.
        Restricting the use of zero-tests in 2-cms often leads not only to decidability, but even
    to PSpace-completeness. Assuming binary encoding, the PSpace-completeness has been
    obtained for 2-vass [2, 1] (assuming unary encoding, we have NL-completeness [7]), but also
    for the further restriction to symmetric 2-vass [15], and for a relaxation of 2-vass to 2-cms
    with just one zero-tested counter [9]. In all these cases the reachability relation is eﬀectively
    semilinear, which is the key for obtaining a PSpace upper bound, but which fails already
    for 3-vass [13]. We do not know the exact complexity of symmetric 2-cms. Neither do we
    know if the reachability relation of a symmetric 2-cm is semilinear, but we believe it is so.
        Contrary to the above-mentioned restrictions of zero-tests, our model leaves the zero-
    tests unrestricted, which rarely results in a decidable model — the primary example being
    a result of Dudenhefner [6], showing that for certain choice of instruction set, the reversible
    2-cms become decidable, in contrast with [24].


     2      Symmetric counter machines
    Let d be a positive integer. A d-counter machine (d-cm in short) M is a ﬁnite-state auto-
    maton whose transitions may perform two kinds of instructions, namely update and zero-test
    instructions, on d counters, namely nonnegative integer values stored as part of the conﬁgur-
    ation. Formally, we deﬁne such a machine as M = (Q, T ), where Q is a ﬁnite set of control
    states, T ⊆ﬁn Q × I × Q is a ﬁnite set of transitions of M, and the set I of instructions is
    given by:

         I = { update(w) | w ∈ Zd } ∪ { zero-test(i) | i ∈ {1 . . . d} }.

    Execution of update(w) amounts to adding the vector w to the counters, and execution
    of zero-test(i) amounts to checking that counter i has value 0. Formally, we deﬁne the
    semantics of M by considering its conﬁgurations (q, v) ∈ Q × Nd , written q(v), and the step
    relation between conﬁgurations determined by transitions of M: q(v) −→ q 0 (v0 ) if
        v0 = v + w, for some (q, update(w), q 0 ) ∈ T , or
        v0 = v and v[i] = 0, for some (q, zero-test(i), q 0 ) ∈ T ,
    where by v[i] we denote the i-th coordinate of the vector v.
K. Jaros, Ł. Kamiński, M. Kądziołka, S. Lasota                                                       5


    We use the term d-vass (vector addition system with states) for a d-cm without zero-
test instructions. The underlying vass of M is obtained by removing from M all zero-test
transitions.
    The symmetric group Sd acts naturally on vectors in Zd and hence also on update trans-
itions: for σ ∈ Sd and t = (q, update(w), q 0 ) ∈ T , we deﬁne σ(t) = (q, update(σ(w)), q 0 ),
where σ(w)[σ(i)] = w[i] for every i ∈ {1 . . . d}. A d-cm M is symmetric if its set of update
transitions is invariant under the natural action: for every σ ∈ Sd and (q, update(w), q 0 ) ∈
T , we have (q, update(σ(w)), q 0 ) ∈ T . Importantly, symmetry is imposed on update trans-
itions, but not on zero-test transitions of M. In the sequel we mostly focus on symmetric
2-cms, namely 2-cms whose set of update transitions is invariant under the swap of coun-
ters.
I Remark 4. Likewise one deﬁnes G-cms, counter machines whose set of update transitions
is invariant under the action of a permutation subgroup G ≤ Sd . Note that this notion
is contravariant: when G is a permutation subgroup of H, every H-cm is automatically a
G-cm, and not vice versa. We investigate this more general model only in Sections 7 and 8.
We deﬁne the reachability relation (between conﬁgurations) as the reﬂexive-transitive closure
−→∗ of −→. The reachability problem asks, given a cm M and two of its conﬁgurations, a
source s ∈ Q × Nd and a target t ∈ Q × Nd , whether s −→∗ t (in which case we say that t
is reachable from s). A reachability certiﬁcate, namely any sequence ρ of conﬁgurations

     q0 (v0 ) −→ q1 (v1 ) −→ · · · −→ qn (vn )

with s = q0 (v0 ) and t = qn (vn ), related by the step relation, is said to be a run from s to t.

Variants. Two natural restrictions of the model are obtained by requiring that: (1) the set
of zero-test transitions is also symmetric, that is invariant under permutations of counters;
(2) all update transitions (q, update(w), q 0 ) are single-counter, that is w is nonzero on at
most one coordinate. As we discuss later in Section 8, the ﬁrst restriction is equally powerful
as the unrestricted model, and imposing both restrictions makes the model essentially degen-
erate to 1-vass, once the source and target vectors are zero. We do not know consequences
of imposing solely the second restriction.

Decomposing a run into segments. For convenience, we perform on M the following
notational preprocessing: we replace every zero-test transition t = (q, zero-test(i), q 0 ) ∈ T
by a sequence of two transitions,

     (q, zero-test(i), qt )    (qt , update(0, 0), q 0 ),

where qt is a new state, unique to the transition t. Notice that reachability in the new
symmetric 2-cm thus obtained is equivalent to the original one, and that it has special states

    qt which can only be entered if the counter zero-tested by transition t is zero; we call them
    zero-testing states. The zero-testing states have the property that they “remember” which
    counter is zero when the control of the machine is at them, and that zero-test transitions
    are exactly those entering a zero-testing state. A fragment (inﬁx) of a run between two
    consecutive zero-testing states consists of only update transitions, except for the very last
    transition which is a zero-test. Such a fragment, called segment in the sequel, may be
    considered as a 2-vass run that ends with one counter 0, and which counter is 0 is determined
    by the ending, zero-testing state. Formally, we will consider a segment to be a run in the
    underlying symmetric 2-vass V, leaving the ﬁnal zero-test implicit in the fact that the ﬁnal
    conﬁguration of a segment has the relevant counter be zero.

    Saturated sets. It is folklore that semilinear sets X ⊆ Q × N are exactly the ultimately
    periodic sets, i.e. those satisfying q(a) ∈ X ⇐⇒ q(a + N ) ∈ X for some N > 0, M ≥ 0, all
    q ∈ Q and all suﬃciently large a ≥ M . We call N and M a period and threshold of X.
        Given N > 0, we deﬁne N -saturated subsets X ⊆ Q × N as those where for all (q, a) ∈ X,
    also (q, a + N ) ∈ X. N -saturated sets are ultimately periodic, and hence semilinear. An
K. Jaros, Ł. Kamiński, M. Kądziołka, S. Lasota                                                    9


N -saturated set X is representable by a function min(X) : Q × {0 . . . N − 1} → N ∪ {∞}
that maps a state q ∈ Q and remainder r < N to the least number n congruent to r such
that (q, n) ∈ X,

   min(X)(q, r) = min { n | (q, n) ∈ X, n ≡ r mod N },

and to ∞ if there is no such number n. We call N a period of X, and the numbers n < ∞
appearing in the image of min(X) its bases. For instance, in consequence of (6) and (7), the

     problem of general 2-cms, which is undecidable [23]. Our reduction readily generalizes to a
     reduction from general (n − 1)-cms to Zn -cms, showing that reachability is undecidable in
     Zn -cms, for n ≥ 3. For concreteness, however, we focus on the case of n = 3.

Proof of Theorem 2. Recall that a Z3 -cm is a 3-cm such that for any transition labeled
by update(x, y, z), the transitions update(y, z, x) and update(z, x, y) are also present
between the same pair of states. Note however, that the Z3 -cm we construct here also
satisﬁes a stronger condition: the zero-test transitions are symmetric as well, eﬀectively
testing that an unspeciﬁed counter is zero. We write this as zero-test(∗).
    For any 2-cm M = (Q, T ), we shall construct a Z3 -cm M0 = (Q0 , T 0 ) that simulates it,
where Q0 is a copy of Q with auxiliary states adjoined.
    For a conﬁguration q(a, b) of M, the corresponding conﬁguration of M0 shall have the
form q(3a+2, 3b+2, 0), with two main counters keeping track of the values of the counters of
M, and one auxiliary counter, which is always zero outside of auxiliary states. Thanks to the
presence of the auxiliary counter, we will be able to “anchor” each transition as it executes,
in such a way that any run making use of an unwanted cyclic rotation of a transition will
get rapidly cut short by a failing zero-test.
    To simulate a transition of the form (q, update(x, y), q 0 ) ∈ T , we include in T 0 the

contains only one zero, executing any cyclic rotation will disturb the auxiliary counter,
making it non-zero. Moreover, because the main counters have a remainder of 2 mod 3
before the transition, they can only have a remainder of 1 or 2 after any cyclic rotation
executes. In particular, none of the main counters may become zero. Hence, should any
unwanted cyclic rotation of the ﬁrst update instruction execute, the zero-test that follows
will fail.

increase the auxiliary counter to 1, and neither of the main counters may decrease to 0, as
the vector is non-negative on all coordinates.

      8        Variants
     We now discuss the consequences of imposing the following restrictions on symmetric cms:
       the set of zero-test transitions is also symmetric, that is invariant under permutations
       of counters;
       all update transitions (q, update(w), q 0 ) are single-counter, that is w is nonzero on at
       most one coordinate.

     Symmetric zero-tests. For simplicity, let’s focus on 2-cms ﬁrst. Symmetric zero-test
     transitions, eﬀectively testing that an unspeciﬁed counter is zero, written zero-test(∗), can
     surprisingly simulate unrestricted ones. To this aim, a conﬁguration q(v1 , v2 ) of a symmetric
     2-cm would be simulated by q(2v1 , 2v2 +1), thus doubling the counter values and distinguish-
     ing them by parity. Consequently, a transition (q, update(w1 , w2 ), q 0 ) is simulated a trans-
     ition (q, update(2w1 , 2w2 ), q 0 ). Therefore the update transitions, even symmetric ones,
     preserve parity of both counters. Due to parity distinction, a transition (q, zero-test(∗), q 0 )
     checking if an unspeciﬁed counter is 0, in fact checks if counter 1 is 0. On the other hand,
     simulation of transition (q, zero-test(2), q 0 ) is done in three steps, using two auxiliary inter-
     mediate states pt , p0t :

          (q, update(1, −1), pt )            (pt , zero-test(∗), p0t )       (p0t , update(−1, 1), q 0 ).   (18)

     Observe that the ﬁrst and the last transition swap parity of counters, and likewise do their
     swapped versions. Therefore a successful zero-test(∗) is only possible on counter 2, which
     rules out possibility of use of swapped versions of the ﬁrst and last transition.
        The above considerations generalize to G-cms, for an arbitrary number d > 2 of counters,
     and arbitrary permutation group G ≤ Sd . To this aim we distinguish between the counters
     by remainders modulo d, simulate a conﬁguration q(a1 , . . . , ad ) by

          q(da1 , da2 + 1, . . . , dad + d − 1),

     and simulate a transition (q, update(v), q 0 ) by (q, update(dv), q 0 ). Furthermore, to cor-
     rectly simulate a zero-test of counter j + 1 > 1, say a transition (q, zero-test(j + 1), q 0 ), we
     replace the vectors (1, −1) and (−1, 1) in the ﬁrst and last transition in (18) by

          (j, . . . , j, −j, j, . . . , j)   and       (−j, . . . , −j, j, −j, . . . , −j),

     respectively, where the unique value of negative (left) or positive (right) sign appears on the
     (j + 1)th coordinate.

Symmetric zero-tests and single-counter updates. We do not know how much weaker
is the fragment with only single-counter update transitions (but unrestricted zero-test
transitions). However, imposing both restrictions results in a degeneracy, namely the model
of d-cms, d ≥ 2, is simulated by (and equivalent to) 1-vass, at least as long as zero source and
target vectors are considered. Indeed, whenever a symmetric cm has a run q(0, . . . , 0) −→∗
q 0 (0, . . . , 0), it also has a simple run, in which all counter updates are performed on counter
1, say, while all zero-tests are (trivially) performed on counter 2, say. The zero-tests are
thus void, and simple runs are essentially runs of a 1-vass.

     binary representation), narrowing the apparent complexity gap is left as future research.
