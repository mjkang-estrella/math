Abstract


We establish an equivalence between the existence of Costas polynomials and the existence of a special kind of orthomorphism such that their compositions are also orthomorphisms. Computations are easier over these orthomorphisms. We provide a lower bound for the number of Costas polynomials and derive some of their properties. We show that Costas polynomials, by virtue of being multiplicative analogs of planar polynomials, can also be used to construct complete families of mutually orthogonal Latin squares.





Keywords:
Costas polynomial; permutation polynomial; Latin square; orthomorphism




MSC 2020: 11T06; 12E20; 05B15




1 Introduction




Let pp be a prime, and q=pnq=p^{n} where nn is a positive integer. Denote by 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q} the finite field of order qq. Every mapping θ\theta on 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q} induces a polynomial f​(x)∈𝔽q​[x]f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x] of the degree less than qq such that θ⁡(s)=f⁡(s)\theta(s)=f(s) for all s∈𝔽q​[x]s\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]. If this mapping is a permutation of 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q}, then we say that f⁡(x)f(x) is a permutation polynomial (PP). Polynomials of the form L⁡(x)=∑i=0n−1ai​xpiL(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}x^{p^{i}} are called linearized permutation polynomials since these are linear operators of 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q} over 𝔽p\mathbb{F}_{p} (see [6]).




A Costas array of order nn is a n×nn\times n permutation array (with exactly one dot in every row and column and blanks elsewhere) such that every vector connecting two dots is distinct. The Costas property ensures that the array has ideal auto-correlation, which makes Costas arrays highly desirable for use in RADAR and SONAR communications.
A special case is the circular Costas sequence, which motivated the definition of Costas polynomials. For more information, see [2, 4, 5, 8, 10].



Definition 1.1.



Suppose f∈𝔽q​[x]f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x] is such that f⁡(0)=0f(0)=0 and f⁡(d​x)−f⁡(x)f(dx)-f(x) is a permutation polynomial for all d∈𝔽qd\in\mathbb{F}_{q}, d≠1d\neq 1. Then ff is called a Costas polynomial.





Without lost of generality we can assume f⁡(0)=0f(0)=0.
According to an existing conjecture (see [5]) the only circular Costas sequence is exponential Welch, or, equivalently, the only Costas polynomial over the prime field is of the form xsx^{s}, with gcd⁡(s,q−1)=1\gcd(s,q-1)=1.
Known classes of Costas polynomials include f⁡(x)=xsf(x)=x^{s} where gcd⁡(q−1,s)=1\gcd(q-1,s)=1 for any field. In the case where 𝔽qm\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} is an extension field and L⁡(x)=∑i=0nai​xqiL(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{q^{i}} is a linearized permutation polynomial of 𝔽qm\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} over 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q}, then L⁡(xs)L(x^{s}) is also a Costas polynomial for all ss such that gcd⁡(s,qm−1)=1\gcd(s,q^{m}-1)=1. It is conjectured in [8] that these are the only Costas polynomials.




A Latin square of order nn on a set SS, where |S|=n|S|=n, is a table (matrix) of dimension n×nn\times n with elements from SS such that in each row and each column the elements are distinct. Usually SS is a finite group with nn elements and nn rows, and columns are labeled by elements of the group. Latin squares are widely used to make schedules, in design theory, etc. (see [3]) . The element in the ii-th row and the jj-th column of the Latin square ℒ\mathcal{L} is denoted by ℒi,j\mathcal{L}_{i,j}. Two Latin squares are said to be equivalent if the second Latin square can be obtained from the first by permuting the rows, columns, and elements. Two Latin squares ℒ\mathcal{L} and 𝒦\mathcal{K} of the same order nn are said to be mutually orthogonal if the ordered pairs (ℒi,j,𝒦i,j)(\mathcal{L}_{i,j},\mathcal{K}_{i,j}) are distinct for all 1≤i,j≤n1\leq i,j\leq n. The maximum number of pairwise mutually orthogonal Latin squares of order nn is n−1n-1, and they can be constructed over finite fields of order n=qn=q by ℒi,ja=i+a​j\mathcal{L}^{a}_{i,j}=i+aj where i,j∈𝔽qi,j\in\mathbb{F}_{q}, a∈𝔽q∗a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}. Let f:G→Gf\colon G\rightarrow G. By Mann ([7]), the Latin squares ℒi,j=i+j\mathcal{L}_{i,j}=i+j and 𝒦i.j=i+f⁡(j)\mathcal{K}_{i.j}=i+f(j), i,j∈Gi,j\in G are mutually orthogonal if both f⁡(x)f(x) and f⁡(x)−xf(x)-x are permutations of the group GG.



Definition 1.2.



Let GG be a finite group, and θ:G→G\theta\colon G\rightarrow G. We say that θ\theta is an orthomorpism if both θ⁡(x)\theta(x) and θ⁡(x)−x\theta(x)-x are permutations on GG.





We say that f⁡(x)f(x) is an orthomorphism in a finite field 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q} if it is an orthomorphism in its additive group (𝔽q,+)(\mathbb{F}_{q},+).

possible (q−2)​(q−1)(q-2)(q-1) checks would be performed for each permutation.




The first optimization is to compare subarrays as mentioned above.




The second optimization is more inclined towards C++. We use bitwise ’or’ and bitwise shift operators for checking the injectivity part, and to keep information on whether the permutation so far is injective (for fixed jj), one machine word (32 bits on most modern computers) is enough to keep the information about the (partial) set of image values – instead of qq bytes or qq words.

5 Construction of Latin Squares




A planar polynomial f​(x)∈𝔽q​[x]f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x] is a polynomial such that f⁡(x+a)−f⁡(x)f(x+a)-f(x) is a permutation polynomial for every a∈𝔽q∗a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}. Planar polynomials cannot be bijective. It is known that if f⁡(x)f(x) is planar, then the family of Latin squares defined by
ℒi​ja=i+f⁡(j+a)−f⁡(j)\mathcal{L}^{a}_{ij}=i+f(j+a)-f(j) forms a complete family of pairwise mutually orthogonal Latin squares (see [1], Theorem 5.3).
Costas polynomials are the multiplicative analogue of planar polynomials.



Theorem 5.1.



Let f​(x)∈𝔽q​[x]f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x] be a Costas polynomial. Then the family of Latin squares defined by




ℒi,jd=i+f⁡(d​j)−f⁡(j),d≠1\mathcal{L}^{d}_{i,j}=i+f(dj)-f(j),\quad d\neq 1



is a complete
family of pairwise mutually orthogonal Latin squares.




Proof.



Note first that there are q−1q-1 of such Latin squares. We compare ordered pairs of elements in the Latin squares ℒd\mathcal{L}^{d} and ℒs\mathcal{L}^{s} where d≠s,d,s≠1d\neq s,d,s\neq 1. Assume




(i+f⁡(d​j)−f⁡(j),i+f⁡(s​j)−f⁡(j))=(k+f⁡(d​t)−f⁡(t),k+f⁡(s​t)−f⁡(t)),i,j,k,t∈𝔽q.(i+f(dj)-f(j),i+f(sj)-f(j))=(k+f(dt)-f(t),k+f(st)-f(t)),i,j,k,t\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.



Then subtracting the equalities




i+f⁡(d​j)−f⁡(j)=k+f⁡(d​t)−f⁡(t)i+f(dj)-f(j)=k+f(dt)-f(t)



and

we obtain

∎

Furthermore, on the multiplicative group 𝔽q∗\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}, the orthomorphism f⁡(d​f−1​(x))=ℒx,df(df^{-1}(x))=\mathcal{L}_{x,d}, where f⁡(x)f(x) is a Costas polynomial, defines a Latin square (d=yd=y) where rows and columns are denoted by x,d∈𝔽q∗x,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}.
