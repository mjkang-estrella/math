                                               THE POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE SQUARE-ENERGY CONJECTURE

                                                                YINCHEN LIU, QUANYU TANG, AND SHENGTONG ZHANG


                                                   Abstract. Let s+ (G) and s− (G) denote the sums of the squares of the positive and negative
                                                   adjacency eigenvalues of a graph G, respectively. We prove the conjecture of Elphick, Farber,
                                                   Goldberg, and Wocjan that every connected graph G on n vertices satisfies
                                                                                   min{s+ (G), s− (G)} ≥ n − 1.
                                                   The proof introduces a new framework for square-energy estimates, in which the Hadamard
                                                   squares of positive semidefinite matrices that encode these spectral quantities are relaxed to
                                                   the full doubly nonnegative cone.

   This conjecture was later highlighted as Conjecture 1 in the survey of Liu and Ning [17].
   By (1), the conjecture states, equivalently, that every connected graph satisfies max{s+ , s− } ≤
2m − n + 1, strengthening Hong’s classical spectral radius bound λ21 ≤ 2m − n + 1 [14]. The
conjectured bound is sharp: every tree satisfies s+ (G) = s− (G) = m = n − 1, and the complete
graph satisfies s− (Kn ) = n − 1.
   Since its formulation, Conjecture 1.1 has attracted considerable attention. The original
paper [11] established it for several classes of graphs, including regular and bipartite graphs,
and Abiad, de Lima, Desai, Guo, Hogben, and Madrid [1] proved it for several additional classes.
Further work has examined structural properties and the asymmetry between the two square
energies [12], as well as a finer conjecture for unicyclic graphs [20]. Substantial progress was
also made on the general case: Zhang [24] established
                                min{s+ (G), s− (G)} ≥ n − γ(G),
where γ(G) denotes the domination number of G—in particular, min{s+ , s− } ≥ n/2—while
Akbari, Kumar, Mohar, and Pragada [2] simultaneously proved the linear bound

   Our main result proves Conjecture 1.1 in full.
Theorem 1.2. Every connected graph G on n vertices satisfies
                                  min{s+ (G), s− (G)} ≥ n − 1.
   Our proof is based on a general relaxation: since the Hadamard squares A± ◦ A± are doubly
nonnegative, bounds on the square energies follow from inequalities valid over the entire doubly
nonnegative cone. At the heart of this paper is one such inequality, for matrices indexed by the
vertices of a connected graph (Theorem 2.1), which we believe to be of independent interest.
   Beyond Conjecture 1.1 itself, Theorem 1.2 resolves several related conjectures from the
literature. More precisely, it proves the disconnected version of the square-energy conjecture [11,
Conjecture 2], Elphick’s conjecture on adjacency inertia recorded in [24, Conjecture 7.4], and
conjectured lower bounds for positive and negative p-energies from [3, Conjecture 2] and [13,
Conjecture 5.4]. It also establishes unconditionally a Nordhaus–Gaddum-type inequality that
Elphick and Aouchiche [10, Theorem 4] derived under Conjecture 1.1. These consequences
are presented in Section 4. In the companion paper [19], the same relaxation yields a positive
square-energy strengthening of Turán’s theorem; the two proofs are otherwise independent.

4.3. Adjacency inertia. For a graph G, let n+ (G), n0 (G), and n− (G) denote, respectively,
the numbers of positive, zero, and negative eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix, counted with
multiplicity. Let ι(G) denote the number of isolated vertices.
   Zhang [24, Corollary 1.5] proved that every graph without isolated vertices satisfies
                       min{s+ (G), s− (G)} ≥ max{n+ (G), n0 (G), n− (G)}.
The following result proves the stronger conjecture of Elphick recorded in [24, Conjecture 7.4]
and extends it to graphs with isolated vertices.
Corollary 4.3. Every graph G satisfies
                  min{s+ (G), s− (G)} ≥ n0 (G) − ι(G) + max{n+ (G), n− (G)}.
Proof. Let η := κ(G) − ι(G) denote the number of connected components of G with at least
two vertices. The adjacency matrix of any such component has at least one positive and at
least one negative eigenvalue. Hence
                                     min{n+ (G), n− (G)} ≥ η.
Since n = n+ (G) + n0 (G) + n− (G), we obtain
            n0 (G) − ι(G) + max{n+ (G), n− (G)} = n − ι(G) − min{n+ (G), n− (G)}
                                                       ≤ n − ι(G) − η
                                                       = n − κ(G).
The result follows from Corollary 4.1.                                                               □

4.5. Graph complements. Let G denote the complement of G. Elphick and Aouchiche [10,
Conjecture 6] conjectured that
                                    s+ (G) + s+ (G) ≤ (n − 1)2
and observed that this inequality would follow from Conjecture 1.1. The following corollary
proves their conjecture and its negative-sign analogue.
Corollary 4.5. Let G be a graph on n ≥ 2 vertices. For every σ ∈ {+, −},
                                    sσ (G) + sσ (G) ≤ (n − 1)2 .
For σ = +, equality holds if and only if G is complete or edgeless. For σ = −, equality holds if
and only if n = 2.
Proof. Fix σ ∈ {+, −}, and let τ denote the opposite sign. Write κ := κ(G) and κ := κ(G).
By Corollary 4.1,
                                sτ (G) + sτ (G) ≥ 2n − κ − κ.
Since
                       s+ (G) + s− (G) + s+ (G) + s− (G) = n(n − 1),
we obtain
                          sσ (G) + sσ (G) ≤ n(n − 1) − 2n + κ + κ.
At least one of G and G is connected, and hence κ + κ ≤ n + 1. Therefore
                                    sσ (G) + sσ (G) ≤ (n − 1)2 .
