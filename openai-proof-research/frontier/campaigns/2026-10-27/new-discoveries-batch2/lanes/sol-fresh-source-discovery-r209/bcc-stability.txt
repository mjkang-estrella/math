                                                            ON SHARP STABILITY OF THE BCC QUANTIZER


                                                                                SENPING LUO AND BINGBING XIE


                                                   Abstract. We prove sharp nonlinear stability estimates for the squared-distance quantization
                                                   energy of the body-centered cubic lattice. The reference Hessian has optimal coercivity constant
                                                   1/96 relative to nearest-neighbor displacement differences, uniformly over all finite-index periods.
                                                   We classify all equality modes and prove cubic cancellation on their span. This yields nonlinear




arXiv:2610.26553v1 [math.AP] 19 Oct 2026
                                                   stability in a neighborhood independent of the period, including volume-preserving strain. The
                                                   loss in the sharp energy coefficient is at most quadratic for each fixed period and exactly linear
                                                   when all periods are allowed. We also determine the transition between these regimes. Finally, we
                                                   prove global optimality and uniqueness of BCC, up to rigid motions, among two-site configurations
                                                   with rectangular period cells of fixed volume.

   Another mechanism selecting BCC appears in the competing interaction model of Ren and
Wei [22], motivated by diblock copolymers and combining perimeter with a nonlocal interaction
generated by (−∆)−3/2 . They proved that the regular part of the corresponding periodic Green
function at the origin is uniquely minimized by BCC among three-dimensional lattices of unit
covolume, using its relation to the height of the dual flat torus.
   More generally, lattice selection is a central question in crystallization; see Blanc and Lewin
[3]. For pair-interaction energies, the first author and Wei proved sharp minimization results for
differences of theta functions [17] and Epstein zeta functions, including Lennard–Jones lattice
energies [18]. Theta derivatives and lattice transitions are treated in [19], and their recent three-
dimensional work [20] concerns theta and Epstein zeta optimization among Bravais lattices. Here
the energy is determined by the nearest-site partition, and the admissible perturbations include
nonlattice configurations.
