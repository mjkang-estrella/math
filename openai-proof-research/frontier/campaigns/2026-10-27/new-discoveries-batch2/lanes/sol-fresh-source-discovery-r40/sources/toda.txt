                                                 ON A SPECIALIZATION OF TODA EIGENFUNCTIONS

arXiv:2502.10655v4 [math.RT] 19 Oct 2026
                                                   Abstract. This paper studies rational functions Jα (q), which depend on a
                                                   positive element α of the root lattice of a root system. These functions arise as
                                                   Shapovalov pairings of Whittaker vectors in Verma modules of highest weight
                                                   −ρ for quantum groups and as Hilbert series of Zastava spaces, and are related
                                                   to the Toda system. They are specializations of multivariate functions more
                                                   commonly studied in the literature. We investigate the denominator of these
                                                   rational functions and give an explicit combinatorial formula for the numerator
                                                   in type A. We also propose a conjectural realization of the numerator as the
                                                   Poincaré polynomial of a smooth variety in type A.

                                                    0

    Moreover, under this bijection, D(m) corresponds exactly to n − 1 minus the
number of valleys of the corresponding Dyck path (where a valley is a step down
followed by a step up). To see this, note first that D(m) is easily seen to count
the number of 1’s in the array m that are not in the bottom row and have no 1’s
directly to their right. One can check that every diagonal contains at most one such
”rightmost 1”, and it contains one if and only if it has the same number of 0’s as
the diagonal directly below7. On the other hand, two consecutive diagonals have the
same number of 0’s if only if there is no valley between them. Since there are n − 1
possible pairs of consecutive diagonals, this proves the claim. For example, for the
array above, there are valleys between diagonals 1 and 2 and between diagonals 2
and 3, and there is a rightmost 1 in diagonal 3.
    It then follows from Theorem 1.2 that Kα is the nth Catalan number and that
(1 − q)2n Jα is the nth Narayana polynomial (which is the generating polynomial for
the ”number of valleys” statistic for Dyck paths).
    Note that the nth Narayana polynomial is also known to arise as the numerator
of the Hilbert series for the homogeneous coordinate ring of the Grassmannian of
planes G(2, n + 2) in its Plucker embedding [23]. This raises the question of a
possible connection (such as a flat degeneration) between this Grassmannian and
the Zastava space Z α which would explain this equality of Hilbert series. We do not
know of such an explanation.

                      7. Conclusion and open problems
   The proof of Theorem 1.1 relies on the representation-theoretic interpretation of
Jα . It would be interesting to have a more elementary proof using the fermionic
recursion directly (which we do not know how to do even in the q → 1 limit of
Remark 1.2). It would also be very interesting to give a geometric explanation for
the denominator, using the interpretation from Section 2. Note that one factor of
(q)α in the denominator comes from the Hilbert series of Aα (see Remark 2.1), but
the other factor of (q)α is not explained. The following conjecture would give a
geometric proof of Theorem 1.1 if proven.
Conjecture 7.1. For every α ∈ Q≥0 , there is a regular sequence
                                  (ai,j )1≤i≤n,1≤j≤ai
                    ON A SPECIALIZATION OF TODA EIGENFUNCTIONS                                     21


in C[Z0α ] such that ai,j is homogeneous of degree j.
   Note that there does exist a regular sequence with the right number of elements
(as Zastava spaces are known to be Cohen-Macaulay [7, Theorem 1.2]), but the
difficult part is getting the right degrees. Conjecture 7.1 would in fact imply the
following stronger form of Theorem 1.1, asserting that the numerator has positive
coefficients:
Conjecture 7.2. For every α ∈ Q≥0 , we have
                                      1
                              Jα ∈        N[q].
                                    (q)2α
   Conjecture 7.2 holds in type A by Theorem 1.2, but we do not know a proof in the
general case. From empirical observations, we in fact believe that, in simply laced
type, the polynomial (q)2α Jα not only has positive coefficients, but is a unimodal
polynomial.
Conjecture 7.3. For simply laced root systems8, the polynomial (q)2α Jα is uni-
modal.
   Conjecture 7.3 would follow from Conjecture 6.1 in type A by the Hard Lefschetz
theorem. Outside type A, unimodality might also be explained by the existence of
a smooth variety with (q)2α Jα as its Poincaré polynomial, though this would require
further investigations as we do not currently have a candidate for such a variety in
types D and E.
   We would also like to see a more conceptual proof of the formula from Theorem
1.2, using either the geometric or representation-theoretic interpretation of Jα . For
example, if one could find a regular sequence (ai,j ) as in Conjecture 7.1 and a
nice basis for the finite-dimensional graded algebra C[Z0α ]/(ai,j ) of a combinatorial
flavor, it could yield a new proof of Theorem 1.2. One reason why a more conceptual
proof is desirable is that it might lead to combinatorial formulas for (q)2α Jα similar
to (4) in types other than type A.
   Finally, let us mention that, assuming Conjecture 7.1, both sides of Conjec-
ture 6.1 are the generating polynomials for the graded dimension of some finite-
dimensional graded algebra: the first is H • (Xα ) and the second is the quotient
C[Z0α ]/(ai,j ). One might therefore hope that, at least for a suitable choice of regu-
lar sequence (ai,j ), Conjecture 6.1 is the combinatorial shadow of an isomorphism
of graded algebras H • (Xα ) ∼= C[Z0α ]/(ai,j ). This hypothetical isomorphism is quite
reminiscent of Hikita’s conjecture, which predicts an isomorphism between the co-
homology ring of a symplectic resolution and a certain finite dimensional quotient
of the coordinate ring of its symplectic dual [24] [26, Section 5.6]. While our study
case does not fit directly in Hikita’s setting (as Zastava spaces do not have sym-
plectic resolutions), the similarity is striking enough to wonder whether there is a
hidden relationship.
