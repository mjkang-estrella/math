                                                                  1. Introduction and Statement of Results

                                           A partition of a positive integer n is a finite non-decreasing sequence of positive integers
                                           λ = λ1 , . . . , λk such that λ1 + . . . + λk = n. The λi ’s are called the parts of the partition and
                                           the weight |λ| of the partition λ is defined to be |λ| = n. We consider the empty sequence
                                           as the only partition of zero. For example, the five partitions of n = 4 are

                                                                   4,    1 + 3,     2 + 2,      1 + 1 + 2,   1 + 1 + 1 + 1.

                                           Depending on context, we sometimes allow zeros to appear in the partition. Clearly, the
                                           zeros have no contribution to the weight of the partition, but the length changes as we add
                                           or take out zeros.
                                           Many partition identities have the following form: “the number of partitions of n satisfying
                                           condition A = the number of partitions of n satisfying condition B” [2]. We recall the famous
                                           partition identity due to Euler [4]:
                                           Theorem 1. The number of partitions of n into distinct parts is equal to the number of
                                           partitions of n into odd parts.

By using the method that we use for constructing generating functions (3), (11) and (13),

generating functions, we mean that we may change the conditions concerning unallowed
initial parts and we can construct generating functions for those partitions. The key point is
that the generating functions (3), (11) and (13) have a nice infinite product representations
as in (27), (28)and (29) (When we take t = 1).
In Section 3, we constructed evidently positive series (9), (17) and (19) as the generating
functions of partitions. When we construct those evidently positive series, we first obtain an
evidently positive series for a key infinite product, namely (14). The idea in the construction
for the key infinite product follows from [6], [7] and [8].

partitions. It would be better if we were able to write a closed formula for the polynomials
P (m1 , m2 , m3 , m + 1; q) without any restriction of the parameters, but at least we know the
functional equations concerning those polynomials. Better than that would be constructing
a monomial of q as the generating function of the base partitions β’s with a given number
of pairs and singletons. It is a quite interesting question and it waits for an answer.
