Abstract
In this paper, we give infinitely many examples of (non-isomorphic) connected kk-regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue in half open interval [−1−2,−2)[-1-\sqrt{2},-2) and also infinitely many examples of (non-isomorphic) connected kk-regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue in half open interval [α1,−1−2)[\alpha_{1},-1-\sqrt{2}) where α1\alpha_{1} is the smallest root(≈−2.4812)(\approx-2.4812) of the polynomial x3+2​x2−2​x−2x^{3}+2x^{2}-2x-2. From these results, we determine the largest and second largest limit points of smallest eigenvalues of regular graphs less than −2-2. Moreover we determine the supremum of the smallest eigenvalue among all connected 3-regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue less than −2-2 and we give the unique graph with this supremum value as its smallest eigenvalue.
1 Introduction
Line graphs are examples of graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least −2-2 (where the eigenvalues of a graph are the eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix). In 1976, P. J. Cameron et al. characterized the graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least −2-2 [2]. They showed (using root lattices):
Theorem 1.1
(Cf. [1, Theorem 3.12.1])
A connected graph GG with smallest eigenvalue at least −2-2 is a generalized line graph (for definition, see next section) or the number of vertices of GG is at most 36.
A. J. Hoffman studied graphs with smallest eigenvalue less than −2-2. He showed that the largest limit point smaller than −2-2 for the smallest eigenvalues of graphs is −1−2-1-\sqrt{2} [4]. More precisely, he showed:
Theorem 1.2
Let θ^k\widehat{\theta}_{k} be the supremum of the smallest eigenvalues of graphs with minimal valency kk and smallest eigenvalue <−2<-2. Then (θ^k)k(\widehat{\theta}_{k})_{k} forms a monotone decreasing sequence with limit −1−2-1-\sqrt{2}.
A consequence of the results of R. Woo and A. Neumaier is that α1\alpha_{1} (where α1\alpha_{1} is the smallest root(≈−2.4812)(\approx-2.4812) of the polynomial x3+2​x2−2​x−2x^{3}+2x^{2}-2x-2) is the largest limit point of the smallest eigenvalues of graphs with smallest eigenvalues less than −1−2-1-\sqrt{2} [7]. More precisely:
Theorem 1.3
Let σ^k\widehat{\sigma}_{k} be the supremum of the smallest eigenvalues of graphs with minimal valency kk and smallest eigenvalue <−1−2<-1-\sqrt{2}. Then (σ^k)k(\widehat{\sigma}_{k})_{k} forms a monotone decreasing sequence with limit α1\alpha_{1}.
We will give proofs of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 in Section 2.
In this paper, we study the limit points of the smallest eigenvalues of regular graphs. Clearly −2-2 is a limit point for regular graphs. Also −3−52\frac{-3-\sqrt{5}}{2} is a limit point for regular graphs as the cartesian product of a pentagon and a complete graph with at least 2 vertices has smallest eigenvalues −3−52\frac{-3-\sqrt{5}}{2}. But it is not known whether this is the largest limit point smaller then −2-2 for regular graphs.
In this paper, we will construct kk-regular graphs which have the smallest eigenvalues in [−1−2,−2)[-1-\sqrt{2},-2) for all kk. And as a corollary, we will show that for regular graphs, the largest limit point of regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue less than −2-2 is also −1−2-1-\sqrt{2}. Our results are:
Theorem 1.4
Let kk be a positive integer at least 3. Then there are infinitely many kk-regular graphs which have smallest eigenvalue in [−1−2,−2)[-1-\sqrt{2},-2). Also, there are infinitely many 33-regular triangle free graphs which have the smallest eigenvalue in [−1−2,−2)[-1-\sqrt{2},-2).
Corollary 1.5
Let η^k\hat{\eta}_{k} be the supremum of the smallest eigenvalues of kk-regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue <−2<-2. Then
limk→∞(η^k)k=−1−2.\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\hat{\eta}_{k})_{k}=-1-\sqrt{2}.
Furthermore, for enough large kk, we will construct kk-regular graphs which have smallest eigenvalues in [α1,−1−2)[\alpha_{1},-1-\sqrt{2}) where α1\alpha_{1} is the smallest root(≈−2.4812)(\approx-2.4812) of the polynomial x3+2​x2−2​x−2x^{3}+2x^{2}-2x-2. And as a corollary, we will show that the largest limit point of regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue less than −1−2-1-\sqrt{2} is also α1\alpha_{1}, as shown for general graphs by R. Woo and A. Neumaier.
Theorem 1.6
There exist NN such that there are infinitely many kk-regular graphs which have smallest eigenvalue in [α1,−1−2)[\alpha_{1},-1-\sqrt{2}) for k≥Nk\geq N.
Corollary 1.7
Let ξ^k\hat{\xi}_{k} be the supremum of the smallest eigenvalues of kk-regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue <−1−2<-1-\sqrt{2}. Then
limk→∞(ξ^k)k=α1\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}(\hat{\xi}_{k})_{k}=\alpha_{1}
where α1\alpha_{1} is the smallest root(≈−2.4812)(\approx-2.4812) of the polynomial x3+2​x2−2​x−2x^{3}+2x^{2}-2x-2.
Theorems 1.4 disproves the following conjecture by R. Woo and A. Neumaier.
Conjecture 1.8
[7, p.590, Conjecture (ii)]
A regular graph with smallest eigenvalue ≥−1−2\geq-1-\sqrt{2} and sufficiently large valency is a line graph or a cocktail party graph.
In order to show Theorems 1.4 and 1.6, we will use Hoffman graphs, which were introduced by R. Woo and A. Neumaier[7].
In the next section we will give definitions and preliminaries. Also in this section we will recall Hoffman graphs and some of their basic theory. In Section 3, we will show Theorems 1.4, 1.6 and Corollary 1.5, 1.7. In Section 4, we will determine η^3\hat{\eta}_{3}. In the last section, we will give concluding remarks.
2 Definitions and preliminaries
All the graphs considered in this paper are finite, undirected and without multiple edges.
A graph GG is an ordered pair (V⁡(G),E⁡(G))(V(G),E(G)) of vertex set V=V⁡(G)V=V(G) and edge set E=E⁡(G)E=E(G), where E⁡(G)E(G) consists of unordered pairs of two adjacent vertices, so we can consider E⁡(G)⊆(V⁡(G)2)E(G)\subseteq\binom{V(G)}{2}.
For two graph GG and HH such that V⁡(H)⊆V⁡(G)V(H)\subseteq V(G) and {x,y}∈E⁡(H)\{x,y\}\in E(H) if and only if {x,y}∈E⁡(G)\{x,y\}\in E(G) for all x,y∈V⁡(H)x,y\in V(H), we call that HH is induced subgraph of GG and GG is super graph of HH.
The adjacency matrix A⁡(G)A(G) of a graph GG is the (0,1)(0,1)-matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by the vertex set V⁡(G)V(G) and the (x,y)(x,y)-entry is 11 whenever xx is adjacent to yy and 00 otherwise. The eigenvalues of GG are the eigenvalues of its adjacency matrix. A graph GG is kk-regular if for any vertex xx, the number of edges which contain xx, we call valency of xx, is kk. A graph GG is regular if it is kk-regular for some integer kk. A graph GG is (k1,k2)(k_{1},k_{2})-semiregular if there is a partition {A≠∅,B≠∅}\{A\neq\emptyset,B\neq\emptyset\} of vertex set such that every vertex in AA has valency k1k_{1} and every vertex in BB has valency k2k_{2}. We denote δ⁡(G)\delta(G) the minimum valency of GG.
For a given graph GG, the line graph of GG, Ł⁡(G)\L(G), has vertex set E⁡(G)E(G) and {x,y}\{x,y\} is adjacent to edge {v,w}\{v,w\} if and only if |{x,y}∩{v,w}|=1|\{x,y\}\cap\{v,w\}|=1. A graph GG is called a line graph if G=L⁡(H)G=L(H) for some graph HH.
For a line graph GG, say G=L⁡(H)G=L(H), we obtain the relation CT​C=A⁡(G)+2​IC^{T}C=A(G)+2I where CC is a vertex-edge incidence matrix of HH and II is an identity matrix. This relation implies that the smallest eigenvalue of a line graph is at least −2-2.
Line graphs can be recognized combinatorially as J. Krausz [6] showed:
Theorem 2.1
(Cf. [3, Theorem 2.1.1])
A graph is a line graph if and only if its edge set can be partitioned into non-trivial cliques such that:
(i)
two cliques have at most one vertex in common;
(ii)
each vertex is in at most two cliques.
In this paper, we are mainly interested in regular graphs. P. J. Cameron et al. [2] characterized the connected regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least −2-2. Their result:
Theorem 2.2
(Cf. [1, Theorem 3.12.2])
Let GG be a connected regular graph with ν\nu points, valency kk, and smallest eigenvalue at least −2-2. Then one of the following holds:
(i)
GG is the line graph of a regular or a bipartite semiregular connected graph.
(ii)
ν=2​(k+2)≤28\nu=2(k+2)\leq 28 and GG is an induced subgraph of E7​(1)E_{7}(1).
(iii)
ν=32​(k+2)≤27\nu=\frac{3}{2}(k+2)\leq 27 and GG is an induced subgraph of Schläfli graph.
(iv)
ν=43​(k+2)≤16\nu=\frac{4}{3}(k+2)\leq 16 and GG is an induced subgraph of Clebsch graph.
(v)
ν=k+2\nu=k+2 and G≅Km×2G\cong K_{m\times 2} for some m≥3m\geq 3.
This theorem means that there are only finitely many connected regular graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least −2-2 which are neither line graphs nor Cocktail party graphs.
2.1 Hoffman graphs
Hoffman graphs can be used as a tool to construct graphs with smallest eigenvalue at least a fixed number. In this subsection, we will define Hoffman graphs (as introduced by R. Woo and A. Neumaier) and discuss their basic theory [7].
A Hoffman graph ℌ\mathfrak{H} is a pair (H=(V,E),μ:V→{f,s})(H=(V,E),\ \mu:V\rightarrow\{f,s\}) satisfying the following conditions:
(i)
H is a graph;
(ii)
every vertex with label ff is adjacent to at least one vertex with label ss;
(iii)
vertices with label ff are pairwise non-adjacent.
We call a vertex with label ss a slim vertex, and a vertex with label ff a fat vertex. We denote by Vs=Vs​(ℌ)V_{s}=V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}) (resp. Vf=Vf​(ℌ)V_{f}=V_{f}(\mathfrak{H})) the set of slim (resp. fat) vertices of ℌ\mathfrak{H}. The subgraph of a Hoffman graph ℌ\mathfrak{H} induced on Vs​(ℌ)V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}) is called the slim graph of ℌ\mathfrak{H}.
Let ℌ\mathfrak{H} be a Hoffman graph and let ℌ1\mathfrak{H}^{1} and ℌ2\mathfrak{H}^{2} be two non-empty induced Hoffman subgraphs of ℌ\mathfrak{H}. The Hoffman graph ℌ\mathfrak{H} is said to be the sum of ℌ1\mathfrak{H}^{1} and ℌ2\mathfrak{H}^{2}, written as ℌ=ℌ1⊕ℌ2\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}^{1}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{2}, if the following conditions are satisfied:
(i)
V⁡(ℌ)=V⁡(ℌ1)∪V⁡(ℌ2)V(\mathfrak{H})=V(\mathfrak{H}^{1})\cup V(\mathfrak{H}^{2});
(ii)
{Vs​(ℌ1),Vs​(ℌ2)}\{V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{1}),V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{2})\} is a partition of Vs​(ℌ)V_{s}(\mathfrak{H});
(iii)
if x∈Vs​(ℌ1)x\in V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{1}) and y∈Vs​(ℌ2)y\in V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{2}), then xx and yy have at most one common fat neighbor and they have one if and only if they are adjacent;
(iv)
if x∈Vs​(ℌi)x\in V_{s}(\mathfrak{H}^{i}) and F∈Vf​(ℌ)F\in V_{f}(\mathfrak{H}) are adjacent in ℌ\mathfrak{H}, then F∈Vf​(ℌi)F\in V_{f}(\mathfrak{H}^{i}) and xx and FF are adjacent in ℌi\mathfrak{H}^{i} for i=1,2i=1,2.
It easily follows that the sum defined above is associative, in the sense that if ℌ=ℌ1⊕(ℌ2⊕ℌ3)\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H}^{1}\oplus(\mathfrak{H}^{2}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{3}), then ℌ=(ℌ1⊕ℌ2)⊕ℌ3\mathfrak{H}=(\mathfrak{H}^{1}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{2})\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{3} and vice versa. Instead of ℌ=((…​(ℌ1⊕ℌ2)⊕ℌ3)​…​ℌn)\mathfrak{H}=((\ldots(\mathfrak{H}^{1}\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{2})\oplus\mathfrak{H}^{3})\ldots\mathfrak{H}^{n}), we write ℌ=⨁i=1nℌi\mathfrak{H}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{H}^{i}.
Let 𝔄\mathfrak{A} be a family of Hoffman graphs. An 𝔄\mathfrak{A}-line graph is an induced subgraph of the slim graph of a Hoffman graph ℌ=⨁i=1sℌi\mathfrak{H}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathfrak{H}^{i} for some ss.
In particular, it can be easily checked that a line graph is a {ℌ2}\{\mathfrak{H_{2}}\}-line graph where ℌ2\mathfrak{H_{2}} is as shown in Figure 1 at the end of this subsection. We define a generalized line graph as a {ℌ2,ℌ3}\{\mathfrak{H_{2}},\mathfrak{H_{3}}\}-line graph where ℌ3\mathfrak{H_{3}} is also shown in Figure 1. This definition is equivalent with original definition in [1, p. 106].
Now, we define eigenvalues of Hoffman graphs.
For a Hoffman graph ℌ=(H,μ)\mathfrak{H}=(H,\mu), let AA be the adjacency matrix of HH,
A=[AsCCT0]A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{s}&C\\
C^{T}&0\end{array}\right]
where AsA_{s} is the adjacency matrix of the slim graph of ℌ\mathfrak{H}. Eigenvalues of ℌ\mathfrak{H} are defined as the eigenvalues of the real symmetric matrix B=B⁡(ℌ):=As−C​CTB=B(\mathfrak{H}):=A_{s}-CC^{T}. By λm​i​n​(ℌ)\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H}), we denote the smallest eigenvalue of ℌ\mathfrak{H}.
From above definitions, we can regard any ordinary graph HH as a Hoffman graph ℌ=(H,μ)\mathfrak{H}=(H,\mu) satisfing μ⁡(x)=s\mu(x)=s for all x∈V⁡(H)x\in V(H), and then the eigenvalues of HH are exactly the same as the eigenvalues of ℌ\mathfrak{H}.
We denote by λm​i​n=λm​i​n​(G)\lambda_{min}=\lambda_{min}(G) the smallest eigenvalue of a given graph GG. Figure 1 shows that the smallest eigenvalues of some Hoffman graphs where α0=−1−2\alpha_{0}=-1-\sqrt{2} and α1≈−2.4812\alpha_{1}\approx-2.4812 is the smallest root of x3+2​x2−2​x−2x^{3}+2x^{2}-2x-2.
The following theorem shows the relation between the smallest eigenvalue of a Hoffman graph and any of its induced subgraph, in particular, its slim graph.
Theorem 2.3
(Cf. [7, Theorem 3.2 and 3.7])
(i)
If 𝔊\mathfrak{G} is an induced subgraph of a Hoffman graph ℌ\mathfrak{H}, then λm​i​n​(𝔊)≥λm​i​n​(ℌ)\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{G})\geq\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H}) holds.
(ii)
If ℌ=ℌ1⊕ℌ2\mathfrak{H}=\mathfrak{H_{1}}\oplus\mathfrak{H_{2}}, then λm​i​n​(ℌ)=min⁡{λm​i​n​(ℌ1),λm​i​n​(ℌ2)}\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H})=\min\{\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H_{1}}),\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H_{2}})\}.
To consider the limit points of smallest eigenvalues, we discuss the following proposition which was shown by A. J. Hoffman and A. M. Ostrowski. (For a proof, see [5]).
Proposition 2.4
(Cf. [7, Proposition 5.3])
Let ℌ\mathfrak{H} be a Hoffman graph and denote by ℌ(n)\mathfrak{H}^{(n)} the clique extension obtained by replacing all fat vertices by slim nn-cliques, all of whose vertices are adjacent to the neighbors of the corresponding fat vertices. Then
limn→∞λm​i​n​(ℌ(n))=λm​i​n​(ℌ).\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H}^{(n)})=\lambda_{min}(\mathfrak{H}).
The following theorem gives a structure theory for graph with smallest eigenvalue a little bit smaller than −2-2.
Theorem 2.5
(i)
(Cf. [4, Theorem 1.1])
For −1−2<λ≤−2-1-\sqrt{2}<\lambda\leq-2, there exists a number f⁡(λ)f(\lambda) such that if GG is a connected graph with minimum valency of GG is at least f⁡(λ)f(\lambda) and λm​i​n​(G)≥λ\lambda_{min}(G)\geq\lambda, then λm​i​n​(G)=−2\lambda_{min}(G)=-2 and GG is a generalized line graph.
(ii)
(Cf. [7, Theorem 5.1])
Let λ≤−1\lambda\leq-1 be a real number larger than α1≈−2.4812\alpha_{1}\approx-2.4812, the smallest root of the polynomial x3+2​x2−2​x−2x^{3}+2x^{2}-2x-2. Then there exists a number κλ\kappa_{\lambda} such that every graph with minimum valency at least κλ\kappa_{\lambda} such that its smallest eigenvalue λm​i​n\lambda_{min} at least λ\lambda is an {ℌ2,ℌ5,ℌ7,ℌ9}\{{\mathfrak{H}}_{2},{\mathfrak{H}}_{5},{\mathfrak{H}}_{7},{\mathfrak{H}}_{9}\}-line graph. In particular, λm​i​n≥−1−2\lambda_{min}\geq-1-\sqrt{2}.
