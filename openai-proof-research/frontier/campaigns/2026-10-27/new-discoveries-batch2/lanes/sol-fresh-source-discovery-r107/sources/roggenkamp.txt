Abstract.
We investigate whether the group algebra of a finite group over a localisation of the integers is semiperfect. The main result is a necessary and sufficient arithmetic criterion in the ordinary case. In the modular case, we propose a conjecture, which extends the criterion.
Key words and phrases: Krull-Schmidt, semiperfect ring, group algebra, idempotent, finite group
1991 Mathematics Subject Classification16S34, 16U40, 20C20, 20C15
Introduction
In the present article we would like to contemplate the following problem, posed by Roggenkamp:
Problem 1.
[10, Kourovka Pr. 4.55]
Let GG be a finite group and ℤ(p){\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)} the localization at pp. Does the Krull–Schmidt theorem hold for projective ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G-modules?
The first point to clarify is what is meant by the Krull-Schmidt theorem here. Note that in the literature the names of Azumaya and Remak are often attached to it as well, while it is normally formulated for the category of finite length modules with two slightly different, yet equivalent for finite length modules statements, cf. [3, 5]
vs.
[1, 6]. We can interpret Problem 1 as three different questions about ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G:
Problem 2.
Is the category of finitely generated projective ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G-modules a Krull-Schmidt category?
Problem 3.
Can every projective ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G-module PP be written as a direct sum of indecomposable projective modules
P=⊕i∈IPiP=\oplus_{i\in I}P_{i}
in a unique, up to a permutation and isomorphisms way?
Problem 4.
Can every finitely generated projective ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G-module PP be written as a direct sum of indecomposable projective modules
(1)
P=P1⊕…⊕PnP=P_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus P_{n}
in a unique, up to a permutation and isomorphisms way?
These problems are interdependent:
Yes to Pr. 2⟹Yes to Pr. 3⟹Yes to Pr. 4\mbox{Yes to Pr. 2}\Longrightarrow\mbox{Yes to Pr. 3}\Longrightarrow\mbox{Yes to Pr. 4}
In the present paper we address only Problem 2.
The short answer to it is no.
The reader can benefit from reading the expository paper by Krause [11]. The following well-known fact contains the definitions of a semiperfect ring and a Krull-Schmidt category.
Proposition 5.
[11, Prop. 4.1],
[2, Th. 2.1],
[3, Ch. 7]
Let RR be a ring, J=J⁡(R)J=J(R) its Jacobson radical. The following conditions are equivalent:
•
RR is semiperfect.
•
The category of finitely generated projective left RR-modules is a Krull-Schmidt category.
•
Every finitely generated projective left RR-module PP admits a decomposition (1)
where each PiP_{i} has a local endomorphism ring.
•
The regular module RR{}_{R}R admits a decomposition (1)
where each PiP_{i} has a local endomorphism ring.
•
Every simple left RR-module admits a projective cover.
•
Every finitely generated left RR-module admits a projective cover.
•
The ring R/JR/J is semisimple artinian and every idempotent in R/JR/J can be lifted to RR.
Now we can explain the negative answer to Problem 2, following Woods [14].
Let G=C3G=C_{3}, the cyclic group of order 33, 𝔽q\mathbb{F}_{q} – the finite field of size qq.
Observe that
(2)
ℚ​C3≅ℚ⊕ℚ⁡(13),𝔽p​C3≅{𝔽p⊕𝔽p⊕𝔽p, if ​p≡1mod3,𝔽p⊕𝔽p2, if ​p≡2mod3.\mathbb{Q}C_{3}\cong\mathbb{Q}\oplus\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{1}),\quad\mathbb{F}_{p}C_{3}\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}\,,&\ \mbox{ if }p\equiv 1\mod 3,\\
\mathbb{F}_{p}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\,,&\ \mbox{ if }p\equiv 2\mod 3.\end{cases}
If p≡1mod3p\equiv 1\mod 3, 𝔽p​G\mathbb{F}_{p}G has 3 primitive idempotents: too many to be lifted, so that ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is not semiperfect.
On the other hand, if
p≡2mod3p\equiv 2\mod 3, both primitive idempotents can be lifted to ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G, making it semiperfect:
13​(e+x+x2),13​(2​e−x−x2), where ​G=⟨x⟩.\frac{1}{3}(e+x+x^{2}),\ \frac{1}{3}(2e-x-x^{2}),\ \mbox{ where }\ G=\langle x\rangle\,.
Yet we are not satisfied with this brief negative answer.
Consequently we reinterpret the original question:
Problem 6.
Let GG be a finite group and ℤ(p){\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)} the localisation at a prime pp.
Find necessary and sufficient conditions on pp and GG for the ring ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G to be semiperfect.
Let us summarize quickly what is already known about it
Proposition 7.
The following statements hold for a finite group GG.
(1)
If N⊴GN\unlhd G and ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect, then ℤ(p)​G/N{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}G/N is semiperfect [2, Lemma 2.2].
(2)
ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect if and only if all idempotents of 𝔽p​G\mathbb{F}_{p}G can be lifted to ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G [4, Prop. 8].
(3)
Suppose N⊴GN\unlhd G, H≤GH\leq G, H​N=GHN=G and NN is a pp-group.
If ℤ(p)​H{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}H is semiperfect, then ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect [14, Prop. 4.2].
(4)
ℤ(p)​S3{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}S_{3} is semiperfect for every pp
[14, Lemma 6.1].
(5)
ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect if and only ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is clean (follows from [8, Th. 1.1]).
Assume further that GG is an abelian group of exponent n=m​pan=mp^{a}, p∤mp\nmid m with a Sylow pp-subgroup PP.
(6)
ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect if and only if ℤ(p)​G/P{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}G/P is semiperfect [14, Lemma 5.1].
(7)
ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect if and only if ℤ(p)​Cn{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}C_{n} is semiperfect [14, Prop. 5.7].
(8)
ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect if and only if
each monic factor of zm−1∈𝔽p​[z]z^{m}-1\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[z]
can be lifted to ℤ(p)​[z]{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}[z] [14, Th. 5.8].
(9)
ℤ(p)​G{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is semiperfect if and only pp is a primitive root of 1 modulo dd for each d|md|m [8, Lemma 2.6].
Notice further that a complete satisfactory answer to Problem 6 is known for abelian GG [8, Th. 2.11 and Th. 3.5], yet we skip it here as it boils down to getting to the bottom of the arithmetic of the last statement of Proposition 7.
For general GG and pp, the ring ℤ(p)​G/J{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G/J is semisimple and artinian – it is even a finite dimensional algebra. So the real issue is lifting of idempotents. The idempotents can be lifted to ℤ(p)^​G\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G, where ℤ(p)^\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}} is the pp-adic integers. This is a classical well-known topic [3, 5]:
ℤ(p)^​G\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}}G is always semiperfect.
Here, in this paper, we are trying to grapple with
the non-complete local ring ℤ(p){\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}.
We can give a fully satisfactory answer to Problem 6 only in the “ordinary” case. The answer can be given in terms of the standard arithmetic information attached to the complex irreducible characters χ∈Irr⁡(G)\chi\in\Irr(G).
Theorem 8.
Suppose pp doesn’t divide |G||G|.
Then the ring ℤ(p)​G\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}G is semiperfect if and only if both of the following conditions hold:
i)
For each χ∈Irr⁡(G)\chi\in\Irr(G) its Schur index ι⁡(χ)\iota(\chi) is 11.
ii)
For each χ∈Irr⁡(G)\chi\in\Irr(G) the prime pp is inert in the field of character values ℚ⁡(χ)\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
The modular case, when p||G|p\mid|G|, is harder
to pinpoint. Yet we propose a conjecture
that yields a workable criterion in the modular case.
To state it, we recall some standard notation.
Let RR be a ring. Let 𝐆⁡(R)\mathbf{G}(R) and 𝐊⁡(R)\mathbf{K}(R) denote the Grothendieck rings over ℤ\mathbb{Z} of finitely generated RR-modules and of finitely generated projective RR-modules, respectively. Let 𝐆+​(R)\mathbf{G}^{+}(R) and 𝐊+​(R)\mathbf{K}^{+}(R) be the corresponding positive Grothendieck semigroups, i.e., ℕ\mathbb{N}-spans of modules inside the Grothendieck groups. Consider the natural maps
(3)
𝐆⁡(ℚ​G)→𝛼𝐆⁡(𝔽p​G)←𝛽𝐊⁡(𝔽p​G)\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q}G)\xrightarrow{\alpha}\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{F}_{p}G)\xleftarrow{\beta}\mathbf{K}(\mathbb{F}_{p}G)
where α\alpha is the decomposition map [5, Def. 16.20]
and β\beta is the Cartan homomorphism
[5, Def. 18.4].
Conjecture 9.
The ring ℤ(p)​G\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}G is semiperfect if and only if
α⁡(𝐆+​(ℚ​G))⊇β⁡(𝐊+​(𝔽p​G))\alpha(\mathbf{G}^{+}(\mathbb{Q}G))\supseteq\beta(\mathbf{K}^{+}(\mathbb{F}_{p}G)).
In the first section we set up preliminaries on liftings of modules, our approach to Problem 6.
In the second section we consider the ordinary case, i.e., p∤|G|p\nmid|G|, in particular, we prove Theorem 8.
The final section is devoted to the general case and contains a discussion of Conjecture 9.

1. Preliminaries
Let 𝕆\mathbb{O} be a discrete valuation ring, 𝕀=(ϖ)\mathbb{I}=(\varpi) – its maximal ideal with uniformiser ϖ\varpi. We also consider its residue field 𝔽≔𝕆/𝕀\mathbb{F}\coloneqq\mathbb{O}/\mathbb{I} and its fraction field 𝕂≔Q⁡(𝕆)\mathbb{K}\coloneqq Q(\mathbb{O}).
Let AA be an 𝕆\mathbb{O}-order, that is, an associative 𝕆\mathbb{O}-algebra, finitely generated projective as an 𝕆\mathbb{O}-module. Given a ring homomorphism ϕ:𝕆→R\phi:\mathbb{O}\rightarrow R, by AϕA_{\phi} or ARA_{R}, when ϕ\phi is clear by default, we denote the specialisation R⊗𝕆AR\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}A.
The two key specialisations come from the two points of Spec​(𝕆)\mbox{Spec}(\mathbb{O}):
the algebras A𝕂A_{\mathbb{K}} and A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} are finite-dimensional of the same dimension.
Lemma 1.1.
The restriction to the Jacobson radicals ψ:J⁡(A)→J⁡(A𝔽)\psi:J(A)\rightarrow J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) of the natural homomorphism ψ:A→A𝔽\psi:A\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{F}} is surjective.
Furthermore, J⁡(A)=ψ−1​(J⁡(A𝔽))J(A)=\psi^{-1}(J(A_{\mathbb{F}})).
Proof.
Since ψ\psi is surjective, ψ⁡(J⁡(A))⊆J⁡(A𝔽)\psi(J(A))\subseteq J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) and J⁡(A)⊆ψ−1​(J⁡(A𝔽))J(A)\subseteq\psi^{-1}(J(A_{\mathbb{F}})).
Now consider a simple AA-module MM. The multiplication by ϖ\varpi is a homomorphism
M→MM\rightarrow M. By Schur’s Lemma, it is either zero or an automorphism. Note further that it cannot be an automorphism. Suppose it is. Then M=𝕂⊗𝕆MM=\mathbb{K}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}M is already an A𝕂A_{\mathbb{K}}-module.
Choose finitely many generators a1,…,ana_{1},\ldots,a_{n} of the 𝕆\mathbb{O}-module AA and a nonzero
m∈Mm\in M. Let M′⊆MM^{\prime}\subseteq M be the 𝕆\mathbb{O}-submodule generated by a1​m,…,an​ma_{1}m,\ldots,a_{n}m. Clearly, M′M^{\prime} is an AA-submodule. By simplicity, M′=MM^{\prime}=M, which is impossible for a 𝕂\mathbb{K}-vector space.
Hence, ϖ​M=0\varpi M=0 and MM is an A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module. It follows that
J⁡(A)J(A) and J⁡(A𝔽)J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) consist of the annihilators of the same set of modules.
Consequently, ψ:J⁡(A)→J⁡(A𝔽)\psi:J(A)\rightarrow J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) is surjective and
J⁡(A)=ψ−1​(J⁡(A𝔽))J(A)=\psi^{-1}(J(A_{\mathbb{F}})).
∎
Corollary 1.2.
The rings A/J⁡(A)A/J(A) and A𝔽/J⁡(A𝔽)A_{\mathbb{F}}/J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) are isomorphic.
We need to talk about lifting of modules. Let M𝔽M_{\mathbb{F}} be an A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module. We say that an AA-module MM is its lifting if MM is a projective 𝕆\mathbb{O}-module and M𝔽≅𝔽⊗𝕆MM_{\mathbb{F}}\cong\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}M as A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-modules. The following observation is useful:
Lemma 1.3.
[5, Th. 30.11]
Suppose MM is a lifting of M𝔽M_{\mathbb{F}}.
The AA-module MM is projective if and only if the A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module M𝔽M_{\mathbb{F}} is projective.
We are ready for our main criterion, a handy supplement to Proposition 5.
Proposition 1.4.
The following conditions for the 𝕆\mathbb{O}-order AA are equivalent.
(1)
AA is semiperfect.
(2)
Every idempotent of A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} can be lifted to an idempotent of AA.
(3)
Every principal indecomposable A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module admits a lifting.
(4)
Every finitely generated projective A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module admits a lifting.
Proof.
The equivalence (1)⇔(2)(1)\Leftrightarrow(2) follows from
Corollary 1.2. Indeed, it ensures that A/J⁡(A)A/J(A) is a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra.
By Proposition 5, AA is semiperfect if and only if all idempotents of A/J⁡(A)≅A𝔽/J⁡(A𝔽)A/J(A)\cong A_{\mathbb{F}}/J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) can be lifted to AA.
It remains to compare idempotents in three algebras
A→𝜓A𝔽→𝜋A/J⁡(A)≅A𝔽/J⁡(A𝔽).A\xrightarrow{\psi}A_{\mathbb{F}}\xrightarrow{\pi}A/J(A)\cong A_{\mathbb{F}}/J(A_{\mathbb{F}})\,.
Indeed, an idempotent e∈A/J⁡(A)e\in A/J(A) can always be lifted
to an idempotent e1∈A𝔽e_{1}\in A_{\mathbb{F}} because J⁡(A𝔽)J(A_{\mathbb{F}}) is nilpotent. Now use (2)(2) to lift it to an idempotent e2∈Ae_{2}\in A. This proves (1)(1).
In the opposite direction, assume (1)(1).
Pick an idempotent e∈A𝔽e\in A_{\mathbb{F}}, lift π⁡(e)\pi(e) to an
idempotent e1∈Ae_{1}\in A. This proves (2)(2).
The implication
(1)⇒(4)(1)\Rightarrow(4) is contained within Proposition 5.
Indeed, let P𝔽P_{\mathbb{F}} be a finitely generated projective A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module.
As an AA-module, it is also finitely generated. Proposition 5
yields us an AA-projective cover π:P↠P𝔽\pi:P\twoheadrightarrow P_{\mathbb{F}}.
Note that AA-projectivity implies 𝕆\mathbb{O}-projectivity.
Since ϖ​P𝔽=0\varpi P_{\mathbb{F}}=0, we conclude that ker⁡π⊇ϖ​P\ker\pi\supseteq\varpi P.
The induced AA-module homomorphism
π′\pi^{\prime} yields a decomposition
π:P→𝜓P/ϖ​P→π′P𝔽.\pi:P\xrightarrow{\psi}P/\varpi P\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}P_{\mathbb{F}}\,.
Since P/ϖ​PP/\varpi P is an A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module,
projectivity of P𝔽P_{\mathbb{F}} implies that π′\pi^{\prime} is split:
P/ϖ​P=M⊕P𝔽P/\varpi P=M\oplus P_{\mathbb{F}} for another submodule MM. Hence,
P=ψ−1​(M)+ψ−1​(P𝔽)⊆ker⁡(π)+ψ−1​(P𝔽).P=\psi^{-1}(M)+\psi^{-1}(P_{\mathbb{F}})\subseteq\ker(\pi)+\psi^{-1}(P_{\mathbb{F}})\,.
Since ker⁡(π)\ker(\pi) is superfluous, P=ψ−1​(P𝔽)P=\psi^{-1}(P_{\mathbb{F}}) and M=0M=0.
Therefore, ker⁡π=ϖ​P\ker\pi=\varpi P
and P⊗𝕆𝔽≅P𝔽P\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}\mathbb{F}\cong P_{\mathbb{F}}, so that PP is a lifting.
To prove (3)⇒(1)(3)\Rightarrow(1), we construct a projective cover of a simple AA-module MM.
By Corollary 1.2, MM is an A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module. Let P𝔽​(M)P_{\mathbb{F}}(M) be its A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-projective cover. Since P𝔽​(M)P_{\mathbb{F}}(M) is a principal indecomposable, it admits a lifting PP.
We claim that PP is an AA-projective cover of MM. Indeed, it is projective by Lemma 1.3 and we have surjective homomorphisms of A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} modules
π:P→𝜓P𝔽​(M)→π′M.\pi:P\xrightarrow{\psi}P_{\mathbb{F}}(M)\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}M\,.
It remains to argue that ker⁡(π)\ker(\pi) is superfluous. Assume L+ker⁡(π)=PL+\ker(\pi)=P.
It follows that ψ⁡(L)+ker⁡(π′)=P𝔽​(M)\psi(L)+\ker(\pi^{\prime})=P_{\mathbb{F}}(M). Since π′\pi^{\prime} is an A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-projective cover,
ker⁡(π′)\ker(\pi^{\prime}) is superfluous and ψ​(L)=P𝔽​(M)\psi(L)=P_{\mathbb{F}}(M).
It follows that L+ker⁡(ψ)=L+ϖ​P=PL+\ker(\psi)=L+\varpi P=P.
Observe that ψ\psi is an 𝕆\mathbb{O}-module projective cover.
Hence, ker⁡(ψ)\ker(\psi) is 𝕆\mathbb{O}-superfluous and L=PL=P.
The final implication
(4)⇒(3)(4)\Rightarrow(3) is obvious.
∎
The final well-known corollary is immediate since completeness of 𝕆\mathbb{O} enables lifting of idempotents.
Corollary 1.5.
If 𝕆\mathbb{O} is complete, AA is semiperfect.
2. The semisimple case
Before specialising to the case of the group algebra, we make several general observations in the case, when A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} is semisimple.
The following observation is not fully routine because semisimplicity (in contrast to separability) is not an open condition:
Lemma 2.1.
If A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} is semisimple, then A𝕂A_{\mathbb{K}} is semisimple.
Proof.
Suppose A𝕂A_{\mathbb{K}} is not semisimple. Then I≔A∩J⁡(A𝕂)I\coloneqq A\cap J(A_{\mathbb{K}}) is a non-zero nilpotent ideal of AA. Since A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} is semisimple, it has no nilpotent ideals. Therefore, ψ⁡(I)=0\psi(I)=0
and I⊆ϖ​A=ker⁡ψI\subseteq\varpi A=\ker\psi, where ψ:A→A𝔽\psi:A\rightarrow A_{\mathbb{F}} is the reduction homomorphism.
Consider now I′=ϖ−1​I≔{x∈A∣ϖ​x∈I}I^{\prime}=\varpi^{-1}I\coloneqq\{x\in A\,\mid\,\varpi x\in I\}.
Clearly, I′I^{\prime} is still a subset of J⁡(A𝕂)J(A_{\mathbb{K}}). Hence, I′=II^{\prime}=I.
Now pick a nonzero element ϖn​a∈I\varpi^{n}a\in I with a∈A∖ϖ​Aa\in A\setminus\varpi A and the smallest possible nn. Since I⊆ϖ​AI\subseteq\varpi A, n≥1n\geq 1. Then
ϖn−1​a∈I′\varpi^{n-1}a\in I^{\prime}, proving that I′≠II^{\prime}\neq I, a contradiction.
∎
In light of Lemma 2.1, the next step is to compare Artin-Wedderburn decompositions. Let us fix them:
(4)
A𝔽≅Mn1​(F1)⊕…⊕Mnk​(Fk),A𝕂≅Mm1​(K1)⊕…⊕Mmt​(Kt)A_{\mathbb{F}}\cong M_{n_{1}}(F_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus M_{n_{k}}(F_{k}),\quad A_{\mathbb{K}}\cong M_{m_{1}}(K_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus M_{m_{t}}(K_{t})
where FiF_{i}, KiK_{i} are finite-dimensional division algebras over 𝔽\mathbb{F} and 𝕂\mathbb{K}, respectively. By ι⁡(Fi)≔dimZ⁡(Fi)Fi\iota(F_{i})\coloneqq\sqrt{\dim_{Z(F_{i})}F_{i}},
ι⁡(Ki)≔dimZ⁡(Ki)Ki\iota(K_{i})\coloneqq\sqrt{\dim_{Z(K_{i})}K_{i}} we denote their Schur indices.
Proposition 2.2.
Suppose that A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} is semisimple with Artin-Wedderburn decompositions as in (4). In addition to Proposition 1.4, the following conditions for the 𝕆\mathbb{O}-order AA are equivalent.
(1)
AA is semiperfect.
(2)
Every simple A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module admits a lifting.
Let us assume further that AA is a maximal order. Then the above conditions are also equivalent to the following two conditions.
(3)
The numbers of simple modules are equal: k=tk=t. Furthermore, after a suitable permutation every simple A𝕂A_{\mathbb{K}}-module KimiK_{i}^{m_{i}} admits an AA-lattice MiM_{i} such that the A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-modules Mi⊗𝕆𝔽M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}\mathbb{F} and FiniF_{i}^{n_{i}} are isomorphic.
(4)
The numbers of simple modules are equal: k=tk=t. Furthermore, after a suitable permutation for every ii we have ni=min_{i}=m_{i}, ι⁡(Fi)=ι⁡(Ki)\iota(F_{i})=\iota(K_{i}) and every division algebra KiK_{i} admits an 𝕆\mathbb{O}-order LiL_{i} such that the 𝔽\mathbb{F}-algebras Li⊗𝕆𝔽L_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}\mathbb{F} and FiF_{i} are isomorphic.
Proof.
The implication
(3)⇒(2)(3)\Rightarrow(2) is obvious: the AA-module MiM_{i} is an explicit lifting
of a simple A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}}-module FiniF_{i}^{n_{i}}.
The equivalence (2)⇔(1)(2)\Leftrightarrow(1) follows from Proposition 1.4.
Since A𝔽A_{\mathbb{F}} is semisimple, its principal indecomposable modules are the same as simple
modules.
By Proposition 1.4, they admit liftings.
To prove the implication
(3)⇒(4)(3)\Rightarrow(4), note that MiM_{i}
is a projective AA-module by Lemma 1.3.
Define LiL_{i} as the endomorphism ring Li≔EndA​MiL_{i}\coloneqq\mbox{End}_{A}M_{i}. Since MiM_{i} is AA-projective, the natural map
Li→EndA𝔽​Fini≅FiL_{i}\rightarrow\mbox{End}_{A_{\mathbb{F}}}F_{i}^{n_{i}}\cong F_{i}
is surjective and Li⊗𝕆𝔽≅FiL_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}\mathbb{F}\cong F_{i}. Observe that the second natural map
ϕ:Li→Li⊗𝕆𝕂→EndA𝕂​(Mi⊗𝕆𝕂)≅EndA𝕂​Kimi≅Ki\phi:L_{i}\rightarrow L_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mbox{End}_{A_{\mathbb{K}}}(M_{i}\otimes_{\mathbb{O}}\mathbb{K})\cong\mbox{End}_{A_{\mathbb{K}}}K_{i}^{m_{i}}\cong K_{i}
is injective.
If w∈Liw\in L_{i} and w≠0w\neq 0,
