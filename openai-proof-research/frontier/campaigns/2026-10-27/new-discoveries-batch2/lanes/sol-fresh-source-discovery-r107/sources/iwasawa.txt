Abstract
Let GG be a nilpotent pp-valuable (compact pp-adic Lie) group. There is an ongoing investigation into the prime ideals of its completed group algebra (Iwasawa algebra) ℤp​[[G]]\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[G]], and there remains an open conjecture that they can all be proved to have a canonical standard form. We very this conjecture for several new classes of nilpotent groups, including those corresponding to the positive subalgebra of almost all classical and exceptional types, curiously excluding those of type CC.

1 Introduction
Fix a prime pp. and let GG be a pp-valuable group in the sense of [10] (see Definition 2.5 below). This is a torsion-free, pro-pp group, isomorphic (as a topological group) to a closed subgroup of some GLn​(ℤp)\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}) consisting of upper-triangular matrices congruent to the identity mod pp (if p>2p>2) or mod 44 (if p=2p=2).
Let RR be a commutative pseudocompact ring. The completed group algebra (also known to number theorists as the Iwasawa algebra) of GG over RR is defined as
R⁡[[G]]=lim←U⁡R⁡[G/U],R[[G]]=\varprojlim_{U}R[G/U],
where R⁡[G/U]R[G/U] is the ordinary group algebra of the (finite, discrete) group G/UG/U with coefficients in RR, as UU runs over the set of open normal subgroups of GG.
Now fix a finite extension of fields K/ℚpK/\mathbb{Q}_{p}, with ring of integers 𝒪\mathcal{O}, uniformiser π\pi, and residue field k=𝒪/πk=\mathcal{O}/\pi. There is an ongoing investigation into the prime ideals of 𝒪​G:=𝒪⁡[[G]]\mathcal{O}G:=\mathcal{O}[[G]], especially when 𝒪=ℤp\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}, beginning with the paper [1], and advanced in several subsequent articles, including [3], [4], [7], [9],[2] and [12].
We say that a prime ideal PP of 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G is standard if we can find a closed normal subgroup HH of GG such that G∗:=G/HG^{*}:=G/H is pp-valuable and the image of PP under the surjection 𝒪​G→𝒪​G∗\mathcal{O}G\to\mathcal{O}G^{*} is centrally generated. We say that PP is virtually standard if P∩𝒪​UP\cap\mathcal{O}U is standard for some open subgroup UU of GG [9, Definition 1.1].
The main conjecture in the study of prime ideals in Iwasawa algebras (see e.g. [9, Conjecture 1.2]) roughly states that all prime ideals in 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G are virtually standard. This is now known for large classes of prime ideals in various groups, but in general it remains open.
To simplify the conjecture, fix a prime ideal PP of 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G and set
P†=ker⁡(G→𝒪​G×→(𝒪​G/P)×),P^{\dagger}=\ker(G\to\mathcal{O}G^{\times}\to(\mathcal{O}G/P)^{\times}),
a closed normal subgroup of GG: then PP contains the augmentation ideal ε=(P†−1)​𝒪​G\varepsilon=(P^{\dagger}-1)\mathcal{O}G, so we can pass to the quotient
P∗=P/ε⊆𝒪​G/ε≅𝒪​G∗,P^{*}=P/\varepsilon\subseteq\mathcal{O}G/\varepsilon\cong\mathcal{O}G^{*},
where G∗=G/P†G^{*}=G/P^{\dagger}. Then P∗P^{*} is a prime ideal of 𝒪​G∗\mathcal{O}G^{*}, and now we have (P∗)†=1(P^{*})^{\dagger}=1, so to prove PP is standard, we only need to prove that P∗P^{*} is centrally generated.
Note: The resulting group G∗G^{*} may no longer be pp-valuable, as it may no longer be torsion-free. However, there is a finite normal subgroup F∗≤G∗F^{*}\leq G^{*} such that the quotient G∗/F∗G^{*}/F^{*} is pp-valuable, and P∗∩𝒪​F∗P^{*}\cap\mathcal{O}F^{*} is the intersection of a GG-orbit of prime ideals of 𝒪​F∗≅𝒪⁡[F∗]\mathcal{O}F^{*}\cong\mathcal{O}[F^{*}], the (ordinary) group algebra of F∗F^{*}. Hence it is considered relatively harmless to assume that F∗=1F^{*}=1, or equivalently to assume without loss of generality from the start that P†=1P^{\dagger}=1.
We say that a prime ideal PP of 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G is faithful if P†=1P^{\dagger}=1. Then setting Z=Z⁡(G)Z=Z(G) as the centre of GG, then our main conjecture can be restated as follows.
Conjecture A. If PP is a faithful prime ideal of 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G, then P=(P∩𝒪​Z)​𝒪​GP=(P\cap\mathcal{O}Z)\mathcal{O}G.
There are two natural cases to consider here.
1.
If π∈P\pi\in P, we may quotient out by (π)(\pi) and pass to k​G:=k⁡[[G]]≅𝒪​G/(π)kG:=k[[G]]\cong\mathcal{O}G/(\pi). This version of Conjecture A was asked as [1, Question O], and has since been proven true for GG nilpotent [4, Theorem 8.4], and for GG of the form ℤpd⋊ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p} [7, Theorem 1.4].
2.
If π∉P\pi\not\in P, we may invert π\pi and pass to K​G:=K⊗𝒪𝒪​G=𝒪​G​[1π]KG:=K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}G=\mathcal{O}G[\frac{1}{\pi}]. Conjecture A is known for prime ideals of infinite codimension when GG is semisimple of type AA or DD [12, Theorem 3], [11, Theorem 2], or for primitive ideals when GG is nilpotent [9, Theorem A].
(Warning: K​GKG is not the same as K⁡[[G]]K[[G]]. For example, if we take ℤp​[[x]]\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x]] and invert pp, we do not get ℚp​[[x]]\mathbb{Q}_{p}[[x]].)
In this paper, we restrict to case 2, about which much less is known. We will also assume that GG is a nilpotent group, and we want to build on the results from [9] and try to prove Conjecture A for all prime ideals, rather than just primitive ones.
In §3, we will recall a certain characteristic abelian subgroup A⁡(G)A(G) of GG, originally constructed in [9, §3.5], containing the centre ZZ. When approaching the conjecture for general prime results, the strongest result to date is:
Theorem. [9, Theorem B] Setting A:=A⁡(G)A:=A(G), if PP is a faithful prime ideal of K​GKG, then P=(P∩K​A)​K​GP=(P\cap KA)KG.
Of course, if A⁡(G)=ZA(G)=Z, then Conjecture A follows immediately from this theorem. There are cases when this is true, for example when GG has nilpotency class 2, but it is sadly false in the vast majority of cases of interest. Indeed, whenever the second centre Z2​(G)Z_{2}(G) is abelian (which is the case for all nilpotent groups arising as positive root subgroups) then A⁡(G)A(G) is strictly larger than the centre.
Moreover, the construction of A⁡(G)A(G) relies on an iterative procedure (which can be of arbitrary finite length), and so it can be time-consuming to compute.
In this note, we will define a smaller subgroup B⁡(G)B(G) of GG, with Z⊆B⁡(G)⊆A⁡(G)Z\subseteq B(G)\subseteq A(G). This subgroup is much more explicitly constructed, and can often be computed more easily. Our main result is very similar:
Theorem 1. (Theorem 3.10) Setting B:=B⁡(G)B:=B(G), if PP is a faithful prime ideal of K​GKG, then P=(P∩K​B)​K​GP=(P\cap KB)KG.∎
Needless to say, how much of an improvement this result is depends entirely on how much smaller than A⁡(G)A(G) the subgroup B⁡(G)B(G) actually is. There are several cases when B⁡(G)=A⁡(G)B(G)=A(G) (including of course all the cases when A⁡(G)=ZA(G)=Z), so this result constitutes no improvement in this setting.
However, in §4, we will explore a number of examples of groups GG for which we can prove that B⁡(G)B(G) is a proper subgroup of A⁡(G)A(G), and in several of them we even have that B⁡(G)=ZB(G)=Z. Using our main theorem this is enough to prove Conjecture A for these groups, and in particular for the majority of those groups that arise from Lie theory:
Theorem 2. (Corollary 4.9) If GG is nilpotent and Lie(G)(G) is the positive root subalgebra of a split-semisimple ℚp\mathbb{Q}_{p}-Lie algebra, containing no component of type CC. Then for any faithful prime ideal PP of K​GKG, (P∩K​Z)​K​G=P(P\cap KZ)KG=P.
It is curious that this approach fails when GG is a positive root subgroup of type CC, though we still believe the conjecture to be true in this case.

2 Preliminaries
In this section, we will recap some of the key concepts we will be using throughout the article. Firstly, with regard to notation, if gg and hh are elements of a group, we will write gh=h​g​h−1{}^{h}g=hgh^{-1}. To distinguish between group commutators and ring commutators, we will write the former as (g,h)=gh⋅h−1=g−1h−1gh(g,h)={\vphantom{h}}^{g}h\cdot h^{-1}=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh and the latter as [x,y]=x​y−y​x[x,y]=xy-yx throughout this paper.
The following facts about group commutators are well known, and can be easily checked directly: we include them only for ease of reference later.

2.1 pp-valuable groups
Definition 2.2.
[10, Ch. III, Définition 2.1.2]
A pp-valuation ω\omega on a group GG is a function ω:G→ℝ∪{∞}\omega:G\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\} such that, for all g,h∈Gg,h\in G,
(i)
ω⁡(g​h−1)≥min⁡{ω⁡(g),ω⁡(h)}\omega(gh^{-1})\geq\min\{\omega(g),\omega(h)\},
(ii)
ω⁡((g,h))≥ω⁡(g)+ω⁡(h)\omega((g,h))\geq\omega(g)+\omega(h),
(iii)
ω⁡(gp)=ω⁡(g)+1\omega(g^{p})=\omega(g)+1,
(iv)
ω⁡(g)>(p−1)−1\omega(g)>(p-1)^{-1}, and
(v)
ω⁡(g)=∞⇔g=1\omega(g)=\infty\Leftrightarrow g=1.
Moreover, we say that the pp-valuation ω\omega is saturated if for all g∈Gg\in G such that ω⁡(g)>1p−1+1\omega(g)>\frac{1}{p-1}+1, there exists h∈Gh\in G with g=hpg=h^{p}.
Note: If GG carries a pp-valuation then it is torsionfree, and if gp=hpg^{p}=h^{p} for any g,h∈Gg,h\in G then g=hg=h. In particular, if ω\omega is saturated and ω⁡(g)>1p−1+1\omega(g)>\frac{1}{p-1}+1, then there is a unique element hh such that hp=gh^{p}=g, we write h=gp−1h=g^{p^{-1}}.
Defined in this way, a pp-valuation is a kind of group filtration. More precisely, we isolate the following properties from [10].
Properties 2.3.
Let GG be a group and ω\omega a fixed pp-valuation on GG.
1.
Given any ν∈ℝ\nu\in\mathbb{R}, write Gν=ω−1​([ν,∞])G_{\nu}=\omega^{-1}([\nu,\infty]) and Gν+=ω−1​((ν,∞])G_{\nu^{+}}=\omega^{-1}((\nu,\infty]). These are normal subgroups of GG, and the quotient group grν​(G)=Gν/Gν+\mathrm{gr}_{\nu}(G)=G_{\nu}/G_{\nu^{+}} is abelian, so we will write its group operation as addition.
2.
Define the associated graded group gr⁡(G)=⨁ν∈ℝgrν​(G)\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}(G)=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{gr}_{\nu}(G) [10, Ch. II, 1.1.7.2]. It follows from Definition 2.2(iii) that gr⁡(G)\mathrm{gr}(G) is an 𝔽p\mathbb{F}_{p}-module, and any non-identity element g∈Gg\in G gives a nonzero homogeneous element gr⁡(g):=g+Gμ+∈grμ​(G)\mathrm{gr}(g):=g+G_{\mu^{+}}\in\mathrm{gr}_{\mu}(G), where μ=ω⁡(g)\mu=\omega(g). Moreover, if ω⁡(g)=μ\omega(g)=\mu and ω⁡(h)=ν\omega(h)=\nu, we may define a bracket operation on gr⁡(G)\mathrm{gr}(G):
[gr⁡(g),gr⁡(h)]:=(g,h)+G(μ+ν)+,[\mathrm{gr}(g),\mathrm{gr}(h)]:=(g,h)+G_{(\mu+\nu)^{+}},
which extends bilinearly to an 𝔽p\mathbb{F}_{p}-Lie algebra structure on gr⁡(G)\mathrm{gr}(G) [10, Ch. II, 1.1.7].
3.
There is also an 𝔽p\mathbb{F}_{p}-linear endomorphism π:gr⁡(G)→gr⁡(G)\pi:\mathrm{gr}(G)\to\mathrm{gr}(G), uniquely defined by its action π⁡(gr⁡(g))=gr⁡(gp)\pi(\mathrm{gr}(g))=\mathrm{gr}(g^{p}) on homogeneous elements. This makes gr⁡(G)\mathrm{gr}(G) into an 𝔽p​[π]\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]-Lie algebra [10, Ch. II, 2.1.1] which is torsion-free [10, Ch. I, Théorème 1.2.3], and we will say that the rank of GG is the rank of gr⁡(G)\mathrm{gr}(G) as a 𝔽p​[π]\mathbb{F}_{p}[\pi]-module [10, Définition 2.1.3].
4.
There is also a notion of completion [10, Ch. II, 1.1.5]: set G^=lim←ν∈ℝ⁡G/Gν\displaystyle\widehat{G}=\varprojlim_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}G/G_{\nu}, and say that GG is complete (with respect to ω\omega) if the natural map G→G^G\to\widehat{G} is an isomorphism. Equivalently, we could define a metric on GG by d⁡(g,h):=ω⁡(g−1​h)d(g,h):=\omega(g^{-1}h), and GG is complete if and only if it is topologically complete with respect to this metric.
Note: If GG is complete with respect to ω\omega then for any pp-adic integer λ\lambda, if we write λ:=∑n∈ℕ​λn​pn\lambda:=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}\lambda_{n}p^{n} with 0≤λn<p0\leq\lambda_{n}<p, then we can define gλg^{\lambda} for any g∈Gg\in G to be the limit of the sequence ∏n=1𝑁​gλn​pn\overset{N}{\underset{n=1}{\prod}}g^{\lambda_{n}p^{n}} as N→∞N\rightarrow\infty.
Definition 2.4.
[4, Definition 4.5]
Let GG be a group and ω\omega a fixed pp-valuation. The degree of an automorphism φ\varphi of GG (with respect to ω\omega) is
deg⁡(φ)=degω⁡(φ)=infg∈G{ω⁡(φ⁡(g)​g−1)−ω⁡(g)}.\deg(\varphi)=\deg_{\omega}(\varphi)=\inf_{g\in G}\left\{\omega(\varphi(g)g^{-1})-\omega(g)\right\}.
We will say that φ\varphi is bounded if deg⁡(φ)>(p−1)−1\deg(\varphi)>(p-1)^{-1}.
Note that, if φ\varphi is conjugation by some fixed h∈Gh\in G, then by Definition 2.2(ii), (iv), φ\varphi is bounded with respect to any pp-valuation ω\omega. So, following Lazard, we now make the following definition:
Definition 2.5.
[10, Ch. III, Definition 3.1.6]
A group GG is pp-valuable if there exists a pp-valuation ω\omega with respect to which GG is complete and has finite rank. Moreover, we say that GG is pp-saturable if ω\omega is saturated.
Example 2.6.
We say GG is a uniform pro-pp group if it a finitely generated, torsionfree pro-pp group, and (G,G)⊆Gp(G,G)\subseteq G^{p} [6, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.5]. In this case, we can define a pp-valuation ω\omega on GG by
ω⁡(g):=sup{n∈ℕ:g∈Gpn}\omega(g):=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{N}:g\in G^{p^{n}}\}
With respect to ω\omega, GG is a pp-valuable, and in fact pp-saturable group.
Proposition 2.7.
[10, III.3.3]
For every pp-valuable group GG, there exists a canonical pp-saturable group S​a​t​(G)Sat(G) such that GG can be embedded as an open normal subgroup of S​a​t​(G)Sat(G).
2.2 Lazard’s equivalence of categories
Let GG be a pp-valuable group, with pp-valuation ω\omega, and let ΛG:=ℤp​G\Lambda_{G}:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}G be the ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Iwasawa algebra of GG. Then ΛG\Lambda_{G} carries a natural topology inherited from ω\omega. Specifically, we know using [10, III.2.3.6] that there exists a separated ring filtration ww on the group algebra ℤp​[G]\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G] such that
•
ww restricts to the pp-adic valuation vpv_{p} on ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p},
•
w⁡(g−1)>1w(g-1)>1 for all g∈Gg\in G,
•
the associated graded ring grw​ℤp​[G]\mathrm{gr}_{w}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G] is isomorphic to the enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra grω​(G)\mathrm{gr}_{\omega}(G), and
•
ΛG\Lambda_{G} arises as the topological completion of ℤp​[G]\mathbb{Z}_{p}[G] with respect to ww.
Therefore, we can of course extend ww to ΛG\Lambda_{G}. Moreover, ww extends further to the rational Iwasawa algebra ℚp​G=ℚp⊗ℤpΛG\mathbb{Q}_{p}G=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Lambda_{G}, where we define w⁡(p−m⊗r):=w⁡(r)−mw(p^{-m}\otimes r):=w(r)-m. However, ℚp​G\mathbb{Q}_{p}G is no longer complete with respect to ww, so we can pass to the completion ℚp​G^\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G} of ℚp​G\mathbb{Q}_{p}G with respect to ww.
Now, inside ΛG\Lambda_{G}, w⁡(g−1)>1w(g-1)>1, so w⁡((g−1)n)≥n≥vp​(n)=w⁡(n)w((g-1)^{n})\geq n\geq v_{p}(n)=w(n), so it follows that the sequence ((−1)n+1n​(g−1)n)\left(\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}(g-1)^{n}\right) converges to 0 in ℚp​G\mathbb{Q}_{p}G, and hence the logarithm series log⁡(g):=∑n∈ℕ​(−1)n+1n​(g−1)n\log(g):=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sum}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}(g-1)^{n} converges in ℚp​G^\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}.
Now, if ω\omega is a saturated pp-valuation, we define the ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie lattice of GG to be the set
log⁡(G):={log⁡(g):g∈G}⊆ℚp​G^\log(G):=\{\log(g):g\in G\}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G}
Theorem 2.8.
[10, IV.3.2.3]
For any pp-saturable group GG, log⁡(G)\log(G) is a free ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie subalgebra of ℚp​G^\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G} of finite rank, equal to the rank of GG. Moreover, for any g,h∈Gg,h\in G, λ∈ℤp\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}:
•
λ​log⁡(g)=log⁡(gλ)\lambda\log(g)=\log(g^{\lambda})
•
log⁡(g)+log⁡(h)=log⁡(limn→∞​(gpn​hpn)p−n)\log(g)+\log(h)=\log\left(\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}{\left(g^{p^{n}}h^{p^{n}}\right)^{p^{-n}}}\right)
•
[log⁡(g),log⁡(h)]=log⁡(limn→∞​(gpn,hpn)p−2​n)[\log(g),\log(h)]=\log\left(\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}{\left(g^{p^{n}},h^{p^{n}}\right)^{p^{-2n}}}\right)
In fact, the functor G↦log⁡(G)G\mapsto\log(G) defines an equivalence (and in fact isomorphism) of categories between the category of pp-saturable groups and a certain category of free ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie algebras known as saturable Lie algebras [10, IV.3.2.6]. This is known as Lazard’s equivalence of categories.
Example 2.9.
If GG is a uniform pro-pp group, then if we let ℒ:=log⁡(G)\mathcal{L}:=\log(G), then ℒ\mathcal{L} is a powerful ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie algebra, i.e. [ℒ,ℒ]⊆p​ℒ[\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}]\subseteq p\mathcal{L}.
Moreover, the logarithm functor restricts to an equivalence of categories between the category of uniform pro-pp groups and the category of powerful ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie algebras of finite rank.
Lazard’s equivalence will prove useful when looking for examples of pp-valuable groups, since it is practically much easier to start with a powerful ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie algebra and pass to its image under the inverse functor exp\exp rather than look for examples directly.
Finally, we define the Lie algebra of a pp-valuable group GG to be the ℚp\mathbb{Q}_{p}-Lie algebra
𝔤G=L​i​e​(G):=ℚp⊗ℤplog⁡(S​a​t​(G))\mathfrak{g}_{G}=Lie(G):=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\log(Sat(G))
Unfortunately, the functor G→𝔤GG\to\mathfrak{g}_{G} is no kind of equivalence of categories, and there are plenty of examples of non-isomorphic groups G,HG,H such that 𝔤G=𝔤H\mathfrak{g}_{G}=\mathfrak{g}_{H} (for example, if HH is any open subgroup of GG). But note that whenever GG is pp-saturable, log⁡(G)\log(G) is a full rank ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}-Lie lattice in 𝔤G\mathfrak{g}_{G}.
Remark 2.10.
If GG is not pp-saturable, then log⁡(G)⊆ℚp​G^\log(G)\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}G} need not be closed under addition or the Lie bracket. Still, we can embed log⁡(G)\log(G) as a subset of log⁡(S​a​t​(G))\log(Sat(G)), so it will still span 𝔤G\mathfrak{g}_{G} as a ℚp\mathbb{Q}_{p}-vector space.
Lemma 2.11.
For any pp-valuable group GG, the map H↦𝔤HH\mapsto\mathfrak{g}_{H} defines an inclusion preserving bijection between the set of closed, isolated normal subgroups of GG and the set of ideals of 𝔤G\mathfrak{g}_{G}.∎
2.3 Right ideals and controlling subgroups
Let GG be a pp-valuable group and let II be a right ideal of 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G (or K​GKG). We say that a subgroup HH of GG controls II if II is generated by a subset of 𝒪​H\mathcal{O}H (or K​HKH), i.e. I=(I∩𝒪​H)​𝒪​GI=(I\cap\mathcal{O}H)\mathcal{O}G (or I=(I∩K​H)​K​GI=(I\cap KH)KG).
Lemma 2.12.
There exist mutually inverse bijections
{prime ideals of ​𝒪​G​ containing ​p}\textstyle{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{prime ideals of }\mathcal{O}G\text{ containing }p\\
\end{array}\right\}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}Φ\scriptstyle{\Phi}Ψ\scriptstyle{\Psi}{prime ideals of ​K​G}\textstyle{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{prime ideals of }KG\\
\end{array}\right\}}}
where Φ⁡(Q):=K⊗𝒪Q\Phi(Q):=K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Q and Ψ⁡(P):=P∩𝒪​G\Psi(P):=P\cap\mathcal{O}G. Moreover, Φ​(Q)†=Q†\Phi(Q)^{\dagger}=Q^{\dagger}, and PP is controlled by a closed, normal subgroup HH of GG if and only if Φ⁡(P)\Phi(P) is controlled by HH.
Proof.
It is straightforward to show that every two-sided ideal II of K​GKG can be written as K⊗𝒪JK\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}J, where J=I∩𝒪​GJ=I\cap\mathcal{O}G, and that II is prime if and only if JJ is prime.
On the other hand, if QQ is a prime ideal of 𝒪​G\mathcal{O}G not containing pp, then let P:=K⊗𝒪QP:=K\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}Q. If GG is a pp-valuable group and 𝔤=𝔤G\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{G} is a positive nilpotent subalgebra of a split simple algebraic Lie algebra of type CnC_{n}, then B⁡(G)=A⁡(G)B(G)=A(G) has rank (n+12)\binom{n+1}{2}, while Z⁡(G)Z(G) has rank 1.
Proof.
We know 𝔤A⁡(G)\mathfrak{g}_{A(G)} is an abelian ideal of 𝔤\mathfrak{g} and B⁡(𝔤)=𝔤B⁡(𝔤)⊆𝔤A⁡(G)B(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}_{B(\mathfrak{g})}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{A(G)}. But 𝔤B⁡(𝔤)\mathfrak{g}_{B(\mathfrak{g})} is a maximal abelian ideal of dimension (n+12)\binom{n+1}{2} by Lemma 4.10, so it follows that 𝔤B⁡(𝔤)=𝔤A⁡(𝔤)\mathfrak{g}_{B(\mathfrak{g})}=\mathfrak{g}_{A(\mathfrak{g})}, and hence A⁡(G)=B⁡(G)A(G)=B(G), and they both have rank (n+12)\binom{n+1}{2}.
But Z⁡(𝔤)=𝔤Z⁡(G)=sθZ(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}_{Z(G)}=s_{\theta} has dimension 1, so Z⁡(G)Z(G) has rank 1.∎
So the B⁡(G)B(G) method affords no improvement if we assume that GG has type CC, but in light of Corollary 4.9, we know that it produces solid results in all other types. The only question remaining is, could a result such as this could be proved using the original A⁡(G)A(G)-method?
The short answer to this is no, because there are examples of groups GG given by Corollary 4.9 such A⁡(G)A(G) is strictly larger than the centre, so the original control theorem (Theorem 3.4) gives us a strictly weaker result.
For example, if GG is the group of unipotent, strictly upper triangular n×nn\times n matrices over ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}, then 𝔤G\mathfrak{g}_{G} is a positive nilpotent subalgebra of type An−1A_{n-1}, but A⁡(G)A(G) consists of all matrices of the form (IkM0In−k)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{k}&M\\
0&I_{n-k}\end{array}\right) where k=⌊n2⌋k=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor. If n>3n>3 then this is never central, so Theorem 3.4 does not imply our Conjecture A for this group, and we need to apply Corollary 4.9.
The following result generalises this example.

4.4 Obstacles to a complete proof
While the methods outlined in this paper allow us to establish our Conjecture A for a wide range of new examples, they are still not sufficient to prove it in full generality, because there are still many examples of nilpotent groups GG such that B⁡(G)≠Z⁡(G)B(G)\neq Z(G), as demonstrated by Corollary 4.11, and also the following result.
Lemma 4.13.
Suppose GG contains a closed, isolated, abelian normal subgroup HH such that G/H≅ℤpG/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}. If GG has nilpotency class at least 3 then B⁡(G)=A⁡(G)=HB(G)=A(G)=H and B⁡(G)≠Z⁡(G)B(G)\neq Z(G).
Proof.
We will show that HH satisfies (

Suppose g∈Gg\in G and (g,(g,H))=1(g,(g,H))=1. If g∈Hg\in H then (g,H)=1(g,H)=1 since HH is abelian, so assume for contradiction that g∉Hg\notin H. Then since G/H≅ℤpG/H\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}, we know that gg and HH generate an open normal subgroup UU of GG. To complete a proof of Conjecture A for nilpotent groups in general, it would be enough to establish that it holds for groups of the form ℤpd⋊ℤp\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}.
We will conclude with some further examples, not arising from Lie theory and not covered by the previous cases.
