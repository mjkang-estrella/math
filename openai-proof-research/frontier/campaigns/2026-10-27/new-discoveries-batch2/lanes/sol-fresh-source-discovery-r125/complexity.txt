 Abstract 
    
 The mutual-visibility chromatic number of a graph  G G  is the smallest number of colors needed to color the vertices of  G G  such that each color class is a mutual-visibility set. In this paper, we prove that determining the mutual-visibility chromatic number of a graph is NP-complete even when restricted to the class of graphs having diameter four and mutual-visibility chromatic number two. We further determine the exact value of the mutual-visibility chromatic number for glued binary trees and glued  t t -ary trees. 
  
 
 
 Keywords:  Mutual-visibility number, mutual-visibility chromatic number, computational complexity, glued binary tree, glued  t t -ary tree

 1  Introduction 

 
 The  no-three-in-line problem  is a classical problem in discrete geometry known from games in various cultures since ancient times  [ 13 ,  24 ] . It has been a subject of mathematical study in combinatorics after Dudeney’s chessboard puzzle which was proposed in the early twentieth century  [ 10 ] . This problem was later generalized to larger grids and planes  [ 12 ,  18 ,  19 ,  20 ] . Graph theoretic version of this problem was introduced independently in  2016 2016  and  2018 2018  under the names  geodesic irredundant sets   [ 6 ]  and  general position sets   [ 17 ] , respectively. Later, the graph theoretic version of the problem is popular under the name general position problem which has been recently surveyed in  [ 5 ] , see also  [ 1 ,  15 ,  16 ,  21 ] . Subsequently, several variations of this problem emerged  [ 22 ] , among which the most extensively studied is the  mutual-visibility  variant, first introduced by Di Stefano in  2022 2022   [ 9 ] . A set  S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G)  is mutually visible if for every  x , y ∈ S x,y\in S , there exists a shortest  x − y x-y  path with no internal vertices in  S S . Beyond its application in computer science, particularly in robot visibility, the mutual-visibility problem connects to combinatorial problems such as Zarankiewicz and Turan problems  [ 7 ,  8 ,  11 ] . 
 
 
 In  2024 2024 , Sandi Klavžar et al. introduced a vertex coloring of graphs in which each color class is a mutual-visibility set  [ 14 ] . The  mutual-visibility coloring  is defined as follows: two vertices  x , y ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) x,y\in V(G)  can be given the same color if there exists an  x − y x-y  geodesic path whose internal vertices have colors different from that of  x x  and  y y . The smallest number of colors required for such a coloring of  G G  is called  mutual-visibility chromatic number , denoted by  χ μ ​ ( G ) \chi_{\mu}(G) . Following this, vertex coloring based on general position sets  [ 4 ] , independent mutual-visibility sets  [ 3 ]  and distance mutual-visibility sets  [ 2 ]  have also been investigated. 
 
 
 It is proved in  [ 4 ]  that the general position problem is NP-complete even when restricted to the class of graphs with diameter two and general position number three. The concept of independent general position coloring is also introduced and proved to be NP-complete even when restricted to the class of graphs with diameter at most three. In  [ 14 ] , computing the computational complexity of mutual-visibility chromatic number is left as an interesting problem for further investigation. The decision problem arising from the independent version of mutual-visibility coloring  [ 3 ]  is proved to be NP-complete even in graphs with universal vertices and the authors conjectured that mutual-visibility coloring is NP-complete. 
 
 
 A  perfect  t t -ary tree ,  T r , t T_{r,t}  of depth  r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 , is a rooted tree in which all non-leaf vertices have  t t  children, and all leaves have depth  r r . It has  t r + 1 − 1 t − 1 \frac{t^{r+1}-1}{t-1}  vertices and  t r t^{r}  leaves. For  t ≥ 2 t\geq 2  and  r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 , a  glued  t t -ary tree ,  G ​ T ​ ( r , t ) GT(r,t)  is obtained from two copies of  T r , t T_{r,t}  by pairwise identifying their leaves  [ 15 ] . The vertices obtained by identification are called  quasi-leaves  of  G ​ T ​ ( r , t ) GT(r,t)  and the set of quasi-leaves of  G ​ T ​ ( r , t ) GT(r,t)  will be denoted by  L ⁡ ( G ​ T ​ ( r , t ) ) L(GT(r,t)) . For a vertex  u u  in  G ​ T ​ ( r , t ) GT(r,t) ,  T u T_{u}  denotes the tree rooted in  u u  and having as leaves all the descendants of  u u  that are quasi-leaves. 
 
 
 The perfect  2 2 -ary and glued  2 2 -ary trees are called perfect binary trees and glued binary trees  [ 17 ] , respectively. We will simplify the notation by setting  G ​ T ​ ( r ) = G ​ T ​ ( r , 2 ) GT(r)=GT(r,2) . We will denote the two copies of  T r , 2 T_{r,2} , from which  G ​ T ​ ( r ) GT(r)  is constructed, by  T r ( 1 ) T_{r}^{(1)}  and  T r ( 2 ) T_{r}^{(2)} , so that  L ⁡ ( G ​ T ​ ( r ) ) = V ⁡ ( T r ( 1 ) ) ∩ V ⁡ ( T r ( 2 ) ) L(GT(r))=V(T_{r}^{(1)})\cap V(T_{r}^{(2)}) . Moreover, we set  V r ( 1 ) = V ⁡ ( T 1 ( 1 ) ) \ L ⁡ ( G ​ T ​ ( r ) ) V_{r}^{(1)}=V(T_{1}^{(1)})\backslash L(GT(r))  and  V r ( 2 ) = V ⁡ ( T 1 ( 2 ) ) \ L ⁡ ( G ​ T ​ ( r ) ) V_{r}^{(2)}=V(T_{1}^{(2)})\backslash L(GT(r)) . 
 
 
 In this paper, the mutual-visibility coloring problem is proved to be NP-complete settling the conjecture. In view of its computational complexity, it is natural to study this parameter in specific graph classes. In the last section we determine the exact values of mutual-visibility coloring for glued binary trees and extend it further the glued  t t -ary trees.

 To study the computational complexity of finding a mutual-visibility coloring of a graph that uses the smallest possible number of colors, we introduce the following decision problem. 
 
 
 Definition 2.1 .

 Instance : A graph  G G , a positive integer  k ≤ | V ⁡ ( G ) | k\leq|V(G)| .

 Lemma 2.2 . 
 
 In a mutual-visibility coloring of  H n H_{n}  with two colors, vertices  c c  and  p p  ( c ′ c^{\prime}  and  p ′ p^{\prime} , respectively) cannot be colored with the same color. In the set of vertices  { p 1 , p 2 , … , p n } \{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\} , at least two vertices are colored with different colors. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Consider a mutual-visibility coloring of  H n H_{n}  using two colors, say red and white. If a pendant vertex and its support vertex are given the same color, then that color cannot be used anywhere else in the graph. Therefore, if  p p  and  c c  (equivalently,  p ′ p^{\prime}  and  c ′ c^{\prime} ) are given the same color, then we require at least three colors to color  H n H_{n} . Suppose vertices  p p  and  c c  are colored red. Therefore,  p p  and  c c  (equivalently,  p ′ p^{\prime}  and  c ′ c^{\prime} ) must be of different colors. Without loss of generality assume that  p p  is white and  c c  is red. Since at least one among  c ′ c^{\prime}  and  p ′ p^{\prime}  is white (equivalently, red), at least one among  { p 1 , p 2 , … , p n } \{p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\}  must be red (equivalently, white) for mutual-visibility. Hence, the lemma.

 Theorem 2.3 . 
 
 MV-coloring  is NP-complete even for instances  ( G , k ) (G,k)  with  diam ( G ) = 4 \mathop{\mathrm{diam}}(G)=4  and  k = 2 k=2 . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Given a coloring of  G G , it is possible to test in polynomial time whether it is a mutual-visibility coloring or not. Consequently, the problem is in NP. 
 
 
 We prove that the  NAE3-SAT  problem, defined below and shown to be NP-complete in  [ 23 ] , polynomially reduces to  MV-coloring . 
 
 A  NAE3-SAT  ( not-all-equal 3-satisfiability ) instance  Φ \Phi  is defined as a set  X = { x 1 , x 2 , … , x q } X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{q}\}  of  q q  Boolean variables and a set  C C  of  r r  clauses, each defined as a set of three literals: every variable  x i x_{i}  corresponds to two literals, that is,  x i x_{i}  (the positive form) and  x ¯ i \bar{x}_{i}  (the negative form). To simplify the notations we will denote by  { ℓ 1 , ℓ 2 , ℓ 3 } \{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\}  the clause with literals  ℓ i , i ∈ [ 3 ] \ell_{i},i\in[3] , without distinction between the orders in which they are listed. A truth assignment assigns a Boolean value ( T ​ r ​ u ​ e True  or  F ​ a ​ l ​ s ​ e False ) to each variable, corresponding to a truth assignment of opposite values for the two literals  x i x_{i}  and  x i ¯ \bar{x_{i}} :  x i ¯ \bar{x_{i}}  is  T ​ r ​ u ​ e True  if and only if  x i x_{i}  is  F ​ a ​ l ​ s ​ e False . 
 The  NAE3-SAT  problem asks whether there is a truth assignment to the variables such that in no clause all three literals have the same truth value. We will say that such an assignment is  satisfying  and the instance  Φ \Phi  is  satisfied . 
 
 
 
 In what follows we assume that in any instance of  NAE3-SAT  each clause involves three different variables. Indeed, if a clause involves a single variable and all the literals are equal, then the  NAE3-SAT  problem has a No answer and if the positive and negative form of a variable are present in a clause we can remove the clause from the instance.
If a clause involves only two variables, and the variable appearing two times in the clauses is present in the same form, e.g.  { l 1 , l 1 , l 2 } \{l_{1},l_{1},l_{2}\} , then the clause is logically equivalent to  { l 1 , l 2 } \{l_{1},l_{2}\}  and it can be substituted with two clauses  { l 1 , l 2 , a } \{l_{1},l_{2},a\}  and  { l 1 , l 2 , a ¯ } \{l_{1},l_{2},\bar{a}\} , where  a a  is an additional variable.

 4  Concluding Remarks 

 
 In this paper we prove the NP-completeness of the mutual-visibility coloring problem, which was left as an open question in  [ 14 ] , reinforcing the theoretical challenge in determining the mutual-visibility chromatic number of arbitrary graphs and providing strong motivation to find the exact value and tight bounds for specific graph classes. We also determine the exact value of glued binary trees and glued  t t -ary trees. Several natural directions remains open for future investigation. In particular finding the mutual-visibility chromatic number of highly symmetric or self similar structures such as the hypercubes, hypercube-like graphs, the Hamming graphs and the generalized Sierpiński graphs, including Sierpiński triangle graphs and Sierpiński gasket graphs are interesting.
