Abstract.
    

Let Φn​(q)\Phi_{n}(q) denote the qq-deformation (in the sense of Morier-Genoud & Ovsienko) of the metallic number ϕn=n+n2+42\phi_{n}=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}. This is a power series with integer coefficients. We prove that the Hankel
continued fraction of the (n+2)(n{+}2)-shifted series Φn(n+2)\Phi_{n}^{(n+2)} is
eventually periodic, and that the corresponding Hankel determinant sequence
Δ(n+2)\Delta^{(n+2)} is 2​n​(n+1)2n(n+1)-periodic if nn is even, 2​n​(n+1)2n(n+1)-antiperiodic if nn is odd, and that it takes only the values
−2,−1,0,1,2-2,-1,0,1,2. This confirms part of a former conjecture of ours and complements similar results we obtained for all ℓ\ell-shifts of Φn\Phi_{n} with 0⩽ℓ⩽n+10\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1. In addition, we formulate a new conjecture of quasi-polynomiality for higher shifts ℓ⩾n+3\ell\geqslant n+3.
    

The determinant values arise as telescoping products of the partial numerators of
the Hankel continued fraction. Most of the technically involved and lengthy
verifications are carried out by a symbolic computation program, whose code is
publicly available on GitHub for independent verification.
  




Key words and phrases: Shifted Hankel determinants, Hankel continued fractions, metallic numbers,
qq-analogues, quadratic irrational series

1. Introduction




The so-called metallic numbers (or metallic ratios, or metallic means) are the quadratic irrationals






(1.1)

ϕn:=n+n2+42=n+1n+1n+1n+⋯=n+(1n+)∗(n⩾1).\phi_{n}:=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}=n+\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\mathord{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$\cdots$}}=n+\left(\frac{1}{n}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\right)^{*}\qquad(n\geqslant 1).



In particular, ϕ1=1+52\phi_{1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} is the golden ratio. Here and thereafter, we use the following classical notation for infinite continued fractions:




α0β0+α1β1+α2β2+⋯:=α0β0+α1β1+α2β2+⋯\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\cdots\quad:=\quad\cfrac{\alpha_{0}}{\beta_{0}+\cfrac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}+\cfrac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}+\cdots}}}



and indicate periodic data between parentheses and with a superscript ∗*.




Thanks to S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko’s fascinating theory of qq-deformed real numbers (see [MGO20, MGO22] for the foundations, [MGO25] for a survey of its numerous occurrences in various branches of mathematics and [Ped26, Eti25, EO26] for recent developments on analytical aspects of qq-numbers), one can attach to any metallic ratio ϕn\phi_{n} an algebraic continued fraction [ϕn]q[\phi_{n}]_{q} called the qq-deformation of ϕn\phi_{n}.




By definition, [ϕn]q[\phi_{n}]_{q} is the following quantisation of the continued fraction (1.1):



(1.2)

[ϕn]q:=[n]q+(qn[n]q−1+q−n[n]q+)∗[\phi_{n}]_{q}:=[n]_{q}+\left(\frac{q^{n}}{[n]_{q^{-1}}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\frac{q^{-n}}{[n]_{q}}\,\mathrel{\raisebox{0.83333pt}{$+$}}\,\right)^{*}



where the polynomial [n]q[n]_{q} stands for the classical Euler-Gauss qq-deformation of the integer nn:




[n]q:=1+q+q2+⋯+qn−1.[n]_{q}:=1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1}.



As expected when speaking of quantisation, letting q→1q\to 1 in (1.2) gives back expression (1.1) of ϕn\phi_{n}.
These algebraic continued fractions [ϕn]q[\phi_{n}]_{q} are called the qq-metallic numbers.




A key fact of the qq-deformation theory is that there exists a sequence of integers (κl​(ϕn))l⩾0(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n}))_{l\geqslant 0}, such that




[ϕn]q=∑l=0+∞κl​(ϕn)​ql,[\phi_{n}]_{q}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})q^{l},



i.e. [ϕn]q[\phi_{n}]_{q} expands about q=0q=0 as a formal power series in ℤ⁡[[q]]\mathbb{Z}[[q]]. Actually, this is a true power series expansion (with non-zero radius of convergence) when qq is a complex variable, see [Eti25, EO26].
To view [ϕn]q[\phi_{n}]_{q} as a function of qq we will use the following notation



(1.3)

Φn​(q)=[ϕn]q=∑l=0+∞κl​(ϕn)​ql\Phi_{n}(q)=[\phi_{n}]_{q}=\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})q^{l}



and still call the function Φn=Φn​(q)\Phi_{n}=\Phi_{n}(q) the nn-th qq-metallic number.




The qq-metallic numbers are studied in depth in the articles [OP25], [HP25], [Ped26]. They reveal strong connections between the sequence of coefficients (κl​(ϕn))l⩾0\bigl(\kappa_{l}(\phi_{n})\bigr)_{l\geqslant 0} in (1.3) and three highly famous sequences of the combinatorial world: the Catalan numbers, the Motzkin numbers and the enumeration of secondary structures of RNA molecules. These connections concern mainly the analytical properties of the sequences and their Hankel determinants.

All these results are based on the expansion of the functions Φn(ℓ)\Phi_{n}^{(\ell)} (for 0⩽ℓ⩽n+10\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1) as a special type of continued fraction introduced in [Han16], called Hankel continued fraction, whose partial numerators determine all the Hankel
determinants of the series; see Section 3 for the precise
definition.




The main purpose of this paper is to treat the case ℓ=n+2\ell=n+2, technically much more difficult than for the lower shifts 0⩽ℓ⩽n+10\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1, and to make a precise conjecture as concerns the shifts ℓ⩾n+3\ell\geqslant n+3, which behave in a
completely different way (see below).

Strategy and organisation of the paper.




To establish Theorem A we use the
approach developed in [Han16]. The main tool is an algorithm NextABC
which builds the Hankel continued fraction (HH-fraction, for short) of a
quadratic power series one step at a time.
This is explained in Section 2, together with the quadratic equation verified by the shifted power series Φn(n+2)\Phi_{n}^{(n+2)} of the qq-deformed metallic ratio (Proposition 2.7).




Section 3 is devoted to the construction of the HH-fraction of
Φn(n+2)\Phi_{n}^{(n+2)} and to the proof of Theorem A; this is the most
technical part of the paper. At each step mm, the algorithm NextABC
produces a quintuple (Am,Bm,Cm,Km,Dm)(A_{m},B_{m},C_{m};K_{m},D_{m}) of polynomials, from which the
mm-th partial numerator and denominator of the HH-fraction are read off. It
turns out that the quintuple at index pn+2p_{n}+2 coincides with the quintuple at
index 22, and this gives the periodicity. But the law which governs the
quintuples is complicated, and not even uniform in mm: for n⩾4n\geqslant 4, the
quintuples belong either to two families, called passes, made of
repeating blocks of nine formulas (one for each residue of mm modulo 99),
or to three short lists of quintuples, called bridges, which occur only
once per period. These explicit lists, the pass-tables, are given in
the appendices, and we prove that they are correct. Because of the size of
the expressions, this proof uses SageMath programs, which certify the
formulas symbolically, uniformly in nn and mm. The small cases n=1,2,3n=1,2,3 are
simpler and are treated separately.




In Section 4 we prove Theorem B by applying
[Han16, Theorem 2.1] (recalled as Theorem 2.2 below), which
expresses the Hankel determinants of a power series in terms of the partial
numerators of its HH-fraction. These partial numerators are known from the
pass-tables, but some care is needed to show that all the nonzero
determinants belong to {−2,−1,1,2}\{-2,-1,1,2\} and to determine the sign of the
(anti)periodicity.




Finally, Appendices A–G list the
explicit tables for the 2 passes and the 3 bridges that are used in Section 3 to prove the periodicity of the Hankel continued fraction of Φn(n+2)\Phi_{n}^{(n+2)}.
