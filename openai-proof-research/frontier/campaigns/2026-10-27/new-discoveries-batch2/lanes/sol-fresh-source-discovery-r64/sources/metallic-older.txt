Abstract.
    

Fix nn a positive integer. Take the nn-th metallic number
    




ϕn=n+n2+42\phi_{n}=\frac{n+\sqrt{n^{2}+4}}{2}


    

(e.g. ϕ1\phi_{1} is the golden number) and let Φn​(q)\Phi_{n}(q) be its qq-deformation in the sense of S. Morier-Genoud & V. Ovsienko. This is an algebraic continued fraction which admits an expansion into a Taylor series Φn​(q)=∑i=0+∞fi​qi\Phi_{n}(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}f_{i}q^{i} around q=0q=0, with integral coefficients. Consider the sequences of shifted
Hankel determinants of Φn\Phi_{n}:
    




Δj(ℓ):=det(fa+b+ℓ)a,b=0j−1,ℓ,j⩾0.\Delta_{j}^{(\ell)}:=\det(f_{a+b+\ell})_{a,b=0}^{j-1},\quad\ell,j\geqslant 0.


    

By using the notion of Hankel continued fraction introduced by the first author in [Han16] we determine explicitly the first n+2n+2 sequences Δj(0),Δj(1),…,Δj(n+1)\Delta_{j}^{(0)},\Delta_{j}^{(1)},\dots,\Delta_{j}^{(n+1)} and show that they satisfy the following properties:
    

1) 



They are periodic and consist of −1,0,1-1,0,1 only.

2) 



They satisfy a three-term Gale-Robinson recurrence, i.e. they form discrete integrable dynamical systems.

3) 



They are all completely determined by the first sequence Δj(0)\Delta_{j}^{(0)}.


    

This article thus validates a conjecture formulated by V. Ovsienko and the second author in [OP25] and establishes new connections between qq-deformations of real numbers and sequences of Catalan or Motzkin numbers.
  




Key words and phrases: Hankel determinants, Hankel continued fractions, metallic numbers, golden ratio, qq-analogues, quadratic irrationals, Gale-Robinson sequences.

First appeared in 1861 [H61], Hankel matrices and determinants (and later on, Hankel and Toeplitz operators) are this kind of objects that arise naturally in various fields of mathematics and often interconnect them: combinatorics [Rad79, Aig99, Tam01, CK11], orthogonal polynomials [Vie83, Jun03, DIK11, BP17], number theory [Wal48, APWW98, Bug11], probability theory [Sch17, BDJ06], complex analysis [Hen74, Pom66, KLS18], functional and applied analysis [Pel03, Sau21]… (among many other valuable references).




In this article we would like to present the remarkable properties of Hankel determinants of a family of power series Φn\Phi_{n} (n∈ℤ⩾1n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}) that are defined as “qq-deformations”, in the sense of S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko (to be specified a bit later), of the so-called metallic numbers, or metallic ratios, or metallic means. Recall that these numbers are the quadratic irrationals

Let us say a word about the proofs of (1.13), (1.14) and (1.15).
Usual techniques to compute Hankel determinants of classical sequences (such as Catalan or Motzkin) rely mostly on continued fraction expansions, orthogonal polynomials theory and/or combinatorial models; see e.g. [Hei46, Kra99, Kra05, Aig01, Bar10, Elo15, BP17]…
In particular, one may think that continued fraction expansions like (1.11) can help to compute the Hankel sequences, since there exists a well-known Hankel determinant formula for regular CC-fractions (also known as Stieltjes continued fractions) and for regular JJ-fractions (also known as Jacobi continued fractions); see [Kra05] §5.4. Unfortunately, it is not possible to expand Φ1\Phi_{1} (nor any Φn\Phi_{n}, actually) as such special continued fractions.
But, in [OP25] as well as in the present work, Hankel determinant formulas are obtained by using the notion of Hankel continued fractions introduced by the first author in [Han16]. These form a family of continued fractions which includes both classical regular JJ-fractions and regular CC-fractions as subcases, and still conveniently comes with an explicit Hankel determinant formula; see Section 2.2 for details.




For instance, it was proved in [OP25] (Lemmas 3.6 and 3.9) that the HH-fraction of the qq-golden number Φ1\Phi_{1} is the 33-periodic continued fraction

Organization of the paper.
Section 2 contains a brief presentation of S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko’s theory of qq-deformation of real numbers, applied in particular to metallic ratios. We recall also from [Han16] some fundamental results on Hankel continued fractions (alias HH-fractions), since it is the main tool we will use to compute Hankel determinants. We also explain how close Hankel continued fractions are to Artin’s regular continued fractions in the context of Laurent series.




In Section 3 we prove Theorem A i.e. the HH-fraction expansion of Φn\Phi_{n}. It will be obtained by using an algorithm well suited to power series that are solution of a quadratic equation with polynomial coefficients. This algorithm appeared first in the paper [Han16] and its application to our qq-metallic numbers Φn\Phi_{n} represents a quite technical work.




Theorems B and C will be obtained with the following strategy: instead of proving them directly for all values of the parameter ℓ\ell, we note that cases 1⩽ℓ⩽n+11\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1 follow at once from the case ℓ=0\ell=0 and Theorem D. This is why Section 4 is devoted to the proof of Theorem B in the case ℓ=0\ell=0 only. The main tool here is the Hankel determinant formula for HH-fractions recalled in Section 2.
It is worth noting that Theorem B will be established without calculating explicitly the Hankel determinants, since for showing periodicity and specifying the image set we need to understand more the pattern they form than their exact values.




Yet, computing the Hankel determinants Δj=Δj(0)​(Φn)\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{j}^{(0)}(\Phi_{n}) is inevitable for our other results and it is done in Section 5. We exploit the (anti-)periodicity shown by Theorem B together with some extra symmetry property to restrict ourselves to calculate the values of Δj\Delta_{j} only for a half-period (more or less), and we obtain explicit formulas similar to the ones in (1.14). They are presented in Theorem 5.1.




From these exact values of the Hankel determinants we then derive their Gale-Robinson dynamics and prove Theorem C in Section 6, still in the case ℓ=0\ell=0. This is an elementary but quite tedious task.




Finally, Section 7 is devoted to shifted Hankel determinants Δj(ℓ)\Delta_{j}^{(\ell)} with ℓ⩾1\ell\geqslant 1. We start by proving Theorem 7.3 which expresses the HH-fraction expansion of the shifted functions Φn(ℓ)\Phi_{n}^{(\ell)} (1⩽ℓ⩽n+11\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1) in terms of the one given in (1.20) for the initial function Φn=Φn(0)\Phi_{n}=\Phi_{n}^{(0)}. This result interconnects the HH-fractions expressions of all functions Φn(ℓ)\Phi_{n}^{(\ell)}, 0⩽ℓ⩽n+10\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1 (see Corollary 7.5) and leads to the proof of Theorem D which, as noticed before, implies eventually the statements of Theorems B and C in the remaining cases 1⩽ℓ⩽n+11\leqslant\ell\leqslant n+1. In this section we also prove Theorem F.




Let us add a comment to our presentation. Although the cases of Φ1\Phi_{1} and Φ2\Phi_{2} were treated in [OP25] with much detail and very explicit formulas, they do not really represent the general case Φn\Phi_{n} which is more complex in nature. Moreover, our proofs are quite technical and lengthy. We thus think it is worthwhile to illustrate our present results on another concrete case, more representative of the general case but not too complicated either. We thus have chosen to focus on the case n=5n=5 throughout the paper to examplify the main results and the more technical proofs.




Note. Since all results stated in this article were proved in the cases n=1n=1 and n=2n=2 in [OP25], from now on it will be convenient to always assume that n⩾3n\geqslant 3, in order to avoid special discussions for the limit case n=2n=2 in some results.

2. Background material



2.1. S. Morier-Genoud and V. Ovsienko’s qq-numbers




Our article is devoted to the continuous fraction expansion and Hankel determinant properties of the qq-deformation of particular real numbers. Let us define precisely what kind of quantization is meant here and give a brief introduction to the subject.




Quantization of integers is a very classical topic which goes back to Euler and Gauss: any integer n⩾0n\geqslant 0 can be deformed as a polynomial



(2.1)

[n]q:=1−qn1−q=1+q+q2+⋯+qn−1.[n]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1}.

A considerable amount of related objects have been based on definition (2.1), e.g. qq-factorials, qq-binomials, qq-hypergeometric functions, qq-calculus, etc. used in combinatorics, number theory, fractals, operator theory, mathematical physics… But until recently we missed a really satisfactory extension to more general numbers (reals, or even just rationals). 2.2. HH-fractions and their Hankel determinants




In this subsection we recall some material from [Han16]. Fix a field 𝔽\mathbb{F}, an indeterminate qq, and let δ\delta be a positive integer. A super δ\delta-fraction is a continued fraction of the form

3. The Hankel continued fraction expansion for Φn\Phi_{n}




We already recalled the examples of HH-fractions (1.17) and (1.18) that were obtained for Φ1​(q)\Phi_{1}(q) and Φ2​(q)\Phi_{2}(q) in [OP25]. In this section we prove that the HH-fraction presentation for Φn\Phi_{n} stated in Theorem A is actually valid for all n⩾3n\geqslant 3. It is a quite difficult problem, and we will solve it by applying a variant of an algorithm which was introduced in [Han16]. Γj(ℓ):=Δj(ℓ)​Δj+2​n+2(ℓ)−Δj+1(ℓ)​Δj+2​n+1(ℓ)+(Δj+n+1(ℓ))2.\Gamma_{j}^{(\ell)}:=\Delta_{j}^{(\ell)}\,\Delta_{j+2n+2}^{(\ell)}-\Delta_{j+1}^{(\ell)}\,\Delta_{j+2n+1}^{(\ell)}+\bigl(\Delta_{j+n+1}^{(\ell)}\bigr)^{2}.



We want to prove the Gale-Robinson recurrence stated in Theorem C, which reads



(6.1)

Γj(ℓ)=0for all j⩾0 and all ℓ∈{0,1,…,n+1}.\Gamma_{j}^{(\ell)}=0\qquad\text{for all $j\geqslant 0$ and all $\ell\in\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\}$}.



Assuming the validity of Theorem D (to be proved in Section 7) it is easily checked that




Γj(ℓ+1)=Γj(ℓ),\Gamma_{j}^{(\ell+1)}=\Gamma_{j}^{(\ell)},



so that it suffices to prove (6.1) in the case ℓ=0\ell=0, and this will be our purpose in this section.




For simplicity we put Γj:=Γj(0)\Gamma_{j}:=\Gamma_{j}^{(0)}.
Because of the (anti-)periodicity property shown in Theorem B, it remains to show that



(6.2)

Γj=0for ​0⩽j⩽2​n​(n+1)−1,\Gamma_{j}=0\quad\text{for }0\leqslant j\leqslant 2n(n+1)-1,



where




Γj:=Δj​Δj+2​n+2−Δj+1​Δj+2​n+1+(Δj+n+1)2.\Gamma_{j}:=\Delta_{j}\,\Delta_{j+2n+2}-\Delta_{j+1}\,\Delta_{j+2n+1}+\bigl(\Delta_{j+n+1}\bigr)^{2}.



By Lemma 4.3 we know which Hankel determinants are nonzero and, by the previous theorem, we know the explicit values of these nonzero determinants, so we just have to check that Γj\Gamma_{j} vanishes in any case. Adding and multiplying five Hankel determinants Δi\Delta_{i} which can be either −1-1, 00 or 11 is certainly a triviality but the difficulty comes from the fact that we have to handle them simultaneously, and there is no clue of how their values are connected in general. In fact, we will distinguish four cases, looking at pairs of successive determinants (Δj,Δj+1)(\Delta_{j},\Delta_{j+1}) and discussing on the nullity of one of them, in order to simplify the expression of Γj\Gamma_{j} when possible. And, because (6.2) must be checked for j⩽2​n​(n+1)−1j\leqslant 2n(n+1)-1 we will have to discuss on Hankel determinants Δi\Delta_{i} with index




i=j+2​n+2⩽2​n​(n+1)−1+2​n+2=2​n​(n+2)+1,i=j+2n+2\leqslant 2n(n+1)-1+2n+2=2n(n+2)+1,



i.e. we need the description of the intersection 𝒮∩{0,1,…,2​n​(n+2)+1}\mathcal{S}\cap\{0,1,\ldots,2n(n+2)+1\}. One the one hand, Lemma 4.3 implies the characterization:



(6.3)

j∈𝒮​ and ​0⩽j⩽2​n​(n+1)⇔j​ satisfies one of the conditions (i)-(v)j\in\mathcal{S}\text{ and }0\leqslant j\leqslant 2n(n+1)\iff j\text{ satisfies one of the conditions (i)-(v)}



where

(i) 



j≡0​(mod​n+1)j\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n+1) and 0⩽j⩽2​n​(n+1)0\leqslant j\leqslant 2n(n+1)

(ii) 



j≡1​(mod​n+1)j\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n+1) and 1⩽j⩽n21\leqslant j\leqslant n^{2}

(iii) 



j≡n⁡(mod​n+1)j\equiv n\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n+1) and n+(n+1)2⩽j⩽n+(2​n−1)​(n+1)n+(n+1)^{2}\leqslant j\leqslant n+(2n-1)(n+1)

On the other hand, the j∈𝒮j\in\mathcal{S} such that 2​n​(n+1)⩽j⩽2​n​(n+2)+12n(n+1)\leqslant j\leqslant 2n(n+2)+1 can be found by Lemma 4.5: they are such that j−2​n​(n+1)∈𝒮j-2n(n+1)\in\mathcal{S} and characterization (6.3) can be applied to these translated numbers.
