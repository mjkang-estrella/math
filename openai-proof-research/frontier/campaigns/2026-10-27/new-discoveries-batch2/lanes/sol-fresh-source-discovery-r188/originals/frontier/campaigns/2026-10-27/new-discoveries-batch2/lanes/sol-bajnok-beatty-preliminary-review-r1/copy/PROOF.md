# Ordinary proof candidate: Bajnok–Beatty Conjecture 1.6

2026-10-29 UTC. Complete ordinary argument claimed below; independent mathematical and substantive-eligibility review **not performed**. Credit 0. No priority certification. In particular, a serious objection is that this is a short two-coordinate refinement of the source's fiber-counting proof. No Lean or manuscript is authorized at this stage.

## Exact endpoint and dependencies

Let s>=3 be an integer. Among all finite noncyclic abelian groups G with a two-element set {a,b} such that every g in G is xa+yb for integers |x|+|y|<=s, the largest order is

- 2s² if 2s+1 is prime;
- 2s²+2s-(p0²-1)/2 otherwise, where p0 is the least prime divisor of 2s+1.

This is precisely Conjecture 1.6 in Bajnok–Beatty, *On the diameter of undirected Cayley graphs of finite abelian groups*, Combinatorics and Number Theory 14 (2025), 1–12, DOI 10.2140/cnt.2025.14.1; actual publisher PDF saved as sources/bajnok-vor.pdf. No new claim about cyclic groups, other generator numbers, or Conjecture 4.1. We use the authors' already-proved lower constructions (Proposition 2.3, Theorem 2.4, Corollary 2.5), and the elementary rank-two structure theorem for finite abelian groups.

## 1. All residue classes in both coordinates

Write D_s={(x,y) in Z²: |x|+|y|<=s}. The homomorphism Z² -> G, (x,y)->xa+yb, is onto and D_s maps onto G. Because G is noncyclic and generated by two elements, there is a prime p for which G/pG is a two-dimensional F_p-vector space. For example, write G=Z_c x Z_ck with c>=2, and take any prime p|c. The images of a,b are a basis of G/pG. Thus each fixed coefficient residue (i,j) modulo p maps into one particular coset of pG. Conversely every element of that coset has a representative in D_s of that same coefficient residue. Each coset contains |G|/p² elements. Consequently

|G| <= p² min_{i,j in F_p} #{(x,y) in D_s: (x,y)=(i,j) mod p}.  (1)

This uses both coefficient residues. Counting just one linear combination, as in the source's Theorem 3.2, loses information.

## 2. The prime 2

By source Lemma 2.1(2), exactly s² points of D_s have x+y of parity opposite to s. These occupy two coefficient classes modulo 2. At least one of those classes contains <=floor(s²/2) points. Equation (1) gives |G|<=4 floor(s²/2)<=2s². This is sufficient for the endpoint.

## 3. Exact minimum for an odd prime

Set L=2s+1=qp+r, 0<=r<p. The integral rotation

u=x+y, v=x-y

is a bijection from D_s onto {(u,v): -s<=u,v<=s, u=v mod 2}. Modulo odd p the rotation is invertible. Let E_i and O_i count the even and odd integers, respectively, in [-s,s] congruent to i modulo p. Therefore the required minimum is

m_p(s)=min_{i,j}(E_i E_j+O_i O_j).  (2)

The p residue progressions in [-s,s] have lengths q or q+1. List their first terms as -s,-s+1,...,-s+p-1. The first r progressions have length q+1, the remaining p-r progressions length q. Since p is odd, parity alternates in each progression.

If q is even, each short progression has E=O=q/2. Every long progression has entries q/2 and q/2+1 in some order. All pairings in (2) are at least q²/2 and two short progressions attain it. Thus m_p(s)=q²/2.

If q is odd, each long progression has E=O=(q+1)/2. Each short one has entries (q+1)/2 and (q-1)/2. If p-r>=2, there are two short progressions with consecutive first terms, hence opposite imbalances. Their pairing gives (q²-1)/2, the smallest of every possible pairing in (2). If p-r=1, the unique short progression paired with itself gives (q²+1)/2; pairing it with a long progression gives q(q+1)/2, and pairing two long ones gives (q+1)²/2. The former is smallest (including equality when q=1). Therefore

m_p(s) = q²/2                           if q even;
         (q²-1)/2                      if q odd and r<=p-2;
         (q²+1)/2                      if q odd and r=p-1.   (3)

This also covers q=0.

## 4. Uniform bounds from (3)

If p divides L, then r=0 and q is odd, so (1) and (3) give

|G| <= (L²-p²)/2 = 2s²+2s-(p²-1)/2.    (4)

If p does not divide L, then r>=1. For even q, p²m_p=(L-r)²/2<=(L-1)²/2=2s². For odd q and r<=p-2, subtracting p² from the same square only strengthens this inequality.

It remains to consider q odd and r=p-1. Here L=(q+1)p-1. Direct subtraction gives

p²m_p - 2s² = -q p(p-2)+2p-2.

For p>=5 and q>=1 this is at most -p²+4p-2<0. For p=3 it equals 4-3q; q=1 would force L=5, s=2, outside the stated domain. Thus q>=3 and the expression is negative. We have proved

p not dividing L => |G|<=2s².           (5)

## 5. Full endpoint and attainment

Choose p from Section 1. If p=2 or p does not divide L, the bound is 2s². If L is prime, p|L would mean p=L, and (4) would give |G|<=0, impossible. Thus for prime L the universal bound is 2s².

For composite L with least prime divisor p0, any p|L has p>=p0, so (4) gives |G|<=(L²-p0²)/2. This also dominates 2s²: L>=3p0 gives 2s=L-1>=3p0-1, and more directly L>=p0² (because the least factor's complementary factor is >=p0), so 2s=L-1>=(p0²-1)/2. Thus all cases satisfy the conjectured composite bound.

For every s>=3, source Proposition 2.3 constructs an s-spanning pair in Z_s x Z_2s, of order 2s². If L is composite, put t=L/p0>=3 and k=(t²-1)/2. Source Corollary 2.5 constructs an s-spanning pair in Z_p0 x Z_(p0 k), of order p0²k=(L²-p0²)/2. These are noncyclic and attain the respective upper bounds. This proves the entire endpoint.

## Falsifiers and eligibility

A failure of the rank-two quotient transport, the exact minimum (3), or the source lower construction would break this proof. Bounded tests in checks.json and fiber-checks.json are diagnostics only. The most serious eligibility objection is classical/source-routine subsumption: replacing one-coordinate counts with all residue pairs is short and may be judged a routine adaptation, which the campaign excludes even if no printed exact duplicate exists. No claim of accepted discovery or independently cleared novelty follows from this argument.
