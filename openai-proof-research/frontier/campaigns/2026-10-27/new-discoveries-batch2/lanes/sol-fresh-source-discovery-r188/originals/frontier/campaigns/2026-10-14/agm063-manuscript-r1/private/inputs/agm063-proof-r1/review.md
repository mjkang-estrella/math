## Independent boundary checks and findings

The support-dimension lemma genuinely needs numerical spanning. On P^1×P^1 with only one ruling family, the proper quotient has one-dimensional base and the endpoint relation has dimension 3=2n−1, but its fibers are the chain classes. This is not a counterexample because the family spans only one of the two numerical directions. Adding the second ruling connects all points. It pinpoints the precise role of the effective-divisor argument.

A bounded horizontal family need not cover the total space. On P_(P^2)(O⊕O(1)), the quotient-O section is P^2, and its lines have degree 2 under −K=2ξ+2H; these curves dominate the base but lie in the section. Fiber lines also have degree 2, and the two collections connect any two points by going to the section, across it, then up a fiber. This geometric boundary example supports the distinction between horizontal dominance and total-space dominance; it does not substitute for the general proof above.
