                      The least common multiple of
                  polynomial values over function fields
                                                by

    Abstract. Cilleruelo conjectured that for an irreducible polynomial f ∈ Z[X] of
degree d ≥ 2 one has
                         log lcm(f (1), . . . , f (N )) ∼ (d − 1)N log N
as N → ∞. He proved it in the case d = 2 but it remains open for every polynomial with
d > 2.
    We investigate the function field analogue of the problem by considering polynomials
over the ring Fq [T ]. We state an analogue of Cilleruelo’s conjecture in this setting: denoting
                     Lf (n) := lcm(f (Q) : Q ∈ Fq [T ] monic, deg Q = n)
we conjecture that
(1)                          deg Lf (n) ∼ cf (d − 1)nq n ,   n→∞
(cf is an explicit constant depending only on f , typically cf = 1). We give both upper
and lower bounds for Lf (n) and show that the asymptotic (1) holds for a class of “special"
polynomials, initially considered by Leumi in this context, which includes all quadratic
polynomials and many other examples as well. We fully classify these special polynomials.
We also show that deg Lf (n) ∼ deg rad Lf (n) (in other words, the corresponding LCM is
close to being squarefree), which is not known over Z.

the condition log lcm(1, . . . , N ) ∼ N is equivalent to the Prime Number
Theorem. This problem later inspired a more general problem of studying
the least common multiple of polynomial sequences. For a linear polynomial
f (X) = kX + h, k, h ∈ Z, k > 0, h + k > 0 it was observed by Bateman

(1.1)            log lcm(f (1), . . . , f (N )) ∼ (d − 1)N log N

    Convention. Throughout the rest of the paper, in all asymptotic no-

   Cilleruelo proved (1.1) for quadratic polynomials and there are no known

    Remark 1.2. The expression (d − 1)nq n is directly analogous to the
product (d − 1)N log N appearing in (1.1). Over the integers (or generally
in characteristic 0), if f is not constant then Vf is always trivial and no

that occurs for “most” polynomials is cf = 1 (i.e. Vf is trivial). A heuristic
justification of Conjecture 1.1 will be given in Section 4.

    We now briefly discuss how our main conjecture and results compare with
the work of Leumi [Leu21], which also studied the function field analogue of
Cilleruelo’s conjecture and influenced the present work. First, [Leu21] states
Conjecture 1.1 without the constant cf . This is certainly false by Theorem
1.3 because it can happen that cf < 1 (see Example 2.2 below). It seems
to have been overlooked that if g ∈ Vf and Q ∈ Mn , n > deg g, then since
f (Q + g) = f (Q), the value f (Q + g) contributes nothing new to the LCM
on the RHS of (1.4) over the contribution of f (Q). Once one accounts for
this redundancy with the constant cf = 1/|Vf |, Cilleruelo’s original heuristic
carries over to the function field case giving rise to Conjecture 1.1. Second, all
results and conjectures in [Leu21] are stated only for an absolutely irreducible
and separable f ∈ Fq [T ][X]. We do not make these restrictions here and it
takes additional technical work to treat the general case. Third, the lower
bound on Lf (n) given in [Leu21] has a smaller constant than ours (thus the
bound is weaker), comparable to the RHS of (1.3), and a lower bound for
the radical of Lf (n) is not stated explicitly. Fourth, in [Leu21] our Theorem
1.8 is stated without the constant cf , which is incorrect in general for the
same reasons explained above, although the arguments therein are essentially
correct in the case cf = 1. Finally, [Leu21] gives a classification of special

polynomials only in the case p ∤ d (and it is stated only for the ring Fq [T ]),
whereas we treat the general case.
