Counterexamples to Conforti’s Subtree Conjecture for Mixed-Integer
                        Bipartite Covers
                                                 Alper Ferudun∗

                                              September 30, 2026


                                                       Abstract
            For a bipartite graph G = (U ∪ V, E), a set I ⊆ U ∪ V and rationals bij , let S(G, I) = {x ∈
        RU ∪V : xi + xj ≥ bij (ij ∈ E), xi ∈ Z (i ∈ I)}, and let k be the least positive integer with
        kb integral. In the 2008 Oberwolfach report on combinatorial optimization, Conforti conjectured
        that conv S(G, I) is the intersection of the hulls conv S(T, I ∩ V (T )) over the subtrees T of G
        whose integral vertices are exactly their leaves. This would place the membership problem for
        conv S(G, I) in coNP. He noted that the case k = 2 follows from work of Conforti, Gerards and
        Zambelli, and that the conjecture was open for every k ≥ 3. We show that it fails for every k ≥ 3.
        For each such k we give two unicyclic counterexamples. One has six vertices. The other has seven,
        and in it every integral vertex is a pendant vertex with a continuous neighbour, so the standard
        normalisation of such sets (splitting integral vertices) does not remove it. In both, an explicit
        point lies in conv S(T, I ∩ V (T )) for every subtree T of G, but violates a facet-deﬁning inequality
        of conv S(G, I) by (k − 2)/(2k − 3). The proofs are short and by hand. We also describe exact
        validity certiﬁcates based on an extended formulation, and use them to certify a further normalised
        counterexample for k = 3. The complexity of the membership problem remains open. This is an
        unrefereed note.


1       Introduction
Let G = (U ∪ V, E) be a bipartite graph, let I ⊆ U ∪ V be a set of integral vertices, and let b ∈ QE .
The other vertices are called continuous. Conforti, Wolsey and Zambelli [CWZ10] study the set of
mixed-integer covers
                      S(G, I) = {x ∈ RU ∪V : xi + xj ≥ bij (ij ∈ E), xi ∈ Z (i ∈ I)}.
It is equivalent to the network-dual sets of Conforti, Di Summa, Eisenbrand and Wolsey [CDEW09],
which contain several mixing-type sets from production planning. Let k be the least positive integer
such that kbij ∈ Z for all ij ∈ E; we call it the denominator parameter. The papers [CDEW09,
CWZ10] give extended formulations of conv S(G, I) whose size grows with k. For k = 2, Conforti,
Gerards and Zambelli [CGZ07] describe the hull in the original space; their setting also includes the
bounds x ≥ 0.
    In his abstract in the 2008 Oberwolfach report [Con08], Conforti asks for the complexity of op-
timizing a linear function over S(G, I), equivalently of deciding membership in conv S(G, I) (his
Problem 1). As Conforti notes, this problem is in NP. For a subgraph H of G put IH = V (H) ∩ I.
Let T = T (G, I) be the family of subtrees T of G such that IT is exactly the set of leaves of T .
Here S(T, IT ) ⊆ RV (T ) is deﬁned by the edges of T , and we identify conv S(T, IT ) with the cylinder
{x ∈ RU ∪V : x|V (T ) ∈ conv S(T, IT )}.

ﬂows [CWZ10, Theorem 23], and for two classes of network-dual sets whose hulls are intersections of
hulls of mixing sets [DW11]. The conjecture appears twice in the UnsolvedMath dataset [ula26], as

     The graph Gk has six vertices; its only cycle is a 4-cycle through an integral vertex. The graph
Gb k has seven vertices. Its continuous vertices induce a 4-cycle, and each of its three integral vertices
is a pendant vertex with a continuous neighbour (Figure 1). The second example matters because of
normalisation. Splitting an integral vertex into copies, one for each incident edge, does not change
the convex hull up to identifying the copies (Remark 2.5, following [CDEW09, DW11]). Splitting
the integral vertex z of Gk gives a tree whose integral vertices are its leaves, and for such a tree
the conjecture is trivial. So Gk refutes Conjecture 1.1 as stated, but not its restriction to normalised
instances, and that restriction would still give the coNP certiﬁcate. The graph G b k refutes both. Hence
the certiﬁcate proposed by Conforti, a single subtree of G or of a normalisation of G, does not exist
in general. This does not decide whether Problem 1 is in coNP; the complexity question (record
OWR-2489-008 of [ula26]) remains open.
     For k = 1 the relaxation P (G) is integral, so conv S(G, I) = P (G) and the identity holds once
P (G) is included on the right-hand side. For k = 2 we rely on Conforti’s statement (Section 6). So
the cases k ≥ 3 left open in [Con08] all have a negative answer.

Lemma 2.2. Let G be connected with exactly one cycle C. If x ∈ conv S(G − e, I) for every edge e
of C, then x ∈ P (G) and x ∈ conv S(T, IT ) for every subtree T of G, in particular for every T ∈ T .

gives x ∈ conv S(T, IT ). Every edge of G lies in G − e for some edge e of C, since C has at least four
edges. So x ∈ P (G).

variable occurs in at most one constraint. We call an instance normalised if every integral vertex has

the integral vertices that become isolated (this does not change the hull, read as a cylinder), and then

6    Concluding remarks
Status in terms of k. For k = 1 the matrix is totally unimodular and b is integral, so conv S(G, I) =
P (G). Thus (1) holds, but the literal identity of Conjecture 1.1 can fail, as for K2 with I = ∅. For
k = 2, Conforti states that the conjecture follows from the description of Conforti, Gerards and
Zambelli [CGZ07]. Their setting includes the bounds x ≥ 0, and their odd I-paths may end at a
continuous vertex, where the bound is used. We have not re-derived the case k = 2 for S(G, I)

                                                      10
without bounds. In random experiments with k = 2 we found no counterexample. For every k ≥ 3
the conjecture fails (Theorem 1.2).
    Complexity. Theorem 1.2 shows that non-membership in conv S(G, I) cannot always be certiﬁed
by one subtree, of G or of a normalisation of G. It says nothing about other certiﬁcates, and the
complexity of Problem 1 of [Con08] remains open. A natural question is whether some other family
of small subgraphs suﬃces, for example subgraphs with at most one cycle, and whether optimization
over them is polynomial.
    How the examples were found. We computed min c⊤ x over conv S(G, I) and over the right-hand
side of (1) by linear programming, using the lifted system of Section 5 for G and for every tree of
T . A random search over bipartite graphs with at most eight vertices ﬁrst found gaps on seven and
eight vertices, for k ∈ {3, 5, 6}. Greedy deletion of edges and integrality constraints, and lowering
of right-hand sides, reduced the one with k = 3 to six vertices. A scan over the remaining right-
hand sides gave the family Gk . The normalised family G  b k was then obtained by replacing z with a
continuous vertex and a pendant integral vertex, as suggested by Remark 2.5. In random experiments
we found no counterexample with at most ﬁve vertices (k ≤ 6), and none among normalised instances
whose continuous part is a 4-cycle with two pendant integral vertices (k ≤ 5). These experiments are
evidence only; we do not know the least number of vertices of a counterexample.

Scope and priority. Theorem 1.2 answers Conforti’s question [Con08, Conjecture 2] negatively
for every k ≥ 3. The answer does not depend on the reading: it holds for the literal statement,
with the linear relaxation added, for normalised instances, for the intersection over all subtrees, and
with the bounds x ≥ 0. We read Conforti’s abstract in the Oberwolfach report, the revised preprint
(November 2009) of [CWZ10], the discussion-paper version (October 2010) of [DW11], obtained from
the CORE website (cdn.uclouvain.be), a preprint (February 2010) of [CCZ10], and a preprint of
[CGZ07]. The revised preprint of [CWZ10] does not restate the conjecture. Its ﬁnal section proposes
a diﬀerent conjecture, on the facets of conv S(G, I) when G is a tree (Conjecture 26 there). Di
Summa and Wolsey [DW11, Section 5] give an example indicating that mixing sets do not suﬃce to
describe such hulls. Their example is a tree whose integral vertices are its leaves, so it is consistent
with Conjecture 1.1. The preprint of [CCZ10] discusses the set S(G, I) and its extended formulation
(Section 8 there) without mentioning the conjecture. We also searched arXiv, zbMATH, Crossref and
OpenAlex, including the works citing [CGZ07, CDEW09, CWZ10] and the Oberwolfach report, and
made three web searches. We found no counterexample and no other discussion of the conjecture.
We did not see the published versions of [CWZ10], [DW11] and [CCZ10], nor the textbook [CCZ14].
This negative search is not a proof of priority.
