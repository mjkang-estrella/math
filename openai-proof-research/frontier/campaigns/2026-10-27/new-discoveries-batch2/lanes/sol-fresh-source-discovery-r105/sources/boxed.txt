 Abstract 
    
 A boxed mesh pattern is a mesh pattern whose selected entries lie in an empty
axis-parallel rectangle. We classify coincidences of boxed patterns with classical and vincular
patterns, exhibit a genuinely bivincular coincidence, and prove that no
boxed–bivincular coincidence occurs for patterns of length at least five.
Together with known results, this shows that every boxed pattern of length at
least five has factorial growth and hence fails the Stanley–Wilf property.
At length four, one exceptional orbit is enumerated by the semi-Baxter
numbers, while the remaining exceptional orbit,  { 2143 , 3412 } \{2143,3412\} , is
unresolved; we conjecture that it has factorial growth. 
    
 For  Box ⁡ ( 123 ) \operatorname{Box}(123) , we derive an exact maximum-insertion identity and prove the
subfactorial upper bound  2 5 ​ n ​ n β ​ n 2^{5n}n^{\beta n} , where  β = log 2 ⁡ ( 2 ​ cos ⁡ ( π / 7 ) ) < 0.85 \beta=\log_{2}(2\cos(\pi/7))<0.85 . A closed enumeration remains open. We also prove a general first-moment
formula for boxed mesh patterns that depends only on the length of the
underlying pattern; in particular, the expected number of  Box ⁡ ( 123 ) \operatorname{Box}(123) 
occurrences in a uniformly random permutation of length  n n  is asymptotic to
 n ​ log ⁡ n n\log n . 
    
 For  Box ⁡ ( 12 ) \operatorname{Box}(12) , we identify the occurrence statistic with the up-degree in
the strong Bruhat order, obtaining its maximum, its mean, and an exact
insertion identity for the distribution polynomials. We conjecture that the
coefficients of these polynomials are unimodal. 
  
 
 
 Keywords.  mesh pattern; boxed pattern; Stanley–Wilf property;
vincular pattern; bivincular pattern; semi-Baxter permutation;
strong Bruhat order.

 1  Introduction 

 
 Let  𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n}  denote the set of permutations of
 [ n ] = { 1 , 2 , … , n } [n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\} , with  𝔖 0 = { ε } \mathfrak{S}_{0}=\{\varepsilon\} . A
 classical pattern  of length  k k  is a permutation
 p = p 1 p 2 ⋯ p k ∈ 𝔖 k p=p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}\in\mathfrak{S}_{k} . An occurrence of  p p  in a
permutation  π = π 1 π 2 ⋯ π n ∈ 𝔖 n \pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}  is a subsequence
 π i 1 π i 2 ⋯ π i k \pi_{i_{1}}\pi_{i_{2}}\cdots\pi_{i_{k}} , where
 i 1 < i 2 < ⋯ < i k i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k} , that is order-isomorphic to  p p ; that is,  π i r < π i s \pi_{i_{r}}<\pi_{i_{s}}  if and only if  p r < p s p_{r}<p_{s}  for  1 ≤ r , s ≤ k 1\leq r,s\leq k .
For example, the pattern  132 132  occurs twice in the permutation  24513 24513 ,
namely as the subsequences  243 243  and  253 253 . 
 
 
 For a pattern  p p , defined in any specified sense, let
 Av n ⁡ ( p ) ⊆ 𝔖 n \operatorname{Av}_{n}(p)\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{n}  denote the set of permutations that
avoid  p p , that is, contain no occurrence of  p p . The  Stanley–Wilf limit  of a pattern  p p , when it exists, is 
 

 
 
 L ⁡ ( p ) = lim n → ∞ | Av n ⁡ ( p ) | 1 / n . L(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}|\operatorname{Av}_{n}(p)|^{1/n}. 
 
 
 Arratia  [ 2 ]  proved that an exponential upper bound for
 | Av n ⁡ ( p ) | |\operatorname{Av}_{n}(p)|  for a classical pattern  p p  implies the existence of this limit, and Marcus and Tardos
 [ 25 ]  proved the required upper bound. Thus the
Stanley–Wilf theorem says that  L ⁡ ( p ) L(p)  exists and is finite for every fixed
classical pattern  p p . For any pattern  p p , not necessarily classical, we shall say that the
 Stanley–Wilf property  holds if 
 

 
 
 lim sup n → ∞ | Av n ⁡ ( p ) | 1 / n < ∞ . \limsup_{n\to\infty}|\operatorname{Av}_{n}(p)|^{1/n}<\infty. 
 
 
 We say that a positive sequence  ( a n ) (a_{n})  has  factorial growth  if  log ⁡ a n = Θ ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) \log a_{n}=\Theta(n\log n) ,
or, equivalently, if  a n = n Θ ⁡ ( n ) a_{n}=n^{\Theta(n)} . If  p p  is a consecutive pattern of length at least three, meaning that the
entries in an occurrence must occupy consecutive positions, Elizalde
 [ 14 , Theorem 4.1]  proved that there exist constants
 0 < c p < d p < 1 0<c_{p}<d_{p}<1  such that, for all sufficiently large  n n , 
 

 
 
 c p n ​ n ! < | Av n ⁡ ( p ) | < d p n ​ n ! . c_{p}^{n}n!<|\operatorname{Av}_{n}(p)|<d_{p}^{n}n!. 
 
 
 Consequently its  n n th root tends to infinity. (Consecutive patterns of
length two are the exceptional trivial cases, with one avoider.) Vincular patterns, defined in Section  2 , already exhibit
both types of behaviour in length three: for example,
 1 ​ 23 ¯ 1\underline{23} , also written  1 ​ - ​ 23 1\text{-}23 , has the Bell numbers as its
avoidance sequence, whereas some other one-bond patterns have Catalan
avoidance sequences  [ 11 ] . Since
 B n 1 / n ∼ n / ( e ​ log ⁡ n ) B_{n}^{1/n}\sim n/(e\log n) , Bell growth also violates the Stanley–Wilf
property. 
 
 
 Mesh patterns, defined in Section  2 , were introduced by Brändén and Claesson
 [ 10 ]  as a flexible language for imposing geometric
conditions on occurrences of classical permutation patterns. They have since
developed in several complementary directions: shading and coincidence
criteria  [ 29 ,  13 ] , systematic and
algorithmic classification  [ 18 ,  5 ] , exhaustive
generation  [ 17 ] , asymptotic containment
 [ 16 ] , and the study of distributions
 [ 22 ,  23 ,  24 ] . 
 
 
 Two patterns are called  coincident  when they impose exactly the same
avoidance condition: every permutation avoids one if and only if it avoids the
other. Thus coincidence identifies the actual avoidance classes, not merely
their counting sequences; a formal definition is given in
 Section   2 . 
 
 
 Avgustinovich, Kitaev and Valyuzhenich  [ 3 ]  introduced  boxed
patterns : all  ( k − 1 ) 2 (k-1)^{2}  internal cells of a mesh pattern of length  k k  are
shaded. They showed, among other things, that the permutations avoiding boxed  132 132 
are exactly those avoiding classical  132 132  and are therefore counted by the
Catalan numbers. They also
showed that an analogue of the Erdős–Szekeres theorem fails for boxed
patterns of length greater than two, and they enumerated permutations
avoiding two or more boxed patterns of length three, where generalized
Catalan numbers appear. More strikingly,
they proved factorial lower bounds for many boxed patterns, showing that the
Stanley–Wilf conclusion for classical patterns does not extend to arbitrary
mesh patterns. The simplest of their constructions doubles each letter: 
 

 
 
 δ ( π 1 ⋯ π m ) = ( 2 π 1 ) ( 2 π 1 − 1 ) ⋯ ( 2 π m ) ( 2 π m − 1 ) . \delta(\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{m})=(2\pi_{1})(2\pi_{1}-1)\cdots(2\pi_{m})(2\pi_{m}-1). 
 
 
 Every pair is a descending pair of consecutive values, and the empty-rectangle
condition shows that  δ ⁡ ( π ) \delta(\pi)  avoids  Box ⁡ ( 123 ) \operatorname{Box}(123) : the unselected sibling
of the middle member of any increasing triple lies inside its rectangle. This
injects  𝔖 m \mathfrak{S}_{m}  into  Av 2 ​ m ⁡ ( Box ⁡ ( 123 ) ) \operatorname{Av}_{2m}(\operatorname{Box}(123)) . The same observation
and its complement handle any boxed pattern having a monotone interval factor
of length three. More generally, factorial growth is inherited from any boxed
interval factor; see Proposition  3.4 .

case, every letter of an arbitrary base permutation is replaced by an

mate either lies in the forbidden rectangle or forces the adjacent member of

complement deal with the other three nonmonotone triples. If  τ \tau  is not

Either it has a monotone factor of length three, in which case the

exceptional permutations below. Together these ideas give factorial lower

 It is important that the same theorem already settles every larger length.
More precisely,  [ 3 , Theorem 3]  proves

classical, vincular or bivincular pattern in lengths at least five is

logically independent proofs: noncoincidence alone would not imply factorial

 Three questions from the final section of  [ 3 ]  are directly relevant
here: determine the Stanley–Wilf behaviour of the four patterns in
( 1 ); enumerate  Box ⁡ ( 123 ) \operatorname{Box}(123) ; and decide when a boxed pattern can be
coincident with a classical pattern. The last question has since been settled:
coincident mesh patterns have the same underlying permutation
 [ 13 , Lemma 4.1] , and the enclosed-diagonal
criterion of Tenner  [ 29 ]  classifies when a mesh is superfluous.
For boxed patterns the complete list is recalled in
Proposition  5.1 . The first two questions remain the main
enumerative motivation for this paper. 
 
 
 Boxed patterns are also called  frame patterns ; under that name their
occurrence statistics have been studied in the cycle structure of
permutations by Jones, Kitaev and Remmel  [ 19 ] . On
the algorithmic side, deciding boxed containment has been studied by Bruner
and Lackner  [ 6 ] , by Cho, Na and Sim  [ 12 ] 
and by Amit, Bille, Hagge Cording, Li Gørtz and Wedel Vildhøj
 [ 4 ] ; see Remark  3.3 .

 A complementary distribution question concerns the boxed pattern  12 12 . A
recent sequence of papers has completed the classification of length-two mesh
patterns by distribution-equivalence and Wilf-equivalence. Su, Kitaev and
Zhang  [ 28 ]  obtained a near-classification; Fang, Fu,
Kitaev, Li, Su and Sun  [ 15 ]  reduced both classifications to a
single remaining case; and Zhang and Zhao  [ 30 ]  settled that
case. The first of these papers records the initial distribution polynomials
for boxed  12 12  but leaves a general formula open. We observe that the number of
boxed  12 12  occurrences is exactly the up-degree of a permutation in the strong
Bruhat order. The standard Bruhat cover criterion, also discussed in
mesh-pattern language by Bouvel, Ferrari and Tenner
 [ 8 ] , therefore brings the results of Adin and
Roichman  [ 1 ]  into the problem. In particular, it gives
the maximum number of occurrences, the number of maximizers, and the exact
mean. We add a short direct proof of the mean and an insertion identity that
isolates the catalytic prefix statistic needed for the full distribution. 
 
 
 The paper is organized as follows. In Section  2 , we
introduce the notation and recall the preliminary results used throughout the
paper. In Section  3 , we translate boxed containment into
consecutive containment after restriction to an interval of values. In
Section  4 , we correct the length-four symmetry reduction,
identify the semi-Baxter orbit, and record the resulting global growth
classification. In Section  5 , we classify classical and
vincular coincidences, exhibit a genuinely bivincular coincidence, and prove
that no boxed–bivincular coincidence occurs in length at least five. In
Section  6 , we give an exact identity, a subfactorial upper bound,
and a general first-moment formula motivated by boxed  123 123 . In
Section  7 , we discuss the remaining orbit and state two
conjectures. Finally, in Section  8 , we connect boxed  12 12  with
covers in the strong Bruhat order and record the resulting distributional
information.

 3  Boxed patterns as interval-consecutive patterns 

 
 For an integer interval  I = [ a , b ] I=[a,b] , write  π | I \pi|_{I}  for the subword of  π \pi 
obtained by retaining entries whose values lie in  I I . 
 
 
 Definition 3.1 . 
 
 Let  τ ∈ 𝔖 k \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{k} . An occurrence
 π i 1 ⋯ π i k \pi_{i_{1}}\cdots\pi_{i_{k}}  of  τ \tau  in  π \pi  is a  boxed occurrence  if
the open axis-parallel rectangle determined by its leftmost, rightmost, lowest
and highest selected points contains no further point of the plot of  π \pi .
The corresponding mesh pattern is denoted  Box ⁡ ( τ ) \operatorname{Box}(\tau) . 
 
 
 
 Because a permutation plot has no repeated abscissae or ordinates, the
condition can equivalently be stated with the closed rectangle: the closed
bounding rectangle contains no plot point other than the selected ones. 
 
 
 Theorem 3.2 . 
 
 For every  τ ∈ 𝔖 k \tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}  and every permutation  π \pi , the following are
equivalent. 
 
 (i)  
 
 π \pi  contains  Box ⁡ ( τ ) \operatorname{Box}(\tau) ; 
 
 (ii)  
 
 for some interval  I I  of values, the word  π | I \pi|_{I}  has a consecutive
factor whose standardization is  τ \tau . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Suppose first that points with positions  i 1 < ⋯ < i k i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}  form a boxed
occurrence, and let  u u  and  v v  be the minimum and maximum of their values.
No other point with value in  [ u , v ] [u,v]  can have position strictly between
 i 1 i_{1}  and  i k i_{k} . Thus the selected entries form a consecutive factor of
 π | [ u , v ] \pi|_{[u,v]} . 
 
 
 Conversely, suppose a consecutive factor of  π | I \pi|_{I}  standardizes to  τ \tau .
Let  u u  and  v v  be the minimum and maximum values in this factor. Any extra
point in its bounding rectangle would have value in  [ u , v ] ⊆ I [u,v]\subseteq I  and
position between the first and last entries of the factor. It would therefore
appear inside the factor of  π | I \pi|_{I} , a contradiction.
∎ 
 
 
 
 The criterion places boxed avoidance beside simsun-type restrictions, but with
all value intervals rather than only initial intervals. It also gives a uniform
way to search for occurrences: restrict to intervals, then use consecutive
pattern matching. A second equivalent formulation, used in
 [ 3 ] , is obtained by repeatedly deleting the leftmost, rightmost,
smallest or largest entry:  π \pi  contains  Box ⁡ ( τ ) \operatorname{Box}(\tau)  if and only if some such
sequence of external deletions ends at  τ \tau . 
 
 
 Remark 3.3 . 
 
 Deciding whether a given permutation contains a given boxed pattern has also
been studied algorithmically. Bruner and Lackner  [ 6 ] 
devote a section to boxed mesh patterns and give an  O ⁡ ( n 3 ) O(n^{3})  algorithm; Cho,
Na and Sim  [ 12 ]  improve this to  O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ m ) O(n^{2}m)  and then to
 O ⁡ ( n 2 ​ log ⁡ m ) O(n^{2}\log m) , where  m m  is the length of the pattern; and Amit, Bille, Hagge Cording, Li Gørtz and Wedel
Vildhøj  [ 4 ]  give an  O ⁡ ( n 2 ) O(n^{2})  algorithm, which is optimal
because the output can have size  Ω ⁡ ( n 2 ) \Omega(n^{2}) . 
 
 
 
 The interval formulation also gives a useful inheritance principle. Say that
 σ ∈ 𝔖 r \sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}  is an  interval factor  of
 τ = τ 1 ⋯ τ k \tau=\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{k}  if, for some  j j  and  a a , 
 

 
 
 { τ j , τ j + 1 , … , τ j + r − 1 } = { a , a + 1 , … , a + r − 1 } \{\tau_{j},\tau_{j+1},\ldots,\tau_{j+r-1}\}=\{a,a+1,\ldots,a+r-1\} 
 
 
 and  σ = st ( τ j ⋯ τ j + r − 1 ) \sigma=\operatorname{st}(\tau_{j}\cdots\tau_{j+r-1}) . Thus the entries forming
 σ \sigma  occupy consecutive positions and consecutive ranks in  τ \tau . 
 
 
 Proposition 3.4 . 
 
 If  σ \sigma  is an interval factor of  τ \tau , then every occurrence of
 Box ⁡ ( τ ) \operatorname{Box}(\tau)  contains an occurrence of  Box ⁡ ( σ ) \operatorname{Box}(\sigma) . Consequently,  Av n ⁡ ( Box ⁡ ( σ ) ) ⊆ Av n ⁡ ( Box ⁡ ( τ ) ) \operatorname{Av}_{n}(\operatorname{Box}(\sigma))\subseteq\operatorname{Av}_{n}(\operatorname{Box}(\tau))  for  n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 . In particular, failure of the Stanley–Wilf property for  Box ⁡ ( σ ) \operatorname{Box}(\sigma)  implies
failure of the property for  Box ⁡ ( τ ) \operatorname{Box}(\tau) . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Take the selected points of a boxed  τ \tau  occurrence that correspond to the
factor  τ j ⋯ τ j + r − 1 \tau_{j}\cdots\tau_{j+r-1} . No other selected point lies horizontally
between the first and last of these points, because the positions of the
factor are consecutive. No other selected point lies vertically between
their lowest and highest points, because their ranks are consecutive. An
unselected point in the smaller bounding rectangle would also lie in the
bounding rectangle of the original boxed occurrence. Hence the selected
factor is a boxed  σ \sigma  occurrence. Taking contrapositives gives the class
inclusion, and the final assertion follows by comparing cardinalities.
∎ 
 
 
 
 For example, Proposition  3.4  and the doubled-letter construction
show immediately that  Box ⁡ ( τ ) \operatorname{Box}(\tau)  has factorially many avoiders whenever
 τ \tau  has an interval factor  123 123  or  321 321 . The “good pattern” argument
of  [ 3 ]  handles many patterns without such a monotone interval
factor and is therefore genuinely stronger than this inheritance principle.

 Contrary to what one might first expect, boxed meshes are not generally free of
enclosed diagonals; see Figure  1 . The two meshes in
Lemma  4.1  have the same nonempty set of enclosed diagonals: the
pointless square  ( 2 , 2 ) (2,2) . Thus the enclosed-diagonal invariant of
 [ 13 ]  is consistent with the coincidence, but
Tenner’s superfluous-mesh theorem  [ 29 ]  does not prove it: that

simultaneous extension  [ 13 , Section 7]  is also a
sufficient local shading criterion, not a converse that would establish this
coincidence. Finally, the coincidence theorem for two vincular patterns does
not apply because the boxed mesh is not vincular. The tightening argument
above is therefore not an immediate consequence of these earlier results; it
supplies the required global movement of the adjacency bond.

 9  Concluding directions 

 
 The growth classification is complete except for one length-four orbit.
Theorem  4.4 , together with the factorial construction of
 [ 3 ] , shows that no boxed pattern of length at least five has the
Stanley–Wilf property. In length four, twenty patterns have factorial lower
bounds, the orbit  { 2413 , 3142 } \{2413,3142\}  has semi-Baxter and hence exponential
growth, and only  { 2143 , 3412 } \{2143,3412\}  remains unresolved. We conjecture that this orbit has factorial
growth; see Conjecture  7.5 . 
 
 
 The coincidence results also reveal a sharp length boundary.
Theorem  5.2  shows that boxed–vincular
coincidences stop in length four, while Theorem  5.4 
shows that the genuinely bivincular coincidence in
Corollary  5.3  is also maximal in length. The
interval-consecutive criterion provides a complementary structural viewpoint:
boxed  τ \tau  avoidance is consecutive  τ \tau  avoidance on every interval of
values. Moreover, Proposition  3.4  transfers factorial
lower bounds from interval factors to larger boxed patterns. 
 
 
 For boxed  123 123 , Theorem  6.1  gives an exact maximum-insertion
identity and five new terms, while Theorem  6.2  bounds its
growth exponent by a constant below  0.85 0.85 . A closed enumeration remains
open. Proposition  6.4  also shows that all boxed
patterns of a fixed length have the same first moment; in particular, the
expected number of boxed  123 123  occurrences is asymptotic to  n ​ log ⁡ n n\log n . 
 
 
 Finally, for boxed  12 12 , Proposition  8.1  identifies the occurrence
statistic with up-degree in the strong Bruhat order. Although
real-rootedness,  γ \gamma -positivity and log-concavity fail in small lengths,
the available data motivate Conjecture  8.5 , which asserts
unimodality of the distribution polynomials.
