TWO q-DEFORMATIONS OF NARAYANA POLYNOMIALS:
ROGERS MOMENTS, HANKEL POSITIVITY, AND q-NARAYANA
KERNELS

arXiv:2610.28691v1 [math.CO] 20 Oct 2026

JIANG ZENG
Abstract. We study two q-deformations of the continuous interpolation between type B and
shifted type A Narayana polynomials, both constructed from the continuous q-ultraspherical
(Rogers) functional. The first consists of affine moments of ordinary powers. For fixed 0 < q < 1
and −1 < β < 1, we prove coefficientwise Hankel total positivity in the polynomial variable by a
continuant comparison criterion. We also determine leading continuants, Hankel determinants,
and extreme coefficients of arbitrary Hankel minors.
The second deformation uses Askey–Wilson basis products and yields a basic-hypergeometric
Narayana kernel. We give two independent moment evaluations and prove that its row polynomials have simple negative zeros and are γ-positive after palindromic normalization. An
Askey–Wilson integral with base q 2 gives a continuous-parameter extension and a moment
proof of the Krattenthaler–Wachs super q-Narayana expansion, including its integrality. An
ordinary-power expansion also proves Pan’s Touchard identity.
For fixed 0 < q < 1 and θ > −1, we prove total positivity of the kernel triangle by a Jackson
q-Bessel–Edrei factorization and strict total positivity of its associated square by a discrete
moment representation. This gives a simultaneous (θ, q)-extension of Wang–Yang’s theorems for
type A and type B Narayana triangles and squares. We further establish coefficientwise total
nonnegativity in q through order two for the endpoint triangles. An exact triangular change of
basis links the two deformations and explains their common classical limit.

The recent combinatorial positivity results for q-Hoggatt numbers are especially relevant
at our endpoint θ = 1. Liu and Mao [24] obtained Schur-positive identities in their study of
Baxter polynomials and proved row- and column-wise q-log-concavity for the q-analogues of the
d-Hoggatt numbers. Chern and Shi [5] subsequently strengthened these statements to strong
q-log-concavity, for both fixed rows and fixed columns, using the theory of Schur functions. In a
separate paper they proved Cigler’s conjecture on certain polynomials arising from q-Hoggatt
numbers: nonnegativity follows from Stanley’s (P, ω)-partition theory and a standard Youngtableau formula, while palindromicity is obtained by an involution on rectangular standard
Young tableaux [4].
These results and Theorem 12.1 are transverse rather than redundant. The Schur-theoretic
results are coefficientwise statements in the formal variable q, but concern specific families of

2 × 2 inequalities. Our theorem gives nonnegativity of all minors of the complete lower-triangular

meeting point strongly suggests that a Schur-theoretic refinement of our total-positivity theorem
may exist.
There is a parallel comparison at q = 1. Mao and Shi [25] proved total positivity of every

13. Further directions
The two constructions pose a further structural question: can the change-of-basis formula (5.4)
be enhanced by an additional positivity-preserving factorization on an appropriate parameter or
variable range? In view of the sign changes of cn,k (t; q), such a factorization would require more
than a naive coefficientwise argument.
The preceding results suggest several questions.
(θ)

(1) Numerical experiments suggest that the zeros of consecutive row polynomials Pn (t; q)
(θ)
and Pn+1 (t; q) strictly interlace. A related question asks whether the normalized rows
(θ)
Pbn strictly interlace; their Rogers representations suggest studying Cn (x; q −n−θ |q) and
Cn+1 (x; q −n−1−θ |q) on the imaginary axis. These are separate interlacing questions, since
the rescaling in (9.2) depends on the row index n. Fang, Zhang and Zhao [11] prove the
corresponding Sturm property at the classical Hoggatt point (θ, q) = (1, 1). Can their
interlacing theorem be lifted to the present continuous-θ, basic-hypergeometric setting,
perhaps through a parameter-shift recurrence for Rogers polynomials?
(2) Can Theorem 12.11 be extended to minors of order at least three, proving Conjecture 12.7?
The skew-rectangle lemma establishes the mixed-index order-two inequalities, but does
not control higher alternating determinants. A flagged-Schur model as in Remark 8.2
could provide the missing all-orders refinement.
(3) Proposition 12.13 gives an Edrei–Viennot network whose weights involve the Jackson
q-Bessel zeros. Is there a purely q-hypergeometric planar network for the same minors,
with edge weights given directly in terms of q and q θ ? Such a model could lead to
coefficientwise total positivity.
(4) Does the kernel (1.3) preserve strong q-log-convexity in a formal-variable sense, thereby
simultaneously extending the two transformations studied by Zhu and Sun?
(5) Is there a multivariate or Askey–Wilson four-parameter extension in which the present
one-parameter interpolation appears as the continuous q-ultraspherical specialization?
