                                                                                             Abstract
                                                       We propose a randomized social choice rule called Mixed Integrated Veto (MIV) with metric
                                                   distortion of 5/2, improving the previous best upper bound of 2.75271. MIV is the equal mixture
                                                   of Maximal Lotteries and Integrated Veto, a new rule built on the Simultaneous Veto process of
                                                   Kizilkaya and Kempe. Rather than returning the candidate surviving longest, Integrated Veto
                                                   assigns each candidate probability proportional to its average score over the whole process.


                                         1        Introduction
                                         In the metric distortion model it is assumed that voters and candidates lie in a common unknown
                                         metric space, and a voter’s cost for a candidate is equal to their distance. A voting rule observes
                                         only each voter’s ranking of the candidates by distance and outputs a lottery. Given a profile and a
                                         metric consistent with it, the distortion of a voting rule is the ratio between the expected social cost
                                         of the lottery it outputs and the cost of the best candidate in that metric. The metric distortion of
                                         the rule is the worst case over all profiles and all consistent metrics.
                                             For deterministic rules the optimal metric distortion is 3 (Anshelevich et al., 2018; Gkatzelis
                                         et al., 2020), while for randomized rules it has remained open. The best known lower bound is
                                         2.1126 (Charikar and Ramakrishnan, 2022), and the best known upper bound is due to Charikar
                                         et al. (2024), who obtained 2.75271 by randomizing between Maximal Lotteries — the equilibria
                                         of the zero-sum game induced by pairwise majority margins — and Random Dictatorship on the
                                         weighted uncovered set.
                                             We improve this bound to 5/2 by using a different mixture. Instead of Random Dictatorship,
                                         we mix a maximal lottery with a new rule, Integrated Veto, which is built on the Simultaneous Veto
                                         process of Kizilkaya and Kempe (2023). In that process, each candidate with positive plurality score
                                         starts with that score, and every voter continuously decrements the score of her least preferred
                                         candidate among those still active, until no score remains. Rather than returning the candidate
                                         surviving longest, Integrated Veto assigns each candidate probability proportional to the average
                                         score it held over the process.
                                             Our analysis rests on a certificate of Charikar et al. (2024), which bounds the metric distortion
                                         of a lottery by comparing the probability it places on candidates that a given set is collectively
                                         preferred to against how far that set is from containing a candidate ranked above everything outside
                                         it by every voter. We show that Integrated Veto satisfies the certificate well when the latter quantity
                                         is small, whereas Maximal Lotteries do so when it is large. Mixing Integrated Veto and Maximal
                                         Lotteries in equal parts guarantees a metric distortion of 5/2, and we show that this bound is tight
                                         for the mixture.

2    Related work
The metric distortion framework was introduced by Anshelevich et al. (2015, 2018), who showed
that no deterministic rule can guarantee distortion below 3 and √     that the uncovered set achieves
5. Munagala and Wang (2019) improved the upper bound to 2 + 5 before Gkatzelis et al. (2020)
closed the gap with Plurality Matching, a deterministic rule achieving the optimal bound of 3.
Kizilkaya and Kempe (2022) subsequently introduced Plurality Veto, a simpler rule with the same
guarantee.
    In the randomized setting, Anshelevich and Postl (2016, 2017) showed that Random Dictatorship
achieves a metric distortion of 3 − n2 , which converges to 3 as n grows, and that no randomized rule
achieves distortion below 2, even on the line. Charikar and Ramakrishnan (2022) improved this
lower bound to 2.1126. Charikar et al. (2024) were the first to break the barrier of 3 by proposing
a rule that guarantees 2.75271. Their analysis proceeds through a biased-metric certificate which
we use as Proposition 3.1. Recently, Cai et al. (2026) showed that uniformly sampling from a
deterministically chosen constant-size multiset of candidates already achieves a metric distortion of
3 − ε for some constant ε > 0.

Veto-based rules and the veto core. Plurality Veto initializes each candidate with its plurality
score and decrements it according to voters’ bottom choices among the candidates still standing
(Kizilkaya and Kempe, 2022). Kizilkaya and Kempe (2023) later introduced the generalized veto
core, which extends Moulin’s proportional veto core (Moulin, 1981), together with a continuous
Simultaneous Veto formulation in which all voters decrement simultaneously and candidates are
eliminated as their support is exhausted. The construction has since been extended, for instance to
the (p, q)-veto core in the learning-augmented setting (Berger et al., 2024) and to k-approval variants
trading distortion against minority protection (Kizilkaya and Kempe, 2025). Integrated Veto inherits
the score dynamics of Simultaneous Veto, but differs in its output: rather than returning the final
survivor, it also takes the intermediate states of the process into account.

Maximal Lotteries. Maximal Lotteries go back to Kreweras (1965) and Fishburn (1984) and
have been widely studied in probabilistic social choice (Brandt, 2017). Charikar et al. (2024) were
the first to consider them in the metric distortion setting.

6    Conclusion
We introduced Integrated Veto and showed that its equal mixture with Maximal Lotteries has
metric distortion exactly 5/2, improving the previous upper bound of 2.75271. Charikar et al. (2024)
ask whether Plurality Veto and its variants could be used to improve their bound; Lemma 4.2 is
one answer.
   One direction to improve this bound further is to let the mixing weight depend on the profile,
which our lower bound does not rule out. Another straightforward approach is to consider mixtures
of more than two rules.
