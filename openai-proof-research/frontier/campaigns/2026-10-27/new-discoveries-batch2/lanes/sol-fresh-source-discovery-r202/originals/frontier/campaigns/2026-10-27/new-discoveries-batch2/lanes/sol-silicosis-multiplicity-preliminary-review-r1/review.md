## SOURCE

The actual object is

\[
P_i(x)=\prod_{j=1}^i\frac{x}{x+j^a+j^b},\quad
H(x)=\sum_{i\ge1}i^{b+1}P_i(x),\qquad F(x)=H(x)/(x+d_0).
\]

The candidate treats every real a>=0, b>-2, d0>0, and every positive level lambda=alpha/r. Source physical coefficients are pi=i^(-p), qi=i^q, ki=i^(-k), p,q,k>=0, with a=q+k and b=k-p. Thus their literal realizable subfamily has b<=a. Conversely every a>=0,b<=a is realizable: choose k in [max(0,b),a], then p=k-b,q=a-k. The unconstrained b>a part is a valid theorem about the displayed series, not an additional physical case licensed by those nonnegative exponents. Arbitrary positive d0 is compatible with positive k0 and p0+q0=k0*d0.

## MATH

I find no ordinary proof gap in the frozen multiplicity theorem. Independent checks of the potentially fragile steps follow.

1. For a>=0, d_j>=1. On any compact K in Re(z)>0 let |z|<=R and Re(z)>=delta>0. Then |z/(z+d_j)|<=R/sqrt(R²+2delta+1)=q<1. Thus both w_i P_i and d_i P_i converge normally: the coefficients have polynomial growth for fixed real a,b. Denominators z+d0 are nonzero there. Holomorphic convergence gives real analyticity and the identity principle needed for strictness. This works for arbitrarily negative b>-2 and for b>a. At 0, for x<=1, P_i<=(x/(1+x))^i; factoring out its first power and bounding the remaining weighted sum at1/2 gives H=O(x), not just a pointwise geometric estimate.

2. P_(i-1)-P_i=d_i P_i/x, with P_N->0, gives sum d_iP_i=x. Consequently q_i=d_iP_i/x is a probability distribution with survival P_i. No monotone ordering of d_i is required. Indeed some allowed d_i need not be increasing, so that missing premise would have been a real gap in an ordering-dependent proof. Here every finite P_i is strictly increasing in x independently of d ordering.

3. Put c=a-b, r(t)=t/(1+t^c). Its derivative has numerator 1+(1-c)t^c. For c<=1 all integer increments are positive. The tail-sum identity E r_N=r1+sum (r_(i+1)-r_i)P_i is justified by monotone convergence and finite expectation at each x. At least its first increment supplies strict increase; x/(x+d0) is also positive and strictly increasing. For c<1, r_i->infinity; for c=1, r_i increases to1 but remains below1. Escape of N_x to infinity, witnessed by P_K(x)->1 for fixed K, proves respectively F->infinity and F->1, with F<1 at equality.

4. For c>1, r(t) first increases and then decreases to0, possibly with its continuous maximum below the first integer index. Thus its discrete strict superlevel set is an interval, including empty or one-point cases and exact zeros at the endpoints. The sequence -lambda*d0, d_i(r_i-lambda) has <=2 variations after deleting zeros. The bidiagonal transform argument is correct even with cancellations. Adjacent output support sets {i-1,i} cannot reverse their selected input indices; equal selections cannot have different signs. Thus it does not quietly assume all coefficients are nonzero.

5. The exact finite object is G_N=sum_(i=0)^N c_iP_i=H_N-lambda*(x+d0)+lambda*x*P_N. The last term is nonzero at finite N. Clearing its strictly positive denominator gives the stated Q_N. The matrix recurrence A_N=L_N diag(A_(N-1),1) has L_N diagonal (d_N,...,d_N,1) and subdiagonal1. It follows directly by multiplying the previous polynomial by X+d_N and adding c_N X^N, including the highest coefficient. Classical Descartes applies to nonzero Q_N; c0=-lambda*d0 and positive product d_i make its constant term nonzero.

6. For c>1, bounded r_i->0 and P_K(x)->1 imply E r_N->0 by splitting i<=K and i>K. Hence F->0 at infinity. Positivity and continuity give a positive attained interior maximum, using small and large cutoffs. If a strict superlevel set disconnects, the candidate's slightly shifted positive level yields five strictly alternating signs. Pointwise convergence at just these five fixed points makes them persist in G_N, giving four distinct roots by IVT, against Descartes' bound2. No invalid preservation of tangencies in the infinite limit occurs.

7. Quasiconcavity implies nondecrease before the maximum and nonincrease after it. Two equal values on one such side force a whole constant interval. Analytic nonconstancy (also witnessed by positivity and the limit0 at the left endpoint) forbids that interval. The same argument makes the maximizer unique. IVT plus the two endpoint limits proves precisely two roots below M, one at M, and none above. This does not assert that the maximum has negative second derivative or that F' has only one zero; stationary inflections are not excluded by strict monotonicity.

The theorem actually established is therefore: b>a-1: strict increase with range(0,infinity); b=a-1: strict increase with range(0,1); b<a-1: strict increase then strict decrease with zero limits and unique M>0. These give the exact level counts in PROOF.md.

## NOVELTY and SUBSTANTIVE: the complete classical bridge

**Bodies read.** [Schoenberg–Whitney, Compositio9(1951)141–160](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1951__9__141_0.pdf), Theorem2 pp143–144 and its §2 proof pp150–152, characterizes variation diminution by sign conditions on compound matrices. Their definition of a definite matrix permits zeros, requiring no opposite-sign entries. Every totally nonnegative matrix satisfies those conditions. The sufficiency proof deletes zero-input columns and merges same-sign neighboring columns by positive combinations, reducing to alternating input. This is actual theorem and proof-body evidence, not a title or an abstract.
