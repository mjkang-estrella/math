                                                   Abstract. We determine the asymptotic order of the observable diameter of ℓp -product spaces
                                                   associated with a probability measure on [0, 1] whose density has a power-type interior zero. Dif-
                                                   ferent vanishing orders are allowed on the two sides of the zero. For every 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞, we obtain

                                                   vanishing orders. In the case p = 2, the transition occurs exactly at the threshold for the spectral
                                                   gap.




                                                                                         1. Introduction
                                              The concentration of measure phenomenon was first discovered by P. Lévy [7] in his study of high-
                                           dimensional spheres and was brought to prominence by V. Milman [8] in the early 1970s through its
                                           applications to asymptotic geometric analysis; see also [9] and [15] for historical accounts and general
                                           background. Within the theory of metric measure spaces (mm-spaces, for short), Gromov [13]
                                           introduced the observable diameter as a numerical invariant of concentration of measure, measuring
                                           how large an mm-space can appear through real-valued 1-Lipschitz functions. He also studied
                                           product spaces from this viewpoint. In particular, his book contains an estimate of the observable
                                           diameter of ℓ1 -product spaces based on the Laplace–Gibbs functional [13, § 3 12 .62]. Gromov also
                                           gave a rough sketch of the corresponding upper estimate for p > 1, and Ozawa and Shioya [16] later
                                           gave a complete proof for all 1 ≤ p < ∞. The resulting general upper bound has order n1/(2p) , and
                                           for p > 1 a matching lower bound holds under a uniform discreteness assumption. Combined with
                                           the phase-transition framework developed in [17], this gives a critical scale of order n−1/(2p) in the
                                           uniformly discrete case. For continuous spaces, however, the corresponding lower bound need not
                                           hold, and the observable diameter can have a substantially smaller order. [14, Problem 9, p. 302]
                                           asks for an estimate of the observable diameter of the n-fold product of the unit interval, equipped
                                           with the Euclidean metric and the product measure induced by a probability density ρ on the
                                           interval. It suggests that the behavior of ρ near its zeros should affect the asymptotic order. The
                                           present paper gives a solution to that problem for densities with a power-type interior zero, while
                                           allowing different powers on the two sides of the zero and, more generally, ℓp -product metrics.
                                              Spectral gaps provide a second viewpoint. The Poincaré inequality tensorizes, and the Gromov–
                                           Milman concentration theorem [12] then yields dimension-free concentration for ℓ2 -product powers.
                                           For a compact connected Riemannian manifold X, the identity λ1 (X n ) = λ1 (X) therefore gives a
                                           dimension-independent upper bound for the observable diameter of X n . This classical consequence
                                           is recorded explicitly in [16].
                                              There is also a converse principle. Gozlan, Roberto, and Samson [5] proved that, for an mm-
                                           space, a nontrivial dimension-free concentration inequality for its ℓ2 -product powers is equivalent
                                           to a Poincaré inequality. Thus the spectral gap is not merely one sufficient source of dimension-
                                           free product concentration: in this sense it characterizes the phenomenon. Since dimension-free

   We briefly indicate the proof strategy. For 1 < p ≤ 2, the upper bounds are based on a weighted
variance estimate. We flatten the density independently on the two sides of a, using separate
regularization scales r− and r+ , and compare the regularized and original product measures in
Wasserstein distance. Choosing the two scales according to the corresponding exponents α− and
α+ yields the sharp upper bounds.
   For the lower bounds, the subcritical case follows from the ℓ1 -product lower bound of Ozawa
and Shioya [16, Theorem 1.3], together with the comparison n1/p−1 d1 ≤ dp . Above the critical
exponent, a majority-type observable is placed on a side where the vanishing order equals α∗ . At
the critical exponent, a one-sided logarithmic ramp on a critical side gives the required Hopf–Lax
estimate, while Berry–Esseen controls its fluctuations.
   For 2 < p < ∞, we use a transport–entropy inequality of type Tp , which controls the p-
Wasserstein distance to the regularized measure in terms of relative entropy. We again regularize
the two sides independently. The corresponding Tp constant is governed by
                                               p−1−α−     p−1−α+
                                     max{1, r−          , r+       }.
The transport costs within the two sides remain uniformly controlled; only the cost of transferring
mass through the bottleneck degenerates. Tensorization, Wasserstein comparison, and the two
optimal regularization scales then give the upper bound.
   These arguments concern 1 < p < ∞. The two endpoints are unchanged. For p = ∞, the metric
diameter of ([0, 1]n , d∞ ) is 1, while a coordinate projection gives a positive lower bound independent
of n. For p = 1, the upper and lower bounds follow from [16, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3], respectively.
   For 1 < p < ∞, the resulting picture may again be viewed as a continuous counterpart to the
phase-transition framework for product mm-spaces developed in [17]: the scale is determined by
the more singular side of the interior bottleneck.

                                          2. Preliminaries
Definition 2.1 (mm-space). An mm-space is a triple (X, d, ν) consisting of a complete separable
metric space (X, d) and a Borel probability measure ν on X.
Definition 2.2 (Partial diameter). For an mm-space (X, d, ν) and 0 < κ < 1, define the partial
diameter by
              PartDiam(X, d, ν; 1 − κ) := inf{diam B | B ⊂ X Borel, ν(B) ≥ 1 − κ}.
We also write PartDiam(ν; 1 − κ) for PartDiam(X, d, ν; 1 − κ) when the underlying mm-space is
clear.
Lemma 2.3. Let ν be a Borel probability measure on [0, 1] such that ν ≪ L1 . Then, for every
0 < κ < 1, PartDiam(ν; 1 − κ) > 0.
Proof. By the absolute continuity of the integral, there exists ε > 0 such that L1 (A) < ε implies
ν(A) < 1 − κ for every Borel A ⊂ [0, 1]. If B ⊂ R is Borel and diam B < ε, then B ∩ [0, 1] is
contained in an interval of length less than ε, and hence ν(B) = ν(B ∩ [0, 1]) < 1 − κ. Therefore
every Borel set B ⊂ R with ν(B) ≥ 1 − κ has diam B ≥ ε.                                         □
