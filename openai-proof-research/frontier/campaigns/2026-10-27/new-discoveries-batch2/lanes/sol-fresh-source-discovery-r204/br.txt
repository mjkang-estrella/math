                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                              Functional inequalities, like the Poincaré, logarithmic Sobolev, and transportation inequal-
                                           ities, play a fundamental role in high-dimensional probability theory and convex geometry.
                                           Among others, they provide important tools for the study of concentration of measure and mix-
                                           ing times of Markov processes [6, 42], with applications to high-dimensional sampling, Bayesian
                                           statistics, statistical physics, and machine learning [21, 57]. The goal of this manuscript is to
                                           introduce a new functional inequality motivated by the recent literature on quantitative stability
                                           of optimal transport maps, and to discuss its connections to Poincaré inequalities.
                                              Recall that a probability measure ρ on Rd , absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue
                                           measure, is said to satisfy a Poincaré inequality if there exists a finite constant C > 0 such that

   Let P2 (Rd ) denote the set of probability measures with finite second moment. The main
object of interest in this paper is the following functional inequality: We shall say that an
absolutely continuous probability measure ρ ∈ P2 (Rd ) satisfies a Brenier stability inequality if
there exists a finite constant C > 0 such that

                          Varρ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) ≤ CW22 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρ, (∇ϕ1 )# ρ),                       (1.3)

for all convex functions ϕ0 , ϕ1 on Rd satisfying ∇ϕ0 , ∇ϕ1 ∈ L2 (ρ). Here, Wp denotes the p-
Wasserstein distance, and F# ρ denotes the pushforward of ρ by a measurable vector field F .
Further notational conventions are recalled in Section 1.5. We denote by CW (ρ) the smallest
constant C > 0 for which equation (1.3) holds. If no such C < ∞ exists, we write CW (ρ) = ∞.
   The Brenier stability inequality is stronger than the Poincaré inequality: for any ρ ∈ P2 (Rd ),

                                         CP (ρ) ≤ CW (ρ).                                      (1.4)

Indeed, since (∇ϕ0 , ∇ϕ1 )# ρ is a coupling between (∇ϕ0 )# ρ and (∇ϕ1 )# ρ, the following well-
know inequality holds:

                          W2 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρ, (∇ϕ1 )# ρ) ≤ ∥∇ϕ0 − ∇ϕ1 ∥L2 (ρ) .                     (1.5)

Hence equation (1.3) implies equation (1.1) with same constant C, for any f of the form
f = ϕ1 − ϕ0 , where ϕ0 , ϕ1 are convex and satisfy ∇ϕ0 , ∇ϕ1 ∈ L2 (ρ). To deduce the Poincaré
inequality (1.1) for arbitrary f ∈ Cc∞ (Rd ), we can apply the same argument to ϕ0 = M | · |2 and
ϕ1 = f + M | · |2 , for a constant M ≥ 0 chosen large enough so that ϕ1 is convex. Then, both
ϕ0 and ϕ1 are convex and satisfy ∇ϕ0 , ∇ϕ1 ∈ L2 (ρ), hence (1.1) holds with C = CW (ρ), which
proves (1.4).
   Another way to see the relationship between the Brenier stability inequality and Poincaré-
type inequalities is to use the following known characterization of the 2-Wasserstein distance,
whose proof is recalled in Appendix A (see also [51, Lemma 2.7]): For all convex maps ϕ1 , ϕ0
with gradients in L2 (ρ), one has

                     W2 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρ, (∇ϕ1 )# ρ) = inf ∥∇ϕ1 − ∇ϕ0 ◦ S∥L2 (ρ)                     (1.6)
                                                 S∈S(ρ)

where S(ρ) denotes the group of measure-preserving maps of ρ, i.e., the Borel maps S : Rd → Rd
such that S# ρ = ρ. As a consequence of this identity, a measure ρ ∈ P2 (Rd ) satisfies a Brenier
stability inequality if and only if it satisfies the bound

                        Varρ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) ≤ CW (ρ) · ∥∇ϕ1 − ∇ϕ0 ◦ S∥2L2 (ρ) ,                    (1.7)

for all S ∈ S(ρ) and all convex functions ϕ1 , ϕ0 whose gradients lie in L2 (ρ). Again, for S = id,
we recover the Poincaré inequality and (1.4), but for general S ∈ S(ρ), equation (1.7) provides
a more general Poincaré-type inequality.

1.1. Main Results. The purpose of this manuscript is to prove that the Brenier stability
inequality holds under natural assumptions on ρ. We will additionally show that for some
(non-uniformly) log-concave ρ, it can happen that CP (ρ) < CW (ρ) = ∞, thus showing that
the Brenier stability inequality is strictly stronger than the Poincaré inequality. Our first main
result is the following.

   Our original motivation for proving Theorem 1.1 comes from the quantitative stability theory
of optimal transport. We briefly recall the aims of this area here and defer a more detailed
discussion to Section 1.3. Given ρ, µ ∈ P2 (Rd ) such that ρ is absolutely continuous with respect
to the Lebesgue measure, Brenier’s theorem [14] asserts that there exists a ρ-almost everywhere
unique solution T : Rd → Rd to the optimization problem

which is nowadays called the Brenier map. Moreover, the map T takes the form T = ∇ϕ for
some convex function ϕ : Rd → R called a Brenier potential. In the rest of this discussion,
we treat the source measure ρ as fixed, and we assume that for any µ, the Brenier potential is
unique up to addition of a constant, which is the case for instance when ρ satisfies the Poincaré
inequality (1.1) (as a consequence of the fact that T = ∇ϕ itself is ρ-a.e. unique). To emphasize
the dependence on the target measure µ, we denote by Tµ the solution R        to the optimization
problem (1.11), and by ϕµ the unique Brenier potential satisfying ϕ dρ = 0.
   It is natural to ask whether the map Tµ or the potential ϕµ are stable with respect to pertur-
bations of the target measure µ. Qualitative answers to this question have been well-understood
since Brenier’s original paper [14], where it was shown that the map µ 7→ Tµ is continuous from
P2 (Rd ) endowed with the 2-Wasserstein distance W2 to L2 (ρ). When ρ satisfies the Poincaré
inequality, this implies continuity of µ 7→ ϕµ as well. Quantitative versions of these stability
results, by contrast, have attracted attention only more recently, as reviewed in Section 1.3. For
Brenier potentials, existing quantitative results typically take the form

                          ∀µ, ν ∈ P(Y ),     ∥ϕµ − ϕν ∥L2 (ρ) ≤ CWp (µ, ν)α                 (1.12)

where p ≥ 1, P(Y ) denotes the set of probability measures supported in a given compact set Y ,
and C, α > 0 are constants which may depend on Y , but not on µ, ν ∈ P(Y ). In this context,
Theorem 1.1 shows that if ρ is uniformly log-concave, then equation (1.12) holds for α = 1 and
p = 2, and P(Y ) can even be replaced by the larger set P2 (Rd ). On the other hand, Corollary 1.3
shows that the Lipschitz exponent α = 1 is generally unachievable in equation (1.12) for log-
concave source measures.
  It was conjectured in [44, Conjecture 3.10] that if ρ is uniform on a convex body (i.e., a
compact, convex set, with non-empty interior), then for any bounded set Y , equation (1.12)
holds for α = 1, p = 2, and some C > 0. A proof of this conjecture was recently claimed in
OpenAI’s document [48, Proposition 5.1], which does not mention [44, Conjecture 3.10]. We
provide in this paper a different proof, close to the one of Theorem 1.1.

1.3. Connections to Stability of Optimal Transport. As already mentioned, one of the
main applications of our results is to the quantitative stability theory of optimal transport. More
precisely, Theorem 1.1 shows that if ρ is κ-uniformly log-concave, then Brenier potentials are
linearly stable with respect to W2 . Said differently, the map taking µ to ϕµ defines a Lipschitz
embedding of the Wasserstein space (P2 (Rd ), W2 ) into L2 (ρ). In contrast, Corollary 1.3 shows
that this is no longer the case if ρ is merely assumed to be log-concave.
   As Corollary 1.5 already suggests, the quantitative stability of Brenier potentials is closely
related to the quantitative stability of Brenier maps, i.e., to bounds of the form

                       ∀µ, ν ∈ P(Y ),       ∥∇ϕµ − ∇ϕν ∥L2 (ρ) ≤ CWp (µ, ν)β                    (1.17)

for C, β > 0, p ≥ 1 and Y ⊂ Rd . Both problems have been the subject of intensive research
in recent years. In what follows, we briefly review this literature through the lens of Brenier
potentials, since these are the objects investigated in the present paper. We refer to [44] for a
broader survey.
   There are two predominant sets of assumptions under which such stability bounds have been
analyzed. First, strong results have been achieved under regularity assumptions on one of the
two Brenier maps. In this line of work, one establishes (1.17) under the assumption that ∇ϕµ
is Lipschitz (and (1.12) then follows if ρ satisfies the Poincaré inequality). The constant C in
(1.17) then depends on the Lipschitz constant of ∇ϕµ . The first result of this type was proved
6                              CYRIL LETROUIT AND TUDOR MANOLE


in Gigli’s work [29], who obtained an exponent of β = 1/2, and several variants of this result
were later derived in the literature on statistical optimal transport [23, 28, 36, 56]. When ϕµ is
additionally assumed to be strongly convex, it is known that the exponent β = 1 can be achieved
in equation (1.17), cf. [7, 32, 47].
   A second line of work establishes bounds of the form (1.12) or (1.17) under the only assumption
that Y is compact. No other hypothesis is made on µ and ν, or their induced potentials ϕµ
and ϕν . We can mention the works [9, 24, 43] which show that if Y is compact and ρ is either
uniformly log-concave or has a density bounded above and below on a nice domain (typically,
a John domain), then (1.12) holds with p = 1 and α = 1/2 (up to a multiplicative logarithmic
term if ρ is uniformly log-concave). Notably, the article [43] was the first to point out the
relation between stability of Brenier potentials and Poincaré-type inequalities. For p = 1, the
exponent α = 1/2 is optimal as shown in [43, Proposition 6.10] (see also [44, Section 3.7]). Of
course, since W1 ≤ W2 , these works imply the same bounds for p = 2 instead of p = 1 in (1.12).
However, for p = 2, the exponent α = 1/2 is no longer optimal. The present work and OpenAI’s
document [48] are the first to establish the optimal exponent when p = 2, namely α = 1. Notice
however that [48] does not handle uniformly log-concave measures and provides a worse constant
than 4R2 in Theorem 1.4.
   The present paper falls under the second line of work, since we make no assumptions on µ, ν.
So far, the predominant proof strategy of the second line of works was to establish some form
of strong convexity for the Kantorovich functional defined by
                                                  Z
                                        Kρ : ψ 7→ ψ ∗ dρ,

with the notable exception of [27] which built on stability of entropic optimal transport maps.
The recent document of OpenAI [48] exploits another approach, based on semi-discrete com-
putations and displacement concavity. The main results of the present paper are proved using
fundamentally different arguments, inspired from the first line of work, notably from [32, 46].
Indeed, our proofs hinge upon the perturbation theory for the Monge-Ampère equation, which
until now has typically relied on some form of regularity on µ and ν. Our proof strategy is
summarized in Section 2.

1.4. Outline. The remainder of this manuscript is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present
an overview of the proofs. In Section 3, we introduce the linearized Monge-Ampère operator Lµ
and show how a coercivity estimate on Lµ , denoted by A(κ), leads to our main results. In Section
3.3, we show that A(κ) is implied by a coercivity estimate B(κ) on an operator Eµ obtained by
pulling back Lµ to the source domain, and which has the advantage of being symmetric. We thus
reduce to proving B(κ). In Section 4, we establish Theorem 1.1 (and deduce Corollary 1.6) by
showing that B(κ) holds for any uniformly log-concave measure ρ. This relies on a generalization
of Bochner’s (and Reilly’s) formula. In Section 5, we establish Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.5 by
showing that B(κ) holds for any compactly supported log-concave ρ. This coercivity estimate is
actually slightly weaker than for uniformly log-concave measures, and depends on the size of the
support of ρ. In Section 6, we then prove Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3, establishing a counter-
example to the Brenier stability inequality for arbitrary log-concave probability measures. In
Appendix A, we prove the formula (1.6) for the Wasserstein distance. In Appendix A.2, we
provide a regularization argument allowing to reduce the proof of our main results to the case
where V is smooth and K has smooth, uniformly convex boundary.

A.2. Regularization of ρ and K. In the proofs of both Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.4, we
assumed that V is C 2 , and in the proof of Theorem 1.4 we also assumed that K has C ∞ boundary
and is uniformly convex. In this appendix, we explain how to reduce to this case. We focus
on the case of Theorem 1.4 since the reduction for Theorem 1.1 is easier and follows from a
straightforward adaptation.
   In the sequel, we assume Theorem 1.4 has been proved under the additional assumptions that
V ∈ C 2 (K) and K has C ∞ boundary and is uniformly convex, and we show this implies Theorem
1.4 in full generality. Without loss of generality, V is finite in the interior of K, which we denote
by Ω. Up to a translation, we may assume B(0, r) ⊂ Ω for some r > 0. We let
                                     p(x) = inf{t > 0 : x ∈ tΩ}
be the gauge of Ω. It is convex, L-Lipschitz with L = 1/r, and Ω = {p < 1}. Consider two
convex functions ϕ0 and ϕ1 such that ϕ0 , ϕ1 ∈ C 1,α (K) for some α > 0.
  Let χ ∈ Cc∞ (B(0, 1)), and χδ = δ −d χ(δ −1 ·) for δ > 0. Let pδ = p ∗ χδ + δ|x|2 , which is smooth
and convex, with |pδ − p| ≤ δ(L + supx∈K |x|2 ) in K. Set
                                        Kδ = {pδ ≤ 1 − 2Lδ},
which is compact, convex, and included in Ω. For 0 < δ < δ0 with δ0 small enough, we have
pδ > min pδ on ∂Kδ , so no boundary point minimizes the convex function pδ . Hence ∇pδ ̸= 0
on ∂Kδ , and ∂Kδ is C ∞ by the implicit function theorem. Moreover, Kδ is uniformly convex, as
a sublevel set of the strongly convex function pδ .
   Let δ ∈ (0, δ0 ) be fixed for a moment. Let ρδ be the probability measure whose density is
proportional to 1Kδ e−V . For ε < dist(Kδ , ∂Ω), let Vε = V ⋆ χε , which is C ∞ in a neighborhood
of Kδ , and convex. Applying Theorem 1.4 to the probability measure ρδ,ε whose density is
proportional to 1Kδ e−Vε , we get

                       Varρδ,ε (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) ≤ 4R2 W22 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρδ,ε , (∇ϕ1 )# ρδ,ε ).
Let us show that taking the limit ε → 0, this implies
                         Varρδ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) ≤ 4R2 W22 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρδ , (∇ϕ1 )# ρδ ).                (A.1)
Since Vε → V uniformly on Kδ , and V is bounded on Kδ , the density ρδ,ε converges uniformly
on Kδ to ρδ as ε → 0, and stays bounded above and below. Hence in the limit ε → 0 we obtain
Varρδ,ε (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) → Varρδ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ). Now we observe that W22 ((∇ϕi )# ρδ,ε , (∇ϕi )# ρδ ) → 0 as
ε → 0 since ϕi ∈ C 1,α (K) (see for instance [19, Proposition in Section 1.3.1]), and (A.1) follows.
For the same reason, we also get
                      W22 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρδ , (∇ϕ1 )# ρδ ) → W22 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρ, (∇ϕ1 )# ρ)
as δ → 0. Since Varρδ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) → Varρ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) as δ → 0 by dominated convergence, we finally
get
                           Varρ (ϕ1 − ϕ0 ) ≤ 4R2 W22 ((∇ϕ0 )# ρ, (∇ϕ1 )# ρ)                    (A.2)
by letting δ → 0 in (A.1). We have obtained (A.2) for any convex ϕ0 , ϕ1 ∈ C 1,α (K). Finally,
this extends to any convex functions ϕ0 , ϕ1 with gradients in L2 (ρ) by the same arguments as
in the proof of Theorem 3.2: first reduce to the case where (∇ϕ0 )# ρ and (∇ϕ1 )# ρ have smooth
positive densities on their support, which is a ball, then extend to the case where (∇ϕ0 )# ρ and
(∇ϕ1 )# ρ are only compactly supported, and then to any ϕ0 , ϕ1 with L2 (ρ) gradients.
