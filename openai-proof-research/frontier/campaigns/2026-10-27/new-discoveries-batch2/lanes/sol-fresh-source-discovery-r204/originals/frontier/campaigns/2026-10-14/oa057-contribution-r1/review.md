### 2. The arbitrary-order action is the same cancellation recipe on a regular cyclic module

Write R₀ for cyclic shift on W=E^{Z/M}, c for an exact-M torsion point, and C=(c,…,c). The candidate's two maps on a pair factor are

    α = R₀,                 β = translation_C ◦ R₀^{-1}.

Since R₀C=C, they commute and αβ=translation_C. Moreover α^M=β^M=1. Put u_k=kc, with indices modulo M. Then u−R₀^{-1}u=C, so

    β = translation_u ◦ R₀^{-1} ◦ translation_{−u}.

Thus both generators have fixed points, and α^tβ^t is a nonzero translation for every nonzero t modulo M. This is the familiar regular-permutation way to provide an automorphism of arbitrary finite order and a torsion coboundary; it preserves exactly the source's fixed-generator/pair-product-translation design. No complex multiplication or classification of free affine group actions is required. The candidate does not assert such a classification.

The replacement is not literally the same elliptic involution at M=2, and the original stronger “every support≥2 element is free” property is lost. Neither fact gives a new mechanism. The original proof only needs an appropriate pair for each designated boundary inertia. With N=n+1 auxiliaries in every boundary block, SNC gives |J|+|K|≤n. Whenever i∈J, at least N−|K_i|≥2 coordinates of that block are absent. A nonzero boundary coefficient a_i has the same nonzero value (M/m_i)a_i in both coordinates. The unchanged pairwise cancellation argument therefore detects it. Mixed auxiliary coefficients cannot alter these two absent coordinates. This is a direct blockwise application of the source's two-missing-coordinates argument, with ordinary Kummer normalization replacing square-root normalization.

The important comparison is the full package of required properties, not merely the formula αβ. It retains fixed points for each auxiliary generator, removes every stabilizer with nonzero boundary character, and leaves the general fibre a product of elliptic curves. All three follow from the same permutation/coboundary calculation and independent pair factors. The action creates no additional global compatibility equation.

### 3. The resolution step is ordinary section lifting, rather than a new singularity-resolution theorem

The source uses its unusually simple scalar-normal involution singularities to exhibit a quotient blowup chart. OA057 avoids that chart. At a general auxiliary divisor there is only one auxiliary inertia generator, and its fixed point in the product abelian variety gives a section of the local coarse quotient. Away from that divisor the quotient is smooth and the chosen resolution is an isomorphism, so this section has a meromorphic lift to the resolution.

A meromorphic section into a proper target over a smooth base extends near the general point of each prime divisor. One may take its graph closure: the projection is proper birational and is an isomorphism in codimension one over the smooth base. Equivalently, properness extends over the local DVR and spreading out supplies a neighborhood. The algebraic operation is stated and proved in [Stacks, tag 0BX7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BX7); the candidate uses the standard local analytic modification version. The lifted section satisfies gσ=id, so dg is surjective there.

This step works already for the source's order-two construction and needs no information about the new mixed higher-codimension auxiliary stabilizers. It does not prove a simultaneous resolution, a global section, a smooth morphism everywhere, or preservation of fundamental groups under an arbitrary resolution. None of those stronger assertions is needed by the frozen target. Consequently avoiding a classification of all auxiliary quotient singularities is useful simplification, but here it is paid by a standard proper-lifting operation with exactly the required divisorial scope.

### 4. Descent before resolution does not introduce a further coupled obstruction

The repeated-coordinate test shows that each actual stabilizer acts trivially on each boundary root line. Apply the source's averaging descent separately to each such line and its invariant section on Z=(S×F)/A. Pulling these genuine line-bundle roots back along any projective resolution preserves the identities T_i^{m_i}=g*O(D_i) and their sections; hence all exceptional valuations have the required divisibility. It is this ordering of standard operations that makes arbitrary resolution harmless. No new descent theorem or exceptional-valuation estimate is being supplied.

The coupling with the previous paragraph is straightforward: boundary characters are handled globally before resolution; auxiliary submersions are tested at general divisorial points after resolution. The latter only uses the generator fixed points already built into α and β. Higher-codimension auxiliary intersections do not enter the submersion test, and the already descended roots handle their exceptional divisors. Thus the two substitutions fit the old transfer interface without an additional compatibility problem remaining to be solved.

Finally the finite SNC differential extension is coordinatewise, followed by the source's Hartogs/saturation argument. Exceptional meridians at an SNC intersection give products of commuting local meridians with individually divisible exponents. The connected-fibre surjection and quotient of a virtually abelian manifold group are the original ending. Increasing the number of components or changing their orders does not change that operation.
