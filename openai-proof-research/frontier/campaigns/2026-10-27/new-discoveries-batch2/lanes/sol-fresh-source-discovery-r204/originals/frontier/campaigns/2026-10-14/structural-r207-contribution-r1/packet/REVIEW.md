The needed probability statement is the classical fact that a limit of sums of a symmetric infinitesimal independent array is infinitely divisible, together with the impossibility of a nonconstant bounded-support infinitely divisible law. The author explicitly credits this as classical. I do too.

Durrett's Theorem 3.9.1 itself is stated for rows of identically distributed terms. I do **not** cite it as a proved general nonidentical-array theorem. Its root-tightness proof and the explicitly derived compound-Poisson formula compose as follows to supply the exact symmetric nonidentical case needed here. This also shows why the comparison is not limited to fixed-law sums.

Let X_ni = a_ni D_ni be a finite row of independent symmetric integrable variables, with E|D_ni| <= 2 and max_i |a_ni| -> 0. Suppose S_n = sum_i X_ni converges in law to Z. For fixed real s put

    z_ni = 1 - E cos(s X_ni),    H_n = sum_i z_ni,
    0 <= z_ni <= eta_n := 2 |s| max_i |a_ni| -> 0.

The finite measure nu_n = sum_i Law(X_ni) defines a compound-Poisson variable T_n. Durrett's explicit calculation gives its characteristic function exp(-H_n). For eta_n < 1 the elementary logarithm inequalities give

    exp(-H_n/(1-eta_n)) <= product_i(1-z_ni) <= exp(-H_n),
    0 <= exp(-H_n) - product_i(1-z_ni)
       <= eta_n H_n exp(-H_n)/(1-eta_n)
       <= eta_n/[e(1-eta_n)].                              (P)

The error is uniform in H_n, including H_n tending to infinity. There is no division by a limit characteristic function and no unproved nonvanishing assertion. Characteristic-function continuity therefore gives T_n => Z.

For each fixed integer m, divide the compound-Poisson intensity measure by m to obtain a symmetric variable Y_nm whose m-fold independent sum has law T_n. The actual root-tightness operation in Durrett's proof is

    P(Y_nm > u)^m <= P(T_n > m u),
    P(Y_nm < -u)^m <= P(T_n < -m u).

Tightness of T_n implies tightness of Y_nm. Extract a root subsequence, then pass to characteristic functions to obtain Z distributed as the sum of m independent copies of a symmetric Y_m. A separate subsequence for each m suffices. If |Z| <= r, positive probability of Y_m > r/m would give positive probability that all m copies exceed r/m, contradicting the support of Z. Likewise Y_m >= -r/m. Thus

    Var(Z) = m Var(Y_m) <= r^2/m,

and symmetry forces Z=0 as m tends to infinity. The variance is used **only after bounded support has been established**, never imposed on the array. Durrett §3.9 also records the bounded-support conclusion as Exercise 3.9.2 with a variance hint; the explicit argument above supplies the operation, rather than claiming that a full solution was printed there. The general Levy–Khintchine proof is referred out in Durrett and was not counted as read.

The submitted Bernoulli-retention proof is the same classical divisibility mechanism in another form. Retain each term with probability 1/m; the root characteristic function is product_i(1-z_ni/m). Jensen on the complement gives E|Y_nm| <= E|S_n|, and
