## Closest public bodies and exact non-subsumption

I read the [MathOverflow 420897 question and all three answer bodies](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/420897/what-is-the-smallest-size-of-a-shape-in-which-all-fixed-n-polyominos-can-fit). The Kakeya comment addresses straight digital segments, not all connected animals. I have not independently re-proved these asymptotic arguments; their direction and quantified objects already distinguish them from the needed upper theorem.

The [2018 Code Golf primary answers](https://codegolf.stackexchange.com/questions/167484/smallest-region-of-the-plane-that-contains-all-free-n-ominoes) give the closest actual optimization/construction bodies inspected. The C#/SAT answer displays a 37-cell n=11 shape and loops over each free piece's orientations inside its placement disjunction (Generate, lines 314–344 in the retrieved text). Thus the logical condition is ∀free P ∃orientation ∃translation, not ∀fixed P ∃translation. Its displayed 11-column, six-row shape cannot contain the vertical 11-cell bar. The general triangular answer gives n(n−1)/2, hence 55 at n=11, but explicitly transposes pieces in its proof; its example pattern has only one direction long enough for the 11-bar. Neither is a fixed-piece 55/37-cell counterexample. These are primary algorithm and construction bodies, not merely sequence-number similarities. [A327094's current internal entry](https://oeis.org/A327094/internal) similarly states the free-piece triangular bound and SAT/CP-SAT data; it does not strengthen the fixed endpoint.

I read the [Barequet–Golomb–Klarner chapter](https://www.csun.edu/~ctoth/Handbook/chap14.pdf), Theorem 14.2.1, Table 14.3.1, and the generation descriptions in §§14.3–14.4 (PDF pages 3–8). The printed embedding theorem gives an n²−n+1 standard-position host, hence 111 at n=11; it cannot imply ≤57. Its displayed coordinate limits appear shifted by one relative to that cardinality. Independently, the usual host is row y=0 with 0≤x≤n−1, plus rows y>0 with |x|+y≤n−1: anchoring the leftmost bottom cell proves containment by a path of ≤n−1 edges, and its size is n+(n−1)². This identifies the actual weaker construction without silently repairing a source claim. The chapter describes classical Redelmeier growth enumeration and identifies the original 1981 paper; I did not retrieve that original paper. Enumeration itself earns no novelty here.

Independently, a spanning-tree center puts every connected 11-cell shape into the full radius-five diamond: a tree on 11 vertices has diameter ≤10 and a vertex of eccentricity ≤5, so all coordinate distances from that vertex are ≤5. That gives 61 cells and leaves the four-deletion obligation unproved. Neither this argument nor the source's n=7 puncture justifies deleting these four cells. This is a comparison argument, not a new general-construction claim. No actual public body inspected implies the candidate's stronger bound.
