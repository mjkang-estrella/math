## Exact endpoint and source qualification

For every integer n>=5, every integer 5<=m<=2n-3, every finite simple undirected graph G with n vertices, m edges and distinct designated terminals s,t, and every independent identical edge-failure probability rho in [0,1], the explicit H has three-constrained terminal reliability at least that of G. H has st and k common neighbors, all other vertices isolated; k=(m-1)/2 for odd m, and k=(m-2)/2 plus one edge between two common neighbors for even m. Thus H really has m edges. Paths have length at most three. Competitors need not have universal terminals, connected underlying graphs, or any particular core.

    U_(2k+1) = (1/2)(3/4)^k,

## Full classical bridge and strongest countercase

The candidate's complete applicable bridge remains: couple any removed edge to st; couple single-neighbor interior edges to missing terminal edges; delete irrelevant edges into unused budget. The retained graph is st, a common neighbors and arbitrary core F, with 1+2a+e<=m. For 0<p<1, conditional failure is rho q^a Z(F), q=1-p^2. Sites have states T<N<S with base masses u,1-2u,u, u=p/(1+p). The opposite-state edge weight rho and other weights 1 form a TP2 matrix. Finite conditional association follows by the monotone likelihood-ratio induction given in PROOF.md. Global S/T symmetry plus the one-site occupancy bound gives each extreme marginal <=u; association of an increasing S indicator with a decreasing T indicator bounds opposite-pair probability by 2u^2.

Under the measure for the **reduced** core F0 after k<=2 edges are removed, the product bound gives Z(F)/Z(F0)>=1-2k p u^2>=1-p^2, using (1-p)^2>=0. Spend the removed edges and 2-k unused units on a new independent double vertex. Its factor q cannot increase failure. The invariant 2a+e+t=m-1 and m<=2n-3 guarantee an isolated vertex at each step. Termination leaves no core edge for odd m, one edge or one unit of unused budget for even m; in the latter case add an edge between two common neighbors. Treat p=0,1 directly. This covers the full fixed-n, fixed-m endpoint. No finite experiment substitutes for this unbounded proof.
