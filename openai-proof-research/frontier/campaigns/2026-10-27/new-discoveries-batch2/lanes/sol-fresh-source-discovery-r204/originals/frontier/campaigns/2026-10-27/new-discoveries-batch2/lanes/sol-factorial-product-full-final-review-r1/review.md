## Full strongest objection and independent adjudication

The complete alternate proof in preliminary review is valid. Here is my check of every bridge, not an arbitrary-integer-kernel objection. Let L=log N, y=N/L², T={2<=t<=N: every prime divisor of t is <=y}. Rosser–Schoenfeld's bound pi(x)<=Cx/log x, C=1.25506, and partial summation yield

    sum_{y<p<=N} 1/p <= C/L + C log(L/log y) = O(log L/L)=o(1).

The union bound on excluded multiples gives |T|=N-o(N); no unproved smooth-number asymptotic is required. For p<=y, |v_p(t!/t^t)|<=N(1+log_2 N), so all subset-sum coordinates range over O(N²log N) integers. Even counting at most y such coordinates costs exp(O(N/L))=exp(o(N)). For p>y and large N, y>sqrt N; p cannot divide t in T and p²>N. Thus the coordinate is exactly floor(t/p), between0 and L².

Choose independent fair membership bits X_t. For coefficients a_t=floor(t/p), sum a_t²<=NL⁴. The elementary cosh moment bound gives

    Pr(|sum a_t(X_t-1/2)|>u) <= 2 exp(-2u²/(NL⁴)).

Take u=L² sqrt(N log(4N)). The union bound over fewer than N primes leaves more than half the subsets in simultaneous intervals of at most2u+2 integer values. The large-prime box has log cardinality at most

    pi(N) log(2u+2) <= (C/2+o(1))N.

Add exp(o(N)) small-prime cost. Since C/2=0.62753<log2, fewer boxes than good subsets exist. Two different subsets share the full prime vector. Unique factorization gives equal positive rational products. Cancel their intersection. Both remainders are nonempty since each t!/t^t lies strictly between0 and1, making equality with an empty product impossible. This is an actual {-1,0,1} relation with disjoint supports and refutes exactly the original conclusion even if lengths happen to agree.
