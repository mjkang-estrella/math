                                                  SPECTRAL DELOCALIZATION AND CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREMS FOR
                                                     FOURIER FUNCTIONALS OF STATIONARY GAUSSIAN FIELDS

                                                                                              LEONARDO MAINI


                                                  Abstract. We characterize Gaussian fluctuations of Fourier-Hermite functionals of stationary Gaussian
                                                  fields on locally compact Abelian groups via spectral delocalization: conditional on the total spectral sum
                                                  being near the probed frequency, no partial sum concentrates. Under suitable assumptions, this reduces
                                                  Gaussian convergence to small-ball estimates for convolution powers of the spectral measure, without model-
                                                  specific contraction estimates.




arXiv:2610.37113v1 [math.PR] 27 Oct 2026
                                                      On Td , the criterion yields CLTs at every fixed frequency for band-limited fields and random trigonometric
                                                  polynomials, and extends the known failure of Gaussian convergence for planar arithmetic random waves
                                                  from the fourth chaos to all even orders q ≥ 4. On Rd and Zd , we establish a Breuer–Major theorem for
                                                  Fourier functionals at every frequency with nonzero limiting variance. Cyclical long-memory models exhibit
                                                  frequency-dependent Gaussian convergence.
                                                      We also establish CLTs and variance asymptotics for singular continuous spectral measures, including
                                                  those of stationary Gaussian solutions of linear PDEs. At zero frequency, weak Ahlfors regularity of the
                                                  spectral support and an L2 density suffice for second-chaos CLTs, covering fractal sets, the Airy surface
                                                  of Gaussian sea waves, and smooth compact manifolds. For a class of smooth hypersurfaces, we obtain
                                                  the same variance rates and CLTs known for classical nonlinear functionals of Berry’s random wave model.
                                                  Finally, for random waves with spherical spectral densities bounded above and below, we classify Gaussian
                                                  convergence at every frequency ∥z∥ ≤ q: it fails only for the planar second chaos when 0 < ∥z∥ < 2.

                                                         1. Introduction
   Gaussian fields arise in a broad range of models and applications, see e.g. [ALW11, EF20, LMR+25,
PS26, CMV26, CP25, DMM26, DMS+26] and the references therein.
   One possible way to investigate their properties is to study sequences of their functionals. Examples
of functionals include partial sums and integral functionals, [BM83, DM79, Taq79]; geometric functionals
such as numbers of critical points or volumes and components counts of excursion sets [AGL+25, BCW19,
BMM20, DST26, NPR19]; Fourier coefficients and periodograms [MP08, PW10, PZ19, FT86].
   In this work, we focus on stationary Gaussian fields on locally compact Abelian groups and on the
asymptotic properties of a class of functionals defined below. Popular examples in the literature include
integral functionals over growing domains in Rd , growing partial sums in Zd , and Fourier coefficients of
subordinated Gaussian fields on compact groups such as the flat torus Td . In all these situations, stationarity
provides a spectral measure on the dual group, and the asymptotic behavior of the corresponding functionals
can be investigated through spectral conditions.

1.1. Motivations. There are three main motivations for this work.
   The first one is to understand whether central limit theorems for functionals of stationary Gaussian
fields that are usually proved by rather different arguments can be explained by a common mechanism.
Classical examples are the Breuer–Major and Dobrushin–Major theorems, high-frequency results for Fourier
coefficients of subordinated Gaussian fields on compact groups, and more recent results for arithmetic and
Euclidean random waves. In these problems, the proofs may involve very different ingredients, such as
integrability of the covariance, special properties of the spectral support, or oscillatory integral estimates,
see e.g. [BM83, CKW20, Gro26, MN24]. One of the purposes of this paper is to show that, at a fixed chaos
order, these phenomena can be described directly in terms of the way the spectral mass is distributed under
the additive constraint (1.1). In several examples, this allows one to recover known central limit theorems
from a direct estimate on convolution powers of the spectral measures.
   A second motivation comes from Gaussian fields with singular spectral measures. An important example
is Berry’s random wave model, the stationary isotropic Gaussian field on Rd whose spectral measure
is uniform on the unit sphere. The model was introduced in [Ber77] and also arises as a universal local
scaling limit for high-frequency random waves on a class of Riemannian manifolds; see e.g. [CH15]. It has
motivated an extensive literature on the geometry and fluctuations of random waves and their functionals;
see e.g. [BH26, GPS26, Gro26, GMT24, MN24, MRZ26, NPR19].
   More generally, stationary Gaussian solutions of constant-coefficient linear PDEs naturally have
spectral measures with support contained in the zero set of the corresponding PDE; see e.g. [EF20]. In the
case of Berry’s random wave, or more generally for anisotropic random waves, this zero set is precisely the
unit sphere associated with the Helmholtz equation. Other examples arising in applications are Gaussian
sea waves, Gaussian fields solving a PDE with zero set given by the Airy surface, see [ALO05, EF20].
This suggests studying Gaussian fields with singular continuous spectral measures supported on lower-
dimensional sets in Rd . Such models lie outside the classical short memory setting and may have slowly
decaying oscillating covariances. We will see that, for a variety of such Gaussian fields, the geometry of the
spectral support controls the small-ball probabilities entering our criterion, yielding a connection between
spectral geometry and Gaussian fluctuations. In particular, for both isotropic (i.e. Berry’s random
wave model) and anisotropic random waves with density bounded below (by a positive constant) and
above, we will give a complete description of the CLTs for Fourier-Hermite functionals at every frequency
z, highlighting that one does not have a CLT only if d = 2, q = 2 and 0 < ∥z∥ < 2.
4                                                    L. MAINI


   The third motivation is to understand the role of the probed frequency z. While z = 0 often corresponds
to "spatial averages" with respect to the window ψT , the case z ̸= 0 has also been extensively studied, in
particular in connection with finite Fourier transforms, Fourier coefficients and periodograms of stationary
processes and random fields; see e.g. [MP08, PW10, PZ19, Ros81, FT86, FT87]. These results, obtained
for different classes of processes and under rather different assumptions, motivate the search for a common
spectral mechanism explaining the dependence of the limiting behavior on z.

Remark 1.3. In [MP08, Theorem 2], Gaussian convergence of high-frequency Fourier coefficients on com-
pact Abelian groups is characterized through a random-walk bridge condition. Conditionally on a q-step

probability uniformly over all positions. In particular, no “privileged path” survives asymptotically.

in [MP08] the spectral measure is fixed and the frequency tends to infinity, whereas here the frequency is fixed
and GT varies. Furthermore, Theorem 1.7 extends this principle to growing-domain functionals, replacing

1.3. Organization of the paper. In Sections 2 and 3 we compare the criterion with previous results
and discuss consequences and applications, respectively in the regimes a) and b). The preliminaries about
topology and harmonic analysis on groups, isonormal representations and fourth moment theorems are
collected in Section 4. The general version of the criterion, Theorem 5.9, is stated in Section 5. Its proof,
together with the proofs of Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.7, is given in Section 6, where we also prove
Theorem 6.6, a version of Theorem 1.7 for indicator functions. Finally, in Appendix A and Appendix B we
collect the proofs of some auxiliary technical results needed in Section 3.
