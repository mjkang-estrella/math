                                                In dimension one the origin is not absorbed if and only if the walk stays strictly positive
                                           or strictly negative up to time n. The fluctuation theory of Sparre Andersen [11, 13] and
                                           Spitzer [14] expresses these probabilities through the laws of the individual partial sums
                                           Sk , and for symmetric continuous increments they do not depend on the distribution at all.
                                           Kabluchko, Vysotsky and Zaporozhets [7] found the multidimensional form of the last fact: if
                                           the increments are centrally symmetric and in general position, then pn is given by a universal
                                           combinatorial expression; see Corollary 5.1. The planar case had been treated in [15]. A proof
                                           based on generating functions and the conic Gauss-Bonnet formula was given in [9], and a
                                           deformation (wall-crossing) proof has appeared very recently in [6].
                                                Without symmetry, pn depends on the distribution of the increments; without general
                                           position, boundary effects appear. We show that much of the structure survives when both
                                           assumptions are dropped.

remaining statements are known, and we derive them to show how they fit together.

Remark 2.1. The proof rests on a duality between the space Rd of the configuration and
the space Rn of the coefficients: the vectors x1 , . . . , xn are replaced by the standard basis
of Rn , and the origin of Rd by the affine subspace Q = ker A ∩ {t1 + · · · + tn = 1}. Since
∆σ = {t : 1 ≥ tσ(1) ≥ · · · ≥ tσ(n) ≥ 0}, the origin lies in conv(s1 (σ), . . . , sn (σ)) if and only if
the linear subspace ker A meets the cone {t : tσ(1) ≥ · · · ≥ tσ(n) ≥ 0} outside the origin. A
similar duality was the starting point of [7]. These cones and their images under the sign
changes of coordinates are the closed Weyl chambers of type Bn , and in [7] the absorption
probabilities of symmetric random walks in general position were obtained by counting the
Weyl chambers of type Bn met by a generic linear subspace of codimension d.
