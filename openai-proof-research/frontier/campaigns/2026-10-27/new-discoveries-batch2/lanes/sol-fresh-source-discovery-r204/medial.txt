                                           1     Introduction
                                           The medial axis was introduced by Blum [3] in 1967 as a biologically motivated tool for shape
                                           representation and recognition. His grassfire transform describes the medial axis through the meeting
                                           of inward-propagating fronts. Medial axes and medial representations have since found applications in
                                           shape analysis, image processing, computer vision, and geometric modeling [14]. Their topology has
                                           also been studied extensively. Lieutier [12] proved that every bounded open subset of R𝑛 has the same
                                           homotopy type as its medial axis, defined by the existence of at least two nearest boundary points. In the
                                           planar setting, Choi et al. [5] established a finite graph structure under suitable piecewise real-analytic
                                           boundary assumptions, using the maximal-inscribed-disk convention.
                                               We are concerned with the size and rectifiability of higher-order medial axes. Following Erdős [6]
                                           and Liang [11], let 𝐸 ⊆ R𝑛 be nonempty and closed. For 1 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1, we define its 𝑘-medial axis
                                           𝑀𝑘 (𝐸) to be the set of points outside 𝐸 whose nearest-point set contains 𝑘 affinely independent points.
                                           Equivalently, the affine dimension of the nearest-point set is at least 𝑘 − 1. In particular, 𝑀1 (𝐸) = R𝑛 \ 𝐸,
                                           while 𝑀2 (𝐸) consists of the points having more than one nearest point in 𝐸.

    Erdős [6, 7] studied the size of these sets, proving in particular that 𝑀2 (𝐸) has Lebesgue measure
zero and that 𝑀𝑛+1 (𝐸) is countable. More generally, for 2 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛, he showed that 𝑀𝑘 (𝐸) is contained
in a countable union of surfaces of finite (𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1)-dimensional measure, and hence that

                                        dim 𝑀𝑘 (𝐸) ≤ 𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1,

where dim denotes Hausdorff dimension. His argument uses a geometric criterion formulated in terms
of contingents. More recently, Liang [11] established the following explicit rectifiability statement,
which provides the starting point for our investigation.

Theorem 1.1 (Liang [11]). For every nonempty closed set 𝐸 of the Euclidean space R𝑛 and every
integer 1 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1, the 𝑘-medial axis 𝑀𝑘 (𝐸) of 𝐸 is (𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1)-rectifiable. In particular,

                                        dim 𝑀𝑘 (𝐸) ≤ 𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1.

    The goal of this paper is to determine how far this conclusion remains valid in finite-dimensional
normed spaces. A direct extension to arbitrary norms fails. Although bi-Lipschitz equivalence of norms
preserves the rectifiability of a fixed set, it does not preserve nearest points. For example, equip R2 with
the infinity norm ∥·∥ ∞ and let 𝐸 = {(−1, 0), (1, 0)}. If |𝑦| > 1 + |𝑥|, then both points of 𝐸 are nearest
to (𝑥, 𝑦), since their distance from (𝑥, 𝑦) is |𝑦|. Thus

                                 {(𝑥, 𝑦) ∈ R2 : |𝑦| > 1 + |𝑥|} ⊆ 𝑀2 (𝐸),

so 𝑀2 (𝐸) has nonempty interior and is not 1-rectifiable. This example shows that extending Theorem 1.1
beyond the Euclidean setting requires additional assumptions on the norm or a modification of the
notion of higher-order medial axis.
    Nearest-point geometry beyond the Euclidean setting has been studied from several perspectives.
Convex-distance Voronoi diagrams provide a computational framework for finite sets of sites [4],
while bisectors, circumspheres, and related proximity structures are central topics in Minkowski
geometry [8, 13]. For arbitrary closed sets, Kolasiński and Santilli [9] studied anisotropic distance
functions and cut loci under the assumption that the norm is 𝐶 2 away from the origin and uniformly
convex in the sense of their Definition 2.8. Here we study the first-order geometry of equidistance
conditions under 𝐶 1 regularity and its role in the rectifiability of higher-order medial axes.
    Let 𝑋 be an 𝑛-dimensional real normed space, with 𝑛 ≥ 1, whose norm ∥·∥ is 𝐶 1 on 𝑋 \ {0}, and
let 𝐷 denote the Fréchet derivative. The dual space and dual norm are denoted by 𝑋 ∗ and ∥·∥ ∗ . The
unit spheres are

contact covectors have affine dimension at least 𝑘 − 1. The dual medial axes thus classify nearest-point
configurations by the rank of their linearized equidistance conditions. This rank is intrinsic to the norm
and the nearest-point configuration. It also admits a characterization through the distance function
itself. Indeed, Proposition 4.3 shows that 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸) is precisely the set of points outside 𝐸 where the
superdifferential of 𝑑 𝐸    2 /2 has dimension at least 𝑘 − 1.

      For norms induced by an inner product, the first-order equidistance conditions characterize exact
equidistance to the fixed contact points: in the differences of squared distances, the quadratic terms
cancel, leaving precisely the linearized equations. This agrees with the identification of contact vectors
and contact covectors through the Riesz isomorphism, whose restriction to the unit sphere is 𝐽. Since this
isomorphism preserves affine dimension, the ordinary and dual definitions coincide: 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸) = 𝑀𝑘 (𝐸).
For general 𝐶 1 norms, the affine dimensions of the contact vectors and contact covectors may differ,
and so may the corresponding medial axes.
      At a point of 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸), the linearized equidistance conditions leave a space of displacements of
dimension at most 𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1, suggesting the dimension in an analogue of Theorem 1.1. However, turning
this calculation for fixed contact points into a rectifiability theorem requires accounting for the variation
of the nearest points with the center. Our first result establishes this rectifiability conclusion for the
dual medial axes. We then examine under what additional assumptions they coincide with the ordinary
higher-order medial axes.
Theorem 1.2. Let 𝑋 be an 𝑛-dimensional real normed vector space whose norm is 𝐶 1 on 𝑋 \ {0}, and
let 𝐸 ⊆ 𝑋 be nonempty and closed. For every integer 1 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1, the dual 𝑘-medial axis 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸) is
(𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1)-rectifiable. In particular, its Hausdorff dimension satisfies
                                            dim 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸) ≤ 𝑛 − 𝑘 + 1.
    We give a direct geometric proof of Theorem 1.2. Working with contact vectors and contact
covectors, we show how affine independence of the latter yields a quantitative cone-exclusion condition
and hence containment in local Lipschitz graphs. The proof identifies the subspaces over which these
graphs are defined and controls the aperture of the excluded cones, making explicit the role of the
contact covectors in the local geometry underlying rectifiability.
    The rectifiability conclusion can also be deduced from the theorem of Alberti, Ambrosio, and
Cannarsa [1] on the rectifiability of subdifferential strata of semiconvex functions. Indeed, 𝑑 𝐸2 /2 is

locally semiconcave on 𝑋 with a general modulus, so the superdifferential characterization of 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸)
described above allows us to apply their theorem after changing sign. We give the details in Section 4.
    We next compare the ordinary and dual medial axes. Under the additional assumption of strict
convexity, the two notions coincide for 𝑘 = 2, 3. Consequently, the conclusion of Theorem 1.1 remains
valid for the ordinary 2- and 3-medial axes in this class of norms.
Theorem 1.3. Let 𝑛 ≥ 2, and let 𝑋 be an 𝑛-dimensional real normed vector space whose norm is
strictly convex and 𝐶 1 on 𝑋 \ {0}. Let 𝐸 ⊆ 𝑋 be nonempty and closed. Then
                              𝑀2 (𝐸) = 𝑀2𝐽 (𝐸)          and        𝑀3 (𝐸) = 𝑀3𝐽 (𝐸).
Hence 𝑀2 (𝐸) is (𝑛 − 1)-rectifiable and 𝑀3 (𝐸) is (𝑛 − 2)-rectifiable. In particular, if 𝑛 = 2, then 𝑀2 (𝐸)
is 1-rectifiable and 𝑀3 (𝐸) is countable.

shows that failure can occur even for smooth strictly convex norms with positively curved unit spheres
arbitrarily 𝐶 ∞ -close to the Euclidean sphere S2 in R3 . We adopt the convention that the Euclidean unit
sphere has principal curvatures +1.

𝐵 𝜂 , such that the following properties hold:
    (i) ∥·∥ 0 is the Euclidean norm;
    (ii) ∥·∥ 𝜂 is strictly convex and 𝐶 ∞ on R3 \ {0}, and 𝜕𝐵 𝜂 has positive Euclidean principal curvatures;

    The construction uses the homothetic-tetrahedron mechanism described by Icking, Klein, Lê, and
Ma [8, Section 5]. Here we realize this mechanism on smooth positively curved unit spheres arbitrarily
𝐶 ∞ -close to the Euclidean sphere and compute the resulting loss of affine dimension of the contact
covectors. Theorem 1.4 implies that the direct analogue of Theorem 1.1 for ordinary medial axes fails
already for (𝑛, 𝑘) = (3, 4), even under these regularity and curvature assumptions. Along the line
segment in our construction, the four contact vectors are affinely independent, whereas their contact
covectors have affine dimension exactly 2. We verify this in Section 6. The construction therefore
exhibits the loss of affine dimension that separates ordinary and dual higher-order medial axes.
    The rest of the article is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the notation and preliminary
results. Section 3 gives the direct geometric proof of Theorem 1.2. Section 4 establishes the
superdifferential characterization of the dual medial axes and derives an alternative proof from the
theorem of Alberti, Ambrosio, and Cannarsa. Section 5 proves Theorem 1.3. Section 6 constructs the
smooth three-dimensional example of Theorem 1.4 in which the affine dimension decreases under the
duality map, yielding a failure of rectifiability for the ordinary 4-medial axis, and explains its extension
to higher dimensions and higher orders. Finally, Section 7 discusses several open problems.

Throughout this section, |·|, ·, and ∇ refer to the standard Euclidean norm, inner product, and gradient
in the displayed coordinates on R𝑛 .
     We treat the case (𝑛, 𝑘) = (3, 4) in full detail, because it displays the rank-loss mechanism in its
simplest geometric form. We prove Theorem 1.4 by constructing a strictly convex smooth norm on
R3 , whose unit sphere has positive Euclidean principal curvatures and is arbitrarily 𝐶 ∞ -close to the
Euclidean unit sphere, together with a four-point set 𝐸 whose 4-medial axis contains a nondegenerate
line segment. In the final subsection, we explain how to extend the construction to every 𝑛 ≥ 3 and
4 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1.
     The homothetic-tetrahedron mechanism underlying the construction appears in Icking, Klein, Lê,
and Ma [8, Section 5], who describe a continuum of such tetrahedra before modifying their construction
to obtain finitely many centers stable under perturbations of the sites. Here we give a localized 𝐶 ∞
realization of this mechanism, retaining positive curvature and arbitrary 𝐶 ∞ proximity to the Euclidean
sphere, and identify the corresponding loss of affine dimension of the contact covectors.
     Along the resulting segment of centers, the four contact vectors are affinely independent, whereas
their contact covectors have affine dimension exactly 2. Thus the segment lies in 𝑀4 (𝐸) \ 𝑀4𝐽 (𝐸),
exhibiting a failure of the ordinary rectifiability conclusion while remaining consistent with Theorem 1.2.

7    Concluding remarks and open problems
Theorem 1.2 gives a natural analogue of Theorem 1.1 in arbitrary finite-dimensional normed spaces
whose norm is 𝐶 1 on 𝑋 \ {0}, with the ordinary medial axis 𝑀𝑘 (𝐸) replaced by the dual medial
axis 𝑀𝑘𝐽 (𝐸), defined through the affine dimension of contact covectors rather than contact vectors.
Theorem 1.3 shows that, for strictly convex norms that are 𝐶 1 on 𝑋 \ {0}, the two notions coincide for
𝑘 = 2 and 𝑘 = 3, so Liang’s rectifiability conclusion remains valid for the ordinary medial axes 𝑀2 (𝐸)
and 𝑀3 (𝐸). By contrast, the construction of Section 6 shows that the corresponding rectifiability
conclusion for ordinary medial axes can fail already for 𝑘 = 4 in dimension three, even for smooth
positively curved unit spheres arbitrarily 𝐶 ∞ -close to the Euclidean sphere. The extension outlined
there exhibits the same rank-loss mechanism for every 𝑛 ≥ 3 and 4 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1.
    Several natural questions remain.
Question 1. Let 𝑛 ≥ 3. What characterizes strictly convex norms that are 𝐶 1 on 𝑋 \ {0} and satisfy
property RP 𝑘 for a given 4 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1? Are there nonellipsoidal examples? Since every affinely
independent collection of unit vectors extends to an affine basis of 𝑋 consisting of unit vectors, property
RP𝑛+1 implies RP 𝑘 for every 2 ≤ 𝑘 ≤ 𝑛 + 1. Does RP𝑛+1 characterize norms induced by inner products?
