Borisov's [distant-perturbation paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0608006v1), Theorems 1.1–1.7 and Lemmas 4.2–4.4, gives a meromorphic local-inverse reduction and secular determinant. Its actual localization assumptions require bounded support domains, and Theorem 1.2 allows eigenvalues converging to zero. The [three-body Casimir calculation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0905.0448v1), equations (1)–(10), supplies trace-log multiple scattering and competition of multi-object terms. [Schaden](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2475v2), equations (5), (15)–(17), (21)–(23), isolates irreducible contributions and a scalar sign theorem. That theorem requires positive scalar scattering potentials and a different vacuum-energy observable; P3R uses attractive noncommuting matrix potentials and a negative spectral moment. Neither can be instantiated to force the claimed sign reversal. They establish the method and nonadditivity, not an exact duplicate.

[Mukunda et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9907080v1), equation (2.8), gives the invariant tr(P1 P2 P3), insensitive to individual vector phases. [Benseny et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.02398v2), equations (2)–(5), makes the loop phase explicit in a three-site Hamiltonian; its continuum discussion uses numerical dynamics on a ring.
