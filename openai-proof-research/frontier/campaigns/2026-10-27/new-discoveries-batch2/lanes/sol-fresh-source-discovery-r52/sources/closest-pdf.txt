    Power Concavity of Low-Dimensional Unimodular Fiber Counts
                                             Scott Schubr

                                          October 22, 2026


                                               Abstract
         We prove a global power-Jensen inequality for unimodular integer ﬁbers whose central aﬃne
     dimension is at most three. For an integrable integer-valued demand A with feasible ﬁbers
     almost surely and integral mean b, a bounded nonempty central ﬁber of dimension at most
     three satisﬁes EN (A)1/6 ≤ N (b)1/6 , and hence logarithmic Jensen. The expectations are ﬁnite
     and countable support is permitted. The proof constructs a monotone concave extension of the
     sixth root of type-A3 lattice counts with all twelve directed bounds varying. Classical cham-
     ber formulas and Lorentzian volume theory combine with a degree-six certiﬁcate, wall gluing,
     shortest-path closure and a common integral rank-three reduction. We also prove a sharp cor-
     rected square-root inequality in dimension two and determine its optimal leading large-count
     exponent deﬁcit. For the complete four-vertex DAG, the classical continuous count extension
     has optimal concavity power 4/13; exact integral examples give a separate cube-root obstruc-
     tion. A product-preserving directed-rounding theorem for opposite-edge deformations and a
     full-dimensional triangle obstruction describe the scope of local balancing methods. Exact ﬁnite
     certiﬁcates accompany the complete analytic and geometric proofs.

Keywords. Unimodular ﬁbers; lattice-point enumeration; power concavity; Lorentzian polynomi-
als; integer ﬂows.

    Theorem 1.4 includes three-dimensional interacting ﬁbers: when s, t, u > 0, all six edges can
carry positive ﬂow. Its chamber enumeration is classical, with an early seven-case treatment in
Tarski’s 1958 report [6, Section VII]. For the formulas used here see De Loera–Sturmfels [7, Section
5] and the earlier computations cited there. We use those polynomials, deriving them again for
completeness, and prove a power-concavity property of their continuous extension. Root-direction
inequalities and normalized Lorentzian series for restricted Kostant functions appear in Khare–
Matherne–St. Dizier [8]; they are diﬀerent from concavity of counts in demand space.
    The geometry behind Theorem 1.2 is also classical. Alcoved triangulations and volume–lattice-
count identities are developed by Lam–Postnikov [9, 10]. The relevant planar count formula follows
from Pick and mixed area. Its continuous concavity belongs to the Brunn–Minkowski/generalized-
Wills framework [11]. General lattice-point Brunn–Minkowski inequalities [12] require an enlarge-
ment that is absent from (1.1). The continuous Wills inequality and chamber-count formulas
provide the classical inputs. Their exact-count applications, quantitative exponents and the global
A3 power inequality are developed here.
    A diﬀerent deformation of type-A3 alcoved polytopes is treated in Section 7: the intervals on
two opposite edges may vary independently, while the other four intervals remain ﬁxed. The proof
establishes a directed-midpoint product inequality for their exact multiplicities and transfers it to
rectangle counts. The directed operation is classical [13], as is the four-functions summation the-
orem [14, 15]; the proof veriﬁes the required interval-envelope compatibility explicitly. For integer
interval multiplicities, Speyer’s L-convex marginal theorem [16] supplies an alternative argument
given below. A triangle-supported example then separates the count-level conclusion from local
balanced-rounding methods. All six intervals are covered by the global theorem below, although
the local directed-rounding mechanism does not extend to them.

Reading the main argument. The global theorem is proved ﬁrst by constructing the type-A3
sixth-root extension, then transferring one common lattice and active face to the whole demand
family, including countable support. The sharper planar powers, the four-vertex DAG and the local
rounding obstruction follow as separate reﬁnements. Monotonicity precedes shortest-path closure
in the global route; the transfer uses its own rank-three classiﬁcation and does not substitute the
planar argument.

2.3   Gluing, raw bounds, and exact boundary values
Inside the strict metric domain, volume is C 1 even across changes of combinatorial type: its deriva-
tive in a ﬁxed support is facet area divided by the ﬁxed normal length, continuously tending to
zero when that facet disappears. Thus V has no derivative jump at a square wall; Q is the same
global quadratic, and L in (2) has only downward derivative jumps. Also F ≥ 1: after the non-
negative gauge, mixed-volume positivity and L ≥ 0 give V, Q, L ≥ 0. The derivative jumps of F 1/6
have the same sign as those of F . Piecewise concavity consequently glues along generic segments;
approximation covers segments in walls and the boundary of C. Hence F 1/6 is concave on all tight
supports.

8    Block products and scope
Corollary 8.1. Let a ﬁnite DAG be assembled at articulation vertices from blocks that each have
cycle rank at most three. Then its integer-ﬂow counts satisfy (1.1) for every admissible family.

Proof. For a leaf block attached at v, its induced demand is the original demand away from v and
minus the sum of those demands at v. The remaining demand at v is the original demand minus
this induced block demand. These maps are integer linear. Restricting ﬂows to the two edge sets is
a Cartesian-product bijection, so counts multiply. Iterate over blocks, apply Theorem 1.1 to each,
and multiply the logarithmic inequalities. Disconnected components factor as well.

    For a ﬁxed number k of blocks, the geometric mean on Rk≥0 is increasing and concave. Applying
it to the block sixth-root inequalities gives power 1/(6k) for the product count. Theorem 1.3 gives
the optimal leading large-count deﬁcit for the universal low-central-dimension class. The optimality
in Theorem 1.4 concerns its speciﬁed continuous extension. Proposition 6.2 bounds that lattice-
only power between 4/13 and α∗ < 1/3 for the complete four-vertex DAG. The opposite-edge result
in Theorem 7.1 addresses another restricted family and does not change this lattice-power bound.
Theorem 1.1 resolves (1.1) in central aﬃne dimension at most three. The structural checkers
exhaustively verify the speciﬁed ﬁnite identities used in the proof. The manuscript supplies the
all-parameter concavity, closure and integrability arguments; the rounding diagnostics illustrate
their stated ﬁnite test ranges.
