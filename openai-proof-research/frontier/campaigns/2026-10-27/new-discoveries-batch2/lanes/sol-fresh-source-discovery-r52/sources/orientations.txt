Introduction In this paper we mostly study orientations and special subgraphs of Eulerian graphs. Recall that a graph G G is Eulerian if each vertex of G G has even degree. In the literature it is often assumed that an Eulerian graph is also connected, but we will not require connectedness in this paper. Let ε ⁡ ( G ) \varepsilon(G) be the number of Eulerian orientations, that is, the orientations where each vertex has in-degree equal to the out-degree. Counting Eulerian orientations has triggered considerable interest in combinatorics, computer science and statistical physics. Probably, the best known result is due to Lieb [ 10 ] who determined the asymptotic number of Eulerian orientations of large grid graphs. Schrijver [ 14 ] gave a lower bound on the number of Eulerian orientations in terms of the degree sequence. Welsh [ 17 ] observed that for a 4 4 –regular graph the Tutte-polynomial evaluation | T G ​ ( 0 , − 2 ) | |T_{G}(0,-2)| is exactly the number of Eulerian orientations since nowhere-zero Z 3 Z_{3} -flows and Eulerian orientations are in one-to-one correspondence for 4 4 –regular graphs. Mihail and Winkler [ 12 ] gave an efficient randomized algorithm to sample and approximately count Eulerian orientations. Let h ⁡ ( G ) h(G) denote the number of half graphs, that is, subgraphs F F such that d F ​ ( v ) = d G ​ ( v ) / 2 d_{F}(v)=d_{G}(v)/2 for each vertex v v . Note that h ⁡ ( G ) > 0 h(G)>0 if G G is not only Eulerian, but each of its connected component has an even number of edges. This condition is clearly necessary to have a half graph, and also sufficient: every second edge of an Eulerian tour will determine a half graph. Recently, M. Borbényi and P. Csikvári [ 4 ] proved that ε ⁡ ( G ) ≥ h ⁡ ( G ) \varepsilon(G)\geq h(G) holds true for all Eulerian graphs with equality if and and only if G G is bipartite. In this paper we give a simple new proof of this fact, and we give identities and inequalities for the number of Eulerian orientations and half graphs of a 2 2 -cover of a graph G G . In fact, we study the number of orientations and factors of 2 2 -covers with prescribed in-degree and degree, respectively. 1.1. Results. Graph cover techniques played important roles in the resolution of many open problems. A. Marcus, D. Spielmann and N. Srivastava [ 11 ] used graph covers to construct Ramanujan graphs. The idea was suggested by Y. Bilu and N. Linial [ 3 ] . Y. Zhao [ 19 ] used the bipartite double cover to prove a conjecture of N. Alon [ 2 ] and J. Kahn [ 9 ] on the number of independent sets. Later he developed his ideas in the paper [ 20 ] . N. Ruozzi [ 13 ] proved a conjecture of A. Sudderth, E. Wainwright, and M. Willsky [ 18 ] on the Bethe approximation of an attractive graphical model by building on an observation due to P. Vontobel [ 16 ] connecting graph covers with Bethe approximation. P. Csikvári [ 6 ] combined graph covers with graph limit theory to prove the so-called Lower Matching Conjecture of S. Friedland, E. Krop and K. Markström [ 7 ] . Next we generalize the concept of Eulerian orientations and half graphs. To every edge of G G assign either o o or s s , that is, orientation or subgraph. Then for all edge we have two choices: if we assigned o o to the edge, then we need to orient it, so we choose one of the end points and add 1 1 to it, and add 0 0 to the other endpoint. (More precisely, we consider the in-degree.) If we assigned s s for the edge, then we need to decide whether we put this edge into subgraph or not, so we either add 1 1 to both endpoints, or add 0 0 to both endpoints. We will call such a configuration a factorientation. We will call the contribution of the edges to the vertex v v the mixed degree of v v , that is, it is the sum of the in-degree from oriented edges and the degree coming from the subgraph. After we choose o o or s s for every edge, we say that a factorientation is balanced, if for every vertex v v the mixed degree is d G ​ ( v ) / 2 d_{G}(v)/2 . Let g ⁡ ( G ) g(G) be the number of balanced factorientations. We can see that this is a generalization of both Eulerian orientations and half graphs, because if we assign o o to each edge, then g ⁡ ( G ) = ε ⁡ ( G ) g(G)=\varepsilon(G) and if we assign s s to each edge, then g ⁡ ( G ) = h ⁡ ( G ) g(G)=h(G) .
