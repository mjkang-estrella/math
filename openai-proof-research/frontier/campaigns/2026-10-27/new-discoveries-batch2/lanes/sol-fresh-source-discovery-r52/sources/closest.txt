For an integrable integer-valued demand A with feasible fibers almost surely and integral mean b, a bounded nonempty central fiber of dimension at most three satisfies E[N(A)^(1/6)]≤N(b)^(1/6), and hence logarithmic Jensen. The expectations are finite and countable support is permitted. The proof constructs a monotone concave extension of the sixth root of type-A₃ lattice counts with all twelve directed bounds varying. Classical chamber formulas and Lorentzian volume theory combine with a degree-six certificate, wall gluing, shortest-path closure and a common integral rank-three reduction. We also prove a sharp corrected square-root inequality in dimension two and determine its optimal leading large-count exponent deficit. For the complete four-vertex DAG, the classical continuous count extension has optimal concavity power 4/13; exact integral examples give a separate cube-root obstruction. A product-preserving directed-rounding theorem for opposite-edge deformations and a full-dimensional triangle obstruction describe the scope of local balancing methods.
