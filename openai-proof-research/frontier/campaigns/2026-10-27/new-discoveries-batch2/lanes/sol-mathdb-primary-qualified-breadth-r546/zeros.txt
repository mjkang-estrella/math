 Largest zero-dimensional intersection of  r r  degree  d d  hypersurfaces 
 
 
 Deepesh Singhal, Yuxin Lin
 

 Abstract. 
    
 Suppose we have  r r  hypersurfaces in  ℙ m \mathbb{P}^{m}  of degree  d d , whose defining polynomials are linearly independent and their intersection is zero-dimensional. Then what is the maximum number of points in the intersection of the  r r  hypersurfaces?
We conjecture an exact formula for this problem and prove it when  m = 2 m=2 . 
    
 We show that this can be used to compute the generalized Hamming weights of the projective Reed-Muller code  PRM q ⁡ ( d , 2 ) \operatorname{PRM}_{q}(d,2)  and hence settle a conjecture of Beelen, Datta, and Ghorpade for  m = 2 m=2 . 
  
 
 
 1.  introduction 

 
 Let  𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q}  be the finite field of size  q q .
Let  S ⁡ ( m , 𝔽 q ) = 𝔽 q ​ [ x 0 , … , x m ] S(m,\mathbb{F}_{q})=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{0},\dots,x_{m}]  and  S d ​ ( m , 𝔽 q ) S_{d}(m,\mathbb{F}_{q})  be its  d t ​ h d^{th}  graded component. Given a positive integer  r ≤ ( m + d d ) = dim 𝔽 q ( S d ​ ( m , 𝔽 q ) ) r\leq\binom{m+d}{d}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(S_{d}(m,\mathbb{F}_{q})) , Beelen, Datta and Ghorpade in  [ 1 ]  define 
 

 
 
 e r ( d , m ; q ) = max { | V ( W ) ( 𝔽 q ) | : W ⊆ S d ( m , 𝔽 q ) , dim ( W ) = r } . e_{r}(d,m;q)=\max\{|V(W)(\mathbb{F}_{q})|:W\subseteq S_{d}(m,\mathbb{F}_{q}),\dim(W)=r\}. 
 
 
 We would like to note that the problem of computing  e r ​ ( d , m , q ) e_{r}(d,m;q) , is equivalent to computing the  r t ​ h r^{th}  generalized Hamming weight of the projective Reed-Muller code  P ​ R ​ M q ​ ( d , m ) PRM_{q}(d,m) .
Beelen, Datta and Ghorpade have conjectured an exact formula for  e r ​ ( d , m , q ) e_{r}(d,m;q) . We will introduce some notations to state their conjecture. 
 
 
 Let  ℕ \mathbb{N}  be the set of non-negative integers. Beelen, Datta and Ghorpade in  [ 1 ]  define 
 

 
 
 Ω ⁡ ( d , m ) = { ( γ 1 , … , γ m + 1 ) ∈ ℕ m + 1 : ∑ i = 1 m + 1 γ i = d } . \Omega(d,m)=\Big\{(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m+1})\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}:\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}\gamma_{i}=d\Big\}. 
 
 
 For  1 ≤ r ≤ | Ω ⁡ ( d , m ) | = ( m + d d ) 1\leq r\leq|\Omega(d,m)|=\binom{m+d}{d} , let  ω r ​ ( d , m ) = ( β 1 , … , β m + 1 ) \omega_{r}(d,m)=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m+1})  be its  r t ​ h r^{th}  largest element under lexicographical ordering. Then, they define 
 

 
 
 H r ​ ( d , m , q ) = ∑ i = 1 m β i ​ q m − i . H_{r}(d,m;q)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta_{i}q^{m-i}. 
 
 
 Let  π m ​ ( q ) = | ℙ m ​ ( 𝔽 q ) | = q m + 1 − 1 q − 1 \pi_{m}(q)=|\mathbb{P}^{m}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|=\frac{q^{m+1}-1}{q-1} . If  m < 0 m<0 , we set  π m ​ ( q ) = 0 \pi_{m}(q)=0 . 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1   (Beelen-Datta-Ghorpade Conjecture) . 
 
 [ 2 ] 
Suppose  m , d , r m,d,r  are positive integers that satisfy  1 ≤ r ≤ ( m + d d ) 1\leq r\leq\binom{m+d}{d} . Pick the unique  1 ≤ l ≤ m + 1 1\leq l\leq m+1 , for which 
 

 
 
 ( m + d d ) − ( m + d + 1 − l d ) < r ≤ ( m + d d ) − ( m + d − l d ) . \tbinom{m+d}{d}-\tbinom{m+d+1-l}{d}<r\leq\tbinom{m+d}{d}-\tbinom{m+d-l}{d}. 
 
 
 Let  j = r − ( m + d d ) + ( m + d + 1 − l d ) j=r-\binom{m+d}{d}+\binom{m+d+1-l}{d} , so  0 < j ≤ ( m + d − l d − 1 ) 0<j\leq\binom{m+d-l}{d-1} .
Then for  q ≥ d + 1 q\geq d+1  we have 
 

 
 
 e r ​ ( d , m , q ) = H j ​ ( d − 1 , m − l + 1 , q ) + π m − l ​ ( q ) . e_{r}(d,m;q)=H_{j}(d-1,m-l+1;q)+\pi_{m-l}(q). 
 
 
 
 
 
 We denote this conjectured formula as 
 

 
 
 f r ​ ( d , m , q ) = H j ​ ( d − 1 , m − l + 1 , q ) + π m − l ​ ( q ) . f_{r}(d,m;q)=H_{j}(d-1,m-l+1;q)+\pi_{m-l}(q). 
 
 
 For fixed  m m ,  d d  and  r r , it is a polynomial in  q q .
We show that there is a simpler way of describing this polynomial. 
 
 
 Proposition 1.2 . 
 
 Given positive integers  m , d , r m,d,r  that satisfy  1 ≤ r ≤ ( m + d d ) 1\leq r\leq\binom{m+d}{d} . Suppose  w r ​ ( d , m ) = ( β 1 , … , β m + 1 ) w_{r}(d,m)=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m+1}) . Let  l l  be the smallest index for which  β l ≠ 0 \beta_{l}\neq 0 . Then we have 
 

 
 
 ( m + d d ) − ( m + d + 1 − l d ) < r ≤ ( m + d d ) − ( m + d − l d ) , \tbinom{m+d}{d}-\tbinom{m+d+1-l}{d}<r\leq\tbinom{m+d}{d}-\tbinom{m+d-l}{d}, 
 
 
 and 
 

 
 
 f r ​ ( d , m , q ) = H r ​ ( d , m , q ) + π m − l − 1 ​ ( q ) . f_{r}(d,m;q)=H_{r}(d,m;q)+\pi_{m-l-1}(q).

In  [ 9 ] , we show that Conjecture  1.1  is true for any  m m ,  d d  and  r r  when  q q  is sufficiently large.

 Given positive integers  d , r d,r  such that  r ≤ ( d + 2 2 ) r\leq\binom{d+2}{2}  and  q ≥ d + 1 q\geq d+1 . We have

Let  S d ​ ( m , κ ) S_{d}(m,\kappa)  be the  d t ​ h d^{th}  graded component of  κ ⁡ [ x 0 , … , x m ] \kappa[x_{0},\dots,x_{m}] .

 2.1.  Cayley-Bacharach Theorem 

 
 Let  κ \kappa  be an algebraically closed field. 
 
 
 Definition 2.1 . 
 
 Let  Γ \Gamma  be a zero-dimensional subscheme of  ℙ 2 ​ ( κ ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(\kappa) . We denote its length as  | Γ | m |\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}  (the subscript  m \mathit{m}  indicates that the points are counted with multiplicity). We denote the number of points counted without multiplicity as  | Γ | |\Gamma| .
We denote the ideal of  Γ \Gamma  as  I ⁡ ( Γ ) I(\Gamma) . We denote the graded coordinate ring as  S ⁡ ( Γ ) = κ ⁡ [ x 0 , x 1 , x 2 ] / I ⁡ ( Γ ) S(\Gamma)=\kappa[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]/I(\Gamma)  and its  k t ​ h k^{th}  homogeneous piece as  S k ​ ( Γ ) S_{k}(\Gamma) . 
 
 
 Suppose that  Γ ′ ⊆ Γ \Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma  are subschemes of  ℙ 2 ​ ( κ ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(\kappa) . Then the residual subscheme of  Γ ′ \Gamma^{\prime}  in  Γ \Gamma  is the scheme whose ideal is 
 

 
 
 I ⁡ ( Γ ′′ ) = Ann ⁡ ( I ⁡ ( Γ ′ ) / I ⁡ ( Γ ) ) . I(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})={\rm Ann}(I(\Gamma^{\prime})/I(\Gamma)). 
 
 
 
 
 
 Note that while we can define this in general, this notion is well behaved only when  Γ \Gamma  is a complete intersection.
For example, we have  | Γ | m = | Γ ′ | m + | ​ Γ ′′ | m |\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}=|\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}+|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}  when  Γ \Gamma  is a complete intersection (see  [ 6 ] ).

 Suppose  I ⁡ ( Γ ) = ( x 2 , x ​ y , y 2 ) I(\Gamma)=(x^{2},xy,y^{2}) , so it is not a complete intersection. Take  I ⁡ ( Γ ′ ) = ( x , y ) I(\Gamma^{\prime})=(x,y) . Then, we have  I ⁡ ( Γ ′′ ) = ( x , y ) I(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})=(x,y) . Note that  | Γ | m = 3 |\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}=3 ,  | Γ ′ | m = 1 |\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}=1  and  | Γ ′′ | m = 1 |\Gamma^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}=1 , so  | Γ | m ≠ | Γ ′ | m + | ​ Γ ′′ | m |\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}\neq|\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}+|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathit{m}} . 
 
 (2)  
 
 Suppose  I ⁡ ( Γ ) = ( x 2 , y 2 ) I(\Gamma)=(x^{2},y^{2}) , so it is a complete intersection. Take  I ⁡ ( Γ ′ ) = ( x , y ) I(\Gamma^{\prime})=(x,y) . Then, we have  I ⁡ ( Γ ′′ ) = ( x ​ y , x 2 , y 2 ) I(\Gamma^{\prime\prime})=(xy,x^{2},y^{2}) . Note that  | Γ | m = 4 |\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}=4 ,  | Γ ′ | m = 1 |\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}=1  and  | Γ ′′ | m = 3 |\Gamma^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}=3 , so  | Γ | m = | Γ ′ | m + | ​ Γ ′′ | m |\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}=|\Gamma^{\prime}|_{\mathit{m}}+|\Gamma^{\prime\prime}|_{\mathit{m}} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 For a zero-dimensional subscheme  Γ \Gamma  of  ℙ 2 ​ ( κ ) \mathbb{P}^{2}(\kappa) , and nonnegative integer  k k , we denote 
 

 
 
 g Γ ​ ( k ) = | Γ | m − dim ( S k ​ ( Γ ) ) = dim ( I k ​ ( Γ ) ) − ( m + k k ) + | Γ | m . g_{\Gamma}(k)=|\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}-\dim(S_{k}(\Gamma))=\dim(I_{k}(\Gamma))-\tbinom{m+k}{k}+|\Gamma|_{\mathit{m}}. 
 
 
 Note that  I k ​ ( Γ ) I_{k}(\Gamma)  is the vector space of degree  k k  polynomials that vanish on  Γ \Gamma . Next,  dim ( S k ​ ( Γ ) ) \dim(S_{k}(\Gamma))  is the Hilbert function, giving us the dimension of the  k t ​ h k^{th}  homogeneous piece of the coordinate ring, and it can also be viewed as the number of independent conditions imposed by  Γ \Gamma  on degree  k k  polynomials. Finally,  g Γ ​ ( k ) g_{\Gamma}(k)  is the failure of the points in  Γ \Gamma  to impose independent conditions on polynomials of degree  k k . 
 
 
 Noether computed the Hilbert function for the zero-dimensional subschemes of  ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2}  that are obtained as the intersection of two curves.

 [ 7 ,  6 ] 
Suppose  X 1 X_{1}  and  X 2 X_{2}  are curves of degrees  a a  and  b b  with no common components. Consider the zero-dimensional scheme  Γ = X 1 ∩ X 2 \Gamma=X_{1}\cap X_{2} . Then for any  k k , the Hilbert function of the scheme  Γ \Gamma  can be written as
