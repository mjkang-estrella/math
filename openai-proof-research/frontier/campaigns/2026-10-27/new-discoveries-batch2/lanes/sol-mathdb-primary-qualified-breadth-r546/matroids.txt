 Abstract. 
    
 Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle  [ 3 ]  announced the following result: let  q = p k q=p^{k}  be a prime power, and let  ℳ \mathcal{M}  be a proper minor-closed class of  GF ⁡ ( q ) \mathrm{GF}(q) -representable matroids, which does not contain  PG ⁡ ( r − 1 , p ) \mathrm{PG}(r-1,p)  for sufficiently high  r r . There exist integers  k , t k,t  such that every vertically  k k -connected matroid in  ℳ \mathcal{M}  is a rank- ( ≤ t ) (\leq t)  perturbation of a frame matroid or the dual of a frame matroid over  GF ⁡ ( q ) \mathrm{GF}(q) . They further announced a characterization of the perturbations through the introduction of subfield templates and frame templates. 
    
 We show a family of dyadic matroids that form a counterexample to this result. We offer several weaker conjectures to replace the ones in  [ 3 ] , discuss consequences for some published papers, and discuss the impact of these new conjectures on the structure of frame templates. 
  
 
 1991 Mathematics Subject Classification 05B35
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 Robertson and Seymour profoundly transformed graph theory with their Graph Minors Theorem  [ 18 ] . Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle are on track to do the same for matroid theory with their Matroid Structure Theory for matroids representable over a finite field (see, e.g.  [ 4 ] ). The theorem they intend to prove is the following: 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.1   (Matroid Structure Theorem, rough idea) . 
 
 Let  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  be a finite field, and let  ℳ \mathcal{M}  be a proper minor-closed class of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -representable matroids. If  M ∈ ℳ M\in\mathcal{M}  is sufficiently large and has sufficiently high branch-width, then  M M  has a tree-decomposition, the parts of which correspond to mild modifications of matroids representable over a proper subfield of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} , or to mild modifications of frame matroids and their duals. 
 
 
 
 The words “tree-decomposition”, “parts”, “correspond to” and “mild modifications” need (a lot of) elaboration, and hide over 15 years of very hard work. Whittle  [ 22 ]  described the proof of Rota’s Conjecture, which has the Matroid Structure Theorem as a major ingredient, as follows: 
 
 
 
 “It’s a little bit like discovering a new mountain – we’ve crossed many hurdles to reach a new destination and we have returned scratched, bloodied and bruised from the arduous journey – we now need to create a pathway so others can reach it.” 
 
 
 
 In this paper we will only focus on the last part of Conjecture  1.1 . Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle announced a theorem about that part  [ 3 , Theorem 3.1]  that we will repeat here as Conjecture  1.2 . First, we require some definitions. An  𝔽 \mathbb{F} - represented matroid  (or simply  represented matroid  if the field is understood from the context) is a matroid with a fixed class of representation matrices over  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  that are row equivalent up to column scaling and removal of zero rows. A  represented frame matroid  is a matroid with a representation matrix  A A  that has at most two nonzero entries per column. The matroids we will be working with in Section  3  are dyadic and therefore ternary. Since ternary matroids are uniquely  GF ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{GF}(3) -representable  [ 2 ] , we will not make any distinction between matroids and represented matroids in that case. We also extend this convention to binary matroids, particularly complete graphic matroids, since every binary matroid that is representable over some field  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  is uniquely  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -representable. 
 
 
 A matroid (or represented matroid) is  vertically  k k -connected if, for every separation  ( A , B ) (A,B)  of order less than  k k , one of  A A  and  B B  spans  E ⁡ ( M ) E(M) . If a matroid (or represented matroid)  M M  is vertically  k k -connected, then  M ∗ M^{*}  is  cyclically  k k -connected. A  rank- ( ≤ t ) (\leq t)  perturbation  of a represented matroid  M M  is the represented matroid obtained by adding a matrix of rank at most  t t  to the representation matrix of  M M . 
 
 
 Conjecture 1.2   ( [ 3 , Theorem 3.1] ) . 
 
 Let  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  be a finite field and let  ℳ \mathcal{M}  be a proper minor-closed class of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -represented matroids. Then there exist  k , t ∈ ℤ + k,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}  such that each vertically  k k -connected member of  ℳ \mathcal{M}  is a rank- ( ≤ t ) (\leq t)  perturbation of an  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -represented matroid  N N , such that either 
 
 (i)  
 
 N N  is a represented frame matroid, 
 
 (ii)  
 
 N ∗ N^{*}  is a represented frame matroid, or 
 
 (iii)  
 
 N N  is confined to a proper subfield of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} . 
 
 
 
 
 
 In this paper we present a counterexample to this conjecture. In particular, we build a family of dyadic matroids that are vertically  k k -connected for any desired  k k , and not a bounded-rank perturbation of either a represented frame matroid or the dual of a represented frame matroid. The construction starts with a cyclically  k k -connected graph  G G , modifies it at a number of vertices that grows with  | V ⁡ ( G ) | |V(G)| , and dualizes the resulting matroid. We detail the construction, and prove its key properties, in Section  3 . 
 
 
 Vertical connectivity and cographic matroids are not very compatible notions. Because of this, our examples, which are very sparse, arise only in situations where one might expect the second outcome of the conjecture to hold. For this reason, the proof of the Matroid Structure Theorem itself is not jeopardized, and versions of Conjecture  1.2  can be recovered. In Section  4 , we provide several such conjectures. Included in Section  4  is Section  4.3 , where we discuss consequences for  [ 10 ]  and  [ 15 ] . In Section  5 , we discuss consequences for the notion of  frame templates , introduced in  [ 3 ]  to describe the perturbations in more detail. 
 
 
 
 2.  Preliminaries 

 
 Unexplained notation and terminology will generally follow Oxley   [ 16 ] . One exception is that we denote the vector matroid of a matrix  A A  by  M ⁡ ( A ) M(A) , rather than  M ⁡ [ A ] M[A] . The following characterization of the dyadic matroids was shown by Whittle in  [ 23 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 2.1 . 
 
 A matroid is  dyadic  if and only if it is representable over both  GF ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{GF}(3)  and  GF ⁡ ( 5 ) \mathrm{GF}(5) .

 As stated in the introduction, our construction makes use of highly cyclically connected graphs. The next two results allow us to specify some additional details about these graphs. The following lemma seems to be fairly well-known; however, we were unable to find an explicit proof in the literature. For the sake of completeness, we state the result and provide a proof, obtained by combining some older results. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.12 . 
 
 For every positive integer  k k , there is a cyclically  k k -connected cubic graph. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 There is a cubic Cayley graph of girth  g ≥ k g\geq k . (See, for example, Biggs   [ 1 ]  or Jajcay and Širáň   [ 11 , Theorem 2.1] . In particular,   [ 11 ]  contains a nice summary of various related results.)  4.3.  Consequences 

 
 We conclude Section  4  by detailing the consequences of our results on previously published papers that were written based on  [ 3 ] . It should be noted that our results in   [ 10 ]  remain correct insofar as they regard templates themselves, as opposed to the applications of Conjecture  4.1 . This means that all of the proofs in  [ 10 , Section 3]  remain valid. In particular, the determination of the minimal nontrivial binary frame templates  [ 10 , Theorem 3.19]  is accurate. (However,  [ 10 , Definition 3.20]  is no longer useful to prove the results in  [ 10 , Section 4] .) 
 
 
 A significant portion of our work in  [ 10 ] , our forthcoming work, and the work of Nelson and Walsh  [ 14 ]  involves the use of the structure theory of Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle to obtain results about the extremal functions (also called growth rate functions) of classes of represented matroids. We will prove that these results are true subject to Hypothesis  4.6 . The  extremal function  for a minor-closed class  ℳ \mathcal{M} , denoted by  h ℳ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}(r) , is the function whose value at an integer  r ≥ 0 r\geq 0  is given by the maximum number of elements in a simple represented matroid in  ℳ \mathcal{M}  of rank at most  r r . We will make use of several results in the literature. The first of these is the Growth Rate Theorem of Geelen, Kung, and Whittle  [ 6 , Theorem 1.1] . 
 
 
 Theorem 4.7   (Growth Rate Theorem) . 
 
 If  ℳ \mathcal{M}  is a nonempty minor-closed class of matroids, then there exists  c ∈ ℝ c\in\mathbb{R}  such that either: 
 
 (1)  
 
 h ℳ ​ ( r ) ≤ c ​ r h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)\leq cr  for all  r r , 
 
 (2)  
 
 ( r + 1 2 ) ≤ h ℳ ​ ( r ) ≤ c ​ r 2 \binom{r+1}{2}\leq h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)\leq cr^{2}  for all  r r  and  ℳ \mathcal{M}  contains all graphic matroids, 
 
 (3)  
 
 there is a prime-power  q q  such that  q r − 1 q − 1 ≤ h ℳ ​ ( r ) ≤ c ​ q r \frac{q^{r}-1}{q-1}\leq h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)\leq cq^{r}  for all  r r  and  ℳ \mathcal{M}  contains all  GF ⁡ ( q ) \mathrm{GF}(q) -representable matroids, or 
 
 (4)  
 
 ℳ \mathcal{M}  contains all simple rank-2 matroids. 
 
 
 
 
 
 If (2) of the previous theorem holds for  ℳ \mathcal{M} , then  ℳ \mathcal{M}  is  quadratically dense . If  M M  is a simple rank- r r  matroid in  ℳ \mathcal{M}  such that  ε ​ ( M ) = h ℳ ​ ( r ) \varepsilon(M)=h_{\mathcal{M}}(r) , then we call  M M  an  extremal matroid  of  ℳ \mathcal{M} .

Let  ℳ \mathcal{M}  be a quadratically dense minor-closed class of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -represented matroids, and let  { Φ 1 , … , Φ s , Ψ 1 , … , Ψ t } \{\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{s},\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{t}\}  be the set of templates given by Hypothesis  4.6 . For all sufficiently large  r r , the extremal matroids of  ℳ \mathcal{M}  are the extremal matroids of the templates in some subset of  { Φ 1 , … , Φ s } \{\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{s}\} . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let  p p  be the characteristic of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} . Since  ℳ \mathcal{M}  is a quadratically dense minor-closed class and since  ε ⁡ ( PG ⁡ ( r − 1 , 𝔽 p ) ) = p r − 1 p − 1 \varepsilon(\mathrm{PG}(r-1,\mathbb{F}_{p}))=\frac{p^{r}-1}{p-1} , for all sufficiently large  r r , no member of  ℳ \mathcal{M}  contains  PG ⁡ ( r − 1 , 𝔽 p ) \mathrm{PG}(r-1,\mathbb{F}_{p})  as a minor. By Hypothesis  4.6 , there are a pair of integers  k , n k,n  such that every simple vertically  k k -connected member of  ℳ \mathcal{M}  with an  M ⁡ ( K n ) M(K_{n}) -minor is a member of at least one of the classes  ℳ ⁡ ( Φ 1 ) , … , ℳ ⁡ ( Φ s ) \mathcal{M}(\Phi_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{s}) . 
 
 
 By Lemmas  4.13  and  4.14 , for every frame template  Φ \Phi  and for all sufficiently large  r r , the size of a rank- r r  extremal matroid of  Φ \Phi  is given by a quadratic polynomial in  r r . Thus, for all sufficiently large  r r , the size of the largest simple rank- r r  matroid that conforms to some template in  { Φ 1 , … , Φ s } \{\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{s}\}  is given by a quadratic polynomial  h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(r) . 
 
 
 By definition,  h ℳ ​ ( r ) ≥ h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)\geq h^{\prime}_{\mathcal{M}}(r) . We wish to show that equality holds for all sufficiently large  r r . Suppose otherwise. Then, for infinitely many  r r , we have  h ℳ ​ ( r ) > h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)>h^{\prime}_{\mathcal{M}}(r) . Theorem  4.9 , with  h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(r)  playing the role of  p ⁡ ( n ) p(n)  and with  k k  playing the role of  s s , implies that, for infinitely many  r r , there is a vertically  k k -connected rank- r r  matroid  M r ∈ ℳ M_{r}\in\mathcal{M}  with  ε ⁡ ( M r ) > h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) \varepsilon(M_{r})>h^{\prime}_{\mathcal{M}}(r) . Thus, these  M r M_{r}  do not conform to any template in  { Φ 1 , … , Φ s } \{\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{s}\} . By Hypothesis  4.6 , these  M r M_{r}  contain no  M ⁡ ( K n ) M(K_{n})  minor. However, by Theorem  4.8 , there is an integer  c c  such that  ε ⁡ ( M r ) ≤ c ​ m \varepsilon(M_{r})\leq cm . This contradicts the fact that  ε ⁡ ( M r ) > h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) \varepsilon(M_{r})>h^{\prime}_{\mathcal{M}}(r)  for all  r r . By contradiction, we determine that  h ℳ ​ ( r ) = h ℳ ′ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)=h^{\prime}_{\mathcal{M}}(r) , for all sufficiently large  r r . 
 
 
 Therefore, we know that, for all sufficiently large  r r , the extremal function  h ℳ ​ ( r ) h_{\mathcal{M}}(r)  is given by a quadratic polynomial. Now, Lemma  4.10  implies that, for all sufficiently large  r r , the rank- r r  extremal matroids of  ℳ \mathcal{M}  are vertically  k k -connected. Thus, by Hypothesis  4.6 , it suffices to show that, for all sufficiently large  r r , the largest simple matroids of rank  r r  contain  M ⁡ ( K n ) M(K_{n})  as a minor. Suppose otherwise. Then, for infinitely many  r r , the largest simple matroids in  ℳ \mathcal{M}  of rank  r r  have no  M ⁡ ( K n ) M(K_{n}) -minor. By Theorem  4.8 , for infinitely many  r r , the largest simple matroids in  ℳ \mathcal{M}  of rank  r r  have size at most  c ​ r cr , for some integer  c c . This contradicts the quadratic density of  ℳ \mathcal{M} .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Because of Lemma  4.15 , the results in   [ 10 ]  regarding extremal functions (  [ 10 , Theorem 1.3] ,   [ 10 , Theorem 4.2] , and   [ 10 , Corollary 4.10] ) now require Hypothesis  4.6 . However,   [ 10 , Theorem 4.2]  was also proved (independently of  [ 3 ] ) by Geelen and Nelson  [ 7 , Corollary 1.6] . Furthermore,   [ 10 , Corollary 4.10]  follows easily from   [ 10 , Theorem 4.2] . 
 
 
 Finally, we modify  [ 10 , Theorem 1.4]  to require Tutte connectivity, rather than vertical connectivity. 
 
 
 Theorem 4.16 . 
 
 If Hypothesis  4.3  holds, then there exist  k , l ∈ ℤ + k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}  such that every simple,  k k -connected, 1-flowing matroid with at least  l l  elements is either graphic or cographic. 
 
 
 
 Another paper that relies on the faulty version of the structure theorem is  [ 15 ] . (It is stated as Hypothesis 4.1 in that paper.) The main result of  [ 15 ]  is  [ 15 , Theorem 1.1] . In order to recover this result,  [ 15 , Lemma 3.1]  will need to be refined in order to deal with represented matroids close to the dual of a represented frame matroid. Since this case is significantly easier than the case in which the represented matroid is close to a represented frame matroid, Nelson and Van Zwam expect that the main result can be recovered, but elect to wait with their fix until the structure theorem has been proven by Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle.

 Definition 5.1 . 
 
 A frame template  Φ = ( Γ , C , X , Y 0 , Y 1 , A 1 , Δ , Λ ) \Phi=(\Gamma,C,X,Y_{0},Y_{1},A_{1},\Delta,\Lambda)  is  reduced  if there is a partition  ( X 0 , X 1 ) (X_{0},X_{1})  of  X X  such that

 Lemma 5.2   ( [ 14 , Lemma 5.6] ) . 
 
 Every frame template is equivalent to a reduced frame template. 
 
 
 
 Although  [ 14 ]  was based on the faulty version of the structure theorem, Lemma  5.2  is a result about templates in their own right, rather than Conjecture  4.1 , and remains true for that reason. We will refer to the partition  X = X 0 ∪ X 1 X=X_{0}\cup X_{1}  given in Definition  5.1  as the  reduction partition  of  Φ \Phi . We also introduce the following definition. 
 
 
 Definition 5.3 . 
 
 A frame template  Φ = ( Γ , C , X , Y 0 , Y 1 , A 1 , Δ , Λ ) \Phi=(\Gamma,C,X,Y_{0},Y_{1},A_{1},\Delta,\Lambda)  is  refined  if it is reduced, with reduction partition  X = X 0 ∪ X 1 X=X_{0}\cup X_{1} , and if  Y 1 Y_{1}  spans the matroid  M ⁡ ( A 1 ​ [ X 1 , Y 0 ∪ Y 1 ] ) M(A_{1}[X_{1},Y_{0}\cup Y_{1}]) . 
 
 
 
 Remark 5.4 . 
 
 Each of the minimal nontrivial binary frame templates given in  [ 10 ]  is reduced (often in trivial or vacuous ways) and, in fact, refined. 
 
 
 
 We wish to show that, for the purposes of using the Hypotheses given in Section  4 , only refined frame templates must be considered. 
 
 
 Lemma 5.5 . 
 
 Let  Φ = ( Γ , C , X , Y 0 , Y 1 , A 1 , Δ , Λ ) \Phi=(\Gamma,C,X,Y_{0},Y_{1},A_{1},\Delta,\Lambda)  be a reduced frame template that is not refined. If  M ∈ ℳ ⁡ ( Φ ) M\in\mathcal{M}(\Phi) , then  E ⁡ ( M ) − Y 0 E(M)-Y_{0}  is not spanning in  M M . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let  A A  be the matrix conforming to  Φ \Phi  such that  M = M ⁡ ( A ) / C \ Y 1 M=M(A)/C\backslash Y_{1} . Since  Φ \Phi  is not refined,  Y 1 Y_{1}  does not span  M ⁡ ( A ⁡ [ Y 0 ∪ Y 1 ] ) M(A[Y_{0}\cup Y_{1}]) . Therefore,  Y 0 Y_{0}  contains a cocircuit in  M ⁡ ( A ⁡ [ Y 0 ∪ Y 1 ] ) M(A[Y_{0}\cup Y_{1}]) . In fact, since the definition of reduced implies that  A ⁡ [ X 1 , E − ( Y 0 ∪ Y 1 ∪ Z ) ] A[X_{1},E-(Y_{0}\cup Y_{1}\cup Z)]  is the zero matrix, and since every column of  A ⁡ [ X 1 , Z ] A[X_{1},Z]  is a copy of a column of  A ⁡ [ X 1 , Y 1 ] A[X_{1},Y_{1}] , we see that  Y 0 Y_{0}  contains a cocircuit in  M ⁡ ( A ) M(A) . This implies that  Y 0 Y_{0}  also contains a cocircuit in  M = M ⁡ ( A ) / C \ Y 1 M=M(A)/C\backslash Y_{1} . Thus,  E ⁡ ( M ) − Y 0 E(M)-Y_{0}  is not spanning in  M M .
∎ 
 
 
 
 Now we prove the main result of this section. 
 
 
 Theorem 5.6 . 
 
 If Hypothesis  4.3  holds for a class  ℳ \mathcal{M} , then the constants  k , l k,l , and the templates  Φ 1 , … , Φ s , Ψ 1 , … , Ψ t \Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{s},\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{t}  can be chosen so that the templates are refined. Moreover, If Hypothesis  4.6  holds for a class  ℳ \mathcal{M} , then the constants  k , n k,n , and the templates  Φ 1 , … , Φ s , Ψ 1 , … , Ψ t \Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{s},\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{t}  can be chosen so that the templates are refined. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Suppose that Hypothesis  4.3  holds, and let  Φ ∈ { Φ 1 , … , Φ s , Ψ 1 , … , Ψ t } \Phi\in\{\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{s},\Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{t}\} . By Lemma  5.2 , we may assume that  Φ \Phi  is reduced with reduction partition  X = X 0 ∪ X 1 X=X_{0}\cup X_{1} . Suppose for a contradiction that  Φ \Phi  is not refined. Choose  k ≥ | Y 0 | k\geq|Y_{0}| . If  M M  is a  k k -connected represented matroid conforming to  Φ \Phi , then Lemma  5.5  implies that  λ M ​ ( Y 0 ) < r M ​ ( Y 0 ) ≤ | Y 0 | \lambda_{M}(Y_{0})<r_{M}(Y_{0})\leq|Y_{0}| . Therefore, by  k k -connectivity, we must have  | E ⁡ ( M ) − Y 0 | < | Y 0 | |E(M)-Y_{0}|<|Y_{0}| . Thus, if we set  l ≥ 2 ​ | Y 0 | l\geq 2|Y_{0}| , we obtain a contradiction and conclude that the constants  k , l k,l , and the templates  Φ 1 , … , Φ s \Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{s}  can be chosen so that the templates are refined. Moreover, since  k k -connectivity is closed under duality, the templates  Ψ 1 , … , Ψ t \Psi_{1},\ldots,\Psi_{t}  can be chosen to be refined as well. 
 
 
 Now suppose Hypothesis  4.6  holds, and choose  k ≥ | Y 0 | k\geq|Y_{0}| . Let  M M  be a simple, vertically  k k -connected member of some minor-closed class  ℳ \mathcal{M} , and let  M M  have an  M ⁡ ( K n ) M(K_{n}) -minor but no  P ​ G ​ ( m − 1 , 𝔽 p ) PG(m-1,\mathbb{F}_{p}) -minor for some positive integer  m m . Part (3) of Hypothesis  4.6  implies that  M M  conforms to a template  Φ ∈ { Φ 1 , … , Φ s } \Phi\in\{\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{s}\} . By Lemma  5.2 , we may assume that  Φ \Phi  is reduced with reduction partition  X = X 0 ∪ X 1 X=X_{0}\cup X_{1} . Suppose for a contradiction that  Φ \Phi  is not refined. By Lemma  5.5 ,  E ⁡ ( M ) − Y 0 E(M)-Y_{0}  is not spanning in  M M . This also implies that  λ M ​ ( Y 0 ) < r M ​ ( Y 0 ) ≤ | Y 0 | \lambda_{M}(Y_{0})<r_{M}(Y_{0})\leq|Y_{0}| . By vertical  k k -connectivity,  Y 0 Y_{0}  is spanning in  M M . Thus,  M M  is an  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -represented matroid of rank  r M ​ ( Y 0 ) ≤ | Y 0 | r_{M}(Y_{0})\leq|Y_{0}| . Since  M M  is simple, we must have  | E ⁡ ( M ) | ≤ | 𝔽 | | Y 0 | − 1 | 𝔽 | − 1 |E(M)|\leq\frac{|\mathbb{F}|^{|Y_{0}|}-1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1} ; therefore, we set  n n  sufficiently large so that  | E ⁡ ( K n ) | = ( n 2 ) > | 𝔽 | | Y 0 | − 1 | 𝔽 | − 1 |E(K_{n})|=\binom{n}{2}>\frac{|\mathbb{F}|^{|Y_{0}|}-1}{|\mathbb{F}|-1} .
