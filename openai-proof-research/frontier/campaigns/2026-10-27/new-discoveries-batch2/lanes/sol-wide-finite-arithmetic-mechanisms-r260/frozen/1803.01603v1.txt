                                                                                      1. Introduction
                                            A partition of n is a finite nonincreasing sequence λ = (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λl ) such that n =
                                          λ1 + λ2 + . . . + λl . A summand in a partition is called part. We say that n is the size of λ
                                          and l is the length of λ. For example, λ = (6, 3, 3, 2, 1) is a partition of n = 15. The parts of
                                          the partition λ are 6, 3, 3, 2 and 1. The size of λ is 15 and the length of λ is 5. A partition
                                          λ is called a partition with d-distinct parts if and only if λi − λi+1 ≥ d.
                                            Partitions can be visualized with Young diagram, which is a finite collection of boxes
                                          arranged in left-justified rows, with λi boxes in the i-th row. The pair (i, j) shows that the
                                          coordinates of the boxes in the Young diagram. The Young diagram of λ = (6, 3, 3, 2, 1) is
                                          as follows:


                                                                             (1, 1) (1, 2) (1, 3) (1, 4) (1, 5) (1, 6)

                                                                             (2, 1) (2, 2) (2, 3)

                                                                             (3, 1) (3, 2) (3, 3)

                                                                             (4, 1) (4, 2)

                                                                             (5, 1)

                                          the sum of the number of boxes exactly to the right, exactly below and the box itself. So,

   A partition λ is called a s-core partition if λ has no boxes of hook length s. For example,
the partition λ = (6, 3, 3, 2, 1) is 7-core but it is not 5-core, since λ has no boxes of hook
length 7, but it has a box of hook length 5 (See the diagram (1)).
   A more general definition, a partition λ is called a (s1 , s2 , . . . , st )-core partition if λ has
no boxes of hook length s1 , s2 , . . . , st . So, the partition λ = (6, 3, 3, 2, 1) is (7, 9)-core.
   There are many studies about core partitions and such partitions are closely related to
posets, cranks, Raney numbers, Catalan numbers, Fibonacci numbers etc. [1, 6, 15, 16].
   Anderson [3] gives the result that the number of (s, t)-core partitions is finite if and only
if s and t are coprime. In that case, this number is

  Amdeberhan [1] conjectures that the number of (s, s + 1)-core partitions with distinct
parts equals the Fibonacci numbers. This conjecture is proved independently by Xiong [12]
and Straub [11]. More generally, Straub [11] characterize the number Nd (s) of (s, ds−1)-core
partitions with distinct parts by Nd (1) = 1,Nd (2) = d and, for s ≥ 3,
                                 Nd (s) = Nd (s − 1) + dNd (s − 2).
Xiong [12] also obtain results on the number, the largest size and the avarage size of (s, s+1)-
core partitions with distinct parts.
   In this paper, we ask the problem of counting the number of special partitions which are
s-core for certain values of s. More precisely, we focus on (s, s + 1)-core partitions with
d-distinct parts. We obtain results on the number, the largest size of such partitions, so we
extend Xiong’s paper in which the results are obtained about (s, s + 1)-core partitions with
distinct parts. Also, we propose the following conjecture about (s, s + r)-core partitions with
d-distinct parts for 1 ≤ r ≤ d. That is, the number Nd,r (s) of (s, s + r)-core partitions with
                                                   2
d-distinct parts is characterized by Nd,r (s) = s for 1 ≤ s ≤ d, Nd,r (d + 1) = d + r, and for
s ≥ d + 2,
                           Nd,r (s) = Nd,r (s − 1) + Nd,r (s − (d + 1))
for 1 ≤ r ≤ d.

                  2. (s, s + 1)-core partitions with d-distinct parts
   Suppose that λ = (λ1 , λ2 , . . . , λl ) is a partition whose corresponding Young diagram has
l rows. The set β(λ) of λ is defined to be the set of first column hook length in the Young
diagram of λ, i.e.,
                                     β(λ) = {h(i, 1) : 1 ≤ i ≤ l} .
For example, if λ = (6, 3, 3, 2, 1), then we get
                        β(λ) = {h(1, 1), h(2, 1), h(3, 1), h(4, 1), h(5, 1)}

by using the diagram (1).
  Now, we generalize the definition of the twin-free set in [11].
Definition 2.1. Suppose that d is a positive integer such that d ≥ 2. A set X ⊆ N is called
d-th order twin-free set if there is no x ∈ X such that
                                  {x, x + k} ⊆ X,         for 1 ≤ k ≤ d.
   If we take d = 1 in Definition 2.1, then we get the twin-free set in [11], that is, we called
it first order twin-free set.

                   {2, 3} , {5, 6} , {10, 11} , {2, 4} , {5, 7} , {10, 12}
are not a subset of X. But, the set {2, 5} is a subset of X, so X is not a third order twin-free
set.

Lemma 2.3. A partition λ is a (s, s + 1)-core partition with d-distinct parts if and only if
β(λ) is a d-th order twin-free subset of the set {1, 2, . . . , s − 1}.
Proof. If a partition λ is a (s, s + 1)-core partition with d-distinct parts then by Lemma 2.2,
β(λ) must be subset of the set {1, 2, . . . , s − 1}. Also, By Lemma 2.1, β(λ) must be a d-th
order twin-free set.
  Conversely, suppose that β(λ) is a d-th order twin-free subset of the set {1, 2, . . . , s − 1}.
By Lemma 2.1, λ is a partition with d-distinct parts. Also, since β(λ) is a subset of the set
{1, 2, . . . , s − 1}, all the hook lengths of corresponding partition smaller than s and s + 1.
This means that λ is a (s, s + 1)-core partition.

Theorem 2.2. The number Nd (s) of (s, s + 1)-core partitions with d-distinct parts is char-
acterized by Nd (s) = s for 1 ≤ s ≤ (d + 1), and for s ≥ d + 2,
                             Nd (s) = Nd (s − 1) + Nd (s − (d + 1)).
Proof.
  Let Xk denote the set of all d-th order twin-free subsets of the set {1, 2, . . . , k − 1}. A

twin-free subset of the set {1, 2, . . . , s − 1} by Lemma 2.3. That is, Nd (s) = |Xs |. Suppose
that X ∈ Xs . If s − 1 ∈ X, then s − 2, s − 3, . . . , s − (d + 1) ∈ / X, since X is a d-th order
twin-free set. So,
