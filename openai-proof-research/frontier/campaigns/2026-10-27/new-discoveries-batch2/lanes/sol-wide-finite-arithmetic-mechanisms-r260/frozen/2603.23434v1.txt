                                                                                                      Abstract
                                                     For a non-decreasing sequence S = (s1 , s2 , . . . , sk ) of positive integers, a packing S-coloring of a graph
                                                 G is a partition of V (G) into V1 , V2 , . . . , Vk such that each Vi has pairwise distance at least si + 1. The
                                                 packing chromatic number (PCN) of a graph G is the minimum k such that G has a packing (1, 2, . . . , k)-
                                                 coloring. The 1-subdivision of G is obtained by replacing each edge of G with a path of two edges. In
                                                 2016, Gastineau and Togni asked an open question whether the 1-subdivision of every subcubic graph
                                                 has PCN at most 5, and later Brešar, Klavžar, Rall, and Wash conjectured it is true. Balogh, Kostochka,
                                                 and Liu proved the first upper bound of 8, and it was later improved to 6 by Liu, Zhang, and Zhang.
                                                     In this paper, we prove that every connected subcubic graph except the Petersen graph is packing
                                                 (1, 1, 2, 2)-colorable. Our result implies a solution to the conjecture of Brešar, Klavžar, Rall, and Wash,
                                                 and answers the question of Gastineau and Togni in the affirmative. Furthermore, our result answers an
                                                 open question of Kostochka and Liu and solves a conjecture of Liu, Zhang, and Zhang.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           An i-independent set in a graph G is a vertex set whose pairwise distance is at least i + 1. For a non-
                                           decreasing sequence S = (s1 , s2 , . . . , sk ) of positive integers, a packing S-coloring of a graph G is a partition
                                           of V (G) into V1 , V2 , . . . , Vk such that each Vi is si -independent. The notion was introduced by Goddard and
                                           Xu [13] and has been widely studied (e.g., see [1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21]). Its edge counterpart
                                           was introduced by Gastineau and Togni [11] and has received much attention recently (e.g., see [15, 17]).
                                           We use exponents to denote repetitions in a sequence. For example, (1, 1, 2, 2, 2) is denoted as (12 , 23 ).
                                               The celebrated Four-Color-Theorem states that every planar graph is packing (14 )-colorable. The square
                                           of a graph G, denote by G2 , is obtained from G by adding edges joining vertices of distance exactly two.
                                           A square k-coloring of a graph G, denoted by χ2 (G), is the minimum k such that G2 can be properly k-
                                           colored. Using the packing coloring notation, it is equivalent to a packing (2k )-coloring. Square coloring of
                                           planar graphs was first studied by Wegner [23] in 1977, who posed a conjecture that if G is a planar graph
                                           of maximum degree at most ∆, then χ2 (G) ≤ 7 if ∆(G) = 3, χ2 (G) ≤ ∆(G) + 5 if 4 ≤ ∆(G) ≤ 7, and


                                                                                                           1
χ2 (G) ≤ ⌊ ∆   2 ⌋ + 1 if ∆ ≥ 8. Thomassen [22], and independently Hartke, Jahanbekam, and Thomas [14]
confirmed Wegner’s conjecture for ∆ = 3. Bousquet, Deschamps, de Meyer, and Pierron [3] proved the
current best upper bound of 12 when ∆ = 4. For non-planar graphs, Cranston and Kim [8] showed that
every connected subcubic graph except the Petersen graph is square 8-colorable, and this bound is tight.
    We notice that a packing (1j , 2k )-coloring can be viewed as an intermediate coloring between proper
coloring and square coloring. Gastineau and Togni [10] proved that every subcubic graph has a packing
(1, 1, 2, 2, 2)-coloring and a packing (1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2)-coloring. They also showed that the Petersen graph
has no packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-coloring and no packing (1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2)-coloring. Liu and Wang [18] proved an
analogue result of Thomassen [22] that every subcubic planar graph has a packing (1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2)-coloring.
Packing colorings of general graphs (non-subcubic) was studied by Choi and Liu [7], as well as Mortada
and Togni [21]. We believe the study of packing (1j , 2k )-coloring can provide insights on how to solve hard
problems in square coloring, such as Wegner’s conjecture.
    The packing chromatic number (PCN) of a graph G, denoted by χp (G), is the smallest positive integer
such that G has a packing (1, 2, . . . , k)-coloring. The notion of PCN was introduced by Goddard, Hedetniemi,
Hedetniemi, Harris, and Rall [12] under the name broadcast coloring. There are more than 50 papers on
the topic (e.g., see [1, 2, 4, 6, 5, 9, 10, 16, 19, 24]). The question whether the PCN of all subcubic graphs
was bounded by a constant was first asked by Goddard et al [12] and garnered significant interest in the
community (e.g., see [5, 10]). It was finally answered in the negative by Balogh, Kostochka, and Liu [1], as
well as Brešar and Ferme [4], using the probabilistic method and explicit construction, respectively.
    The 1-subdivision of a graph G is obtained by replacing each edge with a path of two edges. Gastineau
and Togni [10] asked an open question in 2016 that whether the PCN of the 1-subdivision of subcubic graphs
is bounded by 5. This is one of the most popular open questions in the topic of packing coloring.
Open Question 1 (Gastineau and Togni [10]). Is it true that the 1-subdivision of subcubic graphs is packing
(1, 2, 3, 4, 5)-colorable?
    In 2017, Brešar, Klavžar, Rall and Wash [5] provided a packing (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)-coloring for the 1-subdivision
of the Petersen graph and conjectured Question 1 is true.
Conjecture 2 (Brešar, Klavžar, Rall, and Wash [5]). The 1-subdivision of every subcubic graph has PCN
at most 5.
   Gastineau and Togni [10] observed that if one can prove every connected subcubic graph except the
Petersen graph is packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-colorable, then the 1-subdivision of every subcubic graph has PCN at
most 5. Many progresses has been made toward Question 1 and Conjecture 2, but it still remains open since
2016. In particular, Brešar, Klavžar, Rall and Wash [5] proved Conjecture 2 for all generalized prisms of
cycles. Brešar, Kuenzel, Rall [6] proved the existence of packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-coloring for claw-free subcubic
graphs and thus confirmed Conjecture 2 for claw-free cubic graphs. Balogh, Kostochka, and Liu [1] showed
the first upper bound (8) toward Conjecture 2. Kostochka and Liu [16] proved every subcubic outerplanar
graphs has a packing (1, 1, 2, 4)-coloring, which confirms Conjecture 2 for subcubic outerplanar graphs.
Furthermore, they posed the following open question.
Open Question 3 (Kostochka and Liu [16]). Is it true that every subcubic planar graph is packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-
colorable?
   Very recently, Liu, Zhang, and Zhang [19] proved every subcubic graph is packing (1, 1, 2, 2, 3)-colorable.
Their result implies an upper bound of 6 for Conjecture 2. Furthermore, they posed a stronger conjecture.
Conjecture 4 (Liu, Zhang, and Zhang [19]). Every connected subcubic graph except the Petersen graph is
packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-colorable.

   Zein and Mortada [24] further improved the result of Liu, Zhang, and Zhang [19] by showing that every
subcubic graph has a (1, 1, 2, 2, k) coloring, where k can be any positive integer and is used at most once.
They also proved that every 2-degenerate subcubic graph has a packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-coloring. In this paper,
we confirm Conjecture 4.
Theorem 5. Every connected subcubic graph G except the Petersen graph is packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-colorable.
   As a corollary of our result, Question 1 is answered in the affirmative and Conjecture 2 is completely solved
— after being opened for a decade. Question 3 is answered in the positive as well. Our result characterizes
the Petersen graph as the unique connected subcubic graph whose vertex set cannot be partitioned into two
independent sets and two 2-independent sets (i.e., has no packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-coloring).

appropriate independent sets so that the remaining vertices can be partitioned into two 2-independent sets.

Proof. Suppose u is a red P1 , then C0 − u is a path P . Let u′ be the neighbor outside C ′ of u. By Lemma 13,
the distance between u′ and each of the two red end vertices of P is at least 3. Since N (H) = 1, u′ must be
in a new cycle component in H when we switch u and u′ . Therefore, consider the two possible neighbours
in H of u′ , the new cycle must be formed by u′ together with another path component of H.
    For a path component P in H, let Y be the set of vertices in G whose distance to the red vertices in P
is less than 3. We then define P ∗ := G[Y ]. A black vertex u in P ∗ is called an interface of P if
    (1) u lies at a distance exactly 2 from the set of red vertices;
    (2) under some choice of I1 and I2 satisfying Conditions (I), (II), and (III), u is red with all other vertices
of P ∗ unchanged;
    (3) u and the other red vertices in P ∗ form a cycle in H.
    An inner vertex of P ∗ is a black vertex adjacent to two red vertices, one of which is a red P1 .
Lemma 16. The inner vertices of P ∗ must be in the same independent set.
Proof. If two inner vertices are adjacent to a common red P1 , then they must belong to the same independent
set otherwise an F1 would appear in G. If two inner vertices are adjacent to the end vertices of a red P2 ,
then they must belong to the same independent set, since otherwise an F2 would appear in G. Furthermore,
since P forms a path component of H, this forces all inner vertices into the same independent set.
   We analyze properties of interfaces under the following three cases.
   • When |P | ≥ 3, P ∗ must have inner vertices. By Lemma 16, we assume the inner vertices in P ∗ are all
     in I1 . If a black vertex x is an interface of P . By the definition of interface, x can be turned into red
     with all other vertices in P ∗ unchanged. We turn x into red to form a cycle C in H. By Lemma 8, and
     since every red vertex must be adjacent to a vertex in I2 , it follows that x is adjacent to the end vertices
     of P via two non-inner black vertices z1 , z2 ∈ I1 . Furthermore, C is the unique cycle component in H.
     Consequently, by Lemma 13, P has only one interface (see the first graph in Figure 4).

 (1) If Ci′ is not a T2 , then by Lemma 11, 14 and 15, there are at least two P1 s with good property. We

Claim 18. For j ≤ i − 1, we have Li+1 ̸= L′j .
Proof. Suppose not. Let i be the smallest integer, such that Li+1 = L′j for some j ≤ i − 1. We claim that
yi = xj is impossible, since at step j, xj has two black neighbours and neighbour yj , which can not serve
as xi . Hence, L′j must have two interfaces, with xj being one of them. By the properties of interfaces we
discussed, this forces yi to be one of the blue vertices shown in the middle two graphs in Figure 4. At step j,
Cj′ is the corresponding structure of Cj and contains xj . By Lemma 17, yi can only be the left blue vertex
in the third graph in Figure 4; otherwise, xi and Cj′ would form the structure in Lemma 17, a contradiction.
However, this implies xi is a red vertex at distance 2 from Cj′ (which is a T3 ), contradicting Lemma 8.
     Since the number of path components in H is finite, the above procedure must eventually terminate by
Claim 18, which contradicts N (H) = 1. Therefore, if G is not the Petersen graph, then N (H) = 0. That is,
H can be reorganized into a forest by a good choice of I1 and I2 . We can then color the red vertices with
two colors 2a and 2b , such that the corresponding vertices in H form two independent sets. Consequently,
vertices of the same color (2a or 2b ) maintain a pairwise distance of at least 3 in G. Combining with I1 and
I2 , we obtain a packing (1, 1, 2, 2)-coloring of G.


3    Concluding Remarks
Gastineau and Togni [10] asked whether every connected subcubic graph except the Petersen graph is packing
(1, 1, 2, 3)-colorable. We believe it is true and therefore pose the question as a conjecture.

Conjecture 19. Every connected subcubic graph except the Petersen graph is packing (1, 1, 2, 3)-colorable.
