   A polynomial with complex coeﬃcients is said to be negative-real-rooted if it is either identically zero or
else has all its zeros in (−∞, 0]. An entire function belongs to the Laguerre–Pólya class LP + if it can be
obtained as a limit, uniformly on compact subsets of C, of a sequence of negative-real-rooted polynomials.
Laguerre [19] showed in 1882 that an entire function f belongs to LP + if and only if it can be written in
the form

    Finally, when p = q, Ki and Kim [17, Theorem 3] proved a strong converse to Theorem 2:

Theorem 3 (Ki–Kim 2000). Fix an integer p ≥ 1. If a1 , . . . , ap ∈ R \ (−N) and b1 , . . . , bp > 0, then the

(a) pFp (a; b; · ) has only a ﬁnite number of zeros.
(b) pFp (a; b; · ) has only real zeros.
(c) pFp (a; b; · ) ∈ LP + .
(d) The a can be re-indexed so that ai = bi + mi for 1 ≤ i ≤ p, with all mi ∈ N.

   The main purpose of the present note is to show that Conjecture 4 is false. But what makes the situation
interesting is not only that the conjecture is false; rather, it is as false as it can possibly be. Namely,

by proving the following extension of Theorem 3 to p < q:
