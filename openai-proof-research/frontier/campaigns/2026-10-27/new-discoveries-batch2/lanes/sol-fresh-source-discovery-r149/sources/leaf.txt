                                           Abstract Let k, d be a positive integer, G be a connected graph of order n, T be a
                                           tree. The leaf distance of a tree is defined as the minimum distance between any two
                                           leaves. For v ∈ V (T ), the leaf degree of v in T is the number of leaves adjacent to v, and
                                           the leaf degree of T is defined as maximum leaf degree among the vertices of T . In this
                                           paper, motivated by the conjecture proposed by Kaneko (2001) and its subsequent partial
                                           confirmation by Erbes, Molla, Mousley and Santana (2017), we obtain lower bounds in
                                           terms of the size and the adjacent spectral radius to guarantee that G contains a spanning
                                           tree with leaf distance at least d, where 4 ≤ d ≤ n − 1. Furthermore, we obtain some
                                           tight conditions in G for its size and spectral radius to ensure that G has a spanning tree
                                           with leaf degree at most k, which improves the result of Ao, Liu, Yuan, Ng and Cheng
                                           (2023).

                                           Keywords: Leaf distance; Leaf degree; Spanning tree; Spectral radius; Size

                                                Throughout this paper, we only consider simple, undirected and connected graphs.
                                           Let G = (V, E) be a graph with vertex set V (G) = {v1 , v2 , · · · , vn } and edge set E(G),
                                           where n and |E(G)| are called the order and the size of G, respectively. For S ⊆ V (G),
                                                       S

                                           minimum degree of G is denoted by δ(G). A leaf is a vertex with degree one in a tree.
                                           For a subset S ⊆ V (G), we denote by G[S] the subgraph of G induced by S, and by G − S

every vertex of G1 and every vertex of G2 . For undefined terms, see [3].
   Let G be a graph with n vertices. The adjacency matrix A(G) = (aij ) of G is an
n × n symmetric matrix, where aij = 1 if vertices vi and vj are adjacent in G, and
aij = 0 otherwise. Let D(G) be the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees in G. The matrix
Q(G) = D(G) + A(G) is known as the signless Laplacian matrix of G. The largest
eigenvalue of A(G) (resp. Q(G)), denoted by ρ(G) (resp. q(G)), is referred to as the
adjacency spectral radius (resp. signless Laplacian spectral radius) of G.
   A spanning tree T of a connected graph G is a spanning subgraph of G that is a tree,
including all vertices of G. For an integer k ≥ 2, a k-tree is a tree with the maximum
degree at most k, and a k-ended tree is a tree with at most k leaves.
   Let T be a tree. For a vertex v ∈ V (T ), the leaf degree of v is defined as the number
of leaves adjacent to v, and the leaf degree of T is the maximum leaf degree among all
vertices of T . The leaf distance of T is defined as the minimum distance between any two
leaves in T .
   In mathematical literature, the study on the existence of spanning trees attracted much
attention. For instance, Win [13] established a link between the presence of spanning
k-trees in a graph and its toughness, and proposed a Chvátal-Erdős type criterion for
ensuring that a t-connected graph contains a spanning k-ended tree [12]. Gu and Liu
[8] identified connected graphs with spanning k-trees by utilizing Laplacian eigenvalues.
Fan, Goryainov, Huang, and Lin [7] investigated the existence of a spanning k-tree in a
connected graph through the adjacent spectral radius and the signless Laplacian spectral
radius. Kaneko [9] introduced the concept of leaf degree in a spanning tree and provided
a criterion for a connected graph to have a spanning tree with leaf degree at most k. Ao,
Liu, and Yuan [1] derived the tight spectral conditions for the existence of a spanning
k-ended tree and a spanning tree with leaf degree at most k in a connected graph. Zhou,
Sun, and Liu [15] determined the D-index and Q-index for spanning trees with leaf degree
at most k in connected graphs, and other similar studies have been conducted.
   The present paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some well - known
results and prove several lemmas. In Sections 3 and 4, we discuss the existence of spanning
trees with leaf distance at least d and leaf degree at most k in graphs, respectively, with
respect to the minimum degree.

3     The existence of spanning trees with leaf distance
      at least d with respect to the minimum degree
    Kaneko in [9] posed the following conjecture on the existence of a spanning tree with
larger leaf distance.

Conjecture 3.1 ( [9]) Let d ≥ 3 be an integer and G be a connected graph of order
                         2|S|
n ≥ d + 1. If i(G − S) < d−2  for any nonempty subset S ⊆ V (G), then G has a spanning
tree with leaf distance at least d, where i(G−S) is the number of isolated vertices in G−S.

    It is clear that if the leaf degree of a tree of order n(≥ 3) is one, then its leaf distance
is at least three, which implies [9, Theorem 1] proved Conjecture 3.1 for d = 3. The
following case d = 4 was later proven by Kaneko, Kano, and Suzuki in [10].

                                                3
Theorem 3.2 ([10]) Let G be a connected graph of order n ≥ 5. If i(G − S) < |S| for
any nonempty subset S ⊆ V (G), then G has a spanning tree with leaf distance at least 4.

   Given a graph G, let α(G) denote the independence number of G. For k ≤ α(G),
let δk (G) be the minimum order of the neighborhood of an independent set of order k in
a graph G. That is, δk (G) = min{|NG (A)| | A is an independent set of order k in G}.

the condition in Conjecture 3.1 into the language of neighborhood unions.

4      The existence of spanning trees with leaf degree at
       most k with respect to the minimum degree
     Kaneko [9] introduced the concept of leaf degree of a spanning tree and provided the
following criterion for a connected graph to have a spanning tree with leaf degree at most
k.

Theorem 4.1 ([9]) Let G be a connected simple graph and k ≥ 1 be an integer. Then G
has a spanning tree with leaf degree at most k if and only if i(G − S) < (k + 1)|S| for any
nonempty subset S ⊆ V (G), where i(G − S) is the number of isolated vertices in G − S.

     Let G be a graph of order n with the minimum degree δ(G) ≥ t. Assume that G
contains no spanning tree with leaf degree at most k. Then there exists a nonempty
