## Exact source and quantifiers

Klavžar–Kuziak–Peterin–Yero, *A Steiner general position problem in graph theory*, Computational and Applied Mathematics 40 (2021), 223, DOI 10.1007/s40314-021-01619-y. The author revision is dated August 2, 2021. The published and revised bodies define a k-sgp set S by **every** k-element B contained in S and **every** minimum-edge Steiner B-tree T satisfying V(T) intersect S = B. This is the maximum-cardinality invariant, not a smallest maximal set and not existence of one avoiding optimum. Trees may use arbitrary graph vertices. The stated working range is 2 <= k <= n-1. At k=2 the invariant is gp.

Published/revised Proposition 2.4 (arXiv label 2.3) gives a set which is (k-1)-sgp but not k-sgp. Its wheel has rim edges subdivided k-1 times and spokes k-2 times. At k=3 this is exactly the 13-vertex base used here. The source explicitly warns after the proof and before Problem 7.1 that this does not settle maximum invariants. The candidate respects that warning; its base has gp=sgp3=5. Problem 7.1 asks for a monotony relation between successive values. The candidate refutes either universal direction, even monotonicity in either direction for a single graph; it does not classify monotone graphs or exclude every conceivable weaker relation.

Theorem 5.2 uses j=CEILING(k/n(H)), ell=min(k,n(G)), and for n(H)<k<n(G)n(H) gives sgp_k(G circle H) >= n(H) sgp_[j:ell](G). Its equality guarantee has the strict hypothesis k>(n(G)-1)n(H). Both bodies agree. At P3, K2, k=4: j=2, ell=3, lower bound 4, equality threshold k>4, which does not cover k=4. Problems 7.1 and 7.3 are literally present in both bodies.

## Independent ordinary proof: monotonicity

Write base distinguished vertices as w,a,b,c and let U be the nine degree-two subdivision vertices. Replace only w,a,b,c by independent classes W,A,B,C of size m>=1, with edges lifted between corresponding classes and singleton subdivision vertices. The graph is finite, simple, connected, and has 4m+9 vertices. The projection pi to the base maps each edge to a genuine base edge: there are no clone-class edges. In particular it never creates loops.

Distances between vertices of distinct classes equal base distances. A base path lifts with any prescribed endpoints; the projection of any shorter path would be a shorter base walk, and removing repetitions gives a base path. Base center/rim distances are 2, rim/rim distances 3. Distinct clones within a class have distance 2, since the class is independent and has a common neighbor. Every triple from W union A union B union C has lengths among (2,2,2), (2,2,3), (2,3,3), (3,3,3), all with strict triangle inequalities. Thus **all** geodesics avoid the third selected vertex and gp >= 4m.

For completeness the base Steiner distance of {a,b,c} is 6. In a minimum tree no leaf is a nonterminal; otherwise pruning improves it. Each included degree-two subdivision vertex therefore forces the full subdivided edge between its distinguished endpoints: a partial end would be a nonterminal leaf. Suppress these paths. If w is absent, the tree spans a,b,c with at least two weight-3 rim edges. If w is present, it spans four vertices and has at least three edges, all weight >=2. Either case costs at least 6. The three weight-2 spokes attain 6.

Now choose ANY a' in A, b' in B, c' in C, w' in W. Their lifted spoke star through w' has six edges, seven distinct vertices, and is a tree containing the three rim terminals. Any alleged connected graph H joining those terminals with fewer than six edges projects to a connected base subgraph joining a,b,c. The number of distinct image edges is <= |E(H)|, even when different clone edges collapse onto one base edge. Taking a spanning tree of the image cannot increase edge count. This contradicts the base lower bound. Hence the lifted star is a genuine minimum Steiner tree and contains w' outside its terminal set. No isometry-to-Steiner shortcut is needed, and no injectivity of the projection on H is assumed.

Consequently every 3-sgp set misses at least one of the four classes: if it meets all four, the chosen terminals and w' just exhibited violate its universal condition. Every such set therefore has at most 3m vertices in these classes and at most nine elsewhere. This proves the **global all-set bound** sgp3 <= 3m+9, without assuming a maximum set consists only of clones or has any symmetry. Taking m=10 gives a 49-vertex graph with sgp2 >=40 >39 >=sgp3. Finally every 40-element set is 40-sgp (its only 40-subset is itself, so the intersection condition holds automatically). Thus sgp40>=40 and the same sequence also has a later adjacent increase between indices 3 and 40, all within 2..48. No exact value of either maximum on the 49-vertex graph is asserted.

## Independent ordinary proof: lexicographic example

The graph P3 circle K2 has three layers of size two. The two outer layers induce disjoint edges; each middle vertex is adjacent to every outer vertex and to the other middle vertex. Delete one outer vertex to obtain S of size five. Every four-subset B of S contains a middle vertex and is connected. Therefore d(B)=3; every optimum tree has exactly four vertices, all terminals, regardless of the multiplicity of optimum edge sets. This proves S is 4-sgp with the source's ALL-trees quantifier. The full six-set fails: its four outer terminals require a middle vertex, and either middle choice gives a four-edge optimum. Hence sgp4=5. On P3 the simultaneous [2:3] invariant is 2: every pair qualifies and the full three-set fails the parameter-2 condition. Thus 5>2*2=4, a full counterexample to universal sharpness in Problem 7.3. It neither contradicts Theorem 5.2 nor its strict equality clause.
