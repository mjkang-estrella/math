## MATH: complete independent bridge and substantive assessment

The following is both my derivation of the endpoint and the exact adaptation bridge supporting SUBSTANTIVE EXCLUDE. The judgment is based on how the source proof mechanism extends, not on shortness, the conjecture's apparent difficulty, or naming CRT/Babai alone.

1. For q in {n,m,k}, put an undirected edge {x,q+1-x} for each swapped pair of r_q. Different q give disjoint edges since their endpoint sums differ. Components are generated-group orbits. The companion Theorem 3.1 uses precisely this graph and the tree coloring below. Its edge count is floor(n/2)+floor(m/2)+floor(k/2). If n is odd and m+k<n-1 this is <=n-2, hence disconnected. If n is even and m+k<n it is <=n-1. In the connected case equality forces a tree. Color by parity of r_n edges along the unique root path. Each smaller reversal preserves the colors, including fixed vertices; r_n, fixed-point-free, swaps them. Thus both color classes have n/2 vertices and form a nontrivial invariant partition. Here n>=4 ensures block sizes >=2. Consequently m>=n-k-1 for odd n, m>=n-k for even n. There are at most k+1 and k candidates respectively.

2. For odd k, an odd m and odd n preserve position parity individually, giving intransitivity. At even n, odd m,k preserve position parity and r_n swaps the two equal parity sets, giving imprimitivity. Thus only even m can generate. In the odd-n window of length k+1 exactly (k+1)/2 qualify; in the even-n window of length k exactly (k-1)/2 qualify. This proves the global upper bound with no attainment assumption. This parity mechanism is explicit in original Theorem 2 and companion preliminaries; the window counting is elementary.

3. Here is the full extension from the source modular mechanism to the required sufficiency lemma. Write a=n-m, b=n-k. The original Lemma 5 already uses residues modulo n-m and the reflection s -> n+1-s; companion Lemma 3.2 explicitly writes products of reversals as a translation on an initial interval and a reflection on the tail. For T=r_n r_(n-a), direct substitution gives T(x)=x+a for x<=n-a, T(x)=n+1-x for x>n-a. Hence every residue class modulo a is internally in one orbit, by chaining x,x+a,..., and the final a positions supply every residue, imposing R(s)=n+1-s on orbit equivalence classes. This is an equivalence relation argument, not a false claim that T descends to a globally well-defined permutation on residues.

4. If 1<=a<=k, representatives 1,...,a within the first k positions and r_k impose S(s)=k+1-s on all residues. If a=k+1, representatives 1,...,k impose S on nonzero residues; S fixes the missing zero residue, so no edge is missing. If a=1 the single class already suffices. Then RS(s)=s+n-k=s+b. If gcd(a,b)=1, this translation connects every residue; internal class connectivity proves transitivity. These steps use only the displayed source product/reflection formulas and elementary cyclic residue arithmetic. They add no classification of primitive groups, no construction of a long permutation word, and no unresolved combinatorial sublemma. The n>2k+2 assumption guarantees valid separated indices for the application (the connectivity reasoning itself does not need that full strength).

5. For fixed k, take A={1,...,k+1} if k is even, A={1,3,...,k} if k is odd. For each odd prime l<=k+1 choose c_l outside {0,k} modulo l. At most two residues among l>=3 are excluded, even at l=3; if l divides k there is only one excluded residue. CRT together with c=3 mod4 gives c modulo M=4 product(l). Since c is nonzero modulo every factor, gcd(c,M)=1. Dirichlet gives arbitrarily large primes p=c mod M, so choose p>(2k+1)^2. Every odd prime factor of any a in A is among those l and does not divide p-k. For even k, p-k is odd; for odd k, A contains only odds. Thus gcd(a,p-k)=1 SIMULTANEOUSLY for all a in A.
