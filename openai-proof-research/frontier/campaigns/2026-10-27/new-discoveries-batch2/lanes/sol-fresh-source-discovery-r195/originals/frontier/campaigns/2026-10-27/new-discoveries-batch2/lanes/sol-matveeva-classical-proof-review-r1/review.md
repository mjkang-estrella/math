## Complete classical bridge, independently re-derived

Write A=sum a_k x^k and B=sum (k a_k+b_k)x^k. The coefficient at n of xAS'+BS is sum_(k<=min(n,d))(a_k n+b_k)s_(n-k). Thus it equals a polynomial P supported before the recurrence begins. Since a_0!=0, A is nonzero. Put r=-B/(xA), q=P/(xA). These are rational and S'=rS+q. Analytic global boundedness gives exponential absolute and common-denominator bounds; the affine equation homogenizes, so S is a G-function. Differentiation preserves these conditions: derivative coefficients (n+1)s_(n+1) retain exponential denominator and absolute bounds. Rational S is already finished, including S=0; d=0 is eventually zero directly.

For nonrational S and q=0, D-r is its minimal order-one equation. The imported minimal-G theorem makes it Fuchsian with rational exponents. Hence r has only simple finite poles, no polynomial part, and rational residues, so a normalized rank-one solution is algebraic by Proposition 3.1.

For nonrational S and q!=0, a relation uS+vS'=0 yields (u+vr)S+vq=0. Nonrationality forces both rational coefficients zero, then v=u=0. Therefore L=(D-q'/q)(D-r) is a minimal monic order-two annihilator. Expanded,

    L = D² -(r+q'/q)D + (q'/q)r-r'.

An alternative route makes the cyclic-module issue explicit: (S,1) is a Q(x)-independent G-function vector satisfying Y'=M Y with M=[[r,q],[0,0]]. The rational gauge G=[[1,0],[r,q]], determinant q!=0, maps it to (S,S'). Its companion matrix is

    (G'+GM)G^-1 = [[0,1],[r'-(q'/q)r, r+q'/q]].

Thus this is precisely the cyclic system of L, not an unrelated nonminimal operator. The scalar minimal-G theorem applies directly. No assertion that one integral solution forces an arbitrary operator's other solutions to behave well is needed.

Here is also a direct local check of the rank-one inference, avoiding an unproved assertion about differential subquotients. The rational logarithmic derivative q'/q has only simple finite poles and is O(1/x) at infinity. Fuchsianity of L forces r+q'/q to have only simple finite poles and be O(1/x) at infinity, hence r itself has those properties. At a finite point alpha set rho=Res_alpha r and m=ord_alpha q (an integer). The indicial polynomial is exactly

    z(z-1)-(rho+m)z+(m+1)rho = (z-rho)(z-m-1).

Rational exponents therefore force rho rational, including when q has a zero or pole there. If L is ordinary at alpha, its exponents are integers; the same conclusion holds. This includes alpha=0. At infinity substitute x=1/t in the affine equation: r becomes -r(1/t)/t² and q becomes -q(1/t)/t²; L changes by a rational nonzero scalar factor to the corresponding homogenization. The same argument applies. The infinity residue is minus the sum of finite residues. Equivalently, every normalized solution H'=rH is a finite product of rational powers of linear factors; choose one algebraic normalization.

To cover a possibly singular origin, let rho=Res_0 r and solve h'/h=r-rho/x with h(0)=1. Rational Taylor coefficients and recursive solution give h in Q[[x]], analytic near 0; the finite-product description makes it algebraic over Qbar(x). Algebraicity descends to Q(x): all coefficients of a polynomial relation lie in a number field K; a product of its conjugate polynomials is a nonzero Q(x)-polynomial vanishing at h. Equivalently, rationality of r and its residues makes conjugate-pole residues equal, so some positive integral power of h is a normalized rational function. This supplies descent without an assumption about the rationality of arbitrary algebraic residues.

Choose N with N rho an integer. Then H(t^N)=t^(N rho)h(t^N) and its inverse lie in Q((t)) and are algebraic with finite poles. S(t^N) is globally bounded: a scale C that works for S also works for S(t^N), since C^(Nn) is divisible by C^n. Eisenstein gives bounded Taylor parts of h(t^N) and its inverse. Products and Laurent shifts preserve exponential denominator bounds, allowing a further constant multiplier; analyticity is retained on a sufficiently small punctured disk. Consequently U=S(t^N)/H(t^N) is a globally bounded rational-coefficient Laurent series. Cancellation gives

    U'=N t^(N-1) q(t^N)/H(t^N),

which is algebraic. Subtract its finite rational principal part R to get V in Q[[t]]. This preserves global boundedness and makes V'=U'-R' algebraic. Apply precisely the scaled Theorem 5.6 described above. Then V,U,S(t^N) are algebraic. Since Q(t)/Q(t^N) is algebraic, transitivity and renaming prove S algebraic over Q(x). This closes all finite orders and all initial sequences in the source quantifier.

Two wording corrections are non-load-bearing. An **arbitrary** local homogeneous solution may carry a transcendental multiplicative constant, so it need not be algebraic over Qbar(x); the assertion needed and proved is existence of the normalized algebraic branch H=x^rho h. Also, globally bounded **Laurent** series is shorthand here for bounded Taylor part after finite monomial shift, not Definition 1.5 applied literally to a series with a pole. The explicit removal of R repairs that shorthand before André is applied. Neither requires a new theorem or leaves an open lemma.
