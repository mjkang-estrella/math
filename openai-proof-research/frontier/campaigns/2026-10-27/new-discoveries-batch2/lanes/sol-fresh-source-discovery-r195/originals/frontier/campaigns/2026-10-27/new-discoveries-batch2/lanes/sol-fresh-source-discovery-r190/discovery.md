# Universal reductions and exact missing bridge

1. Dilation preserves P: substitute u=d*t in the integral and split into d periods. Thus divide by gcd(A). For primitive A both parities may occur, or all entries may be odd.

2. If O,E are nonempty parity classes and G_T denotes agreement within T, translation t->t+1/2 reverses every odd sign and no even sign. It exchanges agreement and disagreement of the two class signs within G_O intersection G_E. Consequently P(A)=measure(G_O intersection G_E)/2. In particular a singleton parity class gives P(A)=P(other four)/2>=1/66>1/105, using the public p4 bound. This fully eliminates primitive parity patterns1+4 and4+1. Patterns2+3,3+2 and all-odd remain.

3. For signs e_i=+/-1, expanding [prod(1+e_i)+prod(1-e_i)]/32 shows 16*1_agree=1+sum_pairs e_i*e_j+sum_four_subsets prod e_i. Integrating proves the exact five-frequency identity. There are ten pair and five fourth-order terms. The actual source pair formula is zero when a/g,b/g have different parity, and otherwise chi4(a/g)*chi4(b/g)/((a/g)*(b/g)), g=gcd(a,b). This handles the pair terms, but the five fourth-order terms must be controlled jointly. The individual public -1/3 product bound does not deliver the desired sum.

4. Dou's Lemma2.3 gives if a5>12*lcm(a1,a2,a3,a4), then P(A)>P(first4)/3>=1/99>1/105. This is a complete applicable classical/source bridge for excluding that scale region. It does not bound a1..a4 or give a finite universal search.

5. Independently, on each signed common arc of T the absolute integral of sgn cos(c*x) is at most pi/c, since its primitive has range pi/c. The identity P(T,c)=P(T)/2+E[h_T*sgn cos(c*x)]/2, where h_T is +1 on all-positive, -1 on all-negative, 0 elsewhere, gives P(T,c)>=P(T)/2-N(T)/(4c). If P(A)<=1/105 and T=first4, then c<=1155*N(T)/26. Since N(T)<=2*sum(T), c<=1155*sum(T)/13. These ratio bounds still leave unbounded primitive configurations. They do not license assuming the first frequency1 or a3-pair.

A plausible full-scope route is a new joint fourth-correlation inequality forcing short reduced ratios under P<=1/105, then arithmetic classification plus signed-arc bounds to close all remaining cases. The missing bridge is that UNIVERSAL short-ratio/classification theorem, including the2+3 parity classes. We have not proved it. The n4 source obtains its short-ratio threshold from ONE fourth-order term; five terms cannot be inserted into its argument without a new estimate. We do not call this a routine adaptation or exclude it because it looks short.

# Complete applicable classical bridge: what it actually establishes

Here is the full reduction behind the public uniform bound. Let D be the union of (-1/8,1/8)^5 and its translate by (1/2,...,1/2) in the five-torus. Its Haar measure is2*4^-5. Its difference set is exactly the union of the all-positive and all-negative half-cubes V, up to boundaries. For E_z={t:z+a*t in D}, Fubini gives average_z measure(E_z)=measure(D), hence some z has measure(E_z)>=measure(D). Differences E_z-E_z lie in {t:a*t in V}. The circle sumset theorem gives measure(E_z-E_z)>=min(2*measure(E_z),1). Therefore P(A)>=4^-4=1/256 for EVERY five-tuple. Every application hypothesis is satisfied: measurable open D, Haar measures, integer torus homomorphism, and boundary sets of one-dimensional measure zero because frequencies are nonzero. This is the applicable Kneser/Fubini bridge as printed in the actual2026 proof. It does not yield1/105. The underlying original Kneser1956 body was not separately read; the argument uses the stated circle theorem as a classical input, with no claim of a new proof of that theorem.
