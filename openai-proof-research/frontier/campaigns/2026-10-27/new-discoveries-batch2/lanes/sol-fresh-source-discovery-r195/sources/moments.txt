                                                         Moment representations for generalized Lehmer polynomials

                                                                           KARIN IKEDA AND HIDEKI MURAHARA


                                                   Abstract. We represent normalized generalized Lehmer polynomials as moments of a random
                                                   variable constructed from two independent negative binomial variables and an independent uni-
                                                   form variable. An integral form of their known exponential generating function yields mixtures
                                                   of uniform distributions and convergent double series. We recover Eulerian polynomial iden-
                                                   tities and give a probabilistic interpretation of anti-diagonal sums of poly-Bernoulli numbers.
                                                   We also prove moment determinacy, characterize the Stieltjes parameter range, derive Hankel
                                                   total non-negativity and log-convexity, and identify the boundary distributions.

1.2. Generalized Lehmer polynomials. Ikeda and Kadono [7] introduce a Hurwitz–Lerch
type central binomial series and polynomials pn (a, z) with pn (1, z) = pn (z); we call them gen-
eralized Lehmer polynomials. Our variable z corresponds to their polynomial variable x, which
is the square of the third variable in their series. The initial value is p−1 (a, z) = 0, and the
deﬁning recurrence, valid for n ≥ −1, is
(1.3)       pn+1 (a, z) = 2((n + 1 − a)z + a)pn (a, z) + 2z(1 − z)∂z pn (a, z) + qn (z),
with qn (z) as above. They obtain an Eulerian polynomial expansion and a recurrence for
αn (a) := (2/3)n pn (a, 1/4). In this paper, we represent pn (a, z)/(n + 1) and αn (a)/(n + 1)
as moments; at a = 1, this interprets the known anti-diagonal sums probabilistically.
1.3. Moment representations. Probabilistic representations of classical polynomials include
Sun’s complex moment formulas for Bernoulli and Euler polynomials and gamma moment for-
mulas for Gegenbauer polynomials [12]. Adell and Lekuona [2] use uniform and exponential
variables to represent Stirling numbers, while Adell and Bényi [1] relate potential polynomi-
als to moments of sums of independent variables. For Eulerian polynomials, shifted negative
binomial moments appear in Charalambides [6]; Barry [3] studies moment representations and
Hankel transforms using exponential Riordan arrays. Liang, Mu, and Wang [9] use total positiv-
ity to obtain suﬃcient conditions for Catalan-like numbers to form Stieltjes
                                                                       R     moment sequences.
A Stieltjes moment sequence is a real sequence (mn )n≥0 with mn = [0,∞) xn dµ(x) for every
n ≥ 0, for some ﬁnite positive Borel measure µ on [0, ∞). Our representation combines two
negative binomial variables with an independent uniform variable, and it gives a necessary and
suﬃcient condition for the normalized generalized Lehmer polynomials to form a Stieltjes mo-
ment sequence.

  To study the moment sequences arising from normalized generalized Lehmer polynomials,
we recall some standard notions fromR the moment problem. A real sequence (mn )n≥0 is a
Hamburger moment sequence if mn = R xn dµ(x) for every n ≥ 0, for some ﬁnite positive Borel
measure µ on R with moments of all orders; it is determinate if this measure is unique. It is
a Stieltjes moment sequence if such a measure can be chosen on [0, ∞), as in Section 1. The
Hankel matrix associated with (mn )n≥0 is (mi+j )i,j≥0 . A matrix is totally non-negative if every
ﬁnite minor is non-negative.
