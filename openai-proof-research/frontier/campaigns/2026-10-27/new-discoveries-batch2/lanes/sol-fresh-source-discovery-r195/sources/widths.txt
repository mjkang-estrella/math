                                                             Shape optimization under minimal width
                                                                 and constant width constraints
                                                                                    Beniamin Bogosel∗



                                                                                          Abstract




arXiv:2610.36382v1 [math.OC] 26 Oct 2026
                                                      In this paper we study shape optimization problems for planar convex sets with
                                                   prescribed minimal width or constant width. We prove that the Reuleaux triangle
                                                   minimizes torsional rigidity among sets of constant width. We also show that the first
                                                   Dirichlet-Laplace eigenvalue is maximized by the equilateral triangle under a minimal
                                                   width constraint and by the Reuleaux triangle under a constant width constraint. The
                                                   proofs use subsets consisting of a disk and three straight or circular caps. Reflection
                                                   arguments reduce the study to equilateral configurations, which contain a polygon
                                                   or a disk polygon depending on one parameter. The same cap constructions give a
                                                   common proof of the known area, Cheeger and inner parallel area inequalities, and yield
                                                   sharp bounds for all positive moments of the distance to the boundary. We also prove
                                                   sharp inequalities for concentric disk intersections and decreasing radial integrals, and
                                                   compare interaction energies for kernels close to a positive constant. This includes a
                                                   nonempty interval of positive Riesz exponents. Finally, we investigate whether inclusion
                                                   monotonicity and Minkowski concavity suffice to obtain these comparisons for other
                                                   functionals.

                                           Keywords. Minimal width; constant width; cap bodies; inner parallel sets; Cheeger
                                           constant; torsional rigidity; Dirichlet eigenvalues; Riesz energy; Minkowski concavity.
                                           2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35P15, 49Q10, 52A10, 52A40.


                                           1       Introduction
                                           We are interested in minimizing area and torsional rigidity, and maximizing the Cheeger
                                           constant and the first Dirichlet-Laplace eigenvalue, under two constraints on the width of a
                                           planar convex set. For a planar convex body K with support function hK (e) = maxx∈K x · e,
                                           its minimal width is
                                                                          w(K) = min{hK (e) + hK (−e)}.
                                                                                       e∈S1

                                           It has constant width w if the expression in braces equals w in every direction. The respective
                                           optimizers are the equilateral triangle and the Reuleaux triangle. The proofs use a common
                                           geometric reduction: each admissible body contains a disk with three straight or circular
                                           caps. Inclusion monotonicity reduces the functional comparisons to these families, which
                                           are described by a few geometric parameters. For torsion and the first eigenvalue, reflection
                                           arguments and shape derivatives then determine the optimal cap positions and compare the
                                           resulting equilateral configurations with the reference bodies.

1.1    Previous results
Pál’s isominwidth theorem [45] and the Blaschke–Lebesgue theorem [4, 38] give the area row.
The classical geometry of constant width, including the relations between its inscribed and

                                                2
circumscribed circles, is treated in Yaglom and Boltyanskii [49, Chapter 7] and in [43]. Other
approaches to Blaschke–Lebesgue include [6, 7, 17, 27, 31, 41]. Ball and spindle convexity
provide the curved counterpart of ordinary convex hulls [2].
    The straight-cap construction is described in [49, Chapter 6] and used by Lucardesi
and Zucco [39, Proposition 3.3] in their quantitative versions of Pál’s inequality. Their
Lemma 3.2 also gives the area monotonicity for this family. The same subset is used for
torsion in [8]. The curved three-cap construction and its area comparison already occur
in M. Bezdek’s proof for disk polygons [3, Section 4]. More recently Fodor, Robock and
Sagmeister [20] connected the Pál and Blaschke–Lebesgue inequalities through ball convexity,
using three caps and a disk subhexagon. We use these constructions below to study the
Dirichlet functionals. An earlier analytic approach to minimum area under width and
curvature constraints is given by Malagoli [40]. Henrot and Lucardesi [28] also solved the
distinct area problem with both constant width and inradius prescribed; our intermediate
cap bodies need not themselves satisfy the width constraint.
    Henrot and Lucardesi [29] proved the constant-width Cheeger inequality. The inner-
parallel-area and inner-perimeter comparisons of [9] give another geometric proof. Under a
minimal-width constraint, Ftouhi, Lucardesi and Saracco [24] proved the Cheeger inequality
and its local Hausdorff stability. Their Corollary 4.5 also proves the inner-parallel-area
comparison with its strict equality case. The straight-cap method in [8] gives another proof
of the Cheeger inequality and establishes the torsion inequality. Here explicit erosion of
the two cap families gives a common proof of the known inner-area comparisons. Sharp
inequalities for distance moments follow by integration. The inner-perimeter result in the
constant-width class requires the additional geometric argument of [9].
    For nonlocal functionals, Bogosel, Bucur and Fragalà [11] prove a polygonal Hardy–
Littlewood inequality and study symmetry and symmetry breaking for Riesz energies at
fixed area and number of sides. We consider minimum problems under width constraints,
using the same reduction to indicator kernels for one-point integrals.
    For torsion and the first eigenvalue, Philippin [46] proved the comparisons among regular
Reuleaux polygons. Numerical support-function and convexity-constrained methods [1, 5]
suggest the same extrema more generally. The relationship with diameter-constrained
spectral optimization is discussed in [10], where the Reuleaux triangle was already proposed
as a maximizer of λ1 at fixed constant width. Our first-eigenvalue proof settles the p = 2
case of the constant-width conjecture formulated in [29, Section 5] and the first-eigenvalue
case of the minimal-width conjecture in [8]. It uses positivity and log-concavity of the
ground state. Boundary-flux reflection arguments are also related to the triangle rigidity
results of Fragalà and Velichkov [22]. The roles of inner parallel sets and distance-dependent
test functions in torsion are developed in [21].
    The four functionals, after their natural degree-one normalization, are Minkowski concave
[13,14,18,23]. Concavity and indecomposability have a substantial role in shape optimization
[16]. For constant width, the planar extreme bodies were characterized by Kallay [34]; related
questions in dimension three are studied in [32]. It is therefore natural to ask whether these
properties imply the comparisons in Theorem 1.1. We show that this is not the case, by
constructing counterexamples which also satisfy rigid-motion invariance and strict inclusion
monotonicity.

Lemma 2.1. A planar convex body of minimal width one has inradius 1/3 ≤ r ≤ 1/2. If
r < 1/2, it contains a straight three-cap body (2.1), after translation. If r = 1/3, the original
body is an equilateral triangle of altitude one.

Proof. We use the classical cap construction from [49, Chapter 6], also used in [39, Proposi-
tion 3.3] and [8]. The lower bound on r is the planar case of Steinhagen’s inequality [48].
We recall its proof while constructing the subset.
     We translate a largest inscribed disk so that its center is the origin. The origin belongs to
the convex hull of the contact normals. Indeed, a strict separation would give a direction in
which the disk could be translated and enlarged. Since the disk is contained in the body, we
have 2r ≤ 1. Suppose now that r < 1/2. Two contact normals cannot be antipodal, since the
corresponding support lines would give a width 2r < 1. We can therefore choose three contact
normals n1 , n2 , n3 whose triangle contains the origin in its interior. The corresponding
support half-planes define a triangle P ⊃ K circumscribed about B r . If a is its longest side
and p its perimeter, its least altitude is rp/a ≤ 3r. Hence 1 = w(K) ≤ w(P ) ≤ 3r.
     It remains to choose the three cap vertices. Let Vi be the vertex opposite the side
with normal ni . Since hK (ni ) = r, the width condition gives a point xi ∈ K satisfying
xi · ni ≤ −(1 − r). This point belongs to the corner region at Vi . To see this, observe that
each of the other corner caps is contained in conv(B r , Vj ), where Vj · ni = r. Every point in
such a cap satisfies x · ni ≥ −r > −(1 − r). We also have |xi | ≥ 1 − r, so convexity gives
Ai = (1 − r)xi /|xi | ∈ K. The cap conv(B r , Ai ) \ B r lies in the same corner region of P .
Since the three corner regions have disjoint interiors, the new cap sectors are disjoint as
well. Finally, convexity gives conv(B r , A1 , A2 , A3 ) ⊂ K.
     If r = 1/3, equality must hold in p/a ≤ 3, hence P is equilateral. For each of its three
side normals, the opposite vertex is the unique point of P with support value −2/3. The
width condition therefore requires K to contain all three vertices, and we conclude that
K = P.

    When r = 1/2, the inclusion B 1/2 ⊂ K will be sufficient for the comparisons below. In
the minimal-width class, this inclusion does not imply that the original body is a disk. We
denote the equilateral straight-cap configuration by Ers . We will use these cap bodies as
subsets; they need not have minimal width one themselves.

3        Inner parallel sets and Minkowski concavity
We first derive the inner-parallel-area comparisons and their geometric consequences, then

We now examine whether the inclusion of the cap bodies can give analogous comparisons

concave if
                              F (Kθ ) ≥ (1 − θ)F (K) + θF (L).

                    |K|1/2 ,       h(K)−1 ,       T (K)1/4 ,       λ1 (K)−1/2 .        (3.10)

minimality of the equilateral or Reuleaux triangle. Concavity and indecomposability have

is extreme therefore does not identify the triangle.

                    F (K) ≤ F (B 1/2 )         if K has constant width one.            (3.11)

B 1/2 , and continuity gives (3.11). This argument gives an upper bound for F (K), whereas
the triangle comparisons require a lower bound.

4     Torsional rigidity
We prove both torsion width inequalities by the same angular balancing and paired-flux

from domain monotonicity and scaling.
