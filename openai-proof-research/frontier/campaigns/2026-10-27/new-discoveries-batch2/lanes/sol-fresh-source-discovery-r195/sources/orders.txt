                                                                                                    Abstract

                                                      We examine separation theorems for the increasing convex (stop-loss) order and weak 3-convex order.
                                                  We improve prior proofs for the increasing convex order by using integrated survival functions. We
                                                  extend the separation theorem for weak 3-convex order from non-negative random variables to general
                                                  real-valued random variables with finite second moments via a constructive approach based on higher-
                                                  order integrated survival functions. A counterexample verifies that the 3-convex order and 2-convex
                                                  order chain does not hold for the weak 3-convex order. Our results enrich stochastic order theories for
                                                  actuarial science and risk management.

                                                  MSC2000 subject classification: 60E15, 91B30
                                                  Keywords: Stochastic order; Increasing convex (stop-loss) order; Weak 3-convex order; Separation
                                                  theorem; Integrated survival function



                                           1      Introduction
                                               Stochastic orders are fundamental tools in probability, actuarial science, mathematical economics and risk
                                           theory, which provide systematic ways to compare random variables according to their magnitude, variability
                                           and risk characteristics. Among various classes of stochastic order relations, convex order and increasing
                                           convex order (stop-loss order) attract extensive attention, as they are closely linked to risk aversion, risk
                                           dispersion and optimal decision-making problems. A well-known family of generalized orders, the weak n-
                                           convex orders and n-convex orders, further extend the classical framework to higher degrees, where different
                                           moment constraints and partial moment conditions are imposed to characterize higher-order risk changes.
                                               A pivotal result for increasing convex order is the separation theorem, which decomposes an increasing
                                           convex order relation into a combination of two simpler stochastic orders. Two equivalent decompositions
                                           are widely recognized: one via the usual stochastic order followed by convex order, and the other via convex

Makowski (1994) and Müller (1996).
    Makowski (1994) established relevant results via the martingale representation of increasing convex or-
ders. Müller (1996) discussed both decomposition forms of the separation theorem for non-negative random
variables using integrated survival functions. However, when extending his method to general random vari-
ables, it only works for the first decomposition (Theorem 3.3 (1)), and cannot be applied to the second one
(Theorem 3.3 (2)). Accordingly, one objective of this paper is to provide a rigorous proof for the second
decomposition using integrated survival functions.
    Recently, Denuit et al. (2014) established a separation theorem for the weak 3-convex order, demonstrat-
ing that any weak 3-convex order shift can be decomposed into a 2-convex order followed by a 3-convex order.
However, their construction and proof are confined to non-negative random variables only, which restricts
the applicability of their results to general probabilistic models involving real-valued random variables. Fur-
thermore, when extending the result to the weak n-convex order for n ≥ 4, their proof for Proposition 4.1
relies on stationary-excess operators and Khinchine representations (see, for example, Dharmadhikari and
Joag-Dev, 1988, Theorem 1.3). Since the invertibility and order-preserving properties of these tools are not
fully justified, the argument contains notable gaps.
    Motivated by the above limitations, this paper aims to generalize the separation theorem for weak 3-
convex order to the class of general real-valued random variables with finite second moments. We adopt a
constructive approach using integrated survival functions, which avoids the problematic operator techniques
in previous work. We first revisit the definitions and basic integral properties of higher-order integrated
survival functions, as well as fundamental facts about convex and weak convex orders. Then we revisit
the proofs for the separation theorem of increasing convex order. After that, we establish the separation
theorem for weak 3-convex order over all real-valued random variables by dividing discussions into two cases
based on the essential infimum of distributions. Furthermore, we construct an explicit counterexample to
demonstrate that the reverse decomposition of the weak 3-convex order does not hold, which clarifies the
one-sided nature of this separation property.
    The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects necessary preliminaries, including
the recursive definition of higher-order integrated survival functions, related integral identities, and formal
definitions of n-convex and weak n-convex orders. Section 3 revisits and rigorously proves the separation
theorem for classical increasing convex order via integrated survival functions. Section 4 is devoted to the
main result of this paper: the separation theorem for weak 3-convex order for real-valued random variables,
together with a counterexample for the reverse direction.
    Throughout, “increasing” and “decreasing” mean “nondecreasing” and “nonincreasing”, respectively. All
integrals and expectations are implicitly assumed to exist whenever they are written.

2.2    Higher-degree convex and weak convex orders
   We first recall from Denuit et al. (2014) two families of higher-order stochastic orders: n-convex orders
and weak n-convex orders, which generalize classical convex-type orders to arbitrary integer degrees n ≥ 2.

Definition 2.1. Let X, Y ∈ Ln−1 for n ≥ 2. We say that X is smaller than Y in the weak n-convex order,
written X ≤n-wcx Y , if E[X k ] = E[Y k ] for k = 1, . . . , n − 2, and
                                                [n]          [n]
                                               F X (x) ≤ F Y (x),     x ∈ R,

or equivalently,
                                   E (X − x)n−1   ≤ E (Y − x)n−1
                                                              
                                            +                +     ,           x ∈ R.

   For the weak n-convex order, the equality of the (n − 1)-th moment is not required, which relaxes the
moment restrictions of the strict n-convex order.

Definition 2.2. Let X, Y ∈ Ln−1 for n ≥ 2. We say that X is smaller than Y in the n-convex order,
written X ≤n-cx Y , if X ≤n-wcx Y and E[X n−1 ] = E[Y n−1 ].

   In short, the n-convex order is a strengthened version of the weak n-convex order by additionally imposing
the equality of the (n − 1)-th moment. The inclusion relation below follows directly from the definitions:

                                            X ≤n-cx Y =⇒ X ≤n-wcx Y.

   The n-convex and weak n-convex orders can be characterized via expected utility. A utility function u
is said to have n-th degree risk aversion if (−1)n+1 u(n) (x) ≥ 0 for all x ∈ R.

 (1) X ≤n-wcx Y if and only if E[u(X)] ≤ E[u(Y )] for all (n − 2) times continuously differentiable function
      u such that u(n−2) is increasing and convex.

 (2) X ≤n-cx Y if and only if E[u(X)] ≤ E[u(Y )] for all (n − 2) times continuously differentiable function
      u such that u(n−2) is convex.

These utility-based characterizations explain why higher convex-type orders are widely adopted to analyze
portfolio choice, insurance demand and optimal risk decisions in economics and actuarial science.
   For n = 2, the 2-convex and weak 2-convex orders reduce to the classical convex order ≤cx and increasing
convex order ≤icx (stop-loss order ≤sl ), respectively, which correspond to risk-averse and increasing risk-
averse utility functions in standard risk theory.
   The following theorem gives well-known sample path characterizations of the orders ≤cx and ≤icx .

    The classical separation theorem for the increasing convex order ≤icx is recalled below, where ≤icx is
characterized by the interplay between the usual stochastic order ≤st and the convex order ≤cx .

Theorem 3.3. (Shaked and Shanthikumar, 2007, Theorem 4.A.6) Let X and Y be two random variables.

 (1) X ≤icx Y if and only if there exists a random variable Z such that X ≤st Z ≤cx Y .

 (2) X ≤icx Y if and only if there exists a random variable Z such that X ≤cx Z ≤st Y .

5     Discussion
    In this paper, we revisit the proofs for the separation theorem of the increasing convex order, and establish
the separation theorem for the weak 3-convex order over all real-valued random variables via a constructive
approach based on higher-order integrated survival functions. A counterexample is presented to prove that
the separation of weak 3-convex order is only one-way valid, that is, a weak 3-convex order shift can not be
decomposed into a 3-convex order followed by a 2-convex order.
    Denuit et al. (2014) attempted to generalize the separation theorem to weak n-convex orders with n ≥ 4,
but the corresponding proof remains incomplete. In view of the consistent structural features across low-order
and high-order weak convex orders, we have the following conjecture:

Conjecture 5.1. Given two random variables X, Y ∈ Ln−1 for n > 2, X ≤n-wcx Y if and only if there
exists a random variable Z ∈ Ln−1 such that X ≤(n−1)-cx Z ≤n-cx Y .

    Similar to the notion of n-th degree m-mean preserving stochastic dominance (Liu, 2014; Wang and Wu,
2025), we can define n-th degree m-preserving convex order, denoted as (n, m)-CX order, where m and n are
integers such that 0 ≤ m < n. For X, Y ∈ Ln−1 , X is said to be smaller than Y in the sense of (n, m)-CX
                                      [n]             [n]
order, written X ≤(n,m)-cx Y , if F X (x) ≤ F Y (x) for all x ∈ R, and E[X k ] = E[Y k ] for k = 1, . . . , m.
The weak n-convex order and n-convex order are particular cases of (n, m)-CX order with m = n − 2 and
m = n − 1, respectively.

Conjecture 5.2. Let X, Y ∈ Ln−1 and n > m ≥ 1.

 (1) X ≤(n,m)-cx Y if and only if there exist andom variable Z1 , . . . , Zn−m−1 ∈ Ln−1 such that

                     X ≤(m+1,m)-cx Z1 ≤(m+2,m+1)-cx · · · ≤(n−1,n−2)-cx Zn−m−1 ≤(n,n−1)-cx Y.

 (2) For n > k > m, X ≤(n,m)-cx Y if and only if there exist andom variable Z ∈ Ln−1 such that

                                                 X ≤(k,m)-cx Z ≤(n,k)-cx Y.
