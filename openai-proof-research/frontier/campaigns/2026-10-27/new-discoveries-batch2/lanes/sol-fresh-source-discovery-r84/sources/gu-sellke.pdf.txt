                                                     A Counterexample to the Gaussian Completely Monotone
                                                                          Conjecture
                                                                                    Yuzhou Gu∗                 Mark Sellke†




arXiv:2605.11656v1 [math.PR] 12 May 2026
                                                                                                   Abstract
                                                        We provide an explicit probability measure on R for which the fifth time derivative of the
                                                     entropy along the heat flow is positive at some time. This disproves the Gaussian completely
                                                     monotone (GCM) conjecture (Cheng–Geng ’15) and therefore also the Gaussian optimality
                                                     conjecture (McKean ’66) and the entropy power conjecture (Toscani ’15). Our proof also implies
                                                     the existence of a log-concave probability measure on R for which the GCM conjecture fails at
                                                     some order. The explicit counterexample was found by GPT-5.5 Pro.

                                           The Gaussian completely monotone (GCM) conjecture [CG15] states that

                                                                                   (−1)m Hµ(m) (t) ≥ 0,         m ≥ 1, t > 0.                         (1)

                                           A strengthening of this conjecture is the Gaussian optimality conjecture due to [McK66, Section 12],
                                           which states that the left-hand side of (1) is exactly minimized by Gaussian measures when µ has
                                           fixed variance. A further strengthening is the entropy power conjecture [Tos15], which asserts that

                                                                                        dm
                                                                              (−1)m−1       Nµ (t) ≥ 0,         Nµ (t) = e−2Hµ (t) .
                                                                                        dtm
                                           The same conjectures extend to higher dimension d > 1 (with an additional factor of 1/d in the
                                           exponent of the last expression). It is easy to see that McKean’s conjecture is stronger than GCM,
                                           while [Wan24] showed that the entropy power conjecture implies McKean’s conjecture.
                                              In dimension 1, the GCM conjecture has been proved for derivatives of order m ≤ 4 [CG15].
                                           The higher-dimensional version is true up to order m ≤ 2 via Costa’s entropy power inequality
                                           [Cos85, Vil00], and in dimension d ≤ 4 for m ≤ 3 [GYG22]. The special case of log-concave
                                           probability measures has also attracted considerable interest [Tos15, ZAG18, GYG22, Wan25]. We

these conjectures.
    Our main result is that in dimension 1, the GCM conjecture fails at the fifth order, which is
optimal by the result of [CG15] mentioned above. Thus all three related conjectures are false.
Theorem 1. There exists a probability measure µ on R and a time t0 > 0 such that

                                                   Hµ(5) (t0 ) > 0.

    For log-concave probability measures, we show that the GCM conjecture also fails.
Theorem 2. There exists a log-concave probability measure µ on R, a time t0 > 0, and an integer
m ≥ 1, such that
                                    (−1)m Hµ(m) (t0 ) < 0.
   An explicit order is not obtained, but it must be at least 6, because the GCM conjecture is
known to hold for log-concave probability measures up to the fifth order [ZAG18, Wan25].

3    Proof of Theorem 2
We now show that the preceding counterexample leads to a log-concave counterexample, at a higher
order. The additional ingredient is backward propagation of complete monotonicity for analytic
functions.
Lemma 1 (Backward propagation of complete monotonicity). Let A > 0, and let F be real analytic
on (−A, ∞). Suppose that

                                (−1)m F (m) (s) ≥ 0,       m ≥ 1, s > 0.

Then the same inequalities hold for all m ≥ 1 and all s ∈ (−A, ∞).
Proof. This is a standard consequence of the local Taylor-series characterization of completely
monotone functions; see, e.g., [Wid41, SSV12]. For completeness, we recall the short argument. Set
U = −F ′ . Then U is completely monotone on (0, ∞), and all inequalities (−1)n U (n) ≥ 0 extend to
0 by continuity. If they hold at a point c, then the Taylor expansion at c gives, for h ≥ 0 sufficiently
small,
                                                ∞
                                                X  (−1)n+j U (n+j) (c) j
                          (−1)n U (n) (c − h) =                       h ≥ 0.
                                                           j!
                                                 j=0

Thus the inequalities propagate locally to the left; a standard connectedness argument propagates
them throughout (−A, 0].

Proof of Theorem 2. Let µ be the finitely supported probability measure in (2). We use two
elementary facts. First, if a probability measure is supported in [−R, R], then its heat evolution is
strictly log-concave for every t > R2 /2. Indeed, writing πx for the posterior law of the initial atom
conditioned on the endpoint x, one has
                                                1    Varπx (a)     1   R2
                       ∂x2 log(pt ∗ µ)(x) = −      +           ≤ −   +    < 0.
                                                2t     4t2         2t 4t2
Second, for a finitely supported initial measure, Hµ (t) is real analytic on (0, ∞). Indeed, locally
uniformly for t in a compact subinterval of (0, ∞), the finite Gaussian mixture gt and all its t-
                                                           2
derivatives are bounded by a polynomial in |x| times e−cx . Moreover gt (x) has matching Gaussian
lower bounds coming from the extreme atoms, so | log gt (x)| ≤ C(1 + x2 ). Thus the Taylor expansion
in t may be integrated termwise by Gaussian domination.
    Take T large enough so that ν = PT µ is a log-concave probability measure. Suppose, for
contradiction, that the GCM conjecture holds for ν. Then
                                   dm
                           (−1)m       H(Ps ν) ≥ 0,        m ≥ 1,      s > 0.
                                   dsm

But Ps ν = PT +s µ. Thus the function
                                            F (s) = Hµ (T + s)
is real analytic on (−T, ∞) and satisfies
                               (−1)m F (m) (s) ≥ 0,      m ≥ 1, s > 0.
By Lemma 1, the same inequalities hold for all m ≥ 1 and all s > −T . Taking s = 1/3 − T and
m = 5 gives
                          0 ≤ (−1)5 F (5) (1/3 − T ) = −Hµ(5) (1/3),
which contradicts Proposition 1.

A    Verification code
The following SageMath script gives the rigorous verification used in Proposition 1. The input atoms
and weights are exact rationals. All integrations are carried out with Arb ball arithmetic [Joh17]
through SageMath’s ComplexBallField.integral; the printed balls are rigorous enclosures.

 3 # Increasing PREC tightens the final balls but does not change the input data.
 4 PREC = 256
 5 C = ComplexBallField(PREC)
 6 R = RealBallField(PREC)

 8 tau = QQ(1) / QQ(3)

10 normalization = 1 / (4 * C.pi() * tau_C).sqrt()

15 c = [34, 93, 134, 123, 75, 31, 8, 1]

20     data.append(( a, w))

26 def hermite_phys(n, z):
27     """Physicists’ Hermite polynomial H_n(z), evaluated by recursion."""
28     parent = z.parent()
29     if n == 0:
30         return parent(1)
31     H0 = parent(1)
32     H1 = 2 * z
33     if n == 1:
34         return H1
35     for k in range(1, n):
36         H0, H1 = H1, 2 * z * H1 - 2 * k * H0
37     return H1
38

40 def G(z, analytic):
41     """Ball enclosure for d^5/dt^5 (g_t log g_t) at t = 1/3."""
42     rhos = []
43     for a, w in data:
44         y = z - C(a)
45         rho = C(w) * normalization * (-(y * y) / (4 * tau_C)).exp()
46         rhos.append(rho)
47
48     g = sum(rhos, C(0))
49     posterior = [rho / g for rho in rhos]
50
51     K = [C(0)] * 6
52     K[0] = C(1)
53     sqrt_tau = tau_C.sqrt()


                                                 7
 54     for n in range(1, 6):
 55         s = C(0)
 56         for (a, _), pi_i in zip(data, posterior):
 57             y = z - C(a)
 58             u = y / (2 * sqrt_tau)
 59             s += pi_i * hermite_phys(2 * n, u) / ((4 * tau_C) ** n)
 60         K[n] = s

 62     L = [C(0)] * 6
 63     # Passing Arb’s analytic flag makes log reject invalid branch enclosures.
 64     L[0] = g.log(analytic=analytic)
 65     for n in range(1, 6):
 66         s = K[n]

 68             s -= binomial(n - 1, r - 1) * L[r] * K[n - r]

 71     q = C(0)
 72     for r in range(0, 6):
 73         q += binomial(5, r) * K[r] * L[5 - r]
 74     return g * q
