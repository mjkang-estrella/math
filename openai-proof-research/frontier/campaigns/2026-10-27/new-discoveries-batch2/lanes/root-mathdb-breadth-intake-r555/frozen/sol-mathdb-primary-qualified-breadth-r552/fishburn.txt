 Abstract 
    
 A Fishburn permutation is a permutation which avoids the bivincular pattern  ( 231 , { 1 } , { 1 } ) (231,\{1\},\{1\}) , while an ascent sequence is a sequence of nonnegative integers in which each entry is less than or equal to one more than the number of ascents to its left.
Fishburn permutations and ascent sequences are linked by a bijection  g g  of Bousquet-Mélou, Claesson, Dukes, and Kitaev.
We write  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  to denote the set of Fishburn permutations of length  n n  which avoid each of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  and we write  A n ​ ( α 1 , … , α k ) A_{n}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})  to denote the set of ascent sequences which avoid each of  α 1 , … , α k \alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}.
We settle a conjecture of Gil and Weiner by showing that  g g  restricts to a bijection between  F n ​ ( 3412 ) F_{n}(3412)  and  A n ​ ( 201 ) A_{n}(201).
Building on work of Gil and Weiner, we use elementary techniques to enumerate  F n ​ ( 123 ) F_{n}(123)  with respect to inversion number and number of left-to-right maxima, obtaining expressions in terms of  q q -binomial coefficients, and to enumerate  F n ​ ( 123 , σ ) F_{n}(123,\sigma)  for all  σ \sigma.
We use generating tree techniques to study the generating functions for  F n ​ ( 321 , 1423 ) F_{n}(321,1423) ,  F n ​ ( 321 , 3124 ) F_{n}(321,3124) , and  F n ​ ( 321 , 2143 ) F_{n}(321,2143)  with respect to inversion number and number of left-to-right maxima.
We use these results to show  | F n ​ ( 321 , 1423 ) | = | F n ​ ( 321 , 3124 ) | = F n + 2 − n − 1 |F_{n}(321,1423)|=|F_{n}(321,3124)|=F_{n+2}-n-1 , where  F n F_{n}  is a Fibonacci number, and  | F n ​ ( 321 , 2143 ) | = 2 n − 1 |F_{n}(321,2143)|=2^{n-1}.
We conclude with a variety of conjectures and open problems. 
  
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 A  Fishburn permutation  is a permutation  π \pi , written in one-line notation, with no subsequence  π j \pi_{j} ,  π j + 1 \pi_{j+1} ,  π k \pi_{k}  for which  j + 1 < k j+1<k ,  π j = π k + 1 \pi_{j}=\pi_{k}+1 , and  π j + 1 > π j \pi_{j+1}>\pi_{j}.
In the language of Bousquet-Mélou, Claesson, Dukes, and Kitaev  [ 6 ] , a Fishburn permutation is a permutation which avoids the bivincular pattern  ( 231 , { 1 } , { 1 } ) (231,\{1\},\{1\}).
We will write  f f  to denote this pattern, and we will refer to it as the  Fishburn pattern.
In Figure  1  we have the graph of  f f ; here bold lines indicate required adjacencies. 
 
 
 Figure 1:  The graph of the Fishburn pattern  f f. 
 
 
 As an example,  3142 3142  is a Fishburn permutation, but  35142 35142  is not, since  3 3 ,  5 5 , and  2 2  form a copy of  f f. 
 
 
 Fishburn permutations are named for the fact that they are counted by the Fishburn numbers.
Bousquet-Mélou, Claesson, Dukes, and Kitaev  [ 6 ]  appear to be the first to have proved this, though they do not state the fact explicitly.
The Fishburn numbers may be defined by the fact  [ 38 ]  that their generating function is given by

 they are the terms of sequence A022493 in the Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences (OEIS).

 In addition to Fishburn permutations, the Fishburn numbers are known to count a variety of other combinatorial objects, including ascent sequences  [ 6 ] ,  ( 𝟐 + 𝟐 ) ({\bf 2}+{\bf 2}) -free posets  [ 6 ,  12 ,  13 ] , linearized chord diagrams of degree  n n   [ 38 ] , nonisomorphic interval orders on  n n  unlabeled points  [ 19 ,  20 ] , and upper triangular matrices of any size with nonnegative integer entries and no zero rows or columns in which the sum of all entries is  n n   [ 16 ].
Several authors have studied connections among these objects  [ 14 ,  15 ,  26 ].
Like the set of Fishburn permutations, which is a superset of the set of permutations which avoid  231 231 , many of these objects are supersets of sets known to be enumerated by the Catalan numbers. 
 
 
 Fishburn numbers are also of number theoretic interest.
For example, Andrews and Sellers have shown  [ 3 ]  that they satisfy congruences similar to those which hold for the partition function  p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) , which gives the number of integer partitions of  n n , and Andrews and Jelínek have studied  [ 2 ]  some related  q q -series identities. 
 
 
 Among the combinatorial objects known to be in bijection with Fishburn permutations, we will be especially interested in ascent sequences.
In a sequence  a 1 ⋯ a n a_{1}\cdots a_{n}  of integers, an  ascent  is an index  j j  such that  a j < a j + 1 a_{j}<a_{j+1} ; we write  asc ( a 1 ⋯ a n ) \asc(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})  to denote the number of ascents in  a 1 ⋯ a n a_{1}\cdots a_{n}.
An  ascent sequence  is a sequence  a 1 ⋯ a n a_{1}\cdots a_{n}  of nonnegative integers such that  a 1 = 0 a_{1}=0  and for each  j ≥ 2 j\geq 2 , we have  a j ≤ 1 + asc ( a 1 ⋯ a j − 1 ) a_{j}\leq 1+\asc(a_{1}\cdots a_{j-1}).
For example,  001021301 001021301  is an ascent sequence (of length 9) but  001031201 001031201  is not. 
 
 
 Bousquet-Mélou, Claesson, Dukes, and Kitaev  [ 6 ]  have given a natural bijection between Fishburn permutations and ascent sequences.
To describe it, suppose  π \pi  is a Fishburn permutation of length  n − 1 n-1.
A  site  in  π \pi  is a space between two entries in  π \pi , before the first entry of  π \pi , or after the last entry of  π \pi ; note that  π \pi  has  n n  sites,
We say a site is  active  whenever the permutation we obtain by inserting  n n  into that site is also a Fishburn permutation.
In a Fishburn permutation we number the active sites from left to right, starting with  0 0.
For example,  415326 415326  is a Fishburn permutation whose five active sites are numbered as in Figure  2. 
 
 
 4 1 1 0 ​ 5 2 ​ 3 2 3 ​ 6 4 {}^{0}4\ 1^{1}5^{2}3\ 2^{3}6^{4} 
 Figure 2:  The numbering of the active sites in the Fishburn permutation  415326 415326. 
 
 
 We can construct each Fishburn permutation  π \pi  of length  n n  uniquely by successively inserting  1 , 2 , … , n 1,2,\ldots,n  into active sites.
We write  g ⁡ ( π ) g(\pi)  to denote the sequence  a 1 ⋯ a n a_{1}\cdots a_{n}  of labels of these active sites.
For example,  g ⁡ ( 4175326 ) = 0110132 g(4175326)=0110132.
Bousquet-Mélou, Claesson, Dukes, and Kitaev  [ 6 ]  have shown that  g g  is a bijection between the set of Fishburn permutation of length  n n  and the set of ascent sequences of length  n n. 
 
 
 We will be interested in permutations and ascent sequences which avoid certain permutations or sequences.
In general, we say a sequence  α \alpha  of nonnegative integers  contains  a sequence  β \beta  whenever  α \alpha  has a subsequence with the same length and relative order as  β \beta.
We say  α \alpha   avoids   β \beta  whenever  α \alpha  does not contain  β \beta.
For example, the ascent sequence  0110132 0110132  contains both  000 000  (in the form of the subsequence  111 111 ) and  021 021  (in the form of the subsequence  032 032  and three subsequences  132 132 ) but it avoids  210 210.
Similarly, the permutation  415326 415326  contains  231 231  (in the form of the subsequences  453 453  and  452 452 ) and avoids  2413 2413.
For any sequences  α 1 , … , α k \alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}  of nonnegative integers we write  A n ​ ( α 1 , … , α k ) A_{n}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})  to denote the set of ascent sequences of length  n n  which avoid each of  α 1 , … , α k \alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}.
Similarly, for any permutations  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  we write  S n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) S_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  to denote the set of permutations of length  n n  which avoid each of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k} , and we write  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  to denote the set of Fishburn permutations of length  n n  which avoid each of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}. 
 
 
 In some situations we will be interested in relationships between  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  and  A n ​ ( α 1 , … , α ℓ ) A_{n}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}).
The cases we consider are limited and our arguments are ad hoc, but recently Cerbai and Claesson have described  [ 8 ]  a general method of establishing similar relationships.
In particular, Cerbai and Claesson give an explicit construction which transforms a basis  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  for a set of pattern-avoiding Fishburn permutations to a basis for a corresponding set of pattern-avoiding modified ascent sequences.
Modified ascent sequences are in bijection with ascent sequences, and often this latter basis can be translated to a basis  α 1 , … , α ℓ \alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}  for a set of pattern-avoiding ascent sequences. 
 
 
 In most situations we will be interested in enumerating  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  or  A n ​ ( α 1 , … , α ℓ ) A_{n}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell})  independent of their relationship.
In addition, in some cases we will refine these enumerations by studying generating functions for these sets with respect to specific statistics.
For permutations we will primarily be interested in two statistics: the inversion number and the number of left-to-right maxima.
We recall that an  inversion  in a permutation  π \pi  is an ordered pair  ( j , k ) (j,k)  such that  j < k j<k  and  π j > π k \pi_{j}>\pi_{k}.
We write  inv ⁡ ( π ) \inv(\pi)  to denote the number of inversions in  π \pi.
We also recall that a  left-to-right maximum  in  π \pi  is an entry of  π \pi  which is greater than all of the entries to its left.
We write  lrmax ⁡ ( π ) \ltormax(\pi)  to denote the number of left-to-right maxima in  π \pi.
For example,  inv ⁡ ( 5176243 ) = 12 \inv(5176243)=12  and  lrmax ⁡ ( 3265174 ) = 3 \ltormax(3265174)=3. 
 
 
 Beginning with Duncan and Steingrímsson  [ 17 ] , several additional authors have studied pattern-avoiding ascent sequences  [ 5 ,  7 ,  11 ,  21 ,  27 ,  28 ,  29 ,  30 ,  31 ,  33 ] , statistics on ascent sequences  [ 11 ,  21 ,  24 ] , and connections between these sets and other combinatorial objects  [ 7 ,  37 ].
The study of pattern-avoiding Fishburn permutations was initiated by Gil and Weiner  [ 23 ] , though they are also used as a bridge in work of Chen, Yan, and Zhou  [ 10 ].

This allows us to use results of Simion and Schmidt  [ 34 ]  to recover several results of Gil and Weiner  [ 23 ].
In Section  3  we prove a conjecture of Gil and Weiner  [ 23 ]  by showing that  g g  restricts to a bijection between  F n ​ ( 3412 ) F_{n}(3412)  and  A n ​ ( 201 ) A_{n}(201).

 2  When Fishburn Means  231 231 -Avoiding 

 
 Gil and Weiner  [ 23 ]  have enumerated  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  for a variety of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  of lengths three and four.
In comparing their results with those of Simion and Schmidt  [ 34 ] , one notes that in some cases we have  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) = S n ​ ( 231 , σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})=S_{n}(231,\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}).
In this section we characterize those situations, by showing that this occurs if and only if at least one of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  is contained in  3142 3142.
This allows us to recover several of Gil and Weiner’s enumerations.
We begin by studying Fishburn permutations which contain 231. 
 
 
 Lemma 2.1. 
 
 If  π \pi  is a permutation which contains  231 231  but avoids  f f  then  π \pi  contains  3142 3142. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Suppose by way of contradiction that  π \pi  contains  231 231  but avoids both  f f  and  3142 3142. 
 
 
 Among all copies of  231 231  in  π \pi , consider those in which the largest element is as large as possible.
Among these copies of  231 231 , consider those in which the leftmost entry is as far to the right as possible.
And among these copies of  231 231 , choose the one in which the rightmost entry is as large as possible; let the entries in this copy be  a a ,  b b , and  c c , from left to right. 
 
 
 In Figure  3  we have the diagram of  π \pi  divided into regions according to the positions of  a a ,  b b , and  c c.
We have numbered the regions where other entries of the permutation can be for ease of reference. 
 
 
 Figure 3:  The permutation  π \pi  in the proof of Lemma  2.1  with the chosen copy  a ​ b ​ c abc  of  231 231. 
 
 
 By our choice of  b b , boxes 2 and 3 must be empty.
Similarly, by our choice of  a a , boxes 6 and 10 must be empty.
And by our choice of  c c , boxes 11 and 12 must be empty.
This leaves us with the diagram in Figure  4 , in which the shaded regions must be empty. 
 
 
 Figure 4:  The permutation  π \pi  in the proof of Lemma  2.1  with some empty regions shaded. 
 
 
 Now if box 14 contains an entry  x x , then  a , x , b , c a,x,b,c  is a copy of  3142 3142 , which contradicts our assumption that  π \pi  avoids  3142 3142.
So box 14 must be empty and  a a  and  b b  must be adjacent. 
 
 
 On the other hand, if  a ≠ c + 1 a\neq c+1  then  c + 1 c+1  must be in box 9.
In this case  c + 1 c+1  is the smallest entry in its box.
Hence  c + 1 c+1 , the entry immediately to its right, and  c c , form a copy of  f f.
This contradicts our assumption that  π \pi  does not contain  f f. 
 
 
 Finally, if  a = c + 1 a=c+1  then  a , b , c a,b,c  is a copy of  f f , which is also a contradiction.
∎ 
 
 
 
 We note that Lemma  2.1  is implicit in Gil and Weiner’s proof of  [ 23 , Theorem 3.4]. 
 
 
 Lemma  2.1  allows us to characterize those sets of forbidden patterns for which avoiding  f f  is equivalent to avoiding  231 231. 
 
 
 Proposition 2.2. 
 
 For any permutations  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  the following are equivalent. 
 
 (i)  
 
 F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) = S n ​ ( 231 , σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})=S_{n}(231,\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  for all  n ≥ 0 n\geq 0. 
 
 (ii)  
 
 At least one of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}  is contained in  3142 3142. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 (i)  ⇒ \Rightarrow  (ii)
If  F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) = S n ​ ( 231 , σ 1 , … , σ k ) F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})=S_{n}(231,\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  for all  n ≥ 0 n\geq 0  then in particular  3142 ∉ F 4 ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) 3142\not\in F_{4}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}) , since  3142 3142  contains  231 231  and so is not in  S n ​ ( 231 , σ 1 , … , σ k ) S_{n}(231,\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}).
But  3142 3142  does not contain  f f , so it must contain at least one of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}. 
 
 
 (ii)  ⇒ \Rightarrow  (i)
Since  f f  is a special type of  231 231 , we must have  S n ​ ( 231 , σ 1 , … , σ k ) ⊆ F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) S_{n}(231,\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})\subseteq F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  for all  n ≥ 0 n\geq 0.
To prove the reverse inclusion, suppose  π ∈ F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) \pi\in F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}).
If  π ∉ S n ​ ( 231 , σ 1 , … , σ k ) \pi\not\in S_{n}(231,\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})  then  π \pi  contains  231 231.
By Lemma  2.1  this means  π \pi  contains  3142 3142.
Therefore,  π \pi  does not avoid each of  σ 1 , … , σ k \sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k} , which contradicts the fact that  π ∈ F n ​ ( σ 1 , … , σ k ) \pi\in F_{n}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}).

 10  Open Problems and Conjectures 

 
 The study of pattern-avoiding Fishburn permutations, pattern-avoiding ascent sequences, and the connections between them still includes numerous open problems, some of which we review in this section.
We focus on enumerative and bijective questions that do not involve statistics on permutations; studying statistics on Fishburn permutations offers a variety of additional avenues for future work. 
 
 
 In addition to Fishburn permutations, pattern-avoiding ascent sequences seem to have connections with other families of permutations.
For example, several authors, beginning with Knuth  [ 25 ] , have studied permutations which are sortable using one or more stacks, arranged in various configurations.
Building on work of Atkinson, Murphy, and Ruškuc  [ 4 ]  and Smith  [ 35 ] , Cerbai, Claesson, and Ferrari  [ 9 ]  have generalized this idea.
Given a permutation  σ \sigma , we can construct a sorting machine using two stacks connected in series.
We require that the entries in the first stack always avoid  σ \sigma , when read from top to bottom, while the entries in the second stack must always be increasing (in order to obtain the identity permutation).
If  π \pi  is sortable using this series of stacks, then we say  π \pi  is  σ \sigma -machine sortable.
Cerbai, Claesson, and Ferrari have generated data  [ 9 , Section 6]  that support the following conjecture.
