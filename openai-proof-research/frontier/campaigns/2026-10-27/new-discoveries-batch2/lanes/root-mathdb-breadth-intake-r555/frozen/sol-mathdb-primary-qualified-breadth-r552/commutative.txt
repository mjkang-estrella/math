 Abstract. 
    
 We investigate the structure of maximal commutative subalgebras of the finite dimensional Grassmann algebra over a field of characteristic different from two. 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  Grassmann algebra, exterior algebra, maximal commutative subalgebra
 
 1991 Mathematics Subject Classification 15A75, 05D05, 16W55, 13A02
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 The Grassmann algebra (exterior algebra)  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  over a field  F F  of characteristic different from two is the following finite dimensional associative algebra of rank  n n : 
 

 
 (1) 
 
 G ⁡ ( n ) \displaystyle G(n) 
 = F [ x 1 , … , x n ] / ⟨ x i x j + x j x i ∣ 1 ≤ i , j ≤ n ⟩ F. \displaystyle=F[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/\langle x_{i}x_{j}+x_{j}x_{i}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n\rangle_{F}. 
 
 
 The Grassmann algebra is widely used in ring theory, differential geometry and the theory of manifolds
(for example, see  [ 2 ,  3 ,  9 ,  12 ] ). 
 
 
 It is well known (for example, see  [ 2 ] ) that  dim F ​ G ​ ( n ) = 2 n \mathrm{dim}_{F}G(n)=2^{n}  and the identity  [ [ x , y ] , z ] = 0 [[x,y],z]=0  is satisfied for all  x , y , z ∈ G ⁡ ( n ) x,y,z\in G(n). The algebra  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  has a large center and it is natural to investigate the structure of commutative subalgebras in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n). It was recently studied by Domokos and Zubor  [ 5 ]. Historically, the structure of commutative subalgebras are widely studied in certain rings and algebras (for example, see  [ 1 ,  4 ,  7 ,  8 ,  10 ,  13 ,  15 ] ). 
 
 
 When  n n  is even, the structure of maximal commutative subalgebras (with respect to inclusion) in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  is quite well-understood. In particular, Domokos and Zubor showed that all such maximal commutative subalgebras in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  of even rank  n n  have dimension  3 ⋅ 2 n − 2 3\cdot 2^{n-2}  (Corollary 2.4, Theorem 7.1 (i) in  [ 5 ] ) despite the fact that not all of them are isomorphic (Theorem 7.1 (ii) in  [ 5 ] ). 
 
 
 However, the structure of maximal commutative subalgebras (with respect to inclusion) in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  of odd rank  n n  is less clear. Domokos and Zubor obtained the maximum dimension of commutative subalgebras (Theorem 7.1 (i) in  [ 5 ] ). Some partial results on the structure of maximal commutative subalgebras (with respect to inclusion) were obtained for  n = 5 n=5  and  n = 7 n=7  (Proposition 7.5 in  [ 5 ] ). 
 
 
 The main goal of this paper is to provide new constructions of maximal commutative subalgebras (with respect to inclusion) of  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  of odd rank  n n. Our constructions are based on certain intersecting families of subsets of odd size in the power set of  n n. We make the connection between maximal commutative subalgebras of  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  and such combinatorial objects explicit in Section  2. In Section  3 , we introduce an intersecting family of subsets of odd size based on the classical Fano plane. It is the foundation of our constructions in Section  4  and Section  5. 
 
 
 Based on our results in Section  4  and Section  5 , we make inroads into solving two conjectures posted by Domokos and Zubor (Conjecture 7.3 and Conjecture 7.4 in  [ 5 ] ). Along the way we state a few conjectures, some of which are purely computational in nature, related to our constructions of maximal commutative subalgebras in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  of odd rank  n n. 
 
 
 
 2.  Combinatorics of the Grassmann algebra 

 
 Let  m , n ∈ ℕ m,n\in\mathbb{N}  such that  m ≤ n m\leq n. For two integers  i i  and  j j  ( i ≤ j i\leq j ), we write 
 

 
 
 [ i , j ] = { k ∈ ℤ ∣ i ≤ k ≤ j }. [i,j]=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\mid i\leq k\leq j\}. 
 
 
 The set  [ 1 , k ] [1,k]  is often abbreviated to  [ k ] [k]. The cardinality of a set  J J  is denoted by  | J | |J|. 
 
 
 In the Grassmann algebra  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  of rank  n n  we can choose the basis  { 1 , v 1 , … , v n } \{1,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}  such that 
 

 
 
 G ( n ) = F ⟨ 1 , v 1 , … , v n ∣ v i v j = − v j v i , 1 ≤ i , j ≤ n ⟩. G(n)=F\langle 1,v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\mid v_{i}v_{j}=-v_{j}v_{i},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n\rangle. 
 
 
 For  I = { i 1 , … , i k } ⊂ [ n ] I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\subset[n] , we denote  v I v_{I}  by  v I := v i 1 v i 2 ⋯ v i k v_{I}:=v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}\cdots v_{i_{k}}. For any  v I , v J ∈ G ⁡ ( n ) v_{I},v_{J}\in G(n) , in which  I , J ⊂ [ n ] I,J\subset[n] , we have the following: 
 

 
 (2) 
 
 v I ​ v J \displaystyle v_{I}v_{J} 
 = ( − 1 ) | I | ​ | J | ​ v J ​ v I. \displaystyle=(-1)^{|I||J|}v_{J}v_{I}. 
 
 
 If  | I | |I|  is odd, then  v I v_{I}  is an  odd element  in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n) , otherwise it is an  even element. 
 
 
 Clearly,  G ⁡ ( n ) = G 0 ⊕ G 1 G(n)=G_{0}\oplus G_{1}  is a  ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -graded algebra, where the  even part  is 
 

 
 
 G 0 = ⨁ k ∈ 2 ​ ℕ ∪ { 0 } span F ​ { v J ∣ J ⊆ [ n ] ​ and ​ | J | = k } G_{0}=\bigoplus_{k\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}{\rm span}_{F}\{v_{J}\mid J\subseteq[n]\;\text{and}\;|J|=k\} 
 
 
 and the  odd part  is 
 

 
 
 G 1 = ⨁ k ∈ ℕ ∖ 2 ​ ℕ span F ​ { v J ∣ J ⊆ [ n ] ​ and ​ | J | = k }. G_{1}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus 2\mathbb{N}}{\rm span}_{F}\{v_{J}\mid J\subseteq[n]\;\text{and}\;|J|=k\}. 
 
 
 The collection of all subsets of  [ n ] [n]  (including the empty set  ∅ \emptyset ) and the collection of all subsets of size  m m  in the set  [ n ] [n]  are denoted by  𝒫 n \mathcal{P}_{n}  and  𝒫 n ​ ( m ) \mathcal{P}_{n}(m) , respectively. Set 
 

 
 
 𝒫 n ( ∗ ) = ∪ m = 1 n 𝒫 n ( m ) = 𝒫 n ∖ ∅. \mathcal{P}_{n}(*)=\cup_{m=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{n}(m)=\mathcal{P}_{n}\setminus\emptyset. 
 
 
 The nonempty collection  Δ \mathrm{\Delta}  of subsets of odd size in  𝒫 n ​ ( ∗ ) \mathcal{P}_{n}(*)  is called an  algebraic system  if, for any  A 1 ∈ 𝒫 n ∖ Δ A_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\setminus\mathrm{\Delta}  such that  | A 1 | |A_{1}|  is even and
for any  A 2 ∈ Δ A_{2}\in\mathrm{\Delta}  such that  A 1 ∩ A 2 = ∅ A_{1}\cap A_{2}=\emptyset , we have 
 

 
 
 A 1 ∪ A 2 ∈ Δ. A_{1}\cup A_{2}\in\mathrm{\Delta}. 
 
 
 An algebraic system  Δ \Delta  is called  commutative  if any two elements from  Δ \Delta  have a non-trivial intersection. A commutative system  Δ \Delta  is called  maximal  if, for any  A ∈ 𝒫 n ​ ( ∗ ) ∖ Δ A\in\mathcal{P}_{n}(*)\setminus\Delta  such that  | A | |A|  is odd, there exists  B ∈ Δ B\in\Delta  such that  A ∩ B = ∅ A\cap B=\emptyset. 
 
 
 Example 1. 
 
 For  n = 4 n=4 , let  Δ = { { 1 } , { 1 , 2 , 3 } , { 1 , 3 , 4 } , { 1 , 2 , 4 } } \Delta=\{\{1\},\{1,2,3\},\{1,3,4\},\{1,2,4\}\}  and  𝒟 = { { 2 } , { 2 , 3 , 4 } , { 1 , 2 , 3 } } \mathcal{D}=\{\{2\},\{2,3,4\},\newline
\{1,2,3\}\}. Then  Δ \Delta  is algebraic and  𝒟 \mathcal{D}  is not algebraic because  { 1 , 2 , 4 } = { 2 } ∪ { 1 , 4 } ∉ 𝒟 \{1,2,4\}=\{2\}\cup\{1,4\}\notin\mathcal{D}. 
 
 
 
 A subalgebra in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  is called  homogeneous  if it is spanned by monomials. It is well known that a maximal commutative subalgebra of  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  is a homogeneous subalgebra (see also Theorem 4.3 in  [ 5 ] , which says that each commutative subalgebra in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n)  can be transformed into a homogeneous commutative one by a simple process of linear transformations). 
 
 
 Let  M M  be a maximal commutative subalgebra (with respect to inclusion) of the Grassmann algebra  G ⁡ ( n ) = G 0 ⊕ G 1 G(n)=G_{0}\oplus G_{1} , where  n ≥ 2 n\geq 2. From now on, we focus solely on homogeneous commutative subalgebras of a Grassmann algebra ( see the comment in the previous paragraph). Clearly,  M M  can be written as  M = G 0 ⊕ M 1 M=G_{0}\oplus M_{1}  where  M 1 ⊂ G 1 M_{1}\subset G_{1}. Consider 
 

 
 
 ∇ M := { I ⊆ [ n ] ∣ v I ∈ M 1 }. \nabla_{M}:=\{I\subseteq[n]\mid v_{I}\in M_{1}\}. 
 
 
 If  T T  is a maximal commutative algebraic system in  [ n ] [n] , then we define: 
 

 
 
 M T := span F ​ { v I ∣ I ∈ T }. M_{T}:={\rm span}_{F}\{v_{I}\mid I\in T\}. 
 
 
 We have the following lemma: 
 
 
 Lemma 1. 
 
 If  M M  is a maximal (with respect to inclusion) commutative subalgebra of  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n) , then  ∇ M \nabla_{M}  is a maximal commutative algebraic system. Moreover, if  T T  is a maximal commutative algebraic system, then  G 0 ⊕ M T G_{0}\oplus M_{T}  is a maximal commutative subalgebra in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n). 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Let  M = G 0 ⊕ M 1 M=G_{0}\oplus M_{1}. If  v I , v J ∈ M 1 v_{I},v_{J}\in M_{1} , then  v I v_{I}  and  v J v_{J}  commute if and only if  I ∩ J ≠ ∅ I\cap J\neq\emptyset. Hence  ∇ M \nabla_{M}  is commutative. 
 
 
 Let  S ∈ 𝒫 n ∖ ∇ M S\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\setminus\nabla_{M} ,  | S | |S|  is odd and  S ∩ S ′ ≠ ∅ S\cap S^{\prime}\neq\emptyset  for any  S ′ ∈ ∇ M S^{\prime}\in\nabla_{M}. Then  v S ∉ M v_{S}\notin M ,  v S ​ v S ′ = 0 = v S ′ ​ v S v_{S}v_{S^{\prime}}=0=v_{S^{\prime}}v_{S}  and hence  v S v_{S} ,  v S ′ v_{S^{\prime}}  commute. So  v S v_{S}  commutes with all elements in  M 1 M_{1}  and with all elements in  G 0 G_{0}. So  M ∪ { v S } M\cup\{v_{S}\}  is a commutative subalgebra which contradicts the maximality of  M M. Consequently,  ∇ M \nabla_{M}  is maximal. 
 
 
 Let  v S ′′ ∈ M 0 v_{S^{\prime\prime}}\in M_{0}. Then  v S ′′ ​ M 1 ⊂ M 1 v_{S^{\prime\prime}}M_{1}\subset M_{1}  as  M M  is a subalgebra in  G ⁡ ( n ) G(n). If  v S 0 ∈ M 1 v_{S_{0}}\in M_{1} , then  v S ′′ v_{S^{\prime\prime}}  and  v S 0 v_{S_{0}}  commute by ( 2 ). In particular, if  S ′′ ∩ S 0 = ∅ S^{\prime\prime}\cap S_{0}=\emptyset , then  v S ′′ ​ v S 0 = v S ′′ ∪ S 0 ≠ 0 v_{S^{\prime\prime}}v_{S_{0}}=v_{S^{\prime\prime}\cup S_{0}}\neq 0. Hence  S ′′ ∪ S 0 ∈ ∇ M S^{\prime\prime}\cup S_{0}\in\nabla_{M} , so  ∇ M \nabla_{M}  is algebraic. 
 
 
 The second statement is obvious. ∎

 Lemma 2. 
 
 The following conditions hold:
