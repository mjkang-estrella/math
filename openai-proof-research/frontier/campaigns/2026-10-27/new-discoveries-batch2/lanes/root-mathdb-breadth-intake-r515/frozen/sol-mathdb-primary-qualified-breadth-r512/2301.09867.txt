                                                                                               Abstract
                                                      Consider a distribution of pebbles on a graph. A pebbling move removes two pebbles from a
                                                 vertex and place one at an adjacent vertex. A vertex is reachable under a pebble distribution if it
                                                 has a pebble after the application of a sequence of pebbling moves. A pebble distribution is solvable
                                                 if each vertex is reachable under it. The size of a pebble distribution is the total number of pebbles.
                                                 The optimal pebbling number π ∗ (G) is the size of the smallest solvable distribution. A t-restricted
                                                 pebble distribution places at most t pebbles at each vertex. The t-restricted optimal pebbling number
                                                 πt∗ (G) is the size of the smallest solvable t-restricted pebble distribution. We show that deciding
                                                 whether π2∗ (G) ≤ k is NP-complete. We prove that πt∗ (G) = π ∗ (G) if δ(G) ≥ 2|V 3(G)| − 1 and we
                                                 show infinitely many graphs which satisfies δ(H) ≈ 21 |V (H)| but πt∗ (H) 6= π ∗ (H), where δ denotes
                                                 the minimum degree.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           Graph pebbling is a game on graphs initiated by a method of Saks and Lagarias to answer a number-
                                           theoretic question of Erdős and Lemke, which was successfully carried out by Chung in 1989[4]. Each
                                           graph in this paper is simple. We denote the vertex set and the edge set of graph G by V (G) and E(G),
                                           respectively. A pebble distribution D on graph G is a function mapping the vertex set to non-negative
                                           integers. We can imagine that each vertex v has D(v) pebbles. A pebbling move removes two pebbles
                                           from a vertex v (having at least two pebbles) and places one on an adjacent vertex u. We denote this
                                           move by (v → u).
                                               If σ is a sequence of pebbling moves, then let Dσ be the distribution which we obtain after the
                                           application of σ to distribtuion D. A sequence of pebbling moves is executable under D if for any τ
                                           prefix of σ Dτ is a pebble distribution. In other words, σ never removes two pebbles from a vertex
                                           which does not have at least two pebbles. A vertex v is k-reachable under a distribution D if there is an
                                           executable sequence of pebbling moves σ such that Dσ(v) ≥ k. When k = 1 we write reachable instead
                                           of 1-reachable. We say that a distribution D is solvable if each vertex of the graph is reachable under
                                           D.                                                                                               P
                                               Let |D| denotes the total number of pebbles placed on the graph by D, so |D| = v∈V (G) D(v).
                                           We call this quantity as the size of D. A pebble distribution D on a graph G is called optimal if it is
                                           solvable and its size is the smallest possible. This size is called the optimal pebbling number and denoted
                                           by π ∗ (G).
                                               The optimal pebbling number of several graph families are known. For example exact values were
                                           given for paths and cycles [6] [13], ladders [1], caterpillars [7], m-ary trees [8] and some staircase graphs[9].
                                           The values for graphs with diameter smaller than four are also characterized by some easily checkable
                                           domination conditions [12]. However, determining the optimal pebbling number for a given graph is
                                           NP-hard [10].

     In [2], Chelalli et al. introduced a new version of pebbling. In this setting, a pebble distribution is
t-restricted if no vertex has more than t pebbles. The t-restricted optimal pebbling number, denoted by
πt∗ , is the size of the solvable t-restricted distribution containing the least number of pebbles. It is easy
to see that π2∗ (G) ≥ πt∗ (G) ≥ πt+1∗

      A set S is a dominating set of graph G if S ⊆ V (G) and each vertex of G is contained in S or adjacent
to an element of S. The domination number γ(G) of graph G is the size of the smallest domination
set of G. A function f : V (G) → {0, 1, 2} is called as a Roman domination function if each v ∈ V (G)
for which f (v) = 0 there   P is a vertex u which is adjacent to v and f (u) = 2. The weight of a Roman
domination function is v∈V (G) f (v). The Roman domination number γR (G) is the minimum weight
of a Roman domination function.
      Chelalli et al. proved several bounds on these domination parameters by using the 2-restricted op-
timal pebbling number. They showed that π2∗ (G) ≤ γR (G) [2]. These domination parameters are well
studied and they are in the limelight. Computing the Roman domination number is a hard task. More
specifically, the decision version of the Roman domination number is NP-complete [5]
      Recently Shiue et al. in [3] defined a new version of pebbling called (d, t)-pebbling. In this new
model a pebble distribution D is called (d, t)-solvable if each vertex v can have t pebbles after a sequence
of pebbling moves which only remove pebbles from vertices whose distance from v is at most d. The

optimal (d, t)-pebbling number π(d,t)     (G) of G is the minimum size of a (d, t)-solvable distribution of G.

      Therefore the t-restricted optimal pebbling number is trapped between two graph parameters whose
calculation is an NP-hard task. Somebody may ask that what is the computational complexity of the
2-restricted optimal pebbling number?
      In Section 2 we show that deciding whether the t-restricted optimal pebbling number is at most k is
NP-complete for any t ≥ 2. Note that the t = 1 case is trivial because π1∗ (G) = |V (G)| for all graphs.
We prove that π ∗ (G) = π ∗ (G · Km ) = πt∗ (G · Km ) where · denotes the lexicographic graph product. We
use this result to give a reduction from optimal pebbling to t-restricted optimal pebbling.
      The authors of [2] asked the following interesting question: What graphs have the same 2-restricted
optimal pebbling number and optimal pebbling number? We can also ask what kind of properties implies
the equality of these two parameters. In Section 3 we investigate the role of the minimum degree in this
question and show some partial results.
      We prove that that if δ(G) ≥ 2|V 3(G)| − 1, where δ(G) denotes the minimum degree of G, then
π2∗ (G) = π ∗ (G). For any n which satifies n ≡ 1 mod 4 and n ≥ 9 we present an n-vertex graph H such
that δ(H) = |V (H)|−32    but π2∗ (H) 6= π ∗ (H).

such that for every vertex v which is not 2-reachable under D we have that D(v) = 0.

    We consider the following two decision problems:
    OPN: Given G, k: Is π ∗ (G) ≤ k?
    ROPN: Given G, t ≥ 2, k: Is πt∗ (G) ≤ k?
    Milans and Clark proved that OPN is NP-complete [10]. Using our previous theorem we are going
to show that ROPN is NP-hard. We do not give a precise proof that ROPN is in NP, since it is exactly
the same as the proof that OPN is in NP. In the next paragraph we give the sketch of the proof. If the
reader is interested in the details, then they can be found in [10].
    The witness for π ∗ (G) ≤ k is the following: A solvable 2-restricted pebble distribution D of size k
and for each vertex v a compact transcript of an executable sequence of pebbling moves σv , which satisfy
Dσv (v) ≥ 1. This transcription does not contain the order of the pebbling moves, it just contains the
total number of each possible moves. From this transcription it is possible to decide in polynomial time
whether a compatible executable sequence of pebbling moves exists or not.

iff πt∗ (f (G)) ≤ k. 

  Somebody may ask, that does high minimum degree guarantees that π2∗ = π ∗ ? The answer is yes.
We need a short lemma to prove this.

Lemma 3.1 Let G be a graph. If D is a pebble distribution on G such that D(v) = 3 for a particular
vertex v and D(u) ≤ 1 for any other vertex u, then D is not optimal.

Proof:
   To reach a vertex the third pebble of v is not needed. Any nonempty sequence of pebbling moves,
which can be applied to D, removes two pebbles from v in the first step. Then at each step only one
vertex has more than one pebble and every other vertex has at most as many pebbles as initially. When
the third pebble of v is used each vertex has at most as many pebbles as it has under D. Therefore the
subsequence whose first move removes the third pebble of v and contains all the later moves is executable
under D and reaches the same vertex as the original sequence of pebbling moves. But it removes only
two pebbles from v, so one useless pebble remains there. 
    This lemma immediately gives us a new class of graphs where equality holds between the two inves-
tigated parameters.

  1. Can π2∗ (T ) can be calculated in polynomial time if T is a tree?
  2. Can π ∗ (T ) can be calculated in polynomial time if T is a tree?
  3. What is the maximum c such that δ(G) ≥ c|V (G)| enforces π ∗ (G) = π2∗ (G)?
