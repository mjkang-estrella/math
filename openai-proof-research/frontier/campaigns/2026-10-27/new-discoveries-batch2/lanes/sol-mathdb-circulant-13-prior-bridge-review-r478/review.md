## Exact applicability chain

The target is only Holub–Kopriva Conjecture 11's missing lower bound: for even n >=142 with 12 not dividing n, no L(3,2,1) labeling f:Z_n->{0,...,11}. Source-v2 Proposition 5 gives lambda>=11 for n>=7; Theorem 10 gives lambda<=12 for even n>=142; Conjecture 11 states equality 12 when 12 does not divide n. Theorem 6 covers multiples of 12. No assertion here changes MathDB 372628 or Conjecture 20.

1. **Pull-back.** Fix m=5, rows i=0,...,4, and put g(i,j)=f(j+3i mod n) on P_5 square C_n. Cylinder distance is |i-i'|+delta_n(j,j'). For distinct vertices at distance d<=3 choose a signed shortest cyclic representative b for j'-j and a=i'-i. Then |a|+|b|=d, |b+3a|<=9<n. If b+3a=0, then 4|a|<=3, so a=b=0, contrary to distinctness. Thus the images are distinct. Each cylinder edge maps to an edge of C_n(1,3), so 1<=d_C<=d. Consequently |g(u)-g(v)|>=4-d_C>=4-d. Shortcuts in the circulant strengthen the needed inequality. Cyclic representative ties cannot occur for d<=3 and n>=142. This proof works for every m and every choice of consecutive rows; n>=142 is ample for all local bounds.

2. **Span and row location of zero.** Every row is the whole f in cyclic order, shifted by 3i, and hence has exactly the same label set, minimum and maximum. If f omitted 0 or 11, subtracting its minimum would give a labeling in 0,...,10, contradicting source Proposition 5. Equivalently KHS Proposition 1 (pp. 281–283) on a P_3 square P_6 patch supplies the same normalization. Thus every row contains zero and every restriction to consecutive rows still has span exactly 11. This is stronger than merely knowing that zero occurs somewhere on a cylinder boundary.

3. **All high adjacent differences excluded.** KHS Lemma 2 (pp. 287–288) applies to the entire m=5 cylinder and excludes adjacent 0,11. Lemma 3 (pp. 288–289) excludes adjacent 0,10; label inversion 11-g excludes adjacent 1,11. These are all possibilities for absolute adjacent difference >=10 in 0,...,11. Hence every adjacent pair has difference <=9, on boundary rows, interior rows, horizontal edges including the seam, and vertical edges. If applying Lemma 3 on three rows, any vertical adjacent pair fits in a three-row window and any horizontal pair fits in one containing its row; every such restriction still has span 11. 4. **Lemma 1 alternative checked.** Lemma 1 (p. 284, conclusion proved p. 287) is stronger than “6 divides n”: it says the entire four-row labeling is equivalent to horizontal repetition of A or B in Table 1 (p. 283). Both are explicit six-column arrays, so every row has period 6. Any adjacent pair in P_5 square C_n fits into four consecutive rows, and every such window has span 11. KHS equivalences (p. 281) preserve horizontal periods: inversion is injective on labels, cycle reflection sends p to -p, translation preserves p, and top/bottom reversal permutes rows.
