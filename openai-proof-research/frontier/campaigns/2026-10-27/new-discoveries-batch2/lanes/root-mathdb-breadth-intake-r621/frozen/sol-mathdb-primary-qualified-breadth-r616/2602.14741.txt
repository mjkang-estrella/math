Monotonicity of depth constants in general preferential attachment trees
Christian Mönch
August 24, 2026
Abstract
We study insertion depth and tree height in preferential-attachment (PA) trees driven by a general attachment function f:ℕ0→(0,∞).
Our main result is a monotonicity principle for the log⁡n-order constant of either functional: if f dominates g in “growth-ratio” order (a variant of the hazard ratio order of probability densities) then the typical depth constant for f is no larger than for g, i.e. stronger reinforcement yields a shallower tree.
The proofs use a one-parameter interpolation together with a gauge normalisation that removes a global derivative term, reducing the sign to a weighted correlation inequality for monotone sequences.
Equivalently, the results can be phrased as monotonicity theorems for the corresponding branching process functionals arising from the standard embedding of the PA tree into a Crump–Mode–Jagers process.
1 Introduction and main result
General preferential attachment trees
Fix an attachment function f:ℕ0→ℝ>0. We define a random rooted tree Tn​(f) on vertex set [n]={1,2,…,n} recursively as follows.
•
Initialisation: T1​(f) consists of a single vertex 1, the root.
•
Recursion: Given Tn−1​(f), obtain Tn​(f) by adding vertex n and connecting it by a single edge to a vertex i∈{1,…,n−1} chosen conditionally on Tn−1​(f) with probability
ℙ⁡(n→i∣Tn−1​(f))=f⁡(degTn−1​(f)⁡(i))∑j=1n−1f⁡(degTn−1​(f)⁡(j)).
Here degTn−1​(f)⁡(i) denotes the number of children of i∈[n−1] in the rooted tree Tn−1​(f) (i.e. the out-degree in the orientation away from the root). In particular, every new vertex is born with 0 children, so the value f⁡(0) governs the initial attractiveness of a newborn vertex. (If degTgr⁡(i) denotes the usual undirected graph degree, then degTgr(i)=degT(i)+𝟏{i≠1}.)
This model interpolates between uniform attachment (f≡1) and affine preferential attachment (f⁡(k)=k+δ), and was introduced in 2007 by Rudas, Tóth, and Valkó [26].
Preferential attachment trees (and, more generally, preferential attachment networks) are fundamental objects in random graph theory and have been studied since early work of Szymański [27], and especially following the Barabási–Albert paradigm [2, 1].
We study two logarithmic-order functionals: the insertion depth of the newest vertex and the height of the tree. For a rooted tree T on [n] let 0​p​tT​(v) denote the graph distance from the root to v, and set
Dn​(f):=0​p​tTn​(f)​(n),Hn​(f):=maxv∈[n]⁡0​p​tTn​(f)​(v).
Both insertion depth and height of random recursive trees are classical objects of study; see e.g. the survey by Drmota [16] for an overview. In particular, it is well known that for uniform attachment (f≡1) both Dn​(f) and Hn​(f) are of order log⁡n with explicit constants [13, 14, 25]. For general PA trees, logarithmic order of Dn​(f) under mild regularity conditions follows from the Crump–Mode–Jagers embedding [26], which can also be used to determine the logarithmic order of Hn​(f) in various regimes [25, 17, 3]. For an investigation of the insertion depth random recursive metric spaces that generalise PA trees see recent work of Desmarais [12].
In preferential attachment models which allow cycles, related functionals are the hop-count (corresponding to insertion depth) and the diameter (corresponding to height), which have been invesitgated for a variety of attachment regimes of both affine and general attachment functions [7, 15, 10, 11, 28].
Order of attachment functions and monotonicity of depth.
Throughout we consider nondecreasing attachment functions f,g:ℕ0→ℝ>0.
We say that f dominates g in growth-ratio order, and write
f⪰GRg, if
f⁡(k+1)f⁡(k)≥g⁡(k+1)g⁡(k)for all ​k∈ℕ0.
Equivalently, the ratio h⁡(k):=f⁡(k)/g⁡(k) is nondecreasing in k.
Intuitively, stronger reinforcement towards high-degree vertices should produce a shallower tree.
We propose growth ratio dominance (GRD) as a natural partial order under which this intuition can be made rigorous. Set
𝔼f​[Dn]:=𝔼⁡[Dn​(f)],𝔼f​[Hn]:=𝔼⁡[Hn​(f)].
Conjecture 1.1 (GRD monotonicity).
Let f,g:ℕ0→ℝ>0 be nondecreasing and assume f⪰GRg.
Then for all n≥1,
𝔼f​[Dn]≤𝔼g​[Dn], and ​𝔼f​[Hn]≤𝔼g​[Hn].
Here, we prove a asymptotic forms of Conjecture 1.1.
Under natural analytic regularity conditions collected below the limits
𝖼f:=limn→∞𝔼f​[Dn]log⁡n,𝖼f∗:=limn→∞𝔼f​[Hn]log⁡n
exists and admit representations as parameters of the Crump–Mode–Jagers branching process embedding of Tn​(f); see Section 2 for a brief exposition. Note that usually, the height limit can be extended to probabilistic convergence (in probability or almost surely) of the random variables Hn​(f)/log⁡n themselves.
For clarity we now collect all regularity hypotheses we need in our main results in two families:
•
Assumptions (A1)–(A7) are conditions on a single attachment function f.
•
Assumptions (B1)–(B3) are conditions on the multiplicative interpolation fθ between g and f ensuring differentiability and dominated-convergence interchanges.

To give the reader a better intuition for the regularity conditions, we now discuss some examples and non-examples.
Remark 1.5 (Examples for the regularity conditions).
The assumptions in Lemma 2.3 are standard in CMJ theory.
They are satisfied in essentially all “classical” preferential attachment regimes, but exclude certain
pathological or near-critical choices of f.
(i)
Bounded or sublinear attachment.
If f⁡(k)≍kα with 0≤α<1, then
∑i<n1/f⁡(i)≍n1−α, so the factors
An​(λ)=∏i=0n−1f⁡(i)f⁡(i)+λ decay super-exponentially in n.
In particular, mf​(λ)<∞ for all λ>0, and the derivative condition
0<−mf′​(λf)<∞ holds automatically.
(ii)
Linear attachment (the classical PA regime).
If f⁡(k)∼c​k (or f⁡(k)=k+δ), then ∑i<n1/f⁡(i)∼(1/c)​log⁡n, so
An(λ)≈n−λ/c.
Hence mf​(λ)<∞ iff λ/c>1, and for the unique solution λf of mf​(λf)=1 we have λf/c>1,
which implies ∑n(log⁡n)​An​(λf)<∞ and thus 0<−mf′​(λf)<∞.
(iii)
Too-fast growth.
If f⁡(k)≳k​log⁡k, then ∑i<n1/f⁡(i)≲log⁡log⁡n, so An​(λ) decays only like a power of log⁡n,
and in particular mf​(λ)=∞ for all λ>0 in many such cases. For even faster growth one may also violate non-explosion.
(iv)
Borderline near-critical cases.
It is possible to have mf​(λf)=1 but −mf′​(λf)=∞ when An​(λf) is barely summable,
e.g. An​(λf)≍1n​(log⁡n)2, for which ∑nAn​(λf)<∞ but
∑n(log⁡n)​An​(λf)=∞.
Such behaviour can be engineered by attachment functions that are asymptotically linear but tuned to be close to the critical boundary.
Proof strategy and organisation.
The arguments are organised as follows.
Section 2 introduces the standard Crump–Mode–Jagers (CMJ) embedding of Tn​(f) and
records the identification of the logarithmic depth and height constants with CMJ/BRW functionals in more detail.
Section 3 contains the proof of Theorem 1.2 (depth):
we interpolate between g and f, apply a gauge normalisation that removes a global derivative term,
and reduce the sign of Qθ′ to a weighted Chebyshev inequality for monotone sequences.
Section 4 contains the analogous argument for Theorem 1.3 (height),
based on the BRW speed functional κf and its optimiser λf∗. Appendix A is devoted to analytic identities and domination arguments used in the assumption packages.
2 CMJ embedding and parameter identification
This section records the standard continuous-time embedding of Tn​(f) into a supercritical
Crump–Mode–Jagers (CMJ) branching process, and the corresponding identification of the
logarithmic depth/height constants with CMJ/BRW parameters. All results stated here are
classical; for preferential-attachment trees in this generality see e.g. Rudas et al. [26] and the manuscript by Bhamidi [3],
and for CMJ background see the classical works by Jagers and Nerman [19, 24, 20] and the more recent survey by Janson and Holmgren [18].
Continuous-time embedding
Fix an attachment function f:ℕ0→(0,∞). Consider the continuous-time rooted tree process
(Υf​(t))t≥0 started from a single root at time 0, in which (conditionally on
Υf​(t)) each vertex x gives birth to a new child at instantaneous rate
f⁡(degΥf​(t)⁡(x)), independently across vertices. Let
σn:=inf{t≥0:|Υf​(t)|=n},n≥1,
be the nth birth time. This is the standard “competing exponentials” argument for continuous-time branching constructions [26, 18].
Lemma 2.1 (Embedding).
The discrete-time process (Υf​(σn))n≥1 has the same law as the preferential attachment
tree process (Tn​(f))n≥1.
Laplace transform and CMJ parameters
Fix f:ℕ0→(0,∞) and let (Xf​(t))t≥0 be the pure-birth process on ℤ≥0 started from
Xf​(0)=0, with jump rates k→k+1 at rate f⁡(k). Write τn for the time of the nth jump and
define the reproduction point process
ξf:=∑n≥1δτn.
In the CMJ tree (Υf​(t))t≥0 each vertex reproduces according to an independent copy of ξf,
with birth times measured relative to its own birth time. Thus (Υf​(t))t≥0 is a one-type CMJ
process with reproduction process ξf.
For λ>0 define the Laplace transform
mf​(λ):=𝔼⁡[∫[0,∞)e−λ​t​ξf​(𝑑t)]=𝔼⁡[∑n≥1e−λ​τn].
(2.1)
Since τn=∑i=0n−1Ei with independent Ei∼Exp⁡(f⁡(i)), one obtains the series form
mf​(λ)=∑n≥1∏i=0n−1f⁡(i)f⁡(i)+λ.
(2.2)
Where finite, λ↦mf​(λ) is strictly decreasing. In the supercritical Malthusian regime
(standard CMJ hypotheses) there is a unique λf>0 solving the Euler–Lotka equation
mf​(λf)=1,
called the Malthusian parameter [19].
Assuming differentiability at λf and finiteness of the derivative, set
μf:=−mf′​(λf)∈(0,∞),andQf:=λf​μf=−λf​mf′​(λf).
Remark 2.2.
In CMJ language, mf​(λ)=∫0∞e−λ​t​νf​(𝑑t) is the Laplace transform of the mean
reproduction measure νf​(d​t):=𝔼⁡[ξf​(d​t)], and μf=∫0∞t​e−λf​t​νf​(𝑑t) is the
“mean age at childbearing” under the Malthusian tilt.
Under standard CMJ regularity (in particular an x​log⁡x-type condition ensuring non-degeneracy of the
Malthusian martingale), the insertion depth of the nth individual is asymptotically logarithmic with
constant (λf​μf)−1.
We formalise the correspondence of the insertion depth constant with the CMJ parameters mentioned in the introduction in the following lemma, which also provides an explicit tail representation for the derivative −mf′​(λf) that is used in the proof of Theorem 1.2.
Lemma 2.3 (CMJ parameter identification for depth).
Assume that the attachment function f:ℕ0→(0,∞) is nonexplosive and Malthusian, and subject to the
x​log⁡x-type hypothesis ensuring convergence of the Malthusian martingale. Assume moreover that
0<−mf′​(λf)<∞,
where λf is the unique solution of mf​(λ)=1.
Then the limit
𝖼f:=limn→∞𝔼f​[Dn]log⁡n
exists and equals
𝖼f=1Qf,whereQf:=−λf​mf′​(λf).
Furthermore, the derivative admits the tail representation
−mf′​(λf)=∑i≥0ri(f)f⁡(i)+λf,ri(f):=∑n≥i+1∏k=0n−1f⁡(k)f⁡(k)+λf.
(2.3)
Remark 2.4.
The discrete PA law (and thus Qf) is invariant under scaling: if (c​f)​(k):=c​f​(k) with c>0, then
λc​f=c​λf and Qc​f=Qf. In particular, one may normalise to λf=1 without changing Qf.
We next recall the standard reduction of the CMJ height to an extremal problem in a branching random walk
(BRW). This viewpoint is classical for age-dependent branching processes [22, 4, 5, 18]. For more applications to increasing/PA-type trees, see e.g. Broutin and Devroye [9], Broutin et al. [8] and Janson [21].
Let Zf​(t):=|Υf​(t)| be the CMJ population size. Under the assumptions of Lemma 2.3, we have the almost sure martingale limit
e−λf​t​Zf​(t)⟶Wf,t→∞.
(2.4)
Recall the birth time of the nth individual

Then (2.4) yields the logarithmic size–time correspondence

and κf admits a variational representation in terms of mf, for the details see e.g. Biggins [4] or McDiarmid [23].

Assume the BRW regularity hypotheses ensuring (2.7). For λ>λf define

Then the leftmost-displacement speed κf

Moreover, in the regimes considered in Theorem 1.3 (e.g. the eventually regularly varying

Combining (2.6), (2.5), and the speed law (2.7) yields the height constant

In typical applications one first rescales f and g so that λf=λg=1 (Remark 2.4).

in Assumptions (B1) and (B2).

Lemma 3.2 (Reduction).
Assume (A1)–(A5), (B1), and (B2). If Qθ is nondecreasing in θ, then Qf≥Qg, and hence

Proof.
By construction, Q0=Qg and Q1=Qf. If Qθ is nondecreasing, then Qf≥Qg, for which it is sufficient that Qθ′≥0. ∎
It therefore remains to compute Qθ′ and show it is nonnegative. A direct differentiation produces

(cf. Remark 2.4), but eliminates the problematic global term in Qθ′.
3.2 Gauge normalisation and additional notation
A key technical point is that Qf is invariant under constant rescaling of f
(see Remark 2.4).
We exploit this freedom to choose a θ-dependent rescaling (a “gauge”) that removes the
global λθ′ contribution when differentiating Qθ.
Let fθ be the GRD interpolation (3.1) and let λθ be the unique solution of

Define the gauged family

By the scaling identity mc​f​(λ)=mf​(λ/c), this choice fixes the Malthusian parameter of fθ∗:
mfθ∗​(1)=mfθ​(λθ)=1,θ∈[0,1],
(3.2)
and, moreover, Qfθ∗=Qfθ by scaling invariance.
Product weights and tails.
For λ>0 define
An(θ,∗)(λ):=∏i=0n−1fθ∗​(i)fθ∗​(i)+λ,n≥0,(A0(θ,∗)(λ):=1),

ri(θ,∗)​(λ):=∑n≥i+1An(θ,∗)​(λ),i≥0.

(4.18). In the sublinear regularly varying regime, Lemma 4.7 verifies
(4.18) for all sufficiently large k (uniformly over compact λ–windows), which is the crucial reason for the regular variation assumption (A7).
This yields eventual monotonicity of k↦M~k(s)​(λ), which is sufficient for
the Chebyshev argument via Lemma 3.7. The exponential decay of weights
under regular variation ensures that the error from the finite non-monotone prefix is negligible.
