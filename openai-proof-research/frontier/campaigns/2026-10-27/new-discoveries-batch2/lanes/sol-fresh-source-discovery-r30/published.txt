     We call the graph property A hereditary if it is closed under taking induced subgraphs,
i.e., for any graphs G, H, if G ∈ A and H is an induced subgraph of G, then H ∈ A.
In contrast, replacing the condition ‘H is an induced subgraph of G’ by ‘G is an induced
subgraph of H’, we say that A is co-hereditary. It is obvious that A is co-hereditary if
and only if Ā is hereditary.
     For example, if A is the graph property of containing at least one edge, then Ā is the
graph property of containing no edges. We can see that A is co-hereditary and Ā is hered-

For the non-negative sequences, it is easy to see that these conditions become ever-stronger
starting from the second one (see [6]). Moreover, adding the condition of having no
internal zeros (that is, for any 0 ⩽ i < j < k ⩽ m, ai , ak > 0 =⇒ aj > 0), the log-
concavity implies the unimodality. A polynomial is called unimodal (resp. log-concave,
ordered log-concave, ultra log-concave) if the sequence of its coefficients is unimodal (resp.
log-concave, ordered log-concave, ultra log-concave).
    Let G be a graph and A a graph property. Recently, Makowsky and Rakita [19]
obtained the following results about PA (G, x):

   1. If A is co-hereditary, PA (G, x) is unimodal for almost every graph G ∈ G(n, p) for
      every edge probability p ∈ (0, 1).

   2. If A is hereditary with a graph G ∈ A which is neither a clique nor an edgeless
      graph, then PA (G, x) is real-rooted if and only if G ∈ A.

   It is a natural problem what we can say about the log-concavity or the real-rootedness
of PA (G, x) when A is co-hereditary. In this paper, we consider this problem on de-
pendence polynomials. As mentioned above, dependence polynomials and independence
polynomials are complementary, but for the problem of unimodality, log-concavity or

the electronic journal of combinatorics 33(4) (2026), #P4.9                                  3
real-rootedness, the independence polynomials are more difficult than the dependence
polynomials. The independence polynomials are not necessarily log-concave or unimodal
[2]. Even for bipartite graphs [7] and well-covered graphs [21], there are examples that
the independence polynomials are not unimodal. More recently, the examples of trees
with non-log-concave independence polynomials were constructed [17, 18]. For the real-
rootedness, only very few independence polynomials of graph classes can be proved having
only real roots, like claw-free graphs [12]. In contrast, for dependence polynomials, Hor-
rocks [15] showed that D(G, x) is log-concave for every graph G. However, the stronger
properties of dependence polynomials, like ordered log-concavity, ultra log-concavity and
real-rootedness, have never been studied.
    If G contains no edges, there are no dependent sets in G, i.e., D(G, x) ≡ 0. Thus, we
only consider the graphs which contains at least one edge in what follows.
    We denote the complete graph, the complete bipartite graph, the path and the cycle by
Kn , Km,n , Pn and Cn , respectively. In particular, K1,n is called a star. Let G and H be two
disjoint graphs. The union G∪H is the graph with the vertex set V (G∪H) = V (G)∪V (H)
and the edge set E(G ∪ H) = E(G) ∪ E(H). For a graph G and n ∈ N∗ , nG denotes
the disjoint union of n copies of G. For a graph H, a graph G is called H-free if no
induced subgraphs of G are isomorphic to H. About the ultra log-concavity of dependence
polynomials, we have
Theorem 2. Let G be a graph of order n containing at least one edge. If G is a K2 ∪2K1 -
free graph or has an independent set of size n−2, then the dependence polynomial D(G, x)
is ultra log-concave.
Remark 3. The complement of K2 ∪ 2K1 is K4 − e, which is called a diamond, so the
K2 ∪ 2K1 -free graphs are equivalent to the complements of the diamond-free graphs.
   Let G1 , G2 be the two graphs illustrated in Fig. 1. We give the characterization of
graphs with real-rooted dependence polynomials as follows:
Theorem 4. Let G be a graph of order n which contains at least one edge. The dependence
polynomial D(G, x) is real-rooted if and only if G ∈ {K2 ∪ (n − 2)K1 , P3 ∪ (n − 3)K1 , K3 ∪
(n − 3)K1 , 2K2 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , P4 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , C4 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , G1 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , G2 ∪ (n − 5)K1 }.

4    Conclusions and future work
In the present paper, we proved that, for a graph G of order n, the dependence polynomial
D(G, x) is ultra log-concave if G is a K2 ∪ 2K1 -free graph or has an independent set of
size n − 2. And further, the graphs of order n with real-rooted dependence polynomials
are characterized, which is exactly one of the following eight graphs: K2 ∪ (n − 2)K1 ,
P3 ∪ (n − 3)K1 , K3 ∪ (n − 3)K1 , 2K2 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , P4 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , C4 ∪ (n − 4)K1 ,
G1 ∪ (n − 4)K1 , G2 ∪ (n − 5)K1 .
    Combined with the proof of Theorem 2, we obtain that, for a graph G, D(G, x) is
ultra log-concave if and only if det(∇2 qk ) ⩽ 0. Observing (6), (7) and (8), we notice that
all the coefficients of the quadratic form (7) is nonpositive except the one of t1 t8 . Thus,
if det(∇2 qk ) > 0, ti (i = 2, 3, · · · , 7) must be much less than t1 and t8 . We believe that
there are no graphs satisfying such condition, so we conjecture:
Conjecture 14. For every graph G, D(G, x) is ultra log-concave.
   For the graph polynomials associated with co-hereditary graph properties, Makowsky
and Rakita [19] showed that these polynomials are unimodal for almost all graphs. Mean-
while, they asked: Under what conditions can almost unimodality be improved to uni-
modality or log-concavity?

A}. Since A is co-hereditary, it is easy to see that ([n], I) is an independence system.

     More abstractly, for l ∈ N∗ , we call an independence system M := ([n], I) satisfying
(∗) an l-matroid. By this definition, we can see that if M is an l1 -matroid, M is also an
l2 -matroid for all l2 > l1 . In particular, 1-matroids are commonly referred to as matroids.
For matroids, the following famous conjecture was posed by Mason:

Conjecture 16 (Mason’s Conjecture). [20] Let M be an n-element matroid and ik the
number of independent sets of size k. Then

  (i) the sequence {ik }nk=0 is log-concave;

 (ii) the sequence {ik }nk=0 is ordered log-concave;

 (iii) the sequence {ik }nk=0 is ultra log-concave.

   Adiprasito, Huh, and Katz [1] proved (i) using techniques from Hodge theory and
algebraic geometry in 2018. (ii) was proved by Huh, Schröter and Wang [16] in 2022.
More recently, (iii) was proved by Anari et al. [3], Brändén and Huh [9], and Chan and
Pak [11] in different methods independently. Motivated by the discussions above, our
question is: under what conditions can the analogous version of Mason’s Conjecture hold
true for l-matroids? By Proposition 15, it is a generalization of Makowsky and Rakita’s
question. For the case of l = 2, we conjecture:

Conjecture 17. Let M be an n-element 2-matroid, ik the number of independent sets
of size k in M. Then

  (i) the sequence {ik }nk=0 is log-concave;

 (ii) the sequence {ik }nk=0 is ordered log-concave;

 (iii) the sequence {ik }nk=0 is ultra log-concave.
