Abstract.
The classical bond lattice of a graph was used in a formula of Whitney to compute the chromatic polynomial. In this paper, we use weighted versions of the bond lattice, to introduce and study a new polynomial and a new symmetric function invariant of a graph. Examples of the new polynomial invariant include the classical Narayana polynomials (for the path graph),
the tree-Eulerian polynomials (for the complete graph), and the binomial-Eulerian polynomials (for the star graph). These examples suggest an interesting connection to
hh-polynomials of general graph-associahedra. We prove that for any chordal graph, our polynomial graph invariant is γ\gamma-positive.
Multiweighted bond posets yield a symmetric function graph invariant that is an analog of the chromatic polynomial. The highest degree homogeneous component of this symmetric function is of particular interest. The parking function symmetric function introduced by Haiman arises as an example, as do symmetric functions studied by the first author in connection with multibracketed Lie algebras and with colored exterior algebras. The γ\gamma-positivity result mentioned above is a specialization of an ee-positivity result for the highest degree homogeneous component, which we prove for any chordal graph using the theory of lexicographic shellability. We conjecture that this symmetric function is Schur-log-concave, which specializes to Huh’s log-concavity theorem for the chromatic polynomial.
Key words and phrases: weighted partitions, bond lattice, chromatic polynomial, graph associahedra, gamma-positivity and unimodality, symmetric functions, lexicographic shellability

In [34] Postnikov, Reiner, and Williams establish the important γ\gamma-positivity property for hAG​(t)h_{\mathcal{\mathcal{}}A_{G}}(t) when GG is a chordal graph in order to confirm, for this particular class of polytopes, a well known conjecture of Gal [17], which asserts that every simple flag polytope is γ\gamma-positive.
The γ\gamma-positivity property implies unimodality (and palindromicity), which was established by Stanley [40] for all convex polytopes. In this paper we prove the analogous result for μG​(t)\mu_{G}(t).
Theorem 1.2.
If GG is a chordal graph with nn vertices and kk connected components then
(−1)n−k​μG​(t)(-1)^{n-k}\mu_{G}(t) is γ\gamma-positive.
Our proof of Theorem 1.2 relies on an EL-labeling of the weighted bond poset of a chordal graph and the theory of lexicographic shellability.
Previously this technique was used in [37, 32, 23] to establish γ\gamma-positivity of variations and generalizations of (−1)n​∑j=0nμ𝒲​ℬn​(0^,[n]j)​tj(-1)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mu_{\mathcal{WB}_{n}}(\hat{0},[n]^{j})t^{j} and in [20] to establish γ\gamma-positivity of (−1)n−1​μKn​(t)(-1)^{n-1}\mu_{K_{n}}(t).
Our results hold in greater generality. In [22] González D’León considers a multiweighted generalization of the weighted partition poset 𝒲​Πn\mathcal{W}\Pi_{n} in order to study the multilinear component of the free Lie algebra with more than two compatible brackets. In [23] he also introduces a multiweighted generalization of a variant of the weighted Boolean algebra 𝒲​ℬn\mathcal{WB}_{n} in order to study the multilinear component of colored exterior algebras. In this paper we consider an analogous multiweighted generalization of the weighted bond posets 𝒲​ΠG\mathcal{W}\Pi_{G}. This yields a symmetric function generalization MG​(𝐱)M_{G}(\mathbf{x}) of the Möbius polynomial μG​(t)\mu_{G}(t), which we call the Möbius symmetric function of GG. Indeed, the specialization MG​(𝐱)​(1,t,0,…)M_{G}(\mathbf{x})(1,t,0,\dots) yields μG​(t)\mu_{G}(t). The specialization MG​(𝐱)​(1,0,0,…)=μG​(0)M_{G}(\mathbf{x})(1,0,0,\dots)=\mu_{G}(0) yields the Möbius invariant μ⁡(ΠG)\mu(\Pi_{G}).
When GG is the complete graph KnK_{n}, we have that (−1)n−1​MG​(𝐱)(-1)^{n-1}M_{G}(\mathbf{x}) is equal to the symmetric function studied in [22], whose coefficients give the dimensions of components of the free Lie algebra with multiple compatible brackets. When GG is the star graph with nn vertices, (−1)n−1​MG​(𝐱)(-1)^{n-1}M_{G}(\mathbf{x}) is equal to the symmetric function studied in [23], whose coefficients give the dimensions of components of the colored exterior algebra.
We show that when GG is equal to the path graph with nn vertices, (−1)n−1​ω​MG​(𝐱)(-1)^{n-1}\omega M_{G}(\mathbf{x}) is the parking function symmetric function of Haiman [28], where ω\omega is the standard involution on the ring of symmetric functions taking the complete homogeneous symmetric function hnh_{n} to the elementary symmetric function ene_{n}.
We also obtain a multiweighted generalization of Theorem 1.2 whose proof relies on an EL-labeling of the multiweighted bond poset of a chordal graph.
Theorem 1.3.
If GG is a chordal graph with nn vertices and kk connected components then the symmetric function
(−1)n−k​MG​(𝐱)(-1)^{n-k}M_{G}(\mathbf{x}) is ee-positive, in the sense that when expanded in the basis of elementary symmetric functions {eλ:λ⊢n−1}\{e_{\lambda}:\lambda\vdash n-1\}, the coefficients are nonnegative.
We conjecture that Theorem 1.3 is true for all graphs.
A symmetric function generalization of the chromatic polynomial can also be obtained from the multiweighted bond posets. The maximal degree component of this symmetric function is MG​(𝐱)M_{G}(\mathbf{x}). For chordal graphs GG, we establish a property that we call alternating ee-positivity and we conjecture that it holds for all graphs.
We also present a conjecture asserting that this symmetric function has a property that we call Schur-log-concavity. This conjecture generalizes Huh’s celebrated log-concavity result for the chromatic polynomial [31].
The paper is organized as follows. Sections 2 - 4 deal with the Möbius polynomial μG​(t)\mu_{G}(t) and Sections 5 -  7 deal with the symmetric function generalizations of the Möbius polynomial and the chromatic polynomial.
In Section 2, we present the definitions and basic properties of the weighted bond lattice and Möbius polynomial of a graph.
In Section 3, we present recurrence relations which enable us to show that (−1)n−1​μG​(t)(-1)^{n-1}\mu_{G}(t) is the tree-Eulerian polynomial when GG is the complete graph, the Narayana polynomial when GG is the path graph, and the binomial-Eulerian polynomial when GG is the star graph. We also discuss in Section 3 the connection with graph associahedra.
In Section 4, we present γ\gamma-positivity results for Möbius polynomials of chordal graphs, which are analogous to those of Postnikov, Reiner and Williams [34] for hh-polynomials of graph associahedra of chordal graphs. Proofs of some of the results in Sections 3 and 4 are deferred to Sections 5 and 6
where more general results are proved.
In Section 5, we introduce the multiweighted generalization of the weighted bond poset and establish recurrence relations for the Möbius symmetric functions, which enable us to derive the relationship between the Möbius symmetric function of the path graph and the parking function symmetric function. Section 6 deals with EL-shellability of the multiweighted bond poset and its ee-positivity implications, which include Theorem 1.3. The symmetric function generalization of the chromatic polynomial is discussed in Section 7.

A poset is said to be pure (or graded) if all its maximal chains have the same length, where the length of a chain is one less than the number of elements in the chain. The length ℓ⁡(P)\ell(P) of a pure poset PP is the common length of its maximal chains.
The bond lattice ΠG\Pi_{G} of any connected graph GG is pure and bounded, while the weighted bond poset 𝒲​ΠG\mathcal{W}\Pi_{G} is pure but not bounded even when GG is connected. Indeed if V={v1,v2,…,vn}V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\} then

and if GG is connected, 𝒲​ΠG\mathcal{W}\Pi_{G} it has nn

but no maximum element unless n=1n=1. If G=(V,E)G=(V,E) has connected components Gi=(Vi,Ei)G_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i}), where i∈[k]i\in[k], then
the maximal elements of 𝒲​ΠG\mathcal{W}\Pi_{G} are all the weighted partitions whose underlying partition is
{V1,…,Vk}\{V_{1},\dots,V_{k}\}. Note that
ℓ⁡(𝒲​ΠG)=ℓ⁡(ΠG)=|V|−k.\ell(\mathcal{W}\Pi_{G})=\ell(\Pi_{G})=|V|-k.

Proposition 2.4.

(2)

4. γ\gamma-positivity for chordal graphs

A graph is said to be chordal if it does not have any induced cycles of length greater than 33. Chordal graphs are exactly those that admit a (reverse) perfect elimination order (PEO), i.e., a total order of the vertices v1,v2,…,vnv_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n} such that for every ii the set of neighbors vjv_{j} of viv_{i}, such that j<ij<i, form a clique, see [34, Section 9.2].

Note that every chordal graph is isomorphic to a perfectly labeled chordal graph. The complete graph on [n][n] is a perfectly labeled chordal graph, as is every increasing tree on [n][n], where a tree on [n][n] is increasing if when rooted at 11, every vertex is smaller than its children.

5. Multiweighted bond posets and Möbius symmetric functions
In [22] González D’León considers a generalization of the weighted partition poset based on the observation that a weighted block BuB^{u} of a weighted partition can be written as B(u,|B|−1−u)B^{(u,|B|-1-u)} thereby using weights that are pairs of nonnegative integers rather than single nonnegative integers. To merge two bi-weighted blocks, B1(μ⁡(1),μ⁡(2))B_{1}^{(\mu(1),\mu(2))} and B2(ν⁡(1),ν⁡(2))B_{2}^{(\nu(1),\nu(2))}, now means to form either the bi-weighted block
(B1∪B2)(μ⁡(1)+ν⁡(1)+1,μ⁡(2)+ν⁡(2))(B_{1}\cup B_{2})^{(\mu(1)+\nu(1)+1,\mu(2)+\nu(2))} or the biweighted block (B1∪B2)(μ⁡(1)+ν⁡(1),μ⁡(2)+ν⁡(2)+1)(B_{1}\cup B_{2})^{(\mu(1)+\nu(1),\mu(2)+\nu(2)+1)}. By using weights that are rr-tuples instead of pairs, one gets the more general rr-weighted partition poset studied in [22].
In this section we generalize the notion of weighted bond posets to rr-weighted bond posets. This leads to a symmetric function generalization of the Möbius polynomial μG​(t)\mu_{G}(t) and symmetric function generalizations of the results for μG​(t)\mu_{G}(t) discussed in earlier sections.

6. EL-shellability, ee-positivity, and γ\gamma-positivity
In this section, we prove ee-positivity results for the Möbius symmetric functions. These results reduce to the γ\gamma-positivity results for the Möbius polynomials discussed in Section 4.2. Our proofs rely on the theory of lexicographic shellability, which was first introduced by Björner in [9] and further devoloped by Björner and Wachs in [6, 7, 4, 5].
We briefly review the basics in Section 6.1. See [46] for further information on this topic.
6.1. Review: EL labeling of the multiweighted partition poset
An edge-labeling of a finite bounded poset PP is a function λ:ℰ⁡(P)→Λ\lambda:\mathcal{E}(P)\rightarrow\Lambda from the set ℰ⁡(P)\mathcal{E}(P) of cover relations of PP to some other poset Λ\Lambda. An edge labeling λ:ℰ⁡(P)→Λ\lambda:\mathcal{E}(P)\rightarrow\Lambda of PP is said to be an EL-labeling if it
satisfies the following conditions:
(1) 
In every closed interval [x,y][x,y] of PP there is a unique (strictly) increasing maximal chain, i.e., a maximal chain 𝐜:=(x=x0⋖x1⋖⋯⋖xℓ=y)\mathbf{c}:=(x=x_{0}\lessdot x_{1}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot x_{\ell}=y) such that
λ⁡(x0⋖x1)<λ⁡(x1⋖x2)<⋯<λ⁡(xℓ−1⋖xℓ).\lambda(x_{0}\lessdot x_{1})<\lambda(x_{1}\lessdot x_{2})<\cdots<\lambda(x_{\ell-1}\lessdot x_{\ell}).
(2) 
The word of labels
λ(𝐜):=λ(x0⋖x1)λ(x1⋖x2)⋯λ(xℓ−1⋖xℓ)\lambda(\mathbf{c}):=\lambda(x_{0}\lessdot x_{1})\lambda(x_{1}\lessdot x_{2})\cdots\lambda(x_{\ell-1}\lessdot x_{\ell})
associated to the
unique increasing maximal chain 𝐜\mathbf{c} comes, in the lexicographic order, before the word of labels of any other maximal chain in the interval.
If PP admits an EL-labeling, we say that it is edge lexicographically shellable (EL-shellable). Note that the EL-labeling restricts to an EL-labeling of every closed interval of PP. A maximal chain x0⋖x1⋖⋯⋖xℓx_{0}\lessdot x_{1}\lessdot\cdots\lessdot x_{\ell} of a closed interval of PP is said to be ascent-free if its word of labels has no ascents, that is, for all i∈[ℓ−1]i\in[\ell-1] we have λ⁡(xi−1⋖xi)≮λ⁡(xi⋖xi+1)\lambda(x_{i-1}\lessdot x_{i})\not<\lambda(x_{i}\lessdot x_{i+1}).

EL-shellability has strong topological and algebraic implications for the order complex Δ⁡(x,y)\Delta(x,y) of each open interval (x,y)(x,y) of the poset.

6.3. Generalization to chordal graphs
In this section, we generalize the results of the previous section to multiweighted bond posets of chordal graphs.
Recall the definitions of PEO and perfectly labeled chordal graph from Section 4.
The following observation gives an equivalent definition of a PEO.
Proposition 6.8.
An ordering v1,v2,⋯,vnv_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n} of the vertices of a graph GG is a PEO if and only if whenever i<j<ki<j<k, and {vi,vk}\{v_{i},v_{k}\} and {vj,vk}\{v_{j},v_{k}\} are edges of GG then {vi,vj}\{v_{i},v_{j}\} must also be an edge of GG, i.e., there are no induced subgraphs that are paths of the form vi−vk−vjv_{i}-v_{k}-v_{j}.
Corollary 6.9.
Let GG be a perfectly labeled chordal graph and let HH be an induced subgraph. Then HH is a perfectly labeled chordal graph.
Let GG be a graph on vertex set {v1<⋯<vn}⊆ℤ>0\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}. We say that a sequence vj1,vj2,…,vjmv_{j_{1}},v_{j_{2}},\dots,v_{j_{m}} is an increasing path from vj1v_{j_{1}} to vjmv_{j_{m}} if j1<j2<⋯<jmj_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{m} and {vji,vji+1}\{v_{j_{i}},v_{j_{i+1}}\} is an edge of GG for all i∈[m−1]i\in[m-1].
Lemma 6.10.
Let GG be a connected perfectly labeled chordal graph on vertex set {v1<⋯<vn}⊆ℤ>0\{v_{1}<\dots<v_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}. Then for any j∈[n]j\in[n] there is an increasing path from v1v_{1} to vjv_{j}.
Proof.
Using the fact that any induced subgraph of a perfectly labeled chordal graph is a perfectly labeled chordal graph (Corollary 6.9), we will proceed by induction on the number nn of vertices of GG. In graphs with one vertex the statement is trivial, so we assume that the lemma is true for any graph with less than nn vertices and let GG be a graph with nn vertices. Suppose there are no increasing paths from v1v_{1} to vjv_{j}. Note then in particular, j>1j>1 and {v1,vj}\{v_{1},v_{j}\} cannot be an edge of GG. If the graph G−{vj}G-\{v_{j}\} has more than one component, we can consider the induced subgraph formed by vjv_{j} and the component of G−{vj}G-\{v_{j}\} that contains v1v_{1}. Since this is a connected graph with less than nn vertices, we will find an increasing path from v1v_{1} to vjv_{j}, which gives a contradiction. This implies that G−{vj}G-\{v_{j}\} has to be connected.
Let vkv_{k} be the neighbor of vjv_{j} in GG with kk as small as possible. We know that k>1k>1 but it must happen that k>jk>j, otherwise if k<jk<j we could find an increasing path pp from v1v_{1} to vkv_{k} in G−{vj}G-\{v_{j}\} and then pp concatenated with vjv_{j} is an increasing path from v1v_{1} to vjv_{j} in GG. So let now pp be an increasing path in G−{vj}G-\{v_{j}\} from v1v_{1} to vkv_{k} and let {vi,vk}\{v_{i},v_{k}\} be its last edge. Since pp is increasing i<ki<k.
Taking into account the minimality of kk, the vertices vi,vj,vkv_{i},v_{j},v_{k} provide a violation to the condition of a PEO given in Proposition 6.8, which gives us a contradiction. Hence there must be an increasing path from v1v_{1} to vjv_{j} for all j∈[n]j\in[n].
∎
We will need the following lemma to prove that rr-weighted bond posets of chordal graphs are EL-shellable.
Lemma 6.11.
Let GG be a connected perfectly labeled chordal graph on [n][n]. Let B1,B2,…,BkB_{1},B_{2},\dots,B_{k} be a collection of disjoint subsets of [n][n] such that
• 
min⁡B1<min⁡B2<⋯<min⁡Bk\min B_{1}<\min B_{2}<\cdots<\min B_{k}
• 
G|BjG|_{B_{j}} is connected for all jj
•

Then for every s≤ks\leq k, the subgraph G|B1∪B2∪⋯∪BsG|_{B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup\cdots\cup B_{s}} is connected.

We proceed by induction on ss. If s=1s=1 we have from the hypothesis that G|B1G|_{B_{1}} is connected; so let us assume that G|B1∪B2∪⋯∪Bs−1G|_{B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup\cdots\cup B_{s-1}} is connected. Since G|B1∪B2∪⋯∪BkG|_{B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup\cdots\cup B_{k}} is connected we know from Corollary 6.9 and Lemma 6.10 that there is an increasing path pp from min⁡B1\min B_{1} to min⁡Bs\min B_{s}. Let {j,min⁡Bs}\{j,\min B_{s}\} be the last edge of pp. Since pp is increasing we have that j<min⁡Bsj<\min B_{s} and hence, by the fact that the BiB_{i}’s are listed in increasing order of their minimums, we have that j∈B1∪⋯∪Bs−1j\in B_{1}\cup\cdots\cup B_{s-1}. Corollary 6.13.
If GG is a chordal graph then all closed intervals of ΠGr\Pi^{r}_{G} are EL-shellable for all r∈ℤ>0∪{∞}r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}.
Proof.

∎

6.5. Topological consequences
Although not needed in the rest of the paper, we discuss some topological consequences of the EL-labeling of Theorem 6.12. First we recall the well-known Philip Hall’s theorem stating that given any open interval (x,y)(x,y) of a pure bounded finite poset PP, the value of the
Möbius function μP​(x,y)\mu_{P}(x,y) is equal to the reduced Euler characteristic of the order complex Δ⁡(x,y)\Delta(x,y) of the interval (x,y)(x,y), where the order complex of a poset is the simplicial complex whose faces are the chains of the poset.
An EL-labeling has the following further topological and homological implications for the order complex Δ⁡(x,y)\Delta(x,y).
Theorem 6.27 (Björner [9], Björner and Wachs [4, Theorem 5.9], cf. Wachs [46, Theorem 3.2.4]).
Let λ\lambda be an EL-labeling of a pure bounded finite poset PP. Then for each open interval (x,y)(x,y) of PP we have that,
(1) 
the order complex Δ⁡(x,y)\Delta(x,y) has the homotopy type of a wedge of
|{𝐜∣𝐜​ is an ascent-free maximal chain of ​[x,y]}||\{\mathbf{c}\mid\mathbf{c}\text{ is an ascent-free maximal chain of }[x,y]\}|
ℓ⁡(x,y)\ell(x,y)-spheres, and hence the poset PP is Cohen-Macaulay, i.e., H~d​(Δ⁡(x,y))=0\widetilde{H}^{d}(\Delta(x,y))=0 for every d≠ℓ⁡(x,y)d\neq\ell(x,y);
(2) 
the set
{𝐜¯∣𝐜​ is an ascent-free maximal chain of ​[x,y]}\{\bar{\mathbf{c}}\mid\mathbf{c}\text{ is an ascent-free maximal chain of }[x,y]\}
forms a basis for the reduced cohomology H~ℓ⁡(x,y)​(Δ​(x,y))\widetilde{H}^{\ell(x,y)}(\Delta(x,y)).
The following are therefore topological consequences of Theorems 6.12 and 6.15. These consequences were proved in [22] (r=2r=2 case in [20]) for G=KnG=K_{n}.
Theorem 6.28.
Let GG be a connected chordal graph on [n][n] and let r∈ℤ>0∪{∞}r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}. Then ΠGr^\widehat{\Pi^{r}_{G}} is Cohen-Macaulay.
Theorem 6.29.
Let GG be a connected perfectly labeled chordal graph on [n][n] and let ν∈WCompn−1,r\nu\in{\rm WComp}_{n-1,r}, where r∈ℤ>0∪{∞}r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}. Then the set
{c¯​(𝐓,ρT)∣𝐓∈ℒ​y​nG,ν},\{\bar{c}(\mathbf{T},\rho_{T})\mid\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{L}yn_{G,\nu}\},
where c¯​(𝐓,ρT):=c⁡(𝐓,ρT)∖{0^,[n]ν}\bar{c}(\mathbf{T},\rho_{T}):=c(\mathbf{T},\rho_{T})\setminus\{\hat{0},[n]^{\nu}\} and ρT\rho_{T} is any linear extension of TT, is a basis for top cohomology H~n−3​(Δ⁡(0^,[n]ν))\widetilde{H}^{n-3}(\Delta(\hat{0},[n]^{\nu})) of the order complex Δ⁡(0^,[n]ν)\Delta(\hat{0},[n]^{\nu}) of the open interval (0^,[n]ν)(\hat{0},[n]^{\nu}) of ΠGr\Pi^{r}_{G}.
Proof.
It follows from Theorems 6.27 and 6.15 that
{c¯​(𝐓,τT)∣𝐓∈ℒ​y​nG,ν},\{\bar{c}(\mathbf{T},\tau_{T})\mid\mathbf{T}\in\mathcal{L}yn_{G,\nu}\},
where τT\tau_{T} is the decreasing-minimum linear extension, is a basis for top cohomology of the open interval (0^,[n]ν)(\hat{0},[n]^{\nu}). It follows that the decreasing-minimum linear extension τT\tau_{T} can be replaced by any linear extension ρT\rho_{T}.
∎
7. A symmetric function analog of the chromatic polynomial
We consider a symmetric function analog of the characteristic polynomial of a bond lattice. Thanks to Whitney’s formula in Equation (1.4), this is a symmetric function analog of the chromatic polynomial of a graph, which is different from Stanley’s well-known chromatic symmetric function [44].
7.1. Preliminaries
Given a pure (or ranked) poset PP, the characteristic polynomial of a pure poset PP with a minimum element 0^\hat{0} is defined as
chP​(t):=∑x∈PμP​(0^,x)​tρ⁡(x),{\rm ch}_{P}(t):=\sum_{x\in P}\mu_{P}(\hat{0},x)t^{\rho(x)},
where ρ⁡(x)\rho(x) is the rank of xx, i.e., the length of the interval [0^,x][\hat{0},x].
For any graph GG, let
(7.1)
ΨG​(𝐱)\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x})
=∑𝝅∈ΠG∞μΠG∞​(0^,𝝅)​𝐱w⁡(𝝅)\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Pi_{G}^{\infty}}\mu_{\Pi_{G}^{\infty}}(\hat{0},\boldsymbol{\pi})\mathbf{x}^{w(\boldsymbol{\pi})}
Equivalently,
ΨG​(𝐱)\displaystyle\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x})
=∑π∈ΠGMG|π​(𝐱)\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{G}}M_{G|_{\pi}}(\mathbf{x})
where G|πG|_{\pi} is the subgraph of GG whose connected components are of the form G|BG|_{B} where B∈πB\in\pi. For example,
(7.2)
ΨP3​(𝐱)\displaystyle\Psi_{P_{3}}(\mathbf{x})
=1−2​e1​(𝐱)+e1,1​(𝐱)+e2​(𝐱)\displaystyle=1-2e_{1}(\mathbf{x})+e_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})+e_{2}(\mathbf{x})
The specialization
ΨG​(t,t,…,t⏞r,0,0,…)\Psi_{G}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\overbrace{t,t,\dots,t}}},0,0,\dots) is equal to the characteristic polynomial of the rr-weighted bond poset ΠGr\Pi^{r}_{G}.
By (1.4), the chromatic polynomial χG​(t)\chi_{G}(t) is obtained from ΨG​(t,0,0,…)\Psi_{G}(t,0,0,\dots) by reversing its coefficients. That is
ΨG​(t,0,0,…)=tn​χG​(t−1),\Psi_{G}(t,0,0,\dots)=t^{n}\chi_{G}(t^{-1}),
where nn is the number of vertices of GG. Thus we may think of ΨG​(𝐱)\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x}) as a symmetric function analog of the chromatic polynomial.
There is a well-known classical formula for the characteristic polynomial of ΠKn=Πn\Pi_{K_{n}}=\Pi_{n} given by,
(7.3)
ΨKn(t,0,0,0,…)=(t−1)(t−2)⋯(t−n+1).\Psi_{K_{n}}(t,0,0,0,\dots)=(t-1)(t-2)\cdots(t-n+1).
The formula for the characteristic polynomial of ΠKn2=𝒲​ΠKn\Pi^{2}_{K_{n}}=\mathcal{W}\Pi_{K_{n}} was given in [20, Theorem 2.8],
(7.4)
ΨKn​(t,t,0,0,…)=(t−n)n−1.\Psi_{K_{n}}(t,t,0,0,\dots)=(t-n)^{n-1}.
Question 7.1.
Are there nice formulas for ΨKn​(t,t,…,t⏞r,0,0,…)\Psi_{K_{n}}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle r}}{{\overbrace{t,t,\dots,t}}},0,0,\dots) when r>2r>2?
7.2. Alternating ee-positivity and Schur-log-concavity
The next theorem generalizes, in the case of chordal graphs, the fact that the characteristic polynomials of bond lattices alternate in sign. Given a symmetric function f⁡(𝐱)f(\mathbf{x}), let f⁡(𝐱)|deg⁡df(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d} denote the degree dd homogeneous component of f⁡(𝐱)f(\mathbf{x}). We say that f⁡(𝐱)f(\mathbf{x}) is alternating ee-positive if (−1)d​f​(𝐱)|deg⁡d(-1)^{d}f(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d} is ee-positive for all d≥0d\geq 0.
Theorem 7.2.
If GG is a chordal graph then ΨG​(𝐱)\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x}) is alternating ee-positive.
Proof.
Let nn be the number of vertices of GG.
Note that G|πG|_{\pi} has nn vertices and |π||\pi| connected components for all π∈ΠG\pi\in\Pi_{G}.
Since MG|π​(𝐱)M_{G|_{\pi}}(\mathbf{x}) is homogeneous of degree n−|π|n-|\pi|, for all dd we have
ΨG​(𝐱)|deg⁡d=∑π∈ΠG|π|=n−dMG|π​(𝐱).\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\pi\in\Pi_{G}\\
|\pi|=n-d\end{subarray}}M_{G|_{\pi}}(\mathbf{x}).
Since GG is chordal, each G|πG|_{\pi} is chordal. We can therefore apply Corollary 6.19 to each
G|πG|_{\pi}. Thus, if |π|=n−d|\pi|=n-d then (−1)d​MG|π​(𝐱)(-1)^{d}M_{G|_{\pi}}(\mathbf{x}) is ee-positive. It follows that each (−1)d​ΨG​(𝐱)|deg⁡d(-1)^{d}\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d} is ee-positive giving the desired result.
∎
A similar argument shows that Conjecture 6.21 (1) is equivalent to the conjecture that Theorem 7.2 is true for any graph.
A polynomial ∑j=0daj​tj∈ℝ⁡[t]\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}t^{j}\in\mathbb{R}[t] is said to be log-concave if for all i∈[d−1]i\in[d-1],
(7.5)
ai2≥ai−1​ai+1.a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}.
For sequences of positive real numbers, log-concavity implies unimodality (see [10, Lemma 1.1]).
A longstanding conjecture of Hoggar [29], proved in 2012 by Huh [30], asserts
that for any graph GG, the chromatic polynomial χG​(t)\chi_{G}(t) is log-concave. Since the coefficients of the chromatic polynomial alternate in sign, Huh’s result also settles a conjecture of Read [36] stating that (−1)n​χG​(−t)(-1)^{n}\chi_{G}(-t) is unimodal.
Note that log-concavity of χG​(t)\chi_{G}(t) is equivalent to log-concavity of ΨG​(t,0,0,…)\Psi_{G}(t,0,0,\dots) since the sequences of coefficients are the reverse of each other. This leads to the following conjectural generalization.
We will say that a symmetric function f⁡(𝐱)f(\mathbf{x}) is Schur-log-concave if for all d≥1d\geq 1,
(f⁡(𝐱)|deg⁡d)2−f⁡(𝐱)|deg⁡d−1​f​(𝐱)|deg⁡d+1(f(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d})^{2}-f(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d-1}f(\mathbf{x})|_{\deg d+1}
is Schur-positive (i.e., when expanded in the Schur basis
{sλ(𝐱):λ⊢2d}\{s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x}):\lambda\vdash 2d\}, the coefficients are nonnegative). The following conjecture has been shown computationally for all connected graphs GG with at most 77 vertices.
Conjecture 7.3.
For any graph GG, the symmetric function
ΨG​(𝐱)\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x}) is Schur-log-concave.
Example 7.4.
Checking the Schur-log-concavity condition at d=1d=1 for ΨP3​(𝐱)\Psi_{P_{3}}(\mathbf{x})
from Equation (7.2) yields
(−2​e1​(𝐱))2−(1)​(e1,1​(𝐱)+e2​(𝐱))\displaystyle(-2e_{1}(\mathbf{x}))^{2}-(1)(e_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})+e_{2}(\mathbf{x}))
=3​s1,1​(𝐱)+2​s2​(𝐱),\displaystyle=3s_{1,1}(\mathbf{x})+2s_{2}(\mathbf{x}),
which is Schur-positive.
Note that Schur-log-concavity of ΨG​(𝐱)\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x}) implies log-concavity of the characteristic polynomial ΨG​(t,⋯,t,0,0,…)\Psi_{G}(t,\dotsm,t,0,0,\dots) of ΠGr\Pi_{G}^{r} for all rr. Huh’s result (i.e. the r=1 case) is therefore a specialization of Conjecture 7.3 for ΨG​(𝐱)\Psi_{G}(\mathbf{x}).
For r=2r=2 and G=KnG=K_{n}, log-concavity of ΨKn​(t,t,0,0,…)\Psi_{K_{n}}(t,t,0,0,\dots) follows from (7.4) and the fact that real-rooted polynomials with nonnegative coefficients are log-concave (see [10, Lemma 1.1]).
