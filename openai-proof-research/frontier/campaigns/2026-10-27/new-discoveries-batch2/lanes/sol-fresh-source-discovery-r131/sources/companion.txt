Abstract.
We introduce a poset of weighted hypergraphs on a fixed vertex set which has a remarkable property: the Möbius values of the intervals associated to any hypergraph give, up to sign, the coefficients of the hh-polynomial of its nestohedron. We exploit this relation to provide two new recurrence relations to compute hh-polynomials of nestohedra.
Key words and phrases: Hypergraphs, building sets, nestohedra, hh-polynomials, Möbius functions

1. Introduction
Building sets were introduced in the work of De Concini and Procesi on wonderful compactifications of complements of complex hyperplane arrangements [4]. They capture the notion of connectivity present in many mathematical contexts and have been generalized further from Boolean algebras to general semilattices in the work of Feichtner and Kozlov in [7].
A building set on ground set VV is a collection ℬ⊆𝒫⁡(V)\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(V) of nonempty subsets (blocks) of VV satisfying that
(B1) 
if I,J∈ℬI,J\in\mathcal{B} and I∩J≠∅I\cap J\not=\emptyset, then I∪J∈ℬI\cup J\in\mathcal{B}.
(B2) 
ℬ\mathcal{B} contains the singleton {v}\{v\}, for all v∈Vv\in V.
The most natural examples of building sets are perhaps the ones associated to connectivity in graphs. For a graph G=(V,E)G=(V,E) on vertex set VV and set of edges E=E⁡(G)E=E(G), the family ℬ⁡(G)\mathcal{B}(G) formed by subsets I⊆VI\subseteq V such that the induced subgraph of GG on vertex set II is connected, is a building set. Building sets of the form ℬ⁡(G)\mathcal{B}(G) are known as graphical.
In a greater generality, recall that a (multi) hypergraph HH on V=:V⁡(H)V=:V(H), is a collection of (possibly repeated) nonempty subsets of VV, called hyperedges, where any two hyperedges on the same underlying vertex set are considered to be distinct—to have different identities. For a hypergraph HH on VV, the collection ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}(H) of subsets of VV that are connected by elements of HH (see Section 2 for the definition) also forms a building set. Every building set is of the form ℬ=ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(H) for some hypergraph HH, but this map is not a bijection since there are infinitely many HH satisfing this property. In fact, we can consider building sets ℬ\mathcal{B} as hypergraphs themselves, and in such case—after an abuse of notation—ℬ=ℬ⁡(ℬ)\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}). However, as we argue later, ℬ\mathcal{B} is not the minimal hypergraph HH such that ℬ=ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(H) as long as ℬ≠∅\mathcal{B}\neq\emptyset.

The polytopes PHP_{H} are all simple, and hence their polar duals are simplicial. It follows then from a result of Bruggesser and Mani in [2] that their hh-polynomial has nonnegative integer coefficients. The fact that they are strictly positive and unimodal is a consequence of Stanley’s gg-theorem [13].
Postnikov, Reiner, and Williams proved in [10] that hℬ​(t)h_{\mathcal{B}}(t) can be computed as the descent enumerator of a family of rooted forests associated to ℬ\mathcal{B}, introduced by Postnikov in [11] under the name of ℬ\mathcal{B}-forests. This gives a combinatorial interpretation to the positive coefficients of hℬ​(t)h_{\mathcal{B}}(t). It is also shown in [10] that for a connected chordal building set ℬ\mathcal{B}, hℬ​(t)h_{\mathcal{B}}(t) can be computed as the descent enumerator of a family of permutations associated to ℬ\mathcal{B}, known as ℬ\mathcal{B}-permutations.

The main goal of this work is to provide three new ways of computing hℬ​(t)h_{\mathcal{B}}(t) for any building set ℬ\mathcal{B}: one as a sum of the Möbius values on maximal intervals of a partially ordered set (poset for short), and a pair of recurrences based on the notion of spanning sub-building sets. All our formulas, which we prove in the general context of hypergraphs, are derived from our novel construction of the poset of weighted spanning hypergraphs 𝕎​ℍ​(V)\mathbb{WH}(V) on VV, see Definition 2.4.
We say that a hypergraph H′H^{\prime} is a spanning subhypergraph of a hypergraph HH if V⁡(H′)=V⁡(H)=VV(H^{\prime})=V(H)=V and H′⊆HH^{\prime}\subseteq H. This relation defines an order H′≤HH^{\prime}\leq H on the set of hypergraphs on VV. To define a corresponding order on building sets we rely on a stricter version of the inclusion for hypergraphs. 2. The poset of weighted hypergraphs
In this section we introduce the poset of weighted hypergraphs and derive some properties of its Möbius function. Moreover, we study the auxiliary polynomial MH​(t)M_{H}(t) given by the left-hand side of (1.2) and develop its main properties. This will be essential in the proof of Theorem 1.6. We refer the reader to [12, 14] for undefined terminology and results concerning posets.
2.1. Hypergraphs and connectivity
Formally, a hypergraph HH on V=:V⁡(H)V=:V(H) is a collection of pairs 𝖨=(I,i)∈H\mathsf{I}=(I,i)\in H, called hyperedges, where ∅≠I⊆V\emptyset\neq I\subseteq V and ii is any label that makes the pair unique in HH. We consider two hyperedges (I,i)(I,i) and (J,j)(J,j) as different if either I≠JI\neq J or i≠ji\neq j, and we denote by V⁡(𝖨):=IV(\mathsf{I}):=I the underlying vertex set of 𝖨\mathsf{I}. If there is a single hyperedge 𝖨=(I,i)\mathsf{I}=(I,i) with vertex set I⊆V⁡(H)I\subseteq V(H), we can abuse notation and ignore its label, denoting such hyperedge simply by II. Building sets ℬ\mathcal{B}, and more generally hypergraphs without repeated hyperedges, can be considered as hypergraphs—I∈ℬI\in\mathcal{B} can, for example, be viewed as the hyperedge (I,I)(I,I), with underlying set II and label II.
For any hypergraph HH on VV and ∅≠I⊆V\emptyset\neq I\subseteq V we define the restriction of HH to II as the hypergraph on vertex set II and hyperedges given by
H|I={𝖩∣V⁡(𝖩)⊆I}.H|_{I}=\{\mathsf{J}\mid V(\mathsf{J})\subseteq I\}.
We say that I⊆VI\subseteq V is connected by HH if there is no nontrivial bipartition (separation) I=A⊔BI=A\sqcup B such that H|I=H|A⊔H|BH|_{I}=H|_{A}\sqcup H|_{B}. The set HmaxH_{\max} consists of the inclusion-maximal subsets of VV connected by HH. The elements in HmaxH_{\max} are pairwise disjoint and form a partition of VV. The connected components of HH are the restrictions H|IH|_{I}, for each I∈HmaxI\in H_{\max}. We write κ⁡(H)=|Hmax|\kappa(H)=|H_{\max}| and we say that HH is connected whenever Hmax={V}H_{\max}=\{V\}—and hence κ⁡(H)=1\kappa(H)=1.
The collection
ℬ⁡(H)={I⊆V∣H|I​ is connected}\mathcal{B}(H)=\{I\subseteq V\mid H|_{I}\text{ is connected}\}
forms a building set and, as we mentioned in Section 1, every building set ℬ\mathcal{B} is of this form.

Definition 2.2.

3. On ℬ\mathcal{B}-forests and HH-forests
It was shown in [10]
that hh-polynomials of nestohedra admit a combinatorial interpretation in terms of a family of rooted forests introduced in [11], known as ℬ\mathcal{B}-forests. In this section, we recall this notion in the language of hypergraphs. Recall that the collection of subsets of VV that are connected in a hypergraph HH on VV forms a building set ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}(H), and that this map is not injective. For a hypergraph HH the notion of HH-forest below coincides with the notion of ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}(H)-forest in [11]. These objects are also in bijective correspondence with maximal nested sets in [4] and with constructions in [5].
Recall that a rooted forest is a forest in which a distinguished node (a root) is selected in each connected component (tree). For a rooted forest FF, given two nodes uu and vv of FF, we say that uu is a descendant of vv if vv belongs to the shortest path connecting uu with the root of its connected component. We denote the set of descendants of vv in FF by F≤vF_{\leq v}. Note that v∈F≤vv\in F_{\leq v}. Moreover, if FF has roots r1,…,rMr_{1},\dots,r_{M}, the sets of vertices of the connected components of FF form the partition Fmax:={F≤r1,…,F≤rM}F_{\max}:=\{F_{\leq_{r_{1}}},\dots,F_{\leq_{r_{M}}}\} of VV.
A descendant uu of vv is called a child of vv if uu is a descendant of vv and (u,v)∈E⁡(F)(u,v)\in E(F). Finally, we say that uu and vv are incomparable in FF if neither uu is a descendant of vv, nor vv is a descendant of uu.
Definition 3.1 (c.f. Definition 7.7, [11]).
A rooted forest FF on VV is an HH-forest for a hypergraph HH on VV when
(HF1) 
For every v∈Vv\in V, H|F≤vH|_{F_{\leq v}} is connected.
(HF2) 
For any incomparable nodes u1,…,uku_{1},\dots,u_{k} in FF, k≥2k\geq 2, H|⋃j=1kF≤ujH|_{\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}F_{\leq u_{j}}} is not connected.
(HF3) 
We have that Fmax=HmaxF_{\max}=H_{\max}.
The connected components of an HH-forest are called HH-trees, each of which is associated with a connected component of HH.
Remark 3.2.
Given a hypergraph HH, the previous conditions can be expressed in terms of the corresponding building set ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}(H) as follows. (HF1) asks that F≤v∈ℬ⁡(H)F_{\leq v}\in\mathcal{B}(H), for all v∈Vv\in V. (HF2) means that ⋃j=1kF≤uj∉ℬ⁡(H)\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}F_{\leq u_{j}}\not\in\mathcal{B}(H), for incomparable nodes u1,…,uku_{1},\dots,u_{k}. Finally, (HF3) says that Fmax=ℬ​(H)maxF_{\max}=\mathcal{B}(H)_{\max}.
This shows that if ℬ=ℬ⁡(H1)=ℬ⁡(H2)\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}(H_{1})=\mathcal{B}(H_{2}) for hypergraphs H1H_{1} and H2H_{2}, the H1H_{1}-forests and H2H_{2}-forests are the same, and are simply the ℬ\mathcal{B}-forests defined in [11].
The following is a recursive description of HH-trees originally presented in [11] in terms of ℬ⁡(H)\mathcal{B}(H)-trees.
Proposition 3.3 (cf. Proposition 8.5, [10]).
Consider a connected hypergraph HH on VV, v∈Vv\in V, and let H1,…,HkH_{1},\dots,H_{k} be the connected components of the restricted hypergraph H|V∖vH|_{V\setminus v}.
