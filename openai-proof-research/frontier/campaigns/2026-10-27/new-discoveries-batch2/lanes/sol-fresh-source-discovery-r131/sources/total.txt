Abstract
The total isolation game is played on a graph GG by two players who take turns playing a vertex such that if SS is the set of already played vertices, then a vertex can be selected only if it is adjacent to a vertex that belongs to a (nontrivial) component of the graph G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S) of order at least 22 or a vertex that is isolated in G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S) and belongs to the set SS, where NG​(S)N_{G}(S) is the set of vertices adjacent to a vertex in SS. Dominator wishes to finish the game with the minimum number of played vertices, while Staller has the opposite goal. The game total isolation number ιgt​(G)\iota_{\rm gt}(G) is the number of moves in the Dominator-start game where both players play optimally. We prove that if GG is a connected graph of order n≥3n\geq 3, then ιgt​(G)<56​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)<\frac{5}{6}n. Furthermore if GG has minimum degree at least 22, then we prove that ιgt​(G)≤34​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)\leq\frac{3}{4}n. More generally, if GG is a connected graph of order n≥3n\geq 3 with minimum degree δ\delta where δ≥2\delta\geq 2, then we prove that ιgt​(G)≤(2​δ−13​δ−2)​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)\leq\left(\frac{2\delta-1}{3\delta-2}\right)n. Among other results it is proved that if GG is a graph of order nn with diameter 22, then ιgt​(G)≤23​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)\leq\frac{2}{3}n.
Keywords: Total isolating set; total isolation game; domination; total domination

1 Introduction
A vertex uu in a graph GG dominates a vertex vv if u=vu=v or uu is adjacent to vv in GG. A dominating set of GG is a set SS of vertices of GG such that every vertex in GG is dominated by a vertex in SS. A vertex uu in a graph GG totally dominates a vertex vv if uu is adjacent to vv in GG. A total dominating set of GG is a set SS of vertices of GG such that every vertex of GG is totally dominated by a vertex in SS. Thus, a set SS is a dominating set of GG if every vertex in V⁡(G)∖SV(G)\setminus S is adjacent to at least one vertex in SS, and a set SS is a total dominating set of GG if every vertex is adjacent to at least one vertex in SS. The concept of domination and its variations have been widely studied in theoretical, algorithmic and application aspects. A thorough treatment of (total) domination in graphs and its variants can be found in the books [14, 15].
The open neighborhood of a vertex vv in GG is the set of neighbors of vv, denoted NG​(v)N_{G}(v), where two vertices are neighbors if they are adjacent. Thus, NG​(v)={u∈V⁡(G):u​v∈E⁡(G)}N_{G}(v)=\{u\in V(G)\,\colon uv\in E(G)\}. The closed neighborhood of vv is the set NG​[v]=NG​(v)∪{v}N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}. The open neighborhood of a set S⊆V⁡(G)S\subseteq V(G) is the set of all neighbors of vertices in SS, denoted NG​(S)N_{G}(S), whereas the closed neighborhood of SS is NG​[S]=NG​(S)∪SN_{G}[S]=N_{G}(S)\cup S. Thus,
NG​(S)=⋃v∈SNG​(v)andNG​[S]=⋃v∈SNG​[v].N_{G}(S)=\bigcup_{v\in S}N_{G}(v)\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636ptN_{G}[S]=\bigcup_{v\in S}N_{G}[v].
A set S⊆V⁡(G)S\subseteq V(G) is an isolating set of GG if removing SS and its neighborhood NG​(S)N_{G}(S) leaves no edge, that is, V​(G)∖NG​[S]V(G)\setminus N_{G}[S] is an independent set in GG. The concept was introduced (in a more general setting) in 2017 by Caro and Hansberg [11]. The isolation number ι⁡(G)\iota(G) of GG is the minimum cardinality among all isolating sets of GG. This parameter is equivalent to the vertex-edge domination number, introduced earlier by Peters [18]. Isolation in graphs is studied further, for example, in [1, 3, 10, 12, 13, 17, 19].
A total isolating set is an isolating set SS with the additional property that the subgraph G⁡[S]G[S] induced by SS contains no vertex of degree 00. The total isolation number ιt​(G)\iota^{t}(G) of GG is the minimum cardinality among all total isolating sets of GG. This parameter was originally introduced by Boutrig and Chellali [2] as the total vertex-edge domination number, and was subsequently studied, for example, in [4, 5, 10].
In 2010 Brešar, Klavžar, and Rall [8] published the seminal paper on the domination game which belongs to the growing family of competitive optimization graph games. Domination games played on graphs are now very well studied in the literature. The subsequent rapid growth by the scientific community of research on domination games played on graphs inspired the recent book entitled “Domination games played on graphs” by Brešar, Henning, Klavžar, and Rall [7], which presented the state of the art results at the time and shows that the area is rich for further research. In this paper, we study the total version of the isolation game in graphs.
Recently Brešar, Dravec, Johnston, Kuenzel, and Rall in [6], introduced a game counterpart of the isolation number, called the isolation game, following the ideas of the classical domination game [8]. The isolation game was subsequently studied further in [9] at the Workshop on Games on Graphs III, held in Rogla, Slovenia, in June 2025. In this paper, we introduce and study a game counterpart of the total isolation number.
The total isolation game is played on a graph GG by Dominator and Staller, who take turns selecting/playing a vertex from GG while obeying the rule that if SS is the set of already played vertices, then a vertex vv can be selected only if vv totally dominates a vertex uu that belongs to a (nontrivial) component of the graph G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S) of order at least 22 or vv totally dominates a vertex u∈Su\in S that is isolated in G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S). Such a vertex vv is playable, and a move selecting vv is legal. The game ends when no playable vertex exists. When the game ends, the set of vertices selected forms a total isolating set of GG.
As in the domination game, in the total isolation game Dominator wishes to finish the game by playing the fewest number of vertices, while Staller wishes to delay the process as much as possible. Thus, Dominator seeks to minimize the size of the chosen set while Staller tries to make it as large as possible. If Dominator starts the game, we speak of a D-game, otherwise it is an S-game. The game total isolation number, ιgt​(G)\iota_{\rm gt}(G), and the Staller-start game total isolation number, ιgt′​(G)\iota_{\rm gt}^{\prime}(G), are the number of vertices selected in the D-game and the S-game, respectively, provided that both players play optimally.
For graph theory notation and terminology, we generally follow [14]. Specifically, let GG be a graph with vertex set V⁡(G)V(G) and edge set E⁡(G)E(G), and of order n⁡(G)=|V⁡(G)|n(G)=|V(G)| and size m⁡(G)=|E⁡(G)|m(G)=|E(G)|. We denote the degree of a vertex vv in GG by degG⁡(v)\deg_{G}(v). The minimum and maximum degrees in GG are denoted by δ⁡(G)\delta(G) and Δ⁡(G)\Delta(G), respectively. An isolated vertex in GG is a vertex of degree 00 in GG. A graph is isolate-free if it contains no isolated vertex. A set XX is a packing in GG if for any two distinct vertices u,v∈Xu,v\in X, we have dG​(u,v)≥3d_{G}(u,v)\geq 3, and so NG​[u]∩NG​[v]=∅N_{G}[u]\cap N_{G}[v]=\emptyset.
A trivial graph is the graph of order 11, and a nontrivial graph has order at least 22. A cycle on nn vertices is denoted by CnC_{n} and a path on nn vertices by PnP_{n}. The complete graph on nn vertices is denoted by KnK_{n}. For a subset SS of vertices of a graph GG, we denote by G−SG-S the graph obtained from GG by deleting the vertices in SS and all edges incident with vertices in SS. If S={v}S=\{v\}, then we simply write G−vG-v rather than G−{v}G-\{v\}. The subgraph induced by the set SS is denoted by G⁡[S]G[S]. The diameter of GG, denoted diam⁡(G){\rm diam}(G), is the maximum distance among all pairs of vertices in GG.
2 Continuation Principle
One of the main tools in analyzing the domination game is the Continuation Principle first presented by Kinnersley, West, and Zamani [16]. An important consequence of the Continuation Principle for a given game variant of the domination game is the fundamental property that the number of moves in the D-game and the S-game when played optimally can differ by at most 11. It is known, for example, that the Continuation Principle holds for the total domination game. And as a special case of a more general result, the authors in [6] proved that the Continuation Principle holds in the isolation game.
Consider the total isolation game played on a path P5P_{5}. Let GG be the path P5P_{5} given by v1​v2​v3​v4​v5v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}. In the D-game, Dominator plays as his first move the central vertex v3v_{3}, and so the set of played vertices immediately after Dominator’s first move is S={v3}S=\{v_{3}\}. We note that no component of G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S) is nontrivial and that the vertex v3v_{3} is the only isolated vertex in G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S) that belongs to the set SS. Hence the only legal move available to Staller is either to play the vertex v2v_{2} or the vertex v4v_{4} in order to totally dominate the vertex v3v_{3}. Once Staller plays her first move (either v2v_{2} or v4v_{4}), the game is complete. Hence, ιgt​(G)=2\iota_{\rm gt}(G)=2. In the S-game, Staller plays as her first move the leaf v1v_{1}. If Dominator plays v2v_{2} on his first move, then Staller responds by playing v5v_{5}. If Dominator plays v3v_{3} on his first move, then Staller responds by playing v4v_{4}. If Dominator plays v4v_{4} on his first move, then Staller responds by playing v5v_{5}. If Dominator plays v5v_{5} on his first move, then Staller responds by playing v4v_{4}. In all four cases, one additional move is required by Dominator to complete the game. Hence, ιgt′​(G)≥4\iota_{\rm gt}^{\prime}(G)\geq 4. (One can show that four moves always suffice to complete the S-game, implying that ιgt′​(G)=4\iota_{\rm gt}^{\prime}(G)=4, but we do not need this stronger property here.) Therefore, |ιgt​(G)−ιgt′​(G)|≥2|\iota_{\rm gt}(G)-\iota_{\rm gt}^{\prime}(G)|\geq 2.
Since the total isolation game combines the flavor of the total domination game and the isolation game, and since the Continuation Principle holds for both these games, one might expect that the Continuation Principle holds for the total isolation game. If this were the case, then for every isolate-free graph GG we would have the desirable property that |ιgt​(G)−ιgt′​(G)|≤1|\iota_{\rm gt}(G)-\iota_{\rm gt}^{\prime}(G)|\leq 1. However, since there are graphs GG for which |ιgt​(G)−ιgt′​(G)|>1|\iota_{\rm gt}(G)-\iota_{\rm gt}^{\prime}(G)|>1, we infer the following result.
Observation 1
The Continuation Principle does not hold for the total isolation game.
3 Upper bounds for given minimum degree
We remark that the Continuation Principle is a powerful tool for establishing upper bounds on game domination type parameters, including game domination number, the game total domination number, the game isolation number, to name games where the Continuation Principle holds. However for several variants of the domination games, such as the independent domination game and the connected domination game, in which the Continuation Principle does not hold, it has proved challenging to find good upper bounds on the associated game parameters. Observation 1 therefore suggests that finding good upper bounds on the game total isolation number is likely to be a difficult problem. In this section, our aim is to provide upper bounds on the game total isolation number in terms of the minimum degree of the graph.
During the course of the total isolation game, if SS is the set of selected vertices played to date, then a vertex vv is considered marked if v∈NG​(S)v\in N_{G}(S) or if v∉Sv\notin S and vv is isolated in G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S). We note that a marked vertex may or may not be playable. A vertex is unmarked if it is not marked. At any given stage of the total isolation game, we define the sets SS, MM and UU as follows: 
∙\bullet 
SS is the set of selected vertices.
∙\bullet 
MM is the set of marked vertices.
∙\bullet 
UU is the set of unmarked vertices.
We note that if uu is an unmarked vertex, then u∈Uu\in U and either u∈Su\in S and uu is an isolated vertex in G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S) or uu belongs to a nontrivial component in the graph G−NG​(S)G-N_{G}(S). Thus every playable vertex in the total isolation game is adjacent to at least one unmarked vertex, and after such a vertex is played, at least one new vertex is marked. The game ends when all vertices of GG are marked.
Theorem 1
If GG is a connected graph of order n≥3n\geq 3 with minimum degree δ\delta where δ≥2\delta\geq 2 and maximum degree Δ\Delta, then

In what follows, let Dominator play according to a greedy strategy that on each of his moves he plays a vertex that results in a maximum increase in the number of marked vertices. That is, on each of his moves, Dominator plays a vertex that marks as many previously unmarked vertices as possible. Before Dominator’s first move, all vertices are unmarked, and so U=V⁡(G)U=V(G) and S=M=∅S=M=\emptyset. Let tt denote the number of moves played upon completion of the game when Dominator adopts his greedy strategy.
We consider two stages in the game.
Stage 11.
A move of Dominator belongs to Stage 11 if there exists a playable move available to Dominator that marks at least two new vertices. A move of Staller belongs to Stage 11, if so does Dominator’s previous move.
Stage 22.
A move of Dominator belongs to Stage 22 if every playable move available to Dominator marks exactly one new vertex. A move of Staller belongs to Stage 22, if so does Dominator’s previous move.

5 Closing comments
We pose the following 23\frac{2}{3}-conjecture for the game total isolation number.
Conjecture 1
If GG is a graph of order nn and every component of GG has order at least 33, then ιgt​(G)≤23​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)\leq\frac{2}{3}n.
We remark that if Conjecture 1 is true, then the 23​n\frac{2}{3}n-upper bound on the game total isolation number is best possible. As an example, let GG be a graph of order nn where every component is a path P3P_{3}- or P6P_{6}-component or a cycle C3C_{3}- or C6C_{6}-component. Staller’s strategy is to reply to every move of Dominator by playing in the component that Dominator played in on his previous move. When Dominator plays in a P3P_{3}- or C3C_{3}-component, Staller responds with a playable vertex in that component that marks all vertices. When Dominator plays in a P6P_{6}- or C6C_{6}-component, Staller responds by playing a playable vertex in that component following her optimal strategy, thereby ensuring that four moves (two by Dominator and two by Staller) are played in such components. (Note that if Dominator plays a vertex vv from a C6C_{6}-component CC, then Staller plays the vertex at distance 33 from vv on the cycle CC, thereby forcing two additional moves played on CC.) Upon completion of the game, two moves are played in every P3P_{3}- and C3C_{3}-component and four moves are played in every P6P_{6}- and C6C_{6}-component, implying that ιgt​(G)≥23​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)\geq\frac{2}{3}n. However if Conjecture 1 is true, then ιgt​(G)≤23​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)\leq\frac{2}{3}n. Consequently, ιgt​(G)=23​n\iota_{\rm gt}(G)=\frac{2}{3}n.
