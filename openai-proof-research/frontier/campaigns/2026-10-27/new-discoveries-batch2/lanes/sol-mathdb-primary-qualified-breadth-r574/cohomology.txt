Abstract.
    

It is well known to us that [1], Conjecture 2.5.3 is that for a smooth projective surface X there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that  $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  for every integral curve  $C$  on this surface  $X$ .
    

In this paper, we give a numerical characterization of this conjecture, and verify Del Pezzo surface, self-product of an elliptic curve which has general moduli, smooth projective surfaces  $X$  with Picard number  $\rho(X)\leq 3$  and without any integral negative curves, minimal smooth projective surfaces with Kodaira dimension zero, fiber bundles over a curve satisfy the conjecture.
    

On the other hand, We give a example of smooth projective surface of general type with Picard number arbitrarily large whose construction made us give the generalized SHGH conjecture as follow:
    

Let  $X_{n}$  be blowing-up at n generic points of a smooth projective surface  $X$  with Picard number 1 and  $K_{X}^{2}>0$ , then  $X_{n}$  is (-1)-special, i.e., every integral negative curve is (-1)-rational curve.
    

This conjecture holds for  $n\leq vol(X)$ .
  




Keywords: integral curves, surface, bounding cohomology, Picard number, SHGH Conjecture, Bounded Negativity Conjecture.




1. Introduction




The celebrated survey [1] named that ”Recent developments and open problems in linear series” gives a list of problems containing asymptotic effectively, semi-effectiveness, stability of speciality, regularity for generic monomial zero-schemes, bounding cohomology, algebraic fundamental groups and Seshadri numbers, blow-ups of  $\mathbb{P}^{n}$  and hyperplane arrangements, bounds for symbolic powers and so on.




In this paper, we mainly consider the bounding cohomology(see [1], 2.5).




There are various equivalent versions of the SHGH Conjecture ([15], [10], [8], [11]) which implies the Nagata’s Conjecture (see [6], Lemma 2.4). Here is one:



Conjecture 1.1.



([1], Conjecture 2.5.1)
Let  $C\subseteq X_{n}$  be a prime divisor where  $X_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}$  is the blow-up of generic points  $p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}$ . Then  $h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=0$ .




Problem 1.1.



([1], Problem 2.5.2)
How can we remove the assumption about the points begin generic in the SHGH Conjecture?





The following conjecture which implies the Bounded negativity conjecture (see Proposition 3.1) arose out of discussions between B. Harbourne, J.  $Ro\acute{e}$ , C. Ciliberto and R. Miranda.( [1], 3.1.2 or [7] gives a counterexample.)



Conjecture 1.2.



([1],Conjecture 2.5.3)
Let  $X$  be a smooth projective surface. Then there exists a constant  $c_{X}$  such that for every prime divisor  $C$  we has  $h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq c_{X}h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$ .





The SHGH Conjecture is that  $c_{X}=0$  when  $X$  is obtained by blowing up generic points of  $\mathbb{P}^{2}$ .




Recently, Ciro Ciliberto and his coauthors posted some questions in their note [7]. We firstly recall one of those questions related to our results in this paper.



Question 1.1.



([7],Question 4)
Does there exist a constant  $m=m(X)$  such that  $\frac{D\cdot K_{X}}{D^{2}}<m$  for any effective divisor  $D$  on  $X$  with  $D^{2}>0$ ?





Based this Question 1.1, we define  $k_{C}:=\big|\frac{K_{X}\cdot C}{C^{2}}\big|$  for every curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}\neq 0$ , otherwise  $k_{C}:=\big|K_{C}\cdot C\big|$ . ”integral” means that irreducible and reducible.




Now we note that the main results of Conjecture 1.2 in this paper are the following:



Theorem 1.1.



A smooth projective surface X holds for Conjecture 1.1 if and only if there exists a positive constant m(X) only depended on X with  $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  and  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$  for every integral curve C with either  $k_{C}>1,C^{2}>0$  or  $C^{2}\leq 0$  on X.





By Theorem 1.1 and the following Theorem 1.3, we obtain some examples satisfy Conjecture 1.2.



Theorem 1.2.



Del Pezzo surface,self-product of an elliptic curve which has general moduli, smooth projective surfaces X with Picard number  $\rho(X)\leq 3$  and without any integral negative curves ,minimal smooth projective surfaces with Kodaira dimension zero, fiber bundles over a curve satisfy Question 1.1 and Conjecture 1.2.





Based on a result of Theorem 1.2, we give a answer for Question 1.1.



Theorem 1.3.



(=Theorem 4.1)
Let  $X$  be a smooth projective surface without integral negative curves, if X is very friendly (see Definition 4.1), then  $X$  satisfies Conjecture 1.2.





By Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 1.3 we have the following question.



Question 1.2.



(=Question 4.1)
Does exists a minimal smooth projective surface  $X$  with Picard number  $\rho(X)\geq 4$  and not very friendly satisfies Conjecture 1.2?





Finally, we construct a smooth projective surface of general type with Picard number arbitrarily large. And we have a natural problem which generalize the SHGH conjecture.



Conjecture 1.3.



(=Conjecture 5.1)
Let  $X_{n}$  be blowing-up at n generic points of a smooth projective surface X with Picard number 1 and  $K_{X}^{2}>0$ , then  $X_{n}$  is (-1)-special, i.e., every integral negative curve is a (-1)-rational curve.





Note that Conjecture 1.3 holds for  $n\leq vol(X)$ .





2. Study for  $k_{C}$ 




In this section, we assume that  $X$  is a smooth projective surface. ”integral” means that irreducible and reducible.




We define  $k_{C}:=\big|\frac{K_{X}\cdot C}{C^{2}}\big|$  for every curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}\neq 0$ , otherwise  $k_{C}:=\big|K_{C}\cdot C\big|$ .



Remark 2.1.



If we can find a ample divisor  $D=|m_{1}C_{1}+m_{2}C_{2}|$  on this surface  $X$  such that





 $C_{1}^{2}>0,C_{2}^{2}<0,m_{1}\gg 0,m_{2}\gg 0,0<D^{2}<N(X),\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}>\frac{-C_{2}^{2}}{C_{1}\cdot C_{2}},$ 



then there exists an integral curve D with  $D^{2}>0$  such that  $k_{D}$  can be either arbitrarily large.




We hope this result for some surfaces should be right but we can’t prove it now.




In this section, we mainly obtain the results of  $k_{C}$  on the surface which doen’t have any integral negative curves.




Definition 2.1.



Let  $X$  be a smooth projective surface. An effective divisor  $C$  is numerical connected if  $C=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}C_{i}$  with  $m_{1},\cdots,m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}$  and for every  $C_{i}$  there exists another curve  $C_{j}$  such that  $C_{i}\cdot C_{j}>0$ , where  $C_{1},\cdots,C_{k}$  are integral curves.




Proposition 2.1.



Let X be a smooth projective surface, if  $X$  does not have integral negative curves, then Question 1.1 is affirmative for every effective divisor  $C$  which is numerical connected on  $X$  if only if there exist a positive number  $m(X)$  that  $k_{C}<m(X)$  for every integral curve  $C$ .




Proof.



 $\Longleftarrow$  Let  $D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}C_{i}$  be an effective divisor on  $X$ ,where  $C_{i},i=1,...,k$  are integral curves and





 $\begin{split}C_{i}^{2}>0,i=1,\cdots,t;\end{split}$ 







 $\begin{split}C_{i^{\prime}}^{2}=0,i^{\prime}=t+1,\dots,k;\end{split}$ 







 $\begin{split}D^{2}>0\ implies\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}^{2}C_{i}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j}^{k}2m_{i}m_{j}C_{i}\cdot C_{j}>0.\end{split}$ 



By Hodge index theorem ([4], IV. Corollary 2.15 or [9], V. Theorem 1.9) , we have  $C_{i}\cdot C_{i^{\prime}}>0$  for all the pairs  $(i,i^{\prime})$  with  $1\leq i\leq t,t+1\leq i^{\prime}\leq k$ .




If there exists a positive number  $m$  that  $k_{C}<m$  for all integral curves  $C$  with  $C^{2}\geq 0$ , then we have





 $\begin{split}k_{D}&=\frac{K_{X}\cdot D}{D^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}K_{X}\cdot C_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}^{2}C_{i}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i\neq j}^{k}2m_{i}m_{j}C_{i}\cdot C_{j}}\\
&\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}K_{X}\cdot C_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{t}m_{i}^{2}C_{i}^{2}+\sum_{0<i\leq t,t<j\leq s}2m_{i}m_{j}C_{i}\cdot C_{j}}\\
&\leq max\{max_{i\in\{1,...,t\}}\{\frac{K_{X}\cdot C_{i}}{C_{i}^{2}}\},max_{i^{\prime}\in\{t+1,\dots,k\}}\{K_{X}\cdot C_{i^{\prime}}\}\}<m.\end{split}$ 



In fact, we need to note that the case that  $D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}C_{i}$  with  $C_{i}^{2}=0,m_{i}>0$  for  $i=1,\cdots,k$ .




Though





 $\begin{split}D^{2}>0\ implies\ C_{i}\cdot C_{j}>0\ for\ some\ pairs\ (i,j)\ with\ i<j,\end{split}$ 



which does not implies that there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that





 $\begin{split}k_{D}&=\frac{K_{X}\cdot D}{D^{2}}\\
&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}K_{X}\cdot C_{i}}{\sum_{1\leq i\neq j}^{k}2m_{i}m_{j}C_{i}\cdot C_{j}}\\
&\leq m(X).\end{split}$ 



∎





We need to give some results about  $k_{C}$  of every integral curve C with  $C^{2}=0$  for verifying Conjecture 1.1 by the  $N\acute{e}ron$  Severi groups of some surfaces.



Proposition 2.2.



Let C be an integral curve with  $C^{2}=0$  on a smooth projective surface X, then there exists a positive constant m(X) such that every integral curve  $D\in|nC|$  with  $k_{D}\leq m(X)$  for every  $n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}$ .




Proof.



It is enough to show that there doesn’t exist an integral curve  $D\in|mC|$  for  $m\gg 0$ .




If there exist an integral curve  $D\in|mC|$  for  $m\gg 0$ , then by Lemma 3.1 we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\leq 2,$ 



which implies that





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(mC))\leq 2,for\ every\ m\geq 1.$ 



If  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(mC))=2$ , then by the following Lemma 2.1, we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(2mC))\geq 2h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(mC))-1,$ 



it has a contraction.




So we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(mC))=1,for\ every\ m\geq 1,$ 



Hence, there exists a positive integer number  $m_{0}$ , such that





 $m_{0}C\sim 0,$ 



it also has a contraction.
∎




Proposition 2.3.



Let  $C_{i}$  be the integral curve with  $C_{i}^{2}=0,i=1,2$ , on a smooth projective surface X, then there exists a positive constant m(X) such that every integral curve  $D\in|m_{1}C_{1}+m_{2}C_{2}|$  with  $k_{D}\leq m(X)$  for every  $m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}+$ .




Proof.



If  $D^{2}>0$ , then





 $C_{1}\cdot C_{2}>0,m_{1}\neq 0,m_{2}\neq 0.$ 



Hence,





 $\begin{split}k_{D}&=|\frac{m_{1}K_{X}\cdot C_{1}+m_{2}K_{X}\cdot C_{2}}{2m_{1}m_{2}C_{1}C_{2}}|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{|m_{2}|}k_{C_{1}}+\frac{1}{|m_{2}|}k_{C_{2}}.\end{split}$ 






If there exists an integral curve  $D\in|m_{1}C_{1}+m_{2}C_{2}|$  with  $D^{2}=0$  for  $m_{1},m_{2}\gg 0$ , then also by Lemma 3.1 we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\leq 2,$ 



which implies that





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}C_{1}+m_{2}C_{2}))\leq 2,for\ every\ m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1.$ 



If  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}C_{1}+m_{2}C_{2}))=2$ , then by the following Lemma 2.1, we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(2m_{1}C_{1}+2m_{2}C_{2}))\geq 2h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}C+m_{2}C_{2}))-1,$ 



it has a contraction.




So we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}C_{1}+m_{2}C_{2}))=1,for\ every\ m\geq 1,$ 



Hence, there exists a positive integer number  $m_{i_{1}},m_{i_{2}}$ , such that





 $m_{i_{1}}C_{1}+m_{i_{2}}C_{2}\sim 0,$ 



it also has a contraction.
∎




Lemma 2.1.



Let X be a smooth projective variety, taking effective divisors D and E such that  $h^{0}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\neq 0,h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(E))\neq 0$ , then





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D+E))\geq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))+h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(E))-1.$ 






Proof.



We can assume that  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\geq 2,h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(E))\geq 2$ ,  $|D|$  and  $|E|$  have not fixed parts. Therefore, there exist two divisors  $div(f)\in|D,div(g)\in|E|$  without common components.




Let  $Z$  be a subscheme a intersection of those two divisors, then we have the following short exact sequence





 $0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D-E)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-E)\xrightarrow{(f,\ g)}\mathcal{I}_{Z}\longrightarrow 0,$ 



where





 $(f,g):(u,v)\mapsto fu+gv.$ 



Hence,





 $0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\xrightarrow{(f,\ g)}\mathcal{I}_{Z}(D+E)\longrightarrow 0,$ 



we have a long exact sequence





 $0\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow H^{0}(E)\oplus H^{0}(D)\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(D+E))$ 



which gives the desired result.
∎




Proposition 2.4.



([7], Lemma 11)
If Question 1.1 holds for a smooth projective algebraic surface, then there exists a positive constant  $m_{1}=m_{1}(X)$  such that if  $D$  is any effective divisor with  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=1$  and  $D^{2}>0$ , then one has  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}D))\geq 2$ .




Proof.



By Lemma 2.1, it is enough to show that for every integral curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}>0$  there exists a positive constant  $m_{1}(X)\in\mathbb{N}$  with





 $m_{1}\geq\frac{k_{C}+\sqrt{k_{C}^{2}+\frac{8(1-q(X))}{C^{2}}}}{2}$ 



such that  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}C))\geq 2$ .




Note that  $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=1$  implies that





 $\sqrt{k_{C}^{2}+\frac{8(1-q(X))}{C^{2}}}\geq 0.$ 



By the proof of theorem 3.1, we have





 $\begin{split}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(m_{1}C)&\geq\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-p_{g}(X)+\frac{m_{1}-k_{C}}{2}m_{1}C^{2}\\
&\geq 2.\end{split}$ 



∎






3. The proof of Theorem 1.1




Now we begin to analysis the Conjecture 1.2.



Lemma 3.1.



A minimal smooth projective surface X holds for Conjecture 1.2 if there exists a positive constant m(X) only depended on X with  $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  and  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$  for every integral curve C on X.




Proof.



Note that  $h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}-C))\leq p_{g}(X)$ .
By Riemann-Roch theorem, we have





 $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+q(X)-1+\frac{(k_{C}-sgn(C^{2}))|C^{2}|}{2},$ 



and





 $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+q(X)-1+\frac{K_{X}\cdot C}{2},$ 



where  $C^{2}\neq 0$  and  $C^{2}=0$  respectively.




Note that if  $k_{C}\leq 1,C^{2}>0$  then





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+q(X)-1-h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C^{2}))\leq h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+q(X)-1.$ 



∎




Proposition 3.1.



([7], Proposition 14)
The Conjecture 1.2 implies that Bounded Negativity Conjecture.




Proof.



Let  $C$  be an integral curve such that  $C^{2}\leq 0$ , then  $h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=1$ . If Conjecture 1.1 holds, then  $h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq\alpha(X)$ . By Riemann-Roch theorem we have





 $1+h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=p_{a}(X)+C^{2}-p_{a}(C)+2+h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)),$ 



which implies





 $C^{2}\geq-p_{a}(X)-\alpha(X)-1,$ 



proving the Bounded Negativity Conjecture.
∎




Lemma 3.2.



Let  $C$  be a integral curve on a smooth projective surface  $X$  with  $C^{2}\geq 0$ , then





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{2}+2.$ 






Proof.



Consider the short exact sequence





 $0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{C}(C)\rightarrow 0,$ 



there is a long exact sequence of cohomology groups





 $0\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C))\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\rightarrow\cdots$ 



which implies





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))-1\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C))\leq q(X)+h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))-1.$ 



and by Clifford inequality on curve (see [9], IV. Theorem 5.4.) we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(C))+1\leq\frac{1}{2}C^{2}+2.$ 



∎




Lemma 3.3.



([7], Proposition 15)
There exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$  for every integral curve  $C$  on a smooth projective surface  $X$  which holds for Conjecture 1.2.




Proof.



Let  $C$  be an integral curve on  $X$ , and we may assume  $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq\alpha(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  for some  $\alpha(X)>1$ .




By Riemann-Roch theorem we have





 $\begin{split}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))&\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\\
&=p_{a}(X)+1+\frac{C^{2}-K_{X}\cdot C}{2}+h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\\
&\leq p_{a}(X)+1+\frac{C^{2}-K_{X}\cdot C}{2}+\alpha(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)).\end{split}$ 



If  $C^{2}\geq 0$ , then by Lemma 3.2 we have





 $\begin{split}K_{X}\cdot C&\leq C^{2}+2(p_{a}(X)+1)+2(\alpha(X)-1)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\\
&\leq\alpha(X)C^{2}+2(p_{a}(X)+2\alpha(X)-2).\end{split}$ 



If  $C^{2}<0$ , then by Proposition 3.1





 $h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=1,C^{2}\geq-p_{a}(X)-\alpha(X)-1.$ 



And now we have





 $k_{C}=\frac{|K_{C}\cdot C|}{-C^{2}}\leq|K_{X}\cdot C|\leq 2p_{a}(X)+2\alpha(X)+C^{2}<2p_{a}(X)+2\alpha(X).$ 



∎




Lemma 3.4.



If a minimal smooth projective surface X holds for Conjecture 1.2, then there exists a positive constant m(X) only depended on X such that  $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  for every integral curve  $C$  with either  $k_{C}>1,C^{2}>0$  or  $C^{2}<0$ .




Proof.



By Proposition 3.1, we can taking  $C$  as an integral curve with  $C^{2}>0,k_{C}>1$  on  $X$ .




By Riemann-Roch theorem, we have





 $\begin{split}\frac{(k_{C}-1)C^{2}}{2}&=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))-h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\\
&\leq\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+(\alpha(X)-1)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\end{split}$ 



which implies that





 $C^{2}\leq\frac{2}{k_{C}-1}\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+\frac{2(\alpha(X)-1)}{k_{C}-1}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)).$ 






∎





From Lemma 3.1, Lemma 3.3, Lemma 3.4, we have the following result:



Lemma 3.5.



A minimal smooth projective surface X holds for Conjecture 1.2 if and only if there exists a positive constant m(X) only depended on X with  $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  and  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$  for every integral curve C with either  $k_{C}>1,C^{2}>0$  or  $C^{2}\leq 0$  on X.





Now we begin to consider the cases of surface  $X$  which may not minimal.



Theorem 3.1.



A smooth projective surface X holds for Conjecture 1.2 if and only if there exists a positive constant m(X) only depended on X with  $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  and  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$  for every integral curve C with either  $k_{C}>1,C^{2}>0$  or  $C^{2}\leq 0$  on X.




Proof.



It is enough to prove that the case of every integral curve  $C$  with  $K_{X}\cdot C<0$  on this  $X$ .




 $\Leftarrow$ 




We also by Riemann-Roch theorem to have





 $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+q-1+\frac{K_{X}\cdot C-C^{2}}{2}.$ 



If  $C^{2}\geq K_{X}\cdot C$ , then





 $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+q-1,$ 



which completes the part of the proof.




If  $C^{2}<K_{X}\cdot C<0$ , but





 $(K_{X}+C)\cdot C=2g(C)-2\geq-2,$ 



which makes





 $K_{X}\cdot C=C^{2}=-1,k_{C}=1,$ 



This is a contradiction.




 $\Rightarrow$ 




By Lemma 3.3, we only need to prove that there exists a positive constant m(X) only depended on X with  $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$  for every integral curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}>0$ .




By Riemann-Roch theorem again, we have





 $\frac{-C^{2}(1+k_{C})}{2}=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))+h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))-h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)).$ 



without of loss generality, let  $\alpha(X)>1$  such that  $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq\alpha(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)$ .




Note that  $\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})$  is a constant and  $h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))\leq p_{g}(X)$  and  $1<1+k_{C}\leq\frac{2C^{2}+2}{C^{2}}$ .




Hence,





 $C^{2}\leq\frac{2}{k_{C}+1}(\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+(\alpha(X)-1)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))).$ 



∎





By Theorem 3.1, we can easy obtain the following result.



Corollary 3.1.



Smooth projective surfaces with Picard number 1 satisfy Conjecture 1.2.




Proof.



Now consider an integral curve C with  $C^{2}>0$  on the minimal smooth projective surface  $X$  with Picard number 1, then  $C\equiv r(C)H,K_{X}\equiv m(X)H$  from some  $r(C),m(X)\in\mathbb{Z}$ , where  $H=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)$  is a very ample divisor on  $X$  and ” $\equiv$ ” is numerical equivalent.




Note that  $k_{C}=\frac{|K_{X}\cdot C|}{C^{2}}=|\frac{m(X)}{r(C)}|\leq|m(X)|$ .




On the other hand, let  $C$  be the integral curve on  $X$  such that  $k_{C}>1,C\equiv r(C)H$ , which implies that





 $|r(C)|<|m(X)|,C^{2}<K_{X}^{2}.$ 



∎






4. The proof of Theorem 1.2




4.1. Del Pezzo surface




In this subsection we study surfaces  $X$  such that  $-K_{X}$  is ample satisfy Conjecture 1.2. Theses surfaces have been studied extensively from geometric and arithmetic points of view. Now we recall some facts from [12].



Definition 4.1.



A Del Pezzo surface (over a field k) is a smooth, geometrically irreducible and proper surface X such that  $-K_{X}$  is ample.




Proposition 4.1.



([12],Chapter III,Proposition 3.2)
If X is a Del Pezzo surface, then  $h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0$  and  $\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1$ .




Proposition 4.2.



([12],Chapter III, Exercise 3.9)
Let X be a smooth projective surface over an algebraically closed field. Assume that  $-K_{X}$  is ample. Then we have one of the following cases:


(1) 



 $X\backsimeq\mathbb{P}^{2}$  or  $X\backsimeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}$ ;


(2) 



X is obtained from  $\mathbb{P}^{2}$  by blowing up  $9-K_{X}^{2}$  points of  $\mathbb{P}^{2}$ .







It is enough to show that blow-up at  $9-K_{X}^{2}$  points of  $\mathbb{P}^{2}$  satisfy Conjecture 1.2.




Without loss of generality, let  $f:X\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}$  be the blow-up at n distinct points. Note that the Picard group of  $X$  is generated by  $H=f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)$  and  $E_{1},\cdots,E_{\rho(X)-1}$ , where  $E_{1},\cdots,E_{\rho(X)-1}$  are the exceptional curves of  $f$ .




Consider an integral curve  $C=dH-\sum_{i}m_{i}E_{i}$  but  $H,E_{1},\cdots,E_{\rho(X)}$ .




By [9],V, Example 1.5.1 and Example 3.9.2 we have





 $\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-\sum_{i}\frac{m_{i}(m_{i}-1)}{2}\geq 0,$ 



which implies that





 $\begin{split}k_{C}&=\frac{-K_{X}\cdot C}{C^{2}}\\
&=\frac{3d-\sum_{i}m_{i}}{d^{2}-\sum_{i}m_{i}^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{3d-\sum_{i}m_{i}}{3d+\sum_{i}m_{i}-2}\\
&\leq 3.\end{split}$ 



By Theorem 3.1, we only need to show that for every integral curve  $C$  with  $k_{C}>1,C^{2}>0$  there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that





 $|C^{2}|\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)).$ 



By Proposition 4.1, Kodaira’s vanishing theorem ([9], III. Remark 7.15, or [13], Theorem 4.2.1)and Riemann-Roch theorem, we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=1+\frac{(1+k_{C})C^{2}}{2},$ 



which is the desired result.



Remark 4.1.



Let  $X_{n}$  be the blow-up at n points of smooth projective surface X with Picard number 1. If for some cases of this  $X_{n}$  we can show the above curve  $C^{2}\geq A(X)d+B(X)$ , then  $X_{n}$  also satisfies Conjecture 1.2 (see Section 5).






4.2. Self-product of an elliptic curve




In this subsection, mainly materials come from [13], Section 1.5.B.




Denote by  $E$  a smooth irreducible complex projective curve of genus  $g(E)=1$ . We set





 $X:=E\times E,$ 



with projections  $pr_{1},pr_{2}:X\longrightarrow E$ . Fixing a point  $P\in E$ , consider in  $N^{1}(X)_{\mathcal{R}}$  the three classes





 $F_{1}=\{P\}\times E,F_{2}=E\times\{P\},\Delta,$ 



where  $\Delta\subseteq E\times E$  is the diagonal. These classes are independent, and if  $E$  has general moduli then it is known that they span  $N_{1}(X)_{\mathcal{R}}$ . Intersection among them are governed by the formulae:





 $\Delta\cdot F_{1}=\Delta\cdot F_{2}=F_{1}\cdot F_{2}=1,$ 







 $F_{1}^{2}=F_{2}^{2}=0,\Delta^{2}=0,$ 



Now, we give the main result.



Claim 4.1.



The self-product of an elliptic curve which has general moduli satisfies Conjecture 1.2.




Proof.



[13], Lemma 1.5.4 tells us every integral curve  $C$  can be written as





 $C=xF_{1}+yF_{2}+z\Delta;x,y,z\geq 0.$ 



Since  $\Delta\cdot F_{1}=\Delta\cdot F_{2}=F_{1}\cdot F_{2}=1$ , and by Proposition 2.1 and Proposition 2.2, there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that every integral curve  $C$  with  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$ .




On the other hand, by the following Theorem 4.1, we obtain the desired result.
∎





Based on this example, we can give more general cases for answering Question 1.1.



Definition 4.2.



Let  $X$  be a smooth projective surface. X is very friendly if every integral curve  $C$  can be written as  $C=\sum_{i}m_{i}C_{i}$  with  $m_{i}\in N_{+}$  on the  $X$  is numerical connected, where  $C_{1},\cdots,C_{\rho(X)}$  generate the  $N\acute{e}veri$  Severi group of  $X$ .




Theorem 4.1.



Let  $X$  be a smooth projective surface without integral negative curves, if X is very friendly, then  $X$  satisfies Conjecture 1.2.




Proof.



By Proposition 2.1 we know that there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that every integral curve  $C$  with  $k_{C}\leq m(X)$ .




It is enough to show that there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that every integral curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}>0$  on this surface  $X$  satisfies





 $C^{2}\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C)).$ 



Let  $\rho(X)$  be its Picard number, then





 $C=\sum_{i=1}^{\rho(X)}x_{i}C_{i},x_{i}\geq 0.$ 



There exists a positive number  $\sqrt{\frac{m(X)}{\rho(X)^{2}}}\gg\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})$  such that




if at least one of  $x_{1},\cdots,x_{\rho(X)}$  is bigger than





 $\sqrt{\frac{m(X)}{\rho(X)^{2}}},$ 



then by Serre’s vanishing theorem ([9],III. Theorem 5.2) we have





 $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=0.$ 



And by Riemann-Roch theorem we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=\frac{(1-sgn(K_{X}\cdot C)k_{C})C^{2}}{2}+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}),$ 



which implies that every curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}\geq 0,k_{C}\leq m(X)$  on this surface  $X$  satisfy  $C^{2}\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$ .
∎





By Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 2.1, Proposition 2.2 and Proposition 2.3 we have



Corollary 4.1.



Let X be the smooth projective surface with Picard number  $\rho(X)\leq 3$  and without any integral negative curves, then  $X$  satisfies conjecture 1.2.





By Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.1 we have a natural question:



Question 4.1.



Does exists a minimal smooth projective surface  $X$  with Picard number  $\rho(X)\geq 4$  and not very friendly satisfies Conjecture 1.2?






4.3. Surfaces with  $\kappa(X)=0$ 




Let  $X$  be the minimal smooth projective surface with  $\kappa(X)=0$ , then  $k_{C}=0$  for all integral curves.




By Riemann-Roch theorem, we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+\frac{C^{2}}{2},$ 



where using





 $h^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=h^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+C))=0,for\ i=1,2;$ 



by Kodaira’s vanishing theorem ([9], III. Remark 7.15, or [13], Theorem 4.2.1).



Remark 4.2.



For the smooth projective surface X not minimal and even if blow-up at one point of a smooth projective surface which satisfies Conjecture 1.2 , it is hard to confirm  $X$  satisfies Conjecture 1.2. It is related to the Bounded Negativity Conjecture (see [1],[2]). Recently, T. Bauer and his coauthors (see [3]) consider the relation between bounded negativity and arrangements of lines by the H-constant of X. They posed a problem: Is the bounded negativity property a birational invariant? We will consider the case of blow-up at n generic points of the smooth projective surface with Picard number 1 in the end of the paper.






4.4. Ruled surfaces




Let  $f:X\longrightarrow B$  be a  $\mathbb{P}^{1}$ -fibration. Set  $b=g(B)$ , then we have  $F^{2}=0,K_{X}\cdot F=-2$ .




We need some results of the ruled surfaces for the following study.



Proposition 4.3.



([9], V. Proposition 2.9, Lemma 2.10, Corollary 2.11)
Let  $f:X\longrightarrow B$  be a  $\mathbb{P}^{1}$ -fibration with a section  $C$ , then


(1) 



 $Pic(X)=f^{*}Pic(B)\oplus\mathbb{Z}C$ ,


(2) 



 $K_{X}\sim-2C+(2b-2-e)F$ ,


(3) 



 $K_{X}^{2}=8(1-b)$ ,



where  $e=-C^{2}$  and  $b=g(B)$ .




Proposition 4.4.



([9], V. Proposition 2.20)
Let X be a ruled surface with a section C over a curve B, with invariant  $e\geq 0$ .


(1) 



If  $D=a_{1}C+a_{2}F$  is an irreducible curve  $\neq C,f$ , then  $a_{1}>0,a_{2}\geq a_{1}e$ .


(2) 



A divisor  $D=a_{1}C+a_{2}F$  is ample if and only if  $a_{1}>0,a_{2}>a_{1}e$ .







Let  $D=a_{1}C+a_{2}F$  with  $a_{1}>0,a_{2}\geq a_{1}e$  be an integral curve on this  $X$ .




Note that  $D$  is nef (or ample) divisor if  $a_{2}=a_{1}e$  (or  $a_{2}>a_{1}e$ ) for





 $D^{2}=a_{1}^{2}(-e)+2a_{1}a_{2}\geq a_{1}^{2}e,D\cdot C=a_{1}(-e)+a_{2}\geq 0,D\cdot F=a_{1}\geq 0.$ 



Hence,  $X$  has only  $C$  as an integral negative curve if  $e>0$ .




Case I.  $e>0$ .




When  $a_{2}=a_{1}e$ , we have





 $k_{D}=\frac{|a_{1}(2e+1)(e-2)+2b|}{a_{1}e}\leq 2e+\frac{2b}{e},k_{C}=\frac{|e+2b-2|}{e},k_{F}=2.$ 



.
When  $a_{2}>a_{1}e,e>0$ , we have





 $\begin{split}k_{D}&=\frac{|a_{1}(2b-2+e)-2a_{2}|}{2a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}^{2}e}\\
&=\frac{|2\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}-(2b-2+e)|}{2a_{2}-a_{1}e}\\
&<\frac{|2\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}-(2b-2+e)|}{a_{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{2}{a_{1}}+\frac{|2b-2+e|}{a_{2}}.\end{split}$ 



On the other hand, we need to show that there exists a positive constant  $m(X)$  such that every integral curve  $D$  with  $D^{2}>0$  on this X with  $e>0$  such that  $D^{2}\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))$ .




Note that every integral curve  $D$  is a nef and big divisor.




If  $a_{2}\geq a_{1}e+2b+e-2$ , then  $D-K_{X}$  is a nef and big divisor.




From the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem([13], Theorem 4.3.1), we have





 $h^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=h^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+D-K_{X}))=0,for\ i=1,2.$ 



Hence,





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\chi(X)+\frac{(1-sgn(K_{X}\cdot D)k_{D})D^{2}}{2},$ 



which completes the proof of the part of that  $a_{2}\geq a_{1}e+2b+e-2$ .




If  $a_{1}e+2b+e-2>a_{2}\geq a_{1}e$ , then  $D-K_{X}$  is not an effective divisor.




Next we also use the same way to complete the part of the proof by Serre’s vanishing theorem.
There exists a positive number  $\sqrt{m(X)}\gg\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})$  such that




if at least one of  $a_{1}e,a_{2}$  is bigger than





 $\sqrt{m(X)},$ 



then





 $h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=h^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=0.$ 



And by Riemann-Roch theorem we have





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))=\frac{(1-sgn(K_{X}\cdot C)k_{C})C^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}_{X},$ 



which implies that every curve  $C$  with  $C^{2}\geq 0,k_{C}\leq m(X)$  on this surface  $X$  satisfy  $C^{2}\leq m(X)h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(C))$ .




Case II.  $e=0$ . this case is the corollary of Theorem 4.1.




Finally, we consider those ruled surfaces with  $e<0$ .



Proposition 4.5.



([9], V. Proposition 2.21)
Let X be a ruled surface over a curve C of genus g, with invariant  $e<0$ , and assume furthermore either char k =0 or  $g\leq 1$ .


(1) 



If  $D=a_{1}C+a_{2}F$  is an integral curve  $\neq C,F$ , then either  $a=1,b\geq 0$  or  $a\geq 2,b\geq\frac{1}{2}ae$ .


(2) 



A divisor  $D=a_{1}C+a_{2}F$  is ample if and only if  $a>0,b>\frac{1}{2}ae$ .







This case of the ruled surfaces in Proposition 4.4 is also the corollary of Theorem 4.1.




Now, we summarize the result of this subsection.



Theorem 4.2.



Ruled surfaces satisfy Conjecture 1.2.






4.5. Surfaces with  $\kappa(X)\geq 1$ 




Let  $X$  be a minimal projective surface with  $\kappa(X)\geq 1$  which is fiber bundle over a curve B. Note that  $X$  has Picard number 2 and not any negative integral curves. By Corollary 4.1 we can show that  $X$  satisfies Conjecture 1.1. And by the examples and Theorem 4.2 we have the following result.



Theorem 4.3.



Fiber bundles over a curve satisfy Conjecture 1.2.







5. A remark on SHGH conjecture




Let  $X$  be a smooth projective surface of general type with Picard number 1 and  $vol(X):=K_{X}^{2}$  which is the volume of  $X$ . And let  $X_{n}$  be blow-up at  $n=vol(X)-K_{X_{n}}^{2}$  generic points at  $X$ .




Firstly, we arrest that  $K_{X_{n}}$  is ample.




Let  $f:X_{n}\longrightarrow X$  be blowing up at  $vol(X)-(K_{X}^{2})$  generic points of  $X$ .




Let  $K_{X}=m(X)H,vol(X)-K_{X_{n}}^{2}=n$ , this assumption of very general positions made integral curve only (-1)-rational curves, and when  $d\gg 0$ , there exists a positive number  $l(X)$  such that the following curve  $C$  with





 $l(X)d\leq d^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}.$ 







 $K_{X_{n}}=f^{*}K_{X}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i},K_{X}\cdot E_{i}=-1.$ 



Let  $C=dH-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}$  with  $r\leq n$  be an integral negative curve on this  $X_{n}$ , then





 $K_{X}\cdot C=d\cdot m(X)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}>0.$ 



which implies when  $d\gg 0$ ,





 $\begin{split}k_{C}&=\frac{d\cdot m(X)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}^{2}}{d^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}}\\
&\leq\frac{m(X)}{l(X)}.\end{split}$ 



On the other hand, when  $d\gg 0$ ,





 $h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(C))=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}(dH))=\frac{(1-k_{C})C^{2}}{2}+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{n}}),$ 



This completes the proof.




Based this example, we have generalize SHGH conjecture as follow.



Conjecture 5.1.



Let  $X_{n}$  be blowing-up at n generic points of a smooth projective surface with Picard number 1 and  $K_{X}^{2}>0$ , then  $X_{n}$  is (-1)-special, i.e., every integral negative curve is a (-1)-rational curve.




Remark 5.1.



Based on the example and [6] we know that Conjecture 5.1 holds for  $n\leq vol(X)$ .





On the other hand, by [5], Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.3, we have the following result.



Proposition 5.1.



Conjecture 5.1 holds if and only if  $X_{n}$  has  $d(X)=1$ , i.e., every pseudo-effective divisor D on this surface X has an integral Zariski decomposition.





By Proposition 5.1 and [14], Lemma 3.1, we have the following result.



Theorem 5.1.



([14], Theorem 1.4)
Let  $X_{n}$  be blow-up at n generic points of the smooth projective surface X with Picard number 1. If there exist an integral negative curve  $C$  and another curve  $D$  on this surface  $X_{n}$  with  $C^{2}\nmid C\dot{D}$  and the intersection matrix of C and D not negative definite, then Conjecture 5.1 fails.
