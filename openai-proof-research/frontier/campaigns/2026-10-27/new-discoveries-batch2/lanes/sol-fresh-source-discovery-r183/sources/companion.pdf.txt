1. Introduction

    In extremal graph theory, we seek to determine the extremal values of graph parameters under given constraints.
The study in the field started with Turán’s Theorem in 1941 which ascertained the maximum number of edges in Ks+1 -
free graphs on n vertices. Since then, it has been one of the most studied research fields within graph theory. In 1949,
Zykov [18] found the maximum number of r-cliques, 0 ≤ r ≤ s, in Ks+1 -free graphs on n vertices. He indeed generalized
Turán’s result as edges are nothing but cliques on two vertices. In 1965, Moon [11] determined the maximum number of
maximal cliques in n-vertex graphs. In the same year, Moon together with Moser upper bounded the maximum number
of maximal independent sets [12] in n-vertex graphs. For more examples of similar results, see [5,6,13,15–17].
    Gupta, Raman, and Saurabh [7] showed in 2012 that for any fixed non-negative integer r, there exists a constant c < 2
such that every graph on n vertices has at most c n maximal r-regular induced subgraphs. The value of c for r = 0 was
already known by the aforementioned result by Moon and Moser [12]. Their result states that every graph on n vertices
has at most 3n/3 maximal independent sets and it is attained by any disjoint union of copies of the triangle graph, i.e.,
K3 . To complement this, Gupta et al. in the same article determined the values of c for r = 1 and r = 2. In particular, for
r = 1, they showed that every graph on n vertices has at most 10n/5 ≈ 1.5849n maximal induced matchings. They also
proved that the upper bound is indeed achieved by any disjoint union of copies of K5 .
    Since the extremal graphs given by Moon and Moser contain only copies of the triangle graph, it raised the question of
what if the studied graphs are triangle-free. In 1993, Hujter and Tuza [8] showed that triangle-free graphs on n vertices

have at most 2n/2 ≈ 1.4143n maximal independent sets and the bound is attained by any 1-regular graph. A decade later,
Byskov [2] gave an algorithmic proof of the same result. A similar question arises as the extremal graphs by Gupta et al. for
r = 1 contain only copies of K5 . Hence, it is interesting to discover the maximum number of maximal induced matchings
when the graphs are K5 -free. It becomes more interesting when we see that Basavaraju et al. [1], in 2015, determined
the number for the class of triangle-free graphs. Their result shows that every n-vertex triangle-free graph has at most
3n/3 ≈ 1.4423n maximal induced matchings and the upper bound is obtained by any disjoint union of copies of K3,3 .
Hence, our initial desire was to determine where in the range between 1.4423n and 1.5849n the maximum number of
maximal induced matchings of K5 -free graphs on n vertices lies.
    In Section 3, we prove that every K5 -free graph on n vertices has at most 6n/4 ≈ 1.5651n maximal induced matchings,
which is slightly smaller than the upper bound for general graphs. Furthermore, we show that this bound is tight and the
graphs with exactly 6n/4 ≈ 1.5651n maximal induced matchings are any disjoint union of copies of K4 . Obtaining only
copies of the K4 graph as the extremal graphs, together with the fact that we had the upper bounds for general graphs,
K5 -free graphs, and K3 -free graphs in hand, motivated us to investigate further and extend the result for the class of
K4 -free graphs. In Section 4, we show that every K4 -free graph on n vertices has at most 8n/5 ≈ 1.5158n maximal induced
matchings and the maximum number is attained by any disjoint union of copies of the wheel graph on 5 vertices, i.e.,
W5 .
    Our proof strategy is the same in both cases. We assume that there exist graphs on n vertices with more maximal
induced matchings than the claimed upper bound. Then, we prove a series of lemmas to discover the structural properties
of these graphs. In the end, we conclude that such graphs do not exist and thus the desired upper bound holds. The same
strategy was used in [1] to find the maximum number of maximal induced matchings in triangle-free graphs and in [10]
to find maximum induced matchings in subcubic planar graphs.
    Enumeration problems aim at outputting all solutions to their instances without duplication. Even a simple enu-
meration problem may produce a possibly exponential number of outputs. Therefore, the efficiency of the enumeration
algorithms is often evaluated by output-polynomiality. Polynomial delay algorithms spend polynomial time in the size
of the graph between the two successive outputs of an enumeration problem. It should also take polynomial time to
compute the first solution and to detect that there are no further solutions. There exist polynomial delay algorithms
to list the maximal independent sets of a given graph [9,14]. Together with the aforementioned combinatorial upper
bound by Moon and Moser, this implies that the maximal independent sets of an n-vertex graph can be listed in time
O∗ (3n/3 ) = O(1.4423n ). Then, the upper bound by Hujter and Tuza guarantees that all the maximal independent sets of
triangle-free graphs on n vertices can be listed faster and in time O∗ (2n/2 ) = O(1.4143).
    Our combinatorial upper bounds imply that algorithms listing all maximal induced matchings as their subroutine run
faster in the classes of K5 -free graphs and K4 -free graphs. Cameron [3] remarked that every induced matching in a graph G
is an independent set of the vertices in [L(G)]2 , the square graph of the line graph of G. Combining this with any algorithm
that lists maximal independent sets yields an algorithm to list maximal induced matchings. Therefore, the upper bound
by Gupta et al. for r = 1, yields polynomial delay algorithms that list all maximal induced matchings of n-vertex graphs
in time O∗ (10n/5 ) = O(1.5849n ). Then, our results promise faster polynomial delay algorithms to list all maximal induced
matchings when the input graphs are restricted to the classes of K5 -free graphs and K4 -free graphs. We present this as
corollaries of our main results, Theorems 11 and 26.

Corollary 1. For every K5 -free graph on n vertices, all its maximal induced matchings can be listed in time O∗ (6n/4 ) =
O(1.5651n ) with polynomial delay.

Corollary 2. For every K4 -free graph on n vertices, all its maximal induced matchings can be listed in time O∗ (8n/5 ) =
O(1.5158n ) with polynomial delay.

2. Definitions and notations

    All graphs we consider in this article are finite, simple, and undirected. We refer the reader to Diestel [4] for
terminologies and notations not defined in this section.
    Let G be a graph with vertex set V (G) and edge set E(G). The degree of a vertex v which is denoted by d(v ) is the number
of edges incident with v . We use δ (G) and ∆(G) to denote, respectively, the minimum and maximum degrees in G. For a
vertex v ∈ V (G), we use NG [v] and NG (v ) to denote the       ⋃ closed and open neighborhoods of v , respectively. Let A ⊆ V (G).
The closed neighborhood of A is defined as NG [A] = v∈A NG [v], and the open neighborhood of A is NG (A) = NG [A] \ A.
An induced subgraph of G is a graph whose vertex set A is a subset of V (G) and its edge set consists of all edges of G with
both endpoints in A. We write G[A] to denote the subgraph of G induced by A, and we write G − A to denote the graph
G[V (G) \ A]. If A = {v}, then we simply write G − v instead of G − {v}.
    For any non-negative integer r, we say that G is r-regular if the degree of every vertex in G is r. A 3-regular graph
is called cubic. A path Pn on n vertices is a graph whose vertices can be ordered v1 , v2 , . . . , vn such that the edges are
{v1 v2 , . . . , vn−1 vn }. A cycle Cn on n vertices is constructed by adding edge vn v1 to Pn . A complete graph is a graph in which
each pair of vertices is connected by an edge. We denote a complete graph on n vertices by Kn . The complete graph on 3
vertices is also called a triangle. A graph G is called H-free if H is not an induced subgraph of G.

    A matching in G is a subset M ⊆ E(G) such that no two edges in M share an endpoint. For a matching M in G and a
vertex v ∈ V (G), we say that M covers v if v is an endpoint of an edge in M. An induced matching M in G is a matching
whose endpoints induce a 1-regular subgraph. An induced matching M in G is maximal if there exists no induced matching
M ′ in G such that M ⊊ M ′ . We write MG to denote the set of all maximal induced matchings in G. Let X and Y be two
disjoint subsets of V (G). We define MG (X , Y ) to be the set of all maximal induced matchings of G that cover no vertex of
X and every vertex of Y . When there is no ambiguity we omit subscripts from the notations.

    Let A be a set. A partition of A is a set of p non-empty and pairwise⨄pdisjoint subsets A1 , . . . , Ap of A where A = ∪i=1 Ai .
To denote that A is partitioned into subsets A1 , . . . , Ap , we use A = i=1 Ai .
    Before we present our results on K4 -free graphs and K5 -free graphs, we prove a lemma that holds for every graph G
and results in Corollaries 4 and 12 which are widely used throughout the article.

Lemma 3. Let G be a graph and Y be a subset of vertices of G where G[Y ] is 1-regular. For every X ⊆ V (G) \ Y , it holds that
|MG (X , Y )| ≤ |MG−(X ∪N [Y ]) |.

Proof. Let us denote G − (X ∪ N [Y ]) by G′ . We first show that for every matching M ∈ MG (X , Y ), it holds that
M \ E(G[Y ]) ∈ MG′ . Let M ∈ MG (X , Y ) and M ′ = M \ E(G[Y ]). Since M is a maximal induced matching that covers all
vertices of Y and G[Y ] is 1-regular, all edges of G[Y ] belong to M. This and the fact that M is an induced matching in
G covering none of the vertices of X guarantee that M ′ is an induced matching in G′ . We show that M ′ is maximal. For
contradiction, suppose there exists an edge xy ∈ E(G′ ) such that M ′ ∪ {xy} is an induced matching in G′ . Neither x nor y
belongs to the set X ∪ N [Y ]. This means in particular that there is no edge between the sets {x, y} and Y . Hence, adding
the edge xy to M yields an induced matching in G, contradicting the assumption that M is a maximal induced matching
in G.
   Note that two sets M1 \ E(G[Y ]) and M2 \ E(G[Y ]) are not equal if M1 ̸ = M2 . Hence, it holds that |MG (X , Y )| ≤ |MG′ |
and the proof is complete. □

   Finally, we are ready to present the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 11. Every K5 -free graph on n vertices contains at most 6n/4 maximal induced matchings, and this bound is attained
by any disjoint union of copies of K4 .

Proof. So far, we have shown that G can only be isomorphic to K4 . Clearly, K4 has exactly 6 maximal induced matchings
which is equal to 6n/4 as n = |V (G)| = 4. This contradicts the assumption that G is a counterexample. Hence, there exists
no counterexample graphs and every K5 -free graph on n vertices has at most 6n/4 maximal induced matchings.

positive integer p. Every maximal induced matching in G contains exactly one edge of each connected component of G,
which implies that |MG | = 6p = 6n/4 . This completes the proof.

    By Lemma 25, if counterexample G exists it includes a degree-4 vertex u. Then, Lemma 24 guarantees that all four
neighbors of u are of degree 3 and their neighbors belong to N [u] due to Lemma 22. Combining these together with the
fact that G is K4 -free implies that our counterexample cannot be any graph other than the wheel graph on five vertices,
W5 . We are now ready to present the main theorem that upper bounds the number of maximal induced matchings in
K4 -free graphs. The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 11.

Theorem 26. Every K4 -free graph on n vertices has at most 8n/5 maximal induced matchings, and this bound is obtained by
any disjoint union of copies of W5 .

Proof. We have shown so far that G can only be isomorphic to W5 . Clearly, W5 has exactly eight maximal induced
matchings which is equal to 8n/5 as n = |V (G)| = 5. This contradicts the assumption that G is a counterexample. Hence,
there exists no counterexample and thus every K4 -free graph on n vertices has at most 8n/5 maximal induced matchings.
   It remains to show that the upper bound is best possible. Let H be the disjoint union of p copies of W5 for some positive
integer p. Every maximal induced matching in H contains exactly one edge of each connected component of H, which
implies that |MG | = 8p = 8n/5 . This completes the proof. □
