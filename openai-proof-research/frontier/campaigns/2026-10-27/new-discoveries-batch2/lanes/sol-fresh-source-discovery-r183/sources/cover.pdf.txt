                                                                                      Abstract

                                                       A graph G is a cover of a graph F if there exists an onto mapping π : V (G) →
                                                   V (F ), called a (covering) projection, such that π maps the neighbours of any
                                                   vertex v in G bijectively onto the neighbours of π(v) in F . This paper is the
                                                   first attempt to study the connection between domination parameters and graph
                                                   covers. We focus on the domination number, the total domination number, and
                                                   the connected domination number. We prove upper and lower bounds for the
                                                   domination parameters of G.


                                           Keywords: domination, total domination, connected domination, graph cover.

The following proposition illustrates some properties of covers.
Proposition 1. Let graph G be a cover of graph F via the projection π.
  (i) It holds that degG (v) = degF (π(v)) for each vertex v ∈ V (G), where degG (v) is
      the degree of a vertex v in G.
 (ii) The lift of a tree T into G consists of a collection of disjoint trees isomorphic to
      T . Hence, a cover of a tree is a forest.
(iii) The lift of a cycle Cn into G consists of a collection of disjoint cycles whose lengths
      are multiples of n.

A lot of attention has been paid to graph covers from various perspectives. As purely
graph-theoretic objects, graph covers were studied in [4, 15, 19]. Graph covers have also
appeared in the literature as “lifts” of graphs, see [2].
Let S be a subset of the vertex set of graph F . S is called a dominating set of F if
every vertex not in S is adjacent to at least one vertex in S. The domination number
of F , denoted by γ(F ), is the minimum cardinality of a dominating set of F . A total
dominating set of F is a set S such that every vertex is adjacent to some vertex in S.
Note that by the definition of a total dominating set, it is evident that a graph F admits
a total dominating set if and only if F has no isolated vertices. The total domination
number of F , denoted by γt (F ), is the minimum cardinality of a total dominating set

                                                                2
of F . A connected dominating set S of F is a set such that every vertex not in S is
adjacent with at least one vertex in S and the subgraph induced by S is connected.
The connected domination number γc (F ) is the minimum cardinality of a connected
dominating set of F .
We saw in Figure 1 that the dodecahedron is a cover of the Petersen graph. One can
check that γ(Petersen graph) = 3 and γt (Petersen graph) = γc (Petersen graph) = 4.
Moreover, γ(Dodecahedron) = 6, γt (Dodecahedron) = 8 and γc (Dodecahedron) = 10.
The theory of domination in graphs is one of the main research areas in graph theory.
For an excellent treatment of fundamentals of domination in graphs and recent topics,
we refer the reader to the following books [16–18].
One fundamental area of study in domination in graphs is how domination parameters
change under graph operations. For example, Vizing [25] studied the connection between
the domination number and the cartesian product of graphs, which led to Vizing’s
conjecture, which many believe to be the main open problem in the area of domination
in graphs. Also, see [6, 11, 14, 22, 27] for other similar research. Graph covers give an
interesting way to construct new graphs that are locally isomorphic to a given graph. As
a result, graph covers have a lot of intriguing relationships with different types of graph
parameters. For example, connections between connectivity and graph covers were
carried out in [1, 3, 20]. Also, see [2] for other interesting graph-theoretic connections.
The preceding paragraph serves as the main motivation for this work. Let graph G be
a cover of graph F . Our goal is to study the relation between γ(G) and γ(F ), γt (G)
and γt (F ), and γc (G) and γc (F ). To our knowledge, this is the first paper to discuss
the connections between domination parameters and graph covers.

Greedy Algorithm

There is an intuitive algorithm which constructs a dominating subset of vertices of a
graph F whose cardinality differs from γ(F ) by no more than, roughly, log ∆.
Given a graph F . Let S be a subset of the vertex set of F . We start with S = ∅. Until
S is a dominating set of F , add a vertex v with maximum number of undominated
neighbours. All vertices of F start as white. Anytime we add a vertex to S, we colour
that vertex black, and colour all dominated vertices grey. Note that all undominated
vertices remain white. Let w(u) be the cardinality of the white vertices among the
neighbours of u, including u itself. This idea leads to a greedy algorithm.

Algorithm 1 Greedy algorithm
  1. S := ∅
  2. while ∃ white vertices do
  3. choose v ∈ {x | w(x) = maxu∈V {(w(u)}};
  4. S := S ∪ {v};
  5. end while

2     Results

This section is devoted to establishing relations concerning the domination number, the
total domination number, and the connected domination number with respect to graph
covers. We start with the domination number.

Theorem 9. Suppose that a graph G is a k-fold cover of a graph F . Then γ(G) ⩽ kγ(F ).
Moreover, this bound is tight.

Proof. Suppose that γ(F ) = t. Let π be the projection map, and let S = {v1 , v2 , . . . , vt }
be a minimal dominating set of F such that |S| = t. We define the set S ′ = {π −1 (v) | v ∈
S}. We show that S ′ is a dominating set of G. It is sufficient to show that v ′ ∈ V (G) \ S ′
has at least one neighbour in S ′ . Suppose for a contradiction that there is v ′ ∈ V (G) \ S ′
such that v ′ has no neighbour in S ′ . Then π(v ′ ) has no neighbour in S, but S was chosen
to be a dominating set with the smallest cardinality; a contradiction. Hence v ′ has a
neighbour in S ′ and furthermore, S ′ is a dominating set. It follows that γ(G) ⩽ |S ′ | = kt.

Finally, we consider the connected domination number. Note that by definition, a graph
admits a connected dominating set if and only if the graph is connected.
We observe the following.

Observation 3.
  (i) If a connected graph G is a k-fold cover of a connected graph F , then γc (G) ⩾ k
      because γc (G) ⩾ γ(G).
 (ii) If a connected graph G is a k-fold cover of a connected graph F , then γc (G) ⩾ γc (F ).

Theorem 16. Let F and G be connected graphs. Suppose that G is a k-fold cover of F .
Then γc (G) ⩽ k(γc (F ) + 2) − 2. Moreover, this bound is tight.

Proof. Let S be a connected dominating set of F with a minimum cardinality. Then,
by the definition, the elements of S induce a connected subgraph F ′ of F . Let T ′ be a
spanning tree of F ′ . Then from Proposition 1 (ii), π −1 (T ′ ) is a disjoint union of trees of
G isomorphic to T ′ . Let T1 , . . . , Tk be the connected components of π −1 (T ′ ). Note that
the set of vertices of the subgraph ∪ki=1 Ti ⊂ G is a dominating set of G. We construct a
connected subgraph G′ of G containing the vertices of ∪ki=1 Ti and having no more than
k(γc (K) + 2) − 2 vertices (note that ∪ki=1 Ti has kγc (F ) vertices). The subgraph G′ is
constructed by induction. We start with setting G′1 = T1 and with considering a shortest
path p1 between G′1 and ∪ki=2 Ti . Up to relabelling, we can assume that the endpoint of
p1 not belonging to G′1 belongs to T2 . The path p1 cannot have more than three edges,

as otherwise it would contain a vertex not adjacent to any vertices of ∪ki=1 Ti . We define
G′2 = G′1 ∪ p1 ∪ T2 , and consider a shortest path p2 between H2 and ∪ki=3 Ti . The path

γc (G) = kn − 2 = k(n − 2 + 2) − 2, which proves that the bound is tight.

Example 17. In Figure 3, the black vertices are connected dominating set of a graph
H. So, γc (H) ⩽ 9. Without loss of generality, we consider the top horizontal line in
Figure 3. We can dominate the five vertices with exactly two vertices, and the two
vertices cannot be adjacent. Since we are forming a connected dominating set, each of
the two vertices must be adjacent to a vertex in the connected dominating set. So we
select additional two vertices on the second-top horizontal line. Again, we can dominate
the third-top horizontal line with two vertices and they are not adjacent. So, we have to
select their common adjacent vertex to be in the connected dominating set. Finally, we
dominate the bottom horizontal line with two vertices. Thus, γc (H) = 9. Let a graph
G be the 5 × 8 grid with an edge connecting every pair ui , vi , for i = 1, 2, . . . , 5, where
ui , vi (for i = 1, 2, . . . , 5) are the vertices of the opposite ends of the 5 × 8 grid, and
ui , vi lie on the same straight line in the grid. Then G is a 2-fold cover of graph H. The
connected domination number of the 5 × 8 grid is 17 (see [13]) and hence γc (G) ⩽ 17.
But 17 < 2 × 9 = 18. So in general, it is not true that γc (G) ⩾ kγc (F ) if G is a k-fold
cover of F , where G and F are connected graphs.



3     Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated how three domination parameters behave under graph
covers. We hope that another major direction of research on domination in graphs

is suggested by the results. Furthermore, our results suggest that the problem of
characterising graphs that achieve equality in any of the upper bounds would be
interesting.
