      SINGLE PEAKS IN TRUNCATED STIELTJES INTEGRAL EQUATIONS

                                                ALEX CHENGYU LI


       Abstract. We study maxima of curves deﬁned by normalized, truncated Stieltjes integrals. For
       a positive proﬁle h, strict decrease of its elasticity xh0 (x)/h(x) on its declining side gives a unique
       nondegenerate maximum at every level for which the curve exists. This proves a uniqueness con-
       jecture of Viakhirev and makes its additional existence hypotheses unnecessary. We treat every
       positive power of the kernel, on a ﬁnite interval or the positive half-line: all positive levels occur for
       powers at least one, whereas a uniquely maximized boundary integral gives the exact level threshold
       for smaller powers. We determine the movement and limiting positions of the peak. For log-normal
       densities, the normalized polynomial cutoﬀs proposed by Viakhirev have maximizing locations and
       heights converging at rate O(R−1 ) to limits independent of the cutoﬀ exponent. In contrast, smooth
       strictly unimodal proﬁles can produce arbitrarily many critical points.

integrand has a single peak. Consider a probability density ρ and the equation

The quantity of interest is the dimensionless ratio β(m) = C(m)/m. Viakhirev derives its derivative
and proves the existence of a critical point under an additional sign-change hypothesis. His Con-
jecture 10 asserts that this point is unique when h(x) = xρ(x) is positive and strictly log-concave
[6]. The same paper asks whether the maximizing cutoﬀs obtained by polynomially truncating a
log-normal density converge as the truncation range increases [6].
   We prove the conjecture and the cutoﬀ limit. The main observation is a boundary identity for
the derivative of a dilation average. After division by h(m), its sign is controlled by an average of
the ratios h(mt)/h(m), 0 < t < 1. These ratios increase strictly after the mode under a one-sided
condition on the elasticity xh0 (x)/h(x). Every stationary point is consequently a strict downward
crossing of the derivative. Treating the maximum as a function of the kernel parameter then resolves
the implicit equation at every positive level.
   This argument also determines the position of the peak. It lies strictly between the mode of h and
the maximizer of its running average; for powers at least one, both bounds are attained as limits
when the prescribed level varies. For powers between zero and one, an integrable boundary kernel
instead determines the largest admissible level and the limiting peak at that level. The construction
extends to the half-line, where it applies to log-normal proﬁles although their ordinary logarithm
is eventually convex. We conclude with an explicit three-critical-point example and then a family
with arbitrarily many critical points, all with a strictly unimodal proﬁle. These constructions show
that a single mode imposes no ﬁnite bound on the critical-point count.
   There is a classical connection between concavity and preservation of unimodality. Ibragimov
characterizes densities whose convolution with every unimodal density is unimodal by log-concavity
[2]. The multiplicative counterpart of Cuculescu and Theodorescu is recalled by Simon in terms
of concavity of s 7→ log f (es ) [5]. In logarithmic coordinates, our ﬁxed-parameter integral is also a
convolution with a one-sided decreasing kernel. The arguments below use only the declining side of
