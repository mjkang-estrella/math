                                           Abstract
                                           Determining the maximum number of crossing limit cycles in planar discon-
                                           tinuous piecewise linear systems separated by a straight line is a central prob-
                                           lem in nonsmooth dynamical systems and a natural piecewise-linear counter-
                                           part of Hilbert’s sixteenth problem, yet its sharp uniform upper bound has
                                           long remained an open problem. In this paper, we prove that any such system
                                           admits at most three crossing limit cycles. Combined with known examples
                                           realizing three crossing limit cycles, this result establishes that three is the
                                           sharp uniform upper bound.
                                           Keywords: piecewise linear system, Crossing limit cycle, Poincaré
                                           half-return map, Hilbert’s 16th problem
                                           2000 MSC: 34C05, 34C07, 34A36


                                           1. Introduction
                                              The second part of Hilbert’s sixteenth problem concerns the number of
                                           limit cycles of planar polynomial diﬀerential systems. More precisely, for
                                           each positive integer n, it asks whether there exists a uniform upper bound
                                           H(n) on the number of limit cycles of such systems of degree n. The same
                                           question has received considerable attention for planar piecewise linear sys-
                                           tems, motivated in part by applications in electrical circuits and mechanics.
                                           Although a planar linear system has no limit cycles, a piecewise linear system
                                           may have several ones. We consider planar piecewise linear systems with two

    For continuous systems, the Lum–Chua conjecture [3] asserts that there
is at most one limit cycle, which was proved by Freire, Ponce, Rodrigo, and
Torres [4], and subsequently by Carmona, Fernández-Sánchez, and Novaes [5].
    For discontinuous systems, Han and Zhang [6] constructed two limit cycles
and conjectured that two is the maximum. Huan and Yang [7] found a nu-
merical three-cycle example, whose existence was rigorously proved by Llibre
and Ponce [8]. Further three-cycle constructions and examples were studied
in [9, 10, 11], while upper-bound questions under additional structural hy-
potheses were investigated in [12, 13, 14]. For the unrestricted two-zone class,
Carmona, Fernández-Sánchez, and Novaes [15] established a uniform upper
bound of eight crossing limit cycles, which is not sharp.
    Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1. Any planar discontinuous piecewise linear system (1.1) with
two zones separated by a straight line has at most three crossing limit cycles.

    Together with the three-cycle examples in [8, 9], Theorem 1.1 shows that
three is the sharp uniform upper bound.
    The remainder of this article is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces
the Poincaré half-return maps, establishes their basic analytical properties,
and classiﬁes the parameter regimes of the Liénard canonical form. Section 3
derives the matching equation whose isolated solutions correspond to crossing
limit cycles, and introduces an auxiliary function Y to bound the number of
its solutions. Finally, Section 4 completes the proof of Theorem 1.1 across
each parameter regime.
