                                                                                           Abstract
                                                    Let R = {r + jm : j ≥ 0} and S = {s + jm : j ≥ 0} be disjoint arithmetic progressions of
                                                positive integers, and choose a partition of n uniformly at random. We determine the limiting
                                                probability that the number of parts in R is larger than the number of parts in S. More precisely,
                                                if a = r/m and b = s/m, then this probability converges to I1/2 (a, b), the regularized incom-
                                                plete beta function. We prove the stronger distributional statement that, after multiplication
                                                by the partition saddle-point parameter, the difference of the two numbers of parts converges
                                                to m−1 (log Gb − log Ga ), where Ga and Gb are independent gamma variables. The proof uses
                                                the Boltzmann conditioning device for random partitions, a q-gamma evaluation of the uncon-
                                                ditioned characteristic function, and a uniform Fourier estimate showing that conditioning on
                                                the total size does not change the limit. We also evaluate the finite-truncation limit studied by
                                                Li and Zhao. The two evaluations coincide, proving their conjecture on the commutation of the
                                                truncation and large-size limits.

malization because the finite-truncation input used below is their finite-set theorem, whose marked
parts must have greatest common divisor one. Indeed, for every N ≥ 2,

                                 gcd(RN ∪ SN ) = gcd(r, s, m) = 1,

so the cited theorem applies to all sufficiently large N ; the finitely many smaller values of N do
not affect the limit N → ∞. The unrestricted Boltzmann argument itself only depends on r, s, m
through a = r/m and b = s/m, but the commutation statement is made under the normalization
above.

trigonometric-sum estimate makes the complementary arcs negligible.

records consequences.

3     The Boltzmann model and the unconditioned limit
3.1   Independent multiplicities and exact conditioning
We begin by embedding the uniform measure on partitions of a fixed integer into a one-parameter
family of product measures. This is often called the Boltzmann measure, or the conditioning device
for partitions. A probability measure on P assigns nonnegative weights, of total mass 1, to the
possible partitions. In the Boltzmann model the weight decreases exponentially with the size;
the auxiliary parameter t controls how strongly large partitions are suppressed. Fix t > 0, write
ρk = e−tk , and, independently for each k ≥ 1, let Xk have the geometric distribution

4     Conditioning on the total size
Proposition 3.2 concerns a random partition whose total size fluctuates. We now show that the
same limit holds after conditioning on T = n, provided t = tn . This is the only place where a local,
rather than global, limit estimate is required. The distinction matters: weak convergence (another
name for convergence in distribution) describes probabilities of intervals on the fluctuation scale,
whereas the conditioning event {T = n} is a single lattice point whose probability tends to zero. A
local limit estimate supplies the finer approximation to this individual point probability.

7.4   Extensions
The preceding arguments also suggest extensions, but we record them only as remarks rather than
as additional theorems. If finitely many new fixed part sizes are added to either progression, the
finite-dimensional limit in Proposition 7.3 suggests that the limiting variable is augmented by a
finite sum of independent exponential variables. If fixed part sizes already present in a progression
are removed, one must instead use the joint convergence in Proposition 7.3; the removed coordinates
need not be independent of the remaining coordinates. In either case, the corresponding conditional
estimate can be obtained by repeating the finite-marking argument there.
    More generally, a signed statistic formed from several residue classes modulo the same m has
an unconditioned characteristic function that is a finite product of q-Pochhammer quotients. A
rigorous extension would need to state the coefficient-balance condition under which the powers
of 1 − q cancel and to repeat the uniform Fourier estimates. In the present two-progression case
this balance is automatic, and the limiting comparison reduces to two gamma variables whose
normalized ratio is beta distributed.
    Two natural refinements remain. First, quantitative versions of the Fourier estimates should
yield a rate of convergence in (6). Second, allowing the modulus or the number of marked residue
classes to vary with n would require uniform forms of both the q-gamma asymptotic and the
deconditioning estimate. These questions lie beyond the fixed-parameter regime considered here.
Remark 7.4 (On the coprimality normalization). The condition gcd(r, s, m) = 1 is retained in the
statement of the main theorem only because the finite-set theorem of Li and Zhao used in Propo-
sition 2.1 assumes that the marked part sizes have greatest common divisor one. The Boltzmann,
q-gamma, and conditioning arguments for the unrestricted partition model do not use this con-
dition. For the present truncations the full allowed set is {1, . . . , MN }, whose greatest common



                                                       25
divisor is always one, even when gcd(r, s, m) > 1. Thus a general lattice-point version of the finite-
set theorem, formulated under the weaker hypothesis gcd(A) = 1 for the full allowed set A, should
remove the normalization and leave the same beta constant. Since that generalized finite-set lemma
is not proved here, we keep the normalization in the formal theorem rather than silently enlarging
the range of the cited result.
