                                                                              1. Introduction
                                             Let X be a Banach space consisting of complex-valued functions on a set Ω.
                                           For a self-map φ of Ω, the composition operator Cφ is defined as
                                                                         Cφ (f ) = f ◦ φ for f ∈ X.
                                           Composition operators have been extensively investigated on various Banach
                                           spaces of holomorphic functions over the past half century. The main subject
                                           in the study of composition operators is to describe operator theoretic properties
                                           of Cφ in terms of function theoretic properties of φ (see [2, 6, 7, 12, 15]).
                                              One central problem in the study of composition operators lies in characterizing
                                           the compactness of these operators as well as the compactness of their differences.
                                           Shapiro [13] established a full characterization for the compactness of composi-
                                           tion operators acting on Hardy spaces over the unit disk. MacCluer and Shapiro
                                           [9] derived necessary and sufficient conditions for compact composition operators
                                           on weighted Bergman spaces in terms of angular derivatives. In [14], Wulan et
                                           al. provides a criterion for compactness of composition operator Cφ in terms of
                                           the vanishing behaviour of the norms of the powers of the symbol function φ on
                                           BMOA and Bloch spaces. Moorhouse [10] characterized compact differences of
                                           composition operators on weighted Bergman spaces by the Julia-Carathéodory
                                           type condition and found that two noncompact composition operators can induce
                                           compact differences of composition operators, which is called Moorhouse cancel-
                                           lation property. In [4, 5] this characterization was extended to higher dimensional
                                           polydisks and balls.

   In recent years, numerous papers have investigated the compactness of com-
position operators defined on discrete structures including trees. Allen, Colonna,
and Easley [1] obtained a characterization for the compactness of composition
operators acting on the Lipschitz space of a tree. In [8] Liu and Kong charac-
terized compact differences of composition operators on the Lipschitz space of a
tree and further verified that the Moorhouse cancellation property holds on this
Lipschitz space. Muthukumar and Ponnusamy [11] studied the compactness of
composition operators acting on Hardy spaces of homogeneous rooted trees and
obtained a sufficient condition for such operators to be compact. Motivated by
the above results, we pose the following problem:
Problem 1.1. Find necessary and sufficient conditions that characterize the com-
pactness of composition operators acting on Hardy spaces of the homogeneous
rooted trees.
   The main purpose of the present paper is to establish a complete characteriza-
tion for compact linear combinations of a finite number of composition operators
acting on Hardy spaces of homogeneous rooted trees. As an immediate corollary,
we resolve the aforementioned problem.
   The paper is organized as follows. We refer to Section 2 for some preliminaries
on trees and the definitions of Hardy spaces of homogeneous rooted trees. In
Section 3, we characterize the compactness of arbitrary finite linear combinations
of composition operators acting on these Hardy spaces. As a direct consequence of
our main result, we settle Problem 1.1. Furthermore, we obtain a characterization
of the compactness of linear combinations satisfying the CNC and confirm a
conjecture due to Choe et al. In Section 4, we construct two concrete examples
to illustrate the phenomenon of (double) difference cancellation on Hardy spaces
over homogeneous rooted trees.
                                   2. preliminaries
   A tree is a locally finite connected and simply connected graph T , which, as a
set, we identify with the collection of its vertices. Two vertices u and v are said
to be neighbors if there is an edge connecting them, and we adopt the notation
u ∼ v. The degree of a vertex is the number of its neighbors. A tree is said
to be k-homogeneous if every vertex has degree exactly k. A path between two
vertices u and w is a finite sequence of distinct vertices [u, u1 , u2 , . . . , uk , w] such
that u ∼ u1 ∼ u2 ∼ · · · ∼ uk ∼ w. Since T is a tree, the path between two
vertices is unique.
   Given a tree T rooted at o and a vertex u ∈ T , a vertex v ̸= u is called a
descendant of u if u belongs to the unique path from o to v. In this case, u
is called an ancestor of v. We write v − for the parent of v, namely the unique
neighbor of v which is an ancestor of v. The vertex v is called a child of v − . The
length of a path between two vertices u and w is the number of edges connecting
them. The distance d(u, w) between vertices u and w is the length of the path
connecting u to w. In particular, the length of a path joining the root o to a
vertex w is called the length of w and is denoted by |w|. A function on a tree is
a complex-valued function on the set of its vertices.
