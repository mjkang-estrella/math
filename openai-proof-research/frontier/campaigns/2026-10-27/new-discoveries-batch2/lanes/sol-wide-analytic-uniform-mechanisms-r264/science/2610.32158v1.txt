                                                Abstract. We simplify and improve OpenAI’s proof of the propeller conjecture of Khot
                                                and Naor from 2008: the sum of the squared lengths of the Gaussian moments of any
                                                partition of Euclidean space is at most 9/(8π). We show equality only occurs for three 120
                                                degree sectors in the plane (up to symmetries, measure zero changes, and taking orthogonal
                                                cross products with Euclidean space). We also remove computer assistance from the three-

   The geometric form of Theorem 1.3 is closely related to the Standard Simplex Conjecture
[IM12], which has been verified for a majority of correlation parameters in [Hei26] for three
sets, although the four set and higher cases remain open. The principal difference is that the
Standard Simplex Conjecture constrains the measures of the partition elements, whereas the
Propeller problem does not. (Also [IM12] concerns noise stability, a generalization of F (A).)
   The probabilistic form of Theorem 1.3 is similar to but distinct from the recently resolved
Simplex Mean Width Conjecture [Mul26], and the Sudakov-Fernique inequality [Sud71,
Sud76, Fer75]. In particular, the variance constraints are different, e.g. the former says:
let n ≥ 2 and let (g1 , . . . , gn ) be a mean zero Gaussian random vector with Egi2 = 1 ∀
1 ≤ i ≤ n. Then E maxi=1,...,n gi is largest when Egi gj = −1/(n − 1) for all 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n.
   The complexity theoretic form of Theorem 1.3 originally assumed the Unique Games
Conjecture. Since that conjecture is now proven [Ope26], we do not need it as an assumption.
This optimization problem, known as the kernel clustering problem from machine learning,
originated in [SSG+07].

1.1. Sketch of the Proof of the Main Theorem. The proof proceeds in two steps.
     (i) If A maximizes F in Rn , then A must be a partition with at most four sets of
         positive measure. Using known reductions from [KN08], this implies that we may
         assume A ⊆ R3 . This is proven in Lemma 7.1.
    (ii) In R3 there are only two nondegenerate critical points of F with four sets of positive
         measure (up to rotations, measure zero changes, and relabeling the sets), and both
         critical points satisfy F (A) < 9/(8π). This is proven in Section 3. (Here nondegen-
         erate means that the span of the vectors z1 , . . . , z4 has dimension 3.)
4                                          STEVEN HEILMAN

Steps (i) and (ii) prove Theorem 1.1, since they together imply that CN ≤ C4 for N > 4.
   The proof of (i) uses a sequence of inequalities that must hold for maximizing critical
points. The argument of [HJN13] used a similar strategy, although some parts of [HJN13]
were specific to R3 and the strategy was used differently.
   The proof of (ii) begins with the reformulated critical point condition [HJN13, Lemma
3.14, Corollary 3.15], itself inspired by rubber band configurations on the sphere [Lee57,
Con82, LLW88, CEK+10]. We then completely classify both nondegenerate critical points
for four sets in R3 . The proof uses some ideas from [LV14, Theorem 3.2].
1.2. Future Directions. Theorem 1.1 asserts that C(Ik ) = C(I3 ) for all k ≥ 3, where Ik
is the k × k identity matrix. In other words, the computation of C(Ik ) reduces to a lower-
dimensional optimization problem. If B is close to the identity matrix, it should be the case
that C(B) also reduces to a lower-dimensional optimization problem. Making this statement
precise with a good notion of closeness remains an open problem.
   The measure-constrained version of the Propeller Problem still seems out of reach of
current methods. This problem asks to maximize F (A) in Rn subject to the constraint that
γn (Ai ) = 1/N for all 1 ≤ i ≤ N . The case N = n + 1 is most interesting.
   The proof of the three-dimensional case of the Propeller Problem demonstrates that the
nondegenerate critical points of F are identical to the nondegenerate critical points of four
points on the sphere S 2 connected by rubber bands [HJN13, Lemma 3.14, Corollary 3.15].
Lemma 3.1 gives a complete classification of these nondegenerate critical points. It would
be interesting to try to generalize this result to at least n + 1 points on the sphere S n−1 .
1.3. Statement on AI Use. GPT 6 was used to prepare this manuscript, which is based
upon the solution of the Propeller Conjecture from [Ope26]. Before the release of [Ope26],
we had a somewhat different proof of Theorem 1.1 that was longer and instead relied on the
Gaussian isoperimetric inequality and a second variation argument.

import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

r = np.linspace(0, 2/3, 1001)

q = norm.ppf(P)
lam = norm.pdf(q)/P
U = np.r_[1.0, lam*(q + lam)]

Delta = ((9/(8*np.pi))/(1 + np.sqrt(1-a*a)) - U*(0.415 + 0.15*b)**2)
