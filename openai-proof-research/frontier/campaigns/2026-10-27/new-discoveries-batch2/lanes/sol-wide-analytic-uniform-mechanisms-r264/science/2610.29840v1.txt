                                                                   nonlinear extensions

                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                       We give a simpler proof of Nadirashvili’s theorem (Rayleigh’s conjecture) that among vibrating
                                                   clamped plates of prescribed area in the plane, the disk has the lowest fundamental tone. The
                                                   proof uses a one-shot Talenti type comparison for sign-changing solutions of the Poisson equation
                                                   with mean-zero data, established in a companion paper. The comparison considers just two
                                                   rearranged sources, rather than optimizing over all admissible sources as in the traditional two-
                                                   ball technique. This “paired comparison” simplification yields immediate nonlinear extensions:
                                                   the disk minimizes the clamped quotient ∥∆u∥q / ∥u∥p among bounded open sets of prescribed
                                                   area, for every 1 ≤ p, q ≤ ∞. Equality is characterized for q > 1, while exceptional behavior at
                                                   q = 1 is identified. In higher dimensions, we prove ball minimality for p = ∞ and conjecture
                                                   minimality for all p, q within the compact embedding range. We also establish the corresponding
                                                   interval inequality in one dimension.


                                           1       Introduction and main results
                                           Which vibrating clamped plate of prescribed area has the lowest fundamental tone? Rayleigh
                                           conjectured the disk is the minimizer, which Nadirashvili [23] then proved more than a century
                                           later. We give a significantly simpler planar proof and show that the disk remains optimal when the
                                           L2 norms in the variational formulation are replaced by Lp and Lq norms, for every 1 ≤ p, q ≤ ∞.
                                               Nadirashvili’s proof and the proof of Ashbaugh and Benguria [2] in dimensions two and three use
                                           the two-ball method: Talenti’s elliptic rearrangement [28] is applied on the nodal domains, followed
                                           by minimization over all admissible sources on the coupled balls. Our paired comparison theorem [9]
                                           reduces this optimization to just two source choices: the signed rearrangements of the original
                                           mean-zero source f = −∆u and its negative, each on the equal-area disk. One of the resulting
                                           potentials has Lp norm at least as large as for u, while the Lq norm of the Laplacian is preserved.
                                           This simplified approach applies to all p and q, without using coupled nonlinear Euler–Lagrange
                                           equations that in the nonlinear framework would raise significant difficulties.

Disk minimality for all p, q
Theorem 1 (Disk minimality for all exponents). If Ω ⊂ R2 is a nonempty bounded open set and
1 ≤ p, q ≤ ∞, then
                                    Γp,q (Ω) ≥ Γp,q (Ω∗ ).                               (3)
    When q > 1, equality holds in (3) if and only if Ω is a disk. When q = 1 and 1 ≤ p < ∞,
equality holds if Ω is a disk with a set of H 1 -capacity zero removed. When q = 1 and p = ∞, both
sides of (3) equal zero.

    Figure 1 depicts the parameter square in (1/p, 1/q) coordinates. Section 3 proves inequality (3)
and Sections 7.2 and 7.3 treat equality for q > 1 and q = 1, respectively. We do not know whether
the equality statement when q = 1 and 1 ≤ p < ∞ can be strengthened to “if and only if”.
    Several cases of the theorem were known previously, notably Rayleigh’s vibrating clamped plate
case (p, q) = (2, 2) proved by Nadirashvili [23]. Our proof of this case of Theorem 1 relies on L2
paired comparison, the proof of which is particularly transparent [9, Section 4], hence our claim of a
“simpler planar proof” for Rayleigh’s conjecture.

In one dimension, ∆u means u′′ . Closing up the gaps between components of the domain proves
interval minimality for all parameters p and q as shown in Section 5, with strict inequality for
disconnected sets later proved in Section 7.5.

Proposition 4 (Interval minimality for all p, q). Let Ω ⊂ R be a nonempty bounded open set, so
that Ω∗ is an interval of the same length. If 1 ≤ p, q ≤ ∞ then

                                              Γp,q (Ω) ≥ Γp,q (Ω∗ ).                                 (5)

Equality holds if and only if Ω is an interval.

    The interval eigenvalue has a positive minimizer u that is symmetric about the midpoint and
strictly decreasing toward each endpoint, when 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ and 1 < q ≤ ∞. Every minimizer is a
nonzero constant multiple of u. We omit the proofs. For 1 < p, q < ∞, interval uniqueness also
follows from Benedikt [6, Theorem 8, Lemma 24 and Example 18]. For p = q > 1, Benedikt also
proved that the first clamped eigenfunction has one sign [7, Theorem 1.1]; midpoint symmetry then
follows from reflection and simplicity.

Prior work on Rayleigh’s conjecture
The history of Rayleigh’s conjecture centers around the difficulty of sign-changing fundamental
modes. Szegő proved the inequality under the additional assumption that the fundamental mode
has one sign [26, 27]. Talenti introduced the two-ball nodal domain rearrangement method to avoid
that assumption [29]. Nadirashvili completed the planar proof [23], and Ashbaugh and Benguria
established the result in dimensions two and three [2]. In dimensions four and higher, Ashbaugh and
Laugesen obtained quantitative lower bounds that approach the conjectured sharp constant as the
dimension increases [5]. Leylekian obtained sufficient conditions for Rayleigh’s conjecture in higher
dimensions [21]. Ashbaugh, Benguria and Laugesen [3] survey these plate results and the related
buckling problem. For broader background, see Ashbaugh’s survey [1] on isoperimetric eigenvalue
inequalities and the monograph of Gazzola, Grunau and Sweers [12] on biharmonic problems and
positivity.
    Beyond Euclidean space, Kristály proved sharp Rayleigh comparisons in hyperbolic space for
plates of sufficiently small volume in dimensions two and three [18], and for spherical plates of
arbitrary size in those dimensions or sufficiently large size in higher dimensions [19]. Kristály and
Mondino obtained related inequalities, with rigidity and stability results, on certain nonsmooth
spaces with synthetic nonnegative Ricci curvature [20]. Chasman and Langford proved a nonsharp
analogue in Gauss space for the bi-Hermite operator [10].
