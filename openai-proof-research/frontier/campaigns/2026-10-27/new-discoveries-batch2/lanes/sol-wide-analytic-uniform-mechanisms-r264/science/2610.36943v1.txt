                                                    Abstract. We characterize when a de Branges–Rovnyak space H(b) coincides

                                                                                1. Introduction

                                           H(b) coincides (with equivalence of norms) with a harmonically weighted Dirichlet

   The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 contains the main defi-
nitions and preliminaries. Sections 3 and 4 contain the proofs of Theorems 1.2 and
1.3, respectively. Section 5 collects some remarks. The remaining three sections
are devoted to examples. Notably, we show that the equality H(b) = D(µ) can
hold also for (singular) measures supported on uncountable sets. In Section 6, we
show that if µ is in the class of d-Ahlfors-David regular measures (0 < d < 1), then

the Cantor measure, whose support is uncountable and has no atoms. In Section 7
we consider purely atomic measures, with isolated atoms, whose support may nev-

at accumulation points of other atoms. In each case we show that the associated
Dirichlet space can coincide with a de Branges–Rovnyak space.

   These spaces can be very difficult to treat from a functional point of view. The
structure of H(b) heavily depends on the properties of the function b, and in gen-
eral de Branges–Rovnyak spaces may display very different behaviors. One nice
approach to the theory of H(b) is through the notion of extremality. Recall that
b is non-extreme if log(1 − |b|2 ) ∈ L1 (T, m). The non-extremality of b is equiva-
lent to the fact that polynomials are dense in H(b). This explains why, whenever
H(b) = D(µ), then necessarily b is non-extreme.

a(0) > 0, such that
                   |a|2 + |b|2 = 1         m-almost everywhere on T.
The function a is called the Pythagorean mate of b, and we refer to (b, a) as a
Pythagorean pair.

spaces. In particular, when the Pythagorean pair (b, a) satisfies it, the space H(b)

coincides with the range of the co-analytic Toeplitz operator Tā , that is,

Lemma 5.1. Let b ∈ H1∞ and let a be its Pythagorean mate. If a + b = 1 or
b−1 ∈ H ∞ , then H(b) = M(a) with equivalent norms.
Proof. It is known that M(a) is contractively contained in H(b) [26, Lemma 3].
Conversely, f ∈ H(b) if and only if f ∈ H 2 and Tb f = Ta f + for some f + ∈ H 2 .
Let f ∈ H(b).
      • If a + b = 1, then f = Ta f + Tb f = Ta (f + f + ) ∈ M(a).
      • If b−1 ∈ H ∞ , then Tb is invertible with inverse T1/b , and co-analytic Toeplitz
        operators commute, so f = T1/b Tb f = T1/b Ta f + = Ta T1/b f + ∈ M(a).
Hence H(b) = M(a) as sets. The inclusion M(a) ,→ H(b) is then a bounded
bijection between Hilbert spaces, so its inverse is bounded by the open mapping
theorem, and the norms are equivalent.                                        □
    Both hypotheses of Lemma 5.1 imply the Corona condition: if a + b = 1 then
|a| + |b| ≥ |a + b| = 1, and if b−1 ∈ H ∞ then |b| ≥ 1/∥b−1 ∥∞ . The proof above is a
direct argument that does not use this characterization.

f ≲ g we mean that |f (z)| ≤ C|g(z)| uniformly in z in a specific set, that will be
clear from the context. In particular, f ≍ g if and only if f ≲ g and g ≲ f . Finally,
f+ = max{f, 0}.
