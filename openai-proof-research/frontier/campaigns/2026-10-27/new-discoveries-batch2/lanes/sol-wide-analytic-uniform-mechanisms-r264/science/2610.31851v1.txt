                                                                                       1. Introduction
                                              The Bessis–Moussa–Villani (BMV) conjecture arose in quantum statistical mechanics [2].
                                           In its coefficient formulation [14], it asks for the nonnegativity of symmetrized word traces
                                           in two positive semidefinite matrices. Stahl proved the conjecture [17]. A later refinement
                                           proposed, in particular, that the normalized trace average An,m (A, B) over all words with n
                                           letters A and m letters B should be bounded above by Tr(An B m ). Counterexamples in [5]
                                           show that this upper bound is false. For us, the failure raised a further question: what makes
                                           these counterexamples possible, and is there a natural comparison that remains valid?
                                              The first author studied this question in [6]. The key issue is that the clustered word
                                           Tr(An B m ) need not capture the common commuting part of the matrices. Contributions
                                           from off-diagonal spectral blocks can connect different eigenspaces, and their arrangement
                                           in mixed words can defeat the clustered bound. This suggested keeping the part   P

                                           commutes with A and measuring the contribution of what is discarded. If A = α∈spec A αPα ,
                                           the natural map is the spectral pinching

   An important guide was the work of Otte Heinävaara [12]. He constructed a positive tracial
joint spectral measure for every Hermitian pair (A, B). Through an integral transform, this
measure represents trace functionals of the pencil xA + yB and leads, among other results,
to the classical BMV theorem and to Lp models for two-dimensional Schatten geometry. We
studied this approach because it replaces many separate trace inequalities by a single positive
measure. However, (1) concerns the difference between the pencils xA + yB and xA + yEA (B).
The existence of a positive measure for each pencil does not imply that their difference is
positive. The representation in [12] therefore does not, by itself, establish the corrected
conjecture. We needed a measure adapted to conditional expectation, rather than to a pair of
matrices alone.

   The relation with Heinävaara’s theory is also more precise at this level. His tracial joint
spectral measure describes trace geometry of a Hermitian pair. Our measure describes the
relative second-order loss when one member of the pair is replaced by its pinching. They
are different objects, not two descriptions of the same measure. Nevertheless, for positive
semidefinite matrices, the finite comparison measure obtained from Heinävaara’s integral
representation is a positive radial transform of ζA,B (Theorem 5.1). This identifies a connection
between the two theories while making clear what additional positivity has to be proved for
the pinching comparison.
