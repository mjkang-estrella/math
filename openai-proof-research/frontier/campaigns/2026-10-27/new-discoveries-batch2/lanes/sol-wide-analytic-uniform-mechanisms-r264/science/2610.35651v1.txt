                                                INDEX AND NULLITY OF CRITICAL SPHERICAL CATENOIDS
                                                           IN HYPERBOLIC THREE-SPACE

                                                                     IVAN DOROFEEV AND ALEXANDER PIGAZZINI


                                                   Abstract. We prove that every critical spherical catenoid in a geodesic ball of hyperbolic
                                                   three-space has Morse index four and nullity two. This resolves Medvedev’s index conjecture
                                                   throughout the parameter range of the catenoid family. The kernel consists precisely of
                                                   the normal components of rotations that preserve the center of the ball and tilt the axis of
                                                   the catenoid. As a consequence, the radius of the supporting ball increases strictly along

                                                   the family. The proof combines a global boundary inequality obtained from a Wronskian,
                                                   a positive Dirichlet eigenfunction supplied by the time component of the unit normal, and
                                                   a Riccati comparison for the angular mode k = 2. A ground-state identity then yields
                                                   positivity of every angular mode k ≥ 2.




                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                              The critical catenoid is a basic example in the study of the Morse index of free boundary
                                           minimal surfaces. In the Euclidean unit ball in R3 , its index is four, as proved by Devyver
                                           [3], Smith and Zhou [11], and Tran [12]; another proof was given by Medvedev [6]. Devyver
                                           also proved that every compact orientable free boundary minimal surface in this ball, other
                                           than a flat disk, has index at least four [3]. Thus the critical catenoid attains this lower
                                           bound.
                                              The analogous index problem in curved space forms concerns critical spherical catenoids.
                                           In H3 , these are rotational minimal annuli obtained by free boundary truncation of the
                                           spherical catenoids constructed by Mori [8]. The geometry of rotational hypersurfaces in
                                           spaces of constant curvature was developed further by do Carmo and Dajczer [1], and the
                                           free boundary truncation was considered by Li and Xiong [4]. Lima [5] proved that an
                                           embedded free boundary minimal annulus in a geodesic ball of S3+ or H3 that is invariant
                                           under the geodesic symmetry about the center of the ball is congruent to a critical rotational
                                           annulus; in H3 , these are the critical spherical catenoids. Medvedev [7] proved that the
                                           critical spherical catenoid in a geodesic ball of S3+ has index four, and that its hyperbolic
                                           counterpart has index at least four. He conjectured equality in the hyperbolic case. We
                                           prove this equality throughout the parameter range of the critical spherical catenoid family
                                           in H3 and determine the nullity.
                                              The analysis in [10] determines the index and nullity contributions of the angular mode
                                           |k| = 1 for every parameter. The local result in [9] establishes index four and nullity two
                                           when the parameter is sufficiently close to its lower endpoint. The theorem below establishes
                                           both equalities for every a > 12 .
                                           Theorem 1. For each a > 12 , let Σa be the critical spherical catenoid with angular period
                                           2π, parametrized in Section 2, and let B(r(a)) ⊂ H3 be its supporting geodesic ball. Then
                                                                              ind(Σa ) = 4,         nul(Σa ) = 2.
                                           The Robin kernel is generated by the normal components of the two rotations that tilt the
                                           axis of the catenoid while fixing the center of B(r(a)).

   Here the index and nullity refer to the second variation of area for normal variations
whose boundary remains on the supporting sphere. In particular, the ambient ball is fixed
when taking variations.
   The theorem supplies the global hyperbolic counterpart of the Euclidean index-four results
and identifies all infinitesimal degeneracies with rotations of the ambient ball. At fixed
ambient curvature −1, both the geometry of the annulus and its supporting radius vary
with a. The associated Jacobi operator contains the curvature term +2, and the Robin
coefficient is coth r(a); these terms are recorded in Section 3. The proof controls this family
of boundary value problems over the entire interval a > 12 .
   The proof has three main ingredients. A Wronskian identity gives a strict boundary
inequality valid for every a > 12 . The time component of the unit normal is a positive
Dirichlet eigenfunction with eigenvalue two; a codimension-two min–max argument therefore
determines the radial mode k = 0. Finally, a comparison of logarithmic derivatives, together
with an explicit integral inequality, gives a boundary inequality for the mode k = 2; a
ground-state identity turns it into positivity of that mode, and monotonicity in k 2 completes
the spectral count. The geometric and spectral identities used below are included in the
proof.
   Two consequences are recorded in Section 8. The radius of the supporting ball is a strictly
increasing function of a (Corollary 9). The restriction to angular period 2π is essential: for
m ≥ 2, the m-fold cover of Σa has index at least 4m, and its nullity exceeds two exactly
when mk∗ (a) is an integer, for a threshold frequency k∗ (a) ∈ (1, 2) (Remark 10). This agrees
with the non-rotational free boundary minimal annuli of Cerezo [2], which bifurcate from
multiple covers of critical spherical catenoids.

Remark 11 (Scope of the argument). Lemmas 2 and 6 rest on identities specific to the
rotational family of Section 2, and the separation of variables uses its rotational symmetry.
Three ingredients nevertheless have a wider scope.
   (i) The identity (5.1) uses neither the explicit parametrization nor the rotational symmetry:
for every two-sided minimal hypersurface Σn ⊂ Hn+1 ⊂ Rn+1,1 , the Gauss–Weingarten and
Codazzi equations give ∆Σ ν = −|II|2 ν, hence Jν 0 = n ν 0 for J = −∆Σ − |II|2 + n. If Σ is
free boundary in a geodesic ball centred at e0 , then ν 0 = 0 on ∂Σ. What is specific to the
present family is the sign property of Lemma 3: ν 0 is positive in the interior, hence it is the
first Dirichlet eigenfunction, with eigenvalue 2; this is the input of Proposition 4.
   (ii) The last step of Proposition 7 is an instance of the ground-state representation for
regular Sturm–Liouville problems. Write the Robin conditions as ∂η z = σz at the endpoints.
If a positive solution u of the zero-energy equation satisfies ∂η u/u > σ at both endpoints,
the substitution z = uh writes the quadratic form as a nonnegative interior square plus
boundary terms whose coefficients are positive multiples of ∂η u/u − σ; the form is therefore
positive definite. By symmetry the two conditions reduce here to p2 (T ) > v0 , which is
Lemma 6.
   (iii) The proof of Corollary 9 reflects a general transversality principle. Let Xa be a
smooth family of free boundary minimal hypersurfaces in the geodesic balls of radius r(a)
centred at e0 , parametrized over a fixed manifold with boundary, and let ψ = ⟨∂a Xa , ν⟩L .
Then ψ is a Jacobi field, and differentiating the two free boundary conditions gives
