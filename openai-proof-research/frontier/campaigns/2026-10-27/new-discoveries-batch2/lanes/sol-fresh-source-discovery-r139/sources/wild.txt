Alexander Nappo Note: Villanova University Jessie Tong Note: San Diego State University Christopher Zimbizi Note: Lake Forest College October 15, 2026 Abstract Given an edge-colored graph, the wild number measures how close the graph is to having a spanning tree in every color. We investigate this parameter for complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs, with particular emphasis on minimum proper colorings and equitable 2 2 -colorings. For complete graphs, we determine the full range of wild numbers attainable by equitable 2 2 -colorings and show that, although every value in this range occurs, the expected wild number of a uniformly random equitable 2 2 -coloring tends to 0 0 as the order of the graph grows. For minimum proper colorings of K n K_{n} , we establish a general lower bound, determine the exact wild number for 3 ≤ n ≤ 12 3\leq n\leq 12 , and conjecture that the lower bound is sharp for all n n . We also develop structural conditions on ideal wild sets and use them to obtain upper bounds and analyze the performance of a natural greedy algorithm. For complete bipartite graphs, we prove a sharp upper bound for equitable 2 2 -colorings and determine the wild number exactly for minimum proper colorings. Finally, we apply these results to Cartesian products of complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs, obtaining lower bounds for the wild number under minimum proper and product colorings. 1 Introduction Since their introduction in 1961 by Nash-Williams and Tutte [ 15 , 18 ] , edge-disjoint spanning trees have been instrumental in studying network reliability and performance. The existence of multiple edge-disjoint spanning trees provides independent, yet parallel, paths between vertices, allowing a network to withstand edge failures and avoid bottlenecks. These independent pathways are especially important in networks that wish to support collective communication operations, as they can be used to enhance fault tolerance while managing congestion. Independently, Nash-Williams and Tutte proved that a connected graph G G has k k edge-disjoint spanning trees if and only if, for any partition P P of the vertices of G G , there are at least k ⁡ ( | P | − 1 ) k(|P|-1) crossing edges. Determining conditions that guarantee edge-disjoint spanning trees in various graphs has been studied extensively and remains an active area of research [ 17 , 5 , 12 ] . Edge colorings provide a natural way to organize and allocate the pathways of a network across distinct operations. Tait [ 16 ] showed that the four-color problem is equivalent to 3 3 -edge coloring every planar 3 3 -connected cubic graph, and edge colorings of many types have since been defined and studied [ 19 , 10 , 7 , 11 ] . We refer the reader to a recent survey on edge colorings by Cao, Chen, Jing, Stiebitz, and Toft [ 4 ] for a more detailed overview. The applications of edge colorings are far-reaching, including scheduling, routing, and resource allocation [ 13 , 8 , 14 , 3 ] . These two topics—edge-disjoint spanning trees as a gauge of network reliability and edge colorings as a means of organizing network operations—come together naturally in the study of the wild number of an edge-colored graph. In network terms, it is the minimum number of links that must be upgraded to carry every operation so that each operation has its own spanning tree. This idea, first introduced and explored by Anders, Foster-Greenwood, Garcia, and Krawzik [ 2 ] , was inspired by the algebraic theory of generalized graph splines. See [ 1 ] for more on the connection between graphs that admit only constant splines and the wild number of a graph. The Nash-Williams–Tutte theorem can then be thought of as a characterization of when a graph has a k k -edge coloring that produces wild number 0 0 . In this paper, we investigate the wild number of the complete graph and the complete bipartite graph for minimum proper edge colorings and equitable 2 2 -edge colorings. Complete graphs are a natural setting for many edge coloring problems, for example, all-to-all scheduling. The complete bipartite graph has applications to scheduling as well, where the interest is the possible interactions between two groups. Minimum proper colorings offer the shortest possible conflict-free schedule for operations that cannot be performed simultaneously, and equitable 2 2 -colorings offer insight into distributing workload evenly between two operations to prevent congestion. We begin with preliminary definitions and results in Section 2 . In Section 3 , we investigate complete graphs, determining the range of possible wild numbers for equitable 2 2 -colorings, along with the expected wild number of a uniformly random equitable 2 2 -coloring. We also provide a lower bound on the wild number for minimum proper colorings and verify that this lower bound is sharp for 3 ≤ n ≤ 12 3\leq n\leq 12 , motivating a conjecture that it is sharp for all n n , as well as an analysis of the performance of a natural greedy algorithm for constructing these wild sets. We shift our focus to complete bipartite graphs in Section 4 , proving a sharp upper bound on the wild number for equitable 2 2 -colorings and determining the wild number exactly for minimum proper colorings. In Section 5 , we use our results from Sections 3 and 4 on minimum proper colorings to obtain lower bounds for Cartesian products of complete graphs and of complete bipartite graphs.
