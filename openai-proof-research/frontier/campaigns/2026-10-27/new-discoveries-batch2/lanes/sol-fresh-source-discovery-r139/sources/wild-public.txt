Sharpness of the Wild Number Lower Bound for Minimum Proper Colorings of Complete Graphs of Order at Least 30000001 · AFTD AFTD Auto-Formalizing Theoretical Domains Knowledgebase Research Blogs Summary Reports Open Problems Status GitHub Research Blogs Sharpness of the Wild Number Lower Bound for Minimum Proper Colorings of Complete Graphs of Order at Least 30000001 medium tier 2026-10-28 OP-306 reviewed by Claude Code 中文版 Abstract Let $n \ge 3$ and let $\rho$ be a minimum proper edge coloring of the complete graph $K_n$. arXiv:2610.22142 proves $\operatorname{wld}_\rho(K_n) \ge \lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$, shows that equality holds for $3 \le n \le 12$, and conjectures equality for every $n \ge 3$. We prove the conjecture for every $n \ge 30000001$. For odd $n$ we partition the vertices into $q = \lfloor n/3 \rfloor$ blocks of three or four vertices; joining every vertex to a root of its block gives $n - q$ edges, and these form a color-connecting set as soon as no nonempty proper union of blocks is closed under a color class. A partition with this property exists by a lopsided local lemma for random permutations: once $q \ge 10^7$, the weighted count of bad events through any one position or any one vertex is at most $1/50$. For even $n$ the extra vertex becomes a block of its own, which recovers the paper's reduction from $K_n$ to $K_{n-1}$. The whole proof is verified in Lean 4. 1. Introduction Let $\gamma$ be an edge coloring of a graph $G$. A set $W$ of edges is color-connecting if, for every color $c_i$, the spanning subgraph whose edges are the edges of color $c_i$ together with $W$ is connected. The wild number $\operatorname{wld}_\gamma(G)$ is the minimum size of a color-connecting set. The paper arXiv:2610.22142 studies this parameter for complete and complete bipartite graphs. For minimum proper edge colorings of $K_n$, that is, proper colorings with $\chi'(K_n)$ colors ($n$ colors for odd $n$, $n-1$ for even $n$), Theorem 3.4 of arXiv:2610.22142 proves $$\operatorname{wld}_\rho(K_n) \ge \left\lfloor \frac{4\lceil n/2\rceil}{3} \right\rfloor \qquad (n \ge 3),$$ Theorem 3.5 shows that this bound is attained for $3 \le n \le 12$, and Section 6 conjectures that it is attained for every $n \ge 3$ and every minimum proper coloring. The paper also reduces the upper bound to a partition problem. When $n$ is odd, each color class $M_i$ is a near-perfect matching that misses one vertex $v_i$. Given a partition $P$ of the vertices into blocks of size at least three, a spanning tree in each block gives a set $W$ with $n - |P|$ edges, and Section 3.3.2 shows that $W$ is color-connecting if and only if $P$ is seal-free: no color class $M_i$ perfectly matches a nonempty union of blocks avoiding $v_i$. Writing $n = 3q + a$ with $a \in \{0,1,2\}$, a seal-free partition into $q$ blocks, namely $q - a$ triples and $a$ blocks of four, gives $n - q$ edges, which equals $\lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$. For even orders, Section 3.3.3 shows $\operatorname{wld}_\rho(K_{2k}) \le \operatorname{wld}_{\rho_v}(K_{2k-1})$, where $\rho_v$ is the restriction to $K_{2k} - v$. The paper builds seal-free partitions by hand for small $n$. Beyond $n = 12$ the only instance settled before was $n = 279$ ( wild_number_complete_graph_279_min_proper_coloring_eq ), by an exact certificate for that single order. We prove the conjecture for every $n \ge 30000001$ ( wild_number_complete_graph_large_eq ). The new ingredient is an existence proof for seal-free partitions at every large order. A uniformly random permutation of the vertices places them into the blocks of a fixed partition shape. Each potential seal is an event of the form "the permutation maps the positions of $h$ blocks onto a given set closed under a color". A lopsided local lemma for random permutations shows that, with high enough $q$, some permutation avoids all these events. 2. Preliminaries Definition 1 (Minimum proper edge colorings). For $n \ge 1$ the complete graph $K_n$ is the complete simple graph on $\operatorname{Fin} n$. A proper edge coloring with color set $\operatorname{Fin} k$ is a proper vertex coloring of the line graph of $K_n$. It is minimum when $k$ is the chromatic number of the line graph, $\chi'(K_n)$; this equals $n$ for odd $n \ge 3$ and $n - 1$ for even $n$. Definition 2 (Wild number). For a graph $G$ and an edge coloring $\gamma$, a set $W$ of edges is color-connecting if for every color $c$ used by $\gamma$ the spanning subgraph with edge set $\gamma^{-1}(c) \cup W$ is connected. The wild number simple_graph_wild_number is the infimum of the sizes $|W|$ of finite color-connecting sets. On $\mathbb{N}$ the infimum of the empty set is $0$, but for the complete graphs considered here the full edge set is color-connecting, so the infimum is a minimum. Definition 3 (Block partitions and the cut condition). A block partition of $K_n$ into $q$ blocks is a map $p : \operatorname{Fin} n \to \operatorname{Fin} q$ with roots $r : \operatorname{Fin} q \to \operatorname{Fin} n$ such that $p(r(i)) = i$. A coloring $\gamma$ satisfies the block cut condition for $p$ if for every edge $e$ and every nonempty set $S \subsetneq \operatorname{Fin} q$ of blocks there is an edge $uv$ with $p(u) \in S$, $p(v) \notin S$ and $\gamma(uv) = \gamma(e)$. Definition 4 (Seal events and weights). Fix a block map $b : \operatorname{Fin} n \to \operatorname{Fin} q$ and a proper coloring $c$ with $n$ colors. For a set $T$ of blocks, let $\operatorname{pos}(T) = b^{-1}(T)$. A seal event is a triple $(i, T, f)$ with $1 \le |T| \le \lfloor q/2 \rfloor$ and $f$ an injection of $\operatorname{pos}(T)$ into the vertices whose image is closed under color $i$: every edge of color $i$ with one endpoint in the image has both endpoints there. A permutation $\sigma$ of the vertices realizes the event if $\sigma = f$ on $\operatorname{pos}(T)$. For $d = |\operatorname{pos}(T)|$ the event gets the weight $$\operatorname{wt}_n(d) = \frac{(21/20)^d}{(n)_d}, \qquad (n)_d = n(n-1)\cdots(n-d+1).$$ A uniformly random permutation realizes a given event with probability exactly $1/(n)_d$. Conventions. In Lean, division of natural numbers is floor division. The value $\lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$ is written 4*((n+1)/2)/3 , and $(n+1)/2 = \lceil n/2\rceil$ for every $n$. 3. Main result Theorem ( wild_number_complete_graph_large_eq ). Let $n \ge 30000001$, let $k = \chi'(K_n)$ (the chromatic number of the line graph of $K_n$), and let $c$ be a proper edge coloring of $K_n$ with colors $\operatorname{Fin} k$. Then $$\operatorname{wld}_c(K_n) = \left\lfloor \frac{4\lceil n/2\rceil}{3} \right\rfloor .$$ In Lean the hypotheses are 30000001 ≤ n , the equation between the chromatic number of the line graph of the complete graph on Fin n and k , and a coloring c of that line graph by Fin k . The conclusion is simple_graph_wild_number ⊤ c = 4*((n+1)/2)/3 . The theorem therefore settles the conjecture of Section 6 of arXiv:2610.22142 for every order $n \ge 30000001$, for both parities and every minimum proper coloring. The lower bound is Theorem 3.4 of arXiv:2610.22142, formalized as wild_number_complete_graph_min_proper_coloring_ge . The new part is the matching upper bound at every large order. Before this, the upper bound was known for $3 \le n \le 12$ (Theorem 3.5 of the paper; the case $n = 3$ is wild_number_complete_three_min_proper_coloring_eq ) and for the single order $n = 279$. Proof The lower bound is wild_number_complete_graph_min_proper_coloring_ge , so it remains to show $\operatorname{wld}_c(K_n) \le \lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$. The proof combines three lemmas. Lemma 1 ( simple_graph_wild_number_le_of_block_cut_condition ). If an edge coloring $\gamma$ of $K_n$, with $n > 0$, satisfies the block cut condition for a block partition $p$ into $q$ blocks with roots $r$, then $\operatorname{wld}_\gamma(K_n) \le n - q$. Proof. Take $W = \{\, u\,r(p(u)) : u \ne r(p(u)) \,\}$, the stars joining each vertex to the root of its block. Then $|W| = n - q$ and each block is connected inside $W$. Fix a color. If the subgraph formed by that color and $W$ were disconnected, some component would be a union of blocks forming a nonempty proper set $S$ of blocks, and no edge of the color would cross from $S$ to its complement, contradicting the cut condition. Lemma 2 ( wild_partition_of_resource_bounds ). Let $c$ be a proper coloring of $K_n$ with $n$ colors and let $b$ be a block map into $q$ blocks with roots. Suppose that, for every position $x$, the total weight of the seal events whose domain $\operatorname{pos}(T)$ contains $x$ is at most $1/50$, and that, for every vertex $w$, the total weight of the seal events whose image contains $w$ is at most $1/50$. Then there is a block partition into $q$ rooted blocks for which $c$ satisfies the block cut condition. Proof. Call two seal events neighbours when their domains or their images meet. The lopsided local lemma for random permutations shows that some permutation $\sigma$ realizes no seal event, provided each event $E$ with domain size $d$ satisfies $$\frac{1}{(n)_d} \le \operatorname{wt}_n(d) \prod_{E' \text{ neighbour of } E} \bigl(1 - \operatorname{wt}_n(d')\bigr).$$ The neighbours of $E$ are covered by $2d$ families, one through each of its $d$ domain positions and one through each of its $d$ image vertices, and each family has total weight at most $1/50$. Hence the product is at least $(49/50)^{2d} \ge (20/21)^d$, which is exactly the inequality required. Now relabel the vertices by $\sigma$, so that vertex $v$ goes to block $b(\sigma^{-1}(v))$. Let $S$ be a nonempty proper set of blocks and let $i$ be a color. Replacing $S$ by its complement if necessary, we may assume $|S| \le \lfloor q/2 \rfloor$; the complement of a set closed under the matching $M_i$ is again closed. If no edge of color $i$ crossed $S$, the vertex set of $S$ would be closed under color $i$, and $\sigma$ would realize the corresponding seal event. So every color crosses every nontrivial cut. Lemma 3 ( wild_partition_large_order_resource_bounds ). Let $n$ be odd with $3q \le n \le 3q + 2$ and $q \ge 10^7$. Let $c$ be a proper $n$-coloring of $K_n$, so that each color $i$ misses exactly one vertex $v_i$. Let $b$ be a block map such that every union of $h$ blocks has between $3h$ and $3h + (n - 3q)$ vertices. Then both families of sums in Lemma 2 are at most $1/50$. Proof. Fix $h$ and a domain of size $k$, where $3h \le k \le 3h + 2$. A vertex set $U$ of size $k$ closed under color $i$ consists of $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ edges of $M_i$, together with $v_i$ when $k$ is odd, so there are at most $\binom{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}{\lfloor k/2 \rfloor}$ choices of $U$ and $k!$ injections onto each. Since $k!/(n)_k = 1/\binom{n}{k}$, the events of one color $i$ and one block set $T$ weigh at most $$P(n,k) = \frac{\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}}{\binom{n}{k}} \left(\frac{21}{20}\right)^{k}.$$ Through a fixed position there are $n$ colors and $\binom{q-1}{h-1}$ sets $T$ of $h$ blocks containing its block, and $n \binom{q-1}{h-1} \le 5q\binom{q-1}{h-1} = 5h\binom{q}{h}$. Through a fixed vertex, the closed sets of size $k$ containing it number $k\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ in total over all colors, with $k \le 3h + 2 \le 5h$, and there are $\binom{q}{h}$ sets $T$; this gives the same quantity. So in both cases the contribution of $h$ is at most $5h\binom{q}{h}P(n,k)$, and Lean shows $$5h\binom{q}{h}P(n,k) \le \frac{1}{20000}\, h^2 \left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^h .$$ For $h = 1$ this is a direct estimate with $3 \le k \le 5$. For $h \ge 2$ it follows from four facts: $\binom{q}{h}^3 \le \binom{n}{k}$ and $\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^2 \le \binom{n}{k}$ (both from Vandermonde's identity), $\binom{q}{h} \ge q(q-1)2^{h-2}/(h(h-1))$, and $(21/20)^6 \le 2(5/6)^2$. Together these give $(5h\binom{q}{h}P(n,k))^2 \le C\,h^4(5/6)^{2h}/(q(q-1))$ for a fixed constant $C$, which is small enough once $q \ge 10^7$. Finally $\sum_{h \ge 0} h^2 (5/6)^h \le 330$, which Lean proves through the identity $\sum_{h<N} h^2(5/6)^h + (6N^2 + 60N + 330)(5/6)^N = 330$. Each total is therefore at most $330/20000 < 1/50$. Proof of the theorem, odd $n$. Here $k = n$, and each color misses exactly one vertex, which gives a bijection between colors and missing vertices. Write $n = 3q + a$ with $a \in \{0,1,2\}$, so $q \ge 10^7$. Positions $3j, 3j+1, 3j+2$ form block $j$, and the $a$ leftover positions are added to blocks $0, \dots, a-1$. Unions of $h$ blocks then have between $3h$ and $3h + a$ vertices. Lemma 3 gives the bounds required by Lemma 2, Lemma 2 gives a partition satisfying the cut condition, and Lemma 1 gives $\operatorname{wld}_c(K_n) \le n - q = 2q + a$. Since $n$ is odd, $q$ is odd when $a \in \{0, 2\}$ and even when $a = 1$, and in each case $\lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor = 2q + a$. Proof of the theorem, even $n$. Write $n = m + 1$ with $m$ odd; then $k = m$. Each color class of $c$ is a perfect matching of $K_n$, so restricting $c$ to the first $m$ vertices gives a proper $m$-coloring of $K_m$ in which every color misses exactly one vertex. Write $m = 3q + a$. The odd case provides a partition of $K_m$ into $q$ rooted blocks with the cut property. Make the last vertex a block of its own, giving $q + 1$ blocks of $K_n$. A nontrivial cut that splits the blocks of $K_m$ is crossed by an edge of $K_m$ of each color. Otherwise one side is exactly the new block, and it is crossed by the edge of the given color at the new vertex. Lemma 1 then gives $\operatorname{wld}_c(K_n) \le n - (q+1) = m - q = 2q + a$. This is the reduction of Section 3.3.3 of arXiv:2610.22142. It remains to check that $2q + a = \lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$ with $n = 3q + a + 1$: $a = 0$: $q = 2j+1$ is odd, $\lceil n/2 \rceil = 3j + 2$, and $\lfloor 4(3j+2)/3 \rfloor = 4j + 2 = 2q$. $a = 1$: $q = 2j$ is even, $\lceil n/2 \rceil = 3j + 1$, and $\lfloor 4(3j+1)/3 \rfloor = 4j + 1 = 2q + 1$. $a = 2$: $q = 2j+1$ is odd, $\lceil n/2 \rceil = 3j + 3$, and $\lfloor 4(3j+3)/3 \rfloor = 4j + 4 = 2q + 2$. In both parities the upper bound meets the lower bound, which proves the equality. 5. Discussion The theorem settles the conjecture of arXiv:2610.22142 for every $n \ge 30000001$. The paper had already reduced the upper bound to the existence of seal-free block partitions and constructed them by hand for small orders. The contribution here is a uniform existence argument: the local lemma for random permutations, together with the observation that a set closed under a near-perfect matching is determined by $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ of its edges, which makes potential seals rare. The finite range $13 \le n \le 30000000$ remains open, except $n = 279$. The threshold was chosen so that the estimates close with room to spare, and it is far from what the method can give. The binomial bounds $\binom{q}{h}^3 \le \binom{n}{k}$ and $\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^2 \le \binom{n}{k}$ are crude for small $h$. Summing the weights exactly for a given $n$ instead of using the bound $(1/20000)h^2(5/6)^h$ could lower the threshold, and a computation of exact weight sums, or explicit constructions as in the paper, could cover the orders that remain. The paper also studies complete bipartite graphs. Whether a random block placement argument of the same kind gives the matching upper bounds there is a natural next question.
