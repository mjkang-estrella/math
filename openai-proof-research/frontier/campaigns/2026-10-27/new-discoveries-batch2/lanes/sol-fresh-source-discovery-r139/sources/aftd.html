Sharpness of the Wild Number Lower Bound for Minimum Proper Colorings of Complete Graphs of Order at Least 30000001

Let $n \ge 3$ and let $\rho$ be a minimum proper edge coloring of the complete graph $K_n$. arXiv:2610.22142 proves $\operatorname{wld}_\rho(K_n) \ge \lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$, shows that equality holds for $3 \le n \le 12$, and conjectures equality for every $n \ge 3$. We prove the conjecture for every $n \ge 30000001$. For odd $n$ we partition the vertices into $q = \lfloor n/3 \rfloor$ blocks of three or four vertices; joining every vertex to a root of its block gives $n - q$ edges, and these form a color-connecting set as soon as no nonempty proper union of blocks is closed under a color class. A partition with this property exists by a lopsided local lemma for random permutations: once $q \ge 10^7$, the weighted count of bad events through any one position or any one vertex is at most $1/50$. For even $n$ the extra vertex becomes a block of its own, which recovers the paper's reduction from $K_n$ to $K_{n-1}$. The whole proof is verified in Lean 4.

Unweighted Max-$k$-Cut Games Do Not Always Admit Optimal Strong Equilibria: A 182-Vertex Counterexample for Three Colors

In an unweighted Max-$k$-Cut game on a finite simple graph, each vertex chooses one of $k$ colors to maximize the number of incident edges whose endpoints have different colors. A coloring is optimal if it achieves the maximum cut value, and it is a strong equilibrium if no non-empty coalition of vertices can jointly recolor such that every member strictly improves its utility. While it was established in arXiv:2610.04948 that not every optimal coloring is a strong equilibrium, the paper left open whether every unweighted Max-$k$-Cut game admits at least one optimal coloring that is a strong equilibrium. This post presents a formal negative answer to this question for $k = 3$. We construct a 182-vertex graph, denoted maxcut_pinned_twins_graph, formed by replacing each vertex of the 91-vertex counterexample graph from arXiv:2610.04948 with a pair of independent twin vertices and adding specific pinning edges from core twins to designated anchor twins. We prove that this graph exhibits optimum rigidity: every welfare-maximizing 3-coloring is equal to the doubled initial coloring up to a global permutation of the three colors. Because this unique optimal coloring profile admits a profitable coalitional deviation, the graph possesses no optimal strong equilibrium whatsoever. The main theorem maxcut_op89_optimal_strong_equilibrium_negation and its supporting rigidity lemmas are formally verified in Lean 4.

The Number of Latin Tableaux of Shape $(k, k-1, \dots, 4, 3, 3)$ is $9 \cdot 4^{k-3}$ for Every $k \ge 5$

Latin tableaux generalize Latin squares to Young diagrams. In arXiv:2610.29291, Conjecture 6.3 asks whether for every integer $k \ge 5$, the number of Latin tableaux of shape $\lambda = (k, k-1, \dots, 4, 3, 3)$ and type $\lambda'$ is given by the closed formula $LT(\lambda) = 9 \cdot 4^{k-3}$. In this post, we present the formal verification in Lean 4 of this conjecture, proving the exact formula for all $k \ge 5$. The proof shows that the column contents of every Latin tableau of shape $(k, k-1, \dots, 4, 3, 3)$ follow one rigid pattern, and deduces that every such tableau admits exactly four valid extensions when a new top row of length $k+1$ is adjoined. Consequently, the permanent of the zero-one availability matrix for the top row is identically equal to 4 across all tableaux of that shape, reducing the counting step from shape $k$ to shape $k+1$ to uniform multiplication by 4. Combined with an exact evaluation of the base case $k = 5$, where there are exactly 144 Latin tableaux of shape $(5, 4, 3, 3)$ matching $9 \cdot 4^{5-3} = 9 \cdot 16 = 144$, the general formula follows by induction. In particular every shape of the family is Latin, which the paper notes was not known by any result for $k \ge 6$. All definitions, lemmas, and the overarching induction theorem are verified in Lean 4.

No Integer Square Admits a Perfect Admissible Mondrian Partition with Common Tile Area $q^s r p^a$ for $p > r + 1$

The Mondrian art problem asks for the minimum defect $M(n)$, defined as the difference between the largest and smallest rectangle areas in a partition of an $n \times n$ square into noncongruent integer rectangles with positive integer side lengths. An open question highlighted in arXiv:2610.19193 asks whether there exists any integer $n \ge 3$ admitting a perfect partition, which corresponds to defect $M(n) = 0$ with all tiles sharing an identical area. Previous results have ruled out perfect partitions where the common tile area is a prime power or a product of two prime powers. In this post, we present a formal proof in Lean 4 establishing that no integer square admits a perfect admissible Mondrian partition with common tile area of the form $q^s r p^a$, where $p, q, r$ are distinct prime numbers satisfying $p > r + 1$, uniformly for all natural exponents $a$ and $s$. The proof proceeds by strong induction on the exponent $s$. When $s = 0$, the tile area reduces to two prime factors, excluded by the two-prime theorem. When $s > 0$, total area divisibility forces $q$ to divide the square side $n$. A superincreasing signed-height obstruction across two geometric chains forces $q$ to divide both dimensions of every rectangle, showing that $q^2$ divides the common area, whence $s \ge 2$. Rescaling the canvas by $q$ produces a smaller perfect partition with common area $q^{s-2} r p^a$, which contradicts the induction hypothesis. These results are machine-checked in Lean 4.

For Odd Orders with $h \not\equiv 59 \pmod{60}$, the Negative Three-Stamp Number Equals $n_h(2)$ Minus One, Two, or Three

The postage stamp problem asks for the largest integer $n$ such that every integer in $\{0, 1, \dots, n\}$ can be represented as an $h$-fold sum of elements from a $(k+1)$-element basis of integers. In the classical setting where basis elements are non-negative, the extremal range for three-stamp bases ($k = 2$) is given by $n_h(2) = \lfloor (h^2 + 6h + 1)/4 \rfloor$. When negative integers are permitted in the basis, the corresponding extremal range is denoted by $n♭_h(k)$. In arXiv:2610.19470, it was shown that for all odd orders $h \ge 1$, requiring a negative element strictly decreases the achievable range: $n♭_h(2) < n_h(2)$. However, that paper left the exact value of $n♭_h(2)$ and the size of the deficit $n_h(2) - n♭_h(2)$ undetermined. Here, we present the complete determination of $n♭_h(2)$ for all odd integers $h \ge 5$ with $h \not\equiv 59 \pmod{60}$, verified in Lean 4. The gap $n_h(2) - n♭_h(2)$ depends purely on the residue class of $h \pmod{12}$: it equals $1$ if $h \equiv 9 \pmod{12}$, $2$ if $h \equiv 1, 3, 5, 7 \pmod{12}$, and $3$ if $h \equiv 11 \pmod{12}$. For $h \equiv 59 \pmod{60}$, the gap is proved to be at least $4$. The exact values are obtained by establishing modular upper bounds on ranges of three-element integer bases containing a negative element and matching them with explicit parametric constructions through an extremal sandwiching principle.

Four extensions for every double-three-tail Latin tableau

Conjecture 6.3 in arXiv:2610.29291 asks whether the number of Latin tableaux of shape $\lambda = (k, k-1, \dots, 4, 3, 3)$ and conjugate type $\lambda'$ is given by $9 \cdot 4^{k-3}$ for every integer $k \ge 5$. Earlier investigations in arXiv:2610.29291 established the baseline availability-permanent identity connecting tableau counts to matrix permanents in Lemma 5.4 and demonstrated top-row monotonicity in Proposition 5.5, but left the evaluation of individual availability permanents open. This post presents a formal proof that every Latin tableau of shape $(k, k-1, \dots, 4, 3, 3)$ admits exactly four valid extensions upon adding a new top row of length $k+1$. The proof proceeds by classifying the column-symbol configurations of the tableaux into canonical column profiles. It is shown that every tableau in this family satisfies the canonical profile, and that any filling satisfying this profile yields a $(k+1) \times (k+1)$ binary availability matrix whose permanent equals 4. This establishes a uniform pointwise branching factor of 4 across the entire infinite family. The main result, along with its supporting structural and combinatorial lemmas, has been formalized and mechanically verified in Lean 4.

Hop-Closed and Leap-Closed Edge Subsets Do Not Form the Flats of a Matroid

Question 5.9 of arXiv:2610.18463 asks whether the collection of hop-closed (hop-maximal) edge subsets, or the collection of leap-closed (leap-maximal) edge subsets, of a finite simple graph forms the set of flats of a matroid on its edge set. For a spanning subgraph $H = G[F]$, hop-maximality requires that the addition of any edge from $E(G) \setminus F$ strictly increases the number of hops of the subgraph, while leap-maximality requires that adding any edge from $E(G) \setminus F$ strictly enlarges its leap group $\Lambda_1(H)$. We show that the answer is negative for both notions: neither the family of hop-closed edge subsets nor the family of leap-closed edge subsets forms the set of flats of a matroid for every finite simple graph. The refutation proceeds by demonstrating that on the complete graph $K_3$ on three vertices, neither collection is closed under intersection. Specifically, there exist pairs of hop-closed (respectively, leap-closed) edge subsets whose intersection fails to be hop-closed (respectively, leap-closed). Because the collection of flats of any matroid is necessarily closed under intersection, this failure of intersection closure proves that no matroid on the edge set of $K_3$ can have either of these collections as its flats. All definitions, lemmas, and the main refutation theorem have been formalized and verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant.

No Perfect Mondrian Partition Has a Common Tile Area Supported on Two Primes

The Mondrian art problem asks for the minimum defect, the difference between the largest and smallest tile areas, over partitions of an $n \times n$ square into at least two pairwise noncongruent integer-sided rectangles. A partition is perfect if its defect is zero, and it is an open question, conjectured to have a negative answer, whether any square admits one. We prove that for distinct primes $p$ and $q$ and all exponents $s, t$, no perfect partition of any integer square has common tile area $q^s p^t$. Two consequences follow: no square whose side has at most two distinct prime factors admits a perfect partition, and in any perfect partition the common tile area has at least three distinct prime factors. The proof combines a congruence obtained from a moment identity over the rows of the square with the observation that the tiles of a perfect partition have pairwise distinct heights; together they force $q$ to divide both sides of every tile, after which the coordinates can be divided by $q$ and the exponent of $q$ in the area drops by two. Every result here is formalized and checked in Lean 4.

Every Pure Nash Equilibrium of Round-Robin with at Most $2n+1$ Goods Is Envy-Free up to One Good

In the fair division of indivisible goods under additive valuations, the Round-Robin mechanism guarantees envy-freeness up to one good (EF1) when agents act truthfully. However, strategic agents report rankings to maximize the value of their allocated bundles. In arXiv:2610.23686, it was established that while every pure Nash equilibrium of Round-Robin is EF1 for two agents, equilibria can fail EF1 in general: the paper presented a counterexample with three agents and eight goods and proved a general $1/2$-EF1 approximation bound. That counterexample has $m = 8 = 2n + 2$ goods for $n = 3$; how few goods guarantee EF1 for every number of agents is not addressed there. We prove that for any number of agents $n \ge 1$ and any number of goods $m \le 2n + 1$, every pure Nash equilibrium of Round-Robin under nonnegative additive valuations is EF1. The proof decomposes the analysis by turn counts: when $m \le 2n$, every agent picks at most twice, and equilibrium conditions prevent envy exceeding one good; when $m = 2n + 1$, only agent $0$ receives three items while all other agents receive at most two, and a valuation-free deviation argument shows that every other agent is EF1 towards agent $0$ once agent $0$'s first pick is removed. For three agents the bound is tight: the paper's eight-good counterexample is the smallest possible. These results are formalized and verified in Lean 4.

Every Minimum Proper Edge Colouring of $K_{279}$ Has Wild Number Exactly $186$

For an edge colouring $\rho$ of a graph $G$, a set $W$ of edges of $G$ is colour-connecting if, for every colour $i$ used by $\rho$, the spanning subgraph formed by the edges of colour $i$ together with $W$ is connected; the wild number $\operatorname{wld}_\rho(G)$ is the least size of a colour-connecting set. arXiv:2610.22142 proves that every minimum proper edge colouring $\rho$ of the complete graph $K_n$, $n \ge 3$, satisfies $\operatorname{wld}_\rho(K_n) \ge \lfloor 4\lceil n/2\rceil/3 \rfloor$, shows the bound is attained for $3 \le n \le 12$, and conjectures equality for every $n \ge 3$. We prove the conjecture for $n = 279$: every minimum proper edge colouring of $K_{279}$ has wild number exactly $186$. The upper bound holds for every such colouring, not just for one. It comes from a partition of the vertex set into $93$ triples such that no union of at most $46$ triples is closed under the edges of any one colour; the stars joining each triple to a chosen root then form a colour-connecting set of $279 - 93 = 186$ edges. Such a partition exists by the lopsided local lemma for a uniformly random permutation, whose hypothesis reduces to two exact rational inequalities. The whole argument, including the lower bound and the local lemma, is checked in Lean 4.

Line Cubic Phase Gates Meet the Isotropy Bound $2n+1$ for Every $n \ge 5$

Let $n \ge 5$ and let $f_n(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n-2} x_i x_{i+1} x_{i+2}$ be the line cubic form over $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$. For the diagonal gate $U_{f_n} : |x\rangle \mapsto (-1)^{f_n(x)} |x\rangle$, the minimum number $\delta(U_{f_n})$ of $T$ gates in a Hadamard-free $\{\mathrm{CNOT}, T\}$ circuit with diagonal Clifford gates free satisfies $\delta(U_{f_n}) \ge 2n + 1$ by an isotropy bound, and arXiv:2610.01024 found circuits meeting it for $5 \le n \le 12$ and asked whether $\delta(U_{f_n}) = 2n + 1$ for every $n \ge 5$. We answer yes: for every $n \ge 5$ the line cubic syndrome has a parity representation with exactly $2n + 1$ supports. The representation is built by an explicit recursion. From an 11-support representation at $n = 5$, each step removes the two supports $\{n-2\}$ and $\{n-2, n-1\}$, adds the new index $n$ to every remaining support, and adds the four supports $\{n-2\}$, $\{n-2, n-1\}$, $\{n-1\}$, $\{n-1, n\}$. The construction, the matching lower bound and the transfer to the finite-set formulation are proved in Lean 4.

When Negative Three-Stamp Bases Miss the Classical Range by One

The postage stamp problem asks for the largest consecutive range of non-negative integers $[0, n]$ representable as an $h$-fold sum of a $(k+1)$-element basis. When the basis is required to contain a negative integer, the extremal range is denoted $n^\flat_h(k)$. For bases of size three ($k=2$) and odd $h \ge 5$, it was known from arXiv:2610.19470 that $n^\flat_h(2)$ is strictly smaller than the classical positive-basis extremal value $n_h(2) = \lfloor (h^2+6h+1)/4 \rfloor$, leaving the exact value of $n^\flat_h(2)$ and the size of the deficit $n_h(2) - n^\flat_h(2)$ open. We prove that the minimal deficit $n^\flat_h(2) = n_h(2) - 1$ is attained if and only if $h \equiv 9 \pmod{12}$. The proof constructs an explicit achieving family $\{-(2r+2), 2r+2, 4r+5\}$ when $h = 12r+9$, and shows that for all other odd $h \ge 5$ the deficit is at least $2$. In addition, we bound the deficit at $h = 419$ ($419 \equiv 59 \pmod{60}$), establishing that $n^\flat_{419}(2) \le n_{419}(2) - 6 = 44513$. This bound refutes a formula proposed in this investigation that $n^\flat_h(2) = n_h(2) - 4$ for all $h \equiv 59 \pmod{60}$, because all candidate bases leaving a deficit of 5 or less fail due to non-trivial common divisors in their step differences. All definitions, lemmas, and theorems are formally verified in Lean 4.

Cycle Powers $C_n^7$ Cannot Be Covered by Four Equivalence Subgraphs When $n \ge 22$ Is Not Divisible by Eight

The equivalence covering number $\mathrm{eq}(G)$ of a graph $G$ is the least number of equivalence subgraphs (spanning disjoint unions of cliques) whose edge sets cover $E(G)$. For powers of cycles, arXiv:2610.15635 shows $\mathrm{eq}(C_n^k) \in \{1+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil,\ 2+\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil\}$ for large $n$, and determines which value holds except when $k+1$ is a power of $2$, $n$ is even and $k+1 \nmid n$. We treat $k = 7$: for every $n \ge 22$ with $8 \nmid n$, the graph $C_n^7$ has no cover by $4$ equivalence subgraphs, so $\mathrm{eq}(C_n^7) \ge 5$. With the bounds of arXiv:2610.15635 this gives $\mathrm{eq}(C_n^7) = 5$ for every $n \ge 328$ with $8 \nmid n$, and $\mathrm{eq}(C_n^7) = 4$ exactly when $8 \mid n$, for every $n \ge 22$. The proof is a double count: a weight that charges $2$ for each pair at distance $7$ and $1$ for each pair at distance $6$ or $4$ is at most $n$ in every layer, while a cover needs total weight $4n$. A $4$-cover is therefore tight, which pins down the shape of every class; the layers of the classes are then forced to repeat with period $8$ but not with period $4$, and this is impossible unless $8 \mid n$. The theorem and all its lemmas are proved in Lean 4.

Pure Nash Equilibria of Round-Robin Eliminate Envy up to One Good Towards Any Agent Appearing at Most Twice

Does every pure Nash equilibrium of the Round-Robin picking mechanism guarantee envy-freeness up to one good (EF1) when agents have nonnegative additive valuations? While a published claim asserted that all pure Nash equilibria of Round-Robin are EF1, a recent counterexample with three agents and eight goods showed that this general assertion is false. We show that the property survives in part: in any pure Nash equilibrium of the Round-Robin mechanism with nonnegative additive valuations, an agent experiences envy up to at most one good towards any other agent who appears at most twice in the picking sequence. The proof proceeds by analyzing the bundle of the target agent and the partial allocation before the evaluating agent's initial turn. If the target receives at most one item, envy is trivially EF1 by removing that item. If the target receives two items, the number of goods strictly exceeds the number of agents, and at least one item allocated to the target remains unallocated prior to the evaluating agent's initial turn. By deviating to place that unallocated item at the top position of their reported ranking, the evaluating agent secures it; pure Nash equilibrium ensures their equilibrium bundle is worth at least this item, bounding envy towards the target once the other item is removed. This implies that all equilibria with at most $2n$ goods are EF1. These results have been formalized and verified in Lean 4.

The Local Average Order of Dominating Sets Containing a Vertex is Strictly Less Than $(5n-1)/6$ for $n \ge 4$

Theorem 3.8 of arXiv:2610.11446 established that for every finite simple graph $G$ of order $n$ without isolated vertices and every vertex $v$, the local average order of dominating sets containing $v$, denoted $avd_v(G)$, satisfies $avd_v(G) \le \frac{5n-1}{6}$. While this upper bound is attained when $n = 3$ by an endpoint of the path graph $P_3$, it was left open whether the bound is sharp for any $n \ge 4$. We answer this question in the negative by proving that for every finite simple graph $G$ of order $n \ge 4$ without isolated vertices and every vertex $v \in V(G)$, the strict inequality $avd_v(G) < \frac{5n-1}{6}$ holds. The proof double-counts dominating sets containing each vertex in terms of removable and critical vertices, establishes an injection on neighborhood-intersection singletons to show that at most two-thirds of all dominating sets meet the closed neighborhood of any vertex in a single element, and strengthens this bound when the vertex has degree at least 2. When $v$ has degree at least 2, the bound follows directly; when $v$ has degree 1, structural analysis of vertices outside the closed neighborhood of $v$ yields either a vertex of degree at least 2 or a pair of vertices sharing a critical witness set. The entire proof is formalized and mechanically checked in Lean 4.

Refutation of the Linkedness Conjecture for the Two-Agent Price of Connectivity via an Eight-Vertex Graph

In the fair division of indivisible goods among two agents with additive utilities, goods are modeled as vertices of a connected graph G. The maximin share MMS(u, 2) is the maximum minimum utility an agent can secure over all bipartitions of the vertex set, whereas the connected maximin share G-MMS(G, u, 2) restricts partitions to parts that induce connected subgraphs. The two-agent price of connectivity PoC(G, 2) is the supremum over additive valuations u of MMS(u, 2) / G-MMS(G, u, 2). A graph is (a, b)-linked if any two disjoint sets of terminals of sizes a and b can be extended to disjoint connected vertex sets. Conjecture 3.10 of arXiv:1908.05433 asserts that if an integer k >= 2 and a graph G satisfy that G is (2, k - 1)-linked but not (2, k)-linked, then PoC(G, 2) = 2k / (2k - 1). This post presents an explicit counterexample to this conjecture for k = 4, formalized and mechanically verified in Lean 4. Specifically, there exists an 8-vertex graph that is (2, 3)-linked but not (2, 4)-linked, yet admits a valuation with maximin share 15 and connected maximin share at most 13. This yields PoC(G, 2) >= 15/13 > 8/7, refuting the predicted ratio of 8/7.

The Exact Price and Strong Price of Maximum Nash Welfare for Two Agents Equal 27/23

The price of maximum Nash welfare (MNW) measures the worst-case efficiency loss incurred by requiring an allocation of indivisible goods to maximize the Nash welfare product, defined as the supremum ratio of optimal utilitarian social welfare to the welfare achieved by the best MNW allocation. The strong price of MNW considers the welfare of the worst MNW allocation instead. For two agents with additive, nonnegative, normalized valuations, both the price and the strong price of MNW were shown in arXiv:1905.04910 to lie in the interval $[27/23, 5/4]$. This post presents the resolution of this question: every maximum Nash welfare allocation $N$ and every alternative allocation $O$ satisfy $23 \cdot \mathrm{utilitarian\_welfare}(v, O) \le 27 \cdot \mathrm{utilitarian\_welfare}(v, N)$. The proof partitions the goods into four sets according to their recipients under $O$ and $N$, formulates five quadratic constraints reflecting the maximality of $N$ against $O$ and four bundle-reassignment allocations, and establishes the bound via mnw2_core using the bracket lemma and a polynomial certificate. Together with the known lower-bound instance converging to $27/23$, this establishes that both the price and strong price of MNW for two agents are exactly $27/23$. The theorem and all supporting lemmas have been formally verified in Lean 4 as mnw_two_agents_welfare_le.

Refutation of the Deterministic Lower Bound Tightness Conjecture for Finding Minimal Elements in Width-Two Posets

The problem of finding all minimal elements of an unknown partial order on $n$ elements of width at most $w$ via comparison queries was studied in arXiv:0707.1532, which proved an adversary lower bound of $(w + 1)n/2 - w$ queries and an algorithmic upper bound of $wn$ queries. In Section 4 of that work, it was conjectured that the deterministic lower bound for $k = 1$ is tight up to lower-order additive terms, which for width $w = 2$ predicts a query complexity of $3n/2 - 2 + O(1)$. We present the formal refutation of this conjecture, verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant under the theorem name pm_conjecture_false. The refutation proceeds by showing that for width $w = 2$, every correct deterministic algorithm requires at least $2n - 3$ queries in the worst case, as established by pm_lower_bound. In particular, the exact deterministic complexity at width 2 is $2n - 3$, as shown by pm_exact_complexity. For any putative additive constant $C$, instantiating the problem size at $n = 2C + 3$ forces any correct algorithm to make at least $4C + 3$ queries, whereas the conjectured bound permits at most $4C + 2$ queries, yielding a direct arithmetic contradiction. All definitions, data structures, and theorems are checked by the Lean kernel.

Refutation of the Near-Half-Weight Suboptimality Bound for the Team Order Problem in arXiv:2605.21234

In the Team Order problem of arXiv:2605.21234, two teams of $n$ players compete in $n$ independent matches determined by a bipartite matching, and team 1 wins if it wins at least $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + 1$ matches. Theorem 4(2) of arXiv:2605.21234 claims that when a maximum-weight matching $M^*$ has weight $w(M^*) \in [n/2 - \sqrt{n}\log n, n/2 + \sqrt{n}\log n]$, an optimal line-up $O$ beats $M^*$ in win probability by at most $((4 + o(1))/(n+1)) \sum_{e \in M^*} (p_e - 1/2)^2$. We show this claim is false for every $o(1)$ function $\varepsilon(n)$ and every threshold $N \in \mathbb{N}$. In an explicit $(2m+3)$-player instance, the identity matching is a maximum-weight matching with all edge probabilities equal to $1/2$, so the error term vanishes; but while the identity wins with probability $1/2$, the cyclic rotation line-up wins with probability $9/16$, creating an unbridgeable gap of $1/16$. Furthermore, the existential reading of the claim is also false: in a strengthened instance, the all-$1/2$ identity matching is the unique maximum-weight matching, while the cyclic rotation wins with probability at least $1/2 + 1/40$. Both results are verified in Lean 4.

Failure of Equation (5.4) in Generalized Principal-Agency for Graphs with Independence Number Between Two and the Vertex Count

In the analysis of generalized principal-agent models with costly information acquisition presented in arXiv:2209.01146, Equation (5.4) in Section 5.3 (within the proof of Theorem 5) asserts that the maximum of the objective $u^*(\sigma) - h(\sigma)$ over the probability simplex $\Delta_k$ equals $1/k$ times the maximum of $u^*(x)$ over the unit hypercube $[0,1]^k$ for every $0/1$ matrix. This note presents results verified in Lean 4 demonstrating that this equality fails for the adjacency matrix of every simple graph whose independence number $\alpha$ satisfies $2 \le \alpha < k$. On every such graph on $k$ vertices, the maximum over the simplex equals $1 - 1/\alpha + 1/k$, whereas the maximum over the hypercube equals $\alpha$, yielding a scaled value of $\alpha/k$. The difference between the two expressions evaluates to $(\alpha - 1)(k - \alpha)/(k\alpha)$, which is strictly positive whenever $2 \le \alpha < k$, so the left-hand side is strictly larger than the right-hand side. For the path graph on three vertices, whose independence number is 2, the simplex maximum is $5/6$ while the scaled hypercube maximum is $2/3$, refuting the published equality. In addition, the auxiliary claim from Section 5.3 and Appendix 13.2 of arXiv:2209.01146 that $u^*(x) \le 1$ for all $x \in [0,1]^k$ is refuted on the same graph, where the utility attains 2. These results and their proofs are formalized and machine-checked in Lean 4.

The Binary-Type Publication Choice Problem Admits Multiple Pure-Strategy Equilibria Under Standing Assumptions and Monotone Cost Ratios

In the study of game-theoretic models of scientific publishing, the Publication Choice Problem formalized in arXiv:2511.13678v2 investigates how heterogeneous researcher types allocate their submissions across multiple publication venues. Theorem 4.1 of that paper asserted that every binary-type instance of the problem satisfying the standing assumptions—namely, valid scaling parameters $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ and $\beta > 1$, positive type densities and costs strictly increasing in the venue index—together with Assumption 1 (monotone cost ratios) and Assumption 2 (a non-competitive baseline venue) possesses a unique pure-strategy equilibrium. This note presents a formal refutation of that claim, verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant. Specifically, the theorem publication_choice_binary_equilibrium_not_unique establishes that equilibrium venue impacts are not generally unique. The refutation proceeds via an explicit binary-type counterexample (publication_choice_three_equilibria) with parameter values $\alpha = 1/2$, $\beta = 2$, researcher types $\theta = (1, 8)$, and two publication venues. This concrete instance satisfies all standing assumptions as well as Assumptions 1 and 2, yet it admits three distinct pure-strategy equilibria with strictly positive publication quantities across all types and venues and strictly ordered impact levels at the baseline venue.

Linear Shannon Entropy of Plackett–Luce Models with Geometric Weights for All Decay Ratios in the Open Unit Interval

We consider the question of whether the Plackett–Luce choice model with geometric weights $w = (1, \phi, \dots, \phi^{m-1})$ has Shannon entropy $O(m)$ for all decay factors $\phi \in (0, 1)$, settling a conjecture posed in the study of preference elicitation as average-case sorting. Previously, an $O(m)$ upper bound on the entropy was established only for the restricted parameter regime $\phi \in (0, 1/2]$, because the existing proof relied on an entropy monotonicity property of the normalized weight vector under entry removal that fails when $\phi > 1/2$. We answer the question affirmatively for every $\phi \in (0, 1)$, proving that the Shannon entropy of the Plackett–Luce distribution over rankings of $m$ alternatives is bounded by $C(\phi) \cdot m$, where $C(\phi) = \ln(1/(1 - \phi)) + \ln(1/\phi) \frac{\phi}{(1 - \phi)^2}$. The proof decomposes the ranking distribution into sequential choice steps and bounds the conditional entropy at each step uniformly over all non-empty subsets of remaining alternatives, completely bypassing the weight monotonicity requirement. More generally, the bound holds for the Plackett–Luce distribution on any finite subset of alternatives of cardinality $n$, yielding an upper bound of $C(\phi) \cdot n$. The main theorem and its supporting lemmas have been formally verified in Lean 4.

Impossibility of Quota and Population Monotonicity for Apportionment to Four States at a Common House Size

An apportionment method assigns to each positive population vector of $n$ states and each house size $h$ an integer seat allocation summing to $h$. Proportional representation requires that each state receives either the lower or upper integer bound of its exact proportional entitlement, known as the quota rule. Population monotonicity requires that a state whose population weakly increases does not lose a seat to a state whose population weakly decreases. While Webster's method satisfies both properties for up to three states, quota and population monotonicity are incompatible for five or more states, and for four states when comparisons may span different house sizes. It remained an open question whether a deterministic apportionment solution for four states could satisfy quota and avoid population paradoxes when all monotonicity comparisons are restricted to profiles sharing a single common house size.

We resolve this question in the negative. There exists no apportionment solution for four states that simultaneously satisfies the quota rule and prevents population paradoxes between profiles at the same house size. A witness configuration of twelve specific population profiles evaluated at house size $h = 8$ forces a population paradox under every possible quota-compliant seat assignment. This result holds without imposing anonymity, order preservation, or any regularity conditions across house sizes. The entire refutation is formalized and verified in the Lean 4 proof assistant as the theorem quota_population_monotone_incompatible_four_states_common_house.

The Optimal Receiver-Utility Constant in Bit-String Bayesian Persuasion Is Three Halves

In multidimensional Bayesian persuasion on the Boolean hypercube, a sender observes a state drawn from a prior distribution over bit strings and commits to an obedient signaling scheme, while a receiver guesses each coordinate to maximize the number of correct guesses. The sender derives utility from the total number of coordinates guessed as one. Posed as the open direction in Section 5 of arXiv:2606.22226, the receiver-utility problem asks for the least constant $c$ such that the supremum receiver utility across sender-optimal schemes, $R_{\max}(\mu)$, satisfies $R_{\max}(\mu) \le c R_0(\mu)$ for every prior $\mu$, where $R_0(\mu)$ is the receiver's prior-only utility. The source paper established the upper bound $3/2$ in Theorem 4.5 and demonstrated in Theorem 4.15 that any universal bound must strictly exceed $5/4$, exhibiting a six-bit prior with ratio $39/31$, thereby leaving the optimal constant in the interval $[39/31, 3/2]$. This post presents the formalization and proof that the optimal constant is precisely $3/2$. The result is established by verifying that $3/2$ is a universal upper bound for every finite coordinate set and demonstrating its sharpness through a sequence of slice priors supported uniformly on $2m+1$ states with coordinates indexed by $m$-subsets, achieving the utility ratio $(3m+1)/(2m+2) = 3/2 - 1/(m+1)$, which converges to $3/2$ as $m \to \infty$. The complete theorem has been verified in Lean 4 as persuasion_receiver_ratio_optimal_constant.

Every Minimum EFX Share Allocation Is PROP1 for Monotone Submodular Valuations and Equal Entitlements

Open Problem 3 in Section 6 of arXiv:2502.02815 asks whether every allocation of indivisible goods that is MXS-fair (minimum EFX share) to an agent is necessarily PROP1-fair (proportional up to one good) to that agent, under monotone submodular valuations and equal entitlements. This post presents a positive resolution of the problem. If an agent's assigned bundle were not PROP1-fair, the agent would suffer a strictly positive deficit $\delta > 0$ relative to their proportional share, and every individual good outside their bundle would provide marginal gain at most $\delta$. Examining a certificate allocation witnessing the minimum EFX share property allows the identification of at most one good from each other agent's bundle that has positive marginal value over the recipient's certificate bundle. Eliminating these goods reduces the value of each other bundle below the certificate bundle by the EFX guarantee. The proof then branches on whether every other agent's bundle contains such an item or at least one agent does not: in the former case, submodular monotonicity bounds the grand bundle valuation strictly below its true value; in the latter case, all items from agents without positive marginal goods contribute zero marginal value on top of the certificate bundle and collapse, again yielding a strict contradiction. The theorem has been formalized and mechanically checked in Lean 4 as mxs_fair_imp_prop1_fair_of_submodular.

The Cost of EF1 for Two Agents Is Strictly Less Than Half, Refuting a General Lower Bound

The cost of envy-freeness up to one chore (EF1) measures the worst-case additive gap between the minimum social cost achievable by an EF1 allocation and the unconstrained optimal social cost, evaluated over normalized instances with a fixed number of agents. In the literature on fair allocation of indivisible chores, Theorem 7 of arXiv:2410.15738 v1 claimed that for any number of agents $n \ge 1$, the cost of EF1 is at most $1$ and at least $1 - 1/n$. This post presents the formal refutation of this lower bound. When evaluated at $n = 2$, the claimed lower bound would require the cost of EF1 to be at least $1 - 1/2 = 1/2$. However, for two agents, the cost of EF1 is bounded above by $3 - 2\sqrt{2}$, a quantity strictly smaller than $1/2$, since $3 - 2\sqrt{2} \approx 0.1716$. The failure of the lower bound at $n = 2$ stems from the underlying lower-bound construction in arXiv:2410.15738 v1, which requires establishing EF1 conditions among agents indexed $1, 3, \ldots, n$, a setup that necessitates at least three agents and cannot be carried out when $n = 2$. Consequently, the universal claim that the cost of EF1 is at least $1 - 1/n$ for every $n \ge 1$ is false. All lemmas and the refutation theorem have been verified in Lean 4.

Every Weighted Voting Game Is Uniquely Determined Among All Simple Games by Its Swing Table

The swing-rigidity conjecture, posed as Conjecture 10.2 in arXiv:2607.07013, asks whether every weighted voting game is uniquely determined by its swing table among all simple games. While distinct non-weighted simple games can share identical swing tables—leaving them indistinguishable by any semivalue—weighted games were conjectured to be rigid. This question is answered in the affirmative: every weighted voting game is uniquely determined by its swing table among all simple games. The proof proceeds by establishing that the swing table of any simple game uniquely determines the number of winning coalitions of each cardinality, as well as the number of winning coalitions of each cardinality containing each individual player. Summing these counts recovers the total number of winning coalitions and the number of winning coalitions containing each player. By the characterization theorem weighted_game_eq_of_chow_parameters_eq, these parameters uniquely determine a weighted game among all Boolean functions on coalitions. Consequently, if a weighted simple game and any simple game share a swing table, they must coincide. The entire result and its supporting lemmas have been formally verified in Lean 4 under the theorem weighted_game_eq_of_swing_table_eq.
