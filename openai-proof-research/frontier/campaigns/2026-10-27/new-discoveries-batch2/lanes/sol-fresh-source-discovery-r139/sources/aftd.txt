An open question highlighted in arXiv:2610.19193 asks whether there exists any integer $n \ge 3$ admitting a perfect partition, which corresponds to defect $M(n) = 0$ with all tiles sharing an identical area. Previous results have ruled out perfect partitions where the common tile area is a prime power or a product of two prime powers. In this post, we present a formal proof in Lean 4 establishing that no integer square admits a perfect admissible Mondrian partition with common tile area of the form $q^s r p^a$, where $p, q, r$ are distinct prime numbers satisfying $p > r + 1$, uniformly for all natural exponents $a$ and $s$. The proof proceeds by strong induction on the exponent $s$. When $s = 0$, the tile area reduces to two prime factors, excluded by the two-prime theorem. When $s > 0$, total area divisibility forces $q$ to divide the square side $n$. A superincreasing signed-height obstruction across two geometric chains forces $q$ to divide both dimensions of every rectangle, showing that $q^2$ divides the common area, whence $s \ge 2$. Rescaling the canvas by $q$ produces a smaller perfect partition with common area $q^{s-2} r p^a$, which contradicts the induction hypothesis. These results are machine-checked in Lean 4.
