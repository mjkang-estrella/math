## Eight new distinct family screens

1. **Self-similar random quotient metric growth.** Bäumler–Hutchcroft, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39953v1, Theorems 1.1–1.2: for every random closed identification set satisfying independence of disjoint intervals (I), translation invariance (T), and scale invariance (S), there exists θ∈[0,1] such that expected block distance E D_[0,n](0,n−1) and expected block diameter are n^(θ+o(1)); θ<1 if the set is nonempty with positive probability. **Actual closest proof:** proof of Theorems 1.1–1.2, PDF p17, uses the maximal-distance exponent, regular scales and median comparison; source Section 1.3 gives counterexamples to uniform high-probability strengthenings. Already public, reject. The remaining simple-random-walk trace extension in Remark 1.4 lacks exact scale invariance; proving the Brownian theorem again does not bridge that gap. No tractable new mechanism for that extension was found.

2. **Prescribed-entry low rank over finite fields.** Firdavs Rakhmonov, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39908v1, Theorem 1.1: for every prime p with 2 a primitive root, every three-element L⊂F_p and every n≥1, there is a symmetric zero-diagonal matrix with off-diagonal values in L and rank ≤C_(p,L)n^(1/3). If 0∉L the exponent is optimal. **Actual closest proof:** PDF p7, M=aJ+δBBᵀ, incidence rank ≤1+DQ, then principal submatrix interpolation to arbitrary n. This publicly answers Bukh's named {1,4,6}⊂F_19 case, so reject. Removing the primitive-root hypothesis is a different problem requiring replacement of its geometric parameter lemma; it is not inferred from the public construction or promoted as an attributed open conjecture.

3. **Enumeration of lattice zonotopes.** Bodini–Buffière, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39900v1, Theorems 1.1 and 5.7: for every integer d≥2 the number z_d(n) of lattice zonotopes inscribed in [0,n]^d has the explicit equivalent α_d exp(γ_d)n^(β_d) exp(Q_d(n^(1/(d+1)))+I_crit(d,n)), where Q_d is an explicitly computable degree-d polynomial without constant term and I_crit is the specified contour contribution of nontrivial zeta zeros. The literal normalizations and coefficients are in Theorem 5.7, PDF pp27–28. **Actual closest body:** that theorem and its preceding saddle-point/error calculation. Already public, reject. Eliminating the zeta-zero term would require a new analytic-number-theory estimate, not a routine improvement to a saddle expansion. Campaign alias hits on zonotope support/projection concern geometric inequalities, not this enumeration theorem; no exact prior same-source hit was found in the bounded scan.

4. **Ancient mean-curvature-flow codimension.** Francesco Ruatta, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39877v1, Theorem 1.4, PDF pp2–3: every connected, boundaryless, space-time proper ancient MCF M^n→R^(n+p), n≥2,p≥1, with |B|²≤(4/(3n)−ε₀)|H|² for some ε₀>0, is either a static flat R^n or has |H|>0 and lies in one fixed affine (n+1)-space. For 2≤n≤4 the latter is a shrinking-sphere family. **Actual closest proof:** Section 6, PDF pp12–13, lets the localization radius tend to infinity and uses propagation of affine containment. Already public, reject. Source added note compares Cheng's independent body and explicitly limits the pinching improvements; no endpoint ε₀=0 claim is inferred.

5. **Optimal finite binary codes.** Sascha Kurz, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39870v1, Theorem 4: there is no binary [93,9,44] linear code. **Actual closest proof:** the theorem's body in Section 5 uses divisibility, residual-code exclusions, split weight enumerators, exhaustive LinCode lists and a line projection to [90,7,44]. Already public, reject. This is a concrete all-codes feasibility question, not an invented numerical subcase. The source also supplies its dimension-eight self-orthogonal length classification. Re-running these finite searches would receive no credit, and this screen does not claim independent reproduction of its computation.

6. **Edge-ordered hypergraph Ramsey growth.** Balko–Glišić, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39653v1, Theorem 1, PDF pp2–3: for every h≥2,q≥1 there is C=C(h,q)>0 such that every n-vertex edge-ordered h-uniform F with m≥1 edges satisfies R_edge(F;q)≤(2^(m² ln(m+1)) R_h(F;q)^(mn))^C and ≤R_h(F;q)^(Cm²). **Actual closest proof:** PDF pp8–9, probabilistic host construction and explicit logarithmic estimates, with isolated vertices and q=1/m=1 handled. This already answers Fox–Li's quantitative tower-height question; reject. It is edge order, not vertex order; no theorem for arbitrary vertex-ordered hypergraphs is claimed.

7. **Strong discretization of strict local martingales.** Antoine Jacquier, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39896v1, Theorem 2.10: under its explicit Assumptions 2.1,2.2,2.4,2.7,2.8 and for the source's level h^(−a) killed scheme with every a∈(0,1/(2γ)), E|X_N^h−X_T|≲h^(θa)ℓ(h^(−a))+√h. Here θ and ℓ are the source truncation-tail data; a is renamed to avoid confusion with stable α. **Actual closest proof:** Section 4.2, PDF pp14–16, three-event partition, truncation error, Hölder and high-moment control before killing. Already public, reject. This is not an unconditional Euler theorem for arbitrary superlinear diffusion coefficients. The source itself obtains its CEV consequence, so no new parameter instance is nominated.

8. **Metric-space Sobolev regularity.** Han–Wang, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39644v1, Theorem 1.2, PDF p4: finite-energy Korevaar–Schoen maps from finite-dimensional RCD(K,N) to complete CAT(0), with B_(8R)(o) compactly contained in Ω and X\B_(16R)(o) nonempty, satisfy |du|(x)≤C[E_(2R)(u;o)+P_Ru(x)] almost everywhere on B_R. Uniform boundedness of P_R gives a Lipschitz representative on B_(R/2), with the stated same bound. P_R is exactly the dyadic sum of square roots of normalized harmonic-replacement energy gaps in Definition 1.1. **Actual closest proof:** Section 3.3, PDF pp18–19, iterate (41), sum the dyadic gaps, use Lebesgue differentiation. Already public, reject. Boundedness of the potential is substantive; arbitrary finite-energy maps are not thereby Lipschitz.

## Sole ranked survivor: stable near-unit persistence

**Exact source:** Mendonça–Smii, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.17927v3, Conjecture 6.1, p19: every 0<α<2, iid symmetric α-stable innovations, x>0,0<a<1, prove lim[a↑1]Λ(a,α)/log(1/a)=α/2. All σ>0 are covered by rate scale invariance. Current arXiv id query on the research date still returns v3. First publication is August 18,2026; precise version timestamps are in chronology.json. The r224 handoff is an intentional prior investigation, not evidence of a fresh family.

**Closest actual proofs:** source Lemmas 5.1–5.3 and Theorem 5.4 give the Lamperti transition, continuous rate 1/2, sampled upper bound and existence of the dense limit, but do not identify it. Bogdan–Grzywny–Ryznar0905.2626v4 Example 2 and (38)–(39), read in its actual proof context, give an all-time half-line killed heat-kernel estimate. That classical result alone is not the conjecture: the uniform weighted block bridge is supplied in PROOF.md.

**Mechanism and result:** choose α/2<p<α, w=1+x^p. Prove ||Q_T||_w≤Ce^(−T/2), C independent of T≥1. At fixed T, dyadic sampled killed operators converge to Q_T in weighted operator norm: Dini on compact starting-point sets, boundary regularity at zero, and stable maximal p-moment at infinity. Iterate each fixed sampled block first; refine its mesh next; only then send T→∞. This proves the full requested limit as an ordinary argument, with no finite-time/long-time interchange assumed.

**Next decisive check:** independent adversarial verification of the compact-plus-tail norm convergence and of the all-time half-space scope, followed by contemporary general-semigroup prior/subsumption review. A numerical sweep is not useful for these quantifiers.

**Eligibility objection:** it may be a short routine consequence of classical positive-kernel machinery, or a public general dense-killing theorem may already contain the exact bridge. Current public proof-registry heads, comprehensive citations and final journal intervals are not cleared; forbidden routes remain parked.
