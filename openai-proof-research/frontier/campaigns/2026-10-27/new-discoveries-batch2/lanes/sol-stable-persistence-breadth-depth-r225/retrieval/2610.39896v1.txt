and Mijatović and Urusov [36], in which case the martingale defect reads x0 − E[XT ] =
x0 Q(τ∞ ≤ T ) for the (Föllmer) measure Q under which the process X explodes [3, 32]. A
typical behaviour of such processes is that they ‘take large values’ more often than their
true martingale counterparts. Because of this, two distinct obstructions appear when
using the usual Euler-Maruyama scheme. The first is specific to strict local martingales:
the Euler iterates Xn+1 = Xn + σ(Xn )∆n form a discrete martingale, so E[XN ] = x0 for
every step size, whereas E[XT ] < x0 ; hence ∥XN − XT ∥L1 ≥ x0 − E[XT ] > 0 and the
scheme cannot converge in L1 . The second obstruction is the super-linear growth of σ,
so that moments of the Euler iterates diverge in finite time [27]. The different proposed
schemes, tamed [28, 41], truncated [34, 35], implicit [1, 37], adaptive step-sizes [15, 33] ,
work under some coercivity condition or some dominating Lyapunov function arguments.
However, these conditions fail for our canonical example σ(x) ≡ x1+γ (γ > 0), as moments
greater than 1 are infinite. Explicit schemes for locally Lipschitz coefficients without such
moment assumptions converge in probability [25] but carry no rate, and the lower bounds
in [23] show that for non-Lipschitz σ, arbitrarily slow strong rates can be unavoidable, so
that rates below the usual 21 are to be expected.
   The closest general framework is the monograph by Hutzenthaler and Jentzen [26],
who observed that the Euler iterates nevertheless satisfy Lyapunov-type inequalities on
the complement of rare events and that the probability of leaving this region decays
polynomially in the step, which allows to obtain uniform moment bounds for tamed Euler
schemes, but stressing that no rate can be expected at this level of generality. Our scheme
shares this approach by restricting the analysis to high-probability events and letting the
admissible region grow as a negative power of the step, which is also the idea behind Mao’s
truncated scheme [34, 35]. It differs from [26] though in three aspects: (i) the admissible
Lyapunov function V in [26] must satisfy V (x) ≥ |x|q , that is V (x) ≡ 1 + xq with q ≤ 1
for the CEV model, and the convergence threshold in [26, Theorem 3.15] becomes non-
positive, so no strong convergence holds; (ii) we do not tame the increments but kill the

scheme at a level Rh tied to the step; (iii) we obtain an explicit rate E[|XN   − XT |] ≲ hθα ,
with θ the tail exponent of the truncation error T (R) = E[XT 1{τR ≤T } ] and γ the growth
exponent of σ.
