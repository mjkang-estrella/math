                                                                                         Abstract
                                                     We establish a quantitative criterion for local Lipschitz regularity of Sobolev maps in
                                                 the sense of Korevaar and Schoen from ﬁnite-dimensional RCD(K, N ) spaces into complete
                                                 CAT(0) spaces, without assuming harmonicity. We quantify the failure of local energy
                                                 minimality by the harmonic replacement potential, a dyadic sum of the square roots of
                                                 normalized energy gaps between a map and its harmonic replacements. We prove that this
                                                 potential, together with an averaged energy term, controls the minimal weak upper gradient
                                                 pointwise, with constants depending only on the curvature-dimension bounds and the scale.
                                                 In particular, local boundedness of the replacement potential implies that the map admits
                                                 a locally Lipschitz representative.

                                           Keywords. Harmonic maps; harmonic replacement; RCD spaces; CAT(0) targets; Lipschitz
                                           regularity; Green functions.

1     Introduction
1.1   Background and motivation
    Harmonic maps between Riemannian manifolds are the critical points of the Dirichlet energy.
The study of harmonic maps is closely related to minimal surface theory and to Bochner’s
method; see [32, 41]. For nonpositively curved targets, the Dirichlet energy is convex along
geodesic homotopies. For the classical theory of harmonic maps into nonpositively curved
manifolds, we refer to Eells–Sampson [7], and, for manifolds with boundary, Hamilton [18]. For
general smooth targets, the interior and boundary regularity theory was developed by Schoen–
Uhlenbeck [39, 40]; see also [19, 26, 10, 11] for related variational results.
    The extension to singular spaces requires an energy deﬁned without coordinates on the target.
In the remarkable work of Gromov and Schoen [16], the authors proposed a variational approach
for the theory of harmonic mappings to the setting of mappings into singular metric spaces, along
with important applications to rigidity problems for certain discrete groups. Korevaar–Schoen
[27] developed an intrinsic Sobolev and energy theory for maps from Riemannian domains into
complete metric spaces and established existence and uniqueness for the Dirichlet problem
with nonpositively curved targets. Independently, Jost [22] used averages of squared distances,
equilibrium maps, and Γ-convergence to develop a variational approach already allowing metric
source spaces. He subsequently developed the theory through convex functionals and generalized
Dirichlet forms [23, 24]. Sturm [42, 43] pursued a parallel route through Dirichlet forms and
Markov semigroups. In this paper, harmonic maps are understood to be local minimizers of the
Korevaar–Schoen energy.
    The central regularity question is whether the local Lipschitz continuity proved by Korevaar–
Schoen [27] for smooth Riemannian sources persists on singular domains. Lin [29] obtained
Hölder regularity results for Alexandrov sources with curvature bounded below, while Jost [24]
established related estimates in the framework of generalized Dirichlet forms. Lin conjectured
local Lipschitz continuity in the Alexandrov setting, and Jost posed a related question in a
more general framework [25, p. 38]. Zhang–Zhu [47] resolved Lin’s conjecture by proving local
Lipschitz continuity for harmonic maps from Alexandrov sources with curvature bounded below
into NPC spaces.
    Finite-dimensional RCD(K, N ) spaces [3, 8] provide a framework for studying this regularity
problem under a synthetic lower Ricci curvature bound. Gigli–Tyulenev [14] developed the
Korevaar–Schoen energy and Dirichlet theory in this setting, with complete CAT(0) targets.
Their results on strongly rectiﬁable spaces apply also to collapsed RCD(K, N ) spaces and give
a representation of the energy density in terms of the metric diﬀerential.
    The regularity question is whether a lower Ricci curvature bound suﬃces for quantitative
interior Lipschitz estimates. For smooth Riemannian sources and complete CAT(0) targets,
Zhang–Zhong–Zhu [46] established such estimates using a distributional Bochner inequality
for the squared pointwise Lipschitz constant. In the RCD setting, local Lipschitz continuity,
with quantitative interior estimates, was established in independent works by Gigli [12] and
Mondino–Semola [31]. Gigli proved a version of the Zhang–Zhong–Zhu inequality in this set-
ting. Mondino–Semola also established a Bochner inequality for the squared pointwise Lipschitz
constant containing the nonnegative term |D(lip u)|2 . Boundary regularity for such maps was
studied by Zhang–Zhu [48, 49].
    These Bochner inequalities concern the squared pointwise Lipschitz constant, which, for
smooth maps between Riemannian manifolds, equals the squared operator norm of the diﬀer-
ential. The squared energy density corresponds instead, up to normalization, to the squared
Hilbert–Schmidt norm. This distinction is important for the comparison argument below, where
harmonic replacement energy gaps control the L2 -diﬀerence between the minimal weak upper
gradients of a map and its harmonic replacement.
    Our question concerns maps that need not locally minimize the Dirichlet energy: How much


                                               2
may a Sobolev map deviate from local Dirichlet minimality while remaining locally Lipschitz?
Such maps arise naturally in variational models of phase transitions and free boundaries, where
a Dirichlet term is combined with a potential energy. Beyond these variational applications,
our original motivation for this question came from a diﬀerent direction, the local Lipschitz
regularity of the time discretization of the harmonic map heat ﬂow, whose comparison structure
leads to the same potential.
    The ﬁrst step is to identify a suitable quantity measuring the failure of local energy mini-
mality. On a ball B, we compare u with its unique harmonic replacement hB , which minimizes
the Dirichlet energy with the same boundary values. The resulting nonnegative energy gap
E(u; B) − E(hB ; B) is deﬁned for every ﬁnite-energy map, without reference to a target-valued
equation or a tension ﬁeld.
    To pass from this local energy gap to a pointwise estimate, we adapt the comparison method
of nonlinear potential theory. In the elliptic setting, one compares a solution u of an inhomo-
geneous equation Lu = f on a ball B with its harmonic replacement, the solution of the
homogeneous equation Lu = 0 on B with the same boundary values, and the comparison errors,
accumulated over dyadic balls, are controlled by a potential of the data f (of Riesz or Wolﬀ
type), which yields the pointwise estimates for u; see [28, 6]. In our setting no equation is
available; the harmonic replacement hB still plays the role of the homogeneous solution, and
the energy gap E(u; B) − E(hB ; B) plays the role of the data. Accumulating these gaps over
dyadic balls deﬁnes the harmonic replacement potential; see Deﬁnition 1.1 below.
