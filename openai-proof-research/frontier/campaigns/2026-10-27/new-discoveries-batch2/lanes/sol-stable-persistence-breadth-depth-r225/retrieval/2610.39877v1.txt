                                                                                        1. Introduction
                                              Given n ≥ 2 and p ≥ 1, a one-parameter family of immersed and connected submanifolds
                                           without boundary x(·, t) : M n → Rn+p moves by mean curvature flow if ∂t x = H(·, t), where
                                           the mean curvature vector H(·, t) is defined as the trace of the second fundamental form B
                                           with respect to the induced metric g. A solution is called ancient if it is defined on the time
                                           interval (−∞, T ), where T ≤ ∞.
                                              A submanifold is c-(quadratically) pinched (resp. uniformly c-(quadratically) pinched ) if
                                           |B|2 ≤ c|H|2 (resp. if |B|2 ≤ (c − ε0 )|H|2 for some ε0 > 0). This property is important mainly
                                           for two reasons. First, the pinching condition |B|2 + a < c|H|2 , with a > 0, is preserved along
                                                                            4
                                           closed flows provided that c ≤ 3n  , as shown in [AB10, Lyn26]. Second, suitable choices of the
                                           pinching constant c reflect convexity properties in codimension p = 1. More precisely, every
                                                        1
                                           uniformly n−k  -pinched hypersurface (with 1 ≤ k ≤ n − 1) whose unit normal is chosen so
                                           that the scalar mean curvature H is positive is uniformly k-convex; see [HS15, Lemma 5.1].

                                           was established by Naff [Naf22] through the so-called planarity estimate. More precisely, given

                     6. Codimension reduction for ancient solutions
   The next lemma shows that, along space-time proper flows, containment in an affine sub-
space y + Π propagates forward in time. The natural approach is to prove the vanishing of the
squared distance a = | projΠ⊥ (x − y)|2 , which vanishes at the initial time and is a subsolution
of the heat equation. Then, for closed flows, the maximum principle applied to a gives the
claim. In the noncompact case, we make use of the parabolic Omori–Yau maximum principle
of [Ma18]. To pursue this route we have to consider the quotient a/b, where b dominates a, as
the function a alone grows quadratically and [Ma18, Theorem 1.2] asks for sublinear growth.
If, in addition, b solves the heat equation, the quotient only produces gradient terms, which
vanish along the parabolic Omori–Yau sequence. The idea for the right function b comes
12                                      FRANCESCO RUATTA

from [Eck04, (3.4)]. One then multiplies the quotient by a negative exponential, as in [Ma18,
Theorem 1.3], to get the desired contradiction.
