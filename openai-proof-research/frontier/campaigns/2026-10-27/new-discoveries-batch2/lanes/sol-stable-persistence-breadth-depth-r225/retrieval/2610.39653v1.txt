                                                                                           Abstract
                                                     For an integer h ≥ 2, an edge-ordered h-uniform hypergraph H < is an h-uniform
                                                 hypergraph with a linear ordering of its hyperedges. For an integer q ≥ 1, the edge-
                                                 ordered Ramsey number Redge (H < ; q) of H < is the smallest positive integer N such
                                                 that there exists an edge-ordered h-uniform hypergraph G< on N vertices such that,
                                                 for every q-coloring of the hyperedges of G< , there is a monochromatic copy of H <
                                                 in G< preserving the hyperedge order. In this note, we show that, for every edge-
                                                 ordered h-uniform hypergraph F < on n vertices with m ≥ 1 hyperedges, Redge (F < ; q) ≤
                                                              2
                                                 Rh (F ; q)Cm where F is the underlying unordered hypergraph, Rh (F ; q) is its standard
                                                 q-color Ramsey number, and C = C(h, q) > 0 is a constant. For fixed h ≥ 3 and q ≥ 2,
                                                 this shows that prescribing the hyperedge order does not require an increase in the
                                                 tower height of the classical upper bound. It also settles a problem posed by Fox and
                                                                                               4
                                                 Li (2020), and for h = 2 gives the bound 2O(n log n) , improving their bounds for every
                                                 fixed q ≥ 3.

graphs; see the recent survey [1].
    Graphs with linearly ordered edges, called edge-ordered graphs, have attracted increasing
attention in extremal combinatorics, with a growing body of results concerning their Turán
numbers [9, 12, 14]. Balko and Vizer [3] initiated the study of Ramsey numbers of edge-
ordered graphs and proved their finiteness using the Graham–Rothschild theorem. More
generally, for an edge-ordered h-uniform hypergraph H < , the edge-ordered Ramsey number
Redge (H < ; q) is the minimum number of vertices in an edge-ordered h-uniform hypergraph
such that every q-coloring of its hyperedges contains a monochromatic copy of H < preserving
the hyperedge order. Note that the edge order of the host is chosen as part of the construction,
before the coloring.
    The edge-ordered Ramsey theory remains a relatively young area, with many basic
questions concerning the growth of its Ramsey numbers still open. Fox and Li [8] proved the
bound                                                2q−2

for every n-vertex edge-ordered graph G< and every fixed q ≥ 2, as well as polynomial
bounds in the two-color case for graphs of bounded degeneracy. For higher uniformities, the
finiteness of edge-ordered Ramsey numbers follows from a general structural Ramsey theorem
of Hubička and Nešetřil [11, Section 4.3.4], which allows linear orders on both the vertices
and the tuples in each relation of a finite relational structure. Their proof is constructive,
but the resulting upper bounds are enormous, as noted by Fox and Li [8, Section 8.4], who
explicitly raised the problem of obtaining better quantitative estimates for edge-ordered
hypergraph Ramsey numbers, already in the two-color case.
    A natural quantitative refinement of this problem is to ask whether, for fixed uniformity
h and number of colors q, the edge-ordered Ramsey numbers of n-vertex hypergraphs can
be bounded by a tower of exponentials whose height is independent of n. More precisely,
the classical upper bound Rh (n; q) ≤ towh (O(n)) provides a natural benchmark. Can an
arbitrary prescribed order on the hyperedges be accommodated without increasing the height
of this tower?


2    Our results
In this note, we address the problem posed by Fox and Li [8], and we give a better estimate
for hypergraph edge-ordered Ramsey numbers. Our main result gives an explicit upper bound
for the edge-ordered Ramsey number of an arbitrarily edge-ordered h-uniform hypergraph

its underlying unordered hypergraph F .

by the work of Conlon, Dellamonica, La Fleur, Rödl, and Schacht [4], who used this method
to bound induced hypergraph Ramsey numbers in terms of their classical counterparts. We
assign labels to host hyperedges and encode copies with labels increasing in the prescribed
edge order as hyperedges of an auxiliary hypergraph. The container theorem controls color
classes avoiding these copies, and a random choice of one label for each host hyperedge
produces, with positive probability, an edge order for which such classes cannot cover the
host.

    The proof of Theorem 1 is based on the following result by Saxton and Thomason [13],
called the Container theorem; see Corollary 3.6 in [13] and the paragraph immediately
following it.
Theorem 2 (The Container theorem [13]). For an m-uniform hypergraph H on v vertices
with average degree d > 0, if 0 < η, τ < 1/2 and δ(H, τ ) ≤ η/(12m!), then there is a family
C of subsets of V (H) satisfying the following three conditions:
(a) Every independent set in H is contained in some C ∈ C.
(b) e(H[C]) ≤ ηe(H) for every C ∈ C.
(c) ln |C| ≤ cm ln(1/η)vτ ln(1/τ ), where cm = 800(m!)3 m is permissible.
    Intuitively, the container theorem gives a compact description of all independent sets
in the hypergraph H. Every independent set lies inside one of relatively few containers
C ∈ C, each spanning only a small portion of the hyperedges of H. The codegree condition
δ(H, τ ) ≤ η/(12m!) ensures that hyperedges of H do not overlap too heavily on small vertex
sets. The parameter η controls the portion of hyperedges in each container, while τ controls
the number of containers.
    In typical applications, one constructs a hypergraph whose hyperedges represent forbidden
configurations, so that independent sets correspond to the objects under consideration. A
supersaturation argument then converts the bound on the number of hyperedges in each
container into a bound on its size. One can subsequently count subsets of the containers or
use a union bound over the container family in a probabilistic argument.
