                                                            EPPA FOR REDUCTS OF THE RANDOM GRAPH

                                                                  SOFIA BRENNER, JAN HUBIČKA, AND MATĚJ KONEČNÝ


                                                   Abstract. We prove that finite two-graphs have coherent EPPA and that neither graphs
                                                   modulo global complementation nor two-graphs modulo global complementation have EPPA.
                                                   This completes the classification of EPPA and coherent EPPA for the reducts of the random
                                                   graph, answering a question and confirming a conjecture of Evans, Hubička, Konečný and
                                                   Nešetřil.

                                                                                       1. Introduction
                                               A class of finite structures C has the extension property for partial automorphisms (EPPA),
                                           sometimes also called the Hrushovski property, if for every A ∈ C there is B ∈ C containing A as
                                           an induced substructure such that every partial automorphism of A (that is, every isomorphism
                                           between induced substructures of A) extends to an automorphism of B. We call such B an
                                           EPPA-witness for A and write Aut(B) for its automorphism group.
                                               Coherent EPPA asks in addition that the extensions can be chosen compatibly with com-
                                           position. More precisely, an EPPA-witness B for A is coherent if there is a map Φ from
                                           partial automorphisms of A to Aut(B) such that Φ(p) extends p and, whenever p : D → E and
                                           q : E → F are partial automorphisms,
                                                                                      Φ(q ◦ p) = Φ(q)Φ(p).
                                           A class has coherent EPPA if every member has a coherent EPPA-witness. See Siniora and
                                           Solecki [SS20] for more background on this notion, and [HK26] for a recent survey of EPPA and
                                           related classification problems. One motivation for coherent EPPA is that if M is a countable
                                           homogeneous relational structure whose age has coherent EPPA, then Aut(M ) contains a dense
                                           locally finite subgroup [SS20, Proposition 5.1].
                                              Hrushovski proved in 1992 that the class of all finite graphs has EPPA [Hru92]. This was one
                                           of the ingredients used by Hodges, Hodkinson, Lascar and Shelah in their proof of the small
                                           index property for the random graph [HHLS93], and it initiated a line of work studying EPPA
                                           for concrete classes of finite structures (see, for example, [EHN21, HKN22, EHKN20]). Here
                                           we consider this problem for reducts of the random graph.
                                              Thomas [Tho91] classified the first-order reducts of the countable random graph R up to
                                           first-order interdefinability. Equivalently, he classified the closed permutation groups on V (R)
                                           containing Aut(R). There are five of them. Recall that a two-graph is a 3-uniform hypergraph
                                           in which every four vertices span an even number of hyperedges. On the level of finite structures
                                           we will use the following representatives:
                                             (1) the class G of all finite graphs;
                                             (2) the class S of all finite sets;
                                             (3) the class T of all finite two-graphs;
                                             (4) the class G ± of finite sets equipped with an equivalence relation on unordered pairs with
                                                 at most two classes; equivalently, finite graphs modulo global complementation;
                                             (5) the class T ± of finite sets equipped with an equivalence relation on unordered triples
                                                 with at most two classes, where each equivalence class is a two-graph; equivalently, finite
                                                 two-graphs modulo global complementation.

   Evans, Hubička, Konečný and Nešetřil [EHKN20] proved that finite two-graphs have EPPA.
They asked whether two-graphs have coherent EPPA and, in Remark 8.2, observed that EPPA
for the last two classes in the list above was open and conjectured that neither class has EPPA.
   In this paper we answer the question and confirm the conjecture. In particular, we obtain
the complete classification.
Theorem 1.1. For the five reducts of the random graph, EPPA and coherent EPPA are as
follows.
                         finite structures       EPPA             coherent EPPA
                               G                   yes                 yes
                               S                   yes                 yes
                               T                   yes                 yes
                               G±                  no                  no
                               T±                  no                  no

   In Section 2 we give a simple six-vertex obstruction to EPPA for G ± . In Section 3 we adapt
the method of valuation functions [HO03, EHKN20] to prove coherent EPPA for two-graphs. In
Section 4 we show by a rooting argument that EPPA for T ± would imply EPPA for G ± . Finally,
in Section 5, we discuss EPPA for reducts of random hypergraphs, and EPPA for graphs with
loops modulo complementation.

                               3. Coherent EPPA for two-graphs
   We now turn to two-graphs. A two-graph is a 3-uniform hypergraph (V, T ) such that every
four-element subset of V contains an even number of hyperedges. Equivalently, for a graph G on
V , let T (G) consist of the triples with an odd number of edges; then (V, T (G)) is a two-graph.
In fact, every two-graph is of this form, and T (G) depends only on the Seidel-switching class of
G.

In [EHKN20], a two-graph is first represented as an antipodal metric space of diameter 3, an
EPPA-witness is constructed there, and its antipodal pairs are then identified to recover a
two-graph.
   We make two adjustments to the construction in [EHKN20]:
    (1) Instead of working with antipodal metric spaces of diameter 3, we quotient already on
        the level of valuation functions. This makes it possible to choose extensions such that
        we obtain a homomorphism from the automorphism group of the input two-graph to
        the automorphism group of its EPPA-witness.
    (2) Here X is chosen much larger: its size is divisible by the size of every induced substructure
        of the input. This allows us to extend the automorphism group of each such substructure
        homomorphically by repeating its action on blocks, and then obtain coherence from the
        criterion below.

                                           5. Conclusion
   Although EPPA and coherent EPPA coincide for the five reducts considered here, they
need not coincide in general. Evans asked whether EPPA for the age of a homogeneous
ω-categorical structure implies that its automorphism group contains a dense locally finite
subgroup (see [BM05, Question 3.1] and [Sá24]). Siniora subsequently asked whether EPPA
implies coherent EPPA and whether the existence of a dense locally finite subgroup implies
coherent EPPA [Sin17, Questions 3 and 4]. In forthcoming work on generalised semigeneric
structures, examples separating all three properties are constructed, giving negative answers to
these questions.
5.1. Graphs with loops. Curiously, EPPA holds for a variant of G ± . Concretely, Beliayeu
proved in his bachelor’s thesis [Bel23] that if loops are allowed and global complementation
is required to complement the loops together with all other edges, then EPPA holds. Here a
graph with loops is a finite set with a symmetric binary relation E, with loops allowed; a partial
bijection is a morphism if it either preserves or complements E on its domain, including the
loops. Unlike in the loopless case, a complementing automorphism cannot fix even one vertex,
since it would have to complement the loop at that vertex.
Proposition 5.1 (Beliayeu [Bel23]). Every finite graph with loops has a finite extension in
which every partial morphism extends to a total morphism of the same kind.
Proof sketch. Let A = (V, E) and fix a linear order on V . Let P be the graph on {0, 1, 2, 3}

and range. To see this, suppose that every structure in G ± had a finite extension in which all
such partial automorphisms extend. Given A ∈ G ± , take two disjoint isomorphic copies A0 , A1
of A inside a structure Ae ∈ G ± , and let ι : A0 → A1 be the natural isomorphism. For any partial
automorphism φ of A0 , both ι−1 and ι ◦ φ have disjoint domains and ranges. Extending both
in a common witness for Ae and composing the extensions would extend φ. This would give
EPPA for G ± , contradicting Theorem 2.2.

5.3. Reducts of random hypergraphs. Taking m = 2 in Proposition 5.2 shows that finite
r-uniform hypergraphs modulo global complementation do not have EPPA for every r ≥ 2.
This is one of the negative cases in the higher-arity analogue of Theorem 1.1. Thomas classified
the reducts of the countable random r-uniform hypergraph [Tho96], and in forthcoming work
we give their complete EPPA classification. For every r ≥ 2, the only positive cases are the pure
set, the random r-uniform hypergraph itself, and one natural switching reduct, which can be
represented by (r + 1)-uniform hypergraphs in which every (r + 2)-element set contains an even
number of hyperedges; for r = 2 this is precisely the class of two-graphs. Thus Theorem 1.1 is
the first non-trivial instance of the corresponding higher-arity picture.
