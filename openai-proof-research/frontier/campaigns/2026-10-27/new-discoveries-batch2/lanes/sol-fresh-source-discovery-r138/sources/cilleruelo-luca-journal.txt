                                  For Andrzej Schinzel on his seventy-fifth birthday



      1. Introduction. Let p(n) be the partition function of n, which is the
number of ways of writing n = λ1 + · · · + λk , where k ≥ 1 and 1 ≤ λ1 ≤ · · ·
· · · ≤ λk are integers. There is a huge literature on this function, dealing with
its size, congruence properties, recurrence relations, and so on. Let P (m) be
the largest prime factor of the positive integer m with the convention that
P (1) = 1, and let ω(m) be the number of distinct prime factors of m. In re-
sponse to a question of Erdős and Ivić, Schinzel showed that ω( N

tends to infinity with N (Lemma 2 in [2]). His method used lower bounds
for nonzero linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers. Later, Schinzel
and Wirsing [6] proved the effective result

    Here, we revisit Schinzel’s original argument to prove the following result.
    Theorem 1. The set of n for which

by a different method that P (p(n)) > log log log log log log n for almost all
