Motivated by the Z Z -polynomials of matroids, Ferroni, Matherne, Stevens, and Vecchi introduced the inverse Z Z -polynomial of a matroid. In this paper, we prove several fundamental properties of the inverse Z Z -polynomial, including non-negativity and multiplicativity, and show that it is a valuative invariant. We also provide explicit formulas for the inverse Z Z -polynomials of uniform matroids and a broader class of matroids, namely sparse paving matroids, which include uniform matroids as a special case. Furthermore, we establish the unimodality and log-concavity of these polynomials in the case of sparse paving matroids. Based on the properties of the Z Z -polynomial, we conjecture that the coefficients of the inverse Z Z -polynomial are unimodal and log-concave. Xie, xie@email.tjut.edu.cn 1 Introduction Since the introduction of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of matroids by Elias, Proudfoot, and Wakefield [ 5 ] , these polynomials have attracted much attention. For instance, see [ 4 , 6 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 19 ] and references therein. In [ 20 ] , Proudfoot, Xu, and Young introduced the Z Z -polynomial Z 𝖬 ​ ( t ) Z_{\mathsf{M}}(t) of a matroid 𝖬 \mathsf{M} , which is defined as the weighted sum of the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials for all possible contractions of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} . They also demonstrated that, for realizable matroids, the coefficients of Z 𝖬 ​ ( t ) Z_{\mathsf{M}}(t) can be interpreted as the intersection cohomology Betti numbers of a projective variety. A precise description of this intersection cohomology for any matroid was later provided by Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang [ 3 ] . Within the framework of Kazhdan-Lusztig-Stanley theory, Ferroni, Matherne, Stevens, and Vecchi [ 7 ] introduced the inverse Z Z -polynomial of a matroid. Their work in [ 7 ] primarily focuses on the Hilbert-Poincaré series of the Chow rings and augmented Chow rings of matroids and does not pay much attention to the inverse Z Z -polynomials, despite their many interesting properties that are worth further exploration. The main objective of this paper is to compute the inverse Z Z -polynomials of uniform matroids and sparse paving matroids. In addition, we study the unimodality and log-concavity of the inverse Z Z -polynomials of sparse paving matroids. In fact, based on Theorem 1.2 , we derive a general formula for the inverse Z Z -polynomials of large classes of matroids, including elementary split matroids and (sparse) paving matroids. Let us first review the definitions of elementary split matroids and (sparse) paving matroids. Let 𝖬 = ( E , B ) \mathsf{M}=(E,{B}) be a matroid. An independent set of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} is a set I ⊆ E I\subseteq E contained in some B ⊆ B B\subseteq{B} . If a set is not independent, we will say that it is dependent. A circuit of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} is a minimal dependent set. A matroid 𝖬 \mathsf{M} is called paving if all its circuits have size at least rk ⁡ ( 𝖬 ) \rk(\mathsf{M}) . If both 𝖬 \mathsf{M} and its dual 𝖬 ∗ \mathsf{M}^{*} are paving, then 𝖬 \mathsf{M} is referred to as a sparse paving matroid. A well-known conjecture by Mayhew, Newman, Welsh, and Whittle [ 17 ] affirms that asymptotically, almost all matroids are sparse paving. The class of elementary split matroids is somewhat more complicated to define. First introduced in [ 2 ] , they form a subclass of the split matroids introduced by Joswig and Schröter in [ 16 ] . Ferroni and Schröter [ 9 ] conjectured that the class of elementary split matroids is strictly larger than the class of sparse paving matroids. It is expected that elementary split matroids will dominate even when considering only non sparse paving matroids. The aforementioned classes of matroids can be equivalently defined through the relaxation of stressed subsets. Let 𝖬 \mathsf{M} be a matroid on the ground set E E with rank k k . A subset A ⊆ E A\subseteq E is called stressed if both the restriction 𝖬 | A \mathsf{M}|_{A} and the contraction 𝖬 / A \mathsf{M}/A are isomorphic to uniform matroids. Ferroni and Schröter [ 9 , Section 3] describe how to construct a new matroid on E E by relaxing a stressed subset A A , which generalizes the well-known circuit-hyperplane relaxation (A flat of rank rk ⁡ ( 𝖬 ) − 1 \rk(\mathsf{M})-1 is said to be a hyperplane). Precisely, the cusp of A A is defined as the collection of k k -sets cusp 𝖬 ⁡ ( A ) := { S ∈ ( E k ) : | S ∩ A | ≥ rk ⁡ ( A ) + 1 } , \cusp_{\mathsf{M}}(A):=\left\{S\in\binom{E}{k}:|S\cap A|\geq\rk(A)+1\right\}, and a new matroid is constructed on the ground set E E whose family of bases is given by B ∪ cusp 𝖬 ⁡ ( A ) {B}\cup\cusp_{\mathsf{M}}(A) . This matroid is referred to as the relaxation of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} by A A and is denoted by Rel ⁡ ( 𝖬 , A ) \Rel(\mathsf{M},A) . If A A and A ′ A^{\prime} are two distinct stressed subsets of 𝖬 \mathsf{M} , then A ′ A^{\prime} remains stressed in Rel ⁡ ( 𝖬 , A ) \Rel(\mathsf{M},A) , and the cusp of A ′ A^{\prime} in Rel ⁡ ( 𝖬 , A ) \Rel(\mathsf{M},A) is given by cusp 𝖬 ⁡ ( A ′ ) \cusp_{\mathsf{M}}(A^{\prime}) .
