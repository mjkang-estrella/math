                                          1. Introduction
                                             Let K ⊂ Rd be a compact convex body. The conjecture of Zaporozhets and Tarasov
                                          asks whether
                                                             ∆(K) := E|X1 − X2 | ≤ E|B1 − B2 | =: θ(K)
                                          for every convex body K ⊂ Rd , where X1 , X2 are uniform in K with respect to the
                                          normalized Lebesgue measure and B1 , B2 are uniform on ∂K with respect to the
                                          normalized arclength measure.
                                              Lotnikov proved the planar centrally symmetric case and an analogue for sufficiently
                                          high moments in arbitrary dimension [2]. Tokmachev proved the conjecture, in fact a
                                          convex-order statement, for planar bodies whose area and boundary centroids coincide
                                          and for a class of circumscribed polytopes [4]. Related extremal questions for ∆ and θ
                                          were studied in [1, 3]. In particular, Bonnet, Gusakova, Thäle and Zaporozhets proved
                                          the sharp inequalities
                                                                                7   ∆(K)      1
                                                                                  <        <
                                                                               60   per K     6
                                          for planar convex bodies [1], where per K stands for perimeter of K. It is also shown
                                          that the upper bound is approached by thin rectangles converging to a doubled segment.
                                          According to Tokmachev [4, Eq. (5)], Gusakova and Zaporozhets suggested an analogous
                                          pair of inequalities for θ
                                                                               1   θ(K)     2
                                                                                 <        ≤ 2.

    Prior to that, we prove the Zaporozhets-Tarasov conjecture in the case d = 2, see
Corollary 3.2. The result is mostly self-contained, in particular, it does not use the
aforementioned inequalities on ∆(K) from [1] and is obtained as a corollary of a more
general statement, see Theorem 2.1. We also prove an analogous inequality for the
second moment, see Corollary 3.3. In addition to that, we show that the conjecture is
false in every dimension d ≥ 3, see Example 4.2, Figure 1 and Proposition 4.5.

    Let Kn be smooth strictly convex bodies converging to K in the Hausdorff metric.
Then the normalized area measures on Kn converge weakly to normalized area measure
on K, see e.g. [8]. Likewise, the normalized arclength measures on ∂Kn converge
weakly to normalized arclength measure on ∂K; this follows from the weak continuity
of curvature measures under Hausdorff convergence, see e.g. [7, 8]. Consequently, their
pushforwards under x 7→ ⟨x, u⟩ converge weakly as well. Since all these measures are
supported in a common compact interval, the Gini functionals converge, and passing to
the limit proves the result for K.

4. A long-body observation
    In this section, we construct a three-dimensional counterexample to the Zaporozhets-
Tarasov conjecture, see Example 4.2. This example is then used to construct coun-
terexamples in higher dimensions, see Proposition 4.5. The main observation, discussed
in Remark 4.4, is that in dimension three the area and perimeter profiles of sections
orthogonal to a fixed direction can be controlled almost independently. We exploit
this freedom to make the projected Gini mean difference strictly larger for the volume
measure than for the surface measure. A sufficiently large stretch in the projection
direction then yields the required counterexample.
Lemma 4.1. Suppose a random point has the form Z = (LT, U ) ∈ R × Rm , where
T ∈ [0, 1] and the support of U has diameter at most D. If Z1 , Z2 are independent
copies, then
                    LE|T1 − T2 | ≤ E|Z1 − Z2 | ≤ LE|T1 − T2 | + D.
Proof. The lower bound follows by projection onto the first coordinate. For the upper
bound, use
                  |(L(T1 − T2 ), U1 − U2 )| ≤ L|T1 − T2 | + |U1 − U2 |.
