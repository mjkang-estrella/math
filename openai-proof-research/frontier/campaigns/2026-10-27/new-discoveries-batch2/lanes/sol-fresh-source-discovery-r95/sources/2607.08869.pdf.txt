                                               INEQUALITIES FOR CONVEX FUNCTIONS OF RANDOM POINTS
                                                   INSIDE AND ON THE BOUNDARY OF CONVEX BODIES

                                                                                         A. S. TOKMACHEV


                                                  Abstract. Let K be a convex body in Rd , and let I and B be random points uniformly
                                                  distributed inside K and on its boundary, respectively. We prove that if d = 2 and EI = EB,
                                                  or if K is a circumscribed polytope with the center of the inscribed sphere coinciding with
                                                  EI = EB, then I is dominated by B in the convex order. As a consequence, for any function
                                                  φ convex in each argument, the expectation Eφ(I1 , . . . , Ik ) does not exceed Eφ(B1 , . . . , Bk ).
                                                  This yields, in particular, an inequality between the moments of random chords E|I1 − I2 |p ⩽
                                                  E|B1 − B2 |p for all p ⩾ 1, confirming the Zaporozhets–Tarasov conjecture for the indicated
                                                  class of bodies, and extends to inequalities for mean volumes of random simplices.

   Although both bounds in (1) are strict, they are optimal: the lower bound is asymptotically
attained on a sequence of isosceles triangles whose base is fixed while the height tends to zero,
and the upper bound is asymptotically attained on a sequence of rectangles degenerating to
a segment (which itself has empty interior and does not belong to the class of convex bodies

  In the author’s works [5, 6], the upper bounds in conjecture (5) and related results for ψ were
proved. Namely, the disk maximizes both the normalized mean distance between two boundary
points and the normalized mean area of a triangle with three boundary vertices, for a fixed
perimeter.
   In 2025, A. S. Lotnikov [7] obtained a number of results related to conjecture (4) for centrally
symmetric planar bodies. He showed that for such bodies not only does ∆(K) < θ(K) hold, but
a stronger stochastic domination statement is true: for any n > 1, E|B1 B2 |n ⩾ E|I1 I2 |n . He also
established exact formulas relating moments of interior and boundary chords for circumscribed
polygons.
  The aim of the present paper is to extend the chord-length inequality to a wider class
of bodies. We show that in the plane, the inequality E|I1 I2 |p ⩽ E|B1 B2 |p for all p ⩾ 1
holds under the single condition EI = EB (which is automatically true for centrally symmetric
bodies). Moreover, we prove a substantially more general fact: the inequality holds for arbitrary
functions convex in each argument. As a corollary, we obtain an analogous inequality for the
mean volume of a random simplex with vertices chosen inside and on the boundary of the body.

Theorem 1. Let K be a convex body in Rd satisfying one of the following:
     • d = 2 and EI = EB;
     • K is a circumscribed polytope whose inscribed sphere center O coincides with EI and
        EB.
Let φ : (Rd )k → R be a function convex in each argument. Then
(6)                                 Eφ(I1 , . . . , Ik ) ⩽ Eφ(B1 , . . . , Bk ),

                              3. Reduction to the convex order
   The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem 1 is the convex order between the distributions
of I and B. Recall that X ⪯cx Y (in the convex order) if Eφ(X) ⩽ Eφ(Y ) for every convex
function φ. By Strassen’s theorem [8], X ⪯cx Y is equivalent to the existence of a coupling
with E[Y | X] = X almost surely; in particular, EX = EY is necessary.
  To prove Theorem 1 it suffices to establish I ⪯cx B; the full statement then follows from
independence and properties of conditional expectations. Thus we need only prove:
Theorem 2 (Convex order for I and B). Under the same assumptions on K as in Theorem 1,
(9)                                                  I ⪯cx B,
i.e., Eφ(I) ⩽ Eφ(B) for every convex φ : Rd → R.

  The case of a circumscribed polytope is due to Pasteczka [10] (Theorem 2). The next section
contains the proof for d = 2, which is the main contribution of this note.
                                4. Proof of Theorem 2 for d = 2
  We need two auxiliary facts: the Ohlin lemma and a characterization of the convex order via
one-dimensional projections.
Lemma 1 (Ohlin [11]). Let X and Y be integrable random variables with EX = EY . If their
distribution functions FX and FY cross exactly once, with FX ⩽ FY to the left of the crossing
point and FX ⩾ FY to the right, then Ef (X) ⩽ Ef (Y ) for every convex f : R → R.

  The next fact is well known (see, e.g., [9]).
4                                           A. S. TOKMACHEV

Lemma 2. For X, Y ∈ Rd , one has X ⪯cx Y if and only if for every unit vector u ∈ S d−1 and
every convex f : R → R,
(10)                                    Ef (⟨X, u⟩) ⩽ Ef (⟨Y, u⟩),
where ⟨·, ·⟩ is the scalar product.

  Now we prove the theorem. Let d = 2 and EI = EB. Fix a unit vector u ∈ S 1 and let
Iu = ⟨I, u⟩, Bu = ⟨B, u⟩ be the projections onto the line spanned by u. We verify the Ohlin
condition for Iu and Bu .
  First, EI = EB implies
                                   EIu = ⟨EI, u⟩ = ⟨EB, u⟩ = EBu .
  Second, by Lemma 2 of [12], the distribution functions FIu and FBu cross exactly once, with
the sign pattern required in Ohlin’s lemma (the crossing point x0 is the projection of the “widest
part” of K onto this line). Hence,
                                            Ef (Iu ) ⩽ Ef (Bu )
for every convex f . By Lemma 2, this yields I ⪯cx B, completing the proof of the two-
dimensional case.
