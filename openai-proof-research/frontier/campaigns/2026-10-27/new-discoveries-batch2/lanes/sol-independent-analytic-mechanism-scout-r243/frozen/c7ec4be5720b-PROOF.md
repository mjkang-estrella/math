## A. Incidence minors

A row e_i+e_j-e_k is the sum e_i+(e_j-e_k), including repeated indices. A row e_i has one incidence summand. After deleting any columns, each summand has at most one +1 and at most one -1. A square matrix with such rows has determinant in {0,1,-1}: transpose it; if every column has two entries, all column sums vanish and it is singular; otherwise expand along a zero or singleton column and induct. The empty determinant is 1. Thus every d-by-d minor of d additive rows has absolute value at most 2^d, by row multilinearity. This proves the exact cofactor assertion of source Conjecture 4, including repeated-variable equations and arbitrary column deletion.

## B. Every arrangement vertex is bounded

Consider all real hyperplanes z_i=1 and z_i+z_j=z_k in R^n. At a vertex choose n linearly independent active equations, obtaining a unique solution. If none is z_i=1, the unique solution is zero. Otherwise identify all coordinates fixed to 1 by the chosen equations. This gives d<=n variables, exactly one fixed to 1 and homogeneous additive equations in the remaining variables. The reduced solution is still unique. Select d-1 independent homogeneous equations; their matrix B has rank d-1, and its kernel is spanned by its vector of signed maximal minors. Each minor has absolute value <=2^(d-1) by A. The cofactor of the fixed coordinate is a nonzero integer, since imposing that coordinate to be 1 makes the solution unique. Every coordinate of the solution is therefore a quotient of two such integers with denominator of absolute value at least 1. Its absolute value is at most 2^(d-1)<=2^(n-1). The fixed coordinates also obey this bound. This is a full vertex bound, not merely existence of one small solution of a system.

## C. Conjecture 5, both R and C, full residual scope

For a given real x in R^n form the CLOSED cell P containing x by choosing, for every affine form f(z)=z_i-1 or f(z)=z_i+z_j-z_k, the inequality f(z)>=0 if f(x)>=0, and f(z)<=0 otherwise. If f(x)=0 either choice suffices. These finitely many inequalities define a nonempty polyhedron. Its recession cone K has no line: the coordinate inequalities z_i>=1 or z_i<=1 impose a fixed weak sign on each coordinate of every recession vector, so r,-r in K implies r=0.

Every point of this pointed polyhedron is a convex combination of its vertices plus a recession vector. Here is a direct finite proof in this setting. At a nonvertex p, choose nonzero u annihilating all active constraint normals. The feasible parameters t with p+tu in P form a closed interval containing 0 in its interior. At least one endpoint is finite, since two infinite endpoints would give a line in K. At each finite endpoint the active rank strictly increases. If both endpoints are finite, express p as their convex combination. If one is infinite, express p as a finite endpoint plus a nonnegative multiple of the direction toward infinity. That direction belongs to K because all constraint normals have its required weak sign. Apply the same construction to the finite endpoints, whose active ranks increase at every level. In at most n levels this produces vertices. Convex combinations and sums of recession vectors remain in K. Thus x=z+r with z a convex combination of vertices and r in K.

By B every vertex belongs to [-2^(n-1),2^(n-1)]^n, so z belongs there too. For each defining affine form f with linear part l, the quantities f(z) and l(r) have the same weak sign chosen for this cell. Since f(x)=f(z)+l(r), we obtain |f(z)|<=|f(x)| simultaneously for EVERY source form. In particular if f(x)=0, both terms are zero. Taking y=z proves all three displayed requirements of literal Conjecture 5 over R, with no assumptions on the residuals, support or signs.

For x in C^n let a=Re(x). Apply the real result to a and obtain real y. Real coefficients give |y_i-1|<=|Re(x_i-1)|<=|x_i-1| and |y_i+y_j-y_k|<=|Re(x_i+x_j-x_k)|<=|x_i+x_j-x_k|. The coordinate bound is unchanged and real y is an allowed complex output. Thus the COMPLEX half is also covered; a disk-valued optimization or imaginary-coordinate bound is unnecessary. The doubling chain (1,2,...,2^(n-1)) has zero residuals and forces that exact output, so the uniform bound cannot be decreased.

## D. Conjecture 3, arbitrary additive subgroup G of C

If the consistent system has no equation x_i=1, zero solves it. Otherwise 1 belongs to G. Write it as Ax=b with integer A,b. Smith normal form U A V=D uses integer unimodular U,V. For a given solution x in G^n, y=V^-1 x is in G^n and d_i y_i=(Ub)_i for each nonzero diagonal d_i. Hence those y_i are rational and in G. Set all free coordinates to zero; consistency gives zero on every remaining row. Transform back by V. This constructs a rational solution q in (G intersect Q)^n; no arbitrary rational division within G was assumed.

The subgroup of Q generated by 1 and the finitely many q_i is (1/h)Z for an integer h>=1: take a common denominator L and use Bezout on L and the integer numerators to obtain the generator gcd(L,numerators)/L. In particular 1/h belongs to G and all h q_i are integers.

Identify all coordinates fixed to 1 by the original constant equations, leaving d<=n variables. The reduced rational solution is still in (1/h)Z. Scale all reduced variables by h. We obtain an integer-consistent system with ONE constant equation z_1=h and homogeneous additive equations. Select an equivalent full-row-rank subsystem containing z_1=h, of rank m<=d. Its augmented matrix has first row (e_1,h) and all other rows (e_i+e_j-e_k,0), with repetitions allowed. Every maximal minor not using the augmented column is either zero or, on expanding along the first row, an additive (m-1)-minor. Every maximal minor using the augmented column is h times such a minor. By A their absolute values are at most h*2^(m-1).

Apply the classical sharp integer-solution theorem as literally quoted in Tyszka Lemma 4: an integer-consistent full-row-rank system has an integer solution with all coordinates bounded in absolute value by the largest absolute maximal minor of its augmented matrix. It supplies an integer z with |z_i|<=h*2^(m-1). Divide by h and restore identified coordinates. The result is in (1/h)Z^n subset G^n, is rational, solves EVERY original equation and has |x_i|<=2^(m-1)<=2^(n-1). This is the entire arbitrary-G quantifier, including groups with irrational or nonreal elements. The same doubling chain in G=Z proves sharpness.
