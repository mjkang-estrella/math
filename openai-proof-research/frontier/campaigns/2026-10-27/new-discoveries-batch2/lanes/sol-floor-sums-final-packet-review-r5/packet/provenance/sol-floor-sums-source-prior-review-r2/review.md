For every positive integer m, indexed integers a1,...,a4,K in [0,m−1], the target is

S = sum(j=0..K) sum(T subset [4]) (−1)^(4−|T|) floor((j+sum(i in T) ai)/m) >= −3 floor(m/3).

Repeated entries and the empty subset are included. Thanatipanonda–Wong, JIS 21 (2018), Article 18.1.8, p.15 explicitly conjectures this bound independently of the later Conjecture 17. Table 1 p.2 also records it. This is the full uniform-m four-variable question, not merely the divisible-by-three extremizer example or the full all-n conjecture. No incorrect scope or counterexample was found.

## Explicit classical bridge test

Let H=K+1 and C_H(u)=max(0,min(H,u)). The frozen candidate's exact threshold reduction is F(s)=sum(l=1..4) C_H(s+H−lm); its correctness and the certificate have already been independently audited, and I did not redo the implementation audit.

To test the 2022 absolute-value results rather than dismissing them by title, use the identity

C_H(u) = (|u|−|u−H|+H)/2.

If G(y)=sum(T subset [4]) (−1)^|T| |y+sum(i in T) ai|, cancellation of the constant H yields exactly

S = (1/2) sum(l=1..4) [G(H−lm)−G(−lm)].

This is a bridge to their g4 object. It is not a bridge to their lower-bound conclusion: a lower bound on G at each shift does not lower-bound a difference, and normalizing a negative shift to y0=1 reverses all increment signs, removing the positive-increment hypothesis of Theorem 3. Theorem 4 treats an all-nonpositive normalized case under further size/crossing conditions, but again only bounds a single value below. One must still control the joint differences at all four thresholds, including endpoints and integral residue restrictions. Neither the read proofs nor their statements provide that joint estimate. I have not proved that no combination of their methods could do so.

The same obstruction appears in the older block proofs: the component bounds are individually true but do not control simultaneous extremality of several signed blocks. Wong's own combination improves the crude bound without reaching −3 floor(m/3). For m=3q+r the requested number is −m+r. Even an independently established continuous estimate S>=−m would leave a gap r=1 or 2; integrality alone does not remove it. No universal divisibility-by-three assertion is available (e.g. m=2, all ai=1, K=0 gives S=4). A generic variation/finite-difference inequality has not been exhibited with all these residue steps. EXCLUDE as a known corollary would therefore require an additional explicit mathematical argument, not a name or decidability theorem.
