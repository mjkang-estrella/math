## Independent proof audit

Let T=A5, H=(C2)^m, t=2^m, and G=T^H semidirect H with regular translation action. The supplied multiplication is associative because translation is an action by automorphisms; in characteristic two the displayed plus-translation convention is its own inverse. The cardinality is 60^t t.

The cycle-type counts in T are 1,15,20,24 for orders1,2,3,5. Its exponent is30. The centralizers of a double transposition, 3-cycle and 5-cycle in A5 have orders4,3,5, respectively: hence conjugacy-class sizes1,15,20,12,12. A normal subgroup must be a union of these classes containing identity; none of the proper nontrivial sums divides60. This proves simplicity. T is nonabelian, so its derived subgroup is nontrivial and normal and therefore T; it is nonsoluble and centerless. My checker constructs the 60-element group by closing two overlapping 3-cycles, rather than filtering all permutations, and derives orders by cycle decomposition. It reproduces the counts/classes and divisibility obstruction. Evenness of the generators ensures the generated 60-element subgroup is A5.

Here is the full minimal-normal bridge, without a classification assertion. Put N=T^H. If 1≠M normal in G and M≤N, choose f∈M with nonidentity coordinate x. Centerlessness supplies a∈T_x with [f,a]≠1. This commutator has support only at x and belongs to M∩T_x. That intersection is normal in T_x, so simplicity gives T_x≤M. Translation transitivity then gives every T_y≤M and M=N. To compute C_G(N), a nonzero translation moves T_x to a distinct factor, which conjugation by a base element cannot undo; it cannot centralize N. A base element centralizes N only when each coordinate is in Z(T)=1. Thus C_G(N)=1. For any nontrivial K normal in G, [K,N]≤K∩N. A trivial intersection would put K in C_G(N), impossible. The nontrivial intersection equals N by minimality. Hence every nontrivial normal subgroup contains N. N is the unique minimal normal subgroup and the socle, is nonsoluble, and G/N=H is abelian. No diagonal normal subgroup was silently assumed absent; the coordinate commutator rules it out.

For h=0, orders divide30. For h≠0, translation has exactly t/2 disjoint pairs. In (f,h)^2, the paired coordinates are ab and ba, which are conjugate. Its order is L, the lcm of one product order per pair. If d=ord(f,h), the quotient forces d even, and ord((f,h)^2)=d/2, so d=2L. Hence all orders divide60. Each selected pair product is uniform on A5, independently across pairs: for every prescribed product and every first coordinate there is exactly one second coordinate.
