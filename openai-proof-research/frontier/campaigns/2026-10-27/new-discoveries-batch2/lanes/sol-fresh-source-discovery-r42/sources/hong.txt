                                              GCD-CLOSED SETS AND DIVISIBILITY AMONG POWER LCM
                                                                  MATRICES


                                                                                      GUANGYAN ZHU




arXiv:2606.08075v2 [math.NT] 18 Aug 2026
                                                    Abstract. Let a, b, and n be positive integers and let S = {x1 , · · · , xn } be a set
                                                    of n distinct positive integers. We denote by [S a ] the n × n matrix having the ath
                                                    power of the least common multiple of xi and xj as its (i, j)-entry. For x ∈ S, let
                                                    GS (x) = {d ∈ S : d < x, d|x and (d|y|x, y ∈ S) ⇒ y ∈ {d, x}}. In this article, we
                                                    show that [S a ] divides [S b ] in the ring of n × n matrices over the integers if a|b and
                                                    S is gcd closed (i.e., gcd(xi , xj ) ∈ S for all integers i and j with 1 ≤ i, j ≤ n) and
                                                    satisfies the condition G (that is, for any x ∈ S, either GS (x) contains at most one
                                                    element, or GS (x) contains at least two elements and satisfies that lcm(y1 , y2 ) = x
                                                    and gcd(y1 , y2 ) ∈ GS (y1 ) ∩ GS (y2 ) for any {y1 , y2 } ⊆ GS (x)). This confirms a
                                                    conjecture of Hong proposed in (2026, Bull. Aust. Math. Soc., 113, 231-243).




                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                              Let a, b and n be positive integers. For any integers x and y, we denote by (x, y) the
                                           greatest common divisor of integers x and y and by [x, y] their least common multiple.
                                           Let Z denote the ring of integers and let |T | stand for the cardinality of a finite set T of
                                           integers. Let f be an arithmetic function and let S = {x1 , · · · , xn }. Let (f (S)) and (f [S])
                                           denote the n × n matrices whose (i, j)-entries are f ((xi , xj )) and f ([xi , xj ]), respectively.
                                           Define ⟨n⟩ := {1, · · · , n}. For any real number e, let ξe be the arithmetic function defined
                                           for any positive integer x by ξe (x) = xe . The n × n matrix (ξa (xi , xj )) = ((xi , xj )a )
                                           (abbreviated by (S a )) and (ξa [xi , xj ]) = ([xi , xj ]a ) (abbreviated by [S a ]) are called ath
                                           power GCD matrix on S and ath power LCM matrix on S, respectively. The set S is said
                                           to be factor closed (written as FC for short) if (x ∈ S, d > 0, d|x) ⇒ d ∈ S. We say that
                                           S is gcd closed if S contains (xi , xj ) for all integers i and j with 1 ≤ i, j ≤ n. Obviously,
                                           any FC set contains 1 and is gcd closed but the converse is not true. For instance, if the
                                           set S is FC, then for any integer x > 1, the set xS := {xy|y ∈ S} is gcd closed but not
                                           FC since 1 ̸∈ xS. One hundred and fifty years ago, Smith [26] proved that

   Hong [12] introduced the concept of greatest-type divisor when he solved the fa-
mous Bourque-Ligh conjecture [4]. For any integer x ∈ S, if (y < x, y|z|x and y, z ∈
S) ⇒ z ∈ {y, x}, then y is called a greatest-type divisor of x. Let GS (x) := {y ∈ S :
y is a greatest-type divisor of x in S}. Let Mn (Z) stand for the ring of n × n matrices
over the integers. In 1992, Bourque and Ligh [4] proved that (S) divides [S] in the ring
Mn (Z) if S is FC. Namely, there exists a B ∈ Mn (Z) such that [S] = B(S) or [S] = (S)B.
In 2002, Hong [14] showed that such a factorization is not true when S is gcd closed and
maxx∈S {|GS (x)|} = 2.
   On the other hand, Hong [17] initiated the investigation of divisibility among power
LCM matrices. It was proved in [17] that [S a ]|[S b ] if a|b and S is a divisor chain (that is,
xσ(1) | · · · |xσ(n) for a permutation σ of ⟨n⟩). Evidently, a divisor chain is gcd closed but
not conversely. Tan and Li [27] generalized Hong’s result [17] by proving that [S a ]|[S b ]
holds in the ring M|S| (Z) if a|b and S consists of finitely many coprime divisor chains with
1 ∈ S, and that such divisibility relation is not true if a ∤ b. Zhu and Li [33] confirmed
the conjecture of Hong raised in [17] stating that if a|b and S is a gcd-closed set with
maxx∈S {|GS (x)|} = 1, then [S a ] | [S b ] in the ring Mn (Z). In 2026, Hong [18] established
the same divisibility result when S is FC. As in [18], for any set S of positive integers
and for any x ∈ S with |GS (x)| ≥ 2, we say that the two distinct greatest-type divisors
y1 and y2 of x in S satisfy the condition G if [y1 , y2 ] = x and (y1 , y2 ) ∈ GS (y1 ) ∩ GS (y2 ).
We say that x satisfies the condition G if any two distinct greatest-type divisors of x in
S satisfy the condition G. Moreover, we say that a set S of positive integers satisfies
the condition G if any element x in S satisfies that either |GS (x)| ≤ 1, or |GS (x)| ≥ 2
and x satisfies the condition G. In [18], Hong showed that any FC set is a gcd-closed set
satisfying the condition G. Furthermore, Hong raised the following conjecture in Section
3 of [18].
Conjecture 1.1. [18, Conjecture 3.4] Let a and b be positive integers with a|b and let
S be a gcd-closed set satisfying the condition G. Then [S a ] | [S b ] in the ring M|S| (Z).
   For the case maxx∈S {|GS (x)|} = 1, by the theorem of Zhu and Li [33] we know that
this conjecture is true. For the case maxx∈S {|GS (x)|} = 2, by [28] one knows that
Conjecture 1.1 holds. Furthermore, Zhu, Luo and Wan [34] confirmed Conjecture 1.1
for the case maxx∈S {|GS (x)|} = 3. But Conjecture 1.1 is still kept open when S is the
general gcd-closed set satisfying the condition G.
   In this paper, our main goal is to study the divisibility among power LCM matrices.
We introduce a new method to investigate Conjecture 1.1. The main result of this paper
can be stated as follows.
Theorem 1.2. Let S be a gcd-closed set satisfying the condition G and let a and b be
positive integers such that a|b. Then the ath power LCM matrix [S a ] divides the bth
power LCM matrix [S b ] in the ring M|S| (Z).
   This paper is organized as follows. After introducing some necessary preliminary
results, we proceed to the core of our argument. In fact, we first recall and show in
                                                                                             3


Section 2 some results related to the arithmetic properties of gcd-closed sets that satisfy
the condition G and then present a formula for the inverse of power LCM matrices.
Finally, in Section 3, by proving a key lemma, we present the proof of Theorem 1.2.
    The divisibility of matrices under consideration is invariant under relabeling the ele-
ments of S. Specifically, for any permutation σ on ⟨n⟩, we have [S a ] | [S b ] if and only if
[Sσa ] | [Sσb ]. Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume throughout the rest of
this paper that the set S = {x1 , · · · , xn } is ordered such that x1 < · · · < xn .
