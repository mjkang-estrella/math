Sign conjecture for Steiner distance hyperdeterminants

2 years old · traced to 2024

Let TTT be a tree on nnn vertices, let MMM be its order-kkk Steiner distance hypermatrix, and write sgn⁡(x)\operatorname{sgn}(x)sgn(x) for 111 if x>0x>0x>0, −1-1−1 if x<0x<0x<0, and 000 if x=0x=0x=0.

Sign conjecture. For any tree on nnn vertices,

sgn⁡(det⁡(M))=(−1)n−1\operatorname{sgn}(\det(M))=(-1)^{n-1}sgn(det(M))=(−1)n−1

when this quantity is nonzero.

The conjecture appears in earlier work and has been checked numerically for (k,n)=(4,4)(k,n)=(4,4)(k,n)=(4,4), (4,5)(4,5)(4,5), and (6,4)(6,4)(6,4). It is trivially true for n≤3n\leq 3n≤3 and odd kkk, and follows from Graham–Pollak when k=2k=2k=2; its general status is not established in the supplied text.

References

2 papers in this index state this conjecture (2024–2025). The statement above is taken from the most recent of them; the others are arXiv:2403.02287.

Source: Joshua Cooper and Zhibin Du, “Determinants of Steiner Distance Hypermatrices”, arXiv:2505.10501 (2025).

Progress summary

Refreshed 2 months ago
Open · solution reported

An unverified posted proof claims to settle the sign conjecture for every tree and every order, while the latest published paper still leaves it open.

The conjecture asserts that the sign of the Steiner-distance hyperdeterminant of a tree is (−1)n−1(-1)^{n-1}(−1)n−1 whenever it is nonzero. Cooper and Du’s 2025 paper proves related tree-independence of the determinant’s value, but explicitly retains this sign statement as an open conjecture.

Known results

  • Trivial for n≤3n\leq 3n≤3 and odd kkk.
  • Graham–Pollak proves the case k=2k=2k=2.
  • Cooper and Du prove the case n=2n=2n=2 for every kkk.
  • Numerical checks cover (k,n)=(4,4),(4,5),(6,4)(k,n)=(4,4),(4,5),(6,4)(k,n)=(4,4),(4,5),(6,4); even kkk and n≥2n\geq2n≥2 also give nonzero determinants.

Posted attempt (posted August 21, 2026)

A reader-written argument claims a complete proof for all k≥2k\geq2k≥2 and all trees when the determinant is nonzero, using edge-cut coordinates, separated gradient equations, and a resultant sign computation. The attempt has not been independently verified.

Current status (as of August 2026): The conjecture is proved only in the recorded special cases, while a complete posted proof claim is unverified.

sgn⁡det⁡(M)=(−1)n−1.\operatorname{sgn}\det(M)=(-1)^{n-1}.sgndet(M)=(−1)n−1.

This settles the sign conjecture of Cooper–Du, Conjecture 1. The key is to use edge-cut coordinates: all but one of the gradient equations then separate into one-variable equations. Their nonreal solutions occur in conjugate pairs, while the unique real solution contributes a positive factor.

We use the cut identity from Cooper–Du, Theorem 2.1 and the normalized resultant and Poisson formula recalled in Zheng, Section 2, Theorems 2, 4 and 5. The known vanishing for odd order when n≥3n\geq3n≥3, and the case n=2n=2n=2 for every order, are already recorded in Cooper–Du; the latter is their Proposition 4.1. Thus it suffices to prove the assertion for even k≥2k\geq2k≥2 and n≥2n\geq2n≥2. For n=1n=1n=1, every entry is zero.
