                                                  Abstract. Let G be a multiplicatively written finite group. We denote by E(G) the
                                                  smallest integer t such that every sequence of t elements in G contains a product-one
                                                  subsequence of length |G|. In 1961, Erdős, Ginzburg and Ziv proved that E(G) ≤ 2|G|−1
                                                  for every finite ablian group G and this result is known as the Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv
                                                  Theorem. In 2005, Zhuang and Gao conjectured that E(G) = d(G) + |G|, where d(G)
                                                  is the small Davenport constant. In this paper, we confirm the conjecture for the case
                                                  when G = hx, y|xp = y m = 1, x−1 yx = y r i, where p is the smallest prime divisor of |G|
                                                  and gcd(p(r − 1), m) = 1.

                                                                      1. Introduction and main results
                                             Let G be a finite group written multiplicatively. Let S = g1 · . . . · gℓ be a sequence over
                                           G with length ℓ. We use
                                                               π(S) = {gτ (1) . . . gτ (ℓ) : τ a permutation of [1, ℓ]} ⊆ G
                                           to denote the set of products of S. We say that S is a product-one sequence if 1 ∈ π(S).
                                           Let d(G) be the small Davenport constant of G (i.e., the maximal integer ℓ such that there
                                           is a sequence of length ℓ over G which has no nontrivial product-one subsequence). We
                                           denote by E(G) the smallest integer t such that every sequence of length t over G contains
                                           a product-one subsequence of length |G|. The problem of finding the precise value of the
                                           Davenport constant and what is now known as the Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv Theorem have
                                           become the starting points of zero-sum theory.
                                           Theorem 1.1. (Theorem of Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv) [4] Let G be a finite abelian group. Then
                                           E(G) ≤ 2|G| − 1.
                                              Since that time (dating back to the early 1960s), zero-sum theory has developed into a
                                           flourishing branch of additive and combinatorial number theory. At the present moment,
                                           two conjectures on E(G) can be found in the literature. We briefly discuss some history
                                           and the motivation for studying these conjectures. For more detailed information, the
                                           interested reader may refer to the surveys [2, 7, 14] or the monographs [13, 15]. Although
                                           the main focus of zero-sum theory has initially been on abelian groups, the research
                                           has never been restricted to the abelian setting alone. In 1976, Olson [18] showed that
                                           E(G) ≤ 2|G| − 1 holds for all finite groups. In 1984, Yuster and Peterson [20] showed that

  In 1996, Gao [6] discovered the fundamental relationship between E(G) and d(G) for
abelian groups, i.e., E(G) = d(G) + |G|. In 2005, Zhuang and Gao [21] investigated
this relation E(G) = d(G) + |G| for some non-abelian groups and proposed the following
conjecture.
Conjecture 1.3. Let G be a finite group. Then E(G) = d(G) + |G|.
   They confirmed Conjecture 1.3 for dihedral groups of order 2p where p ≥ 4001 is a
prime. Gao and Lu [11] then improved this result to dihedral groups of order 2n for all
n ≥ 23. Bass [1] extended the method used by Gao and Lu, and proved the conjecture for
all dihedral groups, dicyclic groups, and groups Cp ⋉ Cq , where p, q are primes. Recently,
in 2015 Han [16] verified the conjecture for the case when G is a non-cyclic nilpotent
group or when G ∼ = Cp ⋉ Cpn , where p is a prime. Most recently, in 2019 Han and Zhang
[17] further extended Han’s early result and verified the conjecture for G ∼ = Cm ⋉ Cmn .
It is natural to extend this line of research to the class of metacyclic groups Cn ⋉ Cm
where gcd(n, m) = 1. In this paper, we confirm Conjecture 1.3 for the following class of
metacyclic groups and our main result is as follows
Theorem 1.4. Let G = hx, y|xp = y m = 1, x−1 yx = y r i, where p is the smallest prime
divisor of |G| and gcd(p(r − 1), m) = 1. Then d(G) = m + p − 2 and E(G) = d(G) + |G|.
   We remark that the investigation of the above mentioned class of metacyclic groups is
of special interest as it is closely related to solving Conjecture 1.2. Very recently, Gao,
Li, and Qu [10] made substantial progress on this conjecture and proved that E(G) ≤ 3|G| 2
holds for all non-cyclic groups of odd order by using the minimal counterexample method.
Our main result and some technical lemmas in Section 3 have been used in [10] as the
necessary ingredients to construct a minimal counterexample leading to a contradiction.
In addition, the methods developed in this paper (such as applying a generalization of
Kneser’s Additive Theorem to estimate a partial product of sets Πm (A)) have been used
repeatedly in [10] to help simplify some complicated computations and to achieve the best
possible upper bound on E(G).
