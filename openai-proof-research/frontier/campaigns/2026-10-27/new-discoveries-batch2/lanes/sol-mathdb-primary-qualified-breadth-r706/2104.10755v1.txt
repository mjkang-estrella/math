                                                                                          Abstract
                                                      A nut graph is a simple graph whose adjacency matrix has eigenvalue 0 with
                                                  multiplicity 1 such that its eigenvector has no zero entries. Motivated by a question
                                                  of Fowler et al. [Disc. Math. Graph Theory 40 (2020), 533–557] to determine the
                                                  pairs (n, d) for which a vertex-transitive nut graph of order n and degree d exists,
                                                  Bašić et al. [arXiv:2102.04418, 2021] initiated the study of circulant nut graphs.
                                                  Here we first show that the generator set of a circulant nut graph necessarily contains
                                                  equally many even and odd integers. Then we characterize circulant nut graphs with
                                                  the generator set {x, x+1, . . . , x+2t−1} for x, t ∈ N, which generalizes the result of
                                                  Bašić et al. for the generator set {1, . . . , 2t}. We further study circulant nut graphs
                                                  with the generator set {1, . . . , 2t + 1} \ {t}, which appears to yield nut graphs of
                                                  every even order n ⩾ 4t + 4 whenever t is odd such that t 6≡ 1 (mod 10) and
                                                  t 6≡ 15 (mod 18). This observation is tested here computationally for all t ⩽ 1300,
                                                  and it fully resolves Conjecture 9 from Bašić et al. [arXiv:2102.04418, 2021]. We
                                                  also study existence of 4t-regular circulant nut graphs for small values of t, which
                                                  partially resolves Conjecture 10 of Bašić et al. [ibid.].

1    Introduction
Let G = (V, E) be a simple graph on the vertex set V = {0, 1, . . . , n − 1}. Graph G is
a nut graph if its adjacency matrix AG has eigenvalue 0 with multiplicity 1 such that an
eigenvector corresponding to 0 has no zero entries. We will assume that G has at least
two vertices, as the one-vertex graph is trivially a nut graph. Nut graphs were introduced
as a mathematical curiosity at the end of 1990s in several papers of Sciriha [11–14] and
Sciriha and Gutman [19]. Apparently, the name nut graph first appeared in [19], where
it was also shown that nut graphs are characterized among singular graphs as the graphs
with a deck of nonsingular vertex-deleted subgraphs. Further structural properties of nut
graphs were later given in [15,16]. Chemical justification for studying nut graphs of small
degree in terms of a single non-bonding molecular orbital and strong omni-conducting
behaviour is given in [2, 5, 6, 18]. Small nut graphs were enumerated by Fowler et al. [6],
while Coolsaet et al. [2] devised a special algorithm for generation of nut graphs. Using
this algorithm, they were able to enumerate all nut graphs up to 13 vertices, nut graphs
with maximum degree at most three and up to 22 vertices, cubic nut polyhedra up to 34
vertices and nut fullerenes up to 250 vertices. Many further results about nut graphs are
discussed at length in the monograph [17].
    Structural properties of regular nut graphs were first studied by Sciriha and Fowler [18].
Existence of cubic and quartic nut graphs was considered by Gauci et al. [7], who fully
characterized the orders for which such nut graphs exist: a cubic nut graph of order n
exists if and only if n is an even integer, n ⩾ 12 and n ∈    / {14, 16}, while a quartic nut
graph of order n exists if and only if n ∈ {8, 10, 12} or n ⩾ 14. Gauci et al. [7] posed
the problem to also determine orders for which d-regular nut graphs exist for d ⩾ 5, and
Fowler et al. [4] managed to characterize such orders for all d ⩽ 11. Fowler et al. [4] were
also interested in further restricting nut graphs to be vertex-transitive, and in this aspect
they proved the following result.

Theorem 1 (Fowler et al. [4]). Let G be a vertex-transitive nut graph of order n and
degree d. Then either d ≡ 0 (mod 4), n ≡ 0 (mod 2) and n ⩾ d + 4, or d ≡ 2 (mod 4),
n ≡ 0 (mod 4) and n ⩾ d + 6.

    Using the recent catalogue of vertex-transitive graphs [9], they showed that a vertex-
transitive nut graph exists for all pairs (n, d) that satisfy the above conditions with n ⩽ 42.
Consequently, Fowler et al. [4, Question 11] asked to characterize the pairs (n, d), satisfying
requirements of Theorem 1, for which a vertex-transitive nut graph of order n and degree d
exists.
    Bašić et al. [1] approached this question by considering circulant nut graphs. Recall
that a graph G is circulant if its adjacency matrix AG has the form

As we are interested in simple graphs, this implies that a0 = 0 and aj = an−j ∈ {0, 1} for
j = 1, . . . , n − 1. A circulant graph may be more concisely described via the generator
set S which contains the indices of those entries of the sequence a1 , . . . , a⌊n/2⌋ that are
equal to 1. Hence for the given order n and the generator set S, we denote by Circ(n, S)
the circulant graph with the vertex set V = {0, 1, . . . , n − 1} and the edge set

                                   E = {(i, i ± s) : i ∈ V, s ∈ S},

where i ± s is computed modulo n.
    Bašić et al. [1] proved that a 12-regular nut graph of order n exists if and only n ⩾ 16.
Thanks to the Fowler construction [4, 7], which allows one to construct a d-regular nut
graph of order n + 2d from a d-regular nut graph of order n, for this it was sufficient to
construct 12-regular nut graphs for 16 ⩽ n ⩽ 39. For even n ∈ {16, . . . , 38}, the nut
graphs were constructed as circulant graphs with the generator set either {1, . . . , 6} or
{1, . . . , 5, 8}, while for odd n ∈ {17, . . . , 39}, the nut graphs were obtained computation-
ally by rewiring edges of circulant graphs. They further proved the following result.

Theorem 2 (Bašić et al. [1]). Let n be even and d divisible by 4. Then Circ(n, {1, . . . , d2 })
is a nut graph if and only if gcd(n, d2 + 1) = 1 and gcd( n2 , d4 ) = 1.

    Bašić et al. [1] further conjectured that a 12-regular circulant nut graph exists for
every even n ⩾ 16 ([1, Conjecture 9]) and, more generally, that a d-regular circulant nut
graph exists for every even n ⩾ d + 4 and every d divisible by 4 ([1, Conjecture 10]).
    We are interested here in the properties of circulant nut graphs. In the next section,
we briefly review classical results on eigenvalues and eigenvectors of circulant graphs,
using which we show that the generator set of a circulant nut graph necessarily contains
equally many even and odd integers (Lemma 4). In Section 3 we characterize circulant
nut graphs with the consecutive generator set {x, x + 1, . . . , x + 2t − 1} for x, t ∈ N, which
generalizes Theorem 2. In Section 4 we further study circulant graphs with the almost
consecutive generator set {1, . . . , 2t + 1} \ {t}, which appear to yield nut graphs for all
even n ⩾ 4t + 4 whenever t is odd such that t 6≡ 1 (mod 10) and t 6≡ 15 (mod 18). We
prove that such circulant graphs for odd t and either t ≡ 1 (mod 10) or t ≡ 15 (mod 18)
are not nut graphs, while the other part of this conjecture is tested computationally for all
odd t ⩽ 1300, which also resolves Conjecture 9 of Bašić et al. [1]. In Section 5 we further
study existence of 4t-regular circulant nut graphs for small values of t, which partially
answers Question 11 of Fowler et al. [4] and also partially resolves Conjecture 10 of Bašić
et al. [1].

Remark 5. We note here that if the generator set S of a circulant graph G = Circ(n, S)
consists of t odd and t even integers from {1, . . . , n/2 − 1}, then G has (−1, 1, . . . , −1, 1)
as an eigenvector of the eigenvalue 0 = P (ω n/2). Since this vector has no zero entries,
a necessary and sufficient condition for G to be a nut graph is that the eigenvalue 0
has multiplicity 1, i.e., that P (ω j ) 6= 0 for j 6= n/2. Further, P (1) = 4t > 0, while
P (ω j ) = P (ω n−j ) for j = 1, . . . , n − 1, implies that G is a nut graph if and only if
P (ω j ) 6= 0 for each j ∈ {1, . . . , n/2 − 1}. We will rely extensively on this remark in the
following sections.

5    4t-Regular circulant nut graphs for small values of t
Fowler et al. [4, Question 11] asked to characterize the pairs (n, d) that satisfy requirements
of Theorem 1, for which a d-regular vertex-transitive nut graph of order n exists. In this
section we suggest the answer to this question in the more specialized case of circulant
nut graphs through further computational study.
    Lemma 4 tells us that circulant nut graphs are necessarily 4t-regular, and being vertex-
transitive, they have even order n ⩾ 4t + 4 by Theorem 1. The computational study from
the previous section shows that for most odd values of t ⩽ 1300 there actually exists a
single generator set St = {1, . . . , 2t + 1} \ {t} that yields a 4t-regular circulant nut graph
for any even order n ⩾ 4t + 4. In this section we perform a computational study of
generator sets of 4t-regular circulant nut graphs for the remaining small values of t: even
ones, and those odd t such that either t ≡ 1 (mod 10) or t ≡ 15 (mod 18). Our findings
will suggest that for t ⩾ 2 we will again have a single generator set that will produce
4t-regular circulant nut graphs for all feasible values of n.
    We start with a result that follows from Theorem 6. Although it is applicable to
arbitrary values of t, we use it here to settle the case t = 1.
Theorem 10. Let t ∈ N. If n ⩾ 4t + 4 is an even integer such that gcd( n2 , t) = 1, then
there exists a 4t-regular circulant nut graph of order n.
