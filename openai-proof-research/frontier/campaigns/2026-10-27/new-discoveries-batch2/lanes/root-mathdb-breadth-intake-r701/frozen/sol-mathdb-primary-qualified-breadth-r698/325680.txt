                                             Congruences for 9-regular partitions modulo 3




arXiv:1306.0136v1 [math.CO] 1 Jun 2013
                                                                        William J. Keith
                                                                           June 5, 2019


                                                                               Abstract
                                                  We find that the number of 9-regular partitions of n is divisible by 3
                                               when n is congruent to 3 mod 4, and divisible by 6 when n is congruent
                                               to 13 mod 16. We also prove an infinite family of congruences in other
                                               progressions modulo powers of 4. We conjecture two higher-power con-
                                               gruences and a large family of ”congruences with exceptions” for these
                                               and other regular partitions mod 3.

                                            In recent years there has been a spate of work ([1], [8], [5], [7], [6] and more)
                                         on the divisibility properties of bℓ (n) in arithmetic progression for various ℓ,
                                         especially on their parity. Many Bℓ (q) possess numerous progressions of values
                                         divisible by 2 and 3, in stark contrast to the usual partition function, where such
                                         sequences are unknown and generally held to be almost certainly impossible. In
                                         another contrast, theorems of the form bℓ (An + B) ≡ 0 mod M often have A
                                         and M coprime.
                                            Furcy and Penniston in [5] were able to prove an infinite family of congru-
                                         ences modulo 3 for ℓ ≡ 1 mod 3. Cui and Gu in [8] find parity results for ℓ = 2j
                                         up to 16. Hirschhorn and Sellers in [7] produce results for ℓ = 5 as well as
                                         13. In this paper we continue the project of filling in remaining small moduli,
                                         beginning with ℓ = 9.

4     Higher congruences
Other infinite families of congruences appear from numerical experimentation
to hold. If we extract the coefficients b9 (5n + 3) and reduce them modulo 3, we
find that most are 0, but every fifth coefficient beginning with n = 1 is not; the
nonzero coefficients, however, are exactly the same mod 3 as the coefficients of
B9 (q 5 ). Conjecture 3 would be a corollary of this observation:

ilies modulo 2 (and a few modulo 3), using dissections; Furcy and Penniston
prove infinite families modulo 3, using the properties of modular forms. How-
ever, for ℓ and progression moduli of this size, dissections begin to be unwieldy,
and the modular form machinery almost exclusively gives progressions whose
moduli are powers of the modulus of the congruence, or multiples thereof. An
alternative method of attack would be to try to adapt the work done in recent
years on congruences for (q; q)−1
                                ∞ modulo higher powers of primes – most of this
machinery, however, fails for the primes 2 and 3.
     There are, to numerical inspection, apparently a large number of self-similarities
of this sort. For example, there is an interesting result by Penniston and Lovejoy
(Theorem 3, [5]) from which it can be easily extracted that

Corollary 1 b3 (5n + 2) ≡ b3 (7k + 4) ≡ 0 mod 9 unless n or k are divisible by
5 or 7 respectively.

    Their proof is of an unusual sort for the literature, and their remarks at
the end of the paper pessimistic regarding future applications – but the current
situation suggests that there is more hope of use to gained by adapting that
paper’s arguments. It seems very P   likely that this theorem can be considered a
consequence of the self-similarity n=0 b3 (5n + 2)q n ≡ 2B3 (q 5 ) mod 9. Many
other such similarities exist; a unified method of proof could attack a large
number of them. This problem is the subject of current work by the author.
    A remaining area of related interest, to date apparently completely unex-
plored, is the behavior of l-regular partitions modulo 2j and 3j for higher powers
of j. For instance, the b3 (7n + 4) progression mentioned above may hold mod
27. There is also evidence for progressions in b9 which are divisible by 27 and 54.
However, greater reservations must be expressed for these conjectures. Work of
Gordon and Ono ([4]) informs us that b9 (n) is infinitely often divisible by 3j for
any integer j, and so spurious progressions of this sort would doubtless occur.
    It is a one-line proof that any congruence p(An + B) ≡ 0 mod M for the
ordinary partition function implies one mod M for bAk (An+B) for any multiple
of A, and work of Ono and Mahlburg (with many others) gives us such congru-
ences for powers of any prime at least 5. However, we have no such congruences
for the ordinary partition function with A or M = 2j or 3j , and yet several such
progressions exist for several bℓ . Therefore, it seems that an important open
question is:

Problem: Do there exist progressions bℓ (An + B) ≡ 0 mod 2j or 3j for indefi-
nitely high j?
