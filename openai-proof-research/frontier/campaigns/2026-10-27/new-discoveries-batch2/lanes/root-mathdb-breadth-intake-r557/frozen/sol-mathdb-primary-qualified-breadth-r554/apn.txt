 Zeros of special polynomials and their impact on a class of APN functions 
 
 
 Daniele Bartoli
 
    
 Marco Calderini
 
    
 Giuseppe Marino
 
    
 Francesco Pavese
 

 Abstract 
    
 In 2021, Calderini et al. introduced a construction for APN functions on  𝔽 2 2 ​ m \mathbb{F}_{2^{2m}}  in bivariate form 
    
 

 
 
 
 f ⁡ ( x , y ) = ( x ​ y , x 2 r + 1 + x 2 r + m / 2 ​ y 2 m / 2 + b ​ x ​ y 2 r + c ​ y 2 r + 1 ) \displaystyle f(x,y)=\big(xy,\,x^{2^{r}+1}+x^{2^{r+m/2}}y^{2^{m/2}}+bxy^{2^{r}}+cy^{2^{r}+1}\big) 
 r < m / 2 , gcd ⁡ ( r , m ) = 1 . \displaystyle r<m/2,\gcd(r,m)=1. 
 
 
    
 They showed that this family exists provided the existence of a polynomial 
    
 

 
 
 P c , b ​ ( X ) = ( c ​ X 2 r + 1 + b ​ X 2 r + 1 ) 2 m / 2 + 1 + X 2 m / 2 + 1 , \displaystyle P_{c,b}(X)=(cX^{2^{r}+1}+bX^{2^{r}}+1)^{2^{m/2}+1}+X^{2^{m/2}+1}, 
 
 
    
 with no zeros in  𝔽 2 2 ​ m \mathbb{F}_{2^{2m}} .
For  m ≤ 6 m\leq 6  it was shown that we can have APN functions belonging to this family. However, up to now, no construction of such polynomials is known for  m ≥ 8 m\geq 8 . In this work we provide a non-existence result of such functions whenever  r < m / 8 − 1 r<m/8-1 , by application of techniques from algebraic varieties over finite fields.
In particular, for  r = 1 r=1  we have that the construction of Calderini et al. cannot provide an APN function for  m ≥ 8 m\geq 8 . 
    
 Keywords:  APN functions, Bivariate construction, Zeros of polynomials

 1  Introduction 

 
 Let  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}  be the finite field with  2 n 2^{n}  elements. A function  f f  from  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}  into itself is called almost perfect nonlinear (APN) if for any non zero  a ∈ 𝔽 2 n a\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}  and any  b ∈ 𝔽 2 n b\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} , the equation 
 

 
 
 f ⁡ ( x + a ) + f ⁡ ( x ) = b f(x+a)+f(x)=b 
 
 
 admits at most two solutions. 
 
 
 APN functions play a central role in modern cryptography since they provide optimal resistance against differential cryptanalysis ( [ 3 ] ) when used as substitution boxes in block ciphers. Beyond cryptography, they also appear as optimal objects in coding theory, combinatorics, and projective geometry  [ 13 ,  14 ,  15 ] . 
 
 
 Despite their importance, only a few infinite families of APN functions are currently known, and their classification up to CCZ- or EA-equivalence remains an open problem (see  [ 23 ]  for a list of known APN families and for the definition of these equivalence relations). 
 
 
 Several of the known families that can be defined in even dimension have been obtained using the so called bivariate construction introduced by Carlet in  [ 11 ] . In particular, let  n = 2 ​ m n=2m , we can decompose  𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}  as  𝔽 2 m × 𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}} , and a function from  𝔽 2 m × 𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\times\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}  into itself can be represented using bivariate polynomials, that is,  f ⁡ ( x , y ) = ( f 1 ​ ( x , y ) , f 2 ​ ( x , y ) ) f(x,y)=(f_{1}(x,y),f_{2}(x,y))  with  f 1 , f 2 ∈ 𝔽 2 m ​ [ x , y ] f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}[x,y] . 
 
 
 In  [ 11 ] , the author considered functions  f ⁡ ( x , y ) f(x,y)  where  f 1 ​ ( x , y ) f_{1}(x,y)  was given by the Maiorana-McFarland function  x ​ y xy , and provided some necessary and sufficient conditions for the APN property of  f ⁡ ( x , y ) f(x,y) . He also introduced a class of APN function in bivariate form which was later proved (see  [ 9 ] ) to be equivalent to the hexanomial family constructed in  [ 8 ] .
The bivariate construction was later used for obtaining other classes of APN functions ( [ 9 ,  26 ,  27 ] ). Recently, in  [ 18 ] , Göloğlu
proposed a generalization of the bivariate construction based on the so-called biprojective polynomials. Bi-projective polynomials has been used for constructing several classes of APN functions lately  [ 10 ,  19 ,  24 ] . 
 
 
 Within specific families, the APN property is intrinsically connected to the existence of polynomials with well-defined structural properties. Accordingly, a fundamental problem is to determine whether APN functions derived from these constructions exist in infinitely many dimensions or whether they are restricted to finitely many instances  [ 2 ,  5 ,  7 ,  17 ] . 
 
 
 In particular, the existence of several classes of bivariate APN families constructed to date relies on the fact that a certain projective polynomial, that is a polynomial of type  x 2 r + 1 + x + a x^{2^{r}+1}+x+a , admits no roots over  𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}   [ 10 ,  11 ,  26 ] . 
 
 
 Projective polynomials and their roots have been studied in several works, such as  [ 4 ,  7 ,  20 ,  21 ] .
So, applying Bluher’s results ( [ 4 ] ), one obtains that these constructions yield an APN function in every dimension in which they are defined. 
 
 
 For the case of the APN class introduced in  [ 9 ] , the existence of instances coming from this construction is related to the roots of a certain polynomial, which is not projective. 
 
 
 In particular, the APN class given in  [ 9 ]  is the following: 
 
 
 Theorem 1.1 . 
 
 [ 9 , Theorem 6.2] 
Let  n = 2 ​ m n=2m  with  m m  even, and let  r < m / 2 r<m/2  be such that  gcd ⁡ ( r , m ) = 1 \gcd(r,m)=1 . Consider  b , c ∈ 𝔽 2 m b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}} 
Then 
 

 
 
 f b , c , r ​ ( x , y ) = ( x ​ y , x 2 r + 1 + x 2 r + m / 2 ​ y 2 m / 2 + b ​ x ​ y 2 r + c ​ y 2 r + 1 ) f_{b,c,r}(x,y)=\big(xy,\,x^{2^{r}+1}+x^{2^{r+m/2}}y^{2^{m/2}}+bxy^{2^{r}}+cy^{2^{r}+1}\big) 
 
 
 is APN if and only if 
 

 
 
 P c , b ​ ( X ) = ( c ​ X 2 r + 1 + b ​ X 2 r + 1 ) 2 m / 2 + 1 + X 2 m / 2 + 1 P_{c,b}(X)=\big(cX^{2^{r}+1}+bX^{2^{r}}+1\big)^{2^{m/2}+1}+X^{2^{m/2}+1} 
 
 
 has no zero in  𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}} . 
 
 
 
 The authors showed that for  n ≤ 12 n\leq 12  (so  m ≤ 6 m\leq 6 ) it was possible to produce new APN functions (up to CCZ-equivalence). However, if such functions exist also for higher dimensions is an open problem. 
 
 
 The aim of this work is to investigate such an open question. In particular, we prove that for each  r < m / 8 − 1 r<m/8-1  there are no instances of  b , c ∈ 𝔽 2 m b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}  for which  f b , c , r ​ ( x , y ) f_{b,c,r}(x,y)  is APN. 
 
 
 The main tool is given by application of techniques from algebraic varieties over finite fields.

 2  Preliminary results 

 
 We now recall some basic facts on curves/surfaces over (finite) fields. For more details, we refer to  [ 16 ,  22 ] , or the reader’s favorite algebraic geometry book. As customary, for a field  𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} , we denote by  𝔽 ¯ \overline{{\mathbb{F}}}  its algebraic closure, and by  ℙ m ​ ( 𝔽 ) \mathbb{P}^{m}({\mathbb{F}})  (respectively,  𝔸 m ​ ( 𝔽 ) \mathbb{A}^{m}({\mathbb{F}}) ) the  m m -dimensional projective (respectively, affine) space over the field  𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} . Solutions of one or more polynomial equations form what we call algebraic hypersurfaces or varieties. An algebraic hypersurface defined over a field  𝔽 \mathbb{F}  is called absolutely irreducible if the associated polynomial is irreducible over every algebraic extension of  𝔽 \mathbb{F} . An absolutely irreducible  𝔽 \mathbb{F} -rational component of a hypersurface defined by a polynomial  F F  is an absolutely irreducible hypersurface, associated to a factor of  F F  defined over  𝔽 ¯ \overline{{\mathbb{F}}} . 
 
 
 In two dimensions,  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  is an affine curve over a field  𝔽 {\mathbb{F}}  if it is the zero set of a polynomial  F ⁡ ( X , Y ) ∈ 𝔽 ⁡ [ X , Y ] F(X,Y)\in{\mathbb{F}}[X,Y] . A projective curve  𝒞 \mathcal{C}  over a field  𝔽 {\mathbb{F}}  is the zero set of a homogeneous polynomial  F ⁡ ( X , Y , Z ) ∈ 𝔽 ⁡ [ X , Y , Z ] F(X,Y,Z)\in{\mathbb{F}}[X,Y,Z] . The polynomial  F F  is the defining polynomial of  𝒞 \mathcal{C} .

 5  Concluding remarks 

 
 In this work, using algebraic-geometric tools we proved that the bivariate construction of APN functions introduced in  [ 9 ]  cannot yield APN functions whenever
 r < m 8 − 1 r<\frac{m}{8}-1 .
In particular, for the case  r = 1 r=1 , this implies that the function cannot be APN for  m ≥ 18 m\geq 18 . Moreover, by performing computations in  MAGMA  we established that for  m ≥ 8 m\geq 8  there are no APN functions from this class when  r = 1 r=1 . These results naturally lead to the following conjecture: 
 

 
 
 For  ​ m ≥ 8 ​  and  ​ r < m 2 ,  no APN function arises from Theorem  1.1 . \text{For }m\geq 8\text{ and }r<\frac{m}{2},\text{ no APN function arises from Theorem \ref{Th:APN}.} 
 
 
 Computationally this conjecture holds for  8 ≤ m ≤ 22 8\leq m\leq 22 . 
 
 
 If true, this conjecture implies that the Calderini et al. construction does not generate an infinite family of APN functions, but only sporadic examples for  m ≤ 6 m\leq 6 , where APN functions of this type indeed exist, as shown in  [ 9 ] . 
 
 
 A possible way to investigate this conjecture could be trying to show that for any choice of parameters  b , c b,c , one can always find some  u ∈ μ q + 1 u\in\mu_{q+1}  such that the associated value of  A A , as in ( 4.3 ), does  not  belong to the set 
 

 
 
 { a ​ ( a + 1 ) 2 r + 2 − r ( a + a 2 − r ) 2 r + 1 : a ​  not a cube } . \Biggl\{\frac{a(a+1)^{2^{r}+2^{-r}}}{(a+a^{2^{-r}})^{2^{r}+1}}:a\text{ not a cube}\Biggr\}. 
 
 
 
 
 An equivalent approach could be that of studying the permutation property of certain linearized polynomials. Indeed, projective polynomials are related to linearized polynomials. In particular, noting that  x 2 r − 1 x^{2^{r}-1}  permutes  𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}  when  gcd ⁡ ( r , m ) = 1 \gcd(r,m)=1 , the polynomial  P A ​ ( X ) = X 2 r + 1 + X + A P_{A}(X)=X^{2^{r}+1}+X+A  has no roots over  𝔽 2 m \mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}  if and only if  L A ​ ( X ) = X 2 2 ​ r + X 2 r + A ​ X L_{A}(X)=X^{2^{2r}}+X^{2^{r}}+AX  is a permutation polynomial, since  P A ​ ( X 2 r − 1 ) ​ X = L A ​ ( X ) P_{A}(X^{2^{r}-1})X=L_{A}(X) . 
 
 
 Linearized polynomials of this form and their zeros have been studied in several works (see for instance  [ 12 ,  21 ,  25 ] ).
