 Abstract 
    
 An ordered set  S S  of vertices of a graph  G G  is a  resolving set  for  G G  if every vertex is uniquely determined by its vector of distances to the vertices in  S S . The  metric
dimension  of G is the minimum cardinality of a resolving set. In this paper we study resolving sets tolerant to several failures in three-dimensional grids.
Concretely, we seek for minimum cardinality sets that are resolving after removing any  k k  vertices from the set. This is equivalent to finding  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -resolving sets , a generalization of resolving sets,
where, for every pair of vertices, the vector of distances to the vertices of the set differ in at least  k + 1 k+1  coordinates.
This problem is also related with the study of the  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -metric dimension  of a graph, defined as the minimum cardinality of a  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -resolving set.
In this work, we first prove that the metric dimension of a three-dimensional grid is 3 and establish some properties involving resolving sets in these graphs.
Secondly, we determine the values of  k ≥ 1 k\geq 1  for which there exists a
 ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -resolving set
and construct such a resolving set of minimum cardinality in almost all cases.

 Key words:  Resolving set; metric dimension;  k k -resolving set;  k k -metric dimension; fault-tolerant; three-dimensional grid. 
 
 
 1  Introduction 

 
 Resolving sets can be used to distinguish the vertices of a graph  G G  comparing distances to fixed vertices. Fault-tolerant resolving sets were defined to distinguish the vertices of  G G  even though one of the vertices of the set fails.
Here we consider a more general case, concretely, resolving sets that distinguish the vertices of a graph when any  k k  vertices of the set fail, where  k k  is a fixed integer. 
 
 
 The notion of resolving sets in graphs was defined independently by Harary and Melter  [ 13 ]  and
Slater  [ 25 ]  and have since received a lot of attention due to their applications
in several areas, such as network discovery and verification  [ 3 ] , robot navigation  [ 17 ] , chemistry  [ 5 ]  or games  [ 7 ] .
Fault-tolerant resolving sets were introduced in  [ 14 ]  and have been studied in  [ 2 ,  6 ,  15 ,  19 ,  21 ,  23 ,  24 ,  27 ] .
For more applications and properties on metric dimension and its variants, the reader is addressed to the surveys  [ 20 ,  26 ]  and the references herein. 
 
 
 Let  G G  be a simple finite connected graph. For two vertices  u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u,v\in V(G) , let  d ⁡ ( u , v ) d(u,v)  denote the length of a shortest path from  u u  to  v v .
A vertex  u u  of  G G   resolves  two vertices  x x  and  y y  if  d ⁡ ( u , x ) ≠ d ⁡ ( u , y ) d(u,x)\not=d(u,y) .
A set of vertices  S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G)  is a  resolving set  for  G G  if for every pair of different vertices  x x  and  y y  of  G G  there is a vertex  u u  in  S S  that resolves  x x  and  y y .
The  metric dimension  of  G G , denoted by  dim ( G ) \dim(G) , is the minimum cardinality of a resolving set, and a  metric basis  is a resolving set of cardinality  dim ( G ) \dim(G) .
If  S = { u 1 , … , u k } ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}\subseteq V(G) , we denote by  r ⁡ ( x | S ) r(x|S)  the vector of distances from  x x  to the vertices of  S S , that is,  r ⁡ ( x | S ) = ( d ⁡ ( x , u 1 ) , … , d ⁡ ( x , u k ) ) r(x|S)=(d(x,u_{1}),\dots,d(x,u_{k})) .
Thus,  S S  is a resolving set if and only if  r ⁡ ( x | S ) ≠ r ⁡ ( y | S ) r(x|S)\not=r(y|S)  for every pair of distinct vertices  x , y ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) x,y\in V(G) . The elements of  r ⁡ ( x | S ) r(x|S)  are the  metric coordinates  of  x x  with respect to  S S .
A resolving set  S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G)  is  fault-tolerant  if  S − { u } S-\{u\}  is a resolving set, for every  u ∈ S u\in S . 
 
 
 Resolving sets can be used to locate nodes in a network modeled as a graph, and fault-tolerant resolving sets
can be used to locate nodes even though one of the nodes of the resolving set fails.
Here we consider the possibility of more than one failure.
Note that the set obtained after the removal of any  k k  vertices of a resolving set  S S  remains resolving if and only if every pair of vertices of the graph is resolved by at least  k + 1 k+1  distinct vertices of  S S . This last concept was introduced in  [ 9 ] , concretely,
a set  S S  of vertices of a graph is a  k k -resolving set  if for every pair of vertices  x x  and  y y , the vectors  r ⁡ ( x | S ) r(x|S)  and  r ⁡ ( y | S ) r(y|S)  have at least  k k  different coordinates.
Hence, a set  S S  remains resolving even though  k k  vertices fail if and only if  S S  is a  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -resolving set.
The  k k -metric dimension and  k k -resolving sets of a graph and, concretely, of some product graphs have been studied in  [ 2 ,  9 ,  10 ,  11 ,  12 ,  18 ,  22 ,  28 ] .
In the survey  [ 20 ] , an extensive summary of known results and applications of the  k k -metric dimension is given. 
 
 
 It is worth mentioning that, in contrast to resolving sets or fault-tolerant resolving sets,  k k -resolving sets do not always exist for  k ≥ 3 k\geq 3   [ 9 ] .
Whenever a graph  G G  has at least one  k k -resolving set, a  k k -resolving set of minimum cardinality is a  k k -metric basis , and its cardinality is the  k k -metric dimension  of  G G , denoted by  dim k ( G ) \dim_{k}(G)   [ 9 ] .
With this terminology,  1 1 -resolving sets correspond to resolving sets and  2 2 -resolving sets correspond to fault-tolerant resolving sets. 
 
 
 Here we are interested in finding resolving sets tolerant to  k k  failures, that is,  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -resolving sets and, more precisely,  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -metric bases and the  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -metric dimension of three-dimensional grids.
Grid graphs have been proven to be very useful in diverse areas such as
robotics, video games and telecommunications  [ 1 ,  8 ,  19 ] .
The value of the  k k -metric dimension of two-dimensional grids is determined in  [ 2 ] . 
 
 
 Since a superset of a resolving set is also a resolving set, the following result is obvious and provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a  k k -resolving set. 
 
 
 Remark 1 . 
 
 A graph  G G  has a  k k -resolving set if and only if  V ⁡ ( G ) V(G)  is a  k k -resolving set. 
 
 
 
 Next result gives a lower bound on the  k k -metric dimension of a graph, whenever it is defined. 
 
 
 Proposition 2 . 
 
 [ 9 ] 
If a graph  G G  has a  k k -resolving set, then  dim k ( G ) ≥ dim ( G ) + k − 1 \dim_{k}(G)\geq\dim(G)+k-1 . 
 
 
 
 The paper is organised as follows.
In Section  2 , some properties concerning resolving sets for three-dimensional grids are given. Concretely, we prove that the metric dimension of a three-dimensional grid is exactly 3 and establish lower bounds on the number of vertices resolving a fixed pair of vertices. These bounds will be very useful in Section  3 , devoted to the study of resolving sets tolerant to  k k  failures in three-dimensional grids. Concretely, we determine the  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -metric dimension and describe  ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -metric bases of these grids in almost all cases. 
 
 
 
 2  Resolving sets in three-dimensional grids

We also point out sets of vertices resolving a fixed pair of vertices, that will be
very useful in Section  3 .

 It is known that the metric dimension of an  r r D grid is at most  r r   [ 17 ] .
Also in this paper, it is said that it is exactly  r r  and the proof is left to the reader. However, in general this is not in true. For example, it is known that the metric dimension of the hypercube, that can be viewed as a  r r D grid with  n 1 = ⋯ = n r = 2 n_{1}=\dots=n_{r}=2 , is less than  r r  for  r ≥ 5 r\geq 5   [ 4 ] .
It remains an open problem to determine the exact value of the metric dimension for general grids.
A discussion on asymptotic values of the metric dimension of a grid is included in  [ 16 ] .

 Lemma 6 .
