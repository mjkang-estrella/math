                                                   Abstract. The inquiry into identifying sets of monomials that can be
                                                   eliminated from a generic homogeneous polynomial via a linear change of
                                                   coordinates was initiated by E. K. Wakeford. This linear algebra problem
                                                   prompted C. K. Fan and J. Losonczy to introduce the notion of acyclic
                                                   matchings in the additive group Zn , subsequently extended to abelian
                                                   groups by the latter author. Alon, Fan, Kleitman, and Losonczy estab-
                                                   lished the acyclic matching property for Zn . This note aims to classify
                                                   all abelian groups with respect to the acyclic matching property.




                                                                            1. Introduction
                                             Let (G, +) be an abelian group. Consider nonempty finite subsets A, B ⊆ G.
                                          A matching from A to B is a bijection f : A → B satisfying the condition
                                          a + f (a) ∈
                                                    / A for all a ∈ A. If there exists a matching from A to B, we say A
                                          is matched to B. Obvious necessary conditions for the existence of a matching
                                          from A to B are |A| = |B| and 0 ∈   / B. An abelian group G is said to possess
                                          the matching property if these conditions on A and B are sufficient to ensure
                                          the existence of a matching from A to B. The question arises: which groups
                                          have the matching property? This question was addressed by Losonczy in [11]:
                                          Theorem 1.1. Let G be an abelian group. Then, G possesses the matching
                                          property if and only if G is torsion-free or cyclic of prime order.
                                            For any matching f : A → B, the associated multiplicity function mf : G →
                                          Z≥0 is defined via the rule:
                                                                   mf (x) = |{a ∈ A : a + f (a) = x}|,
                                          for all x ∈ G.
                                             A matching f : A → B is called acyclic if for any matching g : A → B,
                                          mf = mg implies f = g. If there exists an acyclic matching from A to B, we say
                                          A is acyclically matched to B. A group G has the acyclic matching property if for
                                          any pair of subsets A and B in G with |A| = |B| and 0 ∈ / B, there is at least one
                                          acyclic matching from A to B. Alon et al. proved in [5] that the additive group
                                          Zn possesses the acyclic matching property. Losonczy extended this result to
                                          all abelian torsion-free groups by leveraging a total ordering compatible with
                                          the structure of abelian torsion-free groups, as established by Levi [10].

Theorem 1.2. Let G be an abelian torsion-free group. Then G possesses the

   The concept of matching was introduced by Fan and Losonczy in [7] as a
tool to investigate Wakeford’s classical problem regarding canonical forms for
symmetric tensors [12]. In particular, let V be a q-dimensional complex vector
space, S p (V ) be the symmetric tensor of degree p and A be a fixed set con-
sisting of q(q − 1) monomials in S p (V ), where ik > 0 for all k. It is proved
in [7] that the generic element of S p (V ) can be conjugated, via an appropriate
linear transformation, to an element whose support lies outside of A. The main
tool to prove this is by establishing the existence of an acyclic matching in a
specific weighted bipartite graph. A linear analogue of matchings in vector sub-
spaces over field extensions was introduced in [6]. The matroidal counterpart
of matchings was explored in [4].

   In this paper, our objective is to establish a necessary and sufficient condition
for an abelian group to possess the acyclic matching property. In view of
Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, it naturally prompts an inquiry into the acyclic matching
property in Z/pZ. This problem was initially explored in [2], where it was
proved that infinitely many primes p exist for which Z/pZ lacks the acyclic
matching property. Particularly, it was shown that for the following two families
of primes, Z/pZ does not exhibit the acyclic matching property:
      • p ≡ −1 (mod 8)
      • The multiplicative order of 2 modulo p is an odd number.
Considering the latter condition along with a result from [8] concerning the
parity of the multiplicative order of 2 modulo p, it is observed that primes
p where Z/pZ lacks the acyclic matching property have a density of at least
 7
24 . Additionally, employing a rectification principle [9], which posits that a
suitably small subset of Z/pZ can be embedded in integers while preserving
certain additive properties, it is proved in [1] that for A, B p ⊂ Z/pZ, A is
acyclically matched to B provided that 0 ∈ / B and |A| = |B| ≤ log2 p − 1.

                                2. Main results
   In this section, we present our main results concerning the classification of
all abelian groups with respect to the acyclic matching property. In particular,
as outlined in Theorem 2.8 and Theorem 2.10, we establish initially that for
p > 5, Z/pZ lacks the acyclic matching property. Furthermore, we determine
a necessary and sufficient condition for an abelian group to possess the acyclic
matching property. We initiate our investigation by counting the matchings
with the same multiplicity functions in Z/nZ.

Remark 2.7. In Lemma 2.6 we come up with two sets A and B of size n − 3
that are not acyclically matched. However, Example 2.2 in [1] demonstrates
that in an abelian group G if |A| = |B| = |G| − 1 or |A| = |B| = |G| − 2, A must
be acyclically matched with B. Therefore, A and B in Lemma 2.6 represent
the largest possible sets that fail to exhibit acyclic matchings.
   In the following theorem, we classify all cyclic groups of prime order with
respect to the acyclic matching property. This lemma gives a negative answer to
Question 2.4 in [2], and serves as the “finite cyclic group” analogue of Theorem
1 in [5] and Theorem 4.1 in [11].
Theorem 2.8. If p > 5 is prime, then Z/pZ does not possess the acyclic
matching property.
Proof. The proof is immediate from Lemma 2.6.

Remark 2.9. It is worth mentioning that Lemma 2.6 and Theorem 2.8 are
applicable in a more general setting. In essence, we can assert that for n > 5,
Z/nZ lacks the acyclic matching property. The case where n is prime follows
directly from Theorem 2.8. For non-prime n, choose a such that gcd(a, n) 6= 1.
Choose x ∈ Z/nZ \ hai and set A := hai and B := (hai ∪ {x}) \ {0}. Then
A is not matched to B, let alone acyclically matched. Therefore, Z/nZ does
not possess the acyclic matching property. With all this, we are primarily
interested in the scenario where n is prime, in view of Theorem 1.1.
   We are now ready to provide a necessary and sufficient condition for abelian
groups to possess the acyclic matching property. This theorem serves as the
“acyclic matching property” analogue of Theorem 1.1.
Theorem 2.10. An abelian group G possesses the acyclic matching property
if and only if either G is torsion-free or G = Z/pZ, where p ∈ {2, 3, 5}.
Proof. Suppose G possesses the acyclic matching property. Then it also sat-
isfies the matching property. From Theorem 1.1, it follows that G is either
torsion-free or cyclic of prime order. However, according to Lemma 2.8, if G is
cyclic of prime order with acyclic matching property, then p ≤ 5, as claimed.
Conversely, it follows from Theorem 1.2 that torsion-free groups possess the
acyclic matching property. Also, the simulation results in [3, page 153, Algo-
rithm 3] demonstrate that Z/pZ for p = 2, 3, 5 exhibits the acyclic matching
property, thereby completing the proof.                                      
