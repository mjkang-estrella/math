<H3><A NAME="SECTION00040200000000000000">&#160;</A>
<A NAME="sec:szem">&#160;</A>
<BR>
Szemer&#233;di's Theorem
</H3>

<P>
Another well known result of Ramsey theory is <EM>Van der Waerden's
  theorem</EM>:
<BLOCKQUOTE>
If 
<!-- MATH: $\mathbb{N}$ -->
<IMG
 WIDTH="17" HEIGHT="18" ALIGN="BOTTOM" BORDER="0"
 SRC="img12.gif"
 ALT="$\mathbb{N} $">
is colored with finitely many colors, then one of the
  colors contains arithmetic progressions of arbitrary finite length.
</BLOCKQUOTE>Using a method of Shelah, Van der Waerden's theorem is known to be
provable in 
<!-- MATH: $\mathsf{RCA}_0$ -->
<IMG
 WIDTH="45" HEIGHT="33" ALIGN="MIDDLE" BORDER="0"
 SRC="img2.gif"
 ALT="$\mathsf{RCA}_0$">.
The so-called ``density version'' of Van der
Waerden's theorem is due to Szemer&#233;di:
<BLOCKQUOTE>
If 
<!-- MATH: $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ -->
<IMG
 WIDTH="52" HEIGHT="32" ALIGN="MIDDLE" BORDER="0"
 SRC="img8.gif"
 ALT="$A\subseteq\mathbb{N} $">
is such that
  <BR><P></P>
<DIV ALIGN="CENTER">
<!-- MATH: \begin{displaymath}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{|A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}|}n>0
\end{displaymath} -->


<IMG
 WIDTH="200" HEIGHT="47"
 SRC="img55.gif"
 ALT="\begin{displaymath}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\vert A\cap\{1,\dots,n\}\vert}n>0\end{displaymath}">
</DIV>
<BR CLEAR="ALL">
<P></P>
then <I>A</I> contains arithmetic progressions of arbitrary finite
  length.
</BLOCKQUOTE>Essentially nothing is known about the strength of Szemer&#233;di's
theorem.  In particular it is unknown whether Szemer&#233;di's theorem is
provable in 
<!-- MATH: $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ -->
<IMG
 WIDTH="45" HEIGHT="33" ALIGN="MIDDLE" BORDER="0"
 SRC="img41.gif"
 ALT="$\mathsf{ACA}_0$">.
