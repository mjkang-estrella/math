                                           A FURTHER STUDY OF POLYNOMIAL gn,q OVER FINITE FIELDS

                                                                    NERANGA FERNANDO AND BHITALI KOUSIK




arXiv:2606.01037v1 [math.NT] 31 May 2026
                                                   Abstract. Let n ≥ 0 be an integer and q a prime power. The polynomial gn,q was
                                                   introduced in [10] with the purpose of finding new classes of permutation polynomials
                                                   over finite fields. We investigate the permutation behaviour of the polynomial gn,q (X)
                                                   over finite fields of even characteristic. We introduce the multivariate case of the
                                                   polynomial gn,q , and study the permutation polynomials in several variables and local
                                                   permutation polynomials resulting from the polynomials gn,q (X1 , X2 , . . . , Xk ). We also
                                                   present several new identities of gn,q (X), and present some open questions on the
                                                   permutation property of gn,q (X).




                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                              Let p be a prime, e and m be positive integers, q = pm , and Fq the finite field with
                                           q elements. A polynomial f (X) ∈ Fq [X] is a called a permutation polynomial if the
                                           associated mapping from Fq to Fq is a permutation. It is a well-known fact that every
                                           mapping from Fq to Fq can be uniquely represented by a polynomial of degree lees than
                                           q, and the unique polynomial can be found by Lagrange Interpolation. Finding new
                                           classes of permutation polynomials over finite fields has been a hot topic since the 1970s
                                           due to their applications in the areas of cryptography, coding theory, combinatorics,
                                           finite geometry, and computer science.
                                              The study of permutation polynomials over finite fields has a long history. It was
                                           Charles Hermite who studied permutation polynomials over finite prime fields in the
                                           1860s. Leonard Eugene Dickson studied permutation polynomials over finite arbitrary
                                           fields while he was a PhD student in the 1890s. Issai Schur later named these poly-
                                           nomials in Dickson’s honor. Dickson polynomials have played a pivotal role in the
                                           area of permutation polynomials over finite fields, and the permutation property of the
                                           Dickson polynomials is completely known.
                                              In 2009, Xiang-dong Hou, Gary Mullen, James Sellers and Joseph Yucas introduced
                                           the twin of Dickson polynomials, reversed Dickson polynomials, by interchanging the
                                           roles of the variable and the parameter of Dickson polynomials. Polynomial gn,q was
                                           introduced by Xiang-dong Hou in [10] as a q-ary version of the reversed Dickson poly-
                                           nomial, but its permutation behaviour was first studied in [11]. In [10], the author
                                           showed that for each integer n ≥ 0, there exists a unique polynomial gn,q ∈ Fp [X] such
                                           that

   Let Fkq be the k-fold cartesian product of Fq , where k ≥ 1 is an integer. Let X =
(X1 , . . . , Xk ), Xi = (X1 , . . . , Xi−1 , Xi+1 , . . . , Xk ), and Fq [X] denote the ring of polynomials
in k variables over Fq . It is well-known that any map from Fkq to Fq can be uniquely
represented as a polynomial f ∈ Fq [X] such that degXi (f ) < q for all i = 1, . . . , k, where
degXi (f ) is the degree of f as a polynomial in the variable Xi with coefficients in the
polynomial ring Fq [Xi ].
Definition 1.1. A polynomial f ∈ Fq [X1 , . . . , Xk ] is a permutation polynomial in k
variables over Fq if the equation f (X1 , . . . , Xk ) = α has exactly q k−1 solutions in Fkq for
each α ∈ Fq .
Definition 1.2. A polynomial f ∈ Fq [X] is called a local permutation polynomial (or
LPP) if for each i, 1 ≤ i ≤ k, the polynomial f (a1 , . . . , ai−1 , Xi , ai+1 , . . . , ak ) is a
permutation polynomial in Fq [Xi ], for all choices of ai ∈ Fk−1

To the best of our knowledge, local permutation polynomials were first studied by Gary
L. Mullen in [16] and [17]. He gave necessary and sufficient conditions for polynomials
in two and three variables to be local permutations polynomials over a finite prime
field Fp in [16] and [17], respectively. Those conditions were expressed in terms of the
coefficients of the polynomial.
  For two variables, it was shown in [2] that the degree of an LLP in Fq [x, y] is bounded
above by 2(q −2). In [7], the authors showed that the result is true for several variables.

wq (n) > 4, where wq (n) denotes the base 4 weight of n.

Theorem 5.3. ([7]) If f is an LPP, then f is linear if q = 2 and q = 3, and has degree
at most n(q − 2) otherwise.

        tation polynomial.

        is a (local) permutation polynomial.

Proposition 5.6. The polynomial gn,q (X, Y) is a permutation polynomial over FQ if
