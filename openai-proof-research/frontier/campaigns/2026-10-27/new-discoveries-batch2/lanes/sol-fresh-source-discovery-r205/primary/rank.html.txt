If F is a number field and F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots is a tower of Galois extensions with \mathrm{Gal}(F_{n}/F) cyclic of order p^{n}, K. Iwasawa gave a closed formula for the growth of the p-part of the class number of F_{n} whenever n is large enough. We present a new proof of this formula, obtained by reinterpreting its summands in terms of Sylvester matrix rank functions on the Iwasawa algebra \Lambda without resorting to the classification of finitely-generated \Lambda-modules up to pseudo-isomorphism. 1. Introduction The purpose of this expository note is to show how some of the results in the Iwasawa theory of \mathbb{Z}_{p}-extensions of number fields can be interpreted in the language of Sylvester rank functions. In the mid 1950’s, Kenkichi Iwasawa was interested in the following question: given a tower F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots of number fields, how does the size of the class group \mathrm{Cl}(F_{i}) grow? Recall that the ideal class group \mathrm{Cl}(K) of a number field K with ring of integers \mathcal{O}_{K} is defined as the quotient J_{K}/P_{K}, where J_{K} is the group of fractional ideals of \mathcal{O}_{K} and P_{K} is the subgroup of principal ideals. This is always a finite group, and it measures precisely the failure of unique factorization in \mathcal{O}_{K}. In 1956, he gave a talk titled “A theorem on Abelian groups and its application in algebraic number theory.” on the Summer Meeting of the American Mathematical Society in Seattle. In his talk, he annouced the following remarkable theorem: Theorem 1.1 ( [ 2 ] ) . Let F be a number field, and F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots be a tower of finite normal extensions of F such that for L=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}F_{i} we have \mathrm{Gal}(L/F)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}. For each n, let L_{n} be the subfield of L fixed by p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p} and p^{e_{n}} be the highest power of p dividing the order of the class group \mathrm{Cl}(L_{n}). Then, there exist integers \nu, \lambda\geq 0 and \mu\geq 0 such that e_{n}=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu for sufficiently large n. The main algebraic ingredient in a standard modern proof of Iwasawa’s theorem is a classification of finitely generated \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]-modules. Taking G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}, although the completed group ring \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!] is not a principal ideal domain, it behaves very similarly to one. For every finitely generated \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]-module M, there are elements f_{1}\,,\ldots\,,f_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!] and a \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]-module homomorphism \varphi\colon M\to\left(\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]/(f_{i})\right)\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]^{m} such that \ker\varphi and \operatorname{coker}\varphi are both finite; this is the so called classification of \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]-modules up to pseudo-isomorphism . Thus, up to a finite constant error, it suffices to check Theorem 1.1 on modules of the form \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]/(f_{i}). Such modules are very easy to describe since \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!] is a unique factorization domain. Our goal is to give a proof of Theorem 1.1 without resorting to the classification of \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]-modules up to pseudo-isomorphism. The way to go about this is to study three natural families of rank functions on ring \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!] and to realize the quantities appearing in Theorem 1.1 as the “rescaled sides” of a triangle formed by points in these three families (to made clear in Section 3 ). These families all accumulate in a common, canonical rank function on \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!], and computing the precise rate of convergence for this accumulation yields Iwasawa’s formula. The structure of this note is as follows: we collect in Section 2 the definition of Sylvester rank functions and some basic examples, the facts about the Iwawasa algebra \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!] that will be used as well as the input from Global Class Field Theory that allows us to deduce information about the ideal class groups along the tower L/F. In Section 3 , we define the three families of Sylvester rank functions on \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!] which capture the quantities in Theorem 1.1 , and we prove this precise relation in Corollary 3.8 . We then show how this Corollary immediately yields Theorem 1.1 for the case where F contains a single prime over p, totally ramified in L, in Section 4 . The adaptations requried to treat the general case are presented in Section 5 . Sylvester rank functions For this subection, we let R denote an associative ring with unity, not necessarily commutative. We denote the space of all matrices of size n\times m over R by \mathrm{Mat}_{n\times m}(R). For square matrices of size n\times n, we use the shorthand \mathrm{Mat}_{n}(R). The set \mathrm{Mat}(R) denotes the set of all matrices over R of any size, not necessarily square. Definition 2.1 . A Sylvester matrix rank function is a map \operatorname{rk}\colon\mathrm{Mat}(R)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} assigning to each matrix A over R a non-negative real number \operatorname{rk}(A) satisfying: (SMat1) \operatorname{rk}(0)=0 and \operatorname{rk}(1)=1; (SMat2) \operatorname{rk}(AB)\leq\min\{\operatorname{rk}(A)\,,\operatorname{rk}(B)\} for any pair of matrices A\,,B\in\mathrm{Mat}(R) which can be multiplied; (SMat3) \operatorname{rk}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\ 0&B\end{pmatrix}=\operatorname{rk}(A)+\operatorname{rk}(B) for any pair of matrices A and B; (SMat4) \operatorname{rk}\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\ 0&B\end{pmatrix}\geq\operatorname{rk}(A)+\operatorname{rk}(B) for any trio of matrices A, B and C of the appropriate sizes. If R is a field and \operatorname{rk} is the usual notion of rank from linear algebra (i.e. number of pivots in a row-echelon form), then axioms (SMat1) through (SMat4) hold. In fact, in this case, \operatorname{rk} is the unique Sylvester matrix rank function defined on R: it follows from (SMat2) that \operatorname{rk}(U)=n for any invertible matrix U\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}(R), and again that \operatorname{rk}(UA)=\operatorname{rk}(AV)=\operatorname{rk}(A) for any matrix A and any pair (U,V) of invertible matrices of the appropriate sizes. Thus, for an arbitrary rank function \operatorname{rk}, \operatorname{rk}(A) is determined by the rank of any row-echelon form. This must in turn coincide with the number of pivots by (SMat1) and (SMat3). Let \mathcal{M} denote the set of isomorphism classes of finitely presented right R-modules. Rank functions can also be defined in \mathcal{M}: Definition 2.2 . A Sylvester module rank function is a map \dim\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} assigning to each finitely presented right R-module M a non-negative real number \dim(M) satisfying: (SMod1) \dim(0)=0, \dim(R)=1; (SMod2) \dim(M\oplus N)=\dim(M)+\dim(N); (SMod3) For any exact sequence M^{\prime}\to M\to M^{\prime\prime}\to 0 of finitely presented modules we have \dim(M^{\prime\prime})\leq\dim M\leq\dim M^{\prime}+\dim M^{\prime\prime}\,. Observe that if R is Noetherian, then \mathcal{M} coincides with the class of all finitely generated right R-modules. This will be the case for all rings considered in this paper. There is a natural connection between matrix rank functions and module rank functions: Definition 2.3 . Let \dim be a Sylvester module rank function, and for any matrix A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m\times n}(R) define the associated matrix rank function \operatorname{rk}_{\dim}(A)=m-\dim(R^{m}/AR^{n})\,. Conversely, if \operatorname{rk} is a Sylvester matrix rank function on R and M\simeq R^{m}/AR^{n} is a finitely-presented right R-module, define the associated module rank function \dim_{\operatorname{rk}}(M)=m-\operatorname{rk}(A)\,. Proposition 2.4 ( [ 6 , Prop. 3.1.4] ) . The associated module rank function \dim_{\operatorname{rk}}(M) does not depend on the choice of presentation M\simeq R^{m}/AR^{n}, and is a Sylvester module rank function on R. The associated matrix rank function \operatorname{rk}_{\dim} is also a Sylvester matrix rank function, and the maps \dim\mapsto\operatorname{rk}_{\dim}\,,\quad\operatorname{rk}\mapsto\dim_{\operatorname{rk}} induce bijections between the sets of Sylvester module rank functions and Sylvester matrix rank functions on any given ring R. We shall denote by \mathbb{P}(R) the set of all Sylvester matrix/module rank functions on a ring R, where an associated pair \operatorname{rk}/\dim represents the same element of \mathbb{P}(R). It is a convex, compact subspace of a real vector space. For any matrix A over R, the function d_{A}(\operatorname{rk},\operatorname{rk}^{\prime})=|\operatorname{rk}(A)-\operatorname{rk}^{\prime}(A)| defines a pseudo-metric on the set \mathbb{P}(R). We say that a sequence \operatorname{rk}_{i}\in\mathbb{P}(R) converges pointwise to a rank function \operatorname{rk}\in\mathbb{P}(R) if d_{A}(\operatorname{rk}_{i},\operatorname{rk})\to 0 as i\to\infty for any matrix A over R. Example 2.5 . Suppose that R is commutative and N\neq 0 is an R-module of finite length \ell_{R}(N). For any finitely presented R-module M, we have that M\otimes_{R}N is again an R-module of finite length, and the map M\mapsto\dim_{N}(M)=\frac{\ell_{R}(M\otimes_{R}N)}{\ell_{R}(N)} is a Sylvester module rank function on R. The associated Sylvester matrix rank function is defined by \operatorname{rk}_{N}(A)=\frac{\ell_{R}(AN^{n})}{\ell_{R}N} for any matrix A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{m\times n}(R). ∎ Example 2.6 . Let \varphi\colon R\to S be a ring map and \operatorname{rk} be a Sylvester matrix rank function on S. Then, \varphi^{\#}\operatorname{rk}(A)=\operatorname{rk}(\varphi(A)) is a Sylvester matrix rank function on R. If \dim is the associated Sylvester module rank function on S, then \varphi^{\#}\dim(M)=\dim(M\otimes_{R}S) is the module rank function associated to \varphi^{\#}\operatorname{rk}. In particular, if R is a subring of S, the restriction of any rank function on S to \mathrm{Mat}(R) is again a rank function on R. ∎ Example 2.7 . Let \mathbb{F} be a field, V\neq 0 be a finite-dimensional \mathbb{F}-vector space and \varphi\colon R\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V) a ring homomorphism. Then, for any matrix A over R, the map \operatorname{rk}_{V}(A)=\frac{\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb{F}}(\varphi(A))}{\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V} is a Sylvester matrix rank function on R. It is the pullback of the unique Sylvester matrix rank function on \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V), the rank over \mathbb{F} normalized by \dim_{\mathbb{F}}V. ∎ Example 2.8 ( [ 6 , Exmp. 3.1.10] ) . Assume now that R is commutative domain and that S is a multiplicative set of non-zero elements in R. If \operatorname{rk} is a Sylvester matrix rank function on R, then it extends to a Sylvester matrix rank function on S^{-1}R if and only \operatorname{rk}(x)=1 for any x\in S. Moreover, this extension is unique. ∎ A particular consequence of the phenomena in Example 2.8 is the following result: Proposition 2.9 ( [ 6 , Cor. 5.2.5] ) . Let R be a commutative domain with field of fractions \mathcal{Q}, and \operatorname{rk}_{i} a sequence of Sylvester matrix rank functions on R. Then, we have \lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{rk}_{i}=\operatorname{rk}_{\mathcal{Q}} if and only if \lim_{i\to\infty}\operatorname{rk}_{i}(x)=1 for all non-zero x\in R. 2.2. Iwasawa algebras Let \Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![\mathbb{Z}_{p}]\!] be the completed group ring of the pro-p group \mathbb{Z}_{p}. If we write \mathbb{Z}_{p}=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle} and T=\gamma-1\in\Lambda, then we can identify \Lambda with the ring \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![T]\!] of formal power series in T over \mathbb{Z}_{p}. In particular, it is a local Noetherian domain with maximal ideal (p,T). Recall that a distinguished polynomial f(T)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T] is a monic polynomial of the form f(T)=T^{s}+pa_{s-1}T^{s-1}+\cdots+pa_{1}T+pa_{0} for some coefficients a_{0}\,,\ldots\,,a_{s-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}. In particular, the constant polynomial f(T)=1 is distinguished. The following result is known as an instance of the Weierstrass preparation theorem: Theorem 2.10 ( [ 7 , Thm. 7.3] ) . For any non-zero f\in\Lambda, there exists a unique distinguished polynomial h\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T] and a unit u\in\Lambda^{\times} such that f=p^{a}uh\text{ for some integer }a\geq 0\,. We will also make use of the Weierstrass division theorem for the power series ring \Lambda. Proposition 2.11 ( [ 7 , Prop. 7.2] ) . For any f\in\Lambda\smallsetminus p\Lambda, if f=uh with u\in\Lambda^{\times} and h distinguished, then \Lambda/(f) is a free \mathbb{Z}_{p}-module of rank \deg h and basis 1\,,T\,,\ldots\,,T^{\deg h-1}. Since the quotient \Lambda/(p)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![T]\!] is also a domain, the ideal (p) is prime and therefore we can consider the localization \Lambda_{(p)}=\left\{\frac{f}{h}\colon f\,,h\in\Lambda\,,h\not\in(p)\right\}\,. We shall use the following facts about \Lambda_{(p)} and its quotients: Lemma 2.12 . The ring \Lambda_{(p)} is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer p and residue field \mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(T)\!) of Laurent power series in T over \mathbb{F}_{p}. If M is a finitely generated torsion \Lambda_{(p)}-module, then p^{a}M=0 for some integer a\geq 0 and \ell_{\Lambda_{(p)}}(M)=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(T)\!)}(p^{i}M/p^{i+1}M)\,. In particular, \ell_{\Lambda_{(p)}}(\Lambda_{(p)}/(p^{k}))=k for any k\geq 0. Proof. For an element \dfrac{f}{h} in \Lambda_{(p)} we can uniquely write f=p^{a}f_{0} with f_{0}\in\Lambda\smallsetminus p\Lambda and a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}. In particular, f_{0} is a unit in \Lambda_{(p)}, which proves that any element in \Lambda_{(p)} factors a power of p times a unit. If I is any non-zero ideal of \Lambda_{(p)}, then choosing x=p^{a}u\in I with u\in\Lambda_{(p)}^{\times} and a minimal shows that I=(p^{a}): for any y=p^{b}u^{\prime}\in I, we have a\leq b and therefore y=p^{a}\cdot p^{b-a}u^{\prime}\in(p^{a}). This shows that \Lambda_{(p)} is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer p. The residue field of \Lambda_{(p)} can then be identified with the fraction field of \Lambda/(p)\simeq\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![T]\!]. If M=\langle m_{1}\,,\ldots\,,m_{k}\rangle as a \Lambda_{(p)}-module, then since all the m_{i} are torsion elements, there exists a high enough power p^{a} of p that annihilates them: p^{a}m_{1}=\cdots=p^{a}m_{k}=0. This proves that p^{a}M=0. This gives a finite filtration M\geq pM\geq p^{2}M\geq\cdots\geq p^{a-1}M\geq p^{a}M=0 whose successive quotients are finite-dimensional \mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(T)\!)-vector spaces. Since \mathbb{F}_{p}(\!(T)\!) is the only simple \Lambda_{(p)}-module, the claim about length follows. ∎ For every integer n\geq 0, let \omega_{n}=\gamma^{p^{n}}-1=(1+T)^{p^{n}}-1\in\Lambda\,. Observe that each \omega_{n} is a distinguished polynomial in T of degree p^{n}. We can identify the quotient \Lambda/(\omega_{n}) with the group ring \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}]\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}[C_{p^{n}}] of the cyclic group of order p^{n} over \mathbb{Z}_{p}. Lemma 2.13 . For every pair n\geq 0\,,k\geq 1 of integers, the ring \Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})[C_{p^{n}}] is a local Artinian ring and a free \mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}-module of rank p^{n}. Seen either as an \Lambda-module or as a \mathbb{Z}_{p}-module, its length is \ell_{\Lambda}(\Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n}))=\ell_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n}))=kp^{n}\,. For any finitely generated \Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})-module M, we have \ell_{\Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})}(M)=\ell_{\Lambda}(M)=\ell_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M)=\log_{p}|M|=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(p^{i}M/p^{i+1}M)\,. Proof. The isomorphism \Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z})[C_{p^{n}}] is clear from the definitions. Since this is a finite ring, it is necessarily Artinian, and it is also local, being a quotient of the local ring \Lambda. Its description as a group ring shows that it is free of rank p^{n} as a \mathbb{Z}/p^{k}\mathbb{Z}-module. Since \Lambda\to\Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n}) is surjective, the only simple \Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})-module is \mathbb{F}_{p}, which also the only simple \mathbb{Z}_{p}-module, we get equality of lengths \ell_{\Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})}(M)=\ell_{\Lambda}(M)=\ell_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M) for any finitely generated \Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n})-module M. The filtration M\geq pM\geq p^{2}M\geq\cdots\geq p^{k-1}M\geq p^{k}M=0 whose successive quotients are finite-dimensional \mathbb{F}_{p}-vector spaces proves that \ell_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(p^{i}M/p^{i+1}M)=\log_{p}|M|\,. The formula for the length of the ring \Lambda/(p^{k},\omega_{n}) now follows, since its order is (p^{k})^{p^{n}}. ∎ We will also use the following result, which is a particular case of a theorem of M. Harris ( [ 1 ] ): Proposition 2.14 (cf. [ 6 , Thm. 5.2.6] ) . Let M be a finitely generated \Lambda-module, and for each n\geq 0, put \dim_{n}M=\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}((M/\omega_{n}M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p})}{p^{n}}\,. Then, the \dim_{n} are Sylvester module rank functions on \Lambda, and \lim_{n\to\infty}\dim_{n}=\dim_{\Lambda} where \dim_{\Lambda}(M)=\dim_{\mathcal{Q}}(M\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathcal{Q}) and \mathcal{Q} is the field of fractions of \Lambda. Proof. For each n\geq 0, consider the finite-dimensional \mathbb{Q}_{p}-vector space V_{n}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G/G^{p^{n}}] of dimension p^{n}. The left action of G on the quotient group G/G^{p^{n}} induces a ring homomorphism \Lambda\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(V_{n}), and by definition the functions \dim_{n} are the pullbacks of the normalized dimension in \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(V_{n}) as in Example 2.7 . In particular, they are Sylvester module rank functions, and we denote by \operatorname{rk}_{n} the associated Sylvester matrix rank function. To prove that \lim_{n\to\infty}\dim_{n}=\dim_{\Lambda}, it suffices to prove that \operatorname{rk}_{n}(x)\to 1 as n\to\infty for any non-zero x\in\Lambda by Proposition 2.9 . Using Theorem 2.10 , we can write x=p^{a}uh where a\geq 0, u\in\Lambda^{\times} is a unit and h\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[T] is a distinguished polynomial. In particular, \operatorname{rk}_{n}(x)=\operatorname{rk}_{n}(h). Note that by Proposition 2.11 , \mathbb{Q}_{p}[G/G^{p^{n}}]\simeq\Lambda/(\omega_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p} has \{1\,,T\,,\ldots\,,T^{p^{n}-1}\} as a basis. If k=\deg h, then multiplication by h is injective on the subspace spanned by \{1\,,T\,,\ldots\,,T^{p^{n}-1-k}\}, which gives us the bounds 1\geq\operatorname{rk}_{n}(h)\geq\frac{p^{n}-k}{p^{n}}\,. As n goes to infinity, the right hand side converges to 1, proving the claim. Hence, as in Example 2.8 , we have \lim_{n\to\infty}\dim_{n}=\dim_{\Lambda} as desired. ∎ 2.3. Global Class Field Theory Let K be a number field, that is, a finite extension of \mathbb{Q}, and denote by \mathcal{O}_{K} its ring of integers. A fractional ideal of K is a subset I\subset K of the form r^{-1}J, where J is an ideal of \mathcal{O}_{K}. It is invertible if there exists another fractional ideal J\subset K such that IJ=\mathcal{O}_{K}. The set of all invertible fractional ideals forms an abelian group J_{K} under multiplication, and it contains the subgroup P_{K} of all principal fractional ideals \mathcal{O}_{K}x for non-zero x\in K. The quotient \mathrm{Cl}(K)=J_{K}/P_{K} forms an abelian group called the ideal class group of K. The ring \mathcal{O}_{K} is a principal ideal domain if and only if \mathrm{Cl}(K)=1. The class number of K is the order h(K)=|\mathrm{Cl}(K)| of its ideal class group, and is a measure of how much does unique factorization fails in the ring \mathcal{O}_{K}. Assume that L/K is a Galois extension of number fields with Galois group G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/K). Recall the existence of a multiplicative Galois norm map N_{L/K}\colon L\to K given by N_{L/K}(x)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(x)\,. This norm map induces a homomorphism N_{L/K}\colon J_{L}\to J_{K} between the groups of fractional ideals by declaring N_{L/K}(I) to be the fractional ideal of K generated by the set \{N_{L/K}(x)\colon x\in I\} for every fractional ideal I of L. This map sends principal fractional ideals to principal fractional ideals, and thus descends to a homomorphism N_{L/K}\colon\mathrm{Cl}(L)\to\mathrm{Cl}(K) of ideal class groups. Now, let F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots be a tower of finite Galois extensions of a number field F such that for L=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}F_{i} we have G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}. For each n, let L_{n} be the subfield of L fixed by p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}. For an abelian group A, denote by A(p) its p-primary part, that is, the set of all elements annihilated by some power of p. With the norm maps defined above, the class groups \mathrm{Cl}(L_{n}) fit into an inverse system of abelian groups, and we let X=\varprojlim\mathrm{Cl}(L_{n})(p)\,. Since each L_{n} is a Galois extension of F, the Galois group G acts continuously and compatibly with the norm maps on each \mathrm{Cl}(L_{n}) endowed with the discrete topology. Totally ramified, single prime \mathbb{Z}_{p}-extensions We can now turn to the proof of Theorem 1.1 . As such, we recall its notation: F is a number field, F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots is a tower of finite normal extensions of F such that for L=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}F_{i} we have G=\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}. For each n\geq 0, L_{n} denotes the subfield fixed by G^{p^{n}}\simeq p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p} and p^{e_{n}} is the highest power of p dividing the order of the class group \mathrm{Cl}(L_{n}). We let T=\gamma-1\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!], \Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![T]\!], \omega_{n}=\gamma^{p^{n}}-1 and for any abelian group M we denote by M(p) its p-primary part. Bearing in mind the statement of Corollary 3.8 , the ideal scenario would be to find a torsion \mathbb{Z}_{p}[\![G]\!]-module M for which we have |M/\omega_{n}M|=|\mathrm{Cl}(L_{n})(p)| whenever n is large enough. Unfortunately, there is no natural candidate for this M in general (see (4) of Theorem 2.15 ). There is, however, a situation in which this module does exists: whenever there exists a single prime v on L over p, and this prime is totally ramified. It seems to be a tradition to first prove his formula in the totally ramified case, and then to follow through with general \mathbb{Z}_{p}-extensions (see [ 4 , Sec. 3.1.2] ). This pedagogical choice also dates back to Iwasawa’s paper [ 2 ] , which discusses a simplification of the proof under this particular hypothesis at the end. It is not the case that general extensions reduce to the totally ramified case, much to the contrary: the totally ramified case illustrates very clearly the mechanism behind Iwasawa’s formula, and the presence of other primes and residue-field extensions only introduce technicalities one has to work around. As such, we keep on with this tradition. Hence, consider the following hypothesis: (HYP) F has a unique prime above p, which is totally ramified in L/F. Proof of Theorem 1.1 assuming (HYP) . Let v be the unique prime of L over p. In particular, for each n\geq 0, its restriction u_{n} to L_{n} is the unique prime of L_{n} over p as well. The assumption that L/F is totally ramified at p means that for each n the inertia groups I_{v,n}=I(v/u_{n}) of v over L_{n} equals the whole Galois group G^{p^{n}}, and the map \Sigma_{n}\colon I_{v,n}\to G^{p^{n}} of Theorem 2.15 is the identity. Hence, if X=\varprojlim\mathrm{Cl}(L_{n})(p) denotes the inverse limit of the p-primary part of the class groups along the norm maps, by part (4) of Theorem 2.15 we have an isomorphism X/\omega_{n}X\simeq\mathrm{Cl}(L_{n})(p) for each n\geq 0. By Nakayama’s lemma ( [ 6 , Prop. 1.2.5] ), a profinite \Lambda-module M is finitely generated if and only if M/(T,p)M is finite. Since T=\omega_{0}, we see that X/(\omega_{0},p)X\simeq\mathrm{Cl}(F)(p)/p\mathrm{Cl}(F)(p) is finite by part (1) of Theorem 2.15 and thus X is finitely generated. By Proposition 2.14 , we have \dim_{\Lambda}X=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(X/\omega_{n}X)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}}{p^{n}}=0\,, so X is torsion. By Corollary 3.8 , there are integers \mu\geq 0, \lambda\geq 0 and \nu such that for large enough n we have e_{n}=\log_{p}|X/\omega_{n}X|=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu\,.\qed As established in the proof of Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 3.7 , in this case we can express the constants \mu and \lambda in terms of the following “rank-theoretical” invariants of X: \mu=\ell_{\Lambda_{(p)}}(X_{(p)})\,,\quad\lambda=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(X\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p})\,. 5. General \mathbb{Z}_{p}-extensions Keeping with the notation of Section 4 , we now drop the hypothesis on how p splits in F or ramifies in L. By parts (3) and (5) of Theorem 2.15 , there exists an integer m\geq 0 such that all ramified primes in L/L_{m} are totally ramified and the maps X\to\mathrm{Cl}(L_{n})(p) are surjective. Let \mathfrak{p}_{1}\,,\ldots\,,\mathfrak{p}_{s} be the primes in L_{m} which ramify in L/L_{m}. Thus, for any n\geq m, any prime v of L_{n} that ramifies in L/L_{n} lies above a unique \mathfrak{p}_{i}, and there is only one prime of L_{n} above each \mathfrak{p}_{i}. We can therefore label the ramified primes of L/L_{n} by v_{n,1}\,,\ldots\,,v_{n,s}, and in particular their quantity is bounded independently of n. For each v_{n,r}, its inertia subgroup I_{v_{n,r}} equals the whole Galois group G^{p^{n}}, and thus \Sigma_{n}\colon\bigoplus_{r=1}^{s}I_{v_{n,r}}\to G^{p^{n}} is simply the addition map \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{s}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}. In particular, \ker\Sigma_{n}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{s-1} and \operatorname{coker}\Sigma_{n}=0. One could give uniform proof of Theorem 1.1 analogous to the totally ramified, single prime case if one first proves the results of Section 3 in a more general setting, at the expense of working with less explicit rank functions. Concretely, fix an integer m\geq 0 and a distinguished polynomial g dividing \omega_{m}, and for n\geq m put f_{n}=\dfrac{\omega_{n}}{g}. One then replaces the rank functions \operatorname{rk}_{n,k} by those induced from the finite length module \Lambda/(f_{n},p^{k}). Then, there exists an integer n_{0} such that for all n\geq n_{0} we have f_{n+1}L=pf_{n}L. This allows one to establish a version of Corollary 3.8 for the growth of the modules M/f_{n}M of the form \log_{p}|M/f_{n}M|=\mu p^{n}+\lambda n+\nu for large enough n.
