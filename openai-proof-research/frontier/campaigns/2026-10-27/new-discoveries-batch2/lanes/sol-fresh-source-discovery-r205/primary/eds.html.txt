Abstract. In this paper, we give a structural factorization of elliptic divisibility sequences in terms of finitely many modular functions on the modular curve X_{1}(N). More precisely, we express the functions obtained by pulling back division polynomials along the universal torsion section as products of fixed modular factors. Then we show that this factorization is compatible with the natural action of (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times} on the modular curve X_{1}(N) and so descends to the modular curve X_{0}(N) via the norm map. As applications, we obtain a perfect power criterion, local and global growth estimates for the associated elliptic divisibility sequences. Key words and phrases: Elliptic curves, division polynomials, elliptic divisibility sequences. 1991 Mathematics Subject Classification 14H52, 11G05, 11G07, 11B37. Introduction Let E be an elliptic curve defined over a field K given by a Weierstrass equation (1.1) E:y^{2}+a_{1}xy+a_{3}y=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{4}x+a_{6}\text{, } let E(K) denote the group of K-rational points on E, and let \mathcal{O} denote the point at infinity, the identity element of the group E(K). For background on elliptic curves, see [ 16 , 17 , 21 ] . The n- division polynomial \psi_{n} of E is the unique function \psi_{n} \in K(E) with divisor \operatorname{div}(\psi_{n})=[n]^{\ast}(\mathcal{O})-n^{2}(\mathcal{O}). Division polynomials play a central role in the theory of elliptic curves, e.g. they appear in height theory [ 17 ] , in reduction theory [ 16 ] , in algorithms for counting points on elliptic curves over finite fields [ 13 ] , and in the theory of elliptic divisibility sequences (EDSs) [ 24 ] . See also [ 19 ] , [ 1 ] , [ 15 ] . The sequence of values (\psi_{n}(P))_{n\geq 0} of the division polynomials evaluated at a point P satisfies the classical elliptic divisibility recurrence relations, see [ 24 ] . Hence, this gives rise to an elliptic divisibility sequence (W_{n})_{n\geq 0~}associated to the pair (E,P) where W_{n}=\psi_{n}(P). The sequence (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} is called the elliptic divisibility sequence associated to the pair (E,P). Pairs (E,P) with prescribed torsion structure are parametrized by modular curves, providing a geometric way to study families of elliptic curves together with torsion points. This approach leads to a modular interpretation of elliptic divisibility sequences. Furthermore, we use the modular curves Y_{1}(N) and X_{1}(N) to analyze the sequences (W_{n})_{n\geq 0}, where affine curve Y_{1}(N) parametrizes pairs (E,P) with a point P of order N, and X_{1}(N) denotes its smooth projective compactification. The aim of this paper is to interpret elliptic divisibility sequences (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} associated to elliptic curves together with torsion points in terms of modular functions on Y_{1}(N). The terms of an EDS can therefore be viewed as the specialization at (E,P) of modular functions on Y_{1}(N). Thus they are not merely quantities depending on a chosen Weierstrass equation, they arise from universal modular functions on Y_{1}(N). We make this interpretation explicit and show that these functions admit finite modular factorizations with quadratic exponents depending only on n modulo N. Furthermore, if we want these sequences to depend only on the elliptic curve over K and the point P \in E(K), but independent of the chosen model, we are naturally led to a canonical definition of elliptic divisibility sequences, obtained by pulling back the division polynomials \psi_{n} along a section. More precisely, there exists a universal elliptic curve \mathcal{E\rightarrow} Y_{1}(N) with universal section \mathcal{P} : Y_{1}(N)~\mathcal{\rightarrow} \mathcal{E}. Pulling back the n-th division polynomial \psi_{n} along the section \mathcal{P} defines a modular function (1.2) f_{n}=\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\psi_{n})\in K(Y_{1}(N)) where K(Y_{1}(N)) is the function field of the modular curve Y_{1}(N), if n\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{O}, then f_{n} vanishes identically. Now let Y_{1}(N)(K) denote the K-rational points on Y_{1}(N), then for any (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K), evaluating f_{n} at (E,P) recovers the associated elliptic divisibility sequence, i.e., W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P). Elliptic divisibility sequences over function fields have been studied by Ingram et al. [ 8 ] and others [ 2 , 11 , 12 ] . In the present work, we consider the functions f_{n} arising from the universal elliptic curve over Y_{1}(N) together with its universal section. This allows elliptic divisibility sequences to be interpreted in terms of modular functions and the geometry of modular curves. In [ 6 ] , elliptic divisibility sequences associated to elliptic curves over \mathbb{Q} were studied for specific torsion structures using explicit models of elliptic curves. By viewing these sequences as specializations of the functions f_{n} at points of Y_{1}(N), we extend these results to elliptic curves over arbitrary number fields and to arbitrary levels N. We further carry out these results on the modular curves X_{0}(N), which parametrize elliptic curves together with cyclic subgroups C of order N. Thus, we obtain a geometric interpretation of elliptic divisibility sequences both in terms of points and cyclic isogenies. Our first main result gives a structural factorization of EDSs in terms of finitely many modular functions on X_{1}(N). Theorem 1.1 . Let N\geq 2, let Y_{1}(N) be the affine modular curve parametrizing elliptic curves together with a point of order N, and let X_{1}(N) be its smooth compactification with the function field K(X_{1}(N)) over a number field K. Suppose that (E,P) correspond to a K-rational point of Y_{1}(N), and let (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} be the elliptic divisibility sequence associated to (E,P) via division polynomials \psi_{n}. Then there exist finitely many modular functions g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times} depending only on N and on the chosen model of X_{1}(N), such that (1.3) W_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}(E,P)\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ } for all n\geq 1 such that N\nmid n, here each \alpha_{i}(n) is a polynomial in n of degree at most 2 whose values depend only on the residue class of n modulo N, and each function g_{i} has a divisor supported on the cusps and a finite set of points determined by the chosen model of X_{1}(N). The proof relies on the existence of a universal elliptic curve \mathcal{E} \mathcal{\rightarrow} Y_{1}(N) with a universal torsion section \mathcal{P} of exact order N. Pulling back the division polynomials \psi_{n} along the section \mathcal{P} we obtain functions on Y_{1}(N) and extend these functions to rational functions on X_{1}(N). Then we use divisor theory and evaluate these functions at (E,P) to express W_{n} in terms of these modular functions. We can extend Theorem 1.1 to the modular curves X_{0}(N), which parametrize elliptic curves together with cyclic subgroups C of order N. Since X_{0}(N) parametrizes cyclic subgroups without choice of generator, we choose a generator P \in C to lift (E,C) to a point of X_{1}(N); see [ 3 , Section 1.5] . This lifting is well-defined up to the natural action of (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}. Finally, by taking suitable products over all generators of C, we obtain canonical functions on X_{0}(N). Then we show that the factorization structure descends to this setting. The following theorem shows that the evaluation of the modular function f_{n} in ( 1.2 ) at a lifted point gives an elliptic divisibility sequence and that the factorization structure extends to this case. Theorem 1.2 . With notation and assumptions as in Theorem 1.1, let f_{n} be as in ( 1.2 ). Let (E,C) be a point of Y_{0}(N)(K), where C is a cyclic subgroup of order N, and choose a generator P\in C, yielding a lift (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K). Then the sequence W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P) is an elliptic divisibility sequence. Moreover, this sequence satisfies the factorization in ( 1.3 ). Note that choosing a generator P\in C lifts (E,C) to a point of Y_{1}(N). This is compatible with the natural action of (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times} and so descends to X_{0}(N) via the norm map. The next theorem describes this descent in terms of modular functions. Theorem 1.3 . With notation and assumptions as in Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, define F_{n}=\mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))}(f_{n})\text{,} where \mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))} denotes norm for K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N)). Then the function F_{n} in K(X_{0}(N))^{\times} and for any (E,C)\in Y_{0}(N)(K) and any generator P\in C, we have F_{n}(E,C)=\prod_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}f_{n}(E,kP). Furthermore, the functions F_{n} satisfy the following factorization F_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}G_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}, where G_{i}=\mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))}(g_{i})\in K(X_{0}(N))^{\times}. 2. Division Polynomials and Elliptic Divisibility Sequences Recall that for an elliptic curve E over a number field K given by a Weierstrass equation ( 1.1 ) and a point P=(x,y)\in E(K), the division polynomials are defined using the initial values (2.1) \displaystyle\psi_{0}(P) \displaystyle=0\text{,} \displaystyle\psi_{1}(P) \displaystyle=1\text{,} \displaystyle\psi_{2}(P) \displaystyle=2y+a_{1}x+a_{3}\text{,} \displaystyle\psi_{3}(P) \displaystyle=3x^{4}+b_{2}x^{3}+3b_{4}x^{2}+3b_{6}x+b_{8}\text{,} \displaystyle\psi_{4}(P) \displaystyle=\psi_{2}(P)(2x^{6}+b_{2}x^{5}+5b_{4}x^{4}+10b_{6}x^{3}+10b_{8}x^{2} \displaystyle\ \ +(b_{2}b_{8}-b_{4}b_{6})x+(b_{4}b_{8}-b_{6}^{2}))\text{,} together with the recursive relations \displaystyle\psi_{2n+1}(P) \displaystyle=\psi_{n+2}(P)\psi_{n}(P)^{3}-\psi_{n-1}(P)\psi_{n+1}(P)^{3}\text{, \ \ for }n\geq 2\text{,} (2.2) \displaystyle\psi_{2n}(P) \displaystyle=(\psi_{n-1}(P)^{2}\psi(P)\psi_{n+2}(P) \displaystyle\ \ -\psi_{n-2}(P)\psi_{n}(P)\psi_{n+1}(P)^{3})(\psi_{2}(P))^{-1}\text{, \ \ for }n\geq 3\text{.} where b_{i}’s are usual quantities in [ 17 , Chapter III] . In particular the sequence (\psi_{n}(P)) satisfies the recurrence relation ( 2.3 ). An elliptic divisibility sequence (EDS) is a sequence (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} satisfying a nonlinear recurrence (2.3) W_{m+n}W_{m-n}=W_{m+1}W_{m-1}W_{n~}^{2}-W_{n+1}W_{n-1}W_{m~}^{2} and the divisibility property W_{n}|W_{m}\text{ whenever }n|m for all m\geq n\geq 1. See; [ 24 ] , [ 25 ] , [ 4 ] , [ 14 ] , and [ 23 ] for background on EDSs. An elliptic divisibility sequence is called proper if W_{0}=0, W_{1}=1, and W_{2}W_{3}\neq 0. A proper elliptic divisibility sequence is called nonsingular if its discriminant is non zero; see [ 19 ] , [ 20 ] or [ 24 ] for the explicit formula of the discriminant of an EDS. The arithmetic properties of these sequences were first studied by Ward [ 24 , 25 ] . Ward [ 24 , Theorem 12.1] showed that nonsingular elliptic divisibility sequences arise as values of the division polynomials of an elliptic curve. More precisely, he proved that if (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} is a nonsingular elliptic divisibility sequence, then there exist a lattice L\subset\mathbb{C} and a complex number z \in\mathbb{C} such that W_{n}=\psi_{n}(z\text{, }L)=\frac{\sigma(nz\text{, }L)}{\sigma(z\text{, }L)^{n^{2}}}\text{~\ \ for all }n\geq 1\text{,} where \psi_{n}(z, L) and \sigma(z, L) are the n-division polynomial and the Weierstrass \sigma-function associated to the lattice L, respectively. Subsequently, Silverman [ 19 , Proposition 18] reformulated Ward’s result in an arithmetic setting over \mathbb{Q}, relating nonsingular elliptic divisibility sequences to division polynomials evaluated at rational points on elliptic curves. In the present paper, we work over an arbitrary number field K. Thus for an elliptic curve E over K and a point P\in E(K), we use the normalization (2.4) W_{n}=\psi_{n}(P)\text{ \ \ \ for all }n\geq 1 where \psi_{n} is the n-division polynomial on E. Furthermore, the sequence (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} is the elliptic divisibility sequence associated to the pair (E,P). We now relate these arithmetic properties to the modular framework used throughout the paper. Let \mathcal{E\rightarrow} Y_{1}(N) be the universal elliptic curve with universal section \mathcal{P} : Y_{1}(N)~\mathcal{\rightarrow} \mathcal{E}. Pulling back the n-th division polynomial \psi_{n} along \mathcal{P} defines a modular function (2.5) f_{n}=\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\psi_{n})\in K(Y_{1}(N))^{\times} where K(Y_{1}(N)) is the function field of the modular curve Y_{1}(N), if n\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{O}, then f_{n} vanishes identically. For a K-rational point (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K), evaluating f_{n} at (E,P) gives f_{n}(E,P)=\psi_{n}(P)=W_{n}. Thus by evaluating f_{n} at (E,P), we recover the associated elliptic divisibility sequence. 3. Factorization and Applications In this section, we show the factorization of the functions f_{n}=\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\psi_{n}) and obtain a perfect power criterion. Then we discuss the growth of the sequence (W_{n})_{n\geq 0}=(f_{n}(E,P))_{n\geq 0}. We illustrate the results with an explicit example in the case N=13. Further examples for other levels, derived from the models presented in [ 22 , pp. 1144-1145] , which provide relatively simple factorization formulas for elliptic divisibility sequences, are given in the Appendix. 3.1. Proof of Theorem 1.1 Proof of Theorem 1.1. Let \mathcal{E}\rightarrow Y_{1}(N) denote the universal elliptic curve with universal torsion section \mathcal{P} of exact order N. For each integer n\geq 1, we consider the n-th division polynomial \psi_{n} as a rational function on \mathcal{E} with divisor \operatorname{div}(\psi_{n})=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{E}[n]}(\mathcal{T})-n^{2}(\mathcal{O})\text{.} Pulling back \psi_{n} via the universal section \mathcal{P}, we obtain a rational function f_{n}=\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\psi_{n})\in K(Y_{1}(N))^{\times} whose divisor is given by \operatorname{div}(f_{n})=\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\psi_{n}))=\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\operatorname{div}(\psi_{n}))=\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{E}[n]}\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\mathcal{T})-n^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\mathcal{O})\text{.} Since \mathcal{P} has exact order N, an equality \mathcal{P} = \mathcal{T} for some \mathcal{T}\in\mathcal{E}[n] is possible if and only if N~|~n. If N~|~n, then f_{n} vanishes identically and its divisor is undefined. Thus we assume that N\nmid n. In this case, \mathcal{P} never intersects the n-torsion subgroup over Y_{1}(N), thus f_{n} has no zeros or poles on this curve. Consequently, f_{n} is a modular unit, and the support of \operatorname{div}(f_{n}) is contained in the finite set of cusps of X_{1}(N). Moreover, the multiplicities in \operatorname{div}(\psi_{n}) depend quadratically on n. Pulling back along the section \mathcal{P} preserves this growth. Therefore, the coefficients arising in the divisor decomposition of f_{n} at the cusps are polynomials in n of degree at most 2. For certain residue classes modulo N, ramification at the cusps may introduce cancellations that eliminate the quadratic term, reducing these coefficients to linear or constant polynomials. Now passing to the smooth compactification X_{1}(N), the divisors appearing above are supported on a finite set of X_{1}(N) consisting of cusps and finitely many non-cuspidal points arising from the chosen model. The group of divisors supported on this finite set is finitely generated. Now we choose divisors D_{1},\dots,D_{r} generating the subgroup of principal divisors \operatorname{div}(f_{n}). Then there exist polynomials \alpha_{i}(n) in n of degree at most 2 taking integer values on suitable residue classes n modulo N, such that \operatorname{div}(f_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}(n)D_{i}. Since each D_{i} is principal, we can choose functions g_{i}\in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times} with \operatorname{div}(g_{i})=D_{i}, where the divisors of these g_{i} are supported on the cusps along with a finite set of non-cuspidal points arising from the coordinate singularities of the chosen model. Now define F_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}\text{.} Thus \operatorname{div}(F_{n})=\operatorname{div}(f_{n}), which implies \operatorname{div}(F_{n}/f_{n})=0. This means that F_{n}/f_{n} =c_{n} for some c_{n}\in K^{\times}, yielding F_{n}=c_{n}f_{n}. By identifying c_{n}f_{n} with f_{n}, we finally obtain F_{n}=f_{n}. Then evaluating each f_{n} at a K-rational point (E,P) of Y_{1}(N), we have W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}(E,P). ∎ Remark 3.1 . Perfect Power Structure Theorem 1.1 gives a criterion for when the modular function f_{n} is an m-th power in K(X_{1}(N)). After evaluating at a point (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K) yields perfect power terms in the associated elliptic divisibility sequence. Theorem 3.2 . With notation and assumptions as in Theorem 1.1, let f_{n}\in K(X_{1}(N)) be as in ( 1.2 ). Suppose that \operatorname{div}(f_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}(n)\,D_{i}, where each D_{i} is a principal divisor on X_{1}(N), and D_{i}=\operatorname{div}(g_{i}) for g_{i}\in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times}. If for some integer m\geq 2 one has \alpha_{i}(n)\in m\mathbb{Z} for all i=1,...,r, then there exist h_{n}\in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times} and c_{n}\in K^{\times} such that f_{n}=c_{n}h_{n}^{m}. In particular, if c_{n}\in K^{m}, then f_{n} is an m-th power in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times}. Proof. By the proof of Theorem 1.1, the function f_{n} can be written as a product of modular functions g_{i} with polynomials \alpha_{i}(n) in n of degree at most 2 for all i=1,...,r. By assumption we can write \alpha_{i}(n)=m\beta_{i}(n) for some integers \beta_{i}(n). Therefore \operatorname{div}(f_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}m\beta_{i}(n)\operatorname{div}(g_{i}). and so \operatorname{div}(f_{n})=m\operatorname{div}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\beta_{i}(n)}\right). Now set h_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\beta_{i}(n)}. Then \operatorname{div}(f_{n})=\operatorname{div}(h_{n}^{m}) and therefore \operatorname{div}\left(f_{n}/h_{n}^{m}\right)=0. Since X_{1}(N) is complete, every rational function on X_{1}(N) with trivial divisor is constant. Hence f_{n}/h_{n}^{m}=c_{n} for some c_{n}\in K^{\times}. Therefore f_{n}=c_{n}h_{n}^{m}. If c_{n}\in K^{m}, say c_{n}=d^{m} for some d\in K^{\times}, then f_{n}=(dh_{n})^{m}. Hence f_{n} is an m-th power in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times}. ∎ As an immediate result of Theorem 3.2 we have the following corollary. Corollary 3.3 . With notation and assumptions as in Theorems 1.1 and 3.2. For every point (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K) one has W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P)=c_{n}h_{n}^{m}(E,P). Thus, if c_{n}\in K^{m}, then W_{n} is an m-th power in K. Proof. Evaluating at a point (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K) where all functions are defined gives W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P)=c_{n}h_{n}^{m}(E,P). If c_{n}=d^{m}, then W_{n}=(dh_{n}(E,P))^{m}. Thus W_{n} is an m-th power in K. ∎ 3.3. Local and Global Growth Estimates The growth of elliptic divisibility sequences over number fields is classically related to height theory on elliptic curves. In the nontorsion case, logarithms of the values of division polynomials at a point are closely related to local and canonical heights; see, for example [ 5 ] and [ 18 ] . Since we work with torsion points, these nontorsion height estimates do not apply. Instead, the estimates below follow from Theorem 1.1. The following theorem gives a local growth estimate for the sequence (W_{n})_{n\geq 0}. Theorem 3.4 . With notation and assumptions as in Theorems 1.1, let (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K), let (W_{n})_{n\geq 0} be the elliptic divisibility sequence over the number field K associated to (E,P) via division polynomials \psi_{n}, and let \upsilon be a place of K. Then there exists a constant C_{{}_{v}}(E,P)>0 such that \log|W_{n}|_{\upsilon}\leq C_{\upsilon}(E,P)n^{2} for all n such that N\nmid n. Proof. By Theorem 1.1, we know that the modular factors g_{i} are defined and nonzero at (E,P), or equivalently, g_{i}(E,P) \in K^{\times} for all i and that W_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}(E,P) for all n such that N\nmid n. Then |W_{n}|_{\upsilon}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}|g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}(E,P)|_{\upsilon}. Since each g_{i}(E,P) \in K^{\times} for all i, taking logarithms we obtain \log|W_{n}|_{\upsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}(n)\log|g_{i}(E,P)|_{\upsilon}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\alpha_{i}(n)||\log|g_{i}(E,P)|_{\upsilon}|\text{.} Since each function \alpha_{i}(n) has degree at most 2 on residue classes modulo N, there exists a constant C_{i}>0 such that |\alpha_{i}(n)|\leq C_{i}n^{2} for all n such that N\nmid n. Therefore \log|W_{n}|_{\upsilon}\leq n^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}C_{i}\,|\log|g_{i}(E,P)|_{\upsilon}\,|\leq C_{\upsilon}(E,P)n^{2} for some constant C_{\upsilon}(E,P)=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}\{C_{i}|\log|g_{i}(E,P)|_{\upsilon}\,|\}>0. ∎ As another consequence of Theorem 1.1, we obtain a global norm estimate for the specialized EDS terms. Corollary 3.5 . With notation and assumptions of the above theorem, define E_{n}=\left|\mathrm{Nm}_{K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}}(W_{n})\right| for N\nmid n, where \mathrm{Nm}_{K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}} denotes norm for K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}. Then there exists a constant C(E,P)>0 such that \log E_{n}\leq C(E,P)n^{2} for all n such that N\nmid n. Proof. By the proof of Theorem 1.1, for N\nmid n we have f_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)} on each residue class modulo N. Evaluating at (E,P) gives W_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}(E,P). Taking the absolute norm for K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}, we obtain E_{n}=\left|\mathrm{Nm}_{K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}}(W_{n})\right|=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left|\mathrm{Nm}_{K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}}(g_{i}(E,P))\right|^{\alpha_{i}(n)}. Therefore \log E_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}(n)\log\left|\mathrm{Nm}_{K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}}(g_{i}(E,P))\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\alpha_{i}(n)\,|\,|\log\left|\mathrm{Nm}_{K/\mathbf{\mathbb{Q}}}(g_{i}(E,P))\right|\,|. Since each function \alpha_{i}(n) has degree at most 2 on residue classes modulo N, there exists a constant C_{i}>0 such that |\alpha_{i}(n)|\leq C_{i}n^{2} for all n such that N\nmid n. Since the functions \alpha_{i}(n) have degree at most 2 on residue classes modulo N, there is a constant C(E,P)>0 such that \log E_{n}\leq C(E,P)n^{2}. ∎ 4. Lifting and Norm Descent Let (E,C) be a point of Y_{0}(N)(K), where C is a cyclic subgroup of order N and choose a generator P\in C, yielding a lift (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K) and let f_{n} be as in ( 1.2 ). In this section, we establish that evaluating the modular function f_{n} at this lifted point, i.e., W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P) defines an EDS. Furthermore we demonstrade that the resulting factorization structure descents to X_{0}(N) via the norm map. Proof of Theorem 1.2. Let (E,C)\in Y_{0}(N)(K) be given. Since C is a cyclic subgroup of order N, any generator P\in C determines a point (E,P)\in Y_{1}(N)(K), which is a lift of (E,C) along the morphism \phi:Y_{1}(N)\rightarrow Y_{0}(N)\text{, }(E,P)\mapsto(E,\langle P\rangle) obtained by forgetting the generator, but keeping the group it generates; see [ 3 , p.40] . By ( 1.2 ), the functions f_{n}=\mathcal{P}^{\ast}(\psi_{n}) are modular functions on Y_{1}(N). Now evaluating at the lifted point (E,P) gives W_{n}=f_{n}(E,P)=\psi_{n}(P), which is the elliptic divisibility sequence associated to (E,P). By the proof of Theorem 1.1, the f_{n} can be factored as f_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}\text{,} and evaluating this at (E,P) gives W_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}(E,P)\text{,} where g_{i}\in K(X_{1}(N))^{\times} and each \alpha_{i}(n) is a polynomial in n of degree at most 2 taking integer values on suitable residue classes n modulo N. Now let kP be another generator of C, with k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}. Then (E,kP) is the image of (E,P) under the natural action of (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times} on Y_{1}(N). Since this action permutes the factors in W_{n}, the factorization structure is preserved in the sense that the factors are transformed by the induced action while the exponent pattern remains unchanged. ∎ We are now ready to prove Theorem 1.3. Proof of theorem 1.3. By the proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, the functions f_{n} are defined on X_{1}(N) and their evaluation at (E,P) is compatible with the natural action of (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}, respectively. Since the morphism \phi:X_{1}(N)\rightarrow X_{0}(N) is a finite covering, the norm map \mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))}:K(X_{1}(N))^{\times}\rightarrow K(X_{0}(N))^{\times} is well-defined, thus F_{n}=\mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))}(f_{n})\in K(X_{0}(N))^{\times}. On the other hand, the function field extension K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N)) is a Galois extension with Galois group (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}, where the group acting on sections via \mathcal{P}\mapsto k\mathcal{P}, k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}; see [ 3 , Section 7.5.] . Hence, the function F_{n} is defined as the norm of the f_{n} over the group (\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}, which is given by the product of pull-backs along the sections k\mathcal{P} as F_{n}=\prod_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}(k\mathcal{P)}^{\ast}(\psi_{n})\text{,} (see; [ 9 , Section 1.8] , for more details). Evaluating at (E,C) yields F_{n}(E,C)=\prod_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}f_{n}(E,kP). The independence of this factorization from the choice of generator P follows from the proof of Theorem 1.2. Finally, applying the norm to the factorization given in the proof of Theorem 1.1, f_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)} and using multiplicativity, we obtain F_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))}(g_{i}))^{\alpha_{i}(n)}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}G_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(n)}\text{,} where G_{i}=\mathrm{Nm}_{K(X_{1}(N))/K(X_{0}(N))}(g_{i}). ∎ 5.
