## Clause-by-clause re-derivation

### Clause 1a. Free-basis change, q, finite presentation

* **Basis change.** Define χ: A→F(a′,u′₁..u′₉₉) by a↦a′, bᵢ↦a′⁻¹u′ᵢ₋₁⁻¹u′ᵢ (u′₀=1) and ψ in the reverse direction by a′↦a, u′ᵢ↦uᵢ. Then ψχ(bᵢ)=a⁻¹uᵢ₋₁⁻¹uᵢ=bᵢ, because uᵢ=uᵢ₋₁abᵢ. By induction, χψ(u′ᵢ)=χ(uᵢ₋₁)a′χ(bᵢ)=u′ᵢ₋₁a′a′⁻¹u′ᵢ₋₁⁻¹u′ᵢ=u′ᵢ. So {a,u₁,…,u₉₉} is a free basis and q_A exists with the prescribed values.
* **Images.** q(b₁)=q(a)⁻¹q(u₁)=d⁻¹c; q(b₂)=d⁻¹c⁻¹·1=d⁻¹c⁻¹; q(bᵢ)=d⁻¹ for 3≤i≤99; q(w)=q(u₉₉)q(a)=d. These are exactly the scope's values.
* **Amalgam map.** q_B(j,tⁿ)=q_A(j) is a homomorphism of J×⟨t⟩ (it is q_A|J composed with the projection), and it agrees with q_A on J. The universal property gives q, and q(t)=1. The image contains d=q(a) and c=q(u₁), so q is onto F.
* **Finite presentation.** Homomorphisms out of A *_J (J×⟨t⟩) correspond to pairs (φ_A, φ_B) agreeing on J. A homomorphism out of J×⟨t⟩ is a homomorphism of J plus an element commuting with its image. Commuting with all of J is equivalent to commuting with the 100 generators. So presentation (2) has the same universal property. I checked this independently of the source; it needs no fact about J beyond being generated by the listed words.
* **Infinite order of t, countability.** The factor J×⟨t⟩ embeds in the amalgam (general amalgam fact; also source Lemma `actions:normal-form`, read). So t has infinite order and Γ is countably infinite.

### Clause 1b. Conventions, Borel structure, freeness, measure preservation

* (xg)(h)=x(gh) is a right action: ((xg₁)g₂)(h)=(xg₁)(g₂h)=x(g₁g₂h). It equals x·g=g⁻¹·x for the usual left shift, so its orbit relation is that of the left-shift Bernoulli action. Same for Z with F, and (x,z)g=(xg,zq(g)) is a right action because q is a homomorphism. Coordinate permutations preserve product measures, so both actions are p.m.p.
* Freeness on X: for g≠1, xg=x forces x(g)=x(1), a null event because g≠1. Countably many g give an invariant conull Borel set of free points (stabilizers are conjugate along orbits). Freeness of Y follows from the first coordinate.
* Source259 uses the same space [0,1]^Γ, product Lebesgue measure and the same right convention (`introduction.tex`, `group-actions.tex`), and its X is the conull free part. Conull invariant restriction does not change cost. So the source lower bound applies verbatim to the candidate's X.

### Clause 2. Cost exactly 1 for the full orbit relation on Y

* **t-marker.** C_p=(C⁰_p∪N_p)×Z with N_p={x : x(tⁿ)≥p ∀n∈ℤ}. N_p is Borel and t-invariant because (xtᵏ)(tⁿ)=x(tᵏ⁺ⁿ). It is null because the tⁿ are distinct (infinite order) and the first N constraints have probability (1−p)ᴺ. Every t-orbit meets C_p: either some x(tⁿ)<p, giving ytⁿ∈C⁰_p×Z (q(t)=1 leaves z unchanged), or the whole orbit lies in N_p×Z⊂C_p. Coverage is pointwise, including the null exceptional set.
* **B-generation everywhere.** For j∈{b₁,…,b₉₉,w} and any y, pick n with ytⁿ∈C_p. Then y→ytⁿ→ytⁿj→ytⁿjt⁻ⁿ=yj, by tj=jt. Inverses of j-edges come from symmetry of the generated relation. So Φ_p (full t plus 100 restrictions to C_p) generates the B-orbit relation at every point. Its cost is 1+100p.
* **c-marker, one-sided.** z′∈E⁰_ρcⁿ iff z′c⁻ⁿ∈E⁰_ρ iff (z′c⁻ⁿ)(1)=z′(c⁻ⁿ)<ρ. The coordinates c⁻ⁿ (n≥0) are distinct, so the set of z with z(c⁻ⁿ)≥ρ for all n≥0 is null. Adding it to E⁰_ρ gives a Borel set E_ρ of measure ρ with Z=⋃_{n≥0}E_ρcⁿ **pointwise**. Only n≥0 is used, which matches the induction direction below.
* **Availability and the interior exceptional edge.** "D available" quantifies over **every** x∈X and every z∈D. Base: E_ρ is available through the direct a-edges on X×E_ρ. Step D⇒Dc: let v=(x,z), z∈Dc, and y=vu₁⁻¹=(xu₁⁻¹, zc⁻¹), so zc⁻¹∈D. The a-letters of w=ab₁ab₂⋯ab₉₉a sit at prefixes u₀,…,u₉₉. Source vertex yuᵢ has quotient coordinate zc⁻¹q(uᵢ), which is zc⁻¹∈D for i≠1 and z for i=1, where yu₁=v. Hence:
  * prefix: y →(a, at y, available) ya →(b₁) yu₁=v;
  * w-edge: y → yw (B-edge);
  * reverse suffix: yw=yu₉₉a ← yu₉₉ (a-edge at yu₉₉, available), yu₉₉=(yu₉₈a)b₉₉ ← yu₉₈a (b₉₉-edge), …, down to yu₂=(yu₁a)b₂ ← yu₁a (b₂-edge). Every reversed a-step starts at some yuᵢ with i≥2.
  
  So v R y R yw R yu₁a=va. The first coordinate xu₁⁻¹ of y is arbitrary, which is exactly why the all-first-coordinate quantifier is needed and is satisfied. Induction gives availability of E_ρcⁿ for all n≥0. A union of available sets is available, so (3) gives every a-edge at every point. I also checked that the recovery word u₁⁻¹·w·(b₂a⋯b₉₉a)⁻¹ reduces to a, and that exactly one row source (i=1) is exceptional (`word-checks.py`; finite identities only).
* **Full relation.** R contains all edges for a,b₁,…,b₉₉,w,t, which generate Γ, and every map of Ψ is a restriction of a group element. So R equals the full Γ-orbit relation on all of Y, not a sub-relation or a finite-level approximant. R is Borel as a countable union of graphs of finite words.
* **ε bound.** p=min(1/2,ε/400), ρ=min(1/2,ε/4) lie in (0,1), and 100p+ρ≤ε/4+ε/4<ε for every ε>0. Ψ_{p,ρ} is a finite, hence countable, measure-preserving Borel graphing of the full relation with cost <1+ε. So Cost(Y)≤1.

### Clause 3. Spectral gap inequality and strong ergodicity

* **H free and q|H an isomorphism.** Let σ: F(s₁,s₂)→H, s₁↦w, s₂↦b₁ (onto by definition). Then q∘σ sends s₁↦d, s₂↦d⁻¹c, which is a Nielsen basis change of F(c,d) and so an isomorphism. Hence σ is injective, H is free on {w,b₁}, and q|H=(qσ)σ⁻¹ is an isomorphism H→F. The inverse is d↦w, c↦wb₁. This does not depend on the source.
* **Bernoulli identification of X|H.** Γ is the disjoint union of right cosets Hr. The map x↦(h↦(x(hr))_r) carries μ to the product measure on K_X^H with K_X=[0,1]^{H\Γ}, and for g∈H, (xg)(hr)=x(ghr). This is the right H-Bernoulli shift with the same convention. The base is a standard atomless probability space.
* **Z|H and Y|H.** z↦(h↦z(q(h))) is a measure isomorphism Z→[0,1]^H because q|H is a bijection, and (zq(g))(q(h))=z(q(gh)). So Y|H is the H-Bernoulli shift with base K=K_X×[0,1]. This is a statement about the restriction to H, which is all that is used.
* **Tensor basis and regular copies.** With e₀=1 and an orthonormal basis of the separable space L²(K), finitely supported labels ℓ give an orthonormal basis of L²(K^H). U_g f=f(·g) satisfies U_{g₁g₂}=U_{g₁}U_{g₂} and U_g e_ℓ=e_{ℓ(g⁻¹·)}. That is a left translation of labels, and U is a unitary representation. A nonempty finite support S fixed by g gives gⁿs=gᵐs for some n≠m, so g has finite order, and g=1 in the torsion-free H. So each orbit of nonzero labels spans a copy of the left regular representation, and L²₀ is their Hilbert direct sum.
* **Tree estimate.** On ℓ²(H), T=Σ_{s∈{w^{±1},b₁^{±1}}}λ(s) has (Tξ)(h)=Σ_sξ(s⁻¹h), the adjacency of the 4-regular Cayley tree. It has no loops or multi-edges because the four elements are distinct and nontrivial in the free group. Orienting toward a fixed end makes each edge a unique (parent,child) pair, with 3 children and 1 parent per vertex. Then |⟨Tξ,ξ⟩|≤Σ2|ξ(v)||ξ(u)|≤Σ(|ξ(v)|²/√3+√3|ξ(u)|²)=(3/√3+√3)‖ξ‖²=2√3‖ξ‖² for finitely supported ξ, and by density and boundedness for all ξ. Invariant orthogonal summands give the same bound on L²₀(Y).
* **Energy identity.** ‖U_gf−f‖²=2‖f‖²−2Re⟨U_gf,f⟩, and ⟨Tf,f⟩=2Re⟨U_wf,f⟩+2Re⟨U_{b₁}f,f⟩ is real. So the left side of (1) equals 4‖f‖²−⟨Tf,f⟩≥(4−2√3)‖f‖² for every complex mean-zero f. The constant 4−2√3>0 is exactly the scope's constant. The identical argument applies to X|H.
* **Spectral gap and strong ergodicity.** (1) gives spectral gap for Γ with respect to any finite generating set, since w and b₁ are fixed words in it. For asymptotically invariant A_n, f_n=1_{A_n}−µ(A_n) has ‖f_n(·g)−f_n‖²=µ(A_ng⁻¹ΔA_n)→0 for g=w,b₁, so µ(A_n)(1−µ(A_n))→0. Hence Y and X are strongly ergodic.

### Clause 1c and 4. Weak mixing; Bernoulli high cost

* An H-Bernoulli shift with infinite H is mixing. Its diagonal square is the H-Bernoulli shift with base K×K, hence H-ergodic and so Γ-ergodic. Ergodicity of the diagonal self-product is weak mixing. This gives weak mixing of the full Γ-actions on Y and X. There is no Γ-mixing claim for Y, and none is needed. Indeed t∈ker q fixes the Z-coordinate, so Y is not Γ-mixing, consistent with the scope's exclusion.
* Clause 4's high cost: source `intro:main`, read in its actual body, states Cost(R_{Γ↷X})≥1+η for K=e⁹⁶96⁹⁹/95⁹⁵, 0<α<1/200, Kα³<1/2, η=α/100. This is word-for-word the scope's admissibility, for the same group, the same space and the same convention. `conclusion.tex` confirms the chain. Admissible α exist (Kα³→0). Since 1<1+η, X and Y are two essentially free, weakly mixing, spectral-gap p.m.p. actions of the same finitely presented group with different costs.

### Distinctions requested

* **Full relation vs. approximants:** Ψ generates the entire Γ-orbit relation at every point of Y. No finite-height or partial relation is used.
* **Factor vs. extension:** Y is an extension of the Bernoulli X, and Z is a Γ-factor of Y. The spectral argument uses only the H-restriction, where Y itself is Bernoulli. It does not use any claim that Y is a Γ-Bernoulli factor or a factor of i.i.d.
* **Weak mixing vs. mixing:** only weak mixing is claimed and proved for Y.
