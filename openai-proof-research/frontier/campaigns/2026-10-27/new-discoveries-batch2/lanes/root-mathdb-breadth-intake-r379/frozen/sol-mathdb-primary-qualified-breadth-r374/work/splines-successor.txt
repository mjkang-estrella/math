 The Dimension of Trivariate C 1 Splines on Generic Bipyramid Cells
                                                  Alper Ferudun∗

                                               September 30, 2026



                                                      Abstract
            A bipyramid cell is a tetrahedral partition with one interior vertex v0 , n boundary vertices that
        are coplanar with v0 and surround it, and two further boundary vertices on opposite sides of this
        base plane. Colvin, DiMatteo and Sorokina determined the dimension of the space Sd1 (∆) of C 1
        splines of degree at most d on such a cell ∆ in their collinear and coplanar cases. In the generic
        case they gave lower and upper bounds and conjectured that the dimension equals the upper
        bound whenever the two bounds diﬀer. We prove an exact formula for every generic bipyramid
        cell. Let m be the number of distinct lines spanned by the n interior
                                                                          ( )     edges in the base plane. Then
        dim Sd1 (∆) = 1, 4, 11 for d = 0, 1, 2, and for d ≥ 3 it equals 2n d3 + 6d − 1 + (4 − m)+ if m ≥ 3, and
         ( )
        8 d3 + 8d − 3 − (4 − d)+ if m = 2. For d ≥ 3 these are exactly the upper bounds as printed in the
        Oberwolfach abstract of Colvin, DiMatteo and Sorokina, and for d = 2 the upper bound is attained
        unless m = 3. So the conjecture holds in every case except m = 3, d = 2, where the dimension is
        11 and the printed upper bound is 12; the smallest example has a triangular base. We could not
        access the journal version of their paper; if its bounds for d ≥ 3 are the printed ones and it states
        this one case diﬀerently, the conjecture holds as stated there, and in any case the exact formula
        answers the question. An elementary cofactor argument shows directly that dim S21 (∆) = 11 for
        every generic cell. The spline space splits into homogeneous components; the dimensions of those
        of degree at least 5 also follow from a general formula of Alfeld, Neamtu and Schumaker, so the
        new part of the formula is the determination of the components of degrees 3 and 4. The proof
        reduces the problem to bivariate splines on the planar fan in the base plane: each half of the cell
        projects onto this fan, and gluing the two halves forces the trace on the base plane to be C 2 across
        every ray. The same reduction gives a uniform proof of their formulas in the collinear and coplanar
        cases. The formula agrees with Macaulay2 values of DiPasquale and Villamizar for n = m = 5 and
        with exact rational computations. This is an unrefereed note.


1       Introduction
For a tetrahedral partition ∆ of a region in R3 and integers r, d ≥ 0, let Sdr (∆) be the space of C r
functions on |∆| whose restriction to every tetrahedron is a polynomial of degree at most d. Computing
dim Sdr (∆) is a basic problem of multivariate spline theory. For trivariate splines it is diﬃcult already
for the star of a single interior vertex, a cell, in low degree; see [ANS96, Sha14, MV14, DV21].
    Colvin, DiMatteo and Sorokina [CDS16, Sor15] studied bipyramid cells. Following [Sor15], such a
cell ∆ has

    • exactly one interior vertex v0 ;

    • boundary vertices v1 , . . . , vn that are coplanar with v0 and form a polygon surrounding v0 in
      the base plane B;

   • the 2n tetrahedra Ti+ = [v0 , vi , vi+1 , vn+1 ] and Ti− = [v0 , vi , vi+1 , vn+2 ], 1 ≤ i ≤ n, with indices
     of v1 , . . . , vn taken modulo n.

The triangles [v0 , vi , vi+1 ] triangulate the polygon, so the angle at v0 from vi to vi+1 lies in (0, π). Let
m be the number of distinct lines through v0 that contain one of the edges [v0 , vi ], 1 ≤ i ≤ n (the
number of “slopes”). Then n ≥ 3 and 2 ≤ dn/2e ≤ m ≤ n [Sor15]. In particular m = 2 forces n = 4:
for n = 3 any two of the edges are consecutive, so no two of them are opposite and m = 3. Similarly,
m = 3 forces 3 ≤ n ≤ 6. When vn+1 , vn+2 , v0 are not collinear, let P be the plane through them.
Colvin, DiMatteo and Sorokina (CDS) distinguish four cases [Sor15]:

   • generic: vn+1 , vn+2 , v0 are not collinear, and P contains none of v1 , . . . , vn ;

   • coplanar I (II ): they are not collinear, and P contains exactly one (two) of v1 , . . . , vn ;

   • collinear: vn+1 , vn+2 , v0 are collinear.

Method and relation to earlier work. Put v0 = 0. Every ∪           spline on a cell is a sum of homogeneous
splines, a standard fact [ANS96], [DV21, (3)]. The upper half i Ti+ of the cell is an “orange” around
the edge [v0 , vn+1 ], and by the projection formula for oranges [SSVY24, Theorem 1.1] (see also [ASS92])
its splines reduce to bivariate splines on the planar fan F cut out on B by the edges [v0 , vi ] (Lemma 2.3);
the lower half is treated in the same way after a shear. The dimensions of bivariate splines on F are
classical (Lemma 2.1; see [Sch79, LS07, GS98]). The step that we have not found in the literature
is the gluing across B (Proposition 3.1): the two halves glue exactly when their traces on B agree
and the jump of the normal derivatives equals the derivative of the trace in one ﬁxed direction. With


                                                        3
Lemma 3.2, which shows that in the generic case the trace must be C 2 across every ray, this gives
Theorem 1.1. The same two statements give a uniform proof of the CDS formulas in the collinear and
coplanar cases (Theorem 4.2). The supersmoothness in Lemma 3.2 is of the kind studied in [Sor10],
and CDS use additional smoothness of splines on bipyramid cells to prove their upper bound [Sor15];
see Remark 3.3.
     For k ≥ 3r + 2 the dimension of Hkr is known on every cell: by a theorem of Alfeld, Neamtu and
Schumaker [ANS96, Theorem 3], it is given by an explicit formula in which each interior edge enters
through the number of distinct planes spanned by the interior faces around it; see also [DV21, Remark
4.6 and Section 6.2]. (We checked the statement in a preprint version of [ANS96] posted by its authors.)
For bipyramid cells and r = 1 this formula reproduces Proposition 4.1 for all k ≥ 5, and likewise the
homogeneous dimensions used in the proof of Theorem 4.2; see Section 6. The spaces H01 (∆) and
H11 (∆) consist of global polynomials, and H21 (∆) is given by the elementary Proposition 1.3. So the
new content of Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 4.1 is the determination of H31 (∆) and H41 (∆), and,
given [ANS96], the CDS conjecture concerns only the homogeneous degrees 2, 3 and 4. Our proof
does not use [ANS96]; it treats all degrees in the same way.

2.2    Normal form
An invertible linear map A of R3 maps ∆ to a bipyramid cell A∆ of the same case with the same n
and m, and f 7→ f ◦ A−1 maps Hkr (∆) isomorphically onto Hkr (A∆). By the criterion above, Hkr (∆)
depends only on which tetrahedra are adjacent and on the planes spanned by the interior faces. These
do not change if a vertex vj (j ≥ 1) is replaced by a positive multiple.
    Hence we may assume that B = {z = 0}, that vn+1 = e3 = (0, 0, 1) (apply the linear map that
ﬁxes B pointwise and sends vn+1 to e3 ), and that vn+2 = (w, −1) with w = (c, e) ∈ R2 . After
relabelling, v1 , . . . , vn are in counter-clockwise order. We identify B with R2 and regard v1 , . . . , vn as
vectors in R2 . If w 6= 0, the plane P through 0, e3 and (w, −1) meets B in the line Rw. Therefore

   • the cell is collinear if and only if w = 0;

   • it is generic if and only if w 6= 0 and w is parallel to none of v1 , . . . , vn ;

   • it is coplanar I (II) if and only if w 6= 0 and exactly one (two) of v1 , . . . , vn are parallel to w.

Let λi ∈ R[x, y] be a nonzero linear form with λi (vi ) = 0. The interior faces and their linear forms

Scope and priority. Theorem 1.1 determines dim Sd1 (∆) for every generic bipyramid cell and every
d, and Corollary 1.2 answers the question of record OWR-13678-010 [ula26]: in the remaining cases
the dimension equals the upper bound, except for m = 3, d = 2. The bounds we compare with
are those printed in [Sor15, Theorem 4]. The full text of [CDS16] was not accessible to us, so we
cannot say how its Theorem 4 is worded. If it has the same bounds for d ≥ 3 and treats the case
m = 3, d = 2 diﬀerently, the conjecture as stated there is true by Theorem 1.1; for any wording,
Theorem 1.1 decides the question, since it gives the dimension itself. The homogeneous components
