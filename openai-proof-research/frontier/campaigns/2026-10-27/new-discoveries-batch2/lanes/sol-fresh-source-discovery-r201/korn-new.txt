                                                                                                Abstract
                                                       We investigate fractional analogs of Korn’s inequality on bounded Lipschitz domains in sev-
                                                   eral contexts. Constrained and unconstrained inequalities are established; constraints include
                                                   quotient spaces and spaces incorporating various classes of boundary conditions or volume con-
                                                   straints. We also expand the parameters of integrability for which the inequalities hold. Specif-
                                                   ically, a fractional Korn’s first unconstrained inequality holds for vector fields in the fractional
                                                   Sobolev space W s,p (Ω; Rn ) for all p ∈ [1, ∞] and all s ∈ (0, 1). In the cases p = 1 and p = ∞, the
                                                   inequality has no local analog. We quantify this by investigating the consistency of the inequal-
                                                   ities as the differentiability parameter s → 1; the universal constant in the inequality remains
                                                   stable for p ∈ (1, ∞) but degenerates for p ∈ {1, ∞}. We introduce an explicit Lp -bounded
                                                   projection operator onto infinitesimal rigid motions as a key tool to quantitatively strengthen
                                                   the inequality.

                                           Keywords: Fractional Korn inequality, Fractional Sobolev spaces, Nonlocal vector fields, Lipschitz
                                           domains, Rigid motions, Peridynamics.
                                           2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 26D10, 46E35, 35R11, 74B99.

1.1    Statement of main results
Our first main result is a fractional analog of Korn’s first unconstrained inequality on arbitrary
bounded Lipschitz domains. Furthermore, our estimate is stable in the limit s → 1, which in turn
implies the local inequality (1.1). While we previously obtained this inequality in [26] for domains
with sufficiently small Lipschitz constant, both the general domain case and the stability of the
estimate as s → 1 remained unanswered in that work. In the present work, we completely eliminate
this geometric constraint and establish stability in s.

and s ∈ (0, 1). Then there exists a constant C = C(n, p, Ω) > 0 such that for all vector fields
u ∈ W s,p (Ω; Rn ),

1.3   Quantitative improvements of fractional Korn’s inequalities
We describe the roadmap followed in this work to establish the fractional Korn’s inequalities. First,
the fractional Korn’s second inequality and the resulting space equivalence (Theorem 1.3) are de-
duced from (1.8) via the boundedness of the explicit projection operator from Lp onto R. The
fractional Korn’s first constrained inequalities (Corollary 1.4 and Propositions 1.5–1.8) are then
derived from Theorem 1.3 via compactness arguments. The primary analytical challenge is there-
fore proving the fractional Korn’s first unconstrained inequalities (1.8) for p ∈ (1, ∞) and (1.9) for
p ∈ {1, ∞}.
    We prove these inequalities in two major steps. First, we prove (1.8) and (1.9) in the case that Ω
is a Euclidean ball. Qualitatively, the related estimate (1.7) has already been established in previous
works by (a subset of) the authors [26]. In those works, the result relies on constructing a bounded
linear extension operator from B to all of Rn that preserves the X s,p -seminorm, and then appealing
to a fractional Korn-type inequality on Rn proved by (a subset of) the authors in [25]. The universal
constants in this entire space Korn inequality blow up as s → 1, and so any inequality whose proof
makes use of the whole-space estimate as an ingredient inherits this degeneracy. For that reason,
none of the results in this work make use of any extension arguments. Our methods depart from
existing techniques in the literature. The inequality is “built from scratch” via Fredholm theory on
Lp -bounded operators which characterize projected differences. These operators are then localized
on dyadic decompositions around the diagonal x = y of the Gagliardo seminorms; the resulting
scaling gives the explicit dependence on the differentiability s.
    To further illustrate the dependence of (1.8) and (1.9) on s, the explicitly-constructed projection
operator Π : L1 (Ω) → R defined in Lemma 2.1 is shown to be continuous. Therefore, u − Π[u]
belongs to X s,p whenever u ∈ X s,p , and the inequalities hold for the same projection Π for the
entire range of s and p. This is necessary for showing consistency in the nonlocal-to-local limit as
well as passing to the limit in the compactness arguments of the constrained Korn inequalities.
    The second major step is to prove use (1.8) and (1.9) in the case of bounded Lipschitz domains.
For p = ∞ the Korn inequality is proved directly, while for p < ∞ the inequality is first proved
on the bi-Lipschitz image of a ball, making use of a locally-finite covering argument. It bears
similarity to a Whitney decomposition, but uses Euclidean balls rather than disjoint cubes, so that
the same Korn inequalities established in the first major step can be used. The result (Theorem 3.4)
additionally uses a fractional norm characterization for matrices in order to control the difference
in rigid motions across overlapping elements of the cover.
    We highlight key differences between our approach and the recent preprint [1], which proves a
fractional Korn’s inequality of the form (1.7) for p ∈ (1, ∞) on the more general class of uniform
domains. A primary distinction is the choice of projection onto R. In [1], the projection is bounded
only on the smaller space W s,p , making it unclear how to directly deduce the second fractional


                                                               6
Korn’s inequality from (1.7). Moreover, their result applies solely to vector fields in W s,p (Ω), and
not to those that a priori reside only in X s,p (Ω). The use of the projection Π removes both these
difficulties in our arguments. The global covering arguments in this work and [1] also differ in their
implementation. Rather than smoothing Whitney cubes and controlling energies on the chains via
a global discrete Poincaré inequality as in [1], we employ a covering using Euclidean balls from
[16] with established chaining properties and rely on local matrix norm comparisons on intersecting
elements.
     The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we construct the explicit projection operator
ΠB and prove the strengthened fractional Korn’s first unconstrained inequality on Euclidean balls
(Theorem 2.10) for p ∈ (1, ∞). The cases p = 1 and p = ∞ are proved in Sections 2.3 and 2.4,
respectively. In Section 3, we establish the Korn inequality on bi-Lipschitz images of balls (Theorem
3.4) and complete the proofs of Theorems 1.1-1.3 for bounded Lipschitz domains. Finally, in Section
4, we present the proofs of the subspace Korn inequalities and characterizations (Corollary 1.4 and
Propositions 1.7–1.8).

2     Fractional Korn’s first inequality on Euclidean balls
In [11], it was shown that for any ball B(x0 , R) ⊂ Rn , a fractional analog of Korn’s first uncon-
strained inequality holds: specifically, for p ∈ (1, ∞), there exists C = C(n, p, s) > 0 such that

                            inf ∥u − ψ∥W s,p (B(x0 ,R)) ≤ C[u]X s,p (B(x0 ,R))
                           ψ∈R

for all u ∈ W s,p (B(x0 , R)). Here we strengthen this inequality in three ways. First, we show that
the dependence of the constant C on s can be chosen so that the fractional inequality recovers the
classical Korn’s inequality for u ∈ W 1,p (B(x0 , R); Rn ) as s → 1. Second, we improve the fractional
Korn’s first inequality by replacing the infimum over R with an explicit constructed projection
operator ΠB(x0 ,R) , while at the same time still ensuring the constant is stable as s → 1. Third, we
show that the inequality additionally holds true for p = 1 and p = ∞, while capturing the blow up
of the constant C as s → 1, thus remaining consistent with the lack of classical Korn’s inequalities
in those cases.
    Throughout this section, we set B = B(0, 1) ⊂ Rn be the unit ball.
