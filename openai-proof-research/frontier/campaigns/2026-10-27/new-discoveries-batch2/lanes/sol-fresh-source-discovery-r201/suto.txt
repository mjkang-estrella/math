                                               EQUATION WITH HOLOMORPHIC AND STRICTLY
                                                       INCREASING GENERATORS

                                                   Abstract. We study the two-dimensional Matkowski–Sutô equation,
                                                   which asks for two quasi-arithmetic means whose sum is twice the arith-
                                                   metic mean, in two settings. For holomorphic injective generators with
                                                   convex images on a convex domain in the complex plane, the solutions
                                                   are exactly the affine pairs and the exponential pairs with a nonzero
                                                   complex exponent, up to affine changes of the generators. The admissi-
                                                   ble exponents depend on the shape of the domain and are described by
                                                   a curvature criterion for its boundary. In the monotone-operator frame-
                                                   work of Tóth, we construct an infinite-dimensional family of non-affine
                                                   shear pairs on the whole plane. Their generators are strictly increas-
                                                   ing in the sense of monotone operators and need not be differentiable.
                                                   These pairs solve the weighted equation for any number of variables.
                                                   The rigidity of the one-dimensional problem, due to Daróczy and Páles,
                                                   persists under holomorphy but not under monotonicity.




                                                                           1. Introduction
                                              A quasi-arithmetic mean on an interval I ⊂ R is a two-variable mean
                                           of the form Aφ (x, y) = φ−1 12 (φ(x) + φ(y)) , where the generator φ is a
                                           continuous strictly monotone function on I. These means were characterized
                                           axiomatically by Kolmogorov [8], Nagumo [13] and de Finetti [5], and in
                                           the two-variable case by Aczél [1] through the bisymmetry equation. For
                                           characterizations without continuity assumptions, see Burai–Kiss–Szokol [2].
                                           It is well known that two generators define the same mean if and only if they
                                           differ by an affine transformation; see Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya [6, Theorem
                                           83 and Section 3.7].
                                              The Matkowski–Sutô problem asks for all pairs of quasi-arithmetic means
                                           whose sum is twice the arithmetic mean, that is, for all continuous strictly
                                           monotone functions φ, ψ on I such that
                                                              Aφ (x, y) + Aψ (x, y) = x + y,         x, y ∈ I.               (1.1)
                                           The equation expresses that the arithmetic mean is invariant under the mean-
                                           type mapping (Aφ , Aψ ); by the invariance principle of Matkowski [12], this
                                           holds if and only if the Gauss composition of Aφ and Aψ , that is, the common
                                           limit of the iteration xn+1 = Aφ (xn , yn ), yn+1 = Aψ (xn , yn ), x0 = x, y0 = y,
                                           is the arithmetic mean 21 (x + y). The prototype of such an invariance is

solved by Sutô [18, 19] in 1914 for analytic generators of a real variable, by
Matkowski [11] under twice continuous differentiability, and by Daróczy and
Páles [4] under continuity and strict monotonicity alone. Up to affine changes
of the generators, the solutions are the pair of identity maps (t, t) and the
exponential pairs (exp(pt), exp(−pt)) with p ∈ R \ {0}. Sutô’s argument,
which is discussed in Remark 2.6 below, differentiates the equation with
respect to each variable separately and reduces it, through the more general
equation f1 (φ1 (x) + ψ1 (y)) + f2 (φ2 (x) + ψ2 (y)) = x + y, to a functional
equation with separated variables solved in [18]; he also observed that the
invariance of a quasi-arithmetic mean under a pair of quasi-arithmetic means
reduces to (1.1) by a change of variable [19, Section 3], so that (1.1) governs
the Gauss composition within the class of quasi-arithmetic means [4]. The
main contribution in [4] is a highly sophisticated regularity theory which
derives the differentiability of the generators from their continuity.
   In this paper we consider two-dimensional versions of the problem, with
generators defined on subsets of the plane, and we ask whether the rigidity
of the one-dimensional problem persists. The definition of a quasi-arithmetic
mean uses only the arithmetic mean of the values of the generator and an
inverse of the generator, so it makes sense for injective mappings from a
subset of R2 into R2 , provided that the mean of two values lies in the domain
of the inverse.
   We consider two frameworks for defining quasi-arithmetic means on sub-
sets of the plane. In the first one, we identify R2 with C and take as gener-
ators the holomorphic injective functions on a convex domain U ⊂ C whose
image is convex. The inverse is holomorphic on the image, and the result-
ing means are holomorphic in both variables. In the second one, introduced
recently by Tóth [21], the generators are strictly increasing mappings in the
sense of monotone operators, that is, ⟨f (x) − f (y), x − y⟩ > 0 for x ̸= y,
defined on a closed convex set, and the inverse is replaced by the extended
monotone left inverse on the convex hull of the image. Vector-valued quasi-
arithmetic means also appear in [10, 14]. Tóth proved that on the whole
space the arithmetic mean is generated only by affine maps with positive
definite linear part [21, Theorem 4], and posed the equality problem for these
means. The two frameworks are not comparable. A holomorphic generator
with convex image need not be monotone, and a monotone holomorphic map
need not have a convex image; see Subsection 6.1.
   Our main results are Theorems 1.2 and 1.6, stated in Subsections 1.1
and 1.2, respectively. Theorem 1.2 solves the equation completely in the
holomorphic framework: the solutions are exactly the identity pairs (z, z) and
the exponential pairs (exp(pz), exp(−pz)) with p ∈ C \ {0}, up to complex
affine changes of the generators. Thus the rigidity of the one-dimensional
problem persists, and two features are new. The exponent is now complex,
and the existence of nontrivial solutions on a given domain U depends on
whether exp(pz) and exp(−pz) are injective on U and have convex images.
In Section 3, we examine these conditions using elementary properties of the
                                                                                3

exponential function and the standard transformation formula for boundary
curvature under holomorphic maps. The admissible exponents form a closed
disc when U is a disc, a real interval when U is a horizontal strip, and reduce
to p = 0 when U is a bounded convex polygon or a half-plane. No regularity
theory is required, since holomorphy provides it from the beginning, and the
argument differs from that of Sutô (Remark 2.6).
   Theorem 1.6 shows that the situation is entirely different in the monotone
framework. For every function F on R with |F (s) − F (t)| < 2|s − t| for
s ̸= t, the shear mappings (x, y) 7→ (x, y ± F (x)) are strictly increasing on
R2 and form a solution of the two-variable equation with equal weights. In
Section 4, we prove that these pairs also solve the weighted equation in any
number of variables (Theorem 4.2). Unless F is affine, neither of the two
means is the arithmetic mean, so the equation has an infinite-dimensional
family of non-affine solutions on the whole plane, in contrast with the one-
dimensional case. The same construction extends to every real Hilbert space
of dimension at least two, by acting on a two-dimensional subspace and as
the identity on its orthogonal complement; see Remark 4.3(4). Moreover, F
need not be differentiable, so no regularity theory in the spirit of [4] can hold
for monotone generators in two dimensions. For nonconstant F , these shear
generators are not holomorphic, in accordance with Theorem 1.2. On the
other hand, the exponential pairs of Theorem 1.2 are strictly increasing after
a suitable normalization (Subsection 6.1). The rigidity of the Matkowski–
Sutô equation is a consequence of holomorphy rather than of monotonicity.
   As a further property of these solution pairs, Section 5 studies their si-
multaneous iteration. For every initial pair, the iterates converge to the
arithmetic mean in both the holomorphic and shear cases, so their Gauss
composition is arithmetic. For the exponential pairs with nonzero exponent,
a change of variables reduces the iteration to repeated squaring; the shear
pairs reach their common value after at most two iterations.
   The paper is organized as follows. Subsections 1.1 and 1.2 below intro-
duce the two frameworks and state the main results. Section 2 contains
the fourth-order expansion at the diagonal and the proof of Theorem 1.2.
Section 3 describes the admissible exponents. Section 4 introduces weighted
means, proves the full version of Theorem 1.6, and discusses the properties
of the shear solutions. Section 5 studies the Gauss composition of the holo-
morphic and shear solution pairs. Section 6 compares the two frameworks
and discusses open problems concerning the classification of solutions and
the role of the domain.

1.2. Strictly increasing generators. In this subsection and in Section 4,
⟨·, ·⟩ and | · | denote the Euclidean inner product and norm on R2 . Moreover,
conv(A) denotes the convex hull of A ⊂ R2 .
Definition 1.4. Let C ⊂ R2 be a nonempty closed convex set. A mapping
f : C → R2 is increasing if ⟨f (x) − f (y), x − y⟩ ≥ 0 for all x, y ∈ C, and
strictly increasing if ⟨f (x) − f (y), x − y⟩ > 0 for all x, y ∈ C with x ̸= y.
   A strictly increasing mapping is injective. By [21, Theorems 2 and 3],
for every strictly increasing f : C → R2 , there exists a unique increasing
mapping f (−1) : conv(f (C)) → C such that f (−1) (f (x)) = x for every x ∈ C,
and this mapping is continuous. It is called the extended monotone left
inverse of f . If f (C) is convex, it is the ordinary inverse of f .

f : C → R2 be strictly increasing. The two-variable quasi-arithmetic mean

the generators ψ1 ◦ ψ −1 and ψ2 ◦ ψ −1 by the change of variable ξ = ψ(x),

             6. Concluding remarks and open problems
6.1. Comparison of the two frameworks. The holomorphic and mono-
tone frameworks impose different conditions on the generators. Throughout
this subsection, we identify C with R2 and use the Euclidean inner product
and norm. Let φ be holomorphic on a convex domain U . Integrating φ′
along the segment from w to z gives
                                Z 1
                              2
                                    Re φ′ w+t(z−w) dt,
                                                   
 ⟨φ(z)−φ(w), z−w⟩ = |z−w|                                   z, w ∈ U, (6.1)
                                  0
so φ satisfies the strict monotonicity inequality in Definition 1.4 if Re φ′ > 0
on U , and φ is not increasing if Re φ′ takes a negative value.
   A generator in H(U ) need not be increasing, and this cannot always be
remedied by an affine change of the generator as in Lemma 2.1, which does
not change the mean. For instance, φ(z) := z/(1 − z) belongs to H(U ) for
the unit disc U , since it maps U onto the half-plane {w : Re w > −1/2}, and
φ′ (z) = (1 − z)−2 has an argument filling the interval (−π, π) as z ranges
over U ; hence Re(aφ′ ) changes sign on U for every a ∈ C \ {0}, and no
aφ + b is increasing or decreasing. Even a real-linear change L ◦ φ + b with
L ∈ GL(2, R) does not help: the Jacobian of φ at z is |φ′ (z)| times the
rotation by arg φ′ (z), and if L ◦ φ were increasing, the symmetric part of
LRθ would be positive semidefinite for every rotation Rθ with θ ∈ (−π, π);
applied to θ and θ +π this forces LRθ to be antisymmetric for all θ ∈ (−π, 0),
which is impossible for an invertible L.
   Conversely, a holomorphic map satisfying the strict monotonicity inequal-
ity in Definition 1.4 need not belong to H(U ): on the half disc {z : |z| <
1, Re z > 0} the map z 7→ z 2 satisfies Re(2z) > 0, hence the inequality by
(6.1), but its image, the unit disc slit along (−1, 0], is not convex. As shown
                                                                                 19

in [21, Example 1], this map admits no extended monotone left inverse; the
half disc is not closed, so [21, Theorem 2] does not apply.
   The two settings also differ in the domain, which is closed in Definition
1.4 and open in Definition 1.1, and in the equivalence of generators, which
is complex affine in the holomorphic setting.
   On C = R2 , [21, Theorem 4] shows that the only strictly increasing gen-
erators of the arithmetic mean are the affine ones. This is consistent with
PC = {0} (Proposition 3.1).
   In contrast to general elements of H(U ), the solutions of (1.3) admit
increasing representatives. If p ∈ PU \ {0}, then χp (U ) is a convex do-
main not containing 0, hence it is contained in an open half-plane {w :
Re (exp(−iα)w) > 0} for some α ∈ R. The generator f := exp(−iα)p−1 χp
satisfies Af = Aχp by Lemma 2.1 and Re f ′ = Re (exp(−iα) exp(pz)) > 0
on U , so f is strictly increasing by (6.1); the same applies to χ−p . Thus,
up to affine changes of the generators, the exponential pairs of Theorem 1.2
satisfy the strict monotonicity inequality in Definition 1.4, although general
elements of H(U ) need not satisfy it. The shear generators of Theorem 1.6
with nonconstant F are not holomorphic on C (Remark 4.3), so the rigidity
expressed by Theorem 1.2 is a consequence of holomorphy rather than of
monotonicity.
6.2. Open problems. The holomorphic classification and the shear exam-
ples raise two related questions about the two-variable Matkowski–Sutô equa-
tion with equal weights. These questions may be considered on convex sub-
sets of real Hilbert spaces, possibly of infinite dimension, and on Banach
spaces with an appropriate notion of monotonicity. Affine equivalence below
means composition of each generator with an invertible continuous affine
transformation of its values.
Question 6.1 (Additional assumptions for classification). What structural
assumptions on the ambient space, the domain, and the generators, in addi-
tion to affine equivalence to strictly increasing mappings, yield an analogue
of the affine/exponential classification? In general ambient spaces, identify-
ing suitable analogues of the exponential pairs (χp , χ−p ) in (1.4) is itself part
of the problem.
  Section 3 shows that the admissible exponents in the holomorphic setting
depend on the domain, while Remark 4.3(4) gives shear solutions on closed
cylinders. This leads to the following question.
Question 6.2 (Dependence on the domain). How does the classification
depend on the geometry of the domain? In particular, how does it change
when the generators are defined on a proper convex subset of the ambient
space?
