                                           Singularity removal in nonlocal operator inequalities on
                                                          domains and applications
                                                                                     Davit Harutyunyan∗




arXiv:2610.39437v1 [math.AP] 29 Oct 2026
                                                                                            Abstract
                                                         We prove that certain classes of nonlocal integral estimates on domains, involv-
                                                     ing operators, may be reduced to the local analogues without the singular weight on
                                                     cubes. The theory applies to two-sided inverse doubling and quasi-monotone kernels
                                                     with some moment conditions, and with a left-hand-side operator with average domain-
                                                     bi-Lipschitz properties. Direct applications to fractional semi-norm inequalities in Peri-
                                                     dynamics, such as fractional Korn’s inequalities and Geometric Rigidity Estimates are
                                                     presented.

    Coming back to the FGRI, it is easy to prove that the measurable exact a.e. isometries
(rigid motions) of Rn are of the form r(x) = Rx + b, where R ∈ O(n) is an orthogonal
matrix. Consequently, recall the standard notation

            so(n) = {r(x) = Rx + b : Rn → Rn }         with     R ∈ O(n), b ∈ Rn .          (1.9)

of the space of exact isometries of Rn . The FGRE would be the nonlinear analogue of (1.5)
with r instead of a, the space so(n) instead of se(n), and a nonlinear elastic energy (yet to
be defined) on the right-hand side instead of the linear counterpart. The nonlinear fractional
elastic energy has to vanish on orientation preserving isometries of Rn , i.e., on rigid motions,
because those carry zero elastic energy. However, because in general there is no notion of
orientation preservation for L1loc fields, it is natural co consider O(n) instead of SO(n) in the
nonlocal case. A natural energy density candidate that may appear in the nonlinear nonlocal
elastic energy is the quantity

                                   ||u(x) − u(y)| − |x − y||,

which vanishes exactly on exact isometries, i.e., affine fields with a constant gradient in O(n).
The analogous estimate written for SO(n) fails to hold. We refer the reader to [7] for the
classical local formulation (GRE) of the FGRE. The story behind the FGRI is worth telling.
When we were at final stages of typing and polishing the current manuscript and the com-
panion paper [12], where we formulate and prove the FGRI and the FKI in bounded uniform
domains for the case 1 ≤ p < ∞, e.g., [12, Theorems 2.1-2.2], a formulation and proof of the
FGRE was still open in public, and James Scott and Tadele Mengesha kindly shared pdf
copies of their papers [26] and [21] on the FGRE and the FKI with us. The paper [26] estab-
lishes the formulation and proof of the FGRI in open bounded connected Lipschitz subsets
of Rn and with general radial kernes ρ(|x − y|) that covers the standard Peridynamics kernel
ρ(x, y) = |x − y|−n−ps appearing in (1.7), (1.8), and (1.5), see [26, Theorem 2.1], while the
paper [21] proves FKI on bounded Lipschitz domains also for the endpoint cases p = 1, ∞,
see [21, Theorems 1.1-1.3]. Our assumptions on the kernel ρ(x, y) are slightly different and
our theorem on the FGRI proven in [12, Theorem 2.1] based on the localization technique of
the current manuscript is different, and we establish it on uniform domains, which strictly
contains the class of Lipschitz domains. A detailed comparison of [12, Theorem 2.1] and
[26, Theorem 2.1] appears in Section 1 of [12], were we refer the interested readers to. It
is not surprising, that our formulation of the FGRI coincides with that of Scotts’, where
both naturally follow standard principles of mathematics and physics. A general heuristic

    The necessary conditions we would have to impose on the kernel ρ, the operators A and
 B and the domain Ω occur in the proof schemes. It is necessary and natural to assume
 translational quasi-invariance on ρ in the context of (1.4), which is the existence of a fixed
 constant c > 0 such that ρ(x + z, y + z) ≤ cρ(x, y) for all x, y, z ∈ Rn . However, this
 seemingly more ambitious translational quasi-invariance is in fact equivalent to the standard
 translational invariance ρ(x + z, y + z) = ρ(x, y) in the context of (1.4) after replacing the
 kernel ρ by an equivalent translation-invariant kernel ρ′ (x, y) by defining

                                 ρ′ (x, y) = infn ρ(x + z, y + z).
                                            z∈R

 simplicity, we can simply work under the condition

     We will prove one of the main theorems (Theorem 2.2) of the paper in uniform domains.
  These are certain type of John domains that strictly include Lipschitz domains. We recall
  the definitions of John and uniform domains below.
  Definition 1.1 (John domain). A bounded domain Ω ⊂ Rn is called a John domain, if there
  exists a constant 0 < c ≤ 1 and a fixed point x0 ∈ Ω such that for every other point x ∈ Ω,
  there exists a rectifiable curve γ : [0, s] → Ω, parameterized by its arc length, that starts at x,
  ends at x0 , and satisfies dist(γ(t), ∂Ω) ≥ ct for all t ∈ [0, s], where s is the length of γ.
     We refer the reader to the original paper of John [8] and the paper [16] for more details
  on John domains. Next we recall the definition of uniform domains.
  Definition 1.2 (Uniform domain). A domain Ω ⊂ Rn is called a uniform domain, if there
  exists a constant C ≥ 1, such that for every two points x, y ∈ Ω, there exists a rectifiable
  curve α : [0, s] → Ω, that starts at x, ends at y, and satisfies min(ℓ(x, γ(t)), ℓ(y, γ(t))) ≤
  Cdist(γ(t), ∂Ω) for all t ∈ [0, s], where ℓ(z, γ(t)) is the length of the curve between the points
  z and γ(t) for z = x, y.

Proof of Theorem 2.3. The strategy of the proof is similar to that of [14,22,23,1] via a Whit-
ney cube decomposition. In authors [1] extend the fractional Korn’s first inequality from
smoothened cubes to uniform domains through Whitney decomposition through a gluing
technique, due to the an ordering result on Whitney cubes on John domains, which estab-
lishes the arrangement of the cubes into a tree with controlled tails [15]. We will keep track
of the case p = 1 through the proof lines, as some of the results we use are formulated
for p > 1, which we need to address in the case p = 1 and verify their validity whenever
necessary. We now consider a standard Whitney decomposition of Ω into a countable family
of closed dyadic cubes F = {Qj }j∈J satisfying the conditions (C1)-(C3) below:

(C1) 8diam(Q) ≤ dist(Q, ∂Ω) ≤ 40diam(Q) for all Q ∈ F.

(C2) 21 diam(Qj ) ≤ diam(Qk ) ≤ 2diam(Qj ) if Qj ∩ Qk ̸= ∅.

(C3) ∪J∈J Q = Ω.

We refer to [31] for a version of decomposition with constants 1 and 4 in (C1). The constants
8 and 40 can be achieved by further dividing every cube into 2n equal dyadic cubes three
times, which will preserve (C2)-(C3) and yield the current form of (C1). The following lemma
on graphs on Whitney cubes, proven by López-Garćıa in [15, Lemma 5.2] is an important
ingredient in the proof.

Lemma 3.2 ([15, Lemma 5.2]). A bounded domain Ω ⊂ Rn is a John domain if and only if
given a Whitney decomposition F = {Qj }j∈J of Ω, there exist a tree structure for the set of
indices J and a constant K > 1 that satisfy Q ⊆ KQa for any pair (Qa , Q) of cubes, where
Qa is an ancestor of Q through the three structure. In other words, the shadow of Qa is
contained in the expanded set KQa . Moreover, the intersection of any adjacent parent-child
cubes Qp and Qc in the three is always an n − 1 dimensional face of one of these cubes.
    If this three structure is not possible to maintain after further division of the original
cubes as above, we may assume that the three contains repeating vertexes, that can repeat
a bounded number of times with the same parent-child and shadow conditions still holding
while increasing K if necessary. Let Q0 denote the tree root in the above lemma. Then, we
will prove Theorem 2.2 with Q = 2Q0 . The following result, proven by Rutkowski in [24,
Theorem 1.1] will be crucial in reducing the domains of integration to the Carleson boxes.

x ∈ Ω and assume hypotheses (Hρ1’), (Hρ2’), and (Hρ4). Then for every θ ∈ (0, 1), one has
