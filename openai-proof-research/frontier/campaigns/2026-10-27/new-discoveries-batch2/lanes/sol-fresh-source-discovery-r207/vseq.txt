Let X1 , . . . , Xn be independent real-valued random variables with known distribution functions

where the supremum is over stopping times taking values in {1, . . . , n} and adapted to the
natural filtration of X1 , . . . , Xn . In particular, selecting any observation tied for the maximum
is a win.
    This note continues the reduction method of Allaart and Islas (2016). For independent
random variables with continuous distributions, that paper constructs a V -sequence by
replacing the observations one at a time without increasing the optimal win probability. The
first observation of the reduced sequence is constant, and each later observation takes at
most two values. The purpose here is to extend this construction to arbitrary independent
distributions.
    Although ties should make it easier to choose an overall maximum, the original proof
cannot be applied directly when atoms are present. Continuity is used at two steps. First, it
gives a critical value at which the probabilities of winning by stopping and by continuing are
equal. With atoms, the two functions may cross through a jump without being equal at any
point. Second, an intermediate value argument is used to choose constants needed for the
successive replacements. A discontinuous function may skip the required value. Thus the
expected extension of the reduction still requires a proof.
    We overcome these difficulties by first replacing each atom by a uniform observation on
an interval, keeping the intervals for distinct original values strictly ordered. We show that
this replacement cannot increase the optimal win probability, and then apply the original

                                                 2
continuous reduction lemma. On returning to the original observations, its critical value
becomes a common level c, with a possible random split of observations equal to c. The same
splitting probability is used for every observation.
   Hill and Kennedy (1992) established sharp bounds for stopping rewards based on ranks
for arbitrary independent distributions. Their Theorem 1.2, specialized to selection of the
best observation, gives the sharp bound


                                 Vn (X1 , . . . , Xn ) ≥ (1 − 1/n)n−1 .


Their convention assigns rank one to every observation tied for the maximum, agreeing with
the objective considered here. Their proof also uses auxiliary uniforms to split a threshold
atom and transfers the guarantee from randomized to ordinary stopping times. Thus neither
this bound nor the use of auxiliary randomization to handle atoms is claimed as new.
   Related treatments of ties include Esfandiari et al. (2020), who use auxiliary randomization
in threshold algorithms for nonidentically distributed observations, and Gnedin et al. (2023),
whose Section 2.2 compares arbitrary i.i.d. observations with continuous ones through
randomized atom spreading. Our purpose is to give an explicit general-distribution extension
of the Allaart–Islas construction. The comparison uses the same idea of breaking ties at
random. The reduction to a V -sequence is obtained by applying the lemma in Allaart and
Islas (2016).

Remark 4.1. The convention that a tie for the maximum is a win is essential. If success
requires selecting a unique strict maximum, a sequence of identical constants has success
