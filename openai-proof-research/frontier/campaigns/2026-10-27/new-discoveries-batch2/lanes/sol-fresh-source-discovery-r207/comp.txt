                                                   Abstract. Given a positive, non-increasing sequence a with finite sum
                                                   equal to 1, we consider the family of all closed subsets of [0, 1] whose
                                                   complementary open intervals have lengths given by a rearrangement of
                                                   the sequence a. We study the full range of possible θ-intermediate di-
                                                   mensions of these sets and, under suitable assumptions on the sequence,
                                                   we show that this range forms a closed interval, whose endpoints we
                                                   compute explicitly. This paper fills a gap in the literature concerning
                                                   the dimensional properties of complementary sets.




                                                                           1. Introduction
                                              The intermediate dimensions dimθ and dimθ form a continuum of di-
                                           mensions that were introduced in [13] as a way to interpolate between the
                                           Hausdorff and box-counting dimensions; see Definition 2.1 for the precise
                                           definition. These dimensions have been extensively studied in various con-
                                           texts, such as in [6] for stochastic processes, [7] and [2] for results related to
                                           projections of sets, [8] for elliptic polynomial spirals, and more recently in
                                           [1], where these dimensions were extended to measures. These dimensions
                                           are continuous (except perhaps at θ = 0) and increase as a function of θ,
                                           and the following relations hold:
                                                 dimH E ≤ dim θ E ≤ dim θ E ≤ dimB             and    dim θ E ≤ dimB E,
                                           where E ⊂ Rd is compact, and dim        H and dimB represent the Hausdorff and
                                           box dimensions, respectively. Moreover, [4] provides a characterization of
                                           a function h(θ) as the dimension function associated with a set E. These
                                           dimensions were further generalized in [3] to more general settings.
                                              Given a = {an }, a positive non-increasing sequence with a finite sum equal
                                           to 1, the class Ca is defined as the family of all closed subsets of [0, 1] whose
                                           complement in [0, 1] is a union of disjoint open intervals of lengths given by
                                           the entries of a. The sets in Ca are called complementary sets of a. They
                                           can be countable, for example, a sequence of points, and uncountable, for
                                           example, Cantor sets. So it is natural to ask about the possible dimensions
                                           of the sets belonging to Ca .
                                              In the case of Hausdorff dimension, the problem was first studied in 1954
                                           by Besicovitch and Taylor in [5] and was later studied more deeply in [9]

and [15]. In fact, it was proved that the set of possible values for the Haus-
dorff dimension of the sets in Ca is the closed interval [0, dimH Ca ], where
Ca denotes the Cantor set associated with a, see section 3 for the precise
definition. A similar result for the Packing measure was obtained in [16] by
Hare, Mendivil, and Zuberman.
   For the case of the box dimensions, in [11, Chapter 3.2] it is shown that
there exist numbers s ≤ t, depending only on the sequence a, such that every
complementary set E ∈ Ca has lower and upper box dimensions dimB E = s
and dimB E = t. In particular, these values depend only on a and not on
the specific rearrangement of its gaps.
   More recently, Garcia, Hare and Mendivil studied the problem for As-
souad and lower Assouad dimensions. They computed the maximum and
minimum possible values for the Assouad dimension of complementary sets
and, under certain assumptions about the decay of the sequence a, proved
that the set of attainable values for the Assouad dimension of sets in Ca is,
in fact, also a closed interval.
   In this paper, we study the problem from the perspective of θ-intermediate
dimensions. Specifically, we compute the θ-intermediate dimension of the
countable set Da (see Section 3 for the precise definition) and, under natural
conditions on the decay of the sequence a, we are able to compute the θ-
intermediate dimension of the Cantor set Ca .
   Following these computations, we establish that the minimum and maxi-
mum possible values for the θ-intermediate dimension of any complementary
set E ∈ Ca are given by the θ-intermediate dimensions of Da and Ca , respec-
tively. More formally, for all θ ∈ [0, 1], we have the following inequalities:
                        dim θ Da ≤ dim θ E ≤ dim θ Ca .
Similarly, the analogous result holds for the lower θ-intermediate dimension.
   Finally, we address the natural question of whether all possible values of
θ-intermediate dimensions for sets in Ca are attained within the intervals
[dimθ Da , dimθ Ca ] and [dimθ Da , dimθ Ca ]. We prove that this is indeed the
case under certain natural geometric conditions on the sequence a.
   Let sn denote the average length of the closed intervals at the n-th step
in the construction of the Cantor set Ca . Our hypotheses are motivated
by known results for Assouad and lower Assouad dimensions: the Assouad
dimensions of complementary sets form a closed interval if the sequence a
is doubling, while the lower Assouad dimensions form a closed interval if
2 < inf n sn /sn+1 (see [14]). These two conditions—which together require
that the ratios sn /sn+1 be bounded away from both 2 and infinity—ensure
that the intermediate dimensions of complementary sets also form a closed
interval. Specifically, fixed θ, we show that under these conditions,
                   {dimθ E : E ∈ Ca } = [dimθ Da , dimθ Ca ]
and
                   {dimθ E : E ∈ Ca } = [dimθ Da , dimθ Ca ].

That is, fixed θ ∈ [0, 1], for every t ∈ [dim θ Da , dim θ Ca ] (respectively for the
upper intermediate dimension), there exists a set E ∈ Ca , that depends on
θ and t, whose lower (respectively upper) θ-intermediate dimension equals
exactly t.
   As previously discussed, complementary sets have already been studied
extensively with respect to Hausdorff, packing, box-counting, Assouad, and
lower Assouad dimensions.
   This paper complements those results by providing a detailed analysis
of the intermediate dimensions of sets in Ca for an arbitrary sequence a,
characterizing the full range of possible values.

                                Further Work
   In [4], the authors provided necessary and sufficient conditions for a func-
tion h(θ) to arise as the intermediate dimension of a bounded subset of Rn .
Specifically, for 0 ≤ λ < α ≤ n, denote by H(λ, α) the set of all functions
h : [0, 1] → [λ, α] that are non-decreasing, continuous on (0, 1], and satisfy
                  h(θ + ε) − h(θ)   (h(θ) − λ)(α − h(θ))
        lim sup                   ≤                      ,      ∀ θ ∈ (0, 1).
         ε→0+            ε                (α − λ)θ
If λ = α, then H(λ, α) consists only of the constant function h(θ) = λ. The
following theorem was established in [4].
Theorem 6.2. [4, Theorem B] Let F ⊂ Rn satisfy dimL F = λ and
dimA F = α. Then with h1 (θ) = dim θ F and h2 (θ) = dim θ F , we have
h1 , h2 ∈ H(λ, α), h1 ≤ h2 , and h1 (0) = h2 (0).
   Conversely, if 0 ≤ λ ≤ α ≤ n and h1 , h2 ∈ H(λ, α) satisfy h1 ≤ h2 and
h1 (0) = h2 (0), then there exists a compact perfect set F ⊂ Rn such that
  dimL F = λ,         dim θ F = h1 (θ),    dim θ F = h2 (θ),        dimA F = α,
for all θ ∈ [0, 1].
   A natural question connecting [4] with the present work is the following:
for a fixed sequence a, can we describe conditions ensuring that a given func-
tion h(θ) can be realized as the intermediate dimension of a complementary
set E ∈ Ca ?
   In this paper, some partial results in that direction were obtained. For
instance, under the assumption
