                                               THE MONOTONE PART OF CHATTERJEE’S RANK CORRELATION

                                                                                    KARL FRIEDRICH SIBURG


                                                   Abstract. Chatterjee’s rank correlation ξ measures how strongly a random variable Y depends
                                                   functionally on a random variable X, but it does not distinguish increasing, decreasing and non-
                                                   monotone relationships. We ask how much of the dependence measured by ξ is explained by
                                                   an increasing relationship. For continuous marginals, ξ is, up to a factor, a squared distance




arXiv:2610.35340v1 [math.PR] 26 Oct 2026
                                                   from independence in a Hilbert space geometry derived from the Sobolev scalar product for
                                                   copulas. We answer it by the metric projection onto the closed convex set of stochastically
                                                   increasing copulas in this geometry. The projection is explicit: it replaces every vertical section
                                                   by its least concave majorant, and it coincides with the smallest stochastically increasing copula
                                                   dominating the given one. It splits ξ orthogonally into a monotone part ξ ↑ and the squared
                                                   distance to the class of stochastically increasing copulas; dually, ξ ↑ is, up to a factor, the
                                                   squared distance to the polar cone, which corresponds to negative quadrant dependence. The
                                                   monotone part vanishes exactly for negatively quadrant dependent copulas, equals ξ exactly
                                                   for stochastically increasing copulas, and equals one only for the comonotonicity copula. In
                                                   contrast to ξ, it is increasing in the concordance order and continuous with respect to weak
                                                   convergence. Symmetrisation decomposes the measure of mutual complete dependence in the
                                                   same way. Finally, the construction extends to arbitrary distributions via isotonic regression of
                                                   conditional distribution functions. In particular, the plug-in estimator based on the empirical
                                                   distribution is strongly consistent for every copula, without smoothing or tuning parameters.

functional dependence. Both properties, however, hold for the monotone part introduced below.

   Approach and main results. We address this question geometrically. The stochastically in-

sense of Lehmann (1966), form a closed convex set CSI . We project onto CSI with respect to the
distance d1 in which ξ is a squared distance. Our main results are as follows.
    (1) The metric projection PC exists and is explicit: each vertical section C(·, v) is replaced by
        its least concave majorant. Equivalently, ∂1 PC(·, v) is the isotonic regression of ∂1 C(·, v)
        onto non-increasing functions. Moreover, PC is the smallest SI copula dominating C
        pointwise (Theorem 3.3).
    (2) In the tangent space at Π, the directions of stochastic increasingness and those of negative
        quadrant dependence form mutually polar cones, K and K◦ . Accordingly, C − Π =
        (PC − Π) + (C − PC) is the Moreau decomposition of C − Π into its projections onto K
        and K◦ , and the two parts are orthogonal (Theorem 4.3).

        roots, and continuous with respect to uniform (equivalently, weak) convergence of copulas
        (Theorem 4.5). By the polarity in (2), ξ ↑ (C)/6 and (ξ(C) − ξ ↑ (C))/6 are the squared
        distances of C − Π to the cones K◦ and K, respectively; this explains why NQD and SI

ρS , Kendall’s τ and the asymmetric grade correlation of Walz et al. (2025), which reduces to

ρS for continuous marginals, attain their maximal value exactly under increasing functional
dependence. However, they balance positive against negative association, so that their zero
set neither characterises independence nor negative quadrant dependence, and they carry no
information about the strength of non-monotone functional dependence. Measures of functional
dependence such as ξ, the measure ζ1 of Trutschnig (2011) or the rearranged dependence measures
of Strothmann et al. (2024) detect any functional relationship, but they assign the maximal
value to every functional relationship, whether increasing, decreasing or non-monotone. The
monotone part ξ ↑ is not meant as yet another coefficient of increasing functional dependence;
for that purpose ρS and τ suffice. Rather, it answers the question raised above, which neither
type of measure addresses. Accordingly, ξ ↑ is an exact component of ξ itself, it vanishes exactly
under negative quadrant dependence, it equals ξ exactly under stochastic increasingness, and it
attains one exactly under increasing functional dependence. It is linked to concordance by the
sharp bound ξ ↑ ≥ 21 (ρS )2+ (Theorem 4.5(g)), so positive concordance forces a positive monotone
part. The converse fails: the mixture 12 M + 12 W and the copula of (U, |2U − 1|) have ρS = 0, but
ξ ↑ ≈ 0.079 and ξ ↑ ≈ 0.318, respectively (Table 3). Behind the decomposition lies the structural
fact (2), which appears to be new: in the geometry of ξ, stochastic increasingness and negative
quadrant dependence are dual to each other in the sense of convex cones. To the best of our
knowledge, neither the projection onto CSI in the geometry of ξ nor a decomposition of ξ into
an increasing part and a remainder has been considered before; recent work relates ξ to positive
dependence and to concordance through inequalities and exact regions (Ansari and Rockel, 2026;
Rockel, 2026; Orenday Lares and Rockel, 2026), not through a decomposition.
    Statistical relevance. The properties of ξ ↑ matter for statistical practice in four ways. First,
the identity 1 = ξ ↑ + (ξ − ξ ↑ ) + (1 − ξ) is an orthogonal decomposition of the variation of the
indicators 1{Y ≥ t} into a part explained by non-decreasing functions of X, a part explained by
X but not by non-decreasing functions of X, and an unexplained part (Remark 7.6). Thus ξ ↑
is an isotonic counterpart of a coefficient of determination. The middle part contains decreasing
as well as genuinely non-monotone dependence; it splits further as ξ − ξ ↑ = ξ ↓ + ξ ◦ with ξ ◦ ≥
0 (Proposition 4.8), so that ξ ↑ , ξ ↓ and ξ ◦ reveal whether a strong functional dependence is
increasing, decreasing or neither, which ξ alone cannot do (Example 5.5). Second, the residual
ξ − ξ ↑ is an L2 -type measure of departure from stochastic increasingness, a hypothesis that is
routinely tested in econometrics (Lee et al., 2009; Delgado and Escanciano, 2012; Seo, 2018);
Theorem 3.3 shows that the least concave majorant used in these tests is precisely the metric
projection in the geometry of ξ. Third, weak continuity makes ξ ↑ stable under small perturbations
of the distribution and turns the plug-in principle into a consistent estimation method without
smoothing or tuning parameters (Theorem 7.7), whereas the plug-in of the empirical distribution
always yields ξ = 1. Fourth, monotonicity in the concordance order makes ξ ↑ compatible with the
dependence orderings used to compare dependence structures, for instance in risk management,
which ξ is not.
    Related work. The rearranged dependence measures of Strothmann et al. (2024) also map
a copula to an SI copula, namely by monotone rearrangement of ∂1 C(·, v). Rearrangement
preserves ξ and turns any functional dependence into a monotone one, whereas the projection
retains only the part of ξ that is explained by non-decreasing functions of X; the two constructions
are compared in Remark 3.5. The identity ξ = 6 d1 (·, Π)2 links ξ to the strong metrics on
copulas introduced by Trutschnig (2011); see Section 2. Finally, Chatterjee’s rank correlation
and its relation to positive dependence concepts and to concordance measures have attracted
considerable interest; see Ansari and Rockel (2024, 2026); Ansari and Fuchs (2025b); Rockel
(2026) and the references therein.

2.1. Copulas and positive dependence. Let C denote the set of bivariate copulas, Π(u, v) =
uv, M (u, v) = min(u, v) and W (u, v) = max(u + v − 1, 0). For C ∈ C let C ⊤ (u, v) = C(v, u).
We write C1 ≤ C2 if C1 (u, v) ≤ C2 (u, v) for all (u, v) (concordance or pointwise order). Every
copula is 1-Lipschitz in each argument, so for every v ∈ [0, 1] the section C(·, v) is differentiable
almost everywhere with 0 ≤ ∂1 C(·, v) ≤ 1 and

   A copula C is negatively quadrant dependent (NQD) if C ≤ Π and positively quadrant depen-
dent (PQD) if C ≥ Π. It is stochastically increasing (SI) if V is stochastically increasing in U ,
i.e., if P(V ≤ v | U = u) is non-increasing in u for every v; this is Lehmann’s positive regression
dependence. Equivalently, C(·, v) is concave for every v ∈ [0, 1] (Nelsen, 2006, Chapter 5). We
denote by CSI the set of SI copulas. It is convex and closed under pointwise convergence. Every
SI copula is PQD, since a concave function on [0, 1] lies above its chord.
