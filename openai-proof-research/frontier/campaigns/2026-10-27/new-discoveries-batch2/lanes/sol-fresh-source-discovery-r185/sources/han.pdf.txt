   We next explain the lower limit k = 19 in the two stronger conjectures below.
Remark 6.4 (The observed threshold 19). The stronger property in question is coeffi-
cientwise nonnegativity of the numerator polynomial itself, rather than nonnegativity
only after division by 1 − q. For 1 ≤ k ≤ 18, exact computation gives
             polynomial values of k ≤ 18 having a negative coefficient
               Hk (q)                   4, 5, 6, . . . , 18
               Uk (q)                   6, 8, 10, 12, 18.
Both Hk (q) and Uk (q) are coefficientwise nonnegative for every tested value 19 ≤ k ≤
150. Thus 19 is the smallest observed point after which neither sequence has another

failure in the computed range. This is an empirical threshold, not a consequence of
the prefix-sum proof: even at the exceptional smaller values, the prefix sums in (31)
and (34) are nonnegative, which is enough for the two propositions. The conjectures
assert that the stronger numerator positivity seen from k = 19 onward persists for
all larger k.

Conjecture 6.5. For all k ≥ 19, we have Hk (q) ≥q 0, where Hk (q) is defined
