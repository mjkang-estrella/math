## SUBSTANTIVE — recommend EXCLUDE, with complete applicable bridge

The closest primary body is Craven–Csordas, *Complex zero decreasing sequences*, Methods Appl. Anal. 2(4) (1995), 420–441. I read Definitions 1.3 and 1.5, Remark 1.6, Theorem 1.7, the intervening reciprocal-necessity paragraph, Example 1.8, and Theorem 2.13 with its proof. Theorem 1.7 states the equivalence of positivity-preserving reciprocal diagonals, positive polynomial functionals, Hankel determinants and a moment representation. It cites classical moment theory rather than proving all these equivalences afresh. Example 1.8 applies precisely the reciprocal determinant test to 1+k+k², then supplies an explicit forward polynomial counterexample. It does not literally print the present degree-72 inverse-square polynomial.

Here is the full bridge to the modified endpoint, without cancelling a factorial or needing the Hamburger theorem:

1. For any strictly positive diagonal gamma with gamma_0=1, a CZDS would force its inverse diagonal to preserve strictly positive polynomials of positive even degree: if h>0 and f=T^{-1}h, then degree(f)=degree(h), the CZDS inequality would force f to have zero real roots, and its positive leading coefficient would force f>0 everywhere.
2. In particular, for every real polynomial s and every delta>0, evaluating T^{-1}(s²+delta) at 1 must be positive. Writing lambda=1/gamma and sending delta down to zero yields sum s_i s_j lambda_{i+j}≥0. Thus each reciprocal Hankel quadratic form must be nonnegative. This is exactly the necessary positivity/determinant mechanism of Theorem 1.7 and its application in Example 1.8.
3. Substitute the source's gamma_k=(k²+k+1)/k!. Its reciprocal is lambda_k=k!/(k²+k+1), not 1/(k²+k+1). Form the resulting rational Hankel matrix. The supplied negative vector is independently verified directly above; the separate determinant calculation verifies that the same determinant test itself fails at order 37.
4. Given that negative vector, the standard positivity-test polynomial s²+delta, with delta=-q/(2 lambda_0), supplies an explicit inverse-diagonal counterexample. Here lambda_0=1. The positivity, leading sign and IVT argument above complete every step of the literal source refutation.
