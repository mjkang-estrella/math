 Refining invariants of finite groups with class functions 
 
 
 Christopher A. Schroeder
 
 Address:  HUN-REN Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics, Reáltanoda utca 13-15, H-1053 Budapest, Hungary
 
 Email address:  schroeder.chris@renyi.hu 
 
 Date: September 22, 2026 

 Abstract. 
    
 Many numerical invariants of a finite group arise as the multiplicity of the
trivial character in a naturally associated class function, and the remaining
multiplicities carry finer structural information. For the number of conjugacy
classes of elements whose order involves only a chosen set of primes, the
natural such class function is a generalized character interpolating between
the classical conjugating character and the regular character. We prove that it is a character in two important general cases, obtained independently by Robinson, and we rule out a family of groups and characters in which, we argue, a counterexample would be most likely to arise. Furthermore, we characterize when these class functions are compatible with passing to a subgroup and thereby sharpen a theorem of Sangroniz. The analogous class function for real elements is shown to always give a character. Finally, we give a dual construction that, together with the recently proved McKay Conjecture, yields a best-possible criterion for a finite group to have an abelian Sylow  p p -subgroup. 
  
 
 
 1.  Introduction 

 
 There is a long history of using invariants to unravel the structure of mathematical objects. By a group invariant we mean a function assigning to each finite group  G G  a complex number  I G I_{G}  such that  I G = I H I_{G}=I_{H}  whenever  G ≅ H G\cong H . As nonisomorphic groups could have the same value of an invariant, one would often like to refine, or strengthen, an invariant to obtain more detailed information about group structure. We take up this problem here in the following way: Given an invariant  I G I_{G} , we seek a complex-valued class function  𝐈 G : G → ℂ \mathbf{I}_{G}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}  such that, in a chosen orthonormal basis  { e 1 , … , e n } \{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}  of the space of class functions, the class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  has our invariant  I G I_{G}  as the weight  [ 𝐈 G , e 1 ] [\mathbf{I}_{G},e_{1}]  of its fundamental component  e 1 e_{1} . Such a class function always exists, and the construction need not be unique; indeed,  𝐈 G = I G ​ e 1 \mathbf{I}_{G}=I_{G}\,e_{1}  is a trivial way to construct such a class function. However, even trivial constructions can be interesting, as we will see in Example  2.8 . In general, our goal is to find a natural, interesting or useful class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  satisfying  I G = [ 𝐈 G , e 1 ] I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},e_{1}] . 
 
 
 The idea has an analogy in Fourier analysis and music: If our group  G G  were a tuned drum, then  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  captures its frequency spectrum when struck, where  I G = [ 𝐈 G , e 1 ] I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},e_{1}]  is the weight of the fundamental frequency and the multiplicities  I G e i := [ 𝐈 G , e i ] I_{G}^{e_{i}}:=[\mathbf{I}_{G},e_{i}]  ( i > 1 i>1 ) are the weights of the “higher harmonics”. Seen in this light, one might hope that  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  can discriminate between groups that have the same value of  I G I_{G}  in the same way that some musical instruments, such as a trumpet and a cornet, have the same fundamental frequency but can be distinguished by their distributions of higher frequencies, their so-called timbre. 
 
 
 Given such a class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G} , some natural questions immediately arise: 
 
 item (1)(1)  
 
 Is  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  a (generalized) character? Does there exist some  c ∈ ℂ c\in\mathbb{C}  such that  c ​ 𝐈 G c\mathbf{I}_{G}  is a (generalized) character? 
 
 item (2)(2)  
 
 What can we say about the multiplicities  I G e i I_{G}^{e_{i}}  ( i > 1 i>1 )? For example, when are they nonzero? If they are integers, do they encode any interesting combinatorial information? 
 
 item (3)(3)  
 
 What group structure does  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  capture? For example, what is the relationship between a group  G G  and a subgroup  H ≤ G H\leq G  if  𝐈 G = ( 𝐈 H ) G \mathbf{I}_{G}=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G} ?

Note that if we choose  e 1 e_{1}  to be the trivial character  1 G 1_{G} , then the condition that  𝐈 G = ( 𝐈 H ) G \mathbf{I}_{G}=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G}  in Question 3 is a true refinement of  I G = I H I_{G}=I_{H}  since we have by Frobenius reciprocity that 
 

 
 
 I G = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] = [ ( 𝐈 H ) G , 1 G ] = [ 𝐈 H , 1 H ] = I H . \displaystyle I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}]=[(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G},1_{G}]=[\mathbf{I}_{H},1_{H}]=I_{H}. 
 
 
 We will address Questions (1)–(3) for some invariants  I G I_{G}  and refining class functions  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  in this note. 
 
 
 The layout of the paper is as follows. In Section  2 , we define two natural bases for the space of complex class functions on a group. We then describe some previous results from the literature that can be recast in the class function language. In Sections  3 – 4 , we define some new class functions that refine invariants related to conjugacy classes of a finite group, and we address Questions (1)–(3) above for these class functions. Finally, in Section  5 , we consider a dual construction and prove a best-possible criterion for a finite group to have an abelian Sylow  p p -subgroup using an invariant that is naturally suggested by the construction. 
 
 
 Notation.  Our notation is standard. All groups in this paper are finite. Let  G G  be a group. For each element  x ∈ G x\in G , we denote by  x G = { x g ∣ g ∈ G } x^{G}=\{x^{g}\mid g\in G\}  its  G G -conjugacy class. The collection of conjugacy classes is denoted by  Cl ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{Cl}(G) , and their number is  k ⁡ ( G ) k(G) . We let  x i ∈ G x_{i}\in G  for  i = 1 , … , k ⁡ ( G ) i=1,\dots,k(G)  be representatives for the  G G -conjugacy classes. The collection of irreducible complex characters of  G G  is denoted  Irr ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{Irr}(G) . It is known that  k ⁡ ( G ) = | Irr ⁡ ( G ) | k(G)=|\mathrm{Irr}(G)| . Let  χ 1 \chi_{1}  be the trivial character, which we often denote as  1 G 1_{G} . The irreducible characters are orthonormal with respect to the inner product  [ χ i , χ j ] = ( 1 / | G | ) ​ ∑ g ∈ G χ i ​ ( g ) ​ χ j ​ ( g ) ¯ [\chi_{i},\chi_{j}]=(1/|G|)\sum_{g\in G}\chi_{i}(g)\overline{\chi_{j}(g)} . Let  cf ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{cf}(G)  be the vector space of complex-valued class functions on  G G . Now let  p p  be a prime. The collection of  G G -conjugacy classes consisting of elements of order coprime to  p p , also called  p ′ p^{\prime} -elements or  p p -regular elements, is denoted by  Cl p ′ ​ ( G ) \mathrm{Cl}_{p^{\prime}}(G) . The number of  p p -regular conjugacy classes of  G G  is  k p ′ ​ ( G ) = | Cl p ′ ​ ( G ) | k_{p^{\prime}}(G)=|\mathrm{Cl}_{p^{\prime}}(G)| . Let  IBr p ​ ( G ) \mathrm{IBr}_{p}(G)  be the irreducible  p p -Brauer characters of  G G . Now let  π \pi  be a collection of primes. Let  G π ⊆ G G_{\pi}\subseteq G  denote the set of  π \pi -elements of  G G , let  Cl π ​ ( G ) \mathrm{Cl}_{\pi}(G)  denote the collection of  G G -conjugacy classes of  π \pi -elements, let  | G | π |G|_{\pi}  denote the  π \pi -part of the integer  | G | |G| , and let  k π ​ ( G ) k_{\pi}(G)  be the number of  π \pi -conjugacy classes of  G G . 
 
 
 2.  Examples 

 
 In this section, we give some examples from the literature that can be formulated using the class function language described in the introduction. In our first collection of examples, we choose a basis for the space  cf ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{cf}(G)  of complex class functions on a finite group  G G  consisting of the irreducible ordinary characters  Irr ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{Irr}(G) . 
 
 
 Example 2.1 . 
 
 (Element orders.) The invariant  I G = ∑ g ∈ G | g | I_{G}=\sum_{g\in G}|g|  summing the element orders has been much studied from a group theoretical point of view; see  [ 1 ,  2 ,  17 ,  18 ,  29 ,  30 ,  44 ,  45 ]  and the references therein. Moretó studied the class function defined by  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | G | ​ | g | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|G||g|  for all  g ∈ G g\in G  in  [ 36 ] , noting that  I G = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}] . Moretó proved, among other things, that  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  is a generalized character. 
 
 
 The closely related invariant  I G = ( 1 / | G | ) ​ ∑ g ∈ G | g | I_{G}=(1/|G|)\sum_{g\in G}|g|  calculating the average element order has also been much studied; see  [ 18 ,  25 ,  28 ,  31 ] . The class function defined by  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | g | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|g|  satisfies  I G = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}] . Moretó showed that the smallest positive integer  m m  such that  m ​ 𝐈 G m\mathbf{I}_{G}  is a generalized character is  m = | G | m=|G| . 
 
 
 
 Example 2.2 . 
 
 (Conjugacy classes.)
If  I G = k ⁡ ( G ) I_{G}=k(G) , the number of conjugacy classes of  G G , then the class function defined by  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|  for all  g ∈ G g\in G  satisfies  I G = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}] . This class function is the character, often denoted  Π G \Pi_{G} , of the so-called conjugating representation, which is the linear representation obtained by letting  G G  act by conjugation on its group algebra  ℂ ​ G \mathbb{C}G . The study of the constituents of the conjugating representation has a long history; see  [ 15 ,  16 ]  and the references therein. 
 
 
 The closely related invariant  I G = k ⁡ ( G ) / | G | I_{G}=k(G)/|G|  then arises via the class function  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | / | G | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|/|G| . It has been pointed out by many authors that the invariant  d ⁡ ( G ) = k ⁡ ( G ) / | G | d(G)=k(G)/|G|  is equal to the probability that two randomly chosen elements of  G G  commute; see  [ 10 ,  14 ,  26 ,  27 ] . Many studies have shown that this invariant, often called the commuting probability or commutativity degree, encodes important structural information about the group; see  [ 8 ,  13 ,  14 ,  32 ]  and the references therein. For example, Gustafson proved in  [ 14 ]  that if  d ⁡ ( G ) > 5 / 8 d(G)>5/8 , then  G G  is abelian. 
 
 
 
 Example 2.3 . 
 
 (Permutation characters.)
The last example can be put in a more general context. Note that if  G G  acts on a finite set  Ω \Omega  and  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  is the permutation character, then  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | Fix Ω ​ ( g ) | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|\mathrm{Fix}_{\Omega}(g)| , and so by Burnside’s counting theorem 
 

 
 
 I G = { # of  G -orbits on  Ω } = 1 | G | ​ ∑ g ∈ G | Fix Ω ​ ( g ) | = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] . \displaystyle I_{G}=\{\text{\# of $G$-orbits on $\Omega$}\}=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}|\mathrm{Fix}_{\Omega}(g)|=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}]. 
 
 
 Example  2.2  arises from this construction with  G G  acting on  Ω = G \Omega=G  by conjugation. 
 
 
 Now let  A A  be any finitely-generated group, and let  G G  act on  Ω = Hom ⁡ ( A , G ) \Omega=\mathrm{Hom}(A,G)  by post-composition with conjugation, i.e.  g ⋅ ϕ = g ​ ϕ ​ ( − ) ​ g − 1 g\cdot\phi=g\phi(-)g^{-1} . Then  | Fix Ω ​ ( g ) | = | Hom ⁡ ( A , 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) ) | |\mathrm{Fix}_{\Omega}(g)|=|\mathrm{Hom}(A,\mathbf{C}_{G}(g))| , and so the class function defined by  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | Hom ⁡ ( A , 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) ) | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|\mathrm{Hom}(A,\mathbf{C}_{G}(g))|  for all  g ∈ G g\in G  has the associated invariant 
 

 
 
 I G = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] = { # of  G -orbits on  Hom ⁡ ( A , G ) } . \displaystyle I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}]=\{\text{\# of $G$-orbits on $\mathrm{Hom}(A,G)$}\}. 
 
 
 This gives rise to more examples: 
 
 (1)  
 
 If  A = ℤ A=\mathbb{Z} , then  Hom ⁡ ( A , G ) ≅ G \mathrm{Hom}(A,G)\cong G ,  Hom ⁡ ( A , 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) ) ≅ 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) \mathrm{Hom}(A,\mathbf{C}_{G}(g))\cong\mathbf{C}_{G}(g) , and we recover  I G = k ⁡ ( G ) I_{G}=k(G)  and  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|  for all  g ∈ G g\in G  from Example  2.2 . 
 
 (2)  
 
 If  A = F n A=F_{n}  (the free group on  n n  generators), then  Hom ⁡ ( A , G ) ≅ G n \mathrm{Hom}(A,G)\cong G^{n} ,  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | n \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|^{n}  and  I G I_{G}  is the number of conjugacy classes of  n n -tuples under simultaneous conjugation. 
 
 (3)  
 
 If  A = ℤ n A=\mathbb{Z}^{n} , then  Hom ⁡ ( A , G ) \mathrm{Hom}(A,G)  is the set of pairwise-commuting  n n -tuples in  G G , and  G G  acts on these tuples by simultaneous conjugation. So  I G I_{G}  is the number of conjugacy classes of  n n -tuples with pairwise-commuting elements. 
 
 
 The invariant in (3) was considered by Levit and Shwartz in their investigations of the so-called “higher commutativity”  [ 33 ,  34 ] . Commuting  n n -tuples were also considered by Erdős and Straus  [ 9 ] . 
 
 
 
 Example 2.4 . 
 
 (Adams operations.) Given an irreducible character  χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) \chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)  of a finite group  G G , the class function  𝐈 χ , G = χ ( 2 ) \mathbf{I}_{\chi,G}=\chi^{(2)}  defined by  χ ( 2 ) ​ ( g ) = χ ⁡ ( g 2 ) \chi^{(2)}(g)=\chi(g^{2})  for all  g ∈ G g\in G  gives rise to the so-called Frobenius–Schur indicator of  χ \chi ,  I χ , G = [ 𝐈 χ , G , 1 G ] = ( 1 / | G | ) ​ ∑ g ∈ G χ ⁡ ( g 2 ) I_{\chi,G}=[\mathbf{I}_{\chi,G},1_{G}]=(1/|G|)\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{2}) . See  [ 22 , Chapter 4]  for more information about this classical topic. 
 
 
 Recently, Boltje, Kleshchev, Navarro and Tiep proved a reduction of the Feit Conjecture to a local problem on finite simple groups  [ 3 ] . The authors also formulated a stronger form of the conjecture  [ 3 , Conjecture E]  which is connected to an integer-valued invariant  S ⁡ ( G , χ , n ) S(G,\chi,n)  studied by Boltje and Navarro in  [ 4 ] , where  G G  is a finite group,  χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) \chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)  and  n n  is a positive integer. One way to construct this invariant is as the multiplicity of the trivial character in a generalized character constructed from a particular integral linear combination of Adams operations  Ψ k ​ ( χ ) \Psi^{k}(\chi)  of  χ \chi , where  Ψ k ​ ( χ ) ​ ( g ) = χ ⁡ ( g k ) \Psi^{k}(\chi)(g)=\chi(g^{k})  for all  g ∈ G g\in G   [ 4 , Theorem B] . Note that  Ψ 2 ​ ( χ ) = χ ( 2 ) \Psi^{2}(\chi)=\chi^{(2)}  from the last paragraph. 
 
 
 
 Example 2.5 . 
 
 (An example of Frobenius.) Frobenius considered the following example, which is described in  [ 21 , V.19.13–V.19.14] . Let  K K  be a conjugacy class of a finite group  G G , let  n n  be a divisor of  | G | |G| , and let  t t  be the number of elements  g ∈ G g\in G  such that  g n ∈ K g^{n}\in K . The rational number  I G = t / n I_{G}=t/n  is an invariant of the group  G G . Now, if we define the class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  on  G G  by  𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = | G | / n \mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=|G|/n  if  g n ∈ K g^{n}\in K  and  0 0  otherwise, then we have 
 

 
 
 [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] = 1 | G | ​ ∑ g ∈ G 𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = t n = I G . \displaystyle[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}]=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=\frac{t}{n}=I_{G}. 
 
 
 In fact, the class function perspective gives interesting information about the invariant  I G I_{G}  in this case: Our class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  can be expressed as a linear combination of irreducible characters with coefficients in  ℂ \mathbb{C} . However, it is shown in  [ 21 , Theorem V.19.13]  that the coefficients are actually algebraic integers lying in  ℤ ⁡ [ ϵ ] \mathbb{Z}[\epsilon] , where  ϵ \epsilon  is a primitive  | G | |G| -th root of unity. In particular, the coefficient  [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] = I G [\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}]=I_{G}  is an algebraic integer. So,  I G I_{G}  is a rational number and an algebraic integer, which implies that  I G I_{G}  is an integer. This is not at all obvious from the definition. 
 
 
 
 The next example will be one of the main concerns of this paper. We will consider it in detail in Sections  3  and  4 . 
 
 
 Example 2.6 . 
 
 (“Local” conjugating character.)
There has been much interest in a so-called “ π \pi -local” version of the invariant  d ⁡ ( G ) d(G)  from Example  2.2 ; the invariant  d π ​ ( G ) = k π ​ ( G ) / | G | π d_{\pi}(G)=k_{\pi}(G)/|G|_{\pi}  seems to capture much information about the  π \pi -structure of  G G   [ 19 ,  20 ,  46 ] . In particular, if  π = p ′ \pi=p^{\prime}  for some prime  p p , then  d p ′ ​ ( G ) d_{p^{\prime}}(G)  captures information about the modular representations of  G G  over a field of characteristic  p p   [ 41 ] . Sangroniz characterized when  d p ′ ​ ( G ) = d p ′ ​ ( H ) d_{p^{\prime}}(G)=d_{p^{\prime}}(H)  representation-theoretically for  H H  a subgroup of a  p p -solvable group  G G  in  [ 40 , Theorem 3.3] . 
 
 
 Now we define the class function  Π π , G \Pi_{\pi,G}  by 
 

 
 
 Π π , G ​ ( g ) = { | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | if  g ∈ G  is a  π -element 0 otherwise . \displaystyle\Pi_{\pi,G}(g)=\begin{cases}|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|&\text{if $g\in G$ is a $\pi$-element}\\

 We also define the scaled class function  Δ π , G = Π π , G / | G | π \Delta_{\pi,G}=\Pi_{\pi,G}/|G|_{\pi} . A straightforward calculation shows that  [ Π π , G , 1 G ] = k π ​ ( G ) [\Pi_{\pi,G},1_{G}]=k_{\pi}(G)  and  [ Δ π , G , 1 G ] = d π ​ ( G ) [\Delta_{\pi,G},1_{G}]=d_{\pi}(G) . 
 
 
 We also mention Robinson’s closely related generalized character defined by  Ψ 1 , p , G ​ ( g ) = | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) p | \Psi_{1,p,G}(g)=|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)_{p}| , the number of  p p -elements in  𝐂 G ​ ( g ) \mathbf{C}_{G}(g) , if  g ∈ G g\in G  is  p p -regular and  0 0  otherwise  [ 39 ] . Robinson also defines a  π \pi -variant in  [ 39 , Section 8] . 
 
 
 
 Example 2.7 . 
 
 (Local class functions.)
The last example can also be put in a more general context. If  X ⊆ G X\subseteq G  is any subset closed under conjugation by  G G , then we may define 
 

 
 
 Π X , G ​ ( g ) = { | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | if  g ∈ X 0 otherwise . \displaystyle\Pi_{X,G}(g)=\begin{cases}|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|&\text{if $g\in X$}\\

 Then  [ Π X , G , 1 G ] [\Pi_{X,G},1_{G}]  is simply the number of conjugacy classes in  X X . If  X = G π X=G_{\pi} , then we recover  Π π , G \Pi_{\pi,G}  from Example  2.6 . If  X X  is the set of all real, resp. rational, elements of  G G , then  [ Π X , G , 1 G ] [\Pi_{X,G},1_{G}]  is the number of real, resp. rational, classes. We will consider this class function in more detail in Section  4 . 
 
 
 
 To conclude this section, we consider a basis for the space of class functions consisting of characteristic functions, relaxing the condition that the basis vectors be normalized to  1 1 : The collection of class functions  x i ^ \widehat{x_{i}}  for  i = 1 , … , k ⁡ ( G ) i=1,\dots,k(G)  defined by  x i ^ ​ ( g ) = 1 \widehat{x_{i}}(g)=1  if  g ∈ x i G g\in x_{i}^{G}  and  0 0  otherwise is clearly a basis of  cf ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{cf}(G) . It is an orthogonal but not orthonormal basis with respect to the usual inner product, however, since  [ x i ^ , x j ^ ] = δ i ​ j ​ | x i G | / | G | [\widehat{x_{i}},\widehat{x_{j}}]=\delta_{ij}|x_{i}^{G}|/|G| . Let  1 ^ G \widehat{1}_{G}  denote the characteristic function of the  G G -conjugacy class consisting of the identity. Note that  [ 1 ^ G , 1 ^ G ] = 1 / | G | [\widehat{1}_{G},\widehat{1}_{G}]=1/|G| . We now reformulate a result of Sangroniz in the language of class functions using this basis. 
 
 
 Example 2.8 . 
 
 ( p p -regular classes.)
Consider the group invariant  I G = k p ′ ​ ( G ) / | G | I_{G}=k_{p^{\prime}}(G)/|G| . Sangroniz characterized representation-theoretically when  I G = I H I_{G}=I_{H}  for  H ≤ G H\leq G  in  [ 40 , Theorem 2.1] . Now define the class function  𝐈 G = k p ′ ​ ( G ) ​ 1 ^ G \mathbf{I}_{G}=k_{p^{\prime}}(G)\;\widehat{1}_{G}  for a finite group  G G , so that  I G = [ 𝐈 G , 1 ^ G ] I_{G}=[\mathbf{I}_{G},\widehat{1}_{G}] . We can recast Sangroniz’ theorem in the following form using class functions: 
 
 
 Theorem 2.9 . 
 
 [ 40 , Theorem 2.1] 
Let  H ≤ G H\leq G , and let  p p  be a prime. The following are equivalent: 
 
 (i)  
 
 𝐈 G = ( 𝐈 H ) G \mathbf{I}_{G}=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G} . 
 
 (ii)  
 
 Restriction is a surjection  IBr p ​ ( G ) → IBr p ​ ( H ) \mathrm{IBr}_{p}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{IBr}_{p}(H)  and  [ G : H ] [G:H]  is not divisible by  p p . 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
(i)  ⇒ \Rightarrow  (ii): If  𝐈 G = ( 𝐈 H ) G \mathbf{I}_{G}=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G} , then  k p ′ ( G ) = 𝐈 G ( 1 ) = ( 𝐈 H ) G ( 1 ) = [ G : H ] k p ′ ( H ) k_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\mathbf{I}_{G}(1)=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G}(1)=[G:H]k_{p^{\prime}}(H) ; that is,  I G = I H I_{G}=I_{H} . By  [ 40 , Theorem 2.1] , this means that  [ G : H ] [G:H]  is not divisible by  p p  and every irreducible  p p -Brauer character of  G G  restricts irreducibly to  H H , so restriction defines a map  IBr p ​ ( G ) → IBr p ​ ( H ) \mathrm{IBr}_{p}(G)\rightarrow\mathrm{IBr}_{p}(H) . Since every irreducible Brauer character of  H H  lies under an irreducible Brauer character of  G G  by  [ 37 , Corollary 8.3] , we have the desired surjection. 
 
 
 (ii)  ⇒ \Rightarrow  (i): Since all Brauer characters restrict irreducibly and  p ∤ [ G : H ] p\nmid[G:H] , we have  𝐈 G ( 1 ) = k p ′ ( G ) = [ G : H ] k p ′ ( H ) = ( 𝐈 H ) G ( 1 ) \mathbf{I}_{G}(1)=k_{p^{\prime}}(G)=[G:H]k_{p^{\prime}}(H)=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G}(1)  by  [ 40 , Theorem 2.1] . This completely characterizes the equality  𝐈 G = ( 𝐈 H ) G \mathbf{I}_{G}=(\mathbf{I}_{H})^{G}  since both sides vanish off the identity. ∎ 
 
 
 
 3.  The local conjugating character 

 
 In this section, we consider Questions (1) and (2) of the introduction for the “ π \pi -local” conjugating character  Π π , G \Pi_{\pi,G}  and  Δ π , G \Delta_{\pi,G}  from Example  2.6 . We consider Question (3) in the next section. Note that  Π π , G \Pi_{\pi,G}  interpolates between the character  Π G \Pi_{G}  of the conjugating representation and the character  ρ G \rho_{G}  of the regular representation: If  G G  is a  π \pi -group then  Π π , G = Π G \Pi_{\pi,G}=\Pi_{G} , while if  G G  is a  π ′ \pi^{\prime} -group then  Π π , G = ρ G \Pi_{\pi,G}=\rho_{G} .

 Remark 3.4 . 
 
 One can show that the row sums in the ordinary character table are nonnegative integers using the conjugating character  [ 22 , Problem 5.13] . In a nicely parallel result, the proof of Theorem  3.3  shows that the row sums in the  p p -Brauer character table are nonnegative integers by using the “local” conjugating character  Π p ′ , G \Pi_{p^{\prime},G} . This result sharpens  [ 7 , Proposition 2.2(b)] , where it was shown that row sums in the  p p -Brauer table are nonnegative real numbers. 
 
 
 
 Supported by Theorem  3.3  and computational evidence, we state the following conjecture, which would answer a question of Robinson in  [ 39 , Section 8] . 
 
 
 Conjecture 3.5 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a finite group, and let  π \pi  be an arbitrary collection of primes. Then  Π π , G \Pi_{\pi,G}  is a character of  G G ; that is,  [ Π π , G , χ ] ≥ 0 [\Pi_{\pi,G},\chi]\geq 0  for all  χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) \chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G) .

Then  IBr p ​ ( G ) = Irr ​ ( G ) \mathrm{IBr}_{p}(G)=\mathrm{Irr}(G)  by  [ 37 , Theorem 2.12] , and the bijection implies that  | G | = ∑ χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) χ H ​ ( 1 ) 2 = | H | |G|=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi_{H}(1)^{2}=|H| .
∎ 
 
 
 
 To conclude this section, we show that the class function  Π X , G \Pi_{X,G}  is always a character when  X X  is the collection of real elements of  G G ; that is,  X X  consists of all elements of  G G  that are conjugate to their inverse. 
 
 
 Theorem 4.6 . 
 
 Let  G G  be an arbitrary finite group, and let  R ⊆ G R\subseteq G  be the collection of real elements of  G G . Then  Π R , G \Pi_{R,G}  is a character of  G G . 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Recall that the second orthogonality relation  [ 22 , Theorem 2.18]  applied to the elements  g , g − 1 ∈ G g,g^{-1}\in G  states that 
 

 
 
 ∑ χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) χ ⁡ ( g ) ​ χ ⁡ ( g − 1 ) ¯ = { | 𝐂 G ​ ( g ) | if  g  is conjugate to  g − 1 0 otherwise . = Π R , G ​ ( g ) . \displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi(g)\overline{\chi(g^{-1})}=\begin{cases}|\mathbf{C}_{G}(g)|&\text{if $g$ is conjugate to $g^{-1}$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}\;=\;\Pi_{R,G}(g). 
 
 
 Therefore,  Π R , G ​ ( g ) = ∑ χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) χ 2 ​ ( g ) \Pi_{R,G}(g)=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}\chi^{2}(g) . Since products of characters are characters (see  [ 22 , Corollary 4.2] ),  Π R , G \Pi_{R,G}  is also a character.
∎ 
 
 
 
 5.  A dual construction 

 
 Up to now, we have extended a group invariant  I G I_{G}  to a class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  by encoding the original invariant  I G I_{G}  in the sum of values normalized by  | G | |G| ; that is,  ( 1 / | G | ) ​ ∑ g ∈ G 𝐈 G ​ ( g ) = [ 𝐈 G , 1 G ] = I G (1/|G|)\sum_{g\in G}\mathbf{I}_{G}(g)=[\mathbf{I}_{G},1_{G}]=I_{G} . We now consider a dual construction in which an invariant  I G I_{G}  is encoded in a class function  𝐈 G \mathbf{I}_{G}  as the sum of multiplicities of the irreducible characters normalized by  | G | |G| , namely  I G = ( 1 / | G | ) ​ ∑ χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) [ 𝐈 G , χ ] I_{G}=(1/|G|)\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}[\mathbf{I}_{G},\chi] . We consider one interesting example of this dual construction. For a prime  p p , define the class function (character)  𝐃 p ′ ​ ( G ) = ∑ p ∤ χ ⁡ ( 1 ) χ \mathbf{D}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\sum_{p\nmid\chi(1)}\chi , where the sum is taken over all irreducible characters of a finite group  G G  whose degree is not divisible by  p p . This gives rise to an invariant 
 

 
 
 D p ′ ​ ( G ) = 1 | G | ​ ∑ χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G ) [ 𝐃 p ′ ​ ( G ) , χ ] = | Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) | | G | . \displaystyle D_{p^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)}[\mathbf{D}_{p^{\prime}}(G),\chi]=\frac{|\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|}{|G|}. 
 
 
 
 
 We first prove a bound for  D p ′ ​ ( G ) D_{p^{\prime}}(G) . In the proof, we use the recently-proved McKay Conjecture  [ 5 ] . 
 
 
 Theorem 5.1 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a finite group, let  p p  be a prime, and let  P P  be a Sylow  p p -subgroup of  G G . By Schur–Zassenhaus, we may write a Sylow  p p -normalizer as  𝐍 G ​ ( P ) = P ⋊ H \mathbf{N}_{G}(P)=P\rtimes H . We have 
 

 
 
 D p ′ ( G ) ≤ 1 | P ′ | × 1 [ H : 𝐂 H ( P / P ′ ) ] . \displaystyle D_{p^{\prime}}(G)\leq\frac{1}{|P^{\prime}|}\times\frac{1}{[H:\mathbf{C}_{H}(P/P^{\prime})]}. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 Denote the quantity on the right-hand side of the inequality in the statement of the theorem by  b ⁡ ( G ) b(G) . Suppose for contradiction that the theorem is false. Let  G G  be a counterexample of minimal order, so  b ​ ( G ) < D p ′ ​ ( G ) b(G)<D_{p^{\prime}}(G) . We have  | Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) | = | Irr p ′ ​ ( 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ) | |\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=|\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))|  by the McKay Conjecture, so 
 

 
 
 b ⁡ ( G ) < | Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) | | G | = | Irr p ′ ​ ( 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ) | | G | ≤ | Irr p ′ ​ ( 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ) | | 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) | = D p ′ ​ ( 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ) . \displaystyle b(G)<\frac{|\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|}{|G|}=\frac{|\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))|}{|G|}\leq\frac{|\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))|}{|\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)|}=D_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)). 
 
 
 If  | 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) | < | G | |\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)|<|G| , then  𝐍 G ​ ( P ) \mathbf{N}_{G}(P)  satisfies the conclusion of the theorem by the minimality of  | G | |G| . But this is a contradiction since  b ⁡ ( G ) = b ⁡ ( 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ) b(G)=b(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))  depends only on the local subgroup  𝐍 G ​ ( P ) \mathbf{N}_{G}(P) . So we are left to consider the case that  𝐍 G ​ ( P ) = G \mathbf{N}_{G}(P)=G . Now, every  χ ∈ Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) \chi\in\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)  restricted to  P P  must be a sum of linear characters of  P P  by Clifford’s theorem, so  P ′ ≤ ker ⁡ χ P^{\prime}\leq\ker\chi . This means we may consider  χ \chi  a character of  G / P ′ G/P^{\prime} , and so  | Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) | ≤ | Irr ⁡ ( G / P ′ ) | |\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|\leq|\mathrm{Irr}(G/P^{\prime})| . Conversely, since  P / P ′ P/P^{\prime}  is an abelian normal subgroup of  G / P ′ G/P^{\prime} , we have for all  χ ∈ Irr ⁡ ( G / P ′ ) \chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G/P^{\prime})  that  χ ⁡ ( 1 ) \chi(1)  divides  [ G / P ′ : P / P ′ ] = [ G : P ] [G/P^{\prime}:P/P^{\prime}]=[G:P]  by Itô’s Theorem  [ 22 , Theorem 6.15] . So lifting  χ \chi  to a character of  G G  yields an irreducible character of  p ′ p^{\prime} -degree. Hence,  | Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) | = | Irr ⁡ ( G / P ′ ) | = k ⁡ ( G / P ′ ) |\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=|\mathrm{Irr}(G/P^{\prime})|=k(G/P^{\prime}) . 
 
 
 Now we work in the quotient  G / P ′ = G ¯ = P ¯ ⋊ H ¯ G/P^{\prime}=\overline{G}=\overline{P}\rtimes\overline{H} , which has a normal, abelian Sylow  p p -subgroup  P ¯ \overline{P} . Note that  Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) ​ ⊴ ​ G ¯ \Phi(\overline{P})\trianglelefteq\overline{G} , being characteristic in  P ¯ ​ ⊴ ​ G ¯ \overline{P}\trianglelefteq\overline{G} . Moreover,  C H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) ​ ⊴ ​ G ¯ C_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P})\trianglelefteq\overline{G} : it is normalized by  H ¯ \overline{H}  since  P ¯ ​ ⊴ ​ G ¯ \overline{P}\trianglelefteq\overline{G} , and it is centralized, hence normalized, by  P ¯ \overline{P} ; so it is normal in  G ¯ = P ¯ ​ H ¯ \overline{G}=\overline{P}\,\overline{H} . Recall that if  N ¯ ​ ⊴ ​ G ¯ \overline{N}\trianglelefteq\overline{G} , then  k ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≤ k ⁡ ( N ¯ ) ​ k ​ ( G / N ¯ ) k(\overline{G})\leq k(\overline{N})k(\overline{G/N})  by  [ 11 ] . Letting  N ¯ = Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) ​ 𝐂 H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) \overline{N}=\Phi(\overline{P})\mathbf{C}_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P})  so that  | N ¯ | = | Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) | ​ | 𝐂 H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) | |\overline{N}|=|\Phi(\overline{P})||\mathbf{C}_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P})| , these being a  p p -group and a  p ′ p^{\prime} -group, and using the obvious inequality  k ⁡ ( N ¯ ) ≤ | N ¯ | k(\overline{N})\leq|\overline{N}| , we then have 
 

 
 
 k ⁡ ( G ¯ ) ≤ | 𝐂 H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) | ⋅ | Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) | ⋅ k ⁡ ( P ¯ / Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) ⋊ H ¯ / 𝐂 H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) ) . \displaystyle k(\overline{G})\leq|\mathbf{C}_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P})|\cdot|\Phi(\overline{P})|\cdot k(\overline{P}/\Phi(\overline{P})\rtimes\overline{H}/\mathbf{C}_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P})). 
 
 
 Now, the coprime action of  H ¯ / 𝐂 H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) \overline{H}/\mathbf{C}_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P})  on the elementary abelian group  P ¯ / Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) \overline{P}/\Phi(\overline{P})  is faithful by a coprime action theorem  [ 23 , Corollary 3.30] ; so  k ⁡ ( P ¯ / Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) ⋊ H ¯ / 𝐂 H ¯ ​ ( P ¯ ) ) ≤ | P ¯ / Φ ⁡ ( P ¯ ) | k(\overline{P}/\Phi(\overline{P})\rtimes\overline{H}/\mathbf{C}_{\overline{H}}(\overline{P}))\leq|\overline{P}/\Phi(\overline{P})|  by the  k ⁡ ( G ​ V ) k(GV)  theorem  [ 12 ] . Additionally,  H ¯ ≅ H \overline{H}\cong H  since  P ′ ∩ H = 1 P^{\prime}\cap H=1 . Altogether, we then finally have that 
 

 
 
 | Irr p ′ ​ ( G ) | = k ⁡ ( G / P ′ ) ≤ | 𝐂 H ​ ( P / P ′ ) | ​ | P / P ′ | . \displaystyle|\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=k(G/P^{\prime})\leq|\mathbf{C}_{H}(P/P^{\prime})|\,|P/P^{\prime}|. 
 
 
 But then upon dividing through by  | G | = | P | ​ | H | |G|=|P||H| , we find that 
 

 
 
 D p ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ | P / P ′ | ​ | 𝐂 H ​ ( P / P ′ ) | | P | ​ | H | = b ⁡ ( G ) , \displaystyle D_{p^{\prime}}(G)\leq\frac{|P/P^{\prime}||\mathbf{C}_{H}(P/P^{\prime})|}{|P||H|}=b(G), 
 
 
 a contradiction.
∎ 
 
 
 
 As a corollary, we deduce a criterion for detecting abelian Sylow  p p -subgroups. 
 
 
 Corollary 5.2 . 
 
 Let  G G  be a finite group,  p p  a prime and  P P  a Sylow  p p -subgroup of  G G . If  D p ′ ​ ( G ) > 1 / p D_{p^{\prime}}(G)>1/p , then  P P  is abelian. If, in addition,  p p  is the smallest prime dividing  | G | |G| , then  G G  has a normal  p p -complement. 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 By Theorem  5.1 ,  1 / p < D p ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ b ⁡ ( G ) 1/p<D_{p^{\prime}}(G)\leq b(G) ; that is,  p > | P ′ | [ H : 𝐂 H ( P / P ′ ) ] p>|P^{\prime}|[H:\mathbf{C}_{H}(P/P^{\prime})] . Therefore,  | P ′ | = 1 |P^{\prime}|=1  and  [ H : 𝐂 H ( P ) ] < p [H:\mathbf{C}_{H}(P)]<p . In particular,  P P  is abelian. If, in addition,  p p  is the smallest prime dividing  | G | |G| , then  [ H : 𝐂 H ( P ) ] = 1 [H:\mathbf{C}_{H}(P)]=1 . Therefore,  P ≤ 𝐙 ​ ( 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) ) P\leq\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)) , and so  G G  has a normal  p p -complement by Burnside’s transfer theorem  [ 23 , Theorem 5.13] .
∎ 
 
 
 
 This criterion is best possible since an extraspecial  p p -group  P P  of order  p 1 + 2 ​ m p^{1+2m}  has  p 2 ​ m p^{2m}  representations of degree  1 1  and  p − 1 p-1  irreducible representations of degree  p m p^{m} . Therefore,  | Irr p ′ ​ ( P ) | = p 2 ​ m |\mathrm{Irr}_{p^{\prime}}(P)|=p^{2m} , so  D p ′ ​ ( P ) = 1 / p D_{p^{\prime}}(P)=1/p  and  P P  is not abelian.
