                                          Abstract
                                          Let G be a graph with maximum degree ∆ and without isolated vertices. An edge colour-
                                          ing c of G is conﬂict-free if the closed neighbourhood of every edge includes a uniquely
                                          coloured element. The least number of colours admitting such c is the conﬂict-free chro-
                                          matic index of G, denoted by χ′CF (G). In “Conflict-free chromatic number versus conflict-
                                          free chromatic index ” [J. Graph Theory, 2022; 99: 349–358] it was recently proved by
                                          means of the probabilistic method that χ′CF (G) ¬ C1 log2 ∆ + C2 , where C1 > 337 and
                                          C2 are constants, whereas there are families of graphs with χ′CF (G) ­ (1 − o(1)) log2 ∆.
                                          In this note we provide an explicit simple proof of the fact that χ′CF (G) ¬ 3 log2 ∆ + 1,
                                          which is a corollary of a stronger result: χ′CF (G) ¬ 3 log2 χ(G) + 1. For this aim we
                                          prove a few auxiliary observations, implying in particular that χ′CF (G) ¬ 4 for bipartite
                                          graphs.
                                          Keywords: conﬂict-free colouring, conﬂict-free chromatic index, conﬂict-free chromatic
                                          number

argument implying that χ′CF (G) ¬ 3 log2 ∆ + 1. In fact we shall prove something even

For this purpose we study bipartite graphs ﬁrst and a few related issues.
    The problem above grew out of its earlier variant concerning vertex colourings. The
naturally deﬁned graph parameter in vertex setting is called the conflict-free chromatic
number and denoted χCF (G). In fact investigating χ′CF (G) goes down to study of χCF (G)
for the family of line graphs, cf. [3]. Originally the concept of χCF (G) was motivated by
channel assignment in wireless networks, where colours represent available frequencies,
which potentially interfere, and thus one should always have in range a transmitter with
a unique frequency associated, see e.g. [4, 9, 10]. In 2009 Pach and Tardos [8] showed that
χCF (G) = O(ln2+ǫ ∆). This was later improved to χCF (G) = O(ln2 ∆) by Bhyravarapu,
Kalyanasundaram and Mathew [2], while Glebov, Szabó and Tardos [5] constructed a
family of graphs with χCF (G) = Ω(ln2 ∆), legitimating the optimal order of the bound
from [2]. See also [1, 6, 7] for other related results.

2. Results

    A palette including just one colour shall be called monochromatic. Moreover, ginen
a graph G = (V, E) and an edge uv (which does not have to belong to E), we set
G + uv := (V ∪ {u, v}, E ∪ {uv}).
    In [3] it was in particular proved that χ′CF (T ) ¬ 3 for every tree T . Here we rephrase
this result in order to emphasize some detailed features of the colouring implying this
upper bound. By levels of a tree T rooted at r we mean L0 , L1 , L2 , . . . such that Li =
{v ∈ V (T ) : dist(v, r) = i}, where dist(v, r) denotes the distance of v and r in T . If
dT (r) = 1, i.e. r is a leaf, we refer to its only incident edge as the rooted edge. A tree of
order 1 is called trivial.

    We may now prove our main result.

Theorem 6. For every graph G without isolated vertices, χ′sCF (G) ¬ 3⌈log2 χ(G)⌉.

Proof. Let G = (V, E). We prove the theorem by induction. If χ(G) = 2, then the
assertion follows by Theorem 4. Let us thus assume that χ(G) ­ 3 and that the assertion
holds for graphs with smaller chromatic numbers. Colour the vertices of G properly with
χ(G) colours and let Vi denote the vertices coloured with ith colour, 1 ¬ i ¬ χ(G). Set
Vi = ∅ for χ(G) < i ¬ 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ , if any; set k := 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉−1 . Consider a bipartite
subgraph H induced by all edges between A := V1 ∪ . . . ∪ Vk and B := Vk+1 ∪ . . . ∪ V2k
in G and let F be the graph induced by all the remaining edges of G (we do not include
potential isolated vertices in the vertex sets of H or F ). Note that χ(F ) ¬ k, as we may
in a way identify colours of the vertices in Vi and Vk+i for 1 ¬ i ¬ k because there are
no edges between these vertices in F (these were included in F ).
    Since k < χ(G), by induction hypothesis, χ′sCF (G) ¬ 3⌈log2 χ(F )⌉ ¬ 3⌈log2 k⌉ =
3⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ − 3. There thus exists a subgraph of F and its edge colouring with at
most 3⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ − 3 colours which satisﬁes all edges of F . On the other hand, as H is
bipartite, by Theorem 4 there exists a colouring of a subgraph of H with 3 additional
colours which satisﬁes all edges in H. The concatenation of the both colourings implies
the theorem.                                                                             

Corollary 9. For every graph G without isolated vertices, χ′CF (G) ¬ 3⌈log2 ∆(G)⌉ + 1.

Proof. We may assume G is connected. Due to Brook’s Theorem the result follows
directly by Corollary 7 for all graphs except cycles and complete graphs. The remaining
cases follow by Observations 2 and 8.                                                

3. Concluding remarks

   By Corollary 5 and Observation 2 we know that the optimal general upper bound for
the conﬂict-free chromatic index within the family of bipartite graphs is either 3 or 4.
We propose the following conjecture.
Conjecture 10. If G is a bipartite graph without isolated vertices, then χ′CF (G) ¬ 3.
Note that even settling this conjecture in the aﬃrmative might not provide a signiﬁcant
improvement of the general bound in Corollary 7. This would decrease merely by 1 on
the basis of our approach utilized in the proof of Theorem 6, unless we knew something
speciﬁc on the corresponding colourings of bipartite graphs, e.g. implying that.χ′sCF (G) ¬
2 for these.
    We believe that it would also be interesting to solve the following problem

Problem 11. Characterize the family of all trees T with χ′CF (T ) = 3.
