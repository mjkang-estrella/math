4      Anti-Ld(2, 1)-labeling of graphs

Given a simple graph G = (V, E) and a positive number d, an Ld (2, 1)-labeling of G is a function
f : V (G) → [0, ∞) such that whenever x, y ∈ V are adjacent, if |f (x)−f (Y )| ≥ 2d, and whenever
the distance between x and y is two, if |f (x) − f (Y )| ≥ d. The Ld (2, 1)-labeling number λ(G, d)
is the smallest number m such that G has an Ld (2, 1)-labeling f with max{f (v) : v ∈ V } = m.
When d = 1, the Ld (2, 1)-labeling problem is the L(2, 1)-labeling problem. The L(2, 1)-labeling
problem of graphs has been discussed for many graph families, see [2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9].
