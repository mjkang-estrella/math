 Abstract. 
    
 We study rank-2 cluster scattering diagrams through moduli spaces of quiver representations and a recently developed combinatorial framework of tight gradings. Combining quiver-theoretic and combinatorial methods, we prove and extend a collection of conjectures posed by Elgin–Reading–Stella concerning the structural and enumerative properties of the wall-function coefficients. The tight grading perspective also provides a new proof of the Weyl group symmetry of the scattering diagram. 
  
 
 
 Key words and phrases:  scattering diagram, quiver moduli, tight grading, cluster algebra, Weyl symmetry

 1.  Introduction 

 
 1.1.  Background and motivation 

 
 Scattering diagrams  [ 16 ,  14 ]  are combinatorial structures encoding wall-crossing phenomena arising in various sources of mathematics. Cluster scattering diagrams of Gross–Hacking–Keel–Kontsevich  [ 11 ]  are a special class attached to cluster algebras/varieties  [ 9 ,  8 ] , where the wall-functions reflect their mutation structure and positivity properties. In rank 2, they are closely related to the  tropical vertex  of Gross–Pandharipande–Siebert, where the wall-functions are expressed through generating series of relative Gromov-Witten invariants of toric surfaces  [ 13 ]  and Euler characteristics of quiver moduli  [ 22 ,  23 ] . 
 
 
 From the cluster algebra perspective, the coefficients of rank-2 wall-functions lead to
the Laurent positivity in rank-2 cluster algebras  𝒜 ⁡ ( b , c ) \mathcal{A}(b,c)  of Fomin–Zelevinsky  [ 27 ] . This positivity admits both combinatorial descriptions via compatible gradings using edges in Dyck paths  [ 18 ,  17 ]  and more geometric ones via scattering diagrams  [ 11 ,  15 ] . 
 
 
 Based on extensive computational evidence, Elgin, Reading, and Stella  [ 7 ]  formulated a collection of conjectures regarding the wall-function coefficients  τ ⁡ ( i , j ) \tau(i,j)  of the cluster scattering diagram  𝔇 ( b , c ) \mathfrak{D}_{(b,c)}  associated with  𝒜 ⁡ ( b , c ) \mathcal{A}(b,c) . These conjectures, which primarily concern the dependence of  τ ⁡ ( i , j ) \tau(i,j)  on the positive integers  b b  and  c c , form the starting point of the present work. Tight gradings, recently introduced in  [ 4 ,  5 ] , refine the framework of compatible gradings  [ 17 ,  24 ] , and provide a unified combinatorial model for these coefficients.
Our approach uses both quiver representation techniques and the combinatorics of tight gradings to confirm many of these predictions and to derive further generalizations.

 Both  λ ⁡ ( 𝐏 1 , 𝐏 2 ) \lambda(\mathbf{P}_{1},\mathbf{P}_{2})  and  τ k \tau_{k}  admit enumerative interpretations in terms of  tight gradings ; see  Section   4  for a detailed treatment. Tight gradings were introduced in  [ 4 ,  5 ]  to give a combinatorial interpretation of rank- 2 2  scattering diagram coefficients. Each tight grading is assignment of nonnegative integer weights to the edges of a Dyck path satisfying certain conditions (see  Definition   4.8 ). They form a subset of the compatible gradings defined by Lee–Li–Zelevinsky  [ 17 ]  (and extended by Rupel  [ 24 ] ) to study the  greedy bases  of (generalized) cluster algebras. The conditions under which a compatible grading is tight are directly motivated by rank- 2 2  cluster scattering diagrams. Thus tight gradings serve as a bridge between the Lee–Schiffler proof of Laurent positivity using Dyck path combinatorics  [ 19 ]  and the Gross–Hacking–Keel–Kontsevich proof via scattering diagrams  [ 11 ] . 
 
 
 The interpretation of  λ ⁡ ( 𝐏 1 , 𝐏 2 ) \lambda(\mathbf{P}_{1},\mathbf{P}_{2})  and  τ k \tau_{k}  using tight gradings plays an important role in our analysis in  Section   6 . In  Section   5 , we give a new description of tight gradings as arrangements of rectangular tiles, called  footprints . A similar tiling interpretation was outlined in  [ 5 ] , but the footprint framework admits more tiles and we believe better captures the relative “tightness” of tight gradings. 
 
 
 Quiver moduli are algebraic varieties parametrizing isomorphism classes of stable representations of quivers. Despite being interesting in their own right for encoding hard representation-theoretic classification problems, it turned out that their topological invariants encode enumerative invariants such as Gromov-Witten and Donaldson-Thomas invariants. A typical example is the refined GW/Kronecker correspondence  [ 23 ] : both types of geometric invariants are encoded in the so-called tropical vertex, now called a rank  2 2  (generalized) cluster scattering diagram (see the following Section  2 ). This allows us to translate the above conjectures into statements on Euler characteristics of quiver moduli spaces, and to readily apply results obtained in  [ 23 ]  (in fact, we will therefore never need the precise definition of (stable) quiver representations and their moduli spaces). This method is particularly helpful in obtaining closed formulas for  τ ⁡ ( i , j ) \tau(i,j) , as in  C5  through  C10 . In particular, Conjectures  C7  and  C8  above are equivalent to a conjecture of Gross and Pandharipande  [ 12 , Conjecture (1,4)] , proved in  [ 23 , Theorem 9.4] .

 4.  Tight gradings 

 
 Tight gradings were introduced in  [ 4 ]  to give a combinatorial interpretation of the wall-function coefficients in generalized cluster scattering diagrams. In this section, we recall the definition of tight gradings and establish some of their structural properties. 
 
 
 4.1.  Maximal Dyck paths 

 
 Fix  d 1 , d 2 ∈ ℤ ≥ 0 d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} . Consider a rectangle with vertices  ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0) ,  ( 0 , d 2 ) (0,d_{2}) ,  ( d 1 , 0 ) (d_{1},0) , and  ( d 1 , d 2 ) (d_{1},d_{2})  with a main diagonal from  ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0)  to  ( d 1 , d 2 ) (d_{1},d_{2}) . 
 
 
 We first require various notions of paths. 
 
 
 Definition 4.1 . 
 
 Let  d 1 d_{1}  and  d 2 d_{2}  be nonnegative integers. 
 
 •  
 
 A  NE path  is a lattice path in  ( ℤ × ℝ ) ∪ ( ℝ × ℤ ) ⊂ ℝ 2 (\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R})\cup(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}  starting at  ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0)  and ending at  ( d 1 , d 2 ) (d_{1},d_{2}) , proceeding by only unit north and east steps. 
 
 •  
 
 A  Dyck path  is a NE path that never passes strictly above the main diagonal from  ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0)  to  ( d 1 , d 2 ) (d_{1},d_{2}) . 
 
 •  
 
 The  maximal Dyck path   𝒫 ⁡ ( d 1 , d 2 ) \mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})  is the Dyck path proceeding from  ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0)  to  ( d 1 , d 2 ) (d_{1},d_{2})  that is closest to the main diagonal. 
 
 
 
 
 
 Given a subset  C C  of steps (also known as  edges ) in a NE path  𝒫 \mathcal{P} , we denote the set of east (resp. north) steps by  C 𝐄 C_{\mathbf{E}}  (resp. For edges  e 1 ∈ 𝒫 𝐄 e_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{E}}  and  e 2 ∈ 𝒫 𝐍 e_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathbf{N}} , let  e 1 ​ e 2 → \overrightarrow{e_{1}e_{2}}  denote the subpath proceeding east from the left endpoint of  e 1 e_{1}  to the top endpoint of  e 2 e_{2} , continuing cyclically around  𝒫 \mathcal{P}  if  e 1 e_{1}  is east of  e 2 e_{2} . 
 
 
 The Dyck paths from  ( 0 , 0 ) (0,0)  to  ( d 1 , d 2 ) (d_{1},d_{2})  form a partially ordered set by comparing the heights at all vertices. The  maximal Dyck path   𝒫 ⁡ ( d 1 , d 2 ) \mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})  is the maximal element under this partial order.  4.2.  Definition of tight gradings 

 
 Motivated by Lee–Schiffler  [ 18 ] ,
Lee, Li, and Zelevinsky  [ 17 ]  introduced combinatorial objects called  compatible pairs  to construct the  greedy basis  for rank- 2 2  cluster algebras, consisting of indecomposable positive elements including the cluster monomials. Rupel  [ 24 ,  25 ]  extended this construction to the setting of  generalized  rank- 2 2  cluster algebras by defining  compatible gradings . 
 
 
 A function from the set of edges on  𝒫 ⁡ ( d 1 , d 2 ) \mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})  to  ℤ ≥ 0 \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}  is called a  grading . Given a grading  ω : 𝒫 ⁡ ( d 1 , d 2 ) → ℤ ≥ 0 \omega:\mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2})\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}  and a set of edges  C ⊂ 𝒫 ⁡ ( d 1 , d 2 ) C\subset\mathcal{P}(d_{1},d_{2}) , we set  ω ⁡ ( C ) ≔ ∑ e ∈ C ω ⁡ ( e ) \omega(C)\coloneqq\sum_{e\in C}\omega(e) . 
 
 
 Definition 4.5 .

 7.  Weyl group symmetry 

 
 In this section, using tight gradings, we give a bijective proof of the Weyl group symmetry for cluster scattering diagrams, namely 
 

 
 (7.1) 
 
 τ b , c ​ ( i , j ) = τ b , c ​ ( i , b ​ i − j ) ​  and  ​ τ b , c ​ ( i , j ) = τ b , c ​ ( c ​ j − i , j ) . \tau^{b,c}(i,j)=\tau^{b,c}(i,bi-j)\text{ and }\tau^{b,c}(i,j)=\tau^{b,c}(cj-i,j)\,. 
 
 
 This resolves  Problem   1.1  posed by Elgin, Reading, and Stella  [ 7 ] .

 9.  Final remarks 

 
 9.1.  Combinatorics of tight gradings 

 
 In  Section   5 , we described a process for generating a tiling associated to each tight grading. This tiling is only a partial tiling, in that there can be empty spaces in the region between the Dyck path and the frame. It could be interesting to characterize the number of such spaces, and in particular study the case of “tightest gradings” when no such spaces appear. Note that this tiling construction is different from that described in  [ 4 ]  and  [ 5 ] , in that the footprints contain a superset of the tiles in earlier constructions. 
 
 
 As discussed in  Remark   7.8 , the composition of mutation and retractions gives a bijection between the tight gradings in  CG 3 , 2 ⊤ ⁡ ( 3 ​ i + 2 , 2 ​ i + 1 , i , i ) \CG_{3,2}^{\top}(3i+2,2i+1,i,i)  and in  CG 3 , 2 ⊢ ⁡ ( 3 ​ i + 1 , 2 ​ i + 1 , i , i ) \CG_{3,2}^{\vdash}(3i+1,2i+1,i,i) . Each set of tight gradings gives a combinatorial interpretation of  τ 3 , 2 ​ ( i , i ) \tau^{3,2}(i,i) . The two sets of tight gradings correspond to a choice of positive or negative angular momentum broken line in the scattering diagram  𝔇 ( 3 , 2 ) \mathfrak{D}_{(3,2)} , as described in  [ 5 ] . It would be interesting to construct a general bijection between the sets of tight gradings in  CG b , c ⊢ \mathrm{CG}_{\mathit{b},\mathit{c}}^{\vdash}  and  CG b , c ⊤ \mathrm{CG}_{\mathit{b},\mathit{c}}^{\top}  that each give a combinatorial interpretation to the same wall-function coefficient. 
 
 
 
 9.2.  Potential relation with stable trees 

 
 There are several situations where Euler characteristics of quiver moduli spaces can be computed by counting so-called stable trees  [ 26 ] . In fact, this is one of the main techniques in  [ 23 ]  for obtaining the formulas which are used in Section  3 . It is thus natural to ask for bijections between sets of stable trees and sets of tight gradings (maybe suitably weighted), but no general pattern emerged yet. One obstacle for such constructions is the fact that the notion of tight gradings is well-adapted to the natural Weyl group symmetry, whereas stable trees are not (the Weyl group symmetry corresponds to applying reflection functors to quiver representations, which does not preserve the class of tree representations naturally).
