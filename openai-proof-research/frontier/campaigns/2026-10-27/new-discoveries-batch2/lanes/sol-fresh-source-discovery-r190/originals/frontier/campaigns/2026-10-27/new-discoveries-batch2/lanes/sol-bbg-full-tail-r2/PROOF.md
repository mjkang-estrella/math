# Ordinary proof of strict inequality for the literal fixed-radian BBG sum

Status: proposed ordinary proof, with a reproducible exact-arithmetic finite certificate. No Lean proof, independent review, novelty clearance, acceptance or credit is claimed. The known explicit irrationality theorem below is a substantive analytic dependency.

## Statement and source

For positive integer n (in radians), put a_n=((2+sin n)/3)^n/n and S=sum a_n. We prove S<2.1635<Ei(log 3). This refutes precisely López Zapata, arXiv:2603.14548v2, §6 Conjecture 6.5 (TeX label conj:main), and the equality in MathDB373621, without changing angle, sequence, or right-hand constant. All a_n are positive. The current arXiv metadata fetched in this lane is byte-identical to the frozen v2 metadata. Actual §6 and the Boppana proof body were read.

The finite witnesses are in checks.json and constant.json, generated by check.py and constant.py. Decimal fields ending in `diagnostic` and printed decimals are not used in comparisons: the PASS comparison is exact rational arithmetic against 21635/10000.

## 1. Global analytic input and phase normalization

Use the explicit theorem as stated in Boppana, arXiv:2007.11017v1, Theorem `thm:Mahler`: for all integers p,q with |q|>1, |pi-p/q|>|q|^(-20). Boppana attributes this exponent-20, constant-one form to Chudnovsky (1982). This is a global inequality, not an asymptotic irrationality exponent with unknown threshold. Boppana also proves convergence of the literal S; no value is proved there. His 2005 AoPS solution and 2020 publication precede the 2026 conjecture.

Set alpha=1/(2pi), y_n=||n alpha-1/4|| in [0,1/2]. Then sin n=cos(2pi y_n). Concavity of sine on [0,pi/2] gives sin(pi y)>=2y, hence

    1-cos(2pi y)=2 sin²(pi y)>=8y²,
    a_n<=exp(-(8/3)n y_n²)/n.                 (1)

The coefficient 8/3 is correct for this derivation. The root note's suggested immediate 16/3 is not justified: at y=1/2 that stronger underlying cosine inequality would say 2>=4. We use 8/3 throughout.

For any integer h>=1, ||h alpha||>1/(8h^19). Indeed, let k be the closest integer to h/(2pi). If k=0, ||h alpha||=h/(2pi)>=1/(2pi)>1/8. Otherwise k>=1, and 2k<=h (use pi>3 and 2k<=h/pi+1<=h for h>=2; h=1 has k=0). Apply the explicit theorem with p=h, q=2k:

    ||h alpha||=|h-2k pi|/(2pi)
       >(2k)^(-19)/(2pi)>1/(8h^19).          (2)

## 2. Separated-block Gaussian bound

Let m>=2 and delta>0 bound ||h alpha|| from below for all 1<=h<m. In m consecutive phases any two points on the unit circle are at least delta apart. Their number within distance t of 1/4 is at most 1+2t/delta, for 0<=t<=1/2. The same bound holds for t>=1/2 since m delta<=1. Integrating the Gaussian by its nonnegative layer-cake derivative gives, for integer X>0,

    sum over one block exp(-(8/3)X y_n²)
      <=1+sqrt(3pi/8)/(delta sqrt X).

The same holds for a shorter block. Split [X,2X) into ceil(X/m) blocks and use (1) and n>=X:

    sum_[X,2X) a_n
      <= ceil(X/m)/X * (1+sqrt(3pi/8)/(delta sqrt X)).   (3)

We replace sqrt(3pi/8) by 11/10 and sqrt X by floor(sqrt X), both in the upper-bound direction. The first replacement follows from pi<22/7, since 33/28<121/100.

## 3. Exact finite rational grids, not an infinite CF assumption

If gcd(p,q)=1, |alpha-p/q|<=epsilon, and 2<=m<=q, then for 1<=h<m,

    ||h alpha||>=||hp/q||-h epsilon
               >=1/q-(m-1)epsilon=:delta.

Every positive delta obtained this way is valid without a best-approximation theorem. Machin's identity pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239), alternating-series remainders and directed integer rounding at scale 10^510 give rational lower/upper bounds for pi and alpha. Continued fractions of the lower endpoint merely generate convenient coprime rational grids. Epsilon is the maximum distance of p/q from either alpha endpoint. It is never inferred from an approximate CF table.

For each k=30,...,1599, check.py computes (3) with X=2^k for a finite list of grids and block lengths m=q or max(2,floor(q/2)), retaining a positive valid minimum, then rounds upward to 10^-30. Summing these 1570 rational witnesses controls every term 2^30<=n<2^1600. All chosen q,m and rounded upper bounds are recorded in tail_rows. The arbitrary search cutoff may reduce sharpness but cannot invalidate an upper bound.

## 4. Entire remote tail

For X>=2^1600 put m=floor(X^(1/40)). By (2), delta=1/(8m^19) is valid. Since m<=X^(1/40), m>=X^(1/40)/2, and ceil(X/m)<=2X/m, (3) yields

    sum_[X,2X) a_n
      <= (2/m)(1+(44/5)m^19/sqrt X)
      <= (2/m)(1+44/5)
      <40 X^(-1/40).

Consequently

    sum_(n>=2^1600) a_n
      <40*2^-40/(1-2^(-1/40))<2400/2^40.

The last elementary inequality follows from 60^40<2*59^40. This controls the WHOLE remaining infinite tail; there is no unknown constant, remote CF assumption, or numerical equidistribution assumption. This remote rational amount is explicitly included in checks.json's tail_upper.

## 5. Sparse certified finite prefix

The frozen prefix interval encloses sum_1^10000 a_n at scale 10^35, using Machin pi intervals, sine Taylor intervals and directed repeated-squaring integer powers. prefix-replay.py replays that check into prefix-replay.json; it does not rely on floating point.

For k=13,...,29, X=2^k, A=max(10001,X), Z=2X, choose the first rational grid p/q with q>=Z. Let epsilon bound the alpha error and set r>sqrt(24/X) by an integer square root rounded upward at scale 10^30. Omit integers with y_n>r. By (1) each omitted a_n<=exp(-64)/n. Their total in the block is less than 2exp(-64)<2^-63, explicitly added per block.

To capture every remaining integer, enumerate integer residues j in an outward enlargement of

    q/4-q(r+Z epsilon) <= j <= q/4+q(r+Z epsilon).

Map j to n=j*p^(-1) mod q and retain A<=n<Z. Because n<q this enumerates each possible n once; the interval lies inside (0,q) and has length less than q, as checked by verify.py. Any n with y_n<=r must occur. No floating-point test selects terms.

For each retained n, let

    y_low=max(0, |j/q-1/4|-n epsilon), t_low=2*pi_low*y_low.

Both are rational lower bounds for the actual phase distance and its radian distance. Every enumerated point has actual radian distance below 1/2, including the outward residue padding, as asserted in check.py and checked again by verify.py. On [0,1/2], t²/2-t⁴/24 is increasing and <=1-cos t. Thus with

    x_low=n*(t_low²/2-t_low⁴/24)/3,

we have a_n<=exp(-x_low)/n. For speed the final code further rounds y_low and t_low downward at scale D=10^30, using E=ceil(D*epsilon) and PI=floor(D*pi_low). With t the resulting lower scaled radian bound, define t2=floor(t²/D), t4=ceil(t⁴/D³) and v=floor(n*(12*t2-t4)/72). Thus v/D<=n*((t/D)²/2-(t/D)⁴/24)/3<=x_low; positivity is explicitly asserted. This uses an upward t4 in a subtraction. Floor rounding can only enlarge the final upper bound. For 0<=v/10^30<1024,

    exp(-v/10^30)
      <=(1-v/(1024*10^30)+v²/(2*1024²*10^60))^1024.

This follows from exp(-z)<=1-z+z²/2 for z>=0. The script rounds the base upward at scale 10^30 and squares ten times with upward rounding, then divides by n upward. It sums these certified upper bounds and the omitted allowances, together with the original exact prefix upper bound. No evaluation of 10^9 exact transcendental functions is needed.

## 6. Right-hand constant

For integer M>=1, gamma>H_M-log M-1/M (the usual harmonic-integral squeeze). Use M=10000 and round the harmonic sum downward. Compute log3 and log10 by

    log x=2 sum_(j>=0) z^(2j+1)/(2j+1), z=(x-1)/(x+1),

whose remainder after K terms is between 0 and 2z^(2K+1)/((2K+1)(1-z²)). Apply the same formula to the rational lower bound for log3 to bound log(log3) downward. Finally truncate the positive Ei series

    Ei(t)=gamma+log t+sum_(j>=1) t^j/(j*j!).

constant.py implements these rational bounds and verifies Ei(log3)>21635/10000. Together with check.py's strict total_upper<21635/10000, this proves the claimed strict inequality.

## Limits and strongest objection

This is an ordinary analytic/computer-assisted proof proposal, not a kernel certificate or an accepted campaign outcome. It needs independent adversarial verification. Its explicit global irrationality dependency is as stated in the read Boppana body; the original 1982 Chudnovsky proof was not audited. The source errors in exponential decay, normalization and poles are not used and receive no credit.

The strongest eligibility objection is that refuting a weak numerical guess using classical packing, an existing irrationality bound, and certified finite arithmetic may be a routine correction rather than a substantive new discovery. Moreover the closest companion body remains inaccessible (403); current public-registry coverage is bounded, and no journal-counterpart or exhaustive specialist clearance is claimed. Exact whole-target refutation removes the mathematical-tail gap but does not remove these eligibility gates.
