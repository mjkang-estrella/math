## New actual classical bodies and concrete adjudication

**Schaaf (1990), DOI 10.1007/BFb0098346.** The complete publisher PDF (8,651,459 bytes) was obtained. Relevant bodies read: chapter 1 definitions (1-0-1)–(1-0-5), Theorem 1.1.1 and Proposition 1.1.2 (pp.1–6); definition 1.4.1, Theorem 1.4.2 and its entire proof (pp.22–24), examples 1.5.1–1.5.3 (pp.25–27); section 2.1 through Theorem 2.1.3 and period-map discussion (pp.45–51); section 3.3 including its counterexample and Theorems 3.3.1–3.3.2/proofs (pp.91–97). Extracted page subsets are retained.

Theorem 1.4.2 gives positive second derivative of Dirichlet return-time maps for A-B nonlinearities. These maps start at u=0 with velocity p and return to u=0; Theorem 1.1.1 integrates g'(p sin theta) over a fixed angular interval. The trigonometric example in 1.5.1 makes this a relevant possible objection, not an irrelevant broad citation. But its conclusion is about those return times and their p parameter. Our map stops at the nonzero prescribed final velocity, producing the moving spatial endpoint. Neither its definition nor its theorem identifies that partial travel time with the Dirichlet return map.

Moreover, even if a relevant time map were convex in an unsquared velocity p, replacing p by sqrt(z) gives second derivative (p T''(p)-T'(p))/(4p^3), whose sign does not follow from T''>0 alone. This chain-rule observation is an obstruction to a claimed application, not a new theorem. The Neumann results reduce homogeneous zero-velocity endpoint problems to return maps and conclude monotonicity; they do not address this mixed stopping condition. Section 3.3 proves bounded numbers of sign changes for T', not convexity in z. It explicitly demonstrates failure of unrestricted variation diminution for the exponent -1/2 convolution kernel. Thus one cannot use its generic convolution language as a complete exceptional-moment theorem.

**Smoller (1994 second edition), chapter 13, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4612-0873-0_13.** Actual publisher chapter obtained; section D, pp.185–190 and the attribution on p.191 read completely. Theorem 13.11 concerns f(u)=-u(u-a)(u-1), 0<a<1/2, homogeneous Dirichlet endpoints, and asserts exactly one critical point of its time map. Its proof (13.28)–(13.37) establishes S''>0 at stationary points using a multiple of S'. It does not assert global convexity for arbitrary f or arbitrary stopping curves. This resolves the content of the citation itself, beyond merely repeating the journal authors' failed application. It supplies neither the full pendulum theorem nor the complementary M0/M1 identity. A monotone parameter change preserves zeros of a first derivative but does not preserve global convexity; the source cannot be enlarged on that basis.

They supply convexity for different return maps/parameters or uniqueness of stationary points, while leaving precisely the mixed stopping condition, exceptional orders, and full z-domain bridge unproved. Elementary tools and proof length alone do not establish routine-corollary exclusion. Conversely this bounded no-match does not establish literature novelty.
