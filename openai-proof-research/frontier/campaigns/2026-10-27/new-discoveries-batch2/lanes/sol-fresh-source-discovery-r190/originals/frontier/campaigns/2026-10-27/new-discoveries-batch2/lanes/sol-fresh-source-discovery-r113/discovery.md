# Danchev's arbitrary-idempotent-difference question: unresolved

Primary attribution: Peter V. Danchev, *A note on nil-clean rings*, Acta Univ. Sapientiae, Mathematica 12(2) (2020), 287–293, DOI [10.2478/ausm-2020-0020](https://doi.org/10.2478/ausm-2020-0020). Three exact attributed questions screened, all on printed p292 (the earlier p290 also has a separately numbered Problem 1):

1. Problem 1: For every associative unital nil-clean ring R with bounded U(R), must R be (strongly) π-regular? Under the standard finite-exponent meaning of bounded, this is a routine reduction to Danchev–Šter Corollary 3.12, not an investment candidate. If char(R)=2^t and the exponent divides m, write m=2^s d, d odd. For nilpotent a, u=1+a satisfies u^m=1. The factor ((1+a)^(2^s))^d−1 factors as (a^(2^s)) times a polynomial in a with odd constant d, hence an invertible constant plus a nilpotent. Thus a^(2^s)=0 uniformly. The cited corollary already gives strong π-regularity. The source does not define “bounded”; no broader interpretation is claimed settled.
2. Problem 2: For every associative unital nil-clean ring R of characteristic 2 whose unit group is a p-group (or, respectively, a 2p-group), for some prime p, must R be (strongly) π-regular? The 2-group branch is already Danchev–Lam Corollary 4.10 plus Theorem 4.3. For odd p, a nonzero nilpotent would yield a nonzero square-zero power b and the unit 1+b of order 2, impossible; R is then Boolean. The printed “2p-group” branch is not silently identified with either branch, and is not resolved here. No discovery claim for these routine cases.
3. Problem 3, sustained target: For every associative unital ring R such that (i) every a∈R is e+n with e²=e and n nilpotent and (ii) every nilpotent n∈R is e−f for some e²=e and f²=f, must, for every a∈R, there exist an integer k≥1 and b∈R with a^k=a^k b a^k? The source further asks whether adding J(R)=0 forces, for every a∈R, existence of b with a=aba. BOTH clauses remain unresolved here. No finite-dimensional, finite-ring, PI, bounded-index or commuting-idempotent restriction is substituted for these quantifiers.

## Actual closest proofs read

Danchev's Proposition 1 (p288–289) handles commuting idempotents; Theorem 1 (p289–290) handles ef=e, fe=f and forces n²=0. Its following paragraph handles efe=e, fef=f and forces n⁴=0. Lemma 1 proves the reverse implication that regular rings have the difference property, using Jordan/Weyr decomposition. None proves the converse of Problem 3. The article's matrix and triangular examples are already known, uncredited.

Danchev–Šter, [arXiv:1412.4359v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.4359v2), actual Lemma 3.9, Proposition 3.10 and Corollaries 3.11–3.12 proofs were read. They use bounded nilpotence/PI hypotheses to force appropriate prime factors to be semisimple Artinian. Problem 3 supplies neither hypothesis.

Danchev–Lam, [Rings with unipotent units](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/2073/download/), actual Theorem 4.3 proof and Corollary 4.10 proof were read. They require a UU/2-group condition, not merely arbitrary nilpotent differences. The source itself notes M_n(F2), n≥2, has a unit of order 3. The 2021 [matrix-sum paper](https://matstud.org.ua/ojs/index.php/matstud/article/download/123/61/) was retained and its nearby decomposition discussion inspected, without finding a proof of the full target. ## Sustained route and exact obstruction

The initial route was to upgrade arbitrary difference witnesses to the symmetric witnesses in the source and apply bounded-index regularity. In characteristic 2, n=e+f implies

    en+ne = n+n²,    [e,n²]=0.

These identities do not imply the symmetric relations. Let J_n have 1 on its superdiagonal; D have diagonal 1 in positions 1,3,5,…; U consist of J_n's edges starting in odd positions and V of those starting in even positions. Then E=D+U and F=D+V are idempotents over F2 and E+F=J_n. But J_n has nilpotence index exactly n. At n=5, J_n⁴≠0, so NO difference representation of this J_n can satisfy both EFE=E and FEF=F: the source's own identity would force its fourth power to vanish. This rules out that witness-upgrade route, not the source conjecture.

There is also a single-ring obstruction to inferring a bounded nilpotence index. Take the unital direct limit M_(2^k)(F2) under A↦diag(A,A). Every element lies in a finite matrix ring, hence the limit is nil-clean by the source's explicit recalled matrix theorem. Every nilpotent has a Jordan-block decomposition in its stage and therefore is a difference of two idempotents by the preceding alternating construction. Jordan blocks of sizes 2^k give unbounded nilpotence index in this one ring. The limit is regular (stagewise inner inverses), so this is emphatically NOT a counterexample to Problem 3. It invalidates a bounded-index bridge under the actual assumptions.

`checks.py` exhaustively verifies nil-cleanness and the nilpotent-difference property in M2(F2) and M3(F2), checks the anticommutator identity on all pairs of idempotents there, and exactly verifies alternating witnesses at sizes 3,5,8,16. These are diagnostics for the symbolic obstruction, never evidence by extrapolation for the source claim.

## Strongest substantive objection and remaining gap

The strongest objection is that all positive cases obtained here are classical/source reductions, and the newly recorded negative calculations refute only a proposed method. That objection stands. A full solution still needs either a uniform derivation of a^k=a^k b a^k for every element under the two original hypotheses, with the semiprimitive regularity clause handled separately, or an explicit ring satisfying both hypotheses globally and an element whose every positive power has no inner inverse. No such derivation or ring has been supplied. The blocked Šter2018 body also prevents using its examples as certified counterexamples to this more constrained question.
