## 7. Almost-sure termination and expected Turing cost

All deterministic routines, exponent comparisons, integer roots, BFS/DFS, edge maps, route checks, and rational linear algebra have polynomial bit bounds. Values D_j have O(log n) bits. The same holds after accounting for local relabelling and maps to original labels. Long cycles can be stored as explicit vertex lists; prospective stages keep assigned-edge sets so discarding an unused suffix restores the recorded boundary input without guessing edges. Across a stage total box order is <=2n; at most ceil(log2 n) stages are processed. Each stage has O(n) split leaves and at most O(n^2) cycle deletions. This yields a polynomial bound even with straightforward matrix representations, subject to the unresolved path implementation in Section 1.

Finite uniform sampling by rejection from a power-of-two range has mean fewer than two trials; dyadic labels use a fixed number of fair bits. Rejected finite samples may use unboundedly many trials, but have finite expected cost and terminate almost surely. For fixed local data, if an attempt has finite mean cost mu and acceptance probability p>=p0>0, independent retries have expected total cost mu/p: sum the mean contribution of attempt j multiplied by the probability all preceding attempts failed. Current cost need not be independent of current success. Thus the local work per call has expected cost <=A(n+1)^a, once the path routine is supplied.

    sum_j n_j^p <= (n/2)^(p-1) sum_j n_j <=2^(2-p)n^p<=n^p/4.
