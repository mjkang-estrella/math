# Sustained attempt and eligibility

The selected source question is the Section 4 p9 uniform Taylor-domination-to-valency assertion of Friedland–Goldman–Yomdin 1909.04918v1. PROOF.md supplies two complete, different constructions covering its universal quantifiers. This is a mathematical resolution offered for independent review, **not a discovery acceptance**.

First route: pass from |a_k|<=k^p M to constant domination on a smaller disc, then use a zero-count/Bernstein inequality. The maximum of k^p r^k occurs near p/|log r|; its logarithm is of order p log p. The source's Remark 2.4 explicitly records this loss. It cannot deliver the desired O(p) conclusion. The loss is retained as a failed route in exact-checks.json, rather than silently calling it a proof.

Second route: make the late coefficient admissible while forcing many roots. A two-term polynomial z+3^(pt) z^(pt+1), with p=3^t, does exactly this. The index factor k^p pays for an exponential coefficient, while the number of distinct interior roots is pt+1. This establishes a global infinite family, not a finite sweep. It remains valid with the constant coefficient excluded, avoiding the familiar constant-shift issue in passing between zeros and valency.

Third route: remove cancellation/complex-root symmetry as an explanation by constructing a product of distinct real linear factors. Its coefficient l1 norm is the product of 1+c_j. Setting p=5^t again pays for all coefficients simultaneously, and all pt zeros lie between 1/4 and 1/3. No floating point or unproved asymptotic is used.

Strongest objection: these are elementary generic sharpness constructions. Neither shortness nor computational discovery alone excludes credit, but a published sharpness statement or an entire classical bridge that already supplies this source-specific refutation would exclude it. The inspected closest bodies give upper bounds, not the lower witnesses. This limited observation does not clear the whole literature.
