## MATH

First construction: for every integer t>=1 set p=3^t, m=pt and f(z)=z+3^m z^(m+1). Since m>=p, the high index m+1 is strictly beyond the cutoff. Both initial maxima, over 0..p and 1..p, equal 1. The only nonzero tail coefficient satisfies

    3^m = 3^(pt) = (3^t)^p = p^p <= (m+1)^p.

Every other tail coefficient is zero, so the condition holds for all required k, including k above the degree. There is a simple zero at 0 and m pairwise distinct nonzero roots of z^m=-3^(-m), all of modulus 1/3. At a nonzero root, f'(z)=1+(m+1)3^m z^m=-m, which is nonzero. Hence there are m+1 simple zeros strictly in D_(1/2). For any proposed finite K(1), choose an integer t>max(K(1),0); then (m+1)/p=t+1/p>K(1). This is an all-parameter proof of unbounded ratio, not an extrapolation from finite tests. In fact it supplies a lower growth order p log p along p=3^t, matching the order of loss discussed by the source (no optimal constant asserted).

Second construction: for every t>=1 set p=5^t, m=pt and c_j=3+j/(m+1), 1<=j<=m. All c_j lie strictly between 3 and 4 and are pairwise distinct. The product g(z)=product(1-c_j z) therefore has exactly m distinct simple real roots 1/c_j in (1/4,1/3). Its coefficients b_k have alternating signs and positive elementary-symmetric magnitudes, giving

    sum_(k=0)^m |b_k| = product_(j=1)^m(1+c_j) < 5^m = p^p.

For every p<k<=m, |b_k|<p^p<=k^p, while the initial maximum is >=1 because b0=1. Even excluding b0 it is >1 because |b1|=sum c_j>1. For k>m the tail vanishes. At t=1 the degree equals p, so the tail is empty and the root ratio is just 1; that harmless endpoint is not the asymptotic obstruction. For arbitrary t the ratio m/p=t is unbounded. Each factor has a nonzero slope and no other factor vanishes at its root, proving simplicity. This alternative passes independently.

The exponent 2p-1 in Remark 2.4 is at least p for these positive integer cutoffs, so the same witnesses meet that weaker coefficient condition. It is the same universal refutation family. Neither the real-root variant, the remarkable exponent, nor the individual parameter values merits extra credit.

Exact small controls are in checks.json with their reproducible script checks.py. They check arithmetic, tail coefficients, rational root intervals and simplicity for bounded parameters only. The preceding proofs, not these controls, establish unbounded t.

## SUBSTANTIVE

This is not a fixed-p counterexample or a boundary correction: it contradicts the entire uniform linear claim at fixed C and fixed interior radius. The exclusion of a0 and simplicity of all roots make it robust to the most plausible definitional repairs.

The strongest actual objection is **Friedland–Yomdin Example 1.1**, not elementary shortness. It occurs in arXiv:1503.00325v1 (1 March 2015), pp2–3, and the actual 2017 published chapter, DOI [10.1007/978-3-319-45282-1_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-45282-1_8), p124. The printed family is h_(q,N)(x)=x^q+x^N, N>=10q+1, used to separate (s,q)-valency from (s+1,q)-valency by removing a low-degree polynomial. Its proof uses Rouche on a small circle; the subsequent discussion after Theorem 3.2 again uses the large gap to destroy valency after division by x^q.

Here is the complete algebraic bridge to the first candidate: set q=1, N=pt+1, p=3^t and t>=2, and take h_(1,N)(3z)/3. This equals z+3^(N-1)z^N, exactly the candidate. The printed condition N>=11 holds. Solve the nonzero-root equation to obtain modulus 1/3, and apply 3^(N-1)=p^p<=N^p to get the cutoff bound; the ratio N/p=t+1/p is unbounded. Thus the literal polynomial shape is already present in a closely related primary paper, not merely a hypothetical classical analogy.

However, the printed separation does **not itself state** that coefficient-versus-cutoff calculation or the uniform K(C)p refutation. Its proved small-disc valency is for the unscaled h and does not transport to the disc used for this lower bound: h's D_(1/3) becomes D_(1/9). Its high-valency equation subtracts x^q; the candidate instead counts the zeros of the scaled polynomial itself. The bridge above is a new deduction in this review, not a quoted old endpoint. This is strong evidence for a routine source-method adaptation, but polynomial-shape identity plus easy parameter tuning alone does not logically certify an earlier printed resolution or settle the campaign's substantive threshold. I therefore recommend HOLD rather than asserting a proved prior-resolution EXCLUDE. Root may adjudicate the explicit bridge as a source-method exclusion; it must be labelled that way, with no claim that the 2015 authors resolved their later question.
