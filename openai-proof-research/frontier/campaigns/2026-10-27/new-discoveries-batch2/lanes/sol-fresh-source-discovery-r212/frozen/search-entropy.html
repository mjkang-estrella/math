We show that if $\text{length}(Γ) \le λ\, \text{length}(Γ_b)$, then the universal cover of $Γ$ contains a vertex around which, \emph{at every radius} $R\ge 0$, the ball is at least as long as the ball of radius $R$ in the trivalent tree whose edges all have length $3λ$. In particular, for $λ\le\frac13$, this ball is at least as long as the ball of the same radius in the universal cover of $Γ_b$, with no multiplicative loss. This improves an earlier theorem of the author, which gives the weaker lower bound $(1-3λ)$ times the ball in the universal cover of $Γ_b$, and only for $λ\le\frac16$. The proof rests on an elementary comparison principle. Among trees whose vertices have degree at least three and whose edges have length at most $c$, the trivalent tree with all edges of length $c$ has the smallest balls at every radius. As an application, we double the exponential growth rate in the author's earlier uniform lower bound for the area of balls in the universal cover of small-area surfaces of hyperbolic type, from $\ln 2$ to $2\ln 2$, beyond the hyperbolic rate. We also obtain explicit constants in the positive answer to Guth's question for surfaces. Finally, we conjecture the sharp, all-scale version of the minimal volume entropy inequality of Kapovich--Nagnibeda and Lim, and show that it would be optimal. △ Less

In this formulation, the homology condition is imposed at the level of the spacetime: the homology regions are spacetime volumes rather spatial regions. We show in particular that the smallest minimax homology region is the entanglement wedge. The minimax prescription suggests a way to construct a graph model for time-dependent states, a weighted graph on which min cuts compute HRT entropies. The existence of a graph model would imply that HRT entropies obey the same inequalities as RT entropies, in other words that the RT and HRT entropy cones coincide. Our construction of a graph model relies on the time-sheets obeying a certain ``cooperating'' property, which we show holds in some examples and for which we give a partial proof; however, we also find scenarios where it may fail. △ Less

We define binding complexity of a given state as the minimal number of quantum gates that must act between parties to prepare it. To illustrate the new notion we compute it in a toy model for a scalar field theory, using certain multiparty entangled states which are analogous to configurations that are known in AdS/CFT to correspond to multiboundary wormholes. Pursuing this analogy, we show that our states can be prepared by the Euclidean path integral in $(0+1)$-dimensional quantum mechanics on graphs with wormhole-like structure. We compute the binding complexity of our states by adapting the Euler-Arnold approach to Nielsen's geometrization of gate counting, and find a scaling with entropy that resembles a result for the interior volume of holographic multiboundary wormholes. We also compute the binding complexity of general coherent states in perturbation theory, and show that for "double-trace deformations" of the Hamiltonian the effects resemble expansion of a wormhole interior in holographic theories. △ Less
