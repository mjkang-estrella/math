An upper bound for the type of a numerical semigroup,
and a reduction of Wilf’s conjecture

Abstract.
Let SS be a numerical semigroup with multiplicity m\mathrm{m}, conductor c\mathrm{c}, embedding
dimension e\mathrm{e}, type t\mathrm{t} and genus g\mathrm{g}, and let n=c−g\mathrm{n}=\mathrm{c}-\mathrm{g}. Wilf’s
conjecture asserts that e​n≥c\mathrm{e}\,\mathrm{n}\geq\mathrm{c}; the inequality g≤t​n\mathrm{g}\leq\mathrm{t}\,\mathrm{n} of
Fröberg, Gottlieb and Häggkvist settles it when t≤e−1\mathrm{t}\leq\mathrm{e}-1. The Apéry set of
SS with respect to any s∈S∖{0}s\in S\setminus\{0\} carries a partial order whose maximal
elements are the pseudo-Frobenius numbers translated by ss; for s=ms=\mathrm{m} its minimal
elements are the minimal generators other than m\mathrm{m}. Comparing the two extremal
statistics bounds the type by t≤e−1+Ξ⁡(S)≤e−1+Θ⁡(S)\mathrm{t}\leq\mathrm{e}-1+\Xi(S)\leq\mathrm{e}-1+\Theta(S), where
Θ⁡(S)\Theta(S) measures the redundancy of the covering of the gaps of SS by the
pseudo-Frobenius numbers and Ξ⁡(S)\Xi(S) refines it. With an exact decomposition of the
Wilf number this yields the genus bound g≤e−1+t⁡(n−1)\mathrm{g}\leq\mathrm{e}-1+\mathrm{t}(\mathrm{n}-1), strictly stronger
than g≤t​n\mathrm{g}\leq\mathrm{t}\,\mathrm{n} precisely when t≥e\mathrm{t}\geq\mathrm{e}, and reduces Wilf’s conjecture to
an inequality free of c\mathrm{c} and n\mathrm{n}. We determine the equality case of
g≤t​n\mathrm{g}\leq\mathrm{t}\,\mathrm{n}, recovering a classification of Singhal; answer a question of
Moscariello and Sammartano whenever e≥t+1\mathrm{e}\geq\mathrm{t}+1; and correct Kaplan’s
classification of the equality case for c≤2​m\mathrm{c}\leq 2\mathrm{m}, from which an infinite family
is missing.
Key words and phrases: Numerical semigroup, Wilf’s conjecture, pseudo-Frobenius number, type,
embedding dimension, Apéry set, Apéry poset, additive basis

1. Introduction
A numerical semigroup is a submonoid S⊆ℕ={0,1,2,…}S\subseteq\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\} whose
complement ℕ∖S\mathbb{N}\setminus S is finite. The elements of ℕ∖S\mathbb{N}\setminus S are the
gaps of SS, their number g=g⁡(S)\mathrm{g}=\mathrm{g}(S) is the genus, the largest gap
F=F⁡(S)\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{F}(S) is the Frobenius number, and c=c⁡(S)=F+1\mathrm{c}=\mathrm{c}(S)=\mathrm{F}+1 is the
conductor. We write
L=L⁡(S)=S∩[0,c),n=n⁡(S)=|L⁡(S)|,\mathrm{L}=\mathrm{L}(S)=S\cap[0,\mathrm{c}),\qquad\mathrm{n}=\mathrm{n}(S)=|\mathrm{L}(S)|,
so that n+g=c\mathrm{n}+\mathrm{g}=\mathrm{c}. Every numerical semigroup has a unique minimal system of
generators, whose cardinality e=e⁡(S)\mathrm{e}=\mathrm{e}(S) is the embedding dimension, and we
denote by m=m⁡(S)\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{m}(S) the multiplicity, that is, the least positive
element of SS. Standard references are the monographs of Rosales and
García-Sánchez [20] and of Ramírez Alfonsín [19].
In 1978 Wilf [24] posed a question that remains one of the central open
problems in the theory.
Conjecture 1.1 (Wilf).
For every numerical semigroup S≠ℕS\neq\mathbb{N},
e⁡(S)​n​(S)≥c⁡(S).\mathrm{e}(S)\,\mathrm{n}(S)\;\geq\;\mathrm{c}(S).
We record the discrepancy as the Wilf number
W⁡(S)=e⁡(S)​n​(S)−c⁡(S),\mathrm{W}(S)\;=\;\mathrm{e}(S)\,\mathrm{n}(S)-\mathrm{c}(S),
so that Conjecture 1.1 reads W⁡(S)≥0\mathrm{W}(S)\geq 0.
The conjecture has been established in the following cases: e≤3\mathrm{e}\leq 3, due to Fröberg,
Gottlieb and Häggkvist [14]
and to Dobbs and Matthews [8]; c≤2​m\mathrm{c}\leq 2\mathrm{m}, due to Kaplan [16];
2​e≥m2\mathrm{e}\geq\mathrm{m}, due to Sammartano [21]; c≤3​m\mathrm{c}\leq 3\mathrm{m} and n≤10\mathrm{n}\leq 10,
due to Eliahou [9]; 3​e≥m3\mathrm{e}\geq\mathrm{m}, due to Eliahou [10];
n≤12\mathrm{n}\leq 12, due to Eliahou and Marín-Aragón [13]; semigroups with a
large second generator, due to Spirito [23]; semigroups with m|c\mathrm{m}\mid\mathrm{c}
and 4​e≥m4\mathrm{e}\geq\mathrm{m}, due to Eliahou [11]; almost symmetric
semigroups, due to Barucci [1] and, by a different route, to D’Anna and
Moscariello [4]; multiplicity m≤19\mathrm{m}\leq 19, due to Bruns,
García-Sánchez, O’Neill and Wilburne [3] and Kliem and Stump [17]; and
genus at most 100100, verified by Delgado, Eliahou and Fromentin [7].
Numerical semigroups with small or negative Wilf number, and the near-misses among
them, are studied by Eliahou and Fromentin [12] and by Delgado
[5]. Combined with Zhai’s asymptotic count [25], Eliahou’s
theorem on c≤3​m\mathrm{c}\leq 3\mathrm{m} shows that the conjecture holds for asymptotically all
numerical semigroups ordered by genus. We refer to Delgado’s survey
[6] for a systematic account.
1.1. The type and the FGH range
An integer ff is a pseudo-Frobenius number of SS if f∉Sf\notin S and
f+s∈Sf+s\in S for every s∈S∖{0}s\in S\setminus\{0\}. The set of these is denoted PF⁡(S)\PF(S)
and its cardinality is the type t=t⁡(S)\mathrm{t}=\mathrm{t}(S) of SS. The classical approach to
Conjecture 1.1 rests on the inequality of Fröberg, Gottlieb
and Häggkvist [14, Theorem 20],
(1)
c⁡(S)≤(t⁡(S)+1)​n​(S),\mathrm{c}(S)\;\leq\;\bigl(\mathrm{t}(S)+1\bigr)\,\mathrm{n}(S),
which yields Conjecture 1.1 whenever
(2)
t⁡(S)≤e⁡(S)−1.\mathrm{t}(S)\;\leq\;\mathrm{e}(S)-1.
We refer to (2), equivalently to e≥t+1\mathrm{e}\geq\mathrm{t}+1, as the
FGH range. It contains all symmetric semigroups and all semigroups of maximal embedding
dimension; see [20, Chs. 2–3].
Outside the FGH range, the type has been of limited use. As Delgado observes of
(1), “since no upper bound exists for type in higher embedding dimensions,
Proposition 3.1 [that is, (1)] cannot yield further general results”
[6, §3.1].
Large classes containing many semigroups with t≥e\mathrm{t}\geq\mathrm{e} have been settled by other
means, almost symmetric semigroups among them. To the best of our knowledge, however,
no theorem in the literature takes t≥e\mathrm{t}\geq\mathrm{e} as its hypothesis, and none of the
known cases of Conjecture 1.1 is proved by an argument that runs through
the type in that range. We show that the classical argument behind
(1) yields more than the inequality itself, and that the additional
structure identifies the obstruction.
1.2. Results
The proof of (1) discards two nonnegative quantities. For an integer

g⁡(S)+σ⁡(S)≤e⁡(S)−1+t⁡(S)​(n⁡(S)−1),\mathrm{g}(S)+\sigma(S)\;\leq\;\mathrm{e}(S)-1+\mathrm{t}(S)\bigl(\mathrm{n}(S)-1\bigr),

2. Notation and preliminaries
This section fixes notation, records the standard facts used throughout, and computes
the invariants of a family of numerical semigroups that recurs below.
Throughout this paper, SS denotes a numerical semigroup and S∗=S∖{0}S^{*}=S\setminus\{0\}.
We abbreviate m=m⁡(S)\mathrm{m}=\mathrm{m}(S), F=F⁡(S)\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{F}(S), c=c⁡(S)\mathrm{c}=\mathrm{c}(S), g=g⁡(S)\mathrm{g}=\mathrm{g}(S),

the context, and we write G⁡(S)=ℕ∖SG(S)=\mathbb{N}\setminus S for the gap set. We exclude
S=ℕS=\mathbb{N} throughout, so that m≥2\mathrm{m}\geq 2 and F≥1\mathrm{F}\geq 1. By definition of the conductor,
[c,∞)⊆S[\mathrm{c},\infty)\subseteq S.

3. A defect decomposition of the Wilf number
