Abstract
It follows from the Garloff–Wagner Theorem that the set of stable polynomials of degree nn, denoted by ℋn\mathcal{H}_{n}, i.e., those whose all zeros lie in the left open complex half-plane, with the Hadamard product *, forms an abelian semigroup contained in the abelian group ℝn+\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+} of polynomials of degree nn with positive real coefficients. By the idealizer of the set ℋn\mathcal{H}_{n}, we refer to the largest subsemigroup of ℝn+\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+} in which ℋn\mathcal{H}_{n} is an ideal. In this paper, we formulate a conjecture characterizing the idealizer of ℋn\mathcal{H}_{n} and prove it for n⩽5n\leqslant 5. In addition, we show that the proposed condition is necessary for any polynomial to belong to the idealizer and establish, in a distinguished special case, a sufficient condition of a similar nature that supports the conjecture.

Idealizer, Hurwitz polynomials, Hadamard product, Stable polynomials, Multiplier sequences,
1 Introduction
Let ℝn+\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+} denote polynomials of degree nn with positive coefficients. A non-constant polynomial ff of degree nn with real coefficients is called stable if all its zeros lie in the open left half of the complex plane and quasi-stable if all its zeros lie in the closed left half of the complex plane. One of the necessary conditions for stability is that the coefficients of a given polynomial are of the same sign, therefore, for simplicity, when discussing stable polynomials, we only consider the set
ℋn={f∈ℝn+:f⁡(x)=0⇒ℜ⁡(x)<0}.\mathcal{H}_{n}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+}:f(x)=0\Rightarrow\Re(x)<0\right\}.
In the case of quasi-stability, we define the set
ℋn⋆={f(x)=∑i=0naixi,ai⩾0(i=1,…,n−1),a0,an>0:f(x)=0⇒ℜ(x)⩽0},\mathcal{H}_{n}^{\star}=\left\{f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i},\;a_{i}\geqslant 0\;(i=1,\ldots,n-1),\;a_{0},a_{n}>0:f(x)=0\Rightarrow\Re(x)\leqslant 0\right\},
which for the sake of our work represents all quasi-stable polynomials as the assumption a0>0a_{0}>0 does not affect the generality of our reasoning. Details are explained in Subsection 2.3.
It follows from the Garloff–Wagner Theorem [10, Thm. 1] that ℋn\mathcal{H}_{n} with the coefficient-wise product ∗\ast, so called the Hadamard product (see Subsection 2.3), is an abelian semigroup in the abelian group ℝn+\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+}. Only for n=1,2n=1,2 the semigroup ℋn\mathcal{H}_{n} is a group, since ℋ1=ℝ1+\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathbb{R}_{1}^{+} and ℋ2=ℝ2+\mathcal{H}_{2}=\mathbb{R}_{2}^{+}. For n=3n=3, we see that ℋ3\mathcal{H}_{3} does not contain the identity polynomial 𝕀3\mathbb{I}_{3} with respect to the product ∗\ast,
i.e., 𝕀n​(x):=∑j=0nxj\mathbb{I}_{n}(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{n}x^{j}. We must therefore broaden our perspective to include ℋ3⋆\mathcal{H}_{3}^{\star}, which from the Hermite-Biehler Theorem, is a subset of ℝ3+\mathbb{R}_{3}^{+}. It does indeed contain 𝕀3\mathbb{I}_{3}, but it contains no elements that are inverses of stable polynomials (by the Routh–Hurwitz Theorem). That means it is a commutative monoid, but not a group. However, ℋ4⋆\mathcal{H}_{4}^{\star} cannot be a monoid, since 𝕀4∉ℋ4⋆\mathbb{I}_{4}\notin\mathcal{H}_{4}^{\star}.
For each nn, we seek a maximal subsemigroup Xn⊂ℝn+X_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+} with the Hadamard product ∗\ast, such that ℋn⊂Xn\mathcal{H}_{n}\subset X_{n}, 𝕀n∈Xn\mathbb{I}_{n}\in X_{n}, and for m⩽nm\leqslant\ n
∗:Xn×ℋm→ℋm.\ast:X_{n}\times\mathcal{H}_{m}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{m}.
(1)
In other words, we want to characterize
Xn={g∈ℝn+:∀m⩽n,∀f∈ℋmf∗g∈ℋm}={g∈ℝn+:∀m⩽ng∗ℋm⊂ℋm}.X_{n}=\{g\in\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+}:\forall m\leqslant n,\;\forall f\in\mathcal{H}_{m}\;\;\;f\ast g\in\mathcal{H}_{m}\}=\{g\in\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+}:\forall m\leqslant n\;\;\;g\ast\mathcal{H}_{m}\subset\mathcal{H}_{m}\}.
The set XnX_{n} defined this way is the largest submonoid of the group ℝn+\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+}, in which ℋm\mathcal{H}_{m} is an ideal. Such a set is called the idealizer of ℋm\mathcal{H}_{m} with respect to the product ∗\ast.
If n=1,2n=1,2, then Xn=ℝn+X_{n}=\mathbb{R}_{n}^{+}. It is easy to see that for n=3n=3 we have

for polynomials of degree n⩽5n\leqslant 5 or less (see [2],

However, in Example 1, we show that 𝒲¯5≠X5\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{5}\neq X_{5}.
In Section 3, we give a definition of a family of polynomials

and Y4=W4¯=X4Y_{4}=\overline{W_{4}}=X_{4} (see Theorem 8). Therefore, we propose

Conjecture 1.
For any n∈ℕn\in\mathbb{N}, equality Yn=XnY_{n}=X_{n} holds.
The statement is trivial for n=1,2n=1,2, while the case n=3n=3 is readily verified.
We prove that Conjecture 1 is true for n=4n=4 (Theorem 8) and n=5n=5 (Theorem 9). Moreover, we show that Xn⊂YnX_{n}\subset Y_{n} for all nn (Theorem 6). We also investigate the idealizer of the set of quasi-stable polynomials. In addition, for a special case, we show a sufficient condition that coincides with Conjecture 1 (Theorem 10).
The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review the
required background from stability theory. We then show necessary conditions for
belonging to the idealizer XnX_{n} in Section 3. The main results
of the paper are Theorems 8 and 9, presented in
Section 4, where we prove that Conjecture 1 is true
for n=4n=4 and n=5n=5. Section 5 provides additional information on the
idealizer of quasi-stable polynomials, and Section 6 is devoted to
supplementary results in a special case, in support of Conjecture 1. Finally, Section 7 provides examples completing our results.

3 Necessary conditions
When beginning research on any mathematical object, it is often reasonable to
consider its behavior in extreme cases. That is an exact idea behind the
Pólya-Schur Theorem [13, Thm. 5.7.2], where sequences that are
supposed to preserve the reality of polynomial zeros (sometimes called
hyperbolicity) are tested solely on polynomials of the form (x+1)n(x+1)^{n},
whose zeros all coincide at the single point. Such so-called multiplier
sequences continue to attract interest (see [9], [11],
[14]). More generally, there is ongoing research on operators preserving
not only the reality of the zeros but also the sectors or strips in which the
zeros of a polynomial lie. These operators are referred to as zero-strip-preserving
(or reducing) operators or zero-sector-preserving (or reducing) operators (see
[6], [7], [8]). Somewhat surprisingly, these
testing polynomials have remained essentially unchallenged. In our setting, in
addition to preserving the reality of zeros, we must also account for the
interlacing property of the even and odd parts of the polynomial. This
additional requirement naturally leads us to introduce the following class of
objects that are used throughout this paper.
Polynomials of the form

are called basic quasi-stable polynomials of degree kk, and

the mm-shifted basic quasi-stable polynomials of degree kk.

Conjecture 2.
For any n∈ℕn\in\mathbb{N}, equality Yn⋆=Xn⋆Y_{n}^{\star}=X_{n}^{\star} holds.
