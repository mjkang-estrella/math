                                                  Abstract. Consider the Weil sum WF,d (u) = x∈F ψ(xd + ux), where
                                                  F is a finite field of characteristic p, ψ is the canonical additive character
                                                  of F , d is coprime to |F ∗ |, and u ∈ F ∗ . We say that WF,d (u) is three-
                                                  valued when it assumes precisely three distinct values as u runs through
                                                  F ∗ : this is the minimum number of distinct values in the nondegenerate
                                                  case, and three-valued WF,d are rare and desirable. When WF,d is three-
                                                  valued, we give a lower bound on the p-adic valuation of the values.
                                                  This enables us to prove the characteristic 3 case of a 1976 conjecture of
                                                  Helleseth: when p = 3 and [F : F3 ] is a power of 2, we show that WF,d
                                                  cannot be three-valued.

  Much interest has focused on which choices of F and d make WF,d precisely
three-valued, and ten infinite families have been found (see [1, Table 1]).
From these, one finds that if F is of characteristic p, and if [F : Fp ] is divisible
by an odd prime, then there is a d such WF,d is three-valued. However, no
three-valued examples have ever been found for fields F where [F : Fp ] is a
power of 2. This prompted the following conjecture [13, Conjecture 5.2].
Conjecture 1.2 (Helleseth, 1976). If F is of characteristic p with [F : Fp ]
a power of 2, then WF,d is not three-valued.
   Many attempts have been made to test or prove this conjecture in the
case where F is of characteristic 2, and many fruitful discoveries were made
in the process [12, 3, 20, 6, 2, 11]. The p = 2 case was at last proved in [15,
Corollary 1.10]. Now the p = 3 case is proved in this paper as a corollary of
a new bound on the p-divisibility of Weil sums.
   For a nonzero integer n, the p-adic valuation of n, written vp (n), is the
largest k such that pk | n, and vp (0) = ∞. If we extend this valuation to the
cyclotomic field Q(e2πi/p ) where the Weil sums lie, we may state the first
fundamental result on the p-divisibility of Weil sums from [13, Theorem 4.5].
Theorem 1.3 (Helleseth, 1976). If F is of characteristic p, then we have
vp (WF,d (u)) > 0 for every u ∈ F .
  It was recently proved [15, Theorems 1.7, 1.9] that when WF,d is three-
valued, the values must be rational integers.
Theorem 1.4 (Katz, 2012). If F is of characteristic p, and if WF,d is
three-valued, then the three values are in Z, one of the values is 0, and d ≡ 1
(mod p − 1).
  So if WF,d is three-valued, these two theorems show that p | WF,d (u) for all
u ∈ F . Our main result is a much stronger lower bound on the p-divisibility.
Theorem 1.5. If F is of characteristic p and order q = pn , and if WF,d is
three-valued with values 0, a, and b, then one of the following holds:
  (i). vp (a), vp (b) > n/2; or

                          3. Congruences for V1
   As in previous sections, F is a finite field and d is a positive integer
with gcd(d, |F ∗ |) = 1. We recall V from equation (6), whose coefficient V1
appears in Corollary 2.6.iii. We deduce useful congruences for V1 by means
of a group action studied by McGuire [20].
Proposition 3.1. Let V1 = {v ∈ F : v d + (1 − v)d = 1} .
  (i). If |F | ≡ 0 (mod 3), then V1 ≡ 3 (mod 6).
 (ii). If |F | ≡ 1 (mod 3), then
       (a). if 2d−1 = 1 in F , then V1 ≡ 1 (mod 6), but
       (b). if 2d−1 6= 1 in F , then V1 ≡ 4 (mod 6).
(iii). If |F | ≡ 2 (mod 3), then
       (a). if 2d−1 = 1 in F , then V1 ≡ 5 (mod 6), but
       (b). if 2d−1 6= 1 in F , then V1 ≡ 2 (mod 6).
    Before proving the proposition, we interject two remarks.
Remark 3.2. When F is of order 2n , then we have 2 = 0 in F , so that
2d−1 6= 1, and we recover the result of McGuire (cf. [20, Corollary 1]) that
V1 ≡ 4 (mod 6) if n is even, and V1 ≡ 2 (mod 6) if n is odd.
Remark 3.3. When we are dealing with three-valued Weil sums, Theorem
1.4 shows that d ≡ 1 (mod p − 1), in which case 2d−1 = 1 in F by Fermat’s
Little Theorem whenever p 6= 2.
Proof of Proposition 3.1. We are counting modulo 6 the roots of f (X) =
X d + (1 − X)d − 1 in F . First of all, note that 0 and 1 are always roots of
f . Consider the action of the two involutions σ(x) = 1 − x and τ (x) = 1/x
on F r {0, 1}. Note that f (σ(x)) = f (x) and f (τ (x)) = −f (x)/xd , because
gcd(d, |F ∗ |) = 1 makes d odd when |F | is odd. Thus σ and τ map the roots
of f in F r {0, 1} to themselves.
   For generic F , our σ and τ generate a group G isomorphic to S3 , consisting
of the identity, σ, τ , τ ◦ σ(x) = 1/(1 − x), σ ◦ τ (x) = (x − 1)/x, and
                  DIVISIBILITY OF WEIL SUMS OF BINOMIALS                        7

σ ◦ τ ◦ σ(x) = τ ◦ σ ◦ τ (x) = x/(x − 1). The set of roots of f in F r {0, 1} is
preserved by G, so is partitioned into orbits under its action.
   A G-orbit contains 6 elements unless it contains a point fixed by some
nonidentity element of G. An x ∈ F r{0, 1} is fixed by a nonidentity element
of G if and only if it satisfies at least one of the following equations: x = 1−x,
x = 1/x, x = 1/(1 − x), x = (x − 1)/x, or x = x/(x − 1). Equivalently,
x equals at least one of: (i) −1, (ii) 2, (iii) the multiplicative inverse of 2
(which we shall call 1/2 when it exists), or (iv) a root of Φ6 (X) = X 2 −X +1,
the cyclotomic polynomial of index 6.
   If |F | ≡ 0 (mod 3), then −1 = 2 = 1/2, which is the double root of Φ6 .
Furthermore, f (−1) = 0 since d is odd. Thus the roots of f consist of 0, 1,
−1, and orbits of size 6, so that V1 ≡ 3 (mod 6).
   If F is of characteristic 2, then (i) −1 = 1, (ii) 2 = 0, and (iii) the
multiplicative inverse of 2 does not exist, so these are not points in F r
{0, 1}, and (iv) the roots of Φ6 are the primitive third roots of unity, ω and
ω −1 = 1 − ω, which lie in F if and only if |F | ≡ 1 (mod 3). In this case
f (ω) = f (ω −1 ) = 0 since d ≡ ±1 (mod 3), because gcd(d, |F ∗ |) = 1. So
the roots of f consist of 0 and 1 always, the third roots of unity if |F | ≡ 1
(mod 3), and orbits of size 6. Thus V1 ≡ 4 (mod 6) if |F | ≡ 1 (mod 3) and
V1 ≡ 2 (mod 6) if |F | ≡ 2 (mod 3).
   Now suppose that F is of characteristic p ≥ 5. Then (i) −1, (ii) 2, (iii)
1/2, and (iv) the roots of Φ6 are five distinct elements in characteristic p.
The roots ζ and ζ −1 = 1 − ζ of Φ6 lie in F if and only if |F | ≡ 1 (mod 6), or
equivalently, if and only if |F | ≡ 1 (mod 3). In this case f (ζ) = f (ζ −1 ) = 0
since d ≡ ±1 (mod 6) because gcd(d, |F ∗ |) = 1, so the roots of Φ6 are roots
of f if they are present in F . The three elements −1, 2, and 1/2 make up a
G-orbit, and are roots of f if and only if 0 = f (1/2) = (1/2)d + (1/2)d − 1 =
(1/2)d−1 − 1, that is, if and only if 2d−1 = 1. So the roots of f consist of 0
and 1 always, the roots of Φ6 if and only if |F | ≡ 1 (mod 3), the elements
−1, 2, and 1/2 if and only if 2d−1 = 1 in F , and orbits of size 6. Adding
these counts together modulo 6 according to the four possible cases finishes
the proof.                                                                      

                          5. Proof of Theorem 1.6
   Throughout this section, we use the definition of WF,d (u) from (1). We
prove Theorem 1.6 using two results of Aubry, Katz, and Langevin [1]. The
first key result is Corollary 4.2 of that paper.
Proposition 5.1 (Aubry-Katz-Langevin, 2013). Let K be a finite field and
L an extension of K of finite degree, and suppose that d is a positive integer
with gcd(d, |L∗ |) = 1. Then
                 min vp (WL,d (u)) ≤ [L : K] · min∗ vp (WK,d (u)).
                 u∈L∗                          u∈K

     The second result is part of Corollary 4.4 of the same paper.
Proposition 5.2 (Aubry-Katz-Langevin, 2013). Let K be a finite field of
characteristic p, and let L be the quadratic extension of K. Let d be a
positive integer with gcd(d, |L∗ |) = 1, and suppose that d is degenerate over
K but not over L. Then
                          min vp (WL,d (u)) = [K : Fp ].
                          u∈L∗

     We now recall and prove Theorem 1.6.
Theorem 5.3. Let F be a field of characteristic p and order q = pn , with n
a power of 2. Let d be a positive integer with gcd(d, q −1) = 1 such that WF,d
is three-valued. Then there is some u ∈ F ∗ such that vp (WF,d (u)) ≤ n/2.
Proof. By Theorem 1.4, d is degenerate over Fp . But d is not degenerate over
F since WF,d is three-valued (cf. (2)). Proceeding by successive quadratic
extensions from Fp to F , there must be subfields, say K and L, of F with
[L : K] = 2 and d degenerate over K but not L. Then by Propositions 5.1
and 5.2, we have
                  min vp (WF,d (u)) ≤ [F : L] · min∗ vp (WL,d (u))
                 u∈F ∗                          u∈L
                                    = [F : L][K : Fp ],

so that there is some u ∈ F ∗ with vp (WF,d (u)) ≤ [F : Fp ]/[L : K] = n/2.     
