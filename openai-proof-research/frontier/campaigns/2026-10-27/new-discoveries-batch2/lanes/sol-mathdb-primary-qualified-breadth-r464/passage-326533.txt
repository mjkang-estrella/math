12:                                                                                      Abstract
13:                                                    Given a statistical model, the maximum likelihood degree is the number of complex
14:                                                solutions to the likelihood equations for generic data. We consider discrete algebraic
15:                                                statistical models and study the solutions to the likelihood equations when the data
16:                                                contain zeros and are no longer generic. Focusing on sampling and model zeros, we
17:                                                show that, in these cases, the solutions to the likelihood equations are contained in a
18:                                                previously studied variety, the likelihood correspondence. The number of these solutions
19:                                                give a lower bound on the ML degree, and the problem of finding critical points to the
20:                                                likelihood function can be partitioned into smaller and computationally easier problems
21:                                                involving sampling and model zeros. We use this technique to compute a lower bound
22:                                                on the ML degree for 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 tensors of border rank ≤ 2 and 3 × n tables of
23:                                                rank ≤ 2 for n = 11, 12, 13, 14, the first four values of n for which the ML degree was
24:                                                previously unknown.

110:     In this paper we explore the algebraic considerations of maximum likelihood estimation
111: when the data contains sampling and model zeros. In Theorem 10 of Section 3, we show
112: how solutions to the maximum likelihood estimation problems for data with zeros on X are
113: contained in the likelihood correspondence of X. This result gives statistical meaning to the
114: likelihood correspondence when ui is equal to zero and we can use Theorem 10 to compute
115: a lower bound on the ML degree of a variety X.
