17:                                                      A balanced coloring of a graph G means a triple {P1 , P2 , X} of
18:                                                   mutually disjoint subsets of the vertex-set V (G) such that V (G) =
19:                                                   P1 ⊎ P2 ⊎ X and |P1 | = |P2 |. A balanced decomposition associated with
20:                                                   the balanced coloring V (G) = P1 ⊎P2 ⊎X of G is defined as a partition
21:                                                   of V (G) = V1 ⊎ · · · ⊎ Vr (for some r) such that, for every i ∈ {1, · · · , r},
22:                                                   the subgraph G[Vi ] of G is connected and |Vi ∩P1 | = |Vi ∩P2 |. Then the
23:                                                   balanced decomposition number of a graph G is defined as the minimum
24:                                                   integer s such that, for every balanced coloring V (G) = P1 ⊎ P2 ⊎ X of
25:                                                   G, there exists a balanced decomposition V (G) = V1 ⊎ · · · ⊎ Vr whose
26:                                                   every element Vi (i = 1, · · · , r) has at most s vertices. S. Fujita and
27:                                                   H. Liu [SIAM J. Discrete Math. 24, (2010), pp. 1597–1616] proved
28:                                                   a nice theorem which states that the balanced decomposition num-
