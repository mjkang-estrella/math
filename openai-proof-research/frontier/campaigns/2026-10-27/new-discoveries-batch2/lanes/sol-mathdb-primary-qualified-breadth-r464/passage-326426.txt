59:     Games of this form have previously been studied in [9], from the point of view of combinatorics on
60: words. The article in question concentrates on subshifts in which no long enough approximate squares, or
61: patterns approximately of the form ww, occur. The results of the paper state that for certain definitions
62: of ‘approximate’ and ‘long enough’, A has a winning strategy even if every choice for A is followed
63: by some t choices for B. A similar notion has also appeared in [6], where the authors study the set
64: of coordinates of a given configuration of a subshift that can be chosen independently of the rest, and
65: the notion of independence entropy arising from these considerations. Their methods are related to our
66: combinatorial arguments in Section 5.
67:     In this article, we take a different view on the situation. Instead of asking whether a given subshift
68: admits a winning strategy for A when a prescribed order for the turns of A and B is used, we fix a subshift
69: X and study the set of turn orders for which A has a winning strategy on it. We call this set the winning
70: shift of X, since it actually turns out to be a subshift over the alphabet {A, B}. We study the realization
