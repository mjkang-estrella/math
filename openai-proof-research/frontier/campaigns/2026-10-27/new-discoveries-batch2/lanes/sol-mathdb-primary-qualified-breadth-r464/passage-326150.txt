15:                                                                                                Abstract
16:                                                     We consider the problem of coloring the squares of graphs of bounded maximum average
17:                                                 degree, that is, the problem of coloring the vertices while ensuring that two vertices that are
18:                                                 adjacent or have a common neighbour receive different colors.
19:                                                     Borodin et al. proved in 2004 and 2008 that the squares of planar graphs of girth at least
20:                                                 seven and sufficiently large maximum degree ∆ are list (∆ + 1)-colorable, while the squares
21:                                                 of some planar graphs of girth six and arbitrarily large maximum degree are not. By Euler’s
22:                                                 Formula, planar graphs of girth at least 6 are of maximum average degree less than 3, and
23:                                                 planar graphs of girth at least 7 are of maximum average degree less than 145 < 3.
24:                                                     We strengthen their result and prove that there exists a function f such that the square of
25:                                                 any graph with maximum average degree m < 3 and maximum degree ∆ ≥ f (m) is list (∆+1)-
26:                                                 colorable. This bound of 3 is optimal in the sense that the above-mentioned planar graphs with
27:                                                 girth 6 have maximum average degree less than 3 and arbitrarily large maximum degree, while
28:                                                 their square cannot be (∆ + 1)-colored. The same holds for list injective ∆-coloring.

58: Conjecture 1 (Wang and Lih [14]). For any integer k ≥ 5, there exists an integer M (k) such that for
59: every planar graph G satisfying g(G) ≥ k and ∆(G) ≥ M (k), χ2 (G) = ∆(G) +1.

61:     Conjecture 1 was proved in [5, 7, 12] to be true for k ≥ 7 and false for k = 6. An extension
62: of the k-coloring of the square is the list k-coloring of the square, where instead of having the same
63: set of k colors for the whole graph, every vertex is assigned some set of k colors and has to be
64: colored from it. Given a graph G, we call χ2ℓ (G) the minimal integer k such that the square of G
65: admits a list k-coloring for any list assignment. Obviously, coloring is a subcase of list coloring
66: (where the same color list is assigned to every vertex), so for any graph G, we have χ2ℓ (G) ≥ χ2 (G).
67: Thus, in the case of list-coloring, Conjecture 1 is also false for k = 6, and Borodin, Ivanova and
68: Neustroeva [8] proved it to be true for k ≥ 7.
