11:                                                    Abstract. We study certain linear representations of the knot group that induce
12:                                                    augmentations in knot contact homology. This perspective enhances our un-
13:                                                    derstanding of the relationship between the augmentation polynomial and the
14:                                                    A-polynomial of a knot. For example, we show that for 2-bridge knots the poly-
15:                                                    nomials agree and that this is never the case for (non-2-bridge) torus knots, nor
16:                                                    for a family of 3-bridge pretzel knots. In addition, we obtain a lower bound on
17:                                                    the meridional rank of the knot. As a consequence, our results give a new proof
18:                                                    that torus knots and a family of pretzel knots have meridional rank equal to their
19:                                                    bridge number.

22:                                              Let K be a knot in R3 . The knot contact homology HC∗ (K) of K is the Legendrian
23:                                           contact homology of a Legendrian torus over K in the unit cotangent bundle of

26:                                           of the DGA underlying HC∗ (K) are related to the A-polynomial introduced in
27:                                           [CCG+ 94]: the A-polynomial divides the augmentation polynomial AugK (λ, µ2 ).

59:    The meridional rank is bounded above by the bridge number of a knot. Problem
60: 1.11 of [Kir97], which is attributed to Cappell and Shaneson, asks whether every
61: knot with meridional rank n is an n-bridge knot. The lower bound from Theorem
62: 1.1 suggests that KCH representations might be used to study this problem.
63:    A KCH representation ρ : πK → GLn C induces an augmentation ǫ of HC∗ (K) (see
64: Section 3.1). Moreover, KCH representations can be made to have image in SLn C,
65: and from this one sees that the A-polynomial divides AugK (λ, µ2 ) (see section 3;
66: cf.[Ng08]). For m ∈ πK a meridian of K, and ℓ ∈ πK a 0-framed longitude, the values
67: ǫ(µ) and ǫ(λ) of the induced augmentation are particular eigenvalues of ρ(m) and
68: ρ(ℓ), respectively. With this in mind, we use n-dimensional KCH representations
69: to define for each n ≥ 2 the n-dimensional A-polynomial AnK (λ, µ) of K, with zero
70: locus being the closure of points in (C∗ )2 given by these eigenvalues.
