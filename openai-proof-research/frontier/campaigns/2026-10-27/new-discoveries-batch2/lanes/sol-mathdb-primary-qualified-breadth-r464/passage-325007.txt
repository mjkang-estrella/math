10:                                           In this note, I detail some conjectures related to Schubert calculus in types B and D.
11:                                           These are mentioned in [Wys12], but are not spelled out in a precise way there due to the
12:                                           fairly large amount of notation required to give precise statements. They are based upon
13:                                           an as yet conjectural understanding of the weak order poset of L-orbits on G/B, where
14:                                           (G, L) = (SO(2n + 1, C), C∗ × SO(2n − 1, C)) (type B) or (SO(2n, C), C∗ × SO(2n − 2, C))
15:                                           (type D). In each case, L is a Levi subgroup of L which is “spherical”, meaning it acts with
16:                                           finitely many orbits on the flag variety G/B. The idea is to identify the L-stable Richardson
17:                                           varieties with certain of the conjectural orbit parameters.
18:                                           Fix the following notation:
19:                                                • G a complex reductive algebraic group;
20:                                                • B, B − ⊆ G opposite Borel subgroups;
21:                                                • T = B ∩ B − , a maximal torus of G;
22:                                                • W = NG (T )/T , the Weyl group;
23:                                                • P, P − ⊆ G opposite parabolic subgroups containing B, B − , respectively;
24:                                                • L = P ∩ P − the common Levi factor of P, P − .

77: Below, I give specific conjectures on these matters. The conjectures on the orbit sets were
78: suggested first to me by Peter Trapa, so I attribute them to him. They have been verified
79: using ATLAS (available at http://www.liegroups.org/) through reasonably high rank.
80: Assuming those conjectures to be valid, we carry out the remaining steps for the type B and
81: D pairs mentioned above, identifying the L-stable Richardson varieties, matching them with
82: the proper combinatorial invariants for the L-orbit closures, and getting a rule for structure
83: constants from this matching. This portion of the argument can be proven just as we do
84: for the type A case in [Wys12], although I do not write down the proofs here. What makes
85: them conjectures at this point is simply the lack of a proof of Trapa’s conjectures regarding
86: the set of L-orbits.
87: The Schubert calculus conjectures themselves have been tested using C code written by
88: Dominic Searles which implements the algorithm of [Knu03]. Conjecture 6 (type B) has
89: been verified to hold through rank 6, and in rank 7 for all suitable u, v with l(u) + l(v) ≤ 12.
90: Conjecture 10 (type D) has been verified to hold through rank 7.
