29:                                           1       Introduction
30:                                           The problem of finding small sets that block every line passing through a unit square was first
31:                                           considered by Mazurkiewicz in 1916 [34]; see also [3], [22]. Let C be a convex body in the plane.
32:                                           Following Bagemihl [3], a set Γ is an opaque set or a barrier for C, if Γ meets all lines that intersect
33:                                           C. A barrier does not need to be connected; it may consist of one or more rectifiable arcs and its
34:                                           parts may lie anywhere in the plane, including the exterior of C; see [3], [5].
35:                                               What is the length of the shortest barrier for a given convex body C? In spite of considerable
36:                                           efforts, the answer to this question is not known even in the simplest instances, such as when
37:                                           C is a square, a disk, or an equilateral triangle; see [6], [7, Problem A30], [14], [16], [17], [20,
38:                                           Section 8.11], [23, Problem 12]. Some entertaining variants of the problem appeared in different
39:                                           forms in the literature [2, 5, 21, 25, 29, 30].
40:                                               A barrier blocks any line of sight across the region C or detects any ray that passes through
41:                                           it. Potential applications are in guarding and surveillance [8]. Here we focus on the case when
42:                                           C is a square. The shortest barrier known for the unit square, of length 2.639 . . ., is illustrated
43:                                           in Figure 1 (right). It is conjectured to be optimal. The current best lower bound, 2, has been
44:                                           established by Jones [24] in 19641 .
