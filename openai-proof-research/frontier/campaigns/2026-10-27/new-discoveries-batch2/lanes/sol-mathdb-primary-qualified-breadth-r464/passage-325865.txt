14:                                          1         First encounter
15:                                          My ﬁrst encounter with Paul Erdős took place in 1962 at the Mátraháza Guest House
16:                                          of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences. I was a high school student and was rather
17:                                          embarrassed by the solemn formalities, especially at the dinner tables. I was pleased to
18:                                          hear the signs of some unaccepted behavior from a neighboring table, where an “old”
19:                                          man about ﬁfty was regulated by his mother: “Pali you should keep your fork properly!”
20:                                          I soon learned that the unruled boy is a famous mathematician who travels around the
21:                                          world with his mother. Next day I had opportunity to play ping pong against “Pali” and
22:                                          I was very angry upon being beaten by such an old man playing in a ridiculous style. As
23:                                          a consolation he told me what a graph is and what does Turán theorem say about the
24:                                          number of edges in a graph that does not contain Kk+1 . “Adding any edge to the Turán
25:                                          graph we get a Kk+1 but what is the smallest graph with this property?” - asked during
26:                                          the revenge game which I lost again. Then he left but the problem bugged me and about
27:                                          a year later, after a lecture he gave in Budapest, I handed him my typewritten solution
28:                                          for the case when the number of vertices is large in terms of k. It was disappointing
29:                                          to learn that his question was not an unsolved problem, but his result with Hajnal and
30:                                          Moon, [10]. Two years later, at Eötvös University, I listened to Béla Bollobás’ talk at the

58: 2.1    Cyles in graphs without proper subgraphs of minimum de-
59:        gree 3
60: The following observation is due to Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp [9].
61: 
62:     • Graphs with n vertices and 2n−1 edges must contain proper subgraphs of minimum
63:       degree 3 but this fails for graphs with n vertices and 2n − 2 edges, for example the
64:       wheel is such a graph.

78: 2.2     Large chordal subgraphs
79: In [17] we discussed the size of chordal (interval, threshold) subgraphs present in graphs
80: with several ranges of edges. One particular jump is observed at graphs with n vertices
81: and n2 /3 edges.
82: 
83:      • Any graph with n vertices and at least n2 /3 edges contains a chordal subgraph
84:        with at least 2n − 3 edges. The complete tripartite graph shows that this is sharp.
