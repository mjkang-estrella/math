                                               ONE PHASE PROBLEM FOR TWO POSITIVE HARMONIC
                                               FUNCTION: BELOW THE CODIMENSION 1 THRESHOLD

                                                                            ALEXANDER VOLBERG


                                                  Abstract. What can be said about the domain Ω in Rn for which its Green’s
                                                  function G(z) satisfies G(z)  dist(z, ∂Ω)δ ? What can we say about Ω if the
                                                  Boundary Harnack Principle holds in the form u/v = real analytic on the part E
                                                  of its boundary? Here u, v are positive harmonic functions on Ω vanishing on E.
                                                  Is this part of the boundary also nice? We discuss these questions below and give




arXiv:2205.03687v3 [math.AP] 20 May 2022
                                                  answers in very special cases.




                                                                     1. Preliminaries and notations
                                              In recent years several outstanding problems at the edge of harmonic analysis and
                                           geometric measure theory in Rn were solved, see e.g. [ENV], [HMM], [NTV],
                                           [DM], [DFM] and the literature therein. However, these works revealed a new
                                           threshold: the technique works if the codimension of a (very irregular) set in question
                                           has codimension at most 1 in Rn .
                                              Here we present several questions (some of them looking very simple) and a tiny
                                           bit of answers breaking down this threshold. We consider only n = 2 case, but most
                                           of the problems (and some solutions) presented below work for all n.
                                              Two phase problem for positive harmonic function can be vaguely described as
                                           follows: there are two disjoint domains U, V in Rn with a common part of their
                                           boundaries, E called interface. Let O be an open neighborhood of E.There are two
                                           positive harmonic functions: u in O ∩ U and v in O ∩ V. Both functions vanish
                                           on E (in some sense), but there ratio is well defined on E (again in some sense)
                                           and this ratio is a nice function on E. One then concludes that then E is nice
                                           (in some sense). The example of this kind of problem can be found in [KT1],
                                           [AMTV], where u = GU (·, p), v = GV (·, q), p ∈ U, q ∈ V, two Green functions of
                                           corresponding domains and the assumption on the ratio of u and v is formulated in
                                           terms of harmonic measures ωU (·, p), ωV (·, q) of two disjoint domains being mutually
                                           absolutely continuous (and non-zero) on the interface E.
                                              On the other hand, a one phase problem for positive harmonic function defined
                                           near the boundary and vanishing at the boundary (or for harmonic measure) usually
                                           compares the harmonic measure of one given domain with the Hausdorff measure
                                           on the boundary. The literature here is rather vast, see, for example, [AHM3TV],
                                           [KT2], [KT3], [KPT] and citation therein.
                                              We wish to consider a one phase problem in domain Ω, with two positive harmonic
                                           functions u, v in O∩∂Ω, where O is just an open disc (ball), u = 0 = v on E := O∩∂Ω.
                                           The main assumption is that the ratio uv makes a rather nice function on E. We
                                           would wish to prove that then E is nice. One can think about these two harmonic
                                           functions as two Green’s functions of the domain Ω but with different poles. Then
                                           we are considering the comparison of harmonic measures related to two different
                                           poles, rather than comparison of harmonic measure and Hausdorff measure on the
                                           boundary.
                                              Now we need to explain what “nice” means and what kind of domains Ω ⊂ Rn we
                                           consider. Let us remind that if Ω is Lipschitz domain then log uv is Hölder continuous
                                           on E = B(x, r) ∩ Ω, x ∈ ∂Ω, by Jerison–Kenig theorem [JK]. Moreover, by their
                                           result the same is true for NTA domains, see the definition in [JK].

   We will deal mostly (but not exclusively) with plane domains, n = 2, but our Ω can
very well be infinitely connected, with rather complicated (but compact) boundary.
However, we will require several things from Ω to make it “one sided NTA domain”.
   So, we require that ∂Ω has 1) capacity density condition (CDC): cap(B(z, δ)) 
cap(B(z, δ) ∩ ∂Ω) for all small δ and all z ∈ ∂Ω; 2) corkscrew condition, see [JK];
3) Harnack chain condition, see [JK].
   The NTA domains of course satisfy 1), 2), 3). But another example that is impor-
tant in what follows is the domain Ω whose boundary ∂Ω is a sufficiently nice Cantor
set, for example a self-similar set in the sense of conformal dynamical systems.
   Let us explain briefly what is conformal self-similar sets (conformal repellers). We
consider more general sets below, but to have in mind those special examples is a
good idea. So, Let O be a topological discs and O1 , . . . , Od , d ≥ 2, be topological
discs with disjoint closures inside O, we require also that ∂O were disjoint with
closures of all Oj . Let f = (f1 , . . . , fd ) be a collection of conformal maps of Oi
onto O. By considering fi−1 (Oj ), i, j = 1, . . . , d we see d2 topological discs of the
second generation (d groups of d discs each) that are mapped onto O conformally
by iterative power f 2 = f ◦ f (think about f as a piecewise conformal map). By
iterating this construction we have d3 discs of the third generation, et cetera. Finally
we can consider the unions of discs of generation k, call ot Uk and J = ∩k Uk . Domain
Ω = C \ J is a typical example of domains we consider here, and O itself is atypical
example of a neighborhood of J = ∂Ω.

ary Harnack principle (BHP), meaning that for any two harmonic and positive u, v
in Ω∩B(z, r) (z ∈ ∂Ω, r being small) that vanish on B(z, r)∩∂Ω, one has the Hölder
property for u/v on B(z, r) ∩ ∂Ω in the following sense:

    We now list several conjectures in the order of decreasing difficulty.
Conjecture 1.3. Let Ω be our one sided NTA, u, v be as above, in particular, let
(1.2) holds. Then either u = λv for some constant λ or B(z, r) ∩ ∂Ω is real analytic
maybe with the exception of a set of dimension n − 2.
Conjecture 1.4. Let n = 2. Let Ω be our one sided NTA, u, v be as above, in
particular, let (1.2) hold, but let also R = |A|, where A is a non-constant holomorphic
function on B(z, r). Then either u = λv for some constant λ or B(z, r) ∩ ∂Ω is real
analytic maybe with the exception of a set of isolated points.
   This latter conjecture is proved in [VaVo], [VaVo1] for the case of simply con-
nected Ω. The paper uses a very powerful result of Sakai [Sa] that describes the
so-called Schwarz functions in arbitrary planar domains. However, the reduction
of Conjecture 1.4 to Sakai’s theorem works only in simply (or finitely) connected
domains. Any solution of Conjecture 1.4 should first eliminate the possibility of
infinitely connected domain. For the simply connected case Conjecture 1.4 turned

out to be closely related to the so-called Nevanlinna domains, which were exten-
sively studied e.g. in [DK], [BaF], [BeF], [BeBoF], [MM] and the literature cited

Conjecture 1.5. Let n = 2. Let Let Ω be our one sided NTA, u, v be as above, in

  The conclusion is natural. Cantor structure of the boundary unequivocally says
that boundary is not nice and definitely is not real analytic. Therefore, the only
option that should exist for u, v is to be proportional.
Conjecture 1.7. Let n = 2. Let Ω be our one sided NTA, u, v be as above, in
particular, let (1.2) hold, but let also R = |A|, where A is a holomorphic function
on B(z, r). Let, in addition, Ω be such that its Green’s function (with some pole)
satisfies
          c1 dist(z, ∂Ω)δ ≤ G(z, p) ≤ c2 dist(z, ∂Ω)δ ,   for some δ ∈ (0, 1]     (1.8)
for all z ∈ Ω sufficiently close to ∂Ω and for some δ > 0. Then either u = λv for
some constant λ or δ = 1 and B(z, r) ∩ ∂Ω is piecewise real analytic.
   It is easy to see that assumption (1.8) (if δ < 1) implies that ∂Ω is a regular
Cantor set if ∂Ω is a conformal self-similar set, see [AV] . So the proof of Conjecture
1.5 implies Conjecture (1.7) at least for conformal self-similar sets. But we want to
formulate a very “easy" conjecture that claims part of Conjecture 1.7. The following
statement looks very easy, but I can prove it only in one special case (see below). It
is interesting in all dimensions n ≥ 2.
Conjecture 1.9. Let for Ω ⊂ Rn we have (1.8) with δ > 0. Then 1) δ = 1
necessarily; 2) ∂Ω has a certain weak smoothness, at least it is rectifiable.
   Those conjectures appear naturally in a certain unsolved problem in complex
dynamics. Conjecture 1.9 is especially intriguing because it looks so simple. Papers
[DM] and [DFM] consider a very closely related problem of prevalent approximation
of Green’s function by the distance. But for co-dimensions bigger than one cases the
Green’s function is pertinent to a certain degenerate elliptic equation as far as I
understand. Still the technique of these papers can probably be useful for solving
this conjecture.
