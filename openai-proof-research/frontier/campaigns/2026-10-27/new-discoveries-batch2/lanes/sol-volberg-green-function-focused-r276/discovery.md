## Pivot: exact Hausdorff equality (Problem10)

Let Lambda_h use the source's radius convention. Seek a non-polar real compact K of positive Hausdorff dimension with mu_K=Lambda_h restricted to K, or prove none exists. The measure must have total mass one. Rescaling h can fix a scalar normalization but cannot erase a spatially varying density.

Mechanism A: retain DJJ's variable displacement embedding f of {−1,1}^N and its flat logarithmic potential. Its actual Lemma3.3 gives mu_K=f_*Bernoulli, hence mu_K(K_w)=2^{-|w|} for every cylinder K_w. To obtain literal equality one must independently produce ONE gauge h with Lambda_h(K_w)=2^{-|w|} for EVERY finite word w. This is a sufficient and necessary bridge in this family: the disjoint clopen cylinders form a generating algebra; two finite Borel measures agreeing there agree on K (uniqueness on a generating pi-system). Thus the missing assertion is exact cylinder costs, including lower bounds over arbitrary fine covers, not merely upper bounds from the natural cylinder cover. DJJ controls bounded comparability only. No assertion that its displacement parameters also enforce these infinitely many identities is made.

A precise limitation on gauge repair: if h(t)/t^d -> c in (0,infinity), then Lambda_h(E)=c Lambda_{t^d}(E) for every E. Indeed, for every epsilon>0, at sufficiently small cover radii (c-epsilon)t^d<=h(t)<=(c+epsilon)t^d; take infima, fine-cover limits, then epsilon down to zero. Consequently an asymptotically constant change of the power gauge cannot correct a nonconstant Radon–Nikodym density. General non-power/oscillating gauges remain outside this limitation. Neither upper/lower Ahlfors bounds nor bi-Lipschitz equivalence proves the missing identities.

Mechanism B: test the rigidity direction using the exact equilibrium-potential condition. This gives a complete restricted diagnostic, not a universal obstruction. Fix 0<r<1/2 and the homogeneous two-map Cantor set K=rK union (1-r+rK). With d=log2/log(1/r), its normalized d-Hausdorff measure is the equal-weight invariant probability mu. If this mu were equilibrium, its logarithmic potential V(x)=integral log|x-t|dmu(t) would be the same finite constant b on K. The set and measure are d-regular, so V is continuous, and equilibrium equality quasi-everywhere would extend to all K (every relative neighborhood has positive capacity). For x=ru in rK, the left-copy contribution to V(x) is (log r+V(u))/2=(log r+b)/2. The other contribution is F(x)/2, where F(x)=integral log(1-r+rt-x)dmu(t). For x in [0,r], F'(x)=-integral(1-r+rt-x)^(-1)dmu(t)<0; differentiation is justified by separation1-2r>0. Thus V cannot be constant on the left copy containing at least two points. Contradiction. The same argument excludes proportionality of equilibrium to the power Hausdorff measure for this family. It is a classical self-similar diagnostic, not new mathematics and not an answer for variable Cantor geometries or arbitrary gauges.

The two mechanisms therefore meet at a genuine missing lemma: does there exist a positive-dimensional variable geometry whose flat probability logarithmic potential AND exact single-gauge cylinder cover costs can be imposed simultaneously?
