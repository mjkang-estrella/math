                                                                                                       Abstract
                                                Let s be a positive integer. Our goal is to find all finite abelian groups G that contain a 2-subset A for which the
                                             undirected Cayley graph Γ(G, A) has diameter at most s. We provide a complete answer when G is cyclic, and a conjecture
                                             and some partial answers when G is noncyclic.

                                                 Key words: Abelian group, Cayley graph, diameter, sumset, signed sumset.

    When equality holds, we say that A is a perfect s-basis of G: in this case, every element of G arises as the sum of at most
s (not-necessarily distinct) elements of A in a unique way. Trivially, the set of nonzero elements is a perfect 1-basis in any
group G, and the 1-element set consisting of a generator of G is a perfect s-basis in the cyclic group of order s + 1. It turns
out that there are no others:

Theorem 1.2 (Bajnok, Berson, and Just; cf. [2]) If a subset A of a finite abelian group G is a perfect s-basis, then
s = 1 and A = G \ {0}, or G ∼
                            = Zs+1 and |A| = 1.

    Given the scarcity of perfect bases, it is natural to ask for the largest groups with a given diameter and size of generating
set. The paper [9] asks the following questions:

   • What is the largest possible order of an abelian group G with an m-subset A for which the diameter of ~Γ(G, A) is at
     most s?
   • What is the largest possible order of a cyclic group G with an m-subset A for which the diameter of ~Γ(G, A) is at most
     s?

   After a series of papers [10, 5, 8, 9, 4], these two questions have been completely solved for the case of m = 2. We have
the following two results.

Theorem 1.3 (Morillo, Fiol, and Fàbrega; cf. [10]) Let s be a positive integer. The largest possible order of a cyclic
group G with a 2-subset for which the diameter of ~Γ(G, A) is at most s is given by (s2 + 4s + 3)/3 .

Theorem 1.4 (Fiol; cf. [4]) Let s be any positive integer. The largest possible order of an abelian group G with a 2-subset
for which the diameter of ~Γ(G, A) is at most s is given by (s2 + 4s + 4)/3 .

   Observe that the quantities in Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 agree, unless s ≡ 1 mod 3; for that case one can verify that the
group G = Zk × Z3k has the s-basis {(0, 1), (1, 3k − 1)}, where k = (s + 2)/3. However, the question specifically for noncyclic
groups is still open when s 6≡ 1 mod 3.
    Let us now return to the undirected case. When equality occurs in Proposition 1.1, then A is called a perfect s-spanning
set in G. Perfect spanning sets can be easily characterized for m = 1 and s = 1. Note that a(1, s) = 2s + 1 and, clearly, for

                                                               2
any a ∈ Z2s+1 with gcd(a, 2s + 1) = 1, the set {a} is a perfect s-spanning set in Z2s+1 . Also, a(m, 1) = 2m + 1, and for any
group G of order 2m + 1, every asymmetric subset of size m (that is, a set containing exactly one of any nonzero element or
its inverse) is a perfect 1-spanning set in G.
   From (1) we get a(2, s) = 2s2 + 2s + 1, and below we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.5 Let s be a positive integer. The largest possible order of a cyclic group G with a 2-subset for which the diameter
of Γ(G, A) is at most s is given by 2s2 + 2s + 1.

   For noncyclic groups the situation seems a bit more nuanced. We have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6 Let s ≥ 3 be an integer.

   • If 2s + 1 is prime, then the largest noncyclic group with an s-spanning set of size 2 has order 2s2 .
   • If 2s + 1 is composite and p is its smallest prime divisor, then the largest noncyclic group with an s-spanning set of size
     2 has order 2s2 + 2s − (p2 − 1)/2.

In particular, for any noncyclic group G with an s-spanning set of size 2 we have |G| ≤ 2s2 + 2s − 4.

(We add that the largest noncyclic group with a 2-spanning set of size 2 is Z3 × Z3 .)
   Below we prove that the quantities in Conjecture 1.6 are lower bounds.

Theorem 1.7 Let s be a positive integer.

   • The largest noncyclic group with an s-spanning set of size 2 has order at least 2s2 .
   • If 2s + 1 is composite and p is any prime divisor of 2s + 1, then the largest noncyclic group with an s-spanning set of
     size 2 has order at least 2s2 + 2s − (p2 − 1)/2.

    Establishing upper bounds appears to be more difficult, and we only have the partial results that we summarize here. We
first note that a noncyclic group that has a spanning set of size 2 must have rank 2 and thus is isomorphic to Zc × Zkc for
some integers c ≥ 2 and k ≥ 1.
   We say that the group Zc × Zkc is s-regular for some positive integer s, if it possesses an s-spanning set of the form
{(1, u), (1, v)} for some u, v ∈ Zkc . While we have no precise count on the number of s-regular groups, it appears that most
groups of rank 2 that have an s-spanning set of size 2 are s-regular. For example, for s = 10, of the 105 groups of rank 2
that have an s-spanning set of size 2, 103 are s-regular. (The two exceptions are Z8 × Z16 and Z10 × Z20 .) In Theorem 3.1
below we prove for every odd prime c and positive integer k that if Zc × Zkc has an s-spanning set at all, then it is s-regular.
   Our results for upper bounds can be summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.8 Let s be a positive integer, and let G = Zc × Zkc be an s-regular group for some integers c ≥ 2 and k ≥ 1.

   • If c = 3 and s + 1 is divisible by 3, then |G| ≤ 2s2 + 1.

   • If c is odd and s is divisible by c, then |G| ≤ 2s2 + s, and if 2s + 1 is divisible by c, then |G| ≤ 2s2 + 2s − (c2 − 1)/2.
   • In all other cases, |G| ≤ 2s2 .

As a consequence of our results, we can prove that for s ≥ 3, every s-regular abelian group of rank 2 has order at most
2s2 + 2s − 4, with equality if, and only if, it is isomorphic to Z3 × Z(2s2 +2s−4)/3 for some s ≡ 1 mod 3 (See Theorem 3.3
below).
    For c = 2 we have exact results, even without the assumption that the group is s-regular:

Theorem 1.9 Let k and s be positive integers. The largest possible order of a group of the form Z2 × Z2k that has an
s-spanning set of size 2 is 2s2 when s is even and 2s2 − 2 when s is odd.

In Section 4 below we provide some further results on groups of the form Z2 × Z2k .
    For m ≥ 3, the largest order of abelian groups with an m-subset for which the diameter of their Cayley graphs is at most
s is generally unknown, though some small cases for cyclic groups were displayed by Haanpää in [7], by Hsu and Jia in [8],
and by Graham and Sloane in [6].
   Of particular interest are perfect spanning sets. An m-subset A of G is called a perfect s-spanning set, if every element
of G arises as a signed sum of at most s terms in a unique way; in this case we have |G| = a(m, s). Above we mentioned
perfect spanning sets for m = 1, for s = 1, and for m = 2; we believe that there are no others.

Conjecture 1.10 If an m-subset A of a finite abelian group G is a perfect s-spanning set, then one of the following holds:

    • m = 1, G ∼
               = Z2s+1 , and A = {a} for some a ∈ Z2s+1 with gcd(a, 2s + 1) = 1;
    • s = 1, and G is the disjoint union of {0}, A, and −A; or
    • m = 2, G ∼
               = Z2s2 +2s+1 , and A = d · {s, s + 1} for some d ∈ Z2s+1 with gcd(d, 2s2 + 2s + 1) = 1.

   In Sections 2 and 3 below, we present our results on lower bounds and upper bounds, respectively. In Section 4 we treat
the case of the groups Z2 × Z2k in more detail.



2     Constructions and lower bounds

In this section we provide some explicit constructions for s-spanning sets of size 2. We denote the s-span of a 2-subset
A = {a1 , a2 } of G by
                                   hAis = {λ1 a1 + λ2 a2 | λ1 , λ2 ∈ Z, |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s}.
It will be helpful to have the following facts available.

Lemma 2.1 For a given positive integer s, let

                                         I(s) = {(λ1 , λ2 ) | λ1 , λ2 ∈ Z, |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s}.

    1. For a given integer h with |h| ≤ s, the number of ordered pairs (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ I(s) with λ1 + λ2 = h equals s + 1 when s
       and h have the same parity, and s otherwise.
    2. There are (s + 1)2 ordered pairs (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ I(s) where λ1 + λ2 and s have the same parity, and s2 where λ1 + λ2 and s
       have opposite parity.
    3. There are 2s2 + 2s + 1 elements in I(s).



                                                                  4
   Proof. By symmetry, we may assume that h ≥ 0. Observe that the ‘leftmost’ integer point (λ1 , λ2 ) on the line x + y = h
within |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s is
                                         (− ⌊(s − h)/2⌋ , h + ⌊(s − h)/2⌋) ,
and the ‘rightmost’ such point is
                                                   (⌊(s + h)/2⌋ , h − ⌊(s + h)/2⌋) ,
a set of s + 1 points when s and h have the same parity, and s points otherwise. This establishes the first claim, from which
our other two claims follow easily.                                                                                         ✷

Proposition 2.2 For all positive integers s and n with

                                                         2 ≤ n ≤ 2s2 + 2s + 1,

the set {s, s + 1} is an s-spanning set in Zn .

   Proof. The fact that n cannot be larger than 2s2 + 2s + 1 follows from Proposition 1.1. We verify that for every n in the
range given above, we have h{s, s + 1}is = Zn .
   Considering the elements of
                                        S = {λ1 s + λ2 (s + 1) | λ1 , λ2 ∈ Z, |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s}
in Z (rather than Zn ), we see that they lie in the interval [−(s2 + s), (s2 + s)]. Since the index set

                                           I(s) = {(λ1 , λ2 ) | λ1 , λ2 ∈ Z, |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s}

contains exactly 2s2 + 2s + 1 elements (see Lemma 2.1), it suffices to prove that no integer in [−(s2 + s), (s2 + s)] can be
written as an element of S in two different ways.
   For that, suppose that
                                                  λ1 s + λ2 (s + 1) = λ′1 s + λ′2 (s + 1)
for some (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ I(s) and (λ′1 , λ′2 ) ∈ I(s); without loss of generality, we can assume that λ2 ≥ λ′2 . Our equation implies
that λ2 − λ′2 is divisible by s and is, therefore, equal to 0, s, or 2s.
   If λ2 − λ′2 = 2s, then λ2 = s and λ′2 = −s; since |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s and |λ′1 | + |λ′2 | ≤ s, this can only happen if λ1 = λ′1 = 0,
but this case leads to a contradiction with our equation.
   Assume, next, that λ2 − λ′2 = s. Our equation then yields λ1 − λ′1 = −s − 1. In this case, we have λ1 ≤ 0, λ′1 ≥ 0, λ2 ≥ 0,
and λ′2 ≤ 0, and we see that

                       |λ1 | + |λ2 | = −λ1 + λ2 = (s + 1 − λ′1 ) + (s + λ′2 ) = 2s + 1 − (|λ′1 | + |λ′2 |) ≥ s + 1,

but that is a contradiction.
    This leaves us with the case that λ2 = λ′2 , which implies λ1 = λ′1 , as claimed, and therefore the set {s, s + 1} is s-spanning
in Zn .                                                                                                                            ✷

Proposition 2.3 For every positive integer s, the set {(0, 1), (1, 1)} is an s-spanning set in Zs × Z2s .

assume that 0 ≤ b ≤ s. We consider three subcases.
   When b ≥ a, we let λ1 = b − a and λ2 = a. We then have

                                                      λ1 (0, 1) + λ2 (1, 1) = (a, b)

and
                                                     |λ1 | + |λ2 | = λ1 + λ2 = b ≤ s,
so (a, b) ∈ h{(0, 1), (1, 1)}is .
    Next, when 2a − s ≤ b < a, we again let λ1 = b − a and λ2 = a. In this case, we have

                                                 |λ1 | + |λ2 | = −λ1 + λ2 = 2a − b ≤ s,

so again (a, b) ∈ h{(0, 1), (1, 1)}is .
    Finally, when b < 2a − s, we let λ1 = b − a + s and λ2 = a − s. Here we have

                                                     λ1 (0, 1) + λ2 (1, 1) = (a − s, b),

Theorem 2.4 Let c and s be positive integers so that the remainder of s mod c is at least (c− 1)/2. Let u = ⌊s/c⌋, v = ⌈s/c⌉,
and k = 2uv. Then A = {(1, u), (1, v)} is an s-spanning set in G = Zc × Zck .

   Proof. Let H = h(1, u)i be the subgroup of G generated by the element (1, u). Since H has order 2cv, it has cu cosets in
G. We claim that these cosets are λ2 · (1, v) + H, where λ2 = 0, 1, . . . , cu − 1. In order to prove this, we need to show that
these cu cosets are pairwise distinct.
   Suppose, then, that µ1 (1, v) and µ2 (1, v) are in the same coset of H for some 0 ≤ µ1 , µ2 ≤ cu − 1. This implies that
(µ2 − µ1 ) · (1, v) is an element of H, so there exists an integer µ for which

                                                      (µ2 − µ1 ) · (1, v) = µ · (1, u).

From the first coordinates, this means that µ2 − µ1 − µ is divisible by c, and from the second coordinates we see that
(µ2 − µ1 )v − µu, which, since v = u + 1, equals (µ2 − µ1 − µ)u + (µ2 − µ1 ), is divisible by ck and thus by cu. Combining
these, we get that µ2 − µ1 must also be divisible by cu, but that is only possible if µ1 = µ2 .
    Let g ∈ G be arbitrary. According to our claim, we have integers λ1 ∈ [−cv + 1, cv] and λ2 ∈ [0, cu − 1] for which

                                                         g = λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v).

By assumption, we have integers q (quotient) and r (remainder) with (c − 1)/2 ≤ r ≤ c − 1 for which s = qc + r. We then
have

                   |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ cv + (cu − 1) = c(q + 1) + (cq − 1) = 2cq + c − 1 = 2s + (c − 2r − 1) ≤ 2s.                  (2)

If |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s, then g ∈ hAis , and we are done.
   Assume then that |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≥ s + 1. In this case, let λ′1 = λ1 − ǫcv and λ′2 = λ2 − cu, where ǫ equals 1 when λ1 ≥ 0, and
−1 when λ1 < 0. We can compute that

               λ′1 (1, u) + λ′2 (1, v) = (λ1 − ǫcv)(1, u) + (λ2 − cu)(1, v) = λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v) − (ǫcv + cu, (ǫ + 1)cuv),

and since ǫ + 1 equals 0 or 2, in G we have

                                             λ′1 (1, u) + λ′2 (1, v) = λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v) = g.

    Finally, we show that |λ′1 | + |λ′2 | ≤ s. Indeed, we have

                                   |λ′1 | + |λ′2 | = |λ1 − ǫcv| + |λ2 − cu| = cv − |λ1 | + cu − |λ2 |.

From (2) above we see that cv + cu ≤ 2s + 1, and by our current assumption we have |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≥ s + 1. Therefore,
|λ′1 | + |λ′2 | ≤ s, as claimed, and our proof is now complete.                                                   ✷

3     s-regular groups and upper bounds

Recall that we say that the group Zc × Zkc is s-regular for some positive integer s, if it possesses an s-spanning set of the
form {(1, u), (1, v)} for some u, v ∈ Zkc . In this section we provide some upper bounds for the largest possible order of an
s-regular group.

Theorem 3.1 Let G = Zc × Zck for some integers c ≥ 2 and k ≥ 1 that has an s-spanning set for some positive integer s.
If c is an odd prime, then G is s-regular.

    Proof. First, we prove that G has an s-spanning set of the form A = {(a, u), (b, v)} for some a, b ∈ Zc and u, v ∈ Zck with
u 6≡ v mod c. Note that if {(a, u), (b, v)} is an s-spanning set, then so is {(a, u), (−b, −v)}, so if our claim were false, then
we would have u ≡ v ≡ −v mod c for each s-spanning set A = {(a, u), (b, v)} of G. But since c is an odd prime, that would
imply that u ≡ v ≡ 0 mod c, and that is a contradiction with A being a spanning set.
   Suppose then that A = {(a, u), (b, v)} is an s-spanning in G with u 6≡ v mod c. We will prove that, in this case,
B = {(1, u), (1, v)} is also an s-spanning in G.
    Let
                                          I(s) = {(λ1 , λ2 ) | λ1 , λ2 ∈ Z, |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s},
and consider the function f : I(s) → G, given by

                                                   (λ1 , λ2 ) 7→ λ1 (a, u) + λ2 (b, v).

Since A is an s-spanning set, f is surjective; we can thus find a subset I0 of I(s) on which the restriction f0 : I0 → G of f is
bijective. We will prove that g0 : I0 → G, given by

                                                   (λ1 , λ2 ) 7→ λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v)

is also bijective. It suffices to show that g0 is injective. In order to do so, let us assume that the equation

                                              λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v) = µ1 (1, u) + µ2 (1, v)



                                                                    7
holds in G for some (λ1 , λ2 ) and (µ1 , µ2 ) of I0 . We then have

                                                      λ1 + λ2 ≡ µ1 + µ2 mod c,                                                  (3)

and

                                                  λ1 u + λ2 v ≡ µ1 u + µ2 v mod ck.                                             (4)

From (4) we find that
                                      (λ1 + λ2 )u + λ2 (v − u) ≡ (µ1 + µ2 )u + µ2 (v − u) mod ck;
considering (3), this gives us that
                                                      λ2 (v − u) ≡ µ2 (v − u) mod c.
Therefore, since c is prime and u 6≡ v mod c, we get that λ2 ≡ µ2 mod c. From (3) then we get λ1 ≡ µ1 mod c, and thus

                                                   λ1 a + λ2 b ≡ µ1 a + µ2 b mod c.                                             (5)

But f0 is an injection, so (5) and (4) can hold together only when (λ1 , λ2 ) = (µ1 , µ2 ). This completes our proof.             ✷

Theorem 3.2 Let G = Zc × Zck for some integers c ≥ 2 and k ≥ 1, and suppose that G is s-regular for some positive integer
s.

   1. If c is even, then |G| ≤ 2s2 , and equality may occur only when s ≡ 0 mod c.
   2. If c is odd and
         • s ≡ 0 mod c, then |G| ≤ 2s2 + s;
         • s ≡ (c − 1)/2 mod c, then |G| ≤ 2s2 + 2s − (c2 − 1)/2;
         • c = 3 and s ≡ 2 mod 3, then |G| ≤ 2s2 + 1.
      In all other cases, |G| < 2s2 .

    Proof. Let A = {(1, u), (1, v)} be an s-spanning set in G, in which case

                                      {(λ1 + λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v) | (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ Z2 , |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s} = G.

     Recall from Lemma 2.1 that, for a given integer h with |h| ≤ s, the number Λ(h) of ordered pairs (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ Z2 with
|λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s and λ1 + λ2 = h equals s + 1 when s and h have the same parity, and s otherwise. For each i = 0, 1, . . . , c − 1,
we let N (i) denote the sum of all Λ(h) with |h| ≤ s and h ≡ i mod c. Since A is an s-spanning set in G, we must have

                                  {(λ1 + λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v) | (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ Z2 , |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s} = Zc × Zck ,

and thus for each i = 0, 1, . . . , c − 1,

                      {(λ1 + λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v) | (λ1 , λ2 ) ∈ Z2 , |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s, λ1 + λ2 ≡ i mod c} = {i} × Zck .

In particular,

                                                              |G| ≤ cN (i)                                                      (6)

must hold for each i = 0, 1, . . . , c − 1. We now inspect (6) in several cases.



                                                                       8
   When c is even, we consider i = r + 1, where r is the nonnegative remainder of s mod c (with r + 1 understood to be 0
when r = c − 1). To compute N (r + 1), we need to identify the values of h for which |h| ≤ s and h ≡ r + 1 mod c, and these
values are h = s + 1 − jc for j = 1, 2, . . . , jmax , where
                                       jmax = ⌊(2s + 1)/c⌋ = (2s − 2r)/c + ⌊(2r + 1)/c⌋.
Note that ⌊(2r + 1)/c⌋ equals 0 when 0 ≤ r ≤ c/2 − 1, and 1 when c/2 ≤ r ≤ c − 1. Since s and h = s + 1 − jc have different
parity, and thus Λ(h) = s for each j, we have
                                            cN (r + 1) = (2s − 2r + c⌊(2r + 1)/c⌋) s,
and we see that this quantity is at most 2s2 , with equality when r = 0 or r = c/2. But |G| must be divisible by c2 , so
|G| = 2s2 may only occur when r = 0.
   When c is odd and r ≤ c − 2, we consider i = r + 2 (where r + 2 is understood to be 0 when r = c − 2). In this case the
values of h with |h| ≤ s and h ≡ r + 2 mod c are h = s + 2 − tc for t = 1, 2, . . . , tmax , where
                                       tmax = ⌊(2s + 2)/c⌋ = (2s − 2r)/c + ⌊(2r + 2)/c⌋.
Note that ⌊(2r + 2)/c⌋ equals 0 when 0 ≤ r ≤ (c − 3)/2, and 1 when (c − 1)/2 ≤ r ≤ c − 2. Since s and h = s + 2 − tc have
the same parity when t is even and different parity when t is odd, we have
                                     cN (r + 2) = (s − r + c⌊(2r + 2)/c⌋) s + (s − r)(s + 1).
We consider four subcases. For r = 0, this quantity equals 2s2 +s, and when 1 ≤ r ≤ (c−3)/2, it equals (s−r)s+(s−r)(s+1),
which is less than 2s2 . When (c + 1)/2 ≤ r ≤ c − 2, we have
               cN (r + 2) = (s − r + c) s + (s − r)(s + 1) = (s − r)(2s + 1) + cs ≤ (s − (c + 1)/2) (2s + 1) + cs,
which again is less than 2s2 . Our last subcase is when r = (c − 1)/2, when we have
                               cN (r + 2) = (s − (c − 1)/2) (2s + 1) + cs = 2s2 + 2s − (c − 1)/2.
Observe that when s ≡ (c − 1)/2 mod c, then 2s2 + 2s − (c2 − 1)/2 ≡ 0 mod c2 , and since |G| must be divisible by c2 , this is
the largest it can then be.
    Our final case is when c is odd and r = c − 1; we consider i = 0. In this case the values of h with |h| ≤ s and h ≡ 0 mod
c are h = s + 1 − zc for z = 1, 2, . . . , zmax , where
                                              zmax = ⌊(2s + 1)/c⌋ = (2s + 2 − c)/c.
Note that zmax is odd. Since s and h = s + 1 − zc have the same parity when z is odd and different parity when z is even,
we have
                                     N (0) = (zmax − 1)/2 · s + (zmax + 1)/2 · (s + 1),
and thus
                                  cN (0) = (s + 1 − c) s + (s + 1) (s + 1) = 2s2 − (c − 3)s + 1,
which is less than 2s2 , unless c = 3. This completes our proof.                                                            ✷

Theorem 3.3 For s ≥ 3, every s-regular abelian group of rank 2 has order at most 2s2 + 2s − 4, with equality if, and only
if, it is isomorphic to Z3 × Z(2s2 +2s−4)/3 for some s ≡ 1 mod 3.

   Proof. Suppose that G = Zc × Zck is an s-regular group of order at least 2s2 + 2s − 4 for some integers s ≥ 3, c ≥ 2, and
k ≥ 1. Then, by Theorem 3.2, c is odd; furthermore,

   • s ≡ 0 mod c, and 2s2 + 2s − 4 ≤ c2 k ≤ 2s2 + s, or
   • s ≡ (c − 1)/2 mod c, and 2s2 + 2s − 4 ≤ c2 k ≤ 2s2 + 2s − (c2 − 1)/2.

Since the first possibility leads to no solutions, our claim is established.                                                ✷

4     The groups Z2 × Z2k

In this section we provide some results for s-spanning sets in groups of the form Z2 × Z2k , including the exact value of the
largest possible order of such groups. Our general conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 4.1 Let k and s be positive integers. The group Z2 × Z2k has an s-spanning set of size 2 if, and only if, one of
the following holds:

    • s is odd and k ≤ (s2 − 1)/2;
    • s is even and k ≤ (s2 − s)/2;
    • s ≡ 0 mod 4, k is even, and (s2 − s)/2 < k ≤ s2 /2; or
    • s ≡ 2 mod 4, k ≡ 2 mod 4, and (s2 − s)/2 < k ≤ s2 /2.

Theorem 4.2 Let k and s be positive integers. If the group G = Z2 × Z2k has an s-spanning set of size 2, then it has order
at most 2s2 .

    Proof. If G is s-regular, that is, it has an s-spanning set of the form {(1, u), (1, v)} for some u, v ∈ Z2k , then our claim
follows from Theorem 3.2. Since no set of the form {(0, u), (0, v)} can be a spanning set, we can suppose that G has an s-
spanning set of the form A = {(0, u), (1, v)} for some u, v ∈ Z2k . Therefore, each element in hAis has the form (λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v)
for some integers λ1 and λ2 with |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s.
   Clearly, if u and v are both even, then A can only generate elements of G with an even second component, and thus it
cannot be a spanning set.
    Now if u and v are both odd, then λ1 u + λ2 v has the same parity as λ1 + λ2 does. According to Lemma 2.1, the parity
of λ1 + λ2 differs from the parity of s exactly s2 times (and agrees with it (s + 1)2 times) within |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s. This means
that A cannot generate more than s2 elements in Z2 × Z2k whose second coordinate has different parity than s does, so if it
is an s-spanning set, then we must have |G| ≤ 2s2 .
   Next, if u is even and v is odd, then for each element of G that A generates, its two components will have the same parity
(namely, the parity of λ2 ). Therefore, A cannot be a spanning set.
   Finally, we prove that if A = {(0, u), (1, v)} is an s-spanning set of G with u odd and v even, then A′ = {(1, u), (1, v)} is
an s-spanning set as well, meaning that G is s-regular and thus has order at most 2s2 by Theorem 3.2.
    Let a ∈ Z2 and b ∈ Z2k . Since hAis = G, there are integers λ1 and λ2 with |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s for which

                                          λ1 (0, u) + λ2 (1, v) = (λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v) = (a′ , b),

where a′ = a if b is even and a′ = 1 + a when b is odd. Observe that when b is even, then (since u is odd and v is even), λ1
must be even, and thus

                        λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v) = (λ1 + λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v) = (λ2 , λ1 u + λ2 v) = (a′ , b) = (a, b).

Similarly, when b is odd, λ1 must be odd, and

                                             λ1 (1, u) + λ2 (1, v) = (1 + a′ , b) = (a, b).

Therefore, hA′ is = G, as claimed, which completes our proof.                                                                    ✷

Theorem 4.3 Let k and s be positive integers with s odd. Then the group G = Z2 × Z2k has an s-spanning set of size 2 if,
and only if, k ≤ (s2 − 1)/2.

   Proof. The fact that k ≤ (s2 − 1)/2 is necessary follows from Theorem 4.2. We may also recall that Theorem 2.4, applied
with c = 2 and odd s, yields that with u = (s − 1)/2 and v = (s + 1)/2, the set {(1, u), (1, v)} is an s-spanning set in
Z2 × Zs2 −1 . This takes care of the case k = (s2 − 1)/2.
    Our goal below is to show that the same values of u = (s − 1)/2 and v = (s + 1)/2 as integers provide an s-span for the
set Z2 × {0, 1, 2, . . . , k} when k ≤ (s2 − 3)/2. (Here we consider the operation in the first component in Z2 and in the second
component in Z.) By symmetry, we can then conclude that Z2 × Z2k has an s-spanning set of size 2 for k ≤ (s2 − 3)/2 as
well.
    We thus need to establish that for every a ∈ Z2 and b ∈ {0, 1, 2, . . . , k}, there are integers µ1 and µ2 with |µ1 | + |µ2 | ≤ s
for which
                                           µ1 (1, (s − 1)/2) + µ2 (1, (s + 1)/2) = (a, b)                                          (7)
holds in Z2 × {0, 1, 2, . . . , k}.
   Let us recall from the proof of Theorem 2.4 (applied again for odd s and c = 2) that there are integers λ1 ∈ [−s, s + 1]
and λ2 ∈ [0, s − 2] for which one of the following two sets of conditions holds:

  (i) |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≤ s, and equation (7) is satisfied in Z2 × Zs2 −1 with (µ1 , µ2 ) = (λ1 , λ2 ); or
 (ii) |λ1 | + |λ2 | ≥ s + 1, and equation (7) is satisfied in Z2 × Zs2 −1 with (µ1 , µ2 ) = (λ1 − ǫ(s + 1), λ2 − s + 1), where ǫ equals
      1 when λ1 ≥ 0, and −1 when λ1 < 0.

    To complete our proof, we will show that, considering
                                                    B = µ1 (s − 1)/2 + µ2 (s + 1)/2
as an integer (rather than as an element of Zs2 −1 ), we have B = b. Since 0 ≤ b ≤ k < (s2 − 1)/2, it suffices to prove that
                                                    −(s2 − 1)/2 ≤ B < s2 − 1.                                                      (8)

   In case (i), we have µ1 = λ1 ∈ [−s, s + 1] and µ2 = λ2 ∈ [0, s − 2], from which (8) readily follows. For case (ii), we find
that
                             |µ1 | + |µ2 | = (s + 1 − |λ1 |) + (s − 1 − |λ2 |) ≤ 2s − (s + 1) = s − 1
and thus
             |B| = |µ1 (s − 1)/2 + µ2 (s + 1)/2| ≤ |µ1 |(s − 1)/2 + |µ2 |(s + 1)/2 ≤ (|µ1 | + |µ2 |)(s + 1)/2 ≤ (s2 − 1)/2,
so (8) holds again. This completes our proof.                                                                                        ✷

Theorem 4.4 Let k and s be positive integers with s even. Then the group G = Z2 × Z2k has an s-spanning set of size 2 if
k ≤ (s2 − s)/2.

   Proof. Like in the proof of Theorem 4.3, we will prove that there are integers u and v for which the s-span of A =
{(1, u), (1, v)} contains the set Z2 × {0, 1, 2, . . . , k}. (Here too we consider the operation in the first component in Z2 and in
the second component in Z.)
   Since s − 1 is odd, from the proof of Theorem 4.3 we see that with u = (s − 2)/2 and v = s/2, hAis−1 contains the set
Z2 × {0, 1, 2, . . . , k} when k ≤ ((s − 1)2 − 1)/2. Therefore, (1, v) + hAis−1 contains Z2 × {s/2, s/2 + 1, . . . , s/2 + k} for each
k ≤ (s2 − 2s)/2, and since (1, v) + hAis−1 ⊆ hAis , our claim follows for all k ≤ (s2 − s)/2.                                        ✷
