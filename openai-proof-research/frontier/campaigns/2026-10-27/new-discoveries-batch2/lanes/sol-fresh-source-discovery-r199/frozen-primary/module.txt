On module endomorphisms annihilated by residually square-free polynomials over Artinian local PIRs

 Abstract 
 We consider finitely generated free modules and their endomorphisms over an Artinian local PIR, also known as an SPIR.
We characterize submodules of such modules by constructing minimal generating sets from given bases. We prove that any endomorphism possessing a square-free annihilating polynomial modulo the maximal ideal admits a principal annihilating ideal, and establish that every free invariant submodule under such an endomorphism has an invariant complement. Moreover, for such endomorphisms, we detail the construction of invariant submodules and compute the number of free invariant submodules when the ring is finite. Furthermore, we introduce canonical matrices associated with these endomorphisms, yielding a complete characterization of matrix similarity via invariant polynomials in this context. Finally, we address the non-derogatory case, obtaining a sharper characterization of submodule invariance. 
 Keywords– Artinian local PIR, Special principal ideal ring (SPIR), Module endomorphisms, Square-free polynomials, Invariant submodules, Matrix similarity 
 Mathematics Subject Classification (2020) – 13E10, 13F10, 13C05, 15B33, 15A21 
 1 Introduction 
 A special principal ideal ring (SPIR) is a commutative principal ideal ring (PIR) having exactly one prime ideal. It is obviously local and Noetherian. Moreover, as it has Krull dimension 0, it is an Artinian local PIR. Conversely, every Artinian local PIR is an SPIR. Indeed, as such a ring has Krull dimension 0, each of its prime ideals is maximal, hence as it is local, it has only one prime ideal. SPIRs play a significant role in commutative algebra, mainly due to the Zariski–Samuel Structure Theorem for PIRs [ 25 , Volume 1, Chapter 4, Theorem 33] , which states that every PIR is isomorphic to a finite direct product of PIDs and SPIRs.
They arise naturally in several classical contexts. Prominent examples include finite chain rings —such as Galois rings \text{GR}(p^{n},r) (which reduce to \mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z} when r=1 ) and their Eisenstein extensions (see [ 13 , Theorem XVII.5 ] )—as well as quotients of the form k[x]/(f^{n}) , where k is a field and f\in k[x] is irreducible. 
 Study of matrices with coefficients in an SPIR has attracted considerable attention over several decades. Davis  [ 6 ] showed that over \mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z} , the similarity of two square matrices reduces to the similarity of their reductions modulo p, provided both matrices satisfy a common polynomial whose reduction modulo p is square-free. McDonald  [ 14 ] developed the general theory of similarity over SPIRs and demonstrated that a ”rational” canonical form is available for a suitable class of matrices, however, a counterexample was later given by Guralnick in [ 8 ] . Nechaev  [ 17 ] subsequently classified the similarity classes of square matrices of order 3 over \mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}. Kurakin  [ 11 ] established a structured approach to classify similarity over SPIRs by associating to each matrix a system of polynomial ideals over a discrete valuation ring that remains invariant under similarity transformations. Avni  [ 1 ] analyzed the similarity classes of 3\times 3 matrices over a local PIR, producing a classification of representatives and an explicit enumeration in the case where the residue field is finite. Prasad  [ 21 ] developed a theory of normal forms for similarity classes over an SPIR of length two, and described the similarity classes for square matrices with order at most 4. However,
despite this progress, the classification of similarity classes over SPIRs remains incomplete in general: existing results either restrict the length of the ring, the size of the matrices, or the class of endomorphisms considered. 
 On the other hand, the study of invariant submodules of an endomorphism is central to module theory and linear algebra, providing a structural framework to break down complex algebraic systems into simpler components.
Formally, given a module M and an endomorphism \varphi , a submodule N\leq M is \varphi -invariant if it satisfies \varphi(N)\subseteq N. When a module can be decomposed into a direct sum of invariant submodules, the endomorphism can be represented in a block diagonal matrix form, which is easier to work with. The study of invariant submodules has also proved to be useful in coding theory where, for example, polycyclic codes can be seen as invariant submodules of a certain endomorphism of R^{n} (see [ 22 , 23 , 2 , 20 ] ). 
 In this paper, we investigate a class of endomorphisms of finitely generated free modules over an SPIR, namely, those with annihilating polynomials that are square-free modulo the maximal ideal. 
 The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews basic definitions and properties of module endomorphisms over commutative rings, alongside foundational results on local and principal ideal rings used throughout. Section 3 establishes useful results concerning polynomials and finitely generated free modules over Artinian rings. In Section 4 , we describe properties of SPIRs and characterize submodules of finitely generated free modules by constructing minimal generating sets from a given basis. In Section 5 , we demonstrate that over an SPIR, an endomorphism of a finitely generated free module possesses a principal annihilating ideal, provided its annihilating polynomial is square-free modulo the maximal ideal. We also prove that every free invariant submodule under such an endomorphism admits an invariant complement. Continuing the study of such endomorphisms, Section 6 investigates their invariant submodules, detailing their construction from a given module basis and calculating the total number of free invariant submodules in the finite ring case. Additionally, we introduce associated canonical matrices for these endomorphisms, allowing us to characterize matrix similarity via polynomial invariants in this context. Finally, Section 7 addresses the non-derogatory case where a sharper characterization of invariance is given.

 3 Some useful results on Artinian local rings 
 Let R be an Artinian local ring with maximal ideal \mathfrak{m}. In this section, we present some results concerning polynomials over R as well as finitely generated R -modules that will be useful throughout the paper. 
 Let f and g be two polynomials in R[x]. f is said to be regular if it is not a zero divisor, which is the case if and only if it has a non-zero-divisor coefficient. It is said to be basic irreducible if \mu(f) is an irreducible polynomial in k[x]. f is said to be square-free if it is not divisible by the square of any non-constant polynomial in R[x], moreover, it is called residually square-free if \mu(f) is square-free in k[x]. Finally, f and g are said to be coprime if (f,g)=R[x]. 
As R is Artinian, by Nakayama’s Lemma \mathfrak{m} is nilpotent, and is in fact the nilradical of R. A ring whose nilradical is a maximal ideal is called a completely primary ring . Through a series of papers in the 50s, Snapper described the polynomial ideals over this type of rings. Namely the following result follows a characterization that appears on [ 24 , Section 12] . 
 Lemma 2 . 
 ( [ 24 , Section 12] ) 
 (a) 
 Let f be a regular polynomial in R[x]. Then there exists a monic polynomial g\in R[x] and a unit polynomial u\in R[x] such that f=ug.

 While no additional condition is required on a local ring for the generating property of a subset to be transferred via \mu and vice-versa as seen in Theorem 1 , the transfer of linear independence needs more. It turns out, as we will see in Proposition 5 , that for a local ring to have this linear independence transfer property, it needs to satisfy a sort of incomplete basis property. A ring S is said to be weakly semi-Steinitz , if every linearly independent finite subset of a finitely generated free S -module F can be extended to a basis of F. 
See [ 15 , 5 ] for more details about weakly semi-Steinitz and Steinitz rings in general. In particular, [ 15 , Corollary 2.5.] states that a commutative Noetherian ring is weakly semi-Steinitz if and only if every non-zero-divisor is a unit, hence it is clear that an Artinian local ring is weakly semi-Steinitz. As a consequence, we have the following straightforward properties. 
 Proposition 5 . 
 Let F be a finitely generated free R -module. We have the following: 
 (i) 
 If \{w_{1},\dots,w_{r}\} is a linearly independent subset of F, then \{w_{1}+\mathfrak{m}F,\dots,w_{r}+\mathfrak{m}F\} is linearly independent in F/\mathfrak{m}F. 
 (ii) 
 Each linearly independent subset of F with cardinality \operatorname{rank}_{R}(F) is a basis of F. 
 (iii) 
 For any free submodule V of F, V is a direct summand of F.

 4 Finitely generated free modules over an SPIR 
 In this section, we describe the structure of submodules of a finitely generated free module over an Artinian local principal ideal ring. We start with some useful results concerning the structure of these rings. For a detailed discussion about SPIRs see [ 25 , Volume 1, Chapter 4, Section 15] . Let R be an SPIR with maximal ideal \mathfrak{m}, and let \gamma\in R such that \mathfrak{m}=(\gamma). As R is Artinian, the ideal \mathfrak{m} is nilpotent. Let \nu\in\mathbb{N}^{*} denote the nilpotency index of \mathfrak{m}. The ideals of R form a strictly increasing chain 
 (0)=\mathfrak{m}^{\nu}\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}^{\nu-1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\mathfrak{m}\subsetneq R\,.

 Lemma 5 .

 Proof.

 A standard theorem in linear algebra states that the minimal polynomial of a block diagonal matrix is the least common multiple of the minimal polynomials of its diagonal blocks. We adapt this result to our context as follows. 
 Proposition 11 . 
 Suppose there exists a residually square-free polynomial f in N_{\varphi} . If F is a direct sum of \varphi -invariant submodules such that the restriction of \varphi to each summand is a regular endomorphism, then \varphi is regular. 
 Proof. 
 As \mu(f) is square-free, by Lemma 2 we can take f to be monic w.l.o.g . Let f=\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i} be the unique factorization of f into monic pairwise coprime basic irreducible polynomials. As R is local, each summand of the direct sum is free, hence there exists a basis \mathcal{B} of F relative to which the matrix of \varphi is of the form diag(M_{1},\dots,M_{q}) for some regular matrices M_{1},\dots,M_{q}. It is easy to see that \mu(\varphi) is represented by diag(\mu(M_{1}),\dots,\mu(M_{q})) in the ordered basis \mu(\mathcal{B}) of F/\mathfrak{m}F, hence the minimal polynomial of \mu(\varphi) is \text{l.c.m}(\lambda_{\mu(M_{1})},\dots,\lambda_{\mu(M_{q})}). For each i\in\{1,\dots,q\}, as M_{i} is regular and annihilated by f, we have \mu(\lambda_{M_{i}})=\lambda_{\mu(M_{i})} and \lambda_{M_{i}} divides f, so by the uniqueness of the irreducible decomposition of Proposition 4 , there exists a subset J_{i}\subset\{1,\dots,r\} such that \lambda_{M_{i}}=\prod_{j\in J_{i}}f_{j}. Let J=\cup_{1\leq i\leq q}J_{i} and denote g=\prod_{i\in J}f_{i}.  In [ 14 , Theorem III.2] , it was claimed that each regular endomorphism of F can be represented by a matrix that splits as a sum of companion matrices \text{diag}(C_{1},\dots,C_{r}) for some companion matrices C_{1},\dots,C_{r}.  Proposition 13 . 
 If \varphi has a residually square-free annihilating polynomial, then a basis of F may be chosen wherein \varphi is represented by a matrix that splits as a sum of companion matrices. 
 Proof. 
 From [ 17 , Theorem 1] , we have that \varphi may be represented by a matrix that splits as a sum of companion matrices if and only if the R[x] -module M(\varphi) decomposes into a direct sum of cyclic modules, hence all we need to prove is that M(\varphi) has such decomposition.
Let f\in N_{\varphi} be such that \mu(f) is square-free. As (f)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{R[x]}(M(\varphi)), M(\varphi) can be considered as a module over R[x]/(f) in a natural way, moreover, it decomposes as a direct sum of cyclic modules as an R[x] -module if and only if it does as an R[x]/(f) -module. But as \mu(f) is square-free, R[x]/(f) is a PIR by Corollary 2 . Hence by the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PIR [ 3 , Theorem 15.33] , as an R[x]/(f) -module, M(\varphi) is a direct sum of cyclic modules.

 Corollary 3 .

 Proof.

 Proposition 15 . 
 Suppose that \mu(\lambda_{\varphi}) is irreducible. Then M(\varphi), the module associated with \varphi, is free as an R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi}) -module. 
 Proof. 
 As \mu(\lambda_{\varphi}) is irreducible, by Theorem 3 , \varphi is regular and N_{\varphi}=(\lambda_{\varphi}). It is clear that (\lambda_{\varphi}) is the annihilator of M(\varphi), and thus M(\varphi) can naturally be viewed as an R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi}) module. Denote S the ring R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi}). By Proposition 7 , S is an SPIR with ideals \gamma^{k}S=(\gamma^{k}+(\lambda_{\varphi})) for 0\leq k\leq\nu. We have then by the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PIR [ 3 , Theorem 15.33] , there exist an integer q and k_{1}\leq\dots\leq k_{q} in \{1,\dots,\nu\} such that M(\varphi)\simeq\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq q}S/\gamma^{k_{i}}S. Hence, as R -modules, F\simeq\bigoplus_{1\leq i\leq q}S/\gamma^{k_{i}}S, and thus, by Proposition 1 , for all 1\leq i\leq q, S/\gamma^{k_{i}}S is a free R -module, which implies that there exists an element w_{i}\in S/\gamma^{k_{i}}S such that \gamma^{\nu-1}w_{i}\neq 0, but as \text{Ann}_{S}(S/\gamma^{k_{i}}S)=\gamma^{k_{i}}S, this means that for all i, k_{i}=\nu, and thus M(\varphi)\simeq S^{q} is a free S -module.
∎ 
 Theorem 4 . 
 The endomorphism \varphi is semisimple. 
 Proof. 
 Let W be a free \varphi -invariant submodule of F. From Proposition 14 we have that W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}W\cap V_{i} with each W\cap V_{i} free and \varphi -invariant. Hence to prove that W have a \varphi -invariant complement, it suffices to prove that for each i, W\cap V_{i} has a \varphi -invariant complement in V_{i}. We can then w.l.o.g suppose that \lambda_{\varphi} is basic irreducible. Denote S the ring R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi}). Since W is free and \varphi_{\restriction W} is regular of minimal polynomial \lambda_{\varphi}, by Proposition 15 , W can be seen as a free S -submodule of M(\varphi). Moreover, from Proposition 7 , S is an SPIR, hence it is weakly semi-Steinitz and by Proposition 5 (iii), there exists an S -submodule W^{\prime} of M(\varphi) such that M(\varphi)=W\oplus W^{\prime}. But since W^{\prime} is an R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi}) -submodule of M(\varphi), it can be seen as a \varphi -invariant R -module, hence as R -modules, F=W\oplus W^{\prime} with W^{\prime} \varphi -invariant.
∎ 
 Remark 5 . 
 While an endomorphism of a finite dimensional vector space is semisimple if and only if its minimal polynomial is square-free (see [ 9 , Theorem 11, Chapter 7] ), the converse of Theorem 4 does not hold, as shown by the following counterexample. Let R=\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} and consider the matrix A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
2&0\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(R). It is easy to see that \lambda_{A}=x^{2}-2, and \mu(\lambda_{A})=x^{2} is not square-free. We claim that R^{2} has no nonzero proper free \varphi_{A} -invariant submodules. Indeed otherwise, let M be such a submodule. As the rank of M must be 1, let w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in R^{2} such that M=Rw and \mu(w)\neq 0. We have then \varphi_{A}(w)=(w_{2},2w_{1})\in M which means that there exists \alpha\in R such that w_{2}=\alpha w_{1} and 2w_{1}=\alpha w_{2} and this implies that w_{1},w_{2}\in 2R which contradicts the assumption that \mu(w)\neq 0. We have then, \varphi_{A} is trivially semisimple despite the fact that \mu(\lambda_{A}) is not square-free. 
 6 Invariant submodules 
 We begin our investigation of the \varphi -invariant submodules of F by considering the simplest type, namely, the smallest \varphi -invariant submodule containing a given element of F. 
 Definition 2 . 
 For each w\in F, the \varphi -cyclic submodule of F generated by w is the submodule of all elements of the form g(\varphi)(w) with g\in R[x]. We denote it Z(w,\varphi). 
 Remark 6 . 
 For an element w\in F, Z(w,\varphi) can be viewed as an R[x] -submodule of M(\varphi), the R[x] -module associated with \varphi. In this setting it is easy to see that Z(w,\varphi) corresponds to the cyclic module generated by w in M(\varphi), and conversely, for each w\in M(\varphi), R[x]w corresponds to Z(w,\varphi). 
 For a k -vector space V and \psi\in\text{End}_{k}(V), for each vector w\in V, we denote N_{\psi}(w) the ideal of k[x] such that p\in N_{\psi}(w) if and only p(\psi)(w)=0. The next lemma uses the fact that for each w\in F, N_{\mu(\varphi)}(w+\mathfrak{m}F) is a principal ideal that contains 1 if and only if w\in\mathfrak{m}F. 
 Lemma 8 . 
 Suppose that \lambda_{\varphi} is basic irreducible. Then for each nonzero element w\in F, the set \mathcal{B}=\{\varphi^{i}(w)~|~0\leq i\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1\} 
is a minimal generating set of Z(w,\varphi). Furthermore, Z(w,\varphi) is free if and only if w\notin\mathfrak{m}F, and in this case, the restriction of \varphi to Z(w,\varphi) is a regular endomorphism with both minimal and characteristic polynomials equal to \lambda_{\varphi}. 
 Proof. 
 Suppose at first that w\notin\mathfrak{m}F. As \lambda_{\varphi}(\varphi)(w)=0 and \lambda_{\varphi} is monic, it is clear that \mathcal{B} generates Z(w,\varphi). We claim that \mathcal{B} is linearly independent. Otherwise, by Corollary 1 , \mu(\mathcal{B})=\{\varphi^{i}(w)+\mathfrak{m}F~|~0\leq i\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1\} is linearly dependent in F/\mathfrak{m}F. But from Diagram 1 , we see that \mu(\mathcal{B})=\{\mu(\varphi^{i})(w+\mathfrak{m}F)~|~0\leq i\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1\}=\{\mu(\varphi)^{i}(w+\mathfrak{m}F)~|~0\leq i\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1\}. Hence, \mu(\mathcal{B}) being dependent means that there exists a monic polynomial f\in k[x] such that \deg(f)\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1 and f(\mu(\varphi))(w+\mathfrak{m}F)=0, i.e. f\in N_{\mu(\varphi)}(w+\mathfrak{m}F). As w\notin\mathfrak{m}F, N_{\mu(\varphi)}(w+\mathfrak{m}F)\neq k[x], hence as the irreducible polynomial \mu(\lambda_{\varphi}) is in N_{\mu(\varphi)}(w+\mathfrak{m}F) we have N_{\mu(\varphi)}(w+\mathfrak{m}F)=(\mu(\lambda_{\varphi})), and that implies that f\in(\mu(\lambda_{\varphi})) which contradicts the fact that \deg(f)\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1. We have then Z(w,\varphi) is a free submodule of F with basis \mathcal{B}. Moreover, \varphi_{\restriction Z(w,\varphi)} is annihilated by \lambda_{\varphi}, hence by Theorem 3 , it is regular, and as \operatorname{rank}_{R}(Z(w,\varphi))=\deg(\lambda_{\varphi}) and \lambda_{\varphi} is irreducible, \lambda_{\varphi_{\restriction Z(w,\varphi)}}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\varphi_{\restriction Z(w,\varphi)}}=\lambda_{\varphi}. 
 Now suppose that w\in\mathfrak{m}F. By Lemma 5 , there exist 0\leq s\leq\nu-1 and w^{\prime}\in F\setminus\mathfrak{m}F such that w=\gamma^{s}w^{\prime}. It is straightforward to see that Z(w,\varphi)=Z(\gamma^{s}w^{\prime},\varphi)=\mathfrak{m}^{s}Z(w^{\prime},\varphi). So as Z(w^{\prime},\varphi) is free with basis \{\varphi^{i}(w^{\prime})~|~0\leq i\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1\}, multiplying by \gamma^{s} yields by Proposition 8 that \{\varphi^{i}(w)~|~0\leq i\leq\deg(\lambda_{\varphi})-1\} is a minimal generating set of Z(w,\varphi). Moreover, as this minimal generating set is clearly linearly dependent, by Lemma 1 , Z(w,\varphi) is not free.
∎ 
 Lemma 9 . 
 For all 1\leq i\leq r, let w_{i}\in V_{i}, and denote w=w_{1}+\dots+w_{r}. We have Z(w,\varphi)=Z(w_{1},\varphi)\oplus\dots\oplus Z(w_{r},\varphi). Moreover, Z(w,\varphi) is free if and only if, for every i, either w_{i}=0 or w_{i}\notin\mathfrak{m}V_{i}. 
 Proof. 
 It is clear that Z(w,\varphi)\subseteq Z(w_{1},\varphi)+\dots+Z(w_{r},\varphi). For i\in\{1,\dots,r\}, as f_{1},\dots,f_{r} are pairwise coprime, there exists u,v\in R[x] such that uf_{i}+v\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}f_{j}=1. As Z(w,\varphi) is \varphi -invariant, (v\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}f_{j})(\varphi)(w)=(v\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}f_{j})(\varphi)(w_{i})=w_{i}-(uf_{i})(\varphi)(w_{i})=w_{i}\in Z(w,\varphi). Hence, Z(w,\varphi)=Z(w_{1},\varphi)+\dots+Z(w_{r},\varphi), and as Z(w_{i},\varphi)\subseteq V_{i} for all i, by Proposition 10 , the sum is direct. Moreover, as V_{i} is free, using Lemma 8 , if w_{i}\notin\mathfrak{m}V_{i} for all i, then Z(w,\varphi) is free. Conversely, by Proposition 1 , if Z(w,\varphi) is free, then Z(w_{i},\varphi) is also free for all i, hence once again by Lemma 8 , w_{i}\notin\mathfrak{m}V_{i} for all i. 
∎

 \left\{\nu_{R}(Z(w,\varphi))~|~w\in F\right\}=\left\{\sum_{j\in J}\deg f_{j}~|~J\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}\right\}. 
 Having examined the \varphi -cyclic submodules of F, our next goal is to leverage them to characterize general \varphi -invariant submodules. A key step toward this is provided by the following proposition. 
 Proposition 17 . 
 Suppose that \lambda_{\varphi} is basic irreducible, and let m\in\mathbb{N} such that {\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{\mu(\varphi)}=\mu(\lambda_{\varphi})^{m}.  Let w_{1}\in F\setminus\mathfrak{m}F. By Lemma 8 , Z(w_{1},\varphi) is free and of rank \deg(\lambda_{\varphi}). Hence by Theorem 4 , there exists a free \varphi -invariant submodule F_{1}\leq F of rank (m-1)\deg(\lambda_{\varphi}) such that F=Z(w_{1},\varphi)\oplus F_{1}. Furthermore, \varphi_{\restriction F_{1}} is a regular endomorphism of F_{1} with minimal polynomial \lambda_{\varphi}. Hence repeating the same argument yields that there exist w_{2},\dots,w_{m}\in F\setminus\mathfrak{m}F such that F=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}Z(w_{i},\varphi). Moreover, from Proposition 15 , M(\varphi) is a free (R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi})) -module, and it is easy to see that M(\varphi)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}(R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi}))w_{i}. Hence \{w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\} is a basis of M(\varphi) and \operatorname{rank}_{R[x]/(\lambda_{\varphi})}(M(\varphi))=m.

 Remark 7 . 
 An endomorphism with no residually square-free annihilating polynomial may have a characteristic polynomial that cannot be expressed as a product of basic irreducible polynomials.  Remark 8 . 
 While over a field, two linear operators with square-free minimal polynomials are similar if and only if their characteristic polynomials are equal, this does not hold for R in general. This stems from the fact that R[x] is not always a UFD and the characteristic polynomial can have multiple factorizations into basic irreducible polynomials. Consider for example R=\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}, and let A=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\
0&3\end{pmatrix}. We have that both A and I_{2} have residually square-free annihilating polynomials and {\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{A}=(x-3)^{2}=(x-1)^{2}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}_{I_{2}}, yet \lambda_{A}=x-3\neq\lambda_{I_{2}}=x-1, which means that A and I_{2} cannot be similar. 
 7 Invariance in the non-derogatory case 
 A regular endomorphism such that the minimal and characteristic polynomials coincide is said to be non-derogatory . We devote this section to investigating this special case. 
 Lemma 10 .

 (10) 
 Proof. 
 Obviously, each \varphi -cyclic submodule of F is \varphi -invariant. Conversely, let W\leq F be a \varphi -invariant submodule. As \varphi is non-derogatory, from Theorem 5 , for all 1\leq i\leq r, there exists an element w_{i}\in V_{i} such that W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}Z(w_{i},\varphi), which implies by Lemma 9 that W=Z(w_{1}+\dots+w_{r},\varphi). 
 Equation ( 10 ) follows then immediately from lemmas 9 and 10 .
∎ 
 Remark 9 . 
 A polycyclic code of length n associated to a polynomial f over a finite chain ring R can be seen as a submodule of R^{n} that is invariant under the action of the matrix \operatorname{com}(f). When f is residually square-free, the previous theorem corrects a result given in [ 2 , Theorem 4.2] concerning the structure of the polycyclic codes associated to f, where each k_{i} in Equation ( 10 ) was taken to be either \nu or 0. 
 The next corollary follows immediately. 
 Corollary 8 . 
 Suppose that \varphi is non-derogatory. The following hold. 
 (a) 
 \{V_{i}~|~1\leq i\leq r\} is the set of all minimal nonzero free \varphi -invariant submodule of F. 
 (b) 
 Every free \varphi -invariant submodule of F is of the form \bigoplus_{j\in J}V_{j} with J\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}. 
 (c) 
 There are (\nu+1)^{r} \varphi -invariant submodules of F, among which 2^{r} are free. 
 Recall that a submodule W of F is called \varphi -hyperinvariant if it is invariant under every R -module endomorphism \psi\in\text{End}_{R}(F) that commutes with \varphi. 
 Proposition 19 . 
 Suppose that \varphi is non-derogatory. Then every \varphi -invariant submodule W of F is \varphi -hyperinvariant. 
 Proof. 
 Let W be a \varphi -invariant submodule of F and let \psi be an endomorphism of F that commutes with \varphi. Following Theorem 8 , let k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{N} such that W=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{m}^{k_{i}}V_{i}. We have then that for every element w\in W, for each 1\leq i\leq r, there exists w_{i}\in V_{i} such that w=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\gamma^{k_{i}}w_{i}. On the other hand, \psi commutes with every polynomial in \varphi, hence we have for all i, f_{i}(\varphi)(\psi(w_{i}))=\psi(f_{i}(\varphi)(w_{i}))=0, which implies that \psi(w_{i})\in V_{i}. We have then, \psi(w)=\psi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\gamma^{k_{i}}w_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\gamma^{k_{i}}\psi(w_{i})\in\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{m}^{k_{i}}V_{i}=W, and thus W is \psi -invariant.
∎
