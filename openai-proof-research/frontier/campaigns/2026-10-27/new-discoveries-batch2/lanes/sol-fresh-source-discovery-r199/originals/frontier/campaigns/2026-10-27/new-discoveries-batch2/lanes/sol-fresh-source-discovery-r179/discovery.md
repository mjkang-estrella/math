## 1. Yamamura: no authorized fresh reopening

10.5281/zenodo.15283716, arXiv:2401.14138. Actual published PDF pp1–9 was read,

Journal dates: received 2024-12-30, revised 2025-03-02, accepted 2025-04-06,
published 2025-04-25. Current arXiv metadata and journal index are retained.

Conjecture1: for every integer n>=1, the Galois group over Q of
F_n(x)=1+sum_{k=1}^n x^k/k is S_n. Attribution to Yamamura is through the
paper's citation to his review, not a separately authenticated original review.
The actual source reduces n>1 to nonsquareness of the discriminant, using
previous irreducibility/alternating-group containment. With L_n=lcm(1,...,n)
and source P_n, disc(F_n)=(-1)^(n(n-1)/2)nP_n/L_n^(n-1).
Theorem5 requires n=mq, q>m and the exceptional harmonic/product constraints;
it is not an all-n theorem. Prime-square cases retain the same essential
nonsquare-P_n obstruction identified in r130. No decisive new universal
arithmetic route was found here; therefore the old question was retired before
experiments. More finite discriminants or more m would not authorize reopening.
The 2019 underlying proof and Yamamura review were not fetched in this lane;

## 2. Catalan irreducibility: exact public theorem

Zhi-Wei Sun's 2013 OEIS conjecture, cited explicitly by Shin-ichiro Seki,
*Irreducibility of truncations of the Catalan generating function*,
arXiv:2609.10526v1 (2026-09-09), Theorem1.1, is that for every integer n>=1,
P_n(x)=sum_{k=0}^n C_k x^k, C_k=binom(2k,k)/(k+1), is irreducible over Q.
Original OEIS A000108 bytes, current arXiv metadata and actual full HTML are
retained. Latest observed arXiv version is v1. No journal version was
authenticated; no DOI beyond 10.48550/arXiv.2609.10526 is asserted.

The subsumption bridge is identity: Seki defines precisely this polynomial and
asserts the same all-positive-n domain. Replacing it by Q_n=x^n P_n(1/x)
preserves rational reducibility since the nonzero constant term prevents
loss of degree; taking reciprocals sends a nonconstant factorization in either
direction to one in the other direction. This does not claim irreducibility
modulo any bounded prime or a Galois conclusion.

Read the actual introduction, root bounds, main contradiction, local equations
(4.1)–(4.3), forced symmetry and final prime-product coefficient bound. For
n>=320 a hypothetical monic integral factor is forced to satisfy
F(4-x)=(-1)^deg(F)F(x). With W=x²-4x+16 and paired roots alpha,4-alpha,
a=16-|alpha|² and b=16-|4-alpha|² lie in (0,1) and
|W(alpha)|²=a²-ab+b²<1. The resultant is a nonzero integer with absolute value
less than one. The paper supplies the forced-symmetry argument and separately
reports the finite n<320 check. Its supporting classical prime bounds and
finite verifier are not independently replayed here: this is recognition of
an actual earlier full-proof body, not an independent global certification.
r153 already read this source. No new proof or rational-to-bounded-prime
upgrade is claimed, and no such old quantitative question was reopened.

## 3. Fayers divisibility: complete public subsumption bridge

William J. Keith, Rishi Nath and James A. Sellers, *On simultaneous
(s,s+t,s+2t,...)-core partitions*, arXiv:2508.00074v2, updated 2025-12-17;
Discrete Mathematics 349(5) (2026), 114958,
DOI10.1016/j.disc.2025.114958. Actual v1 and latest v2 HTML, arXiv history,
and institutional publication record are retained. Actual v2 Theorem1.8,
Conjecture1.14 and Section4 recurrence were compared. Published journal bytes
were not obtained: ScienceDirect was not retried, and the institutional page
is metadata only. Hence no claim of complete version-of-record equivalence.

Literal target: for every integer t>=2, the integer polynomial f_t(s) is
divisible in Z[s] by s+t+(-1)^t. Its source formula is
f_t(s)=sum_{m=1}^t (t!/m!)binom(t-1,m-1)(s+1)^(overline{m-1}).
This is polynomial divisibility, not only values at positive coprime pairs.

The live MathDB369588 full posted solution was read from its beginning through
the monic-division endpoint, independently of the residual open-status badge.
It explicitly cites this formula and credits the earlier generating function
to Jovovic/OEIS A079638 (2003). The exact all-t bridge is as follows; it is
attributed public mathematics, not a fresh candidate:

Reordering formally finite coefficient sums gives
G(s,z)=sum_{t>=1}f_t(s)z^t/t!
=(((1-z)/(1-2z))^s-1)/s.
The apparent singularity at s=0 is removable coefficientwise. For a fixed
t>=2 set s0=-t-(-1)^t and M=-s0=t+(-1)^t>0. Since s0!=0,
f_t(s0)=0 is equivalent to
[z^t]((1-2z)/(1-z))^M=0.
The invertible formal substitution w=z/(1-z), z=w/(1+w), applied to the
coefficient residue (including dz=dw/(1+w)^2), changes this to
[w^t](1+w)^(t-1)(1-w)^M.
For odd t, M=t-1 and the polynomial is (1-w²)^(t-1), with zero odd coefficient.
For even t=2h, it is (1-w²)^(2h-1)(1-w)^2 and its coefficient is
(-1)^h binom(2h-1,h)+(-1)^(h-1)binom(2h-1,h-1)=0.
Thus f_t(s0)=0 for EVERY literal t>=2. Its coefficients are integral by the
displayed formula, so division by the monic s-s0 has integral quotient and
constant remainder f_t(s0)=0. This closes every quantifier of the selected
preprint statement; no positivity, coprimality or endpoint proviso remains.

The posted solution already existed by r82's recorded October29 investigation,
before this lane's October30 UTC retrieval. Exact upload date/author identity
is not authenticated from that page, and no date is invented. This is enough
for prior-public-proof exclusion. The added Jacobi-matrix claims were not
needed or independently audited. The earlier Jovovic body was not separately
retrieved because the full posted bridge already decides exclusion. Repeating
this bridge would be rediscovery, regardless of its length.
