## Strongest full classical-adaptation objection

There is a complete classical bridge for complete discrete valuation fields, not merely a suggestive analogy. Write q=q0 ⊥ πq1 with unit diagonal entries, residue characteristic different from two. Springer's theorem gives anisotropy iff both residue forms are anisotropic. If q is also universal, it represents every unit u and every πu. Applying the representation criterion to q⊥⟨−u⟩ and q⊥⟨−πu⟩ shows each residue block represents every nonzero residue. Both blocks must be nonempty: otherwise one valuation parity cannot be represented. Conversely anisotropic universal nonempty residue blocks give anisotropy by Springer and represent every square class of the complete field, hence universality. Therefore AU(K)={a+b:a,b∈AU(k)}, m(K)=2m(k), u(K)=2u(k), and UUD is equivalent for K and residue k. This is exactly Cassady Lemma2.6/Corollaries2.7–2.8 and Hu–Liu Proposition3.2. Iteration resolves the analogous iterated Laurent fields completely, with no new credit.

Cassady's strongest additional bridge starts with m_s(k)=u_s(k), proves m_s(K)=2m_s(k) for a COMPLETE discretely valued K, then obtains m(K(t))=u(K(t)). Proposition4.11 uses a regular model over the complete valuation ring and the HHK isotropy local-global principle to find a point where q stays anisotropic. At a closed point its local bound is 4m(residue); at a generic component the residue is a rational one-variable field over a finite residue extension, giving the same bound. Every step uses the model/patching hypotheses. This covers, for example, C(x)((y))(z), not C(x,y,z). Rational extension does not satisfy the completeness premise. Assuming m_s(C(x,y))=u_s(C(x,y)) to apply the one-variable conclusion already includes the missing lower bound for m(C(x,y,z)). This would be circular. Neither C_n upper bounds nor a power-of-two u-invariant supplies a LOWER bound on m. Cassady explicitly discusses fields with m<u, so finite u alone cannot replace the missing hypothesis.

These full applicable adaptations do not settle the source endpoint. We do not exclude it by shortness or classify it as routine; we also do not interpret absence of a matching proof in a bounded search as evidence of novelty.

## Precise plausible uniform route and missing quantifier

A sufficient detection lemma would be: for every anisotropic universal q over either target field of index n≥3, there exists a nontrivial discrete valuation v whose residue field is of the same rational-function family of index n−1 (allowing a finite extension of the finite constant field), such that q remains anisotropic over K_v.

Here is the COMPLETE conditional implication. Universality ascends from K to K_v: for a∈K_v*, density chooses b∈K* in its square class; q represents b, so scaling the representing vector by sqrt(a/b) represents a. Square classes are open by Hensel in residue characteristic≠2. This is also Cassady Lemma2.9. Inductively the residue has m=u=2^(n−1); base index2 has u=4 and UUD by Hu–Liu Example3.1. The completion has m=u=2^n by the preceding residue-block bridge. If q remains anisotropic there, dim(q)≥m(K_v)=2^n; the global u bound gives the reverse inequality. This proves both families and ALL dimensions by induction IF the detection lemma holds. No assumption about a distinguished coordinate valuation can replace its existential v. No proof of this lemma, nor counterexample to it, is supplied. Rational-residue detection is a sufficient research route, not asserted equivalent to UUD or available from HHK patching over these fields. More general residue fields would require additional UUD theorems as well.

## Uniform method countermodel: completion can destroy anisotropy

For C(x1,...,xn), put t1=1+x1 and ti=xi for i≥2, and take q_n=⊗_(i=1)^n⟨1,−ti⟩. In the iterated Laurent field C((t1))...((tn)), repeated Springer starting from ⟨1⟩ proves this form anisotropic, so it is anisotropic over the rational subfield. Its dimension is2^n=u(K); hence it is universal: for any a≠0 the (2^n+1)-dimensional q_n⊥⟨−a⟩ is isotropic, and anisotropy of q_n forces the last coordinate nonzero, yielding a representation of a.

Nevertheless in the coordinate completion C(x2,...,xn)((x1)), 1+x1 is a square, so the first binary factor is hyperbolic and the entire tensor form is hyperbolic. This is not a numerical observation: solve s(x)^2=1+x recursively with s0=1, s1=1/2 and sm=−(1/2)Σ_(i=1)^(m−1)si s_(m−i) for m≥2. This constructs s in EVERY coefficient field of characteristic≠2, without division by factorials.

The finite-field family has the same witness. Choose a nonsquare δ∈F*, start with the anisotropic norm form ⟨1,−δ⟩ and tensor ⟨1,−(1+x1)⟩ and ⟨1,−xi⟩ for i=2,...,n−1. Repeated Springer gives anisotropy at the translated iterated Laurent field. Dimension2^n and u(K)=2^n again give universality. At x1=0 the tensor is hyperbolic. These examples meet the source quantifiers for every n≥3 and every odd finite F. They defeat the fixed-completion shortcut, NOT the existential detection lemma, NOT Question4.4. A different translated valuation does detect these particular examples. The square-series and finite norm checks are diagnostics only; the recurrence and valuation arguments provide the all-order method example, never a full source proof.
