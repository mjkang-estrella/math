## Classical adaptation objection: what is and is not supplied

The source already supplies conditional independence, exponentially abundant diamond decisions, repeated parameter values, binomial maximum-atom decay via Stirling (v1 Lemma4.7, p.20), and averaging over shifted cells. Concentration from those ingredients is a standard adaptation. The simplified single-type partition, Fourier estimate or elementary replacement is not independently credited. The published Lemma8 also retains the raw-block independence wording; the candidate's adaptive domination is a repair for its own argument, not a second discovery.

I inspected Bradley, *Basic Properties of Strong Mixing Conditions. A Survey and Some Open Questions*, Probability Surveys2(2005),107–144, actual arXiv:math/0511078v1 body: Section1.1 pp.108–109, Section2.1–2.2 pp.110–112, Section3 pp.118–119, Section5 Theorem5.1 pp.126–127. It gives exactly the full-sigma-field alpha and upper bound1/4. Its stationary finite-state Markov-chain Theorem3.1 does not apply directly: these are growing finite rows, started from N ones, with absorption, and the split observation is not established Markov. The nonstationary-chain Theorem3.3 assumes a contraction condition, rather than proving it from absorption; independent-pairs Theorem5.1 assumes independent sigma-field pairs, not merely conditional independence inside a random partition. No inspected theorem supplies the following entire bridge.

Here is the precise generic reduction, to make the subsumption question testable. For a padded binary row let T be termination and L its last one. Suppose (i) {T<m} is prefix-measurable for every m, (ii) sup_x P(T in [x,x+3k_N]) ->0, and (iii) P(T-L>k_N)->0. Choose the least m with P(T<m)>=1/2. Condition(ii) makes this probability 1/2+o(1), and P(T>=m+2k_N)>=1/2-o(1). Condition(iii) then gives P(L>=m+k_N)>=1/2-o(1). That suffix event is disjoint from {T<m}; complementing the prefix event gives covariance tending to1/4. Bradley's universal upper bound yields alpha_N(k_N)->1/4. This elementary reduction is complete **conditional on (i)–(iii)**; it is not a literature theorem establishing those hypotheses for this game.

For the candidate, (i) needs the exact deterministic combine count and identity {T<m}={m-sum_(i<=m)X_i>N-Z(N)}; padding alone does not imply observability. The source diamonds plus a standard concentration estimate address(ii). The unresolved adaptation objection therefore concentrates on(iii): the high-token split hazard must be iterated at deterministic times and unioned over possible last-split times; L is not a stopping time. The r284 proof explicitly supplies this for k_N=n^6. Neither the inspected published source nor the inspected successors nor Bradley supplies that application. Generic absorption, Bernoulli variance growth, component Gaussianity, or elementary shortness alone does not close it. Conversely, this analysis does not establish technical depth or worldwide novelty.

**Recommendation:** retain the complete endpoint provisionally; do not exclude merely because its individual tools are elementary.
