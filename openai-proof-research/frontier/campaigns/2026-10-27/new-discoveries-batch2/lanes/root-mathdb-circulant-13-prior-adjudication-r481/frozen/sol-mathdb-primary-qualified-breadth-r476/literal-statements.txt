
ID 336019 The minimal red progression length in Euclidean Ramsey theory
For integers $n,k\geq 1$, let $\mathbb{E}^n$ denote $n$-dimensional Euclidean space, and let $\ell_s$ denote a set of $s$ equally spaced collinear points. Write $\mathbb{E}^n\rightarrow(\ell_s,\ell_k)$ if every red-blue coloring of $\mathbb{E}^n$ contains either a red copy of $\ell_s$ or a blue copy of $\ell_k$. **The minimal-length conjecture.** There is an integer $k$ such that for every integer $n$, 
$$
\mathbb{E}^n\not\rightarrow(\ell_3,\ell_k).
$$
 This conjecture asks whether three is the smallest first progression length for which some fixed blue progression length avoids the corresponding Euclidean Ramsey property in every dimension. The preceding results establish related positive and negative statements for other progression lengths, but the conjectured case $s=3$ remains open.

ID 336274 Trace extension conjecture for forbidden posets
Let $P$ be a poset, let $e(P)$ be the largest integer $k$ such that, for every $j$ and $n$, the family

$$
\bigcup_{i=1}^k\binom{[n]}{j+i}
$$

is $P$-free, and let $Tr_{n-k}(n,P)$ denote the maximum size of a family whose traces on every $(n-k)$-subset are $P$-free. **Trace extension conjecture.** For every integer $k<e(P)$,

$$
Tr_{n-k}(n,P)=(e(P)-k+o(1))\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
$$

Moreover, if $k\ge e(P)$, then

$$
Tr_{n-k}(n,P)=o\left(\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\right).
$$

This generalizes the preceding conjecture from ordinary forbidden subposet problems to traces. The displayed lower bound comes from consecutive levels, while the matching asymptotics and the vanishing assertion remain conjectural.
