   In this paper, we are interested in a generalization of this problem. Let R be a finite group, and
let T ≤ R be a subgroup. Define
                                   Sub(R, T ) = {H ≤ R : T ≤ H}.
Our goal is to establish an upper bound for | Sub(R, T )|. Using the same argument as above, we can
obtain a preliminary bound: if |R| = r and |T | = t, then any chain of subgroups of R containing T
has length at most log2 (r/t). Therefore, each such subgroup can be generated by a set of cardinality
at most ⌊log2 (r/t)⌋, together with T . Thus, there are at most
                                                 r log2 ( rt )
                                              t
subgroups of R containing T . As noted earlier, this bound is not optimal—this is already evident
when T is the trivial subgroup.
  We present here a refined version of this inequality, generalizing the main result of [8].
Theorem 1.1. Let R be a finite group, and let T ≤ R. Then
                                                                   log2 [R:T ]
                                                                               +1.8919
                             | Sub(R, T )| ≤ 7.3722 · [R : T ]          4                .
   We believe that the constant 1.8919 in the exponent can be removed, as in the main result of [3].
   This theorem also admits a natural interpretation in the context of permutation groups. Suppose
that R is a finite transitive group acting on a set Ω, with point stabilizer T and degree n = [R : T ].
It is well known that the systems of imprimitivity of R correspond bijectively to the subgroups of
R containing the stabilizer. Thus, Theorem 1.1 yields the following corollary.
Corollary 1.2. Let R be a transitive group of degree n. Then R has at most
                                                       log2 n
                                                              +1.8919
                                          7.3722 · n      4



systems of imprimitivity.
   The proof of Theorem 1.1 closely follows the argument in [8], which gives an elementary proof
of [2, Corollary 1.6]. A central component of the argument in [2, Corollary 1.6] relies on estimating
the number of maximal subgroups in a finite group. The papers [1, 5] provide valuable results in
this direction, though the presence of implicit constants makes it difficult to extract explicit bounds.
The analogous result for the number of maximal system of imprimitivity can be found in [6].

   For a non-zero integer n, we define λ(n) as the number of prime divisors of n, counted with
multiplicities. We believe that our main result can be improved by replacing log2 ([R : T ]) with
λ([R : T ]).
Conjecture 1.3. There exists a constant c > 0 such that if R is a finite group, and T ≤ R, then
                                    | Sub(R, T )| ≤ [R : T ]λ([R:T ])+c .
  We have little evidence for this conjecture: it is true for nilpotent groups, from the results in
Section 2. The function λ is inspired by its analogous use in the famous and important result of
Pyber in [7].

  A key step in the proof is establishing Theorem 1.1 for p-groups. This is accomplished in Section 2.
The general case is addressed in Section 3, where we first present the subgroup-counting argument
and then develop some arithmetic observations that lead to the final bound.
  Throughout, if k is an integer, R is a group and T ≤ R, we define
                            Subk (R, T ) = {H ∈ Sub(R, T ) : [R : H] = k}.

                                      3. Proof of Theorem 1.1
    We start with a preliminary observation.
Lemma 3.1. Let R be a finite group, let T be a subgroup of R, let p be a prime number, and let
Sylp (R, T ) denote the set of p-Sylow subgroups P of R such that T ∩ P ∈ Sylp (T ). Then the number
of T -orbits under conjugation on Sylp (R, T ) is at most [R : T ]/pc, where pc is the largest power of
p dividing [R : T ].

some x ∈ R such that T ∩ P x ∈ Sylp (T ). Define
