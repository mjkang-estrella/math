1    Introduction
Recall that a polynomial P (x) ∈ Q[x] is called integer-valued, if P (x) ∈ Z for all x ∈ Z.
During the past few years, integer-valued polynomials have been investigated by several
authors (see, for example, [3, 6, 13]). Recently, Z.-W. Sun [14, Conjectures 35(i)] proposed
the following conjecture.

Proof. Denote the left-hand side or the right-hand side of (2.1) by Sn (x). Applying Zeil-

4      Concluding remarks
Z.-W. Sun [14, Conjecture 35(ii)] conjectured that, for all positive integers l and n, the
polynomial

is integer-valued. Here (2l − 1)!! = (2l − 1)(2l − 3) · · · 3 · 1.
    We believe that the following (stronger) result is true.

Conjecture 2. Let l and n be positive integers and k a non-negative integer with k ⩽ n−1.
Then

    Our proof of Theorem 2 implies that the above conjecture is true for l = 1. In view of
(2.1), Sun’s conjecture follows from (4.1) too.
    Recently, q-analogues of congruences have been studied by many authors. See [4, 5, 7,
8, 15] and references therein. For l = 1, we have a q-analogue of (4.1) as follows:

where [n] = 1 + q + · · · + q n−1 is the q-integer and nk = j=1 (1 − q n−k+j )/(1 − q j ) denotes
the q-binomial coeﬃcient. The proof of (4.2) is similar to that of Theorem 2. However, we
cannot ﬁnd any q-analogue of (4.1) for l > 1.
