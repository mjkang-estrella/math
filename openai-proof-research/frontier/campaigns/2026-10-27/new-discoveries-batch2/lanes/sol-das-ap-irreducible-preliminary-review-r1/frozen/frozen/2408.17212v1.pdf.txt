                                                   Abstract. Denote by A(p, n, k) the number of commuting p-tuples of permutations on [n] that have
                                                   exactly k distinct orbits. It was conjectured in [Abd23b] that A(p, n, k) is log-concave with respect to k
                                                   for every p ≥ 2, n ≥ 3, and the log-concavity was proved in “p = ∞” case. In this paper, we prove that for
                                                   k = n − α, the log-concavity for A(p, n, k) holds for every p ≥ 2 for sufficiently large n.




                                                                                             1. Introduction

                                             For n ∈ N, let Sn denote the symmetric group on the set [n] = {1, . . . , n}. For any permutation
                                           σ ∈ Sn , let O(σ) denote the set of orbits under the action of σ on [n]. For any 1 ≤ k ≤ n, let us define
                                                                                  A(1, n, k) = {σ : Sn : |O(σ)| = k} .
                                           That is, A(1, n, k) is the number of permutations σ ∈ Sn that has exactly k orbits. It is well-known that
                                           A(1, n, k) equals the unsigned Stirling number of the first kind, and using this it can be shown (see, for
                                           example, [Abd23a]) that the A(1, n, k) is log-concave, that is, it satisfies
                                                               A(1, n, k)2 ≥ A(1, n, k − 1)A(1, n, k + 1),           2 ≤ k ≤ n − 1,         n ≥ 3.

                                             The quantity A(1, n, k) was generalized to A(p, n, k) as the number of pairwise commuting p-tuples of
                                           permutations such that the subgroup generated by them has exactly k orbits. More precisely, let p, n ∈ N
                                           and let Cp,n be the set of commuting p-tuples of permutations on Sn . That is,
                                                                       Cp,n = {(σ1 , . . . , σp ) ∈ (Sn )p : σi σj = σj σi ∀i, j ∈ [p]} .
                                           Furthermore, for σ = (σ1 , . . . , σp ) ∈ Cp,n , let hσi be the subgroup of Sn generated by σ1 , . . . , σp . Let
                                           O(σ) denote the number of orbits of [n] under the action of hσi. For 1 ≤ k ≤ n, define Cp,n,k to be the
                                           set
                                                                                   Cp,n,k = {σ ∈ Cp,n : |O(σ)| = k} ,
                                           and let A(p, n, k) = |Cp,n,k |. It was conjectured in [Abd23b] that A(p, n, k) is log-concave for all p and n.
                                           More precisely,
                                           Conjecture 1 ( [Abd23b]). Let p ≥ 2 and let n ≥ 3. Then,
                                                                   A(p, n, k)2 ≥ A(p, n, k − 1)A(p, n, k + 1) ,           2≤k ≤n−1.

                                              The case p = 2 was already conjectured in [HN21] and it implies the so-called unimodality conjecture for
                                           Nekrasov-Okunkov polynomials. We refer the reader to [Abd23b, ABDV24] for more in-depth discussion
                                           about this conjecture and [HN19, HN20, HZ21, Zha22] for more discussion as well as the work focused
                                           primarily on the case p = 2.
