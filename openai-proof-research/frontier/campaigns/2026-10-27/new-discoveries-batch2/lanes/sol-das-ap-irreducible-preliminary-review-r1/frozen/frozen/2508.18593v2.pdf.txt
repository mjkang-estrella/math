                                          1    Introduction
                                          It is well known that the tetrahedron K4 , the cube Q, and the truncated
                                          tetrahedron T have only integral eigenvalues when viewed as graphs. The
                                          following fact is less well known: The adjacency operator of the honeycomb
                                          lattice under the periodic boundary condition shown in Figure 1 has the
                                          integral eigenvalue multiset expressed by the Fourier formula,
                                                                                                             
                                                               2πki            2π(−k + 3l)i
                                          S = ± 1 + exp                + exp                   k ∈ Z/6Z, l ∈ Z/2Z .
                                                                 6                  6

                                          We give a unifying interpretation of these facts using the graph Galois theory.
                                          In particular, the Galois theory shows the following multisets relation,

                                                  S ∪ Spec(K4 ) ∪ Spec(K4 ) = Spec(Q) ∪ Spec(T ) ∪ Spec(T ),

                                          where Spec(Γ) is the multiset of the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix of
                                          a finite graph Γ. The integrality of S is derived from the fact that it is
                                          identical to the spectrum of a star graph whose integrality was conjectured
                                          by Abdollahi and Vatandoost [1] and proved by Krakovski and Mohar [9].
                                              Our main aim in this paper is to present a classical but underappreciated
                                          example of a non-abelian Galois cover of K4 with the Galois group S3 , whose
                                          intermediate covers are the cube and the truncated tetrahedron. Although

the graph is small and explicit, this cover seems to be not known in the
literature. We give a Cayley graph realization and compute the relation
between the Ihara zeta functions of the intermediate covers, clarifying its
connection to examples mentioned by Stark and Terras in [10]. We further
show some geometric properties, how this cover embeds in the torus or the

2     Construction of Sn-cover of Kn+1
Definition 1 (Graph Cover). Let X = (V, E) and X̃ = (Ṽ , Ẽ) be finite
simple graphs. A graph X̃ is called a cover of X if there exists a surjective
graph morphism
                                p : X̃ → X
such that for every vertex ṽ ∈ Ṽ , the map p induces a bijection between the
set of edges incident to ṽ and the set of edges incident to p(ṽ). In particular,
the local neighborhood structure is preserved.



                                        2
Definition 2 (Galois Cover). A cover p : X̃ → X is called Galois (or
regular) if there exists a group G acting freely and transitively on each fiber
p−1 (w) for every vertex w ∈ V (X), and such that the quotient graph X̃/G is
isomorphic to X.
    The group G is called the Galois group of the cover, and X̃ is called a
G-cover of X.
    The Sn -cover of Kn+1 we construct here is known as the star graph, which
is the Cayley graph of Sn+1 generated by the star transpositions. The star
graph is studied in [4] in the context of an algorithm generating permutations.
Its spectral properties has been closely studied.
Theorem 1. Let τi = (i, n+1) ∈ Sn+1 be the transposition for i = 1, 2, . . . , n,
and let Xn = Cay(Sn+1 , {τ1 , τ2 , . . . , τn }). Then, Xn is an Sn -cover of the
complete graph Kn+1 .
Proof. Let the vertex set of Kn+1 be V (Kn+1 ) = {1, 2, . . . , n + 1}. Then
there exists exactly one edge from i ∈ V (Kn+1 ) to j ∈ V (Kn+1 ) if and only
if i ̸= j. First we define the map pV : V (Xn ) = Sn+1 → V (Kn+1 ) by
                               pV (ξ) = ξ(n + 1),
which is clearly surjective. Xn has an edge ei from ξ to η if and only if there
exists a τi such that
                                   η = ξτi .
Then, pV (η) = ξτi (n + 1) = ξ(i). Thus pV induces a bijection from Eξ (Xn )
to EpV (σ) (Kn+1 ). These local bijections form a map pE : E(Xn ) → E(Kn+1 )
and p = (pV , pE ) is a covering map.
   Let Gn be a subgroup of Sn+1 defined by
                    Gn = {σ ∈ Sn+1 | σ(n + 1) = n + 1} .
Then Gn is isomorphic to Sn and acts naturally on V (Xn ) from the right.
First we show that this action of σ ∈ Gn on V (Xn ) induces a graph auto-
morphism of Xn . Let ξ and η be two adjacent vertices in Xn . That is, there
is a transposition τi , such that
                                    ξτi = η.
Then for every σ ∈ Gn , we have
                            ξστσ−1 (i) = ξτi σ = ησ,

                                       3
which implies ξσ is adjacent to ησ. Thus σ’s action on V (Xn ) induces a
graph automorphism of Xn .
   It is clear that
                             pV (ξσ) = pV (ξ),
for all ξ ∈ Sn+1 and σ ∈ G, since ξσ(n + 1) = ξ(n + 1). Thus G acts on each
fiber p−1 (i) freely and transitively for i = 1, 2, . . . , n + 1.

5     Concluding comments
In the case of the S3 -cover, the relation among Ihara zeta functions of inter-
mediate covers already determines non-trivial spectral relations. For larger
groups such as S4 or S5 , there exist many intermediate covers, and it is nat-
ural to ask whether the spectra of them can be reconstructed systematically
from the zeta relations and Theorem 3. We leave this direction for future
research.
