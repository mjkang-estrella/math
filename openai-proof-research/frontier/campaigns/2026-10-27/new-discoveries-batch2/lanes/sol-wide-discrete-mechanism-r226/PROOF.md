# Complete uncredited tensor refutation

Source: **Lihua You, Yafei Chen, Pingzhi Yuan**, *Some results of strongly primitive tensors*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.04554v1, submitted 12 May 2017. Definition 1.2 specifies the general product; Question 4.4, PDF p14, asks whether every nonnegative strongly primitive order-m dimension-n tensor, n≥3, satisfies η(A)<(n−1)²+1. No symmetry hypothesis is present. This is a refutation of that full universal assertion, not a classification of η. No nomination: the complete matrix-to-tensor bridge below is elementary and supplies a serious routine-adaptation exclusion. No claim of literature priority.

Let B be any primitive nonnegative n-by-n matrix and d=m−1≥2. Set A_(i,j1,...,jd)=B_(i,j1). Every column of B is nonzero. Write T_k(i,w) for an entry of A^k, where w is a word of d^k leaf labels. Its general-product recursion is

T_(k+1)(i,w1...wd)=Σ_(j1,...,jd) B_(i,j1) ∏_(a=1)^d T_k(ja,wa).

**Route 1: exact entry factorization.** Inductively there is a strictly positive scalar h_k(w), independent of i, such that T_k(i,w)=h_k(w)(B^k)_(i,first(w)). At k=1 take h=1. Given the formula at k, the first factor sums to h_k(w1)(B^(k+1))_(i,first(w1)); every other factor sums to h_k(wa)Σ_j(B^k)_(j,first(wa)). These column sums are strictly positive: every column of every power of B is nonzero. Their product defines h_(k+1)>0. Thus A^k is entrywise positive iff B^k is entrywise positive, and η(A)=exp(B), for every m≥3.

**Route 2: support dynamics.** For a nonempty set S let B(S)={i: ∃j∈S, B_ij>0}. At a parent whose child supports are S1,...,Sd, its support is B(S1), since the other child sets are nonempty. A no-zero-column B maps nonempty sets to nonempty sets. Starting from singleton leaf supports, the root support of EVERY depth-k labeled tree is B^k({first leaf}); consequently full positivity for every leaf assignment is exactly positivity of every column of B^k. This route is qualitative, independent of numerical entry sizes and of the scalar factorization.

For an entirely explicit witness use B=[[0,1,0],[0,0,1],[1,1,0]], n=3. Its powers B^1,...,B^5 have respectively 5,4,2,1,0 zero entries (see exact checks.json; these counts are independently recomputable by integer multiplication). In particular B^4_(1,1)=0 and B^5>0. Therefore for the order-3 tensor A_ijk=B_ij, η(A)=5, contradicting η(A)<5. Earlier powers cannot be positive because positivity persists under multiplication by B, whose columns are nonzero. The same lift works in every order m≥3. The computation is supplementary: the displayed matrix powers can be verified finitely, and the all-leaf/all-order implication is proved above.

The familiar Wielandt cycle-with-chord matrices give a broader all-dimension mechanism, but the counterexample does not rely on citing their general sharp exponent theorem. checks.py verifies n=3,...,12 only; it does not prove an all-n exponent formula. Source 1408.3457v1 Proposition 4.9 uses a DIFFERENT lift, copying the last index only when adjacent indices agree; its actual proof preserves local primitivity, not strong positivity. Our complete bridge is supplied explicitly rather than inferred from that proposition. The 2017 Theorem 3.8 concerns dimension 2 and is consistent with this witness. No tensor supersymmetry or positive-majorization shortcut is assumed.

For direct verification without running code, B⁴=[[0,1,1],[1,1,1],[1,2,1]] and B⁵=[[1,1,1],[1,2,1],[1,2,2]]. Thus the nonpositive fourth power and positive fifth power follow by ordinary integer multiplication; these are not approximate spectral calculations.
