## A. Exact lasso test, repaired prefix criterion

For q of length m, process output letters storing the suffix of length at most m and the age since a completed occurrence of q. Before the first occurrence age equals processed length. Complete q resets age to zero; otherwise reaching age m rejects. This finite safety automaton accepts exactly q-covered outputs: the first occurrence starts at position zero and consecutive occurrence ends have gaps at most m. Reading f(c) as one input transition gives the exact inverse language.

For specified u,v, v nonempty, q-coverage of uv^omega is decided by iterating whole v blocks until either rejection or a repeated boundary state. This is infinite-word certification, not truncation evidence.

**One absent prefix is NOT a nonquasiperiodicity certificate.** W=ab(aba)^omega has prefix ababa absent from (aba)^omega, but aba covers W, with starts 0,2,5,8,... . The failed 196-positive output is preserved in FAILED-prefix-absence-only.json and is INVALID. A short prefix can cover even when a longer prefix never recurs.

A correct finite criterion follows. Put N=|u|+|v| and P=the length-N prefix of W. If P occurs in v^omega, then W is purely periodic: at that occurrence's phase, the first |u| symbols match, and the next |v| symbols identify the phase of the entire periodic tail. Thus this periodic word equals W everywhere. Purely periodic W is quasiperiodic. Otherwise P is absent from the tail and no prefix of length >=N can cover W: such a covering word would have to occur arbitrarily far in the tail, forcing P there. Consequently W is NONquasiperiodic iff P is absent from every tail phase AND every nonempty prefix P[:j], 1<=j<N, fails the exact covering test. This supplies a complete decision procedure for a GIVEN lasso; it does not bound useful lassos in terms of f.

The corrected search tests 196 binary maps with image lengths 1..3, nonempty u,v of lengths <=3 and output q length <=12. It certifies 92 maps; 66 have either oriented unique-letter witness. The inherited 50 used only ab^omega, so its baseline is different. All 92 input nonquasiperiodicity claims now exclude shorter covers. No counterexample or universal theorem follows. Example f(a)=aba,f(b)=b has U=(ab)^omega, and W=a(ab)^omega is nonquasiperiodic (prefix aa unique); f(W) is aba-covered. Thus one tempting bounded-letter periodic map is already weak, not a counterexample.

**Remaining universal bridge:** show that the fixed-point path in some inverse-covering automaton forces an accepted lasso passing the entire finite input-cover exclusion test. A reachable cycle, a non-pure-periodic lasso, or an absent long prefix alone does not do this. An arbitrary cycle may visit all letters. No uniform input/output parameter bound has been proved.

## B. Growing-letter return blocks; an exact synchronization obstruction

If U is q-covered, its occurring alphabet is exactly that of q. Every occurring letter has bounded gaps: choose one occurrence of the letter inside q and translate it through a covering sequence. In particular prolongation letter a has bounded gaps and is growing. Return blocks from one a up to but not including the next a therefore have bounded lengths and form a finite delimiter code. Taking a high power grows a-images, suggesting an all-growing return substitution.

However even a delimiter code with bounded lengths can turn nonquasiperiodic macro-inputs into quasiperiodic ordinary words. Let tau(x)=ab, tau(y)=abb and V=yxy^omega. Every prefix other than y contains the unique x; y fails at that x, so V is nonquasiperiodic. Yet tau(V)=abbab(abb)^omega is abbab-covered, at starts 0,5,8,11,... . Every codeword starts with a and has no internal a; codewords have lengths 2 and 3. Thus bounded return lengths and an explicit delimiter do NOT provide the needed nonquasiperiodicity reflection. return-code-obstruction.json certifies the exact output cycle. This is a failure of the proposed decoding bridge, NOT a preserving-morphism counterexample to the source question.

The arXiv Appendix proves its long-image bridge using three local q-cover gluing properties, then cases on recurrence of the initial letter, nonrecurrent other letters, and two recurrent letters with a return avoiding the other. Its final witnesses use a letter absent from the periodic tail. Those gluing properties require each letter image >=2|q|. Bounded original letters do not satisfy this after any power. Applying the Appendix to return blocks still needs closure of their images at boundaries and a way to choose a witness whose decoded input has no shorter cover. The example above defeats automatic decoding, even for delimiter codes.

Decisive next calculation: augment return-block states by the shorter-cover obligations from mechanism A, and prove an accepted lasso with all obligations failing in the original alphabet. Alternatively produce an actual nonweak f satisfying the fixed-point hypotheses. The current packet does neither.

Strongest classical objection: safety automata, eventually periodic runs, the finite lasso covering test, and return-word encodings are classical tools. The finite criterion and route obstruction are uncredited helpers.
