                                                                                         Abstract
                                                     We prove that the Ehrhart h∗ -polynomials of crown order polytopes are real-rooted,
                                                 settling a conjecture of Lundström and Saud Maia Leite. For each integer r ≥ 3, the
                                                 crown order polytope on 2r elements and the rank-r whirl base polytope have the same
                                                 h∗ -polynomial. The h∗ -polynomials of the rank-r wheel and whirl base polytopes, as
                                                 well as every convex combination of the two, have 2r − 2 distinct negative roots. Their
                                                 coefficient sequences are positive, unimodal, and strictly ultra log-concave of order 2r −2.
                                                 Keywords: Ehrhart polynomial; crown poset; wheel matroid; whirl matroid; real-
                                                 rootedness; ultra log-concavity.
                                                 Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 05A15, 05B35, 06A07, 52B20.

                                           wheel and whirl base polytopes.

                                           extensions [13, Theorem 4.1 and Section 5]. For an integer r ≥ 2, the crown poset C2r is the
                                           poset on [2r] := {1, . . . , 2r} with cover relations

                                                                          1 ≺ 2 ≻ 3 ≺ · · · ≻ (2r − 1) ≺ 2r ≻ 1.

                                                          O(C2r ) := {x ∈ [0, 1]2r : x2i−1 ≤ x2i , x2i+1 ≤ x2i for 1 ≤ i ≤ r},                  (1.2)

    The proof treats all hr,λ , 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1, simultaneously. The crown–whirl identity and

explicit correction. We find points in [−1, 0) at which the crown polynomial alternates in sign
and exceeds the correction in modulus. To establish these bounds, we use a transfer-matrix
formula for the crown lattice-point count. Near t = 0, the largest eigenvalue approximates
the count closely enough to isolate a dominant term in the alternating Ehrhart series. For
the remaining points, including t = −1, we use a partial-fraction expansion of a hyperbolic
secant function: the phase of the nearest conjugate poles fixes the sign, and their contribution
dominates the remaining terms and the wheel correction. Both arguments establish the same
comparison, so the chosen points force alternating signs for every hr,λ and its reversal, giving
r − 1 roots in each of (−1, 0) and (−∞, −1). These 2r − 2 distinct roots exhaust the degree
and are therefore simple.

5    Even cycles, products, and dilations
An affine equivalence transfers the crown theorem to stable-set polytopes of even cycles.
Two preservation theorems then extend the results to products and dilations, including base
polytopes of matroid direct sums.
    For a finite simple graph G, a stable set is a vertex subset containing no pair of adjacent
vertices. The stable-set polytope STAB(G) is the convex hull of the indicator vectors of the
stable sets of G.
    Symmetry and unimodality for the stable-set polytopes of even cycles follow from the
cyclic block construction of Jiang, Yang, and Zhong [8, Theorem 1.5], with block size one and
cycle length 2r. The following specialization of Stanley’s affine equivalence [13, Theorem 2.3]
transfers the crown root result to even cycles.
Remark 5.1. For r ≥ 2, label C2r cyclically by 1, . . . , 2r and consider the affine involution

                           x 7→ y = (x1 , 1 − x2 , . . . , x2r−1 , 1 − x2r ).

For x ∈ {0, 1}2r , the crown inequalities in (1.2) become

                        yi + yi+1 ≤ 1,        1 ≤ i ≤ 2r,         y2r+1 = y1 .

These inequalities say precisely that {i : yi = 1} is a stable set of C2r . The map therefore
bijects the 0/1 points of O(C2r ) with the stable-set indicators of C2r . By [13, Corollary 1.3],
the crown order polytope is the convex hull of its 0/1 points. Hence, taking convex hulls iden-
tifies its image with STAB(C2r ). The map is an integral affine involution, so the equivalence
is unimodular and preserves Ehrhart polynomials. Consequently,

                                   h∗STAB(C2r ) (t) = h∗O(C2r ) (t).                        (5.1)

For r ≥ 3, the common polynomial has simple negative roots by Theorem 1.1; for r = 2, it
equals (1 + t)2 by (3.9).
   For lattice polytopes, real-rootedness of h∗ is preserved under Cartesian products [15,
Theorem 0.2] and positive integer dilations [16, Corollary 3.4].

    For cycle matroids, the same conclusion applies to graphs obtained from wheels by re-
peatedly taking disjoint unions or identifying a pair of vertices, one from each of two disjoint
graphs. Neither operation creates a cycle using edges from both graphs. Thus, the cycle
matroid of the resulting graph is the direct sum of the two original cycle matroids. Adding
a bridge or a loop adds a base-polytope coordinate fixed at 1 or 0, respectively. Deleting the
added coordinate gives a bijection on lattice points in every nonnegative integer dilation, so
the Ehrhart polynomial is preserved.
