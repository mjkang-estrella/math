       Abstract
For Vertex Cover, a minimal blocking set of a graph G is an inclusion-wise minimal vertex set
contained in no minimum vertex cover of G. Bougeret et al. [SIDMA 2022] showed that, for all
minor-closed graph classes G, Vertex Cover has a polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance
to G if and only if G has bounded minimal blocking sets.1
     In this work, we explore how far this connection between minimal blocking sets and kernelization
extends to F-Minor Deletion parameterized by the deletion distance to G. For a fixed nonempty
finite family F of graphs, given a graph G and integer k, F-Minor Deletion asks whether G can
be made free of minors from F by deleting at most k vertices.
     A minimal F-blocking set of G is an inclusion-wise minimal vertex set not contained in any
optimal F-Minor Deletion solution of G. For families F of connected graphs, we show that
if some graph class G closed under disjoint union has unbounded minimal F-blocking sets, then
F-Minor Deletion parameterized by the deletion distance to G has no polynomial kernel. This
generalizes a result of Hols et al. [SIDMA 2022] for Vertex Cover.
     We then show that this criterion does not capture all kernelization lower bounds for F-Minor
Deletion, even for minor-closed graph classes. Motivated by the work of Dekker and Jansen [Discrete
Appl. Math. 2024], we study the more general notion of extended F-blocking sets. Under additional
conditions, we show that G having unbounded minimal extended F-blocking sets rules out polynomial
kernels.
     For every fixed integer η ≥ 1, Pw-η Deletion is the problem of deleting at most k vertices
to reduce the pathwidth of a given graph to at most η. We show that it has no polynomial
kernel parameterized by the distance to graphs with pathwidth at most η + 1. Moreover, for all
families F of graphs, each connected and having at least two vertices, we show that F-Minor
Deletion parameterized by the distance to graphs with pathwidth at most maxF ∈F pw(F ) + 1
has no polynomial kernel. These lower bounds rely in part on a pathwidth analysis of a minor
modification of a construction due to Donkers and Jansen [J. Comput. Syst. Sci. 2021].

        1    Introduction
    Kernelization. Kernelization [14, 23] is the formal study of preprocessing algorithms for
    parameterized problems. Given an instance (I, k) of a parameterized problem (with parameter
    k), the goal is to output an equivalent instance (I ′ , k ′ ) in polynomial time, where both |I ′ |
    and k ′ are bounded by f (k) for some computable function f . If f is a polynomial function,
    then such an algorithm is called a polynomial kernel. It is well-known that a decidable
    parameterized problem is fixed-parameter-tractable (FPT) if and only if it has a kernel, but
    not necessarily one of polynomial size. One of the main goals in the field is understanding
    which problems admit polynomial kernels.

    Vertex cover. Vertex Cover is perhaps the most-studied problem in this paradigm.
    There are many fundamental and different techniques to obtain polynomial kernels for the
    problem when parameterizing by the solution size [11–14, 20, 23, 34]. When going beyond the
    solution size as the parameter, a next natural step is considering the (vertex-deletion) distance
    to a graph class G as the parameter, such that the problem is polynomial-time-solvable on G.
    This follows the paradigm of parameterizing by “distance from triviality” [4, 28, 32].
        Formally, a problem instance of Vertex Cover/dist-to-G consists of a graph G, integer
    k, and set M ⊆ V (G) (a modulator to G) such that G − M ∈ G. The parameter is |M |, and
    the question is whether G has a vertex cover of size at most k. One of the most important
    works in this direction is the result of Jansen and Bodlaender [32] that shows the problem
    admits a polynomial kernel when G is the graph class of forests. This result has led to an
    array of further kernelization results for Vertex Cover/dist-to-G [8,15,24,31,36] showing
    the existence and non-existence of polynomial kernels for the considered parameterization.
    Eventually, the question for which minor-closed graph classes G the problem admits a
    polynomial kernel was settled: Bougeret et al. [6] show that, when G is minor-closed, Vertex
    Cover/dist-to-G admits a polynomial kernel if and only if G has bounded bridgedepth.

    Minimal blocking sets. There is a central concept which kept (sometimes implicitly)
    appearing in these works: minimal blocking sets. For Vertex Cover, a blocking set of a
    graph G is a set X ⊆ V (G) such that no minimum vertex cover of G contains all of X. A
    blocking set is minimal if no proper subset of it is a blocking set. See Figure 1 for a small
    example. Already the work of Jansen and Bodlaender [32] exhibits that bounding the size of
    minimal blocking sets is useful for kernelization, and shows that any minimal blocking set of
    a forest has size at most 2. The same concept similarly plays a central role in subsequent
    papers about the problem [1, 6, 8, 24, 30, 31, 36].2
        Interestingly, minimal blocking sets are powerful both for the design of kernels and for
    showing kernelization lower bounds. In fact, under further (in some sense mild) assumptions
    on G, it is possible to reduce the number of connected components of G−M to a polynomial in
    |M | when there is some constant c bounding the size of any minimal blocking set of any graph
    in G [31]. In contrast, if G is closed under disjoint union and minimal blocking sets of graphs
    in G can be arbitrarily large, then no polynomial kernel for Vertex Cover/dist-to-G
    exists [31]. In fact, the concept of minimal blocking sets is so central to the problem that it
    yields a complete kernelization dichotomy for minor-closed graph classes G: Bougeret et al. [6]
    show that minor-closed G has bounded minimal blocking sets if and only if G has bounded

    Motivated by this importance of minimal blocking sets for Vertex Cover, we explore
the same (and related) concepts for the family of F-Minor Deletion problems. For a fixed
finite family of graphs F, an instance consists of a graph G and integer k, and the question
is whether there is a set S ⊆ V (G) of size at most k such that G − S has no graph of F as a
minor. When setting F = {P2 }, one obtains Vertex Cover; F = {K3 } yields Feedback
Vertex Set, and there are many further fundamental problems which are captured by this
framework; see e.g., Bougeret et al. [4]. Parameterized by the solution size the problem is
known to be FPT for all families F [42]. When F contains a planar graph, Fomin et al. [22]
give a randomized polynomial kernel, which can be derandomized using an algorithm due
to Gupta et al. [29], as noted by Bougeret et al. [4]. But it remains a major open question
whether the problem admits a polynomial kernel for all families F.
    It is also known that, for all fixed families F the problem F-Minor Deletion parame-
terized by the size of a vertex cover admits a polynomial kernel [21]. For every fixed finite
family F of connected graphs and every fixed integer d ≥ 1, the problem even admits a
polynomial kernel when parameterized by the distance to the class of graphs of treedepth at
most d [33].
    When parameterizing by the distance to a minor-closed graph class G, some complete
dichotomies going beyond the one for Vertex Cover are known. When F = {K3 } a
polynomial kernel exists if and only if G has bounded elimination distance3 to the class
of forests [17]. This result has been generalized to show that, when all graphs in F are
biconnected on at least three vertices and at least one graph is planar, then F-Minor
Deletion parameterized by the distance to G admits a polynomial kernel if and only if G
has bounded elimination distance to the class of F-minor free graphs [4]. The latter result is
obtained by combining a new kernel with known lower bounds due to Dekker and Jansen [17].
Recently, in an update to the arXiv version of [4], new results have appeared that give a
similar characterization also for some non-biconnected families [5].
    The main objects of study in this paper are blocking set notions for F-Minor Deletion
problems and the relation to F-Minor Deletion parameterized by the vertex-deletion
distance to some graph class G, specifically to obtain kernelization lower bounds. An F-
blocking set of a graph G is a set X ⊆ V (G) with the property that no minimum set S such
that G − S is F-minor free contains all of X. Our aim is to investigate the usefulness of this
and related notions for kernelization lower bounds. Related blocking set notions already play
an important role in kernelization for F-Minor Deletion and related problems [4, 7, 17, 33].
Extending the earlier result for Vertex Cover [31], Greilhuber and Sharma [26] derive
quantitative kernelization lower bounds for d-Component Order Connectivity from the

    1.1      Our Results
    As our first main result, we obtain kernelization lower bounds for F-Minor Deletion
    from large minimal F-blocking sets. Given a graph G and integer c, the graph c · G is
    the graph created by taking the disjoint union of c copies of G. The problem F-Minor
    Deletion/dist-to-G is F-Minor Deletion parameterized by the size of a set M given
    as part of the input such that G − M ∈ G.

    ▶ Theorem 1.1. Let F be a nonempty finite family of connected graphs on at least two
    vertices, and G a graph class. Assume there is some G ∈ G with a minimal F-blocking set B,
    such that for any integer c ≥ 1 the graph c·G is in G. Then, F-Minor Deletion/ dist-to-G
    has no compression of size O(|M ||B|−ε ), for any ε > 0, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

        Our theorem generalizes the known quantitative kernelization lower bounds obtained
    from minimal F-blocking sets for Vertex Cover [31] and d-Component Order Connec-
    tivity [26]. Note that the assumption that c · G is in G is always fulfilled when G is closed
    under disjoint union, which is the case for many natural graph classes.
        To exhibit the usefulness of Theorem 1.1, let us explain a common approach for showing
    kernelization lower bounds in the literature. The known kernelization lower bounds for Ver-
    tex Cover [6,15,31,36], and also the lower bound for F-Minor Deletion parameterized by
    the distance to graphs of bounded treewidth [19] essentially function this way (for connected
    F). The lower bound is typically shown by a reduction from CNF-SAT parameterized by
    the number of variables (or Hitting Set parameterized by the universe size) for which
    compression lower bounds are known [18]. For each variable a variable gadget of constant
    size is created, and, similarly, for each clause a clause gadget is added. Interestingly, all
    these reductions share a common core: the central property of each clause gadget is that
    it has a minimal F-blocking set X, where |X| matches the number of literals of the clause.
    Theorem 1.1 shows that this “gold-standard” approach for Vertex Cover/dist-to-G
    extends to all F-Minor Deletion problems with connected families F.
        Given Theorem 1.1 and the dichotomy for Vertex Cover by Bougeret et al. [6], it is a
    natural question whether all kernelization lower bounds for F-Minor Deletion problems
    with minor-closed G can be obtained from G having unbounded minimal F-blocking sets.4
    We show that this is not the case: for F = {K3 }, that is Feedback Vertex Set, there is a
    minor-closed graph class G with bounded minimal {K3 }-blocking sets, such that there is no
    polynomial kernel for the problem parameterized by the distance to G. In fact, gadgets used
    for the lower bound of Dekker and Jansen [17, Theorem 2] give rise to such a graph class.

    ▶ Theorem 1.2. Let F = {K3 }. There is a minor-closed graph class G that has minimal
    F-blocking sets of size at most two, such that F-Minor Deletion/ dist-to-G has no
    polynomial kernel, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

       Thus, the basic definition of minimal F-blocking sets is, while being useful, not the right
    notion for all F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G problems, even when G is minor-closed.

Beyond basic blocking sets. To mitigate this situation somewhat, we consider a generaliza-
tion of minimal F-blocking sets. This notion goes back to Dekker and Jansen [17, Lemma 1]
(and even Jansen and Pieterse [33, Lemma 3] in a more general form), and was algorithmically
exploited in their kernel for Feedback Vertex Set. We use OPTF (G) to denote the size of
a minimum set that makes G free of an F-minor.

▶ Definition 1.3 (Extended Blocking Sets). Given a family of connected graphs F and a
graph G, an extended F-blocking set of G is a pair (X, Y ), where X, Y ⊆ V (G) are two
disjoint sets, such that there is no set S ⊆ V (G) such that:
1. |S| = OPTF (G),
2. G − S is F -minor free,
3. X ⊆ S, and
4. no two vertices of Y \ S are in the same connected component of G − S.
An extended F-blocking set (X, Y ) is minimal if, for all X ′ ⊊ X the pair (X ′ , Y ) is not an
extended F-blocking set, and for all Y ′ ⊊ Y the pair (X, Y ′ ) is not an extended F-blocking
set.
     A graph class G has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets if there is some constant
c such that for each graph G ∈ G and each minimal extended F-blocking set (X, Y ) of G
we have |X ∪ Y | ≤ c. If no such constant c exists, then G has unbounded minimal extended
F-blocking sets.

    For an F-Minor Deletion problem, any vertex of a graph is a “partial obstruction”
in the sense that it may be extended to a graph that is a forbidden minor when combining
it with further edges and vertices. Similarly, any path might be a partial obstruction that
corresponds to an edge of a forbidden minor. Hence, Definition 1.3 is a natural extension of
blocking sets which takes not just the simplest partial obstructions, i.e., individual vertices,
but also slightly more complicated partial obstructions, in this case paths, into account. See
Figure 2 for a small example illustrating the two roles played by X and Y .
    In fact, even more general notions of minimal blocking sets emerge from the work of
Jansen and Pieterse [33, Lemma 3] that come with great algorithmic power (see also [4]),
but their usefulness for kernelization lower bounds beyond the notions we study here is not
clear (to us).5

is the central concept for all problem families (and minor-closed G). Crushing this hope, with
the help of an unpublished result personally communicated to us by Bougeret, Brandwein,
and Sau [June 2026], we show that this is not the case. Concretely, in Theorem 4.21 we show
that the family of caterpillar forests has unbounded minimal extended {K1,3 }-blocking sets.
However, the upcoming result of Bougeret et al. will show that the problem does have a
polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance to caterpillar forests. Consequently, this
shows that one cannot simply drop the assumption that the extended F-blocking set (X, Y )
is good from Theorem 1.6.

New lower bounds. We then use our framework to provide new kernelization lower bounds.
The main problem we study is Pw-η Deletion. Here the input is a graph G and integer
k, and the question is whether there is a set S ⊆ V (G) of size at most k such that G − S
has pathwidth at most η. This is an F-Minor Deletion problem obtained by setting F
to the class of minor-minimal graphs with pathwidth η + 1 (a family F is minor-minimal if
no graph of F is a proper minor of another). Since the corresponding family F contains a
planar graph a polynomial kernel exists when parameterizing by the solution-size for each
fixed η [22, 29]. The uniform kernelization complexity of this class of problems was the
focus of recent work [25]. When η = 0, Pw-η Deletion is the same as Vertex Cover.
Kernelization for the special case η = 1 has also been studied in the literature [16, 39, 43]. We
consider the parameterization by the distance to Gpw≤β where Gpw≤β is the class of graphs
with pathwidth bounded by some constant β. For each η ≥ 1, we show that Pw-η Deletion
has no polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance to Gpw≤η+1 . We combine this lower
bound with known upper bounds [22, 32] and a lower bound for the case when η = 0 [15] to
obtain the following kernelization dichotomy.6

▶ Theorem 1.8. When η = 0, Pw-η Deletion admits a polynomial kernel when parameter-
ized by the distance to Gpw≤1 , but no polynomial kernel when parameterized by the distance
to Gpw≤2 , unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.
    When η ≥ 1, Pw-η Deletion admits a polynomial kernel when parameterized by the
distance to Gpw≤η , but no polynomial kernel when parameterized by the distance to Gpw≤η+1 ,
unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

    A similar result is already known for Tw-η-Deletion when G has bounded treewidth [15,
22, 32]. The new results given in Theorem 1.8 are a corollary of a more general and technical
result about F-Minor Deletion problems parameterized by the distance to graph classes
of bounded pathwidth (Lemma 6.15). We do not mention the formal statement of this result
here, but state one more interesting consequence.

    A remark on concurrent related work. Largely concurrently with this work, in an update
    to the arXiv version of [4], Bougeret et al. [5] have generalized the lower bounds of Dekker
    and Jansen [17] to also capture some non-biconnected families F.
         The proof of these new lower bounds, given in Section 7.2 and Appendix B of [5], actually
    also uses gadgets that have unbounded minimal extended F-blocking sets (X, Y ), with
    |Y | = 2, and in general functions similarly to that of Dekker and Jansen [17]. In fact, also
    for those families F and graph classes G for which the new lower bounds were shown it can
    be observed that there is a polynomial kernel for F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G if and
    only if G has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets. They additionally provide results
    (lower and upper bounds) about the size of the kernels in Section 7.3 and Appendix A.
         However, the assumptions we make in Theorem 1.6 are not suitable to obtain these new
    lower bounds as an application from our result. That is, the minimal extended F-blocking
    sets used in the constructions of Bougeret et al. [5] are not necessarily good. At the same
    time, the results we give are orthogonal: Theorem 1.6 can give lower bounds for families F
    that are not in the scope of the new lower-bound results of Bougeret et al. [5]. For example,
    if H is the graph consisting of two disjoint triangles connected by an edge, then our lower
    bound framework (as shown by Theorem 1.9) can be used to show lower bounds when
    F = {H}, but this family is out of the scope of the lower-bound results of [5].

    1.2    Conclusion
    In this work we study the notions of F-blocking sets and extended F-blocking sets. We
    provide a high-level theorem that shows that, when G is closed under disjoint union, G having
    unbounded minimal F-blocking sets immediately rules out polynomial kernels for F-Minor
    Deletion/dist-to-G, and a similar theorem that applies for unbounded minimal extended
    F-blocking sets in some cases. We hope that these theorems can simplify future lower-bound
    proofs.
        As a new application, we show that, for each integer η ≥ 1, Pw-η Deletion has no
    polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance to graphs with pathwidth η + 1. Together
    with known algorithmic results and lower bounds, for any η and β the kernelization complexity
    of Pw-η Deletion parameterized by the distance to Gpw≤β is mapped out. We additionally
    make explicit that the kernelization dichotomies for biconnected families F depend on G
    having bounded minimal extended F -blocking sets.
        To the best of our knowledge, all currently known dichotomies for F-Minor Deletion
    with connected families F parameterized by the distance to minor-closed G can be shown to
    depend on the minimal blocking set notions we study. At the same time, it is highly unclear
    if the considered notions of minimal blocking sets are the right notions for all F-Minor
    Deletion/dist-to-G problems with connected families F and minor-closed G, or if such a
    “catch all” notion even exists. Investigating this further is an exciting and promising topic
    for future work.

2.1    From Extended Blocking Sets to Lower Bounds
First, let us sketch the proof of Theorem 1.6. This proof uses gadgets we call universe gadgets,
specified as follows.

▶ Definition 2.1 (Universe Gadget). Let F be a family of graphs. A universe gadget is a
triple (H, v, t) consisting of a graph H, distinguished vertex v ∈ V (H), and integer t, such
that:
 1. the smallest vertex set S ⊆ V (H) such that H − S is F-minor free has size t,
 2. there is a set S ⊆ V (H) such that H − S is F-minor free with |S| = t, and v ∈ S,
 3. there is a set S ⊆ V (H) such that H − S is F-minor free with |S| = t, and N (v) ⊆ S,
 4. and v has exactly two neighbors in H.

    We show that such a gadget exists for every nonempty family F of connected graphs on at
least two vertices each. The construction is different depending on whether all graphs in F are
biconnected: if they are, we adapt a construction of Dekker and Jansen [17]; otherwise we give
a separate construction. Then, we exploit these gadgets in a linear parameter transformation
from b-Hitting Set parameterized by the size of the universe n, for which no compression
of size O(nb−ε ) exists for any ε > 0 and any fixed b ≥ 2, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly [18].

     2.2      A Dichotomy for Biconnected Families
     Now, let us quickly elaborate on Theorem 1.7, the characterization based on the notion of
     extended F-blocking sets for when F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G with minor-closed G
     and biconnected graphs F admits a polynomial kernel.

     ▶ Theorem 1.7. Let F be a family of biconnected graphs containing at least one planar
     graph, and G a minor-closed graph class. Assume NP ̸⊆ coNP/poly. Then, F-Minor
     Deletion/ dist-to-G admits a polynomial kernel if and only if G has bounded minimal
     extended F-blocking sets.

         If K1 ∈ F, the problem is trivial, and all minor-closed G have bounded minimal extended
     F-blocking sets. Otherwise, if K2 ∈ F, the notion of minimal extended F-blocking sets
     collapses to the notion of minimal F-blocking sets, and then this result follows from the
     work of Bougeret et al. [6]. It remains to consider the case when all graphs in F have at
     least three vertices. In this case, we analyze the lower bound by Dekker and Jansen [17].
     They show that, when F contains a planar graph,7 any minor-closed graph class G that has
     unbounded elimination distance to the class of F-minor free graphs contains arbitrarily long
     F-necklaces.

     ▶ Definition 2.2 (F-Necklace [17, Definition 7]). Let F be a nonempty family of connected
     graphs, and ℓ a positive integer. An F-necklace of length ℓ is a graph G where V (G) can be
     partitioned into sets V1 , . . . , Vℓ , such that:
     1. for each i ∈ [ℓ] the graph G[Vi ] is isomorphic to some graph in F ,
     2. there is exactly one edge between Vi and Vi+1 for each i ∈ [ℓ − 1],
     3. there are no edges between Vi and Vi′ when i′ ∈  / {i − 1, i, i + 1}.

         We observe that the main property exploited by Dekker and Jansen [17] for their lower
     bound is that, for minor-minimal families F of biconnected graphs on at least three vertices,
     the length of an F-necklace is directly tied to the size of good minimal extended F-blocking
     sets, yielding one direction of the characterization. For the other direction, we show that a
     recent result of Bougeret et al. [4, Lemma 6], which bounds the size of certain more complex
     blocking sets, can be exploited to show that, when G has bounded elimination distance to the
     class of F -minor free graphs, then G also has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets.

consider families F containing only a single connected graph F . If F is biconnected on at

pw(F ) that has a good minimal extended F-blocking set with |X| = ℓ and |Y | = 2. In fact,
the graphs we use here are again specific F-necklaces.

▶ Lemma 2.3. Let F be a minor-minimal family of biconnected graphs on at least three
vertices. Then, for each F ∈ F and for each integer ℓ ≥ 1 there is a graph GFℓ with pathwidth

                                                   F
and |Y | = 2. Moreover, each set S that makes Gℓ free of an F -minor is such that each
biconnected component of GF ℓ − S has fewer than |V (F )| vertices.

    If F is not biconnected, and contains at least three vertices, we instead use a slight
simplification of a construction of Donkers and Jansen [19]. Although not made explicit
by them, for each integer ℓ ≥ 1 they give a construction yielding a graph with a minimal
{F }-blocking set of size ℓ. This construction is easily shown to have the same treewidth
as F , which (with some considerable additional work) also yielded one main result of [19].
Luckily for us, due to its linear nature it can also be used for the parameter pathwidth. We
show that, in some specific cases the pathwidth of the construction is the same as pw(F );
in other cases it is at most pw(F ) + 1. As mentioned before, one cannot avoid the additive
constant of 1 in this bound for each graph F .
    Concretely, for our analysis we define the notions of pathwidth criticality and of tidy leaf
blocks that we present next.

▶ Definition 2.4 (Pathwidth Critical). Given a graph G, a vertex v ∈ V (G) is pathwidth
critical if pw(G) > pw(G − v).

▶ Definition 2.5 (Tidy Leaf Blocks). Let G be a connected graph that is not biconnected.
Then, G has a tidy leaf block if there is a smallest (in terms of number of vertices) leaf block
L of G such that there is a path decomposition X = X1 , . . . , Xt of L of width at most pw(G),
where X1 = {c} and Xt = {b}, where c is the cut vertex of G contained in L, and b ̸= c is a
vertex of L that is pathwidth critical for G.

   We also exploit a further notion of Donkers and Jansen [19], which is later needed to
obtain the lower bound for families containing more than one graph, the β-prune of a graph.

▶ Definition 2.6 (Prune [19]). Let β be an integer and H a graph. Then, β-prune(H) is the
possibly empty graph obtained from H by repeatedly choosing a leaf block L of the current
graph containing fewer than β vertices, and deleting the vertices of L that are not cut vertices
of the current graph.

   We then obtain the following result by analyzing the pathwidth of the construction.

▶ Lemma 2.7. Let H be a connected graph that is not biconnected. If H has a tidy leaf
block, set ω = pw(H); otherwise set ω = pw(H) + 1.
    Then, for any integer ℓ ≥ 1 there is a graph G⋆ℓ with pathwidth at most ω that has a
minimal {H}-blocking set X of size ℓ. Moreover, for any v ∈ X there is a set Sv ⊆ V (G⋆ℓ ) of
size OPT{H} (G⋆ℓ ) containing X \ {v}, such that G⋆ℓ − Sv is H-minor free, and each connected
component of |V (L)|-prune(G⋆ℓ − Sv ) is a minor of H, where L is a smallest leaf block of H.

         The remaining case when F = K2 is already handled by the work of Cygan et al. [15].
         To go from families F that contain a single graph to minor-minimal families containing

      3     Preliminaries
     For integers x, z we write [x, z] = {y ∈ Z | x ≤ y ≤ z}. We use [n] as a shorthand for [1, n].
     For a set S and integer b, we use Sb to denote the set of all subsets of S of size exactly b.

     Graph minors and leaf blocks. We use standard graph notation, and will only repeat
     notions here that are especially relevant for the paper. All graphs are finite and simple. For
     a graph G and integer c ≥ 0 the graph c · G is the graph obtained by taking the disjoint
     union of c copies of G. A graph H is a minor of a graph G if H can be obtained from G by
     repeatedly deleting edges, deleting vertices, or contracting edges. If H is a minor of G, we
     also say that G has an H-minor. We call a set of graphs F minor-minimal if there are no
     two distinct graphs H1 , H2 ∈ F such that H1 is a minor of H2 .
          Given graphs G and H, G is H-minor free if H is not a minor of G. Similarly, for any
     family of graphs F, graph G is F-minor free if no F ∈ F is a minor of G. Note that for any
     family F there is a finite family F ′ ⊆ F such that a graph is F-minor free if and only if it is
     F ′ -minor free; this is a consequence of the famous graph minor theorem [41]. Hence, when
     required for the application of a lemma, we may assume that F is finite. Next, we define
     some notions related to biconnectivity, following the convention of Donkers and Jansen [19].

     ▶ Definition 3.1 (Biconnectivity and Blocks). For a graph G a vertex v ∈ V (G) is a cut
     vertex if G − v has more connected components than G. A bridge of G is an edge uv ∈ E(G)
     such that G − uv has more connected components than G.
         A graph is biconnected if it is connected and contains no cut vertex. A biconnected
     component or block of a graph is a maximal biconnected subgraph. A block B is a leaf block
     if B contains at most one cut vertex of G. In particular, a biconnected graph is a leaf block.

        In particular, the used convention gives us that K1 and K2 are biconnected graphs.
        Given graphs H and G, a minor-model of H in G is a function φ : V (H) → 2V (G) , such
     that (1) for distinct u, v ∈ V (H) the sets φ(v) and φ(u) are disjoint, (2) for each v ∈ V (H)
     the graph G[φ(v)] is nonempty and connected, (3) for each edge uv ∈ E(H) there is an
                                                         S
     edge from φ(u) to φ(v) in G. We denote φ(H) = v∈V (H) φ(v). A minor-model φ of H is
     minimal if there is no minor-model φ′ of H with φ′ (H) ⊊ φ(H). This minor-model notation
     was also used by Dekker and Jansen [17].
        The following proposition given by Dekker and Jansen [17] will also be useful.

Parameterized complexity. Let us now define the notions of kernelization and compressions.
We refer to the textbook by Cygan et al. [14] for more background on the basic notions
surrounding parameterized algorithms and kernelization.

▶ Definition 3.4 (Parameterized Problems). Let Σ be a finite alphabet. A parameterized
problem is a language L ⊆ Σ∗ × N. An instance of a parameterized problem is a tuple (I, k)
where I ∈ Σ∗ and k ∈ N, where k is called the parameter of the instance. The instance is a
yes-instance (for problem L) if (I, k) ∈ L; otherwise it is a no-instance.

▶ Definition 3.5 (Kernel). A kernelization (kernel) of a parameterized problem L is an
algorithm that takes instances (I, k) of the problem as input, runs in time polynomial in
|I| + k, and outputs an instance (I ′ , k ′ ) equivalent to (I, k) with |I ′ |, k ′ ≤ f (k) for some
computable function f .
    The function f is called the size of the kernel, and if f is a polynomial function, the
kernel is a polynomial kernel.

▶ Definition 3.6 (Compression). Let L ⊆ Σ∗ × N be a parameterized problem, and Q ⊆ Σ∗ a
language. A compression from L into Q is a polynomial-time algorithm that takes instances
(I, k) ∈ Σ∗ × N as input, runs in time polynomial in |I| + k, outputs I ′ ∈ Σ∗ such that I ′ ∈ Q
if and only if (I, k) ∈ L, and |I ′ | ≤ f (k) for some computable function f .
    The function f is called the size of the compression, and if f is a polynomial function, we
speak of a polynomial compression. When the language Q is not specified, and we speak of the
nonexistence of a compression of a certain size, we mean the nonexistence of a compression
into any target language Q.

   Clearly, a kernel can be seen as a certain type of compression, and hence ruling out
compressions of certain sizes is stronger than ruling out kernels of the same size.

▶ Definition 3.7 (Polynomial Parameter Transformation). Given two parameterized problems
Q1 and Q2 , a polynomial parameter transformation from Q1 to Q2 is an algorithm that
takes instances (I, k) of Q1 as input, runs in polynomial time in |I| + k, and outputs an
equivalent instance (I ′ , k ′ ) of Q2 , such that k ′ ≤ p(k) for some polynomial function p. If p is
a linear function, we call the algorithm a linear parameter transformation.

    Polynomial parameter transformations can be used to transfer kernelization lower bounds
in the natural way.

      4     Lower Bounds From Minimal Blocking Sets
     We now proceed to our study of kernelization lower bounds via large minimal (extended)
     F-blocking sets. First, in Section 4.1 we define the most basic notion of F-blocking sets and
     explain how they yield lower bounds. Then, we exhibit the limits of this notion in Section 4.2.
     To mitigate these limitations to some degree, we introduce minimal extended F-blocking sets
     in Section 4.3. Finally, we establish that it is not possible that the kernelization dichotomies
     for F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G for all connected families F and minor-closed G depend
     on minimal extended F-blocking sets being bounded in Section 4.4.

     4.1    Blocking Sets
     We first define blocking sets in the following most basic sense.

     ▶ Definition 4.1 (Blocking Set). Let F be a set of graphs, and G a graph. A set X ⊆ V (G)
     is a F-blocking set if all sets S ⊆ V (G) with X ⊆ S where G − S is F-minor free have
     size strictly larger than OPTF (G). An F-blocking set is called minimal if it is inclusion-wise
     minimal with respect to the property of being a blocking set.
A. Ghazy, J. Greilhuber, and T. A. Hartmann                                                             15


    A graph class G has bounded minimal F-blocking sets if there is some constant b such
that for each graph G ∈ G each minimal F-blocking set of G has size at most b. If no such
constant b exists, then G has unbounded minimal F-blocking sets.

   If F is clear from the context, we may refer to a (minimal) F-blocking set simply as a
(minimal) blocking set. The strength of minimal blocking sets comes from the fact that having
unbounded minimal blocking sets for G in the problem F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G
immediately yields a kernelization lower bound (under some further mild conditions). We
prove Theorem 1.1 in Section 4.3 as a corollary of a more general result.

▶ Theorem 1.1. Let F be a nonempty finite family of connected graphs on at least two
vertices, and G a graph class. Assume there is some G ∈ G with a minimal F-blocking set B,
such that for any integer c ≥ 1 the graph c·G is in G. Then, F-Minor Deletion/ dist-to-G
has no compression of size O(|M ||B|−ε ), for any ε > 0, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

    Although there are limitations to Theorem 1.1, the result is already useful for providing
lower bounds. For example, the lower bounds for Vertex Cover/dist-to-G parameterized
by the distance to minor-closed G given in the literature [6, 15, 31, 36] can all be obtained
from Theorem 1.1 together with appropriate constructions for the graph G. Similarly, the
result of Donkers and Jansen [19] for F-Minor Deletion parameterized by the distance to
graphs of bounded treewidth can be obtained from Theorem 1.1 for connected families F.
We explain this construction in much more detail later in Section 6.
    However, as mentioned earlier, there are lower bounds that cannot be obtained from
Theorem 1.1. The next subsection shows this in detail.


4.2    Having Bounded Minimal Blocking Sets Does Not Suffice
We observe that for the problem Feedback Vertex Set, obtained by setting F = {K3 },
there is a minor-closed graph class G such that Feedback Vertex Set parameterized by
the distance to G does not admit a polynomial kernel (assuming NP ̸⊆ coNP/poly), even
though minimal {K3 }-blocking sets of G are bounded.
   We remark that for F = {K2 }, that is for Vertex Cover, the work of Hols et al. [31,
Theorem 1.2] shows that there is some graph class G with minimal F-blocking sets of size
one such that the problem does not have a polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance
to G, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly. However, the graph class they used is not minor-closed, and
we remind the reader that for Vertex Cover indeed for any minor-closed graph class G
with bounded minimal F-blocking sets there is a polynomial kernel for Vertex Cover
parameterized by the distance to G [6].
   We first introduce the key graphs and graph classes we consider.

▶ Definition 4.2 (Triangle Necklace and Triangle Path). A triangle necklace of length ℓ is a
graph consisting of ℓ pairwise vertex-disjoint triangles T1 , . . . , Tℓ , such that each triangle Ti
has two distinct vertices xi and yi , and there is an edge from yi to xi+1 for each 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ − 1.
The graph class consisting of all triangle necklaces and their minors is denoted as T .
    A triangle path of length ℓ consists of a central path v1 , . . . , vℓ+1 , and for each 1 ≤ i ≤ ℓ
there is a vertex wi adjacent to exactly vi and vi+1 .

   It is easy to see that T has unbounded elimination distance to forests, and therefore
Feedback Vertex Set has no polynomial kernel when parameterizing by the distance
to T [17]. In fact, the triangle necklaces we defined are a specific instance of the necklaces

     Dekker and Jansen [17] consider in their work, and their lower bound is based on their
     properties.
        Next, we define a certain type of edge set of a graph called an obstruction-avoider. These
     are edge sets which are intuitively not relevant for the existence of any obstructions.

     ▶ Definition 4.3 (Obstruction-Avoiders). Let F be a family of graphs, and G be a graph. An
     F-obstruction-avoider of G is an edge set X ⊆ E(G), such that for any S ⊆ V (G) the graph
     G − S is F -minor free if and only if the graph (G − X) − S is F-minor free.

         Now, we give a somewhat general condition in which a blocking set is not minimal
     based on this notion of F-obstruction-avoiders. It is easy to see that, when F only contains
     connected graphs, any minimal F-blocking set of a graph G must lie within a single connected
     component. This simple observation can be generalized to those components that are obtained
     after deleting F-obstruction-avoiders. The next lemma is a generalization of [26, Lemma 13].

     ▶ Lemma 4.4. Let F be a family of connected graphs, G be a graph, and B ⊆ V (G) be
     an F-blocking set. Assume that there exists a set B ′ ⊆ B such that the graph G − B ′
     has an F-obstruction-avoider X, such that there are two distinct connected components of
     (G − B ′ ) − X that contain vertices of B. Then, B is not a minimal F -blocking set.

     Proof. Towards a contradiction, let us assume that the set B fulfilling the conditions of
     the lemma is a minimal F-blocking set. Let b1 , b2 ∈ B \ B ′ be vertices that are in different
     connected components of (G − B ′ ) − X; by the statement of the lemma they exist. Then,
     there is some optimal solution S1 for G that contains B \ {b2 }. Similarly, there is an optimal
     solution S2 for G that contains B \ {b1 }. Let C be the connected component of (G − B ′ ) − X
     that contains b1 . We argue that then, S ⋆ = (S2 \ V (C)) ∪ (S1 ∩ V (C)) is an optimal solution
     that contains all of B.
         Assume that G − S ⋆ is not F-minor free. Then, let S ′ = S ⋆ \ B ′ . Clearly, (G − B ′ ) − S ′
     is not F-minor free. Then, since X is an F-obstruction-avoider of G − B ′ , some H ∈ F
     must be a minor of (G − B ′ − X) − S ′ too. We have that C is a connected component of
     G − B ′ − X, and therefore H is a minor of C − S ′ , or a minor of (G − B ′ − X − C) − S ′ .
     However, we have chosen S1 so that C − S ⋆ = C − S ′ is F-minor free. Similarly, we have
     chosen S2 so that (G − C) − S ⋆ is F-minor free, and (G − B ′ − X − C) − S ′ is a subgraph
     of (G − C) − S ⋆ . Hence, H cannot be a minor of G − S ⋆ .
         Finally, we need to argue that S ⋆ is of size OPTF (G). We first show that S1 ∩ V (C)
     must be an optimal solution for C. Note that S1 is an optimal solution for graph G that
     contains B ′ . Hence, S1′ = S1 \ B ′ is an optimal solution for graph G − B ′ . Since X is an
     F-obstruction-avoider, we have that S1′ is also an optimal solution for G − B ′ − X. It is clear
     that this means that the restriction of S1′ to a connected component of G − B ′ − X yields
     an optimal solution for that connected component. In particular, S1′ ∩ V (C) is an optimal
     solution for C. Since S1′ ∩ V (C) = S1 ∩ V (C), we have that S1 ∩ V (C) is an optimal solution
     for C. Now, it is clear that |S2 ∩ V (C)| = |S1 ∩ V (C)|, and as S2 is also an optimal solution
     for G, we find that |S ⋆ | = |S2 | = OPTF (G). Therefore, S ⋆ is also of minimum size.         ◀

     ▶ Lemma 4.5. For any graph T ∈ T any minimal {K3 }-blocking set of T has size at most 2.

     4.3    Lower Bounds via Extended Minimal Blocking Sets
     Now, we show that, with a slight generalization of the definition of blocking sets, we can
     capture those kernelization lower bounds for biconnected families F that eluded Theorem 1.1.
     The following notion of blocking sets was (somewhat implicitly) defined in the work of Dekker
     and Jansen [17] for the Feedback Vertex Set problem.

     ▶ Definition 1.3 (Extended Blocking Sets). Given a family of connected graphs F and a
     graph G, an extended F-blocking set of G is a pair (X, Y ), where X, Y ⊆ V (G) are two
     disjoint sets, such that there is no set S ⊆ V (G) such that:
     1. |S| = OPTF (G),
     2. G − S is F -minor free,
     3. X ⊆ S, and
A. Ghazy, J. Greilhuber, and T. A. Hartmann                                                       19


4. no two vertices of Y \ S are in the same connected component of G − S.
An extended F-blocking set (X, Y ) is minimal if, for all X ′ ⊊ X the pair (X ′ , Y ) is not an
extended F-blocking set, and for all Y ′ ⊊ Y the pair (X, Y ′ ) is not an extended F-blocking
set.
     A graph class G has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets if there is some constant
c such that for each graph G ∈ G and each minimal extended F-blocking set (X, Y ) of G
we have |X ∪ Y | ≤ c. If no such constant c exists, then G has unbounded minimal extended
F-blocking sets.

    We show that we can also obtain kernelization lower bounds from this notion of blocking
sets when some additional conditions apply.
    The lower bound always applies if all graphs in F are biconnected. In addition, we consider
specific cases of non-biconnected families in which it also applies. In these non-biconnected
cases, we need to make further assumptions about the graph with a large minimal extended
F-blocking set.

▶ Definition 1.5 (Good Minimal Extended F-Blocking Sets). Let F be a family of connected
graphs, and H a graph with a minimal extended F-blocking set (X, Y ). Moreover, let L be
                                                        S
the set of leaf blocks of the graphs of F; that is, L = F ∈F LeafBlocks(F ). If Y = ∅, then
(X, Y ) is a good minimal extended F -blocking set of H.
    Otherwise, if Y ̸= ∅, then (X, Y ) is a good minimal extended F-blocking set of H if each
graph of L contains at least three vertices, and for each v ∈ X there exists a set Sv ⊆ V (H)
such that:
(1) |Sv | = OPTF (H),
(2) H − Sv is F-minor free,
(3) X \ {v} ⊆ Sv ,
(4) no two vertices of Y \ Sv are in the same connected component of H − Sv ,
(5) for each y ∈ Y \ Sv the connected component of H − Sv containing y is L-minor free.

    Definition 1.5 is inspired by the lower bound of Donkers and Jansen [19], in which leaf
blocks were also central to arguing about the non-existence of minors. We can observe that
Definition 1.5 automatically applies to biconnected families F on at least three vertices,
yielding the following observation.

▶ Observation 4.8. For all families F such that each graph of F is biconnected on at least
three vertices, and any graph H with minimal extended F -blocking set (X, Y ), we have that
(X, Y ) is good.

     4.4    On the Assumption That (X, Y) Is Good
     One might wonder whether the lower bound given in Theorem 1.6 can be obtained when
     dropping the assumption that (X, Y ) is a good minimal extended F-blocking set. That is,
     can we get a similar theorem when we drop the “good” qualification? While this might seem
     reasonable at first, it becomes clear that a proof similar to the one used for Theorem 1.6
     should not work without any further assumptions. Indeed, having edges between vertices
     in the universe gadget and the set Y can cause problems for specific families F. In fact,
     using an unpublished result [personal communication with Bougeret, Brandwein, and Sau,
     June 2026], we can prove that one cannot just drop this assumption under the standard
     complexity assumptions.
         Concretely, upcoming work by Bougeret, Brandwein, and Sau will show that {K1,3 }-
     Minor Deletion has a polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance to a caterpillar
     forest. A caterpillar is a graph consisting of a path to which degree-one vertices are attached
     by an edge, and a caterpillar forest is a forest in which each connected component is a
     caterpillar. Note that caterpillar forests are exactly the graphs with pathwidth at most
     1 [3, Lemma 2.4]. We show that the class of caterpillar forests has unbounded minimal
     extended {K1,3 }-blocking sets, which, together with the aforementioned upcoming result,
     shows that one cannot in general obtain lower bounds for F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G
     just from the assumption that G is minor-closed, closed under disjoint union, and has
     unbounded minimal extended F -blocking sets.

 5     The Dichotomy for Biconnected Families and Minimal Extended
       Blocking Sets
In this section, we explain that the known dichotomies for biconnected families F and
minor-closed G can also be framed in terms of whether G has unbounded minimal extended
F-blocking sets. That is, we establish Theorem 1.7.
▶ Theorem 1.7. Let F be a family of biconnected graphs containing at least one planar
graph, and G a minor-closed graph class. Assume NP ̸⊆ coNP/poly. Then, F-Minor
Deletion/ dist-to-G admits a polynomial kernel if and only if G has bounded minimal
extended F-blocking sets.
   This characterization follows relatively directly from the literature, but since it has not
previously been stated explicitly, we consider it worthwhile to record it here.

Vertex cover. For the case F = {K2 }, that is for Vertex Cover, and minor-closed G,
it is already known that F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G has a polynomial kernel if and
only if G has bounded minimal F-blocking sets [6]. From this, we can easily obtain that
F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G has a polynomial kernel if and only if G has bounded
minimal extended F-blocking sets. Essentially, the notion of extended F-blocking sets
collapses back to the basic notion of F-blocking sets for Vertex Cover.

extended F-blocking sets.

be F-minor free. Hence, OPTF (G) = t.
     Now, we show that (X, Y ) is an extended F-blocking set. Observe that any set S ⊆ V (G)
of size OPTF (G) that contains all of X must be identical to X. However, G − X has a path
from ℓ1 to rt . Indeed, for all i ∈ [t] neither vertex ri nor vertex ℓi is part of X. So, since
each graph of F is biconnected on at least three vertices, for each i ∈ [t] there is a path from
ℓi to ri using only vertices of Vi \ {zi }. This means that in G − S there is a path from ℓ1 to
r1 , from r1 to ℓ2 , from ℓ2 to r2 , and so forth, until vertex rt is reached.
     Next, observe that it follows from our previous arguments that X is itself an optimal
solution. Therefore, each pair (X, Y ′ ) where Y ′ ⊊ Y is not an extended F-blocking set.
     Finally, we need to show that for all sets X ′ ⊊ X the pair (X ′ , Y ) is not an extended
F-blocking set. Let zi be one of the vertices in X \ X ′ . We set S = X \ {zi } ∪ {ℓi }. Since S
selects one vertex from each set Vi , the graph G − S is F-minor free. Moreover, there is no
path from ℓ1 to rt in G − S, since G − {ℓi } either disconnects the vertices ℓ1 and rt , or i = 1
and therefore S contains ℓ1 .
     Hence, (X, Y ) is a minimal extended F-blocking set, and by Observation 4.8 it is also
good.                                                                                          ◀

    This will allow us to establish one direction of Theorem 1.7. For the second direction,
we explain that if G has bounded elimination distance to the graph class of F-minor free
graphs, then it has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets. Interestingly, merely having
a bound on the size of (extended) minimal F-blocking sets seems to be difficult to exploit
algorithmically for general F-Minor Deletion problems. The known state-of-the-art
approaches instead use a different notion (which in fact can also be seen as a type of blocking
set), based on the notion of labeled minors [4, 33]. Let us now define the relevant notions for
these approaches.

▶ Definition 5.4 (Labeled Minors). Let Z be a set. A Z-labeled graph G is a graph where
each vertex v is assigned a set of labels Xv ⊆ Z. A Z-labeled graph H is a labeled minor of a
labeled graph G if H can be obtained from G by repeatedly applying the following operations:
 1. deleting a vertex,
 2. deleting an edge,
 3. contracting an edge uv, where the label set of the vertex z obtained by the contraction is
    Xz = Xu ∪ Xv ,
 4. deleting a label from the label set of a vertex.

   Given a set Q of Z-labeled graphs, and an integer s, the set Q is s-saturated if Q contains
the graph on a single vertex with label set Z ′ for each Z ′ ⊆ Z of size exactly s. Now, we
present the “main lemma” used to obtain polynomial kernels in this setting.

▶ Lemma 5.5 ([4, Lemma 6]). Let F be a nonempty finite set of connected graphs. Let Q be
a set of connected Y -labeled graphs that is minF ∈F |V (F )|-saturated, such that each graph
of Q is on at most maxH∈F |E(H)| + 1 vertices. Moreover, let G be a Y -labeled graph with
elimination distance η to the class of F -minor free graphs.
32   Exploring Minimal Blocking Sets for Kernelization Lower Bounds


         Assume that, for each minimum solution S to F-Minor Deletion on the unlabeled
     graph underlying G, we have that G − S has a graph of Q as a labeled minor. Then, there
     exists a set Q∗ ⊆ Q, whose size only depends on F and η, such that for each minimum
     solution S to F-Minor Deletion on the unlabeled graph underlying G we have that G − S
     has a graph of Q∗ as a labeled minor.

         As already pointed out by Bougeret et al. [4], this set Q∗ can also be seen as a type of
     bounded-size blocking set. Towards completing the proof of Theorem 1.7, we show the not
     necessarily surprising result that a bound on the blocking sets in the sense of Lemma 5.5
     yields a bound on the size of minimal extended F-blocking sets.

     ▶ Lemma 5.6. Let F be a nonempty finite family of connected graphs, and G be a minor-
     closed graph class that has elimination distance at most η to being F-minor free. Then, there
     is a constant c depending only on η, F such that for any G ∈ G and any minimal extended
     F-blocking set (X, Y ) of G we have |X ∪ Y | ≤ c.

     Proof. Assume the contrary. Let α ≥ 1 be the constant from Lemma 5.5 that bounds the
     size of the set Q∗ . Note that α is indeed a constant since both η and F are fixed. By
     assumption, there is some G′ ∈ G such that G′ has a minimal extended F-blocking set (X, Y )
     where |X ∪ Y | > 3α + 1.
         If F contains K1 , then it is easy to see that there cannot be a minimal extended F-
     blocking set of the described size, so we shall assume that each graph of F contains at least
     two vertices.
         We create a labeled graph G from G′ by assigning the label set Xv = {v} to each
     v ∈ X ∪ Y , and the label set Xv = ∅ to each v ∈ V (G) \ (X ∪ Y ). Create a family Q0 that
     contains, for each v ∈ X, a graph on a single vertex labeled {v}, and for each pair of two
     distinct vertices u, v ∈ Y a graph on a single edge, with one vertex labeled {u} and the other
     vertex labeled {v}.
         Let Q be a copy of Q0 at this point. Then, for each subset Z of X∪Y of size minF ∈F |V (F )|,
     add a graph on a single vertex with label set Z to Q; this ensures that Q is minF ∈F |V (F )|-
     saturated.
         Let us observe that the set Q and the graph G now fulfill the conditions of Lemma 5.5.
     Indeed, because (X, Y ) is an extended F-blocking set, no optimal solution S for G′ selects
     all of X and disconnects each pair of vertices of Y . It is clear that in either case, this means
     that G − S has a labeled minor of Q0 , namely either the minor we added for the unselected
     vertex of X, or the minor we added for the non-disconnected pair of vertices of Y . Hence,
     from Lemma 5.5 we obtain a set Q∗ ⊆ Q of size at most α, such that for any optimal solution
     S of graph G′ we have that G − S has a graph of Q∗ as a labeled minor.
         We will conclude the proof by showing that this contradicts that (X, Y ) is a minimal
     extended F-blocking set.
         First, consider that |X| > α. Then, there is some H ∈ Q0 \ Q∗ that is a single labeled
     vertex. Let the label set of the vertex of H be {v}. We have v ∈ X. Since (X, Y ) is a minimal
     extended F-blocking set of G′ , we have that (X \ {v}, Y ) is not an extended F-blocking set
     of G′ . So, there is some set S ⊆ V (G) such that G − S is F-minor free, |S| = OPTF (G), and
     S contains all of X \ {v} and disconnects all of Y . Then G − S has a graph of Q∗ as a labeled
     minor. Clearly, G − S cannot have a graph of Q0 \ {H} as a labeled minor. Therefore, G − S
     must have some graph of Q \ Q0 as a labeled minor. Now, any connected labeled minor
     of G − S can use at most two labels: indeed, the only vertices of G that have labels are
     those of X ∪ Y , and the only vertex of X present in G − S is v. Moreover, the vertices of Y
     are disconnected in G − S. Hence, all connected labeled minors of G − S can use an empty
A. Ghazy, J. Greilhuber, and T. A. Hartmann                                                        33


label set, or a label set that contains a single vertex of Y ∪ {v}, or a label set that contains
vertex v together with a single vertex of Y . If minF ∈F |V (F )| ≥ 3, we are done since then
no graph of Q \ Q0 , and therefore also no graph of Q∗ , can be a labeled minor of G − S. So,
let us consider that minF ∈F |V (F )| ≤ 2. Then, obviously G − S is an independent set (or
the empty graph), and cannot contain any graph of Q \ Q0 as a labeled minor either. This
contradicts the properties of Q∗ .
    Now, consider that |X| ≤ α. Since |X ∪ Y | > 3α + 1, this implies |Y | ≥ 2α + 1. The
set Q∗ can contain at most α labeled graphs, and clearly each labeled graph of Q∗ is a
single labeled vertex, or a single labeled edge. Each labeled edge in Q∗ touches 2 vertices of
Y . Therefore, there exists some vertex u ∈ Y such that Q∗ contains no graph on a single
edge that has u in its label set. We can use arguments essentially analogous to those in the
previous case. Observe that (X, Y \ {u}) is not an extended F-blocking set, and define an
optimal solution S that selects all of X, and disconnects all of Y \ {u}. The only vertices of
G − S that have labels are those of Y . Now, notice that at most one of Y \ {u} may have a
path to u in G − S. Otherwise, two vertices of Y \ ({u} ∪ S) would remain connected through
u in G − S. Therefore, we again find that any connected labeled minor of G − S uses a label
set containing at most two vertices. Hence, if minF ∈F |V (F )| ≥ 3, no graph of Q∗ \ Q0 is
a labeled minor of G − S; if minF ∈F |V (F )| ≤ 2 then G − S is an independent set (or the
empty graph), and cannot contain a graph of Q \ Q0 as a labeled minor either. But, by the
properties of Q∗ , G − S must have some graph of Q∗ as a labeled minor, and hence a graph
of Q0 is a labeled minor of G − S. Clearly, no graph of Q0 that is a single labeled vertex can
be a labeled minor of G − S, as all such vertices are part of X and thus of S. Hence, the
labeled minor is a graph on a single edge with two labeled vertices of Y . However, because
G − S disconnects Y \ {u}, this means the labeled minor must have a vertex labeled {u}.
This contradicts our choice of u. Therefore, G − S cannot have a graph of Q∗ as a labeled
minor, contradicting the properties of Q∗ and concluding the proof.                           ◀

    Now, we are ready to prove Theorem 1.7. As a quick remark, let us mention the same
dichotomy remains valid if boundedness is only tested for minimal extended F-blocking sets
(X, Y ) with |Y | ≤ 2. In fact, the respective dichotomy could be proved without Lemma 5.6.
We allow arbitrary sets Y as in the literature precedent of Dekker and Jansen [17, Lemma 1].

▶ Theorem 1.7. Let F be a family of biconnected graphs containing at least one planar
graph, and G a minor-closed graph class. Assume NP ̸⊆ coNP/poly. Then, F-Minor
Deletion/ dist-to-G admits a polynomial kernel if and only if G has bounded minimal
extended F-blocking sets.

Proof. We can assume that F is minor-minimal and finite.
    For F = {K1 }, the statement is correct since in this case making a graph F-minor free
requires us to delete all vertices of the input graph, and thus F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G
can be solved in polynomial time. Since G is minor-closed, we can also check if M is a
modulator to G in polynomial time by using an appropriate minor-testing algorithm [40].
Hence, there is a polynomial kernel. Similarly, G cannot have unbounded minimal extended
F-blocking sets since all graphs have a single unique set that makes the graph F-minor free.
    Next, consider that F = {K2 }. In this case, Bougeret et al. [6, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
explicitly show that F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G has a polynomial kernel if and only if
G has bounded minimal F-blocking sets. By Lemma 5.1 G has bounded minimal F-blocking
sets if and only if G has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets, concluding the proof in
this case.
34   Exploring Minimal Blocking Sets for Kernelization Lower Bounds


         Now, any family F that contains K1 or K2 collapses to one of the two previous cases
     when making it minor-minimal. Hence, for the rest of the proof assume that each graph
     of F has at least three vertices. First, consider that G has bounded elimination distance
     to the class of F-minor free graphs. Then, by [4, Theorem 1], and the fact that F-Minor
     Deletion admits a polynomial kernel parameterized by the solution size when F contains
     a planar graph [22, 29], there is a polynomial kernel for F-Minor Deletion/dist-to-G.
     Moreover, by Lemma 5.6, G has bounded minimal extended F-blocking sets.
         Now, consider that G has unbounded elimination distance to the class of F-minor free
     graphs. Then, by Lemma 5.2, G contains an F-necklace Nℓ of length ℓ for all ℓ ≥ 1. By
     Lemma 5.3 the necklace Nℓ has a good minimal extended F-blocking set (X, Y ) with |X| = ℓ
     and |Y | = 2. Therefore, G has unbounded minimal extended F-blocking sets. Moreover,
     there is no polynomial kernel by the arguments of [17, Theorem 2].                     ◀


         6    Parameterizing by the Distance to Bounded-Pathwidth Graphs
     In this section, we exhibit new kernelization lower bounds that can be obtained using our
     framework. The parameter of interest is the deletion distance to graphs of bounded pathwidth.
     Recall that the graph class Gpw≤η contains exactly the graphs with pathwidth at most η.
         Although our overall result will be slightly stronger, let us mention two important
     corollaries of it here first. Recall that Pw-η Deletion is F-Minor Deletion where F
     consists of all minor-minimal graphs with pathwidth η + 1.

     ▶ Corollary 6.1. Let η ≥ 1 be an integer. Then, Pw-η Deletion/ dist-to-Gpw≤η+1 admits
     no polynomial kernel, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

         Note that Pw-η Deletion when η = 0 is the same as Vertex Cover, and it is known
     that Vertex Cover admits no polynomial kernel when parameterized by the distance
     to Gpw≤2 [15], unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly. On the other hand, when parameterizing by the
     distance to Gpw≤1 , Vertex Cover does admit a polynomial kernel [32]. Moreover, Pw-η
     Deletion parameterized by the distance to Gpw≤η admits a polynomial kernel [22] since
     the corresponding family F contains a planar graph, and Gpw≤η is F-minor free.8 Hence,
     Corollary 6.1 together with these results from the literature yields a tight dichotomy for the
     problem, which we state in Theorem 1.8.

     ▶ Theorem 1.8. When η = 0, Pw-η Deletion admits a polynomial kernel when parameter-
     ized by the distance to Gpw≤1 , but no polynomial kernel when parameterized by the distance
     to Gpw≤2 , unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.
         When η ≥ 1, Pw-η Deletion admits a polynomial kernel when parameterized by the
     distance to Gpw≤η , but no polynomial kernel when parameterized by the distance to Gpw≤η+1 ,
     unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

         Interestingly, exactly the same dichotomy where pathwidth is replaced with treewidth for
     the Tw-η-Deletion problem has been previously established when combining the lower
     bound of Cygan et al. [15] with the known upper bounds [22, 32]. Moreover, we obtain the
     following lower bound result for all connected families F containing graphs with at least two
     vertices each.

▶ Theorem 1.9. Let F be a nonempty minor-minimal family of connected graphs on

    For our proof, we begin by considering the case that F = {H} for a single non-biconnected

obtain lower bounds for connected families F in Section 6.3.

that are not connected. Donkers and Jansen [19] are able to handle disconnected families F in
which every graph of F contains a connected component on at least three vertices. However,
this requires some additional nontrivial arguments, and as we could not use Theorem 1.6 for
this scenario, the proof would not decompose as nicely. Therefore, we stick to connected
families in this work.

6.1    Families Containing a Single Non-Biconnected Graph
Let us first consider the case that F = {H} for a single graph H that is not biconnected.
In this case, we can show that there is no polynomial kernel parameterized by the distance
to graphs with pathwidth pw(H) + 1. In fact, in some cases there is not even a polynomial
kernel parameterized by the distance to graphs with pathwidth pw(H).
    To show this, we provide a minor adaptation of a construction due to Donkers and
Jansen [19] and give a tight analysis of the pathwidth of the construction. First, let us restate
their result.

▶ Theorem 6.2 (See [19, Theorem 1]). Let F be a finite set of graphs, such that each graph
in F has a connected component on at least three vertices. Let G be the class of graphs
with treewidth at most minF ∈F tw(F ). Then, F-Minor Deletion/ dist-to-G admits no
polynomial kernel, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

    Although Donkers and Jansen do not consider the distance to graphs with bounded
pathwidth as their parameter, the linear nature of their construction makes it a prime
candidate for also showing lower bounds for this parameter. First, we introduce some
definitions we will need for the analysis of their construction.

▶ Definition 2.4 (Pathwidth Critical). Given a graph G, a vertex v ∈ V (G) is pathwidth
critical if pw(G) > pw(G − v).

▶ Definition 2.5 (Tidy Leaf Blocks). Let G be a connected graph that is not biconnected.

L of G such that there is a path decomposition X = X1 , . . . , Xt of L of width at most pw(G),
where X1 = {c} and Xt = {b}, where c is the cut vertex of G contained in L, and b ̸= c is a
vertex of L that is pathwidth critical for G.

6.3     Families Containing Multiple Graphs
After having Lemmas 2.3 and 2.7 at hand, we want to extend these results to families F
containing multiple graphs that are not necessarily biconnected. For the result of Donkers and
Jansen [19], they show that one can choose a graph H ∈ F with tw(H) = minF ∈F tw(F ) such
that the construction for the single graph H essentially works for the full family. However,
because the construction of Lemma 2.7 for a graph H can have pathwidth pw(H) + 1, we
cannot get the same exact result here with treewidth replaced with pathwidth. Nevertheless,
we obtain the following Lemma.

▶ Lemma 6.15. Let F be a minor-minimal family of connected graphs on at least three
vertices each, and H be a graph of F that has a smallest leaf block among all graphs of F.
Moreover, let ℓ ≥ 1 be an arbitrary integer.
    If H is biconnected, then there is a graph Gℓ with a good minimal extended F-blocking
set (X, Y ) with |X| = ℓ and |Y | = 2, and pw(Gℓ ) = pw(H).
    If H is not biconnected, then there is a graph Gℓ with a minimal F-blocking set of size ℓ.
Moreover, the pathwidth of Gℓ is at most pw(H) + 1, and even at most pw(H) if one of the
smallest leaf blocks of H is tidy.

Proof. We use a different construction depending on whether H is biconnected.

H is biconnected. If H is biconnected, Gℓ is the graph GH  ℓ we obtain by applying Lemma 2.3
with family {H}. Then, Gℓ has a good minimal extended {H}-blocking set (X, Y ) with
|X| = ℓ and |Y | = 2. We need to argue that (X, Y ) is also a good minimal extended
F-blocking set.
    Consider an arbitrary pair (X ′ , Y ′ ) with X ′ ⊆ X and Y ′ ⊆ Y , such that at least one of
the two inclusions is proper. Since (X, Y ) is a minimal extended {H}-blocking set of Gℓ ,
there is a set S such that Gℓ − S is {H}-minor free, |S| = OPT{H} (Gℓ ), S contains all vertices
of X ′ , and no two vertices of Y ′ are connected in Gℓ − S. Moreover, by Lemma 2.3, each
biconnected component of Gℓ − S contains fewer than |V (H)| vertices. Let L be the set of
leaf blocks of graphs in F. Then, since H is biconnected and has the smallest leaf block of
44   Exploring Minimal Blocking Sets for Kernelization Lower Bounds


     any graph of F, we know that Gℓ − S is L-minor free. Clearly, this implies that Gℓ − S is
     F-minor free. Hence, we have that OPT{H} (Gℓ ) = OPTF (Gℓ ).
         Now, observe that since (X, Y ) is a minimal extended {H}-blocking set, any set S such
     that Gℓ − S is F-minor free that contains X and disconnects the vertices Y \ S has size
     larger than OPT{H} (Gℓ ) = OPTF (Gℓ ). Hence, (X, Y ) is an extended F-blocking set. By the
     existence of the solution described in the previous paragraph, it is also a good minimal
     extended F-blocking set. The pathwidth bound also follows from Lemma 2.3.

     H is not biconnected. Next, consider that H is not biconnected, and let L be a smallest
     leaf block of H, where L is a tidy leaf block if possible. In this case, we use the construction
     of Lemma 2.7. That is, Gℓ = G⋆ℓ of Lemma 2.7 used with graph H.
         The pathwidth bound is explicitly stated in the lemma. So, it remains to show that Gℓ
     has a minimal F-blocking set of size ℓ.
         By Lemma 2.7, Gℓ has a minimal {H}-blocking set X of size ℓ. Moreover, for each v ∈ X
     there is a set Sv of size OPT{H} (Gℓ ) containing X \ {v}, such that Gℓ − Sv is {H}-minor
     free, and each connected component of |V (L)|-prune(Gℓ − Sv ) is a minor of H. Consider
     an arbitrary H ′ ∈ F, and assume that H ′ is a minor of Gℓ − Sv . By our choice of H, each
     leaf block of H ′ has at least |V (L)| vertices. Now, by an iterated application of Lemma 4.18
     we find that H ′ must be a minor of a connected component of |V (L)|-prune(Gℓ − Sv ): leaf
     blocks of Gℓ − Sv that contain fewer than |V (L)| vertices clearly do not have any leaf block
     of F as a minor. Since each connected component of |V (L)|-prune(Gℓ − Sv ) is a minor of H,
     we must have that H ′ is a minor of H. If H ′ ̸= H, this contradicts that F is minor-minimal.
     If H ′ = H, this contradicts that Gℓ − Sv is {H}-minor free. Hence, we have reached a
     contradiction, and we obtain that Gℓ − Sv is F-minor free. In particular, the argument shows
     that OPTF (Gℓ ) = OPT{H} (Gℓ ).
         We also know that X is a {H}-blocking set of Gℓ . Hence, there is no set S ⊆ V (Gℓ ) of
     size OPT{H} (Gℓ ) such that Gℓ − S is F-minor free and X ⊆ S. From the arguments above,
     we now obtain that X is a minimal F-blocking set.                                              ◀

        Let us now extract two interesting corollaries from Lemma 6.15 and the fact that for
     F = {K2 } the problem F-Minor Deletion has no polynomial kernel when parameterized
     by the distance to graphs with pathwidth at most 2 [15].

     ▶ Theorem 1.9. Let F be a nonempty minor-minimal family of connected graphs on
     at least two vertices each, and set η = maxF ∈F pw(F ) + 1. Then, F-Minor Dele-
     tion/ dist-to-Gpw≤η admits no polynomial kernel, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

     Proof. If K2 ∈ F, then the minor-minimality of F implies F = {K2 }, and the result follows
     from [15]. Otherwise, the statement follows directly from Lemma 6.15 and Theorem 1.6. ◀

         Now, for a second corollary, and in fact the main motivation for the considered parame-
     terization, we consider the Pw-η Deletion/dist-to-Gpw≤η+1 problem.

     ▶ Corollary 6.1. Let η ≥ 1 be an integer. Then, Pw-η Deletion/ dist-to-Gpw≤η+1 admits
     no polynomial kernel, unless NP ⊆ coNP/poly.

     Proof. Recall that Pw-η Deletion is an F-Minor Deletion problem, where F contains
     all minor-minimal graphs with pathwidth η + 1. Moreover, it is well-known that F contains
     a tree (start from a tree with pathwidth > η and contract edges until it becomes a minor-
     minimal graph with pathwidth η + 1). Hence, there is a graph H with a smallest leaf block
     of F that is a tree, and this leaf block L is isomorphic to P2 . Moreover, it is clear that all
A. Ghazy, J. Greilhuber, and T. A. Hartmann                                                    45


vertices of H are pathwidth critical, and that L is a tidy leaf block. Hence, Lemma 6.15
provides, for each integer ℓ ≥ 1, a graph Gℓ with a minimal F-blocking set of size ℓ, such
that pw(Gℓ ) ≤ pw(H) = η + 1. Then, the result follows from Theorem 1.1.                 ◀
