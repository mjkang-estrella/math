                                                                                         Abstract
                                                     Let P be a d-dimensional lattice polytope with Ehrhart polynomial LP (t). Motivated
                                                 by the study of Ehrhart positivity and magic positivity, we investigate the Taylor
                                                 coefficients Aj (P ; k) in the shifted expansion LP (t) = dj=0 Aj (P ; k)(t − k)j about a
                                                                                                              P

                                                 real center k. In this paper, we obtain the following four main results.
                                                     (i) We give exact formulas for these coefficients in terms of the ordinary Ehrhart
                                                 coefficients, the h∗ -vector, elementary symmetric functions, and Stirling numbers.
                                                     (ii) We denote by τ (P ) and τ + (P ) the smallest nonnegative integral centers at which
                                                 all Taylor coefficients are nonnegative and positive, respectively. If s is the degree
                                                 of the h∗ -polynomial, then 0 ≤ τ (P ) ≤ τ + (P ) ≤ min{max{0, s − 1}, ⌊ d−1   2 ⌋}. As an
                                                 application, we slightly improve an upper bound due to Beck, De Loera, Develin, Pfeifle,
                                                 and Stanley. That is, every real root of LP (t) lies in [−d, ⌊ d−1
                                                                                                                  2 ⌋).
                                                     (iii) Let ρ(P ) be the smallest nonnegative real center such that the Taylor coefficients
                                                 are nonnegative. If λR (f ) denotes the largest real zero of f (t), with value −∞ when no
                                                                                                          (j)

1     Introduction
1.1       Basic concepts
A lattice polytope is the convex hull of finitely many points of Zd . Throughout this paper,
we assume that d ≥ 1. Let P ⊂ Rd be a full-dimensional lattice polytope. For t ∈ Z≥0 , its
lattice-point enumerator
                                      LP (t) = |tP ∩ Zd |
counts the number of integer points in the t-th dilation tP = {tα : α ∈ P } of P .

    For a lattice polytope P , let relint(P ) denote its relative interior. The reciprocity theorem

1.2    Motivation
In the ordinary monomial basis, one writes LP (t) = cd td + · · · + c1 t + c0 . It is well known [6,
Corollary 3.20; Theorem 5.6] that cd equals the volume of P , cd−1 equals half of the boundary
volume of P (i.e., half the sum of the relative volumes of its facets), and the constant term is
c0 = 1. Therefore, these three coefficients are always positive.
    The remaining coefficients, however, are more intricate and lack a straightforward geo-
metric interpretation [40]. We refer to the coefficients ci for 1 ≤ i ≤ d − 2 as the middle
Ehrhart coefficients of P . A lattice polytope P is said to be Ehrhart positive if ci > 0 for all
i. Reeve’s tetrahedra provide the classical non-Ehrhart positive examples [43].
    The study of Ehrhart positivity has attracted considerable attention in recent years.
Numerous families of polytopes have been established as Ehrhart positive, including lattice
path matroids [23] (which encompass the hypersimplices [18], minimal matroids [19], Schubert
matroids [17], Catalan matroids [14]), rank-two matroids [21], cross-polytopes [33, Sec-
tion 2], the y-families of generalized permutohedra [41] (which encompass the Pitman–Stanley
polytopes [50]), and cyclic polytopes [32].
    Conversely, non-Ehrhart positive examples have been found among order polytopes [36, 37],
matroid polytopes [20], and smooth polytopes [12]. More non-Ehrhart-positive polytopes are
constructed in [26, 34, 35] See [33] for a survey of Ehrhart positivity.
    A stronger property than Ehrhart positivity is magic positivity, defined as follows.

   Clearly, the magic positivity of a lattice polytope implies the Ehrhart positivity of the

on the magic positivity of polytopes are found in [3, 23, 27, 30, 38]. Many polytopes that are
Ehrhart positive but not magic positive can also be found in [3, 22, 38].

We call the coefficients Aj (P ; k) the Taylor coefficients of the polytope P . We also refer to k
as the expansion center. We say that P is Taylor nonnegative at k if Aj (P ; k) ≥ 0 for every
j, and Taylor positive at k if Aj (P ; k) > 0 for every j.

   The terminology records that Taylor positivity at 0 is precisely Ehrhart positivity.
   Another motivation for introducing the relevant concepts in Definition 1.2 is the following
open problem listed on the website of the American Institute of Mathematics [1].

Problem 1.3. ([1, Problem 1.6]) Let P be a lattice polytope. Fix k ∈ Z>0 and write LP (t)
in the basis {(t − k)i }i . For example, if deg(h∗P (z)) = s, then LP (t) is nonnegative in the basis
{(t − s + 1)i }di=0 . Investigate what happens when changing k from 0 to 1 for a non-Ehrhart
positive polytope (or other values of k).

We derive bounds and exact values for several natural classes, and we analyze explicit non-

span. This convention is particularly important for matroid base polytopes, which lie in

8.3     Smooth polytopes

d incident edges form a lattice basis. Smoothness is preserved by integral dilations. It is also
preserved by Cartesian products: at a vertex (v, w) of P × Q, the primitive edge directions
are
                             (u1 , 0), . . . , (up , 0), (0, z1 ), . . . , (0, zq ),
where the ui and zj are lattice bases at v and w; their union is a lattice basis of the product

9     Concluding remarks
We introduced the Taylor coefficients Aj (P ; k) of lattice polytopes, derived explicit formulas for
them, determined sharp universal bounds for their least nonnegative centers, and established
several structural properties. In particular, these results provide a systematic partial answer
to Problem 1.3.

                                                37
    Several classification problems remain open. It would be useful to characterize natural
classes for which ρ(P ) = 0, especially within the families of matroid base polytopes and
smooth polytopes. One may also ask for a structural description of the polytopes attaining
τ (P ) = ⌊(d − 1)/2⌋, beyond the generalized Reeve constructions and their pyramids.
