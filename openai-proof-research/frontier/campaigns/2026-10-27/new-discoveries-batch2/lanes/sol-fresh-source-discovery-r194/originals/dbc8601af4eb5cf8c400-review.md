## Reviewer reconstruction of the proof

### 1. The integral lattice and the actual generators

This checks candidate §1, especially line 28. Let $M_{\mathbb Z}^{\rho}$ have the row tabloids as its integral basis. A tabloid specifies disjoint sets $T_1,\ldots,T_m$, of sizes $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_m$, partitioning $B$. Send it to the word which has letter $k$ exactly on $T_k$. Conversely, a word of content $\rho$ specifies those sets. This is a bijection of bases, so an integral isomorphism onto the content-$\rho$ coordinate sublattice of $T=E^{\otimes B}$. Moving an entry from position $x$ to $g x$ carries its row letter with it; this is exactly composition with $g^{-1}$. Thus the map is equivariant over $\mathbb Z$.

In tableau $t_R$, the entry named $(i,j)$ occupies cell $(j,i)$. Transposition preserves the triangular diagram, and column $i$ consists of the entries $(i,1),\ldots,(i,m+1-i)$, in that order. Its row word is $j$. For $t_C$, entry $(i,j)$ occupies cell $(i,j)$, column $j$ has the prescribed top-to-bottom order, and its row word is $i$. Therefore the two column antisymmetrizations are exactly $v_R$ and $v_C$.

More explicitly, permuting the positions of an ordered column by $\sigma$ changes its initially increasing letter assignment to $\sigma^{-1}$. The coefficient in the polytabloid is $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma^{-1})$. Relabeling that sum produces precisely the displayed definition of `alt`, with no normalization factor. Tensor regrouping has no graded signs.

Every tableau of this fixed shape is obtained from either fixed tableau by a unique relabeling of its entries in $G$. Moreover $g e_t=e_{g t}$, since its column subgroup is conjugated by $g$ and sign is conjugation invariant. Hence the integral orbit span of either vector is the same lattice: the span of all polytabloids in $M_{\mathbb Z}^{\rho}$. This establishes equality of the two actual embedded lattices, not only an abstract complex isomorphism. Position transpose also sends $v_R$ to $v_C$ with scalar $+1$.

The subgroup $S_{\mathbb Z}^{\rho}\subset T$ is finite free because it is a subgroup of a finite free abelian group. To justify the cube inclusion without any saturation assumption, tensor the injection $S\hookrightarrow T$ successively with $S\otimes S$, $T\otimes S$, and $T\otimes T$. Each of these tensor factors is free, hence flat, over $\mathbb Z$. This gives injections

\[
S\otimes S\otimes S\hookrightarrow T\otimes S\otimes S
\hookrightarrow T\otimes T\otimes S\hookrightarrow T\otimes T\otimes T.
\]

Thus $z_m$ really is in the integral Specht cube $L_m$ with the required diagonal action. No use of complex irreducibility, an integral sign intertwiner, or reduction of a complex subspace is involved.

### 3. Every retained monomial is forced

This checks candidate §3, lines 72–100. In the expansion of $z_m$, each row word $a$, each row word $b$, and each column word $c$ corresponds to one choice of the permutations in its disjoint blocks. Thus each triple occurs once with coefficient the product of its signs.

Consider an arbitrary expansion triple which survives both operations. The local filter gives $a=c$ pointwise. Write $d=a+b-1$. The output contains every pair $(r,s)\in D$ once, so its first coordinates contain $r$ exactly $r$ times. Therefore

\[
\sum_{x\in B}d(x)^2=\sum_{r=1}^m r^3.
\]

In a row of length $r$, both $a$ and $b$ use $1,\ldots,r$ once, giving

\[
\sum_{\text{row}}d=2\frac{r(r+1)}2-r=r^2,
\qquad
\sum_{\text{row}}(d-r)^2=\sum_{\text{row}}d^2-r^3.
\]

There is exactly one row of each length $r=1,\ldots,m$. Summing the second identity gives a sum of nonnegative **integer** squares equal to zero. Each square is zero. Thus every cell in the row of length $r$ has $d=r$, and $b=r+1-a$. This is a support statement about each individual retained monomial. It does not merely compute the coefficient of $e_w$, does not rely on cancellation, and makes no positivity assertion inside a ring of positive characteristic.

The common labeling $a=c$ consequently has palette $1,\ldots,m+1-i$ on row $i$, and palette $1,\ldots,m+1-j$ on column $j$. To prove uniqueness, the top row must place its letter $m$ in column 1, since every other column has smaller maximum. Once its first (k-1) positions hold $m,m-1,\ldots,m-k+2$, its letter (m-k+1) cannot occupy an already filled position or any column (j>k), whose maximum is (m+1-j<m-k+1). It must occupy column $k$. This fixes the full top row.

Deleting it removes the largest allowed letter from each column, leaving exactly the row and column palettes of the triangle with parameter (m-1), after decreasing the row index by one. Induction starts with the one cell for $m=1$. The uniquely forced labeling is therefore

\[
a(i,j)=c(i,j)=m+2-i-j,
\qquad b(i,j)=j.
\]

Conversely, these assignments obey all three block palettes. Their output is exactly $w$ and includes every letter of $D$ once. Thus existence, as well as uniqueness, is proved. There is exactly one surviving expansion triple, not a family of triples with a factorial multiplicity.

In the first layer every row is reversed, giving sign $(-1)^H$, where (H=\sum_{r=1}^m\binom r2$. The second layer is increasing on every row, giving (+1). The third layer reverses every column; column lengths are also (m,m-1,\ldots,1), giving the same $(-1)^H$. Their product is (+1). This proves the full vector identity $\pi_Dq^B(z_m)=e_w$ integrally, including all signs and multiplicities. At (m=1), all three factors and both maps have their one-term definitions and the same computation is the identity.

### 4. Splitting, then arbitrary base change

This checks candidate §4, lines 104–108. Equivariance and the just-proved identity give

\[
P_m(gz_m)=J^{-1}(g e_w)=\delta_g
\quad\text{for every }g\in G.
\]

The map $I_m(\delta_g)=gz_m$ is left $\mathbb Z[G]$-linear and satisfies $P_m I_m=1$. It is injective. For each $x\in L_m$,

\[
x=I_mP_mx+(x-I_mP_mx),
\]

where the second term belongs to $\ker P_m$. The intersection of the two summands is zero by applying $P_m$. This proves the integral direct sum and the independence of all (N!) specified translates.

For any commutative unital $A$, functoriality of tensor product preserves the equality of maps:

\[
(1_A\otimes P_m)(1_A\otimes I_m)=1_{A\otimes\mathbb Z[G]}.
\]

There are canonical (A[G])-equivariant identifications

\[
A\otimes\mathbb Z[G]\cong A[G],\qquad
A\otimes(S\otimes_{\mathbb Z}S\otimes_{\mathbb Z}S)
\cong(A\otimes S)\otimes_A(A\otimes S)\otimes_A(A\otimes S).
\]

The image of the basis vector is precisely the reduction/base change of $gz_m$. An identity of split maps is enough; no preservation of an arbitrary injection by tensoring is assumed. In particular $A$ need not be flat over $\mathbb Z$, and the argument does not require an ambient-word embedding after base change, modular semisimplicity, or division by a factorial. The zero ring also satisfies the identity, with both sides the zero module; no exclusion is needed.
