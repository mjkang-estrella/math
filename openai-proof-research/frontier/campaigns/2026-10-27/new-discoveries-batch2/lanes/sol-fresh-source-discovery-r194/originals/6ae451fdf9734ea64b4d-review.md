## Exact result and source

Let a(n) be the minimum cardinality of a nonempty finite edge-connected subset C of Z² such that every finite edge-connected P of cardinality n has a translate contained in C. Then the verified result is

    a(11) ≤ 57 < (11² − 11 + 6)/2 = 58.

The witness is C = {(x,y): |x|+|y|≤5} minus {(±2,±2)}. There is no assertion a(11)=57, no first-failing-index claim, and no asymptotic improvement. Existence of the minimum follows from the eligible n-by-n square and well ordering of natural cardinalities.

## Complete mathematical proof, including finite computation

The diamond has 1+4(1+2+3+4+5)=61 cells; exactly four distinct cells are removed. Connectivity has a direct proof: decrease absolute coordinates toward zero, avoiding the deleted (2,2) absolute-coordinate pattern. At absolute coordinates (3,2) or (2,3), decrease the coordinate equal to 2 first. All retained cells reach an axis and then the origin. My checker additionally verifies literal graph connectivity.

Define N(P)=P−(min_x P,min_y P). This preserves adjacency and cardinality. For finite nonempty sets, N(P)=N(Q) iff P and Q are translates. No coordinate swap, sign change, rotation or reflection enters N. Define G₁={ {(0,0)} }; Gₖ₊₁ contains N(Q∪{v}) for every Q∈Gₖ and every grid-neighbor v outside Q.

Soundness is induction: adding an adjacent new cell preserves connectivity and increases cardinality by one. Completeness is also induction. Given connected P with k+1>1 cells, take a spanning tree of its finite adjacency graph and remove a leaf v. The remaining tree spans P\{v}, so that induced subgraph is connected. Vertex v has a neighbor remaining in the tree. By induction N(P\{v}) is generated; adding the correspondingly translated v and normalizing yields N(P). This argument allows arbitrary cycles and holes. It does not rely on a removable leaf of the *full* adjacency graph, nor on a count from OEIS.

Every normalized k-cell connected shape has width and height at most k: paths in a spanning tree have at most k−1 edges. More strongly w+h≤k+1, since its tree crosses every horizontal and vertical bounding-box cut using at least w−1 and h−1 distinct edges. Thus all intermediate shapes through k=11 fit in an 11-by-11 coordinate square.

I wrote independent_bitboard.py from scratch without importing or rerunning candidate functions. It represents (x,y) as bit 16y+x. For growth it pads a normalized shape by (1,1), takes four integer shifts, excludes occupied cells, adds each boundary bit, and normalizes by its lowest occupied row and column. Before the last growth, width and height are at most 10: padded coordinates and their neighbors lie between 0 and 11, strictly inside a 16-bit row. Hence shifts never wrap to a spurious row or truncate a negative coordinate. Bitwise OR, set-bit iteration and minimum-row/column removal implement exactly the G recursion. Canonicalization is injective on translation classes in this range.
