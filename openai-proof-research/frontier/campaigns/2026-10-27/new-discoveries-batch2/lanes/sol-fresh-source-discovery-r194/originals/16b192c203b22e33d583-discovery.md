## A. Conjecture 3: random cubic synchronization — public exclusion

For uniformly random labelled simple 3-regular G_n, even n tending to infinity, ask whether the probability that every local minimum of E(theta)=sum_edges(1-cos(theta_i-theta_j)) on the phase torus is synchronized tends to one. Do not replace this model by random graph process, dense minimum degree, large regular degree, or one initialized trajectory.

Colbrook's MD-06 Theorem 1 states the opposite limit. Pinned repository revision fb453c637be58f5c30f19a68d5056e17bbc84c7c contains the manuscript and its explicit original-target binding. Read the actual deterministic obstruction Theorem 7 and random-graph assembly, not just its README status. The mechanism builds a small-gradient phase profile on a clean cycle neighborhood, uses a quantitative positive-Hessian certificate to find a nonsynchronized minimum, and combines fixed-length cycle Poisson limits with the random cubic spectral gap. It takes n to infinity first, then the fixed cycle length through multiples of four. This order is essential: probability of a fixed-length cycle is not itself tending to one.

## B. Conjecture 9: Kikuchi spectral trade-off — public exclusion

Fix even tensor order r and level ell>=r/2, take n to infinity first. With independent standard Gaussian Z_S for r-subsets, set M(lambda)_{I,J}=lambda+Z_{I symmetric-difference J} when that difference has size r, otherwise zero. The question concerns the normalized eigenvalue pop-out threshold becoming arbitrarily small as ell tends to infinity after n.

For the intended r>=4 scope, Kothari–Xu, *Smooth Trade-off for Tensor PCA via Sharp Bounds for Kikuchi Matrices*, arXiv:2510.03061v1 (3 October 2025; SODA26), Definition1.3 and Theorems1.8/1.11, is an exact public resolution claim. Their noise norm is O_r(n^(r/4) ell^(r/4+1/2)); the planted all-ones Rayleigh quotient is lambda times degree binom(ell,r/2)binom(n-ell,r/2), of order lambda n^(r/2)ell^(r/2). Thus the resulting threshold has order n^(-r/4)ell^(1/2-r/4), which tends to zero after normalization for r>2. Read the actual trace-moment expansion and even-edge walk representation in Section2.2, and the precise stated level and normalization conventions. This is exclusion based on an already public same-object proof, not an independent full 31-page verification.

The literal source includes even r=2. At r=2 the exponent is zero and the classical matrix BBP threshold stays nonzero; indeed the source itself distinguishes the matrix situation. This domain issue receives no credit and is not repackaged as a substantive refutation. No claim of a new tensor theorem is made.

## C. Open Problem15: arbitrary-field SK Glauber mixing — sustained, unresolved

The source asks whether the canonical Glauber chain for the SK measure with arbitrary external field mixes in polynomial time throughout fixed beta<1, with high probability over disorder. Write J_ij=beta g_ij/sqrt(n), symmetric, zero diagonal, and mu_{J,h}(x) proportional to exp(x^T Jx/2+h^T x). One discrete attempt selects a uniformly random site and resamples from its exact conditional law. The target is worst-start total variation at any fixed accuracy, with beta fixed before n grows, for arbitrary deterministic field sequences h_n. A single high-probability disorder event valid simultaneously for every h would be stronger; the source's short formulation does not explicitly settle that order of quantifiers. No bounded-field, constant-field, random-posterior-field or warm-start restriction can stand in for the stated arbitrary-field question. This packet proves neither interpretation.

### Route1: integrated two-spin curvature

Let m_i(x)=tanh(h_i+sum_j J_ij x_j), v_i=1-m_i^2, d_i f=(f(x^{i,+})-f(x^{i,-}))/2, a_i=v_i d_i f and b_i=m_i v_i d_i f. Wang's actual assembly bounds

 E[a^T T a]+2E[b^T(T circle T)b]
 <= alpha sum_i E[v_i(d_i f)^2],

where T_ij=tanh(J_ij) and alpha=max(||T||,2||T circle T||). The cubic remainder r3(T) vanishes in SK. Here ||T|| tends to 2beta and ||T circle T|| tends to beta^2. Consequently this exact route only supplies margin 1-2beta, vanishing at1/2. Replacing an absolute matrix by a signed one does not remove this top-eigenvalue cost.

A proposed improvement by pointwise estimates has a decisive obstruction. Choose any spin x*=1, set h=-J1, and take f(x)=c^T x with c a top eigenvector of T. At x*, all m_i=0, v_i=1, a=c, b=0. The ratio of the displayed left integrand to sum_i v_i(d_i f)^2 is exactly lambda_max(T). Thus no pointwise alpha<1 bound can hold for all fields when lambda_max(T)>1. This applies to typical SK T for beta>1/2 for the simultaneous-field route. The chosen h depends on J; it is not a counterexample to a fixed deterministic-field theorem. The exact finite check uses three off-diagonal T entries3/5, c=1: a^T T a=18/5, while the proposed upper bound at alpha1 is3. Realize T by J_ij=atanh(3/5). No limiting or numerical diagonalization is needed.

The obstruction does NOT rule out an averaged inequality: x* may carry small Gibbs mass, and a_i are constrained derivatives of a common f. Resolving the target requires exploiting this averaged compatibility, potentially beyond integrated curvature. Simply asserting Gaussian cancellation for a vector depending on the same disorder is invalid. Dobrushin absolute-row influence is also unsuitable: its expected row sum is beta(n-1)sqrt(2/(pi n)), diverging rather than contracting. These are failures of proof routes, not failed SK mixing.

### Route2: localization, exceptional paths and field transfer

The zero-field gap assembly handles every normalized f using a square-tilted observation law, bounds its mass on bad paths, and retains a positive fraction of variance. An arbitrary initial h changes that observation law. Its uniform square-tilted bad-event estimate and compatible directional covariance estimates have not been proved here. Copying the zero-field conclusion loses those hypotheses. A direct bounded-density transfer between mu_{J,0} and mu_{J,h} can cost exp(O(||h||_1)), not polynomial for arbitrary fields, so elementary comparison does not fill this gap.

Nor does a weak Poincare error automatically disappear. On two states with stationary rare mass d, take P(rare,bulk)=a and P(bulk,rare)=ad/(1-d). Detailed balance holds and the gap is a/(1-d). For every f, Var_pi(f)=d(1-d)(f(rare)-f(bulk))^2<=4d||f||_infinity^2. Thus a weak inequality with error4d can be entirely vacuous about an arbitrarily tiny a. With d=2^-n and a=2^-2n the error is much smaller than any inverse polynomial, yet worst-start escape is exponentially slow. This is a complete generic obstruction, not an SK construction, and earns no discovery credit. Annealing starts from a different distribution and cannot be silently replaced by a worst start at the final temperature.

Exact remaining obligation: an all-functions field-uniform functional inequality sufficient for polynomial worst-start mixing throughout each fixed beta<1, or a direct worst-start argument that avoids it, with the initial-field and disorder quantifiers spelled out. Neither is supplied. No SK counterexample was found. The strongest substantive objection is that a successful short extension might already follow from a yet-unread general arbitrary-field theorem; this bounded search does not exclude that possibility.
