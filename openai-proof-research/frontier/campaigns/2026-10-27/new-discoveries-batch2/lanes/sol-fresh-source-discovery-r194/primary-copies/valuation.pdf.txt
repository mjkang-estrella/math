   Valuation trees have been used to study the p-adic valuations of integer sequences; see, for
instance, [6]. Examples include Stirling numbers of the second kind [2], truncated exponential
sums [1], Eulerian numbers [3], and a sequence arising from a rational integral [8]. Givens
and Moll [5] constructed a tree structure for the FiboLaplace sequence that organizes the
congruence classes of indices according to the p-adic valuations of the corresponding terms.
A vertex of such a tree represents a congruence class of indices. It is called terminal when
the valuation is constant on that class and nonterminal when further reﬁnement is required.
An internal branch is called simple when every level contains exactly one nonterminal vertex.
For even indices, Givens and Moll established a lifting criterion that guarantees the simplicity
of an internal branch. They subsequently asked whether every even internal branch must be
simple.
   The ﬁrst objective of this paper is to answer this question. We show that the answer is
negative by giving an explicit counterexample for p = 971. We also observe that the analogous
statement for odd internal branches already fails for p = 3.
   Our second objective is to provide an algebraic interpretation of the lifting criteria of Givens
and Moll. We associate polynomials Pp (x) and Qp (x) with the even and odd subsequences,
respectively. Their roots modulo p correspond to the internal roots of the associated valuation
trees. Thus, the nonvanishing conditions in the Givens–Moll criteria are equivalent to the
corresponding roots being simple modulo p. It follows that a simple root of Pp (x) or Qp (x)
gives rise to a simple even or odd internal branch, respectively.
   Finally, we use the lifting congruences constructively. Along a simple branch, they determine
the unique surviving residue class at each successive level and therefore provide a recursive
method for computing the corresponding p-adic valuations. We illustrate this procedure in
detail for p = 13, where the four even internal branches are simple and their successive
surviving residues can be computed one base-13 digit at a time.
   We ﬁrst introduce the p-adic valuation trees associated with the FiboLaplace sequence and
illustrate their structure through the case p = 13. After recalling the lifting criteria of Givens
and Moll, we present a counterexample for p = 971 and then give an algebraic interpretation
of the even and odd criteria in terms of the polynomials Pp (x) and Qp (x). This interpretation
leads to simple-root criteria and a partial converse. We conclude by developing a recursive
