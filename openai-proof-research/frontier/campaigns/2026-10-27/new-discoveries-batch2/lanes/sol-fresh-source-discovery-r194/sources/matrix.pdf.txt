                                                                                     1. Introduction
                                              Let R be a noncommutative ring with center Z(R). Its commuting graph, denoted by Γ(R),
                                           is the simple graph with vertex set R \ Z(R) in which two distinct vertices x, y are connected
                                           if xy = yx. Although the commuting graph only preserves the commutativity relation, it is
                                           interesting to know which rings it determines up to isomorphism.
                                              For rings of matrices over finite fields, Akbari, Ghandehari, Hadian and Mohammadian [2]
                                           proposed the following conjecture in 2004.
                                           Conjecture 1.1. Let F be a finite field and let m ≥ 2. If R is a ring with identity such that
                                           (1)                                 Γ(R) ∼= Γ(Mm (F )),
                                           then R ∼
                                                  = Mm (F ).
                                              Abdollahi [1] showed that (1) determines the order of the ring, i.e., |R| = |Mm (F )|. Subse-
                                           quently, the conjecture was established for m = 2 by Mohammadian [5] and later for m = 2a 3b ,
                                           with a ≥ 1 and b ≥ 0, by Dorbidi [3]. For m = 3, Dorbidi and Manaviyat [4] proved the
                                           conjecture with extra assumption that Z(R) is a field. In this paper, as the title suggests, we
                                           resolve the conjecture for every even matrix size.
                                           Theorem 1.2. Let F be a finite field and let n ≥ 1. If R is a ring with identity such that
                                           (2)                                 Γ(R) ∼
                                                                                    = Γ(M2n (F )),
                                           then R ∼
                                                  = M2n (F ).
                                              Our proof idea uses centralizers isomorphic to M2 (E), where [E : F ] = n. The Skolem–
                                           Noether theorem, together with the conjugation action of 1 + J(R) on these centralizers, gives us
                                           a bound on the dimension of the Jacobson radical J(R). We then apply the double centralizer
                                           theorem in R/J(R), which shows that this bound forces J(R) = 0 and obtain the desired
                                           isomorphism.

                                                                                     2. Preliminaries
                                             For X ⊆ R, we denote its centralizer by CR (X) and consider CR (x) when X = {x}.
                                             Our first lemma is a basic observation and establishes the relations between centralizers
                                           coming from a graph isomorphism.

Lemma 2.1. Let ϕ : Γ(T ) → Γ(U ) be a graph isomorphism. For every x ∈ T \ Z(T ),
the restriction of ϕ gives rise to an isomorphism Γ(CT (x)) ∼  = Γ(CU (ϕ(x))). Moreover, the
correspondence CT (x) 7→ CU (ϕ(x)) is a bijection between the distinct centralizers of noncentral
elements in T and U .
Proof. The closed neighborhood of x is CT (x) \ Z(T ) and among its vertices, those adjacent
to all the others are exactly the elements of Z(CT (x)) \ Z(T ). We delete these vertices to
get Γ(CT (x)). The same argument applies to ϕ(x), and ϕ takes the deleted vertices in one
neighborhood to the deleted vertices in the other. Its restriction therefore gives the desired
isomorphism in our lemma.
   For the second part, we recover CT (x) from the closed neighborhood of x by adding Z(T ).
Thus, distinct centralizers correspond to distinct closed neighborhoods, and the same holds
true for U . The bijection between closed neighborhoods given by ϕ therefore gives the desired
bijection between centralizers.                                                              □

   The next lemma is the key to our main theorem. There, we use the Skolem–Noether theorem
and the double centralizer theorem [6] for the proof of the main theorem, which we recall them
briefly here.
   Let B be a finite-dimensional central simple F -algebra. Skolem–Noether says that every
F -isomorphism φ between simple unital F -subalgebras of B has the form φ(x) = uxu−1 for
some invertible element u ∈ B. We use this theorem for ring of matrices. In particular, two
simple unital F -subalgebras of Mm (F ) are conjugate by an element of GLm (F ) whenever they
are F -isomorphic.
   The double centralizer theorem states that if B is a finite-dimensional central simple unital
F -algebra and A ⊆ B is a simple unital F -subalgebra, then

Lemma 2.2. Let F be a finite field, E/F be a field extension of degree n > 1, and B =
M2n (F ). If P ∈ Mn (F ) is a companion matrix of an irreducible polynomial of degree n, then
CB (Q) ∼= M2 (E), where Q = diag(P, P ). Moreover, all centralizers in B isomorphic to M2 (E)
are conjugate under GL2n (F ), and their normalizers are isomorphic to GL2 (E) ⋊ Gal(E/F ).
Proof. The centralizer of the given companion matrix P is precisely F [P ]. Since its characteristic
polynomial is irreducible, we get the F -algebra isomorphism F [P ] ∼    = E. Now, a 2 × 2 block
matrix X = [Xij ]i,j=1,2 ∈ M2n (F ) commutes with Q if and only if each block Xij commutes
with P . Hence, the centralizer of Q is CB (Q) = M2 (F [P ]) ∼
                                                             = M2 (E).
   Now, let A ⊆ B be another centralizer isomorphic to M2 (E). We wish to show it is conjugate
to CB (Q). To see this, note that since Z(A) contains F and is a finite field of degree n over F , we
have A ∼= M2 (E) ∼= CB (Q) as F -algebras. By the Skolem–Noether theorem, an F -isomorphism
from CB (Q) to A extends to an inner automorphism of B, so there exists u ∈ GL2n (F ) such
that uCB (Q)u−1 = A, which proves the second part of the lemma.
   For the last part, let N be the normalizer of CB (Q) in GL2n (F ). Note that every element
of N is preserving the center Z(CB (Q)) = {diag(S, S) : S ∈ F [P ]} ∼     = E by conjugation and
                                                                       ∼
fixing the scalar matrices λI2n with λ ∈ F , so its action on E = Z(CB (Q)) is clearly an
F -automorphism of E. Hence, we get a group homomorphism π : N → Gal(E/F ). Its kernel
consists exactly of the invertible matrices commuting with every element of Z(CB (Q)). Now,
every element of CB (Q) commutes with its center, while every element commuting with this
center also commutes with Q ∈ Z(CB (Q)), so CB (Z(CB (Q))) = CB (Q). Therefore, the kernel
is isomorphic to GL2 (E).

   We next show that every σ ∈ Gal(E/F ) is induced by an element of N . To this end, let
us first identify E with F [t]/(f (t)), where f is the characteristic polynomial of P . In the
basis 1, t, . . . , tn−1 , multiplication by t is represented by P , so the matrices in F [P ] represent
multiplication by elements of E. Let Tσ be the matrix of the F -linear map σ in this basis. For
s ∈ E, let Ms ∈ F [P ] represent multiplication by s. Since σ(s σ −1 (v)) = σ(s)v for every v ∈ E,
we have Tσ Ms Tσ−1 = Mσ(s) . Thus conjugation by Uσ = diag(Tσ , Tσ ) applies σ to each entry of
M2 (E) under our identification with M2 (F [P ]). Hence, Uσ normalizes CB (Q) and acts as σ on
its center, so π(Uσ ) = σ.
   Finally, the matrices Uσ form a subgroup on which the restriction of π to this subgroup is
an isomorphism onto Gal(E/F ), so N ∼          = GL2 (E) ⋊ Gal(E/F ).

Remark 3.1. We proved Conjecture 1.1 for matrices of even size using the known case of size 2.
Indeed, our method extends to every multiple of a prime once the conjecture is known for that
prime size over every finite field. In particular, if the conjecture is proved for every prime size
over every finite field, we can surely deduce it for every size.
