                                                                                                 Abstract

                                                     We establish sharp weighted Rogers–Shephard inequalities for Rényi differential entropy. For
                                                 independent copies X, X ′ of a log-concave random vector in Rn , we prove hp (aX − bX ′ ) ≤ hp (X) +
                                                 n log(a + b) for all a, b > 0 and 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞, where orders 1 and ∞ correspond to Shannon entropy and
                                                 min-entropy, respectively. The Shannon case settles the entropic Rogers–Shephard conjecture of Madiman
                                                 and Kontoyiannis and its higher-dimensional formulation. For 1 ≤ p < ∞, equality holds precisely for
                                                 invertible affine images of random vectors with independent exponential coordinates. At p = ∞, the
                                                 larger equality class is characterized by possibly extended-valued convex potentials that are positively
                                                 homogeneous of degree one. For every 0 < p < 1, uniform laws on sufficiently high-dimensional
                                                 simplices violate the unweighted bound. Thus p = 1 is the critical order for this bound to hold in every
                                                 dimension. The proof combines weighted convex intersections with an exponential lift to a log-affine
                                                 probability density. At the Shannon endpoint, this lift yields an exact deficit decomposition with a
                                                 conditional mutual information term for an additive channel with asymmetric Laplace noise.

    We ask for the sharp upper bound on h(X − X ′ ) − h(X) when X ′ is an independent copy of

more than control of the support. This upper-bound problem complements the lower bounds
supplied by the entropy power inequality [2]–[4]. We determine the sharp upper bound under
log-concavity and characterize all laws attaining it.

the study of additive representation functions and additive energy. Inequalities for sumset and
difference-set sizes have entropy counterparts for independent sums and differences. Madiman

and, more generally, random variables on locally compact Polish abelian groups. More recently,
Li, Gavalakis and Kontoyiannis [6] introduced entropic additive energy for continuous random
variables and established entropy inequalities for sums, products, and sum-product combinations.
This representation viewpoint directs attention to the full difference distribution and its entropy.

C. Deficit and equality

  At the Shannon endpoint, passage to the limit proves the inequality but does not determine
its equality cases. An exact deficit identity supplies the additional argument. Fix a, b > 0 with
a + b = 1. For a log-concave probability density f , write δ1 (f ) = h(X) − h(aX − bX ′ ), where
X, X ′ are independent random vectors with density f . Write f = e−V , where V = − log f is
convex, with value +∞ where f = 0. Its effective domain is dom V = {x : V (x) < ∞}. Take

Y = (X, V (X) + E) has the log-affine density

                                δ1 (f ) = δ1 (F ) + I(B; B + L | D).                           (12)

Both terms on the right are finite and nonnegative. The conditional mutual information vanishes

for a convex body K ⊆ Rn , with equality precisely for simplices. The order-zero entropy is
h0 (f ) = log |{x : f (x) > 0}|. For independent random vectors X, X ′ uniformly distributed on K,
this gives an order-zero entropy bound, since h0 (X) = log |K| and h0 (X − X ′ ) = log |K − K|.

    Related convolution bounds include Rényi entropy power inequalities [28], [29]. Sharp entropy
bounds under moment constraints for symmetric log-concave laws were proved in [30], [31].
Bobkov and Madiman [32] established a reverse entropy power inequality after separate volume-
preserving linear transformations of the summands. Ball, Nayar and Tkocz [33] proved a reverse
entropy power inequality for one-dimensional projections of a log-concave random vector, allowing
dependence. For sums of independent copies, Fu and Li [34, Theorem 3.4] proved the sharp
min-entropy bound h∞ (X + X ′ ) − h∞ (X) ≤ 1 for real-valued log-concave X, with equality
for one-sided exponentials. At p = ∞ in dimension one, the optimal constant for the sum is
therefore 1, whereas the difference bound (6) has optimal constant log 2.
    Section II contains the analytic preliminaries. Sections III and IV prove the weighted inequality
and the deficit identities. Section V classifies the finite-order and min-entropy equality cases.
Section VI gives consequences for signed sums and a comparison with displacement interpolation.
Section VII proves failure below order one by computing the difference densities for uniform
laws on simplices. Section VIII discusses extensions to different laws, positive sums, discrete
distributions, and orders below one.

                                        VIII. O PEN PROBLEMS

     We distinguish questions suggested by the present results from two further directions proposed
to us by Jiange Li after the difference inequality was obtained: positive sums and integer-valued
log-concave laws. Some related problems and partial results already occur in the literature, as
indicated below.


A. Direct extensions

Two different one-dimensional laws. For independent real-valued random variables X, Y with
log-concave densities, is it true that

                                        eh(X−Y ) ≤ eh(X) + eh(Y ) ?                              (60)

Since reflection preserves entropy and log-concavity, this is equivalent to asking whether the
same bound holds for X + Y . Theorem 3 proves (60) for positive dilations and translations of a
common law, with equality when the common law is a one-sided exponential law.
     This question is related to the entropy-norm conjecture of Ball, Nayar and Tkocz [33, Conjecture
1], which assumes joint central symmetry and allows dependence. Here independence is assumed,
while no symmetry is required. The higher-dimensional analogue, with eh(·)/n in place of eh(·) ,
already fails in R2 . Indeed, independent centered Gaussian vectors with covariance matrices
diag(1, ε2 ) and diag(ε2 , 1) give a counterexample for sufficiently small ε > 0.

C. Stability

     For fixed dimension and positive normalized weights, can the two nonnegative terms in the
Shannon deficit of Theorem 6 control, after an invertible affine normalization, the deviation of
the potential from an affine function and the deviation of its support from a simplicial cone?
Such bounds would quantify the equality classification for exponential products.
