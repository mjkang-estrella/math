## Actual independent-clock law to finite uniformization

Here is the required mathematical bridge, independent of naming a power series a probability. For n independent rate-one Poisson clocks, fix t>=0, a count k and an ordered mark word i_1,...,i_k. The joint density on 0<s_1<...<s_k<t with exactly those marks and no other rings up to t is exp(-nt): multiply the n no-extra-ring exponential factors and unit event intensities. Integration over the simplex of volume t^k/k! gives exp(-nt)t^k/k! for **each word**. Simultaneous rings have probability zero; finite clocks are nonexplosive. Summing n^k words gives Poisson(nt) for the count, and conditional marks are iid uniform, independently of the iid initial spins. This proves the actual all-word law, including ineffective updates.

For deterministic Boolean updates U_i, define Th(s)=sum_i h(U_i(s)). With initial weights w/W the marginal is exp(-nt)/W sum_(k>=0) t^k wT^k f/k!. Since ||T||_infinity<=n, convergence and differentiated convergence are uniform on bounded t intervals. Set Q=T-nI; the derivative is the same series with g=Qf. At the observed vertex only its own update changes f, hence |g|<=1. For n=10 this is exactly the preliminary certificate's law. No schedule is omitted and no state-change holding-rate substitution is allowed.

Threshold projection is pathwise for finite update words: for odd pools thresholding a median gives majority; adding the old bit for even degree makes it precisely tie retention. Every opinion remains among the ten initial uniforms. The event that any initial opinion equals fixed 2/5 has probability zero, resolving [0,p] versus [0,p) simultaneously over all words/times. Thus a positive derivative at p=2/5 refutes literal 3.2 and transfers to 3.1. A formal proof is feasible in principle via this finite event-density/simplex argument or proved Poisson superposition/marking plus finite-sum expectations. It is a real probability-law obligation, with series convergence/differentiation and threshold transfer still required in Lean. No implementation or claim of existing library support is made.
