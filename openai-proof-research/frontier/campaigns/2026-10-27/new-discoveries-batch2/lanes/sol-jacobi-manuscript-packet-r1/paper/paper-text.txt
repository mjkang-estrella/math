           The dyadic denominator in shifted Jacobi parameters


                                 Research candidate packet — 28 October 2026

                                                           Abstract
         For the literal formal constants of Area’s Definition 2.1, we prove that the maximum dyadic de-
     nominator exponent in independent shifted parameters is 2r +ν2 ((r −1)!). The argument combines
     a binary multidegree filtration for an integral Riccati recurrence with a normalized primitive in a
     quadratic polynomial algebra. The pure top coeﬀicient attains the bound, using Area’s published
     binomial-sum valuation. A closed Lean theorem accompanies the argument. This is preparation
     for independent final review; candidate credit is zero. No analytic phase identification or priority
     claim is made.

Theorem 1. For every integer r ≥ 1, put Er = 2r + ν2 ((r − 1)!). Every coeﬀicient of 2Er κr (u, v)
belongs to Z(2) . The coeﬀicient [ur ] κr is nonzero and has valuation −Er . Consequently the maximum
dyadic denominator exponent is exactly Er .

  This answers the shifted question in [1, Remark 3.8]. It concerns the power of 2 in the common
denominator; odd factors are allowed. The original-variable exponent 3r − 1 + ν2 ((r − 1)!) belongs to
a different, already proved law [2]. The definition (1) is the scope of this paper. Agreement with an
analytic phase expansion at every order is not proved here.

3     The binary filtration and the two pairings
Let σ(k) be the binary digit sum, with σ(0) = 0. For a nonzero bidegree e = (i, j) set q(e) =
σ(i) + σ(j) − 1 ≥ 0. Binary subadditivity and doubling give

                             q(e + f ) ≤ q(e) + q(f ) + 1,    q(2e) = q(e).                          (4)

Consider parameter polynomials with zero constant coeﬀicient whose ai bj coeﬀicient belongs to
2q(i,j) R. This additive class is preserved by δ, by 2f g, and by f 2 .
   For 2f g, each product coeﬀicient has at least the power of 2 required by the first inequality in
(4). For a square, distinct parameter monomials occur in pairs, giving 2fe ff . A diagonal term is
fe2 , divisible by 22q(e) and hence by 2q(2e) . This proves square closure. Arbitrary multiplication is
insuﬀicient: a, b lie in the class, while ab does not.
  A separate pairing is required in (3): unequal spectral indices i, n−i give 2hi hn−i , and the unpaired
middle term, if present, is a square. Starting with P , strong induction proves
                               [ i j]
                                a b hn ∈ 2σ(i)+σ(j)−1 R       (i + j > 0).                            (5)

This induction pairs spectral indices; the proof of square closure pairs parameter monomials. Neither
can be omitted.
   There are two involutions. The map ρ(x, w) = (x, −w) commutes with δ and replaces b by −b in
P . Thus the ai bj coeﬀicient has w parity j. The map τ (x, w) = (−x, −w) anticommutes with δ;
induction gives τ hn = (−1)n+1 hn . Both follow coeﬀicientwise from the recurrence in the torsion-free
integral normal form.
  All hn also belong coeﬀicientwise to the ideal (w). This holds for P , is preserved by δ(wf ) =
w(−xf + δf ), and is preserved by the products in the recurrence. An element A(x) + wB(x) belongs
to (w) exactly when 1+x2 divides A(x) integrally: multiplication by w sends (C, D) to ((1+x2 )D, C).
    Consequently, for odd n, every coeﬀicient of hn has the integral normal form

                             (1 + x2 )A(x2 ) + wxB(x2 ),      A, B ∈ Z[X].                           (6)

For a coeﬀicient of parameter degree d = i + j, its B is zero if j is even. If j is odd, then

                             σ(i) + σ(j) − 1 = σ(i) + σ(j − 1) ≥ σ(d − 1).

Equation (5) therefore makes every coeﬀicient of B divisible by 2σ(d−1) . The integral normal-form
extraction preserves divisibility, since its polynomial divisor is monic. No comparable gain is needed
for A.

6    Formal correspondence, dependencies, and scope
The source is encoded over rational multivariate polynomials, with Gaussian pairs for the imaginary
part. In SourceDefs.lean, inverseFactorCoeff expands each geometric factor, powerCoeff is
finite convolution, and sourceLogCoeff is the finite logarithm. These definitions require no analytic
interpretation. The final closed interface is

      JacobiShifted.source_claim : JacobiShifted.SourceClaim.

Here SourceClaim states the all-monomial odd-denominator bound at dyadicExponent r and its
failure one exponent lower at Finsupp.single 0 r, for every natural r ≥ 1. The pure coeﬀicient’s
nonzero exact valuation is separately proved.
 The 35 mathematical modules are mapped in detail in the accompanying PROOF.md and FORMAL-MAP.md.
Their principal groups are:

    • MidpointAlgebra, BinaryDigits, Filtration, Riccati, and the midpoint/primitive modules:
      integral normal forms, both pairings, parity, ideal membership, odd-unit inversion, and normal-
      ized uniqueness.
    • Literal*, FiniteLogChain, RiccatiScale, and LogRiccatiIdentification: the finite source,
      Hankel/differential identity, logarithmic chain rule, and actual normalized source bridge.
    • ParameterDegree, CatalanTop, ScaledTop, PurePrimitive, LiteralPureCoefficient: ac-
      tual support bounds and top attainment.
