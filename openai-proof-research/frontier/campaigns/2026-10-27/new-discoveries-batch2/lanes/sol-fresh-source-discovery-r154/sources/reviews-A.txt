1    Review
Correctness. The arguments presented appear to be sound. Every claim is proven, obstructions
to potential approaches are given, and remaining gaps are noted. The model identifies the
counterexamples in the ranges p ∈ (1/3, 1/2) and p ∈ (1/2, 1) and proves the assertion for the
non-trivial collection of rationals p = 1/k and p = 2/k for k ≥ 6. A number of potentially useful
properties of the set of valid p are also proven, including closure under multiplication and taking
limits. Finally, a variety of equivalent formulations of the remaining case p ≤ 1/3 are presented.
Given the substantial partial progress, I would grade the solution as fully correct.

Novelty. The novelty of the solution is impressive. The model identifies the key connection to
subsets with non-negative sums and the Manickam–Miklos–Singhi conjecture under the unique
formulation of a coloring criterion. Previously, a version of this argument had been used to verify
the cases of p = 1/k, and the model gives a new argument to extend this to the cases of p = 2/k .
The multiplicative closure property is a nice observation that does not appear to be readily available
in the literature, and same with the remaining equivalent forms. Overall, the model is able to draw
many connections, notably including the intended one.

Presentation. The main shortcoming of the paper is the lack of citations and connections to prior
literature. Only two quick references are mentioned at the end, and for example the Manickam–
Miklos–Singhi connection that was noted has no elaboration or citation. It would be nice to know
if the model drew some of its ideas from the literature. The mathematical content is laid out in
detail, and the proofs are supplemented with some exposition. The paper lacks a bit of flow and
tends to jump from result to result, but this could be due in part to the lack of a main result, instead
being a collection of partial progress. There are other minor inconveniences, for example equation
references are not hyperlinked.

In terms of mathematical content alone, I would judge this submission as essentially flawless.

Remaining open issues
The negative direction is complete, and the positive direction is complete for the values listed in
Corollary 1, their finite products under Proposition 2, and p = 1/2, 1. Therefore a full solution would
follow from Conjecture 1; combined with Proposition 4, it would give exactly [0, 1/3] ∪ {1/2, 1}.

    The essential missing assertion is Conjecture 1. Mathematically, it is the assertion that every
weighted threshold down-set {A : i∈A wi < p} has p-biased measure at most 1 − p when p ≤ 1/3.

It appears in the document as the unproved implication [0, 1/3] ⊆ G. The approaches recorded above
reduce it to any one of the unit-gap inequality (UG), the strict complement inequality (1), the pivot

    The pairwise coalescence assertion suggested by formula (5) is not available: the equal-weight
examples in the preceding section show that every pair can have negative coalescence gain. A
viable coalescence argument would need either block coalescence or a dichotomy proving that
configurations with all pair gains negative already obey Φq (w) ≤ q.
    The capped slice lemma displayed after (4) is the most focused remaining inductive target.
Exhaustive enumeration of all down-sets on at most five coordinates found no violation of the
convex-hull statement (3), and the extremal separated examples were only the expected dictatorship
equalities and lower-measure singleton-cone examples. These computations are evidence for (3),
but of course not a proof.
