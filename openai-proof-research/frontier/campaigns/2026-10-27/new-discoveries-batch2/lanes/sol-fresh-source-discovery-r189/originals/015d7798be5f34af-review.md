## Independent mathematical audit, every branch

1. **Quotient and restricted surjection.** A spanning pair generates G as a group. Finite abelian structure gives G ≅ Z_c × Z_(ck), c>=2; choose a prime p|c. Then G/pG ≅ F_p². Two generating images form a basis, so the coefficient map F_p² → G/pG is an isomorphism. For any fixed coefficient class w, every element of the associated pG coset has some representative in D_s; the basis property forces that representative's coefficients to lie in w. Thus this restriction is onto a set of |pG|=|G|/p² elements. It need not be injective or uniformly distributed. This proves |G|<=p² min_w |D_s∩(w+pZ²)| without an s-regularity assumption. A quotient merely isomorphic to F_p² without the basis argument would not suffice; the candidate includes it correctly.

2. **p=2.** Opposite-to-s parity of x+y contains exactly s² diamond points (source Lemma 2.1(2), also the elementary shell count). These split between exactly two coefficient residues modulo 2. One class has <=floor(s²/2) points, hence |G|<=4floor(s²/2)<=2s². This handles any even c, not only c=2. It is a sufficient upper bound, not a claim that every such order is attainable.

3. **Odd p and rotation.** For u=x+y,v=x-y, max(|u|,|v|)=|x|+|y|. The inverse halves are integers exactly when u,v have the same parity. Modulo odd p this linear transformation has determinant -2, hence is invertible. Therefore taking minima over coefficient classes equals taking minima over ordered pairs of progression residues in [-s,s]. For residue i let (E_i,O_i) be its even/odd counts; a square fiber has E_iE_j+O_iO_j points. There is no requirement i≠j.

4. **Progression minimum.** If L=qp+r, exactly r progressions have q+1 points, p-r have q points. Their potential starting terms are consecutive from -s through -s+p-1; since step p is odd, parity alternates. For q even, each short vector is (q/2,q/2); long vectors are (q/2,q/2+1) or its reversal. Every dot product is >=q²/2, attained by a short vector paired with itself. This includes q=0: at least one empty class exists, and the minimum is 0; potential starting terms of empty progressions are harmless bookkeeping.

   For q odd put a=(q-1)/2. Short vectors are (a+1,a) or its reversal; longs are (a+1,a+1). With at least two shorts, consecutive starts have opposite imbalances, yielding 2a(a+1)=(q²-1)/2. Every short-short same-type product exceeds this by 1; short-long and long-long products are no smaller. With one short (r=p-1), only its self-product is available, namely (q²+1)/2. Its short-long comparison is q(q+1)/2 minus (q²+1)/2 = (q-1)/2>=0, including equality at q=1. Long-long is also larger. Thus all three candidate formulas, including unique-short and q0, hold for unbounded s,p; tests are not used for this deduction.

5. **p|L.** Then r=0 and q is odd. The fiber bound becomes (L²-p²)/2. If L is prime, the only such p is L and q=1, giving minimum 0. This contradicts nonempty quotient fibers; it excludes this prime choice, not the existence of the lower-bound group.

6. **p does not divide L.** Here r>=1. For even q, p²m=(L-r)²/2<=(L-1)²/2=2s²; for odd q and r<=p-2, subtract p²/2 from the same expression. For odd q,r=p-1, direct subtraction is p²m-2s²=-qp(p-2)+2p-2. At p>=5,q>=1 this is <=-p²+4p-2<0. At p=3 it is 4-3q: q=1 forces s=2, and for s>=3 the next possible odd q is >=3. The exceptional excluded case really occurs: s=2,p=3 gives m=1, hence upper bound 9 instead of 8, attained by Z_3². The s>=3 hypothesis is necessary.

7. **Composite comparison and attainment.** For composite L, p|L implies p>=p0, so the divisor bound is <=(L²-p0²)/2. For p=2 or p∤L use 2s² instead. This is dominated because the difference is L-(p0²+1)/2>= (p0²-1)/2>0, using L>=p0². No assumption that p0 divides c is made or needed. Combine with the exact source constructions above. All groups and all s>=3 are covered.

No mathematical repair is required. A fuller exposition could call empty q0 progressions “potential progressions” and write out u,v in the lower construction, but these are clarity suggestions, not gaps.
