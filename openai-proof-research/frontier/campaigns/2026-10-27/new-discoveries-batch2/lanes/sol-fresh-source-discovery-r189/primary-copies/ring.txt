                                            INTEGER-VALUED POLYNOMIALS OVER STRUCTURAL MATRIX
                                                                  RINGS

                                                                                  VALENTIN HAVLOVEC


                                                   Abstract. We study integer-valued polynomials and null polynomials over structural
                                                   matrix rings, that is, rings whose elements are matrices in which an entry may be
                                                   nonzero only when its row index precedes its column index in a fixed preorder. We prove




arXiv:2608.13242v2 [math.RA] 15 Sep 2026
                                                   that the integer-valued polynomials over a structural matrix ring with entries in an
                                                   integral domain form a ring, and that the null polynomials over a structural matrix ring
                                                   with entries in an arbitrary commutative ring form a two-sided ideal. In both settings,
                                                   we give a characterization of the corresponding polynomials with matrix coefficients in
                                                   terms of scalar-coefficient polynomials. These results extend corresponding theorems
                                                   for full matrix rings and upper triangular matrix rings.




                                                                                   1. Introduction
                                              Over the past two decades, there have been numerous articles studying generalizations
                                           of integer-valued polynomials to noncommutative rings [2, 3, 6–8, 10, 11, 14]. For an
                                           overview, see the survey article [13]. The recent monograph by Chabert [1] covers many
                                           more aspects of integer-valued polynomials. A common framework in the noncommutative
                                           setting is the following [2]: let D be an integral domain with quotient field K, let A
                                           be a module-finite, torsion-free D-algebra such that A X K “ D (where A and K are
                                           canonically embedded in A bD K). Set B “ A bD K and consider the set of integer-valued
                                           polynomials
                                                                   IntB pAq “ tf P Brxs | @a P A : f paq P Au.
                                           Here f paq is defined as right substitution, i.e., if f “ k fk xk , then f paq “ k fk ak .
                                                                                                    ř                      ř
                                              A central question in this approach is to determine whether the set IntB pAq is closed
                                           under multiplication and thus forms a subring of Brxs. This is not a given, since for
                                           polynomials f, g with noncommuting coefficients, we generally have pf gqpaq ‰ f paqgpaq.
                                           That the integer-valued polynomials form a ring is known for quaternions [10], full matrix
                                           algebras Mn pDq [11], upper triangular matrix algebras Tn pDq [3], and certain block
                                           matrix algebras s Ln pDq [7]. Furthermore, until recently no algebra A was known for
                                           which IntB pAq fails to be a ring. However, a finite ring whose null polynomials do not
                                           form a two-sided ideal was constructed in [5]. This finite ring is the reduction modulo
                                           2 of a module-finite, free Z-algebra. Together with the correspondence between null
                                           polynomials and integer-valued ones [12, Theorem 2.4], this yields an algebra A for which
                                           IntB pAq is not a ring.

   Among the positive examples above, the matrix algebras Mn pDq, Tn pDq, and s Ln pDq
share a common form. In each case the algebra A is obtained by choosing a subset Σ of
the set of position indices t1, . . . , nu ˆ t1, . . . , nu, and taking A to be the set of matrices
with arbitrary entries in positions pi, jq satisfying pi, jq P Σ and zeros in all other positions.
Of course, whether the set A forms a unital subring of Mn pDq depends on the choice of
Σ. This occurs precisely when Σ, viewed as a binary relation on t1, . . . , nu, is reflexive
and transitive, i.e., a preorder. Rings constructed in this way are called structural matrix
rings [9]. For a preorder À, we denote the corresponding structural matrix ring over D
by MÀ pDq.
   We prove that for any preorder À, the set IntMÀ pKq pMÀ pDqq of integer-valued poly-
nomials is a ring, thus generalizing [7, Theorem 2.6], which in turn was a generalization
of [3, Theorem 5.4] and [11, Theorem 1.2]. We further give a description of the elements
of IntMÀ pKq pMÀ pDqq in terms of matrices with scalar coefficients. This characterization
specializes to the known description of IntMn pKq pMn pDqq in [2, Theorem 7.2] and of
IntTn pKq pTn pDqq in [3, Corollary 5.3].
   As alluded to before, there is a related question concerning null polynomials: for a
possibly noncommutative ring T , let NpT q “ tf P T rxs | @a P T : f paq “ 0u be the
set of null polynomials over T . Is NpT q a two-sided ideal of T rxs? Again, due to the
absence of a substitution homomorphism, this is not clear. In 2014, Werner first raised
this question for finite rings [12]. He also explained the connection to integer-valued
polynomials for module-finite Z-algebras A, namely that IntB pAq is a ring if and only
if NpT q is a two-sided ideal for all rings T of the form A{dA with d P Zzt0u. Frisch
subsequently extended this connection to the setting of algebras over arbitrary domains,
and also in the direction of integer-valued polynomials over subsets (i.e., polynomials
mapping a proper subset of A back into A) [4].
   Werner answered the question in the affirmative for large classes of finite rings, including
local rings, semisimple rings, and matrix rings over commutative rings, leading him to
conjecture that NpT q is always a two-sided ideal. Further work by Frisch in 2017 showed
that the conjecture also holds for upper triangular matrix rings [3, Theorem 5.2]. No
counterexample was known until recently [5]. We show that for every structural matrix
ring MÀ pRq the set of null polynomials NpMÀ pRqq is a two-sided ideal, and give a
description of its elements in terms of matrices with scalar coefficients.
