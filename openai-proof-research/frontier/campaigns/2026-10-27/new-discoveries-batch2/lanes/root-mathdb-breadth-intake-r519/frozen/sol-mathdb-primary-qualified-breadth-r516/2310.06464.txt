                                                                                          Abstract
                                                    A mixed hypergraph H = (V, C, D) consists of the vertex set V and two families of
                                               subsets of 2V : the family C of co-edges and the family D of edges. H is said to be colorable
                                               if there is a mapping f from V to the set of positive integers such that |{f (v) : v ∈ e}| < |e|
                                               for each e ∈ C and |{f (v) : v ∈ e}| > 1 for each e ∈ D. There exist mixed hypergraphs
                                               which are uncolorable, and quite little about these mixed hypergraphs is known.
                                                    A mixed hypergraph is called a bi-hypergraph if its co-edge set and edge set are the
                                               same. In this article, we first apply Lovász local lemma to show that any r-uniform bi-
                                               hypergraph with r ≥ 4 is colorable if every edge is incident to less than (r − 1)r−1 e−1 − 1
                                               other edges, where e is the base of natural logarithms. Then, we show that among all the
                                               uncolorable 3-uniform bi-hypergraphs, the smallest size of a minimal one is ten, which
                                               answers a question raised by Tuza and Voloshin in 2000. As an extension, we provide
                                               a minimal uncolorable 3-uniform bi-hypergraph of order n and size at most 7n          3 − 4 for
                                               every n ≥ 6.


                                               1     Introduction
                                                  A hypergraph H consists of a vertex set and an edge set, where any member in the
                                               edge set is a subset of its vertex set. As a natural generalization of the concept of the
                                               chromatic number of a graph, Erdős and Hajnal [2] introduced the chromatic number
                                               χ(H) of a hypergraph H as the minimal number of colors needed to color the vertices in
                                               H such that no edge in H has all its vertices with the same color.

    Voloshin [7, 8] extended the concept of the chromatic number of a hypergraph to
the upper chromatic number of a mixed hypergraph. A mixed hypergraph is a triple
H = (V, C, D), where V is a finite set and both C and D are subsets of the power set 2V .
The members of V are called vertices of H, and the members in C and the members in
D are called co-edges and edges of H respectively. In this paper, we always assume that
both C and D are Sperner families (i.e., e1 6⊆ e2 for any distinct e1 , e2 ∈ C or e1 , e2 ∈ D).
    Let N denote the set of positive integers, and for any k ∈ N, let [k] denote the set
{1, 2, . . . , k}.

Definition 1 Let H = (V, C, D) be a mixed hypergraph and f be a mapping from V to
N. For any e in C (or resp., D), e is properly colored in f if |f (e)| < |e| (or resp.,
|f (e)| > 1), where f (e) denotes the set {f (v) : v ∈ e}. Then f is a proper coloring of H
if every member of C ∪ D is properly colored in f , and H is colorable if H has a proper
coloring. In this case, the maximum number of colors that can occur simultaneously in
a proper coloring of H is called the upper chromatic number χ̄(H) of H.

     If C = ∅, then H = (V, C, D) is a hypergraph with edge set D. Thus, a proper coloring
of a mixed hypergraph is an extension of a proper coloring of a hypergraph. If D = ∅ and
H is uniform, χ̄(H) is treated as a particular case of a more general problem raised by
Ahlswede et al. [1], where the authors studied the largest integer, denoted by N (H, k),
such that H has a mapping f : V 7→ N satisfying the conditions |f (V)| = |N (H, k)| and
|f (e)| ≤ k for each edge e ∈ C.
     A proper coloring of a mixed hypergraph H with a certain number of colors is also
called a strict coloring of H. In [4], the concepts of strict coloring and upper chromatic
number are applied to study the Steiner triple systems (STSs). In particular, the authors
studied two different kinds of colorings for STSs. In one of them, they viewed all blocks
of the triple system as co-edges; such systems will be termed CSTS, referring to the
expression ‘co-hypergraph’ and to indicate that monochromatic blocks are allowed. In
the other type, all blocks are treated as edges and co-edges at the same time, called Bi-
Steiner triple systems. Moreover, proper colorings of mixed hypergraphs also have wide
applications in the studies of resource allocation, parallel computing and DNA molecules,
as explained in [6].
     Unfortunately, there exist mixed hypergraphs which do not admit proper colorings,
i.e., uncolorable. Some obvious examples occur when edges of size two arise. For example,
H is uncolorable if there is an co-edge e in H such that every pair of vertices in e form
an edge in H. A less trivial class of uncolorable mixed hypergraphs was identified by
Tuza and Voloshin [5]. For any n, l, m ∈ N, let K(n, l, m) be the mixed hypergraph
([n], [n]

Problem 1 ([6]) For n, l, m ∈ N with n ≥ (l−1)(m−1)+1, consider a mixed hypergraph
H = (V, C, D) of order n with the size of each C-edge l (≥ 3) and the size of each D-edge
m (≥ 2). Suppose that H does not contain K(n, l, m) as a subhypergraph. Then what is
the minimum number of C-edges (D-edges) for H to be uncolorable?

    Towards answering Problem 1, in this article we shall focus on a most ‘non-special’
case when m = l. A mixed hypergraph H = (V, C, D) is called a bi-hypergraph if C = D.
Since every edge in a bi-hypergraph is both an edge and a co-edge, we can simply write
H as (V, E) rather than (V, E, E), and the size of H is equal to |E| rather than 2|E|.
A subhypergraph of a bi-hypergraph H = (V, E) is a bi-hypergraph H′ = (V ′ , E ′ ) with
V ′ ⊆ V and E ′ ⊆ E, and H′ is induced if E ′ = {e ∈ E : e ⊆ V ′ }. Obviously, a proper
coloring f of a bi-hypergraph H = (V, E) satisfies the requirement that 1 < |f (e)| < |e|
for each e ∈ E. Thus, a bi-hypergraph H is uncolorable if it contains an edge with size
less than 3.
    Moreover, Tuza and Voloshin [5] showed their strong interest in minimal uncolorable
bi-hypergraphs, i.e., the bi-hypergraphs which are themselves uncolorable but all of their
proper subhypergraphs are colorable. It is highlighted in [5] that K(n, r, r) is minimal
uncolorable if and only if n = (r − 1)2 + 1. Also, it is not difficult to show that each
minimal uncolorable bi-hypergraph H = (V, E) is 2-edge-connected, i.e., both H and
H − e for any edge e are connected, where H − e = (V, E \ {e}). The following problem
was raised by Tuza and Voloshin [5] in 2000 and remains open.

Problem 2 ([5]) What is the smallest positive integer m = m(r) for which there exists
a minimal uncolorable r-uniform bi-hypergraph with m edges?

    For any n, r ∈ N, let BiHyp(n, r) denote the set of r-uniform bi-hypergraphs of
order n, and let MUC(n, r) denote the set of minimal uncolorable bi-hypergraphs in

is colorable (see [5]), implying that MUC(n, r) = ∅. If n ≥ (r − 1)2 + 1, we guess that
MUC(n, r) is non-empty.

Lemma 4 Let H be a bi-hypergraph with two non-adjacent vertices u and v, and let

Proof. Assume that H = (V, E) and H · uv = (V ′ , E ′ ), where V ′ \ V = {w}.
    Let c be a proper coloring of H · uv. Then let c′ be the mapping from V to N such
that c′ (x) = c(x) for any x ∈ V ∩ V ′ and c′ (u) = c′ (v) = c(w). We shall show that c′ is a

|e ∩ {u, v}| = 1, let e′ be the corresponding edge of e in H · uv, i.e., e′ = (e \ {u, v}) ∪ {w}.
As a result, |e′ | = |e| and |{c(e′ )}| = |{c′ (e)}|, which implies that e is properly colored.
Hence H is colorable.                                                                          ✷

Lemma 10 For any k ≥ 1, the bi-hypergraph H obtained from H2k+1 by adding a new

    Suppose |{c(v1,j ) : j = 1, 2, 3}| = 1. Then due to the edges {v2,j , v1,j , v1,j+t } for
