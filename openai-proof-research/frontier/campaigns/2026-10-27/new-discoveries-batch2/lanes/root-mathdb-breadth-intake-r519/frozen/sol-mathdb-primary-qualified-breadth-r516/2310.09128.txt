                                                                                              Abstract
                                                     Given a set F of graphs, we call a copy of a graph in F an F -graph. The F -isolation
                                                 number of a graph G, denoted by ι( G, F ), is the size of a smallest subset D of the vertex
                                                 set V ( G ) such that the closed neighbourhood of D intersects the vertex sets of the F -graphs
                                                 contained by G (equivalently, G − N [ D ] contains no F -graph). Thus, ι( G, {K1 }) is the dom-
                                                 ination number of G. The second author showed that if F is the            set of cycles and G is a
                                                 connected n-vertex graph that is not a triangle, then ι( G, F ) ≤ n4 . This bound is attainable
                                                 for every n and solved a problem of Caro and Hansberg. A question that arises immediately
                                                 is how much smaller an upper bound can be if F = {Ck } for some k ≥ 3, where Ck is a
                                                 cycle of length k. The problem is to determine the smallest real number ck (if it exists) such
                                                 that for some finite set Ek of graphs, ι( G, {Ck }) ≤ ck |V ( G )| for every connected graph G that
                                                 is not an Ek -graph. The above-mentioned result yields c3 = 41 and E3 = {C3 }. The second
                                                 author also showed that if k ≥ 5 and ck exists, then ck ≥ 2k2+1 . We prove that c4 = 51 and
                                                 determine E4 , which consists of three 4-vertex graphs and six 9-vertex graphs. The 9-vertex
                                                 graphs in E4 were fully determined by means of a computer program. A method that has
                                                 the potential of yielding similar results is introduced.

  The study of isolating sets was initiated in the paper [9] of Caro and Hansberg. It is a
natural generalization of the popular study of dominating sets [11, 12, 15–18]. Indeed, D is
a dominating set of G (that is, N [ D ] = V ( G )) if and only if D is a K1 -isolating set of G, so
the domination number of G, denoted by γ( G ), is the K1 -isolation number of G. One of the
earliest domination results, due to Ore [25], is that γ( G ) ≤ n/2 for any connected n-vertex
graph G ; K1 (see [15]). While the deletion of the closed neighbourhood of a dominating set
produces the graph with no vertices, the deletion of the closed neighbourhood of a K2 -isolating
set produces a graph with no edges. Caro and Hansberg [9] proved that if G is a connected
n-vertex graph, then ι( G, K2 ) ≤ n/3 unless G ≃ K2 or G ≃ C5 . Their seminal paper [9] also
featured several problems. Generalizing the bounds above, the second author of the present
paper, Fenech and Kaemawichanurat [4] showed that for any k ≥ 1, ι( G, Kk ) ≤ n/(k + 1) unless
G ≃ Kk or k = 2 and G ≃ C5 (in [5], they gave a sharp upper bound in terms of the number of
edges). This bound is sharp and settled a problem in [9]. Domination and isolation have been
particularly investigated for maximal outerplanar graphs [6–10, 13, 14, 19, 21–23, 26, 27], mostly
due to connections with Chvátal’s Art Gallery Theorem [10].

   Problem 7.4 in [9] asks for bounds on ι( G, F ) for other interesting sets F . A question that
arises immediately from Theorem 1.2 is how much smaller an upper bound on ι( G, F ) can be

Ek -graph. Also in [3], the second author of the present paper showed that if k ≥ 5 and ck exists,

3 Problems

Theorem 1.3 immediately raises certain questions. The second part of the theorem tells us that
the bound in the theorem is attained by Bn,C4 for every n. It is easy to see that many other
graphs attain the bound, but it would be interesting to know what structures extremal graphs
have. This leads us to our first two problems.

Problem 3.1. Which are the connected graphs that attain the bound in Theorem 1.3?

It is quite likely that Problem 3.1 is significantly challenging for the case where n is not divisible
by 5. On the other hand, the problem for the case where n is divisible by 5 should be tractable,
and this is the following relaxation of Problem 3.1.

Problem 3.2. Which are the connected graphs G such that ι( G, C4 ) = |V ( G )|/5?

  Let C4 denote the set {Ck : k ≥ 4}. In view of Theorems 1.2 and 1.3, we also pose the following
problem.
