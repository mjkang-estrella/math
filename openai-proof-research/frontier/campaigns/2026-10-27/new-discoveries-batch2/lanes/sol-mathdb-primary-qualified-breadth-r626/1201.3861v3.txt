Benjamini-Schramm convergence and the distribution of chromatic roots for
sparse graphs



Miklós Abért

  
Tamás Hubai



Abstract
    

We define the chromatic measure of a finite simple graph as the uniform
distribution on its chromatic roots. We show that for a Benjamini-Schramm
convergent sequence of finite graphs, the chromatic measures converge in
holomorphic moments.
    

As a corollary, for a convergent sequence of finite graphs, we prove that the
normalized log of the chromatic polynomial converges to an analytic function
outside a bounded disc. This generalizes a recent result of Borgs, Chayes,
Kahn and Lovász, who proved convergence at large enough positive integers
and answers a question of Borgs.
    

Our methods also lead to explicit estimates on the number of proper colorings
of graphs with large girth.
  




1 Introduction




Let GG be a finite undirected graph without multiple edges and loops. A map
f:V⁡(G)→{1,…,q}f:V(G)\rightarrow\left\{1,\ldots,q\right\} is a proper coloring
if for all edges (x,y)∈E⁡(G)(x,y)\in E(G) we have f⁡(x)≠f⁡(y)f(x)\neq f(y). For a positive
integer qq let chG​(q)\mathrm{ch}_{G}(q) denote the number of proper colorings of
GG with qq colors. Then chG\mathrm{ch}_{G} is a polynomial in qq, called the
chromatic polynomial of GG. The complex roots of chG\mathrm{ch}_{G} are
called the chromatic roots of GG.




The study of chromatic polynomials and its roots has been initiated by
Birkhoff. Since then, the subject has gotten considerable interest, partially because
of its connection to statistical mechanics. In particular, the chromatic roots
control the behaviour of the antiferromagnetic Potts model at zero
temperature. For a survey on the subject see [13].




For a finite graph GG, a finite rooted graph α\alpha and a positive integer
RR, let 𝐏⁡(G,α,R)\mathbf{P}(G,\alpha,R) denote the probability that the RR-ball
centered at a uniform random vertex of GG is isomorphic to α\alpha. We say
that a graph sequence (Gn)(G_{n}) of bounded degree is Benjamini-Schramm
convergent if for all finite rooted graphs α\alpha and R>0R>0, the
probabilities 𝐏⁡(Gn,α,R)\mathbf{P}(G_{n},\alpha,R) converge (see [3]). This means
that one can not statistically distinguish GnG_{n} and Gn′G_{n^{\prime}} for
large nn and n′n^{\prime} by sampling them from a random vertex with a fixed
radius of sight. An example (that is regularly used in statistical physics) is
to approximate the infinite lattice ℤd\mathbb{Z}^{d} by bricks with all the
side lengths tending to infinity. More generally, amenable vertex transitive
graphs can be obtained as the Benjamini-Schramm limits of their Følner sequences.




For a simple graph GG let μG\mu_{G}, the chromatic measure of GG
denote the uniform distribution on its chromatic roots. By a theorem of Sokal
[12], μG\mu_{G} is supported on the open disc of radius C​dCd around 00,

However, this is not true in general, as the following easy counterexample
shows. Let PnP_{n} denote the path of length nn and let CnC_{n} denote the
cycle of length nn. Then PnP_{n} and CnC_{n} converge to the same object, the
infinite rooted path, while we have

Still, using Theorem 1.1, we are able to prove the weak convergence
of μGn\mu_{G_{n}} for some natural sequences of graphs. For example, let
Tn=C4×PnT_{n}=C_{4}\times P_{n} denote the 4×n4\times n tube, i.e. the cartesian
product of the 4-cycle with a path on nn vertices. TnT_{n} is a 44-regular
graph except at the ends of the tube.



Proposition 1.3



The chromatic measures μTn\mu_{T_{n}} weakly converge.





The proof is as follows: as Salas and Sokal [11] showed, the pointwise
limit XX of supports of μTn\mu_{T_{n}} is part of a particular algebraic
curve, so any subsequential weak limit is supported on XX. The complement of
XX is connected, so by Mergelyan’s theorem [8], every continuous
function on XX can be uniformly approximated by polynomials. Using Theorem
1.1 this yields weak convergence of μTn\mu_{T_{n}}. See Section
4 for details.




In this case one can use the so-called transfer matrix method to control the
support of the chromatic measures (see [11] for various models related
to the square lattice). In general, even for models of the square lattice, the
complement of the limiting set may not be connected, and hence one can not
invoke Mergelyan’s theorem. It is expected, however, that for any model where
the transfer matrix method can be used, the chromatic measures do converge weakly.

For a random rooted graph GG, a finite rooted graph α\alpha and a positive integer
RR, let 𝐏⁡(G,α,R)\mathbf{P}(G,\alpha,R) denote the probability that the RR-ball

A graph sequence (Gn)(G_{n}) of bounded degree is Benjamini-Schramm
convergent if for all finite rooted graphs α\alpha and R>0R>0 the
probabilities 𝐏⁡(Gn,α,R)\mathbf{P}(G_{n},\alpha,R) converge.




The limit of a Benjamini-Schramm convergent sequence of graphs is the random
rooted graph GG satisfying




𝐏⁡(G,α,R)=limn→∞𝐏⁡(Gn,α,R)\mathbf{P}(G,\alpha,R)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}(G_{n},\alpha,R)



for all R>0R>0 and α\alpha. It is easy to see that GG is well defined. In the
most transparent case, GG is just one graph, which then has to be vertex

ℤd=limn→∞(ℤ/n​ℤ)d=limn→∞Bnd\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z)}^{d}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B_{n}^{d}



where (ℤ/n​ℤ)d(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z)}^{d} is the dd dimensional torus and

where ∂Fn\partial F_{n} denotes the external vertex boundary of FnF_{n}. Note
that GG is amenable if and only if it has a Følner sequence. It is easy to
show that any connected vertex transitive amenable graph is the
Benjamini-Schramm limit of its Følner sequences.




Let us consider now the dd-regular tree TdT_{d}, which is in many senses the
farthest possible from being amenable. One can obtain TdT_{d} as the limit of
finite graphs, but it is worth to point out that TdT_{d} can not be
obtained as a limit of finite trees. Indeed, the expected degrees of finite
trees are approximately 22 and this passes on to their limits. It is a good
exercise to understand what the limit of the balls in TdT_{d} is (it is a
fixed tree where the root is random). The right way to approximate TdT_{d} in
Benjamini-Schramm convergence is to take finite dd-regular graphs GnG_{n}
with girth tending to infinity.




Benjamini-Schramm convergence can also be expressed in terms of graph
homomorphisms using the following lemma (see [6], Proposition 5.6).



Lemma 2.2



Let GnG_{n} be a graph sequence of bounded degree. Then GnG_{n}
is Benjamini-Schramm convergent if and only if for every finite connected
graph HH, the limit

Note that one needs connectedness in Lemma 2.2, as hom⁡(H,G)\hom(H,G) may
be the order of |V⁡(G)|c\left|V(G)\right|^{c} where cc is the number of
components in HH.

Remark. As we saw in the introduction, weak convergence does
not hold in general. The phenomenon where an associated measure blows up by a
small change of the graph but keeps its holomorphic moments unchanged also
occurs in the spectral theory of directed graphs. Namely, the weak
limit of the eigenvalue distributions of the directed path of length nn is
the Dirac measure at 00, while for the directed nn-cycle the limit is the
uniform measure on the unit circle centered at 00. In both the chromatic and
the spectral case, the reason is that the change only affects the coefficients
of small index in the corresponding polynomial, and the kk-th moment only
depends on the kk highest index coefficients. It would be interesting to
study this blow-up phenomenon using just abstract polynomials.
