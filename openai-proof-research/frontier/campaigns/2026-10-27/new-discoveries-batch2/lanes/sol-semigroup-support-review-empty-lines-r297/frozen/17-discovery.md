## Independent generator-window review

The repaired d<2a argument is valid. If the first positives a<b<d have d>=2a, necessarily either b<2a,d=2a, or b=2a,2a<d<=3a. In the first case initial residue distinctness forces a nonzero and b zero modulo 3. After 2a the next three distinct values are a+b, min(2b,3a), max(2b,3a), provided 2b!=3a: all further sums are larger, since b>a, b<2a, and 2b<4a. Two of these have residue zero. Equality 2b=3a forces gcd(a,b,2a)=a/2=1, so a=2. In the second case d=3a is nonprimitive. Otherwise the first triple forces d=0 and a!=0 modulo 3; the next triple is 3a,a+d,4a, since 2a<d<3a and 2d>4a. Its latter two entries repeat residue a. These cases exhaust d>=2a; no minimal-generator premise is required. The earlier erroneous a-versus-2a shortcut remains rejected in the frozen record.

## Universal Apéry reductions for three representative unresolved supports

These are ordinary universal identities, not claims of novelty. For an Apéry table A modulo a, membership is n>=A[n mod a]; its conductor is max(A)-a+1. This follows by subtracting the minimal representative, and supplies exact finite checks for any specified parameter pair. gcd assumptions below ensure residues jt mod a are distinct for 0<=j<a.

1. On a-2b+d=0 put b=a+t,d=a+2t with t>0,2t<a,gcd(a,t)=1. For 0<=j<a,

   A[jt mod a]=ceil(j/2)*a+jt.

   Proof: elements have the form qa+jt with 0<=j<=2q. Conversely every such pair is attainable using a+t and a+2t, then copies of a. For fixed j, q_min=ceil(j/2). All other nonnegative t-coefficients in the same residue are j+la, l>=0; their displayed minima increase strictly with l. Therefore j is minimizing. In particular c=(ceil((a-1)/2)-1)*a+(a-1)*t+1, and genus floor(a*a/4)+(a-1)(t-1)/2. This agrees with the interval-block mechanism in source Lemmas4.4 and4.7, but covers arbitrary positive t.

   A useful forbidden-prefix region: if 4t<a, the first two layers (degrees1,2) lie strictly before 3a. Their lengths are3 and5. After the degree-two layer the next element is3a. The aligned triple in positions7,8,9 is (2a+3t,2a+4t,3a). Under initial distinctness t!=0 mod3, it is a permutation only if t=-a mod3. Otherwise it explicitly fails. On retained AP segments this congruence is already imposed, so this lemma does not dispose of the residual t>=3 cases. It is recorded to prevent an incorrect unrestricted layer concatenation argument.

2. On 3b=2d put b=2t,d=3t,3t/2<a<2t,gcd(a,t)=1. Then A[jt mod a]=jt for j=0 or 2<=j<a, and A[t mod a]=(a+1)t. Indeed <2,3>={0} union {j>=2}; every element is ia+jt with j in that set. The least admissible j in a residue is j, except j=1 where it is a+1. Thus c=(a+1)t-a+1. Equivalently write a=2t-s,0<2s<t,gcd(s,t)=1. The source families on this line have s=1 (H2) or s=2 (H15); first residues require a,t both nonzero modulo3. The outstanding problem is to exclude all other admissible primitive (s,t) on the retained segments, not merely large t at fixed s.

3. On 3a-3b+d=0 put b=a+t,d=3t,a/2<t<2a/3,gcd(a,t)=1. For 0<=j<a,

   A[jt mod a]=min_{l=0,1,2} [((j+la) mod3)*a+(j+la)*t].

   Proof: for a fixed t-coefficient J, the least coefficient of a is J mod3, realized using that many copies of a+t and the remainder as copies of3t. Other J in the same residue are j+la. Increasing l by3 preserves J mod3 and adds3at>0; hence only l=0,1,2 can minimize. This reduces the entire unbounded Apéry problem to three affine choices per residue. It does not prove the alignment property. The source H3/H16 subfamilies still have to be isolated by an all-parameter argument.
