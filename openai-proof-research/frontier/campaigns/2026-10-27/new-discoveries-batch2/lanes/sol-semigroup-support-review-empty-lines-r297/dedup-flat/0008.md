## Ranking (research leads, not cleared nominations)

1. **Swanepoel finite c.s.c. Helly question** — strongest because every reduction is now rigorous and the conjectured value (k = 9) is sharp against the source's lower bound. Strongest classical objection: Hadwiger's ordered-transversal theorem covers the only regime where the constraints decouple, giving ≤ 6, and so cannot be the source of large obstructions; any positive proof must handle constraints whose vertex runs interact (sign-pattern formulation). Next step is in discovery.md §6.
2. **Bergman Question 6** — a single number for a single compact metric graph. Plan: (i) a numerical lower-bound search over piecewise-linear nonexpansive maps of the 12-edge skeleton into R²; (ii) an upper bound by the dual characterisation (for convex targets, map-rad = inf over probability measures μ of sup_x ∫ d(x,z) dμ — source abstract), sharpened for Euclidean targets. Objection: the 7/6 bound is attained for the hexagonal norm, so any Euclidean improvement must use strict convexity.
3. **Konyagin–Łaba arbitrary-slope polygonal Falconer question** — needs the post-2004 literature checked first; likely the hardest.
