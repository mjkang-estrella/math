1. **Radial-field Riesz support classification.** U§1 Questions1.1–1.3 pp2–3, Womersley–Saff. For Riesz kernel |x-y|^(-s)/s (logarithmic at0), -2<s<d, field proportional to |x|^alpha, alpha>max(0,-s), classify equilibrium support, including d-3<s<d and alpha below the stated sharp sphere threshold. These subquestions form ONE family. Mechanism: reduce Euler–Lagrange potential to radial integral and determine contact sets, allowing several shells and a bulk component. Theorem1.1 supplies a sharp sphere threshold but does not identify the subthreshold minimizer. No exact author hit found in compact screen. **Defer:** original Potential Analysis2024 proof body not retrieved; a radial ansatz alone proves no global minimality. Next calculation is the potential inequality for a two-shell candidate, not plotting particles.

2. **Repulsive equilibrium on polytopes.** U§3 Problems3.1–3.2 p7, Ryan Matzke: improve the source bounds s<=dim supp(mu)<=q-1 for cube equilibrium in the ranges -2<s<=q-2, q=1,...,d, and determine the support. ONE family, including face-sensitive convex bodies. Mechanism: second variation on each face and comparison across face dimensions. The body states the bound but supplies no cited underlying full proof in this section. **Defer:** “improve” is not a single sharp endpoint; merely restating extreme-point support for negative s is already covered in U. Different object from cube posets/CCT; no such old family is reopened.

3. **Spherical p-frame support discreteness.** U Conjecture4.1 p8, Bilyk–Glazyrin–Matzke–Park–Vlasiuk: for every p>2 not an even integer and every dimension, all probability-measure minimizers of integral integral |<x,y>|^p on S^(d-1) are discrete. Mechanism: exploit the first negative Gegenbauer coefficient and variational exclusion of accumulation. Empty interior, explicitly stated nearby, is weaker than discreteness. **Defer:** JFA2021 and RMI2022 original proof bodies unread here; no quantitative separation of support obtained. No actual compact author/object duplicate located. Generic finite-design existence cannot settle all minimizers.

4. **Flat-torus distance-energy transition.** U Problem4.2 p8: for T² with rho(x,y)=min_{k in Z²}||x-y+k||, determine maximizers of integral integral rho^alpha for alpha>0 and transition points between the source-known Haar case alpha1 and antipodal-pair case alpha2. Mechanism: compute Fourier coefficients of the periodic radial kernel; Haar maximizes exactly when every nonconstant coefficient is nonpositive, but identifying all non-Haar maximizers requires more. **Provisional alternative, unranked:** a coefficient sign obstruction could be decisive, but finite-frequency sign tests are insufficient. Actual newer same-author body https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25442v1,29October2025, defines tensor-product F(t1,...,td)=product f(ti), finite N energies; this is not the literal radial-distance probability energy. Its introduction/definitions were read; full universal-optimality proof was not audited. No complete current citation clearance.

5. **Positive-exponent persistence of Riesz minimization.** U Question5.1 p10, Peter Grabner: on a sphere or projective space with the stated metric rho equivalent to geodesic distance, if Haar sigma minimizes the Riesz s*-energy for some0<s*<d, must it minimize for EVERY s*<s<d? Do not silently fix chordal distance or take s*<=0. **Rank2 provisional, after assigned wavelet.** Original actual proof body https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.22104v1,29October2024, Bilyk–Grabner, Theorem8 pp18–19 and Remarks6–7: the proof integrates positive definite Gaussians from a negative exponent, and obtains the zero-exponent bridge by expanding exp(s(-log rho+C)) and singular Schur. Remark7 explicitly leaves positive starting exponent untreated, even discussing broader compact spaces. Mechanism: Jacobi coefficient positivity or an admissible metric with a coefficient changing sign above s*. Next calculation: characterize whether the metric triangle inequality prevents such a coefficient crossing. Strongest objection: conditional positivity at s* does not imply infinite divisibility of rho^(-s*); entrywise power>1 is not supplied by Schur. Current query returns this exact v1 and no retrieved direct resolution, but noisy/truncated searches and unavailable public-registry heads prevent openness/novelty clearance. Exact source scope has not been solved.

6. **Random interpolatory cubature weights.** U Question6.1 p11, Jordi Marzo. For N=dim P_n(S^r) random fundamental nodes (iid uniform or determinantal), quantify positivity or proximity to1/N of w_i=integral ell_i d sigma. Mechanism: inverse interpolation matrix concentration and conditional determinant formulas. Source gives known Fekete positivity and numerical cases but no quantitative random theorem. **Defer:** two sampling laws are ONE family; point-design existence and equal-weight quadrature do not imply positivity of THESE interpolation weights. No precise tail target is specified, so a later nomination must supply a concrete source-responsive theorem, not an arbitrary estimate.

7. **Explicit approximate Hadamard construction.** U Problem9.1 p15, Steinerberger: explicit all-dimension ±1 matrices with singular values between c sqrt n and C sqrt n for absolute0<c<C, seeking simple construction with small controlled C/c. Actual original https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13202v1,20February2024, pp2–3 proof read: diagonalize a circulant by DFT and take coefficients of a flat Littlewood polynomial. This ALREADY proves existence, including circulant restriction. **Defer, no rank:** source asks construction quality; recycling the Fourier bridge or Rudin–Shapiro upper bound is not a fresh result, and asking condition number->1 would strengthen to a different hard question. Compact Hadamard hits were generic determinant/q-separation references, not an exact same construction, but this does not clear literature.

8. **Nonsmooth two-convex planar sections.** P§1 pp1–2: for every simply connected open two-convex Omega subset R^n (three faces of a tetrahedron imply its entire inclusion), is each connected component of Omega intersect any affine2-plane simply connected? Mechanism: fill planar loops by controlled tetrahedral moves avoiding assumptions on smooth boundary. Actual source proves/argues smooth-boundary and n3 cases and constructs a contractible two-convex Omega in R4 with a NONsimply connected3-plane section, invalidating naive smoothing and all-dimensional section upgrades. **Defer:** no new tetrahedral filling invariant; original referenced topology proof not retrieved. This is not a BCC/projection-packing target.

9. **Finite isotropy conjugacy bound.** P§5 pp4–5: every effective isometric properly discontinuous Euclidean action Gamma on E^n should have at most2^n conjugacy classes of maximal finite subgroups. Mechanism: send each maximal finite subgroup to its simple singular stratum and adapt the Danzer–Grünbaum nonobtuse-set bound. Source explains this reduction but only expects the extremal strata to be points. **Defer:** no bridge from positive-dimensional strata to nonobtuse points, so invoking the classical2^n theorem alone is incomplete. Original Fujioka/Lebedeva proof bodies unread; no fresh rank from a reference list.

10. **Arbitrarily cheap external connections of continua.** P§6 pp5–6: for every compact connected K subset R^n, p,q in K and epsilon>0, is there a curve connecting p,q with length(gamma outside K)<epsilon? Mechanism: cover grid intersections by segments with small exterior length, recursively connect small components. Actual source provides the planar construction/proof but says n>=3 remains open. **Defer:** in higher dimensions the grid boundary is not a finite collection of short segments; the planar argument is not a complete dimension-free bridge. This differs from requiring a path in K, which pseudoarcs forbid. No priority assigned to the known planar case.

11. **Smooth optical fiber rigidity.** P§17 pp10–11: for smooth perfectly reflecting tubes transmitting almost every entering ray to the other end and almost every interior ray out an end, must the tube admit planar orthogonal slices? In particular can the ends be noncongruent? ONE billiard-rigidity family. Mechanism: seek a conserved longitudinal momentum from a parallel normal framing, or construct a smooth scattering tube violating it. Actual body gives the parallel-frame construction and notes piecewise-smooth reassembly permits equidecomposable noncongruent ends; equal end area follows classically from Liouville. **Defer:** equal area and piecewise-smooth constructions do not answer smooth rigidity; no new invariant obtained, and older original MathOverflow discussions were not retrieved.
