                                                                      On Line Sum Optimization
                                                                                         Shmuel Onn ∗




arXiv:2012.02617v1 [math.OC] 4 Dec 2020
                                                                                            Abstract
                                                        We show that the column sum optimization problem, of finding a (0, 1)-matrix with
                                                    prescribed row sums which minimizes the sum of evaluations of given functions at its
                                                    column sums, can be solved in polynomial time, either when all functions are the same
                                                    or when all row sums are bounded by any constant. We conjecture that the more general
                                                    line sum optimization problem, of finding a matrix minimizing the sum of given functions
                                                    evaluated at its row sums and column sums, can also be solved in polynomial time.
                                                    Keywords: majorization, column sum, row sum, matrix, degree sequence, graph
                                                    MSC: 05A, 15A, 51M, 52A, 52B, 52C, 62H, 68Q, 68R, 68U, 68W, 90B, 90C

    The line sum problem is in turn a special case of the following degree sequence optimization
problem: given a graph H = (V, E), integer e ≤ |E|, and functions fv : {0, 1, . . . , dv (H)} → Z
for v ∈ V , where Pdv (H) is the degree of v in H, find a subgraph G = (V, F ) ⊆ H with e
edges minimizing v∈V fv (dv (G)). Indeed, identifying matrices A ∈ {0, 1}m×n with graphs
G = (V, F ) where V = {u1 , . . . , um } ⊎ {w1 , . . . , wn } and F = {{ui , wj } : Ai,j = 1}, the
line sum problem reduces to the degree sequence problem with H = Km,n the complete
bipartite graph. In the case of H = Kn the complete graph and all functions the same,
fv = f for all v ∈ V , the problem was recently shown in [3] to be polynomial time solvable,
using the characterization of degree sequences by Erdős and Gallai [5]. For general graphs
H, the problem was shown in [1] to be NP-hard already when fv (x) = −x2 for all v ∈ V ,
but is polynomial time solvable if the functions are convex [1, 4]. If m or n are fixed, the
problem for any H ⊆ Km,n , and hence in particular the line sum problem, for any functions,
is polynomial time solvable [4]. We conjecture that the line sum problem, which is the degree
sequence problem over H = Km,n , as well as the degree sequence problem over H = Kn , are
polynomial time solvable for arbitrary functions at the vertices, not necessarily identical.

    We use a characterization by Ryser [7] of (0, 1)-matrices with prescribed line sums.

Proposition 2.1 [7] There is a matrix A ∈ {0, 1}m×n with row sum and column sum tuples
r, c if and only if, permuting c to be nonincreasing, it is majorized by the conjugate tuple d.

   For instance, if m = n = 4 and r = (3, 3, 2, 2), then d = (4, 4, 2, 0), and c = (3, 3, 3, 1) is
majorized by d so there exists A ∈ {0, 1}4×4 with row sum tuple r and column sum tuple c.
    Next we note that a matrix with given line sums r and c can be efficiently obtained.

Lemma 2.2 Let r = (r1 , . . . , rm ) and let c = (c1 , . . . , cn ) be such that, permuting c to be
nonincreasing, it is majorized by the tuple d = (d1 , . . . , dn ) conjugate to r. Then a matrix A
in {0, 1}m×n with row sum tuple r and column sum tuple c is computable in polynomial time.

Proof. The problem is solvable either by the efficient simple Gale-Ryser algorithm, see [2,
Chapter 3], or by network flows as follows. Define a directed graph with capacities on the
edges as follows. There are vertices s, t, u1 , . . . , um , and w1 , . . . , wn . There are edges [s, ui ]
for i ∈ [m] with capacity ri , edges [ui , wj ] for i ∈ [m] and j ∈ [n] with capacity 1, and edges
[wj , t] for j ∈ [n] with capacity cj . Then, as is well known, a maximum nonnegative integer
flow from s to t can be computed in polynomial time, see e.g. [8]. Then A is read off from
the maximum flow by taking Ai,j to be the flow on edge [ui , wj ] for all i ∈ [m] and j ∈ [n].
