   Several weaker or more specialized coefficientwise results are known.
Harada and Precup proved graded e-positivity in the abelian case [6,
Corollary 7.26]; Cho and Huh gave a combinatorial proof and explicit
formulas for a subfamily [2, Theorems 3.3 and 4.2]; and Abreu and Nigro
proved e-positivity and e-unimodality for every abelian Hessenberg function
[1, Proposition 3.6]. Colmenarejo, Morales, and Panova gave an elementary
treatment of the relevant q-hit expansions and compared their normalizations
[3, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 and Proposition B.1]. Related manifestly positive
formulas for abelian elementary coefficients were obtained independently
by Lee and Soh [10, Theorem 23 and Corollary 25]. Previously established
log-concavity cases include paths [1, Proposition 5.2], the closed-form family
of Cho and Huh [2, Theorem 4.2], lollipop graphs [8, Proposition 4.4], [1,
discussion following Proposition 5.2], and graphs obtained by gluing cliques
of sizes a and b at one vertex (denoted Ka,b in the cited papers) [14, Corol-
lary 4.14], [12, Example 4.3]. None of these results establishes log-concavity
for the full low-rank abelian class treated here. At q = 1, Matherne, Morales,
and Selover proved that every finite-variable specialization of the ordinary
chromatic symmetric function of an abelian Dyck path is Lorentzian [11,
Theorem 6.8]; that multivariate statement is distinct from log-concavity of
each elementary coefficient as a polynomial in q.
   There are two complementary phenomena. The universal log-concavity
assertion is false: a connected graph on 13 vertices has an elementary co-
efficient whose first three nonzero entries are 1, 6, 38. On the other hand,
log-concavity holds uniformly on a large class naturally singled out by the
abelian q-hit theory. The purpose of this paper is to establish the positive
theorem, explain its algebraic mechanism, and contrast that failure with the
structured positive regime.
   Let h : [n] → [n] be a Hessenberg function and let λ be the partition
whose transpose has parts (λt )i = n − h(i), with zero parts omitted. When h
is abelian, the complement of the associated natural unit interval graph is
Ferrers bipartite. We call

basis coefficient cµ (q) in (1.1) is a nonnegative log-concave polynomial in q

                     3. The exact rank-three reduction
   We now reduce the genuine complement-Ferrers rank-three case of theo-
rem 1.1 to theorem 2.4. The coefficient extraction uses the abelian support
statement and the specialization from theorems 2.2 and 2.3; in particular, no
inversion formula for the full elementary basis is needed.
3.1. The width-three orientation. The width λ1 of the partition in (2.4)
is the number of entries of h that are strictly smaller than n. Transposition
exchanges λ1 and ℓ(λ) and preserves XGh (x; q). Hence a genuine rank-three
shape may be oriented so that λ1 = 3.

            4. Convolution and finite-window smoothing
  This section isolates the two sequence-theoretic tools used throughout the
proof. All sequences are extended by zero outside their displayed support.
Lemma 4.1 (Convolution closure). If two nonnegative log-concave sequences
have interval support, then their convolution is nonnegative and log-concave
with interval support.
Proof. For an interval-supported nonnegative sequence a, adjacent
log-concavity is equivalent to total nonnegativity of order two of its
lower-triangular Toeplitz matrix
                                   T (a)ij = ai−j .
Indeed, on the positive support the adjacent inequalities say that the ratios
aj /aj−1 are weakly decreasing. Cross-multiplication gives every 2 × 2 Toeplitz
minor; conversely, the adjacent minors give the original inequalities, and the
zero padding is harmless because the support is an interval. The Toeplitz
matrix of a convolution is the matrix product
                               T (a ∗ b) = T (a)T (b).
All matrices may be truncated beyond the finitely many rows and columns
meeting a chosen minor, so the following application of Cauchy–Binet is finite.
By Cauchy–Binet, every 2 × 2 minor of this product is a sum of products of
nonnegative 2 × 2 minors. Thus a ∗ b is log-concave. Its support is the sum
of two integer intervals and is therefore an interval.                      □
   In particular, every product of q-integers is log-concave, and a monomial
factor only translates the support.

                10. Consequences and open problems
   The two main results separate the false unrestricted conjecture from a
uniform positive regime: complement-Ferrers rank at most three forces coeffi-
cientwise e-log-concavity in the abelian family, whereas the counterexample
is nonabelian. We do not suggest that every counterexample must be non-
abelian.
   Complement-Ferrers rank four is the first abelian layer not covered by
the present method. This is an open boundary, not a failure boundary: we
know of no counterexample of complement-Ferrers rank four. Rank-three
interpolation is cubic and produces the four-factor difference in (1.3); the
next layer should produce higher interpolation kernels for which the common-
center and wall methods may retain useful structure. Two concrete problems
are therefore:
(1) determine whether every elementary coefficient is log-concave for all
    abelian Hessenberg functions; and
(2) identify graph-theoretic conditions on nonabelian natural unit interval
    graphs that force log-concavity or systematically permit failure.
