     Extended weak order in all rank three Coxeter groups 
     2026-10-28T20:32:59Z 
     
     
     We prove that extended weak order is a lattice for all rank 3 Coxeter groups. This was previously known in finite and affine types, and in the universal Coxeter group of rank 3. All other cases are new to this paper. As in earlier work, the proof proceeds by showing that all rank 3 Coxeter arrangements are clean arrangements, meaning that every biclosed set is weakly separated from its complement by a hyperplane. As a consequence, we also deduce Dyer's Conjecture A for all rank 3 Coxeter groups, which asserts that cover relations in extended weak order are given by toggling a single root.

     Primitive flag-transitive generalized quadrangles 
     2026-10-28T20:15:15Z 
     
     
     Let $\mathcal{Q}$ be a finite thick generalized quadrangle of order $(s,t)$ with a flag-transitive automorphism group $G$. A well-known conjecture of Kantor from the early 1990s asserts that up to duality, $\mathcal{Q}$ is either of classical type, or it is one of two sporadic examples of order $(3,5)$ or $(15,17)$. Although this fundamental problem in finite geometry remains open, it has been resolved in some special cases. For example, a theorem of Buekenhout and Van Maldeghem from 1994 proves Kantor's conjecture under the stronger assumption that $G$ acts distance-transitively on the set of points of $\mathcal{Q}$.
  In this paper, we study Kantor's conjecture in the setting where $G$ acts primitively on both the points and lines of $\mathcal{Q}$. Here a theorem of Bamberg et al. implies that $G$ is almost simple with socle $X$ a finite simple group of Lie type, and several special cases where the Lie rank of $X$ is at most $2$ have recently been settled in a sequence of papers. Our main theorem resolves the problem for all almost simple groups of Lie type, thereby completing the proof of Kantor's conjecture for generalized quadrangles with a flag-transitive automorphism group acting primitively on points and lines.

     On a Conjecture of Fiedorowicz 
     2026-10-28T19:40:57Z 
     
     
     Fiedorowicz conjectured that every finite simply connected CW complex is homotopy equivalent to the classifying space of a finite monoid. We prove this conjecture. Our main construction takes a finite monoid M and a map from a finite connected CW complex into the classifying space of M, and produces a new finite monoid whose classifying space is homotopy equivalent to the mapping cone of the given map. Applying this construction successively to the attaching maps of a finite CW model proves the result. As an explicit example of the algorithm, we construct a 1795-element monoid whose classifying space is homotopy equivalent to two dimensional complex projective space.

     Irrationality of the conjugacy growth on Heisenberg groups 
     2026-10-27T14:41:24Z 
     
     
     We demonstrate that the conjugacy growth series for the Heisenberg groups $H_n(\mathbb{Z})$ is never algebraic, irrespective of the generating set. This makes progress towards the conjecture that all finitely presented groups that are not virtually abelian have irrational conjugacy growth series. Contrary to the group $H_3(\mathbb{Z})$, where the conjugacy growth is $n^2\log(n)$, the conjugacy growth of the other Heisenberg groups is polynomial, making these groups the first known nilpotent groups where the irrationality does not follow from the asymptotic growth.

     Realizing graph automorphisms as geometric symmetries of straight line drawings 
     2026-10-26T23:19:27Z 
     
     
     Given a finite graph $G$, we characterize the automorphisms and automorphism groups of $G$ that can be realized as geometric symmetries and symmetry groups of straight line drawings of $G$ in the plane. In doing so, we correct a previous version of this characterization from the literature. As an application, we show that the graphs recently constructed by the first two authors to disprove the Polycirculant Conjecture, which have orders $16{,}464^k$ for each positive integer $k$, are the first known vertex-transitive graphs with no non-trivial realizable geometric symmetries. We also construct infinite families of graphs that exhibit all possible kinds of geometric symmetry.

     The Andreadakis conjecture 
     2026-10-26T21:27:15Z 
     
     
     For $n\ge2$, let $F_n$ be the free group of rank $n$, and let $\IA(F_n)$ be the group of automorphisms of $F_n$ acting trivially on its abelianization. Two natural filtrations of $\IA(F_n)$ are its lower central series $γ_c(\IA(F_n))$ and the Andreadakis filtration $\mathrm{I}_{c+1}\mathrm{A}(F_n)$, consisting of automorphisms acting trivially on $F_n/γ_{c+1}(F_n)$, $c \geq 1$. Andreadakis conjectured that these filtrations coincide. We disprove this conjecture for every $n\ge3$ and $c\ge4$.

     We construct the first examples of uncountable families of residually finite duality groups of type $FP$, all of which have isomorphic profinite completions, while being pairwise not quasi-isometric. The main new tool is a strong separability result for Bestvina-Brady groups, allowing the extension of any finite quotient of many Bestvina-Brady subgroups to a finite quotient of the whole group.      Triple parabolic cosets have unique Bruhat minima 
     2026-10-22T17:34:42Z 
     
     
     A folklore result says that any parabolic double coset $W_{I} w W_{J}$ in a Coxeter group $W$ is an interval of the Bruhat order, provided that $W_{I}$ and $W_{J}$ are finite. We include a self-contained proof, without assuming that $W$ is finite. Furthermore, we prove that each "triple coset" $W_{I} u W_{J} v W_{K}$ has a Bruhat minimum and -- if $W_{I}, W_{J}, W_{K}$ are finite -- a Bruhat maximum, but (in general) is not a Bruhat interval. This proves and extends a conjecture on MathOverflow #472072.

     The cubic pancake graphs are Cayley graphs over the symmetric group Sn generated by three prefix reversals. There is the folllowing open problem: characterize all the sets of three prefix reversals that generate Sn. Blanco and Golubyatnikov give a complete solution of the problem when any of the two smallest or the two largest lengths but n are included in a triple of prefix reversals. According to their conjectures, we give some conditions implying a triple of prefix reversals does not generate Sn. Moreover, Using the symmetric groups that can already be generated by three prefix reversals, we can derive some recurrence conditions.
