The single pivot is Hogben–Lin–Olesky–van den Driessche, 1807.04874v1, Conjecture3.8. Full literal quantifiers and source-body scopes are in SCOUTING entry2. ## Mechanism A: structured direct-sum masking

Use the source's explicit 3×3 P,Q in Example3.1 as a controlled model of correlations: neither has a unique sepr-sequence, but their direct sum does. This is a public phenomenon, not a candidate discovery. Enumerating determinant monomials exactly gives the signed-determinant status of all principal subpatterns of P⊕Q. A determinant has fixed nonzero sign precisely when all its nonzero permutation monomials have that sign; if both signs occur, a selected matching can be made dominant by making its entries large and all other entries small, so the determinant can take both signs. Zero means no matching. No floating-point evaluation is involved.

For every order with any ambiguous principal subpattern, the exact expansion finds three *fixed* subpatterns with signs+,−,0. Hence this published masking construction meets, rather than defeats, the conjectured criterion. sepr-result.json records all index sets. The failure is substantive: uniqueness of a combined profile is too weak a design principle; the fixed-sign witnesses arise from the blocks and their products before one can challenge necessity. A useful new finite construction would have to force both signs in every realization without supplying fixed principal minors of both signs at that order. The source's existing examples do not do this.

## Mechanism B: simultaneous dominance / perturbation

Try to eliminate all ambiguous negative minors together. For independent positive magnitudes z_e at nonzero positions, each minor is a signed sum of matching monomials. A logarithmic scaling z_e=R^(w_e) with integer weights can select unique leading matchings, yielding a finite strict system of integer linear inequalities. A feasible common weight selecting only positive leading matchings for ambiguous minors would, for large R, remove negative minors when there is no fixed negative minor, contradicting uniqueness. The analogous negative-leading construction handles the missing-positive case.

The exact shared-entry test in sepr_checks.py shows why the source's order2 independent-knob argument does not carry over: the entry(1,2) contributes simultaneously to the principal determinants on{1,2,3} and{1,2,4}; their matching inequalities couple. Treating each ambiguous minor's preferred monomial as independently assignable is invalid. Generic perturbation guarantees simultaneous *nonvanishing* (the source's Lemma3.10), but it does not choose a common desired sign. It cannot fill this gap.

Precise missing non-equivalent sufficient lemma: given a sign pattern and an order k with ambiguous principal minors but no fixed negative minor, there exists a common weight vector such that each ambiguous k-minor has a unique **positive** leading matching (or an alternative explicit positive-magnitude realization making all such minors nonnegative). The weight formulation is a stronger sufficient condition than the conjecture and has extra combinatorial structure; no necessity or equivalence is claimed. Failure of one chosen matching system would not refute the original conjecture. Establishing feasibility for all such patterns is not done, nor is a pattern with an independent all-real mixed-sign certificate constructed. Thus both mechanisms stop at a concrete sign-coherence problem, not at a proof disguised as a reformulation.

No second pivot, no broad brute-force sweep, and no universal inference from finite numerical tests. Presently unresolved status is bounded to the frozen source and limited live arXiv query; current-registry/citing-body completeness remains open.
