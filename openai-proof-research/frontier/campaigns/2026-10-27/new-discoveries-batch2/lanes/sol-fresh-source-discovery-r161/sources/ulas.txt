                                                    Abstract. Let Bn (t) be the nth Stern polynomial, i.e., the nth term of
                                                    the sequence defined recursively as B0 (t) = 0, B1 (t) = 1 and B2n (t) =
                                                    tBn (t), B2n+1 (t) = Bn (t) + Bn−1 (t) for n ∈ N. It is well know that ith
                                                    coefficient in the polynomial Bn (t) counts the number of hyperbinary repre-

                                                    the existence of odd solutions of the congruence
                                                                                        te(n) − 1

                                                    where m ∈ N≥2 and r ∈ {0, . . . , m − 1} are fixed and e(n) = deg Bn (t).

                                                    present results of some numerical computations.

   The paper of Klavžar, Milutinović and Petr motivated a lot of research devoted
to various properties of Stern polynomials (see for example [13, 14, 9, 10, 11, 4,
2, 3] and reference given therein). However, it seems that only the paper [11]
deals with the partition properties of the coefficients of Bn (t). More precisely, the
computation of a(e(n), n − 1) in terms of the (unique) binary expansion of n is
presented [11, Theorem 1]. This is an interesting result which gives value of the
number of hyperbinary representations of n − 1 with maximal possible number of
1’s. As a corollary, Schinzel obtained a striking result which says that for any given
m ∈ N≥2 , the density of these n ∈ N+ such that a(e(n), n − 1) ≡ 0 (mod m) is
equal to 1 (and thus lim supn→+∞ a(e(n), n−1) = +∞). The cited result motivated
our research. However, instead of trying to obtain congruences properties for the
individual coefficient a(i, n − 1), we study the more tractable problem of finding the
numbers n for which all coefficients a(i, n − 1), 1 ≤ i ≤ e(n), are congruent to some
fixed value r (mod m). This is equivalent to finding solutions of the polynomial
congruence

This is a strong property and a question arises whether for a given pair (r, m)
it is possible to find a solution of (1). In fact, we are interested in finding, for
a given pair (r, m) an infinite family of solutions. However, we are interested in
non-trivial families only. More precisely, by a non-trivial family of solutions of (1)
we understand a sequence (an )n∈N of positive odd integers, such that the set of
coefficients of all polynomials Ban (t), n ∈ N, is infinite. This assumption is quite
strong but we believe that that it is reasonable due to the fact that for a given
odd k, the set of coefficients of all polynomials (B2n −k (t))n∈N≥blog2 kc+1 is finite.
Indeed, this is a consequence of the identity
                                        tn−m − 1
                      B2n −k (t) = Bk (t)         − Bl (t)tn−m ,
                                           t−1
where k = 2m + l, obtained in a recent paper of Dilcher, Kidwai and Tomkins [3].
In particular, the number of different coefficients of all polynomials B2n −k (t) is
bounded by k + l, and thus the family (B2n −k (t))n∈N≥blog2 kc+1 is trivial for all k.
For given m ≥ 3 and r ∈ {1, 2} one can also obtain trivial families of solutions of
(1). Indeed, if r = 1, then for n ∈ N we have the identity
                                                n

and the congruence (1) is trivially satisfied. Similarly, if r = 2 we have the identity
                                                  X n

                                                    i=1
and thus for any m ∈ N≥3 there are infinitely many solutions of (1). The existence
of examples of this kind is the reason why we are interested in non-trivial families
only. Thus, in the sequel, by a family of solutions of (1) we mean a non-trivial
family.
   After the above discussion let us describe the content of the paper in some detail.
In Section 2 we prove the existence of an infinite family of non-trivial sequences
(pk,n )n∈N+ , k ∈ N≥2 , of solutions of the congruence (1) for m = 2 and r = 0.
Moreover, we present a lower bound for the number of solutions. In case of m = 2
and r = 1 we prove the existence of a four non-trivial sequence of solutions. We
also present results of our numerical computations.
   In Section 3 we deal with (1) for m = 3. Our main result states that there is a
non-trivial sequence of solutions corresponding to r = 0.
   Finally, in Section 4 we present some examples of solutions of (1) for certain
values m ∈ {4, . . . , 10}, r ∈ {0, . . . , m − 1} and formulate some additional questions
and conjectures.
