11:                                                   Abstract. We analyze the Brill–Noether theory of trivalent graphs
12:                                                   and multigraphs having largest possible automorphism group in a fixed
13:                                                   genus. For trivalent multigraphs with loops of genus at least 3, we show
14:                                                   that there exists a graph with maximal automorphism group which is
15:                                                   Brill–Noether special. We prove similar results for multigraphs without
16:                                                   loops of genus at least 6, as well as simple graphs of genus at least 7.
17:                                                   This analysis yields counterexamples, in any sufficiently large genus, to
18:                                                   a conjecture of Caporaso.

23:                                                                            1. Introduction
24:                                             A graph Γ of genus g is said to be Brill–Noether general if for all
25:                                           positive integers r and d such that the number
26:                                                                     ρrd (g) = g − (r + 1)(g − d + r)
27:                                           is negative, there exist no effective divisors on Γ of degree d and rank at least
28:                                           r. We begin by recalling the following conjecture of Caporaso [5, Conjecture
29:                                           6.6(1)].
30:                                           Conjecture 1. Assume g ⩾ 2. Let Γ be a trivalent graph of genus g with
31:                                           largest possible automorphism group (among trivalent graphs of genus g).
32:                                           Then Γ is Brill–Noether general.
33:                                           The conjecture is motivated by an analogy between the moduli space Mg
34:                                           of Deligne-Mumford stable curves and the moduli space Mtrop    g    of tropical
35:                                           curves. The latter is the space parametrizing genus g metric graphs with
36:                                           integer vertex-weights. The divisor theory of algebraic curves is related
37:                                           to that of graphs by means of Baker’s Specialization Lemma [3] and its
38:                                           generalizations [2].
39:                                              For fixed g, there is a natural order reversing bijection between the set
40:                                           of strata of these two moduli spaces. The classical moduli space Mg is
41:                                           stratified by the combinatorial type of the dual graphs of stable curves,
42:                                           while Mtrop
43:                                                    g    is stratified by the underlying combinatorial type of the metric

54: curve has no automorphisms, so Caporaso proposes that its analog should
55: be a trivalent graph with the greatest possible number of automorphisms.
56: Since a general curve is Brill–Noether general, Caporaso conjectures that
57: the trivalent graphs with largest number of automorphisms are also Brill–
58: Noether general. Here we prove that in any sufficiently large genus, there
59: exists a trivalent graph (or multigraph) with largest possible automorphism
60: group that is not Brill–Noether general.
61:    In what follows, we will refer to trivalent graphs or multigraphs achieving
62: largest possible automorphism group as maximally symmetric.
