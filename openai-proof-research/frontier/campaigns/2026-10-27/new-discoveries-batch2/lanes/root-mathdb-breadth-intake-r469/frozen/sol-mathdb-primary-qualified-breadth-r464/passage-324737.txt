11:                                              A strong edge-coloring of a graph G is an edge-coloring so that no edge can be adjacent to two edges
12:                                           with the same color. So in a strong edge-coloring, every color class gives an induced matching. The strong
13:                                           chromatic index χ′s (G) is the minimum number of colors needed to color E(G) strongly. This notion was
14:                                           introduced by Fouquet and Jolivet (1983, [5]). Erdős and Nešetřil during a seminar in Prague in 1985
15:                                           proposed some open problems, one of which is the following
16:                                           Conjecture 1 (Erdős and Nešetřil, 1985). If G is a simple graph with maximum degree ∆, then χ′s (G) ≤
17:                                           5∆2 /4 if ∆ is even, and χ′s (G) ≤ (5∆2 − 2∆ + 1)/4 if ∆ is odd.
18:                                              This conjecture is true for ∆ ≤ 3 ([1, 6]). Cranston [4] showed that χ′s (G) ≤ 22 for ∆ = 4. Chung,
19:                                           Gyárfás, Trotter, and Tuza (1990, [3]) showed that the upper bounds are exactly the numbers of edges in
20:                                           2K2 -free graphs. Molloy and Reed [8] proved that graphs with sufficient large maximum degree ∆ has strong
21:                                           chromatic index at most 1.998∆2. For more results see [9] (Chapter 6, problem 17).
22:                                              A graph is k-degenerate if every subgraph has minimum degree at most k. Chang and Narayanan (2012,
23:                                           [2]) recently proved that a 2-degenerate graph with maximum degree ∆ has strong chromatic index at most
24:                                           10∆ − 10. Luo and the author in [7] improved the upper bound to 8∆ − 4.
25:                                              In [2], the following conjecture was made
26:                                           Conjecture 2 (Chang and Narayanan, [2]). There exists an absolute constant c such that for any k-
27:                                           degenerate graphs G with maximum degree ∆, χ′s (G) ≤ ck 2 ∆. Furthermore, the k 2 may be replaced by
28:                                           k.
29:                                              In this paper, we prove a stronger form of the conjecture. Unlike the priming processes in[2, 7], we find a
30:                                           special ordering of the edges and by using a greedy coloring obtain the following result.
31:                                           Theorem 1. The strong chromatic index for each k-degenerate graph with maximum degree ∆ is at most
32:                                           (4k − 2)∆ − k(2k − 1) + 1.
33:                                             Thus, 2-degenerate graphs have strong chromatic index at most 6∆ − 5.
