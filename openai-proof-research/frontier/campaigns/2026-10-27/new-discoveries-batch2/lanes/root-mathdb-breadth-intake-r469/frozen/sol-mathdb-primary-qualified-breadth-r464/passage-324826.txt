56: The entries of a bistochastic matrix P ∈ Bn are the probabilities of
57: transition between the states of a classical physical system. Transi-
58: tions amplitudes in quantum physics are complex numbers, and the
59: transition probabilities are their squared absolute values. The tran-

61: unitary n × n matrix U. Let P = ν(U) be the matrix of squared
62: norms of the entries in U. Then P is a bistochastic matrix describing
63: the corresponding classical system. Thus, a bistochastic matrix P is
64: unistochastic if the classical system described by P can be quantized,

101: subscripts and superscripts. For instance, let P = [pji ] be a n × n
102: matrix. The lower (resp. upper) index stands for the column (resp.
103: row). The entries of a matrix may be numbers, vectors, etc.
