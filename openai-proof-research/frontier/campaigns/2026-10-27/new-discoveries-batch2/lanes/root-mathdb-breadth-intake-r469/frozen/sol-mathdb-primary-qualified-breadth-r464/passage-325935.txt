27:                                                                                     1. Introduction
28:                                              Let G = (V, E) be a graph. An equitable k-coloring of G is a proper k-coloring whose
29:                                           color classes differ in size by at most one. A factor of G is a set F of disjoint subgraphs
30:                                           whose union spans G. The subgraphs in a factor are called tiles. A factor F is an H-factor if
31:                                           each of its tiles is a copy of H. If |G| = sk, then the color classes of an equitable k-coloring
32:                                           form a K s -factor. Our story starts in 1963:
33:                                           Theorem 1 (Corrádi & Hajnal 1963 [2]). Every graph G with |G| = n = 3k and δ(G) ≥ 23 n
34:                                           has a K3 -factor.
35:                                              The following generalization was conjectured by Erdős [7] in 1963, and proved seven years
36:                                           later:
37:                                           Theorem 2 (Hajnal & Szemerédi 1970 [9]). Every graph G with |G| = n = ks and δ(G) ≥
38:                                           (1 − 1/s)n has a Ks -factor.
39:                                            Since |G| = ∆(G) + δ(G) + 1, Theorem 2 has the following complementary form, in which
40:                                           Hajnal and Szemerédi stated their proof of Erdős’ conjecture.
41:                                           Theorem 3 (Hajnal & Szemerédi 1970 [9]). Every graph G with ∆(G) ≤ k − 1 has an
42:                                           equitable k-coloring.
