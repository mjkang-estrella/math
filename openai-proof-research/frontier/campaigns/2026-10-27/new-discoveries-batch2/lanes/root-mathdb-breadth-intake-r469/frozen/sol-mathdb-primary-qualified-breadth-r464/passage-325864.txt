62: Theorem 1.1 [45, Theorems 1.1–1.4] Chromatic roots are dense in the whole complex
63: plane. Indeed, even the chromatic roots of the generalized theta graphs Θ(s,p) are dense in
64: the whole complex plane with the possible exception of the disc |q − 1| < 1.1

66:     Biggs, Damerell and Sands [5] were the first to suggest, in the early 1970s, that the
67: degree (i.e. valency) of a regular graph might be relevant to the location of its chromatic
68: roots. They conjectured (on rather limited evidence) the existence of a function f such that
69: the chromatic roots of a regular graph of degree r lie in the disc |q| ≤ f (r). Two decades later,
70: Brenti, Royle and Wagner [18] extended this conjecture to not-necessarily-regular graphs of
71: maximum degree r.2 This latter conjecture was finally confirmed by one of us, who used
72: cluster-expansion techniques from statistical physics to show that taking f (r) ≈ 8r would
73: suffice:
74: 
75: Theorem 1.2 [44, Corollary 5.3 and Proposition 5.4] The chromatic roots of a graph
76: of maximum degree ∆ lie in the disc |q| ≤ 7.963907∆.
