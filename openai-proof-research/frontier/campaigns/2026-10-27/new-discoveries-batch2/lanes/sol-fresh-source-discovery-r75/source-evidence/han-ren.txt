                                                                                    When does Σ(A) = G?

                                               The first question is classical. Following work of Erdős and Heilbronn, Diderrich and Mann,

    For fixed-length covering, several results are known in specific cases. Gallardo, Grekos,
Habsieger, Hennecart, Landreau and Plagne studied two- and three-fold restricted sums in Z/nZ.
In particular, they proved three-fold covering under the condition |A| > n/2 and conjectured
that, for odd n, this could be lowered to 2n/5 + O(1) [11, Theorem 3.1 and Conjecture 3.9]. Lev
subsequently resolved the three-fold covering conjecture for arbitrary finite abelian groups [23,
Main Theorem].
    For groups of even order, Roth and Lempel showed that, apart from the exceptional endpoint
cases, |A| > |G|/2 implies Σk (A) = G throughout the middle range [26, Theorem 3.1 and
Remark 3.1].

that, in an odd-order group, |A| ≥ (N + 3)/2 implies Σ2 (A) = G, and they expected the same

    There is also a direct application to MDS elliptic codes. Let E/Fq be an elliptic curve.
Since E(Fq ) is a finite abelian group, the MDS condition for an elliptic evaluation code can be
expressed as the failure of a prescribed group element to lie in a fixed-length restricted sumset
of the evaluation set; see Lemma 7.1. Covering results for E(Fq ) therefore translate directly into
upper bounds for the lengths of such codes.
    Li, Wan and Zhang conjectured a one-half maximal-length scale for MDS elliptic codes [24,

    The proofs use two main ideas. For the one-third theorem, restricted-sum estimates and
Kneser’s theorem are used to show that suitable sums of fibres fill the required cosets, while
Lemma 2.5 chooses distinct unused elements whose images have any prescribed sum in the
quotient. For the exact least-prime threshold, if some group element has few representations,
arithmetic removal and Kneser’s theorem force almost all of A into one coset of an index-p
subgroup. The one-third theorem then gives the required covering inside the subgroup, while the
elements outside it are used to choose the required quotient class. If every group element has
many representations, Lemma 5.2 produces a subset T ⊆ A with |T | ≤ |A|/2 and Σ2p−2 (T ) = G,
from which all longer lengths in the required range follow.
    The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects the basic tools, and Section 3 treats
the small restricted sums needed later. Section 4 proves the sharp one-third theorem, while
Section 5 proves the exact least-prime threshold. Section 6 deals with shorter prescribed lengths,
and Section 7 gives the application to MDS elliptic codes.

    We shall use one two-fold covering fact directly from Gallardo et al. [11, Lemma 2.2]: if
K has odd order and X ⊂ K satisfies |X| > (|K| + 1)/2, then Σ2 (X) = K. Indeed, for every
z ∈ K, |X ∩ (z − X)| ≥ 2|X| − |K| > 1, while 2x = z has a unique solution in K. Thus the
intersection contains x ̸= z − x, giving a restricted pair of sum z. This is precisely the odd-order
specialization of their lemma.

in Theorem 2.10. Hence part (a) of that theorem covers k ∈ {3, n − 3}, and part (b) covers every
4 ≤ k ≤ n − 4. Thus Σk (P ) = G throughout 3 ≤ k ≤ n − 3, contradicting Lemma 7.1. Hence
n ≤ N/2.
