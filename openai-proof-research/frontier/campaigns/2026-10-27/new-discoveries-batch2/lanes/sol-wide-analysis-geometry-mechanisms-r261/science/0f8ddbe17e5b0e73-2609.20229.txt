                                                                                Abstract
                                                                                                       P
                                                    Let X ∼ Mult(N ; p1 , . . . , pn ) with pi > 0 and  i pi = 1. We study
                                                Pr{X = m} for lattice points near N p from two complementary view-
                                                points. First, we give an exact integer-mode criterion: the multinomial
                                                mode is the Jefferson–D’Hondt divisor apportionment, given when the
                                                mode is unique by mi = ⌊(N + d)pi ⌋, d ∈ [0, n). For n ≥ 3 the mode need
                                                not be obtained by rounding each N pi to a neighbouring integer; the excess
                                                mi − ⌈N pi ⌉ can reach n − 2, so the two-sided localisation |mi − N pi | < 1
                                                stated in a standard reference is not valid in general. Second, by writing
                                                the mass as a gamma quotient with unequal scalings, we derive the com-
                                                plete local expansion of Pr{X = m} in integer powers of N −1 , with all
                                                coefficients in closed Bernoulli-polynomial form. The expansion contains
                                                the known O(N −1 ) local limit theorem, reduces to the binomial local mass
                                                for n = 2 and to the central multinomial coefficient in the symmetric case,
                                                and admits Cesàro averages of the oscillating coefficients in closed ζ-value
                                                form.

                                           1    Introduction
                                                                                            P
                                           Let X = (X1 , . . . , Xn ) ∼ Mult(N ; p), pi > 0, i pi = 1. This paper studies the
                                           probability Pr{X = m} for a lattice point m near the mean (N p1 , . . . , N pn ).
                                           The commonP starting point is the separable concavity of the log-probability on
                                           the slice i mi = N (§2). Its lattice maximiser gives the integer mode (§3),
                                           while its Stirling development gives the complete local expansion (§4). The
                                           results are multivariate counterparts of the binomial analyses in [9, 10].

The mode. We show (Theorem 3.1) that the mode of the multinomial is
exactly the Jefferson / D’Hondt divisor apportionment: the allocation produced
by a single common multiplier λ = N + d, whichPwhen the mode is unique takes
the floor form mi = ⌊λpi ⌋, d ∈ [0, n), fixed by i mi = N (Proposition 3.2; at
a tie no floor form exists, see Remark 3.3). The characterisation itself is known,
and in the apportionment literature it is standard: the transfer inequalities
are the max–min inequality characterising divisor methods, and the multiplier,
highest-averages and tie forms are all in Pukelsheim [4]. In the probabilistic
literature the multiplier form is Finucan’s [12], the greedy “rank the quotients
pi /k” form is White and Hendy’s [19], and the water-level reading is the resource-
allocation view of [17] — all three being the same object. What the identification
with apportionment buys is not a new theorem but access to the associated
notions of quota, large-party bias and tie structure. The consequence we draw is
that the mode is not a rounding of N p to neighbouring integers: a dominant bin
can be rounded above its own ceiling (the Jefferson large-party bias) while smaller
bins remain to their floor. Quantitatively, the excess mi − ⌈N pi ⌉ can reach
n − 2 (Proposition 3.10), showing that the two-sided localisation |mi − N pi | < 1
stated in the standard reference [13, Ch. 35] is not valid for n ≥ 3 (Remark 3.11).
Correcting that localisation is, in our view, the most useful item in §3; the excess
bound itself is a short consequence of the classical lower-quota theorem [3] and
would be routine to a worker in apportionment. The binomial mode ⌊(N + 1)p⌋
is the case n = 2, d = 1; and when every N pi is an integer the mode is the
unique point (N pi ), for every n ≥ 2 (§5).

3.2    The mode is not a neighbour rounding of N p
A natural alternative would be to round each N pi to a neighbouring integer,
choosing the coordinates so that the sum is N ; this is the Hamilton / largest-
remainder apportionment, which is quota-satisfying. The multinomial mode
need not have this form.
Proposition 3.7 (One-sided rounding; quota may be violated). The mode sat-
isfies
               ⌊N pi ⌋ ≤ mi < N pi + npi      (1 ≤ i ≤ n).             (7)
In particular mi is never below the floor ⌊N pi ⌋; but mi may exceed the ceiling
⌈N pi ⌉ for a large bin, so the mode need not be a rounding of N p to neighbours.
Proof. By Theorem 3.1(c) there is λ ∈ [N, N + n] with mi ≤ λpi ≤ mi + 1 for
every i; no floor form is needed, so the argument covers tied cases as well. For
the lower bound, suppose mi ≤ ⌊N pi ⌋ − 1. Then λpi ≤ mi + 1 ≤ ⌊N pi ⌋ ≤ N pi ,

Remark 3.12 (Three notions of uniqueness). Because a mode is a labelled vector
while a histogram shape is not, “the mode is unique” can mean three different
things. Let M denote the set of modes and Sym(p) = {σ : pσ(i) = pi }.
 (a) Labelled: |M| = 1. By Proposition 3.2 this holds iff q(N ) > q(N +1) .
 (b) Up to relabelling equal bins: M is a single Sym(p)-orbit.

 (c) As a histogram shape: all modes have the same multiset of counts, i.e. the
     same vector after sorting.
Trivially (a)⇒(b)⇒(c), since permuting equal bins does not change the multiset.
The first implication is strict — Remark 3.14 gives 21 co-modes sharing one
profile — so a mode may be non-unique for the purely nominal reason that
the units in excess are interchangeable among equal bins. But (c) is not
automatic, and it already fails for the binomial: for p = ( 14 , 43 ) and N = 3 the
two modes are
                               (0, 3) and (1, 2),
whose sorted profiles {0, 3} and {1, 2} are different. Sorting therefore does not
restore uniqueness in general. The reason is the dichotomy of Remark 3.14: co-
modes produced by a symmetry share a profile, while co-modes produced by a
genuine coincidence pi /k = pj /ℓ between bins with pi ̸= pj do not.
    In fact (b) and (c) are equivalent; this is Proposition 3.13 below, which also
records the underlying structural fact that any two modes differ by at most one
unit in every coordinate.

7    Concluding remarks
The two components of the paper use elementary methods: separable lattice con-
cavity for the mode and a gamma-quotient Stirling development for the expan-
sion. The new points are the interpretation of the mode as a Jefferson apportion-
ment, with its sharp quota violation, and the closed all-orders Bernoulli expan-
sion for the multinomial local mass, with the binomial and central-multinomial
cases as specialisations.
    Several questions are left open. In the even (entropy) normalisation of §4.4
the multiplicative coefficients Q
                                b j have not been written out, and it is not clear
whether the size-biased third normalisation of [10] has an n-bin analogue. For
the Cesàro averages, the full µH -average of Qj for j ≥ n — combining the all-bins
correlation (29) with the lower-order product terms — and a closed description
of the rational period-average through its resonance lattice (Remark 6.4) remain
to be done.
