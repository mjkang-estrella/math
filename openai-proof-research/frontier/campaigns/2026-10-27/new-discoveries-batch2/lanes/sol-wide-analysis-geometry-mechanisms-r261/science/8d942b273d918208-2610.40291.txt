                                                   Abstract. For a given λ > 0, a convex body in Rn is called λ-convex if it is the in-
                                                   tersection of balls of radius 1/λ. We show that among all λ-convex bodies in Rn of a
                                                   given surface area, volume is minimized by the λ-convex lens, that is, the intersection of
                                                   two balls of radius 1/λ. This confirms a conjecture of Borisenko in all dimensions n ⩾ 2.
                                                   Moreover, we extend this reverse isoperimetric inequality to all model spaces of constant
                                                   curvature in arbitrary dimension and arbitrary λ > 0.

                                                                                     1. introduction
                                              A convex body in Rn is a compact convex set with non-empty interior. Given λ > 0,
                                           a convex body K ⊂ Rn is called λ-convex if, at every point of its boundary, it is locally
                                           contained in a ball of radius 1/λ whose boundary passes through that point. More precisely,
                                           for each p ∈ ∂K there exist a neighborhood Up of p and a ball Bλ of radius 1/λ such that
                                           p ∈ ∂Bλ and Up ∩ K ⊂ Bλ (see Figure 1). When ∂K is smooth, this condition is equivalent
                                           to requiring that its principal curvatures, computed with respect to the inward-pointing
                                           unit normal, satisfy ki (p) ⩾ λ for every p ∈ ∂K and i = 1, . . . , n − 1.
                                              By the classical Blaschke Rolling Theorem [Bla] (see also [Dr4] and references therein
                                           for a historical account of this result), every λ-convex body is an intersection of balls of
                                           radius 1/λ.
                                              One of the key examples of a λ-convex body is a λ-convex lens defined as the intersection
                                           of two balls of radius 1/λ (see Figure 2).
                                              The geometry of λ-convex bodies has been studied extensively in convex and discrete
                                           geometry over the past two decades. We refer the reader to [BLN] and more recent [AA,
                                           Section 6] for surveys on λ-convexity and related questions (see also [BLNP, AAF]).
                                              This paper focuses on a reverse isoperimetric problem under curvature constraints. The
                                           classical isoperimetric inequality implies that among all λ-convex bodies of a given surface

Borisenko’s Conjecture. Let n ⩾ 2 and λ > 0. Let K ⊂ Rn be a λ-convex body and let
L ⊂ Rn be a λ-convex lens. If |∂K| = |∂L|, then |K| ⩾ |L|, with equality if and only if K
is a λ-convex lens.
   Here |A| denotes the n-dimensional volume of a measurable set A ⊂ Rn and |∂A| denotes
the (n − 1)-dimensional volume of its boundary.
   For n = 2, Borisenko and Drach resolved the conjecture in [BD2] (see also the alter-
native proof in [FKV]), and for n = 3, Drach and Tatarko confirmed it in [DT1]. The
corresponding problem was also fully resolved in all two-dimensional model spaces of con-
stant curvature and in certain natural generalizations of these spaces in [BD3, Dr1, Bo2].
Recently, it was confirmed in every three-dimensional space form in [DST]. We also note
that in [HSch], it was shown that the solution to Borisenko’s conjecture in Euclidean,
spherical, and hyperbolic spaces cannot have a smooth boundary.
   In this paper, we prove Borisenko’s conjecture in every dimension n ⩾ 2. Moreover, we
establish the corresponding reverse isoperimetric inequality in all model spaces of constant
curvature.
Main Theorem. Borisenko’s Conjecture is true in Rn , as well as in spherical space Sn
and hyperbolic space Hn , for any n ⩾ 2 and λ > 0.
  The proof employs the reverse inradius inequality from [DT1] with the spherical com-
parison used in [Be4].

   The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we collect the necessary background on
λ-convexity. In Section 3, we prove the main theorem in Rn , where the main ideas of the
proof can be presented more transparently. Then, in Section 4, we extend the argument
to the spherical and hyperbolic spaces Sn and Hn . In the hyperbolic case Hn , which we
assume to be of curvature −1, extra care will be given to the equidistant case of λ ∈ (0, 1).
This is due to the fact that, contrary to the case when λ ⩾ 1, an intersection of two convex
regions bounded by hypersurfaces of constant normal curvature λ may not be compact.

of constant curvature c. We call M n (c) a space form (of curvature c). Thus, M n (c) is the
standard Euclidean space Rn when c = 0, spherical space Sn (c) if c > 0 and hyperbolic
space Hn (c) if c < 0.

of constant normal curvature equal to λ. A λ-ball Bλ is the convex and topologically closed

2.1. λ-convex bodies. Fix some λ > 0.
Definition 2.1 (λ-convex body). A convex body K ⊂ M n (c) is λ-convex if for each
p ∈ ∂K there exists a neighborhood Up ⊂ M n (c) and a λ-sphere Sλ that passes through p
in such a way that
                                     Up ∩ ∂K ⊂ Bλ ,
where Bλ is the corresponding λ-ball (see Figure 1).
   In this case, we call Sλ a supporting λ-sphere passing through p ∈ ∂K.
   The following classical theorem of Blaschke turns the local condition in Definition 2.1
into a global one; see [Bla] for the original proof in the Euclidean space and [Dr4] for an
overview of further developments and generalizations.
Theorem 2.2 (Blaschke’s Rolling Theorem). Let K ⊂ M n (c) be a λ-convex body. Then
for every p ∈ ∂K there exists a supporting λ-sphere Sλ bounding the λ-ball Bλ such that
p ∈ Sλ and K ⊆ Bλ .
Definition 2.3 (λ-convex polytope). A λ-convex polytope in M n (c) is a λ-convex body K
given as the intersection of finitely many convex regions Bλ . The boundary ∂K is piecewise
smooth, and the closure of each maximal connected smooth part is called a facet of K. A
facet is of dimension n − 1 and is equal to the intersection K ∩ ∂Bλ for some Bλ .
Definition 2.4 (λ-convex lens). A λ-convex lens is a λ-convex body given by the inter-
section of two sets of the form Bλ (see Figure 2).

   Note that in our definition, a λ-convex lens can be a ball (if the two generating sets
Bλ are balls that coincide). Otherwise, a λ-convex lens is a λ-convex polytope that has
exactly two facets. Each lens is reflection-symmetric with respect to the totally geodesic
hyperplane passing through the intersection of the boundaries of the two facets.
   Let K ⊂ M n (c) be a convex body. A geodesic ball of the largest possible radius contained
in K is called an inscribed ball for K. The inradius of K, denoted by r(K), is the radius
of an inscribed ball for K. Note that r(K) is well-defined even though a convex body can
have several inscribed balls.
   We will need the following reverse-inradius inequality, proved in [DT1].
Theorem 2.5 (Reverse inradius inequality). Let n ⩾ 2. Let K ⊂ M n (c) be a λ-convex
body, and let L ⊂ M n (c) be a λ-convex lens. If |∂K| = |∂L|, then r(K) ⩾ r(L). Equiva-
lently, if r(K) = r(L), then |∂K| ⩽ |∂L|.
   Moreover, equality (in each statement) holds if and only if K is a λ-convex lens.
   For 2-dimensional λ-convex bodies in R2 , Milka [Mi2] proved that the λ-convex lens
has the smallest inradius for a given length of the boundary. Much later, this result was
generalized in [Dr3] to arbitrary λ-convex domains in 2-dimensional Alexandrov spaces of
curvature bounded below, thus closing this question in dimension 2. Bezdek [Be4] showed
that among all λ-convex bodies in Rn , n ⩾ 2, the λ-convex lens has the smallest inradius
for a given volume.
