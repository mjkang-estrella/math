                                               6. Open Questions
   While we are able to completely determine the Lebesgue-space boundedness of BMaα and BSHcα , for BHaα
our counterexample in Theorem 1.1 only rules out strong–type boundedness at the L1 endpoint. We therefore
propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6.1. For α ∈ (0, ∞) \ {1} and a : R → R measurable (or smooth and globally Lipschitz with
0 < A < a(x) < B < ∞), BHaα maps boundedly from Lp (R) × Lq (R) into Lr (R) for 1 < p, q, r < ∞ with
1   1   1              1,∞
p + q = r , and into L     (R) when r = 1.

   Proving such a statement is beyond the elementary positive-domination methods that were used to resolve
Theorem 1.2, and instead one will most likely be required to utilize the LGC methodology, first introduced by
Lie in [22] and further refined in many subsequent works; see for instance [1], [4], [5], [9], [10], and [11].
   Our result for BSHcα establishes the existence of measurable functions c which cause the operator to fail all
strong and weak–type estimates. However, this leaves open the question of whether there are regularity and
nondegeneracy conditions one can impose on c that allow boundedness to be recovered. Assuming c ∈ C 1 (R)
so that discussing the derivative is meaningful, we formulate the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6.2. For α ∈ (0, ∞) \ {1} and c ∈ C 1 (R), suppose there are ϵ1 , ϵ2 > 0 such that for all x ∈ R:
   (1) |c′ (x) − 1| > ϵ1 ;
   (2) |1 + α|c(x)|α−1 c′ (x)| > ϵ2 .
Then for

    We notice that conditions (1) and (2) rule out the example constructed in the proof of Proposition 1.4. This
also explains why we do not use the much simpler choice cx = x in that proof, as we wish to demonstrate that
c′ (x) − 1 getting asymptotically close to 0 without vanishing identically is already sufficient to destroy strong
and weak–type bounds. Establishing Conjecture 6.2 will again likely be based on the LGC methodology
discussed.
    One can of course consider the “flat” variants of Conjectures 6.1 and 6.2 by setting α = 1. The conjectures
are still expected to hold, but the proofs of such statements one would expect to be much more technically
challenging and based on wave packet analysis. Thus we propose attacking the curved variants first, namely
γ(t) = t3 , γ(t) = t2 and then γ(t) = t, in increasing order of their expected difficulty.
