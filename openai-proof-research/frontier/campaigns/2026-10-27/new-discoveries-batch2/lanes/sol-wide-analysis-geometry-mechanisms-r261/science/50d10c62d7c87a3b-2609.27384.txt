                                                   Shape without scale: an identifiability dichotomy
                                                  for a bounded tail observed through a non-additive
                                                                 measurement kernel
                                                                                                  Jiarui Qi∗




arXiv:2609.27384v3 [math.ST] 8 Oct 2026
                                                                                                  Abstract
                                                        A latent severity has a bounded lower tail with density of shape α and scale L. It is observed
                                                    only through a fixed Markov kernel K that is biased and non-additive. The relative conditional
                                                    spread of K diverges at the endpoint. Our sample is i.i.d. from the marginal Q alone, with no
                                                    anchoring covariate or instrument. We prove a dichotomy. The shape index α is identifiable:
                                                    for every admissible choice of the class constants, any two observationally equivalent members
                                                    of a lean class share α, determined by a near-endpoint expansion of Q. The rate, namely L and
                                                    the fixed-scale exceedance pτ , does not survive. There exist admissible shared class constants
                                                    and two members of a smaller regularity class whose observed laws coincide exactly. Across the
                                                    pair α agrees, whereas L and pτ move. A degenerate Le Cam two-point bound excludes any
                                                    uniformly consistent estimator of either, and pointwise consistency fails at one member. Only
                                                    the rate needs an anchor. We conjecture that a known kernel family with known edge map
                                                    identifies the rate fiber by fiber if and only if the family satisfies a fixed-scale injectivity clause,
                                                    and we prove the sufficiency direction. In surrogate safety, uncalibrated conflict data give the
                                                    shape of near-crash risk, not its absolute rate.

                                          1        Introduction
                                          1.1        A resolution floor that does not shrink with the signal
                                          Consider a latent scalar S ≥ 0 that measures how close a system came to a catastrophe, where
                                          S = 0 is the boundary and small values of S are dangerous. The variable S is never observed.
                                          Instead, a measurement chain reports each true severity s as a contaminated value S̃ drawn from
                                          a conditional law K(· | s), and we observe an i.i.d. sample from the resulting marginal law Q of
                                          S̃ alone. No covariate, instrument, pairing, or repeated measurement is available. The objects of
                                          interest are the lower-tail features of S at the endpoint 0: its shape and the rate of dangerous,
                                          small-severity outcomes. Under a regularly varying tail fS (s) = L sα (1 + o(1)), the shape index
                                          α ∈ [0, ∞) measures how fast the latent density vanishes at the boundary. The scale L fixes the
                                          near-endpoint mass, while the exceedance pτ = FS ([0, τ ]) at a fixed threshold τ is the probability

of attraction with extreme-value index ξ = −1/(α + 1) (de Haan and Ferreira, 2006, Thm. 1.2.1(2);
Coles, 2001).
     The main difficulty comes from a single structural feature of the measurement: the resolution
floor of the detector does not scale down with the signal. While the absolute sensor error is fixed, the
score it corrupts shrinks toward 0. Hence the relative conditional spread of K(· | s) does not vanish
as s ↓ 0 but diverges there, on the band of severities that carries the estimand. The motivating
instance is surrogate-safety estimation in traffic-conflict analysis (Joo et al., 2024), where the rate
of rare crashes is estimated without observing any crash (Songchitruksa and Tarko, 2006; Zheng
et al., 2014). One scores near-miss conflicts by a continuous severity, canonically time-to-collision
(the encounter-minimum of the ratio of inter-agent gap to closing speed), and extrapolates the tail
of the scores to the contact boundary S = 0. Fixed sensing noise (below ten centimeters, Krajewski
et al., 2018) is propagated through this min-of-ratio functional, whose denominator is itself a small
difference of measured quantities. The result is a state-dependent, non-additive distortion that
grows without bound at the dangerous, small-margin extreme. Two questions organize the paper:
whether the shape α survives in Q, and whether the rate, namely the scale L and the exceedance
pτ , survives with it.

1.3   Mechanism and the anchor
A leading-order heuristic explains the dichotomy. Near the endpoint the bias acts on severity as
an affine map S 7→ cS + a with affine scale c ̸= 1 (Section 2), that is, as a two-parameter group on
(endpoint, scale). The exponent α is an invariant of this group, since a power law reparametrized
affinely keeps its exponent, and the shape survives with it. The free parameters (a, c), by contrast,
absorb same-α differences in (L, pτ ), so the rate does not. The theorems rest on the exact near-
endpoint expansion rather than on this heuristic.
    The heuristic also suggests the remedy: an anchor that pins (a, c) should restore identifiability
of (L, pτ ). We formalize it as Anchor C (Assumption 4.8 of Section 4.1), which has two clauses.
First, the kernel is known to lie in a structured family {Kθ } whose lower edge map ℓ is known, so
that the apparent endpoint and the edge slope are pinned. Second, a fixed-scale injectivity clause
is imposed: within the family, the map F 7→ Kθ F is injective on latent laws supported below the
family’s severity bound (the finite cap on latent severity the family carries). The second clause is
not implied by the first. Pinning ℓ closes the degree of freedom the confounding pair exploits, yet it
does not by itself invert the kernel at the fixed scale τ . With only the first clause in place, in-fiber
confounds for pτ (distinct members with the same observed law, within a single edge-pinned family)
are not ruled out.
    The status of the results is as follows. The shape half is proved over Mreg , indeed over the
leaner M⋆ , with no anchor (Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3). Theorem 4.5 proves the necessity of an
anchor for (L, pτ ), whereas the necessity of Anchor C in particular is not proved. We state the
equivalence as a conjecture (Conjecture 4.9): for every family satisfying the first clause, (L, pτ ) is
identifiable on each of its edge-indexed fibers of Mreg if and only if the family satisfies the second.
Its sufficiency direction is immediate from Anchor C, whose injectivity clause is automatic for
location kernels. Discharging that clause for the non-location kernels (those whose jitter is scaled
rather than translated) is left to forthcoming companion work (Remark 4.10).

The main contributions of this paper are the following.

   • A near-edge exponent lemma with explicit two-sided constants κ± (Lemma 4.1): Q([a, a+

   • Shape identifiability over the lean class M⋆ , for every admissible choice of the class
     constants and on A1/A5− /A6 alone (Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3).

   • Scale and exceedance non-identifiability over Mreg , existential (Theorem 4.5). A de-

   • One witness family, which also serves as a sharpness certificate: the confounding
     pair keeps α′ = α, so it corroborates rather than contradicts the shape theorem. Although the
     pair moves the tail scale, the factor of the near-edge lead coefficient that in general varies from
     member to member comes out equal for the pair’s two members. Both then realize one near-
     endpoint exponent while the scale moves, which is the certificate of sharpness (Remark 4.7).

1.5   Positioning and outline
We place the result by drawing three contrasts. Deconvolution is unavailable: non-additivity re-
moves the fixed error law, and with it the error characteristic function whose non-vanishing identifies
the additive model (Fan, 1991; Stefanski and Carroll, 1990; Meister, 2009), so the classical condi-
tion cannot even be stated here. The error is not asymptotically negligible: in the contaminated
extreme-value literature the measurement error is asymptotically negligible next to the sampling
fluctuation of the order statistics, and tail-index estimators keep their limits (Pere et al., 2024,
2025). Here, however, the conditional spread diverges where the estimand lives. Shape, not loca-
tion: under additive error (α the latent-edge exponent, β the error-support edge exponent there)
the composite α + β + 2 is the minimax-rate denominator for the endpoint location, with α a nui-
sance the convolution does not recover (Goldenshluger and Tsybakov, 2004). That denominator is
the quantity in the exponent that fixes the best achievable convergence rate. In the non-additive
setting of this paper, the moving edge turns the same composite into an exponent read off Q that
identifies the boundary’s shape. The additive location confound widens into a location-and-scale
confound.
    The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Sections 2–7 contain the application, the setup,
the dichotomy (with Anchor C and Conjecture 4.9 in Section 4.1), the witness family and pair, the
related work and the discussion. The Supplementary Material collects the engine, the measure-
theoretic preliminaries, the estimation bridge and sketch, the witness-family and pair proofs, the
certified instance and the axiom accounting.


2     A motivating measurement problem
The axioms of the previous section may read as a list of analytic conveniences. This section shows
that they are not. A single worked problem, estimating rare-crash risk from near-miss traffic
conflicts, forces every one of them. Each axiom is read off that application rather than imposed
for tractability, and the problem makes concrete what “a non-additive measurement kernel with a
moving power-law edge” means. We cite the applied literature only for the method it established
and for the empirical error mechanisms it documents. The kernel K, its moving edge ℓ(s), the
identification of α, and the non-identifiability of (L, pτ ) without an anchor are our own.

2.1   A min-of-ratio severity, measured with bounded bias and jitter
Crash frequency is estimated without observing a crash. Analysts score near-miss encounters (“con-
flicts”) by a continuous surrogate severity, then extrapolate the extreme-value fit of the smallest
scores toward the catastrophe boundary (Songchitruksa and Tarko, 2006, Sect. 4; Tarko, 2018).
The standard score is time-to-collision (TTC), introduced by Hayward (1972). Over an encounter

    The trajectory is never observed directly. It is reconstructed from drone or camera video placed
in space by a fixed projective calibration (a homography, Hartley and Zisserman, 2004, Sect. 8.1.1),
a step that carries measurement error of two kinds. One is a systematic bias, a georeferencing offset
that does not average out. Because projective error depends on an object’s height (Hartley and
Zisserman, 2004, Sect. 13.3, eq. (13.9)), hence on its class, this offset differs between the two agents
even at a common ground location. The other is a bounded random jitter : per-frame tracking
noise with bounded support and no systematic component. In absolute terms the localization error
is small, on the order of ten centimeters after post-processing (Krajewski et al., 2018). Near the
endpoint, however, its effect on the severity scale is neither small nor benign. Joo et al. document
this signal-dependent blow-up empirically (Joo et al., 2024). The score TTC = ∆x/∆s is a ratio
of a differenced gap to a differenced closing speed, where ∆x and ∆s denote tracked position
and speed differences, not the trajectory x and severity s used above. Positional noise therefore
propagates nonlinearly and systematically, skewing the score toward underestimation and biasing
crash risk upward. In the dangerous regime the relative distortion explodes. The smallest-margin
conflicts, which are the most safety-relevant, carry by far the largest relative error, in a “cone-
shaped” scatter that denoising does not remove. That work models the input noise as additive on
position and quantifies the output scatter empirically. The non-additive severity-scale abstraction
and the identifiability question it raises are ours, not claims made there (Joo et al., 2024).

    The application is the near-edge setting treated in Section 4. Here the observed law concentrates

exponent read off Q pins the latent shape α, even though no error law can be deconvolved.
    Section 4 shows that this pipeline identifies shape but not scale. The shape index α, which
governs how fast near-crash danger thins as the surrogate score approaches contact, is recoverable
from scored conflicts alone (Theorem 4.2). By contrast, the rate of that danger is not. Theorem 4.5
exhibits two intersections (two road sites) whose measured conflict scores are exactly identically
distributed. Yet the latent severe-event scale L and the fixed-threshold exceedance pτ each differ
between the sites, by 77.8% in the certified instance. The bias-and-jitter propagation offsets the
change in latent scale by a compensating change in the moving edge, leaving the marginal law of
the scores unchanged. No analysis of the observed conflicts can then separate the two sites, at any
sample size. In practice this is a division of labour: absolute rate, unlike shape, needs an external
calibration that pins the measurement scale, so neither an absolute near-crash frequency nor a
ranking of two sites by near-crash rate should be read from uncalibrated conflict data alone.

 4.1      The dichotomy
 Theorems 4.2 and 4.5 resolve the two components of the estimand in opposite directions. The shape
 α is identifiable over Mreg with no anchor, whereas the scale L and the exceedance pτ are not.
 As described heuristically in the introduction (Section 1), the leading-order mechanism behind the
 split is an affine bias orbit acting on (endpoint, scale), under which α is an invariant but (L, pτ )
 are moved. In this subsection we describe what a full resolution would look like. First comes the
 anchor that a positive answer for (L, pτ ) would require, along with the fibers it cuts out. We then
 state the conjecture that would complete the proved shape half and the necessity direction into a
 single characterization.
 Assumption 4.8 (Anchor C). The kernel belongs to a known family {Kθ : θ ∈ Θ}, with Θ ⊆ Rk
 finite-dimensional. This family has the standing structure (edge maps strictly increasing on [0, ∞)
 and a severity bound s̄K < ∞) and the two properties below.
(C-a) (Known family, known edge.) All non-θ structure of the family (the conditional shape of
      Kθ (· | s) above its edge, the width map w(·), and the jitter law) is known to the analyst, and
      so is the lower edge map ℓ = ℓKθ . Within the family the edge determines θ, so that K is
      known up to the latent FS .

(C-b) (Fixed-scale injectivity.) For every θ ∈ Θ the mixture map F 7→ Kθ F is injective on the
      probability measures on [0, s̄K ].
 Pinning ℓ alone does not invert the kernel at the fixed scale τ (Definition 3.10). Clause (C-b)
 is not implied by (C-a), and it carries the fixed-scale content of the anchor. Without it, in-fiber

                                                      16
confounding pairs are not ruled out for edge-pinned families. Such a pair consists of two distinct
members of one fiber (hence with a common edge) that yield the same observed law. The pair
exhibited in Theorem 4.5 is not of this kind, since it moves the edge slope from c to c/κ and so
crosses fibers. In effect, (C-b) asks for injectivity of the mixture operator F 7→ Kθ F . It is the non-
additive, instrument-free counterpart of the completeness condition that characterizes identification
in nonparametric instrumental-variable models, where identification of the structural function is
equivalent to injectivity of a conditional-expectation operator (Newey and Powell, 2003, Prop. 2.1).
There that operator acts on functions and is indexed by an instrument, whereas here it acts on
measures and is indexed by the known kernel parameter θ at the fixed scale τ . The marginal-only
scheme (3) supplies no instrument.

for some θ ∈ Θ. Each candidate family defines fibers of Mreg indexed by the observed edge:

Remark 4.10 (Status of the dichotomy). The shape half is proved. Theorem 4.2, with Corollary 4.3,
identifies α over Mreg (in fact over the leaner M⋆ ), with no anchor. That some anchor is necessary
is proved as well. Together with Lemma 4.6, Theorem 4.5 exhibits, in its exact existential form,
admissible shared class constants and two members of Mreg with identical observed law but distinct
(L, pτ ). Hence over the unanchored Mreg neither L nor pτ is identifiable. What is not proved here
is that Anchor C in particular is necessary, that is, the direction “(L, pτ ) identifiable on every fiber
MK (ℓ0 ) ⇒ K satisfies (C-b)” of Conjecture 4.9. Indeed, Theorem 4.5 says nothing about candidate
families that satisfy (C-a) but fail (C-b).
    In contrast, the sufficiency direction is immediate from Assumption 4.8 as stated. Suppose
two members of one fiber MK (ℓ0 ) induce the same observed law. By (C-a), within the family
the edge determines θ, so their kernels coincide, say Kθ . In view of (17), both latent laws are
carried by [0, s̄K ], which is the domain on which (C-b) declares F 7→ Kθ F injective. The two latent
laws coincide, and with them ψ = (α, L, pτ ) (Lemma 3.12). All the mathematical content of the
anchored horn lies in discharging (C-b) for a given family, and there the location and non-location
cases differ. For a location family, Kθ (· | s) = Law gθ (s) + W with a jitter W independent of s,

is strictly stronger than knowledge of the edge map. Clause (C-a) supplies the edge, whereas
for non-location kernels the fixed-scale injectivity clause (C-b) is an additional requirement. For
location kernels it is automatic, as just noted.


5    The witness family and the confounding pair
Both theorems of Section 4 concern members of a model class. Theorem 4.2 constrains every
observationally equivalent pair in M⋆ , whereas Theorem 4.5 asserts the existence of a confounding
pair in Mreg . Each theorem is vacuous unless the class is non-empty, and in fact unless it contains
genuinely non-additive members. Indeed, for an additive kernel K(· | s) = µ(· − s) the apparent
endpoint is recovered by classical additive endpoint analysis (Goldenshluger and Tsybakov, 2004),
and the non-convolution character that motivates the construction is lost. One explicit family meets
both requirements. Its members lie in Mreg , hence in M⋆ by the inclusion Mreg ⊆ M⋆ , so neither
theorem is vacuous. The same family also supplies the scale-conjugation pair of Theorem 4.5,
assembled in Section 5.3. That pair leaves the shape α unchanged while moving L and pτ . The
family realizes exactly the affine action S 7→ cS + a0 of (2), that is, a latent severity composed with
a random affine map of scale c ̸= 1 and a bounded jitter. The resulting kernel is non-additive since
the conditional edge then moves with a slope other than 1.
    Recall from Section 3.1 the objects attached to a member (FS , K): the conditional lower and
upper edges ℓ(s) and u(s), the width w(s) = u(s) − ℓ(s), the location offset m(s), and the apparent
endpoint a = ℓ(0+). This section presents the family in full and shows that it lies in Mreg .
The Supplementary Material records two further members. One is a genuine ratio witness, whose
conditional width grows with s, and the other a unit-slope corner witness with c− = c+ = 1 that
realizes every edge index β ≥ 0. The full non-emptiness case split is recorded there as well.

Convention 5.1 (Scope of this section). The construction that follows verifies membership only.
Each constructed pair satisfies A1–A6 and NA, and so lies in Mreg ⊆ M⋆ . By itself it makes no
identifiability claim. The confounding of distinct members that these pairs support, which is the
core of the negative half, is assembled from them in Section 5.3, whereas the shape half applies
to them only through the inclusion, and neither conclusion is drawn here. The only quantitative
by-product recorded is a consistency check, namely that the near-edge expansion of the constructed
Q reproduces the exponent α + β + 2 and, on the affine edge, the exact Beta-function constant of
Lemma 4.1. This checks the Lemma against one instance.

6      Related work
We prove identifiability of the latent lower-tail shape index α through a non-additive kernel K(· | s)
whose edge ℓ(s) moves with the signal and whose relative spread w(s)/s blows up at the endpoint,
from the marginal Q = KFS alone, with no location anchor. Identifiability here means constancy
of α across the fibers of (FS , K) 7→ KFS (the classes of observationally equivalent pairs). It is a
property of the model map, not of any estimator. Adjacent work differs from ours along three axes.
The pushforward there is additive rather than non-additive, and the object is a location rather
than a shape. Its relative spread vanishes at the endpoint as well, whereas ours diverges. The
contaminated marginal does not expose α as its own edge exponent. Over M⋆ the exponent it does
expose, α + β + 2, pins α regardless. We write a := ℓ(0+) = inf supp Q and recall the near-edge law
Q([a, a + h]) ≍ L hα+β+2 , where β ≥ 0 is the conditional edge index of K shared across the class.

                                                  23
6.1   Additive-noise deconvolution
Recovering a latent law from additively contaminated Y = X + ε rests entirely on the error charac-
teristic function: φX = φY /φε is identifiable precisely when the zero set of φε has empty interior,
at a rate set by its decay (Fan, 1991; Meister, 2009). The deconvolution kernel estimator inverts
it (Stefanski and Carroll, 1990). Real zeros, as for the uniform and other common compactly sup-
ported errors, force ridge regularization (Hall and Meister, 2007). This presupposes an additive,
location-invariant error, a fixed µ with K(· | s) = µ(· − s). Deconvolution identifies a density and
needs the error law known through φε . Under axiom NA no such µ exists: there is no φε to divide
out, and the condition “φε has no interval of zeros” is not violated but cannot be stated.

6.2   Endpoint, boundary, and frontier estimation under error
The closest classical analogue is endpoint estimation under additive error (Goldenshluger and Tsy-
bakov, 2004). There the target is the location θ of X (edge density f (x) ≥ L(θ − x)α ), with X
observed as Y = X + ε. Bounded-support error [−r, r] (r known, edge (r − x)β ) gives minimax rate
n−1/(α+β+2) , anchored through the single known edge r. Convolution tail-arithmetic combines in-
dices α, β into a marginal edge α+β +1 with Beta-function lead B(β +1, α+1). This rate exponent
is the classical counterpart of ours. The α + β + 2 of Goldenshluger and Tsybakov (2004), which
there governs the location θ under additive error, equals the exponent we read off Q([a, a + h]),
with the Beta-function constant appearing in both. Yet here the same number governs the shape
α through a non-additive, moving-edge kernel. In the additive setting the extra +1 over α + β + 1
integrates a fixed-support error against a static endpoint. In ours, α + β + 2 = (α + 1) + (β + 1)
splits into integrating sα across the O(h) band whose moving edge ℓ(s) reaches [a, a + h], plus
the edge-mass profile hβ+1 of axiom A6. Two variants swap the anchor. An unknown normal
scale (Leng et al., 2019; Kneip et al., 2015) still needs the error form known and zero-mean, while
unknown but symmetric additive error (Florens et al., 2020) trades the known edge for symmetry.
Symmetry, like the known edge, is then one admissible anchor among several, not indispensable.

6.3   Extreme-quantile estimation under erroneous or approximate observations
A recent strand asks how imperfectly observed values affect tail-shape inference. Pere et al. (2024)
show the Hill and extreme-quantile estimators of heavy-tailed X keep the same asymptotically        √
normal limit once the approximation error is negligible relative to the tail quantile scale at the k
rate, k the effective tail sample size. For a bounded multiplicative relative perturbation of the order
statistics, Pere et al. (2025) bound its effect on the moment estimator of the extreme-value index.
In Morozov (2026), extreme quantiles of a latent heterogeneity law are recovered through a noisy
proxy with vanishing error and no parametric error form, via a tail-equivalence condition under
which the noisy order statistics inherit the latent limit. We take that tail-equivalence condition
as our comparison point for EVT under latent heterogeneity. It is sharp, and once it fails, some
dependence structure makes the noisy proxy miss the latent limit.

6.4   Relation to the present paper
No prior result identifies a bounded extreme-value tail shape index through a non-additive kernel
with a moving power-law edge and endpoint-diverging relative spread when only the marginal is ob-
served and no location anchor is available. Deconvolution and its heteroscedastic variants keep the
contamination additive and independent of the latent value, so the error scale may vary across ob-
servations but never with the severity itself (Delaigle and Meister, 2008, Sect. 2.1). In our regime the


                                                  24
relative spread w(s)/s instead diverges at the endpoint. The additive endpoint/frontier/boundary
literature recovers a location object under an anchor, compounding α to α + β + 1 or obscuring
it. In Goldenshluger and Tsybakov (2004) only that compound is visible at the marginal edge,
and their endpoint estimator (anchored by the known error-support radius) adapts to the unknown
α, which is a nuisance there and the target here. The erroneous-observation strand is estimation,
not identifiability: its contamination is additive-and-vanishing (Pere et al., 2024; Morozov, 2026) or
multiplicative (Pere et al., 2025), so the relative error must vanish, the opposite of our setting. Each
rests on side information our marginal-only scheme forbids. The first needs refinable approxima-
tions of each latent value and the second a growing per-unit sample size T , whereas the third needs
√
   n-consistent location/scatter from elliptical structure. Furthermore, the tail-shape parameter is
recovered by Hill or moment estimators (Pere et al., 2024, 2025), while Morozov (2026) imposes
a known lower bound on the extreme-value index in its rate conditions. All three keep the noisy
extremes on the normalizing scale of the latent ones, so the two limits agree, whereas here the kernel
shifts the near-edge exponent away from the latent one. In the applied traffic-conflict study the
question is error propagation, not identifiability of a contamination kernel. The theorem here closes
this gap: over M⋆ , α is point-identified from Q = KFS , which fixes both the apparent endpoint
a = inf supp Q and the near-edge exponent α + β + 2, the shared class constant β apportioning it
to the latent shape. On the negative half, a standard device is pushed to a degenerate extreme.
The Le Cam two-point method (Le Cam, 1986; Tsybakov, 2009) lower-bounds minimax risk by the
testing affinity of two hypotheses. This affinity is a number between zero and one measuring their
overlap, and it equals one exactly when no test can tell them apart. In the literature on endpoint
estimation under error the method is used in the usual way: two nearby laws, separated just enough
that no test resolves them at the working sample size, which yields a rate (Goldenshluger and Tsy-
bakov, 2004). Our two witnesses are not nearby. They induce the identical sampling law Q⊗n at
every n, so their affinity is exactly 1 and the bound degenerates: the risk floor 12 ∆ of Lemma 4.6
carries no power of n and never decays. The confounding pair therefore certifies non-identification
rather than slow estimation.


7    Discussion and scope
Remark 7.1 (Scope of the identification result). For every admissible choice of the class constants,
Theorem 4.2 identifies α over M⋆ , and Corollary 4.3 carries it to Mreg . This remark fixes the scope
of that statement.
    (i) Shape only, and provably so. Theorem 4.2 identifies the latent lower-tail shape index α, and
nothing else about the tail. The scale L of A1 and the fixed-scale exceedance pτ are addressed
by the other half of the paper, with the opposite conclusion: they are not identifiable over Mreg
without a further anchor (Theorem 4.5). That statement is existential in the sense proved there.
Namely, for suitable admissible shared class constants there exist two members of Mreg with exactly
equal observed laws and equal α, while both L and pτ differ by the factor κ−(α+1) ̸= 1. The near-
edge expansion pins the near-endpoint exponent α + β + 2 and shows that the lead coefficient is
proportional to L (Remark 4.4), but it does not pin L itself. Indeed, both the proportionality
                                                                                          −(α+1)
constant κ± in (15) and, in the affine case, the sharp constant c̄K B(α + 1, β + 2) cℓ           depend
on the kernel and are not extracted from Q by this argument.
    (ii) Identifiability is relative to the class. What is identified is α given the class, in particular
given the shared edge index β. The observed law delivers a single number, the near-endpoint
exponent α + β + 2 of (13). Splitting that number unambiguously between the latent shape α and
the kernel edge index β rests on the class assumption that β is common across members. If β were


                                                   25
allowed to vary across members, the exponent alone would not separate the two. No claim is made
(and none is needed) that α and β are individually recoverable in that wider setting.
    (iii) Identification, not estimation. The shape theorem states that α is constant on the fibers
of the map (FS , K) 7→ Q restricted to the class: observationally equivalent members share α. It
is not an estimator and carries no sampling guarantee or rate. A natural plug-in estimator and a
heuristic consistency argument are sketched in the Supplementary Material, yet a full consistency
or rate theorem is left to future work.
Remark 7.2 (The κ = 1 collapse on unbounded support). Suppose, counterfactually, that the affine
action s 7→ cs +a0 held on an unbounded latent support, with no smax and no off-support extension,
the kernel being Law(cs + a0 + W ) for all s ≥ 0. The offset computation of Lemma 5.3 would then
read m(s) = (c − 1)s + a0 + midW for all s ≥ 0, an affine function of slope c − 1. For c ̸= 1 it satisfies
|m(s)| → ∞ as s → ∞, so that sups≥0 |m(s)| = ∞ exceeds every finite b̄ and axiom A3 fails. A3
would then force c = 1 on member 0 and c/κ = 1 on member 1. Together the two constraints
give κ = 1, i.e. only the trivial pair FS′ = FS , K ′ = K survives. (Even that degenerate “pair”
would leave the class, since c = 1 turns the kernel into the translate family K(· | s) = µ(· − s) with
µ = Law(a0 + W ), which violates NA. This is the content of condition (C5) of Proposition 5.4.)
Hence scale conjugation collapses on unbounded support, and no genuine confound exists there.
    Our proofs use the bounded support together with the unit-slope extension in two places only,
both inside the membership verification (Proposition 5.4, invoked for both members through Propo-
sition S11.3 of the Supplementary Material):

  (i) the A3 verification, through (C3): by (21), the offset m is affine with slope c − 1 only up to
      the break at smax and is constant beyond it. Hence sups≥0 |m(s)| ≤ |c − 1| smax + a0 + midW ,
      which is the finite quantity that (C3) asks b̄ to dominate. For member 1 the same bound
      holds with (c/κ, κsmax ) in place of (c, smax ). Boundedness of the offset for a slope c ̸= 1 is
      available only because the affine regime stops at the break.

 (ii) the A5 global monotonicity: beyond the break the location map, and with it the edge ℓ =
      g + w− of (20), continues with slope 1 > 0. It follows that ℓ is increasing on all of [0, ∞), and
      no distant conditional law returns to the apparent endpoint. This is the global half of A5,
      which the two-point increment condition on [0, sR ] does not cover.

Both members of Definition 5.5 carry their own break (smax and κsmax respectively) and their
own unit-slope tail. That definition and Lemma S11.1 of the Supplementary Material are built
accordingly. It follows that the scale/exceedance confound of Theorem 4.5 is a bounded-support
phenomenon. A latent severity bound together with an off-support translation regime is what
makes a non-unit edge slope, and with it the conjugation c 7→ c/κ, admissible.
Remark 7.3 (What carries the confound, and what would close it). The entire confound is carried
by one degree of freedom: the kernel’s edge slope, moved from c to c/κ to compensate the latent
rescaling S 7→ κS. More precisely, what differs between the two kernels is the observable edge map
ℓ. On the respective corridors, member 0 has ℓ(s) = cs + a′ and member 1 has ℓ′ (s) = (c/κ)s + a′
(Lemma 5.3). The slope moves, while the intercept a′ = a0 + w− stays and the observed law Q
remains fixed (Lemma 5.6). An anchor axiom pinning the edge map ℓ (its slope and intercept)
across the class would therefore close the degree of freedom that our pair uses. Pinning ℓ is the edge
clause of Anchor C (Assumption 4.8), and full repair also needs C’s fixed-scale injectivity clause (C-
b). Granted both clauses, the sufficiency direction of Conjecture 4.9 is immediate (Remark 4.10).
What remains open here is discharging (C-b) for non-location families, along with the necessity
direction of the conjecture. The mechanism is the location-and-scale version, through a non-additive


                                                   26
kernel, of the additive-location endpoint confound of Goldenshluger and Tsybakov (2004). In that
setting a fixed error law shifts an endpoint, whereas here a dilated latent law and a contracted edge
slope trade off against each other, leaving the endpoint, and everything else observable, unchanged.
Remark 7.4 (Scope of the non-identifiability result). The result has four scope boundaries. (i) Not
the shape: the exhibited pair keeps α′ = α (Lemma 5.7). Identifiability of α over Mreg is the positive
half, Theorem 4.2 (with Corollary 4.3), which the pair supports rather than overturns. (ii) Not the
anchored horn (the anchored branch of the identifiability dichotomy): Remark 7.3 only locates the
degree of freedom that an anchor axiom would close. Nothing is proved about anchored sub-fibers
beyond the sufficiency argument of Remark 4.10, nor is (C-b) discharged for non-location families.
(iii) Not estimator construction: the statistical conclusions are the minimax floors and consistency
failures of Lemma 4.6 and Corollary S10.2 of the Supplementary Material. No estimator or rate is
given, and no positive consistency claim is made. (iv) Non-emptiness is supplied : Proposition S11.3
of the Supplementary Material exhibits two members of Mreg for the constructed shared constants,
so the class quantified over is non-empty by construction. The result itself needs no non-emptiness
beyond these constants. For the general record see Section 5.2, whose affine witness covers every
shared-constant regime except the corner c− = c+ = 1, where non-emptiness is settled by the unit-
slope corner witness of Section S12 of the Supplementary Material. The four constructions behind
the result are Proposition 5.4 with Proposition S11.3 of the Supplementary Material, Lemma 5.6,
Lemma 5.7, and the proof of Theorem 4.5 via Lemma 4.6.

Remark S4.3 (Technical notes on the two-sided bound). Uniformity of A6, and why lim inf / lim sup
suffices. The two displays above invoke (9) at the depth δ = δh (s), which depends on s and ranges
over all of (0, h) as s runs through the contributing window (0, h/c− ]. That window itself moves
with h. The single threshold η(ε), valid simultaneously for all s ∈ (0, sR ], lets one h-condition

η(ε, s) > 0, fails. The proof would then need h ≤ inf{η(ε, s) : 0 < s ≤ h/c− }, a self-referential

Remark S6.2 (The choice of M⋆ , and the role of A2–A4 and NA). Because Mreg ⊆ M⋆ , an
identifiability statement quantified over M⋆ is strictly stronger than the same statement over Mreg .
Over M⋆ the conclusion is asserted for every regular member and for the many additional members
that drop A2–A4, NA, and the interior increments of A5. The axiom budget of Section S14 certifies
this upgrade at no cost. Indeed, the proof of Sections S1–S7 consumes only A1, A5− , A6 (and the
setup well-posedness convention), so it transfers verbatim from Mreg to M⋆ .
    This does not demote A2–A4 and NA to irrelevance. They are the modeling axioms that make
the identifiability question non-trivial, and they earn their place in the motivation rather than in
the proof. Non-additivity (NA) is what removes the classical tool. Since K(· | s) is not a translate
µ(· − s) of a fixed error law, there is no error characteristic function to divide out and Fourier
deconvolution does not apply (cf. the deconvolution literature Fan, 1991; Stefanski and Carroll,
1990; Meister, 2009, whose identifying device is that error characteristic function). Tail-amplified
heteroscedasticity (A4), w(s)/s → ∞, places the worst contamination exactly where the estimand
lives, so the question cannot be reduced to a vanishing-noise limit. The bounded-support/no-
escape clause (A2) and the bias clause (A3) are what locate the problem in the regime where the
near-edge expansion is the right instrument. That regime is characterized by a moving, power-law
conditional edge near a shifted apparent endpoint a. A member of M⋆ that violated these would
be a mathematically admissible but modeling-vacuous object. That the conclusion survives for it is
a bonus, not the point. The content of the result is that, on the modeling-relevant subclass Mreg ,
where deconvolution is unavailable, the shape α is nonetheless pinned.

mass (Kallenberg, 2002).

Remark S14.2 (Sharpness of the two-sided form). The result is a two-sided bound, not an asymp-
totic equality, and that is also all the argument yields. Indeed, the axioms permit the edge slope
to wander inside [c− , c+ ] and cK to oscillate between c and c without converging. The proof
correspondingly pins only the lim inf / lim sup of Q([a, a + h])/hα+β+2 , inside [κ− L, κ+ L]. No con-
vergence of that ratio is claimed. (Section S15 illustrates numerically that the ratio can hover inside
the band without settling, on a model with oscillating cK for which no claim of class membership
is made or needed.) The log-log ratio (13) converges regardless, because the possible oscillation
is confined to a fixed multiplicative band, which is crushed by log h → −∞. That is why the
exponent, and hence α, survives as a Q-functional even though the lead constant is not pinned by
the argument.
