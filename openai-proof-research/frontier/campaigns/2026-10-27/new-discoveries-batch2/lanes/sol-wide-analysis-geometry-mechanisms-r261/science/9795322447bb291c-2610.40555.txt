                                                                                                 Abstract
                                                      We consider a drainage network model represented by a system of coalescing random walks ex-
                                                  hibiting long range dependence before coalescence. This model was introduced by Gangopadhyay et.
                                                  al. [GRS04] and Coletti et. al. [CFD09] proved its convergence to the Brownian web under diffusive
                                                  scaling. Santos et.al. [SVZ24] considered a branching version of this model where branching occurs
                                                  with low probabilities varying with the scaling parameter and showed that any sub-sequential limit
                                                  contains a copy of the Brownian net. It was conjectured that the limit is indeed the Brownian net. In
                                                  this work we prove this conjecture. Further, compared to [SVZ24], we explore the question of scaling
                                                  limit in a wider spectrum of scaling regime. This work contributes to the broader understanding of
                                                  the basin of attraction of the Brownian net.


                                           1      Introduction and our result
                                           The Brownian net, introduced in [SS08], arises as the diffusive scaling limit of a system of branching
                                           and coalescing one-dimensional simple symmetric random walks where walks are independent till the
                                           time of coalescence. The Brownian net is a natural extension of the Brownian web, which originated in
                                           the work of [FINR04] while dealing with the scaling limit of coalescing simple symmetric random walk
                                           paths. Loosely speaking, the Brownian web can be described as coalescing Brownian motions starting
                                           from everywhere in the space-time plane of R2 . The system of coalescing Brownian motions starting
                                           from everywhere had been considered earlier too, initially by Arratia [A79], [A81] and later by Tóth and
                                           Werner [TW98]. In the seminal work of [FINR04] a nice Polish space had been suggested and convergence
                                           conditions to the Brownian web were developed. Since then, the basin of attraction of the Brownian web
                                           has become an object of extensive study (see [FFW05], [NRS05], [CFD09], [CV14], [RSS16A], [CSST21],
                                           [VZ23]). In this work we study convergence in distribution for a diffusively scaled branching drainage
                                           network model to the Brownian net. The speciality of our model is that the system of one-dimensional
                                           random walks starting from a random subset of the space and time lattice Z × Z exhibits long range
                                           dependence before coalescence.
                                               Drainage network models are used to explain various scaling relations empirically observed for many
                                           river networks. Scheidegger [S67] was the first to propose a directed network where each source empties
                                           in one of its two neighbours in a preferred direction. Howard [H71] removed the restriction of drainage
                                           to neighbouring sites only and proposed a more general network model which was later analysed by
                                           Gangopadhyay et. al. [GRS04] (see also [CFD09] and [RSS16B]). This random directed network has
                                               ∗ ravi101048@gmail.com † kumarjit.saha@ashoka.edu.in

been referred here as the Howard’s network which gives a natural model of one dimensional coalescing
random walks with long range interactions and non-crossing paths. Coletti et. al. [CFD09] proved that
under diffusive scaling, this model belongs to the basin of attraction of the Brownian web. This result was
later strengthened in two subsequent works, [RSS16B] and [CV14] in different directions. In Subsection
1.1 we describe the Howard’s network precisely.
    It is natural to consider a branching extension of the Howard’s Network where branching occurs (with
‘small’ probability depending on the scaling coefficient) and ask whether it converges to the Brownian
net. Santos et. al. [SVZ24] considered a particular variant of branching mechanism and showed that
under diffusive scaling, the resultant sequence is tight and any sub-sequential limit contains a copy of the
Brownian net. It has been conjectured in [SVZ24] that the Brownian net is indeed the scaling limit. In
this work we consider the same variant of branching event, and prove convergence to the Brownian net.
In addition, we would like to highlight that compared to [SVZ24], we explore the question of scaling limit
in a wider spectrum of scaling regime (see Remark 1.3).
    We should mention here that unlike the Brownian web, the universality class of the Brownian net has
largely remains unexplored. The work by Etheridge et. al. [EFS17] provides the only other example in
the basin of attraction of the Brownian net with a coalescing system of dependent paths where within
a compact window, paths interact with each other. In a major breakthrough, Sun et. al. [SSY24]
proved that the branching coalescing system of one-dimensional non-simple random walks, under the
optimal 3 + ϵ moment finite condition, converge to the Brownian net. Though the random walk paths,
considered in [SSY24], are independent till the time of coalescence, because of the crossing nature of
paths the convergence question becomes much harder to analyse. Our result contributes to the broader
understanding of the basin of attraction of the Brownian net. Our work is based on a careful analysis of
the nature of interaction between two paths in the branching Howard network and our technique gives
an independent and efficient self-contained proof for convergence to the Brownian net (see Remark 6.8).
Our argument is essentially based on the technique used by Etheridge et. al. in [EFS17]. However, it
should be noted that the network of paths considered in [EFS17] are independent as long as distance
between paths are greater than a fixed constant, whereas the paths in Howard network have long rage
interactions. We want to highlight that extending the argument for paths with long range interactions
is non-trivial and to do that we do a closer analysis of joint evolution of Howard paths. We believe that
with suitable modifications, this technique can be applied to some other systems of branching coalescing
dependent paths as well.
    In Subsection 1.1 we describe our model and state our main results. In Section 2 we provide a brief
overview on the Brownian web, Brownian net and we discuss relevant convergence conditions. In Section
3 we describe a dual process for the Howard network and discuss its behaviour under the proposed
branching scheme. The main technical part of this paper is the analysis of the interaction between two
(dual) Howard paths in the branching Howard network, which is presented in Section 4. This analysis
allows us to partition the evolution time of the joint process of two dual paths into three categories which
is done in Section 5. Finally, we prove convergence to the Brownian net in Section 6.

2.2     Brownian net
In this section we describe the Brownian net which generalizes the Brownian web by allowing paths to
branch. The Brownian net was introduced by Sun and Swart in [SS08]. Brownian net with drift parameter
