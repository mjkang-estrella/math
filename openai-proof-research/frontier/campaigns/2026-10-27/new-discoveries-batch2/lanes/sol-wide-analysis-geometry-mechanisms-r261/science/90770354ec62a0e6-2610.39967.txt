                                                                                      1. Introduction

                                             A finite set S in a real normed space X is equilateral if there is a number λ > 0 such that

                                                                            ∥x − y∥ = λ,        x, y ∈ S,   x ̸= y.

                                           Let e(X) denote the largest cardinality of an equilateral subset of X. Petty’s equilateral-set
                                           conjecture [7] asserts that
                                                                                e(X) ≥ dim X + 1
                                           for every finite-dimensional real normed space. The Euclidean regular simplex shows that this
                                           proposed universal lower bound is sharp. Equilateral sets are antipodal [7]. The theorem of
                                           Danzer and Grünbaum [1] therefore yields e(X) ≤ 2dim X . For background on equilateral sets
                                           and their relation to Minkowski geometry, see [9, 11].
                                              The conjecture is immediate in dimension one. In the plane, choose a unit vector u and a
                                           continuous arc γ on the unit circle from u to −u. The function t 7→ ∥γ(t) − u∥ takes the values
                                           0 and 2, and hence takes the value 1. This gives the equilateral triangle {0, u, γ(t)}. Petty [7]
                                           proved the three-dimensional case, and Kobos [4] gave an alternative proof. Makeev [6] proved
                                           the four-dimensional case. The present paper establishes the next case.

                                           Theorem 1.1. Every five-dimensional real normed space contains an equilateral set of six points.
                                           Equivalently, for every norm N on R5 there are x0 , . . . , x5 ∈ R5 such that x0 = 0 and

                                                                            N (xi − xj ) = 1,       0 ≤ i < j ≤ 5.
                                                                                                1
   The theorem concerns the existence of an equilateral set; it does not require a prescribed set
to extend. This distinction is relevant because maximal equilateral sets can be small [7, 10].
Nor do we require the six points to be affinely independent. Configurations of affine rank four
occur in the argument and must be retained.
   We argue by contradiction. The absence of a six-point equilateral set persists under suffi-
ciently small perturbations of a norm. A hypothetical counterexample can therefore be chosen
smooth and strongly convex. Join it to the Euclidean norm and perturb the path, keeping
the endpoints fixed. All centered, labeled unit-equilateral configurations along the path form a
compact oriented eleven-manifold whose boundary is the Euclidean configuration space O(5).
   The main issue is the behavior of the configuration matrix at rank loss. The classification of
Schürmann and Swanepoel [8, Theorem 2.3] excludes six equilateral points in a strictly convex
three-dimensional normed space. Thus every matrix on the bordism has rank at least four. At
rank four its kernel is a line of affine relations. Strict antipodality gives an explicit inequality
for this line. Compactness places the relevant lines in a region with a uniform balance margin,
for which we construct an equivariant graph model.
   To finish, we cut the bordism at a small positive determinant level. Positive polar decompo-
sition maps the resulting quotient bordism to SO(5)/C5 , and is the identity on the Euclidean
boundary. The other boundary factors through a bundle over a graph with fiber SO(5). Since
H9 (SO(5); F5 ) = 0, its tenth homology is generated by fiber classes. Each fiber maps to SO(5)/C5
with degree five, so this boundary has zero image over F5 . It cannot cancel the nonzero funda-
mental class of the Euclidean boundary.
   Section 2 recalls the transversality and homology tools. Section 3 carries out the smooth
reduction, establishes the constraints on six-point configurations, and constructs the bordism
and its relation-line graph. Section 4 computes the required homology, proves the determinant-
cut obstruction, and concludes the proof of Theorem 1.1.


                                        2. Preliminaries

   All vector spaces are real and finite-dimensional. We fix auxiliary Euclidean inner products
and, whenever determinants are used, orientations. The notation | · |, orthogonal complements
and adjoints refer to these inner products. A norm is smooth if it is C ∞ away from zero, strictly
convex if its unit sphere contains no nontrivial line segment, and strongly convex if D2 (N 2 )(u)
is positive definite for u ̸= 0. Strong convexity implies strict convexity: on a segment avoiding
zero, N 2 is strictly convex, and a segment through zero cannot lie in the unit sphere.

Lemma 3.5. Every six-point equilateral configuration in a strictly convex norm on R5 has affine

Proof. By the classification theorem of Schürmann and Swanepoel [8, Theorem 2.3], a normed

with strict convexity. If the affine rank of our tuple were at most three, translating its affine

Remark 4.4. For n ≥ 6, the rank bound and graph reduction above give no corresponding control
of (n + 1)-point configurations. With seven labels, c = (1/3, 1/3, 1/3, −1/2, −1/2, 0, 0) satisfies
S(c) = 1 and osc(c) = 5/6, but four sign cells meet at its two zero coordinates. Thus the pairwise-
intersection construction of Lemma 3.7 fails; appending zeros gives the same example with
more labels. Without suitable rank and relation-space control, Lemma 4.1 does not reduce the
determinant-level homology to fiber classes, so the covering-degree cancellation is unavailable.
This limitation does not decide the conjecture in the remaining dimensions.

Five-dimensional Petty problem
Objective
Prove that every five-dimensional real normed vector space contains six equilateral points. Pre-
cisely, for every norm ∥ · ∥ on R5 , prove that there exist distinct points x0 , . . . , x5 such that

                                ∥xi − xj ∥ = 1        (0 ≤ i < j ≤ 5).

The norm is arbitrary; affine independence of the six points is not required.
Research hint
Seek an appropriate differential-topological reformulation while preserving the original geomet-
ric constraints. Differential geometry and algebraic topology may help; consult Lu–Yang’s A
Solution to Banach’s Isometric Conjecture for methodological inspiration, not a ready-made
argument.
Working requirements

 – Supply a complete, independently checkable proof, including all essential intermediate argu-
   ments and precise references for external results.
 – Distinguish proved statements, conjectures, failed approaches, and unresolved gaps. Never
