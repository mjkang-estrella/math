                                                                                       Abstract
                                                     We characterize the cases in which the minimal eigenvalues of interchange-process
                                                 Laplacians on two Specht modules can be compared up to a positive multiplicative
                                                 constant, independent of the edge rates. In the comparable cases, we establish an
                                                 inverse-polynomial lower bound for this constant. We conjecture that the constant can
                                                 always be taken to be 2/3.
                                                    We apply these ideas to the mixing times of the interchange and exclusion processes.
                                                 Using the comparison method of Hermon, Kozma and Salez, we prove that our
                                                 conjecture implies a conjecture of Oliveira on the mixing time of the interchange
                                                 process. We also obtain unconditional mixing-time bounds for both processes.

Theorem 1.7. There is a universal constant c > 0 such that the following holds. Let G be

1.3    The proof ideas
Theorem 1.3 has two directions. The first implication follows from the following lemma,
which may be of independent interest:

Lemma 1.11. Let G = ([n], w) be a weighted graph, whose connected components have
sizes τ1 ≥ τ2 ≥ · · · ≥ τr > 0. Let σ ⊢ n. Then λmin (G, σ) = 0 if and only if σ ⊵ τ .

    For σ = [n − 1, 1], Lemma 1.11 is equivalent to the fact that G is connected if and only
if λmin (G, σ) ̸= 0. For a general nontrivial σ, it describes the condition that λmin (G, σ) ̸= 0
in terms of weaker connectivity notions.
    Lemma 1.11 is the main resource in proving the direction (1) ⇒ (2) in Theorem 1.3. It
implies, in particular, that if σ ̸⊵ τ , then for a graph G whose connected component sizes
are given by τ , we have λmin (G, τ ) = 0 while λmin (G, σ) ̸= 0.
    The second implication in Theorem 1.3 follows from the 1/n3 bound in Theorem 1.4.
The proof of this bound uses the concept of a reducing graph introduced in [1]. A reducing
graph H for the partitions σ, τ had the property that given a weighted graph G, if H ≤ G
and λmin (G − H, σ) ≤ λmin (G − H, τ ), then λmin (G, σ) ≤ λmin (G, τ ). Therefore, in proving
that σ spectrally dominates τ , we could assume that G does not contain copies of H.
    We develop a parallel concept for inequalities of the form λmin (G, σ) ≤ Cλmin (G, τ )
and use it to prove Theorem 1.4.
    For quasi-complete graphs, we use the fact that the Laplacian is diagonal in the Gelfand–
Tsetlin basis and there are well known formulas for the corresponding eigenvalues. Given
these explicit eigenvalues, we prove the 2/3 bound by a combinatorial argument.
    For the minimal eigenvalue estimate in Theorem 1.7, we start by proving

                                   λmin (G, τ ) ≥ kγ − ck (G),

5     Linear lower bounds on minimal eigenvalues
We now switch gears and turn to bounds on the minimal eigenvalue λmin (G, τ ) in terms of
γ := γ(G) = λmin (G, [n − 1, 1]). In other words, we seek lower bounds on the constants
C(τ, [n − 1, 1]) for arbitrary nontrivial partitions τ . For this purpose, we realize the relevant
Specht modules on ordered tuples and compare their energies with vertex energies. We
then use heat-kernel weights to control the error in that comparison.
