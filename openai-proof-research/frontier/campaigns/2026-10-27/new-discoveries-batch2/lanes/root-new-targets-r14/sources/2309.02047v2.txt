                                                  Abstract. We consider weighted Chebyshev polynomials on the unit circle
                                                  corresponding to a weight of the form (z − 1)s where s > 0. For integer values
                                                  of s this corresponds to prescribing a zero of the polynomial on the boundary.
                                                  As such, we extend findings of Lachance, Saff and Varga [16], to non-integer
                                                  s. Using this generalisation, we are able to relate Chebyshev polynomials on
                                                  lemniscates and other, more established, categories of Chebyshev polynomials.
                                                  An essential part of our proof involves the broadening of the Erdős–Lax in-
                                                  equality to encompass powers of polynomials. We believe that this particular
                                                  result holds significance in its own right.

                                             Keywords Weighted Chebyshev polynomials, Polynomial inequalities, Erdős–
                                           Lax inequality, Powers of polynomials

Chebyshev polynomial corresponding to w. These polynomials exhibit further
structure when E is a real set. In this case, Tnw is a real polynomial characterised
by possessing an alternating set consisting of n+1 points. By this we mean that if
P is a monic polynomial of degree n, corresponding to which there are associated

   Extremal polynomials associated with the sets Em for m ∈ N have a long history
of study. Already Ullman showed that the zeros of the so-called Faber polynomials
for odd degrees corresponding to E2 , distribute “conformally” about the bounding
curve, see [41]. In effect, Ullman’s result says that the normalised zero distribution
corresponding to the sequence of odd-degree Faber polynomials converge weakly
to the equilibrium measure of E2 . He obtained this result by first transforming
the domain to the unit disk, similar to the transformation in (14). Peherstorfer
and Steinbauer established a connection between different classes of weighted
Lq extremal polynomials, q ∈ [1, ∞), on circles and Em , again using the same
change of variables as in (14), see [25, Proposition 6]. Miña Dı́az, in his doctoral
thesis, considered Bergman-Carleman polynomials on the closely related sets {z :
|z m −1| ≤ r} for r > 1 and determined their complete asymptotical behaviour. In
particular, he established that the resulting normalised zero distributions converge
weakly to equilibrium measure on Em for certain subsequences, see [22, Theorem
IV.1.1]. The asymptotics for the Bergman-Carleman polynomials corresponding
to {z : |z m − 1| < r} where r < 1 were determined by Gustafsson et al., see
[13, Proposition 7.3]. We return to considerations of Chebyshev polynomials
corresponding to the lemniscatic family {Em } in the final section.

nomials; the result can be suitably extended to allow for powers of polynomials.
We believe that Theorem 4 is of independent interest and therefore also present
related consequences that our result provides. In particular, we prove a general-
isation of the classical Erdős–Lax inequality which allows for the function f to
have zeros away from the unit circle in addition to the zeros on the boundary.
   Since Lax’s proof several articles on the Erdős–Lax inequality have been writ-
ten. Some of these have provided new ways of proof and others have generalised
the result, see e.g. [4, 19]. However, to the authors’ knowledge, none have con-
sidered non-integer powers of polynomials as in Theorem 4.
1.4. Outline. In Section 2 we prove Theorem 4 and discuss how it relates to
classical results for polynomials. We further extend the equality of Theorem 4 to
an inequality which allows for zeros of the functions to lie outside the closed unit
disk. As a consequence, we show that the classical Erdős–Lax inequality can be
extended beyond the setting of polynomials to powers of polynomials.
