                                                                                            Abstract
                                                       We investigate two conjectured spectral graph theoretic strengthenings of Turán’s
                                                   theorem. Let µ1 ≥ . . . ≥ µn denote the eigenvalues of a graph G with n vertices, m
                                                   edges and clique number ω(G).
                                                       The concise version of Turán’s theorem is that n/(n − d) is a lower bound for the
                                                   clique number ω(G), where d is the√average degree. Our first conjecture is that d
                                                   can be replaced in this bound with s+ , where s+ is the sum of the squares of the
                                                   positive eigenvalues. We prove this conjecture for triangle-free, weakly perfect and
                                                   Kneser graphs and for almost all graphs. We have also used various software tools to
                                                   search for a counter-example.
                                                       Nikiforov proved a spectral version of Turán’s theorem that

                                                                                            2m(ω(G) − 1)
                                                                                    µ21 ≤                ,
                                                                                               ω(G)

                                                   and Bollobás and Nikiforov conjectured that for G 6= Kn

                                                                                                2m(ω(G) − 1)
                                                                                  µ21 + µ22 ≤                .
                                                                                                   ω(G)

                                                   For our second conjecture, we propose that for all graphs (µ21 + µ22 ) in this inequality
                                                   can be replaced by the sum of the squares of the ω(G) largest eigenvalues, provided
                                                   they are positive. We prove the conjecture for weakly perfect, Kneser, and classes
                                                   of strongly regular graphs. We also provide experimental evidence and describe how
                                                   the bound can be applied. Liu and Ning [20] published a wide-ranging paper entitled
                                                   “Unsolved Problems in spectral graph theory”, and these two conjectures were placed
                                                   second and fourth in their list of such problems.

4 Generalising a conjecture due to Bollobás and
Nikiforov
We propose the following conjecture that provides a lower bound for ω(G) using up
to ω(G) eigenvalues. It considers the sum of the squares of the ω(G) largest eigen-
values, provided they are positive. Conjectures 2 and 3 were placed fourth and third
respectively in [20].

Conjecture 2. For any non-empty graph G, the clique number ω(G) satisfies the
bound
                                      2m(ω(G) − 1)
          µ21 + µ22 + . . . + µ2ℓ ≤                ,   where ℓ = min (n+ , ω(G)).     (3)
                                         ω(G)
   This conjecture is exact, for example, for complete regular multipartite graphs.

The obvious question is: why do we set ℓ = min (n+ , ω(G))? The answer, referring to
Section 4.2.1 below, is that if ℓ = n+ then we have a lower bound for 2m(χ − 1)/χ,
so ℓ ≤ n+ . Below we provide an example showing that ℓ must be chosen such that
ℓ ≤ ω(G).
   Conjecture 2 strengthens the following conjecture due to Bollobás and Nikiforov [5]:

Conjecture 3 (Bollobás and Nikiforov). Let G be a Kω(G)+1 -free graph of order at
least ω(G) + 1 with m edges. Then

                                               2m(ω(G) − 1)
                                 µ21 + µ22 ≤                .                         (4)
                                                  ω(G)

As noted in [5], Conjecture 3 is not valid for complete graphs. The conjecture also
places a lot of emphasis on µ2 . Zhang [27] proved Conjecture 3 for regular graphs,
using that µ2 > 0 in his proof. Lin, Ning and Wu [19] proved the conjecture for
triangle-free graphs. It should be noted that the cycle C7 provides an example of a
triangle-free graph for which
                                                  2m(ω(G) − 1)
                              µ21 + µ22 + µ23 >                .
                                                     ω(G)

This example demonstrates that ℓ must be chosen such that ℓ ≤ ω(G). It should be
noted that there exist graphs G with n+ < ω(G) < χ(G). For example, the complement
of the folded 7-cube on 64 vertices has n+ = 8, ω(G) = 22 and χ(G) = 32.

                                                6
    The cycle C5 provides an example of a triangle-free graph for which µ2n + µ2n−1 > m.
A proof of Conjecture 2 or 3 will therefore need to incorporate asymmetry between
positive and negative eigenvalues. For example, the proof in [19] uses that µ1 ≥ −µn .
This contrasts with the symmetry between positive and negative eigenvalues to be
found in some lower bounds for the chromatic number ([1],[11],[25]).
    We have the following experimental and theoretical results to support the conjec-
ture. As proved in Section 4.2.1, Conjecture 2 is true when ω(G) = χ(G). In searching
for a counterexample we therefore focus on graphs with ω(G) < χ(G). We also seek
proofs for graph families with ω(G) < χ(G), but it is hard to find such families where
both ω(G) and the spectrum are known.

with up to 50 vertices, and we found no counterexample. For about 35% of these
graphs, Conjecture 2 outperforms Conjecture 3.

4.3     Applying Conjecture 2
Conjecture 2 is unusual because ω(G) appears on both sides of the inequality. It is
therefore helpful to describe how the Conjecture can be used to bound ω(G), on the
assumption the conjecture is true. This is best done with examples.
    Consider the graph Circulant(16,(1,2,3,4)) on 16 vertices with 64 edges with spec-
trum (81 , 4.0272 , 03 , −0.3322 , −1.1982 , −24 , −2.4962 ). This graph has ω(G) = 5 but for
the purpose of this example we will assume we do not know this. All we can say is
that 2 ≤ ω(G) ≤ χ ≤ 1 + µ1 = 9. We therefore begin with ω(G) = 2 and then keep
increasing ω(G) until the Conjecture is satisfied. So for example since the Conjecture
fails when ω(G) = 2 then we know ω(G) > 2. Note that n+ = 3. Therefore with
   • ω(G) = 2, ℓ = 2 so µ21 + µ22 = 80.2 > 2m(ω(G) − 1)/ω(G) = 64 which implies
     ω(G) > 2.
   • ω(G) = 3, ℓ = 3 so µ21 + µ22 + µ23 = 96.4 > 2m(ω(G) − 1)/ω(G) = 85.3 which
     implies ω(G) > 3.
   • ω(G) = 4, ℓ = 3 so µ21 + µ22 + µ23 = 96.4 > 2m(ω(G) − 1)/ω(G) = 96 which implies
     ω(G) > 4.
   • ω(G) = 5, ℓ = 3 so µ21 + µ22 + µ23 = 96.4 < 2m(ω(G) − 1)/ω(G) = 102.4 which
     implies ω(G) ≥ 5.
    Therefore for this graph Conjecture 2 produces a lower bound for the clique number
which equals the clique number.
    As a second example consider Barbell(8) with n = 16, m = 57 and spectrum
(7.141 , 6.891 , −0.141 , −112 , −1.881 ). It is immediate that ω(G) = χ(G) = 8, but as-
sume we do not know this for the purposes of this example. Note that n+ = 2 so ℓ = 2
for all ω(G), and Conjecture 2 reduces to Conjecture 3. Therefore with:
   • ω(G) = 7, ℓ = 2 so µ21 + µ22 = 98.45 > 2m(ω(G) − 1)/ω(G) = 97.7 which implies
     ω(G) > 7.
   • ω(G) = 8, ℓ = 2 so µ21 + µ22 = 98.45 < 2m(ω(G) − 1)/ω(G) = 99.7 which implies
     ω(G) ≥ 8.
    However ω(G) ≤ χ ≤ 1 + µ1 = 8.14 so using spectral information alone ω(G) = 8.
It is worth noting that the Hoffman [17] lower bound for the chromatic number gives
that:
                                 µ1       7.14
                           1+         =1+      = 4.8 ≤ χ(G).
                                |µn |     1.88
   Therefore in this case both conjectured lower bounds for the clique number outper-
form a well known lower bound for the chromatic number. However the full version of
the Hoffman bound [17] is that µ1 + µn−χ+2 + . . . + µn ≤ 0, which does imply χ = 8.

5     Conclusions
The question posed by Conjecture 1 is whether Wilf’s bound provides another exam-
ple where µ2 can be replaced by s+ ? If so, are there further bounds in which this
strengthening can be made?
    The question posed by Conjecture 2 is whether the sign of eigenvalues is relevant
to lower bounds for the clique number, since it is this refinement to Conjecture 3 that
enables Conjecture 2 to be exact for Kn , whereas Kn is excluded in Conjecture 3. Can
Zhang’s [27] elegant proof of Conjecture 3 for regular graphs be adapted to enable a
proof of Conjecture 2 for regular graphs?
