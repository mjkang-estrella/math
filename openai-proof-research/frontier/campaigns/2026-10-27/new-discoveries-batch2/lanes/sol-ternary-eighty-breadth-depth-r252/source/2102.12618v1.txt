                                                    Abstract. Let E/Q be an optimal elliptic curve, −D be a negative fundamental discriminant
                                                    coprime to the conductor N of E/Q and let E −D /Q be the twist of E/Q by −D. A conjecture
                                                    of Agashe predicts that if E −D /Q has analytic rank 0, then the square of the order of the
                                                    torsion subgroup of E −D /Q divides the product of the order of the Shafarevich-Tate group of
                                                    E −D /Q and the orders of the arithmetic component groups of E −D /Q, up to a power of 2. This
                                                    conjecture can be viewed as evidence for the second part of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer
                                                    conjecture for elliptic curves of analytic rank zero. We provide a proof of a slightly more general
                                                    statement without using the optimality hypothesis.

Proposition 2.1. Let E/Q be an elliptic curve defined over Q of conductor N, and let d 6= ±1
be any square-free integer that is coprime to N. Then E d (Q) cannot contain a rational point
of order 5 or 7.
Proof. We prove the proposition by contradiction. Let ℓ be equal to 5 or 7, assume that E d (Q)
contains a point of order ℓ, and that d 6= ±1. Since E d /Q has a Q-rational point of order ℓ,
Proposition 8.4 in Chapter XV of [6] implies that E d /Q is semi-stable away from ℓ.
   Assume that there exists an odd prime p with p | d. Then p ∤ N because d and N are
coprime. Therefore, Proposition 1 of [4] implies that E d /Q has reduction type I∗0 modulo p.
As a result, since E d /Q is semi-stable away from ℓ, we obtain that p can only be equal to ℓ.
However, Proposition 2.2 below implies that E d /Q cannot have reduction of type I∗0 modulo ℓ.
Therefore, d = ±2 since d is square-free.
   Assume now that d = −2 or 2. We will arrive at a contradiction. Since we assume that d
and N are coprime, we get that 2 ∤ N. Since E/Q has good reduction modulo 2, by Table II
of [4] (see also the hypotheses for this table at the bottom of page 58 of [4]) we obtain that
E d /Q has reduction of type I∗8 or II modulo 2. In [4], good reduction is denoted as usual with
the symbol I0 , and our twist E d is denoted by E χ . Note that Table II is independent of the
existence of a torsion point. We now use our assumption that E d has a Q-rational point of
order ℓ. Since E d has a Q-rational point of order ℓ, it has semi-stable reduction modulo 2,
which is a contradiction. This proves that E d (Q) cannot contain a point of order ℓ.         

Proposition 2.5. Let E/Q be an elliptic curve over Q of conductor N and let d 6= ±1, ±3 be
a square-free integer coprime to N. Then E d (Q) cannot contain a point of order 3.
Proof. The proof is by contradiction. Assume that E d (Q) contains a point of order 3 and that d
has a prime divisor p 6= 3. Assume first that p 6= 2. Since p ∤ N, Proposition 1 of [4] implies that
E d /Q has reduction of type I∗0 modulo p. However, this is impossible because by Proposition
2.4, E d (Q) cannot have reduction of type I∗0 modulo p for p 6= 3. Assume now that p = 2. Since
2 ∤ N, we obtain that E/Q has good reduction modulo 2 and, hence, by Table II of [4] (see also
the hypotheses for this table at the bottom of page 58 of [4]), we get that E d /Q has reduction
of type I∗8 or II modulo 2. However, E d /Q cannot have reduction of type I∗8 or II modulo 2 by
Proposition 2.4. This implies that 2 ∤ d. Therefore, d can only be divisible by 3 so d = ±1 or
±3.                                                                                               
