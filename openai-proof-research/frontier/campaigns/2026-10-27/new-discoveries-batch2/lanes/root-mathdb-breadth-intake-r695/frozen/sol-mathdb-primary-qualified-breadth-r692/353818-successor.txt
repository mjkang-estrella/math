                                                       Conflict-free chromatic index of bipartite graphs
                                                                             Yuxin Jin∗              Yuping Gao†



                                                                                           Abstract




arXiv:2604.24183v1 [math.CO] 27 Apr 2026
                                                       An edge coloring of a graph G is called conflict-free if, for every edge, its closed
                                                   neighborhood contains a color that appears exactly once. The least number of colors
                                                   required for such a coloring is the conflict-free chromatic index of G, denoted by χ′CF (G).
                                                   Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło conjectured that χ′CF (G) ≤ 3 for any bipartite graph
                                                   G without isolated vertices. In this paper, we confirm this conjecture.
                                                   Keywords: conflict-free edge coloring; conflict-free chromatic index; bipartite graph;
                                                   tree


                                           1       Introduction
                                           In this paper, all graphs considered are finite and simple. Let G be a graph. A vertex (resp.
                                           edge) k-coloring φ of G is an assignment of k colors 1, 2, . . . , k to the vertices (resp. edges)
                                           of G. If any two adjacent vertices (resp. edges) receive distinct colors, then we call φ proper.
                                           The least integer k such that G admits a proper vertex k-coloring (resp. a proper edge
                                           k-coloring) is called the chromatic number (resp. chromatic index ) of G, denoted by χ(G)
                                           (resp. χ′ (G)).
                                               Motivated by frequency assignment in cellular networks, Even, Lotker, Ron, and Smorodin-
                                           sky [2] and Smorodinsky [7] initiated the study of the conflict-free chromatic number of
                                           graphs. Let G be a graph. For a vertex v ∈ V (G), denote by NG (v) the set of neighbors
                                           of v and let NG [v] := NG (v) ∪ {v}. The subscript G may be omitted if it is clear from the
                                           context. The least integer k such that G admits a vertex k-coloring in which a unique color
                                           appears in NG [v] for every vertex v ∈ V (G) is called the conflict-free chromatic number of
                                           G, and is denoted by χCF (G).
                                               Dębski and Przybyło [1] proposed the edge variant, known as conflict-free chromatic
                                           index. Let G be a graph and φ be an edge k-coloring of G. We denote by EG (v) the set of
                                           edges incident with a vertex v ∈ V (G), and define EG [uv] := EG (u) ∪ EG (v) to be the closed
                                           neighborhood of any given edge uv ∈ E(G). The subscript G may be omitted if clear from
                                           context. An edge uv is satisfied by φ if there exists a color which appears exactly once in
                                           EG [uv]. We make a convention that we use the same terminology when φ is a partial edge
                                           coloring, that is, when φ assigns colors only to a subset of E(G). The coloring φ is said to

a coloring is the conflict-free chromatic index of G, denoted by χ′CF (G).
    Since every proper edge coloring is conflict-free, we have χ′CF (G) ≤ χ′ (G). Kamyczura
and Przybyło [5] obtained an asymptotically tight upper bound for regular graphs. Kamy-
czura, Meszka, and Przybyło [4] proved that χ′CF (G) ≤ ⌈3 log2 ∆(G)⌉ + 1 for any graph G
and χ′CF (G) ≤ 4 for bipartite graphs. For complete bipartite graphs, the authors observed
that χ′CF (Kn,m ) = 3 whenever min{n, m} ≥ 3. Therefore, a universal upper bound of 3 for
bipartite graphs would be best possible. This observation leads to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1 (Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło [4]). If G is a bipartite graph without
isolated vertices, then χ′CF (G) ≤ 3.

    In this paper, we resolve Conjecture 1.1 in Theorem 1.3 by using a new parameter
proposed by Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło [4]. Let φ be an edge coloring of some
subgraph H of a given graph G. Similarly as before we say that an edge uv ∈ E(G) is
satisfied by φ if there exists a color that appears exactly once in EG [uv] ∩ E(H). Denote by
χ′sCF (G) the least number of colors required for coloring a subgraph of G such that all edges
of G are satisfied. Given such a coloring, we can use one additional color for the uncolored
edges to obtain a conflict-free edge coloring of G. Thus, the following lemma holds.

Lemma 1.2 (Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło [4]). For every graph G without isolated
edges, χ′sCF (G) ≤ χ′CF (G) ≤ χ′sCF (G) + 1.

Theorem 1.3. If G is a bipartite graph without isolated vertices, then χ′sCF (G) ≤ 2 and
χ′CF (G) ≤ 3.

   By Theorem 1.3, we can obtain two new upper bounds for general graphs in Theorems 1.4
and 1.5. The first bound is tight, as demonstrated by Kn,m (n ≥ m ≥ 3).

Theorem 1.4. If G is a graph without isolated vertices, then χ′sCF (G) ≤ 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ and
χ′CF (G) ≤ 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ + 1.

Corollary 1.5. If G is a graph without isolated vertices, then χ′CF (G) ≤ 2⌈log2 ∆(G)⌉ + 1.

    By Brooks’ Theorem, Corollary 1.5 follows directly from Theorem 1.4 for all graphs that
are neither complete graphs nor odd cycles. The results for complete graphs and cycles are
established in the following two lemmas, so we omit the proof of Corollary 1.5. Let Kn and
Cn denote the complete graph and the cycle on n vertices, respectively.

Lemma 1.6 (Kamyczura and Przybyło [5]). For all n ≥ 2, χ′CF (Kn ) ≤ ⌈log2 (n − 1)⌉ + 1.

Lemma 1.7 (Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło [4]). For all n ≥ 3, χ′CF (Cn ) = 2.

   For a tree T , Dębski and Przybyło [1], and independently Kamyczura, Meszka, and
Przybyło [4], proved that χ′CF (T ) ≤ 3. The following problem remains open.

Problem 1.8 (Kamyczura, Meszka, and Przybyło [4]). Characterize the family of all trees
T with χ′CF (T ) = 3.

    Problem 1.8 was partially answered by Guo, Li, Li, and Li [3], who gave a linear-time
algorithm for trees without vertices of degree 2. For general graphs G, Li [6] proved that it is
NP-complete to determine whether χ′CF (G) = 2, even if G is a bipartite graph. We present
a necessary and sufficient condition for a tree T to satisfy χ′CF (T ) = 2 in Theorem 1.9.

Theorem 1.9. Let T be a tree with |E(T )| ≥ 2. Then χ′CF (T ) = 2 if and only if there
exists a nonempty proper subset F ⊆ E(T ) such that, for every edge uv ∈ E(T ), one of the
following holds:

    (i) if uv ∈ F , then dF (u) + dF (v) = 2 or (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) − dF (v)) = 1;

 (ii) if uv ∈
            / F , then dF (u) + dF (v) = 1 or (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) − dF (v)) = 2.

   The proofs of Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.4 are given in Section 2. In Section 3, we
prove Theorem 1.9.


2     Upper bounds on conflict-free chromatic index
In this section, we prove our main results using the concept of a Y -dominating set for a
bipartite graph G := G(X, Y ), introduced in Subsection 2.1.

2.1     Preliminaries
Let G be a graph. For a vertex subset S ⊆ V (G), let G[S] be the subgraph of G induced
by S and G − S = G[V (G) \ S]. Let G := G(X, Y ) be a bipartite graph. A subset D ⊆ X
is called a Y -dominating set if N (D) = Y , where N (D) = ∪v∈D N (v). Note that such a
set exists as N (X) = Y . We call D minimal if for every v ∈ D, N (D \ {v}) ̸= Y . For a
Y -dominating set D ⊆ X and x ∈ D, a vertex y ∈ N (x) is called a private neighbor of x
with respect to D if N (y) ∩ D = {x}. The set of private neighbors of x is denoted by P (x).

Lemma 2.1. Let G := G(X, Y ) be a bipartite graph and let D ⊆ X be a Y -dominating set.
Then the following three statements hold.

    (i) D is minimal if and only if for all x ∈ D, we have P (x) ̸= ∅.

 (ii) If D is minimal, then the collection P = {P (x) : x ∈ D} consists of non-empty,
      pairwise disjoint subsets of Y .

(iii) If D is minimal and P = ∪x∈D P (x), then there exists a matching M in G[D ∪ P ]
      covering D.

Proof. For statement (i), D is minimal if and only if for all x ∈ D, the set D \ {x} is not
a Y -dominating set. Equivalently, there exists a vertex y ∈ Y such that N (y) ∩ D = {x},
meaning y ∈ P (x).
    For statement (ii), we have P (x) ̸= ∅ for all x ∈ D by (i). We show that P (x1 )∩P (x2 ) = ∅
for all distinct x1 , x2 ∈ D. Suppose not, let y ∈ P (x1 ) ∩ P (x2 ). Then {x1 , x2 } ⊆ N (y) ∩ D,
contradicting the definition of private neighbor.

    Statement (iii) follows immediately from Statement (ii) by selecting exactly one edge xy
for each x ∈ D such that y ∈ P (x). This collection of edges forms the required matching
M.

2.2   Bipartite graphs
In this subsection, we construct a partial edge 2-coloring of G such that all edges in G are
satisfied. Theorem 1.3 then follows from Lemma 1.2.

Proof of Theorem 1.3. We may assume that G := G(X, Y ) is a connected bipartite graph.
We prove that χ′sCF (G) ≤ 2 and then χ′CF (G) ≤ 3 by Lemma 1.2. Let D ⊆ X be a minimal
Y -dominating set of G. By Lemma 2.1(iii), G[D ∪ P ] has a matching M that covers D. We
construct a partial edge 2-coloring φ of G as follows. For every xy ∈ E(M ), let φ(xy) = 1.
For every vertex y ∈ Y \ V (M ), we choose exactly one vertex x ∈ N (y) ∩ D (which exists
since D is a Y -dominating set) and let φ(xy) = 2. All other edges in G remain uncolored.
    We claim that all edges of G are satisfied by φ and complete the proof of the theorem.
Note that by our construction, every vertex y ∈ Y is incident to exactly one colored edge.
Let xy ∈ E(G) be an arbitrary edge with x ∈ X and y ∈ Y . We show that EG [xy] contains
a unique color in the following two cases.
    Case 1: x ∈ D.
    In this case, x is incident to an edge xp ∈ E(M ) with φ(xp) = 1, where p ∈ P (x). Note
that xp is the only edge in EG (x) colored by 1, because φ assigns color 1 only to edges in M ,
and M is a matching. If there exists some edge x′ y ∈ E(G) with φ(x′ y) = 1, then y ∈ P (x′ )
and N (y) ∩ D = {x′ }. Since x ∈ N (y) ∩ D, we have x = x′ . It implies that color 1 appears
exactly once in EG [xy].
    Case 2: x ∈ X \ D.
    Since each colored edge is incident to a vertex in D, x is not incident to any colored
edge. If y ∈ V (M ), then y ∈ P (x′ ) for some x′ ∈ D, meaning x′ y is the only edge in EG (y)
colored by 1. So 1 appears exactly once in EG [xy]. Assume that y ∈ Y \ V (M ), then color
2 appears exactly once in EG (y) by the definition of φ. Again, since x is not incident to any
colored edge, color 2 appears exactly once in EG [xy].
    In both cases, xy is satisfied. Thus, χ′sCF (G) ≤ 2.

2.3   Upper bound for general graphs
The proof strategy for Theorem 1.4 is similar to the approach used by Kamyczura, Meszka,
and Przybyło [4]. For the sake of completeness, we include the full proof in this subsection.

Proof of Theorem 1.4. Let G be a graph without isolated vertices. We first prove that
χ′sCF (G) ≤ 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ by induction on χ(G). If χ(G) = 2, then the theorem follows
from Theorem 1.3. Assume that χ(G) ≥ 3 and the theorem holds for all graphs G′ with
χ(G′ ) < χ(G).
    Let t := ⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ and k := 2t−1 . Then χ(G) ≤ 2k. Let φ be a proper vertex
2k-coloring of G with color classes V1 , . . . , V2k , where Vi = ∅ for χ(G) < i ≤ 2k if it
exists. Let A := ∪ki=1 Vi and B := ∪2k
                                     i=k+1 Vi . Let H be the bipartite graph consisting of

all edges between A and B in G, and let F be the subgraph of G consisting of all the
remaining edges, where we do not include potential isolated vertices in V (H) or V (F ).
Since E(F ) ⊆ E(G[A]) ∪ E(G[B]), we have χ(F ) ≤ k < χ(G). By the induction hypothesis,
χ′sCF (F ) ≤ 2⌈log2 χ(F )⌉ ≤ 2⌈log2 k⌉ = 2(t − 1). Thus, there exists an edge 2(t − 1)-coloring
φ1 of a subgraph of F such that all edges in F are satisfied. Furthermore, since H is
bipartite, Theorem 1.3 implies there exists an edge coloring φ2 of a subgraph of H using
colors 2t − 1, 2t such that all edges in H are satisfied. Combining φ1 and φ2 , we obtain
χ′sCF (G) ≤ 2t = 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉. By Lemma 1.2, χ′CF (G) ≤ 2⌈log2 χ(G)⌉ + 1.


3    Proof of Theorem 1.9
In this section, we prove Theorem 1.9 by analyzing the property of conflict-free edge coloring.

Proof of Theorem 1.9. First, assume that χ′CF (T ) = 2. Let φ be a conflict-free edge 2-
coloring of T , and let F := {e ∈ E(T ) : φ(e) = 1}. Since both colors are used on T , F is
a nonempty proper subset of E(T ). Let uv ∈ E(T ) and let ni (uv) denote the number of
edges colored by i in ET [uv] for i ∈ {1, 2}. Since φ is conflict-free, either n1 (uv) = 1 or
n2 (uv) = 1.
    If uv ∈ F , then n1 (uv) = dF (u) + dF (v) − 1 and n2 (uv) = (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) −
dF (v)). So either dF (u) + dF (v) = 2 or (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) − dF (v)) = 1. Condition (i)
follows. If uv ∈
               / F , then n1 (uv) = dF (u) + dF (v) and n2 (uv) = (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) −
dF (v)) − 1. So either dF (u) + dF (v) = 1 or (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) − dF (v)) = 2.
Condition (ii) follows.
    Conversely, assume that there exists a nonempty proper subset F ⊆ E(T ) satisfying
conditions (i) and (ii). Let φ be an edge 2-coloring of T such that φ(e) = 1 if e ∈ F and
φ(e) = 2 otherwise. For any edge uv ∈ E(T ), let ni (uv) denote the number of edges colored
by i in ET [uv] for i ∈ {1, 2}.
    If uv ∈ F , then either n1 (uv) = dF (u) + dF (v) − 1 = 1 or n2 (uv) = (dT (u) − dF (u)) +
(dT (v) − dF (v)) = 1 by condition (i). If uv ∈/ F , then either n1 (uv) = dF (u) + dF (v) = 1 or
n2 (uv) = (dT (u) − dF (u)) + (dT (v) − dF (v)) − 1 = 1 by condition (ii). Therefore, all edges
of T are satisfied by φ and we have χ′CF (T ) = 2.
