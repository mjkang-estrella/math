                                                             ON GARDNER’S CONJECTURE




arXiv:2103.11655v2 [math.MG] 2 Sep 2021
                                                                              GÁBOR KUN




                                                  Abstract. Gardner conjectured that if two bounded measurable sets
                                                  A, B ⊂ Rn are equidecomposable by a set of isometries Γ generating
                                                  an amenable group then A and B admit a measurable equidecomposi-
                                                  tion by all isometries. Cieśla and Sabok asked if there is a measurable
                                                  equidecomposition using isometries only in the group generated by Γ.
                                                  We answer this question negatively.


                                                                          1. Introduction
                                             Let Γ be a set of isometries of Rn . We say that the sets A, B ⊆ Rn are
                                          Γ-equidecomposable if there are finite partitions A = ∪kn=1 An , B = ∪kn=1 Bn
                                          and isometries γ1 , . . . , γk ∈ Γ such that Ai = γi Bi holds for every 1 ≤ i ≤ k.
                                          A and B admit a Γ-equidecomposition if and only if there exists a finite set
                                          of generators in Γ such that the bipartite restriction of the Schreier graph
                                          of the generated subgroup hΓi with respect to the classes A and B admits
                                          a perfect matching. The Hall condition is necessary for the existence of an
                                          equidecomposition, and if Γ is finite then it is also sufficient. An equidecom-
                                          position is measurable if the sets in the partitions are Lebesgue measurable.
                                          We recommend Máthé [9] on measurable equidecompositions, Kechris and
                                          Marks [5] on measurable matchings and Tomkowicz and Wagon [13] on the
                                          Banach-Tarski paradox. The Banach-Tarski paradox [1] claims that any two
                                          bounded subsets of R3 with nonempty interior are equidecomposable. A
                                          group is amenable if it admits no paradoxical decomposition. Von Neumann
                                          introduced amenable groups in order to explain the paradox [11]. Mycielski
                                          showed that if two measurable sets are equidecomposable by an amenable
                                          group then they have equal Lebesgue measure [10]. This is the largest class
                                          of groups where the Hall condition may imply the existence of a measurable
                                          equidecomposition. Gardner made the following conjecture.
                                          Conjecture 1. [3] Consider the bounded measurable sets A, B ⊆ Rn and the
                                          set of isometries Γ. Assume that Γ generates an amenable group. If A and
                                          B are Γ-equidecomposable then they admit a measurable equidecomposition.

   The most famous open question on equidecompositions was Tarski’s circle
squaring problem [12] asking if the disc and the square of unit area admit an
equidecomposition. Laczkovich solved this positively [7]. His construction
was not measurable, and left the measurable circle squaring problem open for
decades. Grabowski, Máthé and Pikhurko have found a measurable equide-
composition [4], while Marks and Unger have given a Borel equidecompo-
sition [8]. Both of these works rely heavily on the details of Laczkovich’s
proof. Gardner’s conjecture would provide a simple solution using only the
existence of an equidecomposition.
   Note that Gardner’s conjecture does not require that the isometries in the
measurable equidecomposition are in Γ. Laczkovich has found an example
of two measurable sets admitting a Γ-equidecomposition but no measurable
Γ-equidecomposition [6]. Cieśla and Sabok have studied the conjecture for
probability measure preserving group actions. They asked if the elements of
hΓi are sufficient (Question 4, [2]), and proved this for abelian groups if the
sets are uniformly distributed. We give a negative answer to their question.
Theorem 2. Consider an irrational number 0 < α < 1 and the following
set consisting of four isometries of R:

            Γ = {x 7→ x, x 7→ x + 2α, x 7→ 2 − x, x 7→ 2α − x}.
The intervals [0, 1] and [α, 1 + α] admit a Γ-equidecomposition, but no mea-
surable hΓi-equidecomposition.
   Technically, our example is not on a probability measure space, but on
the real line. However, it can be easily modified to one on the closed cycle.
Note that there is an equidecomposition using one single translation by α.
The restriction of the bipartite Schreier graph of these four isometries in Γ
is essentially Laczkovich’s construction in [6].

                                2. The proof
   Consider the Borel bipartite graph G whose classes I and J are Borel
isomorphic to the intervals [0, 1] and [α, 1 + α], respectively, and the set
of edges corresponds to the union of the restriction of the graph of the
four isometries. See E(G) ⊆ I × J in the figure. Laczkovich showed that
this graph admits a perfect matching, but no measurable perfect matching.
Hence [0, 1] and [α, 1 + α] admit an equidecomposition, but no measurable
equidecomposition using these four isometries.
   Four vertices of G (in different components) have degree one, the other
vertices have degree two. Laczkovich proved that none of the connected
components is an even path, hence every connected component of G is a
bi-infinite path, a semi-infinite path, an even cycle or an odd path. We will
prove the theorem by contradiction showing that if [0, 1] and [α, 1 + α] ad-
mitted a measurable Γ-equidecomposition then G would admit a measurable
perfect matching. The next lemma shows that elements of hΓi do not move
vertices of G too far.
Lemma 3. For every γ ∈ hΓi and y ∈ [0, 1] such that γ(y) ∈ [0, 1] the
distance of the corresponding vertices in I is at most 2|b| in G, where γ(x) =
ax + 2αb + 2c.
Proof. We prove by induction on |b|. If |b| = 0, that is, γ(x) = ax + 2c then
we can easily check those isometries γ such that γ([0, 1])∩ [0, 1] 6= ∅. If a = 1
then c = 0 and γ is the identity. If a = −1 then either c = 0, γ(x) = −x and
y = γ(y) = 0, or c = 1, γ(x) = −x + 2 and y = γ(y) = 1.
   Now consider γ(x) = ax + 2αb + 2c, where |b| > 0, and y ∈ I. If b < 0 and
γ(y) ≤ 1 − 2α then by induction z = γ(y) + 2α = ax + 2α(b + 1) + 2c ∈ I
satisfies dist(y, z) ≤ 2|b + 1| = 2|b| − 2. Since dist(γ(y), z) ≤ 2 we get
dist(y, γ(y)) ≤ 2|b|. If γ(y) > 1 − 2α then consider z = 2 − (γ(y) + 2α) =
−ay − 2α(b + 1) + (2 − 2c) ∈ I. Since |b + 1| = |b| − 1 we get the same
inequalities as in the previous case. If b > 0 and γ(y) > 2α then we can
use z = γ(y) − 2α = ay + 2α(b − 1) + 2c, since |b − 1| = |b| − 1. If b > 0
and γ(y) ≤ 2α then consider z = (2α − γ(y)) = −ay + 2α(1 − b) − 2c as an
intermediate element using that |1 − b| = |b| − 1.                             
   We will study the union of bi-infinite paths of G. Recall that a ray in a
graph is an infinite sequence of vertices in which each vertex appears at most
once and each two consecutive vertices are adjacent. Given a graph H and a
natural number K denote by H K the graph with vertex set V (H K ) = V (H),
two vertices are adjacent in H K if their distance is odd and at most K.
Proposition 4. Let K be an odd natural number and H be an acyclic
2-regular measure preserving bipartite Borel graph over a standard Borel
probability measure space. If H K admits a measurable perfect matching then
H admits a measurable perfect matching, too.
Proof. We denote the probability measure by λ. Let V (H) = V (H K ) =
A ∪ B be the Borel partition of these bipartite graphs into two independent

   Now assume that a ∈ / r(c) and c ∈/ r(a). First, we find such a pair in the
same component that their distance is minimal. If the distance of a and c
is greater than two then consider a vertex d ∈ A between them. The ray
r(d) can not contain both a and c, and d is not contained in any of the two
rays r(a) and r(c). Hence one of the pairs (a, d) and (c, d) will satisfy the
conditions. Therefore, we may assume that a and c are at distance two. Let
b ∈ B denote the vertex between a and c. If b is matched to a vertex in r(a)
then there is a vertex of S(M ) between this vertex and a. If b is matched
to a vertex in r(c) then there is a vertex of S(M ) between this vertex and
c.                                                                          
  We define a measurable perfect matching of H using M . Consider the
components where for any two vertices a, c ∈ A one of the two rays r(a)
and r(c) contains the other. Let us match every vertex a ∈ A in such a
component to the unique vertex in r(a) adjacent to a. This gives a measur-
able almost perfect matching. It can be extended on the nullset of the other
components to a measurable perfect matching.                              
   Suppose for a contradiction that [0, 1] and [α, 1 + α] admit a measurable
Γ-equidecomposition. The set of vertices in bi-infinite paths of G is measur-
able. Lemma 3 and Proposition 4 imply that this set admits a measurable
perfect matching. The other components are even cycles, odd paths or semi-
infinite paths, since G admits a perfect matching and its maximum degree
is two. Therefore, these admit a measurable perfect matching, too. This
contradicts Laczkovich’s result and finishes the proof of the Theorem.
