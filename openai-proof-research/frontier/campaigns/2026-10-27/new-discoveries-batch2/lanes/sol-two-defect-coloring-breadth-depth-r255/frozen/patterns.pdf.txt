                                            1      Introduction

                                           In [8], Füredi and Hajnal considered the problem of determining the maximum of 1’s in a
                                           0–1 matrix of size n ˆ n excluding a fixed subconfiguration, and they conjectured that the
                                           maximum number f2 pn, P q of 1’s in a 0–1 matrix of size n ˆ n excluding a permutation
                                           matrix P as a subconfiguration is bounded by a linear function of n. Klazar [9] proved that
                                           this conjecture, if true, would imply that the Stanley–Wilf conjecture, which states that
                                           the growth rate of every proper permutation class is singly exponential. Thus by proving
                                           Füredi–Hajnal conjecture, Marcus and Tardos [11] also settled Stanley–Wilf conjecture.
                                                In this paper, we are interested in the generalization of both Füredi–Hajnal conjecture and
                                           Stanley–Wilf conjecture to higher dimensions, that is in their generalization to the setting of t-
                                           dimensional matrices (or tensors), with t ą 2. To state these conjectures, we need to introduce
                                           several notions. Let M be an n1 ˆ¨ ¨ ¨ˆnt t-dimensional matrix, a typical entry will be denoted
                                           as Mi1 ,...,it . For r P rts and 1 ď j ď nr , the pr, jq-slice of M is the n1 ˆ ¨ ¨ ¨ ˆ nr´1 ˆ nr`1 ˆ nt
                                           pt ´ 1q-dimensional matrix N defined by Ni1 ,...,it´1 “ Mi1 ,...,ir´1 ,j,ir ,...,it´1 . A slice of M is
                                           an pr, jq-slice of M for some value of r and j.
                                                A t-pattern P is a t-dimensional 0–1 matrix with the property that if Pi1 ,...,it “ Pj1 ,...,jt “ 1
                                           then pi1 , . . . , it q and pj1 , . . . , jt q are either equal or differ at at least two positions. A t-pattern
                                           is free if every slice contains at most one entry 1. Examples of free t-patterns are t-dimensional
                                           permutations, which are the patterns with exactly one entry 1 in every slice, and examples
                                           of non-free t-patterns (for t ą 2) are t-dimensional Latin matrices, which are inductively
                                           defined as follows: 2-dimensional Latin matrices are the permutation matrices, and, for
                                           t ą 2, t-dimensional Latin matrices are the patterns with the property that every slice is a
                                           pt ´ 1q-dimensional Latin matrix. In particular, t-dimensional Latin matrices contain nt´1

 3       Proof of the main results
§ Theorem 3.1 (=Theorem 1.3). Let t ě 2 be an integer. For every sunflower t-pattern P
we have ft pn, P q “ Opnt´1 q.
Proof. As every 2-pattern is free, the result follows from [11] when t “ 2. So we assume
t ě 3. We prove the statement for all integers t ě 3 by induction on the size of the core of P .
     Assume that the core of P is empty, that is that P is a free k ˆ ¨ ¨ ¨ ˆ k t-pattern. Assume
for contradiction that there exists an n ˆ ¨ ¨ ¨ ˆ n t-dimensional 0–1 matrix M avoiding P
that has more than αt pkq nt´1 entries 1. According to Theorem 2.2, the matrix M has a full
(t-dimensional) k ˆ ¨ ¨ ¨ ˆ k-division. Selecting an entry 1 in each cell corresponding to an
entry 1 of P we find a submatrix of M that does not avoid P , contradicting the hypothesis.
Thus, in this case, f pn, P q ď αt pkq nt´1 .
     Now assume that the property has been proved (for all integers t ě 2) for all sunflower
t-patterns with core of size at most ℓ ě 0. Let P be a sunflower t-pattern with a core S of
size ℓ ` 1, and let pcs qsPS be the fixed values of the indices corresponding to the positions in
the core. Let s P S, let P 1 be the sunflower pt ´ 1q-pattern obtained as the ps, cs q-slice of P .
Assume for contradiction that there exists an n ˆ ¨ ¨ ¨ ˆ n t-dimensional 0–1 matrix M avoiding
P that has more than ft1 pn, P 1 q n entries 1. Then there exists 1 ď i ď n such that the
ps, iq-slice M 1 of M contains more than ft´1 pn, P 1 q entries 1, which leads to a contradiction
as M 1 avoids P 1 . Thus ft pn, P q ď ft´1 pn, P 1 q n. Hence, by induction hypothesis, as the core
of P 1 has size ℓ, we have ft pn, P q P Opnt´1 q.                                                 đ

     4     Remarks
    Marcus–Tardos extremal result is the basic underlying the recently intensively investigated
    twin-width of graphs (and other binary structures), see [5, 3, 2, 4, 6, 12]. The results of
    this paper indicate possibility to generalize the twin-width to hypergraphs (and relational
    structures of higher arities). Particularly they lead to twin width of t-partite t-uniform
    hypergraphs. This can be thought as just the first approximation. In the spirit of Szemerédi
    Regularity Lemma for hypergraphs the defining red edges will have to be considered for all
    intermediate arities. However for ordered structures this is possible. The details will appear
    elsewhere.
