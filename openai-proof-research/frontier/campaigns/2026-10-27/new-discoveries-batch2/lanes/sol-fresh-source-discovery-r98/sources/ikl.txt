Log-concavity of inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of paving matroids



    



Matthew H.Y. Xie1 and Philip B. Zhang2

1 College of Science

Tianjin University of Technology, Tianjin 300384, P. R. China

2 College of Mathematical Science & Institute of Mathematics and Interdisciplinary Sciences,
Tianjin Normal University, Tianjin 300387, P. R. China

Email: 1xie@email.tjut.edu.cn,
2zhang@tjnu.edu.cn

  




Abstract.
Gao and Xie (2021) conjectured that the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of any matroid is log-concave.
Although the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial may not always have only real roots, we conjecture that the
Hadamard product of an inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of degree nn and (1+t)n(1+t)^{n} has only real roots.
Using interlacing polynomials and multiplier sequences, we confirm this conjecture for paving matroids. This result allows us to confirm the log-concavity conjecture for these matroids by applying Newton’s inequalities.




AMS Classification 2010: 05A20, 05B35, 33F10, 26C10




Keywords: Log-concavity; inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial; paving matroid




1 Introduction




The goal of this paper is to prove the log-concavity of the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of paving matroids.
Elias, Proudfoot, and Wakefield [8] introduced the notion of the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of a matroid.
Similarly, Gao and Xie [10] introduced the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of a matroid.
Recently, Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang [1] proved that the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials and the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of matroids have only non-negative coefficients.
However, the log-concavity of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials and inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of matroids is still open and only a few results have been obtained, see [20, 19].
A sequence a0,a1,a2,…,ana_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n} is said to be log-concave and has no internal zeros if it satisfies ai2≥ai−1​ai+1a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1} for any 1≤i≤n−11\leq i\leq n-1, and the indices of its nonzero elements form consecutive integers. A polynomial is said to be log-concave if its coefficients form a log-concave sequence. Gao and Xie [10] conjectured that the coefficients of the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of matroids are log-concave and have no internal zeros. We use QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) to denote the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of the matroid MM.




Conjecture 1.1 ([10]).



For any matroid MM, the coefficients of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) form a log-concave sequence and have no internal zeros.





Elias, Proudfoot, and Wakefield [8] conjectured that the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials have only real roots. However, Gao and Xie pointed out that inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials do not necessarily have only real roots. Nevertheless, we conjecture that a variation of inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials has only real roots.
Recall that a matroid is called paving if the size of every circuit is at least the rank of MM.
The main object of this paper is as follows.




Theorem 1.2.



For any paving matroid MM, the coefficients of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) form a log-concave sequence and have no internal zeros.





We prove Theorem 1.2 via the approach of real-rooted polynomials.
Given a polynomial of degree nn




p⁡(t)=a0+a1​t+⋯+an​tn,p(t)=a_{0}+a_{1}t+\cdots+a_{n}t^{n},



we denote




ℬ⁡(p⁡(t))=(n0)​a0+(n1)​a1​t+⋯+(nn)​an​tn,\B(p(t))=\binom{n}{0}a_{0}+\binom{n}{1}a_{1}t+\cdots+\binom{n}{n}a_{n}t^{n},



which is the Hadamard product of (1+t)n(1+t)^{n} and p⁡(t)p(t).
Note that the operator ℬ\B is not linear for two polynomials with distinct degrees.




Theorem 1.3.



For any paving matroid MM, the polynomial ℬ⁡(QM​(t))\B(Q_{M}(t)) has only real roots.





By Newton’s inequalities, Theorem 1.3 implies Theorem 1.2.
In general, we make the following conjecture, which would imply Conjecture 1.1.




Conjecture 1.4.



For any simple matroid MM, the polynomial ℬ⁡(QM​(t))\B(Q_{M}(t)) has only real roots.





Recall that a sequence satisfying the Turán inequality is also called a log-concave sequence.
The higher order Turán inequalities are defined by
4​(ai2−ai−1​ai+1)​(ai+12−ai​ai+2)−(ai​ai+1−ai−1​ai+2)2≥04(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i-1}a_{i+1})(a_{i+1}^{2}-a_{i}a_{i+2})-(a_{i}a_{i+1}-a_{i-1}a_{i+2})^{2}\geq 0.
In recent years, the higher-order Turán inequalities for combinatorial sequences have attracted significant attention, as seen in [4, 13].
A theorem of Mařík [12] states that if a real polynomial
(n0)​a0+(n1)​a1​t+⋯+(nn)​an​tn\binom{n}{0}a_{0}+\binom{n}{1}a_{1}t+\cdots+\binom{n}{n}a_{n}t^{n} of degree n≥3n\geq 3 has only real roots, then a0,a1,…,ana_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n} satisfies the higher-order Turán inequalities.
By Theorem 1.3, we get the following result.




Corollary 1.5.



For any paving matroid MM, the coefficients of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) satisfy the higher order Turán inequalities.





The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we provide the necessary background on real-rooted polynomials.
Section 3 focuses on the log-concavity of the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials of paving matroids, presenting the main proofs and results.
Finally, in Section 4, we propose several conjectures regarding the degree of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t).





2 Interlacing and multiplier sequence




In this section, we recall some useful results of interlacing polynomials and multiplier sequences.
Given two real-rooted polynomials f⁡(t)f(t) and g⁡(t)g(t) with positive leading coefficients, let {ui}\{u_{i}\} be the set of roots of f⁡(t)f(t) and let {vj}\{v_{j}\} be the set of roots of g⁡(t)g(t). We say that g⁡(t)g(t) interlaces f⁡(t)f(t), denoted g⁡(t)≪f⁡(t)g(t)\ll f(t), if either of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1) 



deg⁡f⁡(t)=deg⁡g⁡(t)=n\deg f(t)=\deg g(t)=n and




vn≤un≤vn−1≤⋯≤v2≤u2≤v1≤u1;\displaystyle v_{n}\leq u_{n}\leq v_{n-1}\leq\cdots\leq v_{2}\leq u_{2}\leq v_{1}\leq u_{1};



(2) 



deg⁡f⁡(t)=deg⁡g⁡(t)+1=n\deg f(t)=\deg g(t)+1=n and




un≤vn−1≤⋯≤v2≤u2≤v1≤u1.\displaystyle u_{n}\leq v_{n-1}\leq\cdots\leq v_{2}\leq u_{2}\leq v_{1}\leq u_{1}.





For convenience, we let 0≪f0\ll f and f≪0f\ll 0 for any real-rooted polynomial ff.
Following Brändén [3],
a sequence of real polynomials (f1,…,fm)(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
with positive leading coefficients is said to be interlacing if fi≪fjf_{i}\ll f_{j} for all 1≤i<j≤m1\leq i<j\leq m.
The following lemma plays an important role in our paper.




Lemma 2.1 ([17], Proposition 3.3).



If f0,f1,f2,…,fmf_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m} are real-rooted polynomials with f0≪fmf_{0}\ll f_{m} and
fi−1≪fif_{i-1}\ll f_{i} for all 1≤i≤m1\leq i\leq m, then the polynomial sequence (f0,f1,…,fm)(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}) is interlacing.





We also need the following lemma.




Lemma 2.2 ([17], Proposition 3.5).



Let ff, gg, and hh be real-rooted polynomials with nonnegative coefficients.

• 



If f≪gf\ll g and f≪hf\ll h, then f≪g+hf\ll g+h.

• 



If f≪gf\ll g and h≪gh\ll g, then f+h≪gf+h\ll g.







We also say that the roots of g⁡(t)g(t) and f⁡(t)f(t) are alternating if f⁡(t)≪g⁡(t)f(t)\ll g(t) or g⁡(t)≪f⁡(t)g(t)\ll f(t).
The main tool we use to prove the property of alternating roots is the following Hermite-Kakeya-Obreschkoff theorem.




Theorem 2.3 (Hermite-Kakeya-Obreschkoff, [2, Theorem 2.4], [7, Theorem 4.1]).



Let p⁡(t)p(t) and q⁡(t)q(t) be two polynomials with real coefficients. The roots of p⁡(t)p(t) and q⁡(t)q(t) are
alternating if and only if for any real numbers λ\lambda and μ\mu, the polynomial λ​p​(t)+μ​q​(t)\lambda p(t)+\mu q(t) has only real roots.





However, in this form of the Hermite-Kakeya-Obreschkoff theorem, we cannot determine whether g⁡(t)g(t) interlaces f⁡(t)f(t) or f⁡(t)f(t) interlaces g⁡(t)g(t). To address this, we next recall the Wrońskian notation of two polynomials.
The Wrońskian of two polynomials f⁡(t)f(t) and g⁡(t)g(t) is defined as W⁡[f,g]=f′​g−f​g′W[f,g]=f^{\prime}g-fg^{\prime}. It is easy to see that for any pair of polynomials f⁡(t)f(t) and g⁡(t)g(t) with interlacing roots, the Wrońskian W⁡[f,g]W[f,g] is either nonnegative on all of ℝ\mathbb{R} or nonpositive on all of ℝ\mathbb{R}. Moreover, if W⁡[f,g]≤0W[f,g]\leq 0, then f≪gf\ll g (see [18, P. 4]). Therefore, we only need to check the signs of W⁡[f,g]W[f,g] for certain values of tt.

which is negative.
∎





By combining the above three lemmas and Lemma 2.1, we prove Lemma 3.2.




Proof of Lemma 3.2.



Let f0=ℬ⁡(QUm,d​(t))f_{0}=\B(Q_{U_{m,d}}(t)) and




fh=ℬ⁡(QUm+1−h,d​(t)−QUm+1−h,d−1​(t))​ for ​1≤h≤m.f_{h}=\B(Q_{U_{m+1-h,d}}(t)-Q_{U_{m+1-h,d-1}}(t))\text{\quad for\quad}1\leq h\leq m.

Although we do not know the specific range of λh\lambda_{h}, this does not affect us to prove Theorem 1.3 and 1.2.




Proof of Theorem 1.3 and 1.2 .



By Lemma 2.2,
we have




ℬ⁡(QUm,d​(t))≪∑h=1mλh⋅ℬ⁡(QUh,d​(t)−QUh,d−1​(t))\B(Q_{U_{m,d}}(t))\ll\sum_{h=1}^{m}\lambda_{h}\cdot\B(Q_{U_{h,d}}(t)-Q_{U_{h,d-1}}(t))



for any nonnegative real numbers λh\lambda_{h}.
Thus, the polynomial




ℬ⁡(QUm,d​(t))−∑h=1mλh⋅ℬ⁡(QUh,d​(t)−QUh,d−1​(t))\B(Q_{U_{m,d}}(t))-\sum_{h=1}^{m}\lambda_{h}\cdot\B(Q_{U_{h,d}}(t)-Q_{U_{h,d-1}}(t))



has only real roots for any nonnegative real numbers λh\lambda_{h}.
Suppose that MM is a paving matroid.
By [1, Theorem 1.5], we know that all the coefficients of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) are nonnegative.
By Theorem 2.5, we conclude that the coefficients of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) forms a ⌊d−12⌋\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor-sequence.
By definition, the degree of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) is less than or equal to ⌊d−12⌋\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor. Again, by Theorem 2.5, we know that

Theorem 1.2 follows from the well-known Newton’s inequalities.
∎





It should be noted that although the degree of QUm,d​(t)Q_{U_{m,d}}(t) and QUh,d​(t)−QUh,d−1​(t)Q_{U_{h,d}}(t)-Q_{U_{h,d-1}}(t) for any 1≤d≤m1\leq d\leq m is ⌊(d−1)/2⌋{\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor}, the degree of ℬ⁡(QM​(t))\B(Q_{M}(t)) may be less than ⌊(d−1)/2⌋{\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor}.
In this sense, the polynomial ℬ⁡(QM​(t))\B(Q_{M}(t)) may not equal




ℬ⁡(QUm,d​(t))−∑h=1mλh⋅ℬ⁡(QUh,d​(t)−QUh,d−1​(t)).\B(Q_{U_{m,d}}(t))-\sum_{h=1}^{m}\lambda_{h}\cdot\B(Q_{U_{h,d}}(t)-Q_{U_{h,d-1}}(t)).



This is why we adopt an indirect argument in the second paragraph of our proof of Theorem 1.3.





4 Concluding remarks




Based on the log-concavity properties established in this paper, we shall study the degrees of the inverse Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials and propose several intriguing conjectures.
A matroid MM is said to be non-degenerate if its Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial has degree ⌊(rk​(M)−1)/2⌋{\lfloor(\mbox{rk}(M)-1)/2\rfloor}, the maximal possible degree, see [11, 16].
Gedeon, Proudfoot, and Young [11] conjectured that there are interlacing properties for the roots of the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial of a non-degenerate matroid MM and those of a contraction M/eM/{e} by one element.
However, similar properties do not hold for ℬ⁡(QM​(t))\B(Q_{M}(t)).
Nevertheless, one can still investigate the relationship between the degrees of PM​(t)P_{M}(t) and QM​(t)Q_{M}(t).
Vecchi [16] obtained the following result for a matroid of odd rank and pointed out that this is not true when MM is of even rank.




Theorem 4.1 ([16]).



Let MM be a matroid of odd rank rr. Then




[t⌊r−12⌋]​PM​(t)=[t⌊r−12⌋]​QM​(t).\left[t^{\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\right]P_{M}(t)=\left[t^{\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor}\right]Q_{M}(t).



In particular, a matroid MM of odd rank rr is non-degenerate if and only if QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) has degree ⌊r−12⌋\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor.





Theorem 4.1 inspires us to propose the following related conjecture.




Conjecture 4.2.



For any matroid MM, the degrees of PM​(t)P_{M}(t) and QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) are the same.





We verified Conjecture 4.2 for all matroids on at most 9 elements, as well as for the cycle matroids of graphs with at most 9 vertices.
Braden, Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang [1] proved that the coefficients of both PM​(t)P_{M}(t) and QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) are nonnegative.
But are the coefficients strictly positive? We provide
the following result.




Proposition 4.3.



For any paving matroid MM, each coefficient of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) is positive, except possibly the coefficient of t⌊rk​(M)−12⌋t^{\lfloor\frac{\text{rk}(M)-1}{2}\rfloor}, which may be zero.





Proof.



The linear combination of two real-rooted polynomials f⁡(t)f(t) and g⁡(t)g(t), each of degree nn, is given by a​f​(t)+b​g​(t)af(t)+bg(t). The degree of this combination is either nn or n−1n-1, provided that a2+b2≠0a^{2}+b^{2}\neq 0.
Then, for any paving matroid MM, the degree of ℬ⁡(QM​(t))\B(Q_{M}(t)) is at least ⌊(r​k​(M)−1)/2−1⌋{\lfloor(rk(M)-1)/2-1\rfloor} by the proof of Theorem 1.3. Thus, the coefficient of t⌊(r​k​(M)−1)/2⌋−1t^{\lfloor(rk(M)-1)/2\rfloor-1} is positive. On the other hand, the constant term of QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) is just the absolute value of the constant term of the characteristic polynomial of MM, which is positive, see [16, Proposition 3.1] and [21, Theorem 7.1.8].
It is well known that if the coefficients of a univariate polynomial are non-negative, log-concave, and have no internal zeros, then they are also unimodal.
Thus, for any paving matroid MM, each coefficient of the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomial QM​(t)Q_{M}(t) corresponding to tit^{i} with i<⌊rk⁡(M)−12⌋i<\left\lfloor\frac{\mathrm{rk}(M)-1}{2}\right\rfloor is positive.
∎





Based on Conjecture 4.2, we believe that an analog of Proposition 4.3 holds for the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials PM​(t)P_{M}(t) as well. That is, for any paving matroid MM, each coefficient of PM​(t)P_{M}(t) is positive, except possibly the coefficient of t⌊rk⁡(M)−12⌋t^{\lfloor\frac{\mathrm{rk}(M)-1}{2}\rfloor}.
