Abstract.
    

We provide a deletion formula for the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial and the inverse ZZ-polynomial of a matroid. Our formulas provide analogues to the deletion formulas of Braden–Vysogorets for Kazhdan–Lusztig and ZZ-polynomials. We discuss several consequences, which include closed formulas and recursions for these invariants on uniform matroids, projective geometries, glued cycles, and arbitrary matroids of corank 22. As a relevant application of our deletion formula, we show the existence of a matroid of rank 1919 which disproves a conjecture of Xie and Zhang concerning a real-rootedness property of inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials.

1. Introduction




The Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial P𝖬​(x)P_{\mathsf{M}}(x) is a fundamental invariant associated with any matroid 𝖬\mathsf{M}. It was introduced by Elias, Proudfoot, and Wakefield in [4], and exhibits formal similarities to the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials defined for Bruhat intervals of Coxeter groups. The coefficients of P𝖬​(x)P_{\mathsf{M}}(x) depend only on the lattice of flats ℒ⁡(𝖬)\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{M}) of the matroid and, in fact, they can be expressed as signed integer combinations of the flag Whitney numbers counting chains of flats with specified ranks (see [18]).




The Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of 𝖬\mathsf{M} plays a significant role in the singular Hodge theory of matroids developed by Braden, Huh, Matherne, Proudfoot, and Wang in [2]. Two further polynomial invariants that are often studied in this context are the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of a matroid, introduced by Gao and Xie [12] and denoted Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x), and the ZZ-polynomial introduced by Proudfoot, Xu, and Young [18] and denoted Z𝖬​(x)Z_{\mathsf{M}}(x). One of the main contributions in [2] consists of interpreting these three polynomials in the following way:

• 



P𝖬​(x)P_{\mathsf{M}}(x) is the Hilbert series of the stalk of the intersection cohomology module of 𝖬\mathsf{M} at the empty set [2, Theorem 1.9].

• 



Z𝖬​(x)Z_{\mathsf{M}}(x) is the Hilbert series of the intersection cohomology module of 𝖬\mathsf{M} [2, Theorem 1.9].

• 



The coefficient of xkx^{k} in Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x) is the multiplicity of the trivial module in the degree rk⁡(𝖬)−2​k\rk(\mathsf{M})-2k piece of the Rouquier complex of 𝖬\mathsf{M} [2, Proposition 8.21].






The above interpretations imply that these three polynomials have non-negative coefficients for every matroid 𝖬\mathsf{M}. However, computing the above polynomials is often a daunting task even for small matroids.




Very recently, Gao, Ruan, and Xie [11] studied a fourth polynomial, called the inverse ZZ-polynomial, denoted by Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x). While the nonnegativity of the coefficients of Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x) follows from the nonnegativity for Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x), at the moment we lack an interpretation for the coefficients of Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x) as graded dimensions (see [11, Problem 1]).

1.1. Main results




Motivated by Theorem 1.1, we were led to consider a deletion formula for both the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x) and the inverse ZZ-polynomials Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x). Even though each of these polynomials, up to a sign, arise by inverting, respectively, P𝖬​(x)P_{\mathsf{M}}(x) and Z𝖬​(x)Z_{\mathsf{M}}(x) in the incidence algebra of the lattice of flats ℒ⁡(𝖬)\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{M}) (see Section 2.2 for more details), it is not at all clear how to deduce the desired deletion formulas from Theorem 1.1.




In order to state the deletion formulas for Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x) and Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x) we need to introduce a counterpart for the sets Si{S}_{i} appearing in equation (1). We define

Theorem 1.4



Let 𝖬\mathsf{M} be a loopless matroid on EE, and let i∈Ei\in E be an element that is not a coloop. Then, the following identities hold:

=Y𝖬∖i​(x)+(1+x)​Y𝖬/i​(x)−∑F∈𝒯i\uptau​(𝖬|F/i)​xrk⁡(F)/2​Y𝖬/F​(x).\displaystyle=Y_{\mathsf{M}\smallsetminus i}(x)+(1+x)Y_{\mathsf{M}/i}(x)-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{T}_{i}}\uptau(\mathsf{M}|_{F}/i)\,x^{\rk(F)/2}\,Y_{\mathsf{M}/F}(x).







Our formulas lead to new recursive computations of Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x) and Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x) for any matroid 𝖬\mathsf{M}, starting from the base case of Boolean matroids 𝖴n,n\mathsf{U}_{n,n}. For these matroids, we have Q𝖴n,n​(x)=1Q_{\mathsf{U}_{n,n}}(x)=1 and Y𝖴n,n​(x)=(x+1)nY_{\mathsf{U}_{n,n}}(x)=(x+1)^{n}.
Note that the deletion formulas for Q𝖬​(x)Q_{\mathsf{M}}(x) and Y𝖬​(x)Y_{\mathsf{M}}(x) are exactly the same, a phenomenon that does not occur in Theorem 1.1.




While there is a geometric intuition behind Theorem 1.1 (see the discussion in [3, Section 1.1]), we lack that intuition in the present framework. In a sense, the proof of Theorem 1.4 is purely algebraic, though combinatorics joins the game when we apply properties of incidence algebras of posets in our computations.




As a notable application of Theorem 1.4, we are able to show (see Theorem 5.3 below) the existence of a matroid of rank 1919 which disproves Conjecture 1.3.




Theorem 1.5



There exists a matroid 𝖬\mathsf{M} of rank 1919 on 2121 elements whose normalized inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial ℬ​(Q𝖬)​(x)\mathcal{B}(Q_{\mathsf{M}})(x) is not real-rooted.





Let us explain how we were led to the construction of the counterexample. Three crucial ingredients were needed:

• 



The valuativity of the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial.

• 



A closed formula for the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of the graphic matroid associated to the graph consisting of two cycles glued along an edge.

• 



A formula for the inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial of an arbitrary matroid of corank 22.






The first item in the above list is a result proved by Ardila and Sanchez [1, Theorem 8.8]. The second item is a consequence of Theorem 1.4 that we derive in the present paper (see Proposition 4.2). The third, stated as Theorem 5.2, relies on the machinery of Ferroni and Schröter [8] on valuative invariants of elementary split matroids.




Besides these two main results, we also provide in Section 4 and Section 5 several computational examples. They include formulas for our polynomials in the cases of uniform matroids, glued cycles, projective geometries with an element deleted, and corank 22 matroids.






2. Preliminaries




We will assume familiarity with the essentials of matroid theory. For undefined terminology on matroids and geometric lattices, we refer to [23, 17]. Unless indicated otherwise, in this paper we will typically assume that our matroid 𝖬\mathsf{M} is loopless, its ground set is EE, and the rank of a subset A⊆EA\subseteq E is denoted by rk𝖬⁡(A)\rk_{\mathsf{M}}(A), or simply rk⁡(A)\rk(A) if 𝖬\mathsf{M} is understood by context.




We will make consistent use of the following notions. Throughout this paper, we denote by ℒ⁡(𝖬)\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{M}) the lattice of flats of any matroid. The characteristic polynomial of a loopless matroid 𝖬\mathsf{M} is the polynomial χ𝖬​(x)∈ℤ​[x]\chi_{\mathsf{M}}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x] given by




χ𝖬​(x):=∑F∈ℒ⁡(𝖬)μ⁡(∅,F)​xrk⁡(𝖬)−rk⁡(F),\chi_{\mathsf{M}}(x):=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{M})}\mu(\varnothing,F)\,x^{\rk(\mathsf{M})-\rk(F)},



where the number μ⁡(∅,F)\mu(\varnothing,F) is the value assigned by the Möbius function of ℒ⁡(𝖬)\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{M}) to the closed interval [0,F]⊆ℒ⁡(𝖬)[0,F]\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathsf{M}) (see [21, Chapter 3]). A relevant specialization of the characteristic polynomial is the so-called Möbius invariant of 𝖬\mathsf{M}, which is in turn defined by




μ⁡(𝖬):=μ⁡(∅,E)=χ𝖬​(0).\mu(\mathsf{M}):=\mu(\varnothing,E)=\chi_{\mathsf{M}}(0).

3. Proof of the deletion formulas




3.1. The Kazhdan–Lusztig basis

Since 𝖢a,b/i\mathsf{C}_{a,b}/i is isomorphic to the disconnected matroid 𝖴a−2,a−1⊕𝖴b−2,b−1\mathsf{U}_{a-2,a-1}\oplus\mathsf{U}_{b-2,b-1}, we have \uptau​(𝖢a,b/i)=0\uptau(\mathsf{C}_{a,b}/i)=0 by [3, Lemma 2.7].

5. A counterexample to Conjecture 1.3




Let 𝖬\mathsf{M} be a matroid of rank 22 without loops. The lattice of flats of 𝖬\mathsf{M} has the empty set as its bottom element and EE as its top element. This guarantees that the rank 11 flats of 𝖬\mathsf{M} form a partition of EE. Conversely, any partition of EE yields a loopless rank 22 matroid on EE.




By taking duals, the above construction yields a procedure that builds a corank 22 matroid on EE without coloops from every set partition of EE.

The loopless rank 22 matroid that has these three sets as its rank 11 flats is the graphic matroid induced by the graph depicted in Figure 2. (If there are more than three parts in the partition, these matroids fail to be graphic.) The corank 22 matroid associated to this set partition is the corresponding matroid dual.
