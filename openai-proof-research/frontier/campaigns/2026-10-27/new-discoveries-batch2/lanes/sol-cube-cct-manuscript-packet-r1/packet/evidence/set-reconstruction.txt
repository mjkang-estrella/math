                                                      Set Reconstruction on the Hypercube




arXiv:1607.05675v2 [math.CO] 27 Oct 2017
                                                                                               Luke Pebody

                                                  Abstract: Given an action of a group G on a set S, the k-deck of a subset T of S is the
                                                  multiset of all subsets of T of size at most k, each given up to translation by G.
                                                      For a given subset T , the reconstruction number of T is the minimum k such that the
                                                  k-deck uniquely identifies T up to translation by G, and the reconstruction number of the
                                                  action G : S is the maximum reconstruction number of any subset of S.
                                                      The concept of reconstruction number extends naturally to multisubsets T of S and
                                                  in [2], the author calculated the multiset-reconstruction number of all finite abelian groups.
                                                  In particular, it was shown that the multiset-reconstruction number of Zn2 was n + 1. This
                                                  provides an upper bound of n + 1 to the reconstruction number of Zn2 . The author also showed
                                                  a lower bound of b n+12 c in the same paper.
                                                      The purpose of this note is to close the gap. The reconstruction number of Zn2 is

                                                                                      bn + 1 − log2 (n + 1 − log2 (n))c.

    For subsets T1 and T2 say that the distinguishing number of T1 and T2 , denoted by dG:S (T1 , T2 ), is the
smallest number k such that T1 and T2 are k-distinguishable. If there is no such k (and so T1 and T2 are
translates), the distinguishing number is ∞.
    For a subset T of S, say that the reconstruction number rG:S (T ) is the smallest number k such that for
any subset U of S, if T and U are k-indistinguishable then U is a translate gT of T , and the reconstruction
number r(G : S) is the maximum value of rG:S (T ) for any subset T ⊆ S.
    One can extend the definition of reconstruction from sets to multisets. Let a multiset from S be a
map φ : S → N from S to the non-negative integers N, and then define the k-deck of φ to be the map
deckk φ : Sk → N defined by

                           deckk φ (s1 , s2 , . . . , sk ) = ∑ φ (gs1 )φ (gs2 ) . . . φ (gsk ).
                                                          g∈G

    Given this definition of a k-deck, we can define k-distinguishable and k-indistinguishable as before,
and for a multiset φ from G, say that the multiset reconstruction number rmG:S (φ ) is the smallest number
k such that for any multiset ψ from G, if φ and ψ are k-indistinguishable, then ψ is a translate gφ
(defined by gφ (s) = φ (g−1 s)) of φ . Finally, define the multiset reconstruction number rm(G : S) to be
the maximum value of rmG:S (φ ) for any multiset φ from G.
    One of the first general results in this area was in [1], where the authors showed that the reconstruction
number of a group action r(G : S) was bounded above by log2 (|G|).
    In this paper we will focus on the action of an abelian group acting on itself by multiplication. For
the specific case of the cyclic group acting on itself by multiplication, it was proved in [3] that the
reconstruction number of r(Zn ) was at most equal to 9 times the number of prime factors of n and was
equal to 3 for prime n. This was improved upon in [2] where the multiset reconstruction number was
calculated for every abelian group and, thereby, it was shown that r(Zn ) ≤ 6.
    We will focus on the group Zn2 which we will identify both, where necessary, with the n-dimensional
vector space over the field of 2 elements and with the power set of the n element set. In [2], it was shown
that the multiset reconstruction number of Zn2 is n + 1, from which it follows that r(Zn2 ) ≤ n + 1. In the
same paper, a lower bound was provided of n+1       2 . In this paper we will show that the reconstruction
number is equal to bn + 1 − log2 (n + 1 − log2 (n))c. This expression may seem a tad unwieldy, but comes
from the following fact.

Theorem 1. For positive integers n and k, the statements

                                     k ≤ bn + 1 − log2 (n + 1 − log2 (n))c

and
                                                     2n+1−k ≥ k
are equivalent.

Proof. Let us suppose that t is the unique non-negative integer that 2t + t ≤ n < 2t+1 + t + 1. Then
if we set k = n − t, we see that 2n+1−k = 2t+1 ≥ n − t = k, but if we set k = n + 1 − t, we see that
2n+1−k = 2t < n + 1 − t = k + 1. Since 2n+1−k is decreasing in k, it follows that 2n+1−k ≥ k if and only if
k ≤ n − t.

2    Lower Bound
To prove our lower bound, we provide a method for creating sets which are not easily distinguishable.

Theorem 2. Suppose that G contains a subgroup H, and that set A is the union of sets A1 , . . . , Ak such
that for each i, Ai is contained in a distinct coset gi H of H.
    Suppose likewise that set B is the union of sets B1 , . . . , Bk such that for each i, Bi is contained in a
distinct coset g0i H of H.
    Suppose finally that for each 1 ≤ i ≤ k, A \ Ai and B \ Bi are translates. Then A and B are not
k − 1-distinguishable.

Proof. Let T be a subset of G of size smaller than k, and for set S ⊆ G denote by f (S) the number of
elements g of G for which gT ⊆ S.
    Note that f (S) counts the number of occurrences of the orbit {gT : g ∈ G} of T in deck|T | (S). To
show that A and B are not k − 1-distinguishable, it is therefore sufficient to show that for any such T ,
f (A) = f (B).
    Let t be the number of distinct cosets of H in which the elements of T lie. Note that t ≤ |T | < k.
Then for any g, the elements of gT lie in t cosets of H. Therefore each translate gT contained in A is
contained in exactly k − t of the sets A \ A j , so (k − t) f (A) = ∑i f (A \ Ai ) = ∑i f (B \ Bi ) = (k − t) f (B)
and hence f (A) = f (B).
    Since this is true for all sets T of size less than k, it follows that A and B are not (k − 1)-distinguishable.

at least k.
