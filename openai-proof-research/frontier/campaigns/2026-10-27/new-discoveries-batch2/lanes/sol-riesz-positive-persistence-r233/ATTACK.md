# Literal scope and two sustained routes

Research date: 2026-10-30. No full proof or source-hypothesis counterexample obtained.

## Source scope comes first

Frozen actual original: Bilyk–Grabner, *Positive definite singular kernels on two-point homogeneous spaces*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.22104v1, 29 October 2024. Read definitions, Theorems 1–5 with their actual proofs, Theorem 8 and Remarks 6–7, and the subsequent critical-exponent discussion. Frozen actual question: Grabner's contribution §5 p10 of *Open problems UP24*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04845v1, 7 April 2025. Exact bytes, original/copy bindings and regenerated PDF text are in provenance.jsonl and text-bindings.json.

UP24 says a sphere S^d or projective space FP^d “equipped with a distance … equivalent to the geodesic distance”. Its Question 5.1 asks whether Haar minimizing at some positive s* implies minimizing for EVERY s*<s<d. It does NOT say that rho is fixed to geodesic distance, does NOT define “equivalent” as merely topological versus bi-Lipschitz, and does NOT explicitly impose transitive-group invariance. These omissions cannot honestly be resolved by adding words to the source. The contribution's heading mentions geodesic and chordal distances; its cited paper develops singular zonal kernels on compact two-point homogeneous spaces. Thus a noninvariant equivalent metric is NOT certified here as satisfying the intended original setting. The fixed geodesic interpretation is an admissible and substantive subcase; proving it alone would not establish every potentially broader reading. No altered metric is nominated as an outcome.

The original paper distinguishes its frameworks. Its introduction defines two-point homogeneity through a metric-preserving group action transitive on equal-distance pairs. The Jacobi machinery is for K(x,y)=F(cos(2κϑ(x,y))), with ϑ the standard geodesic distance. Theorem 8, however, explicitly says arbitrary compact metric space (X,d) of Hausdorff dimension D, and Remark 7 conjectures its extension to positive starting exponent on arbitrary compact metric spaces. This latter statement is about conditional positive definiteness, NOT automatically about the fixed standard Haar measure of a manifold with an arbitrary new metric. Theorem 4's Haar equivalence cannot be transported to a nonzonal metric without a new proof.

Use D for REAL dimension, avoiding the source's overloaded d. On S^D, α=β=D/2−1 and κ=1/2. On FP^q, m=dim_R F, D=mq, α=D/2−1, β=m/2−1, κ=1, with OP^2 the exceptional case D=16, α=7, β=3. Original 2410 notation is FP^(d−1), so q=d−1. UP24's displayed range s<d beside FP^d is ambiguous if d is projective rather than real dimension. A safe literal counterexample would have to satisfy its displayed upper bound too; no dimension-notation correction is an outcome. The natural singular-integrability bound is s<D. The normalization K_s(r)=r^(−s)/s for s≠0 and −log r at zero appears in UP24's general definition and in 2409.16508; 2410 uses sgn(s)r^(−s). They differ by a POSITIVE factor |s| for nonzero s and have exactly the same minimizers. Positive s is essential in the present question.

## Route A: exact fixed-geodesic Jacobi signs

For projective spaces, the unnormalized coefficient whose sign matters is

    J_n(s)=∫_0^(π/2) θ^(−s) P_n^(α,β)(cos 2θ)
                     sin(θ)^(2α+1) cos(θ)^(2β+1) dθ.

The omitted constants are strictly positive. For every source space α,β>−1; near zero the integrand is O(θ^(D−1−s)), so it is integrable exactly in the expected s<D range. Continuity away from zero holds. The original Theorems 3–4 supply the equivalence Haar minimizes iff J_n(s)≥0 for ALL n≥1. The proof's necessity is especially concrete: a negative coefficient gives the probability density 1+ε P_n(cos 2ϑ(x,z)) for small ε, whose energy difference is a positive constant times ε² J_n(s). Sufficiency is the paper's Poisson regularization argument, not an assertion that an unregularized pointwise Jacobi series converges at the singularity. This route applies to zonal kernels with the stated L1 and off-diagonal continuity hypotheses. For a nonzonal metric, these scalar coefficients do not decide the problem.

Degree one is universally positive: P_1 is increasing and has zero weighted mean, while θ(t)^(−s) is strictly increasing in t for s>0. The covariance identity as one half the double integral of (F(t)−F(u))(P_1(t)−P_1(u)) proves positivity; integrability follows as above and P_1 is bounded. This removes n=1 as a witness, but says nothing about higher modes with multiple sign changes. Differentiation in s gives an integral with factor −log θ. It has no automatic sign because the Jacobi weight changes sign. Multiplying by an increasing function does not preserve the sign of an arbitrary signed integral with several alternations.

Fetched and froze the nearest primary predecessor Bilyk–Matzke–Nathe https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16508v1 (24 September 2024). Read Theorem 1.3 and the actual §3 Rodrigues/integration-by-parts argument, including Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.2. Its derivative expansion has nonnegative coefficients multiplying t^c(1−t²)^(−b)(arccos t)^(−u). Pairing t and −t handles odd c when u≥2(α−β); the expansion guarantees u≥s+1. This proves the known sufficient range s≥2(α−β)−1, including RP^q: q−2, CP^q:2q−3, HP^q:4q−5, OP²:7, with s<D. These bounds agree after translating q=d−1 in 2410. They do NOT establish that the unknown positivity set below those sufficient bounds is an upper interval. In particular, knowing a smaller s* has all positive coefficients does not yield the derivative-term inequalities of Proposition 3.2 at arbitrary intermediate s. Naming Rodrigues or Schoenberg is not a complete subsumption bridge.

The fixed sphere and chordal cases are already settled in the actual original: Corollary 2 for sphere geodesic and Corollary 1 for chordal distances, through negative starting exponent. Neither is a new result. The unresolved core is projective geodesic metrics below the sufficient ranges, or a rigorously justified broader metric class.

Runnable checks.py computes 112 (α,β,s) cases, modes 1..64, in 12 geometric parameter pairs including real, complex, quaternionic and octonionic cases. Gauss–Jacobi weights absorb the diagonal singularity using exponent α−s/2. Two independent orders 512 and 1024 are compared. Final values are normalized by weight mass and P_n(1); maximum absolute intergrid disagreement is about 9.19e−8. No sampled all-positive→negative reversal was found. No interval error certification, tail estimate, uniform-in-s argument, or universal inference is claimed. The first unnormalized run had large absolute disagreement (~2904); that diagnostic is preserved in failures.json. Normalization improves interpretability, not mathematical certification.

An endpoint heuristic explains why finite positivity is weak. In t coordinates the diagonal singularity is (1−t)^(−s/2), while the cut-locus expansion has a square-root term at t=−1. Standard endpoint scaling suggests competing coefficient orders n^(s−α−2) and alternating n^(−β−3), before positive normalization factors. A proposed threshold based only on these orders would still need rigorous constants, the equality case, uniform remainders and all finitely many residual modes. No asymptotic theorem is asserted here; no exact critical exponent was derived.

## Route B: powers, singular Schur, and the missing metric bridge

At positive s*, K=rho^(−s*) has positive constant coefficient and nonnegative other coefficients if Haar minimizes in the zonal setting, so it is positive definite. Theorem 5 gives K^k positive definite for integer k when ks*<D, because each intermediate product is integrable. This proves only a discrete set of exponents. It gives no arbitrary real power p=s/s*>1.

The failure of the generic matrix implication is structural. Let x=(0,1,2,3) and A_ij(t)=1+t²x_i x_j. This is PSD of rank at most two. For 1<p<2, expand det(A(t) entrywise^p) at t=0. The leading term is

    t^12 [∏_(k=0)^3 binom(p,k)] [∏_(i<j)(x_j−x_i)^2].

This follows by expanding each analytic entry as Σ_k binom(p,k)t^(2k)x_i^k x_j^k and the determinant multilinearly: the least four distinct indices are 0,1,2,3, of sum 6; repeated indices vanish. For 1<p<2, binom(p,3)<0 and the other three factors are positive, so the determinant is negative for sufficiently small positive t. This is a complete obstruction to the proposed generic Schur extension. It is NOT a source counterexample: A has finite nonzero diagonal, A^(−1/s*) has nonzero diagonal, and no sphere/projective metric, homogeneous action or prescribed near-diagonal singularity has been constructed from it.

The negative/zero bridge is different. Theorem 8 starts from conditional definiteness of −rho^a with a>0 and obtains Gaussian kernels and a Mellin mixture. Remark 6 starts from −log rho+C and expands its exponential, using singular Schur for every integer term and integrability of log powers. Positive definiteness of rho^(−s*) does not give conditional definiteness of log(rho^(−s*)) or infinite divisibility. Taking a logarithm is precisely an unproved extra step. Thus neither bridge settles positive persistence.

Changing a metric also has stricter obligations than changing a kernel: symmetry, diagonal zero, triangle inequality, standard small-scale comparison, and whatever transitive/two-point homogeneity the intended source retains must all be proved. A perturbation with nonconstant row potentials might make Haar stationary at one exponent and not another, but would evade the zonal framework and has not been source-cleared. No such construction is offered as a solution.
