                                                                                               Abstract

                                                         For an n-vertex graph G, and a rooted graph H (v) with v as the root, the rooted
                                                     product graph G ◦ H (v) is obtained from G and n copies of H by identifying the
                                                     root of the ith copy of H with the ith vertex of G for each i. As a refinement of the
                                                     controllability criterion of G◦H (v) obtained recently by Shan and Liu (2025), we obtain
                                                     an explicit formula for the determinant of the walk matrix of G ◦ H (v) . Furthermore,
                                                     for an important family of graphs F that are determined by their generalized spectrum
                                                     (DGS), we introduce the concept of F-preservers and provide a sufficient condition for
                                                     a rooted graph to be an F-preserver. A list of F -preservers of small order is provided,
                                                     which leads to many new infinite families of DGS-graphs using rooted products.

                                          Keywords: walk matrix; rooted product graph; generalized spectrum

                                          Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C50


                                          1          Introduction
                                          Let G be a simple graph with n vertices, and let M (G) be a real symmetric matrix associ-
                                          ated with G. Throughout this paper, M (G) may refer to adjacency matrix A(G), signless
                                          Laplacian matrix Q(G), and the Aα matrix Aα (G). The characteristic polynomial of M (G),
                                          defined as det(xI − M (G)), is called the M -characteristic polynomial of G and is denoted
                                          by ϕ(M (G); x). The M -walk matrix of G, denoted by WM (G), or W (M (G)), is defined to
                                          be the matrix
                                                                         [e, M (G)e, . . . , (M (G))n−1 e],
                                          where e is the all-ones vector. This particular kind of matrix has received increasing attention
                                          in spectral graph theory, as it has deep connection with many important properties of a graph.
                                          For example, a theorem of Wang [15] states that if 2−⌊n/2⌋ det(WA (G)) is odd and square-free,
                                          then G is determined by its generalized spectrum (DGS). In particular, all graphs G with

      A graph G is M -controllable if det W (M (G)) ̸= 0. Similarly, an n × n matrix B is
controllable if det W (B) ̸= 0. Note that for two polynomials f and g, the resultant Res(f, g)
is nonzero if and only if gcd(f, g) Q= 1. Also note that for any matrix M and any polynomial
h(x), it holds that det h(M ) =        h(λ), where λ runs through all eigenvalues of M . An
immediate consequence of Theorem 3 is the following necessary and sufficient condition for
controllability of G ◦ H (v) , which was obtained by Shan and Liu [10].
Corollary 1. The graph G◦H (v) is M -controllable if and only if the following three conditions
hold simultaneously:
(i) G is M -controllable;
(ii) gcd(ϕ(M (H); x), ϕ(M (v) (H); x)) = 1; and
(iii) for any eigenvalue λ of M (G), the matrix B(λ) is controllable.

      We are most interested in Theorem 3 for the case where M (G) refers to the adjacency
matrix A(G), as in this setting it gives a way to construct larger DGS-graphs from smaller
ones, according to the aforementioned theorem of Wang [15]. It is worthwhile to mention
that this construction fails in the setting of generalized Q spectrum; see [12].
     For convenience, we introduce an important family of DGS-graphs.
Definition 1. For an integer n, we define Fn to be the collection
                                                                S of all n-vertex graphs G
                                            ⌊n/2⌋
such that det A(G) = ±1 and det WA (G) = ±2       . Define F = Fn .

      We note that Fn is empty when n is odd, since in this case det A(G) ≡ 0 (mod 2),
contradicting the requirement that det A(G) = ±1. On the other hand, for all even n ≥ 6,
the families Fn are nonempty; see [5, Fig. 1].
      We call a rooted graph H (v) an F-preserver if G ◦ H (v) ∈ F for any G ∈ F. Since any
graph in F is DGS by the theorem of Wang [15], it is straightforward to use F-preservers
                                                                                       (v )
to construct an infinite family of DGS-graphs. Indeed, let G be any graph in F and {Hi i }
be a sequence of F-preservers, not necessarily distinct. Then all graphs in the family

belong to the family F and hence are DGS. The second main result of this paper is a sufficient
condition for a rooted graph to be an F-preserver, which we state as the following theorem
using notation of Theorem 3.
Theorem 4. A rooted graph H (v) is an F-preserver if the following three conditions hold
simultaneously:
(i) Res(ϕ(A(H); x), ϕ(A(v) (H); x)) = ±1;
(ii) det[e, B(λ)e, . . . , (B(λ))m−1 e] = ±λk for some nonnegative integer k; and
(iii) either det A(H) = ±1 and det A(v) (H) = 0, or det A(H) = 0 and det A(v) (H) = ±1.
