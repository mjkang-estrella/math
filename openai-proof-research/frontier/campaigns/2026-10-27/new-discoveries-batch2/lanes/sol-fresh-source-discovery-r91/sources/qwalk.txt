                                                                                               Abstract

                                                        Let G be an n-vertex graph and Q(G) be its signless Laplacian matrix. The Q-walk
                                                     matrix of G, denoted by WQ (G), is [e, Q(G)e, . . . , Qn−1 (G)e], where e is the all-one
                                                     vector. Let G ◦ Pm be the graph obtained from G and n copies of the path Pm by
                                                     identifying the i-th vertex of G with an endvertex of the i-th copy of Pm for each i. We
                                                     prove that,
                                                                        det WQ (G ◦ Pm ) = ±(det Q(G))m−1 (det WQ (G))m
                                                     holds for any m ≥ 2. This gives a signless Laplacian counterpart of the following
                                                     recently established identity [16]:
                                                                                                            m
                                                                          det WA (G ◦ Pm ) = ±(det A(G))⌊ 2 ⌋ (det WA (G))m ,

                                                     where A(G) is the adjacency matrix of G and WA (G) = [e, A(G)e, . . . , An−1 (G)e]. We
                                                     also propose a conjecture to unify the above two equalities.
                                                     Keywords: walk matrix; rooted product graph; Q-walk matrix; Chebyshev polynomi-
                                                     als
                                                     AMS Classification: 05C50



                                          1          Introduction
                                          Let G be a simple graph with vertex set {1, . . . , n}. The adjacency matrix of G is the n × n
                                          symmetric matrix A = (ai,j ), where ai,j = 1 if i and j are adjacent; ai,j = 0 otherwise. For
                                          a graph G, the adjacency walk matrix (or A-walk matrix) of G is

                                                                                 WA = WA (G) := [e, Ae, . . . , An−1 e],

                                          where e is the all-one vector.
                                               Let H be a rooted graph. The rooted product graph [3, 13] of G and H, denoted by
                                          G◦H, is a graph obtained from G and n copies of H by identifying the root vertex of the i-th
                                          copy of H with vertex i of G for i = 1, 2, . . . , n. We are interested in the case that the rooted

graph H is the rooted path Pm of order m (taking an endvertex as the root vertex). For
this special case, the authors established the following formula concerning the determinant
of W (G ◦ Pm ), which was conjectured in [9].
Theorem 1 ([16]). For any graph G and integer m ≥ 2,
                                                          m
                     det WA (G ◦ Pm ) = ±(det A(G))⌊ 2 ⌋ (det WA (G))m .                        (1)

      The main aim of this paper is to give a signless Laplacian counterpart of the above
formula. We recall that the signless Laplacian matrix [2] of G, usually denoted by Q(G)
or simply Q, is A(G) + D(G), where A(G) is the adjacency matrix and D(G) is the degree
diagonal matrix. The signless Laplacian walk matrix (or Q-walk matrix [10]), denoted by
WQ (G), is naturally defined by
                                 WQ (G) = [e, Qe, . . . , Qn−1 e].
The main result of this paper is the following.
Theorem 2. For any graph G and integer m ≥ 2,
                     det WQ (G ◦ Pm ) = ±(det Q(G))m−1 (det WQ (G))m .                          (2)
Remark 1. During the writing of this paper, the authors learned that for the special case
that m ∈ {2, 3}, Theorem 2 was independently proved by Tian et al. [14] using a different
method.

      Note that the power of the first factor in the RHS of Eq. (1) is ⌊ m2 ⌋, whereas the
corresponding number in Eq. (2) is m − 1. It would be desirable to give a general formula
to unify Eqs. (1) and (2). The following definition is essentially the Aα -matrix introduced
by Nikiforov [8].
Definition 1. Aτ (G) = A(G) + τ D(G) for τ ∈ R.

when τ = 0; and Wτ (G) = WQ (G) when τ = 1. We use In to denote the identity matrix of

paper is devoted to proving Theorem 2. The main strategy is similar to our previous paper
[16], which reduces the desired identity on the walk determinants (Eq. (1)) to some identities
on resultants related to the second kind of Chebyshev polynomials. We find that, except for
the computation of resultants of Chebyshev polynomials, most of the previous argument on
the A-walk matrix in [16] can be easily extended to the Q-walk matrix or the more general
Aτ -walk matrix.
