Convexity of the median in the gamma distribution


Christian Berg

  
Henrik L. Pedersen

Note: Research supported by the
Carlsberg Foundation

August 24, 2026

Abstract
    

We show that the median m(x) in the gamma distribution
with parameter x is a strictly convex function on the positive half-line.
  



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification:
primary 60E05; secondary 41A60, 33B15.



Keywords: median, gamma function, gamma distribution.




1 Introduction



The median of the gamma distribution with (positive) parameter x is
defined implicitly by the formula




\int_{0}^{m(x)}e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt.

(1)


In a recent paper (see [5]) we showed the 0<m^{\prime}(x)<1 for all
x>0. Consequently, m(x)-x is a decreasing function, which for
x=1,2,\ldots yields a positive answer to the Chen-Rubin
conjecture. Other authors have solved this conjecture in its discrete
setting (see [2], [1], [3]).



In [4] convexity of the sequence m(n+1) has been established, and
the natural question arises if m(x) is a convex function. The main
result of this paper is the following.


Theorem 1.1


The median m(x) defined in (1) satisfies m^{\prime\prime}(x)>0. In
particular it is a strictly convex function for
x>0.






2 Proofs



The proof is based on some results in [5], which we briefly
describe.
Convexity of m is studied through the function




\varphi(x)\equiv\log\frac{x}{m(x)},\quad{x>0}.

(2)


This function played a key role in [5], and we recall its crucial
properties in the proposition below.


Proposition 2.1


The function x\to x\varphi(x) is strictly decreasing for x>0 and




\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0_{+}}x\varphi(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\log 2,



\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\varphi(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}.





Remark 2.2


Proposition 2.1 is established by showing
(x\varphi(x))^{\prime}<0. It follows that the function
\varphi(x) is itself strictly decreasing and
\varphi(x)<-x\varphi^{\prime}(x).




The starting point for proving Theorem 1.1 is the relation
[5, (10)]




(x\varphi(x))^{\prime}=-e^{g(x)}(A(x)+B(x)),

(3)


where




\displaystyle g(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle x(\varphi(x)-1+e^{-\varphi(x)}),



\displaystyle A(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x\varphi(x)}e^{-s}e^{x(1-e^{-s/x})}\left(1-\left(1+\frac{s}{x}\right)e^{-s/x}\right)\,ds,



\displaystyle B(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}te^{-xt}\xi^{\prime}(t+1)\,dt,



and where \xi is a certain positive, increasing and concave function
on [1,\infty) satisfying \xi^{\prime}(t+1)<8/135 for t>0. To establish
these properties of \xi is quite involved, and we refer to
[5, Section 5] for details.



Before proving the theorem we state the following lemmas, whose proofs
are given later.


Lemma 2.3


For the function g we have




\displaystyle g(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle x\varphi(x),



\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)\quad\text{and}



\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)



for all x>0.



Lemma 2.4


We have for x>0




\displaystyle A(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle\frac{x\varphi(x)^{3}}{6},



\displaystyle-A^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\varphi(x)^{3}-\frac{1}{2}x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)^{2}.





Lemma 2.5


We have for x>0




\displaystyle B(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle\frac{4}{135x^{2}},



\displaystyle-B^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle\frac{8}{135x^{3}}.






Proof of Theorem 1.1. From equation (2) we get




m^{\prime\prime}(x)=-e^{-\varphi(x)}\left(2\varphi^{\prime}(x)+x\varphi^{\prime\prime}(x)-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\right),



so that m^{\prime\prime}(x)>0 is equivalent to the inequality




(x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}<x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}.



Differentiation of (3) yields




(x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}=e^{g(x)}(-g^{\prime}(x))(A(x)+B(x))+e^{g(x)}(-A^{\prime}(x)-B^{\prime}(x)).



By using Lemma 2.4 and 2.5 it follows that




\displaystyle-A^{\prime}(x)-B^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)^{2}+\frac{8}{135x^{3}}-\frac{1}{6}\varphi(x)^{3}




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)^{2}+\frac{\varphi(x)^{2}}{x}\left(\frac{8}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}-\frac{1}{6}x\varphi(x)\right).



Here the expression
in the brackets is positive,
since (x\varphi(x))^{3}<(\log 2)^{3}<48/135. Therefore,
and because \varphi(x)<-x\varphi^{\prime}(x),




\displaystyle-A^{\prime}(x)-B^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\,x\varphi(x)+x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\left(\frac{8}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}-\frac{1}{6}x\varphi(x)\right)




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\left(\frac{8}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}x\varphi(x)\right).



We also have from Lemma 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5,




\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)(A(x)+B(x))
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)\left(\frac{x\varphi(x)^{3}}{6}+\frac{4}{135x^{2}}\right)




\displaystyle<
\displaystyle x^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}\varphi(x)^{2}\left(\frac{x\varphi(x)}{6}+\frac{4}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}\right).



Combination of these inequalities yields




\displaystyle(x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}e^{x\varphi(x)}\left(x\varphi(x)^{2}\left(\frac{x\varphi(x)}{6}+\frac{4}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}\right)\right.





\displaystyle\left.+\frac{8}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}x\varphi(x)\right).





Supposing that x\geq 1, it follows that




\displaystyle(x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}
\displaystyle<
\displaystyle x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}e^{x\varphi(x)}\left((x\varphi(x))^{2}\left(\frac{x\varphi(x)}{6}+\frac{4}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}\right)\right.





\displaystyle\left.+\frac{8}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}x\varphi(x)\right)




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}e^{x\varphi(x)}\left(\frac{(x\varphi(x))^{3}}{6}+\frac{4}{135}+\frac{8}{135(x\varphi(x))^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}x\varphi(x)\right)




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}h_{1}(x\varphi(x)),



where h_{1} is given by




h_{1}(t)=e^{t}\left(\frac{t^{3}}{6}+\frac{4}{135}+\frac{8}{135t^{2}}+\frac{t}{3}\right).



One can show that h_{1} attains its maximum on the interval [1/3,\log 2] at the left end point and that
h_{1}(1/3)=(551/810)\sqrt[3]{e}\approx 0.9494. Therefore it follows that (x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}<x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2} for
x\geq 1.



For 0<x<1 the estimate -g^{\prime}(x)<-x\varphi^{\prime}(x) from Lemma 2.3
is used and in this way
we get




(x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}<x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2}h_{2}(x\varphi(x)),



where




h_{2}(t)=e^{t}\left(\frac{t^{2}}{6}+\frac{4}{135t}+\frac{8}{135t^{2}}+\frac{t}{3}\right).



Since x<1 and x\varphi(x) decreases we have x\varphi(x)>\varphi(1)=-\log\log 2. One can show that h_{2} attains its maximum on
the interval [-\log\log 2,\log 2] for t=-\log\log 2 and
that h_{2}(-\log\log 2)\approx 0.9616. Therefore (x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}<x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2} for x<1.  \square


Remark 2.6


The function h_{2} becomes larger than 1 on the interval [1/3,\log 2], so h_{2} cannot be used to obtain the
inequality (x\varphi(x))^{\prime\prime}<x\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{2} for all x>0.




Proof of Lemma 2.3. It is clear that
g(x)<x\varphi(x). Differentiation yields




\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-\varphi(x)+(1-x\varphi^{\prime}(x))(1-e^{-\varphi(x)})




\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-\varphi(x)+(1-x\varphi^{\prime}(x))\varphi(x)\,=\,-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x),



where we have used 1-e^{-a}<a for a>0.



To find an estimate that is more accurate for x near 0 we use




\displaystyle-g^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)(1-e^{-\varphi(x)})-\varphi(x)+1-e^{-\varphi(x)}




\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-x\varphi^{\prime}(x)-\varphi(x)+1-e^{-\varphi(x)}\,<\,-x\varphi^{\prime}(x).



\square



Proof of Lemma 2.4.
Using that 1-e^{-a}<a and 1-(1+a)e^{-a}<a^{2}/2 for
a>0, we can estimate A(x) by




A(x)<\int_{0}^{x\varphi(x)}e^{-s}e^{x(s/x)}\left(\frac{s^{2}}{2x^{2}}\right)\,ds=\frac{x\varphi(x)^{3}}{6}.



A computation shows that




\displaystyle-A^{\prime}(x)
\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-(\varphi(x)+x\varphi^{\prime}(x))e^{-x\varphi(x)}e^{x(1-e^{-\varphi(x)})}(1-(1+\varphi(x))e^{-\varphi(x)})





\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{x\varphi(x)}e^{-s}e^{x(1-e^{-s/x})}\left(1-\left(1+\frac{s}{x}\right)e^{-s/x}\right)^{2}\,ds





\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{x\varphi(x)}e^{-s}e^{x(1-e^{-s/x})}\frac{s^{2}}{x^{3}}e^{-s/x}\,ds




\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-(\varphi(x)+x\varphi^{\prime}(x))\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x)^{2}+\int_{0}^{x\varphi(x)}s^{2}e^{-s/x}\,ds\frac{1}{x^{3}}




\displaystyle<
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\varphi(x)^{3}-\frac{1}{2}x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\varphi(x)^{3}




\displaystyle=
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}\varphi(x)^{3}-\frac{1}{2}x\varphi^{\prime}(x)\varphi(x)^{2}.



\square



Proof of Lemma 2.5. These estimates follow directly
from the inequality \xi^{\prime}(t+1)<8/135. \square
