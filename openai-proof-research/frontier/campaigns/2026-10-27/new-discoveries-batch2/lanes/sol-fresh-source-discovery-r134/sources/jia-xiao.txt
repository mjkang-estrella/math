                                                   DETERMINED ALGEBRAS ARE GENERATED BY
                                                                 IDEMPOTENTS


                                                                      HONGYU JIA AND ZHANKUI XIAO


                                                  Abstract. Brešar showed that a finite dimensional unital associative algebra
                                                  is zero product determined if and only if it is generated by idempotents. For
                                                  the analogue of zero Jordan product determined algebras, only one direction
                                                  was known: over a field of characteristic not 2, every algebra generated by
                                                  idempotents is zero Jordan product determined. Whether the converse holds

                                                  has remained an open problem.
                                                      In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively in the finite dimensional
                                                  case. Some related open problems are stated at the end.




                                                                               1. Introduction
                                             Let F be an arbitrary field. An F -algebra is a pair (A, ·), where A is an F -linear
                                          space and · : A × A → A is an F -bilinear map. We sometimes write an F -algebra A
                                          for short if there is no confusion on the binary operator “·”. An F -algebra A is called
                                          zero product determined (zpd for short) if for every bilinear map f : A × A → F
                                          with the property that
                                          (1.1)                       f (a, b) = 0    whenever          a · b = 0,
                                          then there exists a linear map Φ : A → F such that
                                          (1.2)                     f (x, y) = Φ(x · y)       for all    x, y ∈ A.
                                             Let A be an associative algebra over F . If we introduce the Jordan product on
                                          A as usual by x ◦ y = xy + yx, then (A, ◦) becomes a Jordan algebra. We say that
                                          A is zero Jordan product determined (zJpd for short) if the Jordan algebra (A, ◦)
                                          is zero product determined.
                                             The concept of a zpd algebra originated from the field of linear preserver prob-
                                          lems (see [19] for instance). In [9], Brešar and Šemrl investigated commutativity
                                          preserving linear maps on central simple algebras, and in [2], Alaminos, Brešar, Ex-
                                          tremera and Villena studied zero product preserving maps and local derivations on
                                          C ∗ -algebras. Both papers share a core strategy: establishing results about certain
                                          linear maps by investigating bilinear maps that vanish on pairs of elements with
                                          zero product. Motivated by the aforementioned works, the concept of a zpd algebra
                                          was introduced in [7]. Since then, zpd algebras have been extensively studied, see

[1, 3, 8, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16, 21, 22] and the references therein. We refer the reader
to Brešar’s monograph [5] for a comprehensive understanding of the subject.
    In [6], Brešar studied the universal properties of the structure of associative zpd
algebras, and established that a finite dimensional unital associative algebra over a
field is zpd if and only if it is generated by idempotents. On the other hand, deciding
whether an algebra is generated by idempotents is itself a highly nontrivial matter,
which has received a lot of attention in the field of representation theory. By intro-
ducing zero action determined modules, Hu and the second author [17] provided a
module theoretic criterion for testing an algebra generated by idempotents.
    Motivated by the progress of associative zpd algebras, researchers began to in-
vestigate the analogous description of zJpd algebras. A step in this direction was
made in [4] (and partially in [13]), where the following result was established.
Theorem 1.1. Let A be an associative unital algebra over a field of characteristic
not 2. If A is generated by idempotents, then A is zJpd.
    However, the converse implication of Theorem 1.1 remains open up to now, as
far as we know. Discussing this problem, in [5, Page 54] Brešar remarked:
        “As a matter of fact, we are facing the same problem as with zpd
        algebras: we do not know of any unital zJpd algebra (over a field
        of characteristic not 2) that is not generated by idempotents. Gen-
        erally speaking, our understanding of zJpd algebras is, at present,
        rather limited.”
This state of affairs naturally raises a central question: does every unital associative
zJpd algebra is generated by idempotents? In particular, in this note we focus
on this question in the finite dimensional case, and we can answer this question
affirmatively as follows.
Theorem 1.2. Let F be a field of characteristic not 2 and A be a finite dimen-
sional associative unital F -algebra. Then A is zJpd if and only if it is generated by
idempotents.
   Consequently, the above theorem becomes the Jordan analogue of Brešar’s char-
acterization of finite dimensional associative zpd algebras (see [6, Theorem 3.7]).
In addition, it clarifies the role played by idempotents in zJpd algebras, which is
not easily discovered in the definition. Let us end the introduction with a termi-
nological convention: by an algebra we will mean an associative unital algebra over
some field.

                                  2. Main Result
   This section is devoted to proving the main result Theorem 1.2. Throughout,
we assume that F is a field of char(F ) ̸= 2. For an F -algebra A, we denote rad(A)
and Z(A) the Jacobson radical and center of A respectively.
   We start our proof by recalling the following equivalent description of zJpd al-
gebras, which is crucial for the application of zJpd algebras in linear preserver
problems.
Proposition 2.1. ([5, Proposition 1.3]) Let A be an F -algebra. Then A is zJpd if
and only if for every F -vector space V and every bilinear map f : A × A → V with
the property f (a, b) = 0 whenever a ◦ b = 0, there exists a linear map Φ : A → V
such that f (x, y) = Φ(x ◦ y) for all x, y ∈ A.

    Taking our target in mind, given a finite dimensional zJpd F -algebra A, we
want to show that A is generated by idempotents. Notice that a module theoretic
equivalent description of finite dimensional algebras generated by idempotents was
established in [17], where one of the main ingredients depends on the studying of
local algebras (see [17, Lemmas 2.10 and 2.13]). This naturally motivates our study
of the zJpd property of local algebras.
    Recall that a finite dimensional F -algebra A is local if the set of all non-invertible
elements forms an ideal of A, which coincides with the Jacobson radical rad(A).
Equivalently, A/ rad(A) is a division algebra. Moreover, by lifting idempotents
modulo nil ideal, we know that A is local if and only if it has no nontrivial idempo-
tents. Accordingly, we need in turn to study the zJpd property of division algebras
and then local algebras.
    We now recall some properties of finite dimensional division algebras, see [12,
Section 22].
    Let A be a finite dimensional central simple F -algebra. Recall that an extension
field K of F is said to be a splitting field for A if K ⊗F A ∼ = Mn (K) for some n ∈ N,
where Mn (K) denotes the n × n full matrix algebra over K. If we further assume
that A is a division F -algebra, then every maximal subfield K of A is a splitting
field for A. Here we would like to remark that the subfield K contains the unity of
A, i.e., K ⊇ F .

field and hence D is a finite dimensional central division Z(D)-algebra. Hence
there exists a splitting field K for D such that K ⊗Z(D) D ∼       = Mn (K), where n =

   We also can get the surjectivity of reduced trace RTr by the non-degeneracy
of B. After the above preparation, we now study the zJpd property of division
algebras, inspirited by [5, Example 3.19] of real quaternions.
Lemma 2.4. If a finite dimensional division F -algebra D is zJpd, then D = F .
Proof. We first remark that when the base field F is of characteristic not 2, a
commutative algebra is zpd if and only if it is zJpd. If D is commutative, then D
is zpd and hence it is generated by idempotents by [6, Theorem 3.7]. Therefore
D = F , since it has to be generated by the only nonzero idempotent 1.
   Now assume that D is noncommutative. We claim that
(2.1)             RTr(u) = 0,   if u is a Jordan zero divisor in D.
In fact, if u ∈ D is a Jordan zero divisor, then there exists a nonzero element v ∈ D
such that u ◦ v = 0. We can get that u = −v −1 uv and
               RTr(u) = RTr(−v −1 uv) = − RTr(v −1 uv) = − RTr(u).
As char(F ) ̸= 2, the claim follows.
  Let us define an F -bilinear map f : D × D → Z(D) by
                             f (x, y) = RTr(x) RTr(y),
for all x, y ∈ D. It follows from the claim (2.1) that f (u, v) = RTr(u) RTr(v) = 0
whenever u ◦ v = 0. Since D is zJpd, by Proposition 2.1, there exists an F -linear
map Φ : D → Z(D) such that f (x, y) = Φ(x ◦ y) for all x, y ∈ D. Consequently,

Lemma 2.5. If a finite dimensional local F -algebra A is zJpd, then A = F .
Proof. Since the base field F is of characteristic not 2, then (A, ◦) is a unital zpd
Jordan algebra, then any homomorphic image of A is also zJpd by [5, Corollary
1.15], as every homomorphism is a Jordan homomorphism. We get that A/ rad(A)
is a finite dimensional zJpd division algebra and hence A/ rad(A) ∼  = F by Lemma
2.4. Furthermore A = F ⊕ rad(A) as the direct sum of F -vector space.
   We assume in reverse that rad(A) ̸= 0. Since A is finite dimensional, then rad(A)
is a nilpotent ideal and hence the ideal rad(A)2 is a proper subset of rad(A). Let
us define an F -bilinear map f : A × A → rad(A)/ rad(A)2 by
                            f (λ + a, µ + b) = λb + rad(A)2
for all λ, µ ∈ F and a, b ∈ rad(A). Taking arbitrarily a pair of Jordan zero divisors
with u ◦ v = 0, we write u = λ + a and v = µ + b with λ, µ ∈ F and a, b ∈ rad(A). It
is clear that 2λµ + 2λb + 2µa + a ◦ b = 0 and furthermore λµ = 0, since char(F ) ̸= 2.
If λ = 0, obviously f (λ + a, µ + b) = 0. If µ = 0, then λb = − 21 (a ◦ b) ∈ rad(A)2 , and
we can also get f (λ + a, µ + b) = 0. Hence the bilinear map f satisfies f (u, v) = 0
whenever u ◦ v = 0. Since A is zJpd, it follows from Proposition 2.1 that there
exists an F -linear map Φ : A → rad(A)/ rad(A)2 such that f (x, y) = Φ(x ◦ y) for
all x, y ∈ A. Choose a ∈ rad(A) \ rad(A)2 and we have
                       f (1, a) = Φ(1 ◦ a) = Φ(a ◦ 1) = f (a, 1).
However,
                             f (1, a) = a + rad(A)2 ̸= 0,
while f (a, 1) = 0. This contradiction shows that rad(A) = 0. Hence A = F .             □
   The remaining tool we need is the theory of lifting idempotents modulo ideals,
see [18, 20]. The classical version of this theory concerns lifting idempotents mod-
ulo nilpotent ideals. However, in the case of finite dimensional algebras, lifting
idempotents can be made modulo any ideals due to the work of Nicholson [20]. We
refer the reader to [5, Lemma 2.25] for a self-contained proof of this fact.
Lemma 2.6. Let A be a finite dimensional unital algebra and I be an ideal of A.
Then every idempotent in A/I is of the form e + I with e ∈ A an idempotent.
   We are now in a position to prove the main theorem.
Proof of Theorem 1.2. We only need to prove the necessity. Let A be a finite di-
mensional zJpd F -algebra. From now on, We denote I the ideal of A generated by
all commutators of the form [e, x], where e is an idempotent and x is an arbitrary
element of A. Let E be the subalgebra of A generated by all idempotents. We want
to show A = E.
   The proof is divided into three steps.
Step 1. If we further assume that every idempotent in A is central, then A ∼           =
F ⊕ ··· ⊕ F.
   Let 1 = e1 + · · · + er be the decomposition of unity into the sum of primitive
idempotents. Since every idempotent is central, this decomposition is also corre-
sponding to the block decomposition of A. In other words, A ∼   = e1 Ae1 ⊕ · · · ⊕ er Aer
and each block ei Aei ∼= EndA (Aei ) is local. Notice that ei Aei is a zJpd F -algebra
for 1 ≤ i ≤ r by [5, Theorem 1.16]. Hence the result follows from Lemma 2.5.
Step 2. We show that A = E + rad(A).

form e + I with e ∈ A an idempotent. Since [e, x] ∈ I for all x ∈ A, it follows that

by Step 1. In particular A/I is semisimple. It follows that rad(A) ⊆ I. Notice that
I ⊆ E, see [5, Lemma 2.26] for example. We have rad(A) ⊆ E and complete the
proof of the main result.                                                        □
   Combining Theorem 1.2 and Brešar’s Theorem 3.7 in [6] (see also [5, Theorem
2.27]), we obtain that for finite dimensional F -algebras, the notion of a zpd algebra
coincides with that of a zJpd algebra, provided that the base field F is of charac-
teristic not 2, which is a quite common restriction in the study of Jordan and Lie
algebras. As far as we know, researchers have not found an example of zpd or zJpd
algebras which is not generated by idempotents. We tend to have a such example,
but we still have
Conjecture 2.7. Let A be an F -algebra (maybe infinite dimensional). Then A is
zJpd if and only if it is zpd.
   Let us end this note with an open problem of zero Lie product determined
algebras.
Remark 2.8. For an associative F -algebra A, denoting the Lie product on A as
usual by [x, y] = xy − yx, then (A, [·, ·]) becomes a Lie algebra. Recall that A is
said to be zero Lie product determined (zLpd for short) if the Lie algebra (A, [·, ·])
is zero product determined.
   Unlike the zpd and zJpd algebras, the zLpd algebras exhibit a substantially
different behavior, see [5, Section 3.2]. Even every finite dimensional complex simple
Lie algebra is zpd [21], we have not found a representation theoretic description of
this fact, see [8]. However, in our opinion, for a zLpd algebra A, there should exist
and hence we ask for a structural property about the generators of (A, [·, ·]) modulo
its Lie ideal Z(A), which analogously can be described by the generators (as an
associative algebra) of the universal envelop algebra of (A, [·, ·]).
