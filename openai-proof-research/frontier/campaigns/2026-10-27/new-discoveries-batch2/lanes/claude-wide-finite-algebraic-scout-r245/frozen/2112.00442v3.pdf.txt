                                                                                                           Abstract
                                                  A real square matrix is algebraically positive if there exists a real polynomial f such that f (A) is a
                                                  positive matrix. In this paper, we give a sufficient condition for a sign pattern matrix to allow algebraic
                                                  positivity, and give some methods to construct higher-order algebraically positive matrices from some
                                                  lower-order algebraically positive matrices. We also propose two conjectures related to the problem of
                                                  allowing algebraic positivity.

                                           Keywords: Algebraically positive, Sign pattern, Allow algebraic positivity, Irreducible matrix, AP-irreducible.
                                           AMS Subject Classifications: 15B35, 15B48, 05C50.

                                           1    Introduction

                                           A real matrix is said to be positive if its every entry is positive. Kirkland, Qiao, and Zhan introduced the
                                           concept of algebraically positive matrices in 2016. A real square matrix A is algebraically positive if there
                                           exists a real polynomial f such that f (A) is a positive matrix. They gave the following characterization of
                                           algebraically positive matrices.
                                           Theorem 1.1 ([5]). A real square matrix is algebraically positive if and only if it has a simple real eigenvalue
                                           and corresponding left and right positive eigenvectors.
                                                A sign pattern matrix is a matrix whose every entry is a symbol from the set {+, −, 0}. The qualitative
                                           class of a matrix A, denoted by Q(A), is defined by the set of all real matrices obtained from A by replacing
                                           ‘+’ by some positive number, ‘−’ by some negative number and ‘0’ by the zero number. A sign pattern
                                           matrix A allows a property P if at least one matrix in Q(A) has the property P and requires property P if
                                           all matrices in Q(A) have the property P .
                                                Kirkland, Qiao, and Zhan [5] characterized symmetric tridiagonal sign pattern matrices that allow alge-
                                           braic positivity. Later, Das and Bandopadhyay [2] generalized this result to find all tree sign pattern matrices
                                           allowing algebraic positivity, and Abagat and Pelejo [1] described all 3-by-3 sign pattern matrices that allow
                                           algebraic positivity. In this paper, we present a class of sign pattern matrices that allow algebraic positiv-
                                           ity and give some methods to construct higher-order algebraically positive matrices from some lower-order
                                           algebraically positive matrices.

We have the following necessary conditions on algebraic positivity associated with the allow problem.
Theorem 3.1 ([5]). Every algebraically positive matrix is irreducible.
Theorem 3.2 ([5]). If a sign pattern matrix A allows algebraic positivity, then either every row and column
of A contains a +, or every row and column of A contains a −.
Theorem 3.3 ([1]). If a sign pattern matrix A allows algebraic positivity, then BA is irreducible.
    We say that an irreducible sign pattern matrix A is AP-irreducible if every row and column of A contains
a + and BA is irreducible. We say that a sign pattern matrix A is minimally AP-irreducible if it does not
remain AP-irreducible after replacement of a nonzero entry with 0.
    Theorem 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3 together imply that if A is algebraically positive, then either A or −A is AP-
irreducible. Whether the converse is true? We do not know the answer yet, but we give some results which
indicate that the converse may be true.
    We will present a class of AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices through Theorem 3.14 that allow algebraic
positivity. On that purpose, we give the following results as prerequisites.
Lemma 3.4 ([5]). If A is algebraically positive, then for every permutation matrix P , P T AP is algebraically
positive.

  Lemma 3.9. A minimally irreducible matrix of order n can have at most 2n − 2 nonzero entries.
  Proof. Let A be a minimally irreducible matrix of order n. Then D(A) is minimally strongly connected. So

5    Conclusion

We have seen that if A is a minimally AP-irreducible sign pattern matrix of order n with all diagonal entries
equal to 0 such that A[α, β] contains no + for any two distinct irreducible components α and β of A+ , then
A has at most 2n − 2 nonzero entries. We have also seen in Section 2 that if A allows an algebraically positive
matrix with a simple positive eigenvalue, then we can construct a higher-order algebraically positive matrix
keeping the condition of AP-irreducibility intact. So we hope that the following results are true.
Conjecture 5.1. If A is a minimally AP-irreducible sign pattern matrix of order n with all diagonal entries
equal to 0, then A has at most 2n − 2 nonzero entries.
Conjecture 5.2. A sign pattern matrix A allows algebraic positivity if and only if either A or −A is AP-
irreducible.
    It should be noted that the above two conjectures are valid for all known algebraically positive matrices
in [1, 2, 5].
    Let AP be the set of all sign pattern matrices that allow algebraic positivity. To prove Conjecture 5.2, it
is enough to show that all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices with zero diagonal allow algebraic positivity.
Proposition 5.3. If AP contains all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices with zero diagonal, then it contains
all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices.
Proof. Let A be an AP-irreducible sign pattern matrix of order n. Let X be a subpattern of A such that X
is minimally AP-irreducible. Since X is minimally AP-irreducible, xii 6= − for all i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , n}. Suppose
that xii = + for i = 1, 2, . . . , k (without loss of generality). For every i ∈ {1, 2, . . . , k}, delete the row and the
column corresponding to the index i, and append two rows and two columns corresponding to the indices i′
and i′′ such that
    1. both (i′ , i′′ )-th entry and (i′′ , i′ )-th entry are +,
    2. for every j 6= i′′ , (i′ , j)-th entry is 0 and (j, i′ )-th entry is the (j, i)-th entry of A,
    3. for every j 6= i′ , (j, i′′ )-th entry is 0 and (i′′ , j)-th entry is the (i, j)-th entry of A.
Let Y be the new sign pattern matrix. Then Y is minimally AP-irreducible and all diagonal entries of Y
are 0, and thus Y allows algebraic positivity. Since (i′ , i′′ )-th entry is the only nonzero entry in i′ -th row, Y
allows an algebraically positive matrix with a simple positive eigenvalue and corresponding left, right positive
eigenvectors. Therefore by Lemma 3.12 and Remark 3.13, X allows an algebraically positive matrix with a
simple positive eigenvalue and corresponding left, right positive eigenvectors. Then by Theorem 1.1 and 3.6,
A allows algebraic positivity. Therefore AP contains all AP-irreducible sign pattern matrices.
