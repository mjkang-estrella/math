                                                                                           Abstract
                                                        We study subsets T consisting of some transpositions (i, j) of the symmetric
                                                    group Sn on {1, . . . , n} such that the Cayley graph ΓT := Cay(Sn , T ) is an integral
                                                    graph, i.e., all eigenvalues of an adjacency matrix of ΓT are integers. Graph prop-
                                                    erties of ΓT are determined in terms of ones of the graph GT whose vertex set is
                                                    {1, . . . , n} and {i, j} is an edge if and only if (i, j) ∈ T . Here we prove that if GT
                                                    is a tree then ΓT is integral if and only if T is isomorphic to the star graph K1,n−1 ,
                                                    answering Problem 5 of [Electron. J. Comnin., 29(2) (2022) # P2.9]. Problem 6 of
                                                    the latter article asks to find necessary and sufficient conditions on T for integral-
                                                    ness of Cay(Sn , T ) without any further assumption on T . We show that if GT is a
                                                    graph which we call it a “generalized complete multipartite graph” then Cay(Sn , T )
                                                    is integral. We conjecture that Cay(Sn , T ) is integral only if GT is a generalized
                                                    complete multipartitie graph. To support the latter conjecture we show its validity
                                                    whenever GT is some classes of graphs including cycles and cubic graphs.

                                           Keywords: Cayley graph; Symmetric group; Transposition; Spectra of graphs; Eigenvalues
                                           of graphs; Integral graphs.

                                           1        Introduction
                                           Let G be a group and S be a subset of G such that S is inverse closed (i.e. s ∈ S ⇔
                                           s−1 ∈ S) and the identity element 1 of G does not belong to S. Then the Cayley graph
                                           Cay(G, S) is the graph whose vertex set is the set of elements of G and two vertices
                                           g1 , g2 ∈ G are adjacent if and only if g1 g2−1 ∈ S. It is well known that Cay(G, S) is
                                           connected if and only if G can be generated by S.

    Let Sn be the symmetric group on [n] := {1, . . . , n}. For a set T of transpositions in Sn ,
GT is the graph with vertex set [n] such that i, j ∈ [n] are adjacent if and only if (i, j) ∈ T .
It is well known that Cay(Sn , T ) is connected if and only if GT is connected. The (adja-
cency) spectrum of a finite simple graph Γ is the multiset of eigenvalues of an adjacency
matrix of Γ. A graph is called integral if and only if the spectrum consists of only integers.

    Here we study the following problems proposed in [13].
Problem 1. [13, Problem 5] Let T be a set of transpositions in Sn such that GT is a tree,
where n ≥ 2. Give a necessary and sufficient condition for Cay(Sn , T ) to be integral.
Problem 2. [13, Problem 6] Let T be a set of transpositions in Sn such that GT is
connected, where n ≥ 2. Give a necessary and sufficient condition for Cay(Sn , T ) to be
integral.
    In Problem 1, a sufficient condition on T is known to be GT ∼   = K1,n−1 the star graph
with n vertices (see [1], [4, Theorem 5.3], [5], [11]). We complete the answer to Problem 1
by showing that the latter condition is also necessary (see part (b) of Corollary 1, below).
    About Problem 2, we prove that if GT is a “generalized complete multipartite graph”
(see Definition 2) then Cay(Sn , T ) is integral. We propose as a conjecture (see Conjecture
1, below) that the converse of the latter holds valid. To support Conjecture 1, we prove
its validity for some classes of graphs including cycles and cubic graphs (see Propositions
4, 5 and 6).


2     Preliminaries
Throughout the paper we use the following notations:
   Cn : the cycle graph with n vertices.
   Kn : the complete graph with n vertices.
   Kn1 ,n2 ,...,nk : the complete multipartite graph with k parts of sizes n1 , n2 , . . . , nk .
   For a given graph Γ:
   V (Γ): the set of vertices of Γ.
   E(Γ): the set of edges of Γ.
   nΓ: the graph which is disjoint union of n copies of the graph Γ.
   Γ1 ∪ Γ2 : the disjoint union of graphs Γ1 and Γ2 .
   Γ1 ∗ Γ2 : the join graph of disjoint graphs Γ1 and Γ2 .
   L(Γ): the Laplacian matrix of Γ which is D − A, where D is the diagonal matrix of
the vertex-degrees and A is the adjacency matrix of Γ with the same row vertex-labeling
as D.
   IΓ : the set of all isolated vertices of a graph Γ.
   Γ# : the induced subgraph of Γ on the set V (Γ) \ IΓ .
   Γ + v: the graph obtained by adding the isolated vertex v to the graph Γ.

Definition 1. Suppose G1 , . . . , Gk , k ≥ 2, are graphs with the same vertex set. A graph
G is called weakly join of G1 , . . . , Gk if there exist subgraphs Γi of Gi , 1 ≤ i ≤ k such that

 (1) for every 1 ≤ i ≤ k, E(Γi ) = E(Gi ),

 (2) for every i 6= j, V (Γi ) ∩ V (Γj ) = ∅, and

 (3) G# = Γ1 ∗ · · · ∗ Γk .

We use the notation WJ (G1 , . . . , Gk ) for the set of all weakly join defined with respect
to G1 , . . . , Gk .

Remark 2. Let T1 , . . . , Tk be some sets of transpositions of Sn . Using Theorem 2 and the
induction on the number of graphs, one can easily see that if for every i = 1, . . . , k, the
graph Cay(Sn , Ti ) is integral, then Cay(Sn , T ) is integral, where GT ∈ WJ (GT1 , . . . , GTk ).
Remark 3. Let S = {(1, 2), (1, 3), . . . , (1, n)} for some positive integer n. It is conjectured
that Cay(Sn , S) is integral in [1]. Using certain properties of the Jucys-Murphy elements,
the validity of the latter conjecture was confirmed in [5]. Since the star graph K1,n−1 ∈
WJ (nK1 , nK1 ), the latter conjecture is a direct consequence of Theorem 2.
    A vertex v of a graph Γ is called a complete vertex if it is adjacent to each vertex
u 6= v of Γ. For a graph Γ a cut vertex of Γ is a vertex v of Γ such that the number of
connected components of Γ − v is more than the number of connected components of Γ.
As a direct consequence of the following result, we give a complete solution to Problem 1.
Corollary 1. Let T be a set of transpositions in Sn . Then
 (a) If GT is a connected graph with a cut vertex v then Cay(Sn , T ) is integral if and
     only if v is a complete vertex and Cay(Sn , T 0 ) is integral, where T 0 is a set of trans-
     positions in Sn such that GT 0 = (GT − v) + v.
 (b) If GT is a tree, then Cay(Sn , T ) is integral if and only if GT is the star graph K1,n−1 .
Proof. (a) Suppose that Cay(Sn , T ) is integral. Then, by Theorem 1 and [10, Corollary
2.1], v is a complete vertex. We know that T 00 is the set of transpositions in Sn such that
GT 00 = nK1 then T 00 = ∅ and Cay(Sn , T 00 ) is integral. Thus as GT ∈ WJ (GT 0 , nK1 ), by
Theorem 2, Cay(Sn , T 0 ) is integral. This proves one direction. Conversely, suppose that
Cay(Sn , T 0 ) is integral and v is a complete vertex. Then again as GT ∈ WJ (GT 0 , nK1 ),
Theorem 2 implies that Cay(Sn , T ) is integral.
    (b) It is clear that if GT = K1 or K2 there is nothing to prove. So we may assume
that GT has at least three vertices. Suppose that Cay(Sn , T ) is integral. Then as GT is a
tree, by part (a), GT has a complete vertex and so it is K1,n−1 . The converse direction is
clear by Proposition 2.                                                                   
Definition 2. a) Let Γ1 , . . . , Γk be graphs with the same vertex set and for each i, Γi
be a disjoint union of complete multipartite graphs. Then a graph Γ is called generalized
complete multipartite graph of type 1 if Γ ∈ WJ (Γ1 , . . . , Γk ).
b) For n ≥ 2, we say that the graph Γ is a generalized complete multipartite graph of type
n, if Γ ∈ WJ (Γ1 , . . . , Γk ), where Γ1 , . . . , Γk are graphs with the same vertex set and for
every i = 1, 2, . . . k, the graph Γi is a disjoint union of generalized complete multipartite
graphs of type at most n − 1.

Proposition 3. Let T be a set of transpositions in Sn such that GT is a generalized
complete multipartite graph of type k, for some positive integer k. Then Cay(Sn , T ) is
integral.

Proof. We prove by induction on k. The case k = 1 is clear by Proposition 2 and
Theorem 2. Suppose that the result is true for every generalized complete multipartite
graph of type at most k − 1. Now the result follows from the induction hypothesis, [4,
Proposition 5.2] and Theorem 2.                                                      
   By the above result in the sequel, we suggest the following conjecture and try to
show that this conjecture is true for some classes of Cayley graphs of symmetric groups
generated by transpositions.

Conjecture 1. Let T be a set of transpositions in Sn . Then Cay(Sn , T ) is integral, if and
only if for some positive integer k, the graph GT is a generalized complete multipartite
graph of type k.

   The following results confirm Conjecture 1 for cycles.

Proposition 4. Let T be a set of transposition in Sm such that GT ∼
                                                                  = Cm . Then
Cay(Sm , T ) is integral if and only if m = 3 or 4.

Proof. It is well-known that the only integral cycles are C3 , C4 and C6 , see for example
[3, p. 43]. Since cycles are regular graphs, the above cycles are the only Laplacian integral
cycles. Thus, one direction is a direct consequence of Theorem 1 and Remark 1. The
converse direction is clear by Proposition 2.                                              

Proposition 5. Let T be a set of transpositions in Sn such that GT is a connected cubic
graph. Then Cay(Sn , T ) is integral if and only if GT is isomorphic to one of the graphs
K4 or K3,3 .

Proof. Since GT is a connected cubic integral graph, GT is one of the thirteen graphs
given in [15]. Suppose that Cay(Sn , T ) is integral. Then GT is isomorphic to K4 or K3,3 ,
by GAP [16], Proposition 1 and Theorem 1. The converse direction is clear by Proposition
2.                                                                                      
   A connected graph with n vertices and m edges is called a k-cyclic graph if k =
m − n + 1. In the following result, we give a characterization of integral Cayley graphs
Cay(Sn , T ), where GT is a k-cyclic graph and k = 1, 2, 3 which confirms Conjecture 1.
