335435 Erdős's smaller subgraphs of minimum degree conjecture
Let $k\geq 2$, let $n\geq k+1$, and define

$$
t_k(n)=(k-1)(n-k+2)+\binom{k-2}{2}.
$$

A graph has minimum degree $k$ if every vertex has at least $k$ neighbors.

**Erdős's conjecture.** For every $k\geq 2$ there exists an $\epsilon_k>0$ such that every graph on $n\geq k+1$ vertices and $t_k(n)+1$ edges contains a subgraph of minimum degree $k$ with at most $(1-\epsilon_k)n$ vertices.

Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp proved the corresponding existence result with a smaller saving of order $\sqrt n$, while this paper improves the guaranteed saving to at least $\Omega(n/\log n)$. The conjecture asks for a positive constant proportion of the vertices to be removed, and remains open in the supplied source.

335756 Kløve's generalized formula for permutation-code sphere polynomials
Let $d$ be a positive integer and $x$ a real number. Define the $d\times 2d$ matrix $A_{d,x}=(a_{i,j})$ by

$$
a_{i,j}=\begin{cases}
x,&1\leqslant j\leqslant d+1-i,\\
1,&d+2-i\leqslant j\leqslant d+i,\\
0,&d+i+1\leqslant j\leqslant 2d.
\end{cases}

R_d=\{(\rho_1,\rho_2,\ldots,\rho_d)\mid 1\leqslant\rho_i\leqslant d+i,01\leqslant i\leqslant d,\ \text{and}\ \rho_r\neq\rho_s\text{ for }r\neq s\},

\Omega_d(x)=\sum_{\rho\in R_d}a_{1,\rho_1}a_{2,\rho_2}\cdots a_{d,\rho_d}.

\Omega_d(x)=\sum_{m=0}^d{d\choose m}(m+1)^d(x-1)^{d-m}.

Kløve introduced this function to obtain bounds for the sizes of spheres in permutation codes under the Chebychev distance. The original conjecture is the specialization $x=2$; the paper proves this generalized identity, settling the conjecture positively.

335773 The scout-number conjecture for effective exploration of integer grids

It is known that $d+1$ scouts suffice for every $d\geq 1$.

The claim extends the paper's theorem for $d\in\{1,2\}$ and, together with the known sufficiency of $d+1$ scouts, would identify the exact minimum number of scouts needed for effective exploration of $\mathbb{Z}^d$.

335919 The sharp constant in the average absolute inner-product inequality
For each dimension $n$ and every $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$, define the average absolute inner product over the sign vectors $v_i\in\{-1,1\}^n$ by

$$
\frac{1}{2^n}\sum_{v_i\in\{-1,1\}^n}|\langle x,v_i\rangle|.
$$
The largest absolute constant $\alpha$ such that this quantity is at least $\alpha\lVert x\rVert_2$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ and all dimensions $n$ is conjectured to satisfy

$$
\frac{1}{2^n}\sum_{v_i\in\{-1,1\}^n}|\langle x,v_i\rangle|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\lVert x\rVert_2,
$$
i.e. $\alpha=1/\sqrt{2}$. This identifies the proposed sharp universal constant in the average problem, which is used to improve the lower bound for the Banach–Mazur distance between the cube and the crosspolytope.
