Problem. For integers \(m,n\ge 2\), give the grid \([m]\times[n]\) nearest-neighbor edges. Starting from \(A\subseteq[m]\times[n]\), repeatedly add every vertex having at least two neighbors already present, and let \(\langle A\rangle\) be the eventual set. Let \(b_{m,n}(k)\) count the \(k\)-element sets \(A\) with \(\langle A\rangle=[m]\times[n]\). Is \((b_{m,n}(0),\ldots,b_{m,n}(mn))\) log-concave for every \(m,n\ge2\)?

Definitions and notation
  • Definition 1

1Problem setup

Remark 1. The grid has edges between pairs whose coordinates differ by one in exactly one position.

Definition 1. A finite nonnegative sequence \((a_k)\) is log-concave when \(a_k^2\ge a_{k-1}a_{k+1}\) at every internal index.

Remark 2. The closure is independent of the order in which eligible vertices are added, because the update rule is monotone.

2What counts as a solution

  • Prove \(b_{m,n}(k)^2\ge b_{m,n}(k-1)b_{m,n}(k+1)\) for all \(m,n\ge2\) and every \(1\le k<mn\), or give specific \(m,n,k\) and exact counts violating the inequality.
  • Any computational counterexample must include code or a certificate that independently verifies closure for every counted initial set.

Notes and companion material

The target asks whether the entire finite-size probability curve has a stable coefficient shape behind the usual threshold results. Layer-by-layer injections, recurrence inequalities, and exact orbit counts under grid symmetries would all be reusable artifacts.

Original intake status. UNKNOWN as of 2026-07-27. The checked bootstrap-percolation sources study thresholds and spanning probabilities, and no source checked states or refutes this coefficient log-concavity claim.

  • 2026-07-27 prior-art search checked the standard grid bootstrap-percolation terminology, spanning-set enumeration, and log-concavity of spanning polynomials; the sharp-threshold paper does not address the coefficient sequence.
  • A proof must use more than monotonicity of the event: coefficient sequences of general monotone Boolean properties need not be log-concave.
  • The polynomial \(\sum_k b_{m,n}(k)p^k(1-p)^{mn-k}\) is the exact spanning probability, so bounds or injections between adjacent layers would remain useful even without a full proof.

Recorded example 1. For the \(2\times2\) grid the count vector is \((0,0,2,4,1)\), which is log-concave.

Computational notes

  • Complete subset enumeration gave count vectors \((0,0,0,10,13,6,1)\) for \(2\times3\), \((0,0,0,8,38,44,26,8,1)\) for \(2\times4\), \((0,0,0,14,70,102,80,36,9,1)\) for \(3\times3\), and \((0,0,0,0,130,1464,4568,8408,10926,10564,7744,4320,1816,560,120,16,1)\) for \(4\times4\). Exact integer checks found no failed log-concavity inequality in these cases.
