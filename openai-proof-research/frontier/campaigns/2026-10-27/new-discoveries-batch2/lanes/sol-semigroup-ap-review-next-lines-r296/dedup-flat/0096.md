## Only ranked scope survivor

**1. Koutschan–Wong, 1904.01925v2, Conjecture 1, p22.** For every integer n>=12 and every map c:[n]->{R,B}, count **ordered** triples (x,y,x+floor(y/2)) entirely in [n] with all three entries the same color. The conjecture states that the minimum over c is floor((n²+5)/6), attained by four blocks with endpoints floor((n+3)/6), floor((n+1)/2), floor((5n+3)/6). Repeated coordinates are allowed. Source explicitly excludes n=11, whose smaller coloring is already public and is not a discovery.

**Status: unresolved research lead, no full proof or counterexample; eligibility HOLD.** Closest source proof, Theorem 5/Section 4, optimizes a *three-block* coloring for a=1/2 and explicitly says arbitrary colorings are outside that argument. Earlier actual primary 1609.07639v2 Theorem 22 handles fixed integer a>=2 with an O(n) error; its nonmonochromatic-pair argument does not imply this exact, floored a=1/2 endpoint. Source body pp4–7 and pp20–22 and companion pair-counting/Theorem22 bodies were read. The source v2 records publication in the 2020 Springer chapter, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-44559-1_13; metadata records its earlier 2019 submission. Current narrow author/topic arXiv queries retain the source and identify nearby, different equations; they do not prove current openness.

Concrete mechanisms sustained: (i) exact weighted max-cut reformulation and its diagonal spectral certificate; (ii) combinatorial weighted-triangle packing, including a general obstruction to its strength; (iii) parity grouping retaining the adjacent y-pair colors. See discovery.md. The first spectral certificate is rigorously refuted by an integer negative direction, not merely floating eigenvalues. The second relaxation provably cannot yield the quadratic bound. The third has an exact identity but lacks a global rearrangement inequality. Next decisive calculation: retain parity-pair states and derive an exact charging/compression inequality with the residue-dependent constant floor((n²+5)/6), or exhibit a block-exchange direction whose exact count is smaller. Strongest objection: Datskovsky/Thanatipanonda–Wong pair-counting may already extend to this floor relation; neither new-to-literature status nor substantive distance from that method is cleared. Current public proof-registry head remains unavailable under inherited denied-route restrictions. Do not formalize or count this lead on the present evidence.

## Compact remaining screens

**2. Kenta Noguchi, 1904.00563v1, introduction Problem 5 attribution.** Is proper orientation number bounded by a function of maximum average degree for every finite graph? Theorem 2 proves the bipartite case when one bipartition has degree at least k+1 and Mad<=2k: start with Hakimi's k-orientation, then raise indegrees on that side to k+1. This proof cannot handle low-degree vertices on both sides or general graphs. Park: no orientation-extension lemma removing those hypotheses. Original Araujo et al. question body not separately retrieved; no universal result nominated.

**3. Mohamed Slim Kammoun, 1904.00725v1, discussion after Proposition 2.** For independent conjugation-invariant permutation laws at every n, can the fixed-point control be removed from liminf E LCS/sqrt(n)>=2? Proposition 2 uses vanishing fixed-point probabilities and the inverse-product/LIS reduction plus comparison with uniform permutations. Arbitrary dependence between the two permutations is not permitted. Park: need a uniform dichotomy between fixed-point common mass and cycle-merging loss. The separate Bukh–Zhou arbitrary-iid sqrt(n) question is not another screen or a new mechanism.

**4. Yasuaki Gyoda, 1904.00779v4, Conjecture 1.10/Theorem 1.11.** Do matching f-vectors determine the cluster? The exact finite-type and rank-two scopes are **already proved in this source**. Proof of 1.11 identifies nonzero f-vectors with positive d-vectors, reconstructs negative simple vectors, then invokes cluster uniqueness. General higher rank is not silently credited. No new nomination.

**5. Gekhtman–Nakanishi, 1904.00971v2, Conjecture 2.3.** For every irreducible exchange matrix and monotone balanced infinite mutation sequence, is there a sequence of strict sign vectors eventually matching each nonzero integer coefficient row (with its own finite starting time)? Monotone is geodesic distance growth; balanced means positive lower mutation frequency at every index. Actual rank-two proof uses Chebyshev recurrences; Markov-quiver proof treats the displayed periodic sequence. Park: no uniform higher-rank projective contraction. Dropping balance gives an already-public counterexample in Remark 4.2, not a nomination.

**6. Jin–Praeger, 1904.01204v1, Problem 1.4.** Classify all finite 3-geodesic-transitive girth-4/5 graphs with quasiprimitive or biquasiprimitive action, and their specified covers (one classification family). Actual normal-quotient reduction and incidence-design proof preserve a large primitive-action problem. Reject investment: broad group-classification gap, no new construction or exclusion mechanism; an example or finite census would not resolve the classification.

**7. Faina Solov'eva, 1904.01282v1, closing Remarks, pp105–106.** Determine all uniformity numbers for uniform coset partitions into binary Hamming codes; missing values include the proposed length-15 seed of log-uniformity 9, if it exists. Uniformity notation is logarithmic in the construction; no numerical seed claim. No seed-by-seed family inflation.

**8. Nenadov–Sudakov–Wagner, 1904.01394v2, introduction Daykin–Häggkvist attribution.** Does every partial Latin square of order n using each row, column and symbol at most n/4 times complete at order n? Source Theorem 1.3 gives an embedding at n+O(sqrt(|L|)) for sufficiently sparse L; actual proof adds vertices and repairs bad degrees before applying a decomposition theorem. Park: the added-vertex repair is forbidden at the claimed endpoint. No fresh mechanism for this longstanding sharp completion problem. Original Daykin–Häggkvist body unread.

**9. Fishel–Hurlbert–Kamat–Meagher, 1904.01436v1, Conjecture 5.9.** Is every rank r<=binom(n,2)/2 level of the right weak Bruhat lattice EKR, with intersecting meaning rank of the meet>=1? Park: need a weighted compression respecting inverse-descent multiplicities. Conjecture 6.2 and whole-lattice Question 6.3 are related, not counted separately. No universal compression proved; current specialist prior screen not complete.
