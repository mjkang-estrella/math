For any field k and finite set S, put P(S)=k[x_s:s in S], I(S)=(x_s^2:s in S) in P(S), and A(S)=k+I(S). This is a unital subalgebra since I is an ideal. An injection relabels variables and carries I(S) into I(T), hence A is a sub-FI-algebra of P. Its monomial basis consists of 1 and all monomials having at least one exponent at least 2.

Let m_j=x_1^2 x_2 ... x_j (j>=1), and J_r be the FI-ideal of A generated by m_1,...,m_r. Then J_r is a sub-FI-module of A over A. In A([r+1]), m_(r+1) does not belong to J_r: each possible monomial summand is an injected m_j times a monomial of A. If the latter factor is constant, its support has size j<=r. If it is nonconstant, it has a squared variable. Multiplying produces either an exponent at least 4 at the squared variable of m_j, or a second variable with exponent at least 2. Neither can equal m_(r+1), which has exactly one squared variable and every other exponent 1. Linear combinations cannot introduce an absent basis monomial. Thus J_1<J_2<... strictly. This proves nonnoetherianity under the FI-ideal definition, in every field characteristic.

This gives a finite description of an infinite strict chain, without a search or computation. If the module follow-up means modules over A, then A itself is free rank one over A, hence finitely presented, and the same chain obstructs noetherianity. If it means finitely presented modules over P, that is a different question: changing the coefficient algebra is not a solution. The literal record does not disambiguate.
