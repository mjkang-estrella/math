       Let f be a polynomial or a rational function over a field K. Arithmetic

dynamics studies the algebraic and number-theoretic properties of its iterates f n :=

f ◦ f ◦ ... ◦ f .



       A basic question is, if f is a polynomial, are these iterates irreducible or

not? We wish to know what can happen when considering iterates of a quadratic

f = x2 + r ∈ K[x]. The most interesting case is when r = 1c , which we will focus on,

and discuss criteria for irreducibility, i.e. stability of all iterates. We also wish to

prove that if 0 is not periodic under f , then the number of factors of f n is bounded

by a constant independent of n, i.e. f is eventually stable. This thesis is an extension

to Q(i) of the paper [1], which considered f over Q.



       This thesis involves a mixture of ideas from number theory and arithmetic

geometry. We also show how eventual stability of iterates ties into the density of

prime divisors of sequences.

Chapter 1: Introduction



         Suppose K is a field with algebraic closure K. In this thesis we study the

arithmetic dynamics of quadratic polynomials f (x) = x2 + r ∈ K[x] under iteration,

i.e.

           f n (x) = (f ◦ f ◦ ... ◦ f )(x) (n compositions)   with f 0 (x) = x.

       Fix α ∈ K and denote by f −n (α) := {β ∈ K : f n (β) = α}. If f n (x) −

α is separable, the disjoint union Tf,∞ (α) := {α} ⊔ f −1 (α) ⊔ f −2 (α) ⊔ · · · is a

rooted tree with root vertex α, and edges assigned according to the action of

f . Denote by Aut(Tf (α)) the tree automorphisms of Tf (α). Since the natural

action of the Galois group GK := Gal(K/K) on f −n (α) commutes with f ∈ K[x],

we obtain a homomorphism Gal(K/K) → Aut(Tf (α)) called the arboreal Galois

representation associated to (f, α).      One of the central problems in arithmetic

dynamics is whether the image of this homomorphism has finite index in Aut(Tf (α));

a dynamical analogue of the renowned Serre open image theorem. This question has

generated a large literature, see [2] for a summary of this and other current research

in the field.



       In this thesis, we attempt to characterize factorizations of f n (x) − α for α = 0.


                                             1
The polynomial function f is called stable over K if every iterate f n is irreducible

over K.

Definition 1. Let K be a field, f ∈ K[x], and α ∈ K. For n ≥ 1 let kn denote the

number of irreducible factors of f n (x) − α over K.

     We call the pair (f, α) (resp. f ) eventually stable over K if there is a

constant C(f, α) (resp C(f, 0)) such that for all n,


                                    kn ≤ C(f, α).


     Eventual stability is applied in some finite-index results for certain arboreal

Galois representations [3, 4], and is equivalent to a bound independent of n on

the number of Galois orbits on f −n (α) [5, Proposition 2.2]. However, this is a

straightforward consequence of the image of the arboreal representation associated

to (f, α) having finite index, so finite index of the arboreal representation implies

eventual stability. The reverse implication is much less clear. Other applications

include finiteness of S-integer points in backwards orbits; see [5, Section 3] for a

discussion of these and other results surrounding the topic.



     The following theorem of Hamblen, Jones, and Madhu [6] establishes eventual

stability of a large class of polynomials over number fields:

Theorem 1.0.1. (Hamblen, Jones and Madhu, [6, Theorem 5]) Let d ≥ 2, let K

be a field of characteristic not dividing d, and let f (x) = xd + r ∈ K[x]. If there is

a discrete non-archimedean absolute value on K with |r| < 1, then f is eventually

stable over K.

        We list some theorems that will make our work considerably easier:


Theorem 2.0.1. If p(x) ∈ Q(i)[x], then any factorization of p into irreducible

polynomials over Q(i) has the same number of factors as p̄(x), p(x) with conjugated

coefficients.


Proof. Since conjugation is a field automorphism of Q(i), it extends to a ring

automorphism of Q(i)[x]. Ring automorphisms preserve irreducibility.


Corollary 2.0.1. The polynomial frn (x) has the same number of factors irreducible

over Q(i) as fr̄n (x).


      This corollary allows us to only consider c in quadrants 1 and 3, reducing our

work load by "half".



      We also will need some way of inferring irreducibility of iterated polynomials.

To this end, Capelli’s Lemma is foundational to our study and an important criterion.


Lemma 2.0.1. (Capelli’s Lemma) Let K be a field, f (x), g(x) ∈ K[x] and let α ∈ K

be a root of g. Then g(f (x)) is irreducible over K if and only if g is irreducible over

K and f (x) − α is irreducible over K(α).

Rigid divisibility is a property of some sequences {zn }n≥1 yielding that the prime

divisors of zj are related to those of zjk in the following way:


Definition 2. Let A = {zn }n≥1 be a sequence in a field K. We say A is a rigid
