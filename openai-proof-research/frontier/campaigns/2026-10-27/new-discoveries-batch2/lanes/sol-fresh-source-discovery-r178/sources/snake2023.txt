   The analogous problem on the real has been very well studied. In that case the limiting
measure µV exists and it is the equilibrium measure in the external field V . That is, µV is the
unique probability measure that minimizes
                           ZZ                          Z
                                      1
                               log         dµ(x)dµ(y) + V (x)dµ(x)

among all probability measures µ on the real line, see e.g. [3].
    This result is not true in the complex plane. Indeed, the support of the equilibrium measure in
an external field on C is a planar domain, see [2, 3], such as a disk or an annulus in case V is radial.
Thus for radial external fields the limit of the normalized counting measures does not coincide with
the equilibrium measure in the external field.
    External fields of the form V (z) = |z|2 − 2c log |z − a| with c > 0 and a ∈ C \ {0} were studied
in [1] and it was found that the limit µV of the normalized zero counting measures exists and µV is
supported on a contour that lies inside the convex hull of the support of the equilibrium measure.
It is expected that this is a general phenomonon. It would be very interesting to identify larger
classes of external fields for which this hapens.

                   Snake polynomials with positive Chebyshev expansions
   Denote by Pm the set of all algebraic polynomials of degree at most m. Without loss of
generality, we can assume that coefficients of the polynomials are real.
   Consider a majorant µ ∈ C[−1, 1], µ(x) ≥ 0, x ∈ [−1, 1]. If there exists P ∈ Pn , P ̸= 0, such
that −µ(x) ≤ P (x) ≤ µ(x), x ∈ [−1, 1], then there exists a unique (up to orientation) polynomial
ωµ ∈ Pn called snake polynomial associated with µ, which oscillates most between ±µ (see Fig 1).
   The n-th snake polynomial ωµ is uniquely determined by the following properties:
      a)   |ωµ (x)| ≤ µ(x)     x ∈ [−1, 1] ;
