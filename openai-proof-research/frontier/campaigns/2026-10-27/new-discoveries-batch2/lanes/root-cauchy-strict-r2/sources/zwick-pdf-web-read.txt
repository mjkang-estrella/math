L8@P0: An n-dimensional linear space of functions defined on a real interval is
L9@P0: called a WT-space (weak Tchebysheff space) if no element has more than
L11@P0: more than n - 1 zeros then it is a T-space. T-spaces have proved to be useful
L12@P0: settings in which to effect the solution of numerous problems in approximation theory. In recent years an effort has been made to extend these
L13@P0: results to families of spline functions and to WT-spaces, of which splines are
L14@P0: an example. Evidently this effort would be facilitated by a more thorough
L15@P0: understanding of the similarities and differences that exist between T-spaces
L16@P0: and WT-spaces, and of the nature of WT-spaces themselves. Basic work in
L17@P0: this area has been done by Sommer and Straufi, Stockenberg, Zalik, Zielke,
L18@P0: and others. It is the goal of this paper to contribute to this growing body of
L19@P0: knowledge.
L20@P0: Section 1 contains a brief list of definitions and propositions without
L21@P0: proofs concerning WT- and T-spaces, and may be safely skipped by those
L22@P0: who are already familiar with this theory. In Section 2 we introduce the
L23@P0: notion of “degeneracy” and identify various conditions under which one may
L24@P0: conclude that a WT-space is in fact a T-space. Section 3 deals with the zero
L25@P0: structure of elements in WT-spaces that contain lower dimensional T-spaces,
L26@P0: generalizing among others a theorem of Stockenberg on WT-spaces that
L27@P0: contain a positive element. In Section 4 we consider the special case of WTspaces with a T-space of dimension one less, and Section 5 concerns
L28@P0: vanishing points, points at which every element of a linear space vanishes.
