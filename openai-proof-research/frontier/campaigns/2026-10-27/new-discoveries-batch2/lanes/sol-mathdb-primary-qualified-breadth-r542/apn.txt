Uniform exclude distributions of Sidon setsThanks: Part of this research was done in completion of an undergraduate thesis at Bard College.



Darrion Thornburgh


Date: August 24, 2026


Abstract.
    

A Sidon set SS in 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is a set such that the pairwise sums of distinct points are all distinct.
The exclude points of a Sidon set SS are the sums of three distinct points in SS, and the exclude multiplicity of a point in 𝔽2n∖S\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S is the number of such triples in SS it is equal to.
We call the function dS:𝔽2n∖S→ℤ≥0d_{S}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} taking points in 𝔽2n∖S\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S to their exclude multiplicity the exclude distribution of SS.
We say that dSd_{S} is uniform on 𝒫\mathcal{P} if 𝒫\mathcal{P} is an equally-sized partition 𝒫\mathcal{P} of 𝔽2n∖S\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S such that dSd_{S} takes the same values an equal number of times on every element of 𝒫\mathcal{P}.
In this paper, we use APN plateaued functions with all component functions unbalanced to construct Sidon sets SS in (𝔽2n)2(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{2} whose exclude distributions are uniform on natural partitions of (𝔽2n)2∖S(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{2}\setminus S into 2n2^{n} elements.
We use this result and a result of Carlet to determine exactly what values the exclude distributions of the graphs of the Gold and Kasami functions take and how often they take these values.
  




1. Introduction




Sidon sets, first introduced by Simon Sidon [26], are an important notion in additive combinatorics.
In this paper, we consider Sidon sets in the nn-dimensional vector space over 𝔽2\mathbb{F}_{2}, denoted as 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.
A Sidon set in 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is a set SS such that a+b=c+da+b=c+d has no solutions (a,b,c,d)∈S4(a,b,c,d)\in S^{4} where a,b,c,da,b,c,d are pairwise distinct.
A Sidon set is called maximal if is not contained in any (strictly) larger Sidon sets.
The maximality of a Sidon set can also be determined by its exclude points.



Definition 1.1.



[11]
Let S⊆𝔽2nS\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be a Sidon set.
The exclude points of SS is the set




𝐗(S)={a+b+c∈𝔽2n:a,b,c∈S are pairwise distinct}\excludes(S)=\left\{a+b+c\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:a,b,c\in S\text{ are pairwise distinct}\right\}



Also, the number of distinct triples summing to x∈𝔽2n∖Sx\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S is known as the exclude multiplicity (or the multiplicity) multS⁡(x)\mult_{S}(x) of xx with respect to SS.





Equivalently, the exclude points of a Sidon set S⊆𝔽2nS\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is the set of points that, if added to SS, violate the Sidon property.
So, SS is maximal if and only if 𝐗⁡(S)=𝔽2n∖S\excludes(S)=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S.




In this paper, we study the exclude points of those Sidon sets that are the graphs of almost perfect nonlinear functions.
An almost perfect nonlinear (APN) function is a function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} such that the equation F⁡(x+a)+F⁡(x)=bF(x+a)+F(x)=b has either 00 or 22 solutions for any a,b∈𝔽2na,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} where a≠0a\neq 0.
Note that it is oftentimes convenient to identify 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} with 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} to gain a multiplicative structure, and most of the known families of APN functions are constructed over finite fields.
APN functions are studied in cryptography due to their optimal resistance against differential cryptanalysis [23].
However, APN functions are also interesting in other areas of research, such as additive combinatorics.
In particular, APN functions are often used in the study of Sidon sets (c.f. [7], [22], [24], [27], or [9]) since a function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is APN if and only if its graph 𝒢F={(x,F⁡(x)):x∈𝔽2n}\mathcal{G}_{F}=\left\{(x,F(x)):x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\right\} is a Sidon set.




It is conjectured that any function obtained by changing the value of an APN function at a single point is not an APN function [3].
Carlet showed in [7] that this conjecture is equivalent to the following.



Conjecture 1.2.



[3] [7]
The graphs of all APN functions are maximal Sidon sets.





To provide an overview of this paper, in Section 2 we introduce preliminaries on Sidon sets and APN functions.
This is also the section where we discuss how we visualize 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} in a planar fashion, using a method first introduced in [11].
In Section 3, we discuss the exclude distributions of Sidon sets, which are defined as follows.



Definition 1.3.



Let S⊆𝔽2nS\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be a Sidon set.
The exclude distribution of SS is the function dS:𝔽2n∖S→ℤ≥0d_{S}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} defined by dS​(x)=multS⁡(x)d_{S}(x)=\mult_{S}(x) for any x∈𝔽2n∖Sx\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S.





In particular, we introduce the notions of local equivalence and uniformity of the exclude distribution.
In short, if SS is a Sidon set and XX and YY are subsets of 𝔽2n∖S\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S such that dSd_{S} takes the same values on XX and YY the same number of times, we say that dSd_{S} is locally equivalent at XX and YY (for a more formal definition, see Section 3).
Also, if 𝒫\mathcal{P} is a partition of some set X⊆𝔽2n∖SX\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S such that dSd_{S} is locally equivalent at any two elements of 𝒫\mathcal{P}, then we call dSd_{S} uniform on 𝒫\mathcal{P}.
We then provide examples of uniform exclude distributions.




In Section 4, we study the exclude distributions of graphs of APN functions.
In particular, we prove in Proposition 4.1 that if S⊆𝔽2nS\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is a Sidon set of size 2n2^{n} (the same size as the graph of an APN function) such that the difference of the maximal and minimal values that dSd_{S} takes is less than or equal to 2n−26\frac{2^{n}-2}{6}, then SS must be maximal.
By using a method which was also used in [7], we express d𝒢Fd_{\mathcal{G}_{F}} in terms of the Walsh transform.




We denote by Qa​(F)Q_{a}(F) the set {a}×(𝔽2n∖F⁡(a))\left\{a\right\}\times(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus F(a)) for any a∈𝔽2na\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, and we let 𝒬⁡(𝔽2n,F)\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},F) be the collection of all sets Qa​(F)Q_{a}(F).
In Section 4.2, we prove the following.



Theorem 1.4.



Suppose F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is an APN function such that F⁡(0)=0F(0)=0.
If d𝒢Fd_{\mathcal{G}_{F}} is locally equivalent at Qa​(F)Q_{a}(F) and Qα​(F)Q_{\alpha}(F) by the permutation (a,b)↦(α,b+F⁡(a)+F⁡(α))(a,b)\mapsto(\alpha,b+F(a)+F(\alpha)) for all a,α∈𝔽2na,\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, then 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F} is maximal.





Following this, we consider an example of the graph of the Gold function and observe that the Gold function has a graph that has an exclude distribution that is uniform on 𝒬⁡(𝔽2n,F)\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}},F).
We generalize this in Theorem 1.5.



Theorem 1.5.



Let F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be an APN function.
If FF is a plateaued function whose component functions are all unbalanced, then d𝒢Fd_{\mathcal{G}_{F}} is uniform on 𝒬⁡(𝔽2n,F)\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},F).
If so, then d𝒢Fd_{\mathcal{G}_{F}} is locally equivalent at Qa​(F)Q_{a}(F) and Qα​(F)Q_{\alpha}(F) by the permutation (a,b)↦(α,b+F⁡(a)+F⁡(α))(a,b)\mapsto(\alpha,b+F(a)+F(\alpha)) for all a,α∈𝔽2na,\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.





Informally, if FF is an APN plateaued function whose component functions are unbalanced, then 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F} has a very strong symmetry in its exclude points.




Using Theorem 1.5 and a result of Carlet, we prove in Section 5 exactly what values the exclude distributions of the graphs of the Gold and Kasami functions take and how many times they take those values.

2. Background and Preliminaries




2.1. Cryptographic functions




A vectorial Boolean function is a function FF from 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} to 𝔽2m\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}, but in this paper, we only focus on the case n=mn=m.
We study the exclude distributions of the graphs 𝒢F={(x,F⁡(x)):x∈𝔽2n}\mathcal{G}_{F}=\left\{(x,F(x)):x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\right\} of almost perfect nonlinear (APN) functions F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} which are those functions such that F⁡(x+a)+F⁡(x)=bF(x+a)+F(x)=b has either 00 or 22 solutions for all a,b∈𝔽2na,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} such that a≠0a\neq 0.
APN functions are an important notion in cryptography as they are those functions that are optimally resistant to a so-called differential attack when used as a substitution box in a block cipher [23].




It is equivalent to say that a function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is APN if and only if 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F} is a Sidon set, that is, a set such that the sum of any pair of distinct elements is distinct.
This connection between Sidon sets and APN functions forms a bridge between additive combinatorics and symmetric cryptography.
For this reason, studying either APN functions or Sidon sets can sometimes lead to results about the other (see, for instance [24] [27]).




Let F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be a function.
Then FF has a unique representation




F⁡(x)=∑I⊆{1,…,n}aI​∏i∈IxiF(x)=\sum_{I\subseteq\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}}a_{I}\prod_{i\in I}x_{i}



where aI∈𝔽2na_{I}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, called the algebraic normal form (ANF) of FF.
The global degree of the ANF of FF is called the algebraic degree of FF.
Moreover, if FF has algebraic degree at most 22 it is called quadratic.
The following conjecture still remains a completely open question.



Conjecture 2.1.



[3]
No APN function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} has algebraic degree nn for all n≥3n\geq 3.





It turns out that if Conjecture 2.1 is true, then any function obtained by changing an APN function at a single value is not APN.



Conjecture 2.2.



[3]
Let F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be an APN function.
If G:𝔽2n→𝔽2nG\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is a function obtained from FF by changing the value of FF at one point, then GG is not APN.





Moreover, Carlet showed in [7] that Conjecture 1.2 and Conjecture 2.2 are equivalent.
It seems reasonable that Conjecture 1.2 is true as it was shown in [24] that the smallest maximal Sidon set in 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is of size O⁡((n⋅2n)13)O((n\cdot 2^{n})^{\frac{1}{3}}) by generalizing a result of Ruzsa [25].
However, as mentioned in [7],
“there seems to be room for the existence of APN functions whose graphs are non-maximal Sidon sets”
since |𝒢F|=2n|\mathcal{G}_{F}|=2^{n} is approximately 2\sqrt{2} times smaller than the best-known upper bounds on the largest maximal Sidon set (c.f. [12]).




The Walsh transform of a vectorial Boolean function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is the function WF:(𝔽2n)2→(𝔽2n)2W_{F}\colon(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{2}\to(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{2} defined by




WF​(a,b)=∑x∈𝔽2n(−1)b⋅F⁡(x)+a⋅xW_{F}(a,b)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}(-1)^{b\cdot F(x)+a\cdot x}



for all a,b∈𝔽2na,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, where x⋅yx\cdot y denotes the standard inner product.
The Walsh transform is useful as it can be used to characterize many important and desirable cryptographic properties [10].
Two families of vectorial Boolean functions that we will refer to throughout this paper are plateaued functions and almost bent functions.



Definition 2.3.



Let F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be a function.
We call FF plateaued if for every v∈𝔽2nv\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, there exists λv≥0\lambda_{v}\geq 0 such that WF​(u,v)∈{0,±λv}W_{F}(u,v)\in\left\{0,\pm\lambda_{v}\right\} for all u∈𝔽2nu\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.




Definition 2.4.



Let F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be a function.
If WF​(a,b)∈{0,±2n+12}W_{F}(a,b)\in\left\{0,\pm 2^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\right\} for all a,b∈𝔽2na,b\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} such that (a,b)≠(0,0)(a,b)\neq(0,0), then we call FF almost bent (AB).





A component function v⋅F:𝔽2n→𝔽2v\cdot F\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2} of a function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is given by (v⋅F)​(x)=v⋅F​(x)(v\cdot F)(x)=v\cdot F(x) for all x∈𝔽2nx\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, where v≠0v\neq 0.
There is a more general notion of plateaued functions in terms of component functions of functions from 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} to 𝔽2m\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}, but our definition coincides with this more general notion for functions F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.




Since the Walsh transform always takes integer values, it follows that AB functions only exist for nn odd.
Also, it also immediately follows observe that all AB functions are plateaued.
Moreover, any AB function is also APN (c.f. [8] [29]).
Conversely, it turns out that all APN plateaued functions are also AB if nn is odd [6, Proposition 163].
However, not all APN functions are plateaued and not all plateaued functions are APN.




By a result of [29], AB functions can be characterized as those APN functions that have a graph with a constant exclude distribution.



Theorem 2.5.



[29]
Let F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} be a function.
Then FF is AB if and only if the system of equations




{x+y+z=aF⁡(x)+F⁡(y)+F⁡(z)=b\begin{cases}x+y+z=a\\
F(x)+F(y)+F(z)=b\end{cases}



has 2n−22^{n}-2 or 3⋅2n−23\cdot 2^{n}-2 solutions (x,y,z)∈(𝔽2n)3(x,y,z)\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{3} for every (a,b)∈(𝔽2n)2(a,b)\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{2}.
If so, then the system has 2n−22^{n}-2 solutions if b≠F⁡(a)b\neq F(a) and 3⋅2n−23\cdot 2^{n}-2 solutions otherwise.





This characterization of AB functions directly implies that any APN function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is AB if and only if every point in (𝔽2n)2∖𝒢F(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n})^{2}\setminus\mathcal{G}_{F} has exclude multiplicity 2n−26\frac{2^{n}-2}{6} with respect to 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F}.
Hence, all AB functions have maximal Sidon sets as their graphs.
Note that it is known that the graphs of APN power functions and the graphs are APN plateaued functions are maximal [3].

3. The exclude distribution




For a Sidon set S⊆𝔽2nS\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}, the function dS:𝔽2n∖S→ℤ≥0d_{S}\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0} defined by dS​(x)=multS⁡(s)d_{S}(x)=\mult_{S}(s) for all x∈𝔽2n∖Sx\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S is called the exclude distribution of SS.
In this section, we provide some elementary results on exclude distributions in general.
Also, we will introduce some definitions that will be used later on in Section 4, where we will study the exclude distributions of graphs of APN functions.
Moreover, in Proposition 4.1, we prove that any Sidon set of size 2n2^{n} (the same size as the graphs of APN functions) whose exclude distribution varies in value by at most 2n−26\frac{2^{n}-2}{6} must be a maximal Sidon set.

3.2. Equivalence of exclude distributions




The exclude distribution of two distinct Sidon sets can also be used as an invariant for affine equivalence.
We call two subsets S,S′⊆𝔽2nS,S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} affinely equivalent if there is an affine permutation 𝒜\mathcal{A} of 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} such that 𝒜⁡(S)=S′\mathcal{A}(S)=S^{\prime}.
Sidon sets of size of less than or equal to 99 are classified up to affine equivalence [11].
However, determining whether two Sidon sets are affinely equivalent or not is a difficult problem, in general.
Hence, invariants help determine the affine equivalence of different Sidon sets.



Definition 3.9.



Let SS be a Sidon set in 𝔽2n\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}. If S′⊆𝔽2nS^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is a Sidon set, we say that SS and S′S^{\prime} are exclude distribution equivalent (ED-equivalent) if there exists a permutation σ:𝔽2n∖S→𝔽2n∖S′\sigma\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S^{\prime} such that dS=dS′∘σd_{S}=d_{S^{\prime}}\circ\sigma.





So, ED-equivalence considers the exclude distributions of two Sidon sets SS and S′S^{\prime} to be equivalent if and only if the number of points with exclude multiplicity kk with respect to SS is equal to the number of such points with respect to S′S^{\prime}.
Equivalently, SS and S′S^{\prime} are ED-equivalent if and only if |dS−1​({k})|=|dS′−1​({k})||d^{-1}_{S}(\left\{k\right\})|=|d^{-1}_{S^{\prime}}(\left\{k\right\})| for all k≥0k\geq 0.



Remark 3.10.



Since AB functions are those whose graph is a (2n−26)(\frac{2^{n}-2}{6})-cover, the graphs of any two AB functions (with the same dimension) are ED-equivalent.
More generally, it follows from Proposition 3.4 that all kk-covers in the same dimension are ED-equivalent.

4. The exclude distribution of 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F}




In this section, we study graphs of APN functions and their exclude distributions.
As previously mentioned, it is conjectured that 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F} is maximal for any APN function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}.
In this section, we prove that the difference between the maximal and minimal values that d𝒢Fd_{\mathcal{G}_{F}} takes is at most 2n−26\frac{2^{n}-2}{6}, then 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F} is maximal.
Furthermore, we prove that the graph of any APN plateaued function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} whose component functions are unbalanced is uniform on 𝒬⁡(𝔽2n,F)\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n},F).
This result highlights a very strong regularity in the exclude multiplicities of 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F}.
Moreover, we will see in Section 5 that this main result allows us to compute the exact values that d𝒢Fd_{\mathcal{G}_{F}} takes and precisely how many times it takes those values when FF is a Gold function or Kasami function.




4.1. The maximal Sidon set conjecture for APN functions




Recall that a function F:𝔽2n→𝔽2nF\colon\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} is APN if and only if its graph 𝒢F\mathcal{G}_{F} is a Sidon set.
It has been shown that the graphs of all APN power functions and APN plateaued functions have graphs that are maximal Sidon sets [3].




To prove maximality of a Sidon set, one can also consider the difference between its minimal and maximal exclude multiplicities.
This is because Proposition 3.3 provides a relation that involves the size of the Sidon set SS, emin​(S)e_{\min}(S) and emax​(S)e_{\max}(S), and also the number of 00-points in 𝔽2n∖S\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}\setminus S.

This remains an open problem, although it may prove difficult to find such a function.
