Daniel Gabric

Note: 
Department of Math/Stats,
University of Winnipeg,
Winnipeg, MB R3B 2E9,

and Jeffrey Shallit

Note: School of Computer Science, University of Waterloo, Waterloo, ON N2L 3G1, Canada; shallit@uwaterloo.ca.



Abstract
    

A palindrome is a word that reads the same forwards and backwards. A block palindrome factorization (or BP-factorization) is a factorization of a word into blocks that becomes palindrome if each identical block is replaced by a distinct symbol. We call the number of blocks in a BP-factorization the width of the BP-factorization. The largest BP-factorization of a word ww is the BP-factorization of ww with the maximum width. We study words with certain BP-factorizations. First, we give a recurrence for the number of length-nn words with largest BP-factorization of width tt. Second, we show that the expected width of the largest BP-factorization of a word tends to a constant. Third, we give some results on another extremal variation of BP-factorization, the smallest BP-factorization. A border of a word ww is a non-empty word that is both a proper prefix and suffix of ww. Finally, we conclude by showing a connection between words with a unique border and words whose smallest and largest BP-factorizations coincide.
  




1 Introduction




Let Σk\Sigma_{k} denote the alphabet {0,1,…,k−1}\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}. The length of a word ww is denoted by |w||w|. A border of a word ww is a non-empty word that is both a proper prefix and suffix of ww. A word is said to be bordered if it has a border. Otherwise, the word is said to be unbordered. For example, the French word entente is bordered, and has two borders, namely ente and e.




It is well-known [1] that the number unu_{n} of length-nn unbordered words over Σk\Sigma_{k} satisfies




un={1,if ​n=0;k​un−1−un/2,if n>0 is even;k​un−1,if n is odd.u_{n}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }n=0;\\
ku_{n-1}-u_{n/2},&\text{if $n>0$ is even;}\\
ku_{n-1},&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

(1)





A palindrome is a word that reads the same forwards as it does backwards. More formally, letting wR=wnwn−1⋯w1w^{R}=w_{n}w_{n-1}\cdots w_{1} where w=w1w2⋯wnw=w_{1}w_{2}\cdots w_{n} and all wiw_{i} are symbols, a palindrome is a word ww such that w=wRw=w^{R}. The definition of a palindrome is quite restrictive. The second half of a palindrome is fully determined by the first half. Thus, compared to all length-nn words, the number of length-nn palindromes is vanishingly small. But many words exhibit palindrome-like structure. For example, take the English word 𝚖𝚊𝚛𝚓𝚘𝚛𝚊𝚖{\tt marjoram}. It is clearly not a palindrome, but it comes close. Replacing the block 𝚓𝚘{\tt jo} with a single letter turns the word into a palindrome. In this paper, we consider a generalization of palindromes that incorporates this kind of palindromic structure.




In the 2015 British Olympiad [2], the concept of a block palindrome factorization was first introduced. Let ww be a non-empty word. A block palindrome factorization (or BP-factorization) of ww is a factorization w=wm⋯w1w0w1⋯wmw=w_{m}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{m} of a word such that w0w_{0} is a possibly empty word, and every other factor wiw_{i} is non-empty for all ii with 1≤i≤m1\leq i\leq m. We say that a BP-factorization wm⋯w1w0w1⋯wmw_{m}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{m} is of width tt where t=2​m+1t=2m+1 if w0w_{0} is non-empty and t=2​mt=2m otherwise. In other words, the width of a BP-factorization is the number of non-empty blocks in the factorization. The largest BP-factorization11

Let ww be a length-nn word. Suppose wm⋯w1w0w1⋯wmw_{m}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{m} is the largest BP-factorization of ww. Goto et al. [3] showed that wiw_{i} is the shortest border of wi⋯w1w0w1⋯wiw_{i}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{i} where i≥1i\geq 1. This means that we can compute the largest BP-factorization of ww by greedily “peeling off” the shortest borders of central factors until you hit an unbordered word or the empty word.




The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section 2 we give a recurrence for the number of length-nn words with largest BP-factorization of width tt. In Section 3 we show that the expected width of the largest BP-factorization of a length-nn word tends to a constant. In Section 4 we consider smallest BP-factorizations in the sense that one “peels off” the longest non-overlapping border. We say a border uu of a word ww is non-overlapping if |u|≤|w|/2|u|\leq|w|/2; otherwise uu is overlapping. Finally, in Section 5 we present some results on words with a unique border and show that they are connected to words whose smallest and largest BP-factorizations are the same.

Cording et al. [11] proved that the expected length of the longest unbordered factor in a word is Θ⁡(n)\Theta(n). Taking this into account, it is not surprising that the expected length of the largest BP-factorization of a word tends to a constant.





4 Smallest BP-factorization




A word ww, seen as a block, clearly satisfies the definition of a BP-factorization. Thus, taken literally, the smallest BP-factorization for all words is of width 11. But this is not very interesting, so we consider a different definition instead. A border uu of a word ww is non-overlapping if |u|≤|w|/2|u|\leq|w|/2; otherwise uu is overlapping. We say that the smallest BP-factorization of a word ww is a BP-factorization w=wm⋯w1w0w1⋯wmw=w_{m}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{m} where each wiw_{i} is the longest non-overlapping border of wi⋯w1w0w1⋯wiw_{i}\cdots w_{1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{i}, except w0w_{0}, which is either empty or unbordered. For example, going back to Example 1, the smallest BP-factorization of 𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊𝚌𝚊𝚍𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊{\tt abracadabra} is 𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊⋅𝚌𝚊𝚍⋅𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊{\tt abra}\cdot{\tt cad}\cdot{\tt abra} and the smallest BP-factorization of 𝚛𝚎𝚊𝚙𝚙𝚎𝚊𝚛{\tt reappear} is 𝚛⋅𝚎𝚊⋅𝚙⋅𝚙⋅𝚎𝚊⋅𝚛{\tt r}\cdot{\tt ea}\cdot{\tt p}\cdot{\tt p}\cdot{\tt ea}\cdot{\tt r}.

5 Equal smallest and largest BP-factorizations




Recall back to Example 1, that 𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊𝚌𝚊𝚍𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊{\tt abracadabra} has distinct smallest and largest BP-factorizations, namely 𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊⋅𝚌𝚊𝚍⋅𝚊𝚋𝚛𝚊{\tt abra}\cdot{\tt cad}\cdot{\tt abra} and 𝚊⋅𝚋𝚛⋅𝚊⋅𝚌𝚊𝚍⋅𝚊⋅𝚋𝚛⋅𝚊{\tt a}\cdot{\tt br}\cdot{\tt a}\cdot{\tt cad}\cdot{\tt a}\cdot{\tt br}\cdot{\tt a}. However, the word 𝚛𝚎𝚊𝚙𝚙𝚎𝚊𝚛{\tt reappear} has the same smallest and largest BP-factorizations, namely 𝚛⋅𝚎𝚊⋅𝚙⋅𝚙⋅𝚎𝚊⋅𝚛{\tt r}\cdot{\tt ea}\cdot{\tt p}\cdot{\tt p}\cdot{\tt ea}\cdot{\tt r}. Under what conditions are the smallest and largest BP-factorizations of a word the same? Looking at unique borders seems like a good place to start, since the shortest border and longest non-overlapping border coincide when a word has a unique border. However, the converse is not true—just consider the word 𝚊𝚕𝚏𝚊𝚕𝚏𝚊{\tt alfalfa}. The shortest border and longest non-overlapping border are both 𝚊{\tt a}, but 𝚊{\tt a} is not a unique border of 𝚊𝚕𝚏𝚊𝚕𝚏𝚊{\tt alfalfa}.




In Theorem 8 we characterize all words whose smallest and largest BP-factorization coincide.
