                                           Abstract. We prove that every transitive graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature has poly-
                                           nomial growth. We deduce from this and a theorem of Brena and Bruè that a finitely generated
                                           group admits a Cayley graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature if and √only if it is virtually
                                           abelian. Beyond the transitive setting, we also prove a quasi-polynomial rO( log r) volume bound
                                           for balls around typical vertices in finite bounded-degree graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci cur-
                                           vature, greatly strengthening a theorem of Salez (GAFA 2022).

See [41, Theorem 4.1] for a proof that these conditions are equivalent. This definition is motivated
by a theorem of von Renesse and Sturm [46] characterising Riemannian manifolds of non-negative
Ricci curvature as precisely those for which the heat semigroup defines a contraction on the space
of Lipschitz functions. Examples of non-negatively curved graphs include Cayley graphs of abelian
groups and, more generally, Cayley graphs of any group taken with respect to a conjugation-invariant
generating set [41, Theorem 4.6]. Non-negative (and positive) Ollivier–Ricci curvature also occurs
in many examples of interacting particle systems, where it is known as the Dobrushin criterion [12]
and has been studied since the 1980s.
    Just as non-negative curvature is a local (i.e. “infinitesimal”) property of Riemannian manifolds,
non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature is a local property of graphs in the sense that it is determined
by the isomorphism classes of balls of radius 2 appearing in the graph. We are interested in
the large-scale geometric consequences of this local property. This is a classical problem in the
Riemannian context, where there are many important and now-classical theorems concerning the
large-scale geometry of manifolds of non-negative Ricci curvature. One of the most significant basic
theorems in this direction is the Bishop–Gromov inequality [35], which states that every Riemannian
n-manifold M of non-negative Ricci curvature has volume growth bounded by that of Rn , and in
fact that r−n Vol(B(x, r)) is monotone decreasing in r for every x ∈ M .
    In his influential survey [31], Ollivier [31, Problem L] asked whether there is any analogue of
the Bishop–Gromov inequality for graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. Progress in this
direction has so far been extremely limited. Indeed, it was proven only recently in a breakthrough

                                                  2
work of Salez [37] that bounded-degree graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature are not
expanders, a qualitative property vastly weaker than the polynomial growth ensured by the Bishop–
Gromov inequality. Subsequent work of the second named author and Lopez [17] established a
quantitative version of Salez’s theorem, but this quantitative version remained too weak even to
conclude that bounded-degree graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature have subexponential
volume growth. Moreover, while Salez proved his result by establishing that the random walk
on a bounded-degree graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature satisfies a sublinear expected
displacement bound of the form EX0 ∼π [d(X0 , Xt )] = o(t), both his methods and those of [17] did
not yield any quantitative version of this estimate. Since Brownian motion on manifolds of non-

Structural consequences. It is a celebrated theorem of Gromov [13] (see also [18,32]) that a group
admits a Cayley graph of polynomial volume growth if and only if it is virtually nilpotent, meaning
that it has a nilpotent subgroup of finite index. Trofimov’s theorem [44] extends this structure
theory to transitive graphs, stating that every transitive graph of polynomial volume growth is
quasi-isometric to a Cayley graph (which must be the Cayley graph of a virtually nilpotent group
by Gromov’s theorem). As such, it follows from Theorem 1.1 that every finitely generated group
admitting a Cayley graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature must be virtually nilpotent and
more generally that every locally finite transitive graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature
must be quasi-isometric to the Cayley graph of a nilpotent group.
    In [4], the first named author and E. Bruè proved that finitely generated, virtually nilpotent
groups admitting Cayley graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature must, in fact, be virtually
abelian, and more generally that transitive graphs of polynomial growth and non-negative Ollivier–
Ricci curvature must be quasi-isometric to Zk for some k ≥ 0. It is also shown conversely that every
finitely generated virtually abelian group admits a Cayley graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci
curvature. As such, Theorem 1.1 has the following very strong structural consequences.

Corollary 1.6. A finitely generated group G admits a Cayley graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci
curvature if and only if it is virtually abelian. Quantitatively, there exists a universal constant C0
such that if a Cayley graph Cay(G, S) has non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature then G admits a
finite-index subgroup isomorphic to the free abelian group Zk for some k ≤ C0 |S|3 .

Corollary 1.7. Every locally finite transitive graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature is quasi-
isometric to a Euclidean lattice. Quantitatively, there exists a universal constant C0 such that if


                                                  4
G = (V, E) is a transitive graph of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature with degrees bounded by d
then G is quasi-isometric to Zk for some k ≤ C0 d3 .

    These corollaries can be thought of as a discrete analogue of the classical fact that fundamental
groups of closed manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature have virtually abelian fundamental
groups (itself a simple consequence of the Cheeger–Gromoll splitting theorem [10]). Analogues of
this theorem have previously been proven for groups satisfying stronger and more algebraic notions
of non-negative curvature in [2, 28].
    While Corollaries 1.6 and 1.7 are vacuous for finite graphs, it is possible to deduce the following
strong finitary statement from Corollary 1.6 together with the fact that for each d ≥ 1 there is a
collection of finitely many degree-d Cayley graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature having
all other such graphs as quotients (Section 4.2).

Corollary 1.8. For every d ∈ N, there exists a constant Cd ∈ N such that the following holds. Let
G be a finitely generated group and let S be a finite, symmetric, set of generators with |S| ≤ d. If
the Cayley graph Cay(G, S) has non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature, then G contains an abelian
subgroup of index bounded by Cd .

    We expect that one can also formulate and prove a similar finitary version of Corollary 1.7,
but this appears to require one to first prove a finitary version of the results of [4] on large-scale
Ollivier–Ricci curvature and we do not pursue this here.
Remark 1.9 (The Lin–Lu–Yau curvature and the role of laziness). Let us briefly discuss the role of
the laziness parameter. Generally, the normalized Ollivier curvature is increasing with the laziness
parameter α. However, for α ≥ 21 , the normalized curvature stays constant in α, see [22]. As such,
the condition of non-negative Ollivier curvature is equivalent for all choices of the laziness parameter
between one half and one. As a consequence, our results also apply to graphs of non-negative Lin–
Lu–Yau curvature [21] since this curvature is non-negative if and only if the Ollivier–Ricci curvature
is non-negative [22, Proposition 2].
Remark 1.10 (Other notions of curvature). The property of having non-negative Ollivier–Ricci
curvature is weaker than that of having non-negative sectional curvature [41, Section 4.2], in which
the L1 Wasserstein metric is replaced by the L∞ Wasserstein metric or, equivalently, the condition
that the random walks can be coupled so that d(Xn , Yn ) is a supermartingale is replaced by the
condition that they can be coupled so that d(Xn , Yn ) is non-increasing. As such, all our results also
apply automatically to graphs of non-negative sectional curvature. It was recently proven by Guo,
Huang, and Huang [14] (see also [3]) that non-negative Bakry–Emery curvature implies volume
doubling and hence polynomial growth for all bounded-degree graphs; this is a different notion of
curvature than Ollivier–Ricci and our result is neither weaker nor stronger than the results of [14].

The grand Poisson coupling. Let S be a countable set. We consider the following simultaneous

4.1   Consequences for diffusivity
In this section we deduce Corollary 1.2 from Theorem 1.1. As mentioned in the introduction, this
deduction will invoke the finitary structure theory of transitive graphs of polynomial growth due
to Breuillard, Green, and Tao [5] and Tessera and Tointon [43]. More concretely, we will use this
theory to deduce that bounded-degree transitive graphs of non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature
are uniformly doubling and then use this doubling property to deduce the diffusive estimate from
Proposition 2.6. Here we recall that a metric space is said to be k-doubling if every bounded subset
S of the space can be covered by subsets S1 , . . . Sk with diam(Si ) ≤ diam(S)/2 for all 1 ≤ i ≤ k.

A Proof of the isoperimetric inequality

In this appendix we prove Theorem 2.3. We stress that the primary purpose of this section is
expository, and that the proof we give here does not significantly differ in mathematical content
from that of [25, Theorem 6.1]. We also note that some key parts of the proof, including the
Laplacian separation principle Theorem A.1, originate in the earlier work of Hua and the third
named author [16]. See also [20, Section 4.3] for an alternative approach to the Laplacian separation
principle.

A.1     The Laplacian separation principle
Given a connected, locally finite graph G = (V, E), we write Lip(1) for the space of 1-Lipschitz
functions on G, noting that {f ∈ Lip(1) : f (x0 ) = 0} is compact in the product topology for each
x0 ∈ V . Throughout this section we will let P denote the Markov operator for the lazy random
walk (indeed, all walks considered will be lazy) and use the sign convention ∆ = P − I for the
Laplacian. Note that if θ ∈ [0, 1] then

                                          f + θ∆f = θP f + (1 − θ)f

is a convex combination of f and P f . As such, if G has non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature and
f is 1-Lipschitz then f + θ∆f is a convex combination of two 1-Lipschitz functions and is therefore
1-Lipschitz also.

Theorem A.1 (Laplacian Separation Principle). Let G = (V, E) be a connected, locally finite graph
with non-negative Ollivier–Ricci curvature. Suppose V is written as a disjoint union V = X ∪ K ∪ Y
for a finite non-empty connected set K such that there are no edges between X and Y . There exists
a function f ∈ Lip(1) and a constant λ ∈ R such that

   1. ∆f = λ on K.

   2. f (x) = min{g(x) : g ∈ Lip(1) : g|K = f |K } and ∆f (x) ≥ λ for every x ∈ X.

   3. f (y) = max{g(y) : g ∈ Lip(1) : g|K = f |K } and ∆f (y) ≤ λ for every y ∈ Y .

                                                         35
    Note that the sets X and Y are not required to be non-empty, and indeed we will later apply
the theorem with X = ∅ via the following corollary.

Corollary A.2. Let G = (V, E) be a connected, locally finite graph with non-negative Ollivier–Ricci
curvature and suppose that K ⊆ V is a non-empty finite connected set. There exists a function
f ∈ Lip(1) and a constant λ ∈ R such that

  1. ∆f = λ on K and ∆f ≤ λ on V \ K.

  2. For every x ∈ V \ K there exists y ∼ x with f (y) ≤ f (x) − 1.

  3. f attains its global minimum on K.
