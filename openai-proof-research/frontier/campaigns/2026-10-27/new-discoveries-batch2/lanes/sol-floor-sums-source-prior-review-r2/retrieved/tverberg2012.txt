Later L. Carlitz [1] and R. C. Grimson [2] studied f and F more closely
and also gave alternative proofs of (1.1). In this paper we first give a very
simple proof of (1.1), and generalize the f ’s (and, correspondingly, the F ’s)
to functions defined by l + 1 parameters a1 , . . . , al ; m. Then we state three
new theorems: Theorem 4.1 gives the analogue of (1.1) for the case l = 3,
while Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 give upper bounds for the generalization of F
in the cases l = 2, 3. All three theorems are sharp.

      4. Theorems
      Theorem 4.1. If l = 3, then F > −2bm/2c.
      Theorem 4.2. If l = 2, then F ≤ bm/2c.
      Theorem 4.3. If l = 3, then F ≤ bm/3c.
   As the proofs are elementary and relatively simple we omit them. It
would be interesting to see corresponding results for higher values of l, and
whether the work by Grimson and Carlitz can be generalized to our general
sums.
