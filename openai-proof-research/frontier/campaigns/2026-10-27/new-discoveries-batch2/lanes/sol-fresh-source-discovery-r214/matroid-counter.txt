                                                  Ehrhart coefficient, disproving the conjecture of Ferroni, Morales, and Panova that every con-

  We recall basic operations on matroids, the corresponding properties of their base polytopes,
and the path representation of multi-path matroids.

                                           3. S-P trees
   We encode series and parallel extensions by finite rooted trees whose leaves have pairwise
distinct labels. Each internal vertex has at least two unordered children and is labeled P or
S; leaves carry no P- or S-label. We call such a tree an S-P tree. We describe basis indicators
recursively on S-P trees and identify their root polytopes as base polytopes of connected
series-parallel matroids.

3.1. Series-parallel extensions and state vectors. Two distinct elements are parallel if
they form a two-element circuit and are in series if they form a two-element cocircuit. A
parallel extension adjoins an element parallel to an existing one, so deleting the new element
recovers the old matroid. A series extension is the dual operation: the new element is in series
with an existing one, and contracting it recovers the old matroid; see [32, Section 5, p. 1546].
   Let M be a connected matroid on E with at least two elements, and fix e ∈ E; then e is
neither a loop nor a coloop. Choose distinct new labels e1 , e2 ∈/ E. Relabel e as e1 , adjoin e2
in parallel or in series with it, and denote the resulting matroid by M ′ . Its ground set is
                                     E ′ = (E \ {e}) ∪ {e1 , e2 }.
The bases of M ′ are given by the following table as B ranges over the bases of M , where an
entry containing ei is taken for each i ∈ {1, 2}.
                           extension  e∈/B            e∈B
                            parallel    B       (B \ {e}) ∪ {ei }                           (3.1)
                             series  B ∪ {ei } (B \ {e}) ∪ {e1 , e2 }
   We record a chosen sequence of series and parallel extensions by a rooted binary tree. We
begin with a P-labeled root and two leaves representing U1,2 . Each subsequent extension
is recorded by replacing the leaf corresponding to the extended element by a new internal
vertex with two leaf children. The new vertex is labeled P for a parallel extension and S for
a series extension. One leaf represents the relabeled element, and the other represents the
new element. The final leaf labels form the ground set of the resulting matroid. Figure 3

unique.

Proof of Theorem 1.3. By Theorem 6.7, every connected multi-path matroid M on at least

   It remains to treat one-element components and disconnected multi-path matroids. Since

coloop has basis indicator 1. The recursion in (3.3) therefore gives PT0 for the base polytope of
a loop extension recorded by T , and PT1 for a coloop extension. Lemma 4.2 and Definition 4.3

KT (t, 0) have nonnegative coefficients.
   Every series-parallel extension of M is a direct sum of extensions of its connected compo-
nents. Multiplicativity of Ehrhart polynomials under direct sums completes the proof.          □

                                     8. Matching polytopes
  Matching polytopes provide a polyhedral formulation of the maximum-weight matching
problem. Edmonds gave their linear inequality description [13, Theorem (P)].
Definition 8.1. Let G = (V, E) be a finite simple graph. A matching of G is a set of edges
no two of which share a vertex. The matching polytope of G is
                    M(G) = conv{1N ∈ {0, 1}E : N is a matching of G}.
The empty matching is included, and we use the lattice ZE .
  The origin and all coordinate unit vectors lie in M(G), so dim M(G) = |E|.
  Let F = (V, E) be a forest, and fix a bipartition V = A ⊔ B. For a matching N , let V (N )
be the set of vertices incident to its edges, and put
                               BN = (A \ V (N )) ∪ (B ∩ V (N )).                           (8.1)
Each edge of N replaces its endpoint in A by its endpoint in B, so |BN | = |A|.
   Liu showed that the sets BN are the bases of a matroid MF on A ⊔ B, and that M(F ) is
integrally affinely isomorphic to P(MF ) [26, proof of Proposition 5.4.1]. In our notation, his
isomorphism is the restriction of the affine map Φ : RE −→ RA⊔B defined by Φ(x) = y, where
                              X                               X
                   ya = 1 −         xe    for a ∈ A,   yb =         xe   for b ∈ B.        (8.2)
                              e∈E                             e∈E
                              a∈e                             b∈e
   The matroid MF is series-parallel. Each tree component with an edge can be built from a
single edge by adding leaves. The single-edge tree gives U1,2 . By the basis description (8.1),
adding a leaf in B adjacent to a ∈ A gives a parallel extension at a, while adding a leaf in
A adjacent to b ∈ B gives a series extension at b; cf. [1, Table 1]. Different components
give direct sums. An isolated vertex in A contributes a coloop, and an isolated vertex in B
contributes a loop.
Corollary 8.2. Matching polytopes of forests are Ehrhart positive.
Proof. If F has no edges, then M(F ) is a point. For a forest with an edge, the integral
affine isomorphism (8.2) gives i(M(F ), t) = i(MF , t). The matroid MF is Ehrhart positive
by Theorem 1.1.                                                                         □
   Number the edges of an even cycle C2m , m ≥ 2, cyclically. Since C2m is bipartite,
M(C2m ) = {x ∈ [0, 1]2m : xi + xi+1 ≤ 1 (1 ≤ i ≤ 2m)}, with indices modulo 2m. The
substitution yi = xi for odd i and yi = 1 − xi for even i identifies M(C2m ) integrally affinely

circular fence posets have order polynomials with nonnegative coefficients [22, Theorem 7.7
and Example 7.8]. Substituting t + 1 in the order polynomial gives the Ehrhart polynomial
of M(C2m ), proving Ehrhart positivity.
