                                                   Abstract. We introduce a new class of Yang–Baxter solutions, which we call non-
                                                   commutative geometric solutions, constructed from quantum group noncommutative
                                                   geometries. These solutions are built from a quantum homogeneous space, endowed
                                                   with a covariant differential graded algebra and a noncommutative Koszul connection
                                                   ∇. If ∇ is a bimodule connection, that is, if its generalised flip map σ is well defined,
                                                   then a simple set of sufficient conditions for σ to be a Yang–Baxter solution is given.
                                                   These results are applied to the cominuscule quantum flag manifolds Oq (G/LS ) en-
                                                   dowed with their Heckenberger–Kolb calculi. The noncommutative complex geometry
                                                   of the calculi decomposes their Yang–Baxter operator σ into three parts: holomorphic,
                                                   anti-holomorphic, and cross-type. For the non-exceptional examples, the holomorphic
                                                   and anti-holomorphic solutions recover, up to scalar multiple, the usual quasitriangular
                                                   braidings of the associated Drinfeld–Jimbo Uq (lsS )-modules. However, we get a new
                                                   extension of these braidings to the direct sum, which remarkably restricts to an invo-
                                                   lutive operator on the cross terms. As an application, we produce a simple uniform
                                                   presentation of the degree two relations of the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi as a quantum
                                                   exterior algebra associated to the generalised flip map σ.

                                    1. Introduction

For any vector space V , the Yang–Baxter equation is a deceptively simple condition for
a linear map
                                    σ : V ⊗ V → V ⊗ V.
Explicitly, it asks for the following equality of linear operators on V ⊗ V ⊗ V
              (σ ⊗ id) ◦ (id ⊗ σ) ◦ (σ ⊗ id) = (id ⊗ σ) ◦ (σ ⊗ id) ◦ (id ⊗ σ).
It was introduced independently by Yang, in the setting of quantum many-body problems
[46], and by Baxter, in the setting of statistical mechanics [2]. Since its introduction,
the Yang–Baxter equation has found applications in a dizzying number of areas. These
span pure mathematics, physics, and more recently quantum information theory. In pure
mathematics, for example, it plays a foundational role in braided monoidal categories and
quantum groups [20, 28, 32], it is deeply connected to knot theory and low-dimensional
topology [44], it appears in von Neumann algebras through the theory of factors [27],
and in many other areas too numerous to mention. Despite its simple form, the equation
is a highly overdetermined system of polynomial equations, so finding explicit solutions
is notoriously difficult. In fact, the first examples of what are now called Drinfeld–Jimbo
quantum groups were discovered by the Leningrad school in the course of developing the
quantum inverse-scattering method and studying the Yang–Baxter equation [21].
The noncommutative geometry of quantum groups has a rich history of interaction with
the Yang–Baxter equation. Two examples close to the spirit of this paper are the Nichols
algebra construction of higher forms for bicovariant differential calculi due to Woronow-
icz [45], and the construction of bicovariant calculi for coquasitriangular Hopf algebras,
see [30, §14.5] and [3, §2.5] for details. In this paper we open a new perspective and
give a concrete new method for constructing Yang–Baxter solutions from quantum group
noncommutative geometry. This introduces a new class of solutions that we call non-
commutative geometric Yang–Baxter solutions.
Our approach to constructing Yang–Baxter solutions centres around the notion of a
bimodule connection for a differential calculus, which we now briefly recall: A differential
calculus is a differential graded algebra Ω• generated in degree zero. In the philosophy
of noncommutative geometry it is viewed as a noncommutative generalisation of the
de Rham complex of a manifold. A left connection for a left B-module F is a linear
map ∇ : F → Ω1 (B) ⊗B F satisfying the standard left Leibniz rule. It is viewed as
                 NONCOMMUTATIVE GEOMETRIC YANG–BAXTER SOLUTIONS                               4


a noncommutative generalisation of a Koszul connection. If F is a B-bimodule, then
a bimodule connection for F , as first considered in the 1990s in [18, 19, 38], is a left
connection ∇ with an additional compatibility between the connection and the right
module structure. Explicitly, the generalised flip map
          σ : F ⊗B Ω1 (B) → Ω1 (B) ⊗B F,                f ⊗ db 7→ ∇(f b) − ∇(f )b,
is required to give a well-defined bimodule map. We call σ the generalised flip map, or
bimodule map, associated to ∇. Bimodule connections have been the focus of renewed
interest in recent years, appearing in a variety of different areas: quantum Riemann-
ian geometry [3, §3.4], quantum group noncommutative geometry [6, 9, 34], monoidal
categories [3, §3.4.2], Hopf algebroids [22], Drinfeld twist deformations [1], and spectral
triples [36, 37]. In the commutative case, σ reduces to the flip map. Thus, for the special
case of F = Ω1 (B), it is natural to ask when σ satisfies the Yang–Baxter equation.
Known examples for which the generalised flip map of a bimodule connection satisfies
the Yang–Baxter equation are relatively rare, but three important examples come from
the theory of Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups Uq (g): Woronowicz’s 3D-calculus for
Oq (SU2 ) [3, Example 3.77], the quantum plane [17], and the prototypical example of
a quantum homogeneous space: the standard Podleś sphere [3, Example 5.79]. In this
paper, motivated by the Podleś example, we undertake a systematic study of bimodule
connections for covariant differential calculi Ω1 (B) over a quantum homogeneous A-space
B, for a Hopf ∗-algebra A. We do so under the assumption that our differential calculi
satisfy the condition Ω1 (B)B + = B + Ω1 (B), where B + is the augmentation ideal of B.
This produces many results, of which we highlight the following: For a left relative Hopf
module F endowed with its canonical right B-module structure, the generalised flip map
associated to a bimodule connection ∇ is determined by the simple identity

(1)        σ(f ⊗ db) = d f(−2) bS(f(−1) ) ⊗ f(0) ,            for all f ∈ F, b ∈ B.
In particular, we see that σ is independent of the choice of ∇.
Covariant first-order calculi over B are dual to and in bijective correspondence with
quantum tangent spaces. Quantum tangent spaces T are noncommutative generalisations
of the tangent space of a homogeneous space at the coset of the identity. By definition,
their unitisation T ⊕ C1 is a coideal of the finitary dual B ◦ , which is moreover closed
under the left L-module structure of B ◦ , where L is the quantum generalisation of the
universal enveloping algebra of the isotropy Lie subalgebra of a homogeneous space.
Dualising the identity (1) to the tangent space setting offers a crucial new perspective.
It allows us to prove that if T is generated by the restriction to B of twisted primitive
elements of A◦ , then σ is always a Yang–Baxter solution. This is the key result of the
paper, giving the noncommutative geometric Yang–Baxter solutions of the title.
In order to convince the reader of the importance of this class of solutions, we must of
course produce a significant family of motivating examples. To this end, we look to the
standard Podleś sphere Oq (S 2 ). The differential calculus of Oq (S 2 ), known as the Podleś
calculus, is the simplest example of a Heckenberger–Kolb calculus Ω•q (G/LS ) for a comi-
nuscule quantum flag manifold Oq (G/LS ). This is a family of Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum
homogeneous spaces, labelled by the cominuscule roots αx of a complex simple Lie alge-
bra g. (Here we have denoted S := Π\{αx }, with Π being the set of simple roots of g.)

For the simplest case of sl2 , with S = ∅, we recover Oq (S 2 ). These differential calculi
form what is arguably the most important class of quantum group noncommutative ge-
ometries. They have been the subject of intense investigation over the last twenty years,
and we are now approaching a reasonable understanding of their structure. One of the
main results of this paper is to apply our general framework to the Heckenberger–Kolb
calculi. Each of these calculi possesses a unique covariant connection [14, Theorem 4.5].
Indeed, this connection has recently been identified as a noncommutative Levi-Civita
connection [6, Theorem 6.14] (in the sense of Beggs and Majid [3, §8.1]) for the canoni-
cal metric of the calculi. (See also earlier work of Matassa for the special case of quantum
projective space [34].) In particular, all of these connections are bimodule connections.
It essentially follows from the construction of Heckenberger and Kolb that their tangent
spaces are generated by twisted primitive elements, and so their generalised flip maps σ
are Yang–Baxter solutions.
The classical flag manifolds are Kähler, and in particular complex manifolds. Much of
this complex geometry survives to the quantum setting. For example, the differential
calculi Ω•q (G/LS ) have a unique covariant double complex structure. This allows us
to decompose the Yang–Baxter operator σ into three parts: a holomorphic term, an
anti-holomorphic term, and a cross term. Each of these maps is diagonalisable, and our
explicit formulae for σ allow us to calculate their spectra. Using this, we show that the
holomorphic and anti-holomorphic solutions recover (up to scalar multiple) the usual
quasitriangular braidings of the associated Drinfeld–Jimbo Uq (lsS )-modules. However,
we get a new extension of these braidings to the direct sum, which remarkably restricts
to an involutive operator on the cross terms. This is quite surprising behaviour for
non-Kac-type quantum groups, where naturally occurring involutive maps are rare.
A very important application of our analysis of σ is to give a conceptual description
of the higher Heckenberger–Kolb forms. We recall that in noncommutative geometry
one usually starts with a first-order differential calculus (an algebraic structure modelled
on the space of 1-forms of a smooth manifold) and then extends it to a higher-order
differential calculus. In the classical setting, this extension is simply the exterior algebra
of the 1-forms. However, in the noncommutative setting things are far more subtle.
There does, however, exist a universal, or maximal prolongation, an extension such
that every other extension arises as a quotient of it. Explicitly describing this maximal
prolongation (for example, determining its space of highest degree non-zero forms) is
a very challenging problem. (In many ways, it is analogous to determining when the
Nichols algebra of a braided vector space is finite-dimensional.) Moreover, even when
one does determine the relations explicitly, it can be difficult to make conceptual sense
of the resulting terms.
This is the case for the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi. In [25] a technically intricate R-
matrix description of the maximal prolongation of the first-order forms was derived, and
it was shown that the calculi have classical dimension. Here we use the generalised flip
map σ to give a new concise conceptual description of the maximal prolongation. In
particular, we show that the space of generating relations

Summary of the Paper. The paper is organised as follows: In §2 we recall some neces-
sary preliminaries about differential calculi, maximal prolongations of first-order calculi,
connections, bimodule connections, and torsion.
In §3 we establish general results relating bimodule connections, their generalised flip
maps, and the degree two relations of the maximal prolongation. We then study invert-
ible generalised flip maps and introduce the notion of ∗-invertibility.
In §4 we specialise to the case of a covariant differential calculus Ω1 (B) over a quantum
homogeneous space B ⊆ A. We show that for a relative Hopf module F , with canonical
right module structure, the generalised flip map of a bimodule connection ∇ satisfies the
identity (1). Moreover, we show that in the ∗-calculus case, the generalised flip map σ
is always ∗-invertible.
In §5 we pass to the dual quantum tangent space picture and show that tangent spaces
generated by twisted primitive elements give rise to generalised flip maps σ satisfying
the Yang–Baxter equation. This constitutes one of the principal results of the paper.
In §6, motivated by the delicate relationship between the Yang–Baxter solution σ and
noncommutative complex geometry of the quantum flags, we undertake a systematic
treatment of noncommutative complex structures and bimodule connections, producing
a number of results of independent interest. For example, we give explicit criteria for
when a covariant first-order almost complex structure can be extended to an almost
complex structure. Surprisingly, we also show that factorisability of the almost complex
structure is equivalent to σ squaring to the identity on tensor products of forms of mixed
holomorphic and anti-holomorphic type.
In §7 we apply our general framework to the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi over the comi-
nuscule quantum flag manifolds. We show that their bimodule maps satisfy the Yang–
Baxter equation and that the calculi are σ-exterior with respect to their unique covariant
connection. Moreover, we recover their almost complex structures and conclude that σ
squares to the identity on mixed forms.
In §8 we calculate the spectrum of σ for the classical series of cominuscule quantum
flag manifolds and (for the non-exceptional examples) identify its restrictions to purely
holomorphic and anti-holomorphic forms as rescalings of the quasitriangular braiding.
We also compare σ with Drinfeld’s normalised braiding, and with the braiding underlying
the Nichols algebra presentation of the anti-holomorphic exterior algebra of the quantum
Grassmannians given in [31].
In §9 we calculate the generalised flip maps and the degree two relations explicitly for
several low rank cominuscule quantum flag manifolds: the Podleś sphere, the quantum
                NONCOMMUTATIVE GEOMETRIC YANG–BAXTER SOLUTIONS                              7


projective plane, the quantum Lagrangian quadric, and the (4, 2)-quantum Grassman-
nian.
Finally, in Appendix A we present some technical tables detailing representation theo-

This section provides basic material on differential calculi, connections, bimodule con-

paper, all algebras are over C and are assumed to be unital. Moreover, all unadorned
tensor products are over C.

2.1. Differential Calculi. A differential calculus, or a dc, is a differential graded algebra
 Ω• ≃ k∈Z≥0 Ωk , d that is generated as an algebra by the elements a, db, for a, b ∈ Ω0 .

When no confusion arises, we denote the dc by Ω• , omitting the exterior derivative d.
For a given algebra B, a differential calculus over B is a differential calculus such that
B = Ω0 .
A ∗-differential calculus, or a ∗-dc, over a ∗-algebra B is a differential calculus over B
such that the ∗-map of B extends to a (necessarily unique) conjugate-linear involution
∗ : Ω• (B) → Ω• (B) satisfying the identity d(ω ∗ ) = (dω)∗ , for all ω ∈ Ω• , and for which
             (ω ∧ ν)∗ = (−1)kl ν ∗ ∧ ω ∗ ,             for all ω ∈ Ωk , ν ∈ Ωl .

A first-order differential calculus (fodc) over an algebra B is a pair (Ω1 (B), d), where
Ω1 (B) is a B-bimodule and d : B → Ω1 (B) is a derivation such that Ω1 is generated as
a left (or equivalently right) B-module by those elements of the form db, for b ∈ B. A
first-order ∗-differential calculus (∗-fodc) over a ∗-algebra B is defined in analogy with
the ∗-dc case.
We say that a dc (Γ• , dΓ ) over B prolongs a fodc (Ω1 , dΩ ) over B if there exists a
bimodule isomorphism φ : Ω1 → Γ1 such that dΓ = φ ◦ dΩ . It can be shown that any
fodc admits a prolongation Ω• that is maximal, in the sense that there exists a unique
differential graded algebra morphism from Ω• onto any other prolongation of Ω1 . We
call this extension the maximal prolongation of Ω1 .

          8 = (2α, 2α) = (β + γ, β + γ) = (β, β) + (γ, γ) + 2(β, γ) = 4 + 2(β, γ).
Thus we see that (β, γ) = 2. Recalling the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we see that
                                               √ √
                            |(β, γ)| ≤ ∥β∥∥γ∥ = 2 2 = 2.
The fact that we have equality implies that β and γ are linearly dependent, which from
the axioms of a root system implies that β = ±γ. Since (β, γ) = 2 we must have that
β = γ, contradicting our assumptions. Thus we see that eα ∧eα = 0. The same argument
establishes that fα ∧ fα also vanishes.                                              □
