                                                 Differential calculi on non-irreducible quantised flag manifolds of the A-series have recently been
                                                 investigated using Lusztig positive root vectors, revealing that several features of classical differential
                                                 geometry persist in the noncommutative setting. We extend this investigation to quantised flag
                                                 manifolds associated with the lowest-rank quantum groups of types C and D whose classical spaces
                                                 are holomorphic contact manifolds. Building on the Heckenberger–Kolb framework, we construct
                                                 covariant first order differential calculi. The maximal prolongations of calculi restricted to contact
                                                 directions have lower dimensions than their classical counterparts. In type C2 , however, adjoining
                                                 the Reeb direction yields a three-dimensional calculus on the contact flag manifold whose maximal
                                                 prolongation has classical dimension. Adjoining the remaining simple-root direction yields a four-
                                                 dimensional calculus on the full flag manifold, again with a maximal prolongation of classical
                                                 dimension. For both calculi, we explicitly present the exterior algebras and identify them with those
                                                 of the corresponding framing calculi on the quantum group. In type D4 , we show that adjoining
                                                 the Reeb direction yields a nine-dimensional calculus on the contact flag. We conjecture that its
                                                 maximal prolongation also has classical dimension.

Introduction
The theory of quantum groups, initiated in the mid-1980s by the foundational work of Drinfel’d [Dri87] and
Jimbo [Jim85] on quantum universal enveloping algebras, and by Woronowicz’s parallel development of compact
quantum groups via the formalism of C ∗ -algebras [Wor98], has by now reached considerable maturity from a
purely algebraic and representation-theoretic standpoint. In parallel, and largely independently, the framework
of noncommutative differential geometry, as systematised by Connes [Con90] and, in the Hopf-algebraic setting,
by Woronowicz [Wor89] and by Majid [Maj88], provides a general apparatus for extending differential calculus
to noncommutative algebras. The natural problem of reconciling these two traditions, that is, of equipping
the coordinate algebras of quantum groups with geometrically meaningful, covariant differential calculi, has
proven to be substantially more difficult than one might initially expect. Indeed, a fundamental obstruction
already appears at the level of the simplest quantum group Oq (SU2 ): Woronowicz’s three-dimensional calculus
fails to be bicovariant [Wor89]. This behavior is not confined to the particular construction, as subsequent
classification results establish the nonexistence of classical dimension bicovariant calculi for general quantum
groups [SS95, HS98]. Nevertheless, rich and geometrically meaningful covariant calculi of classical dimension
do exist. The paradigmatic example is again the three-dimensional left-covariant calculus on Oq (SU2 ) [Wor87],
which restricts to the celebrated two-dimensional calculus on the Podleś sphere [Pod89], and whose behavior
closely mirrors that of the classical geometry of SU2 and S 2 respectively. In view of these difficulties at the
level of the quantum group itself, a more tractable approach is to shift attention to quantum homogeneous
spaces, and in particular to the quantised flag manifolds. This strategy is further motivated by the classical
fibration G → G/P , which expresses a semisimple Lie group in terms of its flag varieties, so that a systematic
understanding of the differential geometry of the latter provides essential structural information about the
former.
   A covariant first order differential calculus on a quantum homogeneous space must be compatible with the
coaction of the underlying quantum group. The general classification of such calculi is well understood [Her98],
and follows essentially from the equivalence of categories established in [Tak79]. However, singling out those
covariant differential structures that carry meaningful geometric content remains a substantially more delicate
problem. Significant progress has nonetheless been made in the setting of the irreducible quantised flag manifolds,
that is, the q-deformations of those classical flag varieties that are of compact Hermitian symmetric type. The
foundational work of Heckenberger and Kolb [HK03] established a complete classification of all covariant first
order differential calculi of classical dimension over these spaces, and subsequently investigated their maximal
prolongations and the resulting higher-order differential geometry [HK06]. This family includes, as its most
studied members, the quantum projective spaces CPnq and the quantum Grassmannians of the A-series, which
have been the subject of intensive investigation by numerous authors [Mat22, CMO23, DDL08].
   More recently, progress has been made towards the understanding of quantised flag manifolds in the non-
irreducible setting. The full flag manifolds of the A-series were studied in full generality by Ó Buachalla and
Somberg in [OS25], who demonstrated that their noncommutative geometry is remarkably faithful to the classical
picture, in particular the exterior algebra of the maximal prolongation recovers the Dolbeault complex, and a
noncommutative version of the Borel–Weil theorem holds. Moreover, restricting such calculi to the corresponding
contact quantised flag manifold, namely the one associated with the parabolic subset obtained by removing
the first and last simple roots, is shown to yield a differential calculus of classical dimension [GO26]. Along
the same lines, in [COR25] the authors carry out a detailed study of the noncommutative complex geometry
of the full flag manifold of Oq (SU3 ). A similar case study is [VY15], where a quantum analogue of the BGG
resolution for the full quantised flag manifold of Oq (SU3 ) is employed to verify the Baum–Connes conjecture with
trivial coefficients for the dual discrete quantum group. Matassa [Mat25] provides a complementary construction
of covariant differential calculi on quantised flag manifolds through equivariant quantisations of the positive
nilradical, recovering the Heckenberger–Kolb calculi in the irreducible case. Beyond the A-series, the irreducible
rank-two setting has also been studied from a spectral perspective: in [WDO22], the quantum BGG resolution is
used to construct a Dolbeault–Dirac operator for the B2 -irreducible quantised flag manifold, yielding a spectral
triple. More directly related to the non-irreducible setting considered here, Carotenuto, Ó Buachalla, and Pagani
[COP26] investigate a Lusztig differential calculus on the type C2 quantised flag manifolds.
   Despite these developments, the covariant differential geometry of non-irreducible quantised flag manifolds
remains comparatively little explored, particularly outside the A-series. These spaces are q-deformations of
partial and full flag varieties whose classical counterparts are not of Hermitian symmetric type. The geometric
and algebraic complications arising beyond the Hermitian symmetric setting are substantial even classically,
while quantum deformation introduces additional constraints on the construction of differential calculi and their
higher-order extensions. The existing examples therefore motivate a broader investigation of how much of the
classical differential geometry survives quantisation, especially for non-irreducible flag manifolds in other Lie
types.
   The present paper constitutes a first step in this direction. We consider the quantisations of those non-
irreducible flag manifolds that carry a holomorphic contact structure [CS09] in the lowest-rank examples of



                                                        2
the C-series and D-series of complex simple Lie algebras, namely those associated to sp4 and so8 , respectively
(and, by the exceptional isomorphism B2 ∼     = C2 , the B-series as well in the rank-two case). Following the
general strategy of Heckenberger–Kolb [HK06], we construct quantum tangent spaces corresponding to the
antiholomorphic contact directions and use them to produce covariant first order differential calculi. We then
study their maximal prolongations, comparing the graded dimensions of the resulting exterior algebras with
those of the classical exterior algebras on the corresponding cotangent spaces. Additionally, for the case of D4 ,
we construct a differential calculus directly at the level of the quantised coordinate algebra Oq (SO8 ), induced
from an antiholomorphic tangent space generated by the Lusztig root vectors associated to a distinguished
reduced decomposition of the longest element of the Weyl group.
   Already for such low-rank examples, directly adapting the Heckenberger–Kolb construction to the contact
directions produces a picture different from that of the previously studied A-series calculi: the exterior algebras
of the maximal prolongations have graded dimensions strictly smaller than their classical counterparts in certain
degrees. The same phenomenon occurs for the calculus considered on Oq (SO8 ). These examples highlight the
need for a careful, case-by-case investigation when extending constructions beyond the irreducible setting, since
the recovery of classical dimension cannot be taken for granted.
   In type C2 , however, the dimension drop can be overcome by adjoining the tangent direction corresponding
classically to the Reeb direction. The resulting three-dimensional first order calculus has a maximal prolongation
of classical dimension. We give an explicit presentation of its exterior algebra and establish that the natural
identification of the cotangent spaces of the flag calculus and its framing calculus extends to an isomorphism of
their exterior algebras. Moreover, adjoining the remaining simple root vector to the corresponding tangent space
yields a four-dimensional calculus on Oq (Sp4 ) whose maximal prolongation also has classical dimension. The
constructions are compatible with restriction to contact and the full quantised flag manifolds respectively, and
classical dimension is preserved.
   A related four-dimensional calculus with a maximal prolongation of classical dimension was constructed in
[COP26] using Lusztig root vectors. That construction, however, does not admit a restrictions to the contact flag
manifold that is of classical dimension. The calculi here analysed, on the other hand, combine the recovery of
classical dimension with compatibility with the quantum homogeneous space structure, in particular with both
the contact and full flag manifolds. This compatibility provides a geometric distinction between the constructions
and makes the calculi of interest natural candidates for studying the noncommutative differential geometry of
type C2 quantised flag manifolds.
   At the same time, our computations indicate that the dimension drop for calculi built from the contact
directions alone should not be understood as a peculiarity of the Lie types outside the A-series. Indeed, when the
analogous construction is carried out for the full flag manifold of Oq (SU3 ), whose underlying classical variety is
also of contact type, a comparable collapse of the exterior algebra occurs. Together with the extended C2 calculus,
this suggests that the recovery of classical dimension depends on how the contact structure is incorporated into
the quantum tangent space, rather than on Lie type alone.
   The results presented here suggest several directions for further investigation. A forthcoming study will carry
out a similar analysis in type D4 , examining whether the Reeb adjoined calculus that we discuss in this paper
has a maximal prolongation of classical dimension. In the contact setting, a natural next step is to explore
whether the differential calculus can be adapted to construct a quantum analogue of the Rumin complex [Rum94],
reflecting the contact structure more closely than the full de Rham complex. Exploring the relationship between
differential forms and quantum BGG resolutions [HK07], together with the full flag example studied in [VY15], is
another promising direction of research. Finally, extending the present analysis to higher-rank contact quantised
flag manifolds would provide a broader testing ground for the phenomena identified here.
  In Section 1 we recall the necessary preliminary material on Hopf algebras and their homogeneous spaces,
quantum groups, quantised flag manifolds, and differential calculi.
  In Section 2 we review the basic structure of the Lie algebra sp4 , together with its quantised enveloping
algebra and quantised coordinate algebra, and explicitly construct a two-dimensional first order differential
calculus on the contact quantised flag manifold of Oq (Sp4 ), computing its maximal prolongation. We then briefly
compare this construction with the analogous one on the full flag manifold of Oq (SU3 ). Returning to type C2 ,
we show that adjoining the Reeb direction yields a three-dimensional calculus whose maximal prolongation
has classical dimension. We subsequently extend this calculus by adding the simple root vector E2 , obtaining
a four-dimensional calculus with the same property. For both calculi, we give explicit presentations of the
exterior algebras and establish compatibility with restriction to the corresponding quantised flag manifolds,
where classical dimension is recovered.
  In Section 3, we recall the relevant algebraic structures and construct an eight-dimensional first order differential
calculus on the contact quantised flag manifold of Oq (SO8 ). We compute its maximal prolongation and show
that it has non-classical dimension. We then extend this calculus by adjoining the Reeb direction, obtaining
a nine-dimensional calculus, whose corresponding maximal prolongation remains undetermined. Finally, we
discuss how Lusztig calculi do not supply geometrically meaningful framings for the Reeb-extended calculus.

1. Preliminaries
In this Section, we collect the algebraic and geometric background required for the developments in the subsequent
sections. Our aim here is not to provide a comprehensive account of the material, but rather to fix notation,
recall the main constructions, and highlight those aspects of the theory that will play a central role in what
follows. In particular, we review the Hopf-algebraic framework underlying quantum homogeneous spaces, the
Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum groups and their associated quantised flag manifolds, as well as the theory of differential
calculi over Hopf algebras and homogeneous spaces. These ingredients provide the natural setting for the general
constructions developed later.
   In Section 1.1 we recall basic notions concerning Hopf modules, quantum homogeneous spaces, and the
categorical equivalences that will be used throughout the paper. In Section 1.2 we review Drinfeld–Jimbo
quantum groups and the construction of quantised flag manifolds. Finally, in Section 1.3 we discuss differential
calculi.

Definition 1.8. Let A be an algebra.

2. Differential calculi on Oq (Sp4 ) and its contact quantised flag manifold
We now specialise the preceding constructions to the contact quantised flag manifold corresponding to the
symplectic Lie algebra sp4 .
   In Section 2.1 we recall the Lie algebra sp4 , its quantised enveloping algebra, and the associated quantum

E2 , producing a four-dimensional contact-adapted calculus on Oq (Sp4 ) and its full flag manifold. For ease of
reference, the dual pairings used throughout this section are collected in a table displayed in Appendix A.

   The differential calculus constructed above does not belong to the category Oqq (SO88 /LS ) Mod0 . Consequently,
determining the relations of its maximal prolongation requires us to follow the approach outlined in Section 2.4,
which relies on the choice of a suitable framing calculus. As in the C2 case, a central step in this procedure is
the construction of an explicit lift of the root vector Eθ . While this was computationally feasible in that case,
carrying out an analogous computation in the present setting has so far proved elusive. This difficulty points to
the need for a more general strategy for constructing such lifts, whose development we leave to future work.
   Nevertheless, the problem of describing a geometrically coherent exterior algebra for the flag manifold under
consideration remains. With this motivation, we explore an alternative approach, following the construction of
[OS25] based on Lusztig root vectors. The authors construct covariant first order differential calculi for quantised
flag manifolds of type A whose maximal prolongations have classical dimension. Their results suggest that
Lusztig root vectors may also provide a useful starting point in the present setting, provided that their choice is
compatible with the root vector Eθ appearing in our construction.

group, the compatibility required above leads to the following question:

       the corresponding root vectors span a quantum tangent space and the root vector associated to the
       highest root restricts to the prescribed Eθ ?
