 We prove that flat Jackiw--Teitelboim (JT) gravity arises as a conformal scaling limit of random flat combinatorial disks. We give a clean criterion, formulated in terms of branching explorations, for a discrete model to converge to this conjecturally universal limit. For simplicity, we apply this criterion to a Boltzmann ensemble of disks with rectilinear boundary, defined by summing over combinatorial types and integrating over lengths. As the length of a distinguished side tends to infinity, the rescaled surface converges in law to a continuum random surface prescribed by Ferrari's path-integral formulation of JT gravity. Convergence is in the topology of smooth convergence of the conformal factors on compact subsets of the interior when uniformizing in a fixed domain; the limiting conformal factor is described by a harmonic Gaussian field closely related to holomorphic multiplicative chaos.
 The crux is a branching exploration of flat JT gravity whose lengths evolve as conditioned Cauchy processes and whose unexplored components are conditionally independent. We construct this exploration using a conformal welding theorem proved in a companion paper, and show that it is the scaling limit of Budd's row-by-row exploration of the discrete disks, analogous to the peeling of random planar maps. Under a tightness condition, our criterion upgrades this convergence to a conformal scaling limit for the discrete disks. Other natural combinatorial disk models admit analogous first-row recursions, providing a route to their scaling limits.

 We develop an algebraic theory of fusion, mobility, and statistics in abelian gapped fracton orders and topological phases. The framework generalizes the algebraic description of abelian anyons to systems with restricted-mobility and spatially extended excitations. We consider translation-invariant bosonic systems whose pointlike and extended excitations have finite fusion order. The basic objects are braided fusion complexes (BFCs): chain complexes encoding fusion and mobility, equipped with a braiding chain map encoding statistics. Their equivalence classes define braided fusion theories (BFTs). Our framework encompasses arbitrary translation-invariant Pauli codes, as well as all abelian anyon theories in two spatial dimensions, including chiral theories. We give evidence for the conjecture that every nondegenerate BFT can be realized as the excitation theory of a gapped phase and that the BFT describing such a phase is a phase invariant. We derive constraints on pointlike fusion and mobility, showing, for example, that neither the Pauli X nor Z sectors of the X-cube and anisotropic models can occur alone in a nondegenerate BFT. For nondegenerate theories with only pointlike excitations, we extract a simpler theory of mutual statistics, consisting of a fusion module and a map to its statistical dual. This theory of mutual statistics is an invariant of the BFT and, at odd fusion order, gives an equivalent description of the full braided fusion theory. We interpret remote detection as the generalization of mutual statistics to fracton orders. In the BFC setting, we give a precise formulation of the excitation-detector principle: every nontrivial excitation can be detected by an operator supported at spatial infinity, and every nontrivial detector detects some excitation. Assuming a unique infinite-space ground state, we prove that this principle is equivalent to nondegeneracy.

 Can entangling codewords across independent uses of a quantum channel improve the rate of classical communication? An old conjecture states that there is no such entanglement advantage for all channels. Although Hastings's result and follow-up works disproved this conjecture , it turns out that enormous dimension is required to construct a channel where entanglement enhances classical communication. This indicates that there might be no entanglement advantage for many physical channels, or for all low dimensional channels. Motivated by recent progress on (generalized) amplitude damping channels, we propose a general Schur-weighted block entropy inequality, and show that there is no entanglement advantage for all phase-covariant channels including multi-level amplitude damping channels, generalized dephasing channels, and damping-dephasing channels. As a by-product, we also recover the recently established result that there is no entanglement advantage for all qubit channels.

 We confirm a conjecture of the first author [SODA'26] on a strong form of pseudo-telepathy for Khot's $d$-to-$1$ games: for every $d\geq 4$, every $ε>0$, and every $k\in\mathbb N$, there is a $d$-to-$1$ label-cover game of classical value less than $ε$ that admits a perfect quantum strategy using finite-dimensional shared entanglement with measurement contexts of radius at least $k$. As a consequence, there exist graphs whose quantum chromatic number $χ_q$ is $3$ and whose classical chromatic number $χ$ is arbitrarily large. This is the first improvement on the quantum chromatic gap since the work of Brassard, Cleve, and Tapp [Phys. Rev. Lett.'99], which produced graphs for which $χ$ grows exponentially in $χ_q$.
 To prove the conjecture , we proceed as follows. We start with a ``stretched'' version of the Mermin--Peres magic square, obtained by spacing out its equations and linking them with additional long equation wires. This operation increases the radius of the initial measurement contexts of a perfect quantum strategy for the game, while preserving classical unsatisfiability. We then apply parallel repetition to make the classical value small, and we turn the system into a pseudo-telepathic multi-layered Kneser hypergraph via a PCP construction of Dinur, Guruswami, Khot, and Regev [STOC'03]. Finally, we feed the resulting hypergraph into the reduction of Fei, Minzer, and Wang from hypergraph colouring to $4$-to-$1$ games, we establish quantum completeness of this reduction, and we show that it preserves context radius.

 The online Steiner Forest problem is a fundamental network design problem in which terminal pairs arrive one at a time and an algorithm must irrevocably purchase edges connecting each pair. The natural greedy algorithm connects each terminal pair by a shortest path after making all previously purchased edges free. Awerbuch, Azar, and Bartal (SODA 1996) proved that this algorithm is $O(log^2 k)$-competitive and conjectured an $O(log k)$ bound, where $k$ is the number of terminal pairs. Bamas, Drygala, and Maggiori (SODA 2022) obtained an $O(\log \NumPairs\log\log \NumPairs)$ bound for greedy on a restricted class of inputs, leaving the general case open.
 In this paper, we resolve the competitive ratio of the greedy algorithm for online Steiner Forest, proving that it is tightly $Θ(log(k) log log(k))$. Specifically, we establish an $O(log(k) log log(k))$ upper bound for arbitrary instances and construct a matching $Ω(log(k) log log(k))$ lower bound. Our results refute the $O(log k)$ conjecture of Awerbuch, Azar, and Bartal and establish a tight $Θ(log log k)$ separation between the greedy algorithm and the best possible online algorithm for Steiner Forest.

 This paper provides a human digestion of a proof of the spectral isometry conjecture for $C^*$-algebras found by GPT-6 Astra. We prove that every unital surjective complex-linear spectral isometry between unital $C^*$-algebras is a Jordan isomorphism. Without the assumption that the map preserves the identity, it is a central unitary multiple of a Jordan isomorphism. The main step is to show that an entire one-parameter group of unital spectral isometries consists of algebra automorphisms. To prove this, we extend the group to the bidual, which splits into finite and properly infinite summands. On the finite part, we use quadratic and cubic trace identities and on the properly infinite part, we apply a structure theorem for maps that preserve square-zero elements. To show that both summands are invariant, we use holomorphic dependence on the parameter together with a uniform resolvent bound, which ensures that self-adjoint elements have images with real spectrum. We then apply the group theorem to inner automorphisms conjugated by the original spectral isometry. This reduces the problem to the commutative case, where Nagasawa's theorem completes the proof.

 Halin conjectured in 1964 that every connected graph has an end-faithful spanning tree. He proved this for countable graphs, but in the early 1990s Seymour and Thomas and, independently, Thomassen found uncountable counterexamples. Both are infinitely connected and hence one-ended, and their unique end has uncountable degree (i.e.\ contains uncountably many pairwise disjoint rays).
 Several authors have since asked how much of the conjecture survives. Diestel asked in 1992 whether a graph has an end-faithful spanning tree as soon as all its one-ended connected subgraphs have one; by a theorem of Polat, this would give end-faithful spanning trees in all connected graphs whose ends have countable degree. Diestel and Kühn introduced topological spanning trees, a topological variant of end-faithful spanning trees that exists in every one-ended connected graph, and asked in 2004 whether every connected graph has one. And Carmesin asked in 2019 whether every graph has a spanning tree that is end-faithful at least for its thin ends, the ends of finite degree.
 We answer all three questions in the negative. We show that the uncountably chromatic graph from an earlier note of ours on a problem of Erdős and Hajnal has only ends of countable degree, each dominated by only countably many vertices, yet it has neither an end-faithful nor a topological spanning tree. A refinement of this graph moreover has no spanning tree that contains a ray from every end of degree~1.

 The last year has seen a shift in AI-assisted mathematics, with frontier language models producing proofs of and counterexamples to long-standing open conjectures . However, studying only the current set of major breakthroughs provides an incomplete picture of LLM capabilities and the computational resources needed to achieve reported results. To address this, we introduce a new dataset, OpenConjecture, which collects conjectures from mathematics papers published on the arXiv since February 2026. We use these conjectures as a source of open mathematical problems, and apply frontier GPT models to a random sample of 200 of these conjectures over three passes using a reproducible protocol without problem-specific mathematical guidance. Rather than attempt expert verification of these candidate solutions, we analyze the types of problems for which LLMs produce solutions and the characteristics of these solutions. The models we test produce candidate solutions for roughly 60% of the conjectures , with counterexamples about 1.3 times more common than proofs. We estimate solution novelty, assess how solutions cite and relate to existing literature, and analyze whether the proposed resolution relies on either an error in or underspecification of the conjecture . We find that models sometimes propose resolutions that are outside the authors' intent (e.g., proposing the trivial group as a counterexample) and that a number of proofs follow closely from existing results, but that some resolutions exhibit at least moderate novelty. Our findings underscore the need for evaluations that go beyond correctness to assess fidelity to the intended statement, attribution, and the nature of the mathematical contribution.
