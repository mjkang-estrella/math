Abstract
A set 
\(R\subseteq V(G)\)
 of a graph 
G
 is a general position set if any triple set 
\(R_0\)
 of 
R
 is non-geodesic, that is, no vertex of 
\(R_0\)
 lies on any geodesic between the other two vertices of 
\(R_0\)
 in 
G
. Let 
\({\mathcal {R}}\)
 be the set of general position sets of a graph 
G
. The general position number 
\(\textrm{gp} (G)\)
 of a graph 
G
 is defined as 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)=\max \{|R|:R \in {\mathcal {R}}\}\)
. In this paper, for an arbitrary maximal outerplane graph 
G
 of order at least 7, we prove that 
\(\textrm{gp} (G)=3\)
 if and only if 
G
 is a straight linear 2-tree. Moreover, we determine the upper bound on the gp-numbers for any maximal outerplane graph and characterize the corresponding extremal graphs.

Introduction
Let 
\(G=(V(G),E(G))\)
 be a graph with 
\(x,y\in V(G)\)
. The 
distance
 
\(d_G(x,y)\)
 is the number of edges on a shortest path between 
x
 and 
y
 in 
G
. A shortest 
x
, 
y
-path is called an 
x
, 
y
-
geodesic
 in 
G
. For 
\(R\subseteq V(G)\)
, a triple set 
\(R_0\subseteq R\)
 is 
non-geodesic
 if no vertex of 
\(R_0\)
 lies on any geodesic between the other two vertices of 
\(R_0\)
 in 
G
. Then, 
R
 is a 
general position set
 of 
G
, if any triple set 
\(R_0\)
 of 
R
 is non-geodesic in 
G
. Let 
\({\mathcal {R}}\)
 be the set of general position sets of 
G
. The 
general position number
 (gp-number for short) of 
G
, denoted by 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\)
, is defined as 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)=\max \{|R|:R\in {\mathcal {R}}\}\)
. Hence, the cardinality of a largest general position set is the general position number of 
G
. A general position set of order 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\)
 is a 
\(\textrm{gp}\)
-
set
 of 
G
.
Now, the general position number has been extensively investigated. From [
19
], we recall that finding a gp-set in 
G
 is an NP-hard problem and several general bounds on the gp-number were presented. The properties of general position sets in graphs are characterized in [
1
]. In this paper, a formula for the gp-number of the complement of a bipartite graph is determined and simplified for the complement of trees, grids, and hypercubes, respectively. The gp-numbers have been focused on other product graphs and connected with strong resolving graphs in [
13
]. And in [
21
], the edge version of the general position problem is introduced and some formulas for the edge general position numbers of hypercubes, trees, and grid graphs are proven. In [
16
], the edge general position numbers of a graph 
G
 equal to 
\(|E(G)|-1\)
 and 3 are, respectively, characterized and sharp upper and lower bounds on the edge general position number are deduced for block graphs. Recently, the problem of the general position set in a graph has been extended to 
d
-general position set [
14
] and 
k
-Steiner general position set [
15
], respectively. Moreover, a related problem is the monophonic position problem [
31
] obtained by replacing “shortest path" in the general position problem by “induced path." Some recent results on this topic are reported in [
10
, 
23
, 
24
, 
29
, 
33
].
An interesting topic related to the gp-number is the Cartesian product operation. Denote by 
\(G^n\)
 the Cartesian product of 
n
 copies of a graph 
G
 and by 
\(P_\infty \)
 the two-way infinite path. The gp-number of Cartesian products of 
\(P_\infty \)
’s is researched [
11
, 
12
, 
20
]; in particular, 
\(\textrm{gp}(P_\infty ^n)=2^{2^{n-1}}\)
 is proven in [
12
]. A sharp lower bound on the gp-number for the Cartesian products of graphs was determined in [
8
]. Also, a sharp upper bound for the 
\(\textrm{gp}\)
-number of Cartesian product of two connected graphs was given [
29
] with equality if and only if they are both generalized complete graphs. Moreover, it was proven [
28
] that the gp-number is additive on Cartesian product of two trees.
A graph is 
planar
 if it can be drawn without crossing edges. A planar graph with a fixed embedding in the plane is a 
plane graph
, which divides the plane into connected regions called 
faces
. If a region is unbounded, then we call it the 
outer face
, otherwise the 
inner face
. Further, a plane graph is called an 
Outerplane graph
 if it has a plane embedding such that all vertices lie on the boundary of its outer face. An Outerplane graph 
G
 is 
maximal
 if 
\(G+uv\)
 is not Outerplane for any two non-adjacent vertices 
u
 and 
v
 of 
G
, and such a graph is called a 
maximal Outerplane graph
 or just an 
MOP
 [
7
]. A fixed Outerplane embedding of a maximal Outerplane graph is called a 
maximal outerplane graph
.
In [
25
], we see that a maximal outerplane graph has a unique Hamiltonian cycle and is a triangulation graph (or a triangulated disk). Hence, the inner faces of a maximal outerplane graph are triangles. Let 
f
 be an inner face of a maximal outerplane graph 
G
. If there exists at least one common edge between 
f
 and the outer face, then 
f
 is a 
marginal triangle
; otherwise, 
f
 is an 
internal triangle
. We say that a maximal outerplane graph 
G
 is 
striped
 if it has no internal triangles.
There have been extensive studies on MOPs with their properties and applications. In particular, some progress has been made in the study of domination on MOPs with its variations. These variations include several additional constraints on domination sets such as connected and weakly connected domination, total and semitotal dominations, independent domination, distance domination, and location–domination [
2
, 
6
, 
9
, 
18
, 
22
, 
30
, 
32
]. Inspired by the application of the domination sets on MOPs, we focus on the 
\(\textrm{gp}\)
-number of MOPs in this paper.
We proceed as follows. In Sect. 
2
, we give further definitions needed and prove some preliminary results. We also determine the formula of the gp-number of fans, which is important in our subsequent proof. In Sect. 
3
, we first prove that 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)=3\)
 if and only if 
G
 is a straight linear 2-tree for any maximal outerplane graph 
G
 of order at least 7. Then, we determine an upper bound on the gp-number for any maximal outerplane graphs and characterize the corresponding extremal graphs for which the upper bound are achieved.
2 
Preliminaries
All graphs considered in this paper are finite, connected, simple and undirected. In this section, we define some concepts and introduce the notation, as well as some results needed later.
The order of a graph 
\(G=(V(G),E(G))\)
 is the number of vertices of 
G
 and is denoted by 
n
. For a vertex 
\(v\in V(G)\)
, let 
\(N_G(v)\)
 be the set of neighbors of 
v
 and 
\(N_G[v]=N_G(v)\cup \{v\}\)
. The cardinality of 
\(N_G(v)\)
 is the 
degree
 of vertex 
v
 and is denoted by 
\(d_G(v)\)
. A vertex 
v
 is a 
k
-
vertex
 of 
G
 if 
\(d_G(v)=k\)
. The maximum degree of graph 
G
 is denoted by 
\(\Delta (G)\)
. For a graph 
G
 with an integer 
\(0\le k\le n-1\)
, we denote by 
\(m_G(k)\)
 the number of vertices in 
G
 with degree 
k
.
For a subset 
\(S\subseteq V(G)\)
, 
G
[
S
] denotes the subgraph of 
G
 induced by 
S
. Let 
\(H_G\)
 (for short 
\(H_G=H\)
) be a Hamiltonian cycle of a maximal outerplane graph 
G
. For two vertices 
u
 and 
v
 of 
H
, the path on 
H
 that goes in the clockwise direction from 
u
 to 
v
 is called the 
u
, 
v
-
segment
 of 
H
, and the other path is called the 
v
, 
u
-
segment
 of 
H
, denoted by 
\(S_{uv}\)
 and 
\(S_{vu}\)
, respectively. The 
interval
 
\(I_{G}(u,v)\)
 between vertices 
u
 and 
v
 is a vertex subset which consists of all vertices lying on some 
u
, 
v
-geodesic of 
G
, that is, 
\(I_{G}(u,v)=\{w\in V(G):d_G(u,v)=d_G(u,w)+d_G(w,v)\}\)
. For a positive integer 
\(i\ge 2\)
, let 
\(P_i\)
 be a path on vertices 
\(p_1\)
, 
\(\ldots \)
, 
\(p_i\)
 with natural adjacencies.
A graph 
F
 is said to be a 
minor
 of graph 
G
 if 
F
 can be obtained from 
G
 by a series of vertex deletions, edge deletions and edge contractions. Given a graph 
F
, a graph 
G
 is said to be 
F
-
minor-free
 if no minor of 
G
 is isomorphic to 
F
. A graph 
G
 is outerplane if and only if it does not contain 
\(K_4\)
 and 
\(K_{2,3}\)
 as minors [
26
]; hence, any maximal outerplane graph is 
\(K_4\)
-minor-free and 
\(K_{2,3}\)
-minor-free. If 
G
 and 
H
 are vertex-disjoint graphs, then the 
join
 
\(G\oplus H\)
 is the graph with vertex set 
\(V(G)\cup V(H)\)
 and edge set 
\(E(G)\cup E(H)\cup \{uv:u\in V(G),v\in V(H)\}\)
. A graph 
\(F_{n-1}\)
 of order 
n
 is a 
fan
 if it has only one 
\((n-1)\)
-vertex, only two 2-vertices, and other vertices (if they exist) have degree 3. Note that, for 
\(n\ge 3\)
, 
\(F_{n-1}=\{v\}\oplus P_{n-1} \)
 (the join of an isolated vertex 
v
 and 
\(P_{n-1}\)
) where vertex 
v
 is called the 
central vertex
 of fan 
\(F_{n-1}\)
. A 
maximal fan
 of a maximal outerplane graph is a fan which cannot be enlarged by adding a vertex. The maximal fan cannot be contained in any larger fan of 
G
. For a positive integer 
\(n\ge 3\)
, if 
\(G_n\)
 is a connected graph with 
\(V(G_n)=\{v_1,\ldots ,v_n\}\)
 and 
\(v_iv_j\in E(G_n)\)
 if and only if 
\(0<|i-j|\le 2\)
, then we call 
\(G_n\)
 the 
straight linear 2-tree
 [
3
], see Fig. 
1
.
Fig. 1
Full size image
Straight linear 2-tree 
\(G_n\)
For all other undefined graph theory concepts, see [
4
]. For a positive integer 
n
, we will use the notation 
\([n]=\{1,\ldots ,n\}\)
. Now, we recall the following results.

                
Lemma 2.1

                
[
5
, Proposition 2] Let 
G
 be a maximal outerplane graph of order 
\(n\ge 4\)
. If 
G
 has 
k
 internal triangles, then 
\(m_G(2)=k+2\)
.

              
                
Lemma 2.2

                
[
5
, Proposition 3] If 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph of order 
\(n\ge 3\)
, then 
G
 has 
\(n-1\)
 faces and the Hamiltonian cycle has 
\(n-3\)
 chords.

              
                
Lemma 2.3

                
[
7
, Corollary 3.2] Let 
G
 be an MOP with 
\(v\in V(G)\)
. Then, 
\(N_G[v]\)
 forms a maximal fan in 
G
 with 
v
 as the central vertex.

              
The order of a largest complete subgraph of a graph 
G
 is called the 
clique number
 
\(\omega (G)\)
. And the maximum order of an induced complete multipartite subgraph of the complement of a graph 
G
 is denoted by 
\(\eta (G)\)
. We will use the following result.

                
Lemma 2.4

                
[
8
, Proposition 4.2] Let 
G
 and 
H
 be two graphs. Then,
$$\begin{aligned} \textrm{gp}(G\oplus H) =\max \{\omega (G)+\omega (H),\eta (G),\eta (H)\}. \end{aligned}$$

              
Next, we recall a characterization of general position sets from [
1
], for which some preparation is required. Let 
\(S\subseteq V(G)\)
 and 
\({\mathcal {P}}=\{S_1,\ldots ,S_p\}\)
 be a partition of 
S
. For any two 
\(i,j\in [p]\)
, if the value 
\(d_G(u,v)\)
 is independent of the selection of 
u
 and 
v
, then 
\({\mathcal {P}}\)
 is 
distance-constant
, where 
\(u\in S_i\)
 and 
\(v\in S_j\)
. Further, if 
\({\mathcal {P}}\)
 is a distance-constant partition, and 
\(i,j\in [p]\)
, 
\(i\ne j\)
, then the distance between two arbitrary pairwise vertices from 
\(S_i\)
 and 
\(S_j\)
 is the distance between the sets 
\(S_i\)
 and 
\(S_j\)
 in 
G
, denoted by 
\(d_G(S_i,S_j)\)
. A distance-constant partition 
\({\mathcal {P}}\)
 is 
in-transitive
 if 
\(d_G(S_i,S_k)\ne d_G(S_i,S_j)+d_G(S_j,S_k)\)
 holds for arbitrary pairwise different 
\(i,j,k\in [p]\)
.

                
Lemma 2.5

                
[
1
, Theorem 3.1] Let 
G
 be a graph with 
\(S\subseteq V(G)\)
. Then, a vertex subset 
S
 is a general position set if and only if the components of 
G
[
S
] are complete subgraphs whose vertices form an in-transitive, distance-constant partition of 
S
.

              
For convenience, we denote by 
H
 the unique Hamiltonian cycle of MOPs in the following two results.

                
Proposition 2.6

                
Let 
\(u'\)
 be an arbitrary internal vertex of an 
u
, 
v
-segment 
\(S_{uv}\)
 on 
H
 of order at least 4. If 
\(d_G(u,v)=1\)
, then 
\(N_G(u^{\prime })\subseteq V(S_{uv})\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
Let 
x
 be an internal vertex of 
\(S_{vu}\)
. Then, 
\(x\ne u, v\)
. If 
\(d_G(u',x)=1\)
, as the unique Hamilton cycle is the boundary of the outer face, then both edges 
\(u'x\)
 and 
uv
 would be internal and hence cross, which is a contradiction. As a consequence, we can conclude that 
\(N_G(u^{\prime })\subseteq V(S_{uv})\)
. 
\(\square \)

              
Symmetrically, we see that 
\(N_G(v^{\prime })\subseteq V(S_{vu})\)
, where 
\(v'\)
 is an internal vertex of the 
v
, 
u
-segment on 
H
. Next, we will prove that there is one common neighbor vertex for two adjacent vertices of 
H
 as follows:

                
Proposition 2.7

                
If 
u
 and 
v
 are two adjacent vertices of 
H
, then 
\(u,v\in N_G(h)\)
 for some 
\(h\in V(H)\)
, where 
\(h\ne u,v\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
Let 
\(V(H)=\{h_1,\ldots ,h_n\}\)
 with clockwise order on 
H
. Assume, without loss of generality, that 
\(u=h_1\)
 and 
\(v=h_2\)
. If 
\(d_G(h_1)=2\)
 (the case 
\(d_G(h_2)=2\)
 is symmetrical), then 
\(d_G(h_2,h_n)=1\)
 and hence 
\(h_1,h_2\in N_G(h_n)\)
. If 
\(d_G(h_1)\ge 3\)
 and 
\(d_G(h_2)\ge 3\)
, then it follows from Lemma 
2.3
 that 
\(N_G[h_1]\)
 forms a maximal fan with 
\(h_1\)
 as the central vertex in 
G
. As 
\(h_1\)
 is adjacent to 
\(h_2\)
 in 
G
, there exists a vertex 
\(h\in N_G(h_1)\)
 such that 
h
 is adjacent to 
\(h_2\)
 in 
G
. By the generality of 
u
 and 
v
, the result holds. 
\(\square \)

              
As observed, the clique number 
\(\omega (G)\)
 is a general lower bound on the 
\(\textrm{gp}\)
-number, that is, 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge \omega (G)\)
. Since each inner face of an MOP 
G
 is triangle, we have 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge 3\)
. In the following result, a general lower bound on the gp-number in terms of 
\(\Delta (G)\)
 is determined for any maximal outerplane graph 
G
.

                
Lemma 2.8

                
If 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph, then 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge \lfloor \frac{2(\Delta (G)+1)}{3}\rfloor \)
.

              
                
Proof

                
Note that 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge 3\)
 since 
\(\omega (G)\ge 3\)
. Let 
\(\Delta (G)=k\)
. If 
\(k\le 4\)
, then 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge \lfloor \frac{2(k+1)}{3}\rfloor \)
 holds clearly. Hence, we may assume that 
\(k\ge 5\)
. Let 
u
 be the maximum degree vertex of 
G
. Then, it follows that 
\(d_G(u)=k\)
. By Lemma 
2.3
, then 
\(N_G[u]\)
 forms a maximal fan 
\(G_u\)
 with the central vertex 
u
 in 
G
. Further, without loss of generality, let 
\(G_u=\{u\}\oplus P_{k}\)
.

                
From Lemma 
2.4
, it follows that 
\(\textrm{gp}(G_u)=\max \{1,3,\eta (P_k)\}\)
. Since 
\(k\ge 5\)
, we have 
\(\textrm{gp}(G_u)=\eta (P_k)\)
. Based on the value of 
k
, we have
$$\begin{aligned} \textrm{gp}(G_u)=\left\{ \begin{array}{rcl} &{}2j, &{}{ k=3j};\\ &{}2j+1, &{}{\text{ otherwise }}. \end{array}\right. \end{aligned}$$
Note that 
\(\lfloor \frac{2(k+1)}{3}\rfloor =2j\)
 if 
\(k=3j\)
 and 
\(\lfloor \frac{2(k+1)}{3}\rfloor =2j+1\)
 if 
\(k\in \{3j+1,3j+2\}\)
. Considering that 
\(G_u\)
 is an induced subgraph of 
G
 and 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge \textrm{gp}(G_u)\)
, we have 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)\ge \lfloor \frac{2(k+1)}{3}\rfloor \)
, as desired. 
\(\square \)

              
From the proof of Lemma 
2.8
, we have the following result immediately:

                
Corollary 2.9

                
If 
\(F_{n-1}\)
 is a fan of order 
\(n\ge 5\)
, then 
\(\textrm{gp}(F_{n-1})=\lfloor \frac{2n}{3}\rfloor \)
.

              
Corollary 
2.9
 also demonstrates the sharpness of the lower bound of Lemma 
2.8
, which plays a crucial role in the subsequent proofs. Next, we will characterize the structure of subgraphs induced by general position sets in MOPs.

                
Lemma 2.10

                
If 
R
 is a general position set of an MOP 
G
 with 
\(|R|\ge 4\)
, then 
G
[
R
] contains no triangle.

              
                
Proof

                
By Lemma 
2.5
, the vertices of 
R
 form an in-transitive, distance-constant partition. Note that 
\(|R|\ge 4\)
. If 
G
[
R
] contains a triangle, then 
G
 would contain a 
\(K_4\)
-minor. It contradicts the fact that 
G
 is 
\(K_4\)
-minor-free. Hence, the result holds. 
\(\square \)

              
Next, we will characterize the structure of maximal outerplane graphs with order at least 7 and 
\(\Delta (G)=4\)
. In essence, this result is proved earlier in [
17
] from the viewpoint of constructing the triangle graph 
T
(
G
) of a graph 
G
 and within the proof of a different result. We include our own proof for completeness.

                
Lemma 2.11

                
If 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph of order at least 7, then 
G
 is a straight linear 2-tree if and only if 
\(\Delta (G)=4\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
If 
G
 is a straight linear 2-tree (see Fig. 
1
), then 
\(\Delta (G)=4\)
 holds clearly. For the converse, assume that 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph with 
\(\Delta (G)=4\)
. Let 
H
 be a Hamiltonian cycle of 
G
 and 
\(h_1,\ldots ,h_{n}\)
 be the consecutive vertices of 
H
.

                
Claim 1.
 
\(m_G(2)=2\)
.

                
Suppose that 
K
 is an internal triangle in 
G
 and let 
\(h_i,h_j,h_k\)
 be the vertices of 
K
 with clockwise order on 
H
. Then, 
\(\min \{d_H(h_i,h_j),d_H(h_j,h_k),d_H(h_i,h_k)\}\ge 2\)
. Since 
K
 is an internal triangle and 
\(\Delta (G)=4\)
, then 
\(d_G(h_i)=d_G(h_j)=d_G(h_k)=4\)
. Note that 
\(n\ge 7\)
. Hence, 
\(\max \{d_H(h_i,h_j),d_H(h_j,h_k),d_H(h_i,h_k)\}\ge 3\)
. Without loss of generality, let 
\(d_H(h_i,h_j)\ge 3\)
 and let 
\(h_i u_1\cdots u_\ell h_j\)
 be the 
\(S_{h_ih_j}\)
-segment on 
H
 with 
\(\ell \ge 2\)
. Since 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph, there exists a vertex 
\(u_s\)
 such that the induce subgraph on 
\(\{h_i,h_j,u_s\}\)
 is a triangle for 
\(s\in [\ell ]\)
. Then, 
\(\max \{d_G(h_i),d_G(h_j)\}\ge 5\)
 as a contradiction. Hence, 
G
 contains no internal triangle. By Lemma 
2.1
, we see that 
\(m_G(2)=2\)
.

                
Claim 2.
 If 
\(N_G(h_i)=\{x,y,z,w\}\)
 with 
\(h_i\in I_H(x,y)\)
, then 
\(d_H(z,w)=1\)
 for 
\(i\in [n]\)
.

                
Assume that 
\(x, h_i, y, z\)

3 
Main Results
In this section, we first prove that 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)=3\)
 if and only if

 is a straight linear 2-tree for any maximal outerplane graph

Recall that the gp-number of any MOP is at least 3 as each inner face of an MOP is a triangle. We will characterize the structure of MOPs with gp-number 3.

\(u\in V(H)\)
. Assume, without loss of generality, that 
\(R=\{h_{3i-2},h_{3i-1}:i\in [k]\}\)
 with order 2
k
. Let 
\(\overline{R}=V(H)\setminus R=\{h_{3i}:i\in [k]\}\cup \{h_n\}\)
 and thus we have 
\(R\cup \overline{R}=V(H)\)
. Since 
\(h_1, h_2, h_3, h_4\)
 are four consecutive vertices on 
H
, by Lemma 
2.10
 and Claim 2, then 
\(\{h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4\}\subseteq N_G[\bar{r}]\)
 for some 
\(\bar{r}\in \overline{R}\)
.

                
Assume first that 
\(\bar{r}=h_n\)
 (the case 
\(\bar{r}=h_{n-1}\)
 is similar). It follows that 
\(h_2\)
 is a 3-vertex of 
G
 and 
\(N_G(h_2)=\{h_1,h_3,h_n\}\)
. Applying Lemma 
2.5
, since 
\(d_G(h_2,h_4)=2\)
, then we have 
\(d_G(h_2,h_5)=2\)
 and 
\(d_G(h_n,h_5)=1\)
. Repeating this procedure, we end up 
\(d_G(h_n,h_{n-2})=1\)
 and then we observe that 
\(G=F_{n-1}\)
 with the central vertex 
\(h_n\)
.

                
Now, assume that 
\(\bar{r}=h_{3i}\)
, where 
\(i\in [k-1]\)
. If 
\(i=1\)
, then 
\(\bar{r}=h_{3}\)
. Hence, 
\(h_2\)
 has exactly two neighbors 
\(h_1\)
 and 
\(h_3\)
 in 
G
 since 
\(d_G(h_1,h_3)=1\)
. It means that 
\(d_G(h_2,h_4)=2\)
. By Lemma 
2.5
, 
\(d_G(h_2,h_5)=2\)
, which implies that 
\(d_G(h_3,h_5)=1\)
. Then, we get that 
\(h_4\)
 also is a 2-vertex of 
G
. Repeating this procedure we end up 
\(d_G(h_3,h_{n-2})=1\)
. Furthermore, if 
\(d_G(h_3,h_{n-1})=d_G(h_3,h_n)=1\)
, then 
\(G=F_{n-1}\)
. Otherwise,
$$\begin{aligned} G\cong \left\{ \begin{array}{rl} F_{3,n}, &{}d_G(h_3,h_n)=1, d_G(h_3,h_{n-1})=2;\\ F_{2,n}, &{}d_G(h_3,h_n)=2, d_G(h_3,h_{n-1})=1. \end{array}\right. \end{aligned}$$
Similar to the above, we have 
\(G\in {\mathcal {F}}\)
 if 
\(i\in [k-1]\setminus [1]\)
.

                
Subcase 2.
 
\(|R^*|=2\)
.

                
Assume that 
\(h_1, h_x\in R^*\)
 with 
\(3\le x\le \lfloor \frac{n}{2}\rfloor +1\)
. Then, 
\(d_H(h_1,h_x)=x-1\)
. The proof is simple if 
\(n\le 9\)
. Hence, we may let 
\(n\ge 10\)
 in the following.

                
Assume that 
\(x=3\)
, that is, 
\(\{h_1,h_3\}\subseteq R^*\)
. By Claim 1, we may, without loss of generality, let 
\(R=\{h_{3i+2},h_{3i+3}:i \in [k-1]\}\cup \{h_1,h_3\}\)
. Then, 
\(\overline{R}=\{h_{3i+1}:i \in [k]\}\cup \{h_2\}\)
. Since 
\(h_3, h_4, h_5\)
, and 
\(h_6\)
 are four consecutive vertices on 
H
, using Claim 2 and Lemma 
2.10
, then 
\(\{h_3,h_4,h_5,h_6\}\subseteq N_G(\bar{r})\)
 for some 
\(\bar{r}\in \overline{R}\)
. If 
\(\bar{r}=h_2\)
, then 
\(h_3\)
 is a 2-vertex and 
\(h_5\)
 is a 3-vertex in 
G
. By Lemmas 
2.5
 and 
2.10
, we have 
\(d_G(h_5,h_8)=2\)
 and 
\(d_G(h_2,h_8)=1\)
. Note that 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph. Hence, 
\(d_G(h_2,h_7)=1\)
. Repeating the similar discussion as above, we get 
\(d_G(h_2,h_{n-1})=1\)
. Then, 
\(d_G(h_2,h_{n-2})=1\)
 and 
\(d_G(h_1,h_{n-1})=2\)
. By Lemmas 
2.5
 and 
2.10
, we have 
\(d_G(h_2,h_n)=1\)
. Thus, 
\(G\cong F_{n-1}\)
.

                
If 
\(\bar{r}=h_{3i+1}\)
, without loss of generality, we may assume that 
\(\bar{r}=h_4\)
. It follows that 
\(d_G(h_4,h_6)=2\)
, that is, 
\(h_5\)
 is a 2-vertex in 
G
. Since 
\(\{h_5,h_6,h_8\}\subseteq R\)
 and 
\(d_G(h_6,h_8)=2\)
, applying Lemma 
2.5
, we have 
\(d_G(h_5,h_8)=2\)
 and hence 
\(d_G(h_4,h_8)=1\)
. Similarly, 
\(d_G(h_4,h_9)=1\)
. Since 
G
 is a maximal outerplane graph, 
\(d_G(h_4,h_7)=1\)
. Repeating the similar discussion above, it is easy to see that 
\(d_G(h_4,h^{\prime })=1\)
 for 
\(h^{\prime }\in V(H){\setminus }\{h_2,h_4\}\)
. Furthermore, we obtain 
\(G\cong F_{n-1}\)
 if 
\(d_G(h_4,h_2)=1\)
, otherwise 
\(G\cong F_{1,n}\)
. Analogously, 
\(G\in \{F_{n-1}\}\cup {\mathcal {F}}\)
 if 
\(\bar{r}=h_{3i+1}\)
 with 
\(i\in [k]\setminus [1]\)
.

                
Assume that 
\(5\le x \le \lfloor \frac{n}{2}\rfloor +1\)
. By Claim 1, we have
$$\begin{aligned} |R|&\le 2\times \big \lfloor \frac{(n-x-2)}{3} \big \rfloor +2+ 2\times \big \lfloor \frac{(x-4)}{3}\big \rfloor +2+2\\&=2\times \big \lfloor \frac{(3k-x-1)}{3}\big \rfloor +2\times \big \lfloor \frac{(x-1)}{3}\big \rfloor +4\\&=2\times \left( \big \lfloor \frac{(-x-1)}{3}\big \rfloor +\big \lfloor \frac{(x-1)}{3}\big \rfloor \right) +2k+4. \end{aligned}$$
According to our assumption, 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)=2k\)
 implies that 
\(\big \lfloor \frac{(-x-1)}{3}\big \rfloor +\big \lfloor \frac{(x-1)}{3}\big \rfloor =-2\)
. It is easy to verify that 
\(x=3y\)
 for 
\(y\in [\lceil \frac{k}{2}\rceil ]\)
. Then, 
\(y\ge 2\)
 since 
\(x\ge 5\)
.

                
Let 
\(R_1=\{h_{3j},h_{3j+1}:j\in [y-1]\}\)
 and 
\(R_2=\{h_{3i+2},h_{3i+3}:i\in [k-1]\setminus [y-1]\}\)
. Since 
\(|R^*|=2\)
, we may let 
\(R=R_1\cup R_2\cup \{h_1,h_y\}\)
. Set 
\(\overline{R}_1=\{h_{3j-1}:j\in [y]\}\)
 and 
\(\overline{R}_2=\{h_{3i+1}:i\in [k]\setminus [y-1]\}\)
. Hence, 
\(V(H)\setminus R=\overline{R}_1\cup \overline{R}_2\)
. Since 
\(\{h_{n-2},h_{n-1},h_n,h_1\}\)
 and 
\(\{h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4\}\)
 are four consecutive vertices sets on 
H
, by Claim 2, 
\(\{h_{n-2},h_{n-1},h_n,h_1\}\subseteq N_G[\bar{r}]\)
 and 
\(\{h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4\}\subseteq N_G[\bar{r}_1]\)
 for some 
\(\bar{r}\)
, 
\(\bar{r}_1\in \overline{R}\)
. Analogously to the previous discussion, we can get 
\(G\cong F_{n-1}\)
 if 
\(\bar{r}=\bar{r}_1\)
.

                
Assume that 
\(\bar{r}\ne \bar{r}_1\)
. We claim that 
\(\bar{r}\not \in \overline{R}_1\)
. If not, it is easy to see that 
\(\bar{r}\ne h_2\)
. Otherwise, it contradicts 
\(\bar{r}=\bar{r}_1=h_2\)
 or there are two intersection edges 
\(h_2h_n\)
 and 
\(h_1\bar{r}_1\)
 in 
G
. Assume that 
\(\bar{r}=h_{x-1}\)
 with 
\(x\ge 5\)
. By Proposition 
2.6
 and Claim 2, then 
\(\{h_x,h_{x-2},h_{x-3}\}\subseteq N_G(h_{x-1})\)
 since 
\(h_x, h_{x-1}, h_{x-2}\)
 and 
\(h_{x-3}\)
 are four consecutive vertices on 
H
. Analogously, we can get 
\(d_G(h_{x-1},h_\ell )=1\)
 for 
\(\ell \in [x-2]\)
. It leads to 
\(\bar{r}=\bar{r}_1\)
, which is a contradiction. Hence, we may assume that 
\(\bar{r}\in \overline{R}_1\setminus \{h_2,h_{x-1}\}\)
, and without loss of generality let 
\(\bar{r}=h_{3j-1}\)
 with 
\(j\in [y-1]\setminus [1]\)
. By the above argument, the conclusion holds clearly if 
\(\overline{R}_1=\{h_2,h_{x-1}\}\)
. While 
\(|\overline{R}_1{\setminus }\{h_2,h_{x-1}\}|\ne 0\)
, then 
\(y\ge 3\)
 and it means that 
\(|R_1|\ge 4\)
. Since 
\(h_{3j-3}, h_{3j-2}, h_{3j-1}\)
 and 
\(h_{3j}\)
 are four consecutive vertices of 
H
, applying Claim 2, we have 
\(\{h_{3j-3},h_{3j-2},h_{3j}\}\subseteq N_G(h_{3j-1})\)
. Using the same argument, we can get 
\(\{h_3,h_4\}\subseteq N_G(h_{3j-1})\)
, that is, 
\(\bar{r}=\bar{r}_1\)
. It is a contradiction, again. Hence, we have 
\(r\not \in \overline{R}_1\)
. By symmetry, 
\(\bar{r}_1\not \in \overline{R}_2\)
 also holds.

                
Next assume that 
\(\bar{r}\in \overline{R}_2\)
 and 
\(\bar{r}_1\in \overline{R}_1\)
. If 
\(\bar{r}=h_n\)
 and 
\(\bar{r}_1=h_2\)
, then 
\(h_{n-1}\)
 and 
\(h_3\)
 are two 2-vertices of 
G
. Similar to the above, we obtain that 
\(d_G(h_n,h_s)=1\)
 and 
\(d_G(h_2,h_{s^{\prime }})=1\)
 for any 
\(s\in [n-1]{\setminus } [x-1]\)
 and 
\(s^{\prime }\in [x]{\setminus } \{2\}\)
. The fact that 
G
 is a MOP implies that either 
\(d_G(h_1,h_x)=1\)
 or 
\(d_G(h_n,h_2)=1\)
. But if 
\(d_G(h_1,h_x)=1\)
, then 
\(d_G(h_{n-1},h_3)=4=d_H(h_{n-1},h_3)\)
, which leads to 
\(h_1\in I_G(h_{n-1},h_3)\)
 contradicting with our assumption. Thus, we have 
\(d_G(h_n,h_2)=1\)
, then 
\(G\cong G_1\)
. For the remaining cases, applying repeatedly the similar method as above, we obtain that 
\(G\in {\mathcal {G}}\)
.


                
Case 3.
 
\(q=2\)
.

                
In this case, 
\(n=3k+2\)
 and 
\(\textrm{gp}(G)=2k+1\)
. We can get 
\(G\cong F_{n-1}\)
, this proof is similar to that of Case 1 and hence omitted here. 
\(\square \)
