Abstract
Let 
G
 be a connected graph and let 
X
 be a subset of 
E
(
G
). Then 
X
 is an edge general position set if as soon as 
P
 is a shortest (
u
, 
v
)-path of 
G
, we have 
\(|E(P)\cap X|\le 2\)
. The cardinality of a largest edge general position set of 
G
 is denoted by 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)\)
 and called the edge general position number of 
G
. In this paper, the edge general position number 4 is completely characterized for general graphs.

Introduction
Motivated by the famous and still open Dudeney’s no-three-in-line problem which is to find the maximum number of points placed in an 
\(n\times n\)
 grid so that no three points lie on a line [
3
], Manuel and Klavžar posed the general position problem in graph theory and introduced a concept of the general position sets for a graph [
10
]. Actually, Chandran S.V. and Parthasarathy in [
2
] studied the concept of the geodetic irredundant sets which is an equivalent concept to the general position sets earlier.
The problem of finding a largest vertex set in a graph such that its any three vertices do not lie on a common shortest path, that is, finding a largest general position set in a graph, is NP-hard [
10
]. In [
1
], the properties of the general position sets for a graph were characterized by Anand, Chandran S.V., Changat, Klavžar and Thomas. Afterwards, a lot of attention has been given to the general position sets in a graph. Determining the general position numbers of (classes of) graphs were also considered, see [
4
, 
11
, 
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
, 
21
, 
22
, 
24
]. The general position numbers of Cartesian products graphs were determined, see [
6
, 
9
, 
20
, 
23
]. The general position sets were generalized to the 
d
-position sets [
7
] and to the Steiner general position sets [
5
].
Similarly, an edge version of the graph theory general position problem was recently introduced by Manuel, Prabha and Klavžar in [
12
]. Let 
\(X\subseteq E(G)\)
. Then 
X
 is an 
edge general position set
 if as soon as 
P
 is a shortest (
u
, 
v
)-path of 
G
, we have 
\(|E(P)\cap X|\le 2\)
. The cardinality of a largest edge general position set of 
G
 is the 
edge general position number
, denoted by 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)\)
, of 
G
. An edge general position set 
X
 of cardinality 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)\)
 is called a 
\(gp_e\)
-
set
 of 
G
.
The formulae for the edge general position numbers of hypercubes, of trees, and of grid graphs were determined in [
12
]. In [
8
], Klavžar and Tan investigated the edge general position sets in partial cubes, where the union of two largest 
\(\Theta \)
-classes of a Fibonacci cube or Lucas cube is the maximal edge general position set. Manuel, Prabha and Klavžar introduced the definition of the edge 
k
-general position sets of a graph, and respectively determined the edge 
k
-general position numbers of torus networks, of hypercubes, and of Benes networks for different values of 
k
 in [
13
]. Tian, Klavžar and Tan determined the bounds for the edge general position number of block graphs in [
19
]. In the same paper, the graphs with some extremal edge general position numbers were characterized, especially for some small edge general position numbers. For example, for the graphs 
G
 with 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)\in \{2,3,4\}\)
, we have the following.

                
Proposition 1.1

                
[
19
, Proposition 2.3] If 
G
 is a graph having at least two edges, then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)=2\)
 if and only if 
G
 is a path.

              
                
Proposition 1.2

                
[
19
, Proposition 2.4] Let 
G
 be a graph. Then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)=3\)
 if and only if 
G
 is a triangle or a tree with three leaves.

              
                
Proposition 1.3

                
[
19
, Proposition 2.5] If 
G
 is a graph with 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)=4\)
, then the following holds. 

                  

                    
(i)

                    
                      
\(\Delta (G)\le 4\)
;

                    
                  

                  

                    
(ii)

                    
                      
If 
\(\Delta (G)=4\)
, then 
G
 is bipartite.

                    
                  

                

              
In this paper, we determine the graphs with edge general position number 4. The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. In Sect. 
2
, some necessary definitions, notation, and known results are given. Then a formula for the edge general position number of 
\(\Theta (\ell _1,\ldots ,\ell _k)\)
, 
\(k\ge 3\)
, is determined. In Sect. 
3
, we determine the graphs with edge general position number 4.
2 
Preliminaries
In this section, we state some concepts, notation, and known results needed. Unless stated otherwise, all graphs 
\(G=(V(G),E(G))\)
 in the paper are simple and connected. The 
order
 
n
(
G
) of 
G
 is equal to |
V
(
G
)| and the 
size
 
m
(
G
) is equal to |
E
(
G
)|. For a positive integer 
k
, we set 
\([k]=\{1,\ldots ,k\}\)
. For a vertex 
\(u\in V(G)\)
, the 
degree
 
\(d_G(u)\)
 of 
u
 is the number of vertices adjacent to 
u
 in 
G
. We denote by 
\(\Delta (G)\)
 and 
\(\delta (G)\)
 the maximum and minimum degrees of 
G
. Vertices of degree one are referred to as 
leaves
 and their incident edges are called 
pendant edges
 of 
G
. The number of pendant edges of 
G
 is denoted by 
\(\ell (G)\)
.
The 
distance
 
\(d_G(u,v)\)
 between vertices 
u
 and 
v
 of 
G
 is the number of edges of a shortest (
u
, 
v
)-path. The 
diameter
 
\(\textrm{diam}(G)\)
 of 
G
 is the maximum distance between all pairs of vertices of 
G
. If 
\(d_G(u,v)=\textrm{diam}(G)\)
, the shortest (
u
, 
v
)-path is called a 
diametral path
 of 
G
 (note that there may be multiple diametral paths in the graph), and the vertices 
u
, 
v
 are called a 
diametral pair
 of 
G
. Let 
\(uv_1\cdots v_k (=v)\)
 be a (
u
, 
v
)-path of 
G
, where 
\(k\ge 1\)
. This (
u
, 
v
)-path is an 
internal path
 of 
G
 if 
\(\min \{d_G(u),d_G(v)\}\ge 3\)
 and 
\(d_G(v_i) = 2\)
 for 
\(i=1,2,\ldots ,k-1\)
. A subgraph 
H
 of a graph 
G
 is 
isometric
 if 
\(d_H(x,y)=d_G(x,y)\)
 holds for every 
\(x,y\in V(H)\)
.
A vertex 
u
 of a connected graph 
G
 is a 
cut vertex
 if 
\(G-u\)
 is disconnected. A 
block
 of 
G
 is a maximal connected subgraph with no cut vertex in 
G
. If a connected graph 
G
 has (at least one) cycles, the 
circumference
 of 
G
 is the length of a longest cycle, denoted by 
c
(
G
). The 
cyclomatic number
 of 
G
, denoted by 
cyc
(
G
), is defined as 
\(m(G)-n(G)+1\)
. If 
\(cyc(G)=1\)
, the graph 
G
 is called a 
unicyclic graph
.
The graph 
G
 is a 
cactus graph
 if each block of 
G
 is either a cycle or an edge. A cactus is a 
chain cactus
 if each block has at most two cut vertices and each cut vertex is shared by exactly two blocks. As shown in Fig. 
1
, all cycles and all edges have at most two cut vertices, each of which is shared by exactly two blocks. Moreover, a chain cactus with no leaf is an 
even cycle chain
 if it satisfies the conditions: (1) all cycles are even, (2) the two vertices of degree at least 3 of any middle cycle are a diametral pair of the cycle. See the right graph of Fig. 
1
 once more.

A chain cactus graph and an even cycle chain graph
We recall some definitions and known results will be needed later. An 
isometric path edge cover
 of 
G
 is a collection 
\({\mathcal {P}}\)
 of isometric paths such that each edge of 
G
 lies on at least one of the paths from 
\({\mathcal {P}}\)
. The cardinality of a smallest isometric path edge cover is the 
isometric path edge number
 of 
G
 and denoted by 
\(\textrm{ip}_\textrm{e}(G)\)
, see [
12
].

                
[Style2 Style1]Lemma 2.1

                
[
12
, Lemma 2.2] If 
G
 is a connected graph, then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)\le 2 \textrm{ip}_\textrm{e}(G)\)
.

              
                
[Style2 Style1]Lemma 2.2

                
[
19
, Lemma 2.1] If 
G
 is a connected graph, then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G) = m(G)\)
 if and only if 
\(\textrm{diam}(G)\le 2\)
.

              
                
[Style2 Style1]Lemma 2.3

                
[
12
, Theorem 3.3] If 
T
 is a tree, then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(T)=\ell (T)\)
.

              
                
[Style2 Style1]Lemma 2.4

                
[
12
, Page 3] For 
\(n\ge 3\)
, 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(C_n) = n\)
 if 
\(n\in \{3,5\}\)
, and 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(C_n) = 4\)
, otherwise.

              
From Lemmas 
2.2
 and 
2.3
, if 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G) = 4\)
, then 
G
 does not contain a subgraph 
H
 with 
\(\textrm{diam}(H)=2\)
 and 
\(m(H)\ge 5\)
, nor does it satisfy 
\(\ell (G)\ge 5\)
. We now present the following results, which will play a crucial role in the proof of our main results.

                
[Style2 Style1]Lemma 2.5

                
If 
H
 is an isometric subgraph of 
G
, then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)\ge \textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(H)\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
Let 
X
 be an arbitrary 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}\)
-set of 
H
. For each shortest path 
P
 in 
H
, we have 
\(|X\cap E(P)|\le 2\)
. Since 
H
 is isometric, the path 
P
 is also a shortest path in 
G
. Now, suppose that there exists a shortest path 
\(P'\)
 in 
G
 such that 
\(|X\cap E(P')|\ge 3\)
. Then 
\(P'\)
 contains a shortest path 
P
 of 
H
. It follows that 
\(X\cap E(P') = X\cap E(P)\)
, implying 
\(|X\cap E(P)|\ge 3\)
. This contradicts our assumption that 
X
 is a 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}\)
-set of 
H
. Therefore, 
X
 is an edge general position set of 
G
, and we are done. 
\(\square \)

              
For positive integers 
\(k\ge 2\)
 and 
\(1\le \ell _1\le \ell _2\le \cdots \le \ell _k\)
, the 
theta graph
 
\(\Theta (\ell _1,\ell _2,\ldots ,\ell _k)\)
 is the graph consisting of two vertices 
a
 and 
b
 which are joined by 
k
 internally disjoint paths of respective lengths 
\(\ell _1, \ell _2, \ldots , \ell _k\)
, where 
\(\ell _2\ge 2\)
. See the left graph of Fig. 
2
, where 
\(\Theta (3,4,5)\)
 is drawn. In particular, if 
\(\ell _1=\cdots =\ell _k=\ell \)
 and 
\(\ell _1\ge 2\)
, the theta graph 
\(\Theta (\ell _1,\ell _2,\ldots ,\ell _k)\)
 is denoted by 
\(\Theta (k;\ell )\)
. See the right graph of Fig. 
2
, where 
\(k=3\)
 and 
\(\ell = 4\)
.
Fig. 2

Theta graphs 
\(\Theta (3,4,5)\)
 and 
\(\Theta (3;4)\)

                
[Style2 Style1]Lemma 2.6

                
Let 
\(\Theta =\Theta (\ell _1,\ell _2,\ldots ,\ell _k)\)
 and let 
\({\mathcal {L}}=\{\ell _i:\ \ell _i\ \mathrm{is\ odd},~i\in [k]\}\)
. If 
\(k\ge 3\)
 and 
\(\ell _2\ge 2\)
, then the following holds. 

                  

                    
(i)

                    
                      
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=2k-2\)
 if 
\(\ell _1 = 1\)
 and 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\ge 4\)
;

                    
                  

                  

                    
(ii)

                    
                      
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=2k-1\)
 if 
\(\ell _1 = 1\)
, 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\le 3\)
 and 
\(c(\Theta )\ne 5\)
;

                    
                  

                  

                    
(iii)

                    
                      
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=2k\)
 if 
\(\ell _2 \ge 2\)
 and 
\(c(\Theta )\ne 5\)
;

                    
                  

                  

                    
(iv)

                    
                      
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=2k+1\)
 if 
\(\ell _i = 2\)
 and 
\(\ell _k = 3\)
 for every 
\(i\in [k-1]\)
.

                    
                  

                

              
                
Proof

                
Let 
\(P_i: (a=)v^i_{1}v^i_{2}\cdots v^i_{\ell _i+1}(=b)\)
 be an internal path of 
\(\Theta \)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\)
. Let 
R
 be a 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}\)
-set of 
\(\Theta \)
. We begin with the following fact, which will be needed later. 
\(\square \)

              
                
Fact

                
For an arbitrary cycle 
C
, if 
C
 is even, there exists a diametral pair 
u
, 
v
 of 
C
 such that 
C
 is covered by two shortest (
u
, 
v
)-paths of 
C
. Otherwise, there exist two diametral pairs 
u
, 
v
 and 
\(u,v'\)
 in 
C
, where 
v
 is adjacent to 
\(v'\)
, such that 
C
 is covered by shortest (
u
, 
v
)-path, shortest 
\((u,v')\)
-path and the edge 
\(vv'\)
.

                
To prove the upper bound, suppose that 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )\ge 2k+2\)
. By Fact, each cycle of 
\(\Theta \)
 which consists of any two internal paths cannot contain 6 edges from 
R
. Hence, there exists at most one internal path containing three edges of 
R
, and we have 
\(|E(P_i)\cap R|\le 3\)
 for exactly one 
\(i\in [k]\)
. If not, assume that 
\(4\le |E(P_i)\cap R|\le 5\)
 for some 
\(i\in [k]\)
. For any internal path 
\(P_j\)
 of 
\(\Theta \)
, the cycle consisting of 
\(P_i\)
 and 
\(P_j\)
 would contain at most 5 edges of 
R
. It follows that 
\(|E(P_j)\cap R|\le 1\)
 for 
\(j\in [k]\setminus \{i\}\)
. Then
$$\begin{aligned} |R|\le 5+(k-1)=k+4<2k+2 \end{aligned}$$
as 
\(k\ge 3\)
. This is a contradiction. Furthermore, if 
\(|E(P_i)\cap R|=3\)
 for some 
\(i\in [k]\)
, we have 
\(|E(P_j)\cap R|\le 2\)
 for every 
\(j\in [k]\setminus \{i\}\)
. Hence
$$\begin{aligned} |R|\le 3+2(k-1)=2k+1<2k+2, \end{aligned}$$
which contradicts our assumption. Thus we have 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=|R|\le 2k+1\)
.

                
Next, we give a claim that is useful to the rest of the proof.

              
                
Claim 1

                
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )\ge 2k\)
 if 
\(\ell _1\ge 2\)
.

                
Setting 
\(t_i=\lceil \frac{\ell _i}{2}\rceil \)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\)
. Let 
\(R_1\)
 be an edge set of 
\(\Theta \)
 if it satisfies the following conditions: 

                  

                    
(1)

                    
                      
for every 
\(\ell _i\in {\mathcal {L}}\)
, 
\(R_1\cap E(P_i)=\{v^i_{t_i-1}v^i_{t_i}, v^i_{t_i+1}v^i_{t_i+2}\}\)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\)
;

                    
                  

                  

                    
(2)

                    
                      
for every even 
\(\ell _i\)
, 
\(R_1\cap E(P_i)=\{v^i_{t_i}v^i_{t_i+1}, v^i_{t_i+1}v^i_{t_i+2}\)
}, where 
\(i\in [k]\)
.

                    
                  

                
Then 
\(|R_1|=2k\)
. We prove that 
\(R_1\)
 is an edge general position set. Consider any two vertices 
u
 and 
v
 from 
\(\Theta \)
. If 
\(u,v\in V(P_j)\)
, where 
\(j\in [k]\)
, by the selection of 
\(R_1\)
, there is nothing to prove. Assume, without loss of generality, that 
\(u\in V(P_j)\)
 and 
\(v\in V(P_{j'})\)
, where 
\(\ell _j\in {\mathcal {L}}\)
, 
\(\ell _{j'}\)
 is even, and 
\(j'\in [k]\setminus \{j\}\)
. These two shortest paths 
\(P_j\)
 and 
\(P_{j'}\)
 form an even cycle 
C
 in 
\(\Theta \)
. It follows that the vertices 
\(v^j_{t_j}\)
 and 
\(v^{j'}_{t_{j'}+1}\)
 are a diametral pair of 
C
. Hence 
\(d_{\Theta }(v^j_{t_j}, v^{j'}_{t_{j'}+1})=\lfloor \frac{\ell _j+\ell _{j'}}{2}\rfloor \)
, then the shortest (
u
, 
v
)-path of 
C
 does not contain three edges from 
\(R_1\)
. By the similar way, we can get the same result for the other cases. Therefore, 
\(R_1\)
 is an edge general position set of 
\(\Theta \)
, we have 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )\ge |R_1|=2k\)
.

              
To prove the main result, we consider four cases.
Case 1.
 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=2k-2\)
 if 
\(\ell _1 = 1\)
 and 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\ge 4\)
.
Set 
\(t_i=\lceil \frac{\ell _i}{2}\rceil \)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
. Let 
\(R_2\)
 be an edge set of 
\(\Theta \)
 if it satisfies the following conditions: 

                

                  
(1)

                  
                    
for every 
\(\ell _i\in {\mathcal {L}}\)
, 
\(R_2\cap E(P_i)=\{v^i_{t_i-1}v^i_{t_i}, v^i_{t_i+1}v^i_{t_i+2}\}\)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
;

                  
                

                

                  
(2)

                  
                    
for every even 
\(\ell _i\)
, 
\(R_2\cap E(P_i)=\{v^i_{t_i}v^i_{t_i+1}, v^i_{t_i+1}v^i_{t_i+2}\)
}, where 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
.

                  
                

              
Note that 
\(|R_2|=2k-2\)
 and 
\(R_2\cap E(P_1)=\emptyset \)
. Using similar arguments as in Claim 
1
, we conclude that 
\(R_2\)
 is an edge general position set of 
\(\Theta \)
. Therefore, 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )\ge |R_2|=2k-2\)
.
Note that 
\(c(\Theta )\ne 5\)
 as 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\ge 4\)
. By Fact, the cycle from 
\(\Theta \)
 consisting of any two internal paths cannot contain 5 edges from the 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}\)
-set 
R
, we have 
\(|E(P_i)\cap R|\le 2\)
 for 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
. If 
\(|E(P_i)\cap R|=4\)
 for some 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
, it follows that 
\(|E(P_j)\cap R|=0\)
 for every 
\(j\in [k]\setminus \{i\}\)
. In this case, we have 
\(|R|\le 4\)
. If 
\(|E(P_i)\cap R|= 3\)
 for some 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
, then 
\(|E(P_j)\cap R|\le 1\)
 for every 
\(j\in [k]\setminus \{i\}\)
. Hence
$$\begin{aligned} |R|\le 3+(k-2)+1=k+2. \end{aligned}$$
And if 
\(|E(P_i)\cap R|=2\)
 for some 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
, then 
\(|E(P_j)\cap R|\le 2\)
, where 
\(j\in [k]\setminus \{1,i\}\)
. Furthermore, if 
\(v^1_1v^1_2\in R\)
, since 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\ge 4\)
, there exists a shortest path in 
\(\Theta \)
 containing three edges of 
R
, it is impossible. So 
\(v^1_1v^1_2\notin R\)
. We have
$$\begin{aligned} |R|\le 2+2(k-2)=2k-2. \end{aligned}$$
Note that 
\(2k-2\ge k+2>4\)
 as 
\(k\ge 4\)
. Hence 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=|R|\le 2k-2\)
.
Case 2.
 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(\Theta )=2k-1\)
 if 
\(\ell _1 = 1\)
, 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\le 3\)
 and 
\(c(\Theta )\ne 5\)
.
Set 
\(t_i=\lceil \frac{\ell _i}{2}\rceil \)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
. Let 
\(R_3\)
 be an edge set of 
\(\Theta \)
 if it satisfies the following conditions: 

                

                  
(1)

                  
                    
for 
\(\ell _1=1\)
, 
\(R_3\cap E(P_1)=\{v^1_{1}v^1_{2}\}\)
;

                  
                

                

                  
(2)

                  
                    
for 
\(\ell _i\in {\mathcal {L}}\)
, if 
\(|{\mathcal {L}}|\ne 3\)
, then 
\(R_3\cap E(P_i)=\{v^i_{t_i-1}v^i_{t_i}, v^i_{t_i+1}v^i_{t_i+2}\}\)
, where 
\(i\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
;

                  
                

                

                  
(3)

                  
                    
for 
\(i\ne i'\)
, if 
\({\mathcal {L}}=\{1,\ell _i,\ell _{i'}\}\)
, then 
\(R_3\cap E(P_i)=\{v^i_{1}v^i_{2},v^i_{t_i}v^i_{t_i+1}, v^{i}_{t_i+1}v^{i}_{t_i+2}\}\)
 and 
\(R_3\cap E(P_{i'})=\{v^{i'}_{t_{i'}}v^{i'}_{t_{i'}+1}\}\)
, where 
\(i,i'\in [k]\setminus \{1\}\)
;

                  
                

                

                  
(4)

                  
                    
for 
\(\ell _i\)

\(C_s\)
, where 
\(z,z'\in X\)
. By a method similar to Case 3, it is impossible. Hence 
\(\ell _{x}(U) = \ell _{x'}(U) = \ell _{x''}(U) = 1\)
.
Suppose that all vertices of 
X
 can lie on a common diametral path of the odd cycle 
\(C_s\)
. We assume, without loss of generality, that 
\(x'\in V(P_{xx''})\)
, where 
\(P_{xx''}\)
 is the shortest path between 
x
 and 
\(x''\)
. Let 
\(e_1\)
 (resp. 
\(e_2\)
) be an edge incident on 
x
 (resp. 
\(x''\)
) that does not lie on the shortest path 
\(P_{xx''}\)
 and 
\(X_j\)
 be the set of leaves such that each one connects 
\(C_s\)
 only through the vertex 
j
 of 
X
 in 
U
. It is straightforward to check that 
\(X_x\cup X_{x'}\cup X_{x''}\cup \{e_1,e_2\}\)
 is an edge general position set of 
U
. Similarly, we get the same result for all vertices of 
X
 can lie on a common diametral path of the even cycle 
\(C_s\)
 and no two vertices of 
X
 are a diametral pair of 
\(C_s\)
. Thus, 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(U)\ge 5\)
 contradicts our assumption. We can conclude that 
\(U\in {\mathcal {U}}_3\)
.
Case 5.
 
\(|X|=4\)
.
Let 
\(X=\{x,x',y,y'\}\)
. By a method similar to Case 4, we can show that 
\(\ell _{z}(U)=1\)
 holds for 
\(z\in X\)
. We claim that all vertices of 
X
 form two diametral pairs of 
\(C_s\)
, where 
s
 is even.
Suppose that 
s
 is odd. There exist three vertices of 
X
 that lie on a common diametral path of the odd cycle 
\(C_s\)
, however, this leads to a contradiction, as established in Case 3. Therefore, 
s
 must be even. Consequently, we can conclude that 
x
, 
\(x'\)
, 
y
, and 
\(y'\)
 form two diametral pairs of this even cycle 
\(C_s\)
, implying 
\(U\in {\mathcal {U}}_4\)
. 
\(\square \)
Combining Lemmas 
3.2
 and 
3.3
, we have:

                
Theorem 3.4

                
Let 
U
 be a unicyclic graph. Then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(U)=4\)
 if and only if 
\(U\in {\mathcal {U}}\)
.

              
Finally, we consider the graphs 
B
 with 
\(cyc(B)\ge 2\)
. Set 
\({\mathcal {B}}=\cup _{i=0}^{2}{\mathcal {B}}_i\)
, where 
\({\mathcal {B}}_i\)
 is defined as follows:

                

                  
\({\mathcal {B}}_0\)
 is the set of all even cycle chain graphs having at least two cycles;

                

                

                  
\({\mathcal {B}}_1\)
 is the set of all graphs which are obtained from an even cycle chain graph having at least three cycles by deleting a vertex of degree 2 (or an edge) from an end cycle;

                

                

                  
\({\mathcal {B}}_2\)
 is the set of all graphs which are obtained from an even cycle chain graph having at least four cycles by deleting a vertex of degree 2 (or an edge) from each of its end cycles.

                

              

                
Theorem 3.5

                
Let 
B
 be a graph with at least two cycles. Then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B) = 4\)
 if and only if 
\(B\in {\mathcal {B}}\)
.

              
                
Proof

                
Assume that 
\(B\in {\mathcal {B}}\)
. Since all blocks of 
B
 are isometric subgraphs and 
\(cyc(B)\ge 2\)
, it follows that 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B)\ge 4\)
. It is straightforward to check that there exist two diametral paths 
P
 and 
\(P'\)
 such that 
\(\{P,P'\}\)
 is an isometric path edge cover of 
B
. By Lemma 
2.1
, we have 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B)\le 2 \textrm{ip}_\textrm{e}(B) = 4\)
, and thus 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B)=4\)
.

                
To prove the converse, assume that 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B) = 4\)
. Under this assumption, we obtain the following claim. 
\(\square \)

              
                
Claim 1

                
All cycles of 
B
 are even.

              
Suppose the contrary that there exists an odd cycle 
C
 induced by the vertices 
\(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots , u_{i},u_{1}\)
 of 
B
, where 
\(i\ge 3\)
 is odd. Since 
\(cyc(B)\ge 2\)
, there exists a cycle 
\(C'\)
 on the vertices 
\(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots v_{i'},v_{1}\)
, where 
\(i'\ge 3\)
. By Lemma 
2.7
, the cycles 
C
 and 
\(C'\)
 are edge-disjoint. Hence there exists exactly one path connecting a vertex 
u
 of 
C
 and a vertex 
v
 of 
\(C'\)
. Assume, without loss of generality, that 
\(u=v_1\)
 and 
\(v=v_1'\)
. Set 
\(Y = \{u_1u_2,\ u_1u_i,u_{\lceil \frac{i}{2}\rceil }u_{\lceil \frac{i}{2}\rceil +1},\ v_1v_2,\ v_1v_{i'}\}\)
. It is straightforward to verify that the set 
Y
 is an edge general position set of 
B
 with cardinality 5, thus 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B) \ge 5\)
. This contradiction implies that all cycles of 
B
 are even.
We proceed by considering two cases as follows.
Case 1.
 
\(\delta (B)=2\)
.
We are going to prove that all vertices of 
B
 lie on some diametral path of 
B
. Let 
u
 and 
v
 be a diametral pair of 
B
 and let 
\(P_{uv}\)
 denote the diametral path of 
B
. Since 
\(\delta (B) = 2\)
 and 
\(cyc(B) \ge 2\)
, it follows that 
u
 and 
v
 lie on two end even cycles of 
B
, respectively. Let 
\(u', u''\)
 (resp. 
\(v', v''\)
) be two neighbors of 
u
 (resp. 
v
) in 
B
. Suppose that there exists (at least) a vertex 
z
 of 
B
 which does not lie on 
\(P_{uv}\)
. Let 
\(z'\)
 be a neighbor of 
z
 in 
B
 and set 
\(Y' = \{uu', uu'', vv', vv'', zz'\}\)
. Since 
\(P_{uv}\)
 is a diametral path and 
\(z\notin V(P_{uv})\)
, the edge 
\(zz'\)
 cannot lie on 
\(P_{uv}\)
. This implies that 
\(Y'\)
 is an edge general position set of 
B
, and consequently 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B) \ge 5\)
 contradicts our assumption. Hence all vertices of 
B
 lie on 
\(P_{uv}\)
 and 
\(B\in {\mathcal {B}}_0\)
.
Case 2.
 
\(\delta (B)=1\)
.
Since the set consisting of all leaves of 
B
 is an edge general position set of 
B
 and 
\(\delta (B)=1\)
, it follows that 
\(1\le \ell (B)\le 4\)
. Since 
B
 has (at least) two cycles, by Claim 
1
 and Lemma 
2.7
, all cycles of 
B
 are even and edge-disjoint. We next claim that two vertices of degree at least 3 from a cycle 
C
 form a diametral pair of 
C
.
Suppose the contrary that there exists a cycle 
C
 such that 
\(x, y\in V(C)\)
, both of degree at least 3, but do not form its diametral pair. Let 
\(x'\)
 and 
\(x''\)
 be two neighbors of 
x
 in 
C
, and let 
\(y'\)
 be the neighbor of 
y
 in 
C
 such that 
\(x', y'\)
 do not lie on the shortest (
x
, 
y
)-path in 
B
. Since 
\(cyc(B) \ge 2\)
, there exists exactly one path connecting a vertex 
u
 of 
C
 and a vertex 
w
 of 
\(C'\)
. Let 
\(w'\)
 and 
\(w''\)
 be two neighbors of 
w
 in 
\(C'\)
. Set 
\(R = \{xx',xx'',yy',ww',ww''\}\)
. If 
\(d_B(x,w) > d_B(y,w)\)
, since 
\(y'\)
 does not lie on the shortest (
x
, 
y
)-path, it follows that the shortest 
\((x,w')\)
-path only contains the edges 
\(xx''\)
 and one of the edges 
\(ww'\)
 and 
\(ww''\)
. Hence 
R
 is an edge general position set of 
B
. Similarly, 
R
 is also an edge general position set of 
B
 when 
\(d_B(x,w) < d_B(y,w)\)
. We thus have 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B)\ge 5\)
, which contradicts 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B) = 4\)
.
Let 
u
 be a vertex from one cycle of 
B
 with a degree at least 3, and let 
Z
 be the set of leaves of 
B
 such that 
u
 does not lie on any shortest 
\((v,v')\)
-path of 
B
, where 
\(v,v'\in Z\)
. Then 
\(|Z|\le 2\)
. If this is not the case, then 
u
 lies on an even cycle. Let 
\(u'\)
 and 
\(u''\)
 be two neighbors of 
u
 from the same cycle, and let 
\(Z'\)
 be the set of pendant edges such that each one is incident to a leaf from 
Z
 of 
B
. It is easily checked that 
\(Z'\cup \{uu,uu''\}\)
 is an edge general position set of 
B
, hence 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B)\ge 5\)
. This contradicts 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(B) = 4\)
. Therefore, 
\(B\in {\mathcal {B}}_1\cup {\mathcal {B}}_2\)
. 
\(\square \)
Combining Theorems 
3.1
, 
3.4
, and 
3.5
, we identify the graphs with edge general position number of 4 as follows:

                
Theorem 3.6

                
Let 
G
 be a graph of order at least 4. Then 
\(\textrm{gp}_\textrm{e}(G)=4\)
 if and only if 
\(G\in {\mathcal {T}}\cup {\mathcal {U}}\cup {\mathcal {B}}\)
.
