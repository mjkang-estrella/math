                                                                                      Abstract
                                                        In this paper, we study two graph convexity parameters: iteration time
                                                    and general position number. The iteration time was defined in 1981 in the
                                                    geodesic convexity, but its computational complexity was so far open. The
                                                    general position number was defined in the geodesic convexity and proved NP-
                                                    hard in 2018. We extend these parameters to any graph convexity and prove
                                                    that the iteration number is NP-hard in the P3 convexity. We use this result
                                                    to prove that the iteration time is also NP-hard in the geodesic convexity even
                                                    in graphs with diameter two, a long standing open question. These results
                                                    are also important since they are the last two missing NP-hardness results
                                                    regarding the ten most studied graph convexity parameters in the geodesic
                                                    and P3 convexities. We also prove that the general position number of the
                                                    monophonic convexity is W [1]-hard (parameterized by the size of the solution)
                                                    and n1−ε -inapproximable in polynomial time for any ε > 0 unless P = NP,
                                                    even in graphs with diameter two. Finally, we also obtain FPT results on the
                                                    general position number in the P3 convexity and we prove that it is W [1]-hard
                                                    (parameterized by the size of the solution).

                                         Keywords: graph convexity, general position number, iteration time.

1       Introduction
Convexity is a classical topic, studied in many diﬀerent branches of mathematics.
A rich source is the book “Theory of Convex Structures” by van de Vel [61]. The
study of convexities applied to graphs has started recently, about 50 years ago.
Accordingly to Duchet [24], the ﬁrst paper on general graphs, published in english, is
the 1981 paper “Convexity in graphs” from Frank Harary and Juhani Nieminem [35],
where it was introduced the iteration time of a graph in the geodesic convexity.
Other important graph convexity parameter is the general position number, which is
related to the 1917 Dudeney’s No-three-in-line problem [26]. The explicit deﬁnition
of it to the graph geodesic convexity was done by Manuel and Klavžar [47].
    To the best of our knowledge, these parameters were deﬁned and studied only
in the geodesic convexity. Our ﬁrst contribution in this paper is the deﬁnition of
them to any graph convexity. We also prove computational complexity results on
these parameters in three important graph convexities (geodesic, monophonic and
P3 ), including the NP-hardness of the geodesic iteration number, a long standing
open question. In order to prove this, we also had to prove the NP-hardness of the
P3 iteration number. For this, we need some terminology.
    A convexity C [61] on a ﬁnite set V 6= ∅ is a family of subsets of V such that
∅, V ∈ C and C is closed under intersections. That is, S1 , S2 ∈ C implies S1 ∩ S2 ∈ C.
A member of C is said to be a C-convex set. Given S ⊆ V , the C-convex hull of S is
the smallest C-convex set convC (S) containing S. It is easy to see that convC (·) is a
closure operator, that is, for every S, S ′ ⊆ V :

(a) S ⊆ convC (S) (extensivity),

(b) S ⊆ S ′ ⇒ convC (S) ⊆ convC (S ′ ) (monotonicity),

(c) convC (∅) = ∅ (normalization1 ) and

(d) convC (convC (S)) = convC (S) (idempotence).

    We say that I : 2V → 2V is an interval function on V if, for every S, S ′ ⊆ V , (a)
S ⊆ I(S) [extensivity], (b) S ⊆ S ′ ⇒ I(S) ⊆ I(S ′ ) [monotonicity] and (c) I(∅) = ∅
[normalization]. It is not diﬃcult to prove that every interval function induces a
unique convexity, containing each set S ⊆ V such that I(S) = S. Moreover, every
convexity is induced by an interval function. In the rest of the paper, we assume that
every convexity C on V is deﬁned by an explicitly given interval function IC (·) on
V . It is well known that the convex hull of a set S in a convexity C can be obtained

by exhaustively applying the corresponding interval function IC (·) until obtaining a

     Given a ﬁnite graph G, a graph convexity on G is simply a convexity C on
V (G) with a given interval function IC (·) on V (G). A standard way to deﬁne a
graph convexity C on a graph G is by ﬁxing a family P of paths of G and taking
the interval function IC (S) as the set with all vertices lying on some path of P
whose endpoints are in S. The most studied graph convexities are path convexities,
such as the geodesic convexity [29, 30, 35], the monophonic convexity [25, 37], the P3
convexity [11] and the P3∗ convexity [3], where P is, respectively, the family of all
geodesics (shortest paths) of the graph, of all induced paths, of all paths of order
three and of all induced paths of order three.
     We use the subscripts g, m, p3 and p3* to refer to the the geodesic convexity,
the monophonic convexity, the P3 convexity and the P3∗ convexity, respectively. In
the following, when we say that some numerical parameter is NP-hard, we mean
that the determination of its value is an NP-hard problem.
     From these deﬁnitions, we can deﬁne the two parameters investigated in this
paper for any graph convexity. The iteration time of a set S ⊆ V (G) in a graph
convexity C, denoted by tiC (S), is the minimum k such that IkC (S) = convC (S), that
is, k applications of the interval function are necessary in order to obtain the convex
hull of S. Also let the iteration time of a graph G in a graph convexity C, denoted by
tiC (G), be the maximum value of tiC (S) among the subsets S of V (G). The iteration
time is one of the ﬁrst graph convexity parameters, introduced in 1981 by Harary
and Nieminem [35] in the geodesic convexity. In [54], bounds were obtained for the
geodesic iteration time. In 2016, Dourado et al. [20] obtained a polynomial time
algorithm to determine the geodesic iteration time on distance hereditary graphs,
which was improved in 2020 by Moscarini [51]. However, the computational com-
plexity of the iteration time was so far open for any graph convexity, including the
geodesic convexity.
     The general position number of a graph G in a graph convexity C, denoted by
gpC (G), is the size of a maximum subset of V (G) in general position, where we say
that a subset S ⊆ V (G) is in general position if z 6∈ IC ({x, y}) for every distinct
x, y, z ∈ S. In the P3 convexity, curiously any subset in general position induces a
subgraph with maximum degree 1 and then gpp3 (G) is equivalent to the dissociation
number diss(G) of the graph G, a parameter introduced in 1981 by Yannakakis [63],
who also proved NP-hardness even for bipartite graphs and planar graphs with
maximum degree four. In the P3∗ convexity, any subset in general position induces a
subgraph whose connected components are cliques and, then, gpp3* (G) is equivalent
to the IUC number (independent union of cliques) of the graph G, a parameter
introduced in 2020 by Ertem et al. [28] and proved NP-hard even in planar graphs.
     The geodesic general position number is a generalization of the No-three-in-

                                          3
line problem in the n × n grid from discrete geometry, which can be traced to the
famous Dudeney’s “Puzzle with Pawns” of his book “Amusements in Mathematics”
[26] from 1917. All the following results are for the geodesic convexity. In 1995,
Korner [44] investigated the general position number on hypercubes, while in [12] it
was considered for the ﬁrst time on general graphs. However, the formalization of
the problem as we know it today and the notation that is in use have been introduced
in [47, 48]. Also see [31, 45, 50, 56] for the related general position subset selection
problem in computational geometry. In 2018, it was proved that determining the
general position number is NP-hard [47]. In 2019, general position sets in graphs were
characterized [1] and, after this, several additional papers on the general position
problem were published, many of them with bounds on the maximum size of a
general position set and exact values in graph products [13, 40–43, 55, 58, 59].
     In this paper, we prove that the iteration time is NP-hard in the P3 convexity.
This result is the basis to prove that the iteration time is also NP-hard in the geodesic
convexity even in graphs with diameter two. Surprisingly, even though this problem
has been deﬁned in 1981, its computational complexity had not been settled yet.
     In addition to these points, we consider these results even more important due
to the fact that they are the last two missing proofs of NP-hardness among the ten
main graph convexity parameters in the geodesic and P3 convexities. In order to
justify this, notice that the following nine graph convexity parameters are NP-hard
in the geodesic and P3 convexities: hull number and convexity number [3, 4, 21],
interval number [11, 34], Carathéodory number [5, 15], Radon number [2, 22], Helly
number [10, 19], general position number [47, 63], rank [38, 57] and percolation time
[2, 49].
     Regarding the monophonic convexity, we prove that the monophonic general
position number is W [1]-hard (parameterized by the size of the solution) and n1−ε -
inapproximable in polynomial time for any ε > 0 unless P = NP, even in graphs
with diameter two. Finally, we also prove that the general position number in the P3
convexity is W [1]-hard (parameterized by the size k of the solution) and we obtain
ﬁxed parameter tractable results regarding the neighborhood diversity, the vertex
convex number and the cliquewidth of the graph.


2     Preliminary results
Given a positive integer n, let [n] = {1, . . . , n}. Given a graph G and S ⊆ V (G), we
say that a vertex v has iteration time k > 0 starting from S if v ∈ ICk (S) \ ICk−1 (S),
and that the vertices of S have iteration time 0 starting from S.

Lemma 2.1. Let n ≥ 4 be an integer. In the complete graph Kn and the cycle Cn ,
we have:

                                           4
   • tip3 (Kn ) = 1 and tig (Kn ) = tim (Kn ) = 0,

   • tip3 (Cn ) = tig (Cn ) = tim (Cn ) = 1.

Proof. Let S be a proper subset of at least two vertices of Kn . In the P3 convexity,
Ip3 (S) contains all vertices of Kn , since every vertex in V (Kn ) \ S has two neighbors
in S. Thus tip3 (Kn ) = 1. In the monophonic convexity, Im (S) = convm (S) = S,
since no vertex is an induced path between two vertices of Kn . Thus tim (Kn ) = 0.
Consequently, in the geodesic convexity, tig (Kn ) = 0.
     Now let S be a proper subset of at least two non-adjacent vertices of Cn . In the P3
convexity, v ∈ Ip3 (S) if v ∈ S or its two neighbors are in S. Then Ip3 (S) = convp3 (S).
Therefore tip3 (Cn ) = 1. In the monophonic convexity, Im (S) contains all vertices
of Cn , since every vertex is in an induced path between two non-adjacent vertices
of Cn . Thus, tim (Cn ) = 1. Moreover, in the geodesic convexity, Ig (S) contains all
vertices if and only if S contains three vertices x, y, z such that the distance between
x and y is smaller than them sum of the distances between x and z, and between z
and y. In this case, tig (S) = tig ({x, y, z}) = 1. Otherwise, Ig (S) = Ig ({x, y}), where
x and y are the vertices of S with maximum distance. Therefore tig (Cn ) = 1.


Lemma 2.2. Let T be a tree with at least three vertices. Then tig (T ) = tim (T ) = 1.
Moreover tip3 (T ) = k ≥ 1 if and only if T has a path v1 , v2 , . . . , vk such that vi has
degree at least 3 for every 1 ≤ i < k and vk has degree at least two.

Proof. In a tree, Im (S) = Ig (S) = convm (S) = convg (S) for every S ⊆ V (T ). Thus,
since T has two non-adjacent vertices, tig (T ) = tim (T ) = 1.
    Now consider the P3 convexity on T . Let S ⊆ V (T ) with ti(S) = k ≥ 1. Then
S contains a path v1 , v2 , . . . , vk such that vi has iteration time i, starting from S,
for every i ∈ [k]. Clearly, for any 1 < i < k, vi must have at least one neighbor
other than vi−1 and vi+1 , and consequently the degree of vi is at least 3. Moreover,
v1 must have at least two neighbors other than v2 , and vn must have at least one
neighbor other than vk−1 . Therefore, vi has degree at least 3 for every 1 ≤ i < k
and vk has degree at least two.
    Finally suppose that T has a path v1 , v2 , . . . , vk such that vi has degree at least
3 for every 1 ≤ i < k and vk has degree at least two. Let S ⊆ V (T ) be such that
S ∩ {v1 , . . . , vk } = ∅ and S contains exactly two neighbors of v1 and exactly one
neighbor of vi for every 1 < i ≤ k. Notice that tip3 (S) = k, since vi has iteration
time i for every i ∈ [k]. Thus, tip3 (T ) ≥ k and we are done.

and P3 convexities in many papers. The lemma below is about this parameter in

the monophonic convexity in simple graphs, such as the wheel graphs Wn for n ≥ 4,

Lemma 2.3. Let n ≥ 4 be an integer. In the monophonic convexity, gpm (Cn ) =
gpm (Pn ) = 2 and gpm (Kn ) = n. Moreover, gpm (W4 ) = 4 and gpm (Wn ) = 3 for
n ≥ 5.

Proof. First consider the monophonic convexity. In the complete graph Kn , no
vertex is in an induced path between two vertices x and y, other than x and y.
Then V (Kn ) is in general position. Thus gpm (Kn ) = n. In the cycle Cn , every
vertex is an induced path between two non-adjacent vertices. Then gpm (Cn ) = 2.
In the path Pn , convm (S) = Im (S) = Im ({x, y}), where x and y are the vertices of S
with maximum distance in Pn . Then gpm (Pn ) = 2.
    In wheel graphs, we have that W4 = K4 and then gpm (W4 ) = 4. For n ≥ 5, every
vertex is in an induced path between two non-adjacent vertices of the main cycle of
Wn . With this, a maximum general position set must have the universal vertex and
two adjacent vertices of the main cycle and, then, gpm (Wn ) = 3 for n ≥ 5.


3     Iteration time is NP-hard in the P3 convexity
We ﬁrst prove a lemma that will be useful in the main theorem of this section.

Lemma 3.1. Let G be a graph and k be a positive integer. If S ⊆ V (G) and
tip3 (S) ≥ k, then the subgraph H of G induced by convp3 (S) contains a path v1 , . . . , vk
of vertices not in S, where v1 is adjacent to two vertices of S, the degree of vi in H
is at least 3 for every 1 ≤ i < k and the degree of vk in H is at least 2.

Proof. Let H be the subgraph of G induced by convp3 (S). Since tip3 (S) ≥ k, then,
starting from S, the vertices of S have iteration time 0 and, for every i ∈ [k], there
is a vertex vi with iteration time i adjacent to a vertex with iteration time i − 1.
Therefore, H has a path v1 , v2 , . . . , vk of vertices not in S such that vi has iteration
time i for every i ∈ [k], v1 is adjacent to two vertices of S and vi is adjacent to a
vertex outside this path whose iteration time is at most i − 1, and we are done.
    The following theorem contains the main result of this section, which is the basis
of the proof of the main theorem of the next section.

Theorem 3.2. Given a graph G and a positive integer k, deciding whether the
P3 iteration time tip3 (G) is at least k is an NP-complete problem even in bipartite
graphs.

4     Iteration time is NP-hard in the geodesic con-
      vexity
The following lemma shows an important reduction from the P3 convexity to the
geodesic convexity, regarding the iteration time and the general position number.

Lemma 4.1. Let G be a graph and let Gu be obtained from G by adding a universal
vertex u. If G is a triangle free graph with at least 3 vertices, then tig (Gu ) =
max{tip3 (G), 1} and gpg (Gu ) = max{gpp3 (G), ω(G) + 1}.

Proof. Since Gu has the universal vertex u, then the diameter of Gu is two. Note
that every shortest path in Gu between two non-adjacent vertices of G is a P3 , which
is induced because G is triangle free.
    If G has no P3 , then tip3 (G) = 0 and tig (Gu ) = 1, where u is the vertex of Gu
with iteration time 1 in the geodesic convexity. So assume that G has an induced
P3 and consequently tip3 (G) ≥ 1 and tig (Gu ) ≥ 1. We prove that tig (Gu ) = tip3 (G).
Let S ⊆ V (G) with iteration time t ≥ 1 in the P3 convexity. Clearly S contains two
non-adjacent vertices and then u has iteration time 1 in the geodesic convexity on
Gu starting from S. Since u is universal, no vertex of G is in a shortest path between
u and other vertex. That is, u does not help to generate other vertices. With this,
we conclude that the iteration time of every vertex of G in the P3 convexity on G
starting from S is equal to the iteration time of the same vertex of G in the geodesic
convexity on Gu starting from S. Thus the iteration time of S in the geodesic
convexity on Gu is equal to the iteration time of S in the P3 convexity on G. From
the other hand, as mentioned above, the iteration time of S in the P3 convexity on
G is equal to the iteration time of S ∪ {u} in the geodesic convexity on Gu , and we
are done.
    Now let us deal with the general position number. Let S be a P3 general position
set of G. Clearly S is also a geodesic general position set of Gu . If S contains two
non-adjacent vertices, then S ∪ {u} is not a geodesic general position set of Gu ;
otherwise, S ∪ {u} is a clique and a geodesic general position set of Gu . From the
other hand, let S ′ be a geodesic general position set of Gu . If S ′ contains u, then
S ′ must be a clique and then S ′ \ {u} is a clique of G. Otherwise, S is also a P3
general position set of G, and we are done.


    We now prove the long-standing open question of NP-hardness of the geodesic

                                          8
iteration time. Notice that the proof strongly depends on the NP-hardness of the
P3 iteration time (Theorem 3.2).
Theorem 4.2. Given a graph G and an integer k, deciding whether the geodesic
iteration time tig (G) is at least k is an NP-complete problem even in graphs with
diameter two.
Proof. Let G be a graph. Consider the graph Gu obtained from the addition of
a universal vertex u in G. From the reduction of Lemma 4.1 from the iteration
time in the P3 convexity, we have that tig (Gu ) = max{tip3 (G), 1}. If the diameter
of G is 0, then tip3 (G) = 0. If the diameter of G is 1 and each vertex has degree
at most 1, then tip3 (G) = 0. If the diameter of G is 1 and there is a vertex with
degree at least two, then tip3 (G) = 1. Since the iteration time in the P3 convexity is
NP-hard from Theorem 3.2, we may assume that G has diameter at least two and
then tip3 (G) ≥ 1. Therefore tig (Gu ) = tip3 (G) and consequently the iteration time
in the geodesic convexity is also NP-hard.


5     General position number is NP-hard in the mono-
      phonic convexity
As mentioned previously, there are many recent papers regarding the general position
number in the geodesic convexity. Moreover, the general position number in the P3
convexity is equivalent to the dissociation number, which has also been extensively
studied in the literature. In this section, we obtain the ﬁrst complexity results on the
general position number in other well investigated graph convexity, the monophonic
convexity, which was introduced by Jamison [37] in 1982. See also the papers [25,30]
from Farber and Jamison in 1986 and Duchet in 1988 with some of the ﬁrst results
on the monophonic convexity.
   We ﬁrst prove in Theorem 5.1 that deciding if a set with 3 vertices is in general
position in the monophonic convexity is a coNP-complete problem. Later we prove
in Theorems 5.2 and 5.3 that deciding if there is a set with k vertices in general
position in the monophonic convexity is W[1]-hard when parameterized by the size
k of the solution and that the general position number gpm (G) of the monophonic
convexity is highly inapproximable.
Theorem 5.1. Given a graph G and a set S ⊆ V (G), determining whether S is in
general position in the monophonic convexity is a coNP-complete problem, even if
|S| = 3.
Proof. A certiﬁcate that S is not in general position consists of three distinct vertices
x, y, z ∈ S and an induced path P from x to y passing through z. The hardness

                                           9
proof is a reduction from the following problem: given a graph H and three speciﬁed
vertices x, y, z ∈ V (H), decide whether there is an induced path from x to y in H
passing through z. This problem is proved to be NP-complete in [33] (Theorem 10).
Let G = H + (Ex ∪ Ey ), where Ex and Ey are sets of edges deﬁned as follows: Ex =
{ab | ab ∈/ E(H) and a, b ∈ NH (x)} and Ey = {ab | ab ∈        / E(H) and a, b ∈ NH (y)}.
In other words, x and y are simplicial vertices of G. In addition, deﬁne S = {x, y, z}.
We prove that there is an induced path from x to y in H passing through z if and
only if S is not in general position in G with respect to the monophonic convexity.
    Suppose ﬁrst that P = x, v1 , . . . , vk , y is an induced path in H passing through
z, with z = vj for some j ∈ {1, . . . , k}. Note that v1 is the only neighbor of x in
V (P ). Similarly, vk is the only neighbor of y in V (P ). Thus, E(P ) ∩ Ex = ∅ and
E(P )∩Ey = ∅, and this implies that P is also an induced path in G. But this means
that S is not in general position in G with respect to the monophonic convexity.
    Conversely, suppose that S is not in general position in G with respect to the
monophonic convexity. Thus, there is an induced path P in G between two distinct
vertices p, q of S such that the third vertex r of S, r ∈    / {p, q}, is an internal vertex
of P . Since x is a simplicial vertex of G, we have that r 6= x. Likewise, r 6= y. Thus,
r = z and P is an induced path from x to y in G passing through z. In addition,
observe that E(P ) ⊆ E(H). Thus, P is an induced path in H, and the theorem
follows.

Theorem 5.2. Given a graph G and an integer k > 0, deciding whether the general
position number gpm (G) in the monophonic convexity is at least k is NP-hard, even
in graphs with diameter two.

Proof. We obtain a polynomial reduction from the Clique problem, which has as
an instance a graph H and a positive integer ℓ and asks whether H has a clique
with size at least ℓ. Let v1 , . . . , vn be the vertices of H in which we may assume
that n ≥ 3 and H does not have isolated vertices. We build a graph G from H, by
adding for each vertex vi a new vertex ui adjacent to every vertex of H except vi .
Moreover, we include the new vertex u adjacent to every vertex u1, . . . , un . Notice
that every induced path of H is also an induced path in G. Let i, j, k ∈ [n] distinct.
    First notice that Im ({vi , ui }) = V (G), since the vertices u and uj belong to the
induced path vi − uj − u − ui and, if vj is adjacent to vi , then vj belongs to the
induced path vi − vj − ui , otherwise, vj belongs to the induced path vi − uk − vj − ui .
    Now suppose that vi and vj are non-adjacent. Thus Im ({ui , uj }) = V (G), since
the vertex u belongs to the induced path ui − u − uj , the vertex vk belongs to the
induced path ui − vk − uj and the vertices vi , vj and uk belong to the induced path
ui − vj − uk − vi − uj . Let us prove that Im ({vi , vj }) = V (G). If vk is adjacent to vi
and vj , then vk belongs to the induced path vi − vk − vj . If vk is adjacent to vi , but
not to vj , then vk belongs to the induced path vi − vk − ui − vj . If vk is adjacent

                                            10
to vj , but not to vi , then vk belongs to the induced path vi − uj − vk − vj . If vk is
non-adjacent to vi and vj , then vk belongs to the induced path vi − uj − vk − ui − vj .
Moreover, the vertex uk belongs to the induced path vi − uk − vj , and the vertices
u, ui e uj belong to the induced path vi − uj − u − ui − vj .
    From this, let S be a subset in general position on the monophonic convexity
with at least 3 vertices. Therefore, S must induce a clique in H and cannot have
two vertices vi and ui for the same i. Also, S cannot have two vertices ui and uj
such that vi and vj are non-adjacent. Moreover, S cannot have three vertices ui , uj
and vk for distinct i, j, k, since vk belongs to the induced path ui − vk − uj .
    With this, we conclude that S consists of (a) a clique of G (formed by a clique of
H and a vertex uk ), or (b) a clique of H and the vertex u, or (c) S ⊆ {u1 , . . . , un }
is such that {vi : ui ∈ S} induces a clique of H. Therefore, H has a clique of size k
if and only if G has a subset in general position on the monophonic convexity with
size ℓ = k + 1.

Corollary 5.3. Let G be a graph with diameter two and let k be a positive integer.
The problem of deciding whether G has a subset of size at least k in general position
on the monophonic convexity is W[1]-hard when parameterized by the size k of the
solution. Moreover, there is no polynomial time algorithm with approximation factor
n1−ε to compute the maximum size gpm (G) of a subset of G in general position on
the monophonic convexity, for any ε > 0, unless P = NP.

Proof. These results come directly from the reduction of the previous theorem, since
it is also an FPT reduction which also preserves approximation and from the facts
that Clique is W[1]-hard [23] and n1−ε -inapproximable for any ε > 0, unless P = NP
[64].


6     General position number in the P3 convexity
In this section, we focus on the general position number in the P3 convexity. Note
that a subset S ⊆ V (G) is in general position in the P3 convexity if and only if G[S]
is a graph of maximum degree 1. Thus, it can be seen as a natural generalization of
the notion of Independent Set, which induces a subgraph of maximum degree equal
to zero. As one may expect, this has been studied in the literature under diﬀerent
names. Probably the most referred one is as a dissociation set. The dissociation
number of G, denoted by diss(G), is the maximum cardinality of a dissociation set
of G, which is then equivalent to gpp3 (G). Another equivalent deﬁnition in the
literature is the notion of 1-dependent set. A k-dependent set is a subset S ⊆ V (G)
such that ∆(G[S]) ≤ k. The k-dependence number of a graph G is the cardinality
of a maximum k-dependent set of G. For a survey, see [14]. Another related notion


                                           11
is that of a 3-path cover. A subset S ⊆ V (G) is a k-path cover of G if the vertex set
of any path on at least k vertices of G is intersected by S. Note that S is in general
position in the P3 convexity if and only if V (G) \ S is a 3-path cover.

Related results. In the sequel, we present results with respect to the previous no-
tions, but we translate them to the context of the general position number in the P3
convexity. In [8, 62], it is proved that computing gpp3 (G) is NP-hard even in bipar-
tite graphs, but polynomial-time solvable in bipartite graphs with no induced “skew
star”. In [53], it is proved that the same holds for planar graphs with maximum de-
gree 4. In [9], polynomial-time algorithms for chordal graphs, weakly chordal graphs,
asteroidal triple-free graphs and interval-ﬁlament graphs are presented. Orlovich et
al. [52] proved that gpp3 (G) is NP-hard even in planar line graphs of a planar bipar-
tite graph with maximum degree 4. They also present polynomial-time algorithms
for restricted graph classes. Some other polynomial-time algorithms to particular
graph classes can be found in [46].
     In [60], it is presented an algorithm with running time O∗ (1.713k ) to decide
whether G has a 3-path cover of cardinality at most k, which is the same as to ask
whether gpp3 (G) ≥ n − k. Thus, note that the dual problem of the general position
number in the P3 convexity is FPT, parameterized by k. In [36], a 4/3-approximation
algorithm to the general position number in the P3 convexity is obtained. Such result
was further studied by [6], which also presented other upper and lower bounds for
gpp3 (G).
     In the following, we prove some results regarding the Parameterized Complexity
of the P3 general position number gp3 (G). First, none of the reductions cited above is
a parameterized one. We did not ﬁnd in the literature the study of the parameterized
complexity of determining gpp3 (G) ≥ k parameterized by k. We start proving the
W[1]-hardness of deciding whether gp3 (G) ≥ k, parameterized by k. Let us deﬁne
the problem we reduce to ours.
     In the Multicolored Independent Set problem, the instance is a graph
G and a positive integer k. Each vertex of G has a color in {1, . . . , k} and the
goal is to ﬁnd an independent set S of G with k vertices, one of each color. Such
independent set is called multicolored k-independent set. This problem is well-known
to be W[1]-hard when parameterized by k [18].
Theorem 6.1. Deciding whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k parameterized by k is W[1]-hard.
Proof. Let (G, k) be an instance of Multicolored Independent Set. We build
a graph G′ from G in linear time such that gpp3 (G′ ) ≥ 2k if and only if G has a
multicolored k-independent set.
   Let Si be the vertices of G with color i for every i ∈ {1, . . . , k}. To build G′ ﬁrst
convert each Si to a clique (often in the literature this is already a hypothesis in

                                           12
the given instance). Then, add k vertices ui , one for each i ∈ {1, . . . , k}, and make
ui adjacent to all vertices in Si . This ﬁnishes the construction of G′ . Clearly, this
construction can be done in linear time.
     Let us now prove that gpp3 (G′ ) ≥ 2k if and only if G has a multicolored k-
independent set.
     Suppose ﬁrst that S is a multicolored k-independent set of G. Let si be the
vertex in S ∩ Si . Deﬁne S ′ as S ∪ {ui | i ∈ {1, . . . , k}}. Note that, since S is a
multicolored k-independent set and the only neighbors of ui are the vertices in Si ,
G[S ′ ] is a graph whose edges are ui si for i ∈ {1, . . . , k}, and induce a matching.
Thus S ′ is a subset with 2k vertices in general position in the P3 convexity in G′
and thus gpp3 (G′ ) ≥ 2k.
     Let S ′ be a subset of V (G′ ) in general position in the P3 convexity with at least
2k elements. By deﬁnition, note that at most 2 vertices of S ′ may lie in a same clique
of G′ . Since V (G′ ) can be partitioned into k cliques Ci = Si ∪{ui } for i ∈ {1, . . . , k},
we deduce that |S ′ | = 2k and that |S ′ ∩ Ci | = 2 for each i ∈ {1, . . . , k}. In case
there are two vertices si , s′i ∈ S ′ ∩ Si such that si 6= ui and s′i 6= ui , note that
(S ′ \ {s′i }) ∪ {ui } is also in general position in the P3 convexity, because N(ui ) =
Si . Consequently, we may assume, w.l.o.g., that ui ∈ S ′ for every i ∈ {1, . . . , k}.
Therefore, S = S ′ \ {ui | i ∈ {1, . . . , k}} must be a multicolored k-independent set
of G, as any edge linking two of these vertices would induce a path on 4 vertices in
G[S ′ ], which is not possible as ∆(G[S ′ ]) ≤ 1.
   After proving Theorem 6.1, the natural question is answered by the following
proposition.

Proposition 6.2. Deciding whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k parameterized by k is in XP.

Proof. Note that gpp3 ≥ k if and only if there is a subset S ⊆ V (G) on exactly k
vertices in general position in the P3 convexity. Thus, one may verify all the nk
subsets and verify in polynomial time for each one whether it is in general position
in the P3 convexity.

Structural Parameters. Since we dealt with the parameterized complexity of
determining whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k parameterized only by k, let us now study other
parameters related to the structure of G.
    A graph G has neighborhood diversity at most d if V (G) can be partitioned into
d sets of twins [18]. In particular, note that each part must correspond to a clique
or an independent set. Moreover, for every pair of parts, either there are all edges
linking vertices from one part to the vertices in the other, or none. Denote by nd(G)
the minimum cardinality of such partition of G.


                                             13
Theorem 6.3. Deciding whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k has a kernel of size O(nd(G) · k)
when parameterized by the neighborhood diversity nd(G) of G plus k.

Proof. Assume k ≥ 3 as otherwise the problem can be solved in polynomial time.
One can ﬁrst obtain in polynomial time such partition of V (G) into nd(G) sets of
twins by just checking, for each pair of vertices, whether they are twins. Then, if
one part corresponds to an independent set of cardinality at least k, return “YES”,
as such independent set is a set in general position in the P3 convexity. While there
is a part corresponding to a clique that has at least three vertices, then one may
remove all but two vertices in this part as at most two of them can be part of any
optimal solution and they are twins. Thus, we obtain an equivalent instance with
at most nd(G)(k − 1) vertices.
    Recall that a vertex cover S in a graph G is a set of vertices such that each
edge in E(G) has at least one extremity in S. The cardinality of a minimum vertex
cover in G is denoted by vc(G). Determining whether vc(G) ≤ k is one of Karp’s
21 NP-complete problems [39], but admits a 2-approximation algorithm [18]. By
deﬁnition, it is also well-known that S is a vertex cover of G if and only if V (G) \ S
is an independent set of G.
    Note that if G satisﬁes vc(G) ≤ k, then nd(G) ≤ 2k + k. Indeed, if S is a
minimum vertex cover of G, one can partition the independent set V (G) \ S into
at most 2k subsets of (false) twins (the ones with the same neighborhood in S) and
complete the partition with the singletons of S. Thus, if one considers the problem
of deciding whether gp3 ≥ k parameterized by vc(G) + k, then Theorem 6.3 implies
an exponential kernel. We can easily improve such kernel.

Theorem 6.4. The problem of deciding whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k has a kernel of size
O(vc(G)+k) when parameterized by vc(G)+k, where vc(G) is the size of a minimum
vertex cover of G.

Proof. One can ﬁrst use the 2-approximation algorithm for Vertex Cover [18] to
obtain in polynomial time a vertex cover S ⊆ V (G) such that |S| ≤ 2 · vc(G). Then,
if |V (G) \ S| ≥ k, then return “YES”. Otherwise, |V (G)| ≤ 2 · vc(G) + k − 1.
    Finally, we obtain parameterized results from two algorithmic meta-theorems in
graphs with bounded local-treewidth and graphs with bounded cliquewidth. Given
a graph G, let GP-Decp3(k) be the problem of deciding whether G has a P3 general
position set of size k.
    The local-treewidth [27] of a graph G is the function ltwG : N → N which
associates with any r ∈ N the maximum treewidth of an r-neighborhood in G.
That is, ltwG (r) = maxv∈V (G) {tw(G[Nr (v)])}, where Nr (v) is the set of vertices at
distance at most r from v. We say that a graph class C has bounded local-treewidth

                                          14
if there is a function fC : N → N such that, for all G ∈ C and r ∈ N, ltwG (r) ≤ fC (r).
It is known that graphs with bounded genus or bounded max degree have bounded
local-treewidth [27]. In particular, a graph with max degree ∆ has ltwG (r) ≤ ∆r
and a planar graph has ltwG (r) ≤ 3r − 1 [7].
    In the following, we express the GP-Decp3 (k) decision problem in First Order
logic. We use lower case variables x, y, z, . . . (resp. upper case variables X, Y, Z, . . .)
to denote vertices (resp. subsets of vertices) of a graph. The atomic formulas are
x = y, x ∈ X and E(x, y) which denotes the adjacency relation in a given graph.
The Boolean connectives are ∧, ∨, ¬, → and ↔ and the quantiﬁers are ∃ and ∀.
Let MSOL be the monadic second order logic (with quantiﬁcation over subsets of
vertices) and let FO be the ﬁrst order logic (with quantiﬁcation over vertices).

Theorem 6.5. Given a graph G having bounded local-treewidth, the GP-Decp3(k)
decision problem is FPT when parameterized by the size k of the solution. More
precisely, it can be solved in O(f (k) · n2 ) time.

Proof. Consider the following ﬁrst order formula gp-Setp3(X), where X ⊆ V (G),
which is true if and only if X is a P3 general position set of G:

      gp-Setp3 (X) := ∀u, v, w ∈ X :        E(u, v) ∧ (u 6= w) → ¬E(v, w) .

Therefore the decision problem GP-Decp3(k) is FO expressible from the following
formula of size O(k 2 ):

  GP-Decp3 (k) := ∃v1 , . . . , vk ∈ V (G) : gp-Setp3 ({v1 , . . . , vk }) ∧   vi 6= vj

Then, from the Frick-Grohe Theorem (see [17]), GP-Decp3(k) is FPT with param-
eter k in time O(n2 ) for graphs with bounded local treewidth.
    Finally, we prove the following theorem on bounded cliquewidth graphs, such
as cographs (cliquewidth 2), distance hereditary graphs (cliquewidth 3), (q, q − 4)-
graphs and bounded treewidth graphs.

Theorem 6.6. Deciding whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k is FPT parameterized by cliquewidth
of G. Moreover, the problem of finding a maximum P3 general position set is poly-
nomial time solvable in bounded cliquewidth graphs.

Proof. In graphs, an optimization problem is LinEMSOL if it wants to maximize (or
minimize) some linear function over the sizes of ℓ subsets (of vertices), which satisfy
an MSOL formula, for ﬁxed ℓ. In [16], it was proved that LinEMSOL optimization
problems are polynomial time solvable in graphs with bounded cliquewidth. The

                                             15
running time is linear if a cliquewidth expression is given. Moreover, a cliquewidth
expression can be obtained in cubic time in bounded cliquewidth graphs.
    Since the P3 general position optimization problem wants to obtain the maximum
subset X ⊆ V (G) satisfying the formula GP-Decp3(k) described previously, which
is a FO and an MSOL formula, then the maximization problem is polynomial time
solvable in graphs with bounded cliquewidth.
    Moreover, given a positive integer k, it is possible to decide if gpp3 (G) ≥ k in
cubic time for bounded cliquewidth graphs, just running the cubic time algorithm
to obtain a cliquewidth expression and running the linear time algorithm for the
optimization problem and checking if k is at most the maximum value. This implies
that deciding whether gpp3 (G) ≥ k is FPT parameterized by the cliquewidth of
G.
   As a consequence, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6.7. The problem of obtaining a maximum general position set in the P3
convexity is linear time solvable in distance-hereditary graphs.

Proof. It is known that a cliquewidth expression of any distance hereditary graph
can be obtained in linear time [32]. From [17], LinEMSOL optimization problems are
linear time solvable in graphs with bounded cliquewidth, if a cliquewidth expression
is given, and we are done.
