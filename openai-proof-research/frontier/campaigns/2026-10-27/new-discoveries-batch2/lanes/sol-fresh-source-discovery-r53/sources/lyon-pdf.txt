                                           1    Introduction
                                           The median νB (α, β) of the beta distribution with shape parameters α and β, the solution x of
                                           Ix (α, β) = 21 , has no closed form except in special cases. Simple approximations to it are useful
                                           because it comes up often: it is the posterior median of a binomial proportion under a conjugate
                                           beta prior, such as the neutral prior Beta( 13 , 31 ) of Kerman (2011b), whose posteriors have both
                                           shape parameters at least 13 ; it is the median of the kth order statistic of a uniform sample of size
                                           n, which has the Beta(k, n − k + 1) distribution, and so gives the median ranks used in probability
                                           plotting; and it is a natural starting value for the numerical inversion of Ix .

2    Background and related approximations
Every ratio form is exact on the diagonal α = β of the (α, β) plane, where the median is 12 ,
and respects the symmetry νB (β, α) = 1 − νB (α, β). When β is large, the beta distribution ap-
proaches a gamma distribution scaled by 1/β, so β νB (α, β) → νΓ (α), the median of the gamma
distribution with shape parameter α and unit scale; a ratio form with g(x) ∼ x correspondingly
has β g(α)/(g(α) + g(β)) → g(α). So a good gamma median approximation g makes a good beta
median approximation at large β. The gamma median has no closed form either, but it has a
well-known asymptotic expansion (Choi, 1994; Berg and Pedersen, 2006),

3    Asymptotic bounds along rays
When both shape parameters are large, every member of the family (2) agrees with the median
to ﬁrst order in 1/α; the members diﬀer at second order, where c1 alone decides whether the
approximation lies above or below the median. Along each ray β = rα with r > 1, the dividing
value of c1 is explicit. The two theorems below are proved in Appendix A.

Bounds. Although ν1/36 is an asymptotic upper bound on every ray (Section 3), it is not an
upper bound everywhere: its negative errors are conﬁned to a region near the diagonal that shrinks
as α grows. The sign of the error just oﬀ the diagonal changes at α ≈ 2.059, and, numerically on
our grid at least, ν1/36 ≥ νB throughout 2.06 ≤ α ≤ β. Paulson’s νWH is also an asymptotic upper
bound on every ray, and on our grid it is an upper bound throughout 1 ≤ α ≤ β, but with six times
the worst error of ν1/36 there.

Smaller shape parameters. Below α = 1 the errors of ν1/36 grow, mainly on the negative
side (Table 1). Over α, β ≥ 31 , its relative error is at most 14.4% in magnitude, while Kerman’s
approximation reaches −100% at α = 13 , where its g vanishes; the minimax member for that domain
(Section 6) keeps the error within 2.44%.

9    Summary
Replacing Kerman’s g(x) = x − 13 by g(x) = (x − 61 )2 /x, that is, adding 1/(36x), keeps the
ratio form simple, symmetric and exact on the diagonal, and reduces the worst relative error over


                                                         8
1 ≤ α ≤ β from 3.8% to 0.21%. Numerically on our grid, and asymptotically by Theorem 2, the
new approximation is more accurate than Kerman’s wherever the latter is deﬁned, with at most 13
                                                                                              32
                                    1                       1
of its error. Theorem 1 places c1 = 36 above the threshold 45 for an asymptotic upper bound on
every ray, the ideal-parameter map of Section 5 shows where it overestimates and underestimates,
and the same g approximates the gamma median within 0.212% for shape parameter at least 1. For
posteriors under Kerman’s neutral prior, the member c1 ≈ 0.0326 keeps the relative error within
2.44% over the extended domain 31 ≤ α ≤ β.

A.1    The median logit
The proofs work with the logit, logit p = log(p/(1 − p)), of the median. Three properties make it
convenient. Because the logit is increasing, it maps the median of a distribution on (0, 1) to the
median of the transformed distribution: logit νB (α, β) is the median of logit X for X ∼ Beta(α, β),
whose density is smooth and log-concave on the whole real line. The logit of a ratio form separates
into a diﬀerence, logit g(α)/(g(α) + g(β)) = log g(α) − log g(β), so it can be compared with the
median one shape parameter at a time. And the symmetry νB (β, α) = 1 − νB (α, β) becomes a
change of sign, with logit νB = 0 on the diagonal.
    Write ∆ = 1/α−1/β, a measure of the departure from the diagonal: it is zero there and positive
for α < β. Along a ray β = rα, we have ∆ = (1 − 1/r)/α, so approximations to the logit of the
median can be ordered by powers of 1/α. We use three, of orders 0, 1 and 2:
