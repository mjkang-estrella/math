                                                  Abstract. In 1888, Hilbert proved that the cone 𝒫n+1,2d of positive
                                                  semideﬁnite forms in n + 1 variables of degree 2d coincides with its
                                                  subcone Σn+1,2d of those forms that are representable as ﬁnite sums of
                                                  squares if and only if (n + 1, 2d) = (2, 2d)d⩾ 1 or (n + 1, 2)n⩾ 1 or (3, 4).
                                                  These are the Hilbert cases. In [12, 13], we applied the Gram matrix
                                                  method to construct cones between Σn+1,2d and 𝒫n+1,2d , deﬁned by
                                                  projective varieties containing the Veronese variety. In particular, we
                                                  introduced and examined a speciﬁc cone ﬁltration
                                                      Σn+1,2d = C0 ⊆ . . . ⊆ Cn ⊆ Cn+1 ⊆ . . . ⊆ Ck(n,d)−n = 𝒫n+1,2d
                                                  and determined each strict inclusion in non-Hilbert cases. This gave us
                                                  a reﬁnement of Hilbert’s 1888 theorem. Here, k(n, d)+1 is the dimension
                                                  of the vector space of forms in n + 1 variables of degree d.
                                                     In this paper, we show that the intermediate cones Ci ’s are closed
                                                  and describe their interiors and boundaries. We discuss the membership
                                                  problem for the Ci ’s, present open problems concerning their dual cones
                                                  and generalizations to cones deﬁned by toric varieties.




                                                                            1. Introduction
                                              A real form (i.e., a homogeneous polynomial) is called (globally) positive
                                           semi-definite (PSD) if it only takes non-negative values and is called a sum
                                           of squares (SOS) if it is a finite sum of squares of half degree real forms. Let
                                           ℱn+1,l denote the vector space of real forms in n + 1 variables of degree l,
                                           where n, l ∈ N. Call f ∈ ℱn+1,l a (n + 1)-ary l-ic. For K ⊆ Rn+1 , f ∈ ℱn+1,l
                                           is called locally PSD on K ⊆ Rn+1 , if f(x) ⩾ 0 for all x ∈ K. In the special
                                           case of K = Rn+1 , f is (globally) PSD. For d ∈ N, the set of all PSD forms in
                                           ℱn+1,2d , denoted by 𝒫n+1,2d , is a closed pointed full-dimensional convex cone
                                           (see [25]) that contains no straight line. The set of all SOS forms in ℱn+1,2d ,
                                           denoted by Σn+1,2d , is also a closed pointed full-dimensional convex cone
                                           (see [25]). Clearly, Σn+1,2d ⊆ 𝒫n+1,2d . It is crucial to determine when the
                                           inverse inclusion holds as well. Hilbert’s 1888 Theorem states that Σn+1,2d =
                                           𝒫n+1,2d exactly in the Hilbert cases (n + 1, 2d) with n + 1 = 2 or 2d = 2 or
                                           (n + 1, 2d) = (3, 4). Therefore, in any non-Hilbert case, Σn+1,2d ( 𝒫n+1,2d .
                                           The first explicit examples of a PSD-not-SOS ternary sextic and a PSD-
                                           not-SOS quaternary quartic were given by Motzkin [21] and Robinson [26],
                                           respectively. Over the years a significant interest in geometric properties
                                           of the cones Σn+1,2d and 𝒫n+1,2d has been developed. For instance, both
