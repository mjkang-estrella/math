                                                                      1. Introduction and Background

                                              Since more than 20 years, Greedy Approximation Theory is one of the most impor-
                                           tant areas in the field of Non-linear Approximation Theory. Within the field, there are
                                           many researchers working on and analyzing greedy-type algorithms, but one of the lead-
                                           ing figures who has been investigating greedy algorithms for over 20 years is Vladimir
                                           Temlyakov (see [22]). The greedy algorithms introduced by V. N. Temlyakov have a sig-
                                           nificant impact on approximation theory and on the understanding of signal and data
                                           compression.
                                              In general, greedy algorithms are a family of approximation algorithms that make
                                           decisions at each step based on the choice that seems best at that moment, with the
                                           hope of finding a globally optimal solution. Although these algorithms do not always
                                           guarantee the optimal solution in all problems, they are especially valuable in scenarios
                                           where finding an exact optimal solution is computationally intractable. Some fields where
                                           these algorithms appear are the following:
                                                • Greedy algorithms in the field of Hilbert and Banach spaces with regarding to
                                                  dictionaries is an important technique since in data and signal compression.
                                                • Greedy algorithms regarding to bases in Banach spaces aim to build approxima-
                                                  tions by iteratively selecting the “most significant” coefficients respect to this basis.
                                                  This process is particularly relevant in the study of sparse approximations, where
                                                  one seeks to represent a function or signal with as few basis elements as possible
                                                  while maintaining a certain level of accuracy.

               2. Bases and dictionaries in quasi-Banach spaces

  To introduce and analyze the different greedy algorithms, we need to fix some concepts.
One the one hand, a quasi-Banach space is a complete quasi-normed vector space over
F = R or C, where a quasi-norm on a vector space X is a function k · k : X → R+ that
satisfies the following properties:
  Q1) kxk = 0 ⇐⇒ x = 0.
  Q2) kαxk = |α|kxk for all α ∈ R or C and x ∈ X.
  Q3) There exists a constant C ≥ 1 such that kx + yk ≤ C(kxk + kyk) for all x, y ∈ X.
   If C = 1, the quasi-norm k · k is a norm, and the quasi-Banach space is called a
Banach space. A typical example of a quasi-Banach space is ℓp , 0 < p < ∞, where this

   On the other hand, a p-Banach space is a generalization of a Banach space where the
norm satisfies a modified version of the triangle inequality. For 0 < p ≤ 1, a p-norm on a
vector space X is a function k · k : X → R+ that satisfies the following properties for all
x, y ∈ X and all scalars α ∈ R or C:
  P1) kxk = 0 ⇐⇒ x = 0.
  P2) kαxk = |α|kxk.
  P3) kx + ykp ≤ kxkp + kykp.
  If p = 1, the p-norm is a norm, and the p-Banach space is a Banach space. Also, since
P3) implies Q3), a p-Banach space is a quasi-Banach space. Thanks to the Aoki-Rolewicz

  Finally, we will talk about dictionaries. A dictionary D is a subset of a quasi-Banach
spaces X with the following properties:

Some examples of dictionaries are:

     • Haar Dictionary: in the space L2 ([0, 1]), the Haar wavelet system is a dictionary.
       The atoms are the Haar wavelets, which are piecewise constant functions that form
       an orthonormal basis for L2 ([0, 1]).
     • Trigonometric Dictionary: in the space L2 ([0, 2π]), the set of functions {einx }n∈Z
       forms a dictionary. These atoms are the complex exponentials, which are used in
       Fourier analysis.
     • Overcomplete Dictionary: in the context of signal processing, an overcomplete
       dictionary might consist of a union of several bases (e.g., wavelet and Fourier bases)
       to provide more flexibility in representing signals.

  Related to the properties of dictionaries, we can mention the following:

     • Redundancy: dictionaries are often redundant, meaning that there can be mul-
       tiple representations of the same element in terms of the dictionary atoms. This
       redundancy can be advantageous in applications such as sparse approximation.
     • Flexibility: dictionaries are not required to be bases, allowing more flexibility
       in the choice of atoms. This makes them suitable for various approximation and
       signal processing tasks where traditional bases may not be ideal.
     • Approximation: dictionaries are used in various greedy algorithms for approxi-
       mation, where elements of X are approximated by selecting atoms from D based
       on certain criteria (e.g., matching pursuit and orthogonal matching pursuit).

                               kxk0 = max{akxk, kT (x)kY }

equality, T has to be linear and the greedy sums are not linear, so for that reason there is
not a theory about renormings for greedy-like bases in Banach spaces. One paper where
this idea is posed is [14], where the authors proved that, for a fixed ǫ > 0, it is possible
to find a renorming in Lp , 1 < p < ∞, such that the Haar system is (1 + ǫ)-greedy, but it
is open whether one can get the constant 1.
