Abstract
This article addresses a conjecture by Schilling concerning the optimality of the
uniform distribution in the generalized Coupon Collector’s Problem (CCP) where, in
each round, a subset (package) of s coupons is drawn from a total of n distinct coupons.
While the classical CCP (with single-coupon draws) is well understood, the group-
draw variant–where packages of size s are drawn–presents new challenges and has
applications in areas such as biological network models.
Schilling conjectured that, for 2 ≤ s ≤ n−1, the uniform distribution over all possi-
ble packages minimizes the expected number of rounds needed to collect all coupons if
and only if s = n−1. We prove Schilling’s conjecture in full by presenting, for all other
values of s, “natural” non-uniform distributions yielding strictly lower expected collec-
tion times. Explicit formulas and asymptotic analyses are provided for the expected
number of rounds under these and related distributions.
The article further explores the behavior of the expected collection time as s varies
under the uniform distribution, including the cases where s is constant, proportional
to n, or nearly n.
Keywords: Coupon Collector’s Problem (CCP), Group Drawings, Uniform Dis-
tribution, Expected Collection Time, Schilling’s Conjecture, Optimal Distribution.

In the classical case of s = 1, Boneh and Hofri [2] proved that the expected number of steps
attains its minimum if and only if the distribution is uniform over all coupons. Here, we
deal with 2 ≤ s ≤ n − 1. Schilling [12] stated
Conjecture A. For 2 ≤ s ≤ n − 1, the uniform distribution leads to the minimal expected
number of drawings if and only if s = n − 1.
2She proved the “if” part of the conjecture. Chang and Fang [7] identified the optimal
distribution in the case where n is even and s = n/2. An optimal distribution in this
case is the uniform distribution over any two disjoint packages of n/2 coupons each. They
also proved that the optimal distribution is unique (up to the identity of the coupons in
each package). There is an analog for this distribution when s | n. Here we take the
uniform distribution over n/s pairwise disjoint packages of s coupons each. Chang and
Fang conjectured that the latter is always an optimal distribution. We call this distribution
the “decomposition distribution” and denote it by Dn,s.
Caron, Hlynka, and McDonald [3] showed that n = 3 is the only case for which the
uniform distribution leads to an optimum of Y2, i.e., they proved the “only if” part of
Schilling’s conjecture for s = 2.
This work takes two directions. The first is dedicated to proving Schilling’s conjecture.
Given n and s, we will find bounds on the expectation under the uniform distribution and
present better distributions (expectation-wise). In the second, we study the asymptotic
expected number of drawings when the distribution is uniform, namely E[Ys], as s varies
from 2 to n − 1.
In Section 2 we state our main results. The proofs are presented in Section 3.

Schilling suggested, roughly speaking, doing so by showing that the Hessian matrix,

cases. (For a more accurate description of what she has done, see [12].)
2. The wishful approach: explicitly define a distribution, prove that it is optimal and that
it yields a strictly smaller expected number of drawings than the uniform distribution.
3. A midway approach: present a specific distribution yielding a lower expected number
of drawings than does the uniform distribution.
We will stick to the third approach, although in some cases we conjecture the distributions
we present are in fact optimal. It is interesting to note that the distributions are of different
types for s ≥ ⌊n/2⌋ and s < ⌊n/2⌋. The following additional distributions are important
for our work:
1. When s ∤ n, we examine the “near-decomposition distribution”, that is similar to the
decomposition distribution, defined above. Let q,r ∈ N be such that n = q · s + r
with 0 < r < s. We divide n − r of the coupons into q pairwise disjoint packages,
of size s each. There are r coupons left (the remainder), which we put in a new
package. To complete this last package to size s, we add to it arbitrary s−r coupons,
different than the r already inside. Thus, we have q+1 packages in total and take the
uniform distribution over them. For simplicity, denote this distribution also by Dn,s,
same as the decomposition distribution in the case where n is divisible by s. In the
sequel, when s | n, we regard the decomposition distribution as a special case of the
near-decomposition distribution.
2. Given n coupons, enumerate them as 1,2,...,n − 1,n. Consider the uniform distri-
bution on the collection of all packages consisting of s “consecutive” coupons (modulo
n), namely
{1,2,...,s − 1,s}, {2,3,...,s,s + 1},..., {n,1,...,s − 2,s − 1}.
We will refer to this distribution as the “arcs distribution”. It is instructive here
to view the coupons as arranged along a circle (see Figure 1). Denote by tn(s) the
expected number of drawings under this distribution. When s is clear from context,
we write tn.
