OPTIMIZATION OF THE SECOND ROBIN EIGENVALUE ON A
PLANAR EXTERIOR DOMAIN UNDER FIXED AREA CONSTRAINT
DAVID KREJČIŘÍK AND VLADIMIR LOTOREICHIK
Abstract. We consider the Robin Laplacian on a planar exterior domain with a negative
boundary parameter. For a negative boundary parameter of a sufficiently large absolute
value this operator has at least two negative eigenvalues below the bottom of the essential
spectrum. We prove that under a fixed area constraint and convexity assumption on the
bounded complement of the exterior domain the second Robin eigenvalue is maximized by
the exterior of the disk. The inequality is strict for every exterior of a non-circular domain
whenever the comparison disk has a negative second eigenvalue. The proof modifies the
parallel-coordinate method by allowing the angular phase of a trial function to vary with
the distance from the boundary. This result resolves the first item in [KL24, Conjecture 1.2]
by the authors under an additional convexity assumption. Relaxing the convexity and the
perimeter constraint remain open problems.
1. Introduction and main result
Optimization of eigenvalues with respect to the shape of the domain is one of the central
topics in spectral geometry. The most classical result in this area is the hundred-year-old
Faber–Krahn inequality stating that the ball minimizes the first Dirichlet eigenvalue under
fixed volume constraint. Changing the boundary conditions makes the optimization problem
much younger and still unresolved in several aspects.
The present paper is concerned with the Robin problem

case and extended by Daners [D06] in 2006 to higher dimensions.
The case of negative α remains mysterious. For α < 0, Bareket [B77] conjectured in 1977
that the disk instead maximizes the lowest Robin eigenvalue under fixed area constraint for
planar domains. However, it turned out in 2015 [FK15] that annuli, and higher-dimensional
spherical shells, outperform the equal-volume ball for sufficiently negative α. The corrected
conjecture [AFK17] involves simply-connectedness assumption in two dimensions. Freitas
and the first author proved in [FK15] the disk maximality for smooth planar domains when
α is sufficiently close to zero, uniformly at fixed area; local maximality results also support
the conjecture [FNT15]. Optimality of the ball for the lowest eigenvalue in the weak coupling
regime and under fixed volume constraint was extended in [GKP26] to convex domains in
every dimension, with the threshold depending only on volume and dimension. Global validity
for every α < 0 remains open for simply connected planar domains and for convex domains in
higher dimensions. Under a different constraint, fixed perimeter, disk maximality for planar

The negative boundary parameter makes the optimization problem for the Robin eigenvalues
meaningful also on exterior domains. Let us discuss this setting in more detail. Let Ω ⊂ R2
be a bounded simply-connected smooth domain. The exterior domain Ωext := R2 \ Ω is the
complement of Ω. It is an unbounded domain with compact boundary. Negative Robin
boundary conditions give rise to the existence of finitely many negative eigenvalues for the
Robin Laplacian on Ωext, while the essential spectrum of this operator coincides with [0,∞).
The authors of the present paper established that the disk is a maximizer for the first Robin
eigenvalue on exterior planar domains [KL18, KL20] under both fixed area and fixed perimeter
constraints. The paper [KL18] dealt with convex domains, while the convexity assumption
was removed later in [KL20]. These optimization results for the lowest Robin eigenvalue on
planar exterior domains are generalized in [BGS26] to the Robin p-Laplacian.
We subsequently conjectured the corresponding fixed-area and fixed-perimeter optimality
of the disk for the second eigenvalue, too [KL24, Conjecture 1.2]. In the same paper, we
proved an isoperimetric inequality for the second eigenvalue under a fixed boundary elastic
energy constraint and under additional assumption on the convexity of Ω, where the proof
was based on an improvement of the method developed in [EL22]. We remark that in space
dimensions d ≥ 3 the exterior of the ball is not a global maximizer of the lowest Robin
eigenvalue under fixed volume or perimeter constraints [KL18]. It becomes a global maximizer
in the class of exteriors of bounded convex domains under a constraint involving the mean
curvature [KL20]. However, locally the ball is still a maximizer for the lowest eigenvalue
under fixed volume constraint in all space dimensions [B25]. Exterior domains can be viewed
as an extreme case of doubly-connected domains, for which the optimization of the Robin
eigenvalues was analyzed in [ABD25]; see also the references therein.

Remark 6.1 (Where convexity enters). Convexity is used in three places: the global exterior
parallel coordinates, the area–variance inequality, and the Taylor estimate with the nonnega-
tive measure κds. The proof does not invoke an orthogonality condition against the unknown
ground state. Instead, it controls the whole shifted form on a two-dimensional trial space.
Remark 6.2 (Relation to the earlier parallel-coordinate method). The trial functions in [KL24,
Section 4] use complexified boundary tangent as their angular factor, and their mixed terms
vanish exactly. In the present construction the phase varies with t, producing the term
C/(p + t)4 in the effective angular potential. The mixed term is generally nonzero, but its
quadratic estimate (5.7) is small enough for (6.1). The same disk radial profile g is used in
both trial functions.
Remark 6.3 (Scope and threshold comparisons). Theorem 1.1 addresses the fixed-area part
of [KL24, Conjecture 1.2] within the convex C∞-smooth domains. Its argument does not
establish the fixed-perimeter comparison, which is a stronger claim and does not follow by
scaling. Exterior Steklov inequalities and their connection with the Robin threshold are
developed in [BGGLP26]. Those results provide complementary comparisons for the critical
coupling constant under fixed perimeter constraint.
