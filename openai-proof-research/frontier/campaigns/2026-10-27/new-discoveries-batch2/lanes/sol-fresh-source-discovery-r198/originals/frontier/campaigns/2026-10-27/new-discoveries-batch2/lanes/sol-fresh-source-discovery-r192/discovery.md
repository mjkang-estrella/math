## Full-quantifier reductions

The per-space and uniform-in-X readings are equivalent for the consecutive question. Suppose no universal constant exists. Choose real normed spaces X_j and integers n_j≥2 with Λn_j(X_j)>j n_j. Form the normed ℓ∞ direct sum Y of the X_j: bounded tuples with supremum norm. The coordinate embedding i_j and projection p_j are contractions with p_j i_j=id. Any retraction on Y(n_j) transfers pointwise to p_j r i_j on X_j(n_j), with no increase in Lipschitz constant. Thus Λn_j(Y)≥Λn_j(X_j)>j n_j. Y cannot satisfy an O(n) bound with one finite constant, contradicting the per-space claim. Conversely, a universal constant plainly gives a constant for each space. Infima need not be attained: apply the transfer to each available retraction and take infima. Completeness is unnecessary. This is a reduction, not a source resolution or a credited theorem.

A sufficient missing operator lemma is precise. For every X,n and positive scale s, seek a permutation-equivariant map T_s:X^n->X^n with (i) a pair at distance ≤s has equal output entries; (ii) max_i||T_s(x)_i−x_i||≤a n s; (iii) when max_i||x_i−x'_i||≤ε, |s−s'|≤2ε, max(s,s')>2ε, outputs differ coordinatewise by at most b n ε. Constants a,b must be universal. Define r(A)=A for |A|<n; otherwise apply T at s=δn(A), the minimum separation, and forget labels. Property (i) reduces cardinality; equivariance makes the map well-defined. If both separations are ≤2ε, the triangle inequality and (ii) bound the output distance by (1+4an)ε. Otherwise the larger separation is >2ε, so nearest-neighbor matching is a bijection of the two n-point sets and |δn(A)−δn(B)|≤2ε. Property (iii) gives bnε. Hence this lemma would solve the full question. The source already supplies (i),(ii), but (iii) currently costs n log n. We have NOT constructed the missing operators.

## Exact failed methods

1. Removing the source averaging step does not keep outputs in X. Take X={(u,v,w) in R³:u+v+w=0}, with the sup norm, and x1=(0,0,0), x2=(2,0,−2), x3=(0,4,−4). The minimum separation is s=2. Source scalar proximal map P_t minimizes 1/2 sum(u_i−a_i)²+t sum_{i<j}|u_i−u_j|; apply P_1 coordinatewise. In coordinate 1 the data (0,2,0) fuse to (2/3,2/3,2/3); in coordinate 2, (0,0,4) become (1,1,2); coordinate 3, (0,−2,−4), becomes (−2,−2,−2). Thus the three output sums are −1/3,−1/3,2/3: none lies in X. These exact minimizers follow from the sorted isotonic formula and satisfy convex directional optimality (also checked independently). The collision succeeds, but there is no valid retraction into X. This does not prove that a different embedding or map fails.

2. Hard closest-pair merging is discontinuous. On the real line compare Aε={0,2,4+ε} and Bε={0,2,4−ε}, 0<ε<1/2. Their distance is 2ε. The unique closest pair in Aε is {0,2}, and in Bε is {2,4−ε}. Replacing that pair by its midpoint gives {1,4+ε} and {0,3−ε/2}; output distance is 1+3ε/2. The ratio diverges as ε->0. No tie-breaking convention repairs this one-sided discontinuity. This defeats that rule, not Question 6.5.

3. The logarithm is forced in a specific radial-weight estimate. Suppose φ≥0 is absolutely continuous on [c,b], φ(b)=0, φ(c)≥n, and |φ'|≤L(φ+1) almost everywhere. Then log(1+φ) is absolutely continuous, with derivative of absolute value ≤L. Integrating gives log(n+1)≤L(b−c). In the source c=(n−1)/2, b=(3n−1)/2, so b−c=n and L≥log(n+1)/n. The source exponential φ(r)=(exp(λ(b−r))−1)_+, λ=log(n+1)/n, attains this lower bound. Thus replacing λ by C/n with fixed C cannot satisfy all THESE hypotheses for unbounded n. This is not a lower bound for every weighting scheme, every source-map Lipschitz constant, or Λn itself. A more geometric cancellation estimate could still remove the logarithm; the source total-variation argument need not be sharp.

## Complete applicable classical/source bridge and its precise limit

There IS a full linear bridge for absolute λ-Lipschitz retracts, already public in source Corollary6.3. Embed a nonempty metric X into ℓ∞(X) by i(x)(z)=d(x,z)−d(o,z). Boundedness follows from the triangle inequality; equality in the isometry follows by choosing z=x. The absolute-retract hypothesis gives p:ℓ∞(X)->i(X) with Lip(p)≤λ and p restricted to i(X) the identity. Source Theorem2.3 supplies R:ℓ∞(X)(n)->ℓ∞(X)(n−1) with Lip(R)≤2n−1. Pointwise p∘R∘i fixes every smaller set, has at most n−1 points, and has constant ≤λ(2n−1). Every hypothesis is accounted for. This covers ℓ∞ and other absolute retracts; it supplies no dimension-independent p for an arbitrary normed space. A scalar McShane extension extends scalar functions, not an X-valued retraction. Hilbert gradient flow uses inner-product monotonicity and yields n^(3/2), still insufficient. No complete classical bridge was found for ALL normed spaces, and no routine/shortness-based exclusion is asserted.
