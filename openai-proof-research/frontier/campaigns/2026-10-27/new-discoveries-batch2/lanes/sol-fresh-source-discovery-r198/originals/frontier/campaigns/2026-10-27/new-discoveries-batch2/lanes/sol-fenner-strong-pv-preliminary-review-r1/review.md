## Independent mathematical reconstruction and attacks

**§1: integer approximation PASS.** Starting with binomial weights, the variance bound is x(1−x)/n≤1/(4n); uniform continuity plus that bound gives ordinary Bernstein convergence. Rounding unnormalized interior coefficients changes the polynomial by at most half the sum of x^k(1−x)^(n−k). For x≤δ<1/2, summing its geometric majorant gives ≤δ/(1−2δ); on [δ,1−δ] each summand is ≤(1−δ)^n. Choose δ first and n second. The two unrounded coefficients preserve the integer endpoints, and expanding every (1−x) power gives a polynomial in Z[x]. No assertion of arbitrary integer interpolation at interior points is used.

**§2: weighted lifting PASS.** For a=x(1−x)q, choose N0,N1 independently so a′(0)+m(0)N0>0 and a′(1)−m(1)N1<0. Nonzero m(0),m(1) make both integer choices possible, including either sign. Thus g rises from 0 at the left and falls to 0 at the right. At the finitely many real roots inside (0,1), positivity of a allows disjoint neighborhoods on which g=a. The intervening intervals can be joined inside the convex interval (0,1). Defining h=(g−a)/(mx(1−x)) gives h=0 near those roots and the integers N0,N1 near the endpoints. Thus there is no quotient singularity or nonintegral endpoint obstruction. The quotient g/[x(1−x)] has positive finite endpoint limits; (1−g)/[x(1−x)] diverges positively. On the remaining compact middle interval both have positive minima. A common positive c exists. Approximating h within c/(2 max|m|) then gives P=a+mx(1−x)H with the claimed strict inequalities and congruence. Merely approximating g in the unweighted norm would fail; this proof does not do that.

A shorter, equivalent presentation is to work with q=a/[x(1−x)], choose continuous h so F=q+mh>0 and 1−x(1−x)F>0 on the entire closed interval, and approximate h preserving its chosen integer endpoints. Local feasible constants glue by a partition of unity because the constraints are affine in h. Compactness then supplies two positive uniform margins without singular quotients. This is exactly the presentation in Jin's Lemma 3. Separability is harmless and holds for the minimal polynomial; this shorter lifting formulation does not even need simple roots.

**§3: hull membership PASS.** The monomial-to-unnormalized-Bernstein identity has integer coefficients binom(n−j,k−j). For a polynomial positive on (0,1), remove all endpoint factors x^r(1−x)^s. The residual polynomial is strictly positive on [0,1], including endpoints by continuity and exact multiplicities. For fixed j the normalized degree-n coefficients (k)_j/(n)_j approach (k/n)^j uniformly, including k<j. They are therefore eventually positive. Restoring endpoint factors preserves nonnegativity. Do this for P and 1−P at a common sufficiently large degree; their coefficients sum to binom(n,k). Each integer coefficient can be realized by choosing that many 1-labeled leaves of weight k in a full depth-n tree. No rational coefficients or limiting infinite tree are substituted for finite generation.

**§4: projection density PASS.** This applies to full-rank arithmetic lattices embedded from K, not every arbitrary Euclidean lattice. For a nondense additive subgroup of a Euclidean space its closure splits into a vector space and a discrete subgroup of the orthogonal complement. A nonzero real functional integer-valued on the closure exists, either by annihilating its span or using a lattice dual. Pulling this functional back from a proper whole-coordinate selection gives a real functional L on full Minkowski space. Its values on a Q-basis of the full-rank lattice are integers; hence its restriction to K is Q-valued and is Tr(b·) for a unique b∈K. The Minkowski images of a field basis are a real basis: a real polynomial relation of degree below d vanishing at the selected representatives also vanishes at their conjugates, contradicting separability. Thus the trace representation extends as a real functional on the entire ambient space. Its omitted-coordinate coefficient vanishes; injectivity of a field embedding forces b=0, a contradiction. A complex coordinate is two real dimensions and must be omitted as a whole. The formula 2 Re(σ(b)z) is the trace pairing, so zero on that factor really forces σ(b)=0.

**§5: necessity PASS.** For an algebraic integer of degree d, monicity makes Z[λ] free over Z on 1,…,λ^(d−1). Multiplication by λ(1−λ)≠0 is a Q-linear isomorphism; hence J is full rank, with a full real embedding basis and a lattice image. If λ is not sPV and lies outside (0,1), retain its physical factor and every real conjugate in (0,1). At least one other whole factor is omitted. Density therefore meets the nonempty product of a physical annulus 0<|z|<1/2 and the internal intervals (1/3,2/3). Its preimage lies in J and has a representative a divisible by x(1−x). The lifting lemma applies at every required real root. It fixes a(λ) and puts that nonzero contraction into the literal hull. Replacing the leaves in a finite expression for v by 0,u proves uv belongs to the hull. Strictly decreasing moduli of powers then give distinct points accumulating at 0. Alternatively density can produce such points for arbitrarily small annuli directly, avoiding multiplication closure. For λ∈(0,1) the powers of λ already suffice. Roots 0,1 cannot occur in the remaining irreducible minimal polynomial.

**§6: sufficiency PASS.** Tree evaluation at each other conjugate in (0,1) lies in [0,1]. A nonzero difference d of generated points is an algebraic integer with nonzero integer field norm. The factors at all internal embeddings have absolute value ≤1. The physical factor is |d| for real λ or |d|² for nonreal λ; hence |d|≥1. Degree-one integers and degree-two nonreal parameters have no internal restrictions and are covered. A real quadratic with a bad real conjugate has a proper one-coordinate projection; with a good conjugate it is already sPV. No low-degree exception is lost.
